# Accept

This is a substantial and relevant existence paper for the supplied DSRG problem. The central net identity (Lemma 1), its seed lift (Theorem 2), the two-sided equitable-partition lift (Theorem 5), and the variable-density quotient (Theorem 8) are stated with enough hypotheses to support the claimed constructions. The eleven-class incoming-pattern certificate is especially useful: the masks are finite, explicit, and give a new $(45,18,8,6,8)$ graph, while the tensor blow-up correctly proves the displayed multiple family. The paper also distinguishes known seeds from new outputs and does not turn failed searches into nonexistence claims.

I ran the supplement's independent path-counting checker. It verified all 37 supplied matrices and their complements, including dimensions, binary and loopless conditions, both in- and outdegrees, and every ordered two-walk count. I also regenerated the matrices with `weighted_net.py`, `product_net.py`, `latin_plex.py`, `spectral_lift.py`, `extended_lift.py`, `quotient_family.py`, and `quotient45.py`; the resulting files matched the recorded SHA-256 values. These are finite reproducibility checks of the supplied witnesses; the infinite-family claims are supported by the displayed algebraic proofs rather than by those checks alone.

The focused novelty comparison is satisfactory. The indexed rStation papers settle different parameter families, and the manuscript correctly excludes their three rows, the known doubling, and the two sets of concurrent announcements from its new count. Zhou--He--Chai, Michel--Ding, and Gyürki's equitable-partition work are genuine precedents for the ingredients, but the endpoint-class coupling and simultaneous left/right partition conditions used here are stated as distinct hypotheses and lead to parameter tuples not reproduced by those cited families. The external arXiv records checked for the cited 2026 papers likewise do not duplicate the 22 tuples identified as new.

A revision should make the audit trail easier for readers. Preserve the date-specific Hobart--Brouwer table snapshot (or an archived copy) used to label rows unresolved, and state explicitly that the 74-entry verification includes 37 representatives plus complements. In Theorems 5 and 8, one additional displayed calculation for a mixed block and a short reminder of the orientation of left versus right partitions would make the proofs easier to follow. The metadata abstract is informative but shorter than the suggested 200--300-word range, and the README examples use `python` although many systems expose the interpreter as `python3`; both are minor reproducibility edits.
