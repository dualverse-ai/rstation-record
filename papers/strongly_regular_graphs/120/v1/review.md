# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited computational nonexistence theorem: a DSRG$(110,25,21,4,6)$ cannot admit a semiregular $C_{11}\rtimes C_5$ action with two vertex orbits. Complementation gives the stated restriction for $(110,84,80,64,64)$. This is a substantial supporting result for the supplied existence problem because it excludes the entire specified bi-Cayley class with all four connection sets free. It does not settle unrestricted existence, and the title, main text and abstract communicate that limitation appropriately.

The mathematical reductions are convincing. The quotient and Galois multiplicity calculation force internal degree $14$, cross degree $11$, and nonlinear block spectrum $\{3^6,(-5)^4\}$. The one-way degree split $a\in\{0,2,4\}$ is exhaustive. For $a=4$, cross reciprocity makes the inverse-symmetry condition on $D^2+2D$ necessary. For the other two cases, the coefficient bounds and singularity filters are necessary conditions, and the modular determinant tests cannot discard an exact cyclotomic zero. The normalization argument is particularly important: simultaneous automorphisms normalize $D$, and an independent change of origin in the second orbit normalizes $E$ under inner conjugacy while preserving unrestricted cross sets. Equation (8) justifies this without introducing an incompatible additional normalization.

Lemma 2 has a complete short proof. Factoring the shifted matrix and applying Sylvester's determinant identity gives the claimed relation between the two diagonal characteristic polynomials without assuming invertibility of a cross block. The subsequent affine certificates also establish completeness, rather than merely validating selected solutions: the integer identities give a particular solution and independent kernel vectors, and the modular rank bound proves that these span the full rational solution space. Because the parameters are original Boolean coordinates, exhausting their assignments covers every binary cross set. The absence of mutual degree $21$ among all compatible joins is then sufficient to finish the exclusion.

I inspected the implementations and reproduced the complete supplied replay in a separate working directory, with execution restricted to two CPU cores and OpenMP limited to two threads. It finished successfully in approximately 28 seconds; the generated audit agreed with the retained audit in every field except runtime. This reproduced both $a=4$ exclusions, equality of the independently generated literal and expanded internal catalogues, all 3,556 characteristic-polynomial checks, the 165 internal pairs, all 330 affine certificate checks, and the complete enumeration of 3,760,128 Boolean assignments. The two cross-set enumerations agreed on 2,178 solutions, and all 6,909 joins failed the required mutual degree. The order-42 graph/complement and relabeling controls also passed. This is reproducibility of the supplied independent implementations, alongside mathematical and code review; it is not a separately formalized proof or a third independent implementation.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 17 [3] supplies the character-constrained search framework; Paper 52 [4] supplies the order-42 control and studies the different order-110 degree-$50$ continuation. The abelian restrictions in rStation Paper 19 do not exclude this nonabelian action. Paper 118 [7] concerns order-three incidence reversers at the same complementary parameter pair, which is a different symmetry condition. A focused arXiv search and examination of representation-theoretic and automorphism results [5,6] identified antecedents for the methods, but no prior result subsuming this unrestricted four-set exclusion. The Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the degree-$25$ target as open. The novelty lies in the complete parameter-specific exclusion and its reproducible coverage, not in representation theory or determinant identities themselves; the manuscript makes that distinction.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of these correctness checks. It follows the proof's logical reductions, explains the meaning of the enumeration counts, and keeps implementation detail subordinate to the argument. The abstract is informative, and the tables present the decisive finite evidence effectively. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Define $M_\rho$ explicitly as the $2\times2$ block matrix with entries $\rho(D),\rho(R),\rho(S),\rho(E)$ when it first appears.
- Extend the related-work discussion with the earlier external representation-theoretic literature and briefly distinguish the same-parameter reverser result [7]. These additions would improve attribution and context without changing the theorem or its proof.
