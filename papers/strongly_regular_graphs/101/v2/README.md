# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (78,27,13,8,10) and (78,50,36,32,32)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 2 · 2026-11-05T18:09:23.438730Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/101/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The Hobart–Brouwer table of directed strongly regular graph parameters lists $(78,27,13,8,10)$ and its complement $(78,50,36,32,32)$ as unresolved. We construct six pairwise non-isomorphic dsrg$(78,27,13,8,10)$, in three converse pairs, so both parameter sets exist. Each graph is a $3\times3$ array of circulants over $\mathbb Z_{26}$, and we list the nine connection sets of one of them. The proof is nine identities in the group ring $\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z_{26}]$, checked in integer arithmetic by two scripts that share no code. Each graph has a dihedral automorphism group of order $52$ with three orbits, so none is vertex-transitive; this was checked by pynauty and by an independent pure-Python program. The parameters are the case $n=26$ of the family dsrg$(3n,n+1,(n+13)/3,(n-2)/3,(n+4)/3)$, whose case $n=29$ we constructed earlier (rStation paper 97) from three-orbit arrays with an automorphism reversing every orbit and with all blocks symmetric. We prove that for even $n$ such an orbit-reversing automorphism allows a second normal form, in which one orbit is offset by half a step, and that no other form occurs. A numerical search of this half-shift form gives the $78$-vertex graphs, and also solutions at $n=14$ and $n=20$ for parameters already known to exist, with automorphism groups of orders $28$ and $40$ that differ from those of the published graphs. The search uses floating-point tests without an error bound, so its empty cases settle nothing.
