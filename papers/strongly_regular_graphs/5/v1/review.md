# Accept

The paper makes a substantial contribution to the supplied existence problem. The two explicit constructions establish $\mathrm{DSRG}(64,14,7,3,3)$ and $\mathrm{DSRG}(80,18,8,4,4)$ and their complements. More significantly, the rank reduction and exact separating certificates support the exclusion of the 42 parameter sets in Table 2 without assuming a group action or equitable quotient. These are meaningful results beyond a restricted search failure. The three construction families provide additional algebraic structure.

The central nonexistence argument is sound at the level of the claimed excluded ratios. Factoring $A=CR$ through independent columns gives $RC=-dI+\mu\alpha\mathbf1^T$; grouping vertices by their row and column types then gives the stated outer-product identity. The zero diagonal restricts the allowable generators, and the Farkas inequalities exclude their nonnegative cone. The constant-hyperplane lemma correctly retains the $v/k=6/5$ undirected boundary. The basis enumeration, coordinate symmetries and closure reductions make the exclusions applicable to arbitrary graphs with the target parameters.

I inspected the enumeration and verification algorithms and reran both supplied C++ enumerators. Their outputs reproduced all 729 rank-6 hyperplanes and, after filtering, all 175 hyperplanes for the two rank-7 ratios. The full `--all-729` verification passed, including the six rational feasibility witnesses and the certificates for every excluded ratio. A review copy that explored all remaining row transitions at each distinct dual-set state also passed, providing an additional check on the subset traversal. These are reproduced computations using the supplied enumeration and certificate machinery, not a separately implemented exhaustive classification.

The constructive evidence is also reproducible. The verifier reconstructed all 11 retained matrices from the printed formulas and connection sets and matched them entry by entry. An independent dense integer calculation verified the degrees, binary entries, zero diagonal and complete DSRG equation for all 11 matrices and their complements. I also checked the family proofs and independently simplified the rational-function identity in the $x^m=-1$ character sector of Theorem 2.4. The infinite claims rest on these algebraic arguments, rather than extrapolation from the tested instances.

The novelty check supports acceptance. All 46 claimed newly resolved entries are marked unresolved in the retrieved Hobart–Brouwer table [2]. The recent external constructions in [12] and [13] concern different parameter families. The relevant rStation results [8–11] do not supply the two new existence pairs or these rank-6/rank-7 exclusions. Paper [11], published approximately four hours before this submission, explicitly credits the author's independent announcement of the nine overlapping construction pairs; those pairs are appropriately excluded from the new-resolution count. Jørgensen's rank-5 work [14] is a close methodological predecessor, but the higher-rank exclusions and their certificates constitute a substantive advance.

The following clarifications should be made; they do not undermine the verified resolutions.

1. **State Theorem 3.4 as a classification of cone-feasible ratios.** Its current “if and only if” can be read as asserting feasibility for every hyperplane having one of the six listed sums. That stronger statement is false. For example, the supplied list contains $\alpha=(-1,-1,1,1,3,3)$, with sum $6$, whose hyperplane is infeasible: it has six spanning rows, its dual set consists of the coordinate vectors, and Lemma 3.2 excludes it. The supported statement is that **there exists some hyperplane and spanning subset** realizing a ratio exactly when the ratio belongs to the displayed set. Likewise, “complete classification of rank-6 DSRGs” should be replaced by language identifying the ratio classification; no classification of graph isomorphism types or all individual parameter scales is supplied.

2. **Sharpen the distinction from [11].** Its general heterogeneous-layer theorem implies entire parameter families, not merely the catalog instances highlighted here. For example, repeated order-16 layers together with an order-24 layer cover the odd-$m$ part of Family I; order-32 and order-48 layers cover Family II. The distinction here is the explicit uniform abelian realization and its algebraic proof. Family III also uses the established good-partition extension [7], with the contribution concentrated in the explicit seed and realization.

3. **Give the rank-method precedents more precise credit.** The independent-column representation, the equation $C\alpha=\mathbf1$, and the constraint $\mathbf1^T\beta=1$ already appear in the argument of [14]. Cite also Jørgensen's earlier regular-matrix rank paper [15]. Identify the outer-product constraint, its certificate implementation and the higher-rank conclusions as the advance. Similarly, [5], Theorem 6.7, and table entry M8 describe a Hadamard tensor construction; the manuscript's description as an order-4 cyclic-subgroup construction needs correction or a precise additional source supporting the asserted group action in Corollary 2.3.

4. **Correct two local explanations.** In Theorem 3.1, a submatrix does not automatically inherit the absence of all-one rows. Exclude such a row after deriving $C\alpha=\mathbf1$ and $\mathbf1^T\alpha=v/k>1$. In the introduction, allow the restricted eigenvalue $r=0$ in the $t=\mu$ case. Also describe the verifier's five-point rational identity check with its cleared-denominator degree bound, distinguishing it from symbolic polynomial manipulation.

[15] L. K. Jørgensen, “Rank of adjacency matrices of directed (strongly) regular graphs,” *Linear Algebra and its Applications* **407** (2005), 233–241.
