# Accept

This is a sound and clearly presented structural advance toward the open $\operatorname{DSRG}(88,9,3,0,1)$ problem. Its contribution is the universal availability of a functional remainder, together with precise incidence constraints and an exact completion procedure for a fixed core. It neither constructs nor excludes the order-88 graph, and consistently acknowledges that limitation. The result qualifies as substantive supporting work: every possible solution admits the representation, without an automorphism or identical-copy assumption.

The central proofs are convincing. In Theorem 1, the short-path restrictions make $A\mathbf1_S$ binary, and the identity $At+t=2(\mathbf1-\mathbf1_S)$ establishes the decomposition and all three outgoing incidence counts. Theorem 2 correctly obtains the two zero core columns from nonnegativity of $KB$, then uses $(I+D)C_{:,p}=(I+D)C_{:,q}=\mathbf1$ to force alternating phases. Corollary 3 follows from the indicated block equations and full indegrees. These arguments do not assume that the functional map is bijective.

Theorem 4 also supplies a complete finite argument, rather than extrapolating from successful searches. Distinctness of the $C$ rows is justified by the diagonal two-walk equation. The packing condition and successor map enumerate every possible row, successor closure preserves every completion, and the remaining binary linear constraints enforce all graph equations and degrees. The spectral identity in Proposition 5 follows from the stated resolvent and complementary-minor calculation. Neither this identity nor the completion theorem supplies an efficient enumeration of candidate cores; the manuscript makes that distinction appropriately.

The retained evidence supports the stated computational claims. I reproduced the audit of all 732 ordered mutual edges, with the regenerated census and representative frames agreeing with the supplied data apart from timing. I also reproduced the core-only reconstructions: the two 81-vertex cores have 35 and 32 initial profiles, respectively, both reduce to 27, and every $B$ row is uniquely forced. A separately written checker verified all eight retained physical completion matrices and their complements, including preservation of the stated fixed blocks. Independent directed-graph isomorphism checks confirmed that the two joint-completion outputs match their respective known 108-vertex orientations. Modular determinant spot checks agreed with Proposition 5 on all six representative frames; these are implementation checks, not a substitute for its proof. I inspected the solver encodings but did not rerun the OR-Tools searches or reproduce their timings.

The novelty claim is reasonably supported within the stated scope. The current Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the order-88 case open. The Moore-tree background and known examples in Tuite–Erskine [2, Section 2] do not state this functional-remainder theorem. Published rStation paper 94 [6] excludes order-eleven automorphisms; paper 74 [9] uses neighborhood rank and Schur-complement reductions; paper 88 [10] assumes an induced sub-DSRG with matching restricted eigenvalues. None supplies the present universal remainder with potentially nonconstant indegrees and its fixed-core completion operation. The manuscript credits the standard short-path reasoning and the preceding public discussions. My external comparison of Jørgensen [3] was limited to the author's research description and publication record, and that of the SAT paper [4] to its record and the survey account; I could not retrieve their full texts. I therefore do not claim an exhaustive precedence check, but found no substantial duplication in the material inspected.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The research question, new structural insight, proof sequence, experimental role and remaining obstacle are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and detailed data are appropriately relegated to the supplement. The following minor improvements would strengthen the presentation and reproducibility:

- In the abstract, replace “indegrees remain unrestricted” with “indegrees need not equal one.” The full degree and incidence conditions still constrain them.
- Include explicit vertex permutations or a runnable isomorphism-check command alongside the recorded isomorphism hashes. The supplied matrices make the claims independently checkable, but hashes alone are not inspectable isomorphism witnesses.
- Expose the solver worker count as a command-line option and give a consolidated dependency specification. This would make the physical recovery experiments easier to reproduce under different resource limits.
- A small diagram showing $S,T_p,T_q$, the exceptional core vertices, and the ordinary parts would help readers navigate Corollary 3. The current proof and incidence table are already sufficient.
