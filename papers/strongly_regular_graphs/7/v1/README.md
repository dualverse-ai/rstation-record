# Nonexistence of DSRGs (50,35,28,23,28), (72,21,7,4,7), (81,24,8,5,8), (81,63,56,47,56), (96,80,75,65,75), (100,35,14,9,14) and all positive multiples

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/7/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove nonexistence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(50m,35m,28m,23m,28m)$, $(72m,21m,7m,4m,7m)$, $(81m,24m,8m,5m,8m)$, $(81m,63m,56m,47m,56m)$, $(96m,80m,75m,65m,75m)$, and $(100m,35m,14m,9m,14m)$ for every positive integer $m$, together with their complements. These results settle twelve unresolved base entries and two doubled entries. A nonnegative representation of the all-ones vector extends to a binary column basis, permitting zero coefficients. Determinant bounds give complete finite lists of possible normals. We retain every actual spanning row support and all rational dual coefficients. The order-50 proof combines binary-indicator bounds with basis exchange: a necessary negative dual must produce another admissible nonnegative normal. This reduces two 32-pattern slices to eight cuts of at most 24 patterns; exact indicator and sign arguments exclude their 2,102,002 spanning support orbits without optimization. A positive-dependence argument extends the exchange reduction to normals with several zero coordinates after rebasing. Other rays use diagonal signs, elementary row-class counting, or integer separating matrices. The sparse order-81 proof covers 759 normals and 470,210,506 spanning supports, with 43,632 exact separators. Independent enumerators check all normal catalogues; distinct rational-arithmetic implementations check support calculations. Standalone proof programs verify complete coverage and exact inequalities without trusting numerical optimization. Realizable rank-eight and rank-nine graphs supply positive controls. All obstructions exclude whole parameter rays.
