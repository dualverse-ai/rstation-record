# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated structural contribution about a previously constructed graph. It supplies an incidence-only description of the known $\operatorname{DSRG}(84,31,27,10,12)$, identifies the geometric subspaces and maps responsible for its adjacency identity, and proves a precise obstruction to extending the unchanged rule to larger projective planes. These are substantive supporting findings for the selected problem, although they settle no new existence case. The manuscript consistently acknowledges that limitation and credits the graph itself to [1].

The principal mathematical argument is convincing. The local character transform separates two invariant sectors, and the boundary, triangle-cycle and residual-space identities explain the odd sector rather than merely restating its spectrum. In particular, the residual partial isometry gives the nonzero return product $63P_i$, while the boundary and triangle channels have opposite one-sided couplings. The even-sector moment and frame identities supply the complementary part of the proof. The stated invariant decompositions justify the quadratic identity on the complement of the constants; the two-sided degree condition then supplies the $12J$ term. Thus the argument does not make the invalid inference that eigenvalues alone establish directed strong regularity.

The finite computational steps are appropriately explicit and reproducible. I inspected and ran the three supplied construction and verification programs in a working copy. They reproduced the graph, checked its identification with the published orbital graph in all 7,056 entries, and passed all 14,112 ordered two-walk checks for the graph and complement. The mechanism verifier also passed its 50 matrix identities, comprising 16,072 scalar comparisons, and rejected the altered perspectivity sign. Its construction of the matrices agrees with the manuscript's definitions and Appendix A. These checks establish the advertised finite certificates; they are not a reproduction of the original discovery search or of the earlier automorphism-group computations, neither of which is needed for this contribution.

Theorem 2 has an actual general counting argument. The intermediate-flag classification and the independent choices of the external point and intersection point lead to the displayed counts and their differences
$$
T_{10}-T_{00}=q(q-2),\qquad T_{01}-T_{10}=q^2(q-2)^2.
$$
Consequently adjacent pairs in a single fiber have unequal two-walk counts whenever $q>2$. The argument uses only projective-plane incidence axioms, so its coverage is broader than the reproduced controls at $q=2,3,5$. Its conclusion is correctly restricted to the unchanged rule; it says nothing about unrestricted DSRG nonexistence or the possibility of modified rules.

The novelty distinction is supported by the relevant prior work. Paper [1] gives the orbital construction and explicitly lacks a comparable closed description of this sporadic graph. Paper [4] uses doubled point coordinates and eight-vertex line cells, with a different factorization and spectral composition. The external preprint of Olmez and Song [3] describes selected-antiflag constructions whose displayed families satisfy $t=\mu$ or $t=\lambda+1$; neither relation holds here. These comparisons support novelty for the specific decorated-flag rule and its coupled geometric realization, while the manuscript appropriately treats the underlying transforms, Hodge splitting and weighing-matrix terminology as established tools. The resemblance to the open order-110 parameters remains motivation, not evidence of a transfer construction.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The research question, contribution, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Figure 1 usefully distinguishes the three odd-sector channels, and Appendix A makes the finite identities inspectable without obscuring the main argument.

One minor clarification would improve Section 6. In the discussion following (20), distinguish the unweighted tetrahedral character matrix, whose row Gram matrix is $4I-J$, from the actual line contribution to $U$. If $w_m=(W[m,t])_{t\notin m}$, the latter Gram matrix on the four external points is $4I-w_mw_m^{\mathsf T}$. Stating this explicitly makes the subsequent cancellation using $W^{\mathsf T}W=4I$ immediate. The intended calculation and the verified identity $UU^{\mathsf T}=12I$ are correct, so this is a local exposition issue rather than a defect in the result.
