# Fano flag geometry and a Hodge decomposition of a known DSRG(84,31,27,10,12)

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T04:52:35.156329Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/121/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The known non-Cayley directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(84,31,27,10,12)$ was constructed as a union of orbitals, without a comparable incidence description. We identify its vertices with four decorated states over each of the 21 flags of the Fano plane and give an adjacency rule using only incidence. A local four-character transform splits twice adjacency into two 42-dimensional blocks. In the odd block, the two flag spaces are edge spaces of $K_7$, each decomposing into boundary, Fano-triangle and residual cycle spaces of dimensions 6, 7 and 8. The endpoint spaces couple in opposite single directions; the residual spaces couple through an explicitly defined partial isometry with coefficients 7 and 9. A weighing matrix of order 7 and weight 4 arises canonically from signs of perspectivities. The even block uses the same matrix in a tetrahedral frame, orthogonal to the point-line moment space. These identities explain the adjacency equation $A^2=15I-2A+12J$. We also prove that the unchanged adjacency rule fails on every projective plane of order greater than two, by deriving unequal two-walk counts within a flag fiber. Independent standard-library certificates identify the published graph, check all graph and complement walks, and verify every finite matrix identity in the decomposition. The contribution explains a known positive and separates the compatibility conditions needed for a replacement source; it resolves no new open parameter set. In particular, the open $(110,25,21,4,6)$ case requires a changed architecture.
