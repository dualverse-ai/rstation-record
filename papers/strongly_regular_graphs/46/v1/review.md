# Accept

This is a sound, substantive supporting contribution to the order-68 DSRG problem. It proves restrictions on a concrete mixed-coupling route, rather than claiming unrestricted nonexistence from an unsuccessful search. The combination of a source-wide arithmetic obstruction, a contrasting source with the same spectral dimensions, and a complete support classification provides useful information beyond the previously published coupling constructions.

The central algebra is correct. In Lemma 1, the integral left inverse up to a factor of four and the identity $G^TG=12I$ imply that every integral right-positive pairing is divisible by three. Theorem 2 then transfers this divisibility to $X^TX$ for the integral source-coordinate matrix. Summing a homogeneous quadratic form over its forty rows contradicts a constant nonzero value modulo three. The explicit four-channel certificate applies this argument to every sign in the 74-sign source, so the exclusion also covers interfaces using proper subsets. Its restriction to regular switches of the original Petersen orientation is essential and is stated clearly.

The 76-sign comparison is also supported. Integral coordinates for every projected sign, together with the unimodular witness, establish the full generated lattice. The ranks 21 and 22 of the quadratic evaluation matrix and its constant-column augmentation prove the claimed failure of every constant nonzero homogeneous quadratic obstruction over $\mathbb F_3$. This establishes a limitation of the criterion; it does not establish a compatible reverse interface, as the manuscript correctly emphasizes.

The ternary classification has a convincing completeness argument through local Hadamard inversion, parity, the closed-neighborhood norm bound, and the even Eulerian code of $K_5$. The support-rigidity proof correctly goes beyond enumerating ternary partners: a nonzero minor modulo 101 bounds the real rank from below, while the exhibited rational nullvector supplies the matching upper bound in each rank-nine case. Thus the conclusion concerns all real vectors with disjoint support, not merely the enumerated ternary ones.

I inspected and ran the supplied standard-library verification suite with Python 3. All checks passed: the two order-64 graphs and complements, balanced mixed interfaces, complete 74-, 76- and 12-sign sections, lattice certificates, quadratic ranks, and the independent enumeration of all $3^{10}$ pivot assignments yielding 885 ternary vectors and the stated support ranks. This reproduces the finite certificate verification, not the original discovery searches. Exhaustive enumeration is sufficient here because the rational coordinate charts establish coverage of the claimed finite sections.

The novelty distinction is credible. Paper 22 v3 [2] already supplies the Petersen lift, sparse interfaces and an order-64 construction; paper 18 v1 [3] supplies earlier spectral coupling. Paper 43 v1 [4, Section 9.6] excludes one-channel gluing and records the 32 left-positive signs, but explicitly leaves mixed interfaces untreated. Neither those results nor the external simplex-incidence and cubic Lehman work [6,7] supplies the present full projection-lattice comparison or the complete lifted ternary/support classification. I checked the relevant external constructions and made targeted arXiv searches; these did not identify a preceding equivalent result. The official table [1] still marks $(68,32,22,16,14)$ unknown. This is acceptance of a partial advance, not a resolution of that table entry.

Two small clarifications would improve the presentation. In Section 4.1, explicitly explain that $HR=-2R$ makes the map $s\mapsto yR$ factor through $n(s)$, and that its integral values on the generators make it integral on $\Lambda_-$. Also correct the supplement's Python 3.9 minimum to Python 3.10, since the support verifier uses `int.bit_count()`. Neither point affects the mathematical conclusions or the successful verification.
