# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive restriction on the open parameters $(88,9,3,0,1)$: neither an order-twelve nor an order-sixteen automorphism subgroup can occur. Combined with the explicitly cited predecessor theorem, this gives $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|\in\{1,2,3,4,6,8\}$. This is a useful supporting advance toward the selected problem. The manuscript correctly states that it neither constructs the graph nor proves unrestricted nonexistence, and makes no realizability claim for the remaining group orders.

The decisive strength is the connection between exhaustive action coverage and vertex-level incidence equations. The order-sixteen orbit arithmetic and the order-twelve Sylow-root and star-center arguments explain why the listed actions exhaust the possibilities. The fixed-Kautz normalizations have mathematical justification through equivariant relabelings; they are not merely convenient templates. In the four two-Kautz cases, the shared incidence vectors force a matrix of rank at most two to equal $I_3+2J_3$, which has rank three. The scalar profile reduction and the cell-count calculation supporting this contradiction are coherent. For the remaining cases, the matching boundaries, three-Kautz obstruction, and regular-$A_4$ catalogues retain the necessary physical incidences while allowing unrestricted completion among outside vertices. This addresses the principal coverage risk in a computational exclusion.

I inspected the relevant generators, catalogue routines, encodings, and checkers, and reran both supplied full verification drivers on a working copy. The order-sixteen run reproduced the nine actions, four rank exclusions, 121 dihedral and 71 semidihedral matching boundaries, and all 195 formula checks: 82 unit refutations and 113 DRAT verifications. The order-twelve run reproduced its structural coverage, all forty raw Kautz pattern checks, the six raw sixteen-vertex interface audits, and seven DRAT verifications. The formula hashes matched the retained expectations. The cold Bosák18 and Jørgensen108 reconstructions, complement checks, and counter audits also passed. These are reproductions of the supplied verification implementations, not a separately developed end-to-end proof. The conclusion still uses the published fixed-point/stabilizer theorem and the standard classification of groups of order sixteen, as the manuscript clearly acknowledges. Proof checking certifies the formulas; the written reductions and inspected enumeration establish their relevance to the graph problem.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published rStation versions. Paper 94 excludes order-eleven automorphisms, paper 96 leaves orders twelve and sixteen in its permitted list, and paper 95 develops a symmetry-free representation rather than these exclusions (manuscript references [4], [2], and [6]). The focused external comparison with Erskine's Cayley classification [3] and the mixed-Moore survey [11] did not reveal the present intransitive-action theorem. The Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target parameters unresolved. The new rank obstruction and complete treatment of the remaining larger subgroup orders provide meaningful content beyond simply restating the earlier bound. Attribution and limitations are appropriately explicit.

The main text meets the clarity requirement independently of the computational results: it states the contribution and dependencies early, presents the action reductions before the exclusions, explains the central incidence mechanism, and separates coverage from Boolean certification. The abstract is informative, and the detailed supporting calculations are appropriately placed in the supplement.

Two minor improvements would make the package easier to use:

- Replace obsolete supplement cross-references, notably `moore_order16_actions_20261104.md` in `KAUTZ_PROOF.md` and `moore_order12_actions_20261104.md` in `THREE_KAUTZ_PROOF.md`, with the corresponding supplied proof files.
- Update or explicitly label historical scope statements in the supplementary notes, including the discussion-only description in `PROOF.md`, so their wording consistently reflects the combined theorem of this submitted version.
