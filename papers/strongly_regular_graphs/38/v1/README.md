# Existence of DSRGs (N,(N-4)/2,(N+2)/4,(N-10)/4,(N-6)/4) at N=38,46,62,86,94 and (N,(N-6)/2,(N+14)/4,(N-14)/4,(N-10)/4) at N=62,66,74,82,86,90,94,98,106,110, and complements

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/38/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We resolve fifteen open complementary pairs of directed strongly regular graph parameters. Five lie on $(N,(N-4)/2,(N+2)/4,(N-10)/4,(N-6)/4)$ at $N=38,46,62,86,94$; ten lie on $(N,(N-6)/2,(N+14)/4,(N-14)/4,(N-10)/4)$ at $N=62,66,74,82,86,90,94,98,106,110$. More generally, the first line exists for every $N\ge26$ congruent to 2 modulo 4, and the second for every $N\ge82$ congruent to 2 modulo 4, together with the three smaller new orders 62, 66, and 74. Directed complements give fifteen further rows. The common construction writes $S=J-I-2A$ and preserves $S^2=q^2I$ with row and column sums $q$. An unbalanced left sign eigenvector cancels the row defect of an added irregular block. The extension preserves all available left interfaces and can therefore combine several block sizes. We give a twelve-point module for $q=3$ and modules of orders 28, 40, and 48 for $q=5$, built from explicit commuting symmetric circulants. Known seeds supply the starting graphs; their constructions and overlapping family members are credited. A further sign eigenvector changes the available imbalance at order 66. This produces seven consecutive admissible orders, after which the twenty-eight-point extension proves the second uniform theorem. The supplement supplies complete finite certificates, solver-free code, adjacency matrices, and scalar and bitset verification of 72 matrices. The irregular modules escape the parity obstruction for coupling two regular seeds. The square-twenty-five order 38 pair remains unresolved.
