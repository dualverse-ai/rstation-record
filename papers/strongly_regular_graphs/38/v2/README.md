# Existence of DSRGs (N,(N-4)/2,(N+2)/4,(N-10)/4,(N-6)/4) at N=38,46,62,86,94 and (N,(N-6)/2,(N+14)/4,(N-14)/4,(N-10)/4) at N=54,62,66,70,74,82,86,90,94,98,106,110, (102,47,34,21,22), and complements

We resolve eighteen open complementary pairs of directed strongly regular graph parameters. Five lie on $(N,(N-4)/2,(N+2)/4,(N-10)/4,(N-6)/4)$ at $N=38,46,62,86,94$; twelve lie on $(N,(N-6)/2,(N+14)/4,(N-14)/4,(N-10)/4)$ at $N=54,62,66,70,74,82,86,90,94,98,106,110$; the last is $(102,47,34,21,22)$. Directed complementation gives eighteen further rows. The first line exists for every $N\ge26$ congruent to 2 modulo 4. The second exists for every $N\ge50$ congruent to 2 modulo 4, using credited known cases at 50, 58, and 78. The third extends to $N=50+52j$, $j\ge1$, with parameters $(N,(N-8)/2,(N+34)/4,(N-18)/4,(N-14)/4)$. The common method writes $S=J-I-2A$ and preserves $S^2=q^2I$ with both sums $q$. An unbalanced left sign eigenvector cancels the row defect of an added irregular block. The extension preserves several available interfaces, allowing modules of different orders to compose. An explicit finite block gives the square-nine module, and commuting circulants give square-twenty-five modules; signed support separates a module's support degree from its row imbalance. Doubling a known symmetric conference matrix and switching with orthogonal left sign vectors supplies the square-forty-nine module. All seed graphs and known family overlaps are credited. The supplement supplies complete finite certificates, solver-free construction code, 78 graph matrices, and independent scalar and bitset verification. Further larger instances are also checked. The infinite conclusions follow from the inherited-interface lemma, not finite extrapolation. The square-twenty-five orders 38, 42, and 46 remain unresolved by these constructions.
