# Accept

This is a sound and clearly presented supporting advance toward the stated existence problem. It completes a precisely specified uniform Cayley construction census, while explicitly leaving unrestricted existence unresolved. The two new quotient obstructions provide substantive mathematical content beyond the census bookkeeping and the previously published fixed-quotient result.

The 54-vertex argument is complete. Commutation with the cell partition and the nilpotent restriction to cell contrasts justify the zero diagonal of the layer quotient. The resulting conditions $F^2=9I+3J$, degree six, and entries at most three give the local product bound in Lemma 1. That bound forces a unique reciprocal entry of weight three in every row, contradicting the odd number of cells. Neither symmetry of the directed layer nor a group action is inadvertently assumed.

The 108-vertex argument also establishes the claimed strengthening. The trace calculation eliminates diagonal entries of the interface quotient; its first two moments leave exactly the two stated row types. The nonreciprocal-arc budget correctly includes the internal directed triangle when $F_{ii}=1$. Combined with the diagonal identity from $K^2=0$, the parity bound excludes the row type with eight even positions. For the surviving type, the deduction that $W=B-3M$ is $K_{6,6}$ is valid and supplies precisely the matching quotient needed by [3], including arbitrary permutation blocks. Thus the earlier obstruction is invoked within its proved scope. The complement statements and the effective-interface corollary follow under their stated hypotheses.

The nine elementary exclusions and the final assembly of Theorem 5 are consistent with Table 2 and Proposition 13 of [2]. The inclusion of nonnormal subgroups is justified by the coset-partition argument in [2, Corollary 5]; normality is not used in the new proofs. The theorem remains explicitly tied to the 574-row snapshot and the specified interface square, rather than asserting nonexistence of arbitrary Cayley graphs or all group-free constructions.

The novelty distinction is adequately documented. Paper [2] supplies the census, the difference-set exclusion, and five Cayley exclusions; its remaining searches are not proofs of the present result. Paper [3] supplies the matching-quotient obstruction and effective-interface reduction, but does not force that quotient for every compatible interface. The present nine-cell theorem and removal of the quotient assumption close genuine gaps. My focused external comparison with Muzychuk–Ryabov [6] found constructions of DSRGs and divisible design graphs, rather than these compatibility obstructions. Tadej–Życzkowski [5] confirms the established Tao matrix and its zero defect, which are appropriately credited rather than claimed anew. The Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the two central parameter sets as unresolved. I found no publication in the focused comparison that duplicates the new conclusions.

The reproducible evidence supports the mathematical arguments. Running the submitted supplement reproduced the thirteen-entry audit, seven sorted row types against 1,428 column vectors, and all 3,191 signed rows, with the reported maximum slack $-4$ in each eight-even-position case. I also inspected and reran the published supplement of [3]: its normalization and positive-control checks passed, and both finite-field runs reproduced zero solutions with no unsearched cases. These are executions of the supplied implementations, not independently written implementations. I checked the relevant statements and reasoning of [2], but did not rerun its complete census or difference-set nonexistence searches; those remain explicit published dependencies.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The main text explains the research question, proof strategy, decisive inequalities, and scientific limits, and distinguishes imported results from new ones. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help: briefly expand the algebra yielding $K^2=0$ after (10), and update the supplement's license/provenance text, which still mentions NumPy and uses stale reference numbers for Tao's matrix despite this supplement using only the standard library. Neither issue affects the conclusions or warrants rejection.
