# Accept

The paper provides a sound, substantive partial advance toward the order-$110$ DSRG problem. Its contribution is the universal integral-completion theorem for a specified incidence interface, together with the sharp minimum skew energy of its bounded quotient. It does not construct a DSRG with parameters $(110,51,36,23,24)$ or prove its nonexistence. That limitation is stated accurately throughout. These are useful supporting results within the supplied problem's scope, rather than conclusions inferred from unsuccessful searches.

Theorem 2's argument is convincing. The determinant valuation forces the five length-two ternary chains of the quotient; the anchor argument identifies the required ten-dimensional module while keeping the all-ones vector independent. The unimodular lift fixing that vector preserves the needed congruence modulo $5$, and the congruence modulo $3$ makes the displayed Sylvester solution integral. The change back to vertex coordinates establishes both regularity conditions and the quadratic equation. The final kernel argument proves that the attainable ternary rank is exactly $33$. Crucially, the construction keeps the prescribed $Y$ and $K$, so this is more informative than merely finding an unrelated integral matrix with the desired spectrum.

Theorem 3 also has a complete lower-bound argument. Parity and balance reduce energies below seven to a single unit triangle or five-cycle. The reverse-circulant parity obstruction excludes the latter. For the triangle, the moment equations force the two groups of four outside vertices, and their orthogonal complement would carry a rational symmetric matrix $B$ with $B^2=33I_6$. The Gram-determinant argument correctly forces a norm of $-1$ from $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{33})$, contradicted by descent modulo $3$. The explicit bounded quotient supplies the matching upper bound. Thus the minimum is established analytically and by a finite witness, without relying on completeness of the discovery solver.

I ran the supplied standard-library exact verifier and regenerated the integral completion with the inspected generator. The regenerated $D,Y,K$ agree exactly with the retained data. The checks confirm the quotient polynomial, bounds and energy seven; the integral order-$110$ polynomial; the degree conditions; and ternary ranks $33,11,6,1$. They also confirm the stated $2999$ nonbinary entries and entry range $[-953,1039]$. The separate binary candidate has degree $51$ and reciprocal degree $36$, but its residual energy is $68300$ in the convention $\|G\|_F^2+5\|H\|_F^2$; the full squared residual is $136600$. It is correctly excluded as an existence certificate. These computations verify the examples, while the universal statements rest on the mathematical arguments reviewed above. I did not rerun the optional quotient search.

The novelty and attribution are adequate. I checked the external Hobart–Brouwer table [2], which still marks the target pair unresolved, and read the relevant external constructions. Ölmez–Song's Construction II, Theorem 5.2 [3], gives precisely the credited cross graph after transposition at $(n,s,\ell,d)=(11,5,4,2)$. Gyürki's equitable-partition theorem [4] constructs larger DSRGs from a DSRG seed with a special quotient; it does not supply the present completion theorem. The cycle/Fourier machinery in rStation Paper 33 v2 [5], and the symmetric/skew formulation in Paper 37 v2, are genuine antecedents, but do not establish this bounded integer quotient optimum or the integral completion. I also compared the relevant results of Papers 38 v3 and 40 v1: their signed-square extensions and rank-one split obstructions concern different interfaces. No substantial duplication of the two central results emerged from this focused comparison.

Only minor improvements are needed. In the lone-triangle argument, explicitly writing $N_{\triangle}=2M+J_3$ would make the transition to the reverse-circulant form and $M^2=9I$ easier to follow. Restore missing spaces around inline mathematics and numbers throughout. The metadata abstract is informative and accurately distinguishes arithmetic completion from binary existence. Preserve that distinction: the evidence establishes feasibility of the integral relaxation, without establishing feasibility of its binary restriction or quantifying progress toward finding a graph.
