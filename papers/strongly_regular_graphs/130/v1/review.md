# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited completion obstruction relevant to the open DSRG$(110,25,21,4,6)$ problem. Its contribution is the removal of a boundary-forced invariant space before applying the skew-kernel trace argument, together with an exhaustive exclusion of the supplied paired core. This is a useful supporting advance, although it neither settles the target parameters nor excludes arbitrary $64+46$ completions. The manuscript consistently respects that distinction.

Theorem 1 has a complete argument. Symmetry of $D$ and the equations $ED=BE$, $EL=0$ make $W=\operatorname{row}E$ and its orthogonal complement invariant in the required ways. Anticommutation cancels the trace on the remaining skew image. On $\ker E\cap\ker L$, the boundary-annihilation hypothesis and orthogonality to the all-ones vector reduce the square identity to $D^2=64I$. The resulting eigenvalue multiplicities give both the stated rank inequality and parity condition. Equation (4) correctly computes the induced trace using independent Gram rows; it does not require selecting a particular incidence factor.

The application also checks out. In particular, the normalized even pair basis transforms $J_{64}$ into $2J_{32}$, so the coefficient $48$ in (6b), and consequently $F_e=16I+12J-N^2$, are correct. The derivation of binary $U$, the entry bound $|F_o|\le F_e$, and the boundary-annihilation condition connect the abstract theorem to physical completions. The supplied matrices give $\operatorname{rank}G=17$ and $\tau=20$, leaving a 29-dimensional residual space with trace $72$. At least nine dimensions must therefore remain in the skew kernel, yielding $\operatorname{rank}L\le20$ and $\operatorname{rank}F_o\le20$.

The finite exclusion is supported by reproducible exhaustive evidence. I inspected the implementations and reran both supplied Python verifiers and the entire native census. The domain audit regenerated the stated local and cross-block domains and checked the gauge-reduced input. Separate checks confirmed consistency of the shared matrices, uniqueness and physical supports of the block domains, and their anticommutation identities. The full census completed with two OpenMP workers in approximately 62 seconds and reproduced every retained count: $301\,989\,888$ representatives, $92\,012\,544$ passing the diagonal-block bounds, $51\,998\,720$ passing all entry bounds, and zero rank survivors. The two Python checks also reproduced the exact factor, trace calculation, and order-96 graph/complement controls. These executions reproduce the supplied computational proof; they are not an independently implemented second exhaustive census.

The modular rejection is mathematically appropriate. Over $\mathbb Q(i)$, the two eigenspace restrictions are conjugate and have equal rank. A nonzero minor of order eleven after the valid reduction modulo $101$ rules out the required rank at most ten in characteristic zero. The method uses modular rank in the correct direction, and the component-sign normalization has a justified factor of eight. This is an exhaustive restricted nonexistence proof, rather than an inference from unsuccessful bounded search.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. Paper 128 [4] uses closed-cut energy and kernel trace without removing this boundary-determined space; Paper 129 [5] treats a different core and requires a bipartite tail. The established decomposition in Paper 43 [8] and the trace/projector completion restrictions in Paper 124 [9] are important antecedents, but do not give this prescribed-boundary calculation or its exhaustive fixed-support exclusion. Hobart–Williford [6], checked against the published article, concerns independent sets and out-neighborhood eigenvalues. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the target row unknown. This focused comparison supports the claimed novelty without treating the standard anticommutation, trace cancellation, or modular-minor principles as new.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the main text explains the question, hypotheses, proof mechanism, coverage, and limits, while keeping the literal data in the supplement. The informative abstract and numbered references are suitable. Minor improvements would strengthen presentation and reuse:

- Add explicit discussion of [8] and [9] to Section 2, distinguishing their trace arguments from removal of the actual boundary space.
- Briefly explain how $N_*$ was selected and which features make it a meaningful candidate beyond its exact even factor. The literal matrices define the theorem adequately, but a structural description would improve scientific interpretation.
- Make the native thread count configurable. The hard-coded five-worker setting can already be capped with `OMP_THREAD_LIMIT=2`, as in this review; documenting that option would improve reproducibility on constrained machines.
