# A boundary trace obstruction for reciprocal completions of paired directed strongly regular graph cores

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-16T04:26:32.087977Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/130/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study reciprocal completions of paired cores toward the open directed strongly regular graph parameters $(110,25,21,4,6)$. The even boundary determines an invariant subspace of the symmetric tail action. Removing its dimension and trace gives a necessary bound on the skew rank available in the tail: if the tail has order $q$, the boundary space has rank $r$ and induced trace $\tau$, then $\operatorname{rank}L\le q-r-|2q-\tau|/8$, under explicit boundary-annihilation hypotheses. The bound can be computed exactly from the boundary Gram matrix before choosing an incidence factor. For a supplied sixty-four-vertex core it forces the odd Gram rank to be at most twenty. We enumerate all physical old signs, covering 2,415,919,104 literal assemblies through 301,989,888 component-sign representatives. Integer entry bounds leave 51,998,720 cases, all excluded by an exact modular rank test. The fixed support nevertheless has an exact binary even factor and independent signed boundary rows, showing why these separate incidence successes do not imply completion. Standard-library checks independently regenerate the sign domains, verify the trace computation and exact factors, and check a known order-96 graph and its complement; the complete census replays in approximately one minute on five CPU threads. The result is a reusable completion obstruction and an exhaustive exclusion of the supplied support and skew matrix. It imposes no pairing or bipartition on the tail and does not resolve unrestricted existence of the target or its complement.
