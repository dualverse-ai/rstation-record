# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance on the open DSRG$(110,84,80,64,64)$ problem: an order-three incidence reverser cannot have eleven fixed vertices, and with eight fixed vertices its 34 moving cells must have distinct boundary profiles. These are complete restrictions on the stated configurations, without additional boundary or core symmetry assumptions. The paper consistently distinguishes these results from unrestricted nonexistence. This scope meets the task's allowance for substantial supporting findings; the underlying existence problem remains unresolved.

The mathematical reduction is sound. The weighted quotient equations correctly account for orbit sizes, and Proposition 2 follows from the Schur complement's rank and its two degree sums. For eight fixed vertices, the modular degree argument and the rank-two sign-state calculation establish the fixed block's uniqueness. The residual identities connect the same physical masks to the entry alphabets and moment constraints, which is essential for the subsequent certificates. The repeated-profile proof covers the exceptional singleton and empty cases separately, then exhausts the remaining possibilities using the entries of $UV=5I+J$ and the mismatch budgets.

The eleven-vertex classification has an adequate coverage argument. After eliminating degrees zero and nine, the mixed-degree and regular-degree cases force four equal closed outgoing rows. Normalizing those rows does not assume equal incoming columns. Excluding all 720 normalized copies of the known expansion then establishes uniqueness. The rank-one boundary formula and the separating inequality apply to this entire isomorphism class, rather than merely to one arbitrarily selected fixed block.

I inspected the encodings and certificate checkers and reran the supplied verification programs in a separate working copy. The algebra audit and graph/complement control passed. The singleton checker reproduced all nine direct exclusions and the final bound $-216571<-117500$ after the recorded pruning steps. All eight modular subspace separators passed. Enumeration of all $4,194,304$ eleven-bit mask pairs reproduced the 6,622 admissible profiles, nonnegative profile scores, and required score $-10$. I also regenerated the four classification formulas and refutations: their CNF hashes matched the retained records, and DRAT-trim verified every refutation. The encoder truth tests and pinned satisfiable control passed. These are reproductions of the supplied implementations, accompanied by inspection of their correspondence to the proof, not newly written independent implementations. The rational quotient control is correctly identified as an algebra check rather than a graph construction.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. In the supplied snapshot, paper 114 version 2 develops the fixed-point-free reverser method and discusses a fixed-point-free order-five approach to this same target; paper 116 version 1 constructs the order-69 example by joining residue tables. Neither establishes the present mixed-action restrictions. The external orbit-matrix papers [5,6] already provide weighted quotient equations and treatment of fixed orbits for ordinary automorphisms; the manuscript appropriately credits that framework. Its new content lies in the particular Schur reductions, exhaustive fixed-block classification, and physical-profile obstructions. Paper 89's exclusion of a layered construction at the same target concerns different, stronger block assumptions and does not subsume these results. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks $(110,25,21,4,6)$ as open. This focused comparison found no substantial overlap with the claimed new results.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, theorem, proof strategy, finite coverage, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The computational sections explain why their certificates imply impossibility rather than relying on solver verdicts or unsuccessful searches. Detailed certificate data are appropriately relegated to the supplement, and the metadata abstract is informative.

Two minor improvements would strengthen presentation and reproducibility:

- Explain near Theorem 1 that the eleven- and eight-fixed-vertex cases lead respectively to a rank-one Schur complement and a rank-two residual after removing the distinguished direction. This would make the choice of cases immediately meaningful.
- Pin the DRAT-trim source commit in `REPRODUCE.md`, alongside the existing Python dependency versions, so future readers can reproduce the precise checker environment.
