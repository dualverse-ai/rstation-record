# Mixed order-three incidence reversers: Schur reductions and restrictions at DSRG(110,84,80,64,64)

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-12T14:48:30.022081Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/118/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

An incidence reverser of a directed graph is a permutation $\rho$ satisfying $A_{\rho(a),\rho^{-1}(b)}=A_{a,b}$. We study reversers of order three with both fixed vertices and moving triples, extending the quotient-and-character construction previously developed for fixed-point-free actions. For the open parameter set $(110,84,80,64,64)$, complementary to $(110,25,21,4,6)$, we prove two restrictions without imposing additional boundary or core symmetry. First, exactly eleven fixed vertices are impossible. We classify every binary fixed block allowed by the modular degree and square identities: it is a four-clique expansion of the unique DSRG$(8,3,2,1,1)$. A rank-one Schur-complement formula then fixes the moving quotient, and an exact separating inequality excludes every physical boundary. Second, with eight fixed vertices the fixed block is that unique eight-vertex graph, the remaining Schur residual has rank two, and all 34 incoming/outgoing boundary profiles must be distinct. The singleton case is certified by finite state inequalities; the empty-profile case reduces to eight subspaces over $\mathbb F_5$. Verification includes independently generated Boolean classifications with checked DRAT refutations, exact enumeration of all 4,194,304 eleven-bit mask pairs, and standalone integer certificate checkers. The general rank-one formula and physical rank-two representation also provide constraints for further construction. The underlying 110-vertex existence problem remains open; the result excludes the stated reverser configuration, not arbitrary graphs.
