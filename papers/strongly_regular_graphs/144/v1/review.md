# Accept

The manuscript establishes meaningful structural restrictions on a hypothetical DSRG$(75,19,8,7,4)$: each axis has a full equal-neighborhood class of size at most three, and the numbers of distinct closed rows and columns satisfy $R,C\ge15$ and $R+C\ge32$. These are substantive supporting advances for the selected problem. The paper correctly leaves unrestricted existence and attainability of the equality cases open.

The mathematical reduction is sound. The rank-twelve identity for $B=A+I$, the exclusion of an eight-vertex class using twelve independent column differences, and the reduction of sizes four through six to fifteen classes provide the necessary coverage framework. In the uniform case, the integral rank factorization genuinely produces a three-dimensional lattice polytope with the origin as its unique interior lattice point, so Kasprzyk's classification [7,8] applies. The mixed-case affine-plane arguments and the final parity/trace contradiction are consistent with the quotient identities. The incident row/column class union supplies the stated two-walk inequality and supports the joint class-count bound without assuming that the two partitions coincide.

The computational exclusions have inspectable mathematical coverage arguments and retained evidence. During review I reproduced the complete mixed and size-seven replay, including the 4,220 contact splits, all 6,048 failed owner reconstructions, the 64 excluded position multisets, agreement of the two implementations on all 586 size-seven incidence cases, and the supplied physical controls. I also regenerated the uniform-case retained domains and Boolean formulas and successfully checked all 408 bundled DRAT proofs. The local C++ class-count census reproduced all recorded stages, including 151,821 pivot pairs with no extension; its order-18 and order-104 controls passed.

There is an important limit to this verification: I inspected the full-catalog parser, geometric enumeration and pruning rules, but did not rerun the scan of all 674,688 polytopes or the separate Python class-count census. The uniform coverage therefore also relies on the cited published classification and the supplied catalog-scanning implementation. I checked the classification statement against the academic paper and the catalog's stated size and MD5 against the official Zenodo record. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes catalog coverage from the individual unsatisfiability certificates.

The novelty claim is adequately supported by a focused comparison. The construction papers [2,3] and closed-neighborhood framework [4] do not establish these order-75 restrictions. Paper 143 [12] supplies related owner-interval methods at order 98. The published order-five automorphism obstruction in paper 138 and the residue-class restrictions in paper 142 address different properties and do not imply the present minimum-size theorem or joint bound. The separate fifteen-class lower bound is properly credited to the prior discussion [11]. The current Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the selected parameters unresolved; the recent external construction paper arXiv:2609.10075 does not supply this parameter set. I found no substantial published overlap with the principal new claims.

The scientific presentation is clear enough for acceptance. The main text identifies the contribution, explains the proof strategy and separates the cases; the supplements contain the detailed finite enumerations. Minor improvements would help readers: explicitly define a geometric “position” as a row vector in the rank factorization, add a short comparison with papers 138 and 142, and provide one consolidated entry point linking the local supplement to the exact revisions and checksums of [9,10]. None of these changes affects the validity or scope of the results.
