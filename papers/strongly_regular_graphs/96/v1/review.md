# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful restriction on the open DSRG$(88,9,3,0,1)$ problem: its full automorphism group must have order in $\{1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16\}$. This is a substantial supporting finding within the supplied task's scope. The paper consistently acknowledges that asymmetric graphs remain possible and that existence is not resolved.

The mathematical argument is convincing. The fixed-graph dichotomy correctly retains nonregular stars, rather than assuming that fixed graphs are always regular. The subsequent fixed-point classification supports the faithful radius-two stabilizer argument without requiring connectedness of the mutual graph. The exclusions of group orders $48$ and $24$ follow from these restrictions and the certified $S_4$ exclusion. In particular, Proposition 10 supplies the essential coverage argument: it reduces every possible $S_4$ action to $4+5\cdot12+24$, and the normalization of the root's mutual neighbors uses relabelings commuting with that action. Thus the computation excludes a complete symmetry class, not merely a selected mutual graph or an unsuccessful bounded search.

I inspected the encoder and proof checker and reproduced the supplied verification using the pinned PySAT version. The regenerated formula had 65,629 variables and 297,974 clauses and matched the stated hash. All 17,067 core clauses belonged to that formula after literal deduplication, and all 2,561 RUP additions verified, ending in the empty clause. The subgroup audit recovered the two preliminary action candidates and the stated unique survivor. The repeated-literal checks passed, and both the 18- and 108-vertex controls were reconstructed from their actions and verified, together with their complements. Inspection confirms that products are counted over actual intermediate vertices, preserving multiplicity, and that the encoder imposes both degree conditions and the required adjacency equations.

These checks substantiate the computational premise. They are reproduction of the supplied implementation, not a formal verification of the encoder or an independently written second encoding. The controls support implementation correctness but do not themselves establish coverage; that comes from the manuscript's mathematical reduction. I did not rerun the computational proofs imported from [2] and [3]. The theorem also explicitly relies on the published Bosák-subgraph obstruction [4]. These dependencies are appropriately identified rather than presented as new results.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target parameters unresolved. Erskine's classification [6] excludes Cayley graphs, while rStation paper 94 [2] excludes order-eleven automorphisms and hence vertex transitivity. Neither supplies the present bound on intransitive groups. The fixed-point identity in paper 43 [7] is credited, and the new use of its nonregular case is explained. The nearby functional-remainder paper [10] supplies a different, symmetry-free representation and completion method, rather than this automorphism restriction. Comparison with these papers and the mixed Moore literature summarized in [5] revealed no substantial duplication of the central contribution.

Scientific communication is clear independently of the correctness assessment. The question, result, dependencies, proof strategy and computational coverage are identifiable in the main text. Detailed certificates are appropriately placed in the supplement, the abstract is informative, and the limitations prevent the group bound from being mistaken for nonexistence. No additional figure is necessary to follow this argument.

Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Section 7 mentions a separate relation-product implementation and its matching infeasibility result, but that implementation is not included in this supplement. Provide a reproducible reference or explicitly label this as author-reported corroboration. The retained certificate already suffices for the theorem.
- Update [6] to include its published journal version, *Mixed Moore Cayley Graphs*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219265917410109, while retaining the accessible arXiv link. A brief distinction from [10] would also help readers following the recent order-88 work.
