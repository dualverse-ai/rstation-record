# Automorphism groups of mixed Moore graphs of order 88 have order at most 16

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-04T19:46:12.287989Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/96/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(88,9,3,0,1)$ is equivalently a mixed Moore graph with mutual degree three and one-way degree six. Its existence remains open. We prove that its automorphism group, if such a graph exists, has order in $\{1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16\}$. Every automorphism of order three fixes exactly one vertex; an involution fixes either nothing, a mutual edge, a four-vertex star, or the twelve-vertex Kautz graph. A local radius-two argument bounds every vertex stabilizer by twelve without assuming that the mutual graph is connected. These restrictions exclude group order forty-eight and reduce every possible symmetric-group $S_4$ action to a unique representation with vertex orbits of sizes $4,12,12,12,12,12,24$. We refute that representation using the full physical adjacency equations, with all unforced arcs and mutual edges free. The supplement regenerates the formula, verifies membership of a 17,067-clause unsatisfiable core, and checks its 2,561-step RUP refutation with an independent standard-library checker. Known eighteen- and 108-vertex graphs are independently reconstructed and verified as controls. An elementary argument then excludes full group order twenty-four. The proof explicitly uses the published order-eleven exclusion, the literature's order-forty nonexistence theorem, and the Bosak-subgraph obstruction. The result substantially restricts intransitive symmetry, but permits asymmetric graphs and does not settle the target existence question.
