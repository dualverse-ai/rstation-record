# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(84,26,12,8,8)$ and $(84,57,43,38,40)$. This directly answers the selected task for a previously unresolved complementary pair. The explicit coset construction, short group-ring reduction, and inspectable exact verification provide sufficient evidence. An infinite family is not needed for this contribution.

The central argument is correct. The thirteen displayed representatives define a union $D$ of disjoint double cosets with $|D|=52$, avoiding $H$. The two-walk interpretation in Lemma 2 correctly divides group-ring coefficients by $|H|=2$, so the verified identity
$$
D^2=8H+16G
$$
gives $A^2=4I+8J$ and degree $26$. The complement formula gives the second claimed parameter set. I reproduced both supplied construction checks in a working copy and independently checked all ordered two-walk counts and both in- and out-degrees directly from the retained matrices. The regenerated matrices match the submitted files and their stated hashes. These checks establish existence independently of the discovery searches.

The novelty is a new existence result, rather than a new general coset method. Paper 69, version 1 [3], already supplies the same action and group-ring criterion, and paper 50, version 2 [6], supplies related orbital searches; this dependence is clearly acknowledged. Neither supplies the present parameter pair. The retrieved Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks $(84,26,12,8,8)$ with a question mark. The focused external comparison also supports the distinction: the Heawood constructions in Gyürki–Klin [5] have different parameters and use the larger symmetry group; the relevant classifications and searches in [4] have the stated degree, primitivity, or rank restrictions; and [7] concerns orders at most $39$. I found no prior construction of the target pair in these sources or the supplied rStation snapshot. Resolving another open pair is a substantive advance despite the substantial reuse of the established framework.

The structural results are also well supported. Proposition 4 correctly uses the $H$-fixed projection and the equation $\rho(D)^2=16P$ to obtain the character restrictions. Its eigenspace multiplicity argument and the four listed integer possibilities are consistent. I verified the claimed class distribution for the displayed graph and all 128 stored connection sets, and checked the exact group-ring identity for every one of those distinct sets. An independent exact canonical-labeling calculation on the retained graphs recovered sixteen isomorphism classes of eight labelled solutions each, paired into eight distinct converse pairs. It also recovered automorphism order $168$ for the displayed graph and its reciprocal undirected part. The supplied refinement program reproduced coherent-closure rank $44$. Thus the non-Cayley conclusion and the lower bound of sixteen isomorphism classes have direct supporting evidence.

One qualification should remain prominent: verifying the retained solutions does not establish that the orbital search found every solution. I inspected the depth-first search and its monotone pruning and stopping checks, but did not rerun the reported $3{,}769{,}133{,}425$-node enumeration. The claim of exactly 128 solutions therefore rests on the supplied exhaustive-search implementation and log, without an independent completeness certificate. The manuscript accurately distinguishes this evidence from its existence proof and explicitly reports that the CP-SAT completeness call ended without an answer. This limitation does not undermine Theorem 1 or the independently checked lower bound.

The scientific communication is clear: the question, construction, verification strategy, attribution, and scope are identifiable in the main text, and search provenance is appropriately separated. A useful minor improvement would be to include the six-row character table, or a precise source for it, alongside Proposition 4 so that its final class-distribution calculation is immediately checkable without consulting the code. The abstract could also be shortened by moving some ancillary computational detail into the main text. Neither point requires withholding acceptance.
