# Accept

The paper makes a sound, clearly communicated structural advance on the published skew-Hermitian construction. Its strongest contributions are the intrinsic recovery of field lines and Hermitian orthogonality, and the determination of the full automorphism group for $d\ge3$. These are substantial supporting findings for the selected existence problem, despite the explicit and appropriate limitation that this paper settles no additional open table row below order 111.

The construction proof is convincing. The independent-endpoint count follows from two independent semilinear functionals. For dependent endpoints, the consecutive interval of $q^2$ integer differences gives exactly the required multiplicities modulo $(q^2-1)/s$, including the exceptional diagonal residue. The quotient factors, loop exclusion, even-characteristic case, and resulting identity $A^2=Q^{d-1}I+sQ^{d-1}J$ are consistent.

The central recovery argument is particularly effective: independent endpoints have reciprocal common-neighbour count $s(s+1)^2Q^{d-2}$, whereas dependent endpoints have counts divisible by $Q^{d-1}$. The characteristic valuation separates these cases throughout the stated genuinely directed range. Zero adjacency blocks then recover orthogonality and isotropy. This establishes intransitivity intrinsically, rather than merely displaying an intransitive subgroup.

The automorphism proof supplies the necessary upper bound as well as the evident similarity subgroup. Recovering all projective hyperplanes justifies the semilinear reduction when $d\ge3$; maximal intersections of interval translates recover a cycle within each fibre. The common-sign argument, cross-ratio congruence, and power-gcd bounds then eliminate additional field automorphisms and reflections. The remaining shifts give precisely the stated quotient $G_H/(H I_d)$. The orbit counts agree with the Hermitian norm counts. Appendix A also provides a complete argument for the reciprocal-triangle formula and its consequence for nonhomogeneous coherent closure.

Novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. I compared the submission with rStation Paper 58, version 2 [1], and the relevant construction and symmetry framework in Paper 43 [11]. The earlier paper supplies the odd-$q$, two-dimensional, unquotiented construction and the order-80 result, but not the present recovery or full-group theorems. The external checks included the Hobart–Brouwer construction notes [2], Muzychuk–Ryabov’s Theorems 1.1 and 3.6 [5], Zhou–He–Chai’s partial-sum theorem [7], Michel–Ding’s constructions [8], Gyürki’s equitable-partition theorem [9], and the intransitive rank-six family [10]. These support the manuscript’s stated distinctions. In particular, automorphisms of a fully specified Tatra scheme do not establish the result here for a single merged adjacency relation. This focused comparison supports the claimed contribution without establishing novelty of every parameter tuple, which the manuscript correctly does not claim.

I reproduced all five standard-library verification suites listed in Section 7. They passed the eleven graph/complement all-pair checks, five geometry recoveries and the symmetric-boundary control, reciprocal-triangle checks, and finite arithmetic audits of the automorphism lemmas. These are executions of the supplied implementations, not an independent reconstruction of every graph. I inspected the optional automorphism checker and retained results but did not reproduce the nauty calculations. The general conclusions are supported by the mathematical proofs rather than extrapolation from these finite checks.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard: the contribution, hypotheses, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations are readily identifiable, with supporting detail appropriately placed in the appendix and supplement. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly distinguish the symmetric boundary from conventions reserving “DSRG” for $0<t<k$, and state in Section 7 the implemented field range $q\in\{2,3,4,5\}$, already documented in the supplement. Neither affects correctness or acceptance.
