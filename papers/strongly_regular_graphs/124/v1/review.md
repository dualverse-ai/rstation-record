# Accept

This is a sound and substantive conditional advance toward DSRG$(110,25,21,4,6)$. It does not settle existence, but the task explicitly welcomes substantial supporting findings. The contribution combines a complete classification under the stated saturated skew-spectrum hypothesis, additional restrictions from integral entries, and a useful tail-completion criterion. The manuscript consistently distinguishes these conclusions from unrestricted nonexistence and from an actual graph construction.

The central argument is convincing. Equality in the spectral-radius and absolute-row bounds reduces each nontrivial skew component to a balanced signed complete bipartite component of side size four or eight. The integral action classification, doubled-entry budgets, and Section 3.3 inequality then leave precisely the two displayed actions on unit-weight $K_{8,8}$ components. These are restrictions on actual matrix entries, rather than merely permissible abstract spectra. The four-vertex budget supplies a further consequential restriction: the negative sign halves for $D_{\mathrm A}$ must be identity blocks. The regular-tail argument subsequently excludes $D_{\mathrm B}$ and confines the remaining case to degree ten.

The trace/projector argument is particularly useful. The multiplicity identity $m_-=(187-q)/8$ and the allocation of $r$ negative eigenvalues to the skew image justify the general bound $\operatorname{rank}(20I+2J-5Z)\le23-r$. In the saturated case, tail rank 21 exhausts the negative projector's rank and therefore determines the active block by the stated Schur-complement formula. The manuscript correctly retains the range condition, entry constraints, margins, and full square equation as separate requirements on a proposed completion. The contrasting explicit tails show that cross-Gram positivity alone does not enforce the new rank restriction; the feasible rank-21 tail establishes attainability of this necessary condition, without implying extendibility.

I reproduced the structure verifier's 36 equal-size actions, six orbits, 36 mixed-size actions, and 2,321 scalar-budget cases. The completion verifier passed the graph/complement walk checks and the exact positivity/rank checks for both tails. Its rational-control stage did not run unmodified with the installed SymPy 1.11.1 because that version does not recognize `inv(method='DM')`. An independent implementation using Python rational arithmetic verified the same projector construction, all 3,025 square-identity entries, and all 1,024 reconstructed active-block entries. These checks support the finite calculations and coefficient conventions; the component classification and general rank theorem additionally depend on the manuscript's mathematical proofs, which I reviewed. No physical order-55 completion or odd signing has been verified or claimed.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. Papers [3], [5], and [6] supply the involution decomposition and earlier component/lattice techniques, but do not contain this weighted saturated classification or its tail restrictions. The order-96 construction [4] is appropriately credited as a control. Li–Lian [7, Section 5] already covers the relevant switching interpretation of bipartite skew spectra, and the manuscript explicitly avoids claiming that principle as new. The published rStation papers on mixed order-three reversers (paper 118) and Frobenius bi-Cayley actions (paper 120) concern different symmetry hypotheses and do not subsume these results. The current Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target and its complement unresolved. The focused literature comparison revealed no substantial duplication of the claimed contribution.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The question, additional hypotheses, main theorems, proof strategy, and remaining construction obligations are identifiable in the main text. The paper is organized around the argument, and its informative abstract accurately describes the limited scope. The following minor improvements would help:

- In Section 3.2, replace “reducing modulo four twice” with the correct parity justification using modulo eight at both stages. Modulo four alone does not exclude four odd summands. The representation claim itself is correct and independently checked by the supplied enumeration.
- State tested Python, NumPy, and SymPy versions, particularly the SymPy requirement for the `DM` inversion method, or provide a compatible exact fallback.
- Briefly mention papers 118 and 120 in the related-work discussion to place this restriction alongside the other recent approaches to the same parameter set.

Acceptance applies to the conditional structural results and exact completion criterion. It does not resolve the open existence case.
