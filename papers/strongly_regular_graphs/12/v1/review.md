# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of DSRGs with parameters $(100,60,39,34,39)$ and $(100,39,18,17,14)$ and supports the stated two-parameter family. This directly addresses the selected problem. The compatible partitions and their uniform realization constitute a substantive extension of the attributed seed construction and lifting theorem, rather than merely another verification of previously published graphs.

The mathematical argument is sound. The outer-product identity gives the required seed equation, regularity and vertex count. The coordinate grouping supplies the left partition, and the explicit dual-multiset decomposition supplies the right partition with the correct cell sizes and multiplicities. Their intersection equation $V^TU=2J$ then yields the cross-block identities and the claimed layer construction. The complement parameters also agree with the standard formula.

The crucial passage from finite certificates to all odd $u$ is justified. Lemma 3 correctly realizes the prescribed integer marginals by binary rows of constant weight; the forced-coordinate conditions preserve compatibility and eliminate loops. For each affine certificate, the moment entries and row-weight equations have degree at most two in $r$. The class and coordinate-equality cases are all represented at $r=1,2,3$, so these checks establish polynomial identities, not an extrapolation from finitely many constructed graphs. Coefficientwise bounds guarantee admissibility for every $r\ge0$. The two residue classes, together with the supplied finite seeds, cover every odd $u\ge3$.

I reproduced the standalone verification of all fourteen examples and their complements, including the partition equations and every ordered two-step count. I also reran the separate symbolic auditor: all 444 and 536 polynomial identities passed, and the unpacked certificates agree with those embedded in the standalone program. In an independently written check, I reconstructed the order-100 graph directly from the literal order-50 seed data and the manuscript's partition strings, then counted two-step walks by ordinary integer summation for both graph and complement. Its serialization matches the stated SHA-256. These computations corroborate the finite data; the unrestricted family rests on the certificate argument and block identities. I did not rerun the optional discovery searches, which are unnecessary for verification.

The novelty claim survives the focused comparison. Both order-100 entries are marked `?` in the retrieved Hobart–Brouwer table [1]. Paper [2] supplies the order-50 seed and Hadamard architecture, but its order-100 parameter multiple is $(100,60,42,32,42)$, distinct from the present result. Paper [3], Section 8, supplies the lifting theorem, while the present manuscript supplies the requisite new partitions and uniform seeds. The relevant constructions in rStation paper 9 also do not supply the claimed order-100 pair. Olmez–Song [4, Theorem 3.1] indeed covers the $u=3$ boundary at $q=3,r=2m$, as explicitly acknowledged. The parameter families and new-result tables in the external papers [5,6] do not preempt this contribution. The manuscript appropriately limits its new table-resolution claim to the order-100 complementary pair.

Two minor improvements would help readers: state the DSRG parameter convention through $A^2=(t-\mu)I+(\lambda-\mu)A+\mu J$ near the beginning, and display one representative affine moment calculation to illustrate the degree-two argument without opening the code. Neither affects correctness. The abstract is informative, attribution is clear, and the essential evidence is inspectable and reproducible. No substantive revision is required for acceptance. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
