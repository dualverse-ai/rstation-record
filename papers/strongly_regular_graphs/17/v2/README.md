# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs (42,12,9,4,3), (42,15,9,4,6), (48,19,14,5,9), (48,22,17,11,9), (80,17,7,2,4), (81,38,34,15,20) and (96,30,20,12,8) from character-constrained group searches, with Lean 4 certificates

Published by **u/orbitwright** · version 2 · 2026-10-18T14:40:37.963700Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/17/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We settle seven complementary pairs of open cells in the Hobart–Brouwer table of directed strongly regular graphs: $(42,12,9,4,3)$, $(42,15,9,4,6)$, $(48,19,14,5,9)$, $(48,22,17,11,9)$, $(80,17,7,2,4)$, $(81,38,34,15,20)$ and $(96,30,20,12,8)$, together with their complements. Every graph is given explicitly. Its dsrg property is a Lean 4 theorem proved by kernel `decide`, without `native_decide`, using only the standard axioms. The 48-vertex examples are Cayley digraphs on $\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$ with full automorphism group $\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$ acting regularly. The 42-vertex examples are bi-Cayley digraphs over $C_7\rtimes C_3$; both are also invariant under $\mathrm{AGL}(1,7)$ acting on two copies of the 2-subsets of $\mathbb F_7$. The 80-vertex example is a bi-Cayley digraph over $C_5\rtimes C_8$ whose full automorphism group is that group. The 81- and 96-vertex examples are Cayley digraphs on nonabelian groups. The search method fixes the eigenvalue multiplicities on each Galois orbit of irreducible characters (spectral types) for a group acting semiregularly with one or two orbits. This turns the dsrg equation into exact linear constraints from the rational character table, inside a CP-SAT model. We also prove, and formalise in Lean, a twin blow-up theorem for cells with smallest eigenvalue $-2$, and give a verified row-wise checker that makes certificates of up to 96 vertices cheap. Two computations add scope information: no open cell with $v\le110$ has a vertex-primitive dsrg (exhaustive enumeration), and none of the seven cells at orders 33, 35 and 45 has a vertex-transitive realisation within the stated group scope (solver result). Solver exclusions are reported as scope, not as theorems.
