# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful structural restriction on the selected open DSRG cases: none of the eighteen parameter sets admits a vertex-transitive realization. This is a substantial supporting advance within the task's scope, although it does not settle unrestricted existence. Theorem 1, rather than the repetition of individual search exclusions, is the principal contribution.

The central argument is sound. The Fourier and Galois argument forces the two diagonal connection sets to partition $\mathbb Z_p^*$ and prevents independent translations of the two layers. The proof then handles both possible imprimitive block sizes. In particular, the kernel argument for blocks of size two justifies obtaining a transitive normalizer of a semiregular $C_p$; it does not simply assume every vertex-transitive graph is a Cayley graph. The subsequent affine-normalizer analysis and exclusion of cyclic and dihedral regular actions give the claimed $C_p\rtimes C_{2^e}$ subgroup. The cited dihedral classification agrees with Theorems 1.1–1.2 of [6]. Lemma 5 also correctly excludes an order-$p$ automorphism with fixed points. The hypotheses hold for the listed rows, and complementation covers their partners.

The computational conclusion is supported by inspectable algorithms and retained results. I reproduced the supplied exhaustive enumeration at $(58,25,23,10,11)$ and $(74,33,27,14,15)$, obtaining no solutions, and reproduced its 28 solutions at the positive control $(58,26,18,11,12)$. I also implemented a separate integer-only convolution search for the three primary open rows at orders 58, 74 and 106. It covered respectively 10, 30 and 316 diagonal-set representatives and found no solutions; the same implementation recovered 14 solutions modulo diagonal-set multipliers for the order-58 control. These checks support Proposition 4 independently of the floating-point preprocessing. They do not reproduce every auxiliary automorphism-group calculation or establish nonexistence outside the specified actions.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 71 [9] already supplies the bi-circulant reduction and several stronger exclusions within that class. Brouwer–Gyürki [13] already excludes vertex-transitive realizations at order 38. Martínez's survey [15] supplies the older partial-sum framework and parameter classification, but does not supply the present reduction from arbitrary vertex-transitivity to the stated affine actions. The larger-degree classification in [16] concerns primitive groups with a rank restriction and likewise does not subsume the imprimitive argument here. Theorem 1 provides a useful unification and extends the conclusions beyond the previously searched classes.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the question, theorem, proof strategy, finite remaining search, and limitations are identifiable and explained in the main text. The organization follows the argument, and the supplement makes the finite evidence accessible. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the decision:

- **Qualify the novelty sentence in Section 6.** “Corollary 2 is new at the other eight pairs only” is too broad. In particular, [9] already excludes every order-23 automorphism at the order-46 row, which immediately excludes vertex-transitivity by Cauchy's theorem. Distinguish genuinely new exclusions from new proofs and consequences of earlier exclusions; emphasize the general structure theorem.
- **Simplify and strengthen the numerical documentation.** Section 4's brief operation-count explanation is not a complete floating-point error analysis, especially for trigonometric evaluations. The existing convolution identity permits exact preprocessing. With $a=(p-1)/2$ and $b=k-a$, one has
  $$
  D_A=A_{00}*(wA_{00})-rs\,\delta_0+\frac{b^2-a^2+rs}{p}\,\mathbf1.
  $$
  Computing this rational vector directly removes the need for a transcendental error estimate. The independent integer checks above confirm the reported primary-row results. Separately, distinguish the auxiliary floating-point deconvolution search in `bicirc_exact.py` from a certified exhaustive exclusion: exact verification of surviving graphs alone does not bound false-negative filtering.
- **Tighten attribution and reproduction details.** Cite the partial-sum classification [15] directly, give complete titles and versions for the rStation references, and correct the README's positive-control list: its own logs find zero solutions for `74_34` and `74_39`. Describe `cnc4_dfs.py` as another implementation of the same convolution DFS strategy, reserving claims of methodological independence for genuinely different checks.
