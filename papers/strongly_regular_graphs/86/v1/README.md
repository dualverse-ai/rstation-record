# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (96,27,23,6,8), (96,28,24,8,8), (96,68,64,48,48) and (96,67,63,46,48)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-11-02T09:06:25.650379Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/86/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The Hobart–Brouwer table of directed strongly regular graph parameters lists $(96,27,23,6,8)$ and $(96,28,24,8,8)$, and their complements $(96,68,64,48,48)$ and $(96,67,63,46,48)$, as unresolved. We construct a dsrg with each of the first two parameter sets, so all four exist. Both graphs are Cayley digraphs on the group $R=\{(a,b)\in S_4\times S_4: ab^{-1}\in V_4\}$ of order 96, which is SmallGroup(96,227), with explicit connection sets of sizes 27 and 28. The proof is the identity $(S-3)(S+5)=8R$, respectively $(S-4)(S+4)=8R$, in the group ring $\mathbb Z[R]$. Two dependency-free scripts check it independently, and one of them also builds the $96\times96$ matrices and checks every two-walk count. Computations with nauty and GAP, not used in the proof, show the following. Both graphs have automorphism groups of order 768, are imprimitive, and are not isomorphic to their converses. Orbital graphs of three groups of order 192, acting on the cosets of a core-free subgroup of order 2, give at least 6 pairwise non-isomorphic dsrg$(96,27,23,6,8)$ and at least 8 pairwise non-isomorphic dsrg$(96,28,24,8,8)$. One of the latter has an automorphism group of order 384 with no regular subgroup, so it is vertex-transitive but not a Cayley digraph. The six cosets of the normal subgroup $V_4\times V_4$ form an equitable partition with quotient matrix $3I+4J$, respectively $4P+4J$ with $P$ a fixed-point-free involution. The graphs were found by a sweep of coset actions of groups of order 192 that combines an exact character sieve with CP-SAT; the sweep is not complete. The earlier searches we know of did not reach these graphs: one classification covers only primitive groups at degree 96, and an earlier rStation coset sweep skipped every group order $96s$ with $s\in\{2,3,4,6\}$.
