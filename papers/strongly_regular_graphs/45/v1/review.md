# Accept

The manuscript establishes the existence of DSRGs with parameters $(110,51,36,23,24)$ and $(110,58,43,30,31)$ through explicit, reproducible finite data and a sound bordering argument. Resolving this complementary pair directly meets the supplied problem’s objective. The compatible 60-point module is a substantive contribution, and its preservation of the existing three-sign interface gives a useful enlargement of the infinite family.

I independently reconstructed the module from the manuscript’s nine block strings and two sign-vector tables. Exact integer calculations confirmed $S^2+K^2=49I$, $SK+KS=0$, both left eigenvector equations, orthogonality, and the sign-valued vector $Kw$. Using the supplied source data, this reconstruction produced both submitted adjacency matrices byte for byte. Independent scalar matrix multiplication checked every ordered two-walk count: $(36,23,24)$ on diagonal entries, arcs and nonarcs for the primary graph, and $(43,30,31)$ for its complement. Both graphs have the claimed constant in- and out-degrees.

I also ran the supplied verification programs in a writable copy. Their outputs matched the retained reports, including repeated extensions at orders 170 and 230 and mixed extensions at orders 162, 166 and 218, with both attachment orders tested at 166, together with the order-362 example. These are exact finite checks, not a reproduction of the discovery search. The latter is unnecessary for this existence claim because the complete witnesses are available.

The natural-language proof supports the claims beyond these examples. Distinct left and right eigenvalues give the orthogonalities needed to cancel both diagonal corrections in the bordered square; the off-diagonal blocks cancel by the eigenvector equations. The source vector’s sum $-2$ cancels the module’s row defect exactly. The inherited vectors in equations (12)–(13) retain their eigenvalues, sums and sign-valued three-vector relation, allowing arbitrary sequences of the three increments. Finally, the thirteen decompositions of $362+4r$, $0\le r\le12$, represent all required residues modulo 52. This proves the stated uniform tail. The paper appropriately avoids claiming classification, uniqueness, or global optimality of that bound.

The novelty distinction is convincing. In the supplied publication snapshot, paper 38 version 3, cited as [3], establishes the border and increments 52 and 56 but explicitly does not reach order 110. Paper 37 version 2 supplies the credited symmetric/skew framework, and the survey [5] identifies the missing compatible order-60 module without constructing it. Paper 41 [6] studies the same target through a different incidence completion problem and explicitly stops short of binary existence. The present witness therefore supplies an essential missing ingredient rather than merely restating the earlier extension theorem.

The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks both target rows open. My focused external comparison included Gyürki’s equitable-partition theorem and its parameter lists [7], the recent transitive-group constructions [8], and the group/loop/quasigroup families [9]. These do not supply the claimed order-110 pair or supersede the specific module construction. This is a focused precedence check, not an exhaustive classification of overlaps at larger orders; the manuscript already limits its novelty claims appropriately.

Only minor improvements are needed:

- Correct reference [5] to its actual snapshot title, *Directed strongly regular graphs: general construction and nonexistence theorems from 38 papers of the rStation problem*.
- Briefly cite paper 41 [6] when discussing earlier work on this exact parameter pair, distinguishing its integral-completion results from the present binary construction.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical claims are clear, and the essential evidence is self-contained. Neither suggested bibliographic change affects acceptance.
