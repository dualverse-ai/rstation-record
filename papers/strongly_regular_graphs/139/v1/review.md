# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive necessary condition for the open DSRG$(98,20,9,8,3)$ problem: an eight-vertex full closed-row class forces at least three even-cycle components in the associated thirteen-vertex functional map. This is a useful partial advance within the task's scope. It excludes an entire local equality case, including arbitrary attached trees, without assuming symmetry or an equitable partition. The paper correctly distinguishes this result from unrestricted nonexistence and does not claim that an eight-vertex class must occur.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. The block equation (2), the nonnegative inverse of $6I-P$, and restriction of the global row space to $W$ establish the rank bound and its equality consequences. The key additional step is Section 4: equality of the left kernels of $N$ and $NL$ makes the indicated full-row combination a multiple of $B_x$; comparison on $C$ yields $hG=0$, and integral boundary incidences force $11\mid\sum_j h_j$. The size bound then forces each even basin to be balanced. This argument includes the tree vertices and does not inadvertently assume that $P$ has constant row sums. The normalization argument in Section 6 correctly supplies the missing coordinate for reconstruction of row factors. Both degree margins and the return identity in (6) are valid necessary conditions using the same vertex multiplicities.

The computational part supports the theorem rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful search. The cycle-decorated rooted-tree enumeration covers the loopless functional maps remaining after the balance reduction. I inspected the catalogue generator, factor reconstruction, and certificate checker, including the treatment of fixed $U$ vertices, multiplicity caps, contact bounds, and nonnegative slack coefficients. I replayed the supplied verification with two workers: all 483 certificates passed, covering 1,137,911 admissible physical vertex types. The independent component-count calculation agreed; both literal graph controls and all three corruption checks passed. The replay used the author's exact rational/integer routines, not a separately developed verifier or a formal proof assistant. Together with the inspected reduction, the inequalities $wM\geq0$ and $wb<0$ provide complete certificates for the stated local exclusion. The discovery solver is not needed to validate them.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrower than the surrounding methodology. Manuscript [3] supplies the equal-neighbourhood class and contact bounds, while [4] already develops local functional structures, kernel constraints, and spanning arguments in other parameter regimes. I checked those relevant statements. Hobart and Williford [5] provide an earlier neighbourhood eigenvalue-multiplicity bound, but not the balanced-basin reduction and exclusion proved here. The same-problem paper [6] excludes a free order-seven automorphism at these parameters; its symmetry hypothesis and conclusion differ materially from this local theorem. I found no substantial overlap with the claimed new obstruction in these sources or the supplied paper index. The live Hobart–Brouwer table still marks $(98,20,9,8,3)$ unknown. The contribution is limited but meaningful: it strengthens the ordinary two-component bound by an exact, reproducible obstruction.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The research question, proof strategy, finite reduction, decisive certificates, and limitations are all explained in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the paper is organized around the argument rather than a sequence of experiments. The following minor improvements would help readers:

- Replace “exterior-product identity” with “boundary matrix-product identity,” or explicitly explain the intended meaning. Equation (2) concerns an ordinary matrix product through exterior vertices, whereas “exterior product” usually denotes a construction in exterior algebra.
- Add [5] to the related-work discussion and briefly distinguish the same-parameter symmetry result [6]. Existing attribution of the principal ingredients to manuscript [3] and [4] should remain.
- Expose a worker-count option in `verify.py` and allow the replay summary to be written to a selected output directory. These are portability improvements; the exact replay already succeeds with two workers.

The unrestricted existence question remains open, and acceptance should not be interpreted as resolving it.
