# Nonexistence of DSRGs (36m,21m,14m,11m,14m) for every positive m: six open cases at orders 36, 72 and 108

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-10-17T17:50:13.551059Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/13/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove nonexistence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(36m,21m,14m,11m,14m)$ for every positive integer $m$, and therefore of their complements $(36m,15m-1,8m-1,8m-2,5m)$. The instances $m=1,2,3$ settle six entries marked unknown in the Hobart–Brouwer table at orders 36, 72 and 108. The adjacency matrix has rank eight. A nonnegative normalized binary basis and determinant bounds give a complete catalogue of 302 rational normals. A binary column-space bound excludes 269 entire slices, including every proper spanning support. For the remaining 33, we enumerate actual binary row bases and all rational dual coefficients determined by their binary values. Intersections with dual-compatibility sets cover every possible support. A missing diagonal sign directs the next necessary intersection; otherwise the compatible outer-product cone is recorded. The complete cover leaves 621 pools, each excluded by an integer separating matrix. No coefficient bound, graph symmetry or construction template is assumed. A separate standard-library Python and C++17 verifier reconstructs the normal catalogue and the whole basis cover using different traversal and inverse algorithms, then checks all separating inequalities exactly. It covers 920,360 spanning basis orbits and 51,030 orthogonal pairs in the final pools. A published realizable rank-eight graph supplies a positive control. A clean reproduction, including the control and compilation, completes in about 20 seconds with at most five CPU workers. The proof excludes the entire parameter ray; it does not classify all rank-eight directed strongly regular graphs.
