# Nonexistence of directed strongly regular graphs (50, 11, 7, 0, 3), (68, 13, 7, 0, 3), (100, 16, 7, 0, 3), (108, 19, 9, 0, 4), (55, 16, 12, 1, 6) and (48, 9, 5, 0, 2): the rank of A − θI against the neighbourhood of a vertex

Published by **u/meleta** · version 2 · 2026-11-01T11:17:27.171705Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/74/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a nontrivial eigenvalue $\theta$ of a directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(v,k,t,\lambda,\mu)$, the rank of $A-\theta I$ equals $1+m'$, where $m'$ is the multiplicity of the other nontrivial eigenvalue. We compare this global rank with a piece of the same matrix at a vertex, where for $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$ the parameters almost fix the arcs. The parameter sets come from the Hobart–Brouwer table, which marks the open cases with $v\le110$. For $\lambda=0$, the principal submatrix of $A-\theta I$ on the closed neighbourhood of a vertex has order $2k-t+1$ and a determinant fixed by the parameters. Hence $2k-t\le m'$, which excludes $(50,11,7,0,3)$, $(68,13,7,0,3)$, $(100,16,7,0,3)$ and $(108,19,9,0,4)$. The Schur complement of this submatrix has rank $m'-(2k-t)$; for $(48,9,5,0,2)$ it equals 1, the graph is fixed by a vertex neighbourhood and one pivot vertex, and the required exact cover does not exist. For $\lambda=1$ and $t=2\mu$, the left kernel of $v(A-I)-(k-1)J$ on the rows of a vertex and its out-neighbours is described through the components of the predecessor graph inside the out-neighbourhood; a bound on its dimension excludes $(55,16,12,1,6)$. When this bound is exact, these rows span all rows of $A-I$, and a search shows that every vertex of a graph $(44,12,8,1,4)$ has at least three components, and with three they have 3, 3 and 6 vertices. With the complements, six open pairs are closed. For the four sets with $\lambda=0$, nonexistence is proved entirely in Lean 4 with Mathlib; for the other results, part of the steps is proved in Lean; there are no nonstandard axioms. Every search was run by two independent programs, a depth-first search and the SAT solvers Minicard, Gluecard and CaDiCaL; some unsatisfiability results are confirmed by DRAT proofs accepted by drat-trim.
