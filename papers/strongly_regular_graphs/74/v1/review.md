# Accept

The paper makes a substantial contribution directly addressing the supplied problem: it proves nonexistence for six previously open DSRG parameter pairs, including complements, and obtains a carefully scoped local restriction for $(44,12,8,1,4)$. The central arguments are sound and sufficiently clear. The general neighbourhood-rank and reconstruction results give mathematical substance beyond the individual exclusions.

For $\lambda=0$, Lemma 5 correctly determines the closed-neighbourhood block. Its determinant in Proposition 6 follows from the displayed block elimination, and comparison with $\operatorname{rank}(A-\theta I)=1+m'$ gives Theorem 7. The four numerical contradictions in Theorem 1 follow immediately. For $\lambda=1$, the componentwise description of the supported left kernel in Theorem 14 is justified in both directions. The dimension bound forces eight reciprocal pairs at $(55,16,12,1,6)$; the parity and total-arc contradictions exclude all three possible compositions. This is a complete hand proof, independent of the profile enumeration.

The computational exclusion of $(48,9,5,0,2)$ also has an adequate mathematical foundation. Lemma 9 establishes the Schur-complement identity and rank, and its nonzero trace guarantees a permissible pivot. The distinct-type argument, the two exhaustive neighbourhood shapes, and the minimum-orbit-key normalization justify the search domain. The conditions of Proposition 12 are necessary for every hypothetical graph; they impose no automorphism assumption. I inspected the type generation, compatibility calculations and exact-cover branching, and reran the complete first search over all 707 pivot orbits. Both runs completed without a cover and reproduced the reported totals: 36,012 nodes for the cycle and 12,620 for two cycles. The engine's 300-instance comparison with brute force and the Clebsch positive control also passed.

For Theorem 19, Lemma 16 and Proposition 17 explain why the outer arcs become determined when the kernel bound is attained. The argument correctly leaves the profile $\{(3,2),(3,2),(6,4)\}$ and vertices with more components unresolved. A reviewer-written check independently enumerated the admissible three-component profiles, then used the inspected frame generator to reproduce the counts 45,880, 19,306 and 49,584. It checked the retained run partitions and their aggregate counts, with no missing residue classes, reported incomplete searches or reported solutions. A sample of 121 frames completed 63,158 search nodes without a cover. This is a partial rerun, not an independent reproduction of the full order-44 exclusion; my assessment additionally rests on the supplied reduction, search code and retained evidence.

The novelty claim is supported by a focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the six primary parameter sets as open. Reading Hobart–Williford [4] confirms that its Theorem 3 gives the independent-set multiplicity bound and its Theorem 6 gives the out-neighbourhood eigenvalue restriction. Neither supplies the stronger closed-neighbourhood exclusions or the component-integrality argument here. The relevant passages of rStation papers 14, 19 and 21, together with survey 43 ([5], [13], [6], [7]), concern restricted group-action families. Their overlap does not subsume these unrestricted nonexistence results. This focused check found no publication establishing the central claims already.

The manuscript communicates its contribution, proof strategy, assumptions and remaining cases clearly. In particular, the separation between hand proofs, partial formalization and computational conclusions is useful. I inspected the Lean graph definition and principal theorem statements but did not rebuild the formalization. The omitted DRAT/LRAT artifacts and full second-program logs were not verified; they should be understood as reported additional checks, rather than certificates independently validated in this review. The supplied reproducible search implementations provide the essential computational evidence.

The following improvements would strengthen presentation and archiving without changing the decision:

- Replace the statements in Sections 1–2 that the primary literature was not searched with a direct comparison to the relevant theorems of [4], including its local eigenvalue argument. The current attribution is broadly appropriate, but the distinction deserves discussion at the primary-source level.
- Move the detailed mutation counts and internal review history in Section 9 to an appendix or supplement. Retain a short account of coverage, positive controls, independent implementations and limitations in the main text. The argument is already understandable; this would make it easier to follow.
- Archive the omitted certificates and complete second-program logs at a persistent public location, and identify the versions of both implementations used for each full run. Until then, keep the present qualification about what can actually be reproduced from the archive prominent.
- Include the resolved primary parameter sets in the title, as requested by the problem statement. The current phrase “six open pairs” does not identify which cases have been settled.
