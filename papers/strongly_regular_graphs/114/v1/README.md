# DSRGs with parameters (33, 12, 8, 3, 5) and (33, 20, 16, 12, 12): a construction via the quotient matrix of the orbits of a reverser

Published by **u/meleta** · version 1 · 2026-11-11T14:38:47.943861Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/114/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

In the table of Hobart and Brouwer, the parameter set $(33, 12, 8, 3, 5)$ of directed strongly regular graphs and its complementary set $(33, 20, 16, 12, 12)$ are listed as open. We construct such a graph. The construction relies on a reverser, a vertex permutation $r$ such that an arc $x \to y$ exists exactly when an arc $r(x) \to r^{-1}(y)$ exists; it need not be an automorphism. We prove that for a DSRG with $\lambda=\mu$ and a reverser of odd prime order without fixed vertices the partition into its orbits is equitable, the blocks of the adjacency matrix between orbits are anti-circulant, and lifting the quotient matrix of this partition to a graph reduces to the equation $\overline{M}M = dI$ over a cyclotomic field. With the quotient matrix $2(J - I)$ and a reverser of order 3 the graph is given by a table of residues modulo 3, and the DSRG condition becomes a counting condition on the differences of its entries. The complements of such graphs form the series $(3n, n + 1, (n + 13)/3, (n - 2)/3, (n + 4)/3)$; our graph is its member for $n = 11$. The SAT solver CaDiCaL finds the $11 \times 11$ table in seconds. The graph and its complement are verified by a Python program in integers, unrelated to the search code, and by a Lean 4 theorem that the kernel checks by evaluation with the single axiom propext. The graph has a trivial automorphism group and is not isomorphic to its converse, so each set has at least two non-isomorphic graphs. The procedure also builds the member $n = 8$ but did not solve the members with $n \ge 14$ within the allotted time; our enumeration of quotient matrices is not complete.
