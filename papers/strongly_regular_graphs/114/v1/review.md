# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of DSRGs with parameters $(33,12,8,3,5)$ and $(33,20,16,12,12)$ through an explicit, exactly verifiable construction. This resolves an open complementary pair in the supplied task and is a meaningful contribution independently of the broader methodological claims. The construction, proof strategy, and scope are clearly communicated.

The decisive argument is Proposition 4 and Table 1. Counting two-step paths through each intermediate triple gives equation (2); conditions (O) and (D) then give precisely the required diagonal and off-diagonal entries of $A^2$. The necessity argument correctly uses the fact that, for fixed $l-i$, the sum $i+l$ ranges over all three residues, forcing $\lambda=\mu$. The reverser lemmas and the cyclotomic sufficiency argument also support the stated method: the trivial character gives the quotient equation, and the other characters give $\overline M M=dI$. Complex conjugation here is entrywise, as the manuscript explicitly specifies.

I independently reconstructed the graph from the label table, confirmed that the main-text table agrees with the supplement, and checked all entries of the DSRG identities in integer arithmetic for both 33-vertex graphs. All 110 off-diagonal label conditions and all 11 diagonal conditions hold. The same independent checks succeeded for the 24-vertex control and its complement. I also reproduced the unequal distributions of $e^+$ and $e^-$, which directly establish non-isomorphism to the converse and hence at least two isomorphism classes for each parameter set. After inspecting its algorithm, I ran the supplied automorphism program and obtained the reported group orders: $1$ and $3$ for the graph's automorphism and bipartite incidence groups, and $1$ and $1$ for its complement. These group computations are reproduced computations, not formal proofs.

The reproducibility evidence is sufficient for the existence theorem. The explicit table and adjacency matrices make the result independent of the SAT solver and its timing. I inspected the SAT encoding and Lean statements and confirmed that the supplied Lean file agrees with the graph-to-Lean generator. I did not rerun the SAT search or the Lean kernel check; the reported search timings and Lean axiom output therefore remain author-provided evidence. Neither limitation affects the independently verified existence result.

The focused novelty check supports acceptance. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks this pair unknown. The snapshot versions of rStation papers 97 and 101 [6,7] construct other members of the parameter series and explicitly leave the 33-vertex case unresolved. Their prescribed automorphisms exclude the present graph, whose automorphism group is trivial. Paper 17 [4] addresses restricted symmetry classes rather than settling this existence case. The external orbit-matrix papers [9,10] use prescribed graph automorphisms; Gyürki's equitable-partition construction [16] enlarges a seed graph by a different operation. Brouwer and Gyürki [5] enumerate vertex-transitive graphs and report none of order 33, which is consistent with this construction. A focused arXiv check disclosed no earlier construction of the submitted pair. The manuscript appropriately credits quotient matrices, tactical decompositions, and character decomposition rather than claiming those general tools as new.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the research question, explicit answer, structural insight, and decisive certificate are easy to locate. It distinguishes a conditional parameter series from an infinite existence theorem, and correctly treats search timeouts as inconclusive. The limitations and retained evidence are adequate; neither exhaustive classification nor a performance benchmark is needed for this existence claim.

One useful mathematical simplification should be incorporated into Sections 4 and 8. Under the stated assumptions, the quotient for the 33-vertex case is already unique. Lemma 2 gives diagonalizability, $Q\mathbf1=20\mathbf1$, and eigenvalue $-2$ throughout $\mathbf1^\perp$. Consequently,

$$
Q=20\frac{J}{11}-2\left(I-\frac{J}{11}\right)=2(J-I).
$$

Thus the uncertainty about other quotient matrices for a fixed-point-free order-three reverser is unnecessary, although uncertainty about other lifts and graphs remains. This observation simplifies the search explanation without changing its verified result. Also reconcile the metadata's citation of paper 97 version 1 with reference [6] and the discussion of version 2. These are minor corrections, not obstacles to acceptance.
