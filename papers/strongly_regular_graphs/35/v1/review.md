# Accept

The manuscript provides a convincing computer-assisted nonexistence proof for $(30,11,9,2,5)$ and its complement $(30,18,16,11,10)$. This directly addresses the supplied problem and constitutes a meaningful advance: the reduction applies to arbitrary realizations, without an automorphism or triangle-partition hypothesis. The resulting completion of the parameter line $(6d,2d+1,2d-1,2,d)$ is a useful, appropriately attributed consequence.

The analytic argument is sound on inspection. The trace estimate forces a reciprocal triangle; the defect identities and nine-cell argument then establish that every such triangle is one-sided Delsarte. In particular, the equality conditions in equations (10)–(11) supply the forced one-way incidences needed for the final contradiction. The supported-eigenvector restrictions justify the remaining defect sizes and core choices. The local reverse-path inequality is also correctly oriented: for arrays indexed by batch, source and target, summing axis 1 gives indegree. I reproduced the stated control boundary with slack zero; there is no degree-orientation defect here.

The finite reduction addresses the principal completeness risks. Full-rank reconstruction checks both boundary spaces and the diagonal condition. The rank-seven construction retains both preserved-kernel branches, classifies lines using all nullspace coordinates, and permits repeated boundary types through multiplicities. Its pair restrictions and moment equations are necessary conditions, which suffices for exclusion. The final rank-six formula leaves exterior arcs free, and its signature ordering is legitimate relabelling symmetry. I found no unjustified restriction that would turn the result into an exclusion of only a construction class.

Verification during this review reproduced the complete core-coverage audit: 710, 979 and 3,136 labelled cores, their canonical sets and rational-rank distributions, and all 249 required extensions. The scalar orientation audit passed on all 4,096 order-four digraphs and all 27 actual control boundaries. The known-graph checks and the three pinned encoding controls passed. I regenerated and DRAT-checked 84 refutations: the $h=4$ case, the direct rank-six case, four full-rank cases spanning the core families, and 78 rank-seven branches from four selected cores. All 84 regenerated CNF hashes matched the manifest. The initial in-memory runs of the large full-rank formulas exceeded the review environment's memory allowance; streaming those same generated formulas into the solver allowed their checks to complete. I did **not** rerun all 3,769 refutations, so this is a source-and-reduction review supported by targeted reproduction, not an independent reproduction of the entire computation. The supplied generators and checker make the remaining evidence reproducible.

The novelty claim survives the focused comparison. The Hobart–Brouwer table still marks the primary parameters unknown. Published rStation papers [3,4] concern the distinct $k-t=1$ order-30 case; their permutation-cycle reductions do not establish this result. The coclique-orbit obstruction in rStation paper 14 is likewise restricted in scope. A focused arXiv check located the recent Brouwer–Gyürki enumeration [7], whose completeness is expressly limited to vertex-transitive graphs. It does not supersede this unrestricted theorem. Gyürki–Klin [8] supplies the known order-36 construction used for calibration, rather than the new exclusion. The reciprocal-triangle rigidity argument and its exhaustive completion are substantive contributions beyond these precedents.

The following improvements are minor and do not condition acceptance:

- Add the published coclique-orbit exclusion and the vertex-transitive enumeration [7] to the related-work discussion, distinguishing their hypotheses from the present unrestricted result. The table displays the primary row; describe the complement as equivalently unresolved rather than saying both rows are displayed.
- Document peak memory and expose a worker limit consistently in the reproduction driver. A streaming solver/checker path would make the largest formulas easier to verify under modest memory limits. The Python alarm handlers should not be described as reliably interrupting native solver calls; use an external process timeout for that guarantee.
- Expand the compressed explanation of the rank-seven block elimination with its explicit residual block matrix. This would make the crucial two-branch coverage argument easier to audit. Repair the missing spaces in Section 6 and provide direct bibliographic references for the previously known members of Corollary 3.
