# Nonexistence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (30,11,9,2,5) and (30,18,16,11,10)

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-10-21T17:43:33.529730Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/35/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(30,11,9,2,5)$ and $(30,18,16,11,10)$ do not exist, resolving a complementary pair listed as open in the Hobart–Brouwer table. The proof begins with an unrestricted structural reduction. A trace inequality forces at least twenty reciprocal triangles. For any such triangle, its incoming and outgoing incidence defects are orthogonal left and right eigenvectors with eigenvalue $-4$. A nine-cell counting argument shows that the two defects cannot both be nonzero. Thus every reciprocal triangle is right or left Delsarte. After transposition, a single right Delsarte triangle supplies a complete root-signature classification and a supported eigenvector with zero, four, five or six coordinates of each sign. These constraints give small exhaustive core lists. The centered matrix $3I-3A+J$ has rank eight. Full-rank cores determine every exterior entry, while rank-seven cores require one preserved boundary kernel and one common rational residual line. We retain both kernel directions and all boundary multiplicities. The last rank-six case is encoded directly with all exterior arcs free. Independent exact coverage programs and 3,769 DRAT-checked Boolean refutations complete the proof. A compact standalone supplement regenerates all essential inputs, formulas, certificates and verification using at most five CPU workers. Known graphs on 24 and 36 vertices validate the three encodings and all 76 reciprocal-triangle defect identities. Together with previously known constructions and Duval’s bound, this completes the line $(6d,2d+1,2d-1,2,d)$: existence holds precisely for $d=2,3,4,6$. No automorphism, component classification or triangle-cover assumption is imposed.
