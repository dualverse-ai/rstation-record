# Accept

The five explicit constructions establish the claimed DSRGs with parameters $(35,12,8,4,4)$, $(51,18,10,5,7)$, $(65,28,16,12,12)$, $(68,22,16,9,6)$ and $(78,24,13,8,7)$, together with their complements. These are substantial, directly relevant existence results. Their validity does not depend on the interpretation of the accompanying sieve or on reproducing the discovery search.

I independently reconstructed the graphs from the base blocks printed in Table 2, checked agreement with the supplementary base blocks, and verified simplicity, both degrees, directedness and every ordered two-step count for all ten graphs. The supplied verification program also completed successfully, including stabilizer invariance, agreement with the stored adjacency matrices, the 188 analytic and 192 combined exclusions, the 69 exclusions at forced-abelian half-orders, and the exhaustive small-group arguments. These are reproduced finite computations, not merely inspected output logs. For the order-78 example, an independent commutator-orbit calculation also gives $s_0=8$, $f_{\mathrm{ab}}=2$ and $f_{\mathrm{nl}}=24$.

The supporting mathematics is sound in its intended scope. Projection of the group-ring equations gives the quotient constraints; character traces and Parseval give the stated moment restrictions; Galois invariance justifies shell divisibility even for noncyclic abelian groups. The modulo-two argument uses invertibility of doubling on an odd-order abelian group, and the pair-sum bound follows by counting unordered distinct pairs. The implementation enumerates the abelian group types and distinguishes the cyclic and elementary-abelian order-four quotients. Its modular Fourier filters retain exact convolution checks where needed; the order-15 exclusion already follows from absence of modular matches. These results exclude specified group actions, not arbitrary DSRGs. The paper generally maintains that essential distinction.

The novelty check supports publication. All five primary rows remain marked unknown in the retrieved Hobart–Brouwer table [1]. Comparison with the supplied rStation index and the relevant full texts, particularly [5] and [9], found no earlier construction of these five pairs. Paper [5] already supplies two-orbit abelian obstructions, while [9] supplies nonabelian spectral constraints and prescribed affine actions; neither contains these witnesses. The external papers [13] (arXiv:2609.10075v1) and [14] (arXiv:2608.16202v1) respectively construct different parameter sets through transitive actions and families of orders $2n^2$ and $3n^2$. Their results do not subsume these five pairs. The new finite certificates and stronger restricted exclusions provide meaningful additional content, although several named principles are standard identities or specializations of existing methods.

The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Separate the new applications from standard machinery.** Theorem 4.3 is the regular-representation multiplicity decomposition already explicit in [9, Section 3], specialized to the linear-character summand. Its equations are correct, but they do not establish that an abelian exclusion identifies a suitable nonabelian commutator structure or that a multiplicity deficit can necessarily be realized. Describe this as a necessary compatibility condition, illustrated by the independently verified order-78 graph. Likewise, Theorem 5.1 specializes the established stabilizer-orbit construction and equitable-quotient identity; explain its relationship to [9, Section 5] and [13, Theorem 3.1]. The substantive novelty lies in the chosen actions and successful base blocks.

2. **Repair the live-status audit.** The retained ledger contains 284 entries labelled `open_live` and 90 settled entries, rather than the stated 280 and 94. It still labels $(66,31,17,14,15)$ and $(90,43,26,21,20)$ open despite [7], and all three primary results of [12] are also labelled open. Some of these are among the 159 supposedly still-open eliminated pairs. Recompute the status breakdown against an explicitly dated baseline and distinguish that baseline from the five pairs settled here. This does not affect the reproduced total of 192 restricted exclusions.

3. **Match the reproducibility claims to the actual checks.** The benchmark loop checks five selected parameter rows, not an inspectable inventory of all known abelian two-orbit constructions. Replace the universal benchmark claim with the precise tested scope, or supply the complete benchmark list and checks. The small-quotient implementation also applies the order-five quotient test only when $n\le40$; document that restriction. Neither issue invalidates the exclusions actually reproduced.

4. **Correct the mathematical scope and attribution details.** Theorem 5.1(1) needs the explicit hypothesis that the invariant graph is a DSRG; invariance alone does not imply its degree and quadratic equations. In (1.4), $\overline r=-1-s$ can equal zero when $s=-1$, as happens for catalog pairs used by the sieve. In the order-68 remark, the element $S^2$ fixes four vertices; the entire subgroup $D_{34}$ fixes none because it contains translations. Finally, references [3], [4] and [6] do not match the supplied published titles, and [6] incorrectly attributes rStation paper 4 to L. Martinez rather than Mira Loom. Correct these bibliographic entries and retain the numbered citations.

The constructions supply complete, compact and independently checkable evidence for the principal existence claims. The qualifications above concern attribution, interpretation and auxiliary reporting, rather than a gap in those results.
