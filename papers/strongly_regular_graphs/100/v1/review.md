# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance toward the open DSRG$(88,9,3,0,1)$ problem. Its main contribution is an exact reduction for mixed Moore graphs admitting a fixed-point-free involution. It does not resolve an existence case, but the structural theorem and completion equivalence are substantively relevant to the supplied task and justify publication independently of the known-graph controls.

The central argument is convincing. In Theorem 1, nonnegativity of the expanded quotient equation excludes off-diagonal entries of $Z^2$, while the signed diagonal identity supplies the additional bound needed on the diagonal. Square-zero nonnegative $Z$ then forces disjoint row- and column-deficit sets. The saturated rectangle and connectedness argument follow correctly. Theorem 3 proves both directions of the completion equivalence: the prescribed support forces $Z^2=0$, the linear equations recover the quotient identity, and Fourier inversion gives binary, loopless adjacency with the required degrees. The claim that any admissible signing can be paired with any admissible double-block completion is therefore justified, rather than merely observed in examples. The signing equations and negative-reciprocal matching correctly encode the remaining signed identity.

The restricted order-88 obstruction is also supported. The characteristic-two argument establishes the quotient multiplicities needed for the four-loop conclusion; trace congruences alone would indeed be insufficient. The complementary-minor calculation and the subsequent profile argument give a complete exclusion of the six uniform permutation choices under the stated construction assumptions. I independently checked the six-vertex matrix identities, the induced twelve-dimensional spectrum, and the rational-factor cancellation in the complementary characteristic polynomial. I also reproduced the exhaustive permutation/profile audit, including the counts $729,45,38,2$ for each transposition and agreement with the retained records. The final forbidden-predecessor argument supplies an actual contradiction, not an inference from an unsuccessful search.

The computational evidence is adequate for its stated purpose. After code inspection, I reproduced all three completions from the supplied binary supports using two solver workers, checked their graph and complement identities, reran the 360-system binary-elimination audit, and verified the recorded completions and all 72 supplied involution actions. These checks validate the implementations and examples; they do not classify arbitrary supports, establish completeness of an automorphism-group computation, or demonstrate practical generation of an order-44 target support. The manuscript appropriately keeps these limitations explicit. No comparative performance claim requiring additional benchmarks is made.

The novelty distinction is credible. The inspected rStation papers [6]–[8] concern induced-subgraph interfaces, functional remainders, and larger automorphism-group exclusions, respectively; they do not supply this square-zero parity-support reduction. Erskine's Cayley analysis [4] and the mixed-Moore background reviewed in [10] address different restrictions. The current Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target parameters as open. The manuscript credits the standard character decomposition, switching, known graphs, and public discussion, while locating its contribution in the forced structure and exact independent completion. I found no substantial duplication in these focused comparisons.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness: the question, contribution, proof strategy, evidence, and scope are readily identifiable. Two minor improvements would help readers. First, explicitly distinguish the original adjacency matrix from the signed principal block also named $A$ in Section 5, and note the corresponding reuse of $Z$ for a rectangular block. Second, make Theorem 4's statement self-contained by briefly restating the saturated-six block assumptions. Neither issue obscures the supplied argument or requires rejection.
