# A triangle family of directed strongly regular graphs on Dih(Z_n^2), and existence of (84,31,27,10,12), (90,31,17,8,12), (90,34,20,14,12), (98,36,21,10,15) and (100,37,33,16,12)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-10-26T09:26:14.100653Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/50/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The Hobart–Brouwer table lists $(84,31,27,10,12)$, $(90,31,17,8,12)$, $(90,34,20,14,12)$, $(98,36,21,10,15)$ and $(100,37,33,16,12)$, together with their complements, as unresolved. We construct directed strongly regular graphs with all ten parameter sets. The main result is an infinite family. Let $n\ge3$, and let $Y\subset\mathbb Z_n^2$ be the lattice points $(a,c)$ with $a,c\ge1$ and $a+c\le n$. Let $X$ be $Y$ without the hypotenuse $a+c=n$. Then the Cayley digraph $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{Dih}(\mathbb Z_n^2),X\cup Yb)$ is a dsrg with parameters $(2n^2,(n-1)^2,\binom n2,\binom{n-2}2,\binom{n-1}2)$. The proof is a short character computation in $\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z_n^2]$ based on the identity $\sum_{c=1}^{n-1}cw^c=n/(w-1)$. The cases $n\le6$ were already known individually, and $n=7$ settles $(98,36,21,10,15)$. We found the 98-vertex graph first as a union of orbitals of $\mathrm{AGO}^+(2,7)$ on 98 cosets of $S_3$, and then read off the triangles from its connection sets. The other four graphs come from the same sweep over coset actions of small groups. Each is a union of orbitals of a group of order $4v$ acting on the cosets of a subgroup of order 4: $C_2\times\mathrm{PSL}(3,2)$ for $v=84$, $A_5\times S_3$ for $v=90$, and $\mathrm{SmallGroup}(400,125)$ for $v=100$. The 84- and 90-vertex graphs are vertex-transitive but not Cayley graphs. None of the graphs is isomorphic to its converse. Each sporadic graph is printed as two generating permutations and one out-neighbourhood. A dependency-free script rebuilds every graph and its complement and counts all ordered 2-walks. A second, independent checker confirms the same graphs.
