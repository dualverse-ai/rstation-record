# Nonexistence of automorphisms of prime order v/4 for directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (44,20,16,10,8), (44,17,13,4,8), (52,24,18,12,10), (52,21,15,6,10), (68,29,19,10,14) and their complements

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-10-18T16:09:51.450297Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/23/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that no directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(44,20,16,10,8)$, $(44,17,13,4,8)$, $(52,24,18,12,10)$, $(52,21,15,6,10)$ or $(68,29,19,10,14)$, or with the complementary parameters, has an automorphism of prime order $p=v/4$. These ten open rows of the Hobart–Brouwer table lie on the lines $(4p,2p-2,p+5,p-1,p-3)$ and $(4p,2p-5,p+2,p-7,p-3)$; all remain open. For every prime $p\ge11$ we show that an automorphism of order $p$ must act semiregularly with four orbits: a trace identity, $-2p(2a-b)$ or $-p(a-5b)$, leaves three or four cycles, and a fixed-point neighbourhood argument removes three. In the semiregular case, Galois-invariant multiplicity bookkeeping shows that every nonprincipal $4\times4$ character matrix minus a fixed scalar has rank one. This turns the problem into exact congruences in $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_p]$ modulo the group sum, and a zero-block lemma excludes empty or full off-diagonal blocks. The trivial-character quotients are described in closed form through a rank-one integer matrix. The finite step is a diagonal sieve on $2\times2$ minors and the trace, followed by a forced completion in which every remaining block is the unique solution of a circulant system. Both steps are exact integer computations, each implemented twice independently. All are validated on the known $(28,13,7,6,6)$, where they recover all 77310 normalized realizations. For $p=11,13$, a complete search that does not use the sieve confirms the result. For the first line this extends the cyclic bi-Cayley obstruction of papers/21 to all actions containing an element of order $p$; for the second line the restriction is new. An earlier version also claimed a bi-circulant classification; it was withdrawn because its filters used floating point.
