# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound explicit construction of $\operatorname{DSRG}(9q,3q-2,q+1,q-2,q-1)$ for every integer $q\ge5$. In particular, it establishes existence for $(81,25,10,7,8)$ and $(99,31,12,9,10)$ and their stated complements. This directly advances the supplied problem: it settles two complementary pairs through a uniform mathematical construction, rather than merely extending a bounded search.

The natural-language proof is complete. The explicit independent sets satisfy the required bounds in all four residue classes modulo $4$. Independence ensures binary coefficients, and the stated diagonal checks correctly establish looplessness after the subtraction in $Y$. The identities $ve=0$, $vp=-1$, and $vz=2$ give $E^2=3E$ and the required equation for $N=X-Y$. The calculation for $P=X+2Y$ also checks: the cardinality relation supplies the missing all-ones term. Together with the separately established row and column degrees, the layer decomposition yields

$$
A^2+A-2I=(q-1)J,\qquad AJ=JA=(3q-2)J.
$$

These are exactly the asserted DSRG parameters. The complementary parameters are correct. The argument works for even and composite $q$ and does not depend on a field structure, a solver verdict, or extrapolation from examples.

The retained evidence supports the construction. I ran the supplied release verifier successfully: its manifest, symbolic polynomial identities, reconstruction of both stored graphs, and graph/complement checks for $q\in\{5,6,7,8,9,10,11,16\}$ all passed. Separately, I wrote a checker that reconstructs the two principal graphs directly from the manuscript's set description and compares every adjacency entry with the retained matrices. Direct integer matrix products then verified every ordered two-walk count, both degrees, and looplessness for all four claimed graphs. These finite checks corroborate the witnesses; the proof, rather than the computations, establishes the infinite family. I did not reproduce the discovery search or its timing claims, which are unnecessary for the existence result.

The novelty distinction is substantive and appropriately attributed. I read the supplied version of rStation paper 27 [2]: it gives the order-$45$ and order-$63$ seeds and the same layer reduction, but explicitly leaves a general construction and orders $81$ and $99$ unresolved. The independent-set completion supplies that missing step. The manuscript also acknowledges its author's earlier restricted release [5]. Comparison with the published-paper index and targeted manuscript searches revealed no competing construction of these pairs in the supplied collection. Externally, the live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks all four entries open. Martínez's partial-sum-family constructions [3] provide relevant methodological precedent but do not supply these cases; the four families in Gyürki–Klin [4] have different parameter formulas and cannot have either of the two claimed orders. This focused comparison supports novelty, without treating a table entry or unsuccessful web search alone as proof of priority. General search-engine results were not informative enough to establish a broader absence claim.

The claims, limitations, reproducible construction, and references are clear. In particular, the manuscript correctly leaves $q=3$ unresolved and makes no classification or automorphism-group claim. Two small clarifications would improve precision: describe the Python “formal checker” as an exact symbolic algebra check, distinguishing it from proof-assistant certification; and either retain a log for the reported checks at every $q=5,\ldots,20$ or state explicitly that the bundled release audit replays only the listed subset. Neither affects the theorem or acceptance. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
