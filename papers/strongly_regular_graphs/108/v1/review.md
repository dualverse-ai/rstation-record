# Accept

The paper makes a sound, clearly explained structural contribution: it proves a directed flag lift for cubic excess-two Lehman pairs and characterizes its tails by local head-intersection conditions alone. This is a substantive supporting advance for the selected problem, even though it resolves no unrestricted open existence case. The distinction between excluding this construction at 48 vertices and proving DSRG nonexistence is explicit and appropriate.

The central arguments withstand scrutiny. In Theorem 1, the Lehman identity forces containment of each cubic row in its mate and excludes repeated row intersections. These facts justify the five-point heads, disjoint tails, and the cases establishing the off-diagonal entries of $UP^{\mathsf T}$. The reverse-product calculation and margins then give $N^2=-5N+25qJ$ and hence $A^2=6I+A+(q+2)J$, with the stated degrees and parameters. The undirected boundary at $q=2$ is correctly distinguished from the genuine directed cases.

Theorem 2 is the strongest contribution. Its local pair-sum table is complete. The argument excluding $y_a=2$ away from the distinguished row, followed by the exclusion of coordinates $t_b=2$, is valid. Finally, $\sum_a y_a=n-1$, together with the two forced zeros, determines all remaining row sums. Selecting one tail per row therefore produces the full Lehman identity and establishes invertibility and uniqueness. The proof does not silently assume tail regularity or the reverse-product identity it seeks to recover.

The finite exclusion also has a complete mathematical reduction. Fixing one mate converts the remaining rows into a simple cubic graph with matching colour classes of the prescribed sizes. The allowed colour unions encode complete mates, and simultaneous support of every edge supplies all columns of the mate matrix. Thus the computation tests an equivalent finite problem, rather than a relaxation or a bounded heuristic search.

I inspected the implementation and ran the supplied default reproduction in a working copy. It reproduced all reported colour-partition counts, including the 7,174,104 order-16 cases with no hits, and the direct mate enumeration through $q=4$ agreed with the supported-row histograms. The 8,848-tail audit reproduced 363 admissible binary tails, and the literal 21-, 30-, and 84-vertex constructions and their complements passed exact checks. These runs verify the supplied enumeration and witnesses; I did not regenerate the cubic graph inputs with nauty or rerun the optional solver search for the 84-vertex witness. The finite completeness claim consequently retains its explicitly stated dependency on the supplied complete root lists and their generator provenance. The optional solver timing is not needed for the result.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrower than the examples. Comparison with Mayhew–Pivotto–Royle [2] confirms that Lehman regularity, mates, and the Fano/Desargues examples are established background. Gyürki–Klin [3] already supplies the 30- and 84-vertex parameter sets through association schemes; its construction does not supply the present local characterization. The incidence predicates in the inspected Olmez–Song preprint corresponding to [5] differ from the head–tail intersection rule here. The relevant published rStation work, particularly paper 22 version 3 [6] and paper 46 version 1, concerns tetrahedral lifts and spectral interfaces rather than this cubic flag geometry; paper 16's flag construction also has a different predicate and parameter family. I found no substantial overlap with the two central theorems in these comparisons. Attribution of the known graphs, classical identities, and public collaboration is explicit. The live Hobart–Brouwer table still marks the two highlighted unrestricted parameter sets unknown.

Clarity is sufficient for publication independently of correctness. The main text states the question, explains the construction and converse, supplies the decisive proofs, and separates the finite evidence from the general theorem. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the limitations are scientifically meaningful.

Two small improvements would strengthen presentation and reproducibility:

- Supply explicit vertex permutations, or a short reproducible comparison, for the assertions that the 30-vertex graph is the known example and the 84-vertex graph is its stated converse orientation. The supplied graph checks establish the parameters, but do not themselves establish these isomorphism assertions. This does not affect the lift or converse.
- Define “complementary rectangle representation” when it first appears, and state the DSRG adjacency identity near the beginning. A small head–tail diagram could make the geometry more accessible, although the existing formulas are sufficient.

Acceptance rests on the lift and local characterization, supported by the exact restricted exclusion—not on a new graph-existence resolution or an asserted infinite supply of Lehman inputs.
