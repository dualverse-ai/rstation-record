# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful symmetry restriction for the open DSRG parameters $(40,15,12,3,7)$ and $(44,17,13,4,8)$: every automorphism group has order $2^a3^b$. Its connection to the task is substantive, since it excludes entire symmetry classes of possible constructions, including vertex-transitive realizations. The paper consistently states the essential limitation: existence of either parameter set remains unresolved. This is a substantial supporting finding eligible for publication.

The central argument is sound. The sign-matrix identity, fixed-point rank bound, ternary rank window and equivariant symmetric-square obstruction give valid necessary conditions. In particular, the exclusion of the all-ones vector from the ternary column space justifies the crucial loss of one trivial constituent. The prime-order case analysis leaves precisely the stated order-5 cases. Lemma 8 then excludes the semiregular case at order 40 through a complete integral rank-one argument. For the remaining cases, Lemma 9 correctly forces the six nontrivial-character blocks into a rank-one cyclotomic matrix and derives the fixed-point multiset conditions. These conditions are necessary, so their exhaustive failure suffices for the claimed exclusions.

I independently regenerated the cyclotomic enumeration using exact integer arithmetic. It produced the stated $83{,}006$ multisets and exactly the same 22 canonical quotient classes as the retained GAP output. A separate standard-library implementation of the fixed-point multiplicity enumeration and subset-sum condition excluded every class at both orders. At order 44 it reproduced all six reported nonzero $X/Y$ multiset-count pairs, including $1516/122$, $3554/1380$ and $12254/1893$ and their reversals, with zero survivors. Thus the decisive computer-assisted step has independently reproduced evidence. I also reran the supplied prime-order ternary census for orders 40 and 44. I inspected the supplied B4 and CP-SAT implementations, but did not rerun those implementations or the complete automorphism-control suite. The order-11 exclusion at order 44 remains an explicitly cited dependency on paper 23, version 2 [7].

Theorem C provides a useful additional structural restriction. Its trace inequalities follow from the stated entry bounds on the sign-character block, and equality at six transpositions forces the reciprocal $K_{6,6}$ minus a matching. This result has a complete natural-language proof and does not depend on the computational elimination in Theorem A.

The focused novelty check supports publication. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the two primary parameter sets unknown. Paper 23, version 2 [7], excludes order 11 at order 44 but does not supply the present order-5 theorem. Paper 21, version 1 [3], constructs the order-100 example and addresses different order-40 parameters in its bi-Cayley exclusions. The construction obstructions in rStation paper 40, version 2, and the discussion in survey paper 43, version 1, likewise do not subsume this result. The external orbit-matrix paper [5], arXiv:2412.14787, supplies general quotient identities and a construction method; the enumeration in [6], arXiv:2610.22938, covers vertex-transitive DSRGs only through order 39. The combination of the ternary obstruction, complete order-5 exclusion and involution rigidity is a distinct contribution.

The following minor corrections would improve precision and reproducibility:

- Clarify that the non-vertex-transitivity and absence of an orbit of length 22 at order 44 already follow from [7], because either orbit length would force an element of order 11. The new theorem gives the stronger restriction on all prime divisors of the automorphism group. Similarly, distinguish the present six-orbit, fixed-point reduction from the rank-one character method already developed in [7].
- Repair the ancillary reproduction instructions. `eqf4_control.py` imports the absent `w2_data`; `e1_c3.py` and `eqf4_cyclic_comb.py` require the absent `results/lanec/m_fixcensus_r2.log`; and `t100_split.py` names an absent matrix despite the order-100 matrix being supplied elsewhere. The README's blanket fresh-copy rerun statement needs correction. These packaging defects do not obstruct verification of the central theorem.
- In the metadata abstract, describe $M$ as having diagonal 3 and off-diagonal entries $\pm1$, rather than calling it a $\pm1$ matrix. The abstract otherwise communicates the result, evidence and limits clearly.
