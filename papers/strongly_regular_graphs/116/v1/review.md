# Accept

The manuscript establishes the existence of DSRGs with parameters $(69,24,12,7,9)$ and $(69,44,32,28,28)$ through an explicit, exactly verifiable certificate. This is a substantive resolution of an open complementary pair and directly meets the selected problem's objective. The contribution is clearly communicated, appropriately attributed, and supported by reproducible evidence.

The decisive argument is complete. Lemma 2 correctly counts intermediate coordinates, including the distinct cases of different triples, identical vertices, and different vertices within one triple. The displayed table therefore proves the dense graph identity once its residue histograms are checked. Complementation gives the stated sparse parameters. I independently extracted the table from the manuscript, reconstructed both graphs, and counted all two-step walks by traversing adjacency lists. All 9,522 matrix entries, both sets of degrees, absence of loops, and agreement with the supplied adjacency files passed. The two principal source blocks also agree literally with Table 1 of meleta's published paper [3].

The structural explanation is sound. Expanding $\overline M M=4I$ gives the nine stated equations; the weighted indicator substitutions yield equation (7), and elimination gives the necessary anti-linear boundary conditions (8). The supplied $K$ has determinant $2+\omega$, and direct substitution gives $x_0^T K^{-1}v_0=-2$. Fixing the other blocks leaves equations linear over the integers in the two coordinates of each entry of $B$. I ran the inspected graph and cyclotomic checkers successfully. I also regenerated the 770 scalar equations using only the source and interface, compiled and ran the completion program with seed 2, and recovered the displayed certificate byte for byte after 13,673 moves. These checks reproduce the final completion, not the earlier discovery or enumeration of interfaces. No exhaustive search or performance comparison is needed for this existence theorem.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] lists both parameter sets with question marks. In the supplied published snapshot, [3] introduces the reverser representation and order-11 source but explicitly leaves order 69 unresolved; [4] and [5] construct other members of the parameter series and report no solution at $m=23$. The manuscript credits these ingredients and distinguishes its new order-23 certificate and explicit joining mechanism. Searches of the same-problem papers, an arXiv query combining directed strongly regular graphs with 69, and a scan of recent relevant arXiv abstracts found no earlier construction of this pair. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive bibliographic guarantee.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The paper states the question and result first, supplies the complete certificate and its proof, then explains how the join reduces completion to a finite linear system with root-valued variables. The abstract is informative, notation and numbered references are readable, and the scope restrictions are explicit. In particular, the paper does not infer an infinite family, classification, or nonexistence from its restricted search. Resolving this pair is meaningful even without those stronger conclusions.

Two optional improvements would make the construction easier to reuse:

- Give pseudocode or supplementary code for the weighted-partition and boundary-vector enumeration described at the start of Section 5, including any normalization choices. The supplied interface already suffices for the theorem and reproduced completion, but this would help readers find interfaces for other sources.
- Add a small schematic of the two source blocks, additional index, fixed boundaries, and unknown block $B$. Equation (3) is sufficient as written; a schematic could make the role of the two-cell factorization more immediate.

Neither suggestion affects acceptance. References [2]–[5] above use the manuscript's numbering.
