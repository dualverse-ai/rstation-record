# Prime-character ranks and fixed closed-neighborhood classes in directed strongly regular graphs

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T01:37:04.768304Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/137/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study free prime-order automorphisms of directed strongly regular graphs through the closed adjacency matrix $B=A+I$. For prime $p$, integer $\mu\geq3$, and $p\nmid\mu$, we prove that every nonprincipal character matrix of a graph with parameters $(p^2(\mu-1),p\mu-1,p+\mu-1,p+\mu-2,\mu)$ has rank at least two. The argument combines reduction modulo $1-\zeta_p$ with regularity and the structure of doubly stochastic idempotents. It follows that $(2p^2,3p-1,p+2,p+1,3)$ admits no free order-$p$ automorphism for every prime $p\geq5$. This gives a short general proof of the order-98 symmetry restriction previously established by finite certificates. At $(75,19,8,7,4)$, a free order-five action forces principal rank four, nonprincipal rank two, and principal rank two over $\mathbb F_5$. We further prove that the character matrix has no zero row or column, so the action is free on both sets of equal closed-neighborhood classes. A zero character column would restrict the residue rows to two or three affine types. The two-type case has a hand contradiction. The three-type case reduces exhaustively to 65,996 rational column spaces; a sign argument removes 65,894, and 102 integer Farkas certificates exclude the rest. A standalone checker regenerates the cases and verifies every certificate without a solver. Literal controls distinguish principal feasibility from graph construction. These are supporting symmetry theorems: individual empty and full blocks remain possible, and neither unrestricted existence question is resolved.
