# Nonexistence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (30,12,11,4,5) and (30,17,16,9,10)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 2 · 2026-10-21T08:04:38.873337Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/31/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The Hobart–Brouwer table lists the directed strongly regular graph parameters $(30,12,11,4,5)$ and their complement $(30,17,16,9,10)$ as unresolved. We prove that no such graph exists. The proof combines a structure theorem for every dsrg with $k-t=1$ and a complete, certified case analysis. When $k-t=1$, the one-way arcs form a permutation $\pi$ without fixed points or 2-cycles, so $A=U+P_\pi$ with $U$ symmetric. Comparing the entries of $A^2$ at $(x,\pi x)$ and $(\pi x,x)$ gives the identity $U_{x,\pi^2x}+U_{\pi^{-1}x,\pi x}+[\pi^3x=x]=\mu-\lambda$. This yields a trichotomy $\mu-\lambda\in\{0,1,2\}$, and when $\mu-\lambda=1$ every cycle of $\pi$ has length 3 or an even length. We also show that $H=A+A^T+(\mu-\lambda)I$ anticommutes with $K=A-A^T$ for every dsrg. Hence, when $k-t=1$, the orbits of $\pi^2$ form an equitable partition of $U$. Up to isomorphism, a putative graph is determined by the cycle type of $\pi$ and the matrix $U$. Of the 331 cycle types, 76 are admissible. The type $3^{10}$ has a large centralizer and splits into 14 cases after normalising one row. All 89 resulting propositional problems are unsatisfiable. Every refutation is certified by a DRUP, DRAT or LRAT proof checked with DRAT-trim or lrat-check, with proofs up to 4.4 GB and about 4.7 CPU hours in total. An independently written CP-SAT model confirms all cases, and also confirms the 255 types excluded by the cycle-length lemma. As controls, the same pipeline reproduces the known nonexistence of $(14,5,4,1,2)$ and $(32,6,5,1,1)$ and finds the known $\mathrm{dsrg}(18,4,3,0,1)$. The two other open rows with $k-t=1$, at orders 54 and 86, remain open.
