# Accept

The paper gives a sound and significant computer-assisted nonexistence argument for $\mathrm{dsrg}(30,12,11,4,5)$ and its complement $(30,17,16,9,10)$. This directly addresses the supplied problem. The contribution is an exhaustive reduction and computational exclusion of arbitrary realizations, including graphs without prescribed automorphisms. The claims about orders 54 and 86 are appropriately left unresolved.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. When $k-t=1$, the oriented layer is necessarily a permutation without cycles of length one or two. Comparing the two directed two-walk counts proves equation (2), and the alternating binary sequence argument correctly excludes odd cycle lengths greater than three when $\mu-\lambda=1$. The centralizer action used to normalize row zero for $3^{10}$ covers every possible neighborhood: rotations are transitive on both singleton and two-element subsets of a triangle, and the remaining triangles may be permuted freely. No automorphism of the completed graph is assumed. I independently confirmed the counts of 331 total cycle types, 76 admissible types, and 14 normalized cases, giving precisely the stated 89 problems. The anticommutation and equitable-partition arguments also hold; the latter is correctly separated from the computational proof's dependencies.

The computational evidence is adequate and reproducible. Inspection of both encodings supports their correspondence with the full adjacency equation: reciprocal variables are symmetric and disjoint from the oriented arcs, row regularity is enforced, products have equivalence constraints, and the two off-diagonal targets are conditioned in the correct direction. Every required case has a verified-status record. Using the specified PySAT version, I regenerated all 97 logged formulas and obtained 97 matching SHA-256 hashes with no mismatches. I also reproduced the positive $(18,4,3,0,1)$ control and independently ran DRAT-trim on newly generated refutations for cycle type $3^8 6$ and the normalized $3^{10}$ case $(n_3,n_2,n_1)=(0,2,7)$; both returned `s VERIFIED`.

These checks are not a reproduction of the entire certified run. The original proofs were deleted, and I did not regenerate the largest refutations or rerun the complete CP-SAT enumeration. The retained checker verdicts are records of the authors' checks, rather than proof certificates themselves. Nevertheless, the transparent exhaustive reduction, inspectable encoders, matching formulas, successful sampled proof checking, positive control, and supplied regeneration procedure together support acceptance. Archiving the proofs would make future verification substantially easier.

The focused novelty check supports the unrestricted result, but the related-work account needs correction. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the pair unresolved. However, “No rStation paper treats them” is false: paper 19v1, Theorem 4.2(2), excludes bi-Cayley realizations over $\mathbb Z_{15}$ [17], and paper 21v1, Section 2.1, excludes a specified affine action with orbit sizes $10+20$ [18]. Brouwer and Gyürki's recent complete vertex-transitive enumeration through order 39 also excludes the target within that class [19, Table 2 and Section 4]. These are restricted exclusions and do not subsume this paper's theorem. The symmetric/skew identities are already credited to manuscript reference [11]. The novelty should therefore be stated as the unrestricted nonexistence theorem and its permutation-layer reduction, with these earlier restrictions acknowledged.

The following corrections would improve the publication without changing the decision:

- Update the README's claim that proofs reach about 160 MB: the supplied records reach approximately 4.45 GB, as the manuscript correctly states. Give peak memory and temporary disk requirements as well as runtime. The original normalized-case records reside in `cert30_sub.jsonl`, so show the summary command with both supplied logs.
- Make the certification driver fail immediately if encoding fails and resume only successfully verified cases. Currently it ignores the encoder's return code and skips every case already present in the log, including failed cases. These are reproducibility weaknesses, not defects demonstrated in the successful submitted records.
- Describe the CP-SAT exclusions of the other 255 types as a consistency check at the target parameters, rather than an independent confirmation of the general cycle-length lemma. Likewise, qualify the assertion that a large centralizer explains computational hardness as an interpretation, not an established causal result.
