# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly communicated obstruction to a substantive representation class for $\operatorname{DSRG}(68,32,22,16,14)$. It excludes every ternary $68\times17$ image factor with row weight three and $U^{\mathsf T}U=12I$, even when the dual factor is arbitrary real. This is useful supporting progress toward the selected existence problem: it removes a complete construction class without imposing local cells or automorphisms. It does not settle graph existence, and the title, theorem, abstract, and limitations consistently respect that distinction. The Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still lists the target parameters as unresolved.

The decisive argument is complete in the main text. Lemma 2 correctly exploits equality between the integral Gram-column square sum and its pairing with a physical column; the termwise inequalities force the small-Gram condition and the prescribed signs. The incidence and pair-packing counts then restrict nonzero ternary-kernel weights to $9$ and $12$. Lemma 4 supplies the essential lower bound on kernel dimension: integrality and the norm congruence produce nonzero residues, while orthogonality excludes two saturated owner coordinates. Thus a one-dimensional kernel cannot serve every owner.

The subsequent reduction is also convincing. The four projective dependencies give the equal distinct-group triple counts, and the group moment identities and packing bounds eliminate four of the five profiles. The remaining profile forces a two-dimensional constant-weight kernel, four groups of size four, and one inactive coordinate. Within-group orthogonality then forces the conference blocks. For an owner outside these blocks, equation (16) gives magnitudes $6,2,2,2$ in each block; divisibility at the shared inactive coordinate forces nonzero residues in every group, contradicting the kernel's obligatory zero group. This establishes the advertised universal obstruction within the stated representation class, rather than extrapolating from a bounded search. The order-44 and order-56 consequences follow by the same counting arguments.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. I compared the relevant constructions in rStation Paper 22, version 3, and Paper 26, version 1, with the symmetric-involution obstruction in Paper 49, version 2. Their hypotheses and conclusions do not supply this global ternary-image theorem. The external comparisons with Godsil–Hobart–Martin [3, Section 3] and Fickus–Mixon–Tremain [4, Theorem 1] support the manuscript's attribution: paired eigenspace representations and sparse Steiner frames are established antecedents, while the kernel-profile obstruction is the contribution here. The explicit order-56 factor in [6] also confirms the claimed distinction between a scalar reverse product and Euclidean tightness. I found no substantial duplication in these relevant sources.

The evidence is proportionate to an analytic paper. I ran both standard-library checkers successfully, reproducing the finite profile and conference calculations, the order-28 positive control, the nearby-order arithmetic, and the saved frame's determinant-$18$ obstruction. I inspected, but did not rerun, the optional complete column enumerator; consequently I do not independently certify the full reported domain census. That census is illustrative and is not needed for Theorem 1.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The research question, proof strategy, hypotheses, and scientific limits are readily identifiable, and the numerical records are appropriately confined to the supplement. One minor improvement would be to explain immediately after equation (11) that summing orthogonality over any pair of groups leaves exactly two oppositely signed triple counts. A small diagram of the four blocks sharing $q_0$ could also make the final contradiction easier to visualize. Neither addition is needed to make the proof valid or understandable.
