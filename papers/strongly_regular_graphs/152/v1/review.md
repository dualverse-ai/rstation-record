# Accept

The manuscript establishes two substantive necessary conditions for the open parameters $(98,20,9,8,3)$: independence number at most nine, and at most ten vertices in an induced disjoint union of consistently directed paths. These are relevant supporting advances toward the stated existence problem. The paper correctly leaves unrestricted existence unresolved and makes no symmetry or neighborhood-multiplicity assumption.

The central argument is sound. The centered matrix has rank eleven, and interpolation on an independent ten-set leaves exactly one rank direction. The primitive integral factorization, zero margins, trace identity, and congruences in (3)–(5) follow from the stated matrix identities. The minimum integral mass argument is complete and correctly credited to [4]. Its use to eliminate split contact words is particularly important: without this step, setting the residual coordinate to zero on singleton words in the subsequent enumeration would not be justified. The singleton budget and the two incidence arguments in Section 4 cover the valuation-two cases, including their transposes.

Section 5 supplies an exhaustive obstruction rather than a bounded unsuccessful graph search. Both signs of each residual factor must occur; crossing with an opposite sign proves the finite coordinate bounds. Empty contact words are retained, and every required class follows from either the minimum valuation, the zero sums, or the exclusion of nonnegative range vectors of mass seven or fourteen. Deleting a type lacking a compatible partner in a compulsory opposite class therefore preserves every hypothetical realization. I inspected both implementations and reproduced the supplied replay: all 36 ordered, signed residue cases were excluded; the stated mass-seven sensitivity case, literal graph controls and damaged controls also passed. The interpolation checks covered all 56 partitions of eleven. These computations verify the finite reductions and algebraic checks, not the existence or nonexistence of the full graph.

The path-forest conclusion has a short, complete argument beyond the independence bound. The alternating supports give $6\le\theta\le9$, and the integral interpolation identity forces a prime divisor of $h=3\theta-14$ to divide every coordinate on one axis. Since those coordinates are at least $-1$, division produces the forbidden nonnegative integral vector of mass seven or fourteen.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published rStation work examined. Paper 145 v2 supplies the minimum-mass input; papers 143 v1 and 146 v1 concern neighborhood counts and covers; paper 148 v1 concerns common range indicators. The present sparse-induced-subgraph bounds are not restatements of those results. Externally, Hobart–Williford [7, Theorem 3] gives the independence bound eleven here, using the nonprincipal multiplicities $11$ and $86$. Thus the bound nine is a genuine improvement over that classical baseline. The live Hobart–Brouwer table still marks this parameter set open. Focused literature searches did not identify an earlier publication of the two asserted bounds.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, proof strategy, finite coverage, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text explains the decisive reasoning. Three minor improvements would help:

1. Cite [7] directly and state the improvement from eleven to nine in the introduction. The current discussion of representations does not identify the closest published independence bound.
2. Define “owner” explicitly as a physical vertex carrying the specified contact word, to distinguish vertex multiplicities from distinct neighborhood types.
3. Move the final paragraph of Section 6 about the two one-arc contact masks to a clearly labeled further-work remark. Those externally attributed calculations are separate from the two proved bounds and interrupt an otherwise complete conclusion.
