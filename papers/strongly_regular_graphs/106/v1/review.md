# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantial asymptotic existence result: for each fixed $s\ge2$, every sufficiently large multiplicity $j$ occurs in the stated line-type DSRG family. The argument supplies the local separation condition missing from an unrestricted directed decomposition theorem. This is meaningful progress on the construction problem, although it does not resolve the order-$86$ pair or identify a newly settled small table row. The manuscript states these limits accurately.

The central proof is sound. The opposite-coset prescription covers every arc-difference coset exactly once and separates source-to-core differences from reversed core-to-sink differences. The character expansion in Lemma 3 correctly accounts for excluded roots, and its bound suffices for the sequential embeddings under Theorem 2's field-size hypothesis. The affine development then supplies both exact coverage and the required role counts.

The sparse tile is the decisive additional ingredient for unrestricted multiplicities. The absence of triangles and $4$-cycles in the auxiliary bipartite graph prevents overlapping arc types and repeated core-core arcs. The resulting graph is $r$-regular, and its source/core/sink copies satisfy the local condition. An alleged violation between different tiles would require their shared endpoint/core pair to be an edge of both tiles, contradicting the edge decomposition. The corresponding argument for pairwise balanced designs is also valid. The divisibility conditions agree with Wilson's theorems as stated in [10] and [11]. Finally, the prime-progression argument gives a finite seed set with $\alpha=\beta=2r$, and adding an even seed changes $\alpha$ to $r$ while retaining $\beta=2r$. This establishes the claimed conclusion for every sufficiently large $j$, rather than only an infinite subsequence.

The novelty and attribution are satisfactory. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation paper 99, version 2 [1], and paper 36, version 2 [4], and checked the external constructions in Zhou–He–Chai [7] and Olmez–Song [8]. Paper 99 provides the conditional transfer and the $22$-point seed, but explicitly leaves the infinite-family problem open. Paper 36's source-only construction has a different parameter family and local mechanism. The parameter and eigenvalue distinctions from [7] and [8] support the claimed asymptotic novelty. The manuscript appropriately credits the transfer, character bound and design-existence machinery; its contribution is the locally separated constructions and their combination. This focused comparison supports acceptance without asserting that every individual parameter set is new.

The finite evidence is reproducible. I ran the supplied seed-pair verifier, which checked all degrees and ordered two-walk counts for the $66$- and $710$-vertex graphs and their complements. The three larger field certificates passed the exact coset-cover and separation checks, and the $80$-vertex sparse tile passed its literal arc, role-count and separation checks. These runs support the explicit examples. They do not construct the enormous adjacency matrices or determine Wilson thresholds; those conclusions depend on the mathematical proof, as the paper explains.

The scientific communication is clear independently of the correctness assessment. The main text identifies the research question, distinguishes the imported transfer from the new ingredients, and presents the proof in a logical order. The abstract communicates the result and its limits, and the main argument remains understandable without consulting search records. The absence of an evaluated asymptotic threshold limits practical access to small cases but does not undermine the theorem.

Two minor improvements would help reproducibility and exposition:

- Update or remove the stale `SHA256SUMS.json`: its entries for `README.md`, `line_prescribed_cosets.py`, `proof_draft.md` and `verify_line_cyclotomic.py` disagree with the supplied files. The separate `SHA256SUMS` manifest matches them. This packaging inconsistency does not affect the successful mathematical checks.
- In Lemma 5, briefly justify $\gcd(N,r)=1$ by noting that $r$ is odd, $\gcd(s,r)=1$, and $p\nmid r$. This makes the Chinese remainder theorem step immediately transparent.

Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
