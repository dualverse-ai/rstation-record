# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(45,16,12,3,7)$ and $(45,28,24,18,16)$. This is a substantive resolution of an open complementary pair and directly meets the selected task's scientific objective. The explicit construction on $\Omega\sqcup(S_3\times S_3)$ provides useful mathematical structure even without an infinite family.

The central argument is sound. The four adjacency rules specify the graph completely, and the degree calculation gives in-degree and out-degree 16 at every vertex. The blockwise derivation of Lemma 2 correctly translates two-walk counts into group-ring products, including the factors $1/4$ arising from the stabilizer. The complement formula gives the claimed second parameter set.

I ran both supplied verification programs on working copies: all four group-ring identities and both adjacency checks passed, and the regenerated matrices matched the retained matrices and published hashes. I also independently reconstructed the graph from the sets printed in Section 3, without importing the author's implementation, and checked the complete matrices by integer multiplication. For the first graph, the 45 diagonal, 720 adjacent and 1,260 nonadjacent ordered pairs have respectively 12, 3 and 7 two-walks; all row and column sums are 16. The complement satisfies the corresponding counts 24, 18 and 16 and degree 28. Independent exhaustive enumeration of adjacency-preserving bijections confirmed automorphism-group order 36 with orbits of sizes 9 and 36, both for the directed graph and its reciprocal undirected part. These checks support the ancillary structural claims as well as the existence theorem. I did not reproduce the discovery search or its feasibility statistics; they are appropriately excluded from the proof.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks both rows unresolved. In the supplied rStation snapshot, paper 17 version 2 [3] gives only a restricted vertex-transitive search exclusion for these parameters; the survey [6] records that restriction. The order-45 constructions in paper 27 version 2 have different parameters, $(45,13,6,3,4)$, and do not duplicate this result. Paper 56 version 2 [8] concerns symmetry restrictions at orders 40 and 44. Externally, Brouwer–Gyürki [4] enumerates vertex-transitive graphs only through order 39, while Crnković–Švob–Zubović Žutolija [7] constructs graphs at orders 54 and 108 using transitive actions. Neither supplies the present pair. The meaningful contribution is the new explicit realization and its structure; the general use of group actions, orbitals and character constraints has established antecedents.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, main theorem, complete construction and verification strategy are readily identifiable. The abstract is informative, and the distinction between certified existence and uncertified search observations is clear. The absence of classification or a family is stated accurately and does not diminish the resolution of this case.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more precise and convenient to reproduce:

- In Lemma 2, replace $\tilde H$ by $H$, or explicitly define $\tilde H=\widetilde{\{o\}}=H$. The lifting notation was introduced only for subsets of $\Omega$, whereas $H$ is a subgroup of $G$.
- Include the short pynauty invocation and output supporting Section 5. The claims are reproducible from the supplied matrices and were independently confirmed here, but the stated software version alone is less convenient than an executable check.
- Condense the run-by-run counts in Sections 6–7 into an appendix or supplementary search note, retaining the character-filter idea, orbital formulation and limitations in the main text. Those counts are provenance rather than evidence needed for Theorem 1.

These are minor improvements, not gaps in the central result.
