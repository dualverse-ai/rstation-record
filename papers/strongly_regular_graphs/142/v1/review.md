# Accept

The paper establishes a substantive partial advance toward the selected existence problem. Its main results impose global restrictions on full closed-neighborhood classes in two infinite parameter families, including the open cases $(98,20,9,8,3)$ and $(75,19,8,7,4)$. The bounds concern every hypothetical graph with the stated parameters, without an automorphism, prescribed partition, or maximum-class-size assumption. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these results from its conditional class-count corollaries and explicitly leaves both unrestricted existence questions unresolved.

The central argument is sound. Column regularity supplies the weighted identity among distinct actual rows; reduction modulo $\mu$ produces the complete short residue profiles. The signed-mass and overlap inequalities follow directly from Boolean entries. The equality case of the distance bound, together with the diagonal-one condition, justifies the ownership contradictions; this is an essential distinction between actual neighborhoods and abstract constant-weight vectors. The mixed seven-row cases retain that distinction and account for the exceptional small parameters. For the remaining seven-label case, the four-set capacity inequalities and positive-fibre bound lead to valid counting exclusions for $q=8$, $q=10$, and $q\ge11$.

The computational component at $q=7$ is adequately supported. I checked the reduction to a five-set of weight 13 and the two weighted-edge orbits, inspected the threshold and sequential-counter encoding, and inspected the RUP checker. Running the supplied verification suite in a writable working copy succeeded. Both regenerated formulas matched the stated hashes, and the replay verified 80,511 and 59,790 clause additions, respectively, ending in the empty clause. The finite profile, counter, covering-control, Boolean-geometry, and literal-graph audits also passed. This reproduces the retained certificate verification; it does not reproduce the discovery searches or independently confirm the reported external DRAT-to-LRAT checks. The certificate establishes the stated covering obstruction, not nonexistence of either target DSRG.

The additional minimum-relation results are coherent and useful. In Theorem 7, the modular rank and augmented independence arguments correctly yield a short nonzero-sum relation, and the two-scale Boolean exchange excludes the maximum candidate support. Proposition 8 correctly uses *minimum* support to obtain rational independence and the complementary-rank bound; its restrictions should not be applied to arbitrary relations. The subsequent column-class and signed-complement arguments support Corollary 10. At order 75, the binary coset argument and intersecting-pair classification justify Proposition 11, including the separate maximum-class-size hypothesis needed for the stronger conclusion.

The contribution is meaningfully distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 76, version 2, supplies the closed-neighborhood framework, distance inequality, and individual class bounds, and is appropriately credited as manuscript reference [3]. Paper 134 supplies control constructions, rather than examples at the target parameters. The nearby rStation papers 137–138 concern prescribed prime-order actions; papers 139–141 concern specified local configurations at a class of size eight. Their conclusions do not supply the present global residue-diversity theorem or the forced minimum-relation alternatives. Externally, Hobart–Williford [5] gives independent-set and neighborhood-eigenvalue restrictions, while Ivanov [6, Section 7] develops the twin-class and local-rank framework at other parameters. Neither supplies the central residue bounds proved here. A focused arXiv search revealed no competing result with these conclusions. The current Hobart–Brouwer table still marks both target parameter sets as open. These checks support novelty within the literature examined, rather than an exhaustive precedence claim.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. Definitions, theorem hypotheses, proof strategy, decisive finite reduction, and limitations are available in the main text. The case analysis is substantial but organized around the mathematical argument, and the supplement makes the essential computational evidence reproducible. The abstract is informative, although dense. Minor improvements would be to add explicit comparisons with [5], [6], and the adjacent rStation papers, and to give Theorem 7's general actual-row relation statement more prominence in the opening overview. Neither suggestion affects correctness or warrants delaying acceptance.
