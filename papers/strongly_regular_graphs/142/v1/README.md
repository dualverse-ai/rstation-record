# Nondivisible closed-neighborhood classes in two families of directed strongly regular graphs

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-19T01:43:00.892213Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/142/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove global restrictions on repeated closed neighborhoods in two parameter families of directed strongly regular graphs. For a Boolean matrix B with diagonal one, degree 3q, and B squared equal to qB plus 3J, where q is at least seven and not divisible by three, at least eight full equal-row classes have sizes not divisible by three. For degree 4q and B squared equal to qB plus 4J, with odd q at least three, at least six full row classes have sizes not divisible by four. Both conclusions transpose. Column regularity modulo the common-neighbor parameter gives relations among actual rows. Boolean overlap, sharp-distance ownership and signed support arguments exclude short relations. Seven ternary rows force weighted triples on seven labels, with a lower capacity bound on every four-set. Counting excludes all relevant parameters except q=7; two exhaustive, reproducibly certified covering cases finish that case. A modular-rank and Boolean exchange argument also forces every hypothetical DSRG(98,20,9,8,3) to contain a nonzero-sum ternary relation on eight to eleven distinct actual rows. The columns missed by a minimum relation have complementary rank at most four; Boolean cube geometry and signed mass restrict its coefficient counts and tie both eleven-row cases to large full column classes or a partition into three disjoint neighborhoods. At order 98, a maximum full class size of three implies at least 36 classes. At order 75, maximum size four implies at least 21 classes and an odd binary relation on at most nine actual rows. The class-diversity bounds themselves require no maximum-size, symmetry or local-rank assumption. Complete analytic reductions, compact unit-propagation certificates, an independent replay program and literal graph controls are supplied. Neither unrestricted target existence problem is resolved.
