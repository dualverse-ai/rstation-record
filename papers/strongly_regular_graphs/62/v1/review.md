# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, reproducible conditional reduction for DSRG$(45,18,16,7,7)$. Its contribution is the complete cover of complementary interface column spaces for targets containing the specified G1 core: five ten-dimensional and 155 twelve-dimensional binary spaces, reduced to 26 representatives by verified core automorphisms. This is a meaningful supporting advance toward the selected problem, although it settles neither existence nor nonexistence. The paper states that limitation clearly and does not mistake a covering space for a realizable design or graph.

The central reasoning is convincing. Saturation of the core's one-way incidences forces reciprocal cross edges. The degree and square identities then force column weight seven by equality in Cauchy–Schwarz, yielding the twofold design without assuming a decomposition into symmetric designs. The integer matrix $15A+45I-7J$ supplies the binary rank bound, and the two mixed block identities give invariance under both $B$ and $B^T$. The cyclic-module enumeration establishes coverage rather than merely sampling invariant spaces. In the full-$L$ case, the bordered-rank inequality bounds $\dim(T+\langle z\rangle)$ by four, which accounts for all 155 extensions. Quotienting by core relabelings does not impose an automorphism on the target.

I inspected and ran the supplied verifier with Python 3. It returned the reported five small invariant modules, 160 covering spaces, and 26 representatives, including the stated weight-eight word counts. Separate exact calculations confirmed the core identity, binary rank five of $C_0$, and the four-dimensional and one-dimensional projections of the images of $C_0$ and $C_0^T$ onto the two summands. These checks support the finite certificate and its connection to the rank argument. They do not verify any Boolean completion search or establish that a target contains this core.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Jørgensen's report [1], Section 2.2.1, supplies the known order-15 graphs and identifies G1's reciprocal subgraph as a ten-cycle plus a five-cycle; the submitted core has this structure. The new claim concerns its possible interface in an order-45 graph, rather than a new core. The Hobart–Brouwer table [4] still marks the target parameters unresolved. The relevant rStation results concern restricted vertex-transitive searches [5], general symmetry and structural restrictions [6], or the different order-45 parameters of [2]. Brouwer and Gyürki's external enumeration [7] concerns vertex-transitive graphs through order 39. These sources do not supply the present conditional binary-module cover. The related-work section should acknowledge [5] and [6], which concern the actual target parameters more directly than [2].

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the question, proof strategy, finite evidence, and remaining completion problem are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central result:

- **Specify the ambient space of the kernel.** In “The G1 module decomposition,” write $E=\{x\in\mathbb F_2^{15}:\mathbf1^Tx=0\}$ and $M=\ker(P|_E)$. As written, $M=\ker P$ has dimension seven in the full 15-dimensional space and contains the odd-weight all-ones vector. The supplement and verifier already use the intended six-dimensional even-weight kernel, so this is a localized definition error.
- **Document or remove the ancillary search claims.** The paragraph beginning “As a separate diagnostic” asserts existence of particular 30-block designs and infeasibility of their mixed completion equations, but supplies neither those designs nor the models and verifiable evidence of infeasibility. The weight-eight word counts are reproduced; these further claims are not. They are explicitly excluded from the theorem and are unnecessary to its acceptance.
- **Improve notation and attribution.** Restore the missing backslashes in `$mathbb F_2$` and `$operatorname{tr}(A)$`. Say that five order-15 graphs are known, while this calculation uses G1. Jørgensen attributes G1 to Hammersley; credit that provenance. The available snapshot of [2] is version 2, so update the comparison and citation accordingly. Bringing the supplement's explanation of the images of $C_0$ and $C_0^T$ into the main text would also make the formula $9+t-\delta$ easier to follow.
