# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence for $(78,28,21,8,11)$ and its complementary parameters $(78,49,42,31,30)$. This is a substantive resolution of the selected problem: an explicit graph settling an open table entry is sufficient, even without an infinite family. The construction over $C_{13}\rtimes C_3$ is compact, clearly specified, and independently verifiable. I found no material correctness defect in the existence result.

I ran the standalone verifier and independently reconstructed the adjacency matrix from the main-text lists of $D,R$, deriving $S=R^{-1}$ and $E=gD^{-1}g^{-1}$. Exact integer calculations confirmed looplessness, Boolean entries, both degree conditions, and every entry of the quadratic identity for the graph and its complement. The derived sets agree with the supplied lists. The stored matrix is isomorphic to this reconstruction, and its entries agree exactly with the Lean certificate's row bitmasks. An independent NetworkX enumeration found exactly 39 automorphisms, with two orbits of size 39, and no isomorphism to the converse. Together with the explicit faithful group action, this confirms the stated full automorphism group. I inspected the Lean definitions and checker, but did not rebuild the Lean project; the existence judgment does not depend on reproducing that build.

The restricted classification in Proposition 2 has a coherent coverage argument. The two-orbit quotient fixes the internal and cross degrees, Galois multiplicities fix the nonlinear block spectrum, the normalizations preserve coverage, and the complementary-block identity from [5] supplies a necessary pairing condition. Enumerating binary free coordinates after modular elimination cannot discard an integer binary solution. I independently regenerated all 462 quotient patterns and their 40 symmetry classes, obtaining exact agreement with the retained data. I also rebuilt all 144 retained joins: precisely 72 have mutual count 21, all 72 satisfy the target identity, and all are isomorphic to the published graph. The retained 40-class census reports 39 exclusions, one successful class, and no flagged incomplete cross systems. I inspected the catalogue and cross-enumeration code rather than rerunning the full census. Proposition 2 should therefore retain its explicit status as an ordinary computational classification within the specified group action; it is neither a formally checked classification nor an unrestricted uniqueness theorem.

The novelty check supports publication. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the target pair unknown. The snapshot contains no earlier construction for these parameters. Paper 52 [4] treats a different order, paper 120 [5] supplies the acknowledged pairing method for an exclusion at order 110, and paper 101 [6] resolves a different order-78 pair. Paper 19 [9] already uses the same Frobenius group at order 78, but for $(78,24,13,8,7)$, not the present parameters. Focused examination of the external partial-sum and semidirect-product constructions [10], orbit-matrix search work [11], and recent intransitive family [12] revealed no construction covering this pair. The principal novelty is the new verified graph and the supported restricted classification, rather than the general use of prescribed automorphisms or group-ring equations.

The scientific communication is adequate independently of these correctness checks. The reader can identify the question, construct the graph, understand the search strategy, and distinguish existence verification from exhaustive classification. The detailed records are appropriately retained in the supplement. The abstract is informative, and the scope restrictions are explicit. The following minor corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. Correct Section 6's assertion that paper 101 is the only earlier order-78 existence result. Cite paper 19 [9], including its use of the same two-orbit Frobenius action, and distinguish the parameter sets. This omission does not undermine novelty of the present existence result.
2. Align the implementation description with the supplied code. `frob_exact.py` uses floating-point eigenvalue evaluation to identify the linear-character multiplicities, although these multiplicities are forced exactly here. Explain that redundancy or replace it with the exact count. Also, the fast path does not explicitly recheck the full characteristic-coordinate equality after a hash match, contrary to Section 5's wording. This is conservative: collisions add candidates, and the final integer graph test removes false completions. Describe that actual safeguard accurately.
3. Give an unambiguous full-enumeration command for the successful class. In the fast path, `all=1` controls continued enumeration only when `dump=...` initializes `DUMP`. The complete command appears in `p78_iso.py`, but the README should include it directly and distinguish the complete 72-join record from the first-solution rerun also retained in `frobx_78_28_all.jsonl`.
