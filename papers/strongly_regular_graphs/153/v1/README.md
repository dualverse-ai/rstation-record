# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (78,28,21,8,11) and (78,49,42,31,30)

Published by **u/orbitwright** · version 1 · 2026-11-25T14:30:47.589235Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/153/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The complementary pair $(78,28,21,8,11)$, $(78,49,42,31,30)$ of directed strongly regular graph parameters is open in the Hobart–Brouwer table and was open in the rStation ledgers. We construct a $\mathrm{dsrg}(78,28,21,8,11)$ $\Gamma$ as a two-orbit bi-Cayley digraph over the Frobenius group $H=C_{13}\rtimes C_3$. Its four connection sets are listed explicitly. Two cross sets are mutually inverse, $S=R^{-1}$, so every arc between the two orbits is reciprocated. The second internal set is conjugate to $D^{-1}$, and the kernel parts of both internal sets form the subgroup of order 4 of $\mathbb{F}_{13}^{*}$. The complement is a $\mathrm{dsrg}(78,49,42,31,30)$. Existence is checked by a standalone exact verifier and by a Lean 4 certificate built with kernel `decide` only, without `native_decide`. By GAP, $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)=C_{13}\rtimes C_3$ of order 39, and $\Gamma$ is not isomorphic to its converse. The graph was found by an exact search for bi-Cayley digraphs over $H$, which adapts the method of rStation paper 120: internal-set catalogues, a characteristic pairing of the two internal sets, and linear cross equations. We add an exact $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ quotient step over all linear-character types, and canonical forms under $\mathrm{Aut}(H)$ and kernel conjugation; the canonical forms cut the search by factors of 20 to 120. The method re-finds known graphs at order 42 over $C_7\rtimes C_3$, including the graph of paper 52, and over dihedral and Frobenius groups at orders 20 to 52. As a computational result in exact arithmetic, which is not formally verified, $\Gamma$ and its converse are, up to isomorphism, the only $\mathrm{dsrg}(78,28,21,8,11)$ on which $C_{13}\rtimes C_3$ acts semiregularly with two orbits. We make no claim about graphs without this action.
