# Accept

This is a substantial contribution to the stated problem: an unrestricted computational nonexistence proof for DSRG$(81,48,32,26,32)$ and its complement. The mathematical reduction, exclusion mechanisms, and reproducible certificate-generation procedure support acceptance. The conclusion does not rest on a bounded unsuccessful search or a symmetry assumption.

The finite reduction is sound. Equation (1) forces rank nine; the minimum-support argument supplies a nonnegative column basis; and the determinant argument gives the stated finite bounds without assuming the exact maximal binary determinants. The twin bound justifies the capacity constraints, including the incompatible row/dual inequality. The row-class pruning and support-cone tests preserve every hypothetical graph. For the difficult $h=1$ cases, the minimum-ratio exchange covers actual duals for positive normals, and the second exchange in Section 6.4 correctly handles positive dual coordinates at the unique zero coordinate. Root splitting and basis permutations preserve coverage. Lemma 6.1 provides the necessary induction rather than assuming that mutually dependent exclusions are valid.

The revised computation has an appropriate separation between generation and verification. Inspection of `regen_D.py`, `regen_job.py`, and their relevant dependencies supports the claim that the regeneration starts with no accepted exclusions and rebuilds candidates using each attempt's recorded prerequisites. Inspection of `verify_all.py` and its verification dependencies confirms that required candidates are reconstructed from those prerequisites, omitted candidates require checked certificates, root coverage is checked, and an exclusion enters the final induction only after its prerequisites do. The retained chain is acyclic and agrees with the retained verifier output for all 136 normals; all 64 files marked as supplied in the code-hash manifest match. These are consistency checks on retained evidence, not independent verification of the omitted artifacts.

The focused computations performed during this review gave the following results:

- Re-enumeration through $h=3$ reproduced all 7,776 normals and their complete slices. An independent partition-counting calculation with Möbius inversion matched the tuple and primitive-tuple counters for every $h=1,\ldots,12$.
- For the 20-pattern normal $a=(1,3,3,4,6,8,8,10,11)$ with $h=2$, the support engine and independent audit agreed on 215,579 spanning supports, 54,875 orbits, 48,814 diagonal exclusions, and the complete set of 6,061 remaining cone cases. This checks support coverage for that normal, not its subsequent cone certificates.
- The supplied complete stage-D example passed `verify_all.py`: 2,145 reconstructed candidates, 107 required duals, and one verified Farkas leaf exclude normal 33 without prerequisites.

I did not reproduce the full catalogue or the full multi-hour certificate computation. An attempted rerun of the row-class generator stopped because OR-Tools is unavailable in the review environment. The remaining computational assessment therefore rests on the inspected programs, the supplied run evidence, and the explicit regeneration procedure. The manuscript accurately discloses the absence of most certificate artifacts and the cost of regenerating them. The supplied source makes the essential evidence reproducible; the lightweight checks should not be described as independent reproduction of Theorem 1 in its entirety.

The novelty is sufficient. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, [3] supplies the finite-basis and exchange methods but settles different parameter rays; [4] identifies this pair as unresolved; and [5] excludes only a fixed-point-free automorphism of order three. The present unrestricted exclusion goes materially beyond those results, with appropriate attribution. The live Brouwer–Hobart table [2] still marks the pair open. A focused arXiv search and comparison with the orbit-matrix work [6] did not reveal an earlier unrestricted resolution. The contribution is the completed exclusion at these parameters, rather than a claim to originate the underlying representation or basis-exchange ideas.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text explains the research question, reduction, necessary conditions, difficult zero-coordinate case, and dependency induction. Its computational tables support this argument rather than substitute for it. The abstract is informative, and the reproducibility limitations are explicit. The following minor improvements would make the verification claims more precise:

- In Section 6.6, distinguish prerequisites accepted before an attempt **starts** from all exclusions accepted before that attempt finishes. Concurrent attempts do not generally have the latter dependency list. The supplied lists satisfy Lemma 6.1, so this wording does not affect the proof.
- Clarify that the `abc=` option checks coverage and consumes the separate stage-B/C verifier summaries. It does not itself replay those certificates; the earlier verification commands remain necessary.
- A persistent public archive of the complete certificates, linked to the supplied hashes, would substantially reduce the cost of future independent verification.

[6] M. Maksimović and S. Rukavina, *Construction of directed strongly regular graphs with nontrivial automorphisms*, arXiv:2412.10901v2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10901. This develops orbit-matrix construction and symmetry-restricted classification, with its principal application at order 22.
