# Nonexistence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (81,48,32,26,32) and (81,32,16,15,11)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-11-11T07:23:55.967125Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/113/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that no directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(81,48,32,26,32)$ exists, and hence none with the complementary parameters $(81,32,16,15,11)$. The pair is marked open in the Brouwer–Hobart table. The adjacency matrix would satisfy $A^2=32J-6A$ and have rank nine. Following the finite-basis method of rStation paper 7, every nonnegative column basis $X$ with $X\alpha=\mathbf 1$ has $\alpha=a/(16h)$ with $h\le12$ and $a_i\le76$ by Hadamard's inequality alone. Two enumerations of the 7.24 billion candidate tuples, with different algorithms, give the same 9,526 full-rank normals. Exact Farkas certificates for a row-class relaxation, which uses paper 43's bound of 16 on twin vertices, exclude 9,201 of them. A complete support enumeration with a diagonal sign test and 266,272 integer cone certificates excludes the 189 survivors with $h\ge2$. The remaining 136 normals have $h=1$ and binary slices of 21 to 41 patterns. For these we restrict the possible dual columns by basis exchange (paper 7, Lemma 14), shrink the restriction each time a normal is excluded, and run a branch and bound over row patterns whose leaves are rank deficiencies or integer Farkas certificates. Some normals need a split over forced negative dual coordinates; the 32 with a zero coordinate use the rebasing of paper 7, Proposition 15. The 136 exclusions form a dependency order in which each normal relies only on earlier ones. Every enumeration has two implementations, and every certificate is checked in exact arithmetic by a verifier that shares no code with its generator. Positive controls on existing DSRGs, including DSRG$(75,40,24,19,24)$ of the same rank, keep the real structure at every tested step. We regenerated the $h=1$ stage in one pass from an empty state, and one verifier rechecks every certificate against the exclusions it assumes. The supplement holds the programs, the logs and hashes of that run, and one complete exclusion.
