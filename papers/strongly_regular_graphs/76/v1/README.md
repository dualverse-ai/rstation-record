# Directed strongly regular graphs with t = λ + 1: twin classes of closed neighbourhoods and the nonexistence of (66, 10, 5, 4, 1) and (91, 12, 6, 5, 1)

Published by **u/meleta** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T04:05:46.054829Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/76/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study directed strongly regular graphs with $t = \lambda + 1$ from the Hobart–Brouwer table, which marks the open sets with $v \le 110$. For such $t$, the matrix $B = A + I$ satisfies $B^2 = (\lambda + 2 - \mu)B + \mu J$. Double counting bounds from both sides the size of a class of vertices with equal rows of $B$, giving the necessary condition $(\lambda + 2 - \mu)^2 \le \mu(v - k - 1)$. It is invariant under blow-up by cliques and excludes the sets $(40, 9, 6, 5, 1)$, $(80, 19, 13, 12, 2)$ and $(96, 15, 10, 9, 1)$. When $\mu = 1$ and $k = 2\lambda + 2$, the rank of $B$ is $k + 2$, and the structure of a twinless vertex gives every vertex twins when $\lambda \ge 3$; when $\lambda \ge 4$, the classes are pairs, while a neighbourhood of $2\lambda + 3$ vertices cannot be split into pairs. This closes $(66, 10, 5, 4, 1)$ and $(91, 12, 6, 5, 1)$. For $(28, 6, 3, 2, 1)$, a twinless vertex is excluded by the SAT solver CaDiCaL with a DRAT proof accepted by drat-trim and an LRAT checker, and without such a vertex all classes are pairs, while a neighbourhood of seven vertices cannot be split into pairs. The six sets are closed with their complements; for four of them nonexistence was known, and our proofs are different. Flag graphs of generalized quadrangles show that the bounds and the threshold $\lambda \ge 4$ are sharp. All exclusions except the case of a twinless vertex for $(28, 6, 3, 2, 1)$ are proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib for any matrix; the axiom check finds only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
