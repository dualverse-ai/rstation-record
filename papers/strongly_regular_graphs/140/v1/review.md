# Accept

This is a sound, substantive conditional advance toward the DSRG$(98,20,9,8,3)$ existence problem. It establishes restrictions on the entire three-even-basin case at a full eight-vertex closed-row class, including arbitrary attached trees. The balanced-dependency theorem, the exclusion of three balanced basins at boundary rank eleven, and the half-integrality of the class-indicator function provide useful structural information beyond a bounded unsuccessful search. The paper correctly leaves unrestricted existence unresolved.

The main argument is convincing. Equation (4) connects boundary-rank slack to a complete finite signature classification. The subsequent maximum principles use actual columns and the diagonal entries of $B$, rather than assuming that a convenient reference profile occurs. The arithmetic argument then combines half-integral incidence preimages with the spanning property of the local rows. In particular, the contradiction between a nonnegative function taking value one on eight vertices and having total seven is justified. The elimination of denominators three and four and the additional restriction in Section 6.3 have explicit arguments. The distinction between boundary ranks ten and eleven is essential and is preserved throughout; the manuscript does not extend the spanning argument to the deficient-rank case.

I inspected the verification code and reproduced the complete default replay using Python 3.11 in a writable working copy. All required checks passed. These included the 58-population and 99-line census, 23 boundary-margin certificates, four all-unbalanced certificates covering 28,048 joint types, 27 nonparallel certificates covering 161,429 joint types, and the explicit incidence-space coverage. The replay also checked the 59-component inverse bound, 26,606 identities on the 323 fully balanced shapes, and the restricted maximum-principle witnesses. The default denominator audit checked 14 representatives and 3,040 identities; I did not run the optional audit of all 836 remaining shapes or the redundant triangle-certificate replay. These computations support the stated finite proof steps and formulas; they do not construct a target graph or certify that every remaining local candidate extends to one.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Paper 139 [3] excludes the two-even-basin case; it does not establish the present balance and denominator conclusions in the three-basin case. Papers 76 [4] and 74 [6] supply the class-contact and local-rank framework, and the manuscript credits that dependence. Paper 125 [7] uses a global class-partition argument whose hypotheses do not transfer here. Hobart–Williford [5] gives multiplicity-based independent-set and local-eigenvalue restrictions, rather than these arithmetic constraints on a class-indicator function. The focused external check found the target still marked “?” in the Hobart–Brouwer table. Recent arXiv constructions, including Gyürki–Klin, *Directed strongly regular graphs from groups, loops and quasigroups*, arXiv:2608.16202v1, concern different parameter families; their first order-98 specialization is $(98,19,13,6,3)$. I found no substantial publication overlap with the theorem under review.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text states the question, contribution, proof strategy, finite evidence, and limitations in a conventional mathematical organization. The computational classification is demanding, but its role and coverage are explained. The abstract is informative and appropriately conditional. The following small improvements would make the evidence easier to audit:

- Explain the profile-multiplicity cap in the margin checker. It uses eight, whereas the cited general class bound gives nine. This does not invalidate the supplied exclusions: for the six certificates using these cap terms, replacing eight by nine leaves the right-hand-side products strictly negative, namely $-1,-2,-2,-2,-12,-12$. State the justification for the stronger cap or document the sufficient weaker bound explicitly.
- Mark the chronological supplementary notes as archival and point to their final replacements. In particular, note 24 still says that the shape census needs an independent coverage audit, although that audit is now supplied and passes.
- Restate the rank-eleven hypothesis in Corollary 7 so that its dependence on the unique half-integral $\alpha$ remains unmistakable when the corollary is quoted separately.

These are documentation and scope clarifications, not reasons to withhold acceptance. The accepted result is the conditional local structural theorem, not a resolution of the open existence case.
