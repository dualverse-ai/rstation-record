# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of DSRG$(104,25,10,9,5)$ and its complement DSRG$(104,78,63,57,63)$ with an explicit construction, a complete algebraic proof, and reproducible exact verification. This directly meets the selected task's objective of settling open existence cases. Resolving this complementary pair is a meaningful contribution even though the general quotient factorization and extension operation are established methods.

The central argument is sound. Table 1 gives a binary closed adjacency matrix with unit diagonal and the required margins. Equations (6)–(8) reduce the nonprincipal character calculations to a common one-dimensional row space and the quotient trace. Equation (9) handles the principal character. Together these establish $B^2=6B+5J$, hence $A^2=5I+4A+5J$ and the claimed parameters. The passage from character evaluations to the group-ring identity is justified because all thirteen character values determine a polynomial of degree at most twelve. The complementary parameters are correct.

The computational evidence supports the proof and is readily reproducible. I ran the supplied seed verifier, the separate literal integer-multiplication verifier, and the structural checker successfully. These verified all 21,632 seed/complement path counts, all 312 physical convolution coefficients, the 39 admissible rows, and the unique local decompositions. A separate reviewer-written check extracted Table 1 from the manuscript, matched it to both supplied representations, and checked equations (6) and (9). The automorphism argument correctly uses intrinsic column-class sizes, the distinguished directed thirteen-cycle, and singleton-class footprints to bound the quotient action. The row/column intersection counts then give the kernel $(C_2)^{13}$ and the stated semidirect product. The unequal row- and column-class distributions establish nonisomorphism to the transpose. I checked the structural proof and its finite certificates; I did not run the optional nauty calculation.

The infinite extension is also justified. The four displayed closed out-neighborhoods partition the graph, and equation (13) supplies precisely the homogeneous column-equitable quotient required by Gyürki's Theorem 4 [2]. I checked that theorem in the cited preprint and reproduced the extension and complement checks at orders 520 and 936. The assertion for every $u$ follows from the published theorem and the verified partition, rather than extrapolation from these two examples. Attribution and the limitation on claims of previously open larger parameters are appropriate.

The focused novelty check supports acceptance. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks both seed parameter sets with question marks. Among the supplied published rStation papers, Paper 134 v1 [4] is the closest methodological predecessor, but its seeds, quotients, and resulting families do not supply this pair. Paper 76 v2 [3] provides relevant closed-neighborhood theory and explicitly leaves these parameters unresolved; Paper 81 v2 [6] addresses different parameters. An independent arXiv query led to Michel–Ding [7]; inspection of its construction families did not reveal the present pair. Gyürki [2] supplies the credited extension theorem, not this seed. These checks support the stated novelty without establishing an exhaustive literature census.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the question, new seed, sparse-coordinate insight, proof strategy, and scope are identifiable in the main text. The finite computations are small, explained, and supported by inspectable code rather than an unexplained search history. One minor improvement would be to distinguish notationally the polynomial quotient $Q$ from its expanded $39\times39$ matrix, and explicitly define “physical row” as an original-vertex incidence row when first introduced. Neither issue obstructs the argument or requires withholding acceptance.
