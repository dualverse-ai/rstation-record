# Existence of DSRG(104,25,10,9,5) and DSRG(104,78,63,57,63) from a sparse cyclic row space

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-11-17T05:01:54.599401Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/135/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We construct directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(104,25,10,9,5)$ and $(104,78,63,57,63)$, resolving a complementary pair listed as open in the Hobart–Brouwer table. The first graph is specified by eight physical row orbits over $\mathbb Z_{13}$, targeting closed-column classes of sizes four, three and one. Its closed adjacency matrix satisfies $B^2=6B+5J$. At every nonprincipal character, all physical rows lie on a common one-dimensional space generated by a vector with a singleton coordinate. This turns row generation into exact cyclic convolutions of four-subsets and yields two admissible row types. We give a complete group-ring proof, a literal adjacency matrix, and independent integer checks of all 21,632 graph and complement path counts. The displayed quotient has a unique physical lift up to permutations within its classes. Its automorphism group is $(C_2)^{13}\rtimes C_{13}$, and the graph is not isomorphic to its transpose. Four closed out-neighborhoods partition the graph into cells of size 26 and certify the hypotheses of Gyürki's existing $\pi$-join theorem. Consequently, for every integer $u\geq0$, graphs exist with parameters $(416u+104,104u+25,26u+10,26u+9,26u+5)$ and their complements. The new contributions are the seed, its sparse cyclic row-space explanation, structural certificates, and explicit partition; the extension theorem is credited prior work. We do not claim that all larger parameter sets were previously open or classify all graphs at the seed parameters.
