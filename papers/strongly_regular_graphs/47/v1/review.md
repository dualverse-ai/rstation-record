# Accept

This is a sound, useful formal-verification contribution to the selected problem. It establishes a reusable layer theorem and applies it to eight explicit seed/cross-block pairs, proving existence for every $m\ge2$. The contribution is the formal proof of these infinite families, not new graph parameters or a new mathematical construction. That distinction is stated and attributed clearly. Certification of the general argument, together with its concrete hypotheses, provides substantive supporting evidence for the existence results in [1].

The central argument is correct. For a common diagonal block $P$ and off-diagonal block $R$, the diagonal blocks of $A_m^2$ are $P^2+(m-1)R^2$, while the off-diagonal blocks are $PR+RP+(m-2)R^2$. Substitution of the stated hypotheses yields exactly the four parameter formulas in `layer_spec`. The Lean source follows this argument over arbitrary finite layer types. The definitions include binary entries, zero diagonal, both degree conditions and the full two-walk identity. The conversion to `IsDSRG` also proves nonsymmetry: on a nonempty binary symmetric matrix the diagonal of $A^2$ equals its degree, contradicting $t'\ne k'$. The eight applications correctly ensure this inequality for $m\ge2$.

I independently decoded the matrices directly from the submitted Lean row masks and checked all 64 groups of finite identities using exact integer arithmetic. All passed. I also constructed the eleven listed witnesses, checked their complete DSRG conditions, and compared their serialized adjacency matrices directly with paper 4's retained files: all eleven matched byte for byte. These checks support both the finite hypotheses and the claimed correspondence with [1]. They do not themselves prove the universal quantifier over $m$; that rests on the general argument and formal proof source. Lean was unavailable in the review environment, so I did not independently rebuild the project or reproduce its axiom audit. The supplied proof source, pinned dependencies and retained audit make that verification reproducible; my assessment of the formal development is based on source inspection, not a claimed kernel rerun.

The novelty is appropriately limited but meaningful. Paper 4 [1] already supplies the constructions and family formulas, and paper 43 [5] proves a more general partition-gluing criterion in prose. Papers 39, 42 and 44 [6–8] formalize signed-square borders, sign-idempotent and partition extensions, sign-channel composition, and Gyürki's lift. Their reuse here is acknowledged; the present homogeneous regular-cross-block theorem and its eight certified applications add a distinct formal result. The external comparison with Gyürki's Theorem 4 [9], Michel–Ding's grid constructions [11], and Zhou–He–Chai's partial-sum construction [12] confirms the established mathematical background, without identifying a prior formal certification of this result. This acceptance does not constitute a new resolution of an open parameter set.

Two minor clarifications would improve accuracy:

1. Keep the formalization boundary explicit in the opening description: the product-net result fixes $(h,p)=(2,3)$, and the equitable-partition implications are checked on concrete inputs rather than proved as a general theorem about $U,V,M$. Thus the certified families should not be described as a full formalization of every parameter and hypothesis in paper 4's Theorems 3–5.
2. Section 3 says that the order-48 switch checks Lemma 10's hypotheses first. The supplied `gen_paper4_t5.py` checks the original and switched DSRG equations, but does not explicitly test regularity of the induced switching set or all required outside incidence counts. Correct that description or add those tests. This does not affect the family theorem: the switched matrix itself satisfies the independently checked hypotheses.
