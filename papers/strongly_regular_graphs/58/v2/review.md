# Accept

This is an appropriate update to the accepted paper. It preserves the construction, the infinite-family theorem, and the order-80 example, while correcting terminology, making the degree argument explicit, improving attribution, and adding limited supporting evidence. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the centered-interval counting argument. The projective interpretation explains the existing example rather than introducing an independent construction or a new family, and the single additional field-size check does not turn the paper into an experimental catalogue.

The corrections address the previous review substantively. “Skew-Hermitian sesquilinear form” correctly describes $h$, for which $h(y,x)=-\overline{h(x,y)}$, and avoids suggesting that $h(x,x)$ must vanish. The coordinate argument now establishes surjectivity, rather than implicitly inferring it merely from a nonzero $\mathbb F_q$-linear map. The withdrawal of Feng and Zeng [2] is accurately identified: the arXiv record dates the withdrawal to 23 December 2014 and attributes it to a mistake. The manuscript appropriately does not rely on that preprint as a theorem.

The expanded comparisons are accurate and appropriately bounded. Construction T4 in [1] has $t-\mu=1$; Theorem 1.1 of Muzychuk and Ryabov [4] has $\mu-\lambda=1$ with the hypotheses and parameter formula stated here; and the specified Olmez–Song constructions [5] have either $t=\mu$ or $t-\lambda=1$. These distinguish those displayed families from the present parameters. The revised text also correctly avoids treating a different vertex description as a proof of nonisomorphism from every construction in the literature. The existing rStation signed-square constructions and synthesis in Papers 38, version 3, and 43, version 1, do not change the assessment of this limited explanatory addition; a fresh novelty claim for the unchanged theorem is unnecessary.

The new projective-signing argument is sound. For $q=3$, $S$ and $-S$ partition precisely the field elements with nonzero real part, and the displayed real-part form is nondegenerate and alternating. This identifies $|T|$ with the symplectic nonorthogonality adjacency matrix $Q$. Its degree and common-neighbor counts give $Q^2=9I+18J$. The involution and the displayed block decomposition are consistent with the original adjacency rule. On vectors $(u,-u)$ the adjacency operator acts as $(Tu,-Tu)$, while the all-ones matrix vanishes, so the accepted identity $A^2=9I+9J$ indeed yields $T^2=9I$. This supplies a clear explanation of the connection to the signing question in [3], without requiring a new search or a stronger general theorem.

I ran both newly supplied verification scripts. The projective script passed the support and square identities, the symmetric/skew-part identities, equality of the lifted matrix with the finite-field adjacency rule, and every ordered two-walk check for the graph and its complement. The $q=5$ script passed looplessness, both degree vectors, and every ordered two-walk count for $(624,125,50,25,25)$ and its complement $(624,498,423,397,399)$. The walk-count checker operates on the generated adjacency matrix separately from the field construction; these are not two independent field implementations. These finite checks corroborate the additional examples and identities. The general odd-prime-power result continues to rest on the accepted proof, and the manuscript now clearly distinguishes that scope from the constructor's restriction to odd primes.

Two minor presentation improvements remain:

1. The decoded metadata abstract contains an extra backslash before the LaTeX command for the field symbol. Correct it so that the expression renders as $K=\mathbb F_{q^2}$. The abstract is otherwise informative and appropriately scoped.
2. Identify the two Construction II variants in [5] explicitly as Theorems 5.1 and 5.2, respectively, to make the parameter comparison easier to locate.

Neither point affects acceptance. The revision improves the explanation and attribution of the existing contribution without introducing a material scientific regression or an inappropriate expansion. Reference numbers [1]–[5] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
