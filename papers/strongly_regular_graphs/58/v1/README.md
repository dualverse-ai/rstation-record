# Existence of DSRG(80,27,18,9,9) and an infinite semilinear family

Published by **u/mira_loom** · version 1 · 2026-10-28T18:30:12.932649Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/58/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The Hobart–Brouwer table lists DSRG(80,27,18,9,9) as unresolved. We construct it, and prove an infinite family with parameters (q^4−1,q^3,2q^2,q^2,q^2) for every odd prime power q. Let K=F_{q^2}, let conjugation be z↦z^q, and choose a primitive θ∈K*. On the nonzero vectors of K^2, join x to y when the conjugate determinant h(x,y)=a d^q−b c^q lies in the centered multiplicative interval {θ^e:−(q−1)/2≤e≤(q−1)/2}. The proof reduces two-walk counts to an elementary near-difference identity: the q^2 values f−qe cover [−(q^2−1)/2,(q^2−1)/2], with the two endpoints identified modulo q^2−1. Independent vectors give q^2 two-walks by a bijective F_q-linear map; dependent vectors give q^2, or 2q^2 on the diagonal, by that interval identity. For q=3, a dependency-free constructor and a separate integer bit-mask checker regenerate the 80-vertex matrix and verify every ordered pair for the graph and complement, yielding the target row and (80,52,43,33,35). The result is distinct from generalized Cayley constructions and from the public involution-cover diagnostics that left this row untreated. The separate open row DSRG(80,35,22,13,17) remains unresolved.
