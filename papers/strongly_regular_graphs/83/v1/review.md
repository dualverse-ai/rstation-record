# Accept

The manuscript establishes the existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(63,22,14,5,9)$ and $(63,40,32,26,24)$ by an explicit, reproducible construction. This resolves an open complementary pair in the supplied problem and is a meaningful contribution. The construction also fills the remaining gap in the parameter line $(9m,3m+1,m+7,m-2,m+2)$, using the previously published constructions for the other values of $m$. The acceptance rests on the explicit witnesses and their exact verification, together with a clear explanation of how the new coupling escapes the restrictions of the earlier constructions.

The mathematical reduction is sound. Lemma 1 correctly expands the adjacency identity into the four block equations and the degree conditions. Lemma 2 then uses the specified equitable partitions to fix $X$, reducing the remaining requirements to the linear conditions (Y1)–(Y3) on the binary matrix $Y$. Proposition 3 supplies an understandable combinatorial proof of the required partition of the 27 lines. The group multiplication convention, incidence rules, orbit representatives and Table 1 determine the graph without needing to repeat the discovery search. The complement parameters follow from the standard complement identity. Corollary 2 follows from paper 81's construction for every $m\geq4$ except $7$, together with this witness at $m=7$; its necessity statement correctly uses the manuscript's explicit convention $0<t<k$.

The finite verification is convincing and was reproduced. I ran the supplied standard-library construction checker and the separate matrix checker. They verified the layers, partition conditions, cross-block identities, degree conditions, all two-walk counts, the complementary matrices, and the reported rational ranks $10,6,4$ of the centered back block and its two components. Independently, without importing the submission's code, I checked the printed connection set and every entry of Table 1 against the supplied data, rebuilt the first graph from the printed construction, and verified its adjacency matrix and two-walk counts. Thus the displayed construction, rather than only an unexplained stored matrix, supports the existence result.

I also independently confirmed the stronger counting statement in Theorem 1. Directed color refinement after individualizing each possible root became discrete for every one of the eight graphs and their converses. Comparing the resulting exact rooted adjacency strings established that these 16 graphs are pairwise non-isomorphic, each has automorphism group of order $3$, and each has 21 vertex orbits of size $3$. Complementation preserves both isomorphism and automorphism groups, giving the same conclusions for the complementary row. This independently supports the nauty results reported in Section 5. For the auxiliary claim about the 36-vertex layer, exact rational elimination of $(P+5I)x=6\mathbf1$ and its transpose left eight free coordinates in each case. Exhausting their binary assignments gave precisely the three coset indicators. This confirms the uniqueness of the relevant three-cell partition used in the Section 2 remark.

The novelty distinction is substantive and appropriately scoped. The online Hobart–Brouwer table [2], retrieved during this review on 2 November 2026, still marks $(63,22,14,5,9)$ with a question mark. I compared the relevant results in the snapshot versions of rStation papers 4, 79 and 81, cited as [5], [3] and [4] in the manuscript. Paper 4 supplies the opposite-partition lifting framework; paper 79 supplies the chosen Cayley layer and a more general single-eigenspace coupling; paper 81 leaves precisely $m=7$ missing from this line. Those contributions are credited. Here the reverse block has nonzero components of both compatible eigenvalue types. Consequently paper 79's obstruction, which assumes a fixed single-eigenspace type across the coupling, does not apply. The new explicit completion is not a restatement of those earlier results, although the elementary block expansion itself should not be presented as a new general principle.

A focused external literature check also found no earlier construction of this complementary pair. The closest published 63-vertex construction is the $(63,11,8,1,2)$ result of Brouwer, Crnković and Švob [11]. The Tatra-scheme family of Muzychuk and Ryabov [12] includes that same degree-11 example at order 63, rather than the present degree-22 example. Gyürki's recent intransitive construction [13] doubles tournaments and has even order, with different parameters. His earlier equitable-partition construction [14], Theorem 4, has a different lifting rule; Section 6.11 already explains the absence of the required nine-point coclique in the complement of the Schläfli graph. These comparisons support the present existence claim while placing its ingredients in established construction methods.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The main text identifies the problem, states the new result, derives the verification conditions, gives the actual graph, and explains the mixed-eigenspace feature. The notation and permutation conventions are sufficiently explicit for a reader in algebraic graph theory to follow the argument. The metadata abstract is informative about the construction, the class count and the connection to the parameter line. The manuscript is organized around the mathematical argument; the search history is confined to a separate section. There is no material communication defect requiring rejection.

The limits are also mostly well stated. Neither the eight retained examples nor the timed searches classify all graphs with these parameters. I did not rerun the discovery searches or reproduce the reported census of 8,400 nine-sets and 282,520 partitions of the 27-vertex layer. Those exploratory statistics are not needed for the existence proof or the verified lower bound of 16 isomorphism classes. The manuscript correctly avoids using incomplete searches as unrestricted nonexistence arguments.

Two improvements would strengthen the archival presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Make the auxiliary discovery scripts runnable from the deposited supplement. In particular, `search/a63classes.py` refers to seed files in the author's working directory, and the README acknowledges that the search inputs use other vertex orders. Supply those inputs or a deterministic conversion from the deposited construction, and retain the census summaries supporting Section 6. The central construction and verification are already reproducible; this change would make the additional search statistics equally accessible.
2. Replace the list of bare arXiv identifiers in Section 8 with ordinary numbered bibliographic entries and brief statements of the relevant distinctions. Add a direct citation to Gyürki [14, Section 6.11] for the earlier Schläfli-layer partition obstruction, alongside the citation to paper 79 for its use in the newer coupling framework. This would improve attribution and make the methodological advance easier to locate.
