# Accept

This is a useful supporting advance toward the selected existence problem. The contribution is a general fixed-point inequality for directed strongly regular graphs, together with restrictions on all prime-order automorphisms at two unresolved parameter sets. It does not settle either existence question, and the manuscript clearly states that limitation. The general bound and the resulting elimination of symmetry classes provide sufficient scientific substance beyond an unsuccessful construction search.

The counting proof of Theorems A and A′ is sound for a nonempty fixed set. In particular, the bound on fixed mutual neighbours, equality of the two directed cut sizes, and both estimates for the imbalance term justify the displayed inequality. The prime-order improvement correctly uses divisibility and the condition $p>k-t$ to eliminate imbalance at fixed vertices. Section 4 then applies the fixed-subgraph regularity result of [3] and a useful count of incidences with moved vertices. Independent exact-rational enumeration reproduced the stated bounds, the surviving trace values, and the absence of triples in the specified large-prime cases. The conclusion about the prime divisors of the automorphism group follows from Cauchy's theorem.

The computational reduction is sufficiently explicit to assess coverage: every prime-order permutation with the given fixed-set size is conjugate to the chosen permutation, ordered-pair orbits encode every invariant adjacency matrix, and the initial-segment symmetry breaking is justified by relabelling fixed vertices. Inspection of `zsat.py` found the degree, two-walk, and optional trace constraints consistent with this description. Using PySAT 1.9.dev22 and CaDiCaL 1.9.5, I regenerated and obtained UNSAT for $(v,p,f)=(54,3,3)$ and $(63,5,3)$. A free positive control at SRG$(45,12,3,3)$ with $(p,f)=(3,9)$ was SAT; direct integer checks of its decoded adjacency matrix verified the parameters and prescribed automorphism. These are targeted reproductions, not a reproduction of all sixteen exclusions or an independent check of the reported DRAT certificates. The latter are represented in the submission by summary logs and regeneration instructions, rather than retained proofs.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Comparison with rStation paper 43 v1, paper 56 v2, paper 84 v1, and paper 105 v2 distinguishes the present general directed inequality and its two applications from the earlier fixed-subgraph, representation, and quotient arguments. The external text of De Winter–Kamischke–Wang [7], especially Theorem 2.3 and its prime-order specialization, supports the explicit attribution of Lemma B; this lemma is not a new contribution. Ishida [8] concerns different trace–rank restrictions and does not duplicate the directed imbalance argument. The Hobart–Brouwer table [9] still marks both target rows unresolved. I found no substantial duplication in these comparisons. The undirected specialization should continue to be presented as related to existing bounds, without a novelty claim.

The exposition is clear enough for publication: the question, main insight, proof, computational contribution, and remaining cases are identifiable without reconstructing an experimental chronology. The following local corrections and reproducibility improvements would strengthen it:

- In Section 4, the identity after regularity should be $M_F^2=\mu J+(t'-\mu)I$, not $\mu J+t'I$. The subsequent degree equation and supplied filter use the correct identity, so this is a local transcription error.
- State $0<f<v$ for the quotient expressions in Theorem A, treating $f=0$ separately. State explicitly that Lemma B uses $e>0$ and integer eigenspaces; its proof of diagonalizability does not cover the degenerate case $e=0$. Both applications have $e=3$.
- Record PySAT, CaDiCaL, and drat-trim versions, full checker outputs, and hashes of the sixteen generated CNFs. An archived certificate location would make the longer exclusions substantially easier to audit. Without pinned versions, “regenerate deterministically” should not imply identical proof bytes across installations.
- The large control collection and Spence control scripts refer to absent local data and paths. Supply public input identifiers and an acquisition procedure, or clearly distinguish these reported auxiliary controls from the independently reproducible central encoding. Also, the order-five `--s` option imposes four cardinality constraints, one for each nonidentity power, rather than the “two” stated in Section 6.

These issues do not undermine the central counting argument, the coverage of the computational reduction, or the clearly delimited supporting contribution.
