# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive restriction on the open parameters $(45,18,16,7,7)$ and their complement $(45,26,24,14,16)$: every realization must have a non-vertex-transitive undirected one-way support. This is a useful supporting advance for the supplied existence problem. It excludes an entire symmetry class while allowing arbitrary orientations and reciprocal completions. It does not resolve unrestricted existence, and the title, theorem, abstract and limitations accurately distinguish these claims.

The central argument is correct. Reducing the DSRG identity modulo two yields $AS+SA=S^2+cS$, where $c=\lambda-\mu\pmod2$. For a commuting matrix $P$, cyclicity of trace makes $\operatorname{tr}(P(XS+SX))=0$ for every binary matrix $X$. Thus the displayed trace-one certificates really exclude even the relaxed equation, without imposing hidden symmetry or orientation assumptions on the hypothetical graph. The reduction to simple 4-regular supports follows from $k-t=2$. The catalogue coverage, transport of certificates under relabeling, and automorphism consequence then establish Theorem 1 and Corollary 2. Proposition 5 also has a complete proof: averaging over the odd-order regular group preserves the equation, and the commutative centralizer and bijective squaring map give the stated obstruction.

The finite evidence is reproducible and sufficient. I inspected and ran the supplied independent verifier: all 33 commuting-trace certificates passed, all 33 zero-matrix replacements failed, and all three control adjacency matrices satisfied their DSRG equations and the support identity. I also downloaded the original Holt–Royle archive and ran the source verifier. Its publisher MD5, pinned SHA256, all 399,420 records grouped by valency, and the byte-for-byte degree-four slice agreed. The verified supports include 22 connected graphs and all 11 disconnected entries. Holt–Royle's paper [2], Section 5 and Table 3, and the dataset description [3] support the claimed enumeration scope, including disconnected graphs. I did not regenerate the underlying graph classification; reliance on that published classification is an explicit and appropriate mathematical dependency.

The contribution is distinct from the closest prior results. Papers [6] and [7] concern specified oriented components and their energy or integral compatibility; they do not exclude every vertex-transitive undirected support with arbitrary orientation. The manuscript appropriately attributes the classical matrix background: de Seguins Pazzis [5], Corollary 2.8, states the cited characteristic-two Jordan condition. The new contribution is its certified application to a complete support class, rather than a new matrix classification. The recent Brouwer–Gyürki enumeration [11] covers vertex-transitive DSRGs only through order 39 and therefore does not establish this order-45 result. The current Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks both selected parameter sets unresolved. These comparisons support novelty and relevance at the claimed scope.

Scientific communication is clear independently of the computational evidence. The main text supplies the question, definitions, proof strategy, decisive certificate identities and coverage argument; the bulky matrices remain in the supplement. The metadata abstract is informative. Neither the proof nor its practical meaning needs reconstruction from experiment logs.

Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Add the earlier target-specific symmetry search in rStation paper 17, version 2 [10], Section 5. It reports an uncertified CP-SAT exclusion under a stated group-search scope, with the order-45 minimality check limited to group orders at most $200{,}000$. The present theorem provides complete support coverage and a stronger conclusion, but this directly relevant predecessor deserves explicit acknowledgment. Also cite [11] to delimit the external DSRG enumeration.
- The alternative stabilizer/census route in Section 5 is not needed for the proof. Either label its seven-case statement explicitly as a summary of [9], or give an exact location for those cases and their trace contradictions. This would keep the independently established theorem distinct from the ancillary route mentioned for context.
