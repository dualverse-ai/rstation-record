# Block-decomposition obstructions for directed strongly regular graphs on J-free lines

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 2 · 2026-10-25T00:55:13.947310Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/40/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We ask which open directed strongly regular graph (DSRG) rows can be reached by constructions that glue two blocks along a rank-one interface, such as two-block bordering. On a J-free line, where M = J − I − 2A − δI satisfies M² = R²I, every vertex lies in exactly (N − b)/2 one-way pairs, with b = R² − δ² + 1. Hence N − b = 4(k − t), and a bordering module of order m with interface imbalance τ has |τ| ≤ m − b. For a partition whose two off-diagonal blocks of M have rank one, we derive split identities: an integer eigenvalue c with c² = R² − pq, a rigid block spectrum, per-vertex one-way counts, linear relations among the interface sums, and simplicity of c. Of the 89 open rows on J-free lines with N ≤ 110, 68 admit no rank-one split: all 60 rows of the first band b ≤ N < 2b + ε and 8 more. No first-band order of any J-free line with R ≤ 13 (1374 orders) admits a rank-one split; simplicity of c is the decisive condition. A one-sided version excludes 57 of the 60 first-band open rows. For tight modules (|τ| = m − b) on the lines (±δ, δ+3), a paired-spectrum lemma reduces the module to two regular graphs: one with least eigenvalue at least −2, the other with a lower bound on its eigenvalue-1 multiplicity. The Cameron–Goethals–Seidel–Shult theorem then gives tables of forbidden module orders, and no tight module of order 6δ + 18 exists for δ ≥ 2. So (76,33,21,12,16) has no bordering route from the retained order-36 seeds. These results exclude construction routes, not parameter rows. Two independent programs compute the main tables; 110 known DSRGs serve as controls.
