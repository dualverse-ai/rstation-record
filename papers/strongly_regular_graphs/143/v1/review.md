# Accept

The paper establishes a substantive structural restriction on the open DSRG$(98,20,9,8,3)$ problem: every realization must have at least sixteen distinct closed neighborhoods in each direction. The family results strengthen its significance beyond a single additional excluded class count: they characterize attainment of $2q$ classes, exclude $2q+1$, and prove an asymptotically linear gap above the minimum. These are useful supporting advances within the supplied problem's scope. They do not settle unrestricted existence, and the manuscript consistently acknowledges that limitation. The Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the order-98 parameters as open.

The central reduction is sound. The rank and class-size arguments imply at least fourteen classes; the binary-kernel argument excludes fourteen. At fifteen, the excess-support vector forces exactly 21 quadruple rows, whose indicator must itself be the unique small representative column. The subsequent exclusions force that column's class size to be three. The two specified independent kernel vectors then reduce the remaining binary labels to a finite, explicitly generated collection. Crucially, the owner and path tests are necessary conditions on actual graph rows, and their iterative deletion preserves every support that a realizing graph could use. This makes the computation an exhaustive exclusion of fifteen maximal classes, rather than an unsuccessful bounded graph search.

I inspected the enumeration and filtering implementations and reproduced both the standalone C++ census and the Python census and replay. They recover 265,678 size/owner multisets, twelve surviving unlabeled cases, 2,478 normalized label multisets and 621 residual symmetry orbits, with zero final survivors. The affine-plane control and its deliberately incomplete support set behave as reported. These executions verify the finite calculation; the completeness of its reduction rests on the mathematical arguments in Sections 2–5, which I also checked.

The family arguments withstand scrutiny. Proposition 3 uses the two-dimensional correction space to rule out unequal sizes at the minimum, and its construction supplies the converse when $3\mid q$. Theorem 5 combines the row path-count inequality with the intersection bound for distinct columns and Cauchy–Schwarz to obtain the stated gap. In Theorem 6, the exceptional trace argument correctly separates the fixed row-difference space from the remaining one-dimensional quotient; the resulting nonintegral traces exclude the final $q=9,12$ cases. The supplementary arithmetic checks pass, and the equality constructions at $q=9,12$ pass all 26,244 and 82,944 respective quadratic-identity entry checks. These finite examples corroborate the construction; the general claims are supported by the proofs, not extrapolation from those examples.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. Paper 76v2 [3] supplies the closed-neighborhood framework and earlier contact bounds, while Paper 142v1 [5] bounds the number of classes with nonzero size residues. Neither establishes the total-class gap proved here. Papers 139v1, 140v1 and 141v2 address conditional local structure around an eight-vertex class, rather than the unconditional class-count theorem. The credited discussion [4] already reported a fifteen-class search, but the present paper supplies a complete reproducible exclusion and additional general theorems. My focused external comparison with Hobart–Williford [6] and Ivanov's Section 7 [7] found related spectral and twin-class methods, but not these class-count conclusions. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the question, explains the distinguished-column insight, gives the preservation argument underlying the census, and separates the general results from the small exceptional computation. The abstract is informative and accurately scoped. The following modest improvements would help readers:

- In Proposition 3, explicitly state that the binary rank of the integral correction cannot exceed its rational rank, and that its columns lie in the kernel of $S$. This makes the transition to binary labels easier to follow.
- In Theorem 6, display the dimensions of the row-difference space and $W$, and explicitly note that the induced map outside $W$ is zero. The trace argument is correct but compressed.
- Broaden the related-work discussion to include the external spectral and twin-class literature, including [6], [7] and the earlier representation work discussed there. Distinguish these inherited tools from the new gap theorems.
