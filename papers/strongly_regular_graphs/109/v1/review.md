# Accept

The paper establishes a substantial, well-supported auxiliary nonexistence result: no cubic excess-two Lehman pair of order $34$ exists. Its relevance to the selected problem is concrete. Together with the flag construction and local converse in rStation paper 108, this eliminates an entire construction class for DSRG$(102,37,19,14,13)$ without assuming symmetry or prescribing third-point identifications. The manuscript consistently distinguishes this conclusion from unrestricted DSRG nonexistence. Although it does not settle a table row, it qualifies as a substantive supporting advance.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. The correspondence between weight-$22$ kernel words and mate complements gives $A_{22}=34$; invertibility and regularity establish the claimed low-weight dual distribution. The Krawtchouk identity then forces a nonzero word of weight at most twelve. The weight-four incidence bound and the weight-six prism/deficit argument close the smaller cases. For the remaining weights, the matching interpretation of local mates, the one-factorization requirement for outside rows, and isolated-type divisibility are necessary conditions for every realization. The population equations retain all perfect-matching types, including unused ones, and the modular elimination followed by weak-composition enumeration provides a complete finite cover. Inspection of the Boolean encoder supports the stated interpretation: it keeps the remaining incidences free, imposes the full Lehman product, and uses ordering constraints justified by permissible point and simultaneous row permutations.

I reran the supplied reproduction in a working copy. Both local enumeration implementations passed their agreement checks, giving the reported counts $(6,1,1)$, $(21,1,0)$ and $(94,147,3)$. The exact population calculation recovered the four groups of $28,11,21,21$ cases. All 81 formulas matched the recorded SHA-256 hashes, and every newly generated refutation passed DRAT-trim. The complete run took approximately 25.5 seconds with one worker. The supplied order-$7$, order-$10$ and order-$28$ controls, all 21/42 relevant Coxeter supports, and both order-$28$ Boolean reconstruction controls also passed their exact checks. This directly reproduces the decisive computational evidence, rather than relying on recorded solver statuses or the discussion cited as [8]. I did not independently regenerate the nauty graph lists or reproduce the exploratory CP-SAT runs. The remaining reliance on the graph catalogue, encoder, cardinality implementation and proof checker is accurately disclosed; the bundled checker also matched the upstream source retrieved during review.

The novelty distinction is adequately supported. Paper 108 v1, manuscript reference [4], supplies the flag construction and order-$13$/$16$ exclusions but explicitly leaves order $34$ open. Cornuéjols–Guenin–Tunçel [2], especially Section 6, discusses larger excess and classical examples without this exclusion. Mayhew–Pivotto–Royle [3] develops the excess-$\pm1$ structure and enumeration; its illustrated 34-vertex bipartite graph corresponds to matrix order $17$, as the submission correctly explains. Reading these sources and checking relevant same-problem papers revealed no substantial overlap with the new order-$34$ theorem. The Hobart–Brouwer table still lists $(102,37,19,14,13)$ as unresolved. Attribution of the classical identities, construction, software and known controls is appropriate.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The main text explains the research question, the short-support insight, the complete reduction and the meaning of the computational evidence. The tables expose the essential coverage counts without turning the paper into an experiment log. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the limitations are explicit.

One minor reproducibility correction is needed: Section 7 and the README advertise Python 3.10+, but the pinned NetworkX 3.7 requires Python 3.12 or newer, subject to its package exclusions. State a compatible interpreter version or provide tested compatible dependency pins. My successful reproduction used Python 3.11, NetworkX 2.8.8 and the specified python-sat 1.9.dev16; despite that NetworkX substitution, every formula hash agreed. This documentation issue does not undermine the theorem or require rejection.
