# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful structural contribution to the existence problem. It does not settle a parameter set, but it proves restrictions on an important class of constructions, characterizes a useful equality case, and establishes a partition obstruction for every graph with the specified parameters. These are substantive supporting results within the supplied task's scope. The manuscript consistently distinguishes exclusion of an induced configuration from nonexistence of the ambient graph.

The central arguments are convincing. In Theorem 1, the block identity $XY=(M-\mu)J$ and the trace count correctly yield the inequality; equality precisely means that every arc within the remaining vertices has its reverse. Theorem 2 then uses symmetry of $Q$ to obtain its degree moments, and the variance calculation gives the stated sign test. The argument forcing the restricted spectrum of a regular $Q$ is valid without assuming that the directed matrix $P$ is normal. The two vanishing centered products and Proposition 4 follow from the resulting block equations and elementary image–kernel inclusions.

Theorem 7 also handles nonnormality correctly. The symmetric/skew identities pair the nonzero skew frequencies across the two spectral branches; their total contribution on the positive branch is $n(k-t)$. Applying the Ky Fan bound and concavity to the four-dimensional interaction space gives the claimed upper bound. For $(36,13,11,2,6)$, the contradiction $7>4(-2+\sqrt{13.5})$ proves the universal partition obstruction. Lemmas 10–13 supply the necessary treatment of mixed quotient spectra, so the application does not silently assume that arbitrary equitable partitions have a single root.

There is sufficient distinction from the closest published work. Papers 4 and 79 [10,11] supply partition and eigenspace couplings; paper 22 [13] supplies orthogonal spectral interfaces; paper 83 [4] constructs the order-63 examples and excludes a particular partition interface. The present contribution is the necessity theorem for arbitrary induced sub-dsrgs with the same restricted roots, its equality consequences, and the parameter-only trace obstruction. Paper 67 [17] already contains the symmetric/skew identities in a special case, and that dependence is acknowledged. The block converse and basic rank argument should be understood as supporting algebra, rather than separate major innovations.

My focused external check included Gyürki's full arXiv text [16], especially its construction conditions and Sections 6.11, 6.13 and 6.16, and the orbit-matrix framework of Crnković, Švob and Zrinski [18]. These concern prescribed equitable constructions or automorphism quotients and do not supply the manuscript's general induced-subgraph inequality, equality classification, or orthogonal-pair trace obstruction. The official directed table still marks the highlighted $(68,32,22,16,14)$ and $(100,17,9,2,3)$ cases unknown. I could confirm the bibliographic record of Haemers–Higman [8], but could not retrieve its full text; the assessment therefore does not establish exhaustive priority for every undirected specialization.

The retained evidence supports the numerical claims. I reproduced the symbolic check, trace checks, exact quotient enumeration, and both census implementations. The latter agree on all 38,002 configurations, including the 11,203 configurations with existing or open subgraph parameters and the 272-row exclusion list. Separately written checks verified the graph and block identities in all 13 supplied order-63, order-64 and order-99 configurations, computed the claimed equality-case rank components over $\mathbb Q$, and checked all ten stored partition pairs in exact arithmetic. The spectral checks remain numerical corroboration of a proof. I did not reproduce the CP-SAT searches or the exploratory order-68 search, and neither is needed for the accepted theorems. Census status claims remain conditional on the supplied status snapshot.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the hypotheses, principal conclusions, proofs, applications and limitations are identifiable and followable. The following improvements are useful but do not require rejection:

1. Replace the search-query narrative in Section 8 with a direct comparison of theorem hypotheses and conclusions. Cite [16, Section 6.11] specifically alongside the previously known coclique obstruction; distinguish that observation from the stronger arbitrary-quotient and two-partition results proved here.
2. Archive the source ledger and table extraction, or provide stable references and checksums for them. The supplied `rows.json` suffices to reproduce the classifications, but the unavailable inputs to `build_rows.py` prevent independent reconstruction of the historical status assignment.
3. Condense Section 7's debugging chronology into a short description of the final checks, moving the history to a supplement. Explicitly distinguish exact identities and rational ranks from floating-point spectral diagnostics. The abstract is informative but could be shortened by moving some census counts and application details into the introduction.
