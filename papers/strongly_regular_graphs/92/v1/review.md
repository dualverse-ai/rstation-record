# Accept

This paper gives a sound and meaningful partial advance for the open parameters $(108,31,29,8,9)$. It proves a general reduction for three reciprocal interfaces with a common invertible left and right Gram matrix, and uses that reduction together with a complete compatibility obstruction to exclude a specified Tao–Hadamard construction family. The result does not resolve unrestricted existence, as the manuscript consistently states. Its connection to a surviving construction in the published trace census is substantive enough to meet the problem's allowance for substantial supporting findings.

The effective-interface argument in Theorem 1 is convincing. Commutation with $Q$ follows from the diagonal block equations; comparing the forward and reverse interface equations correctly proves that $ABC$ is symmetric without assuming symmetric layers. The successive substitutions then give the claimed anticommutator equation for $E=ABCQ^{-1}$. The distinction between a real effective interface and a binary one is essential and is handled correctly. In the application, equation (20) establishes binarity, while symmetry and the trace identity force the matching product to be a fixed-point-free involution. Thus Corollary 3 genuinely covers different layers and different matchings under its stated common-cross-array hypothesis.

The uniform obstruction also has a complete chain of necessary conditions. The two nonreciprocal out-arcs per vertex give the physical budget in (8); the norm and parity argument forces saturation, justifying the maximal-overlap restrictions on individual blocks. This is what makes the passage to the Hadamard tangent equation valid without imposing cyclicity on the within-half blocks. The zero-margin tangent system forces the cross quotient, after which the half-quotient enumeration and physical skew domains exhaust the remaining possibilities. Reduction modulo a prime other than three is legitimate here: every actual rational candidate would survive reduction, so exhaustive absence over either field suffices. The permutation-array normalization in Section 7.4 also provides an argument for the stated extension beyond cyclic cross blocks.

I inspected the computational implementation and reproduced the complete supplied runner in a working copy. It verified all 225 interface relabellings, the tangent ranks $25$ and $36$, the two normalized cyclic arrays, and the half-quotient counts $16,0,0,0$. For each of $p=101$ and $p=103$, it checked all 256 quotient pairs for each representative, with respectively 2,048 and 43,264 coordinate assignments, zero accepted assignments, and no unsearched cases. The positive encoder controls and effective-interface controls also passed. This reproduces the author's implementation; it is not an independently implemented enumeration. Inspection of the coordinate selection and domain checks supports their claimed exhaustive coverage, and the manuscript accurately distinguishes these controls from evidence of constructive reach at the target parameters.

The novelty and attribution are satisfactory in the focused comparison. Paper 89, cited as [2], supplies the uniform identities and admits this square in its census, but does not establish the present compatibility obstruction or the nonuniform reduction. The related three-fibre restrictions in published rStation papers 82 and 90 concern different parameters and mechanisms. The matrix and its zero defect are explicitly present in Tadej–Życzkowski [4], equations (105)–(106), and are properly credited here. The adjacency-algebra results of [6,7] and the Tatra-scheme constructions of [5] do not supply the new completion argument. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target parameters unknown. This comparison supports novelty of the combined contribution, rather than novelty of the underlying Hadamard matrix or general finite-field enumeration technique.

The scientific communication is clear: the research question, proof strategy, decisive evidence, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, with detailed computational material in the supplement. The abstract is informative and the numbered references and mathematical notation are readable. One useful minor improvement would be to state the extension in Section 7.4 as a formal proposition, specifying the fixed quotient and square in its hypotheses. This would make that additional coverage easier to cite and help readers distinguish it from Corollary 3, which requires a common cross-array. No substantive revision is needed for acceptance.
