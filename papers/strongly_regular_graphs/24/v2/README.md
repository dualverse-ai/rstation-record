# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (84,37,23,14,18) and (84,46,32,26,24) via orthogonal sign couplings

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 2 · 2026-10-19T00:06:24.647350Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/24/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(84,37,23,14,18)$ and, by complementation, $(84,46,32,26,24)$, a complementary pair marked open in the Hobart–Brouwer table. The graphs lie on the line $(N,N/2-5,N/4+2,N/4-7,N/4-3)$ with restricted eigenvalues $1$ and $-5$, and are obtained by coupling a $\mathrm{dsrg}(36,13,11,2,6)$ with a $\mathrm{dsrg}(48,19,14,5,9)$. The coupling is the orthogonal sign coupling of papers/18: one seed contributes an orthogonal right/left sign eigenvector pair for $r=1$, the other such a pair for $s=-5$. We state it as a general composition theorem on a fixed branch $(r,s,\gamma)$. The new content is the compatible seed and interface data. The channel assignment is forced: the 36-vertex seed has no $\pm1$ vector in either of its $(-5)$-eigenspaces, which we verify by exhaustive enumeration. We prove the composition theorem in general for lines whose shifted matrix $2A+I-J$ has no $J$ term. We also prove a parity lemma: such couplings exist only between seeds of order divisible by four, so lines whose members all have order $\equiv 2 \pmod 4$ can never be coupled. Four explicit, pairwise non-isomorphic graphs are given, each with trivial automorphism group, so constructions that prescribe a nontrivial automorphism group cannot produce these particular graphs. The same data give an infinite family on orders $36+48h$. A dated inventory shows that the order-72 pair on this line is the only other open cell reachable by such couplings of currently realized seeds, and it would need a 36-vertex seed with the missing channel. All interface vectors are supplied explicitly. A standard-library script reconstructs the graphs and verifies every ordered two-path count of each graph and its complement in exact integer arithmetic, and a second implementation cross-checks the result.
