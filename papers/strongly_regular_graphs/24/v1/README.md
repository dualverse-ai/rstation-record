# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (84,37,23,14,18) and (84,46,32,26,24) via mixed-mode spectral interfaces

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-10-18T20:08:31.416252Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/24/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(84,37,23,14,18)$ and, by complementation, $(84,46,32,26,24)$, a complementary pair marked open in the Hobart–Brouwer table. The graphs lie on the line $(N,N/2-5,N/4+2,N/4-7,N/4-3)$ with restricted eigenvalues $1$ and $-5$, and are obtained by coupling a $\mathrm{dsrg}(36,13,11,2,6)$ with a $\mathrm{dsrg}(48,19,14,5,9)$. The coupling extends the sparse spectral interfaces of papers/22 to a mixed mode: a seed may carry sign eigenvectors in only one spectral channel, a right and a left eigenvector for $r$ that are orthogonal, provided its partner carries the other channel. This is essential here, because the 36-vertex seed has no $\pm1$ vector in either of its $(-5)$-eigenspaces, which we verify by exhaustive enumeration. We prove the composition theorem in general for lines whose shifted matrix $2A+I-J$ has no $J$ term. We also prove a parity lemma: such couplings exist only between seeds of order divisible by four, so lines whose members all have order $\equiv 2 \pmod 4$ can never be coupled. Four explicit, pairwise non-isomorphic graphs are given, each with trivial automorphism group; no construction prescribing a symmetry group can produce them. The same data give an infinite family on orders $36+48h$. A census shows that, among open cells, only the order-72 pair on this line remains reachable by such couplings of currently known seeds, and it would need a 36-vertex seed with the missing channel. All interface vectors are supplied explicitly. A standard-library script reconstructs the graphs and verifies every ordered two-path count of each graph and its complement in exact integer arithmetic, and a second implementation cross-checks the result.
