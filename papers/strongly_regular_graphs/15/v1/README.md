# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (90,43,26,21,20) and (66,31,17,14,15), via partitioned seeds and Gyürki's lifting theorem

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-10-17T21:42:18.128660Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/15/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that directed strongly regular graphs exist with parameters $(90,43,26,21,20)$ and $(66,31,17,14,15)$. Their complements $(90,46,29,22,25)$ and $(66,34,20,17,18)$ then exist too; all four rows are marked "?" in the Hobart–Brouwer table. Both results apply Gyürki's equitable-partition lifting theorem (entry T20 of the table) to an existing seed digraph. The theorem needs a partition of the seed into $a$ equal parts in which every vertex has a prescribed number of in-neighbours in its own part and in each other part. The first seed is a vertex-transitive $\mathrm{dsrg}(30,13,11,6,5)$, obtained as a union of five orbitals of $\mathrm{TransitiveGroup}(30,27)$. It splits into two halves of size 15 with in-neighbour counts 8 and 5. This gives the family $(30(2u+1),\,30u+13,\,15u+11,\,15u+6,\,15u+5)$ for every $u\ge1$, and $u=1$ is the open pair. The second seed is the published $\mathrm{dsrg}(22,9,6,3,4)$ of Byzov and Pushkarev, which has automorphism group $C_3$. It admits two halves of size 11 with counts 5 and 4, and its first lift is $(66,31,17,14,15)$. We found these targets systematically with a recomputable status ledger of the table. The ledger screens every existing parameter set against the numerical conditions of T20 and of the opposite-partition lift from rStation paper 4. In each case the missing ingredient was a partition. A dependency-free verifier checks the seeds, the partition counts and every two-path count of both lifts and their complements. Matrix checks also cover the next members of the order-30 family. The ledger screen also identifies $(72,31,20,11,15)$ and $(108,40,22,16,14)$ as targets for the second lift. For the seeds we had, no suitable partition was found.
