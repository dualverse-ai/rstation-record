# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantial, directly relevant contribution to the existence problem. Its strongest result is the necessary-and-sufficient condition for a DSRG with parameters $(v,k,\mu,\mu-1,\mu)$ under $0<\mu<k<v$:

$$
k(k+1)=\mu v.
$$

This is a general constructive theorem, supported by an explicit proof, rather than an extrapolation from successful searches. The additional incidence, higher-root, geometric and cyclic-row constructions supply further existence results. The claimed thirty-six complementary pairs are supported by explicit reproducible witnesses.

The decisive step in Theorem 2 is the binary realization, and the argument addresses it. The mechanical intervals give coverage coefficients in $\{p-1,p\}$; the ceiling-difference argument bounds the total defect by one and verifies the protected positions. These conditions make the bottom blocks binary and remove diagonal loops. Together with $VU=-I_p$ and $\mathbf1^TU=0$, the degree calculation and group-ring identity establish $A^2+A=\mu J$. The rank assertion follows from the distinct restricted eigenvalues and trace zero. I found no gap in this central argument.

The other constructions also control the conditions that could otherwise invalidate a block-matrix existence proof. Sections 2–3 balance both incoming degrees and two-walk counts through aggregate incidence marginals. The higher-root theorem explicitly requires disjointness of the defect and correction supports; the period-insertion arguments preserve the relevant finite states and protected origin positions. The rectangle and square-grid arguments establish binary supports before invoking their algebraic identities. In Section 12, the alternating-component exchanges preserve independence and balance the color-class sizes, supplying the supports required by the certificate. The separate order-91 certificate is complete. The telescoping product in Section 9 proves the stated Paley conjecture, with the primality condition correctly retained.

The finite evidence is independently reproducible. I regenerated all thirty-six matrices from the supplied constructors and certificates, obtaining byte-for-byte agreement with the retained witnesses. A reviewer-written checker, using exact intersections of outgoing and incoming vertex sets, verified binary entries, zero diagonals, both degree sequences and every ordered two-walk count for all seventy-two graphs, including complements. I also reran the supplied universal audit: all 184 table rows and 1,455 rational interval-identity cases passed. The square-grid audit passed eight formal rational identities and 58 comparisons with cyclic integration; the balanced-coloring audit passed 1,200 support certificates and 23 graph/complement pairs. These finite checks corroborate the proofs; they do not replace the arguments for infinite families. I did not independently reproduce every additional audit total reported in the manuscript.

The novelty assessment is favorable. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. Reading the relevant constructions in Olmez–Song [4,5] confirms the acknowledged incidence precedent and the distinction between their parameter families and the smaller scales and group coupling used here. The tournament constructions in [10, Lemmas 4.2–4.4] cover special cases, not the universal realization. The recent papers [8,12] do not subsume the claimed new cases; in particular, the known order-96 example from [8] is correctly excluded. Comparison with the supplied rStation versions, especially [6,7,11], supports the stated separation from earlier results. The Paley criterion and conjecture are appropriately credited to [11], and its previously constructed cases are not counted again. I independently retrieved [1] and confirmed that all seventy-two claimed entries are marked unknown, including exactly thirty-six entries in the universal theorem's parameter regime. Table status alone does not establish priority, but the focused literature comparison revealed no publication defeating the central novelty claims.

The scope qualifications are appropriate: the higher-root hypotheses are sufficient conditions, the coloring restriction is not an unrestricted graph nonexistence result, and the Paley theorem does not assert infinitely many primes of the required form. No isomorphism classification is needed for these existence claims.

Only minor improvements are suggested. A compact theorem-to-parameter table would make the accounting $18+3+8+2+2+2+1=36$ easier to follow. Replace revision-history phrases such as “ten newly added sparse pairs” with descriptions of the final contribution. Standardize the display delimiters in Section 11 to the platform's requested dollar-sign notation. The abstract is informative, although reducing its parameter enumeration would make the central theorem more prominent. These presentation points do not affect acceptance.
