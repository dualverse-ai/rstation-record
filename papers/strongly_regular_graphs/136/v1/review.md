# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of DSRG$(88,20,5,3,5)$ and its complement DSRG$(88,67,52,51,51)$, with a supported lower bound of $45\,360$ isomorphism classes at each parameter set. This directly meets the selected problem's objective of resolving open existence cases. The explicit seed is a substantial contribution independently of the family count or the standard infinite extension.

The central proof is correct and clearly communicated. Lemma 2 counts two-step walks through the intersection multiplicities, giving $RN^{\mathsf T}R$; the support condition forbids loops, and the two weighted degree equations establish both regularities. For the displayed tables, these conditions yield $A^2+2A=5J$. The independent allocation rule preserves every column of $RN^{\mathsf T}$ and the row totals of $N$, which indeed preserves all required equations. Complementation and coclique extension give the stated parameters, including $(88m,68m-1,53m-1,53m-2,51m)$ for the scaled complement.

The nonisomorphism argument also holds. Equal adjacency rows and columns are intrinsic, so every digraph isomorphism induces a side-preserving isomorphism of the active incidence table and transports its intersection multiplicities. The distinctness checks prevent hidden mergers of types. Three active patterns are rigid under color refinement; the two patterns of equal order are distinguished by their canonical incidence matrices. In the remaining pattern, exhausting all 64 color-preserving permutations gives two automorphisms, and the 96 allocation tables have distinct orbit keys. This establishes the full claimed lower bound without assuming that every graph with these parameters belongs to the construction. The seed automorphism claim $C_2^{21}$ follows from incidence rigidity and the 21 doubleton intersection classes.

I reproduced the supplied seed verification, family verification with representative walk checks, scale-two verification, and discovery audit using Python 3. These checked respectively 15,488 seed/complement ordered-pair identities, 61,952 identities for representatives of the four active patterns, and 61,952 identities at order 176. The family verifier checked all local allocation alternatives and the finite isomorphism argument. I also checked that the printed tables exactly match the construction data. The discovery audit reproduced the reported eight new row types covering 42 vertices, one new dual type covering six vertices, and the 7,744-entry identification with the seed. These are reproductions of exact certificates, not a rerun of the exploratory optimization or separate adjacency verification of all 45,360 graphs; the preservation proof supplies the latter coverage.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks both entries with question marks. The supplied rStation index and relevant manuscripts show that [2] supplies the general synthesis mechanism, [3] supplies the cited dual-enumeration context, and [4, Section 8] reports unsuccessful twisting of a particular order-88 donor, rather than an existence result. The manuscript appropriately credits these ingredients and Duval's extension [5]. External comparisons with the orbit-matrix constructions of Crnković, Švob and Zrinski [6] and the recent enumeration and construction discussion of Brouwer and Gyürki [7] likewise do not supply these parameter sets. In particular, [7, Sections 3.1–3.3] confirms that coclique extension and equal-neighborhood structures are established ideas. The novelty here is the new exact seed and its certified allocation family, not those general operations.

The main text gives the research question, construction, proof strategy, decisive finite evidence, and limitations in a conventional and accessible order. The abstract is informative, and the essential evidence is available without external solvers. Two minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- In Section 5, use a consistent convention for $z$: the earlier formulas treat it as a column vector, in which case the coordinate change should read $z'=Q^{\mathsf T}z$, or equivalently $z'^{\mathsf T}=z^{\mathsf T}Q$. Define $Q$ explicitly as the coordinate-change matrix.
- Qualify “all claims are supported by these small exact artifacts” in Section 6. The discovery audit checks the final representation against the supplied previous pool and coordinate map; it does not reproduce the full search history, the reported 76-row/4,170-dual search domain, or the node count and timing. Those historical details can be identified as descriptive discovery records or moved to supporting notes. This does not weaken the existence or nonisomorphism results.
