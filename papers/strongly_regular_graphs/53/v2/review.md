# Accept

The revision adds a sound, substantive extension to the accepted paper. Theorem 5 explains the four known order-$42$ classes through a fixed Paley cross block, two small sign matrices, and exact recovery of the remaining blocks. Its contribution is the structural reduction and exhaustive classification for that prescribed cross. The manuscript appropriately credits the original graphs to Paper 52 [1] and preserves the scope of the previously accepted rigidity results.

The new algebraic argument is correct. Centrality of $U$ and $Nf(y)$ makes conjugation by $C$ equivalent to conjugation by $V=H-e$, and the displayed inverse supplies exact recovery. With $E=-CDC^{-1}$ and $B=(25e-D^2)C^{-1}$, associativity gives the remaining block square equations. The coefficient indexing separates the two nonzero translation classes into the stated $3\times3$ equations; the zero-translation coefficients and quotient restrictions account for the remaining positions. Consequently the enumeration covers every admissible diagonal block for the specified cross, rather than merely a selected collection of candidates.

I reran the supplied constructor on a working copy. It reproduced all claimed counts: $68$ legal pairs among $512$ sign matrices, $534$ compatible diagonal pairs, and exactly $12$ complete graphs. The exact inverse checks passed, as did the full adjacency and complement checks for every output. All twelve supplied vertex permutations verified, with three outputs mapping to each of the four published sources. I also reran the source-matching verifier using the snapshot's Paper 52 version 2 data through its offline option. It confirmed the published connection arrays, coordinate inversion, first-source normalization, and agreement with the stored skew certificates and accepted reconstruction outputs. Thus the new construction is tied to the actual published sources, not merely to internally supplied reference arrays.

The distinction from [1] is meaningful. That paper reports an incomplete solver enumeration and restrictions on bi-Cayley connection sets; it does not give the normalized Paley factorization or the complete small-matrix assembly established here. The elementary inverse and similarity manipulations are standard, as the revision acknowledges. The added references [9] and [10] also address the previous review's attribution requests, and the source-matching program addresses its reproducibility suggestion.

The completeness qualification remains essential: twelve is the number of labelled completions of this particular cross in the specified coordinates. It is not a classification of all order-$42$ bi-Cayley graphs, nor does it provide existence or unrestricted nonexistence at order $110$. The theorem, abstract, and limitations consistently maintain these boundaries. No new open-case resolution or change to a verified-solution record follows from this update.
