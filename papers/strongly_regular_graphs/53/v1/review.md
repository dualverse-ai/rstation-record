# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive supporting advance toward the open DSRG existence problem. Its principal contribution is a complete restriction on a proposed construction mechanism: for the stated prime half-order cases, any occurrence of $C_p:C_{(p-1)/2}$ must have two regular orbits. This justifies the bi-Cayley search space without assuming its orbit structure in advance. The tournament-block obstruction and the reconstruction certificates provide additional mathematical content. These results do not settle $(110,50,43,22,23)$, which remains marked open in the Hobart–Brouwer table [2] checked during this review. The paper consistently acknowledges that limitation, and substantial supporting findings are within the task's scope.

The general arguments are sound. In Theorem 1, the moved-cycle augmentation space is invariant, and the Frobenius-norm argument remains valid for a nonnormal matrix because it uses Schur triangularization rather than an unjustified orthogonal eigendecomposition. The cyclotomic trace bound excludes the remaining odd-dimensional constituent, leaving only zero, $p$, or $2p$ fixed vertices. The parity argument for $p$ fixed vertices, the neighbourhood-intersection contradiction for $2p$ fixed vertices with $p\ge11$, and the separate $p=7$ argument close all remaining cases.

Theorem 2 supplies a universal obstruction within its stated block algebra, rather than an inference from a failed finite search. The reduction modulo $h$ forces every degree of $H$ to be $h$, and the entry bounds and row sums justify the exact identity (15). The local alternatives in (17) exhaust the complementary supports. Together with the trace condition, they force $U$ to be a fixed-point-free symmetric permutation matrix. The resulting requirement that the complement of a perfect matching be bipartite contradicts its triangles for $m\ge6$. The stabilizer classification and fixed-neighbourhood residues then correctly eliminate the all-natural and mixed affine actions. The restriction to prime $h$ in the complete action classification is essential and is stated clearly.

The finite reconstruction theorem has adequate, inspectable evidence. I reran all three supplied verifiers on working copies. For each of the four sources, the reconstruction checker obtained rank $689$ on $693$ variables, verified four independent integer kernel directions, and found exactly two sign completions among the sixteen possible selected-coordinate sign vectors. Both completions passed the full adjacency identities and degree checks with the claimed parameters. The modular lower bound and rational kernel upper bound establish the real kernel dimension exactly; the selected-coordinate identity makes the sign enumeration exhaustive. I also independently reconstructed the first four published solutions of [1] from their connection sets and confirmed both the supplied skew matrices and the recovered degree-$18$ adjacency matrices under the stated coordinate inversion.

The prime arithmetic audits through $199$ and local audits at $m=6,10,14$ also passed. These are checks on the written proofs, not experimental evidence for their universal quantifiers. Likewise, the reconstruction calculation covers every completion of the four specified labelled skew sources; it does not classify all order-$42$ graphs or supply a source at order $110$. No larger search was reproduced or needed for these claims.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from prior work. Paper 52 [1] constructs the examples and derives restrictions conditional on a bi-Cayley action; it does not establish the action classification or unrestricted completion uniqueness proved here. The character methods in [6,7], the symmetric/skew identities and automorphism tests in [8], and the prime-automorphism arguments of Paper 23 [10] are relevant antecedents, but their stated results do not subsume these theorems. The external comparison with Maksimović–Rukavina [5] confirms the acknowledged connection to orbit matrices and fixed-vertex degree restrictions at different parameters. De Winter–Kamischke–Wang [9] provide an earlier general spectral automorphism framework. The tournament block constructions of Adams et al. [11] concern different parameter families and do not give the present obstruction. I found no substantial duplication in these focused comparisons.

Two minor improvements would strengthen attribution and reproducibility:

- Cite [9] directly for the historical spectral automorphism framework, and Paper 23 [10] for the closely related combination of trace restrictions and fixed-neighbourhood arguments. The present parameter-specific results remain distinct.
- Include a short source-matching check that reconstructs the four graphs of [1] and verifies the coordinate inversion against the certificate data. The matching succeeds, but the supplied reconstruction verifier currently starts from the stored skew matrices.

The abstract is informative, the claims and limitations are clear, and the mathematical notation and numbered references are readable. These suggestions do not affect acceptance.
