# Accept

The weighted-cover bounds and the rigidity of a ten-column cover constitute a meaningful, sound structural advance toward the open DSRG$(98,20,9,8,3)$ problem. They apply without an automorphism assumption and are stronger than a restriction on a chosen construction class. The manuscript correctly leaves existence unresolved. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks these parameters as unknown.

The central proofs withstand scrutiny. Equation (1) forces the mass of an integral range vector to be a multiple of $q$; its maximum-entry inequality reduces a putative mass below $3q$ to a Boolean vector of mass $2q$. The missing-arc count excludes that vector, including the exceptional possibility of a full column on its support. In Section 3, removing weight-one columns produces genuine range indicators, and the excess above double coverage is nonnegative and has nonzero mass modulo $3q$. The subsequent intersection argument correctly excludes the two equality cases. In particular, the cases involving weights greater than one half and weights equal to one half account for all possibilities, and their contradictions hold throughout the stated range $q\ge7$.

Theorem 3 supplies additional substance beyond the numerical cover bound. Independence of a minimum cover leaves a two-dimensional kernel on the row side. The Boolean-square argument excludes four row types on the weight-one support. The resulting three prototypes correctly identify the selected-column span, exclude a second weight-one column, and force at least 44 exterior physical columns into that span. The private-coordinate argument and the four-versus-three internal-successor comparison then establish uniqueness of the exterior full row given its cover memberships. These conclusions are conditional necessary structure, as the manuscript states; they do not establish feasibility of any remaining cover size.

The ternary consequence is also valid. A congruence $u+v=w$ produces a binary range vector $(u+v-w)/3$ of mass $2q$, which is impossible. The passage to outgoing equitable pair partitions uses sums of physical columns, so it does not incorrectly assume incoming equitability. The distinction between the two axes is preserved.

There is an important attribution qualification. Theorem 1 should not be presented as an entirely new unrestricted minimum-mass mechanism. Paper 142 [7], Section 8.2, Lemma 9, already excludes every Boolean vector satisfying $xB=qx+2\mathbf j$ in this same family. Together with the maximum-entry inequality, that result immediately yields the stated nonnegative integral minimum. Paper 142 also contains the centered-rank class cap and related Boolean affine geometry. Add a direct citation and explain this relationship in Section 1 and at Theorem 1. The present weighted-cover bounds and ten-column rigidity remain substantial contributions beyond those results, so this attribution correction does not overturn acceptance.

The other distinctions are appropriate: paper 7 [4] supplies the nonnegative-basis framework and a weaker indicator bound; paper 140 [5] concerns a different, conditional local boundary; and paper 143 [6] counts all distinct neighborhoods rather than the minimum support of a positive cover. The focused external check of the representation and intriguing-set literature [2,3], together with targeted arXiv searches, did not identify the weighted-cover bounds or the ten-column conclusion. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive classification of all related spectral methods.

The essential evidence is the supplied mathematical proof, which is sufficiently complete without a computational supplement. As a limited independent check, I reconstructed the cited three-group construction at $q=3,6,9$ and verified the exact matrix identities, partition covers of sizes $2,4,6$, and ternary pair-sum obstruction. At $q=3$, all 19,380 binary indicators of masses three and six failed the required range equation. These checks corroborate the construction and small examples; they do not replace the general proofs or test a hypothetical order-98 graph.

Clarity is adequate for publication. The questions, theorem statements, proof progression and limitations are identifiable. Two small improvements would help: explicitly introduce “physical column” as a column indexed by an actual vertex, and add a sentence explaining why three distinct Boolean points in the affine plane of Section 4 have independent differences. Also normalize the doubled LaTeX backslashes in the metadata abstract's floor expressions. The abstract is otherwise informative.
