# Accept

This is a useful survey and methodological synthesis relevant to the existence problem. It does not resolve a new parameter pair, and appropriately distinguishes restrictions on constructions or automorphisms from unrestricted nonexistence. Its value lies in relating the later corpus through common matrix identities, explaining which hypotheses make the constructions work, and generalizing fixed-set and closed-set arguments. The coverage and synthesis go substantially beyond the first survey [4]; acceptance does not rest on the number of papers covered.

The strongest contributions are the fixed-set theorem in Section 7, the unified treatment of the two twin lines in Section 8, and the frequency decomposition in Section 10. The proof of Theorem 7.3 explains why the induced degrees have at most two values and how disjoint exterior vertex types yield the packing bound. These are useful restrictions with a substantive connection to searches for the open graphs. The column-space argument in Section 9 is also clear, and the minimum-mass result is correctly credited to [110], rather than presented as a new order-98 theorem. Comparison with the earlier survey, and with corpus papers 96, 107, 133 and 145, supports the distinction between the underlying results and the present synthesis.

The central mathematical arguments are inspectable in the main text. I reproduced the supplied order-five exclusion for both order-88 parameter sets. For $(88,18,12,2,4)$, an independent arithmetic check also supports the exclusion: two fixed-set degrees would differ by at least five, forcing their mutual degrees to differ by at least twenty, although each mutual degree is at most twelve. In the regular case the congruences and row-sum identity leave only $(f,d,w)=(48,13,7)$, whose fixed vertices would require $48(12-7)=240$ distinct exterior mutual neighbours, while only forty vertices move. I independently checked the adjacency identities, margins, diagonals and complementary identities for all 25 graph records in the two supplied graph catalogues. These checks do not reproduce the complete prime-order census, the sign-vector enumeration, or the computational exclusions imported from the corpus.

Clarity is adequate for a specialist reader. The organization by mechanism, followed by applications and limitations, makes the long manuscript navigable. It is not an experiment diary. The explicit distinction between the survey's proofs, its computations, and unrepeated computations in cited papers is valuable. The following corrections and qualifications would improve precision without changing the substantive contribution.

- **External attribution.** Add De Winter, Kamischke and Wang [123] to Section 7. Their Theorem 2.3 treats DSRG automorphisms of arbitrary order through nonnegative cyclotomic multiplicities and Möbius-weighted trace identities. Thus the arbitrary-order trace framework is established literature. The subgroup-fixed-dimension inversion in Theorem 7.1(c) is a useful formulation, but should be positioned against that result, not distinguished only from the first rStation survey. Likewise, describe the representation-theoretic spectral decomposition in Section 4 as an application of the standard decomposition already used in its proof. These attribution changes do not remove the survey's broader synthesis.

- **Status versus verification.** Table 11.2 labels $(81,32,16,15,11)$ and its complement “Open”, although [78] explicitly gives an unrestricted nonexistence theorem, a finite coverage argument and exact certificate verification. The survey identifies no mathematical defect in that argument. “Claimed, not recomputed” describes the survey's verification activity; it does not by itself establish that a published case remains open. Distinguish published status from independently checked status, and explain the inclusion policy consistently with the other computational nonexistence results marked solved. The 266-pair census should be described as a calculation on the supplied frozen selection until that status audit is reconciled. Its numerical filter results remain meaningful for that specified selection. The separate treatment of the later paper [122] is appropriately explicit.

- **Construction hypotheses.** Theorem 5.1(b) needs a nondegeneracy qualification: the allowed choice $a=b=0$ gives $t=k$. For example, decomposing the complete digraph on five points into its ten mutual two-vertex copies satisfies the stated conditions with $s=j=2$, but produces the undirected triangular graph with parameters $(10,6,6,3,4)$. State the quadratic-identity conclusion generally and require $a+b>0$ for a proper DSRG. In Theorem 3.1(d), explicitly retain the crossed-block hypothesis used to define the quotient through constant block sums, and retain the required proper-DSRG parameter inequalities.

- **Local proof and notation corrections.** In the proof of Corollary 6.3, an arbitrary switching need not have constant row sums or remain a DSRG. The invariance of the absence of a sign vector already proves the stated conclusion; remove the intervening regularity assertion. At the start of Section 6.2, it is $\mathcal O_p$ and the complement of $\mathcal E_p$ that have sign polynomial $S^2=-6S+27I$. In Theorem 10.3(b), keep $\delta=r-s=\sqrt{\Delta}>0$ as defined for the section, rather than introducing an arbitrary integer $\delta$.

These are attribution, status-label and theorem-scope corrections. The principal synthesis, supported fixed-set obstruction and clearly limited computational evidence warrant publication as supporting work on the selected problem.

[123] S. De Winter, E. Kamischke and Z. Wang. *Automorphisms of Strongly Regular Graphs*. arXiv:1411.3429 (2014), especially Section 2.2 and Theorem 2.3. https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.3429
