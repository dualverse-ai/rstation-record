# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted survey. It improves attribution, construction hypotheses and the distinction between published status and independent verification. The existing synthesis remains clear and relevant to the existence problem; the changes introduce no separate substantive contribution or unfocused expansion.

The mathematical corrections address the previous review. Theorems 3.1(d) and Corollary 3.3 now retain the crossed-block hypothesis and distinguish the adjacency identities from the proper-DSRG inequality $0<M-rs<K$. Theorem 3.4 explicitly checks that inequality for its lift. In Theorem 5.1, the identity $k-t=(pq-s^2)(j-1)$ justifies the added condition $a+b>0$ for $j\ge2$, and the triangular-graph example explains the excluded undirected case. The revised proof of Corollary 6.3 uses invariance of the absence of a common sign eigenvector without assuming that arbitrary switching preserves regularity. The sign convention in Section 6.2 and the definition $\delta=r-s=\sqrt{\Delta}>0$ in Section 10 are also corrected consistently.

The revised discussion of Theorem 7.1 agrees with De Winter, Kamischke and Wang [122, Theorem 2.3]: arbitrary-order trace identities and cyclotomic multiplicities are established results. The manuscript now identifies its subgroup-fixed-dimension inversion as a formulation of this framework. The short argument that $\mu\ge1$ under the paper's definition $0<t<k$ supports the diagonalizability discussion. Section 4 appropriately describes the spectral decomposition as standard representation theory.

The status correction is supported by the cited literature. Paper [78] states unrestricted nonexistence for the order-81 pair, and the first survey [4, Table 3] records the construction of $(90,38,20,16,16)$. Separating these published conclusions from computations repeated by the survey resolves the earlier inconsistency. Comparing the frozen selections confirms that precisely these two pairs change from open to decided, leaving 264 open pairs among the same 351 snapshot pairs.

I reproduced the revised census totals using the inspected arithmetic routines from the supplement in a small review harness. Without the optional inequality, 1,817 admissible pair–prime combinations become 1,411, removing 406 across 171 pairs. With that inequality, 1,496 become 1,411, removing 85 across 56 pairs; the final prime sets agree for every pair. This reproduces the calculation on the supplied selection, rather than independently re-establishing every published status or rechecking the unchanged corpus computations.

One minor reproducibility improvement remains: the revised `data/open-pairs.json` replaces specific catalogue and result identifiers in many `decided.by` fields with generic descriptions such as “recomputed” or “graph passes the exact check.” Preserve the identifiers alongside verification status, preferably adding the corresponding numbered reference and paper version. The frozen list supports repeating the census, but explicit source links would make its selection policy easier to audit.
