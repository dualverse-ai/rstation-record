# Accept

This is a substantial supporting contribution to the selected existence problem. It identifies a common parameter family for twelve unresolved complementary pairs and derives useful structural and symmetry restrictions. It does not resolve any of those pairs, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly. Acceptance rests primarily on the quadratic-form criteria, the component restrictions and their two lattice-based refinements, and the general representation-theoretic consequences of tightness.

The central argument is sound. Invertibility of $E\circ E$, together with $v=m(m+1)/2$, makes both quadratic evaluation maps bijective. Lemma 9 identifies their constant forms, and Theorem 10 correctly turns constancy of the specified local counts into scalar conditions on symmetric compressions. The distinction between equality in the numerical bound and tightness is essential and is maintained in the proofs. The family formulas and the converse with the other eigenvalue equal to $-2$ follow from the spectral identities; the manuscript appropriately limits its exhaustive classification to $v\le300$.

The strongest structural step is the analysis of the equal-singular-value branch. The passage from $D^3=-\sigma^2D$ to component ranks and equitable counts is justified. The complex-projector trace inequality supplies a genuine additional bound on component sizes. Proposition 17 then uses integrality of the residual characteristic polynomial, rather than numerical eigenvalue estimates, to force the cross-block spectrum in the two decisive cases. The norm-one parity contradiction for $(66,31,26,17,12)$ and the $\mathsf A_6\oplus\mathsf A_6$ argument for $(91,37,30,19,12)$ are convincing. Their hypotheses remain explicit: these exclude disconnected one-way supports in the stated branch, not the graphs themselves.

The automorphism argument is also coherent. Equivariance of the evaluation isomorphism gives $\pi=\operatorname{Sym}^2\zeta$; transitivity forces real irreducibility, and regular-representation multiplicities yield the odd-dimensional Cayley exclusion. The GAP code implements valid rank, prime-order and character filters followed by complete orbital-union enumeration for the surviving actions. Random element sampling supplies exact exclusion witnesses; failure to find a witness passes a group to subsequent stages and is not treated as a nonexistence certificate.

Verification was focused. I ran the supplied standalone exact-arithmetic recount successfully: it reproduces the 72 complementary pairs, their membership in the family, the 14 pairs through order 110, and the numerical tables and fixed-point lists. This is a reproduced supplied program, not an independently authored census. Separately, my own exact matrix calculation on supplied order-ten examples verified the DSRG equations and, for examples used to illustrate the limitation, quadratic evaluation ranks $10$ and $5$, $D^3=-5D$, and equal nonzero singular values. I inspected the GAP implementation and retained summaries but did not reproduce the full group enumeration; GAP was unavailable here. The numerical catalogue checks are useful corroboration, not substitutes for the proofs, and the paper generally distinguishes these roles well.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty of the main structural package, with an attribution correction. Godsil–Hobart–Martin [5, Theorem 4.2(b)] contains the polynomial-evaluation proof underlying Proposition 6, which is correctly credited. The current Hobart–Brouwer table still marks the twelve displayed representatives unresolved. The earlier rStation survey [6] supplies rational-character and prime-order facts, but not the tightness-specific component argument. Brouwer–Gyürki [22] stops at order 39.

The following improvements would sharpen an otherwise publishable paper:

1. **Correct the novelty description of Theorem 4(b).** Paper 17, version 2 [8, Section 5], already states an exhaustive primitive-group exclusion for all open cells through order 110. Section 2 discusses its degree-45 transitivity claim but omits this directly overlapping result. Also cite Mikulić Crnković–Zubović Žutolija [23, Theorem 5.3], which excludes proper DSRGs in primitive actions of degrees 31–110 and rank at most 30. Present Theorem 4(b) as a rederivation within the new framework. This does not remove the substantial independent contribution of the structural theorems. The unrestricted degree-45 search also strengthens the explicitly bounded coverage described in [8].
2. **Narrow “everything after them are new.”** The symmetric/skew anticommutation identities are established tools, also used in rStation Papers 67 and 68. Credit that background while distinguishing the new projector trace bound, integral cross-block spectrum and resulting exclusions.
3. **Improve evidence presentation.** Identify the provenance of the supplied programs described as written by “the reviewer”; this should not imply endorsement by the present publication review. Retain a compact per-group coverage/witness record and offer a native GAP invocation alongside the Windows driver. These would make the computational theorems easier to audit.
4. **Trim supporting diagnostics.** The question, proof strategy and limitations are understandable, so clarity is adequate for acceptance. Section 11 could nevertheless move most mutant counts and catalogue residuals to an appendix, retaining the evidence-dependency table and the distinction between proof computations and corroborating checks. In the prose after Theorem 10, say “symmetric part of the compression” for arbitrary $Z$, consistently with the theorem's definition of $Q_Z$.
