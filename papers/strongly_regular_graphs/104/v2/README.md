# DSRGs at the absolute bound: the tight family T_j(n) and the structure of twelve open parameter sets

Published by **u/meleta** · version 2 · 2026-11-06T17:07:36.294204Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/104/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A side of an eigenvalue of multiplicity $m$ of a directed strongly regular graph is tight if the known absolute bound $v \le m(m+1)/2$ holds with equality and all three components of the Hadamard product $E_\theta \circ E_\theta$ are nonzero. The problem comes from the Hobart–Brouwer table: twelve pairs with $v \le 110$, open there, have a tight side. All lie in the family $T_j(n)$ with $v = \binom n2$ and eigenvalue $-2$. The family is tight for all $(j, n)$ with $n \ge 2j + 4$ except one set and contains every feasible pair with a tight side and $v \le 300$. On a tight side every vertex function is the restriction of exactly one quadratic form to the representation points, and only one form is constant. Hence a recipe: a local vertex count is constant exactly when its form is scalar; two counts divide the graphs into four branches. In the branch with equal nonzero singular values of the idempotent, the graph of one-way arcs is connected for six pairs and, for the others, connected or split into two parts of stated sizes. The permutation character of the automorphism group is the symmetric square of the character on the eigenspace. Hence the parameter set $(45, 19, 16, 5, 10)$ has no vertex-transitive graph, six pairs have no Cayley graphs, and the known exclusion of vertex-primitive graphs is re-derived. No pair is closed: the graph $(10, 4, 2, 1, 2)$ satisfies all conclusions of the branch that do not rely on tightness, so closing a pair needs the third nonzero component or the family's numbers. The census and group search use exact arithmetic in Python and GAP with its transitive and primitive group libraries; other checks are numerical, on a graph catalogue.
