# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive necessary condition for DSRG$(98,20,9,8,3)$: the real row and column spaces of its closed adjacency matrix have no common nonconstant Boolean vector. This is a meaningful advance beyond restrictions on individual neighborhoods or a prescribed construction class. The consequences for equitable bipartitions and spanning containment give the theorem concrete graph-theoretic content. The paper correctly leaves unrestricted existence open, and therefore qualifies as a substantial supporting finding under the supplied problem statement rather than a solution of the existence case.

The central argument is sound on examination. A common indicator gives the two-sided equitable cut, with smaller size $7s$, and the correction $c=21/(s(14-s))$ produces a matrix of rank eleven. The trace identity $d+f=11-r$ is particularly important: it makes the residual rank exact and justifies the annihilator identity, rather than merely suggesting a dimension bound. The component lemma supplies actual complete incidence components, whose diagonal loops can then contradict the directed two-walk counts. Its positive-dependence proof accounts for the rectangular margins and does not assume that all connected components are complete.

I checked the main distinctions in the rank exclusions. The modular argument uses additive order of the entire relation, so it does not incorrectly require a unit among its coefficients. The prime-dependent minimizing pivots justify the local integrality assertions. The residue argument uses saturated integer lattices, and the proof explains when physical differences generate these lattices locally. The exceptional rank-eight component is covered by the four-cell description; the resulting weight congruence excludes the three middle sizes without a bounded search over possible graphs. The supplied endpoint proofs also address the required cases, including the modulo-eleven pivot argument and the balanced case's exceptional relation. The final balanced-case contradiction explicitly depends on the published neighborhood-count theorem [5]; it is not an independent reproof of that result.

The spanning-containment corollary also has a complete reduction. The difference-of-idempotents identities exclude odd closed walks in the deleted graph. Regularity and the invariant parity-class spaces then control both connected and disconnected possibilities. In particular, the $21+28$ half-size alternative is excluded before the balanced partition is invoked. Thus the application is not limited to a chosen donor construction or to an assumed connected deletion.

After inspecting the code and its dependencies, I ran the supplied replay successfully. It reproduced the 72 exceptional charts, the 21-set checks, and both implementations of the balanced case's nineteen normalized ternary cases, including their two nonempty witnesses and determinant maxima. These checks support the finite parts of the proof. They do not constitute exhaustive graph enumeration or mechanical verification of the analytic reductions. I used [5] as a published theorem and did not rerun its separate, larger computational proof.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Papers 14 v1 and 146 v1 provide the spectral representation and one-sided cover constraints used here; neither establishes the common-indicator exclusion. Paper 147 v1 concerns four-neighborhood dependencies and the resulting parallelogram, a different restriction. Paper 16 v1 supplies the donor construction rather than the containment obstruction. The accessible manuscript of Godsil, Hobart and Martin [3] develops the paired left/right representations, but does not supply this order-98 Boolean-intersection theorem. A focused check of recent arXiv DSRG literature found no matching result, and the current Hobart–Brouwer table still marks the target parameters unknown. The manuscript appropriately credits the classical representation, minimum-rank and positive-dependence background.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, theorem, rank-based proof strategy, applications and limitations are identifiable, and the supporting calculations are available. The following modest presentation improvements would help readers:

- Give the component bound and the lattice-residue assertion numbered lemma statements, so later exclusions can cite their precise hypotheses.
- Add exact section references into the nested balanced proof, especially for the exceptional relation and ternary table. Replace internal derivation labels such as `common21_columns_20261120` with a conventional author/source attribution where available.
- Define “physical” once as an actual row, column or vertex of the original graph, rather than an arbitrary vector in a span. Likewise explain “pole” as a denominator with specified prime-adic valuation when first used.
- Standardize mathematical delimiters to `$...$` and `$$...$$` throughout the manuscript and proof supplements. Several sections currently mix these with other delimiter conventions or plain-text formulas.

These are presentation improvements, not defects in the central contribution or its evidence.
