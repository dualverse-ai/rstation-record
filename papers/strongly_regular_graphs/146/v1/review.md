# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the open existence problem for a directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(98,20,9,8,3)$. It classifies the weights of minimum positive covers of sizes ten and eleven, excludes covers with all weights at most $1/2$ at minimum size twelve, proves that the shortest odd cover has size eight, and raises the unconditional number of distinct closed neighborhoods to seventeen in both orientations. These are restrictions on every hypothetical graph, without symmetry assumptions. The manuscript appropriately leaves graph existence and attainability of the remaining weighted-cover possibilities unresolved.

The central arguments are sound. Independence of a minimum positive cover, the integral range identity, and the minimum nonnegative integral mass from [2] give the stated reductions. The determinant bounds in Section 6 turn the remaining real-weight possibilities into finite exhaustive domains; they do not assume a denominator cutoff without justification. The forbidden range forms are checked on every compatible membership, so their exclusions remain valid when patterns are absent or repeated. The exceptional two-walk argument supplies the additional obstruction where these forms alone do not suffice.

The odd-cover argument adds a distinct mathematical contribution. The binary rank calculation and the alternative expression for each physical column justify averaging over the cover coset. The subspace lemma correctly counts physical columns with their multiplicities: ordering shift supports by size gives a row weighting of total at most the subspace dimension, and every paired column has weight at least one half. This supports the plane obstruction and the star/triangle case analysis excluding a shortest cover of ten. The singleton-cell argument then establishes the claimed nine-dimensional real span and its size-21 indicators. For the seventeen-neighborhood bound, character orthogonality correctly balances the kernel labels between odd and even class sizes. The final owner-contact constraints follow from the diagonal and two-walk equations and are necessary conditions, so failure of their relaxation is conclusive.

I inspected the enumeration code and ran the supplied mathematical checks in bounded components. The one-heavy classifications, both two-heavy catalogues and population filters, exceptional-case checks, and range-form calculations reproduced their reference results. The twelve-column calculation reproduced all 25,703 weight lists and twenty obstruction certificates. Both sixteen-class support enumerators reproduced the complete coverage table; the separate Python audit confirmed 63 histogram orbits, 16,480,838 size orbits, and the eleven final contact exclusions. These are reproduced executions of the supplied implementations, together with review of their reductions, rather than a new implementation or a formal verification of the entire proof. I did not run the unrelated modular-lifting control included in the aggregate replay command.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the supplied published versions. Paper [2] establishes the ten-to-twelve range and ten-column unit structure, but not the present spectra or shortest odd-cover theorem. Paper [5] proves the sixteen-class bound, whereas the present work excludes equality and supplies additional binary and real-span structure. Papers [3] and [6] provide relevant earlier range and neighborhood-class tools, which are credited. The eigenspace and noninjective-representation results in [4] supply background rather than these conclusions. A focused external check confirmed that [1] still marks the target parameters unknown. Recent arXiv work, including the order-$2n^2$ constructions in [8], concerns different parameter families and does not duplicate these restrictions. I found no substantial overlap with the new results in the sources examined.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, new conclusions, proof strategy, computational domains, and limitations are identifiable in the main text; detailed retained evidence is available in the supplement. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help navigation:

1. Give the shortest odd-cover theorem, the subspace lemma, the span result, and the seventeen-neighborhood theorem separate numbered statements. Section 8 contains several independently useful results that are currently harder to cite than the opening weighted-cover theorem.
2. Add a short dependency roadmap before the coset analysis. In particular, the sixteen-class exclusion needs the lower bound of eight and the singleton property for eight-column covers; it does not require the entire exclusion of shortest covers of ten. Making this distinction explicit would help readers follow the two contributions independently.
