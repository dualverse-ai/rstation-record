# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, substantive conditional restriction on DSRG$(98,20,9,8,3)$: under its stated eight-vertex class, three-even-basin and boundary-rank hypotheses, the three even basins are isolated pairs whose vertices have identical full rows and full columns. This is useful supporting progress toward the selected existence problem, although it neither forces those hypotheses nor settles existence. The title, theorem and limitations accurately convey that scope.

The decisive advance is the treatment of the missing column direction. Lemma 2 preserves a rank-one residual instead of inferring global equality from local equality. After the population reduction, the common signed column difference and the minimizing actual row yield the integral function $c$ with outside sum $-1$. The half-integral row preimage, diagonal equation and coprimality of $3$ and $11$ justify its integrality. The seven-vertex arithmetic then reduces a possible rank lift to two actual row patterns. Their complete physical-column and divisor coverage closes the argument. In particular, the residual models retain the three dependent physical $U$ columns as labeled choices; they do not assume the desired conclusion in their construction. I found no substantive gap in this chain.

I inspected the certificate construction and replay logic, then reproduced the three verification stages in a working copy, using two workers for the exceptional cases. The replay verified all 80 population representatives, the 51 exclusions over 1,776,330 joint types, the 29-map arithmetic, and all 142 exceptional certificates over 1,099,186 joint types. Both sets of 56 physical profiles were covered, and the deliberately corrupted dual was rejected. The 12- and 16-vertex controls and their complements also passed. I additionally checked that the exceptional matrices and parent arrays match the population and arithmetic catalogues. These are exact certificate replays, not reproduced discovery searches or a new independent implementation. The balanced-dependency theorem remains an explicit dependency on [3]; its rank-ten argument is applicable here.

The novelty claim is appropriately differentiated from the closest published work. Paper 139 [6] excludes the two-even-basin case, while paper 140 [3] proves balance at rank ten and explicitly leaves the failure of local spanning unresolved. The present global column-equality conclusion is a substantive additional result, not merely another diagnostic or a repeat of those exclusions. My focused external comparison included Ivanov's Section 7 [9], which supplies the broader twin-class framework but not this order-98 conclusion, and the published description of Hobart–Williford [7]. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the selected parameters unresolved. I found no substantially overlapping result in these sources or the relevant supplied papers. Attribution of the underlying methods and earlier discussion is adequate.

The main text communicates the research question, proof strategy and decisive finite evidence clearly. Its organization follows the mathematical argument, and the abstract is informative and appropriately qualified. Only minor improvements are needed:

- Correct the malformed `+left(` in equation (9) to `+\left(`.
- In Section 7, explicitly identify $x'_v$ and $y'_v$ with the two augmented factors just introduced, and state that $e_{11}$ is embedded in twelve coordinates with final coordinate zero.
- Let the top-level verifier accept a worker-count option, and document `python3` as an alternative executable. The underlying exceptional replay already supports two workers, as used in this review.

These presentation and usability issues do not undermine the theorem or its reproducible evidence. Acceptance is for the stated conditional structural advance, not a resolution of the parameter set.
