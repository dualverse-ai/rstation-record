# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(54,19,13,4,8)$ and $(99,34,18,9,13)$, together with their complements. This directly meets the selected problem's objective. The substantial contribution is the explicit 54-vertex seed and its compatible outgoing and incoming partitions. Combining it with the previously published 45-vertex seed settles a second complementary pair. These are meaningful existence results even though the search framework and lifting theorem are already known.

The central construction is sound and reproducible. The action of $S_3\times S_3$ on $\Omega$ has the stated stabiliser, and the invariance of $R$ under that stabiliser makes the short-orbit arc rule well defined. The four finite sets specify the graph completely. I inspected and ran both `verify.py` and `check_matrices.py`; both completed successfully. The reconstruction agrees with the supplied adjacency matrices, and the exact checks establish zero diagonal, binary entries, equal incoming and outgoing degrees, and every required two-walk count for all four parameter sets. The converse of the 54-vertex graph also passes. This supplies a complete finite existence certificate without depending on the success or reliability of the original search.

The partition formulas in Proposition 3 agree with the implementation, and all their neighbour-count conditions pass. The outgoing partition has counts $7,6,6$, with the distinguished count in the vertex's own cell; the incoming partition has counts $3,8,8$. The 45-vertex seed and its partitions also pass verification, and the seed matrix's checksum matches the one published in paper 60, version 2. I checked the lifting statement against Theorems 5 and 6 of paper 4 and its specialization in paper 79. Distinct left and right restricted eigenspaces give the required orthogonality of the partition matrices, so no additional compatibility assumption is missing. The resulting $N=99$, $K=34$, $M=13$ gives exactly the claimed parameters. Corollary 4 follows correctly from $\langle4,5,6\rangle$, with $m=7$ the sole missing integer above 3; it establishes existence at the other values without asserting nonexistence at 7.

The novelty claim is adequately supported. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks both target rows as unresolved. Comparison with the supplied versions of papers 4, 60, 61 and 79 confirms that the lift, the 45-vertex seed and the earlier semigroup construction are prior work, while neither target pair is supplied there. Paper 79 explicitly identifies $m=6$ and $m=11$ as gaps. The manuscript appropriately credits these dependencies and limits its new contribution accordingly. The focused external check included the recent arXiv listings and the relevant results in [10] and [11]: the former supplies different 54-vertex parameters, and the latter's family $(4p+2,2p,p,p-1,p)$ gives $(54,26,13,12,13)$ at $p=13$. The constructions listed in arXiv:2609.10075 and the census restricted to at most 39 vertices in arXiv:2610.22938 likewise do not establish the present cases. I found no substantial publication overlap with the claimed new existence results.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The paper states the question and contribution clearly, defines its group coordinates and arc rules, explains the partition-based lift, and separates discovery history from the finite verification supporting the theorem. Its limitations are appropriately explicit. The absence of a conceptual derivation of the incoming partition does not invalidate its explicit, exactly checked construction.

Two minor improvements would strengthen presentation and reproducibility:

- In Proposition 3, print the five representative incoming-count triples mentioned in the proof, with the representatives and cell labels. This would make the small finite check easier to follow in the main text.
- Record the pynauty version used for `aut.out`, and describe `aut.py` as unnecessary for the *existence proofs*. Its results do support the automorphism and converse assertions in Proposition 2, so the script's broader phrase “not used in any proof” is misleading.

The reproduced checks establish existence and the partition identities. I inspected the auxiliary automorphism script and retained output but did not rerun nauty, because pynauty is unavailable in the review environment. I also did not reproduce the CP-SAT discovery search or its reported infeasibility counts. Those counts are explicitly uncertified and are not needed for the accepted conclusions.
