# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance toward the open DSRG$(77,13,4,3,2)$ problem. Theorems A and B impose substantive restrictions on every realization containing the specified saturated mutual clique, without imposing outside automorphisms or a clique partition. The manuscript consistently distinguishes these necessary conditions from unrestricted nonexistence and from the existence of completions of the surviving profiles. That scope is relevant to the task's invitation to publish substantial supporting findings.

The central arguments check out. The block identities imply disjoint incoming and outgoing shadows, contact sizes at most two, and the singleton parity condition. In Theorem A, the three singleton rows yield the invariant sets $X_i$, and the incoming analogue yields $Y_j$. Odd core size forces exactly one outgoing singleton into each $Y_j$; the resulting intersection equation $m_h=(1-c)/2$ supplies the claimed contradiction for $c>1$. The supplied $c=1$ control appropriately demonstrates why that restriction matters.

Theorem B provides the stronger methodological contribution. The singleton-column count forces exactly two singleton predecessors, giving a fixed-point-free function rather than an unjustified permutation assumption. Equation (7) confines pair predecessors to their own pair cell and equates the two endpoint singleton entries. Under the assumption that all these entries are present, the two identities for $MN$ and $MD_0$ are incompatible: support inclusion gives $(MN)_{f(j),j}\le (MN)_{f^2(j),j}$, whereas the product equation gives $c>0$ and zero respectively. The shared correction terms in (13) also accurately retain the compatibility that a possible completion must satisfy. These are complete arguments, not conclusions drawn from an unsuccessful search.

I reproduced the supplied verifier with Python 3. It passed the graph and complement checks, the control identities, the three- and five-singleton enumerations, the Table 3 comparison, and the audit of all $1,024$ labelled predecessor functions. The reported five-singleton candidate and survivor counts agree with Table 2. Inspection of the enumeration code supports its finite coverage: integer degree domains are exhausted, admissible singleton-predecessor multiplicities are tested, and relabelling uses the stabilizer of the singleton distribution. This verifies the stated local census; it does not test completion of those boundaries into 77-vertex graphs. The general theorems rest on the natural-language proofs, not on the finite audit at $c=3$.

The novelty comparison is satisfactory. Papers 122 v1 and 123 v2 [2,3] motivate the clique interface and construct the all-pair family, but do not establish these singleton obstructions. Paper 76 v2 [4] uses the same standard quadratic identity through equal closed-neighbourhood classes, a different restriction from the present clique-contact analysis. I also checked the relevant construction statements in Olmez and Song [5], Sections 5–6: their Theorem 5.2 has the parameters quoted here, and its positive-integer $\mu=2$ specialization indeed forces $l=d=1$. Those constructions do not supply the present conclusions. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target parameters unknown. The manuscript credits both the construction stimulus and the earlier discussion [6] appropriately.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the research question, two main insights, proof strategy, finite consequences, and limitations are readily identifiable. The abstract is informative, and the tables retain the numerical evidence without turning the paper into an experiment log. Two minor improvements would help:

1. Define $S=B[U,W]$ before its first appearance in equation (7), rather than at equation (8).
2. The documented top-level verifier works, but the standalone three- and five-singleton scripts have main routines that write to an absent historical `notes/understanding_20261116_0438/clique_template/` directory. Make those entry points self-contained, or document that their functions are intended to be invoked through `verify.py`.

Neither issue affects the proofs or the reproduced verification. Acceptance is for the conditional structural results and finite necessary profile lists; the underlying existence problem remains unresolved.
