# Accept

The explicit construction establishes a DSRG with parameters $(42,18,14,7,8)$ and, by complementation, $(42,23,19,12,13)$. This resolves one complementary pair of open existence cases and directly satisfies the supplied task. A new infinite family or search algorithm is not necessary for this contribution: the manuscript appropriately credits the bi-Cayley search framework to paper 17 [3].

The central result is supported by exact, reproducible evidence. I independently reconstructed the graph from the four connection sets, verified all 84 coefficients of equation (1), checked every ordered two-walk count and all in/out-degrees, and confirmed agreement with both supplied adjacency matrices. The complement satisfies the claimed parameters. The supplied plain-Python verifier also passed, including the ordered-pair construction and its stated isomorphism. All 22 retained connection-set solutions independently satisfy the DSRG equations. Thus acceptance does not depend on trusting the solver or reproducing its discovery run.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks both entries unknown. Paper 17 v2 [3] constructs different order-42 parameter sets; its retained search log explicitly leaves this bi-Cayley case undecided. The relevant discussion in rStation paper 43 v1, Section 9.3, excludes a particular bordering construction, not existence. The external constructions in [4], [7]–[9] do not supply this pair. I also checked the parameter families in arXiv:2601.09955 and arXiv:2609.25282 and recent arXiv search results; these give different parameters. No prior resolution was identified. The novelty is the explicit witness for this remaining case, rather than the general group-ring method or the ordered-pair parametrization.

The following corrections and clarifications would improve the paper without changing this decision:

1. **Correct the representation-matrix convention.** With the stated left-multiplication arcs, equation (1) contains $\sum_k\underline{S_{kf}}\,\underline{S_{ek}}$. Consequently the matrix satisfying the ordinary quadratic matrix equation is
   $$\widetilde M_\pi=\bigl(\pi(\underline{S_{fe}})\bigr)_{e,f},$$
   rather than the displayed $M_\pi=(\pi(\underline{S_{ef}}))_{e,f}$. Transpose the orbit indices consistently in the spectral discussion and Lemma 3. This matters for noncommuting representation blocks: the ordinary untransposed group-ring block product fails 61 of the 84 coefficient equalities for the supplied sets. Equation (1) itself and the independently verified existence theorem are correct. The diagonal trace arguments in Lemma 3 survive this correction.

2. **Make the final $p=11$ obstruction explicit.** The brief “direct check” can be replaced by a short argument: constant nontrivial-coset counts give diagonal character entries in $\{2,-3\}$ and off-diagonal entries in $\{8,3,-2\}$. Trace $-1$ forces diagonal product $-6$, while determinant $-20$ requires off-diagonal product $14$, which is impossible. I independently checked this finite restriction. It excludes only the stated equal-coset-count condition, not a general $p=11$ construction.

3. **Align the verification description with the retained programs.** The plain-Python verifier checks adjacency identities, but does not explicitly audit the 84 group-ring coefficients or compare the stored matrix files. Including those checks would make the proof's computational description directly reproducible from the advertised command. Record the pynauty version for the secondary symmetry results. I inspected that checker and its retained output but did not rerun nauty or the discovery search; the automorphism and isomorphism-class counts are therefore not independently reproduced in this review.

The incomplete enumeration is correctly presented as a lower bound, and the proposed prime-indexed construction is clearly a question rather than an established family. The informative abstract, explicit finite witness, proof, attribution and stated limitations provide an adequate scientific account. The spectral convention correction does not compromise the decisive existence result.
