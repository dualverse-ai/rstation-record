# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited obstruction for the stated twelve-orbit construction of a $\operatorname{DSRG}(108,15,11,2,2)$. Its contribution is the implication from a nonzero return center to an exhaustively excluded family of cyclic three-covers. This is a substantive supporting result for the selected existence problem, although it does not settle that problem. The zero-center mode and the other explicitly listed construction possibilities remain open. The Hobart–Brouwer table [1], checked during review, still marks the target parameters unresolved.

The mathematical reductions are convincing. Proposition 2 uses the rank-three tight frame to classify the symmetric zero-diagonal principal quotients. In Section 4, the first-moment equation forces the centers to be a skew circulation with zero row sums; the local return classification restricts its support to the empty graph, a triangle, or a four-cycle. The identity coefficient at the isolated negative type correctly eliminates a triangle. Section 5 supplies the decisive additional argument: the character annihilating the common center direction makes the degree-two blocks reciprocal after projection, while the Hermitian/skew-Hermitian anticommutation identity excludes degree-three lines parallel to that direction. Consequently the projected matrix really is a binary symmetric $\operatorname{SRG}(36,15,6,6)$, and the original graph really lies within the cyclic-cover class subsequently excluded.

The computational part provides exhaustive evidence rather than a failed construction search. The normalization in Section 6 uses admissible conjugations and permutations, and retaining all $3^4$ negative-cell markings preserves coverage after normalization. Explicit vertex permutations establish coverage by the twelve bases without relying on negative answers from an isomorphism program. The cover calculation allows independent phases on both directions of every base edge. Its spanning-tree gauge, full linear kernels, and polynomial identities therefore cover every cyclic cover required by the reduction.

I reproduced the complete supplied verification under the two-core and 2 GiB limits. Both enumerators recovered the same 226 normalized matrices; all 18,306 base identifications, twelve phase-space coverage checks, eleven constant contradictions, and 84,042 cubic-certificate coefficient equalities passed. The remaining four-dimensional domain yielded exactly 24 modular survivors, whose literal integer squares were

$$
A^2=(2J_{36}+3I_{36})\otimes J_3.
$$

Thus their mutual degree is five, not eleven. This final integer check is essential: modular feasibility alone would not establish the obstruction. I also independently recomputed the twelve raw phase nullities by ordinary elimination over $\mathbb F_3$, checked every stored weighing matrix's Hermitian orthogonality, and recovered the 216 rank-one-block and ten Hadamard-block counts. The separate nonextremal-block enumeration reproduced 462 initial states and no six-row extensions. These checks verify the retained certificates and finite domains; they do not independently rediscover the certificates or classify unrestricted target graphs.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 82, version 2 [5], already contains the characteristic-three phase equations, cubic-consequence method, and the same wrong-return mechanism for the Latin-square graph of $\mathbb Z_6$. Those ingredients are appropriately credited and should not be presented as new here. The present contribution is the structural route from physical return products to a conserved circulation, then to the complete forced base family, and its exclusion with unrestricted cyclic phases. That route is not supplied by Paper 82. Papers 84 and 85 establish different automorphism restrictions, respectively semiregularity of $3$-subgroups and exclusion of $C_3^3$; neither excludes the present $C_3^2$ construction. The external comparison also supports the stated distinction: the small-weight results of Best–Kharaghani–Ramp [3] and the known classification of the undirected bases [4] do not establish this directed-cover obstruction. The recent constructions in arXiv:2607.07381v2 and arXiv:2608.16202v1 concern other parameter families and do not resolve the degree-15 case.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The assumptions and theorem are explicit, the main text explains each transition in the proof, and the finite calculations are organized around their mathematical purpose. The abstract is informative and accurately limits the claim. The supplementary partial matrices are clearly distinguished from graphs satisfying the target equation. The manuscript does not require reconstruction of an unstated central argument.

The following minor improvements would help readers and reproducibility:

- Add Papers 84 and 85 to Section 2, explaining how the present restriction fits the remaining semiregular order-nine actions without assuming an extension to $C_3^3$.
- Include the nonextremal-block check in the main reproduction command, or document the additional invocation of `runs/enumerate` without arguments. The source supports the structural assertion in Section 6, and that invocation passes, but `reproduce.py` currently invokes only the full-enumeration mode.
- Document a resource-limited invocation using `OMP_THREAD_LIMIT=2`: the C++ rank routine explicitly requests five threads, so `OMP_NUM_THREADS` alone does not impose the stated review limit.
- Specify “degree-two pair” immediately before equation (4), where the subsequent coefficient total of four is used, and expand “MUB” at its first occurrence.

These are contextual and reproducibility clarifications, not defects in the established obstruction. Reference numbers [1]–[7] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
