# Accept

This is a focused supporting revision of the accepted contribution. The new paragraph in Section 8 supplies concrete examples outside the cyclic symmetry class studied by Theorem 1, and Section 9 adds checks for those examples. The classification theorem, its proof and its scope remain unchanged. The added evidence helps explain why excluding an automorphism of order $17$ does not settle existence at $(102,16,6,5,2)$. It does not introduce a separate substantive contribution requiring another submission. The main text remains organized around the original mathematical argument, with the graph data and programs in the supplement.

I independently checked all supplied new adjacency matrices using integer arithmetic, including binary entries, zero diagonal, both degree conditions and every ordered two-step count. An independent directed-graph individualization-and-refinement computation reproduced both the isomorphism-class counts and the full automorphism-group orders: the $58$ order-$44$ graphs are pairwise non-isomorphic, with orders $1,2,4,8,16$ occurring $45,5,3,1,4$ times respectively; the four order-$70$ graphs are pairwise non-isomorphic and rigid. Thus none of the order-$44$ examples admits an automorphism of order $11$. These checks reproduce the properties of the retained graphs, not the search runs that discovered them. I inspected the search implementations but did not rerun the heuristic searches.

The additions fit the existing context: paper 76 [4] already identifies the smaller members of $L(q)$ as existing while leaving the order-$102$ case open. Here the useful additional evidence concerns the symmetry of explicit examples at known feasible parameters. It provides no exhaustive classification or statistical estimate of the distribution of automorphism groups, and the manuscript correctly disclaims any nonexistence conclusion from unsuccessful searches.

Two minor clarifications would improve the revision:

1. In Section 8, replace “a group-imposed class is a thin slice of them” with a precise statement that the retained examples lie outside the order-$11$ symmetry class. “Most of the graphs found are rigid” is supported, but a tabu-search sample does not establish that rigid graphs predominate among all isomorphism classes. The appropriate conclusion at order $102$ is that Theorem 1 alone gives no unrestricted nonexistence conclusion.
2. Update the surrounding reproducibility prose. Section 9 still describes all programs as Python with no random seeds, whereas the new search programs are C and take seeds; the new checkers also require NumPy, pynauty or NetworkX. List tested dependency versions and the order-$70$ checking command. Retain search seeds, settings and budgets if the discovery runs are to be reproducible. Also qualify Section 8's statement that all other computations merely confirm results proved by hand: the new graph and automorphism data are additional computational evidence. These documentation issues do not undermine the independently checkable retained examples.
