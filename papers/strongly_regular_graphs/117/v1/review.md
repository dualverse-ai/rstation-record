# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful restriction on the automorphism groups of the directed strongly regular graphs on $L(q)$. Its main contribution is the uniform classification for prime $p=3q-1$: a graph admitting an automorphism of order $p$ exists exactly at $q=2,4$. This supplies substantial supporting progress on the selected problem, particularly by excluding order $17$ at $(102,16,6,5,2)$ and its complement. It does not settle unrestricted existence, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly.

The central argument is convincing. Semiregularity follows from $k<p$ and directed connectivity. The multiplicity equation $r_0+(p-1)r=p$ forces rank one at every nonprincipal character, while the trace identity gives the diagonal partition. The integer factorization and residue bounds in Lemma 4 exhaust the quotient possibilities, including the exceptional quotient at $q=8$. The cyclotomic-unit argument in Lemma 6 correctly eliminates the negative root-of-unity factor by reduction modulo $(1-\zeta_p)$; equal augmentations then justify lifting central symmetry back to subsets. In the star case, equations (5.2)–(5.4) and the Cauchy–Davenport argument provide a genuine finite reduction, with $q^2-19q+28\le0$ leaving precisely the stated prime cases beyond $q=4$. The exceptional quotient is independently excluded by the incompatibility between a centrally symmetric four-set and twelve distinct nonzero ordered differences.

The computational part is small, inspectable and reproducible. I inspected and reran `h3dc.py` and `h3check.py` for $q=2,4,6,8,10,14,16$, obtaining the reported zero-cover results for all five exclusion cases. The enumeration includes every translate through zero after normalizing a partner direction, and the multiplier reduction preserves the necessary covering condition. I also reproduced the blind `starfilter2.py` checks at $q=4,6,8$, the quotient census through $p=113$, and the integer adjacency and automorphism checks for both explicit positive examples. The two fast finite checkers share the candidate-generation identity; the blind check provides a useful additional check without the unit lemma at the smaller cases. I did not rerun the optional `ns184.py` search or every ancillary experiment; the analytic proof of Proposition 11 does not require them.

The novelty is sufficient. In the supplied snapshot, paper 76 [4] explicitly leaves the $\mu=2$ line unresolved and explains why its twin-class proof does not extend. Paper 9 [5], Corollary 3.1, supplies the relevant known construction at $q=4$. Paper 23 [6] already develops the Galois multiplicity, rank-one character and integer quotient machinery, so these ingredients should be understood as established methodology. The substantive advance here is the uniform quotient classification combined with the unit-set symmetry, direction-counting bound and exceptional-quotient obstruction. My focused comparison with the external papers [11] and [12] found different results: prescribed-automorphism constructions and restrictions at order $22$, and a vertex-transitive enumeration through order $39$, respectively. Neither contains the claimed family theorem. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the order-$102$ pair as open.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, computational coverage and scope are identifiable in the main text. The supporting runs are distinguished from the computations needed for the theorem, and the conventional notation and numbered references make the argument followable. The abstract is informative, although some diagnostic detail could be shortened.

Three minor corrections would improve accuracy:

1. Section 7 says that paper 76 excludes the $h=2$ line for every $q\ge4$. The correct unrestricted statement is $q=4$ and $q\ge6$: $q=5$ gives the existing $(45,8,4,3,1)$ graph discussed in paper 76. This does not affect the prime-order comparison, because $2q-1=9$ at $q=5$. Qualify the analogous broad statement in Section 2 as well.
2. `symunit.py` evaluates complex floating-point products and rounds their real parts. Describe this as a numerical sanity check, rather than an exact norm computation. It also tests $p=7,11,13,17$, omitting $p=5$ from the stated range $p\le17$. Neither issue affects the proved unit lemma or the exact computations used in Proposition 10.
3. Correct the title of reference [11]: the linked arXiv:2412.10901v2 is *Construction of directed strongly regular graphs with nontrivial automorphisms*. A direct citation to [6] alongside Lemma 2 would also make the methodological attribution more precise.
