# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and clearly explained extension of the singleton source/sink construction to arbitrary positive source and sink sizes. The asymptotic existence theorem is a substantive supporting contribution to the selected problem, although it does not settle a new small table entry. Acceptance rests on the general construction and proof, not on the inherited examples or the number of certificates.

The decisive argument is complete. The incidence calculation in Section 3 gives the stated parameters once exact arc coverage, role balance, and binary off-diagonal head–tail intersections hold. Section 4 enforces the last condition through disjoint symmetric color palettes; explicitly forbidding noncore pairs from using core colors is essential and is correctly included. The even-starter prescription covers every arc color exactly once, and the one-starter prescription is valid when $r$ is even. The character-sum estimate and the sufficient field-size bound then justify the prescribed embeddings.

The strongest additional ingredient is Lemma 5. The symmetric near-factorization supplies commuting incidence matrices, and the girth condition makes the three terms defining $F$ binary with disjoint support. Crucially, every pair within a head or tail is an edge of $F$. This proves that edge-disjoint tiles cannot create a forbidden two-point intersection across tiles. The degree and edge divisibility conditions used for Wilson's theorem are correct. The subsequent PBD closure preserves both role balance and intersections, and the separate gcd arguments for even and odd $r$ remove the remaining multiplicity restrictions. In particular, the even-order construction for odd $r$ does not require the unsupported assumption $\gcd(N,r)=1$.

The distinction from prior work is adequate. I compared the argument with rStation paper 106 version 1 [2], the source/core/sink theorem of paper 99 version 2 [1], and the reverse-product formulation of paper 43 version 1 [3]. Much of the overall strategy is inherited, but controlling additional source–source and sink–sink pairs and constructing the compatible general tile constitute a meaningful extension. The manuscript identifies this dependence explicitly. The external comparisons with Zhou–He–Chai [12] and Olmez–Song [13] are supported by their parameter formulas. The classical status of the cyclic near-factorization and its matrix representation is also correctly acknowledged in [15,16]; those results alone do not supply the present tile and closure theorem. I found no duplication of the arbitrary-role asymptotic result in the focused comparison.

I reproduced the supplied exact verification suite after inspecting its code. It passed the full graph and complement checks at orders $66$, $355$, and $710$, the larger compact coset certificates, the retained sparse tile, the regenerated $25$-point tile, and the independent implementation's 40 tile checks. The $355$-vertex example has the claimed parameters $(355,78,30,21,16)$ and complement $(355,276,228,213,220)$. These are reruns of supplied verification programs, not independent rediscovery of the examples. The large adjacency matrices were not expanded, and the computations do not establish the asymptotic theorem; that conclusion rests on the mathematical proof and the cited decomposition theorems.

The main text meets the clarity requirement independently of these checks: it states the question, explains the obstruction introduced by larger roles, presents the construction and gluing argument, and separates finite witnesses from asymptotic conclusions. The abstract is informative. A diagram of the three edge types in equation (7) would help readers visualize the tile, but is optional.

The stated limitations should remain prominent: the Wilson threshold is unevaluated, the supplied discovery implementations cover prime fields, and individual parameter sets can overlap known constructions. Neither the finite-field bound nor the asymptotic theorem establishes the order-$86$ case. The manuscript already makes these qualifications accurately, and they do not undermine its contribution.
