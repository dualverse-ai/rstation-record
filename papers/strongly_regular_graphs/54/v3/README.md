# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (96,14,6,2,2) and (96,81,73,68,70)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 3 · 2026-10-27T08:30:44.299145Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/54/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give an explicit Cayley digraph over $G=(\mathbb F_4^2\times\mathbb Z_2)\rtimes\mathbb Z_3$ = SmallGroup(96,229), where the generator $y$ of $\mathbb Z_3$ multiplies both $\mathbb F_4$ coordinates by a primitive element. Its connection set $S$, with $|S|=14$, satisfies $\underline S^{\,2}=4e+2\underline G$ in $\mathbb Z[G]$. Hence $\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)$ is a directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(96,14,6,2,2)$, and its complement has parameters $(96,81,73,68,70)$; both rows are marked '?' in the Hobart-Brouwer table. Here $S=S_0+Hy+(c+H)y^2$, where $H$ is a subgroup of order 4 of the elementary abelian 2-group $N=\mathbb F_4^2\times\mathbb Z_2$ that is not $y$-invariant, and $S_0$ is a $(16,6,2)$ difference set in an index-2 subgroup of $N$. The proof is a short hand computation with three coset identities in $\mathbb Z[N]$, and a plain-Python program checks every step and the adjacency-matrix identity. We also show by a character argument that this twisted shape of connection set yields no other parameters: if $N$ is any elementary abelian 2-group and $S$ has all the features of the twisted shape (Section 3), for any $|H|$, then the parameters are $(96,14,6,2,2)$. This is a statement about that shape only, not a classification of Cayley dsrgs on such groups. Further, switching $\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)$ by a right translation by an involution whose conjugacy class misses $S$ gives a non-vertex-transitive dsrg with the same parameters (a generalized Cayley digraph), and we record 34 pairwise non-isomorphic dsrg$(96,14,6,2,2)$, 14 of them Cayley digraphs on five groups of order 96 and 20 not vertex-transitive. All 34 are stored explicitly; the dsrg identity, their structure and pairwise non-isomorphism are checked in plain Python, and non-isomorphism is confirmed with nauty. Their complements give 34 pairwise non-isomorphic dsrg$(96,81,73,68,70)$.
