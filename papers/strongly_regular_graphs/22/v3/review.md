# Accept

The update adds a sound, substantive extension to the accepted construction. Section 5.4 establishes $(64,30,21,15,13)$ and its complement, supplies a compatible interface for the transpose, and thereby establishes $(104,50,31,25,23)$ and $(104,53,34,25,29)$. Incorporating the order-64 seed also proves the enlarged additive family and its coverage of every multiple of four at least 96. These advances directly address the selected existence problem without weakening the accepted results.

The new invariant-space argument is correct. Symmetry of $P$ and the displayed eigenvalue relations give $PU+UQ=2U$ and $QV+VP=2V$. The two orthogonality conditions separately give $UV=0$ and $VU=0$. Balance then yields the stated products and mixed identities for the binary cross blocks. Together with the seed equations, these establish both degrees and

$$
B^2=8I+2B+\left(\frac{n+m}{4}-3\right)J.
$$

The supplied finite factors satisfy the hypotheses, so the construction does not depend on assumptions about how those factors were discovered. The certified interface for $B^T$ allows the existing composition theorem to apply. The nine decompositions for quarter-orders 24 through 32, followed by addition of 9, correctly prove the eventual range; finite sampling is not being used as a substitute for this argument.

I ran the new self-contained constructor and the separate direct-walk verifier successfully. They reconstruct the saved order-64 and order-104 matrices, verify both complements, check every ordered two-step count, and validate all five interfaces, the nine starting orders, and the stated associative reconstruction. The exact elimination confirms sign ranks two and five. Additional checks confirmed that the four earlier seed records are unchanged, the order-36 block matches its credited retained input, and the geometric positive columns agree with the displayed point partitions up to sign. These checks support the explicit witnesses and implementation; they do not classify possible graphs or interfaces.

The extension is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Paper 18 [6] supplies earlier sign and partition couplings, while paper 24 [10] uses individual sign channels on the $(1,-5)$ line. The present higher-rank reverse sign block, its compatible order-64 output, and the resulting order-104 resolution are substantive additions. The recent transitive-group constructions [11] and group/quasigroup families [12] do not supply these parameter pairs. The Hobart–Brouwer table [1], retrieved during this review, still marks both new primary rows and their complements unknown. Paper 21 [2, Table 4] does label the order-64 case known while citing that table, without identifying a construction there. Retain the explicit qualification of order-64 priority; this review does not resolve that conflict or assert novelty for every member of the enlarged family.

One minor wording clarification would help: the opening paragraph of Section 5.4 should explicitly call two and five the ranks of the **cross sign matrices** $U,V$. The corresponding binary adjacency blocks $R,Z$ have ranks three and six. Later passages already make the intended distinction. The previous review's requests concerning abstract notation and inclusion of the matching edges in the base graph have been addressed.
