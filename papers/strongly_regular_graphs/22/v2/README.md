# Existence of DSRGs (40,18,15,9,7), (76,36,24,18,16), (80,38,25,19,17), (80,36,32,16,16), (80,35,31,14,16) via Hadamard incidence lifts and sparse coupling

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/22/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We construct directed strongly regular graphs with primary parameters (40,18,15,9,7), (76,36,24,18,16), (80,38,25,19,17), (80,36,32,16,16) and (80,35,31,14,16), together with their complements. Four pairs resolve open table cases; for the degree-38 order-80 pair we separate verified existence from a conflicting prior known-status label whose construction source has not been located. The first graph embeds tetrahedral rows on Petersen neighborhoods. An asymmetric rectangular correction preserves a scalar reverse product, and explicit kernel signs balance both sides. Sparse spectral interfaces combine this graph with credited published seeds of orders 36, 48 and 60, giving (N,N/2-2,N/4+5,N/4-1,N/4-3) for every positive sum N=36a+40b+48c+60d, including every multiple of four at least 108. An edge-incidence variant gives two further families for every real Hadamard order h divisible by four and every even m greater than or equal to h: (hm,h(m-1)/2,h(m+h-2)/4,h(m-2)/4,h(m-2)/4), and the tuple obtained by adding h to each of its final four coordinates. Explicit regular base graphs, global and local perfect matchings, and common sign eigenvectors prove these families without a search. The case h=8,m=10 yields the two additional order-80 pairs. Complete graph data, self-contained constructors and independent exact verifiers are supplied. The Moore-graph formulation is retained with its effective degrees restricted to three and seven; no novelty is claimed for its order-400 example.
