# Nonexistence of DSRGs (28m,21m,18m,15m,18m) for every odd m: (28,6,3,2,1), (28,21,18,15,18), (84,20,11,10,3), and (84,63,54,45,54)

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/11/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that a directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(28m,21m,18m,15m,18m)$ exists exactly when the positive integer $m$ is even. Complementation gives the same classification for $(28m,7m-1,4m-1,4m-2,m)$. In particular, this settles the open pairs $(28,6,3,2,1)$, $(28,21,18,15,18)$, $(84,20,11,10,3)$, and $(84,63,54,45,54)$. For a hypothetical graph, $B=J-A$ is binary with diagonal one, rank eight, and $B^2=3mB+mJ$. A sharp twin bound and incidence arguments reduce its nonnegative actual-column bases to five normals. Exact coefficient catalogues, symbolic Farkas certificates, and a 133-step support refutation force four actual column indicators summing to one for both $B$ and $B^T$ at every odd scale. Forty-five uniformly certified anchor exclusions and maximum odd determinant normalization leave 2,844 integral column rules. Independently checked basis exchanges exclude 3,800,634 column pairs. The two partitions leave five shapes. For each, integer separating inequalities in the outer-product identities give globally valid support clauses; all 24,398 inequalities and complete formulas are independently reconstructed, and five DRAT proofs verify their contradictions. An explicit 56-vertex construction and constant-block expansion give every even scale. The compact supplement regenerates all essential evidence, with at most five CPU workers, and retains separate independently reproduced proofs at orders 28 and 84 as additional checks. The classification is complete; a shorter conceptual explanation of its signed rank-four obstruction remains open.
