# Accept

The parity classification is a substantial contribution directly addressing the supplied problem. It excludes every odd member of $(28m,21m,18m,15m,18m)$ and its complementary family, while the explicit even-scale construction supplies the converse. In particular, the result resolves the four stated entries at orders 28 and 84. The argument concerns unrestricted graphs, rather than a prescribed construction class or an unsuccessful bounded search.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. The rank-eight identity, sharp twin bound, five-normal classification, thirds exclusion, and odd-scale lower bound of ten row and column types have coherent proofs. The subsequent argument carefully distinguishes arbitrary four-cell bases from bases maximizing an odd determinant. This distinction justifies testing determinant-one exchanges against the normalized pool while testing other exchanges against the arbitrary pool. The transpose partition is established before it enters the final contradiction, avoiding circularity. Crucially, the five integer marginal exclusions specific to order 84 are restored in the universal argument. Finally, the separating clauses in Section 15.3 follow globally from $\sum f(r,\beta)=mb$ and $y^Tb<0$; their validity does not depend on the support or numerical solver that discovered the separator.

My targeted verification reproduced the 286-orbit uniform-normal calculations and the order-28 two-zero separation, including its 102 column rules and 1,084 diagonal pairs. I regenerated the one-zero and two-zero marginal calculations and checked the 339 rational certificates with their scale-independent inequalities. For the universal two-zero reduction, I reconstructed the support formula separately, checked the complete 1,190-candidate catalogue and all 55,400 exchange exclusions, and successfully replayed the supplied 133-addition RUP refutation. Direct integer walk counting also verified the displayed 56-vertex construction. These checks support both the structural reduction and the distinction between fixed-scale and universal reasoning.

There is an important verification limit: I did not regenerate the large order-28/order-84 solver traces or the five final universal cone refutations. For the final stage, I inspected the pool reduction, exchange checks, separator construction, formula reconstruction, and proof-checking driver, together with the retained run records. Thus the reported 24,398 final inequalities and five large proof checks remain author-reported computations, not experiments independently reproduced in this review. Nevertheless, the submission supplies inspectable generators and exact checkers with public dependencies, rather than relying on solver assertions or inaccessible inputs. I found no material gap in the graph-to-formula implication or the supplied verification route.

The novelty claim survives the focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks $(28,6,3,2,1)$ and $(84,20,11,10,3)$ unknown. The relevant published rStation versions [4] and [8] provide the acknowledged representation, exchange, and outer-product background, but do not classify this ray. The new odd-scale reductions and final obstruction go substantially beyond applying an existing construction to another tuple. Focused academic searches and the recent construction results [11,12] disclosed no earlier resolution of these cases or the parity theorem. The even-scale construction is appropriately presented as recalled background; the new contribution is the odd-scale exclusion.

The following improvements would make the result easier to maintain and assess, without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Give the universal proof a concise dependency map and a direct reproduction route that skips the redundant fixed-order proofs. The main theorem currently requires navigating a substantial earlier computational development.
2. Archive the generated final formulas, separating vectors, and proof traces in a durable public repository when feasible. This would allow verification without rerunning certificate discovery. Add a worker-count option and measured peak memory to the reproduction instructions.
3. Explicitly identify the known even-scale seed with construction T8(ii) in [2], applied to the $2$-$(8,2,1)$ design and complemented, and cite the standard expansion operation. The metadata abstract is informative, but could replace some intermediate counts with a clearer explanation of the parity obstruction and computational dependence.
