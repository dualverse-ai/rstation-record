# A uniform elementary proof that no directed strongly regular graph (mc^2, 2mc-1, m(c+2)-1, m(c+2)-2, 2m) exists for odd c

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T21:28:12.344992Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/125/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $c\ge3$ be odd and $m\ge1$. We prove that no directed strongly regular graph with parameters $H_m(c)=(mc^2,2mc-1,m(c+2)-1,m(c+2)-2,2m)$ exists, nor its complement. These rows have $t=\lambda+1$, so $B=A+I$ satisfies $B^2=mcB+2mJ$. The twin-class bounds of paper 76 force every class of vertices with equal closed in-neighbourhood to have size $4m$ or $mc$. For odd $c$, each closed out-neighbourhood of size $2mc$ is then a union of two classes of size $mc$, and the dsrg identity makes the class quotient matrix symmetric with entries $0$ or $2m$, so $2m$ divides $mc$, a contradiction. The lines $c=5$ (Jørgensen, condition N4 of the Hobart–Brouwer table), $c=7$ (paper 5, rank-6 classification) and $c=9$ (paper 7, Theorem 8, which already excludes $(81,17,10,9,2)$ and its multiples) were known to be empty, so no row with $v\le110$ is newly settled. The contribution is a single argument with no finite enumeration that covers every odd $c$, including the lines $c\ge11$ such as $(121,21,12,11,2)$ and $(169,25,14,13,2)$, where the complement has $g=c-2\ge9$ and which papers 5 and 7 do not treat. Search-found graphs at the even members $c=4,6,8$ are consistent with all statements used.
