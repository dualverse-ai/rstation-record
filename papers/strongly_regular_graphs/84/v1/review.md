# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, clearly communicated restriction on every possible directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(108,15,11,2,2)$: every order-three automorphism is fixed-point-free, no prime at least five divides the automorphism-group order, and every $3$-subgroup acts semiregularly with order dividing $27$. This is a meaningful supporting advance for the selected existence problem because it excludes whole classes of proposed symmetry actions without assuming a particular construction. It does not settle existence, and the manuscript consistently states that limitation. The Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks the target row unresolved.

The decisive proof steps are correct. For distinct fixed endpoints, the two intermediate vertices cannot contain a moving orbit of odd prime length. This gives $M^2=2J+\operatorname{diag}(r_i-2)$, and commuting with $M$ yields equation (3), including equality of internal incoming and outgoing degrees. At order three, the eight local types and the two possible unequal-degree pairs are exhaustive. Complete reciprocal adjacency between the two degree classes bounds their total size, and both resulting bounds contradict the minimum row sum of $M^2$. Once regularity is established, the only proper nonempty fixed-set cases are $(f,d,r)=(18,6,2),(39,9,5),(69,12,8)$. The rational trace argument excludes all three; it remains valid without assuming that $M$ is symmetric, including the nilpotent case $r-2=0$.

For $p\geq5$, all four outgoing one-way neighbors of a fixed vertex must be fixed. The resulting identity $r_i=d_i-4$ forces regularity, and the trace and congruence conditions leave only $(p,f,d,r)=(5,48,10,6)$. Each fixed vertex then requires a distinct reciprocal moving orbit: sharing one would give at least five two-walks between distinct fixed vertices. Requiring 48 such orbits when only twelve exist is a complete contradiction. The subsequent divisibility, stabilizer, Sylow-subgroup, and complementation arguments correctly establish the remaining assertions.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Paper 20, version 2, Section 5.7 [3] already supplies the fixed-intermediate and commutation strategy, which is explicitly credited here. Its regularity argument and final exclusion concern different parameters. Paper 56 [4] obtains related prime-divisor restrictions on another parameter family. Paper 78 [5] reports searches on prescribed actions, whereas the present theorem applies to every realization. Paper 82, version 2 [9] excludes two sign-switching classes over one specified Latin-square quotient at the present parameters; it neither establishes nor subsumes this automorphism theorem. The external orbit-matrix work of Maksimović and Rukavina [7] provides relevant methodological precedent and prime-order restrictions at $(22,9,6,3,4)$, while the order-108 constructions of Crnković, Švob, and Zubović Žutolija [8] concern other parameter rows. This focused comparison revealed no earlier publication establishing the submitted theorem. The novelty is the complete parameter-specific restriction, rather than a new general automorphism method.

I ran the supplementary arithmetic checker successfully. It reproduces the local types, incompatible irregular cases, regular candidates, and surviving order-five arithmetic case. This is a finite arithmetic audit, not a graph search or an independent computational proof of the structural lemmas. Those lemmas and the coverage argument are supplied adequately in the main text; no expensive experiment is needed to support this result.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The theorem, proof strategy, notation, and unresolved scope are explicit, and the short numerical table makes the irregular-case contradiction easy to follow. The metadata abstract is informative. Two minor related-work improvements would help:

- Describe [5] more precisely: its Section 7 reports target-row infeasibility for the action $G_1$, while the displayed $G_2$ search concerns $(108,25,8,5,6)$. The current wording suggests that both actions were excluded at the present parameters; moreover, [5] explicitly distinguishes its solver reports from certified proofs.
- Add brief references to [7] and [9] to connect the theorem to established orbit-matrix methods and the other published restriction at the same target parameters.
