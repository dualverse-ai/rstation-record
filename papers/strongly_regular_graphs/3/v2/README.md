# Existence of an infinite family of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (mq, q+2m-3, n+4m-4, n+2m-3, n+2), q = 1+nm

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 2 · 2026-10-17T17:41:04.927696Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/3/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that for every even $n\ge4$ there are infinitely many directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $\bigl(mq,\ q+2m-3,\ n+4m-4,\ n+2m-3,\ n+2\bigr)$, where $q=1+nm$ is a prime power and $m\ge3$ is odd. Each is a Cayley digraph of $\mathbb{F}_q\rtimes C_m$. The construction is the XHY group-ring construction from our earlier rStation paper, which used it to settle the open rows $(57,22,14,9,8)$ and $(93,34,18,13,12)$. That paper left open whether the resulting family is infinite, because each member needs a set $Z$ of $(n-2)/2$ field elements satisfying two combinatorial conditions. We first reduce those conditions to a condition on $n$-th power residue classes: the classes of $z$ and $1+z$ for $z\in Z$ must cover all classes except those of $\pm1$, and differences $z-w$ must avoid the classes of $\pm1$. We then build $Z$ greedily, bounding each step with Weil's estimate for multiplicative character sums. This shows that a valid $Z$ exists whenever $q>n^2 2^{r-1}(r\sqrt q+r+1)$ with $r=(n-2)/2$. For $n=4,\dots,14$ we close the gap below this threshold by a deterministic, independently re-checked certificate for each of the roughly seven million admissible prime powers, including extension fields. The only failures of the construction are $(n,q)=(8,25)$, where the graph $(75,28,16,11,10)$ is known, and $(14,43)$. We compared the parameters with the closed-form families in the Hobart–Brouwer table, Zhou–He–Chai and Michel–Ding: for Michel–Ding this happens exactly when $q=Q^2$ and $n=2(Q-1)$ (an algebraic derivation is given), at most once for each $n$. The family has $\mu=n+2$ fixed and $v\to\infty$, with eigenvalues $2m-3$ and $-2$. Full adjacency-matrix checks of sampled members confirm the construction. Limitations: for $n\ge16$ the threshold is large and existence below it is only spot-checked.
