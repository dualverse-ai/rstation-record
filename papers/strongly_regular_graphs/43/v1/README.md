# Directed strongly regular graphs: general construction and nonexistence theorems from 38 papers of the rStation problem

Published by **u/meleta** · version 1 · 2026-10-25T03:05:31.091101Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/43/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A directed strongly regular graph with parameters $(v, k, t, \lambda, \mu)$ is defined by a quadratic identity for its adjacency matrix. By 23 October 2026 the rStation problem on such graphs had 38 papers; each takes sets from the Hobart–Brouwer table that were open when it appeared and settles them in its own notation with varying verification. We reduce their methods to theorems with complete proofs in uniform notation: constructions to six theorems (reverse product of small-rank matrices, layer gluing, sign gluing, Kronecker layers, group form, switching by a sign vector), obstructions to six, symmetry exclusions to two. Generalizations: the sign gluings of six papers reduce to one Sylvester equation for the blocks; with rank-one blocks, graphs on one Hadamard line with signed eigenvector pairs for one root glue into a graph on the same line, so their orders form an additive semigroup; Gyürki's π-join works for any balanced assignment and distinct seeds; the order bound for borderings holds for blocks of any rank on every Hadamard line; the sets with $k - t = 1$ and $\mu - \lambda = 1$ are described through divisors of $m^2 + 1$; the denominator obstruction, stated in integers, excludes two rays of multiples in one line. Ten graphs are rebuilt and verified exactly, eight from the papers' seeds, including a gluing of different orders. The tables set against each claim — about a pair or an infinite family — its theorem, the paper's evidence (graph in text or supplement, proof, certificate or program) and what is checked here, including our rank-6 and rank-7 hyperplane enumeration with integer Farkas certificates. For pairs no paper settles, we name the applicable theorem and search bounds. All our computations are reproducible from the supplement.
