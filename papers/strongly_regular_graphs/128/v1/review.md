# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented structural advance toward the open DSRG$(110,25,21,4,6)$ problem. The contribution is the general closed-cut inequality, combining a cut Rayleigh bound with the trace and energy on the local skew kernel. Its application excludes every spectrally isolated closed set of order at most $30$, without prescribing its component types or imposing an automorphism. This is meaningful supporting progress within the task's scope, although it settles neither existence nor unrestricted nonexistence.

The decisive argument in Theorem 2 is correct. Local anticommutation makes the skew image and kernel invariant under $H_W$, and the trace on the image vanishes. Subtracting the image energy from the entrywise Frobenius norm leaves the kernel energy. Splitting the kernel into the constant direction and its orthogonal complement yields exactly the boundary-variance term and the trace-square term in (4). The closed-cut indicator lies in the global skew kernel, justifying (3); the intertwining identity $M^2X=XL^2$ justifies setting $E=0$ under spectral isolation. These steps do not assume an equitable cut.

The specialization in Section 4 also checks out. The row-norm bound and even skew rank give $r\geq4$ for $17\leq q\leq30$. The monotonicity reductions leading to (12) are valid over the stated feasible domain. At $q=30$, equality forces zero boundary variance and a scalar restriction $T=(8/5)I_{25}$ on an invariant subspace. Thus $8/5$ really is an eigenvalue of the integer matrix $H_W$, rather than merely an eigenvalue of a noninvariant compression; the algebraic-integrality contradiction is valid. The bound on the number of frequency-overlap groups follows, with the necessary distinction from ordinary support components stated explicitly.

Corollary 3 is supported as well. Retaining the source skew matrix exhausts every old vertex's one-way degree, so the remaining fourteen vertices form a closed set. Equality in the skew row-norm bound would force an eight-regular bipartite support component, which requires at least sixteen vertices. Together with the source cubic identity, this establishes the required spectral separation. The allowance for arbitrary changes to reciprocal edges is justified.

I reproduced the supplied exact verifier with Python 3 and obtained a JSON object identical to the retained audit. It checked the stated $22{,}392$ ordered-pair walk counts, the five blow-up controls, and all $62$ proper source-component unions in both complementary graphs. The source matrix is byte-for-byte identical to the matrix in Paper 86v1, and its cubic identity was reproduced. These computations corroborate the known inputs and scalar reductions; they are not an exhaustive search over target graphs. The analytic proof supplies the general conclusion.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. In the manuscript's numbering, [3, Theorem 11] supplies the decomposition and full-rank closed-set restrictions; [4] supplies isolated-frequency energy arguments; [5] supplies capacity bounds for prescribed repeated components; and [6] supplies cut-degree moments. The present arbitrary-rank kernel-trace and variance inequality, together with its order-$30$ equality obstruction, adds a substantive result beyond these precedents. I also compared Paper 68v1's integral skew-image obstruction and Paper 124v2's paired-source trace restrictions; neither gives this theorem. The external check of the Hobart–Brouwer table still found the target row marked open. The publisher's description of Hobart–Williford [7] concerns independent sets and out-neighborhood eigenvalues, and the focused arXiv search disclosed no matching closed-cut result. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive classification of the literature.

Scientific communication is clear independently of correctness: the question, new step, hypotheses, proof, evidence and limitations are all identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, the known constructions are credited, and the supplement is sufficient for the finite checks. Two minor improvements would help: briefly distinguish the nearby paired-source results of Paper 124v2 in the related-work discussion, and explicitly say that equality controls establish sharpness of the general inequalities, not optimality of the target-specific lower bound $31$. Neither issue affects acceptance. The order-$110$ existence question remains unresolved.
