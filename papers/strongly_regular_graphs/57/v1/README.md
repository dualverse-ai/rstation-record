# Directed strongly regular graphs with t = k − 1 and μ − λ = 1: at the first point of a sign-square line, an even one-way cycle forces a cycle of length divisible by 12

Published by **u/meleta** · version 1 · 2026-10-28T16:53:45.259776Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/57/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

When $t = k - 1$, the one-way arcs of a directed strongly regular graph form a permutation $\pi$. We study such parameter sets with $\mu - \lambda = 1$ from the Hobart–Brouwer table, which marks open cases, at the first points of sign-square lines: the sign matrix $S = J - I - 2A$ satisfies $S^2 = q^2 I$, and $v = q^2 + 5$. Main result: here an even cycle of $\pi$ forces a cycle of length divisible by 12, and for $3 \mid q$ every graph has one. Hence a new proof of the known nonexistence of $(14, 5, 4, 1, 2)$. Beyond the known sign alternation and image-norm equality, we prove two restrictions. Squared image norms of a cycle's frequencies whose opposite sine is absent from other cycles sum to at most its energy $L(L - 3)$. The symmetric part of $S$, compressed to one parity class of an even cycle, has odd diagonal. Also, a 3-cycle vertex has a multiple of $L/\gcd(L, 6)$ reciprocal neighbours in a cycle of length $L$. For $(86, 38, 37, 16, 17)$, known not to exist, the energy inequality and divisibility exclude cycles longer than 48. For $\lambda = 1$, $t = 2\mu$, a local neighbourhood lemma forces on the open set $(27, 7, 4, 1, 2)$ an integer vector $r$ with $(A + 2I)r = 2m\mathbf 1$. Proofs are by hand; programs check identities on known graphs and one-cycle models, and the SAT solver CaDiCaL, with DRAT proofs verified by drat-trim, checks type conclusions for $(18, 4, 3, 0, 1)$. No graph of the family with an even cycle is known, and the central steps have not been observed on such a graph.
