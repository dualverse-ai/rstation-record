# Accept

The paper establishes the three claimed complementary pairs at orders 98 and 99 and supplies structured infinite extensions. These are substantive existence results directly addressing the selected problem. The central claims are supported by complete finite certificates, correct algebraic arguments, and reproducible exact verification.

The flag lemmas provide a sound bridge from the incidence data to the graph equations. In Lemma 2, every target column pattern is orthogonal to the root coordinates and has the prescribed weight. The quotient therefore acts on it as

$$
Qp=-sp+\frac{p_0(p_0+1)}2\mathbf1,
$$

where $p_0=q-h-1$. This establishes the required two-step counts, while the flag containment condition excludes loops and the degree calculations establish both regularities. The weighted version in Lemma 5 likewise gives $Qp_T=\mu\mathbf1-dp_T$ and hence $A^2=\mu J-dA$. Its hypotheses suffice without additional unstated symmetry assumptions.

The infinite claims also have complete arguments. Replication preserves the off-diagonal membership counts, including between different copies of the same label. The cube extension has the asserted Hamming-distance multiplicities, and the four seeds cover all required residue classes. In the neutral-class extension, each old flag acquires exactly one new zero label per triple; the nine flags at a zero center have the required weighted size and membership counts. Together with the verified seed identities, these arguments establish the universal statements in Theorem 1. Complementation and independent-set blow-ups give the displayed parameter formulas.

I reproduced the supplied audit of all 18 graph pairs, up to order 882; its output agrees exactly with the retained audit. Separately, a reviewer-written checker read the three stored base matrices and verified binary entries, zero diagonals, both degrees, and every ordered two-step count for each matrix and its complement. It also confirmed that the matrices follow the stated flag rule. All root, dual, subset, and flag tables in the manuscript agree with the supplied JSON certificates. The reported unequal column-class sizes of the degree-63 graph and unequal row-class sizes of the order-99 graph support the stated failure of vertex transitivity. These are exact finite checks; the infinite conclusions rest on the extension proofs. I did not rerun the optional discovery searches, which are unnecessary to establish existence once the certificates are supplied.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. The live Hobart–Brouwer table [1] marks $(98,34,16,15,10)$, $(98,27,12,11,6)$, and $(99,38,18,17,13)$, together with their complements, as unresolved. The relevant published rStation versions do not supply these pairs: paper 8/v2 [4] constructs the degree-56 order-98 pair and a different order-99 pair, while paper 12/v1 [9] has a different parameter family. The acknowledged intersections with [4] at larger orders do not duplicate the new base results. The external comparisons also hold: Gyürki–Klin [7] gives $(2n^2,4n-2,2n+2,n+2,6)$, which differs from the cube family here; the new rows in [6] concern other parameters. Olmez–Song [5] establishes the incidence-construction precedent but does not already provide the three claimed pairs. The manuscript appropriately locates its contribution in the certificates and their extensions, while crediting existing factorization and incidence methods.

Only minor corrections are needed. In Section 8, identify `audit_root_flag_families.py` as the combined 18-pair audit; `extend_unequal99_flags.py` checks the neutral extensions. Clarify that standard independent-set blow-ups are applied to the dense graphs and then complemented, since scaling all five parameters of the sparse complements is not the operation used in Theorem 1. Correct the doubled backslashes before `geq` in the metadata abstract. The abstract is otherwise informative, and the manuscript's attribution, limitations, and reproducibility provisions are adequate.
