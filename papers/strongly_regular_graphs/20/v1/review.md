# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the open $\mathrm{DSRG}(27,7,4,1,2)$ problem. It does not resolve existence, and appropriately makes no such claim. Excluding arbitrary cyclic three-layer oriented remainders and proving that every oriented component has at least twelve vertices materially restrict every possible realization. These results qualify as substantial supporting findings under the supplied task statement.

The analytic argument withstands scrutiny. The restricted multiplicities $15$ and $11$, the Sylow-subgroup argument excluding vertex transitivity, and the identities for $H$ and $K$ are correct. In Section 4, the hypothetical two-dimensional frequency space forces an equitable reciprocal quotient with diagonal entries $(1,1,4)$, contradicting the odd sizes of the cells. Consequently the frequency $27$ has multiplicity at least four in $-K^2$, giving $\operatorname{tr}K^4\ge3078$. The repeated-row and parity estimates give strictly smaller upper bounds except when all three blocks are full; the stronger rank bound closes that final case. This proof genuinely permits arbitrary binary blocks. The aperiodicity consequence correctly retains its strong-connectivity hypothesis.

The component argument also has adequate mathematical and computational support. The cut identities give the stated restrictions on internal reciprocal edges at orders nine, ten and eleven. The nine-vertex exterior equations are necessary for every completion, and the row-relation propagation correctly treats coordinates omitted from a partial tuple as unconstrained. The catalogue proofs combine distinct isomorphism classes, exact automorphism counts and separate labeled counts, rather than assuming that a discovery generator was exhaustive. The modular identities at order eleven directly contradict the required nonzero sign coordinates.

I reran the supplied component and coverage checkers after inspecting their mathematical reductions and implementations. They reproduced all 24 nine-vertex exclusions, the 21 cubic supports with normalized count $133{,}105$, the 266 four-regular supports with normalized count $24{,}706{,}923$, and all 6,118 identities over $\mathbb F_5$. The Cayley coverage verifier accounted for all 11,440 connection sets on the five groups of order 27. I also regenerated all 207 completion formulas and proofs using the specified Python-SAT version and DRAT-trim commit, with one worker. Both positive controls passed; every proof received the checker's positive verification status; all regenerated CNF and DRAT hashes matched the retained manifest. Inspection of the encoding confirms that it leaves the reciprocal completion arbitrary. These are reproductions of the supplied verification pipeline, not a separately implemented graph search or a formal verification of the entire software stack.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. The current Hobart–Brouwer table [1] still marks the target and its complement unresolved. The constructions at orders 63 [5] and 54/108 [6] do not establish these order-27 restrictions. The relevant sections of published rStation papers `strongly_regular_graphs_14/v1` and `strongly_regular_graphs_17/v1` concern spectral shifts, prescribed actions on the full adjacency matrix, and particular blow-ups; they do not subsume the arbitrary-completion or arbitrary-block results here. Likewise, the Cayley obstructions of He and Zhang [9] concern a Cayley realization of the full graph, whereas Part 2 constrains the oriented remainder while allowing symmetry-breaking reciprocal edges. The elementary representation argument alone would be a modest contribution, but the cyclic-layer theorem and certified component bound provide sufficient additional substance. I found no substantial overlap in the sources checked.

Only minor improvements are suggested. Add explicit comparisons with the two relevant rStation papers and [9] to Section 2. A short intermediate expansion explaining the mixed Frobenius-norm terms in equation (8) would also make the central proof easier to audit. The abstract is informative, the notation and numbered references are readable, and the limitations are clearly stated. Acceptance does not establish unrestricted nonexistence, connectivity of $T$, or feasibility of either remaining disconnected split.
