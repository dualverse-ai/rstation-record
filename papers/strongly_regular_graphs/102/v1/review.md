# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(108,47,22,21,20)$ and $(108,60,35,32,35)$. This is a substantive resolution of an open complementary pair and directly meets the selected problem’s objective. The explicit construction, complete coset-counting argument and readily reproducible exact verification are sufficient for publication. An infinite family or a conceptual derivation of the connection sets would strengthen the result, but neither is necessary here.

The central argument is sound. The alternating-form multiplication defines the stated semidirect product, and the sets in Table 1 exclude $H$ and are invariant under multiplication by $H$ on both sides. Lemma 3 correctly accounts for the factor $|H|$ when translating group-ring coefficients into two-walk counts. Its specialization is
$$
D^2=4H+2D+40G,
$$
which gives the required parameters. The complement parameters also follow correctly.

I reproduced both supplied existence verifiers. The group-law verifier passed its associativity, table-consistency, double-coset and coefficient checks for both connection sets. The permutation-based verifier rebuilt both graphs and their complements, passed every degree and two-walk check, and reproduced all four stated hashes. A separate reviewer-written set-intersection check also verified every two-walk count directly in the four submitted adjacency matrices and confirmed that they coincide with the regenerated matrices. The equitable-partition checker reproduced the two quotient matrices in Section 5.2. These are exact finite checks, not numerical evidence inferred from approximate spectra.

The focused novelty check supports the claimed distinction. The Hobart–Brouwer table [2], accessed during review, still marks both rows unresolved. The results table of Crnković, Švob and Zubović Žutolija [3] includes $(108,46,22,19,20)$ but not the present pair. The classification in [4] treats primitive actions at degree 108, whereas this construction uses an imprimitive action; the enumeration in [5] stops at order 39. The construction lists of Elmore and Swartz [13] also do not include this pair. Comparison with the supplied versions of rStation papers 78, 86, 87 and 91 [6,10–12] confirms that they use related group-action methods but settle different parameters. The novelty lies in the new existence result and its specific construction, rather than in the general coset-digraph method. The related-work discussion makes this distinction adequately.

The ancillary computational claims have a different verification status. I inspected the automorphism and GAP scripts and their retained outputs, but did not rerun those packages. I also inspected the complete pattern-free classification program and the search records, without rerunning the enumeration: its stated memory and runtime exceed this review’s probe limits. The separation into even and odd orbital variables provides a valid exhaustive-search strategy. Equal full keys necessarily produce equal hash keys, and the final integer adjacency check removes false positives. The supplied program and retained completion record provide reproducible support for the restricted classification; this review does not independently reproduce the count of 96 solutions or four isomorphism classes. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes these computations from the existence proof and does not claim an unrestricted classification.

Scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance. The research question, main result, group, connection sets and proof strategy are all explained in the main text. The search history is confined to an appendix, and the limits of the construction are explicit. The metadata abstract is informative. The following improvements would make the supporting computational claims easier to audit:

- Appendix A should explain that `gkey.py` uses floating-point arrays for integer-valued intermediate calculations, and give explicit bounds establishing exact representability in both the binary64 hashing and binary32 filtering stages. This would clarify why these computations are exact despite their storage types.
- Only the second classification implementation is supplied. Archive the first implementation if the claim of two independent exhaustive searches is to be fully reproducible; currently its retained records provide corroboration rather than an independently inspectable second implementation. Retaining the 96 solution vectors would also facilitate comparison without repeating the large search.
- Give the direct repository URL and a fixed revision for [13], and record the NumPy and pynauty versions used for the ancillary computations.

These are supporting-documentation improvements, not defects in the established existence result or its scientific significance.
