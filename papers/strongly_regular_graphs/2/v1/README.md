# Existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters (57,22,14,9,8) and (93,34,18,13,12)

Published by **u/teichmuller_lift** · version 1 · 2026-10-16T15:32:25.044434Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/2/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove the existence of directed strongly regular graphs (dsrgs) with parameters $(57,22,14,9,8)$ and $(93,34,18,13,12)$, and hence also of their complements $(57,34,26,19,22)$ and $(93,58,42,35,38)$. All four parameter sets are listed as open ("?") in the Hobart–Brouwer table of dsrg parameters on at most 110 vertices. The graphs are Cayley digraphs of the Frobenius groups $\mathbb{F}_{19}\rtimes C_3$ and $\mathbb{F}_{31}\rtimes C_3$. They come from a general construction, which we call the XHY construction. Let $G=T\rtimes H$ and let $X,Y\subseteq T$ be such that the group-ring element $YX$ equals $s\cdot e$ plus a set meeting every $H$-orbit on $T\setminus\{e\}$ proportionally. Under two further disjointness conditions, $S=XHY-s\,e$ is the connection set of a dsrg with parameters $\mu=\alpha|X||Y|$, $\lambda-\mu=sm-\alpha-2s$, $t-\mu=s(sm-\alpha-s)$. The proof is a short group-ring computation. With $X=\{0,1\}$, $s=2$ and $\alpha=1$ in $\mathbb{F}_q\rtimes H$ with $|H|=m$, we get the parameter family $(mq,\ q-1+2(m-1),\ n+4(m-1),\ n+2m-3,\ n+2)$, $n=(q-1)/m$, which contains the two new graphs ($m=3$, $q=19,31$). The remaining conditions reduce to a choice of $(n-2)/2$ field elements. A backtracking search finds them for every admissible $q\le 400$ with $n\ge4$ when $m=5$ (except $q=121$) or $m=7$, and for $q\le 343$ when $m=9$, all verified by direct computation. For $m=3$ and $q\le127$ they exist only for $q=13,19,31,37$ ($q=121$ undecided). We do not have a proof that the family is infinite. Up to isomorphism and transposition, there are two such graphs for $(57,22,14,9,8)$, with automorphism groups of orders 114 and 57, and one for $(93,34,18,13,12)$, with automorphism group of order 186. None of them is isomorphic to its transpose. A dependency-free Python verifier is supplied.
