# Existence of DSRG(64, 22, 18, 10, 6) and DSRG(64, 41, 37, 24, 30) via Coordinate-Cross Wrap-Around Difference Pairs, Spectral-Shift Column-Basis Duality, and Abelian Orbit Obstructions

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/strongly_regular_graphs/papers/14/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We resolve the open directed strongly regular graph parameter pair $\mathrm{DSRG}(64, 22, 18, 10, 6)$ and its complement $\mathrm{DSRG}(64, 41, 37, 24, 30)$ ($t - \mu = 12 \ne 0$) in Brouwer and Hobart's catalog, and establish the surrounding spectral-shift and semiregular abelian orbit obstruction theory. First, for any DSRG with restricted integer eigenvalues $r > 0 > s$, the spectral shifts $M_\sigma := A - \sigma I_v$ ($\sigma \in \{r, s\}$) have rank $n_\sigma \in \{g+1, f+1\}$ and satisfy $M_\sigma^2 = (\tau - \sigma) M_\sigma + \mu J_v$. Any column basis $Z = M_\sigma[:, \mathcal{J}] \in \{0, 1, -\sigma\}^{v \times n_\sigma}$ admits a unique coprime integer factorization $D M_\sigma = Z B^T$ and hyperplane dual $Z \alpha = L \mathbf{1}_v$ (Theorem 2.2). Exhaustive $2^{n_s}$ pivot enumeration (Proposition 2.3) shows that binary vectors in $\operatorname{colspace}(M_s)$ suffer a strict rank defect on $\mathrm{DSRG}(36, 16, 14, 8, 6)$ ($\dim = 4 < 10$) but span the full carrier on $\mathrm{DSRG}(64, 22, 18, 10, 6)$ ($\dim = 14 = 14$). Second, for semiregular abelian actions with $m = 1 + |s|/r$ coclique orbits, $\operatorname{Tr}(\hat{A}(\chi)) = 0$ forces $\operatorname{rank}(\hat{A}(\chi) - r I_m) = 1$ ($\forall \chi \ne 1$) and $m_1 = ((m-1)|s| - k)/(r-s)$. Auditing all 1,094 open catalog entries ($v \le 110$) yields 45 parameter sets in this regime; Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 obstruct 44 of the 45 while isolating $\mathrm{DSRG}(64, 41, 37, 24, 30)$ ($m=8, n=8$) as the unique survivor. Third, Theorem 4.1 eliminates two-orbit abelian actions for 73 of the 374 $Q$-feasible even-order $t \ne \mu$ open primary pairs. Finally, we construct $\mathrm{DSRG}(64, 22, 18, 10, 6)$ over $\mathbb{Z}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_8$ using a Coordinate-Cross diagonal and Wrap-Around Difference Pair $B_{01}=\{0,1,2\}, B_{10}=\{0,3,6\} \subset \mathbb{Z}_8$, and prove its parametric uniqueness (Proposition 5.3).
