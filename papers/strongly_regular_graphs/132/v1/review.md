# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited structural restriction: a DSRG$(77,13,4,3,2)$ admitting an automorphism of order eleven must have at least $44$ distinct closed in-neighborhoods and at least $44$ distinct closed out-neighborhoods. This is a useful partial advance toward the selected existence problem. It excludes an entire boundary of possible neighborhood-class configurations, rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful search. The target remains marked open in the Hobart–Brouwer table [2] checked during this review. Neither unrestricted existence nor the possibility of an order-eleven automorphism is resolved, as the paper correctly emphasizes.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. The equation $B^2=3B+2J$, the spectrum, the class-size bound, and the fixed-point argument establish the stated setup. The character multiplicity calculation forces nonprincipal rank two, while the principal quotient has trace $17$. These facts exclude two class orbits and, for three class orbits, make $3I-Q(\zeta)$ rank one. The resulting diagonal partition, convolution bounds, and forced-completion formulas are consistent. In particular, the anchor normalizations and the translation of the third orbit preserve the relevant constraints, and the nonzero-denominator argument justifies division without discarding possible graphs. The physical-row conditions correctly retain the information lost in the quotient. Transposition then gives the second neighborhood bound.

I inspected the implementations and ran the complete supplied reproduction from a working copy. The independent column enumeration reproduced $5$, $6$, and $10$ augmentation matrices for weights $(4,2,1)$, $(3,3,1)$, and $(3,2,2)$. The rational and finite-field enumerations reproduced respectively $0$, $0$, and $4$ normalized quotient records. For every surviving target record, each size-two class had $122$ weighted-degree candidates and no valid row; the size-three class had $1497$ candidates and two valid rows, with no required decomposition. The positive control reconstructed a DSRG$(44,10,4,3,2)$ and passed all $3872$ graph and complement entry checks. This reproduces the decisive finite computation. The two quotient implementations share the mathematical reduction, so their agreement is an arithmetic cross-check rather than an independent proof of coverage; coverage is supplied by the accompanying finite-domain and normalization arguments, which are adequately explained. The evidence supports the conditional theorem, not a broader nonexistence claim.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Paper 76 [3] supplies the neighborhood-class framework; Paper 117 [4] supplies related cyclic character arguments and the control graph; Paper 119 [5] establishes a quotient-and-local-splitting construction in a different setting; and Paper 131 [6] restricts saturated-clique contacts at the same target parameters. None supplies this $44$-neighborhood bound. I also compared the rank-one sieve and forced completion with Paper 23v2 [7], and the orbit-matrix construction and refinement method of Maksimović and Rukavina [8, Sections 2–3]. These establish substantial methodological precedent, including the fact that a quotient need not lift. The new content here is the complete weighted three-orbit census and its row-lift obstruction for the order-$77$ target. Within these focused comparisons, I found no prior result duplicating that contribution. Its significance is modest but substantive and appropriately presented.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the question, explains the reduction and enumeration strategy, presents the decisive counts, and separates necessary quotient conditions from graph realizability. Detailed records are appropriately retained in the supplement. The limitations and reproducibility instructions are explicit, and the abstract is informative. The following small improvements would make the presentation and attribution more precise:

1. Add direct references to [7] for the diagonal sieve and rank-one forced-completion precedent, and to [8] for the broader orbit-matrix/refinement context. Describe the weighted class census and its obstruction as the specific new result; the quotient-versus-lift distinction itself is established background.
2. In Section 4, explicitly define $r_0$ and $r$ as the multiplicities of eigenvalue $3$ in the principal and nonprincipal character spaces. State that the principal space has dimension seven and that Galois conjugacy makes the ten nonprincipal multiplicities equal. This would make the short calculation $r_0+10r=21$ easier to follow.
3. Correct the doubled LaTeX backslashes around the finite-field expression in the metadata abstract so that it renders as $\mathbb F_7[z]/(\Phi_{11})$.
