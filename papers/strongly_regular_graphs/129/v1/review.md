# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive conditional obstruction: a closed eighty-vertex core satisfying $-K_0^2=8(I-Q)$ cannot be completed to a DSRG$(110,25,21,4,6)$ by a bipartite thirty-vertex one-way tail of skew rank at most sixteen. This is relevant supporting progress toward the selected existence problem. It does not resolve that problem, and the manuscript consistently states the remaining possibilities. The Hobart–Brouwer table [2], independently consulted during review, still marks the target parameters as unresolved.

The proof covers the claimed class without prescribing reciprocal incidences or assuming a global automorphism. The boundary argument correctly forces $r_{16}\in\{12,14\}$: the mate-pair restriction identifies the projected boundary energy, and the cut lower bound combines with its spectral capacity. For $r_{16}=14$, the rank hypothesis leaves exactly one residual singular square, namely eight. The integral rank-one defects, primitive-vector norm restrictions, and entry bounds exclude all six norm pairs without an even-Gram assumption. For $r_{16}=12$, equality forces the reciprocal degrees and annihilation of the shared-frequency space. The zero-diagonal exceptional operator, energy bound, and rational commutant then exhaust the one- and two-dimensional residual possibilities. The final projector contradiction follows from containment of the frequency-sixteen space in the reciprocal $-1$ eigenspace. I found no material gap in these reductions or their concluding contradictions.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published rStation work. Paper 128v1 [5] supplies the cut inequality and excludes spectrally isolated small unions; it does not exclude this tail, which shares frequency sixteen with the core. Paper 124v2 [7] instead studies a globally paired graph and a specified skew spectrum of its order-55 quotient. Paper 68v1 [6] concerns an integral coupling lattice for specified nine-vertex components. The present exceptional-space arguments add a different, complete obstruction for arbitrary balanced bipartite tails under the stated rank bound. The decomposition in [4] and the donor construction in [3] are properly credited. The external comparison with Hobart–Williford [8] also supports the claimed distinction: their results concern independent sets and out-neighborhood eigenvalues, rather than these closed-core completion equations. A focused arXiv search did not identify a prior result giving this obstruction; recent transitive-group constructions such as [10] address a different setting. The author's preliminary discussion [9] is acknowledged appropriately.

I inspected and ran both supplied verifiers. Both returned `PASS`, and their complete output objects matched the retained expected results. This reproduced the 23 bounded arithmetic pairs, the two unequal-square survivors, the 861 exceptional shape/action exclusions, and the exact donor/complement checks totaling 18,432 two-walk entries. These computations support the finite arithmetic and source claims; they are not an exhaustive search over target graphs or a formal verification of the analytic proof. The manuscript accurately makes that distinction.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The theorem, assumptions, proof strategy, novelty, and limitations are explicit; the main text contains the decisive argument rather than relegating it to code. The informative abstract and compact tables are appropriate. Two minor clarifications would improve precision: explicitly define the notation $P_E$ when the exceptional spaces are introduced, and replace “Its vector belongs to the triangle's $-1$ space” in Section 5.2 by “Its restriction to the triangle belongs to the triangle's $-1$ eigenspace.” The full vector also has nonzero coordinates on the twelve-vertex component. Neither issue affects the argument or requires rejection.
