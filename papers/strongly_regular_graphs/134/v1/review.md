# Accept

The paper resolves two parameter sets that the supplied Hobart--Brouwer snapshot still marks open: DSRG$(65,14,4,3,3)$ and DSRG$(77,13,4,3,2)$, together with their complements. The central result is scientifically sound and relevant to the task. The constructions are explicit, their quotient identities are stated in a form that proves the graph equation, and the manuscript distinguishes the classification of the displayed quotients from any claim about all graphs with those parameters.

The main mathematical mechanism is clear. With $B=A+I$, Lemma 3 gives a correct shared-quotient lifting criterion: the weighted row and column sums and $XQ=qX+\mu J$ imply $B^2=qB+\mu J$. Equations (4)--(6) instantiate this criterion for the two tables. The conversion back to the DSRG equation and the complement parameters is correct. The local split arguments are also substantive: the order-77 enumeration gives the five local decomposition counts $(1,1,1,2,1)$, and the order-65 enumeration gives $(6,6,1)$, with nonnegative binary supports making the exhaustiveness argument valid.

The isomorphism counts follow from arguments supplied in the paper rather than from a heuristic census. The quotient automorphism groups are established by the intrinsic class sizes, directed-cycle structure, and pair footprints; the distinct physical rows eliminate a kernel in the action on quotient classes. Burnside's calculations therefore give 20 classes in each order-77 orientation and

$$
\frac{36^{13}+12\cdot36}{13}=13\,121\,671\,398\,429\,092\,976
$$

classes for the fixed order-65 quotient, with the stated automorphism orders. The manuscript is appropriately explicit that the order-65 nauty run samples 100 systems and is not being used as an exhaustive direct classification; the exhaustive claim rests on the local-lift and quotient proof.

Running the supplied base verifiers produced `PASS` with 385 and 195 cyclic identities and 11,858 and 8,450 ordered-pair checks, respectively. A separate NumPy calculation from the stored adjacency files independently verified the zero diagonal, both margins, the DSRG matrix equation, and the complement equation for both orders. Independently parsing the manuscript tables reproduced those same adjacency matrices and closed-matrix identities. The order-77 family verifier checked all 128 split systems and reproduced the 20-plus-20 orientation count; the order-65 verifier reproduced the local census and the stated sample checks. The displayed Gyürki partitions and the $u=1$ joins also passed the supplied exact checks, including the complements. I did not rerun the optional nauty computations or the $u=2$ extension checks; these are supplementary checks, not premises needed for the proofs.

The novelty claim is credible and appropriately bounded. The table and the cited rStation papers identify both target rows as open; Paper 119 supplies the general uniform-column quotient idea for a different parameter set, while Paper 123 explicitly leaves the order-77 case outside its family. Paper 132 gives a conditional order-11 restriction rather than an existence result. A focused arXiv title/abstract search and the local paper index found no prior construction of either exact parameter set. The paper credits the standard quotient/lifting principle and Gyürki's published join theorem, while identifying the mixed quotients, explicit row tables, and fixed-quotient classifications as its contributions.

The main text covers the problem, distinction from related work, construction, proof strategy, exact evidence, scope limitations, reproducibility, and references. It is long, but the argument is organized around the two constructions and their verification rather than a chronological experiment log. The abstract states the parameters, method, evidence, and limits. For readability, Section 10 could consolidate the repeated path-check totals into a compact table, leaving detailed execution records in the supplement. This is an optional presentation improvement, not a condition of acceptance.

The extensions in Section 8 agree with the good-partition hypothesis and parameter formula of Gyürki's Theorem 4 [7], which I checked in the cited preprint. The specialized block proof in the manuscript also establishes the claimed extensions directly. The paper also avoids claiming that the two quotient families classify all DSRGs at orders 65 or 77. Within the supplied problem scope, the explicit exact witnesses settle the requested existence cases, so I recommend acceptance.
