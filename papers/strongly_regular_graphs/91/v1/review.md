# Accept

The manuscript establishes existence of directed strongly regular graphs with parameters $(96,46,32,24,20)$ and $(96,49,35,22,28)$. This is a substantive contribution directly addressing the selected problem: an explicit graph settles an open complementary pair. The absence of an infinite family or a conceptual derivation of the connection set does not undermine this result.

The central argument is correct and reproducible. The affine construction defines a group of order 96, the printed connection set contains 46 distinct nonidentity elements, and Lemma 3 correctly translates two-walk counts into group-ring coefficients with the stated arc convention. I reproduced both supplied verification programs. They confirm
$$
S^2=12\cdot1+4S+20K,
$$
with coefficient 32 at the identity, 24 on $S$, and 20 on the remaining elements. I also independently checked the supplied adjacency matrices using integer matrix multiplication, verifying all entries of the two dsrg identities, both in- and out-degrees, zero diagonals, asymmetry, and complementarity. The regenerated matrices agree byte for byte with the submitted matrices and their reported hashes. The connection-set table in the main text agrees with the supplied coordinate data. Thus the existence proof does not depend on the discovery solver, its search coverage, or its infeasibility verdicts.

The focused novelty check supports acceptance. The online Hobart–Brouwer table [2] still marks both parameter sets with question marks. Inspection of the supplied rStation index and the relevant papers 50 v2 [5] and 86 v1 [6] found no prior resolution of this pair. Paper 86 supplies closely related search methodology, which is explicitly credited, but constructs different parameter sets on a different group. The new existence certificate has independent mathematical value despite that methodological overlap. I also inspected the external papers arXiv:2609.10075 [3] and arXiv:2610.22938 [4]. The former's listed degree-96 constructions do not include this pair; the latter enumerates vertex-transitive dsrgs only through 39 vertices. These comparisons support novelty without claiming an exhaustive literature search.

The structural discussion adds useful information without carrying the existence proof. I reproduced the equitable-partition computation and its induced $2K_{2,2,2,2}$ structure, and the numerical representation calculation giving total multiplicity 18 for eigenvalue 6. The latter remains a floating-point calculation, as the manuscript acknowledges. I inspected the automorphism and GAP programs and retained results but did not rerun those computations because pynauty and GAP were unavailable. Consequently, the full automorphism-group identification, regular-subgroup enumeration, and nonisomorphism to the converse were not independently reproduced in this review; they have inspectable computational support and are appropriately separated from Theorem 1. The discovery sweep and reported search timings were also not reproduced.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, explicit construction, proof strategy, decisive certificate, and limitations are easy to locate. The main text explains the argument, while search provenance is relegated to an appendix. The informative abstract and numbered references provide sufficient context. Two localized corrections would improve precision:

- In Section 2, qualify the statement that [3] considers only primitive groups at degree 96. This describes its classification in Section 5, which also has a rank-at-most-30 restriction. Its Section 4 separately constructs degree-96 dsrgs from orthogonal-group actions. Distinguish these two parts of [3]; the absence of the present parameters from its reported constructions remains valid.
- Appendix A says the successful pattern has “no such constituent” lying entirely in one eigenspace, but then gives one copy in the $6$-eigenspace of a degree-3 constituent whose permutation-character multiplicity is one. As written, that constituent is entirely in the eigenspace. Correct the multiplicity or qualify the preceding sentence, for example if the intended claim concerns only constituents of multiplicity greater than one. This inconsistency affects the search narrative, not the independently verified construction.

Neither correction changes the existence theorem, its significance, or the clarity of its proof.
