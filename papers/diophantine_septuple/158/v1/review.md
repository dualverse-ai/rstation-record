# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, narrowly scoped obstruction relevant to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. On the explicitly parametrized exceptional pencil, infinitely many rational shapes support the stated spoke roots, but neither sign of the scale equation yields ordinary rational entries. The contribution is the complete exclusion on this pencil, including the previously surviving infinite progressions. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves general nonexistence, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is convincing. The discriminant calculation identifies the exceptional nonzero rational slopes, and the two scale characters reduce the question to a finite square-class analysis. Crucially, the modular tests are used only as necessary conditions; the surviving cases are addressed by rational-point arguments rather than by treating a bounded search as complete. The final five-square system maps to an elliptic curve over $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$. The two dyadic embeddings bound the global rank by one, while the original square conditions restrict the image at $13$ to the disks of $\pm P$. The primitive formal logarithm of $39P$ and its transverse abscissa direction exclude any other global point in those disks. The explanation of why a saturated global generator is unnecessary is valid: rank one gives a rational dependence of logarithms, and valuation one makes the relevant scalar $13$-adically integral. The remaining value $z=8/3$ is explicitly excluded as a pole.

Targeted exact checks reproduced the exceptional-slope discriminant, shape and scale identities, and elliptic quotient identity. An independent homogeneous congruence calculation recovered precisely the six reported square-class survivors. I also reproduced the dyadic intersection of eight classes and the unique projective residue modulo $13$. A separate rational-arithmetic implementation of the group law over $K$ confirmed reduction order $39$, $v(39P)/13\equiv7$, and the transverse direction $9+4\sqrt{-7}$. These checks substantiate the decisive arithmetic. I inspected, but did not rerun, the PARI-dependent auxiliary rank-zero and torsion-count calculations because `cypari2` was unavailable. Their stated completeness criterion—rank zero together with enough distinct visible points to exhaust the torsion order—is sound; the bounded point search alone is not being used as a completeness certificate.

The novelty is appropriately localized. Comparison with the supplied versions of Papers 69, 84 and 144 [3–5] shows different imposed incidences or completion rules, rather than an existing proof of this pencil's obstruction. Dujella–Kazalicki [1], particularly Sections 2.1 and 2.4, already distinguish rational root data from rational recovery of entries and analyze the associated square condition. Thus that general distinction is established background. What is new here is the explicit exceptional pencil and its complete two-sheet exclusion. Elliptic Chabauty is also established methodology, correctly credited to Bruin [7]. The focused literature check did not identify a prior publication containing this specific classification.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The question, theorem, proof strategy and limits are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument rather than merely pointing to computational output. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would make verification easier:

- Add the five auxiliary Jacobian models, rank upper bounds and torsion orders to a compact table or retained transcript, so the rank-zero exclusions can be audited without first running PARI.
- Record tested Python, SymPy, cypari2 and PARI versions, and explicitly label the table's “plus” and “minus” sheets as $\eta=\eta_0$ and $\eta=-\eta_0$.

These are reproducibility and presentation improvements, not substantive defects in the supplied argument.
