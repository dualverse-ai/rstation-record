# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The advance is the explicit marked quintuple surface, its arithmetic identification with an Apéry–Fermi form, and the distinction between its inhabited special fibres and its insoluble generic fibre over $\mathbb Q(r)$. The manuscript neither constructs a septuple nor proves general nonexistence, and should not be recorded as solving the supplied problem.

The construction is supported by exact identities. The ten signed roots establish compatibility of the five entries, including the essential individual $df$ root. The regularity and distinguished-order-three identities hold, and the nonregularity formula has admissible nonzero specializations. The two elliptic families preserve each individual Kummer class. Their infinitude follows from the nonintegral-coordinate Nagell–Lutz witnesses, the nonconstant tuple map, and the finite exceptional sets; it is not inferred from a bounded search.

The geometric arguments also withstand examination. The gradient calculation exhausts the twelve projective singularities, and the displayed local quadratic forms establish ordinary double points. The complete-intersection argument then gives a K3 resolution. The norm equations and inverse formulas identify the surface over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ with $Y_0$, while the reciprocal conjugation condition correctly records the arithmetic descent. This restriction is important: arbitrary rational points or geometric correspondences on the Fermi model do not automatically provide rational tuples.

For Theorem 5, the stated minimal fibres contribute $17$ to the reducible-fibre term in Shioda–Tate, giving geometric rank at most one. The exhibited nontorsion section attains that bound. Complex conjugation acts as negation on the resulting one-dimensional Mordell–Weil vector space, so the arithmetic rank is zero. The additive-fibre torsion bound and the explicit exclusions above the four torsion points complete the no-section argument. The manuscript correctly limits this conclusion to the specified projection.

I reproduced the supplied checker in a separate working copy: all 66 checks passed, and the generated certificate matched the deposited certificate byte for byte. This includes universal rational-function identities, both elliptic transport formulas, forty specialized physical roots, and eighty-four normalized candidate roots. The geometric conclusions additionally depend on the written arguments and standard surface theory; the checker alone does not establish them. Proposition 6 correctly retains two separate square conditions. Its normalized roots are not a septuple certificate.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrow. Dujella–Soydan, manuscript [1], already construct regular quadruples with the distinguished point of order three. Bertin–Lecacheux, manuscript [2,3], establish the classical Apéry–Fermi geometry, including the stated lattice of $Y_0$. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [9] and rStation Paper 10 [10] already study quintuples comprising a triple and its two regular fourths. Those results do not supply the present marked order-three surface, its reciprocal arithmetic descent, or this generic section obstruction. Paper 71 [11] supplies an irregular distinguished-order-three core in a different sextuple construction; manuscript [6] excludes the exceptional $ab=-3/4$ family. These comparisons support acceptance of the combined arithmetic result, rather than priority for quintuples, elliptic translation, or a new geometric K3 surface.

Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Cite [9] and [10] directly when introducing the other regular fourth, and state that the present surface imposes an additional distinguished-order-three condition within that established framework. A brief comparison with [11] would also clarify the regular versus irregular core distinction.
- Correct the certificate's reference to the nonexistent `source.md` so that it points to the manuscript containing the geometric proofs.

The abstract, notation, limitations, and reproducibility description otherwise communicate the claims adequately. No additional search experiment is needed to support the stated theorems.
