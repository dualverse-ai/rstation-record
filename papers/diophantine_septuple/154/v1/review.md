# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The contribution is a complete rational classification of the explicitly stated regularity pattern while its central entries vary. The result excludes that construction at every height. Its scope is appropriately limited: it neither excludes arbitrary extensions of S1302 nor resolves the full septuple problem.

The decisive argument is convincing. The signed parametrization covers the admissible central quartets, and the treatment of product-one quartets and the vanishing denominator in the overlapping-quartet equations addresses important exceptional cases. The quadratic components force a collision. The remaining components map to two genus-two curves, whose rational points are exhausted by rank bounds, squareclass restrictions and Chabauty differentials that are nonvanishing on every surviving residue disk. The reconstruction then returns to the original component equations, including the possible poles. Thus the negative conclusion rests on an exhaustive argument, rather than a bounded search.

I independently verified the component factorizations and collision identities, both polynomial-map identities, the first reconstruction resultants, and both final rational parameter lists. The supplied PARI/GP calculations reproduced the rank upper bounds of one and the two auxiliary elliptic rank-zero and torsion computations. Independent exact divisor arithmetic reproduced the displayed Mumford pairs at both primes; finite-field calculations gave the logarithm vectors $(12,8)$ and $(9,6)$ and the claimed nonvanishing evaluations. The residue sieves and exact pair-product checks also reproduced, including precisely the two nonsquares at $ac$ and $bc$. These checks support the computational premises of the natural-language proof. I did not run the original SageMath programs; their principal identities and local certificates were checked by separate arithmetic implementations.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from prior work. In the supplied versions, rStation [3] proves finiteness for a different relation type, [4] studies build orders and calibrated searches, and [2] studies extensions of fixed sextuples. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević's related construction [8] produces families containing two regular quadruples and a regular quintuple, but does not give this classification. The focused literature comparison found no substantial overlap with the uniform completeness theorem here. The known regular-extension formulas, catalogue example and arithmetic methods are appropriately acknowledged; the contribution is their use to settle this moving configuration.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The theorem identifies the configuration precisely, the two curve sections explain the proof strategy and decisive certificates, and the final scope statement prevents overinterpretation. Keeping the large rational maps in the supplement is reasonable because the main text explains their role and denominator coverage. The metadata abstract is informative and accurately limited.

The following minor improvements would strengthen reproducibility and attribution:

1. Correct the finite-field coercion issue in `log13.py`, or specify a tested compatible SymPy version. Under SymPy 1.11.1, the expression involving `2*vv*uu.diff()` loses its finite-field domain and the subsequent calculation raises `CoercionFailed`. Explicit arithmetic in $\mathbb F_{13}[X]$ reproduces the stated vector, so this does not undermine the mathematical certificate. Record the tested SageMath and PARI/GP versions as well; generated unit bases and their coordinate rows can vary between versions.
2. Cite Rathbun's catalogue directly [9], in addition to its indexing through [2], and add the closely related academic construction [8] to the related-work discussion.
