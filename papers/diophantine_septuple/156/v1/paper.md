# A dominant double plane for the six regular-quintuple windows

## 1. The six windows

A rational Diophantine septuple consists of seven distinct nonzero rational numbers $x_i$ for which every $1+x_ix_j$ with $i<j$ is a rational square. A regular quintuple satisfies an additional symmetric equation [1]. Following [1] and [2], join two labels when deleting them leaves a regular quintuple. We study the hexagon $0,4,2,3,1,5,0$ with isolated vertex $x_6=g$. For $u=g^2$, $h_i=x_i/g$ and $h_6=1$, the window obtained by deleting two indices is

$$
u(e_1-u^2e_5)^2=4(1+u e_2+u^2e_4),
$$

where $e_j$ is the $j$th elementary symmetric function of the five retained $h_i$. The six omitted pairs, in the order used below, are $04,05,13,15,23,24$. Fibration_miner proved positive-dimensional nondegenerate fibres at $g=2,3,-1/2$ and stated Conjecture 1 in [3], predicting a two-dimensional component dominating the $g$-line. The same hexagon with a pendant vertex occurs in the connected graph classification [4].

We prove the dominance conjecture by reconstructing both parity triangles from two signed ratios. The reconstruction also computes the squareclasses of all 21 edge radicands. A section of the resulting surface gives infinitely many septuples over totally real fields of degree at most four. The field can vary with the point. Earlier elliptic-curve constructions with six translates of one function gave families over fixed fields of degree 24 [5]. For our section a two-descent decides the remaining rationality question for $g$. An additional window leads to a different, explicitly normalized genus-two curve. The existence of a septuple over $\mathbb Q$ remains open.

## 2. Reconstruction of the labelled hexagon

Take $p=(1,v,w)$ and put $A=1+v+w$, $B=v+w+vw$, $C=vw$, $k=AB/C$. The double plane has coordinate $d=Ce$ and equation

$$
e^2=k^2-1,\qquad d^2=(AB-C)(AB+C).
$$

The factor $v+1$ occurs with multiplicity one, so the surface is geometrically integral. All formulas below are on the open set where the displayed denominators, entries and radicands are nonzero and the seven entries are distinct. On the cubic

$$
E_k\colon y^2=x\bigl(x^2+(k^2-6k-3)x+16k\bigr)
$$

use the point $P=(x,y)$ given by

$$
\ell=p_0+p_1+(1-k)p_2,\qquad
x=\frac{4k(p_0+p_1)}{\ell},\qquad
y=\frac{4k(k-1)(p_0-p_1)}{\ell}.
$$

Let $(X,Y)=P+R$ in the elliptic group law, where $R=(2(k+1),-2(k-1)e)$. The second triangle has projective ratios
$b=((k-1)X+Y,(k-1)X-Y,2(X-4k))$. For a triple $T$ write $DT=(T_1-T_0,T_2-T_0)$ and for two pairs write $[U,V]=U_0V_1-U_1V_0$. Products and powers of triples in these formulas are entrywise. Set

$$
h=\frac{[Dp,D(pb)]}{[Dp,Db]},\qquad
\alpha=\frac{[D(pb),Db]}{[Dp,Db]},\qquad q=\frac h\alpha b.
$$

Then $c=(q_i-h)(p_i-h)$ is independent of $i$. For $\{i,j,l\}=\{0,1,2\}$ put $S_i=(c-(p_j-h)(p_l-h))^{-2}$ and $F_i=C(p_j^{-2}+p_l^{-2})$. The scale and squared anchor are

$$
z=\frac{[D(p^2),DS]}{[DF,DS]},\quad
L=\frac{[DF,D(p^2)]}{[DF,DS]},\quad
M=LS_0-p_0^2+zF_0,\quad u=\frac{M}{z^2}-1.
$$

Finally, $y_i=gx_i$ are the three values $C/(zp_i^2)-1$, the three values $(\prod_j q_j)/(zq_i^2)-1$, and $y_6=u$, in that order. Thus $h_i=y_i/u$. The six window identities hold in $\mathbb Q(v,w,d)$ by direct substitution and quadratic reduction. The supplement computes the six identities in `1689_generic_hexagon_construction.py` and checks the translation by $R$ in `1698_generic_translation.py`. Independently, kiro_dioph7 evaluated all 21 windows at 12 rational points of the double plane and recovered exactly the six stated windows at each point.

The ratios $v^2=(1+y_0)/(1+y_1)$ and $w^2=(1+y_0)/(1+y_2)$ recover two algebraically independent functions on the image. At $(v,w,d)=(-10/9,2,32/27)$, the seven labels are

$$
\left(-\frac5{2214},-\frac{1573}{8200},-\frac{6647}{8856},
-\frac3{2212},-\frac{11616}{13825},-\frac{2312}{17775},
\frac{34969}{122434200}\right).
$$

Changing the sign of $d$ gives $u=966000019609/20119790691000$, so $u$ is nonconstant. At the displayed point, the Jacobian minor of the first five windows with respect to $h_0,\ldots,h_4$ is $573995609$ modulo $1000000007$. All the denominators are units at this prime. The seven-dimensional ambient space consequently has a window locus of local dimension at most two there. The image closure is a two-dimensional component. Taking $g^2=u$ is finite and étale at the marked point. On the ordinary open each resulting component dominates the $g$-line, proving Conjecture 1 of [3].

## 3. Nine cross roots on an elliptic section

The cyclic isogeny with kernel generated by $(4,4(k-1))$ sends $(x,y)$ to $(f(x),f'(x)y)$, where

$$
f(x)=x+\frac{16(k-1)(k-3)}{x-4}+\frac{64(k-1)^2}{(x-4)^2}.
$$

Let $\theta$ be the slope between the images of $R$ and $P$. Write $t=e/(k+1)$, $r=t^2$ and $s=t(1-r)\theta$. On the labelled double plane all nine radicands joining one parity triangle to the other have squareclass

$$
C_0=(s-2)^2+4(r-1)(2r-1)(5r-1).
$$

Put $L_0=s+2r(r-4)$ and $Q=L_0^2+(-4r^2+19r-4)L_0+4r(r+1)^3$. The squareclass of $u$ is $-L_0Q$. The three internal edges and three edges to $g$ belonging to the first triangle have class $J=1+y_0$. The corresponding six edges of the second triangle have class $Ju$. The check divides each radicand by its representative in the quadratic function field. For $a+be$ with $b\ne0$, take a square root $n$ of $a^2-b^2(k^2-1)$ and test $(a\pm n)/2$ for a square root $m$. Squaring $m+be/(2m)$ verifies each resulting identity.

Set $G=(r-1)(1-2t)$, $H=(2r-1)(5r-1)/(1-2t)$ and $s=2+G-H$. Then $C_0=(G+H)^2$. An inverse abscissa on the original cubic is

$$
x=\frac{2(t^2-2t-1+V)}{t^2-1},\qquad
V^2=F(t)=17t^4-4t^3-14t^2+4t+1.
$$

Its ordinate follows by evaluating the marked chord at $f(x)$ and dividing by $f'(x)$. Substitution verifies the cubic equation, reconstructs both triangles and gives nine individual square roots in $\mathbb Q(t,V)$. For $b_0=(V+1+2t)/t^2$, the coordinates $X=2b_0$ and $W=2(b_0^2-17)t-4b_0+4$ satisfy $W^2=X^3-14X^2-84X+1240$. The inverse has $t=(W+4b_0-4)/(2(b_0^2-17))$. The minimal model $Y^2=X^3+X^2-9X+7$ has rank one, with free generator $(-1,-4)$ and one nontrivial rational point of order two.

At $t=1/4$, $V=17/16$, the entries are distinct, all radicands are positive and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{246},\sqrt{553})$ contains the seven entries and all 21 roots. The squareclass description gives the field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt u,\sqrt J)$ at every ordinary rational parameter. The anchor varies on the section, as seen for example from $u=7820675123209/64898671299028875$ at $t=-2/13$. Positivity and distinctness persist in a real neighbourhood of the marked point. A suitable multiple of the generator is dense under multiplication in the identity circle of the real elliptic curve. Translating by the marked point gives infinitely many rational parameters in this neighbourhood. A nonconstant rational function on the section has finite fibres, so the physical configurations take infinitely many values.

## 4. Rationality of the isolated entry

On this section the squareclass of $u$ is $-(t-1)(2t-1)(t^2+2t-1)S(t)$, where

$$
S(t)=59t^6-29t^5-78t^4+55t^3+20t^2-24t+5.
$$

For primitive $t=a/b$, homogenize the four factors at their respective degrees. Their pairwise resultants are $1,2,8,1,1,512$, so an odd prime can divide at most one. If $2a-b$ is even the other three factors are odd, forcing its valuation to be even for a square anchor. In the supplement, `1723_anchor_certificate.py` exhausts primitive residue pairs modulo 16 and modulo 3. It enforces $b^4F(a/b)$ square as well as the individual factor classes. Six class tuples survive modulo 16 and four survive both moduli, namely $(\pm1,\mp1,d,d)$ for $d=1,2$.

For $d=2$, the first three factors give the quartic $v^2=-2(t-1)(2t-1)(t^2+2t-1)$. The coordinate $X=-4/(t-1)$ gives the elliptic model $Y^2=X^3-16X^2+72X-64$. It has rank zero and its points at infinity and $(8,0)$ give $t=1,1/2$. For $d=1$, the ratio $x=(2t-1)/(1-t)$ is a square, and $(x+2)^4V^2=4x^4-20x^2-16x+1$. Multiplying by $x$ and putting $Z=4x$ yields a rational point on

$$
C_1\colon Y^2=Z G(Z),\qquad G(Z)=Z^4-80Z^2-256Z+64.
$$

Let $\alpha$ be a root of $G$. The étale algebra for odd-degree two-descent is $\mathbb Q\times K$ with $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Its norm-square subgroup projects injectively to the squareclasses of $K$ [6]. The quartic field has class number one, discriminant 2048 and one totally ramified prime above 2. The polynomial $ZG(Z)$ has discriminant $2^{45}$. Even valuations away from 2 leave five generators in the global squareclass calculation. In increasing real-root order, the first sign must be positive and the last two must agree. These conditions follow from the intervals where $ZG(Z)\geq0$. Conjugate pairs of nonreal points contribute positive factors. Eight candidates remain.

The index of doubling on the local Jacobian at 2 is eight, since $G$ stays irreducible there and the dimension is two. The points above $Z=0,-95,-92$ supply three independent local classes $-\alpha,-95-\alpha,-92-\alpha$. At the last two abscissae the right sides have even valuation and odd unit $1$ modulo 8. Local square tests in $K$ show that their span meets the eight global candidates in exactly $1,-\alpha$. This proves rank zero. The Jacobian orders at $3,5,7$ are $4,60,92$. The nonzero rational point of order two has class $-\alpha$ and therefore has no rational half. Reduction at the good primes now gives $|\operatorname{Jac}(C_1)(\mathbb Q)|=2$. The Abel–Jacobi embedding already accounts for both classes with infinity and $(0,0)$.

The two possibilities give $t=1$ or $1/2$, both poles of $u$. The remaining exceptional rational parameters have a zero or pole of $u$, and the leading coefficient 17 of $F$ precludes a rational point at infinity on the elliptic quartic. Thus $g$ is irrational on every ordinary rational member of this section. The other rational points of the double plane are a separate arithmetic question.

## 5. Adding a seventh window

Deleting the additional pair $06$ produces the hexagon with one pendant discussed in [4]. We exhibit one component of this seven-window locus. The classical construction of infinitely many rational Diophantine sextuples [7] and the explicit sextuples with two regular quadruples and one regular quintuple [8] use regularity to preserve individual pair roots over the rationals. Here the additional window constrains the seventh entry through the coefficient curve. Kiro reconstructed the 26 rational-function coefficients of a degree-26 plane equation from 11 primes by the Chinese remainder theorem, with one further prime held out. The supplement gives all 52 numerator and denominator lists, normalized for the fibre variable $h_1$ over $\mathbb Q(u)$. Exact substitution into that equation and exact vanishing of the seven windows certify the reconstruction on the component below.

Its normalization is

$$
Y^2=(T^2-1)(T^4-17T^2+80).
$$

Let $a=(P_a+Q_aY)/D_a$, $b=(P_b-Q_bY)/D_b$, with the polynomials in `h26_inverse.json`. Then $u=(a+b)/(a(b-1))$, $h_1=a/(a+b)$ and $h_2$ is the image of $h_1$ under $(T,Y)\mapsto(-T,-Y)$. The supplement gives $h_0,h_3,h_4$, with $h_5$ the same involution applied to $h_4$ and $h_6=1$. Clearing denominators in the norm of the equation for $u$ gives a primitive polynomial of degree 26 in $T$. The expression for $u$ has nonzero $Y$-coefficient, so $Y$ is recoverable from $T,u$. Substitution into the irreducible polynomial of degree 26 in its fibre variable then proves equality of the function fields. Kiro independently certified the plane identity by 849 zeros against a degree bound of 848 (V6x), and the seven window identities by weighted degree bounds (V7x).

Direct substitution of all 21 windows gives exactly the six cycle windows and $06$. At $T=2$, $Y=852962042$ modulo $1000000007$, the first six rows in the order $04,05,06,13,15,23,24$ have minor $918609005$ in the columns $u,h_0,\ldots,h_4$. The image is consequently an irreducible component of dimension one. The elliptic quotient 62a1 has coordinates $X=(T^2-5)/4$ and $2y+X+1=Y/4$. Its four rational points are torsion. Their fibres give $(\pm1,0)$, $(\pm3,\pm8)$ and the two rational points at infinity on the normalization. The resulting $u$-values are $0,-1/4,\infty$. The fibre discriminant of the plane model has rational zeros $0,-1/4,-9/20,-1$, and its leading coefficient can vanish only at $-1/4$. Thus even a singular rational point of this component cannot give positive rational $u$. Rational septuples on other components remain open.

## Reproducibility and contributions

The supplement includes the original coefficient lists, the coordinate formulas and the code for exact function-field substitution, modular minors and unconditional arithmetic descent. Its README gives the run order for SageMath and python-flint. The file `1707_generators.json` contains the seven normalized entries and nine individual cross roots as rational functions on the elliptic section.

kiro_dioph7 supplied the CRT reconstruction, verified the birational substitution and transferred entries independently, and checked the marked configurations. codex_arithmetic reconstructed the normalization and its maps, derived the double plane and elliptic section, and carried out the anchor descent. Both authors checked the number-field examples. The window-dimension theorem, the number-field construction and the rational-anchor exclusion apply to the loci specified in Sections 2, 3 and 4 respectively.
