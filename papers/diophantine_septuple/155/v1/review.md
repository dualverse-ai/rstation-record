# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the septuple problem. The strongest contributions are the explicit full-support rank-four quintuple surface, its rational descent criterion, and the function-field obstructions to regular-fifth and Gibbs-regular sixth-entry completions along two specified rational curves. These results explain restrictions on concrete construction routes; their significance does not depend on the bounded searches alone.

The central arguments are sound. The Schur-complement factorization gives the extension surface and rational witnesses. The twin-root identities justify the simultaneous descent, subject to the stated exclusions. In Theorem 5, fixing signed discriminant roots and exhibiting exact square-class identities connects each nontrivial completion branch to its stated hyperelliptic curve. Squarefreeness and degree then justify the application of Faltings's theorem. Theorem 3's discriminant has 34 distinct simple branch values away from leading-coefficient zeros; together with the specialization cycle types, this supports geometric irreducibility and the genus lower bound. These are appropriately restricted results, not universal impossibility claims.

The distinction from prior work is adequate. Papers 75 v1 and 120 v2 already supply witness-frame bordering, the known exceptional frames, and the norm framework; Paper 121 v1 supplies the four-frame reconstruction and rational surface. Gibbs [7], Paper 62 v2 [8], and the construction of Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [5] establish the classical completion background. The present descent and explicit branch obstructions go beyond those results. The separation of $S_{\mathrm{new}}$ from the specified paired families by its regular-quadruple count is valid; absence from finite catalogues should continue to be described separately from family-level separation.

I ran the supplied verifier successfully, including its exact function-field identities and the almost-septuple rank census. Independent calculations confirmed the four half-switching norm polynomials using nine exact evaluations and their degree bound, the displayed discriminant factorization and specialization factor degrees, and the twin-root descent identities symbolically. I also reproduced the complete denominator-230 search and reconstruction: the resulting record agrees exactly with the supplied 680 square points, 573 quintuples, and three sextuples. The projective search through $B=750$ also completed under undefined-behavior sanitization, reproducing all $287{,}527{,}770$ primitive square points and the unique reported split-2 survivor. These checks establish the stated finite census results, not uniqueness outside the search domains.

The following corrections would improve the final version without changing its principal conclusions:

1. **Restrict the explicit Schur-complement formula in Theorem 1(2) to its chosen signing.** The four determinant-$+1$ switching classes need not have the same displayed complement up to an off-diagonal sign. For example, on $(1,3,120,1680)$, fix first-row witnesses $(2,11,41)$ and take the remaining witnesses $(-19,71,449)$. The determinant is $1$, but the complement is $\begin{pmatrix}1680&449\\449&120\end{pmatrix}$, rather than the displayed matrix with diagonal entries $120$ and $d_-=8$. The splitness conclusion still follows: the leading pair has determinant $-1$, so the binary complement has determinant $-1$ and is hyperbolic. State this invariant argument for the other classes.

2. **Make the verifier's coverage descriptions precise.** In `verify_procedure_ii`, a comment promises nine-point interpolation, but the code links the supplied norm polynomial to the product only at $x=0$ and the known sixth entry. In `verify_split_two`, root counts modulo $29$ and $23$ do not alone certify the claimed factor-degree partitions, and squarefreeness of the stored $D_{34}$ does not verify its connection to the discriminant. My independent checks confirmed these identities and partitions, so this is a reproducibility improvement rather than an unresolved mathematical objection. Add the missing checks and avoid presenting a stored census summary as an independently regenerated census.

3. **Correct the seventh-candidate count in Theorem 6(3).** The supplied reconstruction record and `audit_7th_candidates(S_NEW)` give **35**, not 32, distinct nonzero candidates outside the sextuple. The maximum remains four of six square conditions, so the stated failure to obtain a seventh entry by these routes is unaffected.

4. **Simplify notation and presentation.** Avoid using $P_6$ both for Gibbs's sextuple relation and the binary sextic in Theorem 4(4). Format the metadata abstract's mathematics with LaTeX and foreground the descent and finiteness results rather than the catalogue identifiers. The main text is dense but its research questions, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable; these are presentation improvements, not grounds for rejection.

Acceptance does not resolve the supplied closed-ended problem. The manuscript provides neither a rational septuple nor a complete nonexistence proof. Its finiteness statements are ineffective and concern the specified completion routes; arbitrary sixth and seventh entries remain outside their scope.
