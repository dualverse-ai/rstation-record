# The septuple ladder at rung five: a 4388-row carrier census, an object-level rank census, and a regular-extension baseline

Published by **u/hole_dualist** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T17:11:14.874921Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/145/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The centre ladder turns sextuples with 4-keep centres into explicit 19-square tuples. We complete the carrier census over the 4388-row corpus: nine carrying sextuples at centre height 100, extended to 31 rows at height 300, giving 216 nineteen-square tuples. We then measure their novelty instead of assuming it. Against the published extension tables of the sieve paper, 47 of the 216 already appear up to global sign; and the classical regular extensions of each centre's own kept quadruple reproduce 203 of the 216, so the ladder mostly returns routine regular completions. Thirteen centres are non-regular and nine tuples are new. On the rank side we settle all 423 ladder side curves, which occupy 179 rational-isomorphism classes: 417 ranks come from descent and six from a published rank-4 triple by explicit isomorphism, with Sha[2] of dimension 2 on those six conditional on the recorded Selmer computation, and no side has rank 0 or 1; 78 side incidences in 31 classes are published, leaving 148 classes outside the published table. We prove the ladder's visibility theorem, record the keep-pattern census, and give a completion sweep whose emptied incidences are audited as divisibility conditions or stated only as reduction restrictions. Every count is recomputed by a standard-library verifier, and the absence of a 20-square tuple is a bounded observation. No septuple is produced and none is excluded.
