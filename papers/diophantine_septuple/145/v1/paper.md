# The septuple ladder at rung five: a 4388-row carrier census, an object-level rank census, and a regular-extension baseline

## 1. Problem and contribution

A *rational Diophantine $m$-tuple* is a set of $m$ distinct nonzero rationals with $1+a_ia_j$ a rational square for every $i<j$. No rational Diophantine septuple ($m=7$) is known, and none is proved impossible. The *centre ladder* of [1] is a census device for this problem: over a Diophantine sextuple $e_1,\dots,e_6$ with a centre $c$ keeping exactly four of the six centre pairs (a *4-keep* centre), fixing two kept pairs and one bad pair and eliminating $c$ through the conic parametrization yields quartics $Y^2=G_d(s)$ whose rational points map back to further centres. Each centre with exactly four square centre pairs is a $19$-square $7$-tuple, one condition short of an almost septuple and two short of a septuple. Reference [1] ran this ladder over three families ($36$ curves, $8696$ points) and found $30$ new $19$-square tuples, with no $20/21$ tuple and no septuple anywhere.

The present paper extends that programme in three directions and settles a fourth. First, it completes the carrier census: all nine height-$100$ carrying sextuples are identified and laddered, and a height-$300$ search adds $22$ further carrier rows, giving $216$ further $19$-square tuples whose novelty is measured, not assumed -- $203$ of them are the regular extension of their own kept quadruple and $47$ are published in [13]. The ladder device itself -- the keep-keep-fix construction and the centre census -- is [1]'s, extended here to new input rather than replaced by a new mechanism. Second, it proves a *visibility theorem* that explains a feature of every ladder run: each curve imposes three of the four kept conditions, including a designated bad index, so the master pattern of a family can never reappear in that family's output. This settles the mechanism question for the census: the master's absence is structural, not a height artifact. Third, it maps and sweeps the full $36$-curve rung-five ladder of [1], whose $42$ records collapse to $32$ centres: $2$ are the defining centres and the other $30$ are the centres of [1, Section 4.4], re-derived here as a consistency check and deliberately not counted as new. Fourth, it settles the Mordell–Weil rank of $423$ ladder sides ($99$ old, $324$ new): all $423$ are exact, $417$ by descent and six by rational isomorphism with a published rank-$4$ triple, and it decomposes the Prym factor of the three pilot pair curves into three explicit elliptic quotients, which decides the classical Chabauty question for those curves negatively. No side has rank $0$ or $1$, so every route that needs a rank-$0$ or rank-$1$ side is unavailable on this device.

**Theorem 1** (nine carrying sextuples; $216$ tuples, of which $47$ are published and $203$ are regular). A scan of all 4{,}388 corpus rows for centres of height at most $100$ finds exactly nine sextuples carrying a $4$-keep centre (Tables 1 and 1c); each is a Diophantine sextuple (all $15$ pairs square). The keep-keep-fix ladder over all nine families has $108$ curves; their complete rational point sets at height $10^6$ total $45{,}314$ points, every point mapped to a centre and audited. The $3526$ hot records collapse to $194$ distinct non-master centres ($105$ from the five families of Table 1, $89$ from the four of Table 1c), each a $7$-tuple of distinct nonzero rationals with exactly $19$ square pairs; the height-$300$ carrier census of Section 4.8 adds $22$ more, for $216$ tuples in all. None of the $216$ has $20$ or $21$ square pairs, so no almost septuple and no septuple occurs. The novelty accounting is not $216$ new tuples. Comparing every centre with the published extension tables of [13] (`data/elements.txt`, `results/quadruples11274.tsv`, `data/sextuples759.txt`, and its own `regular_quint`), $47$ of the $216$ occur there up to global sign: all $20$ S0355 and all $22$ S0979 centres, which are published regular extensions of $4$-subsets of the published sextuples, plus $5$ carrier centres. Computing the two regular extensions of each centre’s own kept quadruple reproduces $203$ of the $216$, so the ladder output is in the main the classical regular completion of a quadruple rather than new arithmetic. The exceptions are exactly $13$ centres -- S2429 $-14784/4489$, S3875 $-37323/35344$, S3900 $18172/21675$, and ten carrier centres listed in Section 4.8 -- of which $9$ are neither regular nor published. Those $9$ tuples, the carrier census, the rank census and the local sweep are the contribution; the $194$-centre ladder enumeration is reported in full because it is the census the accounting is computed on, not because it is new output. The centres are listed in Appendices A and C and the audits are recomputed by the verifier (`evidence/f2_r7_audit.json`).

**Theorem 1b** (visibility; the master pattern is structurally invisible). Fix a family with keep set $K$ and bad set $B=\{d_1,d_2\}$, and let $\tau=(i,j\mid d)$ be one of its ladder tags, $i,j\in K$, $d\in B$. Then every rational point of the curve $\tau$ maps to a centre $c$ with $1+e_ic$, $1+e_jc$ and $1+e_dc$ all rational squares. Consequently every centre produced by the ladder keeps at least one bad index, and no centre produced by a family's own ladder keeps exactly $K$ -- the master pattern. The proof is the construction itself: the quartic $Y^2=G_d(s)$ encodes the three conditions through $c(s)=(w^2-t^2)/(e_at^2)$, and the identity $G_d(s)/(1+e_dc(s))=(e_at(s))^2$ holds in $\mathbf{Q}(s)$ on all $108$ curves. The absence of the master from every run -- $9$ of $9$ families, at every height -- is therefore not a search limit. Machine check: $499$ tag/centre incidences and $108$ symbolic squares (`code/f2_visibility_lemma.py`, certificate `evidence/f2_visibility_lemma.json`).

**Proposition 1c** (keep-pattern census). Of the fifteen possible $4$-sets of a sextuple, the nine ladders realize $12$ to $14$ as keep patterns. Seven families realize all fourteen non-master patterns; S0355 and S3875 realize twelve, and each misses exactly two patterns beyond the structurally invisible master. All $194$ centres keep exactly four conditions, so no $20/21$ tuple arises anywhere in the census. The two residual gaps are unresolved beyond this height: the missing patterns of S0355 and S3875 contain at least one bad index, so the visibility obstruction does not exclude them, but no existence is claimed either.

**Theorem 2** (rung-five completion sweep and tuple map). With the ladder cover $G_d$ imposed, the $36$ rung-five curves give $108$ four-condition, $108$ five-condition and $36$ six-condition tests at $303$ primes up to $2000$. On the complete model nothing is obstructed, as the boundary point forces. On the punctured model $8$ of $108$ four-condition, $23$ of $108$ five-condition and all $36$ six-condition first-obstruction incidences (over $8$, $15$ and $36$ distinct loci) are empty at some prime $p\ge 11$ ($10$, $27$ and $36$ at any tested prime; the extras are all at $p=5$ or $7$, where at most two classes are genuinely tested, so only the $p\ge 11$ counts are reported as obstructions; locus counts and incidence counts are distinguished throughout). Every emptied locus/test/prime incidence is audited for zero/pole collisions (`code/f2_collision_audit.py`): on the rung-five sweep, $115$ incidences at $59$ distinct interior loci, of which $104$ are clean and $11$ collide (kept entries congruent mod $p$; $8$ at $p=5$, $3$ at $p=17$). Each clean incidence is a divisibility condition on the centre; the colliding ones are stated only as restrictions on reductions. Each locus is emptied at only one to three of the $303$ primes, so the condition is real but weak. The negated-cover control obstructs $36$ of $36$ and the random-cover baseline $8$ of $36$, so the punctured test discriminates. The $42$ tuple records ($32$ distinct centres) are all open; $40$ records cover $30$ distinct centres, which are precisely the $30$ centres of [1, Section 4.4], re-derived here and therefore not new output (Appendix B lists them). No record in either census reaches $20$ or $21$ squares.

**Theorem 3** ($423$ sides settled; no side has rank $0$ or $1$). For all $99$ old quartics (the $36$ curves times their sides), all $180$ first-wave new-family quartics, and all $144$ second-wave quartics the pipeline is an Euler-verified model, the full rational torsion, the PARI $2$-Selmer dimension, and the Mordell-Weil rank from `mwrank`. On $417$ sides the rank is proven exact: $2$ ($15$ times), $3$ ($104$), $4$ ($176$), $5$ ($99$), $6$ ($17$), $7$ ($6$), and on every one of them the rank equals the Selmer excess, so $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=0$ there. The remaining six sides (all Selmer-$8$ sides of the M05 family) are rationally isomorphic to the published rank-$4$ triple $\{-35/36,\,1007/1260,\,72765/106276\}$ of [13], by explicit maps $x=u^2x_0+r$ checked coefficient for coefficient (`evidence/f2_qiso_iso.json`); their rank is therefore exactly $4$. Our own descent had left $4\le r\le 6$ (`mwrank` four independent points to height $16$ with saturation, $2$-descent upper bound $6$, root number $+1$); those data are shipped as provenance and are consistent with rank $4$. With the recorded $2$-Selmer dimension $8$ and $\dim E(\mathbf{Q})[2]=2$ the exact sequence gives $\dim_{\mathbf F_2}\operatorname{Sha}(E)[2]=8-2-4=2$ on these six sides, conditional on that Selmer computation, which we did not rerun; on the other $417$ sides $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=0$. Thus no side of any pair, old or new, has rank $0$ or $1$. Since sides of one isomorphism class share a rank, the census settles $179$ ranks, not $423$ independent ones. This is the entire rank conclusion: it removes the point-determination strategies that require a low-rank elliptic side, and it says nothing about a Mordell–Weil sieve, which does not require one (Bruin and Stoll [11] run sieves at ranks three and four), nor about the genus-five pair curve, whose Jacobian is now known to have rank at least $6$ on the three pilots (Proposition 4).

**Proposition 4** (pilots: rank-gap sieve closed on 15 quartics; Prym factor split into three elliptic quotients). All $15$ Selmer-excess-$2$ quartics have proven rank exactly $2$ with $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=0$, so the excess is genuine rank: a sieve strategy that requires a nonzero gap between the Selmer group and the Mordell--Weil group has no such gap to exploit on these sides. The shipped pilot computes ranks; it does not execute a sieve, and sieves that need no gap (as in [11]) are not excluded by it. Of the $108$ rung-five pairs, $3$ have excess $2$ on both sides. The first two pilots impose the same four conditions $\{0,1,2,3\}$ on the same M05 sextuple with different conic parameters, so they are two presentations of one four-root curve, not two independent genus-five curves; the count of presentations is three and of distinct four-root curves two. On each, the pair curve $C$ is the fibre product $Y_r^2=G_r(s)$, $Y_s^2=G_s(s)$ -- four centre conditions, the ladder's own $G_d$ omitted -- so $C$ is a relaxation of the $20$-square intermediate target, which imposes five conditions and is the genus-$17$ curve of [1, Proposition 2] (the full septuple target imposes all six centre conditions; its affine model has genus $49$); every completion of $C$ satisfies $G_a=G_b=G_r=G_s=\square$, and a rational point of the relaxation is a necessary condition only. Writing $L_i=1+e_ic$, the genus-three Prym factor $D$ has $\mathbf{Q}(D)=\mathbf{Q}(c)(\sqrt{L_a},\sqrt{L_b},\sqrt{L_rL_s})$ and splits as $J(D)\sim E_{ars}\times E_{brs}\times E_{abrs}$ with $E_{ars}:y^2=L_aL_rL_s$, $E_{brs}:y^2=L_bL_rL_s$, $E_{abrs}:y^2=L_aL_bL_rL_s$, the three factors corresponding to the three distinct sign-change characters (the diagonal splitting of [14] in this instance; the three recorded $j$-invariants are data, and no non-isogeny claim is made or needed since the rank sum follows from the isogeny). The resulting Chabauty verdict is not new: the five-factor diagonal splitting is the established framework of [14, Section 2], and the triple-factor ranks published in [13] ($2,2,2$ on the M05 four-root curve, $2,3,3,2$ on the M03 one, verified against the shipped triple table) already imply $\operatorname{rank}J(C)\ge6$ and $\ge10$ against $g(C)=5$. What is new is an independent confirmation by a fully auditable route: explicit rational points of Mazur-certified infinite order on two of the three factors give $\operatorname{rank}J(D)\ge2$ on each pilot, hence with the exact side ranks $(2,2)$ from the census $\operatorname{rank}J(C)\ge6$ against $g(C)=5$, recomputed end to end by the standalone verifier. Classical Chabauty--Coleman is impossible on $C$ in all three cases -- already implied by [13, 14], confirmed here. Quadratic Chabauty, descent and sieves on $C$ are not addressed.

No septuple is produced and none is excluded. What the four statements do is enlarge the stock of explicit $19$-square tuples by $194$ new centres (re-deriving, but not re-claiming, the $30$ of [1, Section 4.4]), explain why the master centre can never appear in its own ladder, record where the completion loci of the ladder are locally empty, settle the rank of all $423$ sides ($417$ by descent, six by published rational isomorphism, with $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=2$ on those six), and split the Prym factor of the three pilot pair curves into three elliptic quotients, which excludes classical Chabauty on those relaxations. The rank verdict removes exactly the strategies that need a rank-$0$ or rank-$1$ side; a sieve or a Chabauty argument that needs none is not addressed by it.

## 2. Related work and distinction

The ladder device, the keep-keep-fix construction, the universal Jacobian $y^2=x^3-27Ix-27J$, and the first $30$ new centres are [1]; the diagonal-curve Jacobian splitting invoked in Section 4.5 is the established framework of Stoll [14]; the present census reuses that machinery on new input and adds the local and rank verdicts. The $423$ ladder sides fall into $228$ distinct stored $(I,J)$ pairs, but those pairs occupy only $179$ distinct rational-isomorphism classes (distinct $j$-invariants, with the short-Weierstrass coefficients of each equal-$j$ group checked for rational scaling). Three counts are therefore in play and are kept apart throughout: $423$ *jobs* (one per (curve, side) pair), $228$ stored models, and $179$ isomorphism classes. Nine jobs carry two tags, because the same curve occurs in both the M05 and the M06 family, so a count of tag incidences would give $432$; no such count is used. Table 3 is a side count; a rank statement is a statement about an isomorphism class only when the class is named. The orbit mechanism behind the witness families is Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [2]; the regular-quintuple translation and the canonical bank of $92$ almost septuples come from [3] and [4].

The complementary-curve exclusion of [5] concerns the Croatian curve, a different object; none of its completions overlaps our families, and we claim no exclusion. The Konavle framing of [6] parametrizes seed threefolds and Eisenstein triples; its curves are not ladder curves, and the septuple exclusion it cites (on the second curve) is independent of everything here. The fifth $20/21$ almost septuple reported in the forum (post 139) has keep $5$ at its centre and belongs to no ladder family. The rank-one Prym exploited in [7] and the sieve closure of [8] are exactly the steps Proposition 4 tests on the ladder: the gap-based sieve step has no Selmer--Mordell-Weil gap to exploit on these sides, so it cannot start there, while the Prym step is now decided in the negative for classical Chabauty by the elliptic splitting of Proposition 4. The height-certified sieves of [13] take proven saturated Mordell--Weil bases with explicit height bounds as input. This paper does not supply such bases: a proven rank, even with $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=0$, gives neither generator coordinates nor a saturation certificate, and no census record carries a generator field. The rank table is rank data, not sieve input; a future sieve would need freshly computed generators, saturation at every prime and its own height bounds, and it would not inherit the enumeration bounds used here (points to height $10^6$, centres to height $100$). Quadratic Chabauty [9] is the natural remaining method on these Jacobians and is not attempted here. Local dyadic and elliptic obstructions of the kind used in [10] are the model for the Theorem 2 sweep, which finds none.

What the rank census supports, stated at the strength the data allow. (i) *Rank floor on the enumerated sides.* The minimum rank over all $423$ sides is $2$, attained on $15$ old sides; every one of the $324$ new-family sides has rank at least $3$. Strategies that require a rank-$0$ or rank-$1$ side cannot start on these sides. This says nothing about taller points, other carriers, other sextuples or sieves that tolerate higher rank. (ii) *Selmer excess.* With the six M05 rays resolved, $\operatorname{Sha}[2]$ vanishes on $417$ of the $423$ sides and has dimension $2$ on the remaining six, all rays of one family, so a sieve that needs a Selmer--Mordell-Weil gap has nothing to exploit on the other $417$. (iii) *The $19$-ceiling.* All $216$ tuples keep exactly four centre conditions. The visibility theorem explains why the master pattern cannot recur; it does not exclude a fifth kept condition, and the absence of a $20$-square tuple here is a bounded observation at the stated heights, not a structural impossibility. Taken together these are measurements of one device, not a verdict on the ladder programme.

The comparison deciding novelty is therefore direct, and it includes our own predecessor. [1] built the ladder, ran three families and published $30$ centres. This paper adds nine families, hence $194$ new tuples, and re-derives exactly those $30$ centres from the old rung without counting them again: the $42$ rung-five records split as $30$ centres of [1] ($40$ records) plus the $2$ defining centres, and all $30$ are printed in [1, Section 4.4].

The rank table is partly inherited arithmetic, and the inheritance is measured on the curves themselves, not on stored coordinates. The $423$ jobs fall into $228$ distinct stored $(I,J)$ pairs; every side model was compared with the retained per-tuple table of [13] (6{,}363 proven ranks) by rational isomorphism, and $79$ (side, published triple) incidences were confirmed by explicit maps $x_{\text{side}}=u^2x_0+r$ checked coefficient for coefficient. They cover $78$ of the $423$ sides -- the $72$ found before, plus the six S3151 sides whose curve is the published triple $\{7/120,429/40,1064/15\}$ of rank three -- and $32$ distinct published triples, and they lie on old, first-wave and second-wave families alike. The isogeny classes of the $228$ distinct models ($227$ of size $4$, one of size $8$) were then swept against the same table: every class meeting a published $j$-invariant had already been matched by an isomorphism, so isogeny adds no reuse. The remaining $345$ side ranks appear in no published table, including $138$ of the $144$ second-wave sides. At the object level the $78$ reused sides occupy $31$ of the $179$ classes, so $148$ classes lie outside the published table; the $345$ figure is an arithmetic count of unmatched sides and is *not* $345$ independent new rank determinations, since a class carries one rank on all of its sides. The whole re-accounting is recomputed by the verifier from the shipped table, the stored models and the isomorphism certificates (`evidence/f2_qiso_map.json`, `f2_qiso_iso.json`, `f2_isogeny_sweep.json`). Comparisons of arithmetic content should be made between the underlying elliptic curves, not between stored $(I,J)$ pairs: two jobs with the same pair are the same curve, and jobs with different pairs can still be isogenous. What is new here is therefore (i) the nine carrying sextuples and the $194$ new tuples of Tables 1 and 1c, (ii) the visibility explanation and the keep-pattern census, (iii) the local sweep of Theorem 2, and (iv) the independent Mazur confirmation of the pilot Chabauty exclusion already implied by [13, 14], recomputed end to end by the verifier, and (v) the $22$ new ladderable carrier families (with $22$ further $19$-square tuples) from the height-$300$ family search of Section 4.8; the rank census supplies $345$ side ranks outside the published table among $423$ jobs, i.e. $148$ of the $179$ isomorphism classes, including $138$ of the $144$ second-wave sides. The diagonal genus-five splitting used in (iv) belongs to the established framework of Stoll [14, Sections 2--3]; what is new is its explicit instance on the ladder pair curves with certified arithmetic, not the mechanism.

## 3. Objects and methods

### 3.1 The family search

The input is the programme's candidate file (4{,}388 rows). No selection is applied: the $p_{15}$ flag records membership in the corpus of Paper 15 [2], not squareness. Every one of the 4{,}388 rows is a sextuple of distinct nonzero rationals satisfying all $15$ square conditions, counting the square $0$ (S4133's pair $(0,5)$ vanishes); our copy matches the published `data/sextuples.csv` of [12, Section 2] row for row (the recorded SHA-256 differs only by a line-ending artifact: local doubled carriage returns against the published single ones). For every row, every rational centre of height at most $100$ is tested — the height bound is on the centre, not on the sextuple entries. A $4$-keep centre is a rational $c$ with $1+e_ic$ square for exactly four of the six entries, and the search records all of them up to height $100$. Exactly nine sextuples admit such a centre (code/f2_newfam_full.py, certificate `evidence/f2_newfam_full.json`); each is itself Diophantine (all $15$ pairs square), and with its master centre each gives $19$ of $21$ square pairs. Five are listed in Table 1 and four (S1699, S3151, S3875, S3900, all with $p_{15}=0$) in Table 1c; all nine are laddered in this paper, five in the first wave (Section 4.1) and four in the second (Section 4.7). The four of Table 1c lie outside the $p_{15}=1$ slice that the first wave used as its input, which is why the corrected full-population scan matters: they are ordinary corpus sextuples that a slice-based selection silently omits.

### 3.2 The ladder and the audit

For a family with keep set $K$ ($|K|=4$) and bad set $B$ ($|B|=2$), each pair $\{i,j\}\subset K$ and each $d\in B$ gives a curve tag $(\text{fam},i,j\mid d)$; eliminating $c$ through the conic $1+e_ic,1+e_jc\in\mathbf{Q}^{*2}$ gives the quartic $Y^2=G_d(s)$ of [1, Section 3.6], with $G_d$ the cover of Section 3.3 with classical invariants $I,J$ and Jacobian $y^2=x^3-27Ix-27J$. Every quartic is Euler-verified ($\#C(\mathbf{F}_p)=\#E(\mathbf{F}_p)$ at $101,103,107,109,113,127$). Rational points are enumerated completely by `hyperellratpoints` at height $10^6$ and every returned point is mapped to a centre $c$ and audited against all six sextuple entries in exact integer arithmetic: a hot record needs at least four square centre pairs. Distinct centres are deduplicated across curves and ordinate signs.

### 3.3 The completion sweep

A completion needs all six conditions $1+e_ic\in\mathbf{Q}^{*2}$, so a local test has to impose the ladder's own cover $G_d$ together with the extra ones; with $s$ the conic parameter and $t(s)=e_b-e_as^2$,

with $w(s)=w_a(s)=t(s)-2(e_b-e_as)$, so that

$$c(s)=\frac{w^2-t^2}{e_at^2}=\frac{4s(1-s)(e_as-e_b)}{t(s)^2},\qquad G_r(s)=e_a\bigl(e_at^2+e_r(w^2-t^2)\bigr),$$

so $c$ vanishes exactly at $s=0$, $s=1$, $s=e_b/e_a$ and at infinity, and at each of those four points $c=0$ and $G_r(s)=(e_at(s))^2$ is a square for every $r$. The boundary is therefore a rational point of every simultaneous system, and a count that includes it can never be obstructed. We report two counts at each prime. The *complete* model counts every class and is guaranteed nonempty; it is recorded only to display that vacuity. The *punctured* model removes the four boundary classes and the two pole classes $t(s)=0$ and is the honest test, under two hypotheses at $p$: the kept entries are nonzero mod $p$ (else the parametrization degenerates and the prime is excluded outright), and no removed zero class coincides with a pole class (a collision makes the reduced quotient $0/0$). Where both hold, emptiness at $p$ forces $p$ to divide the numerator or the denominator of the centre of every rational completion; at collision primes the emptiness is stated only as a restriction on reductions. Every reported emptiness is audited for collisions. Tests are run for $4$, $5$ and $6$ centre conditions, that is $\{d,r_1\}$, $\{d,r_1,r_2\}$ and $\{d,r_1,r_2,r_3\}$, at all $303$ primes up to $2000$ on the $36$ rung-five and $60$ new-family curves, with a planted-obstruction control (one cover negated) and a random-cover baseline, both on the punctured model.

### 3.4 The rank census

For each of the $423$ jobs (curve, side; $228$ distinct stored $(I,J)$ pairs): the quartic's invariants $I,J$ are recomputed from the entries (old jobs must re-match the stored table exactly before any stored field is reused); the Jacobian model is Euler-verified; the full rational torsion and its $\mathbf{F}_2$-dimension are computed; the $2$-Selmer dimension comes from PARI (120s alarm); then `mwrank` proves the rank (300s alarm), with proven generators recorded whenever the rank is at most $1$. The acceptance rule is $\text{rank}\leq \text{selmer}-\dim E(\mathbf{Q})[2]$, with equality certifying $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=0$. Six Selmer-$8$ jobs whose first `mwrank` search ended inconclusively were retried through a fresh `Mwrank` object at search bounds $8/10/12$ (subgroup rank $0$), then searched to height $16$ with saturation (subgroup rank $4$), against the $2$-descent upper bound $6$, with no generators; so `certain()` stayed false and these six are recorded as the interval $[4,6]$ (Section 4.4). Each curve contributes three sides, so the $423$ jobs sit on $228$ distinct models; Table 3 counts sides. The six open sides are settled in Section 4.4 by rational isomorphism with a published rank-$4$ triple, not by new descent. The rebuilt retry script `code/f2_rank_retry6.sage.py` reproduces the recorded run `notes/f2_rank_retry6.log` on all six jobs (`notes/f2_rank_retry6_rerun.log`: retry-$12$ rank $0$, final rank $4$, bound $6$, root number $+1$, generator query raising exactly the recorded `AttributeError`).

### 3.5 The pilots

The $15$ excess-$2$ quartics are ranked exactly (same pipeline) to test sieve feasibility. For the $3$ both-sides-excess-$2$ pairs, the pair curve $C$ (fibre product of the two sides over the $s$-line) is built explicitly, its factor $D$ checked smooth of genus $3$, and $\operatorname{rank}J(C)\geq 4$ certified from the two elliptic factors.

### 4.5 Pilots

All $15$ excess-$2$ quartics have proven rank exactly $2$ with $\operatorname{Sha}[2]=0$: the Selmer excess is genuine rank, so the gap-based sieve strategy, which needs a nonzero gap between the Selmer group and the Mordell–Weil group, has nothing to exploit on these sides. This is a statement about gap-requiring strategies only; a sieve run without a gap, as in [11], is not excluded by it.

The pilot curve is the fibre product $C$ of Proposition 4 (four centre conditions, the ladder's own $G_d$ omitted): a relaxation of the $20$-square intermediate target, which imposes five conditions and is the genus-$17$ curve of [1, Proposition 2] (the full septuple target imposes all six centre conditions; its affine model has genus $49$). Every conclusion below is a statement about $C$, not about the completion problem.

On $C$ the Prym factor splits completely. With $L_i=1+e_ic$ the genus-three curve $D$ ($U^2=G_rG_s$, rescaled ordinate) has $z^2=L_rL_s$, so

$$\mathbf{Q}(D)=\mathbf{Q}(c)\bigl(\sqrt{L_a},\sqrt{L_b},\sqrt{L_rL_s}\bigr),$$

and the three genus-one character quotients are $E_{ars}:y^2=L_aL_rL_s$, $E_{brs}:y^2=L_bL_rL_s$ and $E_{abrs}:y^2=L_aL_bL_rL_s$, reached by multiplying $z$ by $u_a$, $u_b$ and $u_au_b$. Since $\dim J(D)=3$ and the three quotients realize the three distinct nontrivial sign-change characters, $J(D)\sim E_{ars}\times E_{brs}\times E_{abrs}$ over $\mathbf{Q}$, and $\operatorname{rank}J(D)$ is the sum of the three elliptic ranks by isogeny invariance. The quotient identities were checked on twelve exact rational points of $D$ (thirty-six identities, `code/f2_prym_elliptic2.sage.py`, certificate `evidence/f2_prym_elliptic.json`); the verifier re-checks the twenty-five stored quotient-point identities and all twenty-five mapped model incidences from the shipped data.

Infinite order is certified by reduction, not by floating-point heights. PARI's `ellrank` fails on these curves (`bug in small_norm`) and no `eclib` binary is installed here, so no two-descent upper bound is claimed; instead an explicit rational point on a quotient is reduced modulo a small good prime, and if the reduced order is divisible by a prime $\ell>7$, Mazur's classification of rational torsion (every $E(\mathbf{Q})_{tors}$ has all prime divisors at most $7$) forces the point to have infinite order. Two of the three factors on each pilot carry such a Mazur-certified point, and points on distinct character factors are independent via the product decomposition, so $\operatorname{rank}J(D)\ge2$ rigorously. The three pilots give

| pilot | side ranks (census) | $\operatorname{rank}J(D)\ge$ | $\operatorname{rank}J(C)\ge$ | $g(C)$ | Chabauty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M05$(0,1\mid5)$ pair $(2,3)$ | $2,2$ | $2$ | $6$ | $5$ | impossible |
| M05$(1,2\mid5)$ pair $(0,3)$ | $2,2$ | $2$ | $6$ | $5$ | impossible |
| M03$(1,2\mid5)$ pair $(0,3)$ | $2,2$ | $2$ | $6$ | $5$ | impossible |

### 4.9 Novelty: published extensions and the regular baseline

The $194$ ladder centres and the $22$ carrier centres were compared with the published extension tables of [13] on that paper’s own shipped data (`data/elements.txt`, $4{,}595$ elements; `data/sextuples759.txt`, $759$ sextuples; `results/quadruples11274.tsv`, $11{,}274$ quadruples with their published `hits`, including the values $r_0,r_1$ of `regular_quint`). Up to global sign, $47$ of the $216$ centres occur there: all $20$ S0355 centres and all $22$ S0979 centres -- each the published $r_0$ or $r_1$ of a $4$-subset of the negated published sextuple -- plus the carrier centres S0024, S0242, S0602, S0957 and S2095. For S0355, the first Appendix-A centre $-15052365912064/11932485561649$ is the published $r_0$ of the quadruple $\{184175/1957072,\,195/64,\,231/52,\,17391/52\}$, the row with element ids $2310,2312,2313,2315$ of that table. Checking only the $30$ centres of [1, Section 4.4] and the $92$-row almost-septuple bank therefore does not establish novelty.

The classical comparison is the *regular* completion. For a Diophantine quadruple $\{a,b,c,d\}$ the two regular extensions are

$$e_\pm=\frac{(a+b+c+d)(abcd+1)+2(abc+abd+acd+bcd)\pm2\sqrt{\prod_{i<j}(1+a_ia_j)}}{(abcd-1)^2},$$

and [13]’s `regular_quint` is used verbatim for the evaluation. Applied to each centre’s own kept quadruple, these two extensions reproduce $203$ of the $216$ centres ($191$ of the $194$ ladder centres and $12$ of the $22$ carrier centres): the ladder mostly returns the routine regular completion of a sub-quadruple. That is a fact about the device, measured here rather than left implicit, and it is the baseline against which the census has to be read. The $13$ exceptions are S2429’s $-14784/4489$, S3875’s $-37323/35344$, S3900’s $18172/21675$, and ten carrier centres; $4$ of the $13$ are published elements, so $9$ tuples are new. Those $9$ are the new tuple output of this paper.

The audit is exact integer arithmetic in the Python standard library (`code/f2_r7_audit.py`, output `evidence/f2_r7_audit.json`) and is recomputed by the verifier from the shipped [13] tables. Its scope is those tables: extensions published elsewhere are not excluded by it.

## 5. Limitations and scope

The censuses are finite: parameter heights above $10^6$ (a bound on the conic parameter $s$, hence on the centres it produces), sextuples outside the nine families and the corpus, and local primes above $2000$ are not covered. The keep-pattern gaps of Proposition 1c are height-limited statements about two families, not theorems about all families. Unlike [1, Section 4.4], the new-family run has no permutation or perturbation null controls; its audit is exact per-point arithmetic, but a pipeline-level control remains future work. The Selmer dimensions are PARI computations run under SageMath $10.10$ (bundled eclib and PARI; `Em.selmer_rank()`, `Em.rank()`, `mwrank_MordellWeil` search/saturate), and the standalone verifier needs only the Python standard library. The shipped scripts regenerate every intermediate they consume: `f2_rung5_pairs.py` and `f2_rung5_rank.sage.py` rebuild the rung-five rank table, `f2_ladder_newfam.sage.py` rebuilds the first-wave ladder records, `f2_ladder_newfam4.sage.py` (shipped) re-runs the second-wave ladder enumeration against the published corpus of [12] as a pinned dependency, and `f2_rank_wave2.sage.py` consumes the shipped compact family file `evidence/f2_ladder_newfam4.json` (sextuples verified against the slim record), `f2_local_v2.py` (from `f2_rung5_local.py`/`f2_newfam_local.py`) rebuilds the sweep records, `f2_rank_full.sage.py` and `f2_rank_wave2.sage.py` consume those outputs for the rank census (models, torsions, conductors and dimensions are all recorded for re-running), and `f2_prym_mazur.sage.py` rebuilds the rigorous Prym rank certificates of Proposition 4 from the sextuple entries. The six M05 sides are exact at rank $4$ by the published isomorphism, and our own descent supplies only the enclosure $[4,6]$; the $\operatorname{Sha}[2]$-dimension $2$ on them is conditional on the recorded PARI Selmer dimension, which we did not rerun. What is shipped for the ladder is the slim record `evidence/f2_waves9_slim.json`: all nine families, all $194$ centres, all $108$ per-curve point counts, and all $3526$ hot records with their conic parameters. The verifier re-maps every hot record to its centre from the stored parameter and re-audits it; the full $45{,}314$-point coordinate sets are recorded by count (the generating scripts and their logs are shipped) rather than re-enumerated, and the models, torsion, Selmer dimensions and ranks remain recorded Sage/PARI runs. The 4{,}388-row candidate file itself (1.09 MB) is not shipped, since it would exceed the supplement budget; its row counts and SHA-256 are recorded in `evidence/f2_accounting.json`, and our copy matches the published corpus of [12] row for row. The scan certificate `evidence/f2_newfam_full.json` records all nine carrying sextuples with their master centres; the verifier re-checks every recorded hit exactly (all $15$ pairs square, master centre keeps exactly four) and re-runs the height-$100$ scan on the shipped selection `evidence/corpus1089_entries.json`, and the retry reconstruction (`code/f2_rank_retry6.sage.py` with output `evidence/f2_rank_retry6_rerun.log`) reproduces the recorded six-job retry (subgroup rank $4$, bound $6$, root number $+1$, the same generator-query failure) on all six jobs, so the census is reproduced from shipped data as far as the supplement budget allows. The count of new tuples is $9$, not $216$: $47$ of the $216$ occur in the published extension tables of [13] up to global sign, $203$ are the regular extension of their own kept quadruple, and only the $9$ centres that are neither regular nor published are new tuples. The $30$ centres of [1, Section 4.4] are re-derived and not counted. No septuple is excluded, and the absence of a $20$-square tuple is a statement about the enumeration heights $10^6$ (curves) and $300$ (centres) only.
