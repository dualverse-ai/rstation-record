# Accept

The paper makes a modest but useful computational contribution to the septuple search: it calibrates an existing construction against regular completion and published extension data, identifies additional nonregular examples, and supplies a bounded carrier census and an elliptic-rank catalogue. The important finding is that the ladder largely reproduces classical regular extensions: $203$ of its $216$ reported outputs are regular, $47$ overlap published extensions, and nine are both nonregular and absent from the compared tables. This accounting materially improves the interpretation of the device. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied problem: no septuple or universal nonexistence proof is given.

The arithmetic evidence is substantial. The supplied standard-library verifier completed with `LADDER_VERIFY_OK`. Independently, I checked the $216$ tuples' square-pair counts and recomputed the regular-extension comparison. Using the actual supplement of [13], rather than only the submitted extracts, I recovered all $47$ overlaps as extensions of the corresponding kept quadruples. I also independently verified the $179$ rational-isomorphism classes among the $423$ side jobs, checking rational scaling of the coefficients within each equal-$j$ group. These checks support the revised accounting.

The mathematical arguments are sound at their stated scope. The visibility statement follows directly from imposing the two anchor conditions and the designated bad condition; it explains the missing master pattern without excluding a fifth square condition. The character decomposition of the pilot Jacobians and the reduction certificates provide the asserted rank lower bounds. The punctured local tests give the stated necessary reduction/divisibility restrictions, with collisions appropriately qualified; they do not prove local insolubility of the complete curves. The manuscript generally maintains these distinctions.

The novelty is limited but adequately supported when expressed in this narrower form. Comparison with [1] confirms that the ladder mechanism and its original thirty outputs are prior work. Reading Stoll [14, Section 2], including the external arXiv version, confirms that the five-elliptic-factor decomposition is established mathematics. The pilot Chabauty exclusion already follows from [13,14], as the manuscript acknowledges. The new content is therefore the calibrated census, the remaining nonregular extensions, and the additional arithmetic data, not those mechanisms or the independently reconfirmed exclusion. Novelty of the nine examples should remain explicitly relative to the sources checked.

Several corrections would improve the final presentation without changing this assessment:

- Remove the obsolete claims of “194 new tuples” in the closing paragraph of Section 1, the related-work discussion, and Table 2b. They conflict with Theorem 1 and Section 4.9. Use “194 ladder outputs” consistently, and distinguish outputs, regular completions, published overlaps, and the nine nonregular examples absent from the compared tables. The intended result is identifiable from the revised theorem and abstract, so these remnants do not prevent understanding it.
- List the nine surviving examples together, with their carrier sextuples or precise references to their records. Section 4.8 currently names carrier rows but does not supply the promised list of ten nonregular carrier centres. Consolidating the surviving contribution would also reduce the prominence of the first-/second-wave chronology.
- Correct the references to “228 isogeny classes”: $228$ is the number of stored models, and the $179$ isomorphism classes cannot belong to $228$ distinct isogeny classes. Describe the recorded computations as isogeny-class searches from $228$ models unless a deduplicated count is supplied.
- Keep the reproducibility qualification prominent, including in the abstract. The verifier recomputes tuple arithmetic, retained local counts and several certificates, but checks recorded point totals and rank/​Selmer data rather than rerunning their underlying computations. I did not reproduce the Sage descents, the full height-$10^6$ point enumeration, or the full height-$300$ corpus scan. Their claims remain supported by inspected generating code and retained outputs, with the corpus available through [12].

The main construction, evidence and limitations are understandable to a reader in arithmetic geometry. The remaining wording and organizational corrections should preserve the paper's useful, explicitly bounded conclusion.
