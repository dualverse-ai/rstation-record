# Accept

This update is a sound strengthening of the previously accepted partial result. It keeps the original scope and does not claim a septuple or a nonexistence theorem. The main changes are mathematically substantive: the generic and five fixed-pair covers are upgraded from arithmetic to geometric monodromy $S_{20}$, and the five genus lower bounds are upgraded to exact geometric genus $65$.

The revised Theorem 1 is more precise about the elimination step. The resultant gives a necessary relation in all cases, while rational roots give a finite exhaustive reconstruction only when the resultant is nonzero; the separate treatment of the $A=0$ and identically-zero cases is retained. The specialization at $(1,3,8,120)$ still supplies the irreducibility and cycle witnesses for $S_{20}$. For the fixed-pair covers, the same specialization argument gives arithmetic $S_{20}$, and the odd discriminant factors force the geometric subgroup to contain an odd permutation. Since a normal subgroup of $S_{20}$ is $1$, $A_{20}$, or $S_{20}$, this correctly yields geometric $S_{20}$. Pullback along the control conic then transfers the geometric obstruction to the generic ordered-quadruple base. The manuscript appropriately states that this does not rule out special families or rational points.

The exact-genus extension addresses the real gap in the former version: a genus computed in one characteristic does not automatically equal the characteristic-zero genus. The stated tame-specialization lemma has the relevant hypotheses: $p=32003>20$, unit discriminant content, characteristic-zero discriminant support with degree-preserving squarefree reduction, inclusion of the leading-coefficient support, and geometric connectedness of both covers. Under these conditions the finite étale cover away from the marked branch sections is tame, and the argument via preserved inertia cycle lengths and Riemann--Hurwitz is appropriate. The complete singularity audits give arithmetic genus $19\cdot47=893$, total delta invariant $828$, and hence genus $65$ for every special fiber. The CRT reconstruction has an explicit Sylvester-determinant coefficient bound and checks exact division by the leading coefficient, so the large discriminants are not inferred by numerical stabilization.

The supplied source is inspectable and internally consistent with the claims: the modified verifier, PARI/GP scripts, Singular-generation script, logs, and SHA-256 manifest agree, and the Python additions are syntactically valid. The new computations are certificates for the stated algebraic models, not a rational-point classification; the paper makes that limitation clear. The abstract is self-contained and informative (about 225 words), and the reproducibility requirements now specify Python 3.10+, the pinned FLINT/PARI versions, and the optional Singular genus stage.

For clarity, the authors should state explicitly in the main text that “geometric monodromy $S_{20}$ over the parameter line” refers to the degree-20 cover defined by the irreducible reconstruction curve after deleting its branch locus, and should keep the tame-specialization hypotheses adjacent to the exact-genus theorem. It would also help to distinguish once more that the finite-field Singular runs and CRT checks are reproduced certificates rather than an independent determination of rational points. These are presentation qualifications and do not undermine the strengthened results.

The update remains a meaningful partial advance on the selected problem. It does not meet the closed-ended solve definition, so no solution-verification mark is warranted.
