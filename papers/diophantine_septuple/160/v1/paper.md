# Mordell–Weil ranks and the three rational sign orbits of 92 five-spoke extension curves

A five-entry core shared by two rational Diophantine sextuples offers a direct route to a septuple. A new common neighbour that also joins either sixth entry gives all 21 squares. The known sixths supply rational points on the full extension curve and many of its quotients. How much Mordell–Weil rank do those points already force?

In each canonical catalogue row AS001–AS092 [1,2], remove the unique pair whose product plus one fails to be a square. Write $a_1,\dots,a_5$ for the common entries and $f,g$ for the removed pair in increasing order. Both $f$ and $g$ extend the five entries. Let $C$ be the smooth projective curve with function field

$$\mathbb Q(X)(\sqrt{1+a_1X},\ldots,\sqrt{1+a_5X}).$$

**Theorem.** For all 92 rows, $C$ has genus 17, the rational Néron–Severi rank of its Jacobian is 16 and its Mordell–Weil rank over $\mathbb Q$ is at least 61. The certified lower bounds for individual rows run from 61 to 72. Every nonzero abelian quotient $B/\mathbb Q$ of this Jacobian satisfies

$$\operatorname{rank}B(\mathbb Q)\ \ge\ \dim B+\rho_{\mathbb Q}(B).$$

Thus the standard sufficient quadratic Chabauty rank inequality $\operatorname{rank}B(\mathbb Q)<\dim B+\rho_{\mathbb Q}(B)-1$ fails on every such quotient [3,4]. The existence of additional rational sixth entries and of a rational septuple remains open.

**Orbit theorem.** The subgroup of $\operatorname{Jac}(C)(\mathbb Q)$ generated by the pairwise divisor differences of the rational lifts of $X=0,f,g$ has rank exactly 48 on 86 rows and exactly 47 on the other six.

Paper 111 [5] proved independence of two marked points on 300 quintic genus-two quotients associated with a different sample of quintuples. Paper 76 [3] determined a quadratic Chabauty rank barrier on one particular antipodal five-set with repeated elliptic isogeny factors. Here the input is the common core of each catalogued seven-set with twenty rational edges. On these cores two sixths are available simultaneously. We determine the rank of their sign orbits, separate every simple factor over $\mathbb Q$ and obtain a quotient-wise rank comparison for the full sample. The statement concerns these 92 rows. Infinite families of almost septuples are known [6].

## Character quotients

The five different finite branch points give independent square classes by their valuations. Infinity is the sixth branch point. Each of the six inertia groups has order two. Riemann–Hurwitz for the degree-32 cover gives $2g-2=-64+6\cdot16=32$.

For each nonempty subset $I$, the associated sign character gives $D_I:Y^2=\prod_{i\in I}(1+a_iX)$. Pulling back its differentials gives a subspace on which signs act by that character. The ten subsets of size three and the five of size four each contribute one dimension, and the subset of size five contributes two. These sum to 17. Thus the product of their Jacobians is isogenous over $\mathbb Q$ to $\operatorname{Jac}(C)$.

On each $D_I$ use the points above zero, the two removed entries, and each common entry outside $I$. Take the product of the nonnegative rational spoke roots for the ordinate. For a triple $a,b,c$ put $m=abc$. The cubic $v^2=(u+ab)(u+ac)(u+bc)$ has coordinates $u=mX,\ v=mY$. For a quartet $a,b,c,d$ put $L=\prod_{i=a,b,c}(1-i/d)$. Use $u=L/(1+dX),\ v=LY/(1+dX)^2$. Its three shifts are $Li/(d-i)$. This places the fourth rational branch point at infinity. For the quintet put $M=\prod a_i$. The monic quintic has shifts $M/a_i$ and coordinates $u=MX,\ v=M^2Y$. Use its rational point at infinity as the divisor origin. Every elliptic quotient has full rational two-torsion.

## Marked rank certificates

At good $p\ne5$ with $5\mid N_p$ and $25\nmid N_p$, multiplication by $N_p/5$ maps a finite reduction group of order $N_p$ onto its cyclic order-five subgroup. Express the image of each mark relative to the first nonzero image. Independent rows prove a rank lower bound once rational five-torsion has been excluded. In the elliptic case this follows from Mazur's theorem and full rational two-torsion. Each genus-two certificate supplies a good prime different from five where the order is prime to five. Paper 111 gives this reduction method and related computations for other curve samples [5].

## Rational Néron–Severi groups and all quotients

Within each row the fifteen elliptic curves have pairwise different vectors of traces at common good primes. Rationally isogenous curves would have identical good traces. For the genus-two factor $A$, two good primes have irreducible degree-four Frobenius polynomials. Already one proves $A$ simple over $\mathbb Q$. A rational isogeny splitting would factor its good Frobenius polynomial over $\mathbb Q$.

Write the good polynomial as $h(t)=t^4+c_3t^3+c_2t^2+pc_3t+p^2$. When $h$ is irreducible, the endomorphism algebra over the residue field is $\mathbb Q(\pi)$. The Frobenius centralizer on a rational Tate module has dimension four and $\mathbb Q[\pi]$ already has that dimension. The Rosati involution sends $\pi$ to $p/\pi$. Its fixed subfield is generated by $\pi+p/\pi$ with discriminant $c_3^2-4(c_2-2p)$.

Specialization embeds rational endomorphisms over $\mathbb Q$ into endomorphisms over the residue field and respects Rosati. If the rational symmetric subspace had dimension two, it would map onto the real quadratic subfield at both primes. The rational algebra it generates would be a quadratic field isomorphic to both. The two positive discriminants have different square classes in every row. Hence $\rho_{\mathbb Q}(A)=1$. Each elliptic factor also has rational Néron–Severi rank one. All sixteen factors are simple and pairwise nonisogenous over $\mathbb Q$. Every rational abelian quotient is isogenous to a subproduct with multiplicities at most one. Its rational Néron–Severi ranks add. The rank of every factor is at least its dimension plus one, proving the quotient inequality.

## Exact rank of the three sign orbits

Every fibre over $0,f,g$ has 32 rational points. For each character, the sign action either fixes or negates the projected Abel–Jacobi class, taking a rational branch point as origin on that quotient. The projection of the subgroup of divisor differences, after tensoring with $\mathbb Q$, equals the span of the three marked classes. A difference of opposite sign lifts of one abscissa gives twice its class, and every difference projects into the span. The subgroup is stable under all signs, hence under the rational character idempotents after tensoring with $\mathbb Q$. Its rank is the sum of the sixteen projected ranks.

Restrict every reduction matrix to the three columns $0,f,g$. The resulting rank is three except in six elliptic characters where it is two. Each of these satisfies the exact relation $2P_0+sP_f+tP_g=O$, giving the matching upper bound. The triples (row, $s,t$) are (AS051,−1,1), (AS053,1,1), (AS054,1,1), (AS068,1,1), (AS085,−1,1) and (AS087,1,−1). AS053 uses a quartet. The other five use triples. The exact subsets are in orbits.json.

There is a common interpolation formula for the six exceptions. Write $F(X)=\prod_{i\in I}(1+a_iX)$, and let $S_j$ be its coefficient of $X^j$, taking $S_4=0$ on the cubics. The parabola

$$L(X)=1+\tfrac12 S_1X+hX^2$$

is tangent to the positive point above zero. At every exceptional marking there is a rational $h$ for which $L(f)=sY_f$ and $L(g)=tY_g$. In catalogue order AS051, AS053, AS054, AS068, AS085, AS087 the coefficients are

$$-20358/14641,\quad 626625/83006,\quad 8107/46208,\quad -1/2,\quad -1333/1445,\quad -1546561/1722368.$$

Put $e=S_1^2/4-S_2$. Factoring $L^2-F$ after dividing by $X^2$ gives

$$ f+g=\frac{S_3-S_1h}{h^2-S_4},\qquad fg=\frac{2h+e}{h^2-S_4}.$$

In particular the remaining mutual edge has radicand $((h+1)^2-(1-e+S_4))/(h^2-S_4)$. This formula retains the scale on the quartet exception. The initial slopes and every asserted contact are checked over $\mathbb Q$. This double contact accounts for the common coefficient two in the six relations.

## Reproduction and references

Run **sage -python verify.py** in the extracted directory with SageMath, cypari2 and NumPy. The verifier rechecks every input edge, each finite-group reduction matrix and its rank, the six rational relations and the good-prime separations. It recomputes the genus-two Frobenius polynomials by point counting over the fields of $p$ and $p^2$ elements, and uses Sage's Jacobian group law. The discovery calculation used PARI Frobenius polynomials, FLINT polynomial arithmetic for Cantor addition and separate elliptic group-law routines.
