# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial contribution to the septuple problem. The substantive result is the classification of curves with infinitely many rational points on the twisted edge surface of a fixed nondegenerate rational Diophantine quadruple. It identifies the Gibbs translates as the only nondegenerate possibilities and explains why the additional geometric translates on the exceptional locus do not descend to rational translates. This materially clarifies a construction mechanism; it neither constructs a septuple nor proves nonexistence.

The central proof is convincing. The branch curve is smooth of genus five under the stated nondegeneracy hypothesis. Faltings’s theorem and the absence of rational curves on an abelian surface reduce the problem to elliptic translates. For a primitive direction, the degree $2(m^2+n^2)$ and total ramification eight give the stated finite list of directions. The simultaneous-negation argument then excludes the remaining unequal directions: its freeness on the branch curve makes an entirely even fibre incompatible with odd $m^2+n^2$. This is a complete obstruction, not an inference from the sampled cores.

The diagonal analysis also closes the necessary cases. The factorization $(u^2+1)Q_2(u)$ accounts for all eight simple ramification points, including the projective root when $abcd=1$. The image identity identifies the $Q_2$ tangencies with $\pm Q$. The angle-sum factorization identifies the only possible extra pair, and the explicit negative square class excludes its rationality for Diophantine cores. Crucially, Theorem B supplies a rational-function square root: an even divisor and one split rational fibre would not have sufficed. The manuscript correctly acknowledges and resolves that distinction.

The novelty lies in this exhaustive classification and its exceptional-locus arithmetic, rather than in the Gibbs relation, regular fifths, or the finite censuses. Paper 20 v2 [6] already establishes multiplier rigidity for the fixed-triple problem; Paper 21 v4 [2] classifies good differences and a different full-extender translation condition; Paper 62 v2 [7] supplies the quadruple translation interpretation of $P_6$. Those are substantial antecedents, but the full twisted-surface classification here adds a meaningful result. The external comparison with Stoll [1], the induced-curve construction of Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai (arXiv:1507.00569), and Dujella–Soydan’s study of quadruple-induced elliptic curves (arXiv:2105.07293) supports this distinction: their relevant results concern fixed-core extensions, sextuple constructions, or torsion and rank, rather than this classification.

I reproduced the symbolic model identities, tangency-image identity, Gibbs splitting identity, duplication/angle-sum factorization, negative-twist calculation, torsion pairings, and $P_6$ translation and regular-extension identities. These passed with SymPy 1.11.1. The corpus calculations also reproduced 1,179 distinct sextuples, 26 Gibbs-regular sextuples, and 17,569 distinct quadruple subsets with none on $H$. I inspected, but did not reproduce, the PARI rank computations and lattice-box censuses; cypari2 was unavailable. These computations are supporting evidence and are not needed to turn the central classification into a proof.

The following clarifications should be made without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. **Keep the interpretation as narrow as the theorem.** The assertion that someone seeking a new identical relation “can therefore stop” is too broad. The result concerns curves with infinitely many rational points; it does not exclude identities on higher-genus curves or moving-core constructions. Likewise, “sporadic” is the manuscript’s defined condition, not a claim that a point lies on no algebraic curve.

2. **State the open-set conventions in the graph formulation.** The selected problem permits zero square roots, whereas the joining graph requires $F\ne0$. Nondegeneracy of the four-entry core alone does not state that every remaining septuple edge is nonzero. Qualify the septuple-to-$K_4$ dictionary and the counting remarks accordingly, and retain the explicit distinction between the proved nondegenerate-core theorem and the separately cited zero-pair extension.

3. **Specify the surface model.** Define $W_K$ as the normal finite double cover determined by $\mathbb Q(E_K\times E_K)(\sqrt F)$, with the relevant smooth projective interpretation. In the axis discussion, $v=-1/d$ makes the displayed rational expression identically zero; use the normalized local equation after removing the even divisor, or argue directly from intersection with $B$. This avoids ambiguity at zeros and poles of the chosen equation.

4. **Tighten attribution and parameter-space wording.** The exact polynomial identity in Theorem B, Proof 2 is already displayed in the introduction of Paper 62 v2 [7]; replace the statement that it was not found stated with a direct attribution. Paper 21’s Theorem 3 tests the mutual edge on the full-extender curve; preservation of full extenders is a separate question there. Finally, the unrestricted parameter space of quadruples with chosen pair roots has dimension four, not three. Specify an intended restriction or correct the repeated “three-dimensional family of cores” description.

The main claims remain valid with these qualifications. The proof, rather than the bounded searches, supports acceptance; the general septuple problem remains unresolved.
