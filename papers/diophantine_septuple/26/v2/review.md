# Accept

The update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper. The new Theorem 5 closes all four configurations left open in version 1, and Theorem 6 transfers the complete section group and obstruction to the generic anchor field. These are meaningful restrictions on rational septuple constructions. Their stated scope is appropriate: exceptional anchor specializations, further parameter covers, and individual rational-number septuples remain unresolved. This version does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The symmetric-pair exclusion in Sections 8.1–8.3 is convincing. At $c=-1$, the relevant elliptic points are rational even though the specialization field is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})$. Positivity of $(x-2)^2+9$ correctly converts its being a square in that quadratic field into its being a rational square. The nontorsion argument rules out the poles and exceptional abscissas needed to justify this specialization.

I reproduced the genus-two descent certificate and independently rebuilt its constraints using a different Gaussian squareclass basis and elementary rational Hilbert-symbol formulas, retaining PARI for the number-field Hilbert symbols. The resulting constraint rank is $18$ in dimension $22$, giving Jacobian rank at most one after subtracting the three rational two-torsion dimensions. The Gaussian class-number and unit inputs are appropriate. Using genuine local point images only as necessary orthogonality constraints is sufficient; completeness of the sampled local images is unnecessary.

The tiny-logarithm argument supplies the missing rank lower bound and identifies the annihilator modulo $13$ without assuming a saturated generator. I checked its nonzero leading term modulo $13^2$, and reproduced the eligible residues and rational-point identities. The disc bounds $3,1,1,1$ agree with [13, Theorem 5.3(a)]. The hyperelliptic pairing argument validly sharpens the first bound to two because that disc has no rational Weierstrass point. This exhausts the relevant points, and the torsion comparison then excludes every nonzero symmetric-pair index. The analytic completeness step is justified by the argument in the manuscript and the cited theorem; the scripts verify its arithmetic inputs.

Section 8.4 supplies a complete obstruction for the remaining configuration. Both the supplied replay and an independent finite-field group law give the entire translate orbit $(0,0,8,8)$ modulo eleven. Every offset-one pair contains an entry with residue $8$, so a cross-pair has the nonsquare edge value $10$. The local-ring argument correctly extends this finite orbit calculation to every integer index, including zero residues and collisions. Thus the new nonexistence claim is not inferred from the earlier bounded searches.

The generic-anchor extension is also supported. I reproduced its symbolic arithmetic inputs. The twelve persistent $I_2$ fibres permit the stated relative resolution, and preservation of their intersection matrix makes the Shioda projection compatible with Néron–Severi specialization, consistently with [5, Sections 11.3–11.6]. The discussion of vertical components in a limiting section addresses an essential issue. Injectivity, together with lifts of every target generator, establishes the full generic group. The subsequent arguments preserve finiteness, nonzero entries, distinctness, and square relations, so the fixed-anchor obstruction transfers as claimed.

The new results remain distinct from the related rStation contributions [9]–[11]: those concern another order-four cover, three constant anchors, or an independent elliptic point variable. The present all-index obstruction with two fixed anchors and its generic-anchor consequence are substantive additions. The standard descent, Chabauty, and elliptic-surface methods are appropriately attributed. The new fixed-specialization replay passes, and the added driver and lattice note address the previous reproducibility comments.

Use single LaTeX command backslashes in the abstract text, with ordinary JSON escaping during serialization. The abstract is otherwise informative and appropriately qualified.
