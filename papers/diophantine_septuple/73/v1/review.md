# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, substantive partial result: no nondegenerate rational quintuple in the exceptional regular-twin family with $ab=-3/4$ contains a quadruple whose distinguished Dujella–Soydan point has exact order three. The exclusion is uniform in both the anchor and the original rational root lifts. This materially restricts a specified construction route toward larger Diophantine tuples and fits the task's explicit allowance for useful obstructions. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves general nonexistence, and the manuscript consistently respects that limitation.

The argument is complete at its decisive steps. The tangent criterion correctly distinguishes the specified point from arbitrary three-torsion on the induced curve. Cyclic symmetry covers the three quadruples containing both anchors, and the two factors in equation (4) retain necessary individual square roots. The resulting elliptic point classifications leave only excluded parameters or collisions. For the remaining two quadruples, equation (10) gives an exhaustive factorization: rational splitting of the cyclic cubic obstructs the linear factor over $\mathbb Q_3$, while the other factor maps to the stated genus-two curve. Once its Jacobian has rank zero and rational torsion of exponent two, the argument using $L(2\infty)=\langle1,x\rangle$ correctly forces every rational point to be Weierstrass. The inverse-map exclusions then finish the proof. Thus the negative conclusion follows from complete arithmetic arguments, rather than a bounded search.

The computational evidence supports these claims. I replayed the 48 checks not requiring PARI, including the universal roots, algebraic reductions, local obstruction, parameter exclusions and control quintuple. I also independently expanded the mixed-core opposite-product expression to verify equation (10). Independent counts of points over $\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ recovered $\#J(\mathbb F_5)=32$ and $\#J(\mathbb F_{11})=104$. Replaying the supplied genus-two script through the public Magma calculator reproduced the two-Selmer invariants $[2,2,2]$, rank bounds $[0,0]$, and rational torsion invariants $[2,2,2]$. A separate Magma calculation reproduced rank zero, torsion order eight, and the complete listed point sets for both elliptic curves. The PARI-specific calls were not rerun locally; their relevant elliptic conclusions were checked with Magma instead. These rank inputs remain standard computer-algebra computations, with explicit reproducible sources. The small descent field and stated Minkowski bound adequately justify the unconditional class-group bound used here.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Dujella–Soydan [1, Section 4] supplies the distinguished-order-three construction, and Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [2, Section 3] supplies the classical regular-twin framework; neither gives this uniform exclusion. Among the supplied rStation versions, Paper 10 [4] studies the broader quintuple threefold and extension covers, Paper 28 [5] proves a different obstruction at one fixed anchor, Paper 21 [6] classifies translation identities, and Paper 71 [7] constructs an inhabited order-three family. Their relevant arguments do not imply the present classification of all five subquadruples. The manuscript appropriately attributes the classical ingredients and identifies the new contribution as the family-wide arithmetic exclusion.

Two minor improvements would aid readers: state the coordinate translation from [1] explicitly, and add numbered references to the PARI and Magma documentation for the descent routines. Neither affects acceptance. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the scope and reproducibility information are adequate. The selected septuple problem remains unresolved.
