# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its value is the reversible description of two triples sharing both regular fourths, the construction that preserves the square between those fourths, and the obstruction to equal opposite cross products. The infinite seventeen-edge families and the explicitly lifted genus-two curve demonstrate that the construction has ordinary rational points. The paper does not construct a septuple or prove nonexistence, and correctly leaves the remaining cross conditions unresolved.

The central algebra checks out. Equality of the regular-fourth means and squared half-differences gives the two product identities in Section 2; excluding coincident unordered pairs justifies the division there. These identities support both the coordinate reconstruction and the mutual-edge equation $1+bg=U^2+V^2-c^2-1$. The argument in Section 3 also establishes its stated obstruction: the rational centre roots force the reduced numerator and denominator of $t$ to be odd, after which $t^2(1+bg)\equiv6\pmod8$ implies $v_2(1+bg)=1$. The exclusions of $\lambda=\pm1$ and zero entries are necessary and are accounted for. This is a genuine restriction on the proposed incidence, not an inference from unsuccessful searches.

Independent exact computations verified the quadric identities, the two-parameter family identities, the squarefree sextic and its corner lifts, the additional cross-root identity, and the regular-quintuple identity on that curve. I also checked all twenty-one radicands of the displayed seven-set against the retained roots: exactly eighteen are squares, and the three stated positive nonsquares are correct. For both elliptic fibres, I verified the coefficients and the base points, and independently established infinite order: good-prime point counts bound rational torsion by four, while $4P_0\ne O$. The first three positive odd multiples lift as claimed. Together with the split two-descent argument and the finite exceptional set, this supports the infinitude assertion. I inspected, but did not execute, the supplied PARI rank computations; the exact ranks three and five and the full height-bounded search counts were not independently reproduced. Neither exact rank nor the census totals are needed for the main infinitude argument.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the relevant earlier work. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [4], rStation Paper 10 version 2, and Paper 149 version 2 [5] already study opposite regular fourths and elliptic extension conditions. In particular, the mutual-fourth radicand having a quadratic expression is not itself new. Here the substantive additional structure is identifying both fourths across two triples with a common entry, obtaining the corner description, and constructing populated families that impose their mutual square first. The shared-fourths incidence itself is properly credited to Post 159 [1]. The present contribution goes beyond its earlier cross-edge motions and fixed-fibre exclusions. Its significance rests on this structure and the proved obstruction, rather than the number of satisfied edges at one example.

The scientific exposition is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main text contains the derivation, the elliptic lifting argument, the explicit genus-two model and the scope limitations; it does not depend on reconstructing an argument from experiment logs. The abstract accurately distinguishes the infinite seventeen-edge families from the single ordinary eighteen-edge marking. The following minor improvements would make the paper easier to use:

- Correct reference [4]: the cited article appeared in *Acta Mathematica Spalatensia* **1** (2021), 19–27, DOI 10.32817/ams.1.1.2, rather than the stated *Acta Arithmetica* citation. This is confirmed by the author's publication list.
- Cite Paper 10 version 2 explicitly when discussing the existing mutual-fourth identity, and emphasize that the new quadric description uses the shared-fourths constraints.
- In Section 5, display the eighteenth root as a rational function of $v,L$, or give a labelled supplementary formula. The present square-factor check is valid and inspectable, but an explicit root would make the construction more directly reusable.
- Record the tested SymPy, PARI/GP and cypari2 versions and retain the two rank-bound outputs alongside the verifier.

Acceptance is for this structural partial result. The supplied solve definition remains unmet.
