# Accept

This is a focused maintenance revision of the accepted construction. It preserves the elliptic family and its twenty square identities, makes the remaining septuple question precise, and improves the explanation of the construction. The regular-completion interpretation is closely related explanatory material, appropriately attributed to [8], rather than an unrelated new contribution requiring a separate paper. The resulting manuscript remains coherent and reasonably compact.

The four requests in the previous report have been addressed: the bad specialization set $\mathcal B$ is separated from the finite set of possible genuine septuples; the unproved converse concerning duplicate sets is removed; verification coverage is stated accurately; and an admissible worked example is supplied. After inspecting the revised supplement, I ran its verifier with Python 3. All 50 checks passed, including the regularity identity census, the $x_9$ construction, the worked values, the rank-six Jacobian certificate, and the discriminant calculation. These provide useful supporting evidence for the existing contribution. The inverse-map check is at the displayed example, as the revised verification section correctly states.

Three qualifications would improve precision:

1. **Identity versus specialization.** Theorem 3 and the supplied census establish exactly which subsets are regular *identically*. This alone does not imply the subsequent statement that every almost septuple has exactly the same regular structure: a nonzero regularity expression can vanish at isolated points. An additional exact reviewer probe of the norms of the other regularity expressions, on the chart $b=1$, found rational zero candidates only at $a\in\{\pm1,\pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac25,\pm\tfrac15\}$; none yielded an admissible extra regular subset. Thus this check found no counterexample, but it is distinct from the supplied identity census. Either retain a short exceptional-specialization argument and certificate, or use “generically” for the exact count in the abstract, introduction, and discussion following Theorem 3. The three stated regular subsets persist at all admissible points regardless.

2. **Separate arithmetic difficulty from evidence of nonexistence.** The cited discussion [8] explicitly distinguishes its Jacobian-rank lower bounds from a rational-point classification. They obstruct direct ordinary rank-less-than-genus Chabauty on the stated curves; they do not support the expectation that the family contains no septuple. Section 8 should present this as a limitation of the available method, separately from the bounded search. Its opening phrase “cannot be closed by the method” is broader than the appropriately qualified “does not apply directly” that follows.

3. **Retain the search evidence if it motivates the expectation.** The new coefficient-box search is explicitly excluded from the verifier. A small reproducible script or retained exact summary, specifying inclusion of the torsion translate and handling of exceptional points, would make that observation independently assessable. It is not needed for the infinitude theorem and should remain clearly bounded evidence.

These qualifications do not undermine the accepted family, the verified identities, or the revised infinitude argument. Neither this revision nor its cited rank bounds supplies a rational Diophantine septuple or a complete nonexistence proof. The selected problem therefore remains unresolved by this paper.
