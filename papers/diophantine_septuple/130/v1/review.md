# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the selected problem. The explicit elliptic family supplies seven generically distinct, nonzero rational entries with twenty certified square conditions, together with a concrete remaining square condition. It does not produce a Diophantine septuple or prove their nonexistence, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition. Its value is the new infinite family and the resulting, precisely specified arithmetic question.

The central argument is convincing. I inspected and ran the supplied exact-arithmetic verifier: all 34 checks passed, including the twenty root identities, three linear relations, three regularity identities, the Weierstrass equation, finite-field point counts, and the identity and squarefreeness of $\Psi$. Additional exact checks confirmed that the seven entry functions are nonzero and pairwise different. The displayed maps recover $a/b$ rationally from $X,Y$, supporting the asserted birational identification. The primes $5,7,23$ are primes of good reduction; their point counts have gcd $2$, and the exhibited non-torsion point consequently proves infinitude without requiring a rank computation. Squarefreeness of the degree-sixteen polynomial gives the claimed genus-seven curve, so the application of Faltings' theorem is valid. Finally, a nonconstant entry function ensures that infinitely many parameter points yield infinitely many different sets.

The novelty is substantive relative to the relevant published work. Paper 125 [1] already established infinitude of almost septuples, so that general existence statement is not new. However, its construction has at least three identically regular quadruples. Exact substitution in the present formulas gives exactly one, $\{x_1,x_3,x_4,x_5\}$, distinguishing the generic families even after permutation or common sign change. Paper 128 [2] excludes septuples only in the first family; its conclusion does not transfer here. Paper 129 [3] concerns a different, genus-four entry component and supplies no ordinary almost septuple. The external literature check included the infinite-sextuple construction [5] and the regular-subtuple construction [6], alongside Dujella's research survey [7]. These establish important antecedents but do not supply this twenty-square elliptic family. A brief explicit distinction of this kind would strengthen the introduction and attribution.

The manuscript is sufficiently clear as a short construction paper: the parameter curve, entry functions, witnesses, infinitude argument and unresolved condition are all available in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the small standalone supplement makes the algebra reproducible. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central result:

1. **Separate invalid specializations from genuine septuples.** Section 7 asks for a square value of $\Psi$ “outside the finite set of Theorem 3.” Literally, that finite set must already contain every genuine septuple, since Theorem 3 calls everything outside it an *almost* septuple. Define the bad set using only zeros, poles and entry collisions, and ask for a square value outside that set. The additional finite set supplied by Faltings should be named separately.

2. **Limit the claim about duplicate sets.** The final paragraph of Section 6 asserts that the displayed involutions account for *every* equality of unordered sets up to common sign. The forward implication follows from the formulas; the converse is not proved or checked in the supplement. Remove “exactly” and retain the demonstrated symmetries, or supply an inverse argument with exceptional specializations handled explicitly. Finite fibres of a nonconstant entry already suffice for infinitude, so this stronger classification is unnecessary.

3. **Match the verification description to its coverage.** The script checks the forward Weierstrass equation, not every geometric assertion. In particular, it does not check the converse above or the claimed independence of the six constraints. I independently obtained Jacobian rank $6$ at $[a:b:c:d]=[8:64:1:447]$, using the displayed signed roots and differentiating $r_{ij}^2=1+x_ix_j$. Adding such a short certificate would document Section 4. An explicit inverse formula and the good-reduction observation would also make Section 5 more self-contained.

4. **Add a worked admissible specialization.** The point $[8:64:1:447]$ gives distinct nonzero entries, whereas the infinite-order point used in the proof lies at a pole of the entry formulas. Showing one ordinary example would help readers use the construction. No exhaustive exceptional-point calculation or new rank computation is needed for the stated theorem.
