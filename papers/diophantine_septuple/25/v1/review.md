# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied problem. The principal contribution is the finiteness theorem for nondegenerate rational points on the order-four locus of class 54, and consequently for its rational septuples with torsion $S$. It neither constructs a rational septuple nor proves general nonexistence. Its restriction to one census class and to torsion $S$ is essential, and is appropriately acknowledged.

The elimination and genus argument support this conclusion. The two halving equations cover the three possible nonzero values of $2S$, using the stated symmetry. Their leading coefficients in $c$ are $\pm ab$, so the projection has finite fibers on the relevant open locus. The addition formulas and the symmetric reduction of the hub give a reproducible route to the retained plane factors. The excluded denominators correspond to invalid orbit entries. For the five large factors, irreducibility over the residue field together with a smooth rational point establishes geometric irreducibility; the simple discriminant roots give the stated Riemann–Hurwitz bounds. Geometric genus cannot increase on the reduced irreducible special component, so these bounds suffice for Faltings over $\mathbb Q$. The remaining octic has no affine real zeros. Finally, Mazur's theorem and $S\in2E(\mathbb Q)$ reduce nondegenerate torsion cases to orders three and four, with order three repeating $d_0$. This is a finiteness proof, not an inference from unsuccessful searches.

The varying-base result over $\mathbb Q(i)$ also has a coherent mechanism. The hub identity identifies the relevant branch, the two rank-one condition curves supply infinitely many bases, and one verified nondegenerate point ensures that the exceptional parameters are finite. Comparing imaginary parts proves the asserted obstruction to rational points on this particular family; it is not an obstruction to all of $W$.

The novelty is sufficient beyond the published predecessors. Paper 18 v2 [2] supplies the census and elliptic seed but not this divisor decomposition or finiteness theorem. Paper 21 v2 [3] already supplies the base parametrization, genus-one square conditions, explicit $\mathbb Q(i)$ examples and an infinite family on a fixed base; it explicitly leaves the ranks of the condition curves uncomputed. Thus larger examples alone would add little, but the hub identity, varying-base infinitude and complete order-four finiteness argument add meaningful content. The snapshot's papers 4 and 24 treat, respectively, other order-four obstructions and specific lifting curves; their conclusions do not subsume this varying-base class-54 result. The classical sextuple constructions [10,11] likewise do not establish this theorem.

Independent exact rational arithmetic reproduced the four displayed bases' septuples, including both new examples, their 21 square conditions over $\mathbb Q(i)$, the order-four condition and the hub relation. Independent evaluation of the retained polynomials confirmed degree preservation and all ten listed smooth modular points. I inspected the symbolic elimination, rank and genus routines and their retained outputs. SageMath/PARI were unavailable here, so I did not independently reproduce the full resultants, irreducibility factorizations, discriminant factorizations or rank computations. The supplied explicit polynomials and computational methods make those central calculations inspectable and reproducible; the limited checks above should not be described as a complete computational replay.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. In Section 2, $x(3P_0)/(abc)$ is an elliptic seed extension, not generally a **regular quadruple** extension. The regular quadruple roots come from $P_0\pm S$, as explained in [2, Section 5]. The subsequent formulas and code use the intended seed, so this terminology error does not invalidate them.
2. Correct the abstract's assertion that nondegenerate rational points of $W$ are automatically septuples. The body explicitly retains the additional square conditions. Describe $W$ as the hub-relation surface and distinguish its points from points satisfying all 21 conditions.
3. In Lemma 6(iii), make the common-resolution argument explicit after finite base extension. A divisorial valuation need not already be a component on the chosen semistable model; exceptional rational components cause no difficulty for the genus inequality. State the conclusion geometrically after extending residue fields.
4. Qualify Section 6's assertion about the “only” low-genus curve by restricting to the nondegenerate order-four locus and accounting for conjugation. The elimination does not classify every boundary component of the closure.
5. Replace the private absolute path in `o4val.py` with the supplied local `c54o4.py`. Document the external census dependencies of `c7hub.py` and `findcls.py`, and give exact search commands. These are packaging defects with identifiable remedies, rather than missing mathematical evidence for the principal theorem.
