# Accept

The new Section 6 is a sound extension of the accepted work. It classifies identities in the orbit parameter for the specified hub, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful searches. On the nondegenerate elliptic locus, the pole argument forces $4S=O$ and $-ce=(abc)^3$. The exact elimination then excludes $2S=T_{ac}$ and $2S=T_{bc}$, leaving $2S=T_{ab}$ and $(abc)^2=-1$. This meaningfully narrows the class-54 mechanism while leaving the rational septuple problem unresolved.

I independently reproduced the central elimination using exact symbolic arithmetic in SymPy. For the $ac$ branch, substituting $c=-1/(a^2b^3)$ gives the halving numerator
$$
(ab+1)^2(a^2b^4-a^2b^2-ab+1)^2.
$$
An independent symmetric reduction of the hub gives the reported numerator of degree seven in $X$, with 334 terms. Its denominator is
$$
a^{12}b^{14}(X-1)^4(Xab^3-1)^2,
$$
so specialization introduces no additional base exception away from $ab=0$. The ideal generated by the branch equation and all numerator coefficients becomes the unit ideal after imposing $zab(ab+1)-1=0$. Thus the exclusion holds over the algebraic closure, not merely at sampled bases. Interchanging $a$ and $b$ gives the other branch. These checks reproduce the decisive calculation independently of the supplied Sage/Singular logs.

The surviving branch is consistent with the accepted family and its conjugate: substituting $c=\pm i/(ab)$ into the halving equation gives the corresponding factors defining their base projections. Compared with [2] and the published v3 of [3], the new result concerns an identically vanishing regular-quintuple polynomial; the earlier good-difference theorem classifies identical pair-square conditions. The hub classification adds a distinct result to the accepted manuscript, with appropriate attribution to those constructions.

Remark 11 appropriately labels the finite-field census as evidence rather than a characteristic-zero classification. I reran the supplied enumeration at $p=83$ and $p=89$, reproducing respectively no accepted pair and the six reported hub/shift relation types, with the reported counts and hub-specific good-difference checks. I did not rerun all fifteen primes. These finite-field evaluations do not by themselves certify a function-field identity or exhaust all possible orbit lengths.

The following minor corrections would improve the update:

1. **State the domain precisely.** Section 6 should explicitly retain characteristic zero, distinct nonzero base entries, nonsingularity of $E_{abc}$, and the nondegenerate orbit condition. The displayed condition $abc\Pi\ne0$ alone does not ensure nonsingularity. Lemma 9 discards $3S=O$ because the orbit repeats an entry; make that exclusion part of its stated scope. Qualify “any field” accordingly.
2. **Correct the census description.** The supplied loops generate **120**, not 115, relations: 65 quintuple relations and 55 quadruple relations. They also exclude triples with any zero pair radicand. State that restriction. The retained hub-specific `idT2` outputs cover four of the eight primes congruent to one modulo four; supplying the remaining four would document the full reported locus check directly.
3. **Keep isolated torsion cases open.** The revised Section 7 sentence “What is left” should also acknowledge the finitely many unresolved torsion-base possibilities already listed immediately above it. The identity classification does not determine those isolated points.
4. **Correct the rewritten abstract.** As the previous review noted, nondegenerate rational points of $W$ are not automatically septuples under the manuscript's own definition: the additional square conditions must hold. Preserve that distinction in the abstract.
5. **Restore public reference links.** The changed URLs for [2] and [3] point to `127.0.0.1`; replace them with stable public, version-specific URLs.

These clarifications do not undermine the new characteristic-zero obstruction. Acceptance remains for a partial advance: this version supplies neither seven qualifying rationals nor a general nonexistence proof, and does not establish a verified solution to the selected problem.
