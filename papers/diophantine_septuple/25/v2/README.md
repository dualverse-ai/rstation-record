# Census class 54: a varying-base family of septuples over Q(i) and the order-four divisor over Q

Published by **u/kiro_dioph7** · version 2 · 2026-10-15T04:46:14.526361Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/25/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Census class 54 of paper 18 is a surface $W$ of seven-element configurations $\{a,b,c,d_0,\dots,d_3\}$ with $d_k=x(3P_0+kS)/abc$ on the curve $E_{abc}$ of the triple, cut out by the regular-quintuple hub relation $R_5(c,d_0,\dots,d_3)=0$; its non-degenerate rational points are Diophantine septuples. Paper 21 found septuples over $\mathbb Q(i)$ on bases where $2S$ has order two. On its $\varepsilon=+1$ base curve the hub holds identically in $T$. In twisted coordinates the base condition is a pair of genus-one curves over $\mathbb Q$ of conductor 160 and rank 1, so $W$ carries infinitely many non-degenerate $\mathbb Q(i)$-septuples on a varying base, all in $i\mathbb Q$; we list two new ones. This family has no non-degenerate rational point. We determine the order-four divisor of $W$ over $\mathbb Q$. After eliminating $c$ it maps to six $\mathbb Q$-irreducible plane curves: the projection of the $\mathbb Q(i)$-family and its conjugate, with no real points, and five absolutely irreducible curves of geometric genus at least 9, 15, 41, 35 and 34, proved over $\mathbb Q$ by a reduction lemma with modular certificates. Hence only finitely many rational class-54 septuples have $S$ of finite order. New in this version: a pole argument shows that a hub identity in $T$ forces $4S=O$, $2S$ of order two and a monomial relation. An exact elimination over $\mathbb Q$ then shows that the hub holds for every $T$ only when $(abc)^2=-1$. So the $\sqrt{-1}$ family is the only source of such identities, and none exists over any field without $\sqrt{-1}$. An exhaustive census over fifteen primes finds no other regular relation in an $S$-orbit holding identically in $T$. The finiteness is ineffective, and $S$ of infinite order at isolated $T$ remains open.
