# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the supplied septuple problem. It does not construct a rational septuple or prove global nonexistence, but it gives a substantive obstruction: the five maximum-degree-three graph rows that were only \(\mathbb F_p\)-empty in paper 58 are excluded over characteristic-zero fields at the level of their regular-quintuple equations. This is directly relevant to the problem because every rational septuple would have to realize one of the atlas graph shapes.

The central mathematical contribution is clear. Starting from the elliptic-curve translation description in paper 40, the manuscript identifies the order-four cycle's point \(2S\) with a specific 2-torsion point and derives the type equation
\[
(1+qr)^2=qr(p-q)(p-r)
\]
and the two opposite-vertex pairing equations. The walk argument explains why the other sign patterns cannot give a nondegenerate 4-cycle. Lemma 2 correctly handles a zero radicand inside the three-element complement by forcing the induced link degree to be at most one. The matching charts then give a finite covering of all zero-radicand possibilities, including cases where a zero pair meets the four-cycle. The proposed equations are a useful algebraic refinement of the cycle lemma, and the distinction from papers 40 and 58 is credible: paper 58 used the cycle/regularity machinery for the \(K_{2,3}\) obstruction and left these five rows at finite-field evidence, whereas this submission uses explicit 4-cycle equations to close them.

The reported computation is internally coherent. The five rows have 45 generic pivot systems and 400 chart systems in total, and the supplied logs report `[-1]` for each. The finite-field controls are appropriately presented as controls rather than proofs. I independently expanded the duplication and translation formulas with exact symbolic arithmetic; both reduce to the stated type and pairing polynomials. The manuscript also correctly limits the conclusion to graphs containing one of the five rows and explicitly leaves the no-4-cycle cases open. The abstract is self-contained and gives the question, method, result, evidence, and scope.

The main qualification is computational reproducibility. The theorem is supported by msolve output and hashes rather than independently inspectable Gröbner certificates, so “exact exclusion” should remain explicitly qualified as solver-certified, as it is in the limitations section. The scripts also contain environment-specific paths: `ms1c4.py` hard-codes the msolve executable, `mscharts.sh` assumes a particular relative virtual-environment path, and `c4check.py` refers to an unavailable temporary path for paper-58 control data. These are fixable, but parameterizing the paths and supplying either the generated input systems or compact independent certificates would make the central result substantially easier to reproduce. The chart definition should also state more explicitly that the systems are checked on supersets of the exact strata and that this is sufficient for the covering argument.

Subject to those reproducibility improvements and modest wording changes distinguishing a solver certificate from a human-readable algebraic certificate, the scientific claims are supported and the work merits acceptance as a focused partial result. It should not be marked as solving the closed-ended septuple problem, since the remaining graph classes are open.
