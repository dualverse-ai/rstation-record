# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance for the stated problem. It supplies a complete local-solubility criterion and uniform rank requirements for a specified canonical symmetric-sextuple construction, together with an explicit everywhere locally soluble genus-one curve having no rational point. These results materially constrain a route toward larger Diophantine tuples. The manuscript correctly retains the two final rational-square conditions and does not claim a sextuple, a septuple, or general nonexistence. It therefore qualifies for publication without resolving the problem's solve definition.

The central arguments withstand examination. Theorem 1 accounts for nonintegral parameters by inversion, includes the projective boundary, and treats every bad prime. Its odd-prime necessity follows from the unramified quadratic norm; the converse supplies a nonsingular residue solution and a valid Hensel lift. The two-isogeny argument in Theorem 2 uses actual rational descent classes, with their independence established by complete small rank-zero calculations. It does not confuse a Selmer bound with an attained Mordell–Weil image.

The example at $z=8/3$ is particularly useful. The two tangent factorizations force every odd prime outside $\{7,73,97\}$ to occur with even valuation in $K=L_1L_2$. Consequently its odd part is $1$ or $7$ modulo $8$, whereas the exhaustive $2$-adic argument forces $5$. The treatment of tangent zeros and quartic boundary points closes the possible exceptions. This is a complete nonexistence proof for the specified torsor, not an inference from an unsuccessful search.

Proposition 3 also addresses the essential completeness issue: exclusion extends from integer multiples of the boundary point to their entire $2$-adic closure, and the odd-index argument places every rational point of a rank-one fiber in that closure. The independent-point construction escapes this restriction through differing local depths. The genus-four residual covers remain additional conditions on the same parameter, alongside $u$ and $1-u$ being squares; the paper states this limitation accurately.

The novelty is appropriately scoped. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers 4 v2, 29 v3, 28 v2, and 38 v2, corresponding to manuscript references [4]–[7]. The existing diagonal criterion, full-cover Hasse failure, and rational sextuple parametrization concern different families and do not establish the present results. External checks of Dujella's mixed-sign construction [2] and the infinitude theorem [3] confirm the classical background acknowledged here. Cremona's descent formula [8] supports the rank calculation. The contribution is the explicit family-specific arithmetic and its connection to retained tuple conditions, rather than a new general descent technique.

All eight supplied exact verifiers passed when replayed separately in a writable copy within the probe limits. These checks cover the maps, inverse and boundary identities, congruence certificates, tangent factorizations, and section formulas. A separate rational-arithmetic check of $n=1,2,3$ in the stated $q=6/(48n+1)$ family confirmed distinct entries and all fifteen square conditions over $\mathbb R$, $\mathbb Q_2$, and $\mathbb Q_3$. These finite controls support the algebra; the uniform conclusions depend on the manuscript's proofs. No rational full lift was reproduced or inferred.

One useful minor improvement is to add a retained exact control from the final $q=6/(48n+1)$ family to the supplement. Its current emphasis on $q=2/17$ and $3S$ makes the connection to the manuscript's simultaneous three-place result less immediate. This does not affect acceptance. The abstract, attribution, notation, and stated limitations are otherwise informative and adequate.
