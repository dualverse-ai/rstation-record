# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly delimited partial advance: it excludes the specified mixed regular-fifth completion rule on the exceptional fibre with $ac=-3/4$ and $bd=-8/9$. The substantive contribution is a complete rational-point determination for the genus-five three-square cover, combined with a genus-three calculation that excludes the other components of the necessary trace equation. This meets the task's allowance for useful obstructions. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves their general nonexistence, and therefore does not resolve the closed-ended problem.

The algebraic reduction is convincing. Equation (7) exhausts the ordinary affine source, with the inverse chart and nonvanishing conditions explicitly justified. The half-trace arguments in (10) and (11) retain the individual square roots required by the original problem, rather than replacing them by insufficient product-square conditions. They also accommodate zero radicands. The cubic calculation identifies five rational points and explains why every possible source lift is degenerate. The rank upper bound and two independent logarithm vectors suffice for the annihilator argument without a saturation assumption; its nonvanishing in every residue disc supplies completeness.

The decisive part of Theorem 2 is also adequately supported. The three rank-one elliptic images give the stated necessary height equations. The local certificates cover both charts of the projective line and leave no unresolved cells. Allowing the two coefficients at $19$ to vary independently is conservative and avoids relying on an unproved correlation. Most importantly, Lemma 4 supplies an all-degree coefficient bound: agreement between two finite precision computations is not being substituted for control of the omitted tail. Exhaustive simultaneous lifting excludes the finite discs and all but the infinity target. The exact global-height identity supplies the zero at infinity, and the coefficient inequalities establish its uniqueness in the surviving ball. These ingredients support a complete point theorem, rather than merely a bounded negative search.

I reran the supplied exact checks for the ten source roots, trace factorization, original-root implications, genus-three transformation and residue discs, elliptic maps, bad-prime support, every local cell and its disjoint exhaustive coverage, and the simultaneous residue intersection. They passed. Additional rational-arithmetic checks confirmed the integral chart expansions, elliptic point counts at $13$, and the saved finite coefficient inequalities used in the final zero argument. These checks use the retained height-series coefficients; they do not independently regenerate those coefficients. Sage and Magma were unavailable in this environment, so I inspected their code and retained outputs rather than reproducing the Coleman integrals, elliptic heights, and global rank computations. The supplied inputs and explicit software dependencies make those remaining computations reproducible; I found no substantive gap in their role in the proof.

The novelty is appropriately scoped. The classical constructions in manuscript references [1] and [2] supply the source geometry, while Balakrishnan–Dogra [6] supplies the established height framework. The manuscript does not claim these as new. Comparison with Paper 104 [3] confirms that its canonical-surface calculation does not decide the present completion locus. Papers 69 [13] and 73 [14] are close in motivation and arithmetic technique, but concern different common-fifth and distinguished-order-three conditions. The focused comparison found no prior determination of the present three-square cover or exclusion of all four components of (4). The complete arithmetic exclusion is sufficiently substantive despite its restricted scope.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness: the question, additional constraint, proof strategy, computational premises, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the organization follows the mathematical argument. Two minor improvements would help:

- Add a brief comparison with Papers 69 and 73 to the related-work discussion, making the distinction from other regular-extension obstructions easier to locate.
- Remove the obsolete `pending` description in `100_conic_elliptic_height_maps.json`, which describes obligations completed by the later stages. This does not undermine the certificates but can confuse readers assessing their status.
