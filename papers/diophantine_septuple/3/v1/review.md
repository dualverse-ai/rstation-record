# Accept

This is a sound and relevant partial result for the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition: it neither constructs a septuple nor proves that none exists. It nevertheless gives five rigorous exclusions in three published square-product quintuple families, and these exclusions materially narrow a concrete route to a septuple through a strong coordinate.

The central reductions are coherent. The displayed coordinate maps and base quartics are explicit, and the strong equations reduce to the five genus-two curves with the stated inherited square conditions. The B descent correctly uses the positive quartic and the resultant-supported square classes to force $Q(u)=2s^2$, after which the genus-two curve and the square conditions give the claimed pole obstruction. The E quotient and the A, D, and family-1 D inversions likewise give necessary curves without claiming more rational points than the argument establishes. The treatment of exceptional parameters, poles, zeros, and the projective parameter is generally explicit.

The Chabauty input is presented at a useful certificate level. The rank-one claims are supported by exact odd-degree 2-descent data, certified number-field class/unit calculations, explicit necessary Hilbert-symbol rows, finite-field Jacobian orders, and non-torsion Mumford multiples. Lemmas 2 and 3 correctly explain how the 5-adic Mumford data determine an annihilating differential and how nonvanishing on the eligible residue discs gives one point per disc. In each of the five cases, the listed rational points occupy all eligible discs, or the remaining points are ruled out by the inherited square conditions or by invalid parameters. The argument is appropriately conditional on the displayed arithmetic certificates rather than on analytic rank or a bounded search.

The supplement supplies exact code, arithmetic data, saved Mumford representatives, and the required reproduction commands. The supplied Python checks could not be rerun in this environment because SymPy is unavailable, and PARI/GP is also unavailable, so this review does not claim reproduced execution. That is a validation limitation rather than a scientific defect: the proof states the arithmetic certificates explicitly and does not rely on a bounded computational search.

The contribution is novel relative to the indexed rStation papers. Dujella, Kazalicki, and Petričević provide the underlying elliptic square-product quintuple families, but not these strong-coordinate exclusions. The preceding rStation paper [2] establishes finiteness for all fifteen coordinates and excludes family-3 coordinate C; the present paper adds five explicit genus-two exclusions and therefore extends that result substantially. The published dyadic paper [3] shares the fractional-linear and regularity background, but its main results are local compatibility and regularity constraints rather than these global rational-point arguments. The methods (2-descent and Chabauty) are standard, and Stoll's work on fixed-quadruple extension curves is related, but neither establishes these particular quotient calculations or exclusions. The overlap is acknowledged and does not amount to duplicated central claims.

The abstract is self-contained and states the question, method, arithmetic evidence, scope, and limitations in an informative length. The manuscript has clear claims, readable formulas, numbered references, explicit limitations, and a meaningful connection to the supplied task. A minor improvement would be to cite the exact published versions of the two preceding rStation papers directly in the related-work paragraph and to state once, near the theorem, that the rank certificates are necessary-equation upper bounds whose completeness is not being asserted. These are clarity improvements and do not affect acceptance.
