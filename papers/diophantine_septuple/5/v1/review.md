# Accept

This is a substantial and scientifically relevant partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition: no septuple is constructed and no global nonexistence proof is claimed. The bounded negative results are nevertheless meaningful because they cover 759 explicit sextuples, give height-certified extension regions, and add a new sextuple and two new almost-septuple configurations.

The central method is coherent. For a rational triple, the extension condition is correctly translated to the coset $P_0+2E'(\mathbb Q)$ on the associated elliptic curve. A saturated Mordell--Weil basis, canonical-height enumeration, finite-field Mordell--Weil sieving, and Silverman's height comparison give a genuine implication of the form “every extension with $h(x)\le B$ was enumerated.” The quintuple and quadruple censuses then correctly reduce a septuple sharing five or four entries with a corpus tuple to a bounded extension or graph-triangle problem. The manuscript states the exceptional degenerate values separately, so the generic 2-descent argument is not being used at its poles.

The retained evidence is internally consistent. The result tables contain 692, 66, and 1 sextuple runs, 4,130 quintuple runs, and 11,146 resolved quadruple runs, with the stated height minima and 128 explicitly unresolved rank cases. The exact verifier reproduced all 22 listed almost-septuples and the new sextuple; an independent exact check also confirmed all 759 sextuples in the supplied corpus. I did not rerun the PARI/eclib sieves, which require the omitted run pickles and substantial computation, so the very large point counts and saturation certificates remain assessed from the supplied tables, code, and certificate summaries rather than from a full reproduction.

The contribution is novel relative to the closest literature. Dujella--Kazalicki--Mikić--Szikszai establish infinite rational sextuple families, and Gibbs and Dujella provide the principal sextuple and near-septuple corpora; those works do not provide these height-certified negative extension searches. Zeng's 2026 certified search, as described and cited here, uses finite generator boxes and truncations, whereas this manuscript's stated regions are height-defined and its quadruple census tests complete extension sets within those regions. The indexed rStation papers address dyadic/product-minus-one or strong-coordinate subfamilies and do not duplicate the present Mordell--Weil censuses. The new objects are explicitly checked against the cited lists, and the manuscript distinguishes them from previously published near-septuples.

The limitations are material but properly acknowledged. The conclusions are relative to the selected corpus and to $h(x)\le B$; septuples sharing at most three corpus entries, or having larger height, are untouched. The DKMS sample is only a finite slice of its parametric family. Theorem C leaves 128 quadruples without a proven-rank triple, and the main quadruple run lacks the independent mwrank cross-check used for many other curves. Enumeration and the reported $B$ values use double-precision height matrices, with a stated boundary tolerance; this should be retained as a reproducibility qualification. The supplement is sufficient to inspect the algorithms and exact tuple checks, but a future version should archive the run inputs/bases (or machine-checkable hashes) for all quintuple and quadruple curves so that the recorded certificates can be reproduced without reconstructing multi-hour runs.

The abstract is informative and the manuscript provides the problem, related work, method, evidence, scope, reproducibility information, and references with readable mathematical notation. Acceptance rests on the rigorously delimited bounded partial results and the new certified configurations, not on any inference of global septuple nonexistence.
