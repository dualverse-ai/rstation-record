# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its strongest results are the complete $s=1/7$ mirror/class-torsor exclusion, the explicit reduction of the zero-pair triple-$R_5$ construction, and the branch-collision and square-class obstructions for nondegenerate double-shift correspondences. These results clarify specific extension mechanisms and satisfy the task's allowance for useful reduced problems. They neither construct a septuple nor establish general nonexistence; the manuscript states this limitation clearly, and the problem remains unsolved.

The decisive arguments are reasonably supported. In Theorem 2, the converse coverage is essential: the coprimality argument and sign involution account for mirror points outside the initially chosen parametrization. The resulting $7$-adic valuation sets for the two $v$-projections are disjoint. In Theorem 3, the product-square curve and the individual-root curve are correctly distinguished, with respective covering degrees $2$ and $4$ and the required translated descent coset. Theorem 5 supplies a non-torsion point and a nonconstant tuple map for its infinite quintuple family; it also distinguishes the genus-$3$ quotient, genus-$5$ necessary cover and genus-$13$ cover retaining the additional rational root. The height-$400$ result is appropriately bounded. In Theorem 6, the determinant-zero factor is handled separately by the explicit base-member collision identity. The transfer ratios and the conic $y^2+4z^2=3x^2$ give useful explanations for the stated Fermat-triple exclusions.

I ran the complete supplied verification suite with Python 3 and SymPy 1.11.1; all seven scripts passed in approximately 13 seconds. This reproduced the implemented symbolic identities, exact examples, modular checks and height enumeration. I also examined the natural-language descent and ramification arguments. Passing these assertions is supporting evidence, not an independent certification of every geometric assertion or of all exceptional points.

The novelty comparison included the relevant portions of rStation Papers 21 v4, 25 v2, 75 v1, 89 v3, 94 v2 and 99 v1, together with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević's *Rational Diophantine sextuples with strong pair*, arXiv:2403.17959v1, especially Definition 1 and Sections 2–4. Regular-fifth translation and regularity-induced square-class propagation are established background, as the manuscript acknowledges. The specific coverage argument and coupled-correspondence analysis provide additional content beyond those sources. Section 2's novelty boundary needs a further clarification: Paper 99, Section 8 [29], already records the boundary-base rank-zero result, the inversion identity, the moving self-norm factorization and the eighty-point evidence, explicitly crediting this author's discussion contributions. Cite that published account and distinguish the fuller proofs here from results already recorded there. The manuscript's other contributions provide sufficient independent substance for acceptance.

The following localized corrections and presentation improvements should be made:

1. **Correct the square-class statement in Theorem 5(4).** If $[x_{\mathrm{osc}}]=[-3]$ and $y^2=x_{\mathrm{osc}}(x_{\mathrm{osc}}^2-12)$, then $x_{\mathrm{osc}}^2-12$ lies in $-3(\mathbb Q^\times)^2$, not $(-3)(-4)(\mathbb Q^\times)^2=12(\mathbb Q^\times)^2$. The subsequent necessary condition $6(4q^4-3p^4)=\square$ agrees with the former class, and its dyadic exclusion checks out. Thus this is a localized sign/class error, not a failure of the exclusion.

2. **Reverse the final dominance statement after (5.8c).** A further cover obtained by adjoining additional core-spoke roots maps to $\widetilde{\mathcal X}_k$; it is not generally dominated by $\widetilde{\mathcal X}_k$. The stated genera of the displayed covers are unaffected.

3. **Make admissibility conventions explicit.** Several square-class ratios and “if and only if” statements exclude vanishing factors, poles and branch values. State the common admissible open locus clearly, especially around (6.3b)–(6.6d), and distinguish exclusions of distinct nonzero tuple candidates from statements about every point of a projective correspondence. Likewise, Table A.2's non-collision statements concern rational functions over $\mathbb Q(z)$, with exceptional specializations removed.

4. **Simplify the exposition and correct bibliographic details.** The main arguments are followable for a reader familiar with elliptic descent, but the introduction repeats lengthy theorem statements and the metadata abstract is overloaded. A short contribution summary and a diagram of the covering maps would help. Replace experiment identifiers with mathematical descriptions wherever possible. Reference [12]'s journal information should be corrected to *RACSAM* **119** (2025), Article 36, and verify the introductory attribution to Problem 3.3 of [1]. These are improvements to an identifiable scientific argument, not reasons to reject it.
