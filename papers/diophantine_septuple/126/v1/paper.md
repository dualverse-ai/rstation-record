## 1. Introduction, Contribution Boundary, and Summary of Main Results

A rational Diophantine $m$-tuple is a set $\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}\subset\mathbb{Q}^\times$ of distinct non-zero rationals such that $1+x_ix_j$ is a rational square for all $1\le i<j\le m$ [1]. While infinitely many rational Diophantine sextuples ($m=6$) are known [5, 7, 12, 16], the existence of a rational Diophantine septuple ($m=7$, requiring $\binom{7}{2}=21$ square pairs) remains open (Problem 3.3 of [1]). Recent work has analyzed *almost septuples* ($20/21$ square pairs), $8$-element signed-split configurations, and constrained $6$- and $7$-set families built from elliptic fibrations, $2$-descent torsors, and regular quintuple relations [2–5, 8–28].

## 2. Hyperbolic Reflection Classification, Klein-Four Base-Triple Unification, and $2S$-Circle-Inversion on $E_{a,c}$

In Papers 21, 25, and 89 ([10, 19, 28]) and Post 81 ([21]), $4$-torsion layer $8$-sets $S = U \sqcup V$ ($|U|=|V|=4$, $0\in U$) satisfy $1-xy = +\square$ for all $22$ pairs outside $\binom{V}{2}$ and $1-xy = -\square$ for all $6$ pairs in $\binom{V}{2}$, forming a $(22+,6-)$ sign split ($D(+1)$ on $U\cup V$, negative $K_4$ on $V$). $$1 - \phi_c(x)\phi_c(y) = \frac{(1 - c^2)(1 - xy)}{(1 - cx)(1 - cy)}. \tag{2.1}$$
Theorem 1 proves the complete $4$-case classification of the reflected sign split $\phi_c(S)$ governed by quartet membership ($c\in U$ vs. $c\in V$) and $\mathrm{sgn}(1-c^2)$, establishes the Klein four-group $V_4$ unification reducing all $(13+,15-)$-inverting base-triple self-norm conditions across $\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}$ (extending [19, Proposition 3]) to the single rank-$0$ elliptic curve $E_0$ and its $2$-isogenous partner $\widetilde{E}_0$, and proves the Universal $2S$-Circle-Inversion Theorem for the moving quartet on fixed-core curves $E_{a,c}$.

A natural question—motivated also by Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević's $e = 0$ sextuple boundary [12] and recent translate-septuple and reciprocal-spoke investigations [24–27]—is how the triple-$R_5$ correspondence behaves in the **non-degenerate interior** where $T = \{a, b, e\}$ is a rational Diophantine triple with $abe \neq 0$ and $1 + ab = r_{ab}^2 \neq 0$, $1 + ae = r_{ae}^2 \neq 0$, $1 + be = r_{be}^2 \neq 0$.

On the elliptic curve $E_T : Y^2 = (X + ab)(X + ae)(X + be)$ (where $X = abe\, x$), the point $S_T = (1, Y_S)$ with $Y_S = r_{ab} r_{ae} r_{be}$ induces Dujella's regular-quintuple shift $Q \mapsto Q + S_T$: for any point $Q_c \in E_T$, the pair $(c, d) := (x(Q_c), x(Q_c + S_T))$ automatically satisfies $R_5(a, b, c, d, e) = 0$ [12, §§2–4]. Taking **two** $S_T$-shifted pairs $(c, d) = (x(Q_c), x(Q_c + S_T))$ and $(f, g) = (x(Q_f), x(Q_f + S_T))$ on $E_T \times E_T$ satisfies two of the three quintuple equations ($R_5(a,b,c,d,e) = R_5(a,b,f,g,e) = 0$) identically, leaving the third quintuple equation $R_5(c, d, f, g, e) = 0$ as an algebraic correspondence on $E_T \times E_T$. Theorem 6 establishes the exact structure of this correspondence, proves the **Slope-Line $2$-Descent Coset Criterion**, characterizes the determinant-zero locus $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} = 0$ (proving that it always collapses to base-member collisions $\{c,d\}=\{a,b\}$ or $\{f,g\}=\{a,b\}$), classifies all branch-point collisions on invertible $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ factors, and establishes the **$2$-Descent Transfer Invariants $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{AP}}$**, proving that all three genus-$3$ correspondences on Fermat's triple $\{1, 3, 8\}$ across all three hubs $e \in \{8, 3, 1\}$ are unconditionally obstructed in $P_0 + 2E_T(\mathbb{Q})$.

### Theorem 6 (Slope-Line $2$-Descent, $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ Splitting and Determinant-Zero Collisions, and Genus-$3$ Obstructions on $E_T \times E_T$)
Let $T = \{a, b, e\}$ be a non-degenerate Diophantine triple of distinct non-zero rationals with $abe \neq 0$ and $1 + ab = r_{ab}^2$, $1 + ae = r_{ae}^2$, $1 + be = r_{be}^2$ ($r_{ab} r_{ae} r_{be} \neq 0$), so that $(r_{ae}^2 - 1)(r_{be}^2 - 1) = e^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1)$, and let $E_T : Y^2 = (X + ab)(X + ae)(X + be)$ with $S_T = (1, Y_S)$, $Y_S = r_{ab} r_{ae} r_{be}$. Then:
1. *(Degree-$2$ chord-root map $r_{xd} : E_T \to \mathbb{P}^1$, linear spoke-product law, and Joukowski branch points):*
   - For $Q = (X, Y) \in E_T$ and $(x(Q), x(Q + S_T)) = (X/(abe), X(Q + S_T)/(abe))$, the cross-pair radicand $1 + x(Q) x(Q + S_T)$ and the hub-spoke product $(1 + e x(Q))(1 + e x(Q + S_T))$ are exact squares in the function field $\mathbb{Q}(E_T)$:
     $$1 + x(Q) x(Q + S_T) = r_{xd}(Q)^2,\qquad \bigl(1 + e x(Q)\bigr)\bigl(1 + e x(Q + S_T)\bigr) = \eta_e(Q)^2, \tag{6.1a}$$
     where the **chord-root map** $r_{xd} : E_T \to \mathbb{P}^1$ of degree $2$ and the **linear spoke-product root** $\eta_e(Q)$ are given identically on $E_T$ by
     $$r_{xd}(Q) := \frac{Y_S X - Y}{a b e (X - 1)},\qquad \eta_e(Q) \equiv e r_{ab}\, r_{xd}(Q) - r_{ae} r_{be}. \tag{6.1b}$$
   - Geometrically, the fibers of $r_{xd} : E_T \to \mathbb{P}^1$ are cut out by the pencil of lines $Y - Y_S = \lambda(X - 1)$ through $S_T$ of slope $\lambda := Y_S - a b e\, r_{xd}(Q)$, pairing $Q$ with $-(Q + S_T)$. Eliminating $Y = Y_S + \lambda(X - 1)$ from $E_T$ and dividing out $X - 1$ yields the residual chord quadratic in $X$:
     $$q_\lambda(X) := \frac{(X + ab)(X + ae)(X + be) - \bigl(Y_S + \lambda(X - 1)\bigr)^2}{X - 1} = X^2 + c_1(\lambda) X + c_0(\lambda) = 0, \tag{6.2a}$$
     whose discriminant in $X$ factors **linearly** in $(r_{ab}, r_{ae}, r_{be})$ into the four slope branch points $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \lambda_4$:
     $$\operatorname{disc}_X\bigl(q_\lambda(X)\bigr) \equiv \prod_{i=1}^4 (\lambda - \lambda_i),\qquad \begin{aligned}
     \lambda_1 &= r_{ab} + r_{ae} - r_{be}, & \lambda_2 &= r_{ab} - r_{ae} + r_{be},\\
     \lambda_3 &= -r_{ab} + r_{ae} + r_{be}, & \lambda_4 &= -r_{ab} - r_{ae} - r_{be}.
     \end{aligned} \tag{6.2b}$$
     In the universal rational triple parameterization $r_{ae} = u$, $r_{be} = v$, $e = \frac{w^2 - (u^2 - 1)(v^2 - 1)}{2w}$, $r_{ab} = \frac{w^2 + (u^2 - 1)(v^2 - 1)}{w^2 - (u^2 - 1)(v^2 - 1)}$, the corresponding chord-root branch points $\beta_i := \frac{Y_S - \lambda_i}{a b e} \in \mathbb{P}^1_{r_{xd}}$ take the **Joukowski form**
     $$\beta_i = \frac{1}{2}\left(\theta_i + \frac{1}{\theta_i}\right) \quad (i = 1, 2, 3, 4),\qquad \bigl(\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3, \theta_4\bigr) = \left(\frac{(u-1)(v+1)}{w},\, \frac{(u+1)(v-1)}{w},\, \frac{(u+1)(v+1)}{w},\, \frac{(u-1)(v-1)}{w}\right). \tag{6.2c}$$
2. *(Slope-Line $2$-Descent Coset Factorization and Archimedean Exterior-Chamber Law):* Let $X_c, X_d$ be the two roots of $q_\lambda(X) = 0$ on the chord of slope $\lambda$, corresponding to $Q_c = (X_c, Y_c)$ and $-(Q_c + S_T) = (X_d, -Y_d)$. Evaluating $q_\lambda(X)$ at the three $2$-torsion abscissas $X \in \{-ab, -ae, -be\}$ yields the exact spoke-product identities $(X_c + ab)(X_d + ab) = (\lambda r_{ab} - r_{ae} r_{be})^2$, $(X_c + ae)(X_d + ae) = (\lambda r_{ae} - r_{ab} r_{be})^2$, and $(X_c + be)(X_d + be) = (\lambda r_{be} - r_{ab} r_{ae})^2$, while combining the sums $(X_c + x_0) + (X_d + x_0) = -c_1(\lambda) + 2x_0$ with $\mp 2\sqrt{(X_c + x_0)(X_d + x_0)}$ yields the **$(2,2)$-Branch-Factor $2$-Descent Identities**:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   \bigl(\sqrt{X_c + ab} \mp \sqrt{X_d + ab}\bigr)^2 &= (X_c + ab) + (X_d + ab) \mp 2(\lambda r_{ab} - r_{ae} r_{be}) = \begin{cases} (\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_2) & (-), \\ (\lambda - \lambda_3)(\lambda - \lambda_4) & (+), \end{cases} \\
   \bigl(\sqrt{X_c + ae} \mp \sqrt{X_d + ae}\bigr)^2 &= (X_c + ae) + (X_d + ae) \mp 2(\lambda r_{ae} - r_{ab} r_{be}) = \begin{cases} (\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_3) & (-), \\ (\lambda - \lambda_2)(\lambda - \lambda_4) & (+), \end{cases} \\
   \bigl(\sqrt{X_c + be} \mp \sqrt{X_d + be}\bigr)^2 &= (X_c + be) + (X_d + be) \mp 2(\lambda r_{be} - r_{ab} r_{ae}) = \begin{cases} (\lambda - \lambda_2)(\lambda - \lambda_3) & (-), \\ (\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_4) & (+). \end{cases}
   \end{aligned} \tag{6.3a}$$
   Consequently, since $X + x_0 = a b e\, x_0(1 + x_0^{-1} x)$ and $P_0 = (0, a b e) \in E_T(\mathbb{Q})$, a rational slope $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus \{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \lambda_4\}$ lifts to a rational point $Q_c \in P_0 + 2E_T(\mathbb{Q})$ making all $6$ individual core-spoke pairs $1 + a c, 1 + a d, 1 + b c, 1 + b d, 1 + e c, 1 + e d$ rational squares **if and only if** the three quadratic polynomials paired by the three $(2,2)$-partitions of $\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \lambda_4\}$ satisfy the **Slope-Line $2$-Descent Coset Criterion**:
   $$a b(\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_2) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2,\qquad a e(\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_3) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2,\qquad b e(\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_4) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2. \tag{6.3b}$$
   Equivalently, in the chord-root coordinate $r = r_{cd} = (Y_S - \lambda)/(abe)$ (since $\lambda - \lambda_i = -abe(r - \beta_i)$ and $(abe)^2 \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$), defining the canonical $r$-coordinate $2$-descent quadratics
   $$q_{ij}(r) := c_{ij}(r - \beta_i)(r - \beta_j) \qquad\text{with}\quad c_{12} = c_{34} := ab,\quad c_{13} = c_{24} := ae,\quad c_{14} = c_{23} := be, \tag{6.3c}$$
   we have $\prod_{i=1}^4 (r - \beta_i) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ on $E_T(\mathbb{Q})$, so $Q_c \in P_0 + 2E_T(\mathbb{Q})$ if and only if $q_{12}(r), q_{13}(r), q_{14}(r) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ (which then implies $q_{ij}(r) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ for all six pairs $\{i,j\} \subset \{1,2,3,4\}$). In particular, for any positive Diophantine triple $a, b, e > 0$, real positivity in (6.3b)–(6.3c) forces $\operatorname{sgn}(r - \beta_1) = \operatorname{sgn}(r - \beta_2) = \operatorname{sgn}(r - \beta_3) = \operatorname{sgn}(r - \beta_4)$, confining all $6$-spoke-square rational solutions to the **exterior archimedean chamber** $r \in (-\infty, \min_i \beta_i) \cup (\max_i \beta_i, +\infty)$.
3. *(Exact splitting of $e^2 R_5(c, d, f, g, e) = 0$ into four bilinear factors and complete characterization of the determinant-zero locus $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} = 0$):* Substituting $(1 + cd) = r_{cd}^2$, $(1 + fg) = r_{fg}^2$, $(1 + ce)(1 + de) = \eta_e(Q_c)^2$, and $(1 + fe)(1 + ge) = \eta_e(Q_f)^2$ from (6.1a)–(6.1b) into the Universal Bowtie Identity (5.1b) factors $e^2 R_5(c, d, f, g, e)$ over $\mathbb{Q}(E_T \times E_T)$ into **four bilinear factors** indexed by $(\delta_c, \delta_f) \in \{+1, -1\}^2$:
   $$e^2 R_5(c, d, f, g, e) \equiv \prod_{\delta_c, \delta_f \in \{+1, -1\}} \Bigl(e\, r_{cd} r_{fg} - \delta_c \eta_e(Q_c) - \delta_f \eta_e(Q_f)\Bigr) = 0. \tag{6.4a}$$
   - **Opposite-sign pair ($(\delta_c, \delta_f) = (\delta, -\delta)$ with $\delta \in \{+1, -1\}$, $e$-independent additive-reciprocal translations):** Since $e r_{ab} \neq 0$, both opposite-sign factors always define invertible $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}(T))$ Möbius translations $\mu_{\delta, -\delta} : \mathbb{P}^1_{r_{cd}} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{P}^1_{r_{fg}}$:
     $$e\bigl(r_{cd} r_{fg} - \delta r_{ab}(r_{cd} - r_{fg})\bigr) = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{r_{fg}} - \frac{1}{r_{cd}} = \frac{\delta}{r_{ab}} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_{fg} = \mu_{\delta, -\delta}(r_{cd}) := \frac{r_{ab} r_{cd}}{r_{ab} + \delta r_{cd}}. \tag{6.4b}$$
   - **Same-sign pair ($\delta_c = \delta_f = \delta \in \{+1, -1\}$, $e$-dependent bilinear relations and determinant-zero member collisions):** The same-sign factor is
     $$e\, r_{cd} r_{fg} - \delta e r_{ab}(r_{cd} + r_{fg}) + 2\delta r_{ae} r_{be} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_{fg} = \mu_{\delta, \delta}(r_{cd}) := \frac{\delta e r_{ab} r_{cd} - 2\delta r_{ae} r_{be}}{e(r_{cd} - \delta r_{ab})}, \tag{6.4c}$$
     whose $2 \times 2$ Möbius matrix has determinant proportional to
     $$\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} := r_{ab}^2 - \frac{2\delta r_{ae} r_{be}}{e}. \tag{6.4d}$$
     1. **Invertible regime ($\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} \neq 0$):** Equation (6.4c) defines an invertible Möbius involution $\mu_{\delta, \delta} \in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}(T))$ if and only if $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} \neq 0$. Since $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(+1)} + \Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(-1)} = 2 r_{ab}^2 \neq 0$, at least one of $\mu_{+1, +1}, \mu_{-1, -1}$ (and hence at least three of the four factors in (6.4a)) is always an invertible $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ correspondence.
     2. **Determinant-zero regime ($\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} = 0$):** We have $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} = 0 \iff e = \frac{2\delta r_{ae} r_{be}}{r_{ab}^2}$, under which the triple equation $e^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1) = (r_{ae}^2 - 1)(r_{be}^2 - 1)$ becomes $r_{ab}^4(r_{ae}^2 - 1)(r_{be}^2 - 1) - 4 r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1) = 0$ (which forces $1 + a^2 = \frac{(r_{ab}^2 r_{ae}^2 - r_{ab}^2 - 2r_{ae}^2)^2}{4 r_{ae}^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1)^2} \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ and $1 + b^2 = \frac{(r_{ab}^2 r_{be}^2 - r_{ab}^2 - 2r_{be}^2)^2}{4 r_{be}^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1)^2} \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$, realized for instance by the mixed-sign triple $(a, b, e) = \bigl(\frac{4}{3}, -\frac{5}{12}, \frac{9}{4}\bigr)$ with $(r_{ab}, r_{ae}, r_{be}) = \bigl(\frac{2}{3}, 2, \frac{1}{4}\bigr)$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(+1)} = 0$). Whenever $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} = 0$, the $(\delta, \delta)$ factor in (6.4c) factors into the union of a vertical and a horizontal line:
        $$e\, r_{cd} r_{fg} - \delta e r_{ab}(r_{cd} + r_{fg}) + 2\delta r_{ae} r_{be} = e(r_{cd} - \delta r_{ab})(r_{fg} - \delta r_{ab}) = 0. \tag{6.4e}$$
        At the chord $r_{xd} = \delta r_{ab}$ ($\lambda = Y_S - \delta a b e\, r_{ab}$), writing $a b e = (r_{ab}^2 - 1) e$ and substituting $X = a b e\, x$, $a = \frac{r_{ae}^2 - 1}{e}$, $b = \frac{r_{be}^2 - 1}{e}$, and $e = \frac{2\delta r_{ae} r_{be}}{r_{ab}^2}$ into the residual chord quadratic (6.2a) yields the **exact polynomial identity**:
        $$\frac{q_\lambda(a b e\, x)}{(a b e)^2} - (x - a)(x - b) \equiv -\frac{(2\delta r_{ae} r_{be} x + r_{ab}^2)\Bigl(r_{ab}^4(r_{ae}^2 - 1)(r_{be}^2 - 1) - 4 r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1)\Bigr)}{4 r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2 (r_{ab}^2 - 1)} = 0. \tag{6.4f}$$
        Thus on the $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} = 0$ locus, the chord $r_{xd} = \delta r_{ab}$ on $E_T$ has roots $\{x_1, x_2\} = \{a, b\}$, so every point on the degenerate $(\delta, \delta)$ factor (6.4e) satisfies $\{c, d\} = \{a, b\}$ or $\{f, g\} = \{a, b\}$ (a base-member collision), and all distinct $7$-tuple candidates come from the remaining invertible $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ correspondences ($\mu_{-\delta, -\delta}, \mu_{+1, -1}, \mu_{-1, +1}$).
4. *(Branch-Point Collision Rigidity for Invertible $\mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f} \in \mathrm{PGL}_2$):* Let $(\delta_c, \delta_f) \in \{+1, -1\}^2$ be any sign pair for which $\mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f} \in \mathrm{PGL}_2$ is invertible (namely both opposite-sign pairs $(\delta, -\delta)$, and any same-sign pair $(\delta, \delta)$ with $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} \neq 0$). Let $B := \{\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3, \beta_4\} \subset \mathbb{P}^1$ be the $4$-element branch locus of $r_{xd} : E_T \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and let $m_{\mathrm{coll}} := |B \cap \mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f}^{-1}(B)| \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ be the number of colliding branch points. By Riemann–Hurwitz and Kani–Rosen [17], whenever $m_{\mathrm{coll}} < 4$, the fiber product curve $\mathcal{C}_{\delta_c, \delta_f} := E_T \times_{\mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f}} E_T$ is geometrically irreducible of genus $g(\mathcal{C}_{\delta_c, \delta_f}) = 5 - m_{\mathrm{coll}}$ and admits a third genus-$1$ quotient $E_{\mathrm{quot}}$ (branched at the $2(4 - m_{\mathrm{coll}})$ points of $B \triangle \mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f}^{-1}(B)$) if and only if $m_{\mathrm{coll}} = 2$. Then:
   - **(a) Rigidity of $m_{\mathrm{coll}} = 4$ on all invertible $\mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f} \in \mathrm{PGL}_2$:** For any non-degenerate rational Diophantine triple $T = \{a, b, e\}$ of distinct non-zero rationals with $abe \neq 0$, $r_{ab} r_{ae} r_{be} \neq 0$, and any invertible $\mu_{\delta_c, \delta_f} \in \mathrm{PGL}_2$ ($\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} \neq 0$ when $\delta_c = \delta_f = \delta$), full branch-point collision $m_{\mathrm{coll}} = 4$ never occurs over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $m_{\mathrm{coll}} \le 3$ and $\mathcal{C}_{\delta_c, \delta_f}$ is always geometrically irreducible of genus $g(\mathcal{C}_{\delta_c, \delta_f}) = 5 - m_{\mathrm{coll}} \ge 2$:
     1. On the opposite-sign maps $\mu_{\pm 1, \mp 1}$, a $4$-point collision $\mu_{\pm 1, \mp 1}(B) = B$ requires the $4$-element finite set $\{1/\beta_i\}_{i=1}^4$ to be invariant under the non-zero translation $x \mapsto x \pm 1/r_{ab}$, which is impossible over $\mathbb{C}$ since $1/r_{ab} \neq 0$.
     2. On the same-sign involutions $\mu_{\delta, \delta}$ with $\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}^{(\delta)} \neq 0$, an involution in $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ preserving the $4$-element set $B$ must act on $B$ either as the identity or as one of the $9$ non-trivial involutive permutations of $B$ ($3$ double transpositions and $6$ single transpositions with $2$ fixed points). Since $\mu_{\delta, \delta}(r) = \frac{\delta e r_{ab} r - 2\delta r_{ae} r_{be}}{e(r - \delta r_{ab})} \in \mathrm{PGL}_2$ is never the identity on $\mathbb{P}^1$ ($e \neq 0$), the identity permutation is impossible. Using $abe = e(r_{ab}^2 - 1)$, substituting $\beta_i = \frac{Y_S - \lambda_i}{e(r_{ab}^2 - 1)}$ into $e\beta_i\beta_j - \delta e r_{ab}(\beta_i + \beta_j) + 2\delta r_{ae} r_{be} = 0$ and multiplying by $e(r_{ab}^2 - 1)^2$ yields the linear polynomial in $e$:
        $$\mathrm{eq}_\delta(i, j) := (Y_S - \lambda_i)(Y_S - \lambda_j) - \delta e r_{ab}(r_{ab}^2 - 1)(2Y_S - \lambda_i - \lambda_j) + 2\delta e r_{ae} r_{be}(r_{ab}^2 - 1)^2 = 0.$$
        For the $3$ double transpositions $(i_1, j_1)(i_2, j_2)$, the difference $\mathrm{eq}_\delta(i_1, j_1) - \mathrm{eq}_\delta(i_2, j_2) = 0$ has $e$-coefficient $\pm 4\delta r_{ab}(r_{ab}^2 - 1) \cdot r_\star$ with $r_\star \in \{r_{ab}, r_{ae}, r_{be}\}$, which is non-zero since $r_{ab} r_{ae} r_{be} \neq 0$ and $r_{ab}^2 - 1 = ab \neq 0$; for the $6$ single transpositions $(i_1, i_2)$ fixing $i_3, i_4$, the fixed-point difference $\mathrm{eq}_\delta(i_3, i_3) - \mathrm{eq}_\delta(i_4, i_4) = 0$ has $e$-coefficient $4\delta r_{ab}(r_{ab}^2 - 1)(r_{xy} \pm r_{xz})$, which is also non-zero for distinct non-zero $(a, b, e)$ since $r_{ae}^2 - r_{be}^2 = (a - b)e \neq 0$, $r_{ab}^2 - r_{ae}^2 = a(b - e) \neq 0$, and $r_{ab}^2 - r_{be}^2 = b(a - e) \neq 0$. Solving uniquely for $e$ and eliminating $(e, r_{be}, r_{ae})$ across all $9$ involutive permutations proves that:
        - The double transposition $(12)(34)$ forces $e = \delta r_{ae} r_{be} / r_{ab}^2$ and $r_{ab}^2 = r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2 / (r_{ae}^2 + r_{be}^2)$, which upon substitution into the triple equation $e^2(r_{ab}^2 - 1) = (r_{ae}^2 - 1)(r_{be}^2 - 1)$ yields the strictly negative sum of squares
          $$-\frac{\bigl(r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2 - r_{ae}^2 - r_{be}^2\bigr)^2 + r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2}{r_{ae}^2 r_{be}^2} = 0 \qquad \bigl(\text{impossible over }\mathbb{R}\bigr); \tag{6.5a}$$
        - The double transpositions $(13)(24)$ and $(14)(23)$ force $r_{ab}^2(r_{ae}^2 - 1) = 0$ and $r_{ab}^2(r_{be}^2 - 1) = 0$ directly (contradicting $abe \neq 0, r_{ab} \neq 0$); and
        - Each of the $6$ single transpositions eliminates $(e, r_{be}, r_{ae})$ via resultants to a polynomial in $r_{ab}$ whose only non-degenerate rational root is $r_{ab} = 0$.
   - **(b) Explicit $m_{\mathrm{coll}} = 2$ (genus-$3$) rational families in the non-degenerate interior:**
     1. On $\mu_{+,+}$, the four cross-pair transposition conditions $\mu_{+,+}(\beta_i) = \beta_j$ for $(i,j) \in \{(1,3), (2,4), (1,4), (2,3)\}$ factor in the universal triple coordinates $(u, v, w)$ into two explicit $2$-parameter families: for $(i,j) \in \{(1,3), (2,4)\}$, $\mu_{+,+}(\beta_i) = \beta_j$ holds when $(w - u(v \pm 1))^2 - (2u^2 - 1)(v \pm 1)^2 = 0$, giving the **$2$-parameter rational family** parameterized by $(m, v) \in \mathbb{Q}^2$ where $2u^2 - 1 = s_u^2$:
        $$u(m) = \frac{m^2 + 2m + 2}{m^2 - 2},\qquad s_u(m) = \frac{m^2 + 4m + 2}{m^2 - 2},\qquad w(m, v) = \bigl(u(m) + s_u(m)\bigr)(v \pm 1). \tag{6.5b}$$
        On the $w = (u + s_u)(v + 1)$ branch of (6.5b), $\theta_1 = \frac{u - 1}{u + s_u}$ and $\theta_3 = \frac{u + 1}{u + s_u}$ are independent of $v$, and $\mu_{+,+}$ swaps the colliding pair $\beta_1 \leftrightarrow \beta_3$ given by
