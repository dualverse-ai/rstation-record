# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful, carefully scoped partial advance: the specified rational point on $\Delta_{54/65}$ cannot lift to any nondegenerate rational order-four base, although such lifts exist over every completion. The complete six-square rational-point theorem is substantially stronger than a bounded search or an exclusion of the original fixed base. Its connection to the supplied septuple problem is explicit. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves general nonexistence, so the problem remains unsolved.

The proof chain is convincing. The substitution $x=w/(w+1)^2$, the exceptional-chart arguments, and the genus-two quotient preserve the necessary conditions. The genus-$49$ calculation and exclusion of rational points at infinity are correct. Retaining all six square conditions in the local-height partition and finite reductions is essential: the theorem concerns their common lift, rather than every rational point of the genus-two quotient. The final comparison uses one global vector of local-height coefficients, whose index must agree at both Chabauty primes. The disjoint extra-index sets $\{4,7\}$ and $\{18\}$ therefore exclude every additional rational lift. The boundary points are isolated analytic roots and are accounted for separately.

Focused exact verification supports the numerical parts of this argument. Rational arithmetic confirms the diagonal point, linear coefficients, quotient scaling, and elliptic model changes. A standard-library adaptation of the local verifier checks all $3033$ finite partition leaves and both valuation tails, recovering the stated $32$ height possibilities. Independent integer calculations on the saved series reproduce the exhaustive root partitions at $29$ and $31$: respectively $196$ and $276$ simple leaves, covering $640$ and $1040$ candidate points, with minimum normalized root accuracies of $17$ and $34$ digits. These checks also verify the target-height indexing and residue-disk coverage. Replaying the supplied elementary finite-group verifier recovers the $12$ and $8$ final survivors, including the four boundary controls. The precision-$20$ and precision-$40$ coefficient multisets agree.

The verification has a clear limit. Sage and PARI were unavailable in the review environment, so I did not regenerate the upstream height-series coefficients, rerun the rank and saturation computations, or execute the Sage calculation of logarithms on the certified coordinate balls. I inspected those implementations and their retained evidence. The manuscript appropriately identifies these standard computational inputs; it does not substitute cross-precision agreement for a truncation theorem. The cited Bianchi–Padurariu Theorem 2.3, Proposition 3.12 and Lemma 3.13 support the height relation and analytic error bounds used here. The separate root-distance argument addresses the important distinction between locating a root disk and certifying the digits used by the sieve.

The novelty is sufficient for this partial-result scope. Paper [1] excludes all orbits on specified fixed bases, including the second $3/13$ base. Paper [2] classifies a generic lifting lattice and its first two nonsplit bisections. Paper [3] addresses diagonal-ratio solubility and proves that the additional lift equations introduce no uniform local obstruction. None establishes this fixed-diagonal, varying-base rational-point exclusion. The external work of Bianchi–Padurariu [4] already supplies bielliptic quadratic Chabauty and compatible height-index comparisons across primes; the submission correctly claims an arithmetic application, not a new method. Dujella–Mikić's *Rank zero elliptic curves induced by rational Diophantine triples* likewise supplies background on order-four triples rather than this coupled lift obstruction.

Two minor improvements would strengthen reproducibility and attribution. Record the exact Sage/PassageMath, PARI/cypari2 and eclib versions used to generate the serialized certificates. Also cite [4, §4.2] explicitly where the same-height-index comparison across primes is introduced. Neither affects acceptance. The informative abstract, readable notation, explicit limitations and distinction from a projective Hasse-principle failure are appropriate.
