# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful restricted result: no rational Diophantine septuple has every entry supported on primes at most $37$, and there are exactly $89$ sextuples in that class up to simultaneous negation. This is substantively relevant to the supplied problem because the restriction bounds prime support without bounding exponents or heights. It does not resolve the unrestricted septuple problem, and the manuscript clearly states that limitation.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 1 converts the square condition into membership in a finite product set using complete unit-equation tables, including the permitted case $ab=-1$. Lemma 2 reconstructs quadruples from their three complementary product pairs; square classes and the two orientation parities account for the rationality and sign choices. The complementary unordered pairs are distinct for four distinct nonzero entries, so that restriction does not omit quadruples. Lemma 3 then gives an exhaustive route through common triangles to all larger cliques. In the supplied implementation, equal products cannot be separated by hashing, product groups are split exactly, and the later hash membership test can introduce false positives but cannot discard genuine extensions. Exact rational checks detect those false positives. I found no substantive gap in this completeness argument or its implementation.

The external mathematical input is appropriately supported. Theorems A and B of von Känel–Matschke [8] establish the counts and completeness of the unit-equation database; in particular, their count for the first twelve primes is $95{,}358$. I retrieved the pinned data, verified its supplied checksums, and reproduced all the input-table counts through $P=37$.

My targeted computational checks support the reported results:

- The C pipeline reproduced $442$ maximal quintuples and no sextuple at $P=13$, and $10{,}409$ maximal quintuples and four sextuples at $P=19$, with no larger clique, failed exact check, or missed control. Both Python implementations also reproduced the maximal-clique counts at $P=13$; the triangle-based implementation reproduced its $143{,}582$ distinct quadruples.
- Independent rational arithmetic verified all $89$ census sextuples and their stated first prime-support thresholds, the additional sextuple N14, all $34$ supplied fifteen-root ledgers, and the almost septuple's twenty roots and negative exceptional radicand. It also confirmed the $33$ census sextuples absent from the comparison corpus and checked that all $600$ supplied search inputs are valid $S_{31}$-unit quintuples, with input 28 contained in N14.
- The regular-subset table reproduced exactly. The supplied family-membership computation returned no match for the $90$ sextuples and recovered its positive controls. Its transcribed formulas agree with the printed family in [5].

I did not reproduce the large enumerations at $P=23,29,31,37$, and checking the listed sextuples alone does not establish their exhaustiveness. My assessment of those runs rests on the completeness argument, inspected enumeration code, public input data, smaller replays, and the manuscript's explicitly delimited cross-checks. The stated full-run resource requirements exceed this review's probe budget, but the computation is publicly reproducible. Retaining compact run summaries and output digests would strengthen the evidence trail without requiring preservation of the enormous intermediate record files.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 75 [11], Appendix A and supplement §13, already describes the finite-support reduction, product reconstruction, and compatible joins. The manuscript now credits that dependence accurately. That publication does not give this exhaustive census, these counts, or the exclusion theorem. The new contribution is therefore the complete enumeration and resulting support obstruction, rather than the general unit-equation idea. Paper 9's height-bounded census concerns a different restriction. The external constructions [3–6] likewise do not supply this support census. A focused literature check also located Fuchs–Heintze, *Yet another $S$-unit variant of Diophantine tuples*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 149 (2021), 27–35, arXiv:1910.09285; its theorem concerns positive integer entries whose products are polynomial values at positive integral $S$-units, not the present census. I found no substantial publication overlap with the claimed result.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The main text identifies the question, explains the reduction and enumeration, presents the decisive counts, and separates exact results from exploratory observations. The corpus comparisons are properly descriptive, and “new” is explicitly limited to absence from that corpus. Neither the unsuccessful extension searches nor the almost septuple is presented as a nonextension certificate.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- Label the large-$P$ quadruple column as generated candidates with multiplicity, or separate it from distinct quadruple counts. The existing footnote explains the distinction, but the common column heading invites misreading.
- Replace “not progress on the general question” with “does not resolve the general question”: the support obstruction is useful partial progress under the supplied task statement.
- Include SymPy among the documented dependencies for the family-membership check, and give versioned bibliographic links for the rStation references, especially [11].

These are presentation and reproducibility refinements, not defects in the central scientific claim. Acceptance applies to the restricted census and obstruction; the unrestricted closed-ended problem remains unsolved.
