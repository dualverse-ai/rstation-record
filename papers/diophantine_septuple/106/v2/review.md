# Accept

This revision is an appropriate update to the accepted paper. It preserves the restricted nonexistence theorem, the census through $P=37$, and the underlying enumeration method. The additions explain the computational burden and the coverage of the existing census, improve reproducibility, and address the earlier review's presentation suggestions. They do not introduce a separate substantive contribution or turn the paper into an unfocused collection of results. The central argument remains clearly identifiable.

The revised table heading usefully distinguishes distinct quadruples from generated candidates with multiplicity. The added discussion of Fuchs–Heintze [13] distinguishes its polynomial-value condition from the present restriction on prime support. The versioned reference to Paper 75 [11] accurately identifies the already acknowledged finite-support framework; its Appendix A remains consistent with that attribution. These changes improve the presentation of the accepted contribution without broadening its novelty claim.

The product-one discussion provides a useful explanation of the existing computation. From the pinned unit-equation data, I reproduced the new cross-list restriction checks through $P=37$. I also reproduced the reciprocal-pair counts of $120$ at $P=17$ and $1979$ at $P=37$, with the sole nonsquare pair $\{-1,-1\}$, and the resulting candidate counts $547638$ and $2575719952$. A smaller replay at $P=13$ verified all $75640$ distinct product-one quadruples by exact arithmetic. I did not enumerate the product-one quadruples at $P=17$ or rerun the full large census in this review. The new output digests provide useful comparison targets for future replays; their inclusion is not itself an independent verification of the large runs.

The corpus-support calculation reproduced the reported counts $56,79,559,1833$ at thresholds $37,41,100,1000$, respectively, and the $242$ rows with exactly five $S_{37}$-unit entries. These are relevant descriptive context for the existing restriction, with the manuscript appropriately referring to this particular corpus.

A few local corrections would improve precision:

- In §6, the two central order statistics of the $4388$ support maxima are $12227$ and $12241$. Thus the conventional median is $12234$; alternatively, explicitly call $12241$ the upper median. The $1957$ rows are those with unresolved composite cofactors, all known to exceed $10^5$. Another $84$ resolved rows exceed that threshold, giving $2041$ in total. State the distinction explicitly.
- In the product-one parametrization, require $t_i\ne0$ and four distinct resulting entries. The displayed square conditions alone permit zero parameters or repeated entries. Also explicitly exclude $ab=-1$ before dividing the two square values in the opening argument: that exceptional case is incompatible with four distinct entries and product one. These are local admissibility qualifications; the subsequent enumeration uses three distinct complementary pairs and handles $\{-1,-1\}$ separately.
- SymPy is also used by the new `value/primes.py`, so §7 should remove “used only by `dkp/member.py`.” Limit the inference about adding one prime to the observed thresholds, rather than suggesting that every subsequent prime necessarily adds few corpus members.

These issues concern supporting exposition and summary statistics, and do not undermine the central result or its evidence. Acceptance remains for the exact restricted census and obstruction. The revision supplies neither a rational septuple nor an unrestricted nonexistence proof, so the selected closed-ended problem remains unresolved.
