# Accept

This revision correctly addresses the local corrections requested in the previous acceptance review. It preserves the accepted contribution: the exact census and restricted nonexistence result for tuples with $S_{37}$-unit entries. The changes improve precision without introducing a new substantive result or expanding the paper into an unfocused collection. The research question, enumeration strategy, evidence and limitations remain clearly communicated.

The added argument in §6 correctly excludes a pair product of $-1$ in a product-one Diophantine quadruple. Under $ab=cd=-1$, the two displayed identities, together with distinctness and nonzero entries, force both $ac$ and $-ac$ to be nonzero rational squares, a contradiction. This justifies the subsequent division of square values. Requiring nonzero parameters and four distinct resulting entries also fixes the converse parametrization's admissibility conditions.

I reran the revised `value/primes.py` on the supplied corpus. It reproduced $2041$ rows with $P_{\max}>10^5$, comprising $84$ resolved rows and $1957$ rows with unresolved composite cofactors, and the two central values $12227$ and $12241$, whose average is $12234$. The unresolved values are all above $10^5$, so their exact ordering cannot affect these central order statistics. The unchanged threshold counts $56,79,559,1833$ and the $242$ rows with exactly five $S_{37}$-unit entries also reproduced. Restricting the inference about adding a prime to the observed increase from $37$ to $41$ is appropriate. The dependency documentation now correctly identifies both uses of SymPy.

These checks concern the revision's supporting argument and statistics; I did not rerun the large clique census. Its previously accepted evidence and stated computational limitations remain unchanged. Acceptance continues to concern the restricted census and obstruction: this version supplies neither a rational septuple nor an unrestricted nonexistence proof, so the selected problem remains unresolved.
