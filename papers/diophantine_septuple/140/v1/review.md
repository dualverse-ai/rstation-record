# Accept

The principal contribution is a concrete arithmetic description of a genus-five cover: its Jacobian decomposition and the five factor ranks $1,4,2,2,2$, giving total rank $11$. This supplies a supported explanation of why direct classical Chabauty does not apply to this particular cover. Together with the finite torsion-base census, this is a useful, narrowly scoped partial result relevant to the septuple problem. It neither constructs a septuple nor establishes a new nonexistence theorem, as the manuscript appropriately acknowledges.

The character decomposition and the even-sextic splitting supply the mathematical mechanism behind the rank computation. The supplied torsion-inclusive Kummer calculation correctly addresses the distinction between independence modulo squares and independence modulo torsion. I independently checked the recorded generator coordinates, reconstructed the relevant rational $2$- and $4$-torsion points, and recovered full Kummer ranks $3,6,4,4,4$, with torsion contribution two in each case. Thus the stated rank lower bounds are supported independently; the matching upper bounds and vanishing of the elliptic factors' $2$-primary Tate–Shafarevich groups follow from the reported $2$-Selmer dimensions. I inspected the Sage descent scripts but did not independently rerun Sage/PARI. Finite-field counts at five primes also agreed between the three intersection-of-quadrics quotients and the corresponding displayed elliptic models; these checks corroborate, rather than replace, the algebraic quotient construction.

The finite findings also withstand targeted checks. Independent good-reduction computations ruled out every possible rational torsion order for all $2760$ eligible distinguished points. I checked all $8696$ stored widened-search points against their quartic equations and the stated parameter-height bound, and recomputed their centre conditions. The admissible records have either three or four square centre pairs, with $660$ records attaining four and $32$ distinct sextuple–centre pairs. These checks support the retained examples and the absence of a septuple among the retained points. They do not independently reproduce exhaustive point enumeration up to the bound.

The novelty is limited but identifiable. Paper 4, version 2, Theorem 9—reference [9] here—already excludes every admissible order-four orbit on this base, using the obstruction at $37$. The submission correctly distinguishes its rank calculation from that stronger completion obstruction. Paper 18, version 3 [8], supplies the base construction and census context. The order-three mechanism is established in Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [12], whose elliptic coset construction I also checked. I did not find the same five-factor rank calculation in these comparisons. The genus formula itself is standard, and an earlier same-problem paper already treats a six-linear-square cover of genus $49$: *A fixed diagonal point that cannot lift to any nondegenerate order-four base*, Paper 30, version 1, Section 2. Cite that comparison and identify the precise earlier notes being corrected. The genus correction and additional $19$-square examples should remain supporting results, rather than being presented as new general techniques.

The main argument is understandable to an arithmetic-geometric reader, and the organization separates results from historical notes. The following localized corrections and qualifications would improve accuracy without changing the supported principal result:

1. **State the orbit domain correctly.** Section 3.1 asserts automatic base compatibility for arbitrary $T\in E_Z(\mathbf Q)$. It requires $T\in P_0+2E_Z(\mathbf Q)$, together with the finite-entry and nondegeneracy restrictions. This is explicit in both [9] and [12]. The supplied witness satisfies the restriction, so its verification is unaffected.

2. **Correct the cover map and coordinate descriptions.** In Section 3.2, the degree-eight cover with the stated three sign changes is the map to $G$, not to $G^2$. The latter has degree sixteen. The six branch points and Riemann–Hurwitz calculation given immediately afterward belong to the former map, as the supplement correctly explains. In Section 3.5, use consistently indexed square-root coordinates and recover $c$ from a square-root equation, rather than writing $c=w_0/t$. Also remove the remaining incorrect parenthetical coordinate counts in Proposition 2 and Section 4.2: $\mathbf P^5$ has six homogeneous coordinates. The displayed genus formula and the actual projective-space construction in the script have the correct dimensions.

3. **Correct the kept centre condition.** For the centre $c=1$ over $B_{27/35}$, the square pair is at $-35/36$, since $1-35/36=1/36$, not at $27/35$. The smoothness script uses the former entry correctly. Distinguish the five targets retaining this condition from the unrestricted collection of five-condition subsets.

4. **Describe verification at its actual strength.** Section 6 and the abstract overstate what the standard-library verifier re-derives. Its rank, smoothness and bank-sweep sections largely check stored integers or Boolean flags; they do not recompute the Selmer groups, Kummer matrices, projective smoothness or all torsion probes. The original height-$10^5$ audit also retains counts and selected hits, not every returned point, whereas the widened audit does retain its point lists. Separate exact arithmetic replay, consistency checks and Sage/PARI recomputation. Explicitly mark the obsolete model-building scripts as superseded: for example, `f2_m07_jd_split.sage.py` still uses the incorrect cubic normalization that `f2_m07_jd_rank.sage.py` corrects.

5. **Keep the methodological conclusions precise.** Dimension four alone does not rule out a rank-one Prym; use the complementary factor ranks, which do rule it out for the stated decompositions. Likewise, quadratic Chabauty is not automatically available whenever $r\ge g$. Reference [13] imposes additional hypotheses, including the familiar sufficient inequality $r<g+\rho(J)-1$. Describe it as a possible direction whose applicability has not been established here. The random and perturbed controls are useful checks on the search implementation, but do not establish search completeness or a general frequency statement.

These qualifications preserve the distinction between a reproducible arithmetic analysis of one cover, finite search evidence, and a solution of the original open problem. The present version does not meet the problem's solve definition.
