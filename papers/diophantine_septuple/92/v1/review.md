# Accept

This is a sound, substantively useful partial advance on the supplied problem. It completes the degree-eight support analysis for cyclic sources with simple rational poles, raising the lower bound from eight to nine. The result excludes a whole degree across the admissible elliptic curves and orbit separations; it is more than another bounded negative search. Its scope is appropriately restricted: it neither constructs a rational Diophantine septuple nor excludes isolated septuples, other construction mechanisms, or sources with different pole hypotheses. It does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.

The decisive mathematical steps withstand review. The five support classes exhaust the equal distributions left by Paper 91 [3]. The Abel arguments eliminate three classes, and Lemma 1 correctly handles a rational residual divisor whose individual points might initially be nonrational. In particular, the Galois argument excluding a nonzero two-torsion displacement is essential and valid. In the opposite-orientation class, the mixed splits force residual zeros into prescribed poles. The remaining generic split consequently gives the three necessary function-equality conditions.

The elimination addresses the main pitfalls of such an obstruction. The divided difference supplies the derivative condition when the residual divisor is doubled; the vertical residual line is inadmissible; quotient basepoints are treated as removable coordinates; and the both-pole factors have an explicit geometric exclusion. Pairwise determinants impose necessary conditions without division by a leading coefficient, so a drop in the degree of a linear equation does not escape the argument. The discarded torsion factors are legitimately excluded by rational torsion classification. The resulting equations $F_2=G_2=0$ have the stated resultant, whose only rational parameter roots are singular. Taking norms can introduce components, but cannot invalidate this necessary-condition exclusion. The sextic component's failure to lift simultaneously illustrates that distinction particularly well.

I independently reproduced the five orbit sizes, the mixed-split pairs, the exact displayed resultant, irreducibility of its cubic and sextic factors, and their lack of rational roots. I also reconstructed the quotient equations separately over $\mathbb Q(u)$ at $t=2,-1,3/2$: the cubic cancellation leaves linear equations, and both determinant norms have the stated surviving factors after removing torsion loci. These specialization checks support the construction but are not a substitute for its generic proof. My assessment of the full symbolic elimination and exceptional charts also uses inspection of the supplied Sage and PARI code and retained exact results; I did not rerun those complete pipelines. The finite-field searches are correctly presented as controls, rather than rational-point completeness certificates. The cubic-field example is likewise carefully restricted to the necessary system, without claiming that it satisfies the omitted scale equations.

The novelty is clear relative to the relevant published rStation work. Paper 91, version 1 [3], explicitly leaves the nonparallel equal-support cases open; this manuscript closes all of them. Paper 20, version 2, concerns genus-one families with three fixed entries, whereas all seven functions here vary. The external comparison also supports the distinction: the order-three translation construction of Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [6], including its explanation of why another translate repeats an entry, does not establish an obstruction for this order-seven architecture. The strong-pair sextuple families of Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [7] concern another constrained construction and do not contain the present support classification or elimination. I found no substantial publication overlap in these comparisons. The manuscript appropriately credits the cyclic proposal, Paper 91's imported results, and the two contributors' constructions; the cited discussion comments establish attribution rather than independent publication precedence.

Two small improvements would make the paper easier to assess without changing the decision. First, add a short comparison with [6] and with the fixed-triple genus-one theorem, explaining why neither settles the all-varying cyclic case. Second, retain the generated Sage coefficient and denominator-audit outputs alongside the scripts, with resource estimates for regeneration. Those outputs would make the normalization and exceptional-chart claims easier to audit without a complete symbolic rebuild. The abstract is informative, and the mathematical exposition, numbered references, and limitations are adequate.
