# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial arithmetic result, with appropriately limited claims. The paper establishes a rank-two, 2-saturated subgroup of the displayed genus-three Jacobian and determines exactly the S-unit portion of its normalized 2-Selmer image. The unresolved class-group contribution is explicitly retained. Neither a rank upper bound nor a rational-point classification follows, and the manuscript correctly avoids claiming either. This acceptance does not resolve the rational Diophantine septuple problem.

The independent small-prime argument for Theorem 1 is particularly convincing. The bitangent characters at 17 and 19 detect the two generator coefficients separately. The counts over $\mathbf F_7$, $\mathbf F_{49}$ and $\mathbf F_{343}$ give $\#J(\mathbf F_7)=907$, excluding rational 2-torsion. These facts prove both integral independence and 2-saturation by the stated division argument; no global rank computation is needed. I reproduced the restrictions, square characters, point counts and good-reduction checks.

The broader descent also has the necessary logical links. Uniqueness of the six-dimensional permutation submodule identifies the geometric $J[2]$ representation. Crucially, global injectivity follows from surjectivity on invariants in $0\to V\to P\to P/V\to0$, rather than from incorrectly treating the finite-group calculation $H^1(G,V)=0$ as an absolute Galois-cohomology vanishing theorem. The good-prime constraints deliberately enlarge ambiguous local images, so they remain valid upper bounds.

The arithmetic evidence supports Theorem 3. The replay verifies integrality and norm support of the 22 generators and their independent squareclass characters; Dirichlet's theorem then establishes completeness of the S-unit squareclass space without assuming a class number. The good-prime equations have rank 18. At 2, the orbit sizes and code weights bound the local Kummer dimension by four, while four certified evaluation classes attain that bound. The finite local-unit computation and the separate local-power replay agree: exactly masks $0,2,8,10$ survive. Exact square-root identities identify the surviving plane with the two rational divisor classes. Thus the upper bound and the known rational lower bound coincide on the S-unit space. Projecting to the S-class group gives precisely the stated remaining obstruction $C_J$.

I reproduced the portable certificate checks, including the quotient identities and explicit canonical-map image identity. The Magma Galois-group identification, complete submodule lattice and canonical-model construction were assessed from their supplied code and retained outputs, rather than independently rerun. The slower incidence Gröbner-basis reconstruction was likewise not rerun; the exact working bitangent square identity was reproduced. These are limits of this review's verification, not missing public reproduction instructions.

The novelty is the explicit arithmetic of this curve. Comparison with the published transport paper [1] shows that its normalization interfaces and genus-five quotient do not already establish these genus-three descent results. Bruin–Poonen–Stoll [2] supplies the general descent framework, theta combinatorics and distinction between S-units and the class-group contribution; those ingredients are properly treated as established theory. Bruin–Lewis [3] studies quartics with all 28 bitangents rational, unlike the present residual degree-27 field. The classical three-isogeny sextuple construction [4] also does not supply the results here. The focused literature comparison revealed no substantial duplication. Determining the complete unit contribution and isolating the remaining obstruction is meaningful progress on the stated coefficient-curve route, despite leaving its rational points and the septuple problem open.

Useful improvements are modest:

- Make the connection to tuple transport more explicit in Section 2: define the source meanings of $r,u$, identify the coefficient matching that produces the displayed equation, and give a precise location in [1] or an additional derivation. This would make the relevance easier to use independently.
- Add [3] to the related-work discussion and emphasize that the weight enumerator is universal theta-code structure, whereas its identification with this particular Galois representation is the application-specific result.
- Specify Python 3.10 or later because the code uses `int.bit_count`, and explicitly set PARI's thread count in the S-unit audit. Its default thread allocation exceeded the review memory limit; setting one thread allowed the arithmetic replay to pass within 2 GiB. Align the scripts' memory settings with the advertised replay budget.

[4] A. Dujella, M. Kazalicki, M. Mikić and M. Szikszai. *There are infinitely many rational Diophantine sextuples*. International Mathematics Research Notices 2017(2), 490–508, especially Section 6. https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.00569.
