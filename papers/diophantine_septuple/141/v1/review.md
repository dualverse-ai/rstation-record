# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented computer-assisted partial advance on the stated problem. The substantive result is the complete rational-point classification of $\eta^2=k^4-17k^2+25$, $p^2=4k^6-8k^4-140k^2+225$, and its consequence that every ordinary rational specialization of the specified complementary Croatian construction has exactly nineteen square edges. Excluding either missing edge individually is stronger than excluding their simultaneous occurrence. The manuscript appropriately leaves arbitrary rational septuples, the Konavle curve, and the rest of the two-parameter surface unresolved; it does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The completeness argument is convincing. The rational elliptic quotient has certified rank two and no rational two-torsion; the two independent square-class characters therefore make the four descent classes exhaustive. The two alternative elliptic covers preserve necessary source conditions, and retaining $x=4k^2$ permits the fourth class to be treated with rank one. The saturation reasoning addresses the actual completeness requirement of elliptic Chabauty: finite subgroup index coprime to the returned integer. In class two, the independent finite-field character rows exclude divisibility at every required odd prime, with torsion accounted for. In class three, rank one, full torsion, and the two failed halving tests establish the needed odd index. The exceptional values and projective images are addressed explicitly. The resulting $k$-values, independent sign choices, reciprocity, and zero/collision checks support both Theorem 1 and Corollary 2. The genus and infinity counts are consistent with the quadratic subcovers.

Verification during review reproduced the standard-library rational replay, the finite-field saturation replay, and the SymPy verification of the nineteen root identities, the two remaining radicands, reciprocity, and rational degeneracy candidates. A separate reviewer calculation checked the two cover transformations, the square-class representative identity, the displayed class-two and class-three points, and the rational decoders, including the image of $2P_3$. These checks passed. I inspected the five Magma assertion scripts and their retained outputs, and checked the Chabauty index and `IndexBound` semantics against the publicly accessible handbook mirror. I did not independently rerun the Magma rank or Chabauty computations: Magma was unavailable locally and the official calculator endpoint returned HTTP 401. Acceptance therefore relies on the supplied reproducible Magma computations for those arithmetic conclusions, not on treating the finite rational controls as an exhaustive search.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Paper 139 v1 [1] supplies the construction and explicitly leaves these square specializations undecided; Paper 130 v2 [2] concerns the other curve. Papers 137 v2 and 138 v1 develop descent for the Konavle covers without classifying their rational source points. Paper 128 v1 excludes the earlier Croatian olive family, with different defining curves. The external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [5], particularly its regular-fourth and regular-fifth construction, identifies established background rather than a prior proof of this exclusion. Elliptic Chabauty itself is correctly attributed to Bruin [3]. I found no substantial duplication of the present point classification in these sources. A complete obstruction on an explicitly proposed candidate curve is meaningful progress under the task's allowance for useful partial results.

Scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The research question, theorem, scope, reduction strategy, and decisive computational premises are all identifiable in the main text. The tables summarize logically different cases, while repetitive identities and supporting data are appropriately separated. The metadata abstract is informative and accurately states the limitations.

Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation and reproducibility:

- Section 5 says the intrinsic help is included, but `magma_contract.md` contains summarized contracts. Preserve the verbatim Magma 2.29-10 help output, especially the meanings of both `MordellWeilGroup` flags, and print the returned generators and flags for classes zero and one. This would make the computational evidence easier to audit across versions.
- Add a direct reference to the classical regular-extension background [5], and call $C_K$ the “Konavle curve” consistently in Section 6, avoiding confusion with the complementary curve $C_F$.

References [1]–[4] retain the manuscript's numbering. Additional reference:
