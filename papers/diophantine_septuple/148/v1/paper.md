# Local square tests and independent missing edges in rational almost septuples

## 1. Statements

Write an *edge* for $1+xy$ at two distinct entries of a seven-element set of nonzero rational numbers. A place of $\mathbb Q$ can be finite or real. We write square classes additively when discussing linear independence in $\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times 2}$. Independence modulo a subspace $V$ means that the product of each nonempty subset has class outside $V$.

**Theorem 1.** Fix a finite set $S$ of places, a finite-dimensional space $V$ of rational square classes, and an integer $l\ge1$. Let $G=(-1,10)$ on the Croatian olive elliptic curve of Lasic and Oblak [1], in the Weierstrass coordinates below. There is a positive integer $D$ and a set of positive integers $n$ of positive lower density with the following properties. Each of the points
$$
(n+jD)G,\qquad 0\le j<l,
$$
gives seven distinct **positive** rational entries, exactly twenty of whose edges are rational squares. Each remaining edge is a square in every $\mathbb Q_v$ for $v\in S$. The $l$ remaining edges have independent rational square classes modulo $V$.

Thus one can prescribe splitting at any fixed finite set of primes in arbitrarily long lists of linearly disjoint real quadratic fields, each generated by the final root of an almost septuple. The tuples vary with the prescribed places. By taking $l=1$ and successively enlarging $V$, one obtains an infinite linearly disjoint sequence. Positive lower density refers to the integer index $n$ in the stated cyclic group.

**Theorem 2.** There is a rational one-parameter family of seven distinct nonzero rational functions with nineteen specified square edges. For given $S,V,l$ as above there is a positive integer $D$ and there are infinitely many rational $w$ such that all the inputs $w+jD$ give ordinary seven-element sets with all edges locally square at $S$. The $2l$ remaining edge classes are independent modulo $V$.

The elliptic family in Theorem 1 comes from [1], which proves that twenty edges are square and gives an open arc of positive tuples of positive frequency. Paper [2] determines the rational points of the untwisted last-edge cover by Chabauty and descent. The family in Theorem 2 comes from the regular-extension component containing AS14 of [3], called AS025 in [4]. It has a rational base and two independent missing conditions. Our new assertions concern simultaneous local tests and independence of the missing classes, including relations across translated points. General Hilbert irreducibility results for ramified covers of abelian varieties appear in [5]. We give direct arguments using branch divisors on curves, Faltings's theorem [6] and rational approximation. The question whether seven rational entries can give twenty-one rational squares remains open.

## 2. Translates on the elliptic curve

Use the model and parameter of [1, Theorems 1--3]
$$
 E:\ Y^2=X^3-23X+78,\qquad k=\frac{2X+46-4Y}{4X+7}.
$$
The function $k$ has degree two and a pole at $O$. Its quartic model is $y^2=f(k)$ with $f=k^4-2k^3+12k^2-96k+128$. Let $\Phi$ be the monic, squarefree, degree-14 polynomial of [1, Appendix A], reproduced in the verifier. The final edge is
$$
 \frac{\Phi(k)}{k^6(3k-4)^2(k^3-4k^2-16k+32)^2}.
$$
Away from eight named exceptions all seven entries are distinct nonzero rationals at rational points of $E$, with the other twenty edges square. The map to unordered tuples has finite fibres [1]. Let $F=\Phi(k)$. The polynomials $f,\Phi$ are coprime. Thus the geometric parity divisor of $F$ on $E$, meaning the formal sum modulo two of the places of odd valuation, consists of 28 distinct points above the 14 roots of $\Phi$. At either pole of $k$ the order of $F$ is even. The genus of the last-edge double cover is 15 [1]. Paper [2] proves the sole rational lifts occur at $k=4/3,\infty$, where the entry chart is exceptional.

**Lemma.** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$, let $F$ have nonempty geometric parity divisor, and let $H$ be a rational point of infinite order. For any nonempty finite set $I$ of integer shifts the product
$$
 F_I(P)=\prod_{j\in I}F(P+jH)
$$
has nonempty geometric parity divisor. Each twist $z^2=cF_I$ with $c\in\mathbb Q^\times$ has a geometrically connected smooth projective model of genus at least two.

Indeed, translation by $H$ acts freely on $E(\overline{\mathbb Q})$. On one orbit meeting the parity divisor, label its points by integers under successive translations and let $B$ be the finite nonempty set of occupied labels. The parity divisor of the product restricts to the sum of the sets $B-j$ modulo two. The label $\max B-\min I$ occurs exactly once. It gives a branch point. A rational function of odd valuation cannot be a geometric square. The number of branch points is even, hence at least two. Riemann--Hurwitz over the genus-one base gives genus $1+r/2$ for $r$ branch points. Multiplication by a constant leaves $r$ unchanged.

For fixed $H$, $l$ and $V$, there are finitely many subsets $I$ of $\{0,\dots,l-1\}$ and finitely many classes in $V$. A product of the corresponding final edges has class $c\in V$ only if $P$ lifts to the twist $z^2=F_I/c$, outside the finitely many zeros and poles in the formulas. Each twist has finitely many rational points by the lemma and [6]. Hence outside a finite exceptional set of rational starting points on $E$, the translated block of classes is independent modulo $V$. This conclusion holds for **every** infinite-order step $H$, including steps chosen to meet local constraints.

## 3. Local square conditions and positivity

At $O$, the function $F/k^{14}$ is regular and equals 1 since $\Phi$ is monic. At each finite $p\in S$, continuity and openness of $\mathbb Q_p^{\times2}$ give an open neighborhood of $O$ in $E(\mathbb Q_p)$ where $F$ is square whenever $k$ is finite and nonzero. This also holds at 2 since $1+8\mathbb Z_2$ consists of squares. Choose an open subgroup in each such neighborhood, using the formal group filtration or the standard basis of open subgroups of $E(\mathbb Q_p)$ [7]. Its index is finite by compactness. Their finite quotient groups give a common positive integer $M$ such that every multiple of $MG$ belongs to the designated subgroups, at every prescribed finite prime. This uses only recurrence in a finite quotient, including at bad reduction primes.

By [1, Theorem 4], $E(\mathbb R)$ is a circle and every $k$ in $(-2,4/3)\setminus\{0\}$ gives strictly positive entries. Choose an open arc of positive tuples and a smaller open arc $U$ with compact closure inside the first. Irrational rotation by $MG$ is equidistributed. Some positive multiple $DG$ with $M\mid D$ is sufficiently close to $O$ that $U+jDG$ is inside the positive arc for all $0\le j<l$. For $n$ divisible by $M$ and $nG\in U$, the points $(n+jD)G$ now satisfy all the local conditions. Positivity makes the last edge positive, hence square over $\mathbb R$. The set of such $n>0$ has density $\mu(U)/M>0$ in the positive integers, where $\mu$ is normalized circle measure. Removing finitely many starting points as in Section 2 preserves this density and proves Theorem 1. The fields generated by independent square classes have compositum of degree $2^l$ by elementary Kummer theory.

For an exact two-tuple example take $n=-15,41$ and the point $G$ above. The verifier recovers the seven entries from [1, Section 4 and Appendix A] and checks all 21 products, positive entries and distinctness separately at each index. For the primes $2,3,5,7,11$, the valuations of $F$ are $(14,-42,4,-14,-14)$ and $(26,-14,4,0,0)$. The units after removing the powers of $p$ pass the local square criteria at each of these primes. The residues of $F$ at 17 are 9 and 14, and its residues at 29 are 26 and 28. At 17 the first is square and the second nonsquare. At 29 the two characters are reversed. This directly certifies independence. The indices and the group law reproduce the example without printing the large coordinates.

## 4. A rational base with two missing edges

Let $w$ be indeterminate and put
$$
 t=\frac{4w+3}{2(1-w^2)},\quad q=1+wt,\quad H=2q^2=2t^2-3t+2,\quad
 \beta=(2-t)/2,\quad r=(t-1)/q,\quad \lambda=(2-t)(2t-1)/t .
$$
On the opposite-sign regular-neighbor component through AS025, use a separate parameter $K$, set $L=H/(tK)$, and define
$$
 a=t/K,\quad b=-K/H,\quad c=(L-K)/2,\quad e=L(2-1/t),\quad f=K(t-2).
$$
Complete the tuple with the two regular fourths
$$
 u_\pm=a+b+c+2abc\ \pm 2\sqrt{(1+ab)(1+ac)(1+bc)}.
$$
For orientation, $t=-75/14$, $q=43/7$, $K=559/138$ gives AS14 in [3]. In that specialization only $1+cf$ fails. To choose a rationally varying $K$ with the two core roots, start with
$$
 k=\frac{q(4(t-1)+2q)}{t(3-2t)},\qquad d=t+kr,\qquad v=r-k/H .
$$
One has $d^2=\beta k^2+q^2$ and $v^2=k^2/(2H)+(2t-1)/(2t)$. On the smooth elliptic curve $y^2=x(x+1)(x+\lambda)$ put
$$
 P=(\beta k^2/q^2,\ 2\beta kdv/q^2).
$$
Then $x(P)+1=(d/q)^2$ and $x(P)+\lambda=4\beta v^2$. The three Kummer classes of $P$ for the split cubic are $(\beta,1,\beta)$. The Kummer map factors through the quotient by doubling [7], so $3P$ has the same three classes. Choose $K^2=q^2x(3P)/\beta$. This is a rational function square, and the other two classes give $K^2(1+ac)$ and $1+bc$ square. Since $1+ab=r^2$, both regular fourths belong to $\mathbb Q(w)$.

We use representatives $A_0=1+cf$ and $B_0=K^2(1+ce)$. Direct multiplication of the seven functions gives the following exact certificate, also proved by the supplied symbolic algorithm.

1. All seven functions are nonzero and distinct. The nineteen pairs other than $cf,ce$ have square radicands in $\mathbb Q(w)$.
2. In the monic factorization of $A_0$, respectively $B_0$, exactly one polynomial has odd exponent. It is a monic irreducible polynomial $A$, respectively $B$, of degree 58 and exponent one. The coefficients of $w^{57}$ are respectively 26 and 29. The constants in these two factorizations are $1/9$ and $1/36$, squares in $\mathbb Q$.
3. At $w=-1/2$ the rational function $K$ vanishes and $A_0=1/9$, $B_0=4/9$. At $w=-2/5$ the entries are all distinct and nonzero, $|K|=160680558/50206985$, and the literal failed edges $1+cf,1+ce$ equal respectively
$$
 \frac{144116289591241426}{22686672085112025},\qquad
 \frac{897169273819351249}{1452276096704514225}.
$$

The certificate is over $\mathbb Q$, with no point counts or floating-point input. Construct the fraction field of $\mathbb Q[w]$, multiply $P$ by 3 and extract the polynomial numerator and denominator squares to recover $K$ and the fourths. For each pair, reduce $1+xy$ in that field and verify the square root by multiplication. Factor the two stated representatives over $\mathbb Q$ and retain odd exponents. These steps and the specializations are executable in the supplement. Each individual twentieth-root cover has genus 28, and the cover for the product has genus 57. Thus only finitely many parameters of this particular section can carry even one additional edge, by [6].

To prove Theorem 2 choose $D$ divisible by sufficiently high powers of every finite $p\in S$. At these places take $w$ sufficiently close to $-1/2$. All the values $A_0(w+jD),B_0(w+jD)$ are squares by item 3 and openness. At the real place both polynomials $A,B$ are monic of even degree and the removed constants are squares. Taking $w$ sufficiently large and positive makes all translated representatives positive. Weak approximation gives infinitely many rational $w$ in the specified open sets. All poles and degeneracies occur at finitely many parameters and shifts.

For an integer $D\ne0$, all $2l$ translated degree-58 polynomials $A(w+jD), B(w+jD)$ are distinct irreducibles. Translation changes the subleading coefficient to $26+58jD$ or $29+58jD$. These numbers are pairwise different. Every nonempty product thus has a squarefree representative of degree at least 58. Its double cover, including every constant twist, has genus at least 28. Faltings's theorem gives finitely many rational parameters in each prescribed class for each nonempty product. Remove the finite union over the classes in $V$ and subsets of the $2l$ polynomials to obtain Theorem 2. The invariant $ab=-t/H$ is nonconstant, so a fixed unordered seven-element set occurs at finitely many parameters.

## 5. Consequences for the search

For either family, every predetermined finite list of local edge tests can accept ordinary rational tuples whose missing classes are independent of any previously fixed finite list. For any particular tuple, a failed global square has an obstruction somewhere. The prime detecting failure depends on the tuple. The elliptic family already has a complete decision for the untwisted last edge [2]. The rational-base family supplies an independent two-edge example and an explicit ramification test for future deformations. Determination of the ordinary twentieth-root parameters of its genus-28 covers remains open.
