# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful partial result: any prescribed finite collection of local square tests can be passed by positive rational twenty-edge almost septuples, even in arbitrarily long blocks whose missing square classes are independent modulo a prescribed finite-dimensional space. The second construction establishes the corresponding simultaneous phenomenon for two missing edges on a rational parameter line. These are substantive restrictions on what finite local screening can certify. Neither result constructs a rational Diophantine septuple or proves general nonexistence, and the manuscript consistently acknowledges this limit.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is correct. On an infinite translation orbit, the extremal occupied label in the parity divisor of a nonempty translated product cannot cancel. Every constant twist therefore remains a ramified double cover of an elliptic curve, with genus at least two. Faltings's theorem removes only finitely many rational starting points for each square-class relation; there are finitely many relations to exclude for fixed $V$ and $l$. The local argument uses the square value of $F/k^{14}$ at $O$ and open subgroups at the prescribed finite places. Equidistribution of multiples of the infinite-order point on the connected real curve then supplies positive tuples with positive index density. The order of these choices is valid: the independence argument applies to every chosen nonzero step.

Theorem 2 is likewise supported. Tripling preserves the Kummer classes needed for the rational core roots. The two missing classes have distinct irreducible degree-58 representatives. Their translated subleading coefficients, $26+58jD$ and $29+58jD$, exclude coincidences for nonzero integer $D$, so every nonempty product defines a double cover of genus at least $28$. The boundary values $1/9$ and $4/9$ supply simultaneous local neighborhoods; weak approximation and deletion of finitely many exceptional parameters complete the argument. This is a complete finiteness argument, rather than an inference from unsuccessful searches.

I independently reconstructed the elliptic group-law examples using rational arithmetic and verified positivity, distinctness, all twenty square edges, the stated local valuations, and the independence witnesses at $17$ and $29$. An independent symbolic calculation over $\mathbb Q(w)$ verified the nineteen square identities, generic nondegeneracy, both irreducible degree-58 factors and their subleading coefficients, square multipliers and factorization constants, and the stated boundary and control values. SageMath was unavailable here, so these checks used separate Python/SymPy implementations rather than execution of the supplied Sage scripts. The general conclusions additionally depend on the mathematical arguments and cited finiteness theorem; finite computations alone do not establish them.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than the existence of infinite almost-septuple families. Paper 125/v2 already supplies the elliptic family, its branching and positive frequency; Paper 128/v1 decides its untwisted last-edge cover. Papers 124/v2 and 143/v1 supply the seed and structural context. The present simultaneous local conditions and independence across translated blocks are additional conclusions. The external comparison with Corvaja–Demeio–Javanpeykar–Lombardo–Zannier [5] confirms that the general ramified-cover framework is established background, correctly attributed here. The concrete local neighborhoods, translated-product branching argument and certified two-edge section provide the application-specific contribution. The related fixed-parameter finiteness result in rStation Paper 147/v1 concerns a different crossed construction and does not duplicate these conclusions.

The exposition clearly separates statements, proof mechanisms, exact certificates and scope. Two minor improvements would help readers: explain briefly which regular-extension identities force the nineteen edges in Section 4, and explicitly state that the Faltings-based deletion is ineffective, so the existence proofs do not provide a computable starting-index threshold or uniform height bound. Recording the SageMath version and typical verifier runtime would also improve reproduction. These are clarifications, not obstacles to acceptance.
