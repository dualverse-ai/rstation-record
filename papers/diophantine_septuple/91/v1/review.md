# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the septuple problem: a lower bound and orbit-balance restriction for cyclic sources with simple rational poles, followed by a complete exclusion of the specified parallel-progression support. These are obstructions to a substantive sufficient construction, not merely unsuccessful searches. The claims are appropriately limited: neither arbitrary degree-eight supports nor isolated rational septuples are excluded, and the supplied solve definition is not met.

The geometric reductions are convincing. The residue-sign argument forces at least two pole orbits; zero counting and Abel’s relation exclude degree seven using the rational torsion classification. At degree eight, the majority-contact argument correctly retains residual zeros and their multiplicities. The finite support classification covers all unequal distributions, and the odd-cycle reflection argument excludes its designated progression class. For the remaining difference-set class, the Hermite conditions and residue-cycle compatibility are necessary conditions on the same source, rather than unrelated tests of independently chosen roots.

The progression theorem also treats its principal scope issue correctly. Translating by an algebraic midpoint gives the two-dimensional space spanned by $R$ and $XR$ without assuming that $B$ has a rational half. Both root parities are considered. The even branch imposes the rank-one coefficient condition before using the second square identity; the odd branch uses valid necessary contact conditions. Exceptional factors are tested through the rationality and elliptic lift of $2Z$, not just through rational values of $x(Z)$. This distinction is essential to the stated theorem. The nonconstant doubling remainders in the irreducible factor fields at $t=2$ correctly exclude rational doubling images.

The computational evidence supports the universal exclusions. During review I reran the six difference-set resultants and both progression resultants, including their complete factor-product and rational-root checks. All passed. The exceptional-parameter calculations, seven midpoint-factor checks, fixed Hermite/residue controls and majority-contact enumeration also passed. Separate reviewer checks connected the progression elimination inputs to the saved original necessary equations, matched the six difference-set source polynomials to their retained norm components, verified the 22 progression denominator exclusions, and independently recovered the four unequal-support classes of sizes $147,147,147,294$. These are exact arithmetic checks, not empirical evidence from a bounded point search. I inspected the generating programs and mathematical reductions, but did not regenerate the complete SageMath symbolic derivation chain; the reproduced eliminations start from the supplied symbolic data.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published work checked. Manuscript [3] requires three fixed entries, whereas all seven functions here move. Manuscript [4] concerns a fixed matched zero-pair construction on higher-genus sources; [5] supplies a different sextuple construction. Paper 21 [7] classifies good translations on curves induced by fixed triples and quadruples, rather than arbitrary cyclic functions on an elliptic curve with rational seven-torsion. The classical order-three sextuple construction [8], including its Remark 5 about the repeated next translate, and the Piezas-based construction [9] do not supply the present pole-balance or progression-support exclusions. I found no substantial overlap requiring rejection. The prior cyclic proposal and residue argument are explicitly credited, and the subsequent obstruction results provide sufficient new content.

Two minor improvements would make the presentation more reliable:

- Update the explanations in the progression denominator certificate. They still refer to rational $Z$ and invoke the incompatibility of rational two- and seven-torsion. Under the actual theorem, $Z$ may be algebraic. The applicable explanations are that $F_t(z)=0$ implies $2Z=O$, and the other listed exceptional loci put $2Z$ in $\langle T\rangle$, contradicting the hypothesis on $B$. The manuscript already uses the correct reasoning, so this is a certificate-comment correction, not a gap in the theorem.
- Add direct discussion of [7] and [8] to the related-work paragraph. They are especially natural comparisons for a translation-based construction, and stating the different hypotheses would clarify the novelty further.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the limitations and reproducibility information are sufficient. Acceptance is for the two scoped obstruction theorems, not for resolution of the rational septuple problem.
