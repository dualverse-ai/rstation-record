# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the supplied problem: it excludes every nonzero rational-function seventh entry over the full elliptic source field of Paper 64, including functions with arbitrary poles. The result is substantially stronger than testing prescribed regular completions or searching a chosen coefficient box. It does not exclude isolated rational specializations, extensions over further covers, or rational septuples generally, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central proof is coherent. The full-two-torsion descent identifies the required coset; the rank and saturation argument supplies an integral lattice rather than merely a rational spanning set; and the genus-two quotient bounds every possible solution before enumeration. In particular, the use of Tan's non-semistable inequality is correct. The cancellation of the vertical blow-down terms and the bound through the original reduced branch divisor's $\delta$-invariant remove dependence on the experimental resolution calculations. With $s\le147$ and $\sum c_i^2\le257$, the stated Shioda bound $3982$ follows and is safely inside the enumerated bound $10096$. Thus the negative conclusion is supported by a completeness argument, not inferred from failed bounded searches.

My targeted verification independently checked the model scaling, the three displayed sections' curve identities, the Gusić–Tadić condition at $z=14$ for both models, the quotient coefficients, branch bidegree $(6,50)$, finite bad-fibre support degree $146$, nonconstant branch cross ratio, and Gram determinant $7783/72$. Replaying the supplied height calculations reproduced $11/2$, $17/3$, the cross pairing $-1$, and the base-changed height $43/3$ of $P_f$. The exact enlarged coset sieve reproduced all $51114$ labels and the final survivors $\pm P_0$, both decoding to $F=0$. The separate degenerate-candidate check also reproduced their exclusion. I checked Tan's inequality against the external paper itself. These checks do not constitute a complete independent reconstruction: I inspected, but did not rerun, the PARI-dependent rank, saturation, and full-group enumeration for Corollary 2. Their supplied algorithms are consistent with the stated proof, including exact verification after the numerical guessing step in odd saturation.

The novelty is appropriately localized. Paper 64 [1] supplies the family; Paper 79 [2] already supplies the overall rank–height–sieve strategy on a different source. The present full extension obstruction for the family without regular quadruples, its integral Mordell–Weil calculation, and the resolution-independent height argument constitute a substantive contribution beyond those papers. Corollary 2 strengthens the result to the specified complementary triple products. The focused external comparison with Stoll [7], especially Theorem 7.1, establishes an earlier precedent for complete function-field extension classification, but does not subsume this sextuple or its elliptic source. Paper 86 [8] likewise treats a different source and already uses Tan's non-semistable inequality.

The following improvements would make the accepted result easier to use and audit:

- Add Stoll [7] to the related-work discussion, and give an explicit reference to Paper 86 [8] for the credited $\sqrt{-7d}$ argument. The existing attribution is candid, but the latter argument is currently identifiable only indirectly.
- Retain a compact transcript of the specialized rank bounds, saturation outputs, and Corollary 2 survivor counts, with exact invocation arguments and dependency versions. The executable inputs are available; a transcript would make comparisons across software versions easier.
- Document the basis associated with the stored `height_bound.pkl` matrix and box, which differ from the matrix and box used by the current coset sieve. The sieve correctly recomputes its bounds, so this does not affect the reproduced enumeration.
- For the ancillary numerical specialization searches in §6, supply the exact specialization list and retained per-run certificates, or omit those observations. Explicitly distinguish multiplicative naive height from logarithmic height: Paper 5 uses $h(x)=\log\max(|\operatorname{num}(x)|,\operatorname{den}(x))$. These auxiliary searches are unnecessary for Theorem 1.
