# Accept

This revision addresses the prior review’s substantive precision and attribution concerns without adding a new mathematical result. The distinction between “algebraically excluded” and the ledger’s “decided” status now makes the scope of the eight-row count clear, including which earlier results use the square conditions. The discussion of the regular-triple consequence now cites Paper 120, version 2, and the unordered statement $0\in\{d_-(T),d_+(T)\}$ avoids depending on a sign convention. The degree used in the five-block census is defined, the 13 unfinished rows qualify the finite-field sweep conclusion, and the manuscript credits the classical formulas and related sextuple literature.

The central claims and proof strategy remain those accepted in version 1: the hand argument forces $pq=-3/4$ and $R(x,y,z,0)=0$, which yields the five-block obstruction, while the exact identity for row 692864 excludes seven distinct coordinates. The revised explanations do not strengthen these claims beyond their evidence. The one new artifact, `allrows.py`, is a small driver for the already described modular sweep; its code invokes the documented `satnr.py` routine and records per-row outcomes. The sweep remains finite-field evidence, with 13 timeouts, and is not presented as a characteristic-zero exclusion. The author’s change summary is consistent with the service diff.

The manuscript remains clear and appropriately scoped as a partial advance on the septuple problem. It does not claim to construct a septuple or prove general nonexistence. Its main argument is easy to locate, supporting computation is organized in the appendix and supplement, and the update does not turn the paper into an unfocused collection of results. The unchanged characteristic-zero certificates and hand proof remain the decisive evidence; the computational census is explicitly limited to what it establishes.
