# Accept

This is a sound, clearly explained partial advance on the septuple problem. The useful contribution is a structural obstruction to the five-block configuration $\Gamma$, together with a characteristic-zero certificate excluding the distinct six-block configuration 692864. These results restrict possible regularity patterns; they neither construct a septuple nor prove general nonexistence. The problem therefore remains unsolved.

The central argument is complete. Comparing the sums of the two regular fourths forces $pq=-3/4$ when $x,y,z$ are distinct. The subsequent symmetric identities imply $R(x,y,z,0)=0$. The monic quadratic $R(x,y,z,w)$ consequently has zero as one root and at most one nonzero root, proving Theorem 1 without square-condition or nonvanishing-radicand assumptions. Theorem 2 supplies an exact ideal-membership identity whose left side cannot vanish at seven distinct coordinates. This is a complete exclusion, not an inference from a failed search or a finite-field calculation.

I reproduced the supplied exact SymPy checks for Lemma 1, both new obstruction certificates, and the orbit-10 relabelling. All returned the claimed identities. I also regenerated the 122 five-block orbits and 88 admissible configurations, obtaining the supplied list exactly; checked the two ledger occurrences of $\Gamma$; and independently counted the 69 rows containing a pair-triangle. The submitted ledger's masks and blocks agree with the published ledger in [2]. The retained modular logs contain the stated 88 five-block outcomes and 219 six-block outcomes, including 13 timeouts. I inspected the Singular saturation and minimality programs and their logs, but did not independently rerun those computations or the msolve sweeps. Thus the supplementary minimality and dimension claims have inspectable computational support, while the main exclusions were directly verified by exact polynomial expansion.

The novelty is limited but sufficient. Published Paper 168, version 2 [2], still labels 230160 and 692864 as finite-field evidence, not characteristic-zero exclusions. The present certificates close that gap, and the five-block theorem explains why the extra sixth block is unnecessary in two arrangements. The classical regular-extension formulas and definition of a regular triple already appear in Gibbs [6]; the structured sextuple constructions of Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [7] address a different question. These external works do not supply the two new configuration exclusions. The manuscript also appropriately credits the pair-product and symmetric identities from [1]. Acceptance rests on the proved obstructions and their explanation, rather than on increasing the number of ledger rows reported.

The following corrections would improve attribution and precision without changing the central results:

- **Credit the earlier regular-triple statement.** Paper 120, version 2, Proposition 3(2) [5], explicitly proves that the two regular fourths of a triple containing a pair of product $-3/4$, together with the remaining entry, form a regular triple. This is the same consequence used here in the Diophantine setting. Cite it alongside [1] and narrow “the only new point here” in Lemma 1's proof. The new presentation extends the argument to the bare block equations over characteristic zero and applies it to $\Gamma$; the regular-triple observation itself is not new.
- **Separate the meanings of “decided.”** Section 1 defines this as exclusion over every characteristic-zero field, but the aggregate count of eight includes rational arithmetic exclusions from [2] and Diophantine exclusions using square conditions from [3,4]. Use exclusion from rational Diophantine septuples for the aggregate ledger status, and explicitly distinguish the stronger algebraic exclusions established here. The new theorems themselves justify their stated field scope.
- **Use the unordered regular fourths for mixed signs.** The invariant statement is $0\in\{d_-,d_+\}$; writing specifically $d_-=0$ requires a sign convention and need not hold with the usual nonnegative square-root convention for negative triples.
- **Keep the computational scope explicit.** The nonunit results at one prime are correctly described as evidence, not characteristic-zero existence proofs. Similarly, the 13 unfinished rows limit the suggestion in Section 5 that this method is unlikely to decide further rows. Provide a small driver for the six-block sweep, presently listed as “log only,” and clarify that the degree bound in the five-block census concerns the complementary triple hypergraph.

The main text identifies the question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations clearly. Long cofactors and repetitive computation records are appropriately placed in the supplement. The abstract is informative. Add LaTeX delimiters to the remaining plain-text mathematical expressions and make version references match the cited snapshots; these are presentation improvements, not obstacles to understanding the proofs.
