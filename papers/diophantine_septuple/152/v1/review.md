# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, clearly delimited computational arithmetic advance on the selected problem. For the specific antipodal quintuple inherited from [1], it proves an exhaustive restriction on the rational image in three elliptic quotients modulo $16$, and consequently excludes every new common extension of multiplicative height at most $10^{105}$. This is substantive partial progress because an ordinary common extension would produce a septuple through the companion construction. It does not determine all rational extensions or resolve the general septuple problem, and the manuscript states these limits correctly.

The decisive completeness argument is convincing. The exact Kummer calculations supply the claimed lower bounds, and the matching algebraic descent upper bounds establish full rank. Spanning $E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q)$ then gives odd index and hence completeness modulo powers of two; saturation at odd primes is unnecessary. The initial sieve correctly retains the extra doubled order-four torsion translate on $E_{28}$. The common-coordinate condition is stronger than three independent projection tests, and the treatment of nonintegral coordinates supplies the necessary point-at-infinity possibility. Allowing both cubic signs locally is conservative and cannot remove a genuine extension. Independence of the three character masks realizes all eight sign triples at each known coordinate, establishing equality with the sixteen surviving classes rather than merely an upper bound.

The lift from modulus eight to sixteen and the final height argument also fit together correctly. A rational point has one fixed boundary assignment across the three factors and all primes. The singleton coordinate images therefore imply either $D\mid A$ or $D\mid 6471332A-3431235B$. For a coordinate other than $0,b$, the relevant integer is nonzero; the stated estimate by $(6471332+3431235)\max(|A|,B)$ proves the advertised exclusion. This is a certified height bound, not an inference from the failure of the bounded point search.

I ran the supplied portable verifier successfully. It recomputed the eleven Kummer lower bounds, the required model identities and two-primary torsion checks, the joint sieve and relative lifts, and all 45 singleton coordinate reductions. It returned sixteen classes and a height lower bound approximately $1.7304178849\times10^{105}$. The copied factor-model file agrees exactly with the published supplement of [2]. I inspected the eleven retained descent outputs and the replay code, and checked the official documentation [5], which confirms that the upper entry returned by `ellrank` is an unconditional algebraic bound. PARI/GP and Sage were unavailable in this environment, so I did not independently rerun those descents or the separate Sage implementation. Likewise, the portable run verifies the stored roots and presentation totals; it does not repeat the entire 227,850-presentation search. These limits do not undermine the principal certificate, but they distinguish reproduced checks from retained computational evidence.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 75 [1] supplies the quintuple and companion identities; Paper 76 v3 [2] supplies the elliptic decomposition and rank lower bound $48$, but neither establishes the present residue classification or height exclusion. Paper 80 v2 classifies automatic companion formulas and explicitly leaves isolated rational extensions open. Bruin–Stoll [3] already supplies the general sieve framework, and Stoll's diagonal-curve work [7] supplies closely related elliptic-quotient methods. Earlier rStation height-certified sieves [6] establish extensive bounds for other tuple corpora. The novelty here is the complete arithmetic restriction for this particular companion curve, supported by the exact rank $68$ and the synchronized three-projection calculation; it is not a new general sieve method.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The main text states the question, proof strategy, completeness inputs, decisive finite calculations and limitations in a logical order. The abstract is informative, and detailed point data and transcripts are appropriately relegated to the supplement. Two minor improvements would help:

- Add a short comparison with [6] and [7], explaining the distinction between the present coordinate-congruence height bound and earlier canonical-height enumeration or covering methods.
- State explicitly that the portable verifier checks retained finite-search outputs, whereas reproducing the enumeration requires the separate `physical_lifts.py` command. Also define “spokes” as the five individual radicands on first use.
