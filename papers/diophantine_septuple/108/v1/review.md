# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its substantive contribution is the combination of a complete finite-support classification with two unconditional nonextension results: the $S_{41}$ census identifies 144 sextuples, and the genus-two argument excludes three of them as subsets of a rational septuple, including the previously settled case. This gives a concrete, exhaustive list of 141 remaining possibilities when six entries have the specified prime support. It does not resolve existence of arbitrary rational septuples, and the manuscript generally states that limitation accurately.

The completeness argument for the census is convincing. Lemma 5 preserves a witness for every 5-clique by choosing its least triangle; keeping all ties makes the argument valid without a uniqueness assumption. Together with the reconstruction and grouping lemmas of [1], this supports exhaustive enumeration rather than a height-bounded search. Inspection of the implementation also shows that product-hash collisions are separated by exact exponent comparisons during quadruple generation, while the resulting quintuples undergo exact rational checks. The dependence on the complete von Känel–Matschke equation lists [5] is explicit and appropriate.

The nonextension proof has the necessary arithmetic ingredients. The local descent uses images of genuine divisor classes and certifies completeness by reaching the known local dimensions, including dimension six at 2. Thus random sampling supplies generators for a certified span rather than a probabilistic rank conclusion. The rank-one cases then have a non-torsion class, the required saturation at the sieve primes, an annihilating differential nonvanishing in each known residue disc, and an empty sieve intersection for the remaining discs. The explicit reciprocal checks address zero square roots, which the task permits. Repeating the Chabauty computation at a second prime is useful corroboration, and the manuscript correctly acknowledges that this is not an independent implementation.

I independently checked all 144 sextuples and their 2,160 supplied pairwise roots, distinctness up to global sign, prime support, the 32 new sextuples absent from the comparison corpus, the absence of rational sextic points at infinity, and the reciprocal exclusions. At least four of the other five factors are nonsquares for every tested reciprocal candidate. I also reproduced the full-record and least-triangle enumerations at $P=13$: both give the same 442 maximal quintuples and the retained SHA-256 digest, with 574,700 versus 143,687 records. Independent point counts over $\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ reproduce all four Jacobian orders in Table 2 and the corresponding numbers of unknown discs. These checks support the retained evidence but do not constitute a rerun of the $P=41$ census. I inspected the descent, Chabauty and sieve code and transcripts; I did not reproduce those computations because `cypari2` is unavailable in the review environment. The large census also exceeds the supplied resource budget.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the published snapshot. Paper 106 v3 [1] already supplies the finite-support reduction and the census through 37; Paper 15 v2 [2] already supplies the descent, quadratic local divisors at 2, saturation criterion and Chabauty–sieve method. Those methods should receive no new-method credit here. The least-triangle reduction, the expanded complete classification, and the two additional nonextension theorems nevertheless form a meaningful combined contribution. The focused external comparison with the von Känel–Matschke data and the sextuple constructions in arXiv:1507.00569, arXiv:1904.00348 and arXiv:2403.17959 did not identify a preceding result covering these claims. The differently defined $S$-unit problem in arXiv:1910.09285 does not duplicate this census. Absence from the comparison corpus is appropriately not advertised as absence from every known family.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, computational evidence and scope well enough for a reader in arithmetic geometry. The following modest clarifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- In §5, replace the blanket statement that closing the remaining cases “needs a method beyond classical Chabauty.” Rank one remains possible for the cases explicitly left unresolved in §1. The current descent does not certify applicability there; it does not prove inapplicability.
- Keep the conclusions drawn from the Frobenius test explicitly heuristic throughout. The script itself disclaims a proof that the endomorphism ring is $\mathbb Z$. Consequently, the statement that basic quadratic Chabauty “does not apply” should retain the same qualification.
- In Corollary 4's proof, remove the premature assertion that distinctness and nonzero entries imply $xe+1\ne0$. The following explicit reciprocal argument supplies the correct justification.
- Describe the storage reduction as approximately a factor of four, since ties are retained. Also make memory and worker counts configurable in the reproduction scripts: the supplied descent allocates a 4 GB PARI stack and the sieve starts five workers.

These are local qualifications and reproducibility improvements, not defects in the central census or nonextension argument.
