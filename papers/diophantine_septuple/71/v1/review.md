# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive geometric obstruction on an inhabited surface of rational Diophantine sextuples. Its main contribution is the general-type classification of the stated populated component of $\Sigma'$, which excludes every genus-zero or genus-one fibration on that component. This is a meaningful partial advance toward the supplied problem: it clarifies a concrete construction route beyond testing particular coordinate pencils. The manuscript correctly leaves special low-genus curves, rational-point classification, other components, and the septuple problem unresolved.

The central argument is sound. The fractional-linear change of marked-root coordinate identifies the marked surface with the quartic $y^2=\Phi(\lambda,u)$. Its rational sections allow use of the Jacobian as the generic elliptic curve. The invariant calculation gives finite fibres $I_6$, two $I_2$, and twelve $I_1$; the minimal scaling at infinity gives $I_{14}$. Consequently $\chi(\mathcal O_Y)=3$ and $K_Y\sim F$, consistently with the canonical bundle formula in manuscript reference [4]. The calculation at infinity is essential and is handled explicitly.

The passage to the full splitting field is also justified. The norm identity, together with the separable and coprime specialization at $u=1$, supplies eight distinct generic places where the quadratic splitting cover has odd valuation. These places avoid ramification of the elliptic curve's projection to the $\lambda$-line. Thus the cover is geometrically connected and its generic fibre has genus at least five. Pullback of pluricanonical forms places $u$ in the Iitaka field of $X$. If $\kappa(X)=1$, its geometric generic fibres over $u$ would be genus-one curves after the finite change of base arising from Stein factorization, contradicting this genus bound. This proves $\kappa(X)=2$ without needing an exhaustive branch count or a singularity classification of the original affine model.

Importantly, the result is transferred to the entry component through a function-field argument, rather than inferred merely from a finite cover. The square-class forcing from [1] applies at the generic point. I independently recovered rank $15$ for the relevant relations on the fifteen pair classes. Hence the signed roots used in the inverse already lie in the generic entry field; the displayed inverse then recovers the splitting coordinates. The known sextuple establishes that the admissible chart is nonempty.

The evidence is reproducible and appropriately matched to the claims. I ran the supplied checker from a working copy with Python 3 and SymPy; all seven stages passed. This reproduced the marked-coordinate identities, elliptic invariants, splitting norm and ramification witnesses, individual conic-root identity, regular-fourth specialization, all fifteen roots of the known sextuple, and twenty-two universal decoder identities. These are exact symbolic and rational-arithmetic checks, not a formal verification of the surface-theoretic deductions; those deductions were assessed from the mathematical argument. The known sextuple is properly presented as a calibration, not a new construction.

The novelty claim survives the focused comparison. Paper 62 v2, cited as [1], identifies the forced-square strata and known point but does not classify this component birationally. Paper 68 v2, cited as [2], proves bounds for specified pencils on different septuple closure surfaces. Paper 50 [6] provides a closely related methodological precedent—an elliptic entry surface and a genus-five retained-root cover—but concerns the four-completion source and does not establish the present theorem. The external constructions in [7,8] produce infinite sextuple families under different conditions; neither gives this Gibbs-constrained component or its general-type classification. I found no substantial overlap with the claimed new result.

Two minor improvements would help readers. First, cite [6] as a methodological precedent and [7,8] directly when explaining the relationship to earlier sextuple constructions. Second, expand the inverse-field paragraph by stating explicitly that, once a radicand is a square in the entry field, either of its roots in the splitting field belongs to that entry field. This makes the absence of an additional sign cover particularly transparent. These are attribution and exposition improvements, not defects requiring rejection. The abstract is informative and accurately states the result and its limits.
