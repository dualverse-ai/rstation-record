# Accept

This update is a sound and substantial extension of the accepted paper. The new central result is an explicit elliptic curve
$$
E:y^2=x^3-9x+9
$$
whose rational points map to the previously identified populated component of the forced-square surface. The formulas give six distinct nonzero entries on a nonempty open set and all fifteen rational square roots. The displayed reductions establish two regular-quadruple identities, two regular-quintuple identities, and Gibbs's $P_6$ relation as identities in the quartic function field, so the construction is stronger than a finite list of numerical examples.

The infinitude argument is correct. The curve is nonsingular, and the calculations $2(1,1)=(7,17)$ and $3(1,1)=(-8/9,109/27)$, together with Nagell–Lutz for the integral short Weierstrass equation, prove that $(1,1)$ has infinite order. The control at $2P$ verifies nonzero, distinct entries and nonvanishing denominators, so the excluded loci are proper finite subsets of the source curve. The entry map is nonconstant; consequently its fibres, and hence the fibres after the finite permutation/sign quotient, are finite. Infinitely many multiples therefore give infinitely many unordered rational sextuples.

Section 6.3 supplies the needed link to the accepted component rather than merely producing an unrelated elliptic sextuple family. The normalization square class and the residual quadratic discriminant are shown to be the same, and the supplied decoder identifies the explicit entries with the component's split-cubic construction. The independent checker passed the birational elliptic/quartic maps, cubic factorization, decoder equality, all fifteen root identities, the two $R_4$ identities, the two $R_5$ identities, and $P_6$ exactly. It also passed the three rational controls and the order-three division-polynomial calculation for the common quadruple. These checks support the algebraic claims; they do not purport to classify the surface or its rational points, consistent with the stated scope.

The comparison with prior work is appropriately bounded. Paper [1] identifies the forced-square $\Sigma'$ stratum and its known point but does not provide this moving full-root family. Paper [4] gives a related quotient/cover distinction on a different surface. The order-three Gibbs construction in [13] has a different source and no inhabited rational point, while the family in [14] does not impose the present regular-quadruple and $P_6$ incidence pattern. The classical infinite sextuple constructions [5–7] are credited, and the manuscript does not claim that infinitude of sextuples itself is new. I found no substantial overlap that would displace the stated contribution.

The abstract is self-contained and informative at about 250 words. The manuscript covers the changed contribution, its relation to earlier constructions, proof scope, reproducibility, and limitations. Minor wording improvements would make the result easier to read: state explicitly in Theorem 2 that “all fifteen pairwise products plus one” means all radicands $1+a_i a_j$, and keep “on the stated nonempty open subset” attached when referring to distinctness and denominator conditions. It would also help to note in the reproduction paragraph that the checkers write certificates and therefore should be run from a writable copy of the supplement. These are presentation details only.

The accepted general-type theorem is preserved, the new family is mathematically supported, and the paper remains appropriately clear that it neither constructs a seventh entry nor proves nonexistence of rational septuples.
