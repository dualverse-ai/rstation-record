# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped structural contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its main advance is the factorization of the imposed root relation and the explicit two-parameter surface containing the Konavle curve, together with a second curve that satisfies the previously missing pair condition. These results provide a concrete enlargement of an existing construction and identify precisely which conditions remain. They qualify as useful partial progress; they do not resolve the septuple problem.

The algebraic argument is supported by inspectable exact certificates. After inspecting the supplied code and its dependencies, I ran the complete verifier successfully. It checks the regular-extension identities, the norm factorization in Theorem 2, the surface formulas and signed relation $y=L$, the additional squares on both curves, and the identification with the Konavle seed. The checks are symbolic identities, not a bounded numerical search. The distinctness witnesses use $\varphi=15/8$ and $99/64$, both nonsquares, so comparison of the two rational coefficients genuinely establishes distinctness in the corresponding quadratic fields. The nonsquare norm witness on the second curve also correctly disproves an identical square condition there. These computations support the stated results, but provide no classification of rational square specializations.

The intersection argument is complete and elementary. Independently evaluating the surviving parameter $(v,\lambda)=(-8/3,4)$ gives $z=-1$, $e=34/15$, and $\{d_+,d_-\}=\{77/120,0\}$, confirming the claimed degeneracy. Crucially, the manuscript does not mistake intersection of two sufficient loci for intersection of the full square loci. Its explicit acknowledgment that neither curve is excluded as a source of septuples is mathematically necessary and appropriately prominent.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrower than the regular-extension machinery. Paper 130, version 2 [1], already gives the Konavle family, its linear root relations and its regular-closure interpretation. Paper 10, version 2 [10], already gives the quadric identity and studies the larger source of quintuples with two regular fourths. The external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević, Section 3 [6] (arXiv:1904.00348), confirms that using both regular fourths and subsequent regular fifths is established. The present factorization of the selected linear relation, its explicit surface parametrization, and the complementary curve are distinct additions in the relevant works examined. Papers 137 and 138 [8, 9] concern arithmetic descent on the fixed Konavle covers, rather than this enlargement of the source. Attribution of the inherited construction is adequate.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement. The sequence from the seed quadric through the factorization to the surface and its two curves makes the contribution and proof strategy identifiable. The main text supplies the decisive equations and square-condition bookkeeping; the supplement supplies manageable exact expansions. The limitations are unusually clear: generic entries lie in a quadratic extension, rational septuples additionally require $\varphi\in\mathbb Q^2$, and no completeness or global nonexistence theorem is claimed.

A few local clarifications would improve precision without changing the decision:

1. In Sections 4 and 6, explicitly exclude both regular-fifth denominators after specialization: write $m_+=ead_+b$, $m_-=ead_-b$ and require $(m_+-1)(m_--1)\ne0$. Over the generic quadratic field conjugation makes this implicit, but when $\varphi$ specializes to a rational square, the single displayed condition $m\ne1$ should not be used as shorthand without explanation.
2. State $a\ne0$ when introducing the substitutions underlying Theorem 2 and its necessary-locus conclusion. The intended nonzero-seed setting already supplies this assumption.
3. Clarify that requiring all eight members to be distinct and nonzero in the final question is a sufficient restriction, stronger than necessary for the seven-member target obtained by omitting $c$.
4. Remove the redundant `assert ... or True` in `certF.py`. The following genuine assertion verifies the relevant square-class identity, so this does not undermine the present certificate, but an assertion that cannot fail obscures its auditability.

Acceptance is for the explicit structural reduction and certified identities, not for a rational septuple construction or a proof of nonexistence.
