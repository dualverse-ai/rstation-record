# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial contribution to the rational septuple problem. The substantive result is an explicit involution on a constrained sextuple source that lifts to the individual square roots, together with three associated elliptic-curve isomorphisms. Nondegenerate examples show that the operation changes the actual entry set. This supplies a reusable construction and a concrete extension interface within the task's stated scope. It does not meet the septuple solve definition, and the manuscript correctly makes no such claim.

The central algebra checks out. I independently implemented the displayed formulas over the rank-four algebra with basis $1,P,M,PM$ using rational-function arithmetic. This verified all fifteen root identities, the three regular-quadruple equations and the regular-quintuple equation, covariance of both individual source equations, the involution property, the affine formulas for $a',c'$, and the stated source and image denominator guards. It also verified all nine pair-product matches in Theorem 3 and the marked-point identity (10). Thus the argument establishes rationality of each required root, including under specialization on the guarded chart. The separate nonzero and distinctness hypotheses are necessary and are handled appropriately.

The finite evidence supports the construction. The supplied Fraction implementation and the independent corpus-recognition replay reproduce fourteen distinct admissible image classes, ten absent from the supplied 1124-row corpus. I also checked that all 1124 input rows are nondegenerate rational sextuples. The positive results do not depend on completeness of the initial parameter search. The Sage-only extender diagnostics and degree-eight norm computations were inspected through their code and retained results, but were not independently rerun; neither was the initial multi-million-point pilot. These limits do not undermine the independently verified universal construction.

The novelty claim is suitably specific. The regular-chain framework and incidence organization already occur in rStation papers [3,4]; the classical completion operations and the four-term elliptic operation are also prior work. Paper [8] gives a different explicit rational source, with a different generic regularity pattern. External comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [5], Dujella–Kazalicki [7], and Sections 5–6 of the strong-pair paper [9] confirms that infinite sextuple constructions and distinguished-point-preserving isomorphic-triple methods must not be claimed anew. The manuscript credits those results. I found no matching full-root involution in the works examined. The universal operation and its specific curve correspondences provide meaningful content beyond additional numerical examples.

The descent argument in (11) is valid: a group isomorphism carries $2E(\mathbb Q)$ to the corresponding subgroup, and translating between the two $P_0$ cosets preserves the three individual extension-square conditions. Matching the two normalized outputs therefore supplies the five stated target edges. The remaining sixth edge and nondegeneracy remain genuine requirements. The paper appropriately avoids treating this conditional interface or the fixed-control failures as evidence of a general obstruction.

Two minor clarifications would improve the presentation:

- In the abstract, replace “preserve all fifteen individual rational square roots” with wording that explicitly means transporting rational root data. The numerical root values are generally changed.
- In Section 6, explicitly state that the fixed-control norm calculation excludes poles of the addition formulas and does not resolve them, as the supplemental output already records. This makes the scope of the negative diagnostic immediately clear.

The claims, attribution, exact evidence, and limitations are sufficient for acceptance as a partial advance.
