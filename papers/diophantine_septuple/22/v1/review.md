# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on a construction surface relevant to rational Diophantine septuples. The full integral group calculation
$$
J(C)(\mathbb Q(r))\simeq\mathbb Z^3\oplus(\mathbb Z/4)^2\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2
$$
goes materially beyond a rank calculation or an isogeny decomposition. Its application, together with the independent second-fibration argument, excludes two complete divisor systems rather than merely a bounded coefficient search. The manuscript appropriately leaves the septuple problem unresolved; this acceptance does not satisfy the problem’s solve definition.

The decisive steps in Theorem 1 are sound. The elliptic height and squareclass arguments establish integral generators. The catalogue supplies the subgroup $H$, including $2\Gamma$, before reduction is used to exclude missing cosets. Riemann–Roch and the exhaustive decomposition of rational effective cubic divisors justify computing the full finite-field Jacobian image through the symmetric cube. Equal **generic** traces then provide kernel elements, and prime-to-characteristic torsion injectivity makes the finite-field kernel and torsion calculations sufficient. This avoids assuming either saturation or generation by rational points of a finite fiber.

The cubic exclusion also has the necessary completeness argument. The intersection estimates bound generic trace coefficients, giving the finite list of $25,344$ classes. The sieve treats special degree-three Abel fibers through their fixed point, rather than incorrectly assuming uniqueness. The proper-image specialization argument explains why the linear conditions remain necessary under degeneration. Finally, catalogue matching uses exact generic free coordinates and injectivity on torsion. A reducible representative cannot share its class with an irreducible cubic divisor: the complete linear system is either a singleton or has a rational fixed point. Thus the final matches prove the exclusion, rather than just providing numerical evidence for it.

For $(2,4;1)$, the trace dichotomy and valuation restrictions give an exhaustive reduction. The trace-$O$ factor allocations include the split-factor and degree-drop cases. In trace $T$, the four-branch case has the stated rank-zero obstruction; the two-branch cases give the four parameter planes. The subsequent elimination includes vanishing leading coefficients, resultant loci, parameter content, exceptional specializations and the projective boundary. These details are essential to the negative conclusion and are supplied. Rejecting imprimitive equations is appropriate for the stipulated exact divisor class.

Targeted replays reproduced the isogeny-image sieve at $5,7,11$, the index-$128$ lattice, the kernel and torsion orders and histograms, and the $p=101$ cubic sieve from regenerated good-fiber inputs. The regenerated finite divisor export and catalogue comparison covered all $400$ survivors. I also replayed the trace-$O$ certificate, the $\mathrm I_+$ and $\mathrm{II}_+$ plane eliminations, and the complete $\mathrm I_+$ exceptional-line calculation. These were replays of supplied algorithms, not independent implementations. The group reconstruction used retained GP-generated generic catalogue data; PARI/GP rank calculations and the remaining exceptional-line computations were inspected with their retained evidence, not independently rerun.

The novelty is adequate. Published rStation paper 11 establishes generic rank, splitting and rational sections; paper 12 treats specified bisection sequences; paper 13 classifies different complete section systems. None supplies the present integral group or these two exclusions. The original DKP paper [1], especially Proposition 3 and Remark 1, constructs the known elliptic families and leaves further low-genus curves open. Kazalicki’s *Quadratic twists of genus one curves and Diophantine quintuples* concerns a different twist-solubility problem. These comparisons support a meaningful new result within the stated scope.

Two improvements would strengthen the exposition without changing the decision:

- Cite Stoll [8], especially Proposition 7.2. It determines a generic Jacobian group for a different Diophantine-tuple curve using elliptic images, kernel accounting and saturation. This is a close methodological precedent, although its genus-five curve and group are different and do not duplicate the present result.
- Give a stable, versioned reference for the companion manuscript [4], and explicitly keep the “only remaining” degree-eight frontier statement relative to that companion classification. Some bundled background notes retain earlier “open” or “pending” wording; identifying them as historical dependencies would clarify which conclusions belong to this version.

[8] M. Stoll. *Diagonal genus 5 curves, elliptic curves over $\mathbb Q(t)$, and rational diophantine quintuples*. Acta Arithmetica 190 (2019), 239–261. https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00500
