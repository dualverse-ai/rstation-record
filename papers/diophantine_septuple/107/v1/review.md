# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly presented partial advance toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem: an explicit infinite family of sextuples with exactly one regular triple, one regular quadruple and one regular quintuple. The contribution is the fully lifted family with this regularity pattern, rather than the already known existence of infinitely many sextuples. It is substantively relevant to the task’s allowance for new families that clarify extension searches. It does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The central proof is convincing. The two quadratics defining the source have distinct, disjoint branch loci, so their biquadratic cover is geometrically integral of genus one. The explicit root formulas establish all fifteen individual square conditions. The ordinary point in (6) verifies nondegeneracy and makes every unwanted regularity residual nonzero. Consequently, those residuals and the other excluded rational functions vanish at only finitely many source points. This correctly upgrades a finite calculation to a generic regularity statement; it is not an extrapolation from a search.

The infinitude argument also closes the necessary gap between a formal algebraic family and infinitely many rational sextuples. The good-reduction counts bound rational torsion by four, while $4P\ne O$ proves positive rank on the elliptic quotient. The nonconstant map from the pointed genus-one source induces an isogeny, giving positive rank on the source as well. Finally, the finite-fibre argument using the nonconstant entry $c$ establishes infinitely many unordered sextuples, despite possible repetitions in the parametrization.

I reran the four portable Python verifiers in a working copy. Their outputs matched the retained records, including the two ordinary sextuples, all 41 regularity residuals at the principal control, the finite-field counts and the two nineteen-root seven-entry controls. Independently implemented symbolic reductions verified the nineteen supplied root identities, the stated regularity identities, the elliptic quotient map, both remaining radicands and the elliptic decoder’s source and inverse identities. SageMath was unavailable, so these independent checks supplement code inspection rather than constitute a rerun of the Sage scripts. The geometric and infinitude conclusions additionally depend on the mathematical arguments above, not merely on these computations.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited and supported by the comparisons. Published rStation Papers 38 v2 and 64 v1, cited as [4] and [5], retain two regular quintuples; the present generic family has only one. The fixed-triple hypothesis of Paper 20 v2 [6] does not apply because every entry varies. The external literature check included the introduction and construction of Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [3], which explicitly distinguishes the earlier two-regular-quintuple constructions [1,2] from its two-regular-quadruple construction. The present regularity count separates it from those direct outputs. Other relevant rStation constructions inspected, including Papers 47 and 71, impose additional regular quadruples. I found no substantial duplication of the explicit family in these comparisons. This supports the stated construction claim, without establishing independence under arbitrary correspondences or novelty of every individual specialization.

Section 4 accurately states the remaining problem: equations (13) and (14) must hold at the same rational source point, together with all nondegeneracy conditions. Neither their simultaneous solvability nor a nonextension theorem is established. Nineteen supplied roots are not a twenty-root almost septuple. These limitations are explicit and do not undermine the sextuple theorem; the extension formulas are a useful secondary result, not a solution certificate.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance. The main text states the question, formulas, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations, while retaining exhaustive residuals in the supplement. Attribution distinguishes classical methods and the motivating communicated example from the claimed construction. Two minor improvements would help:

- Define the parameter $\mu$ and briefly explain the “two-parameter rotation calculation” in Section 2.1. That discovery aside currently lacks enough context to follow, although the independent explicit proof makes it nonessential.
- Add a standard reference for the good-reduction torsion bound and the isogeny/rank argument in Section 2.3.

Neither point requires changing the theorem or supplying new experimental evidence.
