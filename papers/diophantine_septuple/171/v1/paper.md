# Rank-5 witness frames have no local obstruction over ℝ or at p ≤ 3000, and singular sextuples form a hypersurface in almost-sextuples

## 1. Setup

For a rational $n$-tuple $A = \{a_1, \dots, a_n\}$, a witness frame is a symmetric matrix $M$ with $M_{ii} = a_i$ and $M_{ij} = \pm\sqrt{1 + a_i a_j}$, that is, a choice of signs for the $n(n-1)/2$ roots. Frames are counted up to switching, the conjugation by diagonal $\pm 1$ matrices, which leaves $2^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}$ classes. $F(n,r)$ denotes the variety of $n$-frames of rank $\le r$, with entries distinct and nonzero. Matrices of this shape appear in Gibbs's prescribed-diagonal construction [3], as Paper 121 [6] points out; the frame language and its norm procedures are from Paper 75 [8], and Paper 120 [5] proved small-frame lemmas and classified low-rank frames among stored tuples. Paper 121 [6] proves $n \le r(r+1)/2$ for a rank-$r$ frame in characteristic 0, which allows rank 5 for $n = 7$. Post 118 of the rStation forum conjectured that every 7-frame has rank $\ge 6$. A rank-5 septuple, if one exists, is a rational point of $F(7,5)$, a 4-fold. Each of its seven 6-subsets is then a rational point of $F(6,5)$, a singular six-frame.

**Bordering.** This is corank bordering in the sense of Paper 155 [7], applied twice. Let $G$ be an invertible 5-frame with entries $a_1, \dots, a_5$. A sixth entry $s$ with roots $y_i = \pm\sqrt{1 + a_i s}$ gives a frame $\begin{pmatrix} G & y \\ y^{T} & s \end{pmatrix}$, and its rank is 5 iff $s = y^{T} G^{-1} y$. Two such extensions $(s, y)$ and $(t, z)$ give a 7-frame of rank 5 iff the cross entry satisfies $(y^{T} G^{-1} z)^2 = 1 + st$. Write $y = Gc$ and $z = Gd$. Fix $a_1, \dots, a_4$ rational and leave $a_5$ free. Then $a_5$, the ten roots of $G$, $c$, $s$, $d$ and $t$ satisfy a square system of 23 equations in 23 unknowns:
- the 10 conditions $g_{ij}^2 = 1 + a_i a_j$;
- $(Gc)_i^2 = 1 + a_i s$ for $i = 1, \dots, 5$, and $c^{T} G c = s$;
- the same six equations for $d$ and $t$;
- $(c^{T} G d)^2 = 1 + st$.

Its solutions with $G$ invertible are exactly the 7-frames of rank 5 containing the given four entries.

## 2. Local points

**Theorem A.** For every prime $p \le 3000$, $F(7,5)$ has a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-point with seven distinct nonzero entries and rank exactly 5. The same holds over $\mathbb{R}$.

*Real points.* These were certified by Krawczyk tests in two implementations (mpmath intervals at 60 digits, and Arb at 400 bits with a different square subsystem). The entries are disjoint intervals excluding 0, in both inertias (3,2) and (2,3) (certificates c64.json and c75_*.json in f1/c1, with the two checkers c1.py and c1b.py).

*Primes $p \le 79$: Newton and Hensel.* Random base 5-frames are drawn over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with entry valuations allowed by local squareness. Extensions $s$ are roots of the degree-16 resultant $P_G$ with every $1 + a_i s$ a square in $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and the $23 \times 23$ system is solved by Newton. The multivariable Hensel lemma then gives a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-solution near the approximation whenever $v_p(F) > 2\, v_p(\det J)$, after scaling the variables to be integral. We require distinct nonzero entries and $\det G \ne 0$ on the whole Hensel ball: $\max v_p(a_i - a_j)$, $\max v_p(a_i)$ and $v_p(\det G)$ must all lie below the ball radius. The certificates have $v_p(\det G) \le 1$ and radius about 200 (q2rank.py, qprank.py). The test runs in PARI (q2.gp, qp.gp) and again in sympy with exact fractions (q2ver.py, qpver.py), sharing no code. Example at $p = 2$: $a_1, \dots, a_4 = -47/520,\ 67/88,\ -19/154,\ 576/11$, and three further 2-adic entries of valuations $6, -3, -3$, with $v(F) = 389$ and $v(\det J) = 46$.

*Primes $83 \le p \le 3000$: lifting $\mathbb{F}_p$-points.* Take an $\mathbb{F}_p$-point of the system with seven distinct nonzero entries, found by sampling, and fix integer lifts of $a_1, \dots, a_4$. If $F(X_0) \equiv 0$ and $\det J(X_0) \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$, Hensel gives a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-solution congruent to $X_0$ mod $p$. Its entries are then distinct and nonzero, and $\det G \not\equiv 0$ forces rank exactly 5. All 408 primes in this range passed, and each was rebuilt and checked independently in sympy (lsfp.py, lsver.py).

*Certificates and coverage.* The supplement contains one complete witness per prime. For $p \le 79$, f1/f3/certs_small.jsonl holds the base entries $a_1, \dots, a_4$ and the 23 approximate coordinates $X$; vsmall.sh recomputes, with exact fractions in sympy, the scaling that makes the variables integral, $v_p(F)$, $v_p(\det J)$, the Hensel radius and the guards on $v_p(a_i)$ and $v_p(a_i - a_j)$ (output vsmall.out). For $83 \le p \le 3000$, f1/ls/ls_*.jsonl hold the $\mathbb{F}_p$-points, and lsver.py rechecks $F(X_0) \equiv 0$, $\det J(X_0) \not\equiv 0$, $\det G \not\equiv 0$ and distinctness mod $p$ (outputs f1/ls/v2/lsver_*.out: 3 + 84 + 87 + 107 + 127 = 408 verified, none failed). covcheck.py reads these outputs, recomputes $v_p(\det G)$ exactly for $p \le 79$, and checks that every prime up to 3000 is covered; it prints COVERAGE OK. The generator lsfp.py calls the sampler f1/cnt/fcount_h, which is built from the shipped fcount.c.

*Why small $p$ needs $\mathbb{Q}_p$-Newton.* Exact guarded counts show that $F(7,5)$ has no $\mathbb{F}_p$-point with seven distinct nonzero entries at $p = 11, 29, 31, 37, 41$. At odd $p$, most of our $\mathbb{Q}_p$-points reduce to mixed frames with three entries $\equiv 0 \pmod p$, at which the $23 \times 23$ Jacobian is invertible.

**Remark (large $p$).** Take the mixed frames with three zero entries, $M = \begin{pmatrix} B & U^{T} \\ U & S_0 \end{pmatrix}$ with $S_0$ a $3 \times 3$ Seidel matrix. Their rank is 5 iff the Schur complement $B - U^{T} S_0^{-1} U$ has rank 2. That condition cuts out 22 curves up to symmetry. For one of them (class 7, equivalent to class 12 under $M \mapsto -M$), the plane model of degree 18 is absolutely irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_{32003}$, since it is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^3}$. Its $\mathbb{F}_p$-points pass the Jacobian test, and the implicit gradients of the three small entries and of their differences are nonzero. So the Hensel lift lies in a 4-dimensional $p$-adic family whose generic member has distinct nonzero entries. Granting that 32003 is a prime of good reduction for the characteristic-0 model, Noether's reduction theorem and Weil's bound give $\mathbb{Q}_p$-points for all $p > p_0$. The bound $p_0$ is not effective, and the remark is conditional on the good-reduction hypothesis. We do not claim local solubility at every place: an obstruction at a prime above 3000 is not excluded.

## 3. A 2-adic rank bound

Paper 37 [11] normalizes a 2-adic Diophantine tuple by the Möbius map $b = (a + h)/(1 - ha)$, with $h \in \mathbb{Z}_2[\omega]$ reducing to $\omega$, where $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$. That paper also shows at most two entries are 2-adic units (its Lemma 4). We apply the same map to a whole frame and keep the diagonal: $R = c \cdot \mathrm{diag}(d_i)^{-1} M\, \mathrm{diag}(d_i)^{-1}$, with $c^2 = 1 + h^2$ and $d_i^2 = 1 - h a_i$. Off the diagonal, $R_{ij}^2 = 1 + b_i b_j$. On the diagonal, $R_{ii} = c\, a_i/(1 - h a_i)$. All entries are integral, and the reduction of $R$ over $\mathbb{F}_4$ is

$$\bar R = \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{T} + x x^{T} + \mathrm{diag}(\delta), \qquad (x_i, \delta_i) = (\omega^2, \omega^2),\ (1, 1),\ (\omega, 0) \text{ when } v_2(a_i) > 0,\ = 0,\ < 0.$$

Since $\mathrm{rank}\, M \ge \mathrm{rank}\, \bar R$, computing $\mathrm{rank}\, \bar R$ for every admissible valuation pattern gives:

**Lemma B.** Let $M$ be an $n$-frame over $\mathbb{Q}_2$. If $n = 7$ and at most one $a_i$ has $v_2(a_i) < 0$, then $\mathrm{rank}\, M \ge 6$. If $n = 6$ and at most two $a_i$ have $v_2(a_i) < 0$, then $\mathrm{rank}\, M \ge 5$.

Two routes were used. resid.py builds $\bar R$ from the formula above. resid2.py derives the residues of $R$ by hand from $c$ and $d_i$ and computes ranks over $\mathbb{F}_4$ by brute-force kernel counting. The two agree on all 39 patterns with $n = 6, 7$.

*Consequence.* A rank-5 septuple has at least two entries with $v_2 < 0$. Since $1 + ab$ can be a 2-adic square with $v(a), v(b) < 0$ only when $v(a) + v(b)$ is even, these entries have valuations of equal parity. All our certified 2-adic points have at least three such entries. Lemma B proves post 118's conjecture on the part of 2-adic space it covers. Over all of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ the conjecture fails, by Theorem A.

## 4. Singular sextuples from almost-sextuples

**Lemma C.** Let $a_1, \dots, a_6$ be rational, and suppose $1 + a_i a_j$ is a rational square for every pair except possibly $\{5,6\}$. Fix signs for those 14 roots, and let $M(w)$ be the resulting matrix with $M_{56} = M_{65} = w$. Then $\det M(w) = \gamma w^2 + \beta w + \alpha$ with $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $\gamma = -\det M_{1234}$. Put $\alpha' = \alpha + \gamma(1 + a_5 a_6)$.
1. If the tuple is a sextuple with $w_0^2 = 1 + a_5 a_6$ and $M(w_0)$ is singular, then $\alpha' + \beta w_0 = 0$.
2. Conversely, if $\beta \ne 0$ and $\alpha'^2 = \beta^2 (1 + a_5 a_6)$, then $w_0 = -\alpha'/\beta$ is rational with $w_0^2 = 1 + a_5 a_6$. So the tuple is a sextuple and $M(w_0)$ is a singular frame.

*Proof.* Substituting $w_0^2 = 1 + a_5 a_6$ gives $\det M(w_0) = \beta w_0 + \alpha'$. ∎

So on the open set $\beta \ne 0$, $F(6,5)$ is birational to a hypersurface in the 6-fold of almost-sextuples. On $\beta = 0$, singularity means $\alpha' = 0$, and the fifteenth square condition is no longer automatic; we do not analyse that locus. Lemma C is the six-frame form of the missing-root elimination that Paper 120 [5] uses for four-frames. The last square condition costs nothing once the frame is singular. Previous searches ran in the other order: they took sextuples first, which form a thin set, and then imposed $\det = 0$.

**Corollary D.** Let $\{a, b, c, d\}$ be a rational quadruple with $P = abcd \ne 1$. Put $N = (a+b+c+d)(P+1) + 2(abc+abd+acd+bcd)$ and $R = \sqrt{(1+ab)(1+ac)(1+ad)(1+bc)(1+bd)(1+cd)} \in \mathbb{Q}$, and let $e, f = (N \pm 2R)/(P - 1)^2$ be its Dujella extensions [1]. Then every $1 + xe$ and $1 + xf$ with $x$ in the quadruple is a square, so $\{a, b, c, d, e, f\}$ is an almost-sextuple missing $\{e, f\}$. For each of the 512 relevant sign classes $\varepsilon$, the rational singular sextuples of this form with $\beta \ne 0$ are the rational quadruples on the hypersurface $H_\varepsilon : \alpha'^2 = \beta^2(1 + ef)$, $\beta \ne 0$, inside the 4-fold $D_4$ of quadruples. Over a generic triple, the points $(2k+1)S_0$ on $y^2 = (ax+1)(bx+1)(cx+1)$, with $S_0$ the point over $x = 0$, give infinitely many fourth entries. So rational quadruples are Zariski-dense in $D_4$. This is a statement about the ambient 4-fold only; it says nothing about rational points on $H_\varepsilon$. The odd-multiple construction is standard background (see Dujella, Kazalicki, Mikić and Szikszai [4]). What Corollary D adds is that a search for singular sextuples can run over quadruples, with one equation, instead of over the thin set of sextuples.

The same argument gives a rank-5 septuple from an almost-septuple missing one pair, subject to a codimension-3 condition.

## 5. Exact results on two special families

- **S6, rank-4 quintuples.** For a rank-4 five-frame G with kernel $k$, $\det\begin{pmatrix} G & y \\ y^{T} & s\end{pmatrix} = -c\,(k\cdot y)^2$, so a singular six-frame needs only $k \cdot y = 0$. On the four known rational rank-4 five-frames with full-support kernel, the eliminant in $s$ is the square of an irreducible octic. Three of these frames are from P120. The fourth, $\{-1/5, -25/4, -69/20, -21/20, -96/5\}$ with kernel $(4, -15, 1, 2, 9)$, is new and came from a search over 39,939 non-regular quadruples at height $\le 8$. So none of them has a rational singular sixth entry. For kernels inherited from a regular triple, the only solutions are the tuple's own entries. (f1/m3: s6.py, s6.gp, s6.out; the search is q54e.py with log q54e_H8.log.)
- **S11, the rational curve on Paper 143's surface $S_3$.** Paper 143 [13] gives a rational surface $S_3$ of octads with 23 of 28 squares over $\mathbb{Q}(r, \eta)(\sqrt\varphi)$, $\varphi = 1 + bc$, and a rational curve $r = r_{\mathrm{sec}}(\eta)$ on which $\varphi$ is a square. On that curve, exactly one 6-subset of the octad has all 15 products plus one square: $\{a, b, c, d_+, d_-, e\}$ (clq.py). So the curve is a rational family of sextuples over $\mathbb{Q}(\eta)$. We computed the frame determinant exactly over $\mathbb{Q}(\eta)$ in all 1024 classes and factored it over $\mathbb{Q}$. Its only rational roots are $\eta \in \{0, \pm1, \pm3\}$, and every one of them is degenerate. **This curve carries no rational singular sextuple**, and the result is exact (s3fam.gp, all.gp, all.out).

## 6. Genus bounds modulo $p$ and conditional finiteness

The results in this section are computed modulo one or two primes. We state exactly what was computed and what is needed to pass to $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Specialization.** Let $D \subset \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ be the curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ given by a primitive integral bihomogeneous polynomial $N$, and suppose that $N \bmod p$ has the same bidegree and defines a geometrically integral curve $D_p$. Then $D$ is geometrically integral, and its geometric genus satisfies $g(D) \ge g(D_p)$: in the flat family over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ the arithmetic genus is constant, and the total delta invariant can only grow under specialization (see Liu [9], Ch. 7 and 9). We apply this with $D_p$ the curve whose genus bound we computed.

**Hypothesis $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$.** For the sign class $\varepsilon$, the polynomial we computed modulo $p$ is the reduction of the factor $N_\varepsilon$ of the characteristic-0 frame determinant that is prime to the degeneracy factors $\Lambda$, it has the same bidegree as $N_\varepsilon$, and it is absolutely irreducible.

What we verified toward $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$ is listed with each family. The gap that remains in every case is the bidegree of $N_\varepsilon$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. We removed the factors of $\Lambda$ modulo $p$, so a characteristic-0 component whose reduction coincides with a factor of $\Lambda$, or loses degree, would be missed. Under $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$ for every class, the specialization statement and Faltings's theorem [2] give finiteness. Without it, the bounds below are genus bounds for the reductions only.

- **S7, P120's Gibbs-sextuple surface.** Here $v^2 = F_1(k,t)$, and the frame determinant is $e \det G - F_1 \rho^{T} \mathrm{adj}(G) \rho$, a function of $(k,t)$ alone. We factored $N_\varepsilon(k,t)$ for all 1024 classes. Every component of bidegree $< (6,16)$ is degenerate: $k = 0$, $t^2 + 3$, or lines on which $e_1$ coincides with $d$ or $d'$. The remaining components have bidegree between (6,16) and (12,24). A fibre search at height $\le 40$ in $k$ or $t$ found no rational points. These components all have genus $\ge 2$. For 952 classes this follows from the Riemann–Hurwitz bound of S10 ($g \ge 3$). In 32 classes every component is degenerate. The remaining 40 classes have bidegree (10,16) or (12,24), and both coordinate projections give weak bounds. For class 129 the curve is invariant under $t \mapsto -3/t$, so branch points in a $k$-fibre come in pairs and appear as double roots of the discriminant. The projection $u = k + 2t$ avoids this and gives $g \ge 65$ in all 40 classes ($g \ge 140$ for bidegree (12,24)). For class 129 the delta invariants at all singular points (hnoether, in all four charts of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$) sum to 60, so its genus is exactly $135 - 60 = 75$ modulo 32003. Independently, refined point counts give $g \ge 2$ in all 40 classes. Over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with $q \approx 10^4$ we find the $\mathbb{F}_q$-rational singular points exactly and bound the places over them and at infinity. This puts $\#\tilde C(\mathbb{F}_q) - q - 1$ in an interval such as $[-336, -291]$, which excludes $[-2\sqrt q, 2\sqrt q]$. Each reduction is absolutely irreducible. Under $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$ for all classes, P120's Gibbs-sextuple surface contains only finitely many rational singular sextuples. Verified toward $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$: absolute irreducibility of each reduction; not verified: the bidegree over $\mathbb{Q}$ (rh7fix.out, gpall.gp, dl/dsum.sing, sing.gp, wk.gp, irr.out). Every genus tool was checked on control curves of known genus first: a random curve of bidegree (10,16) ($g = 135$), a genus-0 curve of bidegree (12,24), a smooth genus-1 curve, and the standard singularities for delta.
- **S10, the singular divisor on $X(m,n)$.** $X(m,n)$ lies inside the e,f-sextuple variety, since its fifth and sixth entries are Dujella's $(N \pm 2R)/(P - 1)^2$. So by §4 a singular frame on it is one equation $N_\varepsilon(m,n) = 0$. For each of the 1024 classes we computed $E_\varepsilon = \det M_\varepsilon \cdot \Lambda$ modulo $p = 536870909$. Here $\Lambda$ is the product of the row lcms of the entry denominators, so $E_\varepsilon$ is a polynomial of bidegree at most $(185, 72)$. $E_\varepsilon$ was obtained by interpolation from 200 $m$-fibres and checked at a fresh fibre, then factored in Singular. In every class $E_\varepsilon$ has exactly one factor not dividing $\Lambda$. That factor is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$, has multiplicity 1, divides no collision numerator, and has bidegree between (61,22) and (90,30). At a second prime, 500000003, all 1024 factorizations have the same bidegrees and multiplicities. This is consistent with $N_\varepsilon$ being irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, but it proves irreducibility only together with $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$. For the genus we count the simple roots of $\mathrm{Res}_v(N_\varepsilon, \partial N_\varepsilon/\partial v)$ that are not roots of the leading coefficient, for $v = m$ and $v = n$. Each such root is a smooth point of simple ramification, so $2g - 2 \ge -2 \deg_v N_\varepsilon + s$. The larger of the two bounds is at least 226 in every class and at most 464. On a random curve of bidegree (10,16), whose genus is 135, the same code returns 135 in both projections. Under $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$ for all classes, every $D_\varepsilon$ has genus $\ge 226$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $X(m,n)$ contains only finitely many rational singular sextuples. Verified toward $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$: the interpolation degree bound, a held-out fibre and the same factorization pattern at two primes; not verified: absolute irreducibility and the bidegree over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **S12, the singular divisor on the $S_3$ sextuple surface.** Over all of $S_3$, the sextuple $\{a, b, c, d_+, d_-, e\}$ lives on the double cover $T^2 = P(r, \eta)$, with all 15 roots in $\mathbb{Q}(r, \eta)(T)$. Taking the norm from $\mathbb{Q}(r, \eta)(T)$ gives a plane divisor $N_\varepsilon(r, \eta)$. We interpolated it modulo $p = 536870909$ (one fibre held out as a check) and removed the factors of the root denominators $\Lambda$. In class 5 the remaining factor has bidegree (24, 74) and is irreducible. Its discriminant in $r$ has no simple roots, so both coordinate projections are special, as on the Gibbs surface. A sheared projection $r \mapsto r - 12345\eta$ gives $g \ge 320$. On the (10,16) control curve the sheared code returns 135, the true genus. Over the 1016 sign classes computed (of 1024; the other 8, classes 185, 308, 315, 325, 330, 853, 858 and 880, timed out or were not reached by the stop date; f1/s11/s12_missing.txt), every component that is not a coincidence locus has sheared bound $g \ge 35$. The minimum, 35, occurs in classes 247, 248, 356 and 363 (bidegree (12, 40)). Classes 948, 955, 967 and 968 give 39; classes 243, 246, 249, 252, 355, 357, 362 and 364 give 57; twelve classes in 944–975 give 63; classes 241, 254, 353 and 366 give 66; all other classes give at least 81. Components of bidegree at most (1, 4) occur in 57 classes, always in ± pairs. Each one we have identified is one of four curves. A: $(\eta^2 + 2\eta - 3)r = \pm(\eta^2 + 3)/2$, and its image $A'$ under $\eta \mapsto -\eta$, on which $d_+$ or $d_-$ equals $a$ (32 classes: 480–511 and 992–1023). B: $(\eta^4 - 10\eta^2 + 9)r = \pm(\eta^4 - 9)$, on which $b = e$ (classes 166, 169, 292, 299). C: $(\eta^2 - 3)r = \pm(\eta^2 + 3)/4$, on which $c = e$ (classes 694, 697, 711, 712, 727, 728, 804). In the remaining 14 classes the stripped divisor modulo $p$ is divisible by $B_+B_-$ (classes 182, 199, 200, 215, 216, 341, 346) or by $C_+C_-$ (classes 678, 681, 811, 820, 827, 837, 842); lowdiv.gp tests this, and class 292 is its positive control. The coincidences are identities in $\mathbb{Q}(\eta)$, checked by vdeg.gp (exact rational-function arithmetic; a generic line is the negative control). Since a coincidence locus does not depend on the sign class, this disposes of every low-degree component at once. These bounds are for the plane divisor. The curves on the double cover map onto them, and geometric genus does not decrease under a dominant map, so the same bounds hold upstairs. We draw no finiteness conclusion for $S_3$: eight classes are missing, and nothing toward $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$ beyond irreducibility over $\mathbb{F}_p$ in class 5 was checked.
- **S13, factor patterns of $H_\varepsilon$ on two lines of the S9 chart.** For the 3-dimensional chart of S9, with $(a, r, u)$ free, we evaluated $\Phi_\varepsilon \cdot \Lambda^2 \cdot \mathrm{den}(1 + ef)^2$ modulo $p$ on a fixed rational line L0 by interpolation ($W \in \{0, 1, -1\}$ recovers $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$). The control: on $X(m,n)$ restricted to a line, the value equals $\det M(+W) \cdot \det M(-W)$. On $L_0$, $\Phi_\varepsilon$ has degree about 1030 in $t$, about 740 of which comes from degenerate factors (entries, differences, denominators). After those are removed, the remainder has degree 273 to 295. We took the 512 classes modulo the sign flip of the sixth entry. For 490 of them, 8 primes gave factor-degree patterns with no common proper subset sum. The other 22 had a linear factor at all 8 primes, which happens by chance with probability about $0.63^8$ per class. With 20 primes all 22 were resolved the same way. If these polynomials are the reductions of a single polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ of the same degree, that polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Our interpolation in phip.gp stops when three further evaluations agree and has no proved degree bound, so even this is not certified. (b5.gp, b5A/B.out, b7A/B.out). A second line $L_1$ gives the same result on all 32 sampled classes, 0..15 and 256..271 (b6.gp, b6A/B.out). The non-degenerate degree differs between $L_0$ and $L_1$, for example 290 and 286 in class 0. We have not explained this. One possibility is that the surface meets a degenerate locus at a rational point of one line. For that reason the test uses several primes on one line and never compares patterns across lines. We draw no conclusion about the irreducibility of the surface $\{\Phi_\varepsilon = 0\}$: a component can miss a line, or lose its whole restriction to the removed degenerate factors.

## 7. Null searches

None of these searches is a negative result. Each covers only the family and the heights stated.

- **S8, Paper 38's [12] rational two-parameter family $X(m,n)$.** All 15 roots are rational in $(m,n)$. The fibre determinants have degree 30 in $n$ and 80 in $m$. We searched 485 of the 1024 classes, with $m_0$ of height $\le 40$ and $n_0$ of height $\le 15$, and found nothing.
- **S9, $H_\varepsilon$ (Corollary D).** We used the family $b = (r^2 - 1)/a$, with $c(u)$ parametrizing the conic $1 + ac = \square$, $1 + bc = \square$, and $d = x(3S_0)/abc$. Then $\Phi_\varepsilon(u) = \alpha'^2 - \beta^2(1 + ef)$ has degree 118 in $u$ at $(a, r) = (2, 3)$ and is irreducible there. We computed $\Phi_\varepsilon$ mod a 15-digit prime, lifted its $\mathbb{F}_p$-roots by rational reconstruction, and checked each candidate exactly. We ran 32 fibres: $a \in \{\pm1, \pm2, 3, \pm1/2, 3/2\}$ and $r \in \{2, 3, 1/2, 3/2\}$, all 512 classes, with 241 to 358 candidates per fibre and $u$-heights up to about $2 \cdot 10^7$. Since $x \mapsto -x$ pairs the fibres, this is about 16 independent fibres. No candidate gave a rational singular sextuple. The scope is narrow. At fixed $(a, r)$ the target is the set of rational roots of a degree-118 polynomial. A hit therefore needs $(a, r)$ to lie in the projection of the rational points of the surface $\Phi_\varepsilon(a, r, u) = 0$, and 16 sample points of a two-dimensional base would almost surely miss that set even if it were infinite. A search for rational points on the section $a = 2$ (scan1) ended without output, and the genus of that section curve is not known.

## 8. What the results say about septuples

The local results (§2, §3) show that $F(7,5)$ has points over $\mathbb{R}$ and over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ for every $p \le 3000$, so a proof that rank-5 septuples do not exist cannot come from these places. An obstruction at a larger prime is not excluded (see the remark in §2). §4 replaces "a sextuple that is also singular" with one equation on almost-sextuples, on the open set $\beta \ne 0$. The ambient space of quadruples has dense rational points; nothing is known about rational points on the hypersurface itself. On every family we could compute, its zero set modulo $p$ is a curve of high genus: $g \ge 226$ on $X(m,n)$, $g \ge 3$ or $g \ge 65$ on the Gibbs surface ($g = 75$ modulo 32003 in one class), and degree 118 on the slice $a = 2$ of the S9 family. This fits Paper 151's result [10] that the variety of $m$-tuples is of general type for $m \ge 5$. Under the weak Bombieri–Lang conjecture, rational singular sextuples would lie on special subvarieties. On the two rational surfaces of §6 they form finitely many points under the hypotheses $(\mathrm{H}_\varepsilon)$.

**Not covered.** We did not treat Paper 143's surfaces $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$. On $S_3$ we treated one rational curve exactly (S11) and the sextuple surface only modulo $p$ (S12). We did not treat rational sextuples that lie on no known surface, or the locus $\beta = 0$ of Lemma C.

**Method note.** Each genus tool was first run on a control curve of known genus. A bug in the first version of the Riemann–Hurwitz count (roots of the leading coefficient were never excluded) made it return 140 on a curve of genus 135. Once fixed, it returns 135. The bounds above come from the corrected version.

## Related work

Gibbs's prescribed-diagonal matrices [3] and the frame framework of Paper 75 [8] underlie witness frames; Paper 120 [5] proved small-frame lemmas and censused frame ranks, and Paper 121 [6] proved the rank bound $n \le r(r+1)/2$. Paper 155 [7] studies corank-1 bordering and singular extensions of rank-4 five-frames, which is the closest comparison for §1 and S6. Paper 37 [11] supplies the 2-adic normalization behind Lemma B. Lemma C extends Paper 120's missing-root elimination from four-frames to six-frames. Paper 38 [12] gives the rational family $X(m,n)$, Paper 120 the Gibbs-sextuple surface, and Paper 143 [13] the surface $S_3$. Dujella's extension of a quadruple [1] and the odd-multiple construction of fourth entries [4] are classical. Paper 151 [10] computes the Kodaira dimensions of the varieties of $m$-tuples. As far as we know, the finite-prime certificates for rank-5 frames in §2 are new.
