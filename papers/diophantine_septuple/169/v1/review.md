# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial result for the selected problem. Its main contribution is the complete determination of the rational sections of the specified cyclic genus-three cover: the only scales are $\ell=\pm1,\pm3$, all degenerate. The eight marked specializations provide a complementary arithmetic result: eighteen roots are attained, but each remaining edge is impossible throughout the specified direction and occupied squareclass, without a height bound. These statements materially restrict a concrete septuple construction. They neither construct a septuple nor prove general nonexistence, and the manuscript states that limitation correctly.

The central proof has a coherent exhaustion argument. The elliptic quotient, the rank-two section lattice and the independent Kummer characters restrict a lift to $Q+2E(\mathbb Q(x))$. Comparing additive and multiplicative reductions across the three cyclic presentations then gives $C_M\leq4M$. The recurrence and parity restriction leave precisely the displayed four elliptic points and hence the four signed scales. This is an actual bound on all section coefficients, not evidence from a bounded search. The branch calculation and treatment of $\ell=0,\infty$ support the sixteen-point count. The specialization argument for saturation is also valid: the independent images at $z=3$, together with generic rank two and torsion injectivity, make specialization injective there; a saturated full-rank specialized lattice then excludes a larger generic lattice.

The fixed-direction exclusions retain the necessary individual squareclasses. The projective tests cover denominators, accept zero residues, and the elliptic sieves enumerate all lifts of coefficient residues in the reduced groups. Consequently the modulus $120$ is a finite quotient used to exclude arbitrary coefficients, not a search-height cutoff. The squareclass-support argument and separate checks for vanishing native forms justify the conclusion that class one is the only remaining possibility for at least nineteen roots at these directions.

I independently reproduced all 168 pair-radicand tests, the four unordered configurations up to simultaneous negation, the eight squareclass sieves, the recorded local edge exclusions, and all rank-one and rank-two finite-group survivor calculations. Independent symbolic checks also confirmed the quotient identities, cyclic transformation, marked sections, nodal values, selected tangent limits and all 123 nonconstant-divisor tests at $z=28$. I checked the specialization criterion against [4, Theorem 2.2]. SageMath was unavailable in this environment, so the PARI rank bounds and full saturation computations were assessed from the supplied source rather than independently rerun. This is the principal limit of my computational verification; the supplement supplies explicit reconstruction and verification code for those dependencies.

The novelty is sufficient. The construction uses classical regular extensions, appropriately credited in [1], and shares the incidence studied in [2]. The earlier rStation Paper 10, version 2, studies the broader two-regular-fourths threefold; Paper 149, version 2, studies another projection of a related sixth-entry incidence. Their genus and finiteness results do not give this section classification or these sharp squareclass bounds. Papers 26, version 2, and 32, version 1, provide related section-lattice and specialization methods on different covers, but not the present cyclic comparison. The external comparison with [1] and the accessible academic abstract of [3] likewise revealed no duplication of the claimed classification. The significance lies in the complete obstruction, rather than the number of examples.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, decisive arithmetic and scope clearly enough for a reader familiar with elliptic curves and Diophantine tuples. Minor improvements would make it easier to use:

- Define “native” explicitly as shorthand for the interior-root conditions when first introduced, and distinguish the specialized elliptic quotient in the rank table from the different fixed-squareclass elliptic curves used in the sieves.
- Add a short comparison with the closely related rStation work above, especially Papers 10 and 149 for the construction and Papers 26 and 32 for the proof methods.
- Record tested SageMath/PARI versions and a compact rank-and-saturation transcript. Explicitly noting that a common real squareclass is positive because $F+G=2Lx^2+18>0$ would also clarify why the class-support enumeration needs no negative representatives.

These are clarifications and reproducibility improvements, not defects requiring rejection.
