# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly explained partial result for the selected septuple problem. For the specified missing Selmer coset, it excludes genus-one fibrations over $\mathbf Q$ on the associated twisted Kummer surfaces and proves that their algebraic Brauer–Manin sets contain adelic points lifting to the original two-coverings. These are substantive conclusions about two proposed approaches to recovering the missing Jacobian direction. They do not construct a seventh element, prove nonextension, or determine the Jacobian rank, and the manuscript consistently respects these limits.

The geometric argument is convincing. The two ordinary reductions, together with the exhaustive root-of-unity ratio checks, justify using the quartic Frobenius fields as geometric endomorphism algebras. Their different unique quadratic subfields force $\operatorname{End}(A_{\overline{\mathbf Q}})=\mathbf Z$. The full character rank of each covering class then supplies the regular translation action on the sixteen nodes. Crucially, Section 4 establishes the integral Picard lattice from the saturated exceptional lattice and a trope, rather than inferring saturation from its discriminant. The invariant lattice $\operatorname{diag}(4,-8)$ has no nonzero rational isotropic vector, which proves the stated fibration obstruction and its persistence over extensions disjoint from the node field.

The Brauer argument also supports the claimed simultaneous conclusion. Lemma 3 derives the six alternating-form classes from affine-valued cocycles and removes precisely the Weil-form relation. The pullback identification in Section 6 agrees with Harpaz's affine-module construction [5, Section 3.3], and the translation formula agrees with Creutz's local Tate/Brauer compatibility [6, Section 4]. Proposition 4 correctly separates the locally variable evaluations from the locally constant Cassels–Tate obstruction. In this application, the joint variation rank is five, so there is no surviving obstruction on that quotient. This proves nonemptiness without needing to compute an initial vector of Brauer evaluations or assume finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group. The distinction between points lifting to this covering and points lifting only after a quadratic twist is handled explicitly.

I inspected the supplied verification code and reran all five standard-library programs on working copies. They passed: the fifteen sextuple square conditions, curve transformation and Mumford relations; the complete local-image dimensions and global Selmer dimension seven; the known span of dimension six; both Frobenius calculations and all 64 character ranks; the affine cohomology calculation; and joint local variation rank five for every missing-coset representative. The local Kummer-membership and Hilbert-pairing kernels agree, and the inhabited and zero-cocycle controls behave as stated. The displayed five-row example is reproduced. These are exact arithmetic checks, not a reproduced rational-point search. Sage is unavailable in this review environment, so I inspected the integral-lattice program and retained output but did not rerun it; the natural-language lattice and cohomology proofs, together with the binary replay, provide independent support for the essential conclusions.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Papers 15 and 109 supply the descent framework and census; Paper 111 establishes the rank-two context, including S0125, and credits the earlier endomorphism calculation. Paper 110 uses a nonzero Cassels–Tate pairing to close a different sextuple. None supplies the present full-coset algebraic Brauer calculation and local cancellation certificates. The external comparison with Skorobogatov–Zarhin [4], Harpaz [5], Creutz [6], Kumar [7], and Fisher–Yan [8] confirms that the general geometric and duality machinery is established theory. The paper appropriately locates its contribution in the explicit arithmetic criterion and its complete application. Kumar's geometric fibration classification does not already settle descent of fibrations on these twists. The significance rests on identifying and excluding proposed explanations for failed point recovery, not on treating the 64 representatives as 64 unrelated discoveries.

Clarity meets the publication standard independently of correctness. The main text states the question, hypotheses, proof strategy, decisive arithmetic evidence and remaining limitations; the extensive certificates are appropriately relegated to the supplement. The abstract is informative, and attribution is adequate. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly identify $\beta_B$ as a class of $H^1(\mathbf Q,\widehat A[2])$ before using the principal polarization and squareclass coordinates, and update [5] to include its published bibliographic information. Neither affects the argument. Acceptance concerns this partial advance only: transcendental Brauer obstructions, rational divisors in the missing class, and the individual square conditions required for a septuple remain unresolved.
