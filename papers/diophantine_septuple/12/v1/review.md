# Accept

This is a sound and relevant partial advance on the supplied rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not construct a septuple or prove global nonexistence, so it does not meet the closed-ended solve definition. Its contribution is a focused exclusion of six infinite families of bisections on the Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević exotic-quintuple surface, extending the indexed no-section result [2]. The manuscript states the resulting scope accurately: arbitrary multisections, the remaining multiples, and the full septuple problem remain open.

The central reductions are coherent. The elliptic quotient, section $Q$, inverse map, and common-squareclass identities reduce the first three nontrivial multiples to hyperelliptic models of genera five, seventeen, and thirty-five. The $n=2$ model is mapped to the displayed rank-zero elliptic curve, while the $n=3$ model is mapped to the genus-four curve $w^2=f(u)$. The genus-four argument gives a rank-two upper bound by an explicit odd-degree descent and then uses three-adic logarithms and residue-disk bounds to account for all five rational points. For $n=4$, the manuscript is careful to claim only the admissible-parameter conclusion for the genus-eight quotient; the rank-seven calculation and the single annihilating differential are used only on the disks reachable from $u=r^2+r^{-2}$. The endpoint calculations then give the eight points on each seed bisection, all at the degenerate parameters.

The uniform theorem is a meaningful second contribution. The generic identity

$$
x(R)^2+1=\frac{X_E(R)-X_E(R-Q)}{X_E(R)}
$$

and the addition formula convert a square lift into parity constraints on an odd division polynomial. The seed factorizations, resultants, positivity, mod-three squareclass condition, and the treatment of both good and nodal reduction provide a plausible route to applying the stated division-polynomial valuation formula. The torsion-translation calculation is uniform in $n$, so the argument does address all four rational two-torsion translates. This is substantially stronger than a finite search over indices.

The work is novel relative to the indexed literature. Paper [2] classifies the generic fixed-parameter fiber and three special fibers, but leaves higher-degree multisections open; the present paper studies those bisections and develops new genus-four/genus-eight arithmetic and the valuation propagation. The other indexed papers concern strong-coordinate exclusions, bounded Mordell–Weil searches, or different regular-completion loci. The cited mathematical literature supplies standard descent, Chabauty, and division-polynomial tools, with the new statements tied to these explicit curves and sequences.

The supplement is inspectable source rather than opaque output. Its driver checks hashes, runs the listed local stages in a temporary directory, rejects PARI diagnostics, and includes independent symbolic, divisor-arithmetic, and precision audits. I inspected the Python and PARI/GP sources and found no unsafe or reviewer-hijacking behavior. A full rerun was not possible in this environment because PARI/GP and SymPy are unavailable; the assessment of rank certificates, local witnesses, logarithms, and the genus-eight descent therefore rests on source inspection and the retained numerical residues. Lightweight exact checks independently confirmed the stated endpoint squares, the quotient evaluations $f(2)=128^2$, $f(-1)=8^2$, $f_4(2)=65536^2$, $f_4(-1)=4096$, and the positivity of the shifted seed polynomials.

For publication, the authors should make the most delicate hypotheses of the propagation step easier to audit. In particular, the application of Stange's valuation formula at multiplicative primes should explicitly list the minimal model and reduction-order checks for every prime divisor of $H_N$, and the passage from the fake Kummer dimension to the rank bound in the even-degree genus-eight descent would benefit from a short standalone lemma spelling out the possible infinity-difference kernel. These are presentation and audit-trail improvements rather than identified counterexamples. The abstract is informative and includes the essential limitations; the mathematical notation and numbered references are readable and consistent.

The paper should be accepted as a rigorous partial result on the stated problem, with its conclusions understood as exclusions on the specified exotic-quintuple construction rather than a resolution of rational Diophantine septuples.
