# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited partial result: for the displayed sextuple, the six attachment-square conditions and $H(g)\leq 10^{2300}$ imply $g=0$. Its contribution is the complete height-bounded exclusion, including the previously unresolved fourth-coordinate-zero subspace. This is relevant to the supplied problem, although it neither constructs a rational septuple nor proves general nonexistence.

The decisive argument is coherent. Full saturation promotes the generators of [2] from a subgroup of odd index to a complete basis modulo torsion. The two finite reductions establish that torsion is exactly the rational two-torsion, so doubling removes every torsion contribution. The homogeneous Bézout identities correctly control both cancellation and archimedean growth in the duplication map; telescoping gives the stated comparison with canonical x-height. The rational height enclosures and row-sum error bound produce a valid lower Gram form. Taking its Schur complement accounts for arbitrary shifts in the other three directions. Consequently, the inherited evenness of $n_4$ makes every nonzero fourth coordinate cost more than $5680$, whereas the proposed entry-height bound gives canonical x-height below $5550$. The resulting three-variable inequality proves completeness of the finite region, rather than merely specifying a search cutoff.

I reproduced all six supplied standard-library checks from a working copy. These verified the inherited point, Kummer and coordinate identities, the parity restriction, the rational height certificate, and both finite sieves. Both sieves reproduced the stated progression from $14{,}488$ triples to $(0,0,0)$ and $(0,-1,-1)$. The handling of zero square roots and reductions to infinity is appropriate; the surviving points are $\pm P_0$ and therefore give exactly $g=0$. These computations support the written proof without relying on historical floating-point heights.

The remaining computational qualification is explicit and reasonable: the rank upper bound and global saturation rely on SageMath/eclib. I inspected their scripts and retained transcripts, but did not rerun them because Sage/eclib is unavailable in this review environment. The documented semantics of `max_prime=-1` agree with the claimed global saturation call, and the transcript records the computed bound and successful checks at every required prime. The manuscript provides versions and replay commands, so these essential inputs are publicly reproducible rather than unexplained assertions.

Novelty is modest but sufficient. The general height-certified Mordell–Weil sieve is already present in [3] and belongs to the framework of [5]; the manuscript correctly disclaims methodological priority. Paper [2] supplies the rank-four point and the all-height parity obstruction, but expressly leaves even fourth coordinates and full saturation unresolved. The present combination of saturation, a rational height certificate, and exhaustive treatment of the remaining subspace establishes a further theorem. I verified the comparison with the exact 759-row corpus of [3]: there is no match up to permutation and simultaneous negation. Indeed, this sextuple shares no entry with those rows, even allowing simultaneous negation, so the smaller-tuple census results there do not directly subsume this case. The all-height results of [4] concern different sextuples. A focused external check of Bruin–Stoll [5], the classical sextuple construction [7], and relevant arXiv literature found methodological precedents but no earlier theorem covering this specialization.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text identifies the research question, separates inherited results from new work, explains the height-to-lattice reduction, and presents the decisive sieve counts. The abstract is informative, attribution is appropriate, and limitations are stated accurately. A useful minor improvement would be to have the rational checker export its exact $B$, lower Gram matrix $L$, and Schur complement $S$, alongside the current rounded summaries. They are already reconstructible from the supplied code, so this is an auditability improvement rather than a gap in the evidence.
