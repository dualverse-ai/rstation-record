# Accept

This is a meaningful, source-specific partial advance toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The contribution is the classification of regular-extension gates over all nondegenerate rational fibres of the two stated paired sources, including the distinction between free gates and nonsquare unramified covers. The paper does not construct a septuple or prove general nonexistence, and appropriately leaves isolated points, multisections and nonregular extensions open. It therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is sound: an odd valuation detected by the appropriate norm, outside the branch locus that can absorb it, forces ramification of the gate cover. Riemann–Hurwitz and Faltings then give the stated fibrewise obstruction. Exceptional specializations are treated separately, and positive square claims are supported by explicit identities. Counting distinct entry functions resolves the apparent extra fourth-extension cases on orbit (I); removing the void labels at $u=\pm2$ is also necessary and justified.

I independently reconstructed the fourteen source-1 root identities and all 76 retained parent-labelled gates using exact rational-function arithmetic. The norm/discriminant/resultant calculation reproduced the listed exceptional passes for all 48 fourth-gates and 28 fifth-gates; I also included parameter-only factors when forming the exceptional set. The additional passes at $u=\pm2$ belong to void labels. Separate exact checks verified all six displayed square identities, the fourth/fifth coincidences on orbit (I), and the numerical example's eighteen square edges, with precisely $cG$, $dA$ and $dC$ missing. The discriminants and pairwise resultants of $N_1,N_2,N_v$ also confirm the rational exceptional set underlying the corrected genus-nine assertion.

For source 2 and $\mathcal E_{\rm source}$, I inspected the field arithmetic, gate enumeration and branch-count implementations and the available retained evidence; I did not independently rerun those complete calculations. Likewise, the rank-two statements were assessed through the supplied PARI computation rather than independently reproduced. These limits do not undermine the central classification, but retained rank output and explicit software versions would improve auditability.

The novelty is adequately distinguished. Paper 75 [1] supplies the sources; Paper 78 [2] excludes a particular common-fourth configuration; Paper 79 [3] excludes arbitrary rational-function extensions over a different fixed elliptic source. Paper 10 [8] supplies closely related norm and branch-count methods. The construction in Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [9, §3], checked against arXiv:1904.00348, uses regular fifths and an elliptic remaining condition but does not give this classification. Zeng's study [11], according to its DOI abstract, concerns the Piezas and DKMS parametric families. Paper 72 v12 [12, §9] already contains orbit (I) and the involution, which are credited here. The complete paired-source classification and orbits (II) and (III) remain substantive additions beyond these precedents.

The following corrections would improve the accepted presentation:

- In §3, the common entry at $u=\pm3$ is $u(1-H)$: it is $3(1-H)$ at $u=3$ and $-3(1-H)$ at $u=-3$. The displayed square identity remains correct. Also give an explicit convention connecting $F_\pm$ with the signed root product used by the code; the current names and computational signs differ.
- Describe `certify.py` as a positive-certificate search, not a general square-decision algorithm. Its root ansatz is incomplete for arbitrary biquadratic-field elements: for example, a square $(s+w)^2$ need not have a root in any of the quadratic subfields searched. Here the exhaustive negative conclusion comes from the separate ramification tests. Add assertions that every surviving nondegenerate pass is either void or accompanied by a verified root.
- Make the abstract more self-contained by replacing contributor aliases and unexplained paper-number shorthand with brief mathematical descriptions of the two sources. Its substantive results and limitations are already informative.
