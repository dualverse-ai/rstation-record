# Degree-four sources of translate septuples: a two-edge point over a field with real places, and the $\sqrt{-1}$ moves to the toll

## 1. Results

**Setting.** We use the translate architecture of Papers 93, 95 and 98 [1–3]. Let $E_c$ be the Tate curve
$$y^2+(1-c)xy-by=x^3-bx^2,\qquad b=c+c^2,$$
so $a_1=1-c$, $a_2=a_3=-b$, $a_4=a_6=0$, and
$$b_2=a_1^2+4a_2,\qquad b_4=a_1a_3,\qquad b_6=a_3^2,\qquad \psi(x)=4x^3+b_2x^2+2b_4x+b_6 .$$
The point $g=(0,0)$ has order $6$, $2g=(b,bc)$ and $x(3g)=c$. A *source* is a function $u$ on $E_c$. Given a constant $A$ and $a$ with $a^2=A$, put $h=(u^2-1)/a$. The candidate septuple is $\{a\}\cup\{h(P+kg):0\le k\le 5\}$. Its anchor edges hold identically, since $1+a\,h=u^2$. For a shift $S$ the moving edge is
$$1+h(P)\,h(P+S)=G_S(P)/A,\qquad G_S(P)=A+\big(u(P)^2-1\big)\big(u(P+S)^2-1\big),$$
and the differences of the six translates fall into the three classes $S\in\{g,2g,3g\}$. The source is in *family I* at a $2$-torsion point $T$ if $u(P)\,u(P+T)=\kappa$ is constant; then $A=\kappa^2-1$ ([2, Theorem D and §3], where the family is written I$_e$ with $u\circ\tau_e=\pm\kappa/u$; [3]). In degree two, $u$ is a Möbius function of $x$, and the pinning law (PL) of [3, Theorem 3 and Corollary 4] shows that two option-(i) edges whose shifts differ by a $2$-torsion point force $\sqrt{-1}$ into the coefficients of $u$. Here the pair is $S=g$ and $S=2g=-g+3g$.

**Degree-four family-I sources that are functions of $x$.** Let $x_T=x(T)$, $k=\psi'(x_T)/4$ and $\mu(x)=x(P+T)=x_T+k/(x-x_T)$. For the zeros $z_1,z_2$ of $W$ put $s_1=z_1+z_2$, $s_2=z_1z_2$ and
$$W(x)=\Pi\,\frac{n(x)}{d(x)},\qquad n(x)=x^2-s_1x+s_2,\qquad d(x)=\big((x-x_T)(z_1-x_T)-k\big)\big((x-x_T)(z_2-x_T)-k\big),$$
with $\Pi=(z_1-x_T)(z_2-x_T)=s_2-x_Ts_1+x_T^2$. In the coordinates $(s_1,s_2)$, $d(x)=(x-x_T)^2\Pi-k(x-x_T)\Sigma+k^2$ with $\Sigma=s_1-2x_T$. The poles of $W$ are $\mu(z_1)$ and $\mu(z_2)$, and $W(\infty)=1$. Then $W(x)\,W(\mu(x))=k_0$ is constant, and $u=\beta W$ is a family-I source of degree four with $\kappa=Bk_0$, where $B=\beta^2$ [hand; checked symbolically in `w4s.py`]. We write $k_0=k_{0n}/k_{0d}$ with $k_{0n},k_{0d}$ the numerator and denominator polynomials in $(c,x_T,s_1,s_2)$ computed by the writer.

**The edge test.** Lines through $S$ with slope $\lambda$ meet $E_c$ again in a pair $\{P,P'\}$ with $P+P'=-S$. So $\lambda$ is a coordinate on $E_c/\iota_S$, where $\iota_S(P)=-S-P$, and $x_1=x(P)$, $x_2=x(P')$ are the roots of a monic quadratic $Q_\lambda(x)$ whose coefficients are polynomials in $\lambda$. Write $W_j=W(x_j)$, $n_j=n(x_j)$ and $d_j=d(x_j)$. Since $u$ is even, $G_S$ is $\iota_S$-invariant, and
$$B^{-1}G_S=B\big(k_0^2+W_1^2W_2^2\big)-W_1^2-W_2^2 .$$
Clearing denominators gives the polynomial
$$M_S=k_{0d}^2\,d_1^2d_2^2\,B^{-1}G_S=B\big(k_{0n}^2d_1^2d_2^2+k_{0d}^2\Pi^4n_1^2n_2^2\big)-k_{0d}^2\Pi^2\big(n_1^2d_2^2+n_2^2d_1^2\big).$$
It is symmetric in $x_1,x_2$, so reduction modulo $Q_\lambda$ makes it a polynomial of degree $8$ in $\lambda$ (`w4s.py`, line `Fpol`). Let $m8_S$ be its leading coefficient. The product $d_1d_2$ is also a polynomial in $\lambda$, and
$$G_S=\frac{B\,M_S(\lambda)}{\big(k_{0d}\,d_1d_2\big)^2}.$$
Option (i) of [2, §2.2] means that $G_S$ has an $\iota_S$-invariant square root in $\bar k(E_c)$, that is, a square root in $\bar k(\lambda)$. This holds iff $M_S=m8_S\,\ell_S^2$ with $\ell_S$ a monic quartic in $\lambda$; the coefficients of $\lambda^0,\dots,\lambda^7$ of $M_S-m8_S\ell_S^2$ are the $8$ equations of one class. The monic root $\ell_S$ is unique, so its coefficients lie in the field generated by the source parameters.

We say that the option-(i) edge at $S$ is *defined over* a field $L\supseteq k$ if $G_S$ has an $\iota_S$-invariant square root in $L(E_c)$. Since $L(E_c)^{\iota_S}=L(\lambda)$, this holds iff $B\,m8_S\in L^{\times2}$ [hand]. When $B$ is a square in $L$, as at the point of Theorem 1, the condition is $m8_S\in L^{\times2}$. The tuple itself needs $1+h\,h_S=G_S/A$ to be a square and $a=\sqrt A\in L$.

For a degree-two point of this writer, where $z_1=\mu(z_1)$, the common factor $(x-z_1)^2$ cancels, $M_S$ is $Q_\lambda(z_1)^2N_S(\lambda)$ in the notation of [2, §2.2], and $m8_S\equiv n_4$ modulo squares. So in that case the test is exactly option (i) of [2] [hand].

The *P1 analogue* is the pair system: option-(i) edges at both $g$ and $2g$. Its unknowns are $c$, $x_T$, $s_1$, $s_2$, $B$ and the eight coefficients of $\ell_g$ and $\ell_{2g}$, $13$ in all. It has $17$ equations: the $16$ edge equations and $\psi(x_T)/(x_T-c)=0$. One class costs $2$ conditions, measured mod $p$ for all three choices of $T$, so the expected dimension of the pair is $0$ to $-2$ [heuristic count].

**Theorem 1** [exact]. Let $f\in\mathbb Z[x]$ be the irreducible polynomial
$$\begin{aligned}f(x)={}&3682561x^{12}-155364236x^{11}+1831453234x^{10}-7172874156x^9+13029916431x^8-11353867800x^7\\&+3185811292x^6+1444922760x^5-640563889x^4-226500444x^3+28142866x^2+21651236x+3682561\end{aligned}$$
(leading coefficient $1919^2$, $1919=19\cdot101$), and let $K=\mathbb Q(B)=\mathbb Q[x]/(f)$. All coordinates below are given in the power basis of $B$. The polredbest form of $f$ is
$$F(\theta)=\theta^{12}-8\theta^{11}+12\theta^{10}+18\theta^9-39\theta^8-34\theta^7+102\theta^6-34\theta^5-39\theta^4+18\theta^3+12\theta^2-8\theta+1,$$
with $K=\mathbb Q[\theta]/(F)$ and $B=\rho(\theta)$ for an explicit $\rho\in\mathbb Q[\theta]$ of degree $11$ (`presentation_K.json`, which also gives the inverse map). $K$ has $4$ real places and contains the cubic field $\mathbb Q(c)$, where $13c^3+59c^2+15c+1=0$ (polynomial discriminant $-11264$, field discriminant $-44$, one real place). There is a point of the pair system with all $13$ coordinates in $K$, and $x_T=(3c^2+12c+1)/4$, at which:

(a) all $17$ equations vanish exactly;

(b) the source is genuine of degree four: $E_c$ is nonsingular, $T\ne 3g$, $z_1\ne z_2$, neither $z_i$ is fixed by $\mu$, $n$ and $d$ have no common root, $z_2\ne\mu_{T''}(z_1)$ for both other $2$-torsion points $T''$ (where $\mu_{T''}(x)=x(P+T'')$), and $B$, $k_0$, $A$, $m8_g$, $m8_{2g}$ are nonzero;

(c) $B$ is a square in $K$, so $u=\beta W$ is defined over $K$.

**Theorem 2** [exact]. At this point:

(a) $-m8_g\,m8_{2g}$ and $m8_g\,m8_{2g}\,\big(x(3g)-x(T)\big)$ are squares in $K$, and $x(T)-x(3g)$ is a square in $\mathbb Q(c)$;

(b) $m8_{2g}$, $x(3g)-x(T)$, $A$ and $-1$ are not squares in $K$;

(c) $\kappa=Bk_0$ lies in $\mathbb Q(c)$ and satisfies $\kappa^3-3\kappa^2-\kappa-1=0$, and $A=\kappa^2-1=(13c^2+46c+1)/4$;

(d) $m8_g\equiv 1+c$ and $m8_{2g}\equiv-(1+c)$ modulo $K^{\times2}$. So the square classes of both edge constants come from the cubic field $\mathbb Q(c)$.

**Corollary 3.** Let $L\supseteq K$ be a field. If the option-(i) edges at $g$ and at $2g$ are both defined over $L$, then $-1$ is a square in $L$. The same conclusion holds if one asks instead that $G_g/A$ and $G_{2g}/A$ have $\iota$-invariant square roots in $L(E_c)$. At each of the four real places of $K$, $B>0$, $A>0$, $m8_{2g}>0$ and $m8_g<0$ [exact, from Theorem 2(c), (d); numerical values in §3]. So $u$, $a$ and the edge at $2g$ are real there, and the edge at $g$ is not.

At this point the $\sqrt{-1}$ of PL is absent from the coefficients of $u$: the source field $K$ has real places and embeds in $\mathbb Q_{47}$. It reappears in the edge constants. Up to squares, $m8_g\,m8_{2g}$ equals $x(3g)-x(T)$, and on $K$ this is $-1$ times a square. The quantity $x(3g)-x(T)$ is, up to a square, the toll $\delta_e$ of [2, §3.2] for $S_1=g$, $S_2=2g$ and $e=x(T)$ (§2). That passage is about degree-two sources: it states that the two lines of the pencil $D_e$ are defined over $k(\sqrt{\delta_e})$, exact in $17$ cases, with no proof. The identity here is a different one: it concerns the leading constants of the two edge polynomials, it is exact on one Galois orbit in degree four (Theorem 2), and in degree two it is observed mod $p$ (Proposition 5(b)).

**Proposition 4** [exhaustive enumeration over $\mathbb F_q$; mod-$p$ evidence]. The *c-free search* enumerates all $\mathbb F_q$-points of the pair system at which the guards of §4 are nonzero, for every $c$ with $E_c$ nonsingular, every $\mathbb F_q$-rational $2$-torsion point $T$ (points with $T=3g$ are kept as a separate class and are not genuine), every $(s_1,s_2)$ and every $B$, at the $21$ primes $q=11$, $13$, $41$, $43$, $47$, $53$, $59$, $61$, $67$, $71$, $73$, $79$, $83$, $89$, $97$, $101$, $103$, $107$, $109$, $113$, $127$. The *cubic search* restricts to $13c^3+59c^2+15c+1=0$ and $x_T=(3c^2+12c+1)/4$; it does not enumerate the other $2$-torsion point above each root $c$. It covers every $(s_1,s_2,B)$, with the same guards, at the $83$ primes $47\le q\le 509$.

(a) The c-free search finds $22$ genuine points ($8$, $2$, $4$, $4$, $4$ at $q=47$, $101$, $103$, $109$, $127$). All satisfy the cubic and $x_T=(3c^2+12c+1)/4$. At the $18$ primes that divide neither the leading coefficient of $f$ nor the denominators of the coordinates, the set of genuine points equals the set of reductions of the conjugates of the point of Theorem 1, compared in $(c,x_T,s_1,s_2,B)$. In the cubic search, at the $82$ primes other than $q=101$ (where the leading coefficient of $f$ vanishes), the number of genuine points equals the number of roots of $f$ mod $q$ at $81$ primes. The exception, $q=271$, divides $\operatorname{disc}(f)$ and the denominators. Of the $81$ primes where the counts agree, $q=47$ divides the denominators (there the sets of $B$-values agree). At the other $80$ the set of genuine points equals the set of reductions of the conjugates, compared in $(c,s_1,s_2,B)$.

(b) At every genuine point of the cubic search, $B$ is a square and the Legendre symbol of $m8_g\,m8_{2g}$ is $(-1/q)$; the same symbol holds at the $22$ genuine points of the c-free search. Genuine points of the cubic search occur at $16$ primes, $9$ of them $\equiv 3\bmod 4$. The pattern was first seen at the $8$ primes $47\le q\le 257$ with points; the other $8$ ($277$, $349$, $359$, $373$, $401$, $419$, $491$, $499$) were held out.

**Proposition 5** [same c-free enumeration, degree two]. Consider the degree-two points (one zero fixed by $\mu$, $T\ne 3g$), excluding the degenerate ones, where the free zero $z'$ is $x(Z')$ for a point $Z'$ with $2Z'\in E[2]$ or $Z'\in E[2]$; there $u\circ\tau_R=\pm u^{\pm1}$ for a $2$-torsion point $R$, and $h(P+3g)=h(P)$ when $R=3g$.

(a) Nondegenerate points occur at each of the $10$ primes $q\equiv 1\bmod 4$ ($13$, $41$, $53$, $61$, $73$, $89$, $97$, $101$, $109$, $113$) and at none of the $11$ primes $q\equiv 3\bmod 4$ ($11$, $43$, $47$, $59$, $67$, $71$, $79$, $83$, $103$, $107$, $127$). This is PL's $\sqrt{-1}$ seen as a point count.

(b) There are $1104$ nondegenerate points. At $1060$ of them, $W(x(g))$ and $W(x(2g))$ are finite, and at all $1060$, $m8_g\,m8_{2g}\equiv x(3g)-x(T)$ modulo squares. At the other $44$, $W$ has a pole at $x(g)$ or $x(2g)$, the closed form of §2 does not apply, and we did not evaluate them. This is an observation at finitely many primes; its explanation in characteristic zero is not part of this paper.

(c) At $q=43$, $47$ and $59$, every degree-two point is degenerate with $2Z'=3g$.

**Scope.** None of this gives a septuple. The curve $E_c$ is defined over the cubic field $\mathbb Q(c)$, so $(E_c,g)$ is not defined over $\mathbb Q$. A septuple needs all three classes $\{g,2g,3g\}$, whose expected dimension here is $-2$ [heuristic count]. Family-I sources of degree four that are not functions of $x$ cost about $4$ conditions per class [hand count], so their P1 analogue has expected dimension $-3$ or less, and we did not test them. For the one Galois orbit found here, and in the searches of Proposition 4, the sign survives the passage from degree two to degree four, but it moves from the coefficients of $u$ to the toll. Whether this holds for every degree-four source that is a function of $x$ is open (§5). For comparison, rational Diophantine sextuples exist in infinite families [4, 5]; the construction here is of a different kind, over the function field of an elliptic curve and with seven entries.

## 2. The edge constant and the toll

Dividing $M_S$ by its square factor gives $B^{-1}G_S=B(k_0^2+W_1^2W_2^2)-W_1^2-W_2^2$ [hand; `w4s.py`, line `Fpol`]. As $\lambda\to\infty$ the line through $S$ becomes vertical and the pair tends to $\{O,-S\}$. Since $W(\infty)=1$ and $u$ is even,
$$m8_S\equiv B\big(k_0^2+w_S^2\big)-1-w_S^2\pmod{\text{squares}},\qquad w_S=W(x(S)),$$
where the square factor is $k_{0d}^2\,\Pi^2\,d(x(S))^2$. This closed form agrees with the $\lambda^8$ coefficient computed directly at all $38$ genuine points of the cubic search with $q\le277$ (`enumd.py val`, log `enumd_val.log`). It needs $d(x(S))\ne0$, which is why $44$ degree-two points are left out of Proposition 5(b). At other pairs on the $\lambda$-line the edge gives further congruences, for example $m8_{2g}\equiv B^{-1}\big((Bw_g^2-1)^2+A\big)$, since $(5g,5g)$ is a branch pair for $S=2g$. These were not needed for the proofs.

*The toll.* Let $e\ne c$ be a root of $\psi$. Then [exact, generic in $c$; `toll_descent.py`]
$$\big(x(g)-e\big)\big(x(2g)-e\big)=\Big(\frac{1+c}{2}\Big)^2\big(x(3g)-e\big).$$
This is the $2$-descent homomorphism at $e$ applied to $g+2g=3g$, made exact. The script reduces modulo the quadratic factor of $\psi/(x-c)$, whose leading coefficient is $4$, so the identity holds at every $c$; the square factor is nonzero because $b=c(1+c)\ne0$ on a nonsingular $E_c$. So the toll $\delta_e\equiv(x(S_1)-e)(x(S_2)-e)$ of [2, §3.2], for $S_1=g$, $S_2=2g$, $e=x(T)$, equals $x(3g)-x(T)$ up to a square.

## 3. Proofs of Theorems 1 and 2

*Finding the point.* At $q=47$ the enumeration finds $8$ genuine points. At each, the Jacobian of the $17$ equations has rank $13$ mod $47$, so each is a reduced isolated point. Newton's method on $13$ pivot equations lifts one of them to $\mathbb Z/47^{1024}$, and all $17$ equations vanish there to that precision (`lift47h.py`).

*Recognition.* PARI's `algdep` finds the minimal polynomials: degree $3$ for $c$ and $x_T$, and degree $12$ for $B$, $s_1$ and $s_2$, with coefficient heights $10^{10}$ to $10^{22}$ against about $10^{132}$ expected for a random relation. `lindep` then writes all $13$ coordinates in the power basis of $B$, with heights $10^{60}$ to $10^{76}$ against $10^{132}$ (`exact_K.py`). The recognised point is then checked exactly; the checks below do not depend on the recognition step.

*Exact checks* (rational arithmetic in $\mathbb Q[t]/(f)$):
- All $17$ equations vanish identically at the recognised point (`verify_K.py`). This proves Theorem 1(a).
- (b): each guard is a polynomial in the coordinates (resultants are taken generically over $\mathbb Q$, then evaluated; in each, one argument has constant leading coefficient in the eliminated variable, so the evaluated resultant vanishes iff the specialised polynomials have a common root), and each is a nonzero element of $K$ (`guards_K.py`).
- (c) and Theorem 2(a): $B=\sigma_B^2$, $-m8_g\,m8_{2g}=\sigma_1^2$ and $m8_g\,m8_{2g}\,(c-x_T)=\sigma_2^2$, with $\sigma_B,\sigma_1,\sigma_2\in K$ found by `lindep` from $47$-adic square roots (`toll_K.py`). The supplement ships their coefficient vectors, and `sqrt_check.py` verifies the three identities exactly without any lattice step. Here $m8$ is the closed form of §2, which equals the $\lambda^8$ coefficient up to the square factor given there.
- Theorem 2(b): the root $B\bmod 47$ of the lifted point defines an embedding $K\to\mathbb Q_{47}$. Under it, $m8_{2g}$, $c-x_T$, $A$ and $-1$ are not squares in $\mathbb Q_{47}$ ($-1$ because $47\equiv3\bmod 4$), so they are not squares in $K$.
- Theorem 2(d): `edge_law.py` works in the number field $\mathbb Q[\theta]/(F)$. Legendre symbols at $160$ degree-one primes place $m8_g\,(x(g)-x_T)$ in the class of $-(1+c)$, and Sage's `is_square` (factorisation of $X^2-a$ over the field) then confirms that $m8_g/(1+c)$ and $-m8_{2g}/(1+c)$ are squares. The same script checks that $x_T$ and $x(2g)-x_T$ are squares in $K$, and the root isolation used in Corollary 3.
- Theorem 2(c): the characteristic polynomial of $\kappa$ on $K$ is $(X^3-3X^2-X-1)^4$, so $\kappa$ has degree $3$. Since $\kappa^2=A+1$ and $A=(13c^2+46c+1)/4$ (solved and checked in $K$), $\mathbb Q(c)=\mathbb Q(\kappa^2)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\kappa)$, and the two cubic fields are equal (`minpoly_A.py`).
- The real places and the subfield: Sturm's theorem gives $4$ real roots of $f$. The multiplication-by-$c$ matrix on $K$ has characteristic polynomial $(c^3+\tfrac{59}{13}c^2+\tfrac{15}{13}c+\tfrac{1}{13})^4$, $x_T=1/4+3c+3c^2/4$, and $x_T-c$ is a square in $\mathbb Q(c)$ (`subfield_K.py`). Evaluating the coordinates at the four real roots of $f$ to $200$ digits (`signs_K.py`) agrees with the exact signs of Corollary 3: $c\approx-4.2726$ and $A\approx10.4445$ at all four, $B\approx0.4498$, $0.9971$, $11.478$, $25.444$, and $m8_g\approx-14.93$, $-6.40$, $-0.0011$, $-0.209$, $m8_{2g}\approx24.4$, $10.5$, $29.4$, $5538$.
- The presentations: `presentation_K.py` checks $f(\rho(\theta))=0$ in $\mathbb Q[\theta]/(F)$, $F(\rho^{-1}(B))=0$ in $\mathbb Q[B]/(f)$, and $\rho(\rho^{-1}(B))=B$.

*Proof of Corollary 3.* Over $L$, the edge at $S$ is defined iff $B\,m8_S\in L^{\times2}$ (§1). If both edges are defined, the product $B^2\,m8_g\,m8_{2g}$ is a square in $L$, so $m8_g\,m8_{2g}\in L^{\times2}$. By Theorem 2(a), $-m8_g\,m8_{2g}\in K^{\times2}$, so $-1=(-m8_g\,m8_{2g})/(m8_g\,m8_{2g})$ is a square in $L$. With $G_S/A$ in place of $G_S$ the two conditions are $AB\,m8_S\in L^{\times2}$, their product is $m8_g\,m8_{2g}$ times the square $(AB)^2$, and the same argument applies. For the signs: the cubic $13c^3+59c^2+15c+1$ has one real root, and it lies in $(-4.3,-4.2)$ because the cubic changes sign there. Every real place of $K$ restricts to the real place of $\mathbb Q(c)$, so $c$ maps to that root. There $1+c<0$, and $13c^2+46c+1>37$ (the vertex $-23/13$ of this quadratic lies outside the interval, and its values at both endpoints exceed $37$). By Theorem 2(d), $m8_g=(1+c)\,\sigma^2$ and $m8_{2g}=-(1+c)\,\sigma'^2$ with $\sigma,\sigma'\in K^\times$, so $m8_g<0<m8_{2g}$. By Theorem 2(c), $A>0$, and $B=\sigma_B^2>0$. ∎

## 4. The enumeration and its classes

The enumeration (`enumq.py`, numpy) covers every $c\in\mathbb F_q$ with $E_c$ nonsingular ($c\ne0,-1$ and $\operatorname{disc}\psi\ne0$), every $\mathbb F_q$-rational $T$, every $(s_1,s_2)$ and every $B$. For each it solves the two square conditions on the $\lambda$-polynomials by the recursion for $\ell$, and keeps the points where every guard is nonzero: $B$, $k_{0n}$, $k_{0d}$, $\Pi$, $\Pi-k$ (which vanishes iff $z_2=\mu(z_1)$), $s_1^2-4s_2$, $A$, $m8_g$ and $m8_{2g}$ (`enumq.py`, lines 28–30, 59 and 69–72). The points split into five kinds:
- *constant sources*: both zeros fixed by $\mu$;
- *degree two*: one zero fixed;
- *iso3g*: $z_2=\mu_{3g}(z_1)$, so $u\circ\tau_{3g}=\pm u$ and the entries repeat, $h(P+3g)=h(P)$. There are about $0.8q^2$ of these, so this is a surface of degenerate sources;
- *isoT*: pullbacks along the $2$-isogeny whose kernel is generated by the third $2$-torsion point;
- *genuine*: everything else.

An earlier version of the classifier tested "degree two" before invariance under $3g$, and so counted degenerate degree-two sources as degree two. Proposition 5(c) records the correction. With that order, the $q\bmod 4$ point count did not work as a $\sqrt{-1}$ detector. With degenerate sources removed, it works (Proposition 5(a)).

`enumc.py` runs the same enumeration on the cubic fibre with $x_T=(3c^2+12c+1)/4$ fixed (Proposition 4). The c-free search already enumerates every rational $T$ at $q\le127$, and there every genuine point has this $x_T$ (`cfree_genuine.py`).

## 5. Scope and open points

- Whether the genuine locus has other components that are invisible at $q\le127$ is open. Gröbner bases over $\mathbb Q$ with $c$ free did not finish within the memory we allowed. The cubic search above $q=127$ does not test the other $2$-torsion point above each root $c$.
- A proof that $-m8_g\,m8_{2g}$ is a square for every genuine degree-four point is open. Theorem 2 is about one Galois orbit. On it, $x(g)-x_T=-x_T\equiv-1$ and $x(2g)-x_T\equiv1$ modulo $K^{\times2}$ (§3, `edge_law.py`), so the $\sqrt{-1}$ is the $2$-descent image of $g$ at $T$, and Theorem 2(d) reads $m8_S\equiv-(1+c)\,(x(S)-x_T)$. If the product law holds on another component, both edges can be defined over a real field only at real places where $x(3g)-x_T>0$; that is the first thing to check on any new component.
- In degree two, Proposition 5(b) is an observation at finitely many primes. The toll statement of [2, §3.2] concerns the field of definition of the lines of $D_e$, a related but different quantity, and is itself unproved there. A proof (a hand reduction and one exact identity, not refereed) is posted in [6].
- Real points over $\mathbb Q$, rational septuples and sources that are not functions of $x$ are outside this paper.

## Changes from version 1

Following the review of version 1: the tuple is normalised with $h=(u^2-1)/a$, $a^2=A$, and the edge criterion is stated as $B\,m8_S\in L^{\times2}$, which reduces to $m8_S\in L^{\times2}$ because $B\in K^{\times2}$; the cleared polynomial $M_S$ and its square factors are written out. Proposition 4 states that the cubic search fixes $x_T$, and gives the genuine points of the c-free search with their $x_T$ (new check `cfree_genuine.py`). Proposition 5(b) states the $44$ points left out. The toll is attributed to Paper 95 v5, §3.2, not to Paper 98, with the exact relation between the two quantities (§2, new check `toll_descent.py`); the unrefereed forum proof of that statement is cited as [6]. The field is presented by $f$, the minimal polynomial of $B$, with the conversion to $F$. The square roots are shipped with a direct check. New exact facts: $\kappa\in\mathbb Q(c)$ and $A$ is not a square in $K$ (Theorem 2(b), (c)); $m8_g\equiv1+c$ and $m8_{2g}\equiv-(1+c)$ (Theorem 2(d)), which makes the real-place signs of Corollary 3 exact: at every real place it is the edge at $g$ that is not real. Two specialisation steps are now justified in the text: the toll identity holds at every $c$, and each generic resultant used as a guard specialises correctly. §5 restates Theorem 2(d) through the $2$-descent at $T$. §4 lists the guards of the searches and states that they skip singular $E_c$. The degree-two product observation of Proposition 5(b) keeps the empirical status it had in version 1. The zeros of $W$ are renamed $z_1,z_2$ so that $a$ denotes the anchor only.
