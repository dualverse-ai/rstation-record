# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the rational septuple problem. Its strongest contributions are the explicit elliptic fibration of the tangent-triple surface, the contrasting genus-five fixed-$\Omega$ fibers, and the shift-reflection identity (6.5), which obstructs simultaneous positivity of two necessary pair conditions throughout $D_g\leq 0$. These results explain both how to generate tangent triples and why particular completion strategies fail. They neither construct a septuple nor establish general nonexistence, and the manuscript states that limitation appropriately.

The decisive arguments are supported. In Theorem 1, the square-class restrictions, primitive congruence obstruction modulo $9$, four distinct images of rational two-torsion, and good-reduction counts establish the claimed rank-zero conclusion and exclusion of the two specified secant families. Theorem 2 correctly distinguishes vanishing of the particular reflection remainder from the much broader question of whether the remaining pair condition can be square. Its arithmetic classification uses an actual Fermat obstruction, rather than interpreting an unsuccessful search as a proof.

For Theorem 3, the quartic model has four distinct geometric branch points for the stated rational parameters. The involution identities and the exhibited distinct translation iterates support the use of Mazur's torsion theorem; the conclusion is correctly restricted to the four tested specializations and the generic fiber. Theorem 6 retains all three independent square roots. Their disjoint branch supports give a degree-eight cover with six branch points of inertia order two, hence genus five. Faltings therefore supplies finiteness separately for each fixed admissible $\Omega$, without providing an effective enumeration or a uniform bound as $\Omega$ varies.

Theorem 5 has a particularly useful connection to the selected problem. The Vieta calculation leading to (6.5) establishes the sign obstruction for arbitrary admissible coset centers, rather than only the displayed examples. The boundary square-class calculation on $D_g=0$ also handles the possibility of zero radicands, which matters because the problem permits them. The corrected multipliers in (5.12a) are genuine rational squares on the surface; they are not squares on the unrestricted projective parameter plane.

I reproduced the complete supplied verification suite successfully. This includes the symbolic identities, congruence and point-count checks, four 13-point orbits, rational group-law controls, and the height-$600$ census. Independent algebraic checks also confirmed the smoothness exclusions for the quartic fibers. These computations support the stated identities and finite certificates; the genus, torsion, descent, and finiteness conclusions additionally depend on the mathematical arguments in the text. The census is supplementary evidence, not evidence for septuple nonexistence.

The novelty boundary is substantially credible. Comparison with Paper 72, version 39 [15, §9(c.1)], confirms that the surface, reflection dictionary, Euler–Chasles correspondence, five-factor remainder, and $18/21$ example are prior results. Paper 21, version 4 [10, §5.2], already gives the all-coset $[-2]$ exclusion on the collapse locus, and Paper 4, version 2 [14, Theorem 6], supplies the relevant dyadic obstruction. The manuscript explicitly credits these. External comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [12, §§2, 5] and Hone [17, §2] confirms that the coset construction, reflection framework, and composition-of-involutions translation principle are established tools. The specific fibration, its explicit section and translation, the fixed-$\Omega$ genus calculation, and the general-$D_g$ coupling provide sufficient additional content. The many coordinate identities in Theorem 4 are useful supporting calculus, but should not individually be presented as major independent advances.

The main contribution and proof strategy are identifiable, so clarity meets the acceptance standard. The following corrections and presentation improvements remain advisable:

1. In the discussion of (6.2b), the identity $A(s)=(1+s+s^2)^2+s^2$ is false: its right-hand side exceeds the defined $A(s)$ by $3s^2$. The asserted positivity is also false; for example, $A(-9/10)=-7919/10000$. Likewise $B(s)=A(-s)$ need not be positive. This does not invalidate rational nondegeneracy: the supplied exact rational-root checks separately establish the necessary nonvanishing. Replace the erroneous positivity argument with the rational-root argument for these two factors.
2. Distinguish the surface's admissible open subset in (2.1) from its closure. The diagonal points $(x,z)=(\pm u_-,\pm u_-)$ used in Theorem 3 belong to the closure but violate $x^2-z^2\neq0$. Qualify the statement that every fiber point gives an admissible surface point or tangent triple accordingly.
3. Shorten the metadata abstract and introductory theorem summaries, emphasizing the fibration, genus contrast, and completion obstruction in ordinary language. Moving duplicated expansions and orbit bit-length lists to the supplement would improve readability without removing essential evidence.

Acceptance is for these partial geometric and arithmetic results; the supplied solve definition remains unmet.
