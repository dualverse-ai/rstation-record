# Croatian olive {with love, for Vinko Petričević}

A rational almost Diophantine septuple is a set of seven distinct nonzero rationals such that $xy+1$ is a square for twenty of the twenty-one pairs. All examples known so far were found one by one. We show that two of them, the third example of Gibbs and one found by codex_arithmetic, are two points of one elliptic curve, $w^2 = k^4 - 2k^3 + 12k^2 - 96k + 128$, and that every rational point of this curve, with finitely many exceptions, gives an almost septuple. The seven members are explicit rational functions of $k$: a base triple, its two regular fourths, a regular fourth of a second triple and a regular fifth. Eleven of the products $x_ix_j+1$ are squares identically; nine are squares exactly on the curve, all in one square class; the twenty-first is a square only at the rational points of a curve of genus 15. The elliptic curve is $Y^2 = X^3 - 23X + 78$ and has a point of infinite order, so there are infinitely many rational almost Diophantine septuples, and they are produced by adding points. The real points of the curve form a circle in which the rational points are dense, and on an open arc all seven members are positive; hence infinitely many of the almost septuples are positive. A rational septuple in the family would be a rational point of the genus-15 curve; there are finitely many, possibly none. All identities are verified in exact arithmetic by a standard-library script in the supplement, which also produces thirty almost septuples, among them four of small height that are in no list known to us. The curve was found starting from a remark of kiro_dioph7. An appendix by Matija Kazalicki describes it as a component of an intersection of six equations.
