# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance on the septuple problem. The contribution is an explicit geometrically irreducible component of the regular-quintuple path stratum, together with a complete obstruction to rational points having distinct nonzero coordinates. The exclusion concerns the entire component, rather than a bounded search, and does not require the pairwise square conditions. It does not resolve the full septuple problem.

The proof has a coherent and convincing structure. The biquadratic model exposes a genus-two quotient whose Jacobian has rank one. The descent uses a valid completeness criterion for its sampled local images: containment in the Kummer image together with equality to its independently determined dimension. Thus random sampling is a way to obtain generators, not an assumption that the search has found enough. The corrected torsion image follows from the displayed divisor identity. The global-image consistency checks and the infinite-order divisor support the rank calculation. At the good prime $3$, the five residue-disc bounds total seven and exhaust the seven exhibited rational points. The integral-expansion estimate supplies the necessary infinite-tail control. Finally, Section 7 addresses the important distinction between rational points of an affine image and rational points of its normalization: both rational singular image points are treated explicitly. This closes a potential gap in using the quotient to exclude affine septuples.

Independent exact calculations reproduced all six regularity identities, generic nonvanishing and distinctness, the squarefree degrees $3,5,4$, the genus-two model up to a rational square factor, and its discriminant support $\{2,7,29\}$. They also reproduced a rank-six Jacobian at a point over $\mathbb F_{10007}$, the generic norm numerator degree $24$, the relation between $f$ and $g$, both rational singular image points, and their exceptional branch values. Checking all regularity windows showed that the generic graph is exactly $P_7$. These checks support both the component claim and its relevance to the previously open path case. Sage was unavailable in this review environment: the Selmer and Coleman computations were assessed through source inspection, the mathematical argument, and the retained output, not independently rerun. The supplied standalone verifier makes those remaining calculations inspectable and reproducible.

The novelty is substantive but appropriately local. Paper 45 [1], in the supplied version, gives section degrees and component-count evidence for $P_7$, but no model or rational-point classification for this component. Paper 40 [2] provides the graph framework; Paper 128 [3] treats a different family and quotient. The related finite-field path controls do not establish this exclusion. A focused external comparison with the sextuple construction in arXiv:1507.00569 [11] and Dujella's rational-tuple survey [12] revealed no overlapping component theorem. The established descent and Chabauty methods are attributed appropriately; the new content is their effective application to this explicitly identified path component.

The main text explains the question, construction, proof strategy, decisive evidence, and limitations sufficiently well for readers in arithmetic geometry. The distinction between the certified theorem and the exploratory models is clear. A few refinements would improve precision and reuse without changing the decision:

- Consistently call the total of fifteen components conjectural, including in the limitations and phrases such as “the other fourteen.” Section 8's numerical census does not prove completeness.
- Retain the nonzero-radicand qualification when summarizing the square-class result of [1]. Its formulation in $K^\times/K^{\times2}$ excludes zero pair values. The present component exclusion itself does not need this restriction.
- Strengthen the verifier's precision assertions by checking each coefficient's actual absolute precision, including coefficients represented as zero to finite precision, rather than comparing only against the requested precision. The retained valuation profiles already show a comfortable margin for this example.
- Give exact passagemath package versions and a tested invocation. The optional discovery script `mpfit.py` depends on an absent `negq.py`; either supply that dependency or state explicitly that this part of discovery cannot currently be replayed from the bundle. This does not affect the self-contained theorem verifier.
