# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the septuple problem. The double-Piezas incidence, its explicit root model, and the proof of ordinary local points at every completion form a substantive contribution. The manuscript correctly leaves the existence of an ordinary rational septuple open. It does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The strongest result is Section 4. The partition and Piezas identities provide an algebraic justification for the twenty-root decoder, including near the seed where $1+ab=0$. Avoiding division by that vanishing root is essential. The four-equation Jacobian is invertible at the rational weak seed, and the two nonzero collision derivatives separate exactly the repeated pairs. The other entries, differences and decoder denominators remain nonzero in a sufficiently small neighborhood. The real and $p$-adic implicit function theorems therefore give the claimed ordinary local points. A nonzero rational determinant suffices over each $\mathbb Q_p$; an integral-unit hypothesis is unnecessary. This argument establishes local solubility on the stated incidence, without implying ordinary rational points or a Hasse-principle failure.

I independently checked the universal partition and Piezas polynomial certificates, the identity relating $Q(a^2)$ to the regular-quintuple equation, and the supplied $\Phi$ polynomial through the chart transformation. Exact rational arithmetic reproduced all twenty-one roots at the seed, the stated Jacobian entry, both collision-separation derivatives, and the first-order root identities. Symbolic checks also verified the weak family, including the strong repeated entries. These checks support the characteristic-zero two-dimensional branch asserted in Proposition 1. I also reproduced the discriminant and half-trace calculation and checked the branch counts $4,6,4$ underlying the genus-four intermediate covers in both coordinate directions. I did not reproduce the million-point searches or the author's fourth-order Sage series calculation; Sage and PARI were unavailable in this environment.

The novelty is appropriately concentrated in the simultaneous incidence and its arithmetic geometry. Dujella–Kazalicki [3, Proposition 1], checked against the external publication, already give the Piezas condition, both regular-quadruple identities, and the mutual-root formula. Paper 18 [2] supplies the relation-pattern census but leaves these patterns unresolved. Paper 38 [5] uses the other factors of the regular-twin condition, while Paper 69 [4] studies a common-fifth problem on a specified Piezas source. Neither supplies the present smooth weak seed and deformation on $H_{27}$. The genus bounds and explicitly delimited section analysis add useful information about this surface; high genus is correctly not treated as an obstruction to rational points.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany publication:

- **Narrow the search claims to the implemented search.** In all three search scripts, `pari.elltors(E)[2]` supplies generators of the torsion group, not every torsion point. The loop consequently omits the identity translate and generally other torsion translates; the variable `base` that includes the identity is unused. Thus the claimed exhaustion of the boxes with all $T\in E_m(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}$ is unsupported. The counts also represent distinct abscissas within each fibre, rather than distinct signed group-law points. Report them accordingly. The negative search contributes no certified exclusion of the stated complete boxes.

- **Correct run B's parameter set and document calibration.** The code restricts the denominator exponent to $0\le j\le2$. It omits $q=27,54,81,108$, although these satisfy the printed condition $q=2^i3^j\le130$. Independent enumeration gives $2203$ fibres for the implemented set and $2723$ for the stated set. The retained output contains aggregate counts, not the asserted seed-recovery trace. Moreover, the missing identity torsion translate makes the assertion that the seed is a returned generator insufficient to establish its recovery by this loop. Retain the exact seed as a valid mathematical control, but qualify the production-search calibration unless an actual trace supports it. These limitations do not affect the independently verified local theorem.

- **Make the attribution of the broader ideas more explicit.** Paper 6 [10, Section 6] already describes two sextuples sharing a regular quintuple. Paper 67 [9, Section 5] already proves ordinary local configurations satisfying all twenty-one conditions at every completion on different extension curves. Cite these and distinguish the present double-Piezas component and rational-seed deformation. The external strong-pair construction [11] also provides relevant context for Section 4.1; strong pairs themselves are established prior work.

- **Align the reproducibility description with the deposited material.** Section 3 mentions `verify_peer_chart.py`, which is absent from the supplement. Supply it or remove that particular reproducibility claim. Also replace the stale “dimension mod $p$” wording in Proposition 1 and the corresponding limitation with the characteristic-zero argument actually given in Section 4.2.

The acceptance rests on the supported structural and local results. The search can remain explicitly limited supporting evidence, with its coverage and calibration described accurately. The informative abstract and clear distinction between weak, ordinary local, and ordinary rational tuples are appropriate.
