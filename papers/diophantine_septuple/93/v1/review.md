# Accept

The paper makes a meaningful contribution to the supplied septuple problem, although it does not solve the rational case. Its central construction is a new $(1,6)$ architecture: on the Tate normal form with a rational point of order six, one constant $a$ and six translates
$$
h(P+kT)=\frac{u(P+kT)^2-1}{a}
$$
are reduced to three quartic norm conditions. The manuscript explains this reduction, states the necessary guards and square-class conditions, and gives exact number-field certificates for two nondegenerate solutions. The $-31$ solution also has an independently supplied certificate, while the $-23$ solution is checked by the author's exact verifier. The divisor argument showing that the ordered image is birational to the source elliptic curve is short and mathematically appropriate once the explicitly checked conditions $4W\ne O$ and $h(2T)\ne h(O)$ are accepted.

The resulting families are relevant to the task even though their entries are in degree-24 fields rather than in $\mathbb Q$. The reduction-order arguments give points of infinite order on both source curves, and the saved specializations check all 21 square identities, distinctness and nonvanishing. Thus the paper establishes infinitely many septuples over the stated number fields and provides a concrete mechanism for future rational searches. The reflection identity from the extra 2-torsion point is also a useful structural result: it makes $a$ a strong member and explains why the mixed branch can occur.

The novelty claim survives comparison with the supplied literature. Paper 21 already constructs order-four translation families over $\mathbb Q(i)$, and Papers 25 and 89 analyze related branches and strong-member reflections. Paper 49 studies the superficially similar $1+6$ shape through regular-fifth windows and high-genus fibre curves. The present construction is distinguished by the order-six translate source, its quartic norm formulation, the two new cubic parameter fields, and the exact check that its $-31$ and $-23$ examples do not satisfy the base relation of Paper 21. The discussion of Paper 90's tangent-reflection phenomenon is appropriately used as precedent rather than claimed as a duplicate result.

The evidence is adequately reproducible for an acceptance decision. The supplement contains the polynomial construction, number-field data, guards, square witnesses, controls, finite-field logs and the commands needed to replay them. The code and retained outputs are inspectable; I did not independently rerun the Sage/PARI/msolve stack, so the exact claims remain conditional on those implementations. This limitation is especially material for the $-23$ component, which has not been independently replayed, but the paper states that qualification plainly. The rank over $\mathbb Q(c)$ is only supported analytically and by bounded search, whereas the needed lower bound over $K$ is established by the incompatible reduction orders; these are correctly separated.

The paper is also appropriately candid about scope. The modular census at 41 and 71 rules out good reduction for the tested degree-two ansatz, not all rational sources; the full off-symmetry elimination timed out; the degree-$\ge3$ discussion is a parameter count; and no isolated rational septuple or nonexistence theorem is claimed. These limitations do not undermine the stated construction and mechanism, and the main text contains the problem, related work, method, results, limitations and reproducibility information in a conventional order. The abstract is self-contained and informative.

One wording change is important before publication. “For $u$ of degree 2 there are two components” (in the abstract and theorem framing) can be read as an exhaustive classification, but the full-system computation off the reflection-symmetric locus did not finish and the paper explicitly makes no such claim. Say “we exhibit two verified components” or state the precise locus on which the two-component statement is complete. Likewise, keep the labels *exact*, *msolve* and *mod $p$* attached to the corresponding assertions whenever the results are summarized. It would also help to state once, near Section 3.2, that the quartic criterion is first over an algebraic closure and that the additional leading-coefficient square tests are what descend the witnesses to $K(E_c)$. With those clarifications, the contribution is scientifically sound, relevant and sufficiently novel as a partial advance.
