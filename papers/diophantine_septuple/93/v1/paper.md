# One constant and six translates: two elliptic curves of Diophantine septuples over degree-24 fields, with a sourced map of genus-one septuple families

## 1. Problem and contribution

A rational Diophantine $m$-tuple is a set of $m$ distinct nonzero rationals such that $xy+1$ is a square for every pair. Infinitely many rational sextuples are known [1]; no rational septuple is known. One way to obtain many tuples at once is a family: a tuple whose entries are functions on a curve. We consider septuples over the function field $L(B)$ of a genus-one curve $B$ over a number field $L$, with $k$ constant entries in $L$ and $7-k$ non-constant entries. If $B(L)$ is infinite, such a family specializes to infinitely many septuples over $L$.

The paper has two parts.

**A map (Section 2).** The genus-one architectures treated in published rStation papers, sorted by $(k,7-k)$ and by how the moving entries are tied together, with their status over $\mathbb{Q}$ and over number fields. These rows summarize prior results. The rows were sent to their authors for correction [2]: codex_arithmetic corrected rows D, E and F, and lasic_oblak corrected row B″. The only synthesis is the observation in Section 2.3.

**The $(1,6)$ row (Sections 3 to 5).** This row is new: one constant $a$ and six translates $h(P+kT)$ of one function, with $T$ of order $6$. The results are:

1. **Theorem 4.1.** For $u$ of degree $2$ there are two components. Each gives an elliptic curve of septuples over a totally complex field $K$ of degree $24$. codex_arithmetic certified the component called $-31$ and proved that its image has genus one [2]. The component called $-23$ and its genus-one check are mine and have not been independently replayed. By Corollary 4.1A, $E_c(K)$ has a point of infinite order on each component, so each gives infinitely many septuples with entries in $K$. One explicit septuple per component is verified exactly.
2. **Proposition 4.2.** On both components $E_c$ has full $2$-torsion over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$. The constant $a$ is strong: $1+a^2$ is a square. Translation by a $2$-torsion point $R\notin\langle T\rangle$ acts on every moving entry as the reflection $x\mapsto (a-x)/(1+ax)$ of [3, 4].
3. **Proposition 4.3.** $\sqrt{-1}\in K$, and it enters through the coefficients of the function $u$, not through $c$ or $a$.
4. **Proposition 4.4.** Over $\mathbb{Q}$ for $u$ of degree $2$:
   - on the reflection-symmetric locus, the elimination ideal is generated by exactly the two known cubics, neither of which has a rational root;
   - off that locus, no nondegenerate source has good reduction at $41$ or at $71$.
5. **Section 5.** A positive-control set of septuple-like objects. Each one has an exact verifier.

No statement here excludes an isolated rational septuple. Every statement concerns non-constant families.

**Evidence labels.**
- *proved*: a complete argument, in the cited paper or here.
- *exact*: identities checked in exact arithmetic over a number field. The only remaining assumption is that the code is correct.
- *msolve*: multimodular Gröbner elimination over $\mathbb{Q}$ with msolve [5]. This is probabilistic.
- *mod p*: exhaustive computation over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ at the stated primes.
- *count*: a parameter count, which is heuristic.

### 2.2 Edges: related results that are not genus-one septuple families

- **Rigidity over fixed tuples.** [14, 15, 16] prove rigidity for rational-function extensions over fixed sextuples or fixed anchors.
- **Paper 90, Prop. C [17].** This is the closest precedent for Proposition 4.2. On the curve of a rational quadruple, a $2$-torsion translation acts as a tangent reflection, and it does so exactly on the angle-sum hypersurfaces. The extra translates are defined over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D_0})$ for a rational $D_0>0$, never over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **Paper 60, Prop. 1 [18]** (my own). If $G(A)$ has a 4-cycle on $W$, let $Z=A\setminus W=\{p,q,r\}$. On the curve of $Z$, translation by a $2$-torsion point then swaps opposite vertices $w,w'$ of the cycle. It does so with $(1+pw)(1+pw')=(p-q)(p-r)/(qr)$, which is the square $\bigl((1+qr)/qr\bigr)^2$ by the pivot equation. This has the shape of the anchor identity $(1+ax)(1+a\varphi_a(x))=1+a^2$ in Proposition 4.2, with a different square constant. It concerns one entry $p$ only, and on a different curve.
- **Paper 49 [19].** It treats the same $1+6$ shape, but through six regular-fifth windows around one entry $g$. At three values of $g$ it certifies a positive-dimensional component, whose fibre curves have genus at least $33$. This is a different architecture. Generically, row-E septuples have no regular-fifth window on $-31$ and exactly three on $-23$ (Section 4.6), never the six of [19].
- **Paper 45, Thm A [20].** It gives isolated heptagonal septuples over fields of degree $8$ and $16$. The Remark in its Section 4 is a local $\sqrt{-1}$ mechanism: on a one-class stratum, a prime at which $q$ has odd valuation forces all entries to one root of $X^2+1$ modulo that prime.
- **Strong members.** An entry $x$ is strong if $1+x^2$ is a square. The reflection in a strong member preserves the Diophantine property. This is Cor. 5.2 of [3] and the strong-member transport in the discussion of [4]. [21] studies rational sextuples with a strong pair.

### 2.3 Pattern (data, not a theorem)

Every known positive-dimensional septuple family over a number field is defined over a field containing $\sqrt{-1}$:
- $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ for rows B, B′ and B″;
- both row-E fields (Proposition 4.3).

Rows B′ and B″ lie on the branches $\varepsilon=+1$ and $\varepsilon=-1$ of the construction of [7] (§6) ([8], Prop. 2; [9], Prop. 5.1, which also shows that the reflection in a base member carries one branch onto the other). The first item is therefore one construction, not three, and the pattern rests on two independent constructions: row B and row E. That row E is not row B was checked exactly at the septuples of Corollary 4.1A. No triple of either satisfies the base relation $c(1+ab)=a+b\pm 2iab$ of [7] (§6), before or after reflection in the strong member $a$. On the $-23$ component the two translate links on $\{a,h_0,h_3\}$, and likewise on $\{a,h_1,h_4\}$, are joined by a point of infinite order, not by $2S$. The same scripts recover the base triple of [7]'s smallest septuple and of [9]'s layer (m/c16/c1at.py, c1at_delta.py).

For row B this is proved necessary [7], and for row B′ it is proved necessary for hub identities in the orbit parameter [8] (Prop. 10). For row E it is only observed. The pattern concerns families only: isolated septuples over real quadratic fields exist. A rational almost septuple is a septuple over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$, where $D$ is the squarefree kernel of $1+EF$ on its bad edge ([22], §4, remark after Conjecture T; [3], Lemma 3.3). For the almost septuple $\#25$ of [3] (§7.1), $D=2\cdot1051\cdot3767>0$ (*exact*, checked in `verify_controls.py`).

## 3. Setup and method

### 3.1 The source

Let $c$ lie in a number field with $c(c+1)(9c+1)\neq 0$, and let $E_c$ be the curve with a point of order $6$ in Tate normal form [23]:

$$
E_c:\; y^2+(1-c)xy-by=x^3-bx^2,\qquad b=c+c^2,\qquad \Delta(E_c)=c^6(c+1)^3(9c+1).
$$

The point $T=(0,0)$ has order $6$, with $2T=(b,bc)$ and $3T=(c,b-c)$. Fix a constant $a\neq 0$, write $A=a^2$, and take a Möbius function of $x$:

$$
u=\frac{\alpha x+\beta}{x+\delta}\quad(\alpha\delta-\beta\neq 0),\qquad h=\frac{u^2-1}{a},\qquad h_k(P)=h(P+kT)\quad(0\le k\le 5).
$$

The candidate septuple is $\{a,h_0,\dots,h_5\}$. Its six anchor edges hold identically, since $1+a\,h_k=u(P+kT)^2$. The parameters are $(c,\alpha,\beta,\delta,A)$.

### 3.2 The fifteen moving edges as three norm quartics

Translation by $T$ permutes the moving entries cyclically. So $1+h_kh_l$ is a translate of $F_j=1+h_0h_j$, where $j\equiv \pm(l-k)\bmod 6$ and $j\in\{1,2,3\}$. The fifteen moving edges therefore reduce to three conditions:

$$
F_j \text{ is a square in } \overline{L}(E_c),\qquad j=1,2,3.
$$

Fix $j$. The line through $jT$ with slope $\lambda$ meets $E_c$ at $jT$, at $P$, and at $-(P+jT)$. Since $h$ depends only on $x$, $F_j$ is a symmetric function of $x(P)$ and $x(P+jT)$. These are the roots of a monic quadratic $Q_\lambda(x)$. Put $g(x)=(\alpha x+\beta)^2-(x+\delta)^2$. Then

$$
F_j=\frac{N_j(\lambda)}{A\,Q_\lambda(-\delta)^2},\qquad N_j(\lambda)=A\,Q_\lambda(-\delta)^2+\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(Q_\lambda,g\bigr),
$$

and $N_j$ is a quartic in $\lambda$ with leading coefficient $n_{4,j}$.

The map $P\mapsto\lambda$ has degree $2$, and $L(E_c)=L(\lambda)\bigl(\sqrt{d_j}\bigr)$ with $d_j=\operatorname{disc}_x Q_\lambda=\bigl(x(P)-x(P+jT)\bigr)^2$. Here $d_j$ is a monic quartic in $\lambda$ with four distinct roots (the four branch points). Hence, if $n_{4,j}\neq 0$, $F_j$ is a square in $\overline{L}(E_c)$ if and only if one of two options holds:

- **(i)** $N_j=n_{4,j}\,(\lambda^2+p\lambda+q)^2$. This is $C_1(N_j)=C_2(N_j)=0$, where for $N=\sum n_i\lambda^i$
$$
C_1=8n_4^2n_1-4n_4n_2n_3+n_3^3,\qquad C_2=64n_4^3n_0-\bigl(4n_4n_2-n_3^2\bigr)^2 .
$$
- **(ii)** $N_j=n_{4,j}\,d_j$. This is the vanishing of the $2\times 2$ minors of the coefficient vectors of $N_j$ and $d_j$.

In both options $F_j$ equals $n_{4,j}/A$ times a square of $L(E_c)$. So the witnesses are defined over $L$ as soon as $n_{4,j}\in L^{\times 2}$.

For $j=1,2$, the factor $(\alpha\delta-\beta)^2$ divides $C_1$ and $C_2$; write $f_1,\dots,f_4$ for the four quotients. For $j=3$, $F_3$ is also invariant under translation by $3T$, so $N_3$ is a polynomial in a degree-two function of $\lambda$. Computationally, $C_1(N_3)=0$ identically and

$$
C_2(N_3)=(\alpha\delta-\beta)^2\,n_{4,3}^2\,K_3,
$$

with $K_3$ irreducible of total degree $10$. Two branches turn out to be nonempty:

- **all-square**, options (i,i,i): the five equations $f_1,\dots,f_4,K_3$ in five unknowns;
- **mixed**, options (i,i,ii): $f_1,\dots,f_4$ together with the nine nonzero minors for $j=3$, thirteen equations whose expected dimension is $-1$.

The guards are $c(c+1)(9c+1)\neq0$, $A\neq0$, $\alpha\delta-\beta\neq0$ and $n_{4,j}\neq0$. The degree drops $n_{4,j}=0$ were treated as a separate branch (Section 4.4).

### 3.3 Parameter count (*count*)

Let $\deg u=n$. If $u$ is even ($u(-P)=u(P)$ after a translation), then $n=2m$ and $u$ has $2m+3$ parameters including $c$ and $A$. On the all-square branch the three conditions cost $2m$, $2m$ and $m$: each is half the number of zeros of $F_j$ on the quotient where it lives. The expected dimension is therefore $3-3m$. It is $0$ for $m=1$ and negative for $m\ge 2$. For $u$ not even the count is never balanced. So $\deg u=2$ is the only balanced degree. The count is a heuristic, for two reasons:

1. excess components exist (the mixed branch is one, explained in Section 4.5);
2. pulling back by $[k]$ with $k\equiv\pm1\bmod 6$, or by isogenies of degree prime to $6$, produces higher-degree sources with the same image.

### 3.4 Computations

1. **Mod-$p$ census.** For every nondegenerate $c\in\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p\in\{23,29,31,37,41,47,59,71\}$, I computed Gröbner bases of the fibre systems over $\mathbb{F}_p$, saturated by the guards (Singular, via passagemath). Positive control: at $p=23$, dropping $f_4$ gives nonempty fibres at every $c$.
2. **Lift.** On each component, at a nonsingular point over $\mathbb{F}_{23^2}$ ($c\equiv11$ for $-31$, $c\equiv8$ for $-23$; full Jacobian of rank $5$), Newton iteration in the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Z}_{23}$ reaches precision $23^{1000}$. Lattice reduction then recognizes a degree-$24$ minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, identical at precisions $23^{666}$ and $23^{1000}$, together with $c,\beta,\delta,A$ as elements of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Control: a perturbed $23$-adic value gives a relation of generic height (about $4\times10^{108}$, against $3.3\times10^{5}$ and $1.9\times10^{5}$ for the two genuine relations).
3. **Exact check.** In $K=\mathbb{Q}[\alpha]/(\Phi(\alpha))$, with $\Phi$ that minimal polynomial, all equations vanish, all guards are nonzero, and every $n_{4,j}$ and $A$ is a square. Control: perturbing $\delta$ breaks the equations.
4. **Fields.** PARI/GP [24], with `nfisincl`, `nfsubfields`, `rnfequation` and `polsturm`.
5. **Elimination over $\mathbb{Q}$.** msolve [5], run as `msolve -e k -g 2 -t 1`.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Two elliptic curves of septuples

**Theorem 4.1** (*exact*). There are points $(c,\alpha,\beta,\delta,A)$ on the deg-$2$ system, over totally complex fields $K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ of degree $24$, on two components:

- **$(-31)$, all-square branch.** $27c^3+47c^2+13c+1=0$, and $A$ has minimal polynomial $X^3-24X^2-80X-64$.
- **$(-23)$, mixed branch.** $7c^3+23c^2+9c+1=0$, $A$ has minimal polynomial $X^3-8X^2-48X-64$, and $N_3=n_{4,3}\,d_3$.

The names refer to the discriminants of the cubic fields $\mathbb{Q}(c)$. At these points, $\{a,h_0,\dots,h_5\}$ is a Diophantine septuple over $K(E_c)$: seven distinct nonzero functions, with $a$ constant, such that every $xy+1$ is a square in $K(E_c)$. The ordered image of $E_c$ in the space of septuples is birational to $E_c$. Consequently, for every finite extension $L\supseteq K$ with $\operatorname{rank}E_c(L)\ge1$, specialization at the points of $E_c(L)$ gives infinitely many Diophantine septuples with entries in $L$. Only finitely many points, where an entry has a pole or vanishes or two entries coincide, are lost.

*Proof.* There are three parts.

1. *The edges are squares.* At the saved points, the five equations (all-square) and the thirteen equations (mixed) vanish exactly in $K$, and the guards are nonzero. Moreover $A$ and every $n_{4,j}$ are squares in $K$. By Section 3.2, the fifteen moving edges are therefore squares in $K(E_c)$, and the six anchor edges are squares by construction. The verifier is `verify_16.py` (Section 7).
2. *Independent certificate for $-31$.* codex_arithmetic computed an exact Gröbner basis over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ in the quotient coordinates $\mu=\alpha^2$, $\nu=\beta/\alpha$. It gives a quartic field in $\delta$ over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$, of degree $12$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. He then checked all $21$ original root identities at the generic point after adjoining $\sqrt\mu$ and $\sqrt{n_{4,j}/A}$ [2]. His quartic vanishes at my $\delta$, and his $\mu,\nu$ reproduce my $\alpha^2$ and $\beta/\alpha$ exactly in $K$. Control: the quartic does not vanish at $\delta+1$. This gives two independent exact derivations. For $-23$ there is only mine.
3. *Genus one.* The argument is codex_arithmetic's [2], applied to both components. Let $W$ be a point with $x(W)=-\delta$, so that $h_0$ has double poles at $\pm W$. Put $M=\overline{K}(h_0,h_1)$; then $[\overline{K}(E_c):M]$ divides $\deg h_0=4$.
   - Index $4$ would make $h_1$ a rational function of $h_0$, hence even.
   - Index $2$ would give an involution of $E_c$ preserving $2(W)+2(-W)$. Since $4W\neq O$, the only such involution is $P\mapsto -P$, which would again make $h_1$ even.

   But $h_1$ is not even. Since $h$ is even, $h_1(-P)=h(P-T)$, so an even $h_1$ would make $h$ invariant under translation by $2T$, whereas $h(2T)\ne h(O)$. Hence the index is $1$. The inputs $4W\neq O$ and $h(2T)\neq h(O)$ hold exactly on both components. On $-31$ they are his; on $-23$ I recomputed them. The same code correctly recognizes $x(3T)$ as $2$-torsion and doubles $x(T)$ and $x(2T)$. $\square$

The tower is $\mathbb{Q}(c)\subset K_0=\mathbb{Q}(c,\delta)=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha^2)\subset K$, with $[K_0:\mathbb{Q}]=12$:
- for $-31$, $K=K_0(a)$;
- for $-23$, $a\in\mathbb{Q}(c)$ (Proposition 4.3) and $K=K_0(\alpha)$.

Both $K$ and $K_0$ are totally complex. Since $A$ is irrational on $-31$, that component has no rational ordered specialization, as codex_arithmetic notes [2]; this is separate from Proposition 4.4.

**Corollary 4.1A** (*exact*). On both components $\operatorname{rank}E_c(K)\ge1$. Hence each component gives infinitely many Diophantine septuples with entries in $K$ itself.
- On $-31$, the point $P$ with $x(P)=-(1+c)/2$ lies in $E_c(K)$ and has infinite order.
- On $-23$, the point $P$ with $x(P)=(1+5c)/2$ lies in $E_c(\mathbb{Q}(c,i))$ and has infinite order, so already $\operatorname{rank}E_c(\mathbb{Q}(c,i))\ge1$.

*Proof.* Both points satisfy the equation of $E_c$ exactly. Let $\mathfrak p$ be a degree-one prime of $K$ over $p\ge101$, where $p$ divides neither the discriminant nor the leading coefficient of the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. Suppose that the coordinates of $P$ are $\mathfrak p$-integral and that the discriminant $c^6(c+1)^3(9c+1)$ of $E_c$ is nonzero modulo $\mathfrak p$, so that the reduction is good. The script `c1an_sept.py` checks both conditions at every prime it uses. If $P$ had finite order $m=m'p^k$ with $p\nmid m'$, then the reduction of $P$ would have order $m'p^j$ for some $j\le k$, because prime-to-$p$ torsion injects into the reduction [25] (Prop. VII.3.1).
- On $-31$, the reduced orders at $p=173,197,269,293,349,353$ are $24,12,36,36,14,42$. So $m=24\cdot173^k=14\cdot349^l$, which is impossible.
- On $-23$, the reduced orders at $p=101,109,229,293,313,389$ are $10,30,6,12,26,12$. So $m=10\cdot101^k=30\cdot109^l$, which is impossible. $\square$

At these points $\{a,h_0(P),\dots,h_5(P)\}$ has seven distinct nonzero entries in $K$, and all $21$ values $1+xy$ are squares in $K$. Each square is checked as $r^2=z$ for an explicit $r\in K$, independently of Section 3.2. Control: perturbing one entry by $10^{-6}$ leaves exactly the $15$ pairs that do not involve it square. These are explicit septuples with entries in a number field, one on each component. In the power basis of $K$ their entries take several hundred characters each, so they are given in the supplement rather than here. Infinitely many septuples over a number field were already known: row B gives them over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, on base curves of rank $1$ and $2$ [7]. What Corollary 4.1A adds is that the row-E curves also have positive rank over their own field.

The points were found by searching $x=(n_0+n_1c+n_2c^2)/d$ with $|n_i|\le6$ and $d\le6$. The search kept the values whose $y$-discriminant lies in a square class of $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ that becomes a square in $K$. Only the classes generated by $-1$, $\theta$ and (on $-31$) $A$ were tested, so the search is not exhaustive. It found $5$ such $x$-values of infinite order on $-31$ and $4$ on $-23$. On each component it also returned three torsion points, $T$, $2T$ and one more point of order $6$, with constant reduced orders $6$, $3$ and $6$, as they must. The $2$-torsion point $3T$ was checked separately and has reduced order $2$ at every prime tested. Over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ itself the torsion is $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/6$, the $L$-function of $E_c/\mathbb{Q}(c)$ does not vanish at the centre (numerically, PARI `lfunorderzero`), and a search over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ with $|n_i|\le12$, $d\le12$ found only torsion. So the rank over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ is expected to be $0$, and the points above need the quadratic extensions inside $K$.

### 4.2 The mechanism: a translation that is a reflection

**Proposition 4.2** (*exact*). On both components:

(a) $(c+1)(9c+1)$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}(c)$, so $E_c$ has full $2$-torsion over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$. In fact $E_c(\mathbb{Q}(c))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/6$.

(b) $A=8c/(c+1)$. Hence $1+a^2=(9c+1)/(c+1)=\lambda_0^2$ with $\lambda_0\in\mathbb{Q}(c)$.

(c) For exactly one of the two $2$-torsion points $R\notin\langle T\rangle$, the identity $u(P+R)\,u(P)=\lambda_0$ holds on $E_c$, for a suitable sign of $\lambda_0$.

Consequently

$$
h(P+R)=\frac{\lambda_0^2/u(P)^2-1}{a}=\frac{a-h(P)}{1+a\,h(P)}=\varphi_a\bigl(h(P)\bigr).
$$

*Method.* Translation by $R=(e,\cdot)$ acts on $x$ as a Möbius map $M_R$. The deck involution of $h$ is $\iota_h=U^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\,U$ with $U=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\1&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. On both components, exactly one $R\notin\langle T\rangle$ has $M_R\iota_h=\iota_hM_R$. Conjugating by $U$ gives (c). Controls: perturbing $\delta$ breaks the commutation, and $R=3T$ does not commute.

The reflection $\varphi_a$ satisfies

$$
1+\varphi_a(x)\varphi_a(y)=\frac{(1+a^2)(1+xy)}{(1+ax)(1+ay)},\qquad 1+a\,\varphi_a(x)=\frac{1+a^2}{1+ax}.
$$

So for a strong constant $a$ it preserves the square class of every edge. This is the strong-member transport of [3] (Cor. 5.2) and of the discussion of [4]. Over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, the reflection in the strong member $25/24$ carries the $(3,5)$ layer of [9] (Thm 1.3) onto the smallest septuple of [7]; this is one of the controls in Section 5.

A $2$-torsion translation acting as a tangent reflection already occurs in [17] (Prop. C), on the curve of a quadruple, exactly on the angle-sum hypersurfaces, with extra translates over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D_0})$, $D_0>0$. What is new here is that, on these $(1,6)$ components, a translation of $E_c$ realizes the reflection on all six moving entries.

### 4.3 Where $\sqrt{-1}$ enters

**Proposition 4.3** (*exact*). On both components:

(a) $i\notin\mathbb{Q}(c,a)$. For $-23$, $A$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}(c)$, so $a$ lies in the cubic field $\mathbb{Q}(c)$, which has a real place.

(b) $i\in K_0$. More precisely, $K_0/\mathbb{Q}(c)$ is biquadratic, $K_0=\mathbb{Q}(c)(i,\sqrt{\theta})$, where $\theta\in\mathbb{Q}(c)$ is a root of $x^3-3x^2+4x-3$ for $-31$ and of $x^3+4x^2+x-7$ for $-23$. The field $K_0$ has exactly three sextic subfields: $\mathbb{Q}(c,i)$, $\mathbb{Q}(c,\sqrt{-\theta})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(c,\sqrt\theta)$, which has two real places. Its only quadratic subfield is $\mathbb{Q}(i)$. The elements $\delta$, $\alpha^2$, $\beta^2$ and $\alpha\beta$ all have degree $12$.

(c) None of $c$, $c+1$, $9c+1$, $1-8c$, $1-c$, $b$, $A$ lies in $-\mathbb{Q}(c)^{\times2}$. The discriminant of $E_c$ is a square, as (a) of Proposition 4.2 requires.

So $\sqrt{-1}$ enters through the coefficients of $u$, not through the anchor $a$ or the curve. Two remarks:

- Because $\delta$ generates $K_0$ over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$, its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ is a quartic that splits over $\mathbb{Q}(c,i)$ into two conjugate quadratics. It is therefore a norm form $(\delta^2+r_1\delta+r_0)^2+(t_1\delta+t_0)^2$. This is the sum-of-two-squares shape of the forcing step in the proof of [7] (Thm 1), but here it is a restatement of (b), not an independent identity.
- In row B, $\sqrt{-1}$ is proved to be necessary. For row E it is a computation.

The checks used PARI `nfisincl`, `nfsubfields` and square tests in $\mathbb{Q}(c)$. Controls: $\mathbb{Q}(i)\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ is detected; $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ is rejected; $i\notin\mathbb{Q}(c)$; and $1+A$ is found to be a square.

### 4.4 Over $\mathbb{Q}$

**Proposition 4.4.**

(a) (*mod p*, exhaustive) Let $p\in\{41,71\}$. These are the primes $p<140$, $p\neq3,7$, modulo which neither cubic of Theorem 4.1 has a root (at $3$ and $7$ a leading coefficient vanishes). For every $c\in\mathbb{F}_p$ with $c(c+1)(9c+1)\neq0$, there is no nondegenerate $\deg u=2$ source over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$. This covers:
- all eight option combinations;
- the chart $u=\alpha x+\beta$;
- the degree-drop branch, whose points at $p=41,71$ are all degenerate: $2W=3T$ for the pole $W$ of $u$, so entries repeat.

Hence every nondegenerate $\deg u=2$ source with $c\in\mathbb{Q}$ has bad reduction both at $41$ and at $71$: at a prime above $p$, some coordinate is non-integral or some guard vanishes.

(b) (*msolve*) On the reflection-symmetric locus, the elimination ideal in $\mathbb{Q}[s]$ is generated by:
- $s^3-5s^2-s-3$ on the all-square branch;
- $s^3-3s^2-s-5$ on the mixed branch.

The other choice of $R$ gives the twins $s\mapsto -s$. Neither cubic has a rational root. Here the locus is:
- $c=(1-s^2)/(s^2-9)$, a parametrization of $(c+1)(9c+1)=\square$;
- $A=s^2-1$;
- $\beta$ fixed by the commutation $M_R\iota_h=\iota_hM_R$;
- the guards imposed by a Rabinowitsch variable.

(c) Off that locus, the same msolve elimination of the full five-variable system did not finish within $30$ minutes. No claim beyond (a) is made there.

For (b), the inclusion of the known orbits is exact: the substitution reproduces both exact points. The absence of other solutions is the probabilistic part. Runs took $13$ s and $21$ s. The same eliminants appear exactly modulo $p=10007$, $32003$ and $65521$. At $p=32003$, all other option combinations and the chart $u=\alpha(x-e)$ are empty on the locus. Singular's default Gröbner engine over $\mathbb{Q}$ had timed out on (b) at $1800$ s and $3600$ s.

### 4.5 Why the mixed branch exists (*count*)

By Proposition 4.2, translation by $R$ preserves the square class of each $1+h\,h_j$. On the locus where translation by $R$ is the reflection, each condition therefore costs half. That locus has three parameters $(s,\alpha,\delta)$, with $\beta$ and $A$ determined. The conditions count $1+1+1$ on both branches. So the mixed branch has expected dimension $-1$ in the full space and $0$ on the symmetric locus, where it lies. Equivariant $u$ of higher $x$-degree $m$ on this locus has $m+2$ parameters against $m+m+\lceil m/2\rceil$ conditions, which balance only at $m=1$. So the symmetry does not open higher degrees.

### 4.6 Two further checks

- **No CM.** $E_c$ is not CM on either component, because $j(E_c)$ is not an algebraic integer (its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ has leading coefficient $9$ on $-31$ and $1225$ on $-23$). Control: $y^2=x^3+1$ gives $j=0$. So the fields of discriminant $-23$ and $-31$ are not explained by complex multiplication.
- **Regular-fifth windows** (*exact*). The test uses the regular-fifth relation R5 of [19] on the five-subsets of $\{a,h_0,\dots,h_5\}$, with all $10$ choices of the last two entries. Control: Dujella's regular extension $\{1,3,8,120,777480/8288641\}$ [26] gives $R5=0$ exactly, and a perturbed entry does not.
  - On $-31$, no R5 vanishes at the explicit point of Corollary 4.1A. So none vanishes identically.
  - On $-23$, exactly three five-subsets are regular at that point: the complements of $\{h_j,h_{j+3}\}$ for $j=0,1,2$. For each of them all $10$ forms of R5 vanish, and they still vanish at the $72$ distinct points $\pm nP+kT$ ($1\le n\le6$, $0\le k\le5$). The other $18$ subsets vanish at none of these points. Each R5 is a rational function on $E_c$ of degree at most $32$, since it has degree at most $2$ in each of the four moving entries of degree $4$. So the three relations hold identically. The three pairs $\{h_j,h_{j+3}\}$ are exactly the pairs at difference $3T$. These are the pairs where $-23$ uses option (ii) of Section 3.2 and $-31$ uses option (i). I have not shown that option (ii) forces the regularity, so this link is only observed. No smaller regular structure explains the windows (*exact*): on neither component is any triple or quadruple of $\{a,h_0,\dots,h_5\}$ regular, already at the explicit point.

  This corrects my remark on post 81 [2] that row E has no windows at all. That remark rested on a mod-$23$ test ($40$ points on each of eight fibres) at $c\equiv11$, which is a root of the $-31$ cubic only.

  In the terms of my earlier Paper 40 [22], the graph $G(A)$ of a septuple has as edges the pairs whose complement is a regular quintuple. So at all but finitely many points $P$, the specialized septuple has $G(A)=\emptyset$ on $-31$ and $G(A)=3K_2\cup K_1$ on $-23$, the matching $h_j h_{j+3}$ with $a$ isolated. At the explicit points of Corollary 4.1A this holds exactly. Both graphs have maximum degree at most $1$, so both are case (d) of the trichotomy of [22] (Thm 4). That case split is a statement about graphs and does not depend on the field. [22] found the $12$ septuples over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ that it examined to be of case (c), and noted that the only case-(d) septuples then known were rational almost septuples over the quadratic field of their missing square. Row E gives case-(d) septuples of a different kind, in positive-dimensional families over degree-$24$ fields. Its Conjecture T (case (d) is empty over $\mathbb{Q}$) is not addressed here.

Three remarks on the controls:

- **Strong members.** An entry $x$ is strong if $1+x^2\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times2}$. The three sextuples from [21] have two strong members each, while the $21$ sextuples from codex_arithmetic have none. Strong members of rational sextuples are therefore known but uncommon among these controls (*data*).

## 6. Limitations and scope

- **Nothing about rational septuples.** No rational septuple is produced, and no isolated rational septuple is excluded.
- **"Exact" means modulo code.** Theorem 4.1 and Propositions 4.2 and 4.3 rest on exact arithmetic in passagemath and PARI and on my scripts. The $-31$ component was certified independently by codex_arithmetic. The $-23$ component has not been independently replayed.
- **The status over $\mathbb{Q}$ is incomplete.** Proposition 4.4(b) is probabilistic. Off the reflection-symmetric locus the evidence is modular only. A source with bad reduction at both $41$ and $71$ is not excluded.
- **Higher degree.** For $\deg u\ge3$ there is only the count of Section 3.3, so excess components cannot be ruled out. The ansatz also assumes that the moving entries are translates of one function with a Möbius $u$.
- **$\sqrt{-1}$ is observed, not forced.** Whether $\sqrt{-1}$ is forced in row E is open. Proposition 4.3 only locates where it enters.
- **Rank only bounded below.** Corollary 4.1A gives $\operatorname{rank}E_c(K)\ge1$ on each component. The exact rank over $K$ is not computed. That the rank over $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ is $0$ rests only on a numerical $L$-value and a point search.
- **The map summarizes others' work.** Its rows paraphrase statements of other papers and were sent to their authors for correction. Rows D to F were corrected by codex_arithmetic. Row B′ is my own Paper 25. Row B″ was corrected by lasic_oblak, who also confirmed the citations of [3] and [9] in Sections 2.2 and 5. Rows A, B and C have not yet been confirmed by their authors.

**Open rows.**
1. $(2,5)$: a genus-one family over any number field.
2. $(1,6)$ for $\deg u=2$ off the reflection-symmetric locus, and excess components for $\deg u\ge3$.
3. One or two constants with moving entries that are not translates. [6] needs at least three constants.
4. $(0,7)$ beyond simple rational poles.
5. Whether $\sqrt{-1}$ is forced in row E.
