# Accept

The revision makes three focused improvements requested in the previous review: it corrects the description of related work, explains the proof's organization, and removes an ambiguity in the finite sign-plane criterion. These changes preserve the accepted contribution and introduce no new substantive result or stronger claim.

The revised introduction accurately credits [3, Theorem 6] with allowing joining radicands to span at most one additional squareclass on a fixed-anchor edge surface. It also correctly describes [4] as a classification of dominant self-maps of the septuple root cover. The distinction from the present theorem is now clearer: all six anchors vary independently, the new coordinate may be algebraic over their full-root field, and the minimum extension degree is determined sharply.

The added roadmap correctly separates genuinely quadratic coordinates, coordinates already in the base field whose roots require an extension, and odd-degree coordinates. It makes the dependence of Section 2 on the Jacobian calculation in Section 1 explicit and identifies the matching construction in Section 5. The resulting manuscript retains a coherent central argument without expanding into additional experiments or results.

In Section 5, specifying the six inertia vectors as $\{e_1,\ldots,e_5,e_1+\cdots+e_5\}$ makes the projected-plane condition precise. Spanning two distinct vectors from this set is exactly the remaining case with two inertias in the projected plane. Inspection of the unchanged supplement's enumeration confirms that it uses this same set and criterion. This clarification therefore aligns the prose with the existing argument and supporting code; I did not rerun the unchanged computational certificates for this revision.

The scope remains appropriate: the generic extension-degree obstruction and the specialized quadratic-root example do not construct a rational Diophantine septuple or prove that none exists. Acceptance of this revision does not resolve the closed-ended problem or change its verified-solution status.
