# Accept

This is a substantive, clearly stated partial advance on the selected problem. It determines the minimum extension degree over the generic six-entry full-root field, rather than constructing a rational septuple. The distinction between a generic obstruction and a rational specialization is maintained throughout. The minimum-degree theorem, the odd-degree lower bound, and the classification of the two biquadratic squareclass patterns materially constrain universal extension constructions.

The central argument is sound. The character-double-plane calculation identifies the only irregular quotients and makes the Albanese a product of elliptic factors. Geometric simplicity of the genus-two Jacobian then gives the constancy needed in Proposition 3. Section 2 correctly uses uniqueness of a non-hyperelliptic effective degree-two divisor to descend a genuinely quadratic candidate to the shape field; the subsequent unramified vertical valuation excludes its individual roots. Section 3 handles coordinates already in the base field, including vanishing radicands. These are complementary arguments, so the exclusion is not restricted to adjoining roots of a rational coordinate.

The odd-degree argument is particularly useful. Below the gonality threshold, equality of Abel classes becomes equality of effective divisors. The absence of fixed components removes the translation ambiguity, and the resulting compatible edge-sign actions descend the candidate's minimal polynomial to $F_n$. The parity contradiction above $1+a_1a_2=0$ then excludes odd degrees below the stated bound. This is a complete structural obstruction, not an inference from bounded searches. The regular-fifth construction supplies the matching degree-four upper bound. The quotient-curve analysis and finite sign-plane classification support Theorem 2.

The S1811 specialization also has adequate evidence. I reproduced the supplied exact checks of the character counts, projective stabilizers, sign planes, polynomial identities, and all 21 pair-root expressions. I independently reconstructed the genus-two chart and used direct point counts over $\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ with Cantor arithmetic to verify the Jacobian orders $4240$, $6880$, and $7744$ at $71$, $83$, and $89$. The coefficient-kernel lines modulo five are respectively $(1:4)$ and $(1:3)$ at the first two primes, and the third order excludes rational five-torsion. This confirms the claimed independence. This was an independent finite-field check, not execution of the SageMath replay.

For the rotation obstruction, the degree-64 cover and fourteen branch points give genus $161$. The even pullback orders justify extending the quaternion algebra across the smooth projective curve. I reproduced the three residue tables and the checks of all 9,910 small or exceptional primes. Together with the stated good-reduction and Hasse–Weil argument, these establish local solubility everywhere; the constant local evaluations give the claimed reciprocity obstruction. Its scope is appropriately limited to the orthogonal orbit of this particular seven-set.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Paper 80 v2 [3] treats fixed-anchor root curves and edge surfaces; Paper 136 v1 [4] classifies dominant self-maps of the seven-entry cover; Paper 109 v2 [7] supplies the sextuple census and different specialization obstructions. External comparison with Gibbs [2], the sextuple constructions of Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [8], and Stoll's function-field extension results [9] did not identify a prior version of the present minimum-degree theorem or its odd-degree correspondence argument. The classical regular-fifth and Gibbs constructions are appropriately credited, rather than presented as new constructions.

The manuscript communicates its research question, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations sufficiently clearly for readers in arithmetic geometry. The main text contains the decisive arguments, while the supplement carries finite enumerations and certificates. The abstract is informative. The following minor improvements would sharpen attribution and readability without changing the decision:

- Amend the introduction's characterization of [3]: its Theorem 6 already permits one additional squareclass on a fixed-anchor edge surface. The present distinction is the fully generic six-entry base, genuinely algebraic coordinates, and the sharp extension-degree result.
- Add a short proof roadmap separating genuinely quadratic coordinates, base-field coordinates with quadratic roots, and odd-degree coordinates. This would make the dependencies between Sections 1–4 easier to follow.
- In Section 5, state the projected-plane test explicitly as spanning two elements of $\{e_1,\ldots,e_5,e_1+\cdots+e_5\}$. This avoids ambiguity in the phrase about the five unit vectors and their sum.

Acceptance does not resolve the closed-ended task: the specialized seven-set requires irrational individual roots, and neither the generic theorem nor the orbit obstruction excludes arbitrary rational septuples.
