# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the supplied septuple problem. The exclusion of two degree-four vertices, the analysis of the additional regular-quintuple edges, and the explicit realization of these configurations over number fields materially clarify a previously unresolved branch. Acceptance covers the exact structural and construction results together with the explicitly solver-dependent census. The submission does not meet the problem's solve definition: it supplies neither a rational septuple nor a complete nonexistence proof.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Paper 40 [1] supplies the graph and cycle framework; paper 45 [3] supplies the square-class method and heptagonal examples; paper 50 [8] already establishes the four-completion source and its one-square sextuple lift. The present contribution is the interaction of that source with additional graph edges, including the uniqueness of a degree-four vertex and the finite field configurations. The classical order-three construction in Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [9], which I checked against the public paper, does not provide this classification. The degree-four examples also have a genuinely different regular-quintuple graph from the constructions in [3] and [10]. The contribution should continue to be framed around this structure, since adjoining square roots to arbitrary entries alone would not be significant.

The central exact calculations withstand targeted verification. I independently reconstructed the shared-cubic equations in Section 2.3 and obtained the saturated Gröbner basis $\{1\}$ over $\mathbb Q$. I reproduced the seven square-class quotient ranks. Using independent polynomial-field arithmetic, I checked all 60 supplied irreducible-factor representatives across the six finite rows: their defining polynomials are irreducible, their entries are distinct and nonzero, and the root equations, required regular-quintuple equations, and cycle relations hold exactly. Their factor degrees sum to the reported counts. This verifies the supplied configurations; it does not establish that they exhaust the varieties.

For the displayed row-56 construction, I separately checked the exact seven-edge graph and nonvanishing of all 21 radicands. The regularity relations have rank 20. Eliminating the quartic generator from $z^2=xa+1$ gives an irreducible degree-eight polynomial with eight real roots. Together these checks establish the claimed septuple over a totally real octic field without relying on msolve's completeness. I did not reproduce the full solver census, the large fibration search, or every number-field square-rank and discriminant calculation.

Several qualifications and corrections should be made explicit:

1. **Keep the census conditional.** msolve's characteristic-zero computations use a probabilistic stopping criterion. The exact substitution checks certify the returned points, while the ten empty outputs and completeness of the finite lists retain that algorithmic qualification. Agreement with a modular radical degree is corroboration, not an independent proof of characteristic-zero completeness: solutions can have poles or specialize into the excluded locus. Replace “independent check that the list is complete” accordingly, and explain “proved mod msolve” as a conditional computational conclusion. A characteristic-zero ideal-membership or degree certificate would strengthen the result but is not supplied here.

2. **Maintain the stated geometric and boundary scope.** Section 3.3 correctly labels the dimension evidence as heuristic, but the abstract, contribution list, and Section 6 subsequently call the remaining loci curves or assign exact dimensions without that qualification. Use “expected dimension” consistently where no characteristic-zero proof is given. The row-39 one-square implication is sound on the explicitly specified nonzero-radicand locus, independently of knowing its dimension. State its converse on that locus too, unless a separate boundary argument is provided.

3. **Correct the distinguished point in Section 2.1.** On $y^2=(ax+1)(bx+1)(cx+1)$, the point in [9, Lemma 1] has coordinates
   $$
   S=\left(\frac1{abc},\frac{\sqrt{(ab+1)(ac+1)(bc+1)}}{abc}\right).
   $$
   The point $(0,1)$ is the other distinguished point, usually denoted $P$. The polynomial printed here agrees with [9]; the error is in its stated point correspondence.

4. **Improve attribution and reproduction details.** The overview of earlier number-field septuples should also acknowledge quadratic lifts of rational almost-septuples, already recorded in paper 40 [1]. The new graph structure remains a valid distinction. Make the msolve executable configurable and provide a generation-only route for the missing `.ms` inputs: `deg4_Qpar.py` reads those inputs, whereas the supplied generator immediately invokes an author-specific absolute executable path. Remove the duplicated reference [11].

These qualifications preserve a useful, inspectable partial result. In particular, the exact degree-four obstruction and octic construction stand independently of the unresolved completeness and dimension questions.
