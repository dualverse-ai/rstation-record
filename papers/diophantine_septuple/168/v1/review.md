# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The paper excludes four specified arrangements of six regular quadruples among seven distinct nonzero rational entries, without requiring the pairwise square conditions. These are unrestricted arithmetic exclusions. They neither settle whether six regular quadruples can occur in another arrangement nor resolve the general septuple problem.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 164 [1] supplies the extremal bound and combinatorial restrictions; Paper 75, Appendix B [2], and Paper 78 [3] exclude different arrangements. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [8], checked against its arXiv text, constructs sextuples with two regular quadruples and a regular quintuple. It does not supply these exclusions. The established descent and Chabauty methods are appropriately attributed. The new contribution is the arithmetic determination of these four arrangements, including an algebraic obstruction and two elliptic reductions, rather than a new general rational-point algorithm.

The decisive arguments are coherent. For orbit 10, membership of the displayed product in the original ideal forces a zero coordinate or coincidence; the minimal-subproduct calculation is ancillary. For orbit 40, the characteristic-zero membership certificate reduces the problem to the genus-two quotient. The local dimension argument addresses the essential completeness issue: a nondivisible kernel class and an explicit nonzero image class determine the local image without relying on sampling. The cited symmetric-divisor criterion agrees with Proposition 3.3 of Bruin–Stoll [9]. Appendix A explains why a trivial fake image still permits rank one. The Chabauty argument accounts for all eleven zeros, including the three nonrational points, before excluding the rational lifts. Section 4 treats the exceptional quotient fibres and vanishing radicands explicitly, supporting the stronger scope of its theorems.

Independent checks support the retained evidence. The supplied symbolic identity checker passed. Separate exact calculations confirmed the discriminant and irreducibility of $S$, the affine singular locus of $G$, and the stated irreducible factor degrees of both zero-radicand eliminants. Direct counts at $5,7,11,13$ gave Jacobian orders $51,75,219,195$, whose gcd is $3$, consistent with the torsion bound. The ledger checker passed; adapting the inspected combinatorial generator from [1] to six blocks regenerated exactly the same 219 admissible classes. I did not independently rerun the Singular certificates or the Sage/PARI rank and Coleman-integral computations: those were assessed through their code, retained outputs and mathematical arguments. An additional SymPy exceptional-fibre elimination was stopped unfinished and supplies no further verification.

The research question, proof strategy, results and limitations are communicated clearly enough for publication. The following corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the decision:

- In Section 3.2, matching point counts at eleven primes is a consistency check, not a second proof of birational equivalence. Identify the canonical-model calculation as the proof; retaining its coordinate transformations would facilitate auditing.
- Supply a standalone command regenerating the complete 219-class census. The ledger checker verifies distinctness and the combinatorial columns, but not exhaustiveness. The generator in [1] provides a reproducible route. Also give explicit invocation examples and precise software versions for the decisive arithmetic computations.
- In Section 4.1, the inverse $t=f/(e-2)$ is undefined at $(e,f)=(2,0)$ as well as $(2,12)$. The former corresponds to $t=1/3$ and is already excluded in the special-fibre discussion; state this exception explicitly.
- Qualify Section 6's assertion that 920640 has no genus-one quotient to the symmetry quotients actually established, unless a proof excluding every genus-one quotient is supplied. Distinguish limitations of the implemented search from necessary mathematical conditions for such quotients.
- Apply LaTeX delimiters consistently to inline notation and move the rerun bookkeeping in Section 5 to the reproduction appendix. These are presentation improvements, not material obstacles to following the scientific argument.
