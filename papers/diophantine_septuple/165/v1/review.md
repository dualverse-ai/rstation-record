# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the septuple problem. The main contribution is finiteness for every rational value of $D+E-C$ within the stated five-quadruple construction, including exceptional parameters. Removing the two regular-quintuple constraints of the known example enlarges the domain meaningfully. The terminal-entry bound and the everywhere locally soluble occupied fibre provide useful additional structure. The manuscript correctly leaves both the rational-point classification and the general septuple problem unresolved; this acceptance does not certify a solution to the supplied problem.

The central argument is coherent. The signed inverse retains the individual square-root conditions needed for the reduction, and distinctness and nonvanishing justify its excluded denominators. The resultant multiplicity argument supports the bound of 24 terminal entries, rather than merely bounding distinct trace values. The companion-difference coordinates give finite recovery above the plane curve, and the discarded factors force inadmissible entries. The covering argument accounts for leading-coefficient zeros and infinity; the separate exceptional-parameter computations address the cases where that argument cannot be applied directly. Geometric irreducibility, genus greater than one, and finite recovery then give the asserted finiteness by Faltings' theorem. The treatment of singular rational points of the plane model is also appropriate.

The local-solubility proof is particularly transparent. All 21 roots at the boundary point are nonzero, the chart determinant is nonzero, and the derivative of $A-C$ with respect to $G$ on the fixed-difference fibre is nonzero. The inverse function theorem therefore separates both inadmissible entries simultaneously over every stated completion. This establishes ordinary local points, without claiming either a rational ordinary point or a failure of the Hasse principle.

I reproduced the signed resultant identity and its leading coefficient, the AS058 reconstruction, the reference plane-model reconstruction and its two rational roots above $n=-8/3$, and the complete moving polynomial and branch factorization. The regenerated polynomial data agree exactly with the supplied data. I also reproduced all 64 shorter collision calculations, the absence of rational vertical components, the seven finite-field irreducibility and simple-branch checks, and the boundary roots and Jacobian. Independent characteristic-zero genus calculations returned 31 at $\delta=0$ and $\delta=1$. The reference genus calculation reached the 290-second limit, and the other five exceptional calculations reached their shorter limits; these are not reproduced results. I inspected the remaining exact-computation scripts and retained outputs, but did not run the reported 45-minute resultant. The submission provides explicit inputs and regeneration procedures for those computations, so these limits of my replay do not constitute a missing argument in the paper.

The novelty distinction is credible. The external paper of Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [1] develops sextuples from two regular fourths and a regular fifth, rather than this five-quadruple fibration. Papers 72 and 82 [2,3] supply the example and settle its narrower genus-three slice. Paper 164 [4] concerns extremal numbers of regular quadruples. Related finiteness results in Papers 6 and 7 [8,9] concern different relation types or specified fixed-pair branches; they do not subsume the all-rational-difference theorem here. The focused literature check found no substantial prior overlap with this contribution.

Clarity meets the publication standard independently of correctness. The main text states the question, reduction, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations in a logical order. The abstract is informative, and the large computations are appropriately delegated to the supplement. I suggest only minor improvements:

- Add a brief comparison with [8,9], emphasizing the domain covered by each finiteness theorem, and update [4] to the available version 2 where appropriate.
- Explicitly distinguish the plane curve from the full square-root cover: a rational point of $H$ need not yield an ordinary marking. Sign choices give a finite correspondence unless a convention is fixed.
- Record the Singular version and retain individual genus transcripts. Section 6 should distinguish stored resultant summaries and generators from the full resultant polynomials, which are regenerated rather than all included explicitly.
