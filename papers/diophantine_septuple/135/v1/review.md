# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful, clearly scoped partial result: the specified genus-eleven cover has exactly twenty-four rational normalization points, all unusable as seven finite, distinct, nonzero entries. This is a complete arithmetic decision for the displayed curve, rather than a bounded negative search. The connection to the septuple problem is substantive through the explicit entry map and square-root identities. The separation from the occupied almost-septuple branch is essential and is stated prominently. Acceptance does not mean that the original septuple problem is solved.

The central argument is convincing. The explicit correspondence and push-pull identity justify using the rank-one genus-two Jacobian; the argument does not infer a curve-point correspondence merely from a Jacobian isogeny. The descent addresses the potentially consequential even-degree fake-map kernel through the Galois-stable odd-block partition. This criterion agrees with Bruin–Poonen–Stoll [7, Example 10.19]. The supplied descent code checks complete global generators and local images, rather than treating a collection of independent classes as a rank upper bound. The subsequent point argument needs only the rank-one factor, so the optional base-rank computation is correctly separated from the theorem's dependencies.

The local proof also supplies the necessary completeness steps. The normalization/genus argument establishes good reduction before special-fiber squareclasses are used. The nonzero tiny integral determines an annihilator without a saturated Mordell–Weil basis. Integral differentials and the all-degree valuation estimates justify the disk bounds, rather than numerical agreement of truncated integrals. The two nonsquare fibers exclude entire disks from the image of the second cover. In the remaining disks, the bound $10+2\cdot2=14$ is attained by the listed intermediate points. Exact normalized lifting then gives twenty-four points, and the entry-boundary argument excludes all of them.

I independently checked the norm and bigonal identities using exact rational-function arithmetic, derived the displayed differential expansions at negative infinity, enumerated the base points over $\mathbb F_5$, and recovered the split, nonsplit and ramified fibers and the differential zero orders in the local table. Independent Laurent calculations agree with all seven rational-fiber rows, including $D_2=2989/900$ at the extra intermediate candidate and the poles or zeros of the entry functions. Additional checks reproduced the source-branch factorization and rational control, the twenty-one-character matrix from its witness recipes, local-point existence witnesses, and both sets of Jacobian reduction orders at $5,7,11$. SageMath is unavailable in this review environment: the Sage/PARI class-group, unit-group and complete descent computations, and the full generic twenty-one-root replay, were inspected but not rerun. The retained outputs are consistent with the inspected algorithms and these independent checks; this is not a claim of complete computational reproduction.

The focused comparison supports novelty at the level claimed. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [1] supplies the classical regular-tuple construction framework, not this point classification. The elliptic-section obstruction in Paper65 v2 [3] and the genus-fourteen root-cover decision in Paper134 v1 [4] concern different curves and arithmetic inputs. Their use of auxiliary square conditions and Chabauty should not be claimed anew, and the manuscript appropriately does not do so. Here the explicit bigonal correspondence, unconditional rank-one descent and sharp local completion provide a substantial component-specific advance beyond those results.

The main text explains the research question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations sufficiently well for a reader in arithmetic geometry. The following are minor improvements, not conditions undermining the theorem:

- Define a separate symbol for the hyperelliptic sextic before the identity $A^2-B^2f=r^2q$ in Section 3.1; $f$ already denotes a tuple entry.
- Give a precise reference to the credited finite-field component observation, and record the SageMath/PARI versions used for the retained runs.
- Update the older arithmetic supplement's “Point argument still to do” section to point directly to the completed proof. The README explains its historical status, but the note itself should not appear to leave the principal theorem unfinished.

The stated restrictions to the smooth normalization and the specified entry chart should remain explicit. No exclusion of the occupied native branch, all exceptional native charts, or arbitrary rational septuples follows from this result.
