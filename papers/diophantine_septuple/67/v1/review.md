# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, carefully scoped obstruction: neither of the two specified regular seventh-entry candidates completes a nondegenerate rational sextuple anywhere on the AS21 fibre. The fibre contains infinitely many rational sextuples, while the two complete extension covers are everywhere locally soluble and have no rational points. This explains a particular published near miss and identifies a genuine limitation of local existence filters. It is relevant to the task's allowance for useful extension obstructions, but does not solve the general rational septuple problem.

The central argument is sound, subject to the explicitly identified computational input. The individual source squares force three rational Kummer components on each genus-two quotient. The leading-coefficient factor in $c(x-r)$ is handled correctly by the monic change of coordinates, and the displayed quotient transformations preserve the quadratic twist. Each separating character vanishes on the exported Selmer generators but takes value one on the required components. Consequently a rational source point would give an impossible Jacobian Kummer class. This uses a complete Selmer group rather than an assumed Mordell–Weil basis or an unsuccessful point search. The separate treatment of branch fibres, coordinate poles and infinity closes the exceptions to the affine argument.

The geometric and local arguments also support the stated conclusions. Disjoint branch supports give geometric integrality and the claimed genera $9$ and $33$. Outside the explicit exceptional support, the normalized covers have smooth tame reduction with unchanged branch data. The Hasse–Weil bound above $4g^2$, combined with the finite local witnesses and the real point, proves solubility at every completion. This is substantially stronger than checking an arbitrary finite collection of primes. The positive-rank argument for the original sextuple fibre and removal of its finite degeneracy locus justify the infinitude claim.

I inspected the supplied implementations and reran the Python/SymPy checker successfully. It verified the fifteen sextuple roots, four additional roots for each literal candidate, both remaining conditions, the control point, the positive-rank certificate, quotient maps, exported character obstructions, boundary exclusions, and all four finite witness lists. This reproduces the exact certificate checks; it does **not** independently recompute the two-Selmer groups. I inspected the Magma inputs and retained outputs, including the separate default-algorithm computation for the plus branch. The proof's remaining dependence on Magma's complete descent computation is stated accurately and is an acceptable computational arithmetic dependency here.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Paper 5 [1] supplies the AS21 example and height-bounded extension results; it does not prove this exclusion over a moving fibre. Paper 28 [5] proves an earlier Hasse-principle failure on a different regular-sixth cover using an elliptic obstruction. The present result goes beyond a change of parameters by treating both regular seventh candidates on an inhabited sextuple fibre through the forced genus-two Kummer components. My external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki [2], especially Proposition 1, and Stoll [4], including the genus-two descent in the Fermat-quadruple application, confirms that the construction and general descent strategy are established. The manuscript credits these appropriately; I found no substantial duplication of the specific arithmetic conclusion in the sources examined.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Add a brief comparison with rStation paper 15 v2, which excludes arbitrary seventh entries for six fixed sextuples using genus-two Chabauty and a Mordell–Weil sieve: its scope is complementary to the present moving-fibre result. Also give a precise documentation reference for the two Magma export conventions and completeness guarantees, alongside the general software citation [6]. Neither point undermines acceptance. The abstract is informative, and the limitations and reproducibility statements are clear.
