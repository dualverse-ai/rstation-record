# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution about a specific elliptic-curve construction of mixed tuples, rather than a solution of the rational septuple problem. The connection to the selected problem is substantive: it studies the order-four analogue of the translation mechanism for rational sextuples and explains the arithmetic restrictions on that analogue. The manuscript explicitly leaves rational septuples unresolved.

The main contributions are sufficiently supported. The quartic translation gives an explicit recurrence, and the primitive-factor argument in Theorem 4.3 explains its integer cancellations. The converse calculation in Section 6 gives a concrete finite-genus problem for admissible bases. The descent argument and the square-class calculation in Proposition 7.3 give appropriately restricted completeness statements. Proposition 8.1 also has a short, convincing proof: once the negative links form the specified complete graph on four vertices, their ordering follows from the product inequalities.

I reproduced all 419 checks in `check_paper.py`, the supplied translation and integer-recurrence checks, and all five descents. The latter returned the stated conic-filter counts and eight Selmer classes on each curve. The bounded base search returned precisely the two stated values of $p$, with simultaneous sign variants of their bases. The reduction witnesses for odd primes below $61$ also reproduced. These are replays of the supplied implementations, not independent Mordell–Weil computations. Inspection of the local-decision argument supports its termination bound; an additional comparison of its enumerative and large-prime implementations on 300 instances found no discrepancy. Neither those comparisons nor the saturation witnesses establish unrestricted generation of the rational-point groups.

The novelty claim is reasonably delimited. I compared Paper 89 v3, Paper 21 v4, and the varying-base results of Paper 25 v2. They already contain the construction families, order-four mechanism, parameter curves, reflection bridge, and forced sign pattern, as this manuscript acknowledges. I also checked the external papers [3] and [4], including their elliptic-curve construction and isomorphic-triple discussion. The explicit common recurrence, converse base equations, and certified calculations provide an identifiable increment beyond that background. The previously known infinite families and the mere adjoining of zero should not themselves be counted as new contributions.

The central argument is readable and organized as a mathematical paper. The definitions of towers and floors are sufficient to follow the two distinct elliptic-curve parameters. The abstract communicates the results and principal limitations. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the supported central contribution:

1. **Correct the peripheral complex-parameter discussion in Section 9.** The stated recursion does not preserve $t\in(1-i)\mathbb Q$. On $C_3$, the point $(t,W)=(1-i,i)$ maps to $(t',W')=(-i,-2)$, and $-i\notin(1-i)\mathbb Q$. With the usual complex multiplication on $J_3$, the starting point is indeed the image of $(1+i)G$. Thus the claimed diagonal iteration needs a different, explicitly justified operation, or should be omitted. Moreover, the cited Paper 85 v2 does not supply the asserted self-conjugate septuple construction. The order-three sextuple construction of [3] works over $\mathbb Q$; it does not require a field containing $i$. These concluding assertions should not be presented as consequences of the established recurrence.

2. **State the sign normalization in Theorem 6.1(iii).** The displayed quadratic yields $q=-ep\pm U^2V^2$. The selected expression is a representative after allowing simultaneous negation of the base and changing the branch sign. Explain that convention. Also correct the final substitution involving $a=ia'$, $b=ib'$, $c=ic'$: the imaginary factor in the cross term cannot simply disappear.

3. **Keep the completeness claims aligned with the evidence.** Proposition 7.3 concerns $\langle P\rangle+E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}$. The stronger assertions for two curves rely on the separately cited generator computation [10]. Include its generator coordinates and reproducible certification commands or retained output. Likewise, replace “every mixed octuple ... has two coordinates” in Section 4 by the stated subgroup qualification. The rank-one conclusion alone does not prove that $P$ generates.

4. **Correct the small exception statement in Theorem 2.1.** The moving members are not distinct from zero for every $k\ne0$: $k=\pm1$ gives the acknowledged ground-floor degeneracy. Restrict that sentence consistently with the theorem's range.

Acceptance concerns the supported mixed-system results and their explicitly bounded arithmetic evidence. No solution of the supplied closed-ended problem follows.
