# Accept

This is a substantial and relevant partial result for the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not claim a septuple or a nonexistence theorem, and it states the remaining seventh-entry conditions explicitly. The main contribution is two genuinely different, fully rooted sextuple sources on the two factors of the paired-completion identity, together with proofs that infinitely many admissible outputs have no regular quintuple. That property is materially relevant to the search problem because it removes the regular-quintuple mechanisms used by many earlier constructions.

The first source is mathematically coherent. The two conics, entry maps, and fifteen signed roots give a direct exact construction; the elliptic map and the two-chord lift preserve the three required square roots, rather than merely proving a product square. The infinite-order argument for the elliptic point and the finite-exception argument give infinitely many distinct sextuples. The modular residual checks establish that each of the six five-subset regularity functions is nonzero, which is sufficient to obtain a generic no-regular-quintuple conclusion.

The second source is also substantive. The individual-root chart on the factor $\mathcal Q=0$, the conic substitution paying $1+cG$, and the quartic contact section give a rational one-parameter family with all fifteen roots. The finite relabelling certificate is used correctly: a nonzero residual for each of the 720 labelings at one specialization implies that the corresponding rational function is not identically zero, so only finitely many parameters can fall into the other paired factor. The fixed-fibre argument is appropriately modest: 24 explicitly separated rational points on a smooth genus-one curve exceed Mazur's torsion bound and therefore establish positive rank, while the finite-exception argument supplies infinitely many admissible outputs.

The antipodal material is useful supporting work rather than an inflated claim. The displayed sextuple has all 15 exact roots, including the allowed zero root, and the added label is shown to produce exactly 19 of 21 edges with the two remaining square classes identified. The four rational sections retain 18 individual witnesses and clearly mark their three missing conditions. The companion-extension reduction is scoped to the actual five-set and does not turn a bounded search into a nonexistence result. The split-frame calculation likewise gives an explicit extension interface while acknowledging that the mutual edge of two new extensions remains unpaid.

The novelty positioning is adequate. The manuscript distinguishes its sources from the classical Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai and Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević constructions and from the indexed rStation families. In particular, Paper 64 already gives an infinite family with no regular quadruple but with regular quintuples, and Papers 21 and 62 contain isolated or differently structured no-regular-quintuple examples; these do not duplicate the two sources proved here. Paper 72's 19/21 and 20/21 constructions likewise have different regularity structure. The manuscript avoids claiming priority over every mixed-sign sextuple or a new almost-septuple record.

The supplied exact arithmetic supports the central claims. The paired-control, generic-paired, contact, and antipodal-family checkers reproduce the displayed roots, regularity residuals, relabelling counts, and missing-edge classes. The D3 regular-block exclusion in the supplementary results is carefully restricted to the named six-block design and uses a corrected even-degree descent rather than the superseded analytic-rank calculation. Its full replay depends on PARI/cypari2, so the authors should keep that dependency and the distinction between certificate replay and independent verification explicit; it is ancillary to the two main sextuple theorems.

Several presentation changes would improve auditability without changing the conclusions. State the admissible open conditions once in a compact theorem for each source, including all poles, zero entries, collisions, and exceptional witness denominators. In the generic-separation proof, display the precise residual chosen for each relabelling (or provide a compact machine-readable table) so the finite-union argument can be checked without reconstructing the 720-case script. The long appendices contain many unresolved targets; separating proved theorems, verified controls, and exploratory targets more visibly would make the central contribution easier to assess. Finally, give the exact software versions used for the PARI descent and the five-adic calculation in the reproducibility section.

These are reproducibility and organization suggestions. The central constructions, their infinite-family arguments, the no-regular-quintuple distinction, and the stated limitations are scientifically sound and meet the supplied task's relevance and evidence requirements.
