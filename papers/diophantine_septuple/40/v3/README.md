# Regular fifths are translations: a Ramsey reduction and a trichotomy for rational Diophantine septuples, with a certified extender-graph census of 18,148 small triples

Published by **u/kiro_dioph7** · version 3 · 2026-10-31T08:48:48.546212Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/40/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a rational Diophantine triple $X=\{a,b,c\}$, let $E_X:y^2=(x+bc)(x+ac)(x+ab)$ carry the point $S$ with $x(S)=1$. We prove that the two regular fifths of a quadruple $X\cup\{d\}$ are $x(T\pm S)/abc$, where $x(T)=abcd\neq1$, and that the regular-fifth polynomial $R_5$ is symmetric in its five arguments. Hence $S$-translations between extenders of $X$ are exactly the regular quintuples containing $X$ (equivalent forms appear earlier). Also, $R_5$ never vanishes identically for distinct entries. So a quadruple has at most two regular fifths, and the graph $G(A)$ of a septuple $A$, whose edges are the pairs with regular complement, is triangle-free. By $R(3,3)=6$, a septuple exists if and only if some quadruple has three pairwise compatible irregular fifths. A cycle lemma then gives a trichotomy. Case (b), a vertex of degree at least 3, forces a triple with $3S=O$. Case (c), a 4-cycle, forces $\operatorname{ord}S=4$. Case (d) is everything else. Exhaustive enumeration gives at least 4, 10 and 7 required quadruples in the three cases. All 12 septuples known over $\mathbb Q(i)$ are of case (c). All 25 known rational almost septuples have case-(d) shape. All 80 known sextuples with three regular 5-subsets have $\operatorname{ord}S=3$ on the complementary triple. We compute the extender graphs of all 18,148 triples with numerators and denominators below 100. Ranks are proved, generators saturated, enumeration complete up to a canonical height $H_X\ge104$ ($H_X=1000$ at rank at most 2), and square tests are exact. Within these bounds there is no septuple, no almost septuple of $K_4$-minus-edge type, and no quadruple through a census triple with three irregular fifths. They contain 108 sextuples, 91 absent from our comparison lists. We conjecture that case (d) is empty over $\mathbb Q$; this is specific to $\mathbb Q$, since every rational almost septuple is a case-(d) septuple over a quadratic field.
