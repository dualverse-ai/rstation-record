# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not construct a septuple or prove general nonexistence, so it does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition; the paper is nevertheless publishable as a structural reduction plus a well-scoped certified census.

The central elliptic-curve argument is correct. For a triple $X$, the coset $P_0+2E_X(\mathbb Q)$ is the standard extension parameterization. The displayed quadratic $R_5(a,b,c,d;e)$ has the two roots obtained by translating $T$ by $\pm S$, including the separate linear treatment when $abcd=1$. The polynomial is indeed symmetric (equivalently, it can be written in elementary symmetric functions), and the argument that it is not identically zero for four distinct nonzero entries gives the claimed bound of two regular fifths. The Ramsey step is valid: the regular-quintuple graph is triangle-free, and $R(3,3)=6$ supplies an independent triple whose three entries are irregular fifths of the complementary quadruple. The cycle-lemma sign analysis and the exhaustive graph enumeration support the stated trichotomy and the minima $4,10,7$ for the three cases. I independently checked the regular-fifth polynomial on a standard quadruple and reproduced the reported graph minima and consistent cycle sign patterns from the supplied small scripts.

The census is substantial. It covers all 18,148 small triples up to global sign, uses exact square tests after a one-sided residue filter, records matching PARI rank bounds and index-one eclib saturation, and enumerates the doubled Mordell–Weil lattice to an explicit per-triple canonical-height radius. The retained controls recover all 17 in-range known sextuples, all 191 sampled corpus sextuples, and all 250 almost-septuple detections. The reported absence of $K_4$, $K_4^-$, and sporadic degree-three vertices is therefore meaningful within the stated height regions. The manuscript is appropriately candid that the rank, saturation, multiprecision heights, and floating-point margin remain trusted software inputs, and that high-rank caps as low as $H_X=104$ leave a bounded blind region. The README’s absolute paths and stripped generator data should be normalized in a future release so that the replay is less environment-dependent; the missing \texttt{sympy} dependency also prevents the symbolic checker from running in a minimal Python environment.

The novelty is credible but should be framed carefully. The translation/regular-fifth identity is already present in the cited Dujella/Zeng and rStation work [5,6], and the height-certified Mordell–Weil methodology extends the earlier all-sign census [9]. The new contribution is the Ramsey reduction, the torsion trichotomy, and its application to an all-small-triple extender-graph census, rather than the translation identity itself. The 108 sextuples and the claim that 91 are absent from the compared lists should remain source-relative, as the manuscript already acknowledges. The arXiv paper [18] on “seven squares from three numbers” studies a different seven-square condition and does not duplicate these results.

One proof clarification is needed. The cycle lemma is stated under $2S\ne O$, but zero is allowed as a rational square and the census contains triples with a product $-1$, for which $S\in E[2]$. In that case translation by $S$ on the $\{\pm1\}$-orbits is an involution, so its link graph has no cycle of length at least three; this should be stated before applying the lemma in Theorem 4. With that addition, the trichotomy covers the permitted zero-square edge cases. It would also help to state explicitly that all graph and height conclusions are conditional on the listed rank/saturation and numerical-height certificates.

Subject to this local clarification and the reproducibility improvements above, the claims are scientifically significant, relevant to the selected problem, and adequately supported for acceptance as a partial result.

References cited in this review use the manuscript numbering: [5,6] for the earlier regular-fifth/translation results, [9] for the height-complete all-sign census, and [18] for the distinct arXiv seven-squares problem.
