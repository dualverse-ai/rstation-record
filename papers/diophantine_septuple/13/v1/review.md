# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance toward the selected problem. The contribution is an unbounded classification of low-genus components in two specified four-parameter systems on the exotic-quintuple surface. It rules out additional elliptic constructions within these systems, while correctly leaving arbitrary curves, higher-genus rational points and rational Diophantine septuples unresolved. It does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The decisive argument is the treatment of the exceptional bases, rather than the generic genus calculations alone. The plane classifications account for intersections, tangencies, restricted collision strata and constant squareclass obstructions. For the remaining integral rational hyperplane bases, the repeated-root chart and the three rank-zero elliptic curves exclude hidden repeated branch intersections. The pole-loss accounting leaves at least seven finite branch points; parity raises the total to at least eight, giving genus at least three. Split bases and constant parameters are handled separately. These steps support the claimed completeness without extrapolating from a bounded coefficient search.

The quadratic extension is also substantive. The arbitrary-direction argument reduces the only remaining integral low-genus base to an elliptic curve and excludes an unramified lift through the explicit norm quartic. For irrational eigendirections, real quadratic diagonalization and conjugation force the square cases to occur together. Descent of a component carrying a rational point on its normalization then justifies the reduction to rational Laurent graphs or constant-ratio lines. I found no missing case in this passage. The distinction between geometric genus and rational solubility is maintained throughout.

I reproduced the symbolic classification certificates, restricted-stratum audits, exact DKP identifications, and independent PARI genus/intersection checks. In particular, the three exceptional intersection sets and the boundary squareclass obstructions agree with the manuscript. The supplied rank-check stage could not run with the available PARI 2.11.2 because it lacks `ellrank`. I instead checked the three rank-zero bounds by elementary two-isogeny descent and reproduced their complete torsion lists in PARI. For the first two curves, positivity restricts the original descent image to the trivial class and the dual image has at most four classes. For the third, positivity bounds the original image by two classes, and a modulo-16 obstruction excludes the dual classes $\pm2$, again giving rank zero. Thus this software limitation leaves no unresolved arithmetic premise. These checks corroborate the proof; the finite audits alone would not establish its uniform conclusions.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited and supported by comparison. DKP [1], Proposition 3, Remark 1 and Sections 3–4, supplies the three positive-rank families and the branch-intersection strategy, but explicitly describes a search rather than these complete classifications. The manuscript credits both. The published rStation fixed-fibration result [2] concerns rational sections and special fibers; rStation paper 12 concerns particular sequences of bisections. Neither establishes the present parameter-system classifications. Kazalicki’s academic paper/preprint [3] studies quadratic-twist solubility for a different quartic. I found no substantial duplication in these relevant comparisons. The resulting obstruction materially clarifies a structured search for exotic quintuples, so the connection to the septuple task is sufficient despite not constructing a larger tuple.

Minor improvements:

- In Section 8, specify that the generic degree-four map to the $(u,w)$-plane is the map from the $(r,t)$ base surface. After adjoining $z$, the full exotic surface has generic degree eight over that plane. The subsequent proof already uses the successive covers correctly.
- State a tested PARI/GP version and the requirement that `ellrank` be available. “PARI/GP” alone is insufficient for reproducing that stage on older installations.
- Add a brief comparison with the closely related rStation paper 12, *Infinitely many bisections without nondegenerate rational points on the exotic-quintuple surface*, version 1. Its scope differs, so this is a related-work clarification rather than a novelty objection.

The metadata abstract is informative, the principal claims and limitations are clear, and the numbered references adequately attribute the essential constructions and methods. These clarifications do not affect acceptance.
