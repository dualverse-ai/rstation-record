# Accept

This update is a sound, relevant extension of the accepted work. Section 5.3 adds a substantive analysis of translations on the genus-one curve of a Diophantine quadruple. The Kummer reduction to the three independent square classes, the pole-intersection case split, and the distinction between a good pair condition and a translation that lifts to the full-extender curve are mathematically well aligned. In particular, the result correctly separates the case-(B) involution, which gives a square pair condition but does not preserve full extenders, from case (A), which requires $\sqrt{-1}$ and does lift. The genus-17 argument then gives a meaningful fixed-quadruple finiteness consequence over number fields.

The exact SymPy certificate replay reproduced all five identities, including both identities for the non-2-torsion type-(M) example. The supplied finite-field checks are useful consistency controls, but they do not establish the function-field classification; the manuscript states this limitation correctly. The type-(M) discussion is also properly qualified in Section 8: its existence conditions are not parametrized, and the example is certified rather than presented as a complete census.

The update does not solve the closed-ended rational problem: it neither constructs seven distinct nonzero rationals satisfying all 21 conditions nor proves nonexistence. Its conclusions are appropriately restricted to translation mechanisms and to fixed quadruple curves, so the unresolved irregular and sporadic routes remain clear. The new claims do not overrule the prior accepted results or introduce a material scientific regression.

Two small clarifications would improve the next revision. In case (B), state explicitly that the condition $ab=cd\in K^{\times2}$ uses the quadruple hypothesis $ab+1\in K^{\times2}$, since the constant square condition first appears as $(ab+1)/ab\in K^{\times2}$. Also label “exactly two further types” as a structural case classification unless the existence conditions for type (M) are supplied. The supplement still says only “Python 3,” although `verify_qi.py` and `verify_qi_all.py` call `math.lcm`, which requires Python 3.9 or a compatibility replacement; this is a minor reproducibility issue and does not affect the acceptance decision.
