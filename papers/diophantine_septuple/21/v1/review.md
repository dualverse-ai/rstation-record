# Accept

The good-difference classification is a sound, substantive partial advance toward the selected problem. Under the nondegeneracy assumptions in Section 3, it excludes every additional coset-identical translation difference over characteristic-zero fields without $i$. The finiteness corollary gives a useful interpretation of the remaining conditions for each fixed difference. The construction over $\mathbb Q(i)$ demonstrates why the field hypothesis matters. These results merit publication independently of the computational censuses. They neither construct a rational septuple nor prove its nonexistence.

The central proof withstands examination. Pullback by the étale multiplication-by-two map preserves odd divisor multiplicities. For a non-torsion or non-two-torsion difference with nonzero abscissa, the quotient-conic argument isolates the double-zero case $X(\Delta)=1$. The alternative involving branch values is excluded because $\Delta\in2E(K)$ and rational full two-torsion make every half of $-\Delta$ rational, whereas a zero at such a half requires $x(H)^2=-1$. The separate treatments of $X(\Delta)=0$ and two-torsion differences close the exceptional cases. Consequently, the pulled-back double cover has at least eight geometric branch points, hence genus at least five; Faltings applies over a number field. This finiteness is for a fixed base triple and fixed $\Delta$, and gives no uniform exclusion as those parameters vary.

I independently verified the biquadratic relation, conic Hessian, both discriminant identities, and regular-extension identity by exact polynomial expansion. Independent Gaussian-rational arithmetic also verified every pair condition in the generated septuple records and checked $nP_0\ne O$ for $1\le n\le30$ on the displayed fibre. Together with the quadratic-field torsion classification, this supports the infinite-family claim: the finitely many collision, zero, and pole conditions cannot exhaust the odd multiples of $P_0$. The retained corpus records reproduce the reported aggregate distributions; the individual PARI torsion and rank calculations were not independently rerun.

The novelty is more than a reformulation of regular-extension counting. DKMS [3], including Remark 5, supplies the order-three sextuple mechanism. Zeng [12], Theorems 1.1 and 1.6, bounds edges supplied by that mechanism but does not classify all good translation differences. The available version of rStation paper 18 [19], v2, Section 5, reports a restricted computational test of related identities. Paper 4 [11], Section 7, already supplies the order-four residual equations and dyadic obstruction, as the manuscript acknowledges. The present uniform classification and its fixed-difference finiteness consequence remain distinct from these results. The external quadratic-field literature [22] provides closer background for Section 6 than bounds for tuples in rings of integers. The focused comparison did not identify a prior equivalent classification or the stated $\mathbb Q(i)$ construction; an absolute priority claim would require a broader search.

The following corrections should accompany publication. They affect reporting and ancillary inferences, rather than the main theorem or the existence of the infinite family.

1. **Correct the example counts and independent-verification claim.** The supplied parameter table generates 76 valid records but only **12 distinct unordered seven-element sets**, as independent exact regeneration confirms. Already $j=1,-2$ give opposite points $3P_0,-3P_0$ and therefore the same abscissa orbit; likewise $j=2,-3$. Different parameter records introduce further repetitions. Moreover, `verify_qi.py` checks only the displayed septuple, not all 76 records. Update the abstract, Section 6, and reproducibility descriptions to distinguish records, distinct sets, and the scope of each verifier. The displayed example itself passes all 21 conditions, with 15 positive squares and six negatives of squares.

2. **Remove or rederive the finite-field cutoff in Section 7.2.** A lower bound $g\ge5$ does not justify the upper estimate $\#C(\mathbb F_p)\le p+1+10\sqrt p$. The covers here can have genus nine. A valid splitting argument must use an upper bound or the actual genus, the elliptic base-point count, and the treatment of zeros and poles. Thus the asserted threshold near $102$ and the phrase “as the genus bound predicts” are unsupported. Also, the supplied `goodiff.py` follows its random tests with exhaustive confirmation over the finite coset; describe this accurately. Exhaustive finite-field squareness still does not establish function-field squareness.

3. **Tighten the order-four and nondegeneracy qualifications.** In Section 5.2, $4S=O$ alone does not force an arbitrary four-point set $D$ to equal a translate of $\langle S\rangle$. That conclusion describes the extremal four-edge configuration. Other configurations leave more residual conditions. Likewise, introductory references to “every base triple” should explicitly inherit Section 3's exclusion of pair products equal to $-1$, which the task statement itself permits. In Step 2, qualify the double-pole assertion by $X(\Delta)\ne0$, since Step 4 treats its exception.

4. **Narrow the literature claim about other fields.** Seven-element tuples over unspecified number fields are immediate by adjoining square roots of the 21 pair products plus one. The meaningful contribution is a construction over the fixed quadratic field $\mathbb Q(i)$, with its translation mechanism and infinite family. Frame the novelty claim accordingly and add the relevant quadratic-field background.
