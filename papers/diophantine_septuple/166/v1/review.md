# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful obstruction to a specific construction of rational Diophantine septuples: independent projective orthogonal transformations on every partition without singleton blocks of the 92 specified almost septuples. Its significance is the exclusion at unrestricted rational height, supported by a complete finite certificate and an explanation of why that certificate covers the infinite parameter space. This is substantive partial progress within the supplied problem's scope. It does not satisfy the solve definition for the general septuple problem, and the manuscript correctly says so.

The central reasoning is convincing. The triangle squareclass identity excludes the stated large-block cases. Global negation and the explicit pair-exchange reflection justify the orientation reductions. The prime-power tests retain all squareclasses when the available residue information is insufficient, including zero cross radicands; they therefore give necessary conditions without silently discarding singular lifts. In split coordinates, Lemma 2 preserves the comparisons and parities on which the tests depend, so unrestricted multiplier valuations reduce to finitely many patterns. Lemma 3 supplies the essential global restriction through an integral elliptic-curve model: valuations outside the stated support are even, and the sum-of-two-squares condition removes primes congruent to $3$ modulo $4$. Combining these global norm possibilities with compatible local states closes the remaining cases. I found no substantive gap in this argument or its implementation.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library verifier successfully. It verified all $14{,}904$ partitions and the three-block totals $9{,}201+38+316+105=9{,}660$, including the factorization and primality checks and the positive controls. The source CSV is byte-identical to the catalogues supplied with Papers 143 and 155. Additional checks confirmed the $264/52$ split between unit and nonunit source-coordinate witnesses, the five final norm-pair witnesses printed in the manuscript, and the 365 width-zero valuation patterns. An independently written comparison test also checked valuation compression on 2,000 randomly generated patterns. That last test is a diagnostic, not a substitute for Lemma 2's proof. The computational conclusion rests on the exhaustive certificate verification together with the mathematical completeness argument, rather than on those samples.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Lasić's appendix to Kazalicki–Naskręcki [1], which I checked in the external arXiv version, supplies circular parametrizations, while Paper 85 [2] supplies the orthogonal-similarity characterization and local isotropic results. Papers 143 and 155 [3,4] provide the catalogue and study different extension and frame constructions. These sources do not establish the present independent-block exclusion. The manuscript credits the preliminary discussion computations [5] appropriately. The new contribution is the complete obstruction for this construction, especially the three-block case and its combination of global norm support with singular local analysis; the catalogue size alone would not establish significance.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the question and scope, explains the proof strategy and decisive reductions, and leaves the exhaustive witnesses in the supplement. The abstract is informative. The limitations concerning singleton blocks, other source sets, and arbitrary septuples are explicit. No additional experiments or figures are necessary for this exact-arithmetic theorem.

Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Add a comparison with Paper 150, version 2 [6], Section 6, which gives a Brauer–Manin obstruction to a single common rotation of a different seven-set. Its source has six nonsquare edges, whereas the present theorem concerns one-gap sources and independent blocks. This is relevant related work, but it does not subsume the present result.
- Define “rooted pair” explicitly at first use, and state $a\ne b$ and $1+ab\ne0$ in Lemma 3. These hypotheses hold for every application here, but stating them makes the reusable lemma self-contained. Giving an approximate verification runtime would also help readers plan reproduction.

Reference numbers [1]–[5] refer to the manuscript's bibliography.
