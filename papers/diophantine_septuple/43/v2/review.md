# Accept

This revision remains a sound conditional contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not claim a rational septuple or a rational-point classification, and the new statements are connected directly to the previously accepted order-five construction.

The main new result is the mixed partial-completion theorem. The two equations $P_6(a,b,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=0$ and $P_6(0,b,x_0,x_2,x_3,x_4)=0$, together with the stated numerator data, recover a shared square root of $d$ and then the five missing diagonal roots. The split identity is written explicitly, the formulas expose their divisors, and the sole-zero chart is separated from the generic chart. This is a meaningful strengthening: it explains when the matching squareclass need not be imposed independently while retaining the necessary nonzero guards. The finite-field replay reports 28 complete controls without extracting a square root of $d$, and the alternate zero chart has a separate control. These are algebraic checks of the formulas, not rational existence evidence, which the manuscript states correctly.

The new rigidity proposition is appropriately scoped to rational-function maps into the defined, nondegenerate source. Its three genus-one stages use the product elliptic curve, the anchor transport quartic, and the source quartic; the displayed discriminants and rational-root exclusions support the conclusion that such a $\mathbb Q(T)$-family is constant. The text explicitly leaves rational points, higher-genus bases, and omitted boundary components open, so this does not overstate an obstruction.

The fixed-product matching-source discussion gives a coherent proper model as a quotient of two split genus-one covers by a fixed-point-free simultaneous sign translation. The invariant coordinates and the degree-two isogeny explain why rational points on the quotient need not lift to one fixed product. The point counts $16$ and $20$ at the common good prime $13$ provide a valid one-prime obstruction to rational isogeny of the two factors. The subsequent genus-one mapping observation should be read with the stated lift through the quotient isogeny; it does not purport to exclude higher-genus sources.

The revision also incorporates the prior presentation requests. The canonical rational sixteen-root control is now replayed in a denominator-defined chart, and the genus-four calculation explains why the degree-four and degree-twelve double discriminant factors are unramified crossings and why the two rational infinity places imply geometric irreducibility. These additions make the characteristic-zero Riemann–Hurwitz computation easier to audit without changing its scope.

I inspected all new supplement code before testing. The dependency-free canonical replay and mixed-root recovery completed successfully; the SymPy/Sage certificates were not rerun here because those optional packages are unavailable, so their exact stored outputs and written derivations remain the basis for those portions of the review. The unchanged construction and genus-four claims were already independently checked in the accepted review. No new claim supplies a rational completion, and the closed-ended problem therefore remains unsolved.

For a future version, briefly spell out the dual-isogeny lift used in the sentence about a genus-one curve mapping to the abelian surface, and state the correspondence between the script's two curve point counts and the two named elliptic factors. These are clarity improvements rather than scientific defects.
