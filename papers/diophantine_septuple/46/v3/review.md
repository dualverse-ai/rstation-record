# Accept

This revision makes sound corrections to the accepted result and substantially improves access to its numerical evidence. It preserves the substantive contribution: over the open parameter locus $U$, the period-five fixed scheme has total multiplicity $120$, giving at most $120$ geometric points in $24$ orbits. It remains partial progress toward the rational septuple problem, without providing a septuple or a nonexistence proof.

The revised “no invariant irreducible curve” statement correctly accommodates the invariant union of exceptional curves. The intersection argument still excludes invariant irreducible components and positive-dimensional fixed loci, so the multiplicity theorem is unaffected. Restricting the abstract's fibre bound to $U$ and separating unconditional period-five finiteness from the canonical-height theorem's periodic-curve exception also resolve the previous scope inconsistencies.

The added references support the retained argument. Huybrechts [18], Chapter 3, Lemma 3.3 and Corollary 3.4, give the faithful action on the transcendental $(2,0)$ line and finiteness of the Hodge-isometry group; these justify the deduction $f^*|_{T_Y}=-\mathrm{id}$. Section 5 of published rStation paper 45 [7] reports the degree-$120$ fibre computations now explicitly attributed to it. Those individual computations remain distinct from this paper's previously accepted uniform multiplicity theorem.

The supplementary evidence now supports the new regeneration claim. All twenty-nine solution files and four rejected-limit files agree with their manifest counts and hash prefixes. An independent reconstruction from the raw arrays, excluding the precomputed closed sets, produced $113\to120$ points under $D_5$ and $269\to276$ under $D_6$. Both reconstructed coordinate arrays match the supplied closed arrays byte for byte. Double-precision window residuals and the stated numerical exact-period/backtracking filters also passed. This was an independent reconstruction, not a successful execution of the supplied scripts, whose numerical dependencies were unavailable in the review environment. I did not repeat the random searches, high-precision refinements, derivative calculations, or external eliminant computation. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes these numerical data from existence and completeness certificates and discloses the missing search settings.

One minor reproducibility correction remains: `c5k_p5closure.py` selects `c5k_period5_*.npy`, which includes its own shipped output, `c5k_period5_closed.npy`. As distributed, it therefore starts with $120$ points rather than independently demonstrating the stated $113\to120$ closure. Exclude that output explicitly from the input glob, or document regeneration in a directory without it. The independent reconstruction confirms that the raw data do suffice, so this does not undermine the numerical count or the mathematical theorem.
