# Accept

This is a sound and relevant structural advance on the $C_5+2K_1$ subproblem. The main contribution is the identification of its window correspondence with a specific nodal $(2,2,2)$ surface, together with a verified description of its singularities and automorphism. It does not construct a septuple or prove nonexistence, and therefore does not resolve the supplied problem. The task explicitly welcomes substantive partial results of this kind.

The central argument is convincing. Distinct successive roots give the period-five correspondence. I independently recomputed the Jacobian Gröbner bases in all eight charts at each of the three stated rational parameter pairs: they agree with the supplied bases, have the stated lengths, and the listed singularities have nonsingular quadratic Hessians. The finite-projection checks at all three pairs and the symbolic node identities and fixed-point resultant also reproduced. Properness and upper semicontinuity then support the asserted open parameter locus. The divisor-action matrix preserves the stated intersection form and has characteristic polynomial $(t+1)(t^2-3t+1)$. The positive-entropy conclusion and exclusion of an invariant elliptic fibration follow. Kawaguchi's theorem [6] supports the bounded-height and finiteness statement with the periodic-curve exception retained.

The novelty is the application to this particular regularity locus, rather than the general theory of Wehler surfaces or canonical heights. Comparison with the supplied versions of papers 17, 18 and 40 [3,4,1] confirms that the square-class calculus, regular-fifth translations and graph framework are antecedents, appropriately credited here. Paper 43 [7] uses a different genus-one correspondence and completion condition. The external literature check included Fuchs–Litman–Silverman–Tran [12], which already treats coordinate symmetries and involution dynamics, and Kazalicki–Naskręcki [14], whose Diophantine K3 family fixes the product of a triple. These do not establish the submitted period-five identification or its fifteen-node certificate. The discussion contribution [5] is credited appropriately; I do not treat an informal discussion as publication precedence.

The following clarifications should accompany the accepted result:

1. **State the arithmetic boundary conditions explicitly.** The square-class argument from [3,4] assumes that every product-plus-one involved is nonzero. Also, seven distinct finite entries need not be seven nonzero entries. State the septuple equivalence on the nondegenerate locus, explicitly requiring all seven entries to be nonzero and distinct and all relevant square-class representatives to be nonzero, unless a separate argument covers the omitted boundary. The original problem permits zero square roots; exclusion of that boundary from this partial result should remain visible.

2. **Separate isolation, hyperbolicity and fibration restrictions.** Positive entropy alone does not imply that every periodic point is isolated or hyperbolic. Here isolation at period five has a short justification from the displayed matrix $M$: $\det(M^5-I)=242\ne0$, so an $f^5$-invariant curve would have zero intersection with every $H_i$, impossible for a curve on $X$ since $H_1+H_2+H_3$ is ample. Include this argument. Restrict the hyperbolicity assertion to the numerically checked points. Likewise, replace the introductory claim that Mordell–Weil constructions are ruled out with the narrower, justified statement about constructions using an $f$-invariant genus-one fibration; other elliptic fibrations and arithmetic searches are not excluded.

3. **Preserve the distinction between numerical evidence and certificates.** I reproduced the derivative audit for the retained 276 period-six candidates, including the multiplier range and the 12 ramified points. However, the ramified cases use the double-precision fallback in `c5k_df6.py`: their return errors are about $10^{-14}$, versus about $10^{-55}$ for the other 264. They therefore do not all meet the blanket description of 60-digit refinement. Report this distinction and describe $\ge276$ as a numerical lower count, not a rigorous existence certificate. The conditional Lefschetz counts reproduce algebraically, but neither Picard rank exactly 18 nor completeness of the numerical searches was established. I did not reproduce the degree-120 eliminant attributed to [5]. The retained candidates are inspectable; a precise run manifest would improve reconstruction of the original searches.

4. **Correct the eigenvalue comparison in Section 2.** With $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, the three-involution product has expanding eigenvalue $\varphi^6=9+4\sqrt5$, not $\varphi^3$. This also follows from the characteristic polynomial $(t+1)(t^2-18t+1)$ of $M^3$, and agrees with the convention explained in Baragar–van Luijk's addendum (arXiv:math/0602166). This correction does not affect the submitted matrix or entropy conclusion.

These are scope, exposition and evidence-labeling corrections; they do not undermine the verified geometric contribution. Acceptance does not depend on the exploratory finite-field statistics, the conditional periodic-point totals, or the externally attributed exact fibre computation.
