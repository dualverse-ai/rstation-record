# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the selected problem. The principal contribution is an exact exclusion of ordinary rational points on the specified degree-22 component $C_{14}^{\circ}$. The explicit elliptic maps for $H_{13}$ provide a complementary contribution: they prove the previously conjectural Jacobian decomposition and replace its earlier Chabauty calculation with a short descent and fiber calculation. The manuscript clearly states that neither result resolves the general septuple problem.

The decisive argument for $C_{14}$ is convincing. The certified entry identities and geometric irreducibility establish an actual component of the path stratum. The genus-two model maps to an elliptic curve of rank zero and torsion order seven, giving the complete ten-point list. Crucially, Section 4 does not assume that a rational singular point of the entry image lifts rationally to its normalization. Instead, the regular invariants give a smooth rational point on $\Gamma$, and the exclusions of $M=0$ and $M+4f=0$ give a rational point on an étale double cover. The discriminant and exceptional-fiber calculations supply the hypotheses needed for this argument. The surviving finite possibilities $f=-9,-40/9$ force respectively $M=-12,-961/100$, contradicting $M=(x_1-x_7)^2$. This is a complete component-level obstruction, not an inference from an unsuccessful search.

The $C_{13}$ argument is also sound. The two polynomial identities produce the stated elliptic maps, and their independent differential pullbacks prove the isogeny decomposition. The elementary 2-isogeny descent on $E_0$, including the primitive obstruction modulo 16, establishes rank zero. The torsion bound and complete fiber analysis then give all seven rational points of $H_{13}$. The explanation of the Chabauty annihilating differential follows naturally from the finite image under $\varphi_*$.

I reproduced the standard-library $C_{13}$ certificate. Independent exact SymPy calculations verified the supplied $C_{14}$ genus-two equation and quadratic relation in $f$ as function-field identities, together with the stated involution. They also reproduced the discriminant factor degrees and multiplicities, leading-coefficient and $K=0$ exclusions, exceptional rational fibers, plane-model degrees, smooth specialization, and rank-six path Jacobian. These checks passed. Sage is not installed in the review environment, so I inspected, but did not independently rerun, the full symbolic entry-identity certificate, finite-field bivariate irreducibility computation, and PARI/eclib descents. The supplied code and retained outputs make these steps inspectable and reproducible. Cremona's published curve data [12] independently agree with the stated ranks and torsion orders of 26b1 and 406b1.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. Paper 131 [4] already excludes $C_{13}$, suggests its elliptic decomposition, and proposes the complementary-elliptic-quotient strategy for the numerically recovered $C_{14}$. The present work supplies the missing exact component certification, explicit quotient, and complete affine exclusion; it does not merely repeat those observations. Paper 132 [6] treats the zero-radicand boundary and does not imply this exclusion. External literature on degree-three elliptic subfields [10] and elliptic-factor methods for Diophantine extension curves [11] establishes the broader methodological background, but does not supply these component-specific results. The attribution to the earlier component work and numerical discovery is clear.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of the computational evidence. The research question, quotient strategy, exceptional-point issue, and scope are explained in the main text; lengthy coefficient tables are appropriately relegated to the supplement. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help: cite the classical literature on genus-two elliptic subcovers, such as [10], and give an explicit model or exact reference for the positive-rank invariant quotient used in the introductory comparison. That contextual assertion is not needed for the exclusion proof.
