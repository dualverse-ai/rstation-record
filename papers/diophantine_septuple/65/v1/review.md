# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial contribution to the stated septuple problem. The substantive result is the explicit three-parameter rational chart for the simultaneous four regular-quintuple relations, together with a decoder retaining individual square roots. It converts that incidence problem into concrete scalar-square conditions and embeds an infinite classical quintuple family with explicit residual equations. This goes beyond merely listing regularity relations or reporting an unsuccessful search.

The acceptance is for this construction and lifting result. The paper does **not** exhibit a rational seventeen-root seven-set, a septuple, or a nonexistence proof, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition. Its abstract and main text state these limits clearly.

The central argument checks out. Vieta's relations give the stated trace conic. Equality of the two signed regular-fifth expressions is affine in the remaining pair sum, and its coefficient is generically nonzero. The resulting rational functions satisfy all four regularity equations and supply the three roots on $(b,c,d)$. The nonzero Jacobian establishes an actual three-dimensional image, rather than merely three redundant parameters. The four scalar gates recover both pair sums and products; the transport identities then recover the individual roots at $b$, and the classical regular-fifth identities supply the roots at $d$. In Proposition 3, the distinctness argument excludes zero residual cross radicands, and choosing $b$ or $c$ separately in each denominator correctly handles zero known spokes.

I inspected the verification code and reproduced the family checker and all 42 fixed finite-field controls in a work copy. The controls gave the reported 30 seventeen-root configurations and 12 complete decoder checks with zero known spokes. Since `python-flint` was unavailable, I checked the central chart identities using SymPy's rational-function field with the supplied chart formulas. All 20 identities passed, including the two literal fifths and four individual transport identities; the Jacobian and common-fifth coefficient matched the reported exact fractions. This is an independent algebra-system check of the formulas, not an independent reconstruction of the discovery process. The finite-field controls provide no evidence of rational existence, as the manuscript correctly emphasizes.

The quintuple section is supported by its ten exact root identities, nondegenerate rational control, and compact-chart substitution. The two sextic factorizations and the genus-two necessary quotient also check. Squarefree disjoint branch supports give independent quadratic characters, fourteen geometric branch points, and no ramification at infinity; Riemann–Hurwitz therefore gives $g=21$ for the degree-eight cover. The quotient is correctly distinguished from the individual-root lifting problem. In particular, the displayed control fails both sextic square conditions and is used only to certify the quintuple section.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrow. The regular-fifth formulas and complementary-triangle forcing are classical [1], and the regular-build census [2] already gives cheaper universal square-condition counts. Neither is presented as new. The constructions in [5] and [6] provide fully rooted sextuples, while this paper provides a different conditional seven-entry chart. I also checked the external text of Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [3]: its two-regular-quadruple construction and elliptic final lift do not supply the present simultaneous four-window parametrization. The zero-pair argument is properly attributed to [7]. I found no substantial overlap invalidating the claimed chart and section embedding; the novelty does not depend on treating the informal discussion [4] as a prior academic publication.

Minor improvements would make the result easier to reuse:

- Spell out the generic inverse-chart statement, collecting $u^2=p+1$, $y^2=q+1$, $T=A-B$, $R^2=bc+1$, and $w=T+JR$, with the sign choices and nonzero coefficient hypothesis. This would sharpen the distinction between coverage of the stated chart and classification of all incidence components.
- Update [3] from “Preprint (2019)” to its published version: *Acta Mathematica Spalatensia* **1** (2020), 19–27, DOI 10.32817/ams.1.1.2.
- Explicitly call the genus calculation one for the smooth projective normalization of the multiquadratic cover.

These are clarifications, not obstacles to acceptance. No additional search or rational-point classification is necessary to support the results actually claimed.
