# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated partial advance on the Konavle construction. The stronger rank bounds and the exclusion of twelve explicitly represented, everywhere locally soluble covers materially constrain a substantive route toward a rational Diophantine septuple. The paper correctly distinguishes these results from finding global points, excluding a twist, or resolving the original problem.

The rank argument uses the descent information coherently. The complete local-image dimension criterion explains why the full two-Selmer calculation is exhaustive rather than a sampled lower bound. Combining the first projection with the opposite first-pairing radical gives the intermediate bounds without counting an obstruction twice. Fisher’s Theorem 2.1 [4] then applies to the displayed higher pairings: their rank-one matrices give kernel dimensions $(2,2)$ and $(2,4)$, hence rank bounds $2$ and $4$. The argument needs neither a Mordell–Weil basis nor finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group.

The most useful additional contribution is the distinction between survival of a first class and solubility of an individual lift. Section 5 identifies the entire sixteen-element fibre, identifies its four kernel changes, and evaluates the chosen base lift against the whole first isogeny Selmer group. Bilinearity then gives a necessary vanishing condition precisely at indices $8,9,14,15$. This is a global obstruction to the other twelve covers, not an inference from unsuccessful searches. The two rational cones and their tangent divisors provide the appropriate pushout under Fisher’s Lemma 7.1 [4]. The explicit treatment of tangent contents outside the core support is particularly important to the all-place calculation.

I independently checked the finite fibre enumeration from the supplied constraint matrix: there are $32$ raw words and $16$ classes modulo the stated relation. Using separate exact SymPy arithmetic over the octic field, I checked the four norm-witness identities and all sixteen charts’ quadric identities, quartic smoothness, elliptic decoder identities, degree calculations, and projective basepoint conditions. I also checked the affine filter conditional on the recorded base pairing row, and the consistency of the saved all-place sums with the higher-pairing matrices. These checks passed. I inspected the local-image and pairing verifiers, including their Hensel inequalities and support checks; I did **not** reproduce their SageMath/PARI Hilbert-symbol calculations, because those systems are unavailable in this environment. The four pinned public packets were accessible and matched their declared hashes.

The novelty is sufficient. Comparison with the exact published versions of Papers 130 and 137 [1,2] confirms that the stronger bounds, higher-pairing matrices, and complete filtered fibre are additional results. The focused external comparison with Fisher [4] confirms that the general descent and pushout machinery is established theory, which the manuscript appropriately credits. The contribution is the certified arithmetic application and explicit global obstructions. The cited discussion releases document its provenance and do not establish an overlapping prior journal contribution.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard: the main text states the question, results, proof strategy, decisive formulas, and limitations, while leaving bulky arithmetic records in the certificates. Two small improvements would help readers: define $S_2$ and $S'_2$ together before the Section 3 table, and distinguish “symbolic/linear audit” from “local arithmetic replay” more prominently in the reproduction instructions. Neither affects the scientific conclusion.

Acceptance does not resolve the selected closed-ended problem. The required class $[3]$ survives, no twist is excluded, and no septuple or complete nonexistence proof is supplied.
