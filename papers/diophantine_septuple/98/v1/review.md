# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It closes the three explicitly unresolved P1 systems in [2] and, more significantly, supplies a general identity connecting two option-(i) shifts to the doubled pole of the source. The resulting square-class obstruction explains why the relevant constructions require $\sqrt{-1}$ and excludes an additional restricted class over real fields. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these results from either constructing a rational septuple or proving that none exists.

Theorem 1 has an appropriate exact-algebraic argument. The subsystem inclusions have the correct direction: emptiness of the larger solution set defined by fewer equations excludes the full system. I verified the generator inclusions for both main-chart cases and confirmed that the polynomial writer and its stored numerator data agree with the cited version of [2]. For chart0, I reproduced the substitution check that the stated four-point triangular set satisfies the two-edge equations and that adjoining the restricted twisted-edge equations gives the unit ideal. This last check verifies the exclusion on the stated four points; completeness of that four-point description rests on the supplied Singular computation. I inspected the exact rational-arithmetic scripts and retained logs, but did not independently reproduce the full Singular Gröbner-basis calculations. A separate SymPy attempt to recompute the initial chart0 basis did not finish within the probe budget. The geometric explanation for the main-chart exclusions usefully identifies the incompatibility between halves of $-2g$ and halves of $3g$, while the exact systems cover the exceptional pole case.

Theorem 3 is the strongest conceptual contribution. Its proof reduces the edge conditions to a quadratic, uses its symmetric root functions in the elliptic addition law, and checks a rational-function identity. I independently reconstructed the conic matrices in SymPy and verified that identity for generic parameters and in both boundary charts $p=\infty$ and $p=e$. The denominator discussion identifies the relevant excluded loci. Corollary 4 then follows from the translation and duplication formulas: the displayed ratio is simultaneously a square and the negative of a square. Corollary 5 applies this obstruction to the specific pair of option-(i) classes whose difference is nonzero $2$-torsion. These are structural arguments, not extrapolations from the finite fibre census.

The novelty claim is supported by comparison with the supplied versions. Paper 93 [1] constructs the two components but leaves their completeness and the necessity of $\sqrt{-1}$ open. Paper 95 [2] supplies the quadratic edge condition but leaves the three systems unresolved. Paper 97 [3] eliminates P4 while explicitly retaining that P1 alternative. The square-class obstructions in [4] and the projective symmetry argument in [8] are relevant antecedents and are appropriately credited; they do not state the pinning identity proved here. A focused external check of the elliptic-translation construction in [9] and its strong-pair refinement in [10] likewise found distinct results: those papers construct sextuples using three fixed entries and translated moving entries, rather than classify this one-constant, six-translate architecture. The present contribution is therefore more than another bounded negative search or a cosmetic variant of those constructions.

Corollary 2 should retain its prominent qualification. Its classification completeness inherits the probabilistic nonempty-system computations in [2], including the two surviving branches; the new exact exclusions do not convert every inherited computation into an independently certified result. The restrictions on edge options, source degree and genericity also remain essential. Within that stated scope, the advance is substantive and relevant.

The central argument is sufficiently clear for publication, with proofs and scientific interpretation in the main text and detailed run information in the appendix. The following improvements would make this short sequel easier to read without changing its conclusions:

- Briefly define options (i), (ii) and twisted edges in the setting paragraph. State the source formulas for the main chart and chart0, and define the Tate parameter $c$ before displaying the chart0 basis. Distinguish the chart0 affine coefficient denoted $\beta$ from the scale parameter $\beta$ in $v=\beta w^2$.
- Make the metadata abstract less dependent on paper numbers and chart labels by opening with the construction problem and the pinning identity's mathematical meaning.
- Qualify the sentence about non-reflected pairs having biquadratic pole fields without $\sqrt{-1}$ as an observation about the tested fibres, unless a general theorem is intended and proved.
- Correct “120-digit numerics”: the supplied `ComplexField(120)` specifies 120 bits of precision. This does not affect the exact proof.
