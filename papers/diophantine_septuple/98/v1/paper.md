# Degree-two translate septuples: the last three P1 systems are empty, and a pinning law explains the √−1

## 1. Results

**Setting.** A degree-two source on $E:y^2=x(x-1)(x-L)$ is $v=u^2=\beta w^2$, $w=(x-z)/(x-p)$, with $h=(v-1)/\sqrt A$; the translates $h(P+kS)$ and the constant $\sqrt A$ give Diophantine tuples over $k(E)$ when the edge functions $G_S=A+(v-1)(v_S-1)$ are squares [2, §2]. Paper 95 [2] classifies the septuples of translates whose edges use options (i), (ii) and $2$-torsion shifts. Its Theorem C leaves two cases. P4 ($\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/4$) is excluded by Paper 97 [3]. P1 (a coset of $\mathbb Z/6$, classes $\pm g,\pm2g$, $3g\in E[2]$) is reduced to the two components of Paper 93 [1], except for three systems with a twisted edge at $3g$ on which Singular did not finish: chart0 (i,i) with branch pair $I=\{e',e''\}$, and the main chart (i,ii) with either pair. Theorem C′ of [2] is conditional on those three.

**Theorem 1.** [exact over $\mathbb Q$] The three systems are empty over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$.

**Corollary 2.** [Theorem C of [2], Theorem 1, and [3, Corollary 2]] Under the hypotheses of [2, Theorem C], every septuple of translates of a degree-two source lies, up to the $S_3$ symmetry of the Legendre form, on one of the two $(1,6)$ components of [1]. None is defined over a real field.

The exclusions in Theorem 1 are exact. The other ingredient is not: that the two live P1 branches of [2, §6, Step 4] contain only the points of [1] rests on msolve's multimodular algorithm [6, 7], as stated in [2]. Corollary 2 inherits that label.

**The √−1.** The components of [1] are defined over totally complex fields, and the four points left after the first two edges in Theorem 1's chart0 case have $c=(-2\pm i)/5$. Both facts come from one law. Let the source lie in family I$_e$ of [2, §3]: $u\circ\tau_e=\kappa/u$, where $\tau_e$ is translation by $T_e=(e,0)$, so $z=\tau_e(p)$, $\kappa=\beta f'(e)/(p-e)^2$ and $A=\kappa^2-1$. Let $P$ be a point with $x(P)=p$, and put $t=1/(x-e)$.

**Theorem 3 (pinning law).** [hand reduction + one exact identity] Let a source in I$_e$ with $A\ne0$ and $(p-e)^2\ne f'(e)$ have option-(i) edges at $S_1,S_2\notin E[2]$, $x(S_1)\ne x(S_2)$, with both chords of contact through $V_e$. Then
$$t(2P)=t(S_1+S_2)+t(S_1-S_2).$$
This includes the boundary charts $p=\infty$ and $p=e$, where $2P=O$ and $t(2P)=0$.

By Lemma (G) of [2, §3.3] any two option-(i) edges have chords through one $V_{e^*}$ and put the source in I$_{e^*}$ or II$_{e^*}$, so the chord hypothesis only fixes which $e$.

**Corollary 4 (2-torsion differences force √−1).** In Theorem 3 let $S_1,S_2\in E(k)$ and $S_1-S_2=R\in E[2]$. Then $R\ne T_e$, $E[2]\subset E(k)$, and with $\Phi_Q(x)=(x-e_Q)^2-f'(e_Q)$ and $e''$ the third root,
$$\sqrt{-1}=\frac{\Phi_e(p)\,\Phi_{x(R)}(x(S_1))}{\Phi_{x(R)}(p)\,\Phi_{e''}(x(S_1))}\qquad\Big(\text{boundary charts: }\sqrt{-1}=\frac{\Phi_{e''}(x(S_1))}{\Phi_{x(R)}(x(S_1))}\Big).$$

In P1 the classes $g$ and $2g$ satisfy $g-(-2g)=3g\in E[2]$, so every P1 source with option (i) at $g$ and $2g$ in I$_e$ needs $\sqrt{-1}$. This covers both components of [1] and the chart0 points of Theorem 1. Before this law, that $\sqrt{-1}$ was known from computations: certificates on special loci [3] and fibre eliminants.

**Corollary 5.** [hand + Theorem 3] Let a degree-two source satisfy the hypotheses of [2, Theorem D] (those of Lemma (G) for its two option-(i) classes) and lie off the degenerate locus $D$ of [2, Prop. 7.6]; the branch $p=\infty$ is allowed. If it carries a septuple of translates of the (iii′) type $\mathbb Z/2^2\times\mathbb Z$ over $k$, then $\sqrt{-1}\in k$. For real and rational septuples this replaces the 12 one-prime systems of that type in [2, Prop. 7.6]. The 48 systems of type $\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z$ have shift differences outside $E[2]$ and are untouched.

*Relation to the accepted papers.* [1] constructs the two components and does not prove they are the only ones. [2] proves Theorem C with the three systems open. [3] excludes P4 and keeps the three systems open. Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 close the P1 case under the hypotheses of [2]. Theorem 3 is new. The quadratic that cuts out the edge shifts in I$_e$ is [2, §3.4]; what Theorem 3 adds is how its two roots sit relative to the pole. The sign mechanism has the shape of [4, Theorem 1, Step 5] (an edge function equal to $-1$ times a square class). I found no literal dictionary between the two settings.

## 2. Proof of Theorem 1

All three systems are written by the Step 4 writer of [2] (`c1df/p1x.py`), with the same equations and guard as the 13 systems of that step already shown empty. The proof uses one principle. If $J$ is generated by a subset of the generators of $I$, then $V(I)\subset V(J)$. So $J=(1)$ gives $I=(1)$. If $J$ is $0$-dimensional, $I$ is decided by adding the remaining generators to a Gröbner basis of $J$. Both checking scripts assert that the generators of $J$ are literally among those of $I$, and stop otherwise. A reversed pair fails that assertion.

**Chart0 (i,i), $I=\{e',e''\}$.** Drop the twisted edge at $3g$. The two option-(i) edges at $g$ and $2g$ alone give a $0$-dimensional system of degree $4$. Its lex basis is
$$c^2+\tfrac45c+\tfrac15=0,\quad \beta^2=10c+3,\quad A=20c+4,\quad \alpha=\beta(10c+8),$$
with the guard variable determined [exact, Singular]. So $c=(-2\pm i)/5$. Adding all generators of the full system to this basis gives $\{1\}$ over $\mathbb Q$ (Singular std, 0.5 s). Independently, in sympy, the twisted equations restricted to the four points have lex basis $\{1\}$. The same holds for the file with only the guard factors stripped (`ms95`).

*Geometric meaning.* The two option-(i) edges, whose classes differ by $3g\in E[2]$, pin the source to four points, and those points need $\sqrt{-1}$ by Corollary 4. The twisted edge at $3g$ fails at all four.

**Main (i,ii), both pairs.** Drop the option-(i) edge at $g$. Option (ii) at $2g$ and the twisted edge at $3g$ alone give the unit ideal over $\mathbb Q$ (Singular std: 3.7 s for $I=\{\infty,x_T\}$, 17.9 s for $I=\{e',e''\}$; msolve over $\mathbb Q$ agrees). Their 16 generators are among the 18 of the full system.

*Geometric meaning* [hand, for $x(H)\ne p$ below]. Let $Z=\{h^2=-1\}$, which has four $x$-values [2, §2.3]. Option (ii) at $2g$ puts all four halves of $-2g$ in $Z$, and they have four distinct $x$-values since $2g\notin E[2]$. So they fill $Z$. A twisted edge at $T=3g$ has $N_X=\kappa B_I(x-f)^2$, where $f=x(H)$ is a fixed point of $x\mapsto x(\cdot+T)$, i.e. $2H=\pm T$ [2, §6]. At a point $Q$ over $f$ we have $v(Q+T)=v(Q)$, so $G_T(Q)=A+(v(Q)-1)^2=0$ and $Q\in Z$. Then $x(H)=x(R)$ for a half $R$ of $-2g$, so $H=\pm R$ and $3g=\pm2g$. That forces $g=O$ or $5g=O$, which is impossible for $g$ of order $6$. The exact computation also covers the case $x(H)=p$.

**Controls on the same code paths.** Chart0 (i,i,–) by the same std script returns a basis other than $\{1\}$, of degree 4. Adding only the auxiliary equation $s^2=k$ of the twisted edge gives degree 8. In both checking scripts the target and the control are run in the same way.

## 3. Proof of Theorem 3

*Step 1 (one quadratic per family).* Identify unordered pairs $\{x,x'\}$ with points $(1:x+x':xx')$ of $\mathbb P^2=\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathbb P^1$. An option-(i) edge at $S$ means that the numerator conic $N$ of $G_S$ and the addition conic $W_S$ span a pencil containing a double line $\ell_S^2$ [2, §3.1]. Each $\tau_T$ ($T\in E[2]$) acts on $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ as a harmonic homology $J_T$ with centre $V_T=\mathrm{Fix}(\tau_T)$. Every $W_S$ is $J_T$-invariant for all $T$, and in I$_e$ the conic $N$ is $J_e$-invariant [2, §3.3]. In coordinates adapted to the triangle $\{V_T\}$, $N=n_0X_0^2+q(X_1,X_2)$ and $W_\xi=\sum_jw_j(\xi)X_j^2$ with $\xi=x(S)$. Since $\ell_S(V_e)=0$, evaluating at $V_e$ gives $\mu=n_0/w_0(\xi)$. The edge condition is then that $q-\mu(w_1X_1^2+w_2X_2^2)$ is a square, a single equation. The degenerate members are $W_{e'}=X_1^2$ and $W_{e''}=X_2^2$, so $w_1/w_0=c_1/(1+(e-e')t)$ and $w_2/w_0=c_2/(1+(e-e'')t)$, with $t=t(S)$. Clearing denominators turns the edge condition into a quadratic $\chi_N(t)=a_2t^2+a_1t+a_0=0$ with coefficients in the source parameters [exact: `c1et/pl_gen.py` computes the minor of $N-\mu W_\xi$ complementary to $V_e$ directly, and its numerator in $t$ is one irreducible quadratic]. This is the quadratic of [2, §3.4], rewritten in $t$. When $x(S_1)\ne x(S_2)$, $\{t(S_1),t(S_2)\}$ is its root set.

*Step 2 (the addition law with origin $T_e$).* Put $Y=yt^2$. Then $Y^2=t(1+(e-e')t)(1+(e-e'')t)$ is a Weierstrass model with abscissa $t$ and origin $T_e$, and $t(Q+T_e)=b/t(Q)$ with $b=1/f'(e)$. Let $a=(2e-e'-e'')/f'(e)$. The classical sum and product of $x(A\pm B)$ on $y^2=x^3+ax^2+bx$, translated by $T_e$, give
$$t(S_1+S_2)+t(S_1-S_2)=\Psi(t_1+t_2,t_1t_2),\qquad \Psi(s,\pi)=\frac{b\,(2s(\pi+b)+4a\pi)}{(\pi-b)^2},$$
for $t_i=t(S_i)$, and also $t(2P)=\Psi(2t_p,t_p^2)$. [Self-check on a rational pair of $L=-6$.]

*Step 3 (the identity).* With $s=-a_1/a_2$ and $\pi=a_0/a_2$, $\Psi(s,\pi)=\Psi(2t_p,t_p^2)$ holds identically in $\mathbb Q(L,p,\alpha)$, $\alpha^2=\beta$, for $e=1$ [exact, 1 s]. In the boundary charts $\Psi(s,\pi)=0$ [exact over $\mathbb Q(L,\gamma)$]. The statement is invariant under affine changes of $x$, and over $\bar k$ every curve with a marked root is a Legendre curve with that root at $1$, so it holds in general. ∎

*Denominators* [exact]. $a_2=(p-e)^2\Phi_e(p)^2\lambda_-$, $a_0=\alpha^2\Phi_e(p)^2\lambda_-$, and $\pi-b$ has numerator $\lambda_+$, where $\lambda_\mp=0$ exactly when $\kappa=\mp1$, i.e. $A=0$. The other division is by $w_0(\xi)$, which vanishes only for $S\in E[2]$. So the identity specialises at every source allowed in Theorem 3.

*Checks.* At every solution of two fibre systems ($L=-6$, $x(S_1)=-3$, with $S_2=S_1+(0,0)$ and $S_2=2S_1$), the roots of $\chi_N$ are exactly $\{t(S_1),t(S_2)\}$ (120-digit numerics). Theorem 3 holds on all 36 fibre systems of the earlier census, exactly over $\mathbb Q$: curves $L=-6,-9,7$; pairs $(P,2P)$, $(P,3P)$ and $(P,P+R)$ for every $R$; every admissible $e$.

## 4. Proof of Corollaries 4 and 5

*Corollary 4.* If $R=T_e$ then $t(S_2)=b/t(S_1)$, so $\pi=b$, which needs $A=0$. So $R\ne T_e$. Also $x(R)\in k$, and $e\in k$ because $V_e$ is the intersection of two $k$-rational chords [2, §3.2]. So $E[2]\subset E(k)$. Theorem 3 gives $t(2P)-t(R)=t(S_1+S_2)$, hence
$$\frac{x(2P)-e}{x(2P)-x(R)}=\frac{t(R)}{t(R)-t(2P)}=-\frac{x(S_1+S_2)-e}{x(R)-e}.$$
Now $S_1+S_2=2S_1+R$ and $x(Q+R)-e=(x(R)-e)\,\dfrac{x(Q)-e''}{x(Q)-x(R)}$. The doubling formula $x(2Q)-e_Q=\Phi_Q(x(Q))^2/(4f(x(Q)))$ turns the left side into $(\Phi_e(p)/\Phi_{x(R)}(p))^2$ and the right side into $-(\Phi_{e''}(x(S_1))/\Phi_{x(R)}(x(S_1)))^2$. In the boundary charts $t(2P)=0$, so the right side alone is $-1$. ∎

Checked on 12 of 12 reflected fibres and on the row-E control of [1] ($S_1=T$, $S_2=2T$: both sides $-1/4$). For non-reflected pairs the field of $p$ is biquadratic without $\sqrt{-1}$.

*Corollary 5.* In the type $\mathbb Z/2^2\times\mathbb Z$, the placement of [2, Prop. 7.6] has option (i) at $S_A+t'$ and $S_A+t''$ with $t'\ne t''$ in $E[2]$. The difference is $t'+t''\in E[2]$. Also $x(S_A+t')\ne x(S_A+t'')$, since equal $x$ would merge two classes of the type, and $S_A+t\notin E[2]$ because $D$ contains $S_A\in E[2]$. By [2, Theorem D] the source lies in some I$_e$ and in no II$_T$. By Lemma (G) of [2] the chords of these two edges pass through one $V_{e^*}$ and the source is in I$_{e^*}$ or II$_{e^*}$, so it is in I$_{e^*}$. Then $e^*=e$: if $u\circ\tau_e=\kappa/u$ and $u\circ\tau_{e^*}=\kappa^*/u$ with $e\ne e^*$, then $u\circ\tau_{e''}=\pm u$ for the third root $e''$, which puts it in II$_{e''}$ (the sign $+$ is impossible for a Möbius function of $x$), a contradiction. So $e^*$ is the $e$ of [2, Prop. 7.6]. There $D$ contains $A=0$ and $(p-e)^2=f'(e)$ (written $p^2=L$ for $e=0$), so Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 apply. ∎

## 5. Scope

The classification in Corollary 2 is restricted to the hypotheses of [2, Theorem C]: degree-two sources, options (i), (ii) and $2$-torsion edges, (H2), (H3), and $A\notin\{0,-1\}$. Theorem 3 covers family I$_e$ and option (i) only. Still open: option (iii′) in P1 to P4; Conjecture E and the 48 one-prime systems of type $\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z$ in [2, Prop. 7.6] (all 60 over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$); sources of degree greater than two; and isolated rational septuples. Neither result gives a rational septuple or a nonexistence proof for the whole problem.
