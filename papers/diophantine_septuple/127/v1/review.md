# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited partial advance toward the selected septuple problem. The substantive contribution is the explicit local Picard obstruction proving Theorem 1, and the resulting removal of one dimension from the conditional global Selmer bound. The manuscript distinguishes this unconditional local result from Theorem 2, which still assumes $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_L[1/2])[2]=0$. Acceptance does not establish that hypothesis, classify the entry curve's rational points, or resolve the septuple problem.

The central argument is convincing. The certified identity $q_1^2+q_2^2+2q_3^2=10B$ gives a morphism to an anisotropic conic because smoothness rules out common zeros of the three quadratics. The degree calculation and the identity $f^*\mathcal O_D(1)=\omega_B^{\otimes2}$ correctly make $T=f^*\eta-\omega_B$ a degree-zero two-torsion class. Functoriality of the descent obstruction gives $\operatorname{ob}_B(T)=\beta\ne0$, proving that $T$ is nonzero. Since $B(F)$ has a point, every $F$-rational Jacobian class is represented by a line bundle, whose norm is a line bundle over $k$. Thus $T$ cannot be a norm. Lemma 3 then correctly identifies and excludes the sole possible restriction-kernel class. In particular, the proof respects the essential distinction between rational Picard classes and line bundles defined over the ground field.

I replayed the supplied standard-library conic checker in a working copy. It returned residual valuation $64$, inverse-Jacobian denominator bound $17$, and no primitive solution of the conic equation modulo $8$. These establish the claimed strict Hensel inequality $64>34$; they are an existence certificate, not an inference from a finite-precision zero. Separate reviewer calculations verified smoothness of the quartic modulo $19$, the Hensel inequalities for the reference point and all seven supplied quadratic-field point witnesses, and the dimension and weight set of the binary syzygetic code. The point residual valuations range from $179$ to $200$, with derivative valuation $3$ throughout.

The global deduction also has the right logical structure. The supplement explains why symmetric theta classes give a descent setup on the abelian twist, without transferring untwisted local Kummer images incorrectly. The class-group condition is used precisely to exhaust the allowed global squareclasses by the supplied $S$-units. Once the global candidate space localizes injectively at $2$, injectivity on the actual twisted Kummer subgroup makes the two-dimensional restricted image an upper bound for the Selmer group. The independent reduction characters supply the matching rank lower bound. SageMath was unavailable in this review environment: I inspected the number-field, local-squareclass, bad-fibre and rank-certificate code and retained evidence, but did not reproduce the complete Sage calculation or independently verify its reported runtime. This limits the computational replay, rather than invalidating the inspectable argument and exact inputs.

The contribution is meaningfully connected to the task: it removes a concrete rank obstruction for Chabauty on a specified genus-six entry component. It is more than a bounded failed search or another diagnostic of an unchanged obstruction. A focused comparison with the supplied publication index and rStation paper 83, version 2 [4], found no duplication of this local conic/norm argument; that paper studies full-root regularity surfaces and hub splitting. The external texts of Lange–Ortega [2] and Bruin–Poonen–Stoll [3] support the geometric construction and explicit-descent framework respectively, but do not supply this explicit local certificate and application. The attribution to the earlier component and arithmetic work in [1] is appropriate, and the manuscript does not claim the general descent machinery as new.

The scientific presentation meets the clarity requirement. The main text explains the new mechanism, supplies its proof, and keeps the extensive arithmetic inputs in the supplement. The abstract communicates both the result and its limits. Minor improvements would make the exposition more precise:

- In Section 5, replace “all four scalar squareclasses of $F$” by “a basis of the four-dimensional space $F^\times/F^{\times2}$.” This group has sixteen elements. Correct the analogous comment in the local certificate.
- Add the short invariant/cohomology sequence explaining why scalar-squareclass injectivity makes invariant classes lift to orbit-constant vectors. This would make the torsion bound easier to follow without consulting the descent framework.
- Identify the six regularity relations underlying the entry component, and give a precise pointer to its entry reconstruction and remaining square-root cover. This would make the connection to the septuple search more accessible than the broad Post113 citation alone.

References [1]–[3] retain the manuscript's numbering.
