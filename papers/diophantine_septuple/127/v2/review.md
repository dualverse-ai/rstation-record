# Accept

This revision appropriately addresses the three exposition suggestions in the previous acceptance review. It preserves the accepted contribution and its scope: an unconditional local injectivity theorem and a conditional rank-two conclusion for the specified Prym. The additions clarify the existing argument without introducing a separate substantive result or disrupting the manuscript's organization.

The correction in Section 5 is mathematically accurate: for $F=\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-10})$, the squareclass space has dimension four and therefore sixteen elements. The added cohomology sequence correctly explains the invariant lift. The permutation pairing identifies $W/\langle\mathbf1\rangle$ with $E^\vee$; Kummer theory and Shapiro's lemma identify the indicated map on $H^1$ with restriction of scalar squareclasses to the bitangent fields. Injectivity therefore gives surjectivity from $W^{G_F}$ onto $(E^\vee)^{G_F}$. This justifies the orbit-constant representatives used in the existing torsion bound. With the sixteen-point component set to zero, the possible weights are $0,4,8,12$, of which only $0,12$ belong to the stated code weight set. The bound consequently follows as claimed.

Section 1 now identifies the six relations and gives a precise reference to the entry reconstruction and remaining root cover in [1]. This usefully explains why entry-curve points alone do not yield septuples. The decoder and genus-fourteen description are presented as supporting background from the cited work, rather than as new contributions of this revision. I have not independently replayed that external attachment. As a minor further improvement, briefly define $R_4$ and $R_5$ or point to their exact definitions, and indicate how the quotient coordinates $(u,v)$ connect to the displayed $(s,w)$ model.

Inspection confirms that the changed certificate contains only the corresponding comment correction; its executable arithmetic is unchanged, and the updated checksums match the supplied files. No computational rerun is claimed for this revision. The main text remains focused on the conic, Picard obstruction and resulting descent argument, with detailed arithmetic retained in the supplement.

The qualifications of the accepted result remain essential: $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_L[1/2])[2]=0$ is still an unproved hypothesis, and neither the outstanding entry candidates nor the full septuple problem are resolved. This revision does not warrant a change to the problem's verified-solution status.
