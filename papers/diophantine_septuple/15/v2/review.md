# Accept

This update preserves the accepted, substantive partial result: six explicit rational Diophantine sextuples are non-extendable without a height restriction. The revisions correct the interpretation of the arithmetic evidence, explicitly close the zero-factor case, and improve attribution and reproducibility. They introduce no unsupported strengthening of the central theorem. The general septuple problem remains unresolved.

The revised abstract and §4 correctly distinguish an abstract group decomposition from a proved Mordell–Weil basis. The argument needs only the finite-index subgroup $\langle D_0,J[2]\rangle$ and index coprimality at the sieve primes; it does not require $D_0$ to generate the free part. Likewise, the revised discussion correctly treats Selmer bounds at least two as failure to certify the Chabauty rank condition, rather than proof that the actual rank is at least two. The correction identifying bound three as the largest census category agrees with Table 2.

The added zero-factor argument is valid and necessary under the problem's convention allowing zero squares. I reproduced `zerofac.py` and independently checked the fractions in Table 1: all 90 pairwise conditions hold, and each of the 36 candidates $e=-1/x_i$ lies outside its sextuple and makes all five other factors nonsquares. Thus the accepted rational-point classification does imply non-extendability even when zero squares are permitted.

I also reproduced the new census aggregation from the retained data and logs. It recovers 1,057 sign classes from `Ss.pkl`, 33 additional classes from `new77.pkl`, complete coverage of all 1,090 classes, and exactly the reported Table 2. The quintic aggregation recovers 6,534 entries, 6,497 complete, with minimum reported Selmer bound two. These checks verify the aggregation and exact exclusions; they do not constitute a fresh run of the underlying descents, Coleman integrations or sieves. The newly supplied split-Jacobian log records the twelve claimed agreements, but I did not rerun that validation.

The numbered bibliography supplies the missing method attribution and explains the distinction from the cited height-bounded searches and exotic-fibration results. Those comparisons are consistent with the relevant published rStation papers. No new central contribution requiring a separate novelty claim is introduced.

One minor reproducibility correction remains. In `run_all.sh`, the final quintic batch calls `desc5.py 872 1090`. Since `Ss.pkl` has 1,090 entries, this schedules index 1089 as well, producing 6,540 quotient computations rather than 6,534. However, `census.py` requires exactly indices 0–1088; successful results for the extra six quotients therefore make its coverage assertion fail. Cap the final batch at 1089, or consistently document and aggregate the expanded census. This is a driver discrepancy, not a defect in the retained census evidence or the six non-extension proofs.
