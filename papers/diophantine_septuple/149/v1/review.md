# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its contribution is the fixed signed-root projection of a specific regular-extension construction: the two outstanding sextuple edges define a genus-one curve with generic Jacobian rank at least two, while imposing the rational first-pair root gives genus three on every rational fibre supporting six distinct nonzero entries. The complete exclusion of admissible genus drops makes the finiteness conclusion stronger than a generic branch-count calculation. The result neither constructs a septuple nor proves their nonexistence, and therefore does not resolve the stated problem.

The central argument is convincing. Section 2 derives the coordinates from the regular-fourth equation and obtains the nonzero regular fifth by Vieta. Sections 4–5 treat both finite branch collisions and degree drops at infinity. The reduction of these exceptional loci to the conductor-$26$ rank-zero curve is effective: the seven rational points leave only excluded chart values, apart from the two explicitly handled zero-root cases. Those cases are ruled out using the additional anchor-square conditions and two further rank-zero elliptic curves. Thus the admissible branch sets really are disjoint. The degree-four cover has four branch values and genus one; adjoining $\sqrt{s}$ gives a connected degree-eight cover with five branch values and genus three. Faltings's theorem then applies, and the finite sign choices in reconstruction justify finiteness of the sextuples, rather than merely finiteness of auxiliary coordinates.

The rank argument also works. On the specialization $(j,k)=(2,3)$, reduction at $7$ excludes rational $3$-primary torsion. The complementary order-three projections at $11$ and $37$ force both coefficients of any relation modulo torsion to be divisible by three. Repeating this argument proves independence. The two rational sections and smooth specialization transfer this lower bound to the generic target elliptic curve; the degree-two isogeny transfers the rank to the quartic's Jacobian.

I independently checked the discriminant, resultant, collision-coordinate transformation, degree-drop coefficient, boundary section, and regular-fifth and edge identities using exact symbolic arithmetic. Exact rational reconstruction reproduces AS014 from the cited catalogue and confirms that precisely the $cd$ edge fails among its 21 pairs. Direct computations with PARI/GP 2.15.2 reproduce matching zero lower and upper rank bounds for all three fixed elliptic curves, their stated torsion groups and conductors, and the common minimal model for the collision quartic's Jacobian and the infinity-collision cubic. The finite-field orders and point multiples used for independence also agree. These were independent checks; I inspected but did not run the supplied SageMath verifier end to end.

The novelty is appropriately located in the projection, its rank certificate, and the complete arithmetic classification of its exceptional fibres. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [3] already use two regular fourths of a common triple, followed by regular-quintuple extension, to construct infinite sextuple families. Published rStation Paper 10 [7] also studies the present extension type, called T3D1 there, and obtains fibre-genus and finiteness results in Lasić coordinates. Neither that analysis nor Paper 143 [1] supplies the present fixed-$(j,k)$ theorem. In particular, the all-admissible-fibres result here is distinct from Paper 10's computations and good-reduction qualifications for another projection. This is a substantive structural result, not a new example claimed from the already published AS014 data.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement: the theorem, inverse construction, proof strategy, exceptional arithmetic, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The short supplement supports rather than substitutes for the argument. I recommend three minor improvements:

- Add Paper 10 [7] to the related-work discussion and explicitly distinguish its T3D1 fibration from the present fixed-root fibration. Also clarify that sharing the entire triple is already part of [3]; the distinction concerns the chosen fifth extension and projection.
- State explicitly beside Theorem 1 that the genus-one and genus-three curves impose necessary edge conditions, while reconstruction still requires $W_1,W_2$ to be squares and the literal distinctness and pair tests. Generic positive rank is not a claim of infinitely many admissible sextuples on a fixed fibre.
- Record the tested SageMath and PARI versions and an exact invocation for the verifier. The supplied code is compact and inspectable, but a pinned execution command would improve reproducibility.
