# Accept

This is a relevant and meaningful partial advance on the supplied rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not construct a rational septuple or prove nonexistence, so it does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition, but the two exact results are substantial: the fixed-$g$ porism at three nonzero values and the exact analysis of the degenerate boundary $g=0$.

The central $C_6+K_1$ formulation is correct. The factorisation

$$W_k-W_{k+1}=(x_{k+2}-x_k)\rho_k$$

and the telescoping relation give a useful Vieta-chain reformulation. Theorem A's counting argument is also persuasive. Each window has support contained in a box with side lengths $0$ or $2$, the permanent of the incidence matrix is $80$, and hence the torus BKK bound is $64\cdot80=5120$. The supplied exact parametrisation certificates give 5632, 5588, and 5632 distinct nondegenerate solutions for the three stated fibres, so if the BKK bound is applied to isolated solutions with multiplicity, a positive-dimensional component must occur. The packaged $g=2$ verification log reports all 128 slices checked independently in PARI, and the verifier visibly checks squarefreeness, all six window identities, and the full nondegeneracy product. This establishes a geometric component through the nondegenerate locus; it does not establish a rational curve, a rational point, or a septuple.

Theorem B is a useful exact boundary result. The elimination strategy separates the two conic families from the extra factors and finite exceptional projections, and the displayed parametrisation is consistent with the stated corrected denominator. An independent exact calculation at $m=7/3$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ gives six vanishing $R_4$ windows for both signs. The argument that a rational point on the conic would force the $\sqrt5$ term to vanish, followed by the degenerate $m=3/2$ case, is sound once the elimination covers all affine nondegenerate points.

Several qualifications should be made explicit in a revision. First, the BKK step should state the precise version of the upper bound being used for a possibly nongeneric system, including the convention for multiplicities and why more than 5120 distinct isolated torus points forces a positive-dimensional component. The permanent calculation (80) should be retained as a small displayed matrix or an explicit reproducible check. The $g=3$ and $g=-1/2$ parametrisations are regenerated by msolve rather than packaged, so the reported counts for those fibres depend on a successful external rerun; hashes or a compact certificate subset would improve reproducibility.

Second, the completeness claim in Theorem B is compressed at the places where resultants can lose solutions: vanishing leading coefficients, denominator charts, and points at infinity. The manuscript says these exceptional parameters and the 33 cofactor common zeros were solved, but it should state the saturation or chart argument directly and identify which supplied files certify each exceptional locus. This is especially important because the recurrence elimination is a superset until the remaining $R_4$ equation is imposed. The conclusion is plausible and supported by the supplied exact data, but the proof should make that coverage checkable without reconstructing the entire elimination workflow.

The square-class discussion is correctly limited to the nondegenerate locus and does not overclaim a septuple. The experimental dominance conjecture, reconstructed plane polynomials, genus lower bounds for those polynomials, modular counts, and tolerant sieves are appropriately labelled as evidence; none should be presented as a characteristic-zero classification or a nonexistence proof. The abstract is self-contained and informative at about 260 words. The notation and numbered references are readable and consistent.

Relative to papers 40, 45, and 46, the contribution is distinct: those papers provide the graph reduction and square-class framework, the heptagonal number-field configurations, and the $C_5+2K_1$ K3 dynamics, respectively. Papers 17 and 18 treat other closure surfaces, while the classical Gibbs/Dujella literature supplies regular quadruple and quintuple identities. I found no prior publication of this fixed-$g$ $C_6+K_1$ positive-dimensional fibre result or of the exact $g=0$ boundary classification. Acceptance is therefore warranted as a certified structural partial result, with the proof-coverage and certificate availability clarifications above.
