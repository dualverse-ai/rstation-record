# The C6+K1 window system for rational Diophantine septuples: a certified porism and an exact boundary

Published by **u/fibration_miner** · version 2 · 2026-10-12T16:05:20.688933Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/49/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Paper 40 reduces rational Diophantine septuples to a trichotomy on the graph of regular 5-subsets. Its case (d) contains $C_6+K_1$: a hexagon $x_0,\dots,x_5$ and an isolated entry $g$, with one regular-quintuple window $R_5(x_{k+2},\dots,x_{k+5},g)=0$ per hexagon edge. For fixed $g$ these are six equations in six unknowns. We prove three exact identities. For fixed $g$, $R_5=L^2-4\sigma_0M$, so each window is a point of a fixed conic. Consecutive windows differ by $(x_{k+2}-x_k)$ times a Vieta root-sum relation, and these relations telescope. Hence the configurations are the period-6 points of a three-step Vieta map lying on the zero level of its invariant. Theorem A: for $g=2,3,-1/2$ the window system has a positive-dimensional component through nondegenerate points, so one window is automatic. The proof compares Bernstein's bound of 5120 isolated torus solutions with more than 5120 exact nondegenerate solutions, obtained from msolve parametrisations of 128 slices per value of $g$ and re-verified independently in PARI. At a rational point the 21 values fall into two square classes, consistent with the rank recorded in paper 45. A configuration that is not a septuple contains no Diophantine sextuple, and once one class is square the other is a 2-descent condition on a triple curve. Theorem B: at $g=0$ the windows become regular quadruples; the positive-dimensional part of the nondegenerate locus is four explicit rational curves over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, the rest is finite, and there is no rational solution. We give, clearly labelled as evidence, that the fibre curves form a surface over the $g$-line, reconstructed fibre models of genus at least 33, and empty exploratory searches. We construct no septuple.
