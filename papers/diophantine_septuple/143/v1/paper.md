## 1. Introduction

A set $\{x_1, \dots, x_m\}$ of distinct non-zero rational numbers is a *rational Diophantine $m$-tuple* if $1 + x_i x_j$ is a rational square whenever $i \ne j$. Euler constructed infinitely many rational Diophantine quintuples by extending $\{1, 3, 8, 120\}$ to $\{1, 3, 8, 120, 777480 / 8288641\}$, Dujella [1] and Gibbs systematically developed regular extensions of triples and quadruples, and Dujella, Kazalicki, and Petričević [2] constructed infinite families of rational Diophantine sextuples; see Dujella's survey page [3] for the extensive history of the problem. Whether a rational Diophantine septuple exists remains open. Approaching this boundary from below, the community has assembled $92$ pairwise inequivalent *almost-septuples*—sets of seven distinct non-zero rationals for which $20$ of the $\binom{7}{2} = 21$ products $1 + x_i x_j$ are squares—including three examples obtained on the quintuple threefold in [4], an infinite elliptic family ("Croatian olive") in [5] (subsequently proved in [10] to contain no genuine $21/21$ rational septuple), and the Konavle family $\mathcal{C}_K$ in [6].

In two recent papers [7], [8], Lasić and Oblak embedded $\mathcal{C}_K$ and its complementary Eisenstein curve $\mathcal{C}_F$ (whose $19$-edge sharp bound was proved in [9]) into a three-dimensional variety $\mathcal{V}$ parameterizing eight-element configurations $(a, b, c, d_+, d_-, e, f_+, f_-)$—*octads*—built from a seed $(a, b, c)$ with rational roots $1 + ab = r^2$ and $1 + ac = s^2$, its two regular fourths $d_\pm \in \mathbb{Q}(t)$ where $t^2 = \varphi := 1 + bc$ and $1 + d_+ d_- = z^2$, a regular fourth $e$ of $\{b, d_+, d_-\}$ satisfying $1 + ae = y^2$ and $1 + be = X^2$, and the regular fifths $f_\pm$ of $\{a, b, e, d_\pm\}$. Over the quadratic extension generated by $t = \sqrt{\varphi}$, twenty-two of the $\binom{8}{2} = 28$ pairs of an octad on $\mathcal{V}$ universally yield squares; only the six pairs

$$
\{c, e\}, \quad \{c, f_+\}, \quad \{c, f_-\}, \quad \{d_+, f_-\}, \quad \{d_-, f_+\}, \quad \{f_+, f_-\}
$$

remain generic non-squares. Inside $\mathcal{V}$, the projection to the pair space $(a, b)$ with $1 + ab = r^2$ has generic fibre of geometric genus $3$ realized as a $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$-cover of the $\mu$-line, and [7], [8] isolated two distinguished rational surfaces: the *Croatian surface* $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}(v, \lambda)$, cut out by the root-relation factor $F_2 = 0$, and *Euler's surface* $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}(r, w)$, cut out by the symmetric seed condition $\mu = 2r$ (equivalently $4rz = -1$).

At the conclusion of [8] (Sections 6–7), Lasić and Oblak noted that they did not know whether any of the six missing square conditions holds identically along a curve of $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, and posed four structural questions:

- **Question 1.** *Is there a point of Euler's surface $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ at which $\{a, e, f_+, f_-\}$ is regular, as on $\mathcal{C}_F$?*
- **Question 2.** *On which curves of either surface $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ or $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ is $1 + f_- d_+$ a square? (Only $\mathcal{C}_K$ was known on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, where the extra squares are non-regular.)*
- **Question 3.** *Does $\mathcal{V}$ contain a rational surface other than $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, and are its rational points outside them finite in number on a general fibre?*
- **Question 4.** *Is the second seed $(a, b, c')$ of $\mathcal{C}_F$ (closed by $e$ through $a$ with regular fourths $\{f_+, f_-\}$) special to the Eisenstein conic, or does every conic in the line of ratios $A : B$ over which $s, y, X, z$ are rational carry such a curve?*

In this paper we answer Question 1 affirmatively on $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, construct a third rational surface $S_3 \subset \mathcal{V}$ and establish general-fibre finiteness for Question 3, resolve the surface-restricted part of Question 4 on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ while proving the existence of $1$-dimensional off-surface second-seed components in $\mathcal{V}$, classify the regularity mechanism for Question 2 across all $12$ candidate octad pairs on both $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ (constructing explicit infinite regular families on both surfaces), unify the Croatian olive family of [5] with $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, and classify all $92$ canonical rational almost-septuples inside $\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q})$. Specifically:

1. **Croatian olive embedding and census of the $92$ almost-septuples (Section 3).** Working on the $\lambda \leftrightarrow v/\lambda$ involution quotient $\widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}(p, \sigma)$, **Theorem 1** proves that the Croatian olive seed of [5] maps birationally onto the rational plane curve $M_{\mathrm{ol}}(p, \sigma) = 0 \subset \widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}$. Along $M_{\mathrm{ol}}$, the surface-lifting discriminant $\sigma^2 - 4p$, the quadratic radicand $\varphi = 1 + bc$, and $1 + d_+ f_-$ all become rational square multiples of the *same* genus-$1$ quartic $f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) = k^4 - 2k^3 + 12k^2 - 96k + 128$, while $1 + c f_-$ is already an identical square in $\mathbb{Q}(k)$. Although [10] proves that the Croatian olive family lifts to no genuine $21/21$ septuple, Theorem 1 reveals its geometric origin inside $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ as a second non-regular curve carrying $24/28$ generic square pairs alongside $\mathcal{C}_K$. **Proposition 2** then classifies all $92$ canonical almost-septuples in $\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q})$ across all eight single-element omissions: exactly $7$ lift to rational octads—`AS018` on $\mathcal{C}_K \subset S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, `AS004` and `AS066` on $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ol}} \subset S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, none on $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, one (`AS039`) on our new third rational surface $S_3$, and three (`AS025`, `AS053`, `AS079`) strictly outside $S_{\mathrm{Cr}} \cup S_{\mathrm{Eu}} \cup S_3$ over smooth genus-$3$ ambient fibres.
2. **Affirmative answer to Question 1 (Section 4).** **Theorem 3** proves that on $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}(r, w)$, the cleared regularity locus $R_4(a, e, f_+, f_-) = 0$ decomposes outside coordinate poles and degenerate collisions into *four* irreducible curves $F_5, F_6, F_7, F_8$ of bidegrees $(3, 3), (4, 3), (5, 6), (9, 10)$ (listed explicitly in Appendix A), each generically $8$-element non-degenerate on every component of its quadratic cover $t^2 = \varphi$ (certified via smooth $\mathbb{F}_{59}$ Hensel lifts and non-vanishing norms of all $8$ entries and all $28$ pairwise differences, with $\varphi$ non-square on $F_5, F_6$). The lowest-degree component $F_5 = 0$ is birational to a smooth genus-$2$ hyperelliptic curve $\mathcal{H}_5$ and contains the rational points $(r, w) = (11/7, -8/33)$ and $(527/600, -17/434)$, furnishing non-degenerate octads over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{12139})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{199082274001})$ with $\{a, e, f_+, f_-\}$ regular and $1 + f_+ f_- \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$.
3. **Regularity obstructions and split-$\varphi$ curves for Question 2 (Section 5).** While classifying arbitrary non-regular square-producing curves on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ remains open, **Theorem 4** resolves the *regularity* mechanism completely across all $12$ universal-square pairs $\{u_1, u_2\} \subset \{a, b, c, d_-, e, f_+\}$: no non-degenerate curve on either $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ or $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ can satisfy $R_4(u_1, u_2, d_+, f_-) = 0$ without splitting $\varphi = 1 + bc$ into a square in its function field. **Theorem 5** constructs three explicit regular split-$\varphi$ curves—certified by exact symbolic polynomial reductions rather than numerical sampling—namely: (i) a rank-at-least-$1$ elliptic curve $\mathcal{E}_1 \subset S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ along which *both* $R_4(a, b, d_-, f_-) = 0$ and $R_4(a, d_+, d_-, f_-) = 0$ hold identically, producing infinitely many rational octads with at least $23/28$ square pairs ($23/28$ generically); (ii) a smooth genus-$2$ curve $\mathcal{C}_2 \subset S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ with $R_4(a, e, d_+, f_-) = 0$ and three explicit $23/28$ rational octads; and (iii) a smooth genus-$3$ curve $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Eu}} \subset S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ with $R_4(a, b, d_+, f_-) = 0$ covering an elliptic curve $E_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ of rank at least $1$ via an explicit birational equivalence. Together with Theorem 3, Theorem 5(iii) answers the Euler-surface curve question of [8] (Section 6) affirmatively.
4. **Third rational surface $S_3 \subset \mathcal{V}$ and fibre finiteness for Question 3 (Section 6).** Factoring the quadruple relation $R_4(c, e, d_+, d_-) = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}(a, r, \mu)$, **Theorem 6** shows that its middle factor $M_2(a, r, \mu) := 2(4r^2 - \mu^2)a - \mu(\mu^2 - 4r^2 - 1) = 0$ pulls back $Q_1/(2\mu)$ to an identical square and $Q_2$ to a split conic, cutting out a genuine *third rational surface* $S_3(r, \eta) \subset \mathcal{V}$ distinct from $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, with explicit rational parameterization and one-line birational inverse $\eta = (4rs + y)/(\mu s)$. Identically on $S_3$, $1 + ce$ is a square, so $23/28$ octad pairs are squares generically over $\mathbb{Q}(r, \eta)(\sqrt{\varphi})$. Moreover, `AS039` sits over $M_2 = 0$ at $(r, \eta) = (703/7888, -35/23)$ ($24/28$ squares), chord-and-tangent doubling of the boundary section $r = 1$ yields an explicit rational curve $r = r_{\mathrm{sec}}(\eta) \subset S_3$ splitting $\varphi = \square$ (giving infinitely many rational octads in $\mathbb{Q}^8$ with $23/28$ generic squares), and Faltings' theorem guarantees finiteness of $\mathcal{V}_{a, r}(\mathbb{Q})$ on every smooth genus-$3$ ambient fibre.
5. **Surface classification for Question 4, root-cover isomorphism, and Hensel-lifted off-surface second seeds (Section 7).** **Theorem 7** proves that on $\widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, affine lines $\sigma = \alpha p + \beta$ realize every non-degenerate binary quadratic form over $\mathbb{Q}$ *that represents a non-zero rational square*, yet $\mathcal{C}_F$ is the unique second-seed curve on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and no curve on $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ carries a distinct second seed $c' \ne c$—resolving Question 4 completely on both surfaces $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$. Over the rational seed variety $\mathcal{W}_0$, we establish an exact birational isomorphism between its irreducible $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$-root cover $\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_0$ and an irreducible hypersurface $H_{\mathcal{W}}(s, y, X, z) = 0$ under the uniform root convention $X = bz + g$. Finally, using denominator-free integer polynomials $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4 \in \mathbb{Z}[a, r, \mu, s, y]$, multivariate Hensel lifting modulo $103$, the $4 \times 4$ Jacobian smoothness criterion, and Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem, we prove unconditionally that $\mathcal{V}$ contains smooth $1$-dimensional characteristic-zero components of second seeds strictly outside $\mathcal{C}_F \cup S_{\mathrm{Cr}} \cup S_{\mathrm{Eu}} \cup S_3$ (leaving open whether those off-surface components possess $\mathbb{Q}$-rational points).

## 2. Preliminaries: Uniform Root Convention, Fibre Geometry, and Surface Coordinates

### 2.1. Regular extensions, uniform root convention, and fixed-pair fibres

Following [1], [2], [7], [8], a quadruple $\{w, x, y, z\}$ is *regular* if $R_4(w, x, y, z) = 0$, where

$$
R_4(w, x, y, z) := (w + x - y - z)^2 - 4(1 + wx)(1 + yz),
$$

and a quintuple $\{a, b, c, d, e\}$ is *regular* if $R_5(a, b, c, d, e) = 0$, where

$$
R_5(a, b, c, d, e) := (abcde + 2abc + a + b + c - d - e)^2 - 4(1 + ab)(1 + ac)(1 + bc)(1 + de).
$$

Whenever $\{x_1, x_2, x_3\}$ has $1 + x_1 x_2$, $1 + x_1 x_3$, and $1 + x_2 x_3$ equal to squares, the two roots $d$ of $R_4(x_1, x_2, x_3, d) = 0$ automatically make $1 + x_i d$ squares for $i = 1, 2, 3$.

Let $(a, b, c)$ be a seed with $ab \ne 0$, $1 + ab = r^2$, $1 + ac = s^2$, and $\varphi := 1 + bc$, and adjoin $t$ with $t^2 = \varphi$. Put

$$
D := a + b + c + 2abc, \qquad d_+ := D + 2rst, \qquad d_- := D - 2rst.
$$

By [8] (Proposition 1), requiring $1 + d_+ d_- = z^2$ with $\mu := c - z - a - b \ne 0$ parameterizes $c$, $z$, and the regular fourth $e$ closing $\{b, d_+, d_-\}$ rationally in $(a, b, \mu)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&c = a + b + \frac{\mu}{2} + \frac{4ab + 3}{2\mu}, \qquad z = \frac{4ab + 3}{2\mu} - \frac{\mu}{2},\\
&e = b\mu^2 + 2(1 + 2ab)\mu + 4a(1 + ab) = 2D + b + 2b(z^2 - 1) + 2zg,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $g := b^2 - ab - bc - 1$. Throughout this paper and its verification supplement, we fix the **single uniform root convention** of [7], [8]:

$$
X := bz + g = -(b\mu + 1 + 2ab).
$$

Indeed, substituting $c + z = a + b + (4ab + 3)/\mu$ into $g = b^2 - ab - b(c - z) - bz - 1$ gives $bz + g = b^2 - ab - b(a + b + \mu) - 1 = -(b\mu + 1 + 2ab)$, and direct expansion yields $1 + be = X^2$ together with the useful factorization $e = (\mu + 2a)(1 - X)$. Requiring $1 + ac = s^2$ and $1 + ae = y^2$ cuts out the octad threefold $\mathcal{V}$ of [8] (Theorem 2) in coordinates $(a, r, \mu, s, y)$ with $a \ne 0$, $r^2 \ne 1$, $4r^2 \ne 1$, $\mu \ne 0$, and $b = (r^2 - 1)/a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\mu s^2 &= Q_1(a, r, \mu) := a\mu^2 + 2(a^2 + r^2)\mu + a(4r^2 - 1),\\
y^2 &= Q_2(a, r, \mu) := (r^2 - 1)\mu^2 + 2a(2r^2 - 1)\mu + 4a^2 r^2 + 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

When $m_\pm := eabd_\pm \ne 1$, the unique regular fifths $f_+$ of $\{a, b, d_+, e\}$ and $f_-$ of $\{a, b, d_-, e\}$ are given in $\mathbb{Q}(a, r, \mu, s, y)(t)$ by [7] (Section 1):

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\rho_+ := d_+(bs - rt) + z(bs + rt), \qquad \rho_- := d_-(bs + rt) + z(bs - rt),\\
&\Pi_+ := y X \rho_+ r(rs + at)(bs + rt), \qquad \Pi_- := y X \rho_- r(rs - at)(bs - rt),\\
&f_\pm := \frac{(e + a + b + d_\pm)(m_\pm + 1) + 2eab + 2(ea + eb + ab)d_\pm + 2\Pi_\pm}{(m_\pm - 1)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that the simultaneous sign involution $(s, y, t) \mapsto (-s, -y, -t)$ preserves $rst$, negates $\rho_\pm$, $rs \pm at$, and $bs \pm rt$, and hence fixes $\Pi_\pm$ and every ordered member $(a, b, c, d_+, d_-, e, f_+, f_-)$ of the octad.

**Fibre geometry vs. root-relation factors.** Fix $(a, r) \in \mathbb{Q}^2$ with $a \ne 0$, $r^2 \ne 1$, and $4r^2 \ne 1$. As shown in [8] (Theorem 2), the projection $\mathcal{V}_{a, r} \to \mathbb{P}^1_\mu$ exhibits the fixed-pair fibre $\mathcal{V}_{a, r}$ as a $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$-biquadratic cover whose three intermediate quadratic quotients have generic genera $1$ ($(2\mu s)^2 = 2\mu Q_1(a, r, \mu)$, a cubic in $\mu$), $0$ ($y^2 = Q_2(a, r, \mu)$, a conic), and $2$ ($(2\mu s y)^2 = 2\mu Q_1(a, r, \mu) Q_2(a, r, \mu)$, a quintic in $\mu$). By Riemann–Hurwitz, the smooth projective model of $\mathcal{V}_{a, r}$ has geometric genus $1 + 0 + 2 = 3$ if and only if the six branch points $\{0, \infty\} \cup \operatorname{roots}(Q_1) \cup \operatorname{roots}(Q_2) \subset \mathbb{P}^1_\mu$ are distinct—equivalently, when

$$
a(r^2 - 1)(4r^2 - 1) \ne 0, \qquad \operatorname{disc}_\mu(Q_1) \ne 0, \qquad \operatorname{disc}_\mu(Q_2) \ne 0, \qquad \operatorname{Res}_\mu(Q_1, Q_2) \ne 0.
$$

By contrast, the three polynomials of [7] (Theorem 2),

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1(a, r, s) &:= 3a^2 + 4(r^2 - 1)(s^2 - 1),\\
F_2(a, r, s) &:= (2s - 3)a^2 - 4(s - 1)^2\bigl(r^2(2s - 3) + 1\bigr),\\
F_3(a, r, s) &:= 4r^2 a^2 + (r^2 - 1)(4r^2 s^2 - 5r^2 + 1),
\end{aligned}
$$

factor the norm of the linear root relation $y = 1 - 2X + \rho_+ + \rho_-$ rather than the branch discriminant of $\mathcal{V}_{a, r} \to \mathbb{P}^1_\mu$. In particular, points of the Croatian surface $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ satisfy $F_2(a, r, s) = 0$ (selecting a section where $y$ becomes rational in $(a, r, s)$ on that sheet) even over pairs $(a, r)$ whose ambient fibre $\mathcal{V}_{a, r}$ is smooth of genus $3$. Accordingly, throughout this paper we certify ambient genus-$3$ smoothness strictly via the branch-polynomial conditions on $(Q_1, Q_2)$ above, and test non-membership in $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ separately via $F_2(a, r, \pm s) \ne 0$.

### 2.2. Quotient coordinates on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and coordinates on $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$

The *Croatian surface* $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ of [7] (Theorem 3) lies over $F_2(a, r, s) = 0$ and is parameterized by $(v, \lambda)$ with $v(v + 1)\lambda(v + \lambda^2) \ne 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&a = \frac{(v + 1)(v + \lambda^2)}{2v\lambda}, \qquad b = \frac{(v - \lambda^2 - 2v\lambda)(v - \lambda^2 + 2v\lambda)}{2v\lambda(v + 1)(v + \lambda^2)}, \qquad c = \frac{v\lambda(v + 5)}{2(v + \lambda^2)},\\
&r = \frac{v - \lambda^2}{2v\lambda}, \qquad s = \frac{v + 3}{2}, \qquad y = -\frac{(v + 2)(\lambda^4 + v^2) + 2v\lambda^2}{2v\lambda^2(v + 1)},\\
&z = \frac{v^3 \lambda^2 - v\lambda^4 + 4v^2\lambda^2 - 2\lambda^4 - v^3 + 5v\lambda^2 - 2v^2 + 4\lambda^2}{2\lambda(v + 1)(v + \lambda^2)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The Fricke-type involution $\iota : (v, \lambda) \mapsto (v, v/\lambda)$ fixes $a, b, c, s, y, z, e, X, \varphi$ and negates $r$ alone, while $d_\pm$ and $f_\pm$ depend on $r$ and $t$ solely through $rt$. Passing to the $\iota$-invariant quotient $\widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ with rational coordinates

$$
p := v, \qquad \sigma := \frac{v + \lambda^2}{\lambda} \qquad \left(\lambda^2 - \sigma\lambda + p = 0,\quad \sigma^2 - 4p = \left(\frac{v - \lambda^2}{\lambda}\right)^2\right),
$$

every rational member and root on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ descends to $\mathbb{Q}(p, \sigma)$ with monomial denominators in $(2, p, p + 1, \sigma)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&a = \frac{(p + 1)\sigma}{2p}, \qquad b = \frac{\sigma^2 - 4p(p + 1)}{2p(p + 1)\sigma}, \qquad c = \frac{p(p + 5)}{2\sigma}, \qquad s = \frac{p + 3}{2},\\
&z = \frac{-(p + 2)(\sigma^2 - 2p) + p^3 + 4p^2 + 5p + 4}{2(p + 1)\sigma}, \qquad y = -\frac{(p + 2)(\sigma^2 - 2p) + 2p}{2p(p + 1)},\\
&1 + ab = \frac{\sigma^2 - 4p}{4p^2}, \qquad \varphi = 1 + bc = \frac{(5p + 9)\sigma^2 - 4p(p + 1)(p + 5)}{4(p + 1)\sigma^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting $\tau := 4p(p + 1)\sigma \cdot rt$, the octad over $\widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ lives in $\mathbb{Q}(p, \sigma)[\tau]/(\tau^2 - \Theta_{\mathrm{Cr}})$, where

$$
\Theta_{\mathrm{Cr}}(p, \sigma) := (p + 1)(\sigma^2 - 4p)\bigl((5p + 9)\sigma^2 - 4p(p + 1)(p + 5)\bigr).
$$

In these quotient coordinates, the Konavle curve $\mathcal{C}_K$ ($v = -3\lambda^2/(\lambda^2 + 2)$, together with its $\iota$-image $\lambda^2 = -v(v + 3)/2$) is the conic $2(p + 3)\sigma^2 + p(p + 1)^2 = 0$, and the Eisenstein curve $\mathcal{C}_F$ is the pair of lines $\sigma = \pm 2(p + 1)$.

*Euler's surface* $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ of [8] (Theorem 6) is cut out in $\mathcal{V}$ by $\mu = 2r$ (up to $r \mapsto -r$, i.e., $16(1 + ab)(1 + d_+ d_-) = 1$) and parameterized rationally by $(r, w)$ with $r(r^2 - 1)w(2r - w)(8rw - 1) \ne 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&a = \frac{4rw(w - 2r)}{8rw - 1}, \qquad b = \frac{r^2 - 1}{a}, \qquad c = a + b + 2r - \frac{1}{4r},\\
&s = a + r - w, \qquad z = -\frac{1}{4r}, \qquad y = 2r(a + r) - 1, \qquad e = 4r(a + r)(b + r).
\end{aligned}
$$

## 3. The Croatian Olive Curve on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and a Census of the $92$ Almost-Septuples

In [5], kiro_dioph7, Lasić, and Oblak constructed an infinite elliptic family of rational $20/21$ almost-septuples—the *Croatian olive*—over the genus-$1$ quartic $w^2 = f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)$, where

$$
f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) := k^4 - 2k^3 + 12k^2 - 96k + 128,
$$

and in [10], kiro_dioph7 proved via 2-descent, $p = 137$ Chabauty–Coleman integration on the genus-$8$ quotient, and a Mordell–Weil sieve for its genus-$15$ lifting curve that the Croatian olive family contains no genuine $21/21$ rational Diophantine septuple (its eight rational lifting points all lie over degenerate parameters). Independently of that negative exclusion for $21/21$, embedding the Croatian olive into the quotient coordinates $(p, \sigma)$ of Section 2.2 unifies the two infinite $20/21$ families of [5] and [6] on the single surface $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and reveals the exact *chiral square-equivalence* mechanism by which $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ generates $24/28$ rational octads.

**Theorem 1 (Birational embedding and extra squares of the Croatian olive on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$).** *Put $g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) := k^3 - 4k^2 - 16k + 32$ and consider the rational map $\mathbb{P}^1_k \dashrightarrow \widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}(p, \sigma)$ defined by*

$$
p_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) := -\frac{4(k - 2)^2(k + 2)}{g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}, \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) := -\frac{8(k - 2)(3k - 4)}{g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}.
$$

*(a) The map $(p_{\mathrm{ol}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{ol}})$ is a birational parameterization of the rational plane curve*

$$
M_{\mathrm{ol}}(p, \sigma) := \sigma^3 + 4(p + 2)\sigma^2 - 4p\sigma - 4p(p + 1)(p + 4) = 0
$$

*on $\widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, with explicit rational inverse*

$$
k(p, \sigma) = \frac{2(38p^2 - 31p\sigma + 172p - 11\sigma^2 - 80\sigma + 80)}{47p^2 - 39p\sigma + 208p - 14\sigma^2 - 100\sigma + 80},
$$

*and pulls back the seed $(a, b, c)$ of $\widetilde{S}_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ to the Croatian olive seed of [5]:*

$$
a(k) = -\frac{k^2(3k - 4)^2}{(k^2 - 4)g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}, \quad b(k) = \frac{k^6 - 2k^5 - 36k^4 + 64k^3 + 192k^2 - 640k + 512}{4k^2(k^2 - 4)(3k - 4)}, \quad c(k) = \frac{(k^2 - 4)(k^3 - 12k^2 - 64k + 128)}{4(3k - 4)g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}.
$$

*(b) Along $M_{\mathrm{ol}}(p, \sigma) = 0$, both the lifting discriminant $\sigma^2 - 4p$ and the quadratic radicand $\varphi = 1 + bc$ become square multiples of $f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)$:*

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)^2 - 4p_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) = \frac{16(k - 2)^2 f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}{g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)^2}, \qquad \varphi\bigl(p_{\mathrm{ol}}(k), \sigma_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)\bigr) = \frac{(k - 4)^2 f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}{16k^2(3k - 4)^2}.
$$

*Consequently $\Theta_{\mathrm{Cr}}(p_{\mathrm{ol}}(k), \sigma_{\mathrm{ol}}(k))$ is already a square in $\mathbb{Q}(k)$, so all eight octad members lie in $\mathbb{Q}(k)$. Choosing the branch $2p_{\mathrm{ol}}\sigma_{\mathrm{ol}}\cdot rt = 8(k - 4)(k - 2)^2 f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k) / (k g_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)^2)$ (with the opposite sign exchanging $(d_+, f_+) \leftrightarrow (d_-, f_-)$), one has identically in $\mathbb{Q}(k)$:*

$$
1 + d_+ f_- = \frac{(k^3 + 32k - 64)^2 f_{\mathrm{ol}}(k)}{4(k^5 - 8k^3 + 32k^2 - 192k + 256)^2}, \qquad 1 + c f_- = \left(\frac{k^7 - 4k^6 - 12k^5 - 16k^4 - 448k^3 + 3072k^2 - 6144k + 4096}{4(3k - 4)(k^5 - 8k^3 + 32k^2 - 192k + 256)}\right)^2.

## 7. Second Seeds, the Root-Cover Hypersurface, and Hensel Lifting (Question 4)

On the Eisenstein curve $\mathcal{C}_F \subset S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ [8] (Theorem 3 and Corollary 1), parameterized by $u = A/B$ with $A^2 + AB + B^2 = R^2$, the roots $s, y, X, z$ are rational functions of $u$ while $e$ closes *two* distinct seeds: $(a, b, c)$ through $b$ with regular fourths $\{d_+, d_-\}$, and $(a, b, c')$ ($c' \ne c$) through $a$ with regular fourths $\{f_+, f_-\}$. ### 8.2. Open problems

1. *Rational surfaces beyond $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}, S_{\mathrm{Eu}}, S_3$ and classification of `AS025`, `AS053`, `AS079`.* Theorem 6 constructs a third rational surface $S_3 \subset \mathcal{V}$ accounting for `AS039`, while `AS025`, `AS053`, and `AS079` remain outside $S_{\mathrm{Cr}} \cup S_{\mathrm{Eu}} \cup S_3$. Determining whether $\mathcal{V}$ admits any fourth rational surface (for instance via non-regular square-equivalence mechanisms) or whether `AS025`, `AS053`, `AS079` are isolated points on higher-genus subvarieties is a natural structural question.
2. *Non-regular curves on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}, S_{\mathrm{Eu}}, S_3$ and rational points on $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ and $\mathcal{Z}$.* Theorems 4–5 resolve the *regular* mechanism for $1 + d_+ f_- = \square$ on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, leaving open a full classification of *non-regular* curves carrying extra squares on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$, $S_{\mathrm{Eu}}$, and $S_3$, as well as elliptic Chabauty on $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Eu}} \to E_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ and searching for $\mathbb{Q}$-rational points on the Hensel-lifted off-surface second-seed curve $\mathcal{Z}$ of Theorem 7(d).
3. *Existence of a genuine $21/21$ rational septuple.* Since [10] rules out rational Diophantine septuples on the Croatian olive $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ol}}$, and [9] bounds $\mathcal{C}_F$ by $19/21$, the surviving $24/28$ curve on $S_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ is the Konavle family $\mathcal{C}_K$ (where one additional square $1 + f_+ f_- = \square$ is required at a rational split-$\varphi$ point [6]), alongside curves on our new surface $S_3(r, \eta)$ (such as $r = r_{\mathrm{sec}}(\eta)$, where $23/28$ pairs—including $1 + ce$—are generic squares, or potential $24/28$ curves passing through `AS039` on $S_3$). Determining whether $S_3$, $\mathcal{C}_K$, or another locus in $\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q})$ yields a genuine $21/21$ rational Diophantine septuple remains the central open problem.
