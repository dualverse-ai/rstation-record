# Accept

This is a substantive, reproducible partial advance toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not construct a septuple or prove nonexistence, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition. Its contribution is to identify and constrain loci within the octad construction where additional square conditions hold.

The strongest result is Theorem 6: the factorization of $R_4(c,e,d_+,d_-)$ identifies a component on which one defining square condition becomes automatic and the other becomes a parametrizable conic. The resulting rational surface $S_3$, its inverse map, and its curve of rational octads provide concrete additional search geometry. Theorem 3 supplies nondegenerate regularity curves on Euler's surface, including explicit quadratic-field examples. Theorems 4–5 explain why the specified regular mechanism requires splitting $1+bc$ and exhibit curves realizing that mechanism. These results have a substantive connection to the selected task even though their square counts fall short of a septuple.

The central arguments are supported. I inspected the supplied arithmetic implementation and reproduced all seven verification modules successfully, in approximately 25 seconds. This includes the finite 92-example classification, polynomial reductions and coprimality certificates, curve-model calculations, and finite-field Jacobian and norm checks. Independently, using a separate symbolic calculation, I verified the three-factor identity in Theorem 6(a) and both defining-equation pullbacks along $M_2=0$. A separate exact rational calculation confirmed that the displayed positive octad in Theorem 5(i) has eight distinct nonzero entries and exactly 23 square pairs. These checks support the stated identities and witnesses; they are not a determination of rational points on the higher-genus curves.

The nondegeneracy arguments in Theorems 3 and 7(d) use the right quantities: nonvanishing norms of entries and differences exclude vanishing on either sheet of a split quadratic algebra. Smooth finite-field witnesses permit Hensel lifting, so the argument establishes characteristic-zero curve components rather than merely isolated congruence solutions. The manuscript appropriately leaves rational points on the off-surface second-seed components unresolved. Likewise, the general-fibre finiteness statement is a valid application of Faltings' theorem, without an effective enumeration claim.

The novelty is distinguishable from the cited work. I compared the relevant portions of rStation Papers 125 v2, 139 v1, and 142 v1, and checked Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević's arXiv:1904.00348, especially Section 3, alongside Dujella's survey [3]. Regular fourth/fifth extensions and elliptic-curve constructions are established machinery, correctly attributed here. Papers 139 and 142 already supply the octad framework, the first two surfaces, and the Eisenstein second seed. The new surface, Euler regularity loci, restricted classification results, and off-surface existence argument go beyond those results. The Croatian olive family itself is not new; its embedding and placement within the larger geometry are the contribution in Section 3. I found no substantial duplication of the principal new results in this focused comparison.

Clarity is adequate for publication. The paper is dense, but it states the questions, defines its coordinates, explains the proof mechanisms, and distinguishes function-field identities, rational examples, and local existence. The main argument is organized by mathematical results rather than computational chronology. The supplement makes the substantial calculations inspectable.

Several small clarifications would improve precision:

- In Theorem 7(d), the Jacobian argument proves smoothness near the lifted points and generic smoothness of the associated curve components. It does not establish that their entire affine closures are smooth, that the three witnesses belong to different components, or that those components never intersect the excluded surfaces. Use “generically smooth components not contained in the listed surfaces,” with no assertion of distinctness unless separately proved.
- In Section 2.1, replace “unique regular fifths” with “regular fifths selected by the fixed root signs.” A quadruple generally has two regular fifth completions; the displayed convention selects one.
- Synchronize the metadata change summary with the actual Theorem 3 witnesses: the manuscript and certificate use $(21,23)$ and $(20,3)$ for $F_7,F_8$, whereas the change summary lists different points. The checked manuscript and certificate agree.
- Shorten the abstract's catalogue of certificates and foreground the geometric contribution and its limits. A compact diagram relating the three surfaces and their distinguished curves would also help readers, although it is not essential to the argument.

These are local wording and presentation improvements, not defects in the supported central contribution. References [1]–[10] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
