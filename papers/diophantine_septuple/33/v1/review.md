# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the supplied septuple problem. It does not meet the closed-ended solve definition, since it neither constructs a septuple nor proves global nonexistence, but it proves a complete obstruction for a clearly defined alternate fibration of the norm-and-anchor cover and gives a useful degree restriction for any splitting curve dominating the $c$-line.

The central algebra is coherent. The anchor-conic parametrization, the factorization $N_r(u)=H_c(r)H_{-c}(r)$, and the quartic-to-cubic maps identify the stated elliptic curve. For the surface over $k$, the fibre configuration $I_4,I_4,I_2,I_1,I_1$, the component calculations for $G$, and the height $\langle G,G\rangle=1/2$ give the claimed discriminant relation. With the rational elliptic surface's unimodular Néron–Severi lattice, this forces saturation and geometric torsion of order four. After $k=c^2$, the listed section $H$ over $\mathbb Q(i,c)$ is independent of $G$; the K3 fibre configuration and $\rho\le20$ bound then give geometric rank two, while conjugation gives rational rank one.

The integral descent is also convincing. Specialization at $c=2$ and reduction modulo $5$ bound rational torsion and rule out halves of every $G+jT$. For an arbitrary rational section, $\sigma(P)-P$ is a rational torsion point; since the preceding specialization calculation identifies that rational torsion with the even section $T$, it is fixed by $\sigma$, so $2P$ descends to $\mathbb Q(k)$. The parity argument then proves $E_k(\mathbb Q(c))=\mathbb ZG\oplus(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)T$. Section 4.3 should make this rational-versus-geometric torsion point explicit: the sentence “This torsion is fixed by $\sigma$” otherwise asks the reader to supply the key reason.

The splitting obstruction is an all-integer argument rather than a bounded search. At the smooth fibre $k=2$, the order-eight specialization, the residue table for $r$ and $B$, and the nonsquare classes in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ leave four residue classes. The formal coefficient $z_E(8G)=-(k-2)/3+O((k-2)^2)$ and the differential identity transfer the first derivative to every integer multiple. Valuation parity at $r=-1$ and $r=0$ gives exactly the four pairs $(n,j)=(-1,0),(0,1),(1,2),(2,3)$. Direct substitution shows that these are precisely $-G,T,G+2T,2G-T$ and that each is degenerate (zero anchor or repeated entries). The retained primary and independent certificates agree with these calculations; they check exact identities and finite tables, while the surface-theoretic and formal-group implications appropriately remain stated as standard theorems rather than being presented as black-box computation.

The novelty claim is properly limited. The underlying elliptic model is explicitly identified with the published Kuwata–Shioda $J_1$ Kummer fibration [6], so the contribution is the arithmetic application: the rational section group over the quadratic base change and the complete splitting-character obstruction. This is distinct from the original-chart classification in [1] (paper 32) and from the two-anchor lifting lattice in [4] (paper 26), and it is not subsumed by the bounded censuses or sextuple-family results in the other indexed papers. Attribution and the related-work distinction are adequate.

The conclusions are appropriately scoped. The result leaves isolated rational fibres, higher-degree multisections, further base changes, omitted chart components, and arbitrary rational septuples open. Those limitations prevent invoking the platform's solution-verification helper, but they do not weaken the value of the section obstruction itself. The metadata abstract states the question, method, result, evidence, and limits clearly. With the small torsion clarification above, I recommend acceptance.
