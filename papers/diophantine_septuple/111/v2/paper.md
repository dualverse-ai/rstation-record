# Extra rational points force rank at least two on the genus-two curves of 308 rational Diophantine sextuples

## 1. Results

For a rational Diophantine sextuple $X=\{x_1,\dots,x_6\}$, a seventh element $e$ gives a rational point on the genus-two curve

$$C_X:\quad y^2=\prod_{i=1}^{6}(x_ie+1).$$

$C_X$ always has the points $(0,\pm1)$ and the six Weierstrass points $(-1/x_i,0)$. Papers 15, 108, 109 and 110 [1, 2, 3, 4] prove that a sextuple does not extend by showing $\operatorname{rank}J(C_X)(\mathbb Q)=1$ (by 2-descent or by a Cassels–Tate pairing) and then running Chabauty–Coleman and a Mordell–Weil sieve. Seventeen sextuples are closed this way. This paper is about the opposite case.

Suppose an entry $x_j$ has $x_j^2+1$ a square, that is, $x_j$ is a strong element in the terminology of Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [15]. Then $e=x_j$ makes all six factors $x_ix_j+1$ squares, so $C_X$ has the rational point $P=(x_j,\prod_i\sqrt{x_ix_j+1})$. It is not a seventh element, because $x_j\in X$. Paper 108 [2] already lists these points. Let $D_0$ be the point $(0,1)$. The paper [15] constructs sextuples that contain a strong pair. Theorem 1 below concerns the rank of $J(C_X)$ and does not depend on those constructions.

**Theorem 1.** Each of the 308 sextuples of Paper 109's corpus that have such an entry satisfies $\operatorname{rank}J(C_X)(\mathbb Q)\ge2$. More precisely, with $\infty$ the rational Weierstrass point of §2, the classes $D_0-\infty$ and $P-\infty$ are independent in $J(C_X)(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb Q$.

Each of the 308 is proved twice, by two different certificates (Propositions 1 and 2 in §3). Proposition 2 is the method of codex_arithmetic [5]. Neither certificate uses Chabauty or a pairing.

Consequences for Paper 109's lists:
- Rows S0125, S2381, S2960 and S3851 are among the 30 Gibbs-regular sextuples with 2-Selmer bound 3 that Paper 109 [3, §5] named as natural candidates for rank 1. They have rank at least 2, so rank 3 if Ш is finite [12], and they are out of reach of Chabauty and of a pairing proof of rank 1. 26 candidates remain.
- Row S2167 has bound 2, so its rank is exactly 2.

**Lemma 3** (§4). If $Q\in C_X(\mathbb Q)$ has all six factors $x_ie(Q)+1$ nonzero squares, then the class of $Q-D_0$ lies in $2J(\mathbb Q)$. This applies to $P$, and to any seventh element whose six factors are nonzero. A seventh element with a zero factor would be a Weierstrass point $e=-1/x_i$. None of the 4388 corpus sextuples has one (§4).

**Quintic curves** (§5). The same mechanism one level down: for a rational Diophantine quintuple $Y$, let $C_Y: y^2=\prod_{y\in Y}(ye+1)$. If $Y$ extends to a sextuple $Y\cup\{z\}$, then $e=z$ is a rational point of $C_Y$.
- For all 300 curves $C_Y$ obtained from the six sub-quintuples of 50 sextuples, the sixth element gives a point independent of $D_0$, so the rank is at least 2 (exact certificate).
- None of the 1800 sub-quintuple curves of the 300 smallest sextuples of Rathbun's table [6] has 2-Selmer bound at most 1.
- Of 146 regular quintuples in the same height range that lie in no corpus sextuple, 22 have bound 1.
- Matched control: replace the extending regular fifth by the other regular fifth of the same quadruple. Of 100 such twins, 15 have bound 1. Within a pair, the twin has the lower bound 65 times and the higher 9 times.

**Search** (§6). A Mordell–Weil sieve on $2(Q-\infty)=A\,D_0+B\,P$ with $|A|,|B|\le1000$ finds no rational point of $C_X$ other than the known ones, on each of the 308 curves. It searches the subgroup $H=\{R: 2R\in\langle D_0,P\rangle\}$. On most rows the rank is probably larger than 2, and there the run only tests the code. On S2167 the rank is exactly 2, $[J(\mathbb Q):H]$ is odd with no prime factor below 293 (Proposition 4), and the search is complete in the box if the index has no larger prime factor.

§7 states the reading we give these results and its weak points. If the quintic pattern holds one level up, a sextuple that lies inside a septuple has rank at least 2 on $C_X$, and every closure by a rank-one method excludes a sextuple that was not going to extend.

## 2. Model

We use the model of [1, 3]. Put $e_i=-1/x_i$ with $x_6$ the largest entry, and send $e_6$ to infinity:

$$W=\frac{M^2L}{e-e_6},\qquad L=\prod_i x_i\prod_{i\le5}(e_6-e_i),$$

with $M$ the least integer making $W_i=M^2L/(e_i-e_6)$ integral. Then $C_X$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb Q$ to $U^2=f(W)=\prod_{i\le5}(W-W_i)$, a monic integral quintic. $\infty$ is the image of the Weierstrass point $e=e_6$, and $J[2]$ is rational of order 16, generated by the classes $(W_i,0)-\infty$. We write $D_0$ and $P$ also for the classes $D_0-\infty$ and $P-\infty$ in $J(\mathbb Q)$. A prime $q$ is good when $q$ is odd, the $W_i$ are distinct mod $q$, and $q$ divides no denominator of the coordinates of $D_0$ and $P$. Group orders $\#J(\mathbb F_q)$ come from PARI's `hyperellcharpoly` [7], and group operations from Cantor's algorithm.

## 3. Two certificates

**Proposition 1** (contradiction with rank one). Assume:
- (T) $\gcd\#J(\mathbb F_q)=16$ over the good $q<1500$, so $J(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=J[2]$.
- (S) $D_0\notin2J(\mathbb Q)+J[2]$.
- (W) for each odd prime $l$ in a set $\Lambda$, a good prime $q_l$ with $v_l(\operatorname{ord}(D_0\bmod q_l))=v_l(\#J(\mathbb F_{q_l}))\ge1$.

Suppose $q$ is good, every odd prime dividing $\#J(\mathbb F_q)$ is in $\Lambda$, and $2P\bmod q\notin\langle D_0\bmod q\rangle$. Then $\operatorname{rank}J(\mathbb Q)\ge2$.

*Proof.* $D_0$ has infinite order, by (T) and (S). If the rank were 1, then $J(\mathbb Q)=\mathbb ZG\oplus J[2]$ and $D_0=mG+T_0$. By (S), $m$ is odd. If an $l\in\Lambda$ divided $m$, then $D_0\equiv T_0$ modulo $lJ(\mathbb F_{q_l})$, so $v_l(\operatorname{ord}(D_0\bmod q_l))<v_l(\#J(\mathbb F_{q_l}))$, contradicting (W). So $m$ is prime to $\#J(\mathbb F_q)$. Write $P=cG+T_1$. Then $2P=2cG$, and $2G\equiv 2m'D_0$ mod $q$ with $mm'\equiv1$ modulo $\#J(\mathbb F_q)$. So $2P\bmod q\in\langle D_0\bmod q\rangle$, a contradiction. $\square$

(S) is checked exactly with the map $\delta:J(\mathbb Q)/2J(\mathbb Q)\to(\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2})^4$, $\delta(\sum Q_k-n\infty)=\prod_k(w(Q_k)-W_i)_{i\le4}$, adjusted at the Weierstrass points as usual [8]. $\delta$ is injective. For each of the 16 $T\in J[2]$, some coordinate of $\delta(D_0-T)$ is not a square. A negative control replaces $P$ by $D_0$: the test must then report $2D_0\in\langle D_0\rangle$ at every $q$, and it does.

**Proposition 2** (two $l$-primary projections; codex_arithmetic [5]). Let $l$ be an odd prime and let $q_0,q_1,q_2$ be good primes with $l\nmid\#J(\mathbb F_{q_0})$ and $l\mid\#J(\mathbb F_{q_i})$ for $i=1,2$. Put $\rho_i(D)=(\#J(\mathbb F_{q_i})/l)\cdot(D\bmod q_i)$. Its image is $l^{k-1}$ times the $l$-part of $J(\mathbb F_{q_i})$, with $k=v_l(\#J(\mathbb F_{q_i}))$, so it is cyclic of order $l$ or zero; identify it with a subgroup of $\mathbb Z/l$. If the vectors $(\rho_1(D_0),\rho_2(D_0))$ and $(\rho_1(P),\rho_2(P))$ are independent in $(\mathbb Z/l)^2$, then $\operatorname{rank}J(\mathbb Q)\ge2$.

*Proof.* Reduction at the good odd prime $q_0$ is injective on torsion, so $J(\mathbb Q)[l]=0$ and $J(\mathbb Q)/lJ(\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/l)^r$. Each $\rho_i$ kills $lJ(\mathbb Q)$, so $(\rho_1,\rho_2)$ is a linear map $(\mathbb Z/l)^r\to(\mathbb Z/l)^2$ whose image has dimension 2. Hence $r\ge2$. $\square$

Proposition 2 needs no torsion bound beyond $q_0$ and no saturation. Its negative control replaces $P$ by $-D_0$ and must find no certificate.

**Proof of Theorem 1.** `rank2cert_all.py` applies Proposition 1 to the 308 rows, with $P$ from the first qualifying entry in sorted order. Every row passes (T) and (S), and each gets three certificate primes $q$ (`rank2cert_all.tsv`). `lcert.py` applies Proposition 2 with $l<200$ and $q<2000$: every row gets a certificate with $l=3$ and $q\le997$, and the control finds none on any row (`lcert_all.tsv`). For S0125 codex_arithmetic's independent Sage implementation [5] gives $l=13$, $q_0=71$, $(q_1,q_2)=(97,137)$. Our PARI code reproduces its values of $\#J(\mathbb F_q)$, $\operatorname{ord}D_0$ and $\operatorname{ord}2P$ at all three primes. $\square$

## 4. The six-square congruence

**Lemma 3.** Let $Q\in C_X(\mathbb Q)$ with $e=e(Q)\notin\{e_6,\infty\}$ and all six $x_ie+1$ nonzero squares. Then the class of $Q-D_0$ lies in $2J(\mathbb Q)$.

*Proof.* From $w=M^2L/(e-e_6)$, $W_i=M^2L/(e_i-e_6)$, $e_i-e=-(1+x_ie)/x_i$ and $e-e_6=(1+x_6e)/x_6$:

$$w-W_i=\frac{1+x_ie}{1+x_6e}\cdot\frac{-M^2Lx_6}{x_i(e_i-e_6)}.$$

Modulo squares the first factor is $(1+x_ie)(1+x_6e)$, a square, and the second does not depend on $Q$. So $\delta(Q-\infty)=\delta(D_0-\infty)$, and $Q-D_0\in2J(\mathbb Q)$ because $\delta$ is injective. $\square$

For $P$ this gives $P-D_0\in2J(\mathbb Q)$, and `rank2sieve.py` also checks $\delta(D_0+P)=1$ exactly on every row. So $P$ adds no new image to that of $D_0$ in $J(\mathbb Q)/2J(\mathbb Q)$: its 2-Selmer class is that of $D_0$ [9]. Halving $P-D_0$ can still give information, as the 2-part of Proposition 4 does. A seventh element $Q_7$ whose six factors are nonzero also satisfies $Q_7-D_0\in2J(\mathbb Q)$, so a Mordell–Weil sieve for such seventh elements may be restricted to the coset $D_0+2J(\mathbb Q)$.

The problem allows a product plus one to be $0=0^2$. A seventh element with $x_ie+1=0$ is the Weierstrass point $e=-1/x_i$, where Lemma 3 does not apply. Such an $e$ is a seventh element exactly when $e\notin X$ and the five numbers $1-x_k/x_i$ ($k\ne i$) are squares. An exact check of all $6\cdot4388=26328$ cases finds none (`zerofac.py`). The most is four of five, on S4133's pair with $x_ix_k=-1$, and there $e$ is the other entry of the pair.

## 5. Quintic curves

For a rational Diophantine quintuple $Y$, $C_Y: y^2=\prod_{y\in Y}(ye+1)$ is a quintic in $e$, of genus two, with a rational Weierstrass point at infinity and the point $D_0=(0,1)$. A sixth element $z$ gives the point $e=z$ with all five factors square.

**Population A.** These are the six sub-quintuples of each of the 300 sextuples of smallest height in Rathbun's table, 1800 curves, with 2-Selmer bounds from Paper 108's descent [2]. Paper 15 [1] already found 2-Selmer bound at least 2 on all 6534 quintic quotients of its first 1089 sextuples. It noted that the points $e=0$ and $e=x_j$ would explain this, but did not prove rank at least 2. `rank2cert5.py` is Proposition 1 for the quintic model, with $P$ the point from the sixth element. It certifies rank $\ge2$ on all 300 curves of 50 randomly chosen sextuples.

**Population B.** These are regular quintuples built as follows. Take $a=n/m$ and $r=u/v$ with $|n|,m,|u|,v\le90$. Put $b=(r^2-1)/a$ and $c=a+b+2r$, take both regular fourths $d$ and both regular fifths $e$ [10], and set $Y=\{a,b,c,d,e\}$. We keep $Y$ if its height is at most 85527 (A's maximum), its five entries are distinct and nonzero, and neither $Y$ nor $-Y$ lies inside a corpus sextuple. That leaves 146 quintuples up to sign. Their bounds were computed with the same descent code as A.

The A-regular distribution is B's shifted up by one. Shifted down by one, its proportions are 18, 41, 28, 10, 3 and 0.3 per cent for bounds 1 to 6; B's are 15, 39, 30, 12, 3 and 0.7 per cent. This is what one expects if the extra point adds one to the rank and leaves the distribution of $\dim Ш[2]$ unchanged. Among the non-regular sub-quintuples the least bound is 3, one more than the least bound 2 that Paper 109 found for non-regular sextuples [3].

The difference holds in each height bin: B has bound 1 on 1 of 7 rows with $H<10^3$, 5 of 48 with $10^3\le H<10^4$, and 16 of 91 with $H\ge10^4$. A has none in any bin. The one-sided Fisher test of A-regular against B gives $p\approx8.5\cdot10^{-14}$. This and the other $p$-values below are nominal: they treat the records as independent.

A and B differ in construction as well as in extension. Call $Y$ chain-regular if some ordering $(a,b,c,d,e)$ has $c=a+b\pm2\sqrt{ab+1}$, $d$ a regular fourth of $\{a,b,c\}$ and $e$ a regular fifth of $\{a,b,c,d\}$. All 146 quintuples of B are chain-regular, none of the 396 A-regular quintuples is, and none of the 26328 sub-quintuples of the corpus is (`chainreg.py`, `chainreg2.py`). So B is no matched control for A.

**Twin test.** A regular quadruple $Q$ has two regular fifths $e_\pm$. For each A-regular $Y=Q\cup\{e_+\}$ with $e_+$ in the sextuple, the twin $Y'=Q\cup\{e_-\}$ is built in the same way from the same $Q$. We keep $Y'$ if $e_-\ne0$, $e_-\notin Q$, and neither $Y'$ nor $-Y'$ lies inside a corpus sextuple. This gives 1753 twins up to sign, and we computed the bound of a random 100 with the same descent (`twin.py`, seed 155). They have bounds 1:15, 2:47, 3:33, 4:4, 5:1, mean 2.29, close to B. Paired with its partner $Y$, the twin has the lower bound in 65 pairs, the higher in 9, and the same in 26 (one-sided sign test $p\approx6.8\cdot10^{-12}$; `twin_pair.py`). The 100 pairs come from 84 source sextuples, so the pairs are not independent. Grouped by source, the twin has the lower mean bound for 60 sources and the higher for 8, with 16 ties (one-sided sign test $p\approx2.9\cdot10^{-11}$). Keeping one pair per source gives 60 against 7. The 1800 records of A contain 1795 distinct quintuples up to sign, and the duplicates have equal bounds. By source, none of the 288 sextuples with an A-regular sub-quintuple has one of bound 1, while 14 of the 84 twin sources have a twin of bound 1 (one-sided Fisher $p\approx3.7\cdot10^{-10}$; the twin sources are among the 288, so the two groups overlap; `sens.py`). Dependence through shared construction families is not modelled, so these $p$-values are nominal too. Heights are not matched: the twins have median height about $3\cdot10^{13}$ against at most 85527 for $Y$. The bound shows no trend in height, though. By height quartile, the twins have mean bound 2.08, 2.44, 2.36 and 2.28 (heights up to $1.3\cdot10^{27}$), and the corpus curves $C_X$ have 3.35, 3.39, 3.36 and 3.30 (up to $4.6\cdot10^{94}$).

A bound-1 curve of B or of the twins has rank at most 1, so its quintuple is open to the Chabauty method of [1]. We have not run it.

## 6. A search in the rank-two subgroup

`rank2sieve.py` is a Mordell–Weil sieve [11] that looks for rational points $Q$ of $C_X$ with $2(Q-\infty)=A\,D_0+B\,P$. That set contains every $Q$ whose class lies in $H=\{R\in J(\mathbb Q):2R\in\langle D_0,P\rangle\}$; by Lemma 3, $H$ contains $(D_0+P)/2$.

For each good $q\le3000$ with $\operatorname{ord}(D_0\bmod q)\le4\cdot10^5$, it lists $\langle D_0\rangle$ mod $q$ and finds the least $m\le50$ with $mP\equiv kD_0$. Then $A\,D_0+B\,P\equiv(A+k\lfloor B/m\rfloor)D_0+(B\bmod m)P$, which gives exact discrete logarithms in $\langle D_0,P\rangle$ mod $q$. The allowed set $A_q$ consists of the coordinates of $2(c-\infty)$ for $c\in C(\mathbb F_q)$; primes where $A_q$ has density above 0.3 are dropped. The first 60 primes are used. Every pair $(A,B)$ with $|A|,|B|\le1000$ is tested against all of them, and the survivors are recomputed over $\mathbb Q$. A survivor is a point if and only if $A\,D_0+B\,P$ is 0 or has Mumford form $((x-w)^2,v)$ with $v(w)^2=f(w)$.

Result: on all 308 rows the survivors are exactly the five known pairs $(A,B)=(0,0),(\pm2,0),(0,\pm2)$. These come from $\infty$ and the Weierstrass points, the two points over $e=0$, and the two over $e=x_j$. The 60 primes used reach $q=389$ to 641. Control: with the list of known classes emptied, $(\pm2,0)$ and $(0,\pm2)$ are reconstructed over $\mathbb Q$ as the points $e=0$ and $e=x_j$.

**What this covers.** The search is complete only where $H$ has known finite index in $J(\mathbb Q)$. Of the 308 rows, all but S2167 have 2-Selmer bound at least 3 (293 rows) or a descent that timed out (14 rows). There the rank is probably at least 3, $H$ is a rank-2 subgroup of a larger lattice, and a rational point has no reason to lie in it. On those rows the run is a test of the code, not a search of $C_X(\mathbb Q)$.

**Row S2167.** Its bound is 2 and its rank is at least 2 (Theorem 1), so $\operatorname{rank}J(\mathbb Q)=2$ and $J(\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus J[2]$ by (T).

**Proposition 4.** On S2167, $[J(\mathbb Q):H]$ is odd and has no prime factor below 293.

*Proof.* Write $J(\mathbb Q)=\Lambda\oplus J[2]$ with $\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z^2$, and $d,p\in\Lambda$ for the projections of $D_0,P$. Then $[J(\mathbb Q):H]=[\Lambda:H_\Lambda]$, where $H_\Lambda\ni d,\,r=(d+p)/2$.
*2-part.* $d\notin2\Lambda$ by (S). If $r\in\mathbb Zd+2\Lambda$, then $d+p\in4\Lambda$ or $p-d\in4\Lambda$, so $D_0+P$ or $P-D_0$ lies in $4J(\mathbb Q)$. Reduction rules both out. At a good $q$ write $\#J(\mathbb F_q)=2^km$ and let $2^b$ be the order of $m\cdot D$. The 2-Sylow subgroup has rank 4, so $4J(\mathbb F_q)$ has 2-exponent at most $2^{\max(0,k-5)}$, and $b>\max(0,k-5)$ shows $D\notin4J(\mathbb F_q)$. This holds for $P-D_0$ at $q=127$ ($k=5$, $b=1$) and for $D_0+P$ at $q=151$ ($k=8$, $b=4$). The control $4D_0$ passes at no good $q<3000$. So $d$ and $r$ span $\Lambda/2\Lambda$, and $[\Lambda:H_\Lambda]$ is odd.
*Odd part.* For an odd prime $l$ the index of $\langle d,p\rangle$ in $H_\Lambda$ is a power of 2, so $l\mid[\Lambda:H_\Lambda]$ only if some $\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\langle d,p\rangle$ has $l\lambda\in\langle d,p\rangle$, i.e. $aD_0+bP\in lJ(\mathbb Q)$ with $(a,b)\not\equiv(0,0)$ mod $l$. The maps $\rho_1,\rho_2$ of Proposition 2 vanish on $lJ(\mathbb Q)$, so an invertible matrix $(\rho_i(D_0),\rho_i(P))$ excludes this. `sat2.py` finds such a matrix for every odd $l<293$, using good $q<11000$. $\square$

The same run certifies 124 of the 167 odd primes below 1000; the other 43, the smallest being 293, 367 and 439, have too few $q<11000$ with $l\mid\#J(\mathbb F_q)$. Controls: with $P$ replaced by $3P$, $l=3$ stays uncertified and $l=5,7$ are certified ($q<8311$); with $P$ replaced by $-D_0$, none of the 24 odd $l<100$ is certified ($q<4787$).

**Corollary.** If $[J(\mathbb Q):H]$ has no prime factor at least 293, then $J(\mathbb Q)=H$, and every rational point of $C_X$ for S2167 other than the ten known ones (six Weierstrass points, two over $e=0$, two over $e=x_j$) has $2(Q-\infty)=AD_0+BP$ with $\max(|A|,|B|)>1000$.

The prime-factor hypothesis needs an explicit bound on $[J(\mathbb Q):H]$, which needs a bound on the difference between naive and canonical height on $J$ [13]. We have not computed one. An estimate shows what the box reaches. From the naive heights of $2^nD_0$ and $2^nP$ for $n\le6$, $\hat h(D_0)\approx27.2$, $\hat h(P)\approx6.5$ and $\langle D_0,P\rangle\approx-0.73$ (logarithmic height of the Mumford polynomial $u$, divided by $4^n$; the values drift by about 1% at $n=6$). On the boundary of the box, the form takes values above $6\cdot10^6$, so a point outside the box has $\hat h(Q-\infty)$ above about $1.6\cdot10^6$ in this normalisation. This is an estimate, not a bound.

The Frobenius characteristic polynomial of $J$ for S2167 is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ at 41 of the 54 good $q<400$, so $J$ is simple over $\mathbb Q$. Elliptic Chabauty on a quotient of $J$ over $\mathbb Q$ is therefore not available on this row. Moreover $\operatorname{End}(J_{\bar{\mathbb Q}})=\mathbb Z$, by the argument codex_arithmetic used for S0125 [14]. For a good prime $q$, reduction gives an injection $\operatorname{End}(J_{\bar{\mathbb Q}})\otimes\mathbb Q\to\operatorname{End}(J_{\bar{\mathbb F}_q})\otimes\mathbb Q$. At $q=83$ and $q=97$ the Frobenius polynomial $\chi_q$ is irreducible with Galois group $D_4$, and $\operatorname{Res}_x(\chi_q(x),\chi_q(xy))$ has no cyclotomic factor other than $y-1$ (`endrecheck.py`). So no ratio of two Frobenius eigenvalues is a root of unity. Galois permutes the eigenvalues transitively, so every power $\pi^k$ has four distinct conjugate eigenvalues and an irreducible characteristic polynomial, and $\mathbb Q(\pi^k)=\mathbb Q(\pi)$. By Tate's theorem [16], $\operatorname{End}(J_{\mathbb F_{q^k}})\otimes\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q(\pi)$ for every $k$, hence $\operatorname{End}(J_{\bar{\mathbb F}_q})\otimes\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q(\pi_q)$. So $\operatorname{End}(J_{\bar{\mathbb Q}})\otimes\mathbb Q$ embeds in both quartic fields. Their only quadratic subfields are $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt6)$, which differ, so the two fields are not isomorphic and their only common subfield is $\mathbb Q$. Hence $\operatorname{End}(J_{\bar{\mathbb Q}})\otimes\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q$. So $J$ has no real multiplication and does not split over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, its Néron–Severi rank is 1, and quadratic Chabauty in its usual form ($\rho\ge2$) does not apply.

## 7. Reading and weak points

The reading. In population A, the point that makes $Y$ extend is independent of $D_0$ on every tested curve, so the rank is at least 2. Population B shows that rank at least 2 is not automatic for these curves. If the same holds one level up, a sextuple inside a septuple has $\operatorname{rank}J(C_X)\ge2$. For such a sextuple, Chabauty–Coleman (which needs rank below 2) and a pairing proof of rank 1 are both unavailable. The seventeen closures [1–4] remain valid proofs, but by this reading they exclude sextuples that were not going to extend. The 308 sextuples of Theorem 1 are, among the corpus, the ones whose curves look most like those of an extendable sextuple.

Weak points:
- The pattern is empirical, and it is observed one level down, on quintuples, and not on septuples.
- A seventh element whose class lies in $\mathbb ZD_0+J[2]$ is not excluded.
- The $p$-values of §5 are nominal. Grouping by source sextuple leaves them small, but dependence through construction families is not modelled.
- B and A-regular are disjoint construction classes (§5). The twin test removes this difference but not the difference in height.
- The quintuples of B and the twins are only known not to lie in a corpus sextuple.
- For the quintic curves we have Selmer bounds, not ranks.
- The search of §6 is complete only on S2167, and there only if $[J(\mathbb Q):H]$ has no prime factor at least 293.
