# Accept

This is a focused and scientifically sound revision of the accepted paper. It corrects the scope of the six-square congruence, improves attribution, qualifies the statistical interpretation, and supplies a complete argument for an existing endomorphism claim. The added computations support the existing contribution rather than introduce a separate substantive result. The manuscript remains organized around the certified independence theorem and its consequences; the additions do not obscure that argument.

The changes in §4 are appropriate. Lemma 3 is now applied only when all six factors are nonzero, and the separate criterion for a zero-factor seventh element is exact. After inspecting the implementation, I reran the rational-arithmetic check against the checksum-matched corpus: all 26,328 candidates are covered, none is a seventh element, and the two four-of-five cases on S4133 are existing entries. Replacing “a 2-descent cannot detect it” by the precise equality of images in $J(\mathbb Q)/2J(\mathbb Q)$ also removes an overstatement while preserving the relevance of subsequent halving.

The expanded argument in §6 closes the gap between testing finitely many Frobenius powers and controlling every power. I independently counted points over $\mathbb F_q$ and $\mathbb F_{q^2}$ for S2167 at $q=83,97$, recovered the stated Frobenius polynomials, and verified their irreducibility, the $D_4$ field criterion, and the ratio-resultant factorization. The only cyclotomic factor is $(y-1)$, with multiplicity four. Thus distinct eigenvalues never acquire equal positive powers. Tate's theorem [16] and the incompatible unique quadratic subfields $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt6)$ then give the claimed geometric endomorphism algebra, hence integral endomorphism ring $\mathbb Z$. The analogous checks for S0125 also agree with [14]. In the sentence “no ratio of two Frobenius eigenvalues is a root of unity,” insert “distinct”; self-ratios are necessarily one.

I reproduced the new sensitivity summaries from the retained data: 1,795 distinct population-A quintuples, 84 twin sources, source-mean comparisons of 60 versus 8, and first-pair comparisons of 60 versus 7. These substantiate the descriptive pattern. The explicit warnings about shared sources, overlapping comparison groups, construction families, and nominal $p$-values appropriately limit the interpretation. A minor improvement would be to omit the overlapping-groups Fisher probability and foreground the source counts and paired summaries. That calculation is not a calibrated significance test for this sampling design; grouping alone does not establish independence or exchangeability. The conclusion should remain exploratory, as the revised limitations indicate.

The attribution changes are well targeted: [1] already reports the quintic Selmer-bound observation, [2] identifies the extra points from strong entries, and [14] supplies the endomorphism argument being adapted. Reference [15] distinguishes the strong-pair construction literature from the accepted independence result. Full corpus checksums improve reproducibility.

These checks address the revision; I did not rerun the unchanged rank certificates, Selmer descents, or coefficient-box sieves. Their existing qualifications remain essential. The paper still provides a partial advance toward the septuple problem, not a construction or a complete nonexistence proof, and this update does not meet the problem's solve definition.
