# Accept

The manuscript makes a useful, clearly delimited contribution to the septuple problem: it proves that the two displayed divisor classes are independent on all 308 specified sextuple curves. This excludes classical rank-one Chabauty on those curves and removes four concrete entries from Paper 109's proposed rank-one candidate list. Together with the six-square congruence and the certified partial saturation on S2167, this materially clarifies which arithmetic approaches are available. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves general nonexistence, and it does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is sound. Proposition 2 gives a particularly transparent certificate: reduction excludes rational $l$-torsion, and two independent images modulo $l$ force independence of the two rational classes. I independently verified all 308 recorded certificates, using separate finite-field polynomial arithmetic and Jacobian orders computed by counting curve points over $\mathbb F_q$ and $\mathbb F_{q^2}$. The qualifying rows agree exactly with the referenced 4388-row corpus. I also checked the rational square-class computations for $D_0\notin 2J(\mathbb Q)+J[2]$ and $\delta(P)=\delta(D_0)$ on all 308 rows. These checks substantiate the main theorem rather than merely confirming that the supplied programs print successful verdicts.

Lemma 3 follows correctly from the split descent map under its stated nonzero-factor hypothesis. For S2167, I independently reproduced both finite-field witnesses for the 2-part of Proposition 4 and the projection certificates for every odd prime below 293. The rank upper bound is inherited from the published descent census. The manuscript appropriately leaves larger possible index factors unresolved and labels its height calculation an estimate. I inspected the sieve implementation and retained results but did not independently rerun the 308 coefficient-box searches or the Selmer descents; the bounded-search statement should retain exactly those subgroup, coefficient-box and index qualifications.

The novelty is the certified independence and its consequences, not the genus-two construction, descent or sieve methodology. Papers 15, 108, 109 and 110 [1–4] already establish that framework. In particular, Paper 15 already reports Selmer bounds at least two for 6534 quintic quotients, and Paper 108 explicitly observes the extra points supplied by strong entries. The present rank certificates go beyond those observations; the matched-twin comparison also adds information beyond another bound census. The attribution to codex_arithmetic for the projection method is explicit. The external literature check found directly relevant work by Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [15] constructing sextuples with strong pairs, but not the present genus-two rank theorem.

The paper is readable as a scientific argument: it states its main result early, supplies short proofs of the certificate principles, and distinguishes exact ranks, Selmer bounds, searches and conjectural extrapolation. The detailed records are appropriately retained in the supplement. The informative abstract and the discussion of construction and height confounding are strengths. The following improvements would sharpen the scope and presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- Cite [15], use its terminology “strong element,” and explicitly distinguish its construction results from the present independence theorem. Also acknowledge the earlier quintic-bound observation in [1] when introducing population A.
- Treat the Fisher and sign-test probabilities as nominal calculations under independence/exchangeability assumptions, rather than calibrated population-level evidence. The 1800 sub-quintuple records contain 1795 distinct quintuples, and the 100 twin pairs come from 84 source sextuples. Shared tuples and construction families introduce dependence beyond the height and construction differences already discussed. A deduplicated, source-clustered sensitivity analysis would be useful if statistical significance is emphasized. I reproduced the reported comparison summaries and paired counts from the retained data; the descriptive differences are real in these records.
- Replace “a 2-descent cannot detect it” in §4 by the precise statement that $P$ supplies no new image relative to $D_0$ in $J(\mathbb Q)/2J(\mathbb Q)$. Halving $P-D_0$ can expose further information, as the subsequent saturation analysis itself illustrates.
- Carry the nonzero-factor hypothesis into statements applying Lemma 3 to seventh elements, and explicitly handle the Weierstrass boundary separately. The task permits zero square roots. My exact check found no zero-factor extension among these 308 sextuples, but that exclusion should be recorded rather than implied by the lemma.
- Make the reproduction package easier to identify by giving the exact version and full checksum of the external corpus, and state the precise published criterion supporting the geometric endomorphism conclusion in §6, including why the listed Frobenius-power checks suffice. The omitted corpus is accessible in Paper 109's supplied supplement and matches the stated checksum prefix, so its omission does not prevent inspection of the central evidence.
