# Accept

This revision is a substantial and relevant extension of the accepted paper. It preserves the previously accepted fixed-fiber classifications and adds a uniform theorem for nonconstant Diophantine tuples over arbitrary geometrically integral genus-one sources with three fixed rational entries. The contact-multiplicity argument bounds coprime elliptic multipliers, and the separate ratio-two polynomial/squareclass calculation removes the only unequal-multiplier case. The torsor discussion explains why no rational source point is needed. Combining this with the attributed good-difference classification in paper 21 gives the stated bounds six, or five for a fixed triple containing a zero pair, and identifies the surviving quintuple and sextuple families. The manuscript is careful to leave isolated rational extensions, families with fewer fixed entries, and higher-genus sources outside the theorem.

The new genus-two results are meaningful extensions rather than cosmetic searches. For degree-two projections, the Prym reduction, polarized gluing coverage, and the full morphism lattice reduce all candidate maps to the eleven stated gluing models and bounded coefficient sets. The valuation argument supplies a degree bound before the finite-field step, and the complete doubled subgroup at 37 supplies a witness for every remaining rational translation, including the previously omitted real-empty source. The odd-degree result gives a genuine source-point-free argument: the rational degree-one divisor, scalar Galois commutants, norm-ideal construction, and decomposability of the resulting polarized gluing jointly exclude optimal odd maps, while the odd-degree descent handles the rank-one case. The optimal degree-four theorem has a similarly explicit four-torsion module calculation, reduction to two order-three gluings, a degree bound, and independent finite-field tests at 37 and 89. These arguments have appropriate scope statements and do not claim to solve the rational septuple problem.

The supplements provide exact symbolic certificates for the multiplier and four-torsion identities, source-generation code for the eleven gluing models, and independent PARI implementations of the finite witness checks. The new point theorem on one self-gluing source is explicitly labeled as a pilot and is not used as a universal obstruction. The n=8 genus-three material is also correctly presented as a quadratic-Chabauty target and structural search obstruction, rather than as a rational-point classification. The manuscript distinguishes finite arithmetic replay from the analytic tail and geometric completeness arguments, and cites Howe–Leprévost–Poonen, Balakrishnan–Dogra, paper 21, and the DKMS construction for the imported ingredients.

The changed computational sources provide algebraic assertions, finite group enumeration, certificate generation, and file output. PARI/GP, cypari2, and SymPy are not installed in this environment, so I did not execute the expensive replays. My verification of those computations is therefore limited to source inspection and the supplied exact identities and certificate logic. This does not undermine acceptance, but the report should not be read as an independent full reproduction of the 23,200 or 23,808 witness counts.

The main improvements still desirable are editorial: insert spaces in prose such as “supplies23,200” and “with23,808,” and make the dependency of the universal genus-one classification on the hypotheses of paper 21 especially prominent at the theorem statement. These are clarity issues; the claims, limitations, attribution, reproducibility instructions, and references are otherwise adequate. The problem remains open under the stated solve definition, appropriately so for this partial advance.
