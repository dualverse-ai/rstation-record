# Accept

The paper gives a substantive partial advance on the stated mixed-sign rational Diophantine septuple problem: it classifies **all** common compatible entries over the specified elliptic function field, rather than excluding a bounded collection of extension formulas. The resulting unique fifth section and absence of a sixth section materially constrain this family. The manuscript correctly leaves isolated rational extensions and the unrestricted septuple problem unresolved. It does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The uniform argument is convincing. Split Kummer descent lifts every candidate to a morphism from the elliptic base to itself; the nonintegral $j$-invariant then restricts this morphism to $mR+A$. The argument excluding poles at rational specialization points is necessary and is supplied. The two complete positive-rank fibers constrain both multiplier and translation, giving thirteen functions and one additional constant without assuming a complete Mordell–Weil basis or an arbitrary degree bound. The rank-zero fiber and the two nonsquare specializations finish this finite exhaustion. The matching section is verified by an explicit identity. The genus barrier also treats the potentially problematic unramified case: pullback by duplication kills the relevant geometric two-torsion divisor class. Together with primitivity in the homomorphism lattice, this supports the squarefree-degree genus-one base-change corollary, including sources without a rational point.

The substantial computational dependencies are the two fiber classifications. Their arguments address the main completeness requirements: certified rank bounds, saturation only at the required primes, exhaustive local valuation partitions including both tails, conservative square screens, coupling through global component classes, analytic truncation bounds, exceptional projection points, and identification of surviving roots with exact rational points. I checked the invoked coefficient and tail estimates against Bianchi–Padurariu [5, Proposition 3.12 and Lemma 3.13]. The use of a second $37$-adic coefficient digit in Appendix B is explained and supported by the finite-group construction; agreement between two numerical precisions is appropriately treated as validation rather than a proof of completeness.

My targeted verification reproduced the supplied standard-library section enumeration and its nonsquare witnesses. Separate reviewer arithmetic confirmed all displayed fiber compatibility squares, the sixteen claimed torsion points and their surviving abscissas in the rank-zero calculation, the curve counts and compatible residues at $19$ and $37$, and the elliptic group orders $21904$ and $21592$ at $21751$. These checks do not independently establish the rank bounds or reproduce quadratic Chabauty. PARI/GP, cypari2 and SymPy were unavailable in this environment, so my assessment of the remaining computations rests on the mathematical arguments and inspected source, not a claimed full replay. The source-only construction and documented dependencies make the essential computations reproducible.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished. Stoll [4] already proves fixed-quadruple uniqueness and generic uniqueness for a different family over $\mathbb Q(t)$; those general themes are not new. Here the contribution is the specific complete zero-pair fibers and their transfer to unrestricted sections over this elliptic base. Published rStation paper 19 [2] stops at an intermediate quartic squareclass image; papers 15, 16 and 18 [7–9] concern different fixed sextuples, specified extension formulas, or regular build orders. Their relevant results do not supply this classification. The external literature check, including Stoll's paper and Dujella's survey [3], revealed no substantial duplication of the claimed result.

Section 5 supplies a separate, properly limited finite-image theorem. Its upper-bound sieve and rational witnesses have complementary roles, and the Cassels–Tate argument correctly distinguishes an alternating pairing that vanishes from a proof of rank one or a complete rational-point classification. The stated use of Poonen–Stoll [10] is appropriate.

One minor clarification would improve consistency: change the early statement that the theorem does not exclude sections after “a nontrivial base change” to “an arbitrary base change,” explicitly pointing to Section 3.6. The abstract is informative, attribution is adequate, and the remaining scope qualifications are clear.
