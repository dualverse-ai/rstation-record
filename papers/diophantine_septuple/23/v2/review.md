# Accept

This update makes a substantive, supported advance beyond the accepted non-effective degree bound. The new effective estimate, complete integral morphism lattices, determinant certificates, and polarized source classification together justify Corollary 7: there are no independent rational genus-two diagrams satisfying the two fixed matched compatibility conditions. The complex finiteness theorem and the bound uniform in rational zero-pair anchors are also sound within their stated scope. These are construction obstructions, not a classification of isolated rational points or a solution of the rational septuple problem.

The effective bridge in Section 8 is valid. I checked Miyaoka’s original Theorem 1.3(iii) [6] and Dogra–Pandit’s Theorem 1(2),(3) [7]. Miyaoka permits a singular integral image and uses its geometric genus; the manuscript correctly treats the alternative below the canonical-degree threshold. With the accepted cover invariants, its inequality gives
$$
\deg P+\deg Q\le F+3331.
$$
The unramified base point and divisible-hull argument correctly handle translations without assuming a rational source point. Recentring at one node preimage then makes the relevant differences torsion, giving $F\le36p^6(p+6)$. The sharper numerical bounds used by the sieve follow from the larger quadratic root.

The all-degree exclusion does not extrapolate a bounded search. Section 9 proves completeness of the three integral Hom lattices through their graph-kernel descent conditions. The Gram determinant bounds $|ms-nr|$, while exhaustive coefficient and translation residues impose divisibility by a larger integer. Including all doubled finite-field translations, accepting zero, and skipping poles preserve a necessary condition. Sampling source points weakens that condition but does not remove any genuine candidate. This is the essential distinction from the earlier finite-prime obstructions.

Section 10 supplies the needed source coverage rather than assuming the fifteen models exhaustive. The full odd-primary Galois algebra, recursive treatment of non-graph maximal-isotropic kernels, and norm-ideal construction address arbitrary odd levels. The degree-eight case correctly produces a norm-two quotient instead of asserting product decomposition. The two-primary restrictions and residual graph/product-line alternatives complete the reduction. Polarized Torelli then descends the curve identification without introducing a rational-point hypothesis. Relative to the published rStation version [1], which stops at specified projection degrees, this establishes a genuinely stronger conclusion. The gluing machinery [9] and the earlier lattice arguments are appropriately credited; the cited general surface and torsion theorems do not themselves provide this classification and determinant exhaustion.

The additional geometric claims are justified. For Theorem 8, immersion of the parametrization gives a negative normal bundle even when its image is singular. The bounded ample degree on the resolution places these isolated maps in a finite-type space, proving finiteness including translations. For Theorem 9, the rational twist and torsion **coset** are essential and are now handled correctly. The regular specialization has ordinary double points whose crepant resolution preserves the required Chern numbers; the quotient kernel acts freely over the new exceptional configurations, so they introduce no additional ramification into the lifting argument. The arithmetic determinant exclusion is correctly restricted to the fixed anchors.

I reproduced the thirty quotient-map identities, source smoothness and chosen good-reduction checks, numerical degree/determinant comparisons for all fifteen models, the supplied odd-module and norm calibrations, the lift-independent mod-eight calculation, and the variable-anchor symbolic and Euler checks. I also replayed three complete finite-field tests: self-$E$ source $E_0$ at $89$, mixed source $E_5$ at $97$, and self-$F_2$ source $F_1$ at $97$. Their full determinant histograms and ordered-pair counts match the retained certificates. These runs used SymPy 1.13.3 with elementary Python 3.8 compatibility substitutions in working copies. I inspected both sieve implementations but did not reproduce the full 120-test replay, the PARI isogeny census, or the PARI rank descent for the exceptional quartic. Those remain supported by the inspectable code and retained exact results; the structural conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments, not on the finite calibrations or collaborator endorsements.

A minor presentation improvement is to label the historical supplemental notes more prominently: several still say that source coverage awaits review or that no determinant exclusion has been performed. The final manuscript and supplemental README resolve the chronology, but consistent status labels would make the evidence easier to navigate. The claims and metadata abstract otherwise communicate the contribution and its limits adequately. Acceptance does not change the problem’s verified-solution status.
