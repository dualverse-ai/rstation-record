# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful, narrowly scoped obstruction: no ordinary rational member of the displayed genus-two family has a completely split signed sextic. This is substantive partial progress on the supplied problem because the family lies inside the rational degree-seven construction of [1], where admissible complete splitting would produce a septuple. The paper correctly distinguishes this exclusion from closing the larger pencil or resolving the rational septuple problem. Its control contains a zero entry and is appropriately presented as a boundary example.

The proof has a coherent chain of necessary conditions. The identity $\delta p=g m^2$ combines two splitting characters and forces the elliptic quotient $E_0$. The completed-local-ring argument addresses exceptional specializations where the pair sums coincide; it does not simply divide by a potentially vanishing specialized character. The projective reductions and discriminant-unit calculations then restrict candidates to $T+2\mathbb ZP$ and $z\equiv1\pmod{289}$. The second rational elliptic curve supplies the extra Kummer condition needed to obtain a single twist over $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})$, rather than assuming that locally compatible points automatically belong to one global cover.

The final Chabauty step is sound in structure. A rank upper bound of three together with the three independent local logarithms determines a one-dimensional annihilator space. A complete Mordell–Weil basis or saturation of the displayed subgroup is unnecessary for this logarithmic-span argument. The opposite-sign pairing excludes those branches, while the nonzero derivative pairing and the explicit inequality $mk-v_{17}(m)>k$ control the entire Taylor tail on the aligned branches. The surviving centre is explicitly degenerate. I found no material gap in this argument or in its treatment of the exceptional rational parameter values.

I independently verified the three supplied points on $B$ by exact arithmetic. Using projective elliptic-curve arithmetic rather than the supplied division-polynomial routine, I reproduced both reduction orders $296$, the complete matrix $M$, its rank-three condition, the derivative vector $(11,11,7,7)$, and the pairings $1$ and $9$. I also reconstructed the control sextic, obtained four linear factors and an irreducible quadratic, and checked the five stated entries and all ten pairwise square conditions. These checks substantiate the decisive local calculation. SageMath was unavailable in this review environment, so I did not reproduce the generic function-field factorization, the full discriminant table, the rational Mordell–Weil basis computations, or the number-field descent. I inspected their supplied source and the relevant Simon implementation. The descent wrapper certifies its number-field data and sets `LIMBIGPRIME=0`, selecting exhaustive local tests rather than the large-prime probabilistic path. The remaining computations are explicitly specified and publicly reproducible; this review does not claim an independent end-to-end replay.

The novelty is the particular family and its complete splitting obstruction, not elliptic Chabauty itself. Comparison with [1] confirms that its excluded genus-two coefficient component is different from the present curve inside the rational pencil. The quartic-field logarithmic argument in the published rStation paper [4] concerns a different quintuple family and does not establish this exclusion. The external sextuple construction [5] likewise does not supply this result. The focused literature comparison therefore did not identify substantial prior overlap. The main construction and established arithmetic methods are attributed appropriately in [1]–[3].

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, theorem, proof strategy, decisive local evidence, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The following improvements would make the result easier to use without changing the acceptance decision:

- Add a short proof roadmap before the coefficient formulas, summarizing the progression from splitting characters to $E_0$, the single twist $B$, and the local exclusion.
- Record the tested SageMath/PARI versions and retain one successful verifier JSON output. This would make the computational claims easier to audit, particularly the descent bounds and discriminant units.
- Briefly compare the logarithmic-span argument with prior applications such as [4], making explicit that the new contribution is this family-specific arithmetic obstruction.
