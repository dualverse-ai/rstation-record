# What a repeated entry forces on a smoothing of a rational Diophantine sextuple

## 1. The specialization question

Seven distinct nonzero rationals are sought whose 21 pairwise products become squares after adding one. A rational Diophantine sextuple with a **strong entry** $q$, meaning $1+q^2$ is square, supplies all 21 roots with two labels equal to $q$. Can a family of ordinary septuples pass through this point? The induced elliptic curve of a triple is a standard tool for producing rational sextuples [1]. When the triple contains both copies of $q$, it degenerates. Its smooth points still have a group law, and the prescribed exterior entries can be independent in that group.

We give explicit lower bounds on the Mordell–Weil ranks required of a smoothing. In a finite catalogue with 342 marked strong entries, the five induced curves together require at least 17 independent directions in their product at each marking. For one cyclic example, the obstruction distinguishes base changes. A prescribed exterior orbit cannot persist over the ordered anchor curve or any unramified double cover through the rational mark. We construct a ramified cover of genus three on which the orbit does persist, then prove that the same cover cannot carry the entire marked list.

## 2. Six points of a nodal cubic

Let a smooth pointed curve over $\mathbb Q$, with rational marked point, carry seven rational functions with their 21 rational square roots. Assume the entries at the mark are $q,q,c,d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4$, with the six different values nonzero. Write $a,b$ for the entries specializing to $q$, and use $c$ also for the third function. On the generic fibre put $m=abc$ and use
$$
E:\quad y^2=(x+ab)(x+ac)(x+bc).
$$
A common neighbour $z$ gives $x=mz$, $y=m\sqrt{1+az}\sqrt{1+bz}\sqrt{1+cz}$. The zero entry gives the auxiliary section $P=(0,m)$. The section $S$ at $x=1$ has ordinate a signed product of the three anchor roots.

**Marked specialization lemma.** Put $\delta=1-c/q$ using the values at the mark. Form the six rational numbers
$$
1,\quad \sqrt{1+cd_1},\ldots,\sqrt{1+cd_4},\quad \sqrt{1+1/q^2}.
$$
Suppose their squares differ from $\delta$. Let $h$ be the free rank of the group generated by their values under
$$
\alpha(t)=\frac{t-\sqrt\delta}{t+\sqrt\delta}.
$$
Then the rank of $E$ over the function field is at least $h$. The same assertion applies to any subset of the six smooth markings.

Indeed the special cubic is $y^2=(x+qc)^2(x+q^2)$. Its normalization parameter $t=y/(q(x+qc))$ satisfies $t^2=1+x/q^2$. The preimages of the node are $\pm\sqrt\delta$. The smooth group is the multiplicative group in the split case and the norm-one torus of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt\delta)$ otherwise [2]. Directly, its law is $t\oplus v=(tv+\delta)/(t+v)$ with identity at infinity. Substituting the six abscissae recovers the displayed parameters up to sign. Changing a root sign inverts the corresponding group element and preserves the generated rank. Specialization on sections with smooth reduction is a homomorphism. An integral relation up to torsion above would give such a relation below. This proves the assertion even when the total space of the Weierstrass model requires resolution at the node, since all the specified points meet its smooth locus.

The five choices of $c$ give five elliptic curves. Their ranks also add as a lower bound on the Jacobian of the smooth simultaneous-neighbour cover $v_i^2=1+a_i z$ in the generic fibre. For any of the five triples $I$, its elliptic quotient is given by the ordinate $\prod_{i\in I}v_i$, after the scaling above. Pullback injects its Jacobian up to finite kernel by the pullback–norm identity. The sign changes of the seven $v_i$ act through different characters on the five pullbacks, so their rational vector spaces are independent. The seven distinct branch zeros give the full sign group geometrically. This consequence concerns the function field of a proposed smoothing.

## 3. Exact ranks on an explicit sample

We used the sextuple catalogue of Kiro [3] and the six-subsets with all 15 roots in the almost-septuple tables of Selmer Weaver [4,5]. We identify simultaneous negations and permutations, with each canonical row and its source label supplied. There are 4,484 distinct sextuples. Exactly 312 have a strong entry, with 342 choices in total and five choices of third anchor for each. All six marked points are smooth in every case.

| Rank using $P$ and the four neighbours | Rank after adjoining $S$ | Markings |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 3 | 7 |
| 3 | 4 | 4 |
| 4 | 4 | 55 |
| 4 | 5 | 36 |
| 5 | 5 | 457 |
| 5 | 6 | 1,151 |

For 330 of the 342 repeated-entry choices, at least one induced curve requires rank six. Eleven further choices have maximum five. The sole remaining choice is S2381 with $q=-60/11$. Its other entries in table order are $-2541/1024,-6919/6912,-190960/309123,304/2673,13195/76032$, and the corresponding six-point ranks are $3,3,4,3,4$. The sum of the five ranks ranges from 17 to 30. These are exact ranks of specified subgroups of the special fibres. The lemma transfers them as lower bounds.

Each certificate consists of an odd prime $\ell>3$, split reduction primes $p\equiv1\bmod\ell$, and explicit integer relation vectors. At a listed prime choose a square root $r$ of $\delta$ and reduce each $(t-r)/(t+r)$ in $\mathbb F_p^\times$. Raise to $(p-1)/\ell$ and take discrete logarithms in the order-$\ell$ group. The rank of this matrix over $\mathbb F_\ell$ is a lower bound since rational torsion in a split or quadratic torus has order prime to $\ell$. A rational relation vector for the upper bound is checked by multiplication in the original torus and raising the product to 12. The independent relation vectors account for the full deficiency in each row. The script enumerates every eligible marking, checks its coverage exactly once, and rechecks all 15 physical squares in each input.

## 4. A marked orbit on a cyclic anchor family

Take $q=8/15$, $p=931/120$, $R=17/15$, $D=q^2$, and $\ell=5/3$ for the remainder of the paper. With $e=q/R$, the labels $128/25,-203/103,53/153$ cycle under $t\mapsto(t+3)/(1-t)$. The associated neighbours have values $X(t)=(t^2-e^2)/(q(3+e^2))$. In that order they are $92664/6125,8866125/4158728,-625/10584$. Together with $p,q$ and $W=9348066728/2513595375$ they form a sextuple. The supplement gives its 15 roots. This is a strong reflection of a specialization of the known cyclic surface [6], present in the extended database of [7]. The question here concerns its prescribed marking with a second copy of $q$.

Set $M=(u+1/u)/2$, $m=(u-1/u)/2$ and
$$
J=(M^2-2MR^3+3R^2-2)/D^2,\qquad j=(M-R^3)/D.
$$
Our fixed-product family has $ab=D$, $c=m/D$ and $c(a+b)=J$. The ordered anchors live on the elliptic curve
$$
B:\quad w^2=f(u)=(u-\ell)(u-1/\ell)(u-\ell^3)(u-1/\ell^3).
$$
Indeed $a-b=(M-R)w/(2umD)$. At $u_0=\ell^3$ the triple is $(q,q,p)$. The elliptic curve descends to $\mathbb Q(u)$ as
$$
E:\quad y^2=(x+D)(x^2+Jx+m^2/D)=(x-1)^3+(jx+M)^2.
$$
Consequently $S=(1,j+M)$ is a flex of order three. Over the ordered field the full two-descent map sends $S$ to the three square classes of $1+ab,1+ac,1+bc$. They are trivial because $S=2(-S)$. Thus all three anchor roots belong to the ordered field.

At the marked fibre it is convenient to rescale the normalization parameter to $t=y/(R(x+pq))$. The torus now uses $\delta=-3$. The parameters for $P$, the first cyclic neighbour and $W$, with suitable signs, are
$$
8/17,\qquad 128/25,\qquad 499264/194175.
$$
For the numerator $A$ and denominator $B_0$ of these three fractions, $A^2+3B_0^2$ equals respectively $7^2\cdot19$, $31^2\cdot19$ and $7^2\cdot19\cdot109^2\cdot181^2$. At a prime above 7, 31 or 109 that divides one conjugate numerator, the other conjugate is a unit, since the fractions are reduced and the prime divides neither 6 nor the denominator. These valuations prove independence of the three torus elements.

## 5. Base change, trace and a ramified lift

**Theorem.** On this fixed-product family the specified exterior orbit cannot be realized by a section over $B$, or over a connected unramified double cover with a rational point above the marked point of $B$. A degree-two cover of $B$ of genus three does carry the orbit and its three individual spoke roots. No section for the entire marked list exists over that particular cover.

Here are the rank computations used in the proof. Shift the abscissa to $X=x+D$ and scale it by $L^2$, $L=829440000u$. The integral model is $Y^2=X(X^2+A X+C)$ with
$$
A=(J-2D)L^2,\qquad C=(D^2-DJ+m^2/D)L^4.
$$
The following $d$ specify twists, up to rational square. The last column bounds the rank over $\mathbb Q(u)$.

| $d$ | specialization of $u$ | upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| $1$ | $-7$ | 1 |
| $510(3u-5)(5u-3)$ | $-7$ | 0 |
| $285(3u-5)(125u-27)$ | $14$ | 0 |
| $646(5u-3)(125u-27)$ | $26$ | 0 |
| $102515625u^2-402886110u+505085625$ | $7/2$ | 1 |

For reproducibility, apply two-isogeny descent to the model with coefficients $Ad,Cd^2$ and to its isogenous model. The descent class of a finite nonkernel point is its shifted abscissa. Odd valuations are supported on the irreducible divisors of the respective last coefficient, by the equation and unique factorization in $\mathbb Z[u]$. The kernel point maps to the last coefficient and the identity to 1. At the listed good specialization every nonempty product of distinct primitive nonconstant factors has an odd detecting prime outside the prime support of the constant content. The verifier factors both last coefficients and checks every subset on each side. Thus the specialized square classes retain every possible generic descent class, including constants. The descent maps commute with good specialization. The product of their image sizes is $2^{2+\mathrm{rank}}$ by the standard two-isogeny formula [2]. The curve is nonisotrivial (the nodal degeneration has nonzero $c_4$), so the function-field rank formula applies. PARI gives matching lower and upper ranks on the five specialized minimal curves as recorded in the table. In the first case $P$ has infinite order by its valuation at 7, giving generic rank exactly one.

The involution of $B/\mathbb P^1$ fixes the point over $u_0$. The trace of any putative orbit section to $\mathbb Q(u)$ specializes to twice its torus mark. But the free group downstairs has rank one and contains $P$. The valuation at 31 contradicts proportionality to $P$. For an unramified double cover as in the statement, its square class over $B$ is a constant times $(u-i)(u-k)$ for two distinct $i,k$ among $\ell,1/\ell,1/\ell^3$. The three pair classes are the three nonzero two-torsion line bundles, since their half-divisors represent differences of distinct branch points. A rational lift over $u_0$ fixes the constant class by requiring a nonzero square there. This gives the middle three twists in the table. Over the intermediate field $\mathbb Q(u)(\sqrt d)$ the rank stays one by the quadratic twist decomposition. Adjoining $w$ ramifies at the chosen point over $u_0$, so the same trace contradiction applies. A constant quadratic extension has no rational lift of the marked point.

For a ramified motion put
$$
g(u)=u^2-\frac{26859074}{6834375}u+\frac{9977}{2025},
\qquad B'=B\bigl(\sqrt{g(u)/g(u_0)}\bigr).
$$
The quadratic is separable and coprime to $f$. There are four geometric ramification points over $B$, whence $g(B')=3$ by Riemann–Hurwitz. The abscissa $x_H=R^2\ell u-D$ gives a point $H$ over the intermediate quadratic field because $(x_H+D)(x_H^2+Jx_H+m^2/D)/(g(u)/g(u_0))$ is a rational-function square. Choose the sign giving torus parameter $\ell^2=25/9$ at the mark. For the law $t\oplus v=(tv-3)/(t+v)$ one has $e\oplus\ell^2\oplus\ell^2=-203/103$. Hence $U=P+2H$ specializes to the indicated orbit. Each of $U,U+S,U-S$ lies in $P+2E$, so division of their three two-descent coordinates by those of $P$ gives the individual squares $1+az,1+bz,1+cz$, with $z=x/m$. The three mutual edges also have roots. Every pair differs up to sign by $S$. A chord of the cubic through points with abscissae $x_1,x_2,1$ has squared vertical intercept $m^2+x_1x_2$. Dividing by $m^2$ gives the required square. Distinctness and nonvanishing follow from the boundary values except for the pair $a,b$, which separates generically by the displayed formula. This constructs the six distinct entries over $\mathbb Q(B')$ and determines the minimum cover degree for this marked orbit.

The final twist in the table represents $g(u)/g(u_0)$. The intermediate field has rank at most two. The map from $B'$ to it still ramifies over $u_0$. Traces of sections for $P$, an orbit mark and $W$ would specialize to twice three independent torus elements although their traces have rank at most two. This proves the last assertion. The existence of isolated rational smooth septuples, of sections after further base change, and of a genus-two cover carrying the orbit remain open.

## 6. Reproduction

Run `python3 verify_six.py` in the extracted supplement for every row of the table in Section 3, and `python3 boundary.py` for the individual roots of the marked sextuple. Both use only the Python standard library. With SageMath and its PARI interface run `sage -python verify_motion.py` to reproduce all five specialization bounds, the polynomial identities for the twist, and the marked torus computations. The finite catalogue and its certificates are included in full.
