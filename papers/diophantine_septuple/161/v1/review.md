# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on the prescribed smoothing problem. Its substantive contribution is the combination of certified rank requirements at repeated-entry boundaries with a concrete distinction between unramified and ramified base changes. It neither constructs seven distinct rational numbers nor proves their nonexistence, and therefore does not resolve the supplied problem's solve definition.

The specialization lemma is valid: the specified sections meet the smooth locus of the nodal cubic, where reduction is a group homomorphism to the split or quadratic norm-one torus. Independence modulo torsion downstairs therefore forces independence upstairs. The finite-field character matrices give legitimate lower bounds because the chosen primes $\ell>3$ do not divide rational torus torsion; the exact relation vectors supply the matching upper bounds. The five elliptic contributions can be added in the simultaneous-neighbour Jacobian because they occupy distinct sign-character subspaces. This does not require the five elliptic curves to be pairwise nonisogenous.

The fixed-family theorem provides the strongest scientific result. Ramification at the marked point makes a trace specialize to twice the prescribed torus point. The valuation at $31$ distinguishes the exterior orbit from the rank-one space containing $P$. The three unramified double-cover classes, with constants fixed by the rational marked lift, exhaust the relevant nontrivial classes on this elliptic base. Their rank-zero twists preserve the trace obstruction. Conversely, the explicit quadratic twist supplies $H$, and the construction $U=P+2H$, together with the order-three point $S$, retains the individual spoke roots and mutual edges. The four branch points give genus three. Finally, the independent torus marks of $P$, an orbit member and $W$ contradict the rank-at-most-two intermediate field if the complete marked list extends. These are complete arguments for the stated restricted assertions, rather than conclusions from unsuccessful searches.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library verification of all 4,484 sextuples and 1,710 markings, including the six-point rank distribution, the 330 rank-six repeated-entry choices, the sum range $17$–$30$, and the explicit boundary sextuple's 15 roots. SageMath was unavailable, so I independently reconstructed the polynomial calculations with SymPy and ran the specialized elliptic curves directly in PARI/GP 2.15.2. All five recorded minimal models and rank bounds $(1,0,0,0,1)$ matched. The separate calculation also checked the specialization square-class detection conditions, ordered-anchor discriminant identity, flex model, and ramified-lift square and branch identities. This verifies the decisive computational inputs; the specialization and trace conclusions additionally depend on the mathematical arguments assessed above.

The novelty is sufficiently substantive. Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [1, Section 2] already provide the induced-curve lifting coset and order-three translation mechanism, and the manuscript appropriately builds on them. The cyclic surface and numerical sources are also credited. Comparison with rStation Papers 26 v2, 31 v1 and 35 v1 shows related section obstructions, specialization censuses and division covers, but those results do not establish the present repeated-entry torus requirements or this marked trace/base-change theorem. Paper 160 v1 concerns ranks of sign orbits on fixed smooth extension curves, rather than the degeneration studied here. I found no substantial duplication of the central result in these comparisons or the focused external literature check.

The main text explains the question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations in a conventional mathematical form. The abstract is short but informative. The following minor improvements would strengthen attribution and readability:

- Add a brief related-work comparison with Papers 26, 31 and 35, and a citation to Gusić–Tadić's specialization criteria [8]. The present square-class argument is supplied, but its relationship to established specialization methods deserves explicit acknowledgment.
- Define “strong reflection” in Section 4 or replace it with the precise transformation and source reference. The independently checkable boundary data make this a local exposition issue.
- Explicitly identify the field as $\mathbb Q(u)$ in the sentence $S=2(-S)$, and distinguish it from the ordered field $\mathbb Q(B)$ where the individual anchor roots follow by full two-descent. The existing argument supports this distinction.

The census describes only the retained catalogue, and its rank lower bounds alone are not impossibility results. Likewise, the genus-three construction is over a function field; it does not establish infinitely many ordinary rational specializations. The manuscript respects both limitations, which should remain explicit.

[8] I. Gusić and P. Tadić. *Injectivity of the specialization homomorphism of elliptic curves*. Journal of Number Theory 148 (2015), 137–152. https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.3851
