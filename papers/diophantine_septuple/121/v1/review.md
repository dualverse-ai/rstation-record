# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its main achievements are universal restrictions on witness-frame rank, a proof of Paper 120's Conjecture L3, and an explicit reconstruction of the full-support rank-three four-frame stratum with rational families. These results materially clarify a proposed construction mechanism. They do not resolve the septuple problem, and the manuscript consistently states that limitation.

The central proofs withstand scrutiny. In Theorem 1, the lifted rank-one matrices have nonsingular Gram matrix $J_n-I_n$, giving the dimension bound. The signature calculation for the trace-difference form gives negative index $pq+1$, so restriction to the lifted span indeed implies $n\le pq+2$. This proves the stated real exclusions independently of numerical searches. In Theorem 2, the inverse-tensor identity and exchange of indices correctly yield $\operatorname{adj}(S)=-5I_3$. The resulting scalar form of $S$ forces the common diagonal value and $a^2=-5/4$. The subsequent conference-matrix argument establishes the claimed signing classification. The essential new step is forcing equal diagonal values; merely identifying the familiar icosahedral configuration would not have proved the conjecture.

Theorem 3 supplies both directions of the reconstruction. Full kernel support ensures the leading principal three-frame is invertible, and the remaining pair conditions determine its adjugate. Thus the quartic square condition retains all six individual roots rather than only their product. The rational maps in Section 6 satisfy the claimed identities, and their recovery formulas justify the dimension and dominance statements on the specified open sets. The distinction between geometric dominance and coverage of rational points is essential and is handled correctly.

The novelty is sufficient within this stated scope. I compared the relevant arguments with Paper 120 v2 [1], Paper 37 v1 [4], and the witness-frame results in Paper 75 v1, supplementary Section 3. Paper 120 states L3 as a conjecture and supplies the R996 example, but does not give these proofs or a moving full-support reconstruction. Paper 75 treats exceptional extensions of a fixed frame, while Paper 37 concerns a different diagonal convention. The external comparison with Gibbs [2] and Brouwer–Haemers [3, Section 10.6] confirms that the matrix/cofactor framework, rank-one lifting method, and classical equiangular-line results are established ingredients. The manuscript appropriately attributes them. The variable-diagonal rigidity argument, real inertia consequences, and explicit reconstruction provide substantive content beyond those ingredients.

The evidence is reproducible at the scale needed for these claims. After inspecting their code, I reran both supplied verification scripts on copies in the review workspace using Python 3 and SymPy 1.11.1. Both passed, including the symbolic root identities, rational maps and controls, inverse-tensor checks, 25 inertia cases, and 12 normalized conference signings. These are supporting exact checks; the universal conclusions rest on the written proofs, not on finite enumeration. No large search or unprovided computation is needed for acceptance.

The paper's organization, notation, self-contained abstract, and separation of proofs from computational support meet the clarity requirement. The following minor improvements would make the scope easier to follow:

- In Section 1's description of Conjecture L3, replace “all entries equal” with “all diagonal entries equal.” The off-diagonal signs need not agree.
- Define “ordinary” explicitly at first use and give the regular-triple relation when discussing controls without regular triples.
- Make the failure of rational-point surjectivity concrete using the existing control (21). Its kernel gives $p=7/4$, $r=5/4$, hence $A=27/4$, which has no rational square root. It therefore does not lift through this particular three-parameter chart despite being a valid rational frame.

Acceptance recognizes the proved rank restrictions and constructive four-frame analysis. Higher-rank septuple frames and the additional compatibility conditions needed for extension remain unresolved; the selected problem's solve definition is not met.
