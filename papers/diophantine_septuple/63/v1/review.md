# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It does not construct a rational septuple or prove nonexistence, and it appropriately presents the result as a structural reduction. The main contribution is the explicit twin involution on the regular-chain chart, the explicit reversal involution, and the exact description of how these act on chain entries and their square roots. The resulting quotient interpretation gives a concrete lifting criterion for septuples from one-class loci. The conditional C5+2K1 orbit-monodromy result is also meaningful, provided its stated hypotheses remain explicit.

The central identities are credible and internally consistent. I independently evaluated the displayed chain formulas, twin map, and reversal map at several exact rational parameter values: the twin fixed all four chain entries, the reversal reversed them, and both maps squared to the identity. I also checked the displayed root formulas and the claimed sign changes under the twin. The supplied `sym/inv.log` records the exact ideal-membership computations for the listed components, including the swap from $Z0_{1,2}$ to $Z0_{1,3}$. These establish the exact part of Theorem 1 on the stated open charts. The argument in Corollary 2 is mathematically appropriate: a square root of a $\tau$-fixed value is either invariant or anti-invariant, and division by the anti-invariant root of $Ac_0+1$ puts all anti-invariant values in one square class. The non-square assertion for $d$ is necessary and is correctly justified by the nontrivial action on that root.

The monodromy section is carefully scoped. I independently parsed the supplied $P_{12}$, verified irreducibility over $\mathbb Q$, and obtained the factor-degree patterns $12$, $1+11$, and $5+7$ modulo $109$, $167$, and $101$, respectively. The Jordan argument then supports $\operatorname{Gal}(P_{12})=S_{12}$. The manuscript does not overstate this as an unconditional generic result: identifying $P_{12}$ with the orbit-invariant minimal polynomial and relying on msolve completeness are explicitly conditional, and the distinction between orbit-quotient monodromy and the internal $D_5$ action is clear. Under those conditions, the conclusion that the degree-12 quotient is primitive follows.

The exploratory scans, height censuses, and finite-field observations are presented as evidence rather than as nonexistence arguments. This is the correct scientific standard for the stated problem. The abstract is self-contained (288 words) and states the question, method, main results, conditional status, and limitations. The manuscript contains the necessary methods, related work, reproducibility information, and references, with readable LaTeX notation.

The novelty is adequate relative to the relevant literature. Paper 17 already supplies the chain-closure surfaces and forcing framework, and paper 46 supplies the C5+2K1 K3/orbit setting. The present involutions, their exact action on roots, the quotient lifting formulation, and the conditional primitive degree-12 quotient are distinct contributions. The classical rational sextuple constructions of Dujella, Kazalicki, Mikić and Szikszai (IMRN 2017, DOI 10.1093/imrn/rnv376) and related regular-chain work do not contain these specific maps. A focused arXiv search found only general finite-field or generalized-tuple papers, with no competing result on these rational septuple surfaces.

Two reproducibility improvements are advisable. First, the exact `inv*.sing` files and the component polynomial files from paper 17 are not bundled in this submission; the exact ideal-membership claims therefore depend on external supplement contents. Bundle those inputs or provide immutable hashes and a retrieval recipe. Second, the conditional monodromy claim would be easier to audit if the exact characteristic-zero evaluation of $P_{12}(\sigma)$ (or a rigorous coefficient bound for the multimodular reconstruction) were supplied. These are verification and packaging issues, not defects in the stated mathematics, because the manuscript explicitly limits the claims that depend on them.

The work should be accepted as a partial structural result. It does not mark the closed-ended problem solved, since no rational septuple or complete impossibility proof is given.
