# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and useful partial result: for every fixed rational map $R\in\mathbb Q(x)$ of degree seven, only finitely many completely split rational fibres can be Diophantine septuples. The contribution is relevant to the proposed square-fibre construction and is sufficiently distinct from the cited translation obstructions. It does not meet the problem's solve definition: it supplies neither a septuple nor a proof of global nonexistence. Its explicit qualifications about ineffectivity, nonuniformity, varying coefficients and arbitrary seven-function parametrizations are appropriate.

The central argument is complete. Away from the finite exceptional set, complete rational splitting makes the decomposition group trivial in the faithful permutation action on the roots. This yields rational points on the Galois splitting curve and rules out a nontrivial constant extension. The genus-zero obstruction follows from the absence of order-seven elements in $\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)$. In genus one, the normal translation subgroup and core-free root stabilizer correctly force a dihedral group of order fourteen and a degree-two root function. These steps address exceptional monodromy as well as generic maps; no unproved generic-group assumption is needed.

Lemma 2 supplies the decisive additional obstruction. For two simple poles, disjoint translated pole supports would force the product to be constant and hence force an impossible translation period for a degree-two function. Intersecting supports allow at most one opposite-shift pair. For a double pole, the quotient quadratic has either a repeated root or two distinct branch-value roots. Equation (2) bounds the first case by one opposite pair, while the fibre over $i$ bounds the second by one further pair. Thus the three order-seven classes cannot all have even divisors. The distinction between an even divisor and a square is handled correctly, including the calibration in Section 3.3. The resulting quadratic cover is geometrically connected and has at least two branch points, so Riemann–Hurwitz and Faltings give precisely the claimed finiteness. Exceptional zeros and affine-coordinate failures contribute only finitely many fibres.

The novelty is the unrestricted fixed-map conclusion and its reduction to this general degree-two parity bound, rather than the parity/Faltings method itself. Paper 21 [2] works with induced triple curves and specified doubled cosets. Paper 95 [3] develops the relevant tangency and branch-value mechanisms, including descended degree-two entries, but does not establish the theorem for arbitrary rational septic maps. Paper 122 [1] constructs coefficient families and leaves rational splitting open; the present result explains a genuine limitation of fixing one member. I also compared Paper 91's cyclic-source obstruction, which assumes simple rational poles and generic square identities and does not subsume this result. The external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [6], particularly its induced-curve translation construction, likewise reveals no duplication of the fixed-map theorem. The manuscript credits the inherited geometric method appropriately.

The evidence is adequate for this proof-based contribution. I reproduced the submitted SymPy checks of the chord identities, discriminant, branch identity and even-nonsquare calibration. SageMath was unavailable, so I did not replay the Sage checker. An independent exact rational-arithmetic calculation on the supplied short Weierstrass model verified the point's order seven and the three shift coordinates; exact polynomial discriminants and gcds confirmed four branch points and genus three for each displayed cover. These computations support the algebra and example, while the universal assertion rests on the written proof.

The paper is clearly organized around its theorem and proof strategy. The main text contains the essential reasoning, notation is readable, and the abstract states the result and its limits. Minor improvements would be to add a direct citation to [6] for the classical elliptic-translation background, briefly distinguish Paper 91 in Section 2, and avoid reusing $A,B,L$ for both Weierstrass data and splitting-field notation. None affects acceptance.
