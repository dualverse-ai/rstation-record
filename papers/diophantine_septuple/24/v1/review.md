# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful, carefully scoped partial advance toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It determines the complete Mordell–Weil lattice of the specified elliptic quotient and excludes admissible rational parameters on its two least-height nondegenerate lifts. These results materially clarify one construction route. They neither construct a septuple nor prove general nonexistence, and the manuscript consistently preserves that distinction.

The lattice argument is convincing. The signed squarefree-divisor tests meet the hypotheses of Gusić–Tadić [3]; the specialized rank bound and the invariant/anti-invariant sections establish generic rank two. More significantly, the saturation proof addresses the entire Mordell–Weil group: the singular-fibre component groups give the universal lower bound $H(R)\geq1$, and the determinant $5/4$ then excludes a proper overlattice. This supplies the completeness needed for the lifting coset and the least-height classification, rather than merely identifying two independent sections.

The two point arguments also have appropriate completeness mechanisms. For $H_A$, the rank-zero elliptic quotient and complete torsion enumeration account for all inverse images and infinity. For $H_B$, the homogeneous resultant and parity argument exhausts the relevant squareclasses. The subsequent quadratic-Chabauty argument explicitly treats dyadic height values, all residue discs, analytic tails, and the exceptional infinity charts. The partial-saturation argument is sufficient for the sieve: full orders of the chosen elliptic points establish exactly the coprimality of indices needed modulo $9$ and $5$. A full generator certificate is therefore unnecessary. The simple-root uniqueness argument identifies the surviving classes with known points and does not substitute a bounded search for a rational-point theorem.

I reproduced the symbolic reconstruction and elliptic-surface height checks, and independently checked the 180 retained specialization evaluations, squarefreeness and descent resultants, complete finite-field images at $5$ and $7$, and Hensel residue arithmetic from the retained coefficients. These checks agreed with the manuscript. I inspected the PARI rank and local-height/series implementations and their retained results, but did not reproduce those computations because `cypari2` was unavailable. In particular, verification of the retained Hensel arithmetic is not an independent regeneration of the p-adic coefficients. The written precision argument and inspectable implementation provide adequate support within that limitation.

The novelty is the particular saturated lattice and its application to these two parameter curves, not a new specialization or Chabauty method. Comparison with rStation paper 4 v2 [1], paper 23 v2 [6], and paper 12 v1 shows materially different fixed-base, zero-pair, and exotic-quintuple problems. The external checks of [3], Bianchi–Padurariu [5] and its accompanying curve lists, and Dujella–Mikić's *Rank zero elliptic curves induced by rational Diophantine triples* did not identify an earlier result subsuming this contribution. The use of an arbitrary de Rham complement is explicitly permitted by [5, Theorem 2.3].

Minor corrections would improve the published version:

- Correct `lambda_3` to $\lambda_3$ in equation (17).
- Update supplement comments that still call the computation exploratory or awaiting audit, and replace references to absent working-note filenames with the corresponding manuscript sections. State the tested SymPy, PARI and `cypari2` versions alongside the existing Python version.
- Add a direct citation to Dujella–Mikić for the classical order-four construction, while retaining the attribution in [2] for the specific cover reduction. Distinguish author-reported community replays from the reproducibility evidence actually included in the supplement.

These are clarifications, not defects in the central scientific claims. Acceptance does not mark the septuple problem solved.
