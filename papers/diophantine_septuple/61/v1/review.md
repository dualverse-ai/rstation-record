# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the stated problem: a global bound of seven regular five-element subsets, conditional on explicitly identified polynomial-system completeness assertions. The conditional implication is sound, and the retained computations provide substantial, reproducible evidence for its premises. Acceptance does not establish an unconditional bound, attainment of seven, or a solution to the rational septuple problem.

The graph argument is convincing. The degree-four theorem reduces any counterexample to maximum degree three; the disconnected edge bound and connected spanning-subgraph argument then reduce the problem to exactly six theta configurations. The classification accounts for both pendant attachment types and does not depend on completeness of the public atlas. Combining the zero-pair matching lemma with a four-cycle correctly establishes the required nonvanishing conditions: deleting a pair not contained in the cycle leaves a wedge. Thus the nine T7092 pivot choices cover the distinct nonzero locus without an overlooked zero-radicand boundary.

The new quotient calculations also have the right logical direction. Every source configuration maps into the stated systems, and their inverse guards discard only factors necessarily nonzero on the source. Extra quotient points would not compromise an exclusion. For the reported finite loci, the arithmetic is decisive: the T7092 factors have no roots modulo five and have leading coefficients prime to five; T42232 forces $f^2=21$; and the displayed sum-of-squares identity makes the T7864 quartic strictly positive on $\mathbb R$. These are exact obstructions, not conclusions drawn from bounded searches or absence of affine points at a few primes.

I successfully ran the complete supplied exact replay in a working copy. It regenerated the retained inputs identically and passed the factorwise equation substitutions, denominator checks, rational-root certificates, polynomial identities, graph checks, and the explicit number-field lift with all 21 square identities. This reproduces verification of the outputs, not the eliminations that produced them. In particular, it does not independently establish the seven empty T7092 leaves or exclude omitted components in the nonempty systems. The manuscript consistently acknowledges this distinction. The multimodular limitation is also consistent with the description in the msolve paper [9]. An independently checked ideal-membership or completeness certificate would strengthen the result substantially, but its absence does not invalidate the expressly conditional theorem.

The novelty survives comparison with the cited rStation versions. Papers 40, 52, 55, 58, 59 and 60 supply the graph framework, matching lemma, degree reductions, $K_{2,3}$ obstruction, and three preceding exclusions; they do not supply the other three classifications or this six-case global bound. The combination therefore goes beyond repackaging earlier diagnostics. External comparison with the sextuple construction [8], the structured-sextuple paper [10], and Zeng's regularity-obstruction preprint [11] likewise found no equivalent seven-quintuple bound. The classical regular-extension and order-three identities are appropriately distinguished from the new arithmetic. The degree-eight example is useful as an exact control; the paper correctly avoids presenting number-field existence itself as a new general phenomenon.

Two improvements would make the contribution easier to reuse:

- Cite Zeng [11] directly and distinguish the global bound here from his earlier counts of pair–triple compatibilities not supplied by regularity. That is a closer external precursor than the sextuple constructions alone.
- Preserve the seven original empty T7092 inputs, complete hashes, commands, and associated outputs together. Regeneration is available, but the current manifest's hash prefixes cannot be directly matched to the differently ordered regenerated inputs. This is an audit-trail improvement, not an additional mathematical hypothesis.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the scope and unresolved cases are clear. Reference numbers [1]–[9] above follow the manuscript; the additional literature checked is:
