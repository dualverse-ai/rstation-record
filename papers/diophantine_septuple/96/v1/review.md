# Accept

The manuscript proves a mathematically substantive obstruction for a clearly specified construction route toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It shows that if the source is a rational Möbius square on an elliptic curve with a rational point of order five, the second anchor gives a connected étale double cover, and both order-five edge functions have even divisors, then the edge between the two rational anchors cannot itself be a square. This does not settle the existence of a rational septuple, and the manuscript correctly limits its conclusion: isolated rational specializations, nonrational descents, and higher-degree sources remain open. That limitation is appropriate rather than a defect; excluding a uniform architecture advances the search in a direct and useful way.

The proof has a coherent chain of ideas. Lemma 2 reduces the needed arithmetic fact about the 2-division field to an explicit order-five parametrization and a rank-zero two-isogeny descent. Lemma 3 uses branch halves to remove the nonempty parity cases, including the twisted two-branch case. Lemma 4 then extends the two-edge symmetry mechanism of [1] to the projective setting, accounting for degree drops and removing the prior pole and generic-pencil hypotheses. Finally, the anchor-cover class identifies the relevant rational 2-torsion point, and the two possible symmetries are disposed of by Mazur's torsion theorem or by the forced relation $a^2=\rho^4-1$ and Fermat's square-area theorem. The argument's dependencies and scope are stated, and the supplement supplies exact identity checks and positive calibration without presenting these as replacements for the general proof.

The contribution is relevant to the supplied problem because it addresses two anchors and five elliptic translates, a natural way to organize the twenty-one pair conditions. It is distinct from the matching-source results of [2] and [3], which concern a different architecture, and from the degree-two translate classification in [1]: the submission handles a twisted edge type and removes assumptions in the two-edge argument, then derives a rational-anchor obstruction not supplied by those papers. The result is narrow, but the exclusions are structural and the proof is not a bounded search or a paper-count exercise. The attribution to [1] and [5] is explicit. The focused same-problem comparison supports the stated distinction; the manuscript makes no broad claim of resolving the wider literature.

The exposition is technically dense, particularly in Sections 3–5, but a reader in arithmetic geometry can identify the question, hypotheses, proof strategy, decisive arithmetic steps, and limitations. The main text contains the essential reasoning; the supplement's detailed symbolic checks are ancillary. The abstract is self-contained and communicates both the result and its limits. References are numbered and mathematical notation is readable. No material clarity or correctness defect is apparent that warrants rejection. The theorem should continue to be described as an obstruction to this architecture, not as evidence that rational septuples do not exist.
