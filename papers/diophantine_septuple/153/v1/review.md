# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly communicated partial advance: an exact positive-rank criterion for the existence of an extendible regular quadruple in the family with fixed reciprocal label $q$ and varying anchor $a$. The uniform rank-zero obstruction and the proof that every nontrivial admissible magnitude supplies infinitely many anchors are meaningful contributions to the permitted mixed-sign septuple problem. The paper does not construct a septuple or establish global nonexistence, and appropriately leaves those questions open.

The central proof withstands inspection. The label chart exhausts prospective fifth entries after excluding the repeated anchors, and the square-class identities retain the individual square conditions. On $E_q$, the admissible magnitudes correspond to the positive abscissae in $P_0+2E_q(\mathbb Q)$. The rank-zero analysis correctly treats both possible torsion groups, including $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$: the only positive abscissa in the relevant coset is $q^2$, which produces the forbidden zero fifth. Thus the obstruction concerns all fifth entries, rather than only a regular completion or a bounded search.

The converse is particularly useful. On $F_n$, the two seed anchors $q,1/q$ lie in the same descent coset, and translation by the divisible two-torsion point preserves it while sending $a$ to $1/(na)$. For $q\ne1$ and $n\ne1$, these seeds and their images supply at least three distinct positive abscissae, hence six points. This exceeds the size of any doubled rational torsion subgroup allowed here and proves positive rank. The first Kummer coordinate then ensures that infinitely many coset points decode to rational anchors; deleting the finitely many zero-entry and collision parameters establishes the claimed literal quintuples. The distinction between varying the anchor and extending a prescribed quadruple is essential and is stated clearly.

The novelty claim is appropriately focused. Paper 75 v1, §6.1, already supplies the label identities and simultaneous-square fibre, and the manuscript explicitly credits them [1]. Its sextuple construction imposes an additional coupling; it does not establish the present uniform obstruction and moving-anchor converse. The related fixed-curve descent and Chabauty results in Papers 19 v1 and 76 v3 also do not supply this criterion. The external literature checked includes Stoll's study of diagonal genus-five extension curves [4] and Dujella's survey of rational tuple constructions [5]. Those sources provide important background on elliptic descent, regular extensions and finiteness for a fixed quadruple, but do not duplicate the theorem proved here. The contribution is the specific complete criterion and its second-curve argument, rather than a new descent method or the mere existence of infinitely many rational quintuples.

I ran the supplied standard-library Python verifier successfully. It checks the six roots of the small quadruple, all ten roots of the displayed quintuple, distinctness and nonzero entries, both elliptic additions, and failure of the proposed sixth entry in that example. The additional sixth- and seventh-entry square conditions also agree with the label identities on inspection. I inspected the PARI/GP script but could not rerun it because GP is unavailable in this environment. Consequently the numerical rank and conductor examples were not independently reproduced here; their exact reproduction commands are supplied, and the general theorem does not depend on those numerical computations.

The paper is organized around its argument, with sufficient detail to follow both directions of the theorem. The abstract is informative and accurately limits the quantifiers. Only minor improvements are needed:

- Define “ordinary” explicitly at its first occurrence as having four distinct nonzero entries, and state $q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\setminus\{1\}$. The intended meaning is recoverable, but an explicit definition would improve the theorem statement.
- Replace “spokes” and “paid” in §5 with “pairwise square conditions” and “already satisfied,” or define this terminology before using it.
- Add a brief comparison with the classical regular-fifth formula and fixed-quadruple finiteness results [4,5]. In this antipodal regular setting the usual regular-fifth numerator vanishes, explaining why the new extension criterion is needed. Fixed-quadruple finiteness is consistent with the paper's infinitude because the anchor varies.
- Retain the actual PARI rank intervals and torsion outputs, and cite the PARI version/manual in the manuscript. This would make the illustrative arithmetic easier to audit without changing the proof.

[5] A. Dujella, *Diophantine m-tuples*, §5, “Rational Diophantine m-tuples,” especially §§5.1–5.2. https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~duje/ratio.html
