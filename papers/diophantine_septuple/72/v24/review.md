# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound, substantive extension. The half-step operator and its conic parametrization explain additional compatible entries in the regular-chain construction; the bilinear trace elimination gives explicit osculating families and a further Branch-1 sextuple section. These results remain relevant partial progress toward the septuple problem. The ordinary regular-chain recurrence is already present in [4], and the two-sided-quintuple framework in [3, 5]; the useful additions here are the compatibility identities and explicit sections built on that framework. No complete solution of the selected problem is established.

I ran `exp68`, `exp69`, and `exp70` in writable copies; all assertions passed. This reproduced the 640 signed additions, the symbolic half-step and reconstruction identities, the degree-16 irreducibility calculation at $(r,t)=(2,4)$, and the supplied exact specializations of the new families. The added `exp12` regression independently passes: the regular fifths of $\{1,3,8,120\}$ are $0$ and $777480/8288641$, and $(r,\lambda)=(13/6,-4/5)$ yields one sextuple and nineteen extension records, comprising one $20/21$ and eighteen $19/21$ septuples. I did not rerun the full historical census or the unchanged verification suite.

The explicit partition of compatible pairs supplies the general lower bound $12N+2$, with the stated specializations supplying one or two additional pairs. This is an appropriate analytic justification beyond the checks at $N=1,2,3$. The previous review's two local qualifications are also substantially addressed.

The following local corrections and clarifications would improve the revision without changing the acceptance decision:

- In Theorem 20(70)(1), the displayed equality $\kappa=bk$ is incorrect for the stated $k=(Y_0-1)/(X_0-1)$. The definitions give $\kappa=d_-/k$. At the supplied $(2,4,3)$ example, $k=35/32$, $\kappa=48/7$, whereas $bk=7$. The signed reconstruction formulas and their executable checks use the correct definition, so this is a local transcription error.
- State the tower counts as guaranteed automatic squares for arbitrary $N$. The edge partition proves these lower bounds; the finite specialization checks do not by themselves establish exactness for every $N$. Apply the formulas on the open set excluding poles, zero entries and collisions.
- Keep the degree-16/genus-seven conclusion explicitly confined to the stated fixed fibre. Irreducibility there does not exclude lower-genus exceptional specializations or lower-genus quotients, and does not determine the remaining compatibility condition on the separately specialized $Q_5^{\mathrm{Cheb}}(q)$ family.
- Add a numbered reference to Philip Gibbs, *A Generalised Stern–Brocot Tree from Regular Diophantine Quadruples*, arXiv:math/9903035, when discussing the tree construction. Distinguish the new quadratic-map formulation from classical regular completion and tree generation. The classical fourth-element construction is attributed there to Arkin, Hoggatt and Strauss; check the manuscript's “Arkin–Hoggatt–Bergum” attribution.

The expanded final paragraph of Section 9 is difficult to navigate. Separating the cubic model, tower construction and osculating sections into short propositions would make their hypotheses and evidentiary scope much clearer. The abstract would likewise benefit from emphasizing these mechanisms and their limits rather than listing so many internal symbols.
