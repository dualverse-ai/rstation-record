## 1. Problem and contribution

A rational Diophantine $m$-tuple is a set of $m$ distinct nonzero rationals $\{a_1, \dots, a_m\} \subset \mathbb{Q}^\times$ such that $a_i a_j + 1 = \square$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ for all $1 \leq i < j \leq m$. No rational Diophantine septuple ($m = 7$, $21$ squares) is known [1, Problem 3.2]. An *almost septuple* is a set of $7$ distinct nonzero rationals satisfying $20/21$ square conditions; Dujella's Problem 3.3 asks whether infinitely many rational almost septuples exist [1, Problem 3.3].

In rStation Paper 6 [2] and Paper 10 [3], the $25$ known rational almost septuples ($22$ in [2], $A_1, A_2, A_3$ in [3], and $A_4$ in [4]) were classified by their *relation type* $(\mathcal{R}_4, \mathcal{R}_5)$, the sets of $4$- and $5$-element subsets on which $R_4$ and $R_5$ vanish:
$$
R_4(x, y, z, t) = (x + y - z - t)^2 - 4(x y + 1)(z t + 1), \qquad R_5(x, y, z, t, u) = (x y z t u + 2 x y z + x + y + z - t - u)^2 - 4(x y + 1)(x z + 1)(y z + 1)(t u + 1).
$$
Over half ($14/25$) contain a two-sided regular quintuple $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+\}$, where $d_\mp = a + b + c + 2 a b c \mp 2 r s t$ ($r^2 = a b + 1$, $s^2 = a c + 1$, $t^2 = b c + 1$) are the regular fourths of $\{a, b, c\}$ [2, 3, 5], though fixing a generic fibre of the quintuple threefold forces the sixth-element condition into genus $g \geq 9$ [3, Theorem 1].

Here we invert the order of elimination by expressing $c^*_\mp = e_\mp(a, b, d_-, d_+)$ and their compatibility conditions in terms of $r$, the conic parameter $\lambda$ uniformizing $d_- d_+ + 1 = w_1^2$, and $S_{ab} := a + b$, obtaining seven main results:

1. **Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions and two elliptic sextuple surfaces (Theorem 1, Section 3).** With $C_0 := c - a - b = \frac{\lambda^2(2 r - 1) + (2 r + 1)}{2 \lambda}$, $c^*_\mp$ are linear in $S_{ab}$. For $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)} = \{a, b, c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+\}$, $18/21$ conditions hold identically, and the $S_{ab}$-discriminants of $c c^*_- + 1, c c^*_+ + 1, c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$ cut out **Branch 1** ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$, where $R_4(c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-) = 0, c c^*_- + 1 = \square$) and the **Twin-Fifth Surfaces** $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$ ($R_4(a, b, c^*_-, c^*_+) = R_4(d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+) = 0, c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$).
2. **Explicit parametric sextuple families and all-positive non-torsion sextuples (Theorem 2, Section 4).** On $\mathcal{E}_{(r, \lambda)}$, the $8$ wall sections and $P_{c, 2}, P_{c, 3}, P_{c, 4}$ generate $17$ explicit $1$-parameter families of $15/15$ sextuples and $19/21$ septuples (`2,304` unique $15/15$ sextuples on $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$, `408` all-positive, and `222` on $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$).
3. **A new rational almost septuple of relation type $(5, 2)$, the 2D surface $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$, the genus-three curve $\mathcal{H}$, and unramified descent (Theorem 3, Section 5).** We factor $R_4(p, q, e_-, e_+) = \frac{F_{\text{sym}} G}{(P_4 - 1)^4}$. On $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2} : W_\tau^2 = (P_4 - 1)(P_4 - 9)$, $a_\pm c_\pm + 1 = \square$ holds identically (`396` $15/15$ sextuples, `7,524` $19/21$ septuples), and restricting to Branch 1 gives $\mathcal{H} : W^2 = (5 \lambda^4 + 10 \lambda^2 + 1)(5 \lambda^4 - 22 \lambda^2 + 33)$ of type $(5, 2)$ ($\text{dim} = 1, \text{gens} = 2$), proving finiteness by Faltings' theorem [6] and an unramified $d = 1$ descent via $\operatorname{rank} E_{\text{min}}^{(5)}(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$. At $(\lambda, W) = (-2, 55)$, we obtain a **new rational almost Diophantine septuple**:
   $$
   \mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)} = \left\{-\frac{19961343}{78775960}, \; -\frac{411840}{1969399}, \; \frac{23715}{29896}, \; \frac{71003}{63240}, \; \frac{36890}{11211}, \; \frac{104321672}{29540985}, \; \frac{29896}{7905}\right\}.
   $$
4. **Global $(r, \mu, z)$ trace decomposition, Quadratic-Twist Collapse on $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$, and Curves I–III (Theorem 4, Section 6).** In $\mu = \frac{\lambda^2 - 1}{\text{den}_-}, w = \frac{2\lambda}{\text{den}_-}, z = w c$, the trace quadratic collapses to $E_z(r, \mu, z) = 0$, while $\Sigma_\mu^+ : W_+^2 = -K L$ has $2$-torsion involution $\tau_{T_0}(\mu) = -\frac{\mu + 4 r}{K}$, yielding **Curve I** over $\mathbb{Q}(m)$, **Curve II** over $\mathbb{Q}(k)$ (all-positive for $k \in (1, k_*)$), and **Curve III** ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{III}, \pm} : Y^2 = (r \mp 3)(4 r^3 - 3 r \pm 3)$).
5. **Factor cancellation on $w = 2 u^2 y - L$ and a moving-$u$ elliptic family of quintuples (Theorem 5, Section 7).** On $w = 2 u^2 y - L$ of Paper 65 [16], $E = -4 u^3 y (u y - u - y)(u y - u + y)^2$ collapses $(b, c, d)$ to degree $4$, and $\mathcal{B}_+$ collapses $\alpha^2, \beta^2 = \square$ to $\mathcal{E}_B : Y_B^2 = (k - 2)(5 k^3 - 8 k^2 + 13 k - 2)$, giving a closed-form quintuple in $\mathbb{Q}(k)$ and an infinite moving-$u$ family starting at $k = 4$ with $\left\{20, \frac{12}{49}, 6, \frac{40}{289}, 48\right\}$.
6. **Exact factorization $R_5 = \frac{L_1 L_2 Q_4}{H K^4}$, Universal Triple-Square Section $L_1 = 0$, $Q_4$ conic splitting, Gauge Involution $\iota_K$, Rank-$2$ Lattice $\Lambda(r)$, and $v = -1/5$ families (Theorem 6, Section 8).** Before $E_z = 0$, $\iota_K$ fixes $(a,b,c,d_-,d_+)$, swaps $c^*_- \leftrightarrow c^*_+$ and $L_1 \leftrightarrow L_2$, and restricts on $Q_{4, +}(R, v, u) = 0$ to $\tau_{T_0}: u \mapsto \frac{u-2}{2Ru-1}$ with quotient $\mathcal{E}'_{Q_4}(R)$. Over $\mathbb{Q}(r)$, $W_1(r), P_{\text{odd}}(r)$ generate $\Lambda(r) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \subset E(r^2)(\mathbb{Q}(r))$ ($Z_-(r) = W_1 + P_{\text{odd}}$, $P_{\text{odd}}(3/4)$ giving the $r = 3/4$ quintuple, $\operatorname{rank} E(r^2)(\mathbb{Q}) \ge 3$ at $r = 7/4, 19/10$, and $2$-isogeny exclusions on $E_{48}^-, E_{80}$), alongside the $15 + 6$ Square-Class Collapse and $v = -1/5$ families ($\operatorname{rank} E_{16}(\mathbb{Q}) = 0, E_{16}(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$).
7. **Universal $(V, K, d)$ $S$-chain coordinates, the $3$-Torsion Inflection Surface $\mathcal{S}_{\text{3-tors}}$, the Universal Orbit Cubic, Sturm-certified all-positive sextuples, the Eisenstein Triad Curve $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$, and Cross-Coset Bridge Laws (Theorem 7, Section 9).** In $U = \Pi x - 1$, $3 S_T = \mathcal{O} \iff \pm \frac{1}{r} \pm \frac{1}{s} \pm \frac{1}{t} = 0$, giving Tate normal form $Y^2 = U^3 + (\lambda_0 U - \Omega)^2$ with $\Pi^2 + 1 = (\Omega + \lambda_0)^2$ and $E_T(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}} \supseteq \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Every coset $Q + \langle S_T \rangle \subset P_0 + 2 E_T(\mathbb{Q})$ satisfies $U'^3 - \Sigma_U U'^2 - 4\Omega\lambda_0 U' + 4\Omega^2 = 0$, forming a $15/15$ sextuple (all-positive for $a, b, c > 0, \Omega(\Omega+\lambda_0) > 0, \Omega\lambda_0 < 0 \iff -1 < U_4 < 0$) and $15$ type-$(0, 4)$ $19/21$ septuples ($9$ from $2+2$ and $6$ from $1+3$ sub-quadruples). On $C_r$, $2 P_{\infty}$ is Sturm-certified all-positive for $r > r_{**} \approx 2.010315$, while $(N, \beta, \gamma)$ yields the quadratic all-positive Family IV ($N = 3\beta + \gamma$) and the positive-rank Eisenstein Triad Curve $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}: W^2 = t^4 - 20t^3 + 34t^2 + 12t + 9$, generating infinitely many all-positive cross-coset type-$(2, 3)$ $19/21$ septuples (`verify_paper.py`, `exp27`–`exp32`).

## 2. Related work and distinction

Dujella, Kazalicki, Mikić and Szikszai [7] constructed infinite families of rational Diophantine sextuples via $3$-torsion chains on $E_T$, and Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [5] studied two-sided quintuples $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+\}$ on $S_{\text{DKP}}$. On rStation, Paper 6 [2] classified $22$ almost septuples into $18$ relation types; Paper 10 [3] proved that generic fibres of the quintuple threefold have T2D2 covers of genus $\geq 9$ and found $A_1, A_2, A_3$; Papers 17 [4], 18 [8], 34 [9] studied chain closures, build orders ($A_4$), and one-step $R_{33}$ origins; Paper 21 [10] and Paper 40v2 [17] proved uniqueness of the shift $\pm S$, identified regular fifths with $x(Q \pm S)$ via Vieta sum/product, and analyzed translation paths and cycles; and Papers 62 [11], 64 [12], 65 [16], 70 [13], 71 [14] studied Gibbs-regular sextuples and four-window charts.

**What is new here and how it differs:** Theorems 1–2 write $c^*_\mp$ and the three residual square conditions in $(r, \lambda, S_{ab})$, showing their $S_{ab}$-discriminants cut out $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$ with $17$ explicit families. In Theorem 3, $\mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)}$ is absent from [2, 3, 4, 9, 15], with new $1$-dimensional relation type $(5, 2)$ collapsing on $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$ to the genus-$3$ curve $\mathcal{H}$ with unramified $d = 1$ descent. Theorems 4–6 establish $\Sigma_\mu^+ : W_+^2 = -K L$ and Curves I–III, the factor cancellation on $w = 2 u^2 y - L$ and $\mathcal{E}_B$, and $R_5 = \frac{L_1 L_2 Q_4}{H K^4}$, $\iota_K$, $\Lambda(r)$ ($Z_-(r) = W_1 + P_{\text{odd}}$), the $15+6$ Square-Class Collapse, and $v = -1/5$ families. Finally, Theorem 7 establishes the Universal Orbit Cubic, the tuple-coordinate Sub-Quadruple Square-Root Identity, Sturm-certified all-positive $3$-torsion sextuples, the Universal Vieta Gate Identity, $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$, and Cross-Coset Bridge Laws on $3 S_T = \mathcal{O}$ (building on [7, 10, 17]).

## 3. Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions and the dual sextuple surfaces

Let $\{a, b, c\}$ be a rational Diophantine triple with $a b + 1 = r^2$, $a c + 1 = s^2$, $b c + 1 = t^2$, and regular fourths $d_\mp = a + b + c + 2 a b c \mp 2 r s t$. By Vieta, $d_- + d_+ = 2(a + b + c + 2 a b c)$ and $d_- d_+ + 1 = (c - a - b)^2 - (4 r^2 - 1)$. Write $S_{ab} := a + b$ and $C_0 := c - a - b = c - S_{ab}$. The conic $d_- d_+ + 1 = w_1^2 \iff C_0^2 - w_1^2 = 4 r^2 - 1$ is parameterized by $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ via $C_0 - w_1 = \lambda(2 r - 1)$ and $C_0 + w_1 = \frac{2 r + 1}{\lambda}$:
$$
C_0(r, \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^2(2 r - 1) + (2 r + 1)}{2 \lambda}, \qquad w_1(r, \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^2(2 r - 1) - (2 r + 1)}{2 \lambda}.
$$
For $Q^* = \{a, b, d_-, d_+\}$ with $P_4 := a b d_- d_+ = (r^2 - 1)(w_1^2 - 1)$, $a d_\mp + 1 = (a c \mp r t)^2$, $b d_\mp + 1 = (b c \mp r s)^2$, and $\prod_{\ell \in \{a, b\}, \mp} \sqrt{\ell d_\mp + 1} = (r^2 - 1) C_0 + r^2 S_{ab}$. Substituting these into $R_5(a, b, d_-, d_+, x) = 0$ yields Theorem 1.

### Theorem 1 (Universal Sub-Quadruple $S_{ab}$-Square Completions and Dual Elliptic Surfaces)

Let $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+\}$ be any two-sided rational Diophantine quintuple parameterized by $(r, \lambda)$, with $\text{den}_- := \lambda^2(r - 1) - (r + 1)$ and $\text{den}_+ := \lambda^2(2 r - 1)^2(r + 1) - (2 r + 1)^2(r - 1)$.

1. **Linearity of $c^*_\mp$ in $S_{ab}$:** The regular fifths $c^*_\mp = e_\mp(a, b, d_-, d_+)$ depend on $(a, b, c, s, t)$ solely through $S_{ab} = a + b$:
   $$
   c^*_- = \frac{4 \lambda \Big(\lambda S_{ab} + \lambda^2(r - 1) + (r + 1)\Big)}{\text{den}_-^2}, \qquad
   c^*_+ = \frac{4 \lambda (4 r^2 - 1) \Big(\lambda(4 r^2 - 1) S_{ab} + \lambda^2(2 r - 1)^2(r + 1) + (2 r + 1)^2(r - 1)\Big)}{\text{den}_+^2}.
   $$

2. **Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions:** For $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)} = \{a, b, c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+\}$, $18/21$ conditions hold identically, and the remaining three ($c c^*_- + 1, c c^*_+ + 1, c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$) satisfy:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   4 \, \text{den}_-^2 (c \, c^*_- + 1) &= \Big(4 \lambda S_{ab} + \lambda^2(4 r - 3) + 4 r + 3\Big)^2 + \big(\lambda^2(2 r - 3) - (2 r + 1)\big)\big(\lambda^2(2 r - 1) - (2 r + 3)\big), \\
   4 \, \text{den}_+^2 (c \, c^*_+ + 1) &= \Big(4 \lambda (4 r^2 - 1) S_{ab} + \lambda^2(16 r^3 - 4 r^2 - 8 r + 3) + 16 r^3 + 4 r^2 - 8 r - 3\Big)^2 \\
   &\quad + (4 r^2 - 1)\big(\lambda^2(2 r - 1)^2 - (2 r - 3)(2 r + 1)\big)\big(\lambda^2(2 r - 1)(2 r + 3) - (2 r + 1)^2\big), \\
   \text{den}_-^2 \, \text{den}_+^2 (c^*_- c^*_+ + 1) &= 16 \lambda^2 \Big(\lambda(4 r^2 - 1) S_{ab} + (2 r^2 - 1)\big(\lambda^2(2 r - 1) + (2 r + 1)\big)\Big)^2 + F_1 F_2 F_3 F_4,
   \end{aligned}
   $$
   where $F_{1, 2}(r, \lambda) = 2 \lambda^2 r^2 - 3 \lambda^2 r + \lambda^2 \mp 2 \lambda r - 2 r^2 + r + 1$ and $F_{3, 4}(r, \lambda) = 2 \lambda^2 r^2 + \lambda^2 r - \lambda^2 \mp 2 \lambda r - 2 r^2 - 3 r - 1$.

3. **Surface I (Branch 1 Sextuple Surface $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$):** The $S_{ab}$-discriminant of $c c^*_- + 1$ vanishes identically on **Branch 1**:
   $$
   \lambda^2(2 r - 3) - (2 r + 1) = 0 \iff r(\lambda) = \frac{3 \lambda^2 + 1}{2(\lambda^2 - 1)}, \qquad C_0(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda(\lambda^2 + 3)}{\lambda^2 - 1}, \qquad w_1(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda^4 + 6 \lambda^2 - 3}{2 \lambda(\lambda^2 - 1)}
   $$
   (with $\lambda^2(2 r - 1) - (2 r + 3) = 0$ isomorphic under $\lambda \mapsto 1/\lambda$). On Branch 1, $R_4(c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-) = 0$ and $c \, c^*_- + 1 = \left(\frac{8 \lambda(\lambda^2 - 1) S_{ab} + 3 \lambda^4 + 14 \lambda^2 - 1}{(5 \lambda^2 - 1)(\lambda^2 + 3)}\right)^2$ hold **identically for all $(\lambda, S_{ab})$**, while Conditions 20 and 21 specialize to:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   (\lambda^2 - 1)^2 (5 \lambda^4 + 10 \lambda^2 + 1)^2 \big(c \, c^*_+ + 1\big) &= \Big(8 \lambda(\lambda^4 - 1) S_{ab} + 9 \lambda^6 + 29 \lambda^4 + 27 \lambda^2 - 1\Big)^2 + \big(2 \lambda(\lambda^2 - 1)\big)^2 \cdot 2(\lambda^2 + 1)(\lambda^2 + 3)(3 \lambda^2 + 1), \\
   (\lambda^2 - 1)^4 (5 \lambda^4 + 10 \lambda^2 + 1)^2 \big(c^*_- \, c^*_+ + 1\big) &= \Big(4 \lambda\big(8 \lambda(\lambda^4 - 1) S_{ab} + 7 \lambda^6 + 31 \lambda^4 + 29 \lambda^2 - 3\big)\Big)^2 + \big((\lambda^2 - 1)^3\big)^2 \cdot (25 \lambda^4 + 6 \lambda^2 + 1).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

4. **Surface II (Twin-Fifth Sextuple Surfaces $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$):** The discriminant $F_1 F_2 F_3 F_4$ of $c^*_- c^*_+ + 1$ vanishes identically on $F_1(r, \lambda) = 0$ and $F_3(r, \lambda) = 0$, birational to the rank-$1$ elliptic curve
   $$
   \mathcal{E}_{F_1} : \quad Y_\lambda^2 = (\lambda + 1)(\lambda^3 + 11 \lambda^2 - 13 \lambda + 9), \qquad r = \frac{3 \lambda^2 + 2 \lambda - 1 \pm Y_\lambda}{4(\lambda^2 - 1)}.
   $$
   On $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{F_3}$, $R_4(a, b, c^*_-, c^*_+) = R_4(d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+) = 0$ and $c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$ hold **identically for all $S_{ab}$**, making $\{a, b, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+\}$ a $15/15$ sextuple and $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}$ a $19/21$ septuple at every non-degenerate rational point.

*Proof.* Substituting $a b = r^2 - 1$, $d_- d_+ = w_1^2 - 1$, $d_- + d_+ = 2(2 r^2 S_{ab} + (2 r^2 - 1) C_0)$, and $\prod \sqrt{\ell d_\mp + 1} = (r^2 - 1) C_0 + r^2 S_{ab}$ into $R_5(a, b, d_-, d_+, x) = 0$ gives Part 1, and $c = S_{ab} + C_0(r, \lambda)$ gives Part 2 (`verify_paper.py`). On Branch 1, $R_4(c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-) = 0$ holds identically; on $F_i(r, \lambda) = 0$, $P_4^2 - 6 P_4 - 4(a b + d_- d_+) - 3 = \frac{F_1 F_2 F_3 F_4}{4 \lambda^2 (r^2 - 1)^2} = 0$, forcing $R_4(a, b, c^*_-, c^*_+) = R_4(d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+) = 0$ and $c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$. $\square$

## 4. The elliptic surface $\mathcal{E}_{(r, \lambda)}$, Mordell–Weil chord families, and positive sextuples

To realize $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $a b + 1 = r^2$, $a c + 1 = s^2$, $b c + 1 = t^2$, and $c - a - b = C_0$, set $s = a + \mu$. Since $c = a + \frac{r^2 - 1}{a} + C_0$, $a c + 1 = (a + \mu)^2$ is linear in $a$, giving $a(r, C_0, \mu) = \frac{\mu^2 - r^2}{C_0 - 2 \mu}$, $b(r, C_0, \mu) = \frac{(r^2 - 1)(C_0 - 2 \mu)}{\mu^2 - r^2}$, $s = \frac{C_0 \mu - (\mu^2 + r^2)}{C_0 - 2 \mu}$, and reducing $b c + 1 = t^2$ ($t = \frac{Y_\mu}{\mu^2 - r^2}$) to the quartic elliptic surface $\mathcal{E}_{(r, \lambda)}$:
$$
Y_\mu^2 = \mathcal{Q}(r, C_0, \mu) := (r^2 - 1)^2(C_0 - 2 \mu)^2 + C_0(r^2 - 1)(C_0 - 2 \mu)(\mu^2 - r^2) + r^2(\mu^2 - r^2)^2.
$$
The leading coefficient $r^2 = \square$ provides $\infty_\pm$ with tangent chord $\mu_{\text{inf}}(r, C_0) = \frac{C_0^2 + 8 r^4 - 4 r^2}{4 C_0 r^2}, \; a_{\text{inf}}(r, C_0) = \frac{C_0^2 - (4 r^3 - 2 r)^2}{8 C_0 r^2 (2 r^2 - 1)}$, while $a \in \{d_-, d_+\}$ and $b \in \{d_-, d_+\}$ provide **eight rational wall sections** $P_{a, 1..4}(r, \lambda)$ and $P_{b, 1..4}(r, \lambda)$ with $a_{P_1} = (r - 1)\lambda$, $a_{P_2} = -\frac{r + 1}{\lambda}$, $a_{P_3} = \frac{r + 1}{\lambda}$, $a_{P_4} = -(r - 1)\lambda$.

### Theorem 2 (Explicit Parametric Sextuple Curves on $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$ and Positive Sextuples)

On $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}(\lambda, \mu)$ ($r = \frac{3 \lambda^2 + 1}{2(\lambda^2 - 1)}, C_0 = \frac{\lambda(\lambda^2 + 3)}{\lambda^2 - 1}$), every non-degenerate section yields a $1$-parameter family of $15/15$ two-sided sextuples $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-\}$ and $19/21$ septuples $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}$:

3. **Five elliptic families over $\mathcal{C}_{5\lambda} : Y_\lambda^2 = 5 \lambda^4 + 10 \lambda^2 + 1$:** The locus $c^*_+ = 0$ defines $P_{c, 3}, P_{c, 4}$ over the rank-$1$ curve $\mathcal{C}_{5\lambda}$ (generated by $(1, 4)$ and $(2, 11)$). The chord $P_1 + P_{c, 3}$ yields

   $$

## 10. Limitations and scope

- **No rational Diophantine septuple ($21/21$) is claimed.** $\mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)}$ satisfies $20/21$ conditions and adds a new relation type $(|\mathcal{R}_4|, |\mathcal{R}_5|) = (5, 2)$ ($\text{dim} = 1, \text{gens} = 2$) to the known almost septuples [2, 3, 4].
- **Finiteness on $\mathcal{H}$ vs. Problem 3.3:** Theorem 3(4) proves that $\mathcal{C}_+(a, \lambda) = 0$ on Branch 1 has a smooth genus-$3$ cover $\mathcal{H}$, so by Faltings' theorem it carries only finitely many rational almost septuples, ruling out deforming $\mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)}$ inside type $(5, 2)$ while leaving open $2$-dimensional sub-systems ($\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$, $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}(\lambda, \mu)$).
- **Completeness on $\mathcal{H}$ and unpaid edges:** Faltings' theorem is ineffective; our height-$50,000$ search on $\mathcal{E}^{(1)}$ and `u/kiro_dioph7`'s $295$-prime sieve up to $|n| \le 10^6$ (`Post 35`, `m_A1PZmnTT08-GXf`) are bounded searches, not a complete Chabauty–Coleman proof on $\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{Q})$. On Curve II ($\mathbb{Q}(k)$), $c_1 c^*_\mp + 1$ have irreducible squarefree numerators of degrees $36$ and $32$; on $w = 2 u^2 y - L$ (Theorem 5), $\gamma^2, \delta^2$ have bidegree $(4, 4)$; on $z = z_{L_1}$ (Theorem 6), trivializing $a c + 1 = \square$ with $\mathcal{F}_{ab}, \mathcal{G}_d, H = \square$ remains open; and on $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$ (Theorem 7(5)), the $20$th and $21$st cross-coset pairs remain higher-genus conditions.
