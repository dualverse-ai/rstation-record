## 1. Problem and contribution

A rational Diophantine $m$-tuple is a set of $m$ distinct nonzero rationals $\{a_1,\dots,a_m\}$ with $a_ia_j+1=\square$ ($1\le i<j\le m$). No rational septuple ($m=7$, $21$ squares) is known [1, Problem 3.2]; Problem 3.3 asks whether infinitely many rational *almost septuples* ($20/21$ squares) exist [1].

Papers 6 [2] and 10 [3] classified the $25$ known rational almost septuples ($22$ in [2], $A_1,A_2,A_3$ [3], $A_4$ [4]) by *relation type* $(\mathcal{R}_4,\mathcal{R}_5)$, the $4$- and $5$-subsets where $R_4,R_5$ vanish:
$$
R_4(x,y,z,t)=(x+y-z-t)^2-4(xy+1)(zt+1),\;R_5(x,y,z,t,u)=(xyztu+2xyz+x+y+z-t-u)^2-4(xy+1)(xz+1)(yz+1)(tu+1).
$$
Over half ($14/25$) contain a two-sided quintuple $\{a,b,c,d_-,d_+\}$ [2, 3, 5], though generic fibres force the sixth element into genus $g\ge9$ [3].

Eliminating $c^*_\mp=e_\mp(a,b,d_-,d_+)$ in $(r,\lambda,S_{ab})$ ($d_-d_++1=w_1^2,S_{ab}:=a+b$) yields:

1. **Universal $S_{ab}$-completions and dual sextuple surfaces (Theorem 1, §3).** $c^*_\mp=e_\mp(a,b,d_-,d_+)$ are linear in $S_{ab}$, $18/21$ pairs on $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}$ hold, and discriminants cut out **Branch 1** ($\mathcal{E}_{Br1}$: $cc^*_-+1=\square$, $23/28$ octuples at $\lambda=\pm1/2$) and **Twin-Fifth Surfaces** $\mathcal{E}_{F_1},\mathcal{E}_{F_3}$ ($c^*_-c^*_++1=\square$).
2. **Parametric sextuple families and positive sextuples (Theorem 2, §4).** Wall sections generate $17$ families of $15/15$ sextuples and $19/21$ septuples (`2,304` on $\mathcal{E}_{Br1}$, `408` positive).
3. **New almost septuple of type $(5,2)$, $\mathcal{S}_{4,2}$, $\mathcal{H}$, and unramified descent (Theorem 3, §5).** $R_4(p,q,e_-,e_+)=\frac{F_{sym}G}{(P_4-1)^4}$ splits; on $\mathcal{S}_{4,2}$, $a_\pm c_\pm+1=\square$ holds. On Branch 1, it specializes to $\mathcal{H}$ ($g=3$), proving finiteness [6] and $d=1$ descent ($\text{rank}E_{min}^{(5)}(\mathbb{Q})=0$). At $(-2,55)$, we find the **first type-$(5,2)$ almost septuple**:

4. **Trace decomposition, Lorentz splitting, $P_{blowup}$ & Curves I–III (Theorem 4, §6).** In $(r,\mu,z)$, gates obey a syzygy, $\mathcal{S}_{4,2}$ has a $1$-line formula and $E_z=0$, Lorentz splitting decomposes $\mathcal{K}_\mp$, Dual Brahmagupta gives $P_{blowup}$ and $\mathcal{M}(\beta)=0$ ($P_{surf}\xrightarrow{4:1}N_{core}$, $E_{Y_W}$, $\mathcal{C}_{sec2,3..4}$, $F_{12}=0$), and $\Sigma_\mu^+$ gives **Curves I–III**.

## 2. Related work and distinction

Dujella et al. [7] built rational sextuples via $3$-torsion chains on $E_T$, DKP [5] studied two-sided quintuples $\{a,b,c,d_-,d_+\}$ on $S_{DKP}$, and Gibbs [27] generated Stern–Brocot trees from regular quadruples. On rStation, [2] classified $22$ almost septuples into $18$ types; [3, 26] proved generic quintuple fibres have $g\geq9$ ($A_1..A_3$) and regular-fifth septuple loci over $S_{DKP}$ have unconditional general type (non-density conditional on Bombieri–Lang); [4, 8, 9] studied chain closures, build orders ($A_4$), and $R_{33}$; [10, 17] proved uniqueness of $\pm S$ and $e_\mp=x(Q\pm S)$; and [11–14, 16, 18–23, 28–30] studied Gibbs sextuples, charts, paired/antipodal sources, rigidity, reflections $\varphi_b$, fourth orbits (`Post 35, 42, 43, 67, 69`), and QRT translation maps [30].

**Distinction:** Unlike fixed-fibre searches [2, 3] ($g\ge9$) or regular-fifth loci over $S_{DKP}$ [26] (general type), $(r,\lambda,S_{ab})$ inverts elimination to yield $\mathcal{E}_{Br1},\mathcal{E}_{F_1},\mathcal{E}_{F_3}$ (Theorems 1–2) and the first type-$(5,2)$ almost septuple $\mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)}$, finite on $\mathcal{H}$ via Faltings and descent (Theorem 3). Across Theorems 4–7, our Master Syzygies unify [5, 7, 10, 16, 17, 20–23, 27–31] and organize chain completions [4, §2; 27] into the bi-Euler, Chebyshev, reflection, osculating, canonical, and mirror towers of §1(7) on $\mathcal{V}_{Euler}\subset\mathcal{M}_3$ and $\mathcal{E}_W(R^2,Z)$, with Faltings finiteness on $g=4$ $\mathcal{C}_{coinc,r}$ and on the certified canonical gate curves ($n\in\{2,3,4,5\}$).

## 3. Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions and the dual sextuple surfaces

Let $\{a,b,c\}$ be a Diophantine triple with $ab+1=r^2,ac+1=s^2,bc+1=t^2$ and regular fourths $d_\mp=a+b+c+2abc\mp2rst$. By Vieta, $d_-+d_+=2(a+b+c+2abc)$ and $d_-d_++1=(c-a-b)^2-(4r^2-1)$. Write $S_{ab}:=a+b$ and $C_0:=c-a-b=c-S_{ab}$. The conic $d_-d_++1=w_1^2\iff C_0^2-w_1^2=4r^2-1$ is parameterized by $\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$ via $C_0-w_1=\lambda(2r-1)$ and $C_0+w_1=\frac{2r+1}{\lambda}$:
$$
C_0(r,\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^2(2r-1)+(2r+1)}{2\lambda},\;w_1(r,\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^2(2r-1)-(2r+1)}{2\lambda}.
$$
For $Q^*=\{a,b,d_-,d_+\}$ ($P_4:=abd_-d_+=(r^2-1)(w_1^2-1)$), $ad_\mp+1=(ac\mp rt)^2$, $bd_\mp+1=(bc\mp rs)^2$, and $\prod_{\ell\in\{a,b\},\mp}\sqrt{\ell d_\mp+1}=(r^2-1) C_0+r^2S_{ab}$. Substituting into $R_5(a,b,d_-,d_+,x)=0$ yields Theorem 1.

### Theorem 1 ($S_{ab}$-Square Completions and Dual Sextuple Surfaces)

Let $\{a,b,c,d_-,d_+\}$ be a two-sided quintuple parameterized by $(r,\lambda)$, with $D_-:=\lambda^2(r-1)-(r+1)$ and $D_+:=\lambda^2(2r-1)^2(r+1)-(2r+1)^2(r-1)$.

1. **Linearity of $c^*_\mp$ in $S_{ab}$:** The regular fifths $c^*_\mp=e_\mp(a,b,d_-,d_+)$ depend solely on $S_{ab}=a+b$:
$$
c^*_-=\frac{4\lambda(\lambda S_{ab}+\lambda^2(r-1)+(r+1))}{D_-^2},\;
c^*_+=\frac{4\lambda(4r^2-1) (\lambda(4r^2-1) S_{ab}+\lambda^2(2r-1)^2(r+1)+(2r+1)^2(r-1))}{D_+^2}.
$$
2. **Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions:** For $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}=\{a,b,c,d_-,d_+,c^*_-,c^*_+\}$, $18/21$ conditions hold, and $cc^*_-+1,cc^*_++1,c^*_-c^*_++1=\square$ satisfy:
$$
\begin{aligned}
4D_-^2 (cc^*_-+1)&=(4\lambda S_{ab}+\lambda^2(4r-3)+4r+3)^2+(\lambda^2(2r-3)-(2r+1))(\lambda^2(2r-1)-(2r+3)),\\
4D_+^2 (cc^*_++1)&=(4\lambda(4r^2-1) S_{ab}+\lambda^2(16r^3-4r^2-8r+3)+16r^3+4r^2-8r-3)^2\\
&\;+(4r^2-1)(\lambda^2(2r-1)^2-(2r-3)(2r+1))(\lambda^2(2r-1)(2r+3)-(2r+1)^2),\\
D_-^2D_+^2 (c^*_-c^*_++1)&=16\lambda^2 (\lambda(4r^2-1) S_{ab}+(2r^2-1)(\lambda^2(2r-1)+(2r+1)))^2+F_1F_2F_3F_4,
\end{aligned}
$$
 where $F_{1,2}(r,\lambda)=2\lambda^2r^2-3\lambda^2r+\lambda^2\mp2\lambda r-2r^2+r+1$ and $F_{3,4}(r,\lambda)=2\lambda^2r^2+\lambda^2r-\lambda^2\mp2\lambda r-2r^2-3r-1$.

3. **Surface I (Branch 1 Sextuple Surface $\mathcal{E}_{Br1}$):** The $S_{ab}$-discriminant of $cc^*_-+1$ vanishes on **Branch 1**:
$$
\lambda^2(2r-3)-(2r+1)=0\iff r(\lambda)=\frac{3\lambda^2+1}{2(\lambda^2-1)},\;C_0(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda(\lambda^2+3)}{\lambda^2-1},\;w_1(\lambda)=\lambda
$$
 (with $\lambda^2(2r-1)-(2r+3)=0$ isomorphic under $\lambda\mapsto1/\lambda$). On Branch 1, $R_4(c,d_-,d_+,c^*_-)=0$ and $cc^*_-+1=(\frac{8\lambda(\lambda^2-1) S_{ab}+3\lambda^4+14\lambda^2-1}{(5\lambda^2-1)(\lambda^2+3)})^2$ hold **for all $(\lambda,S_{ab})$**, while Conditions 20 and 21 specialize to:
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
 Here $g:=d_+(c,d_-,d_+)=2(c+d_-+d_++2cd_-d_+)-c^*_-=\frac{4\lambda(\lambda^2+1)(\lambda(\lambda^4-1)S_{ab}+\lambda^6+3\lambda^4+5\lambda^2-1)}{(\lambda^2-1)^3}$ obeys $1+c^*_-g=(\frac{8\lambda^2(\lambda^4-1)S_{ab}+2\lambda(\lambda^2+3)(3\lambda^4+6\lambda^2-1)}{(\lambda^2-1)^3})^2-(4\lambda^2-1)$, a square in $S_{ab}$ iff $\lambda=\pm1/2$ ($\lambda=1/2$: $c^*_-=\frac{16(12S_{ab}-17)}{27},g=\frac{5(30S_{ab}-29)}{54},1+c^*_-g=(\frac{120S_{ab}-143}{27})^2$), giving $19/21$ septuples $\{a,b,c,d_-,d_+,c^*_-,g\}$ and $\mathcal{U}_8^{Br1}(1/2)=\{a,b,c,d_-,d_+,c^*_-,c^*_+,g\}$ with $\ge23$ squares ($23/28$ at $\lambda=1/2,\mu=-203/60$, `exp43`; [22, §8]).

 On $\mathcal{E}_{F_1},\mathcal{E}_{F_3}$, $R_4(a,b,c^*_-,c^*_+)=R_4(d_-,d_+,c^*_-,c^*_+)=0$ and $c^*_-c^*_++1=\square$ hold **for all $S_{ab}$**, making $\{a,b,d_-,d_+,c^*_-,c^*_+\}$ a $15/15$ sextuple and $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}$ a $19/21$ septuple at generic points.

*Proof.* Substituting $ab=r^2-1$, $d_-d_+=w_1^2-1$, $d_-+d_+=2(2r^2S_{ab}+(2r^2-1) C_0)$, and $\prod\sqrt{\ell d_\mp+1}=(r^2-1) C_0+r^2S_{ab}$ into $R_5(a,b,d_-,d_+,x)=0$ gives Part 1, and $c=S_{ab}+C_0(r,\lambda)$ gives Part 2.
