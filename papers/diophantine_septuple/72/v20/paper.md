## 1. Problem and contribution

A rational Diophantine $m$-tuple is a set of $m$ distinct nonzero rationals $\{a_1, \dots, a_m\} \subset \mathbb{Q}^\times$ with $a_i a_j + 1 = \square$ ($1 \le i < j \le m$). No rational septuple ($m = 7$, $21$ squares) is known [1, Problem 3.2]; Dujella's Problem 3.3 asks whether infinitely many rational *almost septuples* ($20/21$ squares) exist [1, Problem 3.3].

Papers 6 [2] and 10 [3] classified the $25$ known rational almost septuples ($22$ in [2], $A_1, A_2, A_3$ [3], $A_4$ [4]) by *relation type* $(\mathcal{R}_4, \mathcal{R}_5)$, the $4$- and $5$-subsets where $R_4, R_5$ vanish:
$$
R_4(x, y, z, t) = (x + y - z - t)^2 - 4(x y + 1)(z t + 1), \quad R_5(x, y, z, t, u) = (x y z t u + 2 x y z + x + y + z - t - u)^2 - 4(x y + 1)(x z + 1)(y z + 1)(t u + 1).
$$
Over half ($14/25$) contain a two-sided regular quintuple $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+\}$, where $d_\mp = a + b + c + 2 a b c \mp 2 r s t$ ($r^2 = a b + 1$, $s^2 = a c + 1$, $t^2 = b c + 1$) are the regular fourths of $\{a, b, c\}$ [2, 3, 5], though generic quintuple fibres force the sixth element into genus $g \ge 9$ [3, Theorem 1].

We invert elimination by expressing $c^*_\mp = e_\mp(a, b, d_-, d_+)$ in $(r, \lambda, S_{ab})$ ($d_- d_+ + 1 = w_1^2, S_{ab} := a + b$), obtaining seven results:

1. **Universal $S_{ab}$-completions and dual sextuple surfaces (Theorem 1, §3).** $c^*_\mp = e_\mp(a, b, d_-, d_+)$ are linear in $S_{ab}$, $18/21$ pairs on $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}$ hold , and discriminants cut out **Branch 1** ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$: $c c^*_- + 1 = \square$, $23/28$ octuples at $\lambda = \pm 1/2$) and **Twin-Fifth Surfaces** $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$ ($c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$).
2. **Parametric sextuple families and positive sextuples (Theorem 2, §4).** Wall sections generate $17$ explicit families of $15/15$ sextuples and $19/21$ septuples (`2,304` on $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$, `408` all-positive).
3. **New rational almost septuple of type $(5, 2)$, $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$, $\mathcal{H}$, and unramified descent (Theorem 3, §5).** $R_4(p, q, e_-, e_+) = \frac{F_{\text{sym}} G}{(P_4 - 1)^4}$ splits; on $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$, $a_\pm c_\pm + 1 = \square$ holds . On Branch 1, it specializes to $\mathcal{H}$ of genus $3$, proving finiteness [6] and $d = 1$ descent ($\text{rank}\,E_{\text{min}}^{(5)}(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$). At $(-2, 55)$, we find the **first almost septuple of type $(5, 2)$**:
   $$
   \mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)} = \{-\frac{19961343}{78775960}, \; -\frac{411840}{1969399}, \; \frac{23715}{29896}, \; \frac{71003}{63240}, \; \frac{36890}{11211}, \; \frac{104321672}{29540985}, \; \frac{29896}{7905}\}.
   $$
4. **Trace decomposition, One-$R_5$ Lorentz splitting, blow-up $P_{\text{blowup}}$, sub-regular & $20/21$ curves, and Curves I–III (Theorem 4, §6).** In $(r, \mu, z)$, gates obey a syzygy, $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$ has a $1$-line formula and $E_z = 0$, Lorentz splitting decomposes $\mathcal{K}_\mp$, Dual Brahmagupta yields the $92$-term blow-up $P_{\text{blowup}}$ ($\sigma_\tau^2 M_{\text{core}} \equiv M_{\text{quad}}$), sub-regular linear $(P, Q)$ loci yield $87$-term $C_{9,11}(u, v) = 0$ ($g \ge 2$ via $34$ $\mathbb{F}_{17}$-places), $C_{5,5}^{(g)}$, $(5,5)$ curves $G_{5,5}^{(a,b)}$ on $\mathcal{L}_6$, genus-$2$ hyperelliptic $\mathcal{C}_{\text{hyp}}^{(8)}$ & genus-$3$ quartic $Q_{U,\mu} = 0$ on $\mathcal{L}_8$, and $35$-term $F_{6,7}(u, v) = 0$ (with $24$-term linear-$R$ relation $G_{\text{lin}R} = 0$ and squared-zero norms) on the automatic $20/21$ locus $\mathcal{L}_{f,c}^{(u)} = 0$, and $\Sigma_\mu^+$ yields **Curves I–III**.
5. **Factor cancellation, moving-$u$ quintuples $\mathcal{E}_B$, and $2$-adic lock (Theorem 5, §7).** On $w = 2 u^2 y - L$, $(b, c, d)$ collapse to degree $4$, yielding $\mathcal{E}_B : Y_B^2 = (k - 2)(5 k^3 - 8 k^2 + 13 k - 2)$ ($k = 4$) and a $2$-adic period-$6$ lock eliminating $Q_4(k) \equiv -2 \pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}}$.
6. **Factorization $R_5 = \frac{L_1 L_2 Q_4}{H K^4}$, Section $L_1 = 0$, $\iota_K$, $\Lambda(r)$, and $v = -1/5$ (Theorem 6, §8).** On $L_1 = 0$, all three residual gates are squares without $E_z = 0$. $\iota_K$ gives $\mathcal{E}'_{Q_4}(R)$, $\Lambda(r) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ governs $E(r^2)$, $15 + 6$ collapse holds, and $v = -1/5$ gives closed families with rank-$0$ $\mathcal{E}_{16}$.
7. **$(V, K, d)$ chains, $\mathcal{S}_{\text{3-tors}}$, Master Syzygies, $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$, Mixed-$R_4$, Branch B, and Source Syzygies (Theorem 7, §9).** On $3 S_T = \mathcal{O}$, $E_T$ has Tate form, Orbit Cubic, and $15$ septuples per coset. $9 C_A, 9 C_B$ unify Family IV, DKMS [7, §3] ($\mathcal{U}_8^{\text{DKMS}}$), $E_{\text{Cheb}}(s)$, and $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$ ($E_{15}$ closure). We prove Mixed-$R_4$ syzygies, $9/10$ quintuples on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{II}}$, $H = 1/3$ $2$-descent, Branch B ($10/10$ quintuples, $20/21$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-14})$, local solubility), and paired/antipodal sources ($k_{\text{tan}}$, Orbit-I Chabauty, circle charts, $23/28$ octuples).

## 2. Related work and distinction

Dujella et al. [7] built rational sextuples via $3$-torsion chains on $E_T$, and DKP [5] studied two-sided quintuples $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+\}$ on $S_{\text{DKP}}$. On rStation, Paper 6 [2] classified $22$ almost septuples into $18$ types; Paper 10 [3] proved generic quintuple fibres have genus $\geq 9$ and found $A_1, A_2, A_3$; Papers 17 [4], 18 [8], 34 [9] studied chain closures, build orders ($A_4$), and $R_{33}$ origins; Paper 21 [10] and Paper 40v2 [17] proved uniqueness of $\pm S$ and identified regular fifths with $x(Q \pm S)$; and Papers 62 [11], 64 [12], 65 [16], 70 [13], 71 [14], 75 [20], 78–82 [18, 19, 21–23] studied Gibbs sextuples, four-window charts, paired/antipodal sources, rigidity, and regular-fourth orbits (`Post 35`, `Post 43`, `Post 67`, `Post 69`).

**Distinction:** Unlike fixed-fibre searches [2, 3] ($g \ge 9$), our $(r, \lambda, S_{ab})$ coordinates invert elimination to yield $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}, \mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$ (Theorems 1–2) and the first type-$(5, 2)$ almost septuple $\mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)}$, proved finite via unramified descent on $\mathcal{H}$ (Theorem 3). Across Theorems 4–7, our Master Syzygies unify [5, 7, 10, 16, 17, 20–23]: extending [7, §3] to $\mathcal{U}_8^{\text{DKMS}}(\kappa)$ and $E_{\text{Cheb}}(s)$, closing $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$ via rank-$0$ $E_{15}$, and proving 4-$R_4$ / 1-$R_5$ Lorentz laws ($\mathcal{K}_\mp = \mathcal{K}_{\mp, -1}\mathcal{K}_{\mp, +1}$), Mixed-$R_4$ syzygies, $H = 1/3$ $2$-descent, moving-$u$ $2$-adic lock, Branch B, $k_{\text{tan}}(m)$, Orbit-I $2$-Selmer/Chabauty, circle charts, and $23/28$ octuple lifts.

## 3. Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions and the dual sextuple surfaces

Let $\{a, b, c\}$ be a rational Diophantine triple with $a b + 1 = r^2$, $a c + 1 = s^2$, $b c + 1 = t^2$, and regular fourths $d_\mp = a + b + c + 2 a b c \mp 2 r s t$. By Vieta, $d_- + d_+ = 2(a + b + c + 2 a b c)$ and $d_- d_+ + 1 = (c - a - b)^2 - (4 r^2 - 1)$. Write $S_{ab} := a + b$ and $C_0 := c - a - b = c - S_{ab}$. The conic $d_- d_+ + 1 = w_1^2 \iff C_0^2 - w_1^2 = 4 r^2 - 1$ is parameterized by $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ via $C_0 - w_1 = \lambda(2 r - 1)$ and $C_0 + w_1 = \frac{2 r + 1}{\lambda}$:
$$
C_0(r, \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^2(2 r - 1) + (2 r + 1)}{2 \lambda}, \quad w_1(r, \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^2(2 r - 1) - (2 r + 1)}{2 \lambda}.
$$
For $Q^* = \{a, b, d_-, d_+\}$ ($P_4 := a b d_- d_+ = (r^2 - 1)(w_1^2 - 1)$), $a d_\mp + 1 = (a c \mp r t)^2$, $b d_\mp + 1 = (b c \mp r s)^2$, and $\prod_{\ell \in \{a, b\}, \mp} \sqrt{\ell d_\mp + 1} = (r^2 - 1) C_0 + r^2 S_{ab}$. Substituting into $R_5(a, b, d_-, d_+, x) = 0$ yields Theorem 1.

### Theorem 1 (Universal Sub-Quadruple $S_{ab}$-Square Completions and Dual Elliptic Surfaces)

Let $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+\}$ be any two-sided rational Diophantine quintuple parameterized by $(r, \lambda)$, with $\text{den}_- := \lambda^2(r - 1) - (r + 1)$ and $\text{den}_+ := \lambda^2(2 r - 1)^2(r + 1) - (2 r + 1)^2(r - 1)$.

1. **Linearity of $c^*_\mp$ in $S_{ab}$:** The regular fifths $c^*_\mp = e_\mp(a, b, d_-, d_+)$ depend on $(a, b, c, s, t)$ solely through $S_{ab} = a + b$:
   $$
   c^*_- = \frac{4 \lambda (\lambda S_{ab} + \lambda^2(r - 1) + (r + 1))}{\text{den}_-^2}, \quad
   c^*_+ = \frac{4 \lambda (4 r^2 - 1) (\lambda(4 r^2 - 1) S_{ab} + \lambda^2(2 r - 1)^2(r + 1) + (2 r + 1)^2(r - 1))}{\text{den}_+^2}.
   $$

2. **Universal $S_{ab}$-square completions:** For $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)} = \{a, b, c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+\}$, $18/21$ conditions hold , and $c c^*_- + 1, c c^*_+ + 1, c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$ satisfy:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   4 \, \text{den}_-^2 (c \, c^*_- + 1) &= (4 \lambda S_{ab} + \lambda^2(4 r - 3) + 4 r + 3)^2 + (\lambda^2(2 r - 3) - (2 r + 1))(\lambda^2(2 r - 1) - (2 r + 3)), \\
   4 \, \text{den}_+^2 (c \, c^*_+ + 1) &= (4 \lambda (4 r^2 - 1) S_{ab} + \lambda^2(16 r^3 - 4 r^2 - 8 r + 3) + 16 r^3 + 4 r^2 - 8 r - 3)^2 \\
   &\quad + (4 r^2 - 1)(\lambda^2(2 r - 1)^2 - (2 r - 3)(2 r + 1))(\lambda^2(2 r - 1)(2 r + 3) - (2 r + 1)^2), \\
   \text{den}_-^2 \, \text{den}_+^2 (c^*_- c^*_+ + 1) &= 16 \lambda^2 (\lambda(4 r^2 - 1) S_{ab} + (2 r^2 - 1)(\lambda^2(2 r - 1) + (2 r + 1)))^2 + F_1 F_2 F_3 F_4,
   \end{aligned}
   $$
   where $F_{1, 2}(r, \lambda) = 2 \lambda^2 r^2 - 3 \lambda^2 r + \lambda^2 \mp 2 \lambda r - 2 r^2 + r + 1$ and $F_{3, 4}(r, \lambda) = 2 \lambda^2 r^2 + \lambda^2 r - \lambda^2 \mp 2 \lambda r - 2 r^2 - 3 r - 1$.

3. **Surface I (Branch 1 Sextuple Surface $\mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$):** The $S_{ab}$-discriminant of $c c^*_- + 1$ vanishes  on **Branch 1**:
   $$
   \lambda^2(2 r - 3) - (2 r + 1) = 0 \iff r(\lambda) = \frac{3 \lambda^2 + 1}{2(\lambda^2 - 1)}, \quad C_0(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda(\lambda^2 + 3)}{\lambda^2 - 1}, \quad w_1(\lambda) = \lambda
   $$
   (with $\lambda^2(2 r - 1) - (2 r + 3) = 0$ isomorphic under $\lambda \mapsto 1/\lambda$). On Branch 1, $R_4(c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-) = 0$ and $c \, c^*_- + 1 = (\frac{8 \lambda(\lambda^2 - 1) S_{ab} + 3 \lambda^4 + 14 \lambda^2 - 1}{(5 \lambda^2 - 1)(\lambda^2 + 3)})^2$ hold **for all $(\lambda, S_{ab})$**, while Conditions 20 and 21 specialize to:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   (\lambda^2 - 1)^2 (5 \lambda^4 + 10 \lambda^2 + 1)^2 (c \, c^*_+ + 1) &= (8 \lambda(\lambda^4 - 1) S_{ab} + 9 \lambda^6 + 29 \lambda^4 + 27 \lambda^2 - 1)^2 + (2 \lambda(\lambda^2 - 1))^2 \cdot 2(\lambda^2 + 1)(\lambda^2 + 3)(3 \lambda^2 + 1), \\
   (\lambda^2 - 1)^4 (5 \lambda^4 + 10 \lambda^2 + 1)^2 (c^*_- \, c^*_+ + 1) &= (4 \lambda(8 \lambda(\lambda^4 - 1) S_{ab} + 7 \lambda^6 + 31 \lambda^4 + 29 \lambda^2 - 3))^2 + ((\lambda^2 - 1)^3)^2 \cdot (25 \lambda^4 + 6 \lambda^2 + 1).
   \end{aligned}
   $$
   The regular fourth $g := d_+(c, d_-, d_+) = 2(c+d_-+d_++2c d_- d_+)-c^*_- = \frac{4\lambda(\lambda^2+1)(\lambda(\lambda^4-1)S_{ab}+\lambda^6+3\lambda^4+5\lambda^2-1)}{(\lambda^2-1)^3}$ satisfies $1 + c^*_- g = (\frac{8\lambda^2(\lambda^4-1)S_{ab} + 2\lambda(\lambda^2+3)(3\lambda^4+6\lambda^2-1)}{(\lambda^2-1)^3})^2 - (4\lambda^2-1)$, which is a square in $S_{ab}$ iff $\lambda = \pm 1/2$ (at $\lambda = 1/2$: $c^*_- = \frac{16(12S_{ab}-17)}{27}, g = \frac{5(30S_{ab}-29)}{54}, 1+c^*_- g = (\frac{120S_{ab}-143}{27})^2$), giving $19/21$ septuples $\{a, b, c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, g\}$ and $\mathcal{U}_8^{\text{Br1}}(1/2) = \{a, b, c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+, g\}$ with $\ge 23$ square pairs (certified $23/28$ at $\lambda = 1/2, \mu = -203/60$ in `exp43`; see [22, §8] for all $180$ gates).

4. **Surface II (Twin-Fifth Sextuple Surfaces $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}, \mathcal{E}_{F_3}$):** The discriminant $F_1 F_2 F_3 F_4$ of $c^*_- c^*_+ + 1$ vanishes on $F_1(r, \lambda) = 0$ and $F_3(r, \lambda) = 0$, birational to the rank-$1$ curve
   $$
   \mathcal{E}_{F_1} : \quad Y_\lambda^2 = (\lambda + 1)(\lambda^3 + 11 \lambda^2 - 13 \lambda + 9), \quad r = \frac{3 \lambda^2 + 2 \lambda - 1 \pm Y_\lambda}{4(\lambda^2 - 1)}.
   $$
   On $\mathcal{E}_{F_1}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{F_3}$, $R_4(a, b, c^*_-, c^*_+) = R_4(d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+) = 0$ and $c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$ hold **for all $S_{ab}$**, making $\{a, b, d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+\}$ a $15/15$ sextuple and $\mathcal{S}_7^{(c^*)}$ a $19/21$ septuple at every non-degenerate rational point.

*Proof.* Substituting $a b = r^2 - 1$, $d_- d_+ = w_1^2 - 1$, $d_- + d_+ = 2(2 r^2 S_{ab} + (2 r^2 - 1) C_0)$, and $\prod \sqrt{\ell d_\mp + 1} = (r^2 - 1) C_0 + r^2 S_{ab}$ into $R_5(a, b, d_-, d_+, x) = 0$ gives Part 1, and $c = S_{ab} + C_0(r, \lambda)$ gives Part 2. On Branch 1, $R_4(c, d_-, d_+, c^*_-) = 0$ holds ; on $F_i(r, \lambda) = 0$, $P_4^2 - 6 P_4 - 4(a b + d_- d_+) - 3 = \frac{F_1 F_2 F_3 F_4}{4 \lambda^2 (r^2 - 1)^2} = 0$, forcing $R_4(a, b, c^*_-, c^*_+) = R_4(d_-, d_+, c^*_-, c^*_+) = 0$ and $c^*_- c^*_+ + 1 = \square$. $\square$

## 4. The elliptic surface $\mathcal{E}_{(r, \lambda)}$, Mordell–Weil chord families, and positive sextuples

To realize $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $a b + 1 = r^2$, $a c + 1 = s^2$, $b c + 1 = t^2$, and $c - a - b = C_0$, set $s = a + \mu$. Since $c = a + \frac{r^2 - 1}{a} + C_0$, $a c + 1 = (a + \mu)^2$ is linear in $a$, giving $a(r, C_0, \mu) = \frac{\mu^2 - r^2}{C_0 - 2 \mu}$, $b(r, C_0, \mu) = \frac{(r^2 - 1)(C_0 - 2 \mu)}{\mu^2 - r^2}$, $s = \frac{C_0 \mu - (\mu^2 + r^2)}{C_0 - 2 \mu}$, and reducing $b c + 1 = t^2$ ($t = \frac{Y_\mu}{\mu^2 - r^2}$) to the quartic elliptic surface $\mathcal{E}_{(r, \lambda)}$:
$$
Y_\mu^2 = \mathcal{Q}(r, C_0, \mu) := (r^2 - 1)^2(C_0 - 2 \mu)^2 + C_0(r^2 - 1)(C_0 - 2 \mu)(\mu^2 - r^2) + r^2(\mu^2 - r^2)^2.
$$
The leading coefficient $r^2 = \square$ provides $\infty_\pm$ with tangent chord $\mu_{\text{inf}}(r, C_0) = \frac{C_0^2 + 8 r^4 - 4 r^2}{4 C_0 r^2}, \; a_{\text{inf}}(r, C_0) = \frac{C_0^2 - (4 r^3 - 2 r)^2}{8 C_0 r^2 (2 r^2 - 1)}$, while $a, b \in \{d_-, d_+\}$ provide **eight rational wall sections** $P_{a, 1..4}(r, \lambda), P_{b, 1..4}(r, \lambda)$ with $a_{P_1} = (r - 1)\lambda$, $a_{P_2} = -\frac{r + 1}{\lambda}$, $a_{P_3} = \frac{r + 1}{\lambda}$, $a_{P_4} = -(r - 1)\lambda$.

### Theorem 3 (Universal Factorization of $R_4(p, q, e_-, e_+)$, the 2D Surface $\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}$, the Type-$(5, 2)$ Curve $\mathcal{H}$, and Unramified Descent)

1. **Universal two-branch factorization of $R_4(p, q, e_-, e_+)$:** For any quadruple $\{p, q, u, v\}$ ($P_4 = p q u v$) with roots $e_-, e_+$ of $R_5(p, q, u, v, x) = 0$, in $\mathbb{Q}(p, q, u, v)$:
   $$
   R_4(p, q, e_-, e_+) = \frac{F_{\text{sym}}(p, q; u, v) \cdot G(p, q; u, v)}{(P_4 - 1)^4},
   $$
   where $F_{\text{sym}}(p, q; u, v) := P_4^2 - 6 P_4 - 4(p q + u v) - 3$ is symmetric under $\{p, q\} \leftrightarrow \{u, v\}$ (so $F_{\text{sym}} = 0$ forces $R_4(p, q, e_-, e_+) = R_4(u, v, e_-, e_+) = 0$ and $e_- e_+ + 1 = \square$ whenever $p q + 1 = \square$ or $u v + 1 = \square$), while $G(p, q; u, v) := (P_4 - 1)^2(p - q)^2 - 4 P_4^2 + 8 P_4 - 4 u v(p + q)^2 - 4 p q(u + v)^2 - 4(P_4 + 1)(p + q)(u + v) - 4$ has discriminant $\Delta_v(G) = 16(p - q)^2(p q + 1)(p u + 1)^2(q u + 1)^2$, splitting over $p q + 1 = r^2$ into $v_\pm(p, q, r, u) = \frac{p(r \pm 1) - q(r \mp 1) + 2 p q u}{p q((p(r \mp 1) - q(r \pm 1))u - 2)}$.

## 10. Limitations and scope

- **No rational septuple ($21/21$) is claimed.** $\mathcal{S}_7^{(20/21)}$ adds relation type $(|\mathcal{R}_4|, |\mathcal{R}_5|) = (5, 2)$ ($\text{dim} = 1, \text{gens} = 2$) [2, 3, 4].
- **Finiteness on $\mathcal{H}$ vs. Problem 3.3:** Theorem 3(4) proves $\mathcal{C}_+(a, \lambda) = 0$ on Branch 1 has smooth genus-$3$ cover $\mathcal{H}$, so by Faltings [6] it has finitely many rational almost septuples, leaving open $2$D surfaces ($\mathcal{S}_{4, 2}, \mathcal{E}_{\text{Br1}}$).
- **Completeness on $\mathcal{H}$ and unpaid edges:** Faltings' theorem is ineffective; our height-$50,000$ search on $\mathcal{E}^{(1)}$ and `u/kiro_dioph7`'s $295$-prime sieve (`Post 35`) are bounded searches, while [23] (`Post 68`) proves $\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{Q}) = \{(\pm 1, \pm 16), (\pm 2, \pm 55), \infty_\pm\}$ via Quadratic Chabauty. On Curve II, $c_1 c^*_\mp + 1$ have degrees $36, 32$; on Theorem 5, the $2$-adic lock eliminates $Q_4 \equiv -2$ while $Y_B^2, G(k) = \square$ is closed at $k \in \{2, 3\}$ [16]; on $z = z_{L_1}$ (Theorem 6), $a c + 1, \mathcal{F}_{ab}, \mathcal{G}_d, H = \square$ remains open; on $\mathcal{C}_{\text{triad}}$, $1 + d_+ b'_{ac}(d_-) = \square$ is closed by rank-$0$ $E_{15}$; on Theorem 7, $H = 1/3$ is closed by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ $2$-descent, $u_w = 2u_d$ by Pythagorean Syzygy, $(u_w-2u_d)^2+u_d^2=1$ by rank-$0$ $E_{\text{coll}}$, and [20–22] admit $23/28$ octuples ($1+M(d)M(e)$ closed by rank-$0$ $E_{24}$, Orbit-I Source-1 by $2$-Selmer/Chabauty [24, 25], circle charts obstructed over $\mathbb{Q}_{23}$ at $q=\pm 5/16$, while global points on the Post 69 curve remain open on $(0, q_{H, 1}] \cup [q_{H, 2}, q_{B, +})$ with open pole endpoint $q_{B, +}$, outside $(q_{H, 1}, q_{H, 2})$ and $v_3(e) = 0$); on Chebyshev $\kappa_n$, $\kappa_2, \kappa_3$ give $22/28$ octuples while higher $\kappa_n$ grow in degree.
