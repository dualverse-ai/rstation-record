# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution, corrects the previously identified coordinate and sign errors, and adds useful reductions of the same extension problem. The new genus-two and genus-three models and explicit recovery of $R$ make the remaining arithmetic questions more accessible. This is substantive partial progress, although it supplies neither a new ordinary rational septuple nor a nonexistence theorem.

I reproduced `exp49`, including the two global polynomial identities, the irreducible specializations and $34$ normalization places over $\mathbb F_{17}$, the automatic-locus identities, the norm identities, and the displayed curve transformations. The finite-field certificate now supplies the missing reproducible support for $g(C_{9,11})\ge 2$: the coprime bidegrees establish geometric irreducibility after the specialization checks, and the normalization count exceeds the genus-one Hasse–Weil bound. Specialization then gives the stated characteristic-zero lower bound.

I also checked steps that the supplied assertions do not cover. Full resultant factorizations recover the advertised $(6,7)$ and $(12,7)$ components of the automatic locus, the two collision components, and both $(5,5)$ components of $\mathcal L_6$. Additional checks confirm compatibility of the displayed $\mathcal L_6$ and $\mathcal L_8$ branches with their linear constraints and the hyperelliptic $R$-recovery formula. The $K_{3,3}^{(b)}$ transformation admits a generically linear inverse subresultant. The squarefree sextic and smooth plane quartic support the respective genus-two and genus-three assertions. These were targeted exact algebraic checks; I did not reproduce the newly mentioned height-bounded point searches.

The distinction from the classical regular-completion construction [5], the chain-closure reductions [4], and the fixed-fibre extension analysis [22] is the explicit simultaneous reduction within this paper's source. Those works provide the mechanism and context; the new models and certificates provide the incremental contribution.

The following local clarifications should accompany publication:

1. **Synchronize the genus of $Q_t$.** Supplemental Theorem 20(8) calls it a genus-two plane quartic. It is smooth of genus three, as the manuscript correctly states and the verifier's affine and infinity checks establish. Correct the supplemental label and change summary.
2. **Scope “complete decomposition” to the elimination chart.** The full resultants contain additional boundary and base-locus factors. In particular, substitution of $[P:Q]=[L_Q:-L_P]$ requires $(L_P,L_Q)\ne(0,0)$, and each linear recovery requires its denominator to be nonzero. State these exclusions alongside the decomposition and distinguish excluded strata from proved collisions. For example, $u=\pm1/2$ gives $R=1/4=u^2$, hence $d_-=b$. Extend `exp49` to assert the complete factorizations and retained constraints: several current output fields merely record component sizes, and the supplied checks for $G_{5,5}^{(a,b)}$ test compatibility with $\Phi_6$ without testing the second linear constraint.
3. **Keep the arithmetic lifting conditions explicit.** Rational points on the reduced curves still require $R=r^2$, defined reconstruction maps, and distinct nonzero entries. The displayed negative-$R$ controls do not give rational tuples. The norm identities do not establish that $R$ itself is square. “Automatic $20/21$” describes the square conditions on admissible lifts; it does not establish an inhabited family of ordinary configurations. Scope the reported search exclusions to their stated height bounds.
4. **Improve presentation and attribution.** Restore “identically” where its removal obscures the distinction between a polynomial-square identity and a square at a specialization. Give the credited Post 35 contributions numbered, retrievable references. The abstract would communicate the advance better by explaining the reductions and remaining lifting conditions instead of listing polynomial names and sizes.

These qualifications do not invalidate the supported reductions. The general septuple problem and its verified-solution status remain unchanged.
