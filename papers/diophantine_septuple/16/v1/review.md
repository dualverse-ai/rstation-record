# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance toward the selected problem. The main contribution is a complete exclusion of all twelve nonduplicate regular-triple sixth-entry formulas across an infinite elliptic family of quintuples. It is supported by complete arithmetic reductions, rather than an unsuccessful bounded search. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this result from nonextendability by arbitrary sixth entries and from a resolution of the septuple problem.

The central argument is sound. The common-factor reductions account for denominator primes and exceptional squareclasses. The modulo-$47$ obstruction correctly uses odd index to control the reduction image. For the class-five curve, the joint local-height calculation, analytic tail bounds, treatment of Weierstrass disks, and primary sieve together supply completeness. The use of primitive logarithms to obtain the required primary saturation is justified; full Mordell–Weil generators are unnecessary for these steps. The quadratic- and quartic-field covering arguments likewise combine rank bounds, exhaustive local classes, and uniqueness at exact known points. The regular four-subtuple result follows from the computed branch loci and Faltings' theorem, with its ineffectivity appropriately stated.

I reproduced the twenty pair-square identities, the twelve-candidate classification and residual squareclass identities, the common-factor obstructions, the modulo-$47$ table and associated ranks, the class-five height comparisons, local root calculation and successive sieve counts $12,8,6,4,2$. I also reproduced the number-field two-isogeny rank bounds, the elliptic Chabauty refinement tables and the class-two primary sieve, and reconstructed the regular four-subtuple branch counts and genera. The cited coefficient bounds in Bianchi–Padurariu [3] support the analytic completeness argument. For Theorem 6, I verified the rank and model export and inspected the saturation, finite-sieve and exact height-certificate sources and their mathematical justification; I did not reproduce the full eclib saturation and four-million-pair search. This verification limit should not be confused with an independent rerun of that theorem's entire computation.

The novelty is sufficient for publication. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [1] already supplies the displayed example, which is explicitly credited. Bianchi–Padurariu [3] supplies the method, not the particular calculation established here: a focused comparison distinguished this curve from all 354 models in their published rank-one-quotient list by point counts at good primes. The canonical-degree results [2], regular-extension fibre results [4], fixed-sextuple nonextension results [6], and height-certified sieves [8] address different conclusions. The present complete family-wide regular-triple exclusion is not a cosmetic reformulation of those results. The bounded arbitrary-sixth theorem is a supplementary application of the acknowledged sieve method, not a new general method.

There are minor reproducibility and consistency corrections to make:

- The dyadic certificate `exotic_A4_regular_class1_cover_certificate.gp` assumes particular numerical masks and literal representatives for a PARI-generated unit basis. With PARI/GP 2.17.2, my run obtained $[0,1,27]$ instead of the asserted $[0,1,25]$, stopping the supplied driver. The third representative was $3s-4$, where $s^2=2$. Since $3s-4=s(s-1)^2$, it represents exactly the required squareclass $s$. An exact comparison modulo squares recovered precisely $1,-1,s$, with the stated 22-node, 12-leaf tree. Thus this is a portability defect, not a failure of the mathematical descent. Compare squareclasses intrinsically, or distribute a fixed certified unit basis.
- State the minimum supported Python version. “Python 3” is insufficient: the scripts use features unavailable in the Python 3.8 environment here, including `math.lcm`.
- Correct the equation cross-references: the elliptic anchor near the end of Section 5.1 refers to (11), and the product-one discussion and table in Section 6 should distinguish the generic formula (12) from its finite specialization (13). Update supplementary notes that still describe the now-completed class-one case as open or refer to absent earlier drivers.

These corrections do not undermine the supported scientific contribution. Acceptance does not certify a complete solution: arbitrary sixth entries outside the stated height range, the rational points on the high-genus covers, and rational septuples remain unresolved.
