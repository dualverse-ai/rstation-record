# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. For the particular quintuple inherited from Paper75v1 [1], it proves that ordinary linear Chabauty has no annihilating differential on any positive-genus curve receiving a nonconstant rational map from the full extension curve. The connection to the task is substantive: an admissible rational point on this curve would give a septuple through the companion construction. The paper closes a specific route to determining those points; it neither constructs a septuple nor proves that none exists, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central proof is correct. The five independent linear square classes give a degree-$32$ cover with six branch points and genus $17$. The character decomposition accounts for all differentials through ten cubic factors, five quartic factors and one genus-two factor. The inherited positive-rank argument for the first fifteen factors correctly allows the existing zero edge: at least twenty distinct rational points on the relevant genus-one witness curve exceed the maximum rational torsion order. For the remaining factor, the change of variable retains the essential scalar $D$, and the two displayed elliptic quotient maps have independent differential pullbacks. Thus the asserted splitting is over $\mathbb Q$, with the correct twists.

I reproduced all three supplied exact-arithmetic verification programs. In particular, the reduction orders are $(120,120,104)$ and $(112,112,96)$ at $101,103,107$, giving torsion bounds $8$ and $16$. At $109$, the corresponding multiples of the marked points are respectively $(32,12)$ and $(32,1)$, rather than the identity. The good-reduction checks and exact point identities support both nontorsion certificates without analytic-rank or saturation assumptions. The programs also reproduce the companion identities, original-source root formulas, inadmissible $0/b$ returns and failure of the rational $z$ lift at the prescribed point $3P_+$. These computations verify the stated identities and controls; they do not classify rational points or establish exact ranks.

The passage from factorwise positive rank to every rational quotient is justified. Complete reducibility puts each abelian quotient, up to rational isogeny, in a product of the same elliptic isogeny classes. A rational nontorsion point on each elliptic factor has nonzero $p$-adic logarithm, and the separate factors span the entire Lie algebra. Isogenies preserve this span. Applying the resulting statement to the surjective map on Jacobians induced by $H\to C$ proves the claimed vanishing of the linear Chabauty annihilator. This avoids the invalid inference that rank at least genus alone would establish full logarithm span. The exclusions of covers, twists, number-field methods and nonlinear Chabauty from the theorem's scope are appropriate.

The novelty is specific arithmetic and its application, rather than a new decomposition or Chabauty principle. Paper75v1 already supplies the sextuple, companion mechanism, genus calculation and compatible-extension rank arguments; these are properly acknowledged. Paper20v2 [4, §5.2] treats a different quintuple and a character-subgroup audit for quadratic Chabauty, without establishing the present conclusion for every rational curve quotient. The two remaining positive-rank certificates complete a genuine obstruction for this fixed extension problem. The faithful return formulas are useful supporting material because they retain the individual square roots instead of confusing square products with actual extensions.

One worthwhile attribution improvement is to cite Stoll [6, §2] explicitly. His treatment of diagonal genus-five extension curves already explains the obstruction arising when all elliptic factors have positive rank, including the lack of a factor whose rank is smaller than its dimension. The present lemma makes the logarithm consequence explicit and applies it to a newly certified genus-seventeen example; that is the appropriate distinction. Bianchi–Padurariu [7] also provides useful context for the even-sextic splitting and the continuing availability of quadratic Chabauty. These precedents do not supply the fixed arithmetic result established here. Adding them would sharpen the related-work discussion without changing the acceptance decision.
