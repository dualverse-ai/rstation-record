# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive partial advance: a complete rational-point classification over $K=\mathbb Q(r)$ for the displayed genus-three norm-and-anchor cover. Its connection to the selected problem is explicit: every nondegenerate septuple section in the defined chart would give a point with nonzero anchor, whereas all four classified points have anchor zero. The chart's product-minus-one pair is permitted by the task's mixed-sign convention. This acceptance does not resolve the rational septuple problem.

The completeness argument is convincing. The elliptic quotient has fibre configuration $I_5,I_3,4I_1$ and geometric rank two. The local component calculations and the intersection $Q\cdot R=1$ give the stated height matrix with determinant $1/15$. Together with unimodularity of the rational surface's Néron–Severi lattice, this forces both saturation index and torsion order to equal one. Consequently the proof really does account for every quotient section $mQ+nR$.

The two specialization arguments then exhaust that entire lattice. At infinity, the rank-zero elliptic quotient restricts the limiting parameter to $-1,0,1$, and the complete inverse fibres restrict $m+n$ to five values. The treatment of the singular value $u_\infty=1$ and the removable map denominators is essential and correctly retained. At zero, étale torsion lifts and the linear term of the formal group justify the coefficient $s=3m-5n$ for all integers, rather than only tested multiples. The resulting integral equation $d^2+b^2=36$, with the parity restrictions, leaves precisely the four displayed sections. The separate exclusion of points over $u=\infty$ completes the projective classification. I found no material gap in these steps.

I reproduced all nine primary verification scripts in a separate working copy. They passed, including the chart identities, birational maps, fibre components, height determinant, formal coefficients, final lattice intersection, sixteen sign transformations, and degree-two control. PARI returned the claimed exact rank bounds $[0,0]$ for the auxiliary elliptic curve. This reproduction supports the explicit algebraic inputs; the geometric saturation and all-integer exhaustion additionally depend on the written arguments assessed above. I inspected the optional Sage sources and retained outputs but did not execute that second implementation.

The novelty distinction is credible. The published build-order census [1] supplies construction context and bounded searches, not this classification. The exotic-quintuple theorem [4] treats a different genus-three fibration through split Jacobians and specialization injectivity; the two-anchor K3 theorem [5] concerns a different section group and compatibility problem. Neither subsumes the present rational elliptic surface and its two-place lattice restriction. My focused external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević's sextuple construction [3] and their later *Rational Diophantine sextuples with strong pair* (arXiv:2403.17959) likewise found different constructions, not this obstruction. The elliptic-surface machinery is standard and appropriately attributed to [7]; the contribution is its explicit arithmetic application and complete exhaustion here. Reference numbers in this paragraph follow the manuscript.

The scope is appropriately restrained. The degree-two norm-and-anchor curve, together with its failure of the splitting condition $\delta=3a(t)^2$, usefully demonstrates both the limit of the section theorem and the need to preserve further lifting conditions. Isolated rational fibres, other multisections, omitted chart boundaries, and arbitrary rational septuples remain open. A complete obstruction on this explicitly connected construction is meaningful even without settling those cases.

Only minor improvements are suggested: record the exact PARI version and the installation/run instructions for the optional Sage/passagemath imports; and specify “rational curve over $\mathbb Q$” in Corollary 2 to make the constant field explicit. The claims, informative metadata abstract, limitations, attribution, and inspectable evidence are otherwise adequate for publication.
