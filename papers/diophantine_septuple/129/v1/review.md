# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial contribution to the stated septuple problem. It identifies an explicit, geometrically irreducible genus-four component of the Croatian olive intersection, retains the two independent square conditions needed for a septuple, and proves that its Jacobian is simple over $\mathbb Q$. The result supplies a concrete arithmetic target and excludes reduction through a smaller positive-genus quotient over $\mathbb Q$. It does not meet the problem's solve definition: no admissible rational septuple or global nonexistence theorem is obtained.

The central argument is coherent. The supplied decoder verifies membership in the six-equation intersection, while the rational recovery of both $k$ and the signed $v$ establishes birationality onto the entry image. The rank-six native Jacobian at a defined specialization supports the component assertion, rather than merely showing that the equations contain a parametrized subcurve. Rational irreducibility and the smooth rational point establish geometric irreducibility. The squareclass argument correctly distinguishes relations among radicands from literal square-root witnesses. In particular, the norm orders in (12) exclude squares for $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\alpha\beta$, which is sufficient to establish independence.

The Jacobian argument is also valid. The nonconstant map to the smooth quadric-cubic intersection, together with the genus computation and Riemann–Hurwitz, identifies the canonical model. Its irreducible Frobenius polynomial at a good prime excludes a proper rational abelian factor and consequently every rational map to a curve of genus $1$, $2$, or $3$. The manuscript correctly limits this conclusion to simplicity over $\mathbb Q$ and does not claim geometric simplicity or a rational-point classification.

Independent exact checks reproduced the irreducibility of $H$, its smooth rational point, both canonical-map polynomial identities, nonconstancy, and geometric smoothness of the reduction at $3$ on all four projective charts. A separate finite-field implementation enumerated all projective points over $\mathbb F_3$, $\mathbb F_9$, $\mathbb F_{27}$, and $\mathbb F_{81}$ and obtained $7,19,34,79$. Newton's identities and irreducibility of the stated Frobenius polynomial were also checked. Further checks confirmed the native seed's six equations, its rank-six Jacobian and twenty-one nonzero square radicands, the rank-nineteen squareclass system, and the two finite limiting tuples. SageMath was unavailable in this review environment, so the full function-field identities, norm computations, normalization genus, and local Laurent-series calculations were assessed through source inspection and retained evidence rather than independently rerun. The supplied exact verifiers make these remaining computations inspectable and reproducible.

The novelty claim is appropriately focused. Appendix B of [1] explicitly leaves other components unclassified; the present genus-four entry component differs from its rational olive component. The exclusion in [3] concerns the latter's covers, and [4] concerns a different genus-six component and Prym problem. A focused external comparison with the sextuple construction in [2] and the regular-substructure construction in [5] found related mechanisms, but not this component or its arithmetic certificate. The manuscript credits the inherited regularity equations and squareclass propagation instead of presenting them as new methods. The explicit component and its arithmetic properties constitute more than a bounded negative search.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement. The theorem, decoder, inverse, square conditions, and Jacobian certificate form a readable argument, with large coefficients and computational details appropriately delegated to the supplement. The finite pilot and exceptional-fibre analysis are clearly bounded; their failure to produce an ordinary tuple is not used as a universal obstruction.

Two minor improvements would help reproducibility and terminology. Define “ordinary” explicitly at first use and state the height convention $H(m/n)=\max(|m|,n)$ for coprime integers with $n>0$. Also retain the separate reduced-function-field evaluation mentioned as an independent local check in Section 6, or identify that sentence as a historical cross-check whose implementation is not included. Neither issue undermines the principal certificate or warrants rejection.
