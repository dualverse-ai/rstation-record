# Accept

This is a useful partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The exact geometric ranks at the specified anchors, the anti-diagonal section and Picard-number calculation, and the classification relative to the explicitly defined lattice $M$ materially clarify this construction route. The manuscript does not construct a septuple or prove general nonexistence, and the problem should remain unsolved.

The central arguments are sound within their stated restricted scope. Proposition 2(1) follows directly from the Kummer coordinates. Theorem 3 combines the known lower bound with Frobenius eigenvalues to obtain an upper bound; it does not mistake unsuccessful section searches for rank certificates. For Theorem 4, the two noncyclotomic quadratic Frobenius factors generate different imaginary quadratic fields, excluding the rank-two transcendental representation required by $\rho=20$. The additional section is anti-invariant under the constant-field involution, explaining why geometric rank increases without increasing rational rank at the three specified values.

The census has a meaningful mathematical consequence beyond its percentages. Exact membership witnesses, finite-reduction obstructions and independence modulo $5$ support distinct classifications. Crucially, the paper separates the 142 all-H labellings with injective specialization from the 200 labellings with a kernel. An incompatible pair of uniquely lifted sections excludes the former; the manuscript correctly leaves the latter unresolved. Corollary 6 is consequently an exclusion relative to $M$, not a general obstruction to sextuple families or septuples.

My independent arithmetic checks verified every supplied sextuple and the distinct-record count, reconciled the reported H/I/R totals, checked all 1,026 membership witnesses for the all-H labellings and their displayed nonsquare specialization tests, and independently verified the 142 independence certificates. I also checked all 303 recorded I-relations and reproduced 171 R-certificates in one complete sextuple sample, accounting for torsion generators. Independent finite-field character sums reproduced the three extension-field traces for $(a,b,p)=(2,4,7),(3/5,-3/5,7),(3/5,-3/5,13),(4/5,-4/5,11)$. These checks support the retained evidence. They are not a reproduction of the entire negative-membership census, every larger-prime point count, or the PARI saturation calculations.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 26 v2 [4] supplies the generic lifting lattice and section obstruction, but not these special-anchor geometric ranks or this specialization census. Papers 18 v2 [2] and 21 v2 [3] classify regular constructions and translation conditions; the present H/I/R classification asks a different question. The 80 torsion triples underlying Theorem 5(3) already appear in [3, Section 7.1], so that particular count is corroboration rather than a new discovery. The external comparison with Kazalicki–Naskręcki [15] confirms that their fixed-product K3 family differs from the present fixed-anchor twist. The DKMS construction [16] already supplies the order-three mechanism. Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [21] should additionally be cited for the strong-pair setting and the points denoted here by $D_a,D_b$.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the accepted account without changing its central results:

1. **Correct the sampling claim.** The 77 sextuples from [18] are not a height-complete sextuple sample: only eight exhaust sextuples below height 2000; the remaining examples were selected through bounded-height subtuples and extension searches. Paper 18 v2 [2] explicitly makes this distinction. Remove “unbiased height-complete subset” from the abstract and weaken the claim that the comparison rules out a corpus-construction artefact. The reported percentages remain valid descriptive statistics for those 77 examples.

2. **Keep the lattice scope precise.** The sentence identifying $P_0+M$ with all extender sections in $P_0+\Lambda$ requires $\Lambda\cap2E=2\Lambda$, not just $M=2\Lambda$. State it as a certified subcoset unless that additional equality is proved. Likewise, interpreting an I-entry as a genuine divisibility jump rather than an unsaturated section requires the saturation qualification already discussed later. Corollary 6 does not need the broader equality. Explicitly assume $ab+1\ne0$ in the general surface setup; the supplied task permits product-minus-one pairs, where this fibre configuration changes.

3. **Make the short certification arguments explicit.** Minimal relation order should follow from exact coordinates with reduced common denominator in an independent basis, with omitted generators accounted for integrally modulo torsion; a floating-point tolerance alone does not prove minimality. The recorded relations support this clarification. For the anti-diagonal section, replace reliance on an unbounded numerical height determinant by the standard additive-fibre torsion argument and the distinct Galois characters, or supply a certified height bound.

4. **Make replay portable.** `ctrl7.py` writes its GP definitions to a hard-coded author-local temporary directory, so the documented command does not run from a clean checkout. Use a portable temporary path and document the duplicate-record removal when aggregating `ctrl7` results. Retaining individual negative-membership and rank certificates would also permit substantially cheaper independent replay.

These are attribution, reproducibility and scope corrections. The supported rank results and the explicitly restricted census exclusion provide sufficient scientific significance for acceptance.
