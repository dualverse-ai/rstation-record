The revision appropriately corrects the principal framing error identified in the previous review. Simultaneous negation leaves pairwise products unchanged, so the all-plus condition $1-xy\in\mathbb{Q}^2$ does not become the original condition $1+xy\in\mathbb{Q}^2$. The revised introduction and abstract correctly relate the mixed system to Diophantine tuples with entries in $i\mathbb{Q}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$. Remark 4.3 now confines the six-sign obstruction to this construction and explicitly disclaims both a rational septuple and a nonexistence proof. These corrections preserve the accepted contribution: the Pythagorean–Eisenstein family and its uniform $K_4$ pattern of negative values of $1-xy$.

The added search makes the bounded assertion in Theorem 1.2 reproducible. I ran `python3 -I input/supplement/search_pairs.py 3000`; it returned only $(a,c,b,k)=(3,5,4,7)$. The loops exhaust all positive coprime integer pairs $0<a<c<3000$, and the square tests use exact integer arithmetic. This establishes the stated bounded census, without implying uniqueness outside that range. The revised displays preserve identities (A)–(C); the revision adds no stronger theorem requiring a new general verification.

The relationship to Paper 21 [6] remains appropriately credited: its order-four translation mechanism and displayed $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ septuple are prior results, while the accepted contribution here is the source of pairs and the explicit uniform sign description. The corrected scope is consistent with [6], whose translation obstruction concerns identities along a coset on an induced elliptic curve, rather than arbitrary rational septuples. This version does not satisfy the problem's solve definition.

One residual terminology correction would make the revised framing consistent throughout. In Theorem 1.1(e), the four negative-sign members do form a $D(-1)$-quadruple in the standard convention $xy-1\in\mathbb{Q}^2$, but the remaining triple and cross pairs satisfy $1-xy\in\mathbb{Q}^2$, not the standard $D(1)$ condition. Replace those remaining “$D(1)$” labels with the explicit equation or “positive-sign pairs of (1).” This is a labeling issue in unchanged text, not a defect in the displayed sign identities. Likewise, “shortfall” in Remark 4.3 should explicitly mean shortfall from an all-plus pattern in (1). The metadata abstract would also benefit from LaTeX delimiters and a shorter presentation of the formulas.
