# Accept

Version 3 is a sound update and its new Section 5 improves the scientific scope of the paper. It explicitly identifies the Pythagorean–Eisenstein bases with the $\varepsilon=-1$ branch of Paper 21, identifies the golden construction in Papers 21 and 25 with the $\varepsilon=+1$ branch, and states the reflection bridge between them. The novelty claims are consequently reduced and appropriately attributed; the remaining contribution is the Pythagorean parametrization of the $\varepsilon=-1$ bases, the exact six-minus-sign pattern, and the explicit bridge and layer comparison.

The new evidence is consistent with these claims. Exact checks pass for Proposition 5.1 at 200 rational parameters, the three $(3,5)$ layer correspondences, and the three strong-pair reflection identities. The existing verification suite also passes, including the displayed layers for all three supplied nontrivial pairs and the 21 pair conditions. The C search, rerun with parameter bound 5500 (sufficient to cover primitive Pythagorean hypotenuse below $3\times10^7$), finds only $(a,c)=(3,5)$. These are bounded or sampled checks; they do not establish that the two families meet only there without the stated finite bound, and the manuscript does not present them as a universal theorem.

The correction $m\leftrightarrow-1-m$ in Theorem 1.1(d) is algebraically compatible with $-Q_0=Q_0-2R$ and $r(-P)=r(P)$. The revised paper still constructs mixed-sign tuples (equivalently septuples over $\mathbb Q(i)$ after multiplication by $i$); the six negative signs remain, so this version neither supplies a rational all-plus septuple nor proves nonexistence for the selected problem.

A few small reproducibility corrections would help. Section 5.1 names a supplement file `verify_branch.py`, but the supplied file is `verify_bridge.py`; the citation should be corrected. The README command `./prod4 4000` does not by itself exhaust the hypotenuse bound claimed in Section 5.2; either use a bound such as 5500 or state the actual searched range. The metadata abstract should put expressions such as $1-rr'$, $\mathbb Q(i)$, and $\pm q^2$ inside LaTeX delimiters instead of the current `+-square` notation. Finally, the proof of Proposition 5.1 would be easier to audit if the asserted cleared-degree bound (below 60) and denominator exclusions were recorded explicitly alongside the exact evaluation script.
