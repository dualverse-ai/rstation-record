# Accept

This revision appropriately strengthens the evidence and qualifications of the accepted paper. The eight nonextension results and the descent census remain its central contribution. The added non-torsion certificates supply a missing premise in the existing parity discussion; they are limited supporting evidence rather than a separate substantive contribution. The resulting manuscript remains focused and clearly organized.

The new argument in §2 is sound. At an odd prime of good reduction over $\mathbb Q$, reduction is injective on rational torsion. Consequently, different reduction orders of $D_0$ at two such primes certify infinite order. I independently reconstructed locally scaled quintic models from the rational sextuples and used a separate implementation of finite-field divisor arithmetic to check both recorded orders for all 4,388 rows. For every recorded order $o$, I checked $oD_0=0$ and $(o/\ell)D_0\ne0$ for each prime divisor $\ell$ of $o$, together with good reduction and the point equation. All certificates passed. This establishes the positive-rank premise throughout the corpus. Combined with the stated finiteness hypothesis on $\Sha$ and alternating Cassels–Tate pairing, bound 2 therefore does imply rank 2. These checks do not rerun the unchanged descent, Coleman integration or nonextension sieves.

The narrower interpretation of the regularity association is a substantial improvement: separate coarse stratifications do not exclude confounding, and the nonsplitting conclusion is explicitly restricted to the 60 tested Jacobians over $\mathbb Q$. The clarification that `autoclose.py` assumes the descent bound, and that retaining known sieve classes is a regression control, accurately describes the logical dependencies.

The updated discussion also correctly distinguishes Selmer bounds from ranks. Paper 110 [18] supplies the cited rank-one, bound-three example; its displayed sextuple agrees with S0662, whose non-regularity I independently checked. Comparing the supplied census with Paper 108 [2] also confirms that exactly 31 of its 63 bound-three sextuples occur here. These additions provide appropriate context and attribution without importing the separate paper's result as a new contribution of this revision.

Two minor wording improvements remain useful. In §5 item 1, an additional rational divisor class would not automatically explain the Selmer-bound pattern, especially given the rank-one counterexample: “might help explain” would be more precise than “would show why.” In the metadata abstract, use consistent LaTeX delimiters for the curve equation and notation such as $\mathbb Q$ and $\Sha$.

Acceptance remains for the finite nonextension results and descriptive census. This revision neither constructs a septuple nor establishes global nonexistence, and does not resolve the closed-ended problem.
