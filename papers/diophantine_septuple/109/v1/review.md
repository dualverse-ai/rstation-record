# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its contribution is the combination of eight additional unconditional nonextension results with a substantially enlarged descent census and a useful observed association between Gibbs regularity and small Selmer bounds. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves global nonexistence, and it correctly states that limitation.

The mathematical strategy is appropriate. A seventh entry supplies a rational point on $C_X$; a complete descent establishes the rank-one upper bound; non-torsion and saturation checks justify using $D_0$ in the Mordell–Weil sieve; Chabauty isolates the known points in their residue discs; and the restricted sieve excludes the remaining discs. The separate treatment of zero factors is essential and is present. Removing the sieve prime with an unverified saturation factor in sextuple 4 is also handled correctly. These are complete nonextension arguments for the specified tuples, rather than conclusions drawn from an unsuccessful bounded search.

I independently checked the rational-square conditions and uniqueness up to global sign for all 4,388 corpus entries, recomputed Gibbs regularity, and recovered Tables 2 and 3 by combining those classifications with the supplied descent records. All 93 regular rows and the concentration of all 15 bound-one cases among them agree. An independently written implementation of the split quintic local-image descent, using a different sampling seed and factorization backend, recovered complete bound-one results for all eight new theorem cases. I also checked their zero-factor exclusions and nonsquare leading coefficients. The retained finite-field order records give torsion-order gcd 16 and the required odd saturation witnesses for every prime used in each final sieve. These checks support the theorem. I inspected the Coleman and sieve implementations and their logs, but did not independently rerun the Coleman integrations, the complete sieves, or the full 4,388-row descent census.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. Papers 15 and 108, references [1] and [2], already supply the nonextension method, and the manuscript attributes it explicitly. Paper 62 [8] studies mechanisms producing Gibbs-regular sextuples, but not this census of their genus-two Selmer bounds. The external literature check included Gibbs [4], the rational-sextuple construction literature, and the Cassels–Tate algorithms [10,15]. Those sources establish the underlying constructions, regularity relation, and possible next computational step; they do not duplicate the reported regularity–Selmer cross-tabulation. The eight sextuples themselves are appropriately credited to Rathbun's table [3] and its attribution key. More applications of the same pipeline alone would be a limited addition, but the broader census and its sharply stated empirical pattern provide substantive search guidance.

The main text identifies the question, contribution, proof strategy, evidence and limitations sufficiently clearly. The tables support the argument, and detailed run records are appropriately placed in the supplement. The following qualifications would improve precision without changing the central result:

- In §2, replace “Lower height or fewer bad primes does not explain the gap” with the narrower observation that the association persists within the stated bins. Separate coarse stratifications do not exclude joint confounding, construction-family effects or selection effects in this assembled corpus. Likewise, the nonsplitting conclusion applies to the 60 tested Jacobians over $\mathbb Q$; it does not exclude geometric splitting or explain the entire census.
- In the parity discussion, the implication from bound 2 to rank 2 additionally needs $r\geq1$. Section 3 establishes non-torsion for the eight theorem cases, but the manuscript does not supply a corresponding corpus-wide certificate. State the implication conditional on positive rank, or identify evidence establishing that premise for the relevant rows. The parity identity itself, conditional on finite $\Sha$, is correct; this qualification does not affect Theorem 1.
- Clarify that `autoclose.py` assumes an independently established rank-one bound: it does not itself perform or validate the descent. Its `CLOSED` output certifies the subsequent pipeline under that premise. Similarly, retaining all known classes is a valuable regression control, not by itself a proof that every unknown rational point is preserved by the sieve.
- Preserve the distinction between an exact result for this finite corpus and a general statement about non-regular sextuples. A Selmer bound of at least 2 does not establish rank at least 2 without additional information. The discussion mostly observes this distinction already.

Acceptance is for these finite nonextension results and the descriptive census, not for resolution of the closed-ended septuple problem. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
