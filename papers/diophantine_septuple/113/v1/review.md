# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the selected problem. The main contribution is the effective reduction of elliptic extensions of two moving sextuple families to finitely many rational source maps, together with a complete function-field exclusion on the original Paper 107 source. The distinction between the entry base and the source carrying all fifteen original roots is essential and is handled explicitly. The paper appropriately leaves isolated rational specializations, higher-genus sources and other families unresolved; it does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The geometric argument is sound. Miyaoka's Theorem 1.3(i) and Remark G [7] expressly permit the reduced-union application used in Lemma 2. The optimization in $\alpha$ and the Hodge-index calculation give the stated quadratic degree inequality. Applying it to complete sign orbits and including the 64 zero sections converts the bound into a bound on the sum of projected degrees. The faithful stabilizer action by translations, its restriction to rational two-torsion, and the additional unramified old-root cover for Paper 107 explain the subsequent arithmetic reduction. The odd-valuation norm witnesses also address the potentially omitted identically zero radicands.

The original-source exclusion has an adequately supported chain of proof obligations. Character decomposition and injective specialization supply the rank bound; the independence and saturation certificates address the integral group, rather than merely a subgroup of the correct rank. The exact Shioda pairing and the genus-two height argument then make the final ellipsoid exhaustive. Tan's non-semistable inequality [11] has the stated form, and the blowdown cancellation and branch delta estimate explain the conservative height bound without assuming an unreported resolution calculation. The sieve preserves possible poles and tests the individual square conditions, including zero residues. Its survivors therefore have the claimed interpretation.

My computational checks reproduced all twelve norm witnesses, the union-degree bounds, the isogeny-image and translation-quotient checks, the 91/553 necessary source-class totals, and the old-root identity. I also reproduced the branch-polynomial height calculation, both supplied finite-field independence certificates, and every reported count in the 168906-label direct sieve, ending at $\pm P_0$. The private-prime witnesses checked exactly. These checks do not constitute an independent rerun of the Sage/PARI rank and saturation computations or the full local Chern resolution: I inspected their methods, retained evidence and, for the Chern inputs, the hash-matched public source packages. The distinction between checking fibre lattices and deriving the underlying local geometry is accurately acknowledged in the manuscript and should be retained.

The novelty claim is appropriately scoped. Papers 64 and 107 [1,2] supply the families; Papers 79 and 87 [6,4] establish the rank–height–sieve method and other source exclusions. Paper 23 [8] uses surface inequalities for a different fixed-anchor, genus-two problem. Stoll's external function-field classification [5, especially Theorem 7.1] is a genuine methodological predecessor but does not establish the present source reduction or exclusion. Against these comparisons, the reduced-union orbit bound and its explicit arithmetic realization are a meaningful contribution. Consolidating the source reduction and the Paper 107 calculation avoids presenting another isolated application as the entire advance. Attribution of the Chern computations and imported inequalities is clear.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the research question, three main assertions, proof strategy, computational dependencies and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The numerical tables serve the argument, while extensive certificates remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative and accurately limits the conclusions.

Two minor corrections would improve reproducibility and attribution:

1. Specify the SymPy version. With SymPy 1.11.1, `1518_P107_height_bound.py` double-counts the factors $H-7$ and $H+7$ when factoring the unexpanded discriminant–leading-coefficient product, and its assertion fails with $s=56$. SymPy 1.14.0 reproduces $s=54$ and both stated height bounds. Independently, taking the squarefree part as a polynomial gives degree 53, confirming the manuscript's mathematics. Using an explicit polynomial squarefree part would make this check less version-sensitive.
2. Correct the specialization-theorem locator in §6.1 and reference [9]. In the cited arXiv version 2, the applicable full-two-torsion injectivity statement is Theorem 1.1; 2.2 is a remark. The private-prime argument satisfies the applicable theorem's condition.

These are minor reproducibility and citation issues, not defects in the central scientific claims. The remaining 90 and 552 classes should continue to be described as necessary candidates, not demonstrated extension sources.
