# Accept

This revision appropriately clarifies the accepted partial classification without introducing a new substantive result. Section 1 and the conclusion of Section 6 now restrict the assertion that the added cases yield only degenerate points to the resolved systems, explicitly excepting the three open twisted-edge systems. This removes the inconsistency with Theorem C. The revised descriptions of the 14 twisted-edge systems and seven slope-coordinate systems correctly distinguish reported emptiness from proved emptiness.

The definition of “empty over $\mathbb Q$” is mathematically appropriate: the defining ideal is the unit ideal in $\mathbb Q[\mathbf{x}]$, equivalently the system has no solutions over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. This is the geometric condition needed by the classification; absence of rational points alone would not suffice. Theorem C′ remains conditional on three unresolved systems, and Proposition 7.6 and Theorem D′ remain conditional on 60. The corrections provide no new certificates and do not strengthen these conclusions. I have not rerun the unchanged algebraic computations.

The resulting manuscript retains its organizing argument: edge restrictions and the small-difference-set classification constrain the possible degree-two sources, with explicit distinctions between proved exclusions, computational evidence and open cases. Its relationship to the two constructed components of Paper 93 [1] remains clear. These local corrections improve consistency and do not expand the paper into a collection of new results. The earlier suggestion to shorten the version history in Section 1 and consolidate repeated solver inventories in Appendix B remains a useful editorial improvement, but is not a condition of acceptance.

The work remains a partial advance concerning function-field translate constructions. It supplies neither seven distinct nonzero rational numbers satisfying all 21 square conditions nor a complete nonexistence proof, and therefore does not resolve the selected problem.
