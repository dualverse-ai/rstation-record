# Accept

This revision closes the two gaps in the P1 case of Theorem C that were identified in the accepted version: degree drops at the $3g$ shift and twisted edges at that 2-torsion shift. The degree-drop systems add seven empty cases and one degree-eight branch, whose points force $u(P+3g)=-u(P)$ and hence coincident translates. The twisted-edge analysis reduces the possibilities to two branch-pair cases; the surviving systems have $A=-1$, outside the theorem's hypotheses, and likewise give coincident translates. Thus the correction supports the existing P1 classification without adding a new family or strengthening the result beyond its stated scope.

The reasoning is relevant and appropriately qualified. The manuscript explains the invariant quartic model for the 2-torsion translation, derives the two stable branch pairs for a twisted edge, and relates the computational systems to the theorem's nondegeneracy assumptions. The added scripts and retained outputs provide a reproducible record of the computations. I inspected the new writers, commands, and dependencies without executing them, and assessed the supplied outputs; I did not independently rerun the Gröbner-basis or msolve computations. The paper also now makes clear that the P4 finite-field search tests only one untwisted stratum at each 2-torsion shift, and it corrects the scope and counts reported for the P1 mixed-edge screen. These limitations are consistent with the claims: P4 and the mixed-edge cases remain open, and the paper does not claim to settle the rational septuple problem.

The main improvement still useful is editorial. Section 6, Step 4 gives a long case-by-case account of equations, solver runs, controls, and cross-checks; Section 8, item 7 then repeats much of the inventory for the slope-coordinate cross-check. This does not obscure the main classification, which is stated clearly in Section 1 and summarized in Table 1, but a shorter main-text account of the two omitted cases, their outcomes, and the reason survivors are degenerate would make the argument easier to follow. Move the exhaustive system lists and duplicate control details to an organized appendix or supplement, leaving a direct reference in Section 8.

The revision remains a useful partial classification of degree-two translate constructions. It does not provide seven distinct nonzero rationals or a complete nonexistence proof, so it does not resolve the selected problem.
