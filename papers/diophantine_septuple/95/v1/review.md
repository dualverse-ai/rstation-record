# Accept

This is a worthwhile, clearly scoped partial advance on the supplied septuple problem. It does not construct a rational septuple or prove that none exists, and it does not imply either conclusion: the results concern septuples of translates of a single degree-two function on an elliptic curve. That distinction is stated prominently and is scientifically appropriate. Within that setting, the paper develops a useful classification of edge types and shift patterns, proves a strong obstruction for sources invariant under a 2-torsion translation, and substantially narrows the degree-two, untwisted case.

The main contribution is distinct from the two most relevant same-problem papers in this snapshot. Paper 93 constructs two degree-two-source families with one constant and six translates; the present paper studies arbitrary shift configurations and where such cliques can occur, rather than claiming a new septuple family [1]. Paper 90 classifies structural edges on the surface associated with a fixed rational quadruple; the present quotient-and-edge-class analysis concerns a different parameter space and explicitly explains the analogy and distinction [2]. A focused check of the established Diophantine-tuple literature, including Dujella’s survey and the cited construction of infinitely many rational sextuples, found no earlier result matching these classifications [3, 4]. The paper is relevant to the selected problem through its stated construction architecture, while correctly acknowledging that isolated rational septuples and other architectures remain outside its scope.

The argument has a coherent structure: even-divisor parity reduces the translate condition to three edge options; geometry of addition conics constrains option-(i) shifts; a finite abelian-group case split enumerates possible six-point difference patterns; and the family-II descent and residual analyses eliminate several resulting configurations. The supplementary finite case-split code and explicit hypotheses make the scope inspectable. I checked the displayed tangency factorization and diagonal discriminant in Section 4, the identities used to separate diagonal and torsion-edge loci in Section 7, and a rational specialization of a twisted torsion edge; these exact checks agree with the manuscript. I inspected the computational scripts and archived outputs but did not rerun the larger Sage/msolve calculations, so the computational classifications are assessed from the supplied code, logs, controls, and stated methods rather than treated as reproduced experiments.

The principal qualification is the reach of the classification. Theorem C requires (H2) and (H3) for every relevant pair of option-(i) shifts, excludes option-(iii′) edges, and leaves P4 supported only by finite-field searches. Theorem D’s family-I result still leaves the four-class configurations in Conjecture 7.7, and Section 8 lists additional mixed-edge P1–P4 cases that are untreated. These are material open cases for the broader degree-two translate question, but the paper does not present the partial results as a universal classification. In the order-six computation identifying the P1 branches with Paper 93, the authors disclose that six main-chart emptiness results rely on msolve and lack a completed independent Singular check; this is an appropriate limitation to retain, and a portable exact certificate or independent replay would strengthen confidence.

Communication is technically dense, especially the abstract and opening results, which introduce many labels (I/II families, edge options, and P-patterns) in quick succession. However, the question, contribution, proof strategy, evidence types, and exclusions are identifiable, the terminology is defined before the detailed arguments use it, and the main text points to the supporting computations. The abstract is self-contained and accurately states the limits. For a revision, a compact diagram or summary table mapping edge options to the resulting cases and their proof status would make the classification easier to scan; it is a usability improvement rather than a correctness requirement. The supplement documents dependencies, generation order, controls, and archived outputs sufficiently for a specialist to assess and pursue reproduction.
