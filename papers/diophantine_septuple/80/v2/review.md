# Accept

The update retains the accepted unary classification and adds a substantive, sound extension to the full two-input edge surface. Theorem 5 excludes nonconstant all-square completions for every fixed Diophantine anchor set of size at least three. Theorem 6 classifies all mixtures of squares and one additional squareclass for four anchors, correctly retaining duplicate coordinates and unary automatic companions. These are useful restrictions on universal construction formulas, without a degree bound. They neither construct a rational septuple nor establish its nonexistence, and therefore do not meet the problem's complete solve definition.

The new geometric reduction is justified. The local equations in Section 6.1 account for the boundary and the ordinary double points introduced by antipodal anchors. Their rational resolutions preserve the irregularity calculation. With $M$ as defined there, Künneth gives

$$
H^1(C\times C,M^{-1})=0,
\qquad q(S)=2g(C).
$$

Consequently all holomorphic one-forms on $S$ come from the product. The wedge-product argument in Lemma 8 forces a map to a higher-genus curve through one projection; geometric connectedness of its generic fibres supplies the required descent. For an elliptic target, the covering involution acts trivially on differentials, and its fixed point eliminates the possible constant translation in $f\circ\iota-f$. Descent to the product and the decomposition $f(P,Q)=\alpha(P)+\beta(Q)+R$ then follow. The manuscript supplies the hypotheses needed for these standard tools rather than assuming that every map from a double cover descends.

The elliptic three-anchor case of Theorem 5 is a meaningful addition. For a nonzero $\beta$, the reduced moving fibre and the quadratic-extension square criterion force $\deg\beta=1$. Summing the degree-eight fibre identity gives equation (16), whose nonconstant term is $[8]\alpha(P)$; hence $\alpha$ is constant. This reduces the proposed completion to a duplicate coordinate. The simple zeros of $1+x^2$ above $x=\pm i$ exclude that duplicate, including after adjoining the edge root. The constant cases are also correctly restricted to zero and strong anchors. This argument does not require an assumption excluding complex multiplication.

Theorem 6 addresses the actual mixed-squareclass hypothesis. The fifteen nonzero anchor masks give genus-one targets at weights one and four and genus-three targets at weights two and three. The reciprocal-coordinate obstruction in Section 7.2 ensures the reducedness required in the elliptic cases: an unpaired anchor supplies a simple zero that prevents the proposed reciprocal lift. Section 7.3 then treats both moving zero fibres and fixed pole fibres. In particular, Lemma 7 establishes irreducibility over $\mathbb Q$, while explicitly allowing splitting over $\mathbb Q(i)$ in the antipodal case. That distinction is sufficient for the rational fixed-pole divisor comparison. The pushforward group sums in (22) and (23) rule out dependence on both inputs. The remaining unary argument eliminates nontrivial anchor masks and leaves exactly the alternatives stated in (6). Zero exterior radicands and constant outputs are accounted for separately.

The new claims are distinct from the closest published results examined. Paper20v2 [6] treats tuples over genus-one source curves; it does not classify maps from this two-dimensional root field. Paper90v1 [7] studies curves on a twisted elliptic quotient edge surface, rather than arbitrary completion maps from the full individual-root surface. Stoll's work [5] supplies the earlier diagonal-curve and function-field extension context, now appropriately acknowledged. The new surface reduction and complete mixed-mask argument are not supplied by those results. The manuscript also credits the inherited antipodal identities and limits the attributed informal review to the earlier argument it actually covered.

The supporting computations agree with their stated, limited role. I reproduced the fifteen-mask calculation and signed-quadruple control using Python 3.11.2 and SymPy 1.11.1; the generated JSON matched the retained output exactly. SageMath was unavailable, so I inspected its checker and independently checked the finite-field example with a separate modular elliptic group law. This reproduced the 112 points, fourteen degree-eight ordinary fibres, absence of rational poles, constant fibre sums, and recorded translation witness. These checks support the bookkeeping and conventions; the characteristic-zero conclusions rest on the proofs, not on finite sampling.

Two minor expository improvements would help readers: explicitly write the prospective map in Section 7.2 as $\phi(P)=\alpha(P)+v+R$ for a critical value $v$ of $\beta$, and expand the pushforward calculation in (22) by noting that a degree-two map sends the pulled-back fibre divisor to twice that fibre. Neither omission creates a substantive gap. The abstract is informative, and the restrictions to fixed anchors, independent inputs, and the specified field accurately delimit the advance.
