# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial contribution to the selected problem. It gives an exact recognition criterion for a specified sixteen-edge source, determines its matching arithmetic at an inhabited rational parameter, and proves substantive restrictions on families within that source. These results clarify a concrete route toward a septuple. They neither construct a rational septuple nor prove general nonexistence, and the manuscript consistently preserves that distinction.

The recognition argument retains the individual adjacent roots and all three additional square lifts. The quadratic-interpolation calculation gives uniqueness of a consistent fit, while the positive-closure argument supplies the missing necessity of the order-five normalization. The treatment of singular correspondences is substantive: a distinct rational five-cycle would force an order-five rational Möbius transformation on a genus-zero normalization, which is impossible. The subsequent division-polynomial calculation identifies the actual translation and its normalization condition; it is not merely a check that a convenient point lies on an elliptic curve.

The strongest arithmetic contribution is Theorem 7. The two rank-one specializations do not themselves prove generic rank zero. The paper correctly supplies the additional argument: finite support for both descent images, injective evaluation on those supports, complete specialized images, and explicit generic torsion witnesses. The halving checks for every torsion translate justify the required completeness independently of a bounded saturation call. Together these establish the claimed rank zero over $K=\mathbb Q(k,\sqrt{p_5(k)+1})$; torsion specialization and the recovery maps then identify only boundary anchors. Proposition 8 supplies a genuine degree-two extension carrying all sixteen roots, making the minimal-extension-degree conclusion useful and precise. Its genus-four base and known rational specialization do not imply infinitely many rational specializations.

The fixed-fiber group calculation also handles the rational-lifting issue correctly. Common translation-cover classes cannot be discarded simply because the normalization is isogenous to a product. The complete descent images, together with the order-four physical translation, support both $\mathcal S(\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Z^3\oplus C_{20}\oplus C_2$ and the existence of an untwisted representative for each physical tuple at the specified fiber. For Theorem 9, uniform nonisogeny excludes simultaneous elliptic motion, and the separate pole calculations exclude the remaining constant-factor curves. The argument includes genus-one curves without rational points and correctly restricts the conclusion to geometrically integral curves defined over $\mathbb Q$ in the sufficient $R_5=0$ divisor.

The novelty is adequate after separating inherited material. rStation paper 43, version 2 [1], already contains the forward construction, rational-function rigidity, matching-surface description, and nonisogeny at the displayed fiber; these should not be counted again as new contributions. The manuscript acknowledges that overlap and its attribution. The inverse normalization theorem, complete matching arithmetic, generic anchor obstruction, and uniform completion-divisor exclusion go materially beyond it. The Kummer results in [3] concern a different surface and splitting condition. Inspection of Lasić’s circular-tuple parametrization in [2] confirms that it does not supply the anchored membership criterion here. The external specialization theorem [6] and cyclic-isogeny classification recorded in [5] have the hypotheses and conclusions needed by the proofs.

Targeted exact replays passed for recognition, normalization, fixed-fiber descent images, generic specialization and halving checks, the surface and signed deck identities, nonisogeny identities, completion poles, and the quadratic-extension root construction. The two recognition controls have exactly sixteen square pairs, as reported. The saved arithmetic records agree with their supplied programs and derived checks. I did not independently rerun PARI’s rank descents or number-field certifications; those remain inspectable, reproducible computational inputs rather than freshly reproduced results of this review.

Only minor clarification would improve presentation: a compact theorem-dependency table would make the distinction between symbolic identities, retained rank computations, and external classification theorems easier to follow. The informative abstract and explicit scope statements are appropriate. Acceptance does not mark the original septuple problem solved.
