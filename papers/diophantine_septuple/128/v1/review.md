# Accept

This is a substantive, clearly explained partial resolution of the selected problem. The manuscript determines the rational points on the genus-$15$ covering curve attached to Paper 125's almost-septuple family and thereby excludes septuples throughout that family, without a height restriction. It neither constructs a rational septuple nor proves their global nonexistence, and appropriately states that limitation. Excluding this infinite family is meaningfully stronger than a bounded search.

The central argument is coherent. The even-degree descent accounts for the kernel of the $x-T$ map and rational two-torsion, giving the genus-$8$ Jacobian a rank bound of five. The supplied divisor classes attain that rank. Consequently their logarithms determine the full annihilating space needed for Chabauty; saturation at $137$ is not required. The nonvanishing differential in each of the four relevant residue discs gives the stated uniqueness bound. The elliptic Mordell–Weil sieve then puts every possible lift into those discs. Finally, the four quotient points lift to precisely eight rational points on $D$, all exceptional for the tuple construction. In particular, the proof does not mistake the sieve's large modulus for a global height bound or a completeness theorem by itself.

I checked the proof of Lemma 2 and the residue-disc argument, inspected the computational implementations, and independently verified several decisive calculations:

- Exact arithmetic reproduces the displayed discriminant and resultant factorizations. At $k=4/3$, the positive square roots of $f(k)$ and $\Phi(k)$ are $40/9$ and $204800/2187$.
- Independent linear algebra on the retained local-image and localization matrices reproduces every local dimension in Tables 1–2, both fake Selmer dimensions of five, and the asserted spans of the supplied global divisor images.
- Independent reduction of the retained Coleman logarithms reproduces rank five and the three-dimensional annihilator at both precisions for $137$ and at $1069$, including the displayed nonzero differential evaluations.
- An independent finite-field implementation, using only primes below $10^4$, already reproduces the same four surviving classes modulo the manuscript's full modulus $M$. It also verifies the orders $76$ and $68$ of the reduced elliptic generator at $137$ and $1069$.

These checks support the computational argument but do not constitute independent recomputation of the number-field certification, local-image sampling, or Coleman integrals. Those steps remain supported by the supplied programs and retained outputs. I did not rerun the expensive integrations. The second-prime calculation is useful corroboration, although it shares the integration implementation and global rank input with the first.

The principal contribution is new relative to the relevant supplied papers. Paper 125 [1] constructs the family and proves finiteness, while explicitly leaving the determination of $D(\mathbb Q)$ open. Papers 111 [11] and 127 [12] concern different curves and do not establish this exclusion. The cited forum sieve [2] is credited and is not presented as the new result. The focused external comparison with Bruin–Stoll [7] and Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević's *Rational Diophantine sextuples containing two regular quadruples and one regular quintuple* (arXiv:1904.00348) did not identify a prior determination of this curve's rational points. The novelty lies in completing this particular arithmetic problem, rather than in introducing descent or Chabauty.

One attribution clarification is warranted. Under the manuscript's rational-infinity hypothesis, Lemma 2 is an equivalent reformulation of the odd-degree symmetric-class criterion attributed to Poonen–Schaefer [5, Theorem 11.3] and quoted in Bruin–Stoll [7, Proposition 3.3]. Indeed, if $2A=D_\infty$, then $L=A+[\infty^-]$ is a degree-one class with $\iota^*L=L$, and conversely. An odd-degree symmetric class can be reduced to degree one by subtracting a multiple of $\mathfrak h$. Thus “a different statement” in Sections 2 and 4.2 should be clarified as a different formulation of the same criterion in this setting. The self-contained proof remains useful; this correction does not undermine the new family-exclusion theorem.

Clarity is good independently of correctness: the research question, three-step proof strategy, decisive computations, and scope are identifiable in the main text, while extensive records are confined to the supplement. Two small reproducibility improvements would strengthen the presentation. Document explicitly how to force fresh Coleman runs, since the scripts otherwise load supplied checkpoints. Also retain the defining divisors and precision bounds alongside the compressed local-image basis vectors, so those vectors can be checked without regenerating the full sampling runs. These are improvements to an already inspectable computational submission, not reasons to reject it.
