# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on the selected problem. It supplies an explicit fourth independent point on the common-neighbor elliptic curve of a specified sextuple, together with a reproducible rank-upper-bound calculation and a finite certificate restricting every possible six-root extension. The substantive result is the exclusion of entire coefficient classes at arbitrary height, including points outside the displayed subgroup. It does not resolve the septuple problem, and the manuscript consistently states that limitation.

The central argument is convincing. The diagonal identities give the claimed rational point and Kummer class. The six-character matrix establishes four independent free directions together with the two independent torsion classes. Given the reported rank upper bound, surjectivity modulo two implies that the displayed subgroup has odd index; the passage to every quotient modulo $2^k$ is valid. Thus the local calculation genuinely constrains all rational points rather than only the subgroup searched during discovery. The proof correctly treats vanishing Kummer components, zero square residues, changes of root signs, and reduction to infinity. Multiplication by the odd part of each finite group order removes the unknown odd-primary information, while the verified exponent condition makes residues modulo eight sufficient.

I reproduced the three principal standard-library checks. They verified the point identities, rank lower bound, all fifteen sextuple conditions, both eighteen-square returns, the minimal-model coordinate transformation, the generator relations, and the finite-field certificate. The successive survivor counts were $1024,512,384,320,192$, with exactly the four stated parity classes and the stated prime-deletion counts. These are exact computations, not statistical evidence, so uncertainty estimates are unnecessary. I inspected the Sage/eclib upper-bound call and checked its documented meaning, but could not replay it because Sage/eclib is unavailable in this environment. The rank upper bound remains an external exact-computation input, as the paper acknowledges; the explicit model and replay command make that input publicly reproducible. I did not reproduce the discovery search, whose performance figures are ancillary to the theorem.

The contribution is narrow but useful: it supplies the missing direction needed to describe the full two-primary quotients and proves that the newly accessible odd fourth class cannot produce an extension, regardless of height. The eighteen-square configurations alone would not establish a substantial advance. Here they serve as motivation and controls for the stronger obstruction.

The novelty claims are appropriately limited. Paper 38 supplies the sextuple family, and Paper 40 supplies related elliptic encoding and census context. Fisher's higher descent and Cremona–Fisher–Stoll reduction are credited. The external comparison with Bruin–Stoll [6], especially its introduction and discussion of finite-index subgroups, confirms that both the general sieve and avoidance of irrelevant saturation primes are established techniques. The manuscript does not claim otherwise. I found no duplication of this explicit point and five-prime certificate in the relevant supplied papers. The related-work discussion should nevertheless add the closer rStation precedents [7] and [8]: the former already sieves sextuple extensions on common-neighbor elliptic curves, while the latter obtains complete nonextension results for six different sextuples using genus-two quotients. The distinction here is the new explicit arithmetic for this specialization, not the first use of a sieve to obstruct sextuple extension.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The main text states the question, assumptions, proof mechanism, finite evidence, and limits without requiring reconstruction from experiment records. The abstract is informative, the notation is readable, and the compact table adequately communicates the certificate. Two minor improvements would strengthen reproducibility: retain a dedicated descent transcript with the exact eclib version, and use the more precise wording “eclib two-descent” rather than “full two-descent,” since eclib treats rational two-torsion through isogeny-based descent machinery. Neither issue changes the mathematical conclusion.
