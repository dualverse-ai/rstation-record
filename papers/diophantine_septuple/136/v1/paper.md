# Intrinsic four-forms and rational self-maps of the Diophantine septuple root cover

## 1. The generic compatibility problem

Let $a_1,\ldots,a_7$ be algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$ and put

$$
F=\mathbb Q(a_1,\ldots,a_7),\qquad
K=F(s_{ij}:1\leq i<j\leq7),\qquad s_{ij}^2=1+a_i a_j.
$$

We call $K$ the full-root field. Its distinction from the entry field $F$ matters: a rational operation on entries need not preserve the individual square roots required by a Diophantine tuple. Let $X$ be any smooth projective model of $K$.

**Theorem 1.** The low-degree holomorphic forms of $X$ satisfy

$$
H^0(X,\Omega_X^p)=0\quad(1\leq p\leq3),
\qquad h^{4,0}(X)=56.
$$

After extending constants to $\mathbb Q(i)$, the four-forms have a basis indexed by the five-element subsets of an eight-element set. This basis, through the decomposability of its linear combinations, intrinsically determines the rational entry field.

**Theorem 2.** Every dominant rational self-map of $X$ has degree one. Its action on $K$ is generated by the independent signs of the 21 roots, simultaneous negation of all seven entries, and permutations of the seven entries. In particular,

$$
\operatorname{Bir}_{\mathbb Q}(X)\cong (C_2)^{22}\rtimes S_7.
$$

The same classification holds after extending the constant field to $\mathbb C$.

**Theorem 3.** Let $Y$ be a smooth projective variety over a characteristic-zero field $k$. Suppose rational functions $a_1,\ldots,a_n,s_{ij}\in k(Y)$ satisfy $s_{ij}^2=1+a_i a_j$ for every pair. If $H^0(Y,\Omega_Y^4)=0$, then

$$
\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\leq3.
$$

In particular this holds for rationally connected parameter spaces. The bound is sharp for quadruples.

These are statements about generic operations and parameter spaces, not a solution of the rational septuple problem. An ordinary septuple consists of seven distinct nonzero rational numbers with all 21 specified squares; its existence remains open. Theorem 2 does not exclude rational maps with proper image, maps defined only on proper subvarieties, or genuinely multivalued correspondences. Theorem 3 does not exclude isolated points or families of dimension at most three.

### Related work and distinction

Dujella, Kazalicki, Mikić and Szikszai proved the existence of infinitely many rational Diophantine sextuples [1]. Dujella and Kazalicki related quadruples over finite fields to elliptic curves and modular forms, obtaining exact counting formulas [2]. Those works construct or count tuples; they do not classify rational self-maps of the generic seven-entry full-root field. The present argument does not improve their arithmetic existence results.

Hidalgo, Hughes and Leyton-Álvarez study automorphisms and the intrinsic deck group of generalized Fermat manifolds branched over hyperplanes in general position [3]. Our Cayley arrangement is highly nongeneric, and its full-root field is a particular rank-21 subcover of a 29-hyperplane arrangement. Those smooth general-position results do not directly supply Theorem 2. We instead recover the base from regular four-forms on a smooth model, without assuming that the original deck group is intrinsic.

On rStation, the all-word Vieta obstruction on Gibbs's sextuple hypersurface [4] excludes a specified group of candidate operations on a special sextuple source. The signed-chain surface calculation [5] determines the birational group of a proper two-dimensional source. Neither statement covers arbitrary dominant rational self-maps of the generic seven-entry field. The contribution here is the complete low-degree form calculation and the intrinsic recovery of ratio fields, which remove any assumption that an operation preserves a coordinate subfield.

The imported geometric tools are Mustață's multiplier-ideal formula for arrangements [6], local vanishing [7], and standard finite-cover and quotient-singularity facts [8]. Their application to this arrangement, the Eulerian incidence lemma, and the differential-field recognition are proved below. The regular quadruple extension used for sharpness in Theorem 3 is classical; see, for example, Gibbs's treatment of regularity and the hyperdeterminant [9].

## 2. The Cayley arrangement and explicit four-forms

The 21 radicands are independent modulo squares in $F$: the irreducible divisor $1+a_i a_j=0$ has odd valuation for exactly its own radicand. The same holds over an algebraic closure of the constant field. Thus $K/F$ is geometrically connected and Galois with group $(C_2)^{21}$.

Set $k=\mathbb Q(i)$ and use homogeneous coordinates $t_0,\ldots,t_7$ with

$$
a_j=i\frac{t_0-t_j}{t_0+t_j},\qquad
1+a_i a_j=\frac{2t_0(t_i+t_j)}{(t_0+t_i)(t_0+t_j)}.
\tag{1}
$$

The full branch arrangement in $\mathbb P^7$ consists of the 28 hyperplanes $t_i+t_j=0$, together with the marked hyperplane $t_0=0$.

A character is specified by a subset $T$ of the 21 edges on vertices $1,\ldots,7$. Add the edge $0j$ when $\deg_T(j)$ is odd. This produces an Eulerian simple graph $H$ on vertices $0,\ldots,7$, and every such graph occurs uniquely. Its character branch consists of the pair-sum hyperplanes indexed by its edges, and the pin $t_0=0$ precisely when

$$
\epsilon=|H|\bmod2=1.
$$

Write the even branch degree as $d=|H|+\epsilon=2D$.

For each five-set $S\subset\{0,\ldots,7\}$, take $H=K_5(S)$. This has ten edges and no pin. Its quadratic subfield is represented, in the chart $t_r\ne0$ with $r\in S$, by

$$
w_S^2=\frac{\prod_{\{i,j\}\subset S}(t_i+t_j)}{t_r^{10}}.
\tag{2}
$$

The scalar in (2) is compatible with (1): the ordinary-edge character contains six or ten edges, so its power of 2 is a square. The corresponding cover $D_S$ of $\mathbb P^4$, with coordinates indexed by $S$, has branch degree ten and Cartier canonical divisor zero.

We check that $D_S$ is canonical. A projective flat of this ten-hyperplane arrangement has a zero block of $z$ coordinates and signed nonzero blocks with $p_\ell$ positive and $q_\ell$ negative coordinates. Its codimension and containing-hyperplane multiplicity are

$$
c=z+\sum_\ell(p_\ell+q_\ell-1),\qquad
m=\binom z2+\sum_\ell p_\ell q_\ell.
$$

For a nonempty projective flat, $z\leq4$, and every block has at most five coordinates. Each multiplicity contribution is at most $3/2$ times its codimension contribution, so $m<2c$ at every proper flat. Mustață's formula [6, Theorem 0.1] makes the pair $(\mathbb P^4,B/2)$ klt. The finite-cover discrepancy formula [8, Proposition 5.20] makes $D_S$ klt. Its canonical divisor is Cartier, so its discrepancies are integral and nonnegative. Its canonical section therefore extends to a resolution.

On the chart $t_r=1$ this section is

$$
\alpha_S=\frac{\bigwedge_{j\in S\setminus\{r\}}d(t_j/t_r)}{w_S},
\tag{3}
$$

up to ordering sign. A dominant rational map between smooth proper varieties pulls back regular differential forms regularly: resolve its indeterminacy and use birational invariance of global forms on the source. Consequently (3) gives a regular four-form on $X$. The 56 different characters make these forms linearly independent. Let $W$ be their span. Their cotangent supports at the generic point are

$$
L_S=\operatorname{span}\{d\log(t_j/t_r):j\in S\setminus\{r\}\},
\qquad \dim L_S=4.
\tag{4}
$$

## 3. Completeness through multiplier ideals

We now prove that $W$ is the entire space of four-forms. Work over $\mathbb C$ for Hodge theory; proper base change then gives the same dimensions over $\mathbb Q$ and $k$.

### 3.1. The character cohomology formula

For a nontrivial character let $B$ be its reduced branch arrangement of degree $2D$, and let $X_B$ be a smooth projective resolution of its quadratic cover. Then, for $1\leq p\leq6$,

$$
h^{p,0}(X_B)=h^{7-p}\!\left(\mathbb P^7,\mathcal J(B/2)(D-8)\right).
\tag{5}
$$

To see this at the exact coefficient $1/2$, choose a log resolution $q:Y\to\mathbb P^7$ and define

$$
\mathcal L_Y=q^*\mathcal O(D)\otimes
\mathcal O_Y\!\left(-\left\lfloor q^*B/2\right\rfloor\right).
$$

The normalized pulled-back cover has structure-sheaf pushforward $\mathcal O_Y\oplus\mathcal L_Y^{-1}$. Its local singularities are $z^2=x_1\cdots x_r$, hence finite quotient singularities: take the quotient of affine space by the even coordinate-sign group, with $x_j=u_j^2$ and $z=\prod_j u_j$. They are rational, so a resolution leaves $H^p(\mathcal O)$ unchanged. The invariant summand vanishes for $p>0$ because $Y$ is rational. Serre duality identifies the dual of the remaining summand with

$$
H^{7-p}(Y,\omega_Y\otimes\mathcal L_Y).
$$

Its pushforward is $\omega_{\mathbb P^7}(D)\otimes\mathcal J(B/2)$, with no higher direct images by local vanishing [7, Theorem 2.3]. This proves (5), using Hodge symmetry. No left limit of the multiplier ideal is substituted for its value at $1/2$.

These groups give precisely the nontrivial character contributions on $X$. Indeed, choose an equivariant smooth projective resolution of the full cover. Its quotient by the kernel of a character has quotient singularities, hence rational singularities, and is birational to $X_B$. Finite quotient cohomology computes the corresponding invariants in $H^p(\mathcal O_X)$. The only possible characters there are the trivial character and the specified one; the trivial contribution is zero. The characters are real and self-inverse, so Hodge symmetry preserves their labels.

### 3.2. The only relevant flats

For an arrangement, Mustață's formula is

$$
\mathcal J(B/2)=
\bigcap_A I_A^{\max\{0,\lfloor m_B(A)/2\rfloor-c(A)+1\}},
\tag{6}
$$

where $A$ ranges over intersection flats. We may include the full arrangement's flats: an extra flat gives a redundant condition, by replacing it with the intersection of the $B$-hyperplanes containing it.

For the full 29-hyperplane arrangement, a flat has a zero block of size $z$ and signed nonzero blocks as above. Put $\delta=1$ when the zero block contains 0, and $\delta=0$ otherwise. Its data are

$$
c=z+\sum_\ell(p_\ell+q_\ell-1),\qquad
m=\binom z2+\delta+\sum_\ell p_\ell q_\ell.
\tag{7}
$$

A signed block of codimension cost $r\leq5$ contributes at most $\lfloor(r+1)^2/4\rfloor$, respectively $1,2,4,6,9$, all strictly below $2r$. A positive zero block of size at most four contributes at most $\binom z2+1<2z$. Thus $m<2c$ in codimensions at most four. At codimension five, equality or excess is possible only for a zero block of size five.

The only critical codimension-five flats are therefore the 56 coordinate planes

$$
Z_S=\{t_i=0:i\in S\},\qquad |S|=5.
$$

Their full multiplicities are ten or eleven, so their exponents in (6) are one whenever present. A plane is present for the character graph $H$ exactly when

$$
|E(H[S])|+\epsilon\,\mathbf 1_{0\in S}\geq10.
\tag{8}
$$

Let $U$ be the reduced union of the selected planes. With $\mathcal J=\mathcal J(B/2)$, we have $\mathcal J\subset I_U$, and $I_U/\mathcal J$ is supported in dimension at most one. Consequently, for every integer $t$,

$$
H^3(\mathbb P^7,\mathcal J(t))
\cong H^3(\mathbb P^7,I_U(t))
\cong H^2(U,\mathcal O_U(t)).
\tag{9}
$$

The first isomorphism uses vanishing above the support dimension of the quotient; the second uses the ideal sequence and the intermediate cohomology vanishing of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^7}(t)$. An empty union means $I_U=\mathcal O$ and zero right-hand side. Also $\mathcal O/\mathcal J$ has support dimension at most two, so $H^q(\mathcal J(t))=0$ for $4\leq q\leq6$. Equation (5) proves $H^0(X,\Omega_X^p)=0$ for $1\leq p\leq3$.

### 3.3. The Eulerian incidence lemma

**Lemma 4.** For an Eulerian graph $H$ and $d=|H|+\epsilon\leq16$, the selected five-sets in (8) have the following properties. For $d<10$ there are none. For $d=10$, selection forces $H=K_5(S)$ and is unique. For $d\leq14$ there is at most one selected set. For $d=16$, if there are several selected sets, their complementary triples share a common pair of vertices.

**Proof.** The first assertion is immediate. At $d=10$ the only alternative to a complete $K_5$ would be $K_5$ minus one edge together with a pin; that graph is not Eulerian.

Take distinct selected sets $S,T$, and write $q=|S\cap T|\geq2$. Their complete-graph union $G$ has $20-\binom q2$ edges. At most two edges can be absent from $H$, and none can be absent when $\epsilon=0$. If $q=2$ there are too many edges. If $q=3$, fitting $d\leq16$ requires a pin, exactly two missing edges and no added edges. But $G$ is Eulerian and deletion of two distinct edges cannot preserve even degrees. Hence $q=4$.

Write $S=A\cup\{u\}$ and $T=A\cup\{v\}$ with $|A|=4$. Now $G$ is $K_6$ minus $uv$. Its four vertices in $A$ have odd degree five, while $u,v$ have even degree four. If there is no pin, correcting the odd degrees requires at least four added edges, since all pairs of odd vertices are already joined; hence $d\geq18$.

With a pin, let $M=E(G)\setminus E(H)$. Each selected $K_5$ contains at most one edge of $M$. If $M$ is empty, at least four additions are needed. If $M$ consists of one edge in $A$, the remaining two odd vertices need at least two additions, giving $|H|\geq13+2=15$. If $M$ is one exclusive edge $ua$, four odd vertices remain, and no available added edge joins two of them; at least four additions are needed. Finally suppose $M=\{ua,vb\}$. If $a\ne b$, there are four odd vertices and only $uv$ is an available edge pairing two, so at least three additions are required and $|H|\geq12+3=15$. If $a=b$, there are six odd vertices and only $uv$ can pair two; at least five additions are needed. These exhaust the possibilities. Added edges are outside $G$; an edge in $M$ cannot also be added. Thus two selected sets force $d\geq16$.

At $d=16$, complementary triples therefore meet pairwise in an edge. Such a clique either has a common edge or contains three triples in one four-set without a common edge. In the second case there are selected sets $A\cup\{v_1\},A\cup\{v_2\},A\cup\{v_3\}$ with $|A|=4$. Their complete-graph union has eighteen edges. Fitting the degree bound requires a pin, exactly three deletions and no additions. A deleted edge inside $A$ would consume the allowed omission in all three sets, so the deletions must instead be one cross edge incident to each $v_j$. Each $v_j$ then has odd degree three, a contradiction. This proves the lemma. $\square$

### 3.4. Coordinate-plane cohomology

For any reduced union $U$ of coordinate planes,

$$
H^q(U,\mathcal O_U(t))=0\qquad(q>0,\ t>0).
\tag{10}
$$

Here is a direct proof. Resolve $\mathcal O_U$ by the alternating complex of structure sheaves of intersections of its coordinate components. On every standard affine chart the augmented complex decomposes by monomials. For each monomial it is the augmented simplex on the components allowing that monomial, and is exact. This establishes exactness of the sheaf complex. Every nonempty intersection is a coordinate projective space and has no higher cohomology for twists $t\geq0$. For $t>0$, its global-section complex again decomposes by monomials of degree $t$. Each has nonempty support, and its summands form the simplex on components containing that support. This proves (10). At $t=0$, the global-section complex is the nerve of nonempty intersections; its higher cohomology vanishes if all components share a nonempty coordinate subspace.

Use $t=D-8=d/2-8$ in (9). If $d>16$, (10) gives zero. If $d=16$, Lemma 4 gives one plane or planes with a common coordinate line, so $H^2(\mathcal O_U)=0$. For $d=12,14$, the union is empty or a single plane with twist $-2,-1$, again giving zero. Below degree ten it is empty. At degree ten, each of the 56 complete-five-vertex characters contributes

$$
h^2(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(-3))=1.
$$

All other characters contribute zero. The forms (3) are therefore a basis, proving Theorem 1.

## 4. Recovering ratio fields from decomposability

We work over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero constant field, using finite-data descent to $\mathbb C$ for Theorem 1 where necessary. A four-form is called decomposable if it is a wedge of four one-forms at the generic point. This property is intrinsic and is defined by the Plücker equations.

For distinct five-sets $S,T$, the graph $K_5(S)\mathbin\triangle K_5(T)$ determines the unordered pair $\{S,T\}$. If $|S\cap T|=4$, it is $K_{2,4}$. If the intersection has size three, its complement on the seven used vertices is a triangle and a four-cycle, which recover the intersection and the two exclusive pairs. If the intersection has size two, its two degree-six vertices are the intersection, and the remainder is two disjoint triangles. In particular all $\binom{56}{2}=1540$ pairwise character products are distinct.

Each $\alpha_S$ is decomposable. In the Plücker equations for a constant-coefficient sum $\sum_S c_S\alpha_S$, the mixed term for a pair $S,T$ belongs to its own character eigenspace over $F$. Thus terms from different pairs cannot cancel. A line joining two decomposable four-forms lies in the Grassmannian precisely when their four-dimensional supports intersect in dimension at least three. By (4),

$$
\dim(L_S\cap L_T)=|S\cap T|-1.
\tag{11}
$$

The decomposable locus in $\mathbb P(W)$ is consequently the union of coordinate subspaces supported on cliques of the Johnson graph $J(8,5)$, whose adjacency is $|S\cap T|=4$. Its maximal components are the 70 projective three-spaces indexed by four-sets contained in the labels, and the 28 projective five-spaces indexed by six-sets containing the labels. The elementary clique classification follows by comparing two adjacent labels: every common neighbor either contains their four-element intersection or lies in their six-element union; a maximal clique has one of these two types.

The 56 form axes are exactly the nonempty intersections of two distinct five-dimensional components. Two such components meet when their complementary two-sets share a vertex, so their intersection graph is the line graph of $K_8$. The eight maximal seven-cliques recover its vertices, and every permutation of this configuration is induced by an element of $S_8$. Thus an invertible linear map of $W$ preserving decomposability permutes the axes by a unique permutation of the eight vertex labels.

For a five-set $S$, define its first-integral field

$$
E_S=\{f\in K:df\wedge\alpha_S=0\}.
$$

By the differential criterion for algebraic dependence in characteristic zero, this is the relative algebraic closure in $K$ of $k(t_j/t_r:j\in S\setminus\{r\})$. Equation (11) gives the same assertion for intersections. Every pair $\{i,j\}$ occurs as $S\cap T$, so the recovered axes determine the relative algebraic closures of all 28 fields $k(t_i/t_j)$.

Each of those fields is already relatively algebraically closed in $K$. Indeed, $F$ is purely transcendental over $k(t_i/t_j)$. A nontrivial relative constant-field extension inside the elementary abelian extension $K/F$ would give a quadratic subextension. Its branch divisors must be both fibers of $t_i/t_j$ and members of the original arrangement. The only possible pair-sum hyperplane is $t_i+t_j=0$; the pin $t_0=0$ is additionally possible only when 0 is one of the indices. But a nonempty Eulerian simple graph cannot consist of a single edge, and empty edge support is the trivial character with no pin. A quadratic extension of the constant field is also excluded by geometric connectedness. We have therefore recovered, intrinsically,

$$
E_S\cap E_T=k(t_i/t_j)\qquad\text{when }S\cap T=\{i,j\}.
\tag{12}
$$

## 5. Dominant self-maps and the marked vertex

Let $\phi:K\hookrightarrow K$ be the embedding induced by a dominant rational self-map of $X$. Pullback of regular four-forms is injective, hence an isomorphism of the 56-dimensional constant vector space $W$.

It preserves and reflects decomposability. The extension $K/\phi(K)$ is finite separable, and

$$
K\otimes_{\phi(K)}\Omega^1_{\phi(K)/k}\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{K/k}
$$

is an isomorphism. Its exterior fourth power preserves and reflects the Plücker equations. Section 4 therefore gives a permutation $\sigma\in S_8$ of the form axes. For first integrals we have $\phi(E_S)\subset E_{\sigma(S)}$, and (12) implies

$$
\phi\bigl(k(t_i/t_j)\bigr)\subset k(t_{\sigma(i)}/t_{\sigma(j)}).
\tag{13}
$$

Set $x_i=t_i/t_0$ and $u_i=t_{\sigma(i)}/t_{\sigma(0)}$. Then $\phi(x_i)=M_i(u_i)$ for nonconstant rational functions $M_i$. Equation (13) makes $M_i(u)/M_j(v)$ a function of $u/v$. Simultaneous scaling yields

$$
\frac{M_i(\lambda u)}{M_i(u)}=
\frac{M_j(\lambda v)}{M_j(v)}.
$$

As $u,v$ are independent, the common value depends only on $\lambda$ and satisfies the multiplicative character equation. A rational character of $\mathbb G_m$ is $\lambda^n$ for an integer $n$. Hence

$$
\phi(x_i)=c_i u_i^n\qquad(c_i\in k^*,\ n\ne0),
\tag{14}
$$

with the same $n$ for every $i$.

The known global entry negation inverts all $x_i$, so we may first assume $n>0$. For any $j\ne i$, the image of the radicand in (1) is

$$
\phi(1+a_i a_j)=
\frac{2(c_i u_i^n+c_j u_j^n)}
{(1+c_i u_i^n)(1+c_j u_j^n)}.
\tag{15}
$$

On the torus where all eight homogeneous coordinates are nonzero, the denominator $1+c_i u_i^n$ has $n$ distinct prime divisors $u_i=\zeta$. None cancels with the numerator or the other denominator because $u_i,u_j$ are independent. Among the old branch divisors, only $u_i=-1$ has this direction. If $n\geq2$, (15) therefore has a simple pole outside the original branch locus. The extension $K/F$ is unramified there, so an odd valuation cannot become a square, contradicting $\phi(1+a_i a_j)=\phi(s_{ij})^2$. Thus $n=1$, and the same pole argument forces $c_i=1$. Reintroducing inversion, the remaining base maps are

$$
x_i\longmapsto
\left(\frac{t_{\sigma(i)}}{t_{\sigma(0)}}\right)^{\pm1}.
\tag{16}
$$

The marked vertex must be fixed. For the positive exponent, the transformed radicand is

$$
\frac{2t_{\sigma(0)}(t_{\sigma(i)}+t_{\sigma(j)})}
{(t_{\sigma(0)}+t_{\sigma(i)})(t_{\sigma(0)}+t_{\sigma(j)})}.
\tag{17}
$$

If $\sigma(0)=r\ne0$, (17) has odd valuation along $t_r=0$, where the original full-root extension is unramified. This is again impossible for a square in $K$. The negative exponent gives the same radicands because simultaneous inversion fixes the expression in (1), so it has the same obstruction. Hence $\sigma(0)=0$.

The maps on entries are precisely permutations and simultaneous negation. In particular $\phi(F)=F$. Since $[K:F]=2^{21}$, the intermediate field $\phi(K)$ has the same degree over $F$ and must equal $K$. Its kernel over $F$ is the group of the 21 independent root signs. All the resulting maps are defined over $\mathbb Q$, which proves Theorem 2 over both $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb C$.

## 6. The parameter-dimension consequence

For the generic four-entry field, the character given by all six roots produces the complete-five-vertex double cover from Section 2. Thus on a smooth projective model of the full quadruple cover the form

$$
\beta=\frac{da_1\wedge da_2\wedge da_3\wedge da_4}
{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq4}s_{ij}}
\tag{18}
$$

is nonzero and regular. Its descent to the original constant field is explicit. With $x_j=(i-a_j)/(i+a_j)$, one can take

$$
w=\frac{32\prod_{i<j}s_{ij}}{\prod_j(a_j+i)^2},\qquad
\det\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial a}\right)
=\frac{16}{\prod_j(a_j+i)^2},
$$

so the pullback of the canonical form in (3) is $\beta/2$.

If the entries on $Y$ in Theorem 3 have transcendence degree at least four, select four algebraically independent ones. Their six specified roots define an embedding of the generic quadruple field into $k(Y)$, hence a dominant rational map from $Y$ to a smooth quadruple model. Pullback of (18) is regular and nonzero, contradicting $H^0(Y,\Omega_Y^4)=0$. This uses a dominant map to the four-entry model; it does not assume that an arbitrary special subvariety lifts through a resolution of the entire $n$-entry cover. It also accommodates identically zero pair roots on other entries. The four selected entries themselves cannot have such a relation because they are independent.

For sharpness, use the classical regular extension. With independent parameters $a,r,u$, put

$$
R=r^2-1,\qquad b=R/a,\qquad
z=\frac{2(a^2u-R)}{R-a^2u^2},\qquad
s=1+z,\quad t=1+uz,\quad c=(s^2-1)/a,
$$

and

$$
d=a+b+c+2abc+2rst.
$$

The six roots are $r,s,at+rs,t,bs+rt,cr+st$ in lexicographic pair order. At $(a,r,u)=(1,2,2)$, the entries are $(1,3,8,120)$ and the roots are $(2,3,11,5,19,31)$. The Jacobian of $(a,b,c)$ with respect to $(a,r,u)$ equals $-240$ there, so the family has dimension three. Distinctness, nonzero entries and nonzero denominators hold on a nonempty open set. This proves Theorem 3 and its sharpness statement without claiming a new quadruple construction.

## 7. Reproduction, checks and scope

The proof uses no bounded search for rational tuples. Its graph and arrangement inputs have exact, small independent audits in the supplement:

* `full_root_arrangement_flats.py` enumerates signed partitions over $\mathbb Q$. `full_root_arrangement_flats_linear.cpp` independently enumerates all row spans of the 29 normals over $\mathbb F_3$. Both yield 50,712 projective flats including the ambient space, with exactly 56 critical codimension-five planes. Signed incidence matrices have the same ranks in characteristic zero and odd characteristic; a connected signed component contributes its vertex count, or one less when balanced.
* `full_root_hodge_characters.cpp` examines all $2^{21}-1$ nontrivial characters using Eulerian graphs and simplicial link homology. `full_root_hodge_audit.py` uses ordinary-edge parity arrays instead, and directly verifies the coordinate-plane conclusions. Both give exactly 56 contributing characters, each a complete $K_5$ and each of dimension one. The 315 distinct multiple-plane complexes at degree 16 all share a coordinate edge.
* `full_root_four_form_checks.py` verifies the ten-hyperplane flat calculation, all support intersections, the 1540 distinct character products, and branch-restriction ranks for the 28 ratio fields.
* `full_root_family_dimension_checks.py` verifies (18), its Cayley scalar, the regular-family identities and the displayed Jacobian control.

The JSON outputs and a SHA256 manifest are included. The principal checks use C++20 and Python with SymPy and NumPy, one CPU thread, and small memory. The README gives exact commands. These audits test combinatorics and algebra; the text supplies the geometric argument, especially the exact coefficient in (5) and the sheaf-complex argument in (10).

The result closes a generic source of universal compatibility operations. It gives no arithmetic nonexistence theorem and no new septuple. Proper subvarieties, correspondences that acquire additional roots, and special rational points remain possible. The original projective intersection of quadrics is not assumed canonical; its singular boundary does not enter through an unjustified canonical-polarization argument.
