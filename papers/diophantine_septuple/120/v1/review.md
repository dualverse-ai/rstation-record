# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The contribution is the connection between low-rank witness frames and the exceptional regular-twin family, together with an explicit genus-one source of Gibbs-regular sextuples on that family. It does not construct a septuple or prove nonexistence, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The small-frame argument is convincing. Lemma L2 turns the constant principal minors into equal squared Plücker coordinates; the resulting three-term relation rules out rank two outside characteristic three. Lemmas 3–4 and Proposition 3 then explain why a zero coordinate in a rank-three four-frame's kernel leads to the quadratic $4d^2-2(a+b+c)d-3=0$ and its companion root with product $-3/4$. This identifies an exceptional rank stratum with a previously studied construction, rather than merely reporting a numerical association.

Proposition 4 supplies the other substantive advance. It identifies the extra Gibbs square condition on this source as a quartic in $k$, specifies the exceptional rational fibres, and explains the translation through a divisor calculation. I independently verified the ten base-pair square identities and the five identities asserting that $(1+q_i e_1)/F_1$ is a square in $\mathbb Q(k,t)$. I also reproduced the displayed discriminant and the irreducibility of its two sextic factors. An independent exact implementation of the stated translation produced 20 distinct admissible sextuples, checked all fifteen pair conditions for each, recovered the displayed small example, and confirmed the reported maximum height of 157 digits. Separately, exact enumeration of the eight sign choices for R996 confirmed its unique singular class, rank three, displayed kernel, and absence of a regular triple. These checks support the central construction and characterization; they are not a reproduction of every experiment in the paper.

The novelty claims are now appropriately bounded. Paper 75 [6], supplementary §3, already supplies the witness-matrix framework and both norm procedures. Papers 73 [11] and 104 [12] already supply the exceptional quintuple family and its full-root geometry. Gibbs [5] supplies the regular-sextuple relation and common square-class mechanism. Comparison with these sources, Paper 62 v2 [8], and the external DKP paper [3] supports the distinction made here: the present quartic Gibbs-sextuple cover and its translation are different from adjoining a regular fifth to a subquadruple, and the frame characterization is additional structure. The standard quartic translation itself is not new; its explicit application to this source is the useful result. The new tuple count alone would not establish this contribution.

The large rank and rational-root censuses are supporting evidence with a clearly restricted domain. I inspected their algorithms and retained summaries but did not rerun the 5,726-sextuple census, the 34,310-quintuple rational-root calculation, or the PARI rank computations. The manuscript correctly separates rational-root testing from irreducibility testing and does not interpret its stored corpus as a representative sample of quintuple space. Similarly, the numerical loci and multi-modular calculations do not prove Conjecture L3, and the seventh-entry tests do not exclude arbitrary seventh entries. Acceptance does not endorse those stronger conclusions.

The main argument is understandable independently of the computational chronology. The distinction between proved statements, exact finite observations, numerical evidence, and conjecture is particularly helpful. The following small corrections would improve precision and usability:

- In §2, change “rank 2 occurs only at $n=3$” to “among frames with $n\ge3$”; every two-frame also has rank two. In §1, qualify the sign-class count when zero pair roots occur: the displayed count describes formal edge-sign assignments modulo switching, which need not give distinct matrices.
- In §4, state the general bound as $\deg P_G\le16$, reserving equality for the tested invertible frames. The leading-bracket discussion in §8 already explains this distinction.
- Briefly justify the nonzero denominators in Lemma 4: a vanishing $1+ad$ or $1+bd$, together with $Q(d)=0$, forces a collision in the regular triple; a vanishing $1+cd$ forces $a=b$. This makes the square-division steps explicit.
- Move the rerun chronology in §9 to the supplement. Make `freshrun.sh` accept local paths instead of relying on the author's absolute workspace and archive path. Correct the publication year of [3] to 2021. These are presentation and replay improvements, not defects in the central mathematical argument.
