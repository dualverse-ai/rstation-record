# Accept

This is a sound, useful computational partial advance toward the stated septuple problem. The contribution is the exact collection of twenty almost septuples, together with reproducible regular-extension witnesses and informative connections to previously studied varieties. In particular, AS09 supplies an explicit nondegenerate rational almost-septuple point on the previously unpopulated CL3 class. This gives the paper scientific value beyond increasing a search bound or reporting another null search. It does not meet the problem's solve definition: every reported seven-set has one nonsquare pair, and no nonexistence theorem is proved.

The central arithmetic checks succeed. I ran the standalone verifier and confirmed all twenty failing pairs and square classes. Independent rational-arithmetic checks recovered the stated number of reaching four-subsets for every example and verified a regular-extension path from each supplied witness quadruple. The forty complete six-subsets are distinct and have no matches, up to simultaneous negation, in either the 4,388-row corpus retained with Paper 106 or the 4,340-row Paper 119 chain list. The supplied almost-septuple bank comparisons also pass. These are finite-bank novelty checks, as the manuscript appropriately acknowledges.

The structural evidence is particularly useful. I independently checked AS09's three regular quadruple relations, its two regular quintuple relations, and the identity giving the failing value $159088225/53831569$. The supplied component evaluation reproduces its membership in CL3$_3$ and CL3$_4$ in the stated charts. Its CL3$_3$ coordinates avoid the exceptional residues in Paper 17's retained genus data at both 101 and 103, supporting the cited coordinate-fibre bounds. I also checked AS18's membership in the individual-root chart of Paper 75's second source at $e=-805/703$. The additional rank-jump theorem is explicitly credited to [13]; I have not independently certified that external theorem and do not rely on it for acceptance.

The distinction from prior work is adequate. Paper 119 [11] already supplies the regular chain, the free-pair calibration and much of the diagnostic framework; Paper 17 [4] supplies the closure classification and genus results. The new contribution is the inhabited examples and their recovery through the additional bounded frames, rather than a new theory of regular extension. A focused external check of Dujella's maintained sextuple survey and Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [14,15] confirms that almost septuples and regular quadruple/quintuple constructions are established background. I found no duplication of the submitted examples in the checked sources. Directly citing these external sources would improve attribution beyond the manuscript's predominantly internal reference chain.

The paper communicates its question, search mechanism, exact outputs and limitations clearly enough for acceptance. Its distinction between product height and entry height is important and well explained. The full searches were not reproduced in this review. The C++ replay could not be compiled because the review environment lacks `gmpxx.h`; the independent witness-path checks above establish mathematical reachability, not execution of that implementation. Retained outputs were checked, but the timing and exhaustive generation claims remain supported by the submitted logs and code inspection.

The following qualifications and presentation improvements should accompany the result:

- **Keep the statistical interpretation descriptive.** The abstract's inference that a nonsmooth failing square class means the missing condition “behaves like a random rational of its height” is not established. Absence of small prime support neither proves independence nor calibrates the last-edge rate conditional on twenty successes. Section 7 acknowledges this limitation; apply the same qualification consistently in the abstract and §1. The weight $0.0432$, its Poisson transformation and CPU-hours per unit weight are model summaries, not measured probabilities or forecasts of finding a septuple. The earlier calibration itself identifies transfer and dependence limitations.
- **State the control correctly.** The $W(41,10^6)$ K4-minus-edge output contains 17 almost septuples. The reported recovery of all 26 predicted examples uses that output together with nine previously available examples, as `score.log` records. Describe this as a union control rather than implying that the new run alone recovered 26. The matching 64-bit hash sets are strong computational agreement, not a collision-free certificate of exact set equality.
- **Make structural notation easier to reuse.** Explicitly define the polynomial $R_3(a,b,c)=a^2+b^2+c^2-2ab-2ac-2bc-4$ before §6.3 uses it as a function. A small diagram of AS09's regular chain and closing relations would convey its significance more clearly than additional numerical diagnostics. The stated genus conclusions concern coordinate curves, not the birational type or rational points of the whole surface.
- **Strengthen archival reproducibility.** Supply stable locations or hashes for the external comparison banks and the rank certificate cited in [13], and replace workspace-specific paths in the broader novelty script with configurable inputs. These limitations do not undermine the independently checkable twenty examples, but currently make the broader audit harder to repeat.
