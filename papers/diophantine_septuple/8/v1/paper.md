# A one-quintuple bound with an explicit height gap

## 1. Contribution and relation to the septuple problem

A rational Diophantine tuple consists of distinct nonzero rational numbers whose pairwise products plus one are squares. A coordinate is strong if its square plus one is also a rational square. Strong members of square-product tuples enter a fractional-linear correspondence with mixed-sign tuples containing a product-minus-one pair [1]. The elliptic quintuple families of Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [2] therefore provide a concrete construction route worth examining. Earlier work excludes fourteen of their fifteen coordinate cases [3]. The remaining case is coordinate E of family 2, abbreviated E2.

**Theorem 1.** Among the three fixed quintuple families in [2], at most one nondegenerate quintuple, up to reordering and global negation, has a strong member. It must arise from E2. There are at most four admissible rational E2 parameters.

We do not exhibit this possible quintuple or claim to exclude E2. Theorem 1 bounds the size of an exceptional set in the three named families. A complementary computation, Theorem 2, shows that any remaining parameter has a reduced numerator or denominator with more than 1,350,000 decimal digits. Neither theorem determines that exceptional set. Arbitrary strong square-product quintuples, square-product sextuples and rational Diophantine septuples remain unresolved.

A complementary elliptic curve over a quartic field has rank at most 3 and two known independent points. Instead of finding the missing generator or selecting its actual annihilator, we bound zeros uniformly for every annihilator of the known points. At 7, the elementary square conditions leave a single parameter disk. Every higher projected Taylor coefficient lies in seven times the lattice generated by the first two. Strassmann's theorem then bounds every member of the projective pencil by two zeros. One zero is a known inadmissible parameter. A free Klein-four symmetry identifies all rational parameters above the remaining possible abscissa up to the stated tuple equivalence.

Elliptic Chabauty over number fields is established [4], as is the use of Selmer information without a complete Mordell–Weil group [5]. Our contribution is the explicit uniform coefficient-lattice argument for this remaining family. It uses a rank upper bound and two independent logarithms, but does not require a third generator, a full basis, or a congruence for the missing direction. Stoll’s work on diagonal genus-five curves and Diophantine quintuples [6, Section 3] explicitly identifies rank bound 3 with known rank 2 as a computational gap for elliptic Chabauty. The present calculation handles that gap for this nonsplit cover while giving a bound rather than a full rational-point determination. The class-group input uses the Brauer and regulator approach of [3].

## 2. Original coordinate and fixed complementary cover

Put
$$
r=u^2-2u-2,\qquad h(u)=48(3u^2-2)(u^2+16u+10),
$$
and
$$
T(u)=\frac{9(3u^2-2)(u^2+16u+10)}{r^2}.
$$
The family requires $h(u)\in\mathbb Q^2$. Its E-coordinate has numerator and denominator
$$
\begin{aligned}
n(u)&=-3u(u+1)(u+2)(2u+1)(3u^2-2)(u^2+16u+10),\\
d(u)&=4(u-1)(3u+2)(5u+4)r(3u^2+4u+2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Define
$$q(T)=T^4+106T^3+2025T^2-34992T+629856.$$
Direct polynomial identities give
$$
n^2+d^2=\frac{r^8}{11664}q(T),
$$
and
$$
3T=\frac{9h(u)}{16r^2},\qquad
T+54=\left(\frac{3(3u^2+4u+2)}r\right)^2,\qquad
3(T+48)=\left(\frac{3(5u^2+8u+2)}r\right)^2.
$$
Thus every admissible strong specialization satisfies all four rational square conditions.

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\theta)$ with $q(\theta)=0$, and put $c=27-\theta$. The eligible-point class sieve additionally gives
$$c(T-\theta)\in K^{\times2}.$$
Here is the certified global argument. The genus-three curve
$$v^2=T(T+54)(T+48)q(T)$$
has defining polynomial of discriminant $2^{55}3^{75}11^2 59^2 3011$. Use every bad prime, so $S=\{2,3,11,59,3011\}$. Outside S its monic odd-degree equation forces even valuations in the four Kummer components, including at nonintegral T. The rational components have six-dimensional S-unit squareclass spaces. The quartic field has certified trivial S-class group and thirteen S-unit squareclasses, giving an ambient space of dimension 31. Six norm rows and twenty exact local Hilbert-orthogonality rows have rank 26. Each local witness comes from an actual local point or rational branch point; isotropy makes these necessary constraints without requiring complete local images.

Fixing the rational components to $(3,1,3)$ leaves an affine line, hence two possible quartic squareclasses. Exact field-square tests identify them as $c$ and $c\gamma$, where
$$\gamma=\frac{72215\theta^3+8061275\theta^2}{23328}+\frac{423895\theta}{54}-108206.$$
At the good prime 29 the eligible finite residue parameters are $0,8,10,27$; the derivative convention handles branch residues. Their Kummer classes exclude $c\gamma$. Nonintegral $T$ is impossible at 29 because 3 is nonsquare there. Thus $c$ is the unique global class. A standalone verifier certifies the field, rebuilds the entire upper space, checks the two exact class identifications and performs this complete final residue filter. A local square test by itself would not justify the fixed twist.

Write
$$q(T)/(T-\theta)=T^3+aT^2+bT+d_0.$$
The square conditions lift the parameter to
$$
E:\quad Y^2=X^3+caX^2+c^2bX+c^3d_0,\qquad X=cT.
$$
Indeed $c\,q(T)/(T-\theta)$ is a square when both $q(T)$ and $c(T-\theta)$ are squares. Two independent points are
$$P=(27c,-1944c)$$
and
$$H=\left(\frac{\theta^3+61\theta^2-11664}{16},\frac{27\theta^3+783\theta^2-19440\theta+209952}{16}\right).$$
The point $Q=2H-P$ has $X(Q)/c=-5157/49$.

## 3. Certified arithmetic rank bound

The two-torsion cubic of E is irreducible over K. Its degree 12 absolute root field is the field of ordered pairs of distinct roots of q. An unconditional S-class calculation for $S$ above $2,3,3011$, followed by complete local images, gives a three-dimensional upper Selmer space. The independent Kummer classes of P,H span a plane in it, so
$$2\leq\operatorname{rank}E(K)\leq3.$$

The class-group step cannot be replaced by assuming the output of an uncertified number-field computation. The exact $S_4$ character relation is
$$\zeta_{L_{12}}=\zeta_K^2\zeta_B/\zeta_{\mathbb Q}^2,$$
where B is the sextic field fixed by a cyclic subgroup of order 4. Certified class-quotient bounds, independent unit squareclasses, rigorous regulator intervals and the S-unit valuation index prove that the S-class group of $L_{12}$ is odd. They do not claim an exact ordinary class number for $L_{12}$.

## 4. A uniform annihilator pencil at 7

### 4.1. One parameter disk

The three elementary square conditions force
$$T\in27+7\mathbb Z_7.$$
Indeed, negative 7-adic valuation would make the ratio $3T/(T+54)$ a square in $3+7\mathbb Z_7$, whereas 3 is nonsquare modulo 7. For integral T, testing all seven residues in $3T$, $T+54$ and $3(T+48)$ leaves only $T\equiv6\pmod7$.

The polynomial q is irreducible modulo 7, so K has one unramified quartic completion. The elliptic curve has good reduction there and $\#E(\mathbb F_{7^4})=2420$. The ordinate of P is a unit. Every eligible local point therefore belongs to one of the two branches above this T-disk, reducing to P or $-P$. Negation interchanges these branches and changes every elliptic logarithm by its negative, so they have the same abscissas. It suffices to work on the branch through P.

### 4.2. Coefficient lattice and infinite tail

Identify the local field with $\mathbb Q_7^4$ in the integral basis $1,\theta,\theta^2,\theta^3$. Let L be the $4\times2$ matrix of the logarithms of P,H. Integral row and column reduction has two pivots of valuation 1. A saturated quotient by their span is consequently represented by an integral $2\times4$ matrix $\pi$, with $\pi L=0$.

Since the global rank is at most 3 and these two logarithms are independent, the projected global logarithmic space has dimension at most 1. Thus there exists a nonzero row $\lambda\in\mathbb Q_7^2$ such that $\lambda\pi\log R=0$ for every global point R. We do not determine $\lambda$.

Write $T=27+7z$, let $R(z)$ be the local branch through P, and put
$$F(z)=\pi\log R(z)=\sum_{n\geq1}v_nz^n.$$
Its constant is exactly zero. The first two coefficient vectors form an invertible matrix $M=(v_1\ v_2)$ with
$$v_7(\det M)=3,\qquad v_7(M^{-1}\pi)=-2,$$
where a matrix valuation is the minimum valuation of its entries. These assertions are reproduced from the curve and points by the standalone verifier; no stored logarithms are inputs.

For completeness, these finite calculations control the entire series. Write the invariant differential on the branch as
$$\frac{7c}{2Y(P)}(1+s_1z+s_2z^2+s_3z^3)^{-1/2}\,dz.$$
The cubic variation satisfies $v_7(s_j)\geq j$, and the prefactor has valuation 1. Since $-1/2\in\mathbb Z_7$, every binomial coefficient of its inverse square root is integral. Hence the unprojected integrated coefficient of degree n has valuation at least $n-v_7(n)$. Applying $M^{-1}\pi$ gives
$$v_7(M^{-1}v_n)\geq n-v_7(n)-2>0\qquad(n\geq3).$$
Consequently
$$M^{-1}F(z)=\binom{z}{z^2}+7\sum_{n\geq3}w_nz^n,\qquad w_n\in\mathbb Z_7^2,$$
with coefficients tending to zero.

Normalize the nonzero row $\lambda M$ to a primitive row $\mu\in\mathbb Z_7^2$. The annihilator then has first two coefficients $\mu_1,\mu_2$, at least one of which is a unit, while every later coefficient is divisible by 7. Strassmann's theorem gives at most two zeros in $\mathbb Z_7$, counting multiplicity. One is the known anchor $z=0$. Thus there is at most one further rational abscissa.

This argument also covers rank 2: any nonzero projected functional annihilates the global group. No parity, saturation of the global subgroup, or unidentified point is assumed.

### 4.3. Precision certification

The verifier embeds into the unramified quartic field at working precision $7^{240}$. It multiplies P,H by $2420\cdot7^3$ into formal parameter valuation at least 4 and evaluates the formal logarithm through degree 79. For an integral Weierstrass equation, the coefficient in degree n has valuation at least $-v_7(n)$. The omitted terms therefore have valuation greater than 310 after division by the multiplier. Tracked arithmetic precision is lower but still exceeds 200 digits at the projection check. Pivot reduction and coefficient normalization lose at most a few further digits. All decisive valuations are between -2 and 3, well inside this margin. The infinite Taylor tail is proved by the preceding inequality, rather than inferred from a finite expansion.

## 5. From abscissas to quintuples

The known value 27 gives
$$9(3u^2-2)(u^2+16u+10)-27r^2=36(u-1)(3u+2)(5u+4).$$
Its projective fiber is $1,-2/3,-4/5,\infty$. At each point E2 has a pole with nonzero numerator. Thus only the one possible additional abscissa can contribute admissible original parameters.

The degree 4 map $T(u)$ is invariant under the Klein four group generated by
$$a(u)=-\frac{2(u+1)}{3u+2},\qquad b(u)=\frac{u+2}{u-1}.$$
The third nonidentity map is $ab(u)=-2(2u+1)/(5u+4)$. None has a rational fixed point: their fixed-point polynomials are proportional to $3u^2+4u+2$, $u^2-2u-2$ and $5u^2+8u+2$, whose discriminants are nonsquares. They also have no fixed point at infinity. Every fiber containing a rational parameter therefore consists of its free orbit of four rational parameters.

Direct substitution in all five accepted coordinate formulas shows: a globally negates the tuple and exchanges B,C; b globally negates without permutation; ab exchanges B,C without negation. These maps preserve admissibility by the identities in Section 2. Hence each rational abscissa contributes at most one quintuple modulo ordering and global sign. There are at most four valid u and at most one such quintuple. Combining this with the other fourteen coordinate exclusions in [3] proves Theorem 1. ∎

## 6. An explicit height gap for the remaining possibility

Write $h(a/b)=\log\max(|a|,b)$ for a reduced rational number with $b>0$.

**Theorem 2.** Every admissible strong E2 parameter satisfies
$$h(u)>3\,125\,000.$$
In particular, its reduced numerator or denominator has more than 1,350,000 decimal digits. This lower bound does not prove that the remaining possibility exists or is absent.

### 6.1. A complete rational elliptic basis

The rational quartic $y^2=q(T)$ has Jacobian
$$E_q:\quad y^2+xy+y=x^3-x^2-5861x+173341.$$
A rational 2-descent gives rank 2; the cubic root field used in that descent has certified class number 1. The points
$$G_1=(31,-160),\qquad G_2=(47,-32)$$
form a complete Mordell–Weil basis, as certified below. They agree, up to negation, with the published Cremona generators for 162594a1 [7], but our completeness proof does not assume that database’s saturation result.

Use the canonical x-height normalization
$$H(R)=\lim_{n\to\infty}4^{-n}h(x(2^nR)).$$
The standard duplication polynomials are
$$N(X)=X^4-b_4X^2-2b_6X-b_8,\qquad D(X)=4X^3+b_2X^2+2b_4X+b_6,$$
with the usual Weierstrass invariants of $E_q$. Exact Bézout identities for $(N,D)$ and their reciprocal quartics have common integral right side
$$R_0=40458590208.$$
For coprime integers a,b, the gcd of the two homogeneous duplication values divides $R_0$: the two identities give multiples of $R_0a^7$ and $R_0b^7$. On whichever chart has $\max(|a|,|b|)$ as its denominator, the same identities give an archimedean lower bound. The maximum sum of absolute Bézout coefficients is $5233654627785874064$, while the upper duplication coefficient bound is $36263936$. Therefore
$$|h(x(2R))-4h(x(R))|\leq C,\qquad C=\log(5233654627785874064),$$
and telescoping gives
$$|H(R)-4^{-n}h(x(2^nR))|\leq\frac C{3\cdot4^n}.$$

Exact doubling through $n=8$ and rigorous Arb logarithm intervals certify two inequalities for the height-pairing matrix $M_H$ on $G_1,G_2$:
$$M_H\succeq\tfrac14 I,\qquad\frac{H(G_1)+H(G_2)+2|\langle G_1,G_2\rangle|}{4}<\tfrac12.$$
For the first inequality, both diagonal entries minus the absolute off-diagonal entry exceed $1/4$. The second bounds the height throughout the centered fundamental parallelogram. Since the rank is 2, every coset of $\langle G_1,G_2\rangle$ has a representative R in that parallelogram, hence $H(R)<1/2$. The duplication bound implies
$$h(x(R))<\tfrac12+C/3<\log(3000000).$$
A complete rational-point enumeration to this naive x-height finds 58 affine points. Each is verified by exact elliptic addition to belong to $\langle G_1,G_2\rangle$. Numerical heights only suggest its integer coefficients; exact point equality certifies membership. Infinity belongs to the subgroup as well. Thus every coset is trivial, proving completeness without an assumed index bound.

### 6.2. Complete finite coefficient sieve

At 178 good primes below 2000, enumerate every point of $E_q(\mathbb F_p)$ and retain its residue only if the corresponding T admits the necessary complementary elliptic lift at every residue-field factor of q. At inverse-map exceptional points the filter conservatively retains the residue. These are necessary local conditions, not sufficient conditions for a rational lift.

The complete sieve of
$$R=mG_1+nG_2,\qquad |m|,|n|\leq10000$$
checks all $400040001$ coefficient pairs. Exactly seven survive:
$$(-3,1),\ (-1,-1),\ (-1,0),\ (0,0),\ (1,1),\ (2,1),\ (4,0).$$
Exact inversion, including the exceptional cases, gives only
$$T=-5157/49,\quad -261/7,\quad27,\quad\infty.$$
The negative values violate $3T\in\mathbb Q^2$; the other values are inadmissible in the original family. The source supplement regenerates every finite-group filter and repeats the entire coefficient sweep. Thus any admissible rational point has $\max(|m|,|n|)\geq10001$, and the height bound gives
$$H(R)\geq\frac{10001^2}{4}=25005000.25.$$

### 6.3. Returning to the original parameter

For $T=N/D\neq27$ in lowest terms, write $y=W/D^2$ on the rational quartic. Then W is an integer and $|W|\leq1000\max(|N|,D)^2$. The exact birational map has minimal-model abscissa
$$x(R)=\frac{7558272D^2+384912(N-27D)D+3888W+5004(N-27D)^2}{36(N-27D)^2}.$$
Coefficient bounds give $h(x(R))\leq2h(T)+25$. Since $C<45$, the duplication estimate yields $H(R)\leq2h(T)+40$. The degree-four formula for $T(u)$ has numerator coefficient sum at most 1215 and denominator coefficient sum at most 25, so $h(T)\leq4h(u)+8$. Hence
$$H(R)\leq8h(u)+56.$$
Together with the lower bound, this implies $h(u)>3125000>1350000\log10$, proving Theorem 2. ∎

## 7. Reproducibility, limits and contributions

The source supplement runs without network access or private inputs. Install PARI/GP 2.15 or later and Python with SymPy and python-flint, and a C++17 compiler (`g++`), then run `python verify.py --gp /path/to/gp` from its extraction directory. The seventeen-stage clean run took 88 seconds with PARI/GP 2.15, Python 3.12, SymPy 1.14.0 and python-flint 0.9.0, including the complete finite sieve. The driver checks explicit PASS markers and rejects GP error diagnostics, since PARI may exit with status zero after an error. `README.md` maps the checks to the proof, and `SHA256SUMS` identifies the sources. Number-field structures are regenerated; uncertified structures are not used as certificates of class-group assertions.

The fixed-twist verifier rebuilds the global squareclass upper space using every bad prime and performs the final residue exclusion. The arithmetic scripts certify the auxiliary S-class parity and rank bound. The standalone 7-adic verifier reconstructs the local field, known logarithms, complete residue test, coefficient lattice and infinite-tail hypotheses. The original-coordinate verifier checks the family identities and free orbit interpretation. The height stages certify the rational elliptic basis, regenerate all finite filters, exhaust the coefficient square, and check exceptional inverse maps. Both authors independently checked the local analytic mechanism and its original-family interpretation.

No finite point search enters Theorem 1. The proof gives neither a third generator nor a rational extra zero. It does not establish existence of a strong quintuple in these families, and supplies no exclusion for arbitrary rational Diophantine septuples.
