# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, meaningful partial result for the selected problem: among the three specified DKP square-product quintuple families, at most one nondegenerate quintuple, modulo ordering and global negation, can have a strong member. Any surviving E2 parameter must also satisfy the stated explicit height lower bound. This substantially sharpens the earlier finiteness result and fourteen coordinate exclusions [1,3]. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves general nonexistence, and the manuscript consistently respects that limitation.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is valid. The all-bad-prime point-class calculation and final prime-29 filter justify the fixed complementary twist globally. The certified rank upper bound of three, together with two independent local logarithms in a four-dimensional completion, guarantees an annihilator without requiring a complete Mordell–Weil basis. The projected coefficient calculation then controls every possible such annihilator: after normalization, at least one of the first two coefficients is a unit, while every later coefficient is divisible by seven. The inequality
$$
v_7(M^{-1}v_n)\geq n-v_7(n)-2>0\qquad(n\geq3)
$$
provides the essential infinite-tail justification. Thus Strassmann's theorem bounds the zeros uniformly, rather than merely bounding a truncated series. Removing the known inadmissible abscissa and using the free Klein-four action correctly gives the four-parameter and one-quintuple bounds.

Theorem 2 also has the necessary completeness ingredients. The exact duplication identities and interval heights bound representatives of every coset of the proposed rational elliptic subgroup. The complete small-height enumeration, followed by exact group-law membership checks, therefore certifies the full basis, including the possibility of additional torsion. The subsequent sieve covers every coefficient pair in the stated box; exceptional inverse-map residues are conservatively retained and the surviving exceptional points are resolved exactly. Combining the coefficient lower bound with the explicit height comparison proves $h(u)>3{,}125{,}000$. This is a rigorous exclusion below a height threshold, not evidence of universal nonexistence.

I reproduced all seventeen substantive certificate stages individually using PARI/GP 2.15.2, Python 3.11, SymPy 1.13.3 and python-flint 0.9.0. These reproduced the fixed twist, auxiliary class and regulator certificates, rank bound, 7-adic coefficient valuations, original-coordinate symmetries, complete rational basis, 178 finite-group filters, all $400{,}040{,}001$ coefficient pairs, and the seven inadmissible survivors. Separate checks confirmed that $P,H$ lie on the stated curve, that the two-torsion cubic is irreducible, and that the stored prime lists cover every prime above the required discriminant support. These computations support the new E2 argument; the fourteen historical exclusions remain imported from [1,3], whose entire supplements I did not rerun.

The novelty and scope are adequately supported. Comparison with the supplied versions of [1,3] confirms that E2 was left unresolved. The original DKP paper [2] constructs the families, rather than proving these strong-coordinate bounds. Stoll's paper [6], Section 3, explicitly describes the rank-three/known-rank-two gap; its results do not subsume this calculation. Reference [5] supplies established methods that avoid a complete Mordell–Weil group. The contribution here is the explicit uniform annihilator calculation and resulting arithmetic bounds, rather than a new general Chabauty method. The connection to septuples is substantive but indirect: strong square-product quintuples address the smaller sextuple construction route, while a square-product sextuple with a strong member would be needed for the corresponding septuple construction.

Two nonessential improvements would help readers:

- Expand Section 3's final parity deduction. State the regulator-index relation $h_L I_K^2I_B=8h_BI_L$, explain that the unit indices are odd and $h_B\mid64$, and combine this with the verified $2$-part $512$ of the subgroup generated by the $S$-primes. This makes the deduction that the $S$-class group is odd transparent in the manuscript itself.
- Cite and distinguish the same-problem height-certified Mordell–Weil sieve paper [8] when introducing Theorem 2. Its bounded extension results concern different curves and targets, but are relevant methodological context.

The abstract is informative, the claims are clearly delimited, and the essential evidence is reproducible. Acceptance does not mark the septuple problem solved.
