# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial contribution to the septuple problem. Its principal value is the explicit common four-point relation exhibited across the 25 specified almost septuples, supported by a complete one-step census and a further example outside that corpus. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves nonexistence, and therefore does not satisfy the problem’s solve definition. The manuscript appropriately limits its negative conclusions.

Theorem 1 has a complete argument. A zero-sum divisor of the four specified points is represented in $\mathcal L(4O)=\langle1,t,t^2,w\rangle$. Distinct abscissas and the presence of $P_0$, without $-P_0$, exclude a function involving only $t$. The resulting quadratic interpolation function has the stated norm identity. Conversely, the identity supplies four distinct zeros and a pole of order four, giving the group relation. Evaluating at $y_j$ and $-1/x_i$ proves the column and row identities. The nonvanishing assumptions justify using square classes throughout. The forest argument is correct, and its cycle statement is carefully restricted to what the linear relations determine; it does not assert rational realizability of arbitrary cycle assignments. The exchange of the two triples also does not require either triple to be Diophantine.

I independently checked all 25 Table 2 witnesses using rational elliptic-curve arithmetic, verified their 20 square conditions and single failing pair, and checked both displayed witnesses for $A_4$. An independently written enumeration over the supplied 1123 sextuples reproduced the entire one-step census: 269518 finite candidates, 11622 degeneracies, 235008 three-square cases, 22764 four-square cases, and 124 five-square cases, with no six-square case. It also reproduced the detailed outcome patterns, the regular-fourth-element incidence counts, and the 47 distinct witness pairs. Thus the main finite claims are supported beyond inspection of retained output.

For the larger lattice searches, I inspected the implementation and checked the retained five-square sets, but did not reproduce the 94-million-point enumeration. The implemented torsion descent filter matches the stated square-class condition. The modular sieve accepts zero residues and points reducing to infinity and excludes unsuitable primes, so its rejection rule is one-sided as required. The manuscript correctly explains that a six-of-six sieve does not enumerate every almost septuple in the box. These searches concern the specified generated subgroup and coefficient bounds, not the complete Mordell–Weil group or an intrinsic height region.

The novelty is modest but substantive when framed around the common relation and its exact coverage. Papers [8], [9], and [10] already supply the almost septuples and substantial regular-relation analysis; [7] studies a different specialized-lattice membership question. The present one-step witness census is a distinct result. The external comparison with DKP [3] and Gibbs’s adjugate construction [5] does not identify the same contribution. However, the divisor argument uses standard elliptic-curve theory and the forest criterion is standard incidence-matrix reasoning. Moreover, [6] and [9] already give much stronger height exclusions for substantial parts of this corpus. The additional negative lattice scan should therefore remain supporting evidence, rather than the main novelty claim.

The following improvements would sharpen attribution and reproducibility without changing the decision:

- Include Gibbs’s *Regular Rational Diophantine Sextuples* [11] in the related-work discussion. Its symmetric regular-sextuple equation and square-product identities are relevant antecedents, although they do not establish the present four-point identity and corpus theorem. Explain the distinction explicitly.
- Correct the corpus documentation. The supplied `new77.pkl` contains 34 records, not 77; `Ss.pkl` contains 1090, whereas the cited version of [6] describes a 759-sextuple corpus. The supplied union does reproduce the claimed results, but a short provenance manifest should explain the later additions and which census records are already included.
- Document the transitive dependencies of the census scripts: importing `almost22` reaches `dtup`, which imports both `gmpy2` and `cypari2`. The step scripts should fail if required corpus inputs or imports are unavailable, rather than catch an exception and continue with a smaller corpus. State the expected corpus size as an assertion.
- Qualify “known almost septuples” in the title by the corpus restriction, consistent with the careful discussion of additional DKP examples in the text.

[11] P. E. Gibbs, *Regular Rational Diophantine Sextuples*, preprint, viXra:1609.0425v2 (2016), https://vixra.org/pdf/1609.0425v2.pdf.
