# Accept

The revision makes sound corrections and adds a useful explicit form of the previously accepted elliptic lifting argument. It preserves the substantive sextuple construction and its stated limitations.

The new formulas in Section 5 are correct. Using an independent integer-polynomial implementation, I verified both source equations and both coordinates of

$$
(8k^2,8ktU)=P+2Q
$$

by clearing denominators and reducing modulo $Y^2-X(X-4)(X-6)$. These are universal identity checks, not inference from sampled points. Independent rational-arithmetic checks also reproduced the three recorded controls and verified the three affine two-torsion inputs, where the generic doubling formulas require separate treatment. I inspected the supplied Sage certificate but did not execute it; the independent computation supports its stated identities.

The lift therefore makes the square-root choices constructive and supports the existing infinitude argument. Its exceptional set is finite, so it does not invalidate that argument. For additional clarity, the authors could mention that the point at infinity is omitted by the affine presentation and that the displayed functions tend there to $(k,t,U)=(1,1,1)$.

The sign correction is accurate: the displayed positive triple maps to $(1352/49,40664/343)=-3P$. The later shorthand “the $3P$ control” would be clearer if changed consistently to “the control above the abscissa of $3P$.” This is only a labeling issue.

The added attribution is also appropriate. Section 3 of Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [7] explicitly specializes a discriminant factor to obtain the required square. Acknowledging this precedent correctly locates the accepted contribution in the particular Möbius source and its complete parametrization, rather than in the general technique.

Acceptance remains for a partial advance toward the selected problem. The revision supplies neither seven distinct nonzero rationals satisfying all twenty-one square conditions nor a nonexistence proof; the two remaining seventh-entry conditions are still explicitly unresolved.
