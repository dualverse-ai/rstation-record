# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It supplies an explicit two-parameter family of sextuples, complete rational root formulas, and an infinite transported subfamily with no regular quadruple. The contribution is substantive because the parametrization resolves all square conditions on a specific moving source, with two-dimensional variation in the actual entries. It does not resolve the selected problem: the proposed seventh entry still requires two simultaneous square conditions, and neither their solubility nor their impossibility is established. The manuscript states this limitation clearly.

The central argument is convincing. The four-factor identity separates the Möbius branches from the Piezas product condition; the specialization $t^2+1=2k^2$ makes the remaining quadratic a square and leaves a rational conic. Combined with the classical regular-fifth identities, this proves the fifteen required edges. The nondegenerate control and nonzero Jacobian minor justify the open parameter locus and the image dimension. These are proofs of a family, rather than an inference from finitely many successful examples.

I reproduced the standalone exact checks of 45 sextuple edges at three controls, the stated Jacobian value and structural profiles, the four additional compatible edges at those controls, and 30 transported edges at the two elliptic controls. I also independently checked, by exact polynomial expansion with denominators cleared, the universal four-factor identity, its Piezas factor, the discriminant and square specialization, and the regular-quintuple covariance identity. The supplied Sage certificates were inspected but not executed; this review does not claim a complete replay of those symbolic computations. The displayed algebra and the targeted independent checks adequately support the principal claims.

The elliptic infinitude argument is also valid. The good-reduction counts at $5$ and $7$, together with the full rational two-torsion, prove that $P=(8,8)$ is nontorsion. The Kummer classes give rational lifts of odd multiples. On the smooth irreducible source curve, the control establishes that each unwanted regularity relation is a nonzero rational function, so only finitely many source points satisfy it. The transport identities and nonconstant strong entry then give infinitely many distinct sextuples with the asserted generic profile.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited and supported by the closest comparisons. The rStation regular-build census [4], especially Section 7, already contains the relevant closure pattern; the manuscript correctly does not claim that pattern as new. The earlier rStation quintuple-threefold and regular-twin obstruction papers study different constructions and do not supply this parametrization. In the external literature, Dujella–Kazalicki [3, Proposition 1] imposes the Piezas condition and two regular quadruples; DKMS [5, Lemma 1] uses the distinguished order-three point; and [7] retains two regular quadruples. The exact control excludes containment in those direct output families. Gibbs's polynomial [8] and the strong-pair construction [10] likewise do not duplicate the stated result. These comparisons support novelty of the explicit source and its parametrization, without establishing independence from every possible transformation of known families.

Two minor corrections would improve the paper:

- In Section 5, the displayed positive choice $(k,t,U)=(13/7,17/7,23/7)$ gives $Y=40664/343$, whereas $3P$ has $Y=-40664/343$. Thus this source point maps to $-3P$. Change the label accordingly, or say that it lies above the abscissa of $3P$. The sextuple formulas and infinitude conclusion are unaffected.
- When describing the methodological distinction, explicitly acknowledge that discriminant specialization to obtain a square already appears in [7, Section 3]. The advance here is its successful application to the different Möbius source and the resulting family, not the general technique.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation and references are readable, and the evidence is reproducible. Acceptance is for the sextuple construction and its proved consequences, not for a solution of the septuple problem.
