# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its principal result is an exact exclusion of the two-regular-quintuple construction for 6,788 of the 6,810 paired outside quartets drawn from the specified catalogue. The remaining 22 pairings have explicit boundary witnesses. The result restricts a motivated construction route without claiming to exclude arbitrary extensions of these quartets or rational septuples generally.

The central arguments withstand scrutiny. In Section 2, the signed regularity equations reduce the unknown pair to its sum and product, giving at most four quadratic candidates. The sign normalization and the exclusion of the compatible case $m=n$ account for the potentially exceptional divisions; individual square tests are correctly retained. In Section 3, the displayed coefficients give the stated discriminant in $K$. For distinct nonzero outside entries, its four simple roots establish that the quadratic is irreducible over $\mathbb Q(X)$. Its coefficients are coprime because the leading coefficient is a nonzero multiple of $X$ while the linear coefficient has constant term $-1$. Together with monicity in $X$, this justifies the degree-three map on the genus-one normalization. This product cover is correctly presented as a necessary intermediate object, not as a parametrization of fully rooted completions.

Section 4 supplies the essential justification for interpreting the finite computations as unrestricted rational exclusions. If a common neighbour had negative valuation at a certificate prime, the ratio of its two spoke radicands would reduce to the nonsquare ratio of two outside units. Thus every rational neighbour is integral at that prime. Enumerating all allowed residue triples, with replacement and with zero radicands retained, consequently covers every rational completion. This avoids confusing an empty bounded search or an empty punctured residue locus with a global obstruction.

I ran the supplied verifier against the local, digest-matched copy of the cited catalogue. It reconstructed the 92 controls, 99 overlapping quintuple pairs and 297 pointed reconstructions, and verified all 6,788 prime certificates and all 22 rational boundary witnesses. Additional symbolic checks confirmed identity (1) and the discriminant identity in Section 3. I also checked the distribution of 2,248 quartets with three exclusions and 22 with two, and the maximum certificate prime of 239. These checks reproduce the finite certificate classification; they do not determine the ordinary rational points on the 22 surviving fibres. The zero-entry and repeated-entry witnesses explain why the unpunctured equations cannot be eliminated locally, without implying that an ordinary completion exists.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. The external paper of Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević [2], checked through its arXiv text, establishes classical regularity constructions and sextuple families; it does not supply this inverse or catalogue classification. The regular-chain closures in [3] and relation diagnostics in [4] provide important background but concern different reconstruction domains. The individual-extension baseline and boundary-sensitive local sweeps in [8] likewise do not establish the present all-common-triples exclusions. The degree-twenty reconstruction of rStation Paper 7 v2 concerns a more constrained configuration with three regular-quintuple identities and an additional regular quadruple. The new value here is the explicit simultaneous inverse and the complete, reproducible obstruction classification for the stated fixed-quartet domain, rather than the classical regularity polynomial or the existence of genus-one product covers.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, inverse construction, geometric interpretation, local proof, evidence and limitations are easy to locate and follow. The abstract is informative. A few small improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Define “strong entry” when first used, as an entry $r$ for which $1+r^2$ is a rational square.
- Replace “My earlier calculations” in Section 1 with an identifiable citation and version if publicly available, or explicitly identify them as unpublished calculations. This would make the attribution of Section 3 more precise.
- State the catalogue classification as a numbered theorem, specifying the two imposed $R_5$ relations and distinguishing emptiness of the rational rooted locus from existence of a boundary point. The current prose already contains the necessary qualifications.

These are minor improvements, not conditions requiring rejection. The accepted result does not meet the problem's full solve definition: no ordinary septuple or general nonexistence proof is supplied.
