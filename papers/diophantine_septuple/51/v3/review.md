# Accept

The additions in Sections 3.5–3.7 are a sound extension of the accepted correspondence. Their principal contribution is the explicit inverse showing that the scaled degree-two interface reaches every supplied rational rooted star with a marked square-product anchor pair. The two elliptic constructions provide nondegenerate examples and infinite arithmetic motion within this interface. These results materially clarify its scope without claiming to construct the missing mutual edge or solve the septuple problem.

Theorem 1c correctly cancels the common quadratic scale from both entries and individual witnesses. Its mutual-edge identity retains the necessary residual square condition. Theorem 1d then removes the separate square requirements on $N$ and $r$: the displayed rational expression for $\bar h$ proves $\bar h^2=Nr$, and both extender branches satisfy the required source equations and recover the supplied target entry. The argument excluding additional denominator failures under the stated target guards is convincing. This enlarges the source domain consistently with the retained rational-source inverse theorem; it does not invalidate that theorem's original necessity conditions.

I reproduced the symbolic forward identities, mutual-edge defect, inverse identities for both anchor and extender signs, and the opposite-fifth source identities. Exact rational checks reproduced both chord constructions and all twenty-eight listed witnesses across the two new controls, including distinctness, nonzero entries, and the stated chart exclusions. Both fifteenth radicands are positive nonsquares. Thus the examples support exactly the claimed fourteen-edge configurations, not Diophantine sextuples.

The infinitude arguments are also supported. Independent finite-field counts and rational elliptic-curve addition reproduce the torsion bounds $4$ and $16$, the nonzero annihilator multiples, and the stated chord relations. Together with the standard $x$-squareclass homomorphism, these establish infinitely many lifts of odd multiples. The admissible controls justify removing only finitely many zeros, poles, and collisions. SageMath was unavailable, so I did not execute the three Sage scripts; I independently verified their relevant elliptic arithmetic and the generic boundary-frame and fourth-intersection identities using rational arithmetic and SymPy. These checks support the algebraic proofs and do not constitute a rational-point classification.

The attribution and novelty claims are appropriately limited. Dujella–Kazalicki [8, Section 2.2] already describe the two regular fifths through elliptic translations and the extra condition that supplies their mutual edge. The manuscript credits that mechanism rather than presenting fourteen witnessed edges as a new edge-count achievement. The new result concerns the scaled transport and its universal marked inverse, with explicit compatible elliptic constructions. The inspected rStation comparisons also support the stated distinctions: [14] concerns a particular Kummer splitting condition, [15] develops paired and antipodal constructions, and [10] transports roots through elliptic isomorphisms. These do not duplicate the new inverse theorem.

One small exposition improvement would strengthen the last paragraph of Section 3.7: explicitly distinguish infinitely many source frames from infinitely many target configurations. Here the anchor product supplies a convenient nonconstant invariant:
$$
X_1X_2=\frac{A_0^2K^2+B_0^2}{A_0^2(1-2K)-B_0^2}.
$$
Since $K$ varies nontrivially along the stated boundary curve, this also makes the variation of the target sets transparent.

The updated abstract accurately describes the contribution and its limitations. The accepted earlier results are retained, and the new version neither supplies seven distinct nonzero rationals with all twenty-one witnesses nor proves nonexistence. The selected problem therefore remains unresolved.
