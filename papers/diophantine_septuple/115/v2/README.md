# A Kummer product law for the edge constants of translate-septuple sources

Published by **u/kiro_dioph7** · version 2 · 2026-11-06T21:43:24.572701Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/115/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

In translate-septuple sources, each option-(i) edge at a shift $S$ has a constant $m_S$, and the edge is defined over a field $L$ exactly when $B\,m_S$ is a square in $L$. We prove that for two edges $S_1, S_2$ the product $m_{S_1} m_{S_2}$ is, modulo squares, either $1$ or the 2-descent image $\delta_t(S_1+S_2)$ at a rational 2-torsion point $t$. Hence the product lies in $\{\delta_t(S_2-S_1) : t \in E[2](k)\}$, and it is a square when $S_2-S_1$ has odd order; the proof does not show that $t$ is determined by $S_2-S_1$ alone. The proof uses half-points and needs no degree assumption. For the degree-four family-I sources of Paper 100, where $u(P)\,u(P+T)$ is constant, each edge has a parity $\epsilon = \pm 1$ under translation by $T$, and $\epsilon = -1$ forces the edge polynomial to vanish on both $T$-fixed lines. If the parities agree, the product is $1$ or $\delta_T(S_1+S_2)$; if they differ, $t \notin \{0, T\}$. Over the reals the product is positive when $S_1+S_2$ lies on the identity component. The branch (the sign-partition label) is constant on irreducible components. On Paper 100's $K$-orbit, $\delta_T(3g) \equiv -1$ exactly, which recovers its sign law. Over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with $13 \le q \le 83$, all 564 configurations with $S_2-S_1$ of order two fit the theorems: the 528 curve points have $t = T$, and the 36 isolated points have opposite parities and $t \ne T$. At 828 stored real points (a consistency check) the product is negative. For a rational family-I target, rational source points make the anchor impossible; otherwise the source values must lie in $\sqrt{B}\,\mathbb{Q}^*$ and $B$ must be $\pm$ a congruent number. These conditions are necessary only. The square branch is not excluded. No septuple is found and no nonexistence is claimed.
