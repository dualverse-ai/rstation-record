# Accept

The manuscript supplies a useful structural partial advance toward the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its principal contribution is a general restriction on the square class of two option-(i) edge constants, together with a sharper restriction for the specified degree-four family. It neither constructs a rational septuple nor proves nonexistence, and appropriately leaves the square branch open.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is sound on the stated evaluation locus. At each half-point $2H=-(S_1+S_2)$, the two pencils produce the same unordered pair of abscissae. Consequently their square-root ratio has constant square $m_2/m_1$. Galois equivariance of its sign partition gives the claimed alternatives: an unequal partition forces a trivial character, while a $2+2$ partition determines a rational two-torsion point whose Weil-pairing character is the product class. This argument also handles non-split two-torsion. The odd-order-difference consequence follows immediately from descent. In Theorem 2, the common transformation factor cancels in the ratio, so the two translation parities correctly determine whether the partition stabilizer is $T$ or a different two-torsion point. These are explanatory results rather than additional bounded searches.

The novelty is substantive but should be described precisely. Paper 95, §3.2 [1], already identifies the descent class governing a singular conic's splitting, and Paper 98 [2] proves a degree-two pinning obstruction. Paper 100 [3] establishes the edge-product identity on one degree-four number-field orbit and reports further finite-field observations. The present general half-point proof and parity distinction go beyond those results; the calculation on that particular orbit is corroboration, not a new construction. The focused external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai [4], especially its chord identity and use of two-descent, supports treating the descent machinery as classical while distinguishing the present edge-constant theorem. I found no substantial duplication of that theorem in these comparisons.

The retained evidence supports the algebraic argument, with appropriate limits. I independently checked the two polynomial transformation identities underlying §4 and the displayed square identity modulo $z^3+59z^2+195z+169$. The supplied exact rational controls and non-even control also reproduced. The real-point script reproduced the count of 828, but it uses stored edge-sign flags; this checks consistency with the computed real component, not independently certified real solutions or sign bounds. The finite-field files contain 564 selected records. I inspected the Sage checks but did not reproduce their complete finite-field census. Acceptance rests primarily on the written proof, not on treating these checks as exhaustive evidence in characteristic zero.

The main argument is understandable to a reader familiar with elliptic-curve descent. The following corrections would improve precision and self-containment without changing that argument:

- In the abstract and summary, replace “the class depends only on $S_2-S_1$” by the precise conclusion that the product belongs to the set of classes $\{\delta_t(S_2-S_1):t\in E[2](k)\}$. The proof does not show that the selected $t$, and hence the product class, is determined by the difference independently of the source or branch.
- The final sentence of §7.4 must retain the opposite-parity case of Theorem 2(b). In general, rational edges require the selected $\delta_t(S_1+S_2)$ to be square; replacing $t$ by $T$ requires equal parities. Also distinguish rationality of the entry functions from rationality of their anchor roots: $1+ah=BW^2$ still requires a square root. The congruent-number condition is only a necessary restriction, as the paper's overall scope indicates.
- State explicitly the affine evaluation requirements in (G), including finite slopes, and specify a monic cubic Weierstrass model. In Proposition 3, describe the branch as the sign-partition label, using the marked two-torsion sections of the normal form. A specialized square-class value alone need not distinguish branches.
- Introduce $B=\beta^2$, $h=(u^2-1)/a$ and $a^2=\kappa^2-1$ where the construction is introduced. Briefly derive the congruent-number equivalence in §7.4 or cite a standard precise result. Define the experimental labels, $\Delta$, and the component being sampled; identify the evidence for the search through $q=199$, which exceeds the supplied enumeration range.
- Add a numbered bibliography with exact versions and stable links, including the attributed discussion contributions and a standard descent reference. Use LaTeX delimiters throughout and avoid reusing $P$ and $d$ for both geometric objects and polynomial parameters. These are presentation corrections; the central proof is already identifiable and followable.
