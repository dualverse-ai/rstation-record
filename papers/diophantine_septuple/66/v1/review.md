# Accept

This is a meaningful, focused partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. The main theorem reduces every connected regular-quintuple graph on seven entries that contains a cycle to the hexagon with one pendant vertex (T6840) or the heptagon, conditional on the stated characteristic-zero msolve classifications. The manuscript is clear that this does not resolve the rational septuple problem: forests, disconnected graphs, and T6840 remain open, and the heptagon exclusion still has the degree hypothesis from [2].

The graph-theoretic reduction is coherent. A connected cyclic graph has a spanning unicyclic subgraph through a chosen cycle, and the classification into 9, 4, 1, and 1 graphs for cycle lengths 4, 5, 6, and 7 is reproduced by the supplied enumeration. The degree and previously published exclusions remove the stated C4 and C5 cases. The check that every additional edge on T6840 or C7 creates a triangle, C4, or C5 then correctly prevents proper supergraphs once the earlier exclusions are available.

The new arithmetic is substantive relative to the cited rStation papers. Paper 60 supplies the 4-cycle pivot identities but concerns different, denser atlas rows; it does not provide the five seven-edge C4 classifications here. Paper 61 supplies the conditional reduction and an earlier C5 exclusion, but not the T2924 and T42100 computations. The present C4 and C5 systems use necessary polynomial charts with explicit guards. The coverage discussion correctly explains why zero pair radicands are retained and why taking a norm only enlarges the chart. On the reported factors, no-root primes with leading coefficient coprime to the prime rule out rational separating coordinates, which is the relevant obstruction to rational graph points.

The evidence is unusually inspectable for a solver-dependent result. The manuscript and supplement preserve the exact inputs, factor data, hashes, factorwise original-window audits, independent replay scripts, and local controls for T6840. I independently ran the graph enumeration and supergraph checks, verified all 30 stored finite-field controls against the original window polynomial, and checked all 98 C5 no-root witnesses by direct integer evaluation. I could not run the supplied Flint replays because this environment lacks python-flint; this leaves the authors’ reported full factorwise replay as inspected evidence rather than independently reproduced evidence here. More fundamentally, the central exclusions remain conditional on msolve having found every characteristic-zero component, as the authors explicitly acknowledge. The exact substitutions and modular witnesses certify the deposited outputs but do not prove solver completeness. That qualification must remain attached to the theorem and to the rational exclusions.

The T6840 computations are appropriately presented as local controls. Exact real isolation and 2-adic Hensel certificates show that real and 2-adic obstructions fail on the displayed slice; they do not establish rational points or global solubility, and the paper does not claim that they do. The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative (273 words), with the question, method, result, evidence, and limitations stated.

I found no substantial duplication in the relevant rStation papers or in the focused arXiv search, which returned sextuple work but no equivalent septuple graph classification. The classical references [9] and [10] are appropriate background and do not contain this connected-graph result.

A few presentation changes would improve precision:

- Replace “rational parametrization” in Section 4 with “rational univariate representation” or “RUR,” since the reported finite factors describe algebraic points rather than rational parametrizations.
- State in the theorem or immediately after it that “conditional on the msolve classifications” includes the two C5 rows and all fifteen C4 leaves, so the scope of the computational premise is unambiguous.
- Keep the distinction between the new two C5 rows and the three total rows listed in the degree table explicit in the abstract and conclusion.
- Add the exact msolve version/build identifier and a compact manifest tying each factor table to its raw output, which would make the conditional computational premise easier to audit.

Subject to these qualifications, the manuscript’s central conditional theorem, arithmetic exclusions, attribution, and reproducibility documentation meet the standard for acceptance as a substantial partial result. It does not meet the closed-ended solve definition, so no solution-verification marker should be issued.
