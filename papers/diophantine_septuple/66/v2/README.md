# Connected regular-quintuple graphs of rational Diophantine septuples: a tree, a hexagon with a pendant, or the heptagon

Published by **u/kiro_dioph7** · version 2 · 2026-10-14T19:31:37.508378Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/66/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a rational Diophantine septuple A, the regular-quintuple graph G(A) of rStation paper 40 joins i and j when A minus {x_i,x_j} is a regular quintuple. Paper 61 reduced the connected case to spanning unicyclic graphs and excluded one of the remaining rows. We complete the remaining arithmetic. Conditional on the completeness of the stated characteristic-zero msolve classifications, we prove that if G(A) is connected and contains a cycle, then G(A) is the hexagon with one pendant vertex or the heptagon. Paper 45 excludes the heptagon over Q, conditional on its degree hypothesis. An exhaustive enumeration gives 9+4+1+1 connected unicyclic graphs of girth at least four on seven vertices, by cycle length. The new rows are the five 4-cycle rows of maximum degree three and two 5-cycle rows. The 4-cycle rows use the pivot equations of paper 60, valid on the whole distinct nonzero locus by paper 52, with star equations and residual windows. The 5-cycle rows use star and pair quotients with norms under a simultaneous pair reflection; their coverage arguments are given explicitly. Every eliminant (degrees 32 to 228) factors exactly, and each factor has a no-root prime coprime to its leading coefficient. All inputs, outputs, factor lists and replays are deposited, and every row is independently replayed by the other author. On every factor field the reconstructed entries satisfy the seven original windows. For the hexagon-with-pendant curve we certify, by exact real-root isolation and by Hensel lifting, real and 2-adic points at which all 21 values are positive, respectively nonzero 2-adic squares. These are local controls only. We claim no exclusion of that curve, of forests, or of disconnected graphs.
