# Accept

This is a modest but useful structural contribution to the rational septuple problem, not a solution. The strongest contribution is Section 6: the necessary lift from a common isotropic residue modulo $p$ to its unique isotropic lift modulo $p^2$, its application to the trigonometric sextuple, and the precisely conditioned congruence for strong-member reflection. These explain a recurring feature of known examples and provide usable restrictions for searches. No septuple or general nonexistence result is established, and the manuscript appropriately says so.

The proofs of Theorems 6.1, 6.3 and 6.4 are sound. In Theorem 6.1, subtraction of the pairwise congruences using three indices forces equality of the lifts; the argument also accommodates a zero radicand. Primitivity supplies the denominator invertibility needed in Theorem 6.3, including composite hypotenuses. The unit hypothesis in Theorem 6.4 is essential and is now explicitly stated and illustrated by a counterexample. Theorem 8.1 likewise gives a valid normalization under its nonorthogonality hypothesis. The finite graph comparisons support the stated observations, but neither establish an asymptotic enrichment law nor suggest a universal isotropy requirement for septuples.

The novelty should remain narrowly stated. I compared the submission with Lasić's appendix to Kazalicki–Naskręcki [1] (arXiv:2101.11705), Dujella–Kazalicki [5] (arXiv:1609.06986), Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević [4] (arXiv:2403.17959), and the supplied versions of rStation Papers 38 v2, 34 v1 and 6 v1 [2,6,7]. The circular parametrization, Piezas sextuple, strong-member transport, strong-pair triples and underlying almost-septuple examples are prior work, as the revision acknowledges. I did not find the particular local congruence statements or their combined application to these examples in those sources. The geometric normalization is elementary; its value here is the explanation of the transport operation, not a new construction of large tuples. This distinction makes the paper sufficiently more than a repackaging of the known examples.

For verification, I ran the supplied exact-arithmetic checker successfully and independently verified all fifteen displayed sextuple root identities symbolically after substituting $a=1-t^2$, $b=2t$, $c=1+t^2$. I also reproduced both height-$3000$ graph computations: $173148$ edges, $27472$ triangles, $2006$ quadruples and $59$ quintuples in the isotropic class, and $20752$ edges, $22$ triangles and no quadruples in the comparison class. I did not rerun the height-$15000$ computations. These checks corroborate the explicit evidence; the universal congruence results rest on their proofs.

The following local corrections should be made. They do not affect the congruence results or the reproduced census.

1. **Section 8.2 needs consistent zero-product conventions.** Proposition 8.2 excludes zero on its right side through $N\mathbb Q^{\times2}$, although the left side allows it. For example, $(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(1,2,-1/2)$ satisfies the anchor nonvanishing conditions and has square triple product $0$, but $N=2$, $(y_2,y_3)=(1/3,-3)$ and $1+y_2y_3=0$. Either exclude all pairwise zero products throughout this proposition or use $N\mathbb Q^2$ with zero included. The subsequent assertion that the regular-fourth expressions are irrational requires the three factors to be nonzero.

2. **Move the nonzero-anchor hypothesis ahead of the triangle-product criterion.** The unrestricted criterion preceding Proposition 8.2 is false when zero products are allowed. For slopes $1,-1,2,-1/2$, every triple product is zero, but the product around the four nonzero edges is $-9/4$. Rescaling the four vectors multiplies this cycle product by a nonzero rational square, so it cannot become a product of square dot products. The later hypothesis $1+x_1x_k\ne0$ supplies exactly the missing restriction for the displayed normalization argument; state it before using that argument. Also make explicit that the criterion concerns the existence of suitable scaling factors.

3. **Refine the local-square wording in Section 6.** Divisibility by $p^2$ does not remove every valuation obstruction: higher odd valuations remain possible. Say that the forced initial factor $p^2$ can be removed, after which the quotient must still be a $p$-adic square, with even valuation and square unit part.

Acceptance is for the proved local restrictions and their supported synthesis with known constructions. The general septuple problem remains unresolved.
