# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance on the rational Diophantine septuple problem. It excludes rational outputs from the specified degree-four even reciprocal source architecture, including isolated algebraic source points and functions with nontrivial translation periods. The exclusion uses only the six anchor spokes, without assumptions that the mutual-edge functions have even divisors. This closes a substantive possibility explicitly left open in Paper 115 v2 [2, §7.4]. It does not construct a septuple or prove universal nonexistence, and therefore does not resolve the supplied problem's solve definition.

The central proofs withstand scrutiny. In Lemma 2, conjugation and evenness produce two lists of zeros of $W(Z+D)+W(Z)$. Distinct squared spokes rule out cross-index overlaps, and the only possible same-index overlap has zero spoke. Thus the lower bound $2n-1$ correctly accounts for this exceptional case. Comparing it with the pole-degree bound $2d$ proves the stated pointwise result without restricting the residue degree of the source point.

Theorem 5 supplies the essential arithmetic step. Quotienting by the full translation stabilizer makes a hypothetical sign-changing translation unique, hence rational and of order two. The fixed values on the $x$-line must be individually rational because one is a zero and the other a pole of a rational function. This yields a rational cyclic four-subgroup. Pullback under the specified dual two-isogeny gives an order-eight subgroup with exactly one nonzero two-torsion element, so it is indeed cyclic. Lemma 4 correctly preserves the odd-prime isogeny through the quotients, including when the original kernel is killed. I checked the externally available text of [3, Theorem 4.6]: its classification excludes every degree $8\ell$ for odd prime $\ell$, including the required degree $24$. The argument uses rationality of subgroups, not rationality of their generators.

The remaining square-twist case is also complete. Lemma 7's homogeneous-form calculation includes infinity and gives $[c]=[b]^m$. At degree four this makes $c$ a rational square; a square $B$ would then give $a^2=r^4-1$ with $a\ne0$, contradicting the classical square-area obstruction. These two cases establish Theorem 1 as stated.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. Papers 100 v2 and 115 v2 [1, 2] develop the degree-four source and edge-constant arithmetic but leave the algebraic-source rational-spoke possibility unresolved. Papers 95 v5 and 98 v1 [5, 6] impose geometric edge hypotheses on degree-two sources; Paper 96 v1 [7] concerns the two-anchor/order-five architecture. Their conclusions do not subsume the present pointwise exclusion. The external isogeny classification [3] provides standard arithmetic machinery, while the connection from simultaneous spoke descent to the forbidden isogeny is the substantive new argument. Attribution of the earlier norm and Fermat steps is appropriate.

The main text states the question, assumptions, proof strategy and limitations clearly, and contains the decisive evidence. The abstract is informative. This is a coherent mathematical paper rather than a record of computational searches.

I reproduced the two SymPy controls, `2567_descent_symbolic_check.py` and `2569_period_locus_check.py`; both passed. I inspected the three Sage scripts and their retained outputs but did not rerun them because Sage is unavailable here. This limitation does not leave a gap in the written proofs. The finite-field controls are appropriately described as checks, not completeness evidence.

Two minor improvements would sharpen presentation. In Theorem 5, explicitly note that the fixed $x$-values cannot be branch values of $F\to\mathbf P^1$: a two-torsion point cannot satisfy $Q+R=-Q$ for nonzero $R$. This makes the order-four construction immediately transparent. In §6, report that the retained finite-field controls attain at most one distinct squared spoke in each example; they test the identities and zero-overlap case but do not exercise configurations near the degree bound. Neither point affects acceptance.
