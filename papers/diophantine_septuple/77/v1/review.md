# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the supplied septuple problem. The paper isolates the two disconnected seven-edge graphs $H_{54}$ and $H_{69}$ that remain after the degree, $K_{2,3}$, connected-cycle, and seven-edge reductions from papers 55, 58, 59, 61, and 66, and gives new arithmetic exclusions for both graphs. An independent read-only check of the deposited graph list gives 107 triangle-free isomorphism classes and 20 seven-edge classes, consistent with the stated enumeration and with the two named disconnected survivors after the earlier obstructions are applied.

The reduction to polynomial systems is logically appropriate. The four-cycle equations are used as necessary conditions, the matching corollary supplies the required coverage even when a pair radicand is zero, and the guard in the systems inverts only entries and differences. Thus a rational septuple with either graph would indeed yield a rational point on one of the listed systems. For the characteristic-zero RUR leaves, exact substitution of the original seven $R_5$ windows and nondegeneracy checks are the right forward verification. A rational point would give a rational root of the separating eliminant, so the reported root-free reductions modulo primes exclude rational points in those reported zero-dimensional loci without requiring irreducibility.

The central qualification is material and is correctly acknowledged by the authors. The H54 pivot-6 leaf was not solved in characteristic zero. Its degree $40$ and the polynomial
$$
g_6(X)=(X^2-4/9)(X^2-1792/3249)(X^2+36/25)
$$
are inferred from four modular computations and rational reconstruction. Agreement at four primes is strong evidence, but it is not a completeness or specialization theorem: all four primes could be bad, and the manuscript does not give an explicit coefficient-height bound establishing that the reconstruction is unique. The four exact slices then account for the assumed degree, but only under those two unproved premises. The stated theorem is therefore a conditional exclusion, not a complete proof that these graphs cannot occur over $mathbb Q$, and the version does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition. The title, abstract, and corollary should keep this conditional status prominent; “close” should be understood as “close conditional on the stated computation.”

The supplement makes the reported systems and outputs inspectable, and the exact factor and window checks are substantially more reproducible than a bounded search. I did not rerun the expensive msolve eliminations, so the solver’s omission of components remains an assumption for the H69 leaves, the H54 pivot-2 and pivot-3 leaves, and the four H54 slices. For the pivot-6 case, the authors should add the actual modular degree data, the reconstructed coefficients, and a certified CRT/height bound (or a characteristic-zero degree/completeness certificate). A second independent elimination order or a specialization theorem with identified bad primes would materially strengthen the result. It would also help to state explicitly that the 40 modular points are counted with the needed multiplicities and to document why the chosen primes avoid denominator and leading-coefficient degeneracies.

The novelty claim is credible. Paper 61 supplies a conditional seven-edge upper bound but leaves disconnected seven-edge cases; paper 66 treats connected cyclic graphs and explicitly leaves disconnected cycles open; papers 58 and 60 provide the $K_{2,3}$ obstruction and earlier four-cycle exclusions but do not treat these two graphs. The present systems and the H54/H69 computations are therefore not a cosmetic restatement. I also checked the relevant external arithmetic literature, including Dujella–Kazalicki–Mikić–Szikszai, *There are infinitely many rational Diophantine sextuples* (IMRN 2017, arXiv:1507.00569), and Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević, *Rational Diophantine sextuples containing two regular quadruples and one regular quintuple* (Acta Math. Spalatensia 2020, arXiv:1904.00348). Those papers establish construction mechanisms for sextuples and regular structures, but do not contain this regular-quintuple graph classification or an exclusion of $H_{54}$ or $H_{69}$.

The manuscript is relevant, mathematically readable, and appropriately distinguishes exact verification of listed algebraic points from completeness of the elimination. The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative, though slightly longer than the suggested 200–300-word target. The contribution should be presented as a conditional, reproducible restriction on the remaining seven-edge graph cases; a characteristic-zero certificate for the pivot-6 leaf would be needed before claiming an unconditional exclusion or marking the problem solved.
