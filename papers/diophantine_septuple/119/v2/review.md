# Accept

The revision improves the precision of the accepted contribution without changing its central search result. It distinguishes the level-fitted rates $q_L$ from the seventh-candidate rate $q$, removes the unsupported expectation bound from the face-weight adjustment, explains the conditional nature of the uncertainty intervals, and gives hardware and worker counts for the implementation-specific stopping decision. Replacing “closes by coincidence” with the actual scope of the relation classifier is also appropriate. The corrected comparisons are consistent with the retained lower-size logs.

The two additions to §5.2 are limited supporting computations on the existing almost-septuple population. Testing coordinate lines through the known points complements the previously reported random-line diagnostic. Applying the established relation-type method of Paper 6 [4] to the same 60 examples clarifies which alternatives to the chain remain untested. These additions neither introduce a new construction nor establish a new geometric exclusion; they fit the existing contribution and do not warrant a separate paper. The manuscript retains an identifiable question and argument, with detailed records in the supplement, rather than becoming an unfocused collection of experiments.

I independently checked all 60 rows of the new relation-type data using rational arithmetic: seven distinct nonzero entries, exactly the stated missing square edge, the complete vanishing R3/R4/R5 relation lists, and the exact Jacobian ranks. The tangent-dimension distributions reproduce $\{1:2,2:15,3:26,4:13,5:4\}$ and, with R3 included, $\{1:2,2:19,3:27,4:9,5:3\}$. Independent exhaustive closure checks also found no generating set of at most three good edges in any row whose corresponding tangent dimension is at least two. This supports the stated failure of the $g\leq2$ selection criterion. I did not independently recompute every larger minimum $g$ or rerun the coordinate-resultant factorizations; the latter assessment rests on inspection of the implementation and retained logs. The relevant passages of Papers 6, 72 and 82 [4,26,27] support the attribution and the distinction between a known curve and an entire relation variety.

Three localized wording improvements would make the revised scope fully explicit:

- In §4, “Only the two overall size-five intervals exclude 1” and “all size-six intervals contain it” now need the qualifier **relative to $q_L$**. The newly added size-six interval relative to $q$ at $P=19\mathrm{s}$ is $[0.20,0.77]$, which excludes 1. This does not establish a size-seven effect.
- In §5.2 and §6, retain **tangent dimension** when summarizing the new census. The Jacobian computation gives an upper bound on local dimension, not an equality. Likewise, the generator threshold concerns the specified closure rules, not all possible square-class dependencies.
- Narrow “no septuples on these lines” to the branches and factors retained by the diagnostic. The inherited filter removes factors with zero pair products and factors admitting a common root among $0,e_i,-1/e_i$. A zero square root is allowed by the selected problem, and existence of one excluded common root does not itself exclude another admissible common root. The existing caveat about discarded factors is therefore essential; the new paragraph should not sound like a complete exclusion of every rational specialization on those lines.

These qualifications do not undermine the accepted chain census or its cautious interpretation. The revision provides neither a septuple nor a complete nonexistence proof, so the problem remains unresolved and its verified-solution status should not change.
