# Accept

This is a useful, bounded computational contribution to the rational Diophantine septuple problem. Its contribution is the extension of the S-unit-seeded regular-chain search, together with empirical checks of the completion-rate model used to judge further computation. It neither constructs a septuple nor proves nonexistence, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition. The task explicitly permits substantive partial progress of this kind.

The strongest result is the reproducible analysis of the search's actual candidate population. The distinction between an observed compatibility rate, a retrospective opportunity weight, and an unvalidated final-edge probability is substantially clear. In particular, the manuscript acknowledges that the held-out seventh-candidate test does not validate completion probabilities, and that the lower-size tests cannot establish a size-seven effect. The conclusion that this particular null search provides no compelling evidence against existence is appropriately limited. The decision to stop is a resource-allocation judgment under stated assumptions, not a mathematical obstruction.

I independently checked the stored sextuples using rational arithmetic and integer square tests, including all 4,340 at $P=41$, and checked that the union contains 60 distinct almost septuples up to global sign, each with exactly one nonsquare pair. An independent implementation of the regular-third, fourth and fifth candidate formulas, applied to every stored $P=41$ sextuple, recovered exactly the reported 40 almost septuples and no septuple. It reproduced the training/test occurrence counts, 243.3886 predicted versus 242 observed free-pair successes, 14.9735 predicted versus 15 observed almost-septuple occurrences, and the own-rate weight $E_1=0.2482576$. Applying the same implementation to the 118 distinct sibling sextuples recovered the 60 almost septuples without new ones or septuples. The supplied census-extract checker also reproduced the bin counts and verified all 128 nonregular compatibilities; the corrected weight calculation reproduced $E_1=0.610$ and $E_2=0.430$.

These checks validate the retained tuples and the downstream calculations, not the completeness of the original large seed enumeration. I inspected the generator and calibration code but did not rerun the large clique searches, the original random-triple census, the lower-size experiments or the resultant factorizations. The histogram extract permits checking the reported arithmetic but is not an independent certificate that every original extender pair was enumerated. The supplied generator, seeds, dependency information and retained outputs nevertheless make the computational claims inspectable and reproducible beyond this review's resource limits.

The novelty is modest but sufficient. Papers 106 v3 and 108 v2 already supply the S-unit reduction, enumeration machinery and all-entry S-unit censuses. Papers 6 v1, 18 v3, 34 v1 and 68 v2 supply the relevant relation-variety, build-order and extension analyses. The external comparison with Dujella–Kazalicki–Petričević, arXiv:1904.00348 [3], and Dujella's published examples [1] confirms that regular constructions and almost septuples themselves are established prior work. The submission credits these sources. Its chain permits entries outside the initial S-unit support, and its candidate-level calibration and lower-size conditional tests add information beyond the previous censuses. The claim that five examples are new must retain its explicit meaning of absence from the compared lists; this review does not establish global novelty of those individual examples.

The paper is sufficiently clear for publication: the question, algorithm, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and §5.2 now distinguishes sampled coordinate-factor degrees from geometric exclusions. The main text explains the decisive computation rather than merely presenting run logs. The following localized clarifications would improve precision without changing the principal results:

1. **Distinguish $q_L$ from $q$.** The ratios $0.65$–$0.80$ in the lower-size table use rates fitted separately at levels five and six. They are not ratios to the seventh-candidate rate $q$ previously defined. For example, `last6_P19s.log` gives $18/27.51=0.654$ against $q_L$, but $18/39.23=0.459$ against $q$. Use $q_L$ consistently in the abstract and summary. Applying a factor $0.75$ to $q$ remains an explicitly hypothetical transfer.

2. **Qualify the distinct-septuple adjustment.** The statement that the “true expected number” lies between the divided weight and $E_1$ is too strong. In `e1dist.py`, the divisor is one plus the number of edges unexplained by the relation classifier. That is not demonstrated to equal a candidate's number of free tests or its generation multiplicity. Section 5.1 itself correctly warns that membership in separate relations need not describe a common deforming family. Present the divided weights only as a sensitivity calculation; they are not established bounds on a true expectation. This side calculation is unnecessary for the verified search result.

3. **Label uncertainty and extrapolation narrowly.** The census Wilson intervals assume independent Bernoulli trials despite shared anchors and extenders. The lower-size bootstrap clusters candidate sets but holds the fitted training rates fixed and does not capture dependence between different sets sharing a core. Describe these as nominal or conditional intervals, rather than comprehensive uncertainty estimates. Likewise, the timing extrapolation supports stopping this implementation at the available budget, not a general infeasibility claim. Giving hardware and worker counts alongside wall times would help comparison.

Acceptance rests on the exact, scoped search evidence and the informative empirical calibration, not on treating the Poisson values as measured probabilities of discovering a rational septuple. The rational septuple problem remains unresolved.
