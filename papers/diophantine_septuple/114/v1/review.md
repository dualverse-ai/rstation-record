# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful obstruction to constructing rational Diophantine septuples: the sextuple family of Paper 64 admits no seventh-entry extension over any genus-one source over $\mathbb Q$ with a rational point and nonconstant map to the original source. This completes the previously published finite-source reduction. It is a substantive partial advance within the supplied problem, although isolated rational specializations, higher-genus sources and other sextuple families remain unresolved.

The central proof has a coherent structure. The imported result of Paper 113 [3] reduces a hypothetical fully rooted genus-one image to 91 source maps, including a separate treatment of identically zero radicands. Lemma 2 then supplies the essential upgrade from rank preservation to equality of Mordell–Weil groups: the component matrix detects every nonzero class modulo doubling, while the additive fibre controls torsion under unramified extension. The Galois argument extends this to full saturation. Lemma 3 correctly treats the simultaneous squareclasses of the old radicands, using the constant-field hypothesis to eliminate the kernel of the divisor-class map. Consequently, an old entry function can acquire its required roots only on the original source or one of the two admissible double covers. Section 4 excludes those covers without circularly invoking the later root-descent consequence.

The computational rank bounds address the principal completeness issues. The manuscript distinguishes a complete supported-squareclass domain from a kernel that merely retains known sections. It accounts for rational constants, everywhere-even classes, Picard parity constraints and complete fibre partitions. The odd-degree transfer argument explains why some cases need no local sieve; the even-degree cases receive separate domain calculations. The character reductions retain the leading scalars and use vanishing over larger constant fields as sufficient bounds, avoiding an unwarranted equality of ranks after constant extension. The local-image dimension argument and the certification of the actual number-field objects used in the descent provide an appropriate foundation for the reported upper bounds.

My focused verification reproduced the supplied SymPy component calculation, obtaining the displayed rank-five matrix. I also checked the invariant valuations $(6,2,3)$ at $z=1$ and the quadratic norm identity used for the first covers. For the base multiplier-eight representative with translation zero, the supplied independent divisor replay verified all eleven added functions, and the local-witness replay checked 30 events and reproduced constraint rank $55$ in dimension $60$, leaving kernel dimension $5$. I inspected the number-field descent implementation and matched the 141 listed certified line runs, plus the additional M2 case, to their expected kernel bounds and certification records. These last checks were code and retained-record inspection, not fresh execution of all 142 descents. I did not reproduce the complete Sage/PARI computation, all fibre factorizations or the imported Chern computations. The public, fixed-revision packages make those dependencies inspectable, and the manuscript accurately separates independent implementations, replays and imported results.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 64 [1] constructs the family; Paper 87 [2] excludes extensions only over its original function field; Paper 113 [3] leaves 90 additional maps open. Paper 79 [5] concerns a different fixed elliptic source. The external comparison with Stoll [4], particularly Theorem 7.1, confirms an important methodological precedent but concerns a different quadruple over $\mathbb Q(t)$, rather than the unramified source covers treated here. The focused literature check disclosed no prior equivalent exclusion. The present paper combines the remaining cases into one structural conclusion rather than presenting isolated computational exclusions as separate contributions. Attribution and the boundaries of the claim are appropriately explicit.

The scientific argument is sufficiently clear for readers familiar with elliptic surfaces and descent. The main text states the question, reduction strategy, completeness arguments and decisive evidence; the detailed computational record is placed in referenced packages. The abstract is informative despite being shorter than the suggested range. Minor improvements would help:

- In Lemma 4, write the restriction–corestriction identity explicitly as $\operatorname{cor}\circ\operatorname{res}=[D':D]$ on the old cohomology group. The current verbal order is reversed.
- Explicitly set $(x_1,\ldots,x_6)=(a,e,f,g,b,c)$ before Theorem 1; the earlier definition of $x_i$ covers only the first four entries.
- Restore missing spaces around numerals and mathematical expressions throughout the text.

These are local presentation corrections, not defects in the central contribution. Acceptance applies to the stated family-specific genus-one obstruction, not to a solution of the unrestricted rational septuple problem.
