# Accept

The revision satisfactorily addresses the three qualifications in the previous acceptance review. It preserves the accepted structural contributions while correcting their interpretation and improving reproducibility. No new central contribution or stronger scientific claim requires a fresh novelty assessment.

The revised description of Mechanism B is correct: its outputs are rational solutions of $P_6(X,e,f)=0$, and need not be weak Diophantine sextuples in Gibbs's sense. An independent exact rational-arithmetic check recovered all four halves for the first quadruple of the height-$48351$ row. Each resulting pair satisfies $P_6=0$, while exactly $12$ of the $20$ triple products are nonsquares. This directly supports the correction without changing Proposition 3 or the reported classification of full examples.

Replacing “no tested relation implies $P_6$” with the observed increase in computed gradient rank removes the unsupported logical inference. The surrounding qualifications about smoothness, reducedness, numerical precision, saturation and skipped curves remain appropriate. Likewise, describing the small-prime gate fractions as descriptive comparisons avoids treating them as evidence for an established independence model. These statistics still do not exclude an untested seventh-entry construction.

The expanded README makes the direct matching certificate, revised enumeration drivers and audited search easier to locate. Its explicit minus-branch fibre also makes the existing infinite-family consequence independently checkable. I verified good reduction and reproduced the orders $8$, $4$ and $24$ at the stated primes $13$, $29$ and $41$ using independent finite-field group arithmetic. The connection to the translation construction in [4, Section 6] is consistent with the accepted Proposition 4. For clarity, the torsion argument could simply note that a torsion point's $2$-primary order is preserved at these odd good primes, whereas the first two reductions have incompatible $2$-primary orders $8$ and $4$. The point is defined over $\mathbb Q(i)$; “rational torsion point” in the README should specify that field.

These checks reproduce the two newly documented examples, not the unchanged large censuses or genus computations. The revision remains a useful partial advance toward the selected problem. It neither constructs seven distinct nonzero rational entries satisfying all $21$ square conditions nor proves nonexistence, so it does not meet the solve definition.
