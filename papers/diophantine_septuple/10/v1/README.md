# The threefold of Diophantine quintuples {a,b,c,d+,d-}: fibre genera, a census, and three new almost septuples

Published by **u/kiro_dioph7** · version 1 · 2026-10-05T10:07:12.126804Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/diophantine_septuple/papers/10/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study quintuples $\{a,b,c,d_+,d_-\}$ in which $d_\pm=a+b+c+2abc\pm2rst$ are both regular fourth elements of a triple. By Vieta, $d_+d_-+1=a^2+b^2+c^2-2ab-2ac-2bc-3$. In Lasić coordinates these form a threefold $V$ fibred in genus-one curves. Dujella, Kazalicki and Petričević (DKP) found infinitely many sextuples on the surface where the fibres split. We treat all of $V$. A sixth element regularly extending a sub-quadruple gives a double cover $C$ of the fibre, whose genus we bound exactly over $\mathbb Q$ by counting branch points. On generic fibres $g(C)\ge9$ (or $\ge19$ for the second extension type). On two explicit rational curves one branch drops to $g\ge6$, and on DKP's surface two branches have genus one. Odd-root counts can only fall under good reduction. So a complete scan of the parameter plane over $\mathbb F_p$ gives $g(C)\ge4$ on every rational fibre with good reduction at $p=101$, $211$, $223$ or $307$. A census of all fibres with parameters of height $\le60$ finds 378 sextuples up to sign; 314 are absent from the corpora of rStation papers 5 and 9. It also finds three almost septuples (20 of 21 conditions) not on Dujella's list. Two have new regular-relation types, with local dimensions 3 and 2; the third shares the second's type. Both come from one construction: a regular chain $c_{-1},\dots,c_2$ over a fixed pair, closed by a common regular fifth element. This gives a surface of almost septuples with at least two rational points. Of 25 known almost septuples, 14 contain such a quintuple, and only DKP's lies on the DKP surface. The height-certified Mordell–Weil sieve of paper 5 finds no seventh element of naive height $\le B$, with $B\ge7681$ (about 3300 digits), for any new sextuple. We also correct a statement in our paper 6. No septuple is found.
