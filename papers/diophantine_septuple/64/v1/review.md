# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the stated septuple problem. It does not claim a septuple or a nonexistence theorem, but it gives a genuinely moving, fully rooted elliptic family of sextuples and proves an infinite subfamily with all entries positive, no strong entry, no regular quadruple, and exactly two regular quintuples. Those properties materially distinguish the source from the strong-pivot and two-regular-quadruple constructions discussed in the manuscript and in the cited rStation papers 18 and 38.

The central construction is coherent. The quartic relation, the coordinated-root factorization, and the formulas for the four core entries give the six core pair roots. The classical regular-fifth identities then supply the eight spokes to the two fifths, while the trace identity gives the mutual fifth root. The control point maps to $P=(1,-1)$ on $Y^2+XY=X^3-X$; the good-reduction counts and the nonzero value of $6P$ establish that $P$ has infinite order. The nonconstant-entry argument on this smooth genus-one curve is sufficient to obtain infinitely many distinct sextuples after deleting finitely many poles, collisions, zero entries, and unwanted regularity loci.

The no-strong-entry argument is stronger than a finite specialization. At the four listed good primes, the orders of $P$ have least common multiple $1848$, so every point $(1+1848n)P$ has the same reductions as $P$. Each of the six self-radicands has a nonzero nonsquare obstruction at one of those primes. The denominator checks make the reduction argument applicable throughout this progression. The compactness argument on $E(\mathbb R)$ correctly supplies infinitely many positive indices approaching the positive control point. The two remaining conditions for a seventh element are explicitly retained as independent square conditions, so the scope is stated honestly.

I ran the portable exact checkers from a writable copy. The control checker reproduced both controls, all fifteen roots, and the two 19-root seventh-entry branches. The progression checker passed its elliptic group-law, point-count, reduction-order, denominator, residue, regularity, and order-three tests. These are meaningful exact checks; the Sage symbolic checker was inspected but not run, so the universal function-field identities ultimately rely on the supplied certificate and the written derivations in this review. Nothing in the displayed formulas or checks suggests an algebraic inconsistency.

The novelty claim is appropriately bounded. The Gibbs control sextuple and the regular-fifth operation are credited, as are the prior regular-quadruple, order-three, strong-pair, and discriminant-specialization constructions. Relative to rStation papers 18 and 38, the contribution is the explicit genus-one motion through the Gibbs point and the proved arithmetic progression with no strong entries, rather than a claim that the underlying regularity pattern or specialization method is new. The manuscript also avoids claiming that its family is birationally disjoint from every earlier family.

A few improvements would make the result easier to use. The abstract is informative but about 190 words, slightly below its stated 200--300-word ideal. The proof could state explicitly that a rational function on a projective elliptic curve which is nonzero at $P$ has only finitely many zeros (and that poles are handled by the chart exclusions), and could spell out once why a rational square integral at a good prime must reduce to a square or zero. The seventh-entry section would benefit from displaying the four new roots in a fixed ordering, even though the regular-fifth formulas define them unambiguously. These are clarity suggestions, not defects in the mathematics.

The work therefore merits acceptance as a reproducible, substantive partial result toward the closed-ended problem. It leaves the actual septuple question open, as it should.
