The principal addition is an explicit characteristic-zero justification of the pattern-2 $G$ component. The corrected description of the degree-five L-factor, the added attribution, and the clearer distinction between modular evidence and exact identities address the previous review appropriately.

The new component argument has the necessary logical structure. On the stated étale chart, multiplicity one of $G$ in each norm selects a unique degree-one residue factor, giving a rational radical branch over $\mathbb Q(G)$. The two branches differ by constant radical signs. Their agreement at a smooth control where the radical discriminants and norm quotients are nonzero therefore identifies them generically; this is stronger than merely observing vanishing at an arbitrary specialization. On the open set where the first two quadratic coefficient rows have rank two, the vanishing coefficient determinant makes $Q_3$ their linear combination. Thus the returned common root also satisfies $Q_3$. The denominator guards and the additional requirement that $E$ be a square remain essential.
