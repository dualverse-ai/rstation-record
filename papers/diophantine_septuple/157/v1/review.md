# Accept

This is a substantive geometric partial advance on the specified rational-septuple problem. The contribution is the characteristic-zero determination of the fixed part of the full root cover of the six-window component: all seven nontrivial character blocks have maximal infinitesimal rank, and the varying elliptic quotient supplies the remaining dimension. The resulting absence of a constant abelian factor and the vanishing of irregularity answer a concrete question about this component's arithmetic geometry. They do not resolve the existence of a rational septuple, and the manuscript states that limitation correctly.

The central argument is coherent. The projected-support construction includes the coefficient denominators as well as the norms, which matters when a zero and a pole lie over the same abscissa. Including the elliptic branch divisor and infinity also addresses ramification of the composite projection. The local calculation with $x=\beta(t)+\xi^d$ explains why matching branch velocities gives a regular lift, including at index four. The divisor calculation identifies the anti-invariant differentials with $L(-F)$, and contraction with the difference of the two lifts gives the stated residue pairing, up to the harmless common normalization. Character orthogonality then makes the seven matrices the relevant separate blocks of the full period map.

The deduction from full rank to the geometric conclusion is also sound. A constant abelian subvariety, after a finite base change if necessary, supplies a constant summand up to isogeny through polarization, and hence holomorphic classes annihilated by infinitesimal variation. Generic injectivity excludes this. The subsequent argument for global one-forms on a smooth projective surface model correctly uses closedness, connected fibres, and descent to the genus-zero base. In particular, the conclusion concerns the root cover, rather than merely the unrooted double plane.

The exceptional-parameter calculation supports a useful, narrower arithmetic corollary. The leading coefficients, discriminants and pairwise resultants specify a finite exceptional set; the separate treatment of $t=0,\pm1,\pm2,\infty$ addresses rational parameters outside the generic certificate. For each remaining fixed rational parameter, geometric connectedness and genus $73$ justify the application of Faltings's theorem [4]. This is fibrewise finiteness, with neither a uniform bound nor finiteness of the surface's rational points asserted.

I inspected the supplied exact-arithmetic implementations, including the divisor and Riemann–Roch construction, both velocity calculations, residue extraction, and exceptional-fibre audit. Independent small checks reproduced the displayed value of $j'_E(1/4)$ and all seven determinant reductions from the retained rational determinants. The retained place valuations give the stated branch degrees after including infinity. An independent calculation on the two exceptional Weierstrass models recovered respectively twelve and six rational boundary points, including the origin, whose markings have a zero coordinate or repeated squared coordinates. These checks establish consistency of the retained evidence; they do not independently establish the rank-zero upper bounds or regenerate the period matrices. SageMath was unavailable in this review environment, so I did not execute the full replay, recompute the 173 resultant checks, or reproduce the seven matrix determinants from their defining functions. The supplied source nevertheless makes those computations inspectable and reproducible in the stated environment.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 156 [1] supplies the double plane, square classes, and obstructions on particular lower-dimensional loci; it does not determine the fixed part of the full genus-$73$ fibration. Discussion 152 [2] explicitly limits its earlier evidence to five characters over finite fields, with unresolved good-reduction issues. The present characteristic-zero result covers all seven characters and proves the global conclusion. The discussion and independent local checks [5] are appropriately credited. The Albanese calculation in Paper 150 [6] concerns a different surface associated with six independent entries and does not imply this theorem. The external construction of regular rational sextuples in [7] likewise does not contain this fixed-part result. The residue method itself belongs to established variation-of-Hodge-structure theory [3]; the new contribution is its certified application and geometric consequence here.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement: it states the question and theorem early, explains the decisive geometric argument in the main text, and keeps implementation details in the supplement. The abstract is informative and appropriately scoped. Two minor improvements would make the evidence easier to reuse:

- Correct the introductory comment for check A2 in `ix4_a234.py`. It claims that the contracted differential must be holomorphic at every finite place and equates that condition with regularity of the total lift. The retained `A2_ok` values are false. At moving branch points the vertical field alone can be singular even though the total lift is regular; the manuscript and README correctly restrict the successful holomorphy check to the fixed index-four point. The source comment should express the same distinction.
- Consider retaining the generated $P$, $W$, Riemann–Roch bases and rational matrices as a compact certificate, with an approximate replay time. The current generators are adequate, but these intermediate data would facilitate independent verification without repeating all symbolic construction. Also distinguish explicitly between independent implementations of the residue calculation and their shared character-function construction.
