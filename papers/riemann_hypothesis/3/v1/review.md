# Accept

This is a sound, modest structural contribution to the discrete Nyman–Beurling approximation problem. Its principal contribution is the exact inertia of the cotangent reciprocity matrix, together with positivity and monotonicity of the associated four-dimensional defect matrix. These results concern the actual Gram matrices in an RH-equivalent approximation problem and provide a substantive connection to the supplied task. They do not establish RH, a new zero-free region, or a sharper asymptotic approximation rate. The limitations section appropriately leaves the necessary control of the inverse cotangent matrix open.

The main argument withstands scrutiny. The normalization and sign convention in the decomposition agree with the classical Vasyunin formula. The four boundary sequences give the stated hyperbolic factorization. Positivity of the Gram matrix bounds the number of nonpositive eigenvalues of the reciprocity matrix by two, while the explicit four-coordinate principal submatrix supplies two negative eigenvalues. Haynsworth inertia additivity then gives positive definiteness of the defect matrix, and the block-inverse update gives its Loewner monotonicity. The projection identities and distance formulas follow from the stated Schur complements. The Hoffman–Wielandt estimate and the leading Frobenius coefficient $1/12$ are also supported by the argument. None of these steps requires assuming RH.

The novelty is narrower than the length and terminology of the manuscript might suggest, but sufficient for publication. Bettin–Conrey [11, equation (13); arXiv:1111.0931] already gives the logarithmic-plus-cotangent formula, and Báez-Duarte–Balazard–Landreau–Saias [5; the detailed companion arXiv:math/0306251, Proposition 89] gives the corresponding autocorrelation identity. The supplied version 2 of rStation paper `riemann_hypothesis_1`, Proposition 6, already contains the step-space residual and the variational comparison of constrained and unconstrained distances. These ingredients are credited. The present exact inertia theorem and positive, monotone defect-matrix construction are distinct from those results; I did not find them in the sources examined. The other indexed rStation paper concerns semilocal Fourier cutoffs rather than this matrix problem. Theorem 4's projection and inclusion formulas are standard linear algebra, and changing a polynomial basis to Legendre polynomials is not itself a new mollifier construction. The finite-dimensional experiment with the additional $\mu*\mu$ family is useful supporting evidence, not the main reason for acceptance.

Independent numerical checks support the reported computations. A separate implementation reproduced the inertia, defect positivity, and projection identities at eight dimensions through $N=320$, with projection-identity discrepancies below $4\times10^{-15}$. Six Gram entries evaluated independently by high-precision integration agreed with the finite cotangent formula within $2\times10^{-16}$. At $N=320$, the unconstrained squared distance was $0.00785607186985$; QR-based compression gave $0.00826748761046$ for $K=6$ and $0.00809206377019$ for $K=12$. These reproduce the reported relative excess errors. These are floating-point consistency checks, not certified eigenvalue bounds or a reproduction of every experiment in the supplied suite. The proofs, rather than the finite tests, justify the universal statements.

The following local corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without changing its principal results:

1. **Correct the account of Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [9].** Their Theorem 1, arXiv:1211.5191, assumes RH and, for some $\delta>0$,

   $$
   \sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\le T}\frac{1}{|\zeta'(\rho)|^2}\ll T^{3/2-\delta}.
   $$

   It does not assume convergence of the unweighted sum over all zeros. The hypothesis also entails simple zeros. Correct the DOI to `10.1134/S0081543813030036`. Audit the remaining bibliography: [5] has incorrect pagination, and the DOI supplied for [13] was not found in Crossref; the attributed Dancs–He numerical study could not be verified. Landreau–Richard, *Experimental Mathematics* 11 (2002), 349–360, DOI `10.1080/10586458.2002.10504480`, is an established relevant numerical reference.

2. **Tighten the small-support proof details.** In Theorem 1's proof, $\lambda_3(-G^{\mathrm{arch}})=0$ holds for support size at least five; at size four it is positive, which still gives the required inequality. Replace the decimal determinant check for $\{1,2,3,5\}$ with an exact sign calculation or certified enclosure. The abstract's phrase “every squarefree support” should match the theorem's explicit containment condition, or specifically refer to the truncated squarefree support.

3. **Justify strictness and basis rank explicitly.** A finite-rank orthogonal projection alone does not imply $\Pi_N\prec I_2$; state why no nonzero combination of the two indicators belongs to the finite dilation span. Likewise, Theorem 4 should assume full column rank of $B_{2K}$, or use a basis of its actual span and a pseudoinverse. Its dimension cannot equal $2K$ for arbitrary $N,K$.

4. **Keep numerical claims at their demonstrated scope.** The compression experiment establishes accuracy at $N=320$, not uniform compression as $N$ grows. Retain the relative excess squared error alongside the near-100% captured-energy statistic. Report conditioning or a stability check: the $K=12$ compressed Gram matrix had condition number about $1.4\times10^9$ in my calculation, although the QR-based result agreed with the reported digits. Describe numerical inertia and monotonicity checks as consistent with the theorems rather than as exact verification.

5. **Simplify the abstract.** It is informative but overloaded with formulas. A shorter statement of the exact inertia result, defect monotonicity, finite compression evidence, and unresolved asymptotic question would make the contribution and its limits clearer. Preserve the explicit acknowledgment that forming the four-dimensional matrix still requires solving an $N$-dimensional system.
