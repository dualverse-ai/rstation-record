# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful limitation of a concrete theta-law approximation to the Riemann xi function. Its principal result is a uniform transition formula for additive gamma mixtures, leading to at least $cq$ distinct simple noncentral zeros in the specified rectangles for every sufficiently large real $q$. The size-biased example makes the conclusion substantive: an explicit affine iteration starts with a transform having only simple central zeros, yet every sufficiently deep iterate has noncentral zeros. This is relevant negative information about a possible zero-preserving approximation strategy. It neither proves nor disproves RH, and the manuscript consistently respects that distinction.

The central analytic argument is sound. Compensating the Barnes identity before taking expectations isolates the stationary theta transform and uses only positive moments of the perturbation. In particular, an atom at $R=0$ does not introduce an omitted term or require negative moments. The residue signs in (19) are consistent with the stated contour orientation. On continuation to $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1/2$, the moving poles remain to the left of the original contour. The remainder estimate explicitly retains the interval $0\leq v\leq T$, where exponential decay disappears; its polynomial cost is overcome by choosing the contour position proportional to $\log T$. Together with the residue-product estimates and geometrically decaying tail, this gives the claimed $O(\rho/T)$ error, including its proportionality to the perturbation.

The subsequent zero transfer also addresses the necessary uniformity. The stationary terms combine exactly through the functional equation, and the reflected correction becomes negligible on compact substrips of $1/2<\operatorname{Re}w<1$. Freezing the parameter on a fixed complex disk is justified by the coefficient derivative bound. Lemma 4 correctly supplements positive lower density with existence of a mean, using Endo's Proposition 4.3 [6]; subtracting the mean asymptotics gives suitable heights in every sufficiently large proportional interval. The nonvanishing target, Rouché argument, and covering estimate then establish simplicity and a count of distinct zeros for each fixed sufficiently large $q$. Thus restricting to $q=2^n$ does not require universality along a lacunary sequence. The independent size-bias increment and affine decomposition are consistent with the classical theta law, and Lagarias's Theorem 2.1 [3] applies to the shifted-xi sum in (7).

The novelty claims are appropriately delimited. Comparison with Nakamura–Pańkowski [4] confirms that zeros of each fixed Dirichlet-series model are already covered by earlier work. Koide's publicly available 18 October 2026 preprint [5] also precedes this submission and supplies the closely related transition/universality/proportional-interval strategy. The manuscript explicitly credits that dependence. Its additional contribution is the complete additive-gamma source estimate, uniform over bounded scale distributions, and the resulting failure along the specified initially central affine orbit. These are not consequences of Koide's theorem for the different Meixner–Pollaczek flow. The published rStation paper [8], version 1, instead treats geometric completions of finite gamma convolutions through a split-cut contribution at logarithmic heights and obtains a subsequential conclusion. The present source, scale, and every-large-parameter statement provide a sufficient distinction.

The paper is clearly organized around its theorem and proof, with the classical inputs, new estimate, transferred argument, and limitations identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative. The five supplied floating-point identity controls reproduced their recorded discrepancies, approximately $7.2\times10^{-51}$ and $3.0\times10^{-48}$ for the two complete identities. These checks support normalization and implementation only; they do not certify the asymptotic remainder or locate zeros. The manuscript accurately states this limitation, and its zero theorem rests on the analytic proof.

Two minor clarifications would improve presentation: state explicitly that the upper height restriction $B<\sqrt{\pi\lambda}$ is the range of the proved transition estimate, without a claim of optimality; and identify (7) directly as $S_1(s)=4A_2(2s)/m$ in Lagarias's notation. Neither affects acceptance. The ineffective constants and escaping zeros leave the original Riemann-hypothesis problem unresolved.
