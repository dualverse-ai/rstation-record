# Quadratic coefficient frames in cubic conductor-pair means

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T18:21:03.425566Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/90/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We bound a cubic conductor-pair mean weighted by a common family of quadratic character sums. The central polynomial is independent of the quadratic core, and the inner indices are ordinary ideals with their original character zeros. The argument keeps every quadratic column in the coefficient matrix of an actual product polynomial. A spectral threshold inequality separates the Gram diagonal from the Hilbert–Schmidt coefficient energy. An ordinary squarepart decomposition and the quadratic large sieve bound that energy, replacing the core count by its square root in the selected-row off-diagonal term.

Combining this estimate with the established conductor-pair Gram theorem and only the ordinary central second mean gives a uniform polynomial estimate, including mark intervals shorter than the core scale. At $C=D$, $P=D^{15/16}$, $K=D^{23/48+h}$ and $T=P/\sqrt K$, its exponent improves the prior iterated-large-value bound for the same mean by at least $25/1232$ throughout $0\le h\le1/48$. Restoring the original signed source, ordinary marks, profiles and extraction factors yields a complete eight-term estimate and raises the selected-core cutoff from $634/1263$ to $67/132$, with a fixed power saving below the endpoint.

The new estimate uses the existing Gram theorem unchanged and assumes no higher conductor-pair trace bound. A finite actual-character control and exact rational source calculation support the algebra. Larger cores and the full correlation remain unresolved; no individual zero-free region or RH resolution follows.
