# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful new bound for the weighted conductor-pair mean (1.3), and carries its improvement through the previously defined signed source. The contribution is substantive: keeping the quadratic cores together in the product coefficient matrix reduces the coefficient cost in the selected-row off-diagonal estimate from $K$ to $\sqrt K$ when $K\le P$. This yields the stated improvement over [1] and increases the complete selected-core cutoff from $634/1263$ to $67/132$. The result is a partial advance on the correlation estimates motivating this line of work, not a proof of RH or a new individual zero-free region. The manuscript states these limitations accurately.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 2.1 correctly compares the positive eigenvalues above the diagonal threshold and then applies Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz; it does not require operator monotonicity of the positive-part map. In Lemma 2.2, the decomposition $r=e^2u$ partitions the ordinary rows. The deletion $\mathbf1_{(e,\kappa)=1}$ is a column contraction within each block, so the squarefree quadratic large sieve applies without introducing a false coprimality condition between $e$ and $u$. Summing the block Hilbert–Schmidt bounds gives the claimed $\sqrt K+K/\sqrt P$ estimate. The product map in (2.7) retains all product coincidences, and its divisor-bounded operator norm gives both coefficient estimates in (2.8). Consequently Proposition 3.1 follows from the imported Gram bound for an arbitrary selected subset, with the original character zeros intact.

The integration in Section 4 handles small and large amplitudes and gives the exponents in (1.5). The separate argument for $P<K\le C$ is valid and matters for the source extraction. I compared the imported Gram estimate, ordinary means, source normalization, and block comparisons with the specified version of [1]. Their hypotheses and normalizations agree with the uses here. In Section 5, the nonnegative inner-length powers allow shortened extraction daughters; the annular factors are retained before comparing at $\Delta=\Lambda=1$. The two interpolations in the table give $D^{49/26}K^{11/26}$ and $D^{40/21}K^{11/28}$ as claimed. The latter is the unique binding term at $R=D^{67/132}$, while every other term has positive margin below $D^{101/48}$. Thus a fixed saving below the endpoint follows, with no endpoint saving implied.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. Papers [4] and [5] supply the earlier crossings and source decomposition; [1] supplies the conductor-pair Gram theorem and the stronger iterated benchmark used in the comparison. Paper [6] estimates Schatten moments of a different cubic character matrix and its second-mark row energy, while [7] concerns prescribed coupled products with different coefficient restrictions. These do not duplicate the present selected mixed moment. I also checked the external statements of Goldmakher–Louvel [2, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2] and de Faveri [3, Proposition 8.1]. They support the imported sieve estimates but do not state the varying-quadratic-core weighted bound proved here. The focused external literature check did not identify a substantially overlapping result. The manuscript credits its arithmetic inputs and standard matrix inequalities rather than claiming them as new.

The evidence is reproducible at its stated scope. I reproduced both supplied controls: the actual-character product identity had maximum residual approximately $1.39\times10^{-15}$, and the selected-row matrix and grouping checks passed. The exact rational program reproduced the eight source terms, all endpoint margins, and the minimum exponent improvement $25/1232$. These checks corroborate finite identities and exponent algebra; they do not independently certify the asymptotic sieve estimates, Poisson tails, or profile uniformity. Acceptance rests on the analytic argument and its cited inputs, not on numerical extrapolation.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement. The question, mechanism, theorem, proof, comparison, and unresolved scope are identifiable in the main text. The figure usefully distinguishes an improvement in proved upper bounds from measured character-sum behavior, and the abstract is informative. Two minor additions would help readers: explain briefly why $D^{101/48}$ is the relevant source budget and how the selected source enters the motivating RH program; and add the short squarepart/Minkowski justification, or a precise citation to [1, (2.3)], for the ordinary quadratic mean used in (4.6). Neither omission prevents following the proof or changes the decision.
