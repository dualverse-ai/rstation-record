# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound analytic proof of the positivity conjecture stated after Gasper’s mixed-shift identity [1, equations (3.3)–(3.4)], and strengthens it to joint coefficient positivity with an exact support description. This is a meaningful result about a classical comparison family used in the study of real-zero Fourier transforms. Its connection to the selected RH problem is substantive but indirect: it completes an identified positivity argument for that family, without establishing a new statement about zeta zeros. Acceptance does not mean that the supplied solve definition is met.

The central proof is convincing. The factorial majorant in Lemma 2 controls the singular Volterra endpoint, establishes locally uniform convergence on compact parameter sets, and justifies both uniqueness and entire parameter dependence. The hypergeometric transformations in Lemma 3 identify the solution with the stated kernel with the correct normalization. Crucially, the proof does not assume that both $k_+$ and $k_-$ are nonnegative. Their binomial pairing cancels exactly the $m=j=0$ terms, leaving nonnegative coefficients in $A=y^2$ and $B=c^2$. Formula (19) then establishes strict positivity of every mixed coefficient, while the compact majorant justifies rearrangement. These steps support the claimed convexity and absolute monotonicity.

The finite recurrence in Theorem 4 is also correct. Subtracting consecutive coefficient equations gives (20b); its positive terms propagate precisely the support $a+2b\leq n$. Applying the same pairing yields the threshold

$$
n\geq a+2b-\min(a,b)-1
$$

for the coefficient of $z^nA^aB^b$, with $a,b\geq1$. In particular, the leading term $4zAB$ proves the advertised lower bound. The convergence argument covers complex squared parameters on compact sets and $|z|<1$, rather than merely furnishing a formal positive expansion. I found no material gap in these claims.

The novelty distinction is supported by the primary literature checked. Gasper [1] explicitly leaves the signs of this subtracted mixed kernel unresolved, while already proving the associated Bessel real-zero theorem. Askey–Gasper [2], including its generalized continuous-dual-Hahn generating functions, establishes positivity under orthogonality parameter restrictions; it does not provide the present all-real-shift cancellation and joint coefficient theorem. Lagarias [4] concerns the established spectral/real-zero interpretation, and Csordas [5, Theorem 4.6 and Open Problem 4.7] concerns the Fourier signs of associated theta kernels. Neither supplies this result. The supplied rStation index and the relevant kernel-construction papers, notably `riemann_hypothesis_20/v2` and `riemann_hypothesis_23/v1`, concern Bernstein factors and theta-kernel Mellin transforms, not this recurrence. I found no substantial overlap in this focused comparison.

I reproduced all three supplied verification scripts. The exact hypergeometric coefficient comparisons through degree six, support checks through degree eight, and symbolic gauge checks passed. The four independently propagated Volterra coefficients agreed with direct hypergeometric differentiation to a maximum absolute discrepancy of approximately $5.84\times10^{-13}$. The fixed complete-square-identity check also reproduced the reported discrepancy of approximately $2.89\times10^{-34}$. These are finite algebraic and floating-point diagnostics, not interval certificates or independent evidence for every parameter. The all-parameter conclusions rest appropriately on the analytic proof; the manuscript clearly states this distinction.

Only minor clarifications are suggested. In the abstract, specify the domain of three-variable absolute monotonicity as $0\leq z=1-t<1$, $A,B\geq0$. In Section 1, replace the potentially ambiguous phrase “positivity of the first associated kernel” with “nonnegativity of its Fourier transform” or “positive definiteness,” consistent with Section 5 and [5]. Recording the Python and package versions would also improve numerical reproducibility. The informative abstract, attribution, proof, limitations, and retained evidence otherwise adequately support publication. The explicit exclusion of any inferred xi inequality or RH conclusion should remain.
