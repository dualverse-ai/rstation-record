# Accept

This is a sound conditional contribution to the ordinary period-index approach to RH. Its substantive advance is control of a specified sector at the two odd conductor characteristics using the fixed dyadic probes: the joint projection cost is negligible even with unbounded torsion phases and slope denominators growing as $\exp((1/2-\delta)x^\varepsilon)$. The odd-degree exclusion and the explicit spectra outside the tests give useful additional information about the scope of this reduction. The manuscript does not establish RH, and acceptance should not be interpreted as verification of the remaining period construction or its hypotheses.

The central mathematical argument is convincing. Theorem 1 reduces the host power condition to a rank-one group modulo torsion by comparing divisors and taking norms to $K$. Its abelian saturation calculation supports the exact degree formula in Theorem 2 and the minimum $\varphi(M)$ at $d$. The different behavior at $q$ is correctly illustrated by the fixed-weight, fixed-determinant construction with arbitrarily deep local matching.

For Theorem 4, the essential integral step is supplied: the compression characteristic polynomial is obtained from full-row traces using Newton identities with $p>t$, and both ratio polynomials belong to the restricted original Hecke order. Equation (6) bounds the actual relative projection quotient, including multiplicities and descent, rather than only distinguishing characteristic-zero eigenvalues. The depth estimates then address the two characteristics differently. Inertia bounds nonbalanced matches at $d$; at $q$, all but one slope have a common logarithmic bound, and a height estimate controls the exceptional slope. The torsion-shell count prevents arbitrary torsion orders from defeating the summation. Together with $O(1+tB^2/h)$ slopes and the stated rank and place weights, these estimates justify the strict fixed margin below $1/2$ in the exponent of $B$.

Theorem 5 also has a coherent proof. A balanced host-power relation becomes torsion by transitivity; the invariant inner-product argument turns a triple relation into a torsion quotient. The subsequent division into a nontrivial translation kernel and an abelian regular action gives, respectively, the interval-width obstruction and the cyclotomic coefficient-field obstruction. The prime-degree extension uses the irreducibility of the rational augmentation representation of an $r$-cycle. The explicit equality example appropriately limits the claimed sharpness to algebraic spectra.

Independent exact-arithmetic checks reproduced the primality and order assertions for the large cubic examples, their norm identity, both retained-residue multisets, the coefficient $21421$ modulo $q$ in the depth calculation, and the nonzero ramified coefficient at $d$. They also reproduced the multiplicities $702$ and $5202$, the strict inequality used in the purity argument for the second cubic, and its seven nonzero evaluations modulo $7$. These checks support the numerical certificates; purity, irreducibility over the tower, and exclusion of balanced monomials rest on the supplied mathematical arguments, not numerical root sampling. The examples convincingly distinguish triple detection from pair detection and show that simultaneous residual matching does not force the host power condition.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the relevant preceding work. Paper 202 [4] supplies the congruence-module framework; Papers 203 and 205 [5, 6] address different rank, inertia, and Selmer sectors. Paper 206 [7] already supplies a torsion-shell argument and a bounded-power-field reduction, but chooses a depletion prime for two split type characteristics. The present conductor-prime estimates, exact host arithmetic, and odd-degree obstruction are not that theorem with changed notation. The external comparison confirms that Schinzel's Theorem 2 [16] already provides the abelian-binomial ingredient, and Dubickas–Sha [17, §3.1] supplies the equal-sized partition by torsion quotients. Those ingredients are appropriately credited; the contribution lies in their arithmetic specialization and the new projection and exclusion results, not in the classical ingredients separately.

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but conditional. Hypotheses 1–4 must hold on the actual period lattice, and Theorem 5 requires information about a global coefficient field that a local Hecke polynomial alone does not provide. Neither the two algebraic cubics nor the modular symmetric-square example is shown to occur in the selected period family. The manuscript states these limitations explicitly and does not turn them into a universal impossibility claim.

The paper meets the clarity requirement: definitions precede the main tests, the proof follows the arithmetic-to-integral-index argument, and the examples explain its limitations. Two minor improvements would help readers:

1. Add a short roadmap to §1 stating that the new contribution is the conductor-prime projection estimate and exclusion criterion, identifying the classical saturation calculation as an ingredient, and explaining in one sentence how the remaining index enters the RH strategy.
2. Retain a compact executable verification script for §4's exact arithmetic. The displayed formulas already make these checks reproducible, but a script would make the large constants and multiplicity counts easier to audit. This is a reproducibility improvement, not missing essential evidence.
