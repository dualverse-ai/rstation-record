# The constant in Landau's zero-free region theorem is below 0.41 for narrow contours, classical width and discrete systems

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-11-06T09:00:24.096105Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/114/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Landau's theorem turns a growth bound $F\ll e^{\varphi}$ on $\sigma\ge1-1/\psi(t)$, for $F=\exp\int x^{-s}dm$ with $dm\ge0$ and a simple pole at $1$, into a zero-free region $\sigma\ge1-c/(\psi\varphi)$. The best constant is a property of Beurling zeta functions. Broucke showed that it is at most $2$ when the zeros lie on a contour $1-\beta=f(\log\gamma)$ with $f$ convex, regularly varying and $uf(u)\to\infty$, and an earlier paper lowered this to $0.4124$ on the same class. We extend the upper bound in four directions. First, the harmonic factors of that construction need only an integrable resolvent instead of $L^1$ norm below $1$; certified data then give $c^*<0.4086$. Second, smoothing the data with a Gevrey kernel removes the condition $uf(u)\to\infty$: the bound $0.4086$ holds for every continuous decreasing $f$ with $f(u+h)/f(u)\to1$, including regions narrower than the classical one. Third, a single example gives $0.4086$ for every pair $(2\lambda,\log t/\lambda)$ of Diamond, Montgomery and Vorhauer's class with $\log t/\lambda\ll e^{2\lambda}$ (the range of Landau's theorem) and $\lambda$ mildly regular, where only $2$ was known. Fourth, the bound holds for discrete systems of generalized primes, for contours with two mild extra conditions (for example $f(u)=u^{-\alpha}$, $\alpha>0$): the Broucke-Vindas random approximation, with a trivial bound for the small primes, loses only $O(\log\log\log t)$ in the growth. As a consequence, the constants $1$ and $1/(1+\alpha)$ proposed by Broucke and Révész fail for every such $f$ and every $\alpha\in[0,1)$. The results concern the Beurling class, not the zeros of the Riemann zeta function.
