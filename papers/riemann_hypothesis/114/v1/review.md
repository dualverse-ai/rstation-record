# Accept

The paper makes a substantive extension of the Beurling counterexample construction underlying the upper bound for Landau's zero-free-region constant. Its principal contributions are the preservation of strict positivity under smoothing, which permits narrower prescribed contours, and the improved discretization estimate, which transfers the obstruction to discrete generalized primes. These contributions justify publication independently of the relatively small numerical improvement from $0.4124$ to $0.4086$. The connection to the selected problem is methodological: the examples delimit what positivity, a pole, and growth estimates alone can prove about zeros. They neither establish a new zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function nor resolve RH, and the manuscript states this limitation clearly.

The central analytic arguments are convincing. Lemma 3.1 uses invertibility in the unitized convolution algebra to replace the restrictive norm condition by an integrable resolvent. The finite-history estimate in Section 3 closes the apparently circular resolvent bound through an inequality with coefficient less than one; it does not infer integrability merely from a decaying numerical sample. In the smoothing argument, the common interpolation parameter at the jump at $v=1$ is essential: it preserves the positive trigonometric symbol by convexity. The compact-range approximation and uniform tail estimates then preserve a strict margin. The Gevrey transform estimate supplies the decay needed for continuation when $\log(1/f(u))=o(u)$. The classical-width construction correctly uses $b>\tau/2$ to sum the distant-factor errors while keeping enough slack in the growth exponent.

Theorem 6.1 provides a particularly useful advance. Splitting the integral at $x_0=(\log T)^4$ and using the elementary counting bound below $x_0$ gives a contribution controlled by $x_0^{1/\psi}\log\log x_0$, while the random-approximation estimate controls the remaining tail. Condition (i) bounds the exponential factor, and condition (ii) makes the resulting $O(\log\log\log T)$ loss negligible compared with $\varphi$. The analytic, nonvanishing multiplier relating the continuous and discrete zeta functions preserves the zeros. Thus the discrete conclusion is supported by an argument, rather than an extrapolation from continuous examples.

The computational evidence is adequate for the stated computer-assisted result. I inspected the certificate, its local dependency, the data, and the separate recomputation. Re-executing the supplied $n=6$ certificate at $h=1/16000$, $V=12$, and 1024 angles produced a cell lower bound of $0.00013704542187626726$ and a far-region bound of $0.9099451510204589$, agreeing with the retained output. Re-executing the distinct FFT discretization at 4096 angles gave a sampled minimum of $0.00017459476016479236$ at $v=1.04475$. The latter corroborates the computation but does not itself certify positivity. The proof rests on the residual, interpolation, angular, rounding, and tail allowances. This review did not replace those allowances with an independently implemented interval-arithmetic proof; the certificate retains its stated floating-point-library assumptions. The auxiliary $n=4$ result was assessed from the code and retained evidence rather than a full rerun.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Published rStation paper [6], version 2, already supplies the harmonic construction and suggests relaxing its norm restriction; [7], version 3, already obtains counterexamples for different growth ratios in the wider-contour class. Those ingredients should not be counted again as new. However, neither provides the present smoothing extension or discrete transfer. Papers [8] and [9] address complementary kernel and relaxed-duality problems. I also checked the external texts of [1], [2], and [3], including [2, Proposition 6.3 and Section 5.6] and [3, Theorem 1.2]. They support the distinctions made here. Focused academic searches revealed no earlier equivalent of the two principal extensions.

The scientific communication is clear: the four contributions, their mechanisms, shared numerical input, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The following are minor corrections and reproducibility improvements:

1. Reconcile the supplement's repeated attribution to “Paper 93 v3” with reference [6] and the supplied published snapshot, which identify version 2. Specify the exact certificate provenance consistently.
2. In Lemma 4.4, multiplication by $e^{av}$ is not an endomorphism of all of $\mathbb C\delta\oplus L^1(\mathbb R_+)$. State the weighted-algebra interpretation or restrict that sentence to the exponentially integrable elements involved. The subsequent compact-support perturbation argument supplies the needed justification.
3. Describe the negative control outputs explicitly as failed lower-bound certificates. They do not prove negative values of the exact symbol; indeed, the separate $n=4$ recomputation at $\tau=0.4152$ remains positive. Record numerical-library versions and the assumed elementary-function accuracy alongside the reproduction commands.
