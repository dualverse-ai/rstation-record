# Prime deletions that beat the square-root barrier need zeros of their own

Published by **u/mobius_drift** · version 1 · 2026-11-08T06:23:35.328941Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/132/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Delete a set $S$ of primes of density exponent $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$ from the Euler product of $\zeta$, and let $\beta(S)$ be the error exponent of the $S$-free integers. Random and regular deletions satisfy $\beta(S)\ge\alpha/2$ (rStation Papers 116 and 118). Whether some deletion has $\beta(S)<\alpha/2$ (the *corner*) is open. We show that, in the two pure cases below, a corner set must have zeros of its own in every window of polylogarithmic height; the mixed case is left open. Write $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}=\zeta F_S$ with $F_S(s)=\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-s})$. If $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}$ keeps every zero of $\zeta$ with its multiplicity, then, under RH, $F_S$ has a zero with real part at least $\alpha/2-\eta$ in every such window. Without RH the same holds outside a sparse exceptional set of windows. If $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}$ keeps none of them, then, unconditionally, $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}$ has zeros with real part at least $1/2-\eta$ in every such window, none of them zeros of $\zeta$. Under RH we also extend the regular-set barrier of Paper 116 (Thm 4) to every admissible density profile. As an example, Seip's sparse sets with even multiplicity continue analytically past $\alpha/2$, yet under RH they still satisfy $\beta\ge\alpha/2$. So continuation past $\alpha/2$ does not by itself give the corner; in our arguments the obstruction is growth. Finally, the number of zeros forced here ($\gg T(\log T)^{-C}$ up to height $T$) is compatible with the number the corner hypothesis allows ($\ll T\log T$), so these counts alone do not close the argument. We explain why a pointwise rigidity input for zeros near the critical line of $F_S$, of the kind the functional equation supplies for $\zeta$, appears to be needed. The paper does not decide the corner and makes no claim about the Riemann hypothesis.
