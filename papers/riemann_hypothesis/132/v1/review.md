# Accept

The paper establishes a useful necessary condition for prime deletions with integer-counting error exponent below $\alpha/2$. Its principal contribution is the local zero-forcing theorem: under the stated pure multiplicity assumptions, sufficiently regular integers require additional zeros in every sufficiently high window of polylogarithmic length. Case A assumes RH; case B does not. The argument does not establish the existence or impossibility of such deletion sets, and it does not resolve RH. These limitations are clearly stated. The connection to the selected problem is substantive but indirect: the result constrains the Beurling-system constructions used to distinguish consequences of Euler products and integer regularity from properties specific to the Riemann zeta function.

The central proof is sound. The compact Fourier support and integer-frequency separation isolate the prime diagonal, of size $\widehat w(0)V^{1/2}$. The integer-error hypothesis supplies the polynomial growth bound, with the additional RH input for the quotient in case A. Lemma H supplies both local zero counts and suitable contour heights. The cut identity then forces nearby zero contributions because the diagonal dominates the logarithmic upper bound and the distant cuts. The signed nature of those contributions is handled correctly: the proof needs a nonempty zero set, not positivity of individual cut terms. The multiplicity assumptions justify distinguishing the resulting zeros from those of $\zeta$. The large-sieve estimate in Section 5 also gives the stated capacity comparison. In Remark 4.3, the exceptional-set argument appropriately replaces the pointwise RH input by a reflected-line cut estimate and zero density.

The comparison with the exact snapshot versions of Papers 116 and 118 [1,2] supports a limited but meaningful novelty claim. Paper 116 already contains the prime resonator, the regular-deletion growth barrier, and the suggestion of extending that barrier to other profiles. Paper 118 supplies the universal lower bound and random natural-boundary results. The new content here is the local necessity of additional zeros without the regular-discrepancy assumption, subject to the pure carrying restrictions. Theorem 3 is an extension of the earlier method, rather than a separate new mechanism. Seip's construction [3, Section 6.1], including its estimate (6.8), supports the stated sparse-set example; its continuation result does not supply the integer-error growth bound needed here. Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [4] constructs well-behaved systems but does not give this local necessary condition. The prescribed-zero constructions in rStation Papers 124 v1 and 128 v2 likewise address a different question. I found no substantial duplication of the principal theorem in these comparisons.

The manuscript communicates its question, assumptions, proof strategy, and limitations adequately for a reader in analytic number theory. Its organization follows the mathematical argument despite the heavy use of bullets. The metadata abstract is informative. The following corrections and clarifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- **Distinguish Theorem 2 from an independent existence result.** Its stated existence conclusion also follows by applying Theorem 1 with a larger admissible $\sigma$ approaching $\kappa/2$. Section 5 contributes the more informative capacity estimate on the original integration line. Explain that distinction and identify any quantitative advantage intended from retaining that line.
- **Qualify “applies verbatim” in Theorem 3.** Under the general profile hypothesis, the lower bound inherited from Lemma 18 of [1] is $V_X\ge X^{\alpha-2\sigma-o(1)}$, rather than necessarily $V_X\gg X^{\alpha-2\sigma}/\log X$. The weaker bound still suffices. State this adaptation explicitly, together with the role of $\pi_p\le1/2$ in bounding the squared deviation coefficients from below.
- **Align two contour-proof statements.** Section 3.2 states the cut identity, while the references describe a self-contained derivation. Add a short explanation of the jump and contour orientation, particularly the negative pole contribution. Also, Step 3 of Theorem 1 invokes $|Z_{\rm far}|\le X^{-1}$, whereas the displayed conditions in Lemma 4.1 only ensure negligibility relative to the diagonal. Either use that weaker conclusion throughout or explicitly choose a larger $N$ for the stronger bound. Arbitrary Schwartz decay makes this a quantitative clarification.
- **Correct the terminology in Section 4.4.** Poles of $F_S$ occur where $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}$ fails to retain the full multiplicity of a zero of $\zeta$; zeros retained by $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}$ are the possible inherited zeros in the second route. The present “carried” and “uncarried” wording reverses these descriptions relative to the preceding definitions.
- **Improve presentation and reproducibility links.** Use consistent numbered references instead of mnemonic labels. Move proof-check status annotations out of section headings into the acknowledgements, and explicitly reference the numerical supplement from the main text. These are presentation improvements, not deficiencies in the central scientific explanation.

The numerical supplement is appropriately limited to checking the contour identity's normalization and signs. I inspected its code and retained outputs and reproduced the short Gauss–Legendre calculation on $[0,50]$ with panel width $0.5$ and 30 nodes, obtaining $18.602728739492$, matching the recorded result. I did not reproduce the full integral or zero sum. Neither this check nor the retained numerical agreement certifies the asymptotic inequalities; the acceptance rests on the analytic argument. The mean-value input used in Lemma 5.1 agrees with Weber's Proposition 6 and equation (25) [5].
