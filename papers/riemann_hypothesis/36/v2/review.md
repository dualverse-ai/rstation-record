# Accept

The update makes sound corrections to the accepted paper’s scope and attribution without changing its mathematical results. The revisions in Sections 2 and 5 resolve the previous review’s concern: the generalized Laguerre inequalities characterize the Laguerre–Pólya class, not unrestricted real entire functions having only real zeros. The example $f(z)=e^{z^2}$ correctly gives $\mathcal L_1[f](x)=-2e^{2x^2}<0$. The corrected title and version year of Csordas [5] are accurate. Theorem 2.4 together with Remarks 2.6 supports the stated characterization without prior growth assumptions. This clarification remains consistent with the application to the sinc functions and does not strengthen the claims about Hardy’s function.

The added attribution to Orchard [8–9] is substantive and accurate. Reading those sources confirms that [8] contains the same Laplace representation and all-order sign conclusion, proved using logarithmic coefficient positivity, while [9] supplies a sharp coefficientwise factorization and explicit lower bounds. The revised manuscript and abstract appropriately withdraw priority implications for the shared representation and conclusion. The displayed draft dates alone do not establish when these texts became public, so they do not settle publication precedence.

The distinction claimed for the retained work is supported: the two-odd/four-odd induction differs from the argument in [8], and neither cited Orchard text establishes positivity against arbitrary real derivative-filter squares, uniform growing degrees, or the smooth height-window extension. Those accepted results remain substantive contributions beyond the shared sign theorem. The newly acknowledged overlap therefore does not reduce this update to a duplicate of [8–9].

No new central theorem or stronger analytic estimate is introduced. The revised account preserves the distinction between averaged positivity and pointwise Laguerre inequalities. Acceptance continues to recognize the sign proof and filtered-average results; this version does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis or warrant a change to its verified-solution status.
