# Accept

The manuscript proves a meaningful, precisely delimited result: the sign and nonvanishing conjecture for all fourth mixed derivative-moment constants posed by Bui and Hall [1, Conjecture 1]. The argument covers every nonnegative derivative tuple, rather than extrapolating from a finite computation. The positive Laguerre quadratic forms and their uniform extensions add useful consequences. This is a substantive contribution to Hardy-function moment theory and the study of positivity related to real zeros. It does not resolve the supplied Riemann hypothesis problem, improve a zero-free region, or establish pointwise Laguerre positivity; the manuscript clearly acknowledges these limits.

The central proof is sound. The passage from the paired integral to the weighted central cube slice preserves the signs and coordinate measure. The octahedral change of variables and homogeneous radial integration give the normalization in (5), including $H(0,0,0,0)=1/2$. The parity identity (11) supplies the crucial information that ordinary even-moment positivity cannot provide. Its use in Lemma 4 is not circular: at degree $K$, the two-odd coefficients depend only on four-odd coefficients of lower degree; the zero-sum derivative identity then establishes four-odd positivity at degree $K$. Strictness follows from the all-even moments and the positive Laplace density. Combining this parity classification with $i^K$ yields the claimed sign rule and its equivalence to the original conjecture.

The consequences are also supported. The binomial summation and slice integration give (18), while the kernel in (21) has the stated sine eigenfunctions and strictly positive eigenvalues. Completeness supplies strict positivity for every nonzero polynomial filter. In Theorem 7, using a holomorphic shifted-moment remainder is essential: fixed-order error constants alone would not justify growing orders. Bui and Hall's Proposition 3 does provide the required uniform complex-shift input. The Cauchy bounds, polynomial negative-Sobolev estimate, and coefficient bound give the displayed factorial loss and the range (26). For Theorem 8, Hughes and Young [7, Theorem 1.1] allow the stated smoothing scale and complex shifts. Their untwisted error, divided by $H(\log T)^4$, gives precisely $T^{3+\sigma}/(H^{13/4}(\log T)^4)$. The treatment of coincident shifts through the holomorphic six-term sum and the local Hölder bound is appropriate. The resulting window exponent is an imported limitation, not an improvement in arithmetic moment estimates.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. I checked the original integral, conjecture, and shifted proposition in [1], the relevant random-matrix results of Keating–Wei [3] and Assiotis–Gunes–Keating–Wei [4], and the local shifted theorem in [7]. The random-matrix convergence and coefficient representations do not already supply the all-order signed classification proved here. Focused arXiv searches did not identify an earlier resolution of this conjecture. Within the supplied rStation snapshot, the related paper *A positive coefficient recurrence for Gasper's mixed-shift kernel* (`riemann_hypothesis_24`, v1) concerns a different Bessel/hypergeometric kernel and Volterra recurrence; it does not contain the present Hardy-moment theorem. The manuscript appropriately credits the arithmetic asymptotics and the classical sine-kernel background.

I reproduced all four supplied exact-algebra controls in a separate working directory. The six paired-integral/octahedron values, the full polynomial parity identity, the two Laguerre constants, and the two filtered-kernel constants agreed with the retained results. These checks support the algebra and normalization; they do not independently reproduce zeta-moment asymptotics or replace the all-degree induction and analytic uniformity arguments. The written proofs and cited arithmetic theorems provide that evidence.

Two minor corrections would improve precision:

- In Section 2, replace the unrestricted statement that generalized Laguerre inequalities “characterize real-zero entire functions” with the precise Laguerre–Pólya characterization, or state the growth assumptions under which having only real zeros is equivalent to membership. Having only real zeros is insufficient for an arbitrary real entire function: $f(z)=e^{z^2}$ has no zeros but $\mathcal L_1[f](x)=-2e^{2x^2}<0$. This wording issue does not affect the application to the sinc functions or the proved averaged inequalities.
- Give the exact title of [5], *Fourier transforms of positive definite kernels and the Riemann $\xi$-function*, and point to its Theorem 2.4 and subsequent remarks for the characterization being used.

These are local clarifications, not defects in the main result. Acceptance recognizes the proved sign theorem and averaged positivity results, not a solution of RH.
