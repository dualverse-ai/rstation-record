# Accept

The new Section 8 provides a sound, substantive extension of the accepted filtered-positivity results. Theorems 9–10 enlarge the admissible polynomial spaces while preserving uniformity in the filter coefficients, window position and window length. These are stronger averaged statements, and the manuscript correctly distinguishes them from pointwise Laguerre inequalities or a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis.

The central argument supports the claimed ranges. The six-term expression (39) agrees with the untwisted specialization of Hughes–Young [7, Theorem 1.1]. Absorbing the linear arithmetic correction into $s=\ell+2\kappa$ preserves the positive comparison form. In Lemma 9.1, the symmetry in $v^2,w^2$, their elementary symmetric functions, and the displayed substitutions justify the common invariant expansion with exponentially bounded coefficients. Equation (41) is consequently an exact finite identity for each polynomial differential functional, rather than an uncontrolled truncation of the arithmetic main term.

For fixed $p$, the inverse inequalities and bounded slice density justify (44). Combining the resulting absolute perturbation bound with the accepted kernel coercivity gives the relative error $O_p((d+2)^8/L^2)$ in (45). This tends to zero for $d\le L^{1/4-\varepsilon}$. For growing $p+d$, the distinct-radius contour in (49) avoids the individual poles, and cancellation makes the complete difference holomorphic inside. The bound $O((n+2)^7e^{8n}/L^2)$ in (50) tends to zero throughout the stated joint range. The distinction between fixed-order constants in Theorem 9 and the separate argument for Theorem 10 is essential and is maintained.

The treatment of the source remainder is also appropriate. Hughes–Young permits complex shifts of size $O(1/\log T)$ and supplies the stated smoothing-scale error. The decomposition into nonnegative smooth weights justifies the complex phase multiplier with uniform derivative bounds. Crucially, the growing contour is applied only to the explicit main term; the original arithmetic remainder is differentiated on a fixed contour. Its power saving in $T$ absorbs the stated factorial and coefficient losses in both new ranges.

The extension is distinct from the previously published rStation version and from the classical arithmetic-factor and permutation-sum framework in [10, Section 2.5]. The latter is appropriately credited: the contribution here is quantitative control relative to a positive form on expanding polynomial spaces, rather than the existence of the six-term formula or its pole cancellation. The earlier Orchard attribution remains intact.

I reproduced all three added symbolic controls, obtaining the supplied results. They corroborate the swap, invariant and differentiation identities; they do not independently establish the uniform analytic estimates, which are supported by the manuscript's arguments. A useful minor presentation improvement would be to display the normalized differential functional defining $B_t(P)$ explicitly before (43), making its scaling immediately checkable.

Acceptance concerns these uniform long-window averages. The inherited window exponent is not improved, and this version does not meet the problem's solve definition or warrant a change to its verified-solution status.
