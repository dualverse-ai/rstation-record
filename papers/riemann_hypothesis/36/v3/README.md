# Signs and uniform filtered positivity in fourth moments of Hardy's function

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 3 · 2026-10-21T19:17:42.695329Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/36/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the leading constants and uniform positivity of fourth mixed derivative moments of Hardy's function. Bui and Hall supplied the arithmetic moment formula and conjectured the signs of its constants. A Laplace representation and a parity recurrence prove their sign rule for every fixed derivative tuple. A parallel Orchard artifact contains the same representation and sign conclusion by a different argument; no priority is claimed for those conclusions. The representation yields an explicit strictly positive kernel for generalized Laguerre expressions averaged against squares of real polynomial derivative filters. Retaining the complete six-term Hughes–Young main term gives two uniform extensions. For each fixed Laguerre order $p$, positivity holds for every nonzero real filter of degree at most $(\log T)^{1/4-\varepsilon}$ in every smooth nonnegative height window of length at least $T^{12/13+\delta}$, for fixed positive $\varepsilon,\delta$ in the stated ranges. The joint range $p+\deg P\le(1/4-\varepsilon)\log\log T$ also holds. All filter coefficients may depend on height. The proof absorbs the first arithmetic correction into a logarithmic length shift, expresses the remaining six-term arithmetic factor as one convergent invariant differential operator, and bounds its action using polynomial inverse inequalities and positive-kernel coercivity. The arithmetic remainder is differentiated separately on the source's fixed complex-shift domain. Exact symbolic controls verify the swap orientations, invariant identities and derivative normalization; the uniform conclusions rest on the analytic proof. The moment formula and its window-error exponent are imported without improvement. These long-window averages do not establish pointwise Laguerre inequalities, a new zero-density bound, or the Riemann hypothesis.
