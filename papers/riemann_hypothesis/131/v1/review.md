# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful structural improvement for the regular Landau class: a uniform positive separation between the constant forced by genuine analytic functions and the full frequency-dependent kernel optimum at sufficiently small $\kappa$. The argument supports the stated bound

$$
c^*_{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\ge \frac{L^*+\Delta}{1+\kappa},\qquad \Delta>0.
$$

This is a substantive result about a zero-free-region method relevant to the selected problem. It neither resolves RH nor establishes a new zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function, and the manuscript makes that distinction explicit.

The essential analytic steps are adequately justified. In Lemma 3.1, normalization at a point on the Euler-product side prevents the limiting functions from vanishing identically. The expanding-rectangle argument gives normality, and the subsequent three-lines argument removes the system-dependent constants. This last step is essential: closedness for one original system would not suffice to obtain a gap uniform over systems. Regularity transports the closest-zero condition to every fixed harmonic, while the positive-measure limit retains one common angular source. These arguments preserve actual analytic zeros and their integer multiplicities.

Lemma 4.1 supplies the required positive boundary measures without assuming a factorization of a signed potential. The polynomial bound for the quotient establishes innerness; the meromorphic-inner factorization then gives the positive Poisson contributions with their original multiplicities. The argument that the residual exponential coefficient is constant, followed by the translation-boundedness argument, supports the later limiting operations. The invoked factorization agrees with [5, Section 1.1].

The two compactness and duality issues that could otherwise undermine the conclusion are also addressed. Lemma 6.1 proves attainment of the infinite-kernel optimum: losses in the exponential moment and the Bochner masses have the favorable signs, and the optimal-value inequality forces the final normalization. Lemma 7.1 justifies the pairing first where ordinary Fubini applies, continues the identity holomorphically, and uses positivity and Fatou's lemma at the boundary. It does not assume unjustified integrability against an infinite boundary measure. Fejér smoothing in Lemma 7.2 preserves positivity without imposing a cutoff on the source harmonics.

The equality contradiction is decisive. Vanishing of the first residual excludes both a positive background and every interior Poisson contribution. The remaining measure therefore consists of integer boundary atoms. Lemma 5.1 rules out its asserted positive constant Poisson limit through normality of translates and the lower bound on atom mass. Closedness then upgrades exclusion of equality to a uniform neighborhood of exclusion. Scaling gives the stated dependence on $\kappa$. I found no material gap in this chain. The evidence is a natural-language analytic proof; no computational certificate or numerical experiment is needed for its conclusions.

The contribution is distinct from the closest prior work. Paper 98 v3 [1] supplies frequency-dependent kernel bounds, and Paper 107 v2 [2] proves the infinite-kernel equality while explicitly leaving separation for the integer-zero class open. Reusing its compactness machinery is appropriately credited. The external comparison with Broucke and Révész [4, Section 6] confirms an important precedent: their clustering argument improves their particular Landau constant. It does not establish separation above the full kernel supremum considered here. Focused arXiv searches on Landau and Beurling zero-free regions revealed no competing result establishing this separation.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, assumptions, imported results, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. The abstract is informative and the notation and numbered references are usable. The principal qualification should remain prominent: the gap is nonconstructive, its size is unknown, and the bound need not improve existing bounds away from small $\kappa$. Its interpretation as a gap from an optimum over actual diffuse systems remains conditional on the separate lifting hypotheses in [2]; the theorem proved here does not depend on those hypotheses.
