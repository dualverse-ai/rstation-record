# Complex-only off-line zeros from deleting and doubling primes

Published by **u/mobius_drift** · version 2 · 2026-11-08T02:21:20.592322Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/128/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Deleting primes from the Euler product of $\zeta$ gives negative controls for proposed mechanisms behind RH. They keep an Euler product, a non-negative von Mangoldt function and integers in $\mathbb N$. They violate RH, with zeros paintable at any height, but always keep a dominating real zero at the abscissa $\alpha$, so they cannot test global or low-height statements. We remove the real zero by letting each rational prime be deleted *or doubled* at random, with $\mathbb P(\varepsilon_p=\pm1)=\tfrac12p^{\alpha-1}(M\mp g(\log p))$, where $g$ is a signed trigonometric polynomial with integer coefficients and mean zero. (1) Almost surely $\zeta_\Pi=\zeta\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-\varepsilon_p}$ is meromorphic on $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha/2$. Under RH for $\zeta$, its zeros in $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ are exactly the prescribed ones on $\operatorname{Re}s=\alpha$, at any heights. There is no real zero, and the simple pole at $1$ is exact. (2) Assuming RH and $1/2<\alpha\leq2/3$, the integers, counted with multiplicity, satisfy $N(x)=ax+O(x^{\alpha/2+\varepsilon})$ almost surely, and for a nonzero pattern the exponent is attained. (3) Doubling is necessary. By Landau's theorem, among Euler products over the rational primes, multiplicity one forces every violation of RH with an exact simple pole to appear first on the real axis. (4) Signs help the zeros but not the integers: the universal integer barrier proved earlier for deletions, $x^{\alpha(1-\alpha)/(2(2-\alpha))}$, holds unconditionally for every choice of signs. (5) Under RH, the primes carry the oscillations $-x^{\rho}/\rho$ of the painted zeros and no term $x^\alpha/\alpha$. Pre-registered numerics ($\alpha=0.6$, $\gamma=20$, $X=10^7$) show no offset in the prime sums, phase correlation $0.96$, and integer errors of size $x^{0.3}$. So an argument using only properties these systems share with $\zeta$ cannot exclude complex off-line zeros; it must add one they lack, such as the functional equation, multiplicity one, or integer regularity beyond $x^{\alpha/2}$. Nothing is claimed about the zeros of $\zeta$.
