# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. It preserves the contribution—complex-only prescribed zeros obtained by deleting and doubling rational primes while retaining nonnegative multiplicities—and improves its explanation, attribution, and numerical qualifications. It introduces no new substantive result requiring a separate submission. The resulting manuscript remains focused on the construction, its conditional integer estimates, and the obstruction for multiplicity one.

The corrections resolve the earlier review's main requests. Section 2 now distinguishes the additional integer-asymptotic terms caused by prescribed poles in [5] from the remaining error. Section 4 correctly assigns the displayed local factor to the correction $H$, consistently with the factorization in [3, §5]. The definitions added to §5b clarify the short-interval correlation argument. The opening now distinguishes a real zero from a singularity for arbitrary deletion sets, and the restriction of the natural-boundary statement to independent deletion avoids an overbroad attribution. These changes clarify the existing arguments without strengthening their conclusions.

The new comparison with Seip [15] is useful and substantially accurate. I checked Theorems 1.1 and 1.4 and §6.1: they support the stated prescribed-zero ranges and the earlier single-real-zero deletion construction. The manuscript now correctly acknowledges that complex-only off-line zeros of rational-prime Euler products are already known. The relevant distinction remains the simultaneous retention of nonnegative prime and integer coefficients, multiplicities bounded by two, and the conditional integer-error estimate. Unimodular twists do not supply this combination. This addition improves attribution without displacing the accepted contribution.

The numerical correction is also supported. Independently evaluating the stated prime-density approximation gives
$$
2\int_{10^7}^{\infty}\frac{u^{-2.4}}{\log u}\,du
\approx1.3474\times10^{-11}.
$$
Its square root is $3.6706\times10^{-6}$, yielding normalized shifts of approximately $0.0582\widehat a$ and $0.2916\widehat a$ at $10^6$ and $10^7$. These agree with the revised text and notebook. This checks the uncertainty approximation, not the exact infinite prime sum or a reproduction of the unchanged simulations. Identifying $\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{sd}$ as an amplitude estimate is likewise appropriate.

One minor clarification remains: the computed statistic $R$, defined using $\widehat a$, is fixed by the retained computation. The uncertainty concerns its interpretation as the corresponding maximum using the true density $a$. Describe the quoted $\pm0.3$ as an approximate one-standard-deviation sensitivity scale, not a confidence interval for that maximum. The density error induces a common drift across the curve, rather than independent pointwise noise. The explicit statement that these plots do not establish asymptotic exponents appropriately limits their scientific role.

Acceptance remains for the negative-control construction. The revision neither proves nor disproves the Riemann hypothesis.
