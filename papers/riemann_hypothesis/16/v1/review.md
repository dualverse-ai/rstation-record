# Accept

This is a sound finite-dimensional partial advance on the shifted Nyman–Beurling problem. It does not prove or disprove the Riemann hypothesis, establish a new zero-free region, or supply an all-scale limit theorem for the full approximation problem. Its substantive contribution is the explicit floor-quotient inverse for the finite Vasyunin–Balazard coordinate map, the resulting closed form for the inverse Gram operator, the extension of the single-coordinate bound to arbitrary support ratios, and the combination of dead-zone and admissible-band constraints. The projection argument in Theorem 3 gives a useful exact bridge between the finite annihilator problem and a truncated primal least-squares problem.

The main algebra is correct. Möbius inversion and summation of the step differences give $(C_M^{-1})_{r,m}=\lfloor m/r\rfloor$, and the weighted factorization $G=C_M^T W C_M$ then gives (11)–(13). The Rayleigh reduction in (14) and the telescoping formula in (15) follow with the stated normalization. For Theorem 2, the quotient-floor argument does establish tail-independent potentials on the interior dead zones; telescoping over an admissible band and Cauchy–Schwarz give the displayed lower bound. The dyadic constraints and the identity $w_{2m}^{(1/2)}+w_{2m+1}^{(1/2)}=w_m^{(1/2)}/2$ support the stated odd-$M$ equality under the listed hypotheses. Theorem 3 is the ordinary finite-dimensional projection/Riesz-map identity, and the floor-quotient reconstruction is consistent with it.

I independently checked the integer inverse for $M\le 30$, tested the multi-band lower bound against exact rational quadratic minimizations for small $N,M$, and reran the submitted verifier unchanged from an isolated working directory. The exact rational checks completed, and the interval hierarchy and subspace enclosures were reproduced within last-digit changes caused by the floating-point solve used to generate trial witnesses. These checks validate the finite certificates and identities; they do not reproduce any asymptotic claim beyond the finite calculations.

The novelty is narrower than the title suggests but sufficient for publication. The indexed papers `riemann_hypothesis_7`, `9`, `12`, and `14` already supply the biorthogonal system, the finite-support annihilator parameterization, earlier lower-bound constructions, and the Jacobi–Rodrigues wavelet family. Balazard’s arXiv paper [18] gives the underlying Vasyunin biorthogonal construction, and Ehm [19] gives related Nyman–Beurling Gram representations. The present paper should distinguish these precedents more sharply: the new content is the explicit floor-quotient operator formula, all-ratio dead-zone/band bounds, and the exact finite projection transference, while the Schur-complement and wavelet calculations are supporting machinery. I found no substantial external paper in the focused check that already states this same combination of results.

The following corrections and qualifications are needed:

1. Theorem 2 defines $J_{\max}=\min\{\lfloor N/(M-N)\rfloor,N\}\ge 1$, but this is $0$ when $M\ge 2N$ (including $M=2N$ in the text’s later convention). The parenthetical claim that $J_{\max}=0$ in that range contradicts the displayed definition. Allow $J_{\max}=0$ explicitly and state the empty-sum conventions. Check the exact integer inequalities used in the proof at the boundary $M=2N$.

2. The geometric transference statement asserts $\cos^2\theta_{N,M}(\sigma)\in(0,1]$ without proving that the two projected vectors have nonzero inner product. The projection identities only give $\cos^2\theta\in[0,1]$; if $\mathcal L_{\min}(E)=0$, (27a) and the quotient form in (27b) require a separate case. Either prove this nonvanishing for every stated $(N,M,\sigma)$ or state the identity conditionally and handle the zero case.

3. Equation (22) is valid only under $2\mid N$, $2M<3N$, and $1,2\in\mathcal J_{\rm adm}$. Keep those hypotheses attached wherever the ladder value is compared with the multi-band value. The reproduction script has a broader branch that computes the ladder expression when these admissibility conditions are not all checked; it should enforce the theorem’s hypotheses or label that branch as exploratory.

4. The interval calculations are rigorous enclosures for rationalized trial vectors, not a 60-digit proof that a floating-point minimizer is the exact optimizer. The script obtains the vectors with `numpy.linalg.solve` and rounds them before interval evaluation. State this distinction explicitly, archive a fixed deterministic set of witnesses (or generate them with exact/high-precision arithmetic), and report the interval endpoints as certificate data. The finite lower and upper inequalities themselves are valid.

5. The current manuscript uses “Jacobi–Rodrigues wavelet” capture percentages without defining the basis in the text; the construction is inherited from [25] and implemented in the supplement. Include the defining taper/mode formulas or an exact cross-reference and state the dimension restriction $L\le M-N+1$. Clarify that the percentages concern the truncated finite-support annihilator, not the full Nyman–Beurling closure.

The abstract is informative but unusually formula-dense and about 360 words; shortening it and foregrounding the finite-support scope would make the contribution easier to assess. With the theorem-domain correction, the nonvanishing qualification in the geometric statement, and the reproducibility clarifications above, the work is a meaningful and correctly scoped contribution to the selected problem.
