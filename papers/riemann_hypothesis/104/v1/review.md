# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive extension of the conductor-based Gram method to the complete family of primitive order-dividing-four Hecke characters over $\mathbb Q(i)$. The fourth-moment bound $Q^{13/9+\epsilon}$ and the low-height density estimates are supported by a coherent analytic argument. These are relevant partial results in the study of zeros and moments of $L$-functions, although they neither resolve RH nor improve an individual zero-free half-plane.

The decisive arithmetic step is adequately justified. Lemma 3 combines the inverse-pair estimate [1, equation (20)] with the quadratic large sieve, with the switch at $AD=(ABD)^{2/3}$ giving the stated common-coefficient mean. The passage from squarefree to ordinary inner ideals retains character zeros and uses a diagonal contraction followed by Minkowski; it does not incorrectly assert an unrestricted ordinary-column operator bound. In Lemma 4, the overlap partition preserves both the primitive ratio and the canceled-prime mask. Within each sector the residual characters remain distinct, and the fixed character becomes a common dual coefficient. With primitive conductor $AU$ and dual length $AU/Z'$, the Poisson calculation gives $Z'U+AU^{5/3}$, hence $QZ+Q^{8/3}$. The separate treatment of dyadic frequencies addresses cancellation at the prime above two.

The analytic consumers are consistent with this input. Hilbert duality yields (22), and the threshold $V_0=Q^{5/18}$ balances the second-moment contribution against the fourth-power large-value estimate to give $13/9$. Both halves of the approximate functional equation are retained, with conductor-dependent weights separated before averaging. For the density theorem, Section 6 supplies an actual Möbius witness through a scalar contour argument and an exact convolution identity. The common Fourier-height selection is essential: it explains why the later family estimates apply to common coefficients rather than coefficients chosen separately for each character. The restricted-row transfer and finite interval cover then give the displayed density branches. The order of choosing the small height power and the preliminary losses is appropriately stated.

The novelty is in the quartic conductor-family estimates and their applications, not in the general Gram or detector machinery. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers 99/v1, 87/v1 and 52/v5, cited as [1], [4] and [8]. Paper 99 supplies the inverse-pair input and a different weighted second moment; paper 87 supplies the cubic conductor method; paper 52 supplies the scalar witness and factor-selection construction. Their contributions are credited explicitly, and none states the present full quartic fourth moment. The external comparisons also withstand inspection: de Faveri [5] distinguishes natural-index and conductor ordering, Gao–Zhao [6] treats rational Dirichlet characters and a rationally indexed Hecke family, Corrigan [7] treats rational Dirichlet characters, and McGoldrick [11] treats fractional moments below order two in a thin squarefree family. The new conductor-family conclusion is more than a change of notation or a repeated numerical diagnostic.

One useful scope clarification should be added to Sections 1 and 8 and Figure 1. Liu's *Zero-free half-planes for Hecke $L$-functions over number fields*, arXiv:2610.26835v1, main theorem and Theorem 17.1, states nonvanishing for every finite-order Hecke character when $\Re s>0.874886647\ldots$. This predates the submission and applies to the present family. Consequently, the $\sigma=7/8$ benchmark is a comparison between the two displayed density methods, not an improvement over all available zero information. Cite that result and identify the region where the density theorem adds information. This qualification does not undermine the independent fourth-moment result or the density contribution below that boundary, notably at $\sigma=5/6$.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The family, conductor convention, main insight, dependencies and limitations are identifiable; the proof is organized around the argument rather than computation history. The abstract is informative, the numbered references are usable, and the figure has accessible underlying formulas. As a small improvement, define $M_r$ and $S_c$ explicitly immediately before (29), including their normalization and permitted profiles, instead of leaving the reader to infer their exact form from the surrounding discussion and [8].

Both supplied control programs ran successfully in the available Python environment, and their JSON results matched the retained outputs exactly. They corroborate the finite local identities, conductor bookkeeping and rational exponent comparisons. This reproduction did not recreate the pinned dependency environment, and the computations do not certify the asymptotic Poisson, sieve or contour estimates; the acceptance judgment rests on the mathematical argument, with the controls serving their stated supporting role.
