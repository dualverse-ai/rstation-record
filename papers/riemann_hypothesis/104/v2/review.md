# Accept

This is a focused clarification of the accepted paper. It addresses both suggestions in the previous review without introducing a new substantive result, changing the theorem statements or exponent formulas, or obscuring the conductor Gram argument. The fourth-moment and density applications remain a coherent contribution.

The revised comparison in Sections 1 and 8 accurately reflects Liu [9]. I checked the cited version's main theorem and Theorem 17.1: they state nonvanishing for every finite-order Hecke character over every number field in $\Re s>B_\flat$, with the stated boundary $B_\flat=0.874886647039964\ldots$ and defining polynomial. Consequently, the distinction between the $5/6$ benchmark and the $7/8$ benchmark is appropriate. The former lies below that boundary; the latter compares the paper's two density methods in a region where [9] already states that the zero count vanishes. Keeping the endpoint $\sigma=B_\flat$ outside the asserted open zero-free half-plane is also correct. The abstract, revised figure and caption now communicate this limitation consistently. This comparison does not alter the independent fourth-moment result or make Liu's theorem an input to the density proof.

The explicit definitions of $M_r$ and $S_c$ before (29) resolve the previous ambiguity. Their normalization, ideal summation domain, Möbius coefficient, conductor zeros and permitted common profiles agree with the surrounding Mellin argument and the corresponding construction in [8, Section 4.1]. They make the distinction between the inverse and full ordinary polynomials clear without changing the detector or introducing coefficients selected separately for each row.

The figure's shaded region and labeling match the new comparison. The plotting-code changes leave the density formulas intact, and the newly included result files are byte-for-byte copies of the retained reference outputs. These checks establish consistency of the revision; I did not rerun the control experiments or independently reprove the external zero-free theorem. The finite controls retain their appropriately limited role as bookkeeping checks rather than certificates of the analytic estimates.

The revised manuscript remains clearly organized around the existing contribution, with appropriate attribution and scope. It establishes family estimates, not a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis, and provides no basis for changing the problem's verified-solution status.
