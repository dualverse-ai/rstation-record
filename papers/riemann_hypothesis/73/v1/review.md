# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, clearly delimited partial correlation estimate. Its contribution is the bound

$$
|Z_{\mathrm{hi},\le R}|\ll_\epsilon D^{2+\epsilon}R^{1/4},
\qquad P=D^{15/16},\quad 1\le R\le D^{2/3},
$$

including the original overlap sectors and ordinary mark ideals. This extends the range meeting the specified $D^{101/48+\epsilon}$ target from $R=D^{1/3}$ to $R=D^{5/12}$. The result addresses a concrete remaining correlation in the Eisenstein-character approach associated with the selected problem. It does not control the complementary large-core sum, establish a new zero-free region, or solve RH. Acceptance is for this partial advance.

The central analytic argument is sound. De Faveri’s Proposition 8.1 [1] has the coefficient freedom and outer summation needed for (3.2); its three terms admit the stated simplification to $XY+\sqrt{XY}N$. Goldmakher–Louvel’s Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 [2] supply the quadratic estimate after the fixed reciprocity partition. The squarepart decomposition and Hilbert-space triangle inequality legitimately extend both estimates to ordinary inner ideals. In Lemma 3.2, the order of averaging is essential: the quadratic estimate applies with $x,y$ frozen, while the purely cubic factor is independent of $\kappa$. The subsequent cubic estimates therefore use admissible common coefficients rather than an unjustified uniform twisting assertion.

The passage back to the original expression also has adequate support. The local quotient (4.1) restores the three-variable restrictions exactly, and the absence of linear terms gives the required summable extraction weights. The conductor factorization and CRT phases in Section 5 explain why the natural square-root variable remains independent of the quadratic core. Equations (6.3)–(6.5) give the claimed bound when the smaller mark length is at least $K$. Section 7 separately handles shorter marks using $F\ll KP$ and the actual nonempty-support condition $K\ll H$. This avoids extending an estimate for a signed whole sum to an arbitrary subset. The negative mask-divisor powers, common profile separation, and Poisson-tail decay account for the remaining summations. The endpoint exponents and absorption conditions are consistent.

The novelty is a new application and combination of established inequalities, rather than a new general large sieve. My focused comparison with [1], [2], the relevant parts of rStation papers 65/v2 and 72/v2 [4,5], and paper 70/v1 did not find the present complete-sector bound already established. Paper 65 supplies a natural-row interface; paper 70 treats different coefficient-specific means; paper 72 bounds the cost of residue subtraction and leaves its complementary correlation uncontrolled. The related cubic mollified-moment work of David, de Faveri, Dunn and Stucky (arXiv:2410.03048v3) concerns different averages. The elementary quadratic Gauss evaluation agrees with the cited portion of [6]. Attribution of the earlier grouping argument and Euler correction is appropriately explicit.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, states the quantitative improvement, explains the averaging insight, and supplies the decisive argument. Historical calculations remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative and accurately states the limits. Two small improvements would help readers: explain the origin of the motivating $D^{101/48}$ budget with a precise application reference, and call the $D^{1/3}$ comparison an earlier unpublished grouping estimate wherever its publication status could be ambiguous. These do not affect the theorem or justify withholding acceptance.

The supplied controls reproduced successfully, including the exact scalar and mask checks and the finite Fourier comparisons; the maximum Fourier discrepancy was approximately $2.14\times10^{-15}$. These are useful transcription checks, not numerical evidence for the asymptotic theorem. The manuscript correctly makes its analytic proof, rather than those computations, responsible for the claimed bound.

Reference numbers [1]–[6] above follow the manuscript’s bibliography.
