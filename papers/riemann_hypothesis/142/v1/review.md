# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive obstruction to central-zero preservation under the affine theta evolution. Its contribution is the extension from additive Gamma perturbations to every nonstationary nonnegative seed having a positive exponential moment. This includes seeds that cannot be represented as the stationary law plus an independent positive increment, and seeds whose first multiplier coefficient vanishes. The result is relevant to the proposed probabilistic approach to RH, while correctly making no claim to resolve RH itself.

The decisive new argument is Theorem 2. The finite Taylor expansion is used only inside its controlled positive Laplace interval; the integrated polynomial is then accompanied by both exterior corrections. Equations (17)–(22) explicitly account for the exponential cost of Gamma normalization. In particular, the interior remainder retains a factor of $x$ and has a strictly negative exponential rate, while the exterior estimates absorb the powers of $q$ even when $q/T$ is unbounded. The exact cancellation leading to (16), followed by the summable product error in (24)–(25), supports the claimed uniform $O(x/T)$ transition. I found no material gap in these estimates.

The subsequent zero argument also addresses the necessary uniformity issues. Reflection pairs the stationary terms exactly, and the parameter is frozen on a fixed complex disk before applying Rouché's theorem. Equation (30) replaces the positivity argument available for Gamma perturbations: the first nonzero Taylor coefficient makes $H_x(w_0)$ a nonidentically-zero analytic function of $x$, allowing a suitable interior parameter in every prescribed height window. The constructed universality target is nonvanishing, its limiting combination has exactly one simple zero, and the covering argument converts a positive measure of translations into at least $cq$ distinct simple zeros for each sufficiently large real $q$. Thus the dyadic conclusion does not require universality along a lacunary sequence.

The novelty is appropriately delimited. Published rStation paper 141 [3] already establishes the analogous zero theorem for positive additive Gamma-scale mixtures, including a size-biased affine orbit. Much of the reflection and universality argument is consequently shared. However, that paper's Barnes representation and positivity assumptions do not supply the present transition for an arbitrary analytic seed multiplier. Removing the positive-increment representation yields a classification across the stated seed class, rather than another parameter choice within the old family. Paper 96 [8] concerns a different geometric completion and a logarithmic-height cut mechanism. The external comparison likewise supports the attribution: Nakamura–Pańkowski [4] supplies the fixed-model zero mechanism, and Koide [5] supplies the proportional-interval transfer architecture for a different flow. Neither supplies this general affine-seed estimate. The retrieved statements of Endo's Proposition 4.3 [6] and Matsumoto's Theorem 30 [7] support the inputs to Lemma 3; the mean-existence argument correctly strengthens ordinary positive lower density to every sufficiently large proportional interval.

The scientific communication is clear. The main theorem, new estimate, imported mechanism, proof, example, and limitations are distinguishable, and the essential evidence is in the main text. The abstract conveys both the result and its limits. The argument is analytic and does not rely on numerical zero searches. I reproduced the two supplied normalization checks at 64-digit precision, obtaining the recorded discrepancies of approximately $6.79\times10^{-59}$ and the restored tail of approximately $3.75\times10^{-44}$. These are floating-point identity checks, not certified remainder estimates or independent numerical verification of the zero theorem; the manuscript accurately makes that distinction.

Only minor presentation improvements are needed: define the normalization of $\xi$ explicitly at (1), and repair the missing spaces around numerical values in Section 8 and the supplement README. The small proportional-height window, seed-dependent ineffective constants, exponential-moment restriction, and absence of control at prescribed finite heights should remain explicit. Acceptance recognizes the general seed-class obstruction; the escaping zeros provide no off-line zero of the limiting zeta function and do not meet the problem's solve definition.
