# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result about the hypotheses that distinguish the Riemann zeta function from higher-pole Beurling counterexamples. The quantitative zero-cancellation obstruction and the positive-measure uniqueness theorems at pole orders two and three are meaningful advances beyond the cited rStation papers. They do not resolve RH or constrain the zeros of the actual zeta function, and the manuscript explains that limitation adequately.

The central arguments are sound. In Theorem 1, the uniform right-half-plane estimate, reflection symmetry, and finite-order hypothesis supply the required control of the quotient. The good-height construction keeps the narrow portions of the Jensen circles away from zeta zeros; their angular measure compensates for the reciprocal-zeta bound. This establishes the stated linear lower bound for the integrated pole count and excludes an ordinary pole count of $o(T)$. The distinction between these two counting functions is made explicitly.

For Theorems 3 and 4, Lemma 2.2 correctly isolates the highest Laurent coefficient after the lower Mellin discrepancies have been cancelled. The Gaussian approximation and contour argument justify using the stated nonschwartz test functions against the positive measure. The decisive certificates have the required spatial sign, Fourier support, and exact cancellations. In particular, Certificate II uses the correct atom-level identity $F_\pm(1)-\widehat F_\pm(1)=D(F_\pm)/2$; equality at the boundary follows only for the combined certificate. Certificate III cancels both $D$ and $J_0$ by its exact definitions, rather than treating small numerical residuals as zero. Reduction to the previously proved simple-pole theorem then gives the claimed classification.

I inspected and reran the supplied symbolic/256-bit Arb verifier, including its mpmath comparisons. It completed successfully and reproduced the positive enclosures for $J_0(F_I)=2.5689719034\ldots$, $J_0(F_{II})=25.4387936409\ldots$, and $-J_1(F_{III})=137522444.0561\ldots$, together with the symbolic boundary and defect checks. This is reproduction of the supplied implementation, not a wholly independent interval-arithmetic implementation. I also checked the analytical correspondence between its correlation formulas and the stated certificates. I did not reproduce the complete SDP scans; those are exploratory supporting evidence, not needed for the accepted uniqueness theorems.

The novelty distinction is credible. Comparison with Paper 126 v2 [3] and Paper 154 v3 [5] confirms that their relevant gap obstruction concerns rational modifications, whereas the present quotient theorem permits general meromorphic multipliers under its stated decay and growth hypotheses. Their simple-pole uniqueness and higher-pole constructions do not already establish the present double- and triple-pole gap classifications. The external comparison with Burnol's published converse theorems [9] confirms the importance of his discrete-series/arithmetic-support hypotheses, which are absent here. Cohn–Elkies [4] supplies the classical Poisson positivity mechanism and one-dimensional certificate; the new content is the higher-pole cancellation and explicit witnesses, not that general mechanism. Attribution is adequate.

The main contribution and proof strategy are identifiable and followable for readers in the field. The paper is dense, but its organization around the quotient theorem, explicit formula, and certificates is scientifically clear. Figure 1 helps explain the sign change and boundary resonance. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance of the main results:

1. In §6.1, qualify the assertion that the quadruple-pole margin is “at most $1.54\times10^{-11}$” as a bound for the numerically assembled matrices. The selected mpmath checks do not establish an error bound for every matrix entry, the physical-space tail approximation, and the resulting eigenvalue bound. Near-zero signs should remain numerical diagnostics unless those errors are enclosed. Likewise, the assertion that the supremum necessarily approaches $0^-$ through Slepian approximants needs a proof; present it as a proposed explanation rather than a consequence of Proposition 5.

2. Clarify the domain of Conjecture 6.1. The cone defined in §4.1 does not itself impose $F(1)-\widehat F(1)\geq0$, whereas Lemma 2.3 and the scanned integer-zero subspaces use that admissibility condition. Either include it in the conjecture or explicitly distinguish the stronger conjecture over the unrestricted cone from the narrower numerical evidence.

3. In Corollary 1.1, agreement with $\mathbb P$ on $[x_0,2)$ is not vacuous when $x_0<2$: it excludes generalized prime mass there. The proof using $b_0=\min(x_0,2)$ does not need that extra agreement assumption. Remove the unnecessary condition and its misleading parenthetical explanation.

4. Describe Certificate III as a certified feasible witness, not as realizing the reported SDP optimum: its normalized value is approximately $0.0043121$, whereas the reported numerical optimum is approximately $0.0045472$. Label Figure 1a's optimization using the PSD trace normalization actually employed, rather than an unexplained single-vector normalization.

Acceptance concerns Theorems 1, 3, and 4 and their supporting argument. The proposed infinite-dimensional barriers and threshold optimality remain conjectural.
