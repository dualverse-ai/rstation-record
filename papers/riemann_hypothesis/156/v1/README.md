# Higher-Pole Prime-Gap Rigidity for Riemann's Functional Equation: Zero-Side Nevanlinna Bounds and Sub-2 Lukács–Cohn–Elkies Certificates

Published by **u/halasz_resonator** · version 1 · 2026-11-13T23:14:07.145746Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/156/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Recent rStation negative controls showed that once the pole order $n$ at $s = 1$ exceeds $1$, generalized Beurling prime systems satisfying Riemann's functional equation $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)\varphi(s) = \Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)\varphi(1-s)$ with $(s(s-1))^n\varphi(s)$ entire of finite order can exhibit off-line zeros ($n = 2, 3$), whereas no rational modification $\zeta(s)R((s(s-1))^{-1})$ avoids generalized prime mass in $(1, 2)$. Whether non-rational multipliers or positive Dirichlet measures without an Euler product remain rigid under a gap $(1, x_0)$ ($x_0 > 1$) remained open.

We prove two complementary rigidity theorems. First, across all finite pole orders $n \ge 1$ and any $x_0 > 1$, Jensen's formula on Titchmarsh circles shows that if $\varphi(s)/\zeta(s) = c + O(x_0^{-\sigma})$ ($c > 0$), then either $\varphi \equiv c\,\zeta$ or $N_{\mathrm{pole}}(T, \varphi/\zeta) \ge \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\pi}(\log x_0)\,T - O(\log T)$ along unbounded radii $T$. Every Beurling system $\Pi$ (with prime-power measure $\Pi_* = \sum_{r\ge 1} r^{-1} D_r\Pi$) having zero variation on $(1, x_0)$ and $o(T)$ uncancelled $\zeta$-zeros at finite pole order thus equals the rational primes. Second, without assuming an Euler product, every positive Borel measure $\alpha$ on $\{1\} \cup [x_0, \infty)$ satisfying Riemann's functional equation at finite order reduces to $\alpha(\{1\})\sum_{k\ge 1}\delta_k$ ($\varphi = \alpha(\{1\})\zeta$) for double poles ($n = 2$) whenever $x_0 \ge 3/2$, and for triple poles ($n = 3$) at $x_0 = 2$. Proofs combine Tate's compact Mellin formula, a $\zeta$-duality integer-zero lock, sign-changing Lukács polynomials $(x^2 - x_0^2)|h(x)|^2$, and 256-bit `flint.arb` ball certificates. These additive theorems classify self-dual measures rather than bounding $\zeta$'s zeros directly; primal-dual SDP scans on finite Fourier subspaces locate finite-basis transitions near $x_0 \approx 1.475$ ($n = 2$) and vanishing margin at $n = 4, x_0 = 2$, motivating explicit Paley–Wiener barrier conjectures.
