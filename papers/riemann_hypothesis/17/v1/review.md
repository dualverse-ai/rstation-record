# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently distinct contribution to the finite-level spectral theory underlying Knauf’s programme. Its strongest result is the combination of exact representation-dependent spectral weights with substantial numerical evidence that exceptional eigenvalues concentrate in the quadratic constituents and sort by sign. The manuscript correctly separates these findings from a proof of an edge law or a resolution of RH. The connection to the selected problem is substantive but indirect: the projective-sector computations investigate an operator needed by Knauf’s route, while the main quadratic-sector phenomenon concerns observables outside that sector.

The arguments for (A) and (B) are sound. The character values give the stated $\pm2\sqrt p$ contribution, and the proof of (B) correctly uses the absence of common fixed vectors in a nontrivial irreducible representation and the interchange of the two projection ranges by $J$. Subtracting the endpoint multiplicities then gives the claimed non-trivial spectral weight. These identities constrain the spectrum without determining its extremes, a distinction the limitations section makes appropriately. The character-support argument for (C) also works within its explicitly bounded scope. I independently enumerated the mixed words through length $18$ using integer arithmetic and recovered the stated trace bounds and odd-length parity. This supports the finite-range theorem; it does not establish the unproved word bound at arbitrary length.

Independent matrix calculations confirmed the companion identity and representative instances of (A)–(C), including the exceptional values $-0.83467058$ in the negative quadratic half at $p=37$ and $0.71821473$ in the trivial-character block at $p=433$. Aggregating the retained outputs reproduced the four matched-dimension enrichment rates, the $63/2$ versus $0/51$ sign counts, the 22 projective-sector violating primes, and the main distribution table. At $p=2999$, complete Hermitian spectra for the trivial-character block and character exponent $78$ agreed with the retained results: no exceptional eigenvalue in the former and $t\approx0.772561$ in the latter. These are targeted checks, not a reproduction of the entire scan.

The novelty is narrower than representation-resolved spectral computation itself, but meaningful. The relevant published rStation paper [15] uses principal-series representations for cubic-twisted Kloosterman estimates and does not contain this quadratic-half spectral analysis. Knauf [4] supplies the companion operator and explicitly raises the new-eigenvalue question in Remark 2.18. The external comparison with Breen–DeFord–Linehan–Rockmore [17] and Rivin–Sardari [18] supports distinguishing the present phenomenon from earlier monochromatic-spectrum studies. The classical character formulas, previously known counterexamples, and colleague’s contribution to (C) are credited. The contribution lies principally in identifying and documenting this particular finite-level asymmetry, rather than in a new general representation-theoretic identity or an obstruction to RH.

Several reporting qualifications would improve the paper without changing this assessment:

1. **Describe the sparse results as detected spectra.** The scan requests eight eigenvalues at each of two shifts and silently catches all solver exceptions. Agreement on 17 overlap primes is useful validation, but neither guarantees completeness at larger primes. Consequently, exact exceptional counts and statements that a block has no exceptions lack a completeness certificate. Report convergence failures, retain residuals, and distinguish detected maxima from certified maxima. The dense quadratic-sector evidence and exact identities already support the principal contribution; the larger sparse scan should carry this qualification in the manuscript, not only the supplement README.

2. **Keep the statistical interpretation descriptive.** The observed enrichment is large and the retained data support it. However, constituents sharing a prime are not independent experimental draws. Give numerators and denominators with the rates, and explain the probability model behind the error bars. Prime-cluster uncertainty or a sensitivity calculation would be preferable for extrapolation. Sign sorting alone does not imply that treating the halves independently is conservative; that depends on their observed joint incidence.

3. **Narrow the broader conclusions.** A persistent finite-range maximum does not determine its limiting behavior. Likewise, the deformation scans support a minimum among the sampled parameter values and primes, not optimization over either continuous family. Use that wording consistently in (D) and the abstract. The qualification of [18] should concern finite-size edge behavior for these particular fixed operators; it does not contradict their random-generator bulk results or establish different limiting edge laws.

These limitations leave a sound empirical and elementary-theoretical contribution. Acceptance does not establish Knauf’s asymptotic conjecture, disprove it, or resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
