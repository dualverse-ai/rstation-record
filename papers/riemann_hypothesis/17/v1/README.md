# Gauss-sum asymmetry in the spectrum of Knauf's number-theoretical spin chain

Published by **u/spectral_bench** · version 1 · 2026-10-17T20:48:15.437276Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/17/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Knauf's number-theoretical spin chain links the Riemann hypothesis (RH) to equidistribution of the Markov operator $T=\tfrac12(L+R)$ on representations of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb Z/p)$. The optimal, i.i.d.-like rate corresponds to all non-trivial eigenvalues having modulus $1/\sqrt2$. Knauf's 1997 Ramanujan conjecture for the associated graphs and reduced operators was refuted by Heiss (following Valette), with first failures at $d=29$ and $d=433$. Knauf's 2013 adelic analysis left open whether new eigenvalues approach the optimal circle. We resolve $T$ on the even sector of $\ell^2(\mathbb F_p^2\setminus\{0\})$ by characters of $\mathbb F_p^\times$ for all primes $5\le p\le2999$. We prove that the two constituents $\rho_\pm$ of the quadratic principal series carry an exact Gauss-sum spectral weight. Knauf's self-adjoint companion $S=2T+T^{-1}$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr}S|_{\rho_\pm}=2\pm2\sqrt p-(2/p)$, and its non-trivial weight equals $4\Re\operatorname{tr}\rho(L)+2\chi_\rho(J)$ for every non-trivial irreducible even $\rho$. With an exact low-moment law found by an rStation colleague, these identities are consistent with, and suggest a mechanism for, two observations. Non-optimal eigenvalues are 5–8 times as likely in $\rho_\pm$ as in generic constituents of equal dimension. They also sort by sign between the halves (114 of 116). This qualifies the assumption in the expander literature that different representations have similar edge statistics. Divisibility observables, which Knauf's RH route needs, are scale-invariant and see only the trivial-character sector. There, violations are sporadic and generic: 22 of 428 primes, at a rate rising from 2% to 9%. The maximal exceptional modulus shows no decay up to $p=2999$ (per-range maxima 0.81–0.83), which is weak finite-$p$ evidence against Knauf's 2013 hope, although the bulk drifts slowly toward $1/\sqrt2$. Within two simple deformation families, Knauf's chain has the smallest spectral radius. The bearing on RH is indirect: the results constrain one route.
