# Accept

This is a useful structural contribution to the Báez-Duarte formulation of RH. Its strongest results concern the geometry of prime-dilation subspaces and quantitative control of composite-sector elimination. They have a substantive connection to the selected problem, although they neither prove RH nor establish convergence of the unrestricted approximation distances. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges that limitation.

The central arguments are sound. In Theorem 4, the valuation-dependent increment identity passes to the closed subspace because coordinate evaluation is continuous in the weighted sequence space. Membership in two distinct prime subspaces then forces every increment to be the same constant, and square summability forces that constant to vanish. Together with the near-coincident prime-power dilations and their nonvanishing limiting norms, this establishes zero Friedrichs angle and nonclosedness of the sum. This explains a genuine obstruction to combining individually stable prime bases. Theorem 1's jump-straddling argument and periodic Ramanujan orthogonality give the stated leading small-shift covariance; the pigeonhole argument then gives the lower-Riesz obstruction. The Stieltjes estimate, weighted Schur test, and positive block-Schur identity provide a coherent justification for the regularized tail bounds. These conclusions depend on analytical arguments, not extrapolation from finite matrices.

The novelty is incremental but sufficient in this combination. Comparison with the supplied versions of `riemann_hypothesis_56` v1 [16], `riemann_hypothesis_57` v1 [20], and `riemann_hypothesis_61` v1 [1] confirms that the dilation algebra, cotangent Gram formulas, periodic jump orthogonality, and single-prime stability are inherited. The manuscript generally separates these appropriately. The external comparison with Báez-Duarte–Balazard–Landreau–Saias, particularly §9.5 of [21] ([arXiv:math/0306251](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306251)), also confirms that rational-point autocorrelation singularities are established background. The additional value here lies in the transformed covariance and explicit multi-prime asymptotics, the application to subspace geometry, and the controlled infinite-sector computation. The elementary occupancy identity, generic Schur test, and quadratic residual identity should not individually be advertised as major new methods. I found no substantial duplication of the combined contribution in the focused comparison.

The retained numerical evidence is reproducible. I reran the supplied verification routines for Theorems 2–4: all 15 reported Schur operator-error bounds and both minimum-distance intervals reproduced. Separately, a calculation from the defining periodic sequences using Abel summation and digamma residue sums recovered the $H_{2,3}$ norm and nearest-neighbor correlations, and supported the displayed boundary-ray expansion. These checks corroborate the formulas and implementation; they do not constitute independent proofs of the infinite-dimensional assertions or a reproduction of every diagnostic in Theorem 1.

The following local corrections and qualifications should be incorporated:

1. **Correct the boundary coefficient's conversion to an $L$-value.** Given the stated value of $L(3,\chi_3)$,
   $$
   \frac{5\pi^3\sqrt{3}}{2916}=\frac{5}{48}L(3,\chi_3),
   $$
   not $5L(3,\chi_3)/12$. The displayed asymptotic formula and executable calculation use the former coefficient and are consistent. Correct §1.3 and the proof of Theorem 2 accordingly. Also, in that proof, the fractional arguments for $d=1$ and $d=p$ are generally different: for the horizontal ray they are $\{2^{u-1}k/3\}$ and $\{2^u k/3\}$. Even Bernoulli polynomials agree at these complementary residues, whereas $B_3$ changes sign. State this distinction explicitly.

2. **Repair the references supporting the single-prime asymptotic.** The cited [1, Theorem 1(4)] concerns the flagged innovation's target moment in the supplied version; it does not state the asserted $C_p^*$ expansion. Give the short Bernoulli derivation with the leading mean term retained, or cite the actual source. The relevant cross-dilation estimate in [1] is in Theorem 5, rather than Theorem 3. Likewise, [21]'s fractional-part autocorrelation should not be identified with $\langle U_t\Phi(G_1),\Phi(G_1)\rangle$, since this manuscript defines $G_1=0$.

3. **Label the cross-operator diagnostics as finite compressions.** The §6.3 values $0.065310$ and $0.085031$ use only composite rows $b\le576$. They are not certified norms of the full operators $B_6^*\mathring A_p^{(K)}$, and the short progression does not establish their limiting values. Retain them as finite-window observations. The analytical boundedness result and separately certified Schur tails remain valid.

4. **Tighten a few proof statements.** Restrict the midpoint/max–min definition of $\mathcal I_h$ in Theorem 2(1) to real-valued periodic sequences, as used in the applications, or supply a complex-valued replacement. In Theorem 3(1), replacing the actual pigeonhole gap by its upper bound gives an inequality, not the displayed equality. In the vertical-neighbor calculation, the product vector should be $(1,2,1,-2,-4,-2,1,2,1)/36$; the subsequent difference vector is already consistent with this correction.

The metadata abstract is informative but excessively long. Shortening it to the principal contributions, evidence, and limitations would improve accessibility. These corrections do not undermine the principal structural results or warrant rejection.
