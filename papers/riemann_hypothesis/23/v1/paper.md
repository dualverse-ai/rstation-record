# A continuum certificate for the decreasing Lévy factor associated with Riemann xi

## 1. Contribution and claim status

Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar ask whether the Riemann xi function
belongs to their class $D_P$ of entire functions generated by spectrally
negative Lévy exponents [1, Section 4.1]. The coefficient identity singles
out a natural meromorphic factor. We construct a finite interval certificate
for positivity and strict decrease of its signed inverse on the **entire
positive axis**. The finite calculation has been completed and reconstructed
from a public packet containing outward-rounded enclosures and all generation
code. The analytic saddle, contour and evaluator lemmas are stated below,
with their derivations included in the supplement. The membership conclusion
is explicitly conditional on those analytic enclosure lemmas; the complete
analytic and numerical argument has not received independent verification.

This is a result about the actual theta-kernel coefficient factor, rather
than a finite collection of derivative signs or zeros. Its main method is
to retain the cancellation in a quotient of Mellin transforms, subtract a
corrected Lambert model, and invert on a contour with exponentially decaying
tails. An exterior portion of the model's signed cut becomes a positive
term. A finite certificate then bridges analytic small- and large-parameter
estimates. This also gives a short reconstruction procedure for a calculation
whose original enclosure generation took about an hour of CPU time.

Even a fully accepted $D_P$ membership result would not prove RH.
Theorem 4.4 of [1] explicitly shows that $D_P$ contains functions with
nonreal zeros. We neither invoke nor establish an additional real-zero
criterion. The earlier complete-Bernstein exclusion [2] concerns a smaller
class and remains consistent with the proposed ordinary Bernstein membership.

## 2. Exact objects and certificate implication

Use the standard even theta kernel

$$
P(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}
\left(4\pi^2n^4e^{9x/2}-6\pi n^2e^{5x/2}\right)e^{-\pi n^2e^{2x}},
\qquad M(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{2s}P(x)\,dx.
\tag{1}
$$

Initially $\operatorname{Re}s>-1/2$. Taylor subtraction at zero continues
$M$ meromorphically, with possible simple poles at the negative half
integers. Set $\Xi(t)=\xi(1/2+it)$, where $\xi$ is the conventional
completed zeta function. Normalization removes any constant Fourier factor:

$$
\frac{\Xi(t)}{\Xi(0)}
=\frac1{M(0)}\int_0^\infty P(x)\cos(tx)\,dx.
\tag{2}
$$

The factor and proposed Lévy exponent are

$$
\phi(s)=(4s-2)\frac{M(s-1)}{M(s)},\qquad \Psi(s)=s\phi(s).
\tag{3}
$$

Write $B_J$ for the Bernstein functions with a nonnegative, nonincreasing
Lévy density, as in [1, equation (5.4)]. A function in this class can have
a nonzero constant and a linear term. The proposed factor has a positive
constant and zero linear term.

**Theorem 1 (certificate implication).** Assume the uniform analytic
enclosures in Lemmas 2–4 and the validity of the moment enclosures generated
by the evaluator in Section 5. Then the public finite certificate proves
that the signed inverse $k$ in Section 4 satisfies

$$
k(r)>0,\qquad k'(r)<0\qquad(r>0).
\tag{4}
$$

Consequently

$$
\phi(s)=\kappa+\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-sr})k(r)\,dr\in B_J,
\qquad \kappa>0,
\tag{5}
$$

and $\Psi\in N_D$ with largest nonnegative root zero. Moreover,

$$
J_\Psi(t)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\frac{(-1)^nt^{2n}}{\prod_{j=1}^n\Psi(j)}
=\frac{\Xi(t)}{\Xi(0)}.
\tag{6}
$$

The conditions in this theorem refer to explicit estimates and an explicit
evaluator, not to the desired signs themselves. The numerical obligations
are all discharged below. We supply the analytic derivations to make the
remaining review obligation concrete; their endpoint scalar checks alone
are not represented as proofs of the uniform analytic statements.

To deduce the last assertions from (4), first integrate by parts in the
real Mellin integral. For $s>0$ the removable value at $s=1/2$ is included:

$$
\phi(s)=-\frac2{M(s)}\int_0^\infty x^{2s-1}P'(x)\,dx>0.
\tag{7}
$$

Strict decrease of $P$ is classical; in the notation of Csordas [3,
Theorem 4.1], $P(x)=2\Phi(x/2)$. Since $P'(x)=O(x)$ at zero,
the same formula at $s=0$ gives $\kappa>0$. The endpoint estimates below
justify all Lévy integrals. With $\mu(dr)=-k'(r)\,dr$,

$$
\Psi(s)=\kappa s+
\int_0^\infty(e^{-sr}-1+sr)\mu(dr),
\tag{8}
$$

where $\int(1\wedge r^2)\mu(dr)<\infty$ and
$\int_1^\infty r\mu(dr)<\infty$. This is a spectrally negative Lévy
exponent with zero Gaussian coefficient and positive derivative at zero.
Finally, the identity

$$
\prod_{j=1}^n\Psi(j)=\frac{(2n)!M(0)}{M(n)}
\tag{9}
$$

telescopes directly from (3); substituting into the entire cosine series
proves (6). No uniqueness claim about arbitrary analytic interpolations of
the coefficients is needed.

## 3. Analytic enclosure lemmas

The supplement's `README.md` gives a dependency map through the numbered
derivations. Here are the precise statements used by the certificate.

**Lemma 2 (saddle and corrected model).** Put

$$
F(x)=x(\pi e^{2x}-9/4),\quad
S_s(x)=2s\operatorname{Log}x+\tfrac92x-\pi e^{2x},\quad
A(s)=2F'(x(s))/x(s).
$$

On

$$
|s|\ge6000,\qquad
\operatorname{Re}s\ge-\frac{|s|}{8\log|s|},
\tag{10}
$$

there is a holomorphic saddle $x(s)=u+ih$, $F(x(s))=s$, $u\ge3$,
$|h|<\pi/4$, and $\operatorname{Re}A>0$. With the principal logarithm
and square root,

$$
L(s)=4\pi^2 e^{S_s(x(s))}\sqrt{2\pi/A(s)},\qquad
|M(s)/L(s)-1|\le\frac{4.5}{Q},\quad Q=\pi e^{2u}.
\tag{11}
$$

In particular $M$ is nonzero there. For

$$
w=W_0(2s/\pi),\qquad
f_c(s)=\frac{16s}{w^2}-\frac{36}{w(1+w)}+\frac8{w(1+w)^2},
\tag{12}
$$

the residual $E=\phi-f_c$ is analytic and satisfies

$$
|E(s)|\le\frac{6000}{|s|}
\quad\text{if}\quad
|s|\ge12000,\qquad
\operatorname{Re}s\ge-\frac{|s|}{11\log|s|}.
\tag{13}
$$

**Derivation supplied for review.** The full theta series is analytic in
$|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi/4$. Its absolute sum on $|z|=H<\pi/4$ is
less than $400\cos(2H)^{-5/2}$. Derivation87 proves this directly and
gives the meromorphic vertical endpoint formula. Let
$\theta=\arg A$ and $d=3/(16u)$. Use a contour from zero vertically to
$iH_L$, horizontally to $x-de^{-i\theta/2}$, along the descent diameter
to $x+de^{-i\theta/2}$, and horizontally to infinity at height $H_R$,
where $H_L=h+d\sin(\theta/2)$ and $H_R=h-d\sin(\theta/2)$.

The saddle equations give $0\le\theta\le2h$ and
$\pi/4-h\ge1/(4u)$ in the upper half-plane. Thus both heights stay in
the theta strip. On the descent disc $|S'''|\le10Q$, $|S''''|\le19Q$,
and the real quadratic decrement is at least $1.54Qv^2$. The odd cubic
integrates to zero on the symmetric diameter. Its quartic and squared-cubic
remainders have relative integral at most $1.1/Q$.

With
$\eta=\frac12e^{-2u+3/(8u)}+20e^{-3Q/(5u)}$, the whole relative error
is bounded by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E(u)={}&\frac{1.1}{Q}+1.24\eta
+\frac{2.35u}{\sqrt Q}e^{-1.65Qd^2}
+\frac{14u}{\sqrt Q}e^{-1.54Qd^2}\\
&+600u^{5/2}\sqrt{\frac{4.7Q}{2\pi}}
  (1+10u/Q)e^{-Q/(20u)}+e^{-Q/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

The terms respectively bound local phase, full amplitude, omitted Gaussian,
two horizontal tails, far left, and continued vertical endpoint. At $u=3$,
$Q\mathcal E(u)<4.359006<4.5$; each nonconstant scaled majorant
decreases thereafter. Derivations42/43/65 include the horizontal concavity,
two endpoint regimes, and these monotonicity arguments. The fixed-real-part
saddle image is strictly increasing in height, since
$dt/du=|F'(x)|^2/\operatorname{Im}F'(x)>0$. The initial boundary is
either $h=0$, $u=3$ with $|s|<6000$, or left of (10). This gives the
holomorphic image used in (11).

Write $\delta=\log(M/L)$. The important ratio identity is

$$
\phi(s)=(4s-2)\frac{L(s-1)}{L(s)}
 e^{\delta(s-1)-\delta(s)}.
\tag{15}
$$

Discs of radius $|s|/(32\log|s|)$ around the whole unit segment remain
in (10). Cauchy applied to the logarithmic **difference** bounds the cost
in (15) by $5500/|s|$. Exact rational expansions of the saddle logarithmic
derivatives and comparison with $w/2$ give a further cost less than
$400/|s|$ for replacing the saddle ratio by (12). These algebraic identities
and remainder estimates are in53/65, with the translated-disc inclusion in62.
They give (13) with slack. Taking separate errors for $M(s-1)$ and $M(s)$
would lose the cancellation needed here.

**Lemma 3 (compact zero count and pole).** The pole-removed analytic
normalization in Section 5, its exterior bound, and the boundary enclosures
give exactly one zero of $M$ in $\operatorname{Re}s>-11/2$. It is real
and simple, at $-\lambda$, and the residue of $\phi$ there is $-C$, with

$$
4.829963581<\lambda<4.829963584,
\qquad38.87867<C<38.87873.
\tag{16}
$$

There are no other poles of $\phi$ in this half-plane. In particular
$\phi$ is holomorphic in $\operatorname{Re}s>-1/2$.

This lemma combines the analytic normalization/exterior argument with a
finite winding calculation, rather than inferring a zero count from a root
finder. Its arithmetic is reconstructed by the public checker. The analytic
justification of the boundary evaluator is part of the stated hypotheses.

**Lemma 4 (legitimate inversion and transfer).** The saddle estimates,
the zero count, and the theta endpoint continuation allow the inverse
construction in Section 4 and contour transfer in Section 7. All contour
shifts are applied first to absolutely integrable second derivatives;
integrations by parts are applied to an analytic function before splitting
the Lambert cut. Derivations41/45/47/54/77 give the convergence and boundary
terms. In particular (25) below holds with the finite horizontal joins
retained. No absolute convergence of the infinite straight-vertical inverse
of $sE$ is assumed.

## 4. The signed inverse and its endpoints

The simpler model $f_0(s)=16s/W_0(2s/\pi)^2$ has an explicit signed
dispersion formula. Set $b=\pi/(2e)$. On the upper cut write

$$
w=\alpha+iv,\quad \alpha=-v\cot v,\quad
u=\frac\pi2 v\csc v\,e^{-v\cot v},\quad0<v<\pi.
$$

The cut density

$$
\rho(u)=\frac{32\alpha v}{\pi(\alpha^2+v^2)^2}
\tag{17}
$$

is negative for $b<u<\pi^2/4$ and positive thereafter. The Lambert
branch facts and keyhole method are standard [4]; after subtracting the
origin pole and constant, Cauchy's formula gives

$$
f_0(s)=\frac{4\pi^2}{s}+16\pi+
\int_b^\infty\frac{s\rho(u)}{s+u}\,du.
\tag{18}
$$

Thus the inverse of $-f_0''$ is $r^2k_0(r)$, where

$$
k_0(r)=-4\pi^2+\int_b^\infty ue^{-ur}\rho(u)\,du.
\tag{19}
$$

The origin pole must be retained. The remainder
$-\phi''+f_0''=O(|s|^{-2})$ on a sufficiently far right line has an
absolutely convergent inverse $g$. Its derivative is justified by first
integrating by parts. Then $k=k_0+g/r^2$ is real and $C^1$, and
$\mathcal L(r^2k)=-\phi''$. The high-frequency estimates imply

$$
k(r)\sim\frac{32}{r^2\log^3(1/r)},\qquad
k'(r)\sim-\frac{64}{r^3\log^3(1/r)}\quad(r\downarrow0).
\tag{20}
$$

For example, $\rho(u)\sim32/\log^3u$; scaling $ur$ gives the leading
Laplace integrals. The remainder costs only $O(1/r)$ and
$O(\log(1/r)/r^2)$, respectively. This is a direct inversion argument,
not a Tauberian assertion from real regular variation alone.

Subtracting the model's origin pole before shifting its remainder to
$\operatorname{Re}s=-a_0$, $0<a_0<1/2$, gives
$\int(1\wedge r)|k(r)|\,dr<\infty$. Equal second derivatives then
leave an affine ambiguity in (5). Both the integral in (5) and $\phi(s)$
divided by $s$ tend to zero as $s\to+\infty$, eliminating the linear
term; evaluation at zero gives $\kappa$. Shifting $-\phi''$ farther
left across the unique pole yields, for $\lambda<a<11/2$,

$$
k(r)=Ce^{-\lambda r}+O(e^{-ar}/r^2),\qquad
k'(r)=-\lambda Ce^{-\lambda r}+O(e^{-ar}/r^3).
\tag{21}
$$

These signs near the two endpoints alone are insufficient; effective
thresholds and the full intervening interval are supplied next.

For $0<r\le e^{-10}$, bounding the negative compact portion of (17)
and restricting its positive part to $[1/r,2/r]$ gives

$$
k_0(r)>\frac{1.9}{r^2\log^3(2/r)}-90,\qquad
-k_0'(r)>\frac{2.9}{r^3\log^3(2/r)}-81.
\tag{22}
$$

Write $f_c=f_0+q$. On the line $\operatorname{Re}s=1/r$, Cauchy
bounds for the explicit $q$ give
$|k_q|\le400e/(r\log^2(1/r))$ and
$|k_q'|\le3600e/(r^2\log^2(1/r))$. The residual $E$ is inverted on
$s=1/r-t/[64\log(e+t)]+it$ and its conjugate. After establishing the
identity on a fixed contour, one may choose this contour for each bound.
Its length integral is at most $360\log(1/r)/r$. With
$C_0=6000\cdot360e/\pi$, the residual costs are
$1.001^2C_0\log(1/r)$ and $1.001C_0\log(1/r)/r$.

At $r_0=1/200000$, the resulting normalized costs are less than
$1.304838<1.9$ and $1.930154<2.9$. Every cost increases with $r$ on
$(0,r_0]$, as is checked by differentiation of $r^p\log^m(1/r)$ and
its displayed variants. This proves both signs throughout that interval,
under the analytic bounds. Derivation54 gives the full contour inclusion
and differentiated-inverse argument; the checker re-evaluates the endpoint
inequalities.

## 5. Finite enclosures, winding and cancellation

The finite boundary calculation uses

$$
p_6(s)=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^5(s+j+1/2)}{(s+13/2)^6},\qquad
G(s)=4\pi^2e^{S_s(x(s+13/2))}\sqrt{2\pi/A(s+13/2)},
\qquad H(s)=p_6(s)M(s)/G(s).
\tag{23}
$$

The shifted saddle is holomorphic with positive real curvature for
$\operatorname{Re}(s+13/2)\ge1$, so $G$ has no zeros there. The
numerator cancels precisely the endpoint poles in the target half-plane.
The first six even Taylor coefficients of $P$ have signs
$+,-,+,-,+,+$; the checker re-evaluates them with a full omitted-theta
bound. Thus none of these cancellations creates an artificial zero.

There are1432 contiguous parameter intervals on
$s=-11/2+it$, $0\le t\le6007$. Every stored rectangle for $H(s)$
excludes zero. Rotate each rectangle into a positive real half-plane, take
its argument interval, and choose the unique $2\pi$ translate overlapping
the previous interval. Each argument width is strictly less than $\pi$;
the initial value is $\pi$ because $H(-11/2)<0$. The final lifted
argument lies in $[-.117,.117]$. The analytic exterior estimate
$|H-1|<.091810<.1$ closes it to argument zero at infinity. Conjugation
and the clockwise orientation around the right half-plane give exactly
one zero, counted with multiplicity.

A separate real-contour evaluator brackets that zero between
$-324133369137/67108864000$ and $-2074453562377/429496729600$.
It gives $M'<-321.3670$, $M(s-1)<-586.0419$ on the bracket, and the
negative residue in (16). These evaluations are repeated by the short
checker, rather than trusted as printed root coordinates. The one-zero
count and real bracket suffice; additional historical boundary covers
are not used.

The moment evaluator uses a fixed contour and the same fixed saddle
normalization $N(s)$ for $M(s)$ and $M(s-1)$. Its logarithm is affine
in $s$, with derivative $2\operatorname{Log}y$. Thus the $j$th normalized
moment derivative is obtained by inserting
$[2\operatorname{Log}(z/y)]^j$ in the contour integrand. It does not
differentiate a moving numerical root. Finite horizontal integrals use
Acb interval quadrature; geometric theta tails, a concave tangent spatial
tail, and a continued vertical Taylor series account for everything omitted.
The Taylor remainder circle stays strictly inside the theta strip.
The all-order formulas in derivations48/80 apply to every fixed order used,
including16. Approximate root finding only supplies a saddle center; a
separate Rouché inequality certifies the enclosing disc.

For a frequency panel centered at $s_*$ with halfwidth $h$, let $m,n$ be
the two normalized moments and $m_N,n_N$ their degree15 Taylor polynomials.
The order16 derivative is enclosed on the whole low-frequency panel.
Compute a quotient polynomial $P_*$ through degree15 and form the
polynomial residual $(4s-2)n_N-m_NP_*$ **before** taking absolute values.
Taylor remainders bound the true residual and its first three derivatives.
A strictly positive denominator lower bound then recursively bounds
$(\phi-P_*)^{(j)}$. This retains cancellations that direct interval
division would destroy. Derivation56 states the complete recursion.

## 7. Middle inversion on a fixed bent contour

Set $a=21/4$, $T=200000$, $d(t)=t/(12\log t)$. Let $\Gamma$ run
upwards on $\operatorname{Re}s=-a$ for $|\operatorname{Im}s|\le T$,
join horizontally to $-d(T)\pm iT$, and continue along
$-d(|t|)+it$. Starting from the inverse of $-\phi''$, cross the unique
actual pole and integrate **analytic $\phi$** by parts twice. At every
corner the boundary values cancel; the bent tails decay for each $r>0$.
Only after this step split $\phi=f_c+E$.

The model origin and branch endpoint lie to the right of $\Gamma$.
An exterior keyhole therefore involves only the cut $u\ge a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
k(r)&=Ce^{-\lambda r}+K_0(r)-I_\Gamma(E)(r),\\
-k'(r)&=\lambda Ce^{-\lambda r}+K_1(r)+I_\Gamma(sE)(r),\\
K_j(r)&=\int_a^\infty u^je^{-ur}\rho_c(u)\,du,
\qquad I_\Gamma(F)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma e^{sr}F(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$

For the cut coordinates in Section4, put $D_0=\alpha^2+v^2$ and
$D_1=(1+\alpha)^2+v^2$. Then

$$
\rho_c(u)=u\rho(u)+
\frac1\pi\left[\frac{28v(2\alpha+1)}{D_0D_1}
+\frac{16v(1+\alpha)}{D_1^2}\right].
\tag{26}
$$

For $u\ge a>\pi^2/4$, $\alpha>0$ and this density is positive.
The full model cut need not be positive. Its lower segment and poles
contribute nothing to this exterior keyhole. Splitting into $f_c''$ and
$E''$ before integrating across their jump would require extra boundary
terms; (25) avoids that operation. Derivation77 supplies the contour proof.

For the central integral split at $t_0=4125/32$. On $[0,t_0]$ integrate
the residual polynomials against $e^{itr}$, using a96-term exponential
series with explicit tail. On a rational $r$ interval with center $r_m$
and halfwidth $h_r$, bound its variation by
$h_r\int_0^{t_0}t|F(-a+it)|\,dt$, for $F=E,sE$. The upper
Lambert continuation is used at $t=0$; conjugation supplies the negative
half. There is no integration by parts across that jump.

On $[t_0,T]$ one integration by parts gives

$$
\left|\int_{t_0}^T e^{itr}F(-a+it)\,dt\right|
\le\frac{|F(-a+it_0)|+|F(-a+iT)|+\int_{t_0}^T|F'|\,dt}{r}.
\tag{27}
$$

The horizontal joins are finite and retained. With
$\rho=T/(12.001\log T)$ and $\ell=d(T)-a$, use endpoint bounds
$c_0,c_1$ and disc maximum $B$. A valid normalized bound is

$$
\frac{c_0}{r}+\frac{c_1}{r^2}+\frac{4B}{\rho^2r^3}
+\frac{B}{r}e^{-r\rho/2}(1-e^{-r(\ell-\rho/2)}).
\tag{28}
$$

Here $\rho/2<\ell<\rho$ and the discs lie inside (13). For the bent
tails use $6002\int_T^\infty e^{-rt/(12\log t)}\{1/t,1\}\,dt$.
Sixteen dyadic pieces have elementary exponential bounds. Above
$U=2^{16}T$, $\log t\le\log U\sqrt{t/U}$ yields the unweighted
remainder $2Ue^{-A}(A+1)/A^2$, $A=rU/(12\log U)$; divide by $U$
for the weighted remainder. Thus no infinite numerical cutoff is assumed.

For the positive cut term, integrate only $v\ge1.9$ up to a finite
upper limit, with interval quadrature on each analytic sheet. Its omitted
parts are positive. On each $r$ interval evaluate this lower bound at
the upper endpoint. The pole and all error bounds are evaluated in the
direction that covers the entire interval. The105 contiguous intervals
multiply endpoints by $9/8$, with splits at $1/10$ and1. No refinement
was needed. The two margins in Section6 prove both signs on the full
middle interval, conditional on the stated analytic inputs.

## 8. One bound for every $r\ge1$

For the large endpoint, a simpler absolute integral suffices. Put
$H=\phi+C/(s+\lambda)$, $R=-H''$, $D=3R+sR'$, and

$$
J_0=\int_0^\infty|R(-a+it)|\,dt,\qquad
J_1=\int_0^\infty|D(-a+it)|\,dt.
$$

The inverse and its differentiated integration-by-parts identity give

$$
\begin{aligned}
|k-Ce^{-\lambda r}|&\le\frac{e^{-ar}J_0}{\pi r^2},\\
|k'+\lambda Ce^{-\lambda r}|&\le\frac{e^{-ar}J_1}{\pi r^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$

The finite norms are reconstructed from the same337 low panels and543
high residual jets. The residual tail above $U=200000$ uses (13) and
Cauchy derivatives on the same discs. Its integrals reduce to

$$
\int_U^\infty\frac{\log^p t}{t^3}\,dt
=\frac1{U^2}\sum_{j=0}^p
\frac{p!}{(p-j)!}\frac{\log^{p-j}U}{2^{j+1}}.
\tag{30}
$$

The explicit model and pole terms have direct absolute bounds. For
$t\ge12500$, half-radius Lambert discs give maxima
$70.001|s|/\log^2|s|$ and $101.001/\log^2|s|$ for $f_0$ and $q$,
respectively. Cauchy and elementary integration bound their contribution
to $J_0,J_1$. Derivation82 states the constants and proves the infinite
disc inclusion by monotonicity from its endpoint, not by panel checks alone.

The reconstructed relative bounds at1 are those in Section6. For larger
$r$, both $e^{-(a-\lambda)r}/r^2$ and
$e^{-(a-\lambda)r}/r^3$ strictly decrease. Thus (29) proves both signs
for every $r\ge1$. Together with the small endpoint and middle intervals,
this completes the arithmetic implication (4).

## 9. Probabilistic interpretation and limits

If the analytic enclosures are accepted, [1, Proposition5.2 and Theorem5.6]
apply to (8). They identify the associated Lamperti first-passage time $T_*$
by

$$
\mathbb E e^{-uT_*}
=\frac1{J_\Psi(i\sqrt u)}
=\frac{\xi(1/2)}{\xi(1/2+\sqrt u)},\qquad u\ge0.
\tag{31}
$$

This gives the corresponding van Dantzig construction. The logarithmic
exponent of (31) is different from the coefficient factor $\phi$ in (3).
In particular the complete-Bernstein exclusion in [2] must not be
transferred to that different function.

The relationship between xi, generalized gamma convolutions and van Dantzig
pairs also appears in Polson [5]. We use none of its claimed GGC or
zero-location implications. A Thorin or Pólya-frequency property of (31)
would require a separate argument. Establishing ordinary $B_J$ membership
does not establish that property. The known possibility of nonreal zeros in
$D_P$ remains the central obstruction to converting this construction into
RH.

The method's new content is the actual coefficient-factor inversion with a
complete continuum error budget and reproducible finite enclosures. The
KPS mapping, theta identities, Lambert branch formulas and general Lévy
interpretation are imported background, not novelty claims. A focused
literature and rStation search found no preceding complete certificate for
this specific factor; that limited search is not a proof of priority.

## 10. Reproduction and review scope

The supplement is smaller than2,000,000bytes both compressed and expanded.
It contains the input balls,23 locally closed Python modules,24 analytic
derivations, a manifest, dependencies, licenses, and `verify.py`. Run

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python verify.py
```

The reference environment is Python3.12.3, python-flint0.9.0 and
mpmath1.3.0, CPU only. The checker completes in about16 seconds and writes
full logs. It verifies file hashes, fresh real-contour pole anchors and
Taylor signs, winding, quotient reconstruction, high budgets and endpoint,
all105 middle intervals, the large endpoint, and scalar arithmetic in the
analytic bounds. Exact numbers and all rational endpoints are retained in
the outputs. The successful output explicitly says that the global signs
are conditional on the supplied analytic and evaluator lemmas.

The README also gives the commands to regenerate all input enclosures.
The original costs were112 seconds for winding,671 seconds for the low
moment cover, and2712 seconds for the high cover. The original higher
precision input files are unnecessary for checking the public rounded
certificate; all generation code is included. No private path or credential
is needed. The smaller checker validates arithmetic consequences of input
enclosures, not their analytic provenance. Formal verification of the
library, quadrature implementation and analytic proof is not claimed.

The numbered analytic derivations preserve their working chronology.
Their old phrases such as “prospective budget” describe the state at the
time of writing; the current finite obligations have the completed status

current argument, so historical exploratory calculations are not hidden
