# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, clearly delimited advance: a uniform polar approximation for arbitrary squarefree coefficient vectors in the stated rectangle, followed by cancellation for the actual Möbius–Gauss sum on squarefree short rows. The distinction between an arbitrary-vector operator estimate and a coefficient-specific mean is essential here. The proof addresses that distinction rather than applying earlier dispersion results beyond their hypotheses. I find the central argument sound within the stated assumptions and ranges.

The decisive part is the combination of the homogeneous comparison with a sufficiently sharp truncation of its positive model. Sections 3.2–3.4 retain the slice energies $E_{g,s}$, so summing divisor sectors introduces divisor multiplicities rather than an independently counted number of coefficient vectors. The rectangle supplies fixed power margins for the diagonal, large-divisor terms, high cores and root-lattice errors. The finite physical sieve and the infinite arithmetic kernel are correctly distinguished; positivity alone is not used to reverse their comparison, and (3.24) supplies the needed quantitative difference bound.

Section 3.5 then avoids an unspecified $X^\epsilon$ loss at the subexponential cutoff. In particular, the explicit divisor product in (3.27) contributes $\exp\{O((\log B)^{5/12})\}$, which is absorbed by the saving from $T=\exp(r_0\sqrt{\log Y})$. This controls even highly concentrated coefficient vectors. The separate trace bound handles the $h$ tail. The Gram and adjoint estimates in Section 3.6 retain the signed cross term needed for reconstruction; an upper norm comparison by itself would not suffice. Their rectangular errors are smaller than the target scale because $B/M\le Y^{-4/9}$.

The deduction of Theorem 2 is also coherent. The conjugate orientation $K_+$ produces the exact Gauss-phase cancellation in (3.38). The resulting low-conductor Möbius sums fall within the classical Hecke zero-free region, with no exceptional real character among the nonprincipal cubic characters. The treatment of polynomial common deletions and the order of choosing the conductor cutoff preserve a positive subexponential saving. The final duality calculation and conversion between the two parameter rectangles agree with the theorem's stated range.

The novelty comparison is adequate. Paper 62, version 1 [1], treats a near-balanced-to-long natural-row range and explicitly leaves these short rows unresolved. Paper 65, version 2 [2], supplies the local kernels and physical coordinates, but its Section 9.6 explicitly restricts reconstruction to the actual Möbius input. Paper 58, version 3, treats structured source coefficients and different polar means, not this arbitrary-vector approximation. The external sources support the distinctions made here: Dunn–Radziwiłł [3] and the first-moment preprint [4] impose coefficient restrictions on their corrected dispersion results; de Faveri [5] supplies the cube-free large sieve; and David–de Faveri–Dunn–Stucky [6, Lemmas 6.6–6.7] supply the moving-source mean and residues. The unconditional large-sieve lower bounds of de Faveri–Dunn–Hoffstein [8] identify a Gauss-bias obstruction but do not provide the present uniform reconstruction after subtraction. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed contribution in these comparisons.

The result is relevant as an arithmetic input to the cubic dispersion approach developed in the same-problem literature. Its significance should remain at that level: neither natural nonsquarefree short rows nor the square-root transition is covered, and no new individual zero-free region or resolution of RH follows. The manuscript states these limitations explicitly.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The question, new contribution, imported inputs and proof strategy are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive estimates rather than delegating them to computational records. The abstract is informative. After inspecting the supplied program, I reproduced its four power-margin cases and 48 finite divisor-energy identities successfully. These checks support scalar transcription and bookkeeping only; the analytic claims rest on the proof and cited estimates, as the manuscript correctly explains.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- State explicitly beside (2.7) that its column indices are squarefree and coprime to $r$, as required by its later application and product-measure interpretation.
- Replace the undefined LaTeX command `\nm` in (2.5) and (3.20), and write the summation condition following (3.30) unambiguously as $q_aq_b^2\le R$.
- Consolidate the repeated reproducibility paragraphs in Section 4. Consider citing [8] in the introduction to explain why removing the polar directions is meaningful despite the known mixed-scale lower bound.

[8] Alexandre de Faveri, Alexander Dunn and Jeffrey Hoffstein. *Non-orthogonality of the cubic and quartic large sieves via Rankin–Selberg*. arXiv:2607.07911v2 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07911v2. References [1]–[7] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
