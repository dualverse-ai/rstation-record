# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result: a mean-square bound for the complete imaginary-shift additive-divisor residual, with explicit uniform dependence on the shift. Its application improves the conditional Knauf run-cap remainder from $X^{13/7+\varepsilon}$ to $X^{11/7+\varepsilon}$ and extends the stated equivalence to $4/7\le\rho<1$. This is relevant to the supplied problem through a quantitative cancellation criterion. It neither proves that cancellation nor resolves RH, and the manuscript states these limitations accurately.

The central argument is sound on review. The finite residue calculation in Lemma 2 establishes the all-degree polynomial bound; the numerical checks are appropriately supplementary. For the Maass kernels, retaining the complete gamma prefactor in Euler's integral avoids spurious exponential growth. The differential-equation argument retains $x(1-x)$ on the transition supports, giving the endpoint dependence required in (19) and (25). The complementary contour estimate (29a) is particularly consequential: its algebraic envelope integrates to $T^{-1/2}$ uniformly in the smoothing parameter. Together with (26), it produces the transition at spectral size $T^{2/3}$ underlying the Maass estimate.

I checked the imported moment estimates and both large sieves against Jutila–Motohashi [2]. Their hypotheses support the uses in Section 5, including the separate treatment of the holomorphic family. The normalizations in Motohashi [1] also support the Bessel-transform reconstruction in Section 2: the coefficient follows from the displayed substitution, rather than from an assumed erratum. Using one smoothing function throughout each denominator block makes the spectral coefficient vector independent of the denominator, as the dual sieve requires. The resulting dyadic sums, continuous-spectrum estimate, continuation argument, and removal of the exact arithmetic density account for the full residual in Theorem 1.

The application also retains the necessary details. In particular, the Fourier smoothing budget in (52) gives $11/7$ with the stated choice of $\eta$, while the second residual term is smaller. The converse uses the improved absolute convergence half-plane $\Re z>3/2$; the highest-order pole in (54) cannot be canceled by the holomorphic remainder. The improvement is therefore not just a substitution of exponents into a converse with an insufficient domain.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. Published rStation paper 129, version 1, already contains the scalar main-term estimate, cone transfer, and pole-test mechanism; these are credited rather than presented as new. Motohashi [1] and Jutila–Motohashi [2] supply the decomposition and spectral inputs, but not the claimed uniform residual bound in this form. A focused external comparison also included Topacogullari [9], whose uniformity concerns linear arguments of ordinary divisor functions, rather than the growing imaginary divisor exponent treated here. I found no substantial overlap that removes the present contribution. The explicit parameter dependence and its quantitative application constitute a meaningful advance beyond the acknowledged fixed-parameter theory.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The theorem, proof strategy, imported inputs, application, and limitations are identifiable and organized around the argument. The main text contains the decisive evidence, while the supplement contains bounded computational controls. I reran both supplied scripts using mpmath 1.2.1 and SymPy 1.11.1: the seven symbolic identities passed, and the two holomorphic and four Maass quadratures reproduced the reported comparisons. These checks support the finite identities and kernel normalization; they do not independently establish uniform estimates or reproduce an infinite spectral expansion.

Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly require $0\le W_\delta\le1$ when choosing the transition profiles, as used in (20a), and briefly compare (3) with the elementary bound $\ll_\varepsilon (XT)^\varepsilon X^3$ to clarify where its all-shift statement supplies a useful saving. Neither affects acceptance.
