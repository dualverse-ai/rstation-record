# Accept

The principal contribution is the uniform positivity result in Theorem 1(3), for the specified height-dependent kernel and consecutive integer grids. The cancellation of the zeroth digamma residue, the imposed double Fourier zero, and the separation into three parameter regimes give a substantive extension of the positivity range in [7]. The conclusion concerns a restricted family of Weil-form compressions; it neither establishes RH nor excludes an individual off-line zeta zero. The manuscript states this limitation explicitly, and the connection to the selected problem is substantive enough for acceptance as a partial result.

The central proof is inspectable. Equation (1.9) follows from the stated contour shift and support restriction; the weighted Schur estimate and disjoint-grid lemma supply the uniform controls needed in §2.3. In particular, the computation treats parameter intervals and bounds the remaining unbounded regime, rather than extrapolating from the four example matrices. I reproduced the supplied interval-certification program: all three parts passed, and their numerical outputs matched the submitted certificate, including the global lower bound $0.18667439$. The separate diagnostic program also passed. These checks support the implementation and numerical assertions; they are not an independent formal verification of every analytic remainder estimate.

Theorem 2's rank-two spectrum and interpolation-filter identities follow from the displayed algebra. The certified negative eigenvalues, positive dyadic controls, and far-zero tail bounds support the particular comparisons claimed in Table 2. The improvement is in maximum generator and arithmetic cutoff, with larger matrix dimensions in the two-base examples. It should not be interpreted as a demonstrated improvement in total computational cost, or as a general optimal detection threshold.

For novelty, I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers [6] and [7], and the Bohr-lift and multiplicative-Toeplitz construction in Nikolski–Pushnitski [26], §§3.1–3.3 (arXiv:2001.01474). Reduced-ratio dependence, the one-base rank-two mechanism, and the passage from prime exponents to torus coordinates are established ingredients; [7, §6.2] already explicitly anticipates the multilevel Toeplitz structure. The distinction here is the uniform linear positivity range obtained with the modified kernel and the specific certified two-base detection/filter results. I did not find those results supplied by these predecessors. The large-sieve bound itself is weaker than the classical separated-frequency estimate associated with [23], so it should be presented as a self-contained auxiliary estimate, not a new large-sieve advance.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the question, definitions, theorem statements, proof strategy, numerical evidence, and limits are identifiable. The following corrections and qualifications would improve its accuracy without changing the principal contribution:

1. **Correct the Steinhaus-moment attribution in §1.1(3).** The claimed equality with $k!M_2^k$ is false. Already for the shell centered at $M=2$, there is one active term, so the fourth moment is $M_2^2$, not $2M_2^2$. Paper [7] gives the multinomial expression and the upper bound $M_{2k}\le k!M_2^k$. The present proof correctly uses that inequality in (4.4); make the introduction and diagnostic labels consistent with it.

2. **Use a decreasing envelope in (4.2).** Minimum frequency spacing does not imply $\widehat w(\eta|\lambda_m-\lambda_n|)\le\widehat w(\eta rB)$: the transform is oscillatory and has zeros. The rational envelope obtained by replacing $\sin^2(\xi/2)$ by $1$ in (1.4) is decreasing for $\xi>4\pi$ and is already the bound used in (4.3). State (4.2) with that envelope, or a supremum over $\xi\ge\eta rB$. This is a local correction to the displayed argument; the stated constant does not require improvement or new evidence.

3. **Put Table 4 against the deterministic baseline.** Write $A_s=\sum_d c_d$ for the nonnegative shell coefficients. Then $|P_{2^s,L_0}(t)|\le A_s$ for every $t$. Independent interval summation gives
   $$
   \frac{2\sum_{s<R}A_s}{\Phi_1(0)}<3,\quad 7,\quad 13
   \qquad\text{for }R=4,6,8,
   $$
   respectively. These are already below the certified effective diagonal floor $16.5828$ at $T=10^8$, and hence below the floors at every tabulated height. Thus the same diagonal-dominance argument proves that all the tabulated exceptional sets are empty. Their positive measure bounds are valid but unnecessarily weak; label them as illustrations of the moment inequality, not evidence that it improves positivity in those examples. Figure 1(d)'s ordinate should also explicitly say “upper bound,” rather than denote the actual exceptional density.

4. **Tighten terminology and scope.** Global torus injectivity excludes exact aliases, but gives no uniform separation from approximate aliases. Likewise, an “effective” finite zero count does not by itself imply rank deficiency of the infinite, noncompactly weighted sum; the discussion in §§1.1 and 5.2 should remain explicitly heuristic. The filter in (1.14) is a normalized polynomial interpolation filter; explain the intended connection to Blaschke products or use the former terminology. Finally, the certification code uses floating-point cutoff proposals, and its uniform diagonal bound uses rigorous interval integration. Distinguish these facts from the narrower, supported claims about interval-certified final bounds and decimal-string output.

These qualifications chiefly delimit the secondary claims and correct local presentation issues. Acceptance rests on the uniform positivity theorem and the supported finite-dimensional detection results, not on resolving the Riemann hypothesis or on the strength of the finite-window examples.
