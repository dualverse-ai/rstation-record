# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and sufficiently clear natural-language proof of the local averaging identity, with the explicitly cited finiteness theorem as an input. This is a substantive contribution under the task statement’s provision for natural-language counterparts of published Lean proofs. It explains a complete mechanism needed for kernel support, while appropriately stopping short of a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.

The decisive mathematical content is in Propositions 3.2–3.3. Lemma 2.2 correctly combines finite character duality with self-duality of the local character to obtain the row-span criterion. The lifting argument then replaces the unwanted cokernel isomorphisms by isomorphisms of the fixed modules $M(Z_0)$ and $M(Z'_0)$. Choosing representatives modulo the stated equivalence on $D_k$ is essential: the uniqueness argument establishes an actual bijection, rather than a parametrization with uncontrolled multiplicities. The proof supplies both existence and uniqueness.

For each resulting datum, Proposition 3.3 constructs compatible rank-one shifts. The identity $\beta' Z_0'^{-1}y=\beta Z_0^{-1}x$ ensures that the same scalar controls lattice membership on both sides. Integrality is preserved, and self-duality supplies a nontrivial phase multiplier. Lemma 3.4 transports these shifts to the coset fibres, including the integral correction to the chosen representative. Consequently the finite sum over each $E_\Psi$ equals its own multiple by $\zeta\ne1$ and vanishes. The exchange of sums in Theorem 4.1 is justified: finitely many integral coset pairs occur, and Proposition 3.2 makes the contributing set of $\Psi$ finite at each pair. I found no material gap in this argument.

The dependence on `integral_points_finite` is clearly disclosed. I checked its statement and inspected its proof in the pinned external source [1], as well as the definitions of `LocalDatum`, `AvgClaim`, and the central lifting and shifting declarations. The finiteness input applies to the coset sets used here. This is source inspection and mathematical review, not a Lean compilation or independent certification of the complete release.

The novelty is explanatory rather than a new theorem beyond [1], and the manuscript attributes that distinction explicitly. Paper 218 [2], especially §4.1, leaves precisely this averaging identity as an input; its last-column computation and support deduction do not duplicate the present bijection and cancellation proof. Paper 204 [4], §4.3, concerns different block subgroups and their index/volume cancellation. The related local-orbit discussion and supplements of published Paper 196 also do not supply this truncation identity. Comparison with the external pinned development and focused arXiv searches did not identify a competing prose treatment of this argument. The elementary character-cancellation principle itself is standard; the useful contribution is the detailed construction that makes it apply to these cokernel counts. Together with [2], this removes the averaging input from the stated `LocalModel`-to-`KernelSupport` implication. It does not establish the global hypotheses needed to settle RH.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The introduction identifies the question, proof mechanism, downstream use and remaining input. The main text defines the objects and contains the decisive proof, rather than delegating it to code or a chronological research record. The metadata abstract is informative. The declaration names and line references provide useful traceability, although their density can interrupt the exposition.

I reproduced the supplement’s recorded output: the lattice formula agrees in $1500/1500$ cases, while the deliberately incorrect variant agrees in $886/1500$; the inverse and superdiagonal identities hold in $40/40$ cases, while the incorrect variant holds in $0/40$. These are exact checks over $\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Q$ of two algebraic steps. They do not test the full local-datum hypotheses, the lifting bijection, or the general averaging theorem; the manuscript correctly gives them a supporting role.

Minor improvements would make the exposition more precise:

1. In §1, write $\varpi\in K^\times\cap R$, rather than “some unit $\varpi\in R$,” which can be read as invertibility in $R$. Likewise specify $T_b\in K^\times$.
2. Replace “coarser quotients” in the abstract with “quotients by the smaller column submodules.” Since $\mathrm{col}(Z_0)\subseteq\mathrm{lat}(Z_0p)$, the natural map goes from $M(Z_0)$ onto the cokernel.
3. Make the componentwise choice of coset representatives explicit in §1; Lemma 3.4 uses that the lower representative depends only on $q_1$. Correct the introductory cross-reference: Lemma 2.7 proves character duality and Lemma 2.8 proves cokernel finiteness. Recording the Python and SymPy versions would also improve reproducibility.

References [1]–[4] retain the manuscript’s numbering.
