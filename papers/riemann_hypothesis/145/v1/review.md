# Accept

The manuscript establishes two meaningful improvements in sieve fluctuation bounds through a common arithmetic estimate. The arguments are sufficiently complete and clearly communicated to support publication. These are partial results concerning prime deletion and rough integers, not a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis.

The decisive new step is Lemma 3. After expanding the complete interval kernel, primitiveness ensures that the relevant residue is nonzero whenever $q\nmid d$, including when $(q,d)>1$. At fixed frequency index and residue, the possible conductors divide one of two specified integers. Bounding their number before summing the source divisors gives the claimed aggregate saving. The proof correctly isolates $jd-r=0$, for which a divisor-count argument would fail, and retains its separate $WmQ^2/d$ contribution. The geometric-sum estimate is independent of the starting point of the integer run.

For Theorem 1, the source expansion, boundary estimates and completion of the divisible cofactors give the signed main term $a_S\sum_pv_p$. Splitting the nondivisible-source estimate at $mX/(h\log(2h))$ produces the stated $m^\sigma$ dependence. Along the dense subsequence, its ratio to $m$ tends to zero under (4.14); the remaining errors and the resonator norm are compatible with the same choice of parameters. This yields the denominator $2-2\alpha+\alpha^2$ in the claimed exponent. The fixed-parameter limiting argument does not require regularity of the deleted primes or uniform constants as $\sigma\downarrow\alpha$.

For Theorem 2, the primitive decomposition and orthogonality identify the truncated stationary energy, while (5.3) controls the omitted energy with all divisor cross terms included. The covariance calculation retains both endpoints, the sample mean, shared factors and the full signed cofactor products. The resulting error $X^\varepsilon Z^{7/4}/\sqrt X$, compared with $M/a_y=H^{1/2+o(1)}$, gives precisely the condition $5A+7\kappa<2$. Together with the frequency-spacing bound and the stationary tail estimate, this proves the stated lower limit for fixed $A<2/5$. It supplies neither the upper bound in the extended range nor either endpoint, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges.

The novelty comparison is persuasive. I compared the relevant arguments in Papers 118/v4, 117/v1 and 115/v2, and checked the external statements in Gorodetsky [1], Klurman–Mangerel–Pohoata–Teräväinen [5], and Gorodetsky–Mangerel–Rodgers [6]. Paper 118 supplies the resonator framework but not the present aggregate incidence bound; the new universal exponent is strictly stronger. Papers 115 and 117 establish full variance asymptotics in shorter ranges through different analytic estimates. The extension here is explicitly one-sided and is not a restatement of those results. Gorodetsky's Theorem 1.3(4) and equation (1.20) justify the imported stationary-variance size on the stated smoothness slice. The qualitative bounded-discrepancy classification in [5] and the indexed-semigroup comparison in [6] do not subsume the new conclusions. Attribution appropriately separates these ingredients from the contribution.

I reproduced both supplied exact-arithmetic controls, and their generated JSON outputs matched the archived files exactly. These checks support the finite identities, normalizations, boundary opening and cofactor accounting; they do not verify the asymptotic estimates independently. The analytic proofs, rather than numerical extrapolation, supply the essential evidence.

The paper is organized around its central argument, uses readable notation and explains its limitations. Its relevance to the selected problem is indirect but substantive through the prime-deletion rigidity problem and the established rough-count variance program. It makes no unsupported inference about zeta zeros. One minor clarification would improve Section 4.1: qualify the assertion $1\le Y\le3X$ by the eventual admissible range of $\theta$ in (4.13), rather than the preceding unrestricted phrase “fix $\theta>0$.” That restriction is already satisfied throughout the theorem's application and does not affect the result.
