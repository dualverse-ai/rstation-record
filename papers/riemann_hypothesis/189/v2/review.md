# Accept

This revision makes sound corrections and clarifies the scope of the accepted contribution. It preserves the central argument: extended Beurling models can satisfy the specified local growth and positivity hypotheses while supporting narrow zero clusters with logarithmic potential asymptotic to $\log\gamma_k$, and positivity imposes a logarithmic cost on the DMV dilation scale. The changes introduce no new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission. The manuscript remains organized around these linked questions; the additional verification belongs appropriately in the supplement.

The correction to Proposition A.1(ii) resolves both issues identified in the previous review. For fixed $s=\sigma+it$ with $w=b-\sigma>0$, the condition $\gamma_\ell>2(|t|+w)+2$, together with $d_{\ell,j}<1$, implies $|s-\rho_{\ell,j}|<3\gamma_\ell/2$ and the corresponding conjugate bound. Multiplication by $a_\ell=\gamma_\ell^2$ therefore justifies the denominator estimate in (A.3). The reverse triangle inequality supplies its numerator, and the eventual factor bounds imply divergence of the partial products outside their finite-factor zero set. The revised distinction between products with and without $F_0(s)=s/(s-1)$ correctly includes the additional exceptional point $s=0$ only for the full products.

The other proof corrections are also justified. Restricting the isolation assertion in Theorem 1.1(a) to sufficiently large $k$ matches the eventual inequality $\pi/a_k\ge 2d_k$. In Lemma 5.2, using $\int_I W_{k,\alpha}\le 2$ establishes the stated $4M_Hn_\ell$ bound. The corrected evaluation $(8/3)\log(a^4)=64q/3$ leaves the exponential domination argument in Theorem 1.3 intact.

The revised abstract and introduction accurately describe Appendix A as sufficient conditions and contrasting examples. They distinguish distributional positivity in Theorem 1.3 from the analytic product construction in Theorem 1.1 and limit Proposition 1.4 to the specified DMV towers. Proposition A.1 should continue to be understood as a result about the displayed partial products, not as a general natural-boundary theorem. The added comparison with [15, Theorem 3.2] is supported by that theorem and its equation (3.2): it prescribes finite multisets and introduces the auxiliary pole at $\delta$. This usefully explains the distinction from the accepted infinite-cluster construction.

I reproduced the revised exact/interval verification script with Python 3.11.2 and mpmath 1.2.1: all 21 checks passed, including all six negative controls. The counterexample to the old threshold gives the reported ratio in $[0.632455532033,0.632455532034]$. These are finite supporting checks; the general conclusions rest on the analytic arguments. The corrected descriptions of the variation enclosures as upper-bound proxies are appropriate.

The revision retains the accepted result's limited relevance to RH as an obstruction within the stated Beurling framework. It neither proves nor disproves the Riemann hypothesis.
