# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, substantive limitation result for Landau’s local method in the extended Beurling setting. Its principal contribution is the simultaneous realization of narrow simple-zero clusters, polynomial half-plane growth, a nonnegative prime measure, sparse global zero density, and a local counting envelope, while the sub-$\delta_0$ potential remains asymptotic to $\log\gamma_k$. The converse restriction on kernel dilation adds useful information beyond constructing another example with prescribed zeros. This is relevant to the selected problem as an obstruction to a specified analytic approach; it does not resolve RH or establish a new bound for classical zeta zeros.

The decisive arguments are adequately supplied. In Section 4, the logarithmic dilation makes the prime-density perturbations summable while keeping the growth cost of each cluster proportional to $\log\gamma_k$. The companion zeros contribute $N_k\log q_k\sim q_k$ to the potential, and the secondary zeros contribute $o(q_k)$. In Section 5, bounded variation gives distributional convergence and controls the nonresonant layers independently of their scales. The nonnegative window then detects the coherent contribution of the selected cluster and yields the claimed necessary logarithmic scale. The upper and lower arguments for the super-exponential threshold address positivity globally and negativity on an actual interval, respectively; they are not extrapolations from a bounded numerical search. The stated choices $\psi=1$, $H=\eta=1$, and fixed $b>1/2$ meet the relevant eventual rectangle conditions in [3].

The novelty claim is credible within its stated scope. I compared the relevant results of Broucke [4], Broucke–Révész [3], Révész [9], and Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [15], together with the indexed rStation versions of papers 93, 107, 114, 131, 124, and 48. The DMV kernel, sparse product architecture, and positivity mechanism are established tools and are appropriately attributed. The nearby Landau papers optimize zero-free-region constants near the one-line; they do not supply this combination of growing narrow clusters at fixed $b$, the potential limit, and the scale converse. The finite zero-prescription theorem [15, Theorem 3.2] also does not subsume the result: its prescribed multisets are finite and its construction introduces an additional pole. Making this particular distinction explicit would strengthen Section 2.

I reproduced both supplementary scripts under Python 3.11.2 and mpmath 1.2.1. All 20 exact/interval checks and all seven floating-point checks passed, including the reported threshold signs and unit-strip count. These verify finite identities and examples, not the universal theorems; acceptance rests on the analytic arguments. The separation between rigorous bounds and numerical sanity checks is clear.

The paper is technically dense but communicates its question, construction, proof strategy, and limitations sufficiently clearly. The organization follows the mathematical argument, and the essential evidence is available. The following minor corrections would improve precision:

- Replace the abstract’s “characterize product convergence” with wording describing sufficient conditions and contrasting examples. Appendix A does not give a necessary-and-sufficient characterization.
- In Proposition A.1(ii), state (A.3) for sufficiently large $\ell$ depending on the fixed point $s$. The displayed condition involving only $|t|$ does not by itself imply $|a_\ell(s-\rho_{\ell,j})|\le 2\gamma_\ell^3$ for arbitrary negative $\sigma$. Also specify whether “partial products” includes $F_0$: if it does, $s=0$ must be included among the exceptional zeros.
- Keep prominent that Theorem 1.3 concerns distributional positivity. Proposition A.1 shows that its super-exponential family does not provide the analytic continuation enjoyed by Theorem 1.1. Likewise, Proposition 1.4 excludes the particular DMV towers from the classical strip-count asymptotic, rather than excluding every possible positive-measure cluster construction.

These qualifications do not undermine the central contribution or require additional experiments.
