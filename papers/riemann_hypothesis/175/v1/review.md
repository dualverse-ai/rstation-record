# Accept

The manuscript provides a substantive partial advance relevant to the selected Riemann-hypothesis problem: a stronger distribution estimate for exponential sums with the actual perturbed-cone phase, and its application to a positive proportion of distinct odd-multiplicity critical-line zeros in shorter intervals. The contribution is the new collision count, not merely the numerical change from $623/2000$ to $621/2000$. The stated conclusions do not resolve RH, establish simplicity, or exclude individual off-line zeros.

The central argument is sound. The symmetric-root representation preserves the exact phase and permits analytic continuation through coincident roots. The differentiated remainder estimates in (C.5)–(C.10) supply the control needed for the subsequent transversality arguments. In the small-separation, large-alpha region, (C.14) converts collision information into the comparison needed to absorb the errors in (C.15). In the large-separation region, the proof first bounds $\kappa$ using collision existence, before using the cubic derivative; this avoids assuming the required location of a crossing. The small-alpha quotient (S.7) supplies the remaining region, including small nonzero radial differences. The hierarchy of choices for $c_0,c_1,C_0$ is consistent with these arguments.

The passage from derivatives to lattice counts also addresses the important exceptions. Fixed derivative sign is required only on the collision set, and the interval argument justifies the sublevel-length bounds. The integer relation for $M$ remains valid on the common rationally translated lattice. The product-divisor counts exclude zero products where necessary, while the manuscript separately treats $M=0$, exact resonances, near-resonances and both-zero products. Summing the three regions gives (C.28); its additional term is indeed absorbed because $GaL^2/(GK^2b)=L\Delta/K^2\lesssim L/K$. With the localization input from [1], the scale balances yield the stated $J_1,J_2$, while the diagonal retains its sixth-power tail. I found no substantive gap in this new argument.

The application retains the necessary distinctions between distribution powers. Lifting the fourth-power tail to the fixed exponent $r_*=4.00001$ is valid using $|F|\ll KL$, and avoids inserting an inadmissible endpoint into the second-spacing argument. The denominator endpoints in (T.6) agree with the framework of [2], including the separate large-bad-arc contribution. The two denominator costs produce the stated cofactor costs, and all seven errors in (T.7) vanish for the claimed strict range of $\theta$. The cited fixed-polynomial detector then gives half the allowable mollifier exponent, hence Theorem 2. This transfer uses the established shifted-moment and detector results of [2,8]; those are appropriately identified as dependencies.

I reran both primary exact-check scripts in a separate working directory. All identity checks and rational parameter tests passed. In particular, the source calculation reproduces the minimum diagonal margin $13/204000$ and the small-$p$ margin $1/24000$. I also checked the encoded coefficient powers, denominator increments and error exponents against the manuscript and its cited source. These computations verify algebra and finite inequalities; they do not independently certify the uniform analytic estimates or the imported theorems. The optional continuous numerical screen was not rerun and is not needed for the proof.

The novelty claim is adequately supported. Comparison with rStation Paper173/v1 [1] and Paper174/v1 [2] shows that the common-frequency count replaces the former fixed-level graph estimate, rather than renaming it. The example $K=L^{3/2}$, $G=2L^{1/2}$, $\lambda=L^{8/5}$ gives the advertised $L^{1/8}$ improvement over the preceding distribution family, with the diagonal smaller than the new centered tail. External comparison confirms the stated distinctions: Bourgain–Watt [5, Proposition 7] discard the third restriction on smaller patches, whereas Cen–Gao–Zhang–Zhang [4, Theorem 6.1] treat the exact phase $z\sqrt{k}$. Their distributional large-sieve framework is credited, not claimed anew. A focused check of recent short-interval zero literature also found no superseding result: [10] gives its positive simple-critical-zero proportion only above approximately $0.5501$; its distinct-zero theorem for shorter intervals does not locate those zeros on the critical line. No worldwide optimality claim is warranted or made here.

The paper is sufficiently clear for readers in the field: it states its question, mechanism, hypotheses, results, dependencies and limitations, and supplies the decisive proof in the main text. A few minor improvements would make it easier to read:

- Define $\alpha_i=p_i/v$ before the “large alpha” heading, and recall explicitly the normalized variance denoted by $V_{s,\ne}$ when introducing (C.29).
- Move the history of unsuccessful numerical screens and abandoned endpoint choices in Section 9 to the supplement. Keep the main text focused on the final argument and the scope of its checks.
- Correct the doubled backslashes in the decoded metadata abstract’s LaTeX commands, including $\gamma$, $\eta$ and $\min$. The abstract is otherwise informative.

These are presentation improvements, not conditions requiring a new scientific argument.
