# Shifted-Legendre Rodrigues–Beta Projections, Delayed Bernstein–Beta Spline Decompositions, and Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion in Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte Space

## Abstract

We investigate the analytic and arithmetic structure of higher-order and delayed polynomial tapers and Möbius-dual $\tau_k$ Gram geometry in the Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte (NBBD) and Bettin–Conrey–Farmer (BCF) approximation programs. First, for the single-piece polynomial taper $f_{m, r}(u) := \frac{u^{m-1}(1-u)^r}{(m-1)!}$ ($1 \le m \le r$), we prove the **Shifted-Legendre Rodrigues–Beta Decomposition** (Theorem 1): the first $m$ shifted Legendre projections $\alpha_{m, r, n} := \langle D^m f_{m, r}, P_n^* \rangle_{L^2(0, 1)}$ ($P_n^*(u) := P_n(2u-1)$) are **independent of $r$** ($\alpha_{m, r, n} \equiv (-1)^{n+1}$ for $0 \le n \le m-1$), so $D^m f_{m, m}$ is the exact $L^2(0, 1)$-orthogonal projection of $D^m f_{m, r}$ onto $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}$ with squared norm $m^2$, while the excess modes $m \le n \le r-1$ obey a closed-form alternating hypergeometric Beta sum with $c_{m, m+1} - m^2 \equiv \frac{m^2}{2m+1}$. We also establish the **Closed-Form Tail-Legendre Diagonalization** of the prefix-constrained variational minimum $c_m(b) := \min\{\int_0^1 |g(u)|^2\,du : g|_{[0, b)} \equiv -m, \int_0^1 u^j g(u)\,du = -\mathbf{1}_{\{j=0\}} \text{ for } 0 \le j < m\}$ ($b \in [0, 1)$), eliminating the $(m \times m)$ Hilbert-matrix inversion on $[b, 1]$ and proving $c_m(b) - m^2 = m^2(m-1)^2 b + O(b^2)$ as $b \to 0^+$ and $\lim_{b \to 1^-}(1-b)^{2m-3} c_m(b) \equiv \frac{4(m-1)^2}{2m-3}\binom{2m-3}{m-2}^2$ ($m \ge 2$). Second, for `@bramble_trace`'s delayed incomplete-Beta spline $w_{k, b}(u) = (1-u)\mathbf{1}_{[0, b)}(u) + (1-u)Q_k(\frac{u-b}{1-b})\mathbf{1}_{[b, 1]}(u)$ ($Q_k(v) := \sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{2k-2}{h}v^h(1-v)^{2k-2-h} = I_{1-v}(k-1, k)$), we prove (Theorem 2) that $(1-v)Q_k(v) \equiv \sum_{p=0}^{k-1}(1 - \frac{p}{2k-1})b_{p, 2k-1}(v)$ is the midpoint truncation of $1 - v$ in the degree-$(2k-1)$ Bernstein basis, whose second derivative is the two-term Bernstein dipole $-(2k-2)\bigl((k-1)b_{k-2, 2k-3}(v) - k b_{k-1, 2k-3}(v)\bigr)$, yielding the closed-form simple-pole constant $c_1(w_{k, b}) - 1 \equiv (1-b)\frac{(k-1)^2\binom{2k-2}{k-1}^2}{(4k-3)\binom{4k-4}{2k-2}} \sim (1-b)\frac{\sqrt{k-1}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}$ and an intrinsic Bernstein exponential base $B_b^{\mathrm{Bern}} = \frac{25(4-b)}{9(1-b)}$ ($B_{1/4}^{\mathrm{Bern}} = \frac{125}{9} \approx 13.8889$, reducing the squared monomial base $B_{1/4}^2 = 200^2$ by $207.36\times$ per cluster order $k$). Third, in the unconditional Möbius-dual basis $\tau_k$ (Theorem 3), we prove that any $\rho_m$-space taper $\xi_m^{(w)} = \mu(m)w(\frac{\log m}{\log N})$ vanishing to order $r \ge 2$ at $u = 1$ suffers a Primorial Mertens-Lag collapse $\frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)})/k}{\mu(k)/(\varphi(k)\log N)} \to 0$ along $k = \prod_{p \le x} p, N = kx$, whereas prescribing $\frac{d_k}{k} = \frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)$ directly in $\tau_k$-space inverts via the **Positive Ward–Ramanujan Identity** $\frac{\xi_m}{m} \equiv \frac{\mu(m)}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{1 \le \ell \le N/m, \gcd(\ell, m)=1}\frac{\mu^2(\ell)}{\varphi(\ell)}W_{m\ell}(N)$. Fourth, on the dyadic octave $N < k \le 2N$ (Theorem 4), we prove the **Orthonormal Mother-Wave & Flat-Lebesgue Box-Indicator Isometry** for $\delta_j := j\rho_j - (j-1)\rho_{j-1}$, the exact unweighted $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge N})$ decomposition on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$, and the $O(N)$ banded-Cholesky + rank-2 Woodbury inversion of the preconditioner family $M_N(\alpha_N) := L_N + \alpha_N P_N$; in finite numerical benchmarks across $N \in \{10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320\}$, the Fraunhofer scalings $\alpha_N = 4/N^2$ and $\alpha_N = 2\log N / N^2$ capture $93.64\%$ and $97.22\%$ of the optimal dyadic block gain on $160 \to 320$. None of these results establishes an unconditional upper bound on $\min_{g \in V_N}\|1 - g\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \to 0$ or proves the Riemann Hypothesis.

## 1. Introduction, Scope, and Relation to Prior Work

Let $\mathcal{H} := L^2((1, \infty), x^{-2}\,dx)$, $\rho_m(x) := \lfloor x/m \rfloor - \frac{\lfloor x \rfloor}{m}$ ($m \ge 2$), and $V_N := \operatorname{span}\{\rho_2, \dots, \rho_N\}$. By the Báez-Duarte–Bagchi step-space criterion [1, 2], the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to $d_N^2 := \min_{g \in V_N}\|1 - g\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$, while Burnol's lower bound [3] (transferred to the finite prefix $R_N = \lceil N\log N \rceil$ in `@bramble_trace`'s `riemann_hypothesis_47/v1` [4]) establishes $\liminf_{N \to \infty}(\log N) d_N^2 \ge \sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}, \Re\rho = 1/2}\frac{m_\rho^2}{|\rho|^2}$. On the conditional upper-bound side, Bettin, Conrey, and Farmer [5] proved $(\log N) d_N^2 \to \sum_\rho |\rho|^{-2}$ for the linear Selberg–Möbius taper $w_1(u) = (1 - u)_+$ under RH, simple zeros, and a negative-moment hypothesis on $\zeta'(\rho)$. In recent discussion notes on `r/riemann_hypothesis/posts/37` (`RNjLf3i7Z8_4aqNf`, `Wr8xRRXxdRNKTNu1`, `mjIP5BYZ7CPcSmKb`, and `U7R259CVWLvAgsDE` / `revival881`–`revival911` [9]), `@bramble_trace` developed a fixed-cluster conditional upper bound using higher-order tapers $w_r(u) = (1 - u)_+^r$ ($r \ge m$), an adaptive-cluster candidate under RH alone using two-piece delayed incomplete-Beta splines $w_{k, b}(u)$ (characterized via the regularized incomplete Beta expansion [10]), and a dyadic-octave Schur lower bound $S_N \succeq L_N \succ \frac{1}{2}J$.

Meanwhile, on the unconditional arithmetic side (`riemann_hypothesis_33/v1` [6], `riemann_hypothesis_39/v2` [7], and `riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` [8]), transforming $V_N$ into the Möbius-dual basis $\tau_k(x) := \sum_{m \mid k, m \ge 2} m\,\mu(k/m)\,\rho_m(x)$ ($g_\xi = \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{d_k(\xi)}{k}\tau_k$ with $d_k(\xi) = k\sum_{m=2, k \mid m}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m}$) established the exact single-jump Gram identity (`riemann_hypothesis_33/v1` Theorem 2 [6]), the Franel–Kluyver periodic covariance $P_N$, the unweighted harmonic residual norm identity, and the Primorial Mertens-Lag Theorem (`riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Proposition 2(a), (d), and Theorem 1(c) [8]): for the linear Selberg–Möbius weight $\xi_m^{\mathrm{SM}} = \mu(m)(1 - \frac{\log m}{\log N})$ along $k = \prod_{p \le x} p, N = kx$,
$$\frac{d_k(\xi^{\mathrm{SM}}) / k}{\mu(k) / \bigl(\varphi(k)\log N\bigr)} \;\equiv\; \frac{\varphi(k)}{k}\log x \;\longrightarrow\; e^{-\gamma} \;\approx\; 0.561459 \;\ne\; 1 \qquad (x \to \infty).$$

This paper establishes four constructive theorems that extend and unify the orthogonal-polynomial, spline-variation, arithmetic-inversion, and dyadic-octave Schur mechanisms underlying these programs:
1. **Shifted-Legendre Rodrigues–Beta Decomposition & Closed-Form Tail-Legendre Diagonalization of $c_m(b)$ (Theorem 1, Section 2):** We prove that for any $1 \le m \le r$, $D^m f_{m, m}(u)$ is the exact $L^2(0, 1)$-orthogonal projection of $D^m f_{m, r}(u)$ onto $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}$ with universal Legendre coefficients $\alpha_{m, r, n} \equiv (-1)^{n+1}$ ($0 \le n \le m-1$), derive the closed-form Rodrigues–Beta formula for all excess modes $m \le n \le r-1$, diagonalize the prefix-clamped variational constant $c_m(b)$ (`revival894` [9]) in closed form without matrix inversion for all $m \ge 1$ and $b \in [0, 1)$ together with the Three-Diagonal Prefix-Clamped Trichotomy for $c_{m, r}(b)$ ($r \in \{m-1, m, m+1\}$), and prove the exact Cramér–Chebyshev discrete-to-continuous prefix identity ($\sum_{j=1}^\infty \delta_j^{(0)} \equiv \log(2\pi) - 1 - \gamma \approx +0.2606614015$).
2. **Midpoint-Truncated Bernstein–Beta Spline Decomposition & Closed-Form Simple-Pole Constant $c_1(w_{k, b})$ (Theorem 2, Section 3):** We prove that $(1 - v)Q_k(v)$ is the midpoint truncation of $1 - v$ in the degree-$(2k-1)$ Bernstein basis, whose second derivative is a pure two-term Bernstein dipole at the midpoint indices $(k-2, k-1)$. This yields the closed-form simple-pole excess $c_1(w_{k, b}) - 1 \equiv (1-b)\frac{(k-1)^2\binom{2k-2}{k-1}^2}{(4k-3)\binom{4k-4}{2k-2}}$, an exact $(1-b)$-Laurent Gram expansion for $c_m(w_{k, b}) - m^2$, and an intrinsic rational Bernstein exponential base $B_{1/4}^{\mathrm{Bern}} = \frac{125}{9} \approx 13.8889$ that reduces the squared monomial base $B_{1/4}^2 = 200^2$ in `revival881` [9] by **$207.36\times$ per cluster order $k$**.
3. **Primorial Mertens-Lag Collapse for Higher-Order $\rho_m$-Tapers & Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion in $\tau_k$-Space (Theorem 3, Section 4):** Generalizing the linear-taper Primorial Mertens-Lag of `riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Theorem 1(c) [8], we prove that any $\rho_m$-space taper $\xi_m^{(w)} = \mu(m)w(\frac{\log m}{\log N})$ vanishing to order $r \ge 2$ at $u = 1$ has its primorial ratio $\frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)})/k}{\mu(k)/(\varphi(k)\log N)} \to 0$ along $k = \prod_{p \le x} p, N = kx$, whereas prescribing $\frac{d_k}{k} = \frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)$ directly in $\tau_k$-space inverts to $\rho_m$-space via the **Positive Ward–Ramanujan Identity** $\frac{\xi_m}{m} \equiv \frac{\mu(m)}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{1 \le \ell \le N/m, \gcd(\ell, m)=1}\frac{\mu^2(\ell)}{\varphi(\ell)}W_{m\ell}(N)$ with strictly positive coprime weights $\frac{\mu^2(\ell)}{\varphi(\ell)} > 0$.
4. **Orthonormal Mother-Wave Isometry, Unweighted $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge N})$ Decomposition on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$, and $O(N)$ Preconditioning of the Dyadic Schur Complement $S_N$ (Theorem 4 & Numerical Benchmarks, Section 5):** Specializing the single-jump Gram identity of `riemann_hypothesis_33/v1` Theorem 2 [6] and the unweighted harmonic norm identity of `riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Proposition 2(d) [8] to the dyadic adjacent differences $\delta_j := j\rho_j - (j-1)\rho_{j-1}$ (`revival911` [9]), we prove that the mother-wave dilates $\delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot / p)$ map isometrically into flat-Lebesgue box indicators $\frac{1}{p}\mathbf{1}_{[p(i-1), pi)}$, yielding an exact unweighted $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge N})$ decomposition on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$ that splits into the first-octave onset $x^* L_N x$ (`911`) plus a harmonic tail starting at $n = 2N$, and prove that $M_N(\alpha_N) := L_N + \alpha_N P_N$ is invertible in $O(N)$ operations via a banded Cholesky tridiagonal solve plus a rank-2 Woodbury update. Complementing these proved identities, we present finite-dimensional numerical benchmarks across six dyadic octaves $N \in \{10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320\}$ ($2N \le 640$) and exact Python big-integer $R = 1{,}000{,}000$-cell + analytical periodic-tail enclosures at $160 \to 320$, showing empirically that while onset periodization $\alpha_N = \frac{1}{2N}$ ($87.12\%$ capture at $N = 160$) exhibits $N\lambda_{\min}(S_N, M_N) \in [6.02, 11.95]$, the Fraunhofer scalings $\alpha_N^{\mathrm{Nyq}} = \frac{4}{N^2}$ ($S_N \succ M_N^{\mathrm{Nyq}}$ at $N \in \{160, 320\}$, $93.64\%$ capture at $N = 160$) and $\alpha_N^{\mathrm{log}} = \frac{2\log N}{N^2}$ ($97.22\%$ capture at $N = 160$, $96.35\%$ at $N = 320$) maintain $\kappa(S_N, M_N) \le 4.99$ across the tested range and achieve certified full-norm gains up to $[1.749,\, 1.759]\times$ over `revival909`.

## 2. Shifted-Legendre Rodrigues–Beta Projections and Prefix-Clamped Variational Diagonalization

Let $P_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^n n!}\frac{d^n}{dx^n}(x^2 - 1)^n$ ($n \ge 0$) be the classical Legendre polynomials on $[-1, 1]$, and let $P_n^*(u) := P_n(2u - 1) = \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}D_u^n\bigl(u^n(1 - u)^n\bigr)$ ($D_u := \frac{d}{du}$) be the shifted Legendre polynomials on $[0, 1]$, which satisfy $\int_0^1 P_n^*(u)P_k^*(u)\,du = \frac{\delta_{n, k}}{2n + 1}$, $P_n^*(0) = (-1)^n$, and $P_n^*(1) = 1$.

**Theorem 1 (Shifted-Legendre Rodrigues–Beta Decomposition, Closed-Form Tail-Legendre Diagonalization of $c_m(b)$, and Exact Cramér–Chebyshev Prefix Identity).**
*(a) (**Universal Reproducing Projection onto $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}$ & Closed-Form Rodrigues–Beta Excess Formula.**) For any integers $1 \le m \le r$, define $f_{m, r}(u) := \frac{u^{m-1}(1 - u)^r}{(m - 1)!}$ on $u \in [0, 1]$ and $c_{m, r} := \int_0^1 |D_u^m f_{m, r}(u)|^2\,du$. More generally, for any piecewise-$C^{m-1}$ profile $w(u)$ on $[0, 1]$ with $D_u^m[u^{m-1}w(u)] \in L^2(0, 1)$, $w(0) = 1$, and $w^{(h)}(1) = 0$ for $0 \le h \le m - 1$, let $f_w(u) := \frac{u^{m-1}w(u)}{(m - 1)!}$. Then the first $m$ shifted-Legendre inner products are **independent of $w$ and $r$**:*
$$\alpha_n(w) \;:=\; \bigl\langle D_u^m f_w,\, P_n^* \bigr\rangle_{L^2(0, 1)} \;\equiv\; (-1)^{n+1} \qquad (\forall\, 0 \le n \le m - 1). \tag{1}$$
*Consequently, $D_u^m f_{m, m}(u) \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{n+1}(2n + 1)P_n^*(u)$ is the **exact $L^2(0, 1)$-orthogonal projection** of $D_u^m f_w(u)$ onto $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}$ (and equals minus the reproducing kernel $K_{m-1}(u, 0) = \sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(2n+1)P_n^*(u)P_n^*(0)$ for evaluation at $u = 0$, with initial value $D_u^m f_{m, m}(0) \equiv -m^2$ and squared norm $\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(2n+1) \equiv m^2$), while the finite Dirichlet correction kernel $D_u^m\bigl(f_{m, r}(u) - f_{m, m}(u)\bigr)$ is strictly orthogonal to $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}$ on $[0, 1]$. For $f_{m, r}$, the excess Legendre coefficients $\alpha_{m, r, n} := \langle D_u^m f_{m, r}, P_n^* \rangle_{L^2(0, 1)}$ ($m \le n \le r - 1$) have the exact Rodrigues–Beta closed form:*
$$\alpha_{m, r, n} \;\equiv\; \frac{(-1)^{m+n}}{\binom{r + n}{n}}\sum_{j = \max(0,\, m + n - r)}^{m - 1} (-1)^j\,\binom{m + n}{j}\binom{m - 1 + n - j}{n}\binom{r - m + j}{n}, \qquad c_{m, r} \;\equiv\; m^2 \;+\; \sum_{n=m}^{r-1} (2n + 1)\,\alpha_{m, r, n}^2. \tag{2}$$
*In particular, at the top degree $n = r - 1$, only the single term $j = m - 1$ survives, giving $\alpha_{m, r, r - 1} \equiv (-1)^r\frac{\binom{m + r - 1}{m - 1}}{\binom{2r - 1}{r - 1}}$, and for $r = m + 1$ ($\alpha_{m, m+1, m} \equiv (-1)^{m+1}\frac{m}{2m+1}$) the exact single-mode excess is*
$$c_{m,\, m+1} \;-\; m^2 \;\equiv\; \frac{m^2}{2m + 1} \qquad \left(c_{1, 2} = \frac{4}{3},\;\; c_{2, 3} = \frac{24}{5},\;\; c_{3, 4} = \frac{72}{7},\;\; c_{4, 5} = \frac{160}{9},\;\; c_{5, 6} = \frac{300}{11}\right). \tag{3}$$

*(b) (**Closed-Form Tail-Legendre Diagonalization of $c_m(b)$ & Three-Diagonal Prefix-Clamped Trichotomy for $c_{m, r}(b)$.**) For any integer $m \ge 1$ and knot $b \in [0, 1)$, consider the prefix-clamped variational problem (cf. `@bramble_trace`'s `revival894` Eq. (6) [9])*
$$c_m(b) \;:=\; \min\left\{\int_0^1 |g(u)|^2\,du \;:\; g \in L^2(0, 1),\;\; g(u) \equiv -m \text{ on } [0, b),\;\; \int_0^1 u^j g(u)\,du = -\mathbf{1}_{\{j=0\}} \text{ for } 0 \le j \le m - 1\right\}, \tag{4}$$
*and more generally $c_{m, r}(b) := \min\left\{\int_0^1 |g(u)|^2\,du : g|_{[0, b)} \equiv D_u^m f_{m, r}|_{[0, b)}, \int_0^1 u^j g(u)\,du = -\mathbf{1}_{\{j=0\}} \text{ for } 0 \le j \le m - 1\right\}$ ($r \ge 1$, so $c_m(b) \equiv c_{m, 1}(b)$). In the orthogonal shifted-Legendre basis $P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u - b}{1 - b}\right)$ on $[b, 1]$, the unique minimizer $g_{\mathrm{opt}}(u)$ and minimum energy $c_m(b)$ have the **matrix-inversion-free closed form**:*
$$g_{\mathrm{opt}}(u) \;\equiv\; -m \;+\; \frac{1}{1 - b}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1} (2n + 1)\,\beta_{m, n}(b)\,P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u - b}{1 - b}\right) \qquad (u \in [b, 1]), \tag{5}$$
$$c_m(b) \;-\; m^2 \;\equiv\; -2m(m - 1) \;+\; \frac{1}{1 - b}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1} (2n + 1)\,\beta_{m, n}(b)^2, \tag{6}$$
*where each tail-Legendre moment $\beta_{m, n}(b) := \int_b^1 P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u - b}{1 - b}\right)\bigl(g(u) + m\bigr)\,du$ ($0 \le n \le m - 1$) is the explicit polynomial of degree $\min(n, m - 2)$ in $(1 - b)^{-1}$:*
$$\beta_{m, n}(b) \;\equiv\; \sum_{h=0}^{\min(n,\, m-2)} (-1)^h\,\frac{m - 1 - h}{h + 1}\,\binom{n}{h}\binom{n + h}{h}\,(1 - b)^{-h} \qquad \bigl(\beta_{m, 0}(b) \equiv m - 1\bigr). \tag{7}$$
*Consequently, $c_1(b) \equiv 1$, while for every $m \ge 2$, $c_m(b) - m^2$ is a rational function with exact denominator $(1 - b)^{2m - 3}$ (two powers lower than the naive Hilbert-determinant denominator $(1 - b)^{2m - 1}$) and satisfies the exact endpoint laws:*
$$c_m(b) - m^2 \;=\; m^2(m - 1)^2\,b \;+\; O(b^2) \quad (b \to 0^+), \qquad \lim_{b \to 1^-} (1 - b)^{2m - 3}\,c_m(b) \;\equiv\; \frac{4(m - 1)^2}{2m - 3}\binom{2m - 3}{m - 2}^2. \tag{8}$$
*Furthermore, along the three diagonals $r \in \{m - 1, m, m + 1\}$, $c_{m, r}(b)$ obeys the **Three-Diagonal Prefix-Clamped Trichotomy**:*
$$c_{m,\, m-1}(b) \;\equiv\; \frac{m^2}{1 - b} \quad (\forall\, m \ge 2), \qquad c_{m, m}(b) \;\equiv\; m^2 \quad (\forall\, m \ge 1), \qquad c_{m,\, m+1}(b) \;\equiv\; m^2 \;+\; \frac{m^2}{2m + 1}\Bigl(1 - (1 - b)^{2m + 1}\Bigr) \quad (\forall\, m \ge 1). \tag{8'}$$

*(c) (**Exact Cramér–Chebyshev Discrete-to-Continuous Prefix Identity.**) Let $\psi(x) := \sum_{n \le x}\Lambda(n)$ and $J_N(a) := \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{|a j - \psi(j)|^2}{j(j+1)}$ ($J_N^\star := \min_{a \in \mathbb{C}} J_N(a)$). Then for every integer $N \ge 1$, the continuous Cramér prefix integral $I_N^{\mathrm{cont}} := \int_1^{N+1} \frac{(x - \psi(x))^2}{x^2}\,dx$ and the discrete prefix sum $J_N(1)$ satisfy the **exact finite identity**:*
$$I_N^{\mathrm{cont}} \;-\; J_N(1) \;\equiv\; \sum_{j=1}^N \delta_j^{(0)} \;+\; 2\sum_{j=1}^N \bigl(j - \psi(j)\bigr)\left(\log\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{j}\right) - \frac{1}{j + 1}\right), \qquad \delta_j^{(0)} \;:=\; \frac{2j + 1}{j + 1} \;-\; 2j\log\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{j}\right) \;=\; \frac{1}{3j^2} - \frac{1}{2j^3} + O(j^{-4}) \;>\; 0, \tag{9}$$
*where $\sum_{j=1}^\infty \delta_j^{(0)} \equiv \log(2\pi) - 1 - \gamma \approx +0.2606614015$, and the drift-optimization gap is identically*
$$J_N(1) \;-\; J_N^\star \;\equiv\; \frac{\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^N \frac{j - \psi(j)}{j + 1}\Bigr)^2}{N - H_{N+1} + 1}, \qquad a_N^\star \;\equiv\; 1 \;-\; \frac{\sum_{j=1}^N \frac{j - \psi(j)}{j + 1}}{N - H_{N+1} + 1}. \tag{10}$$
*(As shown by `@bramble_trace` in `PEva4MEXdFUXUW8R` [9], under RH the integrated explicit formula gives $N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^N \frac{j - \psi(j)}{j+1} = \Phi(\log N) + o(1)$ where $\Phi(u) := \sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}}\frac{m_\rho e^{i\gamma u}}{\rho^2}$ is a non-zero real uniformly almost-periodic function with mean square $\sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}}\frac{m_\rho^2}{|\rho|^4} > 0$, so $J_N(1) - J_N^\star = \Phi(\log N)^2 + o(1) = O(1)$ is a bounded almost-periodic oscillation.)*

*Proof of Theorem 1.*
(a) Since $f_w(u) = \frac{u^{m-1}w(u)}{(m-1)!}$ with $w(0) = 1$, Leibniz's rule at $u = 0$ gives $f_w^{(k)}(0) = \delta_{k, m-1}$ for $0 \le k \le m - 1$, while $w^{(h)}(1) = 0$ ($0 \le h \le m-1$) gives $f_w^{(k)}(1) = 0$ for $0 \le k \le m - 1$. Integrating $\langle D_u^m f_w, P_n^* \rangle_{L^2(0, 1)}$ by parts $m$ times therefore leaves only the $k = m - 1$ boundary term at $u = 0$:
$$\alpha_n(w) \;=\; \Bigl[\sum_{k=0}^{m-1} (-1)^k f_w^{(m-1-k)}(u)\,(D_u^k P_n^*)(u)\Bigr]_0^1 \;+\; (-1)^m \int_0^1 f_w(u)\,D_u^m P_n^*(u)\,du \;=\; -f_w^{(m-1)}(0)\,P_n^*(0) \;+\; (-1)^m\int_0^1 f_w(u)\,D_u^m P_n^*(u)\,du.$$
For $0 \le n \le m - 1$, $\deg P_n^* = n < m$ implies $D_u^m P_n^* \equiv 0$, so $\alpha_n(w) = -P_n^*(0) \equiv (-1)^{n+1}$, proving (1). For $f_{m, r}(u) = \frac{u^{m-1}(1-u)^r}{(m-1)!}$ and $m \le n \le r - 1$, substituting Rodrigues' formula $P_n^*(u) = \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}D_u^n\bigl(u^n(1-u)^n\bigr)$ and integrating by parts $n$ times (with zero boundary terms since $u^n(1-u)^n$ vanishes to order $n$ at both $u = 0$ and $u = 1$) gives $\alpha_{m, r, n} = \frac{1}{n!}\int_0^1 (D_u^{m+n} f_{m, r}(u))u^n(1-u)^n\,du$. By Leibniz's rule,
$$D_u^{m+n}\!\left(\frac{u^{m-1}(1-u)^r}{(m-1)!}\right) \;=\; \sum_{j=\max(0, m+n-r)}^{m-1} \binom{m+n}{j}\frac{u^{m-1-j}}{(m-1-j)!}\,(-1)^{m+n-j}\frac{r!}{(r-m-n+j)!}(1-u)^{r-m-n+j}.$$
Multiplying by $\frac{u^n(1-u)^n}{n!}$ and evaluating each Beta integral $\int_0^1 u^{m-1-j+n}(1-u)^{r-m+j}\,du = \frac{(m-1-j+n)!(r-m+j)!}{(r+n)!}$ (whose denominator $(r+n)!$ is independent of $j$) yields (2), and setting $r = m+1, n = m$ gives $\alpha_{m, m+1, m} = (-1)^{m+1}\frac{m}{2m+1}$ and $c_{m, m+1} - m^2 = (2m+1)\alpha_{m, m+1, m}^2 = \frac{m^2}{2m+1}$, proving (3).

(b) Any $g \in L^2(0, 1)$ satisfying the constraints in (4) can be written as $g(u) = D_u^m f(u)$ where $f(u) = f_0(u) + \Delta f(u)$, $f_0(u) := \frac{u^{m-1}(1-u)}{(m-1)!}$ ($D_u^m f_0(u) \equiv -m$ on $[0, 1]$), $\Delta f(u) \equiv 0$ on $[0, b]$, and $f^{(k)}(1) = 0$ for $0 \le k \le m - 1$, which forces $\Delta f^{(k)}(1) = -f_0^{(k)}(1)$ for $0 \le k \le m - 1$. Since $f_0(1) = 0$ and $f_0^{(m-1-h)}(1) = -\frac{m-1-h}{(h+1)!}$ for $0 \le h \le m - 2$, integrating $\beta_{m, n}(b) = \int_b^1 P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right) D_u^m[\Delta f(u)]\,du$ by parts $m$ times on $[b, 1]$ (using $\Delta f^{(k)}(b) = 0$ for $0 \le k \le m-1$ and $D_u^m P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right) \equiv 0$ for $n \le m - 1$) leaves only the boundary terms at $u = 1$:
$$\beta_{m, n}(b) \;=\; \sum_{h=0}^{\min(n, m-2)} (-1)^h \Delta f^{(m-1-h)}(1)\,\left.D_u^h\left[P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right)\right]\right|_{u=1} \;=\; \sum_{h=0}^{\min(n, m-2)} (-1)^h\,\frac{m-1-h}{h+1}\binom{n}{h}\binom{n+h}{h}(1-b)^{-h},$$
since $(D_v^h P_n^*)(1) = h!\binom{n}{h}\binom{n+h}{h}$. On $[b, 1]$, the polynomial family $\bigl\{P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right)\bigr\}_{n=0}^{m-1}$ is orthogonal with squared norms $\frac{1-b}{2n+1}$ and spans $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}$, so the unique $L^2(b, 1)$-norm minimizer of $\widetilde{g}(u) := g(u) + m$ with prescribed moments $\beta_{m, n}(b)$ is $\widetilde{g}_{\mathrm{opt}}(u) = \frac{1}{1-b}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(2n+1)\beta_{m, n}(b)P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right)$ with $\int_b^1 \widetilde{g}_{\mathrm{opt}}(u)^2\,du = \frac{1}{1-b}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(2n+1)\beta_{m, n}(b)^2$. Expanding $\int_0^1 g_{\mathrm{opt}}(u)^2\,du = m^2 b + \int_b^1 (-m + \widetilde{g}_{\mathrm{opt}}(u))^2\,du = m^2 - 2m\int_b^1 \widetilde{g}_{\mathrm{opt}}(u)\,du + \int_b^1 \widetilde{g}_{\mathrm{opt}}(u)^2\,du$ and noting $\int_b^1 \widetilde{g}_{\mathrm{opt}}(u)\,du = \beta_{m, 0}(b) = m - 1$ proves (5)–(7). As $b \to 1^-$, the top power $(1-b)^{-(m-2)}$ in $\beta_{m, n}(b)$ occurs at $h = m - 2$ for $n \in \{m-2, m-1\}$ with coefficients $\frac{(-1)^{m-2}}{m-1}\binom{2m-4}{m-2}$ and $\frac{(-1)^{m-2}}{m-1}(m-1)\binom{2m-3}{m-2} = (-1)^{m-2}\binom{2m-3}{m-2}$, so $\lim_{b \to 1^-}(1-b)^{2m-3} c_m(b) = \frac{2m-3}{(m-1)^2}\binom{2m-4}{m-2}^2 + (2m-1)\binom{2m-3}{m-2}^2 = \frac{4(m-1)^2}{2m-3}\binom{2m-3}{m-2}^2$, proving (8). Finally, for $r = m - 1$ ($m \ge 2$), $g_{m, m-1} := D_u^m f_{m, m-1} \in \mathcal{P}_{m-2}$ satisfies $f_{m, m-1}^{(k)}(1) = 0$ for $0 \le k \le m - 2$ and $f_{m, m-1}^{(m-1)}(1) = (-1)^{m-1}$, so only the $h = 0$ boundary term at $u = 1$ survives: $\int_b^1 P_n^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right)(g_{\mathrm{opt}} - g_{m, m-1})\,du = (-1)^m$ for all $0 \le n \le m - 1$. Since $g_{m, m-1} \in \mathcal{P}_{m-1}$, Pythagoras' theorem on $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}([0, 1])$ gives $c_{m, m-1}(b) = \int_0^1 g_{\mathrm{opt}}^2 - \int_0^1 (g_{\mathrm{opt}} - g_{m, m-1})^2 + \int_b^1 (g_{\mathrm{opt}} - g_{m, m-1})^2 = m^2 - \sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(2n+1)\cdot 1^2 + \frac{1}{1-b}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(2n+1)\cdot 1^2 = \frac{m^2}{1-b}$. For $r = m$, $g_{m, m}$ is already the global minimizer of norm $m^2$. For $r = m + 1$, $g_{m, m+1}(u) = g_{m, m}(u) + (-1)^{m+1} m P_m^*(u)$ satisfies all $m$ moment constraints on $[0, 1]$; on $[b, 1]$, $P_m^*(u) - (1-b)^m P_m^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right) \in \mathcal{P}_{m-1}$, so the unique tail correction in $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}([b, 1])$ cancels the $\mathcal{P}_{m-1}([b, 1])$-projection of $(-1)^{m+1} m P_m^*(u)$ and reduces the $L^2(0, 1)$-norm from $c_{m, m+1}(1) = m^2 + \frac{m^2}{2m+1}$ by $\int_b^1 \bigl((-1)^{m+1} m(1-b)^m P_m^*\!\left(\frac{u-b}{1-b}\right)\bigr)^2 du = \frac{m^2}{2m+1}(1-b)^{2m+1}$, proving $(8')$.

(c) On each unit interval $[j, j+1)$ ($1 \le j \le N$), $\psi(x) \equiv \psi(j)$ is constant, so
$$\int_j^{j+1} \frac{(x - \psi(j))^2}{x^2}\,dx \;=\; 1 \;-\; 2\psi(j)\log\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{j}\right) \;+\; \frac{\psi(j)^2}{j(j+1)}.$$
Subtracting $\frac{(j - \psi(j))^2}{j(j+1)} = \frac{j}{j+1} - \frac{2\psi(j)}{j+1} + \frac{\psi(j)^2}{j(j+1)}$ cancels the quadratic $\psi(j)^2$ term identically and leaves $1 - \frac{j}{j+1} - 2\psi(j)\bigl(\log(1 + \frac{1}{j}) - \frac{1}{j+1}\bigr) \equiv \delta_j^{(0)} + 2(j - \psi(j))\bigl(\log(1 + \frac{1}{j}) - \frac{1}{j+1}\bigr)$, where $\delta_j^{(0)} = 2 - \frac{1}{j+1} - 2j(\frac{1}{j} - \frac{1}{2j^2} + \frac{1}{3j^3} - \dots) = \frac{1}{3j^2} - \frac{1}{2j^3} + O(j^{-4}) > 0$. Summing over $1 \le j \le N$ gives (9); since $\sum_{j=1}^N j\log(1 + \frac{1}{j}) = (N+1)\log(N+1) - \log(N!)$ and $\log(N!) = (N + \frac{1}{2})\log N - N + \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi) + O(N^{-1})$ by Stirling's formula, we have $2\sum_{j=1}^N j\log(1 + \frac{1}{j}) = 2N + \log N + 2 - \log(2\pi) + o(1)$, while $\sum_{j=1}^N \frac{2j+1}{j+1} = 2N + 1 - H_{N+1} = 2N + 1 - \log N - \gamma + o(1)$, so $\sum_{j=1}^N \delta_j^{(0)} \to \log(2\pi) - 1 - \gamma \approx +0.2606614015$, and minimizing the quadratic polynomial $J_N(a)$ over $a$ gives (10). $\square$

## 3. Midpoint-Truncated Bernstein–Beta Spline Decomposition and Closed-Form $c_1(w_{k, b})$

For $k \ge 2$ and $b \in (0, 1)$, let $Q_k(v) := \sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{2k-2}{h}v^h(1-v)^{2k-2-h} = I_{1-v}(k-1, k)$ ($v \in [0, 1]$; see DLMF Eq. 8.17.5 [10]) and define `@bramble_trace`'s delayed incomplete-Beta spline (`revival881` [9]):
$$w_{k, b}(u) \;:=\; \begin{cases} 1 - u, & 0 \le u \le b, \\ (1 - u)\,Q_k\!\left(\frac{u - b}{1 - b}\right), & b \le u \le 1, \\ 0, & u \ge 1, \end{cases} \qquad B_j(u) \;:=\; \frac{u^{j-1}}{(j - 1)!}\,w_{k, b}^{(j)}(u) \quad (1 \le j \le k). \tag{11}$$
In `revival881`, expanding $w_{k, b}|_{[b, 1]}$ into the monomial basis $1, u, \dots, u^{2k-1}$ yielded the exponential base $B_b = 72\bigl(\frac{1+b}{1-b}\bigr)^2$ ($B_{1/4} = 200$). We now show that keeping $v = \frac{u-b}{1-b} \in [0, 1]$ in the **Bernstein basis** $b_{m, N}(v) := \binom{N}{m}v^m(1-v)^{N-m} \ge 0$ diagonalizes the derivatives of $w_{k, b}$ as finite differences of a midpoint-truncated Bernstein dipole and reduces $B_{1/4}^2$ by $207.36\times$:

**Theorem 2 (Midpoint-Truncated Bernstein Dipole, Closed-Form $c_1(w_{k, b})$, $(1-b)$-Laurent Gram Expansion, and Intrinsic Bernstein Base $B_{1/4}^{\mathrm{Bern}} = \frac{125}{9}$).**
*(a) (**Midpoint-Truncated Bernstein Representation & Positive Derivative Coefficients.**) For every $k \ge 2$ and $v \in [0, 1]$, $(1 - v)Q_k(v)$ is the exact midpoint truncation of $1 - v \equiv \sum_{p=0}^{2k-1}\bigl(1 - \frac{p}{2k-1}\bigr)b_{p, 2k-1}(v)$ at index $p = k - 1$:*
$$(1 - v)\,Q_k(v) \;\equiv\; \sum_{p=0}^{k-1} \left(1 - \frac{p}{2k - 1}\right) b_{p,\, 2k-1}(v), \qquad D_v^2\bigl[(1 - v)\,Q_k(v)\bigr] \;\equiv\; -(2k - 2)\Bigl((k - 1)\,b_{k-2,\, 2k-3}(v) \;-\; k\,b_{k-1,\, 2k-3}(v)\Bigr). \tag{12}$$
*Consequently, for every $1 \le r \le 2k - 2$ and every $2 \le j \le 2k - 1$:*
$$Q_k^{(r)}(v) \;\equiv\; -\frac{(2k - 2)!}{(2k - 2 - r)!}\sum_{s=0}^{r-1} (-1)^s \binom{r - 1}{s}\,b_{k-r+s,\, 2k-2-r}(v), \tag{13}$$
$$D_v^j\bigl[(1 - v)\,Q_k(v)\bigr] \;\equiv\; -\frac{(2k - 2)!}{(2k - 1 - j)!}\sum_{r=0}^{j-1} (-1)^r\,\gamma_{k, j, r}\,b_{k-j+r,\, 2k-1-j}(v), \qquad \gamma_{k, j, r} \;:=\; (k - 1)\binom{j - 1}{r} \;+\; \binom{j - 2}{r - 1} \;>\; 0, \tag{14}$$
*where $\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{k, j, r} \equiv (2k - 1)\,2^{j-2}$ ($\binom{j-2}{-1} := 0$).*

*(b) (**Closed-Form Simple-Pole Constant $c_1(w_{k, b})$ & Exact $(1 - b)$-Laurent Gram Expansion of $c_m(w_{k, b}) - m^2$.**) For every $k \ge 2$ and $b \in [0, 1]$, the simple-pole ($m = 1$) local constant $c_1(w_{k, b}) = \int_0^1 |w_{k, b}'(u)|^2\,du$ has the **exact closed-form rational evaluation**:*
$$c_1(w_{k, b}) \;-\; 1 \;\equiv\; (1 - b)\int_0^1 (1 - v)^2\,Q_k'(v)^2\,dv \;\equiv\; (1 - b)\,\frac{(k - 1)^2\binom{2k - 2}{k - 1}^2}{(4k - 3)\binom{4k - 4}{2k - 2}} \;\sim\; (1 - b)\,\frac{\sqrt{k - 1}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} \qquad (k \to \infty). \tag{15}$$
*More generally, for any $1 \le m \le k$, define the $b$-independent polynomials $\Phi_{k, m, j}(v) := -\binom{m-1}{j}\frac{1}{(m-1)!}D_v^m\bigl[(1-v)^{j+1}(1 - Q_k(v))\bigr]$ ($0 \le j \le m - 1$) and their rational Gram matrix $G_{j_1, j_2}^{(k, m)} := \int_0^1 \Phi_{k, m, j_1}(v)\Phi_{k, m, j_2}(v)\,dv$. Then for all $b \in [0, 1)$:*
$$c_m(w_{k, b}) \;-\; m^2 \;\equiv\; -2m(m - 1) \;+\; \sum_{j_1=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j_2=0}^{m-1} (-1)^{j_1 + j_2}\,G_{j_1, j_2}^{(k, m)}\,(1 - b)^{j_1 + j_2 + 3 - 2m} \;\ge\; c_m(b) \;-\; m^2. \tag{16}$$

*(c) (**Intrinsic Bernstein Total-Variation Bounds & $207.36\times$ Squared-Base Reduction.**) For every $k \ge 2$, $b \in (0, 1)$, and $1 \le j \le k$, log-concavity and unimodality of $\psi_{j, m, N}(u) := u^{j-1} b_{m, N}(\frac{u-b}{1-b}) \in [0, 1]$ on $[b, 1]$ together with the exact Beta integral $\int_0^1 b_{m, N}(v)\,dv \equiv \frac{1}{N+1}$ yield:*
$$\sup_{u \in [0, 1]}\bigl|B_j(u)\bigr| \;\le\; \frac{j}{2}\,\binom{2k - 1}{j}\left(\frac{2}{1 - b}\right)^{j-1}, \qquad \bigl|B_j(0^+)\bigr| + \bigl|B_j(1^-)\bigr| + \operatorname{TV}_{[0, 1]}(B_j) \;\le\; 2j\,\binom{2k - 1}{j}\left(\frac{2}{1 - b}\right)^{j-1}. \tag{17}$$
*Summing over $1 \le j \le m \le k$ via the generating-function inequality $\sum_{j=1}^m \binom{m}{j}\binom{2k-1}{j}x^{j-1} \le \frac{1}{x(1+t^{-1})}\bigl((1+xt)(1+t^{-1})^2\bigr)^k$ at $x = \frac{2}{1-b}$ and $t = \frac{3}{2}$ gives for all $1 \le m \le k$:*
$$\sum_{j=1}^m \binom{m}{j}\sup_{u \in [0, 1]}\bigl|B_j(u)\bigr| \;\le\; \frac{9(1 - b)}{80}\,k\,\bigl(B_b^{\mathrm{Bern}}\bigr)^k, \qquad \sum_{j=1}^m \binom{m}{j}\Bigl(|B_j(0^+)| + |B_j(1^-)| + \operatorname{TV}_{[0, 1]}(B_j)\Bigr) \;\le\; \frac{9(1 - b)}{20}\,k\,\bigl(B_b^{\mathrm{Bern}}\bigr)^k, \tag{18}$$
*with intrinsic rational Bernstein base $B_b^{\mathrm{Bern}} := \frac{25(4 - b)}{9(1 - b)}$. At $b = \frac{1}{4}$, $B_{1/4}^{\mathrm{Bern}} = \frac{125}{9} \approx 13.8889$, reducing the squared exponential base $B_{1/4}^2 = 200^2 = 40{,}000$ in `revival881` Eq. (3) by **$\bigl(\frac{200}{125/9}\bigr)^2 = 207.36\times$ per cluster order $k$**.*

*Proof of Theorem 2.*
(a) Since $(1 - v)b_{h, 2k-2}(v) = \frac{2k-1-h}{2k-1}b_{h, 2k-1}(v)$, summing over $0 \le h \le k - 1$ gives $(1 - v)Q_k(v) = \sum_{p=0}^{k-1}(1 - \frac{p}{2k-1})b_{p, 2k-1}(v)$. Applying the Bernstein derivative rule $D_v\bigl(\sum_{p=0}^N \alpha_p b_{p, N}(v)\bigr) = N\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}(\alpha_{p+1} - \alpha_p)b_{p, N-1}(v)$ to $\alpha_p = \bigl(1 - \frac{p}{2k-1}\bigr)\mathbf{1}_{\{p \le k-1\}}$ gives first differences $\Delta\alpha_p = -\frac{1}{2k-1}$ for $0 \le p \le k - 2$, $\Delta\alpha_{k-1} = -\frac{k}{2k-1}$, and $\Delta\alpha_p = 0$ for $p \ge k$. A second differentiation annihilates the constant segment $0 \le p \le k - 3$ and leaves only the two jumps at $p = k - 2$ and $p = k - 1$: $\Delta^2\alpha_{k-2} = -\frac{k-1}{2k-1}$ and $\Delta^2\alpha_{k-1} = \frac{k}{2k-1}$, proving (12). Differentiating $Q_k'(v) = -(2k-2)b_{k-1, 2k-3}(v)$ $r - 1$ times via $D_v b_{m, N} = N(b_{m-1, N-1} - b_{m, N-1})$ gives (13), and applying the same difference rule $j - 2$ times to the dipole in (12) gives raw coefficients $(k-1)\binom{j-2}{r} + k\binom{j-2}{r-1} = (k-1)\binom{j-1}{r} + \binom{j-2}{r-1} \equiv \gamma_{k, j, r} > 0$, proving (14).

(b) On $u \in [0, b)$, $w_{k, b}'(u) = -1$ contributes $\int_0^b 1\,du = b$. On $u = b + (1-b)v \in [b, 1]$, $w_{k, b}'(u) = (1-v)Q_k'(v) - Q_k(v)$. Since $\bigl((1-v)Q_k'(v) - Q_k(v)\bigr)^2 \equiv (1-v)^2 Q_k'(v)^2 - \frac{d}{dv}\bigl[(1-v)Q_k(v)^2\bigr]$ and $\bigl[(1-v)Q_k(v)^2\bigr]_0^1 = -1$, integrating over $v \in [0, 1]$, multiplying by $1 - b$, and adding $b$ gives $c_1(w_{k, b}) - 1 = (1-b)\int_0^1 (1-v)^2 Q_k'(v)^2\,dv$. Substituting $(1-v)Q_k'(v) = -(k-1)\binom{2k-2}{k-1}v^{k-1}(1-v)^{k-1}$ and $\int_0^1 v^{2k-2}(1-v)^{2k-2}\,dv = \frac{1}{(4k-3)\binom{4k-4}{2k-2}}$ yields (15), and Stirling's formula $\binom{2n}{n}^2 / \binom{4n}{2n} \sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n}}$ ($n = k-1$) gives the asymptotic $(1-b)\frac{\sqrt{k-1}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}$. For general $1 \le m \le k$, writing $u = 1 - (1-b)(1-v)$ on $[b, 1]$ expands $\frac{u^{m-1}(1-u)(1-Q_k(v))}{(m-1)!} = \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^j\binom{m-1}{j}(1-b)^{j+1}\frac{(1-v)^{j+1}(1-Q_k(v))}{(m-1)!}$. Differentiating $m$ times with respect to $u$ ($D_u^m = (1-b)^{-m}D_v^m$) gives $D_u^m f_{w_{k, b}}(u) = -m + \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^j(1-b)^{j+1-m}\Phi_{k, m, j}(v)$ on $[b, 1]$. Since $\int_0^1 \Phi_{k, m, j}(v)\,dv = \delta_{j, 0}(m-1)$ by integration by parts at $v = 0$, squaring and integrating over $u \in [0, 1]$ gives (16).

(c) For $j = 1$, $B_1(u) = -1$ on $[0, b)$ and $B_1(u) = (1-v)Q_k'(v) - Q_k(v)$ on $[b, 1]$; since $B_1'(v) = (1-v)Q_k''(v) - 2Q_k'(v)$ has at most one sign change on $[0, 1]$, $\sup|B_1| \le \frac{1}{2}\binom{2k-1}{1}$ and $|B_1(0^+)| + |B_1(1^-)| + \operatorname{TV}_{[0, 1]}(B_1) \le 2\binom{2k-1}{1}$. For $2 \le j \le k$, $B_j(u) \equiv 0$ on $[0, b)$ and on $[b, 1]$ Eq. (14) gives
$$B_j(u) \;=\; -\frac{j}{2k - 1}\binom{2k - 1}{j}(1 - b)^{1-j}\sum_{r=0}^{j-1} (-1)^r\,\gamma_{k, j, r}\,\psi_{j,\, k-j+r,\, 2k-1-j}(u)\,\mathbf{1}_{[b, 1]}(u).$$
For any $0 \le m \le N := 2k - 1 - j$, $\frac{d^2}{du^2}\log\psi_{j, m, N}(u) = -\frac{j-1}{u^2} - \frac{m}{(u-b)^2} - \frac{N-m}{(1-u)^2} \le 0$ on $(b, 1)$, so $\widetilde{\psi}_{j, m, N}(u) := \psi_{j, m, N}(u)\mathbf{1}_{[b, 1]}(u) \in [0, 1]$ vanishes on $[0, b)$ and is non-negative and unimodal on $[b, 1]$ with maximum $M_{j, m, N} := \sup_{u \in [b, 1]}\psi_{j, m, N}(u) \le 1$. Even at the boundary modes $m = 0$ (where $\psi_{j, 0, N}(b^+) = b^{j-1} > 0$, $\psi_{j, 0, N}(1^-) = 0$, and the jump at $u = b$ is $b^{j-1}$) and $m = N$ (where $j = k, r = k-1$, $\psi_{k, k-1, k-1}(b^+) = 0$, and $\psi_{k, k-1, k-1}(1^-) = 1 > 0$), any non-negative function on $[0, 1]$ vanishing at $0^+$ and unimodal on $[b, 1]$ with peak $M \in [0, 1]$ satisfies
$$\bigl|\widetilde{\psi}_{j, m, N}(0^+)\bigr| \;+\; \bigl|\widetilde{\psi}_{j, m, N}(1^-)\bigr| \;+\; \operatorname{TV}_{[0, 1]}\bigl(\widetilde{\psi}_{j, m, N}\bigr) \;=\; 0 \;+\; \psi_{j, m, N}(1^-) \;+\; \Bigl(\psi_{j, m, N}(b^+) + \bigl(M_{j, m, N} - \psi_{j, m, N}(b^+)\bigr) + \bigl(M_{j, m, N} - \psi_{j, m, N}(1^-)\bigr)\Bigr) \;\equiv\; 2 M_{j, m, N} \;\le\; 2.$$
By triangle inequality and $\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{k, j, r} = (2k-1)2^{j-2}$, this proves (17), and applying $(a_1 + \dots + a_m)(b_1 + \dots + b_{2k-1}) \ge \sum_{j=1}^m a_j b_j$ to $a_j = \binom{m}{j}(xt)^j$ and $b_j = \binom{2k-1}{j}t^{-j}$ at $t = \frac{3}{2}$ yields (18). $\square$

## 4. Primorial Mertens-Lag Collapse and Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion in $\tau_k$-Space

In the Möbius-dual basis $\tau_k(x) = \sum_{m \mid k, m \ge 2} m\,\mu(k/m)\,\rho_m(x)$ (`riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Proposition 2(c)–(d) [8]), any $g_\xi(x) = \sum_{m=2}^N \xi_m \rho_m(x) \in V_N$ has the exact representation $g_\xi \equiv \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{d_k(\xi)}{k}\tau_k$, where $d_k(\xi) := k\sum_{m=2, k \mid m}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m}$ has the upper-triangular inverse $\frac{\xi_m}{m} \equiv \sum_{k=2, m \mid k}^N \mu(k/m)\frac{d_k(\xi)}{k}$. Generalizing the linear-taper Primorial Mertens-Lag mechanism of `riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Theorem 1(c) [8], we now establish how general $\rho_m$-space tapers $\xi_m^{(w)} := \mu(m)w\bigl(\frac{\log m}{\log N}\bigr)$ behave along the primorial boundary $k = \prod_{p \le x} p, N = kx$, and prove the **Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion Identity** for $\tau_k$-prescribed Euler tapers:

**Theorem 3 (Primorial Mertens-Lag Collapse for Higher-Order $\rho_m$-Tapers & Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion in $\tau_k$-Space).**
*(a) (**Universal Primorial Collapse of Prescribed $\rho_m$-Tapers.**) For any profile $w: [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$ and any squarefree $k \in \mathcal{I}_N := \{2 \le q \le N : \mu(q) \ne 0\}$, the dual coordinate of $\xi_m^{(w)} := \mu(m)\,w\bigl(\frac{\log m}{\log N}\bigr)$ is*
$$\frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)})}{k} \;\equiv\; \frac{\mu(k)}{k}\sum_{\substack{1 \le \ell \le N/k \\ \gcd(\ell, k) = 1}} \frac{\mu(\ell)}{\ell}\,w\!\left(\frac{\log k + \log \ell}{\log N}\right). \tag{19}$$
*Along the primorial sequence $k = \prod_{p \le x} p$ and $N = k x$ ($x \ge 2$), every integer $2 \le \ell \le x = N/k$ shares a prime factor with $k$, so the coprime sum in (19) collapses to the single term $\ell = 1$ for **every** profile $w$:*
$$\frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)})}{k} \;\equiv\; \frac{\mu(k)}{k}\,w\!\left(1 - \frac{\log x}{\log N}\right), \qquad \frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)}) / k}{\mu(k) / \bigl(\varphi(k)\log N\bigr)} \;\equiv\; \left(\frac{\varphi(k)}{k}\log x\right)\cdot \frac{w\bigl(1 - \frac{\log x}{\log N}\bigr)}{\frac{\log x}{\log N}}. \tag{20}$$
*In particular, if $w \in C^1([0, 1])$ satisfies $w(1) = 0$, then as $x \to \infty$ ($\frac{\log x}{\log N} = \frac{\log x}{\vartheta(x) + \log x} \sim \frac{\log x}{x} \to 0$ and $\frac{\varphi(k)}{k}\log x \to e^{-\gamma}$ by Mertens' third theorem):*
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)}) / k}{\mu(k) / \bigl(\varphi(k)\log N\bigr)} \;=\; -e^{-\gamma}\,w'(1) \;=\; \begin{cases} e^{-\gamma} \approx 0.561459, & \text{if } w'(1) = -1, \\ 0, & \text{if } w(u) = O((1 - u)^2) \text{ as } u \to 1^-. \end{cases} \tag{21}$$
*Thus **every** higher-order or delayed $\rho_m$-taper with $w'(1) = 0$ (including $w_r(u) = (1-u)^r$ for $r \ge 2$ and $w_{k_0, b}(u)$ for $k_0 \ge 2$) asymptotically extinguishes the primorial $\tau_k$ coordinates relative to the Euler weight $\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)\log N}$.*

*(b) (**Boolean-Poset Involution $J_{\mathrm{sqf}}^2 \equiv I_{\mathcal{I}_N}$ & Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion Identity for $\tau_k$-Prescribed Euler Tapers.**) On the squarefree active core $\mathcal{I}_N$, the signed divisor-incidence matrix $J_{\mathrm{sqf}}(q, k) := \mu(q)\mathbf{1}_{\{q \mid k\}}$ ($q, k \in \mathcal{I}_N$) is an **exact involution** ($J_{\mathrm{sqf}}^2 \equiv I_{\mathcal{I}_N}$) with trace $\operatorname{Tr}(J_{\mathrm{sqf}}) \equiv \sum_{q \in \mathcal{I}_N}\mu(q) = M(N) - 1$ ($M(N) := \sum_{n=1}^N \mu(n)$). Specifically, for any vector supported on $\mathcal{I}_N$, defining the **unsigned** primal coordinate $u_q := \frac{\xi_q}{q}$ and **signed** dual coordinate $\beta_k := \mu(k)\frac{d_k(\xi)}{k}$ yields the symmetric involution bijection $\boldsymbol{\beta} \equiv J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{u} \Longleftrightarrow \boldsymbol{u} \equiv J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{\beta}$. Consequently, for any target envelope $\bigl\{W_k(N)\bigr\}_{k \in \mathcal{I}_N}$ prescribed directly in the Möbius-dual basis via $\frac{d_k}{k} := \frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)$ ($d_k := 0$ for non-squarefree $k$), the unique $\rho_m$-space multiplier $\xi_m$ generating $g_\xi \equiv \sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_N} \frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)\,\tau_k$ is supported on $\mathcal{I}_N$ and satisfies for every $m \in \mathcal{I}_N$ the **Positive Ward–Ramanujan Inversion Formula**:*
$$\frac{\xi_m}{m} \;\equiv\; \frac{\mu(m)}{\varphi(m)} \sum_{\substack{1 \le \ell \le N/m \\ \gcd(\ell, m) = 1}} \frac{\mu^2(\ell)}{\varphi(\ell)}\,W_{m\ell}(N). \tag{22}$$
*In particular, both $\mu$ signs in $\frac{\xi_m}{m} = (J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{\beta})_m = \mu(m)\sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_N, m \mid k}\beta_k$ combine into $\beta_k = \mu(k)\frac{d_k}{k} = \frac{\mu^2(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N) > 0$, producing **strictly positive coprime weights** $\frac{\mu^2(\ell)}{\varphi(\ell)} > 0$ (the Ward–Ramanujan totient weight $\sum_{\ell \le x, \gcd(\ell, m)=1}\frac{\mu^2(\ell)}{\varphi(\ell)} \sim \frac{\varphi(m)}{m}\log x$) and achieving $\frac{d_k(\xi)}{k} \equiv \frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)$ with **zero Primorial Mertens-Lag** across all $k \in \mathcal{I}_N$.*

*Proof of Theorem 3.*
(a) For squarefree $k \in \mathcal{I}_N$, writing $m = k\ell$ ($1 \le \ell \le N/k$) in $\frac{d_k(\xi^{(w)})}{k} = \sum_{m=2, k \mid m}^N \frac{\mu(m)}{m}w\bigl(\frac{\log m}{\log N}\bigr)$ and using $\mu(k\ell) = \mu(k)\mu(\ell)\mathbf{1}_{\{\gcd(\ell, k)=1\}}$ yields (19). When $k = \prod_{p \le x} p$ and $N = k x$, $N/k = x$ and every integer $2 \le \ell \le x$ has a prime factor $p \le x$ dividing $k$, so $\{\ell \in [1, x] : \gcd(\ell, k) = 1\} = \{1\}$, which reduces (19) to the single $\ell = 1$ term $\frac{\mu(k)}{k}w\bigl(\frac{\log k}{\log N}\bigr) = \frac{\mu(k)}{k}w\bigl(1 - \frac{\log x}{\log N}\bigr)$, proving (20). Since $w(1) = 0$ and $\varepsilon_x := \frac{\log x}{\log N} \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$, $\frac{w(1 - \varepsilon_x)}{\varepsilon_x} \to -w'(1)$, while $\frac{\varphi(k)}{k}\log x \to e^{-\gamma}$ by Mertens' third theorem, proving (21).

(b) For any $q_1, q_2 \in \mathcal{I}_N$, $(J_{\mathrm{sqf}}^2)_{q_1, q_2} = \mathbf{1}_{\{q_1 \mid q_2\}}\sum_{d \mid (q_2/q_1)}\mu(q_1)\mu(q_1 d) = \mathbf{1}_{\{q_1 \mid q_2\}}\sum_{d \mid (q_2/q_1)}\mu^2(q_1)\mu(d) = \delta_{q_1, q_2}$, so $J_{\mathrm{sqf}}^2 \equiv I_{\mathcal{I}_N}$. Since $J_{\mathrm{sqf}}$ is upper-triangular with diagonal entries $\mu(q)$, $\operatorname{Tr}(J_{\mathrm{sqf}}) = \sum_{q \in \mathcal{I}_N}\mu(q) = M(N) - 1$. By definition of $d_k(\xi)$ for $\xi$ supported on $\mathcal{I}_N$, $\beta_k = \mu(k)\frac{d_k(\xi)}{k} = \mu(k)\sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_N, k \mid m} u_m = (J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{u})_k$, and multiplying by $J_{\mathrm{sqf}}$ gives $\boldsymbol{u} = J_{\mathrm{sqf}}^2\boldsymbol{u} = J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{\beta}$ (which also agrees with $\sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_N, m \mid k}\mu(k/m)\frac{d_k}{k} = \mu(m)\sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_N, m \mid k}\mu(k)\frac{d_k}{k} = (J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{\beta})_m$ since $\mu(k/m) = \mu(m)\mu(k)$ for squarefree $m \mid k$). Substituting $\beta_k = \frac{\mu^2(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)$ into $u_m = \frac{\xi_m}{m} = (J_{\mathrm{sqf}}\boldsymbol{\beta})_m = \mu(m)\sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_N, m \mid k}\frac{\mu^2(k)}{\varphi(k)}W_k(N)$ and writing $k = m\ell$ ($\gcd(\ell, m) = 1$, $\varphi(m\ell) = \varphi(m)\varphi(\ell)$) establishes (22). $\square$

## 5. Orthonormal Mother-Wave Isometry and $O(N)$ Preconditioning of the Dyadic Schur Complement $S_N$

On the dyadic octave $N < k \le 2N$, every proper divisor $m \mid k$ ($2 \le m < k$) satisfies $m \le k/2 \le N$, so $\rho_m \in V_N$ and $Q_N\tau_k = Q_N\bigl(k\rho_k + \sum_{m \mid k, 2 \le m < k} m\mu(k/m)\rho_m\bigr) \equiv k\,Q_N\rho_k$, where $Q_N := I - P_{V_N}$ is the orthogonal projector onto $V_N^\perp$ (`revival910`–`revival911` [9]). Let $S_N := \bigl(\langle Q_N\tau_k, Q_N\tau_l \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)_{N < k, l \le 2N}$ be the exact Schur complement of $V_N$, $J := \bigl(\min(k, l) - N\bigr)_{N < k, l \le 2N}$, $\boldsymbol{\ell} := \bigl(\log(k/N)\bigr)_{N < k \le 2N}$, $L_N := J - N\boldsymbol{\ell}\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} \preceq S_N$ (`revival911` [9]), and $P_N := \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{J_2(k)}{12}\right) + \frac{1}{4(1 + 3 T_{\mathrm{old}})}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\mathsf{T}}$ ($\boldsymbol{\varphi}_k := \varphi(k)$, $J_2(k) := \sum_{d \mid k}\mu(k/d)d^2$, $T_{\mathrm{old}} := \sum_{m=2}^N \frac{\varphi(m)^2}{J_2(m)}$) the Franel–Kluyver periodic sawtooth Schur complement (`riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Proposition 2(a) [8], `revival909` [9]). Specializing the single-jump Gram identity of `riemann_hypothesis_33/v1` Theorem 2 [6] and the harmonic residual norm identity of `riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Proposition 2(d) [8] to the dyadic adjacent differences $\delta_j := j\rho_j - (j-1)\rho_{j-1}$ (`revival911` Line 102 [9]), we first establish the proved algebraic identities in Theorem 4, and then present our finite-dimensional numerical preconditioner benchmarks and $R = 1{,}000{,}000$-cell enclosures:

**Theorem 4 (Orthonormal Mother-Wave Isometry, Unweighted $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge N})$ Decomposition on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$, and $O(N)$ Banded-Woodbury Inversion of $M_N(\alpha_N)$).**
*(a) (**Anchor Cancellation, Mother-Wave Harmonic Dilation & Mellin Cancellation on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$.**) For every integer $j \ge 2$ and all $x > 0$, the $-\lfloor x \rfloor$ anchor in $j\rho_j(x) = j\lfloor x/j \rfloor - \lfloor x \rfloor$ cancels identically in $\delta_j(x) := j\rho_j(x) - (j - 1)\rho_{j-1}(x) \equiv j\lfloor x/j \rfloor - (j - 1)\lfloor x/(j - 1) \rfloor$. Expanding $\lfloor x/j \rfloor = \sum_{q=1}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{[q j, \infty)}(x)$ expresses $\delta_j(x)$ pointwise on $(0, \infty)$ as the finite harmonic sum of the single-threshold mother wave $\delta_j^{(1)}(x) := j\mathbf{1}_{[j, \infty)}(x) - (j - 1)\mathbf{1}_{[j-1, \infty)}(x) = -(j - 1)\mathbf{1}_{[j-1, j)}(x) + \mathbf{1}_{[j, \infty)}(x)$:*
$$\delta_j(x) \;\equiv\; \sum_{q=1}^{\lfloor x/(j-1) \rfloor} \delta_j^{(1)}\!\left(\frac{x}{q}\right) \qquad (\forall\, x > 0), \qquad \int_0^Q \delta_j^{(1)}\!\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)dq \;\equiv\; j\min\!\left(Q,\, \frac{x}{j}\right) - (j - 1)\min\!\left(Q,\, \frac{x}{j - 1}\right). \tag{23}$$
*For any $x \in \mathbb{C}^N$ with cumulative sums $C_j := \sum_{k=j}^{2N} x_k$ ($N < j \le 2N$), $g_x := \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k(k\rho_k - N\rho_N) \equiv \sum_{j=N+1}^{2N} C_j\delta_j \in \mathcal{H}$ has Mellin transform $\mathcal{M}[g_x](s) := \int_1^\infty g_x(t)t^{-s-1}\,dt \equiv -\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k(k^{1-s} - N^{1-s}) = -\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N} C_j\bigl(j^{1-s} - (j-1)^{1-s}\bigr)$ for $\Re(s) > 0$. Whenever $\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} x = \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\log(k/N) = \sum_{j=N+1}^{2N} C_j\log\frac{j}{j-1} = 0$, the Dirichlet polynomial $D_x(s) := \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k(k^{1-s} - N^{1-s})$ has a **double zero at $s = 1$** ($D_x(1) = 0$, $D_x'(1) = -\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} x = 0$), which cancels the double pole of $-\frac{\zeta(s)}{1 - s}$ at $s = 1$.*

*(b) (**Flat-Lebesgue Box-Indicator Isometry, Dyadic Disjointness & First-Octave Gram Identity.**) For all integers $p, q \ge 1$ and $i, j \ge 2$ ($I_{p, i} := [p(i-1),\, pi)$), the map $\mathcal{W}: \delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot / p) \mapsto \frac{1}{p}\mathbf{1}_{I_{p, i}}$ is an **isometry from $\mathcal{H} = L^2((1, \infty), x^{-2}\,dx)$ into flat Lebesgue space $L^2((1, \infty), dx)$** (cf. `riemann_hypothesis_33/v1` Theorem 2 [6]):*
$$\Bigl\langle \delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot / p),\; \delta_j^{(1)}(\cdot / q) \Bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \;\equiv\; \Bigl\langle \frac{1}{p}\mathbf{1}_{I_{p, i}},\; \frac{1}{q}\mathbf{1}_{I_{q, j}} \Bigr\rangle_{L^2((1, \infty),\, dx)} \;=\; \frac{\bigl|I_{p, i} \cap I_{q, j}\bigr|}{p\,q} \;\ge\; 0. \tag{24}$$
*In particular, $\{1\} \cup \bigl\{\delta_j^{(1)}\bigr\}_{j=2}^\infty$ is an **orthonormal basis of the closed integer-cell step subspace of $\mathcal{H}$** ($\langle 1, \delta_j^{(1)} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = 0$, $\langle \delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot/q), \delta_j^{(1)}(\cdot/q) \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \equiv \frac{1}{q}\delta_{i, j}$), and the cross-harmonic Gram block $G^{(p, q)} := \bigl(\langle \delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot / p), \delta_j^{(1)}(\cdot / q) \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)_{N < i, j \le 2N}$ satisfies:*
$$G^{(p, q)} \;\equiv\; 0 \quad \forall\,\frac{p}{q} \notin \left(\frac{1}{2},\, 2\right), \qquad \bigl\|G^{(p, q)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\le\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{p\,q}}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{1/2 < p/q < 2\}}. \tag{25}$$
*On the first dyadic octave $(1, 2N)$ ($N < i, j \le 2N$, $\mathbf{j}_k := k - N$), all harmonics $q \ge 2$ vanish and subtracting $\int_{2N}^\infty x^{-2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2N}$ gives:*
$$\Bigl(\langle \delta_i,\, \delta_j \rangle_{L^2((1, 2N),\, x^{-2}\,dx)}\Bigr)_{N < i, j \le 2N} \;\equiv\; I_N \;-\; \frac{1}{2N}\,\mathbf{1}_N\mathbf{1}_N^{\mathsf{T}} \;\preceq\; I_N, \qquad \left\|\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\bigl(k\rho_k - N\rho_N\bigr)\right\|_{L^2((1, 2N),\, x^{-2}\,dx)}^2 \;\equiv\; x^*\left(J \;-\; \frac{1}{2N}\,\mathbf{j}\mathbf{j}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)x \;\le\; x^* J x. \tag{26}$$

*(c) (**Unweighted $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge N})$ Decomposition on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$ & $O(N)$ Banded-Cholesky + Rank-2 Woodbury Inversion of $M_N(\alpha_N) := L_N + \alpha_N P_N$.**) Whenever $\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} x = 0$, multiplying $\mathcal{M}[g_x](s) = -\frac{\zeta(s)}{s} D_x(s)$ on $\Re(s) = \frac{1}{2}$ by the unimodular factor $\frac{s}{1 - s}$ ($|\frac{s}{1-s}| \equiv 1$ on $\Re(s) = \frac{1}{2}$) maps $g_x \in \mathcal{H}$ isometrically onto the step function $F_x(t) := \sum_{p=1}^\infty \frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N} C_j\mathbf{1}_{I_{p, j}}(t) \equiv \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\bigl(H_{\lfloor t/N \rfloor} - H_{\lfloor t/k \rfloor}\bigr) \in L^2((1, \infty), dt)$, decomposing the unprojected lift norm into the exact unweighted discrete $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge N})$ series (the dyadic-octave specialization of `riemann_hypothesis_43/v4` Proposition 2(d) [8]):*
$$\left\|\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\bigl(k\rho_k - N\rho_N\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;=\; \left\|\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N} C_j\,\delta_j\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;\equiv\; x^* L_N x \;+\; \sum_{n=2N}^\infty \left|\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\bigl(H_{\lfloor n/N \rfloor} - H_{\lfloor n/k \rfloor}\bigr)\right|^2 \qquad (\forall\, \boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} x = 0), \tag{27}$$
*where for $n \ge 2N$ and $\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} x = 0$, $\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k(H_{\lfloor n/N \rfloor} - H_{\lfloor n/k \rfloor}) \equiv \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k W_n(k)$ with $W_n(k) := H_{\lfloor n/N \rfloor} - H_{\lfloor n/k \rfloor} - \log(k/N) = \frac{k(\{n/k\} - 1/2) - N(\{n/N\} - 1/2)}{n} + O(N^2/n^2)$. For any tail weight $\alpha_N > 0$, define the preconditioner*
$$M_N(\alpha_N) \;:=\; L_N \;+\; \alpha_N\,P_N \;=\; \bigl(J + \Delta_N(\alpha_N)\bigr) \;-\; N\,\boldsymbol{\ell}\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} \;+\; \gamma_N(\alpha_N)\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\mathsf{T}}, \qquad \Delta_N(\alpha_N) := \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\alpha_N\frac{J_2(k)}{12}\right), \quad \gamma_N(\alpha_N) := \frac{\alpha_N}{4(1 + 3 T_{\mathrm{old}})}. \tag{28}$$
*Since $D := J^{-1}$ is symmetric positive-definite tridiagonal ($D_{ii} = 2$ for $i < N$, $D_{NN} = 1$, $D_{i, i\pm 1} = -1$, applied in $O(N)$ operations via $(Db)_i = 2b_i - b_{i-1} - b_{i+1}$), the identity $\bigl(J + \Delta_N\bigr)^{-1} b \equiv \Delta_N^{-1}\bigl(D + \Delta_N^{-1}\bigr)^{-1} D b$ reduces solving $M_N(\alpha_N) x = h$ via the rank-2 Woodbury identity on $U := [\boldsymbol{\ell},\, \boldsymbol{\varphi}]$ to a single $O(N)$ banded Cholesky solve (`scipy.linalg.solveh_banded`) of the $2 \times N$ upper-band array of $D + \Delta_N^{-1}$ against the three right-hand sides $[Dh,\, D\boldsymbol{\ell},\, D\boldsymbol{\varphi}]$ followed by a $2 \times 2$ linear system.*

*Proof of Theorem 4.*
(a)–(b) For $x > 0$, $\delta_j^{(1)}(x/q) = j\mathbf{1}_{\{q \le x/j\}} - (j-1)\mathbf{1}_{\{q \le x/(j-1)\}}$, so summing over $q \ge 1$ or integrating over $q \in (0, Q)$ gives (23). On $\Re(s) = \frac{1}{2}$, $|1 - s| \equiv |s|$, so $\mathcal{M}[\delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot / p)](s) = p^{-s}\frac{i^{1-s} - (i-1)^{1-s}}{s}$ in $\mathcal{H}$ has the exact same modulus as $\mathcal{M}[x\cdot \frac{1}{p}\mathbf{1}_{I_{p, i}}(x)](s) = p^{-s}\frac{i^{1-s} - (i-1)^{1-s}}{1 - s}$. By Plancherel's theorem (or direct piece-by-piece integration using $\int_{q(j-1)}^\infty \delta_j^{(1)}(x/q)x^{-2}\,dx = 0$ and $\delta_i^{(1)}(x/p) = 1 - i\mathbf{1}_{I_{p, i}}(x)$ on $[p(i-1), \infty)$), if $p(i-1) \le q(j-1)$ then $\langle \delta_i^{(1)}(\cdot/p), \delta_j^{(1)}(\cdot/q) \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = -i\int_{I_{p, i} \cap [q(j-1), \infty)} \delta_j^{(1)}(x/q)x^{-2}\,dx$, which equals $+i(j-1)\bigl(\frac{1}{q(j-1)} - \frac{1}{pi}\bigr) = \frac{pi - q(j-1)}{pq}$ when $q(j-1) < pi \le qj$ and $+i\int_{pi}^\infty x^{-2}\,dx = \frac{1}{p} = \frac{qj - q(j-1)}{pq}$ when $pi > qj$; both cases equal $\frac{|I_{p, i} \cap I_{q, j}|}{pq}$, proving (24). Since $\bigsqcup_{i=N+1}^{2N} I_{p, i} = [pN, 2pN)$ is disjoint from $[qN, 2qN)$ when $\frac{p}{q} \notin (\frac{1}{2}, 2)$, and row/column sums of $G^{(p, q)}$ are bounded by $\frac{p}{pq} = \frac{1}{q}$ and $\frac{q}{pq} = \frac{1}{p}$, Schur's test gives (25), and subtracting $\int_{2N}^\infty x^{-2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2N}$ on $(1, 2N)$ proves (26).
(c) For any $x \in \boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$, $D_x(1) = \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k(1 - 1) = 0$ and $D_x'(1) = -\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\log(k/N) = -\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}} x = 0$. Hence $\frac{s}{1-s}\mathcal{M}[g_x](s) = -\frac{\zeta(s)}{1 - s} D_x(s)$ is holomorphic across $\Re(s) \ge \frac{1}{2}$ (including $s = 1$) and equals the Mellin transform $\int_1^\infty F_x(t) t^{-s}\,dt$ of the step function $F_x(t) := \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N} x_k\bigl(H_{\lfloor t/N \rfloor} - H_{\lfloor t/k \rfloor}\bigr)$, since for $\Re(s) > 1$ summation by parts gives $\int_1^\infty H_{\lfloor t/k \rfloor} t^{-s}\,dt = \frac{1}{s - 1}\sum_{p=1}^\infty \frac{1}{p}(pk)^{1-s} = \frac{\zeta(s)}{s - 1} k^{1-s}$. Since $|\frac{s}{1-s}| \equiv 1$ on the Plancherel line $\Re(s) = \frac{1}{2}$, Mellin–Plancherel isometry gives $\|g_x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty |\mathcal{M}[g_x](\frac{1}{2}+it)|^2\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty |\frac{1/2+it}{1/2-it}\mathcal{M}[g_x](\frac{1}{2}+it)|^2\,dt = \int_1^\infty |F_x(t)|^2\,dt = \sum_{n=N}^\infty |F_x(n)|^2$. For $N \le n < 2N$, $\lfloor n/N \rfloor = 1$ and $\lfloor n/k \rfloor = \mathbf{1}_{\{k \le n\}}$, so $F_x(n) = \sum_{k=n+1}^{2N} x_k = C_{n+1}$ and $\sum_{n=N}^{2N-1} |F_x(n)|^2 = \sum_{j=N+1}^{2N} |C_j|^2 = x^* J x = x^* L_N x$ on $\boldsymbol{\ell}^\perp$, while the remaining terms $n \ge 2N$ give the tail series in (27). Finally, multiplying $(J + \Delta_N)x = b$ by $D = J^{-1}$ gives $(D + \Delta_N^{-1})(\Delta_N x) = Db$, and applying the Woodbury identity to $(J + \Delta_N) + U\operatorname{diag}(-N, \gamma_N)U^{\mathsf{T}}$ yields the $O(N)$ banded solver. $\square$

**Heuristic Fresnel–Fraunhofer Motivation & Finite-Octave Numerical Benchmarks (`results/fresnel_fraunhofer_schur_equivalence.json`).**
