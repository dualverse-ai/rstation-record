# The cyclotomic host and the ordinary prime ramp

## Introduction

Write $\Lambda(n)$ for the von Mangoldt function and $\gamma$ for Euler's constant. The ordinary prime ramp at a real $x\ge1$ is

$$
G_z(x)=\sum_{1\le n\le x}\Lambda(n)(x/n-1)-x\log x+(1+\gamma)x.
$$

For a primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet character $\chi$, write $\ell_\chi=L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)$ and

$$
G_\chi(x)=\sum_{1\le n\le x}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)(x/n-1)+x\ell_\chi .
$$

Paper I proves that a one-sided bound for $G_z$ on arbitrarily late power intervals excludes zeta zeros to the right of the corresponding exponent. Here the ordinary character is the primitive character of conductor one. [1]

**Theorem (ordinary prime comparison).** Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/52$ and $A\ge1$. For every sufficiently large real $Y$, a single pair $q,d$ in the progressions of §2 has $v_2h(-4qd)=v_2h(-8qd)=3$ and satisfies (1) throughout $[Y,Y^A]$, with the zero-free rectangles of §2. At each cutoff $x$ in that interval choose the host with $n=2^{\lceil(2+8\varepsilon)\log_2 x\rceil}$ and the sets of §4, using $J=A$. For any heights and weights satisfying (3) and the rank ranges of §4 on that host, the finite prime excess $E(u)$ of §5 has uniform translate mean $\overline E$ satisfying

$$
\left|\frac{\overline E}{Q}-G_z(x)\right|
\le 3x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2}+O(1+\log x)+\frac{2}{x\delta_2},
\qquad Q=\frac{n_Tmb\,\delta_2}{2}.
$$

Here $n_T$ is the top count, $m$ is the number of weights at each top and translate, $b$ is the reference weight, and $\delta_2$ is the density of the thick set in the cyclotomic group. The more precise signed comparison is (6)–(7). If the same data also satisfy the upper bound (9) for $\overline E$, then for fixed $e_D,e_L\ge0$ and sufficiently large $x$,

$$
G_z(x)\le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}.
$$

The scalar implication is pointwise. The cohomological length hypotheses in §3 and the simultaneous availability of the fitted data and geometric bound at the requisite cutoffs are additional inputs to the proposed application.

## 1. The field and its character group

**Proposition 1 (proved here).** Let $e\ge2$ be an integer. Let $q,d$ be primes with $q\equiv3\bmod8$, $d\equiv7\bmod16$, $q\mid d+1$, and $q\ne3$. Put $B=2^{e+2}$, $J_r(a)=(a/r)$ for $r=q,d$, and let

$$
H=(\mathbb Z/B\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\},\qquad
E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_B)^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}),\qquad
K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d}),\qquad F=E^+ .
$$

Then $H$ is cyclic of order $n=2^e$, $E$ is CM with $[E:\mathbb Q]=4n$, and $E$ has conductor $Bqd$. In the source's arithmetic frame the dyadic level is $QS=B$ and the level index $o$ is one. [3]

**Proof.** The three moduli $B,q,d$ are coprime. Classical cyclotomic Galois theory, the quadratic Gauss sum, and the Chinese remainder theorem give a surjection

$$
(\mathbb Z/Bqd\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow (\mathbb Z/2)^2\times H,
\qquad
a\longmapsto \bigl(\epsilon_q(a),\epsilon_d(a),[a]\bigr),
\quad (-1)^{\epsilon_r(a)}=J_r(a).
$$

Its fixed field is $E$. Equivalently its kernel consists of the classes with $a\equiv\pm1\bmod B$ and $J_q(a)=J_d(a)=1$. This is exactly the fixed-field condition `IsChiField` for the character of conductor one. An ambient cyclotomic field of any larger admissible modulus gives the same fixed field by reduction of units. The group $H$ is generated by the class of $5$: the identity $v_2(5^{2^j}-1)=j+2$ follows by repeated squaring, and the $2^e$ distinct powers of $5$ give all units congruent to $1\bmod4$. A faithful character of $H$ detects $1+B/2$ and hence has conductor $B$. The characters $J_q,J_d$ have conductors $q,d$. Their least common multiple with $B$ is the conductor of $E$. In the release $B\mid QS\mid\operatorname{lcm}(B,1)$, which also gives $QS=B$. Complex conjugation negates both square roots and fixes the real cyclotomic field. Its fixed field $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_B)^+(\sqrt{qd})$ is totally real, and $E/F$ is totally imaginary quadratic. [3]

For the averaging argument write the two bits in the order $(\epsilon_d,\epsilon_q)$. The source's Artin map stores the $q$-sign first and the $d$-sign second, and corrects its last coordinate by the class of the $q$-sign. This correction is trivial in $H$ at conductor one. Thus in our coordinates the group is $G=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\times H$, complex conjugation is $c=((1,1),0)$, and $\Phi=\{((0,b),h):b\in\mathbb Z/2,\ h\in H\}$ is the group over $K$. It indexes the CM embeddings after a choice sending $\sqrt{-d}$ to $i\sqrt d$. Its cardinality is $f=2n$. In the release $H_0\subset H$, the subgroup used for translates, has index $o$. Since the powers of the conductor-one character form a singleton, $o=1$, the kept set is $\{\mathbf1\}$, and $H_0=H$. Every translate has full top share, because $t-u\in H_0$ for all $t,u\in H$ and the prescribed share is $4/(3o+1)=1$.

**Ramification and splitting (proved here).** For $p\nmid Bqd$, the three components of Frobenius are the two Legendre signs and $[p]$. The inertia at $d$ is the order-two subgroup generated by $((1,0),0)$, since the other two factors are unramified there. Both inertia and a lift of Frobenius at $2$ and $q$ belong to $\Phi$: $K$ is unramified and split there by $(-d/2)=1$ and $(-d/q)=1$, the latter from $q\mid d+1$. For every odd prime $p\mid d+1$ the same congruence gives $(-d/p)=1$. For $r=q,d$, quadratic reciprocity identifies $J_r$ with the primitive character of discriminant $-r$, including the value at $2$ by the supplementary law. The $q$-row therefore has the eigenvector $\sqrt{-q}$ with the specified sign, $QS\le n^2$ for $e\ge2$, and the condition on odd primes dividing $QS$ is vacuous. These are the ordinary specialization of the field and splitting rows.

If $\vartheta$ is a character of $G$, its primitive Dirichlet character is the product of the primitive representative of its $H$-factor and the indicated powers of $J_q,J_d$. Indeed the product agrees on the units by the Artin map, and primitive characters inducing the same common-level character agree. Equivalently at $p^k$ its value is the average of $\vartheta$ over $k\operatorname{Frob}_p+I_p$, where $I_p$ is inertia and $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ is a lift. If the restriction to $I_p$ is trivial, this is its unramified value. Otherwise the average vanishes by orthogonality and the primitive local Euler factor is absent. In particular the principal representative takes value $1$ also at $2,q,d$. These primitive values give the same $L$-functions and centers at $1$ as the corresponding host characters.

## 2. The auxiliary quadratic primes

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/52$ and a real $A\ge1$. At a real scale $Y>1$ put $z=Y^{\varepsilon/5}$, $z'=z(\log Y)^2$, $U_1=e^z$, $U_2=e^{z'}$. Choose $q$ in $(\sqrt{U_1},U_1]$ with $q\equiv3\bmod8$. For each such $q$ take $d$ in $(\sqrt{U_2},U_2]$ in the reduced class $a\bmod M$ specified by

$$
M=16q\prod_{3\le p\le z,\ p\ {\rm prime}}p,\qquad
a\equiv7\bmod16,\qquad a\equiv-1\bmod M/16.
$$

Eventually $q>z$, $d>q$, and $\log M\ll z$ uniformly by the elementary Chebyshev bound for $\sum_{p\le z}\log p$. The boxes use heights $Q_0=8U_1$ and $Q_1=8U_1U_2$. For every sufficiently large $Y$ the released selection gives the following inequalities for one pair, uniformly at every real $y\in[Y,Y^A]$:

$$
|G_\chi(y)|\le y^{1/2+\varepsilon/2},
\qquad |\ell_\chi|\le4\log y
\quad(\chi=J_q,J_d,J_qJ_d).
\tag{1}
$$

It also gives $L(s,\rho J_q^iJ_d^j)\ne0$ for $\Re s>399/400,\ |\Im s|\le Q_j$, for every primitive real $\rho$ of conductor dividing $8$ and every $(i,j)\in\{0,1\}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$. All these products are primitive at their product conductors. The extra real bases are needed for estimates involving the full host dual. [3]

### Progression counts and the exceptional kernel

**Published inputs.** For a modulus $D\ge8$ divisible by $8$, apply Thorner and Zaman's relative prime-counting theorem to $\mathbb Q(\zeta_D)/\mathbb Q$, with the full Galois group as its abelian subgroup. The fixed field is $\mathbb Q$, and the largest character conductor is at most $D$. A reduced class $b$ is a singleton Frobenius class. For $\log U\gg\log D$, the theorem gives

$$
\pi(U;D,b)=\frac{\operatorname{Li}(U)-\chi_D(b)\operatorname{Li}(U^{\beta_D})}{\varphi(D)}
 (1+O(\mathcal R_D(U))),\qquad
 \mathcal R_D(U)=e^{-c\log U/\log D}+e^{-c\sqrt{\log U}} .
$$

Here $\operatorname{Li}(U)=\int_2^U dt/\log t$, and the term with the real nonprincipal character $\chi_D$ and its real zero $\beta_D$ is omitted if the theorem specifies no such pair. The character at level $D$ is induced by a primitive real character. The release proves a relative progression estimate of the same form under $C\log D\log(\log D+2)\le\log U$. The argument below fits this latter range as well. We also use Page's theorem in its conductor-uniform form: among primitive real characters of conductor $\le V$, at most one can have a zero in $(1-c_P/\log V,1)$ for an absolute $c_P>0$. Finally Siegel's theorem gives $1-\beta\gg f^{-1}$ for a primitive real character of conductor $f$, with an ineffective constant. At a larger modulus the additional Euler factors have no zeros at a positive real $\beta$, so both zero estimates apply to the inducing character. [5] [6]

Write $L=\log Y$, $Z_*=(Y^A)^{400}$, $N_*=\lfloor Z_*\rfloor$ and $V=m_0Z_*^2$. In stage one let $(m_0,a_0,U,\varepsilon_*)=(8,3,U_1,\varepsilon)$. In stage two, with $q$ fixed in its candidate set, use $(M,a,U_2,6\varepsilon/5)$. In each case $m_0>0$, $8\mid m_0$ and $a_0$ is reduced. The stage parameters are $0<\varepsilon_*\le1/4$, $K=A\ge1$, $\tau=\varepsilon/2>0$ and any fixed discard share $0<\nu<1$. The height constraints are

$$
\log U\ge \tfrac14L^2\log m_0,\qquad
 Y^{\varepsilon_*/6}\le \log U\le Y^{\varepsilon_*/5}L^2 .
$$

For the fixed modulus $8$, these constraints follow from $\log U_1=z$ and $z/L^3\to\infty$. For the growing modulus $M$, $\log M\le\log16+\log q+z\log4\le4z$ eventually. Hence $\tfrac14L^2\log M\le zL^2=\log U_2$. Also $Y^{\varepsilon_*/6}=z$ and $Y^{\varepsilon_*/5}=zY^{\varepsilon/25}$ at the second stage, giving the other two bounds. In addition, the statement of `stage` requires every prime divisor $p$ of $m_0$ to satisfy $p\le w=Y^{\varepsilon_*/5}$ or $p>Z_*$. The only prime divisor at stage one is $2$, and at stage two $2$ and the primes in the product are at most $z\le w$. The remaining prime $q$ satisfies $\log q>z/2>400AL=\log Z_*$ eventually. Thus the divisor primes that can support a term of the short sum are at most $w$, as used in the second moment below. [3]

At stage one $D=8s\le8Y^{800A}=8(\log U_1)^{4000A/\varepsilon}$, so ordinary Siegel–Walfisz is available at this stage. Its log-power saving can be chosen to absorb both the $\varphi(s)$ classes in the fiber and the desired factor $Y^{-(A+1)}$. At stage two even the base modulus exceeds every fixed power of $\log U_2$, since $\log M\ge\log q>z/2$ and $\log\log U_2=(\varepsilon/5)L+2\log L$. We use the relative estimate above at the full modulus $D=m_0s$ in both stages, as in the release's `twistK`. Indeed $\log D\le\log V=\log m_0+800AL$. Eventually $\log V\le z/L^2$ at stage one and $\log V\le5z$ at stage two, so $\log U/\log V\ge L^2/5$ in either case. Since $\log\log U=O(L)$ and $\log U\ge z\gg L^4$, this gives $C\log D\log(\log D+2)\le\log U$ for any fixed $C$ and $\mathcal R_D(U)=O(e^{-c'L^2})$. The same bounds give $\sqrt U>N_*^2$ eventually, so every $r$ in $P=\{r:\sqrt U<r\le U,\ r\equiv a_0\bmod m_0\}$ exceeds $N_*^2$. These estimates are uniform in $q$ with $\varepsilon,A$ fixed. [3] [5] [12]

**Lower bound for the candidate sets (proved here).** At $D=m_0$, if the exceptional term is subtracted, then

$$
\operatorname{Li}(U)-\operatorname{Li}(U^\beta)
 \ge \frac{U(1-U^{\beta-1})}{\log U}
 \gg \frac{U}{\log U}\min(1,(1-\beta)\log U).
$$

The first inequality follows by integrating on $[U^\beta,U]$, where the possible exceptional $\beta$ is near $1$ and $U^\beta\ge2$ eventually. With $f\le m_0$, Siegel's bound gives $\min(1,(1-\beta)\log U)\gg m_0^{-1}$. Dividing by $\varphi(m_0)\le m_0$ yields a lower bound $\gg U/(m_0^2\log U)$ for the main term in all three cases. The relative error is $o(1)$ even in the subtractive case. Because $\log m_0/\log U=O(L^{-2})$, subtracting at most $\sqrt U$ small primes leaves $|P|\ge U^{9/10}$ eventually. The estimate holds uniformly in the allowed first primes.

**Progression correlation (proved here).** For odd squarefree $1<s\le N_*^2$ coprime to $m_0$, let $\xi_s(b)=(b/s)$ on $(\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z)^\times$. Its conductor is exactly $s$, since its local character at each prime dividing $s$ is nontrivial. On the reduced classes modulo $D=m_0s$ lying over $a_0$, sum the progression theorem weighted by $\xi_s(b)$. The ordinary main term sums to zero. The Chinese remainder theorem writes $\chi_D=\chi_{m_0}\chi_s$, with the first factor constant on this fiber. The exceptional main term sums to zero unless $\chi_s=\xi_s$, by character orthogonality on the second factor. In the remaining case the base conductor of $\chi_D$ has prime-to-$m_0$ part exactly $s$. Thus at most one $s=s_*$ can have both this matching condition and $1-\beta_D<c_P/\log V$, by Page's theorem for the inducing characters of conductor $\le V$. The Page threshold is common to all $s$, and the exceptional kernel is independent of the short-sum coefficients and cutoff $y$.

For each $s\ne s_*$, the exceptional term either cancels or has $\operatorname{Li}(U^{\beta_D})/\operatorname{Li}(U)\ll (\log U)e^{-c_P\log U/\log V}$, by $\operatorname{Li}(U)\gg U/\log U$ and $\operatorname{Li}(U^{\beta_D})\ll U^{\beta_D}$. This ratio and $\mathcal R_D(U)$ are both $O(e^{-c''L^2})$ after reducing $c''>0$. To control the error relative to the actual number of primes, sum the *nonnegative* main terms over the fiber. When the exceptional term matches $\xi_s$, its unweighted sum is zero, so the total main term is $\operatorname{Li}(U)/\varphi(m_0)$. If the exceptional term does not match, the weighted main sum is already zero and the sum of the absolute errors is at most $O(\mathcal R_D(U))$ times the total main term, even when the latter is small. In both cases the total main term is within a factor $1+O(\mathcal R_D(U))$ of the unweighted prime count in that fiber up to $U$. The count can omit primes dividing $s$, all of which are below $\sqrt U$. Removing the other primes through $\sqrt U$ changes either sum by at most $\sqrt U$, with $\sqrt U/|P|\le U^{-2/5}$. Thus for all sufficiently large $Y$, for every such $s\ne s_*$,

$$
\left|\sum_{r\in P}(r/s)\right|\le |P|\,Y^{-(A+1)} .
 \tag{1a}
$$

This is the bound needed from `twistK`. [3]

### The second moment

**Proposition (proved here).** There is an absolute $C$ such that, at either stage for every sufficiently large $Y$, every real $Y\le y\le Y^A$, and every complex sequence $|\kappa(n)|\le1$, one has

$$
\sum_{r\in P}|S_r(y)|^2\le C y\log y\,|P|,\qquad
 S_r(y)=\sum_{1\le n\le N_*}c_y(n)\kappa(n)J_r(n),\qquad
 c_y(n)=\Lambda(n)\min(1,y/n).
 \tag{1b}
$$

The onset can depend on $\varepsilon,A$ and is uniform in the allowed $q$ at the second stage. This is the ordinary specialization of the released moment, including its uniformity over arbitrary bounded coefficients.

For $n,n'$ in the sum, let $s(n,n')$ be the product of the primes outside $m_0$ occurring to odd exponent in $nn'$. Since $r>N_*^2$, no prime dividing $nn'$ equals $r$. Quadratic reciprocity and the supplement at $2$ give

$$
J_r(n)J_r(n')=\omega(n,n')\,(r/s(n,n')),
$$

The sign $\omega(n,n')$ is constant on $P$, since the residues of $r$ modulo $8$ and modulo each $p\mid m_0$ are fixed. Each $s(n,n')$ is odd, squarefree, coprime to $m_0$, and at most $N_*^2$. On expanding the square and applying $|\kappa|\le1$, the upper bound divided by $|P|$ is

$$
\sum_{s(n,n')=1}c_y(n)c_y(n')
 +\sum_{s(n,n')=s_*}c_y(n)c_y(n')
 +Y^{-(A+1)}\left(\sum_n c_y(n)\right)^2 .
$$

The first two terms use the trivial bound $|P|$ for the correlation, with the second term zero when Page's exception is absent. The final term uses (1a). By the Chebyshev estimate $\sum_{n\le t}\Lambda(n)\ll t$ and partial summation, $\sum_{n\le N_*}c_y(n)\ll y(1+\log(Z_*/y))$. Thus the last term is $O_A(y(\log Y)^2/Y)$, which is $\le y\log y$ times an absolute constant eventually. [3]

Only prime powers have positive mass. Put

$$
W_p=\sum_{p^j\le N_*,\,j\ge1} c_y(p^j),\qquad
 R=\sum_{\substack{p^j\le N_*,\,j\ge1\\p\mid m_0\ \mathrm{or}\ 2\mid j}}c_y(p^j).
$$

For $p\le y$, the powers $p^j\le y$ cost at most $\log y$, and the remaining geometric series at most $2\log p$. For $p>y$, $W_p\le 2y\log p/p$. So $\sup_p W_p\ll\log y$. The same-prime contribution is bounded by $\sum_p W_p^2\ll y\log y$. Indeed for $p\le\sqrt y$ use the bound $O(\sqrt y\log^2 y)$, for $\sqrt y<p\le y$ use $\log^2 y\le2\log y\log p$ and Chebyshev, and for $2^j y<p\le2^{j+1}y$, $j\ge0$, use

$$
\sum_{2^jy<p\le2^{j+1}y}\frac{\log^2p}{p^2}
 \ll \frac{\log(2^{j+1}y)}{2^j y}.
$$

The latter estimate follows by taking one factor $\log p/p^2$ at its upper bound and using Chebyshev on the remaining $\log p$. Its sum gives the desired contribution for $p>y$.

The mass of all even powers is at most $\sum_{h\ge1}\Lambda(h)\min(1,y/h^2)\ll\sqrt y$, since $\Lambda(h^2)=\Lambda(h)$ and Chebyshev applies first to $h\le\sqrt y$ and then on the dyadic intervals above it. The powers on primes dividing $m_0$ cost $O(w\log y)$ by the individual prime bound and the bound on primes that divide $m_0$ and occur in the sum. Thus $R\ll\sqrt y+w\log y\ll\sqrt y$ because $w\le y^{1/5}$.

To classify the pairs, each prime power contributes a kernel label. It is $1$ for the mass in $R$, or its prime base $p$ for an odd power with $p\nmid m_0$. The pair kernel multiplies labels modulo squares. It equals $1$ only for two label-$1$ terms or two terms with the same prime label, giving a total weight at most $R^2+\sum_p W_p^2$. If $s_*$ is prime, its only pairs join that label to label $1$ and have weight at most $2W_{s_*}R$. If $s_*$ is a product of two distinct primes, its pairs join those two labels and have weight at most $2(\sup_p W_p)^2$. No other $s_*$ occurs among the pairs. In particular the exceptional weight is always $O(\log^2 y+\sqrt y\log y)$. Combining these estimates proves (1b). [3]

### The maximal estimate and the two discards

For $[T,W]\subset[Y,Y^A]$ with $W\le2T$, the finite ramp $S_r(y)$ is continuous and absolutely continuous on the block, with right derivative

$$
S'_r(v)=v^{-1}\sum_{n\le N_*}c_v(n)\kappa(n)\mathbf1_{n>v}J_r(n).
$$

The bounded coefficients in (1b) may depend on $v$. Thus almost everywhere $\sum_{r\in P}|S'_r(v)|^2\le C(\log v)|P|/v$. Absolute continuity and Cauchy–Schwarz bound each square increment from $T$, uniformly at every point in the block, by $(W-T)\int_T^W|S'_r(v)|^2\,dv$. Hence

$$
\sum_{r\in P}\max_{T\le y\le W}|S_r(y)|^2
 \le 2\sum_{r\in P}|S_r(T)|^2+
        2(W-T)\int_T^W\sum_{r\in P}|S'_r(v)|^2\,dv
 \le 4CT\log(2T)|P|.
 \tag{1c}
$$

Put $\tau=\varepsilon/2$. The fraction violating $|S_r(y)|\le y^{1/2+\tau}/2$ anywhere on one block is $O(T^{-2\tau}\log(2T))$. Summing over the blocks beginning at $T=2^jY$ and stopped at $Y^A$ gives $O_\varepsilon(Y^{-\tau})$, using $\log(2T)\ll_\tau T^\tau$ and the geometric series. This is the source's finite-block argument and works at both endpoints of the full interval.

The independent discard uses **published zero density**. Thorner and Zaman's explicit Bombieri estimate, Theorem 1.2, bounds the number of zeros with multiplicity among primitive characters of conductor $\le Q$, with $\Re s\ge\sigma$ and $|\Im s|\le Q$, by $1088(10^{421}Q^{99})^{1-\sigma}$ for $Q\ge3$ and $\sigma\ge39/40$. At $\sigma=399/400$ this is $O(Q^{99/400})$, hence $O(Q^{1/4})$, including a possible real exceptional zero. Each character failing the box contributes at least one zero. The release obtains the same discard bound by zero detection with a mollifier. [6]

At stage one the coefficient families are the primitive representatives $\rho$ of the characters modulo $8$. At stage two they are the primitive products $\rho J_q^i$ with $i=0,1$ for the already selected $q$. Their conductors $f_0$ are respectively $\le F_1=8$ and $\le F_2=8U_1$. There are at most four and eight coefficients, respectively. In the fresh variable $r$, the product with $J_r$ is primitive of conductor $f_0r$ by coprimality and the Chinese remainder theorem. With $Q=FU$, $F=F_i$, one has $F\le\sqrt U$ eventually. Choose one bad character of conductor $f_0r$ for each $r$ that fails the box for any coefficient. A conductor cannot witness two different primes $r>\sqrt U$, since both would then divide the conductor and each cofactor would exceed $F$. Hence the density discard has relative size $O((FU)^{1/4}/U^{9/10})=O(U^{-21/40})$. By (1c) and the finite union over coefficients, the total fraction removed in either stage tends to zero.

### Centering at $1$ and selecting both primes

Put $Z=Z_*=Y^{400A}$ and $X_*=Y^A$. For a primitive product $\chi(n)=\kappa(n)J_r(n)$ surviving the box discard, write $\mathcal T_\chi(Z)=-\ell_\chi-\sum_{n\le Z}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)/n$. We use the finite Perron argument of Paper I, Proposition 3. At both stage heights $Y^{\varepsilon_*/6}\le\log Q\le Y^{\varepsilon_*/4}$ eventually. Set $z_0=\lfloor Z\rfloor+1/2$, $T=z_0^3$, $b_0=1/\log z_0$, and $F(w)=-(L'/L)(1+w,\chi)$. Truncated Perron inversion of $F(w)z_0^w/w$ on $\operatorname{Re}w=b_0$, through height $T$, gives the sum up to $Z$ with error $O((\log z_0)^2/T)$. Outside $[z_0/2,2z_0]$ this follows from $\sum_{n\ge2}(\log n)n^{-1-b_0}\ll b_0^{-2}$. Inside that interval use $|\log(z_0/n)|\gg |z_0-n|/z_0$ and $\sum_{z_0/2\le n\le2z_0}|z_0-n|^{-1}\ll\log z_0$. [1] [9]

Move the line to $\operatorname{Re}w=-1/400+\delta_0$, with $\delta_0=\varepsilon_* /(1600A)$. For large $Y$, $T+2<Q$. In the classical local partial-fraction formula for $F$, the $O(\log Q)$ zeros with ordinates within one of $\operatorname{Im}w$ are at horizontal distance at least $\delta_0$ from the whole rectangle. Thus $F=O_{\delta_0}(\log Q)$ there. The residue at $w=0$ is $-\ell_\chi$. With $\beta_0=1/400-\delta_0$, the vertical integral is $O_{\delta_0}(z_0^{-\beta_0}\log Q\log T)$ and the horizontal integrals are $O_{\delta_0}((\log Q)/T)$. Multiplying by $X_*$, the first of these errors is $O_{\varepsilon,A}(Y^{\varepsilon_*/2}\log Y)$ because $X_* z_0^{-\beta_0}\ll Y^{\varepsilon_*/4}$. The other errors times $X_*$ tend to zero. Therefore

$$
X_*|\mathcal T_\chi(Z)|\le Y^{\varepsilon_*}
\tag{1d}
$$

eventually, uniformly over the surviving products at either stage. Splitting the finite sum at $y$ gives

$$
G_\chi(y)=-S_r(y)-y\,\mathcal T_\chi(Z)
\qquad (Y\le y\le X_*).
$$

Since $\varepsilon_*\le 6\varepsilon/5<1/2$, the tail is at most $y^{1/2+\tau}/2$ for large $Y$. Together with the maximal estimate this gives the ramp bound on the whole real interval. At $y=Y$ the definition of $G_\chi$ gives

$$
|\ell_\chi|\le Y^{-1/2+\tau}+\sum_{n\le Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}n
\le 4\log Y
$$

eventually by partial summation and the Chebyshev bound $\sum_{n\le u}\Lambda(n)\le3u$. This upper bound is also at most $4\log y$ throughout the interval.

We have proved the two relative share assertions used below. For every fixed $0<\nu<1$, every sufficiently large real $Y$ has a first-stage subset of share at least $1-\nu$. For each $q$ in that subset, its own second-stage good set has share at least $1-\nu$. The thresholds are uniform in $q$. The selection control comes from separate box and maximal discards at each stage. Choosing $q$ first and then $d$ gives (1), using the trivial $\rho$, and gives all the real-base boxes using the full primitive family from modulus $8$. These are the conductor-one contents of `GoodQ`, `GoodD` and `PairGood`. [3]

### Selecting a pair with prescribed quadratic class numbers

**Proposition 2 (proved here using classical and published inputs).** For every $\varepsilon,A$ as above, for every sufficiently large $Y$ the pair in (1) can be chosen with $v_2h(-4qd)=v_2h(-8qd)=3$, where $h(D)$ is the class number of the imaginary quadratic field of fundamental discriminant $D$. This refinement uses Stevenhagen's Rédei reciprocity and the Thorner–Zaman effective Chebotarev theorem. It strengthens the released pair choice for this specialization. [7] [5]

**Proof.** Write $q=u^2+2v^2$ with $u,v>0$. The norm-Euclidean ring $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-2}]$ gives this representation because $(-2/q)=1$. For a root $w^2=q\bmod d$, define $\sigma(q,d)=(\frac{q+uw}{d})$. The root exists because $(d/q)=(-1/q)=-1$ and quadratic reciprocity gives $(q/d)=1$. The two possible arguments have product $2qv^2\bmod d$, a nonzero square since $d>q$ and $(2/d)=1$. The sign is independent of the root.

Use the three ramified primes $2,q,d$ as columns of the Rédei matrices, and order the rows by the discriminants $-4,-q,-d$ or $-8,-q,-d$. Encoding $+1$ as $0$, the two matrices are

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&1&1\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\1&0&1\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For example, $(-q/2)=-1,\ (-d/2)=1,\ (-q/d)=(d/q)=-1,\ (-d/q)=1$. The other off-diagonal entries follow from the signs of $-4,-8$ at $q,d$, and each column sums to zero. Genus theory gives $2$-rank two and $4$-rank one, so the $2$-primary class group has the form $C_2\times C_{2^a}$, $a\ge2$. In both fields the product of the ramified ideals over $2,d$ is the nonzero element of $\mathrm{Cl}[2]\cap2\mathrm{Cl}$. Its vector $(1,0,1)$ belongs to each kernel and differs from the principal relations $(0,1,1)$ and $(1,1,1)$ from the quadratic radicals. The genus character corresponding to $-d$ spans the genus characters admitting a quartic lift. The one-entry $8$-rank matrices therefore have entries $[-d,q,2d]$ and $[-d,2q,2d]$ in additive Rédei notation. [7]

Rédei reciprocity and linearity identify both entries with $[q,2q,-d]$. In detail, the second-kind identities give $[-d,2,2d]=0$ and $[-d,q,qd]=0$, so

$$
[-d,2q,2d]=[-d,q,2d]=[-d,q,2q]=[q,2q,-d].
$$

Here the required Hilbert pairings are $(-d,q),(-d,2),(q,2d),(q,2q)$. At the odd primes their values are $+1$ by $(-d/q)=(q/d)=(2/d)=1$, $(2d/q)=(2/q)(d/q)=1$, and $(q,2q)=(q,-2)$ with $(-2/q)=1$. The real symbols are trivial, so the product formula gives the dyadic values. Also $(-d,d)=1$ and $(2,2d)=(2,-d)=1$. These identities verify the pairings for both applications of linearity. The three discriminants in each triple have common gcd one. The second-kind decompositions $-8d=(-d)\cdot 8$ and $-4qd=(-d)\cdot4q$ give the two vanishing symbols by Proposition 7.10. For the two class-group entries, the associated cyclic quartic extension of the quadratic base is unramified. The Artin action of the ideal of norm $2d$ in Definition 4.4 therefore gives the symmetric symbol of Definition 7.8 with second squareclass $q$ or $2q$. [7]

Let $k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, $D=k(\sqrt q)$, and $L=D(\sqrt{q+u\sqrt q})$. The norm $2qv^2$ of $q+u\sqrt q$ shows that neither that element nor its quotient by $2$ is square in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt q)$, hence $[L:D]=2$. Over $k$, the map $\sqrt q\mapsto-\sqrt q$, $\sqrt{q+u\sqrt q}\mapsto v\sqrt{2q}/\sqrt{q+u\sqrt q}$ has square equal to the nontrivial automorphism $\iota$ over $D$. The involution negating $\sqrt2$ while fixing $\sqrt q$ and the chosen square root conjugates that order-four map to its inverse. This gives the dihedral extension over $\mathbb Q$ for the pair $(q,2q)$. Away from $2q$ the conjugate radicands have disjoint prime supports and their product has even valuation, giving the required unramified condition. The discriminants $4q,8q$ are respectively $4,0\bmod 8$, so Definition 7.6 imposes no further condition at $2$ for this pair. The field is totally real since both radicands are positive. Thus the infinite-place factor in the Rédei symbol for the negative third argument $-d$ is trivial. The prime $d$ splits in $D$, and its relative Artin symbol in $L/D$ is nontrivial precisely when $\sigma(q,d)=-1$. An entry $1$ gives $8$-rank zero, hence $a=2$ in each of the two $2$-class groups. [7]

To count this sign let $C=\mathbb Q(\zeta_M)$, with $M$ from the second stage. It contains $D$, and $C\cap L=D$ because $D$ is the maximal abelian subfield of $L$. The automorphism $\sigma_a$ of $C$ fixes $D$, so its two lifts $g_+=(1,\sigma_a)$ and $g_-=(\iota,\sigma_a)$ to $LC$ are central. The group $H'=\operatorname{Gal}(LC/k)$ is abelian. In $H'$ their quotient is a square. Indeed choose the order-two automorphism $b$ of $C$ acting by $-1$ on the $q$-factor and trivially on the complementary factor. It fixes $i,\sqrt2$ and negates $\sqrt{-q}$, hence negates $\sqrt q$. Pair $b$ with a generator $r$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)$, which has the same restriction to $D$. Then $(r,b)^2=(\iota,1)$. In particular every real character of $H'$ takes equal values on $g_+$ and $g_-$. [5]

The finite Hecke conductors for $LC/k$ divide $M\mathcal O_k$. For $L/k$, odd ramification is tame at $q$ and absent elsewhere. At the dyadic prime $\mathfrak p$ of $k$, the $2$-adic logarithm identifies the fourth powers of $1+\mathfrak p^3$ with $1+\mathfrak p^7$, since $v_{\mathfrak p}(4)=4$. A quartic character is trivial there and $16\mathcal O_k=\mathfrak p^8$. For $C/k$, the characters extend to Dirichlet characters at modulus $M$ and pull back by the norm. A character on $H'$ extends to the product of the two abelian groups over $k$, giving the same conductor bound. Thus the maximum conductor norm is at most $M^2$, and $k$ has discriminant $8$ and degree $2$. [5]

Apply effective Chebotarev with this common abelian subgroup and the two singleton classes, at $U_2$. The size threshold holds since $\log U_2=z(\log Y)^2$ and $\log M=O(z)$. The main terms, including the possible exceptional term, agree by the square calculation. Each has relative error $O(\exp(-c(\log Y)^2))$ with $c>0$ independent of $q$. All ramified primes divide $M$, so the two counts add to the progression count. Removing primes through $\sqrt{U_2}$ changes either count by at most $\sqrt{U_2}$, which is $o(|P|)$ for the second-stage candidate set $P$. Thus each sign has relative share $1/2+o(1)$ uniformly in the first prime. Choose $\nu=1/8$, take any first-stage good prime and intersect its second-stage good set with the negative sign. The share is at least $3/8-o(1)>0$, proving the assertion. [5]

## 3. From prime selection to real cutoffs

For a prime $\ell$ let $\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E,j)$ be the kernel of localization at all places over $\ell$ in degree one with coefficients $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(-j)$ for the Galois group unramified outside those places. The host construction asks for finiteness of each length for $1\le j<t$ and the bound, for every finite prime set $S$,

$$
\sum_{\ell\in S}\log\ell\sum_{1\le j<t}\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E,j)\le B_* .
\tag{2}
$$

For odd $\ell$ the two eigenspaces for complex conjugation on the twisted coefficients have signs $(-1)^j$ and $(-1)^{j+1}$. Write $N_E=Bqd$ for the conductor. The released cohomological assembly uses three estimates at $t(v)=\lfloor v^{401}\rfloor$: [3]

- The unweighted sum of the dyadic lengths is at most $\exp(v^{4\varepsilon/5})$.
- The unweighted sums in the reference towers over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)$ have the same bound. Given these reference bounds, the required odd plus estimate, for the sign $(-1)^j$, is $(N_Et(v))^C+C\exp(v^{4\varepsilon/5})$ for the weighted sum. It is pointwise for $q,d>2t(v)+1$ and primes beyond the fixed level and reference exclusions. This estimate, including finiteness, is an input here. Its released derivation uses deformation-ring and cotangent comparisons and the realization and counts of automorphic systems at controlled levels. [3]
- The other odd weighted sum uses a pointwise estimate bounded by $C'\exp(8v^{\varepsilon/4})$ in the range $\log(N_Et(v))\le 2v^{\varepsilon/4}$. The conductor-one alternative is the bound $[E:\mathbb Q]t(v)^2\log(t(v)N_E)$ by Poitou–Tate duality and equivariant Coates–Sinnott. The assembly below only requires either bound. [3] [10]

**Late windows from the released window input.** Put $\alpha=2+8\varepsilon$ and take $e(v)=\lceil\alpha\log_2 v\rceil$, so $v^\alpha\le2^{e(v)}\le2v^\alpha$. For fixed $\varepsilon$ the window input fixes $J\ge1$ and $X_0$ before $X\ge X_0$ and the pair are chosen.  \qquad \log(2^{e(v)+2}t(v))=O(\log v).
$$

Thus $\log(N_Et(v))\le2v^{\varepsilon/4}$ eventually, and all the budgets sum to less than $B_*=\exp(v^\varepsilon/2)$ after including the factor $\log 2$ in the dyadic term. The lower bounds for $q,d$ also give $q,d>2X^{401}+1$ and the fixed exclusions eventually. Choose the pair by (1) with $A=J$ before taking $W$. There is no cutoff rescaling at conductor one since $n=2^e$. For $x\in[W,W^{3/2}]$ the same pair has $Y\le x\le X$, $x^{1/J}\le Y$, and (2) with $t=\lfloor x^{400}\rfloor$ by monotonicity. The starts $W\ge X^{1/J}$ are unbounded. [3]

**Prescribed starts at conductor one.** The additional use of Proposition 2 is to make the dyadic budget uniform before locating a window. Let $S_a=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^a},\sqrt{qd})$ and $L_a=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^a},\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$ for $a\ge2$, and let $X_S,X_L$ be the norm limits of the $2$-primary class groups. The tower inputs for this deduction are that $X_S,X_L$ are free of ranks two and three and that the rational cyclotomic action on $X_L$ is the action on $X_S$ together with a trivial direction. These ranks use the imaginary biquadratic tower calculation and the unramified direction from $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, where $2$ splits. If $D$ is the action of the generator $\gamma_2$ of cyclotomic character $5$ on $X_S$, the first two layers give $\det(D-1)$ and $\det(D^2-1)$ as coinvariant orders up to $2$-adic units. Their $2$-parts are those of $h(-4qd)/2$ and $h(-4qd)h(-8qd)/4$, respectively, from the relative class number formula with Hasse unit index one. Consequently $v_2\det(D-1)=v_2h(-4qd)-1$ and $v_2\det(D+1)=v_2h(-8qd)-1$. [3] [11]

With the selected sign, both determinant valuations are $2$. If $p=\det(D-1)$ and $s=\operatorname{tr}(D-1)$, then $4+2s+p=\det(D+1)$ implies $s\equiv2\pmod4$. Thus the characteristic polynomial of $(D-1)/2$ has coefficients in $\mathbb Z_2$ and reduces to $T^2+T+1$. Each eigenvalue of $D$ is $1+2z_0$ with $\overline{z_0}\in\mathbb F_4\setminus\mathbb F_2$. For all positive integers $j$, both $5^j(1+2z_0)-1$ and $5^j(1+2z_0)+1$ have valuation one. The first squaring gives valuation two for the difference from $1$, and each further squaring adds one. The two eigenvalues contribute $2(e+1)$ to the cokernel length of $(5^j\gamma_2)^{2^e}-1$ on $X_L$. The trivial factor contributes $e+2+v_2(j)$.

Here the full layer is $L_{e+2}=E(i)$. Inflation–restriction and class field theory thus give the upstairs strict group's dual as a quotient of the cokernel just computed, by imposing splitting at $2$. This bounds the dyadic length of the host by $3e+4+v_2(j)$. The sum is at most

uniformly for $t=t(v)$ and $e=e(v)$, fitting below $\exp(v^{4\varepsilon/5})$ eventually. [3]

### Arithmetic data for the host

Fix the field and frame of §1 at a cutoff $x$ satisfying $Y\le x\le Y^J$ for some fixed $J\ge1$. The set constructions below apply to this arithmetic data. The geometric construction uses $m$ lifts, $k$ columns and two further ranks, denoted $R.r$ and $R.t$. Its parameters satisfy

$$

The released rank relation has the form $R.r+R.t=8fR.r+4+D_0$, with $D_0$ a nonnegative integer. Hence $R.r\le R.t$ and $q,d>4R.t\,R.r+1$ eventually by the lower bounds on $q,d$ and $Y\ge x^{1/J}$. The progression in §2 ensures that all primes through $\lfloor Y^{\varepsilon/5}\rfloor$ split in $K$, including $2$ by §1. Its norm primes, auxiliary level primes, and compatible geometric objects belong to the construction invoked for (9). [3]

### Thick digits and translated orbits

Use the coordinates $a\mapsto[5^a]$ of §1 on $H\simeq\mathbb Z/n$, where $n=n_0=2^e$ and $H_0=H$. Write $S_2$ for the set of nonnegative integers stored as the host's thick digits and $T_2\subset H$ for their image. The required length is

$$
L_w=10\lceil405\log x\rceil+2\lceil30/\varepsilon\rceil+22.
$$

Each $s\in S_2$ must satisfy $4L_w s<n$, so the digit map is injective. The condition $\mathrm{NoRel}(S_2,L_w)$ excludes

$$
j(s_2-s_1)=i(s_3-s_1)
$$

for pairwise distinct $s_1,s_2,s_3\in S_2$ and distinct integers $i,j$ with $1\le |i|,|j|\le L_w$. It excludes three-term progressions and triples with unequal gaps satisfying the same coefficient bound. The source requires an absolute $C_{T_2}>0$ with

$$
\frac{n}{L_w}\exp\!\big(-C_{T_2}\sqrt{\log n\,\log L_w}\big)\le |T_2|,
\qquad x^{-\varepsilon}n\le |T_2|\le\frac{n}{4L_w}.
$$

The raw digit interval permits $0$ and excludes $n/(4L_w)$.

**Construction.** For integers $n\ge1$ and $L\ge2$, put $M=\lfloor n/(4L)\rfloor$ and $\lambda=\log(8L)$. If $M=0$ or $M<\exp(4\lambda)$, take $S_2=\{0\}$. Here $\log n\le5\lambda\le20\log L$, using $M\ge n/(8L)$ for $M\ge1$. Hence $\log(n/L)\le15\sqrt{\log n\,\log L}$.

In the other case put $A=\log M$, $D=\lfloor\sqrt{A/\lambda}\rfloor$, $m_0=\lfloor M^{1/D}/(4L)\rfloor$, and $Q=4Lm_0$. Then $D\ge2$, $M^{1/D}\ge8L$, and $M^{1/D}/(8L)\le m_0\le M^{1/D}$. Encode $v\in\{0,\ldots,m_0-1\}^D$ by $\sum_{a=0}^{D-1}v_aQ^a$. Take the encodings of all vectors of a most frequent squared length. The encodings are distinct and lie in $[0,Q^D)\subseteq[0,M)$. There are at most $D(m_0-1)^2+1\le Dm_0^2$ squared lengths, so this gives at least $m_0^D/(Dm_0^2)$ digits.

For the forbidden relation with coefficient bound $L$, each coefficient at a power of $Q$ has absolute value at most $(|i|+|j|+|i-j|)(m_0-1)<Q$. Reducing modulo $Q$ forces the first coefficient to vanish. Dividing by $Q$ and repeating gives the vector relation $j(v_2-v_1)=i(v_3-v_1)$. The three vectors would be distinct collinear points on the same sphere. Squared distance to the center along a line is strictly quadratic, so there are at most two such points.

Since $\sqrt{A/\lambda}/2\le D\le\sqrt{A/\lambda}$, the cardinality satisfies

$$
\log|S_2|\ge A-D\lambda-2A/D-\log D\ge A-6\sqrt{A\lambda}.
$$

Using $M\ge n/(8L)$, $\lambda\le4\log L$, and $\log 8\le3\sqrt{\log n\,\log L}$ gives the required lower bound with $C_{T_2}=15$. Both constructions have $S_2\subseteq[0,M)$ when $M\ge1$, so $4L|S_2|\le n$ in that range. At $L=L_w$, we have $2\le L_w=O_\varepsilon(\log x)$ and $n\in[x^{2+8\varepsilon},2x^{2+8\varepsilon}]$. Thus $\log(n/|S_2|)\le\log L_w+15\sqrt{\log n\,\log L_w}=o(\log x)$ uniformly. This gives $\delta_2=|T_2|/n\ge x^{-\varepsilon}$ eventually. [3]

For $t,u,h\in H$ and $\operatorname{ord}(h)>4L_w$, every half-open integer window $[a_0,a_0+L_w)$ contains at most two indices $a$ with $t+ah\in u+T_2$. Three hits $a<b<c$ would give distinct digits and

$$
(c-a)(s_b-s_a)-(b-a)(s_c-s_a)\equiv0\pmod n .
$$

The absolute value on the left is $<n$ by the digit range, contradicting $\mathrm{NoRel}$ after lifting to $\mathbb Z$. The argument includes wraparound in $H$. If $d_h=\operatorname{ord}(h)\le4L_w$, the whole orbit meets $u+T_2$ in at most one distinct point. Two hits give $d_h(s_1-s_2)\equiv0\pmod n$, and the digit range forces $s_1=s_2$. This point can recur in the index sequence. Also, for every $v\in H$ there are exactly $|T_2|$ translates $u$ with $v\in u+T_2$, using $u=v-s$ for $s\in T_2$.

### Tops and weighted Frobenius-orbit estimates

Put $\delta=x^{-\varepsilon/8}$, $\alpha=2+8\varepsilon$, and fix $C_0\ge J$. We need $\delta n/2\le |TT|\le2\delta n$ and the weighted bad-orbit estimate below on this same set. The source's coset-share condition measures the discrepancy from proportions $4/(3o+1)$ on the identity coset and $3/(3o+1)$ on other cosets, with tolerance $x^{-1+\varepsilon}|TT|$. At conductor one the only coset is $H$, so the discrepancy is zero for every translate. The orbit tests for $TT$ allow three-term progressions.

Set $X'=\exp(x^{\varepsilon/3})$. Let $\mathcal P$ be a fixed set of primes $p<X'$ inert in $K$, so $J_d(p)=-1$, with $d_p=\operatorname{ord}(h_p)\ge x^{\varepsilon/4}$. Here $h_p=[p]\in H$ is the cyclotomic coordinate of arithmetic Frobenius. The proof works for the host's eligible subset by taking that subset as $\mathcal P$ before choosing $TT$. The prime weights are $\omega_p=(\log p)/p$. The splitting in §§1–2 shows that every inert prime exceeds $Y^{\varepsilon/5}$. Consequently the short window length satisfies

$$
L_2=2\sup_{p:\,J_d(p)=-1}\left\lceil\log_p((kx^{26})^4)\right\rceil
\le L_b=2\left\lceil\frac{620C_0}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil .
$$

The supremum is over all inert-class primes. The rank bound gives $4\log k+104\log x\le124\log x$ eventually, and $Y\ge x^{1/J}$ gives $\log p>(\varepsilon/(5J))\log x$.

For each orbit $t+\langle h_p\rangle$, use its fixed representative in the source and number the positions $0,\ldots,d_p-1$ in steps of $h_p$. Use the parameters

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_1=\left\lceil\frac{8(\alpha+2)}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil,\qquad
r=K_1+1,\qquad U=\lceil1/\delta\rceil,\qquad W_T=(6r+1)U,\\
M_0=\left\lceil(\alpha+3)10^{6r+1}\log x\right\rceil,\qquad G'=(M_0+5)W_T,\\
N_1=1+\left\lceil\max\big(6\exp(1)G'\delta,(\alpha+3)\log_2 x\big)\right\rceil .
\end{aligned}
$$

The source's good-orbit predicate is the conjunction of the following three tests on the indices selected by $TT$. [3]

1. Every cyclic window of $L_2$ indices contains at most $K_1$ selected indices.
2. Use the $\lfloor d_p/W_T\rfloor$ complete tiles of $W_T$ indices starting from $0$. Each tile has $6r+1$ cells of length $U$, numbered starting at $1$. A tile is marked if every odd cell is empty and every even cell contains exactly one selected index of even parity in the orbit numbering. For every tile index $s_0$, one of the tiles $s_0+j$, $0\le j\le M_0$, is marked, with addition modulo the number of complete tiles.
3. Every cyclic window of $G'$ indices contains at most $N_1$ selected indices.

**Simultaneous selection.** Select each point of $H$ independently with probability $\delta$. For fixed $p\in\mathcal P$ and $t$, condition on selecting $t$. The other indices in its orbit still have independent Bernoulli laws. Uniformly for the orbits tested, $G'$ and $(M_0+1)W_T$ are $O_\varepsilon(x^{\varepsilon/8}\log x)$ and less than $d_p$ eventually. Also $L_2\le L_b<d_p$. Thus individual windows have distinct indices, and the trials in test 2 use distinct tiles.

For test 1, a window containing the conditioned point requires $K_1$ further selections to fail, and at most $L_2$ windows contain that point. Each other failing window requires $K_1+1$ selections. The union bound is at most

$$
L_2\binom{L_2}{K_1}\delta^{K_1}
+d_p\binom{L_2}{K_1+1}\delta^{K_1+1}
\le (L_b+2)2^{L_b}x^{-2},
$$

as $d_p\le2x^\alpha$ and $\delta^{K_1}\le x^{-(\alpha+2)}$.

For test 2, discard the trial tile containing the conditioned point if there is one. The other at least $M_0$ tiles are independent. An empty cell has probability $(1-\delta)^U$. A cell with exactly one selected index, of even parity, has probability at least $\lfloor U/2\rfloor\delta(1-\delta)^{U-1}$. For $\delta\le1/100$, the first exceeds $1/3$, by $\log(1-\delta)\ge-\delta/(1-\delta)$ and $U\delta\le1+\delta$. The second exceeds $1/10$, since $\lfloor U/2\rfloor\delta\ge(1-\delta)/2$. The marking probability is at least $10^{-(6r+1)}$. Summing over the at most $d_p$ starting tiles, failure costs at most $d_p\exp(-M_0 10^{-(6r+1)})\le2x^{-3}$.

For test 3, the conditioned count in a window is dominated by $1+\mathrm{Bin}(G',\delta)$. With $N_*=N_1-1\ge1$, the binomial tail at $N_*$ is bounded by

$$
\sum_{\nu=N_*}^{G'}\left(\frac{\exp(1)G'\delta}{\nu}\right)^\nu
\le \frac65\,6^{-N_*}\le \exp(-N_*) .
$$

This uses $N_*\ge6\exp(1)G'\delta$. Multiplying by the at most $d_p$ windows gives at most $2x^{-3}$, since $N_*\ge(\alpha+3)\log_2 x$.

Write $B_p(TT)$ for the number of selected tops on bad orbits at $p$. The conditional failure probability for each $p,t$ is at most $C_bx^{-2}$ for $C_b=(L_b+2)2^{L_b}+4$, independent of $x$. Hence

$$
\mathbb E\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\omega_p B_p(TT)
\le C_bx^{-2}\delta n\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\omega_p .
$$

By Markov the chance of exceeding four times this bound is at most $1/4$, with zero weighted count almost surely if the bound is zero. The size has mean $\delta n$ and variance at most $\delta n$. Chebyshev gives probability at most $4/(\delta n)$ of leaving $[\delta n/2,2\delta n]$. Since $\delta n\to\infty$, one choice satisfies the size interval, the exact ordinary coset shares, and

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\omega_p B_p(TT)
\le 4C_bx^{-2}\delta n\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\omega_p .
$$

This is the source's $\mathrm{BadOK}$ estimate. To obtain the endpoint estimate's bound $|TT|x^{-2+\varepsilon}$, use $\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\omega_p\le2\log X'=2x^{\varepsilon/3}$ and $|TT|\ge\delta n/2$. Indeed, with $M_X=\lfloor X'\rfloor$, the prime contributions $\lfloor M_X/p\rfloor\log p$ to $\log(M_X!)$ give this prime-weight bound using $\lfloor M_X/p\rfloor\ge M_X/(2p)$. [3]

The marked tiles supply the endpoints on a good orbit. Each contains $3r$ even selected indices, at least $U$ from the left endpoint, with $i+U$ strictly before the right endpoint and successive gaps $>U>L_2$ for large $x$. For successive marked tiles, write their increasing lists as $f,f'$, indexed from $0$, and use $f(r)$ and $f'(2r-1)$. Lift the latter by $d_p$ on wraparound. Apply test 2 starting at the tile immediately after the first marked tile. The next marked tile is at most $M_0+1$ steps away, and there are more than $M_0+1$ complete tiles because $G'<d_p$. The final incomplete tile, of length $<W_T$, can occur once in the segment. Thus the forward endpoint distance is $<(M_0+3)W_T<G'$, and the inclusive segment contains at most $N_1$ selected indices. [3]

### Carrying the sets into the geometry

The host installs $S_2$ at offset $u_0=0$. Its top places are $((0,0),t)$ for $t\in TT$. At translate $u$, the thick places are $((0,1),s+u)$ for $s\in T_2$. Their distinct quadratic colors make the two supports disjoint for every $u$, even when their projections in $H$ intersect. The stated orbit and density bounds apply to the same $T_2$ at all translates. In §5 the thick partner coordinate $s+u-t$ covers $H$ once as $u$ varies with $s,t$ fixed. [3]

The local theta estimates use the thick-orbit test on the letters $\tau\phi^i$. If the cyclotomic step has order $>4L_w$, the thick letters satisfy the at-most-two condition in every window of $L_w$ indices. At depth $j$ with $1\le j$ and $2j+2\le L_w$, three thick letters linked by successive gaps at most $j$ would lie in one such window. The twisted dual page-word estimate inserts the resulting cluster bound and $j+1\le\operatorname{ord}(\phi)$ into its local hypothesis $\mathrm{HypA}(j)$, along with the numerical weight bounds.

For the comparison take positive heights $a_t$ for $t\in TT$ and positive weights $w_{u,j}(\rho)$ at $\rho\in\Phi$ for every $u\in H$ and $1\le j\le m$. Write $n_T=|TT|$, $n_2=|T_2|$, $a_E=xb$, $b'=a_E/X'$, $N=\lceil2a_E\rceil$, and $\theta=\exp(-c_6x^{\varepsilon/8})$ with fixed $c_6>0$. With $\eta\ge0$ the weight tolerance, impose

$$
\begin{aligned}
|a_t/b-x|\le1,\qquad a_t/w_{u,j}(\rho)\le N,\qquad
b\le w_{u,j}(\rho)\le b+\eta+1\ \text{at thick partners},\\
w_{u,j}(\rho)\le2b'\ \text{at thin partners},\qquad
2f(x+1)\theta\le 1/x,\qquad 6f(x+1)(\eta+1)\le b/x,\\
n_T\ge n x^{-\varepsilon/8}/2,\qquad \delta_2\ge x^{-\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

The first cost bound follows eventually because $f=2n=O(x^{2+8\varepsilon})$. The second constrains the fitted $\eta$. The last line follows from the two set constructions above. In the geometric application the period and norm assertions must use the same field, sets, lifts, weights and truncation. [3]

**Thin estimate (Paper I, Proposition 4).** At all $y\ge X'/3$, for every primitive character $\chi$ of the fitted host, including the principal case interpreted as $G_z$,

$$
|G_\chi(y)|\le y\theta .
\tag{4}
$$

The conductor bound $\log(Bqd)\le2x^{\varepsilon/5}(\log x)^2$ follows eventually from $Y\le x$, the bounds on $q,d$, and $B=O(x^{2+8\varepsilon})$. A real character of the dyadic units is trivial on squares, hence on all units congruent to $1\bmod8$, so its primitive conductor divides $8$. Every real host character thus has the form $\rho J_q^iJ_d^j$ by the Chinese remainder theorem. For those ramified at $q$ or $d$, the boxes in §2 give real nonvanishing above $399/400$. The finitely many remaining nonprincipal real characters have a common interval below $1$ free of zeros by classical nonvanishing at $1$ and continuity. These bounds verify the hypotheses of Proposition 4 of Paper I. In its contour estimate, with $V=\log y$ and height $T=\exp(\sqrt V)$, the zero-free strip has width $\gg V^{-3/5}(\log V)^{-2}$. Multiplication by $V$ gives a decay exponent $\gg V^{2/5}(\log V)^{-2}$. Both this and $\sqrt V$ dominate $x^{\varepsilon/8}$ since $V\ge x^{\varepsilon/3}-\log 3$, giving (4) for any fixed $c_6>0$. [1] [3]

**Cutoff domain.** Fix $\varepsilon,J$ and let $\mathcal W$ consist of all real cutoffs admitting the arithmetic data in §3. The released pointwise interfaces use this same scale set in the host, auxiliary-level, object, and weight constructions. The required uniform existence assertion is that above a common threshold the resulting family supplies data at every $x\in\mathcal W$ with the same cutoff and the fixed parameter bounds. Under the late-window input, $\mathcal W$ contains arbitrarily late power intervals. Under the prescribed-start inputs it contains a tail. A pointwise existence assertion on $\mathcal W$ would preserve either domain above its threshold. [3]

## 5. The finite prime-power identity and the thick mean

Fix a fitted cutoff with $x\ge3$. For any character $\vartheta\in\widehat G$ let $G_\vartheta$ and $\ell_\vartheta$ use its primitive representative, treating the principal ramp as $G_z$. For $\rho\in\Phi$ take a positive weight $w(\rho)$, and suppose $a>0$ and $a/w(\rho)\le N$ for all $\rho\in\Phi$. For a top $t\in H$ write $t_* =((0,0),t)$ and $P_t(g)=c+g+t_*$, a bijection from $G\setminus\Phi$ to $\Phi$. The finite prime sum $\mathrm{ASK}$ uses the factor $(a/p^i-w(P_t(g)))_+$ at a class $g\notin\Phi$. For $p\nmid Bqd$ it takes $g=i\operatorname{Frob}_p$ with coefficient $2\log p$. For $p=d$ it sums over the complementary classes in $i\operatorname{Frob}_d+I_d$ with coefficient $\log d$. At $2,q$ its contribution vanishes. Only $p^i\le N$ can contribute. Define

$$
\mathrm{MAIN}(a,w)
=a\left(\log a-1-\gamma-\frac1f\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\log w(\rho)+\ell_{J_d}\right).
$$

The finite balance is

$$
\mathrm{ASK}-\mathrm{MAIN}
=\frac1f\sum_{g\notin\Phi}\sum_{\vartheta\in\widehat G}
  \vartheta(-g)\,w(P_t(g))G_\vartheta\bigl(a/w(P_t(g))\bigr).
\tag{5}
$$

[3]

**Proof of (5).** First use the uncentered prime ramps. Fourier inversion gives, at $p^i$, the coefficient

$$
\frac1f\sum_{\vartheta\in\widehat G}\vartheta(-g)
     \frac1{|I_p|}\sum_{h\in I_p}\vartheta(i\operatorname{Frob}_p+h)
=\frac{2}{|I_p|}\,\mathbf1_{g\in i\operatorname{Frob}_p+I_p},
$$

since $|G|=2f$. The inertia and splitting calculations of §1 now give all the displayed prime coefficients. The principal center subtracted from the uncentered sum is $a\log(a/w)-(1+\gamma)a$, averaged over the $f$ partners. For nonprincipal characters, the center added to the uncentered sum for a given $g$ is $a\ell_\vartheta\vartheta(-g)/f$, independent of the partner's weight. The sum of $\vartheta(-g)$ on $G\setminus\Phi$ is zero unless $\vartheta$ is trivial on $\Phi$. The unique nonprincipal character trivial there is $J_d$, with value $-1$ on the complementary coset, so its sum is $-f$. This accounts for $+a\ell_{J_d}$ in $\mathrm{MAIN}$ upon subtraction. [3]

For each $u,t,j$ use $a=a_t,\ w=w_{u,j}$ in (5). The thick partners are $((0,1),s+u)$ for $s\in T_2$. Thus their classes $g$ in (5) are $g_s(u,t)=((1,0),s+u-t)$, each occurring once. Put $y_t=a_t/b$, and let $\mathrm{Th}_{ut}$ denote the right side of (5) with thin partners omitted and all thick weights replaced by $b$. Summing over $u\in H$ kills the characters nontrivial on $H$. For each surviving character $\vartheta$, the phase in every summand is its value at $((1,0),0)$, independent of $s,u,t$. In order $\mathbf1,J_q,J_d,J_qJ_d$ the four signs are $+,+,-,-$. Consequently (**proved here**)

$$
\frac1n\sum_{u\in H}\mathrm{Th}_{ut}
=\frac{bn_2}{f}\,G_{\rm sig}(y_t),\qquad
G_{\rm sig}=G_z+G_{J_q}-G_{J_d}-G_{J_qJ_d}.
\tag{6}
$$

The four terms are real. The factor $n_2/f=\delta_2/2$ persists after taking the mean. [3]

### The comparison error

We first bound the change at a fixed $u,t,j$, with $a=a_t$. For $w\ge b$, the function $v\mapsto(a/n-v)_+$ is decreasing and $1$-Lipschitz. It has zero contribution for $n>a/b$ at either weight. For a nonprincipal character the two centers $a\ell_\vartheta$ cancel in the weighted difference. Thus by the classical Chebyshev estimate $\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)\le3y$ the change has modulus at most $3(a/b)(w-b)$. In the principal case the uncentered difference from $b$ to $w$, taken as its value at $b$ minus its value at $w$, belongs to $[0,3(a/b)(w-b)]$. From it one subtracts $a\log(w/b)$, which lies in the same interval. This proves the same bound in the principal case. At a thin partner, (3) gives $a/w\ge X'/3$ because $a\ge(x-1)b$, $w\le2b'$, and $x\ge3$. The omitted term has modulus at most $a\theta$ by (4).

There are $n_2$ thick and $f-n_2$ thin partners and $2f$ characters in each term of (5). Thus the error at $u,t,j$ has modulus at most

$$
2\left[n_2\cdot3y_t(\eta+1)+(f-n_2)a_t\theta\right]\le b/x
$$

by (3). The factor $2$ comes from the full character sum and the denominator $f$. Let $E(u)=\sum_{t\in TT}\sum_{j=1}^m(\mathrm{ASK}-\Re\mathrm{MAIN})$ at translate $u$, and let $\overline E$ denote its uniform mean. Put $Q=n_Tmb\,\delta_2/2>0$. Summing the error and using (6) yields

$$
\left|\frac{\overline E}{Q}-\frac1{n_T}\sum_{t\in TT}G_{\rm sig}(y_t)\right|\le \frac{2}{x\delta_2}.
\tag{7}
$$

The release also uses the weaker allowance $4/(x\delta_2)$ by separately budgeting both errors using all partners.

For the move to the common cutoff, the classical Mertens estimate $\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)/n=\log y+O(1)$ implies $G_z'(y)=O(1)$ away from integers, since the derivative of its center cancels $\log y$. Each nonprincipal ramp has derivative of modulus at most $\log y+O(1)+|\ell_\vartheta|$. The ramps are continuous and piecewise differentiable, including at the newly admitted integer where the new ramp term vanishes. Since $|y_t-x|\le1$, (1) at $x$ gives, uniformly in the host,

$$
|G_{\rm sig}(y_t)-G_{\rm sig}(x)|\le C(1+\log x)|y_t-x|.
$$

Together with (1) and (7) this proves the ordinary comparison (**proved here**)

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle
\left|\frac{\overline E}{Q}-G_z(x)\right|
 \le 3x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2}+C(1+\log x)+\frac{2}{x\delta_2}.}
\tag{8}
$$

The sharper signed comparison uses (7) and the cutoff move alone. The bounds at $x$ suffice even when a particular $y_t$ lies outside the selected interval of analytic bounds.

### The geometric upper bound needed at the interface

**Geometric hypothesis.** On the same fitted host require the following upper bound for the finite excess $E(u)$ defined above, with fixed allowances $e_D,e_L\ge0$ for the finite divisibility and norm losses:

$$
E(u)\le x^{(e_D+e_L+3)\varepsilon}\mathcal B
 \quad\text{for every }u\in H,\qquad
 \mathcal B=n_T a_E(1+m^2/k)+m\left(2nmbx^\varepsilon
                    +\frac{n_2kb x^{2\varepsilon}}{n^2}\right),
\tag{9}
$$

The same right side for $\overline E$ alone suffices for the scalar implication. The released geometric route requires integral period maps on the selected lattices, slope and height bounds for these maps, control of the common divisor and finite-prime losses, and uniform supply and compatibility for the translates. The bound (9) and the joint existence of data realizing it and (3) are hypotheses of this paper's application. [3] [4]

To check the size explicitly, divide (9) by $Q$ and use (3). The four terms in $\mathcal B/Q$, in order, are

$$
O\left(
\frac{x^{1/2+\varepsilon/16}}{\delta_2}
+\frac{x^{-3-161\varepsilon/16}}{\delta_2}
+\frac{x^{1/2+17\varepsilon/16}}{\delta_2}
+x^{1/2-31\varepsilon/8}
\right).
$$

The last term uses $n_2/\delta_2=n$ before the bound $n/n_T\le2x^{\varepsilon/8}$. With $\delta_2^{-1}\le x^\varepsilon$, the largest exponent after the factor in (9) is $1/2+(e_D+e_L+81/16)\varepsilon$. Equation (8) therefore implies $G_z(x)\le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}$ for sufficiently large $x$ at fixed $\varepsilon$. [3]

**Power-interval implication.** Fix $e_D,e_L\ge0$. Suppose that for each sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ and each requested lower cutoff there is a power interval $[W,W^{3/2}]$ above that cutoff, such that at every real $x$ in it there are compatible data for (1), (3), and (9), with all parameters in their stated ranges and thresholds uniform in $x$. The host and fitted objects may vary with $x$. For any $1/2<\beta<1$, choose $\varepsilon$ with $(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon\le\beta-1/2$. The pointwise conclusion gives $G_z(x)\le x^\beta$ on arbitrarily late such intervals. Paper I, Theorem 1 then excludes zeros to the right of $\beta$. All $\beta>1/2$ together with the functional equation give RH under this joint hypothesis. The prescribed-start deduction in §3 can provide the arithmetic cutoffs under its stated inputs. The geometric hypothesis and fitted-data requirements apply at each of those cutoffs. [1] [3]
