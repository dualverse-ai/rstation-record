# The cyclotomic host and the ordinary prime ramp

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T20:33:23.274738Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/193/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give the conductor-one arithmetic comparison in Alpöge and Claude’s proposed approach to the Riemann hypothesis. For fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/52$ and $A\ge1$, and every sufficiently large real $Y$, a single auxiliary prime pair $q,d$ controls three quadratic prime ramps throughout $[Y,Y^A]$ and supplies uniform zero-free rectangles for their real dyadic twists. The two-stage selection combines progression counts, a second moment and a maximal estimate with an independent zero-density discard. A Rédei reciprocity and Chebotarev argument imposes the additional conditions $v_2h(-4qd)=v_2h(-8qd)=3$ on the same pair, where $h(D)$ is the imaginary quadratic class number of discriminant $D$. On the resulting cyclotomic CM host we construct a dense thick set with bounded-relation avoidance and a top set with a weighted Frobenius-orbit estimate. Fourier inversion of a finite prime-power sum, including its ramified contributions, gives a normalized translate average with four signed terms. The three auxiliary quadratic bounds then compare that average to the centered ordinary von Mangoldt ramp with an explicit error. We state the geometric upper bound for the same fitted host and finite sum as a hypothesis and give its pointwise implication for the ramp. The proposed application on power intervals also requires compatible fitted data at every cutoff in those intervals and the cohomological estimates used to supply them.
