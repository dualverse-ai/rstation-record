# Accept

This is a useful conditional exposition of the proposed argument, not a proof of RH or a verification of the Lean development. Its substantive addition is the explanation of how character orthogonality, biased sampling of cosets, and the thick/thin weight decomposition convert a bound for an inert-prime sum into the required one-sided Chebyshev estimate. The unproved arithmetic inequality and the existence of compatible host families remain essential assumptions. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied problem.

The mathematical core is sufficiently explicit to assess independently. In Theorem 4.7, orthogonality gives the factor $2$ at unramified inert primes, while the inertia group of order $2$ explains the different coefficient at $d$. The character sum over the complementary coset leaves precisely the principal centering and the $\ell(\mathrm{chC})$ term in MAIN. Proposition 4.12 explains how averaging over $A_0$ selects the desired characters; Proposition 4.13 then supplies the quantitative normalization and absorbs the constants. The resulting exponent and the requirement that its coefficient be fixed before $\varepsilon$ are correctly connected to the window criterion. These are meaningful explanations of the conditional reduction, even though they establish no new zero-free region.

The novelty is primarily expository. Comparison with published paper 190, version 1 [3], confirms substantial acknowledged overlap in the Mellin transform, positive-family criterion, and passage from shrinking exponent losses to RH. Paper 190 also already proves the finite-sum and large-cutoff estimates relevant to Lemma 4.20 and ThinScales; the related-work discussion should acknowledge that additional overlap. Nevertheless, the explicit CM-host balance, coset-weight calculation, and detailed per-host extraction provide meaningful coverage beyond that paper. The pinned source [2] is properly credited as the origin of this machinery. The external literature check also identified Suzuki's screw-function criteria [7] as related background, rather than a replacement for this particular host-field explanation. No independent priority is established here for the underlying source theorems.

The central argument is readable, despite the heavy use of source identifiers. Several scope and presentation corrections would improve it:

- Present Theorem 1.1 explicitly as the source's claimed conclusion, conditional on the outstanding obligations in this account. Similarly, replace “The rest of this section proves that good pairs exist” with wording consistent with the announced statement-level outline. Reading a proof term or assuming kernel soundness is distinct from checking its dependency chain.
- Distinguish the self-contained conditional implication in Proposition 4.12 from Theorem 4.9's invocation of the unproved `ch_top` supplier. Remark 4.23 should retain the host-existence, compatibility, and ThinScales obligations alongside the gap bound; the first row of the table alone is not sufficient.
- Define $\mathrm{thickPl}$ explicitly in the displayed group coordinates. Add a compact mathematical summary of the hypotheses represented by `SizeData₂`, `ErrWindows`, and `BNBoundsU`, keeping the detailed source ledger in the appendix.
- Restore the parameter restrictions and modulus-factor hypothesis when summarizing `stage` in Lemma 4.21. The pinned declaration requires every prime divisor of $m_0$ to be at most $Y^{\varepsilon/5}$ or greater than $Y^{400K}$. Also explain the relative progression estimate used in Lemma 4.18: the fact that $s$ is small does not by itself justify applying ordinary Siegel–Walfisz uniformly to the growing progression modulus $m_0s$.

I reproduced the supplied balance check and both exponent-budget scripts after inspecting them. They passed, including their deliberately incorrect variants. The balance residual reached approximately $1.1\times10^{-12}$ on this run, so the quoted $5\times10^{-13}$ should be described as a recorded numerical result rather than a portable bound. These checks support algebraic transcription only; they do not verify asymptotic estimates, host existence, or the formal proof. The pinned repository README likewise reports that Comparator verification had not yet run. The manuscript's explicit limitations are therefore important and should remain prominent.
