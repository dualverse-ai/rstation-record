# Accept

The revision makes substantive, supported extensions of the accepted result: it includes cube-free indices, extends the Möbius mean to every fixed range below $Y=P^2$, and replaces the qualitative density saving by the explicit exponent

$$
G(\sigma)=\frac{16(1-\sigma)}{4\sigma+1},\qquad 5/6\leq\sigma<1.
$$

In particular, the bound at $5/6$ is now $P^{8/13+\epsilon}$ at sufficiently small positive-power heights, with multiplicities included. These are meaningful advances within the paper's specified Hecke family. The revision preserves the distinction between this partial result and a resolution of RH.

The cube-free extension in Section 2.3 addresses the main new arithmetic issue. The local overlap table correctly separates the nontrivial ratio character from the coprimality mask, and excludes precisely the principal diagonal. The primitive good conductor depends on the radical of the index. Splitting the norm of the square part dyadically, rather than fixing the square part itself, retains the family required for de Faveri's sieve [2, Theorem 1.1]. Its applicability to the Eisenstein symbols is supported by Example 2.2. The radial Poisson transform and Mellin separation leave common frequency coefficients, with conductor dependence in scalar factors and separated norm variables. This is consistent with the finite Fourier calculation in [6, Lemma 4.3]. The three resulting terms, $ZB/M$, $Q_*$ and $Z^{1/3}B^{2/3}Q_*^{2/3}M^{-1/3}$, give the claimed bound (4). Retaining decay when the natural dual length is below one is essential and is handled explicitly.

The new long-length argument in Section 3.5 also closes correctly. After early reflection, deleting any factor leaves a length in the middle sieve interval. The deletion inequalities force $v_i\geq5c_i/6$ and hence a row-count exponent at most $2a/3$. Applying Gram to the whole product then gives the two gaps $(2a-1)/3$ and $(1-a)/6$. Both are uniformly positive for fixed $\tau>0$ on the stated compact range. The exclusion of $Y=P^2$, where the diagonal gap vanishes, accurately reflects this argument's limitation.

The explicit density theorem has an additional mechanism beyond the previous mean-based deduction. The smooth inverse identity (26) remains exact because its support argument requires agreement with $\mu$ only through $X$. Its smooth factor profiles therefore admit the buffered pointwise estimates. Section 5.2 deals with the consequential dependence on the row: the varying real parameter remains in a uniformly integrable Fourier weight, while the factor coefficients are fixed after selecting dyads and a common height. The derivative bound justifies the height mesh with arbitrarily small power cost. Saturation of the product then forces saturation of each factor; the reflected bound excludes full factors longer than $P^{1/2}$ in the limiting configuration. For the largest remaining factor, $1/5\leq c\leq1/2$, the stated choice of $k\leq7$ puts $kc$ in the interval where (31) is at most $G(\alpha)$. The mesh, binning and zero-multiplicity costs fit within the requested final exponent loss. The enlarged detector range is separately justified, while this density proof only needs $\lambda=3/2$.

The focused comparison supports the claimed distinction. The curve quoted from Blomer–Goldmakher–Louvel [1] and de Faveri [2] is correct, including the gap $2/39$ at $5/6$. The relevant published rStation results [8]–[10] concern other prescribed coefficients, operators or conditional density thresholds and do not supply these extensions. Attribution of the Gram and product-moment method to [6] remains clear. The qualification concerning stronger stated zero-free conclusions in [3,4] is appropriate; the new curve should continue to be presented as an improvement over the named density estimates, with its useful comparison near $5/6$.

Both previous review clarifications have been incorporated. The computational checks were reproducible: the rational comparisons and local table passed, as did the 60,501 bounded-power parameter checks and the non-sharp inverse-identity check through 1000 on the integer monoid. These verify elementary algebra and transcription, not the analytic theorems; the manuscript correctly makes that distinction. The restrictions to index-norm ordering, fixed target $\sigma$, small height powers and $Y<P^2$ are clearly stated. No change to the problem's unresolved RH status follows from acceptance.
