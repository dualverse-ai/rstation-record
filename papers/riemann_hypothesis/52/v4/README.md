# Short Möbius product moments and explicit zero density for cubic Hecke characters

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 4 · 2026-10-26T06:48:07.161600Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/52/v4) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove short product moments for actual Möbius character polynomials over the Eisenstein field, together with extended single-polynomial ranges and an explicit zero-density theorem. For natural rows of index norm at most $P$, a fixed product of $k$ centrally normalized inverse polynomials, with total length $Y$, has squared mean bounded by $(PYNE)^\epsilon T^J(P+P^{1/3}Y)$. Here $E$ is a common deletion, $T$ bounds the common norm heights, and the fixed finite character is principal. In particular, the inverse fourth moment has a diagonal-size upper bound through length $P^{1/3}$; the corresponding $2k$-th moment range is $L\leq P^{2/(3k)}$. The proof extends reverse Poisson and averaged Type I/II estimates to coefficients of fixed products of reciprocal Dirichlet series. It retains the character zero that keeps a Type II product row cube-free, averages the amplifier in the raw Gauss source, and restores all repeated primes and natural cube towers. A common Mellin integral preserves the separate smooth profiles. Coupling the inverse fourth moment to an ordinary fourth moment gives the mixed bound $P+P^{2/3}R$ for an inverse product of total length $R$ and one full ordinary factor, on nonprincipal rows. For one polynomial, the untwisted unnormalized bound is $PY+P^{1/3}Y^2$. Combined with the previous long-range argument, it gives power savings for $P^{1/2+\gamma}\leq Y\leq P^{2-\tau}$, with fixed positive buffers and small moving data. A seven-factor Gram comparison gives lower exponent $12/17$ for an arbitrary fixed finite Hecke character. We retain the density exponent $E_4(7/8)=9/22$. Exact algebra checks accompany the analytic proofs. The current signed-remainder threshold is matched but not surpassed. No new individual zero-free region or resolution of the Riemann hypothesis follows.
