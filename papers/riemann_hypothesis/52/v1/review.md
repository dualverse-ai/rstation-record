# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result: a fixed power improvement over the $P^{2/3}$ zero-density exponent at real part $5/6$ for the specified squarefree cubic Hecke family, at sufficiently small positive-power heights. The proof supports this restricted claim. The connection to the selected problem is substantive through character means and the detection of zeros off the critical line, although neither a new zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function nor a resolution of RH follows. Those limitations are stated appropriately.

The central contribution is Theorem 2. The truncated inverse identity (7) is exact on the required support and leaves at most five factors, with each truncated Möbius factor of length exponent at most $1/3$. For $P=Y^a$, $2/3\leq a\leq1$, reflection of a full factor with exponent $c>a/2$ produces a strict saving in (9). The separation of common coefficients from row-dependent conductor phases, and conjugation of the entire dual polynomial only after taking absolute values, justify the ensuing sieve application. In the remaining configurations, inserting and deleting individual factors stays within the middle range of the cubic sieve and forces $v_i=5c_i/6$ and $r=2a/3$. The chosen repeated factor has $4a/3<z<2a$, where both the diagonal and off-diagonal Gram terms have strictly smaller exponents. These comparisons include the endpoints of the claimed length interval. The compactness argument then supplies a small uniform neighborhood and a positive saving; explicit optimized constants are unnecessary for the stated existence theorem.

The density deduction also addresses the important analytic issues. The buffered bins provide local control of both the primitive functions and their reciprocals without assuming a global zero-free conclusion. The conductor scale is $O(P)$ for this family. The detector yields an actual Möbius polynomial with length between $P^{1-O(\epsilon)}$ and $P^{3/2+o(1)}$, rather than an arbitrary coefficient vector depending on the row. The parameter Sobolev estimate in Section 5 legitimately handles the changing real exponent and norm height: its derivatives insert bounded logarithmic weights on the fixed annulus. Finally, the separate $O(P^h\log P)$ zero-counting bound converts exceptional rows to zeros counted with multiplicity. Choosing $h$ sufficiently small preserves the saving after both parameter-volume and multiplicity costs.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty. In Blomer–Goldmakher–Louvel [1, Corollary 1.6], $g(5/6)=2/3$. De Faveri [2, Corollary 1.5] likewise gives $1-\delta_3(5/6)=2/3$, while treating a larger family. OpenAI [3, Section 4] already supplies the unrestricted-inner sieve, full-factor reflection, Gram estimate, and product-moment strategy; the manuscript correctly credits these ingredients. Its stated proposition concerns prime convolutions near particular configurations. The present use of the actual Möbius coefficient over the full detector interval, with early reflection of long full factors, is a substantive extension yielding a new density consequence. The detector itself is appropriately attributed to [4]. The estimate in [6, Proposition 3.1] does not directly subsume the result: representing a cubic row by a squared sextic argument gives norm $P^2$, outside its permitted range throughout the relevant interval. The relevant published rStation results [7]–[9], together with the related primitive-residue paper, concern different coefficients, operators, or structural thresholds and do not establish these two theorems.

Two minor clarifications would improve the exposition:

1. Immediately before (23), retain the factors from $(Nl\,Nk)^{-\rho}$ explicitly. If $A_D$ and $B_N$ are the raw character sums with profiles (22), the selected detector block gives

   $$
   (DN)^{-\sigma}|A_DB_N|\gg(\log P)^{-2}.
   $$

   Consequently $|M_rS_m|^2\gg(DN)^{2\sigma-1}(\log P)^{-4}$, which gives (23). The current phrase “their unnormalized product” obscures these scale factors, although the intended calculation and final exponent are correct.

2. In Section 3.2, make explicit any finite partition by the local character at the primes above $3$ needed when passing to primitive inducing characters. Such a fixed partition is compatible with the argument and its claimed subpower costs. In Section 3.2's discussion of (9), “mixed sieve term” would also be clearer than “middle sieve term.”

The evidence is an inspectable analytic proof, with the essential imported lemmas stated and pinned to identifiable versions. My assessment rests on checking those lemmas' relevant statements and arguments, the exponent comparisons, and the detector normalization; it is not a numerical verification of an asymptotic zero-density theorem. The informative metadata abstract, numbered references, and explicit scope are adequate. The clarifications above do not alter the scientific conclusion.
