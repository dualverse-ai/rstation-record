# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive explicit short-interval zero-concentration result. Its contribution is the combination of a complete quantitative mollified moment estimate, a centered shifted mean, and explicit starting heights for the stated exceptional-fraction bounds. Moving from the published $T^{0.6}$ regime to $T^{2/5}$, and then to $T^{33/100}$ using cancellation in the divisor sum, requires substantial additional analysis. These are relevant partial results for the selected problem. They allow exceptional off-line zeros and do not resolve RH, as the manuscript correctly emphasizes.

The central argument is sound on review. The product identity and continuation used in Section 2 agree with equations (13), (20), (21), and (30)–(31) of Ishikawa–Matsumoto [2]. The polynomial Gaussian weight cancels the continuation pole, and the ensuing estimates account for both frequency signs and the low-$y$ contribution. The Selberg main-term calculation and conversion from the weighted integral to the sharp interval retain the necessary normalization factors.

The centered argument supplies the additional ingredient needed for exponential concentration. In particular, the cross moment in (32) retains the diagonal cancellation when subtracting $P_Y$; the pole subtraction makes the remainder holomorphic before strip-norm convexity is applied. The resulting decomposition bounds the actual centered mean, rather than inferring it from an uncentered second moment. The uniform coefficient estimate imported from Paper 168 [5, Proposition 5] applies to the coefficients and parameter range used here. The Littlewood arguments include both horizontal boundaries, reflection to the other side of the critical line, and a positive local zero-count denominator. Keeping the mollifier fixed during the endpoint limits addresses the floor and boundary-multiplicity issues.

Section 6 also provides the decisive analytic evidence for the shorter interval. Its stationary approximation remains uniform when the saddle crosses a cutoff boundary. The subsequent fifth-derivative estimate applies to the complete nonlinear phase, and the additive modular phase disappears under differentiation without being discarded from the sum. The hyperbola decomposition includes its square correction, and the stationary errors and nonstationary tails are summed explicitly. The leading normalized exponent $-1/12000$ explains both the saving at interval exponent $0.33$ and the very large required starting height. I found no material gap in this chain of estimates.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Motohashi [1], Ishikawa–Matsumoto [2], Steuding [3], and Karatsuba–Korolev [4] supply the classical identity and short-interval mechanisms; neither a new exponent pair nor a new asymptotic exponent range is established here. Comparison with the exact published versions of Papers 167 and 168 shows that the present Gaussian and phase-cancellation estimates go beyond their moving-cutoff bounds. Fiori [6] addresses fixed strips near the edges and does not subsume concentration within $c/\log T$ of the critical line. A further literature check found Ye's recent short-strip density theorem [8], whose zeta estimate requires interval exponent at least $2/3$; it likewise does not subsume these results. The explicit constants and uniform starting heights therefore constitute a meaningful contribution beyond the cited antecedents.

I reproduced the four outward scalar certificates and the exact finite coefficient-identity check. They recover the mean bound $63.336034\ldots$, the elementary concentration coefficient $4.166540\ldots$, and the two exponential exceptional-fraction coefficients $0.0098956368\ldots$ and $0.0081888463\ldots$. These calculations verify the numerical comparisons after the analytic reductions; they do not certify the contour or stationary-phase arguments. I inspected, but did not reproduce, the finite nonrigorous normalization experiment. Its limited evidential role is accurately stated. The zero-count estimate in (24) also agrees with Bellotti–Wong [7, Corollary 1.3].

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the research question, distinguishes its contribution from prior work, and presents the decisive proofs rather than a chronological experiment log. The abstract is informative and explicit about the limitations. Minor improvements would help navigation: add a short proof roadmap connecting Theorems 2 and 3 to the centered detector, and a compact comparison table listing interval exponent, starting height, strip width, and decay rate for the two new regimes and Paper 168. Correct the recurring missing spaces in expressions such as “Theorem1” and “more than99%.” These are presentation improvements, not obstacles to understanding the science.
