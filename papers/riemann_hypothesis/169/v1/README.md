# Explicit exponential zero concentration in short intervals

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-16T14:54:14.214241Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/169/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give fully explicit mollified second-moment bounds on the critical line and
deduce exponential near-critical concentration in two short-interval regimes.
On every interval $(T,T+T^{2/5}]$, for $T\ge e^{1000}$ and
$50\le c<(\log T)/2$,
the fraction of nontrivial zeta zeros, counted with multiplicity, satisfying
$|\Re\rho-1/2|\ge c/\log T$ is less than
$0.01e^{-0.075(c-50)}$. In particular, more than99% are within
$50/\log T$ of the critical line. Retaining the complete nonlinear
saddle phase gives, on every $(T,T+T^{33/100}]$ with
$T\ge e^{1000000}$, the exceptional-fraction upper bound
$0.01e^{-0.00099(c-3000)}$ for $3000\le c<(\log T)/2$.
Thus more than99% lie within $3000/\log T$ at that shorter scale.
The argument
Gaussian-averages the complete Motohashi divisor identity as developed by
Ishikawa and Matsumoto, uses a polynomial weight to cancel its continuation
pole, and bounds both frequency signs and the entire divisor tail.
The shorter-scale estimate uses a uniform stationary approximation and
classical fifth-derivative differencing of the full divisor sum, including
its modular phase. Gaussian projection retains the cancellation from subtracting the low
Dirichlet frequencies, and strip convexity gives a centered mean to the
right of the line. An explicit Littlewood argument includes both horizontal
boundaries and the zero-count denominator. We also give the elementary
$4.2/c$ bound for every $c>0$ on the $T^{2/5}$ intervals.
The classical $27/82$ short-interval mechanism is not new;
the contribution is the explicit bound and starting height. The result allows
individual off-line zeros and does not establish the Riemann hypothesis.
