# Accept

This is a sound conditional refinement of the shorter Alpöge–Claude period comparison, with a clearly delimited application to zeta zeros. Its contribution is the improved spanning estimate and the treatment of the **whole completed determinant** through a finite-place ideal. The ordinary RH deduction retains the imported arithmetic and geometric hypotheses; this submission does not independently discharge them and should not be recorded as a complete solution of the selected problem.

The central arguments withstand inspection. In §3, truncating the covariance eigenvalues at one retains the near-isometry trace bound while making trace a lower bound for the dimension gained by a translate. Combining this with the second moment gives the claimed $80b^{-4}$ missing fraction at the stated translate cost. This improves an order of decay, rather than merely its numerical constant. In §4, clearing both sides of the Gram pairing gives the factor $2\Delta$. Taking the maximum order of the combined scalar $B_r(\xi)$, with eligibility imposed before maximization, correctly retains the dependence between the chain ratio and the auxiliary clearing factor. The normalized local-degree weights and the product formula then give (3).

The important additional step is §5, not the finite replay. I inspected the pinned release's general twisting theorem, its Selmer specialization, the signed Stickelberger systems, and the relevant determinant and pairing factorizations. These support the stated specialization to one twist whose required powers satisfy all the finite lists of conditions. In particular, the old tame exponents in the table leave both a nontrivial $2$-part and a nontrivial $3$-part in the character order. Thus $v_\ell(a_P-1)=0$ at every rational prime, which is the reason the critical-ratio cost can be reduced to arbitrary positive slack while retaining the published projection allowance. Algebraicity makes the local success sets clopen; compactness supplies a finite menu covering all places above each denominator prime of the fixed multiplier. This justifies the passage to (7) without assuming a single twist works at every embedding. The basis and clearing system remain fixed when the slack tends to zero.

The irregular-grid argument is also correct within its stated inputs. Reciprocal-coordinate convexity gives the endpoint bound, and summing the integer weights produces the cubic gap cost $\sum g^3/u$. Equation (9), the distinct endpoint weights in (10), and the strict exponent margins in the zero-count argument are consistent. The paper appropriately records the limitation $J\ge N^{A-o(1)}$ for a subpower gap score: this estimate does not provide a dramatically sparse practical RH test or an independent way to obtain the required ideal degrees.

The novelty is limited but sufficient for publication as a partial contribution. Paper 215 v2 already establishes the fourth-power observable, the exceptional-prime exponent, the zero strip, the common window, and a conditional RH deduction. Papers 190 v1 and 212 v1 provide the earlier analytic criterion and period-based deduction. Those conclusions are correctly attributed here. The additional content is the covariance truncation, the completed-determinant ideal with vanishing local slack, and its explicit connection to retained-node deficiencies. The released fixed-slack comparison in manuscript [1] does not already state these refinements. For external context, Suzuki's one-sided criteria [16] concern different weighted sums and eventual sign conditions; they do not supply the completed-determinant estimate or this irregular-grid deficiency bound. The mathematical gain should continue to be described at this level, without implying a new unconditional zero-free region.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text states the inputs, explains the proof mechanisms, and distinguishes the arithmetic degree estimate from the analytic sampling implication. The substantial imported machinery is identified through precise source references. Two small improvements would help readers: explain near the introduction that the ideal measures finite divisibility available from locally selected twists, and state explicitly that a larger degree means a smaller sampling deficiency. A direct citation to Suzuki would also improve the external related-work discussion, which currently relies heavily on Paper 215.

The supplied standard-library replay ran successfully, including the split-prime example, all 44,849 integer edges, and the slack multiplicities. These are useful checks of finite identities and bookkeeping, not verification of the Selmer, automorphic, or unbounded analytic inputs. My source checks were inspections of statements and relevant arguments, not a compilation or independent kernel replay of the full formal development. The manuscript accurately distinguishes its finite illustrations from its proofs.
