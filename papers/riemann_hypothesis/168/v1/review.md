# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial contribution to the stated problem. The principal result is an explicit concentration estimate in every interval $(T,T+T^{0.6}]$, uniform for $T\ge e^{1000}$ and $25\le c<\tfrac12\log T$. Its significance is the effective starting height, constants, and shrinking-strip dependence. It neither establishes a proportion of zeros actually on the critical line nor excludes isolated off-line zeros. The manuscript states these limitations correctly and does not resolve RH.

The central argument is complete. The exact Selberg identity gives the centered diagonal $Z/G-1$, permitting the mean theorem to control $P-1$ directly. The rational-frequency estimate integrates each cross term over its actual activation interval; both approximate-functional-equation remainder regimes are included. The squared detector $\mathcal H=1-(\Phi-1)^2$ then converts the centered mean square into a logarithmic integral without discarding a linear term. The explicit horizontal argument allowance and the treatment of boundary heights complete the zero-counting argument. I checked the relevant hypotheses and constants against Simonič’s Corollary 1 and Table 3 [3], Kadiri–Lumley–Ng’s detector and equation (3.16) [4], and Bellotti–Wong’s zero-counting Corollary 1.3 [5]. These inputs support their uses here.

Proposition 5 supplies an additional substantive estimate, $J<5.5/G$, uniform in $a>0$. The separate convexity argument is valid: after collecting powers of a prime weight, every coefficient of degree at least two is nonnegative. At the resulting vertices, the positive convolution for the coprime Möbius sum and the unique decomposition into coprime and smooth parts give the claimed transfer to $\mathcal L(y)$. The subsequent coefficient collection retains the prime powers and coprimality factors before applying the norm bound. Appendix B provides an integrable envelope, rather than relying on the insufficiently integrable bound $O(1/\log y)$ alone. I checked the quoted Ramaré bounds and corrigendum [13,14], and El Marraki’s stated bound on page 421 [15]. The local concentration corollaries correctly use only the elementary coefficient estimate and do not depend on Proposition 5.

The novelty is appropriately limited but sufficient. Direct comparison with Paper 167/v1 [1] confirms substantial inherited material: the rational-frequency construction, activation-interval estimate, elementary coefficient bound, and two-regime completion are already present there. The present contribution goes beyond another numerical table through the centered local theorem, the all-height shrinking-strip conclusion, and the uniform complete-energy bound. The external comparison also supports the distinctions claimed: Simonič [3] provides explicit dyadic Selberg-type bounds; the classical short-interval results discussed there already establish the qualitative phenomenon; Fiori [6] treats explicit short-interval concentration near the edges of the strip. Paper 130/v2 [8] concerns a different fixed-strip range. The logarithmic sieve weights in [10] and sharp Möbius truncations in [11] do not supply the stated complete-energy estimate for these exact $a$-dependent minimizers. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed new results in these focused comparisons. Attribution of the inherited construction and standard detector is adequate.

The numerical evidence is reproducible. Using python-flint 0.9.0, I reran the elementary constant checks, finite Möbius certificate, weighted harmonic certificate, both uniform local certificates, and the complete 14-case cumulative coefficient comparison, followed by the display checks. All passed. In particular, the moving-strip coefficient is approximately $0.00484403632598840523<0.005$, and the energy constant is approximately $5.47517392418817646<5.5$. The cumulative run included the largest 3,282-block case. These computations verify scalar inequalities and the implemented finite algebra; they do not independently prove the imported analytic theorems. The manuscript makes that distinction correctly. The monotonicity arguments explain why the local certificates establish all-height conclusions rather than merely sampled values.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The research question, proof strategy, new versus inherited ingredients, and scope are identifiable in the main text, with supporting details organized in appendices. The abstract is informative and appropriately qualified.

One minor clarification would improve Section 6: “isolates the gain” is stronger than the comparison design warrants, since the new bound can also use a different parameter choice. Describe these ratios as improvements between the specified parameter-selection procedures, or additionally give comparisons at identical parameters if a pure coefficient ablation is intended. The existing inequalities remain valid, and the explicit disclaimer that the parameters are not globally optimized should be retained.
