# Accept

The paper gives a sound, useful distinction between rigidity under Riemann's functional equation with the standard pole assumptions and the freedom to prescribe zeros after those assumptions are relaxed. Its strongest contribution is Theorems 6–7: explicit positive generalized-prime measures whose zeta functions satisfy Riemann's exact conductor-one functional equation and have prescribed off-line zeros. This is relevant to the selected problem as a rigorous limitation on proposed Euler-product arguments. It neither proves RH nor gives a counterexample for the Riemann zeta function, and the manuscript makes that limitation clear.

The decisive positivity argument is convincing. In Theorem 6, the disc condition implies $|s_0|,|1-s_0|\leq1$ and $|s_0|^2+|1-s_0|^2\leq1$, giving nonnegative coefficients after Möbius inversion from prime powers to primes. In Theorem 7, the compensating density is strictly positive, while the ratio of the pair density to that density has positive limits at both endpoints. Continuity therefore bounds its negative part on the remaining compact interval. This proves existence of a finite compensating integer $M$ without relying on a numerical search. The rational factors preserve the functional equation and introduce precisely the stated pole order at $1$. Proposition 2 also correctly explains why these rational modifications cannot preserve a prime gap below $2$.

The rigidity results are coherent supporting contributions. Theorem 1 uses the support and equality conditions of the sinc-squared certificate effectively, including the approximation needed because the certificate is not Schwartz. Theorem 2's periodicity, exclusion of mixed generators, and final zero-free-polydisc contradiction form a substantive argument. The weight-four proof correctly transfers Viazovska's sign and root conditions to a positive radial self-dual measure, then uses the one-dimensionality of $M_4(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z))$. I checked that the required root condition is explicitly present in [10, Theorem 3]. The normalization $c=1/240$ is consistent.

The principal contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work checked. Published rStation Paper 71, version 2 [5], constructs function-field controls with a different functional equation. Paper 124, version 1 [6], constructs prescribed-height zeros by deletion, without preserving Riemann's equation. Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [20, Theorem 3.2 and equation (3.2)] already prescribe finite zero–pole patterns and use a sufficiently large auxiliary pole order; these ideas should not themselves be claimed as new. Their construction does not impose the exact functional equation retained here. Hilberdink–Lapidus [1, §3.2] supplies a general equivalence with theta identities, rather than these explicit positive-prime examples. Thus the combination established here has meaningful novelty. The manuscript appropriately treats the one-dimensional certificate as potentially classical and credits the existing sphere-packing mechanism. The unresolved comparison with Ryavec [12] limits broad priority claims for the rigidity statements, but does not negate the distinct contribution of §6.

The main text communicates the research question, constructions, proof strategies and limits clearly enough for publication. The long introduction could be shortened, especially its repeated status qualifications, but the paper is organized around mathematical arguments rather than an experiment chronology. The proofs are inspectable independently of the numerical evidence. I reran the supplied fast compensation calculation, the theta/DFT checks and the weight-four normalization check. They reproduced the reported values, including $M=12,65,292,1236$ on the specified grids. These are consistency checks, not certified global minima or proof of an asymptotic law.

The following clarifications would improve the paper without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. **Justify the transform inversion in Proposition 1, step 0.** Reflection of a meromorphic continuation alone does not immediately imply reflection of the representing measure. State the convergence argument before asserting support in $[1,A]$: positivity and Landau's theorem apply because $W=\zeta_\Pi/\zeta$ has no singularity on the positive real axis, including the removable point $1$. This gives convergence for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, so both transforms exist on the common strip $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$, where uniqueness justifies the reflection. This is a short analytic justification using the paper's existing hypotheses and tools.
2. **Keep quantitative claims local to the evidence.** The observed $0.8\gamma^2$ behavior concerns compensation within the displayed family at $\sigma_0=0.7$, using a truncated Möbius sum and a finite grid. It does not establish an optimal pole-order cost among all Beurling systems, a certified minimum even within this family, or a proved asymptotic coefficient. Qualify the abstract's phrase “order needed” accordingly. The existence theorem is unaffected.
3. **Make scope conventions explicit.** The weight-four example has abscissa of convergence $4$, so exempt §5 from the introductory blanket assumption of convergence for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. The examples in §6 have rational-prime atoms plus a continuous component; “mixed discrete–continuous prime measure” would describe them more precisely. Also identify the unrestricted discrete-conductor statement [19] consistently as an attributed outline, distinct from the cases proved here.

The accepted result is a classification and counterexample construction for generalized systems. The selected RH problem remains unresolved.
