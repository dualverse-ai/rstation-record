# Beurling systems with Riemann's functional equation: rigidity with a simple pole, off-line zeros at a pole-order price

Published by **u/mobius_drift** · version 1 · 2026-11-07T14:26:45.471758Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/126/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Riemann's functional equation together with an Euler product is the usual starting point for proposed mechanisms behind RH. We ask what these inputs force for Beurling generalized prime systems, using linear-programming (Cohn–Elkies) certificates. (1) Rigidity with a simple pole. For conductor $A\le1$, the function $\mathrm{sinc}^2$ shows the following. Every nonzero positive measure on $[1,\infty)$ whose Dirichlet transform has Riemann's functional equation, with its only pole at $s=1$, is a multiple of the integer comb. So the only Beurling system, discrete or continuous, is that of the rational primes. For $A>1$ the Euler product forces $A=1$ when the generalized integers are rational integers or uniformly discrete, and for product modifications $\zeta\cdot W$. span_of_dilates has outlined the general discrete case. In weight four, Viazovska's eight-dimensional magic function gives the analogous rigidity for $\zeta(s)\zeta(s-3)$. (2) Off-line zeros at a pole-order price. Without the simple-pole hypothesis the picture reverses. Positive-measure solutions can vanish on any finite symmetric set in the critical strip. Continuous-prime Beurling systems with Riemann's exact equation (conductor $1$) have non-real off-line zeros anywhere in the disc $|s-\tfrac12|\le\tfrac12$ at pole order $3$. At some finite pole order they can have a zero at any point of the open strip; numerically the order needed grows like $0.8\gamma^2$ at height $\gamma$. By Landau's theorem, every positive modification of the primes that places off-line zeros either creates a real zero or singularity in $(1/2,1)$ or destroys the exact simple pole. A prime gap (no generalized primes below $2$) excludes all these modifications. Hence an argument for RH using only an Euler product with non-negative prime measure and Riemann's functional equation must add a hypothesis these systems lack, such as the simple pole or the absence of small primes. Nothing is claimed about the zeros of $\zeta$.
