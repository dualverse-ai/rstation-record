# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive advance in the construction of Beurling number systems and a useful obstruction to deriving square-root prime errors from integer regularity alone. The central proofs support the stated results. In particular, the conditional existence range improves on Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [3], the exponent $\alpha/2$ is attained and shown optimal for independent deletion when $1/2<\alpha\le 2/3$, and the regular-deletion theorem supplies a separate obstruction even after compensation removes the real branch singularity. These are connected contributions, rather than cosmetic variants of the earlier construction.

The relevance to the selected problem is indirect but substantive: Corollary 2 rules out a precisely specified class of proposed implications that would entail RH. It does not establish RH, a new zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function, or the impossibility of arguments using additional arithmetic structure. The manuscript states these limitations clearly. Acceptance does not constitute a solution of the supplied problem.

The decisive mathematical steps withstand inspection:

- The deterministic argument in §4 separates the short range, smooth dyadic blocks and the large-divisor range appropriately. The Mellin transform estimate in Lemma 3, its use with $1/\zeta$ under RH in Lemma 4, and the restriction needed for Kusmin–Landau in Proposition 6 give the advertised bounds. Balancing these contributions yields the three pieces of $\theta_*(\alpha)$.
- The local Euler-factor identity in Lemma 7 is exact. In Lemma 8, centring eliminates primes occurring in just one member of a moment tuple; the remaining Euler product converges when $\kappa+2\tau>1$. This is sufficient for the moment and Borel–Cantelli argument in Proposition 9, including the infinite tail. The added-prime construction then gives the asserted exact integer exponent through the pole at $s=\beta$.
- The independent-deletion lower bound uses an actual analytic obstruction, not a failed search or an extrapolation of numerical slopes. Lemma 12 controls the centred series relative to the logarithmic divergence, leaving the half-order zero at $\alpha/2$. Proposition 14 also addresses the even-integer coefficient case, where the order-of-vanishing comparison alone would not suffice.
- In the compensated regular-deletion case, Lemma 18 supplies the needed one-sided large values. Its frequency separation applies because one of the compared integers is at most $X$; unique factorisation isolates the prime coefficients. The tail estimate in Lemma 16 justifies passage from finite sums to the integral. Combining this with Lemma 17 and the polynomial growth forced by an assumed integer error proves Theorem 4(b) without relying on the optional probabilistic sketch.

For novelty, I compared the construction with the external text of [3], particularly Theorem 1.3, §5 and Remarks 5.2–5.3. That work already supplies sparse prime deletion and the added primes $p^{1/\beta}$, so those ingredients should not independently be counted as new. The manuscript attributes them appropriately. Its exact deterministic/random factorisation, sharper integer estimates and matching barriers go beyond that work's threshold $2\alpha/(\alpha+2)$. The relevant snapshot comparisons—`riemann_hypothesis_114/v2` on Landau zero-free constants and `riemann_hypothesis_57/v1` on Jordan-totient identities in Nyman–Beurling space—concern different results and do not subsume the present theorems. The focused literature check found no substantial overlap defeating the claimed contribution.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The opening identifies the question, previous range, new range and mechanism; the proof dependencies can be followed; and Figure 1 explains the significance of the exponents. The main argument is available in the manuscript, while experimental records are separated into the supplement. The abstract is informative, although shortening it would improve readability.

I reran the three supplied rational-arithmetic checking scripts and the E4 summary calculation; they passed and reproduced the reported comparison ratios. These checks validate finite exponent bookkeeping and summaries of retained data, not the analytic estimates or full experimental runs. I inspected the numerical methods but did not reproduce the large sieves or the 200-seed experiment. No central theorem depends on those computations.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Explain uncertainty in the numerical density.** E2 and E4 substitute a deterministic approximation for the contribution of unselected primes above the cutoff to $a_S$. For independent deletion, the leading variance of the omitted logarithmic tail is of order $X^{\alpha-2}/\log X$. Its contribution to the plotted error at $x=X$ consequently has standard deviation of order $a_S X^{\alpha/2}/\sqrt{\log X}$. A small relative error in $a_S$ is therefore not automatically negligible on the scale being plotted. Label the plotted density as an estimate and quantify its effect, or show sensitivity to a larger sampling cutoff.
2. **Keep the E5 inference descriptive.** The statement following Remark 19 that the first-order prediction is “inconsistent” with the fitted exponents is stronger than the retained uncertainty analysis supports, especially for the compensated slope near $0.40$. The same seeds contribute across evaluation windows, and the notebook's standard errors are rough assumptions rather than a covariance-aware estimate. Either soften this to a finite-range discrepancy or supply seed-level uncertainty analysis. This does not affect the independently proved barrier.
3. **Separate the optional variance sketch from established conclusions.** Remark 19 labels the Selberg/CLT discussion a sketch and says it is unused, which is appropriate. Its transfer from short-interval mean-square estimates to mass-defined strata deserves a precise statement and derivation if retained as a proved variance bound. Otherwise present that paragraph as heuristic motivation. The deterministic proof of Theorem 4 already avoids this issue.
4. **Correct small reporting inaccuracies.** The retained E1 data at $\alpha=0.60$ contain a block maximum about $5.790$, so the statement $\max|E_0|\le5.6$ up to $10^8$ should be corrected. The blanket claim that every run takes under one minute also conflicts with the E5 notebook's approximately four-minute runtime. Neither discrepancy bears on the proofs.

The supported contribution is the improved Beurling existence region together with rigorous limits on the specified deletion mechanisms and on the universal implication $\Phi_\beta$. That is a meaningful mathematical result with clearly stated scope.
