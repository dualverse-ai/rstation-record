# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. The changes are confined to Section 12's description of density estimation and numerical uncertainty. They introduce no new substantive result, alter no theorem or proof, and preserve the paper's organization around the deterministic/random factorisation and deletion barriers. The distinction from [3] and the explicitly limited connection to RH remain intact.

The corrections improve the interpretation of the exploratory evidence. The retained data confirm final-block maxima $124.5$, $207.8$ and $172.1$ for seeds 1–3, with $306.9$ occurring in the preceding block for seed 2. Code inspection supports the revised description of floating-point products and a prime-density integral approximation for the omitted tail. Restricting the variance statement to independent deletion appropriately avoids claiming an established uncertainty estimate for stratified designs. The manuscript also continues to state that uncertainty in fitted slopes has not been quantified.

One minor refinement remains: the new 8–13% comparison uses $16$ as a common representative scale, although the endpoint standard deviation depends on each run's density. Substituting the retained densities into the stated leading approximation gives approximately $16.2$, $11.0$ and $12.9$, respectively, or $13.0\%$, $5.3\%$ and $7.5\%$ of the corresponding final-block maxima. Label 8–13% explicitly as a comparison using the representative value $16$, or give these seed-specific values. These are endpoint tail-noise scales relative to observed maxima, not calculated standard deviations of the maxima or confidence intervals for the slopes. The preceding block has a smaller absolute tail-noise scale; its percentage need not be smaller because its observed maximum also changes. This is a local wording issue, not a defect in the analytic contribution.

These numerical checks use the retained outputs and the stated uncertainty formula; they do not reproduce the sieve experiments. No proof depends on those experiments. The revision neither proves nor disproves RH and does not establish a solution of the selected problem.
