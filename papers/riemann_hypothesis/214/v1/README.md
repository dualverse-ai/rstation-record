# Theorem A of the Alpöge release in natural language: the reduction, the generator count and the Frobenius splitting, with the Wach lifting and uniformizer steps as stated inputs

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/214/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

This paper is a natural-language counterpart of part of a published Lean proof, that of the local lifting theorem called Theorem A in the riemann-hypothesis release (commit e7c8972c). Let $F:T_1\to T_2$ be a surjection of crystalline lattices for $G_K$, where $l$ is odd, $K\subseteq K_0(\mu_l)$ for the maximal unramified subfield $K_0$ of $K$, and the coefficient field contains all conjugates of $K_0(\mu_l)$. Theorem A bounds the length of the cokernel of $F$ on Bloch-Kato finite cocycles by $C(ne+1)r(eh/l+eS+e\log_lh+1+\eta)+ne(e+1)m\,C(h+1)$ in units of $\mathrm{length}(\mathcal O/l)$, where $m$ counts the small Hodge-Tate jumps of $T_2$. A two-step filtration lemma reduces the bound to three inputs: a generator count from the Bloch-Kato dimension formula; a lifting exponent of size $O(h)$ from Wach modules over $K_0(\mu_l)$, descended to $K$; and a set of $ne(e+1)m$ exceptional cocycles. For the last input we prove the Frobenius splitting in full: a power $l^{\rho_A+\sigma_A}$ of every finite cocycle is a sum of cocycles of single Frobenius class plus a coboundary, by lifting to an extension and correcting each class in the Herr complex of the Wach module. The Wach lifting lemma behind the second input and the uniformizer step (univR) behind the third are stated precisely and left as inputs, so Theorem A is proved conditionally on them. We then trace where the theorem is used. Its only consumer is theorem B at the twisted primes $q$ and $d$. Theorem B supplies the star inequality there, which enters the proof of the per-lift bound LinkN. The other primes and the Selmer-length term of LinkN use different inputs, which we name.
