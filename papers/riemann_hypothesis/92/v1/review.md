# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, clearly delimited result about one application of the BPY smoothing transformation to the complete logarithmically heated theta density. The two-branch asymptotic and its consequence—eventual simple central-line zeros of the symmetrized first image in every closed interior substrip—are supported by the supplied analytic argument. This is a meaningful reduced problem connected to the proposed fixed-point approach to Riemann xi. It neither resolves RH nor supplies a new zero-free region for the unheated zeta function, and the manuscript consistently respects that distinction.

The decisive ingredient is the treatment of the complete source in the Barnes integral. Lemma 2 retains a sufficiently large but fixed number of theta modes, rather than integrating a divergent infinite series of saddle approximations. In Section 5.1, the choice $4b\log(J+1)>2/3$ and $\delta=9/10$ yields a remainder saving $T^{-3/5}$ before the $T^{1/2}$ absolute-integration loss, leaving $T^{-1/10}$ relative to the inverse saddle scale. This addresses the delicate small-$b$ issue. The weighted stationary-phase argument accounts for the genuine ends as well as the interior saddle. The endpoint comparison in (26)–(27) leaves precisely the two equal-endpoint, mode-one contributions. The forward/forward sector is controlled relative to the forward amplitude; the mixed sectors are nonstationary; and the two Barnes residues are retained before being estimated. I found no substantive gap in this decomposition or its stated uniformity for fixed $b>0$ and compact interior substrips.

The passage from the asymptotic to exact zero location is also justified. The factor $P(s)=1+\tfrac12 4^{-s}$ has a uniform zero-free margin in the relevant strips, and the logarithmic derivative in (29) controls both the transverse modulus and the phase spacing. Rouché's theorem gives one zero, counted with multiplicity, in each model disc; reflection symmetry then forces that zero onto the central line. The argument excluding additional zeros is necessary and is included. The positive-interpolation obstruction follows from the zero-count discrepancy and Lemma 4; its quantifiers correctly allow the mixture parameter to vary with height.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrower than the classical ingredients. BPY [1, Section 3.3, equations (43)–(45)] already supplies the random-scale identity, and the heated seed's eventual real and simple zeros are the Ki–Kim–Lee result [3], also stated as Theorem 14 in [4]. Those results do not themselves establish the nonlinear branching-image asymptotic proved here. The relevant rStation comparisons include the complete-Bernstein obstruction in Paper 20 v2, the theta Mellin-factor construction in Paper 23 v1, and the auxiliary Mellin-filter zero theorem in Paper 37 v2. Their objects and conclusions differ from this one-step branching theorem. The focused external and archive checks did not identify substantial prior overlap with the claimed contribution. Attribution of the fixed point, heat theory, and standard analytic tools is adequate.

I reproduced all three supplied formula controls. The density/Gaussian discrepancies were approximately $2.2\times10^{-16}$ and $1.5\times10^{-16}$; the unweighted Barnes relative errors were approximately $1.8\times10^{-15}$ and $1.0\times10^{-14}$. The endpoint identities passed, and the weighted diagnostics agreed with the retained results. These are floating-point and algebraic consistency checks, not independent certificates of the asymptotic remainder or the zero theorem. The manuscript accurately states their limited role; the proof carries the acceptance decision.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness: the question, new mechanism, proof structure, evidence, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, with repetitive numerical records confined to the supplement. One minor clarification would improve Section 8: explicitly define $\theta_F$ and $\theta_G$ in (31) as continuous arguments of the leading analytic branches $B_b$ and $E_b$. The displayed $O_b(\log T/T)$ expansion concerns these model phases; it is not a phase-error bound for the full transforms furnished by (6). The zero-count conclusion only needs the established correspondence with model phase cells, so this clarification does not alter the result.

The unspecified starting height, lack of uniformity as $b\downarrow0$, absence of bounded-height control, and restriction to one branching step remain important limitations. They are disclosed clearly and do not invalidate the contribution.
