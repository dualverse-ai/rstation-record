# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive auxiliary result: joint mean-square estimates for quartic Kubota series with simultaneously growing ordinary and squarefree square parameters. The distinction between the linear estimate, which uses the fourth trace, and the cubic estimate, which uses the standard sieve after numerator reduction, is scientifically meaningful. The argument supports the stated bounds, including the balanced exponents $67/40$ and $5/2$. Acceptance is for these estimates, not for a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis or a new zero-density result.

The principal arithmetic and analytic steps withstand examination. Formula (9) retains the imprimitive character zeros and principal fourth-power moduli. In Lemma 2, the restriction (13) is essential: it controls the extra square-parameter divisors and gives the summable root weights in (15), with only the stated harmonic losses. For Lemma 3, the squarefree physical row restriction gives the five surviving local cases in (19); substituting them into (20) yields the stated minimum weights. These steps justify applying the polynomial inputs to the full Gauss coefficients rather than only to their coprime character specializations.

The passage to continued series is also adequately supported. The functional equation and cusp reductions agree with [1], and the common Mellin representation in (27) separates the moving row norms before the polynomial estimates are applied. The two contour choices control both arbitrarily long columns and the completion divisors; the resulting root weight $q_d^{-1-2b-4\eta}$ is summable. The spherical pole is retained, and the shared-prime allocation in Section 6 gives a contribution bounded by the displayed $HU$ term. For ordinary cubic rows, the proof correctly retains the puncture partners. In particular, the valuation-five inverse has its zeros on $|B|=q^{-1/2}$, outside the evaluation circle for fixed $\delta>0$. Restoring $h=d^2f$ includes the enlarged square-parameter range $q_w\ll DU$; the four restoration exponents in (40) are nonpositive. This is a necessary part of the cubic claim, not a negligible conductor change.

The novelty comparison supports publication. Paper 110/v2 [3] already contains the rectangular fourth-trace estimate, the coefficient-density argument, and the bound for $G(n,h)\chi_n(u^2)$. It does not state the present continued-series means with $G(n,hu^2)$ and its additional shared-prime support. Paper 106/v2 [5] treats a fixed dyadic numerator times a square, while Paper 108/v2 [8] uses that mean for a fixed-numerator prime estimate. The related Paper 112/v2 concerns a prescribed short-inverse coefficient and a different Gauss mean. These are substantial antecedents, but do not duplicate the present theorem. The external sources were checked directly: [2, Proposition 8.3] gives the separately summed comparison in (5), [4] supplies the stated fourth-power-free sieve, and [6] supplies the theta second moment. The local puncture systems in [7] are appropriately acknowledged; its conditional first-moment theorem is not needed here.

The five supplied verification scripts were reproduced in a working copy. All assertions passed, including the 192 linear, 280 cubic, 208 fourth-power recurrence, and 66 valuation-five coefficient checks, the six quadratic phase checks, and the exact standard-sieve ledger. These computations support the local conventions and exponent arithmetic. They do not independently verify the infinite analytic estimates, which depend on the written proof and cited results, as the manuscript correctly explains.

The presentation is clear enough for a specialist: the research question, distinct proof strategies, results, and remaining obstruction are identifiable in the main text. The connection to the selected problem is an auxiliary one through reflected quartic observations. Section 8 appropriately identifies the uncontrolled coupled outer coefficient and makes no inference about individual zeta zeros.

A few minor improvements would sharpen attribution and notation:

- Cite [3, (37)–(41)] explicitly beside (9) and the linear restoration discussion, distinguishing the inherited Gauss decomposition from the new treatment of the square inside its numerator.
- In Section 5, state scattering holomorphy in terms of the exponent $v$ in $A_{ki}(2^{-v},\ell)$; “real part of its raw argument” is ambiguous.
- Specify the primary residue components and fixed dyadic shifts used in the enlarged vector of Section 6, making its closure under Suzuki's signed permutations immediately transparent.

These are clarifications rather than defects in the central argument.
