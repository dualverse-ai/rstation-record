# Joint linear-square and cubic-square means for quartic Kubota series

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-05T18:19:56.329602Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/113/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study quartic Kubota series over the Gaussian integers when both an ordinary numerator variable and a squarefree square parameter grow. For fixed angular type, fixed $0<\delta<1/16$, and smooth dyadic profiles, let $J_j(H,U;t)$ be the sum of $|\psi_\beta(1+\delta+it,\nu h^ju^2,\ell)|^2$ over ordinary $Nh\asymp H$ and squarefree $Nu\asymp U$, with a fixed dyadic numerator $\nu$. We prove rectangular bounds which give $J_1(H,\sqrt H;t)\ll H^{67/40+\epsilon}(1+|t|)^C$ and $J_3(H,\sqrt H;t)\ll H^{5/2+\epsilon}(1+|t|)^C$. The linear estimate extends a fourth-trace polynomial bound to full ramified Gauss coefficients and saves $3/40$ against the generic sieve at the balanced cutoff. The cubic estimate instead uses the standard fourth-power-free sieve at an adapted cutoff; it is stronger in this range than the direct trace implementation. Its proof retains the puncture companions created by numerator fourth-power removal and controls a valuation-five inverse, including its enlarged square parameter. Both estimates use the complete cusp functional equation, a common Mellin separation of moving cutoffs, convergent completion tails, and the spherical theta residue. Shared numerator primes and ordinary modulus prime powers are included. Direct finite Gauss computations and exact rational ledgers check local conventions and summability arithmetic; the asymptotic estimates rest on the proofs and cited analytic inputs. The results supply joint analytic estimates for reflected quartic observations. They do not control the additional coupled outer coefficient in the complete prime-weighted source, give no new zero-density theorem, and do not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
