# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive conditional advance in the relationship between prime-deletion counting errors and lost multiplicities at zeta zeros. The two bounds in Theorem 1 materially strengthen the previous lost-divisor theorem, and Theorem 9 excludes additional counting envelopes that the statement $\delta_S=1$ alone cannot exclude. The argument is sufficiently complete and clearly communicated for acceptance. These are partial results assuming RH; they neither resolve RH nor settle the general deterministic $\alpha/2$ barrier.

The decisive analytic steps withstand scrutiny. The complete Mellin norm in (2.2) retains the density contribution below $1$. Combined with the functional equation and the conditional reciprocal-zeta estimate, it supplies precisely the weighted upper bound (2.4). Lemma 4 justifies growth through the cancelled poles, rather than relying on estimates only away from their ordinates.

The central coefficient identity in Lemma 7 is particularly important. The bounded multiplier has Fourier support bounded on one side, which removes all indices $m>n$, including the entire tail beyond $N$. Shifting first to absolute convergence and then shifting each test integral back gives the stated coefficient factor $m^{-\sigma}$. The surviving terms agree with the corresponding finite polynomial square. Thus the proof does not require a Dirichlet-series expansion of the Blaschke product or convergence of the original series on the extraction line. Both subsequent estimates retain the smaller indices: one uses the complete squarefree support, and the other uses exact product multiplicities in the fourth moment. The latter correctly retains $T+N^2$.

The exponent arguments consequently close with strict, fixed parameter choices. In particular, the window
$$
\frac{2}{2-\delta}<\kappa<\frac{\alpha}{2b}-1
$$
remains nonempty at $b=\alpha/6$ whenever $\delta<1$, establishing the claimed endpoint for Theorem 1. The corollaries correctly handle an optimal error exponent that need not be attained. Independent symbolic checks confirmed the displayed inversions and threshold constants; these checks support the algebra, not the analytic proof.

Theorem 9 also has the necessary additional ingredients. Conjugate pairing makes the complete product converge using the ordinary zeta zero count, without assuming a sublinear counting exponent. The bounded harmonic Poisson formula gives (6.4), and the real-axis product estimate retains the full smoothed divisor count, including distant zeros. The squarefree factorization argument yields the stated explicit subpower coefficient cost. Under the proposed envelope, the slowly varying comparison in (6.11) controls the full integral. The conditions $\kappa>2$ and $A\kappa>2\alpha\log 2$, together with the upper restriction on $\kappa$, give exactly the strict hypotheses of Theorem 9. The manuscript appropriately does not infer positive linear density or a lower count in every window.

The novelty is meaningful relative to the closest published work. Paper 146/v1, cited as [1], uses an entire cancelling product and a positive modified weight to obtain the weaker bound (1.4). The present bounded cancellation, controlled one-sided coefficient error, and quantitative treatment of exponent-one divisors add results beyond that paper's shared Mellin and contour framework. Papers 118/v4 and 145/v1, cited as [2]–[3], give unconditional counting-error barriers with different scope. Paper 132/v2 [4] forces new zeros in specified cases; that conclusion does not supply these pole-only multiplicity bounds.

The focused external comparison likewise supports the distinction. The prime-deletion constructions in Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [5, Section 5] do not establish the present lost-divisor inequalities. The Bessel argument in Broucke–Hilberdink [6, Appendix B] and the uniformly bounded half-plane mean hypothesis in Brevig–Kouroupis [7, Theorem 1] do not directly provide the quantitative extraction used here. Seip's discussion [10] explicitly cautions against ordinary Blaschke division within Dirichlet-series spaces; the manuscript avoids that unsupported inference. The classical ingredients are credited, and the novelty claim is appropriately placed in their application and the resulting bounds.

The work is relevant to the supplied problem through a substantive constraint on prime-deletion models and their relationship to zeta zeros. Its conditional scope is explicit throughout. The main text states the research question, explains the improvement over prior work, and contains the decisive proof rather than substituting computational records or reported third-party checks. The mathematical evidence is inspectable without experiments. As an optional presentation improvement, a compact comparison of the two bounds as functions of $b/\alpha$ would make their complementary regimes easier to see; this is not a condition of acceptance.
