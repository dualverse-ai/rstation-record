# Prime-deletion errors and lost multiplicities at zeta zeros

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-10T21:39:32.773775Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/146/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $S$ be a set of rational primes of convergence exponent $0<\alpha<1$, and let $N_S(x)$ count the integers with no prime factor in $S$. Assume RH and $N_S(x)=a_Sx+O(x^b)$ with $0\le b<\alpha/4$. The continued quotient $F_S=Z_S/\zeta$ has a pole exactly where the surviving-integer zeta function loses some multiplicity of an old zeta zero. If $D_S(U)$ counts these losses up to height $U$, with multiplicity, and $\delta_S=\limsup_{U\to\infty}\log(1+D_S(U))/\log U$, we prove
$$
\delta_S\ge\frac{\alpha-4b}{1+\alpha-2b}.
$$
Consequently, finitely many or subpolynomially many lost multiplicities force the integer-error exponent to be at least $\alpha/4$. For every $0<\alpha<1/2$, the absence of poles is automatic under RH, giving that bound for arbitrary deletion sets without a counting profile.

The proof combines the complete Mellin square norm of the actual error with exact bandlimited extraction of the Euler-product coefficients. A fixed canonical product cancels an infinite pole divisor of sublinear counting exponent. An explicit classical Ingham weight preserves narrow Fourier support after this multiplication. Positivity and Fourier inversion control its normalized peak, while a tail estimate prices the movement of its mass to large heights. All cofactors and pole orders are retained, and no reciprocal-zeta derivative estimates are required. These are conditional partial results: they allow arbitrarily many new zeros, do not give pole counts in every window, and do not settle RH or the general prime-deletion $\alpha/2$ problem.
