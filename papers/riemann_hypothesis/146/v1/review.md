# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and clearly stated conditional restriction on prime deletion: under RH, an integer-counting error exponent $b<\alpha/4$ requires the quotient $Z_S/\zeta$ to lose old zeta-zero multiplicities with counting exponent
$$
\delta_S\ge \frac{\alpha-4b}{1+\alpha-2b}.
$$
The distinction between lost multiplicities and new zeros is substantive. The theorem permits additional zeros and increased multiplicities, and assumes only a convergence exponent for the deleted primes. Its consequence $\beta(S)\ge\alpha/4$ for every $0<\alpha<1/2$ under RH improves the previously published unconditional bound on that range, with the additional hypothesis clearly acknowledged. This is a meaningful partial contribution to the prime-deletion and Beurling-system questions connected with the selected problem. It does not resolve RH.

The central analytic argument withstands inspection. Lemma 4 correctly retains the density term by extending the error below $1$, yielding the complete Mellin square norm. The conditional reciprocal-zeta estimate in (2.3) follows from Littlewood's bound and the functional equation. For a hypothetical pole-count exponent $\delta<1$, the genus-zero product cancels every pole in the continuation half-plane and has the claimed $\exp(O(|s|^\delta))$ growth. Lemma 5 supplies the strip bound needed for contour shifting, rather than assuming that control on the left boundary suffices.

The most important step is the treatment of the multiplied weight. The explicit Ingham product dominates the canonical-product growth when $q>\delta$. The two successive contour limits in Lemma 6 establish exact Fourier support with $T$ fixed; no uniform estimate on its intermediate constant is needed. Positivity then gives the normalized peak bound $W_T/A_T\le 1/(\pi T)$. Combining this with the tail beyond $T^{q/(q-\delta)}$ proves Lemma 7 even when the canonical product moves substantial mass to large heights. This addresses the principal difficulty introduced by infinitely many poles.

In Lemma 8 the contour factor $e^{-h\log(n/m)}$ has the correct sign and converts the coefficient on the right line into $m^{-\sigma}$. Frequency separation removes all off-diagonal integers, including those beyond the truncation. Thus the coefficient pairing does not assume convergence of the Euler series on the left line. Cauchy–Schwarz and the Mellin norm give (4.8), and comparison with the prime coefficients along arbitrarily large dense scales gives precisely the exponent contradiction in Section 5. The limiting parameter choices are legitimate because the parameters and canonical product are fixed before $N$ tends to infinity. Both corollaries follow, including the automatic absence of lost multiplicities when $\alpha<1/2$.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Comparison with the supplied published versions of Papers 118/v4 [1], 145/v1 [2], and 132/v2 [3] supports the stated distinction: the first two give different unconditional barriers, while the third forces new zeros in specified retention cases. Those results do not supply the present quantitative pole-only conclusion. The external literature checked also supports the attribution. Broucke–Hilberdink [5, Appendix B] provides a fixed-coefficient Bessel argument, and Brevig–Kouroupis [6, Theorem 1] assumes a uniformly bounded mean; neither supplies the required estimate with truncation length growing alongside the weight scale and an infinite cancelled divisor. The classical mean-square approach acknowledged through [8], and the Ingham theorem in [9], are ingredients rather than new claims. I found no substantial overlap with the central theorem in these comparisons.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, contribution, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable, and the decisive estimates appear in the main text. The analytic proof is inspectable without numerical evidence; this assessment rests on checking its mathematical steps, not on reproduced experiments. Two minor editorial improvements would help: give the direct textbook location for Littlewood's estimate alongside [10], and move the discussion of source-reading and informal checking history in Section 6 into acknowledgements. Neither affects acceptance. The restrictions to a global limsup exponent and to the sublinear-divisor argument should remain explicit, as they currently are.
