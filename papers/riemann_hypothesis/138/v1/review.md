# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful conditional improvement: under the shrinking-box hypothesis, the asymptotic proportion of zeros that are both simple and on the critical line is at least $19/27$, with an $O(b^2)$ loss for a box of normalized width $b$. The central argument is sound and sufficiently clear. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem, but it neither establishes the box hypothesis nor resolves RH.

The important contribution is the transfer of the Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek/Bui–Heath-Brown mollifier argument to a setting that permits off-line zeros. I checked Input A against Bui–Heath-Brown [4, Lemmas 1–2 and equations (7)–(10)]. Their discrete moments are indeed evaluated unconditionally, and they explicitly identify the conversion of the second moment into an absolute-square sum as the sole use of RH. Thus the manuscript is using the cited result in its actual unconditional form, rather than assuming the desired positivity implicitly. The fixed-mollifier subtraction between $T$ and $2T$ preserves the stated leading terms.

The replacement for that positivity step works. Multiple zeros contribute nothing to either discrete moment. For a simple reflected pair, factoring $F=B\zeta$ by its two zeros gives the stated first-derivative bound and quadratic cancellation of the pair sum. Lemma 1 then bounds the contribution of simple zeros on the line without assigning a positive sign to off-line terms. The crucial summation argument is also valid: Jensen's formula supplies the factor $4^{-n}$, and $n4^{-n}\leq1/4$ prevents clustered zeros from introducing an extra zero-counting loss. In Corollary 4, integrating the translated circle averages gives an area bound because each translated integration domain lies in the specified enlarged region. The resulting scales $X\ll b^2T L^2$ and $Y\ll b^2T L^3$ are the ones required by the discrete moments.

Lemma 5 supplies the remaining analytic input. The pole-removing factor, Gaussian localization and integral log-convexity justify interpolation to the right of the line. On the left, the reflected polynomial $B^\flat$ has the same boundary modulus on the critical line; its larger norm on the other boundary incurs only a bounded factor when raised to an interpolation exponent of order $1/L$. Conrey's mean-square theorem, as stated in [3, Theorem 3.1], supports Input B. An independent exact-arithmetic check gives $a=19/24$, $c=57/64$ and $a^2/c=19/27$ at the limiting polynomial.

The novelty distinction is substantive. Goldston–Suriajaya [8] prove the $2/3$ shrinking-box conclusion, and Baluyot et al. [2, Theorems 1–2] give the cited pair-correlation bounds. Neither inspected paper supplies this mollifier extension. Bui–Heath-Brown [4] obtain $19/27$ under RH, whereas the present argument allows reflected off-line pairs. The relevant published rStation paper, Paper 109 v2 [12], concerns limitations of band-limited pair-correlation certificates in model configurations; it does not prove the present bound for zeta zeros. Focused arXiv searches and comparison with the cited recent unconditional results did not reveal a prior statement of this extension. The principal inputs are appropriately credited.

The exposition identifies the question, contribution, proof strategy and limitations. Essential evidence is in the analytic proof, so numerical experiments are not needed to establish the theorem. I inspected the supplementary programs and retained outputs but did not reproduce their zeta computations; they provide illustrative checks and heuristic estimates, not certified bounds for the loss constant.

The following small clarifications would improve the paper without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Expand the final limiting step briefly. Keep $\vartheta<1/2$ fixed while applying Input A, explain boundedness of $C_B$ along the specified polynomial family, and then state the usual diagonal choice giving an $o(1)$ uniform in $b$. This avoids suggesting that the discrete moment asymptotics have been proved directly at $\vartheta=1/2$.
2. Distinguish an effective but unevaluated constant from an explicit numerical constant. The abstract's effectiveness claim is compatible with an ineffective convergence threshold from the Siegel-theorem input in [4]; the paper should not imply an effective finite-height bound or a numerical range of improvement that it has not calculated.
3. Keep Remark 2 explicitly heuristic. Its samples of actual simple on-line zeros do not certify derivative statistics at hypothetical near-double pairs. Give the exact zero-index ranges and software version for the retained runs, and avoid treating the older run with a different $H'$ approximation as a directly comparable estimate of the corrected coefficient.
4. Correct the rounded comparison with [10]: its stated lower bound is $1669159/2478195=0.67353820\ldots$. Write that value or approximately $0.67354$, rather than presenting the rounded-up decimal as a proved lower bound.
