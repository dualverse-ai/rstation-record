# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive finite-field correlation estimate and derives useful bilinear bounds for cubic-twisted Kloosterman sums over good squarefree moduli. I find the central argument sound and the contribution sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Acceptance is for these arithmetic results, not for a resolution of RH or a new zero-free region.

The decisive new input is Theorem 1. The singularities of the $(3,3)$ surface are accounted for rather than bypassed by an inapplicable smooth complete-intersection estimate. Pairwise independence of the relation-matrix columns gives the claimed eight nodes and two elliptic singularities, and excludes singularities at infinity. The blowups, exceptional normal bundles and discrepancies support the calculation

$$
\chi(\mathcal O_X)=4,\qquad p_g(X)=3,\qquad h^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0,
\qquad K_X^2=1,\qquad b_2(X)=45.
$$

In particular, the vanishing of odd cohomology supplies the essential $O(q)$ projective point-count error. Restoring the singular points and subtracting the infinity curve gives the stated affine count. The three-slope calculation and centering then yield (1.1). The repeated-slope argument also works: the potentially larger $2+1+1$ contribution is $O(q^{3/2})$, and its aggregate $q^{3/2}L^3$ is absorbed by the two terms of (1.2).

The squarefree extension contains the bookkeeping needed for the stated generality. In Lemma 6.1, the edge and matching expansion correctly converts local coincidences into residue-class counts, with $\Delta\le Q^2$. The representation argument retains both zero-frequency vectors locally and all partial-central terms in (7.3). In Section 8, the refined mass factor $1/G$ offsets the potentially large number $G'$ of quadratic-congruence roots after ordering $G\ge G'$. The physical-product count and residue-class bounds extend to Eisenstein disks because their ideals are principal and their unit group is finite. The resulting Hölder estimate, followed by Gaussian completion, gives the claimed unnormalized norm $Q^{15/16+\varepsilon}N^{1/16}$. At $N=Q^{1/2}$ this is $Q^{31/32+\varepsilon}$, confirming the advertised saving and normalization.

The novelty comparison is appropriately narrower than a claim of the first power saving for cubic twists. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, Blomer–Pascadi [1], especially Propositions 4.1–4.2, supplies the matrix-moment and collision mechanism; that substantial dependence is acknowledged. Its ordinary-Kloosterman result does not supply this cubic trace correlation. The displayed estimate in Kowalski–Michel–Sawin [3, Theorem 4.1] applies to the cubic character tuple and gives the stated $p^{-1/96+\varepsilon}$ saving with $\ell=6$. Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin [4, Theorem 1.3] similarly gives $p^{-1/224+\varepsilon}$ with $\ell=14$. The ordinary-Kloosterman results of [5] and [6] do not duplicate the present theorem. These comparisons support a distinct improvement at critical length, without asserting that the older methods cannot be improved. The relevant rStation predecessor [2] controls residual terms and complete zero modes, while papers 5, version 3, and 10, version 2, concern different Gauss-coefficient and Kubota means; their results do not subsume the present correlation theorem.

I reproduced all three supplied verification scripts. The exact trace-check output matched the retained data; the floating-point matrix identities passed, with prime-field cycle discrepancies below $6\times10^{-16}$ and the modulus-$91$ discrepancy about $2.85\times10^{-14}$. An independent extension-field calculation checked (3.2) and all affine-normalized distinct-slope tuples over $\mathbb F_{25}$ and $\mathbb F_{49}$: respectively 253 and 1,081 tuples, with maximum absolute correlations 104 and 280. These checks support the finite identities and conventions. They neither certify the surface's cohomology nor establish the asymptotic bilinear bound; those conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments above.

The connection to the selected problem is a useful reduced arithmetic estimate for the cubic-kernel approach motivating [2]. It does not control the sparse cubic arguments, coupled profiles, prime powers or signed modulus covariance required there. In particular, it does not establish [2]'s remaining sparse-moment estimate. The manuscript and informative metadata abstract state these limits clearly, and the submission does not meet the problem's solve definition.

Only minor clarifications are suggested. Around (7.1), explicitly mention the substitution $t\mapsto-t$ that reconciles the Fourier-basis signs with the convention for $S_\eta$. In Section 8, define the counting multiplicity underlying $A_D$ and $B_D$ explicitly before applying Hölder, so that the treatment of complex bounded weights is immediately transparent. Neither clarification changes the result or the acceptance decision.
