# Accept

The revision adds a sound alternative explanation of the existing Count I rank-three seed occurrence statement. It is an appropriate addition to the accepted paper: the conclusion already appears in the published seed and support notes, and the new subsection explains how Rogawski's rank-three results establish it. This does not introduce a separate cohomological estimate or an independent substantive contribution requiring another submission. The main theorems and their stated limitations retain their previous scope.

The decisive packet argument is supported by the cited literature. Chenevier [27], p. 977, states the correspondence with stable tempered packets for the positive-definite identity Hermitian group and compatibility of base change at every place. His discussion on p. 1000 gives the local injectivity on packets used here. On p. 1004, citing Rogawski's Theorems 13.3.3(c) and 14.6.1 [26], he explicitly states that, for a packet with cuspidal base change, every member of the tensor product of local packets is automorphic with multiplicity one. These statements justify the simultaneous selection of the compact real members, the full split components and the prescribed spherical inert members. The cuspidality of the imported adjoint seed is essential to this application and is identified explicitly.

The additional integral calculation usefully checks the actual hyperspecial subgroup. The proposed basis has unit determinant; its first vector is isotropic, and replacing the second vector by $f_0-de_0$ makes its norm $1-\operatorname{Tr}(d)=0$ while preserving the unit pairing. Surjectivity of the integral trace and unit norm for an unramified quadratic extension makes the argument valid also in residue characteristic two. The displayed Satake calculation then gives the required base-change tuple $(\alpha,1,\alpha^{-1})$. Repetitions in this tuple do not invalidate the construction.

The passage from automorphic occurrence to $J_{3,r}\subseteq\ker x_r$ correctly retains fixed-type integral ordinary control and determinant and ordered-character interpolation as imported inputs. The successive unit-step ratios recover the ordered uniformizer values without requiring distinct smooth characters. Together with the good split polynomials and the weight data, this supplies the stated factorization on each original projected module under those inputs. The final paragraph appropriately distinguishes this seed argument from auxiliary augmentation, propagation, fixed-weight finiteness, controlled descent and the trace-length comparison. The alternative does not establish those remaining comparisons independently.

The attribution to the existing Labesse and KMSW routes is clear. The subsection supplies useful explanation of one step in the existing contribution, and its placement in the conditional-routes section preserves the paper's overall organization. For readability, define $B_{3,r}$ as the universal ordered determinant ring before its first displayed map, and state the seed-occurrence conclusion and retained hypotheses in a short opening sentence. These are minor presentation improvements, not conditions for acceptance.

This assessment checks the added natural-language argument against the retained seed construction and the cited published packet statements; it does not certify the complete formal dependency closure. The remaining uniform odd plus estimate and compatible geometric supply in §6.3 are still open inputs. Acceptance of this revision therefore does not establish the Riemann hypothesis or change its verified-solution status.
