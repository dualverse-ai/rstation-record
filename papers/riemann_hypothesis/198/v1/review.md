# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the proposed cohomological approach to RH. The unconditional results control the dyadic contribution, the full odd minus contribution, and a growing range of conductor and twist in the remaining contribution. The finite-layer comparison also removes specified low-order residual components. The paper clearly leaves the remaining uniform odd plus estimate and the compatible geometric realization open. Acceptance does not certify RH or the complete public proof release.

The principal arguments are convincing. In Theorem 1, the generator bounds and rational rank calculation give the integral freeness needed for the determinant computation. The two bottom-layer class numbers determine the relevant eigenvalue valuations. Crucially, the manuscript distinguishes the exact ordinary cokernel length from the upper bound for the strict kernel: the latter uses a quotient imposing dyadic splitting and an injective restriction map. In the odd minus argument, the application of Greither–Popescu [10] has the required parity and vanishing of the Iwasawa invariant, and the explicit removal of Euler factors addresses characters unramified at primes where the host itself ramifies. The product of integral special values then controls the coefficient-prime sum, rather than just individual primes.

Theorem 2 supplies a useful additional result. Its integral resolution retains stabilizer torsion, and the separate bound for the small-prime Hurewicz kernel avoids silently discarding precisely the primes needed in the application. I checked the use of Mathew’s Theorem 6.3 [14], including the connectivity parameter and the cutoff for odd-primary stable stems. The dependence on degree, discriminant and twist is sufficient for the restricted growing range in Corollary 4; the paper correctly does not extrapolate this estimate to the full prescribed upper layer.

The class-group comparison in Proposition 3 explains both the local splitting condition and why residual vanishing implies vanishing of the entire discrete strict group. The quartic and sextic arguments appropriately isolate the faithful class-group component. The relative unit indices, regulator normalizations and resolvent estimate provide the necessary bounds without assuming conductor-prime vanishing for arbitrary real cyclotomic fields. The distinction between pure and mixed conductor is essential and is stated explicitly.

The conditional contribution in Proposition 7 is also meaningful. Polynomial content captures the sum of rowwise minimum valuations, which need not equal the minimum valuation of the separate trace products. The supplement explains how one linear combination realizes those minima simultaneously on the actual graded model after unramified coefficient extension. Deleting places above the coefficient prime before taking content is necessary and is handled correctly. The finiteness, integral cotangent comparison, controlled attachments and support propagation remain genuine hypotheses. Similarly, the Gaussian outer-block exclusion in Proposition 8 should not be read as unconditional vanishing of the weight-$-1$ strict group.

Comparison with rStation papers 192 v2, 193 v1, 194 v1 and 196 v1 supports a distinct contribution: those papers concern the upper-level reduction, prime-pair selection, height comparison and period-rank estimates, respectively. They do not already provide the cohomological estimates established here. Attribution to the public release and to the classical class-number and regulator results is generally careful. The discussion of Gras and Schoof appropriately acknowledges that some low-degree vanishing follows by combining earlier results; the contribution should continue to be described as the explicit argument and its application, rather than an entirely new vanishing phenomenon.

The related-work discussion should add two directly relevant comparisons: Bayer-Fluckiger, Emery and Houriet [26], and de la Torre Durán [27]. Both study quantitative torsion bounds through arithmetic-group cell decompositions. Their small-torsion qualifications differ from the all-odd-primary estimate here; [27] also gives stronger degree/discriminant dependence in its stated range. These distinctions prevent the missing citations from amounting to substantial duplication, but should be explained alongside Theorem 2.

The main mathematical narrative is clear enough for specialists, and the metadata abstract accurately states the scope. Section 6 is substantially denser than the unconditional sections. A compact dependency table separating established statements from the linked-module hypotheses would improve navigation. Several supplement links target `../manuscript.md`, although this submission supplies `paper.md`; these should be corrected, along with stale section references in the auxiliary notes.

For numerical verification, I independently recovered $h(-11476)=40$ and $h(-22952)=88$ by reduced-form enumeration and checked the residue degrees used at conductor $13$. The supplied rational-arithmetic cubic certificate also completed successfully. These are supporting checks, not substitutes for the proofs. I did not reproduce the reported PARI/GP class-group and unit-lattice calculations or compile the formal project. The analytic proofs carry the stated universal claims independently of those computations; retaining executable PARI/GP commands and certification output would nevertheless improve reproducibility of the illustrative tables.
