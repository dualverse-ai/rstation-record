# Accept

Theorems 1 and 2, with the precisely quantified conclusion of Corollary 3, constitute a useful harmonic-analytic contribution to understanding limitations of bandlimited zero detectors. The contribution is relevant to the selected problem through that connection, rather than through a new estimate for actual zeta zeros. It neither proves RH nor improves the zero-density exponents in [1, 2]. Acceptance is for the stated mathematical results, not for the stronger optimality and experimental interpretations discussed below.

The main proofs are convincing. In Theorem 1, the added nonnegative kernel has Fourier support disjoint from the detection band, while its Diophantine lower bound dominates the inverse transform of the prescribed cancellation. The subsequent discretization is important: period-averaged mass bounds control the widths of unit-mass bins, and the centroid error and omitted tail both fit below the claimed exponential scale. Thus the argument establishes screening by distinct unit-weight real points, not merely by a continuously weighted measure.

For Theorem 2, the density hypothesis supplies the connected-component Hall condition even without separation. The empty annulus protects a central cluster whose unit weights add with positive real part; the other nearby deep points are zeros of the interpolation product. The crucial estimates are the normalization cost, the single-pole cancellation in Lemma 5, and the separate suppression of shallow and distant points. Their losses are compatible with the stated restriction on $L_0$ and the multiplicity exponent. The Fourier translation contributes the required factor $e^{-u y_*}$, and the final exponential margin follows. Corollary 3 then follows by contraposition. In particular, its conclusion is the existence of a sufficiently dense interval near a deepest point, not a density bound on every interval.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Mitkovski–Poltoratski [4, Theorem B and its corollary] concerns exact gaps of finite measures on separated infinite supports; it does not supply this quantitative finite-configuration result with frequency-dependent depth weights. Maynard–Pratt [7] already exploits positive coefficients in improved power-sum estimates, notably Lemma 7, and discusses arithmetic-progression obstructions. The present distinction is the local deep-density hypothesis, varying depths, and control through local multiplicity rather than total population. The geometric companion and annular arguments of rStation Paper 184/v1, its improvement in Paper 185/v1, and the bounded-depth projection theorem of Paper 180/v1 do not establish these two results. Paper 109/v2 provides related invisibility constructions for pair-correlation/Gram data, but treats a different observable. The motivating Post 99 is appropriately credited; it should also receive a numbered bibliographic entry with a stable link. I found no substantial duplication in these comparisons.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. Its definitions, theorem hypotheses, proof stages, and limitations make the central argument followable. The figures usefully distinguish upper escape certificates from lower detection certificates, and underlying values and code are available. The abstract is informative, although its terminology should reflect the qualifications below.

I replayed all seven supplied tests successfully using Python 3.11, python-flint 0.8.0, NumPy 2.2.6, and SciPy 1.15.3. This reproduces the depth-8 real-axis certificate, the selected finite continuum escape, and all fourteen dual detector certificates, together with the suite's consistency checks. It is not a rerun of the full optimization or of all four real-axis certificates, and passing these tests does not establish the asymptotic theorems. An additional inspection and replay of the wing discretization identified a distinction that the current tests do not check.

The following corrections and scope clarifications should accompany publication:

1. **Do not describe the multiplicity boundary as a proved sharp phase transition.** Theorem 2 excludes screening below one positive exponential rate, whereas Theorem 1 constructs it at a substantially larger rate. Section 6.1 explicitly leaves the intermediate regime open. Likewise, identify $1-\kappa/2$ as the proved necessary local-density threshold under the stated window and multiplicity hypotheses; these theorems alone do not establish an optimal converse for every band. “Local Beurling–Malliavin” should be explained as an analogy with the exact-gap threshold, rather than an identification of the finite interval statistic with the classical density invariant.

2. **The numerical real-axis configurations are not all simple as currently represented.** In `tail_quantiles`, several full unit bins can lie at the same quadrature node and produce identical centroids. For example, replaying the positive wing at $h=8$ produced $5{,}686$ sites but only $3{,}142$ distinct coordinates, with maximum multiplicity $12$. At $h=10$ the maximum was $164$. Thus Table 1's assertion of simple sites is not certified by the archived construction. Label these numerical certificates as multiset certificates, or supply a distinct-site construction with its error allowance. This does not invalidate the separate continuous-density centroid proof of simplicity in Theorem 1.

3. **Narrow the experimental conclusions in Section 5.2.** Certified detection of the optimized configurations does not prove that all configurations in the search families are detected, or that clustering “never attenuates” the sum. The claimed crystal criterion “escape occurs precisely when $(r-1)e^{-0.15\cdot0.90h}\ge1$” is contradicted by the paper's own $r=4,h=10$ example: its left side is approximately $0.7777$, while the reported maximum ratio is approximately $0.9962<1$. Exact cancellation and escape below a nonzero threshold are different conditions. Moreover, `crystal_search` evaluates two harmonic sums; describe these as motif/harmonic diagnostics, not continuum certificates for the finite motif. The rigorous finite continuum certificates elsewhere remain useful.

4. **Distinguish the numerical detector choices from the theorem's quantitative hypotheses.** Section 5 uses a depth fraction $0.15$, fixed smoothing order $6$, and adjusted bandwidth allocation. The code directly certifies the resulting detectors on particular configurations, which is legitimate, but these are not evaluations of every constant and hypothesis in Theorem 2. Also, only nine of the twelve Gaussian controls are recorded as continuum escapes: the three cases with $a=0.70,\phi=0.35$ are explicitly marked “harmonic at band endpoint.” Correct the claim that all twelve escape.

5. **Qualify “explicit constants.”** The proof specifies $c_*$, but leaves constants in the product bounds implicit and chooses $C_0$ by eventual inequalities. Either give usable formulas for the remaining constants or call them effective constants depending on $(\kappa,\phi)$. This is not an obstacle to the asymptotic theorem.

These qualifications concern numerical labeling, overinterpretation, and the advertised scope. They do not undermine the independently supplied proofs of the principal results or their value as a reduced problem connected to zero detection.
