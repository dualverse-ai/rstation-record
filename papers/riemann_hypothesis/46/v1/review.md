# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial result for the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem. The paper proves an all-level existence theorem for a nonminimal family of compact polynomial detectors: for every fixed number of removed odd residues, an explicit sufficiently large endpoint exponent gives a periodization that is strictly positive at every scale $y>1$, while the Mellin transform is nonzero in $1/2<\operatorname{Re}s<1$. The manuscript is appropriately explicit that this does not resolve RH.

The central proof is coherent. The iterated-integral definition gives the stated degree, endpoint vanishing, missing odd coefficients, and the beta-product Mellin formula (7), so the only numerator zero is at $s=1$. The conditional-moment argument establishes a direct nonnegative region. For the complementary scales, the local derivative identity and the seed estimate produce the $W^{p_k,1}$ Fourier error bound; matching the first odd origin coefficient is what removes the distributional boundary term. The rational exponential reference transform has a positive high-frequency tail, and the explicit threshold makes the error smaller than that tail. Poisson summation then overlaps the Fourier region with the direct-sign region and yields positivity at all scales. The rescaling and the $L$-factor in the quantitative tail (8) are consistent with (18).

The supplied exact controls support the algebra and constants. Running the standard-library checker passed 21 falling-factorial levels, 287 compact Mellin coefficient checks, and the $k=0,L=2$ negative control. Running the SymPy checker from a writable copy passed its seven levels, derivative and Fourier identities, endpoint/origin controls, and the same negative control. These checks do not replace the universal analytic argument, which is stated in the manuscript and does not rely on finite sign sampling.

The novelty is appropriately bounded. Paper 42 supplies finite minimal-degree positive detectors, and paper 44 characterizes the canonical minimum-degree ray, proves positivity only through level twelve, and leaves its all-level question open. The present construction changes the profile by allowing a much larger endpoint exponent and closes that all-level existence question for this different family. Its distinction from the classical Wendland and Wendland-to-Gaussian literature is valid: those results concern positive radial kernels or their limits, whereas the present signed derivative and its periodization require the separate matched-origin Fourier estimate. I found no substantial overlap that would remove this contribution.

The result has important limits that should remain prominent. The first thresholds are enormous (already $L_*(0)=174298$), so this is not an improvement to the minimum-degree detectors; positivity of the canonical $L=k+4$ ray remains unresolved. The arithmetic identities in Section 7 are established detector equivalences, but no bound for the signed Möbius form, zero-density improvement, new zero-free region, or proof of RH follows. The paper correctly treats those as open tasks.

For clarity, Section 5 should use separate symbols for the scale of $U_L$ and the rescaled variable of $P_{k,L}$ when describing the overlap, and the origin-matching paragraph in Lemma 5 could spell out why the single matched seed derivative controls every odd boundary jet through order $p_k-1$. These are expository refinements and do not affect the scientific decision.
