# Bernoulli-2 Farey Large-Sieve Neutral Descent and Supercritical Truncated Divisor Covariance

## Abstract

We establish unconditional theorems on multi-probe Selberg–Möbius approximation in the Nyman–Beurling Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = L^2((1, \infty), y^{-2}\,dy)$ and on supercritical truncated divisor sums:

1. **Bernoulli-2 Farey Large-Sieve, Loewner Domination & Sobolev-Regularized Neutral Descent (Theorem 1):** In [1], the codimension-2 neutral subspace $W_K = \{u = \sum_{m=2}^K c_m\rho_m : \sum_{m=2}^K c_m a_m = \sum_{m=2}^K c_m T_m(1) = 0\}$ was shown to eliminate the slow harmonic moment terms identically, giving a zero-free-region-free tail identity and the $L^1$ triangle-inequality bound $\|P_{W_K}((\log N)(1 - g_N) - q)\|_{\mathcal{H}} \ll K^{5/2}/N$ for $K \le N^{2/5-\varepsilon}$. We prove that the quadratic wave $b_m(k) := \frac{m^2 - 1 - 6r(m - r)}{12m}$ ($r = k \bmod m$) has zero discrete period sum $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} b_m(r) \equiv 0$, satisfies the exact forward-difference identity $b_m(k+1) - b_m(k) \equiv -f_m(k) = -(\rho_m(k) + a_m)$ at **every** integer node $k \ge 1$, and admits the exact $m$-periodic cubic antiderivative $s_m(k) := \frac{r(m-r)(m+3-2r)}{12m}$ ($s_m(k+1) - s_m(k) \equiv b_m(k)$), giving by summation by parts the exact kernel decomposition $K_m(k) \equiv \frac{b_m(k)}{k(k+1)} - 2\sum_{j=k+1}^\infty \frac{b_m(j)}{(j-1)j(j+1)}$. Over common periods $\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)$, $b_m(k)$ and $b_n(k)$ have the closed-form GCD correlation $R_{m, n} \equiv \frac{(\gcd(m, n)^2 - 1)(\gcd(m, n)^2 + 11)}{720 m n}$, which vanishes whenever $\gcd(m, n) = 1$. Using the sharp all-$d$ majorant $\phi_4^{(B)}(d) \le (d^2 - 1)(d^2 + 11) \le \frac{45}{16}d^4$ ($d \ge 2$) and **Weighted Schur's Test** ($p_d = d, q_b = 1$) on the divisor-jump matrix $M_{d, b}$, we prove the explicit universal operator bound
   $$\sup_{u \in V_K \setminus \{0\}} \frac{\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2}}{\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}} \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{8}\,K^2\,(1 + \log K)^{3/2} \;\ll\; K^2 (\log K)^{3/2} \qquad (\forall K \ge 2).$$
   Combining this with the Montgomery–Vaughan Hilbert inequality [4] on the Farey set $\mathcal{F}_K$ by keeping $j \ge 1$ outside in $\sum_{n > N}\mu(n)\log(n/N)\sum_{j=1}^\infty K_u(jn)$ and embedding $\{jn : M < n \le 2M\} \subset (jM, 2jM]$ ($M = 2^s N$, $s \ge 0$), we eliminate the Ramanujan divisor factor $(\log N)^{3/2}$ via $\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^{-3/2} = \zeta(3/2) < \infty$ and prove unconditionally that for all $u \in W_K$:
   $$\bigl|\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q,\, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr| \;\le\; \left(\frac{C_1}{N} + \frac{C_2 K}{N^{3/2}}\right)\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2}, \qquad \left\|P_{W_K}\bigl((\log N)(1 - g_N) - q\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}} \;\ll\; \frac{K^2 (\log K)^{3/2}}{N} \qquad (\forall\, 2 \le K \le N^{1/2}),$$
   with explicit constants $C_1 = 4\log 2\,\zeta(3/2) + 12(\log 2)^2 \approx 13.0085$ and $C_2 \approx 5.1357$, extending the unconditional polynomial range of subspace-norm neutral descent from $K \le N^{2/5-\varepsilon}$ to $K \le N^{1/2}$. Furthermore (**Theorem 1(d)**), we prove the **exact Loewner matrix domination** $R_K \preceq \bigl(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\bigr)^2 D^2 = \bigl(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\bigr)^2 \operatorname{diag}(2^2, 3^2, \dots, K^2)$ and show that the Sobolev-regularized neutral approximant $u_{\lambda_K} \in W_K$ minimizing $\|q - u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c\|_2^2$ on $W_K$ ($\lambda_K \to 0^+$) recovers the full asymptotic neutral projection ceiling $\lim_{K \to \infty}\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \lim_{K \to \infty}\langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \sup_{M \ge 4}\|P_{W_M} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$, suppresses the terminal coordinate via $|c_{\lambda_K, K}| = o\bigl((K\sqrt{\lambda_K})^{-1}\bigr)$, and satisfies $\bigl(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K}\bigr)^{1/2} \le \frac{\pi^2}{96}\lambda_K^{-1/2}\|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}$ at every $K \ge 4$ together with $\bigl(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K}\bigr)^{1/2} = o(\lambda_K^{-1/2})$ as $K \to \infty$, extending unconditional second-order norm descent for $g_{N, K}^{\mathrm{reg}} := g_N + (\log N)^{-1} u_{\lambda_K} \in V_N$ across $K \le N^{1-\varepsilon}$ (for $\lambda_K = K^{-1}$) and up to the full critical scale $4 \le K \le N$ (for $\lambda_K = (\log K)^{-1}$). In **Proposition 1(c)–(d)**, we establish the exact dilation law $b_m(g\ell) \equiv g b_{m/g}(\ell) + \frac{g^2-1}{12m}$, the second-order Gram expansion $T_m(n) = \frac{B_m(n)}{n^2} + R_m^{(3)}(n)$ with $|R_m^{(3)}(n)| \le \frac{\zeta(3)(4m^2+m)}{12n^3}$, the **Truncated Chebyshev Step Identity** $1 - g_N(j) \equiv -\frac{\psi_N(j)-j}{\log N} + j\mathcal{E}_1(N)$, and the **Exact Finite-$N$ Báez-Duarte Extraction Identity** isolating $2 + \gamma - \log(4\pi)$ and the explicit second-order excess $\langle q, 1 - g_N \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \sim \frac{C_{\mathrm{second}}}{\log N}$ ($C_{\mathrm{second}} \approx 1.314217$) at every finite $N$.
2. **Uniform Supercritical Two-Cutoff Covariance & Explicit Goldston–Yıldırım Profile (Theorem 2):** Using the divisor-complementation and squarefree-coprime sieve mechanism of de la Bretèche, Dress, and Tenenbaum [2, §2] and going beyond the $O(N)$ supercritical second-moment estimate of Graham [3] (see Goldston and Yıldırım [6, §5]), we establish the explicit unequal-cutoff covariance profile $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$ and the explicit supercritical Goldston–Yıldırım mean-square error profile $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$ with uniform absolute-error bounds:
   - $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N < n \le 2N} c_{X_1}(n) c_{X_2}(n) = \mathcal{D}_2(N/X_1, N/X_2) + O\bigl(N^{-1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}\,(\log(2Y_1))^2\bigr)$ uniformly for all $N^{1/2} < X_1 \le X_2 \le N$ ($Y_i := N/X_i$), and
   - $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N < n \le 2N}(\Lambda_{X_1}(n) - \Lambda(n))^2 = \mathcal{L}_2(N/X_1) + O\bigl(N^{-1/2} Y_1 (\log(2Y_1))^2\bigr)$ uniformly for all $N^{1/2} < X_1 \le N$, where the dependence of the logarithmic Stieltjes weight $W(t) = \log(u_2 t / X_1)\log(u_1 t / X_1)$ on $(g, u_1, u_2)$ is retained via Tonelli integration to avoid losing two logarithmic powers, giving an $o(\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1))$ relative-error asymptotic throughout the range $Y_1\log(2Y_1) = o(N^{1/2})$.

No unconditional bound on the leading residual norm $\|1 - g_N\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$, off-diagonal shift correlation, or proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is claimed.

## 1. Introduction and Comparison with Prior Literature

In the Nyman–Beurling formulation of the Riemann Hypothesis [10, 9, 8, 7], the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} := L^2((1, \infty), y^{-2}\,dy)$ carries the uncentered fractional-part generators
$$\rho_m(y) \;:=\; \left\lfloor \frac{y}{m} \right\rfloor - \frac{\lfloor y \rfloor}{m} \;=\; -\frac{\lfloor y \rfloor \bmod m}{m} \qquad (m \ge 2),$$
with centered counterparts $f_m(y) := \rho_m(y) + a_m$ where $a_m := \frac{m-1}{2m}$. For the anchored Selberg–Möbius approximant $g_N(y) := \sum_{n=2}^N \mu(n)\bigl(1 - \frac{\log n}{\log N}\bigr)\rho_n(y)$, it was shown in [1] that for any fixed $m \ge 2$, $\langle 1 - g_N, \rho_m \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \frac{C_m}{\log N} + O_m((\log N)^{-B})$, where $q(y) := \log(2\pi) - \{y\} + \frac{1}{2}\log(1 - y^{-2}) \in \mathcal{H}$ and $C_m := \langle q, \rho_m \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$. Moreover, [11, Theorem 2(c) and Theorem 4(d)] decomposed the single-coordinate inner product into harmonic and tail terms and established the unit-step subspace ceiling $\|P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \approx 1.27905 < \|q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \approx 1.36543$.

Recently, [1, Section 7] introduced the codimension-2 neutral subspace
$$W_K \;:=\; \left\{u = \sum_{m=2}^K c_m\rho_m \in V_K \;:\; \sum_{m=2}^K c_m a_m = 0,\;\; \sum_{m=2}^K c_m T_m(1) = 0\right\} \qquad (K \ge 4),$$
where $T_m(n) := \sum_{j=1}^\infty K_m(jn)$ and $K_m(k) := \int_k^\infty f_m(y)\,y^{-2}\,dy$. Note that whereas Ehm [12, §§8.1–8.2] studied Gram decompositions, Möbius inversion, and moment-adjusted Selberg *approximant coefficients* $\xi_n$, the construction in [1] and the present paper leaves the anchored approximant $g_N$ untouched and imposes neutrality on the *observation probe* $u \in W_K$. On $W_K$, both harmonic terms $\frac{a_m\log n - T_m(1)}{n}$ in $G(n, m) = \langle \rho_n, \rho_m \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$ vanish identically at every integer $n \ge 2$, and $-\sum_{k=2}^\infty K_u(k) = K_u(1) = -\sum_{m=2}^K c_m\frac{\log m}{m} \equiv \langle 1, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$ cancels the $d = 1$ divisor term identically, leaving the exact finite-$N$ tail identity [1, Proposition 6]
$$\langle 1 - g_N, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \;-\; \frac{\langle q, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\log N} \;\equiv\; -\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{k = N+1}^\infty \bigl(\Lambda_N(k) - \Lambda(k)\bigr)\,K_u(k) \;\equiv\; \sum_{n > N} \mu(n)\left(1 - \frac{\log n}{\log N}\right) T_u(n),$$
where $\Lambda_N(k) := \sum_{d \mid k, d \le N}\mu(d)\log(N/d)$. Using unit-step jump inversion $c_m = \sum_{b \mid m}\mu(m/b)\Delta u_b$ [1, Eq. (39)] and the pointwise $L^1$ triangle inequality $|K_u(k)| \le \frac{1}{8k^2}\sum_{m=2}^K m|c_m| \ll \frac{K^{5/2}}{k^2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}$, [1, Theorem 7] proved the uniform bound
$$\left\|P_{W_K}\bigl((\log N)(1 - g_N) - q\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}} \;\le\; C\,\frac{K^{5/2}}{N},$$
permitting polynomial growth up to $K = \lfloor N^{2/5-\varepsilon} \rfloor$ with zero appeal to any zero-free region of $\zeta(s)$.

### Contributions of This Paper

1. **Beating the $K^{5/2}/N$ Bound via Bernoulli-2 Farey Large Sieve, Ungrouped $\zeta(3/2)$ Tail Summation, Loewner Domination & Sobolev-Regularized Neutral Descent (Theorem 1 & Proposition 1):** The $K^{5/2}$ factor in [1] stems from bounding $|K_u(k)|$ by the $L^1$ sum of amplitudes $\sum_{m=2}^K m|c_m|$, which ignores phase cancellation among the periodic waves $K_m(k)$ across different periods $m \le K$. In **Theorem 1(a)–(c)**, we prove that the leading wave $b_m(k) := \frac{m^2 - 1 - 6r(m - r)}{12m}$ ($r = k \bmod m$) has **identically zero discrete mean** over every period, satisfies the **exact forward-difference identity** $b_m(k+1) - b_m(k) \equiv -f_m(k)$ across **all** integer nodes $k \ge 1$, and admits the exact $m$-periodic cubic antiderivative $s_m(k) := \frac{r(m-r)(m+3-2r)}{12m}$ satisfying $s_m(k+1) - s_m(k) \equiv b_m(k)$, converting the kernel into the exact summation-by-parts decomposition $K_m(k) \equiv \frac{b_m(k)}{k(k+1)} - 2\sum_{j>k}\frac{b_m(j)}{(j-1)j(j+1)}$ with zero remainder. We prove that $b_m(k)$ and $b_n(k)$ have the exact closed-form GCD correlation $R_{m, n} \equiv \frac{(\gcd(m, n)^2 - 1)(\gcd(m, n)^2 + 11)}{720 m n}$ (vanishing whenever $\gcd(m, n) = 1$). Using the sharp all-$d$ bound $\phi_4^{(B)}(d) \le (d^2 - 1)(d^2 + 11) \le \frac{45}{16} d^4$ ($d \ge 2$) and **Weighted Schur's Test** ($p_d = d, q_b = 1$), we prove that the $L^2$ tail operator norm satisfies $\sup_{u \in V_K \setminus \{0\}} \frac{(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2}}{\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}} \le \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{8} K^2(1 + \log K)^{3/2}$ for all $K \ge 2$. Keeping $j \ge 1$ outside in $\sum_{n > N}\mu(n)\log(n/N)\sum_{j=1}^\infty K_u(jn)$ and applying the **Montgomery–Vaughan Hilbert inequality** [4] on $\{jn : M < n \le 2M\} \subset (jM, 2jM]$ ($M = 2^s N$, $s \ge 0$), we eliminate the Ramanujan divisor factor $(\log N)^{3/2}$ via $\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^{-3/2} = \zeta(3/2)$ and prove unconditionally:
   $$\bigl|\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q,\, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr| \;\le\; \left(\frac{C_1}{N} + \frac{C_2 K}{N^{3/2}}\right)\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2}, \qquad \left\|P_{W_K}\bigl((\log N)(1 - g_N) - q\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}} \;\ll\; \frac{K^2 (\log K)^{3/2}}{N} \qquad (\forall\, 2 \le K \le N^{1/2}),$$
   where $C_1 = 4\log 2\,\zeta(3/2) + 12(\log 2)^2 \approx 13.0085$ and $C_2 \approx 5.1357$, extending the permissible subspace-norm cutoff range to **$K \le N^{1/2}$** (or $K = o(N^{1/2}(\log N)^{-3/4})$ for second-order norm descent of $P_{W_K} q$). In **Theorem 1(d)**, we establish the **exact Loewner matrix domination** $R_K \preceq \bigl(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\bigr)^2 D^2$ ($D := \operatorname{diag}(2, 3, \dots, K)$) and show that the **Sobolev-regularized neutral approximant** $u_{\lambda_K} \in W_K$ minimizing $\|q - u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c\|_2^2$ ($\lambda_K \to 0^+$) satisfies $\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \to \sup_{M \ge 4}\|P_{W_M} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$, $(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2} \le \frac{\pi^2}{96}\lambda_K^{-1/2}\|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}$, and $(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2} = o(\lambda_K^{-1/2})$ (with terminal-coordinate suppression $|c_{\lambda_K, K}| = o((K\sqrt{\lambda_K})^{-1})$), extending unconditional second-order norm descent for $g_{N, K}^{\mathrm{reg}} := g_N + (\log N)^{-1} u_{\lambda_K} \in V_N$ across $K \le N^{1-\varepsilon}$ ($\lambda_K = K^{-1}$) and up to the full critical scale $4 \le K \le N$ ($\lambda_K = (\log K)^{-1}$). Furthermore, in **Proposition 1(c)–(d)**, we prove the exact dilation law $b_m(g\ell) \equiv g b_{m/g}(\ell) + \frac{g^2-1}{12m}$, the second-order Gram expansion $T_m(n) = \frac{B_m(n)}{n^2} + R_m^{(3)}(n)$, the Truncated Chebyshev Step Identity $1 - g_N(j) \equiv -\frac{\psi_N(j)-j}{\log N} + j\mathcal{E}_1(N)$, and the Exact Finite-$N$ Báez-Duarte Extraction Identity.
2. **Uniform Supercritical Two-Cutoff Covariance and Explicit Goldston–Yıldırım Profile (Theorem 2):** For the truncated Möbius divisor sum $c_X(n) := \sum_{d \mid n, d \le X}\mu(d)$, Dress, Iwaniec, and Tenenbaum [5] established the limiting constant $L = \lim_{z \to \infty}\sum_{m, n \le z}\frac{\mu(m)\mu(n)}{\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \prod_p\bigl(1 - \frac{1}{p}\bigr)\bigl(1 + \frac{|1 - p^{-it}|^2 - 1}{p}\bigr)\frac{dt}{t^2} \approx 0.440729$, and de la Bretèche, Dress, and Tenenbaum [2, §§1–2] proved the uniform two-parameter asymptotic $\sum_{n \le x} c_z(n)^2 = (L + O((\log \min(z, x/z))^{-1/2}\log\log\min(z, x/z)))x$ ($1 \le z < x$) and used divisor complementation $m = n/d$ and squarefree-coprime counting (with squarefree density factor $6/\pi^2 \approx 0.607927$) to evaluate the single-cutoff supercritical variance $\sum_{n \le x} c_z(n)^2$ for $z > x^{1/2}$. For the truncated von Mangoldt sum $\Lambda_R(n) := \sum_{d \mid n, d \le R}\mu(d)\log(R/d)$, Graham [3] proved $\sum_{n \le N}\Lambda_R(n)^2 = N\log R + O(N)$ across the entire range $1 \le R \le N$ (removing the $O(R^2)$ error, as noted by Goldston and Yıldırım [6, §5, immediately after Theorem 5.1]). In the supercritical regime $X_1 > N^{1/2}$ ($Y_1 := N/X_1 < N^{1/2}$), where $\Lambda_{X_1}(p) = \log X_1$ on primes $p \in (N, 2N]$ so that $(\Lambda_{X_1}(p) - \Lambda(p))^2 = (\log(p/X_1))^2 \asymp (\log Y_1)^2$ is absorbed into Graham's $O(N)$ term when $Y_1$ is bounded, **Theorem 2** establishes: (i) the explicit **unequal-cutoff supercritical covariance profile** $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$ with uniform absolute remainder $O\bigl(N^{-1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}\,(\log(2Y_1))^2\bigr)$, and (ii) the explicit **supercritical Goldston–Yıldırım mean-square error profile** $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$ (isolating the exact order-1 coefficient beyond Graham's $O(N)$ bound) with uniform absolute remainder $O\bigl(N^{-1/2} Y_1 (\log(2Y_1))^2\bigr)$ by retaining the $(g, u_1, u_2)$-dependence of the logarithmic Stieltjes weight via Tonelli integration.

## 2. Bernoulli-2 Farey Large-Sieve Neutral Descent

### 2.1 Exact Discrete-Mean-Zero Antiderivative, Weighted Schur Test, Loewner Domination, and Sobolev-Regularized Descent

For every integer $m \ge 2$, let $\rho_m(y) = -(\lfloor y \rfloor \bmod m)/m$, $a_m = \frac{m-1}{2m}$, $f_m(y) = \rho_m(y) + a_m$, and $K_m(k) = \int_k^\infty f_m(y)\,y^{-2}\,dy = \sum_{j=k}^\infty \frac{f_m(j)}{j(j+1)}$. For every integer $k \ge 1$, put $r := k \bmod m \in \{0, 1, \dots, m-1\}$ and define the $m$-periodic quadratic wave and its $m$-periodic cubic antiderivative:
$$b_m(k) \;:=\; \frac{m^2 - 1 - 6r(m - r)}{12m}, \qquad s_m(k) \;:=\; \frac{r(m - r)(m + 3 - 2r)}{12m}.$$

**Theorem 1 (Bernoulli-2 Farey Large-Sieve, Loewner Domination & Sobolev-Regularized Neutral Descent Theorem).**
*(a) (**Exact Discrete Antiderivative, Periodic Cubic Antiderivative & Kernel Summation-by-Parts Identity.**) For every integer $m \ge 2$, $b_m(k)$ has zero discrete period sum $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} b_m(r) \equiv 0$, $s_m(k)$ has period sum $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} s_m(r) \equiv \frac{m^2 - 1}{24}$ (period mean $\frac{m^2 - 1}{24m}$), and both satisfy the exact forward-difference identities at **every** integer $k \ge 1$ (including across period boundaries $m \mid (k+1)$, where $r(m - r)$ vanishes at both $r = 0$ and $r = m$):*
$$b_m(k+1) - b_m(k) \;\equiv\; -f_m(k) \;=\; -\bigl(\rho_m(k) + a_m\bigr), \qquad s_m(k+1) - s_m(k) \;\equiv\; b_m(k) \qquad (\forall m \ge 2,\;\forall k \ge 1).$$
*Consequently, for every integer $k \ge 1$, $K_m(k)$ decomposes in closed form as the exact summation-by-parts series*
$$K_m(k) \;\equiv\; \frac{b_m(k)}{k(k+1)} \;-\; 2\sum_{j=k+1}^\infty \frac{b_m(j)}{(j-1)j(j+1)}, \qquad |b_m(k)| \;\le\; \frac{m^2 - 1}{12m} \;<\; \frac{m}{12}.$$
*(b) (**Cosecant-Squared Fourier Series, GCD Correlation Matrix $R_K$ & Weighted Schur Bound.**) For every $m \ge 2$, $b_m(k)$ admits the exact finite Fourier expansion*
$$b_m(k) \;\equiv\; \sum_{a=1}^{m-1} \frac{e(a k / m)}{4m\sin^2(\pi a / m)} \qquad (\forall k \in \mathbb{Z}).$$
*Over a common period $L = \operatorname{lcm}(m, n)$, the correlation of $b_m(k)$ and $b_n(k)$ equals in closed form ($g := \gcd(m, n)$):*
$$R_{m, n} \;:=\; \frac{1}{\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)}\sum_{k=1}^{\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)} b_m(k)\,b_n(k) \;\equiv\; \frac{\bigl(g^2 - 1\bigr)\bigl(g^2 + 11\bigr)}{720\,m\,n} \;=\; \frac{1}{720\,m\,n}\sum_{\substack{d \mid \gcd(m, n) \\ d \ge 2}} \phi_4^{(B)}(d),$$
*where $\phi_4^{(B)}(d) := \sum_{a \mid d}\mu(d/a)(a^4 + 10a^2 - 11) \equiv 45\sum_{1 \le c < d,\, \gcd(c, d)=1} \sin^{-4}(\pi c / d) > 0$. In particular, $R_{m, n} = 0$ whenever $\gcd(m, n) = 1$. For any $u = \sum_{m=2}^K c_m\rho_m \in V_K$, the leading tail wave $\Phi_u(k) := \sum_{m=2}^K c_m b_m(k)$ has correlation energy $c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c \equiv \frac{1}{720}\sum_{d=2}^K \phi_4^{(B)}(d)\,|\beta_u(d)|^2$ ($\beta_u(d) := \sum_{1 \le q \le K/d} \frac{c_{qd}}{qd}$), which satisfies the explicit universal operator bound*
$$\sup_{u \in V_K \setminus \{0\}} \frac{\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2}}{\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}} \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{8}\,K^2\,(1 + \log K)^{3/2} \;\ll\; K^2 (\log K)^{3/2} \qquad (\forall K \ge 2).$$
*(c) (**Ungrouped $(j, n)$ Zeta-(3/2) Large-Sieve Bound & $K \le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ Subspace-Norm Descent.**) Grouping $a/m = c/d$ in lowest terms over the Farey set $\mathcal{F}_K := \{c/d : 2 \le d \le K,\, 1 \le c < d,\, \gcd(c, d)=1\}$ (which has minimum spacing $\delta_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_K) \ge K^{-2}$), the Montgomery–Vaughan Hilbert inequality [4] gives $\sum_{A < k \le B} |\Phi_u(k)|^2 \le (B - A + K^2) c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c$ on every interval $(A, B]$. Keeping $j \ge 1$ outside in $\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = -\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty \mu(n)\log(n/N)\sum_{j=1}^\infty K_u(jn)$ ($u \in W_K$) and embedding $\{jn : M < n \le 2M\} \subset (jM, 2jM]$ across dyadic blocks $M = 2^s N$ ($s \ge 0$), every $u \in W_K$ ($K \ge 4, N \ge 2$) satisfies unconditionally (with zero appeal to any zero-free region of $\zeta(s)$ and zero $(\log N)^{3/2}$ divisor loss):*
$$\bigl|\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q,\, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr| \;\le\; \left(\frac{C_1}{N} + \frac{C_2 K}{N^{3/2}}\right)\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2}, \qquad \left\|P_{W_K}\bigl((\log N)(1 - g_N) - q\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}} \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{8}\,(C_1 + C_2)\,\frac{K^2(1 + \log K)^{3/2}}{N} \;\ll\; \frac{K^2 (\log K)^{3/2}}{N} \quad (\forall\, 4 \le K \le N^{1/2}),$$
*where $C_1 := 4\log 2\,\zeta(3/2) + 12(\log 2)^2 \approx 13.0085$ and $C_2 := \frac{\pi^2\log 2}{6(1 - 2^{-3/2})^2} + \frac{(\log 2)^2(1 + 2^{-3/2})}{(1 - 2^{-3/2})^3} \approx 5.1357$. Consequently, the prescribed perturbation $g_{N, K}^\star := g_N + (\log N)^{-1} P_{W_K} q$ achieves the unconditional second-order norm descent*
$$\|1 - g_{N, K}^\star\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;-\; \|1 - g_N\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;=\; -\frac{\|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2}{(\log N)^2} \;+\; O\!\left(\frac{K^2 (\log K)^{3/2}}{N(\log N)^2}\right) \qquad (\forall K \le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}).$$
*(d) (**Exact Loewner Matrix Domination & Sobolev-Regularized Neutral Descent up to $K \le N$.**) Let $D := \operatorname{diag}(2, 3, \dots, K)$. For every $K \ge 2$, the correlation matrix $R_K$ satisfies the exact Loewner matrix inequality*
$$R_K \;\preceq\; \left(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\right)^2 D^2 \;=\; \left(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\right)^2 \operatorname{diag}(2^2, 3^2, \dots, K^2), \qquad \bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2} \;\le\; \frac{\pi^2}{96}\left(\sum_{m=2}^K m^2 |c_m|^2\right)^{1/2} \quad (\forall c \in \mathbb{R}^{K-1}).$$
*For any regularization schedule $\lambda_K > 0$ ($\lambda_K \to 0^+$ as $K \to \infty$), let $u_{\lambda_K} = \sum_{m=2}^K c_{\lambda_K, m}\rho_m \in W_K$ be the unique minimizer on $W_K$ of the Sobolev-regularized functional $\mathcal{J}_{\lambda_K}(u) := \|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c\|_2^2 - 2\langle q, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$, given in closed form by $c_{\lambda_K} = (G_K + \lambda_K D^2)^{-1} C_K - (G_K + \lambda_K D^2)^{-1} A_K^{\mathsf{T}}\bigl(A_K(G_K + \lambda_K D^2)^{-1} A_K^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)^{-1} A_K(G_K + \lambda_K D^2)^{-1} C_K$. Then $u_{\lambda_K}$ satisfies the exact optimality identity and finite-$K$ Loewner energy bound*
$$\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;+\; \lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2 \;\equiv\; \langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \;\le\; \|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2, \qquad \bigl(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K}\bigr)^{1/2} \;\le\; \frac{\pi^2}{96}\,\lambda_K^{-1/2}\,\|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}, \qquad \bigl(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K}\bigr)^{1/2} \;=\; o\bigl(\lambda_K^{-1/2}\bigr),$$
*and recovers the full asymptotic neutral projection ceiling $\lim_{K \to \infty}\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \lim_{K \to \infty}\langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \sup_{M \ge 4}\|P_{W_M} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 > 0$. Consequently, for every $4 \le K \le N$, the regularized perturbation $g_{N, K}^{\mathrm{reg}} := g_N + (\log N)^{-1} u_{\lambda_K} \in V_N$ satisfies unconditionally:*
$$\|1 - g_{N, K}^{\mathrm{reg}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;-\; \|1 - g_N\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;=\; -\frac{\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + 2\lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2}{(\log N)^2} \;+\; O\!\left(\frac{\lambda_K^{-1/2}\bigl(1 + K N^{-1/2}\bigr)}{N(\log N)^2}\right),$$
*yielding unconditional second-order norm descent across all polynomial scales $K \le N^{1-\varepsilon}$ for $\lambda_K = K^{-1}$ ($(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2} = o(K^{1/2})$) and up to the full critical scale $4 \le K \le N$ for $\lambda_K = (\log K)^{-1}$ ($(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2} = o((\log K)^{1/2})$).*

*Proof.* (a) Since $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r(m - r) = m\frac{(m-1)m}{2} - \frac{(m-1)m(2m-1)}{6} = \frac{m(m^2 - 1)}{6}$, we have $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(m^2 - 1 - 6r(m - r)) = m(m^2 - 1) - 6\frac{m(m^2 - 1)}{6} \equiv 0$. Next, for $0 \le r \le m - 2$, $(k+1) \bmod m = r + 1$, so
$$b_m(k+1) - b_m(k) \;=\; -\frac{6\bigl((r+1)(m - r - 1) - r(m - r)\bigr)}{12m} \;=\; -\frac{m - 1 - 2r}{2m} \;=\; -f_m(k),$$
and $(r+1)(m - r - 1)(m + 1 - 2r) - r(m - r)(m + 3 - 2r) = m^2 - 1 - 6r(m - r)$, giving $s_m(k+1) - s_m(k) = b_m(k)$. When $r = m - 1$, $(k+1) \bmod m = 0$. Since the factor $r(m - r)$ vanishes at both $r = 0$ and $r = m$, $(r+1)(m - (r+1))\big|_{r=m-1} = 0 = 0(m - 0)$, so $b_m(0) - b_m(m - 1) = \frac{m - 1}{2m} = -f_m(m - 1)$ and $s_m(0) - s_m(m - 1) = 0 - \frac{(m - 1)(1)(5 - m)}{12m} = \frac{m^2 - 1 - 6(m - 1)}{12m} = b_m(m - 1)$, confirming both forward-difference identities across all integers $k \ge 1$ without boundary jump discrepancy. Summing $f_m(j) \equiv -(b_m(j+1) - b_m(j))$ by parts against $\frac{1}{j(j+1)} = \frac{1}{j} - \frac{1}{j+1}$ with $\frac{1}{j(j+1)} - \frac{1}{(j-1)j} = -\frac{2}{(j-1)j(j+1)}$ gives part (a).

(b) For $1 \le a \le m - 1$, put $\zeta := e(-a/m) \ne 1$ ($\zeta^m = 1$). Expanding $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \zeta^r = \frac{m}{\zeta - 1}$ and $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r^2 \zeta^r = \frac{m^2}{\zeta - 1} - \frac{2m\zeta}{(\zeta - 1)^2}$ cancels the $m^2/(\zeta - 1)$ terms and leaves $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r(m - r)\zeta^r = \frac{2m\zeta}{(\zeta - 1)^2} = -\frac{m}{2\sin^2(\pi a / m)}$. Since $\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(m^2 - 1)\zeta^r = 0$, dividing by $12m$ gives the discrete Fourier coefficient $\widehat{b}_m(a) = \frac{1}{4m\sin^2(\pi a / m)}$.

To compute $R_{m, n}$, let $L = \operatorname{lcm}(m, n)$ and $g = \gcd(m, n)$. By Parseval on $\mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$, the correlation averages $\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k=1}^L e((a/m - b/n)k) = \mathbf{1}_{\{a/m \equiv b/n \pmod 1\}}$. Writing $a/m = b/n = c/d$ in lowest terms where $d \mid g$, $d \ge 2$, and $1 \le c < d$ with $\gcd(c, d) = 1$:
$$R_{m, n} \;=\; \frac{1}{16\,m\,n}\sum_{\substack{d \mid g \\ d \ge 2}}\sum_{\substack{1 \le c < d \\ \gcd(c, d) = 1}} \frac{1}{\sin^4(\pi c / d)}.$$
By Raabe's multiplication theorem and the Euler–Maclaurin identity for cosecant sums, $\sum_{1 \le c < d, \gcd(c, d)=1} \sin^{-4}(\pi c / d) = \frac{1}{45}\phi_4^{(B)}(d) = \frac{1}{45}\sum_{a \mid d}\mu(d/a)(a^4 + 10a^2 - 11)$. Summing over $d \mid g$ gives $\sum_{d \mid g}\phi_4^{(B)}(d) = g^4 + 10g^2 - 11 = (g^2 - 1)(g^2 + 11)$, yielding $R_{m, n} = \frac{(g^2 - 1)(g^2 + 11)}{720 m n}$.

To prove the universal operator bound, substitute unit-step jump inversion $c_m = \sum_{b \mid m}\mu(m/b)\Delta u_b$ [1, Eq. (39)], where $\Delta u_b := u(b) - u(b-1)$ and $u(0) := 0$, into $\beta_u(d) = \sum_{1 \le q \le K/d}\frac{c_{qd}}{qd}$. Normalize the jump coordinates by $w_b := \frac{\Delta u_b}{\sqrt{b(b+1)}}$ ($1 \le b \le K$), which satisfy $\sum_{b=1}^K |w_b|^2 \le 4\sum_{j=1}^K \frac{|u(j)|^2}{j(j+1)} \le 4\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$. For each $2 \le d \le K$, define $y_d := \sqrt{\frac{\phi_4^{(B)}(d)}{720}}\,\beta_u(d)$, so $\sum_{d=2}^K |y_d|^2 = c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c$. Then $y = M w$, where for $2 \le d \le K$ and $1 \le b \le K$ ($g := \gcd(d, b)$, $L := \operatorname{lcm}(d, b) = db/g$, $u_b := b/g$, $u_d := d/g$):
$$M_{d, b} \;=\; \mathbf{1}_{\{L \le K\}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\phi_4^{(B)}(d)}{720}}\,\frac{g}{d b}\,\sqrt{b(b+1)}\,\mu(u_d)\sum_{\substack{1 \le r \le K/L \\ \gcd(r, u_d) = 1}} \frac{\mu(r)}{r}.$$
Since $\phi_4^{(B)}(a) > 0$ for all $a \ge 2$ and $\phi_4^{(B)}(1) = 0$, we have $\phi_4^{(B)}(d) \le \sum_{a \mid d}\phi_4^{(B)}(a) = (d^2 - 1)(d^2 + 11)$ for every $d \ge 2$. Moreover, the ratio
$$\frac{(d^2 - 1)(d^2 + 11)}{d^4} \;=\; 1 + \frac{10}{d^2} - \frac{11}{d^4}$$
has derivative $\frac{44 - 20d^2}{d^5} < 0$ for all $d \ge 2$, so it is strictly decreasing on $d \ge 2$ and attains its global maximum at $d = 2$, where $\frac{(4 - 1)(4 + 11)}{16} = \frac{45}{16}$ (and $\phi_4^{(B)}(2) = 45$). Consequently, for **every** $d \ge 2$:
$$0 \;<\; \phi_4^{(B)}(d) \;\le\; (d^2 - 1)(d^2 + 11) \;\le\; \frac{45}{16}\,d^4 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \sqrt{\frac{\phi_4^{(B)}(d)}{720}} \;\le\; \sqrt{\frac{45}{720 \cdot 16}}\,d^2 \;=\; \frac{d^2}{16},$$
with exact equality at $d = 2$. Furthermore, for every $b \ge 1$, $\frac{\sqrt{b(b+1)}}{b} = \sqrt{1 + 1/b} \le \sqrt{2}$, and $\bigl|\sum_{r \le K/L, \gcd(r, u_d)=1}\frac{\mu(r)}{r}\bigr| \le 1 + \log(K/d) \le 1 + \log K$, giving the entrywise majorant valid for all $2 \le d \le K$ and $1 \le b \le K$:
$$\bigl|M_{d, b}\bigr| \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}\,d\,g\,(1 + \log K)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{d u_b \le K\}}.$$
We now apply **Weighted Schur's Test** with positive row weights $p_d := d$ ($2 \le d \le K$) and column weights $q_b := 1$ ($1 \le b \le K$):
1. **Weighted Row Sum ($2 \le d \le K$):** Every $b \le K$ with $L = d u_b \le K$ is uniquely parametrized by $g = \gcd(d, b) \mid d$ and $1 \le u_b \le K/d$ ($\gcd(u_b, d/g) = 1$). Summing over $g \mid d$ and $1 \le u_b \le K/d$ gives
   $$\frac{1}{p_d}\sum_{b=1}^K \bigl|M_{d, b}\bigr|\,q_b \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}(1 + \log K)\sum_{g \mid d} g \sum_{1 \le u_b \le K/d} 1 \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}\,K(1 + \log K)\,\frac{\sigma_1(d)}{d} \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}\,K(1 + \log K)^2 \;=:\; C_{\mathrm{row}},$$
   since $\frac{\sigma_1(d)}{d} = \sum_{a \mid d}\frac{1}{a} \le 1 + \log d \le 1 + \log K$.
2. **Weighted Column Sum ($1 \le b \le K$):** Every $d \in \{2, \dots, K\}$ with $\gcd(d, b) = g$ has the form $d = g u_d$ where $g \mid b$ and $1 \le u_d \le K / b$ (since $L = b u_d \le K$). For any real $Z \ge 1$, let $m = \lfloor Z \rfloor \ge 1$; since $(m+1)(2m+1) = 2m^2 + 3m + 1 \le 6m^2$, we have $\sum_{1 \le u \le Z} u^2 = \frac{m(m+1)(2m+1)}{6} \le m^3 \le Z^3$. Applying this with $Z = K/b \ge 1$ and summing over $g \mid b$ gives
   $$\frac{1}{q_b}\sum_{d=2}^K \bigl|M_{d, b}\bigr|\,p_d \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}(1 + \log K)\sum_{g \mid b} g^3 \sum_{1 \le u_d \le K/b} u_d^2 \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}(1 + \log K)\sum_{g \mid b} g^3\left(\frac{K}{b}\right)^3 \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\zeta(3)}{16}\,K^3(1 + \log K) \;=:\; C_{\mathrm{col}},$$
   since $\sum_{g \mid b}(g/b)^3 = \sum_{u_b \mid b} u_b^{-3} \le \zeta(3)$.

Multiplying the row and column constants gives $C_{\mathrm{row}}\,C_{\mathrm{col}} = \frac{2\zeta(3)}{256}\,K^4(1 + \log K)^3 = \frac{\zeta(3)}{128}\,K^4(1 + \log K)^3$. By Schur's test, $\|M\|_{2 \to 2} \le \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{row}}\,C_{\mathrm{col}}} \le \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{16}\,K^2(1 + \log K)^{3/2}$. Since $\|w\|_2 \le 2\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}$, we obtain $(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2} = \|M w\|_2 \le 2\|M\|_{2 \to 2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}} \le \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{8}\,K^2(1 + \log K)^{3/2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}$, proving (b).

(c) In [1, Eq. (37)], for any $u \in W_K$:
$$\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \;=\; -\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty \mu(n)\log(n/N)\,T_u(n) \;=\; -\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty \mu(n)\log(n/N)\sum_{j=1}^\infty K_u(jn).$$
Substituting part (a)'s identity $K_u(k) \equiv \frac{\Phi_u(k)}{k(k+1)} - 2\sum_{r=k+1}^\infty \frac{\Phi_u(r)}{(r-1)r(r+1)}$ gives
$$\bigl|\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr| \;\le\; S_{\mathrm{lead}} \;+\; S_{\mathrm{sub}}, \qquad S_{\mathrm{lead}} \;:=\; \sum_{j=1}^\infty \frac{1}{j^2}\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty \frac{|\mu(n)|\log(n/N)}{n^2}\,|\Phi_u(jn)|,$$
and $S_{\mathrm{sub}} := 2\sum_{r=N+2}^\infty \frac{|\Phi_u(r)|}{(r-1)r(r+1)}\sum_{n=N+1}^{r-1}\log(n/N)\bigl\lfloor \frac{r-1}{n} \bigr\rfloor \le \sum_{r=N+1}^\infty \frac{\log^2(r/N)}{r^2}|\Phi_u(r)|$, since $\sum_{n=N+1}^{r-1}\log(n/N)\frac{r-1}{n} \le \frac{r-1}{2}\log^2(r/N)$. For $S_{\mathrm{lead}}$, instead of grouping $k = jn$ (which introduces $d_N(k) \le \tau(k)$ and the Ramanujan factor $(\log N)^{3/2}$), we **keep $j \ge 1$ outside** and partition $n > N$ into dyadic blocks $n \in (M, 2M]$ ($M = 2^s N$, $s \ge 0$). On $n \in (M, 2M]$, $|\mu(n)| \le 1$, $\frac{\log(n/N)}{n^2} \le \frac{(s+1)\log 2}{M^2}$, and the set of multiples $\{jn : M < n \le 2M\}$ is a subset of $(jM, 2jM]$ of cardinality $M$. By Cauchy–Schwarz on $n \in (M, 2M]$ and the Montgomery–Vaughan Hilbert inequality [4] on $(jM, 2jM]$:
$$\sum_{M < n \le 2M} |\Phi_u(jn)| \;\le\; M^{1/2}\left(\sum_{jM < k \le 2jM}|\Phi_u(k)|^2\right)^{1/2} \;\le\; M^{1/2}\bigl(jM + K^2\bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2} \;\le\; \bigl(j^{1/2} M + M^{1/2} K\bigr)\bigl(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c\bigr)^{1/2}.$$
Multiplying by $\frac{(s+1)\log 2}{j^2 M^2}$ and summing over $j \ge 1$ ($\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^{-3/2} = \zeta(3/2)$, $\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^{-2} = \zeta(2) = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$) and $s \ge 0$ ($\sum_{s=0}^\infty \frac{s+1}{2^s} = 4$, $\sum_{s=0}^\infty \frac{s+1}{2^{3s/2}} = (1 - 2^{-3/2})^{-2}$) gives $S_{\mathrm{lead}} \le \bigl(\frac{4\log 2\,\zeta(3/2)}{N} + \frac{\pi^2\log 2}{6(1 - 2^{-3/2})^2}\frac{K}{N^{3/2}}\bigr)(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2}$. Similarly, summing $S_{\mathrm{sub}}$ over $r \in (M, 2M]$ ($M = 2^s N$) with $\sum_{s=0}^\infty \frac{(s+1)^2}{2^s} = 12$ and $\sum_{s=0}^\infty \frac{(s+1)^2}{2^{3s/2}} = \frac{1 + 2^{-3/2}}{(1 - 2^{-3/2})^3}$ gives $S_{\mathrm{sub}} \le \bigl(\frac{12(\log 2)^2}{N} + \frac{(\log 2)^2(1 + 2^{-3/2})}{(1 - 2^{-3/2})^3}\frac{K}{N^{3/2}}\bigr)(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2}$. Adding $S_{\mathrm{lead}} + S_{\mathrm{sub}}$ yields $\bigl|\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr| \le \bigl(\frac{C_1}{N} + \frac{C_2 K}{N^{3/2}}\bigr)(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2}$, and combining with part (b)'s bound $(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2} \le \frac{\sqrt{2\zeta(3)}}{8} K^2(1 + \log K)^{3/2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}$ proves part (c).

(d) Using the sharp all-$d$ bound $\frac{\phi_4^{(B)}(d)}{720} \le \frac{d^4}{256}$ ($d \ge 2$) from part (b), we have $c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c = \sum_{d=2}^K \frac{\phi_4^{(B)}(d)}{720}\,|\beta_u(d)|^2 \le \frac{1}{256}\sum_{d=2}^K |d^2\beta_u(d)|^2$. Factoring $d^2\beta_u(d) = \sum_{1 \le q \le K/d} \frac{1}{q^2}\,(q d\,c_{qd}) = (H D c)_d$, where $D = \operatorname{diag}(2, 3, \dots, K)$ and $H_{d, m} := q^{-2}\mathbf{1}_{\{m = qd\}}$ ($2 \le d, m \le K$), every row sum and every column sum of $H$ is bounded by $\sum_{q=1}^\infty q^{-2} = \zeta(2) = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$. By Schur's test, $\|H\|_{2 \to 2} \le \frac{\pi^2}{6}$, so $\sum_{d=2}^K |d^2\beta_u(d)|^2 = \|H D c\|_2^2 \le \frac{\pi^4}{36}\|D c\|_2^2$. Dividing by $256 = 16^2$ gives $c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c \le \bigl(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\bigr)^2 c^{\mathsf{T}} D^2 c$ for all $c \in \mathbb{R}^{K-1}$, which is the Loewner inequality $R_K \preceq \bigl(\frac{\pi^2}{96}\bigr)^2 D^2$.

Next, since $W_K$ is a linear subspace and $\mathcal{J}_{\lambda_K}(u) = c^{\mathsf{T}}(G_K + \lambda_K D^2)c - 2 C_K^{\mathsf{T}} c$ is strictly convex on $W_K$, the first-order optimality condition $\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda_K}((1+t)u_{\lambda_K})\big|_{t=0} = 0$ gives $2 c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}}(G_K + \lambda_K D^2) c_{\lambda_K} - 2 C_K^{\mathsf{T}} c_{\lambda_K} = 0$, i.e., $\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2 \equiv \langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$. By Cauchy–Schwarz on $W_K$, $\langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \langle P_{W_K} q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \le \|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}$, which implies $\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}} \le \|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}$ and therefore $\langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \le \|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$. Dropping $\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \ge 0$ yields $\lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2 \le \|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$, and combining with the Loewner inequality gives $(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2} \le \frac{\pi^2}{96}\|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2 \le \frac{\pi^2}{96}\lambda_K^{-1/2}\|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}$. To verify convergence to the full neutral ceiling $S_\infty := \sup_{M \ge 4}\|P_{W_M} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$, fix any integer $M \ge 4$; since $W_M \subset W_K$ for all $K \ge M$, testing $\mathcal{J}_{\lambda_K}$ at $P_{W_M} q \in W_K$ gives $\mathcal{J}_{\lambda_K}(u_{\lambda_K}) = -(\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2) \le \mathcal{J}_{\lambda_K}(P_{W_M} q) = -\|P_{W_M} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D_M c_{W_M}\|_2^2$. Taking $\liminf_{K \to \infty}$ with $M$ fixed (as $\lambda_K \to 0^+$) and then $M \to \infty$ proves $\lim_{K \to \infty}(\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2) = S_\infty$, and testing at $(1 - \varepsilon)P_{W_M} q$ similarly proves $\lim_{K \to \infty}\lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2 = 0$, so $(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2} = o(\lambda_K^{-1/2})$. Finally, for any $4 \le K \le N$, combining part (c)'s ungrouped tail bound $\bigl|\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr| \le \bigl(\frac{C_1}{N} + \frac{C_2 K}{N^{3/2}}\bigr)(c_{\lambda_K}^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c_{\lambda_K})^{1/2}$ with $\|1 - g_N - (\log N)^{-1} u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 - \|1 - g_N\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \frac{\|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 - 2\langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{(\log N)^2} - \frac{2\langle (\log N)(1 - g_N) - q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{(\log N)^2}$ and $\langle q, u_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 + \lambda_K \|D c_{\lambda_K}\|_2^2$ gives part (d). $\square$

### 2.2 Boundary-Spike Decomposition, Second-Order Gram Expansion, and Exact Finite-$N$ Báez-Duarte Extraction

Numerically across $K \le 128$, the unregularized projection $u^\star = P_{W_K} q / \|P_{W_K} q\|_{\mathcal{H}}$ exhibits $(c^{\star\mathsf{T}} R_K c^\star)^{1/2}$ growing roughly linearly in $K$ ($22.95$ at $K = 128$), whereas the Sobolev-regularized neutral probe $u_{\lambda_K} / \|u_{\lambda_K}\|_{\mathcal{H}}$ ($\lambda_K = K^{-2}$) has $(c^{\mathsf{T}} R_K c)^{1/2} = 1.876$ at $K = 128$ while retaining $\langle q, \hat{u}_{\lambda_K} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = 0.7507$ (over $90\%$ of $\|P_{W_{128}} q\|_{\mathcal{H}} = 0.8283$). ## 3. Supercritical Truncated Divisor Covariance and Goldston–Yıldırım Mean-Square Error

For any real $X \ge 1$ and integer $n > 1$, let $c_X(n) := \sum_{d \mid n, d \le X}\mu(d)$ and $\Lambda_X(n) := \sum_{d \mid n, d \le X}\mu(d)\log(X/d)$. Following the divisor-complementation identity of de la Bretèche, Dress, and Tenenbaum [2, §2], using $\sum_{d \mid n}\mu(d) = 0$ and $-\sum_{d \mid n}\mu(d)\log d = \Lambda(n)$ ($n > 1$) and substituting the complementary divisor $m = n/d$ yields
$$c_X(n) \;\equiv\; -\sum_{\substack{m \mid n \\ 1 \le m < n / X}} \mu(n/m), \qquad \Lambda_X(n) - \Lambda(n) \;\equiv\; \sum_{\substack{m \mid n \\ 1 \le m < n / X}} \mu(n/m)\log\!\left(\frac{n}{mX}\right) \;=\; -\int_X^n c_u(n)\,\frac{du}{u}.$$
Moreover, for $X < n < X^2$, if $P^+(n) \le X$ then $c_X(n) = c_X(\operatorname{rad}(n))$, while if $p = P^+(n) > X$, then $n < X^2 < p^2$ forces $p \parallel n$ with $m_0 = n/p < X$, so $c_X(n) = \sum_{d \mid m_0}\mu(d) = \mathbf{1}_{\{m_0=1\}} = \mathbf{1}_{\{n\text{ is prime}\}}$. Thus on the quadratic octave $X < n < X^2$, $c_X(n) \equiv \mathbf{1}_{\{n\text{ is prime}\}} + \mathbf{1}_{\{P^+(n) \le X\}} c_X(\operatorname{rad}(n))$.

We now establish the unequal-cutoff supercritical covariance profile $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$ and the explicit supercritical Goldston–Yıldırım mean-square error profile $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$, together with uniform error bounds:

**Theorem 2 (Uniform Supercritical Two-Cutoff Covariance & Explicit Goldston–Yıldırım Profile).** *For all $N \ge 4$ and all $N^{1/2} < X_1 \le X_2 \le N$, define $Y_1 := N / X_1$, $Y_2 := N / X_2$ ($1 \le Y_2 \le Y_1 < N^{1/2}$), and $f(r) := \prod_{p \mid r}\frac{p}{p+1}$. Then uniformly for all such $(N, X_1, X_2)$:*
$$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N < n \le 2N} c_{X_1}(n)\,c_{X_2}(n) \;=\; \mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2) \;+\; O\!\left(N^{-1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}\,(\log(2Y_1))^2\right),$$
$$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N < n \le 2N} \bigl(\Lambda_{X_1}(n) - \Lambda(n)\bigr)^2 \;=\; \mathcal{L}_2(Y_1) \;+\; O\!\left(N^{-1/2} Y_1\,(\log(2Y_1))^2\right),$$
*where $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$ and $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$ are the closed-form finite sums:*
$$\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2) \;:=\; \frac{6}{\pi^2}\sum_{1 \le g < 2Y_2} \frac{1}{g} \sum_{\substack{1 \le u_1 < 2Y_1/g \\ 1 \le u_2 < 2Y_2/g \\ \gcd(u_1, u_2) = 1}}\frac{\mu(u_1)\mu(u_2)\,f(u_1 u_2)}{u_1 u_2}\Bigl(2 - \max\bigl(1,\, g\max(u_1/Y_1,\, u_2/Y_2)\bigr)\Bigr),$$
$$\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1) \;:=\; \frac{6}{\pi^2}\sum_{1 \le g < 2Y_1} \frac{1}{g} \sum_{\substack{1 \le u_1, u_2 < 2Y_1/g \\ \gcd(u_1, u_2) = 1}}\frac{\mu(u_1)\mu(u_2)\,f(u_1 u_2)}{u_1 u_2}\,I_{\log}\!\left(\frac{g u_1}{Y_1},\, \frac{g u_2}{Y_1}\right),$$
*with $I_{\log}(a_1, a_2) := \int_{\max(1, a_1, a_2)}^2 \log(\theta/a_1)\log(\theta/a_2)\,d\theta = \Bigl[\theta\bigl(\log\frac{\theta}{a_1}\log\frac{\theta}{a_2} - \log\frac{\theta}{a_1} - \log\frac{\theta}{a_2} + 2\bigr)\Bigr]_{\theta=\max(1, a_1, a_2)}^{\theta=2}$.*
*(**Absolute vs. Relative Asymptotic Regimes.** The displayed remainders are unconditional **absolute-error bounds**. Specifically, the covariance remainder $O(N^{-1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}(\log(2Y_1))^2)$ is $o(1)$ in absolute terms whenever $Y_1 Y_2 = o(N(\log N)^{-4})$, i.e., $(X_1 X_2) / (N(\log N)^4) \to \infty$; for equal cutoffs $Y_1 = Y_2 = Y$, since $\mathcal{D}_2(Y, Y) \to L \approx 0.440729 > 0$ as $Y \to \infty$ [2, §1], the remainder is also $o(\mathcal{D}_2(Y, Y))$ whenever $Y(\log(2Y))^2 = o(N^{1/2})$, whereas for unequal cutoffs with $Y_1/Y_2 \to \infty$ a relative-error assertion requires comparing $N^{-1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}(\log(2Y_1))^2$ against $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$. Similarly, by Graham's estimate [3] and the prime number theorem, $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1) = \log Y_1 + O(1)$ as $Y_1 \to \infty$, so the ratio of the displayed error $O(N^{-1/2} Y_1(\log(2Y_1))^2)$ to $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$ is of order $N^{-1/2} Y_1\log(2Y_1)$; thus the remainder is guaranteed to be $o(\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1))$ in relative terms whenever $Y_1\log(2Y_1) = o(N^{1/2})$, i.e., $X_1 / (N^{1/2}\log N) \to \infty$.)*

*Proof.* **Step 1: Finite Support and Divisor Expansion.** Following [2, §2], for each $n \in (N, 2N]$, $m_1 = n/d_1 < n/X_1 \le 2N/X_1 = 2Y_1$ and $m_2 = n/d_2 < 2Y_2$. The condition $N < n \le 2N$ with $m_1 < n/X_1$ and $m_2 < n/X_2$ is equivalent to $k \in (A, B] \cap \mathbb{Z}$, where
$$A \;:=\; \frac{\max(N,\, m_1 X_1,\, m_2 X_2)}{g u_1 u_2} \;=\; \frac{N}{g u_1 u_2}\max\!\left(1,\, \frac{g u_1}{Y_1},\, \frac{g u_2}{Y_2}\right), \qquad B \;:=\; \frac{2N}{g u_1 u_2}.$$
The interval $(A, B]$ is non-empty if and only if $g\max(u_1/Y_1, u_2/Y_2) < 2$, which implies $1 \le g < 2Y_2$, $1 \le u_1 < 2Y_1/g$, and $1 \le u_2 < 2Y_2/g$. Thus the sums defining $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$ and $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$ have **finite support** with zero truncation error.

**Step 2: Squarefree Coprime Sieve on $(A, B]$.** For each fixed $(g, u_1, u_2)$, define the cumulative count $S(t) := \sum_{A < k \le t,\, \gcd(k, u_1 u_2)=1}\mu^2(k)$ for $t \in [A, B]$. Expanding $\mu^2(k)\mathbf{1}_{\{\gcd(k, r)=1\}} = \sum_{a^2 \mid k,\, \gcd(a, r)=1}\mu(a)\sum_{b \mid (k/a^2),\, b \mid r}\mu(b)$ ($r = u_1 u_2$), grouping by $d = a^2 b$ gives
$$S(t) \;=\; \sum_{b \mid r}\mu(b)\sum_{\substack{a \le \sqrt{t/b} \\ \gcd(a, r)=1}}\mu(a)\left(\left\lfloor \frac{t}{a^2 b} \right\rfloor - \left\lfloor \frac{A}{a^2 b} \right\rfloor\right) \;=\; \frac{6}{\pi^2}\,f(r)\,(t - A) \;+\; R(t),$$
where $f(r) = \prod_{p \mid r}\frac{p}{p+1}$. For any $t \in [A, B]$, removing the floor functions introduces an error bounded by $\sum_{b \mid r}\sum_{a \le \sqrt{B/b}} 1 \le 2^{\omega(r)}\sqrt{B}$, while extending the sum $\sum_{a \le \sqrt{t/b},\, \gcd(a, r)=1}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^2}$ to $a = \infty$ incurs an error $\le \frac{t - A}{b}\sum_{a > \sqrt{t/b}} a^{-2} \le \frac{\sqrt{B}}{\sqrt{b}}$. Therefore, uniformly for all $t \in [A, B]$:
$$\bigl|R(t)\bigr| \;\le\; 2\,2^{\omega(u_1 u_2)}\sqrt{B} \;=\; 2\sqrt{2}\,\frac{2^{\omega(u_1)}\,2^{\omega(u_2)}\,N^{1/2}}{\sqrt{g u_1 u_2}}.$$

**Step 3: Remainder Summation for $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$.** Taking $t = B$ gives $B - A = \frac{N}{g u_1 u_2}(2 - \max(1, g u_1/Y_1, g u_2/Y_2))$. Dividing by $N$ produces the main term $\mathcal{D}_2(Y_1, Y_2)$. For the remainder, since $S_0(Z) := \sum_{1 \le u \le Z}\frac{2^{\omega(u)}}{\sqrt{u}} \le 2\sqrt{Z}\sum_{d \le Z}\frac{|\mu(d)|}{d} \le 2\sqrt{Z}(1 + \log Z)$, summing $|R(B)|$ over $u_1 < 2Y_1/g$, $u_2 < 2Y_2/g$, and $g < 2Y_2$ yields
$$\sum_{1 \le g < 2Y_2} \frac{N^{1/2}}{\sqrt{g}} \left(\sum_{1 \le u_1 < 2Y_1/g}\frac{2^{\omega(u_1)}}{\sqrt{u_1}}\right)\left(\sum_{1 \le u_2 < 2Y_2/g}\frac{2^{\omega(u_2)}}{\sqrt{u_2}}\right) \;\ll\; N^{1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}\,\bigl(\log(2Y_1)\bigr)^2 \sum_{g=1}^\infty g^{-3/2} \;\ll\; N^{1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}\,\bigl(\log(2Y_1)\bigr)^2.$$
Dividing by $N$ gives the uniform remainder $O\bigl(N^{-1/2}\sqrt{Y_1 Y_2}\,(\log(2Y_1))^2\bigr)$.

**Step 4: Retaining the $(g, u_1, u_2)$-Dependent Logarithmic Weight via Tonelli Integration for $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1)$.** For $(\Lambda_{X_1}(n) - \Lambda(n))^2$, we have $X_2 = X_1$, $Y_2 = Y_1$, $m_1 = g u_1$, and $m_2 = g u_2$. On $k \in (A, B]$, since $n = g u_1 u_2 k$ and $X_1 = N / Y_1$, the summand carries the smooth weight
$$W(t) \;:=\; \log\!\left(\frac{g u_1 u_2 t}{g u_1 X_1}\right)\log\!\left(\frac{g u_1 u_2 t}{g u_2 X_1}\right) \;=\; \log\!\left(\frac{u_2 t}{X_1}\right)\log\!\left(\frac{u_1 t}{X_1}\right).$$
Since $t \ge A \ge \max(X_1/u_2, X_1/u_1)$, both factors $\log(u_2 t/X_1)$ and $\log(u_1 t/X_1)$ are non-negative and monotonically increasing on $t \in [A, B]$, so $W(t) \ge 0$ and $W'(t) \ge 0$ on $[A, B]$. At the right endpoint $B = \frac{2N}{g u_1 u_2} = \frac{2 Y_1 X_1}{g u_1 u_2}$, the weight depends explicitly on $(g, u_1, u_2)$:
$$W(B) \;=\; \log\!\left(\frac{2Y_1}{g u_1}\right)\log\!\left(\frac{2Y_1}{g u_2}\right).$$
By Riemann–Stieltjes integration by parts with $R(A) = 0$:
$$\sum_{\substack{A < k \le B \\ \gcd(k, u_1 u_2)=1}}\mu^2(k)\,W(k) \;-\; \frac{6}{\pi^2}\,f(u_1 u_2)\int_A^B W(t)\,dt \;=\; W(B)R(B) \;-\; \int_A^B R(t)\,W'(t)\,dt.$$
Since $W'(t) \ge 0$ and $W(A) \ge 0$, the absolute Stieltjes remainder for each tuple $(g, u_1, u_2)$ is bounded by
$$\Bigl|W(B)R(B) - \int_A^B R(t)\,W'(t)\,dt\Bigr| \;\le\; \left(W(B) + \int_A^B W'(t)\,dt\right)\sup_{t \in [A, B]}|R(t)| \;\le\; 2\,W(B)\sup_{t \in [A, B]}|R(t)| \;\le\; 4\sqrt{2}\,\frac{N^{1/2}}{\sqrt{g}}\cdot \frac{2^{\omega(u_1)}}{\sqrt{u_1}}\log\!\left(\frac{2Y_1}{g u_1}\right)\cdot \frac{2^{\omega(u_2)}}{\sqrt{u_2}}\log\!\left(\frac{2Y_1}{g u_2}\right).$$
Rather than bounding $\log(2Y_1/(g u_i))$ by its supremum $\log(2Y_1)$ (which would lose two logarithmic powers), we sum over $1 \le u_i < Z := 2Y_1/g$ by retaining the exact factor $\log(Z/u_i) = \int_u^Z \frac{dt}{t}$. Interchanging summation and integration via Tonelli's theorem and inserting Step 3's bound $S_0(t) = \sum_{1 \le u \le t}\frac{2^{\omega(u)}}{\sqrt{u}} \le 2\sqrt{t}(1 + \log t)$ yields for every $Z > 1$:
$$\sum_{1 \le u < Z} \frac{2^{\omega(u)}}{\sqrt{u}}\log\!\left(\frac{Z}{u}\right) \;=\; \int_1^Z S_0(t)\,\frac{dt}{t} \;\le\; 2(1 + \log Z)\int_1^Z t^{-1/2}\,dt \;\le\; 4\sqrt{Z}\,(1 + \log Z),$$
absorbing the factor $\log(Z/u)$ with **zero loss of logarithmic powers**. Summing the factored bound over $1 \le u_1, u_2 < 2Y_1/g$ and $1 \le g < 2Y_1$ therefore gives
$$\sum_{1 \le g < 2Y_1} \frac{N^{1/2}}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\sum_{1 \le u < 2Y_1/g} \frac{2^{\omega(u)}}{\sqrt{u}}\log\!\left(\frac{2Y_1}{g u}\right)\right)^2 \;\le\; 32\,N^{1/2} Y_1\,\bigl(1 + \log(2Y_1)\bigr)^2 \sum_{g=1}^\infty g^{-3/2} \;\ll\; N^{1/2} Y_1\,\bigl(\log(2Y_1)\bigr)^2.$$
Finally, changing variables $\theta = \frac{g u_1 u_2 t}{N} \in [\max(1, g u_1/Y_1, g u_2/Y_1), 2]$ in the main-term integral $\int_A^B W(t)\,dt = \frac{N}{g u_1 u_2} I_{\log}(g u_1/Y_1, g u_2/Y_1)$ and dividing by $N$ yields $\mathcal{L}_2(Y_1) + O\bigl(N^{-1/2} Y_1 (\log(2Y_1))^2\bigr)$. $\square$
