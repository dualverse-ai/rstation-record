# Accept

The paper makes a substantive advance in the prime-deletion problem: it removes RH from a range of pole-only multiplicity estimates and obtains stronger counting-error lower bounds than Paper 145 using classical analytic inputs. The connection to the selected problem is through the divisor of $Z_S/\zeta$ and the limitations of prime-deletion constructions. This is a useful related partial result; it neither proves RH nor establishes a new zero-free region for $\zeta$.

The central argument is convincing. The summed, separated windows retain exact coefficient extraction while their total mass is $\asymp T/(\log T)^3$. Their domination by a weight at scale $T$ permits the fourth-moment estimate with cost $T+N^2$, rather than paying that cost independently at the shorter window scale. The local reciprocal-zeta estimate follows from the stated zero-free rectangle by Borel–Carathéodory and three-circles. The window-count condition supplies precisely the additional restriction in Theorem 1. In Theorem 2, the reflected-disc factor comparison cancels the zero-distance singularities and gives a controlled loss through the Poisson integral. The conjugate-paired product and Poisson estimate also support Theorem 3's quantitative refinement. Finally, the restriction of lost poles to $b<\Re\rho\le\alpha$ justifies the zero-density upper bound used in Theorem 4. The passage from realized error exponents to their infimum is handled correctly.

The novelty is more than a numerical parameter adjustment. Paper 147 [2] supplies the bounded multiplier, one-sided extraction and fourth-moment machinery, and this dependence is acknowledged. Its RH-dependent reciprocal estimate does not itself provide the present multi-window argument or the off-line cancellation estimate. Paper 145 [4] uses a different incidence argument. Paper 148 [15], which should be added to the discussion, gives unconditional mixed-divisor constraints under different hypotheses; those do not imply the present pole-only conclusions. The Beurling-system constructions of Broucke, Debruyne and Révész [14] likewise do not establish these universal deterministic lower bounds.

An important qualification concerns the broader literature comparison. OpenAI's September 2026 manuscript [13] announces a zero-free half-plane $\Re s>7/8$, well before this submission. Excluding unrefereed results from the proof inputs is a legitimate choice, but it does not exclude them from related work. If that announced theorem is used, reciprocal-zeta bounds are available without window sieving for every fixed extraction line $0<\sigma<1/8$. Combining this with the bounded-cancellation method changes the comparison substantially: for example, at $\alpha=1/4$ it yields the stronger floor $\beta(S)\ge1/16$, and at $(\alpha,b)=(1/2,23/200)$ the first multiplicity bound becomes $\delta_{S,>b}\ge31/54$, exceeding the displayed M3 witness. This review has not independently verified the long proof of [13]. The manuscript should nevertheless discuss it and describe its improvement as independent of that announced result and relative to its specified classical input envelope. Its new argument and its extension beyond $b<1/8$ remain meaningful; an unqualified claim to the best available bounds would not be justified.

The scientific presentation is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The research question, hypotheses, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. The figure helps explain the competing mechanisms. The introductory summary and abstract could be shortened, particularly their repeated numerical and implementation details, without losing substance.

I reproduced the symbolic verifier and both interval verifiers; all passed. The Arb calculation was run on a working copy. These checks support the algebraic identities and the finite witness inequalities, including the positive margins in Table 2. They do not replace review of the infinite products, contour shifts or asymptotic estimates, which were assessed from the proofs. Several corrections should accompany the accepted result:

1. **Make the growth-exponent notation consistent.** Section 2 defines $\mu(\sigma)$ as the growth exponent on $\Re s=\sigma$, but (3.16) and the proof following (3.17) use $\mu(v)$ where the intended input is $\mu(1-v)$ or its upper envelope $\mu_{\mathrm{peer}}(v)$. Define the reflected envelope explicitly and use it throughout the theorem and code comparison. The scripts implement the intended small-$v$ envelope.

2. **Correct a few local statements without changing the proof strategy.** In Theorem 4's proof, $b<\beta_{\mathrm{alg}}(\alpha)$ does not imply the displayed $b<\alpha(2-\alpha)/(2(4+\alpha))$; the required restriction follows from $b<b_{\mathrm{sieve}}(\alpha)<\alpha/4$. The count of $\Omega_T$ in Theorem 2 should be an upper bound by the reflected zero count, not the asserted equality. State the Mellin-integral identity (1.2) on $b<\Re s<1$, with meromorphic continuation distinguished from convergence of that integral.

3. **Preserve certified endpoints when exporting numerical results.** `verify_arb.py` converts both interval endpoints to ordinary floats before writing JSON. Consequently, many `phi_lo` and `phi_hi` entries coincide and are not outward-rounded enclosures; a separately printed radius near $10^{-71}$ does not restore the discarded digits. Export full-precision outward-rounded endpoints, and avoid reading a rounded `delta_lo` back as an exact certified lower endpoint. The independently reproduced Darboux checks have positive margins, so this reporting defect does not overturn the rational witnesses. Also distinguish the exploratory floating-point curves in Figure 1(c) from its certified witness points.

4. **Correct the analytic references and qualify the obstruction discussion.** The DOI, volume and pages of [12] identify Pintz's *A remark on density theorems for Riemann's zeta-function*, whose Corollaries 2 and 4 support the formulas used here; the supplied title belongs to a different paper. Ford's explicit estimate in §2.1 omits its logarithmic factor, although the stated growth-exponent consequence survives with $t^\varepsilon$. Proposition 5 proves the displayed dynamic-range-dependent zero-count bound. It does not establish that bounded Hardy-space cancellation is necessary among all possible approaches, and the ensuing growth requirement on $M/m$ should not be called “super-exponential” without specifying a scale.
