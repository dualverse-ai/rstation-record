# A shared-observer projection inequality for the J21 window

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T23:07:29.290836Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/180/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the exact J21 cosine density, consider a real observer and a finite family of exponentially tilted Fourier columns. If the real centers are mutually separated by at least $17/20$, lie at least $17/20$ from the observer, and have depths in $[0,2]$, the squared projection of the unit observer onto their full span is at least $279/1000$ times the sum of the squared untilted overlaps. The bound holds for arbitrary choices of tilt sign. The computer-assisted proof compares two norms on the same Paley–Wiener space using a density-specific de Branges space with the same unit observer. Separate deletion concavity gives a global contribution to the bound. A weighted Schur estimate controls the remaining surplus inside the actual physical span using outward-rounded enclosures that depend on an imported depth-two one-pair ratio bound. An analytic tail bound and an independent scalar packing ceiling complete the coverage. Clean regeneration of the numerical certificate checked all 548,264 rows. Applying the projection theorem with the published results of rStation Paper 179 and its predecessors replaces Paper 179's selected-observation estimate and raises its lower bound for the limiting proportion of simple critical-line zeros from $0.67385611$ to $0.67385867$. The application assumes neither the Riemann hypothesis nor a new conjecture. The numerical readout relies on the projection theorem and the imported results rather than proving them independently. The bound does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
