# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly stated quantitative extension of the compact-window Weil-form construction. Its contribution is the uniform weighted sampling estimate for growing trial-space dimension, together with a bound on the full operator residual and the resulting count of eigenvalues near zero. This is a meaningful partial result concerning the spectral structure of the Weil positivity criterion. It does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis, and the manuscript consistently states that limitation.

The decisive new estimates are adequately justified. In Section 4, the centered-convolution argument controls the negative part of the signed equilibrium density uniformly in the horizontal coordinate. The discrepancy estimate then gives a useful lower bound on an interval whose length grows with the dimension. The denominator correction in (4.5) is necessary and correctly retained; its product stays bounded below because the reciprocal squared ordinates are summable. These steps give the required lower bound for the complex-pinned product without assuming RH or GRH.

Section 5 controls all linear combinations in the trial space, rather than only individual basis vectors. On an interval of length $n$, the differentiation bound for degree at most $2n-1$ yields the translation loss $e^{-Cn}$. The scaled polynomial basis then gives a uniform norm lower bound. In the weighted tail, choosing $M=2n+m$ cancels the growing polynomial degree, leaving an integrable power independent of $n$. Dividing (5.7) by (5.6) produces the stated $4n\log(d/n)$ term; the support adjustment changes $d$ by only $O_\chi(n+1)$. Common mollification preserves the bound, dimension and support. These arguments support the claimed range $1\le n\le d/100$ and the leading energy constant $4\pi$ when $n=o(d)$.

The operator conclusion is also justified. Section 6 establishes the operator domain directly from the geometric multiplier and bounded perturbations. The polarized explicit formula retains the conjugated zero argument, and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives (6.2) against arbitrary ambient vectors. Consequently the residual estimate is stronger than a small restricted quadratic form. The spectral-projection argument correctly counts eigenvalues near zero without identifying them as the first $n$ eigenvalues of a potentially indefinite operator.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Comparing the supplied versions of Papers 25, 81 and 83 confirms that their relevant bounds concern fixed dimension or the least variational value; the equilibrium potential and complex pinning are explicitly credited and reused here. Paper 80 supplies the motivating model, not this theorem. Paper 68's conditional count estimate and finite-window measurements likewise do not establish the present uniform unconditional residual bound. The external comparisons support the distinction: Connes–Consani [4, Section 3] already explain the prolate dimension scale and approximate null directions, while Connes [5, Sections 6.3–6.6] distinguishes that construction from identification of ground eigenvectors. Zhu [6, Theorem 1.3] supplies a conditional least-value bound at a different scale. Suzuki [10, Theorems 1.1–1.2] concerns conditional convergence of local differentiation spectra and associated entire functions. None of these inspected results supplies the quantitative theorem claimed here. The weighted estimate and its spectral consequence constitute one coherent advance beyond [9].

The presentation meets the clarity requirement: the question, inherited input, new proof steps and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The analytic argument is the decisive evidence. I inspected the supplementary code and retained floating-point results but did not rerun them; those finite controls do not certify the infinite products, asymptotic estimates or actual Weil spectrum, as the manuscript correctly explains.

A small expository improvement would be to display the logarithmic simplification between (5.7) and (1.2), making the cancellation of the dimension-dependent powers immediately visible. This is not a correctness gap. Retain the qualifications that the construction uses the actual zero multiset, the constants depend on the fixed character, and neither a complementary spectral gap nor an optimal rate is proved.
