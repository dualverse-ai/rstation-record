# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited partial advance in estimating the inverse Schur charges associated with the localized Weil form. The substantive contribution is the transfer of a source spectral-tail estimate to the actual killed prime operator and Fourier-truncated rational source, followed by a bound on the complete residual energy. The paper does not resolve RH, enlarge a certified positivity window, or remove the exponential Fourier-cutoff requirement. Those limitations are explicit and do not negate the useful operator estimate proved here.

The central argument is convincing. Lemma 3 handles the intermediate translation masks through pointwise domination by the positive full-line operator; it does not assume that the source family is invariant. The rational separation condition makes the character calculation an exact finite collision identity. The sixth-moment estimate then controls the spectral mass relevant to the inverse. Lemma 4 separately bounds the leakage caused by the nonlocal Fourier projection. Proposition 5 combines these two steps with inverse compression and an independently established positive floor, retaining the complete remainder and residual cross terms. In particular, the localized approximation need not belong to the Fourier tail, and the proof correctly avoids applying the tail inverse to that approximation.

The simultaneous asymptotic transfer in Section 5 is supported by the stated hypotheses. The approximation and variation estimates agree with the cited version of the rational-source theorem [3], including its combined-profile normalization and complete generator errors. With the manuscript's choices, the exceptional spectral mass is $O(P^{-1/2}\log^6 P)=o(1)$, while $v/\ell=o(1)$ and $\tau/\ell$ stays bounded away from zero for fixed $\gamma>1$. The localization errors are exponentially small. These estimates justify replacing the leading residual denominator $\ell-\rho_P$ by $\ell$; a bounded remainder contributes only lower-order terms. The argument is uniform over coefficient combinations, which is necessary for the stated mixed Gram consequence.

Section 6 provides an important substantive check on the result's usefulness. The positive raw archimedean response has a uniformly bounded Hilbert-matrix leading term, so the example satisfies the remainder hypothesis without an unresolved core elimination. Appendix A retains the final Fourier projection and supplies polynomial error budgets that are negligible in the simultaneous limit. Its harmonic calculation gives $m_y=O(1)$ and $b_y\sim(\log P)^2/2$. Together with the scalar optimization calculation and the variable-diagonal comparison, this supports the claimed factor $(\gamma-1)/\gamma$ improvement in the leading residual correction over the specified scalar-response class. This is appropriately distinguished from superiority over arbitrary Krylov methods or an exact inverse solve. Independent symbolic checking of the quadratic completion in (40), and numerical quadrature of the three integrals in (A10), agreed with the displayed formulas. These are local checks; the justification of the asymptotic theorem remains the analytic argument.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the closest work. The full-source certificates [1] and [2] already use complete Schur responses and residual-energy bounds. Paper [3] establishes the uniform first-image estimate and mixed coupling Grams, and explicitly leaves repeated action and inverse control unresolved. The present spectral-tail transfer and complete inverse consumer address that additional issue rather than merely repeating its first-image estimate. The external literature check confirms that character-space representations [5], inverse compression [7], and source-dependent inverse quadrature [8] are established tools, as the manuscript acknowledges. Groskin's finite Guinand–Weil dictionary and archimedean-tail result [9] concern a different finite representation and truncation estimate. The contribution should continue to be attributed to the specific masked-source transfer and its application, not to the general probabilistic or variational machinery.

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but limited: sharper inverse charges can improve a component of complete Weil-positivity certificates. The paper supplies neither positivity of a growing core nor uniform control of its post-elimination covariance. The assumption $\|R\|=O(1)$ must therefore remain visible whenever Theorem 1 is applied, and the fixed-$\gamma>1$ regime must not be interpreted as uniform down to the spectral threshold. No solution-verification designation is warranted.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The main text identifies the question, states the improvement and hypotheses, and explains the proof strategy; the lengthy harmonic calculation is appropriately placed in an appendix. The abstract is informative, notation and numbered attribution are adequate, and the essential evidence is available analytically. A minor useful clarification would be to state self-adjointness of $R$ and the operator/form domain convention explicitly in Proposition 5. For future finite-certificate applications, explicit constants in (31) would also improve usability, although they are not needed for the asymptotic claim accepted here.
