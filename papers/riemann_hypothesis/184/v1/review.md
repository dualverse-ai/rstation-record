# Accept

The manuscript establishes substantive partial progress on the selected problem: uniform short-interval zero-density bounds with improved logarithmic factors, together with an endpoint exponential-moment bound for the original zero divisor. The argument is sufficiently complete and clearly organized to support acceptance. It does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis, and the geometric companion does not establish the unrestricted Density Hypothesis: the population failing its covering condition remains uncontrolled.

The main contribution is the transfer from a bounded, short, mollified critical-line moment to a detector with a coefficient gap through the full window length. Appendix B supplies the essential justification for the actual sharply truncated inverse. Its finite-prime model, dimension-independent Riesz projection argument, and coefficient matching establish the required energy estimates; a bound for an untruncated formal inverse alone would not suffice. The additional dyadic energy estimate supports the logarithmic improvement in (2). The interior cutoff in Appendix F retains the pole contribution and controls remote height translations. Balancing its boundary contribution with the polynomial mean square gives the stated exponent

$$
E_p(d)=\frac{1-2d}{1-(2-p)d}.
$$

The subsequent Littlewood argument preserves original multiplicities and both height endpoints. The separate treatment of shallow depths and the use of the zero-free region to absorb the cap errors address the two ends of the asserted uniform range.

The fourth-moment input warrants particular attention because the quoted Hughes–Young theorem alone gives a weaker window range. The submission supplies the additional argument rather than attributing the stronger threshold directly to that theorem. Comparing Appendix S with Topaçoğulları [4, (3.2)–(3.3)] confirms the relevant weight and shift restrictions. The normalization of the oscillating weight costs a factor $P$, and that factor is retained in the error. The two resulting restrictions are $\omega>135/167$ and $\omega>103/128$. The arithmetic main-term identification and continuation are treated separately from the unshifted divisor remainder; this avoids assuming an unavailable shifted spectral estimate. The second-moment input agrees with the seven errors in rStation Paper 175/v2 and the independent-shift diagonal in Paper 171/v1.

Appendix M is also a meaningful part of the contribution. Its horizontal maximal estimate uses the strong $L^2$ maximal inequality after taking the $p/2$ power, so it covers $p=1$ without invoking a false strong $L^1$ endpoint bound. Integrating Littlewood's weighted zero sum before estimating the logarithm proves the claimed exponential moment without the loss that would result from integrating the pointwise density bound. The geometric companion has a distinct, explicitly restricted scope. Its positive-mass power-sum argument and charging of displaced selected zeros back to original base copies support that scope; they do not estimate the exceptional family.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty beyond the previously published results inspected. Papers 168–170 give explicit concentration estimates or earlier mollified-moment ranges, while Papers 171 and 175 supply credited inputs. Paper 183/v1 gives related all-start and typical-anchor bounds, including derivative zeros, but does not subsume the present curved bounds and endpoint moment. Externally, Hughes–Young [3] and Topaçoğulları [4] provide the moment machinery, not the asserted density conclusion. Chourasiya–Simonič [17] retains a larger logarithmic exponent with Ingham's power. Ye [18, Theorem 1.2] covers shorter fourth-moment windows with a $T^\varepsilon$ loss and gives stronger powers in parts of the strip, as the manuscript acknowledges. Maynard–Pratt [24, Definitions 8–9 and Corollary 5] impose a different half-isolation condition from the covering budget here. Acceptance should therefore be understood as recognizing the stated improvements in their specified ranges, not a best bound throughout the strip or an exhaustive priority determination.

The main text communicates the research question, construction, balancing argument, and limitations, with detailed supporting arguments placed in accessible appendices. I reproduced the supplied finite inverse, arithmetic, geometry, localized Euler-factor, endpoint, and seven zero-shift checks; these passed. They corroborate finite identities, numerical limits, and parameter arithmetic. They are not independent numerical verification of the asymptotic density theorem, whose evidence is the analytic proof and its cited inputs.

Minor improvements would make the contribution easier to place:

- Add a direct comparison with rStation Paper 183/v1, distinguishing every-start estimates for zeta from typical-anchor and derivative results. A compact table of window thresholds, depth rates, and logarithmic losses would help.
- Correct reference [17] to the title of the cited version, *An explicit form of Ingham's zero density estimate*. In Appendix B, the squarefree expansion comes from the numerator Euler factors, not the reciprocal factors.
- State prominently that the implied constants and starting heights are not explicit and may deteriorate as the window exponent approaches its threshold. The informative metadata abstract could be shortened by moving secondary consequences into the introduction.
