# The endpoint case of the discrete Beurling criterion in weighted L^p and Lorentz spaces

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 3 · 2026-10-28T17:43:04.676274Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/63/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Báez-Duarte's criterion: RH holds iff the indicator of $(0,1]$ lies in the $L^2(0,1)$ closure of the functions $\rho_k(t)=\{1/(kt)\}-\{1/t\}/k$, $k\ge2$. We prove analogues at the exact exponent in weighted $L^p$ and Lorentz spaces, with zeros allowed on the boundary. Throughout, the step space is the closure of the finite linear combinations of the indicators of $I_m=(1/(m+1),1/m]$.

(1) Let $1<p\le2$, $p/2-1\le\gamma<p-1$ and $\theta=(\gamma+1)/p$. Then $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$ iff the indicator lies in the closed span of the $\rho_k$ in $L^p((0,1),t^\gamma dt)$, iff that span is the step space. Necessity is known (Gallardo-Gutiérrez–Seco; Manzur–Noor–Quintero). The new part is sufficiency at $\theta=\Theta:=\sup\operatorname{Re}\rho$ over the zeros. $\theta=1/2$ gives RH criteria in $L^p(t^{p/2-1}dt)$, $1<p\le2$.

(2) Lorentz scale: for $1<p\le2$ and $1<q\le\infty$, the span is dense in the step space of $L^{p,q}$ iff $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/p$. For $q=\infty$ the step space contains every bounded function constant on each $I_m$, but not every unbounded one. For $p=2$, $q=1$ the span is never dense.

(3) If finitely many zeros have real part $>\theta$, the closed span is the set of step-space functions whose Mellin transforms vanish there with multiplicity. Its codimension is their number, with no zero-free strip below $\theta$ assumed. For $L^p$ ($p<2$) and $L^{p,q}$ ($1<q<2$) we found no earlier proof.

(4) Method: damped Möbius approximants on $\operatorname{Re}s=\theta-\varepsilon/4$, where a Hadamard-product bound beats the Hölder loss. For (3) the target is a Dirichlet polynomial vanishing at the exceptional zeros.

(5) For $p=2$, the distances of Paper 6 and of Dimitrov–Oliveira agree up to a factor $a^{-1/2}$, so $d_N(\Theta)\to0$.

No rate in $N$ is proved; nothing locates the zeros.
