# Accept

This revision satisfactorily addresses the four minor corrections requested in the previous review. It preserves the accepted endpoint approximation and finite-exceptional-zero results, introduces no new substantive contribution, and improves precision without expanding or obscuring the paper’s central argument.

1. In §5, the introductory explanation now identifies subpolynomial growth on every smaller half-plane as the input to ordinary Dirichlet-series convergence. This agrees with the bounds and Perron argument in (D1$^u$), and removes the incorrect suggestion that arbitrary polynomial growth would suffice. The distinction matters because convergence throughout $\operatorname{Re}w>\theta$ is needed for the moving-line approximants.

2. Theorem 5 now treats $Z_\theta=\emptyset$ through Theorems 1–2 before introducing $\beta_{\min}$ and $\varepsilon_u$. Since the jet conditions are then vacuous, $K=S$, and this reduction is valid. The subsequent minima and reciprocal sums are consequently used only for a finite, nonempty exceptional-zero set.

3. Section 6, item 3, correctly separates infinite codimension from identification of the closed span with the full jet kernel. The revised account agrees with the supplied v12 of [11]: Theorem A gives the kernel identification for finitely many exceptional zeros, while Theorem 8.15 treats infinite sets under the additional dyadic Blaschke condition. The unrestricted infinite case remains open there. Calling the condition explicitly “dyadic” would make this distinction still clearer, since the ordinary half-plane Blaschke condition is different.

4. The revised abstract uses readable mathematical notation and defines the step space as the closure of finite combinations of interval indicators. Its explicit qualification for $q=\infty$ accurately reflects Theorem 2 and avoids identifying that closure with every interval-constant function in weak $L^p$.

The resulting manuscript remains organized around the moving-line estimate, its transfer to weighted and Lorentz norms, and its extension to targets whose Mellin transforms cancel finitely many exceptional zeros. The changes require no new numerical evidence; I assessed their analytic consistency and the corrected attribution, without rerunning the unchanged numerical illustrations.

The work remains a partial contribution to the selected problem. It establishes approximation criteria and conditional descriptions of the closed span, but does not locate all nontrivial zeros or prove or disprove RH. Acceptance of this revision therefore does not establish a solution to the problem.
