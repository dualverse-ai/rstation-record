# The endpoint case of the discrete Beurling criterion in weighted $L^p$ and Lorentz spaces

## 1. Setting and results

For $k\ge2$ let $\rho_{1/k}(t)=\{1/(kt)\}-k^{-1}\{1/t\}$ on $(0,1)$. With $I_m=(\frac1{m+1},\frac1m]$, $\rho_{1/k}=\{m/k\}$ on $I_m$, so every $\rho_{1/k}$ is a step function constant on each $I_m$. For $1\le p<\infty$ and $\gamma>-1$ let $L^p_\gamma=L^p((0,1),t^\gamma dt)$. Let $S^p_\gamma$ be the closure in $L^p_\gamma$ of $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_{I_m}:m\ge1\}$; it contains $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$ and every bounded function constant on each $I_m$. Let $V^p_\gamma\subset S^p_\gamma$ be the closure of $\operatorname{span}\{\rho_{1/k}:k\ge2\}$. Put
$$\theta=\theta(p,\gamma)=\frac{\gamma+1}p .$$
Báez-Duarte [1] proved that RH holds iff $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V^2_0$. Let $\Theta=\sup\{\operatorname{Re}\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ \operatorname{Im}\rho\neq0\}\in[\frac12,1]$.

**Necessity is known.** If $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$ then $f\mapsto\int_0^1f(t)t^{\rho-1}dt$ is bounded on $L^p_\gamma$, by Hölder, because $\int_0^1t^{(\operatorname{Re}\rho-1-\gamma/p)p'}dt<\infty$ exactly when $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$. For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ the Mellin transform of $\rho_{1/k}$ is $-(k^{-s}-k^{-1})\zeta(s)/s$, which vanishes at every zero, while that of $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$ is $1/s$. So $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V^p_\gamma$ forces $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$. In the coefficient spaces $X^p_\alpha$ of Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Seco this is [6, Theorem 2.1]; their $\ell^p_\alpha$ norm of a sequence $(x_m)$ is equivalent to the $L^p_\gamma$ norm of $\sum_mx_m\mathbf 1_{I_m}$ with $\gamma=-\alpha-2$, because $\int_{I_m}t^\gamma dt\asymp_\gamma m^{-\gamma-2}$, and their range $\alpha\in(-1-p,-1-\frac p2]$ is $\theta\in[\frac12,1)$. For $p=2$, $\gamma=2a-1$, the Mellin transform maps $L^2_\gamma$ isometrically (up to $\sqrt{2\pi}$) onto the Hardy space $H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>a)$; for $a=\frac12$ this is Bagchi's form [15] of [1]. In this form the statement is [9, Proposition 3.1] of Manzur, Noor and Quintero, who write that the converse for $\frac12\le a\le1$ "lies beyond the reach of our analytical methods". Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Seco state an equivalence for $p=2$ [6, Theorem 5.5]. The direction "zero-free $\Rightarrow$ closure" there reduces, through their bijection $T_{a,g}$ [6, Prop. 5.1, Lemma 5.3], to the converse of their Theorem 2.1, which we could not find proved in [6].

**Theorem 1.** Let $1<p\le2$ and $\frac p2-1\le\gamma<p-1$, so that $\frac12\le\theta<1$. The following are equivalent:
(a) $\zeta(s)\ne0$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$;
(b) $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V^p_\gamma$;
(c) $V^p_\gamma=S^p_\gamma$.
Zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=\theta$ are allowed in (a). More precisely, if (a) holds and $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_\theta:=\min(\frac18,\frac{1-\theta}2)$, the anchored Balazard–de Roton approximants [4]
$$e_{N,\varepsilon}=\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}+\sum_{2\le k\le N}\mu(k)k^{-\varepsilon}\rho_{1/k}$$
satisfy $\limsup_{N\to\infty}\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^p_\gamma}\le C_{p,\gamma}\,\varepsilon^{1-1/p}$ if $p<2$, and $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^2_\gamma}\le C_\theta\,\varepsilon^{1/2}$ if $p=2$.

The new content is (a)$\Rightarrow$(b) at the endpoint $\theta=\Theta$ for $p<2$; for $p=2$ it also follows from [5] through Proposition 3 below. For $\theta>\Theta$ it already follows from the power rate [12, Theorem K, (K+)] on a line $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma\in(\Theta,\theta)$ and Hölder's inequality ((2.2) below with $c=\theta-\sigma$ fixed). For $\theta<\Theta$ both sides of the equivalence are false. So Theorem 1 says that the threshold is attained:

**Corollary 1.** For $1<p\le2$ and $\frac p2-1\le\gamma<p-1$: $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V^p_\gamma$ iff $\theta(p,\gamma)\ge\Theta$.

Since $\Theta\ge\frac12$, the case $\theta=\frac12$, that is $\gamma=\frac p2-1$, gives a one-parameter family of Báez-Duarte criteria, of which [1] is $p=2$:

**Corollary 2.** For each $p\in(1,2]$: RH holds iff $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$ lies in the closed span of $\{\rho_{1/k}:k\ge2\}$ in $L^p((0,1),t^{p/2-1}dt)$.

For $p<2$, $L^2(0,1)$ is not contained in $L^p(t^{p/2-1}dt)$: $t^{-1/2}|\log t|^{-\beta}\mathbf 1_{(0,1/2)}$ lies in $L^2$ iff $\beta>\frac12$ and in $L^p(t^{p/2-1}dt)$ iff $\beta>\frac1p$. The same holds for the step functions $\sum_mm^{1/2}(\log(m+1))^{-\beta}\mathbf 1_{I_m}$. So Corollary 2 is not a formal consequence of [1].

For $\gamma=0$, Theorem 1 is the discrete form of Beurling's theorem [2] at the exact exponent: $V^p_0=S^p_0$ iff $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/p$. (Beurling's theorem itself uses all dilations $\{\theta/t\}$, $0<\theta\le1$; the discrete form uses only $\theta=1/k$.) It bears on the question of Balazard and Saias recorded as [3, Question 1.1]: whether, for a specific natural approximation, the weak implication of Lee [8] and Balazard–Saias [14] ($\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/p$ gives approximation in $L^r$ for every $r<p$) can be strengthened to $L^p$. Báez-Duarte [3, §4] showed that the undamped sums $S_n$ and $V_n$ diverge in $L^p$ when a zero has real part $1/p=\Theta$. Theorem 1 gives the strong implication for a sequence $e_{N_j,\varepsilon_j}$. This sequence depends on $p$ and is not explicit (§6), so it does not answer [3, Question 1.1] for any fixed natural approximation. For $\gamma=0$ and $p<2$ the endpoint $1/p=\Theta$ is relevant only if RH fails.

**Theorem 2** (Lorentz scale). Let $1<p\le2$ and $1<q\le\infty$, and let $S_{p,q}$ be the closure in $L^{p,q}(0,1)$ of the finite span of $\{\mathbf 1_{I_m}:m\ge1\}$, as for $S^p_\gamma$, and $V_{p,q}$ the closure of $\operatorname{span}\{\rho_{1/k}\}$. (Every bounded $f$ constant on each $I_m$ lies in $S_{p,q}$, also for $q=\infty$: $f-\sum_{m\le M}f(I_m)\mathbf 1_{I_m}$ is bounded by $\|f\|_\infty$ and supported in $(0,\frac1{M+1}]$, so its $L^{p,q}$ norm is at most $C_{p,q}\|f\|_\infty(M+1)^{-1/p}$. In particular $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$ and every $\rho_{1/k}$ lie in $S_{p,q}$. An unbounded function constant on each $I_m$ need not lie in $S_{p,\infty}$.) Then $V_{p,q}=S_{p,q}$ iff $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/p$. If $\zeta\ne0$ there and $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_{1/p}$, then $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p,1}}\le C_p$, uniformly in $\varepsilon$, and $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_p\le C_p\varepsilon^{1-1/p}$.

At $p=2$, Theorem 2 is [11, Prop. 8.17(iii)–(iv)]: for every $q>1$, RH $\iff\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V_{2,q}$, while $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\notin V_{2,1}$ unconditionally. The present proof is that one, moved to the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac1p-\frac\varepsilon4$. The case $q\ge2$ is contained in [11, Prop. 8.16(iii)], which reduces it to the $L^2$ case through the embedding $\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}\le C\|f\|_{L^2(t^{2/p-1}dt)}$. The new range is $1<q<2$, which contains $L^p=L^{p,p}$ for $p<2$.

**A known zero-free half-plane.** If it is known that $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>c$ for some $c<1$, Theorems 1 and 2 give, with no further hypothesis, $V^p_\gamma=S^p_\gamma$ whenever $\theta(p,\gamma)\ge c$ in the range of Theorem 1, and $V_{p,q}=S_{p,q}$ for $1<p\le\min(2,1/c)$, $q>1$. For $\theta>c$ the density in $L^p_\gamma$ already follows from [12, Theorem K] as above. At $\theta=c$ the endpoint case is needed, because $\Theta=c$ is not excluded; for $p=2$ it is also available from [5] through Proposition 3. OpenAI [19, Theorem 1.1] states $c=7/8$, with a Lean formalization; we have checked only the statement, not the proof. Granting it, $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$ lies in the closed span of the $\rho_{1/k}$ in $L^p(t^{7p/8-1}dt)$ for every $p\in(1,2]$, in particular in $L^{8/7}(0,1)$.

**Finitely many zeros beyond $\theta$.** If only finitely many zeros have real part $>\theta$, then $V^p_\gamma$ and $V_{p,q}$ are the jet kernels at those zeros, and their codimension is the number of those zeros counted with multiplicity (Theorem 5, §5). Zeros with real part $\le\theta$ are unrestricted. It was left open in [11, §8.18] for $L^p$ with $p<2$ and for $L^{p,q}$ with $1<q<2$. We found no earlier proof (see the literature paragraph in §5).

**The Hilbert case.** For $p=2$, (a)$\Rightarrow$(b) of Theorem 1 also follows from a theorem of Dimitrov and Oliveira [5, Theorem 2] about the *unconstrained* boundary distance, which they prove for Dirichlet $L$-functions. We record the transfer, since neither [9] nor [6] nor our [12] uses it.

**Proposition 3.** Let $\frac12\le a<1$ and $G_k(s)=(k^{-s}-k^{-1})\zeta(s)/s$. Let $d^{\rm DO}_N(a)=\inf\|(1-A\zeta)/s\|_{L^2(\operatorname{Re}s=a)}$ over all $A=\sum_{n\le N}b_nn^{-s}$, and let $d_N(a)$ be the same infimum over $A$ with $A(1)=0$; equivalently [12, §1], $d_N(a)$ is the distance in $H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>a)$ from $1/s$ to $\operatorname{span}\{G_k:2\le k\le N\}$. Then
$$d^{\rm DO}_N(a)\le d_N(a)\le a^{-1/2}\,d^{\rm DO}_N(a)\qquad(N\ge1).$$
Hence, by [5, Theorem 2] with $\chi$ principal mod $1$ and $p=1/a$: if $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>a$, then $d_N(a)\to0$ and $1/s\in\overline{\operatorname{span}}_{H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>a)}\{G_k\}$. In particular, if $\frac12<\Theta<1$, then $d_N(\Theta)\to0$, the case that [12, Corollary K] left undecided, and the exponent $\kappa(a)=(a-\Theta)^+$ of [12, Corollary K] holds for $d^{\rm DO}_N(a)$ at every $a\in(\frac12,1)$, including $a=\Theta$.

*Proof.* Norms on $\operatorname{Re}s=a$ carry the factor $\frac1{2\pi}$, so by Plancherel the Mellin transform is an isometry from $L^2((0,\infty),t^{2a-1}dt)$ onto $L^2(\operatorname{Re}s=a)$, and functions supported in $(0,1)$ go onto $H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>a)$. For $n\ge1$ and $\operatorname{Re}s>0$,
$$\frac{n^{-s}\zeta(s)}s=\frac{n^{-1}}{s-1}+\mathcal M\big[-n^{-1}t^{-1}\mathbf 1_{(1/n,1)}-\{1/(nt)\}\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}\big](s),$$
since $\lfloor1/(nt)\rfloor=\frac1{nt}-\{\frac1{nt}\}$ on $(0,\frac1n]$ and $\int_0^{1/n}\frac{t^{s-2}}n\,dt=\frac{n^{-s}}{s-1}$. So for $A(s)=\sum_{n\le N}b_nn^{-s}$ we get $(1-A\zeta)/s=F_A-A(1)/(s-1)$, where $F_A\in H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>a)$ is the transform of a bounded function on $(0,1)$. On $\operatorname{Re}s=a<1$, $\frac1{s-1}$ is the transform of $-t^{-1}\mathbf 1_{(1,\infty)}$, which is orthogonal to everything supported in $(0,1)$ and has norm $(2-2a)^{-1/2}$. Hence
$$\Big\|\frac{1-A\zeta}s\Big\|^2_{L^2(\operatorname{Re}s=a)}=\|F_A\|^2+\frac{|A(1)|^2}{2-2a}.$$
Put $A'=A-A(1)=\sum_{2\le n\le N}b_n(n^{-s}-n^{-1})$, which has $A'(1)=0$ and the same length. Then $(1-A'\zeta)/s=E-\sum_{n\ge2}b_nG_n$ with $E=1/s$, and also $(1-A'\zeta)/s=F_A+A(1)\big(\frac{\zeta(s)}s-\frac1{s-1}\big)$. The bracket is $-\mathcal M[\{1/t\}\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}]$, of norm less than $(\int_0^1t^{2a-1}dt)^{1/2}=(2a)^{-1/2}$. By Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\Big\|E-\sum b_nG_n\Big\|\le\|F_A\|+\frac{|A(1)|}{\sqrt{2a}}\le\Big(1+\frac{1-a}a\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\|F_A\|^2+\frac{|A(1)|^2}{2-2a}\Big)^{1/2}=a^{-1/2}\Big\|\frac{1-A\zeta}s\Big\|.$$
Taking the infimum over $A$ gives the upper bound; the lower bound is trivial. The consequences follow from [5, Theorem 2], which says $d^{\rm DO}_N(a)\to0$ iff $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>a$, and from [12, Corollary K] for $d_N$. $\square$

[5] proves its Theorem 2 by dominated convergence on the line itself, with damped Möbius approximants of the same form as here. That argument has no analogue for $p<2$: the Hölder step below costs $c^{-(1/p-1/2)}$ with $c=\theta-\operatorname{Re}s$, which is infinite on the line. The point of §3 is a bound on a line at distance $c=\varepsilon/4$ that beats this cost.

## 2. Inputs

Throughout, $\frac12\le\theta<1$, $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$, $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_\theta$, $c=\varepsilon/4$ and $\sigma_0=\theta-c$. Then $\operatorname{Re}(s+\varepsilon)=\theta+3c$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0$, and $\theta+3c<1$. Write $s=\sigma_0+i\tau$. Constants $C_\theta$ and implied constants depend only on $\theta$ unless indicated.

**(D1)** [5, Theorem 4 and Lemmas 1–2], for $\chi$ the principal character mod $1$. For $\delta,\eta>0$: $|\zeta(w)|^{\pm1}\ll_{\theta,\delta,\eta}(2+|\operatorname{Im}w|)^\eta$ on $\theta+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}w\le1$, $|\operatorname{Im}w|\ge1$; $\sum_k\mu(k)k^{-w}$ converges to $1/\zeta(w)$ on $\operatorname{Re}w>\theta$; and $M_N(w)=\sum_{k\le N}\mu(k)k^{-w}\ll_{\theta,\delta,\eta}(2+|\operatorname{Im}w|)^\eta$ uniformly in $N$ on $\theta+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}w\le1$. This is Littlewood's argument [13, §14.2] with $\theta$ in place of $\frac12$; it uses only the zero-free half-plane. [5] states the bound uniform in $N$ (its Lemma 2) without proof; the case $Q=1$ of (D1$^u$) in §5 proves it.

**(D2)** Convexity: $\zeta(\sigma+i\tau)\ll(2+|\tau|)^{(1-\sigma)/2+\delta}$ for $0\le\sigma\le1$, $|\tau|\ge1$, uniformly in $\sigma$.

**(D3)** [13, Theorem 9.6(A)]: for $|\tau|\ge2$ and $-1\le\operatorname{Re}w\le2$, $\operatorname{Im}w=\tau$, $(\zeta'/\zeta)(w)=\sum_{|\gamma-\tau|\le1}(w-\rho)^{-1}+O(\log|\tau|)$, and $[\tau-1,\tau+1]$ contains $\ll\log(2+|\tau|)$ ordinates.

**Mellin formula.** $e_{N,\varepsilon}$ is a bounded step function, and $\hat f(s)=\int_0^1f(t)t^{s-1}dt$. With $\hat\rho_{1/k}(s)=-(k^{-s}-k^{-1})\zeta(s)/s$,
$$s\,\hat e_{N,\varepsilon}(s)=1-\zeta(s)\big(M_N(s+\varepsilon)-M_N(1+\varepsilon)\big)\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>0).\tag{2.1}$$
(Numerically checked to relative error $1.5\cdot10^{-11}$ at five points, `lp_note_check.py mellin`.)

**Two norms.** For $f$ bounded on $(0,1)$ put $\phi(y)=f(e^{-y})e^{-\sigma_0y}$, so $\hat\phi(\tau)=\hat f(\sigma_0+i\tau)$.
- Plancherel: $\int_0^1|f|^2t^{2\sigma_0-1}dt=\frac1{2\pi}\int|\hat f(\sigma_0+i\tau)|^2d\tau=:\|\hat f\|^2_{2}$.
- Weighted Hölder. Let $1<p<2$ and $\theta=(\gamma+1)/p$. With exponents $\frac2p$ and $\frac2{2-p}$,
$$\int_0^1|f|^pt^\gamma dt\le\Big(\int_0^1|f|^2t^{2\sigma_0-1}dt\Big)^{p/2}\Big(\int_0^1t^{(\gamma-p\sigma_0+p/2)\frac2{2-p}}dt\Big)^{\frac{2-p}2}.$$
Since $\gamma=p\theta-1$, the last exponent is $\frac{2(\gamma-p\sigma_0)+p}{2-p}=-1+\frac{2pc}{2-p}$, so the integral is $\frac{2-p}{2pc}$, and
$$\|f\|_{L^p_\gamma}\le\Big(\frac{2-p}{2pc}\Big)^{\frac1p-\frac12}\|\hat f\|_{2}.\tag{2.2}$$
For $p=2$ and $\gamma=2\theta-1$, $\|f\|_{L^2_\gamma}\le\|\hat f\|_2$, since $t^{2\theta-1}\le t^{2\sigma_0-1}$ on $(0,1)$.
- Lorentz. Let $\gamma=0$, $\theta=1/p$, and $\|f\|_{L^{p,1}}=\int_0^1s^{1/p}f^*(s)\frac{ds}s$. Hausdorff–Young for $\phi$ with exponents $3,\frac32$ gives $\int_0^1|f|^3t^{3\sigma_0-1}dt=\|\phi\|_3^3\le\|\hat f(\sigma_0+i\cdot)\|_{L^{3/2}(d\tau)}^3$. Write $f=gw$ with $w=t^{-a}$, $a=\sigma_0-\frac13>0$, so that $\|g\|_3^3$ is the last integral. Since $(gw)^*(s)\le g^*(s/2)w(s/2)$ and $\big(\frac1p-1-a\big)\frac32=-1+\frac32c$, Hölder with exponents $3,\frac32$ gives
$$\|f\|_{L^{p,1}}\le2^{1/p}\int_0^{1/2}u^{\frac1p-1-a}g^*(u)du\le2^{1/p}\|g\|_3\Big(\int_0^{1/2}u^{-1+\frac32c}du\Big)^{2/3}\le2c^{-2/3}\|\hat f(\sigma_0+i\cdot)\|_{L^{3/2}}.\tag{2.3}$$

## 3. The moving-line estimate

**Proposition 4.** Under the standing assumptions, on $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0$ we have $\hat e_{N,\varepsilon}=H_\varepsilon+R_N$ with
$$sH_\varepsilon(s)=1-\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}+\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)},\qquad R_N\to0\ \text{in }L^{3/2}\cap L^2(\sigma_0+i\mathbb R)\ (N\to\infty),$$
and $\|H_\varepsilon\|_{L^{r}(\sigma_0+i\mathbb R)}\le C_\theta\,\varepsilon^{1/r}$ for $r\in\{\frac32,2\}$.

*Proof.* **Remainder.** By (D1), $M_N(w)\to1/\zeta(w)$ on $\operatorname{Re}w=\theta+3c$ and at $w=1+\varepsilon$, so (2.1) gives $\hat e_{N,\varepsilon}\to H_\varepsilon$ pointwise on the line, with $sR_N=-\zeta(s)\big(M_N(s+\varepsilon)-\frac1{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}-M_N(1+\varepsilon)+\frac1{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)}\big)$. By (D1) with $\delta=3c$, $\eta=c$, and (D2) with $\delta=c$, $|R_N(s)|\ll_{\theta,\varepsilon}(2+|\tau|)^{-1+(1-\sigma_0)/2+2c}\le(2+|\tau|)^{-3/4+5c/2}$ uniformly in $N$, and $\frac32(\frac34-\frac52c)>1$ because $c\le\frac1{32}$. Dominated convergence gives $R_N\to0$ in $L^{3/2}$ and $L^2$.

**Hadamard quotient.** For $|\tau|\ge2$,
$$\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}=\pi^{-\varepsilon/2}\,\frac{(s+\varepsilon)(s+\varepsilon-1)}{s(s-1)}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s+\varepsilon}2)}{\Gamma(\frac s2)}\,\lim_{T\to\infty}\prod_{|\gamma|\le T}\frac{s-\rho}{s+\varepsilon-\rho}.$$
For a zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, $|s-\rho|\le|s+\varepsilon-\rho|$ iff $\beta\le\sigma_0+\frac\varepsilon2=\theta+c$, which holds for every zero. So the product has modulus $\le1$. The rational factor is $\le4$ for $|\tau|\ge2$, and Stirling's formula gives $|\Gamma(\frac{s+\varepsilon}2)/\Gamma(\frac s2)|\le C(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2}$ for $0\le\varepsilon\le1$. Hence
$$\Big|\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}\Big|\le C(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2}\qquad(|\tau|\ge2),\tag{3.1}$$
with $C$ absolute. Zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=\theta$ are allowed here. (Numerically, the left side divided by $(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2}$ is at most $0.955$ on $2\le\tau\le200$ for $\theta\in\{0.5,0.6,0.75\}$, $\varepsilon\in\{0.2,0.1,0.05\}$.)

**The term $H^2$.** Write $H_\varepsilon=H^1+H^2$ with $sH^2=\zeta(s)/\zeta(1+\varepsilon)$. Here we need a majorant independent of $\varepsilon$, so we use (D2) with the fixed exponent $\delta=\frac1{64}$, not $\delta=c$. Since $c\le\frac1{32}$, every admissible line has $\sigma_0\ge\frac{15}{32}$, so $(1-\sigma_0)/2\le\frac{17}{64}$ and
$$\Big|\frac{\zeta(s)}s\Big|\le C_\theta\,(2+|\tau|)^{-23/32}\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0),$$
with $C_\theta$ independent of $\varepsilon$ (for $|\tau|\le1$ use $|s-1|\ge1-\sigma_0\ge1-\theta$ and $|s|\ge\frac{15}{32}$). The right side lies in $L^{3/2}\cap L^2(d\tau)$ because $\frac32\cdot\frac{23}{32}>1$. Since $\zeta(1+\varepsilon)>1/\varepsilon$, $\|H^2\|_r\le\varepsilon\|\zeta(s)/s\|_r\le C'_\theta\,\varepsilon$ for $r\in\{\frac32,2\}$.

**The term $H^1$, large $|\tau|$.** Let $T_1=\varepsilon^{-4}$. By (3.1), $|H^1|\ll(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2-1}$ for $|\tau|\ge2$, so $\int_{|\tau|>T_1}|H^1|^r\ll T_1^{1-r(1-\varepsilon/2)}\le T_1^{-5/16}=\varepsilon^{5/4}$.

**The term $H^1$, $2\le|\tau|\le T_1$.** Here (3.1) gives $|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)|\le C'$ with $C'$ absolute, since $(2+T_1)^{\varepsilon/2}\le(3/\varepsilon)^{2\varepsilon}\le3$. If $\tau$ is not an ordinate, the segment $[s,s+\varepsilon]$ meets no zero, and $\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)=e^z$ with $z=-\int_0^\varepsilon(\zeta'/\zeta)(s+u)du$. Since $|e^z|\le C'$, $|1-e^z|\ll\min(1,|z|)$. By (D3), $|z|\le\sum_{|\gamma-\tau|\le1}\varepsilon/|\tau-\gamma|+O(\varepsilon\log|\tau|)$, because $|s+u-\rho|\ge|\tau-\gamma|$. Hence
$$|1-e^z|\ll\sum_{|\gamma-\tau|\le1}b_\gamma(\tau)+\varepsilon\log(2+|\tau|),\qquad b_\gamma(\tau)=\min\Big(1,\frac\varepsilon{|\tau-\gamma|}\Big),\qquad\|b_\gamma\|_r^r=\frac{2r\varepsilon}{r-1}.$$
For $j\in\mathbb Z$ the interval $[j-1,j+2]$ contains $\ll\log(2+|j|)$ ordinates, so Minkowski's inequality gives $\int_j^{j+1}|1-e^z|^rd\tau\ll\varepsilon\log^r(2+|j|)$. Dividing by $|s|^r$ and summing over $2\le|j|\le T_1$ gives $\int_{2\le|\tau|\le T_1}|H^1|^r\ll\varepsilon$.

**The term $H^1$, $|\tau|<2$.** No zero has $|\gamma|<14$, and $\theta+3c<1$. So $\zeta'/\zeta$ is bounded by $C_\theta$ on $\{\sigma_0\le\operatorname{Re}w\le\theta+3c,\ |\operatorname{Im}w|\le2\}$, which avoids the pole because $1-\theta-3c\ge(1-\theta)/4$. Thus $|z|\le C_\theta\varepsilon$, $|1-e^z|\ll_\theta\varepsilon$, and $\int_{|\tau|<2}|H^1|^r\ll_\theta\varepsilon^r$.

Together, $\|H_\varepsilon\|_r^r\ll_\theta\varepsilon$. $\square$

The bound is sharp in $\varepsilon$ for $r=2$ when a zero $\rho_0$ lies on $\operatorname{Re}s=\theta$: every $F\in E+\operatorname{span}\{G_k\}$ in $H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>\sigma_0)$ has $F(\rho_0)=1/\rho_0$, and the reproducing kernel at $\rho_0$ has norm $(2(\theta-\sigma_0))^{-1/2}=(2c)^{-1/2}$, so $\|F\|_2\ge|\rho_0|^{-1}(2c)^{1/2}$. Numerically, with $\theta=0.75$ (no zero near the line), $\|H_\varepsilon\|_2/\varepsilon=0.85,\,0.88,\,0.91$ for $\varepsilon=0.2,0.1,0.05$ (truncated at $|\tau|\le200$, so numerical estimates; $\varepsilon=0.2$ is outside $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_\theta$).

## 4. Proofs of Theorems 1 and 2

**Theorem 1, (a)$\Rightarrow$(b).** By Proposition 4 and Plancherel, $\limsup_N\|\hat e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_2=\|H_\varepsilon\|_2\le C_\theta\varepsilon^{1/2}$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0$. For $p=2$, $\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^2_{2\theta-1}}\le\|\hat e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_2$. For $p<2$, (2.2) with $c=\varepsilon/4$ gives
$$\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^p_\gamma}\le\Big(\frac{2(2-p)}{p\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac1p-\frac12}C_\theta\varepsilon^{1/2}=C_{p,\gamma}\,\varepsilon^{1-\frac1p}.$$
Since $e_{N,\varepsilon}\in\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}+\operatorname{span}\{\rho_{1/k}\}$ and $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V^p_\gamma$.

**(b)$\Rightarrow$(c).** Let $D_n f(t)=f(nt)\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}(t)$. Then $\|D_nf\|_{L^p_\gamma}=n^{-\theta}\|f\|_{L^p_\gamma}$, $D_n\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}=\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}$, and $D_n\rho_{1/l}=\rho_{1/(ln)}-l^{-1}\rho_{1/n}$ (on $(0,1/n]$ both sides equal $\{1/(lnt)\}-l^{-1}\{1/(nt)\}$, the $\{1/t\}$ terms cancelling; on $(1/n,1)$ the right side is $\frac1{lnt}-\frac1{lnt}=0$). So $D_n$ maps $V^p_\gamma$ into itself, and (b) gives $\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}\in V^p_\gamma$ for all $n$. Since $\mathbf 1_{I_m}=\mathbf 1_{(0,1/m]}-\mathbf 1_{(0,1/(m+1)]}$, $S^p_\gamma\subset V^p_\gamma$.

**(c)$\Rightarrow$(b)** is trivial, and **(b)$\Rightarrow$(a)** is the necessity argument of §1.

**Theorem 2.** Necessity: for $\operatorname{Re}\rho>1/p$, $|t^{\rho-1}|$ lies in $L^r(0,1)$ for some $r>p'$, hence in $L^{p',1}$, which pairs boundedly with $L^{p,\infty}\supset L^{p,q}$. So $f\mapsto\hat f(\rho)$ is bounded on $L^{p,q}$ for every $q$, and the argument of §1 applies. Sufficiency: by Proposition 4 and (2.3), $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p,1}}\le2c^{-2/3}C_{1/p}\varepsilon^{2/3}=C_p$, and by Theorem 1 with $\gamma=0$, $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_p\le C_p\varepsilon^{1-1/p}$. For $q\ge p$, $\|g\|_{L^{p,q}}\le\|g\|_p$ because $s^{1/p}g^*(s)\le\|g\|_p$. For $1<q<p$, Hölder for $s^{1/p}g^*(s)$ on $ds/s$ gives $\|g\|_{L^{p,q}}\le\|g\|_{L^{p,1}}^\lambda\|g\|_p^{1-\lambda}$ with $\frac1q=\lambda+\frac{1-\lambda}p$, $\lambda<1$. So $d(\mathbf 1_{(0,1]},V_{p,q})\le C_p\varepsilon^{(1-\lambda)(1-1/p)}$ for every $\varepsilon$. $D_n$ has norm $n^{-1/p}$ on $L^{p,q}$, and the dilation step is as before. $\square$

## 5. Finitely many zeros beyond $\theta$

For $\rho$ with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>0$ and $r\ge0$ write $\hat f^{(r)}(\rho)=\int_0^1f(t)t^{\rho-1}(\log t)^rdt$. Let $Z_\theta$ be the multiset of zeros of $\zeta$ with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$, each counted $m(\rho)$ times, and put ($K_{p,q}\subset S_{p,q}$ is defined in the same way)
$$K^p_\gamma=\{f\in S^p_\gamma:\ \hat f^{(r)}(\rho)=0\ \text{for}\ \rho\in Z_\theta,\ 0\le r<m(\rho)\}.$$

**Theorem 5** (finitely many zeros beyond $\theta$). Let $1<p\le2$ and $\frac p2-1\le\gamma<p-1$. If $Z_\theta$ is finite, then $V^p_\gamma=K^p_\gamma$ and $\operatorname{codim}_{S^p_\gamma}V^p_\gamma=|Z_\theta|$. If $Z_\theta$ is infinite, the codimension is infinite. The same holds for $V_{p,q}\subset S_{p,q}$, $1<q\le\infty$, with $\theta=1/p$.

No hypothesis is made on zeros with $\operatorname{Re}\rho\le\theta$; they may lie on $\operatorname{Re}s=\theta$ or accumulate there. The implication (a)$\Rightarrow$(c) of Theorem 1 is the case $Z_\theta=\emptyset$. For $p=2$, $\gamma=0$ the statement is the finite case of [11, Theorem A]. In $L^p=L^{p,p}$ and in $L^{p,q}$, the count was known when only finitely many zeros have real part $>\frac1p-\delta$ for some $\delta>0$ [11, Prop. 8.16(ii)], and for $q\ge2$ without that hypothesis [11, Prop. 8.16(iii)]. The case of finite nonempty $Z_{1/p}$ with $1<q<2$ was left open in [11, §8.18] and in v1 of this note. We have not found the continuous-dilation analogue in $L^p$, $p\ne2$, in [3], [5], [15], [17] or in Báez-Duarte's general criterion (arXiv:math/0505453), which leaves the $L^p$ case aside. We did not obtain Beurling's note [2], Bercovici–Foias [16] or Balazard–Saias [18], so we rely on secondary accounts for them. By [7, §1], [2] proves the density statement in $L^p(0,1)$, and [16] proves that the $L^2(0,1)$ closure of the continuous family is the set of $f$ whose Mellin transform divided by $\zeta$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac12$; [7, §1] refers to [18] for possible extensions of this formula to $L^p(0,1)$. The citing sentences for [2] and [16] that Semantic Scholar lists (121 and 41 citing papers) give the same picture. None of them attributes an $L^p$ closure or codimension statement to [2] or [16]. In $L^2$ the jet-kernel description of the continuous-dilation closure is thus [16]; Burnol [17, §2] derives it from the Beurling–Lax theorem.

The proof changes one thing in the proof of Theorem 1. The target $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$, whose Mellin transform is $1/s$, is replaced by a step function whose transform is $Q(s)/s$, with $Q$ a Dirichlet polynomial vanishing on $Z_\theta$. The Möbius coefficients are replaced by those of $Q/\zeta$, which is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$. The arithmetic input is the classical fact that a Dirichlet series with bounded coefficients converges wherever its sum continues holomorphically with polynomial growth; for $Q=1$ this is Littlewood's theorem [13, §14.25].

*Proof of Theorem 5.* Let $F=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}:n\ge1\}$. It contains every $\mathbf 1_{I_m}=\mathbf 1_{(0,1/m]}-\mathbf 1_{(0,1/(m+1)]}$ and lies in $S^p_\gamma$ and in $S_{p,q}$ (for $S_{p,q}$ by the parenthetical remark in Theorem 2, also when $q=\infty$), so it is dense in each.

**Step 1: jets.** Let $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$ and $r\ge0$. The Hölder computation of §1 gives boundedness of $f\mapsto\hat f^{(r)}(\rho)$ on $L^p_\gamma$: the factor $|\log t|^r$ does not affect convergence, because the inequality $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$ is strict. On $L^{p,q}$, $|t^{\rho-1}(\log t)^r|\le C t^{-a}$ on $(0,1)$ for some $a<1-\frac1p$, and $t^{-a}\in L^{p',1}(0,1)$, which pairs boundedly with $L^{p,q}$. Each $\hat\rho_{1/k}=-(k^{-s}-k^{-1})\zeta(s)/s$ vanishes to order $m(\rho)$ at $\rho$. So $V\subset K$, and $K$ is closed. (Here and below, $V,S,K$ stand for $V^p_\gamma,S^p_\gamma,K^p_\gamma$ or their Lorentz versions.)

**Lemma 6.** Let $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_J$ be distinct with $\operatorname{Re}\rho_j>0$, and let $P_1,\dots,P_J$ be polynomials such that $\sum_jP_j(\log n)n^{-\rho_j}=0$ for every $n\ge1$. Then every $P_j$ is $0$.

*Proof.* Multiply by $n^{-s}$ and sum over $n$. Since $(-\frac d{ds})n^{-s}=(\log n)n^{-s}$, this gives $\sum_jP_j(-\frac d{ds})\zeta(s+\rho_j)=0$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, hence for all $s$ by continuation. If $P_j(x)=cx^d+\dots$ with $c\ne0$, then $P_j(-\frac d{ds})\zeta(s+\rho_j)$ has a pole of exact order $d+1$ at $s=1-\rho_j$, with leading coefficient $c\,d!$. The other terms are holomorphic there. This is a contradiction. $\square$

Let $\Lambda=\sum_{\rho\in Z'}\sum_{r<m(\rho)}c_{\rho,r}\,\delta^{(r)}_\rho$ be a nonzero combination of jets, $\delta^{(r)}_\rho(f)=\hat f^{(r)}(\rho)$, over a finite set $Z'$ of distinct zeros with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$. Since $\widehat{\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}}(s)=n^{-s}/s$, Leibniz's rule gives $\Lambda(\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]})=\sum_\rho P_\rho(\log n)n^{-\rho}$ with $P_\rho(x)=\sum_rc_{\rho,r}\sum_{i\le r}\binom ri(-x)^i(1/s)^{(r-i)}|_{s=\rho}$. If $r_*$ is the largest $r$ with $c_{\rho,r}\ne0$, the coefficient of $x^{r_*}$ in $P_\rho$ is $(-1)^{r_*}c_{\rho,r_*}/\rho\ne0$. By Lemma 6, $\Lambda$ does not vanish on $F$. So the jets at any finite set of zeros with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\theta$ are linearly independent on $F$, and they vanish on $V$. If $Z_\theta$ is infinite, this gives $\operatorname{codim}_SV=\infty$; if $Z_\theta$ is finite, $\operatorname{codim}_SK=|Z_\theta|$.

**Step 2: the approximants.** In Steps 2 and 3 the standing assumption $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$ of §2 is replaced by finiteness of $Z_\theta$; (D2), (D3) and (2.2)–(2.3) do not use it. Let $Z_\theta$ be finite, let $u=\sum_{n\le M}a_n\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}\in F\cap K$, and put $Q(s)=\sum_{n\le M}a_nn^{-s}$ and $\|a\|_1=\sum|a_n|$. Then $\hat u=Q/s$, so $Q$ vanishes to order $m(\rho)$ at each $\rho\in Z_\theta$, and $|Q(w)|\le\|a\|_1$ for $\operatorname{Re}w\ge0$. Let $b_k=\sum_{d\mid k,\,d\le M}a_d\,\mu(k/d)$, so $|b_k|\le\|a\|_1$, and $\sum_kb_kk^{-w}=A(w):=Q(w)/\zeta(w)$ for $\operatorname{Re}w>1$. $A$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}w>\theta$: there, the zeros of $\zeta$ are those in $Z_\theta$, cancelled by $Q$, and $1/\zeta$ vanishes at $w=1$. Put $B_N(w)=\sum_{k\le N}b_kk^{-w}$ and
$$e^u_{N,\varepsilon}=u+\sum_{2\le k\le N}b_kk^{-\varepsilon}\rho_{1/k}\in u+\operatorname{span}\{\rho_{1/k}\}.$$
As for (2.1), for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$,
$$s\,\hat e^u_{N,\varepsilon}(s)=Q(s)-\zeta(s)\big(B_N(s+\varepsilon)-B_N(1+\varepsilon)\big).\tag{5.1}$$
(The term $k=1$ would contribute $b_1(1-1)=0$.) Let $\beta_{\min}=\min_{\rho\in Z_\theta}\operatorname{Re}\rho$ and
$$\varepsilon_u=\min\Big(\varepsilon_\theta,\ \frac{\beta_{\min}-\theta}2,\ \frac{5\log2}{8\sum_{\rho\in Z_\theta}(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\theta)^{-1}}\Big),$$
and keep $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_u$, $c=\varepsilon/4$, $\sigma_0=\theta-c$.

**(D1$^u$).** For $\delta,\eta>0$: $B_N(w)\to A(w)$ on $\operatorname{Re}w>\theta$, and $B_N(w)\ll_{u,\theta,\delta,\eta}(2+|\operatorname{Im}w|)^\eta$ uniformly in $N$ on $\theta+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}w\le1$.

*Proof.* The proof of [5, Theorem 4] bounds $|\zeta(w)|^{-1}$ at height $t$ using $\log\zeta$ only on discs centred at $2+it$ of radius $<2-\theta$ (Borel–Carathéodory) and on circles centred at $\log\log t+it$ of radius $<\log\log t-\theta$ (Hadamard's three circles). All of these lie in $\{\operatorname{Re}w>\theta,\ |\operatorname{Im}w-t|\le\log\log t+2\}$, which misses the finite set $Z_\theta$ once $|t|\ge t_0(Z_\theta,\theta)$. So $|\zeta(w)|^{-1}\ll(2+|\operatorname{Im}w|)^\eta$ on $\operatorname{Re}w\ge\theta+\delta$, $|\operatorname{Im}w|\ge t_0$ ([5, Theorem 4] is stated for all $\sigma>\frac1p+\delta$, with $\frac1p$ in the role of $\theta$; for $\operatorname{Re}w\ge1+1/\log\log t$ its proof bounds $\log\zeta$ by the Dirichlet series), and $|A|\le\|a\|_1|\zeta|^{-1}$ there. On the compact set $\{\theta+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}w\le2,\ |\operatorname{Im}w|\le t_0\}$, $A$ is holomorphic, hence bounded; for $\operatorname{Re}w\ge2$, $|A|\le2\|a\|_1$. Hence $|A(w)|\ll_{u,\theta,\delta,\eta}(2+|\operatorname{Im}w|)^\eta$ on $\operatorname{Re}w\ge\theta+\delta$.

[5, Lemma 2] is stated there without proof, so we give the Perron argument of [5, Lemma 1] with the dependence on the height kept; it uses only $|b_k|\le\|a\|_1$ and the bound on $A$, with no Euler product. Let $w=\sigma+it$ with $\theta+\delta\le\sigma\le1$ (for $\sigma>1$ the series converges absolutely), $x=N+\frac12$, $T=x^3$, $\kappa=\sigma-\theta-\frac\delta2\ge\frac\delta2$, and $0<\eta\le\delta/12$ (larger $\eta$ follow). [13, Lemma 3.12] with $c=2$ and $\sum_k|b_k|k^{-\sigma'}\le\|a\|_1\zeta(\sigma')$ gives $B_N(w)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{2-iT}^{2+iT}A(w+z)x^z\frac{dz}z+O(x^2/T)$. Moving the segment to $\operatorname{Re}z=-\kappa$ passes the pole at $z=0$, with residue $A(w)$. On the new sides $\operatorname{Re}(w+z)\ge\theta+\frac\delta2$ and $|A(w+z)|\ll(2+|t|+T)^\eta\le2(2+|t|)^\eta x^{3\eta}$. So the horizontal sides contribute $\ll(2+|t|)^\eta x^{2+3\eta}/T=(2+|t|)^\eta x^{3\eta-1}$ and the vertical side $\ll(2+|t|)^\eta x^{3\eta-\kappa}\log(3T/\kappa)$. Since $3\eta\le\kappa/2$,
$$|B_N(w)-A(w)|\ll_{u,\theta,\delta,\eta}(2+|t|)^\eta\,x^{-\delta/4}\log(3x),$$
which tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$ and is $\ll(2+|t|)^\eta$ uniformly in $N$. With the bound on $A$ this gives both claims. $\square$

**(3.1$^u$).** On $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0$, $|\tau|\ge2$: $|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)|\le2C(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2}$, with $C$ from (3.1).

*Proof.* In the Hadamard quotient of §3 every zero with $\operatorname{Re}\rho\le\theta+c$ contributes a factor of modulus $\le1$. For $\rho=\beta+i\gamma\in Z_\theta$, $\beta\ge\beta_{\min}\ge\theta+2\varepsilon>\sigma_0+\varepsilon$, so
$$\Big|\frac{s-\rho}{s+\varepsilon-\rho}\Big|\le\frac{\beta-\sigma_0}{\beta-\sigma_0-\varepsilon}=1+\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta-\theta-3c}\le1+\frac85\,\frac\varepsilon{\beta-\theta},$$
because $3c=\frac34\varepsilon\le\frac38(\beta-\theta)$. The product of these finitely many factors is at most $\exp\big(\frac85\varepsilon\sum_{Z_\theta}(\beta-\theta)^{-1}\big)\le2$. $\square$

**Proposition 7.** On $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0$, $\hat e^u_{N,\varepsilon}=H^u_\varepsilon+R^u_N$ with
$$sH^u_\varepsilon(s)=Q(s)\Big[1-\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}\Big]+\big[Q(s)-Q(s+\varepsilon)\big]\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}+Q(1+\varepsilon)\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)},$$
$R^u_N\to0$ in $L^{3/2}\cap L^2(\sigma_0+i\mathbb R)$, and $\|H^u_\varepsilon\|_r\le C_{u,\theta}\,\varepsilon^{1/r}$ for $r\in\{\frac32,2\}$.

*Proof.* The three terms sum to $Q(s)-\zeta(s)A(s+\varepsilon)+\zeta(s)A(1+\varepsilon)$, the limit of (5.1). The remainder is handled as in Proposition 4, with (D1$^u$) for (D1). First term: $|Q(s)|\le\|a\|_1$, and $[1-\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)]/s$ is the $H^1$ of Proposition 4. Its proof uses (3.1) only through the bounds $|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)|\ll(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2}$ and $\le C'$, which hold with $2C$, $2C'$ by (3.1$^u$). It also uses $|s+x-\rho|\ge|\tau-\gamma|$ for $0\le x\le\varepsilon$, (D3) and the absence of zeros with $|\gamma|<14$, which hold for every zero. Second term: $|Q(s)-Q(s+\varepsilon)|\le\sum|a_n|n^{-\sigma_0}(1-n^{-\varepsilon})\le\varepsilon\sum|a_n|\log n$. By (3.1$^u$) for $|\tau|\ge2$, and $|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)|=|e^z|\le e^{C_\theta\varepsilon}$ for $|\tau|<2$ (proof of Proposition 4), the factor $\zeta(s)/(s\,\zeta(s+\varepsilon))$ is $\ll(2+|\tau|)^{\varepsilon/2-1}$, whose $L^r$ norm is bounded for $r\ge\frac32$, $\varepsilon\le\frac18$. So this term has $L^r$ norm $\ll_u\varepsilon$. Third term: $|Q(1+\varepsilon)|\le\|a\|_1$, so it is at most $\|a\|_1$ times $H^2$, of norm $\ll\varepsilon$. $\square$

**Step 3: conclusion.** Proposition 7 with (2.2), as in §4, gives $\limsup_N\|e^u_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^p_\gamma}\le C_{u,p,\gamma}\varepsilon^{1-1/p}$ for $p<2$ and $\le C_{u,\theta}\varepsilon^{1/2}$ for $p=2$. With (2.3) and the interpolation in the proof of Theorem 2, it gives $d(u,V_{p,q})\le C_{u,p}\varepsilon^{(1-\lambda)(1-1/p)}$ for $1<q<p$, and the $L^p$ bound for $q\ge p$. So $F\cap K\subset V$. By Step 1 there are $g_1,\dots,g_J\in F$, $J=|Z_\theta|$, with $\Lambda_i(g_l)=\delta_{il}$ for the jet functionals $\Lambda_i$. For $f\in K$ take $f_n\in F$ with $f_n\to f$; then $f_n-\sum_i\Lambda_i(f_n)g_i\in F\cap K$ and tends to $f-\sum_i\Lambda_i(f)g_i=f$. So $K=\overline{F\cap K}\subset V\subset K$. $\square$

## 6. Remarks and open points

1. **No rate.** The remainder $R_N$ is controlled for fixed $\varepsilon$ only, through the constants in (D1), which are not made explicit. The proof of (D1$^u$) gives the decay $x^{-\delta/4}\log(3x)$ in $N$, but its constant comes from the bound on $1/\zeta$ in [5, Theorem 4], whose dependence on $\delta$ and $\eta$ is not explicit. So a sequence $e_{N_j,\varepsilon_j}$ converging in $L^p_\gamma$ exists, but $N_j$ is not computed. For $\theta>\Theta$, [12] gives the rate $N^{-(\theta-\Theta)+o(1)}$ in the Hilbert case.
2. **Plain partial sums.** Nothing is proved about $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}+\sum_{k\le N}\mu(k)\rho_{1/k}$ ($\varepsilon=0$), the sums studied numerically in [9]. For $\gamma=0$ this is $\chi+V_N$ in the notation of [3], and by [3, §4] it diverges in $L^p$ when a zero has real part $1/p=\Theta$, in particular in $L^2$. [3, Remark 4.2] leaves open whether a subsequence converges.
3. **Zeros strictly beyond $\theta$.** Theorem 5 settles the finite case. If $Z_\theta$ is infinite, the codimension is infinite, but we do not identify $V$ with the jet kernel; in $L^2$, $\gamma=0$, this identification is [11, Theorem A] for the discrete family and [16] (see [7, §1]) for the continuous one. The constants in the proof of Theorem 5 depend on the target $u$, through $\|a\|_1$, $\sum|a_n|\log n$ and $t_0$, so no rate is given.
4. **$q=1$, and Lorentz spaces between.** At $p=2$, evaluation at a zero on the critical line is bounded on $L^{2,1}$, so $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\notin V_{2,1}$ unconditionally [11, Prop. 8.17(iv)]. Theorem 2 therefore cannot be extended to $q=1$. The obstruction needs both the scale $t^{1/p}$ and $q=1$; once the fundamental function is $o(t^{1/p})$ at $0$, no second index is needed. Let $\varphi$ be concave and increasing on $[0,1]$ with $\varphi(0)=0$, let $\Lambda_\varphi$ be the Lorentz space with norm $\int_0^1g^*\,d\varphi$, and define $S_\varphi$, $V_\varphi$ as $S_{p,q}$, $V_{p,q}$. Suppose $h(t)=\varphi(t)t^{-1/p}\to0$ as $t\to0$. If $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/p$, then $S_\varphi=V_\varphi$.
*Proof.* Concavity gives $\varphi'(s)\le\varphi(s)/s$. So for $0<t_0<1$ and $H(t_0)=\sup_{s<t_0}h(s)$,
$$\|g\|_{\Lambda_\varphi}\le\int_0^1s^{1/p}g^*(s)h(s)\frac{ds}s\le H(t_0)\|g\|_{L^{p,1}}+\|h\|_\infty\log\frac1{t_0}\,\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}},$$
with $\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}}=\sup_ss^{1/p}g^*(s)\le\|g\|_p$. A bounded $f$ constant on each $I_m$ lies in $S_\varphi$, because $\|f\mathbf 1_{(0,1/M]}\|_{\Lambda_\varphi}\le\|f\|_\infty\varphi(1/M)\to0$; this applies to $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}$ and the $\rho_{1/k}$. The proof of Theorem 2 gives $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p,1}}\le C_p$ and $\limsup_N\|e_{N,\varepsilon}\|_p\le C_p\varepsilon^{1-1/p}$. Choosing $t_0$ with $H(t_0)C_p$ small and then $\varepsilon$ small gives $\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\in V_\varphi$. Since $(D_nf)^*(s)=f^*(ns)$ for $s<1/n$ and $0$ after, $(D_nf)^*\le f^*$ and $D_n$ has norm $\le1$ on $\Lambda_\varphi$, and the dilation step is as before. $\square$
If moreover $1/\varphi\in L^r(0,1)$ for every $r<p$, the converse holds. Indeed $f^*(t)\varphi(t)\le\int_0^tf^*d\varphi\le\|f\|_{\Lambda_\varphi}$, so for $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\beta>1/p$ and $1/\beta<r<p$, $|\hat f(\rho)|\le\int_0^1f^*(t)t^{\beta-1}dt\le\|f\|_{\Lambda_\varphi}\|1/\varphi\|_r\|t^{\beta-1}\|_{r'}<\infty$, and the necessity argument of §1 applies. Examples are $\varphi(t)=t^{1/p}\log(C/t)^{-b}$, $b>0$, and $\varphi(t)=t^{1/p}/\log\log(C/t)$, concave for $C=C(p,b)$ large. The second space contains no $L^{p,q}$ with $q>1$: $f(t)=t^{-1/p}/\log(e^p/t)$ is decreasing and lies in every such $L^{p,q}$, while $\varphi'\ge\varphi/(pt)$ gives $\|f\|_{\Lambda_\varphi}=\infty$. So Theorem 2 does not reach it. If instead $\inf h>0$, put $G(t)=pt^{1/p}$, so $G\le(p/\inf h)\varphi$. For $\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/p$, the layer-cake formula $\int_0^1f^*dG=\int_0^\infty G(|\{|f|>\lambda\}|)\,d\lambda$ and the same formula for $\varphi$ give $|\hat f(\rho)|\le\int_0^1f^*dG\le(p/\inf h)\|f\|_{\Lambda_\varphi}$. By the argument of §1, a zero on $\operatorname{Re}s=1/p$ then excludes density; for $p=2$ such zeros exist. So for $p=2$, and $h$ either tending to $0$ or bounded below, density in $\Lambda_\varphi$ requires $h\to0$, and is then equivalent to RH when $1/\varphi\in L^r$ for all $r<2$. $L^{2,1}$ ($h=1$) is on the blocked side. The other spaces with fundamental function $\asymp t^{1/2}$, the $L^{2,q}$ with $q>1$, are dense under RH (Theorem 2), so there the second index decides. We do not treat $\varphi$ with $\liminf_{t\to0}h=0<\limsup_{t\to0}h$.
5. **Other settings.** The Hardy-space statement of Ghosh, Kremnizer, Noor and Santos [7, Theorem 4.8] for $H^p(\mathbb D)$, $1<p<2$, is not covered: Noor's unitary map [10] is special to $p=2$, and we have not compared $H^p(\mathbb D)$ with $L^p_\gamma$ on steps. For Dirichlet $L$-functions, (D1) holds by [5, Theorem 4] and the Perron argument in the proof of (D1$^u$); we have not checked the Hadamard step (3.1) for nonprincipal characters.
6. **What the result does not do.** Corollary 2 is an equivalent form of RH, like [1]. Its proof uses only the zero-free half-plane and standard properties of $\zeta$, so it gives no information about where the zeros are.

Proposition 4 needs $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_\theta=\min(\frac18,\frac{1-\theta}2)$. At $\theta=\frac12$ the value at $\varepsilon=0.05$ exceeds the value at $\varepsilon=0.1$. We read this as slow convergence in $N$: the remainder is driven by the tail of $\sum\mu(k)k^{-w}$ on $\operatorname{Re}w=\frac12+\varepsilon$, which under RH should decay like $N^{-\varepsilon}$ [heur], only $0.63$ at $N=10^4$, $\varepsilon=0.05$. We had predicted monotonicity in $\varepsilon$ at $N=10^4$ for $\theta=\frac12$, and that the $\theta=0.75$ norms would be below half the $\theta=\frac12$ norms; both predictions failed (the ratio is $0.76$ at $\varepsilon=0.2$).
