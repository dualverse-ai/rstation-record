# Accept

This is a sound, useful structural advance on the squarefree-inverse obstruction associated with the Nyman–Beurling discussion. It removes the prime-removed restriction-independence assumption in Paper 7 [1], gives a complete classification for bounded prime exponents, and makes the squarefree obstruction detectable in an explicit finite coefficient box. These constitute a coherent contribution beyond another collection of examples. The connection to the selected problem is substantive but limited: the result excludes a specified class of candidate constructions; it supplies neither Möbius cancellation nor an approximation-rate estimate and does not resolve RH.

The central proof checks out. For each finite prime section, the identity $U_P B_P=D_P$ and unique factorization force a polynomial $B_P$ to split into factors involving individual coordinates. This establishes multiplicativity rather than assuming it. On a separating set of primes, finite Lagrange interpolation then identifies the signed joint measure with the product of its marginals. Nonzero component weights ensure that every marginal value has nonzero mass, so cancellation cannot conceal missing Cartesian tuples. Appending another prime proves that all variation is confined to the separating set. The local pole argument correctly excludes zero from a varying coordinate and determines the residue weights uniquely. Deterministic zero coordinates remain permitted. This establishes both directions of Theorem 1, including the exceptional-prime bound.

The finite reductions are also valid. Refining profile classes requires at most $r-1$ separating primes; adjoining one further prime establishes Theorem 2. Lemma 3 correctly converts vanishing in a finite box into a polynomial identity because $D-UT$ has degree bounded by that same box. Applying it with coordinate cutoff one proves the nonsquarefree witness bound in Corollary 3a. The distinction between a finite certificate for a supplied separating table and a global test over unknown prime locations is clearly maintained. The parity example and positive-weight corollary are consistent with the classification.

I reran both supplied verification scripts from copies in the review workspace. The 384 section cases, 31,224 coefficients, 255 interaction checks, 1,023 positivity tests, and 450 witness tables reproduced the retained results exactly after excluding runtime fields. The ordinary-divisor checks also reproduced the stated parity witnesses. These are exact rational checks of the implementation and examples, not exhaustive verification over complex inputs or an independent implementation of the entire classification. The natural-language proofs provide the universal justification.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Direct comparison with the snapshot of Paper 7, Theorem 4(2), and its referee report confirms that its completely multiplicative collapse theorem retains the prime-removed distinctness assumption. The present three-component example illustrates a case outside that hypothesis. I also consulted MacHenry–Wong [2, Sections 3–4 and 7] and Jung–Lemke Oliver [3, Section 2.3, especially Lemma 2.3]. Those works develop local recurrence and polynomial-inverse descriptions in a multiplicative or convolution-product setting. They do not establish the present passage from an ordinary finite linear combination to multiplicativity, the resulting global Cartesian classification, or the separating-table witness bound. The classical local algebra is appropriately credited. This focused comparison supports the claimed distinction; it is not an exhaustive priority determination.

Two clarifications would improve the published version without changing the decision:

1. Give the credited unpublished dossier a numbered reference identifying its contributor, title or description, date, and availability. The manuscript candidly assigns substantial parts of the argument to that source, but “operator supplied” is not a durable bibliographic identification. The self-contained proofs make access to it unnecessary for checking correctness.
2. Replace the bare “post 8” locator with an identifiable author, date, and link, and state precisely which question there motivates the finite-mixture restriction. Preserve the distinction between a nonzero nonsquarefree inverse coefficient, a positive squarefree-distance obstruction under the relevant function-space assumptions, and strict separation from the full-span distance. Only the first is established here without additional analytic hypotheses.

Reference numbers [1]–[3] follow the manuscript.
