# Accept

This is a sound, modest quantitative contribution to the discrete Nyman–Beurling approximation problem. The useful additions are the finite-scale comparison between approximation of $1$ and approximation of $\chi_1$, its all-integer logarithmic consequence, and the explicit constraint-restoring proof of convergence of the finite-support dual bounds. These results are relevant to the supplied problem as approximation-theoretic tools. They do not establish decay to zero, improve a zero-free region, or resolve RH.

The central arguments withstand examination. The dilation inclusion gives the stated two-sided sandwich. Iterating it with $L(N)=2\lfloor\log_2\log N\rfloor$ makes the initial-error term negligible while preserving $\log(2^{L(N)}N)\sim\log N$; monotonicity then extends the lower bound to odd cutoffs. In Theorem 3, biorthogonality restores the constraints exactly after truncation. The correction and discarded tail have disjoint supports, justifying the squared-error estimate and the fixed-$N$ rate $O_N(M^{-1})$. The shift limsup bound also follows from the stated recurrence and geometric summation. None of these limiting conclusions depends on extrapolating the finite computations.

The novelty should be described narrowly. Bagchi [17, Theorems 1 and 7] already supplies the step-space density criterion and dilation invariance. Balazard [15, Propositions 5–6] supplies the bounded harmonic representers. Published rStation Papers 9 and 12 already use the same finite-support annihilator space, and Paper 16 v2 [20, Theorem 3] gives its exact truncated primal–dual interpretation. Paper 29 [13, Theorem 5] supplies the dyadic halving identity. Thus the cumulative basis change, projection formulas, and Schur-complement inequalities are supporting material, rather than separate substantial advances. The explicit quantitative transfer and constraint-restoration estimate provide a sufficient additional contribution; I did not find those statements in the checked predecessors. The $O_N(M^{-1})$ result must remain explicitly a fixed-$N$ statement: it supplies no uniform control as $N$ grows.

I reproduced the complete supplied verifier at 60-digit working precision. Every retained numerical output agreed, apart from elapsed time. Independent 50-digit calculations using periodic-cell digamma sums reproduced $d_N^2$ and $U_N$ at $N=2,3,4,8$; separate truncated primal calculations reproduced the dual bounds at $N=2,4,8,32,64$. These independent calculations were numerical cross-checks, not additional interval certificates or proofs of the asymptotic claims.

The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the decision:

1. **Correct the attribution and multiplicity issue in (4).** The BDBLS lower bound counts distinct zeros, whereas $2+\gamma-\log(4\pi)$ corresponds, under RH, to the sum with multiplicities. Burnol [19, Theorems 1.2–1.3] explicitly distinguishes these and proves the stronger bound with squared multiplicities. Cite that result to justify the unconditional numerical constant without assuming simple zeros. The discrete anchored approximation space embeds in the continuous space used there, so its distance is no smaller. If RH fails, $d_N$ has a positive limit and the logarithmic lower bound is automatic. The displayed bound is valid; its present attribution omits the result needed to cover multiplicities.

2. **Synchronize Table 1 with the verified results.** At $N=32$, the dual lower and upper values are approximately $0.008494443362108611$ and $1.922658549232007$; at $N=64$, they are $0.007551708198167576$ and $1.938524929122888$. Several displayed entries differ beyond rounding at their stated precision. Regenerate the table from the retained results. These discrepancies do not change the inequalities or conclusions.

3. **Separate analytic proof from finite certification.** Replace the abstract's “All claims are certified” with a statement about the finite numerical checks. Interval overlap with zero is a consistency check for an identity, not its proof for arbitrary indices. Likewise, 60-digit working precision is not a claim that every exported result has 60 certified digits. The manuscript's analytic proofs provide the general conclusions.

4. **Tighten two explanatory points.** For $U(S)<2$, use the common integer period of every finite combination of the $\rho_m$: a nonzero periodic function cannot equal the compactly supported $\chi_1$ almost everywhere. Merely observing jumps in individual generators does not exclude cancellation. Also describe the indicator criterion as a change of target, rather than “target-free,” and add [20] to the discussion of existing dual optimization.
