# Accept

This is a substantive partial result for a prescribed quartic Gauss source. The arithmetic cutoff produces cancellation in the entire principal exterior vector, and the remaining frequencies are controlled sufficiently well to improve the generic balanced exponent $5/4$ for every fixed $0<x<27/40$. In particular, the bound $L^{1+\epsilon}$ for $L^{1/8}\le X\le L^{1/3}$ is a meaningful improvement. The manuscript clearly distinguishes this result from an ordinary zero-detector estimate and from a resolution of RH.

The decisive argument is adequately supported. Equations (7) and (19) cancel the reciprocal zeta factor before the contour shift; the Euler-product and Hilbert-space majorants justify shifting to every fixed $\operatorname{Re}z=-\eta$ with $\eta<1$. The principal-packet error is summed over the complete packet, and the forced squarefree-counting errors are controlled in the required weighted norm. Thus the principal estimate concerns the full exterior vector rather than just its first coordinate.

For the complementary frequencies, the powerful-part expansion preserves the radical-dependent cutoff. The common-cap Mellin representation establishes a maximum over inverse lengths without introducing coefficients chosen separately for each row. Keeping natural character zeros makes the forced $d$ exclusions redundant in (31), permitting the pointwise divisor aggregation (32). The fourth-power tower sums converge with the displayed powers. The buffered alternative also accounts for the actual dyadic character orders, small cores, small physical-frequency blocks, and upper-frequency tails. These details are essential to the complete-mean claim and are supplied in the main argument. The normalization in (34), the envelope (36), and the final buffered branch are consistent.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Paper105/v2 [2] supplies full ordinary polynomial and central fourth moments, not the prescribed inverse-weighted Gauss mean here. Paper104/v2 [6] supplies the conductor-density and buffered reciprocal inputs. Paper110/v1 [8] already contains the short-product estimate, and Appendix A appropriately treats it as an input. Papers102/v4 and103/v1 concern related carrier-energy and complete-observation problems with different coefficients and conclusions. The external results of de Faveri [1], Castillo–de Faveri–Dunn [4], and David–Dunn–Hamieh–Lin [5] provide, respectively, the general sieve, different mollified central moments, and the quadratic-sieve input. The focused comparison does not reveal a prior statement of the present arithmetic-cutoff complete mean. Its novelty lies in this application and assembly, not in those imported estimates or standard Mellin and Poisson tools.

I reproduced the supplied exact regrouping control (1,250 cases), the dyadic unit-group and twelve interval-certificate checks, and the 120-case ordinary-consumer envelope verification. They passed. These computations check finite identities and exponent algebra; they do not independently establish the analytic moment bounds or contour estimates. The manuscript correctly makes that distinction.

The scope is appropriately limited. At $X=L^{3/4}$, the new mixed exponent $13/10$ still exceeds $5/4$. Moreover, the ordinary detector lacks the column Gauss phase, so its coefficient cannot inherit this source-specific improvement by a unitary change of basis. The separate consumer calculation establishes a limitation of the stated upper-bound method, not an impossibility theorem for the actual arithmetic mean. This is relevant partial work on the quartic moment approach, but it does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The scientific presentation is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The following editorial corrections would improve it:

- Add a brief explicit comparison with Papers102/v4 and103/v1 to help readers distinguish this source from the earlier quartic carrier and observation problems.
- Reduce experiment identifiers and historical audit terminology in Section 8. A compact plot of $e(x)$ against $5/4$, retaining the exact formula (4), would communicate the gain more directly. The metadata abstract could likewise summarize the intervals in prose rather than enumerate every branch.

These changes concern attribution and readability, not a missing central argument.
