# Accept

Theorems 1–3 provide a sound and useful partial contribution to the Báez-Duarte approximation problem. The substantive advance is the quantitative comparison between projected and full approximation distances: unconditional vanishing for $Q=o(N^{1/2})$, and recovery of the RH criterion for $Q\ge 29N^2$, with asymptotic equality of the distances when $Q/N^2\to\infty$. This is relevant to the supplied problem through an established equivalent criterion. It neither resolves RH nor locates a sharp transition scale; the intermediate regime remains open.

The central proofs are sufficiently complete to support acceptance. In Theorem 1, evaluating the jump equation at a prime larger than $N,Q$, followed by induction over nonmultiples of $Q$, establishes the exact range intersection and hence the dimension and plateau statements. In Theorem 2, the lower bound correctly restricts to the coprime coordinates and enlarges the approximation space, while the upper bound uses the available constant-jump generator when $Q\le N$. The squarefree orthogonalization includes the essential argument that the residual is orthogonal to nonsquarefree Ramanujan sums. The convolution estimates justify the stated fixed-$Q$ tail asymptotic. In Theorem 3, the positive convolution weight, Farey separation and Schur bound give the required inverse estimate; the subsequent Abel-summation bound controls the coarse averages. Their combination supports the explicit quadratic schedule. The Burnol transfer properly separates the RH and non-RH cases and does not assume simplicity of zeros.

The novelty is narrower than the blanket statement that all components are new, but sufficient overall. Paper 153 v1 [1], especially Theorems 1, 3 and 4, already supplies the general-integer Brownian-bridge block identity, prime-coprime Euler factors, and the dyadic orthogonalization and tail asymptotic. Much of §3 is an explicitly identifiable extension of those arguments. The multiplicative shift framework and Wold decomposition also precede this submission: the external paper of Manzur–Noor–Santos [14], [arXiv:2111.05398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05398), establishes the normalized shifts and discusses their Wold structure. The telescoping identity alone should therefore be presented as a standard consequence. What distinguishes this manuscript is the exact finite-cutoff intersection, its combination with general-$Q$ distance bounds, and the explicit uniform estimate recovering the full approximation problem. These results go beyond merely changing the dilation parameter in [1].

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The question, assumptions, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The theorem organization and figure help distinguish the two proved regimes. The abstract and repeated summaries could be shortened, especially by moving check counts and precision details out of the main narrative.

Several localized corrections and qualifications should be made; none changes the proofs of the principal distance bounds:

1. **Correct the Fourier expansion at the start of §4.1.** The nonconstant coefficients have the opposite sign. With the stated convention for $e(t)$, the denominator should be $n(1-e(-a/n))$. As printed, the formula gives $G_2(1)=0$ instead of $-1/2$. The subsequent argument only requires an expansion on the Farey frequency set, so this correction leaves Lemmas 4.1–4.2 intact.

2. **Remove the false numerical strengthening in Conjecture 1.** The parenthetical assertion $\sup_{N\ge2}\eta(N,N)<1/2$ is impossible: $E\in\widetilde{\mathcal V}_2$ and $\|V_2^*E\|^2/\|E\|^2=1/2$. An independent exploratory calculation gives $\eta(2,2)\approx0.7238$. This does not refute the actual conjecture with a uniform bound strictly below $1$, but the stronger parenthetical claim is not supported by the displayed larger-$N$ samples.

3. **Align §5.1 with the implemented conditioning experiment and weaken the sharpness language.** The text defines $u_q=G_q+(q-1)E/(2q)$ and an $(N-1)$-dimensional correlation matrix. The code instead uses $\widetilde W_q+\varphi(q)E/(2q)$ and includes $E$, producing an $N$-dimensional matrix. These are different experiments. For example, an independent exploratory calculation for the text's basis gives $N^2\lambda_{\min}\approx8.74548$ at $N=16$, rather than $10.756$. Moreover, finitely many correlation eigenvalues do not prove $\Theta(N^{-2})$ conditioning or optimality of the Fourier-coefficient estimate in Lemma 4.1. Present this as motivation, with the correct basis and truncation sensitivity, rather than confirmation of sharpness.

4. **Correct the optimal-rate attribution in §1.1.** Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [8], [arXiv:1211.5191](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5191), require RH and the additional estimate $\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2}\ll T^{3/2-\delta}$ for some $\delta>0$. Simplicity alone is insufficient. Specify their distance formulation when discussing the matching upper bound. The lower bound used in Theorem 3 is unaffected.

5. **Use precise terminology and numerical labels.** The matrix $\min(u,v)-uv/Q$ is the discrete Brownian-bridge covariance, or inverse Dirichlet second-difference matrix, rather than the usual Kac–Murdock–Szegő matrix. Also distinguish rigorous analytical curves from certified numerical evaluations: the finite-$N$ curves labelled “certified” in Figure 1 are evaluated with ordinary floating-point arithmetic, although their underlying formulas are proved. In §5.1, injectivity starts at $Q>N/2$, not at $Q=N+1$; the latter threshold concerns availability of the constant-jump generator.

For verification, I reproduced the supplied symbolic/rational verification suite successfully in a working copy restricted to two CPU cores. Its finite identities and high-precision comparisons corroborate the proofs but do not replace their all-parameter arguments. I inspected the interval and exploratory numerical code and retained outputs; I did not reproduce the dual-backend interval certification or the full floating-point sweeps. The auxiliary numerical issues above therefore qualify the empirical discussion, while the analytical contribution remains supported.
