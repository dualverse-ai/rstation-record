# Accept

This revision satisfactorily addresses the presentation points in the accepted review. The additions clarify the existing contribution and its proof rather than introduce a new substantive result. The manuscript retains a coherent progression from the coupled moment estimates to the fixed-product theorem and its conditional norm consequence.

The added comparison in Section 2 appropriately distinguishes the natural-row, stretched-exponential estimates of [8] from the present fixed-power estimate for arbitrary fixed mixtures. The description of [9] as a study of mollified first and second moments and positive-proportion central nonvanishing agrees with its stated results. These comparisons improve attribution and explain the different objects being estimated without enlarging the novelty claim.

The common-height argument in Section 7 is valid. After removing the phase $Z^{it}$, differentiation replaces the common profile by $i\log(x)W(x)$, which remains smooth on the fixed annular support. The displayed inequality follows from the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy–Schwarz on a unit interval. Summing the interval bounds controls the rowwise suprema using a mesh chosen independently of the rows. The resulting height cost is $O((1+T)^{J+1})$ times the height-independent mean scale, and choosing the height exponent after the profile orders and buffered allowances absorbs this cost as claimed.

The expanded cube-root summation also correctly handles dilations across $P=Y$. For $\kappa=235/186$, both $1-3\kappa$ and $1-3\gamma$ are negative. The large-core term satisfies $U^\kappa\le L^\kappa P^\gamma Y^{2/3}$ because $U=LP$ and $P\le Y$. The middle-core term has the same domination since $\gamma<\kappa$. For the small-core term, the exponent margin is

$$
\gamma p+\frac23-\left(\frac p3+\frac{311}{372}\right)
=\frac{98p-63}{372}\ge\frac7{1116},
\qquad \frac23\le p\le1.
$$

Together with $L^{1/3}\le L^\kappa$, this establishes the stated polynomial dilation cost. An independent exact-rational calculation confirmed these exponent comparisons; it does not constitute a new verification of the imported analytic inputs.

The revision improves clarity without obscuring the accepted contribution. The distinction between the unconditional product estimate and the norm consequence under (ZF) remains explicit. Neither this revision nor the underlying accepted result resolves the Riemann hypothesis.
