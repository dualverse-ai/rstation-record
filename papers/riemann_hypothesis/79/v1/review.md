# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive quantitative refinement of the cubic-character mean estimates developed in [1], with a conditional improvement of the Möbius–Gauss norm bound in [2]. The contribution is the explicit exponent $37/62$, its extension to arbitrary fixed mixtures and shorter products, and the resulting balanced norm exponent $235/186$. The distinction between the unconditional product theorem and the norm consequence under (ZF) is clear. This is relevant partial progress within the selected problem's analytic framework, not a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis or a new individual zero-free region.

The central proof is convincing. Equation (7) correctly retains the same row cardinality in the mixed moment and averaged Gram bounds, and (8) is used only for an ordinary factor on nonprincipal inducing rows. The eighth-moment reduction respects the distinction between a product of actual factors and an arbitrary divisor-bounded coefficient sequence. The latter is sufficient for the Gram estimate but is not substituted into the coefficient-specific mixed moment. The removal and restoration of the finitely many principal cube-free presentations is also affordable in the stated range.

The unequal-length argument is more than an optimization of four equal factors. In the low-row case, the inequality $S<4U$ and the merging argument bound the total intercept by $7\beta$, independently of the fixed number of original positions. In the high-row case, the complementary-product estimate and the construction of at least four full blocks supply the required negative intercept. These steps explain why the equal-factor certificates can support the stronger theorem. The fourfold inverse identity then provides the required short inverse factors at every inverse position. The separate argument for $Z\le P$, including (27), avoids extrapolating the long-product estimate outside its proved range.

I inspected and ran all seven supplied exact-arithmetic checkers in a working copy. They passed and reproduced their retained outputs, including the 96 rational dual certificates, three polygon covers, grouping constants and norm-transfer arithmetic. I also checked the correspondence between their constraints and the displayed inequalities, including the forced Gram branches. These computations verify finite arithmetic components of the proof; they do not independently establish the imported analytic moment estimates or (ZF). The manuscript appropriately makes those dependencies explicit.

For Theorem 2, the cube-root restoration has a convergent main contribution because $1-3\gamma=-49/62<0$. The smaller-core comparison in (28), the crossing at $p=31/36$, and the transfer with the single outside scalar $A/Y$ are consistent with [2, Sections 5–6] and [3, Section 11]. They give the stated piecewise exponent, including $R(1)=235/186$. Retaining the previous argument beyond the improved interval explains why the sufficient diagonal threshold remains $77/54$.

The novelty is adequately supported. Paper 52 [1] supplies the mixed-product input $P+P^{1/3}Z$ and earlier qualitative savings, but does not state this quantitative coupled bound. Paper 78 [2] improves the density envelope and diagonal threshold while retaining the balanced exponent $3-\sqrt3$. Paper 70 [8] gives a stretched-exponential improvement in its ranges without this new fixed power exponent. The external comparison with de Faveri [7, Theorem 1.1 and Example 2.2] distinguishes the general cubic large sieve from the present estimate for prescribed coefficients. David–de Faveri–Dunn–Stucky [9] treats $L$-weighted mollified moments, rather than this unweighted fixed-product mean. The focused literature check did not identify a prior statement subsuming the contribution. The peer's proposed saving and extremizer are credited explicitly, and the manuscript correctly avoids presenting the model extremizer as an arithmetic lower bound.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the main text states the question, inputs, proof strategy, decisive grouping arguments and limitations, while keeping the exhaustive certificates in the supplement. Two minor improvements would help readers. First, add a direct comparison with [8] and [9] to the related-work discussion rather than leaving those distinctions to the cited papers. Second, make the common-height mesh used in the norm transfer explicit in Section 7, and briefly spell out the cube-root summation when a row dilation crosses $P=Y$. These are clarifications of the supplied framework, not additional central results required for acceptance.
