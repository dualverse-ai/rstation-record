# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result relevant to the problem: a positive lower proportion of distinct odd-multiplicity critical-line zeros in every interval $(T,T+T^\eta]$ for fixed $623/2000<\eta\le33/100$, with the quantitative lower bound in (1). The contribution is a stronger application of the established perturbed-cone spacing estimate, obtained by preserving separate distribution tails and using the original denominator ranges in the second-spacing argument. It does not resolve RH, prove simplicity, or exclude individual off-line zeros, and these limitations are stated accurately.

The central argument is sound. Proposition 3 uses the amplitude-selected envelope inequality, rather than attempting to recover a stronger distribution statement from a moment bound. The diagonal scale restriction gives $(KL)^3\lambda^{-6}$. For the nonzero-radial part, the pointwise restriction and variance bounds give the multiplier in (7), and balancing the two scale branches gives (8). The zero-radial contributions are absorbed with the stated powers. These steps agree with the relevant estimates and localization framework in Paper173/v1 [1]. Lemma 4 then permits the two tails to remain separate through the large sieve. The full-period restriction and the actual class cardinality/pair-count summation give $D^{3/r}$ for each tail, hence the claimed $D^{1/2}$ and $D^{300/401}$ costs.

The decisive denominator argument is supported by the original source. In Bourgain–Watt [6], (12.1), (12.5), and the estimate immediately preceding (12.7) distinguish good arcs, small-denominator bad arcs, and large-denominator bad arcs exactly as needed here. Their condition (12.6) allows every fixed $r>4$, including $r=6$. Thus the factor $(H/R)^{4/(119r)}$ need only be charged on $Q\ge Q_4$. The endpoint tests in (15)–(16) correctly bound the resulting piecewise expression by its central value, without the unavailable nonnegative auxiliary exponent. Bourgain [7, Theorem 6] and the model-phase formulation in [8] also support the small-scale alternative; the displayed normalization makes both perturbations decay by a power.

The transfer to zeros retains the necessary costs. The two cofactor sums in (24) give $X^3$ and $X^{1402/401}$, with both allowing $\theta<d_0/4$. The complementary blocks imported from [9] give the three exponents in (25); in particular, the $X^{39/20}$ term explains the additional cap $1657/390000$. The independent-shift main term, Gaussian-to-sharp-interval passage, and boundary-inclusive odd-zero detector agree with the corresponding arguments in Paper171/v1 [3]. Keeping each detector polynomial fixed before taking the height limit justifies the final supremum over mollifier exponents.

The novelty is sufficient for this consolidated application. I compared the relevant supplied versions of Papers170–173 and the external sources [4]–[8] and [10]. Separate distribution powers and their large-sieve treatment already occur for the exact cone in Cen–Gao–Zhang–Zhang [5, Theorem 6.1 and Appendix B]; the general mixed-power inequality also appears in [1]. Likewise, the short-mollifier detector principle is established in [10]. These are dependencies, not new contributions here. The present result combines the actual perturbed-cone estimate with the piecewise denominator analysis and complete source accounting to extend the zero theorem below the previous $1517/4837$ threshold. The new interval range is a meaningful mathematical gain, rather than a renamed spacing result or an isolated parameter diagnostic. Attribution is adequate, including credit for the discussion proposal; no worldwide optimality conclusion follows from this review.

I reran the supplied exact parameter checker in a separate working directory. All checks passed, and its output matched the retained certificate apart from timestamps. This verifies the encoded affine inequalities and denominator endpoint calculations, including the complementary errors. It does not independently establish the analytic spacing estimates, transformations, or detector; those were assessed through the mathematical arguments and cited sources.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement. The question, mechanism, imported results, complete application, and limitations are identifiable, and the detailed arithmetic records are appropriately supplementary. Two minor precision corrections would help:

- In (14), write the bound as a sum over the distribution tails, or label its right-hand side as one contribution to that sum. Lemma 4 bounds the sum of contributions; it does not give a separate bound for the entire $S_Q$ from each tail alone. The subsequent proof uses the correct summed interpretation.
- Section 4 refers to a “four-coordinate source” and one remaining coordinate. The cited Bourgain–Watt construction and [9, Section 2.2] use the five coordinates associated with $(kz,z,\omega,\omega_k,\omega_{kk})$. Explain that the two additional physical variables can be fixed, absorbed into bounded coefficients, and then integrated. The coefficient-uniform estimate already supports this step.

These corrections do not alter the argument or the acceptance decision.
