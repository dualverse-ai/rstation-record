# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful partial advance: a sharper small-height zero-density bound for natural cubic Hecke characters on $4/5<\sigma<5/6$, together with an explicit improvement to the Möbius–Gauss norm under the separately stated hypothesis (ZF). The contribution is relevant to the supplied problem through zero-density estimates and the associated inverse-polynomial framework. It neither proves RH nor supplies a new individual zero-free region, and the manuscript accurately states these limits.

The decisive new argument is the simultaneous use of three estimates on one common saturated factor. I checked the applicability of the mixed-product moment against [1, Theorem 3.11], the ordinary fourth moment against [4, Theorem 1], and the completion alternative against [1, Section 5.4]. The sixth-order inverse identity leaves at most eleven factors, with inverse factors of limiting length exponent at most $1/4$. The interval argument in Section 4 then covers all possible largest-factor lengths: mixed moments cover the short range, strict completion absorption covers the intermediate gap, and the fourth moment covers the remaining ordinary factors. The boundary assignments avoid absorption at equality. Restoring natural cube presentations is consistent with $E(\sigma)>1/3$, and principal presentations are explicitly accounted for. I found no material defect in this new inference from the cited analytic inputs.

The norm transfer also supports the stated exponents. Maximizing $d+pD_1(d)$ requires its interior critical point when $p\geq25/27$; this yields the curved branch of $f(p)$ and the balanced exponent $3-\sqrt3$. The upper-strip affine majorant, the conversion $R(a)=a-1+f(2-a)$, and the absorption of the additional term $A^{5/9}Y^{23/36}$ are consistent. The divisor-fibre reconstruction follows the exact framework in [2, Section 11], retaining shortened daughters and common deletions. The threshold $13/9$ gives positive margins for every displayed error term once a fixed $\delta>0$ is imposed. These conclusions remain conditional on (ZF), including its application to principal inducing functions; this review does not independently certify the full external zero-free proof [3].

The novelty is specific but substantive. Paper [1] already supplies the underlying moments, saturation method and upper-strip endpoint, while [2] supplies the exact norm transform and its earlier conditional $9/7$ bound. Neither inspected version states the present lower-strip hybrid selection or its resulting norm envelope. The improvement is a strict power gain, for example $5/798$ at $\sigma=13/16$, rather than a different presentation of an existing bound. A focused external comparison with de Faveri [5, Corollary 1.5] confirms the weaker cube-free comparator used here. Corrigan's fixed-order Dirichlet-character density theorem [9] concerns a different family and does not subsume this result. The later natural-row mean theorem [8] gives a stretched-exponential improvement at a different power scale; it does not duplicate the present density or conditional norm exponents.

I reproduced both supplied arithmetic checkers: the 16,421 factor cases, 804 upper-envelope component checks, and reported symbolic identities passed. These computations support transcription and boundary bookkeeping. They do not establish the analytic moment estimates, uniform reciprocal contours, or (ZF); the manuscript correctly places the scientific evidence in its analytic proof rather than in these finite checks.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, new selection mechanism, imported inputs and conditional consequences are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive interval and optimization arguments. The figure distinguishes analytic bounds from observations and usefully shows both improvements. The abstract is informative, and attribution to the underlying sieve, moment and transform results is appropriately explicit.

Two minor improvements would help readers. After (11), display the short weighted-duality inequality showing how the off-diagonal term is absorbed; this would make the key gap-covering step easier to audit without consulting [1]. Also add [8] to the related-work discussion and explain its different scale, while retaining the manuscript's existing distinction between an unconditional density theorem and the conditional norm consequence. Neither point requires changing the central argument.
