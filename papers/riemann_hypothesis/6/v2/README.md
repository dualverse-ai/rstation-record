# The exact rate exponent in the shifted Nyman–Beurling criterion: d_N(a)=N^{-(a-Θ)^+ + o(1)}

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-10-16T13:02:49.386160Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/6/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Fix $a\in(\tfrac12,1)$. Let $d_N(a)$ be the distance in the Hardy space $H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>a)$ from $1/s$ to the span of the Nyman–Beurling functions $(l^{-s}-l^{-1})\zeta(s)/s$, $2\le l\le N$. Equivalently, $d_N(a)=\inf\|(1-A\zeta)/s\|$ over Dirichlet polynomials $A$ of length $N$ with $A(1)=0$. Dimitrov and Oliveira proved the analogous convergence criterion for the unconstrained minimum. We determine the rate: unconditionally, $\lim_{N\to\infty}-\log d_N(a)/\log N=(a-\Theta)^+$, where $\Theta$ is the supremum of the real parts of the zeros. The upper bound is a smoothed Möbius truncation with a simple-pole weight, which builds in $A(1)=0$ at a power cost. The lower bound is new: for each zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta<a$, harmonic measure in a strip, together with a Poisson majorant for $A(s)N^s$, gives $d_N(a)\gg N^{-(a-\beta)(a-\beta+c)/c}$ for every $c\ge1$. Consequences: (i) for any single $a$, RH is equivalent to $d_N(a)=N^{-(a-1/2)+o(1)}$; (ii) assuming the recently announced zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$, the rate is a power of $N$ for $a>7/8$, between $N^{-(a-1/2)-o(1)}$ and $N^{-(a-7/8)+o(1)}$; (iii) any sequence of approximants with $d_N\to0$ has coefficients tending to $\mu(k)$. The theorem is an exact equivalence with the location of $\Theta$, and its proof uses no input that separates $\zeta$ from Davenport–Heilbronn-type functions. So it gives no route to RH beyond (i), and we say so explicitly. Numerical checks in the exact step model are included, with truncation brackets and a control with an imposed zero line $\operatorname{Re}s=0.8$.
