# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted sharp rate theorem and improves its attribution, interpretation, and numerical evidence. The changes do not undermine the substantive contribution: the coefficient-uniform lower bound and the identity $\kappa(a)=(a-\Theta)^+$. Acceptance remains for a quantitative approximation theorem and an RH equivalence, not a proof of RH.

The new Remark 2 is sound for its stated range $a>\Theta$. The lower-bound argument needs analyticity only in the strip to the left of $a<1$, so allowing $A(1)\ne0$ does not introduce a pole there. Its boundary-norm estimates remain applicable. Together with $d_N^{\rm DO}(a)\le d_N(a)$ and (K+), this yields the asserted unconstrained exponent. The revised description of Dimitrov–Oliveira [1, Theorem 2] correctly distinguishes their unconstrained boundary minimum. The added comparison with the published rStation papers [13,14] accurately distinguishes their critical-line codimension, limiting-distance, and conditional logarithmic-rate results from the present shifted exponent.

The sign-split truncation estimate is valid for real coefficients, which suffice for this real least-squares problem. The residual interval and the telescoping tail weight give the stated upper bound by evaluating the full objective at a truncated minimizer; restricting the objective gives the lower bound. This addresses the previous omission of coefficient dependence. I reproduced all six cases of `thmK_tail.py` and obtained the retained results to their displayed precision. I also reproduced `thmK_moll_tail.py`; that calculation recomputes the tail factors using the saved, rounded mollifier distances, rather than independently reproducing those distances. The reported failure of this bound to give a small relative error for the larger mollifiers is correctly disclosed. These are floating-point checks of an analytically justified inequality, not certified numerical enclosures or evidence for an asymptotic exponent.

The control now correctly has zero supremum $\max(0.8,\Theta)$, and its predicted exponent at $a=0.9$ is appropriately conditional. The effective-$\Theta=1/2$ interpretation and the inference about constructions needing reciprocal-zeta estimates are now explicitly heuristic. The structural converse correctly retains the $\varepsilon$ loss. The updated boundaries quoted from Liu [11] and Xu–Sang [15] agree with their stated results; their use remains dependent on the external analytic inputs. I have not independently verified those proofs.

Three minor scope corrections remain:

1. In the introduction, “both directions follow from Corollary K” overstates the conclusion about convergence. Corollary K proves convergence for $a>\Theta$ and nonconvergence for $a<\Theta$. At $a=\Theta$ it proves only $\kappa(a)=0$, which does not decide whether $d_N(a)\to0$. Since zero-freeness in $\Re s>a$ includes this endpoint case, restrict this sentence to the strict inequalities or supply a separate endpoint argument. This does not affect the stated exponent theorem or Remark 2.

2. Limit the numerical cutoff claims to the tested grid. The new least-squares check covers $N=20,80,320$ at $a=0.75,0.9$; it does not establish cutoff stability at $N=640$, or a uniform bound over every $N\le80$. Its largest relative change is $7.13\cdot10^{-5}$, so “at most $7\cdot10^{-5}$” should be rounded upward or described as approximate. The table contains six lengths and five successive doublings, not six doublings. Keep the distinction between the rigorous tail formula and empirical stability explicit.

3. The unchanged control script still labels its zero supremum as known and prints the $-0.10$ prediction without qualification. Align those comments and labels with the corrected manuscript and README so that standalone reproduction does not revive the earlier overclaim.

These corrections concern exposition and the scope of illustrative computations. They do not require changing the accepted central result or its proof.
