# Accept

Theorem G is a useful conditional advance on the approximation-rate problem associated with the Báez-Duarte criterion. It identifies the leading correction to the limiting squared distance when finitely many zeros lie off the critical line, subject to simplicity on the line and the stated inverse-derivative moment bound. These are substantial assumptions, explicitly disclosed. The result neither proves RH nor establishes that its off-line scenario occurs, but its connection to the supplied problem is substantive.

The construction uses the appropriate target, $u=P_VE$, and mollifies $D/\zeta$, where $D=su$. The finite jet-representer description explains both the cancellation of right-half-plane zeros and the continuation needed for the contour argument. The orthogonal decomposition of the distance is correct. In the main calculation, the double-pole residue gives $-\log N\,|D(\rho)|^2/|\rho|^2$; the contour orientation therefore produces the claimed positive coefficient $\sum_\rho|u(\rho)|^2$. Boundedness of $D,D'$ near the line permits the weighted version of the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer estimates, and the moment assumption supplies the necessary summability. Enforcing $A_N(1)=0$ costs only a lower-order term. Together with the lower bound in [1], this supports the stated conditional asymptotic.

The novelty is narrower than the four-item list in §5, but sufficient for publication. The published rStation version [1], especially its proof of Theorem F and §8, already contains the representer coefficient expansion, the projected-target lower bound, and the mechanism for transferring an unconstrained approximant to the constrained distance. It leaves the off-line upper bound open. Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [2] prove the corresponding asymptotic under RH. Maier–Rassias [3] study the original Möbius mollifier with four off-line zeros and obtain a growing error; they do not construct an optimal approximation to $P_VE$. The new contribution here is the projected-target mollifier and its conditional upper bound, rather than a new general contour method.

The following corrections and clarifications would improve the final version without changing that assessment:

1. **Separate strip width from growth exponents.** Lemma 2(b), as used later, compresses two different small parameters. On $\sigma=1/2-\varepsilon$, the functional equation and Lindelöf give $\zeta(s)\ll |t|^{\varepsilon+\eta}$ for every $\eta>0$, rather than the asserted bound with exactly exponent $\varepsilon$. Use, for example, $2\varepsilon$ on the strip and track the resulting exponents in Lemma 5 and §4. The available strict inequalities allow this adjustment. Also treat $Z^+=\varnothing$ separately wherever $\beta_0=\min\operatorname{Re}\rho'$ is used.

2. **Expand the far-left bookkeeping in Lemma 4.** State the choice of left contours and make the dependence on their real part explicit before sending them to the left. Polynomial growth in each fixed vertical strip alone is not a uniform estimate for that limit. Explain convergence of the infinite residue sums using the representer coefficient expansion and functional-equation estimates. In particular, at a reflected off-line zero $1-\bar\rho'$, a pole of $D$ can coincide with a zero of $\zeta$, producing higher-order poles of $D/\zeta$ and powers of $\log N$. Explicitly account for their absorption into $N^{\eta}$. This would make the extension beyond [2] considerably easier to verify.

3. **Tighten attribution and presentation.** Cite [3] directly and distinguish its target from yours. Credit the continuation mechanism and constraint correction already present in [1]. Replace the manuscript's mixed citation labels with consistent numbered references, and supply a reference for the Grenander–Rosenblatt statement. Describe extension to other Dirichlet series strictly as an unverified prospect.

4. **Keep the numerical evidence in its proper scope.** Inspection of the supplied scripts supports their description as coefficient and toy-model checks. An independent exact-arithmetic calculation for $Q=(1-z)^m$, using the proposed weight, gave $N\|1-QW_N\|^2=1$, $4.0074875$, and $9.061324633$ at $N=400$ for $m=1,2,3$, respectively, consistent with the proposed limits $m^2$. This is finite-size corroboration of the toy model, not experimental verification of Theorem G. The supplied experiments were not reproduced in full. List `mpmath` alongside the other dependencies and retain representative outputs.
