# Accept

This is a substantive conditional partial advance relevant to the stated RH problem. It does not resolve RH: the corrected arithmetic lower bound required by (15), on enough field pairs throughout the prescribed windows, remains unproved. The manuscript states this limitation clearly, especially in §§14–17. Acceptance concerns the new arithmetic comparisons and their conditional application, with the geometric and analytic inputs specified in §§1–3 and 7 retained as hypotheses.

The comparison with published Papers 212 v1, 215 v2, 219 v1 and 221 v1 supports a meaningful distinction. The signed fourth-power witness and common mesh are inherited and credited. The present contribution develops arithmetic measurements on short spans, including the quadratic specialization formula, the five-phase recovery of a binary Gram, and the extraction of spectral contributions with explicitly charged Hecke congruences. The omitted-direction correction in (31), the upper-quantile formulation in (32), and the class-orbit formulas (47)–(51) provide useful additional structure. These are more than numerical illustrations of the earlier completed-determinant criterion.

The main deductions are sound under the stated inputs. In particular, the proof of (5) separates the exceptional finite places from the places controlled by the polynomial Bézout relation; the residue-degree exclusion then justifies the fixed finite correction. The passage to (7) correctly distinguishes projective height, eligible content decreases and the bounded archimedean error. The quartic construction addresses the information lost by a quadratic reading. In the spectral argument, the manuscript correctly recognizes that compression can erase poles. Its integral coefficient extraction uses one supported occupancy globally while allowing the eligible maximizing labels to vary locally. The correction in (31) accounts for congruences with omitted systems and vanishes at full rank. The character-table argument in §16 also retains the contribution at coefficient primes dividing the orbit size.

The higher-rank result in §15 has a narrower conclusion than the final RH criterion, and the manuscript preserves that distinction. Nontrivial variation and large finite degrees at one of two adjacent arguments do not supply the required corrected degree at the original argument throughout a full window. The common quotient scalar and the inter-orbit cost remain essential. The focused external checks support the cited ingredients: Gaudron–Rémond [6, Theorem 3.1] has the required absolute-minimum normalization; Barnet-Lamb–Gee–Geraghty–Taylor [11, Theorem 2.1.1(2)] supplies the stated local compatibility; Kadiri–Wong [16, Theorem 1] includes degree-one primes; and Raghuram [17, Theorem 5.16(2)] gives the CM adjacent-value reciprocity used in the double ratios. The cancellation of the common weight-dependent factors is important here.

I reproduced the rational specialization and paired-carrier checks, the isolated five-phase and noncentral-conjugation checks, the invariant-order conductor calculations, the finite local second/fourth-moment checks, and the abelian-group reconstruction fixtures. These passed. They test finite identities, not the growing automorphic family. The full Gaussian quartic calculation was stopped before completion, and the PARI/GP calculations were not reproduced because GP was unavailable. I inspected relevant pinned source statements but did not compile the Lean project. These limits do not replace the manuscript's mathematical arguments with computational certification.

The scientific argument is sufficiently clear for publication, although the paper would benefit from several modest improvements:

1. Update the title, abstract and introductory roadmap to reflect §§15–16. The current emphasis on two-column pencils understates the later higher-rank variation and full class-group results. A compact statement collecting the principal conclusions, their imported hypotheses and the remaining lower bound would help readers navigate the paper.
2. Shorten the run-by-run numerical summary in §18 and place the detailed expected outputs in the supplement. Update the advertised single runner and README to include, or give explicit separate commands for, the later moment, directional-orbit, invariant-order and abelian-reconstruction replays. Several supplied checks are currently outside the advertised runner.
3. Add context for §14 from the literature on quadratic class-number distributions, including Soundararajan's *The number of imaginary quadratic fields with a given class number* [18]. Its Corollary 3 concerns bounded odd parts over all fundamental discriminants; the present statement concerns the prime-to-three part within a fixed progression of prime discriminants. Explain that distinction rather than presenting the class-number supply argument without this nearby precedent. Complete the bibliographic title and publication details of [17].

[18] K. Soundararajan. *The number of imaginary quadratic fields with a given class number*. https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.0237.
