# Two-column source pencils on signed fourth-power windows

*Binary source pencils, exterior minors, and mixed determinants*

## 1. Introduction

The shorter period construction bounds a signed sum of prime powers by comparing volumes of automorphic forms and their twists. The comparison in the ordinary-zeta application [1] uses a completed target basis. Paper 215 v2 supplies a bounded integral vacuum and an analytic test at fourth powers [2]. Paper 219 studies the completed determinant and finite menus of its twist ratios [3]. Paper 221 v1 fixes common level primes and a divisor mesh across quadratic fields and gives a common signed witness on the original fourth-power windows [4, §§6, 8–10]. We retain those windows, their rank assignment and their short-column estimates from the original construction [5].

We obtain a sufficient arithmetic criterion using two algebraic linear combinations of short source columns at each node. For each member of a finite menu of genuine twists, the input consists of the trace and the two off-diagonal entries of its Gram on these two combinations. They give a quadratic polynomial. Remove the common polynomial gcd across the menu. The limiting normalized degree of the pencil is the absolute projective height of the three remaining reference coefficients, plus a nonnegative sum of eligible decreases in their finite-place orders, up to an archimedean error between $-\log 2$ and $\log 3$. Before taking the limit, a cyclotomic resultant gives the degree exactly at every sufficiently large prime torsion order. The primes contributing to the ideal can vary with this order while the menu of characters stays fixed. A quartic pencil chosen from five phases recovers the height and eligible content drops of all four entries of the binary Gram. Whenever a genuine twist restricts nonproportionally on the chosen plane, its limiting degree differs from this binary arithmetic input by an error in $[-\log 30,\log 15]$. A direct height inequality supplies the same binary input to the node comparison for every menu, including proportional twists.

The reduction has two parts. First, an index estimate for a rectangular family of short columns yields a paired adelic lattice for the entire menu. Absolute successive minima give two low-height coefficient vectors. Second, passage to the pencil degree preserves the signed node inequality and hence the signed secant test at the original fourth powers. One may use the image of the identity translate, so the two columns can be formed directly in the ramified source. The proof imports the short-image, index and injectivity results of [5] and the common mesh and signed witness of [4]. The independent estimates of the arithmetic inputs on sufficiently many field pairs remain open.

Two further tests use the original short tuple. The exterior test uses all minors of a fixed order, with a half-rank estimate from Smith layers. The mixed determinant combines the actual twist Grams in separate variables, retaining the positive denominator orders of relative eigenvalues. For an unscaled completion its bound includes both auxiliary carrier costs. Sections 9–10 give these estimates on the same signed windows.

We next relate the spectrum of the raw full Gram to the short space itself. Sections 11–12 identify the order generated by the integral final Hecke correspondences with the split input Hecke order at each coefficient place. Its projection indices control the cost of extracting a specified exterior coefficient, including a correction involving the deficient systems and their congruences with the fully occupied systems. An upper quantile of the corrected global degrees already suffices on an inverse-subpolynomial fraction of the good space. Section 13 identifies the algebraic contrast of the empty standard critical values along an unramified class-character orbit and bounds the common quotient-period cost. Section 14 proves that free prime-to-three class-character orbits exist on a relative density-one set of the original prime discriminants. These results give a further sufficient condition for (15) using only empty reference values and congruence costs. The finite rank-one CM example establishes actual finite-place spread. The lower bound for corrected degrees on the growing original ranks and field pairs remains open.

We retain the statement-version-2 ordinary RH question and the distinction in [1–4] between an implication from the geometric and analytic inputs of the shorter construction and an independent arithmetic proof. Throughout, we assume the short-image, simultaneous adelic index, injectivity, norm and exchange identities of [5], including their conjugate bounds and admissibility inequalities, and the common-level and signed analytic witness results of [4]. The following arguments are ordinary mathematical deductions from these inputs and the cited classical theorems. No new geometric kernel construction or formal verification is required for the deductions.

## 2. Nodes and rectangular Gram determinants

Fix $q=18480$. The eligible fields are indexed by primes $d\equiv9239\bmod q$, with $K_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. In the block $X/4<x\le X$, where $m=\sqrt X$ is a power of two, let $R$ be the least power of three at least $x^5$. Write $i=(X,R)$ and $k=R+1-m$. The common divisor mesh of [4, §8] includes the endpoints of every occupied rank interval and has maximal within-rank gap $G_i=O(X^{1/2+o(1)})$. The level primes, heavy set of size $t$, and heavy weight $b$ stay fixed across a block, with $a=xb$ and $L=\log b$. In field $d$ the second cutoff is $x_{1,d}=B_dx$. Let $f$ count the infinite places of the construction's CM field, let $T_0$ be the size of the available place class in [4, §2], and let $V$ be the block scale there. We use $f/t\ll_q V^6$ and, when $D_N=o(\log N)$, $V^6L^4=N^{o(1)}$ uniformly on the window.

Use the field and rank's exact main amount $M_{d,i}=mk(J-\Sigma)$ of [4, §8], where $J$ is the factorial and normalization sum and $\Sigma$ is the prime sum at the fixed target weights. Its sums at orders $y'\le b$ have support at prime powers $\nu<B_d$ since each target weight is at least $b/B_d$. This makes $M_{d,i}$ common to the nodes of $i$. Let $T_x$ be the signed long-order remainder of [4, §6] in field $d$ and set
$$
\mathcal L_x=f(xb)L^4,\qquad B_x=M_{d,i}-mT_x+\mathcal L_x.
$$
The original short-image estimates give independent normalized images $y_1,\ldots,y_h$ in the good target. For the empty twist their lifted columns have squared norm at most $\exp(B_x)$ under every complex automorphism in rational final coordinates. Their index is at most $\Delta_x$ in the following sense. There are $e_\ell\ge0$ with $\sum_\ell e_\ell\log\ell\le\Delta_x$. At each field isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ there is an integral invertible $h\times h$ carrying matrix for the tuple, of determinant valuation at most $e_\ell$ with $v_\ell(\ell)=1$. It carries the tuple into the integral target module simultaneously for every eligible label. The release gives paired integrality for the carried families at $\iota$ and $\iota c$, where $c$ is complex conjugation. These statements are the shortness, family-index and integral-pairing inputs of [4, §8] and [5, source locations in References].

Use a finite menu $\mathcal T$ of genuine $m$-fold twists including $\emptyset$, the trivial global character with empty declared modulus. Write $Q_\xi$ for the norm of the declared twist modulus and use $Q_\emptyset=1$. A label is eligible over $\ell$ when $\ell\nmid Q_\xi$. Keep the same tuple for every label. If $U_\xi$ is its rectangular matrix after the lower chain $\Theta_\xi$, define $K_\xi=U_\xi^*P_\xi^{\rm cls}U_\xi$ and $g_\xi^s=\det K_\xi$. The metric $P_\xi^{\rm cls}$ is the direct sum of the Weyl-fibre metrics over the final classes at the level of $\xi$, with each block weighted by reciprocal stabilizer order. The final stabilizers of the released construction are trivial. The common finite-order character on the columns cancels in each pairing. Injectivity of $\Theta_\xi$ on the good target gives $K_\xi>0$ at the original embedding. The determinant ratio $g_\emptyset^s/g_\xi^s$ is invariant under changes of basis of this fixed span.

In column convention let $U,V$ be the transported carrying matrices at $\iota,\iota c$. The paired integrality input says
$$
 V^{\mathsf T}\iota(K_\xi)U\in M_h(\mathcal O_{\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell})
 \qquad(\ell\nmid Q_\xi).
$$
Taking determinants gives $v_\ell(\iota(g_\xi^s))\ge-2e_\ell$. Multiplying by the two integral adjugates gives $v_\ell(\iota(K_{\xi,jk}))\ge-2e_\ell$ entrywise. For each label use any one eligible prime and all isomorphisms above it. If an entry were transcendental over $\mathbb Q$, it could be taken to a transcendental element of arbitrarily negative $\ell$-adic valuation by extending an isomorphism of transcendence bases to their algebraic closures. The valuation bound therefore proves entrywise algebraicity. The same argument includes the empty twist.

In a common coefficient field $F$, use $v_w(\ell)=1$ and $|x|_w=\ell^{-v_w(x)}$ at $w\mid\ell$. Write $\omega_w=[F_w:\mathbb Q_\ell]/[F:\mathbb Q]$, $e(w/\ell)$ for ramification index and $\operatorname{ord}_w=e(w/\ell)v_w$. Put
$$
 k_w^s=\max_{\ell\nmid Q_\xi}\operatorname{ord}_w(g_\emptyset^s/g_\xi^s),
 \qquad I^s=\prod_w\mathfrak p_w^{k_w^s},\qquad
 \deg I^s=\frac{\log\operatorname{Nm} I^s}{[F:\mathbb Q]}
 =\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,\frac{k_w^s}{e(w/\ell)} .
$$
Sums indexed by $w\mid\ell$ in degree and height formulas range over all rational primes and their finite places. The empty label gives a zero score, and finiteness gives finite support. Relative ramification multiplies uniformizer orders and the weights above each old place sum to its weight. Thus the degree is invariant on further extension.

At $w$ a maximizing label gives $\log|g_\emptyset^s|_w\le (2e_\ell-k_w^s/e(w/\ell))\log\ell$. At infinity extend an embedding $\sigma$ to the complex coordinates. Since the reference class metric is rational, $\sigma(g_\emptyset^s)$ is the mixed determinant pairing the columns of $c\sigma c(U_\emptyset)$ and $\sigma(U_\emptyset)$ in that metric. Exterior Cauchy–Schwarz and Hadamard give $\log|\sigma(g_\emptyset^s)|\le hB_x$. Counting all complex embeddings with weight $1/[F:\mathbb Q]$, the product formula gives
$$
 h mT_x\le hM_{d,i}-\deg I^s+h\mathcal L_x+2\Delta_x. \tag{1}
$$
All columns fit in the final coordinate space regardless of whether they span it. The completed determinant of [3, 4] includes a complementary Schur determinant and auxiliary clearing. Even in a diagonal integral model, take the two lifts to have diagonals $(p^j,1,\ldots,1,1)$ and $(1,1,\ldots,1,p^j)$ at the reference and an eligible twist, respectively. With the first $h$ of $h+1$ columns selected, the short degree is $2j\log p$ and the full degree is zero. This is why (1) is used as a separate node comparison.

## 3. Identity translate and the final class metric

One admissible tuple uses $h=D_R-\lfloor4D_R/b^2\rfloor$ independent columns among the short images of the chosen translates, with $D_R$ the good target dimension. Its index satisfies
$$
 \Delta_x\le 10D_R\log(1+2b^2)f(xb)L^3.
$$
There is also a construction with one translate. Let $S$ be the ramified source of dimension $a_0$, let $D_X$ be the full source dimension, and write $\mathfrak p$ for the normalized Poincaré map at the identity, including the good projection $e_0$. It differs from the projected scaled map on $S$ by a nonzero scalar in isometric coordinates. For any subspace $A$ of the full source, the projected scaled map satisfies
$$
 \|(e_0P_{\rm sc})\operatorname{pr}_A\|_{\rm HS}^2
 \ge\dim A-5D_X/b^4 .
$$
Apply this inequality to the kernel of $\mathfrak p|_S$ in the full source coordinates. Since $D_X\le 2a_0$, the dimension $r$ of $Y=\mathfrak p(S)$ satisfies
$$
 r\ge a_0-\lfloor5D_X/b^4\rfloor\ge (1-10/b^4)a_0>0
$$
in the mesh range. The short integral ramified source forms span $S$, so a basis of $Y$ can be selected from their images. The one-translate index is $\Delta_x\le2a_0 f(xb)L^3$ for this tuple. In both constructions $\Delta_x/h=O(\mathcal L_x)$, with $h=r$ in the identity case. The rank comparison for the identity is relative to $S$.

For a fixed spanning list $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_j$ of $S$, let $H_\xi$ be the final Gram of the list $\Theta_\xi\mathfrak p\phi_\nu$. Writing $C$ for its source-image coordinates in a basis of $Y$ gives $H_\xi=C^*K_\xi C$. The coefficient of $T^{j-r}$ in $\det(TI_j+H_\xi)$ is $\det K_\xi\sum_{|J|=r}|\det C_J|^2$ by the principal-minor expansion. The factor multiplying $\det K_\xi$ is positive and common to the menu. The ratio of these coefficients gives $g_\emptyset^s/g_\xi^s$. At a finite place use the same characteristic-zero exponent $j-r$, including when the reduced rank changes or this coefficient vanishes modulo the place.

For a two-column calculation take the actual class sum of the lifted values. Equivalently, the upper seesaw of [5] writes the value at the final representative $g$ as
$$
 c_B |\bar G|\sum_{\tau\in\bar G}e_0(\tau)\,
 (B^{\rm up}_\xi\phi)\bigl(g\,\operatorname{firstBlockLift}_\xi(\tau)\bigr),
$$
where $B^{\rm up}_\xi$ uses the common seesaw datum for this twist. Here $\bar G$ is the finite group defining the projection and $c_B$ is the gain scalar. The reciprocal seesaw scalar is included in $\mathfrak p$. Taking the class sum after this projection retains its cross terms. The whole-chain adjoint formula gives another evaluation of a pairing on the good target, $c_\xi c_{Q,\xi}^{m}\langle y,\mathcal R_\xi y'\rangle$, with the full product $\mathcal R_\xi$ of the Rallis operators and the same twist on both columns [5].

Conversion from a normalized Petersson calculation on these same rows multiplies each matrix entry by the Weyl dimension times $[K_{0,m}:K_\xi^{\rm lev}]$, with $K_{0,m}$ the final standard level and $K_\xi^{\rm lev}$ the actual final level. If the row computation also omits the lower-stage source indices, the correction to each entry includes their squared product for the same point kernels and measures. Level and flag indices can be nonunits at eligible primes. For instance the index of a line parabolic in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_5)$ is $6$, divisible by $3$. Pure powers of $Q_\xi$ are units at eligible primes, including rational powers after a field extension. All node size and index estimates use the actual reference normalization. A trivial new component at enlarged declared level still uses that level's row and can differ from the empty reference. The theorem below uses genuine labels with the simultaneous index and injectivity above.

## 4. One adelic bundle for a finite twist menu

The paired clearing can be descended even if the original carrying matrices have transcendental complex entries. Let $S_{\rm bad}$ consist of rational primes where some eligible $K_\xi$ has a nonintegral entry at some coefficient place. Take a normal coefficient field $K$ containing the entries and an imaginary quadratic field splitting every prime of $S_{\rm bad}$. Such a quadratic field is obtained from the squarefree part of a negative integer congruent to $1$ modulo $8\prod_{\ell\in S_{\rm bad},\,\ell\ne2}\ell$. Complex conjugation then exchanges distinct places $w,cw$ above these primes.

For one $w$ from each conjugate pair over $S_{\rm bad}$, fix a source isomorphism inducing $w$ and take the associated carriers $U,V$. Approximate their entries at this isomorphism by images of global algebraic numbers to valuation $>2e_\ell$. For instance, density follows by approximating the coefficients of a separable local minimal polynomial by rationals and applying Hensel's lemma near its simple root. The approximations are integral, their determinant orders agree with the original orders, and the perturbations to $V^{\mathsf T}K_\xi U$ are integral by the entrywise bound from §2. The same precision works for the finite menu. Write $U_w,V_w$ for the approximating matrices with algebraic entries and enlarge $K$ to a finite normal field $F$ containing them all.

Let $W_0$ be the place of $F$ above $w$ induced by the isomorphism used for the approximation. For every $W\mid w$ choose $\tau_W\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)$ with $\tau_W W_0=W$. Put $U_W=\tau_W(U_w)$ and $V_W=\tau_W(V_w)$. The identity $v_W(\tau_W z)=v_{W_0}(z)$ preserves the integral entries and determinant orders of the carriers. Since $\tau_W$ fixes $K_\xi$, it also preserves the valuation bounds for $V_W^{\mathsf T}K_\xi U_W$. Use the ball $U_W\mathcal O_{F_W}^h$ at $W$ and the ball $c(V_W)\mathcal O_{F_{cW}}^h$ at $cW$. Complex conjugation exchanges the places, and $c(K_\xi)=K_\xi^{\mathsf T}$ gives
$$
 c(U_W)^{\mathsf T}K_\xi c(V_W)
 =c\bigl(V_W^{\mathsf T}K_\xi U_W\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
$$
This proves paired integrality at $cW$ for the same eligible labels, including when $c$ is noncentral. Outside $S_{\rm bad}$ choose standard balls. If $D_W$ generates the chosen ball, its finite norm is $\|s\|_W=\|D_W^{-1}s\|_{\sup,W}$. Every ball is contained in the standard integral ball. Its determinant cost satisfies
$$
 \delta=\sum_{W\mid\ell}\omega_W v_W(\det D_W)\log\ell
 \le\sum_\ell e_\ell\log\ell\le\Delta_x .
$$
Each place contributes its own determinant order. In particular $\sum_{W\mid\ell}\omega_W=1$ gives this cost even after splitting the conjugate pairs or enlarging the normal field.

Give the bundle $E$ Euclidean norms at infinity. Its normalized degree is $-\delta$ and its logarithmic height $\mathrm h_E$ dominates ordinary projective height. Over any finite extension of $F$ these norms are pulled back and each old weight is split among the places above it. We can use normal extensions so that $c$ permutes the places.

For an algebraic coefficient vector $s\ne0$ put $g_\xi(s)=c(s)^{\mathsf T}K_\xi s$, which is positive at the original embedding. Use the same eligible maxima as in §2 for the ratios $g_\emptyset(s)/g_\xi(s)$ and denote the ideal by $I(s)$. At a finite place, paired integrality gives $|g_\xi(s)|_W\le\|s\|_W\|s\|_{cW}$. If $k_W(s)$ is the maximal uniformizer order of the eligible ratios, then
$$
 \log|g_\emptyset(s)|_W
 +\frac{k_W(s)}{e(W/\ell)}\log\ell
 \le\log\|s\|_W+\log\|s\|_{cW}.
$$

At infinity the two matrices used for Cauchy–Schwarz are the reference columns at $\sigma$ and $c\sigma c$. Using the squared column bound $\exp(B_x)$ and Cauchy–Schwarz on their coefficients gives
$$
 |\sigma(g_\emptyset(s))|\le h e^{B_x}\|\sigma(s)\|_2\|(\sigma c)(s)\|_2 .
$$
The finite-place sum counts conjugate places with equal weights, and the archimedean sum counts all embeddings, permuted by $\sigma\mapsto\sigma c$. The product formula therefore gives
$$
 \deg I(s)\le M_{d,i}-mT_x+\mathcal L_x+\log h+2\mathrm h_E(s). \tag{2}
$$

For $h\ge2$, let $Z_j(E)$ be the absolute Zhang minima in multiplicative height and $\mathcal H_h=\sum_{j=1}^h 1/j$. Gaudron–Rémond [6, Theorem 3.1] gives $\sum_j\log Z_j(E)\le\delta+\frac h2(\mathcal H_h-1)$. Since $\log Z_1(E)\ge0$ and the minima are increasing, $(h-1)\log Z_2(E)$ is at most that same right side. For any $\epsilon>0$ put
$$
 J_E=\frac{\delta+\frac h2(\mathcal H_h-1)}{h-1}+\epsilon.
$$
By the definition of $Z_2$ the Zariski closure of vectors with $\mathrm h_E\le J_E$ has dimension at least two, so it contains such vectors $u,v$ that are linearly independent. Scale each so one coordinate is $1$. The height is unaffected and every local norm of each scaled vector is at least one. For any root of unity $\zeta$, the local norms of $u+\zeta v$ are bounded by the maximum at a finite place and twice the maximum at infinity. The maximum is at most the product of the two norms. Thus
$$
 \mathrm h_E(u+\zeta v)\le 2J_E+\log 2 . \tag{3}
$$
This uses every place and applies uniformly to all torsion orders. The matrices, menu and two vectors are fixed while the torsion order varies.

The mesh absorbs the cost in (3). In the identity case the full source dimension is at most the class count times the Weyl dimension on rank $k$, whose local factors are $\binom{a_\tau+k-1}{k-1}$ with $a_\tau=a$ or $0$. The class count at the source level is at most $\exp(x^{30})$ by [5]. Hence $\log h\le x^{30}+f(k-1)\log(1+a)$. For several translates, $h\le D_R$, the good dimension on the definite rank-$R$ row. The same class count applies at its level. The largest difference in the decreasing Weyl parameter is at most $b+2mR^8+k$, so the Weyl dimension formula gives $\log D_R\le x^{30}+f\binom{R}{2}\log(b+2mR^8+k)$. With $R<3x^5$, $m<2\sqrt{x}$ and $b\ge x^{10^{12}}$ these bounds are $o(\mathcal L_x)$ uniformly. Also $\mathcal H_h-1\le\log h$ and $\Delta_x/h=O(\mathcal L_x)$. Taking a fixed bounded $\epsilon>0$ gives $\log h+4J_E+2\log 2=O(\mathcal L_x)$. For $h=1$, the degree in (1) is the ordinary line degree.

## 5. The quadratic and its exact specialization

Enlarge $F$ to include $u,v$ and their conjugates. Write $\langle s,t\rangle_\xi=c(s)^{\mathsf T} K_\xi t$ and define
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 P_\xi(T)
 &=\langle v,u\rangle_\xi+
   \bigl(\langle u,u\rangle_\xi+\langle v,v\rangle_\xi\bigr)T+
   \langle u,v\rangle_\xi T^2,\\
 g(T)&=\gcd_{\xi\in\mathcal T}P_\xi(T)\quad\text{(monic)},&
 B_\xi(T)&=P_\xi(T)/g(T).
 \end{aligned} \tag{4}
$$
For any root of unity $\zeta$, $c(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1}$ and $P_\xi(\zeta)=\zeta g_\xi(u+\zeta v)$. The positive definite forms make this value nonzero. Cancellation of $g(\zeta)$ preserves all the eligible ratio orders. The gcd is taken over the entire menu before imposing the placewise eligibility test.

At the original embedding each quadratic has the form $C T^2+A T+\overline C$, with $A>2|C|$ by positive definiteness and independence. If $C=0$ the polynomial is $AT$. Otherwise it has two distinct nonzero roots exchanged by $\alpha\mapsto 1/\overline{\alpha}$, with neither on the unit circle. Thus two nonproportional pencil polynomials have gcd $1$. Indeed a shared root of quadratics forces its distinct partner to be shared, and a polynomial of degree one here has its sole root at zero. Consequently the menu gcd has positive degree exactly when all pencil polynomials are proportional, in which case all $B_\xi$ are constant. Their degree can still include a finite content contribution. The projective reference height for a constant polynomial is zero.

For a nonzero $B\in F[T]$ write $\mu_w(B)=\min_j v_w([T^j]B)$, counting zero coefficients with order $+\infty$. Put
$$
 \nu_w=\min_{\xi\in\mathcal T,\,\ell\nmid Q_\xi}\mu_w(B_\xi),\qquad
 C_{\rm fin}=\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,\nu_w .
$$
The sum can have either sign. Its terms vanish at all but finitely many places. Use the normalized logarithmic Mahler measure
$$
 H_\infty(B)=
 \frac1{[F:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
    \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|B^\sigma(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta .
$$

**Exact specialization.** For all sufficiently large prime orders $p$ and every primitive $p$-th root $\zeta_p$,
$$
 \deg I(u+\zeta_p v)=
 \frac{\log\left|\mathrm N_{F/\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_p,B_\emptyset)\right|}
      {[F:\mathbb Q](p-1)}
 -C_{\rm fin}. \tag{5}
$$
The field norm in (5) is allowed to be rational. The computation uses an exact resultant in the fixed coefficient field and requires no factorization of the primes introduced by the torsion specialization.

To prove (5), choose a Bézout relation $\sum_\xi A_\xi B_\xi=1$ over $F[T]$. Take a finite set $S_0$ of rational primes containing all conductor primes and denominators of the $A_\xi,B_\xi$. Outside $S_0$ every label is eligible and integrality of the relation gives minimum order zero both for the coefficient tuples and for their evaluations at a root of unity. For $w$ above $S_0$ let $q_w=\#k_w$ be the residue field size and $b_*=\max_\xi\deg B_\xi\le2$. Exclude $p\in S_0$, all primes ramified in $F$, and prime divisors of $q_w^j-1$ for $1\le j\le b_*$ at every $w$ above $S_0$. This finite exclusion is explicit. At any place of $F(\zeta_p)$ over such a $w$, reduction of $\zeta_p$ has order $p$, and its degree over $k_w$ is $\operatorname{ord}_p(q_w)>b_*$. If $b_*=0$, only the positive degree of the extension element's minimal polynomial is needed. Divide each polynomial by a coefficient of minimum valuation. Its nonzero reduction of degree at most $b_*$ cannot vanish at the reduced torsion point. Thus the evaluation order of each $B_\xi$ above these $w$ equals $\mu_w(B_\xi)$. In particular the minimum over the labels allowed there is $\nu_w$. The order of the reference evaluation itself need not vanish outside $S_0$.

The degree at every extending place is the weighted reference evaluation order less the weighted eligible minimum. By the product formula its reference part equals the average archimedean log absolute value. Since $p$ is unramified in $F$ and totally ramified in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$, these two fields intersect in $\mathbb Q$. Each embedding of $F$ pairs with every primitive root, so the archimedean average is precisely the norm of the resultant in (5). At the finite places the weights above an old place sum to its weight. This yields the stated correction. Any prescribed finite set of coefficient primes can be included in $S_0$. Their eventual contributions to (5) are exactly $\omega_w\log\ell\,(\mu_w(B_\emptyset)-\nu_w)$, which can be subtracted to obtain the complementary degree.

**Mahler limit.** The limit of (5) as $p\to\infty$ through the allowed primes is
$$
 D=H_\infty(B_\emptyset)-C_{\rm fin}. \tag{6}
$$
For each conjugate polynomial with leading coefficient $b_0$, factor it using its complex roots $\alpha$, with multiplicity. Jensen's formula gives logarithmic Mahler measure $\log|b_0|+\sum_\alpha\log^+|\alpha|$. The corresponding resultant average has contribution $\log|\Phi_p(\alpha)|/(p-1)$ per root. For $\alpha\ne1$ this is $\log|(\alpha^p-1)/(\alpha-1)|/(p-1)$, converging to $\log^+|\alpha|$ off the unit circle. For $\alpha=1$ it is $\log p/(p-1)$, and at other roots of unity it tends to zero since the numerator takes finitely many nonzero values for large primes. For an algebraic nontorsion root on the unit circle, use fixed logarithms of $\alpha,-1$ and subtract $2j\log(-1)$ from $p\log\alpha$ with $j=O(p)$ chosen to make its imaginary part at most $\pi$ in absolute value. The form is nonzero. Baker–Wüstholz [7] gives a lower bound of the form $p^{-C_\alpha}$ up to a fixed factor, and comparison of $|e^{it}-1|$ with $|t|$ on this interval gives the same type of bound for $|\alpha^p-1|$. Hence its normalized log also tends to zero. This argument covers every coefficient embedding. The limit is taken at a fixed node and pencil, so no uniformity in the number fields, root heights or conductor menu is needed.

**Projective formula.** Let $\mathbf b$ be the three coefficients of $B_\emptyset$, padded with zeros. In the same absolute normalization as (5),
$$
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 D=h_{\rm proj}(\mathbf b)
    +\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,\bigl(\mu_w(B_\emptyset)-\nu_w\bigr)
    +\varepsilon_\infty,\qquad
 -\log 2\le\varepsilon_\infty\le\log 3 .} \tag{7}
$$
Indeed the finite part of $h_{\rm proj}(\mathbf b)$ is $-\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,\mu_w(B_\emptyset)$. At a complex embedding each coefficient is bounded by twice the multiplicative Mahler measure by expanding the root product in degree at most two. On the unit circle the polynomial is bounded by three times the largest coefficient. Consequently the difference of the mean log Mahler measure and the mean log coefficient maximum lies in the claimed interval. Common rescaling preserves (6) by the product formula and preserves both terms in (7). Every finite difference is nonnegative because the empty label is eligible everywhere. Also $D\ge0$ by (5) and (6). One can certify a lower bound for $D$ using the projective term and any subset of the eligible finite drops, with error at most $\log 2$ in the lower direction.

For the low-height pair in §4, apply (2) and (3) to $u+\zeta_p v$, then take the limit (6). This gives at the original node
$$
 mT_x\le M_{d,i}-D+\mathcal L_x+\log h+4J_E+2\log 2. \tag{8}
$$
In particular the fixed-node theorem requires three scalar coefficient readings per label for its arithmetic input, even when the original coefficient space has large dimension.

## 6. Recovering the binary Gram with five phases

Let $H_\xi=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A_\xi&B_\xi\\ C_\xi&D_\xi\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ be the Gram of the same ordered pair $(u,v)$ under the actual pairing for $\xi$. Adjoin the fifth roots of unity to $F$. For $\eta\in\mu_5$ put $w_+(T)=T+\eta T^2$, $w_-(T)=T^{-1}+\eta^{-1}T^{-2}$ and $s_\eta(T)=u+w_+(T)v$. On the original unit circle $w_-(T)=\overline{w_+(T)}$, and $(1,w_-)H_\xi(1,w_+)^{\mathsf T}$ is strictly positive. After multiplying by $T^2$, the resulting polynomial $P_{\xi,\eta}$ has its coefficients in increasing order as follows.
$$
 (p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)
 =(\eta^{-1}C_\xi,\ C_\xi+\eta^{-1}D_\xi,\ A_\xi+2D_\xi,\
     B_\xi+\eta D_\xi,\ \eta B_\xi). \tag{9}
$$
In particular $C_\xi=\eta p_0$, $B_\xi=\eta^{-1}p_4$, $D_\xi=\eta(p_1-C_\xi)$ and $A_\xi=p_2-2D_\xi$. The map and this inverse use coefficients integral at every finite place, including above $2$ and $5$. If $\mu_w(H)$ denotes the minimum of its four entry valuations, then $\mu_w(P_{\xi,\eta})=\mu_w(H_\xi)$. In the archimedean maximum norm the forward and inverse bounds are $\|\mathbf p\|_\infty\le3\|H_\xi\|_\infty$ and $\|H_\xi\|_\infty\le5\|\mathbf p\|_\infty$. These bounds hold at every coefficient embedding.

Suppose one genuine menu member has $H_\xi$ nonproportional to $H_\emptyset$. A common zero in $T\ne0$ determines a right line $r=(1,w_+)^{\mathsf T}$ and a left line $\lambda=(1,w_-)$. Both $H_\emptyset r$ and $H_\xi r$ lie in the kernel of $\lambda$. Since $H_\emptyset$ is invertible, the possible right lines are eigenlines of $H_\emptyset^{-1}H_\xi$, a nonscalar operator with at most two eigenlines. For each, the left line is uniquely determined by $H_\emptyset r$. Only pairs with both displayed affine coordinates finite can arise. Write $U=\eta T$. Then
$$
 w_+=\eta^{-1}(U+U^2),\qquad
 w_-=\eta(U^{-1}+U^{-2}),\qquad
 w_-w_+=2+U+U^{-1}.
$$
The pair $(0,0)$ occurs at $U=-1$ and evaluates to $A_\emptyset>0$. Each other common pair determines at most two values of $U$ from the product equation, and each fixes $\eta=(U+U^2)/w_+$. Hence at most four phases are forbidden. Test all five fifth roots over $F$ and choose one for which the two polynomials have monomial gcd. Then the gcd of the entire menu is a power of $T$. In fact this power is $T$ when every $H_\xi$ is diagonal, and $1$ otherwise, by (9) and $D_\xi>0$. For the chosen $\eta$ let $S_{\xi,\eta}$ be the polynomials with that common power removed. The gcd identity holds under every embedding. Positivity at the original embedding ensures nonzero torsion evaluations, and the conjugate pairings use the transported involution $\sigma c\sigma^{-1}$.

Let $\nu_w^H=\min_{\xi:\,\ell\nmid Q_\xi}\mu_w(H_\xi)$. Apply the proof of (5) with these polynomials and the degree bound four. In the finite set of conductor and denominator primes, exclude prime torsion orders dividing $(\#k_w)^j-1$ for $1\le j\le4$, as well as the primes in that set and those ramifying in $F$. The evaluated minimum is $\nu_w^H$ at places above the finite set by the residue-degree test and equality of contents. Away from the set it is zero by the Bézout identity. For every remaining prime $p$ this gives
$$
 \deg I(s_\eta(\zeta_p))=
 \frac{\log\left|\mathrm N_{F/\mathbb Q}
               \operatorname{Res}(\Phi_p,S_{\emptyset,\eta})\right|}
      {[F:\mathbb Q](p-1)}
 -\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,\nu_w^H. \tag{10}
$$
Cyclotomic disjointness and the Baker–Wüstholz argument of §5 apply with degree four. Thus the limit $D_\eta$ replaces the first term by $H_\infty(S_{\emptyset,\eta})$. A common monomial shift preserves both coefficient content and logarithmic Mahler measure.

Define the binary arithmetic input and its relation to the limit by
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal J(u,v)
 &=h_{\rm proj}([A_\emptyset:B_\emptyset:C_\emptyset:D_\emptyset])
   +\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,
      \bigl(\mu_w(H_\emptyset)-\nu_w^H\bigr),\\
 D_\eta&=\mathcal J(u,v)+\varepsilon_\eta,\qquad
     -\log 30\le\varepsilon_\eta\le\log 15 .
 \end{aligned} \tag{11}
$$
Indeed, each coefficient of a polynomial of degree at most four has modulus at most six times its multiplicative Mahler measure, and its circle supremum is at most five times its largest coefficient. Combine these with the forward and inverse bounds for (9) and compare the archimedean part of the projective height with the Mahler measure. The finite part is minus the weighted content of $H_\emptyset$. The finite drops in (11) depend on the two Gram columns and the eligible labels alone. For the limiting degree outside a prescribed fixed set of places, omit their finite drops after including those places in the specialization argument.

Take the representatives of $u,v$ normalized as in §4. At torsion $T$ the coefficient $w_+(T)$ has absolute value at most one at finite places and at most two at infinity. The same argument as (3) gives $\mathrm h_E(s_\eta(T))\le\mathrm h_E(u)+\mathrm h_E(v)+\log3\le 2J_E+\log3$. Applying (2) and taking the limit gives the node bound with $D_\eta$ in place of $D$ in (8) and $2\log 3$ in place of $2\log 2$. One may use $\mathcal J(u,v)$ in the same node inequality with an additional $\log30$ error. Nonproportionality is a condition on the chosen low-height plane. The absolute minima argument provides independence alone. When all $H_\xi$ are proportional to $H_\emptyset$, the torsion evaluations yield the constant ratios of the scalar multipliers, as in §5.

### Direct height inequality, including proportional twists

The quantity $\mathcal J(u,v)$ in (11) is defined for every menu. Put $N_w=\max(\|u\|_{E,w},\|v\|_{E,w})$ at finite places and $N_\sigma=\max(\|\sigma(u)\|_2,\|\sigma(v)\|_2)$ at infinity. Let $\mathrm h_E(u,v)$ denote the sum of the weighted logarithms of these local maxima. The finite part of the projective height in (11) cancels the reference content, so $\mathcal J(u,v)$ is the average of $\log\|\sigma(H_\emptyset)\|_\infty$ minus $\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,\nu_w^H$. At $w$ every eligible entry satisfies $|\langle s,t\rangle_\xi|_w\le N_wN_{cw}$ for $s,t\in\{u,v\}$ by paired integrality. Hence $-\nu_w^H\log\ell\le\log N_w+\log N_{cw}$. At infinity the two maps from coefficient coordinates into the reference columns at $\sigma$ and $c\sigma c$ each have Euclidean operator norm at most $\sqrt h\,e^{B_x/2}$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\|\sigma(H_\emptyset)\|_\infty\le h e^{B_x}N_\sigma N_{\sigma c}$. Summing, using invariance of the weights under the two place permutations, gives
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 mT_x
 &\le M_{d,i}-\mathcal J(u,v)+\mathcal L_x+\log h+2\mathrm h_E(u,v)\\
 &\le M_{d,i}-\mathcal J(u,v)+\mathcal L_x+\log h+4J_E .
 \end{aligned} \tag{12}
$$
The second bound uses the representatives of §4, whose local norms are at least one. The logarithm of their local maximum is then at most the sum of their logarithms. The argument includes an empty-only menu and every proportional menu. If $H_\xi=\lambda_\xi H_\emptyset$, the eligible finite part in (11) is the scalar ratio degree and the projective term still records the spread of the reference entries. For example, the empty-only algebraic model $H_\emptyset=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ell^a)$ with $a>0$ gives $\mathcal J(u,v)=a\log\ell$ while every pencil ideal is the unit ideal.

## 7. The signed test on fourth powers

Let $A>1$, $D_N\to\infty$ and $D_N=o(\log N)$. Use the common blocks of [4] on $N\le n\le\lfloor N^A\rfloor$, with eligible primes $d\in(D_N,2D_N]$. At each node take the arithmetic input $D_{d,i,x}$ to be the limiting degree in §5 or §6 for a low-height pencil, the binary quantity $\mathcal J(u,v)$ in (12), or the rectangular degree $\deg I^s/h$ from (1). For $h=1$ the rectangular degree is the line degree. Put
$$
 z_{d,i,x}=\frac{M_{d,i}-D_{d,i,x}}{X_i b_i t_i}.
$$
The signed comparison uses the centered ramp
$$
 R_{q',d}(x)=
 \sum_{\substack{\nu\le x\\\nu\equiv1\bmod q'}}
   \Lambda(\nu)(1-(\nu/d))\left(\frac1\nu-\frac1x\right)
 -\frac{\log x}{\varphi(q')}-c_{q',d},\qquad q'\in\{q,1\},
$$
where $c_{q',d}$ makes the remainder tend to zero and $(\nu/d)$ is the Legendre symbol. At a node of field $d$, [4, §6] gives
$$
 R_{q,d}(x)=\frac{T_x}{(xb)t_i}
   +R_{q,d}(B_dx)-\frac{T_0}{t_i}R_{1,d}(B_dx).
$$
The two tails are $O_q(X_i^{-2})$, with $T_0/t_i\ll V_i^6$. Inserting the node inequalities of §§2, 5 and 6 with the bounds on $f/t_i$ and the height allowance gives
$$
 R_{q,d}(x)\le \frac{X_i}{m_i x}z_{d,i,x}
 +O_q\left(\frac{1+V_i^6 L_i^4}{m_i}\right). \tag{13}
$$
This is an upper bound for the signed ramp.

For $s=n^4$ in rank interval $i$, take its bracketing mesh nodes $x_j$ and reciprocal interpolation coefficients $\lambda_j\ge0$ satisfying $\sum_j\lambda_j=1$ and $\sum_j\lambda_j/x_j=1/s$. At an exact node the coefficient is one. In the reciprocal variable the prime-power sum is convex, since each term is a nonnegative multiple of $(1/\nu-1/x)_+$. The remaining logarithm has second derivative bounded below by $-v^2/\varphi(q)$ for a bracket $[u,v]$. Adding a quadratic with this curvature to the ramp and using its chord yields an upper interpolation error $(v/u-1)^2/(8\varphi(q))$. Define the interpolated deficits
$$
 Z_{d,n}=\sum_j\frac{s\lambda_j}{x_j}\,z_{d,i,x_j}.
$$
The coefficients are probabilities. The contribution from (13) at $s$ is bounded by $X_i Z_{d,n}/(m_i s)$ with the same sign for every $Z_{d,n}$. For any common weights $0\le a_n\le n$ this yields
$$
 \sum_n a_n R_{q,d}(n^4)
 \le \sum_n\frac{a_nX_i}{m_i n^4}Z_{d,n}+N^{o(1)}. \tag{14}
$$
All sums here and below are over the given fourth-power window. In each block $\sum n\le m_i$, so the curvature contribution is $O_q(X_i^{-1/2+o(1)})$. There are $O_A(\log N)$ blocks and $V_i^6L_i^4=N^{o(1)}$ uniformly. This controls the error independently of menus and pencil fields.

**Sufficient arithmetic condition.** Suppose on an unbounded sequence of these windows there exist $D_N^{1-o(1)}$ disjoint eligible pairs $\{d,e\}$ with admissible node inputs and with
$$
 \sum_n\frac{nX_i}{m_i n^4}
 \left[\frac{Z_{d,n}+Z_{e,n}}2\right]_+\le N^{o(1)} \tag{15}
$$
uniformly over the pairs. Then ordinary RH follows with the mesh and analytic inputs stated above. The expression on the left is the exact maximum of the signed paired main term in (14) over the sample-weight box $0\le a_n\le n$. It clips only after averaging the fields and interpolating at the samples. A coarser sufficient test uses the largest $z_{d,i,x}$ for each field and rank, equivalently the smallest degree at that field and rank, before forming the paired average.

Indeed, given a putative zero with real part $\beta>1/2$, [4, §9] gives common weights in this box with signed sum greater than $N^{4A(\beta-1/2)-\eta}$ for all but $O(D_N^{\alpha(\beta)+\epsilon})$ eligible fields, where $\alpha(\beta)=3(1-\beta)/(2-\beta)<1$. Take fixed $0<\eta<4A(\beta-1/2)$ and $0<\epsilon<1-\alpha(\beta)$. Disjointness leaves at least one selected pair untouched by the exceptional set for all sufficiently late windows of the sequence. Averaging (14) on that pair and applying (15) contradicts the witness. The functional equation supplies reflection across the critical line.

Formula (7) gives a second sufficient test. At every pencil node replace $D_{d,i,x}$ by
$$
 h_{\rm proj}(\mathbf b)
 +\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}\omega_w\log\ell(w)
             \bigl(\mu_w(B_\emptyset)-\nu_w\bigr)-\log 2 \tag{16}
$$
for any certified set $\mathcal W$ of finite places, or by the larger of this value and zero. This lower bound for $D_{d,i,x}$ increases the node deficit, so (15) still suffices. In particular one may use the projective height alone with the displayed absolute allowance. For the binary input in (12) one may retain the projective term and any certified subset of the nonnegative eligible drops in (11). This applies even to a menu consisting of the empty label alone. Verifying (15) for the original nodes on enough growing-field pairs remains open, both for the torsion degrees and for the direct binary heights.

## 8. A Gaussian three-class illustration

Consider the definite Gaussian $U(2)$ lattice with determinant level at the oriented split place over $13$. There is one full-level class. Indeed a self-dual rank-two Hermitian Gaussian lattice in its Euclidean realization has covolume one, so Minkowski's convex body theorem gives a nonzero vector of squared norm less than $2$ in real dimension four. Its squared norm is an integer, hence one, and it splits off an orthogonal unimodular line. The remaining line is standard since the Gaussian integers are principal. The full-level automorphism group consists of permutations and unit scalings, of order $32$ with determinant image of order $4$. At this level the local determinant quotient is $\mathbb F_{13}^{\times}$ modulo the image of Gaussian units. There are three classes and the stabilizer of each has order $8$. Use the resulting metric $I_3/8$. At the retained split pair of norm $Q=101=10^2+1$, multiplication by the oriented determinant class of $10+i$ acts by the cycle
$$
 D_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The residue logarithm at $13$ with $i=5$ and generator $2$ is $1$ modulo $3$ for $(10+i)/(10-i)$. At the split ray over $8101$, orient by $i=90$ and use generator $6$. Its order is $8100$ and $4\cdot675\mid8100$, so the norm-one ray admits a character of order $675$ trivial on the Gaussian units. The log of $(10+i)/(10-i)$ is $592$ modulo $675$. Write $\varpi=\zeta_{45}$ and let the two effective components have values $\varpi^5,\varpi^9$ at $6$, of orders $9,5$. The phase at the retained split pair is $\theta=(\varpi^5\varpi^9)^{592}=\varpi^8$. Its conductor is the oriented prime over $8101$. The empty phase is $1$.

On this weight-zero class space the split unramified Hecke operators for the constant, first and second terms of the Euler polynomial act as $I_3,(Q+1)D_3,D_3^2$. The middle factor $Q+1$ counts the lines in the two-dimensional residue fibre, each with the same determinant translation. Thus the spherical polynomial at the split vacuum parameter has factors $I_3-Q^{-j}\theta^{-1}D_3$ for $j=5,6$ [5]. If $A_\theta$ is their product, the insertion Gram is $G_\theta=A_{\theta^{-1}}^{\mathsf T}(I_3/8)A_\theta$. The determinant is
$$
 \det G_\theta=8^{-3}\prod_{j=5,6}
 (1-Q^{-3j}\theta^3)(1-Q^{-3j}\theta^{-3})
$$
by the three eigenvalues of $D_3$. This is a class Gram for one spherical insertion. Applying the node test to the period construction would require the evaluated normalized short columns, projection and remaining lift stages of §§2–4.

Take the pencil $u=e_1,\ v=e_2$ and the two phases $1,\theta$. Put $L_0=Q^{11}$, $s_0=(Q+1)Q^5$, $h_0=s_0(L_0+1)$, $A_0=h_0-L_0$ and $b_0=2(L_0^2+s_0^2+1)$. Direct multiplication and clearing by $C_0=8Q^{22}$ give
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 B_\emptyset(T)&=-A_0(T^2+1)+b_0T,\\
 B_\xi(T)&=(L_0\theta^{-2}-h_0\theta)T^2
        +b_0T+(L_0\theta^2-h_0\theta^{-1}).
 \end{aligned} \tag{17}
$$
The field is $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{45})$ of degree $24$. The two polynomials have gcd $1$, and $b_0>2(L_0+h_0)$ ensures positivity on the unit circle after division by $T$ at all embeddings. The exact replay verifies that $C_0$ is the minimal common rational clearing, the reference content is the prime $10303$, and $\gcd\bigl(b_0,\big|\mathrm N_{F/\mathbb Q}(L_0\theta^{-2}-h_0\theta)\big|\bigr)=1$. The twist has zero content order everywhere, and its exclusion at $8101$ leaves the eligible minimum zero there since the reference has zero order as well. Thus the cleared $C_{\rm fin}$ is zero and the only surviving coefficient drop is $\log10303$. Before clearing, $C_{\rm fin}=-\log C_0$.

Let $\lambda=(b_0+\sqrt{b_0^2-4A_0^2})/2$ and $\rho=A_0/\lambda$. The reference factorization $B_\emptyset(T)/T=\lambda(1-\rho T)(1-\rho/T)$ gives
$$
 D=\log\lambda=102.22579855106765\ldots,\qquad
 h_{\rm proj}(\mathbf b)=\log(b_0/10303),\qquad
 \varepsilon_\infty=\log(\lambda/b_0).
$$
At the source torsion order $p=7$ the two evaluated resultant norms have gcd $1$ and the reference resultant is prime to $8101$. This certifies zero evaluated eligible minima directly. The resultant of the reference is
$$
 N_7=(b_0^3+A_0b_0^2-2A_0^2b_0-A_0^3)^2,
$$
by the cubic for $\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1}$. Hence the degree is $\log N_7/6=102.22579855108366\ldots$. Let the fixed support consist of the rational primes dividing the nonzero coefficient norms, the common clearing, the discriminant, the determinant level, the conductor, and the norm of the resultant of the two pencil polynomials. The replay verifies that $N_7/10303^6$ is coprime to this support, and checks that $29$ is outside it with exact order $v_{29}(N_7)=4$. The degree outside this support is $\log N_7/6-\log10303$, with contribution $(2/3)\log29$ at $29$. The replay also certifies the residue-degree condition of §5 for $p=2^{44497}-1$ via Lucas–Lehmer and an upper bound on all the relevant residue field sizes using the resultant norm. For any fixed finite set of rational primes, the limiting degree outside the set is at least $\log(\lambda/10303)=92.98560815712258\ldots$ by (7) and the local specialization proof.

For the five-phase construction put $m_0=b_0/2$. The cleared binary reference is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}m_0&-A_0\\-A_0&m_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The cleared twisted binary form has the same diagonal and the off-diagonal entries in (17). The independent replay finds gcd $1$ for every fifth root, so take $\eta=1$. The reference quartic is
$$
 -A_0(T^4+1)+(m_0-A_0)(T^3+T)+3m_0T^2 .
$$
The binary content is again $10303$. The replay checks coprimality of the twisted off-diagonal entry with $\Phi_{45}$ modulo $10303$. The twist is eligible there and lowers the coefficient order by one at every extending place. Away from $10303$ the reference has content order zero and all eligible rows are integral. At $8101$ the eligible minimum is taken on the reference alone. The cleared quartic pairing on the unit circle is at least $m_0-4A_0>10303\cdot10^{40}$. Thus even after removing the contribution at any fixed finite set of places, the limiting degree exceeds $40\log 10$.

At torsion order $7$, put
$$
 N_7^{(4)}
 =\operatorname{Res}_Y
     \bigl(Y^3+Y^2-2Y-1,\ m_0(3+Y)-A_0(Y^2+Y-2)\bigr)^2 .
$$
This is the reference resultant by substituting $Y=T+T^{-1}$ and pairing inverse primitive seventh roots. The exact replay verifies that $N_7^{(4)}$ is coprime to both the twisted resultant norm and $8101$. At a place where the reference evaluation has positive order, the twist is eligible and has order zero. The cleared evaluated minimum is therefore zero everywhere and the exact degree is $\log N_7^{(4)}/6$.

A complementary calculation tests local entry minima of this insertion. For a finite set of matrices $H$ of size $r$ and coefficient places $w$, take exponents $d_a=ba+a^2$ for $0\le a<2r$ with an integer $b>2(2r)^2$. Nonzero ordered differences $d_j-d_i$ are distinct, since equality first forces equality of $j-i$ and then of $j+i$. With $z$ primitive of sufficiently large odd prime order $P$, set $u_j=z^{d_{2j}}+z^{d_{2j+1}}$ for $0\le j<r$. In the Laurent polynomial for $c(u)^{\mathsf T}Hu$, the coefficient at $d_{2j+1}-d_{2i}$ is exactly $H_{ij}$. All coefficients have valuation at least the matrix's entry minimum. After multiplying by $T^{d_{2r-1}}$ and dividing by an entry of minimum valuation, the nonzero reduction has degree at most $2d_{2r-1}$. If $\operatorname{ord}_P(\#k_w)>2d_{2r-1}$, the reduction argument of §5 gives the entry minimum exactly on this single vector at every extending place. This local statement carries no node height allowance.

The local replay uses $P=1607$ and computes orders at $3,5$ in the full class frame, the saturated sum-zero frame, the constant line and the primitive line $(-4,1,6)^{\mathsf T}$. With the order-$45$ phase, the primitive line's orders drop from $(1,2)$ to $(0,0)$. The full frame's determinants drop from $(6,10)$ to $(0,0)$, while its entry minima are $(0,0)$ at both readings. In this example the full determinant order can change entirely through the quotient of the determinant by the product of its diagonals. The corresponding replay also tests conjugation at split places of a non-CM field. These computations illustrate two different contributions to the arithmetic of short forms. The torsion pencil in (17) yields moving prime divisors, while the local calculation isolates orders in specified frames at fixed primes.

## 9. All minors of the original short Gram

Retain the original $h$ short columns at a node and the finite eligible menu of §2. For $1\le r\le h$, write $K_\xi^{[r]}=\bigwedge^r K_\xi$ in the exterior-coordinate basis. Its entries include the minors with different row and column sets. For a matrix $H$, let $\mu_w(H)$ denote its minimum entry valuation and write $\operatorname{vec} H$ for the list of its entries. Set
$$
 \mathcal J_r=h_{\rm proj}([\operatorname{vec} K_\emptyset^{[r]}])
   +\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\left(
     \mu_w(K_\emptyset^{[r]})-\min_{\xi:\,\ell\nmid Q_\xi}\mu_w(K_\xi^{[r]})\right).
$$
At an eligible finite place, $C_\xi=V^{\mathsf T}K_\xi U$ is integral for the common carriers of §2. Each $r$-minor of $U^{-1}$ is, up to sign, a complementary minor of $U$ divided by $\det U$, and the same holds for $V^{-1}$. Taking exterior powers of $K_\xi=V^{-\mathsf T}C_\xi U^{-1}$ therefore gives $\mu_w(K_\xi^{[r]})\ge-2e_\ell$. At an archimedean embedding, each reference minor pairs two wedges of $r$ short vectors, in the two readings of §2. Exterior Cauchy–Schwarz and Hadamard bound its modulus by $e^{r B_x}$. Expanding the projective height cancels the reference content and yields
$$
 mT_x\le M_{d,i}-\frac{\mathcal J_r}{r}+\mathcal L_x+\frac{2\Delta_x}{r}. \tag{18}
$$

There is a singular-value and Smith form of this test. For an embedding $\sigma$ let $s_{\sigma,1}\ge\cdots\ge s_{\sigma,h}$ be the singular values of $\sigma K_\emptyset$. Let $b_{w,\xi,1}\le\cdots\le b_{w,\xi,h}$ be the local Smith exponents of $K_\xi$ in the normalization $v_w(\ell)=1$, allowing negative exponents. Define
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 A_{\sigma,r}&=\frac1r\sum_{j\le r}\log s_{\sigma,j}
                    -\frac1h\log|\sigma(\det K_\emptyset)|,\\
 F_{w,r}&=\frac1h v_w(\det K_\emptyset)
                   -\min_{\xi:\,\ell\nmid Q_\xi}\frac1r\sum_{j\le r} b_{w,\xi,j},\\
 \mathcal S_r&=\frac1{[F:\mathbb Q]}\sum_\sigma A_{\sigma,r}
                   +\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,F_{w,r}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{19}
$$
The first two terms defined in (19) are nonnegative by the ordering of the exponents and eligibility of the empty label. Locally the minimum valuation of the $r$-minors is the sum of the $r$ least Smith exponents. The exterior operator norm at infinity is the product of the largest $r$ singular values. It lies between the largest entry modulus and $\binom hr$ times that modulus. The product formula for $\det K_\emptyset$ then gives
$$
 0\le\mathcal S_r-\frac{\mathcal J_r}{r}\le \frac{\log\binom hr}{r}.
$$
Thus (18) holds with $\mathcal S_r$ in place of $\mathcal J_r/r$ and with the additional term $\log\binom hr/r$. Decreasing $r$ increases both nonnegative terms of (19), and $\mathcal S_h=\deg I^s/h$. For $r=\lceil h/2\rceil$ the two charges are at most $4\Delta_x/h$ and $2\log2$. The signed criterion (15) can use $\mathcal S_{\lceil h/2\rceil}$, including a lower estimate coming from any chosen subset of the nonnegative contributions in (19).

**Layer counts.** At $w$ with ramification index $e=e(w/\ell)$, clear the empty matrix by a local scalar and let $n_j$ be the number of its integral Smith slots of uniformizer order at least $j$. For the empty-only menu, the sum of all orders is $\sum_{j\ge1}n_j$ and that of the first $r$ is $\sum_{j\ge1}[n_j-(h-r)]_+$. Consequently
$$
 F_{w,r}=\frac1e\sum_{j\ge1}
     \min\left(\frac{n_j}{h},\frac{(h-r)(h-n_j)}{rh}\right). \tag{20}
$$
A common rescaling shifts all the orders together and changes only layers whose summand is zero. For even $h$ and $r=h/2$ the summand is $\min(n_j,h-n_j)/h$. Formula (20) is a lower bound for a larger eligible menu. These counts can use a completion in the same basis and metric. If $M_j$ denotes the number of completed orders below $j$ and $m_j$ the corresponding short count, deleting $c_a$ rows and $c_a$ columns gives $M_j-2c_a\le m_j\le M_j$. Indeed the count is the minimal number of generators of the matrix image modulo $\pi^j$. Deleting inputs removes at most $c_a$ generators. Projection to the retained outputs has kernel a submodule of $(\mathcal O_w/\pi^j)^{c_a}$, generated by at most $c_a$ elements. The generator count of submodules and quotients of a finite module over this principal ideal ring is bounded by that of the module. In particular, $U$ unit slots and $V$ slots of depth at least $t$ in the completion contribute at least
$$
 \frac{t}{e}\min\left(\frac{(V-c_a)_+}{h},\frac{(h-r)(U-2c_a)_+}{rh}\right)
$$
to the short $F_{w,r}$.

**Moving Gaussian split primes.** On the three indicators of §8, replace $101$ by a split prime $q'$ whose determinant-uniformizer action is a nontrivial class cycle $D_3$ or $D_3^{-1}$. ## 10. Mixed determinants of the actual twist Grams

The source pencils of §§5–6 vary a vector in a two-plane before forming a line Gram. Here the source tuple is fixed and the indeterminates combine distinct twist Grams. Let
$$
 F(\mathbf T)=\det\left(K_\emptyset+\sum_{\xi\ne\emptyset}T_\xi K_\xi\right),
 \qquad P(\mathbf T)=F(\mathbf T)/\det K_\emptyset.
$$
At $w\mid\ell$ obtain $P_{\mathrm{elig},w}$ by setting $T_\xi=0$ whenever $\ell\mid Q_\xi$. Define

 s_w(P)=-\min_\alpha v_w([\mathbf T^\alpha]P_{\mathrm{elig},w}),
 \qquad
 D_{\rm mix}(P)=\sum_{w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,s_w(P). \tag{21}
$$
The constant coefficient is $1$, so the score is nonnegative. The finite support follows from the algebraicity of the Gram entries in §2. Multiplying by the local ramification index turns each score into a uniformizer order. The normalized degree is invariant under extension, and one common change of basis multiplies both determinants by the same factor. The pure coefficient of $T_\xi^h$ is $\det K_\xi/\det K_\emptyset$. Its negative valuation recovers the signed determinant score, so (21) dominates $\deg I^s$.

The same two carriers in §2 make every eligible coefficient matrix integral before taking the determinant. In particular the eligible unnormalized polynomial has minimum coefficient valuation at least $-2e_\ell$. Normalization then gives $s_w(P)\le v_w(\det K_\emptyset)+2e_\ell$. $$
This permits $D_{\rm mix}(P)/h$ as the arithmetic input to (15) with the same allowance as (1). For the identity translate one can compute the polynomial on a fixed spanning list of length $n$. If the image coordinate matrix is $C$ of rank $h$ and the list Grams are $H_\xi=C^*K_\xi C$, then

The principal-minor argument of §3 proves the identity. Dividing by its value at $\mathbf T=\mathbf0$ gives $P$, using the characteristic-zero exponent also at finite places.

### Cofinite probes and transfer of the order

At a coefficient place $w\mid\ell$ work in a splitting DVR $O$ with fraction field $K_w$ and normalization $v(\ell)=1$. Define $\mathbb T_w\subset O^s$ to be the unital $O$-algebra of tuples of split Hecke eigenvalues on the input, using all split places off the chosen levels (including their conjugates) and above rational primes other than $\ell$. The finite rank of $O^s$ implies that this subalgebra is a finite $O$-module and is closed in the valuation topology.

Here the exact classical local comparison is important. The good projector on the input is transferred to the definite standard rank-$R$ row in [5]. At each of the two distinct marked rational primes the primitive finite cuspidal label inflates to a depth-zero supercuspidal type for the split $\mathrm{GL}_R$. The automorphic constituents on this definite row are cuspidal, of odd rank $R$ and regular infinitesimal character. Harris–Labesse [10, Theorem 2.2.2] gives cuspidal conjugate-self-dual base change to $E$ with the split local identification. The transferred weight is regular algebraic. The compatible representations of [11, Theorem 2.1.1(2)] then have exact unramified Frobenius polynomials at the split probes away from $\ell$. We use their common Satake normalization, including the factors $q_v^{\binom{k}{2}+k(R-k)/2}$ for the coefficient of degree $k$ in the unnormalized spherical convention. Equivalently one converts from the Frobenius polynomial with a common cyclotomic normalization. All the conversion factors are units at $w$ since $q_v$ is prime to $\ell$, and the half exponent in the displayed convention is integral for odd $R$. These hypotheses concern the actual good systems, including the prescribed primitive types. The approximation of Frobenius roots alone would be insufficient for the order assertion.

Delete any finite further set of probe primes. For each deleted probe and each precision $\mathfrak m_O^n$, continuity of the finitely many characteristic polynomial functions, together with the cyclotomic character in the normalization, gives an open conjugacy-invariant neighborhood of its Frobenius element where the tuple agrees at that precision. Here the comparison representations are at the very coefficient characteristic $\ell$ of the order. Choose their coefficient embeddings extending the given reading $w$ of the Hecke eigenvalues; a common finite local extension contains their coefficients and normalization factors. Chebotarev [9] gives primes of $E$ in the neighborhood outside the deleted set. Primes of absolute residue degree greater than one have density zero among primes of $E$, hence the probes may be taken of degree one and split over $E^+$ away from ramification. The removed tuple lies in the closure of the retained order, which is the order itself. Faithfully flat scalar extension of DVRs preserves the orders and their indices in normalized units, and equality descends. For characteristic-zero separation at any split off-level probe over $\ell$, one can instead compare at an auxiliary coefficient characteristic different from that probe. Thus cofinite separation gives full rank, while the comparison at $\ell$ gives equality of the integral orders, even when two systems have identical residual representations.

The unnormalized Hecke operators are finite sums of right translations. At a split probe off both the original marked levels and the declared twist modulus and its conjugate, the lower-chain point kernel is the local vacuum and both splitting characters are unramified. The scalar level normalization commutes with translations. In step $j$, with $n_j=\dim_E Y_j$ and with the two rank-twisted Frobenius character values denoted $\alpha_j,\beta_j$, the transfer is
$$
 P(X)\longmapsto
 \left(\prod_{a=0}^{t-1}(1-q_v^a\alpha_jX)\right)P(q_v^t\alpha_j X/\beta_j).
$$
The splitting-character parity identity gives $q_v^t\alpha_j/\beta_j=q_v^{t/2}\upsilon(\varpi_v)^{(n_j\bmod2)-(n_{j-1}\bmod2)}$. Both character values acquire the same finite twist factor at the two ends of this chain step. The algebraic character norm formula makes the coefficients integral and the substitution factor a unit away from the rational probe prime. The final polynomial therefore has the form $A_{\xi,v}(X)P_{\pi,v}(U_v X)$ with $A_{\xi,v}(0)=1$, integral $A_{\xi,v}$ and unit $U_v$, common to the systems at that label. The algebraic realization intertwines the translations. These are the split transfer identities of [5], with source locations below.

If $C_j$ is the input tuple of degree $j$, the final tuple of that degree is $U_v^j C_j+\sum_{a<j}[X^{j-a}]A_{\xi,v}\,U_v^a C_a$. Successive subtraction and division by units recover all input tuples. Higher final coefficients already belong to the input order. The normalizing factors $q_v^{\binom{k}{2}}$ converting polynomial coefficients to the counting-measure Hecke action are units as well. Delete the finite union of all menu moduli from the probes. The preceding cofinite argument proves that the algebra of tuples of final Hecke actions on the injective image $\Theta_\xi V$ is exactly $\mathbb T_w$ for every eligible label.

For these operators the required integral action is on the actual gauge lattice. Each coset representative is supported at the probe and integral with its inverse above $\ell$. The gauge translation identity and the ultrametric inequality give preservation of integral forms for their sum. At the chosen level, a translated class representative is rewritten by a rational unitary element and a level element; in the algebraic Weyl basis the matrix is the rational-point representation times the finite level character value. This proves algebraicity. Taking class representatives integral above $\ell$ identifies the integral coordinate ball with the gauge lattice and proves integrality of the eigenvalues by the monic characteristic polynomial. This argument works for ramified coefficient places and primes dividing an old level, since the probe and the coefficient prime are distinct. Eligibility for the paired lift remains $\ell\nmid Q_\xi$. [5]

Every tuple in $\mathbb T_w$ is thus realized, at each label, by an integral right action on its full final lattice. It suffices to use finitely many probes and polynomial expressions because the order is finite over $O$. For a single valuation calculation one can work in the completion. Equivalently, the lattice of tuples of monomials in a finite probe packet is defined over a localization of an algebraic coefficient field, and Smith reduction there realizes its normalized minima. A choice of polynomial may depend on the place and label. There is no need for an upper bound on the sizes of the new probes.

### Congruence indices

Let $\delta_H$ denote the normalized determinant index of $\operatorname{pr}_H\mathbb T_w\subseteq O^H$ for a subset of systems $H$, with $\delta_\varnothing=0$, and write $\delta=\delta_{\{1,\ldots,s\}}$. The full order has rank $s$ by separation in characteristic zero. For $\pi\notin H$ put
$$
 b_w(\pi\mid H)=\delta_{H\cup\{\pi\}}-\delta_H,\qquad
 b_{\pi,w}=b_w(\pi\mid\{\pi\}^c). \tag{26}
$$
Projecting the first lattice onto the second has kernel the coordinate ideal of values at $\pi$ of tuples vanishing on $H$. Comparing determinants proves (26). In particular $\eta_{\pi,w}=\{a\in O:ae_\pi\in\mathbb T_w\}$ is a nonzero ideal of valuation $b_{\pi,w}$. It is the coordinate of the conductor of the split order in $O^s$. The transfer result identifies this with the minimum clearing order for an individual spectral projector by an integral polynomial in the specified final Hecke operators. This minimum is uniform in the twist. It need not be the minimum among arbitrary lattice endomorphisms. Flat extension of DVRs preserves the defining kernels and normalized index differences. All these costs vanish outside a finite set for fixed input data, by interpolation at a finite separating packet away from its probe primes and the divisors of its interpolation scalars.

For the minimum generator-difference order $t_{\pi\rho,w}$ between two characters of the input order, one has $\max_{\rho\ne\pi}t_{\pi\rho,w}\le b_{\pi,w}\le\sum_{\rho\ne\pi}t_{\pi\rho,w}$. Congruence of evaluations on generators implies congruence on the algebra, yielding the lower bound. Multiply one difference-attaining factor for each other system to obtain the upper bound. For a single system the cost is zero. All the indices can be computed from maximal minors of any finite spanning matrix of the order, including its products.

There is also an aggregate estimate
$$
 \sum_{\pi\in H}b_{\pi,w}\le \delta+\delta_H-\delta_{H^c}. \tag{27}
$$
For the trace dual $T^\vee$ of $T=\mathbb T_w$ in the coordinate pairing, its projection to $\pi$ is $\eta_{\pi,w}^{-1}$ by duality of lattices. After a finite unramified extension to a residue field of size greater than $s$, choose $y\in T^\vee$ attaining every coordinate minimum. Each failure is a proper linear subspace modulo the uniformizer, and their union cannot fill the larger residue space. Since $T$ is an order, $yT\subseteq T^\vee$. Project to $H$ and use $\operatorname{pr}_H T^\vee=(T\cap K_w^H)^\vee$. The determinant valuations of the two projected lattices are $\delta_H-\sum_{\pi\in H}b_{\pi,w}$ and $-(\delta-\delta_{H^c})$. Inclusion gives (27), and flatness preserves the indices on passage back. Summation over the superlevel sets of an integer multiplicity vector yields the weighted version. In particular the unweighted total is at most $2\delta$.

### An exterior coefficient of the actual empty Gram

Use a finite separating packet at the empty label. There exist algebraic ambient projectors $\Pi_\pi$ in the final class coordinates restricting to the spectral projectors of the empty image and mutually orthogonal at every complex embedding. To establish this, let $M$ be the rational positive final class metric from §3. At empty modulus the level character is trivial and every unnormalized split operator has a matrix over the CM normal closure of $E$: rewriting each translated representative by a rational point uses the Weyl representation at its embeddings. The adjoint for $M$ is the inverse double coset, which is spherical at the conjugate split place. Hence these operators are commuting normal matrices. Over a CM field containing $i$, take their commuting self-adjoint real and imaginary parts. Every embedding preserves the adjoint identity and their eigenvalues are totally real. Adjoining the finitely many eigenvalues of these parts still gives a CM field. Polynomial spectral interpolation followed by products gives the joint orthogonal projectors over that field. On the image they act as desired by the transfer and separation. On unused ambient joint spaces their sum may be zero. Their idempotence, pairwise orthogonality and self-adjoint identities persist at every embedding. This supplies the projectors on the ambient space even if the theta image has only been defined in the original embedding. [5]

Write $X$ for the column matrix of the empty images in those coordinates. The norm bound under all complex automorphisms and the positive rational metric imply algebraicity of each entry: a transcendental coordinate could be sent to arbitrarily large modulus by extending an embedding of transcendence bases. Define the unnormalized grouped coefficient
$$
 D_k=[\mathbf Z^k]\det(X^*M\textstyle\sum_\pi Z_\pi\Pi_\pi X),
 \qquad 0\le k_\pi\le d_\pi,\quad \sum_\pi k_\pi=h.
$$
At the original embedding choose an empty-orthogonal eigenbasis of the full good space and write the short coordinates as a $d_V$-by-$h$ matrix. Cauchy–Binet expresses $D_k$ as the sum of squared minors of row set size $h$ with $\pi$-occupancy $k_\pi$, weighted by the empty basis norms. It is nonzero exactly when one such minor is nonzero. Their sum is the positive empty determinant. At every complex embedding,
$$
 |\sigma D_k|\le \exp(hB_x). \tag{28}
$$
Indeed, the coefficient operator of $\mathbf Z^k$ in the $h$-th exterior power of $\sum Z_\pi\sigma\Pi_\pi$ is an orthogonal projector onto the wedges of that occupancy. The two vectors paired by this operator are the wedges of the columns of $c\sigma c(X)$ and $\sigma(X)$, where $c$ denotes complex conjugation. The Weyl metric is rational and the two individual column bounds apply. Exterior Cauchy–Schwarz proves (28) without a binomial factor. The same proof applies to any subset of $a$ columns with $\exp(aB_x)$ in place of $\exp(hB_x)$.

For an eligible menu, set $L_{\pi,w}(\mathbf T)=\sum_{\xi:\ell\nmid Q_\xi}\lambda_{\pi,\xi}T_\xi$ and $s_{\pi,w}=-\min v_w(\operatorname{coeff}L_{\pi,w})\ge0$. Define the raw pole degree and corrected degree
$$
 A_\pi=\sum_{\ell,w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\,s_{\pi,w},\quad
 C_\pi=\sum_{\ell,w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell\, b_{\pi,w},
 \qquad W_\pi=A_\pi-C_\pi. \tag{29}
$$
Work in a common finite normal number field $K$ for the projectors, Grams and eigenvalues, with $v_w(\ell)=1$ and $\omega_w=[K_w:\mathbb Q_\ell]/[K:\mathbb Q]$. This field contains the CM projector field and can itself be non-CM. The CM property is used only for the ambient projectors in (28). At a finite place we may enlarge $K_w$ for the comparison representations, transfer constants or residue field choices. Formula (26) and the normalized valuations of the fixed coefficients are independent of such local extension. In the global sums the weights remain those of $K$, and any global enlargement gives the same sums by additivity of local degrees. The raw Grams are algebraic by §10, and the finite sums in (29) have finite support. These weights use the same signs at every place, including the cost of the empty projector where all relative eigenvalues are units.

For each nonzero $D_k$, the integral individual projectors give
$$
 \sum_\pi k_\pi W_\pi\le hB_x+2\Delta_x. \tag{30}
$$
At $w$, select for each occupied system an eligible twist maximizing the pole. Insert on the right its cleared projector of scalar $a_\pi$ of valuation $b_{\pi,w}$. Give each inserted matrix its own indeterminate. Cauchy–Binet in the empty eigenbasis identifies the coefficient of the chosen occupancy as $D_k\prod_\pi(a_\pi\lambda_{\pi,\xi})^{k_\pi}$. Both original carriers clear the polynomial matrix. Its determinant coefficients have orders at least $-2e_\ell$, since the right correspondences preserve the lattice. This gives $v_w(D_k)\ge\sum k_\pi(s_{\pi,w}-b_{\pi,w})-2e_\ell$; summing and using (28) proves (30). The fixed coefficient $D_k$ is used at every place, although the integral insertions vary.

In particular (30) bounds the sum of the $h$ smallest global values $W_\pi$, counted with multiplicity. Sort the distinct systems in decreasing order of $W_\pi$ and let $r_j$ be the projection rank of $Y$ on the prefix of the first $j$ systems, with $r_0=0$. Extend independent rows successively in this order. The resulting supported occupancy is $k_j=r_j-r_{j-1}$, and every other supported occupancy retains at most $r_j$ prefix rows. Summation by parts shows that the maximum left side of (30) equals $hW_s+\sum_{j<s} r_j(W_j-W_{j+1})$. It uses the actual characteristic-zero ranks, with $r_j\ge[\sum_{i\le j} d_i-(d_V-h)]_+$. Taking a global minimum over occupations is at least as strong as summing placewise minima.

In contrast, compression can remove every pole from an uninserted pencil. A rank-two matrix example at an odd prime $p$ is $G_0=p^a I_2,\ G_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,p^a-1)$ for $a\ge1$. ## 12. Omitted directions and upper quantiles

The correction for a fixed supported occupancy $k$ can be reduced. With $J=\{\pi:k_\pi<d_\pi\}$ one has $|J|\le d_V-h$. Define
$$
 C_w(k)=\sum_{j\in J}k_j b_w(j\mid J\setminus\{j\})
          +\sum_{i\notin J}d_i b_w(i\mid J).
$$
Then
$$
 \sum_{\ell,w\mid\ell}\omega_w\log\ell
       \left(\sum_\pi k_\pi s_{\pi,w}-C_w(k)\right)
       \le hB_x+2\Delta_x. \tag{31}
$$

To prove this choose $t\in\mathbb T_w$ vanishing on $J$ and simultaneously attaining $b_w(i\mid J)$ at each $i\notin J$. In the module of tuples vanishing on $J$, each failure to attain the scaled coordinate minimum is a proper linear subspace modulo $\mathfrak m_O$. Enlarge the residue field by a finite unramified extension as in (27); the normalized costs remain the same. Use $t=1$ when $J$ is empty and $t=0$ when its complement is empty. For each $j\in J$ with $k_j>0$ choose $u_j\in\mathbb T_w$ whose coordinates on $J$ are $a_j e_j$, where $v_w(a_j)=b_w(j\mid J\setminus\{j\})$. Put $H(\mathbf Z)=t+\sum Z_j u_j$. Its coordinate on a wholly omitted system is zero and on a partially retained system $j\in J$ it is $a_jZ_j$.

Use the transferred right actions of this polynomial at each eligible label and take the determinant of the sum of the resulting short Grams with variables $T_\xi$. Its expansion is
$$
 \sum_{k'}D_{k'}\prod_\pi L_{\pi,w}(\mathbf T)^{k'_\pi}H_\pi(\mathbf Z)^{k'_\pi}.
$$
The coefficient of $\prod_{j\in J,\,k_j>0} Z_j^{k_j}$ only receives the term with $k'=k$. Indeed the wholly omitted coordinates must have occupancy zero, the remaining coordinates in $J$ have occupancy at most $k_j$, and outside $J$ they have occupancy at most $d_i=k_i$. Both totals are $h$. Thus this coefficient is the polynomial
$$
 D_k \prod_{j\in J,\,k_j>0}a_j^{k_j}\prod_{i\notin J}t_i^{d_i}
          \prod_\pi L_{\pi,w}(\mathbf T)^{k_\pi}.
$$
The minimum coefficient valuation is $v_w(D_k)+C_w(k)-\sum_\pi k_\pi s_{\pi,w}$ by multiplicativity of polynomial content over a DVR. The same two carriers clear the entire polynomial matrix, so the displayed minimum is at least $-2e_\ell$. Apply (28) and the product formula. This proves (31) with a single common occupancy at all places.

By (26) each term costs at most its individual-projector counterpart. At full rank $J=\varnothing$ and the cost is zero. For $J=\{j\}$ the cost is $\sum_{i\ne j}d_i b_w(i\mid\{j\})$, so there is no internal cost on $j$, even if several of its directions are retained. The latter indices are precisely the orders of the pairwise difference ideals. In a monogenic order $O[\theta]$ with distinct values $\theta_i$, monic division by the factors $X-\theta_j$ gives $b_w(i\mid H)=\sum_{j\in H}v_w(\theta_i-\theta_j)$. If all the omitted systems are wholly omitted, $C_w(k)$ is the order of the resultant of $\prod_{i\notin J}(X-\theta_i)^{d_i}$ with $\prod_{j\in J}(X-\theta_j)$. For a rank-three linear algebra example with simple eigenvalues $0,p^a,1$, the rank-two span of the first two eigenvectors has zero omitted-direction cost at $p$, whereas the two individual costs are each $a$. The operator $\theta-1$ kills the last system and is a unit on both retained systems. The supplement computes the order indices directly and checks coefficient extraction with partially omitted multiple systems, including a case where the individual costs are strictly greater.

## 14. Free orbits in the prime-discriminant windows

The original eligible primes have $d\equiv9239\bmod q$, $q=18480$. ### The tail of a quadratic $L$-value

Fix $z$ greater than every prime divisor of $q$, and let $Q=\operatorname{lcm}(q,\prod_{\ell\le z}\ell)$. For each reduced class $a\bmod Q$ extending the eligible class set
$$
 C_a=\prod_{\ell\le z}(1-\chi_{-d}(\ell)/\ell)^{-1},\qquad
 Y_d=L(1,\chi_{-d})/C_a,\qquad \chi_{-d}(n)=(-d/n).
$$
The finite product depends only on $a$, including at 2 since $8\mid q$. For primes in this fixed class and window we claim
$$
 \limsup_{D\to\infty}\mathbb E_{a,D}|Y_d-1|^2
 \le\prod_{\ell>z}\frac{1+\ell^{-2}}{(1-\ell^{-2})^2}-1\ll z^{-1}. \tag{40}
$$
Use the conditionally convergent series $Y_d=\sum_{(n,Q)=1}\chi_{-d}(n)/n$. Pólya–Vinogradov [12] and partial summation, with finite Möbius inversion over divisors of $Q$, bound the tail after $H=\sqrt{2D}(\log D)^3$ by $O_Q((\log D)^{-2})$ uniformly. Set $U=(\log D)^8$. For a dyadic block $M<n\le 2M$ (which may be shortened at either end), the Jacobi symbol for odd denominators gives
$$
 \sum_{D<d\le2D}
 \left|\sum_{\substack{n\ {\rm in\ block}\\(n,Q)=1}}\frac{(-d/n)}n\right|^2
 \ll D\frac{\log(2M)}M+M\log(2M). \tag{41}
$$
Here the outer sum is over all integers. In the square expansion, $nl$ square corresponds to $n=su^2,l=sv^2$ with common squarefree $s$. There are $O(M\log(2M))$ such pairs by summing $O(M/s)$ over $s$. Otherwise the imprimitive character in $d$ has squarefree kernel $s>1$ with $nl=st^2$. Inclusion-exclusion for the coprimality conditions from $t$ costs at most $\tau(\operatorname{rad}t)\le t$ primitive Pólya–Vinogradov bounds $O(\sqrt{s}\log(2s))$. Each nonsquare inner sum is $O(M\log(2M))$. The constant sign from $-1$ is harmless. Summing with weights $1/(nl)$ gives (41).

There are $\asymp_Q D/\log D$ primes in the class. Divide (41) by that count and apply the triangle inequality in the mean-square norm over the $O(\log D)$ blocks between $U$ and $H$. Their square roots have sum $O_Q((\log D)^2/\sqrt U+(\log D)^2\sqrt{H/D})=o(1)$. Thus the entire tail after $U$ has vanishing mean-square norm.

In the remaining sum of the square of $Y_d-1$, a pair $1<n,l\le U$, both coprime to $Q$, has character product one if $nl$ is square, because the tested prime $d$ exceeds $U$. Otherwise the squarefree kernel $s>1$ is coprime to $Q$. Multiplying its primitive quadratic character by any Dirichlet character modulo $Q$ gives a nonprincipal character of modulus at most $QU^2$. The character form of Siegel–Walfisz [13] applies uniformly since $Q$ is fixed and $U$ is a power of $\log D$. Removing the prime log weight by partial summation gives mean $O_{Q,B}((\log D)^{-B})$ for any fixed $B$. The total coefficient weight is $O((\log U)^2)$. The surviving square-pair sum is bounded above by the sum over all positive $n,l$ coprime to $Q$ with $nl$ square, excluding $(1,1)$. For each $\ell>z$ the unrestricted local even-exponent series is $\sum_{k\ge0}(2k+1)\ell^{-2k}=(1+\ell^{-2})/(1-\ell^{-2})^2$. This proves (40), with absolute bound by summing the $O(\ell^{-2})$ logarithms. The estimate is uniform over the finitely many reduced classes for a fixed $Q$.

The class number formula for $d>3$ is $h(-d)=C_a\sqrt d\,Y_d/\pi$ [12,13]. Fix $0<\epsilon<1/10$ and a positive integer $k$. Among the good readings $|Y_d-1|\le\epsilon$, the equality $h(-d)=k3^v$ places $k3^v$ in
$$
 [C_a(1-\epsilon)\sqrt D/\pi,\ C_a(1+\epsilon)\sqrt{2D}/\pi].
$$
The ratio of endpoints is strictly less than three. Hence at most one integer $v\ge0$ occurs in this class and window. For that value, the possible $d$ lie in an interval of length $O(\epsilon D)$ by squaring the two bounds on $\sqrt d$. The prime number theorem in the fixed progression gives a relative proportion $O(\epsilon)+o_Q(1)$ in such an interval, uniformly in its location in $[D,2D]$, by subtracting the prime-counting functions at the endpoints. By (40) and Chebyshev, the limiting proportion outside the good readings is $O(1/(z\epsilon^2))$.

For fixed $K_0$, sum the interval bounds over $k\le K_0$ and count the exceptional readings once. Recombining the classes gives limiting upper proportion $O(K_0\epsilon+1/(z\epsilon^2))$ for $h(-d)/3^{v_3(h(-d))}\le K_0$. First take $D\to\infty$ for each fixed $z,\epsilon$, then let $z$ tend to infinity, and finally $\epsilon$ to zero. This proves the claimed growth in probability. Genus theory gives an odd class number for a single prime discriminant [8,15]. With $K_0=1$ this supplies a prime divisor $p\ge5$ of $h(-d)$ on all but $o(D/\log D)$ eligible primes, using the prime number theorem in the original fixed progression. Truncating $L(1,\chi_{-d})$ at $H$ as above gives the elementary upper bound $p\le h(-d)\ll\sqrt D\log D$.

### The actual split Hecke orbits

For a surviving discriminant, choose an order-$p$ character $\psi$ of $\mathrm{Cl}(K_d)$. Let $H_d$ be its full Hilbert class field and let $E=K_d(\zeta_{q_1})$ be the original abelian CM host at any of the nodes. The Galois group of $H_d/\mathbb Q$ is generalized dihedral, with commutator $\mathrm{Cl}(K_d)$ since conjugation acts by inversion on this odd-order group. As $E/\mathbb Q$ is abelian, $E\cap H_d=K_d$. Choose a class $A$ with $\psi(A)$ primitive of order $p$. By Chebotarev in $EH_d$, there are rational primes splitting completely in $E$ and having an oriented prime of $K_d$ of class $A$. The conjugacy class also allows the opposite orientation. All old levels and any prescribed finite set can be avoided. Take the norm-one uniformizer at a split pair of $E$-places over such a rational prime. Its norm to $K_d$ has ideal class $A^2$, by the residue-degree-one property and inversion under conjugation. Thus the pullback of $\psi$ along the norm to $K_d$ on the norm-one torus gives an unramified weight-zero character $u$ of exact order $p$. The character is trivial on finite integral elements and on the real components, so defines each genuine empty-modulus label in §13. The argument works separately in each host with the same character $\psi$. [8,9]

On the definite rank-$R$ good row the operator of multiplication by $u^a\circ\det$ preserves the marked types and rotates the full joint eigenspaces bijectively. At the chosen split probe the top Hecke operator is an invertible central translate, with eigenvalue on the $a$-th translate multiplied by $u(t_v)^{aR}$, where $t_v$ is the norm-one uniformizer at that pair (up to reversal of orientation). Because $R$ is a power of three and $p\ge5$, the $p$ factors are distinct. This proves freeness for the joint systems, including all their good multiplicities. It is enough to use one split probe to distinguish the translates, and multiplicities are constant along each orbit. The construction holds at every rank and node of the field's original window.

One system per orbit accounts for a fraction $1/p$ of the full good dimension. For $D=D_N=o(\log N)$, the bound $p\ll\sqrt D\log D$ gives $1/p\ge N^{-o(1)}$. The surviving fields can be paired arbitrarily into $\gg D/\log D=D^{1-o(1)}$ disjoint pairs. The quantile test of §12 now has a particularly concrete sufficient arithmetic condition. If a union of orbits of inverse-subpolynomial dimension fraction at every selected node contains, in each orbit, at least one system with
$$
 \frac{n(D_a)}p-2m^2x_1-C_{\pi_a}\ \ge\ u_{d,i,x},
$$
then selecting one such system per orbit still gives an inverse-subpolynomial fraction, by constant multiplicities and the bound on $p$. Hence the signed paired test (15) with $u_{d,i,x}$ as node input suffices for ordinary RH under the geometric and analytic inputs specified in §§1–3 and 7. One can also use the actual quotient cost (38), or the supported occupation bound (31) when it improves the arithmetic input. The supply theorem guarantees free orbits and enough fields, while this inequality is a further quantitative condition on their corrected periods. Neither local phase variation nor absolute convergence and nonvanishing of the standard values ensures positive finite numerator degree: active products may agree, or their contrasts may be algebraic units. The growing-field corrected-period lower bound needed for an independent ordinary RH proof remains open.

## 15. Original higher-rank variation and adjacent finite denominators

We now give a quantitative variation statement at the actual original rank and critical argument. It concerns the standard product with the common quotient scalar divided out. The finite-degree conclusion will compare two critical arguments, with the same unramified twist and reference throughout.

### Detecting primes on selected blocks

Use an eligible discriminant $d$ and an order-$p$ class character as in §14, with $p\ge5$. In a block $X=m^2$, $m$ a power of two, assume $m>p$ and $1\le m\bmod p\le p-2$. Write $s_*=(5R+1)/2$, $f=[E^+:\mathbb Q]$ and choose any occupied rank $R\asymp X^5$. At a split off-level pair $v$ write $u(t_v)=\zeta^{h_v}$ with $\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p)$ and $q_v$ its absolute norm. Let $Q$ be the least norm of a pair with $h_v\ne0$, keeping the old Euler omissions. For sufficiently large blocks, uniformly for $d\in(D,2D]$, $D=o(\log X)$, we have
$$
 Q\ge16X^{11},\qquad \log Q\ll pf\log X,\qquad
 (p_0p_1)^{2f}>(2R)^{2f}(Q^2)^8. \tag{42}
$$
Here $p_0,p_1$ are the two original marked rational level primes. For the lower bound, at nondyadic places the residue field contains the $2^a$-th roots of unity with $2^a=16X^{11}$. At dyadic places the pinned $3^c$-th roots with $c=\lfloor\log_3(22\log_2m)\rfloor+6$ force residue degree divisible by $2\cdot3^{c-1}$, giving an even larger norm.

For the upper bound, let $K/E$ be the unramified cyclic extension detected by the order-$p$ base-change character, and $M=E(\zeta_{2^{a+1}})$. Since $d\equiv7\bmod8$, the quadratic factor splits at 2, and the next cyclotomic step is a nontrivial quadratic extension ramified above 2. The two old rational primes split in $M/E$ by the level congruences. The extensions $K,M$ are disjoint over $E$. Kadiri–Wong's degree-one least-prime theorem [16] applied to $KM/E$ supplies Frobenius nontrivial in both factors, hence a detecting prime off the old levels. Degree one and the abelian host ensure a split pair, avoiding the finite ramified primes: an odd prime of degree one is $1\bmod2^a$ whereas the ramified odd rational primes are below $2^a$ in the pinned scales. The relative discriminant of $M/E$ divides $4\mathcal O_E$, so $\log|\Delta_{KM}|\le4pf\log\operatorname{rd}(E)+2pf\log4$. The theorem bounds the logarithm of the prime norm by $310\log|\Delta_{KM}|$ for these large discriminants. The pinned $\operatorname{rd}(E)\le X^{12}$, $\log(p_0p_1)\ge20\log b$, and $\log b\gg_B(\log X)^B$ for every fixed $B$, together with $p\ll\sqrt D\log(2D)$, imply (42). [4, §2] [5]

There are arbitrarily late common blocks satisfying the residue condition for all the chosen fields. First choose increasing $D$, then take $\log_2m$ sufficiently large and divisible by the orders of 2 modulo all odd primes up to the class-number bound for that interval. This gives $m\equiv1\bmod p$ and $D=o(\log X)$. The relative density-one result of §14 supplies $\gg D/\log D$ eligible fields on such blocks. For a fixed field at least one of two consecutive powers of two meets the residue condition once $m>p$. The common-block selection does not yet supply the estimates for every node in the full fourth-power window of (15).

### The joint logarithmic moments

For a prime-order orbit index the references by $a\in\mathbb F_p$. Let $F_j(a;t)>0$ denote the incomplete standard value of (33) at unitary argument $t\ge s_*$ (with the same omitted places and any common stage normalization). To avoid conflict with the pinned lift increment, the variable $t$ in this subsection is only the critical argument. For $r,c\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ set
$$
 X_{r,c}(t)=\frac1p\sum_a\zeta^{-ca}\sum_{j\equiv r\bmod p}\log F_j(a;t).
$$
At a split pair the two unitary Satake tuples are contragredient, and the two logarithms of the Euler factors are conjugate. The nonsplit retained factors are independent of $a$. At first power the contributing orientation is unique for each nonzero $c$ because $p$ is odd. Only pairs of norm at least $Q$ can contribute, also at higher powers.

Retain the first powers at norms at most $Y=Q^2$. On the full definite principal space their operator at $t=s_*+k$, $k\ge0$, is $Z_{r,c}(t)=\sum_v z_v q_v^{-k}T_v$, where $T_v$ is the colength-one spherical operator at the chosen orientation and
$$
 |z_v|=q_v^{-3R}\left|\sum_{j\equiv r\bmod p}\mu_j(t_v)^{-1}\right|.
$$
The power $-3R$ is $-s_*-(R-1)/2$ in the counting-measure Hecke normalization. The lift characters form a geometric progression $\mu_j=\mu_0\psi^j$. If $m=lp+s_0$ with $1\le s_0\le p-2$, among nonzero residues there are both progression lengths $l$ and $l+1$. At a pair of norm $Q$ the corresponding sums, after removing initial unit phases, differ by a term of modulus one. At least one has modulus $\ge1/2$. This provides a nonzero first-power contribution without any assumption on the distance of an individual phase from a root of unity.

Normalize trace by the full principal dimension. For $Z=\sum_v a_v T_v$ using these pairs and $n_v=(q_v^R-1)/(q_v-1)$, the second and fourth moments are
$$
 \mathbb E|Z|^2=V_{r,c}=\sum_v|a_v|^2n_v,\qquad
 \mathbb E|Z|^4=2V_{r,c}^2-\sum_v|a_v|^4n_v. \tag{43}
$$
Indeed a principal class return for a word of at most four translations has the form $ga=\gamma gk_0$, where $\gamma$ is rational unitary and $k_0$ belongs to the principal level. The trace difference $\operatorname{tr}\gamma-R$ is divisible at every place above both old rational primes, and its denominator norm is at most $Y^8$ since each letter costs at most one uniformizer at each of the two places in its pair. Its modulus is at most $2R$ at every embedding by definiteness. The norm bound (42) forces trace $R$, and a unitary matrix with trace $R$ is the identity. Thus the returns in the trace have exactly the local identity coefficients, with the compact coefficient fiber also acted on by the identity. The full principal class stabilizers are trivial. At one pair there are $n_v$ index-$q_v$ superlattices of a lattice. The two-step operator $T_v^*T_v$ has identity coefficient $n_v$ and $n_v(n_v-1)$ distinct off-diagonal endpoints of coefficient one: the intermediate superlattice for distinct endpoints is uniquely their sum. Squaring gives $2n_v^2-n_v$. Determinant valuation rules out locally unbalanced words. For distinct pairs the balanced terms have four orderings and independent local coefficients. Expansion proves (43), on the simultaneous Hecke decomposition.

Put $V=\max_{r,c\ne0}V_{r,c}$ at $s_*$. Since $n_v\ge q_v^{R-1}$, the preceding phase comparison gives $\sqrt V\ge Q^{-s_*}/2$. The discarded terms in each Fourier coefficient on every good system are $O(fmR Q^{-4R})$ uniformly for $t\ge s_*$. To see this, use the unitary bound $|\alpha_i|\le q_v^{(R-1)/2}$ and sum first powers beyond $Q^2$ and powers at least two from $Q$. The expansion variable has modulus at most $q_v^{-(2R+1)}$, and at most $2f$ prime ideals have any given norm. As $R\asymp X^5$, $f\ll X^{11}(\log X)^{O(1)}$ and $p\ll\sqrt D\log(2D)$, the error is $o(\sqrt V/p^2)$.

For a maximizing pair the second and fourth moments, by Cauchy–Schwarz on the event $|Z|^2\ge V/2$, show $|Z_{r,c}(s_*)|\ge\sqrt{V/2}$ on at least $1/8$ of the principal dimension. At $s_*+k$, $k\ge1$, the second moment is at most $Q^{-2k}V$ for every pair. Markov's inequality and the union bound over all $k\ge1$ and at most $p^2$ pairs discard a fraction at most $256p^6/(Q^2-1)$ to enforce $|Z_{r,c}(s_*+k)|\le\sqrt V/(16p^2)$ simultaneously. The bad marginal dimensions at the two marked primes sum to at most $b^{-8}$ of the full principal dimension by [5]. The retained good orbits therefore cover at least $1/9$ of the good dimension for sufficiently large selected blocks. Changing the orbit origin multiplies each tested Fourier reading by a phase, so the events are orbit invariant. The source equality of good multiplicities transfers the statement from the definite row to the actual good systems.

### Nonvanishing of differences and finite denominators

Define the normalized standard product $U_{a,b}(t)=\prod_{j=1}^m F_j(a-jb;t)/F_j(a;t)$. At $s_*$ this is $\lambda_{a,b}/c_b$ from (34). Fourier inversion gives
$$
 \log U_{a,b}(t)=\sum_{r,c\ne0}X_{r,c}(t)
      \big(\zeta^{c(a-rb)}-\zeta^{ca}\big). \tag{44}
$$
The characters with nonzero $b$-frequency are distinct because $p$ is prime. On each retained orbit a Fourier coefficient at $s_*$ has modulus $>\sqrt V/2$, so some pair $(a,b)$ has $|\log U_{a,b}(s_*)|>\sqrt V/2$. The higher-argument bounds give $|\log U_{a',b'}(s_*+k)|<\sqrt V/4$ simultaneously for every $a',b'$ and integer $k\ge1$, for sufficiently large blocks. In particular the same $a,b$ witnesses $U_{a,b}(s_*)\ne1$ and $U_{a,b}(s_*)/U_{a,b}(s_*+k)\ne1$ at all higher critical integers. This is a variation statement for the actual rank-$R$ standard product. Nonvanishing of the individual critical values follows from the absolute convergence in §13.

For $1\le k\le R^8-s_*$ consider $\beta_k=U_{a,b}(s_*)/U_{a,b}(s_*+k)$. Raghuram's adjacent critical-value reciprocity theorem [17, Theorem 5.16(2)] applies stage by stage to the same cuspidal base change and the two rank-one twists. The strong purity conditions follow from the conjugate-self-dual weights, the cohomological cuspidal summands are strongly inner, and the critical lattice is integral at odd $R$. The angular exponents of §13 give the entire interval $[1-R^8,R^8]$. The weights and hence the signs in reciprocity are the same for both twists. Discriminant and gamma factors cancel in the double ratio. Use the exact coefficient transport of the finite Euler factors to restore the common old omissions after applying the theorem to completed values. More explicitly, for common omitted $F$-places $S_M$ let $P_{a,v}(t)$ be the product of the local standard factors over all $E$-places above $v$ for the character at reference $a$. For the ratio of an adjacent quotient at $a-jb$ to that at $a$, multiply the completed finite-value comparison by
$$
 \prod_{v\in S_M}
 \frac{P_{a,v}(t)P_{a-jb,v}(t+1)}
      {P_{a-jb,v}(t)P_{a,v}(t+1)} .
$$
At the two marked split supercuspidal places the local factors are one because $R>1$ and the twist is unramified there. Other omitted old factors use the actual algebraic local standard polynomial (or the normalized local Godement–Jacquet generator) and its transport. The finite twist does not change the weights. This proves exact all-embedding equivariance and algebraicity of $\beta_k$ by multiplying adjacent comparisons. At the starting argument $U_{a,b}(s_*)$ is algebraic by (34) and (36), so each higher product is algebraic as well. The calculation of $\beta_k$ uses adjacent reciprocity, without requiring an absolute all-embedding comparison for $U_{a,b}(s_*)$ itself.

Every transported unramified character is a primitive power of $u$ with the same detecting support, and the transported cuspidal spectrum has the same unitary bounds. Thus in a common coefficient field, summing the Euler logarithms from $Q$ gives $|\log \sigma\beta_k|\ll fmR Q^{-2R}$ at every embedding; the transported analytic double ratios are positive. By the same estimate for the inverse, both numbers differ from one by at most $O(fmR Q^{-2R})$ at all embeddings and are distinct from one. For $z$ algebraic let $\operatorname{den}(z)=\sum_w\omega_w\log\ell\,[-v_w(z)]_+$ and $\operatorname{num}(z)=\operatorname{den}(1/z)$. At finite places $z$ and $z-1$ have identical negative valuation parts. Applying the product formula to $\beta_k-1$ and $\beta_k^{-1}-1$ gives
$$
 \min(\operatorname{den}\beta_k,\operatorname{num}\beta_k)\ge
 T,\qquad T=2R\log Q-O(\log(2fmR)). \tag{45}
$$
The error is uniform in the critical integer and chosen orbit on these blocks. Every coefficient prime is eligible for the class twists. This inequality concerns algebraic ratios of standard values, without estimating the common quotient scalar.

For fixed $b$ and argument $t$, $\prod_a U_{a,b}(t)=1$ by permutation of the references. Hence the numerator degree at one reference is at most the sum of the denominator degrees at the others. The two bounds (45), subadditivity of finite degrees, and this permutation identity yield
$$
 \sum_i\big(\operatorname{den}U_{i,b}(s_*)+
            \operatorname{den}U_{i,b}(s_*+k)\big)\ge 2T     \tag{46}
$$
for the very same $b$ at every $1\le k\le R^8-s_*$. Thus either the orbit mean denominator at $s_*$ is at least $T/p$, or it is at least $T/p$ at every higher critical integer in this interval. Choosing the more numerous of the two kinds of orbit, weighted by their good multiplicities, gives at least one member per orbit on a set of dimension fraction $\ge1/(18p)$ at the node, with one common choice of argument $s_*$ or $s_*+1$. The twist may depend on the orbit. The statement at the higher arguments controls finite denominators but only $s_*$ is used by the original Gram.

## 16. The full class group and exact orbit congruence cost

Let $H$ be the character group of the prime-to-three part of $\mathrm{Cl}(K_d)$ and write $h_H=|H|$ to distinguish it from the short dimension $h$. The norm map on the norm-one torus classes is surjective onto $\mathrm{Cl}(K_d)$ by the Hilbert class field argument of §14: a degree-one split probe in any prescribed class maps to its square, and squaring is bijective since the class group is odd. As $R$ is a power of three, multiplication by $R$ is invertible on $H$ and its dual. Thus the top split Hecke translations distinguish all members of each $H$-orbit, so every orbit is free and its multiplicities are constant. Choosing a single member of each orbit retains fraction $1/h_H\ge N^{-o(1)}$ under the field bound of §14.

### Integral invariant order

At $w\mid\ell$ keep the input order $\mathbb T=\mathbb T_w$ of §11 over a splitting DVR containing all character values. Twisting permutes its coordinates because each degree-$i$ split Hecke generator scales by the $i$-th power of the corresponding local character. Let $A=\mathbb T^H$ be the invariant suborder on the orbit set. For a member $a$ of orbit $o$, let $e_a,e_o$ be the respective coordinate idempotents (the latter constant on each orbit). We claim
$$
 \{z\in O:ze_a\in\mathbb T\}
   =h_H\{z\in O:ze_o\in A\}. \tag{47}
$$
For every $\chi\in\widehat H$ a top split Hecke translate supplies a unit $U_\chi$ in $\mathbb T$ transforming under $H$ by $\chi$. The probe can be chosen away from every excluded prime including $\ell$, by §14 and the surjective class map. Its inverse belongs to the integral order since the translate is invertible and its eigenvalues are units there (also by its monic characteristic polynomial with unit constant). Take $U_1=1$. Each monomial in the ordinary split Hecke generators is homogeneous under $H$. Multiplication by the inverse of the appropriate unit takes it into $A$. Their character spaces are independent in the fraction algebra, giving an integral direct sum $\mathbb T=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat H} A U_\chi$ with no division by $h_H$.

On orbit $o$ coordinatized by $b\in H$, the unit has values $u_{\chi,o}\chi(b)$ with $u_{\chi,o}\in O^\times$. The decomposition of $ze_a$ in the fraction algebra has coefficients $(z/h_H)\chi(-a)u_{\chi,o}^{-1}e_o$. Membership in the integral decomposition is equivalent to $(z/h_H)e_o\in A$, since the other multipliers are units. This proves (47), including places dividing $h_H$. The determinant of the evaluation basis also gives, for $s_o$ the number of orbits,
$$
 \delta(\mathbb T)=h_H\delta(A)+s_o\,\frac{h_H}{2}v_w(h_H). \tag{48}
$$
Indeed the change of basis on each orbit is the character table times unit diagonal factors, whose squared determinant valuation is $h_H v_w(h_H)$ by multiplication with the inverse-character transpose. The $h_H$ copies of an $A$-basis contribute the first term. All index valuations have the normalization of (26). The full order transfers to eligible final images by §11. In particular the exact individual cost in (29) is
$$
 C_a=B_o+\log h_H,\qquad
 B_o=\sum_w\omega_w\log\ell\,v_w\{z\in O:ze_o\in A\}. \tag{49}
$$
The within-orbit price is just $\log h_H$ at every reference, and $B_o$ is the remaining inter-orbit cost. Neither its size nor the corrected valuation bound follows from freeness alone.

### Recovering all unramified relative values

Use additive coordinates $a,b\in H$ on one free orbit and put $L_{a,b}=\prod_j F_j(a-jb)/F_j(a)$ at the original $s_*$. Formula (34) extends to this group with $\lambda_{a,b}=c_b L_{a,b}$. Its algebraicity follows exactly as in §13, including the scalar $c_b$.

For each subgroup $K\subset H$ whose quotient $C=H/K$ is cyclic of order $n>1$, write $\bar a=a+K$. For $0<r<n$ and $i\in C$ define
$$
 S_r(i)=\prod_{\bar a=i}\prod_{j\equiv r\bmod n}F_j(a),\qquad
 D_{K,r,i}=\prod_{d\mid\operatorname{rad}(n)}
    \left(\prod_{u\in C[d]} S_r(i+u)\right)^{\mu(d)n/d},
$$
where the stage indices range from 1 to $m$, $C[d]$ is the subgroup of order $d$, and $\mu$ is the Möbius function. Then
$$
 D_{K,r,i}\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_{>0},\qquad
 L_{a,b}^{h_H}=\prod_K\prod_{r=1}^{|H/K|-1}
 \frac{D_{K,r,\bar a-r\bar b}}{D_{K,r,\bar a}}. \tag{50}
$$
For the proof let $E_d$ average functions over $C[d]$, and $P=\prod_{p\mid n}(1-E_p)$, the projection to characters of exact order $n$. Then $\log D=nP\log S_r$. Form the algebraic line product $M_r(i)=\prod_{\bar a-r\bar b=i} L_{a,b}$. At stage $j$, its log is $\sum_{b\in H}(f_j(i+(r-j)\bar b)-f_j(i+r\bar b))$, where $f_j(i)=\sum_{\bar a=i}\log F_j(a)$. On a primitive character of $C$ the first sum is $h_H$ times the character if $j\equiv r\bmod n$ and zero otherwise. The second sum is zero. Thus $nP\log M_r=h_H\log D_{K,r,\cdot}$. The operation $nP$ has integral multiplicative exponents, proving algebraicity of $D^{h_H}$ and hence of $D$ by positivity. Every nonconstant character of $H$ has a unique kernel $K$ and a primitive character on the cyclic quotient. Its grouped projection is obtained by averaging over $K$ and applying $P$. Multiplication by $h_H=n|K|$ gives $nP$ applied to the sum over $K$. Apply to each translated logarithmic difference. Constant components and stages with $n\mid j$ have zero difference on that quotient. Summation and exponentiation prove (50). For prime $h_H$ the construction keeps the separate affine-line contrasts of the stages grouped by residue, allowing variation even when their combined reference product is constant.

With $d_{K,r,i,w}=v_w(D_{K,r,i})$, the raw local pole at reference $a$ is exactly
$$
 s_{a,w}=\max_{b\in H}\left\{
 h_H^{-1}\sum_{K,r}(d_{K,r,\bar a,w}
 -d_{K,r,\bar a-r\bar b,w})-v_w(c_b)\right\}. \tag{51}
$$
The empty label gives zero. In particular the best corrected global score in orbit $o$ is at least
$$
 \frac1{h_H}\sum_{a\in H}\sum_w\omega_w\log\ell\,s_{a,w}
             -B_o-\log h_H. \tag{52}
$$
The sum over $a$ can be replaced by the maximum of the global degree of one reference. The same reference is then used at every coefficient prime, while the maximizing twist in (51) may vary by place. All characters have empty finite modulus, so all places are eligible and field extension preserves the degree. For a concrete rank-one illustration the primitive values at unitary argument 3 over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$, of angular weight $6+2n$ for $n=30+14{,}473{,}836t$ with $t\ge0$, have valuations $(1,0,0)$ at the twelve places above 13. Assign their cyclic shifts to the three stages $1,2,4$ with $c_b=1$. The combined reference product is constant but (51) gives pole contributions $(1,1,0)\log13$. The exact seed and recurrence are reproduced in the supplement. This is a rank-one, three-reference calculation with specified stage products, distinct from the original growing-rank chain.

## 17. Scope of the resulting criterion

The geometric and analytic dependencies of §§1–3 and 7 remain in force. The higher-rank variation and finite denominator result of §15 use the full original good spaces and their actual critical weights and old local omissions. They provide growing finite degrees at one of two adjacent arguments on sufficiently many selected blocks, with a simultaneous alternative for all higher critical arguments on the same orbit and twist. The class group and invariant order of §16 give an exact within-orbit cost and a way to read the relevant pole at the original argument using all empty class characters. The remaining condition is a quantitative lower bound for (51) at $s_*$ after the actual common quotient scalar and inter-orbit price $B_o+\log h_H$, on a union of orbits of inverse-subpolynomial dimension fraction. The bound must meet (15) for $D_N^{1-o(1)}$ disjoint eligible pairs along arbitrarily late full windows. The denominator result at the higher arguments, and coverage of selected blocks, do not establish this condition. An independent ordinary RH proof therefore remains open. The proofs here use the stated source identities and classical theorems; Lean was not rerun.
