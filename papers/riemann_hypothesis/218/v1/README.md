# Kernel support and per-tower theta domination in the Alpöge release, in natural language: the local-model axioms

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T10:45:18.188384Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/218/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

This paper is a natural-language counterpart of part of a published Lean proof, the riemann-hypothesis release at commit e7c8972c. The release's assembler t_MainRow obtains four facts about its tower data from the theorem t_AxiomsOfLocalModel and passes all four into the tower recursion: the kernels K_i are positive semidefinite (KrPSD), their off-diagonal blocks vanish unless t^{-1} - t'^{-1} lies in a lattice (KernelSupport), the inflations satisfy 0 ≤ e_i ≤ 1 (EBounds), and every finite tower has a dominating theta family (DomAfter). We prove all four from the hypothesis LocalModel and four local facts: the positivity and support of the block kernels N_s, the existence of local theta families with seven properties, N_0 = 1 on GL(R), and the positivity of the local kernel. The proofs show that local systems satisfying six clauses (Ok) are closed under finite products, scalings and phase twists, and that pullback, top and outside systems are Ok. For KernelSupport we give a shorter argument than the release's: it needs only the first clause and the support half of the block-kernel fact, and no compactness. We also reduce the block-kernel fact to three inputs, the positivity of the local kernel, the multiplicativity of the modulus, and the release's averaging identity AvgClaim, by proving the two further inputs of its supplier (TripleForm and TTEnd, an inverse-last-column computation). So KernelSupport rests on LocalModel and AvgClaim alone. Paper 204 assumes KernelSupport and a global form of theta domination; we explain why its Theorem 5.3 needs only the per-tower form DomAfter that the release supplies and this paper proves. The local facts themselves are not proved here. A supplement checks the product clause numerically and the last-column computation exactly, each with a wrong variant that fails.
