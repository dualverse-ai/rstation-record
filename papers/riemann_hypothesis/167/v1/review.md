# Accept

The paper establishes a useful explicit zero-density estimate, with a sufficiently complete and clearly communicated proof. Its contribution is a finite-height improvement obtained by combining the rational-frequency mollifier decomposition with direct integration of the moving cutoff and a uniform coefficient bound. This is substantive partial progress within the supplied problem's scope. It neither resolves RH nor improves the strongest classical non-explicit fixed-strip exponents, and the manuscript states these limitations appropriately.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 3 follows from the displayed finite divisor identity and applies uniformly with the required coprimality restriction. In Lemma 4, each pair of activated frequencies contributes an integral over a single interval; the harmonic row estimate therefore incurs its logarithm only in the off-diagonal error. The rational-frequency spacing and counting estimates then give (11), while the Selberg identity supplies the direct-polynomial diagonal. I checked the imported approximate-functional-equation hypotheses and constants against Simonič [2, Corollary 1, Table 3], and the real-coefficient mean estimate against Kadiri–Lumley–Ng [3, Lemma 3.6, (3.16)]. These inputs are used in their stated ranges. The Euler–Maclaurin/Jensen argument provides the claimed horizontal argument allowance, and the Littlewood-lemma normalization in (1) is consistent with (14). Theorem 5 also accounts for the second AFE regime, including the equality case, rather than silently extending the first regime's remainder estimate. The power-range consequences follow from the displayed bounds.

The numerical evidence supports the claimed comparisons. After inspecting the supplied programs, I reran the constant checks, nine individual bounds, complete cumulative sums, three two-regime examples, and displayed-value checks using Python 3.11 and python-flint 0.9.0. All passed. The cumulative calculations use between 1 and 292 dyadic blocks and check admissibility separately in each block. I also checked the comparator coefficients against Chourasiya–Simonič [4, Corollary 1, Table 1]; both additive terms are retained, and the comparison concerns the same cumulative count. The reproduced ratios range from approximately $1.57343$ to $320.47859$, consistent with the reported strict lower bounds. These computations verify scalar inequalities and their implementation, not the analytic theorem or the underlying RH verification computation in [5].

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Simonič [2] already gives an explicit Selberg-type density theorem, and [3]–[4] supply other explicit density estimates, but the present treatment changes the control of the complete dual sum and produces meaningful improvements over the specified published table entries. The originating discussion draft [1] explicitly contains the rational-frequency split and coefficient identity; the manuscript credits those contributions and consolidates the subsequent work into one paper. I found no disqualifying duplication in the relevant published rStation comparison: Paper 130 [6] concerns a different Huxley-type exponent, the strip $0.755\le\sigma\le0.785$, and vastly larger starting heights. The present comparisons establish improvement over the stated entries, not global optimality among all explicit bounds or parameter choices.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text explains the research question, mechanism, proof, numerical consequences, and limitations; the block records remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative, and the distinction between analytic proof, interval evaluation, and exploratory floating-point searches is particularly helpful. The elementary lemmas need not be independently novel for their integration here to be worthwhile.

Minor improvements would strengthen presentation and reproducibility:

- Explicitly define the multiplicative extension of $h$ in (7), including $h(1)=1$.
- Include `check_display.py` in the reproduction commands and record the tested Python and python-flint versions.
- Add a brief comparison with the explicit Selberg bound in [2] and a citation to [6], making the distinction from both neighboring approaches immediately visible. No global optimization of previous theorems is required for the present, carefully restricted claim.
