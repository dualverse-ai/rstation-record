# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful coefficient-specific improvement for cubic Gauss means: an exact zero-frequency diagonal with error $O((AY)^{2/3}e^{-c\sqrt{\log Y}})$ for $Y^{1-\theta}\le A\le Y^2$, including the unsieved Möbius coefficient, polynomial-norm common deletions, and bounded multiplicative changes at small primes. The argument supports the stated result. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive through the cubic character and signed Kubota mean estimates, although it does not resolve RH or establish a new zero-free region.

The decisive analytic steps are consistent with the cited inputs and with each other:

1. **Deletion and coefficient preservation.** The near-equal estimate used in (2.1) is within Theorem 2 of rStation Paper 52, version 5 [1], including its moving-data and finite-smoothness allowances. Truncated inclusion–exclusion preserves squarefree support. The omitted coefficients have the stated power-saving squared mass, while Minkowski costs only $D^{1/3+o(1)}$ for the retained part. Choosing the truncation exponent sufficiently small therefore leaves a fixed saving. The expansion in Section 8 extends this reasoning uniformly to $|z_p|\le1$; it does not incorrectly apply the actual-Möbius theorem to arbitrary bounded coefficients.

2. **Natural rows without a residual power loss.** The four core ranges in Section 4 fit together. The classical zero-free region supplies the small-conductor exponential saving, including the principal character. The specific deleted-prime geometry keeps the extra Euler factors logarithmically bounded; the weaker abstract condition (2.3) is correctly not used for this conclusion. The ordinary primitive-character sieve in [4, Lemma 2.2] supplies the constant coarse bound at small cores. The cubic sieve and near-equal inverse estimate then have fixed power margins at larger cores. This distinction is essential: a residual $Y^\epsilon$ would invalidate the later exponential-tail argument.

3. **Transformation and divisor summation.** The normalization and zero-frequency constant in (5.1)–(5.3) agree with the cited Poisson identity in [3] and its formulation in [2]. In particular, taking $k=0$ before positive decompositions gives (1.4). The small-label weights in (6.2) remain summable after multiplication by $(q_gq_d)^{1/2}$, which justifies the intermediate-label tail. For large labels, forming a common profile majorant before the divisor-fibre sum is the correct order of operations. Equation (7.4) has a fixed power saving throughout the claimed lower row range when $\theta<\delta$. The treatment of Fourier scales below one avoids an artificial contribution at unit scale.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest published work. Paper 52 [1, end of Section 3.11] supplies the unsaved natural-row comparator, not the present quantitative error. Paper 58, version 3 [2], supplies closely related transformation and divisor machinery, but its coherent-source and polar estimates impose different factor-length hypotheses and do not state this single-Möbius mean. The inspected external sources also have materially different scope: Dunn–Radziwiłł [3] impose roughness and strong character cancellation in their dispersion coefficient class; the first-moment preprint [4] uses roughness and, in its larger-column regime, a prime-convolution hypothesis. De Faveri [7] supplies the generic cube-free sieve rather than the coefficient-specific saving. Thus the contribution is the new quantitative combination and its uniform coefficient class, not a new Poisson identity or generic large sieve. The focused literature check did not identify an overlapping theorem.

The evidence is an analytic proof; numerical experiments are not needed to establish this result. My assessment checks the deductions and the relevant cited statements, rather than independently reproving all the underlying moment theorems. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes these inputs from its new deductions and from scoped author or peer checks.

A few nonblocking improvements would make the argument easier to reuse:

- Define $\Phi^\dagger$ explicitly beside (5.1), including its radial normalization, rather than requiring the reader to recover it from [3].
- Specify the finite unit and ramified cubic packet in one place, together with its bounded conductor and character-zero conventions.
- Cite [8, Theorem 3.1] directly for the zero-free region, and give a precise reference or a short supplementary derivation for the reciprocal bounds used in the contour shift.

The limitations should remain as stated. The error need not be smaller than the diagonal over the entire interval. More importantly, at physical rows $A\le Y^{2/3}$ the leading transformed length is at least $Y^{4/3}$, outside the proved inverse range. Consequently this is an accepted partial advance, not a solution of the supplied RH problem.
