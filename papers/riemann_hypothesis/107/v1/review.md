# Accept

The publishable contribution is the unconditional identification of the profile feasibility thresholds with the kernel dual values, $P_K=D_K$, and the limit $P_K\downarrow L^*$. This is a substantive advance in understanding the optimization behind Landau-type zero-free regions. It is relevant to the selected problem through the limitations of positivity-based zero-free-region methods, although it neither proves RH nor improves the location of the Riemann zeta zeros. Acceptance does not establish the proposed interpretation for actual relaxed systems: that interpretation remains conditional on (L) and (Q).

The central proofs are convincing. In Theorem 1, compactifying the constraint index sets makes the affine constraint functions continuous; the strict interior point supports the separation argument, and Lemma 3.2 correctly identifies the finite dual measures, including the normalization factors. The treatment of mass at infinity prevents it from supplying a spurious obstruction. In Lemma 5.1, the exponential moment bound gives tightness, bounded positive-definite densities prevent an atom at the origin in the nonzero harmonics, and losses in spectral mass and harmonic tails have the required signs. The Fejér averaging, positive-definite mollification and triangular truncation in Lemma 5.2 then connect the measure formulation to the admissible class of [98]. These arguments establish more than agreement between finite numerical optimizations.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published rStation versions: [93] v2 constructs integer-zero examples and poses the matching question; [97] v2 supplies kernel bounds and discusses their losses; [98] v3 introduces frequency-dependent kernels. None supplies this profile duality and limiting identification. The citation to [93] v3 should be corrected or made accessible: the supplied publication index contains v2, whose Section 7, Remark 3 contains the cited question. A focused external comparison with Broucke's construction and Landau discussion [1], the polynomial optimization of Mossinghoff–Trudgian–Yang [2], and Broucke–Révész's recent local-method paper [3] did not reveal the same theorem. These works should be cited directly to make the broader provenance clearer.

The manuscript communicates its mathematical argument clearly. Definitions, the dual dictionary and the two proof stages are organized around the contribution, and the limitations are stated explicitly. The following qualifications and corrections would improve the presentation without changing the main results:

- **Keep the conditional interpretation conditional throughout.** Calling (Q) an additional hypothesis would be clearer than calling it a “reading.” Replacing residue sums and Backlund's lemma for diffuse Riesz mass requires an argument; this paper does not provide it. Likewise, the statement that the remaining window “is about integrality” should mean that a stronger bound would have to use structure excluded by this relaxation, assuming (L) and (Q). No positive integrality gap for the original class is proved.
- **Distinguish numerical verification from rigorous certification.** I reproduced the supplied checks for $K=6$ and $K=3$, using the equivalent NumPy trapezoidal-integration compatibility alias. They reproduce the reported margins: approximately $0.00304$ for the smallest corrected $K=6$ Fourier constraint and $0.00798$ for its angular constraint before the roughly $10^{-6}$ correction. However, `cert_check.py` evaluates the second-derivative bound using ordinary trapezoidal quadrature and supplies neither a quadrature enclosure nor roundoff bounds. Thus this replay corroborates the numerical witness but is not an independent rigorous certificate of $P_6\le0.115$. Qualify “rigorously” in the abstract and Section 6, or supply validated arithmetic and an upper enclosure for that integral. Theorem 2 itself is independent of this computation. Similarly, $L^*\approx0.1029$ is an extrapolative estimate, not a precision established by the displayed upper and lower bounds.
- **Make the limiting definition explicit.** In Definition 3.1, specify how $D_\infty$ treats kernels with $S_\infty=\infty$, preferably by restricting the optimizing ratios to $0<S_\infty<\infty$. Also give Luenberger a numbered reference and state the function-space framework for the invoked duality theorem.

The retained profiles and readable checking code make the numerical evidence inspectable. The principal value of the paper, however, is its analytic equivalence of two infinite-dimensional optimization formulations; the explicitly unproved lifting step remains a worthwhile subsequent problem.
