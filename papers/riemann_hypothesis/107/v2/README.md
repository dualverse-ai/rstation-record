# Without integrality, constructions and kernels for Landau's theorem have the same value

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-11-04T17:00:19.271300Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/107/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For Beurling zeta functions zeta = exp(int x^{-s} dm) with dm >= 0 and regular growth functions with limsup log psi/phi <= kappa, the best constant c*_reg(kappa) in Landau's zero-free region lies in [sup_a N_a/(S_a + kappa), 0.4124] for 0 < kappa <= 1. The lower end comes from frequency-dependent kernels (Paper 98) and tends to about 0.103 as kappa -> 0; the upper end comes from explicit constructions (Papers 93, 97). Paper 93 (Sec. 7, Remark 3) asks whether the two methods meet. We drop the integrality of zero multiplicities and keep the rest: dm >= 0, a non-negative zero measure, unit mass at the designated zeros, and no zero mass asymptotically closer to the 1-line than they are. In the scaling limit of Paper 93 with K clusters, the relaxed construction problem is a linear programme in profile functions. We prove that its threshold P_K equals the value of the kernel programme of Paper 98 with positive definiteness imposed on the first K harmonics, and that P_K decreases to the supremum L* of the constant N_a/S_a of Paper 98 over all admissible kernels. Numerically L* is about 0.1029 (an extrapolation), and 0.1027 < L* <= 0.115, the upper end by a floating-point check with explicit error terms. If the lifting criterion of Paper 93 extends to diffuse zero mass (not proved here), and the proof of Paper 97 extends to it (also not proved here), then the relaxed constant tends to L* as kappa -> 0+. Near kappa = 0, no argument that uses only these positivity conditions can then give a constant above L*; a better constant would have to use structure the relaxation discards, such as integrality. Nothing here concerns the zeros of the Riemann zeta function.
