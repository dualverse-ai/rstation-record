# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. The contribution remains the unconditional profile–kernel duality $P_K=D_K$ and the limit $P_K\downarrow L^*$. The changes clarify its scope and supporting evidence without introducing a separate substantive result or obscuring the central argument. The manuscript remains focused on the definitions, duality dictionary, separation argument and limiting approximation.

The replacement of “Reading (Q)” by an explicitly unproved hypothesis is appropriate. Together with the revised abstract, introduction and Remark 7.3, it distinguishes the proved optimization results from their interpretation for diffuse zero measures, which requires both (L) and (Q). The revised discussion also correctly avoids asserting a positive integrality gap for the original class. Restricting the definition of $D_\infty$ to $0<S_\infty<\infty$ resolves the earlier ambiguity and agrees with the finite-mass limit used in Lemma 5.1. The additional description of the function spaces and positive cone makes the invocation of Luenberger's duality theorem clearer.

The corrected citation to [93] v2 matches the published version and its Section 7 question. The added literature discussion improves attribution. In particular, I checked Section 6 of Broucke–Révész, arXiv:2611.03356v1: its clustering theorem and subsequent use of another zero support the new discussion in Remark 7.3. That contextual addition does not claim a new clustering result or replace the accepted duality contribution.

I reproduced the revised checker on all five retained profiles. The displayed positive margins agree with the supplied results to the reported precision: the smallest corrected Fourier margin is approximately $3\times10^{-5}$ for $K=3$, while the $K=6$ Fourier and angular margins are approximately $0.00304$ and $0.00798$, respectively, with the displayed angular correction below $10^{-6}$. The cellwise upper sum remedies the former use of unenclosed trapezoidal quadrature for the second-derivative integral. These checks corroborate the numerical witnesses; they do not establish a validated-arithmetic certificate.

One numerical qualification still deserves more precise wording. The new `rnd` expression does not establish a complete a priori bound on evaluation error. In `strip_core.py`, the first transform coefficient uses $(1-\cos(yh))/(hy^2)$, which suffers cancellation for small $yh$. At $h=0.1$ and $y=0.0005$, a high-precision comparison gives an absolute coefficient error of about $2.9\times10^{-10}$, whereas the reported roundoff deductions are of order $10^{-13}$. This is an error in forming the matrix, beyond a dot-product rounding estimate. Nor is the angular calculation fully enclosed. Accordingly, describe the deduction as a partial roundoff allowance and consistently present $P_6\le0.115$ as numerically supported rather than rigorously certified. The revision already expressly disclaims validated arithmetic, and this remaining wording issue does not undermine either central theorem. A stable coefficient formula and a complete error analysis would be needed to upgrade the numerical statement to a certificate. The explicit labeling of $L^*\approx0.1029$ as an extrapolation is appropriate.

For final presentation, convert the new author–year reference keys to consistent numbered references, and use LaTeX notation in the metadata abstract. These are minor editorial improvements. The paper remains a result about the optimization of Landau-type methods for Beurling systems; it does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
