# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to the shifted Nyman–Beurling approximation problem, rather than a resolution of RH. Its principal advance is the explicit two-taper construction at every prescribed rational scale: adjoining the knot derivative prevents simultaneous cancellation of the Mellin transforms at any nontrivial zeta zero. This removes the exceptional-scale restriction from the earlier constructive lower-bound argument while keeping the same linear support. The contribution is sufficiently distinct and substantive despite substantial inherited machinery.

The central reasoning is sound. The stated hypergeometric differential equation has regular coefficients at each $z\in(0,1)$, so a solution and its derivative cannot both vanish there unless the solution is identically zero, contradicting $w_s(0)=1$. The derivative taper preserves exact annihilation and has the regularity needed for the norm estimates inherited from [24]. Polarization supplies the Gram asymptotics, and restriction to the interval containing only the first Möbius summand establishes positive definiteness. The Mellin-pole argument then gives the claimed infinitely-often lower bound under $\Theta<\sigma<1$. The bounded-gap sampling justification in [24] also applies to the added taper. Separately, factoring the Jacobi modes into a positive endpoint weight times polynomials of increasing degree gives the stated finite-support exhaustion by a Vandermonde argument.

The novelty is narrower than the title suggests, but identifiable. In the supplied versions, [14] already establishes the distance exponent, [17] describes the finite-support annihilator, and [24] supplies the smoothing, norm asymptotics, generic-scale Mellin argument, and multiscale superpositions. These should remain credited as inherited results. The present addition is the derivative pair that works at a specified scale and its embedding in a systematic polynomial family. The Jacobi identities and polynomial interpolation themselves are classical. My focused external comparison included Balazard’s biorthogonal-system discussion and Propositions 5–6 in [18] ([arXiv:1812.04309](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.04309)), and the polynomial approximation and generalized criterion in [20] ([arXiv:2006.02953](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02953)). Those works provide relevant antecedents but do not supply this same fixed-support derivative-pair construction. This was a focused comparison, not an exhaustive precedence search.

The computational evidence supports the finite examples. I ran the supplied verification program within the resource limits. Its algebraic, Rouché, Mellin, and Gram-determinant outputs reproduced, as did all 18 integer witnesses, exact annihilation residuals, and interval-certified lower endpoints. Differences in the full-subspace comparison values were confined to floating-point rounding. The full-subspace percentages and larger-$N$ progressions remain numerical comparisons, not interval-certified optimality statements. These calculations corroborate the finite constructions; the asymptotic claims depend on the analytic proof.

The following corrections and qualifications should be made. They do not require a new central argument or additional experimental evidence.

1. **Correct the normalization in (11) and its proof.** With definition (10), the first identity is
   $$
   W_{r+2,\theta}-W_{r+1,\theta}
   =-\frac{2}{r+2}\binom{2r+1}{r}\Psi_{r,\theta,2}.
   $$
   The printed coefficient $-\binom{2r+1}{r}/(r+1)$ gives $-10/3$ at $r=2$, contradicting the correctly stated and reproduced coefficient $-5$. The span inclusion survives this normalization error; the intervening differentiated identity must also be corrected.

2. **Narrow the phase and nodal language.** Reflection parity in the linear coordinate $u$ does not imply exact Mellin phase quadrature: the oscillation is in $\log u$, and the Mellin amplitude is not reflection invariant. Section 4 establishes approximate quadrature for particular transforms at $\rho_1$ and $\delta=1/4$, together with improvements at the sampled $N$. It does not establish universal quadrature of consecutive modes or uniform elimination of finite-$N$ near-zeros. State those observations at their demonstrated scope, including in the metadata abstract and figure captions. Likewise, the pair has no common exceptional scale; the proof does not show that the original single-taper set $\mathcal E_r$ is empty.

3. **State the finite-dimensional restrictions.** At fixed $N$, the dimension of $\mathcal W_{N,\delta}^{(r,L)}$ is $\min\{L,N_\delta-N+1\}$, not $L$ for arbitrary $L$. The claimed two-dimensional jet subspace requires $N_\delta>N$; otherwise the derivative functional is zero. These restrictions leave the fixed-$L$ asymptotic results intact.

4. **Correct the small numerical inconsistency.** At $\delta=1$, $N=1280$, the retained output and reproduced calculation give the single-taper value approximately $2.41629\times10^{-7}$, not $1.8\times10^{-7}$. The reported gain of approximately $18{,}980.66$ agrees with the former.

Keep the existing scope qualification prominent: the norm asymptotics and rate realization require $\Theta<\sigma<1$, the rate assertion is a limsup statement, and vanishing unnormalized dual norms do not establish approximation of the target. The paper provides neither a proof of RH nor a new restriction on the location of its zeros.
