# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance in the spectral analysis of the complete compact-window Weil form. The principal result improves the loss in the near-full eigenvalue count to $O((1+L)(1+L+\log(1/\varepsilon)))$, retains the contribution from repeated zeros, and counts actual eigenvalues in $[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$ without assuming RH. The connection to the selected problem is substantive through the Weil criterion and its spectral obstruction, although the theorem does not prove positivity or resolve RH.

The decisive argument in Sections 2–5 is convincing. The disk-sampling estimates correctly incorporate zero multiplicities through the unconditional local zero count. The inverse logarithmic weight makes sampling of arbitrary ambient window functions bounded, while the logarithmic weight on the trial functions controls the full operator residual. The conjugated zero argument in (2.2) is retained throughout; the argument does not assume a positive zero-sampling representation.

The concentration-to-strip passage supplies the essential improvement. The inverse concentration construction establishes (3.2) for every coefficient combination, including the jumps introduced by zero extension. The concavity argument then yields the weighted leakage estimate (3.3). In Section 4, the reproducing cutoff equals one on the original support, so its larger support incurs a frequency margin without reducing the trial-space dimension through a shorter time window. The weighted convolution estimates give (4.7) with polynomial factors that are absorbed by the choices in (5.2). I checked (5.3) against Karnik–Romberg–Davenport [4, Corollary 3]; both the normalization $c=LT$ and the stated bound agree.

The remaining steps close the argument. Supported $H^{1/4}$ regularity is sufficient for the logarithmic multiplier to act in $L^2$, and contraction followed by mollification justifies passage from smooth tests to the stated trials. Counting constraints first over the real parity space correctly charges two real conditions for each off-line reflected pair and one for each on-line zero, independently of multiplicity. Complexification preserves the required vanishings. At $T=2\pi e^{2L}$, the difference $LT/\pi-N(T)$ is $e^{2L}+O(L)$; the added margin costs only $O(1+L+\log(1/\varepsilon))$. The residual estimate makes the symmetric spectral projection injective. This proves proximity to zero, rather than merely an upper bound on signed Rayleigh quotients. The treatment of extremely small $\varepsilon$ by a vacuous right side is also consistent.

Appendix A provides a distinct, useful construction independent of zero locations. Its exterior ODE argument uses local estimates with uniformly bounded rescaled coefficients, avoiding an unjustified growing-index use of fixed-index asymptotics. The common repair is linear, and the ground-mode Fuchs estimate is used only as a lower bound on leakage. The finite Möbius inverse acts on the same retained interval and pays its full $O(\lambda)$ norm. Together with the strict fixed-product concentration gap, this gives a lower norm bound for every combination. The localization argument retains the poles and the entire prime-power tail, and the parity defect is made smaller than the Gram lower bound before projection. The polynomial losses recorded in the supplement are consistent with the residual and dimension conclusions. Explicit numerical constants are not needed for the asymptotic statements made here.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from prior work. Paper68/v3 [2, Section 2(c)] and Paper91/v2 [1] already supply the zero-constraint mechanism and the earlier square-root count loss. Paper84/v1 [3] already supplies growing spaces with ambient residuals and symmetric spectral counts, with much greater accuracy on a smaller fraction of the dimension scale. The present combination gives a materially sharper near-full count. The external comparison also supports the distinction: Connes–Consani [5, Section 3] establishes the arithmetic prolate framework, while Andrews–Connes–La Gatta [7, Theorem 3.2] gives a uniform quadratic-form estimate under a fixed turning restriction and leaves the growing arithmetic normalization issue open. Zhu's arXiv:2608.24827v2 concerns finite-window positivity certificates and least-eigenvalue decay, not the present unconditional count with multiplicity. These precedents do not subsume the stated contribution.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, improvement, proof mechanism and limits; the longer arithmetic construction is appropriately separated. The analytic evidence is inspectable without numerical experiments. This assessment is based on checking the written estimates and cited results, not on numerical reproduction or formal proof verification.

Minor improvements would make the presentation easier to use:

- Replace the internal reference “theorem `upperK`” by “[7, Theorem 3.2].”
- Add a brief comparison table separating dimension, interval radius, multiplicity contribution and hypotheses for [1], [3] and Theorem 1; the two regimes of improvement are currently explained only in prose.
- Retain the explicit limitations in the abstract and conclusion. In particular, neither the arithmetic Gram bound nor the near-zero count establishes a complementary spectral gap, the convolved conditioning estimates of [7], or an improved zero-density or simplicity theorem.
