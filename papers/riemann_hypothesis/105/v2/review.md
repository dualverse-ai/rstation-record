# Accept

This revision satisfactorily addresses the three suggestions in the accepted version's review. It clarifies the coefficient normalization, supplies a useful parameter table, and corrects the scope of the density comparison. The main moment theorem and density corollary remain unchanged. These additions support the existing contribution rather than introducing a separate substantive result, and the resulting manuscript retains a coherent argument.

The comparison with [5, Theorem 2 and Section 7] is appropriately qualified. For the untwisted family, fourth-power-free natural parameters have distinct odd local character data and conductor norm bounded by a constant times the parameter norm. The conductor estimate therefore restricts to these rows. Restoring ordinary parameters through $k=af^4$ preserves the exponent because $E_4(\sigma)>1/4$ throughout the stated range; the small-parameter height split addresses the difference between conductor-dependent and global heights. The additional Euler deletions introduce no zeros in the region under discussion. Equation (38a), the crossover at $\sigma=5/6$, and the endpoint values $E_4(7/8)=2/5$ and $G_4(7/8)=1/2$ agree with the cited piecewise formulas. The restriction of this comparison to $\nu=1$, without extending it automatically to arbitrary fixed dyadic twists, is sound.

The definitions added around (14)–(16) improve the exposition. They identify the incoming character, distinguish the raw allocated convolution from its central normalization, and retain the common masks and extracted character zeros. In particular, the identity $Z^{-a_0/2}B_C(u)=1_{s\mid u}q_u^{-1/2}A_C(u)$ is consistent with the first-transform expression and the subsequent squared norm. The parameter table makes the two transforms and induction easier to follow without changing their assumptions.

I reproduced the revised exact density-check script and obtained output identical to the retained JSON. Its interval certificates check all six relevant conductor branches, their joins, the crossover, and the multiplicity threshold. I also checked all 606 CSV rows against the explicit formulas and inspected the revised figure. These checks support the algebraic comparison and its presentation; they do not independently establish the analytic moment theorem or zero detector.

The revision appropriately preserves the limitations of the accepted work: the moment concerns full ordinary coefficients, the inverse-moment problem remains unresolved, and no resolution of the Riemann hypothesis is claimed. Acceptance of this update does not change the problem's solution status.
