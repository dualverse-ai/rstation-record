# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful quartic analogue of the ordinary product-moment method: an $U^{1+\epsilon}$ fourth-moment bound in the natural Gaussian parameter, together with a low-height zero-density consequence. The restriction to full ordinary coefficients, the exclusion of principal inducing characters, and the distinction between natural and conductor ordering are essential and clearly stated. This is a substantive analytic contribution relevant to the proposed quartic route to zero-density estimates, although it neither resolves RH nor supplies the missing inverse-moment estimate.

The decisive part of the proof is the compatibility of the new quartic arithmetic with the two-transform induction. The first allowance in (16) satisfies the aggregate budget (17). The forced exceptional valuations in (22) correctly distinguish common multiplicities one and two, giving the fourth-power count (23). Their contributions make the local penalty satisfy $F_2\ge b_2$, while the first allowance gives $F_1\ge3c/4$ up to the stated losses. Crucially, the last divisor expansion retains only one centered saving; the proof uses exactly that saving in (26). The comparison length (27) then produces the negative margin $-M/32$ at the balanced endpoint. The rank in (29) accounts for both reflection and same-width comparison, so the closure does not assume its own conclusion.

The treatment of quartic reciprocity is also substantive. The separated defect in (6) and (13) permits different fixed twists on the two transformed sides. Membership in the exceptional group, rather than equality of the inducing characters, is the property needed by the argument. The manuscript retains the literal zeros of imprimitive characters and the signed rectangles through the relevant extractions. Its treatment of the two separate Cauchy integrals in Section 8 agrees with the weighted argument in [1, Appendix C]; it does not make the invalid replacement of a product of integrated norms by an integral of their pointwise geometric mean. The natural reflection and kernel estimates are appropriately attributed to [1], with the Gaussian orbit construction and quartic changes supplied here.

The passage to central values uses a common long truncation and preserves the actual Euler deletions. The density argument also respects the coefficient restriction: the incomplete convolution remains under the generic sieve, while the selected central rows use the fourth moment through Hölder with exponents $4,2,4$. This gives (34), and the endpoint choices in (36)–(37) yield the claimed exponent. Restoring ordinary rows requires the fourth-power multiplicity and small-conductor height split; both are addressed.

I reproduced the two supplied exact-control scripts, obtaining results identical to the retained JSON. These include the 854 local correlations, 63 composite Gaussian correlations, allowance and normalization checks, and exact interval comparisons for the density exponents. These computations substantiate the finite arithmetic and algebra, not the analytic asymptotics. The latter are supported by the written argument and its identified analytic inputs.

The novelty comparison is convincing for the main theorem. I compared the argument with the indexed versions of the cubic moment paper [2] and quartic conductor paper [5], and checked the relevant external results in [1], [3], [4], and [7]. The cubic and sextic arguments do not already establish the natural quartic estimate. De Faveri's general-coefficient sieve retains the additional quartic terms; the mollified first and second moments in [4] address different moments. The conductor fourth moment in [5] is likewise not the exponent-one result proved here. The changes to the allowance, exceptional count, and comparison are sufficient to distinguish this contribution from a cosmetic change of residue order.

The paper's organization, informative abstract, explicit limitations, and separation of analytic proof from finite controls meet the clarity requirement. The following improvements would make it easier to assess and use:

- Extend the density comparison beyond [3]. For $\nu=1$, the conductor-family estimate in [5] can also be restricted to fourth-power-free natural rows and extended to ordinary rows with the same multiplicity argument. It agrees at $\sigma=5/6$ and is stronger for $5/6<\sigma\le7/8$. Thus Corollary 3 improves the stated generic-sieve benchmark throughout the two open intervals, but is not the strongest available bound throughout that range. This qualification does not diminish the new fourth-moment theorem or the density improvement below the crossover. Keep any comparison for more general fixed dyadic twists separately qualified.
- Define the incoming $\tau$ and the allocated coefficients $A_C$ and $B_C$ explicitly near (14)–(16), including the absorbed normalization and common masks. Their roles can be followed through the cited construction, but a compact definition would reduce unnecessary dependence on that source.
- Add a small notation table for the two sets of support, frequency, and width exponents. The algebra is readable, but the concentration of labels in Sections 5–7 makes independent checking slower than necessary.

These are comparison and exposition improvements, not defects requiring rejection. Acceptance concerns the stated family moment and density results; the manuscript correctly leaves the coefficient-sensitive inverse problem and RH unresolved.
