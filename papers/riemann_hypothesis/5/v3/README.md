# Structured cubic and sextic means for angular Gauss coefficients

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 3 · 2026-10-17T03:51:31.530722Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/5/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove coefficient-specific mean-square estimates for angular Gauss sums over the Eisenstein integers. For $C_\ell(n)=\alpha(n)^\ell\gamma_2(n)$ with any fixed integer $\ell\ne0$, a normalized smooth sum over squarefree columns of norm length $D$, twisted by sextic residue characters, has squared mean $K(KD)^\varepsilon$ over all nonzero element rows of norm at most $K$, for every $D\ge1$. With a normalized positive average of fourth-power labels of norm $F$ and a column puncture $Q$ fixed during the average, the bound is $(KDFNQ)^\varepsilon\min\{K+DF,K+KF\sqrt{NQ}\}$. Sextic Poisson cancels the Gauss coefficient and exposes a multiplicative inverse polynomial. Complete common-divisor expansions and a divisor bound for the new frequency fibre first yield $K+DF$. Combining this with a completed-theta reflection estimate before exact cube inversion removes the long-column term. The same estimate controls a complete unmarked quadratic-theta norm, including every cube factor and the three reflected cusp families. We retain the earlier cubic-character bound $N+B^3/N+N^{2/3}B^{1/2}$ for $\mu\alpha^{\pm2}\gamma_2$. The analytic inputs are the pinned quasi-Riemann-hypothesis manuscript's inverse-moment and theta-reflection framework and its fixed-angular extension proved here; its final zero-free theorem is not used. These results concern prescribed coefficients, not general large-sieve operator norms. Prime-divisor marks leave a distinct marked inverse polynomial, so no new zero-free region or resolution of RH is claimed. Finite Bessel and Gauss computations check conventions and zero masks.
