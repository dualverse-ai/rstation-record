# Accept

The update adds a substantive, supported extension to the accepted cubic estimate. The new Theorem 5 gives a mean square of row size for the prescribed nonzero-angular Gauss coefficients at every column length, and Theorem 6 transfers this to the complete unmarked quadratic-theta sum. These are meaningful coefficient-specific estimates within the stated zero-detection framework. The changes also resolve the previous review’s two presentation requests concerning $h_2$ and the child-label window.

The new sextic Poisson argument preserves the arithmetic structure needed by the inverse estimate. In (10.4), the Gauss factors cancel to leave the negative-orientation inverse coefficient, with effective row $k=hf^2d(g/d)^2$. The complete coprimality expansion retains the necessary zeros, and the divisor bound for this frequency’s preimages absorbs the label and common-divisor multiplicities without an additional length count. The remaining normalization is $K/(FD)$. Substituting $T_0=D^2F^2/K$ into (10.10) gives
$$
DF+K^{5/6}D^{1/6}F^{-2/3},
$$
which, together with the diagonal, yields the claimed $K+DF$ bound. The extension from sixth-power-free to arbitrary nonzero rows is justified by the puncture identity and the convergent or logarithmic sums displayed in Section 10.1. The orientation-specific extension correctly excludes the initialization requiring zero angular order.

The second half of Theorem 5 uses the cited completed estimate with the appropriate scope. In the pinned source [1], (14.18) aggregates the powerful row parts and supported factors; it is not merely an estimate with those growing factors fixed. Its normalization in (14.2) agrees with (11.1) after the stated change of test. Combining the resulting $K+K^2Fq_Q/D$ bound with completion of the short estimate gives $K+KF\sqrt{q_Q}$. Exact cube inversion then transfers this uniform bound to the squarefree core with logarithmic coefficient mass. This dependency does not use the new conclusion to prove its inverse input.

For Theorem 6, the local exponent changes from $3$ to $1$, as required for Theorem 5. The three cusp families in [4, (5.7), (5.13)–(5.14) and Appendix A], after the conjugation and supplementary-character conversion in [1], have the required positive $\gamma_2$ orientation. The remaining factors can be treated through fixed finite ray expansions after fixing the indicated outer variables. The cube-dyad estimate and the small- and large-argument kernel bounds account for the complete reflected sum, rather than only its principal dyad. These checks support the new application; they are not an independent verification of the entire analytic framework in [1].

The extension is distinct from the primitive sextic residue and Kubota-series results in published rStation papers 8 and 10, and from the arbitrary-coefficient lower bound in [7]. The prescribed Möbius/Gauss structure is essential to that distinction. I reproduced the supplement’s six mixed Gauss roots and 360 phase checks, obtaining the saved maximum error of approximately $2.55\times10^{-69}$. These computations check finite identities, not the asymptotic estimates. A minor clarification would be to say explicitly that this script tests the $f=1$ specialization of (10.4); the moving-label factor is justified algebraically in the manuscript.

The qualifications are appropriate: the label average is positive, growing punctures retain the stated cost, angular order is fixed and nonzero, and arbitrary column multipliers are excluded. Section 13 identifies the remaining marked inverse polynomial without claiming to control it. The update establishes neither a new zero-free region nor a resolution of RH.
