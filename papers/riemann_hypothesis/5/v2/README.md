# A cubic mean-square bound for angular sextic Gauss sums

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 2 · 2026-10-16T11:45:16.074428Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/5/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study a cubic-character mean square whose coefficient is the angular sextic Gauss sum $\overline{\alpha(b)}\gamma_{-1}(b)$ over squarefree Eisenstein ideals. For column norm length $B$ and unrestricted element-row norm length $N$, with the column normalization $q_b^{-1/2}$, we prove the upper bound $(NB)^\varepsilon(N+B^3/N+N^{2/3}B^{1/2})$. It reaches the diagonal scale $N$ for $N\ge B^{3/2}$. On squarefree rows, the generic cubic large sieve reaches that scale only for $N\ge B^2$; at $N=B^{3/2}$ our estimate saves $B^{1/6}$ in its principal exponent. The result allows fixed finite ray factors, fixed punctures with explicit subpower cost, and the Euler weight $\varphi(b)/q_b$. We first extend the unmarked inverse-moment argument in OpenAI's September 30 quasi-Riemann-hypothesis manuscript to the fixed angular coefficients $\mu\alpha^{\pm2}$. Higher pure derivatives of cubic theta preserve the reflection's conductor power, and the two Poisson transformations preserve the angular coefficient class. A further cubic Poisson step cancels the Gauss coefficient. Its complete common-divisor calculation sends those divisors into the new row index, leaving only divisor multiplicity. The coefficient occurs in a coherent-label branch of a mixed inverse/plain detector, but controlling that branch at all simultaneous scales remains a separate problem. We claim no new zero-free region or resolution of the Riemann hypothesis. Finite Bessel and Gauss-sum computations check normalizations and zero masks; the exponent is proved analytically.
