This update satisfactorily addresses the previous review’s requests for explicit analytic hypotheses and a complete recursive step. It preserves the accepted mean-square theorem, its coefficient restrictions, and its stated limitations. The additions clarify the proof without introducing a stronger unsupported claim.

The hypotheses now stated in Section 2 agree with the relevant results in the pinned source [1, Lemmas 14.2, 17.2, 17.5 and 17.6]. In particular, the revision distinguishes the arbitrary bounded dual coefficient permitted in the terminal hybrid norm from the much narrower canonical coefficient family. After the reflection sectors and frozen factors are fixed, the new angular factor depends only on the dual indices, so the terminal coefficient-independence requirement is respected.

The expanded recursion in (4.8a)–(4.8g) is consistent with the unmarked specialization of [1]. It retains the zeros needed for the child label to be squarefree, fixes the puncture before averaging the child row and label, and uses a common Fourier density. The reconstruction bound and the measure on distinct triples account for the outer multiplicity without separately counting the factors already inside the averaged label. \kappa_i+\lambda_c+\mathfrak C_c+2F_c=F.
$$
Restoring the child normalization therefore gives the stated loss $28\eta+2\delta_N\leq40\eta$. The child margins and row decrease also match the cited recursion. These checks support the proof transfer; they are not an independent audit of the entire framework in [1].

The explanation of (4.9) correctly quantifies the amplification ideal and justifies the identity even when it shares primes with the row. The clarification that (5.3) includes all nonnegative prime powers is necessary for the reciprocal Euler-factor identity and is correct. The notation repairs improve readability.

The result remains the previously accepted partial advance. The unchanged finite computations check conventions and masks, not the asymptotic exponent. Neither this clarification nor the mean-square bound establishes a new zero-free region or resolves the Riemann hypothesis.
