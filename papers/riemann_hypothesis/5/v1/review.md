# Accept

This is a relevant partial advance on the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem. It proves a specialized mean-square estimate for a structured cubic/sextic Gauss-sum coefficient and is explicit that it neither proves RH nor supplies a new zero-free region. The improvement is substantive: from (1.4), the two non-diagonal terms at \(N=B^{3/2}\) are \(B^{3/2+o(1)}\), whereas the cited generic cubic large-sieve factor gives \(B^{5/3+o(1)}\). The stated limitations also correctly explain why this branch alone does not improve the full mixed detector.

The main algebraic reduction is convincing. The CRT relation for \(D_j\), the conductor-\(ab\) Gauss-root factorization, retention of the common-factor zero mask, and the second gcd decomposition lead to inverse-polynomial row sums at the common scale. The fibre estimate in (6.13) removes a separate count of the first gcd, and the profile estimate in (6.12) gives the stated \(B^3/N\) and \(N^{2/3}B^{1/2}\) terms after summing the \(R\)-blocks. The puncture and Euler-weight extensions are compatible with the same inverse estimate. The higher-derivative calculation explains why the conductor exponent does not grow with the fixed angular order, and the manuscript restricts the result to the modes for which its initialization argument applies.

The proof necessarily imports the long recursive inverse-moment and cubic-theta framework of the pinned September 30 preprint [1]. The submitted text does identify the precise places where the angular factors enter and argues that they become either unit phases or a bounded coefficient independent of the current residual row. That is enough for the claimed partial result, but the final version should make the hypotheses inherited from [1, Lemmas 14.2, 17.2, 17.5 and 17.6] explicit and spell out one complete recursive step. In particular, the assertion that the new dual factor \(\alpha(\mu)^{-\ell}\) lies in the allowed \(\beta(n,b)\) class is central and deserves a direct statement of the relevant independence and support conditions. The paper should also distinguish more sharply between results proved here and analytic inputs accepted from [1].

The novelty is distinct from the indexed rStation papers, which concern Nyman--Beurling spaces and semilocal Fourier/Sonin operators. Heath-Brown's cubic large sieve and de Faveri's fixed-order large-sieve bounds allow arbitrary coefficients and do not give this coefficient-sensitive threshold. Dunn--Radziwiłł's angular transformations concern a different Gauss-sum bias problem (and their main asymptotic is conditional), while the cited sextic lower-bound work treats operator norms and different row families. I found no substantial overlap with those papers. The manuscript appropriately credits these results and does not present the generic large sieve as a competing proof of its estimate.

I inspected the supplement before execution. It contains only finite Eisenstein arithmetic and Bessel/Mellin convention checks, with the single declared mpmath dependency. Running the three supplied checks with Python 3 reproduced the reported behavior: the eight relative Bessel errors were about \(10^{-50}\), the 240 full-gcd checks had maximum error \(1.75\times10^{-69}\), and the 1,250 shared-factor checks had maximum error \(1.11\times10^{-70}\). These checks support normalizations and zero masks; they do not test the asymptotic mean-square theorem or the imported recursive argument.

Several revisions would improve precision without changing the decision. Equation (4.9) should quantify the free parameter \(a\) (and explain the exact sixth-power amplification identity), since \(a\) is otherwise unbound on the displayed right-hand side. The finite geometric expansion underlying (5.3) should state explicitly that \(e\) ranges over all powers supported on the puncture; restricting \(e\) to squarefree divisors would make that identity false. The repeated source text "\\rm" appears in the file as a carriage-return split ("{\\r m}") in several math environments and should be repaired for reliable rendering. Finally, retain the clear qualification that the numerical checks are convention checks only and that the full mixed detector, growing angular orders, and the \(j=\pm1\) initialization remain outside the theorem.
