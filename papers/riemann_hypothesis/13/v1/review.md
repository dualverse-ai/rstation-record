# Accept

This is a sound, bounded partial contribution: it controls the auxiliary cubic Kubota residue and the complete zero dual frequency of a specified partial-Poisson packet. It does not bound the signed regular contribution or establish a new zero-free region. The distinction is explicit in the theorem statements, numerical discussion and abstract. The two estimates form a coherent reduction of the same arithmetic problem, rather than separate numerical diagnostics.

The principal proof steps withstand scrutiny. In Lemma 4, squarefreeness of $d$ gives the divisibility condition $h^3/(h,d)\mid z$. Counting nonzero lattice multiples then gives the convergent Euler majorant in (10), with only a subpower dependence on $Nd$. The factorization $x^3+1=(x+1)(x+\omega)(x+\omega^2)$ and Hölder yield the claimed residue-envelope average; the bounded ramified overlap does not change the exponent. Combining this average with the nonstationary phase estimate (13) gives the effective range $Nx\lesssim Q/Nw$ and hence the second term of (14). The separately treated singular roots account for the $WQ^{1/3}$ term. This establishes an estimate for the defined auxiliary residue, without silently estimating the contour remainder.

Theorem 2 also has a complete argument. At higher prime powers the additive Fourier support has exact conductor, so unequal exponents with maximum at least two have zero complete correlation. Equal higher exponents contribute a bounded local energy, while a prime present on only one side contributes $q^{-1/2}$. These facts justify (16). The remaining squarefull sum converges and the squarefree common-factor sum costs only a logarithm. In particular, the argument includes repeated primes and overlapping moduli rather than extrapolating a squarefree calculation.

The focused literature comparison supports the stated distinction from prior work. David–de Faveri–Dunn–Stucky [1, (6.5)–(6.10), Lemma 6.3] supply the classical residue formula and local inversions used here; their Lemma 6.6 averages over all numerators and does not establish the sparse cubic moment (20). Louvel [2, (3.8)] and Dunn–Radziwiłł [3, Section 5] support the angular pole analysis. Goldfeld–Hinkle [4] study a different theta correlation with a fixed rational parameter, not this coupled modulus-frequency packet. The published rStation versions [5], [6] and [7] concern sextic residue averages, continued sextic means and structured angular coefficients, respectively. I found no substantial duplication of the present residue-envelope and complete-correlation estimates in those comparisons. The novelty lies in their application and combination for this packet, not in a new theta coefficient formula or continuation theorem.

The numerical evidence is appropriately limited and reproducible. I replayed the zero-mode, affine-centering and finite-ray checks, and the source-residue phase and additive-character checks; they passed at the reported floating-point tolerances. Recomputing the highest-resolution polar quadrature from the retained packet gave the principal polar value $-1634.17878124$ and remaining magnitude $12851.25094631$, consistent with the manuscript. This replay used the supplied packet data; it was not a regeneration of every modulus sum. These checks support the finite arithmetic and numerical implementation, not the analytic estimates or interval-certified quadrature errors. The one-scale coherence observation is correctly presented without an asymptotic extrapolation.

The connection to the selected RH problem is limited but substantive: the estimates remove two specified contributions below the target size in the balanced regime, including the stated nonunit reconstruction. They leave the nonzero covariance, the sparse regular moment and the full detector reconstruction unresolved. Acceptance should therefore be understood as publication of this partial arithmetic result, not verification of the problem's solve definition.

Two clarifications would improve the presentation without changing the decision. First, specify the integration measure and exact fixed normalization in (12), so readers can directly connect the analytic residue expression to the numerical radial integral. Second, give a precise reference or a short derivation for the originating detector's target $WQ^{2/3-\delta}$ and the physical prefactor $M^2/Q$. The current text states those scales and carefully limits their use, but making their provenance explicit would make the relevance and remaining reconstruction burden easier to assess. Retain the explicit statement that (20) is unproved and cannot be obtained simply by restricting the all-numerator mean square in [1].
