# The lattice-sum bound of the Alpöge release in natural language: a box count in totally complex fields, and the LatticeSums clause of paper 204

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T06:47:45.100615Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/216/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

This paper is a natural-language counterpart of part of a published Lean proof, the riemann-hypothesis release at commit e7c8972c. The release's tower theorem needs a bound, called LatticeSums, on sums of the majorant maj(ρ,l) = ∏_v min(1, (ρ_v/|l|_v)^K) over the nonzero points of a lattice. Paper 204 states this bound as part of its Hypothesis 5.1 and does not prove it. We give complete proofs of the three release theorems that supply it. First, BoxSum: in a totally complex field with d infinite places, if Θ > 2, K ≥ 3 and Θ^d = √N(b)/∏x_w, then the sum over nonzero l in the fractional ideal b is at most 800^d Θ^{-d(K-1)}. The proof uses only N(b) ≤ |N(l)|, a box count with constant 50^d, and a dyadic decomposition. Second, a rescaled form for sizes whose product is at most (a/Θ)^d √N(b). Third, LatticeSumsJoint: LatticeSums with ε = (800 a^{K-5/2} Θ^{-(K-5/2)})^f for every tower data whose lattices lie in fractional ideals of the right size (the condition IdealSizes). At the release's concrete instance, the Lean theorem instance_sizes supplies IdealSizes with a = 2 and K = 5; we summarize its proof, prove two of its six hypotheses, and leave four to a companion paper. This gives 204's clause with C_1 = 800·2^{5/2}. We match the two statements term by term. A supplement checks the constants numerically in Q(ζ_8), together with wrong variants that fail. The other clauses of 204's Hypothesis 5.1 are not treated.
