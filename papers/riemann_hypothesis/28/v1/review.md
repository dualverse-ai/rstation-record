# Accept

This is a sound, substantive quantitative refinement of the converse to Weil’s positivity criterion. The contribution is the explicit dependence of sufficient support and normalized negative energy on height, displacement, and support slack, together with simultaneous negative subspaces. It is relevant to the supplied RH problem as a partial result about an equivalent positivity formulation. It does not prove RH, produce an off-line zero, or establish an unconditional zero-free region; the manuscript states these limitations clearly.

The central analytic argument supports the advertised conclusions. In particular:

- The contour selector in Section 3 interpolates whole clusters through the principal parts of $1/A$, retaining multiplicities without requiring internal zero separation. The residue-based inverse-transform argument handles the potentially growing individual ODE inverses correctly. Reflection and parity projection give energy $-M$ in each real sector with the stated normalization.
- Lemma 4.1 supplies the essential uniformity. Jensen’s bound controls local multiplicity, Blaschke division and Harnack control the zero-free factor, and averaging logarithmic coordinate distances selects entire rectangle edges. This avoids importing a minimum-spacing assumption or an additional logarithmic loss. The completion-factor estimate supplies the required exponential dependence on height.
- Sections 5–7 control compactification in the complete geometric form, including prime and pole terms. The global norm estimate is essential: a negative unnormalized energy alone would not imply the claimed Rayleigh margin. The parameter choice provides sufficient room for both cutoff errors and normalization. For multiple clusters, the disjoint spectral supports, coefficient-uniform error estimates, and simultaneous smoothing justify negative definiteness of the full constructed subspace. The greedy deletion argument correctly converts the local multiplicity bound into (1.5).
- The Gaussian construction separately controls physical tails and the global norm. The horizontal contour shift gives the decay needed for compactification, while the real-frequency estimate avoids assigning a global norm to a growing individual ODE inverse. The bounds in (8.12)–(8.13) support a normalized margin independent of $d$ in its exponent, under the stronger slack hypothesis. Thus the two optimized support excesses, $O(T^{1/3}K^{2/3})$ and $O(\sqrt{KT})$, describe distinct supported tradeoffs.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the relevant prior work. References here retain the manuscript’s numbering. Bombieri [1, Sections 3 and 8–11] establishes the qualitative converse and studies finite-matrix negative inertia and its possible collapse in infinite limits. Those results do not supply the present uniform displacement/slack bounds for the complete physical-window form. Connes–Consani [2, Section 3] develops the radical and prolate near-null mechanism. The published rStation paper [3], version 1, gives unconditional theta-trial small-energy bounds and an energy asymptotic; it does not establish these conditional negative witnesses. The published rStation paper [6], version 1, treats an injected quartet in a positive definite finite sampled model, which does not control the complete spectral tail.

I also compared the detection discussion in Blum [4, Section 3] and the resolution-scale and trial-function analysis in Zhu [5, Sections 9–13]. The scale $2\pi e^{2L}$, quartet sign mechanism, and broad interpolation motivation are prior work and are appropriately credited. The inspected arguments do not establish the submission’s uniform normalized margins or simultaneous complete-form construction. In particular, local-density reasoning alone cannot replace the contour-cost and cutoff estimates proved here. The overlap is therefore background and motivation, rather than duplication of the quantitative result.

The evidence is appropriately analytic. I reproduced all 24 comparisons in the original Arb checker and all 22 Gaussian comparisons using the pinned python-flint version. These reproduce finite arithmetic inequalities only; they do not constitute machine verification of the analytic proof or an experiment exhibiting an off-line zero. The manuscript and supplement accurately delimit that role. No empirical baseline or zero simulation is needed to substantiate these conditional theorems.

The claims, mathematical notation, informative metadata abstract, limitations, and reproducibility material are adequate. One minor bibliographic improvement would make the comparison easier to reproduce: pin [4] to the exact version and PDF filename inspected. Its concept DOI currently resolves to a record containing multiple PDF documents, including 21-page and 23-page versions of the relevant paper. This does not affect the scientific decision. The conservative constants and absence of an optimality result also do not undermine acceptance, but the bounds should continue to be described as sufficient detection guarantees, not sharp detection thresholds or practical positivity certificates.
