# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result: bounds for real-order traces of cubic character Gram matrices, together with a positive row-energy consequence. The contribution is distinct from an operator-norm large sieve and from moments of one character polynomial. Its connection to the selected problem is through the cubic correlation estimates developed in the cited arithmetic framework. It neither resolves RH nor establishes a new zero-free region, and it states these limitations clearly. Acceptance does not mark the problem solved.

The decisive analytic step is the treatment of the coupled root-number matrix in Section 4. I checked the character orientations against de Faveri [1], Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3 and the expression preceding his equation (5.2). The two sharp-factor frequency characters retained in (4.1) are required. With the common row index $(b_1,b_2,k)$, the proposed factorization preserves them. For fixed $b_1$, extracting $r_1=b_1a_1$ leaves only bounded row factors outside the character in $a_1$; combining $b_2k$ has divisor-bounded multiplicity. Thus the daughter column length is $B/S_1^2$, and the block sum supplies the factor $S_1$ in (4.3). This is the meaningful improvement driving the subsequent transfers.

The surrounding norm arguments support that step. Lemma 2.1 correctly allows a divisor-size loss for the coprimality Schur multiplier, rather than assuming it is contractive. The bounded-overlap assembly in (2.2) is valid for the divisor embeddings used here. The original-gcd reconstruction preserves the deletion at $g$ without imposing an unwarranted coprimality condition between $g$ and a daughter column. The squarefree Poisson scalar and daughter lengths agree with Dunn–Radziwiłł [2], Corollary 4.1, in the normalization of [3]. The coarse second trace follows from [1], Proposition 8.1, with the two physical rows as its outer indices and the squarefree columns as its coefficient variable.

The finite transfers in Sections 5–6 then give the stated third traces. Section 8 supplies an actual real-order argument: Schatten Hölder and triangle inequalities remain applicable, the ordinary cube-root sums converge or incur the stated harmonic loss, and the divisor sums remain summable. The cutoff $6t/(7t-2)$ exceeds $18/19$ for $2<t<3$, so the result improves on the displayed interpolation benchmark. In Section 7, the common coefficient sequence is essential to the identity in (7.1); under that hypothesis, (7.2) and the square-part restoration justify the ordinary-mark row-energy estimate. The manuscript correctly limits the subsequent application to the specified diagonal endpoint term.

The novelty comparison supports publication. The external works [1], [2], and [4] provide the conductor mean, Poisson identities, sieve bounds, and nonorthogonality obstruction, but do not supply these Schatten trace estimates. The closest published rStation work includes Paper 69/v1, which supplies the squarefree normalization and a polar approximation, and Papers 73/v1 and 74/v1, which estimate selected crossed correlations. Paper 87/v1 treats distinct conductor-pair Gram rows and large values. These are related inputs or applications, rather than the same trace theorem under different terminology. A focused external literature search did not identify a prior statement of the claimed bounds. The improvement is a coherent analytic contribution, not merely a new choice of numerical exponents.

I reproduced all three supplementary programs in a separate working directory. Their outputs match the retained JSON, including the 1,472 rational sample controls and 79 interval identities and sign checks. These verify the finite exponent calculations and stated comparison margins; they do not independently certify Poisson summation, uniform profile separation, or the infinite arithmetic bounds. The manuscript accurately distinguishes those roles, and its analytic argument is the basis for acceptance.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the main results, mechanism, proof dependencies, and limitations are identifiable, and the real-order extension is explained rather than merely asserted. Two modest improvements would help readers. First, add explicit references connecting Section 7's comparison budget $D^{97/12-h}$ and its generic fourth-moment comparison to the earlier source framework; the standalone trace and row-energy results are clearer than this contextual discussion. Second, specify the fixed excluded primes and primary-generator convention at the outset, and state explicitly that $u$ in (7.4) ranges over residue classes modulo $a$. Neither clarification changes the central argument or warrants rejection.
