# Accept

The paper establishes a useful, limited computational advance for an RH-equivalent approximation problem. Its main result is a rational enclosure of the **infinite-horizon** distance, including a rigorous tail allowance and a lower-bound correction for an inexact optimizer. The certified finite-$N$ separation from the squarefree subspace is supported. Neither these separations nor the prime/composite cancellation diagnostics establish an asymptotic approximation rate or resolve RH; the manuscript appropriately disclaims such a resolution.

The central argument is sound. The extended Fourier representation includes the constant mode, and $r_0=1$ supplies the essential constant in (9). The evaluation of $U(-1)$, summation by parts, and weighted Hilbert bound give (13)–(14). The completion of squares supplies a positive diagonal Hessian lower bound for $M\ge N^2$, which justifies the gradient-defect certificate (16). The coordinate inversion in (24) is correct, and its preservation of squarefree support establishes (25). Restriction of the quadratic envelopes to the stated coordinate subsets then justifies the finite comparisons in Theorem 3.

I reproduced all three supplementary programs within the probe limits. The certificate endpoints, subspace separations, and reported diagnostic tables agree with the retained results; differences in the main output were confined to timings and tiny floating-point identity-check errors. Independently written exact-arithmetic checks recovered the boundary identity from complete periods for $N=2,\ldots,8$ and directly recomputed the rational certificate endpoints at $(N,M)=(10,100),(20,400)$. These checks corroborate the implementation; the general tail and convexity assertions depend on the analytical proof, not on finite testing.

The novelty is modest but identifiable. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, rStation [13], especially (44), (44′), and (47)–(48′), already contains the periodic Hilbert primitive, retained boundary correction, global-spacing bounds, and finite-prefix rational certificates. The coordinate dictionary and Bernoulli machinery also substantially precede this submission in [9,12]. External comparison with Báez-Duarte [3], Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [5], and the rational autocorrelation formula in [20, Proposition 89] confirms that the criterion, logarithmic Möbius profile, and exact Gram geometry are established background. The present contribution is their explicit extension to a rational, coercive certificate for the unconstrained full norm, with reproducible applications to several subspaces. This is more useful than additional floating-point distance estimates. The quotient-hyperbola identities are elementary supporting decompositions, not a separate breakthrough in Möbius cancellation.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication. They do not invalidate the principal certificates.

1. **Correct the density statement at the start of Section 4.** The stated spaces consist of integer step functions and cannot be dense in all of $\mathcal H$. Nor should squarefree target approximation be replaced casually by density in the entire step space. State the RH equivalence as $1\in\overline{\bigcup_N V_N^{\mathrm{sqf}}}$, equivalently $d_N^{\mathrm{sqf},2}\to0$. Keep the finite separations explicitly restricted to the tested values. When discussing [5], name its RH and reciprocal-derivative-moment assumptions rather than leaving “conditional” unspecified.

2. **Distinguish exact certificates from numerical summaries consistently.** The unscaled intervals in Tables 1 and 3 are outward-rounded rational certificates. The final column of Table 3 is generated using `math.log`, floating-point conversion, and nearest rounding, so its label “Certified” is unsupported by that computation. Label it a numerical summary or supply interval bounds for the logarithm and directed rounding. Likewise, the supplementary $N=3$ check uses a finite sum plus a mean-tail approximation; it is a numerical periodicity check, not an exact evaluation of the infinite norm. Neither issue affects the rational inequalities used to prove strict separation.

3. **Tighten two short proof passages.** In the proof of (10), torus distance is not bounded below by the unadjusted absolute difference. Use $\min_{j\in\mathbb Z}|b\ell-b'k-jk\ell|/(k\ell)$; the integer numerator is nonzero for distinct fractions modulo one. For (21), the argument on only the upper half of the window establishes the order of growth but does not alone establish the displayed leading coefficient. Add the elementary partial-summation estimate over the whole window. These are local proof clarifications with the stated conclusions intact.

4. **Make the comparisons and reproduction instructions precise.** The $2.11$–$2.63$ width improvement is relative to the explicitly chosen baseline (4); it does not isolate the gain from local spacing versus retaining the boundary term, already present in [13]. A boundary-retaining unweighted comparison would clarify this. The prime-power and first-radical-harmonic spaces are not nested: $8$ belongs only to the former and $12$ only to the latter, although their certified distance ordering remains valid. Remove contrary “filtration” comments in the code. Document that the squarefree program reads `results/transcendental_free_infinite_certificates.json` relative to the working directory, independently of `--output-dir`. Finally, Table 2's program rounds $N^{3/2}$ rather than taking its floor, so the stated cutoff convention should match the actual experiment.
