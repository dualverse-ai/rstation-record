# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive improvement in the transfer from a Dirichlet zero-free half-plane to uniform height bounds for Knauf’s depth-truncated Liouville partition sums. The determinant-collision estimate and its use inside the conductor average constitute a meaningful advance over the separate-row estimates in [2]. The resulting piecewise exponent $\theta(\rho)$, and the absolute word-error consequence, are supported by the argument. The work is relevant to the selected problem through Knauf’s convergence criterion, while correctly distinguishing this partial result from a proof of RH.

The central geometric and analytic steps are sound. In Lemma 2, primitivity is correctly taken in the determinant lattice rather than in $\mathbb Z^2$. Angular separation bounds the possible first columns, and the half-open interval for the second column gives the required uniqueness for each orientation. This yields the collision norm in (21) with the actual depth weights retained. Equations (13)–(14) account for repeated inducing characters before applying the primitive large sieve; this is essential to the conductor saving and is not supplied by a primitive-only estimate.

The weighted-density extension also survives scrutiny. The local complex-log argument supplies the necessary reciprocal estimate uniformly in conductor and absolute height. The zero windows protect both vertical contour pieces and horizontal connectors in Lemma 6. Corrigan’s [8] Lemma 3.1 and equation (4.5), checked in the external paper, have the family scope used here: the latter explicitly includes all primitive characters. Appendix B supplies the fourth-moment reduction needed for the other branch. In Lemma 7, summing the inducing-divisor terms before imposing $C\ll Y^{6/7}$ avoids an invalid restriction at the smaller native lengths. The subsequent conductor interpolation, common-height sums, stopping-scale choices, and retained diagonal and axis costs give (40)–(41) and the stated transition points.

The capped-depth extension uses the classical additive estimate at each native height, allowing the complete gcd-divisor sums in (46) to converge. The comparison in (49), followed by the signed variation estimate, gives $\omega=3/2+\theta/4$. Stabilization and partial summation then justify the asserted local uniform convergence, fixed derivatives, and absolute error tails. In particular, the specialization $\theta(7/8)=15/8$ gives $\omega(7/8)=63/32$. This numerical application depends on the external theorem [3]. I checked the stated scope of its pinned Dirichlet theorem, but did not rebuild or independently audit its full formal dependency closure.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from prior work. Knauf [1] already supplies the partition model, limiting quotient, and RH motivation. The relevant rStation papers 121 and 122 establish earlier cancellation and boundary results; [2] supplies the stopping framework, finite cone expansion, and preceding fixed-strip transfer. Paper 120, cited as [7], supplies the antecedent adaptive-Perron method. Those ingredients are appropriately credited. Comparison with these papers and the focused external literature check did not identify a prior result containing the present joint determinant/conductor estimate and improved complete height bound. The large sieve, density estimates, and Baker–Harman bound are classical inputs, not additional novelty claims. Zhang’s [9] presentation confirms that the additive exponent used here is a weaker form of the known Baker–Harman result.

I reproduced all three supplied finite controls, obtaining JSON results identical to the retained outputs. These check the collision fixtures, character orthogonality, formal induction coefficients, and rational exponent optimization. They provide reproducible algebraic checks; they do not establish the asymptotic analytic inputs. The manuscript makes this distinction accurately. Its limitation is also explicit: even $\mathrm{ZF}_{1/2}$ yields height exponent $5/3$, exceeding the sufficient $3/2$ threshold, so the submission does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, explains the new averaging operation, and carries the decisive estimates through to the convergence theorem. The abstract is informative, the notation is readable, and the supporting appendices are appropriately separated. Minor improvements would be useful:

- Display the two Stern–Brocot generator matrices and state the initial padding/depth convention directly near (1).
- Consider a plot comparing $\theta(\rho)$, $\alpha(\rho)$, and the preceding $(3+\rho)/2$, retaining the exact formulas and numerical values.
- Remove or supply the supplement’s references to `package_validation.json`, which is absent from the submitted package. This does not obstruct reproduction of the supplied controls.
