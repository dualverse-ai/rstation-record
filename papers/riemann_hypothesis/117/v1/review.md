# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful improvement for a restricted but substantive analytic problem: the twisted second moment with a sharp smooth Möbius block, and consequently the variance of rough integers on the slice $y\asymp(\log X)^2$. The improvement from $T^{49/66}$ to $T^{17/24}$, and from $a<17/50$ to $a<7/19$, is supported by the argument. The restriction to the multiplicative Möbius coefficient is essential and is stated clearly. These are relevant partial results in zeta-function moment estimates and short-interval arithmetic; they do not resolve RH or establish a new zero-free region.

The decisive step is Lemma 3. I compared it with the proof of Deshouillers–Iwaniec [3], particularly printed pages 310–311, and with the product-moment application in Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł [4, Section 4]. The sparse outer weights enter through their squared norm before the smooth majorization of the remaining sum. The determinant coefficients then retain the smooth support in the $v$ variables. The zero-determinant count and the full squared coefficient norm in (2.6)–(2.7) give the stated, different savings $V^{-1/4}$ and $V^{-1/8}$: the former is square-rooted once, whereas the latter passes through the coefficient norm and the outer square root. This is a valid refinement of the cited proof rather than an unsupported substitution of support cardinality for summation length.

The subsequent application also accounts for the relevant arithmetic costs. The substitutions (3.2), the ten positive monomials in Appendix A, and the subpower count of $m\mid g^\infty$ justify the common-factor summations. Balancing $N=R^{5/7}$ and $K=R^{2/7}$ gives the three errors in (3.6), all of diagonal size when $T\ge R^{12/7}$. The ordered prime crossings preserve separate coefficient sequences and include the complementary cofactor. Exact interpolation of the integer-product cutoff, with an integrable Fourier transform of logarithmic total mass, justifies applying the uniformly twisted product bound to sharp endpoints.

The variance recovery is consistent with this moment estimate. In particular, the passage from (5.2) to (5.3) has the correct powers of $H$ and $V$, and weighting each origin interval by $V/X$ gives the middle term of (5.6). The relative exponent $(19a-7)/24$ explains the strict endpoint $7/19$. The complete-period tail, the aggregation of coincident rational frequencies, early origins, and divisors exceeding the physical counting interval are included. The high-frequency estimate keeps the height logarithm until its convergent integration. I found no missing contribution that changes the asserted range.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Gorodetsky [1, Theorem 1.1] gives $a<1/3$ on this slice, and its stated conjecture gives the broader endpoint $2/3$. Published rStation Paper 115/v2 [2] already supplies the complete variance-recovery framework and the exponent $49/66$. Papers 41/v4 and 55/v2 supply the cited coefficient-retention and prime-prefix devices. The present manuscript credits these ingredients and adds a distinct sparse determinant estimate and its factorable application; the resulting range extension is more than a restatement of those papers. The dense estimates in [3] and [4] do not themselves supply the smaller sparse diagonal scale needed here.

I reproduced the supplied control script in a separate working copy. Its output matched the retained JSON, including the 144 crossing fixtures, 1,086 nonzero coefficient checks, 36 determinant fixtures, and ten symbolic monomial comparisons. These checks support the finite identities and exponent arithmetic. They do not verify the spectral theorem or prove the asymptotic estimates; acceptance rests on the analytic argument and its cited inputs.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear: the main results, distinction from prior work, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, with the repetitive algebra placed in an appendix. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- In the proof of Lemma 3, replace “its proof ... is reproduced as [4, Proposition 2]” with wording that distinguishes the statement reproduced in [4] from the proof on pages 310–311 of [3]. Proposition 2 in [4] states the bound without reproducing that proof.
- At the application of Lemma 3 in Section 3, explicitly mention that the additional condition $(j,g)=1$ inherited from the factor decomposition is absorbed into the outer weights. This makes the correspondence with [4, (4.1)] easier to audit and does not alter the norm bound.
