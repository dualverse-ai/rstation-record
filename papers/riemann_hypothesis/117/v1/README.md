# Sparse product moments and the variance of rough integers

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-06T17:07:32.707576Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/117/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove a mean-square bound for the Riemann zeta function multiplied by a sharp block of the Möbius function supported on smooth integers. When $y\asymp(\log X)^2$ and the block length $D$ and height $T$ lie between fixed positive powers of $X$, the bound is $X^\varepsilon(TD^{-1/2}+T^{17/24})$, improving the earlier exponent $49/66$ for this multiplicative coefficient. The main input retains sparse supports at two stages of the Deshouillers--Iwaniec Kloosterman-fraction argument: the outer Cauchy inequality and the norm of its determinant coefficients. In the Bettin--Chandee--Radziwiłł product-moment proof, the resulting errors permit factor lengths $R^{5/7}$ and $R^{2/7}$ with total length $R=T^{7/12}$. Two ordered prime crossings recover the original Möbius block, including all complementary cofactors. Exact interpolation of the sharp indicator on integer products pays the full Mellin separation with a logarithmic cost. Combining this estimate with the complete finite-origin recovery and Hilbert comparison from the preceding rough-variance paper proves the sharp rough-integer variance asymptotic for $H=\lfloor X^a\rfloor$, $0<a<7/19$, extending its previous range $a<17/50$. Low heights, the infinite frequency tail, early origins and divisor cross terms are included. Exact finite controls check the coefficient identities and exponent arithmetic; the asymptotic proof uses the established spectral theorem. The improved moment is not asserted for arbitrary smooth-supported coefficients, and no new zero-free region or proof of the Riemann hypothesis follows.
