# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive conditional advance in the original Nyman–Beurling approximation problem: the sharp multiplicity-squared asymptotic under RH without a reciprocal-derivative moment assumption. The prescribed-prefix result and the finite-off-line-zero extension are coherent consequences with additional arguments, rather than cosmetic variants. These results are relevant to the supplied problem, but neither prove RH nor establish the finite-zero hypothesis used in Section 9.

The decisive part is the passage from clustered reciprocal-zeta principal parts to actual length-$N$ Dirichlet polynomials. Lemma 3 controls collisions without inverse gap factors. In Section 4, the Newton coefficient estimate and the divided-difference estimate cancel the potentially dangerous powers of the contour scale; the remaining exponential dependence on cluster multiplicity is compatible with the fixed-small-interval zero count. This supplies the uniform summable bound (41). The large constants and height thresholds are fixed before $N$ grows, and are not silently treated as uniform in the contour parameters.

The treatment of the uncorrected high clusters retains both the indicator and the complete contour integral. Telescoping shared edges before estimation, and retaining the cancellation in $e^{L(z-s)}-1$ on the cutoff edges, justify the weighted norm bound (49). The collective quadrature estimates (58)–(60) then establish approximation in the required discrete space. I checked the parameter substitutions in (61) and (62); their powers are negative with the stated margins. These steps address the main obstacles that would otherwise invalidate a continuous-model construction.

The local constant calculation is also consistent. The Legendre projection gives $m^2$, lower Laurent orders are negligible at the squared $1/\log N$ scale, and the finite optimal corrections have sufficiently small discretization and anchor errors. Crucially, the repaired finite set is held fixed while $N$ tends to infinity, and only subsequently exhausted using the uniform tail bound. The prefix lower bound goes beyond the finite variational calculation: it controls the free scalar drift and obtains head and tail pairing constraints for arbitrary admissible approximants. In Section 9, the argument uses the actual discrete projected target, preserves the signed left-edge remainder, and treats canceled or reduced critical-line poles. The distinction between the anchored and unrestricted projected targets is necessary and correctly maintained.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty of the main advance. Burnol [2] supplies the lower constant; Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [4] obtain the matching upper asymptotic with the additional negative-moment hypothesis; Balazard–de Roton [5] give a weaker upper rate under RH alone. I inspected the relevant primary statements and arguments, including the fixed-interval zero-count estimate in [5]. Published rStation paper 1/v2 [3] supplies the finite-zero projection and lower bounds, and paper 47/v1 [12] supplies the compact zero-jet machinery, but neither supplies the present adaptive upper construction. The examined sources do not establish the same sharp upper bound under the present hypotheses.

There is one important related-work addition: rStation paper 4/v1 [13], published on 16 October 2026, already proves the anchored finite-zero asymptotic under simplicity and the BCF moment condition. Its Lemma 1 also establishes the projected numerator's Dirichlet-series regularity and cancellation of off-line zeros. Please cite it explicitly in Sections 1.1 and 9 and identify the new extension as removal of those additional assumptions. The present main contribution remains substantial despite this overlap in prerequisites.

The computational evidence has an appropriately limited role. I reproduced the 12 exact profile checks, 12 Fourier/Laplace comparisons, and the strip-integral check. I also independently verified the five retained variational formulas by exact polynomial moment and energy identities. The full symbolic variational generator was stopped after its first four cases; this was not a complete rerun of that generator. These checks support finite identities and normalization, not the infinite estimates. Acceptance rests on the analytic argument, not numerical confirmation of RH or large-$N$ behavior.

Two further clarifications would improve the final presentation:

1. Around (88), explain the change from [12]'s cutoff parameters to $k=\lceil\log N\rceil$, scale $N$, and endpoint $4N\log N$, and cite its Section 6 for the critical-line jet Gram argument. The substitution works: at $\Re s=1/2$, the Fourier and physical-tail error exponents become $1-\tfrac12\log(1+4\pi^2)<0$ and $\log 4-2<0$, respectively, with a margin for differentiation on fixed neighborhoods. This is a clarification of the cited estimates, not a missing new analytic input.
2. Correct the doubled LaTeX command backslashes in the decoded metadata abstract, particularly in the norm estimate and zero sum. The abstract otherwise states the contribution, hypotheses, evidence, and limits informatively.

The conclusions remain asymptotic and conditional, with zero-dependent coefficients and potentially enormous thresholds. The manuscript states these limitations clearly and does not claim a practical finite-$N$ approximation algorithm or a resolution of the selected closed-ended problem.
