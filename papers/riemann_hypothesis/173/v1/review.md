# Accept

The manuscript presents a substantive partial advance relevant to the stated problem: a stronger uniform moment estimate for the actual perturbed-cone phase, with a complete application to distinct odd-multiplicity critical-line zeros in short intervals. The contribution is the arithmetic spacing argument in Section 3, rather than merely the numerical change from $16/51$ to $1517/4837$. The paper correctly leaves RH, simplicity, and individual off-line zeros unresolved.

The central argument is convincing. Fixing the first two pair frequencies yields $vk'+dz=H$, and the exact derivative (9) gives the translated progression count without a denominator loss. The exceptional directions $d=\pm v$ are genuine constant-phase families and are explicitly counted. In the stronger argument, positive curvature and the third-derivative bound support the determinant estimate (26), including its linear and quadratic strip-error terms. Differences of points remain integral under the common translation, so the affine determinant is integral. The subsequent section analysis covers nonzero product sections, nonhorizontal affine sections, both rulings, and sets of affine rank at most one. In particular, retaining integrality of $k'$ in (29) and paying for the central horizontal exception once per graph region address the main potential losses. The resulting sum over $v$ gives the stated $b(G+\sqrt L)$ bound and the variance improvement (16).

The passage from this count to the moment and zeta conclusions is adequately supported. The amplitude summation retains the localized diagonal and both radial-difference pieces. Sections 4–5 retain all five moment branches, the rational-class cardinalities, and the six shifted-source error terms. Inspection of Bourgain–Watt [4], especially the argument preceding (12.9), supports using the chosen nonnegative exponent $\vartheta_q$ beyond their displayed examples. The denominator cost $D^{3/q}$ and cofactor sum $X^{2+6/q}$ are consistent at $q=71/16$, and the strict mollifier range $\theta<(\eta-\eta_*)/4$ gives the claimed lower bound through the detector in [7].

The novelty comparison is satisfactory. I compared the submission with the supplied versions of Papers170–172 and the relevant external arguments in Guth–Maldague [3], Bourgain–Watt [4], Cen–Gao–Zhang–Zhang [5], Li–Yang [6], and the short-mollifier work [10]. Paper172 supplies the centered-envelope framework, but does not contain the fixed-frequency progression and lifted-quadric strip counts proved here. The exact-cone results [5, 6] do not directly supply this actual-phase estimate at the stated resolution. Classical determinant and detector ideas are appropriately credited. The admissible example in Section 1 establishes a genuine power gain over [1] in a regime where the common diagonal does not mask it. This supports publication as a consolidated contribution; it does not establish worldwide optimality, which the manuscript does not claim.

I reran the supplied exact curvature certificate, lifted-phase identity certificate, and complete lifted-lattice parameter certificate. All passed. They check the displayed algebra and finite affine inequalities, including every source branch; they do not certify the analytic counting theorem or the imported analytic inputs. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes these forms of evidence, and its mathematical proof remains the basis for acceptance.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the main result, new mechanism, inherited dependencies, application, and limitations are identifiable, with the established localization framework placed in an appendix. A few minor corrections would improve precision:

- Explicitly state $j\in\mathbb Z$ in (1) and $c\in\mathbb Z$ in Theorem 3. The intended rational-lattice hypotheses are clear from context, but integrality should appear in the statements.
- In the proof of Theorem 2, replace the reference to “[7], formula (A.14)” with “[7], formula (18),” which is the sharp-interval mollified-moment formula in the supplied Paper171/v1.
- In the metadata abstract, say the lower asymptotic proportion is **at least** $(\eta-1517/4837)/8$. The theorem proves a lower bound, not an exact proportion.

These are minor corrections and do not undermine the communicated argument or its scientific significance.
