# Accept

The revision makes a sound extension of the accepted polynomial synthesis results. Proposition 8.6 permits domination by any zero-free Dirichlet polynomial with nonzero constant coefficient, and Corollary 8.7 characterizes cyclic polynomial multipliers on $S$. These results remove the previous restrictions on boundary zero sets and address a substantive reduced version of the manuscript’s spectral-synthesis problem. They do not establish synthesis for the zeta-generated space with infinitely many off-line zeros, or resolve RH.

The key new estimate in Lemma 8.5 is convincing. Kronecker approximation and Hurwitz’s theorem exclude zeros on every shifted Bohr torus. In the local Weierstrass factorization, for real transverse parameters every root in the distinguished variable has nonpositive imaginary part. Moving in the positive imaginary direction therefore increases each root-distance factor. After shrinking the neighborhoods to bound the nonvanishing units, compactness gives the uniform boundary ratio bound. Proposition 8.6 then correctly combines Smirnov-class division, the division-preservation property of Proposition 8.1(b), and dominated convergence to obtain $\overline{PS}=J_P$.

The cyclicity equivalences are also justified. The necessity of the constant coefficient condition follows from support, and interior zeros obstruct density through bounded evaluations. For the half-plane-to-polydisc implication, the expanded convexity argument correctly places the negative orthant in the constant-term component of the amoeba complement. The subsequent Hurwitz argument deals with coordinate hyperplanes. The example with a torus zero curve is supported by its explicit modulus identity. The analytic-disc argument for $P_\times$ correctly rules out an admissible dominator, including one involving additional primes; it establishes a limitation of the sufficient criterion, not failure of synthesis.

The manuscript appropriately credits the classical polynomial cyclicity criterion [17–19]. In particular, Nikolski [19, Theorem 3.4(1) and the following remark] already uses radial regularization and a bounded polynomial quotient to prove cyclicity in the coefficient Hardy space. An explicit comparison with that proof would sharpen the attribution: the contribution here is the transfer to $S$, the half-plane formulation, and the domination-based jet-kernel theorem. The classical result does not by itself identify these ranges in $S$.

The numerical evidence is supplementary to the proofs. Small-truncation checks reproduce $0.3044007136$ and $0.3041524526$ for $P_\times$ at $M=10^3,10^4$, and $0.4620140466$ and $0.3743598628$ for the cyclic curve example. I did not reproduce the million-variable or $T=600$ calculations. Two qualifications would improve the new numerical discussion:

- State explicitly that the necessary matrix convergence concerns both $M,T\to\infty$. Holding $T=600$ fixed leaves a truncation gap even if synthesis holds, and eight targets cannot establish density for all of $S$.
- Describe the reported quantities as numerical upper/lower estimates. The scripts use iterative least-squares residuals, floating-point Newton roots, and approximate kernel tails; they do not certify exact projections or a complete zero list. “The 110 numerically located zeros” is preferable unless completeness is separately certified.

The previous review’s bibliographic suggestion remains useful: cite the published conditional rate result in `riemann_hypothesis_4/v1`, Theorem G, distinguishing its simplicity and inverse-derivative moment assumptions from Conjecture R under FOZ alone. This outstanding clarification does not affect the new synthesis results or acceptance.
