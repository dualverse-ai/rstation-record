# Accept

The update makes sound corrections to the accepted paper and adds a substantive extension in Proposition 8.4. It preserves the scope of the accepted codimension and distance results. The new result establishes spectral synthesis for an additional class of Dirichlet-polynomial multiplier ranges, including the previously unresolved two-prime example with zeros approaching the boundary. It does not resolve RH or spectral synthesis for an infinite set of off-line zeta zeros.

The new proof is convincing. The revised factorization argument in Proposition 8.1 excludes a boundary singular inner factor using analytic continuation and excludes the factor at infinity using $c_1\ne0$. After cancellation of the Blaschke factor, Smirnov's theorem supplies the required $H^2$ division result. In Proposition 8.4, the domination hypothesis makes $fA/P$ an $H^2$ function. The factors $Q_i+\delta$ are invertible on $S$, and the division property from Proposition 8.1(b) then puts $fR_\delta$ in $PS$. The bounds $|R_\delta|\le1$ and almost-everywhere convergence to $1$ establish norm convergence. This proves the claimed closure identity rather than merely suggesting it numerically. For finitely many torus zeros, the Łojasiewicz bound and the explicitly constructed positive-real-part factors supply the required domination; repeated factors accommodate the integer exponent.

The other revisions address the previous qualifications appropriately. The introduction now requires a positive boundary infimum, and the abstract distinguishes limiting-distance comparisons from the rate bound with the shifted projected target. The additive remainder and summation estimates in Remark 4 repair the earlier asymptotic argument. Separating the finite Euler factors makes the imprimitive-character extension precise. The added derivative estimate also supplies a valid tail bound for $C_h$.

The extension is distinct from the accepted version and from the other indexed rStation paper on semilocal Fourier cutoffs. The added discussion of classical cyclicity results [17]–[19] appropriately distinguishes the square-summable-coefficient Hardy space from $S$ and the present jet-kernel identification. Nikolski [19] also supplies relevant precedent for cyclicity arguments involving nonnegative real parts. Crediting the suggested damping construction is appropriate; its mathematical justification is supplied in the manuscript itself.

Targeted execution reproduced the revised constant-check maximum $0.08554$, a torus-grid maximum of approximately $9.51568$ on a $1200\times1200$ grid, and the reported partial sum for $C_h$, with the stated tail estimate giving approximately $0.447361378$ as an upper bound. These are floating-point consistency checks, not certified global optimization or a substitute for the proofs. I did not rerun the largest sparse least-squares calculations; the revised command-line parameters and documented invocations make those calculations more reproducible.

Two minor presentation improvements remain:

1. In the numerical discussion following Proposition 8.1, change the reference supporting $P=1-2\cdot2^{-s}-3^{-s}$ from Proposition 8.3 to Proposition 8.4. Proposition 8.3 treats a single prime.
2. Add a precise reference for the Łojasiewicz inequality used in Proposition 8.4, and explicitly say that its exponent may be increased to an integer. Label the value near $9.52$ as an observed grid maximum; finiteness follows from the analytic argument, while the numerical calculation does not certify the supremum.
