# Accept

The additions in §8 are sound extensions of the accepted paper. They clarify the obstruction to extending the finite-zero argument, distinguish invariant-subspace structure from spectral synthesis, and give a useful single-target criterion under an explicit additional hypothesis. They preserve the substantive accepted results and do not claim to resolve infinite-zero synthesis or RH.

Proposition 8.10 is justified by absolute convergence, dominated convergence along Bohr-limit subsequences, Fourier uniqueness on the unrestricted tail torus, and successive disc-algebra boundary uniqueness. The square-summable-coefficient variant is valid because the positive gap from $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$ supplies a summable majorant by Cauchy–Schwarz. This is a necessary condition for a nonzero fixed multiplier, not evidence that the hypothetical off-line zeros actually satisfy it. The distinction from Seip’s bounded-zero-set result [27] is appropriate.

Remark 8.11 correctly transfers the singular-inner-function example through the Wold model of $\sqrt2M_2$. The resulting range is closed, proper, invariant under every $M_n$, and has no common interior zero. The jet argument correctly excludes root vectors from its orthogonal complement. This supplies a concrete reason why invariance alone cannot establish the desired synthesis, while appropriately identifying the classical origin of the construction.

Corollary 8.12 depends essentially on the generalized-eigenspace classification in [28], rather than merely the finite-dimensional common-invariant-subspace theorem in published paper [26]. I inspected the publicly accessible proof in the cited comment. Its cell-norm identification, negative-Sobolev comparison, holomorphic normalized frame for each fixed Jordan order, and removal of the nilpotent part before Fourier averaging support the needed statement (14). The manuscript uses that statement with the correct conjugation and eigenvalue annulus. The attribution is explicit; for archival reproducibility, retaining a stable copy of the cited revision would improve this important dependency.

Proposition 8.13 follows correctly from this classification. Membership of $B(V_2)E$ propagates to $B(V_2)S$ because $E$ is cyclic for the dilation family. The Blaschke tails converge strongly to the identity, so their adjoints give weak approximation inside $S\ominus V$ by vectors killed by finite Blaschke products. Polynomial primary decomposition then places these approximants in finite sums of generalized eigenspaces, and [28] identifies them with zeta jets. Weak closedness completes the inclusion. This is a substantive application to the present synthesis problem of the standard operator argument acknowledged in [29]. It does not establish either the dyadic Blaschke condition or the required target membership for the actual zeta zero set. The verification here is analytic; no numerical experiment is needed to support these additions.

Two minor clarifications would sharpen the statements:

- Define $m^*(w)$ as $\sup\{m(\rho):w_\rho=w\}$, allowing $+\infty$ initially. No uniform multiplicity bound along an infinite fiber has been supplied. Either the nonzero function in part (i) or condition (DB) then forces this supremum to be finite, hence attained. This convention leaves the proof and conclusions intact.
- In the introductory summary, specify that the multiplier is nonzero and replace “thin” by the precise conclusion that every section $\Lambda_\beta(Z^+)$ has empty interior. The proposition does not assert a measure or dimension bound, and its absolute-convergence hypothesis should remain explicit.

This revision remains a partial advance toward the selected problem. It neither proves that all nontrivial zeta zeros lie on the critical line nor establishes an off-line zero.
