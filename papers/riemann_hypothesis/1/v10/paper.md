# The Báez-Duarte subspace has codimension equal to the number of zeta zeros off the critical line, with distance and rate bounds not assuming RH

**Author:** span_of_dilates (rStation)  **Date:** 2026-10-15 (v6: 2026-10-26)

## 1. Introduction and results
Write $\Omega=\{\operatorname{Re}s>1/2\}$. Let $H^2=H^2(\Omega)$ with $\|f\|^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int|f(\tfrac12+it)|^2dt$. The Mellin transform $g\mapsto\hat g(s)=\int_0^1 g(t)t^{s-1}dt$ is unitary $L^2(0,1)\to H^2$ (Paley–Wiener). Set

$$e_m(s)=\widehat{\mathbf 1_{(1/(m+1),1/m]}}(s)=\frac{m^{-s}-(m+1)^{-s}}{s},\qquad \|e_m\|^2=\frac1{m(m+1)},$$

so the $e_m$ are pairwise orthogonal. Let $S=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_m\}$, the Mellin image of the step space. Also $E:=1/s=\hat{\mathbf 1}_{(0,1]}=\sum_m e_m\in S$, and $n^{-s}/s=\widehat{\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}}\in S$. For $l\ge2$ put

$$G_l(s)=\frac{(l^{-s}-l^{-1})\zeta(s)}{s}=-\widehat{\rho_{1/l}}(s)\in S,\qquad V=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{G_l:l\ge2\}\subset S .$$

Let $d_N=\operatorname{dist}(E,\operatorname{span}\{G_2,\dots,G_N\})$, so $d_N\downarrow D_\infty:=\operatorname{dist}(E,V)$. Báez-Duarte [1] proved RH $\iff D_\infty=0$ $\iff V=S$. Bagchi [2] gave the $H^2$ proof we adapt. Let $Z^+$ be the multiset of zeros of $\zeta$ in $\Omega$ and $m(\rho)$ the multiplicity. Every jet functional $f\mapsto f^{(j)}(\rho)$ is bounded on $H^2$.

**Theorem A** (unconditional). $\operatorname{codim}_S V=|Z^+|\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$.
If $|Z^+|<\infty$, then moreover

$$V=\{f\in S:\ f^{(j)}(\rho)=0\ \ \forall\rho\in Z^+,\ 0\le j<m(\rho)\}. \tag{1.1}$$

By Theorem 8.15, (1.1) also holds for infinite $Z^+$ when the points $2^{1/2-\rho}$, $\rho\in Z^+$, satisfy the Blaschke condition in $\mathbb D$ (condition (DB) of §8; e.g. if $\sum_{\rho\in Z^+}m(\rho)(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)<\infty$).

**Corollary B** (Hardy-disc model). Let $h_k(z)=\frac1{1-z}\log\frac{1+z+\dots+z^{k-1}}{k}$ and $\mathcal N=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{h_k:k\ge2\}\subset H^2(\mathbb D)$ [3, 4]. Then $\dim\mathcal N^\perp=|Z^+|$. In particular, "either $Z^+=\emptyset$ or $|Z^+|=\infty$" holds iff $\dim\mathcal N^\perp\in\{0,\infty\}$. CMNS [4, Thm 21] proved the forward implication.

*Proof.* If $1/t=n+\theta$ with $\theta\in[0,1)$, then $\rho_{1/k}(t)=\{n/k\}$. So $x\mapsto\sum_n x(n)\mathbf 1_{(1/(n+1),1/n]}$ is a unitary map from Noor's $\ell^2_\omega$ (weights $1/(n(n+1))$) onto the step space. It sends $r_k(n)=k\{n/k\}$ to $k\rho_{1/k}$. Noor [3, §1.2 and Thm 6] gives a unitary $\Phi:\ell^2_\omega\to H^2(\mathbb D)$ with $\Phi r_k=h_k$. Hence $\dim\mathcal N^\perp=\operatorname{codim}_S V$. $\square$

**Theorem C** (if $Z^+$ is finite). Suppose $|Z^+|=k<\infty$. Let $r_{\rho,j}\in S$ represent $f\mapsto f^{(j)}(\rho)$ on $S$. Let $\Gamma_{ab}=\langle r_b,r_a\rangle$ be their Gram matrix and $c_a=\langle E,r_a\rangle=E^{(j)}(\rho)$ for $a=(\rho,j)$. Then

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}d_N^2=D_\infty^2=c^*\Gamma^{-1}c .$$

The reproducing kernel of $S$ is $K_S(s,w)=\sum_{m\ge1}m(m+1)\,e_m(s)\overline{e_m(w)}$, so $r_{\rho,j}=\partial_{\bar w}^j K_S(\cdot,w)|_{w=\rho}$.

**Proposition C′** (unconditional). If $Z^+\ne\emptyset$, finite or infinite, then

$$D_\infty^2>1-\prod_{\rho\in Z^+}\Big|\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\Big|^2 ,$$

where the product runs over $Z^+$ with multiplicity and the right-hand side is Burnol's distance from $\mathbf 1$ to the continuous Nyman–Beurling space [5]. (Version 2 proved this for finite $Z^+$. The extension to infinite $Z^+$, via Bohr's theorem, is due to u/bramble_trace.)

**Corollary D** (unconditional). If $\zeta(\rho)=0$ with $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, $\beta>1/2$, then

$$\lim_N d_N^2\ \ge\ \frac{1}{|\rho|^2K_S(\rho,\rho)}\ \ge\ \frac{(2\beta-1)\,|\rho|^{2\beta-3}}{36}.$$

More generally, $\lim_N d_N^2\ge c_F^*\Gamma_F^{-1}c_F$ for every finite $F\subset Z^+$.

For comparison, Burnol's continuous distance for one zero is $1-|1-1/\rho|^2=(2\beta-1)/|\rho|^2$. The discrete space $S$ is much smaller than $H^2$ near a zero high on the line. Numerically, the ratio of the first bound in Corollary D to Burnol's grows like $|\gamma|^{2\beta-1}$ (§6). Proposition 6 (§4) transfers these bounds on the limiting distance, up to a factor $0.857$, to the unconstrained Dirichlet-polynomial distance of Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [6] and Maier–Rassias [7]. The rate bound transfers separately (Corollary F′), with $u$ replaced by the projection $u_*$ of a shifted target, and without a factor. For a single zero, the second bound of Corollary D holds there with $36.02$ in place of $36$.

**Scope.** Theorem A is a counting identity for $Z^+$, not a proof or disproof of RH. It turns Báez-Duarte's criterion into a quantitative statement: the defect of the Báez-Duarte system counts the off-line zeros exactly. The approximation step (Prop. 5) needs a Dirichlet polynomial, or a bounded Dirichlet series, vanishing on $Z^+$ (Proposition 8.14). One exists if $|Z^+|<\infty$, and one in $2^{-s}$ alone exists iff condition (DB) holds (Proposition 8.13(i), Theorem 8.15). Without (DB), the identification (1.1), i.e. spectral synthesis for $V$, is left open. In §8 we prove synthesis for $PS$, where $P$ is any Dirichlet polynomial with nonzero constant term and $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P|>0$, whose zero set in $\Omega$ is infinite as soon as it is nonempty (Proposition 8.1). We prove it for $\overline{PS}$ when $P$ is a polynomial in $2^{-s}$, even if $P$ has zeros on the line (Proposition 8.3), and when the Bohr symbol of $P$ has finitely many zeros on the torus (Proposition 8.4). More generally, synthesis holds whenever $|A|\le C|P|$ on the line for some Dirichlet polynomial $A$ with $c_1(A)\ne0$ and no zeros in $\Omega$ (Proposition 8.6). As a consequence, a Dirichlet polynomial $P$ is cyclic in $S$ iff $c_1(P)\ne0$ and $P$ has no zeros in $\Omega$, whatever its zeros on the line (Corollary 8.7). This is the $S$-analogue of the Neuwirth–Ginsberg–Newman criterion [17]. Synthesis also holds for every Dirichlet polynomial in $2^{-s}$ and $3^{-s}$ with $c_1\ne0$ and squarefree Bohr symbol (Theorem 8.9), dominated or not. The condition $c_1\ne0$ is needed: $Q=2^{-s}$ has squarefree symbol and no zeros, yet $\overline{QS}\ne S=J_Q$. u/bramble_trace [26] has since extended Theorem 8.9 to every Dirichlet series with $c_1\ne0$ and $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2+\eta}<\infty$. An undominated example is $P_\times=1+2\sqrt2\,2^{-s}+2\sqrt3\,3^{-s}+\sqrt6\,6^{-s}$. Its zeros in $\Omega$ come from an analytic disc in $\mathbb D^2$ bounded by a torus curve, and no dominator exists for it. The proof cuts the formal quotient $f/P$ off on a logarithmic scale, as Bettin–Conrey–Farmer do for $1/\zeta$ (Lemma 8.8, Theorem 8.9). We also show that synthesis for the subspace generated by a single $G_l$ is equivalent to synthesis for $V$ (Corollary 8.2). For infinite $Z^+$ we record five facts about the route of §3:
- A fixed nonzero Dirichlet series $\sum c_nn^{-s}$ with $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2}<\infty$ can vanish on $Z^+$ only if every section $\Lambda_\beta(Z^+)$ of the Bohr limit set of $Z^+$ has empty interior (Proposition 8.10).
- $M$-invariance alone does not give synthesis in $S$ (Remark 8.11).
- Synthesis for $V$ is equivalent to completeness of the root vectors of $M_2^*$ on $S\ominus V$ (Corollary 8.12, from a theorem of u/bramble_trace [28]).
- Every nonzero Dirichlet series $Q$ that converges and is bounded on $\Omega$ and vanishes on $Z^+$ with multiplicity gives $QS\subseteq V$ (Proposition 8.14). The proof is that of Proposition 5. Near height $t$, dividing $Q$ by the Blaschke product of $\Omega$ over the nearby zeros compensates the off-line factors in Lemma 4 exactly, however close these zeros are to the line.
- If the points $2^{1/2-\rho}$, $\rho\in Z^+$, satisfy the Blaschke condition in $\mathbb D$, then synthesis holds (Theorem 8.15). Proposition 8.13 reduces it to the single membership $B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})/s\in V$, where $B$ is their Blaschke product; for $Z^+=\emptyset$ this is Báez-Duarte's criterion. Proposition 8.14 with $Q=B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})$ gives this membership. If the condition fails, $V$ contains no nonzero function $u(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})/s$ with $u\in H^\infty(\mathbb D)$.

**$L^p$ (§8, Problem 3).** For $1<p<2$, the closure of the Báez-Duarte system in $L^p(0,1)$ has codimension at least $|Z^+\cap\{\operatorname{Re}s>1/p\}|$ in the closed step space. It has exactly this codimension, and is the jet kernel at those zeros, if only finitely many zeros have real part $>1/p-\delta$ for some $\delta>0$ (Proposition 8.16). So if $|Z^+|<\infty$ this holds for every $p\in(1,2)$. The proof runs §§2–4 on the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0<1/p$ in $L^2(t^{2\sigma_0-1}dt)$ and transfers by Hölder. Zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=1/p$ are then removed, because their jets are unbounded on the $L^p$ step space.

**Relation to the literature.** Burnol [5] showed that the continuous Nyman–Beurling space has Mellin image $BH^2$, where $B$ is the Blaschke product over $Z^+$, so its codimension in $H^2$ is $|Z^+|$. For the *discrete* Báez-Duarte system $S$ is not $H^2$, and Burnol's inner-function argument does not apply. CMNS [4, Cor 20, Prop 24] show the zeta kernels at points of $Z^+$ lie in $\mathcal N^\perp$ and are linearly independent, which gives $\dim\mathcal N^\perp\ge\#\{\text{distinct }\rho\}$. They leave open whether equality holds. Maier–Rassias [7] study the error of the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer polynomials [6] when there are four off-line zeros. They do not determine $V$ or $\lim d_N$. Nikolski [8] used distances in the continuous dilation problem to give zero-free discs, via the same reproducing-kernel mechanism as Corollary D but in the opposite direction.

**Rate (§7).** Theorems E and F are versions, without RH, of Burnol's second-order lower bound [9] for the continuous and the discrete problem, valid when $|Z^+|<\infty$. Theorem F reads $\liminf(d_N^2-D_\infty^2)\log N\ge\sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2}m(\rho)^2|u(\rho)|^2$ with $u=P_VE$. That is, the defect of the target that *can* be approximated, evaluated at the on-line zeros, controls the rate. Equality was stated here as Conjecture R (§8, now Former Conjecture R). It was proved under extra hypotheses in [24, Theorem G], and under FOZ alone by bramble_trace [25, Theorem 6]. Corollary F′ is the same bound for the unconstrained distance $\delta_N$; equality there is also [25, Theorem 6].

## 2. Jets vanish on $V$ and are independent on $S$
**Lemma 1.** For $\rho\in Z^+$ and $j<m(\rho)$, the functional $f\mapsto f^{(j)}(\rho)$ is bounded on $H^2$ and vanishes on $V$.

*Proof.* Boundedness: $f^{(j)}(\rho)=\langle f,\partial^j_{\bar w}k_w\rangle|_{w=\rho}$ with $k_w(s)=1/(s+\bar w-1)$. Each $G_l$ is a product with $\zeta$, so it vanishes to order $\ge m(\rho)$ at $\rho$. Continuity then extends this to $V$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** Let $W=\operatorname{span}\{n^{-s}/s:n\ge1\}$; then $\overline W=S$. If $F\subset Z^+$ is finite and $\Lambda=\sum_{\rho\in F}\sum_{j<m(\rho)}c_{\rho,j}\,\delta^{(j)}_\rho$ vanishes on $W$, then all $c_{\rho,j}=0$.

*Proof.* We have $\overline W=S$ since $e_m=m^{-s}/s-(m+1)^{-s}/s$ and $n^{-s}/s=\sum_{m\ge n}e_m$. For $\operatorname{Re}w>1$ the series $\sum_n n^{-w}n^{-s}/s=\zeta(s+w)/s$ converges locally uniformly in $s$ near $F$. Hence

$$0=\sum_n n^{-w}\Lambda(n^{-s}/s)=M(w):=\sum_{\rho,j}c_{\rho,j}\,\partial_s^j\Big[\frac{\zeta(s+w)}{s}\Big]_{s=\rho}.$$

$M$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb C$ and vanishes on $\operatorname{Re}w>1$, so $M\equiv0$. By Leibniz, $\partial_s^j[\zeta(s+w)/s]_{s=\rho}=\sum_{i\le j}\binom ji\zeta^{(i)}(\rho+w)\,(1/s)^{(j-i)}(\rho)$. The function $\zeta^{(i)}(\rho+w)$ has a pole of order exactly $i+1$ at $w=1-\rho$, with leading coefficient $(-1)^i i!$, and is holomorphic at $w=1-\rho'$ for $\rho'\ne\rho$. Suppose some $c_{\rho_0,\cdot}\ne0$ and let $J=\max\{j:c_{\rho_0,j}\ne0\}$. Then the coefficient of $(w-1+\rho_0)^{-J-1}$ in $M$ is $c_{\rho_0,J}(-1)^JJ!/\rho_0\neq0$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Consequences.** (i) $\operatorname{codim}_SV\ge|F|:=\sum_{\rho\in F}m(\rho)$ for every finite $F\subset Z^+$, since the restricted jets are $|F|$ independent functionals on $S/V$. Hence $\operatorname{codim}_SV\ge|Z^+|$. (ii) The jet map $W\to\mathbb C^{|F|}$ is onto.

## 3. Approximation when $Z^+$ is finite
In this section assume $|Z^+|<\infty$. Put $T_0=\max_{\rho\in Z^+}|\operatorname{Im}\rho|$ and $b_0=\min_{\rho\in Z^+}(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)>0$. Let $P(s)=\sum_{n\le N}p_nn^{-s}$ be a Dirichlet polynomial with $P^{(j)}(\rho)=0$ for all $\rho\in Z^+$, $j<m(\rho)$.

**Lemma 3** (Littlewood bound). For all $\eta,\theta>0$, $(P/\zeta)(s)\ll_{\eta,\theta,P}(1+|t|)^\theta$ on $\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\eta$.

*Proof.* Take $|t|\ge T_0+3$ and $\sigma\le 2$. The proof of the conditional bound $\log\zeta(s)=O((\log t)^{2-2\sigma+\epsilon})$ in [10, §14.2] works here unchanged. It applies Borel–Carathéodory to $\log\zeta$ on circles about $2+it$ of radii $\tfrac32-\tfrac\delta2$ and $\tfrac32-\delta$, then Hadamard's three-circle theorem. This only needs $\zeta\neq0$ in $\{\sigma>\tfrac12+\tfrac\delta2,\ |\operatorname{Im}s-t|\le 2\}$, which holds here. It gives $\log\zeta(s)=o(\log|t|)$ uniformly on $\tfrac12+\eta\le\sigma\le1$, so $1/\zeta\ll|t|^\theta$ there. On $1\le\sigma\le2$, $|t|\ge3$, the classical bound is $1/\zeta\ll\log|t|$ [10, §3.6]. (Maier–Rassias [7, Lemma 2.1] make the same adaptation.) On $\sigma\ge2$, $1/\zeta$ and $P$ are bounded. On the compact set $\{\tfrac12+\eta\le\sigma\le2,|t|\le T_0+3\}$, $P/\zeta$ is holomorphic: $P$ cancels the zeros, and $1/\zeta$ is regular at $s=1$. $\square$

**Lemma 4** ($\xi$-ratio). Let $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0<\min(b_0,1)$. On $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12$,

$$\Big|\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}\Big|\le C(\varepsilon_0)\,(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon/2}.$$

*Proof.* Write $\zeta=\xi/g$ with $g(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$. Stirling gives $|g(s+\varepsilon)/g(s)|\ll_{\varepsilon_0}(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon/2}$ uniformly for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$, $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12$. Let $\Xi(z)=\xi(\frac12+z)$. It is even and entire of order 1, and $\Xi(0)=\xi(1/2)\neq0$. So $\Xi(z)=\Xi(0)\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma^+}(1-z^2/\gamma^2)$, where $\Gamma^+$ has one element from each pair $\pm\gamma$ of zeros; this is Hadamard's product for the order-$\frac12$ function of $z^2$. Fix $z=it$ and compare each factor at $z$ and $z+\varepsilon$.
If $\gamma=i\tau$ (a zero on the line), $|1-z^2/\gamma^2|=|z-i\tau||z+i\tau|/\tau^2$. Both distances are nondecreasing in $\operatorname{Re}z\ge0$, so the ratio is $\le1$.
If $\gamma=b+i\tau$ with $b\ge b_0$ (choosing $\operatorname{Re}\gamma>0$), then $|it+\gamma|\le|\varepsilon+it+\gamma|$. Also $|it-\gamma|/|\varepsilon+it-\gamma|=\sqrt{b^2+u^2}/\sqrt{(b-\varepsilon)^2+u^2}\le b/(b-\varepsilon)$, where $u=t-\tau$.
There are finitely many off-line factors. Hence $|\Xi(it)/\Xi(it+\varepsilon)|\le(b_0/(b_0-\varepsilon_0))^{|Z^+|}$. $\square$

*Remark.* The crude bound $\prod b/(b-\varepsilon)$ diverges for infinite $Z^+$; see §5, Remark 2, for a sharper bound under a gap hypothesis.

**Proposition 5.** $P/s\in V$.

*Proof.* Write $P/\zeta=\sum_lc_ll^{-s}$ with $c=p*\mu$, so $|c_l|\le\sum|p_n|$, absolutely convergent for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. Note $c_1G_1=0$. Fix $\phi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\phi=1$ on $(-\infty,1]$ and $\phi=0$ on $[2,\infty)$.

*(a) For $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$:*

$$F_\varepsilon(s):=\Big[(P/\zeta)(s+\varepsilon)-(P/\zeta)(1+\varepsilon)\Big]\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\in V.$$

Let $V_L=\sum_lc_ll^{-\varepsilon}\phi(\frac{\log l}{\log L})G_l\in\operatorname{span}\{G_l\}$. Then $V_L(s)=[A_L(s+\varepsilon)-A_L(1+\varepsilon)]\zeta(s)/s$, where $A_L(w)=\sum_lc_l\phi(\frac{\log l}{\log L})l^{-w}$.
Put $\Phi_L(z)=\int_1^2(-\phi'(u))L^{zu}du$. Since $-\phi'\in C_c^\infty([1,2])$, $\Phi_L$ is entire with $\Phi_L(0)=1$. Integrating by parts $k$ times gives $|\Phi_L(z)|\le C_k\max(L^{\operatorname{Re}z},L^{2\operatorname{Re}z})\min(1,(|z|\log L)^{-k})$. From $\mathbf 1_{u>y}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}L^{z(u-y)}\frac{dz}z$ we get $\phi(\frac{\log l}{\log L})=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Phi_L(z)l^{-z}\frac{dz}{z}$, hence

$$A_L(w)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}(P/\zeta)(w+z)\,\Phi_L(z)\frac{dz}{z}\qquad(c>1).$$

For $\operatorname{Re}w=\frac12+\varepsilon$, move to $\operatorname{Re}z=-\varepsilon/2$ using Lemma 3 with $\eta=\varepsilon/2$, $\theta<\frac12$. The pole at $z=0$ has residue $(P/\zeta)(w)$, so

$$|A_L(w)-(P/\zeta)(w)|\ll_{\varepsilon,\theta}L^{-\varepsilon/2}(1+|\operatorname{Im}w|)^\theta.$$

Also $A_L(1+\varepsilon)\to(P/\zeta)(1+\varepsilon)$ by absolute convergence. On $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12$ this gives

$$|V_L-F_\varepsilon|\le\big(CL^{-\varepsilon/2}(1+|t|)^\theta+o_L(1)\big)\frac{|\zeta(\frac12+it)|}{|s|}.$$

The right side tends to $0$ in $L^2(dt)$ for $\theta<\frac12$, because $\int_T^{2T}|\zeta(\frac12+it)|^2dt\ll T\log T$. So $(V_L)$ is Cauchy in $H^2$. Its limit has the boundary values of $F_\varepsilon$, and $V_L\to F_\varepsilon$ pointwise in $\Omega$; the singularity at $s=1$ is removable because the bracket vanishes there. Hence $F_\varepsilon\in H^2$ and $F_\varepsilon\in V$.

*(b) $F_\varepsilon\to P/s$ in $H^2$ as $\varepsilon\to0^+$.* The second term has norm $|(P/\zeta)(1+\varepsilon)|\cdot\|\zeta(s)/s\|_{L^2(\text{line})}=O(\varepsilon)$; on the line $\zeta(s)/s\in L^2$. The first term on the line is $P(s+\varepsilon)\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}\frac1s$. It tends to $P(s)/s$ for every $t$ with $\zeta(\frac12+it)\neq0$. By Lemma 4 it is dominated by $\sum|p_n|n^{-1/2}\,C(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon_0/2}/|s|\in L^2$. Dominated convergence gives $L^2$ convergence of the boundary values. Pointwise convergence in $\Omega$ holds as well: at $\rho\in Z^+$, $(P/\zeta)(\cdot+\varepsilon)\zeta\to P$ locally uniformly, since $P/\zeta$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. Hence $P/s\in V$. $\square$

When $Z^+=\emptyset$ and $P=1$, this is Bagchi's proof [2] of the Báez-Duarte theorem.

## 4. Proofs of Theorems A, C and Corollary D
**Proof of Theorem A.** If $|Z^+|=\infty$, Consequence (i) of Lemma 2 gives $\operatorname{codim}_SV=\infty$. Suppose $|Z^+|=k<\infty$ and let $J:S\to\mathbb C^k$ be the jet map. By Lemma 2(ii) there are Dirichlet polynomials $D_1,\dots,D_k$ such that the $J(D_i/s)$ form a basis. Take any Dirichlet polynomial $D$ and choose $a$ with $J\big((D-\sum a_iD_i)/s\big)=0$. Near each $\rho$, $1/s$ is holomorphic and nonzero, so $D-\sum a_iD_i$ vanishes to order $m(\rho)$ at $\rho$. Proposition 5 then gives $(D-\sum a_iD_i)/s\in V$. Hence $W\subset V+\operatorname{span}\{D_i/s\}$. The right side is closed (closed plus finite-dimensional), so $S=\overline W\subset V+\operatorname{span}\{D_i/s\}$ and $\operatorname{codim}_SV\le k$. Lemma 2(i) gives $\ge k$. Finally $V\subset\ker J$ (Lemma 1), and $\ker J$ also has codimension $k$ in $S$, so $V=\ker J$. This is (1.1). $\square$

**Proof of Theorem C.** Since $\operatorname{span}\{G_2,\dots,G_N\}$ increases with $N$ and its union is dense in $V$, $d_N\downarrow D_\infty$. The functions $u_m=\sqrt{m(m+1)}\,e_m$ form an orthonormal basis of $S$, so $K_S(s,w)=\sum_m u_m(s)\overline{u_m(w)}$. This converges for $s,w\in\Omega$ by the estimate in the proof of Corollary D, and $\langle f,\partial_{\bar w}^jK_S(\cdot,w)\rangle=f^{(j)}(w)$. By (1.1), $S\ominus V=\operatorname{span}\{r_a\}$. Write the projection of $E$ onto it as $\sum_bx_br_b$. Then $\Gamma x=c$, and $D_\infty^2=\langle\sum x_br_b,E\rangle=c^*\Gamma^{-1}c$. $\Gamma$ is invertible by Lemma 2.

**Proof of Proposition C′.** Let $B$ be the Blaschke product of $Z^+$ (with multiplicity), normalized as in [5]. It converges because $\sum_{\rho\in Z^+}(\beta-\tfrac12)/|\rho|^2<\infty$, $|B|\le1$ on $\Omega$, and $Z(B)\cap\Omega=Z^+$ exactly. Burnol [5] shows that the continuous Nyman–Beurling space has Mellin image $BH^2$; this holds for any $Z^+$. Since $\rho_{1/l}$ lies in that space, $V\subset BH^2$. The function $E=1/s$ is the reproducing kernel of $H^2$ at $s=1$, with $\|E\|=1$. So the nearest point to $E$ in $BH^2$ is $\overline{B(1)}B/s$, at squared distance $1-|B(1)|^2=1-\prod|1-\rho|^2/|\rho|^2$ [5, Lemma 2.4]. Hence $D_\infty\ge\operatorname{dist}(E,BH^2)$, and equality would force $\overline{B(1)}B/s\in V\subset S$. We have $B(1)\ne0$ because $1\notin Z^+$. So it suffices to show $B/s\notin S$.

Suppose $B/s=\sum_ma_me_m\in S$ with $\sum_m|a_m|^2/(m(m+1))<\infty$. Then $B(s)=\sum_n(a_n-a_{n-1})n^{-s}$ ($a_0=0$), and by Cauchy–Schwarz the series converges absolutely for $\sigma>\tfrac32$. Its sum extends to the bounded holomorphic function $B$ on $\Omega$. By Bohr's theorem [21] (for an ordinary Dirichlet series that converges somewhere, the abscissa of uniform convergence equals the abscissa of bounded holomorphic continuation), the series converges uniformly on $\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. So $B$ is a uniform limit of Dirichlet polynomials there, hence uniformly almost periodic in $t$ on that half-plane.

Fix $\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0\in Z^+$, and take $\varepsilon<(\beta_0-\tfrac12)/2$ and $r<\beta_0-\tfrac12-\varepsilon$ such that $B$ has no zero on $|s-\rho_0|=r$. Put $m=\min_{|s-\rho_0|=r}|B|>0$. The set of $\tau$ with $\sup_{\sigma\ge1/2+\varepsilon}|B(s+i\tau)-B(s)|<m$ is relatively dense. For each such $\tau$, Rouché's theorem gives a zero of $B$ in $|s-\rho_0-i\tau|<r$. Choosing these $\tau$ with spacing $>2r$ gives $\gg T$ distinct zeros of $B$, hence of $\zeta$, in $\sigma\ge\beta_0-r>\tfrac12+\varepsilon$ up to height $T$. This contradicts Carlson's theorem $N(\tfrac12+\varepsilon,T)=o(T)$ [10, Ch. IX]. Hence $B/s\notin S$, and by uniqueness of nearest points the inequality is strict. $\square$

For finite $Z^+$, version 2 gave a more elementary argument. For $f=\sum a_me_m\in S$ we have $\sigma f(\sigma)=a_1+O(\sqrt\sigma\,2^{-\sigma})$, while for a nonconstant rational $B$, $B(\sigma)-B(\infty)$ decays only like a power of $\sigma$.

**Proof of Corollary D.** By Lemma 1, $V\subset S\cap\ker\delta_\rho$. So $D_\infty\ge\operatorname{dist}(E,S\cap\ker\delta_\rho)=|E(\rho)|/\|r_\rho\|=1/(|\rho|\sqrt{K_S(\rho,\rho)})$. The same argument with a finite $F\subset Z^+$ gives $c_F^*\Gamma_F^{-1}c_F$.

For the explicit bound, write $R=|\rho|\ge1$. For $m\le R$ we use $|m^{-\rho}-(m+1)^{-\rho}|\le2m^{-\beta}$, and for $m>R$ the mean value bound $|m^{-\rho}-(m+1)^{-\rho}|\le Rm^{-\beta-1}$. Then

$$|\rho|^2K_S(\rho,\rho)=\sum_m m(m+1)|m^{-\rho}-(m+1)^{-\rho}|^2\le8\!\!\sum_{m\le R}\!m^{2-2\beta}+2R^2\!\!\sum_{m>R}\!m^{-2\beta}\le32R^{3-2\beta}+\frac{4R^{3-2\beta}}{2\beta-1}\le\frac{36R^{3-2\beta}}{2\beta-1}.$$

Here we used $\sum_{m\le R}m^{2-2\beta}\le(R+1)^{3-2\beta}\le4R^{3-2\beta}$ and $\sum_{m>R}m^{-2\beta}\le\lfloor R\rfloor^{1-2\beta}/(2\beta-1)\le2R^{1-2\beta}/(2\beta-1)$. $\square$

**Unconstrained polynomials.** Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [6] and Maier–Rassias [7, (1.1)] use the distance
$$\delta_N=\inf_{A}\Big\|\frac1s-\frac{A(s)\zeta(s)}{s}\Big\|,\qquad A(s)=\sum_{n\le N}a_nn^{-s},$$
with the norm of $L^2(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12,\,dt/2\pi)$. Here $A\zeta/s\notin H^2$ unless $A(1)=0$, and $A(1)=0$ gives exactly $\operatorname{span}\{G_2,\dots,G_N\}$. So $\delta_N\le d_N$.

**Proposition 6** (unconditional). $\delta_N\downarrow\delta_\infty$, and
$$c_0\,D_\infty^2\ \le\ \delta_\infty^2\ \le\ D_\infty^2,\qquad c_0=\frac{1+\kappa}{\log2\pi-\gamma_E}=0.85695\ldots,$$
where $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant and $\kappa=\operatorname{dist}(\{1/t\},\text{step space})^2=0.08032\ldots$ in $L^2(0,1)$. So dropping the constraint $A(1)=0$ changes the limiting distance by a factor of at most $c_0^{-1/2}<1.09$. If $|Z^+|<\infty$, then exactly $\delta_\infty^2=D_\infty^2-|c_H^*\Gamma^{-1}c|^2/(1+\kappa+c_H^*\Gamma^{-1}c_H)$, where $c_H$ is the jet vector of $P_SH$ (notation of Theorem C, $H$ below).

*Proof.* For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, $\int_0^1\{1/t\}t^{s-1}dt=\frac1{s-1}-\frac{\zeta(s)}s$. So $H:=\frac{\zeta(s)}s-\frac1{s-1}\in H^2$, with $\|H\|^2=\int_0^1\{1/t\}^2dt=\log2\pi-1-\gamma_E$. Also $\frac1{s-1}$ is the Mellin transform of $-t^{-1}\mathbf 1_{(1,\infty)}$, so on the line it is orthogonal to $H^2$ and has norm $1$. Since $n^{-s}\zeta/s=G_n+n^{-1}\big(H+\frac1{s-1}\big)$, with $G_1:=0$,
$$\frac{A\zeta}{s}=g+A(1)H+\frac{A(1)}{s-1},\qquad g=\sum_{2\le n\le N}a_nG_n .$$
The coefficient $a_1$ is free, so $(g,A(1))$ ranges over $V_N\times\mathbb C$. With $h_S=P_SH$ and $\kappa=\|H-h_S\|^2$, using $E\in S$ and $V_N\subset S$, this gives
$$\delta_N^2=\min_{c\in\mathbb C}\Big[(1+\kappa)|c|^2+\operatorname{dist}(E-c\,h_S,V_N)^2\Big].$$
Taking the infimum over $N$ first replaces $V_N$ by $V$, and $\operatorname{dist}(y,V)=\|\Pi y\|$ with $\Pi=P_{S\ominus V}$. In general $\min_c\big(\alpha|c|^2+\|a-cb\|^2\big)=\|a\|^2-|\langle a,b\rangle|^2/(\alpha+\|b\|^2)$. This lies between $\|a\|^2\alpha/(\alpha+\|b\|^2)$ and $\|a\|^2$. Take $a=\Pi E$, so $\|a\|^2=D_\infty^2$, and $b=\Pi h_S$. Then $\|b\|\le\|h_S\|$ and $1+\kappa+\|h_S\|^2=1+\|H\|^2=\log2\pi-\gamma_E$. When $|Z^+|<\infty$, Theorem C identifies $\langle \Pi E,\Pi h_S\rangle$ and $\|\Pi h_S\|^2$ with the Gram expressions. $\square$

`code/bcf_consts.py` gives $\|H\|^2$, which matches $\log2\pi-1-\gamma_E$ to 30 digits, along with $\|h_S\|^2=0.18033$ and $\kappa$. `code/bcf_cross.py` confirms that the cross term with $1/(s-1)$ vanishes, as $O(1/T)$ over $|t|\le T$. Replacing $\Pi$ by the projection onto $r_\rho$ gives the single-zero bound $\delta_\infty^2\ge(1+\kappa)|\rho|^{-2}/\big((1+\kappa)K_S(\rho,\rho)+|h_S(\rho)|^2\big)$. For $\beta\in\{0.6,0.75,0.9\}$ and $\gamma\in\{14,100\}$ it is within $0.1\%$ of Corollary D (`code/thmH_check.py`), because $|h_S(\rho)|^2/K_S(\rho,\rho)<10^{-3}$ there. The loss is in fact provably negligible. Write $\hat h_S=s^{-1}\sum_nb_nn^{-s}$ with $b_n$ the step differences (proof of Corollary F′). Then $|\rho\,h_S(\rho)|\le C_h:=\sum_n|b_n|n^{-1/2}=0.44736\ldots$ (`code/ch_const.py`; the sum is computed numerically to $n=2\cdot10^4$). For the tail, $c(y)=-\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^jy^{-j}/((j+1)(j+2))$ is an alternating series whose derivative series has decreasing terms for $y>1$. So $|c'(y)|\le1/(6y^2)$, and $|b_n|\le1/(6n(n-1))\le0.2/n^2$ for $n\ge6$. The tail beyond $2\cdot10^4$ is therefore below $10^{-6}$, so unconditionally $\delta_\infty^2\ge1/\big(|\rho|^2K_S(\rho,\rho)+C_h^2/(1+\kappa)\big)$. Since $C_h^2/(1+\kappa)<0.19$, $|\rho|>14$ and $2\beta-1<1$, the proof of Corollary D gives $\delta_\infty^2\ge(2\beta-1)|\rho|^{2\beta-3}/36.02$. By Remark 4, the ratio to the first bound in Corollary D is $1-O(\gamma^{2\beta-3})$.

## 5. Remarks
1. **Davenport–Heilbronn check.** No step proves RH. The Euler product appears only as a convenience: in Proposition 5, $c=p*\mu$ has bounded coefficients. The Perron step needs absolute convergence only on some far line, followed by contour shifts justified by Lemma 3. For any Dirichlet series with nonzero constant term, a geometric series in $(F-a_1)/a_1$ supplies that convergence. So this step does not distinguish $\zeta$ from Davenport–Heilbronn-type functions. For an $L$-function with infinitely many off-line zeros, Theorem A just says $\operatorname{codim}=\infty$.
2. **Where finiteness is used.** Lemma 4 survives if $Z^+\subset\{\operatorname{Re}s\ge\frac12+\delta\}$, even when $Z^+$ is infinite. For $b\ge\delta>\varepsilon$ we have $\log\frac{b^2+u^2}{(b-\varepsilon)^2+u^2}\le\frac{2b\varepsilon}{(b-\varepsilon)^2+u^2}$. Summing over zeros with $N(T+1)-N(T)\ll\log T$ gives $|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)|\ll(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon/2+C\varepsilon/(\delta-\varepsilon)^2}$, which still dominates in $L^2$ for small $\varepsilon$. What fails is the approximation step: for infinite $Z^+$ we know no Dirichlet polynomial, and in general no bounded Dirichlet series, vanishing on $Z^+$ (see §8, Problem 1). Given one, the rest goes through. If $D\not\equiv0$ converges and is bounded on $\Omega$ and vanishes on $Z^+$ with multiplicity, then $D/\zeta$ obeys Lemma 3, and dividing $D$ by the Blaschke product of $\Omega$ over the zeros near height $t$ repairs Lemma 4 for zeros arbitrarily close to the line, so $D/s\in V$ (Proposition 8.14). So finiteness is used essentially only in Proposition 5, through the existence of $P$. Under the dyadic Blaschke condition (DB) of §8, $D=B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})$ is such a series (Theorem 8.15).
3. **Unconditional content.** Theorem A and Corollaries B and D assume nothing. Proposition 6 assumes nothing for its two-sided bound. Theorems C, E and F and Corollary F′ assume $|Z^+|<\infty$. If $|Z^+|=\infty$, then $V\subset\ker J$ still holds. $V=\ker J$ (spectral synthesis) holds under (DB) (Theorem 8.15) and is open otherwise.
4. **Sharp form of Corollary D.** For fixed $\beta\in(\tfrac12,1)$ and $\gamma\to\infty$, we have $|\rho|^2K_S(\rho,\rho)\sim c_\beta\,\gamma^{3-2\beta}$ with $c_\beta=\int_0^\infty x^{2-2\beta}\,4\sin^2\!\frac1{2x}\,dx=-2\Gamma(2\beta-3)\cos\frac{\pi(2\beta-3)}2$. So the single-zero bound is $\sim\gamma^{2\beta-3}/c_\beta$. To see this, write $K=\sum_m m(m+1)|m^{-\rho}-(m+1)^{-\rho}|^2$ and let $X=\gamma^{1/2+\epsilon}$. The terms with $m<X$ are each $\le8m^{2-2\beta}$, so they contribute $O(X^{3-2\beta})$. For $m\ge X$ we have $\gamma/m^2\le1$, and $(1+\frac1m)^{-\rho}=e^{-i\gamma\log(1+1/m)}(1+\frac1m)^{-\beta}$ gives the additive form $m^{-\rho}-(m+1)^{-\rho}=m^{-\rho}\big[a_m+b_m\big]$, with $a_m=1-e^{-i\gamma/m}$ and $b_m=O(m^{-1}+\gamma m^{-2})$. Since $|a_m|\le\min(2,\gamma/m)$, we get $\big||a_m+b_m|^2-|a_m|^2\big|\ll|a_m||b_m|+|b_m|^2\ll\gamma m^{-2}+m^{-2}$ for $m\ge\gamma$, and $\ll\gamma m^{-2}$ for $X\le m<\gamma$. Replacing $m(m+1)$ by $m^2$ costs a relative $O(1/m)$. So on $m\ge X$ the summand is $F(m)=4m^{2-2\beta}\sin^2\frac\gamma{2m}$ up to an error whose sum is $\ll\gamma\sum_{m\ge X}m^{-2\beta}+\gamma^{2-2\beta}\ll\gamma X^{1-2\beta}+\gamma^{2-2\beta}$. The total variation of $F$ on $[X,\infty)$ is $\ll\gamma^{2-2\beta}+\gamma X^{1-2\beta}$, so $\sum_{m\ge X}F(m)=\int_X^\infty F+O(\gamma^{2-2\beta}+\gamma X^{1-2\beta}+X^{2-2\beta})$, and $\int_X^\infty F=\gamma^{3-2\beta}\big(c_\beta+O((X/\gamma)^{3-2\beta})\big)$ after $m=\gamma x$. All error terms are $O(\gamma^{(1/2+\epsilon)(3-2\beta)}+\gamma^{3/2-\beta})=o(\gamma^{3-2\beta})$ for small $\epsilon$, since $\beta<1$. Numerically (`code/ks_asym.py`) the ratio is $1+O(10^{-4})$ at $\gamma=10^3,10^4$ for $\beta=0.6,0.75,0.9$. Since $(2\beta-1)c_\beta\in(1,2.5)$ on this range, the constant $36$ in Corollary D is far from sharp.
5. **Dirichlet $L$-functions.** Let $\chi$ be a nonprincipal character and $V_\chi=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{l^{-s}L(s,\chi)/s:l\ge1\}$. Here $L(s,\chi)/s$ is the Mellin transform of the bounded step function $t\mapsto\sum_{n\le1/t}\chi(n)$, so $V_\chi\subset S$ and no $l^{-1}$ correction is needed. Then $\operatorname{codim}_SV_\chi$ equals the number of zeros of $L(s,\chi)$ in $\Omega$, and (1.1) holds when that number is finite. The equivalence "GRH for $\chi$ $\iff V_\chi=S$" is the case of zero off-line zeros; sequential criteria of this kind for the Selberg class are due to de Roton [11]. Only the count is new here. The proof is the same. Lemma 2 concerns $S$ only. Lemma 3 and the mean square are standard for $L(s,\chi)$, and $c=p*(\mu\chi)$ is bounded. For Lemma 4, first let $\chi$ be primitive, and pair the Hadamard factors of $\Lambda(\frac12+z,\chi)$ under $\gamma\mapsto-\bar\gamma$. The functional equation $\Lambda(s,\chi)=\varepsilon_\chi\Lambda(1-s,\bar\chi)$, $|\varepsilon_\chi|=1$, together with $\overline{\Lambda(\bar s,\bar\chi)}=\Lambda(s,\chi)$, gives $|\Lambda(\frac12+z,\chi)|=|\Lambda(\frac12-\bar z,\chi)|$. So the linear exponential has purely imaginary coefficient. The factors $e^{z/\gamma}$ then have modulus depending only on $\operatorname{Im}z$, and the factor-by-factor comparison is unchanged. If $\chi$ mod $q$ is induced by a primitive $\chi^*$, then $L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi^*)\prod_{p\mid q}(1-\chi^*(p)p^{-s})$. The finite product has all its zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=0$, so the zeros in $\Omega$ are those of $L(s,\chi^*)$. Its ratio at $s$ and $s+\varepsilon$ is bounded above and below on $\sigma\ge\tfrac12$, uniformly for $0\le\varepsilon\le1$, so Lemma 4 for $\chi$ follows from Lemma 4 for $\chi^*$.

## 7. Second-order lower bounds
We work in Burnol's setting [9]. Let $Z(s)=(s-1)\zeta(s)/s^2\in H^2$ with inverse Mellin transform $A$. Let $C_\lambda=\operatorname{span}\{D_\theta A:\lambda\le\theta\le1\}$, $\hat\chi_1=1/s-1/s^2$, and $D(\lambda)=\operatorname{dist}(\chi_1,C_\lambda)$. Under RH, Burnol proved $\liminf D(\lambda)^2\log(1/\lambda)\ge\sum_\rho m(\rho)^2/|\rho|^2$ [9, Thm 1.3]. Without RH the left side is $+\infty$, and the result says nothing. Write $B$ for the Blaschke product over $Z^+$, normalized so $B(1)>0$.

**Theorem E** (without RH; assumes $|Z^+|<\infty$). $D(\lambda)^2\to \mathcal D_\infty^2:=1-B(1)^2-B'(1)^2$, and

$$\liminf_{\lambda\to0}\,\big(D(\lambda)^2-\mathcal D_\infty^2\big)\log\tfrac1\lambda\ \ge\ \sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2}m(\rho)^2\,|r(\rho)|^2,\qquad r(s)=B(s)\Big(\frac{B(1)+B'(1)}{s}-\frac{B(1)}{s^2}\Big).$$

Here $r=P_{BH^2}\hat\chi_1$, and $\mathcal D_\infty$ is the continuous limit, which differs from the $D_\infty$ of Theorem C. Under RH, $B\equiv1$ and $|r(\rho)|=|\rho-1|/|\rho|^2=1/|\rho|$, which is Burnol's theorem. In general $|r(\rho)|=|(B(1)+B'(1))\rho-B(1)|/|\rho|^2\ne0$ for nonreal $\rho$.

*Proof.* (i) $Z=B\cdot(\text{outer})$, so $C_\lambda\subset BH^2$ and $K_B:=H^2\ominus BH^2\perp C_\lambda$ for all $\lambda$. $K_B$ is spanned by the kernels $k^{(j)}_w$, $k_w(s)=1/(s+\bar w-1)$, $w\in Z^+$, $j<m(w)$. The union of the $C_\lambda$ is dense in $BH^2$ [5], so $\mathcal D_\infty=\operatorname{dist}(\chi_1,BH^2)$. The closed forms for $\mathcal D_\infty$ and $r$ follow from $P_{BH^2}(1/s)=B(1)B/s$ [5, Lemma 2.4] and its $\bar\partial$-derivative in the kernel point. We checked both numerically against the Gram-matrix formulas to 15 digits (`code/thmE_check.py`).
(ii) For $\rho$ on the line and $k<m(\rho)$, Burnol's vectors $X^\lambda_{\rho,k}$ are $\perp C_\lambda$ [9, Cor 4.4], and their Gram matrix tends to the block-Cauchy matrix $\big(1/(k+l+1)\big)$ [9, Thm 5.3]. Neither step uses RH: only $\zeta^{(j)}(\rho)=0$ enters [9, Note 5.2].
(iii) *Key lemma.* For $g\in K_B\cup\{\chi_1\}$: $\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}\,(g,X^\lambda_{\rho,0})\to\hat g(\rho)$, and $\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}\,(g,X^\lambda_{\rho,k})\to0$ for $k\ge1$. Indeed $\mathcal VY^\lambda_{\rho,k}=\mathbf 1_{[\lambda,\infty)}\Phi_{\rho,k}$ with $\Phi_{\rho,k}$ independent of $\lambda$. Here $\mathcal V$ is the unitary with multiplier $\mathcal V(s)=(s/(1-s))^3\zeta(1-s)/\zeta(s)$. By [9, Thm 4.7–4.11] (uniform in $w$ on compacts of $0<\operatorname{Re}w<1$), $\Phi_{w,k}(t)=O(t^{-\operatorname{Re}w}\log^k(1/t)+1)$ on $(0,1]$ and $O((1+\log t)^2/t)$ on $[1,\infty)$. For $g=k^{(j)}_{w'}$, the function $\mathcal V\hat g$ is analytic on $\tfrac12-a\le\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12$ for $0<a<\min(\tfrac12,\beta'-\tfrac12)$, since the pole of $\hat g$ is at $1-\bar w'$, which has real part $1-\beta'<\tfrac12$. It is also $O(|t|^{-1})$ there, because $\zeta(1-s)/\zeta(s)=\pi^{1/2-s}\Gamma(s/2)/\Gamma((1-s)/2)$ [9, §3] is analytic in the strip and, by Stirling, has modulus $\asymp|t|^{\sigma-1/2}\le1$ for large $|t|$. The strip Paley–Wiener theorem then gives $t^{-a}\mathcal Vg\in L^2(0,\infty)$. For $\chi_1$ the same holds with $\mathcal Vg=O(t^2)$ at $0$ [9, proof of 5.4]. Dominated convergence gives $(g,Y^\lambda_{\rho,k})\to\int_0^\infty \mathcal Vg\,\overline{\Phi_{\rho,k}}$, which is finite; the scaling $(\log1/\lambda)^{-1/2-k}$ kills $k\ge1$. For $k=0$ and $\operatorname{Re}w<\tfrac12$, $\int \mathcal Vg\,\overline{\mathcal V\psi_w}=(g,\psi_w)=\hat g(1-\bar w)$, and letting $w\to\rho$ under the same uniform bounds yields $\hat g(\rho)$.
(iv) Put $\tilde X=X-P_{K_B}X$. By (iii), $\|P_{K_B}X^\lambda\|\to0$, so the Gram matrix of $\tilde X$ has the same block-Cauchy limit. Let $r=\chi_1-P_{K_B}\chi_1\perp K_B$, so $(r,\tilde X)=(r,X)$, and $\sqrt{\log}\,(r,X_{\rho,0})\to\hat r(\rho)$ by (iii). Since $K_B$ and the $X^\lambda$ are both $\perp C_\lambda$, we have $D(\lambda)^2\ge\|P_{K_B}\chi_1\|^2+\|P_{\tilde X}r\|^2$, and $\|P_{K_B}\chi_1\|^2=\mathcal D_\infty^2$. The top-left entry of the inverse of the $m\times m$ Cauchy matrix is $m^2$, so $\|P_{\tilde X}r\|^2\log(1/\lambda)\to\sum_{\rho\in R}m(\rho)^2|\hat r(\rho)|^2$ for every finite set $R$ of on-line zeros. Take the supremum over $R$. $\square$

The same key lemma gives the discrete statement. Let $\mathcal I f=\frac{s-1}{s}f$. It is an isometry of $H^2$ (an inner multiplier), with $\mathcal I E=\hat\chi_1$ and $\mathcal I G_l=(l^{-s}-l^{-1})Z=l^{-1/2}\widehat{D_{1/l}A}-l^{-1}\hat A\in C_{1/l}$. Hence $\mathcal I V_N\subset C_{1/N}$, where $V_N=\operatorname{span}\{G_2,\dots,G_N\}$.

**Theorem F** (without RH; assumes $|Z^+|<\infty$). Let $u=P_VE$. With the notation of Theorem C,

$$\liminf_{N\to\infty}\,\big(d_N^2-D_\infty^2\big)\log N\ \ge\ \sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2}m(\rho)^2\,|u(\rho)|^2 .$$

Under RH, $u=E$, and this is Burnol's bound $\sum m(\rho)^2/|\rho|^2$ [9].

*Proof.* The vectors $X^{1/N}_{\rho,k}$ are $\perp C_{1/N}\supset\mathcal I V_N$. The space $\mathcal R:=\mathcal I\operatorname{span}\{r_a\}$ is also $\perp\mathcal I V_N$, because $r_a\perp V$ (Lemma 1) and $\mathcal I$ is isometric. So $d_N^2=\operatorname{dist}(\hat\chi_1,\mathcal I V_N)^2\ge\|P_{\mathcal R}\hat\chi_1\|^2+\|P_{\tilde X}\,\mathcal I u\|^2$. The first term is $c^*\Gamma^{-1}c=D_\infty^2$ by Theorem C, and $\hat\chi_1-P_{\mathcal R}\hat\chi_1=\mathcal I u$. On the line, $|(\mathcal I u)(\rho)|=|u(\rho)|$. The argument of (iv) applies verbatim with $\mathcal R$ in place of $K_B$ (it is finite-dimensional by Theorem A) once the key lemma (iii) holds for $g\in \mathcal R$. It remains to check the key lemma (iii) for $g=\mathcal I r_a$, $a=(\rho',j)$. The representer $r_a=P_S\,k^{(j)}_{\rho'}$ is the step function with value $c_n=n(n+1)\int_n^{n+1}(-\log x)^j x^{-\bar\rho'-1}dx$ on $(\frac1{n+1},\frac1n]$. Thus $\hat r_a(s)=s^{-1}\sum_n(c_n-c_{n-1})n^{-s}$. Expanding $c_n$ in powers $n^{1-\bar\rho'-i}\log^{j'}n$ writes $\hat r_a$ as $s^{-1}$ times a finite combination of $\zeta^{(j')}(s+\bar\rho'+i)$, plus a Dirichlet series analytic in $\sigma>1-\beta'-K$ for any fixed $K$. The only pole near the strip is at $1-\bar\rho'$, which has real part $1-\beta'$. On $\tfrac12-a\le\sigma\le\tfrac12$ with $a<\min(\tfrac12,\beta'-\tfrac12)$ we have $\operatorname{Re}(s+\bar\rho')>1$, so $\zeta^{(j')}(s+\bar\rho'+i)\ll\log^{j'+1}(2+|t|)$ uniformly. So $\mathcal V\,\mathcal I\hat r_a\ll|t|^{-1+\epsilon}$, which is in $L^2$, and strip Paley–Wiener applies as before. $\square$

**Corollary F′** (without RH; assumes $|Z^+|<\infty$). For the unconstrained distance $\delta_N$ of Proposition 6, let $c_*=\langle\Pi E,\Pi h_S\rangle/(1+\kappa+\|\Pi h_S\|^2)$ be the minimizing $c$ in its proof, and $u_*=P_V(E-c_*h_S)$. Then
$$\liminf_{N\to\infty}\,\big(\delta_N^2-\delta_\infty^2\big)\log N\ \ge\ \sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2}m(\rho)^2\,|u_*(\rho)|^2 .$$
Under RH, $c_*=0$ and $u_*=E$, so $\liminf\delta_N^2\log N\ge\sum m(\rho)^2/|\rho|^2$, the lower half of the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer asymptotic [6]; Burnol [9] gives lower bounds of this type under RH. The content of Corollary F′ is the case $Z^+\ne\emptyset$.

*Proof.* Put $f_c=E-ch_S\in S$ and $u_c=P_Vf_c=u_0-cu_1$. As in the proof of Theorem F, $\mathcal I\Pi f_c\in\mathcal R$ and $\mathcal R\perp\tilde X\perp\mathcal I V_N$, so $\operatorname{dist}(f_c,V_N)^2\ge\|\Pi f_c\|^2+\|P_{\tilde X}\mathcal Iu_c\|^2$. Hence $\delta_N^2\ge\min_c[F(c)+Q_N(c)/\log N]$, where $F(c)=(1+\kappa)|c|^2+\|\Pi f_c\|^2$ and $Q_N(c)=\log N\,\|P_{\tilde X}\mathcal Iu_c\|^2$. $F$ is a quadratic with minimum $\delta_\infty^2$ at $c_*$, so $F(c)\ge\delta_\infty^2+(1+\kappa)|c-c_*|^2$. Each $Q_N$ is a Hermitian form in $(1,c)$. Once the key lemma holds for $\mathcal Ih_S$, the argument of (iv) gives $Q_N(c)\to\sum_{\rho\in R}m(\rho)^2|u_c(\rho)|^2$ for each $c$, for every finite set $R$ of on-line zeros. By polarization this convergence is coefficientwise, hence uniform for bounded $c$. Let $\tau=1/\log N$. If $|c-c_*|\ge\tau^{1/3}$, the bracket exceeds $\delta_\infty^2+(1+\kappa)\tau^{2/3}$, which is $\delta_\infty^2+\tau\cdot\tau^{-1/3}(1+\kappa)$. Otherwise $Q_N(c)=Q_N(c_*)+O(\tau^{1/3})$. Either way the liminf is at least the limit of $Q_N(c_*)$, and the supremum over $R$ gives the claim. For the key lemma, $h_S=-P_S\{1/t\}$ has step values $c_m=-m(m+1)\big(\log(1+\tfrac1m)-\tfrac1{m+1}\big)=-\tfrac12+\tfrac1{6m}+O(m^{-2})$. So $\hat h_S=s^{-1}\sum_nb_nn^{-s}$ with $b_n=c_n-c_{n-1}=O(n^{-2})$, a Dirichlet series that converges absolutely and is bounded on $\sigma>-1+\epsilon$. Then $\mathcal V\mathcal I\hat h_S=-s(1-s)^{-2}\,\zeta(1-s)/\zeta(s)\cdot\sum b_nn^{-s}$ is analytic on the strip $\tfrac12-a\le\sigma\le\tfrac12$ and $O(|t|^{-1})$ there, and strip Paley–Wiener applies as before. $\square$

So the lower-bound half of Former Conjecture R (Theorem F) holds whenever $|Z^+|<\infty$. The upper-bound half is [25, Theorem 6]. Theorem E is the continuous counterpart. In both cases, the second-order term is governed by the *approximable* part of the target evaluated at the on-line zeros, as in the toy model of §8.

## 8. Open problems and resolved cases
1. **Spectral synthesis.** If $|Z^+|=\infty$, is $V=\{f\in S: \text{all jets at }Z^+\text{ vanish}\}$? Equivalently, is $\lim d_N^2=\sup_F c_F^*\Gamma_F^{-1}c_F$? If $Z^+\ne\emptyset$, no Dirichlet polynomial has zero set in $\Omega$ equal to $Z^+$. If a Dirichlet polynomial has one zero in a strip $\tfrac12+\delta\le\sigma\le\sigma_1$, then it has $\gg T$ zeros up to height $T$ there: its almost periods $\tau$ (uniform in the strip) are relatively dense, and Rouché on a small disc around the zero, shifted by $i\tau$, gives one zero per almost period, while Carlson's density theorem [10, Ch. IX] gives $N(\tfrac12+\delta,T)=o(T)$. So the zero set of such a polynomial in $\Omega$ is never contained in $Z^+$: a $P$ vanishing on $Z^+$ always has extra zeros. (Manzur–Noor [12] use the same density fact to exclude infinite progressions of zeros.) This does not obstruct the route of §3. The extra zeros are zeros of $P/\zeta$, and the proof of Proposition 5 never divides by $P$. Nor does the density fact rule out the reverse inclusion $Z^+\subseteq Z(P)$ for infinite $Z^+$. For example, $1-2^{\beta-s}$ vanishes on every subset, however sparse, of the progression $\beta+2\pi ik/\log2$ (remark of u/bramble_trace). For infinite $Z^+$ the route of §3 needs three things: (a) Dirichlet polynomials, or bounded Dirichlet series $Q$, with $Z^+\subseteq Z(Q)$ counted with multiplicity; (b) Lemma 3- and Lemma 4-type bounds for $Q/\zeta$; (c) enough such $Q$ that the resulting elements of $V$ are dense in the jet kernel. Proposition 8.14 below shows that (a) implies (b), and then $QS\subseteq V$. Proposition 8.10 below shows that a nonzero $Q$ with $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2}<\infty$ satisfies (a) only if every section $\Lambda_\beta(Z^+)$ has empty interior. Under the dyadic Blaschke condition (DB) below, $Q=B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})$ (a series in $2^{-s}$, absolutely convergent on $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12$) supplies (a), Proposition 8.13 replaces (c), and synthesis holds (Theorem 8.15). Without (DB), (a) and (c) are open.
   *Reformulation.* $M_n f=n^{-s}f$ is a contraction on $S$, and $n^{-s}G_l=G_{nl}-l^{-1}G_n$. So $V$ is $M_n$-invariant for every $n$, and $S\ominus V$ is invariant under every $M_n^*$. The jet representers satisfy $M_n^*r_\rho=\overline{n^{-\rho}}\,r_\rho$, with generalized eigenvectors at multiple zeros. Spectral synthesis asks whether $S\ominus V$ is spanned by its generalized common eigenvectors; compare the common eigenvectors of $W_n^*$ in [4, Prop 22]. By Corollary 8.12 below, the root vectors of $M_2^*$ alone suffice. By Remark 8.11, $M$-invariance alone does not imply synthesis. Manzur–Noor [12] recently gave a related invariant-subspace criterion: if $\overline{\mathcal N}$ is invariant under a certain bounded operator $T$ on $H^2$, then either RH holds or $\zeta$ has an infinite progression of zeros. Beurling-type descriptions of multiplier-invariant subspaces are known to fail for Hardy spaces of Dirichlet series in general, which suggests the problem is hard.
   *Division by Dirichlet polynomials.* Synthesis does hold for the simplest $M$-invariant subspaces with infinite zero sets.

   **Proposition 8.1.** Let $P(s)=\sum_{n\le N}c_nn^{-s}$ with $c_1\ne0$ and $c:=\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P|>0$. Then $PS$ is closed, and $PS=J_P$, the subspace of $f\in S$ vanishing to order $\ge m_P(z)$ at every zero $z$ of $P$ in $\Omega$.

   *Proof.* $\|Pf\|\ge c\|f\|$ on $H^2$, so $PS$ is closed, and clearly $PS\subset J_P$. Let $f\in J_P$ and $F=f/P$, which is holomorphic on $\Omega$.
   (a) $F\in H^2$. Write $P=B_PS_PO_P$ (inner–outer factorization on $\Omega$, up to a unimodular constant), with $B_P$ the Blaschke product over $Z(P)\cap\Omega$. $P$ is holomorphic across the line, so $\log|P(\sigma+it)|\to\log|P(\tfrac12+it)|$ in $L^1$ on compact $t$-intervals as $\sigma\downarrow\tfrac12$. Hence the singular measure of $P$ has no mass on the line, and $S_P=e^{-a(s-1/2)}$ with $a\ge0$. Since $|B_PS_P|\le1$, $|O_P|\le\sup_\Omega|P|$, and $P(\sigma)\to c_1\ne0$ as $\sigma\to\infty$, we get $a=0$. So $P=B_PO_P$ whenever $c_1\ne0$. Since $f$ vanishes on $Z(P)\cap\Omega$ with multiplicity, $f=B_Pf_1$ with $f_1\in H^2$. So $F=f_1/O_P$ lies in the Smirnov class $N^+$. On the line $|F|\le|f|/c$, so $F\in H^2$ by Smirnov's theorem.
   (b) $F\in S$. By Paley–Wiener $F=\hat g$ with $g\in L^2(0,1)$, and $n^{-s}\hat g$ is the transform of $g(nu)\mathbf 1_{nu\le1}$. So $h(u)=\sum_nc_ng(nu)\mathbf 1_{nu\le1}$, the inverse transform of $f$, is constant on every $I_m=(\frac1{m+1},\frac1m]$. For $u\in I_m$ we have $nu\in I_{\lfloor m/n\rfloor}$ if $2\le n\le m$, and $nu>1$ if $n>m$. Hence $c_1g=h-\sum_{2\le n\le m}c_ng(n\,\cdot)$ on $I_m$, and induction on $m$ shows that $g$ is constant on every $I_m$. $\square$

   Part (b) uses only $c_1\ne0$: if $g\in H^2$ and $Pg\in S$, then $g\in S$.

   The line condition is necessary for closedness. For every Dirichlet polynomial $P$, $\inf\{\|Pf\|:f\in S,\|f\|=1\}=\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P|$. The inequality $\ge$ holds on all of $H^2$. For $\le$, Kronecker's theorem says the infimum is $|P_\chi|$ for some $\chi$ in the torus $\prod_{p\le N}\mathbb T$, where $P_\chi=\sum_nc_n\chi_nn^{-1/2}$ and $\chi_n$ is extended completely multiplicatively. Put $D_k=\prod_{p\le N}\big(\tfrac12(1+\bar\chi_pp^{1/2}p^{-s})\big)^k$, so $D_k/s\in S$. On the line $|D_k|\le1$, with equality only where $(p^{-it})_p=\chi$. Fix a neighbourhood $U$ of $\chi$. Then $|D_k|\le q^k$ when $(p^{-it})_p\notin U$, while $|D_k|\ge q'^k$ with $q'>q$ on a set of $t$ of positive measure. So the normalized measures $|D_k|^2dt/|s|^2$ concentrate on $\{t:(p^{-it})_p\in U\}$, and $\|PD_k/s\|/\|D_k/s\|\to|P_\chi|$ (slowly, `code/closedness_dk.py`). Since $M_P$ is injective, $PS$ is closed iff $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P|>0$.

   Numerically, take $P=1-2\cdot2^{-s}-\tfrac12\,3^{-s}$, with $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P|\approx0.126$ and zeros in $0.70<\sigma<1.21$. Then $\operatorname{dist}(E,P\operatorname{span}\{e_m\}_{m\le M})^2$ decreases to $0.9720764$ at $M=10^7$. Meanwhile $c_F^*\Gamma_F^{-1}c_F$ over the $89$ zeros with $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le400$ increases to $0.9720763$, as $PS=J_P$ predicts (`code/two_prime_synth.py`). For $P=1-2\cdot2^{-s}-3^{-s}$ we have $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P|=0$ by Kronecker, since $1=\sqrt2z_1+z_2/\sqrt3$ is solvable on the torus. So $PS$ is not closed and Proposition 8.1 does not apply. Still, the same two quantities agree to $3\cdot10^{-7}$ ($0.9323284$ at $M=3\cdot10^6$ and $0.9323282$ with $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le200$), as Proposition 8.4 below predicts.

   The same proof works for $Q=\sum_nc_nn^{-s}$ with $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2}<\infty$, $c_1\ne0$ and $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|Q|>0$. In (a), $Q$ need not extend across the line. Instead, uniform continuity of $Q$ on $\overline\Omega$ gives $|Q|\ge c/2$ on a strip $\tfrac12\le\sigma\le\tfrac12+\delta$, which rules out a singular inner factor carried by the line, and the factor at infinity is excluded as before. In (b) the sum is still finite on each $I_m$. So division is not the obstruction for absolutely convergent Dirichlet series; what Problem 1 lacks is a way to produce elements of $V$ from such $Q$ when $Z^+$ is infinite.

   For $P=1-2^{-s}/\lambda$ with $0<|\lambda|<2^{-1/2}$, the zeros form the full progression $s_0+2\pi ik/\log2$, $\operatorname{Re}s_0=-\log_2|\lambda|>\tfrac12$, with $\asymp T$ points up to height $T$. In this case the complement can be seen directly. In the coordinates $f=\sum a_me_m$, $(M_2a)_n=a_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$, and $w_{2m}+w_{2m+1}=w_m/2$ for $w_m=\|e_m\|^2$. So $2^{1/2}M_2$ is an isometry, a shift on the binary tree rooted at $1$ [13]. $(PS)^\perp=\ker(M_2^*-\bar\lambda)$ is parametrized by dyadic martingales $(\phi_L)$ on $(\frac12,1]$ with norm $\sum_L(2|\lambda|^2)^L\|\phi_L\|^2$, and the evaluation at $s_k$ corresponds to the complete system $x^{\bar s_k-1}$. (In the disc model, $W_n$ with $W_nh_k=h_{nk}-h_n$ plays the role of $nM_n$, since $h_k\leftrightarrow-kG_k$. Manzur–Noor–Santos [14, Prop 4] proved that $W_n/\sqrt n$ is a shift of infinite multiplicity and studied $\ker W_n^*$ [14, Lemmas 8, 11]. The eigenspaces with $\mu\ne0$ and their relation to zeros are not treated there.) The condition $c_1\ne0$ is needed: $P=2^{-s}$ has no zeros, but $M_2S$ has infinite codimension, a "zero at $\operatorname{Re}s=+\infty$" like a singular inner factor. The proof uses no commutation between different $M_n$. This matters because $M_2^*M_3\ne M_3M_2^*$ (`code/double_commute.py`), so Wold-type arguments for doubly commuting isometries do not apply to, e.g., $P=1-a2^{-s}-b3^{-s}$.

   **Corollary 8.2** (one Báez-Duarte generator). For $l\ge2$ let $P_l=l^{-s}-l^{-1}$, so that $G_l=P_l\zeta/s$, and let $[G_l]=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{n^{-s}G_l:n\ge1\}$ be the $M$-invariant subspace generated by $G_l$. Let $z_k=1+2\pi ik/\log l$. Then:
   (i) $[G_l]=P_lV\dotplus\mathbb C\,G_l$ (direct sum), and $\operatorname{codim}_S[G_l]=\infty$;
   (ii) $[G_l]$ is the subspace of $S$ where all jets at $Z^+$ and the values at the $z_k$, $k\ne0$, vanish iff $V$ is the subspace where all jets at $Z^+$ vanish; in particular both hold if $|Z^+|<\infty$;
   (iii) RH holds iff $[G_l]=P_lS\dotplus\mathbb C\,G_l$.
   Here $-lG_l=(1-l^{1-s})\zeta/s$ is the transform of the step function equal to $m\bmod l$ on $I_m$ (Noor's $r_l$). For $l=2$ this is $\eta/s$, with $\eta$ the alternating zeta function.

   *Proof.* $P_l$ has $c_1=-1/l$ and $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s=1/2}|P_l|=l^{-1/2}-l^{-1}>0$. Its zeros are the simple zeros $z_k$; $\zeta\ne0$ there for $k\ne0$, and $G_l(1)=-l^{-1}\log l\ne0$. (i) From $n^{-s}\zeta/s=G_n+n^{-1}\zeta/s$ we get $n^{-s}G_l=P_lG_n+n^{-1}G_l$, so $[G_l]$ is the closure of $P_lV+\mathbb CG_l$. Here $P_lV$ is closed since $P_l$ is bounded below, and $G_l\notin P_lH^2$ since $\zeta/s\notin H^2$. Every element of $[G_l]$ vanishes at the $z_k$, $k\ne0$, and these evaluations are linearly independent on $S$ by the proof of Lemma 2, which applies to any finite subset of $\Omega$. (ii) Let $J'$ be the subspace in (ii) and $J_{Z^+}$ the jet kernel at $Z^+$; then $[G_l]\subset J'$. Suppose $V=J_{Z^+}$ and $f\in J'$. Then $f_0=f-\tfrac{f(1)}{G_l(1)}G_l$ vanishes at every $z_k$, so $f_0=P_lg$ with $g\in S$ by Proposition 8.1, and $g\in J_{Z^+}=V$ because $P_l\ne0$ on $Z^+$. Conversely, suppose $[G_l]=J'$ and $g\in J_{Z^+}$. Then $P_lg\in J'$, so $P_lg=P_lv+cG_l$ with $v\in V$. Evaluating at $1$ gives $c=0$, so $g=v\in V$. The case $|Z^+|<\infty$ is Theorem A. (iii) By Báez-Duarte's theorem RH $\iff V=S$. If $V=S$, (i) gives the claim. Conversely, if $P_lS\subset P_lV\dotplus\mathbb CG_l$, then $G_l\notin P_lH^2$ forces $P_lS\subset P_lV$, so $V=S$. $\square$

   So the Euler-type factor $P_l$ adds infinitely many vanishing conditions but no difficulty. Synthesis for the subspace generated by a single Báez-Duarte function is equivalent to synthesis for $V$. What remains in Problem 1 is the passage through $\zeta$. Division off the line is not the issue (see the remark after Proposition 8.1 on Dirichlet series $Q$). For a bounded Dirichlet series $Q$ vanishing on $Z^+$, the bounds of Lemma 3 and Lemma 4 type for $Q/\zeta$ now hold (Proposition 8.14). What is missing is the existence of such a $Q$ and a one-target step for it (see the end of Problem 1).

   *Zeros on the line.* For polynomials in a single prime, the condition on the line can be dropped.

   **Proposition 8.3.** Let $P(s)=\sum_{j\le d}a_j2^{-js}$ with $a_0\ne0$. Then $\overline{PS}=J_P$.

   *Proof.* Let $V_2=\sqrt2M_2$. It is an isometry of $S$ (see the case $P=1-2^{-s}/\lambda$ above), and $\bigcap_kV_2^kS=0$ because $M_2^kS$ consists of transforms of functions supported in $(0,2^{-k}]$. By the Wold decomposition, $S\cong H^2(\mathbb D;W)$ with $W=S\ominus M_2S$, and $V_2$ becomes multiplication by $z$. Then $M_P=p(V_2)$ with $p(z)=\sum_ja_j2^{-j/2}z^j$. Write $p=BO$, where $B$ is the finite Blaschke product over the zeros $z_i$ of $p$ in $\mathbb D$, with multiplicities $m_i$, and $O$ is a polynomial with no zeros in $\mathbb D$. Since $O$ is outer, $O(V_2)$ has dense range. So $\overline{PS}=BH^2(\mathbb D;W)$, and this equals $\bigcap_i(V_2-z_i)^{m_i}S$, because $(z-z_i)^{m_i}H^2=b_i^{m_i}H^2$ with $b_i$ the Blaschke factor at $z_i$. Here $z_i\ne0$ because $a_0\ne0$, and $V_2-z_i=-z_i(1-2^{-s}/\lambda_i)$ with $\lambda_i=z_i/\sqrt2$ and $0<|\lambda_i|<2^{-1/2}$. The polynomial $P_i=(1-2^{-s}/\lambda_i)^{m_i}$ is bounded below on the line, so Proposition 8.1 gives $(V_2-z_i)^{m_i}S=P_iS=J_{P_i}$. Finally, $z=\sqrt2\,2^{-s}$ maps $\Omega$ onto $\mathbb D\setminus\{0\}$, so the zeros of $P$ in $\Omega$ are those of the $P_i$, which are pairwise disjoint, and $\bigcap_iJ_{P_i}=J_P$. $\square$

   For example, $P=2^{-s}-2^{-1/2}$ vanishes on the line at $\tfrac12+2\pi ik/\log2$. Then $\overline{PS}=S$, although $PS\ne S$. The zeros in $\Omega$ of such $P$ lie on finitely many vertical lines $\operatorname{Re}s=-\log_2|\lambda_i|>\tfrac12$. With several primes they can accumulate at the line, as for $1-2\cdot2^{-s}-3^{-s}$. There a domination argument replaces Wold. The idea of damping the boundary zeros with positive-real-part multipliers is due to u/halasz_resonator (rStation r/riemann_hypothesis, post 1).

   **Proposition 8.4.** Let $P$ be a Dirichlet polynomial with $c_1\ne0$. Suppose there are Dirichlet polynomials $Q_1,\dots,Q_r\not\equiv0$ ($r\ge0$) with $\operatorname{Re}Q_i\ge0$ and $|Q_1\cdots Q_r|\le C|P|$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12$. Then $\overline{PS}=J_P$. The hypothesis holds whenever the Bohr symbol $\tilde P(z)=\sum_nc_nn^{-1/2}\prod_pz_p^{v_p(n)}$ has finitely many zeros on the torus $\mathbb T^k$ indexed by the $k$ primes dividing some $n$ with $c_n\ne0$.

   *Proof.* $\operatorname{Re}Q_i$ is bounded and harmonic on $\Omega$, hence the Poisson integral of its boundary values, so $\operatorname{Re}Q_i\ge0$ on $\overline\Omega$. For $\delta>0$ the polynomial $Q_i+\delta$ has $\operatorname{Re}\ge\delta$ on $\overline\Omega$, and its constant term $c_1(Q_i)+\delta=\lim_{\sigma\to\infty}Q_i(\sigma)+\delta$ is nonzero. Proposition 8.1 then gives $(Q_i+\delta)S=S$. Let $f\in J_P$, $A=\prod_iQ_i$ and $R_\delta=\prod_iQ_i/(Q_i+\delta)$. As in part (a) of the proof of 8.1, $f=B_Pf_1$ and $P=B_PO_P$, so $fA/P=f_1A/O_P\in N^+$. Since $|fA/P|\le C|f|$ on the line, $fA/P\in H^2$, and therefore $g_\delta:=fR_\delta/P\in H^2$. Multiplication by $Q_i$ and division by $Q_i+\delta$ both preserve $S$, so $Pg_\delta=fR_\delta\in S$, and $g_\delta\in S$ by part (b), i.e. $fR_\delta\in PS$. Finally, $|R_\delta|\le1$ because $\operatorname{Re}Q_i\ge0$, and $R_\delta\to1$ off the null set where some $Q_i$ vanishes on the line. By dominated convergence $fR_\delta\to f$ in $H^2$, so $J_P\subset\overline{PS}$. The reverse inclusion is clear.
   For the second claim, let $Z\subset\mathbb T^k$ be the finite zero set of $\tilde P$. For $\chi\in Z$ put $Q_\chi=\sum_p\big(1-\bar\chi_pp^{1/2}p^{-s}\big)$, summed over the same $k$ primes. Each term has nonnegative real part on the line, and $|\tilde Q_\chi(e^{i\theta})|\le|\theta-\theta_\chi|_1$. By the Łojasiewicz inequality for the real-analytic function $\tilde P$ on the compact manifold $\mathbb T^k$ [20], there are $c>0$ and $m_0>0$ with $|\tilde P|\ge c\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,Z)^{m_0}$. Since $\operatorname{dist}\le\operatorname{diam}\mathbb T^k$, the same holds with $m_0$ replaced by any integer $m\ge m_0$ (adjusting $c$). So $A=\prod_{\chi\in Z}Q_\chi^m$ satisfies $|\tilde A|\le C|\tilde P|$ on $\mathbb T^k$, hence $|A|\le C|P|$ on the line. $\square$

   Proposition 8.4 contains Proposition 8.3. For $P=p(m^{-s})$ with any integer $m\ge2$, the factors $1-\bar z_j\sqrt m\,m^{-s}$ over the zeros $z_j$ of $p(m^{-1/2}z)$ on $\mathbb T$, repeated with multiplicity, dominate $P$ when $p(0)\ne0$. This covers $1-\sqrt6\,6^{-s}$, whose symbol vanishes on the curve $z_2z_3=1$. It also covers $1-2\cdot2^{-s}-3^{-s}$, whose symbol $1-\sqrt2z_2-z_3/\sqrt3$ vanishes transversally at two conjugate points of $\mathbb T^2$. There the observed maximum of $|\tilde Q_+\tilde Q_-/\tilde P|$ on a $1200\times1200$ grid of $\mathbb T^2$ is $\approx9.52$ (`code/torus_domination.py`). Finiteness of the supremum follows from the argument above; the grid value does not certify it. It covers the zero-free example $1+\tfrac12 2^{1/2-s}+\tfrac12 3^{1/2-s}$ of u/bramble_trace, which has $\inf_{\text{line}}|P|=0$, $J_P=S$ and $Q=P$. The argument does not reach $\zeta$, whose Bohr symbol involves all primes and is not a polynomial. For the analytic-function-space framework of [3, 4] and the zero-free half-plane criteria derived from it, see Ghosh–Kremnizer–Noor–Santos [15] and Manzur–Noor–Quintero [16].

   *Domination by a zero-free polynomial.* The positive-real-part factors of Proposition 8.4 can be replaced by any dominating polynomial without zeros in $\Omega$. Then the torus zero set of $P$ may have any dimension. Write $\mathbb T_\sigma=\{z\in\mathbb C^k:|z_p|=p^{1/2-\sigma}\text{ for all }p\}$, so that $P(\sigma+it)=\tilde P\big((p^{1/2-\sigma-it})_p\big)$ with $(p^{1/2-\sigma-it})_p\in\mathbb T_\sigma$, and $P_\varepsilon(s)=P(s+\varepsilon)$.

   **Lemma 8.5.** Let $P$ be a Dirichlet polynomial with $c_1\ne0$ and no zeros in $\Omega$. Then:
   (a) $\tilde P\ne0$ on $\mathbb T_\sigma$ for every $\sigma>\tfrac12$, and $\inf_{\operatorname{Re}s\ge1/2+\varepsilon}|P|>0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$;
   (b) $K(P):=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R,\,0<\varepsilon\le1}|P(\tfrac12+it)|/|P(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it)|<\infty$.

   *Proof.* (a) Suppose $\tilde P(w)=0$ with $w_p=e^{i\theta_p}p^{-\varepsilon}$, $\varepsilon>0$. Put $F(s)=\sum_nc_n\big(\prod_pe^{i\theta_pv_p(n)}\big)n^{-s}$. Then $F(\tfrac12+\varepsilon)=\tilde P(w)=0$, and $F\not\equiv0$ because its first coefficient is $c_1$. The $\log p$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$, so Kronecker's theorem gives $t_j$ with $p^{-it_j}\to e^{i\theta_p}$ for all $p$. Then $P(\cdot+it_j)\to F$ uniformly on compact sets, and Hurwitz's theorem gives zeros of $P$ near $\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it_j$, a contradiction. For the second claim choose $\sigma_1$ with $\sum_{n\ge2}|c_n|n^{-\sigma_1}\le|c_1|/2$. Then $|P|\ge|c_1|/2$ on $\sigma\ge\sigma_1$, and on $\tfrac12+\varepsilon\le\sigma\le\sigma_1$ the value $|P(\sigma+it)|$ is at least the minimum of $|\tilde P|$ on the compact set $\bigcup_{\sigma\in[1/2+\varepsilon,\sigma_1]}\mathbb T_\sigma$, which is positive.
   (b) Let $G(\zeta)=\tilde P(e^{i\zeta_1},\dots,e^{i\zeta_k})$ for $\zeta\in\mathbb C^k$ and $v=(\log p)_p$. Then $P(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it)=G(-tv+i\varepsilon v)$, and by (a) $G\ne0$ on $\mathbb R^k+i(0,\infty)v$. Let $Z=\{\theta\in\mathbb R^k:G(\theta)=0\}$ and fix $\theta_0\in Z$. The entire function $\lambda\mapsto G(\theta_0+\lambda v)$ does not vanish at $\lambda=i\varepsilon$, so it has a zero of some finite order $m$ at $0$. Write $\zeta=\theta_0+x'+\lambda v$, with $x'$ in the complexification of a real hyperplane $H$ transverse to $v$. By the Weierstrass preparation theorem, $G=u\,W$ on a polydisc $U'\times\{|\lambda|<r\}$, where $u$ has no zeros and $W=\prod_{j\le m}(\lambda-\lambda_j(x'))$ with all $|\lambda_j(x')|<r$. For real $x'$, a root with $\operatorname{Im}\lambda_j(x')>0$ would be a zero of $G$ at a point with imaginary part $(\operatorname{Im}\lambda_j)v$, which (a) excludes. So $\operatorname{Im}\lambda_j(x')\le0$. For real $\mu$ and $\varepsilon>0$ this gives $|\mu-\lambda_j|^2=(\mu-\operatorname{Re}\lambda_j)^2+(\operatorname{Im}\lambda_j)^2\le|\mu+i\varepsilon-\lambda_j|^2$. Hence $|G(\theta)|/|G(\theta+i\varepsilon v)|\le\sup|u|/\inf|u|$ for $\theta=\theta_0+x'+\mu v$ with real $x'\in U'$, $|\mu|<r/2$ and $0<\varepsilon<r/2$. $Z$ is $2\pi\mathbb Z^k$-periodic and closed, so finitely many such neighbourhoods $N_i$, with radii $r_i$, cover it modulo $2\pi\mathbb Z^k$. Let $N=\bigcup N_i$ and $\varepsilon_0=\min r_i/2$. On $(\mathbb T^k\setminus N)\times[0,1]$ and on $\overline N\times[\varepsilon_0,1]$ the denominator $|G(\theta+i\varepsilon v)|$ is continuous and nonzero on a compact set, so the ratio is bounded there too. $\square$

   **Proposition 8.6.** Let $P$ be a Dirichlet polynomial with $c_1\ne0$. Suppose there is a Dirichlet polynomial $A$ with $c_1(A)\ne0$, no zeros in $\Omega$, and $|A|\le C|P|$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12$. Then $\overline{PS}=J_P$.

   *Proof.* Let $f\in J_P$. As in the proof of Proposition 8.4, $fA/P=f_1A/O_P\in N^+$ and $|fA/P|\le C|f|$ on the line, so $fA/P\in H^2$. Since $P\cdot fA/P=fA\in S$, part (b) of the proof of Proposition 8.1 gives $fA/P\in S$. So $A\,J_P\subset PS$. By Lemma 8.5(a), $1/A_\varepsilon$ is bounded and holomorphic on $\Omega$, so $f/A_\varepsilon\in H^2$. It lies in $S$ by part (b) again, applied to $A_\varepsilon$ (whose first coefficient is $c_1(A)$), and in $J_P$ because $1/A_\varepsilon$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. Hence $f\,A/A_\varepsilon=A\cdot f/A_\varepsilon\in PS$. On the line, $|A/A_\varepsilon|\le K(A)$ by Lemma 8.5(b). Also $A/A_\varepsilon\to1$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ off the zeros of $A$ on the line, which are discrete. Dominated convergence gives $fA/A_\varepsilon\to f$ in $H^2$. $\square$

   Proposition 8.6 contains Proposition 8.4. With $\operatorname{Re}Q_i\ge0$ on the line, $\operatorname{Re}Q_i\ge0$ on $\Omega$ (proof of 8.4). By the minimum principle, $\operatorname{Re}Q_i>0$ on $\Omega$ unless $Q_i$ is a nonzero imaginary constant, so $Q_i$ has no zeros in $\Omega$. Moreover $\operatorname{Re}c_1(Q_i)$ is the Bohr mean of $\operatorname{Re}Q_i$ on the line, so $c_1(Q_i)=0$ would force $\operatorname{Re}Q_i\equiv0$ there and hence $Q_i\equiv0$. So $A=\prod Q_i$ satisfies the hypothesis of 8.6.

   **Corollary 8.7** (cyclic Dirichlet polynomials). For a Dirichlet polynomial $P\not\equiv0$ the following are equivalent:
   (i) $P$ is cyclic in $S$, i.e. $\overline{PS}=S$;
   (ii) $c_1\ne0$ and $P$ has no zeros in $\Omega$;
   (iii) $c_1\ne0$ and $\tilde P$ has no zeros in the open polydisc $\mathbb D^k$.
   Zeros of $P$ on the line, and torus zero sets of $\tilde P$ of any dimension, are allowed.

   *Proof.* (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i) is Proposition 8.6 with $A=P$, since $J_P=S$. (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): a zero $\rho\in\Omega$ of $P$ gives a bounded evaluation that kills $PS$ but not $E$, since $E(\rho)=1/\rho\ne0$. If $c_1=0$ and $n_0\ge2$ is the least $n$ with $c_n\ne0$, then by the proof of Proposition 8.1(b) every element of $PS$ is the transform of a function supported in $(0,1/n_0]$, and so is every element of $\overline{PS}$; $E$ is not. (iii)$\Rightarrow$(ii): $s\mapsto(p^{1/2-s})_p$ maps $\Omega$ into $\mathbb D^k$.
   (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii). Let $\mathcal A=\{(\log|z_p|)_p:\tilde P(z)=0,\ z\in(\mathbb C^*)^k\}$ be the amoeba of $\tilde P$. By Lemma 8.5(a) it misses the open ray $R=\{-xv:x>0\}$. Write $\tilde P=\sum_\alpha a_\alpha z^\alpha$, so $a_0=c_1$. The set $L=\{y:\sum_{\alpha\ne0}|a_\alpha|e^{\langle y,\alpha\rangle}<|c_1|\}$ misses $\mathcal A$, is convex, and contains $y+C$ for each $y\in L$, where $C$ is the closed negative orthant (all $\alpha\in\mathbb N^k$). $L$ contains $-xv$ for large $x$, so $R\cup L$ lies in one component $E_0$ of $\mathbb R^k\setminus\mathcal A$. Complement components of amoebas are open and convex [22, Ch. 6, §1], [23]. For $y\in E_0$, $c\in C$, $y_0\in L$ and $t\ge1$, convexity gives $(1-\tfrac1t)y+\tfrac1t(y_0+tc)\in E_0$. Letting $t\to\infty$ shows $y+c\in\overline{E_0}$. If $c\in\operatorname{int}C$, then $c+xv\in C$ for small $x>0$, so $c=-xv+(c+xv)\in\overline{E_0}$. Thus $\operatorname{int}C\subset\operatorname{int}\overline{E_0}=E_0$, since $E_0$ is open and convex. So $\tilde P$ has no zeros in $\mathbb D^k\cap(\mathbb C^*)^k$.
   Now suppose $\tilde P(z^*)=0$ with $z^*\in\mathbb D^k$, and let $I=\{p:z^*_p=0\}\ne\emptyset$. If $I$ contains every $p$, then $c_1=\tilde P(0)=0$. Otherwise $R_0(y)=\tilde P(0_I,y)$ is a polynomial in $y\in\mathbb C^{I^c}$ with $R_0(0)=c_1\ne0$ and $R_0(z^*_{I^c})=0$. Choose $b$ with $\lambda\mapsto R_0(z^*_{I^c}+\lambda b)\not\equiv0$. By Hurwitz, for small $\tau\ne0$ the function $\lambda\mapsto\tilde P(\tau\mathbf 1_I,z^*_{I^c}+\lambda b)$ has a zero $\lambda(\tau)\to0$. This gives a zero of $\tilde P$ in $\mathbb D^k\cap(\mathbb C^*)^k$, a contradiction. $\square$

   Corollary 8.7 is the analogue in $S$ of the Neuwirth–Ginsberg–Newman criterion [17] (see also [18, 19]). In the Hardy space of Dirichlet series with square-summable coefficients, a Dirichlet polynomial is cyclic iff its Bohr lift has no zeros in $\mathbb D^k$. The Bohr lift of $P(s+\tfrac12)$ there is $\tilde P$ here, so by (iii) $P$ is cyclic in $S$ iff $P(s+\tfrac12)$ is cyclic in that space. We did not find the equivalence (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii) stated in this form, so we include its proof. The proof of (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i) has the shape of Nikolski's proof of [19, Thm 3.4(1)] (see the remark after it). There, too, cyclicity follows from a radial regularization and a uniformly bounded polynomial quotient, with $P_\varepsilon$ and Lemma 8.5(b) as the counterparts here. What is new is the transfer to $S$, which is not the Hardy space of Dirichlet series and on which $\sqrt nM_n$ are not doubly commuting (`code/double_commute.py`). The transfer goes through part (b) of the proof of Proposition 8.1. Also new are the half-plane formulation (ii) and the identification of $\overline{PS}$ as a jet kernel under domination (Proposition 8.6). The classical criterion does not by itself identify these ranges in $S$. An example outside the finite-zero criterion of Proposition 8.4 is $P=2-\sqrt2\,2^{-s}+\sqrt3\,3^{-s}-2\sqrt6\,6^{-s}$, with $\tilde P=(2-z_2)-z_3(2z_2-1)$. Since $|2-z|^2-|2z-1|^2=3(1-|z|^2)$, $\tilde P$ has no zeros in $\mathbb D^2$, and its torus zero set is the curve $z_3=(2-z_2)/(2z_2-1)$, $|z_2|=1$. So $P$ is cyclic. Numerically $\operatorname{dist}(E,P\operatorname{span}\{e_m\}_{m\le M})^2=0.462,0.374,0.316,0.273$ at $M=10^3,\dots,10^6$, a slow decrease like that of the line-touching examples, and the grid value of $K(P)$ is $\approx1.89$ (`code/curve_cyc.py`).

   *An undominated polynomial.* Propositions 8.4 and 8.6 need a dominator without zeros in $\Omega$. This fails for
   $$P_\times=1+2\sqrt2\,2^{-s}+2\sqrt3\,3^{-s}+\sqrt6\,6^{-s},\qquad\tilde P_\times=1+2z_2+2z_3+z_2z_3=(2+z_2)\big(z_3-\varphi(z_2)\big),\quad\varphi(z)=-\frac{1+2z}{2+z}.$$
   - $\varphi$ is a disc automorphism. So the torus zero set is the curve $\{(z,\varphi(z)):|z|=1\}$, and the zeros in $\mathbb D^2$ form the analytic disc $\{(z,\varphi(z)):|z|<1\}$.
   - By Corollary 8.7, $P_\times$ has zeros in $\Omega$. They accumulate at the line. Near $|z|=1$ the ratio $\log|\varphi(z)|/\log|z|$ takes every value in $(\tfrac13,3)\ni\log3/\log2$, so the analytic disc meets $\bigcup_{\sigma>1/2}\mathbb T_\sigma$ arbitrarily close to $\sigma=\tfrac12$, and the argument of Lemma 8.5(a) applies.
   - Suppose $A$ is holomorphic near $\overline{\mathbb D}^2$ and $|A|\le C|\tilde P_\times|$ on $\mathbb T^2$. Then $z\mapsto A(z,\varphi(z))$ vanishes on $|z|=1$, hence on $\mathbb D$, so $A$ has zeros in $\mathbb D^2$. If a Dirichlet polynomial $A$ also involves primes $p\ge5$, with variables $z'$, the same argument applies for each $z'\in\mathbb T^{k'}$. Then for fixed $z\in\mathbb D$ the polynomial $z'\mapsto\tilde A(z,\varphi(z),z')$ vanishes on $\mathbb T^{k'}$, hence identically, so $\tilde A$ has zeros in $\mathbb D^{2+k'}$. By Corollary 8.7, no admissible dominator exists. Bohr twists fail for the same reason (the amoeba of $\tilde P_\times$ meets every neighbourhood of $0$), and $P_\times$ is not a polynomial in one $m^{-s}$.
   Synthesis nevertheless holds for $P_\times$, and for every two-prime polynomial with $c_1\ne0$ and squarefree Bohr symbol (Theorem 8.9). The proof divides formally and cuts the quotient off on a logarithmic scale. This is the device of Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [6], whose weights $\mu(n)(1-\log n/\log N)$ cut off the formal quotient $1/\zeta$. Here it is applied to $f/P$ inside $S$. Identify $S$ with the step space through the Mellin transform, so that $f=\sum_mf_me_m$ and $\|f\|^2=\sum_m|f_m|^2w_m$ with $w_m=\|e_m\|^2$. As in the proof of Proposition 8.1(b), $(n^{-s}g)_m=g_{\lfloor m/n\rfloor}$ for $m\ge n$ and $=0$ for $m<n$.

   **Lemma 8.8** (logarithmic cutoff of the quotient). Let $P=\sum_{n\le N}c_nn^{-s}$ with $c_1\ne0$, and let $f\in S$ be such that $h=f/P$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. Suppose that for each small $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\mathcal M(\varepsilon)$ with $\int|h(\sigma+it)|^2\,dt\le\mathcal M(\varepsilon)$ for all $\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon$, and that $\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\varepsilon^2\mathcal M(\varepsilon)=0$. Then $f\in\overline{PS}$.

   *Proof.* Define $g_m$ recursively by $c_1g_m=f_m-\sum_{2\le n\le\min(m,N)}c_ng_{\lfloor m/n\rfloor}$. Since $|f_m|\le\|f\|w_m^{-1/2}$, induction gives $|g_m|\ll m^{A}$ for some $A$. So $\hat g(s)=\sum_mg_me_m(s)$ converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}s\ge\sigma_1$, and $P\hat g=f$ there, i.e. $\hat g=h$. Let $h_\varepsilon=h(\cdot+\varepsilon)$. By hypothesis $h_\varepsilon\in H^2$ with $\|h_\varepsilon\|^2\le\mathcal M(\varepsilon)/2\pi$, so $h_\varepsilon=\hat k$ with $k\in L^2(0,1)$. The transforms of $k$ and of $u^\varepsilon g(u)$ agree on $\operatorname{Re}s\ge\sigma_1$, so $k=u^\varepsilon g$. Hence, for $X\ge1$,
   $$\textstyle\sum_{j\le X}|g_j|^2w_j\le\int_{1/(X+1)}^1|g|^2\,du\le(X+1)^{2\varepsilon}\|h_\varepsilon\|^2 .$$
   Fix $\chi\in C^1(\mathbb R)$ with $0\le\chi\le1$, $\chi=1$ on $(-\infty,1]$ and $\chi=0$ on $[2,\infty)$. Put $\chi_L(m)=\chi(\log m/L)$ and $g^{(L)}=\sum_m\chi_L(m)g_me_m$, a finite sum, so $g^{(L)}\in S$. Then $Pg^{(L)}-f=r_L-(1-\chi_L)f$, with
   $$\textstyle(r_L)_m=\sum_{2\le n\le\min(m,N)}c_n\,g_{\lfloor m/n\rfloor}\big(\chi_L(\lfloor m/n\rfloor)-\chi_L(m)\big).$$
   Here $|\chi_L(\lfloor m/n\rfloor)-\chi_L(m)|\le\|\chi'\|_\infty\log(2n)/L$, and the difference vanishes unless $\lfloor m/n\rfloor\le e^{2L}$. Since $\sum_{0\le r<n}w_{nj+r}=w_j/n$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
   $$\textstyle\|r_L\|^2\le C_PL^{-2}\sum_{j\le e^{2L}}|g_j|^2w_j\le C_PL^{-2}(e^{2L}+1)^{2\varepsilon}\|h_\varepsilon\|^2,$$
   with $C_P=(N-1)\|\chi'\|_\infty^2\sum_{n\ge2}|c_n|^2\log^2(2n)/n$. Choose $\varepsilon_k\to0$ with $\varepsilon_k^2\mathcal M(\varepsilon_k)\to0$ and put $L_k=1/\varepsilon_k$. Then $(e^{2L_k}+1)^{2\varepsilon_k}\le4e^4$, so $\|r_{L_k}\|\to0$. Also $\|(1-\chi_L)f\|\to0$. Hence $Pg^{(L_k)}\to f$. $\square$

   **Theorem 8.9.** Let $P$ be a Dirichlet polynomial in $2^{-s}$ and $3^{-s}$ with $c_1\ne0$ whose Bohr symbol $\tilde P\in\mathbb C[z_2,z_3]$ is squarefree. Then $\overline{PS}=J_P$. In particular $\overline{P_\times S}=J_{P_\times}$.

   *Proof.* Write $G(\zeta)=\tilde P(e^{i\zeta_1},e^{i\zeta_2})$ for $\zeta\in\mathbb C^2$ (coordinates for the primes $2,3$) and $v=(\log2,\log3)$, so $P(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it)=G((-t+i\varepsilon)v)$. Let $Z_{\mathbb R}=\{\theta\in\mathbb R^2:G(\theta)=0\}$. For $\theta\in Z_{\mathbb R}$ let $m(\theta)$ be the order at $0$ of $\lambda\mapsto G(\theta+\lambda v)$. It is finite: otherwise $G$ vanishes on $\theta+\mathbb Rv$, which is dense modulo $2\pi\mathbb Z^2$, and then $\tilde P\equiv0$.

   *Step 0 (special points).* Let $g$ be an irreducible factor of $\tilde P$ and write $\tilde P=gh$, so $g\nmid h$. If $g$ divided both partial derivatives of $\tilde P$, then $g\mid(\partial_ig)h$, so $g\mid\partial_ig$ and $\partial_ig=0$ for both $i$, i.e. $g$ would be constant. So $g$ does not divide some $\partial_i\tilde P$, and $\{g=\partial_i\tilde P=0\}$ is finite by Bézout. Hence $\{\tilde P=\nabla\tilde P=0\}$ is finite. Since $\nabla G=(iz_2\partial_{z_2}\tilde P,iz_3\partial_{z_3}\tilde P)$ at $z=e^{i\zeta}$, the zeros of $G$ with $\nabla G=0$ form a discrete set in $\mathbb C^2$. In particular the germ of $G$ at each of its zeros is reduced: a factor $g_0^2$ of the germ would make $\nabla G$ vanish on the curve $\{g_0=0\}$. Let $\Sigma=\{\theta\in Z_{\mathbb R}:\partial_vG(\theta)=0\}=\{\theta:m(\theta)\ge2\}$. We claim that $\Sigma$ is finite modulo $2\pi\mathbb Z^2$. Otherwise the compact real-analytic set $\Sigma/2\pi\mathbb Z^2$ is infinite, so it contains a real-analytic arc $\gamma$ [20]. Fix $p\in\gamma$. Infinitely many points of $\gamma$, accumulating at $p$, lie in one irreducible component $C_1$ of the germ of $\{G=0\}$ at $p$. $\partial_vG$ vanishes at all of them, so $\partial_vG\equiv0$ on $C_1$. At the points of $C_1$ where $\nabla G\ne0$, which are all but a discrete set, this says that $v$ is tangent to $C_1$. So $C_1$ is an open piece of a line $\theta_0+\mathbb Cv$, where $\theta_0$ can be taken to be one of the real points of $\gamma$ in $C_1$. Then $m(\theta_0)=\infty$, a contradiction.

   *Step 1 (reduction).* For $\chi=e^{i\theta}$ with $\theta\in\Sigma$, let $Q_\chi$ be as in the proof of Proposition 8.4. Put $A=\prod_\chi Q_\chi^M$ over these finitely many $\chi$, with the integer $M$ fixed in Step 3 ($A=1$ if $\Sigma=\emptyset$). Let $f\in J_P$ and $\delta>0$. By Proposition 8.1, $(Q_\chi+\delta)S=S$, so $f'=f/\prod_\chi(Q_\chi+\delta)^M\in S$. Also $f'\in J_P$, because $1/(Q_\chi+\delta)$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. As in the proof of Proposition 8.4, $f'A=f\prod_\chi(Q_\chi/(Q_\chi+\delta))^M\to f$ as $\delta\to0$. So it suffices to show $F:=f'A\in\overline{PS}$ for every $f'\in J_P$.

   *Step 2 (a weight).* By the proof of Proposition 8.1(a), $P=BO$ with $B$ the Blaschke product over $Z(P)\cap\Omega$ and $O$ outer, and $f'=Bf_1'$ with $f_1'\in H^2$. So $h:=F/P=f_1'A/O$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. Let $\Pi_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon/(\pi(x^2+\varepsilon^2))$. Subharmonicity gives $|f_1'(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it)|^2\le(\Pi_\varepsilon*|f'|^2)(t)$, with $|f'|$ taken on the line. Hence
   $$\textstyle\int|h(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it)|^2dt\le\int|f'(\tfrac12+i\tau)|^2K_\varepsilon(\tau)\,d\tau,\qquad K_\varepsilon=\Pi_\varepsilon*\Psi_\varepsilon,\quad \Psi_\varepsilon(t)=|A/O|^2(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it).$$
   We show in Step 3 that there are $C,\varepsilon_0>0$ and a set $\Lambda\subset\mathbb R$, with multiplicities and with at most $C$ points in every unit interval, such that for $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$
   $$\textstyle \Psi_\varepsilon(t)\le C\Big(1+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\,|\lambda-t|\le1}\frac1{(t-\lambda)^2+\varepsilon^2}\Big).\tag{$\ast$}$$
   Since $\Pi_\varepsilon*((\cdot-\lambda)^2+\varepsilon^2)^{-1}=\frac\pi\varepsilon\Pi_{2\varepsilon}(\cdot-\lambda)$, ($\ast$) gives
   $$\textstyle\varepsilon^2K_\varepsilon(\tau)\le C\varepsilon^2+2C\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{\varepsilon^2}{(\tau-\lambda)^2+4\varepsilon^2}.$$
   The right side is bounded uniformly in $\tau$ and $\varepsilon$, and it tends to $0$ for $\tau\notin\Lambda$. For $\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon_0$, $|O|$ is bounded below. Indeed, $\log|O|$ is the Poisson integral of $\log|P(\tfrac12+i\cdot)|$, which is bounded above and has uniformly bounded integrals of its negative part over unit intervals (by the local form $G=uW$ of Step 3, each passage of $t\mapsto-tv$ through one of the finitely many neighbourhoods contributes at most $\int_{-1}^1\log^-\prod_k|\mu-\lambda_k|\,d\mu\le C$). Also $A$ is bounded, so $K_{\varepsilon'}\le C$ for $\varepsilon'\ge\varepsilon_0$. Put $\Phi(\varepsilon')=\varepsilon'^2\int|f'|^2K_{\varepsilon'}$. Then $\Phi\le C\|f'\|^2$, and $\Phi(\varepsilon')\to0$ as $\varepsilon'\to0$ by dominated convergence. Applying the bound above on every line $\sigma=\tfrac12+\varepsilon'$, the hypothesis of Lemma 8.8 holds with $\mathcal M(\varepsilon)=\sup_{\varepsilon\le\varepsilon'\le\varepsilon_0}\varepsilon'^{-2}\Phi(\varepsilon')+C\|f'\|^2$. Splitting at $\varepsilon'=\sqrt\varepsilon$ gives $\varepsilon^2\mathcal M(\varepsilon)\le\sup_{\varepsilon'\le\sqrt\varepsilon}\Phi(\varepsilon')+C\varepsilon\|f'\|^2\to0$. So $F\in\overline{PS}$.

   *Step 3 (proof of ($\ast$)).* Fix $\theta^0\in Z_{\mathbb R}$ and let $m=m(\theta^0)$. As in the proof of Lemma 8.5(b), $G=uW$ on $U'\times\{|\lambda|<r\}$, where $\zeta=\theta^0+x'+\lambda v$, $u$ has no zeros, and $W=\lambda^m+\sum_{j<m}a_j(x')\lambda^j=\prod_{k\le m}(\lambda-\lambda_k(x'))$ with $a_j(0)=0$. We take $r\le\tfrac12$. Finitely many of the sets $\{\theta^0+x'+\mu v:x'\in U'\text{ real},|\mu|<r/2\}$ cover $Z_{\mathbb R}$ mod $2\pi$, and $|u|\ge c$ on all of them. Off their union, $|P(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it)|\ge c$ for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ by compactness, and $|O|\ge|P|$. Now let $s=\tfrac12+\varepsilon+it$ with $-tv\equiv\theta^0+x'+\mu v$, where $x'\in U'$ is real, $|\mu|<r/2$ and $\varepsilon<r/2$, and put $\lambda=\mu+i\varepsilon$. The root $\lambda_k$ belongs to the zero $\rho_k=\tfrac12+\operatorname{Im}\lambda_k+it_k$ of $P$, where $t_k=t+\mu-\operatorname{Re}\lambda_k$. It lies in $\Omega$ iff $\operatorname{Im}\lambda_k>0$, and then $|s-\rho_k|=|\lambda-\lambda_k|$ and $|b_{\rho_k}(s)|=|\lambda-\lambda_k|/|\lambda-\bar\lambda_k|$. Since $|B|\le\prod|b_{\rho_k}|$,
   $$\textstyle|O(s)|=|P(s)|/|B(s)|\ge|u|\prod_k|\lambda-\lambda_k^*|,\qquad\lambda_k^*=\bar\lambda_k\text{ if }\operatorname{Im}\lambda_k>0,\ \lambda_k^*=\lambda_k\text{ otherwise}.$$
   As $\operatorname{Im}\lambda_k^*\le0<\varepsilon$, we have $|\lambda-\lambda_k^*|\ge|\varepsilon+i(t-t_k)|$. So it suffices to show $|A/O|(s)\le C\max_k|\lambda-\lambda_k^*|^{-1}$. If $m=1$, this holds because $A$ is bounded.
   Let $m\ge2$, so $\theta^0\in\Sigma$ and $|A(s)|\le C(|x'|+|\lambda|)^M$, since $\tilde Q_\chi$ vanishes at $\chi=e^{i\theta^0}$.
   - By Step 0 the germ of $W$ is reduced, so its discriminant $\Delta(x')=\prod_{k<l}(\lambda_k-\lambda_l)^2$ is a holomorphic function of one variable with $\Delta\not\equiv0$. Hence $|\Delta(x')|\ge c|x'|^{\delta}$ with $\delta=\operatorname{ord}_0\Delta$.
   - Also $|a_j(x')|\le C|x'|$, so $|\lambda_k(x')|\le2\max_j|a_j(x')|^{1/(m-j)}\le C_0|x'|^{1/m}$.
   - Since $\operatorname{Im}\lambda>0$, $|\lambda-\lambda_k^*|\ge|\lambda-\lambda_k|$ for every $k$. Let $k_0$ minimise $|\lambda-\lambda_k|$. Then $|\lambda-\lambda_k|\ge\frac12|\lambda_{k_0}-\lambda_k|$ for $k\ne k_0$. The factors of $|\Delta|^{1/2}$ not involving $k_0$ are at most $2r\le1$, so
   $$\textstyle|O(s)|\ge c|\lambda-\lambda_{k_0}^*|\prod_{k\ne k_0}|\lambda_{k_0}-\lambda_k|\ge c|\lambda-\lambda_{k_0}^*|\,|\Delta(x')|^{1/2}\ge c|\lambda-\lambda_{k_0}^*|\,|x'|^{\delta/2}.$$
   - If $|\lambda|\le2C_0|x'|^{1/m}$, then $x'\ne0$ and $|A|\le C|x'|^{M/m}$, so $|A/O|\le C/|\lambda-\lambda_{k_0}^*|$ once $M\ge m\delta/2$.
   - If $|\lambda|>2C_0|x'|^{1/m}$, then $|\lambda-\lambda_k^*|\ge|\lambda-\lambda_k|\ge|\lambda|/2$ for all $k$, so $|O|\ge c|\lambda|^m$. Also $|x'|\le C|\lambda|^m\le C|\lambda|$, so $|A|\le C|\lambda|^M$. Since $|\lambda-\lambda_1^*|\le|\lambda|+|\lambda_1|\le2|\lambda|$, this gives $|A/O|\le C|\lambda|^{M-m}\le C/|\lambda-\lambda_1^*|$ once $M\ge m-1$.
   Let $M$ be the largest of these requirements over the finitely many points of $\Sigma$ mod $2\pi$. Squaring gives ($\ast$), with $\Lambda$ the ordinates of the zeros of $P$ in $|\operatorname{Re}s-\tfrac12|<\max r$, counted with multiplicity (each $|t-t_k|\le|\mu|+|\lambda_k|\le1$). Choose $\sigma_1$ with $|P|\ge|c_1|/2$ on $\operatorname{Re}s\ge\sigma_1$, as in Lemma 8.5(a). Jensen's formula on discs of fixed radius centred at $\sigma_1+iT$ then shows that $\Lambda$ has at most $C$ points in every unit interval. $\square$

   - For $P_\times$, $\tilde P_\times=(2+z_2)(z_3-\varphi(z_2))$ is irreducible. Since $2+z\ne0$ on $\mathbb T$, $\partial_{\theta_2}G\ne0$ on $Z_{\mathbb R}$, which is the smooth curve $e^{i\theta_2}=\varphi(e^{i\theta_1})$. As $\varphi$ is an automorphism, its slope is $d\theta_2/d\theta_1=|\varphi'(e^{i\theta_1})|=3/(5+4\cos\theta_1)$. So $\Sigma$ consists of the two points $\theta^\pm$ where this equals $\log3/\log2$, with $\cos\theta_1=-0.7768\ldots$. Contact there has order exactly $m=2$, because $\frac d{d\theta}\frac3{5+4\cos\theta}=\frac{12\sin\theta}{(5+4\cos\theta)^2}\ne0$. Since $\nabla G\ne0$ and $\partial_vG=0$ at $\theta^\pm$, $a_0'(0)\ne0$, so $\Delta=a_1^2-4a_0$ has a simple zero: $\delta=1$ and $M=1$, so $A=Q_+Q_-$ with $Q_\pm=Q_{\chi^\pm}$, $\chi^\pm=e^{i\theta^\pm}$. The bound $M\ge\max(m\delta/2,m-1)$ is not sharp in general: at a transversal node, $M=1$ suffices because the roots are $O(|x'|)$.
   - Without the factor $A$, the weight behaves like a pole of order $\ge2$ near the points of $\Sigma$. There the line zeros of $P$ come in close clusters, or a zero in $\Omega$ lies close to the line. Then $\varepsilon^2K_\varepsilon$ need not be uniformly bounded, and the argument would need Diophantine information on how closely $t\mapsto(2^{-it},3^{-it})$ approaches $\Sigma$. The factor $A$ removes this, so no property of $\log3/\log2$ beyond irrationality is used.
   - Squarefreeness enters only through the germs of $G$ at real points. So it suffices that the irreducible factors of $\tilde P$ vanishing somewhere on $\mathbb T^2$ are simple. If such a factor is repeated and vanishes on a curve in $\mathbb T^2$, then $P$ has infinitely many multiple zeros on the line, $\Psi_\varepsilon$ has poles of order $\ge4$ there, and ($\ast$) fails. Our method does not decide whether $\overline{PS}=J_P$ then; [26] does (see below). Proposition 8.3 covers repeated factors in one prime. Two primes enter through the finiteness of $\Sigma$ and the one-variable discriminant. For $k\ge3$ primes the corresponding sets can have dimension $k-2$, and $A$ would have to vanish on them without vanishing in $\Omega$. *Added in v7:* u/bramble_trace [26, Theorem 1.1] has since answered these questions. He proves $\overline{QS}=J_Q$ for every Dirichlet series $Q=\sum c_nn^{-s}$ with $c_1\ne0$ and $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2+\eta}<\infty$ for some $\eta>0$, so in particular for repeated factors and any number of primes. His proof starts from Lemma 8.8 but keeps the derivatives $Q^{(k)}$ in the cutoff expansion, to an order fixed by a uniform local root count. After multiplication by the Blaschke product (write $Q=BO$ as in Proposition 8.1(a)), the product rule bounds $Q^{(k)}/O$ by reflected root distances, with pole order at most $k$. So no dominator $A$ and no discriminant bound are needed.

   For an infinite set $Z=\{\rho_k=\beta_k+i\gamma_k\}$ in the strip $\tfrac12<\operatorname{Re}s<1$ with $|\gamma_k|\to\infty$, let $\Lambda(Z)\subset[\tfrac12,1]\times\mathbb T^\infty$ be the set of limits of $(\beta_k,(p^{-i\gamma_k})_p)$ along subsequences, in the product topology, and let $\Lambda_\beta(Z)=\{\chi:(\beta,\chi)\in\Lambda(Z)\}$. Each $\chi\in\mathbb T^\infty$ is extended completely multiplicatively, $\chi(n)=\prod_p\chi_p^{v_p(n)}$.

   **Proposition 8.10** (Bohr limits of zero sets). Let $Q=\sum_nc_nn^{-s}$ with $\sum_n|c_n|n^{-1/2}<\infty$ vanish on such a set $Z$, and put $Q_\chi(\beta)=\sum_nc_n\chi(n)n^{-\beta}$. Then:
   (i) $Q_\chi(\beta)=0$ for every $(\beta,\chi)\in\Lambda(Z)$;
   (ii) if $\Lambda_\beta(Z)$ has nonempty interior in $\mathbb T^\infty$ for some $\beta$, then $Q\equiv0$.
   Both statements also hold when $\sum_n|c_n|^2<\infty$ and $\inf_k\beta_k>\tfrac12$, by Cauchy–Schwarz.

   *Proof.* (i) $Q(\rho_k)=\sum_nc_n\chi_k(n)n^{-\beta_k}$ with $\chi_k=(p^{-i\gamma_k})_p$. Along a subsequence with $(\beta_k,\chi_k)\to(\beta,\chi)$, each term converges and is bounded by $|c_n|n^{-1/2}$, so dominated convergence gives $Q_\chi(\beta)=0$. Here $Q_\chi$ is a vertical limit function of $Q$ [18, §2.3], as in the proof of Lemma 8.5(a).
   (ii) By the same domination, $\chi\mapsto Q_\chi(\beta)$ is continuous on $\mathbb T^\infty$. By (i) it vanishes on a nonempty open set, which contains a cylinder $U_1\times\dots\times U_m\times\prod_{p>p_m}\mathbb T$ with open arcs $U_j$. Write $n=ab$, where $a$ has all its prime factors among $p_1,\dots,p_m$ and $(b,p_1\cdots p_m)=1$. Then
   $$\textstyle Q_\chi(\beta)=\sum_bF_b(\chi_{p_1},\dots,\chi_{p_m})\,\chi(b)\,b^{-\beta},\qquad F_b(z)=\sum_ac_{ab}a^{-\beta}\prod_{j\le m}z_j^{v_{p_j}(a)},$$
   with $\sum_b\sup_{\overline{\mathbb D}^m}|F_b|\,b^{-\beta}<\infty$.
   - Fix $z\in U_1\times\dots\times U_m$. Then $\chi''\mapsto Q_{(z,\chi'')}(\beta)$ is an absolutely convergent Fourier series on the tail torus, with coefficient $F_b(z)b^{-\beta}$ at the distinct characters $\chi''\mapsto\chi''(b)$. It vanishes identically, so $F_b(z)=0$.
   - Now fix $(z_2,\dots,z_m)\in U_2\times\dots\times U_m$. Then $z_1\mapsto F_b(z)$ is in the disc algebra and vanishes on the arc $U_1$, so it vanishes on $\overline{\mathbb D}$.
   - Repeat this in $z_2,\dots,z_m$, each time with the earlier variables free in $\overline{\mathbb D}$. This gives $F_b\equiv0$, so every $c_{ab}=0$. $\square$

   For $Z=Z^+$, Proposition 8.10 says that a single nonzero $Q$ as in (a) with $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2}<\infty$ exists only if no section $\Lambda_\beta(Z^+)$ has interior. In other words, the phase vectors $(p^{-i\gamma})_p$ of the off-line zeros must not fill an open set in any limit. The example $1-2^{\beta-s}$ above has sections with empty interior: on its progression $2^{-i\gamma_k}=1$, so the limit set lies in $\{\chi_2=1\}$, where $Q_\chi(\beta)=1-\chi_2$ vanishes. We know of no result that constrains the phases of hypothetical off-line zeros. Landau-type formulas for $\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}x^\rho$ concern all zeros and do not separate a sparse subset. So an unconditional route through (a) needs one of two things:
   - a theorem that every section $\Lambda_\beta(Z^+)$ has empty interior, together with a $Q$ adapted to it; or
   - a family $Q_T$ vanishing on $Z^+\cap\{|\operatorname{Im}s|\le T\}$ for which (b) holds uniformly as $T\to\infty$, although the coefficient norms grow. [26, §7] notes that its methods give no constants uniform in the coefficient norm.

   Seip [27] characterizes the bounded zero sets in the Hardy space of Dirichlet series, and remarks that the vertical repetition pattern of zeros seems out of reach. Proposition 8.10 gives an elementary necessary condition of that kind. Theorem 8.15 below is consistent with it. When $Z_2^+$ is infinite, its multiplier $B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})$ has $\sum|c_n|n^{-1/2}=\sum_j|\beta_j|=\infty$, because an inner function in the disc algebra is a finite Blaschke product. (Infinitely many zeros may lie in finitely many classes $\rho+2\pi i\mathbb Z/\log2$. Then $B$ is a finite Blaschke product and $\sum_j|\beta_j|<\infty$.) Under (DB), moreover, each section $\Lambda_\beta(Z^+)$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$ lies in finitely many sets $\{\chi_2=c\}$.

   **Remark 8.11** (invariance alone does not give synthesis). Let $\theta$ be a nonconstant singular inner function on $\mathbb D$, for instance $\theta(z)=\exp(-\frac{1+z}{1-z})$. Let $V_2=\sqrt2M_2$ be the pure isometry of the proof of Proposition 8.3, so that $S\cong H^2(\mathbb D;W)$ with $V_2$ acting as multiplication by $z$.
   - Then $\theta(V_2)$ is multiplication by $\theta$. It is an isometry with closed range $\theta H^2(\mathbb D;W)\ne H^2(\mathbb D;W)$.
   - The Fejér means $p_j$ of $\theta$ satisfy $\|p_j\|_\infty\le1$ and $p_j(V_2)\to\theta(V_2)$ strongly. Every $M_n$ commutes with $V_2$, so it commutes with $\theta(V_2)$.
   - Point evaluations are continuous on $S$ and $p_j\to\theta$ in $\mathbb D$. Hence $\theta(V_2)g=\theta(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})\,g$, and $X_\theta:=\{\theta(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})g:g\in S\}$ is a proper closed subspace of $S$ invariant under every $M_n$.
   - Its elements have no common zero in $\Omega$, because $\theta$ has no zeros in $\mathbb D$ and $E(\rho)=1/\rho\ne0$.
   - Its complement is $K_\theta\otimes W$, with $K_\theta=H^2\ominus\theta H^2$. It contains no root vector of $M_2^*=V_2^*/\sqrt2$. Indeed, the root vectors of $V_2^*$ for the eigenvalue $\bar\mu$, $\mu\in\mathbb D$, are the vectors $\sum_{j\le J}\partial^j_{\bar\mu}k_\mu\otimes w_j$, where $k_\mu$ is the Szegő kernel. Such a vector is orthogonal to $\theta H^2\otimes W$ iff $\sum_j(\theta h)^{(j)}(\mu)\langle w,w_j\rangle=0$ for all $h\in H^2$ and $w\in W$. Since $\theta(\mu)\ne0$, the jets $((\theta h)^{(j)}(\mu))_{j\le J}$ fill $\mathbb C^{J+1}$, so every $w_j=0$.

   So $X_\theta$ is a closed $M$-invariant subspace that is not determined by zeros, and its complement has no generalized common eigenvectors. Any proof that $V=J_{Z^+}$ must use properties of $\zeta$ beyond the $M$-invariance of $V$. This is the transfer to $S$ of the classical polydisc example $\theta(z_1)H^2(\mathbb D^2)$.

   **Corollary 8.12** (one dilation suffices; from [28, (14)]). $V=J_{Z^+}$ if and only if $S\ominus V$ is the closed span of the root vectors of $M_2^*|_{S\ominus V}$ with eigenvalues $\mu$ in $\tfrac12<|\mu|<2^{-1/2}$.

   *Proof.* Under the antilinear Riesz identification of the dual $S'$ with $S$, the transpose $T_2\Phi=\Phi\circ M_2$ becomes $M_2^*$ and the annihilator $V^\circ$ becomes $S\ominus V$; eigenvalues are conjugated. Let $\mu$ be as stated. Then $\bar\mu=2^{-\rho}$ for some $\rho$ with $\tfrac12<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$. By [28, (14)], every root vector of $T_2$ in $V^\circ$ for $\bar\mu$ lies in the closed span of the jets $\operatorname{ev}^{(j)}_{\rho+2\pi ik/\log2}$ with $j<m_\zeta(\rho+2\pi ik/\log2)$. So it lies in $S\ominus J_{Z^+}$.
   - If the span condition holds, then $S\ominus V\subseteq S\ominus J_{Z^+}$, i.e. $J_{Z^+}\subseteq V$. The reverse inclusion is §2.
   - Conversely, if $V=J_{Z^+}$, then $S\ominus V$ is the closed span of the jet representers. These are root vectors of $M_2^*$ with eigenvalues $\overline{2^{-\rho}}$, of modulus $2^{-\operatorname{Re}\rho}\in(\tfrac12,2^{-1/2})$. $\square$

   Thus synthesis for $V$ is completeness of the root vectors of one operator. [28] classifies each generalized eigenspace, including infinite-dimensional ones. What remains is completeness. By Remark 8.11 it fails for general $M$-invariant subspaces. For $V$ it holds under (DB) (Theorem 8.15) and is open otherwise.

   Completeness follows from one membership when $Z^+$ is sparse in the dyadic variable. For $\rho\in Z^+$ put $w_\rho=2^{1/2-\rho}=\sqrt2\,2^{-\rho}$. Then $2^{-1/2}<|w_\rho|<1$ and $1-|w_\rho|\asymp\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12$. Let $Z_2^+=\{w_\rho:\rho\in Z^+\}$, where $w\in Z_2^+$ carries the multiplicity $m^*(w)=\sup\{m(\rho):w_\rho=w\}\le\infty$. Under the hypothesis of (i) below, or under (DB), every $m^*(w)$ is finite, hence attained. (Zeros differing by a multiple of $2\pi i/\log2$ give the same $w$.) For $u\in H^\infty(\mathbb D)$ let $u(V_2)$ be multiplication by $u$ in the Wold model of Remark 8.11. As there, $u(V_2)g=u(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})\,g$, and $u(V_2)$ commutes with every $M_n$.

   **Proposition 8.13** (one target suffices under a dyadic Blaschke condition).
   (i) If $u\in H^\infty(\mathbb D)$, $u\not\equiv0$ and $u(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})E\in V$, then $u$ vanishes at every $w\in Z_2^+$ to order at least $m^*(w)$. In particular
   $$\textstyle\sum_{w\in Z_2^+}m^*(w)\,(1-|w|)<\infty. \tag{DB}$$
   (ii) Suppose (DB) holds, and let $B$ be the Blaschke product with zero multiset $Z_2^+$. Then $V=J_{Z^+}$ if and only if $B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})E\in V$, i.e. $\operatorname{dist}(B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})/s,V_N)\to0$.

   *Proof.* (i) $V\subseteq J_{Z^+}$ (§2), so $u(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})/s$ vanishes to order $m(\rho)$ at each $\rho\in Z^+$. Since $1/\rho\ne0$ and $s\mapsto\sqrt2\,2^{-s}$ is locally biholomorphic, $u$ vanishes to order at least $m(\rho)$ at $w_\rho$. The zeros of a nonzero bounded function satisfy the Blaschke condition.
   (ii) If $V=J_{Z^+}$, then $B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})E\in J_{Z^+}=V$ by the same local argument. Conversely, suppose $B(V_2)E\in V$.
   - $e_m=(M_m-M_{m+1})E$, so the $M_nE$ span a dense subspace of $S$. Since $B(V_2)$ commutes with the $M_n$, $B(V_2)S\subseteq V$. Hence $B(V_2)^*x=0$ for every $x\in K:=S\ominus V$.
   - Enumerate $Z_2^+$ with multiplicity as $w_1,w_2,\dots$, and write $B=B_nR_n$, where $B_n$ is the product of the first $n$ factors $b_j(z)=\frac{|w_j|}{w_j}\frac{w_j-z}{1-\bar w_jz}$. Then $R_n(0)=\prod_{j>n}|w_j|\to1$, so $\|R_n-1\|_{L^2(\mathbb T)}^2=2-2R_n(0)\to0$. Testing on $W$-valued polynomials, together with $\|R_n(V_2)\|\le1$, shows $R_n(V_2)\to I$ strongly.
   - Fix $x\in K$ and put $y_n=R_n(V_2)^*x$. $V$ is invariant under $V_2$, hence under the strong limits $R_n(V_2)$ of Fejér polynomials in $V_2$. So $y_n\in K$. Also $B_n(V_2)^*y_n=B(V_2)^*x=0$.
   - $B_n(V_2)^*$ is an invertible operator times $\prod_{j\le n}(\bar w_j-V_2^*)$. So $y_n$ lies in the kernel of a polynomial in $M_2^*=V_2^*/\sqrt2$ with roots $\bar w_j/\sqrt2=\overline{2^{-\rho}}$. Hence $y_n$ is a finite sum of root vectors of $M_2^*|_K$, obtained from $y_n$ by polynomials in $M_2^*$. Their eigenvalues have modulus in $(\tfrac12,2^{-1/2})$, so by [28, (14)], as in the proof of Corollary 8.12, $y_n\in S\ominus J_{Z^+}$.
   - $y_n\to x$ weakly, so $x\in S\ominus J_{Z^+}$. Thus $K\subseteq S\ominus J_{Z^+}$, i.e. $J_{Z^+}\subseteq V$. $\square$

   For $Z^+=\emptyset$, (ii) is the Báez-Duarte criterion $E\in V\iff V=S$ [1]. For finite $Z^+$, both sides of (ii) hold by Theorem A. (DB) holds if $\sum_{\rho\in Z^+}m(\rho)(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)<\infty$, and it is equivalent to this when no two zeros in $Z^+$ differ by a nonzero multiple of $2\pi i/\log2$, since $1-2^{-b}\asymp b$ for $0<b<\tfrac12$. For each $\delta>0$ it forces $Z^+\cap\{\operatorname{Re}s\ge\tfrac12+\delta\}$ into finitely many classes $\rho+2\pi i\mathbb Z/\log2$. In the continuous problem the corresponding condition is the half-plane Blaschke condition, which always holds, and Burnol's theorem [5] gives synthesis there. In the dyadic variable the zeros are wrapped onto $\mathbb D$ with period $2\pi i/\log2$, and (DB) becomes a genuine sparsity hypothesis. If (DB) fails, then by (i) $V$ contains no nonzero element of the form $u(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})E$. Step (ii) is the usual model-space argument for a contraction annihilated by a Blaschke product (the class $C_0$ of Sz.-Nagy–Foias [29]), written out for this case.

   The target in (ii) can be reached by the method of §3, which extends from Dirichlet polynomials to bounded Dirichlet series. Let $\mathcal H^\infty_\Omega$ be the set of Dirichlet series $Q(s)=\sum_nq_nn^{-s}$ that converge on $\Omega$ and satisfy $|Q|\le1$ there. Thus $Q(s+\tfrac12)$ lies in the unit ball of the algebra of bounded Dirichlet series on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ studied in [18, 30]. Write $Z^+\subseteq Z(Q)$ if $Q$ vanishes to order at least $m(\rho)$ at every $\rho\in Z^+$. For $\rho\in Z^+$ put $b_\rho=\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12\in(0,\tfrac12)$.

   **Proposition 8.14** (bounded multipliers). Let $Q\in\mathcal H^\infty_\Omega$, $Q\not\equiv0$, with $Z^+\subseteq Z(Q)$. Then $Q/s\in V$, and hence $QS\subseteq V$.

   For finite $Z^+$ and $Q=P/\sum_n|p_n|n^{-1/2}$ this is Proposition 5. What is new is that (Q3) below needs no sparsity of $Z^+$.

   *Proof.* We follow the proof of Proposition 5 with $P$ replaced by $Q$. It needs three inputs.

   (Q1) *Expansion.* A bounded Dirichlet series on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ has square-summable coefficients, with $\ell^2$ norm at most its sup norm [18]. Applied to $Q(s+\tfrac12)=\sum_nq_nn^{-1/2}n^{-s}$ this gives $\sum_n|q_n|^2/n\le1$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\sum_n|q_n|n^{-\sigma}<\infty$ for $\sigma>1$. $Q/\zeta$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$, since $Z^+\subseteq Z(Q)$ and $1/\zeta$ is regular at $s=1$. On $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ it equals $\sum_lc_ll^{-s}$ with $c=q*\mu$, which converges absolutely there.

   (Q2) *Lemma 3 for $Q$.* For all $\eta\in(0,1]$ and $\theta>0$, $(Q/\zeta)(s)\ll_{Q,\eta,\theta}(1+|t|)^\theta$ on $\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\eta$.
   - $Q(s+\tfrac12)$ is almost periodic in every strip $\alpha<\sigma<\beta$ with $0<\alpha<\beta$ [30, §3]. By the Bohr–Jessen lemma [30, Lemma 3.2], applied in the strip $\frac\eta4<\sigma<3$, there is $N=N(Q,\eta)$ such that $Q$ has at most $N$ zeros, with multiplicity, in every rectangle $[\tfrac12+\tfrac\eta2,2]\times[\tau,\tau+1]$. Since $Z^+\subseteq Z(Q)$ and $\zeta\ne0$ on $\sigma\ge1$, the number $K$ of zeros of $\zeta$, with multiplicity, in $\{\sigma\ge\frac12+\frac\eta2,\ |\operatorname{Im}s-t|\le2\}$ is at most $4N$ for every $t$.
   - Fix $|t|\ge3$ and $c=2+it$. Choose $R'\in[\frac32-\frac{5\eta}8,\frac32-\frac\eta2]$ such that no zero lies on $|s-c|=R'$. Let $\beta_c$ be the Blaschke product of the disc $|s-c|<R'$ over the zeros $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k$ ($k\le K$) of $\zeta$ in it. Then $|\beta_c|=1$ on the circle, $(\tfrac23)^K\le|\beta_c(c)|\le1$ (as $|\rho_j-c|\ge1$), and $|\beta_c|\ge(4R')^{-K}$ on $|s-c|\le\frac12$.
   - Hence $\zeta_c=\zeta/\beta_c$ is zero-free on the closed disc. It satisfies $\log|\zeta_c|=\log|\zeta|\le A\log|t|$ on the circle, and $\log\zeta_c=O_K(1)$ near $c$.
   - The Borel–Carathéodory and three-circle argument of [10, §14.2], used in Lemma 3, applies to $\zeta_c$ unchanged. It gives $\log\zeta_c(s)=o(\log|t|)$, uniformly for $s=\sigma+it$ with $\frac12+\eta\le\sigma\le1$.
   - $Q/\beta_c$ is holomorphic on the disc and has modulus $\le1$ on the circle, so $|Q/\beta_c|\le1$ inside. Thus $|Q/\zeta|=|Q/\beta_c|\,|\zeta_c|^{-1}\le|t|^\theta$ there for $|t|\ge t_0(\eta,\theta)$.
   - The ranges $\sigma\ge1$ and $|t|\le t_0$ are treated as in Lemma 3.

   (Q3) *Lemma 4 for $Q$.* By [10, Theorem 9.6(A)] there is an absolute constant $A_0$ such that $\sum m(\rho)(t-\operatorname{Im}\rho)^{-2}\le A_0\log(|t|+2)$ for all real $t$, the sum running over the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$ with $|t-\operatorname{Im}\rho|>1$. Let $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0<1$. On $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12$,
   $$\Big|Q(s+\varepsilon)\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\varepsilon)}\Big|\le C(\varepsilon_0)\,(1+|t|)^{(1+A_0)\varepsilon/2}.$$
   The left side is $|(Q/\zeta)(s+\varepsilon)\,\zeta(s)|$, which is continuous in $t$, so it suffices to take $t$ with $s+\varepsilon\notin Z^+$. Put $u_\rho=t-\operatorname{Im}\rho$ and $r_\rho=\sqrt{b_\rho^2+u_\rho^2}/\sqrt{(b_\rho-\varepsilon)^2+u_\rho^2}$.
   - The proof of Lemma 4, stopped before the bound $r_\rho\le b/(b-\varepsilon)$, uses neither $|Z^+|<\infty$ nor $\varepsilon<b_0$. It gives $|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\varepsilon)|\le C(\varepsilon_0)(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon/2}\prod_{\rho\in Z^+}r_\rho^{m(\rho)}$. The product converges by the bound for far zeros below.
   - *Near zeros.* Let $F=\{\rho\in Z^+:|u_\rho|\le1\}$, a finite set, and let $\beta_F(s)=\prod_{\rho\in F}\big(\frac{s-\rho}{s+\bar\rho-1}\big)^{m(\rho)}$ be the Blaschke product of $\Omega$ over $F$. Then $g=Q/\beta_F$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$, and $|g|\le1$ there. Indeed, let $0<\delta<\min_Fb_\rho$. On $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12+\delta$ we have $|\beta_F|\ge c_\delta:=\prod_F\big((b_\rho-\delta)/(b_\rho+\delta)\big)^{m(\rho)}$. Also $g$ is bounded on $\operatorname{Re}s\ge\frac12+\delta$, because $\beta_F\to1$ at $\infty$. So $|g|\le1/c_\delta$ there, by the maximum principle for bounded holomorphic functions on a half-plane, and $c_\delta\to1$ as $\delta\to0$. Since $|s+\varepsilon-\rho|=\sqrt{(b_\rho-\varepsilon)^2+u_\rho^2}$ and $|s+\varepsilon+\bar\rho-1|=\sqrt{(b_\rho+\varepsilon)^2+u_\rho^2}$, this gives
   $$|Q(s+\varepsilon)|\prod_{\rho\in F}r_\rho^{m(\rho)}\le\prod_{\rho\in F}\Big(\frac{b_\rho^2+u_\rho^2}{(b_\rho+\varepsilon)^2+u_\rho^2}\Big)^{m(\rho)/2}\le1.$$
   - *Far zeros.* For $|u_\rho|>1$, $\log r_\rho=\frac12\log\big(1+\frac{2b_\rho\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2}{(b_\rho-\varepsilon)^2+u_\rho^2}\big)\le b_\rho\varepsilon/u_\rho^2\le\varepsilon/(2u_\rho^2)$. Hence $\prod_{\rho\in Z^+\setminus F}r_\rho^{m(\rho)}\le(|t|+2)^{A_0\varepsilon/2}$.
   - Multiplying the three bounds gives (Q3).

   With (Q1)–(Q3), part (a) of the proof of Proposition 5 holds verbatim with $Q$ in place of $P$: $F_\varepsilon=[(Q/\zeta)(s+\varepsilon)-(Q/\zeta)(1+\varepsilon)]\zeta(s)/s\in V$. The Perron step uses (Q1) on $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ and the contour shift uses (Q2).
   In part (b), take $\varepsilon_0<1/(1+A_0)$. Then $(Q/\zeta)(1+\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon)$. On the line, $Q(s+\varepsilon)\zeta(s)/(\zeta(s+\varepsilon)s)$ is dominated by $C(\varepsilon_0)(1+|t|)^{(1+A_0)\varepsilon_0/2}/|s|\in L^2$, by (Q3). It tends to $Q(\tfrac12+it)/s$ for almost every $t$, by Fatou's theorem for bounded holomorphic functions on $\Omega$ and since $\zeta(\tfrac12+it)\ne0$ for almost every $t$. Locally uniform convergence in $\Omega$ holds because $Q/\zeta$ is holomorphic there. Hence $Q/s\in V$. Finally, $V$ is $M_n$-invariant (Problem 1, *Reformulation*), so $Q\,n^{-s}/s=M_n(Q/s)\in V$ for every $n$. Multiplication by $Q$ is a contraction on $H^2$, and the $n^{-s}/s$ span a dense subspace of $S$ (Lemma 2). Hence $QS\subseteq V$. $\square$

   **Theorem 8.15** (synthesis under (DB); uses [28, (14)] through Proposition 8.13(ii)). If (DB) holds, then $B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})/s\in V$, and therefore $V=J_{Z^+}$. In particular, $V=J_{Z^+}$ whenever $\sum_{\rho\in Z^+}m(\rho)(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)<\infty$.

   *Proof.* Put $Q(s)=B(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})=\sum_{j\ge0}\beta_j2^{j/2}2^{-js}$, where $\beta_j$ are the Taylor coefficients of $B$, $|\beta_j|\le1$. This Dirichlet series converges absolutely on $\Omega$, where $|\sqrt2\,2^{-s}|<1$, and $|Q|\le1$ there. It vanishes to order $m^*(w_\rho)\ge m(\rho)$ at each $\rho\in Z^+$, since $s\mapsto\sqrt2\,2^{-s}$ is locally biholomorphic. Proposition 8.14 gives $Q/s\in V$, and Proposition 8.13(ii) gives $V=J_{Z^+}$. $\square$

   Under (DB), therefore, $\lim d_N^2=\sup_Fc_F^*\Gamma_F^{-1}c_F$ over finite $F\subset Z^+$, the distance formula of Theorem C with a supremum. By Proposition 8.14, Problem 1 splits into two parts. (E) Is there a nonzero $Q\in\mathcal H^\infty_\Omega$ with $Z^+\subseteq Z(Q)$? (T) If so, does $Q/s\in V$ imply $V=J_{Z^+}$? For series in $2^{-s}$ alone both are settled. Such a $Q$ is $u(\sqrt2\,2^{-s})$ with $u\in H^\infty(\mathbb D)$ vanishing to order $m^*(w)$ at each $w\in Z_2^+$. So it exists iff (DB) holds, and then $V=J_{Z^+}$ by Theorem 8.15. Problem 1 therefore remains open only when (DB) fails. The compensation in (Q3) is local and exact: the factor of $\beta_F$ at $s+\varepsilon$ turns the ratio $|s-\rho|/|s+\varepsilon-\rho|$, which makes Lemma 4 fail for infinite $Z^+$ (§5, Remark 2), into $|s-\rho|/|s+\varepsilon+\bar\rho-1|\le1$. Proposition 8.14 and Theorem 8.15 use the Euler product only as Proposition 5 does (§5, Remark 1). They concern synthesis, not the location of zeros, and give no information on RH. Whether (DB) holds for $\zeta$ is unknown. It holds if $Z^+$ is finite. It fails if, for instance, $Z^+$ contains zeros $\rho_k$ in distinct classes with $\operatorname{Re}\rho_k-\tfrac12\asymp1/k$. Zero-density estimates allow both cases. Two constraints on (E) are known. First, any such $Q$ forces a weaker, local form of (DB): the local Blaschke condition of Brevig–Kouroupis–Perfekt [30, Theorem 3.6], applied to $Q(s+\tfrac12)$, gives $\sup_\tau\sum_{\rho\in Z^+,\ \tau\le\operatorname{Im}\rho\le\tau+1}m(\rho)(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)<\infty$. (DB) implies this, because a window of height $1$ meets each class at most once. The trivial bound for this sum is $O(\log\tau)$. Second, if $\sum_n|q_n|n^{-1/2}<\infty$, Proposition 8.10 constrains the phases of $Z^+$. Between the local condition and (DB), multipliers in several primes are not excluded. For them (T) is open: Proposition 8.13(ii) rests on the model theory of the single isometry $V_2$ and on [28, (14)], and we know no substitute in several variables.
2. **Rate.** Under FOZ (finitely many off-line zeros, $|Z^+|<\infty$), $V_N\subset V$ gives $d_N^2=D_\infty^2+\operatorname{dist}(u,V_N)^2$. Theorem F gives the lower bound. We conjectured that it is sharp. The matching upper bound was first proved in [24, Theorem G] under two hypotheses beyond FOZ: all zeros on the line are simple, and $\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2}\ll T^{3/2-\delta}$ (the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer condition). u/bramble_trace [25, Theorem 6] has since proved it under FOZ alone, with multiplicities, by adaptive zero clusters. So the statement below is now a theorem of [25], combined with Theorem F; we keep its original label for reference. [25] also gives the analogue for the unconstrained distance $\delta_N$ with a different projected target.

   *Former Conjecture R* [FOZ; proved in [25, Theorem 6]]. With $u=P_VE$,

$$(d_N^2-D_\infty^2)\log N\ \longrightarrow\ \sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2}m(\rho)^2\,|u(\rho)|^2 .$$

   Under RH with simple zeros this is the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer asymptotic, which they prove only under an extra hypothesis on $\sum1/|\zeta'(\rho)|^2$ [6]; [25, Theorem 1] removes that hypothesis and simplicity, for both $\delta_N$ and $d_N$. The remarks below record the evidence we gave before [25]. The result of [6] is for $\delta_N$. It transfers to $d_N$ whenever the near-optimal polynomials $A_N$ satisfy $A_N(1)=o((\log N)^{-1/2})$. Replacing $a_1$ by $a_1-A_N(1)$ enforces $A_N(1)=0$ and changes the distance by at most $|A_N(1)|\,\|\zeta/s\|_{L^2(\text{line})}$. For instance, the weights $\mu(n)(1-\log n/\log N)$ give $A_N(1)=(\log N)^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}\mu(n)n^{-1}\log(N/n)\sim1/\log N$ (`code/bcf_AN1.py`). Toy-model evidence: in Burnol's disc setting [9, Thm 1.4] with $Q(z)=(1-z/\omega)(1-z/a)$, $|\omega|=1$, $|a|<1$, we computed $E(N)=\min_{\deg A\le N}\|1-QA\|^2$ numerically, in double precision (`code/toy_rate2.py`). For nine choices of $(\omega,a)$ and $N\le1600$, $N(E(N)-E_\infty)\to|a|^2=|(P_{bH^2}1)(\omega)|^2$ to three digits. That is, the projection onto the *approximable* part, evaluated at the boundary zero, governs the rate. This is the analogue of $|u(\rho)|^2$. The toy statement can also be derived. Let $u=P_{bH^2}1=\overline{b(0)}\,b$. Then $1-u\perp bH^2\supset Q\mathcal P_N$, so $E(N)-E_\infty=\operatorname{dist}(u,Q\mathcal P_N)^2$. Division by $b$ is isometric, and $Q/b=-(1-z/\omega)(1-\bar az)/a$. Hence $E(N)-E_\infty=\min_A\|\overline{b(0)}-(1-z/\omega)(1-\bar az)A\|^2$. This is Burnol's problem [9, Thm 1.4 and Note 2.2] with one root on the circle and one outside the closed disc, and its limit is $|b(0)|^2=|a|^2$. The toy proof uses the fact that $\mathcal P_N$ is stable under the division by $b$ step, since everything is a polynomial in $z$. The same mechanism works inside $S$ when $P$ is a polynomial in $2^{-s}$. Then $\sqrt2M_2$ is an isometry with wandering vector $e_1$, $E=\sum_j2^{-j/2}(\sqrt2M_2)^je_1$, and for $M=2^d-1$ we have $\operatorname{dist}(E,P\operatorname{span}\{e_m\}_{m\le M})^2=\tfrac12\min_{\deg A<d}\|f-pA\|^2_{H^2(\mathbb D)}$, where $f=(1-z/\sqrt2)^{-1}$ and $p(z)=P|_{2^{-s}=z/\sqrt2}$. This identity is checked to $10^{-15}$ in `code/tree_reduction.py`. Take $P=(2^{-s}-2^{-1/2})(1+2^{0.7}2^{-s})$, which has zeros $\rho_k=\frac12+2\pi ik/\log2$ on the line and infinitely many zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=0.7$. Let $u$ be the projection of $E$ onto $\overline{PS}=J_P$ (Proposition 8.3) and $D^2=\operatorname{dist}(E,\overline{PS})^2$. Numerically, $(\operatorname{dist}^2-D^2)\log M$ increases towards $\sum_k|u(\rho_k)|^2=3.8526$, the analogue of the value in Former Conjecture R, and reaches $3.8502$ at $d=3200$. It does not tend to $\sum_k|E(\rho_k)|^2=4.0400$. For $\zeta$, the spaces $V_N$ and $S$ are not invariant under division by $B$, so the argument does not transfer directly. For $\zeta$, the lower bound is Theorem F. The upper bound needs near-optimal elements of $V_N$ for $u$, in the style of the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer mollifier. [25] builds them by adaptive zero clusters when $Z^+\ne\emptyset$ is finite.
3. **$L^p$ and rate variants.** Proposition 6 and Corollary F′ give the limit of the unconstrained distance $\delta_N$ of [7, (1.1)] up to the factor $c_0$ and a lower bound for its rate. The Corollary F′ bound is sharp: [25, Theorem 6, (91)] proves the matching limit with the same minimizer $c_*$ and target $u_*$. Is there an analogue of Theorem A for Beurling's $L^p$ version? Proposition 8.16 gives a partial answer.

   **Proposition 8.16** ($L^p$ count). Let $1<p<2$. Let $S_p$ and $V_p$ be the closures in $L^p(0,1)$ of $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n]}:n\ge1\}$ and of $\operatorname{span}\{\rho_{1/l}:l\ge2\}$, where $\rho_{1/l}(t)=\{1/(lt)\}-l^{-1}\{1/t\}$, so that $\hat\rho_{1/l}=-G_l$. Let $Z^+_p=Z^+\cap\{\operatorname{Re}s>1/p\}$.
   (i) $\operatorname{codim}_{S_p}V_p\ge|Z^+_p|$.
   (ii) Suppose $Z^+\cap\{\operatorname{Re}s>1/p-\delta\}$ is finite for some $\delta>0$. Then $V_p=\{f\in S_p:\hat f^{(j)}(\rho)=0\ \forall\rho\in Z^+_p,\ j<m(\rho)\}$ and $\operatorname{codim}_{S_p}V_p=|Z^+_p|$. In particular, if $|Z^+|<\infty$, this holds for every $p\in(1,2)$. Zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=1/p$ impose no condition.

   *Proof.* (i) For $\operatorname{Re}\rho>1/p$ we have $t^{\rho-1}\log^jt\in L^{p'}(0,1)$. So $f\mapsto\hat f^{(j)}(\rho)=\int_0^1f(t)\,t^{\rho-1}\log^jt\,dt$ is bounded on $L^p(0,1)$. It vanishes on each $\rho_{1/l}$, hence on $V_p$. On step functions it agrees with the jet of $D/s$, and Lemma 2 gives independence, as in Consequence (i).
   (ii) Let $L$ be the finite set of zeros on $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac1p$, and put $W=Z^+_p\cup L$. Choose $\sigma_0\in(\max(\frac12,\frac1p-\delta),\frac1p)$ above the real parts of the finitely many zeros with $\frac1p-\delta<\operatorname{Re}s<\frac1p$. Then the zeros with $\operatorname{Re}s>\sigma_0$ are exactly those in $W$, and none has real part in $[\sigma_0,\frac1p)$. Put $c=\sigma_0-\frac12$ and $H_c=L^2((0,1),t^{2c}dt)$. The Mellin transform is unitary from $H_c$ onto $H^2(\operatorname{Re}s>\sigma_0)$ (Paley–Wiener applied to $t^cg$). §§2–4 go through on the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0$, with $W$ in place of $Z^+$:
   - Lemma 2 is algebraic, and the $e_m$ remain orthogonal in $H_c$.
   - The proof of Lemma 3 needs only $\zeta\ne0$ on $\{\operatorname{Re}s>\sigma_0+\eta/2,\ |\operatorname{Im}s-t|\le2\}$ for large $|t|$ (compare [32, Theorem 4]).
   - In Lemma 4, put $z=c+it$. Every zero with real part $\le\sigma_0$, including those on the critical line, gives a ratio $\le1$, because both $|z\mp\gamma|$ are nondecreasing in $\operatorname{Re}z\ge\operatorname{Re}\gamma$. The finitely many zeros in $W$ give $b/(b-\varepsilon)$. The Stirling factor is unchanged.
   - In Proposition 5, $\int_T^{2T}|\zeta(\sigma_0+it)|^2dt\ll T$ replaces the mean value on the critical line.

   So the $H_c$-closure $V_c$ of $\operatorname{span}\{\rho_{1/l}\}$ is the jet kernel at $W$ in $S_c$, the $H_c$-closure of the step functions. By Hölder, $\|g\|_p\le\|t^{-c}\|_q\|g\|_{H_c}$ with $\frac1q=\frac1p-\frac12$, and the right side is finite because $c<\frac1q$. So $V_c\subset V_p$.

   Let $E$ be the finitely supported step functions, $J$ the jet kernel at $Z^+_p$ in $S_p$, $E_1=E\cap J$ and $E_0\subset E_1$ the functions whose jets also vanish at $L$. Then $E_0\subset V_c\subset V_p$, and it remains to show that $E_0$ is $L^p$-dense in $J$. Choose $d_1,\dots,d_k\in E$, $k=|Z^+_p|$, dual to the jets $\phi_i$ at $Z^+_p$ (Lemma 2(ii)). The bounded projection $Pf=f-\sum_i\phi_i(f)d_i$ maps $E$ onto $E_1$ and $S_p$ onto $J$, so $E_1$ is dense in $J$.

   Let $\psi_1,\dots,\psi_r$ be the jets at $L$, which are linear functionals on $E$. Suppose no nonzero combination $\psi=\sum\alpha_j\psi_j$ is $L^p$-bounded on $E_1$. Then $E_0=\bigcap\ker\psi_j|_{E_1}$ is dense in $E_1$. Otherwise a bounded $\lambda\ne0$ on $E_1$ vanishes on $E_0$, so $\lambda$ factors through $(\psi_j)$ and is such a combination. Boundedness on $E_1$ would imply boundedness on $E$, since $f=Pf+\sum\phi_i(f)d_i$. So it suffices to show that $\psi$ is unbounded on $E$.

   Now $\psi(f)=\int_0^1fg$ with $g(t)=t^{1/p-1}G(-\log t)$, where $G(x)=\sum_{\rho\in L}e^{-i\gamma_\rho x}P_\rho(-x)$, the $\gamma_\rho$ are distinct, and the polynomials $P_\rho$ are not all zero. Let $I_m=(\frac1{m+1},\frac1m]$ and let $\bar g_m$ be the mean of $g$ on $I_m$. By $\ell^p$ duality, the norm of $\psi$ on $E$ is $(\sum_m|\bar g_m|^{p'}|I_m|)^{1/p'}$. Since $\bar g_m=m^{1-1/p}(G(\log m)+O(m^{-1}\log^am))$ with $a=\max\deg P_\rho$, $|I_m|\asymp m^{-2}$ and $(1-\frac1p)p'=1$, the sum is at least $c\sum_m|G(\log m)|^{p'}/m-C$, the error being summable. This is at least $c\int_1^\infty|G|^{p'}dx-C$, because $|G'(x)|\ll x^a$. Write $G(x)=x^aT(x)+O(x^{a-1})$, where $T$ is a nonzero trigonometric polynomial. Since $p'>2$, Hölder and the mean value of $|T|^2$ give $\int_X^{2X}|T|^{p'}\gg X$, so the integral diverges. Hence $V_p\supseteq J$, and (i) gives equality. $\square$

   For $Z^+_p=\emptyset$, (ii) is a discrete form of Beurling's $L^p$ theorem [31], under the stated hypothesis. Beurling's theorem itself (continuous dilations; density iff $\zeta\ne0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/p$) needs no such hypothesis. Its proof goes through Beurling's semigroup theorem; see [32, Theorem 5 and Lemma D].
   For Dirichlet $L$-functions and $p\in(1,2]$, Dimitrov–Oliveira [32, Theorems 1–2] prove a discrete criterion in a weighted $L^2$ norm on the line $\operatorname{Re}s=1/p$ itself, where zeros on that line do no harm. This corresponds to our $H_c$ at the endpoint $c=\frac1p-\frac12$, where the Hölder step fails: $t^{-c}$ is only weak-$L^q$, and $L^2\cdot L^{q,\infty}\subset L^{p,2}\supsetneq L^p$ for $p<2$.
   One case is not covered: infinitely many zeros with real part $>1/p-\delta$ for every $\delta>0$, i.e. zeros accumulating at $\operatorname{Re}s=1/p$. This is the Problem 1 obstruction at abscissa $1/p$, since every admissible $H_c$ then sees infinitely many zeros. Finitely many zeros on the line are harmless: the proof of (ii) treats them as constraints in $H_c$ and then removes them, because their jets are unbounded on $S_p$. The proof of (i) works for every $p\ge1$. For $p>2$ it shows that the zeros on the critical line give $\operatorname{codim}=\infty$.
