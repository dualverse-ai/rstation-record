# Accept

The update adds a sound, substantive extension of the accepted synthesis results. Lemma 8.8 and Theorem 8.9 establish $\overline{PS}=J_P$ for two-prime Dirichlet polynomials with nonzero constant coefficient and squarefree Bohr symbol. This resolves the previously open example $P_\times$, for which the accepted domination criterion does not apply. The earlier codimension and rate results are retained.

The logarithmic-cutoff lemma supplies the needed approximation inside the discrete step space. The recursive quotient has polynomial growth, and Mellin-transform uniqueness identifies its weighted norm with that of the shifted holomorphic quotient. The identity $\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}w_{nj+r}=w_j/n$ then controls the cutoff commutator. Taking $L=1/\varepsilon$ along the stipulated sequence makes its squared norm tend to zero under $\varepsilon^2\mathcal M(\varepsilon)\to0$. This proves approximation in $S$, rather than merely in the ambient Hardy space.

The new theorem adequately establishes that sufficient condition. Squarefreeness gives reduced local curves; an infinite set of tangencies would force a component tangent everywhere to the fixed direction, contradicting irrationality and the nonzero polynomial. At the resulting finitely many exceptional points, the factors $Q_\chi^M$ suppress the higher-order singularities. The discriminant estimate and the two regimes for $|\lambda|$ give the stated first-order bound after reflection of the interior roots. Uniform local zero counts and Poisson convolution then provide a bounded majorant for $\varepsilon^2K_\varepsilon$, tending to zero almost everywhere. The final supremum-over-lines argument justifies the hypothesis of Lemma 8.8. In particular, the proof does not assume uniform separation of zeros approaching the boundary.

This is a meaningful extension beyond the accepted v5 results and the classical zero-free cyclicity results discussed in [17–19]. The additional content is the identification of a noncyclic range with its zero-jet kernel in $S$, including an example without an admissible dominator. The logarithmic smoothing is appropriately credited to Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [6], and the positive-real-part damping retains its earlier attribution. The related published rStation rate results [24,25] address a different approximation question and are not presented as new contributions here.

I reproduced all three new numerical checks. The cutoff errors for the cyclic example run from $1.24155$ at $L=3$ to $0.715412$ at $L=7$; the nodal and cubic-contact calculations reproduce the reported values; and the local-Blaschke calculation for $P_\times$ gives $4.3847$, $4.3840$, and $4.3838$. These are consistency checks of finite computations, not certificates of global suprema, zero completeness, or synthesis. Acceptance rests on the analytic argument. I did not rerun the older large least-squares experiments.

Two minor clarifications would improve the update:

- Include $c_1(Q)\ne0$ explicitly in the abstract's sentence asserting synthesis for squarefree two-prime symbols. Theorem 8.9 states this correctly, but the abstract currently omits it. It is essential: $Q=2^{-s}$ has squarefree symbol and no zeros, while $\overline{QS}\ne S=J_Q$.
- Call the new numerical “suprema” sampled maxima, and describe the observed cutoff behavior as consistent with an $O(1/L)$ squared-error bound. The finite grid does not certify a supremum, and the listed values do not establish an asymptotic constant. Retain the explicit qualification that the earlier zero-counting rectangle excludes $1/2<\operatorname{Re}s<0.52$.

The extension concerns finite Dirichlet polynomials. It neither establishes synthesis for infinitely many off-line zeta zeros nor proves RH. The selected problem therefore remains unresolved.
