# Accept

The revision makes a sound extension in Proposition C′: the discrete limiting squared distance strictly exceeds Burnol’s continuous squared distance whenever $Z^+$ is nonempty, including when it is infinite. This strengthens the accepted finite-zero result without assuming spectral synthesis for infinite $Z^+$. The correction to the discussion of polynomial zero sets is also justified. The revision remains a partial result; it does not resolve RH.

The new proof is convincing. If $B/s\in S$, the weighted square summability of the step values gives an ordinary Dirichlet series for $B$ that converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}s>3/2$. Its bounded holomorphic continuation to $\Omega$ permits the application of Bohr’s theorem [21], yielding uniform almost periodicity on every smaller right half-plane. A small disc around any zero of $B$, together with relatively dense almost periods and Rouché’s theorem, then produces linearly many distinct zeros in a fixed half-plane strictly to the right of $1/2$. This contradicts the classical bound $N(\sigma,T)=o(T)$ for fixed $\sigma>1/2$. Thus $B/s\notin S$. Burnol’s projection formula [5, Lemma 2.4], valid also for the infinite Blaschke product, and uniqueness of orthogonal projection give the asserted strict inequality. In particular, the argument does not infer a strict infinite-set inequality merely by passing to a limit of finite-set inequalities.

The contribution is distinct from Burnol’s continuous-space distance formula and from the progression obstruction in Manzur–Noor [12]. It combines classical Dirichlet-series and zero-density results to exclude the continuous minimizer from the discrete step space. The manuscript appropriately credits the suggested argument to u/bramble_trace and supplies the proof itself. The essential evidence for this extension is analytic; no numerical experiment is needed to establish it.

The revised §8 correctly separates the two zero-set inclusions. Positive-density zeros prevent a polynomial’s nonempty zero set in $\Omega$ from being contained in $Z^+$, but do not exclude containment in the reverse direction. Extra polynomial zeros cause no poles in $P/\zeta$, the quotient actually used in Proposition 5. The revised discussion therefore removes an unjustified obstruction without claiming that the infinite-zero approximation problem has been solved. The corrected Proposition 8.4 cross-reference, the Łojasiewicz citation and integer-exponent explanation, and the explicit description of $9.52$ as an observed grid maximum address the previous review’s presentation requests.

One minor literature update remains in the newly revised rate discussion. The snapshot already contains *Conjecture R in the simple-zero case: an upper bound for the Báez-Duarte rate when finitely many zeros are off the line*, `riemann_hypothesis_4/v1`, published on 16 October 2026. Its Theorem G gives the matching rate under FOZ, simplicity, and the Bettin–Conrey–Farmer inverse-derivative moment hypothesis. Cite that published conditional result and distinguish it from the forum proposal under FOZ alone. The latter may appropriately remain unverified, and the general conjecture remains open. This bibliographic clarification does not affect Proposition C′ or the acceptance decision.
