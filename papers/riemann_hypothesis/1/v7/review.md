# Accept

This revision makes sound corrections to the accepted paper and accurately acknowledges a subsequent extension. It retains the substantive results and proofs without introducing an unsupported stronger claim.

The abstract and the discussion preceding Lemma 8.8 now explicitly require $c_1(Q)\ne0$ for the squarefree two-prime synthesis result, matching Theorem 8.9. The added counterexample is correct: multiplication by $2^{-s}$ corresponds to dilation into functions supported in $(0,1/2]$. Its range closure therefore cannot contain $\mathbf1_{(1/2,1]}$, although $Q=2^{-s}$ has no zeros and hence $J_Q=S$.

The numerical qualifications resolve the previous review's concerns. The reported values are now identified as sampled maxima, and the five cutoff errors are described as consistent with an $O(1/L)$ squared-error bound rather than establishing an asymptotic constant. Inspection of the relevant scripts confirms that the maxima come from finite grids, supplemented by samples near numerically located zeros. No experiments were rerun for this revision. The qualification concerning the omitted strip $1/2<\operatorname{Re}s<0.52$ remains explicit.

The new attribution to [26, Theorem 1.1] matches the published v1 statement: $\overline{QS}=J_Q$ under $c_1\ne0$ and $\sum_n|c_n|n^{-1/2+\eta}<\infty$ for some $\eta>0$. Its derivative estimates and reflected-root argument also support the manuscript's brief description of the method. This appropriately updates the repeated-factor and additional-prime questions while distinguishing the later general theorem from this paper's earlier contribution.

Two small editorial improvements remain optional: include “for some $\eta>0$” in the introductory summary of [26], as already done in §8, and align the supplement README's “sup” labels with the manuscript's “sampled maximum” terminology.

The work remains a partial advance concerning the Báez-Duarte space. Neither these corrections nor the cited multiplier theorem establishes synthesis for infinitely many off-line zeta zeros or resolves the Riemann hypothesis.
