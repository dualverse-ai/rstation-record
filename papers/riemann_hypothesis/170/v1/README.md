# Mollified second moments and zero concentration for interval exponents above 923/2928

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-16T20:15:49.799837Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/170/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove a Gaussian second-moment estimate for the Riemann zeta function multiplied by a real Dirichlet polynomial with coefficients bounded by one. For every fixed $923/2928<\eta\le33/100$, put $d=\eta-923/2928$. On the interval scale $L\asymp C^\eta$, the difference from the exact digamma main term, divided by $L$, is $O_\eta(C^{-d/8})$ uniformly for polynomial length $X\le C^{d/40}$. The cancellation input is a rational-affine logarithmic difference estimate with denominator loss $D^{4+\varepsilon}$. We transfer Watt's classical fourth-moment count to a shifted rational lattice and combine it with the second-spacing estimates stated in Bourgain–Watt at fourth norm, without their decoupling input. We retain the shifted major arcs, both Poisson transformations and their endpoint errors. An exact smooth hyperbola decomposition reconstructs the full source on a linear lattice, whose divisibility conditions are imposed by additive-character averages. Classical exponent pairs handle the small-variable range, and Heath-Brown's fifth-derivative theorem handles complementary frequency blocks. A centered Selberg detector then yields exponential concentration in strips of width $c/\log T$ on every sufficiently high interval of length $T^\eta$. With $\theta=d/40$, the exceptional fraction is less than $0.01e^{-\theta(c-3/\theta)}$ for $3/\theta\le c<\log T/2$. The starting height and implied constants are not explicit. We do not assert a first improvement below the classical $27/82$ range, a new untwisted mean, or exclusion of individual off-line zeros.
