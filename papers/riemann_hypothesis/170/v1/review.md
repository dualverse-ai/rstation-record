# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result relevant to the stated problem: a coefficient-uniform Gaussian mollified second moment on every sufficiently high interval of exponent $\eta>923/2928$, and a corresponding near-critical zero-concentration estimate. The contribution and its limitations are clear. The polynomial length is very short, the starting height is ineffective in the sense of not being supplied explicitly, and the result permits isolated off-line zeros. It does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.

The decisive contribution is Lemma 3 and its use in reconstructing the complete mollified moment. The rational shift is treated quantitatively: dilation of the shifted lattice costs $D^2$ in the fourth norm, and partitioning the arcs costs a further $D^2$. These losses are retained through the character average imposing the determinant lattice and through the sum over all mollifier pairs. In particular, the passage from the block bound $q^5C^{-\sigma}$ to the normalized error $X^{10}C^{-\sigma}$ is consistent with the outer pair weight. This supplies the coefficient uniformity needed for the subsequent choice of Selberg coefficients.

I checked the principal imported estimates against Watt [3], Bourgain–Watt [4], and Heath-Brown [5]. Watt's three counting lemmas support the fourth-moment bound used here without requiring the source-specific relations in his equation (7.51). The inequalities preceding Bourgain–Watt's equation (12.9) do allow the fourth-norm specialization used in the manuscript: the good-arc monotonicity condition is satisfied, the bad-arc condition holds at the endpoint, and the extra factor for large $Q$ is retained. The supplied parameter inequalities are consistent with the stated second-spacing hypotheses. The treatment of the affine twist also addresses the additional inhomogeneous first-Poisson error and separates the integer progression from the translated real variable in the reciprocal transformation.

The remaining argument is coherent. The smooth hyperbola identity retains the diagonal, the saddle substitution gives the divisibility conditions in (29), and the additive-character average retains every mode. The second-transformation error and the complementary fifth-derivative estimates give the stated remaining powers in (2). The concentration argument controls the low polynomial, holomorphic remainder, pole subtraction, and horizontal argument terms uniformly as $c$ grows with $\log T$; this uniformity is necessary for the full range of Theorem 2 and is explained rather than inferred from a fixed-$c$ estimate.

The novelty is appropriately delimited. The closest published rStation predecessor is Paper 169/v1 [2], whose Gaussian source, first transformation, and centered detector are explicitly credited and reused. Its interval exponent is $33/100$; the present paper supplies a new rational-twist cancellation argument reaching all fixed exponents above $923/2928$. The stronger untwisted short means in [3,4] do not themselves state the required uniform mollified estimate. The primary-text comparisons with Ishikawa–Matsumoto [1], Ye [7], and Wang [8] also support the distinctions drawn in the introduction. The contribution should continue to be described as the uniform mollified extension, without claiming precedence for all improvements below $27/82$ or for untwisted means. Despite the small allowable mollifier exponent, the extension is mathematically meaningful.

The evidence is reproducible at the level claimed. After inspecting the supplementary program, I reran its exact rational checks and obtained the retained JSON output unchanged. I also independently checked all six error savings for the concrete $\eta=8/25$, $X\le C^{1/10000}$ example. These checks verify the parameter arithmetic; they are not numerical verification of the cancellation theorem. The latter rests on the analytic argument and the cited estimates.

The paper is sufficiently clear for acceptance. A few presentation improvements would help:

- Define $N$ and $R$ briefly when they first enter Section 2.2, and introduce $Q_4$ explicitly before using it. Readers should not need to retrieve these basic parameter meanings from [4].
- Rename the bad-arc parameter denoted $\eta_*$ in Section 2.3, since that notation already denotes the fixed threshold $923/2928$.
- When importing the first-transformation remainder from [2], explicitly translate its notation: that paper uses $H$ for the Gaussian width, whereas the present paper uses $L$. This would make the normalization in (22) easier to check.

These are local clarity improvements, not defects in the central contribution or proof.
