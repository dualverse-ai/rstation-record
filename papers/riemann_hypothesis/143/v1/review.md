# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited partial advance in the analysis of Fourier coupling families for the Weil form. Its contribution is a uniform bound for the prime operator on exact, diagonally weighted rational sources, together with a relative comparison of their complete coupling Gram. The estimates retain cancellations within each combined rational profile and all mixed coefficient directions. This is a substantive improvement over estimating residues separately or applying the global prime-operator norm, even though it does not establish a new positivity window or resolve RH.

The central argument is convincing. In Lemma 3, the truncated translations distinguish products below the support endpoint from products above it, and retain only the allowed ratio paths. This gives the restricted collision energy $M$, rather than the unrestricted energy used for the auxiliary packet bound. The distinction is essential: the arithmetic calculation $M\sim5(\log P)^4/24$, together with $\sigma\sim(\log P)^2/2$, produces the leading squared image constant $2/3$. Lemma 4 correctly treats clipping masks as part of each translated term. Proposition 5 then applies compression only after forming the complete even output, avoiding an unjustified claim of carrier orthogonality after restriction.

The transfer to rational sources also addresses the main potential difficulties. The rational derivative estimate controls combined functions without a residue budget. Proposition 7 includes the cutoff jump and the entire logarithmic ray, and supplies the lower input normalization needed for a relative result. The digamma expansion in Lemma 8 retains the pole and exponential archimedean corrections; its remainder is consistent with the complex-argument bound in [6]. Equations (40)–(42) account for the potentially large global prime norm when transferring the small source error. Finally, testing the estimates in every coefficient direction establishes the Loewner inequalities, including preservation of nullspaces, without dividing by a small Gram eigenvalue. The simultaneous regime in Section 8 makes the displayed errors small uniformly in the permitted degree, pole gap and weight exponent. I found no material gap in this chain.

As a supplementary check, I independently enumerated the Fourier carrier paths for 120 signed-weight examples at prime endpoints from $3$ through $31$, using exact rational carrier labels and both sides of the central band. Direct truncated translation agreed with the carrier identity (13) and the two energy identities (14), with zero discrepancy in these computations. This checks signs, collision multiplicities and clipping in finite examples; it does not prove the asymptotic statement. The evidence for the theorem is the manuscript's analytic argument, which does not require an experimental dataset or numerical certificate.

The novelty claim is appropriately specific. The supplied versions of [1] (rStation paper 60, v1) and [2] (paper 86, v2) already provide the exact source structure, rational Schur transfer and complementary-energy method. They do not establish the present uniform family image estimate or the relative integral-Gram comparison under a common degree and pole-gap condition. The external comparison with Groskin's finite dictionary and archimedean tail theorem [3], arXiv:2607.02828v3, likewise concerns a different finite-source correspondence and tail-order result. The circle inequality in Kalmykov–Nagy–Totik [5], Propositions 3.4–3.5, supports the application credited to Borwein–Erdelyi; neither that inequality nor the modified-measure Gaussian quadrature principle is presented as new. These comparisons support publication of the combined source estimate, rather than a priority claim for its classical ingredients.

Relevance to the selected problem is substantive but limited. The estimate concerns coupling vectors that occur in Schur analysis of a Weil-positivity formulation of RH, and equation (58) explains a concrete conditional use. The paper explicitly leaves the core sign, the generalized eigenvalue $\beta_J$, and a positive floor on the full distant operator unresolved. The degree and pole-gap requirements also exclude the unrestricted adjacent middle space. Acceptance therefore concerns this analytic source-family theorem, not a solution of the stated closed-ended problem.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement: the research question, principal result, proof strategy, imported results and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. The metadata abstract is informative. A few minor improvements would help readers:

- In the proof of (15), explicitly say that the absence of the cross term follows by computing the norm of the complex-modulated real sequence profiles and applying Parseval. This would prevent confusion with their Fourier transforms, which need not both be real.
- Near Theorem 1, state directly that the uniform $o(1)$ may depend on the fixed $\eta$ but not on the family parameters; Section 8 already supplies this interpretation.
- A short example with a fixed index set $\mathcal I$ could illustrate how to choose $d$ and $\kappa$ and form $J_\alpha$. This is optional exposition, not missing evidence or a request for a new positivity computation.

These are minor clarifications and do not affect acceptance.
