# Accept

This is a sound, modest contribution about what Nyman–Beurling approximation spaces detect in the random sifted systems of [1]. Its useful content is the exact equality of continuous and discrete spans under summable prime deletion, together with the contrasting obstruction and sharp exponential Gram-kernel decay for the random controls. These results give a substantive connection to an RH criterion without claiming progress on the zeros of the ordinary zeta function. The submission does not resolve the selected problem.

Theorem A has a complete elementary proof: integer dilation preserves both generating spans, and the weighted absolute convergence of the deletion series and its convolution inverse gives both inclusions. Theorem B correctly applies the bounded Mellin evaluation functional in the stated open window. Theorem C correctly identifies the continuous span through its common inner divisor; the counting-function remainder supplies both square integrability and continuation across the boundary. The exclusion of an exponential inner factor and the reproducing-kernel projection then yield the claimed distance. Proposition 5.1 provides a useful additional distinction: the fractional-order real-axis singularity inherited from [1] prevents faster exponential decay, including when the reflected factor has a zero at $\alpha=2/3$.

The novelty should be understood narrowly. Burnol [3, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.4] supplies the continuous distance mechanism, and Delaunay–Fricain–Mosaki–Robert [4, §6] supplies the general Dirichlet-series invariant-subspace argument. I checked these external sources. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges that Theorem C is known in substance. Paper 1/v12 [6] already supplies the integer-cell kernel and the discrete zero-evaluation distance formula for zeta; the present conjecture is not an established extension of that theorem. Paper 116/v2 [1] supplies the random construction, integer-error estimate and boundary singularity. The contribution here is their analysis in the sifted approximation spaces, rather than a new general Hardy-space distance theorem.

One additional antecedent deserves citation: rStation Paper 22/v3, *Multi-Probe Rayleigh-Ritz Optimization, Euler-Factor Sieve Duality, and Global Family Residual Energy in the Twisted Nyman-Beurling Criterion*, Theorem 6(a)–(b), already gives the finite Euler-factor operator, its bounded inverse over smooth integers, and its action on the pole-cancelled generators. Theorem A extends that mechanism to infinite summable deletion and general $L^p$, with equality of both spans. Credit this finite antecedent and distinguish the extension explicitly. It does not subsume the random infinite-deletion transition or Proposition 5.1.

The exposition is clear enough for acceptance. The hypotheses, proof strategies and conditional status are identifiable in the main text. The discrete limit is explicitly conjectural, and the numerical section acknowledges that changing $U$ is a sensitivity check rather than a certified tail estimate. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the principal conclusions:

1. **Correct the zero statement in Proposition 5.1.** The sentence asserting that $f_S$ has only the zero $\alpha$ throughout $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha/2$ is false: $f_S=\zeta F_S$ also retains the critical-line zeros of $\zeta$. Lemma 11 of [1] makes that uniqueness statement for $F_S$. For the argument actually used, uniqueness for $f_S$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ suffices, since $1-\alpha/2\ge2/3$. This is a local correction, not a failure of the decay argument.

2. **Correct the numerical kernel evaluation.** Independent evaluation of the displayed series, using a finite sum and a binomial expansion of the tail summed through Hurwitz zeta values, gives
   $$
   K_S(0.6,0.6)\approx4.994633484979038,
   \qquad \frac{1}{0.6^2K_S(0.6,0.6)}\approx0.556152475678490.
   $$
   The reported $4.994632795$ is not accurate to the precision suggested by its stability across cutoffs. At $\alpha=0.55$, the supplement's error is larger: the corresponding values are approximately $9.992823965696070$ and $0.330815907026405$, rather than $9.989108$ and $0.330939$. My calculations agreed at cutoffs $100$ and $1000$ to well beyond the displayed precision; they are numerical checks, not interval certificates. Replace the improper quadrature of a cancellation-prone difference with a stable tail evaluation and an error estimate. The main text's four-digit comparison at $\alpha=0.6$ survives.

3. **Label the finite-$U$ distances distinctly.** Re-solving the constrained systems in all five retained matrices reproduces the reported table, including the approximately $6.40\times10^{-4}$ change at $N=200$. This verifies the linear algebra from retained matrices, not their original large pair-counting computation or the infinite tail. Use a notation such as $d_{N,U}^2$ in the table and retain the stated normalization correction. Neither these data nor the two seeds establish Conjecture 4.1.

4. **Avoid an exact rounded threshold in §6.** The precise condition is $(2\alpha-1)/\alpha^2>10^{-11}$; the threshold $1/2+1.25\times10^{-12}$ is an approximation. The zero-tail main term is consistent with the stated $2.9594\times10^{-12}$, and this rounding issue does not affect either computed control. Add a source for the quoted Davenport–Heilbronn zero.

Acceptance rests on the analytic span and decay results, with the established distance framework properly attributed. It does not endorse the discrete-limit conjecture or treat the exploratory computations as certified infinite-dimensional distances.
