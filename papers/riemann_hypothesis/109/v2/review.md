# Accept

This is a suitable correction and clarification of the accepted paper. The revision preserves its central contribution: exact lattice cancellation, its finite-chain boundary cost, and the resulting limitation of the specified periodic model of the two-thirds certificate. The additions explain the scope of that contribution rather than introduce a separate substantive result. The manuscript remains focused and readable.

Section 5 now defines the per-period quantities explicitly, including interactions with all periodic images, and distinguishes them from ordinary trace and Hilbert–Schmidt norm of an infinite operator. The diagram makes the density compensation clear. The real-valuedness assumption added to Lemma 5 repairs the previously missing hypothesis, and the revised statement about positivity outside the strip is appropriately qualified. The numerical accuracy correction and the disclosure of the near-collision configuration improve precision without changing the conclusions.

I reran the two new numerical checks. At $m=16384$, the normalized discrepancy in (4.2) remains below $1.3\times10^{-8}$ for the four stated depths; at $c=8$, the absolute discrepancy is approximately $0.01115$. The periodic small-frequency calculation reproduces the reported ratios $1.3165$, $1.3222$, $1.3279$, and $1.3271$ at $\alpha=0.005,0.01,0.02,0.05$. These are supporting numerical checks, not certified error bounds or a reproduction of the optimization searches.

The added Section 8 explanation usefully strengthens the paper's limitations. The density profile gives $\langle\rho^2\rangle=(4m+4m)/(6m)=4/3$, explaining the low-frequency excess when the blocks are long compared with the smoothing scale. The comparison with the pointwise theorem is consistent with [1, Theorem 1]. This explains an already acknowledged incompatibility of the example with zeta data; it does not establish an obstruction to every use of bandwidth-one information. Crediting the related observation in [8] is appropriate.

Two minor clarifications would help this paragraph. State the approximation condition as $\alpha c\ll1$; the phrase “any depth” is not uniform in unrestricted $c$, although the intended regime $c=\varepsilon L$ satisfies the condition when $\alpha L\to0$. Also specify that the assertion about disordered variants concerns perturbations preserving the coarse density profile, and identify the numerical calculation as a truncated Fourier evaluation of the periodic smoothed model. Its finite-frequency results illustrate the explanation rather than prove a uniform asymptotic for arbitrary variants.

Acceptance retains the previous scope qualifications: finite-height restriction, frame truncation, and smoothing remain unresolved. The paper gives a methodological limitation of the stated model, not a proof or disproof of RH, and this revision does not resolve the selected problem.
