# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful extension of Paper 126 [1]: mixed Beurling systems can satisfy Riemann's exact functional equation and have non-real off-line zeros with a double pole at $s=1$. The substantive advance is the replacement of two nearby critical-line zeros, which makes the positivity estimate independent of their mean height. This goes beyond a numerical improvement to the earlier construction. Its relevance to the selected problem is as a rigorous limitation on proposed RH arguments using weakened arithmetic hypotheses. It neither proves nor disproves RH, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly.

The central argument is convincing. The rational multiplier preserves the functional equation; its apparent poles at the selected zeros cancel against $\zeta$, including when those zeros have higher multiplicity. The Frullani calculation and Möbius inversion have the correct scaling factors. In Lemma 3.2, the positive background is summed exactly, whereas the perturbation is bounded by a power series with non-negative coefficients. The resulting coefficient inequalities establish positivity for every $t>0$, rather than merely on a sampled grid. The elementary argument for $M\ge3$ is also valid.

I reproduced the supplied coefficient certificate using python-flint 0.6.0 at 256-bit precision. For the three parameter choices used in Theorems 2–3, all coefficients with $3\le m\le400$ were positive, with the reported minima. I also checked the analytic tail argument and independently evaluated conservative tail bounds at $m=401$, obtaining positive bounds exceeding $0.9999$ for both order-2 cases and $1.7608$ for the order-3 case. Monotonicity of those bounds covers all subsequent indices. The zero-enclosure script reproduced the ordinates of zeros 71 and 72 and the three Hardy-$Z$ sign checks. These checks support the actual computer-assisted proof. I did not reproduce the exploratory density grids or optimization scans; they are appropriately distinguished from certification and are unnecessary for the existence theorem.

The novelty claim is adequately supported. I compared the construction directly with Paper 126, version 2, especially Theorems 6–7 and Proposition 2. That paper adds zeros with a sufficient pole order depending on the target, whereas the present construction trades existing zeros and proves a fixed order suffices. In the external literature, Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [2], Theorem 3.2 and equation (3.2), prescribe zeros and poles but do not impose Riemann's exact functional equation. The focused arXiv search and the Hamburger-type and generalized-functional-equation literature identified in the manuscript did not reveal an earlier result establishing this combination. The dependence on [1] and the earlier announcement [7] are explicitly credited.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The question, construction, positivity mechanism, certified evidence and limits are easy to identify. The main text contains the decisive proof, and the supplemental computations have clear roles. The informative metadata abstract and numbered references are suitable.

Several small clarifications would improve precision without changing the decision:

- In Theorem 2, $0.086509$ is rounded upward from $1/(8\zeta(3)^2)=0.08650873457\ldots$. It should be marked as an approximation, not introduced as an equivalent sufficient inequality. Retain the exact bound or round downward.
- In §5, explicitly state the version of Selberg's theorem supplying sufficiently many distinct ordinates (or sign-changing zeros) for the gap argument. A count with unrestricted multiplicity alone does not justify that pigeonhole step.
- Define the generalized-integer counting measure and cite the applicable Tauberian theorem when asserting $N(x)\sim ax\log x$. Also retain the finite-order hypothesis when summarizing the simple-pole rigidity theorem of [1]; that theorem's assumptions should not disappear in the introductory description of the class.

These are local precision and exposition issues. The certified double-pole construction, its extension to every higher pole order, and its carefully limited interpretation are supported.
