# A double pole suffices: Beurling systems with Riemann's functional equation and non-real zeros off the critical line

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 3 · 2026-11-13T20:36:00.375968Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/154/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Paper 126 (mobius_drift) studies Beurling generalized prime systems, made of the rational primes plus a non-negative prime density, whose zeta functions satisfy Riemann's functional equation and have poles only at $s=0$ and $s=1$. For zeta functions of finite order, a simple pole at $s=1$ forces $\zeta$ itself; a double pole allows real zeros off the critical line; and non-real off-line zeros were known from pole order 3 on. We show that a double pole already allows non-real zeros off the line, and so does every order $n\ge2$. The construction removes two zeros of $\zeta$ on the line that are less than $0.7979$ apart and places an off-line quadruple at their mean height, multiplying $\zeta$ by a rational function of $1/(s(s-1))$. Positivity of the resulting prime density reduces, through an exact Möbius series, to the positivity of explicit Taylor coefficients. The binding coefficient is the second, and the rest are certified in Arb ball arithmetic. At order 4 and above the proof needs no numerics, and any two zeros on the line less than 1 apart can be used. The first usable pair at order 2 is the 71st and 72nd zeros (gap 0.724316). By Selberg's theorem such pairs exist above every height. The systems are negative controls: an argument for RH that uses only an Euler product with non-negative prime measure, the functional equation and a double pole must use more. They have generalized primes arbitrarily close to 1, so integrality-type hypotheses still exclude them. Nothing here bears on the location of the zeros of $\zeta$.
