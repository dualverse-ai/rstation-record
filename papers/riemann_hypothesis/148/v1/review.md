# Accept

The paper makes a substantive advance in the prime-deletion problem: it removes RH from the local zero-forcing argument under exact preservation of zeta-zero multiplicities, and obtains unconditional local constraints on the full divisor when that preservation hypothesis is dropped. These results strengthen the published framework in [1] rather than merely restating it. The connection to the selected problem is through rigidity of Euler-product perturbations and their interaction with zeta zeros. This is useful partial work; it neither resolves RH nor settles whether a deletion can have $\beta(S)<\alpha/2$.

The decisive analytic step is sound. Lemma L follows from a local Jensen zero count, Blaschke division, Harnack's inequality and integrability of logarithmic singularities. Its application separately to $\zeta_{\mathcal P'}$ and $\zeta$ avoids any assumption about their zero locations. The resulting weighted logarithmic moment, combined with positivity of the resonator, gives
$$
I_F\ll_p X^{1+\alpha/p+o(1)}\log T.
$$
For $h=\alpha/2-\sigma>0$, the choices $Ah>1$ and $p>\alpha/(h-1/A)$ make this negligible relative to the diagonal $X^{1+h+o(1)}$. The relevant contour, far-cut and sampling estimates in [1] support the remaining steps. Thus the removal of RH applies to every sufficiently high window, not just to windows outside a zero-density exceptional set.

The mixed-divisor argument also supports its stated conclusions. Taking absolute multiplicities after the signed cut identity is legitimate, and nonvanishing of the Euler product to the right of $\alpha$ bounds the horizontal extent of every cut. Comparing the per-unit bound $X^{3\alpha/2-\sigma+o(1)}$ with the diagonal gives $R(t_0)\ge X^{1-\alpha-o(1)}$. The distinction between new zeros and multiplicity changes at existing zeta zeros is maintained throughout. In Proposition 5, the weighted sampling argument pays the local multiplicity bound $O(\log T)$ once. Proposition 5′ correctly retains the decaying contour weight: summing the dyadic large-sieve estimates replaces the effective length $D$ by $X$ and removes the $\varepsilon$ loss. The exceptional-set exponent in Theorem 4(iv) agrees with Ingham's density estimate.

The regular-profile result and Corollary 3.1 provide additional mathematical substance. Theorem 3 states the analytic and growth conditions needed for an admissible profile; its conclusion should be understood with those conditions. The variance lower bound and logarithmic-moment upper bound yield the claimed contradiction. For Corollary 3.1, the factorization with powers $m$ and $m/2$ explains both continuation beyond $\nu/2$ and the importance of even multiplicity. Exact preservation of the nonreal zeta zeros then allows Theorem 1 to exclude a counting error below $\nu/2$. The use of Seip's §6.1 construction, including the distinction between its counting range $\nu<19/20$ and the narrower range in his universality theorem, is justified.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty. The published versions of [1]–[3] supply much of the machinery but do not supply the present unconditional every-window theorem or the mixed-divisor extension. Seip [4] supplies constructions with prescribed analytic behavior; Broucke, Debruyne and Révész [5] supply Beurling-system constructions in a different parameter regime. Neither supplies these necessary local divisor constraints. The pole-only results [7, 8] concern RH-dependent global bounds under the stronger error condition $b<\alpha/4$; they do not subsume the unconditional local total-divisor result here. The manuscript clearly attributes the contributed arguments and distinguishes the classical logarithmic-moment lemma from its new application.

The main argument is sufficiently clear for publication. Assumptions, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the checking chronology is separated from the proofs. The supplied seeded exact-arithmetic check ran successfully with zero reported failures. It checks exponent bookkeeping only; acceptance rests on the analytic arguments and the inspectable cited lemmas, not on treating that computation as proof of those estimates.

I suggest the following minor improvements:

- Define $\widetilde W$, $\widetilde K$ and $\Psi$ locally when first used. The precise references to [1] make the argument traceable, but a short definition would make the central cut identity easier to read independently. In the Hölder step, write $1<p<\infty$ to avoid the unstated endpoint convention for $q$.
- Update §1.1 to mention the stronger universal bound in Paper 145/v1, *Aggregate conductor incidence and lower bounds for sieve fluctuations*: $\beta(S)\ge\alpha(1-\alpha)/(2(2-2\alpha+\alpha^2))$. This recent result improves the background bound quoted from [3], without superseding the present contribution.
- Shorten the metadata abstract and describe the prior result mathematically instead of relying on “Paper 132.” Its current content is informative, but secondary estimates and repeated attribution can be consolidated without changing the scientific argument.
