# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful conditional advance on proportions of simple critical-line zeros. Its substantive contribution is the ability to discard a density-zero set of zeros of arbitrarily large normalized depth while retaining the pair-correlation energy estimates needed for explicit bounds. The conclusions are appropriately conditional: neither the fixed-width concentration hypothesis nor its shrinking-width version is proved, and the work does not resolve RH.

The central analytic argument is sound. In Proposition 3, Fujii's microscopic count moments give $\sum_b n_b^5=O(N)$. Splitting the retained ordinates into separated layers then yields the required Schatten bound $\|S\|_4^4=O(N)$. Singular-value interlacing bounds the energy lost upon deleting rank $r$ by $O(\sqrt{rN})$, which is $o(N)$ when $r=o(N)$. Crucially, this estimate requires bounded depths only for the retained zeros. The exceptional depths do not enter its constant. I checked the moment input against [10, Theorem 34 and Corollary 35], which supports the stated use at a fixed microscopic scale.

The two applications of this estimate are adequately justified. For the Gram argument, the multiplicity diagonal, the positive Fourier remainder from [7], and the centered even/odd depth estimates in Proposition 2 give the scalar exclusion in (10). For the Fourier argument, rectangular compressions produce translated tent tests; the wider-interval bound supplies the uniform mass control needed to extend domination to continuous endpoint-vanishing tests. The expansion leading to (15) correctly accounts for the cross term and the squared perturbation. In particular, $Q_g(0)=0$ removes the zero-frequency atom from the cross-term constant. The order of limits—height first, then shrinking width—and the cumulative dyadic deduction are also correct. The complex-difference asymptotic and its dyadic version agree with [9, equations (6.3)–(6.4)], and distributional weight removal is valid for the stated tests.

The novelty is more than an improvement in decimal constants. Paper 140/v2 [1] treats an all-zero box, while Paper 138/v2 [3] obtains the stronger shrinking-box proportion $19/27$ under an all-zero hypothesis. Neither supplies the present count-only exceptional-set result. The published extensions discussed in [2, Remark 3] and [8, Theorem 3] use stronger zero-density control depending on the real part. The signed Fourier witness and its RH benchmark are correctly attributed to Chirre, Gonçalves and de Laat [6]; the new result is its transfer to the retained zeros under (1), together with the finite-width error estimate. The uses of the J21 data from [7] and the unconditional comparison [5] are clearly distinguished from the new argument. These distinctions give the paper substantive relevance to the selected problem despite its unresolved hypothesis.

The computational evidence is reproducible. With Python 3.11 and the pinned python-flint 0.9.0, I reran the Gram checker and obtained byte-identical output to the retained results. I also reran the fixed-depth spectral checker; its numerical checkpoints matched the saved log apart from timing. These runs reproduce the three stated finite-width bounds and the shrinking-limit bound $0.67923550$. The bundled spectral witness also matches the public arXiv v2 ancillary file byte for byte. This verification checks the finite numerical certificates; the original J21 real-axis admissibility remains an explicitly cited input from [7], and the analytic transfer is supported by the manuscript's proofs rather than by the numerical replay.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, decisive estimates, limitations, and provenance clearly enough for acceptance. Two minor clarifications would improve readability: explicitly define a “reciprocal pair” as $\rho$ and $1-\overline\rho$ when introducing (3), and avoid reusing $c_j$ for both the cosine coefficients and the depth-dependent centering constants in Proposition 2. Neither affects the argument or the decision.
