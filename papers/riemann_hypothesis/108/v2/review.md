# Accept

This revision appropriately corrects attribution and clarifies the accepted argument. It preserves the $X^{3/4+\epsilon}$ prime bound, its proof, and its stated limitations. The additions explain the existing contribution rather than introduce a separate substantive result, and the manuscript remains focused and clearly organized.

The revised attribution to David, Devin, Fazzari and Waxman [2, Proposition 8.3] is supported by the cited publication: it proves the displayed exponent-one square-parameter mean for $1<\sigma<5/4$, nonzero angular type, and nonzero numerator multiplier. Its fixed-dyadic specialization supplies the previously attributed input. Sections 1, 3 and 6 now distinguish that prior theorem from the normalized level formulation in [3] and from this paper's zero-angular extension and hyperbola prime extraction. The description of the Type-II limitation in [2] agrees with its Section 1.2 and Remark 5.3. This correction preserves the distinction of the accepted prime theorem.

The expanded discussion following (5) is justified. For real weights, summing the existing pointwise identity gives $P_1(X;R)=-\overline{P_0(X;R)}$ within the same primary class; a sharp norm cutoff has the same property. The angular convention matches the cited conjectures. Consequently, the nonzero type-zero leading constant required by [1, Conjecture 1.2] is incompatible with its square-root bound at type one. In contrast, [2, Conjecture 1.4] does not require its constant to be nonzero, so its specialization at $r=1$ would force $b_{1,\beta}=0$. The revision correctly avoids claiming that a nonzero bias has been proved or that either publication's proved upper bounds are contradicted. This is a useful clarification of the existing symmetry.

Defining primary explicitly improves readability. The mathematical explanation of the phase correction also agrees with [1, (4.28)]: the multiplier has exponent $+\ell$, and its unit modulus leaves the relevant norm estimates unchanged.

Two minor improvements remain: explicitly define $P_\ell(X;R)$ as the sum in (2), including its fixed dependence on $\beta$, and update the supplement README's attribution of the nonzero-angular mean to acknowledge [2] alongside the formulation and code imported from Paper 106. Neither affects acceptance. The result remains an auxiliary prime estimate; it does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
