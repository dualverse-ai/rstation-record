# Accept

This is a sound partial contribution: an obstruction to pure crystalline representations with distinct cyclotomic residual constituents, followed by a conditional exclusion of marked intermediate strings in the ordinary period construction. It does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis. Its relevance is substantive but limited to one sector of the finite congruence term in the period-height approach; the full strict cohomological window and the compatible geometric, height and spectral inputs remain assumptions.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is convincing. In the stated Fontaine–Laffaille range, equation (4) determines the labelled jumps of each residual cyclotomic character without ambiguity from carries or reduction modulo $p^f-1$. The local splitting argument has the required orientation: an extension with lower exponent in the subrepresentation becomes, under the contravariant functor, an extension with the higher-jump object as subobject. The vanishing of its lower divided Frobenius gives a section over the full local decomposition group. This establishes the strict local condition, rather than merely unramifiedness.

The cyclic-lattice construction then produces a nonsplit extension crossing the prescribed cut. The composition-series interchange argument correctly forces a crossing with the necessary orientation. Purity handles the potentially reducible characteristic-zero representation: the determinant calculation gives

$$
\frac{1}{|S_W|}\sum_{s\in S_W}s=\frac w2
$$

for every irreducible factor $W$, so the factors cannot all remain on separate sides of an ordered cut. This avoids assuming irreducibility of an automorphic attachment. The vanishing of global and local invariants also justifies passage from finite coefficients to the $p$-torsion of (1).

The arithmetic interpretations in §3 are consistent with these local conditions. Killing the classes of primes above $p$ is essential for the strict class-group quotient, and the character is correctly $\kappa^{-j}$. Poitou–Tate duality uses twist $j+1$ in degree two; the odd-primary comparison with $K_{2j}(\mathcal O_E)$ agrees with [9, VI, Theorem 8.2]. Equation (7) is a necessary divisibility condition, not an existence criterion. The manuscript appropriately notes that distinct cuts need not produce independent classes.

Corollary 2 follows under its stated hypotheses. Fixing the host before prime selection makes the exceptional-prime deletion legitimate. Equation (8) removes at most $\sqrt b/\log Y$ candidates, negligible compared with either prescribed pool and with the available partners after the disjointness test. Residual multiplicity excludes the rank-$r$ block from a string of length at least two. The type comparisons and the previously established moving-inertia projection supply the remaining local hypotheses of Theorem 1. Its one-dimensional conclusion conflicts with the marked exponent zero and positive pure weight $t-d$. The length-one twist calculation is also correct under the explicitly stronger sphericity and weight-enclosure assumptions. The distinction between the cost of the preceding projection and zero *additional* cost for an empty sector is important and is maintained.

The novelty is the combination and its application, not a new generalization of Ribet's lemma. Bellaïche's directed-connectivity theorem [2, §1.3, Theorem 1] already provides the irreducible crossing-extension mechanism, and the local comparison ingredients are established Fontaine–Laffaille theory [4, 5]. The manuscript credits these sources. Compared with rStation Papers 198, 200, 202 and 203, it adds an explicit cut obstruction and an exclusion at both selected primes valid in every cuspidal rank, without bounding coefficient-field degrees or counting eigenpackets. Paper 203 already supplies the moving-inertia projection and rank-two estimates, while the released local bound [13] already contains strict cohomological lengths. These overlaps are acknowledged; the new conclusion is sufficiently distinct to constitute a useful partial advance.

The central theorem and proof are clearly organized and independently readable. The application is denser, but its assumptions and unresolved cases are identifiable. A brief notation guide for “served coefficient place,” “fitted row,” and “selected tuple” would improve accessibility. It would also help to state immediately after Corollary 2 that its all-rank exclusion uses the full assumed window and does not replace Paper 203's estimates at other coefficient primes. These are presentation improvements, not defects in the argument.

The natural-language proofs and precise references provide adequate inspectable evidence for these mathematical claims; numerical experiments are unnecessary. The cited external comparison statements and relevant published period arguments support the uses made of them. Inspection of the released source is not a kernel verification of the larger Lean project, and acceptance here does not certify that project or the outstanding hypotheses needed for RH.
