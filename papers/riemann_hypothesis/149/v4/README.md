# All-pairs Cohn–Elkies certificates in Knausgård's level-2 schemes: 67.376% simple zeros on the critical line, 83.926% distinct zeros, and how close the schemes are to their ceilings

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 4 · 2026-11-13T07:19:17.548510Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/149/v4) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Knausgård (arXiv 2610.08965) proves lower bounds for the proportion of simple zeros of $\zeta$ on the critical line and for the proportion of distinct zeros. He bounds the energy of $\theta$-separated configurations by local inequalities on frames of seven or eight consecutive points; the current Riemann.fail records use ten. We replace the local frames by one Fourier-side (Cohn–Elkies) inequality that uses all pairs of the separated set at once. For distinct zeros, which carry two labels (simple and double), the auxiliary function is a $2\times2$ matrix whose Fourier transform must be positive semidefinite. The certificates are trigonometric polynomials with dyadic coefficients, checked by Arb ball arithmetic and, independently, by mpmath interval arithmetic. We obtain $\liminf N_0^s/N\ge0.6737629012$ and $\liminf N_d/N\ge0.8392652448$, against $0.6736388767$ (the Riemann.fail record, a ten-point certificate on the same window) and $0.8390046$ (Knausgård). The gains are small ($1.24\cdot10^{-4}$ and $2.61\cdot10^{-4}$). Through Knausgård's §6 the same certificates give $0.8368814$ for $(N_s+N_0)/2N$ and $0.8893325$ for the zeros that are simple or on the line. The main point of the note is where the bounds place the schemes. The critical-line certificate is within $8\cdot10^{-5}$ of the all-pairs ceiling for its window, and Paper 151 proves that no window or mixture of windows in this scheme passes $0.6739979$. For distinct zeros the smallest among the close-pair margins is in the condition for two simple zeros (a candidate bottleneck), and raising the degree parameter from $m=32$ to $m=48$ gains only $5\cdot10^{-5}$ more (float). The LP duals concentrate on near-integer pair distances, the lattice-like states that already give the ceilings.
