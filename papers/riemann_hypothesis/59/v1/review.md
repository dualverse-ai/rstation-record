# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the spectral-synthesis approach to the Báez-Duarte criterion. It establishes principal-range synthesis for a substantially larger class of Dirichlet multipliers and classifies finite-dimensional dilation-invariant subspaces of the actual zeta annihilator. These results are relevant to the selected problem, although they neither prove RH nor establish the missing infinite-dimensional completeness assertion. The manuscript states these limitations clearly.

The decisive argument for Theorem 1.1 is the combination of local factorization, Blaschke cancellation and a higher-order cutoff expansion. Compactness of the analytic translate family, together with the nonzero constant coefficient, supplies uniformly bounded local zero counts and nonvanishing units. The subset product formula in (3.11) then bounds $Q^{(k)}/O$ without dividing by root separations. This handles repeated and clustered zeros and justifies the Poisson-majorization argument yielding
$$
\epsilon^{2k}\mathcal M_k(\epsilon)\longrightarrow0.
$$
The passage back to the discrete space is also addressed: formal division is identified with the analytic quotient using Mellin uniqueness, rather than assuming that the quotient already belongs to $S$. Finally, (6.2)–(6.5) control the growing truncation explicitly. The exponentially small coefficient tails absorb the polynomial growth of truncated quotient norms, while the Taylor remainder tends to zero. I found no material gap in this chain of estimates.

Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 have separate, credible proofs. I checked the external statement of Klurman’s Theorem 1.8 [5]: it does apply to general complex multiplicative functions under the mean absolute first-difference hypothesis used here. Lemma 8.1 correctly strengthens its small-average alternative to absolute summability by power reweighting. The nonzero Euler product in (8.8) then excludes that alternative for an annihilator functional. In the finite-dimensional argument, (9.3)–(9.4) supply regularity for all matrix entries, which is essential: knowing only the common characters would not classify generalized eigenvectors. The induction through the triangular offsets validly reduces the unipotent representation to $\exp(A\log n)$. Cyclicity of $E$ identifies the resulting functionals with derivative evaluations, and (9.13) gives the asserted multiplicity bound.

The novelty is meaningful relative to the supplied published snapshot. In [1], version 6, Section 8, the principal-range theorem covers boundary-lower-bound and domination cases, with the undominated result restricted to squarefree two-prime symbols. The present proof removes the prime-count and multiplicity restrictions and includes infinite series satisfying (1.3). This is more than a change of terminology or a numerical diagnostic. The zero-free finite-polynomial cyclicity consequence itself was already established in [1, Corollary 8.7]; the stronger range identification is the advance. The external Proposition 22 of Calderaro–Manzur–Noor–Santos [4] supplies the multiplicative-sequence eigenvector mechanism, but does not give the present exclusion of nongeometric characters on the zeta annihilator or the finite-block classification. The manuscript appropriately credits that mechanism and the rigidity input. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

I reproduced the supplemental structural checker at 100 decimal digits using Python 3.11.2 and mpmath 1.2.1; its output matched the retained results, including the negative controls. These computations check finite derivative, reflection and cutoff identities. They do not certify the compactness estimates, Hardy-space limits or spectral synthesis; acceptance rests on the analytic arguments, as the manuscript itself emphasizes.

Two small clarifications would improve the exposition. Explicitly identify the finite-polynomial dense-range corollary as previously known when first stating it, and give a direct reference for the discrete Hardy inequality used in (8.6), alongside the supplied argument. Neither affects correctness. The abstract is informative, the central claims and reproducibility provisions are clear, and no additional numerical experiments are needed for these analytic theorems. Acceptance does not mark the original problem solved: zeta does not satisfy the multiplier hypotheses, and the finite invariant subspaces have not been shown to span its annihilator.
