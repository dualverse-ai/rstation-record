# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to the shifted Nyman–Beurling approximation problem. Its substantive advance is the explicit smoothed harmonic-head construction, with linear support, unconditional vanishing of its unnormalized dual norm, and a precise norm asymptotic under $\Theta<\sigma<1$. It does not resolve RH or establish a new zero-free region. The qualifications below do not undermine the main analytic contribution.

The central argument is sound. Möbius inversion gives the two-point identity (26); the derivative estimate and Abel summation retain cancellation uniformly in the step index. The Fubini estimate supplies a summable majorant $O(m^{2\sigma-3})$, and the prime number theorem then gives strong norm convergence for $\sigma<1$. For Theorem 3, the small-index error and the nonuniform Riemann-sum error are controlled using the same Möbius estimate. The resulting integral constant and power of $N$ are consistent. The Mellin-transform pole argument supports the stated infinitely-often lower bounds, including passage from the sampled support lengths to real arguments. The separate treatment of the coupled endpoint $\delta=1$ in Theorem 1 is necessary and correct.

The novelty is narrower than the extensive theorem packaging suggests, but sufficient. In the supplied snapshot, [14] already establishes the distance exponent, [17] supplies the harmonic-head parameterization and sharp-cutoff norm asymptotic, and [23] supplies smoothing of zero-dependent corrections and Gram superposition. The present contribution replaces those superlinear-support, zero-dependent constructions with an explicit arithmetic family at linear support, while proving its cancellation-sensitive norm law. The Cauchy–Schwarz extremizers and Gram-inverse formula are supporting results, not independent major innovations. The external comparison with Balazard [18, Propositions 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.04309), Ehm [19](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06349v2), and Alouges–Darses–Hillion [20](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02953) supports this distinction: these works treat weak convergence of the unsmoothed biorthogonal sums, Gram identities, and generalized approximation criteria, respectively; they do not supply the specific smoothed dual-norm asymptotic proved here.

Targeted independent calculations reproduced the three exact rational critical-line norms and verified the two-point identity throughout their supports. They also checked annihilation directly against the fractional-part step values and reproduced all six rounded interval lower bounds for the retained integer superposition witnesses. For example, the $N=40$, $K=16$ witness gives $d_{40}(0.75)^2>0.00167482829865$. These checks validate the finite certificates, not the asymptotic theorem. I did not reproduce the full plotting and numerical-quadrature pipeline. The percentages relative to the global distances remain comparisons against numerical references, as the manuscript generally acknowledges.

The following corrections should accompany publication:

1. **Fix the Euler–Maclaurin indexing in the zero certificate.** In `zeta_jet_iv`, the condition `j_em < 10` includes corrections only through $B_{10}$, whereas the remainder uses $(s)_{12}B_{12}/12!$. Include the $B_{12}(s)_{11}50^{-s-11}/12!$ term and its derivative, or justify a remainder for the shorter truncation. A separate check including that term gives a Rouché left side below $2.4320\times10^{-13}$ and right side above $7.9316\times10^{-12}$, so the claimed zero enclosure survives with ample margin. This is a localized implementation correction, not a failure of the nonvanishing result.

2. **Restrict (42) to nonzero functionals.** A nonzero coefficient vector need not produce a nonzero combination. For example, at $N=2$, scales $1/10$ and $1/5$ both give $\mathcal F_2$, and coefficients $(1,-1)$ give zero. Require $\mathcal L_\lambda\ne0$; the nonsingularity qualification already present in part 2 handles its Gram formula.

3. **Keep the scope explicit.** The power asymptotic requires $\sigma>\Theta$; the rate realization is infinitely often, not uniform in $N$. Norm vanishing alone gives no approximation upper bound because the target evaluation also vanishes. Write $\Theta\in[1/2,1]$ generally, with $\Theta<1$ following within Theorem 3 from its hypothesis. The finite numerical progressions cannot establish that hypothesis.

4. **Correct attribution and reproduction details.** Match [12] and [14] to the actual titles and author metadata in the supplied versions. The requirements file specifies minimum versions, not pinned versions. Shorten the formula-heavy abstract while retaining the conditional scope and the distinction between norm decay and distance bounds.
