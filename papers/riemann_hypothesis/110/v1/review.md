# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result on quartic character families: a fourth-trace estimate that yields a mixed polynomial moment for common divisor-bounded coefficients, followed by a natural-family zero-density improvement. The argument supports the stated scope. In particular, the pointwise coefficient restriction in Theorem 2 is essential, the density assertion has a sufficiently small height power depending on the requested loss, and none of these results resolves the Riemann hypothesis or gives an individual zero-free region.

The decisive arithmetic step is the coupled matrix construction in (19)–(21). Keeping the sharp part inside the radical leaves squarefree columns of length $B/(RS)$ and only $O(R)$ sharp labels. The common-row factorization permits Schatten Hölder before the complete coprimality projector is applied. The gcd embeddings retain divisor multiplicity rather than treating signed gcd sectors as positive matrices. Together with the inverse-pair input [2], these steps justify the finite transfer in (27). The prime-pool argument supplies the additional inverse-pool-size factor in (29): its column projection lower bound and bounded overlap of dilated ordinary rows are compatible with the character zeros. Balancing at $Y=L^{16/15}$ gives the claimed $L^{26/5+\epsilon}$ fourth trace.

The passage to ordinary coefficients also addresses the relevant obstructions. Identity (37) retains the imprimitive Gauss amplitudes, principal fourth powers, and primes shared by the primitive and fourth-power parts. Equations (40)–(45) provide summable restoration weights. In the first Poisson application, retaining the physical frequency powers gives the convergent original-gcd sums in (49); this is necessary for the unrestricted mixed moment, not merely a refinement of its bookkeeping. Substitution of $H=N^4$, $U=N$, and $V=N^2$ into (46) gives the largest exponent $147/20$, as asserted.

The zero-density conclusion is supported by an actual common-coefficient witness rather than a row-dependent choice of coefficients. Section 10 explains the buffered scalar construction, the common height selection, and the truncated inverse identity. The short moments, second-trace sixth moment, and improved sixth moment then cover the required inverse lengths. The endpoint equality at $\rho=11/15$ in (66) is consistent with the final $\epsilon$ loss: the cover is strict for every fixed excess above that exponent. The use of the earlier ordinary fourth moment [6] and scalar detector [8] is explicit.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. The published cubic Schatten paper [3] supplies the matrix strategy, but does not contain the quartic radical/sharp decomposition, prime-pool trace gain, or the resulting mixed moment. The quartic inverse-pair paper [2] supplies a second-trace input; the conductor-family paper [8] and natural fourth-moment paper [6] have different moment or coefficient scopes. I also checked de Faveri's Theorem 1.1 and root-number Lemma 3.3 in arXiv:2610.04045v1 [1], and the mollified-moment results of Castillo, de Faveri and Dunn in arXiv:2604.01316v2 [7]. These support the cited inputs and distinctions and do not subsume the present result. The improvement from $3/4$ to $11/15$ at real part $3/4$ is modest but meaningful; it is not merely a repackaging of a sieve bound. Its relevance to the supplied problem is through improved family zero counts, with the limitation concerning individual zeta zeros stated correctly.

I reran all six supplementary controls successfully. They reproduce the finite coupled-root, imprimitive-tower, shared-support and prime-pool checks, as well as the rational trace and density calculations. These computations corroborate local identities and exponent arithmetic; they do not certify asymptotic estimates. The analytic justification remains the manuscript's proof and its identified inputs.

The exposition is technically dense but organized around the argument, with the central constructions and evidence in the main text and repetitive checks in the supplement. The abstract is informative, notation is generally consistent, and attribution is adequate. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Define $\mathcal M(H,U,V)$ explicitly immediately before (46), and specify the fixed divisor-bound convention and the corresponding dependence of implied constants.
- The coupled-matrix control currently tests the $S_3$–$S_6$ Hölder inequality. Aligning that ancillary check with the $S_4$–$S_8$ inequality used here would make its relationship to (10) clearer. The exact matrix-factorization check and the standard analytic inequality already supply the relevant justification.
