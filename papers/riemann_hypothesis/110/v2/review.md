# Accept

This is a focused clarification of the accepted paper, addressing both suggestions in the previous review. The definition of $\mathcal M(H,U,V)$ before (46) explicitly identifies the ordinary mixed moment already asserted in Theorem 3. The fixed ideal divisor-function bound specifies the coefficient class and the dependence of implied constants without strengthening the estimates or changing their exponents. Retaining ordinary odd primary indices and literal character zeros is consistent with the surrounding common-divisor and powerful-part restoration arguments.

The supplementary matrix control now evaluates the $S_4$–$S_8$ Hölder inequality used in (10). I inspected the modified program and its arithmetic helper and reran the control successfully. It gives $\|V_1^*CV_2\|_4\approx34.1704507008$ and $\|V_1\|_8\|V_2\|_8\approx242.3144032791$, with the diagonal weight matrix satisfying $\|C\|=1$. The retained coupled-character, matrix-assembly and gcd checks also pass, agreeing with the submitted results to floating precision. This is an appropriate ancillary consistency check; it does not establish the asymptotic trace or density estimates, and the manuscript continues to distinguish those roles accurately.

The resulting manuscript retains its organization around the fourth-trace argument, ordinary-column restoration, mixed moment and density application. The additions improve specification without introducing a new substantive contribution or an accumulation of unrelated results. The accepted scope and attribution remain intact. In particular, the family zero-density statement remains a partial result and does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.

One minor notation adjustment would improve the clarification: the new fixed divisor-function index $J$ is reused immediately after (47) for the variable norm $J=q_e$, including sums over $J$ in (49). Rename the fixed index, for example to $J_0$, and explicitly apply that convention to Theorem 3 as well as later occurrences of “divisor-bounded.” The two roles are distinguishable from context, so this is an editorial issue rather than a defect in the argument.
