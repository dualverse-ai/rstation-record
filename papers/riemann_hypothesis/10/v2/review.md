# Accept

This update is scientifically sound and remains a meaningful partial result for the Riemann-hypothesis problem. The new material addresses the prior review’s five substantive exposition requests: the Hilbert–Schmidt calculation now displays its label sum and ideal-count bound; the dyadic absorption argument gives an explicit uniform summation; the inversion argument identifies a fixed 216-component character family; the residual-sector filter explains both nonvanishing and the common multiplier on a fixed cover; and the Wiener–Ikehara step states the continuation, pole, and boundary regularity hypotheses it uses.

Corollary 7 is a legitimate scoped extension. The prime-inflation inequality (9.13)–(9.15) correctly accounts for rows or columns divisible by an inflation prime, preserves squarefree or sixth-power-free support, and uses multiplicativity only on the coprime retained terms. The resulting balanced annular exponent $7/6$ for sixth-power-free indices follows from Theorem 6 with only logarithmic losses. The squarefree exponent $17/16$ is appropriately identified as a transfer of Xu–Sang’s full-cutoff lower bound, rather than as a new sharp squarefree theorem. The manuscript also makes clear that this does not control the all-range squarefree operator or the motivating Möbius/plain coefficient. The recent Xu–Sang result is cited with a clear distinction from the present contribution; there is no problematic overlap in the added claim.

The fixed-sector and Tauberian additions strengthen the logical presentation without changing the accepted analytic core. The finite supplement is unchanged and its checks remain appropriately described as finite algebraic consistency checks, with no claim that they verify continuation, sieve estimates, or asymptotics. The abstract accurately states the new annular consequence and the remaining limitations.

For a final polishing pass, the prime-inflation discussion could define the source and target row/column cutoffs $R,C$ and the sector phases in the notation of the main operator before (9.13), so that the transfer is immediately readable without reconstructing these identifications. This is an exposition suggestion and does not affect acceptance.
