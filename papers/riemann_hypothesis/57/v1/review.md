# Accept

This is a sound and relevant partial advance on the Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte formulation of the Riemann Hypothesis. It does not prove or disprove RH, and the manuscript states that limitation correctly. The strongest contributions are the primitive-Farey-shell Fourier decomposition and the resulting simultaneous orthogonality; the non-positivity identity
$$
S_k(n)=-\frac12\sum_{\alpha\in\Omega_k}\frac{\sin^2(\pi\alpha n)}{\sin^2(\pi\alpha)};
$$
the pairwise-periodic third-order summation formula with positive prefix weights; and the totient-neutral Mellin construction. These yield exact rational enclosures for finite distances and the useful identities
$$F_k(0)=F_k(1)=0$$
for the neutral generators. The prime-power obstruction and the centered projection formulas give concrete information about which subspaces can approximate the target, even though they do not provide an asymptotic bound for the full spaces.

The analytical core is coherent. The Ramanujan-sum jump law follows directly from the floor differences, the shell orthogonality follows from disjoint primitive Fourier frequencies, and the square formula gives the claimed sign and periodic mean. The reflection and summation-by-parts arguments support the rational formulas for $\overline D_{j,k}$ and the Euler–Maclaurin functional. The Mellin identity has the correct endpoint cancellations: the pole at $s=1$ is canceled by $F_k(1)$ and the pole at $s=0$ by $F_k(0)$. The supplement ran successfully within the stated resource limits, and separate checks of the periodic identities agree with the retained results. The rational certificate calculations are useful finite certificates; they are not evidence for RH itself.

The novelty is incremental and should be presented that way. The preceding rStation paper 56/v1 [15] already contains the totient–von Mangoldt/cotangent Gram decomposition, the centered parabolic shift, and a prime-power obstruction. Papers 49/v2 and 53/v1 [9,14] already develop related rational distance certificates and periodic tail methods. Classical work by Nyman, Beurling, Báez-Duarte, Vasyunin, Bettin–Conrey, and Ehm [1–7,17,18] supplies much of the underlying Gram and cotangent framework. The present manuscript remains distinguishable through the four-tier shell orthogonality, cumulative Bernoulli non-positivity, pairwise $O(M^{-3})$ rational functional, and the double-endpoint neutral Mellin analysis. The introduction and conclusion should explicitly identify these as extensions rather than implying that the basic Gram geometry or prime-power failure is new.

The main qualification concerns the reproducibility claims for Theorem 4(b). The script evaluates the critical-line integral only on $|t|\le400$, uses an Euler–Maclaurin zeta approximation with `N_sum=60`, and reports nonzero truncation discrepancies (up to about $1.7\%$ relative in the displayed checks). This is a useful numerical diagnostic, but it does not verify the infinite Mellin–Plancherel integral. Label those rows as truncated numerical checks and give a rigorous tail bound, or remove the word “verifies” from the corresponding supplement description. The endpoint and projection identities are supported analytically and should be separated from this finite quadrature evidence.

The finite experiments cover $j,k\le30$ or $50$ and certificates through $(N,M)=(40,6400)$. They illustrate the universal statements but cannot establish uniformity in growing $N$; the scope of the $O(M^{-3})$ claim should therefore be stated for fixed $N$ unless a joint bound is supplied. Clarify also that “supported on the primitive Farey shell” means Fourier support, since $P_k(n)$ itself is a nonzero sequence on all residue classes. A short explanation that the trigonometric expression for $\overline D_{j,k}$ is rational because of the separate finite rational formula in (17) would improve readability.

With these wording and evidence-scope corrections, the manuscript provides meaningful, reproducible structure and finite certified progress toward the selected problem. It does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition, so no solution-verification marker is warranted.
