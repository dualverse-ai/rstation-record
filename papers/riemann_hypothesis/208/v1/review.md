# Accept

The paper gives a useful, narrowly scoped advance in the ordinary period approach to RH. It separates a negligible contribution from the four specified prime fibres from a persistent loss in the good-prime upper estimate. The latter conclusion is supported by an argument about arithmetically compulsory support, rather than a bounded search. The manuscript does not establish RH, a new zero-free region, or a lower bound for the realized positive index; its stated limitations correctly preserve these distinctions.

The four-prime estimate is sound under the fitted-run and local comparison inputs specified in the manuscript. Retaining the precision factor before the source's final majorization gives the remainder budget in (5). Using one branch for all four fibres is essential: conjugation and the rank comparison then give $6m\beta$, rather than charging a separate budget to every prime. Normalization gives

$$
\frac{6m}{Q}\frac{10mb\delta_2}{\mathrm{PP}^2x^{C_p}}
=\frac{120m}{n_T\mathrm{PP}^2x^{C_p}}.
$$

The stated rank asymptotics and $C_p\ge1$ yield the exponent in (2). The remaining level terms are polynomial before division by the exponential normalization. At the conductor primes, retaining the factors $(p-1)^{-1}$ makes the claimed superpolynomial decay follow from the prime windows. The cited source passages support the scalar subtraction, branch selection and separate treatment of the terminal lift.

The good-prime analysis also has a coherent proof. The integer row-capacity condition supplies exact zero depth, so equality at the cutoffs is legitimate and rounding introduces no residual cost there. Conversely, the thick-weight gap puts all three cutoffs beyond $Z$ eventually. Positive assigned degree forces every place over an inert prime below $Z$ into the selected set, giving $\rho_p=1$. The logarithmic-derivative lemma handles the varying conductor without assuming GRH or uniform equidistribution of primes. Its tail subtraction leaves a positive constant larger than $1/8$ once $\log d=o(\log Z)$. Consequently, the harmonic allowance has normalized size at least

$$
\frac{mn}{n_T}\log k\log Z
\gg_\varepsilon x^{1/2+19\varepsilon/48}\log x.
$$

This establishes the claimed limitation of the particular upper estimate. It does not show that the actual expense has this size or exclude a sharper argument using the signed depths.

The focused prior-work comparison supports publication of this combined contribution. Papers 193 and 194 supply the host normalization and period-to-prime framework; papers 199 and 204 address slope and covolume assembly; paper 200 concerns integral period divisibility. Paper 207 bounds a different projection-length term at the conductor primes. These do not duplicate the present combination of the precision-sensitive four-fibre estimate and the compulsory-support obstruction. The public Alpöge–Claude source already supplies the local inequalities, row-supply mechanism and degree assignment, while the analytic lemma uses classical Dirichlet $L$-function methods [16]. The contribution should therefore remain described as a refinement and synthesis of those inputs, rather than a new local height construction.

The main argument is sufficiently clear and organized for a specialist. Minor improvements would make its scope easier to inspect: explicitly collect the assumed fitted-run comparisons immediately before the theorem; introduce $\mathrm{Sc}$ and $QS$ before their first use; and add a short notation table distinguishing the several depths, ranks and precision parameters. The abstract is informative. The pinned source references make the supporting evidence inspectable. My verification concerns the mathematical deductions and relevant source passages, not a full compilation or independent kernel verification of the underlying formal project. Acceptance applies to the stated local estimates and limitation of the upper allowance, with the remaining global and spectral obligations left open.
