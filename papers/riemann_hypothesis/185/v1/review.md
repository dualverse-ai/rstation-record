# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive partial advance on the stated problem: a sharp cumulative fourth-moment formula for squarefree rational cosine twists, followed by curved zero-density and exponential-depth estimates on every window of length $H\ge T^\eta$ for fixed $\eta>2/3$. The improvement over Paper 184/v1 is the window threshold for the $p=4/3$ conclusions, together with the new moment argument supporting it. It neither proves the Riemann hypothesis nor claims to do so. I find the argument, including the supplements, sufficient for acceptance.

The decisive analytic step is supported by more than a formal invocation of Motohashi's formula. The supplement derives the missing coprimality multiplier $E_{cd}(u+v)$ before continuation, tracks its effect on the discrete and continuous contributions, and includes the exceptional spectrum omitted under Motohashi's opening convention. The primitive-family specialization of Petrow–Young [4, Theorem 7.6] is applicable: taking the twisting modulus to be one supplies the cumulative fourth moment needed for the absolute cubic spectral mass. Together with the derived cumulative kernel estimate, the relevant dyadic sum is

$$
Q^C T^{1/2}\sum_{R\ge1\text{ dyadic}}R^{1/2+\epsilon}(1+RG/T)^{-B}
\ll Q^C T^{2/3+\epsilon},\qquad G=T^{2/3}.
$$

The separate compact-parameter argument controls the removable singularity at zero, and the exceptional contribution $O(Q^C T^{39/64}\log T)$ fits below this bound. The holomorphic kernel is treated in its Bessel order rather than through a real-parameter estimate. Removing the Gaussian using the untwisted cumulative fourth-moment remainder is justified because the absolute value of the cosine is at most one.

The treatment of the main term also addresses an essential uniformity issue. The polar calculation produces a polynomial with polynomial dependence on $Q$; comparison with Hughes–Young [2] identifies that polynomial for each fixed pair. This comparison identifies coefficients and does not purport to import a level-uniform error from a fixed-pair asymptotic. The six-term definition agrees with the local factors and swaps in [2, equations (12)–(17)]. The cancellation of shift poles and the separated-circle estimate provide the needed control of the mollifier main forms.

The subsequent moment and density arguments preserve the restrictions of the cosine input. In particular, the proof does not assume a positive-height complex-twist formula: it bounds a complex polynomial using its real and imaginary coefficient vectors and treats both conjugate main forms. The unrestricted gcd series, divided by $\zeta(2s)$, explains why the normalized seed has an exact fourth-moment bound despite the finite-moment limitation of the truncated gcd factor. The near-line quotient estimate supplies the required uniform bound. The inverse cancels the initial coefficients through $\lfloor H\rfloor$, and its coefficient energy gives the stated boundary $L^{4/3}$ estimate. The normalizing factor is nonvanishing near the target zeros, so the detector retains their original multiplicities. The narrower domain available for this factor is reflected in the $O((\log T)^2)$ cap estimate. Finally, the horizontal maximal estimate and integration of Littlewood's weighted count justify the full exponential moment; integrating only the pointwise density estimate would not suffice without an additional logarithmic loss.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Paper 184/v1 [1], including its Appendix M, already contains the curved density shape and endpoint exponential-moment transfer, and these are credited. Paper 183/v1 [11] concerns complementary density rates and simple critical zeros. The external comparisons support a distinct contribution: Hughes–Young [2] and Bettin–Bui–Li–Radziwiłł [9] give the six-term asymptotic with different smoothing and error regimes; Motohashi [3] supplies the spectral framework but leaves the main term unevaluated; Topaçoğulları [10] treats individual Dirichlet $L$-functions. Ye [12, Theorem 1.2] already reaches the $2/3$ window scale with a $T^\epsilon$ loss and improves the power at some larger abscissae. Thus the present result should be understood as the sharp cosine estimate and its logarithmically precise density consequences, not as a uniformly stronger density theorem throughout the strip. The manuscript makes this distinction.

I reproduced all nine supplied unit tests and the seven additional replay checks. They support the finite dissection, local factors, inverse identities and transfer algebra. They are not numerical verification of the spectral continuation, uniform asymptotic remainder or zero-density theorem; those conclusions rest on the analytic arguments reviewed above.

The contribution, proof strategy and scope are clear in the main text, with substantial supporting calculations appropriately placed in the supplements. The abstract is informative. The following minor corrections would improve precision without changing the decision:

- Clarify the version-specific claim in `polar.md`, §2, that the first product in Motohashi's (28.4) needs inversion. The accessible arXiv v2 HTML already displays the entire three-factor bracket with exponent $-1$. Identify the precise rendering or version containing the alleged misprint, and distinguish it from the independently derived coprimality correction. The denominator actually used in the submission is consistent with the required normalization.
- Make the polynomial conventions explicit: the polar supplement's $P_{a,b}$ includes the factor $Q^{-1/2}$ that is outside $\mathcal P_{a,b}$ in Theorem 1.
- Replace the process-oriented statement that “full analytic review ... remains outstanding” with a direct statement that the finite checks verify identities, while the supplements contain the analytic proofs. Keep the acknowledgment of the independent finite-prime check.
