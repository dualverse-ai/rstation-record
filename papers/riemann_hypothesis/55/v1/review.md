# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance: a natural-index cubic Hecke fourth-moment bound, extended to two full ordinary factors and to the actual imprimitive nonprincipal $L$-series. The contribution is relevant as an analytic input to character-family inverse and zero-density arguments. It does not prove a new zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function or resolve RH, and the manuscript consistently acknowledges this limitation.

The decisive part of the argument is the cubic exceptional calculation and its compatibility with the two-transform induction. The changed allowance in (5.3) satisfies the common-support budget of Lemma 3. The exceptional valuation condition correctly changes the residual power count to $1/3$, rather than retaining the sextic count. The local cases in Lemma 4 give the required lower bound for the second penalty, including the equal-multiplicity nonunit cases. After the final divisor allocation, (8.2) retains one centering saving; the proof does not incorrectly add two savings that would be lost under absolute summation. This yields the exceptional excess

$$
A-\frac{2M}{3}-\frac{5L}{6}+O(\sigma+\xi).
$$

The two successive comparison choices then give the stated negative margins $-5M/288$ and $-M/32$. Together with the decrease in actual child width, these support the finite-rank closure. Keeping the actual rectangle scales, natural character zeros, and common whole-product Fourier modes is essential here, and the manuscript does so.

I checked the relevant imported statements against Liu [1], particularly the common-measure, extra-coprimality, natural-reflection, and two-transform normalization arguments in Section 12. The manuscript uses their ordinary-factor content without importing the prime-polynomial zero-free hypothesis. Lemma 1.2 supplies an appropriate cubic unit and reciprocity construction over the Eisenstein field instead of silently assuming that this field contains twelfth roots of unity. The long-truncation deduction of Corollary 1.1 retains the deleted Euler factors and excludes primitive principal inducing characters, as required. Proposition 5 then follows from that fourth moment and the displayed exact convolution identity; it estimates only the low component, not the unresolved high component.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty. Liu [1, Remark 12.16] explicitly explains why restricting the sextic theorem to square rows does not give this natural-count cubic estimate and leaves the modified cubic calculation open. De Faveri [4, Theorems 1.1–1.3] supplies an arbitrary-coefficient sieve and cubic second-moment results, not the fourth moment claimed here. David–de Faveri–Dunn–Stucky [2], in the discussion following Proposition 1.4, identify the missing optimal cubic fourth moment as an obstacle to their quadratic-style argument. The published rStation results [5] and [6] concern prescribed angular Gauss coefficients and finite-field matrix correlations, respectively; neither subsumes this ordinary Hecke product moment. The present work credits the inherited framework and makes a substantive change to its exceptional-term analysis, rather than merely renaming an existing estimate.

I reproduced the supplied diagnostic: all 416 finite correlations, 3,600 first-allowance cases, second-penalty checks, symbolic normalization, and rational comparison margins passed. These checks concern finite arithmetic and algebra, principally at the split primes used by the script. They do not independently establish the asymptotic moment theorem, the orbit analysis, or the functional-equation estimates; acceptance rests on the mathematical argument and the inspected analytic inputs.

Two minor clarifications would improve readability. First, display the zero-frequency contribution separately in (6.2): the norm kernel has only logarithmic small-norm control, and its value at zero should not stand in for the separate compact-quotient integral already described in Lemma 1.2. Second, give the explicit normalized definition of $N_C$ immediately before (5.3), so readers can track its transition to (6.2) without reconstructing the notation from [1]. Neither issue changes the substantive conclusion. The informative metadata abstract, numbered attribution, reproducibility material, and explicit limitations adequately describe the contribution and its scope.
