# Accept

The revision makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted ordinary cubic product moment. The new contribution is the joint estimate with short prime factors, followed by arbitrary bounded coefficient blocks supported on ideals with sufficiently small prime divisors. The capacity condition $n_1+n_2+3z\le M$ and the dependence of the smoothness mesh on the requested loss are essential qualifications. These results provide meaningful additional control of structured inverse coefficients. They do not estimate the complete signed inverse remainder, establish a new zero-free region, or resolve RH.

The cubic amplification in Section 11 is consistent with the full imprimitive Gauss formulas. Multiplication of a frequency by a new prime cube changes precisely the positive valuations $1,3,4$, with the centrally normalized squared coefficients in (11.8). The pool lies outside the surviving amplifier supports, and its graph multiplicity gives the stated main-branch density without an additional prime-count cost on the error branches. The normalization in (11.13) supplies both a strict decrease in actual width and the allowance needed to restore capacity. The finite comparisons (11.24)–(11.28) have positive scale and exceptional-term margins for each fixed $C>3$. The endpoint follows from the separately paid initial removal, rather than from uniformity as $C$ approaches three.

The extension in Section 13 addresses the main additional difficulty posed by arbitrary coefficients. Genuine extractions and the complete signed divisibility expansion preserve bounded coefficient slices and the common character and zero masks. The prefix factorization is unique, including repeated primes and distinct primes of equal norm. Crucially, (13.7) pays the entire Hilbert-triangle coefficient mass: its squared cost is $Z^{t+\eta}$, up to the stated small losses. The removed divisors are therefore not an uncounted additional family. One partial-block overshoot suffices, and the mesh is chosen after the finite induction depth. The exact-scale bookkeeping in (13.10a)–(13.10b) also retains the single divisor count. On exceptional rows, fixing the amplifier ideals before applying the ordinary centered mean preserves the residue cancellation; restoring their absolute counts afterward retains the one saving required by the comparison argument.

The two new applications have appropriately limited scope. Theorem 3 uses actual common prime-amplitude hypotheses, and its long truncation retains the imprimitive Euler deletions. For Proposition 7, the imported individual correlation estimate agrees with [8, Section 2.4, (B7)]: its conductor exponent gives the stated $b_m$. Weighted Gram duality and Hölder yield (14.6), and expanding its two terms gives (14.3). The reduction $k=k_0t^3$ avoids applying an off-diagonal estimate to cube-equivalent labels; the resulting sums converge, with only the stated logarithmic endpoint when $j=2$. The affine calculation in Corollary 4.1 gives $2599/2400$, but this numerical saving applies only to the specified length region and does not control the complementary rough terms.

The focused literature comparison supports the distinction of the additions. Liu [1, Sections 12 and 14] supplies the credited amplification, normalization, and numerator-selection framework, but his sextic theorem does not supply the natural-count cubic extension or the arbitrary smooth-supported coefficient class proved here. Blomer–Humphries–Khan–Milinovich [7, Theorem 4] concerns a squarefree-supported twisting polynomial over Dirichlet characters modulo a rational prime. Xu–Sang [9, Proposition 7.2] concerns bounded rough prime convolutions in a sextic second moment, with a lower prime cutoff and a bounded number of factors. Neither subsumes the new joint moment.
