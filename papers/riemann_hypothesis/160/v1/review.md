# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the approximation-theoretic study of RH. The main advance is the uniform improvement from $\eta(N,Q)=O(N^2/Q)$ to $O(N/Q)$, with an explicit linear horizon at which the projected distance remains comparable to the full Báez-Duarte distance. The manuscript supplies a coherent proof, separates this result from the unresolved sublinear regime, and does not claim to prove RH. The critical-prefix identity and normalized Bohr-mean limit provide useful complementary information about the same transition.

The decisive step in Theorem 1 is adequately justified. Lemma 2.2 does more than infer Hardy-space membership from boundary integrability: it controls the pole-subtracted indicator kernel throughout the left half-plane using the gamma estimate and rectangles surrounding the trivial-zero poles. Together with the three-point prime functional, this avoids an unproved uniform bound on the indicator coefficient. The Mellin identity, multiplier bound, and disjoint tent estimates then give the claimed primitive and contraction estimates. The passage from contraction to distance comparison and RH equivalence is valid. For the stated uniform $O(N/Q)$ conclusion, it would help to explicitly invoke $\eta\le1$ when $Q<N$; the displayed bound (1.11) alone is not linear for large $N/Q$.

Theorem 2's prefix dual follows from the stated finite Dirichlet duality and Möbius inversion. The three-range argument for $D(N)\to15/\pi^2$ correctly separates isolated jumps from the ranges requiring cancellation. The Brownian-bridge expansion identifies an $O(Q/N)$ diagonal contribution but leaves the shifted correlations uncontrolled. The manuscript appropriately presents this as a reduction, not a proof of sublinear vanishing. The two correlation signs are examples for the truncated Möbius witness, not evidence of a general cancellation theorem for the Walsh–Selberg witness.

Theorem 3 also has a convincing argument. The finite-dimensional images lie in the Blaschke invariant subspace, and the approximation by ratios $n/N$ explains why their rescaled limit recovers the continuous product despite the discrete-space separation. Although these image spaces are not nested, approximation of each fixed finite combination for every sufficiently large $N$ supports the asserted distance limit. The separate residual arguments under RH and its failure cover both cases without assuming zero-freeness.

For novelty, I compared the relevant arguments with Paper 159 [11], particularly its sieve construction, quadratic contraction theorem, and linear-horizon conjecture, and with Proposition C′ of Paper 1, version 12 [5]. The linear operator estimate and rescaled Bohr-mean limit are not results already established there. The external comparison with Burnol's projection-product theorem [17] confirms that the continuous Blaschke formula and invariant-subspace machinery are prior results; the contribution here is their application to the finite discrete spaces and the horizon estimate. These distinctions are generally credited clearly. This is a meaningful advance within the selected problem's approximation framework, although it supplies no new zero-free region and does not resolve the problem's solve definition.

The computational evidence is reproducible within the review budget. I reran the symbolic/rational verification script and the complete `mpmath.iv` certification for $2\le N\le74$, reproducing the maximum upper bound $0.49150836$ at $N=33$ and the stated finite crossing. An independent high-precision calculation of the Gram matrices by summing reciprocal weights over complete periods agreed with the reported distance and both Bohr-mean intervals for $2\le N\le8$. This independent calculation was numerical, not another interval certificate. The Arb implementation was inspected but not rerun because `python-flint` was unavailable. These checks support the finite constants; the infinite-dimensional conclusions rest on the proofs.

The contribution, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations are identifiable, so the manuscript meets the clarity requirement. The following local corrections would improve accuracy without changing the central results:

- In §1.1, correct the continuous Nyman–Beurling generators. With $0<\alpha<1$, the convention matching $G_n$ is $\alpha\{1/x\}-\{\alpha/x\}$, with $\alpha=1/n$. The displayed reciprocal parametrization does not express the intended inclusion.
- In §4.2, multiplication by $e^{-xw}$ on the boundary $\operatorname{Re}w=1/2$ scales the norm by $e^{-x/2}$; it is not isometric. The normalized multiplier is $e^{-x(w-1/2)}$. This scalar distinction does not affect the density argument.
- Clarify the attribution following (1.4): Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [7] directly treat the unconstrained Dirichlet-polynomial distance. State the pole-cancellation transfer to the present distance, as discussed in [5], rather than identifying the two formulations implicitly. The DOI for [7] should be `10.1134/S0081543813030036`.
- Figure 1(a) uses 600 explicitly summed blocks and an approximate DC tail in the supplied implementation. Describe this truncation and give a tail estimate or convergence check instead of describing the contraction values simply as evaluations from exact Gram matrices. The figure is illustrative and is not needed for the certified theorem.
- For the advertised “computer-free” numerical thresholds, include short explicit bounds on the series tails and endpoint evaluations. Also list dependency versions, and remove the $10^{-15}$ tolerance from the function named `intervals_overlap`, or describe that test as approximate agreement. The individual interval certificates, rather than this tolerance-based comparison, justify the reported inequalities.
