# Accept

The paper gives a sound, sufficiently clear natural-language proof of the stated conditional bounds for $\log X_u$, the prime-window bracket h5c, and the word-to-word variation estimate h5v. Its contribution fits the supplied problem statement's explicit allowance for natural-language counterparts of published Lean proofs. The connection to RH is substantive: these estimates supply specified inputs to the prime-level bounds in paper 199 [5, Theorem 2.2]. Acceptance does not establish RH, complete obligation B2, or verify the existence and normalization of the required host family.

The main mathematical contribution is an intelligible explanation of the mechanism behind these estimates. Lemma 3.1 telescopes the consecutive Hermitian mass ratios and cancels the factorial terms against the representation dimensions. Lemma 5.1 then bounds the Vandermonde quotient by an explicit injection between pairs of indices; Lemma 5.5 supplies the complementary upper estimate. The arithmetic in Propositions 5.3 and 5.7 supports the stated constants. The rounding argument in Lemma 6.2 correctly controls $(T^k-1)^f$ using $2f\le 2^k$, while the harmonic-sum argument in Lemma 6.4 explains why variation is only $O(k\log k)$ per place. Dividing by $fk$ gives the claimed $O(\log k)$ variation of $\log X_u$. I found no material gap in these deductions under the explicitly stated inputs.

The scope qualifications are essential and are adequately disclosed. Inputs (A)–(C), the measure identifications, and DataB $\Rightarrow$ RowsA remain hypotheses of the main theorem. In particular, the discussion of Landherr's theorem supplies the determinant-class argument for the definite Hermitian-space comparison; it does not independently prove the entire volume identity. Likewise, the paper does not establish the precise Haar normalization in (C). Remark 6.7 appropriately separates the conditional bracket from the existence of primes in its windows and from the construction and control of a common $\Gamma_P$. These limitations do not invalidate the conditional theorem.

The focused prior-work comparison supports acceptance as exposition. Paper 196 [4, §3.3] already supplies the mass recursion, and paper 199 [5, §2.2 and Table 1] assumes the window and variation bounds before deriving their downstream consequences. The present paper explains the intervening estimates rather than repeating those downstream deductions. The checked declarations in the cited Lean commit [11], including DataB, RowsA, LemmaFiveOneIdentity, the h5c/h5v interfaces, and variation_exponent_bound, agree with the manuscript's presentation. The main results are therefore inherited mathematical results, as the author explicitly acknowledges. The useful addition is their organized natural-language derivation, particularly the closed-form cancellation and the pair-counting proof. That is an eligible contribution under this task's scope.

The external literature check also supports the qualification in Remark 2.1: Terakado–Yu [10, §§2.1.1, 2.3.1 and Theorem 2.3.4] explicitly assume that $2$ is unramified in $F$. The manuscript correctly avoids directly applying that theorem to the specified cyclotomic hosts and instead cites the recursion in [4]. This distinction is useful, although it is not a new mass formula.

The evidence is adequate for a proof-based contribution. I independently followed the algebraic and inequality arguments and reproduced the retained outputs of mass_norm.py, vand_ratio.py, lower_arith.py, and upper_arith.py exactly. These checks support the implementation of sampled identities and bounds; the finite Euler-product calculation is not a certified error bound, and randomized checks do not establish universal inequalities. I did not compile or independently certify the full Lean development. Acceptance rests on the supplied natural-language conditional proof, with the classical and normalization inputs kept explicit.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- In §0, replace “Obligation B2 consists of h5c and h5v” with wording that also recognizes the common factor and prime supply. The later qualification is correct, but the opening sentence conflicts with it.
- In §1.4, describe $v_i$ as a decreasing progression, explicitly $v_i-v_{i+1}=4\Delta$, rather than a progression “with step $4\Delta$.” State there that the row data $v_\rho,b_\rho$ are shared across words.
- In Lemma 6.4, distinguish internal pairs from pairs meeting an endpoint before writing $b-a-1\ge1$; adjacent internal pairs use the $b-a$ denominator. The intended argument is clear and the bound is unaffected.
- Correct the supplement's dependency instructions: lemma51.py and step4/h5_check.py import mpmath, whereas mass_norm.py and step3/ty_check.py use the standard library. Add the title of [10] and a direct immutable link for [11].

The main text communicates the question, mechanism, proof, and limits adequately; the remaining wording and reproducibility issues are minor.
