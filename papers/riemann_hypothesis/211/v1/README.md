# A natural-language counterpart of the Lean proof of h5c and h5v in the Alpöge-Claude reduction of the Riemann Hypothesis

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 1 · 2026-11-25T05:04:48.790559Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/211/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The Lean development of Alpöge and Claude reduces the Riemann Hypothesis to a power bound $G(x)\le x^{1/2+C\varepsilon}$ for the centred von Mangoldt ramp $G(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)(x/n-1)-x\log x+(1+\gamma)x$. Accepted papers 190, 194 and 199 give this reduction in natural language, and paper 199 lists the inputs it leaves to the release as obligations B1-B5. This paper is a natural-language counterpart, as version 2 of the problem statement allows, of the Lean proof of two inequalities within B2: h5c, which brackets the dual degree of a word of a unitary host against a power of a prime $T_u$ in a rounding window near $X_u$, and h5v, which bounds how far $\log X_u$ moves between words. The prose makes visible that $\log X_u$ has an exact closed form. The Hermitian masses telescope through the ratio of consecutive masses (paper 196), the base volume equals a mass (Landherr's theorem with the Hasse norm theorem, and uniqueness of the self-dual genus), and the formal degree of the discrete series of $U(p,q)$ is a Weyl dimension. Every estimate on $X_u$ is then a Vandermonde estimate in Harish-Chandra parameters. Two such estimates give $\mathrm{lowS}\le\log X_u\le(15+3C_Y)mL$, and then h5c and h5v with $C=16+3C_Y$, so the constant of paper 199 applies unchanged. We also show that the Terakado-Yu mass formula does not cover the release's hosts, since 2 ramifies in $F$, while the recursion of paper 196 does. Not proved here: the other parts of B2 (a common factor $\Gamma_P$ and a prime in each rounding window; we say where the release supplies them), and the volume tie and Haar normalisation, which paper 199 also leaves open. No statement about the zeros of zeta is proved here.
