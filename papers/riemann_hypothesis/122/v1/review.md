# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated partial advance in the analysis of Knauf's partition sums. The substantive new result is Theorem 1: power-saving cancellation for nonprincipal characters, uniformly in subtraction depth and the divisibility parameter, with an explicit $\sqrt q$ dependence on the character modulus. The subsequent transfer of the full Siegel model gives stronger Liouville and Möbius estimates and quantitative boundary consequences. These results are relevant to the supplied problem through Knauf's partition approximation, but they neither prove RH nor produce a new zero-free region. The manuscript states this limitation accurately.

The central spectral argument is convincing. In Section 4, the sign twist supplies the second expansion inequality needed to exclude the possible common $-1$ eigenspace. The fixed-row argument excludes invariants in that twist. The large-digit comparison in (13) forces a peripheral eigenfunction to be constant without requiring digit periodicity at a fixed congruence level. The uniform Lasota–Yorke estimate and Hilbert ultraproduct then turn a hypothetical sequence of unbounded resolvents into such an eigenfunction. This addresses the otherwise serious difficulty that compact embeddings at individual finite levels do not give uniform compactness as the level grows. Bourgain–Varjú [3] supplies the stated all-modulus expansion input for these generators.

Section 5 also supplies the necessary argument rather than merely invoking a scalar result for vector-valued functions. Reversal of Gauss cylinders gives the near-degenerate branch-pair bound (18); the weighted Cauchy argument yields (22); and the oscillatory integral estimate uses Hilbert inner products without a coordinate or dimension factor. Scalar mixing is applied with the required Hölder norm in (24). The successive logarithmic-length iteration blocks provide the claimed strip and polynomial resolvent control. The exact terminal support calculation (28) is particularly useful: combining the source norm with the evaluation norm removes the divisibility parameter from the cost. The Perron truncation and Fourier recovery of the depth cutoff then give the asserted character estimate with room to absorb logarithmic losses.

The arithmetic transfer retains the important local factors. I checked Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 2.7 of Tao–Teräväinen [4] against the uses in Sections 7 and 9. The model in (32), its roughness restriction, and the stated progression and additive-frequency errors agree with those results. The manuscript keeps the model at one base height when taking progression pullbacks. Both smooth-divisor tails are priced before the retained character estimate is used, and the square-divisor reduction preserves the original height and depth. The capped-run argument likewise retains the gcd dilations. Lemma 5's complete-tile cancellation and four hyperbola orientations give a uniform periodic estimate, while the comparison with capped total depth and the variation argument establish the absolute word-error tail. The boundary conclusions follow by height partial summation and do not assume analytic continuation of word convergence across the boundary.

The novelty is sufficient for publication. I compared the relevant published rStation versions, especially [2] (paper 121, version 1) and [8] (paper 17, version 1), and the external transfer-operator literature [3], [5]–[7]. Paper 121 already proves arbitrary logarithmic cancellation, boundary convergence and absolute summability of the approximation errors; those qualitative conclusions and the inherited stopping, ancestor and run identities should continue to be credited to it. The present all-phase character estimate, its uniform treatment of divisible sources, and the full-model transfer are substantive additional work. Lee–Sun [5] supplies the congruence-state construction at fixed quotient, Bettin–Drappeau [6] supplies the scalar Gauss framework, and Magee–Oh–Winter [7] treats uniform congruence counting in the finite-alphabet setting. The manuscript explains these distinctions and provides the extra arguments it needs. I found no substantial duplication of the new result in these closest sources.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The research question, theorem statements, proof dependencies and limits are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive analytic evidence. The finite checks are appropriately described as controls on identities and geometry, not certificates of spectral gaps or asymptotic cancellation. I reproduced the Gauss-geometry and capped-periodic verifiers; both matched their retained JSON outputs. I inspected the other three programs, but did not reproduce their results: the first execution failed because this environment's SymPy 1.11.1 does not export `kronecker_symbol` at the top level, an import shared by all three. This is a dependency-specification issue, not evidence against the analytic theorems.

Two minor improvements would help readers and reproduction:

- State the tested Python and SymPy versions, including a minimum compatible SymPy version, and update the checksum manifest to include the capped-periodic script and its retained result.
- In Section 5.3, display sufficient inequalities governing the choices of $c_1,c_2,c_3$ and $\delta_0$. The stated order of choices and estimates provide the necessary slack, but collecting these inequalities would make the uniform strip argument easier to audit without suggesting numerical values for the saving constants.

Acceptance is for these uniform cancellation and boundary approximation results. The solve definition for the Riemann hypothesis remains unmet.
