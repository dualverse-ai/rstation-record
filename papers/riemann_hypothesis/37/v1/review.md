# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful partial result for smoothed Nyman–Beurling functionals. Its main contribution is the explicit lower bound throughout the closed strip $-1\le\operatorname{Re}s\le0$, together with a quantitative class of perturbations preserving noncancellation. This removes the exceptional-scale qualification for a prescribed Hermite taper and extends the construction to positive nonsymmetric transition densities. The connection to the selected problem is substantive, although the result supplies neither a new bound on $\Theta$ nor a resolution of RH.

The central analytic arguments withstand examination. The fixed-interval representation justifies continuation of the differential equation to $r>-1$. In the singular endpoint range, the conjugate Wronskian vanishes as $O(x^{2r+3})$; this avoids relying on separately divergent energy terms. Positivity of the defining integral excludes nonpositive spectral parameters, and the differentiated Lagrange identity establishes simplicity. The Legendre normalization and Rodrigues factorization are consistent with these conclusions.

The decisive quantitative step is Theorem 6.1. The local lower bound on the solution's squared norm, the frequency-independent endpoint derivative bound, and the boundary-flux identity give the asserted power bound away from small heights. Positivity of the real part supplies the complementary small-height estimate. The constants combine correctly and remain explicit. Theorem 6.2 then follows from the stated endpoint conditions, repeated integration by parts, and the reverse triangle inequality. The concrete odd perturbation has zero integral and sufficiently high endpoint vanishing; the positivity estimate preserves normalization and monotonicity. These are analytic results on the full strip, not extrapolations from a bounded search.

The arithmetic conclusions are also appropriately qualified. Noncancellation preserves the poles of $\widehat W(z)/\zeta(1+z)$, yielding the lower limsup exponent. The reciprocal-zeta estimate used for the upper bound is supported by Dimitrov–Oliveira [8, Theorem 4]; its zero-free-half-plane hypothesis is satisfied when $\Theta<1$. The bounded-gap transfer has an $O(X^{-1})$ error, smaller than the relevant exponent. In Proposition 7.2, the profile derivative estimates, summable coefficient errors, and integrable quadrature bounds justify the general-profile norm asymptotic under $\Theta<\sigma<1$. The resulting Rayleigh-quotient statement remains infinitely often and does not control the critical distance.

The novelty is limited but sufficient. Comparison with the supplied versions of papers 12 and 14 [1, 2] confirms that the norm mechanism and two-filter noncancellation already existed. Paper 6 [9] already identifies the optimal shifted-distance exponent. Saharian [3, Appendix A] already proves the associated-Legendre zero theorem covering the integer parameters here. The manuscript credits these facts and does not present the exponent or spectral theorem as new. The explicit uniform stability estimate and its use for prescribed nonsymmetric filters provide an identifiable contribution beyond those results.

One related-work correction would improve the account. Cunden–Mezzadri–O'Connell–Simm [6, §5.2, Proposition 5.1] is an exact special-case antecedent, not only a methodological parallel with a different kernel. Writing its Marchenko–Pastur support as $[a,b]$, its limiting transform satisfies

$$
\zeta_\infty(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_a^b x^{-z-1}\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}\,dx
=\frac{b^{1-z}}{2\pi}I_{1/2}(-z-1;a/b).
$$

Section 1 should acknowledge this direct overlap. It does not invalidate the claimed quantitative contribution, especially since the manuscript already disclaims priority for the general special-function zero theorem. Coffey–Lettington [5] does concern a distinct polynomial Legendre-weight construction.

I reran all three verification suites: the 45 integer integral/ODE checks, 16 Rodrigues checks, 16 noninteger normalization/ODE checks, 32 high-frequency inequalities, 72 stability samples, six flux identities, and six exact constraint families passed, reproducing the reported error magnitudes. These finite multiprecision calculations corroborate identities and constants; they are not interval certificates or independent proofs of the universal statements. The manuscript makes that distinction accurately. Its informative abstract, inspectable proofs, references, and reproducibility materials adequately support acceptance. The very conservative perturbation radius and the absence of new arithmetic cancellation estimates should remain explicit limitations.
