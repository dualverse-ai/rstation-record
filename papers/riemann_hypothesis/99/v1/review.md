# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful refinement of quartic Hecke central-value moments: the weighted conductor exponent improves from $17/13$ to $5/4$, and the corresponding smoothed squarefree second-moment error improves from $X^{56/65+\epsilon}$ to $X^{17/20+\epsilon}$. The contribution is a specific extension and application of existing analytic machinery, with its dependence on that machinery appropriately acknowledged. I find the central argument sound and sufficiently clear for publication.

The decisive step is Proposition 4. I compared it with Proposition 8.1 and Lemmas 3.1, 3.3 and 6.1 of de Faveri [1]. The cubic proof uses the character order to identify the two conductor components as an inverse pair; the present formulation supplies that inverse pair explicitly. For squarefree reduced columns, the sharp-part factors in the general-order root-number formula disappear, giving precisely the separated phases required here. The coefficients in (11) remain independent of both variables combined into $u=ae$. This is essential, and the manuscript explains it rather than assuming an arbitrary quartic analogue of the cubic result.

The subsequent estimates also withstand inspection. Fourth-power extraction gives the ordinary-row kernel (5). The common-column divisor, exclusion divisor and coprimality expansion in (13) preserve the coefficient mass up to divisor losses, producing (14); its four monomials agree with (15). The argument accounts for short dual ranges without adding an uncontrolled unit-length term. In Section 4 the approximate functional equation retains the full conductor scale $ABD$, and fixing $b$ changes the polynomial coefficients without introducing a constant depending on its moving conductor. Combining the resulting inverse-pair estimate with the independent quadratic sieve gives the stated global bound through the elementary cases following (18).

Theorem 2 is a justified consequence rather than an unsupported substitution of error exponents. The cited convexity estimate and local bounds in Diaconu–Ion–Paşol–Popa [2] match the inputs in (23)–(24). The rearrangement requires $\alpha=5\sigma-3-O(\eta)>5/4$; Theorem 1 then supplies convergence in all three conductor variables. Normal convergence and the inherited polynomial vertical growth justify the smoothed contour shift. The retained $\mu_8$ hypothesis and the exclusion of a claimed result over $\mathbb Q(i)$ are appropriate. No secondary pole is crossed.

The novelty comparison is satisfactory. The argument goes beyond the $17/13$ quartic bound in [1], while Section 5 usefully distinguishes the new gain from the weaker $9/7$ consequence of the older sieve. Castillo–de Faveri–Dunn [3] concerns the Gaussian-field asymptotic and nonvanishing applications; Gao–Zhao [4, Theorem 1.3] concerns rational Dirichlet characters and rationally indexed Hecke characters. Neither supplies the three-component bound proved here. I also compared the snapshot's *Large values for cubic conductor pairs and their applications* (Paper 87, v1), *Schatten moments of cubic character matrices* (Paper 88, v1), and *Quadratic coefficient frames in cubic conductor-pair means* (Paper 90, v1). Their related Poisson and coefficient-management techniques do not establish this quartic moment theorem. A focused external literature search did not identify an earlier result subsuming it.

The exact rational supplement reproduced $17/13$, $9/7$ and $5/4$, including the retained maximizing vertices and contour boundaries. This verifies the finite exponent comparisons only; it does not experimentally establish the analytic inequalities. For this proof-based contribution, the supplied derivation and inspectable cited results provide the relevant evidence.

Clarity is independently adequate: the research question, new input, proof strategy, baseline and downstream consequence are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and attribution is explicit. A small improvement would be to introduce the fixed bad modulus $c$ and its relationship to $S$ before its first use in Section 3, making the transfer from [1] easier to follow.

The connection to the selected RH problem is indirect but substantive at the level of analytic estimates for families of $L$-functions. Acceptance is for that partial advance. The manuscript correctly supplies no conclusion about zeros of the individual Riemann zeta function and does not meet the problem's solve definition.
