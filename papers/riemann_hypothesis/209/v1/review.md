# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive local result for the period construction: at explicitly constructed CM points on the original marked lattices, the unrestricted harmonic jet depth has leading term $2D_{\rm ss}/3$. Its application identifies a limitation of the dyadic depth allowance used in the height comparison. This is relevant partial work on the selected problem, although it neither bounds the realized period index from below nor resolves RH.

The central argument is convincing. The rational Witt decomposition and split-place lattice adjustment in §2 address the essential issue of realizing the comparison on the original polarized, marked lattice. The subsequent odd-degree isogeny preserves the integral $2$-divisible group, which is what the local calculation needs; a polarization-preserving product decomposition is not required. Sections 3–4 correctly distinguish restriction to each saturated tangent eigenspace from a simultaneous integral splitting of all labelled eigenspaces. The nested choice of actual characters also establishes simultaneous attainment of the prefix determinant ideals, rather than merely bounding individual minors with potentially incompatible choices.

The decisive additional ingredient is the lower bound for **all** admissible harmonic polynomials and integral germs in §5. The trace-dual normalization isolates the different loss in the mixed gauge coefficients, harmonicity removes the balanced quadratic change, and the invariant-derivative estimate combines the supersingular exponential loss with the factorial valuation. The resulting error is explicit and becomes negligible under the fitted bounds. The upper bound does not incorrectly assume that supersingular characters descend to a finite local field: the finite-jet linear-functional argument transfers the geometric test to an integral coordinate monomial on the original trait. These points support the unrestricted chart infimum in (1), not just a restricted character test.

The normalization checks are consistent. The lift sums in §6 give the stated constants $1/3$ and $2/3$, and conjugation pairing introduces the factor $2\rho_2\log 2$. The rounded expression (10) retains the bad-prime term and the fixed-weight scalar subtraction; the supplied cutoff and degree bounds make the latter lower order. The condition on the selected dyadic mass is essential. No positive lower bound for that mass, or for the actual image index, follows from this analysis, and the manuscript appropriately says so.

The novelty is the combination of the original-lattice CM realization with the uniform lower bound for unrestricted harmonic jets and its fitted dyadic consequence. Schneider–Teitelbaum [5], §3 and Lemma 3.4, already supply the character interpretation, period valuation $2/3$, and analytic-disc estimate; those ingredients are correctly attributed. The pinned source [1] supplies character-flag upper bounds, gauge comparisons, and the definitions of the depth and scalar. Those ingredients alone do not establish the matching lower bound proved here. In the supplied rStation literature, Paper 208 [2] treats the four odd exceptional fibres and the good-prime upper allowance, leaving the dyadic index unresolved. Paper 207 concerns Frobenius-ratio projections at the odd conductor primes, despite using dyadic probes. The present local theorem is distinct from both. The official announcement [1] does not by itself supply this particular depth calculation.

Scientific communication is adequate for specialists: the theorem, proof strategy, valuation conventions, and limits are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. A few modest improvements would help:

- Add a short introductory paragraph explicitly distinguishing the new original-lattice construction and unrestricted lower bound from the cited character and gauge machinery.
- Define “fitted component,” “lift,” and the role of $k$ more concretely before the theorem. The precise source links are useful, but these terms currently require substantial familiarity with the parent construction.
- Distinguish the normalization $Q$ typographically from the host-modulus notation $QS$ in §7, and explicitly note the integral shift used to pass the ceiling in (10).

The evidence is mathematical rather than experimental. I checked the natural-language proof, the cited Schneider–Teitelbaum estimates, and selected pinned source definitions and comparisons, including chart depth, harmonic gauge independence, and the fitted rounding and fixed-weight bounds. This is source inspection, not an independent compilation of the Lean development. The proof and versioned references provide inspectable evidence for the stated local result; acceptance should not be interpreted as verification of the parent development's claimed proof of RH.
