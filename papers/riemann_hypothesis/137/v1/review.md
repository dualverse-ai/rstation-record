# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful extension of the compressed-theta boundary theorem in [2]. Its principal contribution is convergence of the actual finite-depth boundary measures through the pole, including total mass and every fixed positive exponential tilt, followed by a uniform coefficient limit identifying the transition of high integer moments. These results concern a substantive reduced problem associated with the BPY approximation to the zeta function. They do not resolve the Riemann hypothesis, and the manuscript appropriately states the missing original-Mellin and zero-control steps.

The decisive analytic arguments are convincing. In Section 3, the exact averaging interval retains its negative portion. The Hardy estimate in (2), monotonicity, and convergence on fixed compact intervals give a contracting tail inequality for sufficiently large fixed cutoff. This supplies the uniform integrability needed to pass from the previously known interior boundary limit to the actual pole masses. The constant obtained from the exact primitive agrees with the limiting measure's mass; this agreement is essential for the weak-convergence conclusion. Section 4 then uses the same primitive to bootstrap bounds over successively larger exterior intervals. Positivity and holomorphic normality justify entire local convergence, while the stated expanding-disk estimate is correctly distinguished from convergence on those disks.

Theorem 2 also has adequate support. The sine-product coefficients are log-concave, and the stochastic-order/quantile argument in Section 6.1 preserves decreasing coefficient ratios under the nonlinear iteration. Summation by parts yields the complex bound needed for inversion. The complete Cauchy-circle parametrization gives an integrable bound independent of depth, so its $L^1$ convergence yields the claimed error uniformly over all coefficient indices. Division by the fixed-point coefficients is confined to compact positive scaled-index intervals, where it is justified. The boundary Mellin conclusions use suitable small-value bounds and smoothing; they neither provide relative errors near zeros nor apply to the additional rapidly varying multiplier in (20).

The novelty claim is sufficiently delimited. I compared the submission with the exact published version of [2], which explicitly leaves unweighted pole-mass convergence and exterior control open, and with the related fixed-depth and large-heat branching papers in the supplied collection. Those results address different limits. I also checked Chauvin–Rouault [3], particularly Section 6.1, and Drmota [4], particularly Lemmas 18–19 and 24–28. The cascade connection, entire delay functions, coefficient-variation bounds, and the general coefficient-extraction strategy have substantial classical precedents. The manuscript credits these precedents and supplies the additional arguments for the complete theta iteration. The new mass control and preserved finite-depth order make this more than a renaming of the classical limiting functions.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement. The main results and proof strategy appear before the technical arguments, inherited inputs are explicitly identified, and the normalization identities are appropriately placed in an appendix. The distinction between the independent-sibling cascade $W$ and complementary-sibling cascade $V$ is particularly helpful. The abstract is informative, and the limitations are scientifically precise.

I reproduced all fourteen 60-digit Gamma-law normalization checks, obtaining the retained results. These support the density Jacobians, endpoint constants, and complex Mellin identity in Appendix A. They are not experiments establishing theta convergence; the new convergence theorems rest on the written proofs. The source comparison and interior theorem remain explicitly dependent on the versioned results and certificate in [2].

Minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- State explicitly near the introduction that the normalization gives $\mathbb E U_*^s=2\xi(2s)$, and briefly explain how the boundary problem arises in studying approximating Mellin transforms. This would make the connection to the selected problem self-contained.
- Cite Drmota's Lemmas 24, 27, and 28 alongside Lemma 25 when discussing the classical coefficient-limit strategy. The distinctive work here is establishing its hypotheses for the theta iteration, not the general passage from local transform convergence to coefficient asymptotics.
- In Section 6.1, use $q_{n-1,j}$ in the summation-by-parts explanation for $T_n=AG_{n-1}$, or explicitly state that the displayed calculation uses a generic ratio sequence. The present index shift is harmless but avoidable.

These are minor clarifications and do not undermine the central results or their stated scope.
