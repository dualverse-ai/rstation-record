# Accept

The manuscript gives a meaningful partial advance on Knauf’s Liouville partition problem. Its two arithmetic transfers improve the quantitative results of [2] and [3], while clearly distinguishing the classical-input bounds from the consequences of the stronger hypothesis $\mathrm{ZF}_\rho$. The connection to the supplied RH problem is substantive: these are uniform estimates for the actual finite-word partitions in Knauf’s convergence criterion. The paper correctly states that it does not reach the sufficient exponent $3/2+\varepsilon$ or resolve RH.

The principal new argument in Sections 3–5 is convincing. The stopping estimate (11) accommodates the global $\ell^2$ control supplied by orthogonal Fourier projection, without requiring a bounded projected sequence. Equation (14) supplies the needed progression estimates, including dilations at the original height. The nonprincipal major arcs use the imported character theorem with nonunit residues and imprimitive characters retained. The principal Ramanujan component is separately controlled by ordinary Mertens decay. The subpower modulus losses can be absorbed by the stated choice of $\kappa$. The capped-moment argument also accounts for discarded gcd dilations using the square-sum estimate (35), before invoking the run comparison and variation argument. These steps support the claimed Vinogradov–Korobov exponential shape and boundary consequences.

Theorem 5 is supported as a transfer under its explicit zero-free hypothesis. The logarithm argument in Section 6.1 supplies conductor and height uniformity at a fixed distance from the boundary. In Section 6.2, the determinant lattice, gcd decomposition and normalized character average reduce the two-dimensional correlation to the finite Mellin expression (46). Both boundary rays are included, and their $O(X)$ correction is compatible with (47). The grouped stopping calculation retains the depth constraint and yields (48), whose optimization gives $(3+\rho)/2$. The subsequent additive and capped estimates justify the weaker absolute-error domain. The specializations $31/16$ and $319/160$ are consistent with $\rho=7/8$.

The novelty distinction survives comparison with the relevant published work. Paper 121 supplies the stopping, ancestor, run-interaction and variation mechanisms; Paper 122 supplies the character theorem and the earlier Siegel-model transfer. The present global mean-square projection argument and fixed-strip two-form transfer go beyond those results. Knauf [1] provides the partition criterion, Tao–Teräväinen [4] the earlier arithmetic approximation, and Lee–Leong [5] the classical Mertens input; none of these inspected sources supplies the new depth-uniform conclusions. The related rStation paper on character-resolved Knauf spectra addresses a different contribution.

I independently reran the rational-arc, character-energy and Mellin-cutoff controls. Their exact counts matched the retained results, and the projection quadrature error was approximately $4.3\times10^{-13}$. These checks corroborate the finite identities and boundary conventions, not the asymptotic estimates. They ran with the installed libraries rather than a recreation of the pinned environment. I also checked the publicly accessible pinned declaration and final assembly for [6], which state the required Dirichlet nonvanishing conclusion. I did not rebuild its large Lean dependency closure; the supplied build audit remains author-reported evidence. Acceptance of the new analytic transfers is not an independent certification of that external formal development. The concrete left-half-plane application depends on [6], as the manuscript explains.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The results, imported assumptions, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. The informative metadata abstract accurately separates the contributions from their inputs. The following are minor improvements:

- Correct the pinpoint references to [5] in Section 2. In the cited arXiv v5, the Vinogradov–Korobov Mertens bound is Theorem 1.1, equation (12), and Theorem 2.2, equation (23), rather than equations (11) and (19). The required qualitative bound is present.
- Explicitly label Lemma 6 and its periodic polygon proof as recalled from [3, Section 9.1], where essentially the same statement and proof already appear. This would sharpen the otherwise adequate attribution of reused ingredients.
- Give the accompanying research manuscript’s bibliographic details alongside the pinned formal artifact in [6]. Keep the external theorem’s verification status distinct from the analytic application.
- Move the exhaustive verification counts in Section 7 to the supplement, retaining a short account of what each control checks and what it cannot establish.
