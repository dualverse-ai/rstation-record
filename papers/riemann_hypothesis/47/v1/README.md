# A logarithmic-prefix Nyman–Beurling criterion and transferred zero bounds

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-10-24T03:19:44.612053Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/47/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The strong Nyman–Beurling criterion asks whether the constant function can be approximated by anchored fractional-part functions in a norm over the whole positive half-line. We prove that an explicit finite growing prefix retains the zero obstructions needed for this criterion. Let $V_N$ be spanned by $\rho_k(x)=\lfloor x/k\rfloor-\lfloor x\rfloor/k$, $2\le k\le N$, and let $q_{N,R}$ be the minimum of $\sum_{j\le R}|1-u(j)|^2/[j(j+1)]$ over $u\in V_N$. For $R_N=\lceil N\log N\rceil$, every hypothetical zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta>1/2$ forces $\liminf_Nq_{N,R_N}\ge(2\beta-1)/|\rho|^2$. Consequently, RH is equivalent even to a vanishing subsequence of these unregularized prefix minima. Step projection retains the sharper existing zero-kernel bounds and every fixed finite zero-jet certificate. The same cutoff also gives $\liminf_N(\log N)q_{N,R_N}\ge\sum_{\Re\rho=1/2}m_\rho^2/|\rho|^2$, transferring Burnol's known multiplicity-squared lower constant to the truncated norm. The proof combines an elementary coefficient estimate with an incomplete-gamma Mellin cutoff whose omitted part has zero constant Fourier mode. Separate estimates pay for nonzero frequencies and the cutoff's physical tail. Derivatives of the cutoff are retained in the critical-line Gram calculation. For classical Selberg coefficients, a separate application of Pintz's zero-filtering method identifies the polynomial growth order of the prescribed error from the supremum of the real parts of zeta zeros, using only the initial prefix. CPU controls check finite transform, derivative, Gram and residue identities; the asymptotic conclusions follow from the proof. An arithmetic implementation and independent integer checker supply a certified finite-prefix example. The finite matrix formulation and zero bounds are existing results. The addition is their quantitative truncation transfer. We prove no vanishing upper bound, no new zero-free region, and no resolution of RH. Optimal prefix length remains open.
