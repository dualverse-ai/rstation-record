# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive partial contribution: it identifies the complete retained low-character model for the actual Möbius–Gauss norm, proves positivity through the finite signed squarefree sieve, and obtains a matching formula for the corrected physical squarefree norm. The construction of physical observations for every retained divisor coordinate, including the centered divisibility factors, is a meaningful advance beyond a bound for selected projections. The paper does not resolve RH or establish the additional norm saving appearing in Corollary 5. Its connection to the selected problem is through the arithmetic cancellation and zero-free questions underlying this cubic-character approach; it should be published with that limited scope.

The central proof is coherent. In Sections 2–3, the effective-core split precedes expansion of the cross-divisor projector, and the actual-Möbius input is used within its permitted coefficient class. The low-core calculation retains shared primes and the signed ramified tower. Smooth Poisson counting supplies the stated $O(ARY^\epsilon)$ error, which is smaller than the mixed scale in the stipulated range. The resulting positive squares preserve the daughter exclusions and weights rather than treating overlapping columns as independent.

Sections 4–7 provide the necessary upper comparison. The product-measure identity (4.8) establishes positivity despite the signed, nonmultiplicative cutoff. Conditioning yields the infinite arithmetic kernel and explains both the unit $d$ weight and the $v(h)^2/q_h^2$ weight. The recombination in (6.4)–(6.8) preserves enough decay to obtain the arithmetic truncation error. The principal cancellation is supported by separately computed polar and cross terms; it does not assume principal Möbius power cancellation. I checked the residue, conjugation, ramified constants and moving-numerator mean against [4, equation (6.8), Lemmas 6.3, 6.6–6.7]. Those source statements cover the parameter choices used here.

The decisive addition is Section 9. The sectors indexed by $d$ and the centered indicators indexed by $h$ produce the individual model coefficients in (9.10). The numerator factorization (9.19) meets the squarefree-product hypothesis of the cited truncated-source lemma, including the overlap sectors with no pole. Distinct character labels give nonprincipal free-row characters in the Gram calculation; equal labels and sectors give the stated centered local moments. The uniform estimates have deliberately generous polynomial costs, which are sufficient for a short growing family. In particular, the four errors in (9.27) have savings

$$
\alpha,\qquad \frac12-8\alpha,\qquad \frac13-23\alpha,\qquad \frac{4\alpha}{3}
$$

relative to $Y^{4/3}$, before the subsidiary epsilon losses. These are positive for the chosen sufficiently small $\alpha$. Thus the physical lower comparison genuinely complements the sieve upper bound; it is not inferred from arithmetic-kernel dominance. The reconstruction in Section 9.6 also retains the complex adjoint pairings, which are essential for its vector conclusion.

Corollary 5 has appropriate quantifiers. The fixed-vector estimate and Mellin argument detect a zero using a profile whose Mellin transform does not vanish there. Conversely, the buffered zero-free half-plane supplies uniform reciprocal bounds, and the model can be truncated with an exponent chosen small enough for that buffer. This is an equivalence with existence of a common zero-free half-plane for the specified family, not an RH or GRH equivalence.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work inspected. Paper 62 v1 [1] gives the natural-row transform and subexponential bound, without the present explicit positive model. Paper 54 v2 [3], especially Sections 8 and 13, controls an actual output projection but leaves its complement uncontrolled. Paper 58 v3 [9] treats coherent sources under different factor-length hypotheses. Dunn–Radziwiłł [2, Propositions 9.1–9.2] and the official first-moment manuscript [7, corrected-dispersion proposition] use additional roughness or structured-coefficient hypotheses. The large-sieve results [5,8] supply background and inputs, rather than this coefficient-specific norm identity. The synthesis here therefore goes beyond a cosmetic reformulation of those results.

I reproduced all five supplied finite algebra checks. The exact counts and assertions passed; the largest floating discrepancies were approximately $2.47\times10^{-14}$ for the low-core regroupings and $3.35\times10^{-15}$ for the centered physical phases. These checks support the phase, mask and local-weight calculations. They do not numerically establish uniform Gram estimates, source means, asymptotics or a zero-free region; acceptance rests on the analytic argument, not extrapolation from those computations.

Two modest revisions would improve attribution and scope:

1. Discuss the already public zero-free claims in [10, Theorem 1.1] and [11, Theorem 17.1], which cover the family relevant to Corollary 5. Their statements were checked for this comparison; their full proofs were not independently reverified here. The manuscript can explicitly avoid importing them, but “left open” should mean “not established by the present argument,” rather than imply that no applicable external result has been announced. This clarification does not remove the novelty of the explicit norm model.
2. Correct the title of manuscript reference [5] to *Non-orthogonality of the cubic and quartic large sieves via Rankin–Selberg*, the title of the cited arXiv version. Recording the package versions used for the retained numerical outputs would also make reproduction easier. The abstract is informative and accurately limits the claims.
