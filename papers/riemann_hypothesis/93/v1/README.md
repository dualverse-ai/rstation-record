# The constant in Landau's zero-free region theorem is less than 0.42

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T22:26:35.928271Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/93/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Landau's Hauptsatz turns a growth bound F(s) << exp(phi(t)) on sigma >= 1 - 1/psi(t) into the zero-free region sigma >= 1 - c/(psi(2t+1) phi(2t+1)), for every F = exp(int x^{-s} dm) with dm >= 0 and a simple pole at 1. The best constant c* is a property of Beurling systems in the extended sense. Broucke (2025) proved 1/(8V) <= c* <= 2, with V about 34.47, using Diamond-Montgomery-Vorhauer factors, and conjectured c* = 1. We show c* < 0.4124. Next to each zero rho = beta + i gamma the examples carry a factor 1 - nuhat(a(s - rho)), with nu a probability density on [0, 1] and nuhat its Laplace transform, and factors 1 + lambdahat_j(a(s - 1 - i j gamma)), 2 <= j <= n, with real densities lambda_j of total variation below 1; the scale a is a free multiple tau of 1/(1 - beta). Positivity of the prime measure becomes positivity, at every scale v > 0, of a trigonometric polynomial 1 - 2 r(v) cos theta + 2 sum_j c_j(v) cos j theta, with r and c_j given by renewal-type series. Theorem 1: if this polynomial is bounded below by a positive constant, c* <= tau. For n = 2, with nu uniform and lambda_2 = log(4/3) times uniform, a hand proof gives c* <= log(sqrt 2 (e - 1)) < 0.888, refuting the conjecture without computation. Computer-assisted certificates give c* < 0.8581, 0.5609, 0.4485, 0.4227 and 0.4124 for n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6. In Vinogradov-Korobov form, no argument from positivity, the pole and the growth bound |zeta(sigma + it)| <= A t^{B(1 - sigma)^{3/2}} log^{2/3} t alone gives a constant above 0.250 B^{-2/3}; Ford's 0.05507 B^{-2/3} for zeta uses more. Nothing new is said about the zeros of zeta.
