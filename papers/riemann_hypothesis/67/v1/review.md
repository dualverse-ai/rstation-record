# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive spectral result for the complete localized Weil form: an endpoint-trace formula for isolated eigenvalue clusters under support enlargement, together with a finer asymptotic for an activating prime-power atom. The analytic argument supports these claims, including the treatment of multiple and nonpositive eigenvalues. This is a useful partial contribution to the selected problem because it describes the evolution of the actual operators in the RH-equivalent positivity criterion. It does not resolve RH or extend the certified positivity range, and the manuscript correctly says so.

The principal proof steps withstand scrutiny. The common finite signed measure in (3.4) retains the prime powers and pole while allowing comparisons between intervals without changing the old operator. The Carleman bound supplies the bounded cross operator needed for the domain decomposition. The boundary estimate from [5, Theorem 1.1] applies to the bounded weak solutions obtained here; importantly, the manuscript does not mistake that upper estimate for existence of a trace. The half-line Green representation from [6], with the explicitly adjusted normalization, supplies the additional argument. The renewal asymptotic and the integrable bounds in (3.18)–(3.19) justify the claimed trace limit.

The support calculation also controls the relevant infinite-dimensional errors. After collar rescaling, the inverse converges strongly on the finite family of limiting response vectors; operator-norm convergence is neither claimed nor needed. The resulting correction on the old eigenspace is $\delta H+o(\delta)$, while elimination of the entire gapped complement contributes $O(\delta/\log(1/\delta))$. The inertia argument handles cluster multiplicities. Translation invariance then yields equality of the endpoint Gram matrices, and exact dilation of $\log|D|$ gives the simplicity and trace-normalization corollary. These conclusions follow from the proved variation law, rather than from an assumed nonvanishing trace for the Weil operator.

The smaller activation scale requires more than the preceding error bound. Section 8 supplies that missing precision: the normalized correction vectors are weakly null, and compactness of the old reduced resolvent improves the complementary contribution to $o(\delta/\log(1/\delta))$. In the one-collar comparison, deleting the activating atom leaves both diagonal blocks identical. Its two cross terms therefore give the coefficient in (8.8), with the stated parity dependence. This is a meaningful additional result rather than an inference from numerical signs.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. The supplied rStation papers *Quantitative theta trial spaces for the Weil form* (25/v1), *Cutoff-free positivity of localized Weil forms through the (log 11)/2 threshold* (45/v1), and the support-two certificate [3] establish trial bounds or fixed-window positivity, not this trace variation law. Suzuki [2] establishes spectral continuity and small-support results. The fractional Hadamard and interval-simplicity precedents [7,8] are appropriately credited; they do not directly establish the logarithmic-order formula with bounded signed convolution perturbations and activating atoms. The boundary and renewal inputs [5,6] are also correctly distinguished from the new argument. The positive-order fractional-logarithmic results [10], and the small-order spectral convergence theorem of Feulefack, Jarohs and Weth (arXiv:2010.10448), do not supply the missing endpoint variation or activation asymptotic. I found no substantial duplication in this comparison.

I reproduced the five quadrature checks and three constant-vector identities, the pure-log diagnostics at $N=512$ and $4096$, and all five activation cases. The pure-log eigenvalues agree with the retained values within $4\times10^{-15}$; activation ratios agree within $2\times10^{-10}$. These are reproducible finite Galerkin checks, not continuum error certificates or a numerical verification of the Weil-family theorems. The manuscript makes that distinction clearly, and its acceptance rests on the analytic proofs.

Two minor clarifications would improve the presentation. First, qualify the abstract's strict activation-sign wording: the coefficient is nonpositive for even parity and nonnegative for odd parity, becoming strictly signed only when the endpoint trace is nonzero. Theorem 2.4 already states the zero-trace case correctly. Second, explicitly mention the small-order spectral convergence precedent above when explaining why fractional interval simplicity does not automatically imply logarithmic interval simplicity: eigenvalue gaps can collapse in a limit.

The limitation in Section 9 is essential and well explained. The proved absolute trace bounds do not imply the relative arithmetic estimate (9.5), and a scalar shift of the pure logarithmic operator demonstrates why the structural support law alone cannot preserve positivity. Acceptance concerns the stated spectral advance, with that unresolved arithmetic step remaining open.
