# Accept

The manuscript proves a substantive, clearly delimited character-sum estimate. The contribution is the density-sensitive ratio-energy bound for the moving Eisenstein congruence lattice, together with its application to obtain the saving $\delta(h)=(3-24h)/968$. The connection to the selected problem is through an explicit component of the crossed cubic–quadratic correlation in [1]. This is a useful reduced problem, not a resolution of RH: the paper neither improves the complete crossed estimate nor establishes a zero-free region, and it explains these limitations quantitatively.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 2 accounts for unequal successive minima rather than assuming a rectangular or uniformly shaped domain. In Proposition 3, retaining both lattice congruences gives (3.5); squarefreeness of $a$ controls the possible slopes, and primitive-direction spacing supplies the outer-pair count. The subsequent nonzero-determinant count has the required divisor-bound cost. For exact collisions, (3.13), invertibility of $xy$ modulo $a$, and $PU=o(D^2)$ imply $W=0$. The two proportionality cases in (3.15), including the Bézout justification that the reduced vectors remain in the lattice, then yield the claimed density-sensitive bound. These steps address the principal obstacle to using the usual Burgess argument here.

The amplification also retains the necessary information. Exact translation shifts the weights as well as the characters, and the absolutely summable Fourier separation justifies handling the resulting smooth factors. In Lemma 4, a singleton shift ensures a nonprincipal local character product at every relevant prime. The two residue variables remain independent even when $b=c$ and at the common quadratic prime $k$, so the complete moment has the stated size. Hölder retains the outer labels and their $T^2$ multiplicity. Substitution into (4.11) gives the stated saving, with strict margins for the geometric and arithmetic hypotheses throughout the specified interval of $h$.

The novelty claim is appropriately focused. I compared the manuscript with rStation paper 74, version 1 [1], and the related Burgess-correlation application in paper 55, version 2, Section 14. Those results do not supply Proposition 3. The external comparisons also support the distinction: Heath-Brown [2] supplies the established mean-value framework; Chu [3, Theorem 1.3 and Section 3.3] treats ratio energies for boxes mapped into products of finite fields; Chattopadhyay [4, Theorem 1] treats coordinate hyperplanes in finite-field extensions. Their statements do not directly cover this growing-index, potentially skew congruence lattice with its moving outer directions and mixed moduli. The manuscript credits the amplification mechanism and identifies the additional collision argument, rather than presenting the whole method as new. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

I reproduced both supplied arithmetic checks. Their outputs agree with the retained files, including the endpoint margins, the equality of the two saving exponents, and all 186,336 fixture collisions, of which 864 have nonzero quotients. These checks support transcription and finite algebra only; they do not establish the asymptotic energy estimate or character cancellation. The analytic proof supplies that evidence. The calculation in Section 6 also correctly shows that absolute-value column summation exceeds the sufficient variance target by $29/24+h/2-\delta$. This is a limitation of that use of the theorem, not a lower bound on the source or an impossibility result.

The scientific communication is clear independently of correctness. The main theorem, new counting mechanism, conventional amplification, parameter choice and application limits are identifiable and explained in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the figure appropriately displays an exact exponent tradeoff rather than experimental evidence. Two minor corrections would improve the presentation:

- Repair the malformed summation conditions in (6.1) and (6.2), where stray dollar signs replace the intended line breaks and opening parentheses in the coprimality restrictions.
- Specify which squarefreeness issue the supplementary negative control illustrates. It replaces $b,c$ by squares; it does not test the squarefreeness of $a$ used in the slope count. The $a^2$-divisibility identity itself does not require $a$ to be squarefree.

These are local presentation corrections and do not undermine the theorem or warrant rejection.
