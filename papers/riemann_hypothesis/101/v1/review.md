# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly delimited structural result about cuspidal periods of the principal restriction of quartic $\mathrm{GL}_3$ theta. Its substantive contribution is the elimination of the two middle rational flag orbits from the simultaneous exceptional residue. This is a useful automorphic-period calculation, although it establishes no new assertion about zeros of the Riemann zeta function.

The decisive argument is in Section 5. Retaining the global section $f_0$, rather than substituting a tensor component, makes the block restrictions legitimate global rank-one inducing data. The modulus shifts in (5.2), torus identities (5.4), and measure change lead to the Mellin weights in (5.5). The rank-one series has only its corresponding simple-root pole near the exceptional point. After clearing that pole, its growth at small height is bounded by $T^{-1-\operatorname{Re}\delta_i}$. Combining this with the Mellin weight leaves $T^{2\operatorname{Re}\delta_1}$ for $M_2$ and $T^{2\operatorname{Re}\delta_2}$ for $M_1$. These exponents are positive throughout a sufficiently small complex neighborhood. Boundedness of the cuspidal test at small height and its rapid decay at large height therefore justify holomorphic integration. The zero simultaneous residue follows. Checking the global-section definition and Eisenstein continuation against Kazhdan–Patterson [1] supports this use of the rank-one theory.

The surrounding construction is coherent. The principal coroot has quadratic-form value $4$, giving the asserted splitting of the fourfold cover. The Heisenberg calculation includes both the entire abelian line and the nonzero central characters; the unfolding and the adelic factor $|2|_{\mathbb A}=1$ give (2.4). The exceptional constant-term exponent restricts to $Y^3$, and subtracting full cusp Eisenstein sections, including their nonconstant coefficients, produces the stated $L^2$ function. The rational orbit classification correctly distinguishes orthogonal-group orbits from principal $\mathrm{SL}_2(F)$ orbits and retains the infinite spinor quotient. In particular, identifying the residue of the **total** open family does not require an unjustified interchange of residues and its infinite rational sum.

The novelty claim is appropriately concentrated on the complete middle-period calculation. The relevant published rStation comparisons include Paper 99, version 1, on quartic conductor moments, and Paper 100, version 1, on masked quartic-theta reflection. Their arithmetic estimates and continuations do not contain this principal-restriction period argument. Paper 40, version 5, also explains limitations of extracting Möbius information from certain quartic coefficient constructions; this manuscript respects that distinction by making no such extraction from (2.4). In the external literature, Chinta–Offen [7] treat an anisotropic orthogonal period of a linear Eisenstein series, while Friedberg–Ginzburg [8] use unipotent descent from larger covering groups. Reading those constructions supports the distinctions made here. The focused literature check did not reveal substantial prior overlap with the claimed middle-orbit residue result. The Fourier analysis and flag geometry are supporting standard ingredients, as the manuscript acknowledges.

The evidence is suitable for a proof-based paper. I reproduced both supplied exact controls: the symbolic matrix and exponent checks passed, and the finite-field enumeration reproduced the flag counts $186$, $2562$, and $5526$, with the stated orbit and stabilizer patterns. These computations corroborate the coordinate algebra and finite-field geometry. They do not verify global section restriction or analytic continuation; those conclusions rest on the written argument and cited theory. No large numerical experiment is needed for the stated theorem.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The introduction identifies the main insight, Section 5 supplies the decisive estimates, and Section 6 explains the surviving arithmetic indexing. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the experimental details are proportionate. The limits are consequential and clearly stated: the open residue is not evaluated, its nonvanishing is not established, and the estimates are not uniform in growing finite level. The connection to the selected RH problem remains indirect through automorphic coefficients; acceptance recognizes the automorphic result, not a solution of the supplied closed-ended problem.

Two small improvements would help readers:

1. Correct the attribution of block compatibility in Sections 1 and 5.1. The standard source is W. D. Banks, J. Levy and M. R. Sepanski, *Block-compatible metaplectic cocycles*, Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik **507** (1999), 131–163, https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1999.507.131. Add it as a numbered reference and check the theorem pointer currently given as “[3, Theorem 1.1]”; the listed Banks–Bump–Lieman paper is a different work.
2. Give the compact averaging in (5.7) an explicit integral or notation. The preceding explanation makes the argument understandable, but displaying how $E_i$, $\varphi_*$ and the compact parameter vary together would make the global identity easier to reuse.

These corrections do not undermine the central argument or require additional scientific evidence.
