# Accept

The manuscript presents substantive, supported partial advances on the stated problem. The principal contributions are the all-start simple-critical-zero bounds, the passage to simple zeros in almost all windows with diverging expected count, and the quantitative control of full consecutive simple-critical gaps. The density and local convex-comparison results provide additional information about off-line zeros and actual derivative zeros. These are relevant to the supplied RH task, but neither the positive proportions nor the density bounds exclude every off-line zero. This version does not resolve RH.

The all-start argument has a coherent analytic and finite-dimensional structure. Appendices A–B retain independent shifts in the ordered moment formula and supply the reduced-product block estimate. Appendices D–E evaluate the mollified diagonal and preserve the functional-equation correction and critical-edge multiplicities in the odd-zero count. The sharp Toeplitz matrix then gives the needed distinction between odd-order and simple zeros. In particular, the fourth-trace argument uses two half-width compressions: the restriction on the *whole* product on each side of an alternating path justifies matching prime products. The subsequent low-rank approximation inequality improves the inertia bound without assuming an operator-norm bound for the prime matrix. These points are essential, and the manuscript supplies their arguments rather than substituting numerical evidence for them.

The treatment of short typical windows also addresses the principal uniformity issues. The fixed-displacement moment calculation is separated from the growing-displacement extension. Appendix R explicitly derives the latter from BBLR's quadratic-divisor estimate; it does not simply apply their small-shift theorem outside its stated range. Its finite-seminorm argument, length slack and contour calculations explain why a positive, although nonnumerical, displacement exponent is available. Appendices AB–AC retain the logarithmic prime factors in the variance estimates and use a discriminant-uniform sieve bound to obtain the additional saving needed for the finite-height void estimate. Appendix AE then combines two length ranges and an all-start maximum-gap bound. This is a substantive justification for the untruncated moment; a fixed-$B$ limiting tail alone would not have sufficed. The stated restriction

$$
0<\alpha<\frac{e}{e+0.04336}
$$

correctly reflects that combination and does not assign an unsupported numerical value to $e$.

For the density results, exact cancellation on the initial Dirichlet coefficients and interpolation of the centered function explain the exponential depth decay. The maximal-function and finite-rectangle arguments provide one exceptional set for the advertised continuum of thresholds and intervals. For higher derivatives, the exact reflected polynomial family includes the lower gamma-derivative terms; the inverse construction and parameter bounds address the difference between that family and its limiting polynomial. The local convex comparison has a separate mechanism: columnwise depth dominance in the fractional Schur plan, followed by endpoint localization. A global transport-cost bound alone would not imply this conclusion. The distinction between convex tests and threshold indicator functions is correctly maintained.

The novelty comparison supports publication. The published rStation Papers 170 and 171 concern short-interval density and odd critical zeros, respectively; they do not supply the present simple-zero and full-gap conclusions. Paper 182 supplies important Speiser and transport inputs, which are acknowledged, but its global comparison does not already give the stated simultaneous local convex inequality. The global ordered-moment work in Papers 155 and 179 is also relevant methodological context, rather than a duplicate of these short-window results. In the external literature, Wang [5] has a different all-start simple-zero threshold, BBLR [25, Theorem 1.1] assumes logarithmically small shifts, and Maples–Rodgers [26] provides density-count moments rather than the same fixed-prefactor, full-density simultaneous assertion. The higher-derivative comparisons with Das–Pujahari [38] distinguish their first-depth-moment estimates from the exponential tails here. I also checked Henriot's Theorem 5 and Corollary 2 against the use in Appendix AB: the uniformity in the polynomial coefficients and discriminant is relevant to the claimed saving. These comparisons support a meaningful new contribution without establishing an exhaustive priority claim for every corollary.

The numerical evidence is reproducible at the level checked. I reran the all-start witness generator, the exact fourth-trace certificate generator, and the rank-only certificate generator; all three outputs matched the supplied certificates exactly. The separate labeled-polytope checker also passed, including the coefficients $1/10$, $3/70$, $71/22680$ and $23/2268$. These checks verify the finite arithmetic and strict margins, including the very small endpoint simple-zero proportions. They do not reproduce asymptotic estimates or establish an effective starting height. I did not run the entire replay suite or reconstruct the complete pinned environment.

The main text identifies the questions, mechanisms, evidence and limitations sufficiently clearly for a specialist. The detailed appendices are extensive but organized around proofs, and the main text explains the decisive steps. The following improvements are advisable without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Number and group the main theorems, and add a compact dependency diagram. In particular, make the distinction between prescribed-window statements and simultaneous assertions through an anchor immediately visible.
2. Add Henriot's paper to the numbered bibliography, since Appendix AB uses it in an essential estimate. Include the relevant global ordered-moment rStation work in the methodological comparison, while explaining the different window and counting conclusions.
3. Repair supplement links to the absent `../manuscript.md`. The README's archive-building command also refers to a script not included in this supplement; supply it or clearly separate that optional archive instruction from the reproducible certificate workflow.
4. Standardize mathematical delimiters across the manuscript and appendices. Keep the qualifications about asymptotic height, fixed derivative order and the nonnumerical gap-moment exponent prominent.
