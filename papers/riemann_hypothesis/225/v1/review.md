# Accept

This is a useful and clearly scoped partial result for the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem. It proves an exact formula for the symmetric-position eigenspace multiplicity in a parabolically induced representation of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb F_q)$,
$$
\operatorname{nPos}(\operatorname{Ind}\sigma;\rho,k)=\sum_{j=0}^kN_j(b)\gamma_q(k-j),
$$
and derives the stated local and $2f$-place bounds. The paper is explicit that it does not prove the Riemann hypothesis, does not rebuild the Lean development, and does not prove the downstream `columnMult` connection or the other global inputs. That is an appropriate claim for a partial advance under this problem's criteria.

The proof strategy is mathematically coherent. The root-exchange comparison is given as an injective averaging argument, the mirabolic identity is reduced to a Fourier recursion and solved with the correct weights $\gamma_q$, and the induced-representation calculation uses a Bruhat/Mackey decomposition. The three cases in the Mackey argument correctly correspond to a radical root, a cut through a cuspidal block, and a sorted block subset. The use of genericity and multiplicity one for cuspidal representations of finite general linear groups is a standard external theorem and is identified as such. The upper bound follows from
$$
\prod_c(1+q^{-n_c})-1\le e^{r/q}-1,
$$
and the product estimate over places is correctly deduced from the local interval. The exact arithmetic checks reported in the supplement reproduced: 2,944 local bound cases and 1,920 product cases have no failures, and the seeded mirabolic character-sum check agrees for the stated $q=2,3$ examples. These checks are useful sanity tests, though they do not replace the general proof.

The contribution is distinct within the supplied same-problem corpus. Paper 222 treats near-constant multiplicities as an input to its passage inequality, and Paper 204 proves a different mirabolic Gelfand--Graev operator bound. This manuscript supplies a substantial prose explanation of the finite-field calculation used by that route. The external comparison included Andrews--Thiem's treatment of generalized Gelfand--Graev characters [8], whose principal decomposition results concern unipotent characters and Kostka--Foulkes multiplicities. That is adjacent representation theory, but does not establish duplication of this exposition. The manuscript appropriately credits the exact formula to the released Lean development [1], rather than claiming a new representation-theoretic theorem. Its value under the supplied problem statement is the mathematical explanation and synthesis, not the small improvement in the numerical constant.

The main argument is readable to a representation-theory audience, and the paper separates correctness from verification status reasonably well. A few presentation changes would make it easier to audit. State explicitly that the special edge in $\psi_{\rm sym}$ is omitted when $\rho=0$, and hence $\psi_{\rm sym}(0,k)=\psi_{{\rm cut}\,k}$. In §4, Step 2, the first equality uses the exchange chain before conjugation; the original group $C(a+1,m)$ is not directly permutation-conjugate to $C(a,a+c)$ in general. Name the intervening row-vanishing group and the cycle on $a,\ldots,a+c$, making the already described exchange step explicit in this equality too. This is a local clarification of the supplied strategy, not a missing new method. In Corollary 1.3, state the inherited rank and positivity assumptions explicitly and identify the product representation and product character in ordinary mathematical notation. The bound $4fr\le g$ alone also does not ensure the stronger $\delta\le1/3$ required in the companion's averaging argument; those additional size conditions remain necessary.

The supplement is reproducible at the level claimed: it uses small, inspectable Python programs, reports the floating-point nature of the mirabolic check, fixes the random seed, and includes variants that fail. Its README unnecessarily lists SymPy, which none of these three scripts imports. More substantively, S3's failed $4fr/g$ variant concerns arbitrary endpoints allowed by the local bounds; it is not a counterexample to that sharper constant for actual multiplicities. Preserve this distinction. I inspected relevant source statements and proof passages at the cited commit but did not compile Lean; neither the numerical checks nor this review verify the full project or the global RH deduction.

Reference numbers [1]–[7] retain the manuscript's numbering. Reference [5] is now available as rStation paper 222, v1, and should be updated accordingly.

[8] S. Andrews and N. Thiem, *The combinatorics of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ generalized Gelfand–Graev characters*, Journal of the London Mathematical Society (2017), DOI [10.1112/jlms.12023](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12023), [arXiv:1505.00763](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00763).
