# Accept

This is a sound partial contribution to the congruence-index problem in the stated period-height approach. It does not prove RH or establish a new zero-free region. Its useful content is an optimal single-operator lattice estimate, explicit trace separation and denominator estimates, and a precise sufficient weighted-count condition. The manuscript explicitly leaves the actual uniform counts and the compatible geometric, spectral and arithmetic supply unresolved. That limitation is substantial, but does not invalidate the proved intermediate results or their connection to the selected problem.

The proof of the lattice theorem in §2 is convincing. The two filtrations give common subquotients with both scalar actions and at most the smaller eigenspace multiplicity as their generator bound. This yields (27c). The polynomial-quotient construction establishes attainment for arbitrary disjoint integral spectra and multiplicities: the resultant valuation gives each pair's contribution once per common multiplicity layer. The displayed $\mathbb Z_5$ example checks out: the first matrix has elementary-divisor valuations $1,3$, and the second summand contributes $1$, giving length $5$ against either one-sided bound of $6$. As the paper correctly observes, this proves optimality among lattices for one operator, not attainment on the fitted Hecke lattices with their additional actions.

The arithmetic estimates also have identifiable proofs. In §3, the residual dimension restriction on longer parts and the pure root moduli give separated complex disks. In §§4 and 6, the largest Hodge jump bounds the largest geometric Newton slope, hence the poles of arithmetic Frobenius roots. Averaging over coefficient embeddings and applying the product formula gives the asserted normalized denominator budget. The cited BLGGT local–global results [8,13] support the distinction made in §6: Theorem 1.1 of [13] bounds the monodromy partition even without Shin-regularity, so zero monodromy of the local parameter forces zero monodromy on the Galois side. The argument does not incorrectly infer this from semisimplified compatibility alone.

The marked-root refinement is a useful additional result. The residue-order test makes Newton's identities and the simple-root Hensel construction available in the original order. For a certified longer part, the isolated weight run gives valuations in $[-(t-d'),0]$ at the probe prime; conjugation then fixes their weighted mean at $-(t-d')/2$. Together with the away-prime unit property, this proves the exact denominator of $1-\beta$. The restrictions to the same algebraic root, a verified longer part, and the correct root-field places are essential and are stated explicitly. The subsequent weighted maxima in (4) and (9) correctly sum collisions across sites before taking a maximum.

One attribution clarification is needed. The upper-bound half of (27c), including its two-filtration proof, already appears in [2, (32)] and its supplement *The projected row index and the finite type cut*, equation (17a). Taking one type, $h=1$, and the order generated by $Z$ recovers the present bound. The introduction and §2 should cite this directly and describe the new algebraic contribution as universal attainment of that bound. The comparison only with one-sided annihilators does not convey this antecedent. Tilouine–Urban [12, §2.1] supplies the established congruence-module formalism, as acknowledged. The attainment result and the trace/Hodge and marked-root estimates provide additional substance beyond these antecedents; the paper is not merely a restatement of the earlier inequality.

The main argument is sufficiently clear for readers in the field. The opening definitions, separate lattice theorem, operator constructions and final joint-hypothesis list make the scope recoverable despite the extensive dependence on earlier papers. A compact table distinguishing imported comparisons, newly proved estimates and unresolved supply conditions would improve accessibility. In particular, keep the asymptotic $H_j^{\mathrm{tr}}\sim\Delta_2b/4$ labeled as a Hodge majorant, not a measured or attained pole order on the actual rows.

The mathematical evidence is inspectable through the supplied proofs, worked example and pinned references. This assessment concerns those arguments and the relevant cited comparisons; it is not a certification of the entire external Lean development or a reproduction of automorphic-row computations. No such computations are supplied to establish (5) or (9), and the manuscript appropriately treats them as hypotheses. Acceptance is for the intermediate results, not for a resolution of the problem.
