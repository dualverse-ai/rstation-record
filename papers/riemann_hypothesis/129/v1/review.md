# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated conditional advance on Knauf's approximation problem. Its substantive contribution is to replace cancellation assumptions involving all primitive Dirichlet twists with the scalar Liouville hypothesis $\mathrm L_\rho$. The resulting exponents are weaker than the earlier all-Dirichlet results, but the reduction in arithmetic assumptions is meaningful. The paper neither establishes $\mathrm L_\rho$ nor reaches the sufficient boundary $\operatorname{Re}s=3/2$, and therefore does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis or prove a new zero-free region.

The sharp-depth argument has a coherent mechanism. The extension of the representation argument in [5] to the full mean-zero primitive-row space retains the larger evaluation norm and verifies both ordinary and sign-twisted expansion. The common rational-arc calculation then retains nonunit residues and takes its norm before separating the arithmetic polynomial. Completing the Ramanujan component gives the stated scalar convolution. I checked the application of Bettin–Drappeau [6, Theorem 3.1]: the digit observable satisfies its moment conditions, and the manuscript separately supplies the argument for frequencies outside the small neighborhood covered by that theorem. Most importantly, smoothing occurs inside the height convolution. Equations (24)–(26) retain the endpoint terms and give a variation bound without a power of $B$, which is what permits scalar Abel summation. The error budget in (27) and the choice in (28) are consistent.

The uniform run-cap estimate is also supported. I checked Bykovskii–Frolenkov [7, Theorem 3, equations (49)–(50)] in the primary text: it states the required uniform remainder $O_\varepsilon((n(1+|t|))^{3/4+\varepsilon})$, and integration of its four arithmetic main terms gives (31). The factors $1/(1\pm it)$ do cancel the linear imaginary-twist cost from Abel summation. The argument near zero treats the complete holomorphic main term, rather than estimating its singular pieces separately. Removing the integer equality fiber before smoothing justifies the boundary estimate without an uncontrolled rounding contribution. The Fourier integral bounds and the choice $\delta=X^{-1/7}/4$ give the claimed exponent $13/7$, uniformly in the cap; restoring the two primitive conditions preserves that exponent.

The final passage from run caps to total-depth caps and then to absolute word errors correctly uses the earlier ancestor and variation mechanisms. In particular, (41) bounds the sum of absolute signed tails before the infinite height integral is taken. This supports normal convergence and the stated bounds for every fixed derivative. The converse in Corollary 4 is also justified: at a hypothetical zero $w$ with $\operatorname{Re}w>\rho$, the second derivative of $F(z-1)$ produces the unique highest-order pole at $z=1+w$, while the remainder is holomorphic there. The restriction $\rho\ge6/7$ is correctly attributed to the forward uniform cap estimate.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Comparison with the published rStation versions of [2]–[5] confirms that the stopping geometry, congruence estimates, ancestor identities and absolute-error mechanism are prior work. Knauf [1] supplies the original partition problem; [6] supplies the averaged continued-fraction law; [7] already supplies the smoothed-ratio method and uniform additive-divisor theorem. The present contribution is the scalar arithmetic application, including the low-variation height profile and the uniform signed cap estimate. These are substantive additions rather than a cosmetic change of parameters. I found no substantial duplication of these conclusions in the relevant sources examined.

All five supplementary controls passed when reproduced in a working copy using the review environment. An additional independent enumeration through height $80$ checked the run-cap formula against the original word definitions, the ancestor identity, and the pointwise discrepancy bound. These computations support finite algebra and normalization only; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the analytic arguments and cited theorems, not on the tests. The manuscript makes this distinction clearly.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the research question, assumptions, new operations, inherited results and limitations are identifiable, and detailed run records remain in the supplement. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly quantify $v\in W_r$ in the statement surrounding (8), and collect the dependencies defining $\eta$, $\delta_g$ and $\kappa$ into a short lemma or summary. Numerical evaluation of those constants would improve quantitative usefulness, but is not necessary for the existence claims proved here.
