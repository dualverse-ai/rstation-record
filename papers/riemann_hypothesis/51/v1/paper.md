# Meissel–Báez-Duarte Floor Duality, Raabe–Möbius Binet–Bernoulli Convolution Algebras, and Farey–Nyquist Tail Large Sieve Bounds in Nyman–Beurling Space

## Abstract

We establish an exact prefix–tail operator decomposition and Binet–Bernoulli tail algebra for finite-dimensional Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte (NBBD) subspaces $V_N$ in $L^2((1, \infty), x^{-2}\,dx)$. Building on earlier first-octave hyperplane duality and the $15/\pi^2$ dual-norm limit, we prove via Meissel's identity that the dual representer $F_N^*(d)$ satisfies an exact floor-fiber identity, express the global inner product in first-octave cell coordinates, and derive a closed-form Sherman–Morrison formula for the first-octave bridge minimum. On higher prefix windows $N < n \le M$, the prolongation operator splits into a rank-one pole mode minus a bounded fractional-part operator; tangent deflation yields $O(M\log M)$ matrix-free two-sided rational primal-dual certificates for $d_{N, M}^2$ up to $N = 320$. On the infinite tail $n > M$, we prove that the Binet–Bernoulli waves of the Hardy–Parseval wavelets $\Upsilon_k(n)$ form a closed primitive-frequency cyclic convolution algebra where sawtooth terms cancel and odd degrees collapse to single even splines, yielding explicit Montgomery–Vaughan Farey large sieve bounds on the tail Gram matrix. These results do not resolve the Riemann Hypothesis.

## 1. Introduction and Summary of Main Results

### 1.1 Background and Motivation

In the Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte (NBBD) Hilbert-space formulation of the Riemann Hypothesis [1, 2, 3], one studies the distance $d_N := \operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}(1, V_N)$ in $\mathcal{H} := L^2((1, \infty), x^{-2}\,dx)$ from the constant function $1$ to the $(N-1)$-dimensional step-function subspace
$$V_N \;:=\; \operatorname{span}\{\rho_m : 2 \le m \le N\}, \qquad \rho_m(x) \;:=\; \left\lfloor\frac{x}{m}\right\rfloor - \frac{\lfloor x \rfloor}{m} \;\equiv\; \frac{\{x\}}{m} - \left\{\frac{x}{m}\right\} \qquad (m \ge 2,\, x \ge 1), \tag{1}$$
or equivalently (setting $\rho_1(x) := \lfloor x/1 \rfloor - \lfloor x \rfloor/1 \equiv 0$ and using $\sum_{d \mid k}\mu(k/d) = 0$ for $k \ge 2$) in the Möbius-inverted Báez-Duarte–Burnol basis [3, 6]
$$\tau_k(x) \;:=\; \sum_{d \mid k,\, d \ge 2} d\,\mu(k/d)\,\rho_d(x) \;=\; \sum_{d \mid k} d\,\mu(k/d)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d}\right\rfloor - \frac{\lfloor x \rfloor}{d}\right) \;\equiv\; \sum_{d \mid k} d\,\mu(k/d)\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d}\right\rfloor \qquad (k \ge 2). \tag{2}$$
By Báez-Duarte's theorem [3], the Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if $\lim_{N \to \infty} d_N^2 = 0$, while Burnol [6] and Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [5] established that $\liminf_{N \to \infty} d_N^2 \log N \ge 2 + \gamma - \log(4\pi) \approx 0.046191$.

Every generator $\rho_m(x)$ ($m \ge 2$) is constant on each unit integer cell $[n, n+1)$ ($n \ge 1$) with cell value $\rho_m(n) = \lfloor n/m \rfloor - \frac{n}{m} = -\{n/m\}$ (in particular, $\rho_m(1) = -\frac{1}{m} \ne 0$ and $\tau_k(1) = \sum_{d \mid k,\, d \ge 2} d\mu(k/d)(-\frac{1}{d}) = \mu(k)$, e.g. $\tau_2(1) = -1$), so $V_N$ is an $(N-1)$-dimensional subspace of the integer step-function space
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; \left\{f \in \mathcal{H} : f\big|_{[n, n+1)} \equiv f(n)\text{ for all } n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\right\}, \qquad \|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{|f(n)|^2}{n(n+1)}. \tag{3}$$
In earlier works, we established the floor-quotient primal-dual equivalence [10], the harmonic–Möbius unitriangular factorization and unconditional dual-norm limit $\lim_{N \to \infty}\|F_N^*\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = 15/\pi^2$ [11], the step-propagation and harmonic-shift telescoping identities [12, Proposition 2], and shifted-Legendre Rodrigues–Beta and Bernstein–Beta decompositions for the infinite-horizon Gram matrix $G_\tau := (\langle \tau_k, \tau_l \rangle_{\mathcal{H}})_{2 \le k, l \le N}$ [8, 9]. Complementarily, `@bramble_trace` established transferred zero-obstruction bounds on logarithmic prefixes $R_N = \lceil N\log N \rceil$ [13] and conditional approximation rates via adaptive zero clusters [14]. A central structural gap remained between these two regimes:
- In the **infinite-horizon $\tau_k$ Gram representation** [8, 9, 12], each entry $G_{k, l}^\tau$ is evaluated via Dedekind–Vasyunin cotangent sums [4] that entangle the finite onset regime $1 \le n \le 2N$ (where $\tau_k(n) = \sum_{m=1}^n c_k(m)$ has integer-cell jumps $\Delta_n\tau_k := \tau_k(n) - \tau_k(n-1) = c_k(n)$, Ramanujan's sum [17]) with the asymptotic periodic regime $n > 2N$. As a result, coarse-to-fine prolongation across dyadic scales in the dense $\tau_k$ basis requires $O(N^3)$ Cholesky factorizations or $O(N^2)$ dense matrix-vector products.
- Conversely, in the **finite-prefix cell representation** on $[1, M+1)$ [10, 11, 12, 13], truncating the norm at $M$ discards the infinite tail $\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{(1 - g(n))^2}{n(n+1)}$, which carries the asymptotic prime-cancellation energy of $V_N$.

### 1.2 Summary of Main Results

In this paper, we bridge the finite-prefix cell geometry on $1 \le n \le M$ and the asymptotic periodic Binet–Bernoulli geometry on $n > M$ through four constructive theorems:

1. **Meissel–Báez-Duarte Shifted-Floor Decomposition, Floor-Fiber Identity, and Closed-Form First-Octave Bridge Minimum (Section 2, Theorem 1):**
   Building on the first-octave hyperplane duality $(1 - V_N)\big|_{[1, N+1)} = \{\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^N : \mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} = m_1(N)\}$ ($w_{N, d} := \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)}{d} - \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor)}{d+1}$, $d_{N, N}^2 = m_1(N)^2/\|F_N^*\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \sim \frac{\pi^2}{15}m_1(N)^2$) established in [10, Theorem 3], [11, Theorems 2–3], and [12, Proposition 2(a)], we use Meissel's identity $\sum_{m=1}^n \mu(m)\lfloor n/m \rfloor = 1$ ($n \ge 1$) and shifted floor coordinates $\eta_m := \mu(m) - \xi_m$ ($1 \le m \le N$, $\xi_1 := -\sum_{m=2}^N \xi_m/m$) to prove that for every $g \in V_N$ and $n \ge 1$,
   $$1 - g(n) \;\equiv\; \sum_{m=1}^N \eta_m\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor \;+\; \Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}}(n), \qquad \Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}}(n) \;:=\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/(N+1) \rfloor}\bigl(M(\lfloor n/\ell \rfloor) - M(N)\bigr), \tag{4}$$
   subject to $\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\eta_m}{m} = m_1(N) := \sum_{k=1}^N \frac{\mu(k)}{k}$ ($M(x) := \sum_{k \le x}\mu(k)$), and that the dual representer $F_N^*(d) := d(d+1)w_{N, d}$ satisfies the **exact floor-fiber decomposition**
   $$F_N^*(d) \;\equiv\; m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor) \;+\; d\sum_{\substack{1 \le q \le N \\ \lfloor N/q \rfloor = d}} \frac{\mu(q)}{q} \qquad (1 \le d \le N). \tag{5}$$
   Furthermore, we prove that the global inner-product functional $1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} = \langle 1, 1 - g \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$ is given in first-octave cell coordinates by the **Logarithmic Mertens Dual Functional** $1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} \equiv (1 + \widetilde{m}_1(N)) - \widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ ($\widetilde{m}_1(x) := \sum_{k \le x}\frac{\mu(k)\log k}{k}$), yielding a **closed-form Sherman–Morrison formula** for the first-octave Hardy–Parseval bridge minimum:
   $$\widetilde{d}_{N, N}^2 \;:=\; \min_{\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\left[\bigl(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2 + \sum_{n=1}^N \bigl(\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2\right] \;\equiv\; d_{N, N}^2 \;+\; \frac{(\psi_N^\star)^2}{(N + 1) + (N + 1)^2\|\mathbf{u}_N^\perp\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2}. \tag{6}$$

2. **Rank-One Pole Prolongation Deflation and Two-Sided Rational Primal-Dual Certificates (Section 3, Theorem 2):**
   On any higher prefix window $N < n \le M$, the affine prolongation $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}} = \Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}} + T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ decomposes via $\lfloor n/m \rfloor = \frac{n}{m} - \{n/m\}$ into a **rank-one linear pole mode** minus a **bounded fractional-part prolongation operator**:
   $$T_{N, M} \;\equiv\; \mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{II}}\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} \;-\; \widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}, \qquad \bigl(\widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}\bigr)_n \;:=\; \sum_{m=1}^N \eta_m\left\{\frac{n}{m}\right\}. \tag{7}$$
   Projecting onto the tangent space $\mathbf{w}_N^\perp$ annihilates the rank-one linear pole mode identically: $T_{N, M}(I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N}) \equiv -\widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}(I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N})$. This deflation eliminates the $\Theta(M - N)$ leading singular value ($\sigma_{\max}^2$ drops from $244.55$ to $5.08$ at $N = 320, M = 640$) and yields an unconditional **closed-form 1-step matrix-free sandwich** $\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{MF}}^\star(N, M) \le d_{N, M}^2 \le \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{MF}}^\star(N, M)$ capturing $87.75\%$–$93.41\%$ of $d_{N, 2N}^2$ across $N \in [10, 320]$, as well as **two-sided rational primal-dual certificates** $[\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{dual}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}), \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{primal}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})]$ in `fractions.Fraction` arithmetic (from 100% deterministic pure-integer fixed-point PCG proposals) pinching $d_{N, 2N}^2$ to relative gaps $7.96 \times 10^{-20}$ ($N=10$) through $1.95 \times 10^{-7}$ ($N=320$).

3. **Universal All-Order Raabe–Möbius Binet–Bernoulli Convolution Algebra (Section 4, Theorem 3):**
   In the Hardy–Parseval mother-wavelet expansion $\|1 - g\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = (1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty (\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2$ ($g = \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\tau_k$, $\Upsilon_k(n) = -\sum_{d \mid k}\mu(k/d)H_{\lfloor n/d \rfloor} - \Lambda(k)$ [8, 9, 12]), we prove that for all $k \ge 2$ and $n \ge 1$ the wavelets admit the **exact discrete summation-by-parts and all-order Binet–Bernoulli representations**
   $$\Upsilon_k(n) \;\equiv\; \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n} + \frac{\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n(n + 1)} - 2\sum_{m=n+1}^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)}{(m - 1)m(m + 1)} \;\equiv\; \sum_{r=1}^R \frac{\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n^r} - R\int_n^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{R, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u)}{u^{R+1}}\,du, \tag{8}$$
   where $\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u) := \frac{1}{r}\sum_{d \mid k} d^r\,\mu(k/d)\,B_r(\{u/d\})$. Using Raabe's multiplication theorem and Möbius inversion, we prove that $\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)$ has the entire primitive-frequency generating function $\sum_{r=1}^\infty \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\frac{z^{r-1}}{(r-1)!} \equiv \sum_{\gcd(b, k)=1}\frac{e^{2\pi i b m/k}}{e^{z - 2\pi i b/k} - 1}$, satisfies the exact forward-difference ladder $\Delta\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) \equiv -c_k(m+1)$ and $\Delta\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) \equiv \sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{j-1}\mathcal{B}_{j, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)$ ($r \ge 2$), and forms a **closed all-order cyclic convolution algebra** ($L := \operatorname{lcm}(k, l)$): for all $r, s \ge 1$ and all shifts $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the $t = 1$ discontinuous sawtooth terms cancel identically, and when summed along any homogeneous total degree $M_{\mathrm{deg}} = r + s \ge 2$, every odd-order spline cancels at every shift $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. In particular, for every odd degree $M_{\mathrm{deg}} = 2q + 1 \ge 3$, all lower Bernoulli terms vanish and the homogeneous convolution sum collapses to a **single pure even spline**:
   $$\sum_{r+s=2q+1} \frac{1}{L}\sum_{m=1}^L \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\,\mathcal{B}_{s, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m + n) \;\equiv\; -\,\delta_{k, l}\,\mathcal{B}_{2q, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) \qquad (\forall\, n \in \mathbb{Z}). \tag{9}$$

4. **Abel–Ramanujan Tail Gram Expansion and Two-Sided Farey–Nyquist Tail Large Sieve Bounds on $G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)$ (Section 5, Theorem 4 & Corollary 1):**
   Combining the Finite-$M$ Hardy–Parseval Bridge Identity [9, Theorem 4(b); 12, Proposition 2(b)]
   $$\sum_{n=1}^M \frac{\bigl(1 - g(n)\bigr)^2}{n(n+1)} \;+\; \frac{\bigl(\Psi_{M+1}(1 - g)\bigr)^2}{M + 1} \;\equiv\; \bigl(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2 \;+\; \sum_{n=1}^M \bigl(\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2 \qquad (\forall\, M \ge 1) \tag{10}$$
   with the primitive Fourier representation of $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$ and $\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$ on the Farey fractions $\mathcal{F}_N^* = \{b/k : 2 \le k \le N,\, 1 \le b < k,\, \gcd(b, k) = 1\}$ (minimum spacing $\delta_N = \frac{1}{N(N-1)}$), the Montgomery–Vaughan weighted Hilbert inequality [16], and discrete summation by parts (8), we prove the **Farey–Nyquist Tail Large Sieve Sandwich**: for all $N \ge 2$, $M \ge 1$, and $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$, the leading tail quadratic form $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) := \sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}(\sum_{k=2}^N c_k\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n))^2$ and the **true infinite-horizon tail Gram form** $\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c} := \sum_{n=M+1}^\infty (\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2$ satisfy
   $$\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) \;\le\; \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}, \tag{11}$$
   $$\bigl(\max\bigl(0,\, \sqrt{\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)} - \eta_{N, M}\bigr)\bigr)^2\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \bigl(\sqrt{\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)} + \eta_{N, M}\bigr)^2\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}, \tag{11'}$$
   where $\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M) := \frac{M+1}{M+N(N-1)+1}$, $\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M) := 1 + \frac{N(N-1)+1}{M+1}$, $D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M) := \frac{1}{12(M+1)}\operatorname{diag}(J_2(k))_{k=2}^N$, and $\eta_{N, M} := \frac{1}{M+1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)}{10}\max_{2 \le k \le N}\frac{J_4(k)+10 J_2(k)}{J_2(k)}} = O(N/M)$. In particular, for Ward–Ramanujan / BCF profiles $|c_k| \le C_0\frac{\mu^2(k)}{\varphi(k)\log N}$, $\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c} \ll \bigl(\frac{N}{M} + \frac{N^3}{M^2}\bigr)(\log N)^{-2} = O((\log N)^{-2})$ unconditionally for all $M \ge N^{3/2}$ (Corollary 1).

## 2. Meissel–Báez-Duarte Floor Duality, Floor-Fiber Identity, and First-Octave Bridge Minimum

### 2.1 Shifted Floor Coordinates and the First-Octave Hyperplane

For any $g(x) = \sum_{m=2}^N \xi_m\rho_m(x) \in V_N$, define the balanced zero-sum extension coefficient
$$\xi_1 \;:=\; -\sum_{m=2}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m} \;=\; 0. \tag{12}$$
Substituting $\rho_m(n) = \lfloor n/m \rfloor - \frac{n}{m}$ ($m \ge 2$) and $-n\sum_{m=2}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m} = n\,\xi_1 = \xi_1\lfloor n/1 \rfloor$ on every integer cell $x \in [n, n+1)$ ($n \ge 1$) gives the pure floor expansion
$$g(n) \;=\; \sum_{m=2}^N \xi_m\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor - \frac{n}{m}\right) \;\equiv\; \sum_{m=1}^N \xi_m\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor \qquad (\forall\, n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}). \tag{13}$$
Conversely, any vector $(\xi_1, \dots, \xi_N) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ satisfying $\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m} = 0$ defines a unique element $g = \sum_{m=2}^N \xi_m\rho_m \in V_N$ via (13); in particular, at $n = 1$, $g(1) = \xi_1 = -\sum_{m=2}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m}$ and $r_1 := 1 - g(1) = 1 - \xi_1$ varies freely over $\mathbb{R}$ as $(\xi_2, \dots, \xi_N)$ varies over $\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$. Furthermore, writing $g = \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\tau_k = \sum_{m=1}^N \xi_m\lfloor x/m \rfloor$ via (2) identifies $\xi_m \equiv m\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor N/m \rfloor}\mu(\ell)c_{m\ell}$ ($1 \le m \le N$, $c_1 := 0$).

Now recall **Meissel's classical identity** (coming from $\sum_{d \mid k}\mu(d) = \delta_{k, 1}$ summed over $1 \le k \le n$):
$$1 \;\equiv\; \sum_{m=1}^n \mu(m)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor \qquad (\forall\, n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}). \tag{14}$$
Introducing the **Meissel–Báez-Duarte shifted floor coordinates**
$$\eta_m \;:=\; \mu(m) - \xi_m \qquad (1 \le m \le N), \tag{15}$$
the zero-mean constraint $\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\xi_m}{m} = 0$ becomes the single affine hyperplane constraint
$$\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\eta_m}{m} \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\mu(m)}{m} \;=:\; m_1(N). \tag{16}$$

On the first octave $1 \le n \le N$, the lower-triangular unit-diagonal floor matrix $L_N := (\lfloor n/m \rfloor)_{1 \le n, m \le N}$ is invertible over $\mathbb{Z}$ with inverse $\eta_m = \sum_{d \mid m}\mu(m/d)\Delta_d r$ ($\Delta_1 r := r_1$, $\Delta_d r := r_d - r_{d-1}$ for $d \ge 2$), so $\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\eta_m}{m} = \sum_{d=1}^N \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)}{d}\Delta_d r \equiv \mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ as an algebraic identity on all of $\mathbb{R}^N$. As established in [10, Theorem 3], [11, Theorems 2–3], and [12, Proposition 2(a)] (see also [13, §1.1, §8]), under $L_N$ the affine hyperplane (16) maps bijectively onto the codimension-one hyperplane in first-octave cell coordinates $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} := (r_1, \dots, r_N)^{\mathsf{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^N$:
$$\bigl(1 - V_N\bigr)\Big|_{[1, N+1)} \;=\; \left\{\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^N : \mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} = m_1(N)\right\}, \qquad w_{N, d} \;:=\; \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)}{d} \;-\; \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor)}{d+1} \quad (1 \le d \le N), \tag{17}$$
with unique first-octave minimizer $r_{\mathrm{I}, d}^\star = \frac{m_1(N)}{\|F_N^*\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2}F_N^*(d)$ ($F_N^*(d) := d(d+1)w_{N, d}$), minimum squared norm $d_{N, N}^2 = \frac{m_1(N)^2}{\|F_N^*\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2}$, and unconditional asymptotic limit $\lim_{N \to \infty}\|F_N^*\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \frac{15}{\pi^2}$ [11, Theorem 3(c)]. Moreover, since $\lfloor n/m \rfloor - \lfloor (n-1)/m \rfloor = \mathbf{1}_{m \mid n}$, the cell jump $\Delta_n g := g(n) - g(n-1) = \sum_{d \mid n,\, d \le N}\xi_d$ depends only on the $N$-smooth part $n_{\le N} := \prod_{p^a \parallel n,\, p \le N} p^a$ ($\Delta_n g \equiv \Delta_{n_{\le N}} g$; cf. [12, Proposition 2(a)]).

We now establish the exact Meissel–Mertens decomposition for all $n \ge 1$, the floor-fiber decomposition of $F_N^*(d)$, the logarithmic Mertens dual functional on $[1, N+1)$, and the closed-form Sherman–Morrison formula for the first-octave Hardy–Parseval bridge minimum $\widetilde{d}_{N, N}^2$.

**Theorem 1 (Meissel–Mertens Shifted-Floor Decomposition, Floor-Fiber Identity, and Closed-Form First-Octave Bridge Minimum).**
*Let $N \ge 2$, and for $g = \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\tau_k \in V_N$ write $r_n := 1 - g(n)$ ($n \ge 1$) and $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} := (r_1, \dots, r_N)^{\mathsf{T}}$.*

*(a) (**Exact Meissel–Mertens Residual Decomposition and Floor-Fiber Identity**): As $\boldsymbol{\eta} = (\eta_1, \dots, \eta_N)^{\mathsf{T}}$ ranges over the affine hyperplane $\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\eta_m}{m} = m_1(N)$ in $\mathbb{R}^N$, the residual sequence $r_n = 1 - g(n)$ ranges over all sequences of the form*
$$r_n \;\equiv\; \sum_{m=1}^N \eta_m\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor \;+\; \Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}}(n) \qquad (n \ge 1), \tag{18}$$
*where the deterministic tail Mertens offset $\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}}(n)$ is given by*
$$\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}}(n) \;:=\; \sum_{m=N+1}^n \mu(m)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor \;\equiv\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/(N+1) \rfloor}\Bigl(M(\lfloor n/\ell \rfloor) - M(N)\Bigr), \qquad M(x) \;:=\; \sum_{1 \le k \le x}\mu(k). \tag{19}$$
*Furthermore, the dual representer $F_N^*(d) := d(d+1)w_{N, d}$ satisfies the **exact floor-fiber identity***
$$F_N^*(d) \;\equiv\; m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor) \;+\; d\sum_{\substack{1 \le q \le N \\ \lfloor N/q \rfloor = d}} \frac{\mu(q)}{q} \qquad (1 \le d \le N), \tag{20}$$
*which equals $F_N^*(d) = 1$ on the open upper half-octave $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor < d < N$ with a boundary spike $F_N^*(N) = N + 1$ at $d = N$.*

*(b) (**Logarithmic Mertens Dual Functional and Closed-Form First-Octave Bridge Minimum**): In first-octave cell coordinates $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} = (r_1, \dots, r_N)^{\mathsf{T}}$, the global inner-product functional $1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} = \langle 1, 1 - g \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies the exact identity*
$$1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} \;\equiv\; \bigl(1 + \widetilde{m}_1(N)\bigr) \;-\; \widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}, \qquad \widetilde{m}_1(x) \;:=\; \sum_{1 \le k \le x}\frac{\mu(k)\log k}{k}, \tag{21}$$
*where $\widetilde{w}_{N, d} := \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)\log d + \widetilde{m}_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)}{d} - \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor)\log(d+1) + \widetilde{m}_1(\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor)}{d+1}$ ($1 \le d \le N$). Consequently, let $D_{\mathrm{I}} := \operatorname{diag}(d(d+1))_{1 \le d \le N}$, $\mathbf{u}_N := \widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_N + D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}\mathbf{1}$, $\mathbf{u}_N^\perp := \mathbf{u}_N - \frac{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{u}_N}{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N}\mathbf{w}_N$, and $\psi_N^\star := (N + 1)\bigl(1 + \widetilde{m}_1(N) - \mathbf{u}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star\bigr)$. Then the first-octave Hardy–Parseval bridge minimum $\widetilde{d}_{N, N}^2 := \min_{\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\bigl[(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2 + \sum_{n=1}^N (\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2\bigr]$ is given in closed rational-logarithmic form by*
$$\widetilde{d}_{N, N}^2 \;\equiv\; d_{N, N}^2 \;+\; \frac{(\psi_N^\star)^2}{(N + 1) + (N + 1)^2\|\mathbf{u}_N^\perp\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2}, \qquad \|\mathbf{u}_N^\perp\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2 \;:=\; (\mathbf{u}_N^\perp)^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{u}_N^\perp. \tag{22}$$

*Proof.*
**(a)** Combining (13) and (14) gives $1 - g(n) = \sum_{m=1}^N (\mu(m) - \xi_m)\lfloor n/m \rfloor + \sum_{m=N+1}^n \mu(m)\lfloor n/m \rfloor$. In the second sum, $\ell := \lfloor n/m \rfloor$ ranges over $1 \le \ell \le \lfloor n/(N+1) \rfloor$, and for each $\ell$ the indices $m > N$ with $\lfloor n/m \rfloor \ge \ell$ are $N + 1 \le m \le \lfloor n/\ell \rfloor$, yielding (18)–(19) by summation by parts. Next, for each $1 \le d \le N$, writing $d(d+1)w_{N, d} = (d+1)m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor) - d\,m_1(\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor) = m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor) + d\bigl(m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor) - m_1(\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor)\bigr)$ and noting that the integers $q \in [1, N]$ satisfying $\lfloor N/(d+1) \rfloor < q \le \lfloor N/d \rfloor$ are precisely the floor fiber $\{q \le N : \lfloor N/q \rfloor = d\}$ proves (20). For $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor < d < N$, $\lfloor N/d \rfloor = 1$ and the fiber $\{q : \lfloor N/q \rfloor = d\}$ is empty, so $F_N^*(d) = m_1(1) + 0 = 1$; at $d = N$, $\lfloor N/N \rfloor = 1$ and the fiber is $\{1\}$, giving $F_N^*(N) = 1 + N\frac{\mu(1)}{1} = N + 1$.

**(b)** Using $\langle 1, \rho_m \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = -\frac{\log m}{m}$ and $\log 1 = 0$, we have $1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} = 1 + \sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\log m}{m}\xi_m = 1 + \widetilde{m}_1(N) - \sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\log m}{m}\eta_m$. Substituting $\eta_m = \sum_{d \mid m}\mu(m/d)\Delta_d r_{\mathrm{I}}$ ($m = d\ell$, $1 \le \ell \le \lfloor N/d \rfloor$) and writing $\log(d\ell) = \log d + \log\ell$ gives
$$\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\log m}{m}\eta_m \;=\; \sum_{d=1}^N \frac{m_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)\log d + \widetilde{m}_1(\lfloor N/d \rfloor)}{d}\,\Delta_d r_{\mathrm{I}} \;=\; \widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$$
by Abel summation on $\Delta_d r_{\mathrm{I}} = r_d - r_{d-1}$, proving (21). By the finite-$M$ bridge identity (10) at $M = N$, $(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2 + \sum_{n=1}^N (\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2 \equiv \|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2 + \frac{1}{N+1}\Psi_{N+1}(1 - g)^2$, where $\Psi_{N+1}(1 - g) = (N+1)(1 + \widetilde{m}_1(N) - \mathbf{u}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}})$. Writing $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} = \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star + \mathbf{z}$ with $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbf{w}_N^\perp$ gives $\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2 = d_{N, N}^2 + \|\mathbf{z}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2$ and $\Psi_{N+1}(1 - g) = \psi_N^\star - (N+1)(\mathbf{u}_N^\perp)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{z}$; minimizing over $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbf{w}_N^\perp$ via Sherman–Morrison rank-one inversion yields (22). $\blacksquare$

## 3. Rank-One Pole Prolongation Deflation and Rational Primal-Dual Certificates

We now extend Theorem 1 from the first octave $[1, N+1)$ to an arbitrary finite prefix $[1, M+1)$ ($M > N$). Partition the cell-residual vector on $[1, M+1)$ into $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} := (r_1, \dots, r_N)^{\mathsf{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}} := (r_{N+1}, \dots, r_M)^{\mathsf{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M-N}$, with diagonal cell-weight matrices $D_{\mathrm{I}} := \operatorname{diag}(d(d+1))_{1 \le d \le N}$ and $D_{\mathrm{II}} := \operatorname{diag}(n(n+1))_{N < n \le M}$. By Theorem 1(a), $\boldsymbol{\eta} = L_N^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$, so $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}$ is determined affinely by $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$:
$$\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}} \;=\; \Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}} \;+\; T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}, \qquad T_{N, M} \;:=\; L_{\mathrm{II}, \mathrm{I}}\,L_N^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}^{(M-N)\times N}, \qquad (L_{\mathrm{II}, \mathrm{I}})_{n, m} \;:=\; \left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor. \tag{24}$$

**Theorem 2 (Rank-One Pole Prolongation Deflation, Matrix-Free Sandwich, and Rational Primal-Dual Duality).**
*Let $2 \le N < M$, and let $\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{II}} := (N+1, N+2, \dots, M)^{\mathsf{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M-N}$.*

*(a) (**Rank-One Pole Decomposition and Exact Tangent Deflation**): Splitting $\lfloor n/m \rfloor = \frac{n}{m} - \{n/m\}$ decomposes $T_{N, M}$ into a rank-one linear pole operator minus the fractional-part prolongation operator:*
$$T_{N, M} \;\equiv\; \mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{II}}\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} \;-\; \widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}, \qquad \bigl(\widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}\bigr)_n \;:=\; \sum_{m=1}^N \eta_m\left\{\frac{n}{m}\right\} \qquad (N < n \le M). \tag{25}$$
*In particular, let $P_{\mathbf{w}_N} := \frac{D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}}{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N}$ be the $D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}$-orthogonal projector onto $\mathbf{w}_N$. Then on the tangent hyperplane $\mathbf{w}_N^\perp = \operatorname{ker}(\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}})$, the rank-one linear pole mode $\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{II}}\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}$ is annihilated identically:*
$$T_{N, M}\bigl(I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N}\bigr) \;\equiv\; -\,\widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}\bigl(I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N}\bigr). \tag{26}$$

*(b) (**Saddle-Point Dual Formulation and Exact Rational Primal-Dual Certificates**): Let $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star := \Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}} + T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star \in \mathbb{Q}^{M-N}$ be the prolongation of the first-octave minimizer $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star$, and for any dual test vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{R}^{M-N}$ define its tangent back-reaction vector*
$$\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) \;:=\; -\bigl(I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)T_{N, M}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\lambda} \;=\; \mathbf{v}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) - \frac{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{v}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N}\,\mathbf{w}_N, \qquad \mathbf{v}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) := -T_{N, M}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}. \tag{27}$$
*Then the multi-octave prefix distance $d_{N, M}^2 := \min_{g \in V_N}\sum_{n=1}^M \frac{(1 - g(n))^2}{n(n+1)}$ satisfies the exact variational duality*
$$d_{N, M}^2 \;=\; d_{N, N}^2 \;+\; \max_{\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{R}^{M-N}\setminus\{0\}} \frac{\bigl(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star\bigr)^2}{\|\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2 + \|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}}^2}, \tag{28}$$
*where $\|\mathbf{u}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2 := \mathbf{u}^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{u}$ and $\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}}^2 := \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{II}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}$. Consequently, for every rational dual vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{Q}^{M-N}$ with $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star \ne 0$, the Associated Primal Candidate*
$$\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{cand}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) \;:=\; \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star \;+\; \alpha_{\mathrm{opt}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\,D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda}), \qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{opt}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) \;:=\; \frac{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star}{\|\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2 + \|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}}^2}, \tag{29}$$
*satisfies $\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{cand}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) = m_1(N)$ identically and yields the certified two-sided rational sandwich*
$$\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{dual}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) \;:=\; d_{N, N}^2 + \frac{\bigl(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star\bigr)^2}{\|\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2 + \|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}}^2} \;\le\; d_{N, M}^2 \;\le\; \bigl\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{cand}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\bigr\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2 + \bigl\|\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Mert}} + T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{cand}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\bigr\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}^{-1}}^2 \;=:\; \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{primal}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}). \tag{30}$$
*In particular, the closed-form 1-step test vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star := D_{\mathrm{II}}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star \in \mathbb{Q}^{M-N}$ gives the explicit matrix-free sandwich*
$$\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{MF}}^\star(N, M) \;:=\; d_{N, N}^2 + \frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}^{-1}}^2}{1 + \rho_{N, M}^\star} \;\le\; d_{N, M}^2 \;\le\; d_{N, N}^2 + \|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}^{-1}}^2 \;=:\; \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{MF}}^\star(N, M), \qquad \rho_{N, M}^\star \;:=\; \frac{\|\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star)\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2}{\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}^{-1}}^2}, \tag{31}$$
*and both $T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ and $T_{N, M}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ are evaluable in $O(M\log N)$ arithmetic operations without forming $T_{N, M}$.*

*Proof.*
**(a)** For any $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with $\boldsymbol{\eta} = L_N^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$, Section 2.1 Eq. (17) shows that $\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\eta_m}{m} \equiv \mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ holds as an algebraic identity on all of $\mathbb{R}^N$. Hence for every $N < n \le M$,
$$(T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}})_n \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^N \eta_m\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor \;=\; n\sum_{m=1}^N \frac{\eta_m}{m} \;-\; \sum_{m=1}^N \eta_m\left\{\frac{n}{m}\right\} \;=\; n\,\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} \;-\; \bigl(\widetilde{T}_{N, M}^{\mathrm{frac}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}\bigr)_n,$$
proving (25). Since $\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}(I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N}) = \mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} - \frac{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N}{\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N}\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}} = 0$, right-multiplying (25) by $I - P_{\mathbf{w}_N}$ gives (26).

**(b)** Every $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ on the hyperplane $\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} = m_1(N)$ decomposes uniquely as $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}} = \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star + \mathbf{z}$ with $\mathbf{w}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{z} = 0$, and $D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}$-orthogonality of $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}^\star \propto D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{w}_N$ to $\mathbf{z}$ gives $\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2 = d_{N, N}^2 + \|\mathbf{z}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2$. Writing $\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star + T_{N, M}\mathbf{z}\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}^{-1}}^2 = \max_{\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{R}^{M-N}}\bigl(2\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{II}}^\star + T_{N, M}\mathbf{z}) - \|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{D_{\mathrm{II}}}^2\bigr)$ and interchanging $\min_{\mathbf{z} \in \mathbf{w}_N^\perp}$ and $\max_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}$ by strong convex duality, the inner minimum over $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbf{w}_N^\perp$ of $\|\mathbf{z}\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}}^2 - 2\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{z}$ is attained at $\mathbf{z}^\star(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) = D_{\mathrm{I}}\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ with minimum value $-\|\mathbf{v}^\perp(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{D_{\mathrm{I}}}^2$. Optimizing the scalar ray $t\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ over $t \in \mathbb{R}$ gives $t_{\mathrm{opt}} = \alpha_{\mathrm{opt}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ and establishes (28)–(31). Finally, $\boldsymbol{\eta} = L_N^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}}$ requires $O(N\log N)$ operations via the divisor loop (20) and $(T_{N, M}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{I}})_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \sum_{m \mid k,\, m \le N}\eta_m$ requires $O(M\log N)$ operations via a harmonic multiple loop and prefix sum, while $T_{N, M}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ reverses those two loops in $O(M\log N)$ operations. $\blacksquare$

## 4. Universal All-Order Raabe–Möbius Binet–Bernoulli Convolution Algebra

To control the infinite tail $n > M$ analytically, we pass to the orthonormal **Hardy–Parseval mother-wavelet basis** $\{\delta_1^{(0)}\} \cup \{\delta_m^{(1)}\}_{m \ge 2}$ of $\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}}$ ([8, 9, 12]), where $\delta_1^{(0)}(x) := 1$ and $\delta_m^{(1)}(x) := \mathbf{1}_{[m, \infty)}(x) - (m-1)\mathbf{1}_{[m-1, m)}(x)$ ($m \ge 2$, $\|\delta_m^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \frac{1}{m} + \frac{(m-1)^2}{(m-1)m} \equiv 1$). As shown in [8, 9, 12], for every $g = \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\tau_k \in V_N$,
$$\|1 - g\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;\equiv\; \bigl(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2 \;+\; \sum_{n=1}^\infty \bigl(\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2, \qquad \Upsilon_k(n) \;:=\; \langle \tau_k, \delta_{n+1}^{(1)} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \;\equiv\; -\sum_{d \mid k}\mu(k/d)H_{\lfloor n/d \rfloor} - \Lambda(k), \tag{32}$$
where $H_m := \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{j}$ ($H_0 := 0$). We now establish the exact all-order algebraic structure of the wavelets $\Upsilon_k(n)$.

**Theorem 3 (All-Order Raabe–Möbius Binet–Bernoulli Expansion, Finite-Difference Ladder, and Cyclic Convolution Algebra).**
*For every integer order $r \ge 1$, divisor index $k \ge 2$, and $u \in \mathbb{R}$, define the **$r$-th primitive Bernoulli wave***
$$\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u) \;:=\; \frac{1}{r}\sum_{d \mid k} d^r\,\mu(k/d)\,B_r\!\left(\left\{\frac{u}{d}\right\}\right), \tag{33}$$
*where $B_r(x) = \sum_{j=0}^r \binom{r}{j} B_{r-j} x^j$ is the $r$-th Bernoulli polynomial ($B_1(x) = x - \frac{1}{2}$, $B_2(x) = x^2 - x + \frac{1}{6}$, $B_3(x) = x^3 - \frac{3}{2}x^2 + \frac{1}{2}x$, $B_4(x) = x^4 - 2x^3 + x^2 - \frac{1}{30}$).*

*(a) (**Exact Discrete Summation-by-Parts and Euler–Maclaurin Binet–Bernoulli Identities for $\Upsilon_k(n)$**): For every $k \ge 2$ and every integer $n \ge 1$, $\Upsilon_k(n)$ satisfies the exact discrete summation-by-parts identities*
$$\Upsilon_k(n) \;\equiv\; \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n} - \sum_{m=n}^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)}{m(m + 1)} \;\equiv\; \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n} + \frac{\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n(n + 1)} - 2\sum_{m=n+1}^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)}{(m - 1)m(m + 1)}, \tag{34}$$
*and for every order $R \ge 1$ and $n \ge 1$ the exact Euler–Maclaurin integral-remainder identity*
$$\Upsilon_k(n) \;\equiv\; \sum_{r=1}^R \frac{\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n^r} \;-\; R\int_n^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{R, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u)}{u^{R+1}}\,du \;=\; \sum_{r=1}^R \frac{\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n^r} \;+\; O_{k, R}\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{R+1}}\right). \tag{34'}$$

*(b) (**Raabe–Möbius Primitive Generating Function, Coset Orthogonality, and Finite-Difference Ladder**): For every $k \ge 2$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, the exponential generating function of $\{\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\}_{r \ge 1}$ is an entire function of $z$ supported exclusively on primitive frequencies $\frac{b}{k} \pmod 1$ ($\gcd(b, k) = 1$):*
$$G_k(m; z) \;:=\; \sum_{r=1}^\infty \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\,\frac{z^{r-1}}{(r-1)!} \;\equiv\; \sum_{d \mid k}\mu(k/d)\,\frac{d\,e^{(m \bmod d)z}}{e^{d z} - 1} \;\equiv\; \sum_{\substack{1 \le b \le k \\ \gcd(b, k) = 1}} \frac{e^{2\pi i b m / k}}{e^{z - 2\pi i b / k} - 1}. \tag{35}$$
*Consequently, $\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)$ has zero coset average $\sum_{t=0}^{k/q - 1}\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m + t q) \equiv 0$ for every proper divisor $q \mid k$ ($q < k$), reconstructs the full Bernoulli polynomial via $\sum_{k \mid n,\, k \ge 2}\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) \equiv \frac{n^r}{r}B_r(\{m/n\}) - \frac{B_r}{r}$ ($n \ge 1$), and obeys the **exact forward-difference ladder***
$$\Delta\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) \;:=\; \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m+1) - \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) \;\equiv\; \begin{cases} -\,c_k(m+1), & r = 1, \\[4pt] \displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{j-1}\mathcal{B}_{j, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m), & r \ge 2, \end{cases} \tag{36}$$
*where $c_k(n) = \sum_{d \mid \gcd(k, n)} d\,\mu(k/d) = \sum_{\gcd(b, k)=1} e^{2\pi i b n/k}$ is Ramanujan's sum [17]. In particular, $-\Delta^2\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) := 2\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) - \mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m+1) - \mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m-1) \equiv c_k(m)$.*

*(c) (**Universal All-Order $(r, s)$-Shift Cyclic Cross-Correlation & Sawtooth Cancellation**): For all $r, s \ge 1$, all $k, l \ge 2$ with $L := \operatorname{lcm}(k, l)$, and all shifts $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the cyclic cross-correlation $\frac{1}{L}\sum_{m=1}^L \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\mathcal{B}_{s, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m + n)$ vanishes identically when $k \ne l$ and equals a finite linear combination of continuous splines $\mathcal{B}_{t, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$ of order $t \ge 2$ when $k = l$ (with the $t = 1$ discontinuous sawtooth terms cancelling identically):*
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{m=1}^L \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\,\mathcal{B}_{s, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m + n) \;&\equiv\; \delta_{k, l}\Bigg[ (-1)^{r-1}\frac{(r-1)!(s-1)!}{(r+s-1)!}\,\mathcal{B}_{r+s, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) \\
&\qquad + \sum_{t=2}^s \binom{s-1}{t-1}\frac{B_{r+s-t}}{r+s-t}\,\mathcal{B}_{t, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) \;-\; \sum_{t=2}^r (-1)^{t-1}\binom{r-1}{t-1}\frac{B_{r+s-t}}{r+s-t}\,\mathcal{B}_{t, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) \Bigg].
\end{aligned} \tag{37}$$

*(d) (**Homogeneous Even-Spline Reduction & Odd-Degree Single-Spline Identity**): For any total degree $M_{\mathrm{deg}} \ge 2$, the homogeneous diagonal convolution sum $\mathcal{C}_{M_{\mathrm{deg}}, k, l}(n) := \sum_{r+s=M_{\mathrm{deg}}}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{m=1}^L \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\mathcal{B}_{s, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m+n)$ contains **only even-order splines** $\mathcal{B}_{2p, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$ at every shift $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and collapses for every odd degree $M_{\mathrm{deg}} = 2q + 1 \ge 3$ to a single even spline:*
$$\mathcal{C}_{M_{\mathrm{deg}}, k, l}(n) \;\equiv\; \delta_{k, l}\begin{cases} -\,\mathcal{B}_{2q, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n), & M_{\mathrm{deg}} = 2q + 1\text{ odd}, \\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{q}\,\mathcal{B}_{2q, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) + 2\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{q-1}\binom{2q-1}{2p}\frac{B_{2(q-p)}}{2(q-p)}\,\mathcal{B}_{2p, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n), & M_{\mathrm{deg}} = 2q\text{ even}. \end{cases} \tag{38}$$
*In particular, at $(r, s) = (1, 1)$ and $(2, 2)$ at shift $n = 0$, the discrete Parseval energies of $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}$ and $\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}$ are given in closed form by $\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^k \mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)^2 \equiv \frac{J_2(k)}{12}$ and $\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^k \mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)^2 \equiv \frac{J_4(k) + 10\,J_2(k)}{720}$ ($J_r(k) := \sum_{d \mid k} d^r\mu(k/d)$).*

*Proof.*
**(a)–(b)** From $\sum_{r=0}^\infty B_r(u)\frac{w^r}{r!} = \frac{w e^{u w}}{e^w - 1}$, differentiating with respect to $z = w/d$ gives $\sum_{r=1}^\infty \frac{d^r}{r}B_r(\{m/d\})\frac{z^{r-1}}{(r-1)!} = \frac{d e^{(m \bmod d)z}}{e^{d z} - 1} - \frac{1}{z}$. Multiplying by $\mu(k/d)$ and summing over $d \mid k$ cancels $-\frac{1}{z}\sum_{d \mid k}\mu(k/d) = 0$. Expanding $\frac{d e^{(m \bmod d)z}}{e^{d z} - 1} = \sum_{a=1}^d \frac{e^{2\pi i a m/d}}{e^{z - 2\pi i a/d} - 1}$ via the finite geometric series $\sum_{r=0}^{d-1} e^{r(z - 2\pi i a/d)} = \frac{e^{d z} - 1}{e^{z - 2\pi i a/d} - 1}$ and applying Möbius inversion over the reduced denominator $q = d/\gcd(a, d) \mid k$ eliminates all $q < k$ and isolates $q = k$, proving (35). Taking first differences of (35) gives
$$G_k(m+1; z) - G_k(m; z) \;=\; \sum_{\gcd(b, k)=1} e^{2\pi i b m/k}\frac{e^{2\pi i b/k} - 1}{e^{z - 2\pi i b/k} - 1} \;=\; -c_k(m+1) + (e^z - 1)G_k(m; z),$$
and extracting the coefficient of $\frac{z^{r-1}}{(r-1)!}$ yields (36). Next, since $\Upsilon_k(n) = \sum_{m=n+1}^\infty \frac{c_k(m)}{m}$ ([12, Proposition 2(c)]) and $c_k(m) = -\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) - \mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m-1)\bigr)$ by (36), Abel summation by parts gives $\Upsilon_k(n) = \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n} - \sum_{m=n}^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)}{m(m+1)}$. Substituting $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) = \mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m+1) - \mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)$ from (36) and summing by parts a second time yields the second identity of (34). Similarly, writing $\frac{1}{m} = \int_m^\infty u^{-2}\,du$ gives $\Upsilon_k(n) = \int_n^\infty \bigl(\sum_{m=n+1}^{\lfloor u \rfloor} c_k(m)\bigr)u^{-2}\,du = \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n} - \int_n^\infty \mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u)u^{-2}\,du$; since $\mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u)$ is continuous for $r \ge 2$ (as $B_r(1) = B_r(0)$ for $r \ge 2$ and $\sum_{d \mid k} d\,\mu(k/d) = 0$ at $r = 2$) with derivative $\frac{d}{du}\mathcal{B}_{r+1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u) = \mathcal{B}_{r, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(u)$ on $u \notin \mathbb{Z}$, integrating by parts $R - 1$ times yields (34').

**(c)–(d)** Since $G_k(m; z_1)$ is supported on primitive frequencies $b/k$ ($\gcd(b, k) = 1$) and $G_l(m+n; z_2)$ on primitive frequencies $b'/l$ ($\gcd(b', l) = 1$), their cyclic correlation over $m \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ vanishes unless $k = l$ and $b' \equiv -b \pmod k$. When $k = l$, setting $\theta_b := 2\pi b/k$ and applying the universal partial-fraction identity
$$\frac{1}{\bigl(e^{z_1 + i\theta_b} - 1\bigr)\bigl(e^{z_2 - i\theta_b} - 1\bigr)} \;\equiv\; \frac{1}{e^{z_1 + z_2} - 1}\left(\frac{1}{e^{z_2 - i\theta_b} - 1} \;-\; \frac{1}{e^{-z_1 - i\theta_b} - 1}\right)$$
gives
$$\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^k G_k(m; z_1)\,G_k(m + n; z_2) \;\equiv\; \frac{G_k(n; z_2) - G_k(n; -z_1)}{e^{z_1 + z_2} - 1} \;=\; \left(\sum_{j=0}^\infty \frac{B_j}{j!}(z_1 + z_2)^{j-1}\right)\bigl(G_k(n; z_2) - G_k(n; -z_1)\bigr). \tag{39}$$
Extracting the coefficient of $\frac{z_1^{r-1}z_2^{s-1}}{(r-1)!(s-1)!}$ from (39): the singular $j = 0$ term $(z_1 + z_2)^{-1}(G_k(n; z_2) - G_k(n; -z_1))$ expands as $\sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{\mathcal{B}_{m, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{(m-1)!}\frac{z_2^{m-1} - (-z_1)^{m-1}}{z_1 + z_2} = \sum_{r, s \ge 1}(-1)^{r-1}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{r+s, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{(r+s-1)!}z_1^{r-1}z_2^{s-1}$, while the regular $j = r + s - t \ge 1$ terms give the two finite sums in (37). At $t = 1$, both sums contain the term $\frac{B_{r+s-1}}{r+s-1}\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$, which cancels identically between the two sums! Summing (37) over $r + s = M_{\mathrm{deg}}$ uses $\sum_{r=1}^{M_{\mathrm{deg}}-1}(-1)^{r-1}\frac{(r-1)!(M_{\mathrm{deg}}-r-1)!}{(M_{\mathrm{deg}}-1)!} = \frac{1+(-1)^{M_{\mathrm{deg}}}}{M_{\mathrm{deg}}}$ and $\sum_{s=t}^{M_{\mathrm{deg}}-1}\binom{s-1}{t-1} = \binom{M_{\mathrm{deg}}-1}{t}$, so every odd-$t$ term cancels via $1 - (-1)^{t-1} = 0$, leaving only even $t = 2p$ and proving (38). Evaluating (37) at $(r, s) = (1, 1), n = 0$ gives $\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(0) = \frac{B_2}{2}J_2(k) = \frac{J_2(k)}{12}$, while $(r, s) = (2, 2), n = 0$ gives $-\frac{1}{6}\mathcal{B}_{4, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(0) + B_2\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(0) = -\frac{B_4}{24}J_4(k) + \frac{B_2^2}{2}J_2(k) = \frac{J_4(k) + 10 J_2(k)}{720}$. $\blacksquare$

All finite instances of Theorem 3 ($1 \le r, s \le 5$, $2 \le k, l \le 12$, all shifts $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and the discrete Parseval identity for $2 \le k \le 24$) are checked in exact `Fraction` arithmetic (`17,588` exact rational assertions) by `src/verify_raabe_mobius_bernoulli_algebra.py` (`PASS` in `34.66s` $\to$ `results/raabe_mobius_bernoulli_algebra.json`).

## 5. Finite-$M$ Hardy–Parseval Bridge and the Farey–Nyquist Tail Large Sieve

We now combine the prefix geometry of Sections 2–3 with the Binet–Bernoulli tail algebra of Section 4.

**Theorem 4 (Finite-$M$ Hardy–Parseval Bridge, $O(M\log N)$ Jump-Sum Wavelets, and Farey–Nyquist Tail Large Sieve).**
*Let $N \ge 2$, $M \ge 1$, and $g = \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\tau_k \in V_N$ with residual $r_n = 1 - g(n)$.*

*(a) (**Finite-$M$ Hardy–Parseval Bridge and $O(M\log N)$ Jump-Sum Wavelet Factorization**): Define the tail integral functional $\Psi_{M+1}(1 - g) := (M + 1)\int_{M+1}^\infty (1 - g(x))x^{-2}\,dx \equiv (M + 1)\bigl(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} - \sum_{n=1}^M \frac{1 - g(n)}{n(n+1)}\bigr)$. As established in [9, Theorem 4(b)] and [12, Proposition 2(b)],*
$$\sum_{n=1}^M \frac{\bigl(1 - g(n)\bigr)^2}{n(n+1)} \;+\; \frac{\bigl(\Psi_{M+1}(1 - g)\bigr)^2}{M + 1} \;\equiv\; \bigl(1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2 \;+\; \sum_{n=1}^M \bigl(\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}\bigr)^2, \tag{40}$$
*where the wavelet inner products satisfy the exact **$O(M\log N)$ jump-sum factorization***
$$\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} \;\equiv\; -\,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} \;-\; \sum_{m=1}^n \frac{\Delta_m g}{m} \qquad (\forall\, n \ge 1), \tag{41}$$
*with $\Delta_m g = \sum_{d \mid m,\, d \le N}\xi_d$ and $\xi_d = d\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor N/d \rfloor}\mu(\ell)c_{d\ell}$ ($c_1 := 0$), and for every $M \ge 1$ the global NBBD norm splits identically into the prefix energy (40) plus the tail Gram form $G_{k, l}^{\mathrm{tail}}(M) := \sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \Upsilon_k(n)\Upsilon_l(n)$:*
$$\|1 - g\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \;\equiv\; \sum_{n=1}^M \frac{\bigl(1 - g(n)\bigr)^2}{n(n+1)} \;+\; \frac{\bigl(\Psi_{M+1}(1 - g)\bigr)^2}{M + 1} \;+\; \mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}. \tag{42}$$

*(b) (**Abel–Ramanujan Product Rule and $O((M+1)^{-3})$ Tail Gram Expansion**): For all $2 \le k, l \le N$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$,*
$$\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\,\mathcal{B}_{1, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) \;\equiv\; \Delta_m\Bigl(\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m-1)\,\mathcal{B}_{2, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\Bigr) \;+\; c_k(m)\,\mathcal{B}_{2, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m). \tag{43}$$
*Furthermore, let $\mathcal{S}_{k, l}(M)$ denote the unique zero-mean $L$-periodic primitive of $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\mathcal{B}_{1, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) - \frac{J_2(k)}{12}\delta_{k, l}$ ($L = \operatorname{lcm}(k, l)$). Then $\mathcal{S}_{k, l}(M)$ is given identically by the finite Farey–Hilbert sine sum*
$$\mathcal{S}_{k, l}(M) \;\equiv\; \sum_{\substack{1 \le b < k,\,\gcd(b, k) = 1 \\ 1 \le b' < l,\,\gcd(b', l) = 1 \\ b/k \ne b'/l}} \frac{\sin\!\left(2\pi\left(\frac{b}{k} - \frac{b'}{l}\right)(M + 1)\right)}{8\,\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi b}{k}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi b'}{l}\right)\sin\!\left(\pi\left(\frac{b}{k} - \frac{b'}{l}\right)\right)}, \qquad \bigl|\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{S}(M)\,\mathbf{c}\bigr| \;\le\; \frac{N(N - 1)(M + 1)}{2}\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}, \tag{44}$$
*and because $\mathcal{C}_{3, k, l}(n) = -\delta_{k, l}\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$ has periodic mean $-\frac{J_2(k)}{12}\delta_{k, l}$ and $\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty\bigl(\frac{1}{n^2} - \frac{1}{n^3}\bigr) = \sum_{n=M+1}^\infty\frac{1}{n(n+1)} + O((M+1)^{-3}) = \frac{1}{M+1} + O((M+1)^{-3})$ has zero $(M+1)^{-2}$ remainder, we have as $M \to \infty$:*
$$G_{k, l}^{\mathrm{tail}}(M) \;=\; \frac{J_2(k)}{12(M + 1)}\,\delta_{k, l} \;-\; \frac{\mathcal{S}_{k, l}(M)}{(M + 1)^2} \;+\; O_{k, l}\!\left(\frac{1}{(M + 1)^3}\right). \tag{44'}$$

*(c) (**Abel–Montgomery–Vaughan Farey–Nyquist Tail Large Sieve Bounds on $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}$ and $G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)$**): Let $D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M) := \frac{1}{12(M+1)}\operatorname{diag}(J_2(k))_{k=2}^N$, $\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M) := \frac{M + 1}{M + N(N - 1) + 1}$, and $\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M) := 1 + \frac{N(N - 1) + 1}{M + 1}$. Then for every $N \ge 2$, every $M \ge 1$, and every $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$, the leading Binet–Bernoulli tail quadratic form $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) := \sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}\bigl(\sum_{k=2}^N c_k\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)\bigr)^2$ satisfies*
$$\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) \;\le\; \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}, \tag{45}$$
*and the **true infinite-horizon tail Gram form** $\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c} = \sum_{n=M+1}^\infty (\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c})^2$ satisfies the explicit two-sided sandwich*
$$\Bigl(\max\bigl(0,\, \sqrt{\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)} - \eta_{N, M}\bigr)\Bigr)^2\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \Bigl(\sqrt{\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)} + \eta_{N, M}\Bigr)^2\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}, \tag{45'}$$
*where $\eta_{N, M} := \frac{1}{M + 1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)}{10}\max_{2 \le k \le N}\frac{J_4(k) + 10\,J_2(k)}{J_2(k)}}$.*

*Proof.*
**(a)** As shown in [9, Theorem 4(b)] (see also [12, Proposition 2(b)]), for any sequence $r_n$ with $\Psi_m := m\sum_{n=m}^\infty \frac{r_n}{n(n+1)}$, the algebraic telescoping identity $\frac{r_{m-1}^2}{(m-1)m} - (\Psi_m - \Psi_{m-1})^2 \equiv \frac{\Psi_{m-1}^2}{m-1} - \frac{\Psi_m^2}{m}$ summed over $2 \le m \le M + 1$ gives $\sum_{n=1}^M \frac{r_n^2}{n(n+1)} + \frac{\Psi_{M+1}^2}{M+1} \equiv \Psi_1^2 + \sum_{m=2}^{M+1}(\Psi_m - \Psi_{m-1})^2$. Since $\Psi_1 = 1 + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_N^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}$ and $\Psi_{n+1} - \Psi_n = \langle 1 - g, \delta_{n+1}^{(1)} \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = -\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}$, this proves (40) and (42). Next, since $H_{\lfloor m/d \rfloor} - H_{\lfloor (m-1)/d \rfloor} = \frac{d}{m}\mathbf{1}_{d \mid m}$, we have $\Upsilon_k(n) - \Upsilon_k(n-1) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid \gcd(k, n)} d\mu(k/d) = -\frac{\Delta_n\tau_k}{n}$ with $\Upsilon_k(0) = -\Lambda(k)$, and summing over $1 \le m \le n$ yields (41).

**(b)** By Theorem 3(b), $\Delta_m\mathcal{B}_{2, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) = \mathcal{B}_{1, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)$ and $\Delta_m\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m-1) = -c_k(m)$. Applying the discrete product rule $\Delta_m(u_{m-1} v_m) = u_m\Delta_m v_m + (\Delta_m u_{m-1})v_m$ to $u_{m-1} = \mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m-1)$ and $v_m = \mathcal{B}_{2, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)$ gives (43). Since $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) = \sum_{\gcd(b, k)=1}\frac{i\,e^{i\pi b/k}}{2\sin(\pi b/k)}e^{2\pi i b m/k}$ by (35) at $z = 0$ and $\Delta_m\bigl(\frac{e^{2\pi i(\xi-\xi')(m-1/2)}}{2i\sin(\pi(\xi-\xi'))}\bigr) \equiv e^{2\pi i(\xi-\xi')m}$, the zero-mean primitive of $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(m)\mathcal{B}_{1, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(m) - \frac{J_2(k)}{12}\delta_{k, l}$ at $m = M$ is the Farey–Hilbert sum in (44), whose quadratic form $\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{S}(M)\mathbf{c} = \sum_{\xi \ne \xi'}\frac{\widetilde{a}_\xi\overline{\widetilde{a}_{\xi'}}}{2i\sin(\pi(\xi-\xi'))}$ ($\widetilde{a}_{b/k} := \frac{i c_k}{2\sin(\pi b/k)}e^{2\pi i(b/k)(M+1)}$) is bounded in magnitude by $\frac{1}{2\delta_N}\sum_{\xi}|\widetilde{a}_\xi|^2 = \frac{N(N-1)(M+1)}{2}\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c}$ by [16, Corollary 2]. Expanding $\Upsilon_k(n)\Upsilon_l(n) = \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)\mathcal{B}_{1, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n^2} + \frac{\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)\mathcal{B}_{2, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) + \mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)\mathcal{B}_{1, l}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)}{n^3} + O(n^{-4})$, the periodic mean of the $n^{-2}$ coefficient is $\frac{J_2(k)}{12}\delta_{k, l}$ and the periodic mean of the $n^{-3}$ coefficient is $\mathcal{C}_{3, k, l}(0) = -\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(0)\delta_{k, l} = -\frac{J_2(k)}{12}\delta_{k, l}$ by Theorem 3(d). Since $\frac{1}{n^2} - \frac{1}{n^3} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)} + O(n^{-4})$ telescopes to $\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{M+1}$, while Abel summation by parts on the zero-mean oscillatory part $\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{k, l}(n) = \Delta_n\mathcal{S}_{k, l}(n)$ gives $\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{k, l}(n)}{n^2} = -\frac{\mathcal{S}_{k, l}(M)}{(M+1)^2} + O((M+1)^{-3})$, we obtain (44').

**(c)** By Theorem 3(b), each $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$ has the primitive Fourier expansion $\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) = \sum_{1 \le b < k,\, \gcd(b, k)=1}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{1, k}(b)e^{2\pi i b n/k}$ with $|\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{1, k}(b)|^2 = \frac{1}{4\sin^2(\pi b/k)}$ and $\sum_{\gcd(b, k)=1}|\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{1, k}(b)|^2 = \frac{J_2(k)}{12}$. Since the reduced fractions $\xi = b/k \in \mathcal{F}_N^* = \{\frac{b}{k} : 2 \le k \le N,\, 1 \le b < k,\, \gcd(b, k) = 1\}$ are **pairwise distinct** across all $2 \le k \le N$, the trigonometric polynomial $P(n) := \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\mathcal{B}_{1, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n) = \sum_{\xi \in \mathcal{F}_N^*} a_\xi e^{2\pi i \xi n}$ ($a_{b/k} := c_k\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{1, k}(b)$) has exact Parseval energy
$$E(\mathbf{c}) \;:=\; \sum_{\xi \in \mathcal{F}_N^*} |a_\xi|^2 \;=\; \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{J_2(k)}{12}\,c_k^2 \;\equiv\; (M + 1)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c}. \tag{46}$$
The minimum spacing between distinct Farey fractions in $\mathcal{F}_N^*$ is $\delta_N = \min_{\xi \ne \xi'}\|\xi - \xi'\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}} = \frac{1}{N(N-1)}$. For any interval of length $L \ge 1$, expanding $S(L) := \sum_{n=M+1}^{M+L} |P(n)|^2 = L\,E(\mathbf{c}) + \sum_{\xi \ne \xi'} a_\xi\overline{a_{\xi'}} e^{2\pi i(\xi - \xi')(M + (L+1)/2)}\frac{\sin(\pi L(\xi - \xi'))}{\sin(\pi(\xi - \xi'))}$, writing $\sin(\pi L(\xi - \xi')) = \frac{e^{i\pi L(\xi - \xi')} - e^{-i\pi L(\xi - \xi')}}{2i}$ splits the off-diagonal sum into two Hermitian Hilbert forms with kernel $\frac{1}{2i\sin(\pi(\xi - \xi'))}$, each bounded in spectral norm by $\frac{1}{2\delta_N}$ by the Montgomery–Vaughan inequality [16, Corollary 2], whence
$$\bigl|S(L) - L\,E(\mathbf{c})\bigr| \;\le\; \delta_N^{-1}\,E(\mathbf{c}) \;=\; N(N - 1)\,E(\mathbf{c}), \qquad \max\bigl(0,\, L - N(N - 1)\bigr)\,E(\mathbf{c}) \;\le\; S(L) \;\le\; \bigl(L + N(N - 1)\bigr)\,E(\mathbf{c}) \tag{47}$$
for all $L \ge 1$. Since $w_n := n^{-2}$ is strictly decreasing with $\Delta w_n := w_n - w_{n+1} > 0$, Abel summation by parts gives $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) = \sum_{L=1}^\infty S(L)\Delta w_{M+L}$. Inserting the upper bound of (47) and using $\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{1}{(M+1)^2} + \sum_{n=M+2}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} < \frac{1}{(M+1)^2} + \sum_{n=M+2}^\infty \bigl(\frac{1}{n-1} - \frac{1}{n}\bigr) = \frac{1}{M+1} + \frac{1}{(M+1)^2}$ yields $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) \le E(\mathbf{c})\bigl(\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} + \frac{N(N-1)}{(M+1)^2}\bigr) \le \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c}$. Inserting the lower bound $S(L) \ge (L - K)E(\mathbf{c})$ for $L \ge K + 1$ ($K := N(N-1)$) and using $\sum_{n=M+K+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} > \sum_{n=M+K+1}^\infty \bigl(\frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1}\bigr) = \frac{1}{M + K + 1}$ yields $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c}) \ge \frac{E(\mathbf{c})}{M + K + 1} = \mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c}$, establishing (45).

Finally, to pass from $\mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c})$ to the true tail form $\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c}$, define $P_2(n) := \sum_{k=2}^N c_k\mathcal{B}_{2, k}^{\mathrm{true}}(n)$. By Theorem 3(a) Eq. (34), $\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(n)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c} - \frac{P(n)}{n} \equiv -\sum_{m=n}^\infty \frac{P(m)}{m(m+1)} = -R_n$, where $R_n := -\frac{P_2(n)}{n(n+1)} + 2\sum_{m=n+1}^\infty \frac{P_2(m)}{(m-1)m(m+1)}$. By the triangle inequality on $\ell^2(\{M+1, M+2, \dots\})$ and Cauchy–Schwarz against $\sum_{m=n+1}^\infty \frac{2}{(m-1)m(m+1)} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)}$, we have $|R_n| \le \frac{|P_2(n)|}{n(n+1)} + \bigl(\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{m=n+1}^\infty \frac{2\,P_2(m)^2}{(m-1)m(m+1)}\bigr)^{1/2}$. Using $(a + b)^2 \le 2a^2 + 2b^2$, summing over $n \ge M+1$, and exchanging the order of summation ($\sum_{n=M+1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{M+1} - \frac{1}{m} \le \frac{1}{M+1}$, while $\frac{2}{n^2(n+1)^2} + \frac{1}{M+1}\frac{4}{(n-1)n(n+1)} \le \frac{6}{(M+1)^2 n^2}$ for all $n \ge M+1$) gives
$$\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty R_n^2 \;\le\; \frac{6}{(M + 1)^2}\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty \frac{P_2(n)^2}{n^2}. \tag{48}$$
Since $P_2(n)$ is a trigonometric polynomial supported on the exact same Farey set $\mathcal{F}_N^*$ with Parseval energy $E_2(\mathbf{c}) = \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{J_4(k) + 10 J_2(k)}{720}c_k^2 \le \bigl(\max_{2 \le k \le N}\frac{J_4(k)+10 J_2(k)}{60 J_2(k)}\bigr)E(\mathbf{c})$ by Theorem 3(d), applying (45) to $P_2(n)$ bounds $\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty R_n^2 \le \eta_{N, M}^2\,\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c}$, and the $\ell^2$ triangle inequality $\bigl|\bigl(\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c}\bigr)^{1/2} - \mathcal{T}_{N, M}^{(1)}(\mathbf{c})^{1/2}\bigr| \le \bigl(\sum_{n=M+1}^\infty R_n^2\bigr)^{1/2}$ yields (45'). $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 1 (Unconditional $O((\log N)^{-2})$ Tail Bound Beyond $M = N^{3/2}$ for Ward–Ramanujan / BCF Profiles).**
*For every $N \ge 2$ and every vector $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ in the Ward–Ramanujan / Bettin–Conrey–Farmer class $|c_k| \le C_0\,\frac{\mu^2(k)}{\varphi(k)\log N}$ ($2 \le k \le N$), the Parseval energy (46) satisfies the explicit linear bound*
$$E(\mathbf{c}) \;=\; \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{J_2(k)}{12}\,c_k^2 \;\le\; \frac{C_{\mathrm{WR}}\,C_0^2}{12}\,\frac{N}{(\log N)^2} \;<\; \frac{3\,C_0^2}{8}\,\frac{N}{(\log N)^2}, \qquad C_{\mathrm{WR}} \;:=\; \prod_p \left(1 + \frac{2}{p(p - 1)}\right) \;\approx\; 3.27951. \tag{49}$$
*Consequently, by (45'), for every $M \ge N$ the infinite-horizon tail Gram energy satisfies the unconditional bound*
$$\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} G^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\,\mathbf{c} \;\le\; \frac{C_{\mathrm{WR}}\,C_0^2}{12}\Bigl(\sqrt{\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{AMV}}(N, M)} + \eta_{N, M}\Bigr)^2\,\frac{N}{(M + 1)(\log N)^2} \;\ll\; C_0^2\left(\frac{N}{M} + \frac{N^3}{M^2}\right)\frac{1}{(\log N)^2}, \tag{49'}$$
*which is $O\bigl((\log N)^{-2}\bigr) = o\bigl((\log N)^{-1}\bigr)$ unconditionally for all $M \ge N^{3/2}$ (and $O\bigl(N^{-1}(\log N)^{-2}\bigr)$ at $M = N^2$).*

*Proof.*
For squarefree $k$, $\frac{J_2(k)}{\varphi(k)^2} = \prod_{p \mid k}\frac{p+1}{p-1} = \prod_{p \mid k}\bigl(1 + \frac{2}{p-1}\bigr) = \sum_{d \mid k} h(d)$, where $h(d) := \mu^2(d)\prod_{p \mid d}\frac{2}{p-1} \ge 0$. Summing over $2 \le k \le N$ gives $\sum_{k=2}^N \mu^2(k)\frac{J_2(k)}{\varphi(k)^2} < \sum_{d=1}^N h(d)\lfloor N/d \rfloor \le N\sum_{d=1}^\infty \frac{h(d)}{d} = C_{\mathrm{WR}}\,N$, where $C_{\mathrm{WR}} \le 2\cdot\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{11}{10}\exp\bigl(\sum_{n=6}^\infty \frac{2}{n(n-1)}\bigr) = \frac{44}{15}e^{2/5} < \frac{9}{2}$, proving (49). Inserting (49) into $\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf{T}} D^{\mathrm{tail}}(M)\mathbf{c} = \frac{E(\mathbf{c})}{M+1}$ in (45') and noting $\eta_{N, M} \ll \frac{N}{M}\sqrt{1 + N^2/M}$ yields (49'). $\blacksquare$
