# A complete quartic Gauss observation: resonance and a uniform remainder

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-03T11:50:28.727775Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/103/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We evaluate a completed bilinear observation of quartic Gauss sums over the Gaussian integers, retaining a squarefree first modulus, an ordinary second modulus, shared primes, imprimitive character zeros and all dyadic phases. A smooth profile removes the theta pole while preserving a reciprocal coefficient. Reflecting the full 24-cusp vector and applying Poisson summation in the common numerator identifies two resonant cusps. Their actual multipliers produce an explicit signed main term. Grouping the auxiliary fourth powers gives an entire Mellin transform with a nonzero value at $1/4$ and an exact finite-height resonance formula. For the remaining frequencies, a second Poisson summation in the ordinary reflected modulus yields a remainder uniform in the numerator cutoff. Its complete continuous density scales exactly as the cutoff to the power $1/4$; independently established convergence forces the total density coefficient to vanish. Consequently, for $B\ge C\ge2$ and every $A,\varepsilon>0$, the complete observation satisfies $I(C,B)=K^*B^{3/4}\widehat\Phi_0(3/4)S_C+O_{A,\varepsilon}((CB)^\varepsilon C^{1/2}B^{3/4}(C/B)^A)$, with explicit $K^*$ and $S_C=\sum_c\mu(c)\eta(c)\prod_{p\mid c}(1-(Np)^{-1})(Nc)^{-1/4}\Phi(Nc/C)$. The estimates hold for arbitrary bounded coefficients on the squarefree first modulus. Exact CPU controls check residue convolutions across all cusps, and high-precision controls check Mellin constants. At balanced scales the comparison error is $X^{5/4+\varepsilon}$, while the independent main-term envelope remains $X^{3/2+\varepsilon}$. The result isolates the reciprocal signal but does not supply an independent signed bound, a new zero-free region or a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis.
