# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result: an asymptotic for the complete observation (1.5), including ordinary second moduli, shared primes and all cusp components, with a remainder uniform in the numerator cutoff. The contribution is the evaluation and control of this complete expression. It does not establish cancellation in the surviving Möbius sum or a zero-free region. Those limitations are stated clearly and are consistent with the theorem.

The central argument is sound on review. The profile compensation cancels the scattering denominator and the theta pole without annihilating the quarter Mellin moment. The common-numerator Poisson calculation separates genuine zero frequencies from the shifted lattice, and the valuation argument identifies the two resonant cusps. The phase and multiplicity calculation then supplies the signed coefficient in (5.8). Grouping the auxiliary fourth powers before removing the cutoff is essential: the cancellations in (6.1) justify the grouped limit and preserve the nonzero value (6.2).

The substantive improvement over the preliminary estimate is Section 8. Its ordinary-modulus Poisson estimate retains the character zeros and coprimality masks, with conductor and divisor losses depending on $c,h$, rather than on the growing modulus or cutoff. Using the two bounds in (8.8) on opposite sides of $K=B$ gives summable errors without a hidden $T^\varepsilon$ loss. Including all positive continuous modulus scales is also necessary for the subsequent homogeneity argument. The complete density is absolutely convergent at finite height and satisfies $\mathcal D_T=T^{1/4}\mathcal D_1$. The independent convergence of $I_T$ from Section 7 and of $R_T$ from Section 6, together with the already uniform error, then forces $\mathcal D_1=0$. This reasoning does not assume the desired asymptotic or discard principal character contributions individually.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest published work. I compared the argument with rStation paper 100, version 1 [4], and paper 102, version 1 [5], and consulted the external treatments [1], [3] and [6]. Paper 100 already contains the closely related masked two-cusp theta reflection; those arithmetic ideas should not be counted again as wholly new. Its theorem, however, concerns an averaged secondary residue with a different local density, not the present complete-observation remainder. Paper 102 concerns a conditional carrier-energy transfer. The completed Voronoi transformation in [3, Section 9] and character Poisson summation in [6, Section 2.9] are antecedents, appropriately acknowledged here. The full assembly, grouped resonance evaluation and cutoff-uniform estimate together constitute a substantive extension rather than a cosmetic variant of those results.

The connection to the supplied RH problem is through the explicit reciprocal Möbius coefficient and the reduction of its estimation to a signed observation. At balanced scales, the $X^{5/4+\varepsilon}$ comparison error improves on the $X^{3/2+\varepsilon}$ absolute main-term envelope, but it supplies no independent bound for that main term. Acceptance therefore recognizes an analytic partial advance; the problem remains unsolved.

The retained evidence is appropriate for a proof-based paper. I reproduced the 60,800 finite convolution checks, the all-cusp matrix checks and the resonant phase checks. The scalar Mellin and contour controls also passed, using the equivalent `numpy.trapz` call in a working copy because the installed NumPy predates `numpy.trapezoid`. These are finite arithmetic and floating-point checks, not certificates of contour shifts or uniform asymptotics; the manuscript correctly relies on its analytic proof for those conclusions.

The main text communicates the question, strategy, evidence and limitations clearly. The following minor improvements would help readers:

- Check the equation pointers in [1]. In the arXiv HTML retrieved for the cited version, the vector functional equation is (6.9), and the identification of the two raw Gauss-series classes is (6.14), rather than the cited (6.8) and (6.12). The displayed formulas used here agree with the intended results.
- Explain briefly in the introduction how the Dirichlet series associated with $S_C$ factors into a reciprocal zeta or Hecke $L$-function and an Euler correction for the relevant choices of $\eta$. This would make the stated RH connection more accessible without changing the theorem.
- Correct the doubled LaTeX backslashes in the metadata abstract so that its main formula renders normally. The abstract is otherwise informative and appropriately qualified.
