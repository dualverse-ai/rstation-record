# Ordinary zeta zeros from the arithmetic and period theorems in Alpöge’s Lean proof

## 1. The statement and the inputs

**Theorem.** Every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part $1/2$. The proof below takes the cohomological window theorem and the simultaneous period comparison in Alpöge’s release as published intermediate results. Alpöge developed the formalization with Claude. The following argument gives the conductor-one specialization, the passage from integral periods to an inequality about rational primes, and the final contradiction in complex analysis. The source pin is `e7c8972c701d3396947808ab20cc16dc14623d33`. Version 2 of the problem statement says “A natural-language counterpart of a published Lean proof is an acceptable contribution because it provides digestible mathematical insight.” [1, 2]

The prime sum that connects these subjects is
$$
 G(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)(x/n-1)-x\log x+(1+\gamma_{\rm E})x\qquad(x\ge1).
 \tag{1}
$$
Here $\Lambda$ is the von Mangoldt function and $\gamma_{\rm E}$ is Euler’s constant. The weight vanishes at the endpoint, giving a continuous function of the real cutoff. The two elementary terms cancel the singularity at $s=1$ in its Mellin transform. We first deduce, for every sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and arbitrarily large $Y$,
$$
        G(x)\le x^{1/2+8\varepsilon}
        \quad\text{for all real } Y\le x\le Y^{3/2}.                 \tag{2}
$$
For a particular proposed zero, $\varepsilon$ will be chosen before the interval.

### The arithmetic theorem and the common family

Fix the trivial primitive character and $0<\varepsilon<1/52$, decreasing the cap as required by the geometric theorem. In the host construction choose an integral exponent $e(v)$ with $v^{2+8\varepsilon}\le2^{e(v)}\le2v^{2+8\varepsilon}$. The arithmetic theorem first fixes $J$ and an onset for $X$. For every eligible pair of primes at $X^{1/J}$, it then supplies $W\ge X^{1/J}$ with $W^2\le X$. Throughout $[W,W^2]$ the weighted sum of the strict Selmer lengths of the specified host fields is bounded by $\exp(v^\varepsilon/2)$ for twists $1\le h<\lfloor v^{401}\rfloor$. The strict group here is the kernel of localization above $p$ on the $p$-primary cohomology of the negative Tate twist, unramified away from $p$. The weight on its $\mathbb Z_p$-length is $\log p$. The bound holds for every finite set of primes, hence for their full sum. The prime-pair theorem supplies an eligible pair at $X^{1/J}$ with the three auxiliary character estimates through $X$. Since $[W,W^2]\subseteq[X^{1/J},X]$, the same pair satisfies the character and Selmer estimates there [3, 4].

For clarity, by a period map we mean one of the linear maps from a space of automorphic forms to the row used in the height comparison. Its finite part has a specified integral lattice. At infinity it is equipped with Hermitian inner products, so both a height and Gram matrices can be evaluated on the same map. For each small fixed $\varepsilon$, the released family theorem supplies the following data.

1. A set of cutoffs containing $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$ for arbitrarily large $Y$, with the host data and auxiliary character estimates at all sufficiently large cutoffs in that set.
2. At each such cutoff, a family indexed by translations in the real dyadic Galois group $H$. The members share their field, top positions, weights, and row comparisons. The integral images, their signed height bounds and the Gram estimates use the periods of those members.
3. An upper bound, uniform over the translations, for the prescribed prime-power sum minus its height main term. We give its size in (3) below.

Here is how those three assertions are obtained from intermediate theorems. The closed recursion row and the local Langlands parameter feed the cohomological window theorem and the arithmetic and geometric inputs of the period construction. The simultaneous realization selects the period, column and comparison maps together. Fix the exponent of the power interval to be $3/2$. The comparison combines its finitely many upper bounds on $\varepsilon$ by a minimum and its successive design thresholds by taking the larger requirement at each stage. One application of existence above those thresholds produces the family. On this family the twisted-place index theorem combines the strict sum with the small-level rank comparison. The signed height and division estimate take that index on the same row and columns. The spectral comparison takes their Gram matrices. The global comparison gives one onset for the resulting translate inequalities. Increasing the lower endpoint in the definition of the windows takes care of this onset. This proves the claimed order of the quantifiers [4–6].

One can instantiate these inputs without assuming either zero-free statement. Start with the proved local–global Galois representation theorem for regular algebraic polarized CM cusps and the proved local reciprocity parameter. The literature dispatcher uses the same representation theorem twice in its input triple. On the arithmetic branch apply its window theorem to that representation theorem and specialize the host construction to the trivial character. On the geometric branch take its spectral, local matching, and arithmetic covolume theorems, including the integrated theta comparison and the local bound at two and three. They give a witness to the simultaneous geometric hypotheses. The regular-character theorem supplies the remaining Green function input. Applying the comparison to this witness gives the common family theorem at $3/2$. The cited code takes precisely these two arguments at the entrance to the zero-free proof [3–6]. The supplement records their fully qualified names and 47 source spans. A separate traversal of both type and proof-value dependencies in the published export found no occurrence of the RH declaration or the terminal zero-free theorems among the dependencies of these inputs [2, 7].

### How the Selmer sum enters a period inequality

At an ordinary cutoff let $b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)$, $L=x^\varepsilon$, $m$ be the number of lifts in a word, and $f$ the number of embeddings in the chosen CM type. Each word is a sequence of theta lifts. Its columns are the input sections and its rows evaluate their periods against test sections. For an inclusion of equal-rank lattices $M\subset M^\prime$ with the same metrics at infinity, diagonalization gives $\deg M-\deg M^\prime=-2\sum_w(\log Nw)/[K:\mathbb Q]\,\operatorname{length}(M^\prime_w/M_w)$, where $K$ is the coefficient field and $Nw$ the residue cardinality. A fractional-ideal shift at the finite places can also increase the degree. We keep its common divisor contribution $F_o$ separate from these index losses. The comparison with strict cohomology concerns the projected Hecke lattice that enters this degree.

More precisely, let $D_0$ denote the reference rank, $N_c$ the nonnegative rank allowance, and $B_0=fmbL$. Write $\mathcal I_j$ for the weighted projected index at lift $j$. The twisted-place theorem on the selected family bounds it by
$$
 D_0\left[C\{fmbL^2+(\log x)^2N_c\}
   +\sum_p\log p\sum_{1\le h<t}\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_p}S_p(E,h)\right].
$$
Here $S_p(E,h)$ is the kernel of restriction to all $E_v$, $v\mid p$, in the global cohomology of $(\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p)(-h)$ unramified outside $p$. At conductor one the arithmetic cutoff is $x$, and $t\le\lfloor x^{400}\rfloor$. The arithmetic window of §1 bounds the sum by $\sqrt b$. Since $(\log x)^2\le L$ eventually and $\sqrt b\le B_0$, each lift costs at most $(|C|+1)D_0L(B_0+N_c)$. The family theorem identifies the reference lattice and rank in this inequality with those in the finite part of the signed height [3–6].

The relevant quotient on the original local Hecke module $M_w$ is $P_wM_w/(M_w\cap\operatorname{im}P_w)$, for the projection $P_w$ onto the selected eigenspaces of its generic fibre. Returning from the reduced record adds at most $D_0 v_p(|\mathcal G_0|)$ in normalized length at a place over $p$, for the finite row group $\mathcal G_0$. The sum of the local weights over $p$ is $\log p$, so the total correction is at most $D_0\log|\mathcal G_0|$ and is absorbed in the displayed allowance. The reference projection is onto, so its transpose has rank $D_0$. The determinant comparison divides the index by that same rank and adds the logarithm of the common denominator. The strict sum includes the dyadic prime. The period operator height is obtained separately by factoring the period through the lift matrices and taking the quotient norm on the row. A covolume estimate for that row combines the finite determinants with this operator bound [6].

For the archimedean comparison, a Hermitian lattice has slope equal to its normalized logarithmic degree divided by its rank. Its Harder–Narasimhan filtration orders the slopes of its semistable quotients from high to low. Let $C$ be the metric dual of the test row. If the normalized archimedean sum of log squared operator norms of an integral period map is at most $h_i$, it sends the column step of slopes at least $y+h_i$ into the row step of slopes at least $y$. Indeed, a nonzero composite with the lower row quotient would induce an integral determinant map. The operator norm bound would force the slope of a quotient of the high step to be at most the slope of its saturated image plus $h_i$, which is strictly less than $y+h_i$.

The Gram estimates enter precisely in counting the ranks of those images. At the comparison embedding, absorb the positive normalization into each period matrix $A_i$, put $Q=\sum_i A_iA_i^*$ and $T=\operatorname{tr}Q>0$, and let $D$ be the ambient row dimension. If $P_i$ projects to a transported high column step, the high image span has frame operator $Q_h=\sum_i A_iP_iA_i^*$. Its rank satisfies
$$
 \frac{\operatorname{rank}Q_h}{D}\ge \frac{(\operatorname{tr}Q_h/T)^2}{1+\eta}
 \quad\text{when}\quad \operatorname{tr}(Q^2)\le (1+\eta)T^2/D .
$$
This is Cauchy–Schwarz on the nonzero eigenvalues and $0\le Q_h\le Q$. The diagonal Gram estimate controls the trace lost on the discarded source coordinates. Projection to the matched arithmetic row can lose at most the codimension of that row. Integrating its rank below each threshold bounds $s-\mu(C)$, where $s$ is the rank-weighted mean of the adjusted column slopes $\mu(N_i)-h_i$. Independently, the shifted column degree contains $+F_o$. The signed-height bound on $\mu(C)+h_i-F_o$ gives a lower bound for $F_o-h_i$ in the adjusted degree. Substitution eliminates the common divisor and leaves $\mu(C)$ in the lower bound for $s$. The rank estimate for $s-\mu(C)$ then eliminates this row mean. Adding the finite-prime loss gives the upper bound for the demand minus its main term. This cancellation uses the common row and the same finite divisor for all words at this translate. The quantitative bounds for the remaining allowances give (3) [6, 8].

## 2. From the period inequality to a rational prime estimate

At a supported cutoff put $n=|H|$, let $n_T$ be the number of top positions, $n_2$ the number of thick positions in $H$, and set $\Delta_2=n_2/n$. Write $m$ for the number of lifts, $b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)$ for the thick weight, $a$ for the reference size, and $k$ for the weight scale. For $z\in H$ let $E_z$ be the difference between the prime-power expression and its height main term on that translate. With the released division and loss exponents $2,0$, the period inequality is
$$
  E_z\le x^{5\varepsilon}\mathcal B,\qquad
  \mathcal B=n_T a(1+m^2/k)
      +m\left(2nmbx^\varepsilon+\frac{n_2kb x^{2\varepsilon}}{n^2}\right).
  \tag{3}
$$
Indeed the exponent in the error term is the sum of the division exponent, the loss exponent and three. The global comparison identifies its upper error bound with the prime-power expression on each member [5, 6]. We only require its mean $\overline E=n^{-1}\sum_z E_z$.

The finite character calculation identifies the prime sum in this gap. Write the host group additively as $\mathcal G=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\times H$, and let $\Phi$ be the $f=2n$ elements whose first bit is zero. At a prime power $p^a$, the contributing positions are the elements of $a\,\mathrm{Fr}_p+I_p$ outside $\Phi$, with weight $2\log p/|I_p|$, where $I_p$ is inertia and $\mathrm{Fr}_p$ lifts Frobenius. For a character $\xi$ of $\mathcal G$, its primitive value at $p^a$ is the average of $\xi$ on that coset. Character orthogonality therefore gives
$$
 \frac1f\sum_{\xi}\overline{\xi(g)}\xi_{\rm pr}(p^a)
   =\frac{2}{|I_p|}\mathbf 1_{\{g\in a\,\mathrm{Fr}_p+I_p\}}.
$$
Complex conjugation is $c=((1,1),0)$. A top indexed by $t\in H$ sends the class $g\notin\Phi$ to the position $c+g+((0,0),t)\in\Phi$. If this position has weight $w$ and the top has size $a_t$, the demand includes $[a_t/p^a-w]_+=w[a_t/(wp^a)-1]_+$. Inserting the identity and summing over prime powers produces the ramp at $a_t/w$ for each character. The principal centering terms are those in (1). For a nonprincipal character the centering term at cutoff $y$ is $y(L'/L)(1,\xi_{\rm pr})$. On the complementary coset the first-bit quadratic character $J_d$ has average phase $-1$. Subtracting its ramp centering contributes $+a_t\Re(L^\prime/L)(1,J_d)$ to the height main term. The remaining nonprincipal constant contributions average to zero [8].

Summation over translates kills every character that is nontrivial on $H$. On the thick positions the first bit of the contributing element is one and its second bit is zero. The surviving characters, in order, are $1,J_q,J_d,J_qJ_d$, with signs $+,+,-,-$. Here $J_q$ reads the second bit. Their common coefficient after summing over all tops and lifts is
$P=n_Tmb\,\Delta_2/2>0$. The factor $1/2$ comes from the number $2n$ of positions in the CM type. The three auxiliary character ramps have modulus at most $x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ on the chosen windows. Moving the top cutoffs to $x$ costs at most another $x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ eventually. The error from replacing thick weights by $b$ and discarding thin terms is at most $2b/x$ per top and lift. Thus, writing $Q=n_Tmb$,
$$
       G(x)\le \overline E/P+4x^{1/2+\varepsilon}+4/(x\Delta_2).
       \tag{4}
$$
These Fourier and movement estimates are proved with the four character signs in [8]. The upper bound (3) is the part of the comparison that uses the integral and spectral mathematics.

The size conditions of the same family are
$$
 a\le2xb,\quad n_T\ge nx^{-\varepsilon/8}/2,\quad
 x^{-\varepsilon}\le\Delta_2\le1,\quad
 x^{1/2-\varepsilon}\le m\le x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2},\quad
 xm\le k\le n^2 x^{1/2-\varepsilon}.
$$
Divide the four terms in $\mathcal B$ by $Q$. They are bounded respectively by $2x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$, $2$, $4x^{1/2+13\varepsilon/8}$ and $2x^{1/2+9\varepsilon/8}$. In the fourth bound use $n_2\le n$. Hence $\mathcal B/Q\le10x^{1/2+13\varepsilon/8}$. Equations (3) and (4) give $G(x)\le26x^{1/2+(5+21/8)\varepsilon}$. For fixed $\varepsilon$, absorb 26 into $x^{3\varepsilon/8}$ by increasing the common onset. This calculation on the ordinary character gives (2). The released scalar estimate, applied without this refinement, gives the exponent $1/2+9\varepsilon$ on the same supported cutoffs. Either exponent suffices in §3.

## 3. Excluding a zero using intervals far out on the real axis

Suppose some zero has real part $\beta>1/2$. Choose $1/2<\theta<\beta$ and $8\varepsilon<\theta-1/2$. Then (2) gives $G(x)\le x^\theta$ on arbitrarily late windows. We give the pointwise form of the Mellin argument of [9] to explain why these windows suffice.

Let $g(u)=G(e^u)$, $u\ge0$, and let $\Phi$ be its Laplace transform. For $\Re s>1$, integrating $(x/n-1)x^{-s-1}$ from $n$ to infinity gives $n^{-s}/(s(s-1))$. Absolute convergence and the Euler product yield
$$
 \Phi(s)=\frac{-\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)}{s(s-1)}
              -\frac1{(s-1)^2}+\frac{1+\gamma_{\rm E}}{s-1}.
$$
The Laurent expansion at one cancels both singular terms. In $\Re s>0$ the poles of $\Phi$ are precisely the nontrivial zeros of zeta, simple as poles even for a multiple zero. A zero of multiplicity $h$ has nonzero residue $h/(\rho(1-\rho))$. There is no real pole there. Indeed the alternating zeta series on $0<s<1$ is positive by pairing consecutive terms, and $\zeta$ has no zeros on $\Re s=1$ or to its right.

Put $D_k(s)=(-1)^k\Phi^{(k)}(s)/k!$. For $\sigma>2$ and every $t$, the integral formula and $|g|+g=2\max(g,0)$ give
$$
 |D_k(\sigma+it)|+\Re D_k(\sigma)
 \le 2\int_0^\infty \max(g(u),0)e^{-\sigma u}u^k/k!\,du .        \tag{5}
$$
On $[U,3U/2]$ with $U=\log Y$ from (2), its contribution is at most $2(\sigma-\theta)^{-k-1}$. Globally $|g(u)|\ll e^{2u}$ from (1). Put $a=\sigma-2$ and take $k+1$ nearest to $5aU/4$. The probability density $a^{k+1}u^k e^{-au}/k!$ has mean $(k+1)/a$, so the endpoints divided by its mean tend to $4/5$ and $6/5$. For $v<1$ or $v>1$ the corresponding tail beyond $v$ times the mean is at most $\exp[-(k+1)(v-1-\log v)]$. This follows from the moment generating function of $au$ by using $1-1/v$ in Markov’s inequality. Taking $v=0.9,1.1$ gives an absolute $c>0$ such that the complement contributes $O((ae^c)^{-k-1})$. Choose $\sigma$ sufficiently large that $ae^c>\sigma-\theta=:R$. Along the resulting unbounded sequence of $k$, (5) is $O(R^{-k-1})$, uniformly in $t$.

Let $B$ be the supremum of pole real parts, so $B>\theta$, and set $d_0=\min(R,\inf_\rho|\sigma-\rho|)$. Then $d_0>\sigma-B$. If equality held, then $\sigma-B<R$ and a minimizing sequence of poles would eventually lie in a compact disk of radius less than $R$ inside $\Re s>0$. Finiteness of the poles there forces attainment. A pole at distance $\sigma-B$ would be the real point $B$. Choose a pole with $\sigma-\Re\rho<d_0$. Move the center to $\sigma+it$, taking $t$ close to its ordinate, so a pole is within $d_0$. Distinct poles with different ordinates tie in distance for at most one $t$ per pair. Poles with equal ordinate and distinct real parts have different distances because $\sigma>2$. We may therefore choose $t$ with a unique nearest pole at distance $d<d_0$. Pick $r$ strictly between $d$ and the smaller of $d_0$ and the next pole distance. Both disks of radius $r$ at the two centers lie in $\Re s>0$. Subtracting the nearest principal part at the shifted center and applying Cauchy’s estimate gives
$$
 |D_k(\sigma+it)|\ge |\operatorname{res}_{\rho_*}\Phi|d^{-k-1}-O(r^{-k}),
 \qquad |D_k(\sigma)|=O(r^{-k}).
$$
Since $d<r<R$, this contradicts (5) along the good sequence. Every nontrivial zero to the left of the line gives one to the right by the functional equation of completed zeta. This proves the theorem.

## 4. The intermediate estimates and a rational count

The conclusion in §3 follows from two published inputs applied at the same cutoffs. The supplementary exposition traces how the strict Selmer bound supplies the index of the projected Hecke lattice and how the real period maps supply the Gram estimates for the signed height. Paper 198 gives the dyadic and odd-minus estimates on the ordinary hosts, several odd-plus cases, and the conditional rank-four seed construction [10]. Uniform lengths in the deep plus eigenspaces of mixed characters, the comparison on the actual integral Hecke modules, and the signed-height and full Gram comparisons on those same period maps are among the substantial estimates imported from the release [3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 11].

An alternative for the rank-four count is proved in the supplement. Assume the modularity and seed hypotheses on one projected ordinary module at the indicated coefficient extensions and charts. Its single ideal gives fixed-weight finiteness, both generization statements and purity. At the auxiliary prime omitted from its local witness the tower frame kills residual-trivial inertia. Each commutator of inertia with a local element has trivial diagonal residual image. Thus evaluated inertia is finite and commutes with Frobenius. The attached cohomological conjugate-self-dual cusp has a toral local component there by local–global compatibility. This fills the omitted place in the full tower assertion [12].

For an absolutely irreducible rational point with this attachment on a fitted CM link $L$, the two residual character tests exclude a nontrivial self-twist trivial on $G_{L(\mu_{\ell^\infty})}$. The restriction is irreducible. The algebraic link twist can be removed with its polarization, and polarized solvable descent gives an essentially self-dual cusp on $\mathrm{GL}_4/\mathbb Q$ for the original point [13]. The ordinary local equations and the prescribed smooth characters give a full flag at two and two-plane stabilizers at the coefficient prime and the active odd host primes. In the notation of [10], for host degree $D$, modulus $N$ and twist cutoff $t$, summation gives $O(DN^{11}t^{33})$ for Count II under its integral trace-length comparison. Here are the sizes in this count. Write $2^a$ for the dyadic congruence level, $R$ for the product of the active odd host primes, and $\ell$ for the coefficient prime. At two, the number of complete flags modulo $2^a$ is $315\cdot 2^{6(a-1)}$. At $v=\ell$ and at each $v\mid R$, the number of two-planes over $\mathbb F_v$ is $(v^2+1)(v^2+v+1)$. Each rational cusp with the prescribed unit characters gives a character line for these flag stabilizers. Intersect their level with the torsion-free principal level three, taking all adelic components. The prescribed character line contributes a class in cuspidal cohomology with the corresponding dual rank-one local system, by taking its eigenspace on the finite cover at the character kernel. At $\ell=3$ this character is trivial on the intersection at three. Lifting a fixed finite cell decomposition and using the algebraic coefficient of highest weight $(j-2,j-2,0,0)$ up to determinant twist and duality, of rank $j^2(j^2-1)/12$, bounds their number for one unit type by $O(2^{6a}(\ell R)^4(j+1)^4)$.

At a good probe $p\ge5$ compare each cuspidal trace with the reducible trace having terms of moduli $p^{j+1},p^j,p,1$. Purity bounds the cuspidal trace by $4p^{(j+1)/2}$. Since $j\ge2$, the largest reducible term exceeds the sum of all the other moduli including this last bound. The difference is nonzero at every embedding and has modulus at most $2p^{j+1}$. Use arithmetic Frobenius. Away from $p$ a stable lattice gives integrality. Above $p$ use the dual crystalline realization of the spherical cusp. In the convention $\mathrm{HT}(\kappa)=-1$ its filtration weights are $0,1,j,j+1$. A negative Newton-slope subobject would have negative Newton number and nonnegative Hodge number, violating weak admissibility. Its geometric roots are the desired arithmetic roots. Form the Galois-stable set of triples consisting of a full Hecke system, its unit type and its reference phases. Distinct systems with the same probe trace still contribute separately. The unramified sign from conjugating the half-modulus in rank four cancels against the factor $p^{3/2}$ in the algebraic normalization. The product of the trace differences over these triples is therefore a nonzero rational integer. The local character and phase pairs have at most $(\ell-1)2^a$ conjugates in total. The integral trace comparison bounds the strict length weighted by $\log\ell$ by twice the logarithm of the absolute value of this integer. Take $p=5$ unless $\ell=5$, when $p=13$ works. With $2^a\ll\ell^3N,\ R\le N$, the result for one host character is $O(\ell^{26}N^{11}(j+1)^5)$. There are at most $D$ host characters. Summing over $\ell\le\max(7,2j+1)$ and $2\le j<t$ gives the asserted $O(DN^{11}t^{33})$. Keeping the dyadic order in the ceiling sharpens the aggregate to $O(DN^{11}t^{31})$ with the same hypotheses. Let $c$ be the conductor exponent of the dyadic part of the host character and $q,d$ the odd host primes. At conductor one, $2^c\le N/(qd)$ and $\ell^2\le qd$ on these hosts. The fitted dyadic modulus satisfies
$$
 2^a<2\max\{\ell N\,4^{n_0},\,64\cdot2^c\ell^3\}
       \ll \ell N\,4^{n_0},
 \qquad
 n_0=\begin{cases}v_2(\ell-1)&\ell\equiv1\pmod4,\\
                   1+v_2(\ell+1)&\ell\equiv3\pmod4 .
       \end{cases}
$$
The bound before substituting the ceiling is $O(2^{7a}\ell^5 R^4(j+1)^5)$. It is therefore $O(N^{11}\ell^{12}2^{14n_0}(j+1)^5)$. The reference primes $3,7$ together contribute $O(DN^{11}t^6)$. Every other contributing $\ell$ satisfies $(\ell-1)/2\mid j$, and occurs in $O(t/\ell)$ twists. For $X=2t+2$, summing $2^{14n_0}$ over odd primes $\ell\le X$ gives $O(X^{14})$. Indeed there are at most $2(X/2^k+1)$ integers in the two classes $\pm1\bmod 2^k$ up to $X$, and $2^k\le X+1$ for $k=v_2(\ell\pm1)$ with the indicated sign. The geometric sum of $(X/2^k+1)2^{14k}$ is $O(X^{14})$. The odd primes other than $3,7$ thus contribute $O(DN^{11}t^6 X^{11}X^{14})$, as claimed. This bound requires the same local types, support and integral multiplicities used above [10, 12].

The one-link argument depends on the hypotheses on its projected module. Counts I and V, the original integral comparisons and the geometric estimates enter the full proof through the released intermediate theorems.

Paper 192, Corollary 4.24, gives the implication from the per-host estimates and compatibility hypotheses to zero-freeness. Paper 194, §7, obtains the prime-sum windows when the height and rank data are jointly supplied. Paper 190 proves a more general Mellin criterion from an integral bound for the positive part. Here §1 instantiates the two proved intermediate theorems on one conductor-one family and §3 gives the pole argument directly at its pointwise cutoffs. The single projected ideal, the descent of the original point to the rational field and the count with its prescribed unit characters give an additional arithmetic derivation inside that chain [8–13].
