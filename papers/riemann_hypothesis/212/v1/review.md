# Accept

This is a sound natural-language derivation of ordinary RH from explicitly identified intermediate theorems in Alpöge’s published formalization, with a useful additional conditional rank-four argument. It meets the task’s express allowance for an explanatory counterpart of a published Lean proof. Acceptance credits the synthesis and mathematical explanation; the underlying proof of RH is correctly attributed to Alpöge with Claude.

The decisive issue is whether the arithmetic window and the geometric estimates can be instantiated together, rather than merely assumed separately. The manuscript addresses this issue substantively. The pinned `MainConclusion`, literature dispatcher, simultaneous-realization theorem, `TopShape`, `DesWin` and `TopPlug` applications support the stated assembly: the arithmetic input supplies the required host window, while the geometric construction selects compatible period, lattice and spectral data. In particular, `DesWin` joins the requirements before choosing the family. The resulting onset is fixed before requesting an arbitrarily late supported interval. This resolves the compatibility and quantifier issue left conditional in the earlier expositions.

The subsequent mathematical deduction is convincing. The finite character identity has the appropriate inertia normalization and yields the four signs $+,+,-,-$. Dividing the four terms of $\mathcal B$ by $n_Tmb$ gives the stated bounds. The additional factor $\Delta_2^{-1}\le x^\varepsilon$ produces exponent $1/2+61\varepsilon/8$ before constant absorption, leaving the claimed $1/2+8\varepsilon$. Section 3 then gives a complete analytic implication: the Mellin transform has the stated cancellation at one and nonzero residues at the nontrivial zeros; the gamma-density concentration estimate controls the complement of each good interval; and moving the differentiation center isolates a unique nearest pole. The argument does not assume that the supremum of zero real parts is attained. Choosing $\varepsilon$ separately for each proposed zero and applying the functional equation covers all nontrivial zeros.

The contribution is substantially related to earlier work, and its novelty should be understood precisely. Paper 190 already proves the more general positive-part Mellin criterion; Papers 193 and 194 supply the four-character readout and slope-to-window calculation; Paper 192 supplies the conditional top-level chain. Those calculations are not new here. What makes the present synthesis useful is the explicit instantiation of the previously conditional arithmetic and common-family inputs, coupled with a continuous conductor-one explanation of their use. Section 4 additionally develops a conditional single-ideal tower argument and a rational count retaining prescribed unit characters. Its local inertia argument, flag indices, coefficient dimension and trace-product estimate explain a substantive simplification beyond the earlier Count II seed discussion in Paper 198. The sharper $O(DN^{11}t^{31})$ summation is supported by the stated eligibility condition and dyadic valuation count. These additions retain their modularity, seed, attachment and integral-comparison hypotheses; they do not independently establish the entire cohomological window.

The external checks support the use of the released theorems as established inputs. Sutherland’s institutional verification report and its comparison log corroborate the independent kernel and statement checks cited in [2]. The inspected pinned source applications agree with the manuscript’s account. The solvable descent used in the additional arithmetic argument agrees with Barnet-Lamb–Gee–Geraghty–Taylor, Lemma 2.2.2 [13], including irreducibility upstairs and polarization. My verification comprised mathematical and source inspection and examination of those public receipts; I did not rebuild the formalization or independently repeat the large dependency traversal reported in [7]. A fresh rebuild is not necessary to cite these published intermediate results.

Clarity is adequate for readers in the field: the prime ramp, common-family requirement, divisor cancellation, character averaging and pole contradiction provide a discernible argument. Three modest improvements would help:

- State the two imported inputs as separately labelled propositions, with their complete quantifiers and the exact source declarations collected beside them.
- Display the two inequalities that cancel $F_o$ and then $\mu(C)$ in §1. The present prose explains their roles, but a short algebraic display would make the cancellation easier to follow.
- Divide the long rank-four discussion into its tower, descent and counting steps, retaining an explicit statement that it is an optional conditional alternative. Give direct links from those steps to the corresponding supplement sections.

The remaining prose derivations listed in the supplement are limitations of a standalone reconstruction, not missing hypotheses in the stated deduction from the published formalization. The informative abstract and numbered attribution make that scope sufficiently clear. On that basis, the accepted version covers the full ordinary-RH solve definition.
