# Simultaneous ordinary CM specialization at conjugate dyadic places

## 1. The two-place question

The dyadic height calculation in the ordinary period construction of Alpöge and Claude uses the signed orders of a theta-image lattice and an integral target at conjugate coefficient places [1, 2]. Framed evaluations give quotients of both lattices. A finite matrix of power series on an ordinary deformation space records these evaluations as the moduli point varies. Specializing a nonzero minor close to the least valuation of its coefficients at one place leaves a choice of global points. The second place must evaluate those same points if the two matrices are to describe a common list of abelian varieties. The two connected parts of the seed interchange at the conjugate places. Even the special fibre of a graph isogeny at the second place depends on its direction.

We give a simultaneous specialization theorem on the signed product seeds of strictly rectangular signature. It applies to the original finite list of framed readings whenever these have bounded denominators in the indicated ordinary coordinates. The existing results on dyadic harmonic jets at constructed CM points give bounds on individual differentiated readings [3]. Here a rational isometry on the larger definite factor returns the opposite special fibre to the prescribed *marked* seed. The left and right compact factors of this isometry prescribe the two deformation directions. The result controls which valuations can occur on the same list of CM points. The numerical sizes of the two coefficient ideals enter as arithmetic inputs.

### The seed

Let $F$ be totally real of degree $f$, with one place $v$ above two. Let $E/F$ be CM, split at $v$, and contain an imaginary quadratic field $K_0$ in which two splits. Write $K=F_v$, $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_K$. A seed is a principally polarized abelian variety with $\mathcal O_E$-action over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, an orthogonal product of $P$ pure lines of one elliptic CM type and $q$ of the other, with $0<q<P$. A pure line has one-dimensional rational $E$-module. Its integral model is obtained from a projective $\mathcal O_{K_0}$-module with scalar elliptic complex type and full elliptic CM order. On a negative line the host acts through the conjugate elliptic embedding. Use good ordinary reductions at two embeddings $w,\bar w$ over the opposite primes of $K_0$. We fix an exact odd level structure, transported under prime-to-the-level identifications. Any finite additional odd congruences can be included. The distinguished symmetric line in the period application is determined functorially by the principal polarization, the given host action and the prescribed rigidification. Indeed the pullback of the rigidified Poincaré bundle along $(1,h)$ has polarization $\lambda(h+h^\dagger)=\lambda$ for the specified host element $h+\bar h=1$, as in the cubical description of the original line [1, 3]. Odd shifts, core half-level quotients and their induced markings are included by taking the corresponding shifted product as the seed.

These hypotheses specify the variety used in the theorem. In the conductor-one host of [2, 3], the two completions above $v$ are copies of $K$. At the fitted nonterminal signatures considered here $P=k+j-1$, $q=m-j$ and $0<q<P$. Only these signatures and product points enter the following argument. The use of the pure factors will also specify which rational isometries act at the opposite reduction. A host-linear rational endomorphism on $P$ lines of the same type is a matrix over $E$. Indeed an $E$-linear rational map between their underlying lines respects the common complex type, and hence is an abelian-variety homomorphism up to isogeny. The polarization on this block is a positive definite Hermitian form.

Fix compatible generators of the $2$-power roots of unity and integral bases at both places. All ordinary parameters are taken with the special fibre identified. Over the geometric residue field the ordinary Serre–Tate extension, its polarization and its endomorphisms are described by the pairing of the étale Tate modules [4]. The connected and étale elliptic idempotents split the seed, giving the canonical lift. In the polarized host-linear deformation torus only the pairings of the two opposite host halves survive. Balancing over $\mathcal O$ and symmetry identify its character lattice with the tensor product of free modules of ranks $P$ and $q$, using conjugation on one action. Its dimension is $g=fPq$. We use trace-dual bases for the $\mathbb Z_2$-valued pairings. This includes the inverse different inside the dual lattice and gives integral coordinates even for ramified $K$.

Call a $P$-by-$q$ matrix over $\mathcal O$ primitive if its reduction modulo the maximal ideal has rank $q$. Write $\mathcal D_+$ for this open subset of $\mathcal O^{Pq}$, and $\mathcal D_-$ for the primitive $q$-by-$P$ matrices. A direction at depth $a$ specifies a torsion parameter by raising a primitive $2^a$-th root to its $g$ entries in integral $\mathbb Z_2$ coordinates. It depends only on the matrix modulo $2^a\mathcal O$. Primitivity gives exact order $2^a$. A cell at an auxiliary place in this theorem is a member of a finite partition of the graph points. In the period application it can record a framed, superspecial auxiliary reduction with prescribed odd level. The polarized superspecial locus has finitely many geometric classes at fixed dimension and polarization degree, and full finite level has finitely many choices. With a fixed rational host representation the possible integral host data here are also finite up to polarized isometry. For instance, the images of a fixed set of integral host generators have bounded positive definite Rosati norm in the integral endomorphism lattice of each fibre. The proof permits any finite refinement, including the reductions of several fixed central translates.

**Theorem 1.** There is an increasing sequence of depths such that, for every nonempty pair of open residue neighborhoods in $\mathcal D_+\times\mathcal D_-$, every sufficiently late depth on the sequence supplies a single algebraic CM graph quotient of the seed with the following properties.

1. At each of $w,\bar w$ its reduction is isomorphic to the respective seed reduction with its polarization, host action and exact odd marking.
2. In the corresponding identified Serre–Tate charts its directions at $w,\bar w$ lie in the specified neighborhoods.

For a finite partition into auxiliary cells, one cell, a nonempty open product of neighborhoods and a subsequence can be fixed so that every finite collection of smaller open neighborhoods in this product is hit in that cell at every sufficiently late depth of the subsequence. The points for a finite collection can be chosen mutually nonisomorphic. For a finite collection of seeds one can choose a common subsequence and a cell for each seed. The depth assertions are qualitative.

**Corollary 2.** On products of these charts at the two places take finite lists of nonzero power series, each with bounded denominators over a fixed local coefficient DVR. Use independent variables for independent point samples. Multiple readings of one point use the same variables, including readings pulled back from its central translates. Given $\eta>0$, a single ordered list of CM points at a common depth makes *each* series have valuation within $\eta$ of its own least coefficient valuation, at its specified place. Each seed may use the one auxiliary cell of Theorem 1 for all its samples. The conclusion holds for minors on prescribed integral source and target intersections whenever their corresponding series are nonzero.

## 2. The graph at the opposite place

Work in one host half of the seed's rational $2$-adic Tate module over $K$. Choose the half where the connected and étale lattices at $w$ have ranks $P$ and $q$, and denote them $C=\mathcal O^P$ and $U=\mathcal O^q$. On the other half use the paired dual spaces. The elliptic CM idempotents are interchanged at $\bar w$, so on the first half $U$ becomes connected and $C$ étale. The same two idempotents exist on the full-order elliptic products after the odd modifications. Changing the place of the graph point keeps its geometric isogeny kernel fixed.

Let $L=2^a$ and take an integral matrix $Z:U\to C$. In the normalized isogeny coordinates the new Tate lattice on this half is
$$
 \mathcal M=\{(c+Zu/L,u):c\in C,\ u\in U\}.                         \tag{1}
$$
On the other half use its dual for the polarization. The two graph matrices in connected-first order are transposes in balanced coordinates, up to the fixed sign convention in the alternating pairing. The full shear is host-linear and symplectic. The rational comparison is the identity at odd places. Concretely the geometric isogeny from the seed is the quotient by $L^{-1}\mathcal M/(C\oplus U)$ on this half and the corresponding subgroup on the other half. This subgroup is killed by $L^2$. Self-duality of the normalized lattice says that the full kernel is maximal isotropic for the $L^2$ Weil pairing. The resulting isogeny $f$ pulls the principal polarization of the quotient back to $L^2$ times that of the seed. The transport of odd markings uses $f/L$. The subgroup is defined over a finite torsion field, and the quotient is isogenous to the CM seed. Potential good reduction permits the finite extensions used to read both special fibres.

At $w$, (1) has connected intersection $C$ and étale projection $U$. Its extension class in the canonical rational splitting is $u\mapsto Zu/L\bmod C$. To see the torsion coordinate, lift an étale vector to the graph and subtract its lift in the split lattice. Pairing the resulting connected class with the dual module gives the Serre–Tate parameter using the chosen generator of roots of unity. Equivalently the morphism criterion for multiplication by $L$ from the canonical lift says that the parameter is killed by $L$, and the kernel over $u/L$ is the set of $L$-th roots of this parameter on $u$. This gives exactly (1). It fixes the graph sign convention for both charts [4].

At $\bar w$ the connected intersection and étale projection of the *same* lattice are
$$
 U_Z=\{u\in U: Zu\in LC\}, \qquad W_Z=C+L^{-1}ZU.                 \tag{2}
$$
In the split rational comparison the canonical lift of this opposite special fibre has lattice $U_Z\oplus W_Z$ in connected-first order. Indeed the finite flat closure of the graph kernel has multiplicative intersection described by $L^{-1}U_Z/U$ and étale projection described by $L^{-1}W_Z/C$. One can see the projection by extending the map to the constant étale quotient over a splitting trait. The projection has the same image on geometric special and generic fibres because its schematic image is the union of the components reached generically. Each reached component has nonempty special fibre by properness. On each such component translation by an integral torsion section identifies its fibre with the connected-side kernel. The latter is a subgroup of a diagonalizable group and has the indicated character-lattice description. Over a perfect residue field an ordinary finite subgroup splits into its multiplicative and reduced étale parts. This determines the geometric kernel. At the chosen place the same argument gives the special-fibre kernel of multiplication by $L$ and the marked identification via $f/L$. Over the opposite split reference, the extension in (1) sends $x=c+Zu/L\in W_Z$ to $u\bmod U_Z$. Thus it factors through the inverse of
$$
 U/U_Z \ \longrightarrow\ W_Z/C,\qquad u\longmapsto Zu/L\bmod C. \tag{3}
$$

For primitive $Z$, $U_Z=LU$. In higher rectangular rank the enlarged $W_Z$ still records the image of $Z$ modulo $L$. To identify the opposite special fibre with a fixed marked object one must identify this enlarged lattice and its paired dual by an allowed rational map.

## 3. Returning to the marked seed

Let $J:\mathcal O^q\hookrightarrow\mathcal O^P$ be the first $q$ columns of the identity. Choose an odd modulus containing the original levels and all desired odd congruences, stable under conjugation. A power $\mathfrak p_{\rm other}^{h}$ of the other elliptic prime above two is principal in the ray class group of $K_0$, with generator $\delta\equiv1$ modulo this modulus. Then $\theta=\delta/\bar\delta$ has norm one, selected valuation $-h$ at the chosen host half, and acts identically on the specified odd levels. At two its selected elliptic component lies in $\mathbb Q_2^\times$. With $n=2^j$ put
$$
 \beta=\theta^n,\qquad \alpha=\beta^P,\qquad a=hPn,\qquad
 \epsilon_a=L\alpha_v.
$$
Thus $\epsilon_a=(2^h\theta_v)^{Pn}\to1$ in $\mathbb Z_2^\times$. The reciprocal component acts on the dual host half. Central use of these elements on an entire pure block preserves its rational Hermitian form.

Put $d_a=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_v I_q,I_{P-q})$ and $b_a=\beta_v^{-q}d_a\in\mathrm{SL}_P(K)$. Let $\Gamma$ be the subgroup of the rational special unitary group of the *positive* pure block preserving all complementary integral data and acting trivially on the odd level. At $v$ the special unitary group splits as $\mathrm{SL}_P(K)$. At every real place the form is definite, which makes the group anisotropic over $F$. The arithmetic lattice theorem gives a lattice $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm{SL}_P(K)$ with compact quotient. Equivalently one takes the stabilizer of the complementary compact opens in the $S$-arithmetic quotient, with $S$ consisting of $v$ and the archimedean places. Preserving extra finite data passes to finite index. This use of the arithmetic lattice theorem is valid for the matrix endomorphism group of the actual positive product, including nonprincipal integral ideal factors [5].

For any two nonempty compact-open sets $B_1,B_2\subset\mathrm{SL}_P(\mathcal O)$, all sufficiently large $a$ on this progression satisfy
$$
 \Gamma\cap B_1 b_a B_2^{-1}\ne\varnothing.                        \tag{4}
$$
Indeed the images of both sets in $\Gamma\backslash\mathrm{SL}_P(K)$ have positive measure. Since $0<q<P$, $b_a$ leaves every compact subset, with contracting and expanding diagonal entries. The right regular representation on the orthogonal complement of the constants has no fixed vectors. The Howe–Moore theorem gives convergence of the correlation of the two indicators, with right translation by $b_a$, to the product of their means. A point in the overlap gives (4) [6]. Chai's theorem on ordinary symplectic isogeny classes supplies a related density statement in the moduli space over a field of positive characteristic [7]. Equation (4) is used here on a definite block to prescribe two $2$-adic directions of one characteristic-zero isogeny.

Take $\gamma_1=k_1 b_a k_2^{-1}\in\Gamma$ as in (4), with $k_i\in B_i$. The rational isometry $\gamma$ given on the positive and negative pure factors by
$$
 \gamma_P=\beta^q\gamma_1,\qquad \gamma_q=\alpha^{-1}I_q
$$
has selected dyadic components $k_1 d_a k_2^{-1}$ and $L\epsilon_a^{-1}I_q$, respectively. It preserves the complementary lattices and the odd marking. Set $Z=k_1J$ in (1), with its entries reduced modulo $L$ to specify the actual graph. Equations (1)–(2) give
$$
 W_Z=\gamma_P C,\qquad U_Z=\gamma_q U .
$$
On the other host half the corresponding lattices are their paired duals. The rational endomorphism $[L]\gamma^{-1}$ of the seed is integral. At two this follows from the displayed elementary divisors, including those of the dual, and at odd primes it follows from preservation of the integral lattices. Equivalently, a denominator can be divided out in the endomorphism ring once the numerator kills its full torsion kernel. At $\bar w$ the reduction of $[L]\gamma^{-1}$ has étale and multiplicative kernel lattices $L^{-1}W_Z/C$ and $L^{-1}U_Z/U$ on the first half, with the paired dual descriptions on the other. This endomorphism preserves the rational slope splitting and has pure $2$-power degree. The splitting of finite ordinary subgroups above gives equality of its geometric special-fibre kernel with $\ker\bar f$. Factoring the two isogenies by this same kernel yields an *integral isomorphism* from the opposite seed to the graph reduction. As a quasi-isogeny it is $(\bar f/[L])\bar\gamma$. It preserves the polarization, the host and the given odd marking. Exact full odd level at least three ensures uniqueness of the marked identification. Functoriality of the canonical lift identifies the two canonical reference objects. In particular the opposite parameters now use a fixed marked seed.

In its transported coordinates take $x\in C$ and write $v'=k_2^{-1}x$. To lift $\gamma_P x$ in (1) one needs
$$
             u\equiv\epsilon_a J^{\mathsf t} v'\pmod{LU}.
$$
In fact the difference $\gamma_Px-k_1Ju/L$ is integral precisely for this choice. Transporting back by $\gamma_q^{-1}$ gives the connected class $\epsilon_a^2 J^{\mathsf t}k_2^{-1}x/L\bmod U$. Thus the paired direction matrices modulo $L$ are
$$
                 k_1J,\qquad \epsilon_a^2 J^{\mathsf t}k_2^{-1}.       \tag{5}
$$
A different compatible generator of roots of unity, orientation, or trace-dual basis at either place acts by a fixed integral coordinate isomorphism. One can fix these choices in advance in both charts.

Every primitive column frame occurs as $k_1J$ with $k_1\in\mathrm{SL}_P(\mathcal O)$. Complete the columns to an integral basis and adjust the determinant on a spare column. Transposing gives the corresponding row statement for $J^{\mathsf t}k_2^{-1}$. Given prescribed neighborhoods, choose compact-open preimages, small enough that the second neighborhood survives multiplication by $\epsilon_a^2$ for all large $a$. Equations (4) and (5) prove the two direction assertions of Theorem 1.

## 4. One auxiliary cell and common depths

The primitive direction spaces have a countable basis of compact-open residue balls. Choose lifts of the matrices in (5) for the purpose of these neighborhoods. Whether a graph hits a fixed ball is independent of this lifting choice once $a$ exceeds the ball's precision. For each ball in the product and each auxiliary cell record whether it is hit at a given depth. Successive extraction from the depth sequence, followed by a diagonal subsequence, makes each of these countably many binary records eventually constant. This also works simultaneously for finitely many seeds, after using a common multiple of their ray moduli and $hP$ in the original progression. For distinct $P$, take depths divisible by each $hP$ and then multiply those depths by powers of two.

For a cell let $D_{\rm cell}$ consist of directions all of whose basis neighborhoods are eventually hit in that cell on the subsequence. This is closed. The finitely many such closed sets cover the paired direction space. Otherwise a direction outside all of them would have a common sufficiently small neighborhood that every cell eventually misses, contradicting (4)–(5). Some closed set has nonempty interior. Choose a product ball inside it. Each smaller neighborhood in that ball is then hit at every sufficiently late depth. Finitely many samples may be chosen independently at that same depth. To ensure geometric distinctness, refine the first-place balls to disjoint residue orbits under the finite polarized host-linear automorphism group of the seed. Each nonempty open ball contains infinitely many such orbits. Isomorphic lifts in one tube induce an automorphism of the special fibre carrying one direction to the other. Between a finite number of isomorphic seed presentations one may similarly avoid the finitely many isomorphisms of their polarized special fibres. This proves the last assertion of Theorem 1. The cell and the sufficient depth depend on the finite partition and the neighborhoods. Their selection by subsequence and mixing is qualitative.

## 5. Simultaneous coefficient valuations

Normalize both valuations by $\nu(2)=1$. For a nonzero bounded-denominator series $F$ over a fixed coefficient DVR let $m(F)$ be its least coefficient valuation, which exists by discreteness. We use the following fact about a formal multiplicative torus of dimension $d$. In every open ball of primitive directions in $\mathbb Z_2^d$ there is a smaller ball and an integer $j_F\ge0$ on which the torsion evaluations of $F$ have value
$$
                 \nu(F)=m(F)+j_F 2^{1-a}                              \tag{6}
$$
at every sufficiently large depth $a$. One ball works for a finite list, with its respective $j_F$.

To prove this, choose a congruence $c\bmod 2^b$ within the given ball, with some odd entry. Complete a lift of $c$ to the first column of $C_0\in\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb Z_2)$. In the formal torus change coordinates by $1+T_i=\prod_j(1+Y_j)^{(C_0)_{ij}}$. The inverse substitution is given by $C_0^{-1}$. Both are integral, so they preserve least coefficient valuations. Divide each series by a coefficient attaining its minimum and reduce modulo the uniformizer. For each nonzero residue series select its least exponent in lexicographic order starting at the last coordinate. Choose a power of two $B$, divisible by $2^b$, larger than every coordinate of the finitely many chosen exponents. The weights $(1,B,\ldots,B^{d-1})$ make each selected exponent the unique one of minimum weight in its series. At the last differing digit the increase dominates all possible decreases in the earlier digits. Substituting $1+Y_i=(1+T)^{B^{i-1}}$ sends the residue parameters to $T^{B^{i-1}}$ in characteristic two. The resulting one-variable series first has a unit in degree $j_F$ equal to that minimum weight. The resulting direction in the original coordinates is congruent to $c\bmod 2^b$.

If $2^b$ is enlarged to a precision $2^{b'}>\max j_F$ around this new direction, every direction in the new ball gives the same reduced substitution through those degrees. Indeed $(1+T)^{2^{b'}}=1+T^{2^{b'}}$ in characteristic two. At a primitive $2^a$-th root minus one the valuation is $2^{1-a}$. Once $j_F2^{1-a}$ is below the coefficient uniformizer valuation, all terms of lower degree have strictly higher valuation than the first unit term. Higher degree terms also have strictly higher valuation. This proves (6), including $j_F=0$.

Apply this fact to each place in the full product of independent samples inside the product balls of §4. Each sample has primitive directions, and the combined parameter vector has an odd coordinate. The finitely many series at a place admit a common smaller open neighborhood, which contains a product of residue balls for the independent samples. Theorem 1 hits the two sets of requirements at once with all samples at a common depth and their assigned cells. Central odd transports are applied to the chosen samples and all their readings are pulled back before testing for nonvanishing. Equation (6) at both places proves Corollary 2.

For the determinant consequence, fix the fraction kernel of the old rows locally and intersect both the theta-image lattice and the original saturated target with this kernel. Fix a list of readings on these intersections. At each place and for each lattice take the ideal generated by all Taylor coefficients of the minors using the chosen rows and as many columns as rows. Its order is a minimum over finitely many minors since the lattices are finitely generated. The variables for all rows at one sample agree in these determinants. Suppose all four ideals are nonzero. Choose a minimizing minor for each. Every evaluation has order bounded below by the relevant coefficient order. Corollary 2 simultaneously brings each of the four minimum evaluated orders arbitrarily close to its coefficient order. The sum over the two places of the signed theta-minus-target quotient orders therefore tends to the sum of the two signed coefficient-ideal differences. The statement also applies to a finite list of prefixes with nonzero ideals. It is an infimum statement for the individual minima along a common sequence. Bounding the signed differences on the residual rank of the original period space requires estimates on its actual theta and target coefficients. This is the open arithmetic input to the height comparison [2].
