# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive qualitative specialization result for the explicitly restricted class of ordinary CM product seeds. Its contribution is the simultaneous prescription of two Serre–Tate directions for the **same algebraic graph quotient**, while returning both reductions to the prescribed polarized, marked seeds. This addresses a genuine compatibility issue in the period-index application. It is a useful partial advance, not a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis: the required estimates for the actual theta and target coefficient ideals remain open.

The central argument is sound under the stated seed hypotheses. At the opposite place, the connected intersection and étale projection are correctly distinguished from those at the first place. For primitive $Z$, the identities $U_Z=LU$ and $W_Z=C+L^{-1}ZU$ explain why merely interchanging the two blocks would not suffice. The construction in §3 supplies the missing global comparison: the positive-block rational unitary isometry and the scalar on the negative block match these lattices and their paired duals. Comparing the special-fibre kernels of $f$ and $[L]\gamma^{-1}$ then gives an integral polarized identification. The full-order elliptic CM and pure-product assumptions are essential here.

Recomputing the transported extension gives the two directions in (5), including the factor $\epsilon_a^2$. The use of Howe–Moore mixing is appropriate: the definite global special unitary group supplies the arithmetic lattice, while $0<q<P$ makes $b_a$ escape compact subsets of $\mathrm{SL}_P(K)$. The spare column also permits determinant-one completion of every primitive frame. Together these facts justify independent prescriptions in fixed open neighborhoods. The subsequent finite-cell extraction proves the stated conclusion: one unspecified cell on a smaller open product along a subsequence.

The coefficient argument in §5 is also sufficient. After an integral torus coordinate change, the lexicographically selected exponent becomes the unique least-weight exponent for a sufficiently large power-of-two weight base. Stability of the first nonzero reduced coefficient on a smaller direction ball yields (6). Applying this to finitely many series and independent samples gives Corollary 2. The determinant consequence correctly retains the original source and target intersections and approaches their individual coefficient minima simultaneously. Convergence of the signed difference does not itself give a numerical bound on that difference, a limitation the manuscript explicitly recognizes. These conclusions were assessed through the mathematical arguments; no computational experiment or verification of the full external RH release is claimed.

The result is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work checked. Paper 208 [2] estimates selected prime expenses and a limitation of a uniform allowance; Paper 209 [3] studies harmonic jet depths at constructed CM points. Neither supplies the present simultaneous directional prescription. Classical Serre–Tate theory [4] and the local CM-density results discussed by Shankar–Zhou [8] provide important background but do not prescribe conjugate-place directions of one global quotient. Chai's result [7] concerns Zariski density in characteristic $p$. Aka–Luethi–Michel–Wieser [9] prove simultaneous supersingular reduction results as CM discriminants grow; their setting and conclusion differ from the fixed-seed ordinary deformation problem here. Adding a short comparison with [8] and [9] would improve attribution and positioning without changing the novelty judgment.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The paper identifies the obstruction, isolates the marked-fibre repair, and derives the valuation application in a logical order. The abstract describes both the result and its limits. Minor improvements would help: explicitly say whether $v$ is the unique dyadic place, so that the use of $f$ in the coordinate dimension is unambiguous; state when an auxiliary full odd level at least three is imposed for uniqueness; and distinguish the conditional period application from the unconditional specialization theorem directly in the final determinant paragraph. In particular, that application requires bounded-denominator expansions and nonzero coefficient ideals on the selected charts, not merely nonvanishing somewhere in the ambient moduli space. These are scope and presentation clarifications, not grounds for rejection.
