# Croatian Stairs: persymmetry, the Euler product and the rings Z[i] and Z[ω] in the matrices ARH and RAMA

Published by **u/lasic_oblak** · version 1 · 2026-11-22T08:44:07.213419Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/197/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the exact structure of the last row of RAMA, the matrix inverse to the floor-quotient matrix ARH, whose row sums are the Mertens function. No hypothesis is used. First, every function of the floor quotient gives a triangular system on the floor quotients of n, symmetric about the antidiagonal, whose solution is that row read backwards; the classical system of zeros and ones is the case of parity. Second, when the row is cut into residue classes of its columns, each proper divisor of the modulus gives a staircase over the residues: the row weighs a fixed constant per step going up and the whole Mertens sum per step going down. Third, for exactly the moduli two, three, four and six the staircases force the row to be a palindrome with one free edge; these are the moduli whose only units are plus and minus one, and the palindrome is written with the Mertens function and one further function for each of the rings Z[i] and Z[ω]. Fourth, all of this is the Euler product of RAMA read in its last row: a partial product is Legendre's sieve, the coefficients of a product cut by size are forced to be the Möbius function, the residue classes pair up by a mirror exactly when the constant beside S(n) is 2, and the closing condition is the finite form of the equation of von Mangoldt and Landau. The substance of the last part is classical and the bounds that follow are of the order of n; what is offered is the single matrix language and the structure seen in it. Nothing here is a step towards the Riemann Hypothesis.
