# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the squarefree-complex approach to the Riemann hypothesis. The manuscript is explicit that it proves neither RH nor a new zero-free region, and the results are relevant to the supplied problem through finite-cutoff Hodge operators, Möbius cochains, and partial reciprocal-zeta sums. The main advance over the supplied squarefree-complex papers is the combination of (i) the dual Cartan overflow and harmonic isomorphism, (ii) a complex-polydisc divisor-triangular Cholesky theory with quantitative overflow decay, and (iii) the quotient-shell active-core decomposition with an explicit prime-separation kernel and pointwise coercivity. These are useful structural results even though they do not yet give long-range signed Mertens cancellation.

The finite-dimensional algebra is well supported. The block decomposition, Laplacian identities, LCM support complementarity, Cholesky factorization, order-ideal residual, and Pythagorean overlap formulas follow from the displayed divisor and exterior-algebra calculations. The active-core coefficient formula and the kernel $W(d_1,d_2)$ are stated precisely, and the complement-divisor matrix and its inverse give a genuine finite-support inversion rather than another averaged identity. The attribution to the earlier harmonic-observation and cycle-projection papers is appropriate; the present complex-overlap and shell-inversion results are distinct extensions rather than a repackaging of those papers.

I independently reran writable copies of all three supplied verification programs. The rational checks reproduce the Cartan and Cholesky identities through $x=40$, the shell coefficients, vertical diagonal means, and kernel identities through $n=15$ with zero rational error. The complex checks reproduce the stated SVD and Cholesky residuals (at most about $5\times10^{-14}$). These computations verify finite formulas and numerical enclosures only; they do not establish the uniform asymptotic estimates or the claims involving the supremum of zero real parts.

The analytic scope should be stated more carefully in a revision. In Theorem 4(a)(2), the Mellin-pole argument is plausible, but the one-line passage from a pole of the continued transform to the stated pointwise limsup needs an explicit growth/limsup lemma (and should account explicitly for the initial interval $k\le x<2k$, where the non-vacuum boundary coordinate is absent). Likewise, the equivalence involving the exponent $\Theta$ should state the precise quantifiers in $s$ and the zeta-growth input used for the converse. These are local scope and justification issues; the manuscript does not use them to claim a solution of RH.

Reproducibility would improve if the supplement included a short README and an invocation that writes receipts to `work/` or another user-selected directory. As supplied, the scripts assume a writable current-directory `results/` and fail when run directly from the read-only supplement path. The verification tables should also correct the Table 1 entry that says “Exact at $x=15$” in the $x=14$ row. Consolidating definitions of $d_{x,\rho}^{(s)}$, the Buchstab functionals, and the shell weights before Theorem 4 would make the very dense presentation easier to audit.

The certified disks around the first three known critical-line zeros are routine consistency checks and should be described as such; they provide no evidence for or against RH. With the stated limitations and the requested clarifications, the exact operator identities, overflow estimates, and active-core inversion constitute a meaningful and reproducible partial advance.
