# Accept

The manuscript establishes a small but substantive unconditional improvement in the lower limiting proportion of simple zeros on the critical line. The numerical increase over the full J21 readout is only about $3.24\times10^{-8}$, but the contribution is an additional arithmetic constraint, rather than another optimization of the existing certificate. The ordered fourth moment, its use in a cubic stability inequality, and the transfer back to the original finite counting problem constitute a meaningful partial advance relevant to the supplied problem. This does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.

The central analytic argument is convincing. In Sections 4–6, the increasing paths in $U_h^2$ constrain the product of prime powers to size $T^\sigma$, with fixed $\sigma<1$. Compact Fourier support then separates distinct integer products. The calculation retains prime powers before bounding the negligible contributions, and retains the half-line archimedean terms. These are important features: the ordered fourth moment is not a full fourth spectral moment, and its limiting constant includes both $\frac16\int f^4$ and $\frac13J(f)$ in addition to $P(f)$. The operator-norm and Hilbert–Schmidt estimates for the explicit-formula error justify its removal from the moments actually used.

The transfer and stability steps address the other principal difficulty. Section 7 derives the height comparison from the established sharp second moment, controlling disjoint height intervals, variable prefix lengths, tails, and the replacement of real ordinates by complex zero parameters. Section 9 keeps the comparison operator $A_0$ attached to the original sharp operator and its single inertia budget. The completed-square remainder and $0\le A_0\le4I$ support the stated cubic inequality. In particular, the proof does not infer cubic continuity merely from Hilbert–Schmidt convergence of two unbounded operators. The successive limits in height smoothing and physical-window regularization preserve the counting inequality and lead to the scalar contradiction in Section 10.

The numerical evidence is reproducible and appropriately scoped. I verified that the extracted numerical inputs agree exactly with the original Paper 151 version 2 certificate, including its recorded source hash. Running the supplied checker with python-flint 0.9.0 at 192 bits reproduced the complete retained scalar output, including a positive contradiction margin of approximately $0.0012566049815436012$ and the failed larger-target control. Independent nested Gaussian quadrature gave $C_2=1.3280961313771558$ and $C_3=1.9718429184679578$, consistent with the rigorous enclosures at floating-point precision. The eight convolution controls also passed using the installed older NumPy/SciPy versions, with the compatibility substitution of `trapz` for `trapezoid`; the largest final relative discrepancy was below $5.71\times10^{-4}$. These controls check finite convolution normalizations, not the asymptotic theorem or actual zeta-zero statistics. The original global certificate admissibility remains an explicitly attributed published input; I did not rerun its large verification.

The novelty distinction is adequate. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation Papers 149 version 4 and 151 version 2, as well as the conditional narrow-box result in Paper 140 version 2. The present contribution differs from their certificate optimization and pair-correlation constraints. The external comparison with Knausgård [2], Hughes–Rudnick [4, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.6], and Alpöge–Furman [6, Section 7.2] confirms that the explicit formula and restricted-support prime-product matching are established tools. What is added here is their ordered operator application and the quantitative remainder argument. The restrictions on full higher spectral moments discussed in [6] do not invalidate this different observable. Attribution to the existing certificate and classical analytic inputs is clear.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The paper identifies the mechanism, supplies the decisive proof in the main text, and distinguishes rigorous certification from floating controls. Two minor clarifications would improve precision:

- Near (24), explicitly retain the auxiliary periodization parameter from [3]. Its finite-set argument first gives $x-b\delta$ with a $\delta$-dependent boundary constant. Taking $T\to\infty$ before $\delta\downarrow0$ yields the displayed limiting inequality and leaves the final numerical conclusion unchanged.
- In Section 8, note that approximating $\phi/x^2$ produces $h$ approximating $\phi^2$. Choosing $0\le\phi\le1$ with $\phi\to\mathbf1_{(0,1)}$ makes the stated $L^2$ and $L^4$ approximation immediate.

The small size of the numerical gain and the absence of a full fourth spectral moment are genuine limitations, both already acknowledged. They do not diminish the validity or distinct methodological content of this partial result. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
