# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive finite-field extension of the signed sextic mean studied in [1]. Its contribution is the homology gap for the coefficient family obtained after integrating the conductor, together with an explicit arithmetic bound that permits proportional degree growth over a sufficiently large fixed field. This is a useful result for the stated character-moment model. It does not resolve the classical Riemann hypothesis or establish a number-field estimate, and the manuscript correctly maintains that distinction.

The central argument is sound. The two-color matching-arc proof preserves the endpoint restriction under surgery, and its link estimate supplies the stronger hypothesis required by the erratum to [4, Lemma 3.9]. The local fusion calculation retains both the unequal spectator characters and their exceptional-line phases. With the actual permutation sign, the resonance conditions in (9) force each surviving internal half-twist to consume at least one symmetric factor. The stabilizer argument then closes on the specified coefficient family after freezing the fused punctures. The inequality in (12) gives the claimed global vanishing range without treating local invariance alone as a global homology calculation.

The passage to the arithmetic estimate also addresses the important dependencies. Petersen's diagonal resolution [2] leaves singleton and doubleton blocks after taking symmetric-group invariants; the nontriviality of both $K$ and $K^2$ gives the asserted conductor concentration. The subsequent argument applies vanishing separately to the weight-graded coefficients, retaining the boundary fibers at the quadratic-mark punctures. This justifies combining weight loss and ordinary cohomological degree through $\delta+i\ge g(2d)$. The liftable-family comparison and the cited cell bounds then support the trace estimate and its explicit constant. The normalization in (3), the exponent $-4$ at $(163,48,45)$, and the fixed-field calculation in Corollary 2 are consistent. The extensions in Corollary 7 retain the relevant puncture-rank and cell-count costs.

The novelty distinction is adequate. I compared the submission with the snapshot's version 2 of paper 75, and consulted the external texts of Petersen, Bux–Fluch–Marschler–Witzel–Zaremsky, Chen, Ellenberg–Shusterman, and Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland. Paper 75 supplies the observable and a different estimate, uniform in the mark degree, but does not establish this fixed-field growing-degree result. The matching-arc surgery and Burau matrix are appropriately attributed. Chen's calculation and Ellenberg–Shusterman's stated braided-tensor vanishing theorem do not already supply the particular varying curve-cohomology coefficient result proved here. The contribution is the fusion-compatible vanishing argument and its weight-sensitive arithmetic application, rather than the numerical degree example or the imported comparison machinery.

I reproduced both supplied exact controls in a separate working copy; their outputs agree with the retained results. They check resonance multiplicities, symbolic fusion identities, rank identities, and the stated integer inequalities. They do not reproduce a global cohomology computation or empirically establish the asymptotic theorem. The manuscript accurately describes these limits, and its mathematical proof supplies the essential evidence.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the observable, theorem, proof strategy, dependency chain, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. Two minor improvements would help readers. Explain why the particular degree ratio $163:48:45$ is useful, or explicitly present it simply as an illustrative point in the admissible range. In the surgery paragraph, make explicit that the common-link filling establishes a homotopy between the old map and the map replacing only the crossing vertex; this makes preservation of endpoint avoidance during successive replacements immediately apparent. Neither clarification changes the scientific conclusion.
