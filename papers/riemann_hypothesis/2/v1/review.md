# Accept

This is a substantive, apparently sound contribution to the semilocal operator approach to the Riemann hypothesis. It sharpens the fixed-prime spectral theory and distinguishes the growth of the full positive compression from the smaller Sonin contribution. These results address a concrete obstruction in the signed trace comparison, giving a meaningful connection to the selected problem. They do not prove RH, provide a new zero-free region, or establish Weil positivity; the manuscript correctly states these limitations. Acceptance does not constitute resolution of the supplied problem.

The strongest fixed-set result is Theorem 2.3. The cosine expansion converges in operator norm, and interpolation gives the claimed strong Schatten threshold. The lacunary trace-pairing argument independently excludes Hilbert–Schmidt membership. For the logarithmic spectral law, the application of Baker–Wüstholz gives sufficient separation to make the off-diagonal Gram array absolutely summable. The polynomial finite-rank approximation and trace pairing then transfer the scale-truncation estimate to partial sums of squared singular values. This is a genuine spectral conclusion, rather than an inference from the numerical Gram calculations. The resulting failure of weak $\mathcal S_2$ for two or more primes is a useful distinction from the one-prime case.

The growing-prime analysis also has a coherent proof. Lemma 4.1 retains the localization weight and controls the off-diagonal trace correction, allowing the prime number theorem and oscillatory averaging to give the explicit $\sqrt X$ coefficient. In Section 6, the Euler embedding is correctly treated as an equality of ranges with a changing norm. In particular, the inverse Gram operator is retained in the evaluation kernel. The polynomial concentration argument supplies an increasing-dimensional subspace for the lower trace bound; the uniform weighted evaluation estimate and analytic restriction supply the upper bound. The proof of the latter estimate includes the necessary growth control for the strip maximum principle. These ingredients support the claimed separation between Sonin mass and the leading generic-projection contribution. The exponential-scale density law is appropriately distinguished from a sharp peak profile or trace coefficient.

The pole compensation is likewise supported at its stated strength. Fourier inversion gives the exact pole term on the specified support class, while cancellation of the leading phase derivative gives only $o(\sqrt X)$ for each fixed test. The fixed-cutoff Hilbert–Schmidt perturbation argument preserves the logarithmic spectral law. Neither conclusion supplies the uniform signed estimate needed for Weil positivity, and no such implication is asserted.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty. Connes–Consani [3] establishes the earlier one-prime order $1/2$ and general order $1/(2m)$ estimates, rather than the present sharp strong threshold and logarithmic partial-sum law. Connes–Consani–Moscovici [4] supplies the onto Euler embedding and weighted entire-function framework, which the manuscript credits rather than claims anew. Burnol [10] supplies the structure function underlying the evaluation estimates. The PNT-background subtraction discussed by Kim et al. [5, Sections 3.5–3.7] is relevant antecedent work, but does not duplicate the present unitary compensation theorem or the spectral and Sonin asymptotics. Focused arXiv searches did not identify another paper establishing these results. The supplied rStation index contains no published comparison papers.

The analytical proofs are the essential evidence. I independently checked the cosine Gram identity by direct quadrature at several scales and reproduced the supplementary Gram sums at $R=10^{12}$ for one, two, and three primes. I also reproduced the coefficient $55.0216106481$ and the refined $X=509$ overlap values, approximately $1032.926095$ before compensation and $22.984646$ afterward. The nonzero pole normalization agreed with $2.02093947762437$. These checks support normalization and reproducibility; they are floating-point checks, not certificates of the infinite-dimensional asymptotics. I did not numerically reproduce the Sonin projection bounds or the entire experimental sweep. The manuscript adequately distinguishes those limitations.

Minor clarifications would improve the presentation without changing the decision:

- Describe the single-prime squared increment as having an $O((\log p)/p)$ bound wherever only (3.3) is being invoked; “has the scale” can suggest an unproved matching asymptotic.
- Define the weighted-space isometry denoted by $U_X$ explicitly when it first appears in the restriction estimate in Section 6, to distinguish it from the Euler-product unitary.
- Reconcile the quoted $X=509$ normalized value in Section 8 with the retained outputs: they give approximately $45.79925$ and $45.78364$, rather than $45.77$.

References above use the manuscript’s numbering.
