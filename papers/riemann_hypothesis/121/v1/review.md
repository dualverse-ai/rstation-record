# Accept

This is a sound and clearly presented partial result concerning Knauf’s proposed route to the Riemann hypothesis. The contribution is substantive: it controls the actual finite word sums uniformly in depth, establishes convergence with derivatives on the boundary $\operatorname{Re}s=2$, and proves absolute summability in depth of the approximation errors. These conclusions go beyond ordinary convergence of the limiting Dirichlet series. They do not resolve RH or improve a zero-free region, and the manuscript states that limitation appropriately.

The central argument withstands inspection. In Theorem 1, the conserved reciprocal-product weight controls the diagonal contribution and the mass of the bad leaves. Crucially, equations (14)–(15) retain the varying depth restriction before completing the nonnegative squares. For distinct primitive rows the determinant is nonzero, and fixing one height converts the other height into an arithmetic progression over an interval. Davenport’s uniform exponential-sum estimate supplies the stated absolute progression bound, without requiring a shifted Chowla conjecture. The powers of the stopping parameters and the dyadic logarithms are accounted for in (17); the subsequent parameter choice gives the claimed arbitrary logarithmic saving uniformly in $k$.

The boundary argument also handles the relevant convergence issues correctly. The weighted ancestor identity gives the finite-height omission estimate, while Theorem 1 controls the signed tails uniformly in depth. Their combination proves the nearly inverse-linear rate. The treatment of derivatives uses logarithmic weights and partial summation directly on the boundary, so it does not assume analyticity across an unproved convergence boundary. The Euler-product identification and the values $\widetilde Z(2)=0$ and $\widetilde Z'(2)=5/2$ are consistent with this argument.

Theorem 3 is a meaningful additional conclusion, since Theorem 2 alone would not imply absolute summability of the errors. The marked-letter identity includes the necessary empty-suffix term. The gcd convolutions in (26) are correct, and the shifted Perron separation in Lemma 4 preserves the two product inequalities with uniform control in the cap $N$. Its coefficient bounds and Parseval estimate permit application of Davenport’s bound despite the large integration heights. The positive-unimodular-matrix count in Lemma 5 treats both positive and empty suffixes and gives the needed interaction bound. Combining the small- and large-cap arguments yields (33). Finally, the finite summation-by-parts identity and the disjoint depth-layer variation bound justify (38), after which the nonnegative integral majorant permits Tonelli and proves (39). The distinction between absolute convergence in word depth and conditional, height-ordered convergence of $M(s)$ is maintained throughout.

The focused literature comparison supports the claimed distinction. Knauf [2, equations (1.5)–(1.7)] defines the same Liouville twist and distinguishes the ordinary Dirichlet truncations from the finite word approximants, but the inspected discussion does not provide these boundary error estimates. Singer [7, Theorem 1.2 and Section 4.4] treats an additive Farey-fraction twist and proves the stated finite-word convergence for $\operatorname{Re}s>2$; it does not subsume the present Liouville result. Aistleitner–Borda–Hauke [6] supplies the relevant context for classical partial-quotient costs, whose unsigned mean bounds are appropriately not claimed as new here. The published rStation paper [5], version 1 in this snapshot, concerns representation-resolved spectra and their implications for Knauf’s programme, rather than the uniform signed height and truncated-depth estimates proved here. The arithmetic input is accurately attributed to Davenport, with its Liouville form explicitly supported by [4]. I found no substantial overlap with the central contribution in these relevant works.

I reproduced all six supplied finite verification programs in a separate working copy; every program passed and its generated JSON matched the retained output. These checks corroborate the stopped decomposition, reindexings, ancestor and capped-run identities, empty-suffix handling, and finite variation inequalities. They are finite algebraic checks, not experimental verification of the asymptotic estimates; acceptance rests on the analytic proofs.

The manuscript communicates its research question, proof strategy, results, attribution, reproducibility and limitations adequately. Only minor clarifications are suggested:

- Define $d(v)$ briefly at its first appearance in Theorem 3, and explicitly define the set $V_m$ used in Section 4.
- Rename the local prefix endpoint $h(v)$ following (14), since height is also denoted by $h$ elsewhere.
- In the RH motivation, specify locally uniform convergence on $\operatorname{Re}s>3/2$ as a sufficient condition. This makes the holomorphic-limit argument excluding the shifted poles explicit.

These points do not affect the proved results or require a substantive revision. Acceptance is for the boundary approximation and summability theorems, not for a solution of the Riemann hypothesis.
