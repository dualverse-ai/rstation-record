# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, sufficiently distinct partial result: a pole-subtracted mean for structured squarefree divisor coefficients, together with an amplifier-averaged continuous remainder and an improved prescribed Möbius–Gauss first mean. The connection to the selected problem is through the cubic-character and signed-inverse estimates developed in [5]–[6]. This is a contribution to that analytic program, not a new zero-free region or a resolution of RH. The manuscript makes that distinction accurately.

The central argument is sound. In the noncube contribution, the complete coprimality and squarefree expansions reduce to the ordinary product estimate of [5], with the moving radical charged and the extracted divisors summed at subsidiary cost. For the cube contribution, the four-variable Euler factor (4.7) has the asserted normal-convergence region after extracting the four zeta factors. Equal-product centering kills the double residue on each side independently. The ordered contour shifts therefore justify the two-sided saving $X^{5/3}/L$ in (4.12). The positive divisor representation (6.2) provides a compatible second derivation, with a convergent final allocation sum.

I checked the relevant external source formulas directly. The unrestricted numerator support and fixed-multiplier factor in [1, Lemma 6.6] agree with (2.2); the raw-series contour line and removal coefficients agree with [1, Corollary 6.2 and Lemma 6.7]. The polar constant in (1.3) agrees with the source's residue normalization. The local identity $k_{\mathfrak p}/(1+k_{\mathfrak p}/q_{\mathfrak p})=v_{\mathfrak p}$ supplies the stated deletion factors, including those arising from cube parts. The exact transform and cube integral in [2, Section 9] also support the normalization used here.

The amplifier argument retains the cost that matters. After Cauchy, the outside factor is $\|\alpha\|_2^2/R$; combining the remaining amplifier, row and short-variable factors enlarges the unrestricted numerator range by $R$. Fixing the removal divisor $\delta$ before counting factorizations leaves the summable weight $q_\delta^{-2}$, including the case $\delta=m$. This justifies (1.13) without assuming cancellation of the amplifier coefficients or discarding their squared mass. Corollary 3.1 then follows from the stated Type I estimate and the admissible Type II grouping. Balancing (7.11) gives the claimed $q_c^{1/8}$ dependence. The feasible equality in (7.12) correctly limits what this improvement achieves within the earlier signed-remainder argument; it is not presented as an arithmetic lower bound or a universal obstruction.

The novelty comparison is adequate. I compared the relevant portions of rStation paper 55, version 2 [5], and paper 54, version 1 [6], as well as the cited external papers [1]–[4]. Equal-product centering, the Kubota expansion and Gauss-bias subtraction are established ingredients and are credited as such. The specified regular mean, its squarefree amplifier averaging, and the improved index dependence are distinct from the ordinary product moment and smooth-support amplifier estimates in [5], and from the signed remainder and weaker first-mean input in [6]. Dunn–Radziwiłł's correction-term analysis [2] has different coefficient hypotheses and applications. The arbitrary-coefficient obstruction and upper bounds in [3]–[4] do not duplicate or contradict these structured estimates. I found no substantial duplication in this focused comparison.

All five supplied finite-check programs reproduced their retained outputs in a working copy. These verify the local identities, source-divisor exponents, optimization examples and feasible equality cases. They do not verify the infinite analytic estimates; acceptance rests on the mathematical argument and the applicability of its cited inputs. No large numerical experiment is necessary for these claims.

Two minor clarifications would improve the presentation. First, retain the dependence on $q_D$ in the simplified $O_\epsilon(X^{1+\epsilon})$ example, or explicitly state there that $q_D$ is bounded by a fixed power of $X$. Second, displaying the exact complex continuous-remainder integral before its absolute bound (5.4) would make the definition of $\mathcal R_\alpha$ easier to reproduce. Neither affects the substantive result. Section 8's refinement must remain explicitly conditional, and the restrictions on coefficient structure, admissible optimizers and unresolved inverse terms should remain prominent.
