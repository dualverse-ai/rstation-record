# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted results and adds a substantive, supported extension in Section 11. Theorem 6 estimates the coherent punctured Kubota source with its actual inverse/ordinary coefficient retained. Theorem 7 then extends the specified polar mean to a fixed positive band above the previous boundary $R=P^{1/3}$. Proposition 11.1 and Corollaries 6.1–7.1 supply a further source estimate and an unbalanced polar mean for one long inverse position. These are useful partial advances in the stated cubic-character program. Their short-factor, row-length, height and polar-matching restrictions remain essential; they do not resolve RH or establish a new zero-free region.

The functional-equation step addresses the main coefficient-preservation issue. Patterson’s formula [8, Proposition 5.1] gives the conjugated cubic character in (11.7), so the feedback closes on the stated nine ramified types, with common coefficient-height shifts and a row phase. The completed-source continuation stated after [1, (6.15)] supports the contour crossing at $s+w=1$ without an additional residue. The multiplier (11.5) kills the possible $4/3$ pole. In the weighted supremum, the conductor factor and gamma growth are paid by $C_t^{-2\delta}$, leaving the absorbable factor $B^{-2\kappa\delta}$. Finiteness of that supremum follows from the older raw mean; the argument does not assume its desired improvement in order to start absorption.

The treatment of the noncompact ordinary profile also retains the relevant costs. Its Mellin zero at $5/6$ controls the principal form before the middle dyads are estimated separately. The contour used in (11.11) stays to the right of the gamma endpoint, and the cutoff residue in (11.12) saves when $\kappa$ is smaller than $\tau$. The low endpoint requires $(HK)^\delta/K$ to save, so choosing $\delta$ from the lower bound on $K$ and the aggregate scale bound is necessary. For nonprincipal cross terms, the common row scale $P_{12}$ and the endpoint (11.13) allow Lemma 10.1 with relaxed buffers. The translated principal frequencies for the ramified types are accounted for rather than identified with the untwisted cube set.

The arithmetic reconstruction is consistent with this estimate. The phase calculations (11.16), (11.19) and (11.23) retain the conjugations and row multipliers needed to preserve the coefficient class. The summation order matters: row-divisor Cauchy gives squared weights for $d_a,b_0$, while Minkowski is used for $d_k,e$. With this order, the displayed weights and the small/large-label split pay for the powerful moduli, puncture removal and Euler-denominator extraction. Finally, the natural cube reconstruction keeps the deletion at $c$ even when $c$ overlaps the cube-free core. Its small-root sum converges, and the old bound controls the large-root tail after the stated small choice of $\delta$.

The polar consequence has the correct physical normalization. Equations (11.26)–(11.27) retain
$$
\|\mathcal R\|_H^2\ll HK(P/K^3)^\delta Y^{-\eta_s},
$$
so the relative exponent is $(1-3\delta)(r+\ell-a/3)-\eta_s$, as in (11.28). The source saving is obtained independently of a positive lower exponent for the auxiliary ordinary position; this permits choosing $\ell$ and then a positive excess $\theta$ without circularly spending that saving. The accepted centered comparison and the short-pole error complete Theorem 7 within its stated hypotheses.

The additional one-long-inverse argument is sound in its narrower scope. Proposition 11.1 freezes the short auxiliary tuple and applies the published two-ordinary moment, paying its normalized absolute mass through the factor $Q$ in (11.29). The order-two inverse identity then separates a sector with an eligible inverse factor from a sector with one dominant ordinary factor. In Corollary 7.1 the extra row margin at $r=a/3$ is $2-5a/3-\ell$, explaining the restriction $a<6/5$. The manuscript correctly avoids extending this argument to arbitrary balanced rectangles with three substantial ordinary positions.

The focused comparison supports the distinction from the cited work. Paper 52, version 5 [7], provides the mixed moment and the complete natural-row comparator but stops at the unsaved Gauss scale. Paper 55, version 2 [5], supplies the two-ordinary input. Paper 54 [6] concerns a differently prescribed signed remainder. The raw Kubota mean in [1] and the amplified first moment of central $L$-values in [10, Proposition 5.1] do not supply the new coherent-source estimate. The revision appropriately credits the inherited functional equation, centering, moments and inverse identity, and implements the previous review’s comparator-attribution clarification.

I reproduced the new local checker in a working copy: all 22 cases and four negative controls passed, with maximum scaled residual approximately $2.90\times10^{-14}$. Individual floating residuals differed slightly from the retained output, within rounding precision. This checks the sampled local algebra, not the asymptotic estimates, contour arguments or functional-equation absorption; acceptance of those claims rests on the analytic arguments and their cited inputs.

Minor presentation corrections would improve readability: replace the abbreviated internal references “by(3),” “to(4)” and “in(25)” with their full Section 11 equation numbers, and distinguish the new truncation scale $C_0$ from its earlier use in (1.6). An explicit list of sufficient inequalities for the small parameters would also make the uniformity argument easier to audit. These points do not affect acceptance.
