The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound, substantive extension in Section 10. Theorems 4–5 establish a fixed power saving for genuine mixed Möbius/ordinary coefficients in the stated sector, first after equal-product centering and then after subtraction of the explicitly specified ordinary-pair pole. The restrictions on inverse lengths, ordinary lengths, common heights and total inverse length are essential and are stated clearly. This is progress within the cubic-character analytic program; it does not resolve the complete signed remainder, establish a new zero-free region, or solve RH.

The new arithmetic input, Lemma 10.1, is supported by its argument. The mixed moment in [7, Theorem 3.11] applies to the actual remaining factors after removal of the selected factor. Near-saturation would therefore force that factor close to its trivial bound and require sufficiently many rows. Raising its raw polynomial to a bounded integer power produces a common divisor-bounded coefficient vector to which the cube-free Gram estimate [7, (4)] applies. The two branches of (10.13) contradict those requirements with a uniform positive gap once the core norm exceeds a fixed power of $Y$. For smaller cores, the nonprincipal ordinary sum supplies the required decay through (10.15). Restoring cube roots retains their Euler deletions. The whole-squarefree correction has no linear local terms, and its convergent coefficient masses justify the small-extraction argument and the tail bound. The strict upper-length buffer and the margin $a_1<3/2$ are used at precisely these steps.

The transfer to the complete Gauss mean also accounts for the relevant losses. In Section 10.2, reindexing by $j=g\ell h'$ makes the $g$ deletion redundant while leaving the common deletion $dE$. The divisor fibres have subsidiary multiplicity, and the remaining $d$ weight is harmonic. The Sobolev majorant (10.18a) is formed before the arithmetic summation, so the moment is applied to common profiles rather than profiles chosen separately for each row. The treatment of bounded cubic numerator packets and Fourier tails supplies the extensions needed by the actual transform.

For the principal-frequency contribution, fixing the inverse tuple before centering leaves the ordinary double residue independent of the rectangle's aspect ratio. The common ordinary height is what preserves this cancellation. The extraction in (10.22) introduces no extra power of $R$, and the final positive divisor sum converges with weight $w(d)q_d^{-2+\epsilon}$. Together with the nonprincipal-frequency bound, this proves the centered saving in (10.24).

The polar comparison retains both amplifier costs. Substituting $\|\alpha\|_2^2\ll RY^\epsilon$ and $|\mathcal M|\ll R$ into the accepted source estimates gives

\|\mathcal F(L,X/L)-\mathcal P(X)\|_H^2

Thus the earlier $YR$ comparison term is replaced by a proved centered estimate, rather than discarded. The example (10.26) satisfies both strict buffers and supports the asserted $Y^{10/9-\eta}$ bound for some fixed, unoptimized $\eta>0$.
