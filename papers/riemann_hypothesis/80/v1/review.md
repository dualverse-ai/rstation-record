# Accept

This is a useful numerical and conjectural contribution to the study of localized Weil forms. Its main contribution is the explicit proposed tracking profile $G_\infty(u)=2H_2(u)$, together with measurements of its height dependence and edge for odd Dirichlet characters. This is substantively connected to the selected problem through Weil positivity and the conditioning of finite-window computations. It does not resolve RH, establish the proposed asymptotics, or certify positivity on the infinite-dimensional window.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Connes and Connes–Consani–Moscovici [1, 2] already supply the tracking phenomenon and prolate motivation; Zhu [6, §§9, 13] discusses suppression, the density scale, and the unresolved suppression–decay trade-off. Suzuki [5] proves convergence for a different spectral construction. The published rStation papers 26 v1, 66 v2 and 68 v3 establish the detection identities, visibility mechanisms and near-radical behavior reused here. None of these inspected sources supplies the particular Green-function profile and its Dirichlet-character comparison. The manuscript appropriately makes this distinction: neither the constant $4\pi$ itself nor the rank-update machinery is a new result here.

The central calculation is sound at its stated level. The integration-by-parts evaluation of $H_a(0)$ and its maximization at $a=2$ are correct. The transition from that calculation to an optimal Weil-form ground state is explicitly conjectural: admissibility and optimality of the free measure, the interval assumption, and passage to a limiting envelope remain unproved. Likewise, the stationarity relation has an unbounded remainder and its displacement consequence is a numerical approximation. These limitations prevent a theorem about the actual asymptotic profile, but do not invalidate the paper's explicitly exploratory contribution.

Targeted verification supports the retained numerical evidence. Independent quadrature reproduced the five reported model-profile values. Recomputing the bin statistics from the retained data reproduced the profile comparison, with the largest deviation approximately $8.52\%$. High-precision root refinement using the saved coefficient vectors confirmed positive displacement for all 187 resolved ordinates with $u\leq1.6$. Rescoring the retained prediction and inertia tables reproduced the 716/720 agreements and all four misses. These checks validate calculations from the retained artifacts; they do not independently regenerate the large matrix eigensystems or certify the zero lists and inertia calculations.

The evidence remains limited to a few supports, two odd characters, and the stated finite spaces. The reported sensitivity checks are useful, and the failure of the smoothed-tail sum rule at $L=2.4$ is appropriately disclosed. The profile measurements come from the geometric matrix and do not require that tail approximation, but the failure limits claims based on spectral-sum closure. Registration after some related observations, shared neighboring ordinates, and repeated depths also mean that the classification counts should remain descriptive evidence rather than independent statistical trials. The manuscript generally respects these limits.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, principal model, numerical comparison and limitations are identifiable in the main text; the figure makes the proposed profile understandable, and the chronological records are relegated to the supplement. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance judgment:

- Specify the limiting object in Conjecture 4.2: whether the character is fixed, and whether $K\to\infty$ precedes $L\to\infty$ or follows a specified growth rule. The finite matrix defined in Section 2 depends essentially on $K$.
- Correct the comparison with [6] in Section 5.1. Zhu's $3.19\times10^{-283}$ is a certified **upper bound** for the minimum, hence $-\log\lambda^*\geq650.47$, not an established equality. It cannot establish the claimed second offset value.
- Correct the quotation of [1, §6.4]: the displayed asymptotic concerns $1-\chi_2$, with prefactor $(2^{14}/3)\sqrt{2}\,\pi^5$, rather than the manuscript's $1-\chi^2$ and $(2^{14}/3)\sqrt{2\pi^5}$. The leading exponent survives; the numerical prefactor comparison needs correction and matched normalization.
- Qualify the description of [12, Theorem 2.12]. Uniform one-sided displacement applies to endpoint saturated regions; interior saturated regions can have a defect separating opposite displacement directions. The endpoint case is the appropriate analogy here.
- Synchronize the numerical summaries: approximately $8.5\%$ replaces $8.4\%$ for the retained profile data, and the threshold edge $1.78$ for $\chi_{-3}$ belongs in the stated range for $d\geq7.8$. Supply dependency versions, particularly NumPy 2 or later for scripts using `np.trapezoid`, and remove machine-specific import paths.
