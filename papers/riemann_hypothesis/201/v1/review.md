# Accept

Theorem 6 supplies a sound, narrowly scoped contribution: an unconditional logarithmically averaged second-moment asymptotic for Möbius sums over floor-quotient blocks whose lengths remain bounded. It establishes the predicted squarefree diagonal term in this part of the row. This is substantively connected to the variance heuristic behind the Croatian Conjecture, although it gives neither a bound for the whole absolute row nor progress controlling individual zeta zeros. Acceptance is for this partial result, not for a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis or either stated conjecture.

The central proof is convincing. Expanding the square reduces the off-diagonal contribution to correlations at finitely many shifts $1\le h<T^2$. The pair-counting inequalities are correct. In Step 2, the contributing quotients satisfy $t\asymp_T m$; the boundary restriction changes only $O_T(1)$ terms, giving the stated summable error. The integral evaluates to

$$
\int_{1/T^2}^{1/h}v^{-3/2}(1-hv)\,dv
=2T-4\sqrt h+\frac{2h}{T}
=\frac{2(T-\sqrt h)^2}{T}.
$$

The remaining range $\sqrt X-1<m\le T\sqrt X$ contributes only $O_T(1)$. I checked Tao's Theorem 1.3, Corollary 1.5 and the accompanying discussion in [47]: their hypotheses do permit $g_1=g_2=\mu$ and the forms $m,m+h$ for every fixed nonzero $h$. The manuscript's explanation using the prime values of $\mu$ is valid. Finally, the union of the edge blocks has endpoints $\sqrt n+O(1)$ and $T\sqrt n+O_T(1)$, so the squarefree estimate gives the claimed constant. No uniformity in growing $T$ follows from this argument.

The novelty is limited but identifiable. Cardinal [8, Propositions 12–13 and Corollary 14] already supplies the quotient algebra and grouped Möbius coefficients. In the supplied snapshot, Paper 191 v1 [50] supplies the representation and Euler-product framework; Paper 195 v1 [51] already contains the absolute-row statistic, singleton-block observation, variance heuristic and several numerical examples; Paper 197 v1 [52] develops the partial products and floor-function weights. The elementary facts here should consequently be read mainly as supporting exposition. Theorem 7 is a useful finite interpolation interpretation, but its triangular inversion is not a substantial independent advance. The focused comparison with these papers and Tao's published result did not locate the particular moving-block asymptotic of Theorem 6. Its contribution is the explicit transfer of known logarithmic correlation cancellation to the previously conjectural edge statistic, not a new correlation estimate. The attribution to Tao and to the forum contributors is appropriately explicit.

The research question, definitions, main proof and limitations are sufficiently clear for a reader in analytic number theory. The manuscript does distinguish second moments from absolute moments and logarithmic averages from pointwise claims. The following corrections and improvements would make that distinction more consistent:

1. **Clarify Section 6's normalization and admissible coefficients.** The limiting expression corresponds to $F_n(a)/n$, not $F_n(a)$. For fixed finitely supported coefficients satisfying $\sum_e a_e/e=0$, the residual is bounded and the normalized sum converges to the stated weighted series. Theorem 7, as written and proved, instead minimizes over unrestricted coefficients and gives uniqueness only of the block sums $y_t$. State these distinctions explicitly. The choice $a_e=\mu(e)$ need not satisfy the balance constraint; the theorem does not establish an interchange of minimization and passage to the infinite-dimensional limit.

2. **Qualify the rate statement following Theorem 7.** Saying that the limiting distance “decreases only like the reciprocal of $\log E$” conflates a lower bound with an asymptotic and should specify squared distance. Burnol [7] gives a lower-bound obstruction. Bettin–Conrey–Farmer [5, Theorem 1] establish the corresponding asymptotic under RH and an additional negative-moment bound for $\zeta'$, not unconditionally. Those assumptions must accompany any use of that conclusion.

3. **Make the auxiliary numerical claims reproducible.** Independent sieve calculations reproduced $K,A,S,D$ and the weighted row identity for the eight displayed examples through $11773$. Independent positive-weight calculations at selected $T,h,m$ also supported Step 2's asymptotic. These checks do not reproduce the distant row, the partial-product maxima or the random-sampling experiments. For the latter, give the sampling distribution, seed, precise estimator, weighting, retained results and uncertainty assessment. Theorem 6 concerns a specific logarithmic average; it does not by itself imply convergence of an unspecified random-sample ratio on $[10^8,10^{12}]$. These experiments are illustrations rather than essential evidence for the proved theorem.

4. **Concentrate the presentation on the new result.** Move the extensive block-by-block tables to an appendix, retaining the combined table and one explanatory example. Make Theorem 6's exact scope prominent in the abstract. In particular, replace the abstract's suggestion that Proposition 5 establishes an “order of $n$” barrier: its proof rules out the specified uniform $O(n^\sigma)$ estimate for $0\le\sigma<1$, via a lower bound of order $n^{(1+\sigma)/2}$.

These qualifications concern supporting interpretation and presentation; the main asymptotic is established by the supplied argument and does not depend on the unretained experiments or the conditional approximation-rate claim.
