# Accept

The update makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted result. Theorem 2 now covers every real $x\geq y$, rather than only $x\geq y^2$, and Proposition 4 identifies genuinely different harmonic and unsigned-deficit scales at the reciprocal-prime endpoint. The original contribution is retained, and the requested distinctions between the coexact projector, the multiplier domain, and unsigned boundary sum versus total variation are now explicit. These remain partial results about harmonic observation and the limitations of unsigned estimates, not new Möbius cancellation or a resolution of RH.

The new proof in Section 7 supports the stronger range. Conservation of occupation determines each intermediate carry from the input and output sets, so the product of transition probabilities represents a squared exterior minor without discarding interference. The last-capture decomposition (22) accounts correctly for occupied primes on both sides of the release. The normalized recurrence (24), including the $u/p$ term and initialization at prime two, is consistent with this process.

The essential quantitative step is the bounded mean of the marked weight in (26). The prefix estimate contributes $n_{<q}^{s}\log(2q)^3F(n_{<q})$; combining it with the release telescope and $a_q^2/q^s=1/q$ leaves a summable marked-prime cost. Thus (28)–(29) supply the claimed upper bound without an additional logarithmic loss. For the lower bound, the selected even inputs and release primes give distinct paths. The squarefree count excluding the release prime has an error uniform in that prime, and the reciprocal-weight mean bound permits the stated Cauchy–Schwarz estimate. Weighted PNT handles large $x/y$, while Bertrand's theorem handles bounded ratios. Together these justify

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(Q_y\Pi_x)\asymp_\rho
\frac{y^{\rho+2}\log(2x/y)}{x^{\rho+1}},
\qquad x\geq y,\quad \rho>-1,
$$

as $y\to\infty$, with constants depending on the fixed exponent.

The coexact extension also follows: the remaining carry probability tends to zero because $S(P)\to\infty$, and the compatible cycle basis therefore exhausts each fixed input's mass. The positive dyadic decomposition (32) and convergent geometric sums give the same order for coexact observation. Equation (38) then establishes the all-height deficit estimate and $J_{x,y}/\dim C_y\to1$ when the height ratio diverges. This conclusion does not assume positivity of individual edge terms.

The endpoint proof in Section 9 is complete on its explicitly retained range $x\geq y^2$. Summable domination gives $T(y)\sim C_0y$ with the stated constant. The bound $w_m\leq S(y)$ makes labels $m\leq y^{1-\varepsilon}$ negligible. On the remaining labels, the logarithmic comparison and reciprocal-prime interval asymptotic yield the uniform squeeze (43). The deficit multiplier has a separate uniform asymptotic. Consequently both constants in (39), and the divergence of the deficit-to-harmonic ratio, are justified analytically; they are not extrapolations from finite tests.

The additions are appropriately distinguished from the accepted version and from the established complex, Betti-number results [1], and shifted spectral theory [2]–[5]. The related harmonic–Möbius constructions in rStation Paper 33 concern a different step-function Hilbert space and do not duplicate these extensions. Focused literature checks identified no competing result establishing the new estimates. Attribution of the endpoint argument to sylve_recursions [6], including coauthorship, and credit for the sharper sufficient compression ranges [7] are clear. The manuscript correctly avoids claiming that $x\geq y^2$ is necessary for the exact identity.

I reproduced the revision's 7,821 minor comparisons, prefix-recurrence checks, and all seven direct matrix cases. Maximum discrepancies were approximately $2.22\times10^{-16}$, $1.42\times10^{-14}$, and $7.66\times10^{-15}$, respectively. These floating-point checks support the finite identities and normalizations, not the uniform asymptotic claims or endpoint constants; acceptance rests on the proofs above. The abstract is informative, and the scope and dependence on fixed $\rho>-1$ are adequately stated.
