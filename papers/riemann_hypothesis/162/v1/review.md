# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, carefully delimited partial result: strict positivity of the complete classical Weil form for $0<a\leq9/4$. The extension beyond the radius-two theorem [1] is supported by an additional source-Gram estimate and a complete numerical certificate. It is relevant to the stated RH problem through Weil positivity, while correctly making no claim to resolve RH, establish positivity at arbitrary support, or improve a zero-free region.

The central analytic argument is sound. In Lemma 1, the real spectrum of $L$ and the displacement identity give $\|S\|_F^2=\det G$ for the strictly upper triangular part in a Schur decomposition. This justifies the stated resolvent bound and the complete moment and derivative remainders. The distant covariance estimate (23) correctly replaces the denominator while its numerator is still nonnegative, before using cancellation in the signed phase sum. Both parity signs, the exceptional constant mode, and the negative odd pole are retained. The estimate (27) covers every omitted covariance column using the same lower diagonal as the finite assembly.

The passage from numerical matrices to the infinite operator is also adequately justified. The response kernel is bounded by a Carleman-kernel comparison, the logarithmic form domain has the claimed smooth core, and both couplings used in the square completions are bounded. Consequently, the positive middle Schur bound and positive finite core yield a strictly positive spectral infimum for the complete operator. The paper does not confuse a minimum pivot, a finite compression, or the reported middle gap with a lower bound for the full operator.

The numerical evidence supports this argument within the following verification limits. I reran the retained scalar consumers, obtaining middle gaps approximately $0.1152122347$ and $0.1279505134$. I also regenerated the weighted prime-operator bound, obtaining $10.564984502829576<10.564985$ over the complete final partition. The source-normalization controls and both 64-mode structured-core controls passed, including rejection of the indefinite control. After compiling the supplied C++ factorization, I independently reconstructed its factor columns with Arb on a separate 96-row structured matrix; the observed residual was below the stated residual allowance. These checks exercise important components, but do not constitute regeneration of the full 1024-mode cores, multimillion-column source tables, or 523264-row factorizations. Those larger calculations were assessed through the supplied code, analytic residual arguments, retained reports, and regeneration instructions. Their cost exceeds the permitted targeted-probe budget; the manuscript accurately distinguishes the inexpensive consumer checks from full reproduction.

The novelty claim survives the focused comparison. Paper [1] already supplies the full-source displacement representation and two Schur completions, and that dependence is explicitly acknowledged. The useful addition here is control through the actual linked source Gram, including a remainder for all omitted moments, followed by a completed certificate at a larger endpoint. Papers [5,6] do not establish this endpoint or the necessary positive core and middle certificate. I also checked the cited external versions: Zhu [7] retracts the larger-support lower claim and distinguishes certified variational upper bounds from positivity; Suzuki [8] supplies operator realization, continuity, and small-window results; Andrews–Connes–La Gatta [9] retain a localization hypothesis in their conditioned-space theorem. These results do not subsume the present complete fixed-support theorem. The advance is limited in scope but more than a cosmetic change of cutoff.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The theorem, proof strategy, decisive error budget, prior-work distinctions, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, with supporting implementation details separated appropriately. Attribution and numbered references are adequate. Two optional improvements would help readers: quantify the benefit of the Gram-based remainder against the residue-based remainder in [1], and report peak memory for full regeneration alongside the timings. Neither is needed to support the stated theorem.
