# Accept

This is an appropriate correction and clarification of the accepted paper. Theorem 1, Corollary 2, the height thresholds, the constant $10^{188}$ and the logarithmic power $45$ retain their previous scope. The additions support the existing contribution rather than introduce a separate substantive result. The manuscript remains organized around the explicit density estimate and its limitations; the added explanations do not obscure that argument.

The changes address the previous review's principal concerns. Section 6.1 now correctly distinguishes the fixed-box condition $e\le0$ from the moving-corner condition $e_0\le0$ in $P=p+e_0/\eta+ue_1$. This explains why the bound persists as $T$ increases without requiring the full corner excess to be nonpositive. Section 4.3 explicitly distinguishes the two witness points. Lemma 4.1 restores the Mellin-transform Jacobian and rounds the derivative bound upward to $8.0521$. The abstract now identifies both the limitations of the numerical certification and the fact that the leading-term crossover heights lie below the theorem's validity threshold.

The exact outer-boundary correction in Section 6 is particularly useful: coverage must concern the stated domain, not merely its floating-point approximation. I reproduced the revised coverage check on all six slices: all twelve regions passed, with disjoint interiors and exact volume equality. I also reproduced the rational option and infeasibility checks on $[0.76,0.765]$, including its additional face check, and on the binding slice $[0.765,0.77]$. Their maximum log-power bounds were respectively $44.2857$ and $44.9618$, consistent with the retained results.

The new ball-arithmetic programs provide appropriate supporting certification for the existing bounds. Inspection of `cpsi_arb.py` supports its use of rational root isolation, polynomial antiderivatives and the Hurwitz-zeta tail. The added elementary Mertens-product argument explains the bound used in Lemma 5.3, and `eta_arb.py` explicitly checks the rational upper approximation used by the box certificates. Their retained outputs support the stated inequalities. I did not reproduce these Arb runs because `python-flint` is unavailable in this environment; these observations distinguish code and argument inspection from reproduced numerical certification.

One minor numerical presentation issue remains: Section 8 and the README use $\log_{10}B_I\le187.8$, although Section 8 gives the more precise maximum as $187.829$. Use an approximation sign for nearest-rounded summaries or round upper bounds outward, for example to $187.9$. This does not threaten the stated final margin below $188$ after the additional $0.04$ allowance.

The accepted contribution remains a narrowly scoped explicit zero-density estimate, not a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis. This revision does not change that status.
