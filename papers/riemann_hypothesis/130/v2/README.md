# An explicit Huxley-type zero-density estimate for $0.755\le\sigma\le0.785$, and why it does not help the explicit applications we examined

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-11-08T07:44:27.331970Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/130/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove a fully explicit zero-density estimate with Huxley's exponent on a short range. For $\sigma\in[0.755,0.785]$ and $T\ge T_1$, where $T_1=10^{175000}$ for $\sigma<0.765$ and $T_1=10^{160000}$ otherwise, the number of zeros of $\zeta$ with $\beta\ge\sigma$ and $T_1<\gamma\le T$ is at most $10^{188}\,T^{3(1-\sigma)/(3\sigma-1)}(\log T)^{45}$. A corollary bounds $N(\sigma,T)$ for $\log_{10}T\ge182721$. The proof uses Montgomery's mollified detector, a smooth dyadic block lemma that removes the zero-dependent weight at constant cost, an explicit form of Huxley's large-values estimate, explicit coefficient mean squares, and a case analysis over four exponent variables. An appendix derives the finite inequalities that place every detected zero in the certified domain. A box decomposition, re-checked in exact rational arithmetic, verifies the case analysis. The main analytic constants and the comparison behind the corollary are ball-arithmetic enclosures; the remaining constants are floating-point evaluations with stated margins. The log power is tracked term by term, and each used term has nonpositive excess (in the moving corner near $a=1/2$, nonpositive base excess), so the bound at $T_1$ holds for all larger $T$. We then compare the result with the explicit applications we examined, and it improves none of them. Its leading term beats the explicit Ingham-shape bound of Chourasiya and Simonič only above $10^{6645}$ to $10^{42700}$; these crossover heights lie below the threshold $T_1$ from which Theorem 1 holds. At the heights used by a conditional prime-gap threshold ($T\approx10^{210}$), the whole Huxley–Ingham exponent gap is worth $0.7$ to $4.3$ powers of $\log T$. At the Korobov–Vinogradov edge, in a model of Cully-Hugill's argument, Bellotti's near-unity bound beats every Huxley-shaped bound whose log power exceeds about $6.2$. This quantifies, for these applications, Bellotti's observation that log powers can outweigh exponent gains. We make no claim about applications or implementations outside those analysed.
