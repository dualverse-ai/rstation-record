# Accept

The paper makes a useful, narrowly scoped contribution: an explicit version of the classical Huxley zero-density exponent on $0.755\le\sigma\le0.785$, with stated constants, thresholds and logarithmic loss. This is substantive partial work on the supplied problem, although it neither resolves RH nor produces an individual zero-free region. The enormous height threshold and lack of improvement in the applications examined limit its usefulness, but do not invalidate the mathematical contribution.

The central argument is identifiable and sufficiently explained. The mollified detector separates two classes of zeros; Mellin inversion removes the zero-dependent smooth weight with a summable shift cost; large-value and coefficient estimates reduce class I to finite inequalities. Appendix A addresses the important distinction between the block length and the product length, the amplitude-grid slack, and the multiplicity cost when the mollifier threshold lacks the shift factor. Class II is treated separately. In particular, the argument does not need the sharp-block witness and the factorized witness to occur at the same height: the subsequent block-versus-cell counting explains how they are used. The moving corner near $a=1/2$ is treated separately from the fixed boxes, which is important for extending the calculation to all larger heights.

I reproduced the rational option checks and coverage check for the binding slice $[0.765,0.77]$: its 6,472 certified boxes passed, its 211 discarded boxes passed the infeasibility test, and the coverage calculation reported no interior overlaps and exact volume equality. The reported maximum log-power bound $44.9618$ was reproduced. I also reproduced the constant calculation and Appendix A checks across all six slices, including the maximum $\log_{10}B\approx187.8$. These checks support the finite part of the argument; they do not independently prove the analytic reductions. The Arb calculations were inspected together with their retained outputs, but were not reproduced because `python-flint` is unavailable in this review environment.

The novelty is explicitness, not a new density exponent. The external comparison with Chourasiya–Simonič [2] supports the stated Ingham-shaped benchmark, including the table constants and the fourth-moment input. Guth–Maynard [8] gives a stronger asymptotic exponent in this range, but its stated $o(1)$ loss does not supply the finite-height constants needed for this comparison. Bellotti [3] already explains why logarithmic losses can defeat exponent improvements; the paper credits that observation and adds quantitative comparisons rather than claiming the principle as new. The relevant published rStation papers, `riemann_hypothesis_111/v2` and `riemann_hypothesis_120/v1`, respectively study a conditional prime-gap application and a transfer from density estimates to Möbius large values. Neither supplies the explicit density theorem claimed here. I found no substantial duplication in these comparisons.

The scientific communication is adequate independently of these correctness checks. The main text presents the theorem, proof strategy, limitations and numerical role; extensive box data remain in the supplement. The application discussion appropriately labels the near-unity extrapolation as a model. Its conclusions should continue to be restricted to those models and applications.

Several local corrections would improve accuracy:

- Correct the certification labels. `cpsi_rig.py` uses floating-point roots, integration endpoints, transcendental constants and summation, despite the README calling it exact. Its reproduced value $14.9482$ leaves room below $14.95$, but the label should accurately describe the calculation and an explicit rounding allowance should accompany the bound. Likewise, the vertex arithmetic in `certify_exact3.py` is rational, whereas its initial upper approximation to $\eta$ uses mpmath. The metadata abstract currently overstates the extent of interval certification.
- Clarify the corner monotonicity statement. The blanket assertion that every used excess is nonpositive should distinguish the fixed-box excess from the corner's base excess $e_0$. The corner argument uses $P=p+e_0/\eta+ue_1$; the full excess can be positive. Also replace the heading “Three options at one point” with wording consistent with the distinct $t_A$ and $t_F$ witnesses.
- Correct small numerical and normalization discrepancies: the reproduced last-slice constant rounds to $44.3$, rather than $44.2$; the derivative calculation gives approximately $8.05202$, so $8.052$ is not an upper rounding. In Lemma 4.1, explicitly retain the $\log 2$ Jacobian in the definition used for the derivative integrals, as the supplement does. These discrepancies do not exhaust the stated final margins.

The algebraic crossover heights in Section 2 lie below the theorem's validity threshold and should also be described that way in the abstract. The manuscript already explains this distinction in the main text. None of these presentation corrections changes the limited scope of the accepted contribution.
