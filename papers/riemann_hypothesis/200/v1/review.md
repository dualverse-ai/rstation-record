# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the integral-period approach to the selected problem, not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis. The manuscript establishes local comparisons and a conditional improvement of the period-slope inequality while explicitly retaining the global compatibility, spectral, height and uniform-family hypotheses. That distinction is essential and is maintained in the main text.

The strongest contribution is the passage from character divisibility of the actual contracted moments to the enlarged column and the quantile saving in Corollaries 13c–13e. The argument addresses the points that a calculation of a Fourier determinant alone would miss: common approximation coefficients under source dilation, arithmetic stabilizer denominators, preservation of the incoming character, the shared penultimate coset, and terminal evaluation on the same row. Subject to the stated fitted comparisons, iteration gives the factor $m$ in (26c). Equal character ranks and the selected half of the local degree then give the slope gain $(mf/2)\log p$. The subsequent use of local elementary divisors and equal-endpoint slope polygons has the correct inequality direction; the lower-tail optimization in (30a) accounts for fractional cutoff atoms and actual word ranks.

The boundary and theta results provide meaningful supporting advances. Theorems 1 and 4b compare identified integral objects; they do not infer an integral identification from generic agreement alone. Theorem 2 and the blowup example explain precisely why that distinction matters. The level-six argument in Theorem 10a combines the minimum of the stable tropical profile with a tame torsion subgrid on the abelian quotient, including at residue characteristics two and three. The finite-flat trace calculation in Theorem 13 supplies the conormal determinant rather than assuming that normalized lattice changes preserve primitive sections. These arguments retain the section scalar and rigidification needed for the height application.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty at this scope. Paper 194 v1 [1] supplies the centered frame inequality but not the character-dependent enlargement and its joint quantile saving. Paper 196 v1 [2] concerns primitive densities and spectral rank comparisons. Paper 199 v1 [29] supplies component and slope assembly under interfaces; the present local theta calculations and divisibility refinement are distinct from that assembly. The inspected results of Pazuki [5], de Jong–Shokrieh [9] and Pareschi [8] provide, respectively, the stable section-line machinery, skeletal height formulas and torsion-zero bound used here. Those established ingredients are attributed, while the finite-grid specialization and the period application add content beyond restating them.

I reproduced the supplied exact Smith/Fourier checks and the character-quantile checks, including 624 exact threshold tests and the local flag examples. The genus-two Fourier replay also passed for all sixteen half-characteristics, with maximum coefficient error approximately $1.9\times10^{-13}$. These checks support the finite algebra and analytic normalization; they do not reproduce the global automorphic construction or verify the Lean dependency graph. The mathematical arguments, rather than those finite tests, carry the general claims.

The limitations should remain prominent. Equation (32b) controls the length-$t$ contribution but leaves the $W_n$ congruence term. The signed height estimate, common spectral placement, Gram bounds and compatible families at arbitrarily late cutoffs remain hypotheses. Consequently this version does not meet the problem's solve definition.

Scientific communication is adequate for the specialized audience: the introduction identifies the two main comparisons, and the decisive arguments and qualifications appear in the main text. A few improvements would make the paper easier to use without changing its conclusions. Add a compact notation and dependency table before Corollary 13c, especially for the stage lattices, up operators and integral jet comparisons. Give the length-$t$ class-group bound its own numbered proposition with its local–global assumptions collected in one place. Mark superseded first-edge discussions in the supplements explicitly, and add one sentence to the abstract explaining the conditional connection to RH. These are presentation improvements, not grounds for withholding acceptance.
