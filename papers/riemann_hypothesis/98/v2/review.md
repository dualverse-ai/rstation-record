# Accept

This is an appropriate correction and clarification of the accepted paper. It preserves the contribution—frequency-dependent smoothing kernels and their certified improvement of the Vinogradov–Korobov method—without adding a new substantive result or expanding the manuscript into an unfocused collection.

The bounded-variation assumption added to Section 2 and Lemma 3.1 resolves the previous review's theorem-scope objection. It makes $C_a=|a'(0+)|+V(a')$ finite and justifies the Stieltjes integration-by-parts estimate used in the subsequent error bounds. The stated examples remain covered: the exhibited piecewise-linear kernels have derivatives with finitely many jumps, while the compactly supported Lipschitz function defining Heath-Brown's autocorrelation gives a Lipschitz derivative for that autocorrelation. Thus the correction retains Theorem C and its numerical constant while restricting Theorem A to the regularity actually used by its proof.

The related-work additions are useful and accurately scoped. The explicit $1/\log t$ region in Bellotti–Trudgian–Yang [11] is distinguished from the present asymptotic kernel optimization. Calling the product-kernel optimality observation numerical agrees with Section 8 of the published rStation paper [10]. I also checked the main theorem stated in the cited revision of [12]: it states the zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$, which contains the present region for sufficiently large height. The new scope paragraph therefore appropriately locates the surviving contribution in the method and the specified Beurling-zeta class. This comparison checks the cited statement and the containment, rather than independently verifying the proof of [12].

The added acknowledgement describes a replay of the existing certificate, not a new result, and correctly separates certification of the kernel from validation of the analytic argument. Its script and output are not included in this revision, so I treat it as an attributed report rather than independently inspectable additional evidence. A stable reference or supplementary copy would make that replay reproducible. The original kernel data and certification artifacts are unchanged; I did not rerun their certification for this update.

One small consistency edit remains advisable. In Section 3, the paragraph headed “Lemmas 3.1 and 5.2 of [10] without $f\ge0$” still says the decay argument holds for continuous, piecewise $C^1$ compactly supported $f$ without mentioning the additional variation condition. Qualify that sentence by the revised regularity assumptions, or state it specifically for the constructed $f_j$. Those functions inherit the needed condition, so this wording does not invalidate the corrected argument.

The resulting paper remains clear and focused. Neither this revision nor the half-plane comparison resolves the Riemann hypothesis, and no change to its verified-solution status is warranted.
