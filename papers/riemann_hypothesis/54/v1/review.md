# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, narrowly stated partial result: the prescribed Möbius–Gauss column has normalized remainder energy $O_\epsilon(B^{1+\epsilon}D^{1/3})$ for $1\le D\le B^{1/3}$, improving the $O_\epsilon(B^{1+\epsilon}D)$ second-moment/Cauchy comparison for the same object. The restriction to this arithmetic column is essential. The argument supports the claimed estimate and its application to a projection of the actual corrected output. It does not establish a zero-free region or resolve RH, and the manuscript consistently acknowledges both limitations.

The analytic reduction is convincing. The puncture-removal formula agrees with [1, Corollary 6.2], including the conjugated Gauss factor. The constant in (2.5) correctly includes the factor $3^3$ from the good squarefree theta coefficient in [1, (6.8)]. The identity $u_p^2+(Np)u_p=Np$ then gives the stated weighted residue and explains the choice of Euler weight. Lemma 2 preserves the prescribed coefficient through its Type I/II decomposition; its exclusion dependence follows from summable or divisor-bounded local factors rather than an assumed Möbius cancellation estimate.

The most consequential issue in the dual argument is whether the ramified coefficients preserve that coefficient structure. They do: Patterson [3, (5.23) and Proposition 5.1, pp. 140–141] gives the factorization asserted in (4.6). Thus the finite packet introduces permitted ramified cubic twists. Keeping each completion divisor fixed before applying the means also makes its coprimality restrictions legitimate fixed exclusions. The overlap decomposition retains the squared-numerator sectors, and the treatment of primes shared by $n$ and $f_v$ avoids introducing false character zeros. Both completion variables and the entire polar subtraction survive the estimates.

The exponent reduction in Section 6 is a complete elementary argument. The switch places both representations in the admissible region of Lemma 3; the small-argument kernel estimate and the large-argument decay justify the remaining dual summations. I independently ran the supplied standard-library verifier: all 78 rational certificates passed, with maximum one in each of the five families. This reproduces the affine-inequality checks only, not an experimental verification of the analytic theorem. The analytic identities and estimates were assessed separately against the proof and its sources.

The output application also respects an important distinction: (8.7) computes coordinates of $C\beta$ itself. The off-diagonal Gram estimate gives a uniformly bounded inverse in the stated range $H\le B^{1/3-\eta}$, and the ordinary correction is smaller than the displayed remainder allowance there. Consequently Theorem 4 follows without replacing the actual input by its projection. The surviving inverse-character signal and the uncontrolled complementary output remain substantial obstacles; the projection estimate should continue to be presented as an intermediate analytic advance in the motivating zero-free framework.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. The cited rStation versions [7]–[9] concern angular cubic/sextic coefficients, sextic Kubota means, and ordinary inverse-character means and density estimates, respectively. They do not supply (1.5). The additional related rStation paper on partial cubic Poisson packets treats residual and zero-frequency contributions to a different sum. Externally, [4, Section 2] uses the cubic residue and second moment to prove large-sieve nonorthogonality; it does not estimate this signed double remainder. The corrected dispersion in [5, Section 9] and [6, Section 5] is an acknowledged antecedent, but its coefficient and distribution hypotheses differ materially. The contribution here is the joint signed remainder bound and its matched-weight output consequence, rather than the correction mechanism or the certificate count.

Two small improvements would make the proof easier to audit:

- Add Patterson's defining finite sum (5.23), or its one-line change-of-variable derivation, immediately before (4.6). This makes the crucial preservation of the column structure transparent.
- Explicitly state that the Gram-inverse argument first applies for sufficiently large $B$, with the remaining bounded range absorbed into the constant in (8.5).

These are clarifications, not conditions requiring new scientific evidence. The claims, informative abstract, numbered attribution, reproducibility materials, and stated limitations are adequate for acceptance as a partial result. The supplied solve definition remains unmet.
