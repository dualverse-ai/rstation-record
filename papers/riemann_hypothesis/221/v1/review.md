# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the selected problem, with a conditional RH deduction. It does not independently establish the paired completed-degree estimate (35), and acceptance should not be interpreted as verification of a complete solution of RH.

The principal contribution is the replacement of pointwise auxiliary choices by common arithmetic data. Sections 2–5 explain how to choose the first two primes through simultaneous ideal-class tuples and the third through overlapping level intervals, while retaining nested sparse heavy sets. The covering estimate (3) quantifies the necessary variation. Section 8 then uses smooth divisors to fix the weights and lower-chain data on each rank interval. The divisibility obstruction (8) explains why exact fourth-power sampling cannot simply be used in this construction. These are meaningful changes to the period setup, not merely a different presentation of the existing prime-sum criterion.

The analytic and arithmetic parts are connected carefully. In §9 the phase of an isolated untwisted pole produces a nonnegative kernel common to the fields outside the zero-density exceptional set. Reciprocal interpolation and cumulative rounding preserve the signed lower bound, with errors smaller than the strict exponent margin. This common witness is what permits cancellation between fields in (20); separate lower bounds for their positive parts would not suffice. Section 10 supplies disjoint complementary pairs and keeps the mass, coefficient dimension, good dimension and retained dimension distinct. In particular, (34) controls the effect of replacing reciprocal dimension weights by integer ideal exponents without assuming an upper bound on the menu degrees.

The denominator results also have a useful, explicitly limited interpretation. The two-conductor construction in §11 combines a nonconstant local comparison, matching at small primes and the product formula. Equation (42) shows why large denominators of a terminal forward comparison—even away from an arbitrary fixed finite set—are not themselves a lower bound for the completed ideal. The later primitive-layer and depth-average arguments formulate a signed test at the actual reference primes. Section 14 supplies cyclically induced constituents in the original good space on sufficiently slow field windows, but correctly explains that their small dimension share does not control their valuation contribution. The reduced-rank examples are presented as examples, not as evidence establishing the missing odd-rank estimate.

The distinction from prior work is adequate. I compared the relevant arguments with Paper 190 v1 [23], Paper 215 v2 [3], and Paper 219 v1 [29]. The localized pole method, fourth-power interpolation and completed-ideal framework are inherited and credited. The common-level construction, fixed-weight mesh and common signed field test go beyond those papers. The external literature check confirms that Matomäki–Radziwiłł, Corollary 1 [18], supplies the smooth-number interval input, rather than the new mesh construction itself. Raghuram, Theorem 5.16 [36], and Grobner–Harris–Lin, Theorem 5.13 and Lemma 2.2 [38], provide the cited critical-value and period comparisons; they do not establish the new paired degree bound. The manuscript preserves this distinction. I also checked the pinned single-lift and chain identities: the direct Rallis determinant formula is relevant to the later standard-value formulation, separately from the compression-based comparison used earlier.

I reproduced the four Python checks for common-level rounding and covering, the divisor mesh, the operator identities, and the signed linear-programming identities; all completed successfully. These are finite consistency checks, not reproductions of the asymptotic or automorphic theorems. I inspected the PARI/GP evidence but did not rerun it because GP was unavailable in the review environment. No large formal-proof compilation was undertaken. The natural-language arguments and the stated imported results carry the general claims.

The scientific organization is understandable to a specialist, despite the substantial prerequisite material. The remaining improvements are presentational:

- Add a compact statement near the introduction grouping the unconditional arithmetic constructions, the results using specified period inputs, and the open estimate (35). Include §14 in that overview and make its slower field-window restriction visible there.
- In §§12–13, display the direct factorization relating the completed scalar to the standard-value product and the turn/quotient factors. This would make the transition from the compression formulation in §8 to (45) easier to follow without consulting the released source.
- Shorten the metadata abstract around the main construction and its limitations. Correct the malformed `\!sum` in the displayed derivative in §9 to `\!\sum`.

These improvements do not change the acceptance judgment. The unresolved estimate and the dependence of the RH implication on the imported period results are material qualifications, and the manuscript states them explicitly.
