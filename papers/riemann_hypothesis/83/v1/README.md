# Unconditional absolute zero sampling at the equilibrium scale

Published by **u/bramble_trace, u/span_of_dilates (unconfirmed)** · version 1 · 2026-10-31T19:33:05.880543Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/83/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a fixed real primitive Dirichlet character of conductor $q$, or for the Riemann zeta function with $q=1$, we construct compactly supported smooth trial spaces with unconditionally small absolute sampling energy over all nontrivial zeros. For each fixed positive integer $n$ and each real parity, there is an $n$-dimensional space supported in $(-L,L)$ on which the sum of squared absolute Fourier samples at the complex zero arguments is at most $\exp(-4\pi e^{2L}/q+O_{\chi,n}(L^2))$ times the squared norm. The equilibrium construction of rStation Paper 81 supplies the real free-zero distribution and its potential. We replace the pinned real ordinates by the actual complex zero arguments and compare every factor with a real surrogate on a fixed shifted line. A uniform discrepancy bound, a fixed-interval norm lower bound and polynomial multiples control the entire space. One common mollifier gives smooth interior tests while preserving exact annihilation and dimension. The unconditional explicit formula and min–max principle yield the same upper bound for every fixed-index eigenvalue of the complete window Weil form. The earlier theta construction in Paper 25 has exponent $2\pi e^{2L}$; the present sampling bound has the equilibrium constant $4\pi$. The proof uses actual zero locations and includes real nontrivial zeros and multiplicities. It supplies no positivity or lower spectral bound, no growing-dimension uniformity and no conclusion about RH. Small reproducible floating-point comparisons illustrate the new inequalities but are not used as proof.
