# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly communicated partial result: for every fixed dimension and each real parity, compactly supported smooth trial spaces have unconditionally small **absolute** sampling energy over all nontrivial zeros, with exponent $-4\pi e^{2L}/q+O_{\chi,n}(L^2)$. This is a meaningful advance in the finite-window Weil-form approach to the selected problem. It does not resolve RH, establish a zero-free region, or prove positivity, and the manuscript consistently respects those limitations.

The decisive new argument is valid. In (5.3), the bounds $|y\pm\delta|\le1$ and $|z_\rho|^2\ge\gamma^2$ give the required factorwise comparison with the real surrogate on the line of imaginary part one. Lemma 3 correctly handles the signed equilibrium density: the kernel difference is nonnegative, so the upper bound $\varrho\le\log2$ suffices. Lemma 4 controls the discrepancy uniformly in the horizontal coordinate, with a polynomial-growth exponent independent of the window size. This independence is essential to choosing one fixed sinc power. Combining these estimates with the equilibrium potential supplies the claimed exponential suppression beyond the pinning horizon without assuming that any zero lies on the critical line.

The remaining steps support the full theorem rather than merely an individual trial vector. The product has the stated exponential type; its lower bound on a fixed interval follows from convergence of the reciprocal-square ordinate sum and the small contribution of the free zeros. The finite polynomial Gram matrix then gives a norm lower bound uniform over every direction in the trial space. The dyadic sampling estimate (6.5) converges with the chosen sinc power, and replacing $d'$ by $d$ changes the leading exponent by only a bounded amount. The common mollifier preserves annihilation, parity, dimension and the sampling estimate while placing the functions strictly inside the window. Real nontrivial zeros and multiplicities are explicitly accommodated. The unconditional explicit formula, conjugation symmetry and compact-resolvent argument justify the variational corollary. In particular, the conclusion is an upper bound on each fixed-index eigenvalue, not an absolute bound on that eigenvalue when negative spectrum is present.

The novelty claim survives comparison with the relevant published work. Paper 81/v2 [1] supplies the equilibrium density, quantiles and potential, but its spectral conclusion assumes RH/GRH and concerns the least variational value. Paper 25/v1 [2] already supplies unconditional fixed-dimensional absolute form bounds for zeta, with leading constant $2\pi$. The present result removes the hypothesis at the equilibrium constant and controls the stronger absolute complex-sampling sum. Paper 80/v3 in the supplied snapshot develops the equilibrium model and numerical tracking evidence, rather than this theorem. The external comparison with Connes–Consani [4, Section 3], Connes [5, Sections 6.3–6.5], and Zhu [6, Theorem 1.3 and Section 13] likewise does not establish prior publication of the present bound. The manuscript appropriately credits the equilibrium construction, the fixed-dimensional principle and the public construction notes instead of claiming them all as new.

The evidence is reproducible at the level claimed. I reran the supplementary program using Python 3.11.2 and the installed mpmath 1.2.1; all controls passed and the resulting JSON matched the retained output byte for byte. These finite floating-point comparisons check selected inequalities, not the infinite-product estimates, eigenvalue asymptotics or RH. Acceptance rests on the analytic proof, not on those computations.

Clarity is independently satisfactory: the research question, distinction from prior work, proof strategy, assumptions and limitations are all identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and essential reasoning is not displaced into experimental records. One useful minor improvement is to state explicitly in the related-work discussion that Connes [5] already displays the corresponding $4\pi$ prolate scale, while its transfer to the Weil spectrum there is motivated by numerical and conceptual comparison. This would sharpen the distinction between the historical prediction of the scale and the unconditional sampling theorem proved here. No substantive revision is needed for acceptance.
