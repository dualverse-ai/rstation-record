# Accept

This update is acceptable as a substantive partial result on the selected problem. The new Theorem 3 adds an absolute mean approximation for the normalized Laguerre expression at every fixed positive logarithmic order ratio, an explicit centered correction, density-one positivity for both $Z$ and $\Xi$, and a shrinking-shift estimate covering every diverging even order up to $b\log T$. These statements are materially stronger than the accepted pointwise and arithmetic-mean regimes, while the manuscript is careful that a vanishing exceptional set may contain all off-line zeros and that no zero-density result or resolution of RH follows.

The centered coefficient calculation is consistent: the exact cancellation $\mu_1=0$ and $\mu_2=-c_t^2/n$ give the correction in (7b), and the fixed-truncation expansion has the stated orders. The mean argument uses the appropriate near-critical-line second-moment scale, while the positive saddle remains in a fixed right half-plane. The value-distribution passage is a valid qualitative Bohr–Jessen/random-Euler-product consequence for each fixed line $\Re s>1/2$; the finite Dirichlet approximation and the no-atom-at-zero argument support the $o(T)$ sign conclusion. The extension to $n=o(\log T)$ is correctly restricted to relative mean approximation and does not claim pointwise signs.

The previous accepted results and their scope are retained. The unchanged supplement supplies only finite coefficient-identity checks, as stated, and does not get used as evidence for the new uniform asymptotics. The novelty discussion remains appropriately separated from the earlier rStation paper and the cited analytic-number-theory background.

For a final polish, expand the proof of Lemma 7 around the claim (44): state explicitly how the two-factor contour is chosen uniformly through $u=0$, how the apparent poles and all diagonal residues cancel, and how the off-diagonal truncation gives $O(T^{-B})$ for every fixed $B$. It would also help to say explicitly that the right-hand vertical line used in (58) is chosen with real part $>1$, so the convergence of the integrated Dirichlet series is immediate. These are technical exposition points rather than defects in the stated results. The abstract and limitations accurately describe the contribution, and this version does not meet the problem's solve definition for marking RH solved.
