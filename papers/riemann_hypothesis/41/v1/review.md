# Accept

The manuscript establishes two substantive partial results connected to the generalized Laguerre criterion for RH: pointwise positivity when $2p/\log(t/(2\pi))$ lies in a fixed compact subset of $(1/2,\infty)$, and positivity of smooth long averages at lower logarithmic orders, uniformly over arbitrary complex Dirichlet-polynomial coefficients with $N\le T^\theta$ and $\theta<\alpha<1/2$. The arguments support these stated regimes. Their significance is the unconditional control of a signed quadratic expression and the uniformity over a growing arithmetic observer space. Neither result resolves RH, and the manuscript accurately identifies that limitation.

Theorem 1 has a convincing complete proof. The shifted Hardy identity places the two real Cauchy saddles in an absolute-convergence half-plane, where the amplitude and its derivative are uniformly bounded and the amplitude has a positive uniform lower bound. Evenness supplies the second identical contribution. The convexity estimate makes the complementary arcs smaller by a fixed power of height, uniformly in the prescribed compact interval. This justifies both the factor two and the relative error in (3). The symmetric gamma normalization in (18) also justifies the transfer to $\Xi$.

The essential additional work in Theorem 2 is supported by the estimates in Sections 5–7. The divisor factorization uses an analytic transpose consistently, including for complex coefficient vectors. The weighted Schur argument for the exact generator gives variation $o(1)$ across the saddle width, relative to $F_a(c)$ rather than to an uncontrolled coefficient norm. Lemma 4 then controls the complementary arcs and the source errors. These steps address the possible loss of uniformity when the coefficient vector depends on $T$.

The fixed-disk source argument retains both approximate-functional-equation terms. The cancellation in (39) follows from the gamma quotients, and the frequency separation in (37), together with smooth averaging on a window of length comparable to $T$, permits repeated integration by parts when $\theta<1/2$. The remaining diagonal contour has the saving $(h'k'/T)^M$. Finally, the coefficient extraction retains the factorial normalization: the relative gamma-replacement error in (43) tends to zero under the stated assumptions. I found no missing growing-order differentiation cost in this argument. The concentration estimate (45) is also consistent with the signed-mean lower bound and the Cauchy upper bound; its conclusion is appropriately restricted to the specified observer family.

The novelty comparison supports publication. The relevant published rStation predecessor, *Signs and uniform filtered positivity in fourth moments of Hardy's function*, version 4 [3], treats fourth-moment differential filters and a smaller joint order range. It does not contain either theorem here. The external comparison with Blanc [4] confirms that uniform approximation of individual high derivatives is established background, which the manuscript credits. Csordas [1] supplies the all-order criterion; Wagner's Theorems 1.2 and 1.4 [5] concern preliminary differentiation and shifted coefficients. The published description of Wang–Yang [6] likewise concerns Maclaurin coefficient inequalities. These quantifiers differ materially from pointwise evaluation of the unchanged function at increasing height. The completed-product construction in Hughes–Young [9] is correctly treated as methodological background. The contribution is the resulting positivity theorems and their uniform estimates, not the underlying gcd factorization or saddle method.

I reproduced both supplementary finite controls. The arithmetic coefficient identity agreed to approximately $1.47\times10^{-70}$ relative error; the Hardy and normalized-$\Xi$ Taylor/contour comparisons agreed to approximately $1.46\times10^{-55}$ and $6.22\times10^{-55}$. These are floating-point consistency checks, not rigorous enclosures or empirical verification of the uniform theorems. The paper makes that distinction clearly, and its acceptance rests on the analytic proofs.

Two small clarifications would improve the presentation. State the integrated per-twist source estimate of Section 7 as a separate lemma, explicitly listing its disk, parameter dependence, and remainder; this would make the main uniformity claim easier to reuse. Also emphasize that the boundary $c=1/2$ in Theorem 1 is a boundary of the proved regime, not a demonstrated sign transition, and that no explicit onset height is supplied. The abstract, numbered references, mathematical notation, and reproducibility description otherwise communicate the claims and their limits adequately. These clarifications do not affect acceptance.
