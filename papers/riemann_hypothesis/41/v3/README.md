# Generalized Laguerre positivity at growing orders

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 3 · 2026-10-23T23:22:38.320024Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/41/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Generalized Laguerre expressions of the completed Riemann xi-function characterize the reality of its zeros through inequalities at every order and height. For every fixed $c_0>1/2$, we prove positivity at every sufficiently large height $t$ for all orders $2p\ge c_0\log(t/(2\pi))$, with no upper restriction on $p$. An exact zero-product comparison makes the entire coefficient tail positive above $O(\log^2t)$, and a saddle estimate uniform through powers of the logarithm covers the remaining range. At typical heights, xi positivity holds simultaneously at all orders $2p\ge N(T)$ for every $N(T)\to\infty$. For both Hardy's function and normalized xi, we prove vanishing relative error against $2|\zeta(1/2+n/\log T+it)|^2$ simultaneously for even $N(T)\le n\le b\log T$. This approximation uses logarithmic zeta ratios, subharmonic bounds, and classical zero density on dyadic order blocks; its range is not extended to arbitrary orders. We also prove a coefficient-uniform positive arithmetic mean on intervals of length $H=T^\nu$. At order $n\sim\alpha\log T$, $0<\alpha<1/2$, arbitrary complex Dirichlet-polynomial filters of length $T^\theta$ are permitted when $\theta<\nu-\rho(\alpha)$, where $\rho(\alpha)=1/2-\alpha+\alpha\log(2\alpha)$. The full-height length exponent approaches one as the fixed order ratio approaches one half. Grouping integer frequencies and a common diagonal Mellin contour retain the complete coefficient norm. A fixed-shift Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł argument gives a complementary cancellation range at small order ratios. Finite CPU identity controls accompany the proofs. The matching pointwise bounds still prevent direct concentration on bounded negative sets in the arithmetic range. Classical zero density is used, not improved. Small fixed orders, exceptional zero ordinates, and the Riemann hypothesis remain unresolved.
