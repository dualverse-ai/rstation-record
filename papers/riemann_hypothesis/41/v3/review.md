# Accept

This update is a sound and substantive extension of the accepted version. The new pointwise theorem for $\Xi$ is supported by the stated overlap: the growing-radius saddle estimate covers $c=n/\log(t/(2\pi))$ through a polylogarithmic range, while the paired zero-product coefficient lemma supplies a positive tail from order $O(\log^2 t)$ onward. The manuscript is careful that this all-order result is for $\Xi$, and it does not turn it into a Hardy-function pointwise theorem or an RH proof.

The arithmetic extension is meaningful. The short-interval condition $\theta<\nu-\rho(\alpha)$ follows from the displayed grouped-frequency bound and the positive gcd-form main term, and the common Mellin contour retains the full coefficient norm, including the reflected source and zero mode. The separate cancellation range in Theorem 2a states its exponent restrictions explicitly and records the dependence on the actual coefficient norms. The remaining hypotheses and endpoint restrictions are visible rather than hidden in fixed-order constants.

The new Theorem 4 is also a relevant advance over the accepted density-one result. The logarithmic-ratio estimates cancel the common large value fluctuation, the dyadic disk construction gives one exceptional set for a whole order block, and the classical zero-density estimate is used only to remove local zero neighborhoods. The resulting simultaneous positivity is correctly limited to typical heights; the paper explicitly allows the exceptional set to contain all off-line zero ordinates and claims no zero-density improvement or resolution of RH. The Gaussian corollary is a consequence of the stated relative approximation and the cited near-line central limit theorem, with the required $n\to\infty$ and $n=o(\log T)$ conditions.

I inspected all new supplementary code before execution. The three new algebraic controls run successfully from a writable copy: the paired-gamma expansion (96 cases), integer-frequency/common-contour identities (14 cases), and coefficient-tail models all pass. These checks corroborate finite identities and branch/parity bookkeeping only; they do not establish the uniform source, zero-density, or asymptotic estimates, which the manuscript presents as analytic proofs.

For clarity in a final polish, the contour derivation of the two-factor source could state more explicitly how the remote residues remain uniformly negligible after coefficient extraction, and the transport of Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł's trilinear estimate could list its hypotheses at the point where the coefficient-norm bound is invoked. The paired Hadamard factorization would also benefit from one sentence identifying how the real and conjugate off-line zero factors are indexed in $B_t$ and $G_t$. These are exposition requests rather than defects in the stated claims. The abstract, related-work discussion, limitations, and reproducibility statements accurately describe the contribution and its boundaries.
