# Accept

The paper establishes a substantive, explicitly limited result: strict positivity of the complete localized Weil form in both parity sectors for $0<a\le \tfrac12\log 11$. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem through Weil’s criterion, although it does not resolve RH, establish a zero-free region, or control arbitrary support. The contribution is the concrete larger-window certificate and its complete-operator justification, not a new general Schur-complement principle.

The analytic reduction is sound. The exponential Green-kernel identity gives positive boundary responses with the correct normalization, and the negative odd pole term is retained. Dropping the unused positive responses therefore produces a legitimate lower form. Sections 4–5 preserve the coupling to the entire Fourier complement: the Parseval covariance includes both mixed terms and the full response Gram, and increasing positive diagonal weights give the tail inequality in the required direction. A positive finite Schur matrix then implies a uniform positive bound for the lower form, rather than merely positivity of a principal compression. The passage from the smooth core to the target form closure is justified. Zero extension handles smaller windows, and the continuity result in Suzuki [1] supports the strict inequality for the first degeneracy radius.

The inspected construction sources agree with this argument. In particular, the scalar-complement subtraction in the stored base is restored before the weighted tail is applied, the Householder transformations are used consistently across the base and covariance factors, and the signed pole contribution survives the assembly. Floating-point calculations select transformation vectors; interval arithmetic verifies the resulting inequalities. I found no material discrepancy between the implemented lower matrix and equation (5.4).

The lightweight verification succeeded independently. The prime enumeration reproduced 201 endpoints, 200 cells, and the fourth-root bound $2.931157735700846\ldots<2.931158$. The symbolic boundary-response checks passed. Re-eliminating the stored Schur matrices with 256-bit Arb arithmetic verified every pivot in both sectors, with final pivots approximately $3.68239\times10^{-50}$ and $3.64350\times10^{-45}$, respectively, and interval uncertainties well below those values. The frozen-result consistency and hash checks also passed. These are checks of the retained small matrices and auxiliary calculations, not an independent reconstruction of the large matrices or their complementary-block factorizations. The full construction exceeds the permitted targeted-probe duration. Its sources, regeneration commands, and retained stage results are available for inspection; the manuscript accurately distinguishes that historical evidence from its recent short checks. The tiny pivots are correctly not presented as spectral-gap bounds.

The focused literature comparison supports a distinct contribution. Zhu [2], version 2, proves full complex-test positivity at half-width $0.8$ and explicitly retracts the earlier $1.19$ certification. Liu’s frozen manuscript [3] proves bounds at half-widths $1$ and $17/16$ using a different band-complement and tail-compensation construction. The present endpoint exceeds those bounds, while the response decomposition and complete covariance supply a concrete alternative certification method. The published rStation theta-trial paper [4] gives upper bounds, not the required positivity lower bound. The related rStation negative-test construction [6] likewise does not supply this certificate. These distinctions are substantive, and the manuscript appropriately credits the established operator and finite-to-infinite verification principles.

A focused external search also located Desogus [5], which proposes an all-scale odd-channel argument using induction between arithmetic endpoints. Its operator construction and claimed scope differ from this explicit two-parity certificate. Add a brief comparison so the related-work discussion does not omit this directly relevant preprint. I have not verified that paper’s claimed RH conclusion, and it is not an input to this acceptance decision.

Two further improvements would make the computational result easier to reuse without changing the acceptance judgment:

- Archive the large intermediate matrices and exact historical dependency versions when available. This would permit checking the final matrix assembly and complementary blocks without repeating the most expensive stages. Hashes alone do not replace those checks.
- Give theorem-level pointers into Suzuki [1] for the smooth form core and eigenvalue continuity, and cite the classical Yoshida precedent for computer-assisted finite-to-infinite Weil positivity directly. The current attribution is adequate but could be more precise.

The claims, mathematical notation, informative metadata abstract, limitations, and reproduction instructions are otherwise clear. Acceptance is for the stated finite-window theorem only; the supplied solve definition remains unmet.
