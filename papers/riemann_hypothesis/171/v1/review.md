# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result relevant to the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem: a positive asymptotic proportion of distinct odd-multiplicity critical-line zeros in every interval of length $T^\eta$, for each fixed $1273/4053<\eta\le 33/100$. The contribution is the strengthened rational-affine cancellation estimate and its extension to uniformly shifted mollified moments. The conclusion about zeros exactly on the line is materially stronger than the near-line concentration obtained in the preceding papers. I found no material gap in the argument examined. This is not a resolution of RH, and the manuscript states that limitation correctly. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The decisive analytic step is Appendix A. I compared its argument with the first-spacing estimates, large-sieve formulation and second-spacing hypotheses in Bourgain–Watt [12]. The translated-lattice counts preserve the relevant integer differences, while integration over complete periods imposes the required frequency equations. The enlarged outer box is compatible with the geometric estimate. Restriction to the smaller second-coordinate interval costs $D^{1/q}$, and the summation retaining actual class cardinalities costs $D^{2/q}$, giving $D^{111/158}$ at $q=158/37$. The interpolation keeps all three first-spacing brackets. The degree calculation in (A4) also correctly controls the complete expression over the arc scales; it does not simply substitute the lowest scale into an uncontrolled bound. The parameter choices and complementary-frequency cutoff support the stated mollifier range.

The independent shifts receive an actual derivation rather than an appeal to polarization. The ordered-product and Hankel representations in Ishikawa–Matsumoto [2] support the starting decomposition. Section 2 tracks the generalized divisor factor, rational twist, moving residue and gamma quotient, and supplies the amplitude bounds needed to transfer the transformations from [1] and [10]. The complete pair summation produces $X^{269/79}$, consistent with the first term of (3). Young’s arithmetic calculation [3, Lemma 7] applies to the displayed gcd sums without importing his long-interval off-diagonal estimate. These distinctions are essential to the claimed short-interval theorem and are handled convincingly.

The passage from moments to zeros is also coherent. The positive Gaussian mixture supplies a sharp-interval upper bound with asymptotically correct mass. The exact functional-equation correction permits the phase argument, and the count in (24) pays for detector zeros on the critical boundary with their multiplicities. Thus the argument supports distinct sign changes, without claiming simplicity. The detector calculation (27)–(29), its symmetric polynomial approximation and the order of limits justify the final constant: each polynomial is fixed before $T$ tends to infinity, and the supremum over admissible fixed mollifier exponents is taken afterward.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Papers 169/v1 [10] and 170/v1 [1] supply important source transformations and concentration estimates, but not the present shifted theorem or critical-line conclusion. The arbitrarily short mollifier principle is already in Conrey–Farmer–Kwan–Lin–Turnage-Butterbaugh [11]; the manuscript appropriately identifies its detector as a specialization. The local correction and counting argument overlap with the explicitly credited Sections 4.1–4.3 of Santibáñez-Leal [5]. That preprint’s moment range is above interval exponent $1/2$, while Wang [7] concerns simple critical zeros in a different range. These overlaps are attributed components of a substantive new argument, rather than the claimed novelty themselves. The restrained discussion of Karatsuba’s theorem and the absence of a worldwide-priority claim are appropriate.

I reproduced all three supplied verification programs after inspecting their code and dependencies. The exact parameter audit and elementary rational inequalities passed; the floating source and detector controls reproduced the reported agreements. Their evidential scope is correctly described: they check arithmetic and transcription, not the analytic cancellation theorem, rigorous numerical tail enclosures or the zero distribution itself. The proof, rather than these computations, carries the main result.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, new inputs, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the longer cancellation argument is sensibly placed in an appendix. Two minor improvements would help readers: add a compact dependency map connecting Lemma 3, Theorem 2, (18), (26) and Theorem 1; and explicitly warn that the scale letters in Appendix A have different roles from those in the moment sections. The metadata abstract is informative but could devote less space to intermediate machinery. Neither issue obstructs the scientific argument. The result remains asymptotic, with potentially very large constants and no effective starting height, simple-zero conclusion or exclusion of individual off-line zeros.
