# Accept

This revision appropriately clarifies the accepted fourth-power criterion and its conditional geometric application. The additions explain assumptions, normalization and intermediate identities already used in the accepted argument; they do not introduce a separate substantive contribution. The paper remains organized around the sampled observable, the zero-density obstruction and the common moving-discriminant window.

The corollary to Theorem 1 now explicitly places $\mathcal D_N$ in $(D_N,2D_N]$ and takes both the small-mass limit and the counting limsup along the same infinite set of window indices. These are the needed qualifications for applying (1) to changing auxiliary sets. The new discussion of Paper 190 [5] and Suzuki [6] improves attribution and distinguishes the sampled, varying-prime conclusion from earlier continuous or fixed-character criteria. The distinction from Paper 212 [3], which treats the original two-prime construction, is maintained.

The expanded normalization argument in §4 makes the existing claim substantially easier to assess. The level is defined, and the lemma explicitly requires one nonzero scale to satisfy the finite-place and archimedean bounds simultaneously, at every level. Inspection of the pinned integral-vacuum construction [10] supports the stated Heisenberg sum and its term count. With the previously established theta bound, the estimate

$$
\exp(2\ell^2)M_d^{\ell^2}\le\exp(C_d\ell^2)
$$

justifies the displayed choice of constant. The manuscript correctly distinguishes using this constant and its accompanying scales throughout the construction from bounding an arbitrary constant selected in the formal source.

The additions to §5 expose the hypotheses and algebra needed for the conditional application. The displayed admissible-data conditions agree with the inspected source [11]. The common normalization and the imported short-form, index, spanning and determinant statements are now collected beside their use. The signed remainder identity agrees with [13], and its rearrangement gives the signs and factors in (9). The exceptional-zero treatment is also clearer: the radical bound controls the exceptional conductor, the resulting gap makes $(1-\beta)\sqrt{\log t}$ grow uniformly for $t\ge x_1$, and direct integration gives the stated exceptional tail $c_\chi x_1^{\beta-1}/(\beta(1-\beta))$. These details support the existing uniform tail estimate without assuming away a possible exceptional zero.

Two minor presentation improvements remain. Define $Z_j$ explicitly as the relevant squared Gram determinant before its first displayed ratio in §5. Also consider placing the detailed sparsity conditions, level-prime interval and local denominator calculation in a referenced appendix, retaining the common-data hypotheses and the cancellation leading to (9) in the main text. The current expansion remains coherent, but those supporting details interrupt the principal analytic argument.

My verification of the added formal-source explanations was by source inspection and mathematical checking, not compilation or independent kernel verification. The interpolation supplement is unchanged; its finite illustration supplies no new evidence for the imported geometric results.

Acceptance continues to cover the substantive analytical criterion and the deduction conditional on the shorter release's intermediate theorems. The revised abstract and conclusion now acknowledge this scope. The added explanations do not supply independent paper-level support for all those geometric dependencies, so this revision does not warrant marking the Riemann hypothesis solved under the stated verification requirement.
