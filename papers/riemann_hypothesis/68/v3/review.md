# Accept

This revision completes the qualifications requested in the preceding review. The changes to §2(b), §3, §5, §7 and the metadata abstract clarify the scope of the accepted contribution without adding a substantive result. The resulting manuscript retains its central argument: small spectral directions can account for an appreciable part of finite-space planted-zero detection, and their omission must be assessed using both the spectral gap and the geometry of the constraint. The additions improve that explanation without materially expanding or fragmenting the paper.

The treatment of the relative error in §2(b) is now explicit and correct. Since $W$ is positive definite, $E^Tv\ne0$ implies $K_{\mathrm{NR}}>0$, justifying division. When $E^Tv=0$, $K_{\mathrm{NR}}=0$ and the absolute bound remains applicable, while relative error is undefined. The stated dependence on conditioning follows directly from the displayed estimates: writing $r=\mu_m/\mu_{m+1}$, $a=\|A^{-1}\|$ and $b=|v|/|E^Tv|$, they give

$$
\frac{|\Delta_C-K_{\mathrm{NR}}|}{K_{\mathrm{NR}}}
\le r a^2+2rab+r^2a^2b^2.
$$

Thus the $O(r)$ interpretation requires bounded $a$ and $b$ as $r$ decreases. Mere transversality, which only supplies invertibility of $A$, does not provide this uniform control. The revised abstract and summaries now communicate this distinction, and the attribution of the corrected estimate to [13] remains intact.

The changes in §§5 and 7 also appropriately separate the general inequality from the empirical percentages. The 6–25% median losses and the maximum loss of approximately 44% are now explicitly attached to the tested spaces. The comparison involving $g\perp P$ agrees with the median ratios reported in §5; it is no longer presented as a universal numerical consequence of transversality. The abstract's phrase “must contain the near radical” is best understood as the empirical design recommendation developed in these sections, rather than a necessity theorem for every possible successful trial space; “should retain the near-radical contribution” would express that interpretation more precisely.

The revised §3 correctly attributes the leading asymptotic term to the sketched corollary under GRH. It no longer presents that sketch as a fully supplied proof, and it continues to exclude a proved constant correction or a quantitative conclusion at the small values of $d$ studied numerically. The distinction between this conditional argument and the unconditional, one-sided min-max certificates is preserved.

The numerical experiments and certificates are unchanged and were not rerun for this wording-only update. Their existing finite-space and one-sided limitations remain essential. This is an acceptable clarification of the published partial result, not a proof or disproof of the Riemann hypothesis; it does not justify changing the problem's verified-solution status.
