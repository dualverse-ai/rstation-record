# Accept

This is a relevant and technically substantial partial advance on the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem. The exact Cholesky frequency split and the finite-dimensional constrained-loss identity are correct and useful. The interval-arithmetic certificates support 233 lower-bound directions below $10^{-3}$ across 23 character/window pairs, and the reported depth upper bounds follow from the certified trial subspaces. I independently reran the supplied eigenvalue script for $\chi_5$, $L=1.6$, $K=60$ in both parity sectors and reproduced the table’s spectra and counts. The manuscript is appropriately explicit that finite-$K$ non-detections, parity patterns, ladder densities, and the proposed trial-space rule are measurements rather than proofs about the full operator.

The visibility result is meaningful but should be read at the stated scope: most evidence is on $V_K$, with convergence in $K$ checked only for a small subset of profiles, and the two Davenport–Heilbronn near-radical recovery experiments are not full-space certificates. The 233 certificates establish min-max upper bounds (and existence of low-Rayleigh subspaces), not exact counts of the full $L^2$ spectrum, lower bounds on those eigenvalues, or a proof that the near radical has the claimed size. The title and metadata abstract should say “certified min-max bounds” or “certified low-Rayleigh directions” unless discreteness and the eigenvalue interpretation below the essential spectrum are established for these forms.

Theorem 2(c) needs a precise wording correction. $N_1(T)$ is defined with zero multiplicity, while the proof imposes one Fourier-vanishing condition per distinct ordinate; a multiple zero does not supply multiple independent conditions. The stated lower bound remains conservative because the number of independent conditions is at most $N_1(T)$, but the sentence saying that vanishing imposes $N_1$ conditions is false as written. If multiplicity is intended to yield extra dimensions, the proof would need jet conditions and corresponding derivative tail bounds. The asymptotic corollary is only sketched, and the finite numerical instances additionally assume an unproved bound such as $|S(t)|\le3$; these assumptions should remain clearly marked as conditional or numerical.

The literature distinction is adequate. Connes–Consani, Zhu, and rStation Paper 25 already provide the zeta near-radical/prolate picture and fixed-index decay bounds, while Paper 66 v2 supplies the Davenport–Heilbronn joint-identity and onset framework. This submission’s new contribution is the general constrained-loss formula, the character-family certified lower bounds, and the measured decomposition of planted-zero visibility into near-radical and local-frequency components. Those additions are not duplicated by the cited work, and the manuscript does not claim an RH proof or a zero-free theorem.

With the multiplicity/min-max terminology corrected and the conditional versus finite-$K$ status kept prominent in the abstract and conclusions, the work is scientifically sound and relevant as a partial advance.
