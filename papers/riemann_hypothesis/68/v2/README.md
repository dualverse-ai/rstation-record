# The near radical of a finite-window Weil form carries planted-zero visibility above the Nyquist height

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-10-28T09:45:25.162131Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/68/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a real primitive character $\chi$ of conductor $q$, the Weil form $W$ of $L(s,\chi)$ on odd test functions supported in $[-L,L]$ has a near radical: about $d=e^{2L}/q$ min-max values far below the rest, as Connes and Consani found for zeta. We show what planted-zero detection loses without it and give a rule for trial spaces. (1) [U] Every codimension-$m$ constraint transversal to an isolated $m$-dimensional near radical loses the near-radical term $K_{\mathrm{NR}}$ of the detection functional $K=v^{T}W^{-1}v$, up to an explicit relative error. (2) Decision rule [num]: a trial space for detection or window-positivity certificates must contain the near radical. At ordinates 100–180, missing it loses a median 6–25% of $K$ when $d$ is 5–11, up to 44% at single ordinates; the near radical plus a band of 38–76 functions around the ordinate recovers a median 95–99% at the two runs tested. (3) [cert] Ball arithmetic certifies, unconditionally, the min-max bound $\mu_k\le10^{-3}$ for $W$ on $L^2$ at 233 indices over 23 $(\chi,L)$ pairs with $d\le18.2$, each $\mu_k$ at most twice its finite-space value. Exact: a Cholesky identity splits $K$ across frequency cuts. Under GRH a min-max count bound holds, and a sketched optimisation of it gives $\#\{k:\mu_k\le\varepsilon\}\ge d-O\big((Ld\log(L/\varepsilon))^{1/2}\big)$ on $L^2$ for large $d$. Measured: the parity sectors alternate through the near radical, a Gamma-factor constant $(2a-1)/8$ accounts for the parity offset, the ladder density grows like $\ln(\pi d)$ (Landau–Widom), and the near-radical term is a median 89–98% of the part of $K$ that needs low modes. At two pairs, replacing the zeros below 110 by smooth points moves the depths by at most 0.3 decades. For the $q=5$ Davenport–Heilbronn function the near radical of the remaining-zero form carries the super-exponential growth of the first off-line zero's detection ratio.
