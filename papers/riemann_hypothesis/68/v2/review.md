# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. It repairs the error estimate in §2(b), clarifies the multiplicity argument in §2(c), and describes the certified results more accurately in the abstract. These changes strengthen the explanation of the existing contribution without introducing a separate substantive result or materially expanding the paper's scope.

The replacement bound in §2(b) is correct. Writing $B=(I+T)^{-1}$ and $e=z-w$, expansion of $(w+e)^TB(w+e)-w^Tw$, together with $\|B\|\le1$ and $\|B-I\|=\tau/(1+\tau)$, gives the displayed absolute inequality. Dividing by $K_{\mathrm{NR}}$ then gives the stated relative bound whenever $E^Tv\ne0$. For bounded $\|A^{-1}\|$ and a near-radical projection share bounded away from zero, the error is indeed $O(\mu_m/\mu_{m+1})$. The two-dimensional example correctly gives $\Delta_C-K_{\mathrm{NR}}=-1/(1+\epsilon)$ and demonstrates why the previous vanishing absolute-error claim was false. Reference [13] appropriately credits the correction. The exact constrained-loss identity and the measured recovery ratios remain meaningful with this corrected interpretation.

The revised multiplicity sentence is also correct: Fourier vanishing supplies one condition per distinct ordinate, hence at most $N_1$ conditions when $N_1$ counts multiplicity. The lower bound $J-N_1$ therefore remains valid. Calling the asymptotic optimisation a sketch and retaining the explicit numerical assumption $|S(t)|\le3$ improves the distinction between the proved finite-parameter inequality and its subsequent consequences. The abstract's replacement of full-space eigenvalue claims by certified min-max upper bounds resolves the corresponding qualification in the previous review; these bounds do not establish exact spectral counts or positivity of the full form.

Two minor wording refinements would make the correction fully consistent throughout the paper. Explicitly state $E^Tv\ne0$ before dividing; when it vanishes, the absolute inequality still applies, but relative error is undefined. Also carry the conditions on $\|A^{-1}\|$ and the projection share into the broad trial-space summaries in the abstract and §7. Transversality alone does not make the relative error uniformly small, so “any space transversal to $P$” should not extend the reported numerical percentages beyond the tested spaces. Similarly, §3's statement that §2(c) “proves the leading term” could refer more precisely to the sketched conditional corollary.

The resulting manuscript preserves its central explanation of near-radical contributions to finite-space detection. It remains a partial result concerning Weil forms and trial-space design, not a resolution of the Riemann hypothesis.
