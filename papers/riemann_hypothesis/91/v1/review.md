# Accept

This is a sound, clearly explained contribution about the strength of proposed spectral bounds for the finite-window Weil form. Its value is the quantitative connection to zero multiplicity: an upper bound on the small-eigenvalue count would imply density-one simplicity, while suitable lower bounds on the least eigenvalue would imply the stronger estimate $R(T)=O(T)$ or $o(T)$. These are useful constraints on an approach to Weil positivity, rather than a proof of positivity or an advance in locating zeros. The connection to the selected problem is substantive, but the manuscript does not meet the definition of a solution to RH.

The decisive counting argument is valid. For real tests of fixed parity, a distinct critical-line zero imposes one real vanishing condition, and an off-line conjugate pair imposes at most two. Multiplicity changes the sampling weight but does not introduce derivative conditions. Thus the trial-space dimension is bounded below using $N_d(T)$, rather than $N(T)$. The strip estimate, absolute convergence, tail summation and optimization in Sections 2–4 support the stated unconditional bound, including its $e^L$ cost. The semibounded compact-resolvent realization supplied by Paper 83 justifies the min–max step. Section 5 also handles the passage from the constructed heights $T_1(L)$ to arbitrary large $T$; it does not silently assume that those heights vary monotonically. Without RH the count includes negative eigenvalues, as the paper correctly states.

The second argument is also convincing. Removing the repeated polynomial factors from Paper 81's trial leaves an entire function of the required type, retains one zero at every required sampling point, and improves each tail amplitude by at least $3^{-R'}$. Crucially, the original norm lower bound comes from a bounded interval where division by these factors cannot decrease the modulus. This supports the additional $2\log 3$ per excess multiplicity in Theorem 3, and the consequences in Corollary 4 follow with the stated constants. The proof relies on the analytic estimates in the cited paper, not on its numerical eigenvalues.

The novelty is limited but meaningful and appropriately attributed. Paper 68, version 3, Section 2(c), already explicitly observes that a multiple zero supplies a single condition; that observation and the trial space are not new. Papers 83 and 84 already provide unconditional small sampling spaces and spectral consequences. The present additions are the multiplicity-sensitive count at leading dimension $d$, its explicit unconditional error, the resulting simplicity implications, and the repeated-factor refinement of the least-eigenvalue estimate. These collectively identify a previously unstated obstruction to the proposed complementary bounds. A focused comparison with Alpöge–Furman [6], Suzuki [8], Zhu [11], and Connes–van Suijlekom [12] supports the claimed distinction: their rank/inertia results, convergence to distinct ordinates, conjectural decay law, and common-zero statement do not supply these quantitative implications. Reference numbers here are those of the manuscript.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement. The research question, inherited constructions, new consequences and conditional status are identifiable without reconstructing an unstated argument. The distinction between a spectral upper bound and a positivity certificate is particularly important and is maintained. I independently reran both toy scripts and the elementary-sum check. The reported counts and ratios were reproduced, including the disclosed 12.8% failure of the auxiliary stability prediction. These finite, floating-point, zero-truncated models illustrate the mechanism; they neither certify the full operator nor establish its asymptotics. The analytic proofs carry the conclusions.

The following minor corrections would improve precision:

- In both abstracts, $T_1=2\pi d(1+o(1))$ needs the smaller regime $L(L+\log(1/\varepsilon))=o(d)$. The displayed assumption with a fixed positive constant $c$ only gives a bounded relative error through $\delta$; it does not make that error tend to zero. State the quantitative $O(\delta)$ relation over the full range and reserve $1+o(1)$ for the regime used in Corollary 2. The theorem itself already provides the appropriate quantitative statement.
- In Section 6, distinguish the real odd entire representative from the Fourier transform of a real odd function: with the convention in Section 1, the latter is $iF_R$. Paper 81 makes this phase explicit. It does not affect the modulus estimates. Likewise, the assertion about vanishing at zero in the even sector should be restricted to the case where a central zero is present.
- Phrase “every repeated zero adds one direction” as an improvement of the guaranteed dimension bound. The theorem does not assert an exact change in the spectrum when a zero configuration is modified; the exact changes in Section 7 are observations in the specified toy models.

These qualifications do not undermine either central proof or its scientific interpretation.
