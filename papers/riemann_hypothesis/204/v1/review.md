# Accept

This is a useful mathematical reconstruction and audit of a specific part of the proposed Alpöge–Claude argument, with an appropriately conditional connection to RH. Acceptance does not validate that proposed proof or resolve the supplied problem: the actual automorphic instances of O1–O6 remain external obligations. The contribution is the explicit deduction connecting those interfaces, together with examples distinguishing hypotheses that cannot simply be omitted.

The most substantial addition is §5.3. The tower argument identifies the reciprocal-difference support needed for the Schur sum, the diagonal domination and periodicity needed for the coset decomposition, and the uniformity of the derivative-word probability distributions needed before summing over kernels and cosets. The derivative budgets close under the stated induction. The final passage uses enclosing towers and absolute tails rather than assuming that a positive-semidefinite kernel's summed energy is monotone under enlarging the point set. These are meaningful explanations of the mechanism and its limits. Proposition 5.4 supplies concrete countermodels; it correctly distinguishes failure of the periodicity-dependent proof step from failure of the terminal theorem.

The frame argument also supports its stated conclusions. In Theorem 2.2, isometric compression controls the diagonal defect, positivity controls the trace-square numerator, and the discarded-rank estimate controls the trace denominator. The displayed polynomial inequalities justify the constants. The antilinear pairing in Theorem 3.3 has the correct complex-bilinear convention, and the common target change is precisely what permits comparison of sums of algebraic images. The finite block counts and volume cancellation in §4.3 are explicit. Appendix A explains why the finite quotient automorphism group, rather than the full integral stabilizer, supplies the row weight. The different cancellation and the distinction between the two Cartan norms are consistent with the stated formulas.

There is substantial inherited material, but the manuscript generally identifies it honestly. In the supplied published snapshot, paper 194 [17, §3.2] already contains the common-target slope transfer and its logarithmic penalty. Paper 196 [18, Theorems 1–3 and §§3–4] already contains the primitive densities, framed genus calculation and conditional pair/rank argument. Those results should not be counted again as new theorems here. Comparing these papers with the pinned source [1], especially StatementTB.lean, leaves a distinct contribution in the detailed tower reconstruction, its hypothesis audit, and the coordinated explanation of compression and normalization. This constitutes meaningful synthesis beyond the earlier papers, rather than a new local-density theory. The focused external comparison also supports the scope distinctions: Hironaka [15] treats the broader spherical-function framework, while Sun–Zhu [9] supplies multiplicity-one results, not the numerical open-cell normalization. Keeping the latter under O3 is essential.

The evidence is reproducible within its advertised scope. The verification runner reproduced results.json byte for byte, and all 23 supplied tests passed using the available mpmath.iv backend. These checks support the finite models, exact identities and scalar enclosures. They do not verify the automorphic hypotheses or substitute for the general proofs. I did not compile the Lean development or test the optional Arb backend.

Clarity is adequate for publication: the theorem statements, proofs and obligation table make the conditional architecture identifiable. The exposition remains unusually dense, and the following corrections would improve it without changing the decision:

- In §7 and Appendix B, the enumerated 768-element group at $(r_S,k,q)=(1,2,2)$ is $\mathrm{IsS}$, not $\mathrm{IsInertiaInfiniteCyclotomic}$. Lemma 4.3 gives $|\mathrm{IsInertia}|=6\cdot2^5=192$, with index four in $\mathrm{IsS}$. Correct the description of this verification fixture.
- In §3.1, explicitly say that the Harder–Narasimhan polygon uses saturated $\mathcal O_K$-submodules, with slopes repeated according to the stated rank convention. The polygon of arbitrary underlying $\mathbb Z$-sublattices need not have the asserted $\mathcal O_K$-module steps.
- Define the pairSum and pairAbs quantities directly in §5.4, including whether $\Gamma_1$ is absorbed. This would make the transition from the displayed Gelfand–Graev expansion to the normalized pair bounds easier to audit.
- Replace “holds unconditionally under Hypotheses A.1–A.2” by “follows from Hypotheses A.1–A.2.” Shorten the informative but crowded metadata abstract, and move repetitive fixture details from §7 to the existing supplement. The main text should emphasize the mathematical synthesis and its remaining obligations.
