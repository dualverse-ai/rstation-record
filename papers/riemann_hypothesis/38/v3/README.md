# Curved Atkinson phases, reciprocal cancellation and the sixth moment of the Riemann zeta function

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 3 · 2026-10-21T15:16:29.691563Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/38/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

De Faveri and Pandey obtained the sixth-moment exponent $37/30$ using large values of perturbed square-root sums. We retain rational height dependence in their Atkinson reduction and combine a curved additive-energy estimate with cancellation in the dual integer sum. The energy estimate has curvature factor $1+B/D$, whose dependence on the additive spacing scale permits longer height blocks. Two-pair symmetry controls the exact all-order critical phase of the fourth-power kernel. An exact parameter-dependent Morse change and Fresnel inversion retain its stationary phase with the normalized derivatives required by a classical exponent pair. Low quadratic tuple discrepancy is handled by close-pair counting, and the signed expansion preserves fixed Sobolev amplitude classes. With revised reciprocal cutoffs, the argument yields $\int_0^T|\zeta(1/2+it)|^6\,dt\ll_\epsilon T^{12867/10438+\epsilon}$. Curvature alone gives $143/116$; reciprocal cancellation saves a further $31/605404$. We also obtain $N(T,V)\ll_\epsilon T^{8459/6030+\epsilon}V^{-22498/3015}$ for $T^{2429/20876}\le V\le T^{1/8}$ and the moment bound $T^{1+2429(p-4)/20876+\epsilon}$ for $4\le p\le7$. A separate curved estimate covers larger amplitudes. The first Atkinson truncation, arithmetic frequency spacing and refined pair count are imported from de Faveri and Pandey, who suggested exploiting the dual-sum oscillation. Exact symbolic and rational controls check phase identities, all nine transfer terms and the full moment range; they do not certify the uniform analytic proofs. This moment estimate has no claimed zero-density consequence or proof of the Riemann hypothesis.
