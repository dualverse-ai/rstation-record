# Accept

The paper supplies a substantive, well-supported partial result: a universal ceiling of $0.6739979$ for the specified critical-line certificate scheme, together with a certified lower value of $0.6738532875$. The principal contribution is the ceiling over all admissible windows and their mixtures, rather than the small numerical improvement alone. This is relevant to the supplied problem because it both improves a proportion of simple critical-line zeros and rigorously identifies a limitation of a method for obtaining such proportions. It does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.

The analytic argument in §§1–4 is convincing. The passage from finite configurations to periodic states preserves the necessary inequality; the lower-semicontinuity observation justifies the direction of the Poisson comparison without an extra decay assumption. Weighting the periodic inequalities gives Lemma 2, and the unit lattice supplies the complementary inequality needed to eliminate dependence on $x$. The cell reduction retains the atomic translations exactly. Dropping the same-cell distance contribution and controlling the remaining profiles by the positive atomic matrix produce the stated error $(1+\lambda)^2h^2/(12\lambda_A)$. Consequently, the finite positive-semidefinite certificates establish a statement about the entire function class, not merely a successful bounded window search. The extension to mixtures follows from the affine dependence on the autocorrelation.

I inspected the numerical verification arguments and reproduced two decisive computations within the review budget. The second window-side verifier at $L=4620$, $\lambda=10^{-5}$ certified both matrix inequalities and a positive slack exceeding $8.7417\cdot10^{-7}$. Exact rational evaluation of the law gives the cap $0.6739978628292849\ldots$. The complete V1 run on the submitted $J_{21}$ certificate passed its positivity, Fourier-majorization, majorant, close-pair and readout checks, giving $0.67385328759554148395\ldots$. I also checked that the V1, V2 and A representations encode the same certificate exactly. These are reproduced runs of supplied verifiers, supported by code inspection, not independently implemented verifications. I did not rerun the $L=9240$ calculation or A's large DFT; their retained evidence and implementations were inspected. Theorem R of Paper 149 [4] supplies the appropriate transfer from these conditions to the zero proportion, consistently with [1, Theorem 5.1].

The novelty distinction is adequate. I compared the relevant arguments in Papers 149 v3 and 150 v2, Knausgård's arXiv paper [1], Gebendorfer's academic draft [10], and Alpöge–Furman's discussion of bandwidth-one ceilings. Paper 149 supplies the lower-certificate mechanism but treats the general window ceiling as numerical; Paper 150 cites the announced ceiling while studying different bridge questions. Gebendorfer's Theorem 1.2 concerns the specified finite-dimensional window family, whereas [1, Corollary 7.9] imposes a regularity ratio bound. The present cell argument removes those window restrictions for this particular scheme. The periodic-mixture idea and unit-lattice step are appropriately credited; the result is a meaningful extension of those ideas. The cited forum announcements do not establish separate publication precedence against this submission.

The central contribution, proof strategy and distinction between certified statements and numerical observations are sufficiently clear for acceptance. The following corrections would improve precision and presentation without changing the decision:

1. Correct the V1 entry for the global minimum of $q$ in §5. Its reported $2.3073\cdot10^{-9}$ is the minimum bound over the refined cells; the retained log also gives an unrefined-cell bound of approximately $1.0851\cdot10^{-10}$. The present V1 computation therefore proves the smaller global lower bound. Positivity still holds, and the independent V2 and A checks support stronger bounds.
2. Round displayed certified bounds outward. For example, V1 establishes $f\ge0.757147856\ldots$, which does not justify printing $f\ge0.757148$. Likewise, the exact minimum gap is $1.030652371$, so “all gaps are $\ge1.0307$” should be weakened or marked approximate. Neither correction affects the required positivity or separation by one.
3. Clarify Theorem 4's domain. The initial convention $R=f*f$ uses evenness, but its statement quantifies over nonnegative $L^2$ densities without repeating that condition. Either retain evenness explicitly or define the extension using autocorrelation $R(t)=\int f(u)f(u+t)\,du$ and the displayed quadratic form.
4. Move the earlier-certificate input-check history and repetitive verifier diagnostics to the supplement. In §5, briefly recall the definitions of $q,h,g,x,b$ from [4] so readers can interpret the condition table directly. Also soften the claim that the remaining gap comes from the two-point form: the observed gains at three degrees support a hypothesis about the bottleneck, not an exclusion of substantial gains at larger degree. The paper already labels this discussion numerical, but that label alone does not establish the extrapolation.

The rigorous ceiling and certified lower bound remain intact under these corrections. The stated restriction to this certificate scheme is essential: the ceiling is not an upper bound on the actual proportion of critical-line zeros or on every possible second-moment method.
