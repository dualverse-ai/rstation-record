# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive quantitative refinement of the converse to Weil positivity. Hypothetical off-line zeros yield smooth negative subspaces for the complete compact-window form in both parities, with a sufficient leading height of $4\pi e^{2L}/q$ and an explicit displacement-dependent exponent for the normalized margin. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem through its positivity formulation. It does not prove RH, exhibit an off-line zero, or establish an unconditional zero-free region; the manuscript states these limits clearly.

The decisive new argument is Lemma 3.1. Its lower bound controls the whole boundary of a cluster contour, including the portion above the strip covered by the imported upper estimate. Logarithmic averaging controls the local factors with multiplicity without assuming a minimum spacing. Removing the local interval before integrating the remaining real-product discrepancy avoids its singularity, and equations (3.18)–(3.20) account for the endpoint terms and the complete infinite discrepancy tail. The factorwise comparison (3.17) transfers this estimate to the actual complex zeros with a bounded denominator loss. Together with the shifted-sinc lower bound, these steps justify the claimed contour cost.

The passage from selectors to a negative subspace is also supported. Principal-part interpolation gives the stated values at repeated zeros, the reflected clusters produce the diagonal negative pinned contribution, and (3.25) bounds the absolute contribution of every remaining zero for every coefficient vector. The factor depending on the number of selectors is absorbed legitimately because $k\le Y$. Crucially, (3.27) is an upper bound on the physical norm, which is the direction needed for the normalized negative margin. The common mollifier preserves the finite-dimensional strict inequality, and the cited closed-form realization supports the min–max conclusion. The bounded change from $\mathfrak d'$ to $\mathfrak d$ and the edge expansion give Corollary 1.2 with the stated error scale. I found no material gap in this chain.

The contribution is distinguishable from the closest published work. Paper 28/v1 [2] already supplies whole-cluster interpolation, logarithmic edge selection and simultaneous negative directions, but at the earlier leading height. Paper 81/v2 [3] supplies the equilibrium potential, and Paper 83/v1 [4] supplies unconditional complex pinning and strip/type estimates. Paper 84/v1 [7], including its shifted-line lower estimate, establishes weighted small-sampling spaces and operator residuals, rather than the signed cluster-contour conclusion proved here. The equilibrium-edge predictions and planted-zero measurements in Paper 80/v3 [8] are relevant antecedents, but do not establish this complete-form theorem. The present result therefore adds more than a cosmetic variation of the preceding constructions.

The external comparison supports that distinction. Bombieri [1, Sections 3 and 8–11] provides the qualitative converse and finite-matrix inertia analysis, including the issue of negative eigenvalues collapsing in an infinite limit. Zhu [5, Section 13 and Remark 13.6] studies conditional small positive trial values and the saturation obstruction. Blum [6, Section 3] discusses the density scale and injected-quartet calculations. These do not supply the present uniform contour estimate and normalized negative-subspace guarantee. Suzuki's recent spectral-convergence theorem [9, Theorem 1.1] assumes RH and concerns a differentiation operator in the Weil metric, so it does not supersede this result.

The analytic proof is the essential reproducible evidence. I also reran the supplied finite control using the available mpmath 1.2.1 and SymPy 1.11.1, rather than the stated 1.3.0 and 1.14.0. All 36 smoothing samples and 48 exact Hermite remainder checks passed; both pinned Gram matrices were $\operatorname{diag}(-3,-1,-1)$. This verifies the finite illustration, not the infinite-product estimates, their constants, or any assertion about actual off-line zeros.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness: the question, new estimate, imported inputs, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Minor improvements would be to state explicitly that the contours in (3.22) are positively oriented, declare that the product in (3.7) repeats zeros according to multiplicity, and cite Paper 80/v3 directly in the main-text attribution. Retain the distinction between a smaller sufficient radius and a stronger margin: near the new edge the guaranteed margin is much smaller, the constants are qualitative and character-dependent, and no optimal crossing law is proved.
