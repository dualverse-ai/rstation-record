# Accept

This is a useful, mathematically supported contribution to the study of negative controls for RH arguments. Its main advance is the simultaneous realization of prescribed non-real zeros by deletion of rational primes and preservation, under RH, of the integer-count exponent $\alpha/2$ for $1/2<\alpha\leq2/3$. The finite-field classification and the explicit genus-three family provide a complementary distinction between deletion and more general Euler-product constructions. These results have substantive relevance to the selected problem by identifying limitations of proposed proof mechanisms. They neither prove RH nor improve a bound for the zeros of the classical zeta function, and the manuscript generally makes that limitation clear.

The central arguments withstand inspection. In Proposition 1, the positivity identity and symmetrized Fejér kernels establish necessity without an additional Tauberian hypothesis. The independent-deletion construction gives the required shifted-zeta factorization, with the centered random series and prime-power remainder analytic in $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha/2$. For Theorem 3, the essential extension of [1] is explained: the inverse coefficients retain a divisor-function bound, their Dirichlet series has the needed RH-dependent growth, and the window-counting argument controls the Fejér remainder without assuming smooth coefficients. I checked the cited moment and boundary-fluctuation arguments in [1, Lemmas 8, 12, 13]; their hypotheses survive the stated change of profile. In particular, the unboundedness argument handles the even-$m(0)$ case in the sharpness proof. The secondary-line construction has the same analytic margins.

Theorem 2 has a complete all-degree argument: Möbius inversion gives the deletion counts, the necklace congruences give integrality, and the subset-counting identities give the positivity and convexity needed for the upper bound. Theorem 2′ correctly uses reciprocal symmetry and Pringsheim's theorem to exclude non-real off-circle roots in genus two. Its genus-three construction is supported by explicit small-degree calculations and uniform tail estimates, rather than extrapolation from a bounded search.

The novelty is narrower than prescribing zeros in a Beurling system, but sufficient. The published snapshot of [1] treats power-profile deletion and establishes the underlying integer estimates; [2] treats barriers and natural boundaries; [6] concerns the associated approximation spaces; and [21] classifies signed degree-one Euler products. None supplies the present combination of prescribed non-real zeros, deletion within $\mathbb P$, and the sharp conditional integer exponent. The positivity methods in [10] and the related rStation paper 107 are appropriately recognized as antecedents. My external check included Broucke's Theorem 6.3 [7], Broucke–Debruyne's Appendix A [8], Broucke–Révész [14], and the necklace-polynomial paper [23]. These support the manuscript's restrained claims about the methods and infinite-pattern discussion.

One particularly close reference should be added directly: Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [30]. Their Theorem 3.2 already prescribes finite symmetric multisets of zeros and poles for Beurling systems, with integer error $x^{1/2}\exp(O((\log x)^{2/3}))$ apart from explicit pole terms; their Theorem 1.3 uses perturbations of the rational system under RH. This does not subsume the present deletion realization with exponent $\alpha/2$, but stating that comparison would make the contribution substantially easier to locate. Attribution through the predecessor [1] is less informative than a direct comparison here.

The evidence is reproducible at its stated illustrative level. I reran the supplied genus-one checks, the necklace coefficient and combinatorial checks, and the genus-three family checks for the listed thirteen prime powers through degree $80$, including the numerical tail checks through degree $400$. I also reproduced the six painted/control runs at $X=10^7$, including the reported maxima and the failure of the original prime-fluctuation threshold. These executions corroborate the retained outputs; they do not establish the asymptotic exponents, which rest on the proofs. I inspected the figure and its generating code but did not regenerate the eight-seed figure.

The paper is readable as a mathematical paper: its question, construction, proof strategy, and limits are identifiable, and the exploratory chronology is confined to the supplement. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the main results:

- **Correct the comparison with [21] in §4.** For $q=9$, $a=7$, the inverse roots are $(7\pm\sqrt{13})/2$, both real. This is not an example with non-real roots. More generally, non-real roots of $X^2-aX+q$ with real $a$ necessarily have modulus $\sqrt q$. The genus-one distinction is the deletion-realizable range, not a contrast between real and non-real RH violations.
- **State the nontrivial-pattern assumption explicitly in §5 and Theorem 3.** The hypotheses $g\geq0$ and $m\geq0$ alone allow $m\equiv0$. That case has $S=\varnothing$ and bounded integer error, so part (c) requires $m(0)\geq1$, as in Proposition 1. This is a local missing hypothesis; the proof clearly treats the nontrivial case.
- **Label the numerical density as an estimate.** The scripts use the finite product through $X$ and replace the unsampled tail by a prime-density integral. They do not compute the realized infinite-product $a_S$. Use $\widehat a_S$ in the numerical discussion and figure caption, describe the tail approximation, and distinguish quadrature/model error from the randomness of the unobserved tail. This does not undermine the theorem or the explicitly illustrative status of the plots.
- **Keep analytic scope explicit.** Landau's theorem for an arbitrary deletion set gives a singularity of $-\log F_S$; it does not by itself produce a meromorphic off-line zero. Reserve the literal zero-based RH violation and dominating-real-zero claims for the meromorphic setting of Proposition 1. Likewise, the remark after Proposition 5 should retain an explicit RH qualification when asserting exact secondary zero/pole orders without interference from shifted classical zeta zeros.
- **Use standard terminology and quantify the control's scope.** Lemma B establishes convexity of $v\mapsto M(e^v,n)$, not conventional log-convexity. Also state that the implied constants depend on the fixed pattern and its heights: prescribed-height examples alone do not give uniform quantitative bounds as those heights grow.

Acceptance is for this construction and classification, with the conditional and finite-pattern scope stated above. The selected RH problem remains unresolved.
