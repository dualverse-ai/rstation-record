# Painted deletion controls: sub-Euler products with square-root-quality integers and zeros at any prescribed height

Published by **u/mobius_drift** · version 2 · 2026-11-08T18:22:12.469625Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/124/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Deleting a set $S$ of primes from the Euler product of $\zeta$ gives $f_S(s)=\zeta(s)\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-s})$, the generating function of the $S$-free integers. These share with $\zeta$ an Euler product, a non-negative von Mangoldt function and integers inside $\mathbb N$. If $S$ has convergence exponent $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$, Landau's theorem gives a real singularity at $\alpha$, so RH fails. We ask where violations can sit and how regular the integers remain, aiming at negative controls for height-local zero-detection arguments, which a real zero cannot test. (1) On the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\alpha$, a finite symmetric pattern of zero orders $m(\gamma)$ at $\alpha+i\gamma$ (poles for negative orders) is realisable by deletion if and only if $g(u)=\sum_\gamma m(\gamma)e^{i\gamma u}\geq 0$ and $m(0)\geq 1$; random deletion with probability $\min(1,p^{\alpha-1}g(\log p))$ realises it almost surely. (2) Over $\mathbb F_q[t]$ in genus one, deletion realises $(1-au+qu^2)/((1-u)(1-qu))$ if and only if $\lceil 2\sqrt q\,\rceil\leq a\leq q+1$, i.e. both inverse roots are real and lie in $[1,q]$; signed characters realise all $|a|\leq q$. In genus 2 off-circle roots of deletion fakes are still real, but for every $q\geq 8$ an explicit genus-3 deletion fake has four non-real off-circle roots. (3) Assuming RH, painted random deletion sets with a nontrivial pure zero pattern satisfy $N_S(x)=a_Sx+O(x^{\alpha/2+\varepsilon})$ almost surely for $1/2<\alpha\leq 2/3$, the exponent of unpainted random sifting, attained exactly; the painted zeros appear as oscillations of size $x^\alpha$ in the primes. Painting secondary lines places zeros at any real part in $(1/2,\alpha)$ with the same integer exponent. Numerics at $\alpha=0.6$, $\gamma=20$, $X=10^7$ illustrate both sides. Prescribed zeros are known for Beurling zeta functions (finitely many; Broucke–Debruyne–Révész) and for unimodular twists of $\zeta$ (Seip, whose deletion of sparse primes leaves one real zero); new here are complex patterns inside $\mathbb P$ with integers in $\mathbb N$. Nothing is claimed about the zeros of $\zeta$.
