# Accept

This revision appropriately corrects and clarifies the accepted contribution. It preserves the realization of non-real zero patterns by deleting rational primes, the conditional integer-count estimates, and the finite-field classification. The additions explain those results and qualify their numerical illustration; they do not introduce a separate substantive contribution. The resulting manuscript remains organized around its mathematical argument, with detailed computational records in the supplement.

The important corrections are sound. The genus-one comparison now distinguishes the realizable ranges rather than incorrectly contrasting real and non-real RH violations. The negative-root example with $q=9$, $a=-7$ agrees with the signed-character classification in [21] and lies outside the deletion range. The explicit hypothesis $m(0)\geq1$ removes the empty-set counterexample to Theorem 3(c). The remark after Proposition 5 correctly separates unconditional meromorphic continuation from the RH-dependent assertion of exact prescribed orders. The revised terminology for Lemma B and the stated dependence of constants on the pattern and its heights also improve precision.

The added attribution materially improves the paper. I checked [30, Theorems 3.2 and 1.3] and [33, §6.1 and the relevant main theorem statements]. These support the comparisons: general Beurling systems already admit prescribed finite zero/pole sets, and Seip already constructs a sparse rational-prime subproduct with a single real zero. The retained distinction is complex patterns realized by deletion within $\mathbb P$, their positivity constraint, and the conditional integer exponent. The withdrawal notice for [31] is accurately reported. These additions narrow the novelty claim without removing the accepted contribution.

The density relabeling in §5 and Figure 1 is necessary and useful. After inspecting the source and its dependency, I reproduced the new calculation through $Y=10^8$. It gives the reported finite-interval discrepancies $-1.953\times10^{-8}$ and $-4.005\times10^{-9}$, and estimated tail standard deviations $3.533\times10^{-6}$ and $3.666\times10^{-6}$. This corroborates the retained numerical output, not a rigorous bound for the infinite tail or an asymptotic exponent.

A few local qualifications remain:

- **State the numerical uncertainty as an estimate.** The script replaces the variance beyond $Y$ by an integral and does not certify the prime-density model error beyond $Y$ or separately bound quadrature error. Thus “less than $0.001$” for the total model/quadrature effect should be labeled an estimate unless an explicit bound is supplied. Two standard deviations describe a fluctuation scale, not a guaranteed bound. The largest reported two-standard-deviation shift is $0.1211$, so use “about $0.12$” rather than “at most $0.12$.” These qualifications do not alter the illustrative conclusion.
- **Make the analytic wording consistent throughout.** Landau gives a singularity of $-\log F_S$; $f_S$ itself may instead be analytic with a zero there. Replace the residual blanket wording “every deletion has a real singularity” in §§1, 6–7 by “a real zero or singularity,” and qualify the abstract's “so RH fails” accordingly. In §2, the non-negative logarithmic coefficients belong to $-\log F_S$, not $\log F_S$.
- **Correct the supplementary chronology.** The new notebook labels its pre-registration 09:52 UTC but its results 09:51 UTC. As written, this does not establish that the predictions preceded the calculation. Correct the timestamps from retained records or describe the calculation without the pre-registration claim.

These are minor precision issues in an otherwise appropriate corrective revision. Acceptance concerns the stated negative controls and conditional results; the Riemann hypothesis remains unresolved.
