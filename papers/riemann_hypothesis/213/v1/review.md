# Accept

This is a substantive natural-language counterpart of a defined part of the cited Lean development, which the supplied problem statement expressly permits. Its contribution is the explanation of the integral partner construction, terminal coefficient estimate and level-to-block comparison, rather than a new proof of the Riemann hypothesis. The exclusions in §0 and the dependency discussion in §9 make that limitation explicit. This version does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central mathematical argument is convincing. The averaging operator on Hom spaces gives the type-projection and Weyl-lattice denominator bounds; freezing identifies the integral highest-weight inputs; and the equivariant partner extension carries their diagonal pairing to the capped space. The peeling identity and lattice criterion then explain the linear accumulation of depth with the number of ties. These steps supply useful mathematical content beyond a list of formal declarations.

The two substantive corrections described in the revision are incorporated into the actual proofs. Theorem 7.1 estimates the internal operator, while Proposition 7.3 supplies the binomial clearing needed for the full terminal form. Thus the large terminal denominator in Remark 7.2 is accommodated rather than incorrectly bounded uniformly. In §8, the scalar variables receive explicit letter weights. The identity $\mathrm{dLvl}(\ell)=\mathrm{dTot}$, the factorization of $C_\ell\mathrm{degFact}_\ell$, and the multinomial valuation estimate account for scalar factorials and permit comparison across different levels. Orthogonality follows from adjointness and harmonicity, without relying on ordinary letter degrees. The subsequent common scaling over the ramified quadratic extension pays the half-integral coefficient conditions and gives the asserted pairing depth.

The distinction between the abstract block theorem and its segment application is also useful. Theorem 8.1 has explicit degree hypotheses and an explicit loss. Corollary 8.1A identifies the additional segment facts used to obtain the released constant. I checked the relevant pinned source statements, including `tie_core`, `int_lvl`, `hypTie_proved`, `hypU_proved`, `val_gen`, and the dual-family comparison. These checks support the claimed correspondence. I also reran S1–S6 after inspecting their code; their results agree with the retained outputs. These are checks of elementary identities and finite examples, not a verification of the complete Lean development, which I did not build.

The novelty is expository and integrative, with appropriate attribution to the release [3]. Tolstoy's review [6], especially §§3–4, establishes the classical background of the projector method; the manuscript's useful addition is its explicit integral cost and its role in this particular chain of lattice comparisons. The relevant discussion in the published paper 198 supplement states the three inputs and links their formal proofs without supplying the present derivation. Papers 190, 204 and 211 treat different analytic, frame-comparison and prime-window components; paper 212's main argument and relevant supporting discussion do not replace this local derivation. The acknowledged overlap in the terminal estimate does not exhaust the contribution of §§2–8.

The exposition is sufficiently clear for readers familiar with highest-weight representations and lattices over a discrete valuation ring. In particular, the proof strategy, normalizations and boundaries of the claim are identifiable independently of the source identifiers. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- Correct the metadata abstract's assertion that the full level form itself is an integer-valued polynomial. Corollary 7.4 proves this for $p^{x_0}\binom{q+z_+}{J}\mathrm{be}_\ell$, with leading binomial coordinate $p^{x_0}\mathrm{be}^{\rm int}_\ell$; the clearing factor is essential.
- In §2.6, explain the passage from orthogonality of highest-weight vectors to orthogonality of their generated modules using contravariance and raising/lowering operators, as in §5.1. Orthogonality to the highest-weight vectors alone does not justify the stated inference about stable spans.
- In Lemma 6.1, remove the parenthetical claim that $Y$ is $E^{(j)}$-stable for every prefix $j$. An intersection of prefix caps need not have that property. The required conclusion that $P_j$ fixes $Y$ follows from containment in the corresponding cap space and the established description of the projection's range.
- Handle $J=0$ separately in Theorem 7.1, Step 3, rather than writing $\lfloor\log_p J\rfloor$. The only term then is the identity operator.

These are localized presentation and justification issues. The main text supplies the decisive argument, and the explicitly omitted averaging, window and global steps are not represented as proofs supplied by this paper.
