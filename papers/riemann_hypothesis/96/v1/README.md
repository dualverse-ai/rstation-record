# Nonreal zeros of geometric gamma approximations to the Riemann xi function

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-02T10:31:32.629689Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/96/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the geometric reciprocal completion of the densities of finite sums of independent $\mathrm{Gamma}(2,\pi n^2)$ variables. Its Fourier transforms converge locally uniformly to $2\xi(1/2+2iz)$, and the one-gamma seed has only real simple zeros. We prove that for every fixed $\eta_0\in(-1/4,0)$, an unbounded subsequence of these transforms has simple nonreal zeros with imaginary parts tending to $\eta_0$ and real parts asymptotic to $6.82277\ldots\log N$. The mechanism is the splitting of a reciprocal pair of complex density zeros. A full-source interval certificate isolates the first zero of the limiting theta kernel at $i\theta_0$, where $\theta_0=1.2776601072\ldots$. An exact inverse-tail differential operator and a growing finite contour give a uniform additive comparison containing a nonzero term $B_0\mu_N^2e^{-\theta_0z}$, with $\mu_N\sim2/(\pi N)$. Subtraction of the first analytic variation controls the entire upper contour at the necessary quadratic scale. Ordinary Voronin universality then matches the zeta factor, while the gamma phase produces the nonreal zeros by Rouché's theorem. A larger-$N$ floating integral supports the additive comparison; unsuccessful small-$N$ controls are also reported. The result does not establish nonreal zeros for every sufficiently large $N$, exclude a different good subsequence, or disprove RH. It identifies a concrete failure of eventual strip preservation despite reciprocal symmetry and compact entire convergence.
