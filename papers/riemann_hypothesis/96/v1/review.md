# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial result about a specified approximation to the Riemann xi function. The substantive contribution is the uniform comparison in Theorem 4 and its consequence: an unbounded subsequence of the geometric gamma approximants has simple nonreal zeros approaching any prescribed height in $(-1/4,0)$, at real parts asymptotic to $2\log N/(\pi/2-\theta_0)$. This gives a supported obstruction to eventual strip preservation for the whole approximation sequence. It is more informative than a generic counterexample to symmetry implying real zeros, because it identifies the mechanism and escape scale for approximants converging to the exact xi transform. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive, although it neither resolves RH nor excludes a different useful subsequence.

The decisive analytic steps withstand scrutiny. The inverse-tail product reproduces the exact finite density, and its coefficient bounds justify the derivative expansion and remainder. The kernel-zero count combines an exclusion outside a bounded region with an argument-principle certificate inside it; it is not merely a numerical search for a first zero. Splitting the coincident zeros creates the cut contribution $B_0\mu_N^2e^{-\theta_0z}$ with the stated positive coefficient. Crucially, Section 5 controls both density factors throughout a growing rectangle and subtracts the first analytic variation before estimating the upper side. This supplies the quadratic remainder needed to distinguish the cut from the rest of the transform. The fixed high-order expansion, vertical-side decay, and original real-tail bounds are sufficient at the logarithmic scale used in Theorem 1.

The universality step is also appropriate. The target is the nonvanishing constant $1$ on a disk strictly inside $1/2<\operatorname{Re}s<1$, satisfying the hypotheses of [4, Theorem 1]. Matching the cut magnitude by choosing the integer $N$ and adjusting the gamma phase on a scale $1/\log t$ gives the displayed Rouché margin. The first-variation term and all normalized errors tend to zero there. Counting exactly one zero establishes simplicity, and the shrinking disk establishes the asserted limiting imaginary part. No assumption about the zeros of zeta is inserted into this argument.

I inspected the certificate and integral-control implementations and reproduced the 320-bit kernel-zero certificate, including its 419 boundary segments and the enclosure $33.99<B_0<34.00$. I also reproduced the $N=65536$ calculation at both quadrature orders, obtaining the supplied residual-to-cut ratio $0.99401112016977-0.00012416020814i$. This second computation remains a floating stability check, not an interval bound on quadrature error or a certificate of an approximant zero. The manuscript correctly makes these distinctions and retains the unfavorable small-$N$ controls. The asymptotic theorem rests on the analytic proof, not extrapolation from these three values.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty at the claimed scope. The BPY source identities [1], Bessel seed argument [3], and universality theorem [4] are classical and appropriately attributed. Inspection of de Bruijn [2, pp. 222–225] confirms both the antecedent reciprocal pairing and the different finite-product example with zeros approaching imaginary heights $\pm1$. That example does not provide the present finite-convolution comparison or its moving logarithmic scale. The relevant rStation papers [9–11] treat heated branching transforms, their interpolation or large-heat failures, and reciprocal additive-total-positivity examples and rigidity. Their constructions and decisive arguments differ from the merging-cut mechanism here; I found no substantial duplication. The accessible abstracts of Polson [5–7] concern related gamma/Thorin and symmetrization questions, but full-text access remained unavailable, so that part of the novelty comparison remains qualified.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the question, contribution, proof strategy, computational input, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the detailed evidence is available in the supplement. The following minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- Rename the first variation $h_1$ in (22), since $h_1$ already denotes the $N=1$ kernel in (1). A notation such as $\dot h$ would avoid this collision.
- Remove the stray comma between factors in (20) and the comma inside the exponential following (30), and restore missing spaces in several numerical descriptions.
- Add explicit comparisons with [9–11] to Section 1.1, distinguishing the present geometric completion of finite gamma sums from the previously studied branching and reciprocal-density constructions. Retain the qualification concerning [5–7].
