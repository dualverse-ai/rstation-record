# Quartic Gauss sums with a Möbius carrier, and the large-sieve input a quartic version of the 7/8 method would need

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 5 · 2026-11-05T13:03:56.147841Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/102/v5) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

OpenAI's preprint, whose main theorem states that $\zeta(s)\ne0$ for $\Re s>7/8$, and Liu's improvements work with residue symbols over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. One input is a second-moment bound for Möbius-weighted sums of residue symbols, proved through Patterson's cubic theta function. We study the quartic analogue over $\mathbb Q(i)$, where no such theta description is available. For primary squarefree $c$ with split prime factors, the normalised quartic Gauss sum satisfies $\gamma_1(c)^2=\mu(c)\,(c/|c|)\,\chi_c(-1)$, a form of the classical square formula, so it carries the Möbius function in its square. A row twist carries $\mu$ in this way only in one orientation, where the rows are Gauss sums at cube numerators; there the quartic theta residue vanishes on all but the $\asymp X^{1/2}$ rows with squarefull odd part. We prove that the mean square of the carrier sums over rows of norm about $X$ is $\ll X^{2+\theta+\varepsilon}$ whenever twisted quartic Möbius large sieves have excess $B^{\theta}$ uniformly in twists of conductor up to $X^{1/2-2\theta}$ and in coprimality conditions of norm up to $2X$. Without the last restriction the hypothesis is false. The largest shortfall occurs where rows and columns have norm about $X$, and there the Möbius sieve is the Poisson dual of the carrier energy. De Faveri's quartic large sieve gives $X^{9/4+\varepsilon}$, and GRH gives $X^{2+\varepsilon}$; heuristically, for rows and columns of equal norm and with pointwise control of $1/L$, a zero-density estimate with exponent $\kappa$ gives excess $\max(0,1-2/\kappa)$. In a transcription of Liu's optimisation, labelled a model, every admissible point has exponent at least $9/10$ for the generic excess $1/4$, and beating Liu's $0.87446830769$ needs $\theta<0.18617$; the energy-based Cauchy–Schwarz estimate does not establish the model's direct-side assumption for the one complete quartic observation known. Up to $X=2^{15}$ the carrier energy is within $6\%$ of the random-phase expectation. No zero-free region is proved.
