# Accept

This revision appropriately addresses the qualifications in the previous review. The carrier identity, conditional transfer theorem, optimisation model and numerical evidence are unchanged. The changes refine the interpretation of the existing contribution; they introduce neither a new substantive result nor an unsupported stronger claim. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the carrier energy, its relation to Möbius sieves, and the analytic input still missing from a quartic argument.

The abstract, introductory item 5 and Section 5 now correctly distinguish failure of an estimate to establish (T2) from falsity of (T2). The shell calculation reaches $(CB)^{3/4}$ at the completion scale, consistent with the envelope in [14]. It therefore identifies a limitation of this energy-based Cauchy–Schwarz argument without ruling out signed cancellation or another direct estimate. The threshold $0.18617$ remains explicitly a value within the model. As a minor editorial improvement, the paragraph heading “Evidence against (T2)” could likewise become “Limitation of the energy-based estimate.”

Section 4 now restricts the transfer from [15] to the untwisted family or fixed admissible twists, with constants depending on the twist conductor. This removes the earlier ambiguity about uniformity for growing twists. The stated order of choosing the density loss and then the height cutoff agrees with [15, Theorem 2]. Its Section 7 confirms the decreasing exponent and $E_4(7/8)=2/5>1/4$, which explains why the small-conductor contribution can be absorbed in the relevant range. For complete notational precision, fix the finite mesh in $\sigma$ first and use the minimum of its finitely many positive height powers when choosing the cutoff; a separate symbol for the mesh width would avoid overloading $\varepsilon$.

Separating the conditional density calculation from the published comparisons improves readability. The essential qualifications remain visible: the calculation concerns $A=B$, requires additional pointwise reciprocal-$L$ control, and does not establish the full uniform M4 hypothesis. The revised reference to [15] supports these qualifications. These checks concern the analytic interpretation and cited theorem statements; no new numerical reproduction is claimed.

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and improves its scope and clarity without expanding it into an unfocused collection. It does not prove a new zero-free region or resolve the Riemann hypothesis, and provides no basis for changing the problem's verified-solution record.
