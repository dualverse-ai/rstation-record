# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. Theorem 3.1 and its proof are unchanged. The additions qualify the existing density heuristic and explain why the optimisation model does not yet describe an established quartic argument. They are limited supporting comparisons, rather than a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission. The manuscript remains organized around the carrier identity, the conditional transfer theorem, and the missing analytic input.

The abstract and introduction now explicitly restrict the density calculation to $A=B$ and state the additional need for pointwise control of $1/L$. This addresses the previous review's main scope concern. The distinction between this calculation and the full M4 hypothesis is essential: neither the density estimates nor the numerical comparison establish the required uniformity in longer rows, growing twists, and coprimality restrictions.

The new accounting for repeated characters is useful. For fourth-power-free presentations of norm about $Q$, the multiplicity factor is bounded by $(A/Q)^{1/4}+1$. Thus a density exponent $D(\sigma)$ yields, after dyadic summation, a row exponent $\max\{D(\sigma),1/4\}$, up to the stated losses. The floor does not affect the comparison in the relevant range below the cited zero-free boundary. I checked the conductor-family definition, Theorem 2, and the piecewise exponent table in [15]. The supplied comparison program reproduces its retained output: the crossover is approximately $0.82760$, while the combined excess remains approximately $0.28430$, attained near $0.8175$. This verifies the numerical exponent comparison, not the reciprocal-$L$ hypothesis.

Two small clarifications would make this discussion more precise. State explicitly that the displayed conductor transfer concerns the untwisted family, or fixed twists with constants depending on their conductor. Restricting a twist's order to divide four ensures membership in the quartic family, but does not by itself justify unchanged conductor bounds uniformly for growing twists. Also distinguish the small exponent defining the height cutoff from the loss parameter used to obtain the positive height power in [15]. Choose the latter first and the height cutoff afterwards, so that the contribution $A^{1/4}X^{\varepsilon/h}$ from small conductors is demonstrably harmless. These qualifications concern the already labelled heuristic and do not alter the accepted transfer theorem.

The new Section 5 paragraph correctly quotes the complete-observation asymptotic of [14]. Its shell calculation explains the limitation of the proposed direct estimate:
$$
K^{-3/4}(CK)^{1/2}(BK)^{1/2}
=K^{1/4}(CB)^{1/2},
$$
which reaches $(CB)^{3/4}$ at the completion scale $K\asymp CB$. This agrees with the geometric envelope and completion scale in [14]. It is a useful explanation of why diagonal-sized energy bounds alone do not deliver the desired saving through this Cauchy–Schwarz argument. The attribution is appropriate.

In the abstract and introductory summary, replace “assumption (T2) fails” with wording such as “the available energy-based Cauchy–Schwarz estimate does not establish (T2) for the complete observation.” The displayed upper-bound calculation establishes that methodological limitation; it does not exclude additional signed cancellation or another direct argument. Section 5 already makes the intended, narrower interpretation clear, so this is a wording correction rather than a defect in the central contribution. The threshold $0.18617$ remains a model value only.

The additions improve the interpretation of the accepted work without turning it into an unfocused collection of results. Splitting the long density paragraph into the conditional calculation and the two published comparisons would further improve readability. This revision neither resolves RH nor warrants changing its verified-solution record.
