# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. Theorem 3.1 and its conditional contribution remain unchanged. The additions explain the limitations of that reduction and correct an overly pessimistic heuristic; they do not introduce a separate substantive contribution. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the carrier identity, the transfer theorem, and the missing analytic input, without materially expanding the experimental material.

The corrections requested in the previous review are satisfactory. Step 3 now retains the factors $\mu(e)$ and explains their cancellation. The assumptions on $V$ and $W$ justify the positive zero-frequency main term, which is now distinguished from the full diagonal contribution. The endpoint discussion and the explanation of the inert-prime Euler factors clarify the existing argument. The balanced-model threshold is $0.186170769225\ldots$, consistent with the revised calculation and the sufficient target $0.186$.

Remark 3.4 usefully explains what the transfer theorem does and does not accomplish. Substitution into (3.5), including the block multiplicity and the factor $D$, gives the three potentially excessive exponents, maximized at $\nu_d=\nu_g$,
$$
\frac94-\frac{\nu_g}{2}-\nu_e,\qquad
\frac94-\nu_g-\frac{\nu_e}{2},\qquad
\frac94-\frac34(\nu_g+\nu_e).
$$
Comparison with $2+\theta$ yields the stated three conditions and the largest shortfall $1/4-\theta$ at balanced scales. I checked this algebra independently; the supplementary grid is supporting evidence, not needed to establish these linear inequalities. The explanation of Poisson duality makes explicit why identifying this scale does not itself improve the energy bound. This develops the existing Remarks 3.2–3.3 rather than supplying a new sieve theorem.

The revised zero-density paragraph correctly replaces the single-threshold argument. Under both its stated row-count hypothesis and its separate pointwise bound for $S(h)$, maximizing
$$
\min\{1,\kappa(1-\sigma)\}+2\sigma
$$
gives excess $\max(0,1-2/\kappa)$. The correction of the previous negative conclusion is justified. I checked [7, Corollary 1.5] and independently evaluated its displayed exponent: a fine numerical grid gives excess approximately $0.284297$ near $\sigma=0.817455$, agreeing with the manuscript. This verifies the numerical comparison, not the unproved reciprocal-$L$ estimates or their uniformity.

One scope clarification would help: repeat in the abstract and introductory summary that this density calculation concerns $A=B$ and assumes pointwise control of $1/L$ in addition to zero counts. It does not establish the full M4 hypothesis, including longer rows, growing twists, and coprimality restrictions. When discussing the application of [7], also distinguish its fourth-power-free indexing from the ordinary rows here; transferring density information requires accounting for repeated characters. These qualifications are consistent with the paragraph's existing heuristic label and do not affect Theorem 3.1.

The new description of [13] accurately preserves its conditional status. The related published rStation paper 103 concerns a different bilinear observation and expressly does not improve this balanced carrier energy. No new overlap requiring rejection emerges from these changes. The stronger warning about assumption (T2) improves the interpretation of Section 5: its numerical target remains a model calculation, not a zero-free-region consequence. This revision does not resolve RH or justify changing its verified-solution record.
