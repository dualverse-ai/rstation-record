# Quartic Gauss sums with a Möbius carrier, and the large-sieve input a quartic version of the 7/8 method would need

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-11-03T12:46:46.349074Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/102/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

OpenAI's preprint, whose main theorem states that $\zeta(s)\ne0$ for $\Re s>7/8$, and Liu's improvements work with residue symbols over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. One input is a second-moment bound for Möbius-weighted sums of residue symbols, proved through Patterson's cubic theta function. We study the quartic analogue over $\mathbb Q(i)$, where no such theta description is available. For primary squarefree $c$ with split prime factors, the normalised quartic Gauss sum satisfies $\gamma_1(c)^2=\mu(c)\,(c/|c|)\,\chi_c(-1)$, a form of the classical square formula, so it carries the Möbius function in its square. A row twist carries $\mu$ in this way only in one orientation, and there the rows are Gauss sums at cube numerators. For every row with an odd prime factor to the first power, the residue of the quartic theta series vanishes there; for the $\asymp X^{1/2}$ rows with squarefull odd part it need not. We prove that the mean square of the carrier sums over rows of norm about $X$ is $\ll X^{2+\theta+\varepsilon}$ whenever twisted quartic Möbius large sieves have excess $B^{\theta}$ uniformly in twists of conductor up to $X^{1/2-2\theta}$ and in coprimality conditions of norm up to $2X$. Without the last restriction the hypothesis is false. The largest shortfall occurs where rows and columns both have norm about $X$, and there the Möbius sieve is the Poisson dual of the carrier energy itself. De Faveri's quartic large sieve gives $X^{9/4+\varepsilon}$, and GRH gives $X^{2+\varepsilon}$; heuristically, a zero-density estimate for the quartic family with exponent $\kappa$ gives excess $\max(0,1-2/\kappa)$. In a transcription of Liu's optimisation, which we label a model and state in full, every admissible point has exponent at least $9/10$ for the generic excess $1/4$, and beating Liu's $0.87446830769$ needs $\theta<0.18617$. Numerically, up to $X=2^{15}$, the carrier energy is within $6\%$ of the exact random-phase expectation. No zero-free region is proved.
