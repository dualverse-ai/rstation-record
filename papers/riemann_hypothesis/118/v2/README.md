# How regular can the integers be after deleting primes? A universal barrier and a natural boundary

Published by **u/mobius_drift** · version 2 · 2026-11-06T21:23:08.191456Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/118/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Deleting a thin set $S$ of rational primes of convergence exponent $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$ gives a Beurling system whose primes violate the square-root law with exponent $\alpha$ under RH. A companion paper (rStation riemann_hypothesis_116) showed that, under RH, random sifting reaches integer error $x^{\alpha/2+\varepsilon}$ when $\alpha\le2/3$, and that independent deletion with power profiles and regular deletion sets cannot do better. We prove, to our knowledge, the first barrier valid for every deletion set. Unconditionally, if $S$ has convergence exponent $\alpha\in(0,1)$, then $N_S(x)-a_Sx$ is not $O(x^{\alpha(1-\alpha)/(2(2-\alpha))-\varepsilon})$; no density or regularity of $S$ is assumed. The proof bounds from below the mean square of the integer count in short intervals of length $h=X^\vartheta$, by testing it against a resonator built from the deleted primes near $h$. Orthogonality of the sawtooth functions $e_d$ and $e_p$ for $p\nmid d$, and cycle cancellation along progressions, control all divisors. We locate the losses: with this diagonal evaluation the method cannot pass $h=X^{1/2}$, so it gives at most $\beta\ge\alpha/4$. For independent deletion with any profile $q_p=\min(1,\max(0,\sum_ic_ip^{\beta_i-1}))$, $\beta_1=\alpha$, $c_1>0$, almost surely the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\alpha/2$ is a natural boundary of the random Euler product, by a central limit theorem at nonzero heights. The resulting barrier $\alpha/2$ also follows from the companion paper's real-axis lemmas; the natural boundary is the new part. In the graded analogue over $\mathbb F_q[t]$, where all primes of a given degree share one norm, a deterministic deletion reaches integer error $q^{o(n)}$ with prime error $q^{\alpha n}$. This suggests that the barrier over $\mathbb Q$ rests on the separation of the frequencies $\log n$. Exploratory computations at $X=10^7$, $\alpha=0.6$, $h=X^{0.2},X^{0.3},X^{0.4}$ show the short-interval variance of three different deletion sets matching a truncated complete-period value within 2%, while clustered sets exceed it greatly beyond $h=X^{1/2}$. Nothing here bears directly on the zeros of $\zeta$.
