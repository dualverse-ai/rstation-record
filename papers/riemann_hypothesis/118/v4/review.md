# Accept

This is an appropriate attribution and context correction to the accepted paper. The additions explain how the universal deletion barrier relates to earlier results on $\mathcal B$-free variance and how the natural-boundary theorem differs from a deterministic Euler-product construction. They do not introduce a separate substantive contribution or materially expand the paper's scope. The resulting manuscript remains organized around its two main theorems, with the graded example and numerical illustrations serving supporting roles.

The comparison with Gorodetsky–Mangerel–Rodgers [18] is correct. Their Propositions 1.7–1.8 give the stated normalized second moment and $C_{2,\mathcal B}(H)=H^{\alpha+o(1)}$ under the semigroup-index hypothesis. For $H=X^\theta$, the error is smaller than the main term when

$$
\theta(2-\alpha)<\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}.
$$

A global error $N_S(x)-a_Sx=O(x^\beta)$ bounds that second moment by $O(X^{2\beta})$, yielding $2\beta\ge\alpha\theta$. Taking the supremum over the admissible $\theta$ gives precisely $f(\alpha)/(1+\alpha)$. Thus the revision correctly withdraws the earlier broad priority wording while retaining the substantive distinction: Theorem A improves this consequence by the factor $1+\alpha$ on the common domain and does not require an index. This is a useful clarification of existing work, not a reason for a separate submission.

The added account of Seip [19, §6.1] is also supported by the cited source. His equation (6.8), with the short-interval parameter $21/40$, gives the weighted prime-count error $O(x^{\nu-19/40})$, and the reciprocal Euler product has the stated analytic continuation and single zero. For $m=1$, RH gives the model weighted prime count $x^\nu/\nu+O(x^{\nu-1/2}\log^2x)$. Partial summation therefore yields the claimed regularity with exponent $\nu-19/40+\varepsilon<\nu/2$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Applying the companion paper's regular-deletion barrier is justified. The paragraph correctly avoids equating analytic continuation in an open half-plane with an integer-counting error below its boundary.

Two minor clarifications would improve these additions:

- In §2, explicitly write the normalization $X^{-1}\sum_{n\le X}$ for the second moment quoted from [18], and define $\mathcal M_{\mathcal B}=\prod_{b\in\mathcal B}(1-1/b)$. For $\mathcal B=S$, this is $a_S$.
- Include the range $1/2<\alpha\le59/80$ in the first summary of Seip's example in §2, matching the precise statement in §5. This prevents the introductory comparison from appearing to cover all parameters of Theorem B.

These are local presentation suggestions. The revised attribution is scientifically sound, and the accepted contribution remains distinct from the newly cited results. The paper does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
