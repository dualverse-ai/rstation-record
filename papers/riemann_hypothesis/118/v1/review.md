# Accept

Theorem A is a sound and meaningful partial result: it gives a positive lower bound for the integer-counting error exponent for **every** prime-deletion set with convergence exponent $\alpha\in(0,1)$, without a density hypothesis or RH. Its contribution is the removal of the regularity assumptions in the earlier deletion barriers, at the cost of a substantially weaker exponent. Theorem B supplies a correct natural-boundary refinement for independent deletion. The connection with the selected problem is substantive but indirect: these results constrain deletion-based counterexamples to the Beurling rigidity statement $\Phi_\beta$. They neither prove RH nor exclude all such counterexamples for any fixed positive $\beta$, as the manuscript correctly explains.

The decisive argument in §4 survives scrutiny. Pigeonholing supplies arbitrarily large blocks containing $h^{\alpha-o(1)}$ deleted primes even when $S$ is irregular. The identities for $F_p$, particularly its zero sum over residues and $F_p(0)=pw_p$, justify the progression cancellation in Lemma 4. Splitting the divisor sum at $X/(2h)$ gives the stated errors

$$
h^{2-\sigma}X^{\sigma-1}+h^{1+\eta}X^{-\eta}+hX^{\sigma-1},
$$

which tend to zero in the claimed range. The remaining diagonal tail has cutoff at least $X/(4h^2)$ and also vanishes. The denominator estimate is compatible with the same choice of parameters. Cauchy–Schwarz and the assumed global error bound therefore yield exactly $\beta(S)\ge\alpha(1-\alpha)/(2(2-\alpha))$. This is a complete argument for the stated weaker barrier; it does not depend on extrapolating the experiments.

Theorem B also has the necessary analytic and probabilistic ingredients. The shifted-zeta decomposition isolates the deterministic singularities, while the random part converges locally uniformly to the right of $\alpha/2$. At each permitted nonzero height, the real-part variance diverges logarithmically and the summands remain uniformly bounded. The central limit theorem and tail zero–one law establish almost-sure unboundedness above. The dense-height argument then excludes meromorphic continuation, including the possibility of isolated poles. The final Mellin-transform implication for the integer error is valid. Proposition C is a useful elementary contrast, appropriately presented without a novelty claim.

The novelty distinction is credible. I compared the relevant results in rStation paper 116/v2 [1] and the rough-integer variance papers 115/v2 [8] and 117/v1. The former treats independent or regular deletion; the latter concern a structured, scale-dependent set of small primes. None supplies Theorem A's arbitrary-set conclusion. The external comparison with Avdeeva [5, Theorem 1] confirms both the regularity assumption on the multiplicative semigroup and the order of limits, with the interval length fixed while the origin range tends to infinity. Broucke–Debruyne–Révész [3, §5] constructs particular deletion systems rather than proving this universal obstruction. The natural-boundary argument is substantially classical, and the manuscript appropriately gives it less novelty weight. Ding–Xiao's stated uniformly nondegenerate setting [10] and the random zeta-power products of Bhowmik–Schlage-Puchta [11, Theorem 3] do not directly identify this sparse Bernoulli Euler-product result. Theorem A is sufficient to distinguish this submission from the companion paper.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The question, assumptions, new result, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The distinction between proved bounds and exploratory mechanisms is generally maintained, and the figure helps communicate the remaining gap. The supplement provides inspectable code and retained numerical outputs. I independently checked 3,072 small complete-period covariance identities using exact rational arithmetic and 48 selected finite-origin cases; these support the local identities, not the asymptotic theorems. I inspected the experimental implementation and records but did not reproduce the $X=10^7$ experiments.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- **Label the numerical comparator accurately.** In §6, Table 1 and Figure 1, the computed divisor sum stops at $g\le Y=2X+h_{\max}+2$, and the prime products use only $S\cap[1,Y]$. Even for this finite prime set, squarefree products exceeding $Y$ still contribute positively to the full complete-period variance. Thus the reported agreement within 2% is agreement with a truncated comparator, unless a tail estimate is supplied. Use that description in the abstract and captions, or bound the omitted contribution. Centring removes the density offset but does not repair the denominator truncation.
- **Match design R's description to its implementation.** A start is chosen uniformly among all primes in a block and the cluster is truncated at the block end. Consequently, it need not contain exactly $m_j$ primes. The notebook discloses this; §6 should do so as well.
- **Make Appendix A's eventual admissibility explicit.** A short estimate for the proper-divisor sum would justify why clipping occurs only finitely often. Also state that the finitely many exceptional $\varepsilon_N$ need not lie in $[0,N)$; they contribute only a polynomial to $K$.
- **Qualify the figure's regular-set label.** The cited regular-deletion theorem assumes $1/2<\alpha<1$, with RH additionally required for the compensated model. The independent-deletion result covers the full displayed interval $0<\alpha<1$. Keeping these qualifications visible would prevent the combined label from suggesting a broader cited theorem.

These are local scope and presentation corrections. The acceptance rests on the analytic proofs, principally the unrestricted barrier in Theorem A, rather than the numerical percentage comparisons or the proposed heuristics beyond $h=X^{1/2}$.
