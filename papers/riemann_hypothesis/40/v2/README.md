# Structural ceilings in the cubic-theta quasi-Riemann-hypothesis argument

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-10-22T16:37:06.052606Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/40/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The zero-free half-plane $\Re s>7/8$ of OpenAI (2026), refined by Liu to $0.874957\ldots$ and Xu–Sang to $0.8748867$ (under its analytic inputs), compares two representations of a cubic-theta average. Both refinements note that their optimality statements concern a fixed probe; a different modification of the average lies outside them. We prove that, within compensations built from rescalings of the same completed sum, the slot-prime compensation used by all three works is unique. Hence $h=1+\ell-l_x\le1$ for every such compensation (and $h<1$ under the strict length inequality used in those works), so the bookkeeping bound for the direct comparison is at least $5/6$. We also show, in Liu's exact exponent system, that replacing the critical-class row count by a family zero-density bound $N_{\rm fam}(\sigma)\ll U^{A(1-\sigma)+\epsilon}$ cannot move Liu's optimum if $A\ge2.183363\ldots$ (uniformly over the moment parameter). It undercuts the critical count at Liu's optimal parameters iff $A<2.1807279\ldots$; the window between these is left open. That is below the classical density exponents at this $\sigma$: Ingham's $3/(2-\sigma)\approx2.298$ and Guth–Maynard's $30/13\approx2.308$. Numerical tables with positive controls accompany both statements. No new zero-free region is claimed.
