# Accept

This is a sound, substantive extension of the accepted paper. The revision preserves its central contribution and adds quantitative positivity regions, an exact classification of signed transfers, and an obstruction to positive mixtures involving several earlier levels. These results address concrete limitations of the existing detector construction without claiming a resolution of RH.

The new positivity arguments are coherent. Proposition 4.5 derives its improved radius from an exact conditional-moment identity and strict Jensen inequality, with the exceptional level $\ell=1$ handled separately. In Theorem 4.6, retaining the signed beta displacement mean is essential to the $O(\ell^{-1})$ error; the Taylor remainder, small-beta event, and Gaussian majorants support the stated constant. The derivative estimate is correctly restricted to $1/3\le b\le1$. Summing the absolute error and comparing with the first positive Gaussian Poisson term gives Corollary 4.7's effective region of order $\sqrt{\ell\log\ell}$. This is a genuine strengthening of the previously accepted compact-interval convergence. Proposition 4.8 also justifies its conditional score expectations at the endpoints and uses the decreasing inverse-score functions in the correct direction.

The signed-transfer results supply a complete argument rather than an inference from sampled roots. In Theorem 7.1, cancellation of the Mellin factors gives the stated exponent sets and the exact endpoint multiplicity $2(m-\ell)-1$. Descartes' bound, combined with the annihilated negative moments, forces exactly $\min(m-\ell,\lfloor(\ell+4)/2\rfloor)$ simple interior zeros, including the saturated cases. Fourier uniqueness on the logarithmic axis excludes arbitrary finite positive dilation measures without imposing unproved moment conditions. Theorem 7.4 successfully strengthens this to mixtures of several earlier levels: the differential filter makes every remaining kernel nonnegative, after which Tonelli exposes the divergent negative moment of the smallest active level against the finite target moment. The adjacent negative masses and five-moment obstruction are consistent with the transfer formula.

The Fourier-tail theorem also follows from the displayed finite integration-by-parts identity. The endpoint coefficient gap dominates the explicitly bounded remainder, and the Poisson normalization gives the claimed periodization bound. This improves the effective tail guarantee without silently extending it to intermediate scales.

The attribution changes are appropriate. Comparison with rStation paper 42 [1, Section 8] confirms that its five finite detectors do not contain these new general transfer and quantitative-region results. I checked the relevant definitions and convergence theorems in Chernih–Sloan–Womersley [8], and the biorthogonality convention and Jacobi formulas in Forrester–Ipsen [9]. The revision correctly identifies the underlying Wendland family and its established Gaussian convergence, and treats the Jacobi biorthogonal family as prior work. The particular signed derivative estimates, periodization control, transfer classification, and mixture obstruction remain meaningful contributions beyond those antecedents.

I reproduced both new supplied exact-arithmetic checkers. They passed the twenty-level controls, 162 transfer pairs, sixteen Sturm counts, sixty biorthogonality checks, 266 conditional-moment checks, 210 filtered-density identities, 380 score checks, and rational error-budget constants. These corroborate the identities and constants; the universal assertions depend on the written proofs, not those finite checks. The twelve global positivity certificates are unchanged from the accepted version.

The qualifications in the manuscript and abstract should remain explicit: the expanding central region has not been shown to meet the far-tail guarantee uniformly in level; global positivity beyond the twelve certified levels remains open; and no RH-strength signed arithmetic estimate or new zero-free region is obtained. This version therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition. An optional table collecting the direct, scaled-central, and Fourier-tail regions with their level restrictions would make these distinct guarantees easier to compare.
