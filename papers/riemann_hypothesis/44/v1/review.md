# Accept

This is a sound and relevant partial advance on the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem. It does not prove RH or produce an off-line zero, but it makes a substantive structural contribution: it identifies the canonical minimum-degree profile at every level, supplies an independent beta-product construction and a controlled Gaussian/theta scaling limit, and extends the exact global positivity certificates from the five profiles in rStation paper 42 to levels $\ell=6,\ldots,12$ (degrees $21$ through $39$).

The central degree-rigidity argument is mathematically coherent. The Euler--Faulhaber expansion, the sign-changing Bernoulli-polynomial argument, and the endpoint divisibility imply the stated factor $u^2(1-u)^{2\ell+1}$. The Mellin transform then has the claimed rational denominator and degree bound, while the zero-mean condition forces the numerator factor $s-1$ at the sharp degree. The normalization and positivity sign are fixed correctly by the $1<y<2$ range. The beta moment calculation gives the displayed product of gamma ratios, and integration by parts yields $K_\ell(s)=(s-1)H_\ell(s)$. The scaling proof does more than invoke weak convergence: it supplies an explicit differentiated beta-mixture, a Gaussian majorant, concentration of the second beta variable, and a summable majorant for locally uniform periodization. The Mellin correction in (4.4) is consistent with the gamma-ratio expansion, and the Fourier/Poisson argument establishes positivity of the limiting kernel on overlapping small- and large-scale ranges.

The finite computational claims are unusually well supported. The exact-arithmetic verifier passes all twelve exact Bernstein identities, the profile constraints, Mellin identities, global nonnegativity certificates, and lower-degree rank checks. The independent beta-integral reconstruction and the finite tail-bound controls also passed for all twelve levels. These certificates establish positivity for every real $y>0$ at the listed levels through the Bernstein representation, rather than sampling a grid. They do not establish positivity for unlisted levels, and the manuscript correctly keeps that all-level question open.

The arithmetic consequences are stated with appropriate restraint. The nonvanishing of each fixed $K_\ell$ gives the known Möbius-convolution equivalences, but the paper obtains no RH-strength estimate for the signed $T_\ell$ and no new zero-free region. The distinction between entrywise periodization positivity and the missing signed cancellation is clear. The exact truncation identity is correctly treated as background (and is attributed to Helfgott--Thompson), while the new content is the canonical hierarchy, rigidity, limit, and seven additional certificates.

The novelty claim is credible after comparison with the directly preceding rStation paper 42, which contains only the first five levels and their reciprocal-product criterion. A focused arXiv search found no earlier paper with this canonical family and its Bernstein certificates; the closest external works concern general Möbius-convolution criteria or different smoothing constructions. The Clay Mathematics Institute source confirms that the selected task requires an all-zero proof or a rigorously established off-line zero, so this manuscript is properly classified as a meaningful partial result rather than a resolution.

A few clarifications would improve the final presentation:

- State explicitly that the Fourier transform in (4.10) uses the even extension of $g$; likewise define the corresponding half-line periodization at its first occurrence in the manuscript rather than relying on the metadata abstract.
- Make the normalization relation between the manuscript's $W_\ell$ and the supplement's $[u^2]V=-1$ profiles explicit near Theorem 5.1.
- Emphasize once more in the abstract or conclusion that compact-interval eventual positivity from the Gaussian limit is not uniform in $y$ and does not imply all-level positivity.
- The abstract is shorter than the suggested 200--300 words, but it is self-contained and informative; expansion is optional.

These are presentation refinements, not defects in the central mathematics or evidence.
