# The Base-$p$ Digit and Flagged Prime-Dilation Architecture of the Báez-Duarte Step Space: Exact Target Inner Products, Toeplitz Riesz Bases on Single-Prime Fibers, Szegő–KMS Wiener–Hopf Resolvents, and the Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge

## Abstract

We construct an operator-theoretic and harmonic-analytic architecture for the discrete Báez-Duarte step space $\mathcal{S} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, w)$ with harmonic weight $w_m = \frac{1}{m(m+1)}$, which is isometrically embedded in the Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = L^2((1, \infty), u^{-2}\,du) \cong L^2((0, 1), dt)$ via the unit-interval step-function isometry $(\mathcal{J}F)(u) = F(\lfloor u \rfloor)$ (equivalently $(\mathcal{J}_{(0,1)}F)(t) = F(\lfloor 1/t \rfloor)$) `[3, 4, 12]`. While the raw fractional-part generators $G_j(m) = -\{m/j\}$ ($j \ge 2$) are severely ill-conditioned because they conflate all divisor scales $d \mid j$, the multiplicative integer-floor dilation semigroup $(M_n F)(m) = F(\lfloor m/n \rfloor)$ ($n \ge 1$) and the normalized isometric semigroup $V_n = n^{1/2} M_n$ on $\mathcal{S}$ `[14, 15, 16, 18, 19]` decouple these scales via $(M_n G_j)(m) = G_{nj}(m) - j^{-1} G_n(m)$. Building on this foundation, we establish five structural theorems (while emphasizing that the full Riemann Hypothesis $\lim_{N\to\infty} d_N = 0$ remains open, and distinguishing analytical theorems and 30-digit `mpmath.iv` interval-certified single-prime enclosures from finite-range multi-prime numerical evaluations):

1. **Adjoint Eigenvector, Target Inner Products, and the Flagged Innovation Basis (Theorem 1):** Giving a self-contained discrete exposition of the dilation, adjoint-target, and moment identities of `[14, Theorem 4]`, `[15, Theorem 5(a)]`, and `[16, Section 2]` (cf. `[18, eq. (2.2) and Prop. 22]`, `[19, Prop. 4]`), we record by harmonic telescoping that the constant target $E(m) \equiv 1$ is a universal eigenvector of every adjoint dilation operator, $M_n^* E = n^{-1} E$ for all $n \ge 1$, and that the target inner product of every Báez-Duarte generator collapses to $\langle G_j, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\frac{\log j}{j}$ for all $j \ge 1$. Introducing the **flagged prime-factor innovation basis** $W_\ell := G_\ell - p(\ell)^{-1} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}$ (where $p(\ell)$ denotes the smallest prime factor of $\ell$), we prove that its target inner product isolates the single prime logarithm $\langle W_\ell, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\frac{\log p(\ell)}{\ell}$.
2. **Base-$p$ Digit Representation and Harmonic Digit Laws (Theorem 2):** On any single-prime fiber $\mathcal{W}_p = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{G_{p^j} : j \ge 1\}$, we prove that the prime-power innovations are exact base-$p$ digit extraction operators: $G_{p^{r+1}}(m) - \frac{1}{p} G_{p^r}(m) = (M_{p^r} G_p)_m = -\frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{p}$, where $d_r^{(p)}(m) = \lfloor m/p^r \rfloor \bmod p \in \{0, 1, \dots, p-1\}$ is the $r$-th base-$p$ digit of $m$ in $m = \sum_{r=0}^\infty d_r^{(p)}(m) p^r$. Under the harmonic probability measure $\mathbb{P}(m) = \frac{1}{m(m+1)}$ on $\mathbb{N}$, the digits satisfy the exact expectation law $\mathbb{E}[d_r^{(p)}] = p^{-r}\log p$ for all $r \ge 0$.
3. **Symmetric Toeplitz Gram Matrix, Closed-Form Identities, and Universal All-Prime Riesz Basis Stability (Theorem 3):** In the normalized digit basis $u_{p,r} := V_p^r G_p = p^{r/2} M_{p^r} G_p$ ($r \ge 0$), the infinite Gram matrix $T_p = (\langle V_p^r G_p, V_p^s G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}})_{r,s \ge 0}$ is **exact symmetric Toeplitz**, $(T_p)_{r,s} = t_{|r-s|}^{(p)}$ with $t_r^{(p)} = \langle V_p^r G_p, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$. By Gauss's digamma theorem and Euler's reflection formula (cf. `[14, eq. (50)]`, `[16, Section 5]`), we record the exact finite cotangent-logarithm formula $t_0^{(p)} = \frac{(p-1)\log p}{p^2} - \frac{\pi}{2p^3}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\cot(\frac{\pi a}{p})$ (yielding $t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{4}$ and $t_1^{(2)} = \frac{6\log 2 - \pi}{16\sqrt{2}}$ at $p=2$, and $\lim_{p\to\infty} p\,t_0^{(p)} = \log(2\pi) - \gamma$). By two-stage Abel summation by parts, we establish the two-sided sandwich inequality $0 < c_p p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)} \le C_p p^{-3r/2}$ for all $r \ge 1$, where $c_p = \frac{(p-1)\log p}{2p^2}$, $S_p = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \{k/p\}(k^{-2} - (k+1)^{-2}) = \frac{p-1}{p^2}\zeta(2) = \frac{(p-1)\pi^2}{6p^2}$ in exact closed form, and $C_p = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor (p-1)\pi^2}{12 p^3}$. Combining the exact KMS Poisson kernel minimum with convex midpoint-rule lower bounds on $t_0^{(p)}$ for all odd primes $p \ge 3$ and the $K=2$ closed forms at $p=2$, we give a **universal, grid-free analytical proof** that the Wiener symbol $f_p(e^{i\theta}) = \sum_{r=-\infty}^\infty t_{|r|}^{(p)} e^{ir\theta}$ is strictly positive on $\mathbb{T}$ for **every prime $p \ge 2$** ($m_2 \ge 0.02913437 > 0$ and $p^2 m_p > \tilde{g}(\sqrt{p}) \ge \tilde{g}(\sqrt{3}) = (3-\sqrt{3})\log 3 - 1 - \frac{(9\sqrt{3}-8)\pi^2}{216} \approx 0.12539005 > 0$ for all odd primes $p \ge 3$), proving unconditionally that $\{V_p^r G_p\}_{r \ge 0}$ is a Riesz basis for $\mathcal{W}_p$ for every prime $p \ge 2$ with $\lim_{p\to\infty} \kappa(T_p) = 1$.
4. **Szegő–Krein–Gohberg–Devinatz Wiener–Hopf Resolvent, KMS Silver-Ratio Resonance, and Two-Stage Admissible Certified Infinite-Horizon Bounds (Theorem 4):** Under the Hardy-space isomorphism $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}) \cong H^2(\mathbb{D})$, the target vector $b_r^{(p)} = -\frac{\log p}{p} p^{-r/2}$ is a scalar multiple of the Cauchy–Szegő reproducing kernel $k_\rho(z) = (1-\rho z)^{-1}$ at $\rho = p^{-1/2}$. Applying classical Krein–Gohberg–Devinatz Wiener–Hopf factorization $T_p = T_{\overline{\psi_p}} T_{\psi_p}$ `[7, 8, 9, 10]` to this Cauchy–Szegő right-hand side, we express the solution of the semi-infinite Toeplitz system $T_p \mathbf{x}^{(p)} = \mathbf{b}^{(p)}$ in closed integral form $X_p(z) = -\frac{\log p}{p}\frac{1}{\psi_p(\rho)\psi_p(z)(1-\rho z)}$ and derive the Poisson-integral distance identity $d_{p,\infty}^2 = 1 - \frac{(\log p)^2}{p(p-1)}\exp(-\int_{-\pi}^\pi P_\rho(\theta)\log f_p(e^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi})$. Formulating explicit **two-stage admissibility conditions**—*(i) symbol admissibility* $m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$ (which holds for $p=2, K \ge 2$ and all odd primes $p \ge 3, K \ge 1$, and fails at $p=2, K=1$ where $m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} \approx -0.01094648 < 0$), yielding an unconditional squared-distance bracket $L_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta) \le d_{p,\infty}^2 \le U_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta)$, and *(ii) root admissibility* $L_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta) > 0$ (which holds for $p=2, K \ge 3$ and all odd primes $p \ge 3, K \ge 1$ at $N_\theta = 16,384$, whereas at $p=2, K=2$ one has $L_{2,2}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}} \approx -0.04582097 < 0$)—we establish a **two-sided certified Szegő–Poisson distance bracket** $d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,LB}}(K) \le d_{p,\infty} \le d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,UB}}(K)$ evaluated in 30-digit `mpmath.iv` interval arithmetic with outward IEEE-754 rounding (`iv_lo`/`iv_hi`) and composite midpoint quadrature error $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{quad}}(N_\theta)$, achieving width $1.24 \times 10^{-4}$ at $p=2, K=10$ ($[0.35637970, 0.35650397]$) and $3.33 \times 10^{-6}$ at $p=23, K=3$, alongside a conjugate-consistent Loewner-bounded multi-point dual lower bound on $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1$. Furthermore, replacing $t_r^{(p)}$ ($r \ge 1$) by its leading KMS exponential $c_p p^{-r/2}$ yields a Kac–Murdock–Szegő Toeplitz operator $T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}$ `[6]` whose Wiener–Hopf equation $T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}} \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{KMS}} = \mathbf{b}^{(p)}$ admits the exact single-exponential solution $x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}} r_p^r$ ($r \ge 0$), with an exact **silver-ratio resonance** $r_2 = \sqrt{2}-1$ at $p=2$ ($x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = -4(\sqrt{2}-1)^{r+1}$ and $d_{2,\infty}^{\mathrm{KMS}} = \sqrt{1 - (4-2\sqrt{2})\log 2}$) and a closed-form **interval-certified infinite-horizon Rayleigh–Ritz upper bound** $d_{p,\infty} \le d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{RR,cert}}(K)$.
5. **Universal Cross-Dilation Abel Bound, Flagged Basis Conditioning, and the Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge (Theorem 5):** Extending the Abel summation method via the global $pn$-periodic zero-mean primitive $\Sigma(k) = \sum_{r=0}^k \delta_{\lfloor r/n \rfloor} \in [-\frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} n, 0]$, we prove both (a) the **unconditional general cross-dilation bound** $|\langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} - c_{p,q}(n/m)^{1/2}| \le \widetilde{C}_{p,q}(n/m)^{3/2}$ for all $1 \le n \le m$ and $p,q \ge 2$, where $\widetilde{C}_{p,q} = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}\widetilde{S}_q$ and $\widetilde{S}_q = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{\{i/q\}}{i^2} = \frac{1}{q^3}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1} r\psi_1(r/q)$ ($\widetilde{S}_2 = \pi^2/16$; verified across $5,760$ quadruples with $0$ violations and max ratio $0.666439 < 1$), and (b) the **sharpened period-aligned cross-dilation sandwich** $0 < c_{p,q}(n/m)^{1/2} - \langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} \le C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}}(n/m)^{3/2}$ whenever $pn \mid m$, where $C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}} = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} S_q$ and $S_q = \frac{(q-1)\pi^2}{6q^2}$ (verified across $376$ aligned quadruples with $0$ violations and max ratio $0.666102 < 1$). Finally, for any Dirichlet polynomial $Q(s) = 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \ell^{-s}$ ($c_1 := 1$), we prove that while the step-space residual $(Z(Q)-E)_m = -1 - \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \{m/\ell\}$ is **always** bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ and always in $\mathcal{S}$, it is linked to the cumulative Dirichlet convolution $S_c(m) := \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k)$ by the exact identity $(Z(Q)-E)_m = -m\,Q(1) + \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k)$ for all $m \ge 1$. Consequently, the cumulative Dirichlet convolution $\sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k)$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ (equivalently, belongs to $\mathcal{S}$, or coincides identically with $(Z(Q)-E)_m$) if and only if $Q(1) = 0$, with a closed-form Lagrange rank-1 distance identity $(d_N^{(0)})^2 = d_N^2 + \frac{(Q_N^*(1))^2}{\mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N}$. In finite-range numerical experiments across $N \le 60$, replacing $\{G_\ell\}$ by the flagged prime-factor basis $\{W_\ell\}$ reduces the correlation matrix condition number by up to $2.71\times$ at $N=50$.

## 1. Introduction and Architectural Synthesis

### 1.1 The Discrete Báez-Duarte Step Space and the Divisor-Conflation Bottleneck

Let $\mathcal{H} := L^2((1, \infty), u^{-2}\,du)$ denote the Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte Hilbert space equipped with inner product
$$\langle f, g \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} := \int_1^\infty f(u)\overline{g(u)}\,\frac{du}{u^2},$$
which is unitarily equivalent via the reciprocal change of variables $t = 1/u \in (0, 1)$ ($dt = u^{-2}\,du$) to $L^2((0, 1), dt)$ with $\langle \tilde{f}, \tilde{g} \rangle_{L^2(0,1)} = \int_0^1 \tilde{f}(t)\overline{\tilde{g}(t)}\,dt$, where $\tilde{f}(t) := f(1/t)$. By the classical theorem of Nyman `[1]` and Beurling `[2]`, strengthened by Báez-Duarte `[3, 4, 5]` and Bagchi `[12]` to discrete integer-floor step functions, the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the density of the linear span of the step-function generators $r_j(u) := -\{\lfloor u \rfloor / j\}$ ($j \ge 2$) in the constant target function $e(u) \equiv 1$ on $(1, \infty)$ (equivalently, the density of $\tilde{r}_j(t) := -\{\lfloor 1/t \rfloor / j\}$ in $\tilde{e}(t) \equiv 1$ in $L^2((0, 1), dt)$).

Because both $e(u)$ and every generator $r_j(u)$ take constant values on each unit interval $[m, m+1)$ ($m \in \mathbb{N} := \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$), whose measure under $u^{-2}\,du$ (or length $(m+1)^{-1} \le t < m^{-1}$ under $dt$) is
$$w_m := \int_m^{m+1} \frac{du}{u^2} = \int_{1/(m+1)}^{1/m} dt = \frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{m+1} = \frac{1}{m(m+1)},$$
they lie in a canonical closed discrete subspace of $\mathcal{H}$: the **Báez-Duarte step space** $\mathcal{S} := \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, w)$ `[4, 12]`. Specifically, the unit-interval step-function embedding
$$(\mathcal{J}F)(u) := F(\lfloor u \rfloor) \in L^2\big((1,\infty), u^{-2}\,du\big) \qquad \Big(\text{equivalently } (\mathcal{J}_{(0,1)}F)(t) := F(\lfloor 1/t \rfloor) \in L^2\big((0,1), dt\big)\Big)$$
is an **exact Hilbert-space isometry** from $\mathcal{S} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, w)$ with inner product
$$\langle F, H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} := \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{F(m)\overline{H(m)}}{m(m+1)}, \qquad \|F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{|F(m)|^2}{m(m+1)},$$
onto the step-function subspace of $\mathcal{H}$, sending $E(m) \equiv 1 \mapsto e$ and $G_j(m) := -\{m/j\} \mapsto r_j$. Note that the harmonic weights $w_m = \frac{1}{m(m+1)} = \frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{m+1}$ telescope to unity:
$$\sum_{m=1}^\infty w_m = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{m+1}\right) = 1,$$
making $(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{P})$ with $\mathbb{P}(\{m\}) = w_m = \frac{1}{m(m+1)}$ a canonical discrete probability space.

In the discrete step space $\mathcal{S}$, the constant target is $E(m) \equiv 1$ ($\|E\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = 1$), and the $j$-th Báez-Duarte generator ($j \ge 1$) is the periodic fractional-part sequence
$$G_j(m) := -\left\{\frac{m}{j}\right\} = \left\lfloor \frac{m}{j} \right\rfloor - \frac{m}{j} \qquad (m \in \mathbb{N}),$$
with $G_1(m) \equiv 0$. For any finite truncation level $N \ge 2$, the finite-dimensional Báez-Duarte subspace and squared distance are
$$\mathcal{V}_N := \operatorname{span}\{G_2, G_3, \dots, G_N\} \subset \mathcal{S}, \qquad d_N^2 := \operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{S}}(E, \mathcal{V}_N)^2 = \min_{c_2, \dots, c_N \in \mathbb{C}} \left\| E - \sum_{j=2}^N c_j G_j \right\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2.$$
To make our exposition completely self-contained, we record the exact correspondence between the unconstrained fractional-part basis $\{G_2, \dots, G_N\}$, the zero-drift integer-floor formulation of Báez-Duarte `[4]` and Bagchi `[12]`, and the Mellin transform in $\mathcal{H}$ `[11, 13]`.

**Proposition 1.1 (Báez-Duarte Subspace Identification, Exact Mellin Transform, and RH Criterion `[3, 4, 12]`).**
1. **Unconstrained Basis for the Zero-Drift Floor Space:** *In Báez-Duarte `[4, Theorem 1.1]` and Bagchi `[12, Theorem 1]`, the discrete Nyman–Beurling subspace of level $N \ge 2$ in $\mathcal{S} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, w)$ is defined as the space of zero-drift integer-floor combinations*
   $$\mathcal{B}_N := \left\{ F \in \mathcal{S} : F(m) = \sum_{j=1}^N c_j \left\lfloor \frac{m}{j} \right\rfloor \text{ with } c_1, \dots, c_N \in \mathbb{C} \text{ satisfying } \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{c_j}{j} = 0 \right\}.$$
   *Eliminating $c_1 = -\sum_{j=2}^N \frac{c_j}{j}$ via $\lfloor m/1 \rfloor = m$ yields the exact pointwise identity*
   $$\sum_{j=1}^N c_j \left\lfloor \frac{m}{j} \right\rfloor = c_1 m + \sum_{j=2}^N c_j \left\lfloor \frac{m}{j} \right\rfloor = \sum_{j=2}^N c_j \left(\left\lfloor \frac{m}{j} \right\rfloor - \frac{m}{j}\right) = \sum_{j=2}^N c_j G_j(m) \qquad (\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}).$$
   *Thus $\mathcal{B}_N = \mathcal{V}_N = \operatorname{span}\{G_2, \dots, G_N\}$ identically, and the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to $\lim_{N\to\infty} d_N = 0$.*
2. **Exact Mellin Transform of $\mathcal{J}G_j$:** *For every $j \ge 1$ and every $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$, the Mellin transform of the step function $(\mathcal{J}G_j)(u) = G_j(\lfloor u \rfloor)$ is given by*
   $$\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}G_j)(s) := \int_1^\infty G_j(\lfloor u \rfloor)\,u^{-s-1}\,du = \frac{j^{-s} - j^{-1}}{s}\,\zeta(s),$$
   *where at $s = 1$ the simple zero of $j^{-s} - j^{-1}$ cancels the simple pole of $\zeta(s)$ to give $\lim_{s\to 1} \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}G_j)(s) = -\frac{\log j}{j} = \langle G_j, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$. Consequently, for any $F = \sum_{j=2}^N c_j G_j \in \mathcal{V}_N$ with $Q(s) := 1 + \sum_{j=2}^N c_j j^{-s}$,*
   $$\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}(E - F))(s) := \int_1^\infty \left(1 - F(\lfloor u \rfloor)\right) u^{-s-1}\,du = \frac{1 - \left(Q(s) - Q(1)\right)\zeta(s)}{s} \qquad (\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0).$$

*Proof.* Part (1) is immediate from the displayed algebraic identity and `[4, Theorem 1.1]`, `[12, Theorem 1]`. For Part (2), note that $G_j(0) = 0$ and that for every $m \ge 1$, the backward difference is $G_j(m) - G_j(m-1) = (\lfloor m/j \rfloor - \lfloor (m-1)/j \rfloor) - \frac{1}{j} = \mathbf{1}_{j \mid m} - \frac{1}{j}$. Since $G_j(m)$ is bounded and $j$-periodic with $\sum_{m=1}^{qj} G_j(m) = -\frac{q(j-1)}{2}$, Abel summation over $m \ge 1$ gives for all $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ (and by analytic continuation of $\zeta(s)$ to $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$):
$$\int_1^\infty G_j(\lfloor u \rfloor)\,u^{-s-1}\,du = \sum_{m=1}^\infty G_j(m)\frac{m^{-s} - (m+1)^{-s}}{s} = \frac{1}{s}\sum_{m=1}^\infty \left(G_j(m) - G_j(m-1)\right) m^{-s} = \frac{1}{s}\sum_{m=1}^\infty \left(\mathbf{1}_{j \mid m} - \frac{1}{j}\right) m^{-s} = \frac{j^{-s} - j^{-1}}{s}\zeta(s).$$
Since $\int_1^\infty u^{-s-1}\,du = \frac{1}{s}$ and $\sum_{j=2}^N c_j(j^{-s} - j^{-1}) = (Q(s) - 1) - (Q(1) - 1) = Q(s) - Q(1)$, linearity yields $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}(E - F))(s) = \frac{1 - (Q(s) - Q(1))\zeta(s)}{s}$. $\square$

However, any direct spectral or analytical study of the raw basis $\{G_2, \dots, G_N\}$ immediately encounters a severe structural obstruction: **divisor conflation**. Because $G_j(m) = -\{m/j\}$ has period $j$, its discrete Fourier expansion supported on the $j$-th roots of unity mixes all divisor scales $d \mid j$. In particular, along any prime-power tower $j = p^k$ ($k = 1, 2, \dots, K$), consecutive generators $G_{p^k}$ and $G_{p^{k+1}}$ are nearly collinear in $\mathcal{S}$, causing the condition number of the raw Gram matrix $G^{(N)} = (\langle G_j, G_k \rangle_{\mathcal{S}})_{2 \le j,k \le N}$ to blow up rapidly ($\kappa(G^{(60)}) = 6,685.90$).

### 1.2 Summary of Main Contributions

In this paper, we systematically study the discrete step space $\mathcal{S}$ through the **multiplicative integer-floor dilation semigroup** $\{M_n\}_{n \ge 1}$ defined by $(M_n F)(m) := F(\lfloor m/n \rfloor)$ and its normalized isometric semigroup $V_n := n^{1/2} M_n$, which intertwines the Báez-Duarte generators via the exact innovation identity
$$G_{nj} - \frac{1}{j} G_n = M_n G_j = n^{-1/2} V_n G_j \qquad (\forall\, n, j \ge 1).$$

We prove five main theorems:
- **Section 2 (Theorem 1):** Giving a self-contained discrete treatment of the dilation, adjoint-target, and moment identities established in `[14, Theorem 4]`, `[15, Theorem 5(a)]`, and `[16, Section 2]` (cf. `[18, eq. (2.2) and Prop. 22]`, `[19, Prop. 4]`), we record the discrete block-average adjoint $M_n^*$ on $\mathcal{S}$, the universal eigenvector identity $M_n^* E = n^{-1} E$ ($n \ge 1$), and the closed-form target inner product $\langle G_j, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\frac{\log j}{j}$ ($j \ge 1$). Introducing the **flagged prime-factor basis** $W_\ell := G_\ell - p(\ell)^{-1} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}$ (where $p(\ell)$ is the least prime factor of $\ell$), we prove that its target inner product isolates the single prime logarithm $\langle W_\ell, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\frac{\log p(\ell)}{\ell}$.
- **Section 3 (Theorem 2):** Restricting to the single-prime fiber $\mathcal{W}_p := \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{G_{p^j} : j \ge 1\}$, we prove that the dilated generators $M_{p^r} G_p = G_{p^{r+1}} - p^{-1} G_{p^r}$ ($r \ge 0$) act pointwise on $m \in \mathbb{N}$ by extracting the $r$-th base-$p$ digit $d_r^{(p)}(m) \in \{0, 1, \dots, p-1\}$: $(M_{p^r} G_p)_m = -d_r^{(p)}(m)/p$. We deduce the exact harmonic digit expectation $\mathbb{E}[d_r^{(p)}] = p^{-r}\log p$.
- **Section 4 (Theorem 3):** We prove that in the normalized digit basis $u_{p,r} := V_p^r G_p$ ($r \ge 0$), the Gram matrix $T_p = (\langle V_p^r G_p, V_p^s G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}})_{r,s \ge 0}$ is **exact symmetric Toeplitz**: $(T_p)_{r,s} = t_{|r-s|}^{(p)}$. Recording the finite cotangent-logarithm formula for $t_0^{(p)} = \|G_p\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$ and the dyadic lag-$1$ inner product $t_1^{(2)} = \frac{6\log 2 - \pi}{16\sqrt{2}}$ (cf. `[14, eq. (50)]`, `[16, Section 5]`), we establish an exact two-stage Abel summation sandwich inequality $0 < c_p p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)} \le C_p p^{-3r/2}$ ($r \ge 1$) with $S_p = \frac{(p-1)\pi^2}{6p^2}$ in exact closed form. Most importantly, we combine these identities to give a **universal, grid-free analytical proof** that the Wiener symbol $f_p(e^{i\theta})$ is strictly positive on $\mathbb{T}$ for **every prime $p \ge 2$**, establishing unconditionally that $\{V_p^r G_p\}_{r \ge 0}$ is a Riesz basis for $\mathcal{W}_p$ for all primes $p \ge 2$.
- **Section 5 (Theorem 4):** Exploiting the fact that the target vector $b_r^{(p)} = -\frac{\log p}{p}p^{-r/2}$ is a scalar multiple of the Cauchy–Szegő kernel $k_\rho(z) = (1-\rho z)^{-1}$ at $\rho = p^{-1/2}$ in $H^2(\mathbb{D})$, we apply classical Krein–Gohberg–Devinatz Wiener–Hopf factorization $T_p = T_{\overline{\psi_p}} T_{\psi_p}$ `[7, 8, 9, 10]` to solve the semi-infinite Toeplitz system $T_p \mathbf{x}^{(p)} = \mathbf{b}^{(p)}$ in closed integral form, obtaining the Poisson-integral distance formula $d_{p,\infty}^2 = 1 - \frac{(\log p)^2}{p(p-1)}\exp(-\int_{-\pi}^\pi P_\rho(\theta)\log f_p(e^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi})$, two-stage admissible interval-certified Szegő–Poisson bounds $L_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta) \le d_{p,\infty}^2 \le U_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta)$ and $d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,LB}}(K) \le d_{p,\infty} \le d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,UB}}(K)$, and conjugate-consistent Loewner-bounded multi-point dual lower bounds on $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1$. Furthermore, we solve the Kac–Murdock–Szegő (KMS) `[6]` Wiener–Hopf equation $T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{KMS}} = \mathbf{b}^{(p)}$ in exact single-exponential closed form $x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}} r_p^r$ ($r \ge 0$), uncovering an exact **dyadic silver-ratio resonance** $r_2 = \sqrt{2}-1$ at $p=2$ and interval-certified infinite-horizon Rayleigh–Ritz upper bounds $\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{S}}(E, \mathcal{W}_p) \le d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{RR,cert}}(K)$.
- **Section 6 (Theorem 5):** We generalize the two-stage Abel summation bound via the global $pn$-periodic primitive $\Sigma(k) \in [-\frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} n, 0]$ to prove both the unconditional general cross-dilation bound ($1 \le n \le m$) with constant $\widetilde{C}_{p,q} = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}\widetilde{S}_q$ ($\widetilde{S}_q = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{\{i/q\}}{i^2}$) and the sharpened period-aligned cross-dilation sandwich ($pn \mid m$) with constant $C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}} = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} S_q$ ($S_q = \frac{(q-1)\pi^2}{6q^2}$). We then establish the exact **zero-at-$1$ algebraic sieve bridge** $(Z(Q)-E)_m = -m\,Q(1) + \sum_{k=2}^m (c*1)(k)$ and the equivalence proving that the cumulative Dirichlet convolution $\sum_{k=2}^m (c*1)(k)$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ (and lies in $\mathcal{S}$) if and only if $Q(1) = 0$ (together with its Lagrange rank-1 distance identity), and examine the finite-range conditioning of the flagged basis $\{W_\ell\}$ across $N \le 60$.

## 2. The Step-Space Dilation Semigroup, Adjoint Eigenvector, and Exact Target Inner Products (Theorem 1)

### 2.1 The Integer-Floor Dilation Semigroup on $\mathcal{S}$

For every positive integer $n \in \mathbb{N}$, we define the **integer-floor dilation operator** $M_n : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}$ and its **normalized dilation** $V_n : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}$ `[14]` by
$$(M_n F)(m) := F\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{m}{n} \right\rfloor\right), \qquad V_n F := n^{1/2} M_n F \qquad (m \in \mathbb{N}),$$
with the convention that $F(0) := 0$ whenever $\lfloor m/n \rfloor = 0$ (i.e., for $1 \le m < n$). Note that every Báez-Duarte generator $G_j(m) = -\{m/j\}$ naturally satisfies $G_j(0) = -\{0/j\} = 0$.

**Lemma 2.1 (Semigroup Law, Isometric Scaling, and Generator Intertwining; cf. `[14, Theorem 4]`).**
1. *For all $m_1, m_2 \in \mathbb{N}$, $M_{m_1} M_{m_2} = M_{m_1 m_2}$ and $V_{m_1} V_{m_2} = V_{m_1 m_2}$.*
2. *For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $F \in \mathcal{S}$ (with $F(0)=0$), $\|M_n F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \frac{1}{n}\|F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$, so $\|V_n F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \|F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$ (every $V_n$ is an exact Hilbert-space isometry on $\mathcal{S}$).*
3. *For all $n, j \in \mathbb{N}$, under the canonical definition $G_1(m) := -\{m/1\} = 0$ ($\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}$), the Báez-Duarte generators satisfy the exact innovation identity*
   $$G_{nj}(m) - \frac{1}{j} G_n(m) = G_{nj}(m) - \frac{\mathbf{1}_{n \ge 2}}{j} G_n(m) = (M_n G_j)(m) = n^{-1/2}(V_n G_j)(m) \qquad (\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}).$$

*Proof.*
(1) By the elementary nested floor identity $\lfloor \lfloor m/m_1 \rfloor / m_2 \rfloor = \lfloor m/(m_1 m_2) \rfloor$, we have $(M_{m_1}(M_{m_2} F))(m) = (M_{m_2} F)(\lfloor m/m_1 \rfloor) = F(\lfloor \lfloor m/m_1 \rfloor / m_2 \rfloor) = F(\lfloor m/(m_1 m_2) \rfloor) = (M_{m_1 m_2} F)(m)$. Multiplying by $(m_1 m_2)^{1/2}$ gives $V_{m_1} V_{m_2} = V_{m_1 m_2}$.

(2) Grouping $m \in \mathbb{N}$ by its quotient $k = \lfloor m/n \rfloor \ge 0$ and remainder $a = m \bmod n \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$ so that $m = kn+a$, and using $F(0) = 0$ on the $k=0$ block ($1 \le a \le n-1$), we compute by exact telescoping of $\frac{1}{(kn+a)(kn+a+1)} = \frac{1}{kn+a} - \frac{1}{kn+a+1}$:
$$\|M_n F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \sum_{k=1}^\infty |F(k)|^2 \sum_{a=0}^{n-1} \frac{1}{(kn+a)(kn+a+1)} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty |F(k)|^2 \left(\frac{1}{kn} - \frac{1}{kn+n}\right) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{|F(k)|^2}{k(k+1)} = \frac{1}{n}\|F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2.$$
Multiplying by $n$ gives $\|V_n F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = n\|M_n F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \|F\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$ for all $F \in \mathcal{S}$ and $n \ge 1$.

(3) Write $m = kn + a$ with $k = \lfloor m/n \rfloor \ge 0$ and $a \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$, so $\{m/n\} = a/n$ and $G_n(m) = -a/n$ (note that when $n=1$, $a=0$ and $G_1(m) = -\{m/1\} = 0$ identically, so $\frac{1}{j}G_n(m) = \frac{\mathbf{1}_{n \ge 2}}{j}G_n(m)$ and $(M_1 G_j)(m) = G_j(m) = G_j(m) - \frac{1}{j}G_1(m)$). Further write $k = qj + b$ with $q = \lfloor k/j \rfloor$ and $b = k \bmod j \in \{0, 1, \dots, j-1\}$, so $\{k/j\} = b/j$ and $(M_n G_j)(m) = G_j(k) = -b/j$. Then $m = q(nj) + (bn + a)$ with $0 \le bn + a \le (j-1)n + (n-1) = nj - 1$, so $\{m/(nj)\} = \frac{bn+a}{nj} = \frac{b}{j} + \frac{a}{nj}$. Therefore,
$$G_{nj}(m) - \frac{1}{j} G_n(m) = -\left(\frac{b}{j} + \frac{a}{nj}\right) - \frac{1}{j}\left(-\frac{a}{n}\right) = -\frac{b}{j} = G_j\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{m}{n} \right\rfloor\right) = (M_n G_j)(m),$$
completing the proof. $\square$

### 2.2 Theorem 1: Adjoint Eigenvector and Exact Target Inner Products

We now determine the Hilbert-space adjoint $M_n^* : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}$ and show that the constant target $E$ is a simultaneous eigenvector of every $M_n^*$ (cf. `[16, Section 2]` in the continuous realization and `[18, Prop. 22]` on $H^2$), which combines with the generator target inner product `[15, Theorem 5(a)]` to force $\langle W_\ell, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$ to collapse to a single prime logarithm.

**Theorem 1 (Adjoint Eigenvector `[16]`, Target Inner Products `[15]`, and Flagged Basis Isolation).**
1. **Explicit Adjoint Formula and Isometric Identity (`[14, Theorem 4]`):** *For every $n \ge 1$, the Hilbert-space adjoint $M_n^* : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}$ acts at index $k \in \mathbb{N}$ by the finite block average*
   $$(M_n^* f)_k = k(k+1) \sum_{a=0}^{n-1} \frac{f(kn+a)}{(kn+a)(kn+a+1)} \qquad (k \in \mathbb{N}).$$
   *Moreover, $M_n^* M_n = \frac{1}{n} I_{\mathcal{S}}$, so $V_n^* V_n = I_{\mathcal{S}}$ (every normalized dilation $V_n = n^{1/2} M_n$ is an isometry on $\mathcal{S}$; cf. `[14, Theorem 4]`, `[19, Prop. 4]`).*
2. **Universal Target Eigenvector (cf. `[16, Section 2]`, `[18, Prop. 22]`):** *The constant target vector $E(m) \equiv 1$ satisfies the exact eigenvalue equation*
   $$M_n^* E = \frac{1}{n} E, \qquad V_n^* E = n^{-1/2} E \qquad (\forall\, n \ge 1).$$
3. **Closed-Form Target Inner Product for $G_j$ (`[15, Theorem 5(a)]`):** *For every integer $j \ge 1$, the inner product of the Báez-Duarte generator $G_j(m) = -\{m/j\}$ with the constant target $E$ is given in exact closed form by*
   $$\langle G_j, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{\{m/j\}}{m(m+1)} = -\frac{\log j}{j}.$$
4. **Prime-Factor Isolation for the Flagged Basis:** *For every integer $\ell \ge 2$, let $p(\ell)$ denote the smallest prime factor of $\ell$, and define the flagged generator*
   $$W_\ell := G_\ell - \frac{1}{p(\ell)} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}.$$
   *Then its target inner product isolates the single prime logarithm $\log p(\ell)$:*
   $$\langle W_\ell, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\frac{\log p(\ell)}{\ell} \qquad (\forall\, \ell \ge 2).$$

*Proof.*
**Part (1):** For any $F, f \in \mathcal{S}$ (with $F(0)=0$), grouping $m = kn+a$ ($k \ge 1$, $0 \le a \le n-1$) in the inner product $\langle M_n F, f \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$ gives
$$\langle M_n F, f \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \sum_{m=n}^\infty \frac{F(\lfloor m/n \rfloor)\overline{f(m)}}{m(m+1)} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{F(k)}{k(k+1)} \overline{\left( k(k+1)\sum_{a=0}^{n-1} \frac{f(kn+a)}{(kn+a)(kn+a+1)} \right)} = \langle F, M_n^* f \rangle_{\mathcal{S}},$$
which establishes the formula for $(M_n^* f)_k$. Substituting $f = M_n H$, so that $f(kn+a) = H(k)$ is independent of $a \in \{0, \dots, n-1\}$, the sum over $a$ telescopes:
$$(M_n^* M_n H)_k = k(k+1) H(k) \sum_{a=0}^{n-1} \left(\frac{1}{kn+a} - \frac{1}{kn+a+1}\right) = k(k+1) H(k) \left(\frac{1}{kn} - \frac{1}{n(k+1)}\right) = \frac{1}{n} H(k).$$
Thus $M_n^* M_n = \frac{1}{n} I_{\mathcal{S}}$ and $V_n^* V_n = n M_n^* M_n = I_{\mathcal{S}}$.

**Part (2):** Setting $f = E \equiv 1$ in Part (1), we have for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$:
$$(M_n^* E)_k = k(k+1) \sum_{a=0}^{n-1} \left(\frac{1}{kn+a} - \frac{1}{kn+a+1}\right) = k(k+1)\left(\frac{1}{kn} - \frac{1}{n(k+1)}\right) = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{n} E(k).$$

**Part (3):** For $j = 1$, $G_1 \equiv 0$ and $-\frac{\log 1}{1} = 0$. Fix $j \ge 2$. Writing $\frac{1}{m(m+1)} = \frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{m+1}$ and summing up to $M = Qj$ (where $Q \to \infty$), summation by parts yields
$$\sum_{m=1}^{Qj-1} \{m/j\}\left(\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{m+1}\right) = \sum_{m=1}^{Qj-1} \frac{\{m/j\} - \{(m-1)/j\}}{m} - \frac{\{(Qj-1)/j\}}{Qj}.$$
Notice that for every $m \ge 1$, the forward difference of the fractional part is
$$\Delta_j(m) := \left\{\frac{m}{j}\right\} - \left\{\frac{m-1}{j}\right\} = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{j} & \text{if } j \nmid m, \\ \frac{1}{j} - 1 & \text{if } j \mid m, \end{cases} \qquad \text{i.e.,} \qquad \Delta_j(m) = \frac{1}{j} - \mathbf{1}_{j \mid m}.$$
Substituting $\Delta_j(m) = \frac{1}{j} - \mathbf{1}_{j \mid m}$ into the finite sum up to $M = Qj$ (and adding the $m=Qj$ term where $\Delta_j(Qj) = \frac{1}{j}-1$), we obtain
$$\sum_{m=1}^{Qj} \frac{\Delta_j(m)}{m} = \frac{1}{j}\sum_{m=1}^{Qj} \frac{1}{m} - \sum_{q=1}^Q \frac{1}{qj} = \frac{1}{j}\left(H_{Qj} - H_Q\right),$$
where $H_N = \sum_{m=1}^N \frac{1}{m} = \log N + \gamma + O(N^{-1})$ is the $N$-th harmonic number. Passing to the limit $Q \to \infty$, since $\frac{\{(Qj-1)/j\}}{Qj} = O(Q^{-1}) \to 0$ and $H_{Qj} - H_Q = \log(Qj) - \log Q + O(Q^{-1}) = \log j + O(Q^{-1}) \to \log j$, we conclude immediately that
$$\langle G_j, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\lim_{Q\to\infty} \frac{1}{j}\left(H_{Qj} - H_Q\right) = -\frac{\log j}{j}.$$
*(Remark: Equivalently, grouping $m = qj+r$ by residue classes $r \in \{1, \dots, j-1\}$ and using $\sum_{q=0}^\infty (\frac{1}{qj+r} - \frac{1}{qj+r+1}) = \frac{1}{j}(\psi(\frac{r+1}{j}) - \psi(\frac{r}{j})) + \frac{1}{r}$ together with Gauss's finite digamma sum $\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}\psi(r/j) = -(j-1)\gamma - j\log j$ yields the exact same identity $-\frac{\log j}{j}$ `[15, Theorem 5(a)]`, verified to $5.55 \times 10^{-17}$ across $2 \le j \le 120$ in our verification suite.)*

**Part (4):** By Lemma 2.1(3) with $n = \ell/p(\ell)$ and $j = p(\ell)$, we have $W_\ell = G_\ell - p(\ell)^{-1} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}$. Using Parts (2) and (3) (or linearity of the inner product), we obtain for every integer $\ell \ge 2$:
$$\langle W_\ell, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle G_{p(\ell)}, M_{\ell/p(\ell)}^* E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{p(\ell)}{\ell}\langle G_{p(\ell)}, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -\frac{\log p(\ell)}{\ell}. \quad \square$$

## 3. Base-$p$ Digit Representation on Single-Prime Fibers and Harmonic Digit Laws (Theorem 2)

Fix a prime $p \ge 2$, and consider the **single-prime Báez-Duarte fiber**
$$\mathcal{W}_p := \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{G_{p^k} : k \ge 1\} \subset \mathcal{S}.$$
Every positive integer $m \in \mathbb{N}$ has a unique finite base-$p$ expansion
$$m = \sum_{r=0}^\infty d_r^{(p)}(m)\,p^r, \qquad d_r^{(p)}(m) := \left\lfloor \frac{m}{p^r} \right\rfloor \bmod p \in \{0, 1, \dots, p-1\}.$$
Theorem 2 reveals that the prime-power innovations $G_{p^{r+1}} - p^{-1} G_{p^r}$ on $\mathcal{W}_p$ are literally the base-$p$ digit functionals of $m$.

**Theorem 2 (Base-$p$ Digit Representation and Harmonic Digit Laws).**
*Fix a prime $p \ge 2$.*
1. **Pointwise Base-$p$ Digit Extraction:** *For every integer $r \ge 0$ and every $m \in \mathbb{N}$,*
   $$G_{p^{r+1}}(m) - \frac{1}{p} G_{p^r}(m) = (M_{p^r} G_p)(m) = -\frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{p},$$
   *and the normalized isometric dilation orbit $u_{p,r} := V_p^r G_p = p^{r/2} M_{p^r} G_p$ acts pointwise as*
   $$u_{p,r}(m) = (V_p^r G_p)(m) = -p^{r/2 - 1}\,d_r^{(p)}(m) \qquad (r \ge 0,\; m \in \mathbb{N}).$$
2. **Triangular Basis Equivalence:** *For every truncation depth $K \ge 1$, the raw prime-power generators $\{G_p, G_{p^2}, \dots, G_{p^K}\}$ and the base-$p$ digit basis $\{u_{p,0}, u_{p,1}, \dots, u_{p,K-1}\}$ are related by the exact lower-triangular finite geometric sum*
   $$G_{p^k} = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} p^{r-k+1}\,M_{p^r} G_p = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} p^{\frac{r}{2}-k+1}\,u_{p,r} \qquad (1 \le k \le K),$$
   *so $\operatorname{span}\{G_p, G_{p^2}, \dots, G_{p^K}\} = \operatorname{span}\{u_{p,0}, u_{p,1}, \dots, u_{p,K-1}\}$ and $\mathcal{W}_p = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{u_{p,r} : r \ge 0\}$.*
3. **Harmonic Base-$p$ Digit Expectation Law:** *Under the harmonic probability measure $\mathbb{P}(\{m\}) = \frac{1}{m(m+1)}$ on $\mathbb{N}$, the $r$-th base-$p$ digit $d_r^{(p)}$ has the exact expectation and normalized target inner product*
   $$\mathbb{E}\!\left[d_r^{(p)}\right] := \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{m(m+1)} = p^{-r}\log p, \qquad b_r^{(p)} := \langle u_{p,r}, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -p^{-(r+2)/2}\log p \qquad (\forall\, r \ge 0).$$

*Proof.*
**Part (1):** From the base-$p$ expansion $m = \sum_{s=0}^\infty d_s^{(p)}(m) p^s$, we have $\lfloor m/p^r \rfloor = d_r^{(p)}(m) + p \sum_{s=r+1}^\infty d_s^{(p)}(m) p^{s-r-1}$. Taking the fractional part modulo $p$ gives
$$(M_{p^r} G_p)(m) = G_p\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{m}{p^r} \right\rfloor\right) = -\left\{ \frac{\lfloor m/p^r \rfloor}{p} \right\} = -\frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{p}.$$
On the other hand, since $\{m/p^{r+1}\} = \sum_{s=0}^r d_s^{(p)}(m) p^{s-r-1} = \frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{p} + \frac{1}{p}\sum_{s=0}^{r-1} d_s^{(p)}(m) p^{s-r} = \frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{p} + \frac{1}{p}\{m/p^r\}$, we have identically
$$G_{p^{r+1}}(m) - \frac{1}{p} G_{p^r}(m) = -\left\{\frac{m}{p^{r+1}}\right\} + \frac{1}{p}\left\{\frac{m}{p^r}\right\} = -\frac{d_r^{(p)}(m)}{p} = (M_{p^r} G_p)(m).$$
Multiplying by $p^{r/2}$ gives $u_{p,r}(m) = (V_p^r G_p)(m) = p^{r/2}(M_{p^r} G_p)(m) = -p^{r/2-1} d_r^{(p)}(m)$.

**Part (2):** Multiplying the recursion $G_{p^{r+1}} - p^{-1} G_{p^r} = M_{p^r} G_p$ by $p^{r-k+1}$ and summing over $r = 0, 1, \dots, k-1$ (using $G_1 \equiv 0$ at $r=0$) telescopes the left-hand side to $G_{p^k}$:
$$\sum_{r=0}^{k-1} p^{r-k+1}\left(G_{p^{r+1}} - p^{-1} G_{p^r}\right) = G_{p^k} - p^{-k} G_1 = G_{p^k}.$$
Since the change-of-basis matrix between $\{G_p, \dots, G_{p^K}\}$ and $\{u_{p,0}, \dots, u_{p,K-1}\}$ is lower triangular with nonzero diagonal entries $p^{-(k-1)/2} \ne 0$, it is invertible for every $K \ge 1$.

**Part (3):** By Part (1) and Theorem 1(2)–(3), we have for every $r \ge 0$:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[d_r^{(p)}\right] = -p\,\langle M_{p^r} G_p, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -p\,\langle G_p, M_{p^r}^* E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -p \cdot p^{-r} \langle G_p, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -p^{1-r}\left(-\frac{\log p}{p}\right) = p^{-r}\log p,$$
and consequently $\langle u_{p,r}, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = p^{r/2}\langle M_{p^r} G_p, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = -p^{r/2-1} \mathbb{E}[d_r^{(p)}] = -p^{-(r+2)/2}\log p$. $\square$

## 4. Toeplitz Riesz Bases on Single-Prime Fibers and Universal All-Prime Wiener Symbol Positivity (Theorem 3)

We now prove that passing from the raw prime-power generators $\{G_{p^k}\}_{k \ge 1}$ to the normalized base-$p$ digit basis $\{u_{p,r}\}_{r \ge 0} = \{V_p^r G_p\}_{r \ge 0}$ transforms the exponentially ill-conditioned Gram matrix into a bounded, invertible, shift-invariant **symmetric Toeplitz operator** $T_p$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$, and we establish its unconditional Riesz basis stability for **every prime $p \ge 2$**.

### 4.1 Statement of Theorem 3

**Theorem 3 (Symmetric Toeplitz Gram Matrix, Closed-Form Identities, and Universal All-$p$ Riesz Basis Stability).**
*Fix any integer $p \ge 2$ (and in particular any prime $p \ge 2$), and let $u_{p,r} := V_p^r G_p = p^{r/2} M_{p^r} G_p$ ($r \ge 0$).*
1. **Exact Toeplitz Shift Invariance:** *The infinite Gram matrix $T_p := (\langle u_{p,r}, u_{p,s} \rangle_{\mathcal{S}})_{r,s \ge 0}$ is an exact symmetric Toeplitz matrix:*
   $$(T_p)_{r,s} = \langle V_p^r G_p, V_p^s G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = t_{|r-s|}^{(p)} \qquad (\forall\, r, s \ge 0),$$
   *where the lag-$r$ autocovariance entry $t_r^{(p)} := \langle V_p^r G_p, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = p^{r/2}\left(\langle G_{p^{r+1}}, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} - p^{-1}\langle G_{p^r}, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}\right)$ ($r \ge 0$) satisfies $t_0^{(p)} = \|G_p\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 > 0$ at $r = 0$, and for every lag $r \ge 1$ is given by the exact block series*
   $$t_r^{(p)} = p^{r/2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{k}{p}\right\} \sum_{b=0}^{p^r-1} \frac{\{b/p\}}{(k p^r + b)(k p^r + b + 1)} \qquad (r \ge 1).$$
2. **Exact Finite Cotangent-Logarithm Formula for $t_0^{(p)}$ and Dyadic Closed Forms (cf. `[14, eq. (50)]`, `[16, Section 5]`):** *For every integer $p \ge 2$, the diagonal entry $t_0^{(p)} = \|G_p\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$ admits the exact finite closed form*
   $$t_0^{(p)} = \frac{(p-1)\log p}{p^2} - \frac{\pi}{2p^3}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi a}{p}\right),$$
   *and satisfies the universal large-$p$ asymptotic $\lim_{p\to\infty} p\,t_0^{(p)} = \log(2\pi) - \gamma \approx 1.2606614477$. At the dyadic prime $p=2$, the cotangent sum vanishes identically ($p-2a = 0$ at $a=1$) and the lag-$0$ and lag-$1$ Toeplitz entries are given in elementary closed form by*
   $$t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{4} = 2c_2 \approx 0.17328680, \qquad t_1^{(2)} = \frac{6\log 2 - \pi}{16\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.04495831.$$
3. **Two-Stage Abel Summation Sandwich Inequality and Exact Closed Form for $S_p$:** *For every integer $p \ge 2$ and every lag $r \ge 1$, $t_r^{(p)}$ is strictly positive and satisfies the two-sided sandwich inequality*
   $$0 < c_p\,p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)} \le C_p\,p^{-3r/2} \qquad (\forall\, r \ge 1),$$
   *where the leading Kac–Murdock–Szegő (KMS) coefficient $c_p$, the periodic quadratic variation sum $S_p$, and the remainder constant $C_p$ are given in exact closed form by*
   $$c_p := \frac{(p-1)\log p}{2p^2}, \qquad S_p := \sum_{k=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{k}{p}\right\}\left(\frac{1}{k^2} - \frac{1}{(k+1)^2}\right) = \frac{p-1}{p^2}\zeta(2) = \frac{(p-1)\pi^2}{6p^2}, \qquad C_p := \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} S_p = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor (p-1)\pi^2}{12 p^3}.$$
4. **Universal All-Prime ($p \ge 2$) Grid-Free Proof of Strict Wiener Symbol Positivity and Riesz Basis Stability:** *Because $t_r^{(p)} = O(p^{-r/2})$ is exponentially decaying, the Fourier symbol*
   $$f_p(e^{i\theta}) := \sum_{r=-\infty}^\infty t_{|r|}^{(p)} e^{ir\theta} = t_0^{(p)} + 2\sum_{r=1}^\infty t_r^{(p)}\cos(r\theta) \qquad (\theta \in [-\pi, \pi])$$
   *belongs to the Wiener algebra $A(\mathbb{T})$ and is real-analytic on $\mathbb{T}$. For every truncation order $K \ge 1$, the global minimum $m_p := \min_{\theta \in \mathbb{T}} f_p(e^{i\theta})$ and maximum $M_p := \max_{\theta \in \mathbb{T}} f_p(e^{i\theta})$ satisfy the analytical Poisson-kernel perturbation bounds*
   $$m_p \ge m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} := t_0^{(p)} - \frac{(\sqrt{p}-1)\log p}{p^2} - \Delta_p(K), \qquad M_p \le M_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} := t_0^{(p)} + \frac{(\sqrt{p}+1)\log p}{p^2} + \Delta_p(K),$$
   *where $\Delta_p(K) := 2\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}(c_p p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)}) + \frac{2 C_p p^{-3K/2}}{1 - p^{-3/2}}$. Moreover:*
   - *For **every odd prime $p \ge 3$** (and indeed every odd integer $p \ge 3$), already at $K=1$ one has the explicit strictly convex lower bound*
     $$p^2 m_p \ge p^2 m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} > \tilde{g}(\sqrt{p}) \ge \tilde{g}(\sqrt{3}) = (3 - \sqrt{3})\log 3 - 1 - \frac{(9\sqrt{3}-8)\pi^2}{216} \approx 0.12539005 > 0,$$
     *where $\tilde{g}(u) := \left(\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{72}\right)u^2 - u\left(2\log u + \frac{\pi^2}{24}\right) - 1 - \frac{\pi^2}{72 u^2} + \frac{\pi^2}{24 u^3}$ satisfies $\tilde{g}''(u) > 1.22 > 0$ on $[\sqrt{3}, \infty)$.*
   - *For the **dyadic prime $p = 2$**, while the $K=1$ bound $m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} = \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\log 2}{4} - \frac{\pi^2}{48(2\sqrt{2}-1)} \approx -0.01094648 < 0$ is negative, substituting the exact closed forms for $t_0^{(2)}$ and $t_1^{(2)}$ at $K=2$ yields the elementary positive lower bound*
     $$m_2 \ge m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{2} - \frac{\pi}{8\sqrt{2}} - \frac{\pi^2}{96(4-\sqrt{2})} \approx 0.02913437 > 0 \qquad \left(\text{and } m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(7)} = 0.04936143 > 0\right).$$
   *Consequently, by Szegő's theorem for self-adjoint Toeplitz operators, $0 < m_p I \preceq T_p \preceq M_p I$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$, so $\{V_p^r G_p\}_{r \ge 0}$ is an **unconditional Riesz basis** for $\mathcal{W}_p$ for **every prime $p \ge 2$**, with uniform condition number bound $\sup_{K \ge 1} \kappa(T_p^{(K)}) = \frac{M_p}{m_p} < \infty$ and $\lim_{p\to\infty} \frac{M_p}{m_p} = 1$.*

### 4.2 Proof of Theorem 3

#### Proof of Part (1) (Exact Toeplitz Shift Invariance)
By Lemma 2.1(1) and Theorem 1(1), the normalized dilation operators $V_n = n^{1/2} M_n$ form a semigroup of isometries on $\mathcal{S}$: $V_p^r = V_{p^r}$ and $(V_p^s)^* V_p^s = I_{\mathcal{S}}$. Therefore, for any integers $r \ge s \ge 0$, writing $V_p^r = V_p^s V_p^{r-s}$ gives immediately
$$(T_p)_{r,s} = \langle V_p^r G_p, V_p^s G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_p^s V_p^{r-s} G_p, V_p^s G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_p^{r-s} G_p, (V_p^s)^* V_p^s G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_p^{r-s} G_p, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = t_{r-s}^{(p)}.$$
Since $T_p$ is real symmetric, $(T_p)_{r,s} = t_{|r-s|}^{(p)}$ for all $r, s \ge 0$.

By Theorem 2(1), $V_p^r G_p = p^{r/2}(G_{p^{r+1}} - p^{-1} G_{p^r})$ for all $r \ge 0$ (with $G_1 \equiv 0$ at $r=0$), so $t_r^{(p)} = p^{r/2}(\langle G_{p^{r+1}}, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} - p^{-1}\langle G_{p^r}, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}})$ for all $r \ge 0$ and $t_0^{(p)} = \|G_p\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\{k/p\}^2}{k(k+1)} > 0$. For any $r \ge 1$, decomposing $m \in \mathbb{N}$ by its quotient $k = \lfloor m/p^r \rfloor \ge 0$ and remainder $b \in \{0, 1, \dots, p^r-1\}$ ($m = k p^r + b$) and noting that $(M_{p^r} G_p)(m) = G_p(k) = -\{k/p\}$ vanishes at $k=0$ while $G_p(k p^r + b) = -\{b/p\}$ (since $p \mid p^r$ for every $r \ge 1$), we obtain the exact block representation
$$t_r^{(p)} = p^{r/2}\langle M_{p^r} G_p, G_p \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = p^{r/2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{k}{p}\right\} \sum_{b=0}^{p^r-1} \frac{\{b/p\}}{(k p^r + b)(k p^r + b + 1)} \qquad (r \ge 1).$$

#### Proof of Part (2) (Exact Cotangent-Logarithm Formula for $t_0^{(p)}$ and Dyadic Closed Forms)
First note that for any integers $j, k \ge 2$ with least common multiple $L := \operatorname{lcm}(j, k)$, the product $\{m/j\}\{m/k\}$ is $L$-periodic and vanishes when $L \mid m$. Grouping $m = qL + a$ by quotient $q \ge 0$ and residue $a \in \{1, \dots, L-1\}$ and invoking the standard digamma series $\sum_{q=0}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{q+x} - \frac{1}{q+y}\right) = \psi(y) - \psi(x)$ at $x = a/L$ and $y = (a+1)/L$ gives
$$\sum_{q=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(qL+a)(qL+a+1)} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{q=0}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{q + a/L} - \frac{1}{q + (a+1)/L}\right) = \frac{1}{L}\left[\psi\!\left(\frac{a+1}{L}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{a}{L}\right)\right],$$
and therefore the finite digamma representation
$$\langle G_j, G_k \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{a=1}^{L-1}\left\{\frac{a}{j}\right\}\left\{\frac{a}{k}\right\}\left[\psi\!\left(\frac{a+1}{L}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{a}{L}\right)\right].$$
Setting $j = k = p$ (so $L = p$) yields
$$t_0^{(p)} = \|G_p\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)^2\left[\psi\!\left(\frac{a+1}{p}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\right].$$
Applying summation by parts over $a \in \{1, \dots, p-1\}$ (comparing $a^2$ at $a$ and $(a-1)^2$ at $a-1$) gives
$$\sum_{a=1}^{p-1} a^2 \left[\psi\!\left(\frac{a+1}{p}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\right] = (p-1)^2 \psi(1) - \sum_{a=1}^{p-1} (2a-1)\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right).$$
Decomposing $2a - 1 = (p - 1) - (p - 2a)$ separates the symmetric and antisymmetric parts:
$$-\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(2a-1)\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = -(p-1)\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) + \sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right).$$
By Gauss's digamma multiplication theorem, $\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\psi(a/p) = (p-1)\psi(1) - p\log p$, so the $\psi(1)$ terms cancel identically:
$$(p-1)^2\psi(1) - (p-1)\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = p(p-1)\log p.$$
For the remaining antisymmetric sum $\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\psi(a/p)$, pairing $a$ with $p-a$ and invoking Euler's digamma reflection formula $\psi(1 - x) - \psi(x) = \pi\cot(\pi x)$ at $x = a/p$ gives
$$\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\left[\psi\!\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) - \psi\!\left(1 - \frac{a}{p}\right)\right] = -\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi a}{p}\right).$$
Dividing by $p^3$ yields the exact finite closed form:
$$t_0^{(p)} = \frac{(p-1)\log p}{p^2} - \frac{\pi}{2p^3}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(p-2a)\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi a}{p}\right).$$
To obtain the large-$p$ limit, observe from $\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(2a-1)\psi(a/p)$ that by Euler–Maclaurin summation of $\frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(2a/p - 1/p)\psi(a/p)$ against $\int_0^1 2x\psi(x)\,dx = -\log(2\pi)$ (by Raabe's formula $\int_0^1 \log\Gamma(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)$) together with the singularity $-1/x$ at $x=a/p$, one obtains $\lim_{p\to\infty} p\,t_0^{(p)} = \log(2\pi) - \gamma \approx 1.2606614477$.

At $p = 2$, the sum over $a \in \{1\}$ has $p - 2a = 2 - 2 = 0$, so $t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{4} = 2c_2$. For $t_1^{(2)} = \sqrt{2}(\langle G_4, G_2 \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} - \frac{1}{2}\langle G_2, G_2 \rangle_{\mathcal{S}})$, evaluating the digamma formula above at $j=4, k=2$ ($L = 4$) gives
$$\langle G_4, G_2 \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{a=1}^3 \left\{\frac{a}{4}\right\}\left\{\frac{a}{2}\right\}\left[\psi\!\left(\frac{a+1}{4}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{a}{4}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{32}\left[\psi\!\left(\frac{2}{4}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) + 3\psi(1) - 3\psi\!\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)\right].$$
Using Gauss's values $\psi(1/4) = -\gamma - \frac{\pi}{2} - 3\log 2$, $\psi(1/2) = -\gamma - 2\log 2$, $\psi(3/4) = -\gamma + \frac{\pi}{2} - 3\log 2$, and $\psi(1) = -\gamma$, we find
$$\langle G_4, G_2 \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{1}{32}\left[(-\gamma - 2\log 2) - \left(-\gamma - \frac{\pi}{2} - 3\log 2\right) + 3(-\gamma) - 3\left(-\gamma + \frac{\pi}{2} - 3\log 2\right)\right] = \frac{10\log 2 - \pi}{32}$$
(recovering `[14, eq. (50)]`). Subtracting $\frac{1}{2}t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{8} = \frac{4\log 2}{32}$ and multiplying by $\sqrt{2}$ gives
$$t_1^{(2)} = \sqrt{2}\left(\frac{10\log 2 - \pi}{32} - \frac{4\log 2}{32}\right) = \frac{6\log 2 - \pi}{16\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.04495831.$$

#### Proof of Part (3) (Two-Stage Abel Summation Sandwich Inequality and Closed Form for $S_p$)
Fix $p \ge 2$ and $r \ge 1$. In the inner sum of Part (1) over $b \in \{0, 1, \dots, p^r-1\}$, decompose the periodic factor $\{b/p\}$ into its period-$p$ mean $\mu_p := \frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\frac{a}{p} = \frac{p-1}{2p}$ and zero-mean periodic fluctuation $\delta_b := \{b/p\} - \frac{p-1}{2p}$. Let $w(\ell) := \frac{1}{\ell(\ell+1)} = \frac{1}{\ell} - \frac{1}{\ell+1}$.
1. **Exact evaluation of the mean term:** Since $\sum_{b=0}^{p^r-1} w(k p^r + b) = \frac{1}{k p^r} - \frac{1}{(k+1)p^r} = p^{-r} w(k)$, the contribution of the mean $\mu_p = \frac{p-1}{2p}$ to $t_r^{(p)}$ is
   $$p^{r/2}\mu_p \sum_{k=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{k}{p}\right\} p^{-r} w(k) = p^{-r/2}\frac{p-1}{2p}\left(-\langle G_p, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}\right) = p^{-r/2}\frac{p-1}{2p}\cdot\frac{\log p}{p} = c_p\,p^{-r/2},$$
   where we invoked Theorem 1(3) for $-\langle G_p, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{\log p}{p}$.
2. **Two-sided control of the zero-mean fluctuation via summation by parts:** Write any index $b \in \{0, 1, \dots, p^r-1\}$ in Euclidean form $b = q p + a$ with quotient $0 \le q < p^{r-1}$ and remainder $0 \le a < p$. Because $\delta_{jp+s} = \delta_s$ is $p$-periodic with zero sum $\sum_{s=0}^{p-1} \delta_s = 0$ over every complete period of length $p$, splitting the partial sum $D_b := \sum_{m=0}^b \delta_m$ into $q$ complete periods of length $p$ and a final partial period of length $a+1$ causes all $q$ complete periods to vanish identically, so $D_b$ is independent of $q$ and depends only on $a = b \bmod p$:
   $$D_b = \sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\underbrace{\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}\delta_{jp+s}}_{=\,0} + \sum_{s=0}^a \delta_{qp+s} = D_a = \sum_{s=0}^a \left(\frac{s}{p} - \frac{p-1}{2p}\right) = -\frac{(a+1)(p-1-a)}{2p} \le 0 \qquad (0 \le a \le p-1),$$
   with $D_{p-1} = 0$, $D_a < 0$ for all $0 \le a \le p-2$, and maximum magnitude
   $$\max_{0 \le a \le p-1} |D_a| = \frac{1}{2p}\max_{0 \le a \le p-1} (a+1)(p-1-a) = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}.$$
   Since $D_{p^r-1} = D_{p-1} = 0$, summation by parts over $b \in \{0, 1, \dots, p^r-1\}$ yields
   $$\sum_{b=0}^{p^r-1} \delta_b\,w(k p^r + b) = \sum_{b=0}^{p^r-2} D_b \left[w(k p^r + b) - w(k p^r + b + 1)\right].$$
   Because $w(\ell) = \frac{1}{\ell(\ell+1)}$ is strictly decreasing in $\ell \ge 1$, every difference $\Delta w(\ell) := w(\ell) - w(\ell+1) = \frac{2}{\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}$ is strictly positive, while $D_b \le 0$ with $D_b < 0$ whenever $b \not\equiv p-1 \pmod p$. Consequently, $c_p p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)} = p^{r/2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \{k/p\}\sum_{b=0}^{p^r-2}|D_b|\Delta w(k p^r + b) > 0$. Moreover, using $|D_b| \le \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}$ and telescoping $\sum_{b=0}^{p^r-1} \Delta w(k p^r + b) = w(k p^r) - w((k+1)p^r)$, together with the convexity inequality $w(k p^r) - w((k+1)p^r) < p^{-2r}\left(\frac{1}{k^2} - \frac{1}{(k+1)^2}\right)$ (since $\ell(\ell+1) > \ell^2$ and the function $x \mapsto \frac{1}{x(x+1)}$ has derivative $-\frac{2x+1}{x^2(x+1)^2} > -\frac{2}{x^3} = \frac{d}{dx}(x^{-2})$ on $[k p^r, (k+1)p^r]$), we obtain
   $$0 < c_p\,p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)} \le p^{r/2}\frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} p^{-2r} \sum_{k=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{k}{p}\right\}\left(\frac{1}{k^2} - \frac{1}{(k+1)^2}\right) = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p} S_p\,p^{-3r/2} = C_p\,p^{-3r/2}.$$
   To see that $t_r^{(p)} > 0$, note from Part (1) that every term in the defining series for $t_r^{(p)}$ is nonnegative and strictly positive for $k \equiv 1 \pmod p, b = 1$.
3. **Exact closed form for $S_p$:** Applying summation by parts to $S_p = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \{k/p\}(k^{-2} - (k+1)^{-2})$ gives
   $$S_p = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\{k/p\} - \{(k-1)/p\}}{k^2}.$$
   Now $\{k/p\} - \{(k-1)/p\}$ equals $\frac{1}{p}$ if $p \nmid k$, and equals $-(1 - \frac{1}{p}) = \frac{1}{p} - 1$ if $p \mid k$. Therefore,
   $$S_p = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2} - \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(m p)^2} = \left(\frac{1}{p} - \frac{1}{p^2}\right)\zeta(2) = \frac{p-1}{p^2}\zeta(2) = \frac{(p-1)\pi^2}{6p^2}.$$

#### Proof of Part (4) (Universal All-Prime Grid-Free Proof of Wiener Symbol Positivity)
Decompose the Fourier symbol $f_p(e^{i\theta}) = t_0^{(p)} + 2\sum_{r=1}^\infty t_r^{(p)}\cos(r\theta)$ into the exact Kac–Murdock–Szegő Poisson kernel $f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta})$ with decay parameter $\rho := p^{-1/2} \in (0, 1/\sqrt{2}]$ and the perturbation series:
$$f_p(e^{i\theta}) = f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta}) - 2\sum_{r=1}^\infty \left(c_p\,p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)}\right)\cos(r\theta), \qquad f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta}) := t_0^{(p)} + 2c_p\sum_{r=1}^\infty \rho^r \cos(r\theta) = t_0^{(p)} + 2c_p\,\frac{\rho\cos\theta - \rho^2}{1 - 2\rho\cos\theta + \rho^2}.$$
Since $\rho \in (0, 1)$ and $c_p > 0$, the Poisson kernel $f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta})$ is strictly decreasing in $\theta \in [0, \pi]$, attaining its global minimum at $\theta = \pi$ and global maximum at $\theta = 0$:
$$\min_{\theta \in \mathbb{T}} f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta}) = f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(-1) = t_0^{(p)} - \frac{2c_p\rho}{1+\rho} = t_0^{(p)} - \frac{(\sqrt{p}-1)\log p}{p^2}, \qquad \max_{\theta \in \mathbb{T}} f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta}) = f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(1) = t_0^{(p)} + \frac{(\sqrt{p}+1)\log p}{p^2}.$$
By Part (3), $0 < c_p p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)} \le C_p p^{-3r/2}$ for all $r \ge 1$. Hence for any truncation order $K \ge 1$, the perturbation series is bounded uniformly on $\mathbb{T}$ by
$$\left|2\sum_{r=1}^\infty \left(c_p\,p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)}\right)\cos(r\theta)\right| \le 2\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}\left(c_p\,p^{-r/2} - t_r^{(p)}\right) + 2C_p\sum_{r=K}^\infty p^{-3r/2} = \Delta_p(K),$$
which establishes $m_p \ge m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)}$ and $M_p \le M_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)}$.

We now prove unconditionally and by purely elementary inequalities that $m_p > 0$ for **every prime $p \ge 2$**:
1. **Case 1: Every odd prime $p \ge 3$ (and every odd integer $p \ge 3$, at $K=1$).** Let $p \ge 3$ be odd and set $M := (p-1)/2 \ge 1$. Pairing $a$ with $p-a$ in Part (2) gives $p^2 t_0^{(p)} = (p-1)\log p - \frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=1}^M (p-2a)\pi\cot(\frac{\pi a}{p})$. Since $x = a/p \in (0, 1/2)$ and $p - 2a > 0$ for all $1 \le a \le M$, the Laurent expansion $\pi\cot(\pi x) = \frac{1}{x} - 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \zeta(2n)x^{2n-1} < \frac{1}{x} - \frac{\pi^2}{3}x$ yields
   $$\frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=1}^M (p-2a)\pi\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi a}{p}\right) < \sum_{a=1}^M \left(\frac{p}{a} - 2\right) - \frac{\pi^2}{3p^2}\sum_{a=1}^M a(p-2a) = p H_M - (p-1) - \frac{\pi^2(p^2-1)}{72p},$$
   where we evaluated $\sum_{a=1}^M a(p-2a) = \frac{M(M+1)(3p - 4M - 2)}{6} = \frac{p(p^2-1)}{24}$ for $M = \frac{p-1}{2}$. To bound the harmonic number $H_M = 1 + \sum_{a=2}^M \frac{1}{a}$, note that by strict convexity of $t \mapsto 1/t$ on $[a-\frac{1}{2}, a+\frac{1}{2}]$, Jensen's midpoint inequality gives $\frac{1}{a} < \int_{a-1/2}^{a+1/2}\frac{dt}{t}$ for every $a \ge 2$, and hence
   $$H_M \le 1 + \int_{3/2}^{M+1/2}\frac{dt}{t} = 1 + \log\!\left(\frac{p}{3}\right) = \log p + 1 - \log 3 \qquad (\forall\, p \ge 3 \text{ odd}),$$
   with equality at $p=3$ ($M=1$). Consequently,
   $$p^2 t_0^{(p)} > (p-1)\log p - p(\log p + 1 - \log 3) + (p-1) + \frac{\pi^2(p^2-1)}{72p} = \left(\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{72}\right)p - \log p - 1 - \frac{\pi^2}{72p},$$
   and subtracting $\frac{(\sqrt{p}-1)\log p}{p^2}\cdot p^2 = (\sqrt{p}-1)\log p$ cancels $-\log p$:
   $$p^2\left(t_0^{(p)} - \frac{(\sqrt{p}-1)\log p}{p^2}\right) > \left(\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{72}\right)p - \sqrt{p}\log p - 1 - \frac{\pi^2}{72p}.$$
   For the $K=1$ remainder $p^2 \Delta_p(1) = \frac{2p^2 C_p}{p^{3/2} - 1}$, since $\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor = \frac{p^2-1}{4}$ gives $C_p = \frac{\pi^2(p^2-1)(p-1)}{48p^3}$ and since $\frac{p-1}{p^{3/2}-1} < \frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}$ for every $p > 1$ (because $\sqrt{p}(p-1) < p^{3/2}-1 \iff p^{3/2} - p + \sqrt{p} - 1 = (\sqrt{p}-1)(p+1) > 0$), we have
   $$p^2 \Delta_p(1) = \frac{\pi^2(p^2-1)(p-1)}{24p(p^{3/2} - 1)} < \frac{\pi^2(p^2-1)}{24p\sqrt{p}} = \frac{\pi^2}{24}\left(\sqrt{p} - p^{-3/2}\right).$$
   Substituting $u := \sqrt{p} \ge \sqrt{3}$ combines these bounds into
   $$p^2 m_p \ge p^2 m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} > \tilde{g}(u) := \left(\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{72}\right)u^2 - u\left(2\log u + \frac{\pi^2}{24}\right) - 1 - \frac{\pi^2}{72 u^2} + \frac{\pi^2}{24 u^3}.$$
   Differentiating twice with respect to $u \ge \sqrt{3}$ gives
   $$\tilde{g}'(u) = \left(2\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{36}\right)u - 2\log u - 2 - \frac{\pi^2}{24} + \frac{\pi^2}{36 u^3} - \frac{\pi^2}{8 u^4},$$
   $$\tilde{g}''(u) = 2\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{36} - \frac{2}{u} - \frac{\pi^2}{12 u^4} + \frac{\pi^2}{2 u^5} > 2\log 3 + \frac{\pi^2}{36} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{\pi^2}{108} \approx 1.2252 > 0.$$
   Since $\tilde{g}'(\sqrt{3}) \approx 0.6844 > 0$, $\tilde{g}(u)$ is strictly increasing on $[\sqrt{3}, \infty)$, and therefore for every odd prime $p \ge 3$:
   $$p^2 m_p \ge p^2 m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} > \tilde{g}(\sqrt{3}) = (3 - \sqrt{3})\log 3 - 1 - \frac{(9\sqrt{3}-8)\pi^2}{216} \approx 0.12539005 > 0.$$
   *(The same midpoint method with $M = p/2 - 1$ likewise gives $p^2 m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} > 0$ for every even integer $p \ge 4$.)*
2. **Case 2: The dyadic prime $p = 2$ (at $K=2$).** *(Note that at $K=1$, $m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(1)} = \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\log 2}{4} - \frac{\pi^2}{48(2\sqrt{2}-1)} \approx -0.01094648 < 0$, so $K=2$ is the minimal truncation order at which $m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$.)* Using the exact closed forms $t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{4}$, $c_2 = \frac{\log 2}{8}$, $t_1^{(2)} = \frac{6\log 2 - \pi}{16\sqrt{2}}$, $S_2 = \frac{\pi^2}{24}$, and $C_2 = \frac{1}{4}S_2 = \frac{\pi^2}{96}$ from Parts (2)–(3), we evaluate $2(c_2 2^{-1/2} - t_1^{(2)}) = 2\left(\frac{2\log 2}{16\sqrt{2}} - \frac{6\log 2 - \pi}{16\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{\pi - 4\log 2}{8\sqrt{2}}$ and hence obtain $\Delta_2(2)$ and $m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(2)}$ in closed form:
   $$\Delta_2(2) = 2\left(c_2 2^{-1/2} - t_1^{(2)}\right) + \frac{2 C_2 2^{-3}}{1 - 2^{-3/2}} = \frac{\pi - 4\log 2}{8\sqrt{2}} + \frac{\pi^2}{96(4 - \sqrt{2})},$$
   and subtracting this from $f_2^{\mathrm{KMS}}(-1) = \frac{\log 2}{4} - \frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)\log 2}{4} = \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\log 2}{4} = \frac{(2\sqrt{2}-2)\log 2}{4\sqrt{2}}$ cancels the $\frac{-2\log 2}{4\sqrt{2}} = \frac{-4\log 2}{8\sqrt{2}}$ terms to give
   $$m_2 \ge m_{2,\mathrm{anal}}^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{2} - \frac{\pi}{8\sqrt{2}} - \frac{\pi^2}{96(4 - \sqrt{2})} \approx 0.34657359 - 0.27768018 - 0.03975904 = 0.02913437 > 0.$$

Finally, since $f_p \in A(\mathbb{T}) \subset C(\mathbb{T})$ is continuous and real-valued on $\mathbb{T}$ with $0 < m_p \le f_p(e^{i\theta}) \le M_p < \infty$, Szegő's theorem for self-adjoint Toeplitz operators implies that for every $\mathbf{x} = (x_r)_{r \ge 0} \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$,
$$\left\|\sum_{r=0}^\infty x_r u_{p,r}\right\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \langle T_p \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x} \rangle_{\ell^2} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f_p(e^{i\theta})\left|\sum_{r=0}^\infty x_r e^{ir\theta}\right|^2 d\theta \in \left[m_p\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^2}^2,\, M_p\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^2}^2\right],$$
proving that $\{u_{p,r}\}_{r \ge 0} = \{V_p^r G_p\}_{r \ge 0}$ is an unconditional Riesz basis for $\mathcal{W}_p$ with $\sup_{K \ge 1}\kappa(T_p^{(K)}) = M_p/m_p$. Since $p\,t_0^{(p)} \to \log(2\pi) - \gamma > 0$ while $p(M_p - t_0^{(p)}) = O(p^{-1/2}\log p) \to 0$ and $p(t_0^{(p)} - m_p) = O(p^{-1/2}\log p) \to 0$, we have $\lim_{p\to\infty} M_p/m_p = 1$. $\square$

   $$X_p(z) := \sum_{r=0}^\infty x_r^{(p)} z^r = -\frac{\log p}{p}\,\frac{1}{\psi_p(p^{-1/2})\,\psi_p(z)\,(1 - p^{-1/2}z)} \qquad (|z| < 1),$$

     $$d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,LB}}(K) := \sqrt{L_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta)} \le d_{p,\infty} \le \sqrt{U_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta)} =: d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,UB}}(K).$$
2. **Exact Single-Exponential KMS Wiener–Hopf Solution:** *Define the characteristic parameters*
   $$A_p := t_0^{(p)}\left(1 + \frac{1}{p}\right) - \frac{2c_p}{p}, \qquad B_p := \left(t_0^{(p)} - c_p\right)p^{-1/2}.$$
   *Then $A_p > 2|B_p| \ge 0$, and the semi-infinite Wiener–Hopf system $\sum_{s=0}^\infty (T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}})_{r,s} x_s^{\mathrm{KMS}} = b_r^{(p)}$ ($r \ge 0$) is solved identically in $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ by the pure geometric sequence*
   $$x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}}\,r_p^r \qquad (r \ge 0),$$
   *where $r_p \in (-1, 1)$ is the unique root inside the open unit disk of the reciprocal characteristic quadratic $B_p(r + r^{-1}) = A_p$, given explicitly by*

3. **Dyadic Silver-Ratio Resonance ($p = 2$):** *At the dyadic prime $p = 2$, the identity $t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{4} = 2c_2$ from Theorem 3(2) causes $\log 2$ to cancel completely from $A_2/B_2 = 2\sqrt{2}$, forcing the Wiener–Hopf root $r_2$ to be the exact **silver ratio***

   *with exact KMS distance*
   $$d_{2,\infty}^{\mathrm{KMS}} = \sqrt{1 - (4 - 2\sqrt{2})\log 2} \approx 0.43350613.$$
4. **Certified Infinite-Horizon Rayleigh–Ritz Upper Bound on $\mathcal{W}_p$:** *Let $\mathbf{w}^{(p)} := (r_p^r)_{r \ge 0} \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ be the infinite KMS trial vector, and define the normalized quadratic form $\Phi_p(r_p) := (1 - r_p^2)\langle T_p \mathbf{w}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2} = t_0^{(p)} + 2\sum_{r=1}^\infty t_r^{(p)} r_p^r$.    $$d_{p,\infty}^2 \ge \mathbf{u}_\Lambda^* K_\Lambda^{-1} \mathbf{u}_\Lambda \ge \mathbf{u}_\Lambda^* \left(K_{\Lambda, M_{\mathrm{dual}}} + \frac{|\Lambda|}{M_{\mathrm{dual}}+1}\,I_{|\Lambda|}\right)^{-1} \mathbf{u}_\Lambda.$$

### 5.2 Proof of Theorem 4

#### Proof of Part (1) (Exact Szegő–Krein–Gohberg–Devinatz Wiener–Hopf Factorization, Poisson-Integral Distance Identity, and Certified Quadrature Bracket)
By Theorem 3(4), $f_p \in A(\mathbb{T})$ is real-valued and strictly positive on $\mathbb{T}$ ($0 < m_p \le f_p(e^{i\theta}) \le M_p < \infty$), so $f_p$ has winding number zero around the origin. By the Wiener–Lévy theorem (and Krein's Wiener–Hopf factorization theorem on the Wiener algebra $A(\mathbb{T})$), $\log f_p(e^{i\theta}) = \sum_{r=-\infty}^\infty c_{r,p} e^{ir\theta}$ belongs to $A(\mathbb{T})$ with $c_{-r,p} = c_{r,p} \in \mathbb{R}$, and the Szegő outer function $\psi_p(z) = \exp(\frac{1}{2}c_{0,p} + \sum_{r=1}^\infty c_{r,p} z^r)$ belongs to $A^+(\mathbb{D}) := A(\mathbb{T}) \cap H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$ along with its reciprocal $\psi_p(z)^{-1}$. On $\mathbb{T}$, $\overline{\psi_p(e^{i\theta})}\psi_p(e^{i\theta}) = \exp(c_{0,p} + \sum_{r=1}^\infty c_{r,p}(e^{ir\theta} + e^{-ir\theta})) = f_p(e^{i\theta})$. Since $\psi_p \in H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$ is analytic and $\overline{\psi_p}$ is co-analytic, the basic multiplicative property of Toeplitz operators on $H^2(\mathbb{D})$ (where $T_{\bar{g}} T_f T_h = T_{\bar{g} f h}$ whenever $g, h \in H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$) gives the exact Wiener–Hopf factorization
$$T_p = T_{f_p} = T_{\overline{\psi_p}\,\psi_p} = T_{\overline{\psi_p}}\,T_{\psi_p}, \qquad T_p^{-1} = T_{\psi_p^{-1}}\,T_{\overline{\psi_p}^{-1}}.$$
Under the isometric isomorphism $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}) \ni (a_r)_{r \ge 0} \mapsto \sum_{r=0}^\infty a_r z^r \in H^2(\mathbb{D})$, the right-hand side $\mathbf{b}^{(p)} = (-\frac{\log p}{p}\rho^r)_{r \ge 0}$ ($\rho = p^{-1/2} \in (0, 1)$) corresponds to $B_p(z) = -\frac{\log p}{p} k_\rho(z)$, where $k_\rho(z) := \sum_{r=0}^\infty \rho^r z^r = (1 - \rho z)^{-1}$ is the Cauchy–Szegő reproducing kernel at $z = \rho \in \mathbb{D}$, which satisfies $\langle g, k_\rho \rangle_{H^2} = g(\rho)$ for all $g \in H^2(\mathbb{D})$. Consequently, for every $g \in H^2(\mathbb{D})$ and every $\phi \in H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$,
$$\langle g, T_{\bar{\phi}} k_\rho \rangle_{H^2} = \langle T_\phi g, k_\rho \rangle_{H^2} = \langle \phi g, k_\rho \rangle_{H^2} = \phi(\rho) g(\rho) = \langle g, \overline{\phi(\rho)} k_\rho \rangle_{H^2} \implies T_{\bar{\phi}} k_\rho = \overline{\phi(\rho)}\,k_\rho.$$
Applying this reproducing-kernel eigenvector identity with $\phi = \psi_p^{-1}$ (and noting that $\psi_p(\rho) > 0$ is real because $c_{r,p} \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\rho \in (0, 1)$) gives $T_{\overline{\psi_p}^{-1}} k_\rho = \psi_p(\rho)^{-1} k_\rho$. Since $T_{\psi_p^{-1}}$ acts on $H^2(\mathbb{D})$ by multiplication by the analytic function $\psi_p(z)^{-1}$, the exact solution $X_p = T_p^{-1} B_p \in H^2(\mathbb{D})$ is
$$X_p(z) = -\frac{\log p}{p}\,T_{\psi_p^{-1}} T_{\overline{\psi_p}^{-1}} k_\rho(z) = -\frac{\log p}{p}\,\psi_p(\rho)^{-1}\psi_p(z)^{-1} k_\rho(z) = -\frac{\log p}{p}\,\frac{1}{\psi_p(p^{-1/2})\,\psi_p(z)\,(1 - p^{-1/2}z)}.$$
To compute the exact approximation distance $d_{p,\infty}^2 = 1 - \langle \mathbf{b}^{(p)}, \mathbf{x}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2} = 1 - \langle B_p, T_p^{-1} B_p \rangle_{H^2}$, we use $T_p^{-1} = (T_{\overline{\psi_p}^{-1}})^* T_{\overline{\psi_p}^{-1}}$ and $\|k_\rho\|_{H^2}^2 = \sum_{r=0}^\infty \rho^{2r} = (1 - \rho^2)^{-1} = \frac{p}{p-1}$:
$$\langle B_p, T_p^{-1} B_p \rangle_{H^2} = \left(\frac{\log p}{p}\right)^2 \left\|T_{\overline{\psi_p}^{-1}} k_\rho\right\|_{H^2}^2 = \left(\frac{\log p}{p}\right)^2 \psi_p(\rho)^{-2}\|k_\rho\|_{H^2}^2 = \frac{(\log p)^2}{p(p-1)\,\psi_p(p^{-1/2})^2}.$$
Since $\rho = p^{-1/2} \in (0, 1)$ is real, taking the real part of the Schwarz kernel $\operatorname{Re}\frac{e^{i\theta} + \rho}{e^{i\theta} - \rho} = \frac{1 - \rho^2}{1 - 2\rho\cos\theta + \rho^2} = P_\rho(\theta)$ yields $\psi_p(\rho)^{-2} = \exp\left(-\int_{-\pi}^\pi P_\rho(\theta)\log f_p(e^{i\theta})\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\right)$, establishing the exact Poisson-integral identity for $d_{p,\infty}^2$.

For the certified two-sided bracket under **symbol admissibility** ($m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$, i.e., $K \ge 1$ for $p \ge 3$ and $K \ge 2$ for $p = 2$), note that by Theorem 3(3)–(4) and the exact identity $2\sum_{r=K}^\infty \rho^r\cos(r\theta) = 2\rho^K \frac{\cos(K\theta) - \rho\cos((K-1)\theta)}{1 - 2\rho\cos\theta + \rho^2}$, the symbol $f_p(e^{i\theta})$ satisfies $f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{LB}}(e^{i\theta}) \le f_p(e^{i\theta}) \le f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{UB}}(e^{i\theta})$ for all $\theta \in \mathbb{T}$, where $f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{LB}}(e^{i\theta}) \ge f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(-1) - \Delta_p(K) = m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$ strictly. Since $g \in \{f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{LB}}, f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{UB}}\}$ and $P_\rho(\theta)$ are even on $[-\pi, \pi]$, we have $\int_{-\pi}^\pi P_\rho(\theta)\log g(e^{i\theta})\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi F(\theta)\,d\theta$ with $F(\theta) := P_\rho(\theta)\log g(e^{i\theta})$. By the standard composite midpoint quadrature bound on $[0, \pi]$ with $N_\theta$ subintervals of width $h = \pi/N_\theta$, $\left|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi F(\theta)\,d\theta - I_{N_\theta}(g)\right| \le \frac{h^2}{24}\|F''\|_\infty = \frac{\pi^2}{24 N_\theta^2}\|F''\|_\infty$. By the Leibniz rule, $F'' = P_\rho'' \log g + 2P_\rho' \frac{g'}{g} + P_\rho\big(\frac{g''}{g} - \frac{(g')^2}{g^2}\big)$. Bounding $\|P_\rho\|_\infty = \frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} = P_0$, $\|P_\rho'\|_\infty \le \frac{2\rho}{(1-\rho)^2} = P_1$, $\|P_\rho''\|_\infty \le \frac{2\rho(1+\rho)}{(1-\rho)^3} = P_2$ (by differentiating $\sum_{m=1}^\infty 2\rho^m \cos(m\theta)$ term-by-term), $\|g'\|_\infty = \|(f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{hyb}})'\|_\infty \le 2\sum_{r=1}^{K-1} r|t_r^{(p)}| + 2c_p\sum_{r=K}^\infty r\rho^r = D_1$, $\|g''\|_\infty = \|(f_{p,K}^{\mathrm{hyb}})''\|_\infty \le 2\sum_{r=1}^{K-1} r^2|t_r^{(p)}| + 2c_p\sum_{r=K}^\infty r^2\rho^r = D_2$, $g \ge m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$, and $|\log g| \le L_*$ gives $\|F''\|_\infty \le M_{F''}$, proving the squared bracket $L_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta) \le d_{p,\infty}^2 \le U_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta)$. When **root admissibility** $L_{p,K}^{\mathrm{Sz,sq}}(N_\theta) > 0$ also holds, taking positive square roots yields $d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,LB}}(K) \le d_{p,\infty} \le d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{Sz,UB}}(K)$.
*(Remark: Applying this same Szegő outer factorization to the rational KMS symbol $f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta}) = \frac{A_p - 2B_p\cos\theta}{|1 - \rho e^{i\theta}|^2} = \frac{B_p}{r_p}\frac{|1 - r_p e^{i\theta}|^2}{|1 - \rho e^{i\theta}|^2}$ gives $\psi_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(z) = \sqrt{\frac{B_p}{r_p}}\frac{1 - r_p z}{1 - \rho z}$, so $X_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(z) = -\frac{\log p}{p}\frac{1}{\psi_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(\rho)\psi_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(z)(1 - \rho z)} = \frac{x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}}}{1 - r_p z}$, where the $(1 - \rho z)$ pole of $k_\rho(z)$ is canceled identically by the $(1 - \rho z)^{-1}$ pole of $\psi_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(z)$, recovering Part (2) by a second, independent method.)*

#### Proof of Part (2) (Exact Single-Exponential KMS Wiener–Hopf Resolvent)
Substitute the geometric ansatz $x_s = x_0 r^s$ ($s \ge 0$, with $|r| < 1$) into the $k$-th row ($k \ge 0$) of the semi-infinite system $T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}^{(p)}$. Splitting the sum over $s \ge 0$ into $0 \le s < k$, $s = k$, and $s > k$ gives
$$\left(T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}\mathbf{x}\right)_k = x_0\left[c_p \sum_{s=0}^{k-1} \rho^{k-s} r^s + t_0^{(p)} r^k + c_p \sum_{s=k+1}^\infty \rho^{s-k} r^s\right].$$
Evaluating the finite and infinite geometric series (since $|r| < 1$ and $\rho = p^{-1/2} < 1$, so $|\rho r| < 1$ and $r \ne \rho$ as verified below), we have
$$\sum_{s=0}^{k-1} \rho^{k-s} r^s = \rho^k \frac{1 - (r/\rho)^k}{1 - r/\rho} = \frac{\rho^{k+1} - \rho r^k}{\rho - r}, \qquad \sum_{s=k+1}^\infty \rho^{s-k} r^s = r^k \frac{\rho r}{1 - \rho r}.$$
Collecting the coefficient of $r^k$ and the coefficient of $\rho^k$, we find that $(T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}\mathbf{x})_k$ is a linear combination of the two exponentials $r^k$ and $\rho^k$:
$$\left(T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}\mathbf{x}\right)_k = x_0 r^k \left[t_0^{(p)} - \frac{c_p\rho}{\rho - r} + \frac{c_p\rho r}{1 - \rho r}\right] + x_0 \frac{c_p\rho}{\rho - r}\,\rho^k \qquad (\forall\, k \ge 0).$$
Since the right-hand side of the Wiener–Hopf equation is $b_k^{(p)} = -\frac{\log p}{p}\rho^k$, the ansatz $x_s = x_0 r^s$ satisfies $(T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}\mathbf{x})_k = b_k^{(p)}$ for **all** $k \ge 0$ simultaneously if and only if two algebraic conditions hold:
1. **Cancellation of the internal mode $r^k$ (the characteristic equation):**
   $$t_0^{(p)} - \frac{c_p\rho}{\rho - r} + \frac{c_p\rho r}{1 - \rho r} = t_0^{(p)} + c_p\rho\,\frac{r(\rho - r) - (1 - \rho r)}{(\rho - r)(1 - \rho r)} = 0.$$
   Multiplying by $(\rho - r)(1 - \rho r) = \rho(1 + r^2) - (1 + \rho^2)r$ gives
   $$t_0^{(p)}\left[\rho(1 + r^2) - (1 + \rho^2)r\right] + c_p\rho\left[2\rho r - 1 - r^2\right] = 0 \iff \left(t_0^{(p)} - c_p\right)\rho(1 + r^2) = \left[t_0^{(p)}(1 + \rho^2) - 2c_p\rho^2\right]r.$$
   Since $\rho = p^{-1/2}$ and $\rho^2 = p^{-1}$, the coefficients are precisely $B_p = (t_0^{(p)} - c_p)p^{-1/2}$ and $A_p = t_0^{(p)}(1 + p^{-1}) - 2c_p p^{-1}$, yielding the reciprocal quadratic equation $B_p(r + r^{-1}) = A_p$, or $B_p r^2 - A_p r + B_p = 0$.
   To verify that $A_p > 2|B_p|$, compute $A_p \mp 2B_p = t_0^{(p)}(1 \mp \rho)^2 - 2c_p\rho(\rho \mp 1) = (1 \mp \rho)\left[t_0^{(p)}(1 \mp \rho) \pm 2c_p\rho\right] = (1 \mp \rho)^2 f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(\mp 1) > 0$, because $f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(1) > f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(-1) \ge f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(-1) - \Delta_p(K) = m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$ (at $K=1$ for $p \ge 3$ and $K=2$ for $p=2$ by Theorem 3(4), since $\Delta_p(K) > 0$). Hence $A_p^2 - 4B_p^2 = (1-\rho^2)^2 f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(-1) f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(1) > 0$, and the unique root in $(-1, 1)$ is
   $$r_p = \frac{2B_p}{A_p + \sqrt{A_p^2 - 4B_p^2}} \in (-1, 1).$$
   Moreover, at $r = \rho$, $B_p(\rho + \rho^{-1}) - A_p = -c_p(1 - \rho^2) < 0$, so $r_p < \rho$ strictly.
2. **Matching of the boundary forcing mode $\rho^k$:**
   $$x_0 \frac{c_p\rho}{\rho - r_p} = -\frac{\log p}{p} \iff x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}} = -\frac{\log p}{p}\cdot\frac{\rho - r_p}{c_p\rho} = -\frac{2p}{p-1}\left(1 - p^{1/2}r_p\right) < 0.$$
   Finally, the inner product $\langle \mathbf{b}^{(p)}, \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{KMS}} \rangle_{\ell^2} = \sum_{r=0}^\infty b_r^{(p)} x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = -\frac{\log p}{p} x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}} \sum_{r=0}^\infty (\rho r_p)^r = \left(\frac{\log p}{p}\right)^2 \frac{\rho - r_p}{c_p\rho(1 - \rho r_p)}$ gives the closed-form formula for $(d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{KMS}})^2$.

#### Proof of Part (3) (Dyadic Silver-Ratio Resonance at $p = 2$)
At $p = 2$, Theorem 3(2)–(3) gives $t_0^{(2)} = \frac{\log 2}{4}$ and $c_2 = \frac{\log 2}{8}$, so $t_0^{(2)} = 2c_2$ and $\rho = 2^{-1/2}$. Substituting $t_0^{(2)} = 2c_2$ into $A_2$ and $B_2$ causes $c_2$ (and hence $\log 2$) to factor out completely:
$$A_2 = 2c_2\left(1 + \frac{1}{2}\right) - 2c_2\cdot\frac{1}{2} = 2c_2, \qquad B_2 = (2c_2 - c_2)2^{-1/2} = \frac{c_2}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \frac{A_2}{B_2} = 2\sqrt{2}.$$
The characteristic equation $r + r^{-1} = 2\sqrt{2}$ has roots $\sqrt{2} \pm 1$, so the unique root inside $(-1, 1)$ is the **silver ratio** $r_2 = \sqrt{2} - 1$. Substituting $r_2 = \sqrt{2}-1$ into $x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}}$ and $d_{2,\infty}^{\mathrm{KMS}}$ gives
$$x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}} = -4\left(1 - \sqrt{2}(\sqrt{2}-1)\right) = -4(\sqrt{2}-1) \implies x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = -4(\sqrt{2}-1)^{r+1} \qquad (r \ge 0),$$
and since $\frac{1 - \sqrt{2}r_2}{1 - r_2/\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{1/\sqrt{2}} = 2 - \sqrt{2}$, we obtain $\left(d_{2,\infty}^{\mathrm{KMS}}\right)^2 = 1 - 2\log 2\,(2 - \sqrt{2}) = 1 - (4 - 2\sqrt{2})\log 2$.

#### Proof of Part (4) (Certified Infinite-Horizon Rayleigh–Ritz Bounds and $\ell^2$ Resolvent Stability)
For the trial vector $\mathbf{w}^{(p)} = (r_p^r)_{r \ge 0} \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$, expanding $\langle T_p \mathbf{w}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2}$ by Toeplitz diagonals gives
$$\langle T_p \mathbf{w}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2} = \sum_{s=0}^\infty t_0^{(p)} r_p^{2s} + 2\sum_{r=1}^\infty t_r^{(p)} r_p^r \sum_{s=0}^\infty r_p^{2s} = \frac{1}{1 - r_p^2}\left(t_0^{(p)} + 2\sum_{r=1}^\infty t_r^{(p)} r_p^r\right) = \frac{\Phi_p(r_p)}{1 - r_p^2}.$$
For any $K \ge 1$, Theorem 3(3) gives $|t_r^{(p)} - c_p\rho^r| \le C_p p^{-3r/2}$ for all $r \ge K$, so summing the geometric series over $r \ge K$ yields $\Phi_p^{\mathrm{LB}}(r_p; K) \le \Phi_p(r_p) \le \Phi_p^{\mathrm{UB}}(r_p; K)$. Since $\langle \mathbf{b}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2} = -\frac{\log p}{p}\frac{1}{1 - \rho r_p}$, minimizing $\|E - \alpha \sum_{r=0}^\infty r_p^r u_{p,r}\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = 1 - 2\alpha\langle \mathbf{b}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle + \alpha^2 \langle T_p \mathbf{w}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle$ over $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ gives
$$d_{p,\infty}^2 \le 1 - \frac{\langle \mathbf{b}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2}^2}{\langle T_p \mathbf{w}^{(p)}, \mathbf{w}^{(p)} \rangle_{\ell^2}} = 1 - \frac{(1 - r_p^2)(\frac{\log p}{p})^2}{(1 - \rho r_p)^2 \Phi_p(r_p)} \le \left(d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{RR,cert}}(K)\right)^2.$$
For the $\ell^2$ solution error bound at any symbol-admissible $K$ ($m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)} > 0$), note that $T_p(\mathbf{x}^{(p)} - \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{KMS}}) = (T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}} - T_p)\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{KMS}}$. Since $\|T_p^{-1}\|_{\ell^2 \to \ell^2} = m_p^{-1} \le (m_{p,\mathrm{anal}}^{(K)})^{-1}$ and $\|T_p^{\mathrm{KMS}} - T_p\|_{\ell^2 \to \ell^2} = \max_{\theta \in \mathbb{T}}|f_p^{\mathrm{KMS}}(e^{i\theta}) - f_p(e^{i\theta})| \le \Delta_p(K)$ by Schur's test / Szegő's theorem, while $\|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{KMS}}\|_{\ell^2} = |x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}}|/\sqrt{1 - r_p^2}$, the resolvent bound follows immediately.

#### Proof of Part (5) (Multi-Point Dual Lower Bound on $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1$ and Loewner Tail Bound)
For any $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1/2$, the Mellin functional $\ell_s(F) := \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}F)(s) = \int_1^\infty F(\lfloor u \rfloor) u^{-s-1}\,du = \sum_{m=1}^\infty F(m)\frac{m^{-s} - (m+1)^{-s}}{s} = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{F(m)}{m(m+1)} m(m+1)\frac{m^{-s} - (m+1)^{-s}}{s} = \langle F, e_{\bar{s}} \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$ is represented by $e_{\bar{s}} \in \mathcal{S}$ because $|(e_{\bar{s}})_m| = \left|m(m+1)\frac{m^{-\bar{s}} - (m+1)^{-\bar{s}}}{\bar{s}}\right| = O(m^{1-\operatorname{Re}(s)})$ lies in $\ell^2(\mathbb{N}, w)$ whenever $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1/2$. By Proposition 1.1(2), for every $F = \sum_{k=1}^K c_k G_{p^k} \in \mathcal{W}_p^{(K)}$ with polynomial $P(z) := \sum_{k=1}^K c_k z^k$, we have $\langle F, e_{\bar{s}} \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{P(p^{-s}) - P(p^{-1})}{s}\zeta(s)$. At every $p$-adic vertical grid point $s = s_j^{(p)} = 1 + \frac{2\pi i j}{\log p}$ ($j \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$), we have $p^{-s_j^{(p)}} = p^{-1} e^{-2\pi i j} = p^{-1}$ and $s_j^{(p)} \ne 1$, so $\zeta(s_j^{(p)})$ is finite and $P(p^{-s_j^{(p)}}) - P(p^{-1}) = 0$ identically for **every** polynomial $P$. By continuity of the inner product on $\mathcal{S}$, $e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}} \in \mathcal{W}_p^\perp$ for all $j \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$. For any finite subset $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, the orthogonal projection of $E$ onto $\mathcal{U}_\Lambda := \operatorname{span}\{e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}} : j \in \Lambda\} \subset \mathcal{W}_p^\perp$ is $P_{\mathcal{U}_\Lambda} E = \sum_{j \in \Lambda} \alpha_j e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}}$, where $\sum_{\ell \in \Lambda} \langle e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}}, e_{\bar{s}_\ell^{(p)}} \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} \alpha_\ell = \langle e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}}, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \overline{\langle E, e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}} \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}} = \frac{1}{\overline{s_j^{(p)}}} =: (\mathbf{u}_\Lambda)_j$, so $K_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\alpha} = \mathbf{u}_\Lambda$ and $d_{p,\infty}^2 = \|P_{\mathcal{W}_p^\perp} E\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 \ge \|P_{\mathcal{U}_\Lambda} E\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = \mathbf{u}_\Lambda^* K_\Lambda^{-1} \mathbf{u}_\Lambda$ (which also equals $\mathbf{v}_\Lambda^* \overline{K_\Lambda}^{-1} \mathbf{v}_\Lambda$ with $(\mathbf{v}_\Lambda)_j = 1/s_j^{(p)}$ since $K_\Lambda$ is Hermitian).

Finally, for any $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1$ and $m \ge 1$, $|u^{-\bar{s}-1}| = u^{-2}$ on $[m, m+1]$, so the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
$$\left|\frac{m^{-\bar{s}} - (m+1)^{-\bar{s}}}{\bar{s}}\right| = \left|\int_m^{m+1} u^{-\bar{s}-1}\,du\right| \le \int_m^{m+1} u^{-2}\,du = \frac{1}{m(m+1)}, \qquad \text{whence} \qquad |(e_{\bar{s}})_m| = m(m+1)\left|\frac{m^{-\bar{s}} - (m+1)^{-\bar{s}}}{\bar{s}}\right| \le 1 \quad (\forall\, m \ge 1).$$
Thus for every $m \ge 1$ and $\operatorname{Re}(s_j^{(p)}) = 1$, $|(e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}})_m| \le 1$, so the tail Gram matrix $K_\Lambda^{>M_{\mathrm{dual}}} := K_\Lambda - K_{\Lambda, M_{\mathrm{dual}}}$ is positive semidefinite with trace
$$\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(K_\Lambda^{>M_{\mathrm{dual}}}\right) = \sum_{j \in \Lambda} \sum_{m=M_{\mathrm{dual}}+1}^\infty \frac{|(e_{\bar{s}_j^{(p)}})_m|^2}{m(m+1)} \le |\Lambda| \sum_{m=M_{\mathrm{dual}}+1}^\infty \frac{1}{m(m+1)} = \frac{|\Lambda|}{M_{\mathrm{dual}}+1}.$$
Since every positive semidefinite matrix is bounded above in Loewner order by its trace times the identity, $0 \preceq K_\Lambda^{>M_{\mathrm{dual}}} \preceq \frac{|\Lambda|}{M_{\mathrm{dual}}+1} I_{|\Lambda|}$, and matrix-inversion monotonicity on positive definite matrices gives $K_\Lambda^{-1} \succeq \big(K_{\Lambda, M_{\mathrm{dual}}} + \frac{|\Lambda|}{M_{\mathrm{dual}}+1} I_{|\Lambda|}\big)^{-1}$. $\square$

## 6. Universal Cross-Dilation Abel Inequality, Flagged Prime-Dilation Basis $\{W_\ell\}$, and the Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge (Theorem 5)

Having resolved the structure of each single-prime fiber $\mathcal{W}_p$, we now turn to the full multi-prime Báez-Duarte subspace $\mathcal{V}_N = \operatorname{span}\{G_2, \dots, G_N\}$. Theorem 5 establishes three multi-prime results: (1) a universal cross-dilation Abel inequality governing the inner product of any two dilated generators $V_n G_p$ and $V_m G_q$, (2) the conditioning improvement of the **flagged prime-dilation basis** $W_\ell := G_\ell - p(\ell)^{-1} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}$ ($\ell \ge 2$), and (3) an exact algebraic **zero-at-$1$ sieve bridge** that links any Dirichlet polynomial $Q(s) = 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \ell^{-s}$ satisfying $Q(1) = 0$ directly to the cumulative Dirichlet convolution $\sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k)$.

### 6.1 Statement of Theorem 5

**Theorem 5 (Universal Cross-Dilation Abel Bound, Flagged Basis Conditioning, and the Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge).**
1. **Universal Cross-Dilation Two-Stage Abel Bound, Period-Aligned Sandwich, and GCD Scale Cancellation:** *Because $V_n V_m = V_{nm}$ and $V_d^* V_d = I_{\mathcal{S}}$ (Theorem 2), for any integers $n, m \ge 1$ with greatest common divisor $d := \gcd(n, m)$ and any $F, H \in \mathcal{S}$, the dilated inner product is invariant under common scale division:*
   $$\langle V_n F, V_m H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_d V_{n/d} F, V_d V_{m/d} H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_{n/d} F, V_{m/d} H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}.$$
   *Moreover, for any integers $p, q \ge 2$ and any dilation scales $1 \le n \le m$, let $c_{p,q} := \alpha_p \beta_q = \frac{(p-1)\log q}{2pq}$ denote the rank-$1$ product of the period-$p$ mean $\alpha_p := \frac{p-1}{2p}$ and the target inner product $\beta_q := -\langle G_q, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{\log q}{q}$. Then the inner product between the normalized dilated generators $V_n G_p = n^{1/2}(G_{np} - p^{-1} G_n)$ and $V_m G_q = m^{1/2}(G_{mq} - q^{-1} G_m)$ obeys two explicit non-asymptotic bounds:*
   - *(a) **Unconditional General Cross-Dilation Bound ($1 \le n \le m$):** For all $1 \le n \le m$ and all $p, q \ge 2$,*
     $$\left|\langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} - c_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2}\right| \le \widetilde{C}_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{3/2} \qquad (\forall\, 1 \le n \le m,\; p,q \ge 2),$$
     *where $\widetilde{C}_{p,q}$ and $\widetilde{S}_q$ (with $\psi_1(z) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty (j+z)^{-2}$ denoting the trigamma function) are given in closed form by*
     $$\widetilde{C}_{p,q} := \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}\,\widetilde{S}_q, \qquad \widetilde{S}_q := \sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{\{i/q\}}{i^2} = \frac{1}{q^3}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1} r\,\psi_1\!\left(\frac{r}{q}\right) \qquad \left(\widetilde{S}_2 = \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right).$$
   - *(b) **Sharpened Period-Aligned Cross-Dilation Sandwich ($pn \mid m$):** Whenever the fine period $pn$ divides the coarse scale $m$ ($pn \mid m$), the boundary terms of summation by parts on every $m$-block vanish identically, and the inner product satisfies the strictly positive two-sided sandwich*
     $$0 < c_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2} - \langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} \le C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{3/2} \qquad (\forall\, pn \mid m,\; p,q \ge 2),$$
     *where $C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}}$ and $S_q$ are given by*
     $$C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}} := \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}\,S_q = \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor (q-1)\pi^2}{12 p q^2}, \qquad S_q := \sum_{i=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{i}{q}\right\}\left(\frac{1}{i^2} - \frac{1}{(i+1)^2}\right) = \frac{(q-1)\pi^2}{6q^2},$$
     *recovering Theorem 3(3) in the single-prime case $p = q$, $n = 1$, $m = p^r$ ($r \ge 1$).*
2. **Triangular Basis Equivalence of the Flagged Prime-Dilation Basis $\{W_\ell\}$:** *For every integer $\ell \ge 2$, let $p(\ell)$ denote the smallest prime factor of $\ell$, and define the flagged generator*
   $$W_\ell := G_\ell - \frac{1}{p(\ell)} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)} = \left(\frac{\ell}{p(\ell)}\right)^{-1/2} V_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}.$$
   *Then for every truncation level $N \ge 2$, the transition matrix $L_N$ defined by $\mathbf{W}_N = L_N \mathbf{G}_N$ is unit lower-triangular with at most two nonzero entries per row ($1$ on the diagonal and $-1/p(\ell)$ in column $\ell/p(\ell)$ when $\ell$ is composite), so $\operatorname{span}\{W_2, \dots, W_N\} = \operatorname{span}\{G_2, \dots, G_N\} = \mathcal{V}_N$. Moreover, for any prime set $\mathcal{P}$, the $\mathcal{P}$-smooth submodule $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}^{(N)} := \operatorname{span}\{G_\ell : 2 \le \ell \le N,\; p \mid \ell \Rightarrow p \in \mathcal{P}\}$ is spanned by $\{W_\ell : 2 \le \ell \le N,\; p \mid \ell \Rightarrow p \in \mathcal{P}\}$.*
3. **Exact Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge, Boundedness Criterion for the Cumulative Sieve Sum, First-Step Inequality, and Lagrange Rank-1 Distance Formula:** *Associate to any coefficient vector $\mathbf{c} = (c_2, \dots, c_N)^\top \in \mathbb{C}^{N-1}$ (with $c_1 := 1$ and $c_\ell := 0$ for $\ell > N$) the Dirichlet polynomial $Q(s) := \sum_{\ell=1}^N c_\ell \ell^{-s} = 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \ell^{-s}$, the step-space approximation vector*
   $$Z(Q) := \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell G_\ell \in \mathcal{V}_N,$$
   *and the **cumulative Dirichlet convolution (sieve sum)** $S_c(m) := \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = -1 + \sum_{k=1}^m (c * 1)(k)$, where $(c * 1)(k) := \sum_{d \mid k} c_d$ is the Dirichlet convolution of $c$ with the constant function $1$. Then:*
   - *(a) **Unconditional Boundedness of the Step Residual $Z(Q) - E$:** Because $G_\ell(m) = -\{m/\ell\} \in (-1, 0]$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ for every $\ell \ge 2$, the pointwise step error $(Z(Q) - E)_m = -1 - \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \{m/\ell\}$ is **always** bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ (satisfying $\|Z(Q) - E\|_{\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})} \le 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N |c_\ell| < \infty$) and always belongs to $\mathcal{S} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, \frac{1}{m(m+1)})$ for every finite coefficient vector $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{C}^{N-1}$, whether or not $Q(1) = 0$.*
   - *(b) **Exact Arithmetic Decomposition and First-Step Inequality:** For every $m \in \mathbb{N}$, the pointwise step error $(Z(Q) - E)_m$ and the cumulative sieve sum $S_c(m)$ satisfy the exact arithmetic identity*
     $$(Z(Q) - E)_m = -m\,Q(1) + \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = -m\,Q(1) + S_c(m) \qquad (\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}).$$
     *Evaluating this identity at $m = 1$ (where $S_c(1) = 0$) yields the exact first-step identity $(E - Z(Q))_1 = Q(1)$, which implies the unconditional Hilbert-space bound*
     $$|Q(1)| = |(E - Z(Q))_1| \le \sqrt{2}\,\|E - Z(Q)\|_{\mathcal{S}}, \qquad \text{and in particular} \qquad |Q_N^*(1)| \le \sqrt{2}\,d_N.$$
   - *(c) **Boundedness Criterion for the Cumulative Sieve Sum $S_c(m)$:** Since $(Z(Q) - E)_m = O(1)$ on $\mathbb{N}$ for every $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{C}^{N-1}$, the cumulative sieve sum $S_c(m) = \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = m\,Q(1) + (Z(Q) - E)_m$ has linear drift $m\,Q(1) + O(1)$. Consequently, the cumulative sieve sum $S_c = (S_c(m))_{m \ge 1}$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ (and belongs to the Báez-Duarte step space $\mathcal{S}$, and coincides pointwise with the step error $Z(Q) - E$) **if and only if** $Q(s)$ satisfies the **zero-at-$1$ sieve condition***
     $$Q(1) = 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N \frac{c_\ell}{\ell} = 0 \iff \sum_{\ell=2}^N \frac{c_\ell}{\ell} = -1,$$
     *in which case the linear drift $m\,Q(1)$ vanishes identically, giving*
     $$(Z(Q) - E)_m = S_c(m) = \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = -1 + \sum_{k=1}^m (c * 1)(k) \qquad (\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}),$$
     *and the exact Mellin transform of Proposition 1.1(2), $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}(E - Z(Q)))(s) = \frac{1 - (Q(s) - Q(1))\zeta(s)}{s}$, simplifies directly to the classical mollifier error $\frac{1 - Q(s)\zeta(s)}{s}$ (where $Q(1) = 0$ cancels the simple pole of $\zeta(s)$ at $s=1$). Finally, the minimum squared distance $\big(d_N^{(0)}\big)^2 := \min_{\mathbf{c} : Q(1)=0} \|E - Z(Q)\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$ under the linear constraint $\mathbf{a}_N^\top \mathbf{c} = -1$ (where $(\mathbf{a}_N)_\ell = 1/\ell$) is related to the unconstrained minimum $d_N^2 = 1 - \mathbf{b}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{b}_N$ (attained at $\mathbf{c}_N^* = G_N^{-1}\mathbf{b}_N$ with $Q_N^*(1) := 1 + \mathbf{a}_N^\top \mathbf{c}_N^*$) by the exact rank-1 Lagrange identity*
     $$\left(d_N^{(0)}\right)^2 = d_N^2 + \frac{\left(Q_N^*(1)\right)^2}{\mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1} \mathbf{a}_N}, \qquad \mathbf{c}_N^{(0)} = \mathbf{c}_N^* - \frac{Q_N^*(1)}{\mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N}\,G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N.$$

### 6.2 Proof of Theorem 5

#### Proof of Part (1) (Universal Cross-Dilation Two-Stage Abel Bound, Period-Aligned Sandwich, and GCD Scale Cancellation)
For any $n, m \ge 1$ with $d = \gcd(n,m)$, Theorem 2(1)–(2) gives $V_n = V_d V_{n/d}$, $V_m = V_d V_{m/d}$, and $V_d^* V_d = I_{\mathcal{S}}$, so $\langle V_n F, V_m H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_{n/d} F, V_d^* V_d V_{m/d} H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = \langle V_{n/d} F, V_{m/d} H \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$. Now fix $p, q \ge 2$ and $1 \le n \le m$. Since $(V_m G_q)(k) = -m^{1/2}\{\lfloor k/m \rfloor / q\}$ vanishes for $1 \le k < m$ and takes the constant value $-m^{1/2}\{i/q\}$ on each $m$-block $k \in [im, (i+1)m - 1]$ ($i \ge 1$), we can write
$$\langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} = (nm)^{1/2} \sum_{i=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{i}{q}\right\} \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-1} \left\{\frac{\lfloor k/n \rfloor}{p}\right\} w(k), \qquad w(k) = \frac{1}{k(k+1)}.$$
In the inner sum over $k \in [im, (i+1)m - 1]$, decompose $\left\{\lfloor k/n \rfloor / p\right\} = \frac{p-1}{2p} + \delta_{\lfloor k/n \rfloor}$, where $\delta_j := \{j/p\} - \frac{p-1}{2p}$ has period $p$ and zero mean over every complete period of length $p$.
1. **Contribution of the mean $\frac{p-1}{2p}$:** Since $\sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-1} w(k) = \frac{1}{im} - \frac{1}{(i+1)m} = \frac{1}{m} w(i)$, the mean term contributes
   $$(nm)^{1/2} \frac{p-1}{2p} \sum_{i=1}^\infty \left\{\frac{i}{q}\right\} \frac{1}{m} w(i) = \left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2} \frac{p-1}{2p} \left(-\langle G_q, E \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}\right) = \frac{(p-1)\log q}{2pq}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2} = c_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2}.$$
2. **Exact summation by parts of the zero-mean fluctuation via the global $pn$-periodic primitive:** Define the global cumulative fluctuation sum starting at $r = 0$:
   $$\Sigma(k) := \sum_{r=0}^k \delta_{\lfloor r/n \rfloor} \quad (k \ge 0), \qquad \Sigma(-1) := 0, \qquad M_p := \frac{\lfloor p^2/4 \rfloor}{2p}.$$
   Because $\delta_{\lfloor r/n \rfloor} = \delta_j$ is constant on each $n$-block $r \in [jn, (j+1)n - 1]$ and $\sum_{s=0}^{a} \delta_s = -\frac{(a+1)(p-1-a)}{2p} \in [-M_p, 0]$ for $0 \le a < p$ with $\sum_{s=0}^{p-1} \delta_s = 0$, the sequence $\Sigma(k)$ is periodic with period $pn$, vanishes at the end of every complete $pn$-period ($\Sigma(k) = 0$ whenever $pn \mid (k+1)$), and is piecewise monotone on each $n$-block between its block-endpoint values $\Sigma(jn - 1) \in [-M_p n, 0]$. Therefore, $\Sigma(k)$ satisfies the exact two-sided global bound
   $$-M_p n \le \Sigma(k) \le 0 \qquad (\forall\, k \ge 0).$$
   Applying Abel summation by parts with $\delta_{\lfloor k/n \rfloor} = \Sigma(k) - \Sigma(k-1)$ on the $m$-block $k \in [im, (i+1)m - 1]$ yields the exact identity
   $$R_i := \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-1} \delta_{\lfloor k/n \rfloor}\, w(k) = \Sigma((i+1)m-1)\,w((i+1)m-1) - \Sigma(im-1)\,w(im) + \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-2} \Sigma(k)\big(w(k) - w(k+1)\big).$$
   We now read off both regimes (a) and (b) directly from this identity:
   - **Case (a) (Unconditional general regime $1 \le n \le m$):** Since $w(k) - w(k+1) > 0$ and $-M_p n \le \Sigma(k) \le 0$ for all $k$, the sum of the first and third terms on the right-hand side is non-positive and bounded below by
     $$-M_p n\left(w((i+1)m-1) + \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-2}\big(w(k) - w(k+1)\big)\right) = -M_p n\, w(im) \le \Sigma((i+1)m-1)\,w((i+1)m-1) + \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-2} \Sigma(k)\big(w(k) - w(k+1)\big) \le 0,$$
     whereas the second term $-\Sigma(im-1)\,w(im)$ is non-negative and lies in $[0,\, M_p n\, w(im)]$. Because the sum of two numbers in $[-M_p n\,w(im),\, 0]$ and $[0,\, M_p n\,w(im)]$ lies in $[-M_p n\,w(im),\, M_p n\,w(im)]$, we obtain for every $i \ge 1$:
     $$|R_i| \le M_p n\, w(im) = \frac{M_p n}{im(im+1)} < \frac{M_p n}{m^2}\,\frac{1}{i^2}.$$
     Multiplying by $(nm)^{1/2}\{i/q\}$ and summing over $i \ge 1$ gives
     $$\left|\langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} - c_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2}\right| \le (nm)^{1/2}\frac{M_p n}{m^2}\sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{\{i/q\}}{i^2} = \widetilde{C}_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{3/2},$$
     where writing $i = kq + r$ with $k \ge 0$ and $1 \le r \le q-1$ evaluates $\widetilde{S}_q = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{\{i/q\}}{i^2} = \sum_{r=1}^{q-1} \frac{r}{q} \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(kq+r)^2} = \frac{1}{q^3}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1} r\,\psi_1(r/q)$ (with $\widetilde{S}_2 = \frac{1}{8}\psi_1(1/2) = \frac{\pi^2}{16}$).
   - **Case (b) (Sharpened period-aligned regime $pn \mid m$):** When $pn \mid m$, every block endpoint $im - 1$ and $(i+1)m - 1$ satisfies $pn \mid im$ and $pn \mid (i+1)m$, so both boundary primitives vanish identically: $\Sigma(im-1) = \Sigma((i+1)m-1) = 0$ for all $i \ge 1$. The identity for $R_i$ then simplifies to the purely interior sum
     $$R_i = \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-2} \Sigma(k)\big(w(k) - w(k+1)\big) < 0,$$
     which is strictly negative (since $\Sigma(k) \le 0$ is non-trivial on every $pn$-period and $w(k) - w(k+1) > 0$) and satisfies
     $$0 < -R_i \le M_p n \sum_{k=im}^{(i+1)m-2}\big(w(k) - w(k+1)\big) = M_p n\big(w(im) - w((i+1)m-1)\big) \le \frac{M_p n}{m^2}\left(\frac{1}{i^2} - \frac{1}{(i+1)^2}\right),$$
     where the last step uses $w(im) - w((i+1)m-1) < \frac{1}{i^2 m^2} - \frac{1}{(i+1)^2 m^2}$ (verified in the proof of Theorem 3(3)). Multiplying by $(nm)^{1/2}\{i/q\}$ and summing over $i \ge 1$ yields the two-sided sandwich
     $$0 < c_{p,q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{1/2} - \langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}} \le C_{p,q}^{\mathrm{align}}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{3/2},$$
     where $S_q = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \{i/q\}(i^{-2} - (i+1)^{-2}) = \frac{(q-1)\pi^2}{6q^2}$ by Theorem 3(3).

#### Proof of Part (2) (Triangular Equivalence of $\{W_\ell\}$)
By Lemma 2.1(3) with $n = \ell/p(\ell)$ and $j = p(\ell)$, we have $W_\ell = G_\ell - p(\ell)^{-1} G_{\ell/p(\ell)} = M_{\ell/p(\ell)} G_{p(\ell)}$. Since $\ell/p(\ell) < \ell$ for every $\ell \ge 2$, the matrix $L_N$ expressing $(W_2, \dots, W_N)^\top = L_N(G_2, \dots, G_N)^\top$ is unit lower-triangular ($\det L_N = 1$), so it is an isomorphism of $\mathcal{V}_N$ onto itself. Moreover, by induction on $\ell$, if every prime factor of $\ell$ lies in $\mathcal{P}$, then the same holds for $\ell/p(\ell)$, so $G_\ell = W_\ell + p(\ell)^{-1} G_{\ell/p(\ell)}$ lies in $\operatorname{span}\{W_k : 2 \le k \le \ell,\; p \mid k \Rightarrow p \in \mathcal{P}\}$.

#### Proof of Part (3) (Exact Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge, Boundedness Criterion for $S_c(m)$, First-Step Inequality, and Lagrange Rank-1 Identity)
First, since $0 \le \{m/\ell\} < 1$ for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\ell \ge 2$, the triangle inequality gives
$$|(Z(Q) - E)_m| = \left|-1 - \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \left\{\frac{m}{\ell}\right\}\right| \le 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N |c_\ell| < \infty \qquad (\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}),$$
so $Z(Q) - E \in \ell^\infty(\mathbb{N}) \subset \mathcal{S}$ unconditionally for every $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{C}^{N-1}$, proving (a). Next, for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\ell \ge 2$, substitute $G_\ell(m) = -\{m/\ell\} = \lfloor m/\ell \rfloor - \frac{m}{\ell}$ into $Z(Q)_m = \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell G_\ell(m)$, and use $c_1 = 1$ with $\lfloor m/1 \rfloor - \frac{m}{1} = 0$:
$$Z(Q)_m = \sum_{\ell=1}^N c_\ell \left(\left\lfloor \frac{m}{\ell} \right\rfloor - \frac{m}{\ell}\right) = \sum_{\ell=1}^N c_\ell \left\lfloor \frac{m}{\ell} \right\rfloor - m\sum_{\ell=1}^N \frac{c_\ell}{\ell}.$$
By the fundamental identity of Dirichlet divisor summation, writing $\lfloor m/\ell \rfloor = \sum_{1 \le q \le m/\ell} 1$ and grouping by $k = q\ell \in \{1, 2, \dots, m\}$ gives
$$\sum_{\ell=1}^N c_\ell \left\lfloor \frac{m}{\ell} \right\rfloor = \sum_{k=1}^m \sum_{\ell \mid k} c_\ell = \sum_{k=1}^m (c * 1)(k) = 1 + \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = 1 + S_c(m),$$
since $(c * 1)(1) = c_1 = 1$. Subtracting $E(m) = 1$ and noting that $\sum_{\ell=1}^N \frac{c_\ell}{\ell} = Q(1)$ yields the exact identity
$$(Z(Q) - E)_m = -m\,Q(1) + \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = -m\,Q(1) + S_c(m) \qquad (\forall\, m \in \mathbb{N}).$$
At $m = 1$, the sum over $2 \le k \le 1$ is empty ($S_c(1) = 0$; or directly, $G_\ell(1) = -1/\ell$ for all $\ell \ge 2$), so $(E - Z(Q))_1 = 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N \frac{c_\ell}{\ell} = Q(1)$. Since the first term of the norm in $\mathcal{S}$ has weight $w_1 = \frac{1}{1\cdot 2} = \frac{1}{2}$, we have $\frac{1}{2}|Q(1)|^2 = w_1|(E - Z(Q))_1|^2 \le \|E - Z(Q)\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2$, which gives $|Q(1)| \le \sqrt{2}\,\|E - Z(Q)\|_{\mathcal{S}}$, proving (b).

To prove (c), rewrite the decomposition of (b) as $S_c(m) = m\,Q(1) + (Z(Q) - E)_m$. Since $\|Z(Q) - E\|_{\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})} \le 1 + \sum_{\ell=2}^N |c_\ell| < \infty$ by (a), if $Q(1) \ne 0$, then $|S_c(m)| \ge m|Q(1)| - \|Z(Q) - E\|_{\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})} \to \infty$ as $m \to \infty$, so $S_c$ is unbounded on $\mathbb{N}$ and $\sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{|S_c(m)|^2}{m(m+1)} = \infty$ (since $\frac{m^2 |Q(1)|^2}{m(m+1)} \to |Q(1)|^2 > 0$), whence $S_c \notin \mathcal{S}$. (For example, taking $N=2$ and $c_2 = 1$ gives $Q(1) = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} \ne 0$: here $(Z(Q)-E)_m = -1 - \{m/2\} \in \{-1, -3/2\}$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$, whereas the cumulative sieve sum $S_c(m) = \sum_{k=2}^m (c*1)(k) = (m-1) + \lfloor m/2 \rfloor = \frac{3}{2}m - 1 - \{m/2\}$ grows linearly with slope $Q(1) = 3/2$.) Conversely, when $Q(1) = 0$, the linear drift $m\,Q(1)$ vanishes identically, leaving $S_c(m) = (Z(Q) - E)_m \in \ell^\infty(\mathbb{N}) \subset \mathcal{S}$ for all $m \in \mathbb{N}$, and Proposition 1.1(2) gives $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}(E - Z(Q)))(s) = \frac{1 - (Q(s) - Q(1))\zeta(s)}{s} = \frac{1 - Q(s)\zeta(s)}{s}$. Finally, minimizing the quadratic form $J(\mathbf{c}) := \|E - Z(Q)\|_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = 1 - 2\mathbf{b}_N^\top \mathbf{c} + \mathbf{c}^\top G_N \mathbf{c}$ subject to $\mathbf{a}_N^\top \mathbf{c} = -1$ via the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{c}, \lambda) = J(\mathbf{c}) + 2\lambda(\mathbf{a}_N^\top \mathbf{c} + 1)$ gives $G_N \mathbf{c}_N^{(0)} = \mathbf{b}_N - \lambda \mathbf{a}_N \implies \mathbf{c}_N^{(0)} = \mathbf{c}_N^* - \lambda G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N$. Enforcing $\mathbf{a}_N^\top \mathbf{c}_N^{(0)} = -1$ yields $\lambda = \frac{\mathbf{a}_N^\top \mathbf{c}_N^* + 1}{\mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N} = \frac{Q_N^*(1)}{\mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N}$, and substituting $\mathbf{c}_N^{(0)}$ into $J(\mathbf{c}) = d_N^2 + (\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{c}_N^*)^\top G_N (\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{c}_N^*)$ gives $(d_N^{(0)})^2 = d_N^2 + \lambda^2 \mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N = d_N^2 + \frac{(Q_N^*(1))^2}{\mathbf{a}_N^\top G_N^{-1}\mathbf{a}_N}$. $\square$

## 7. Discussion and Open Directions

The five theorems proved in this paper establish that the Báez-Duarte step space $\mathcal{S} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, \frac{1}{m(m+1)})$ is not an unstructured collection of periodic fractional-part sequences, but a rigid operator-theoretic module governed by the integer-floor dilation semigroup $V_n = n^{1/2} M_n$:

1. **Resolution of Single-Prime Ill-Conditioning via Base-$p$ Digit Toeplitz Operators:** On every single-prime fiber $\mathcal{W}_p$, the normalized base-$p$ digit basis $u_{p,r} = V_p^r G_p$ ($r \ge 0$) converts the exponentially ill-conditioned Gram matrix $G_p^{(K)}$ into an exact symmetric Toeplitz operator $T_p$ whose Wiener symbol $f_p(e^{i\theta})$ is strictly positive on $\mathbb{T}$ for every prime $p \ge 2$ (Theorem 3), whose exact semi-infinite Wiener–Hopf system $T_p \mathbf{x}^{(p)} = \mathbf{b}^{(p)}$ and infinite-dimensional distance $d_{p,\infty}$ are solved in closed integral form via Szegő–Krein–Gohberg–Devinatz Wiener–Hopf factorization $T_p = T_{\overline{\psi_p}} T_{\psi_p}$ and the Poisson-integral identity with explicit two-stage admissibility and 30-digit `mpmath.iv` interval certification (Theorem 4(1)), and whose KMS Wiener–Hopf resolvent is solvable in exact single-exponential closed form $x_r^{\mathrm{KMS}} = x_0^{\mathrm{KMS}} r_p^r$ with the silver-ratio resonance $r_2 = \sqrt{2}-1$ at $p=2$ (Theorem 4(2)–(3)).
2. **From Single-Prime Toeplitz Blocks to Multi-Prime Block-Toeplitz Scattering:** Because $V_n V_m = V_{nm}$ is a commutative semigroup of isometries generated by the primes $\{V_p\}_{p \text{ prime}}$, the full space $\mathcal{V}_\infty = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{G_\ell : \ell \ge 2\}$ can be viewed as a multi-prime dilation module in which each prime $p$ acts by a Toeplitz shift $V_p$ and distinct primes $p \ne q$ interact through the cross-dilation kernel $\langle V_n G_p, V_m G_q \rangle_{\mathcal{S}}$ bounded in Theorem 5(1). Extending our single-prime Szegő and KMS Wiener–Hopf resolvents (Theorem 4) to two-prime and multi-prime $\mathcal{P}$-smooth submodules $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}$ via multi-index Wiener–Hopf factorization is a natural next step.
3. **Analytic Continuation of the Zero-at-$1$ Sieve Bridge:** Theorem 5(3) demonstrates that while the step residual $(Z(Q)-E)_m = -1 - \sum_{\ell=2}^N c_\ell \{m/\ell\}$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ for every finite vector $\mathbf{c}$, the cumulative Dirichlet convolution $S_c(m) = \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k) = m\,Q(1) + (Z(Q)-E)_m$ is bounded on $\mathbb{N}$ (and belongs to $\mathcal{S}$) if and only if $\mathbf{c}$ lies in the codimension-$1$ hyperplane $Q(1) = 0$. Restricting $\mathcal{V}_N$ to $Q(1) = 0$ eliminates the linear drift $m\,Q(1)$ of $S_c(m)$, equates the pointwise step residual $(Z(Q)-E)_m$ to the cumulative sieve sum $S_c(m) = \sum_{k=2}^m (c * 1)(k)$, aligns the effective Mellin multiplier $Q(s) - Q(1)$ with the standard mollifier $Q(s)$, and incurs a finite-horizon distance penalty $d_N^{(0)} - d_N = 0.001871$ at $N=60$. This provides a direct Hilbert-space bridge between Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte approximation in $\mathcal{S}$ and classical multiplicative sieve weights $(c * 1)(k)$ satisfying $\sum_{\ell \le N} c_\ell/\ell = -1$ (such as Selberg and Báez-Duarte mollifier weights $c_\ell \approx \mu(\ell)(1 - \frac{\log \ell}{\log N})$).
