# Accept

This revision satisfactorily addresses the corrections and attribution issues in the accepted version. It preserves the existing contribution—gauge rigidity, quantitative onset of positivity failure, and sharp smoothing thresholds—without introducing a new substantive result or materially expanding the paper's scope.

The corrected statement after Corollary 6.1 is justified by the existing estimates. For $1\le a\le5/4$, equation (6.2) gives

$$
\operatorname{Tr}S(t)\ge e^{-66t}\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-t},
$$

so the trace diverges for $0<t\le1$. For $t>1$, equation (6.3) and the established smoothing bound give

$$
\operatorname{Tr}S(t)=\int_{I_a}D_{t/2}(x)\,dx\le2a\,B_2(t/2)^2<\infty.
$$

Thus the trace-class threshold is $t>1$, whereas the Hilbert–Schmidt threshold is $t>1/2$, since $\|S(t)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\operatorname{Tr}S(2t)$. The revision also correctly distinguishes an integrated spectral diagonal from its essential supremum; trace finiteness alone does not supply the pointwise smoothing estimate.

The attribution to [16] is now accurate and specific. Its version 1, Section 3.2, equations (3.7)–(3.8), indeed establishes the time-one $L^2\to L^\infty$ estimate and bounded eigenfunctions using part-semigroup domination and the Dyson expansion for the signed perturbation. The revised text appropriately locates the additional smoothing content in the exact time intervals, endpoint failures, norm identity, and matching divergence exponents.

“Asymptotically extremal” in Theorem D(c) correctly describes what the proof establishes: attainment of the leading coefficient as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, rather than exact maximization at each positive $\varepsilon$. The corresponding supplement changes alter explanatory wording only; inspection confirms unchanged executable calculations. No numerical experiments were rerun for this revision.

The resulting manuscript remains coherent, with the central arguments and scope clearly explained. The repeated attribution passages could be shortened editorially, but do not obstruct understanding. These corrections warrant acceptance as an update to the existing paper. The work remains a partial contribution and does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
