# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive conditional advance on the remaining period-index problem in the proposed RH framework. Its principal contribution is a bound for deleting an entire marked rank-two sector that does not grow with the number of distinct eigenpackets. This improves on the single-operator, weighted-spectrum criteria in Paper 202, rather than merely restating them. Acceptance concerns the stated projection estimates under the fitted comparisons. The manuscript neither proves RH nor supplies the compatible global family required by Paper III, and it correctly says so.

The central proof is coherent. Residual multiplicity and polarization identify the two endpoint characters; the small-weight crystalline argument identifies their labelled weights and ordinary flags. The two polynomial Chinese-remainder constructions isolate the marked representation while retaining integral word traces, without assuming that the matrices commute. Determinant interpolation and class-group averaging remove the nontrivial determinants. For the remaining labels, quadratic descent, the residual choice of descent, and the trace identities bring the Hilbert Hecke values into the original order. This last point is essential: an annihilator in an unrelated larger order would not suffice for the claimed lattice estimate.

The Eisenstein step supplies the required scalar rather than a separate charge for each cusp system. The prime-to-$p$ coefficient-saturation argument uses the partial-theta divisibility criterion and minimal-weight cone in the stated strict range. The cited Diamond–Sasaki theorem and coefficient formula support this use. Biduality then realizes the constant functional as $C_d e_{\mathrm{Eis}}$, and the Dasgupta–Kakde normalization gives the asserted constant. The projection quotient in §6 is indeed annihilated by the retained scalar and has at most $\dim U$ generators. The rational numerator estimate and the coefficient-place rank sum consequently yield (1)–(2), including arbitrary multiplicities. The polynomial bound is negligible against the stipulated exponential size allowance.

The supplements make meaningful extensions with explicit limitations. The split-type argument separates its pre-projection determinant width from the smaller width available afterwards; its torsion-map estimate is uniform in the number of exterior representations. The ramified argument appropriately uses ramification index two, a separate missing-weight separator, and the stricter Hilbert weight range. The Caruso–Savitt polygon inequality, Liu's torsion comparison, and Gao–Liu's integral amplified Sen operator support the respective steps; ordinary unramified Fontaine–Laffaille theory is not silently substituted at these primes. Appendix F's inertia test also works without commutativity: dimension intersections produce the required eigenvalues, and conservation of total multiplicity produces both vanishing evaluations. The descent and Frobenius catalogues in Appendices D–F impose their stated restrictions; they do not bound the entire surviving index.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the published snapshot. Paper 200 already supplies the length-$t$ class-group projection and the general scalar-ideal method. Paper 202 supplies sharp single-trace lengths and conditional weighted counts. The present work adds the rank-two descent and uniform scalar bound, together with the supplementary local estimates and simultaneous tests. The general connection between Hilbert Eisenstein constants and congruence modules is older: Shih [19] explicitly constructs constant-term Hecke operators and computes ordinary congruence modules. That work should be cited, but it does not itself supply the transfer to this projected period lattice or the full collection of local estimates here. The novelty should remain framed around those contributions, not the general constant-term principle.

The scientific argument is readable for the intended specialist, and its essential proofs are available in the manuscript and supplements. This assessment is based on the natural-language arguments and focused checks against the mathematical sources; it is not a kernel verification of the released Lean construction. The fitted lattice, attachment, level, and uniform-family comparisons remain hypotheses wherever the manuscript declares them.

Useful minor improvements are:

- Begin the metadata abstract with the period-index question and the improvement over counting individual eigenpackets. Its current opening assumes substantial familiarity with the series.
- Add a compact table of covered sectors, coefficient-prime ranges, additional hypotheses, and remaining sectors. This would make the several distinct weight inequalities easier to locate.
- Distinguish locators in published Paper III/v1 from additions in its linked reading edition consistently. Several displayed-equation locators refer only to the latter; a pinned copy would improve reproducibility.

[19] Sheng-Chi Shih, *On congruence modules related to Hilbert Eisenstein series*, Mathematische Zeitschrift (2020), DOI: [10.1007/s00209-020-02486-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-020-02486-9); [arXiv:1801.01674v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01674v4), especially §§1 and 6.
