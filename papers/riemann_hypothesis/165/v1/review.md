# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the spectral analysis of explicit-formula tests. Its strongest results are the constant-order asymptotics at both edges of $S_X$, the explicit Ramanujan quasimodes, and uniform positivity of the complete zero sum in a specified growing-dimensional regime. These results explain a genuine limitation of this family of tests, rather than extrapolating non-detection from a bounded search. The manuscript correctly states that it neither proves RH nor improves a zeta zero-free region. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied problem.

The central arguments are sufficiently complete. The weighted Schur calculation reduces the strict logarithmic bound to an explicit arithmetic potential; the boundary-adjusted weight supplies the upper estimate needed for the exact top-edge constant. The local diagonalization and harmonic sampling argument establish quasimodes without asserting the still-unproved matching bounds for higher ordered eigenvalues. For the bottom edge, Lemma 3.2 controls the normalization at constant order, while the boundary-mass estimate (3.21) supplies an essential ingredient beyond local tensor-product calculations. The closed-walk expansion has an explicit summable majorant, and the Hilbert–Schmidt tail estimate justifies Wasserstein convergence independently of moment determinacy. The Fejér mean-value estimate controls the full archimedean remainder uniformly in dimension. Finally, the dyadic compression argument proves both signs of spectral blowup from a zero to the right of $1$.

The novelty claims are appropriately differentiated in the main text. I compared the relevant rStation treatments of sampled detection [6], Davenport–Heilbronn windows [20,21], modular-surface Gram matrices [5], and complete compact-window Weil operators [29,30]. They do not supply the discrete edge asymptotics or Ramanujan sampling result proved here. The external comparison with Nikolski–Pushnitski [11], Guo–Li–Zhou [12, Theorem 1.11], and Hilberdink–Pushnitski [13] also supports the stated distinction: bounded-symbol spectral limits and compact LCM operators do not already establish these results for the full prime-weighted matrix. The Hoffman–Wielandt extension is a standard technique, but its application accompanies a substantive arithmetic spectral analysis. Lee [19, introduction and Theorems 1.2–1.3] confirms the Bombieri–Mueller precedence for the Epstein boundary and earlier existence/counting results. The contribution should therefore continue to be presented as the operator mechanism and explicit localized certificates, not the discovery of off-line Epstein or Davenport–Heilbronn zeros. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I ran the supplied interval-certification program on a working copy: all non-timing certificate fields matched the retained output exactly, including $C_-\in[1.655912357,1.655913375]$, both bulk moments, and the localized upper bounds $-1.1783397740$ and $-10.8658287689$. I also reproduced the floating diagnostics, with an equivalent `numpy.trapz` compatibility alias for the installed older NumPy; numerical fields agreed within $10^{-8}$. These runs check the implemented finite arithmetic. The infinite-dimensional conclusions depend on the analytic arguments and tail estimates, which I reviewed separately; the finite zero-sum diagnostic is not a certified census or an independent proof of the explicit formula.

The paper is long but organized around its mathematical argument, with identifiable assumptions, proof strategies, limitations, and accessible supporting data. The distinction between proved quasimodes and conjectural higher-edge convergence is particularly important and is made explicitly. The following local corrections would improve accuracy and presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- In §6.3, the quoted complex digamma remainder uses the wrong secant exponent. The standard bound is $|R_\psi(w)|\le |B_2|\sec^3(\arg(w)/2)/(2|w|^2)$, not the stated $\sec^2$ bound; see [31]. Accordingly, the displayed derivation of $26/(3T^2)$ needs correction. This does not invalidate the certificate's actual allowance $14/T^2$: using $\sec^3(\arg(w)/2)<2\sqrt2$ gives, for Davenport–Heilbronn, $(6+8\sqrt2/3)/T^2<14/T^2$, and a smaller bound for Epstein.
- Correct the outer-product ordering in §7.1(1) to agree with (1.21): the contribution is proportional to $e(\rho)e(1-\overline\rho)^*$ with the stated weight. The norm discussion is unaffected. Also remove §5.2's assertion that $D_k$ can exceed $\log k$; its definition gives $D_k\le\sum_{d\mid k}\Lambda(d)=\log k$.
- Describe the width of the certified $C_-$ interval accurately: approximately $1.018\times10^{-6}$, not “8+ digits” as claimed in the supplement. State the required NumPy version for `numpy.trapezoid`. Shortening the abstract and moving implementation-specific audit detail out of the main narrative would also improve readability.
