# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result for the theta-branching approach: convergence of the **complete nonlinear Mellin error** at the exponential scale $e^{-\pi|T|/2}$, with geometric decay in depth, whenever $n\ge A|T|/\log|T|$ and $A>\pi/2$. The hypotheses and quantifiers are explicit, and the proof supports the stated result. This is relevant to the selected problem through the exact identity $M_*(s)=2\xi(2s)$, but it neither resolves RH nor establishes a new zero-free region. The manuscript correctly maintains that distinction.

The decisive argument is the combination of Sections 3–5. The signed second primitive converts the actual nonlinear difference into positive transport with contraction factor $7/12$. Uniform real moments then support the weighted estimates. The two-endpoint Euler identity supplies a common positive logarithmic analyticity radius for the iterates and the normalized signed primitives; the generating-function inductions justify this without assuming the desired uniform analyticity. The primitive bounds also make the regularized transform valid across the apparent Gamma singularities at $s=0,1$.

The subsequent improvement to the Gamma scale is convincing. In (4.4), the quadratic contribution retains both scale averages. Their uniform $L^2$ bounds combine with the submultiplicative capped weights to control the entire quadratic integral, including opposite-sign frequencies and contributions created at the latest step. The complete kernel estimate has only logarithmic loss in the contour displacement. Consequently, the three terms in (5.12) close the induction: the high-frequency coefficient tends to zero, the low-frequency term uses the already established baseline, and the quadratic term is absorbed after the initial contraction period. The real-part intervals accommodate all shifts, and the cap on $m$ preserves uniformity when depth is arbitrarily large relative to height. Dividing (5.15) by $\Gamma(-s)$ gives the announced exponential factor and a fixed polynomial loss. The complete-sector estimates in Section 6 adequately verify that the specified heated sources satisfy the hypotheses.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work examined. Biane–Pitman–Yor [1, Section 3.3] supplies the classical probability identities. Fill–Janson [4, Theorems 2.1, 3.2, 3.5 and 5.1] establishes smoothing and geometric convergence for the different Quicksort recurrence; Zacharovas [6, Theorem 1] establishes exponential characteristic-function decay for its fixed law. Neither supplies this theta Mellin error estimate or its depth–height window. Within the supplied rStation snapshot, [2] concerns fixed-depth eventual zeros, while [7,8] concern the pole boundary layer, high integer moments, and boundary-measure transforms. In particular, [8, Section 7] explicitly distinguishes those transforms from the original Mellin integral. The present theorem addresses that missing comparison through a separate argument, rather than merely restating those limits.

The limitations are scientifically important and accurately explained. The constants and starting height depend on the fixed source and are not evaluated. The estimate is additive, including near zeros; it does not provide a relative bound there. Section 7 correctly explains why the earlier fixed-depth zero theorem cannot simply be combined with the growing-depth comparison. No zero-preservation or solution claim is warranted.

I reproduced all three supplemental controls, obtaining results identical to the retained JSON records: 180 and 1,923 exact rational checks, and 4,072 additional rational checks with four Laplace/Barnes comparisons. These support normalization and implementation consistency. They do not independently verify the analytic theorem, estimate actual-source thresholds, or constitute interval-certified quadrature; the manuscript describes their evidential scope appropriately.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, main result, proof strategy, source examples, and unresolved zero issue are identifiable and organized around the argument. The abstract is informative, notation is sufficiently defined, and essential evidence is available. Two minor improvements would help:

1. Explain near Theorem 1 that the baseline (3.10), by itself, gives a Gamma-scale comparison only at depth proportional to height after allocating part of the geometric decay. This makes the improvement to depth of order $|T|/\log|T|$ immediately apparent.
2. Cite [8, Section 7] alongside Lemma 2, where essentially the same uniform-moment argument already appears. Its inclusion here is useful for self-containment, but local attribution would sharpen the distinction between reused foundations and the new comparison theorem.
