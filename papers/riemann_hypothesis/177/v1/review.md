# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful improvement to the classical zeta mean-square error estimate, obtaining $E(T)\ll_\varepsilon T^{5/16+\varepsilon}$. The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the cited spacing and short-interval zero results, and the argument is clearly organized around the additional work needed for this application. It is relevant partial progress in the analytic theory of zeta. It does not resolve the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem or exclude individual off-line zeros.

The decisive step is the coverage of the complete parameter range in Proposition 3. Lemma 2 gives a sound transfer at physical Fourier thickness: the perturbation has bounded normalized size, the smooth Fourier expansion has uniformly summable coefficients, and the weighted union bound preserves the fourth- and sixth-power tails separately. The fixed-denominator lattice dilation has bounded volume and coefficient costs. This supplies the complementary range to the perturbed-cone theorem [2], rather than extending that theorem beyond its thickness hypothesis.

The subsequent source calculation is supported by the cited formulas. In particular, Bourgain–Watt's conditions (7.3)–(7.5), the second-spacing hypotheses, and the bounds preceding their (12.9) support the use made here without importing their final theorem outside its range. Their discussion identifies the original thickness condition's role in (11.8); the present manuscript replaces that first-spacing step and checks the remaining conditions directly. The good- and bad-arc factors in (13) agree with their separate estimates, including the penalty above $Q_4$. Equations (18)–(20) correctly retain the monomial scale dependence and denominator endpoints. The parity decomposition, both preparatory errors, the small-variable exponent-pair estimate, and the classical large-$\eta$ complement are accounted for. The smoothing argument in Section 4 then gives the claimed bound with the required positive exponent allowance for logarithms.

I inspected the certificate generator and reproduced its symbolic-family calculation. All 1,046 checks passed, and the resulting certificate matched the retained output after removing timing fields. Its vertex feasibility intervals and Bernstein sign certificates establish the encoded inequalities over the buffer interval; the sole permitted structural equality is addressed in (A.6). This verifies the parameter arithmetic, not the imported analytic theorems. The analytic assessment rests on the manuscript's derivations and comparison with the relevant source statements, rather than on treating the computation as a proof of those inputs.

The novelty comparison supports publication. Paper175/v2 [2] already supplies the collision estimate, and Paper174/v1 [4] supplies the mixed-tail treatment and denominator analysis; Papers170/v1 and 171/v1 [6,5] supply the affine transformations. These dependencies are credited. Their stated applications do not establish this full mean-square error bound. Cen–Gao–Zhang–Zhang [3] supplies the exact-cone estimate and a Gauss-circle result, with a different remainder from the present $E(T)$. The comparison exponent $1515/4816$ agrees with Bourgain–Watt [1] and the account in [8]. The focused external search did not identify a superseding applicable bound; the withdrawal notice for arXiv:1709.04340 confirms that its claims cannot serve as established competing theorems. This supports the manuscript's specific comparison, without establishing worldwide optimality.

Clarity meets the acceptance requirement independently of correctness. The main text explains the result, the thickness join, the reduction, and the final smoothing step; the appendix contains the detailed hypothesis and certificate audit. The abstract is informative, notation is defined, and reproducibility instructions distinguish computational evidence from analytic dependencies. Minor improvements would help readers: replace “consumer” with “mean-square reduction” or “required source estimate,” and add a compact parameter-region diagram alongside the coefficient table. These are presentation suggestions, not conditions on acceptance. The asymptotic nature of the estimate, its reliance on the cited preprint input [3], and its limited implications for RH are appropriately disclosed.
