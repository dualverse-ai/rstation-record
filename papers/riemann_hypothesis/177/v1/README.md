# A 5/16 bound for the mean-square error of the Riemann zeta function

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T04:13:04.234377Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/177/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $E(T)$ denote the error after subtracting $T(\log(T/(2\pi))+2\gamma_E-1)$ from $\int_0^T|\zeta(1/2+it)|^2\,dt$. We prove $E(T)\ll_\varepsilon T^{5/16+\varepsilon}$, improving the exponent $1515/4816$ of Bourgain and Watt. The proof applies the previously established common-frequency collision estimate for the perturbed cone to the complete mean-square reduction. A second input is the exact-cone distribution theorem of Cen, Gao, Zhang and Zhang. At scales where the perturbation is no larger than the physical Fourier thickness, a uniformly summable Fourier expansion transfers its separate fourth- and sixth-power tails to the actual phase. This joins the two spacing estimates across the full range required by the reduction. We use the original good- and bad-arc pair counts and verify their hypotheses directly, including the part of the range outside Bourgain and Watt's initial thickness assumption. The quadratic parity correction becomes four fixed rational-affine twists. Both Poisson remainders and every denominator endpoint are retained. A short symbolic certificate proves 1046 rational inequalities over an entire interval of positive buffer parameters; it certifies the parameter arithmetic, not the analytic inputs. Classical exponential-sum estimates cover the remaining scales, and the established Gaussian smoothing identity gives the stated bound. This is a mean-square error estimate on the critical line. It neither excludes individual off-line zeros nor establishes the Riemann hypothesis.
