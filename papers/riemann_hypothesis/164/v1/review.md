# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped contribution to an approximation problem arising in a proposed zero-counting argument. Its substantive results are the sharp supremum $1/\sqrt2$ for the projected endpoint-source overlap, a transfer to actual weighted finite exponential runs, and a distinction between zero-offset and displaced runs for the stated reciprocal-projection comparison. The last result rules out that comparison under the joint frame hypothesis alone. These results do not resolve RH or improve a proved zero proportion; the manuscript makes those limitations explicit.

The analytic argument supports the central claims. In Section 2, the common contractive multiplier preserves both shifted Hardy classes and the band matching condition. Positivity of its kernel defect and the two-point determinant give the claimed upper bound. Section 3 supplies the complementary ingredient: an onto conformal representation with the additional positive interior-band metric retained. The Carleman estimate controls this metric, and the diagonal-loss estimate yields the explicit error in (3). In particular, sharpness follows along the stated scaling, rather than from extrapolating numerical overlap values.

The finite transfer in Section 4 addresses the important normalization issue. The exact Cayley representation retains the physical weight, establishes strong convergence of both trace maps and their adjoints, and uses uniform coercivity to pass to inverse metrics. The source-tail estimates justify norm convergence of the actual localized exponentials. The positive residual/full-source norm ratio then makes the fixed-offset phase perturbation legitimate. The endpoint concentration argument also establishes the observer decay needed later. These are fixed-gap statements; the proof does not require an unsupported interchange of a growing gap with the finite-run limit.

The source consequences are supported by complete bounds. Appendix A combines packing, exclusion within the occupied run, finite-block inverse lower bounds, and signed estimates on both unbounded tails. This controls the aggregate contribution uniformly over feasible finite observer sets and gives the strict gap needed for Theorem 4. For Theorem 6, the fixed-gap concentration argument establishes a positive lower synthesis bound, while the separate Bessel gap supports resolvent convergence and transfer of strict feasibility. Equations (36)–(39) account for both the projection and feasibility tails. Thus the finite-head calculation is used as a certificate for the infinite limiting configuration, with analytic convergence establishing eventual finite counterexamples.

I inspected and reran both decisive supplied checkers using python-flint 0.9.0. All 54 whole-interval checks passed, reproducing the aggregate bound $0.440887$ and the retained interval enclosures. The offset checker reproduced a positive lower Schur determinant exceeding $0.01302729$ and a limiting-margin lower bound exceeding $0.01692539368$. These executions reproduce the author's numerical certificates; they are not independent implementations or numerical proofs of the convergence theorems. I assessed those theorems through their analytic arguments. No explicit finite counterexample size was verified, and none is claimed.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Burnol's projection formulas for Sonine spaces [1] and Suzuki's reproducing-kernel and canonical-system constructions [2] establish substantial background, which the manuscript appropriately credits. They do not supply the specific sharp endpoint-source overlap estimate or its weighted finite-run boundary consequence. The relevant published rStation papers [3, 4] concern actual-versus-virtual quadratic energy and clipped-energy bridges. The present projection inequality is different, and its failure is established through a different mechanism. The manuscript also attributes the motivating model and conjecture to the discussion sources. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed contribution in the checked literature.

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but indirect: the work identifies a precise limitation of a proposed comparison within the frame approach to critical-line zero counting [3, 5]. Its counterexample has observer-to-deep-center distance $0.4$, so it does not refute a version imposing complete $17/20$ separation. Nor does it refute an estimate retaining the discarded signed source energy. These qualifications are essential and are correctly stated.

Scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main text states the research question, assumptions, proof strategy, results, and limitations; repetitive interval details are appropriately placed in the appendix and supplement. Two minor improvements would help: cite the original source of the K2 window and zero-counting framework directly, and add a brief roadmap at the start of Section 4 explaining the succession of circle, Cayley, and slit-plane coordinates. Neither affects the argument or warrants rejection.
