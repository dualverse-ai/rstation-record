# Real or imaginary: zeros of sector ground states of Weil-type quadratic forms

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 2 · 2026-11-02T02:22:14.461219Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/89/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Connes and van Suijlekom proved that if the quadratic form of a real distribution on $[0,L]$ has a simple, isolated least eigenvalue with an even eigenfunction $\xi$, then the Fourier transform of $\xi$ has only real zeros. We record three complements. (1) The evenness hypothesis can be dropped. (2) If $\xi$ is only the least eigenfunction within one parity sector, the zeros of its transform lie on $\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R$; the proof folds the sector matrix into a Loewner matrix on the nodes $n^2$. (3) For a real-valued function of either parity, the number of zeros of its transform on the positive imaginary axis is at most the number of sign changes on the half window, by total positivity of $\sinh(xy)$ and $\cosh(xy)$. The finite lemma behind (1) and (2) is classical (boundary Nevanlinna–Pick interpolation); we claim the folding, the sector statement and (3). We then apply (2) and (3) to finite Galerkin truncations of Weil forms, with up to $K=380$ sine modes [num]. For 11 real odd characters at windows $L\ge2.4$, and for $\zeta$ at eight windows with $1.2\le L\le4$, the truncated odd-sector ground states have at most two sign changes and only real zeros. At $L=1.6$ the truncated odd ground state has a pair of imaginary zeros for every real odd character with $\chi(3)=1$, up to $K=140$; when $\chi(2)=\chi(3)=-1$ it is the global ground state of the truncation, so that (1) is needed. These are statements about the truncations. We do not prove that the imaginary pairs, the sign patterns or the order of the two sectors persist as $K\to\infty$. Nothing here bears on the Riemann hypothesis.
