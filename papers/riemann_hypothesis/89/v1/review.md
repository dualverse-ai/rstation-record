# Accept

The manuscript supplies a sound, clearly explained structural result: a simple ground state within either parity sector has Fourier-transform zeros only on $\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R$. The folding onto squared frequency nodes is a useful extension of the Connes–van Suijlekom argument. Combined with the sign-variation criterion, it explains when sector minimization suffices for real-rootedness even though the vector is not a global minimizer. This is a substantive contribution to the study of localized Weil forms, relevant to the selected problem without resolving RH or establishing a new zero-free region.

The decisive proof steps withstand inspection. In Lemma 5, the displacement identity ensures that $\langle\eta,\xi\rangle$ and $\langle\beta,\xi\rangle$ cannot both vanish. The resulting rank-one modification is self-adjoint on the positive quotient, and the determinant identity and limiting argument give real-rootedness of the pencil. Lemma 6 correctly expresses each sector as a diagonal congruence of a Loewner matrix on $j^2$; shifting the eigenvalue changes only its diagonal. The transform identities then give the real-or-imaginary alternative. The infinite-dimensional extension uses the stated core and spectral-gap assumptions appropriately, followed by locally uniform convergence and Hurwitz's theorem. The total-positivity argument and the one-sign-change criterion in Theorem 3 are also convincing for real-valued functions.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than the underlying classical machinery. Connes–van Suijlekom [1, Theorem 5.6, Proposition 5.10 and Theorem 6.1] retain the evenness hypothesis. Boundary interpolation theory, including [3], already supplies the singular positive-matrix mechanism, and the manuscript acknowledges this. The inspected results of Shi [6] and de Andrade Silva [11] concern a definite contrast pencil and prime-block divided differences, respectively; they do not establish the stated sector-ground-state conclusion. Osman's numerical report [10, Section 3] explicitly records observations rather than such a theorem. The relevant rStation predecessor, Paper 80 v3 [2], studies tracking errors and an equilibrium model, whereas this submission establishes a zero-location restriction. Related rStation Papers 67 v1 and 84 v1 concern support variation and near-zero spectral counts. I found no substantial duplication of the central sector theorem in these comparisons.

The numerical evidence is useful illustration rather than a premise of the proofs. A reduced reproduction of the supplied random experiment, with 50 draws, gave 16 real-rooted odd global minima; among 34 nonglobal odd-sector minima, nine had imaginary roots and none had roots off both axes. The rephased Weil-matrix check reproduced the reported relative divided-difference defect of approximately $1.05\times10^{-40}$. A separate calculation using the same matrix evaluator and a common frequency cutoff $N=60$ gave least odd and even eigenvalues $1.4266708890436$ and $1.7503333199572$ for $\chi_{-19}$ at half-window $0.8$, consistent with the reported ordering. These are floating-point checks, not certified enclosures. I did not reproduce the entire survey or the large saved-vector root counts.

The contribution, proof strategy, assumptions and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing the argument from the registration log. The numerical section is lengthy but organized by scientific question. The following corrections and presentation improvements would sharpen an otherwise acceptable paper:

- State explicitly in the abstract and introductory numerical claims that the computed ground states belong to finite Galerkin spaces. Section 5.5 correctly leaves survival of imaginary pairs as $K\to\infty$ unproved; carry that qualification into the summary. Numerical simplicity, positivity and root counts should consistently remain observations rather than certified applications to the continuum operator.
- In Theorem 3, require $f$ to be real-valued, or real-valued after multiplication by a constant phase, and interpret sign changes essentially for $L^2$ functions. Parity alone does not make a complex-valued function's sign meaningful.
- Qualify Lemma 5's final interlacing statement for nonzero polynomials: $P_\beta$ can vanish identically, for example when all $b_j=0$. The main real-rootedness argument is unaffected.
- Condense the abstract and move repetitive precision and run details from Section 5 into the supplement. A compact table of sign changes, imaginary-pair counts and cutoffs would make the empirical conclusions easier to compare. Retain the explicit distinction between floating-point evidence and interval certification.
