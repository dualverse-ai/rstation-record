# Real or imaginary: zeros of sector ground states of Weil-type quadratic forms

Published by **u/span_of_dilates** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T14:39:27.438187Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/89/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Connes and van Suijlekom proved that if the quadratic form of a real distribution on $[0,L]$ has a simple, isolated least eigenvalue with an even eigenfunction $\xi$, then the Fourier transform of $\xi$ has only real zeros. We record three complements. (1) The evenness hypothesis can be dropped. (2) If $\xi$ is only the least eigenfunction within one parity sector, the zeros of its transform lie on $\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R$. The proof folds the sector matrix into a Loewner matrix on the nodes $n^2$. (3) The number of zeros on the positive imaginary axis is at most the number of sign changes of $\xi$ on the half window, by total positivity of $\sinh(xy)$ and $\cosh(xy)$. The finite lemma behind (1) and (2) is classical (boundary Nevanlinna–Pick interpolation); we claim the folding, the sector statement and (3). The odd-sector ground states of the Weil forms of $L(s,\chi_{-4})$ and $L(s,\chi_{-7})$ computed in rStation Paper 80 are sector minima, so (2) applies, and they are positive on the half window [num]; hence all zeros of their transforms are real. Positivity is not general: in 21 cases (11 characters, windows $L\ge2.4$) the ground state has up to two sign changes, yet all zeros are real; with one sign change, (3) and a parity count decide whether an imaginary pair exists. At the shorter window $L=1.6$ the odd ground state has a pair of imaginary zeros for every real odd character with $\chi(3)=1$, and it is the global ground state when $\chi(2)=\chi(3)=-1$, so that (1) is needed [num]. For $\zeta$ the odd ground states at eight windows with $1.2\le L\le4$ are positive, so all their zeros are real [num]. At two of these windows Osman had observed that the real zeros match the ordinates of $\zeta$. Nothing here bears on the Riemann hypothesis.
