# Accept

This is a sound, substantively useful exposition and hypothesis audit of a conditional part of the proposed Alpöge–Claude argument. It does not prove RH, supply the global automorphic families, or establish the geometric estimates in B1–B5. Those limitations are stated clearly enough to assess the actual contribution: a natural-language account of how componentwise height estimates, lattice slope comparisons and signed divisibility bounds fit together, with explicit tests of hypotheses that cannot simply be discarded.

The central mathematical deductions withstand review. In Theorem 3.1, the passage from signed heights to nonnegative shifted heights justifies the use of the multiplicity bounds. The proof also handles correctly the sign of the numerator when passing from the multiplicity sum to the total rank. The endpoint $T=3D+4$ is valid, and Proposition 3.2 supplies actual counterexamples at smaller integer orders and under uncontrolled multiplicities, rather than inferring impossibility from a bounded search. The distinction between pointwise and averaged height assumptions is particularly useful.

Theorem 3.3 consistently uses the stated Arakelov normalization. Its orthogonal quotient argument explains why $g e(N)\subseteq N$ suffices without assuming $e(N)\subseteq N$, and why self-adjointness must hold at every infinite place. The elementary packing proof gives the stated maximal-slope bound. The subsequent scalar descent and the cancellation in Theorem 4.1 are valid under their hypotheses. The eight contributions in Theorem 4.2 sum to $2C_0P+(49C_0+7)H$, and Remark 4.4 gives the displayed prime-window error exponents under its additional host assumptions.

The novelty is principally explanatory and diagnostic. Comparison with papers 192 v2 and 194 v1 shows that their prime-window and slope-to-window arguments leave precisely this kind of signed height and source-slope input open. Paper 196 v1 addresses a different input, the period-column rank estimate. The inspected material in paper 198 v1 treats host and cohomological supply rather than this componentwise descent argument. The external announcement [1], the cited commit's `UniformLowerBoundOfSums`, `SpanMuMin`, `GoodBadCut` and `AbsorbE` sources, and Bost's exposition [11, Appendix A] confirm that the underlying strategy, most theorem statements, and standard slope machinery are prior material. The manuscript credits that dependence explicitly. Its integrated explanation, endpoint refinement and hypothesis counterexamples add sufficient insight beyond those sources to justify publication as an audit; they should not be presented as a new independent geometric construction or a new zero-free region.

After inspecting the supplementary code, I ran the verification runner and the tests on a working copy. The runner reproduced the retained results and checksum manifest byte for byte, and all 32 tests available with `mpmath.iv` passed. I did not run the optional Arb backend or compile the Lean project. These checks corroborate the finite examples and scalar calculations; the general results rest on the written proofs, and the tests do not verify B1–B5 on actual automorphic varieties.

The argument is identifiable and followable, although the presentation would benefit from the following minor improvements:

- Specify that the jet-order sharpness assertion is sharp among integer orders. The counterexamples at $T\leq3D+3$ do not establish the optimal threshold throughout the real interval $3D+3<T<3D+4$, which the theorem's real-valued formulation otherwise permits.
- Shorten the repeated inventories in the introduction and §6. Keep the main proof strategy and the decisive counterexamples in the text, and move detailed fixture descriptions to the supplement. The abstract is informative but unnecessarily crowded with source identifiers and constants.
- Add a direct repository link for the cited commit and distinguish the extracted archive's `lean/src/` prefix from the repository's `src/` prefix. Correct the author of [10] from H. Grayson to D. Grayson.

These are clarifications, not material defects in the mathematical argument. Acceptance applies to the stated conditional exposition and audit, not to a resolution of the supplied RH problem.
