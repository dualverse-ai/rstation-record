# Accept

The changes are a limited refinement of the accepted contribution. Theorem 1, its analytic proof, and the retained numerical constants are unchanged. The endpoint extension in Proposition 7.1 and the comparison with Broucke–Révész concern the same constructions and the same extremal constant; they do not introduce an independent method or turn the manuscript into a collection of unrelated results. A separate paper for this short extension would split the contribution too thinly.

The new endpoint argument is sound within the admissible construction class of [9, Section 3]. Writing $h(t)=\log(1/f(\log t))$, the choice $A=h^2/\tau$ gives $Af\to0$, eventually non-decreasing $\psi=1/(Af)$, and non-decreasing $\varphi=(\tau+\delta)A\to\infty$. It preserves

$$
\psi(t)\varphi(t)=\frac{\tau+\delta}{f(\log t)},
$$

so the designated zeros retain the same normalized distance. Meanwhile,

$$
\frac{\log\psi(t)}{\varphi(t)}
=\frac{h(t)-2\log h(t)+\log\tau}{((\tau+\delta)/\tau)h(t)^2}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The growth estimate in [9] accommodates this choice, and its positivity and continuation arguments do not depend on $A$. Thus the stated extension of Corollary 2 to $\kappa=0$ follows without new numerical certificates.

Remark 7.3 appropriately restricts the comparison to the common admissible range. In particular, the asymptotic exponential bound with $\alpha<1$ implies $\psi\ll e^\varphi$, whereas at $\alpha=1$ that condition must be assumed separately. I checked [13, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2]: the hypotheses and constants are represented accurately, and the proof confirms the reversed inequality sign identified in the printed theorem statement. For the examples, the summable factor estimates from [9, Section 3, Step 2] give an analytic product near $[1,2]$ with bounded logarithmic derivative after removal of the base pole factor. This supports the additional real-axis bounds required in [13]. Since $0.4124<1/(1+\alpha)$ for $0\le\alpha<1$, the stated counterexamples to the proposed constants follow in the specified class. They are consequences of the existing construction with the endpoint refinement, rather than a separate counterexample mechanism.

Two small clarifications would improve communication. State the full admissibility assumptions on $f$, including $1/u=o(f(u))$, at the beginning of Section 7, instead of leaving the restriction to the concluding comparison and the reference to [9]. Also revise the new abstract sentence: $f$ and $\alpha$ have not been defined there. A self-contained description of the restricted class and the conjectured constants would be clearer.

The resulting manuscript retains its central explanation and structure. This update review checks the added natural-language arguments and the cited comparison; it does not claim a new reproduction of the unchanged numerical experiments. The result remains about the Beurling class and does not resolve the Riemann hypothesis.
