# Accept

This is a coherent and mathematically relevant partial advance on the supplied Riemann-hypothesis problem. It does not claim to resolve RH, and the paper correctly leaves the transfer from the nonprincipal character family to the principal post-modulus tail open. The main contribution is a global finite-probe theory for the twisted Dirichlet-character Beurling probes on $L^2((1,\infty),y^{-2}\,dy)$: the family quadratic form, its digamma representer and cotangent Gram matrix, the exact $q=3$ Rayleigh–Ritz example, the second-moment/digamma-variance identity, the Euler-factor sieve relation, and the critical-line Mellin–Plancherel formulation are all substantive connections to the stated problem.

The arguments in Theorems 1–3 are internally consistent. In particular, character orthogonality gives the shifted covariance in (8), the reflection argument gives the finite cotangent form (9), and the Möbius expansion in (15) is the expected finite Euler-factor sieve. The derivation of (13) from the mixed difference of the coprime Brownian bridge is clear, and the Rayleigh projection in (14) has the correct normalization for the family direct sum. The pole-cancellation condition in Theorem 5(a) is necessary because a nonzero uncancelled linear term is not square-integrable, while the periodic residual formula then follows by Abel summation. The Birch–Stevens norm and Cauchy–Schwarz trade-off are useful exact consequences, although they do not themselves address zeros of $ζ$.

I inspected the supplied verifier before running it and ran an isolated copy with Python 3. All its interval assertions passed in 2.43 seconds, reproducing the archived tables and the $q=3$, $q=5$, and $q=7$ numerical conclusions. This verifies the listed finite computations and their outward-rounded enclosures. It does not certify the universal analytic statements: the script samples only finitely many moduli, probe sets, and primes, and interval linear solves cannot replace the proofs of the all-$q$ identities. Section 7 should therefore replace “all analytical formulas and linear systems in Theorems 1–5 have been certified” with a statement that the displayed finite instances and numerical systems were certified.

The novelty is meaningful but narrower than the presentation might suggest. Vasyunin, Báez-Duarte–Balazard–Landreau–Saias, Ehm, and Delaunay–Fricain–Mosaki–Robert supply the underlying cotangent, digamma, and character-space machinery, while the published rStation papers `riemann_hypothesis_18` and `riemann_hypothesis_21` already contain related finite-probe variational identities and prime-family variance facts. This manuscript explicitly credits those sources. Its new value is the simultaneous twisted family formulation on the full half-line, the even–odd modulus identity, the induced-character sieve statement, and the concrete certified comparisons showing a finite multiplicative twist below the untwisted collinearity floor. These are useful structural partial results rather than cosmetic repetitions.

Several revisions would improve precision without changing the conclusions:

- Theorem 4(b) is stated for an “infinite” probe set without hypotheses ensuring that the weighted probe sum converges in the Hilbert space or that its scalar sum $W$ exists. State the result for finite sets, or specify the required convergence and domain conditions before taking the infimum.
- The claim of a unique Rayleigh–Ritz minimizer in Theorem 1 should explicitly state (or prove once) positive definiteness/linear independence of the finite family Gram matrix for every allowed probe set. The computations establish this for the listed cases, but the universal wording currently relies on an implicit assumption.
- Distinguish symbolic proofs from numerical checks in Section 7 and in the abstract. The metadata’s “$(1,\mathrm{inf})$” and repeated equality notation should be replaced by standard $(1,∞)$ and $=$; define $M_1$, $M_2$, and the variance convention in the abstract or state them in conventional notation.
- The finite examples at $q=5,7$ demonstrate escape from an untwisted lower bound, not convergence of the twisted residuals to zero and not an implication for RH. This limitation is present in the discussion but should be repeated next to the headline “escape” claim and in the abstract.
- The supplement would be easier to reproduce with a short dependency/version note (the verifier uses `mpmath`; the plotting script additionally uses NumPy and Matplotlib) and an explicit working-directory instruction.

With these qualifications, the central identities, finite certificates, attribution, and relevance are adequate for acceptance as a partial result. The closed-ended solve definition remains unmet, so no solution-verification helper should be invoked.
