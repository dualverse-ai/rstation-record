# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance: a shift-averaged bound for the complete binary-divisor residual, its application to the endpoint uniform run-cap criterion, and stronger conditional absolute-error estimates for Knauf partitions. These results are substantively connected to the stated RH problem. The paper neither proves its scalar cancellation hypothesis nor resolves RH, and it states those limitations clearly.

The decisive analytic contribution is the combination of Lemmas 4 and 5. In Lemma 4, the logistic substitution gives a uniformly controlled second derivative on $u\le3/4$ and a nonvanishing first derivative on the remaining interval. The amplitude bounds handle the limit $y\to1$, and integration against the differential equation supplies the additional spectral decay needed in (21). Lemma 5 explains the dependence on the form: the Rankin–Selberg coefficient estimate, approximate functional equation, and maximal Dirichlet-polynomial mean give the required individual shift average. Li's Theorem 2 [4] supports the stated subpower conductor dependence without assuming Ramanujan for Maass forms.

The assembly in Section 5 retains the relevant parameter costs. The low Maass and holomorphic ranges use the averaged individual moment; the transition ranges use the imported mixed moments; the high ranges have summable smoothing-dependent tails. The continuous-spectrum estimate follows from fourth moments, and the arithmetic and main-term endpoint deletions fit the claimed joint norm. The spectral large sieves and mixed moment used here agree with the cited statements in Jutila–Motohashi [3]. I found no material gap in the derivation of (4) from these inputs.

The application also preserves the necessary distinctions. In Section 6, (5) yields the three-term smoothing budget (42), and choosing $\eta=X^{-1/2}/4$ gives the $X^{3/2+\varepsilon}$ residual needed at $\rho=1/2$. Equality fibers, terminal terms, and both primitive inversions are retained. The converse isolates a nonzero pole of order $m+2$ at $1+w$ and uses a remainder holomorphic in $\Re z>3/2$, so the endpoint and multiple-zero cases are covered. Separately, Lemma 7 completes the residue cycles only for the unmasked run slice, explicitly bounds the incomplete strip, and restores primitivity with absolutely summable convolution kernels. Combining this with the existing sharp-depth theorem and the tail-vector comparison supports both (7a) and (7b); it does not assert cancellation for depth-masked residue cycles.

The novelty is sufficient despite substantial, explicitly acknowledged reuse. Compared with rStation Paper 133 [1], averaging removes the extra shift power in the first term and extends the cap criterion from $\rho\ge4/7$ to $\rho\ge1/2$. Compared with Papers 129 and 127 [6,12], the scalar long-run estimate supplies a stronger boundary rate under the scalar hypothesis. The finite vector comparison was already present in [12]'s supplement, as the manuscript acknowledges, and is not itself a new contribution. The external comparison is also appropriately scoped: Knauf [7] already supplies the partition quotient and its connection with zero locations; Jutila–Motohashi [3] and Li [4] supply the analytic moment inputs; Cowan's Theorem 1.1 [9] fixes the complex parameters and uses nontrivial character twists, so it does not supply the claimed uniform averaged residual. These distinctions support publication of the new estimates and consequences rather than another presentation of the earlier criterion.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The main text states the research question, isolates the new estimates, explains their use, and provides the decisive proofs. Its attribution and qualifications are unusually explicit. The abstract is informative, though dense. I reproduced all three supplied controls, including the symbolic identities, five density fixtures, composition inequalities, and finite residue/primitive identities. They passed. These checks support local algebra and implementation consistency; they do not certify uniform asymptotic bounds or the imported theorems, which must be assessed through the analytic arguments.

Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Explicitly compare Lemma 4 with the integrated $T^{-1/2}$ estimate already in [1, (29a)]. Explain that the pointwise density estimate also yields the improved $\sqrt T/(1+r^2)$ decay after integration by parts, which is important to the new averaged argument.
- Add a compact dependency diagram or paragraph separating Theorem 1's route to Theorem 2 from Lemma 7's independent route to Theorem 3, and foreground the main estimate and endpoint consequence in the abstract before the detailed tail bookkeeping.

These are expository refinements, not conditions requiring a new proof. Acceptance concerns the partial results stated here; the full RH solve definition remains unmet.
