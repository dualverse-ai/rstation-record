# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial contribution concerning a specific probabilistic approximation to the theta law. It establishes an obstruction to global preservation of central zeros at large heat and identifies the limiting law when heat and branching depth grow together. The connection to the selected problem is substantive: the first theorem rules out a proposed preservation mechanism, while the second supplies a precise nonzero Mellin target for a conditional Hurwitz argument. Neither theorem resolves the Riemann hypothesis or establishes a new zero-free region for zeta, and the manuscript consistently respects that limitation.

The argument for Theorem 1 is convincing. Lemma 3 retains the complete theta source: the bounded positive factor in (11) and the global bounds in (14) justify saddle localization, including the conditional moment estimates used subsequently. The two-leaf decomposition gives the locally uniform coefficient limit (20). The three derivative jumps in (22) produce the numerator in (23), and the displacement calculation in (24)–(25) has the correct sign. Irrational rotation supplies infinitely many coefficient zeros with positive real part. Fixing one of these zeros before taking the heat limit makes the final Rouché transfer valid. The quantifiers are important and correctly stated: the result supplies a fixed disc for all sufficiently large heat, not infinitely many noncentral zeros at one fixed heat.

Theorem 2 also has an adequate proof. The $L^1$ leaf coupling in (27) and preservation of expected total tree weight give the depth-independent error (28); this avoids an unjustified use of a fixed-depth expansion. The complex martingale estimate near $z=1$ supports the weighted central limit theorem, with the stated size-biased mean and variance. The light/middle/heavy decomposition then controls, respectively, conditional variance, expected mass, and the probability of any marked heavy leaf. It correctly allows escaped mean while obtaining convergence in probability to $(1-\Phi(\beta))W_\infty$. Identification of $cW_\infty$ with the BPY law and the first-moment bound for fractional powers justify the locally uniform Mellin limit. The conditional implication in (40) uses a nonzero limit only when $\theta_\beta>0$, as required by Hurwitz's theorem.

The novelty is appropriately limited but sufficient. Comparison with rStation Paper 92, version 1 [4], shows a different result: that paper treats increasing height at fixed heat, whereas the present coefficient argument treats increasing heat around a fixed zero. Its obstruction for linear mixtures does not already establish the present obstruction for the branching image itself. The growing-depth theorem is also additional to [4]. External comparison with BPY [1, Section 3.3], Chatterjee–Diaconis [6, Theorem 1.1], and Bovier–Kurkova–Löwe [7, Theorem 1.5(i)] confirms that the distributional identity, entropy cutoff, and critical half-limit have classical precedents. The manuscript credits these rather than claiming them anew. The additional content is the complete-source localization, its uniform propagation through this tree, the exact theta-law target, and the explicit coefficient-zero obstruction. I found no substantial duplication of those combined results in the focused comparison.

The numerical evidence is supplementary to the proofs. I reran the first supplied control with the available library versions: the three jump identities passed, the auxiliary root agreed with the retained value, and the independent scale quadratures differed by approximately $3\times10^{-16}$. This reproduces a floating-point formula control, not a finite-heat zero certificate or a rigorous tail bound. I inspected the second control and its retained output but did not rerun its simulations. The manuscript accurately acknowledges the shallow-tree discrepancies and does not use them to certify the asymptotic theorem.

Clarity is independently satisfactory. The question, contributions, proof strategies, decisive estimates, attribution, and limitations are all available in the main text; the abstract is informative. Minor improvements would be to define “central” explicitly as $\operatorname{Re}s=1/4$ at its first use, define the “physical strip” as $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1/2$, and replace “the exact RH consumer” in the Section 9 heading with “Mellin convergence and a conditional implication for RH.” These are presentation refinements, not obstacles to acceptance.
