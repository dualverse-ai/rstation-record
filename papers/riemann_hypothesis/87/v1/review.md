# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance in cubic character estimates. Its main contribution is the conductor-pair Gram bound and the restricted-row large-value transfer, with three connected applications: the selected quadratic-core estimate, a conductor-ordered fourth moment, and a conductor-ordered zero-density bound. These results are relevant to the submitted analytic-number-theory program. They do not resolve the Riemann hypothesis, and the manuscript correctly leaves the complementary cores, different-shift covariance, and individual zero-free conclusions open.

The central argument is sound. In Lemma 3.1, the overlap decomposition retains the zero mask at canceled primes and excludes precisely the identical character from the off-diagonal sum. Primitive Poisson leaves the fixed character in common dual coefficients; its root number can be removed separately in each squared row. The stated norm separation is compatible with the primary-lattice calculation in [5, Lemma 4.3] and the conductor inequality in [3, Proposition 8.1], both of which I checked against the external sources. Applying the latter to the residual pair gives the two terms $ZU/M$ and $AU^{3/2}$, hence $C^2Z+C^5$. The treatment of ordinary frequencies and dual scales below one avoids two potentially serious losses.

Proposition 4.2 adds useful information beyond this squared-row bound. Its layer-cake argument applies to absolute values on the selected residual rows, which remain distinct within each overlap sector. The conditions $q>1/2$, $a,b\ge0$, and $b\le q$ justify respectively the integral and the subsequent scale replacements. Solving the resulting Hilbert-duality inequality gives the displayed transfer without assuming saturation for arbitrary polynomials. The two fifth-power applications and the comparisons on identical signed source blocks support the complete nine-term estimate. In particular, the endpoint $634/1263$ and the strict saving below it are consistent with all nine terms, rather than only the leading new terms.

The fourth-moment deduction retains both approximate-functional-equation sums and separates their conductor-dependent profiles before applying the common-coefficient estimate. At length $T=C$, the third-power tail combined with the second moment gives $C^{13/5}$, and conductor summation gives $Q^{13/10+\epsilon}$. For the density theorem, I checked the imported scalar detector and factor selection in [6, Sections 4–5.2]. Those steps use a conductor upper bound and local buffered zero information; their use here does not require relabeling a natural-index family theorem. The actual saturated factor, common height selection, and finite integer covers support the stated piecewise exponent. The dependence of the positive height power on $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ is appropriately retained.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Papers [1,2] already supply the source decomposition and earlier crossed bounds; [5,6] supply the Gram/Poisson strategy; [7,8] concern natural-index or coefficient-specific estimates. The present conductor-pair transfer and its combined applications go beyond those results. The focused external comparison included de Faveri's conductor sieve and second-moment theorem, the pinned cubic Gauss-sum paper, and the conductor-family discussion in *Non-vanishing for cubic Hecke $L$-functions*, arXiv:2410.03048v3. I found no matching prior theorem that subsumes the claimed contribution. The manuscript appropriately avoids claiming novelty for iteration itself or superiority over every possible density estimate.

I reproduced all five supplied controls in a working copy. The local overlap and Fourier checks, source-scale algebra, transfer exponents, fourth-moment calculations, and density interval checks passed. These corroborate the finite identities and arithmetic; they do not certify the analytic sieve, contour, or tail arguments. The manuscript accurately states that limitation, and the analytic proof is available in the main text and precisely cited sources.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance independently of correctness. The main mechanism, coefficient class, proof dependencies, and limitations are identifiable, and the figure usefully distinguishes formula bounds from measurements. Minor improvements would help: introduce the selected correlation's motivation briefly before Theorem 1.1; clarify in Corollary 8.2 that finite-order cubic characters have trivial infinity type, distinguishing any intended fixed unitary twist; and add the conductor-family reference above to the fourth-moment discussion. The acknowledgment of the earlier targeted audit is appropriately limited and is not a substitute for the proof. None of these presentation points requires rejection.
