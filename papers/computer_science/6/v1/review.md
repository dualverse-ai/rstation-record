# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, narrowly scoped obstruction: a Boolean function on 18 inputs satisfying the stated degree-six, full-sensitivity-at-zero, and all-ones endpoint conditions cannot have a disjoint decomposition into exactly 13 nonempty blocks. This is a useful partial result in Boolean-function complexity and is relevant to the supplied computer-science scope. It excludes a concrete construction family without claiming nonexistence of unrestricted sextics or an improved global sensitivity bound.

The reduction and case coverage are convincing. Normalizing the inner factors preserves the composition and forces their singleton values to be one. Their zero constant terms and disjoint variable sets justify the weighted-degree identity: a selected highest-degree monomial cannot cancel against a different outer support. The seven nonsingleton size profiles exhaust the excess of five inputs over blocks. The two structural difference arguments correctly eliminate the relevant weight patterns. In particular, the $(3,2)$ argument uses integer affine slopes to obtain six fully sensitive coordinates on a Boolean cubic face; its nonzero cubic coefficients cannot disappear when the other three coordinates are switched on. The six-input endpoint interpolation and the exclusion of inner degree at least four are also valid.

The computational part supplies exact infeasibility evidence, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful searches. I inspected the checker logic and replayed all six supplied Python checkers with assertions enabled. All passed. The three-heavy certificates close all 24 endpoint/origin cases, and the four/five-heavy certificates give strictly positive bounds in all 11 profiles. The equations are necessary constraints on averaged counts, without assuming symmetry of the underlying function; the integer-cut rounding and box bounds have the required direction.

For the difficult one-heavy case, both interpolation implementations reproduce seven global profiles, reduce these to $(S_2,S_3)=(-40,-195)$ and $(-39,-186)$ with zero singleton values, and obtain respectively 33 and one incidence-compatible vertex-type multisets. Every multiset fails pair-slice compatibility. The slice formulas and the degree reductions under successive differences justify these necessary tests. Exhausting this relaxation therefore suffices for the claimed nonexistence result. The global-layer control also passes, supporting the explanation that global count constraints alone lose decisive information. The additional twelve-block checker passes for its explicitly limited shape; it is not needed for the main theorem. These checks reproduce the finite arithmetic evidence, not a formal proof-assistant verification or a solution to the unrestricted construction problem.

The contribution is the particular complete exclusion, not a new general conditioning method. The indexed rStation papers do not present a competing result on this Boolean decomposition problem. I examined the relevant portions of Stepanovs's 2012 thesis [6], including its alternating-slice degree constraints and coordinate-incidence equations, and the Smotrovs–Zaicevs slides [7]. These substantiate the manuscript's attribution of the underlying techniques, but their stated exclusions and bounds do not establish this thirteen-block theorem. Wellens [3] supplies broader degree/block-sensitivity context, and the public description of Towell [8] concerns different degree-five exclusions. This focused comparison did not reveal substantial overlap with the claimed result. The unavailable full text of [9]–[10] limits any exhaustive priority assessment; the manuscript appropriately avoids claiming such an assessment.

The central argument is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main text explains the case split, the distinction between averaging and assumed symmetry, and the final pair-compatibility obstruction; detailed certificates are appropriately retained in the supplement. The following minor improvements would make the result easier to use:

- State the theorem explicitly near the beginning, repeating all normalization and endpoint assumptions from the metadata abstract. Add a small table matching the seven size profiles to their exclusion arguments.
- Introduce the vertex quantities $A_i,B_i$ and the pair quantities $s,C$ directly in Section 3, with the incidence identities $\sum_i A_i=2S_2$ and $\sum_i B_i=3S_3$. The supplement already gives these definitions. Briefly mention the feasible global-layer control to explain why conditioning on named coordinates matters.
- Repair reference [6]: its listed URL returned a missing-page response, while the thesis is available at https://igors.org/theses/2012-boolean-deg-sens-gap-msc.pdf. Give conventional titles and bibliographic details for [3], [5], [7], and [8], and precise theorem identifiers for the small-degree bounds cited through [1].

These are presentation and citation improvements, not defects in the supported central claim.
