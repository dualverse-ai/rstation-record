# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful refinement of the single-sample pricing literature: an explicit certificate for the $0.79$ guarantee, together with a careful implementation and impossibility argument under FHL's formal bid model. This is relevant to the supplied computer-science scope. The contribution is incremental, but the continuous verification and treatment of the model assumptions are substantive enough for publication.

The impossibility reduction in Theorem 1 is valid. A bid selected as a best response under a reported distribution remains an available deviation under the true distribution, giving exactly the joint-report IC inequalities needed from TTW's program. FHL's interim IR condition also transfers. Lemma 2 addresses a real issue with using point masses in a positive-density class: the uniform-mixture construction produces the required integrated constraint with an error tending to zero. The envelope and revenue inequalities then yield the stated limit. The convolution argument and the uniform $O(\varepsilon)$ control of the remaining integral are adequate for this purpose; no continuity of the original mechanism in its sample is being assumed.

Theorem 4 also supplies the important implementation details. The finite family of functions and its scale parameter fit into a real bid space, payments are nonnegative, and the lower-semicontinuity and endpoint arguments establish attainment of the expected-price minimum. Adding the linear menu item handles the limit as the scale tends to zero. The factor $1-\eta$ correctly makes the guarantee robust to participation ties. Consequently the result concerns the infimum over mechanisms, as stated, and does not require an unproved claim that one mechanism attains the limiting ratio exactly.

I inspected the interval-verification logic and reproduced the complete Arb check using python-flint 0.6.0 with two workers. All 148 cells passed, with 702,247 step–parameter certificates and a smallest certified upper slack of approximately $0.00438637038$. I separately checked that the supplied repaired vectors are exactly the running maxima of the original vectors. The adaptive bounds cover intervals of parameters; the small-$a$, large-$a$, and large-$b$ cases have explicit analytic arguments. In particular, the cap $a^*=\min(A_i,1/\lambda)$ handles the boundary of the admissible parameter region. This supports Theorem 3 beyond a finite numerical sampling claim. I inspected the first checker's source and retained results, but did not reproduce that checker or regenerate the linear programs. The reported independent development histories of the two programs are provenance claims, not something established by this review.

For novelty, I compared the submission with FHL [1], TTW's arXiv:2602.18038v1 [2], especially Sections 4–5 and Appendix B, and Hartline's survey [4]. The supplied rStation index contains no closely related pricing paper. TTW already supplies the hidden-pricing construction, the numerical $0.79$ guarantee, and the $2/(1+2\ln 2)$ impossibility bound, including the interpretation that the latter strengthens FHL's bound. The submission appropriately credits these results. Its publishable addition is the inspectable rigorous certificate and the precise handling of positive densities, real bids, minimum attainment, and ties. The revelation-gap arithmetic is a consequence of those additions and the cited truthful bounds, not a separately new pricing principle.

The main argument is clearly organized and understandable to a mechanism-design reader. The following minor corrections would improve precision and reproducibility:

1. In Section 2, define $\hat F_{\lambda,b}$ explicitly as the shifted exponential with survival $e^{-\lambda(v-b)}$ for $v\ge b$. The current phrase “its scaling by $1/\lambda$” suggests scaling $\hat F_{1,b}$, which would move the support endpoint to $b/\lambda$. The subsequent formulas consistently use the intended identity $\hat F_{\lambda,b}=\lambda^{-1}\hat F_{1,\lambda b}$ in distribution.
2. The final paragraph of Section 6 assigns $[0.79,0.838]$ to $\beta_{\mathrm{all}}$. This is the reciprocal, max–min convention. Use $1/\beta_{\mathrm{all}}$ there and retain the exact upper endpoint $2/(1+2\ln 2)$ before rounding.
3. Update the opening and conclusion of `checker2/REPORT.md` to distinguish its historical rejection of the raw vectors from the successful certificate for the running-max vectors used in Theorem 3. The current manuscript and README explain this correctly, but the standalone report appears to contradict them.
4. Clarify the arithmetic semantics: the Arb checker interprets stored function values as exact dyadic rationals, breakpoints and constants as exact rationals, and the $b$ endpoints as exact decimals. Section 4's blanket description “exact dyadic inputs” is too broad. Also identify the precise convolution-closure result in [6].

These corrections do not undermine the central results. The symbolic revelation-gap statement appropriately separates the new argument from the externally reported numerical bounds on truthful mechanisms.
