# A Single Modular Character Is Complete for SYM∘LTF Counting

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 2 · 2026-10-26T13:28:06.967371Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/computer_science/papers/1/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Williams' algorithmic method turns nontrivial algorithms estimating acceptance probabilities into circuit lower bounds, and the strongest known gate-count lower bounds for depth-two threshold circuits follow this route. For circuits $C(x)=f(\sum_{i=1}^{q}T_i(x))$ with an arbitrary symmetric top $f:\{0,\ldots,q\}\to\{0,1\}$ and linear threshold bottom gates $T_i$ on $h$ variables, a natural plan is to estimate a single modular character of the bottom-gate sum and recover $f$ by linearity. We show that this primitive is as hard as the general problem. Let $R$ be the least power of two larger than $q$ and $\omega=e^{2\pi i/R}$. In deterministic polynomial time we construct $M=O(q^2)$ linear threshold functions $U_1,\ldots,U_M$ on $h+2\log_2 R+1$ variables, appearing with unit coefficients in the character exponent (their internal threshold weights remain general), such that $\mathbb E\,\omega^{\sum_{j}U_j}=R^{-1}\Pr_x[C(x)=1]$. The compression comes from logarithmically many uniform selector variables, combining character orthogonality with exact cancellation of rejected top values. Conversely, the character expectation is a Fourier combination of $R$ residue probabilities, each a symmetric-top counting query, so the primitives are polynomially interreducible when $R$ and $M$ are polynomially bounded. Estimating the acceptance probability within $\varepsilon$ requires the character expectation within $\varepsilon/R$, and in the reverse direction accuracy $\varepsilon/R$ per residue suffices. Deterministic algorithms transfer to deterministic algorithms; randomized ones transfer with a union bound over at most $R$ queries. The character expectation is complex-valued and differs from the Boolean acceptance probability of a single $\mathrm{MOD}_R$ gate. The equivalence is polynomial and need not preserve a particular gate-size exponent or precision regime. The result is a representation theorem: it gives no new algorithm or circuit lower bound and makes no claim about characters with a fixed modulus.
