# Accept

This update makes sound clarifications to the accepted representation theorem and preserves its substantive contribution. The construction and proof are unchanged: arbitrary $\mathrm{SYM}\circ\mathrm{LTF}$ counting is encoded in one growing-modulus character expectation with $O(q^2)$ gates and $O(\log q)$ additional variables. The revision introduces no unsupported extension of that result.

The new precision and determinism paragraph is correct. Multiplying the forward character estimate by $R$ amplifies additive error by $R$. Conversely, since $|\omega^t|=1$, summing $R$ residue estimates each accurate within $\varepsilon/R$ incurs error at most $\varepsilon$. The stated randomized success bounds follow from the single forward query and a union bound over the reverse queries; independence is unnecessary. Explicitly warning that polynomial equivalence does not preserve a particular gate-size exponent or restricted precision regime appropriately limits the algorithmic consequence.

Replacing “unweighted LTFs” with unit coefficients in the character exponent resolves an important ambiguity: the threshold gates still have general internal weights. Distinguishing the complex character expectation from a single Boolean modular acceptance probability is also correct. The supplement now accurately describes its floating-point checks at tolerance $10^{-9}$; these finite checks support implementation consistency, while the general identity rests on the retained proof. The numerical calculation itself has not changed.

The revised attribution improves the paper. Separating the algorithmic lower-bound route [2] from the Kane–Williams restriction method [3] corrects the earlier overgeneralization. The added comparison with Gopalan–Kane–Meka [7] identifies the relevant distinction: their Fourier shapes factor across separate coordinates, whereas this construction permits overlapping LTFs and uses selectors to encode an arbitrary symmetric profile. These changes address the previous review's qualifications without asserting a new algorithm, circuit lower bound, or fixed-modulus result. Acceptance remains warranted for this narrowly scoped structural contribution.
