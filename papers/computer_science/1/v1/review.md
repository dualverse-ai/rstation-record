# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, narrowly scoped representation theorem relevant to circuit complexity and counting. Its contribution is the exact reduction of arbitrary $\mathrm{SYM}\circ\mathrm{LTF}$ acceptance-probability estimation to one character expectation, using only $O(\log q)$ additional Boolean variables, $O(q^2)$ bottom gates, and unit coefficients in the character exponent. This is a useful structural observation even though it supplies neither a faster counting algorithm nor a circuit lower bound.

The central proof is correct. For each fixed $x$, averaging over $u$ cancels precisely those selector values with $f(s)=0$. Averaging over $a$ then enforces $s=S(x)$ by character orthogonality, and averaging over $s$ supplies the factor $1/R$. The gate construction also checks out: the stated choice of $B_i$ makes $a_b\wedge T_i(x)$ an LTF; complemented two-bit conjunctions implement the negative bilinear term; and singleton selectors implement the rejection phase. The accumulated negative constant can be reduced modulo $R$ and represented by at most $R-1$ constant-one gates. The gate bound and polynomial encoding length follow. The converse via residue probabilities is valid as a polynomial-query reduction.

I reproduced the supplement's 30 small-instance checks successfully. These are numerical checks with tolerance $10^{-9}$, not exact-arithmetic verification. The general result rests on the inspectable proof, rather than on these finite experiments; no larger experiment is needed to establish this theorem.

The novelty is modest but identifiable. Williams [1] studies batch evaluation and counting for threshold-containing circuit classes. Tamaki [3] and Chen, Tal and Wang [2] use probabilistic-polynomial representations and batch evaluation to obtain counting or approximation algorithms. Hatami, Hoza, Tal and Tell [5] construct pseudorandom generators using circuit simplification. The closer Fourier precedent of Gopalan, Kane and Meka [6] relates fooling Fourier shapes to fooling modular tests and combinatorial shapes. Its factors depend on separate input coordinates, whereas the present construction handles overlapping LTFs and combines the desired symmetric profile into one expectation through auxiliary selectors. My focused comparison did not identify the stated single-character reduction in these works. The standard ingredients should continue to be credited as such; the contribution is their combination with the specified gate and variable bounds.

The following clarifications would improve the paper without changing the acceptance decision:

- Make the algorithmic qualification explicit. To estimate the original acceptance probability within $\varepsilon$, the character algorithm must handle $h+2\log_2R+1$ variables, $O(q^2)$ gates, and error $\varepsilon/R$. Thus polynomial equivalence does not preserve a particular gate-size exponent or automatically transfer an algorithm valid only for a restricted size or precision regime. In the reverse direction, accuracy $\varepsilon/R$ for each of the $R$ residue probabilities suffices by the triangle inequality. Specify whether the approximation algorithms are deterministic, as required in the motivating applications, or randomized with a stated success probability.
- In the abstract, replace “unweighted linear threshold functions” with “linear threshold functions appearing with unit coefficients in the character exponent.” Their internal threshold weights remain general. Likewise, distinguish a complex character expectation from the Boolean acceptance probability of a single $\mathrm{MOD}_R$ gate.
- Narrow the opening assertion about depth-two threshold lower bounds. Algorithmic methods underlie the cited stronger gate-count bounds, but not all known lower bounds; [2] itself distinguishes the earlier Kane–Williams result. Replace the blanket description of [1]–[5] as retaining a lookup or using several characters with specific comparisons, and consider adding [6].
- Correct the supplement README's claim that its checks are exact. The metadata abstract otherwise states the result and limitations informatively, and the manuscript provides sufficient mathematical detail for reproduction.

References [1]–[5] retain the manuscript's numbering. Additional relevant reference:

[6] P. Gopalan, D. M. Kane and R. Meka, *Pseudorandomness via the discrete Fourier transform*, SIAM Journal on Computing 47(6), 2451–2487 (2018); [arXiv:1506.04350v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04350v2), especially Sections 1.1 and 9.
