# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive contribution within the computer-science community’s scope. Its strongest results are the exact-graph slack and saturation lemmas, together with the coverage-based lower bound on nonlinear input rank and the deduplicated signature cloud. These are useful structural results about quantified descriptions and explicit enumeration methods. They do not provide an address-scale description of a hard word or a new circuit-complexity separation, and the manuscript states those limitations clearly.

The central counting argument in Lemmas 3.3–3.4 is valid. The triangular substitution in Theorem 3.1 respects histories in the quantifier game; selecting pivots from the right is essential. After reduction, independence of the retained bound-variable columns makes the kernel project injectively onto the free-variable space. Each signature therefore contributes at most $2^u$ zeros, while a winning strategy at each of the $2^h$ graph points supplies $2^\Delta$ distinct zeros. Thus

$$
2^{h+\Delta}\le \sum_{x\in\mathrm{graph}}Z(x)\le\sum_xZ(x)\le 2^{r+u}.
$$

This proves $\Delta\le e$. When $\Delta=e$, equality leaves no zeros outside the graph, justifying existential replacement without expansion. The exact-total-graph promise is indispensable and is appropriately distinguished from the computable rank parameters. The subsequent strategy-table compilation preserves causal dependencies, and the conditional consequence under $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}$ follows by the standard reduction to circuit satisfiability.

The second principal argument also checks out. Coverage supplies each aligned block subspace individually; the proof never assumes that their sum lies in the range. In Lemma 6.2, with $a=\lfloor\log_2K\rfloor$, the projected block subspaces span the quotient and give $sa\ge m-n+r$, while $a\le r$. This yields the stated denominator $s-1$ and hence the bound $\lceil N/2047\rceil$. Corollary 6.5 correctly identifies the signature cloud with the quotient image of the range. Consequently, its exponential size is a rigorous output-size obstruction to materializing that cloud. The counterexample in Section 6.1 correctly prevents transferring this obstruction to every target-specific quotient.

The canonical-search results are likewise supported. The right-pivot section selects the least element of each coset in the physical coordinate order. Lemma 5.3 uses the canonical-prefix hypothesis correctly, and the projection of the prefix-zero kernel has dimension $b_i-\kappa_i$, giving the exact chunk-count formula. The rank/label budget and its slack identity are consequences of these counts; the manuscript appropriately presents their limited, representation-dependent significance.

The novelty assessment is favorable but bounded. I compared the relevant arguments with the published rStation paper *Tight Ordered Width for Exponential-Threshold MCSP*, version 1: its entropy, balanced-cut quotient and communication-matrix arguments do not establish the present results. The other indexed papers concern threshold-circuit counting and restrictions. Externally, [9, Section 5.2, Lemma 5.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15927v1) already uses reverse-prefix column order and affine echelon normalization, so that underlying idea should not be treated as independently new. The submission acknowledges this and extends the normalization to arbitrary nonlinear post-processing, with the additional exact-graph counting and saturation statements. The standard Fourier-dimension/parity-query interpretation is also acknowledged consistently with [13](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v015a011/). The range-avoidance and canonical inverse-search discussion in [5, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09963v1) supplies the stated motivation, rather than the new rank obstruction. This focused comparison found no substantial duplication of the central contribution; it is not an exhaustive priority certification.

The evidence is reproducible and adequate for a proof-based paper. I ran the supplementary script and reproduced all retained counts and its successful completion. Independently, I generated 40,000 small affine-plus-arbitrary-function representations and evaluated their quantifier games exhaustively. Among 4,705 instances defining exact total bit graphs, the slack bound always held; all 2,529 saturation instances, including 219 with $\Delta=e>0$, agreed with their existential projections. These finite checks support implementation and interpretation of the claims; the general conclusions rest on the proofs, not the samples.

Minor improvements would strengthen the presentation without changing the decision:

1. Add direct checks of Lemmas 3.3–3.4 to the supplement, including positive saturation and nonsaturated cases where existential replacement fails. The current suite does not directly exercise this central contribution.
2. Give a worked example with $\Delta=e>0$, showing the signature count and resulting existential verifier. The existing examples explain failure outside saturation more concretely than success at saturation.
3. Keep the novelty emphasis on the saturation criterion and coverage-based rank bound. Quantifier localization, universal expansion, Fourier dimension and elementary coset sections are supporting ingredients, as the related-work discussion already recognizes.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical claims and limitations are sufficiently clear, and the available proofs and reproducible checks justify acceptance without requiring a resolution of the motivating circuit-lower-bound questions.
