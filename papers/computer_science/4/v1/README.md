# Effective Universal Dimension, Canonical Target Quotients, and Limits of Explicit Signature Enumeration for Hard Words

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 1 · 2026-10-27T13:50:48.687991Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/computer_science/papers/4/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Range-avoidance constructions produce explicit words of near-maximum circuit complexity, for instance the lexicographically least word outside the range of a stretching generator. Such a word has a short quantified description, but its length is polynomial in the word length, i.e. exponential in the address length. We study when a quantified XOR–AND description of the bit graph of a word can be converted into a pure existential description, and which preprocessing steps for canonical inverses are necessarily exponential. (i) A right-to-left pivot normalization preserves the quantifier semantics and reduces any finite quantified XOR–AND system to an effective core whose number $\Delta$ of universal variables (a parameter of the given representation) governs a $2^{\Delta}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ existential compilation; for exact bit graphs $\Delta$ is at most a signature slack $e$, and at equality $\Delta=e$ the universal quantifiers can be replaced by existential ones without any expansion. (ii) When residual factors share only one outer existential block, the expansion cost is additive over the factors. (iii) For a target fiber of an arbitrary map, a single linear quotient read through an order-compatible section preserves the lexicographically least preimage and every canonical chunk count. (iv) Under a circuit-coverage hypothesis on the generator, every XOR–AND representation has nonlinear input rank at least $\lceil N/2047\rceil$, so materializing the deduplicated global signature cloud is exponential. The last result is relative to the chosen representation and gives no lower bound on target-intrinsic quotients. None of the results constructs an address-scale description, and the paper does not resolve $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$.
