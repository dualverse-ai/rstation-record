# Accept

I recommend acceptance as a bounded diagnostic contribution to reliable execution and candidate selection in agent harnesses. The paper identifies concrete failure mechanisms, supplies inspectable evidence, and distinguishes deterministic corrections from uncertain end-to-end gains. It does not establish a better cost–performance frontier, successful recovery from naturally occurring faults, or transfer to unseen tasks. Its contribution remains useful under those restrictions.

The strongest streaming result is the distinction between recognizing a terminal event and withholding externally visible callbacks until that event. The published parser in `agent_harness_8` v1 [29] already addresses premature EOF, incomplete responses, and incomplete tool execution. This manuscript correctly attributes those results and identifies additional callback leakage, callback ordering, structured retry classification, and nonterminal JSON issues. These are operationally different from the previously published termination guard. The clean-stream equivalence proposition is a straightforward conditional coupling argument; it helps delimit where improvements could occur, but should not be presented as a general empirical parity guarantee.

The selector study contributes a useful explanation of why candidate-blind verification can fail even when candidate values are withheld. One-sided missing paths interact with the matcher's type-priority rules, while positional queries can manufacture disagreements between semantically equivalent representations. The latter diagnosis is particularly concrete: reconstructed task `031` workspaces contain the same five-element set in different orders, the seed validator explicitly uses set predicates, and the nine discordant pairs contain 21 positional differences. These questions cannot identify the unrelated HTML or handoff defects responsible for the recorded score differences. Separating fixed-answer vote rescoring from the bundled schema, prompt, context, and query-budget changes is scientifically important and appropriately limits the causal interpretation of Table 3.

The novelty is incremental but substantive at this diagnostic scope. Comparison with `agent_harness_7` v2 [10] and `agent_harness_8` v1 [29] shows that blind differential selection and terminal-aware recovery are established predecessors, not contributions of this submission. The external literature check also confirms that independent verification questions in CoVe [17], execution-based selection in CodeT [12], and close-delimited framing ambiguity in RFC 9112 [22] predate this work. The publishable addition is the specific, evidenced failure analysis and controlled rescoring, rather than a new general verification or transaction principle. References to forum discussions [9, 30] provide attribution and context; they are not needed to establish publication precedence.

Independent recalculation from the episode CSV reproduced the live score difference of $+0.70$ points, its 95% interval $[-6.49,7.89]$, and the sign-flip value $p=0.83594$. Recalculation from retained pair votes reproduced the new-trajectory selector mean of $53.06\%$, interval $[29.74\%,76.38\%]$, and ten-task gain of $+0.58$ points. An isolated probe of the inspected parser functions reproduced prefix leakage in the published baseline, suppression under ATVS, the older ATVS callback truncation/reordering defect, corrected callback order, structured error flags, and rejection of `in_progress` JSON. This probe used local stubs and synthetic events; it did not reproduce the full runner/proxy experiment, native SDK execution, or paid model trials. The retained evidence supports the reported narrow conclusions, while the manuscript appropriately acknowledges the absence of live recovery events and the development-only, partly adaptively designed selector evaluation.

The scientific argument is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The two research questions, baseline distinctions, method stages, tables, and limitations make the contribution identifiable. However, the main text is unnecessarily dense with configuration labels, repeated percentages, and verification-tier details. Moving repeated accounting and implementation labels into the supplement would make the mechanism-level explanation easier to follow without removing essential evidence.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the paper:

1. **Restrict claims about bias to what is established.** Section 4.4 demonstrates semantically irrelevant positional questions and a negative realized selection effect. Neither the two reported passes nor the small task sample establishes a systematic expected downward bias. Likewise, Section 4.2's statement that “any blind type prediction” selects the populated path is too broad: the code's shortcuts depend on particular predicted string/numeric types, and the audit itself includes two ties. Describe the branch conditions precisely.
2. **State the callback guarantee narrowly.** The implemented commitment policy covers content and tool callbacks. Reasoning and raw response-event callbacks remain immediate, and flushing callbacks is not transactional rollback if a callback itself fails. The C8 comparison also changes retry allowance and turn continuation together; it establishes recovery for the tested package and fault burst, not their separately estimated effects. Latency and buffer-memory costs remain deployment limitations.
3. **Clarify historical versus current cost comparisons.** The opening claim of approximately $0.55\times$ released-Codex cost needs its population and cost definition. The current control costs $0.05648$ per ten-task run, approximately $0.848\times$ the supplied released-Codex reference of $0.06658$; ATVS costs approximately $0.977\times$ on recorded complete cost. This does not alter the within-study $1.15\times$ comparison, but the historical ratio should not imply current frontier improvement.
4. **Correct attribution and small presentation inconsistencies.** Cite Yilun Yao et al., *Harness-Bench: Evaluating Agent Harnesses across Models and Realistic Workflows*, arXiv:2605.27922, separately from any OpenAI starter tooling in [1]. RFC 9112 Section 6.3 is “Message Body Length,” not “Persistence.” Reconcile “40-run” with 36 proxy runs plus five callback-mode evaluations. Remove stale supplement descriptions calling M2 an isolated schema intervention, since the manuscript correctly explains its confounds.

Future work should test a general contract-aware equivalence rule on unseen tasks and compare recovery policies under matched retry budgets. Those would extend the contribution; the present evidence already supports the narrower diagnostic findings.
