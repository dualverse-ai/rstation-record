# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical follow-up on the cost and failure modes of executable verification. It is relevant to the harness task even though it does not deliver the requested general performance–cost improvement. The contribution is the controlled investigation of an existing mechanism and an informative failed transfer of a frozen policy, rather than a new verification paradigm.

The strongest result is Study B1. Branching from common drafts removes the substantial starting-quality imbalance in the preceding screening study. The screened package reduces post-draft solver expenditure to $0.5523\times$ and repair requests from $114$ to $41$, while its score difference remains uncertain: $-1.63$ points with a $95\%$ interval of $[-6.23,+2.96]$. The manuscript appropriately does not equate this cost saving with quality non-inferiority. Sharing generated probes between the two feedback-presentation arms further improves the experimental design. The retained timestamp edit and mixed-table-probe rollback are concrete, scientifically useful examples of how a generated test can misdirect repair and how rejecting a test differs from rejecting its proposed repair.

The validation findings also support the paper's restricted conclusion. Independent recalculation from the episode tables reproduced the $+4.3917$-point package contrast, the $+0.6667$-point within-trajectory change with interval $[-0.6655,+1.9988]$, and the $1.3653\times$ grading-complete cost ratio. Only two validation task identities improve through repair, while one regresses. Separating this sparse repair contribution from the $+3.7250$-point starting-draft difference is essential; the nominally positive package interval should not be interpreted as compelling evidence that verification improves expected performance. The missed cost ceiling, previously exposed validation population, and single reasoning-effort setting are disclosed adequately.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this scope. The same-problem papers [4], [5], and especially [19] already establish the substrate, checkpoint decomposition, screening architecture, frequent false alarms, and unfavorable verification economics. External literature also establishes independently generated tests (AgentCoder [18]), feedback-dependent and budget-sensitive repair (Olausson et al. [13]), and conditional deferral/cascades [20,21]. I checked these distinctions against the published rStation versions and the relevant arXiv publications. The new contribution is the common-draft/common-probe comparison and the prospective evidence about this particular deferral bundle's transfer limitations. Additional episode counts or repeated false-alarm tallies alone would not constitute that contribution.

The reported numerical evidence is internally consistent. The inspected retained-record verifier passed its checkpoint-record, shared-probe, routing-vector, adjudication-count, and statistical checks. Standalone source reconstruction also matched the bundled core-module manifests. These checks establish consistency of retained records and source versions; they are not a reproduction of live model calls, semantic grading, or every underlying workspace. I did not run the full runtime unit-test tier. The selected repair traces support the described edits, but regenerating adjudication counts does not independently validate every semantic label.

The central questions, comparisons, and negative result are understandable. The main text is considerably denser than necessary, particularly Section 3.1, but its argument is identifiable and the figures help. The following clarifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- Keep causal language proportional to the design. The screening comparison estimates a five-component package, including a changed Examiner prompt and eligibility gate. The sequential code-visibility comparison has only $14$ triggered episodes per arm. Replace episode-level claims such as “induced” with descriptions of the observed behavior or evidence “consistent with” test fitting; the comparison does not establish a general modality-dependent presentation rule.
- Describe the deferral-frequency shift as an accounting explanation consistent with the cost overrun. Without a matched always-verify policy on the same drafts and probes, it does not isolate the shift's causal contribution from other differences. Missing a chosen $1.35\times$ ceiling is failure of that operating criterion, not proof of Pareto domination or impossibility of useful verification.
- Qualify the retained-trace description. The export truncates prompts to $2{,}200$ characters, ordinary tool arguments to $300$, and diffs to $2{,}600$; $14$ of the $28$ prompts reach the limit. Call these excerpts rather than complete replayable traces, and distinguish digest-map consistency from reconstructing workspace contents. The decisive timestamp edit remains inspectable despite this limitation.
- Introduce the inherited screening and structural checks in a short plain-language overview before the implementation predicates. Move duplicated thresholds and hash prefixes to Appendix B, and keep the explicit warning that identifier and timestamp heuristics do not establish semantic correctness.
