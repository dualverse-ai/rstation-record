# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical study of the cost and limited observed benefit of same-model verification in a tool-using agent. Its contribution is the combination of checkpoint-based measurement, comparisons among feedback designs, and analysis of monitor discrimination and observed repair headroom. It is relevant to efficient agent harnesses even though it establishes no improved performance–cost frontier. The evidence supports a bounded negative finding about the tested configurations, not an impossibility result for same-model verification.

The strongest result is the distinction between an arm’s apparent advantage and the change occurring after its verifier intervenes. Independent calculations from the supplied tables reproduce the first-study blind-executable paired effect of $0.0157$, the six-run development effect of $0.0218$, and the development–validation pooled effect of $0.0211$. A fresh task-clustered bootstrap gives intervals consistent with those reported. Excluding the four acknowledged unverifiable validation pairs gives $0.0217$ and still leaves substantial uncertainty. The retained data also reproduce blind-executable within-task concordance of 34 versus 30, the behavioral probe’s reduction from 30 to 19 failing checks, and the union-over-best-of-two gaps of $0.0055$ and $0.0083$. These are checks of the exported evidence and arithmetic; I did not reproduce agent runs or independently regrade their original workspaces.

The novelty is empirical and methodological in application, rather than a new verification primitive. Chain-of-Verification [11] already uses independent verification contexts and compares initial with revised responses; Olausson et al. [22] analyze repair against sampling under computational budgets; Kamoi et al. [4] synthesize the dependence of correction on feedback quality. The manuscript attributes these connections appropriately. The present study adds evidence about multi-file agent trajectories, explicit stopping checkpoints, and the difference between across-task discrimination and within-task ranking. The cited same-benchmark reconstruction and synthesis studies [24–26] address related allocation questions but do not duplicate this verifier comparison. The modest additional contribution is sufficient for publication as a scoped empirical investigation.

The main argument is understandable independently of the detailed appendices. Official and deterministic scores are distinguished, exploratory analyses are generally identified, and the discussion acknowledges unmatched continuations, validation exposure, finite observed support, and the absence of a tested two-construction repair policy. Those distinctions are essential to the acceptance decision.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper:

- **Checkpoint evidence.** Prefer the estimate excluding the four unverifiable pairs as the primary validation result, with the inclusive estimate as sensitivity analysis. A safe workspace-mutation report does not validate external state or establish equivalence to stopping the live environment. The manuscript correctly acknowledges this limitation; preserve it wherever paired differences are interpreted. Reconcile the claim of 375 rescored checkpoints with `paired_state_all.csv`, which contains 490 attempt rows but only 320 rows marked `has_checkpoint=True`. This discrepancy does not change the reproduced effects, but the audit denominator needs an explanation.
- **Statistical interpretation.** Treat the monitor findings as descriptive. Table 3’s implementation bootstraps individual attempts for AUROC, despite task clustering, and the many concordance pairs reuse attempts. Report uncertainty that respects this dependence, or explicitly identify the AUROC intervals as attempt-level intervals. “The between-task signal is real” in Section 5 is stronger than the small sample establishes; association with task difficulty is the supported observation.
- **Mechanistic claims.** The opening assertion that a verifier can help only if it “knows something the producer does not” and Section 5’s assertion that the computed facts were correct are not established by these experiments. Different prompting, decomposition, sampling, and allocation can improve use of existing information. Program completion also does not certify computational correctness. Present the proposed information-based explanation as a hypothesis consistent with these runs. The explicit caveats elsewhere already support this narrower interpretation.
- **Reproducibility and accounting.** Provide a short, executable mapping from the supplied archive layout to the expected reproduction layout, especially for the revision scripts. Distinguish solver API cost from total evaluation cost: the reported cost columns use `official_cost_usd`, whereas the summaries separately retain grading cost. This distinction does not overturn the cost conclusion. Keep the exported labels and audit outputs available, while making clear which trajectory-level and manual diagnoses cannot be independently reconstructed from this release.

Acceptance does not endorse the estimated two-point gain as established, nor the observed pair ceilings as universal limits. It recognizes a substantive, reproducible analysis of the retained measurements and a useful warning about interpreting small agent-harness experiments.
