# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical and methodological contribution to understanding efficient harnesses. It does not demonstrate a new performance–cost frontier or a deployable router. Its contribution is to distinguish cross-package clause complementarity from sampling variation and task specialization, test a specification–grader mismatch under controlled conditions, and measure reasoning replay on a fixed lean harness. These findings have substantive relevance to deciding which harness interventions merit further investment.

The central mathematics is correct. Under the stated independent-draw model, the distinct-pair estimators are unbiased, the signed sample decomposition holds, and

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{route}}^\star \leq \sqrt{D_{\mathrm{task}}} \leq \sqrt{\Delta_{\mathrm{div}}/2}.
$$

The explicit negative-estimate example and the distinction between quadratic divergence and linear routing gains prevent important misinterpretations. My independent calculation from the retained clause vectors reproduced the pooled excess union of $0.78625$ percentage points, its nominal interval $[-1.02,2.59]$, the $7.7275$-point within-package gain, and the $27.81125\%$ joint failure rate. It also reproduced the cross-fitted routing gain of $2.8125$ points over the package average, with interval $[0.09,5.53]$. The latter is an informative diagnostic despite its uncertain advantage over Mode 50 alone.

The synthetic evidence is inspectable and supports the narrow conclusions. After inspecting the relevant code and retained programs, I re-graded all 160 final outputs and 120 reconstructed initial drafts with the supplied deterministic grader. Every final score matched; the only draft-to-final score change was the reported Mode 50 MO-1b improvement from $0.75$ to $1.00$. The seven initial MO-4 algorithm failures were distributed as reported: five in Mode 55 and one each in Modes 50 and 54. The specification and grader source establish that MO-4b deliberately removes three required reason literals while retaining exact-string grading. This is a controlled illustration of representation mismatch, not evidence that all persistent benchmark failures have that explanation.

Independent recomputation also reproduced Experiment W's $-0.20$-point score difference and $[-5.97,5.57]$ interval, and its solver costs agree with the retained token counts and price schedule. The decision not to advance replay under the declared gate is reasonable. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges that these data neither establish equivalence nor exclude its $+3.50$-point target. My checks reproduce calculations and deterministic grading of retained artifacts; they do not reproduce live model generation or independently authenticate the original execution history.

The distinction from previous work is sufficient, although the theoretical ingredients are classical. The published same-problem paper [4], especially Sections 4.6 and 4.10, already established convention-sensitive failures, within-package pair unions, and limited merging headroom. Papers [5], [7], and [8] supply the lean substrate, cost accounting, and related intervention evidence. The present cross-package bias-corrected analysis, same-task cross-fitting, controlled string-removal experiment, and replay intervention add evidence beyond those results. External literature likewise limits the novelty claim: unbiased quadratic two-sample statistics are established [11, 21], and learned cost-aware routing is established [22]. Here the novelty lies in the empirical application and distinctions between estimands, rather than a new statistical theory or routing algorithm. The cited self-correction literature [13–15] provides context without duplicating this particular intervention study.

The research questions, estimands, experimental comparisons, and decisive evidence are understandable in the main text. The paper is unusually dense and repeats many numerical results in the introduction and discussion, but this does not materially obscure the argument. The following clarifications would improve the accepted work:

- **Keep routing and union claims diagnostic.** Cross-fitting holds out repetitions of the same task identities, not unseen tasks. The text acknowledges this; make it equally explicit wherever routing results are summarized. Clausewise union also assumes oracle access to which clauses pass and is not an implemented ensemble's score. No new prospective routing or composition benefit has been demonstrated.
- **Qualify confidence statements.** The task-level Student-$t$ intervals and transformed routing upper limit are nominal approximations under the stated independence and stability assumptions. Concurrent scheduling alone does not establish whole-run exchangeability. For the pooled zero-pass results, explain the estimand across heterogeneous harnesses: the reported Clopper–Pearson limits are not bounds on every individual package, and the post-selected collection of zero-pass clauses does not receive simultaneous 95% coverage.
- **Narrow one causal sentence.** Section 6.1 says the audit proves that specification placement caused guard variation. Draft timing rules out receipt-induced initial guards, but does not isolate the cause of their creation. Describe ambiguous placement as a plausible explanation unless a wording intervention is tested. The observed failure of receipts to repair these five guards remains supported.
- **Align presentation with the contribution.** Shorten the metadata abstract and introductory numerical inventory, and describe the estimator as a classical U-statistic application. Adding the connection to linear-kernel squared mean discrepancy [21] and distinguishing same-task retrospective routing from learned prospective routing [22] would sharpen attribution.
