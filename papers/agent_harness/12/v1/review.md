# Accept

The paper provides a sound, clearly delimited empirical contribution to efficient agent harnesses: it separates information available to the model when writing a program from filesystem access available to that program during execution. On the studied forty-task roster, supplying indexed contents before a single executable response produces a large improvement while holding the response interface and execution opportunities fixed. This is a useful controlled result even though the proposed service fails its registered deployment criterion and does not establish a general performance–cost frontier improvement.

The central comparison is convincing within this scope. The acquisition wrapper maps both single-response services to the same terminal policy, schema, execution path and delivery mechanism; the treatment modifies the opening message with bounded file contents. The design uses paired task assignments, two repetitions and reversed within-task service order. Missing grades remain in the denominator and receive their full $[0,100]$ range. The resulting gain of $45.29$–$55.29$ points survives these bounds, and the whole-task bootstrap envelope of $[29.40,66.74]$ remains strongly positive. Runtime access alone is therefore insufficient to reproduce the supplied-content service's performance in this setting.

I independently recomputed the main service means, costs, missing-grade bounds, request and full-credit counts, attachment totals, optimal pooled lottery and 10,000-draw pooled bootstrap envelopes from the supplied scalar records. These agree with the manuscript. The primary estimator and historical-contrast estimator also regenerated outputs matching their archived hashes. This verifies the numerical analysis of the released observations; it is not an independent reacquisition of model responses, regrading of delivered workspaces, or reproduction of the native Docker rehearsal. The released implementation and acquisition instructions make those stronger checks substantially more concrete, but they require the separately provisioned benchmark environment and model access.

The contribution is distinguishable from the closest published work. Batch-Index [5] already established the inventory and batch-read reminder, and its role is properly credited. The earlier rStation inlining study [4] jointly changed the input and available tools, preventing attribution to supplied information alone. This paper resolves that specific ambiguity under a fixed executable interface. My focused check of RepoCoder [6], CodeAct [2] and Agentless [3] confirms substantial precedents for retrieval before generation, executable actions and staged localization. Thus neither supplying source text nor using Python actions is itself novel. The incremental contribution is the controlled measurement, its magnitude, and its comparison with the price and quality of an adaptive opening. I did not find substantial duplication of that experiment in these relevant publications.

Several qualifications are essential to the acceptance:

- The $27.65\%$ saving against the model-chosen service does not establish equivalent quality. In repetition two, even the most favorable completion of missing grades violates the one-point tolerance. The paper appropriately reports failure rather than claiming successful noninferiority.
- The $8.24$–$15.74$ point advantage over the affordable lottery is a result for the observed roster and finite comparator menu. Its refitted bootstrap envelope, $[-4.75,24.54]$, includes zero; historical acquisition times and the unidentified charge further limit broader frontier claims.
- These are reused validation identities, not an untouched test of generalization. The paired bootstrap describes task variation conditional on this study; it does not remove selection from prior exposure. Likewise, the contrast with the six-response experiment does not isolate a causal interaction with response budget, because interfaces, notices and acquisition dates also differ. The manuscript explicitly recognizes these limits.

Scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of numerical correctness. The research question, three services, decisive contrast and failed deployment criterion are understandable in the main text. Figure 1 usefully distinguishes missing-data bounds from sampling uncertainty, and the abstract is informative. The paper is organized around its argument rather than an experiment log.

Minor improvements would increase its usefulness: label construction-request counts in Table 1 explicitly as totals across 80 assignments, and present construction-only cost alongside the grading-inclusive price used for benchmark comparison. A small illustrative task example showing what information the index-only program lacked would also help explain the large effect, provided it is presented as illustration rather than evidence of a general failure mechanism. These additions are not necessary for acceptance of the bounded result reported here.
