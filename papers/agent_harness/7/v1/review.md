# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the efficient-harness problem. Its strongest result is the matched adjudication study: mechanically discover disagreements between candidates, ask the model to resolve them from the specification, and vary candidate exposure while retaining the queries and decision rule. The evidence supports investigating this separation of disagreement discovery from judgment. It does not establish a better end-to-end performance–cost frontier, and the manuscript appropriately disclaims that achievement.

The central argument is understandable, and the revised evidence is inspectable. I recomputed all four development arms from the retained answer tuples using the inspected leaf matcher, validated the 495 exported queries, and checked reversal of candidate labels on those answers. The reported accuracies reproduce without discrepancies or order-asymmetric matches in this retained sample: 55.00% versus 33.33% for blind versus sighted Python adjudication, and 59.58% versus 47.92% for structured-data adjudication without versus with companion prose. These are selector accuracies against workspace scores, rather than independently established correctness rates for every oracle answer.

Independent calculations also reconstructed the ten similarity matrices from the retained atom sets and reproduced medoid scores of 54.80%, 57.05%, 58.06%, and 57.08% at $k=1,3,7,19$. Enumerating ordered triples reproduced the 18.60% exact-grid recovery result on task 062. The prospective episode records reproduce the $+2.63$-point contract-prompt contrast, its nominal interval $[-7.72,+12.99]$, and the $1.0625\times$ complete-cost ratio. These checks verify retained-data calculations; they are not fresh model experiments, independent benchmark regrading, or a full execution of the supplied verification suite.

The novelty is narrower than the breadth of the title. Same-problem papers [3] and [8] already establish weak within-task verification, consensus baselines, clause-union diagnostics, and specification-related failures; [7] already investigates controlled visibility effects during repair. MBR-EXEC [12] supplies execution-based consensus, and [16] establishes non-monotonic voting behavior. The meaningful additional contribution here is the concrete differential-witness pipeline and its matched exposure experiment on multi-file workspaces. The expanded consensus curves and contract-prompt experiment provide supporting context, rather than separate fundamental mechanisms. This distinction is sufficient for a useful partial advance, despite the absence of a validation-stage frontier improvement.

Several qualifications should accompany publication:

1. **Treat inferential claims conservatively.** The Python exposure contrast spans only three tasks, with a task-level interval that includes zero. The quoted pair sign-flip values reproduce arithmetically, but “exact” enumeration does not establish the required null symmetry. In particular, the blind estimate uses one stochastic call while the sighted estimate averages two; equal expected accuracy alone does not make these arm summaries exchangeable. State the test assumptions and distinguish conditional evidence on the selected pairs from generalization to fresh tasks and candidate draws. Candidate workspaces also recur across pairs. The descriptive contrast remains useful without presenting its pair-level significance as definitive population evidence.

2. **Avoid equivalence claims from nonsignificance.** Section 4.2 calls structured-data exposure “neutral” and says the prompt comparison “refut[es]” an explanation. The intervals support “no detected difference” or “no demonstrated improvement,” not equivalence or exclusion of a beneficial prompt effect. Likewise, 50/50 successful control episodes demonstrate observed preservation, not a zero underlying regression probability.

3. **Add the closest missing methodological antecedent.** Chain-of-Verification [23], especially its two-step and factored variants, generates verification questions after seeing a draft and then answers them without the draft. This directly anticipates the information separation motivating the present method. Explain the narrower distinction: mechanical, swap-symmetric discovery from two executable candidates, followed by specification labeling and workspace selection. This omission does not erase the new controlled evidence, but the general separation principle should not be presented as new.

4. **Keep the endpoints and implementation limits explicit.** The table-voting result concerns matching observed full-credit grids, not demonstrated semantic invalidity of every newly assembled table. The manuscript now makes this distinction clearly. Similarly, “untruncated” should refer to the retained witness arguments and outcomes: extraction still uses query caps, bounded perturbations, file limits, and heuristic matching. These are reasonable engineering choices, not completeness guarantees. Briefly document how the original 90 pairs were sampled so the expected two-draw gain can be interpreted against a specified sampling population.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance. The questions, pipeline, controlled factors, and limitations can be followed without reconstructing the argument from code. Nevertheless, Section 1 and Table 5 repeat much of the detailed results, while Table 2 is difficult to scan. Shortening that repetition and foregrounding the exposure experiment would make the contribution clearer. These are presentation improvements rather than grounds for rejection.
