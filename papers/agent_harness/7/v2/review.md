# Accept

This is an appropriate clarification of the accepted contribution. The narrower title and revised introduction foreground differential witness discovery and the matched candidate-exposure study. Splitting Table 2 improves readability, and the supporting consensus and contract-prompt results remain subordinate to that argument. The revision does not introduce a separate substantive contribution or materially expand the paper into an unfocused collection.

The revisions address the previous review's principal qualifications: workspace-selector accuracy is distinguished from oracle-answer correctness; recurring workspaces and the one-call versus two-call comparison are acknowledged; pair sign-flip values are no longer presented as definitive population evidence; nonsignificance is no longer equated with equivalence; and extraction limits and pair sampling are explained. The added attribution to Chain-of-Verification [23] accurately recognizes its separation of draft-conditioned question generation from draft-blind answering. The narrower distinction—mechanical discovery from two workspaces followed by specification labeling and differential matching—is clear and consistent with the accepted contribution and the related rStation work [3, 8].

The new Section 4.4 is a limited diagnostic of existing retained answers, suitable for inclusion in this revision. Using the inspected comparison functions in isolation, I reproduced the reported flag counts: $18/29$ full-credit candidate sides and $101/151$ sub-full-credit sides, with mean disagreement fractions $0.45353$ and $0.52548$. I also confirmed that the 180 sides represent 120 unique workspaces. This checks the retained-data calculation, not fresh model generation, independent benchmark grading, or the complete verification suite.

The following qualifications should accompany this diagnostic and its summaries:

1. **Distinguish a single-candidate matcher from a single-candidate policy.** The matcher receives one candidate outcome, but its queries were discovered using both candidates. These results therefore characterize absolute matching on pair-selected disagreement loci; they do not evaluate a deployable $k=1$ gate or establish its cost. Make this dependence explicit in Section 4.4 and Table 5. The existing disclaimer about other $k=1$ policies is helpful, but does not by itself explain this selection issue. Likewise, these comparisons are not a direct evaluation of CoVe [23].

2. **State the denominator and abstention convention.** Six full-credit sides and 26 sub-full-credit sides have no evaluable loci under the new matcher. The implementation counts these as unflagged and assigns disagreement fraction zero. Thus the headline rates combine coverage and disagreement. Among sides with evaluable loci, the corresponding rates are $18/23$ and $101/125$. Retain the all-side calculation if desired, but disclose this convention and avoid implying 180 independent, successfully checked workspaces. The $4.8$-percentage-point difference is a difference in flag rates, not a “specificity/sensitivity gap.”

3. **Clarify the task examples and causal interpretation.** The quoted inversions on 066 and 080 compare higher-scoring versus lower-scoring sides: neither task contributes a full-credit side to this sample. Label them accordingly rather than allowing the preceding full-credit/sub-full-credit contrast to carry over. The roughly $45\%$ mean disagreement on full-credit sides does not independently identify benign representation differences as its cause. Incorrect oracle predictions and limitations of benchmark coverage can also contribute. Present the proposed representation explanation as a hypothesis unless individual disagreements are adjudicated.

4. **Keep the Python contrast's sign consistent.** The revised abstract and introduction attach $[-25.77,+69.10]$ percentage points to an exposure effect of $-21.67$ points. That interval describes the positive blind-minus-sighted contrast. For sighted-minus-blind, use $[-69.10,+25.77]$, or explicitly label the interval as the blind advantage, as Section 4.2 does.

These are bounded reporting and interpretation clarifications. The accepted central evidence is preserved, communication improves overall, and the paper appropriately continues to disclaim an established end-to-end performance–cost frontier improvement.
