# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the efficient-harness problem. The supported result is lower API expenditure for two specified Nanobot packages on the evaluated task population, with unresolved effects on task quality. The comparison against C-Clean makes the study more informative than a stock-versus-modified demonstration: it separates the large savings available from ordinary interface cleanup from the smaller incremental savings associated with the inventory, guidance, and receipt package. The passive-inventory comparison adds a useful implementation-specific observation about whether an exposed capability is actually used. Acceptance does not establish non-inferiority, a new Pareto frontier, or completion of the broader research goal.

The principal numerical findings are supported by the retained measurements. I ran the inspected statistical script and independently reconciled episode costs with all 34 run summaries and their supplied original summary files. The recorded token counts reproduce solver expenditure under the supplied price schedule. The validation complete-cost ratios of $0.7180$ for C-Clean versus Stock, $0.6500$ for WAMD versus Stock, and $0.9053$ for WAMD versus C-Clean reproduce, as do the reported paired score intervals. Aggregating repetitions within task before inference is appropriate. The manuscript correctly acknowledges that WAMD versus C-Clean permits a score loss as large as approximately $5.86$ points under its reported interval. These checks reproduce arithmetic from retained records, not the underlying model experiments or trajectory extraction.

The contribution is distinguishable from the published same-problem paper [4], which studies post-stop verification, continuation, and correlated failures. External comparison with SWE-agent [8], Agentless [9], LLMCompiler [10], and CodeAct [13] confirms that interface engineering, reducing sequential calls, and programmatic actions are established ideas. The novelty here is the measured package decomposition and passive-inventory contrast in this heterogeneous setting, rather than a new general principle of batching or context economy. The cited community posts appropriately credit borrowed components; they are not substitutes for academic precedence. Add discussion of *The Complexity Trap* [20], particularly its treatment of observation masking, trajectory length, and cache economics. Its results are relevant context, but do not duplicate this study's particular comparison.

The scientific argument is identifiable and sufficiently clear: the question, architecture differences, statistical unit, results, and limitations can be followed without reconstructing an unstated method. Table 1 and the exact dispatch templates are particularly useful. Nevertheless, Section 4.1 overemphasizes the chronological development record. Moving most intermediate configurations and closure diagnostics to the appendix would let the main text lead with the two package comparisons and the passive-inventory experiment. The abstract is informative but overloaded with numerical contrasts and internal mode identifiers.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the report:

1. **Describe execution order precisely.** The supplied S7 runner executes Stock, C-Clean, and WAMD successively, then repeats that order; S6 similarly alternates successive control and treatment runs. Thus “concurrent” should be explained as controls collected within the same planned study, not simultaneous or randomized arm execution. Report timestamps and acknowledge possible order effects. The sign-flip tests also require an exchangeability or symmetry justification; task pairing alone does not create randomized treatment assignment. Label the reported significance levels as nominal rather than familywise guarantees.

2. **Keep the mechanism conclusion bounded.** Section 4.3's “directly refutes” and “cost was unchanged” are stronger than warranted by a historical comparison and a nonsignificant test. The observed passive index did not reproduce WAMD's savings or batch-read uptake. That is informative, but neither an equivalence result nor identification of which bundled component caused the savings. The manuscript's explicit qualifications elsewhere should govern these passages too. A concurrent batch-reminder-only control is the most useful next experiment, rather than another elaborate package.

3. **Reconcile the receipt statistic.** Section 4.4 reports $34/184=18.5\%$ immediate stops after completion receipts. However, summing `wamd_ok_receipts` across the 80 WAMD validation rows gives 88, while `wamd_immediate_stop_after_receipt` sums to 34. Explain whether these counts use different event definitions, include duplicated log appearances, or contain an error. Supply the extraction code and relevant trace excerpts. The receipt association is secondary and already appropriately disclaimed as noncausal, but its denominator is currently unauditable.

4. **Narrow the exact-reproduction claim.** Applying the patch to the supplied stock source reproduces all 14 source hashes in the Index-Clean manifest. It does not reproduce 13 of the 16 hashes in the earlier WAMD development manifest. Many differences are explained by adding Mode 49 to mode lists, but the distributed reconstruction script verifies only the later manifest. Distinguish the latest shared implementation from exact historical source snapshots, and retain the earlier snapshots or explicit version deltas. Likewise, `verify_paper_stats.py` recomputes the principal contrasts but does not itself perform all the reconciliation and mechanism-table checks claimed in Section 6.

5. **Complete the evaluation description without expanding the claim.** State reasoning effort and output cap explicitly, and distinguish the configured $200{,}000$-token context window from the supplied price schedule's $272{,}000$-token long-context threshold. Add wall-clock measurements when available. The current configuration points are not performance–cost curves across budgets, and the study does not compare against the supplied Codex baselines or observation masking. Those omissions limit conclusions about the strongest available harness or general frontier; they do not invalidate the narrower Nanobot package comparisons.

The disclosed validation exposure, missed development gate, and exclusion of the ineligible configuration from the primary claims are important limitations. They support treating this as a partial systems finding on a familiar benchmark population, with fresh-task confirmation and quality-preservation testing still outstanding.
