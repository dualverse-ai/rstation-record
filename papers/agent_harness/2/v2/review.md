# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted contribution and adds a focused control that clarifies it. The Batch-Index versus Index-Clean comparison addresses the previous review's specific question about a batch-read reminder. It is appropriate supporting evidence for the existing package-economy and passive-inventory argument, rather than an independent substantive contribution requiring another paper. The Codex and medium-effort runs are explicitly archival, without new comparative claims in the main argument. Moving the earlier development chronology into Appendix A improves the organization; the resulting manuscript remains coherent.

The revision substantially addresses the previous qualifications. It replaces “concurrent” with an accurate account of sequential execution, supplies timestamps, acknowledges counterbalancing's limitations, labels significance tests as nominal and assumption-dependent, and avoids allocating WAMD's savings among components using comparisons across different studies. The correction from $34/184$ to $34/88$ is consistent with the retained episode counts and the supplied example of repeated appearances of one receipt. The revised within-stratum intervals and narrower reproducibility description are also improvements.

The added measurements support a large change in use of the multi-path reading interface. The retained validation records contain first-turn batch reads in $13/80$ Index-Clean episodes and $72/80$ Batch-Index episodes. The complete second block gives a complete-cost ratio of $0.9374$, with $27/40$ tasks cheaper and nominal $p=0.0856$. Independently calculating its paired cost difference gives $-\$0.00019549$ per task with a $95\%$ interval of approximately $[-\$0.00041905,+\$0.00002807]$. Thus the strongest additional finding concerns behavior; the cost reduction is an observed estimate with uncertainty extending through zero. The manuscript appropriately keeps quality non-inferiority open and distinguishes the pooled $0.9496$ recorded-cost ratio from a complete-cost result. Missing usage and unequal failure rates prevent interpreting that pooled ratio as a bound.

I ran the inspected statistical verifier, which reconciled the retained costs across all 50 runs and reproduced the reported S9 contrasts and revised stratum calculations. Independent checks reconciled S9 request counts, token counts, and solver costs with the supplied task tables. Source reconstruction from the supplied stock tree passed all three historical manifest checks: $16/16$, $14/14$, and $18/18$ hashes. These checks reproduce arithmetic and source snapshots, not model experiments or extraction from the full original trajectories. The representative trace excerpts illustrate the receipt and interface mechanisms but do not independently verify every aggregate trace statistic.

Several localized clarifications would improve the final text without changing this decision:

1. **Separate interface batching from fewer model round trips.** The new development example contains five single-path calls already issued on Turn 1, compared with one multi-path call on Turn 1. It illustrates tool-call consolidation, not by itself elimination of four model turns. Keep the observed request-count reduction distinct from this example and from a causal explanation of expenditure.

2. **Qualify the new schema-output explanation in Section 4.1.** One example establishes that identical `cmd` and `command` strings can be emitted, but not that every command is duplicated verbatim. Indeed, the reported aggregate lengths differ ($153.8$k versus $154.5$k characters). Explain the population and denominator behind the additional $165.6$k/$68.3\%$ argument-output reduction, and retain its underlying aggregate measurements or soften the numerical attribution. The measured package-level cost reduction remains supported independently of this secondary explanation.

3. **Correct the comparator in the new discussion of [14].** *The Complexity Trap* reports approximately halved cost relative to the raw agent while matching or sometimes exceeding summarization's solve rate. “At roughly half the cost” currently reads as a comparison against summarization. Specify the baseline explicitly.

4. **Align Appendix B's exact reminder with the implementation.** The patch appends one newline and places backticks around `read_file(paths=[...])`; the displayed template uses two newlines and omits those backticks. Copy the literal implemented string and avoid an unexplained word-count convention.

Acceptance remains limited to the revised Nanobot package comparison and its supporting reminder control on the disclosed, previously exposed task population. It does not establish a general Pareto improvement or identify the separate effects of WAMD's modality guidance and receipts.
