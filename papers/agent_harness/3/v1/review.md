# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of the cost and coverage consequences of screening model-generated tests. It supports publication as a partial advance on efficient agent harnesses. It does not establish a better end-to-end performance–cost frontier, and the manuscript now makes that limitation explicit.

The central contribution is the combination of requirement-level adjudication and a controlled comparison of the bundled screening policy. Screening suppresses many unsupported assertions, but also discards useful checks and leaves substantial residual false alarms. The live comparison connects this imperfect screening to fewer repair requests and lower expenditure, while appropriately leaving repair-quality differences unresolved. That explanation of where verification expenditure goes adds scientific value beyond simply reporting another unsuccessful verifier.

The principal numerical claims withstand independent checks. From the episode records, I reproduced the validation trajectory improvement of $+2.3917$ points, its task-level $95\%$ Student-$t$ interval $[0.2989,4.4844]$, and nominal sign-flip $p=0.03125$. The same records reproduce the lower final score than Batch-Index, $67.75\%$ versus $68.4583\%$, at approximately $1.4207\times$ complete cost. For the screening comparison, I reproduced 49 versus 100 repair requests, approximately $0.8065\times$ complete cost, and the screened-minus-unscreened repair-gain estimate of $+0.3333$ points with interval $[-9.0512,9.7178]$ and $p=1$. The cost-difference calculation reproduces nominal $p=0.048828125$. These results support an intervention-cost finding, not quality non-inferiority.

I also reconciled all 70 run-level cost totals with their retained summaries and all 73 committed first-draft scores with the checkpoint CSVs. After inspecting their code, I independently replayed historical probes #55 and #118 against the bundled candidate modules. Both stop on the allegedly invalid transition to a valid constraint with complete matching backfill. The supplied migration contract requires non-regression and complete backfill; it does not require an intervening observed `not_valid` snapshot. Thus the revised false-alarm diagnosis for these observed failures is justified. This does not certify the candidate implementations on other inputs, nor independently validate every historical adjudication.

Novelty is incremental but sufficient for the stated contribution. The supplied `agent_harness_1` v3 [5] already establishes candidate-blind checks, paired checkpoints, modest repair gains, and verification overhead. `agent_harness_2` v2 [4] supplies the economical construction substrate. The external AgentCoder paper [18] explicitly separates test design from candidate code, CodeT [15] uses generated tests for selection, and Olausson et al. [13] establish the importance of feedback quality and cost-aware repair comparisons. None of these checked sources supplies this particular screening-policy comparison and requirement-grounded analysis of its coverage losses. The manuscript attributes the inherited ideas and avoids presenting blind verification itself as new. Informal lineage posts are useful attribution, rather than the basis for a publication-precedence objection.

The qualifications are consequential and should remain prominent. The historical probe corpus was used in design; the validation population had prior exposure; sequential counterbalancing does not remove provider drift; and the screening treatment changes five components together. Its independent starting drafts also differ substantially. Consequently, the results do not isolate lexical filtering, source-code visibility, or rollback individually. Zero observed regressions is a sample observation, not a guarantee. The manuscript's distinction between the evaluated versions and subsequent rollback patches is essential: code inspection and archived transition checks do not constitute a new end-to-end evaluation of v1.5. I did not rerun the live benchmark, the complete unit suite, or the unbundled workspace replay.

Scientific communication is adequate: the research questions, method, estimands, evidence, and limitations can be followed without reconstructing the experiment history. The remaining improvements are minor:

- Explain the dependence among historical checks alongside the precision–coverage percentages. Three blocked useful checks concern the same CSV corruption, and the two retained Python checks concern the same defect across repetitions. These are check-level proportions, not independent defect-level estimates or population confidence bounds.
- Apply the stated complete-block reporting convention consistently: give the grading-complete screening cost ratio, $0.7984\times$, equal prominence to the all-block $0.8065\times$ figure.
- Compress repeated rollback bug histories in Sections 1, 3, and 6 into one appendix account, retaining the evaluated-version distinction and snapshot assumptions in the main text. Simplify the abstract similarly; its substantive result is informative, but implementation labels obscure it.

Branching identical saved drafts across treatments and adjudicating the live probes would be the most informative next experiment. A fresh task population and several budgets would be needed before making a general efficiency or frontier-improvement claim.
