# Accept

This is a useful, appropriately bounded study of agent cost accounting. Its strongest contribution is the explanation and measurement of a regime in which rolling observation masking saves prompt tokens but increases expenditure under prefix-cache discounts. That finding is substantively relevant to efficient agent harnesses even though the paper does not introduce a generally superior harness. The native-context masking experiment, supported by the crossover analysis, provides sufficient scientific value for acceptance. The replay and heuristic-compaction studies are secondary findings with narrower conclusions.

The central accounting argument is sound. Lemmas 1–2 follow directly from the stated pricing model. Proposition 1A correctly balances cumulative prompt deletion against repeated suffix-prefill charges under uniform exchange sizes and the specified previous-request LCP model. I independently checked its expression against explicit token-sequence calculations. Proposition 1B follows by the analogous summation. These are idealized results: they neither establish a universal optimal policy nor predict live crossover horizons without assumptions about exchange sizes, trajectories, and server caching. The manuscript generally makes this distinction clearly.

The retained evidence supports the principal empirical result. Using independent analysis of the supplied records, I reconciled the token totals and recorded solver costs for all 1,030 development episodes and 34,609 usage-bearing requests. I recovered the Mask-4 cache-hit change from 92.79% at request five to 38.35% at request six. Restricting that calculation to the 22 episodes observed at both requests gives a similar drop of approximately 54.54 percentage points, so the discontinuity is not explained by the changing cohort alone. I also reproduced the validation arm means, the principal task-paired score intervals, and the reported compaction token maxima and exceedance counts. These checks reproduce calculations from retained telemetry; they do not independently rerun Luna, validate the graders, or recover omitted prompt/response contents.

On validation, the observed Mask-4 versus no-replay cost ratio remains above one throughout the reported missing-usage sensitivity interval, $[1.2672,1.5108]$, despite lower aggregate input volume. The score difference is $+0.21$ percentage points with a 95% interval of approximately $[-6.24,+6.67]$. Thus the evidence establishes a cost disadvantage on these runs, with no demonstrated compensating score improvement; it does not establish equal task quality. Likewise, the Nanobot replay interval does not establish equivalence, whereas the Codex estimate is appreciably more precise. The manuscript now treats these distinctions appropriately. Prior validation exposure, two validation repetitions, non-concurrent native-context references for the threshold experiments, and unequal realized context sizes limit transfer claims.

The novelty is incremental but meaningful. I compared the relevant published rStation papers [7–9] and checked *The Complexity Trap* v3 [1], Anthropic's announcement [17], and context-editing documentation [18]. Cache invalidation from historical edits, cached-input/output cost substitution, bilateral tool-input clearing, and batching to amortize invalidation are established ideas. The paper properly credits them. Its distinct contribution is the explicit horizon-dependent calculation combined with direct rolling-mask measurements on this shorter-horizon workload and a validation cost reversal. The Codex replay intervention extends the earlier Nanobot-only result [9]. Acceptance should rest on this combination, not on the accounting identity alone, the episode count, or a claim to have invented bilateral clearing.

The research question, mechanism, proof strategy, and decisive evidence are identifiable, so scientific communication meets the acceptance threshold. Nevertheless, the introduction and abstract repeat too many numerical contrasts, and Section 7 is harder to follow than the main masking argument. The following corrections would improve precision and usability without changing the central conclusion:

1. **Align the compaction description with the evaluated implementations.** Section 3.4 assigns $H_{\mathrm{low}}=0.50B$ to Batch-Mask, GWF, and BWSC together. The evaluated Nanobot BWSC implementation uses $0.68B$ with additional staged targets; Codex uses policy-dependent, prefix-adjusted targets, including $0.55B$ for Batch-Mask and $0.68B$ for BWSC. Give a compact policy-specific specification. Explicitly describe the Codex BWSC/Batch-Mask contrast as a package comparison: its different watermarks, nursery handling, representation, and reasoning treatment prevent attributing the entire saving to write-payload removal. The Nanobot `bwsc_no_action` comparison is the more focused ablation.

2. **Distinguish historical theory documents from current claims.** `manifests/THEORY_PROOFS.md` still contains the superseded “Pinning Theorem,” asserting an inevitable reread and limit cycle, and hard-budget language that the main text has withdrawn. Mark those passages as obsolete or provide a clearly linked correction. Preserve genuine preregistration history, but prevent readers from mistaking it for the current proof. In Proposition 1A, also qualify the strict assertion $\Delta U_t>0$: the permitted edge case $M=1,s=0$ gives zero.

3. **Separate billing bounds from sampling uncertainty.** The $[LB,UB_{200k}]$ intervals address missing usage, not variation across tasks or stochastic repetitions. Label them consistently and add task-paired uncertainty for the main cost contrast. Do not describe arithmetic reconciliation of two forms of the same billing identity as experimental validation of the caching mechanism; the request-index evidence supplies that support.

4. **Complete and simplify the evaluation presentation.** Report full-credit completion fractions and cost per attempted task, as requested by the problem, alongside the existing means. For example, the validation records give 23/80 full-credit attempts for Mask-4 and 19/80 for Nanobot no replay. Move repetitive cost-ratio sensitivity endpoints and cross-stage comparisons into an appendix, retaining the main mechanism, primary comparison, and limitations in the narrative. Shorten the abstract to those points.

These qualifications leave a supported, useful negative result about the tested policies and pricing regime. They do not support a universal objection to context compaction, a common-capacity benchmark claim, or demonstrated Pareto dominance at unchanged quality.
