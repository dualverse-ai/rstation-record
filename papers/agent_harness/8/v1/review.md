# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped diagnostic contribution to reliable agent execution. It does not establish an improved cost–performance frontier or a task-compliant general repair method. Its publishable contribution is the combination of checkpoint-level explanations for score-neutral and harmful repairs with a concrete transport-to-agent failure mechanism. The explicit development-only scope, disclosure of task-derived specializations, and inconclusive primary comparisons are appropriate.

Independent calculations from the retained episode records reproduce the primary task-weighted contrasts: $+6.27$ points with a $95\%$ interval of $[-0.90,13.44]$ for ACK-MCDC and $+1.20$ points with $[-3.70,6.10]$ for Inline-Verify. The recorded checkpoint outcomes support the $+63$-point rescue, $-30$-point regression, and $+1.10$-point average within-trajectory change. The diffs and check diagnostics provide informative examples of partial repairs that leave conjunctive grading conditions false. The missing-request ledger also reproduces the stated configured-cap calculations and opposite-endpoint cost ratios, conditional on the stated billing assumptions.

The streaming diagnosis is particularly useful. Inspection of the public benchmark proxy at the cited commit confirms its HTTP/1.0 close delimiting and 64 KiB forwarding reads. Focused execution of the inspected parser functions, with synthetic SSE and SDK event streams and without importing the full harness, reproduced the unguarded clean-EOF success, the frozen guard's rejection of legitimate incomplete responses, and the corrected parser's distinction between missing termination, incomplete completion, and explicit failure, including retained usage. This verifies parser behavior; it is not a reproduction of the 120 live episodes, the complete runner integration, or the 90-workspace audit.

The novelty is modest but identifiable. The same-problem papers [3], [5], and [7] already establish weak same-model repair, false alarms, and the inability of incomplete rechecks to prevent regressions; [10] supplies the differential-witness antecedent. External work on CoVe [17], CodeT [12], and cost-adjusted self-repair [20] likewise precludes presenting blind checking or differential execution as new principles. The additional value here is the detailed attribution of this repair implementation and the specific proxy–parser–finalization interaction. The ambiguity of close-delimited HTTP completion itself is established protocol knowledge [22]; the application-level failure chain and its retained evidence are the distinct finding.

The central argument is understandable and the manuscript has the necessary methods, evidence, limitations, attribution, and reproducibility information. Several reporting corrections and interpretive qualifications should nevertheless be made:

1. **Reconcile the completed-only statistics across text, tables, and figures.** Table 1's ACK-MCDC estimate is correct: $+1.58$ points, $95\%$ interval $[-0.71,3.87]$. Section 3.3 and Figure 3 instead show $+5.83$ and $[-4.40,16.07]$, which do not follow from the retained completed episodes. For Inline-Verify, Table 3's interval $[-10.40,3.33]$ is correct; Section 5.2 and Figure 3 incorrectly give $[-8.58,1.52]$. These discrepancies leave the primary all-attempt conclusions unchanged, but the secondary estimates must have one consistent source.

2. **Correct the concrete survival-weighting example.** For Study 2 task 154, the retained control scores are $0,75,60$ across replicates, with only $r_2$ completed; treatment scores are $60,75,40$, with $r_1$ and $r_3$ completed. Thus the completed means are indeed $75$ versus $50$, but the all-attempt means are $45$ versus $58.33$, as Table A1 states. Section 5.2's replicate identities, scores, survival counts, and all-attempt contrast are wrong. Also, task 015 has an all-attempt treatment-minus-control contrast of $-5$ points, not zero. Distinguish these between-arm contrasts from the zero post-stop change when Pass-2 never activates.

3. **Keep the collapse comparison descriptive.** The $6/60$ versus $0/60$ counts and Fisher calculations are reproducible, but they compare different studies, intervention mixtures, concurrency settings, and time periods. Equal counts of episodes with an IncompleteRead do not equalize interruption timing or severity. The source inspection and synthetic probes support the failure mechanism; the Fisher values do not independently identify a causal population effect of the guard. In Section 4.1, also correct the first task-062 interrupted transaction: the retained trace records 65,536 forwarded bytes, not zero. The second transaction records zero, so the buffering explanation remains supported.

4. **Separate grader disagreement from semantic defect.** The owner-name and identifier examples demonstrate changes in exact grading predicates. Prior work [3] already identifies underspecified conventions in these tasks. Calling all remaining mismatches “semantic” defects, or every successful sub-check change a genuine task repair, goes beyond that evidence. The narrower observation—that incomplete repairs and representation changes can leave the benchmark score unchanged—is well supported.

5. **Maintain the reproducibility boundaries.** The synthetic 20/20 result is a retained historical count, since the generated solutions were not saved; running calibration solutions through its graders does not reproduce those model trials. Similarly, the revised terminal-aware parser was not the parser evaluated in Study 2. The manuscript discloses both limitations and should preserve them in captions and summaries. For readability, move mode numbers and long function identifiers out of the main explanation wherever the ordinary-language algorithm already suffices.

These corrections do not require a new central argument or reverse the supported finding. Acceptance is for the bounded diagnostic advance, with generalization and frontier improvement left unresolved.
