# Checkpoint Attribution of Inconclusive Differential Self-Repair and Streaming Termination Diagnostics in an Agent Harness

## 1. Research Question, Definitions, and Delimited Scope

On multi-step software and data-reconciliation tasks in Harness-Bench [1, 2] with `gpt-6-luna`, a lean single-pass solver (`Batch-Index` [4], operating near `0.53x–0.57x` of `Released Codex 0.166.0` recorded cost) leaves roughly `15–20` percentage points of multi-sample headroom (`54.80%` single-draw mean vs. `74.48%` `Best-of-15` oracle maximum on the `10`-task `development` split) [8, 9, 10]. In prior work on this benchmark, candidate-blind differential adjudication (*Chain-of-Verification*, Dhuliawala et al. [17]; McKeeman [16]) across *two* independent trajectories improved pairwise selection over sighted judging (`55.00%` vs. `33.33%`), whereas single-candidate blind checking flagged `62.1%` (`18 / 29`) of `100%`-scoring workspaces due to benign representation freedom [10]. Independently, `feeling_of_knowing` [3] reported across `150` `development` attempts that check-gated Pass-2 commits left `47 / 60` episodes score-unchanged and retained `4 / 5` regressions, while `abstract_repair_architect` [5, 7] documented probe false positives and repair regressions under sighted test generation; externally, Olausson et al. [20] showed that self-repair gains are bounded by feedback quality.

Rather than claiming a new general-purpose harness or an unseen-task frontier advance (`$0.00` `validation` spend), this diagnostic paper investigates **deliverable-level checkpoint attribution and streaming-transport failure modes** when attempting single-trajectory verification on the `10` `development` tasks. Throughout, we define **$D_0$** as our zero-LLM deterministic Python check suite (`code/nanobot/agent/eswv_proof_checker.py`) combining *syntactic/contractual checks* (declared `Create`/`Modify` deliverable existence, JSON/Python parseability, RFC-4180 CSV row width matching header width, and read-only seed-file preservation) with *field-name heuristics* (`count == 0` paired with `sum > 0` and multi-word prose detection in identifier-list fields). Across `120` counterbalanced `development` episodes, paired $(W_1, W_2)$ workspace checkpoints, `90` live-regenerated workspace audits, and `1,161` provider transaction records, we establish three narrowly scoped contributions:

1. **Checkpoint Attribution of Inconclusive In-Episode Differential Repair (`ACK-MCDC`, `Section 3`):** In a `60`-episode paired `development` comparison against concurrent `Pass-1 Control`, an explicitly specialized counter-kernel differential repair implementation (`ACK-MCDC`, `NANOBOT_SCPC_MODE = 58`) yields an inconclusive all-attempt contrast of **`+6.27` points (`95% CI [-0.90, +13.44]`**, `+1.58` points on completed runs) at `2.09x` recorded cost (`$0.00733` vs. `$0.00351` per attempt; worst-case opposite-endpoint billing-ratio interval `[0.60x, 5.76x]`). Because a higher-cost, higher-score point estimate with wide intervals spanning `[-0.90, +13.44]` points neither demonstrates a reliable gain nor proves Pareto domination, score comparisons alone are inconclusive. The diagnostic contribution lies in inspecting **all `12` committed $(W_1, W_2)$ diffs** (`Table 2`), which explain why within-trajectory repair changes averaged `+1.10` all-attempt points (`0.00` completed median): **`7 / 12` commits genuinely repaired failing sub-clauses or input cohorts while sibling defects held conjunctive checks `False`**, **`3 / 12` reflected false-alarm or incomplete oracle witnesses**, **`1 / 12` rescued missing deliverables after a Pass-1 stream drop (`+63.0` points)**, and **`1 / 12` introduced a `-30.0`-point ordering regression** from literal counter-kernel exception phrasing.
2. **Two-Hop `HTTP/1.1 -> HTTP/1.0` Proxy Framing and Terminal-Event-Aware SSE Guarding (`Section 4`):** Tracing `ModelBudgetProxy` (`model_budget_proxy.py`) explains why upstream `HTTP/1.1` chunked `IncompleteRead` drops reach the container as clean `HTTP/1.0` `Connection: close` EOF (rather than raising an iterator transport exception) and why sub-`64 KiB` partial reads record `response_byte_count = 0`. In stock `Nanobot 0.3.0`, clean EOF without `response.completed` defaults to `finish_reason = "stop"`, stripping tool schemas (`tools=None`) on `6 / 60` Study 1 attempts (`6 / 13` `IncompleteRead` episodes) vs. **`0 / 60` (`0 / 13`, Fisher exact $p = 0.0149$)** under a missing-terminal guard. Furthermore, synthetic protocol probes expose a defect in Study 2's frozen guard (`code/parsing_c15_frozen.py`), which falsely raised `ConnectionError` on valid `response.incomplete` events [21] (`0 / 1,161` live occurrences); we ship and unit-test a corrected terminal-event-aware parser (`code/nanobot/providers/openai_responses/parsing.py`) covering `response.incomplete`, `response.failed`, partial tool calls, direct iterator exceptions, and live loopback `HTTP/1.0` proxy framing.
3. **Scope and Heuristic Limits of Zero-LLM Inline Checks (`Inline-Verify`, `Section 5`):** Attaching zero-LLM $D_0$ checks inline onto mutating tool calls (`Inline-Verify`, `NANOBOT_SCPC_MODE = 59`) resolves targeted CSV-quoting and identifier-slug defects during Pass-1 (`6 / 30` episodes, `30 / 30` pre-stop fast-exits), but shows no demonstrated overall score improvement over matched SSE-guarded `Pass-1 Control` (`+1.20` all-attempt points, `95% CI [-3.70, +6.10]`, at `1.20x` recorded cost, `[0.24x, 2.23x]`) and exhibits three verifiable false-positive modes on valid schemas (`Section 5.1`).

### 1.1 Statistical Estimands and Defensible Missing-Usage Billing Bounds

- **Unconditioned all-attempt (`N = 30`) vs. conditioned `completed`-only estimands, and pooled vs. task-weighted means:** Harness-Bench's `summarize.py` marks any episode whose forwarding proxy logged an upstream `IncompleteRead` as `status = "infrastructure_error"` and excludes it from `completed`-only summaries—even when the container retried and exited cleanly (`returncode = 0`). Because stream-drop exposure depends on trajectory length and reasoning duration, **`completed`-only contrasts condition on a post-treatment variable** and cannot resolve performance–cost trade-offs. Moreover, when `IncompleteRead` drops create unequal surviving replicate counts across tasks (`1` to `3`), our **10-task paired estimator** $\frac{1}{10}\sum_{t=1}^{10}(\bar{Y}_{t,\text{treat}} - \bar{Y}_{t,\text{ctrl}})$ weights each task equally (`1/10`) and therefore differs algebraically from the **pooled completed-episode mean** $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i} Y_i$ (which weights each surviving episode `1/n`; `Section 5.2` decomposes the resulting `+0.28` pooled vs. `-3.53` task-weighted divergence in Study 2). Paired Student-$t_9$ `95%` confidence intervals are primary; two-sided $2^{10} = 1{,}024$ task-level sign-flip values are reported strictly as **descriptive task-cluster permutation summaries** because `AB, BA, AB` replicate alternation under shared workers is not independent per-task randomization.
- **Why `response_byte_count = 0` and `HTTP 524` do not bound upstream generation, and how we bound billing (`data/missing_usage_cost_reconciliation.json`):** OpenAI Responses SSE emits token `usage` only in terminal events (`response.completed`, `response.incomplete`, `response.failed`). Across all `1,161` solver/oracle requests (`0` `response.incomplete` or `response.failed`), `1,083` (`93.3%`) recorded exact `usage` (plus `23 / 23` semantic-judge grading calls with `0` missing usage). The remaining **`78` solver/oracle requests** failed via `IncompleteRead` (`51`), Cloudflare `HTTP 524` gateway timeout (`24`), or `RemoteDisconnected` (`3`). Crucially, `ModelBudgetProxy._forward()` (`model_budget_proxy.py:L441`) reads upstream HTTP/1.1 responses in **`64 KiB` (`65,536`-byte) chunks**: when an upstream chunked stream drops before filling the first `64 KiB` read (e.g., `IncompleteRead(35568 bytes read)` on Study 1 Control `062 r1` tx 2), `http.client.HTTPResponse._read_chunked(65536)` raises `IncompleteRead(partial)` *before* `response_body.extend(chunk)` (`L444`) executes, logging `response_byte_count = 0` (`31 / 78` missing-usage calls)! Likewise, a `918`-byte Cloudflare `HTTP 524` timeout (`24 / 78` calls) indicates an origin header timeout after `~120–145`s, during which upstream `gpt-6-luna` may have generated substantial unreturned reasoning tokens; nor is an empirical bytes-per-token ratio among completed responses a guaranteed bound for interrupted streams. Because upstream billing for aborted streams is unobserved, we bound each arm's true cost within **`[L, U]`** (`prices.json`: `$0.10/M` uncached input, `$0.01/M` cached input, `$0.50/M` output including reasoning):
  1. **Recorded Lower Bound ($L$, unbilled-abort assumption):** `0` charge on aborted/timed-out requests without `response.completed`, plus exact usage on all completed solver and grading calls.
  2. **Elapsed/Byte Sensitivity Scenario (illustrative non-bound):** Reconstructs partial SSE bytes `response_byte_count + incomplete_read_partial_bytes` at median completed `344.72` B/out-token, or `elapsed_sec * 37.31` tok/s (median completed rate) when SSE bytes are zero or `HTTP 524`, plus `ceil(request_byte_count / 4.71)` uncached input tokens.
  3. **Configured-Cap Billing Upper Bound ($U$, request-limit ceiling):** `ModelBudgetProxy._rewrite_body()` (`L244–254`) rewrites a hard `max_output_tokens` ceiling into every `/v1/responses` JSON payload (`32,768` on solver/$K_B$ turns; `6,144` on `sym_blind` turns). Because valid UTF-8 JSON requests satisfy `input_tokens <= request_byte_count` (`>= 1` byte per BPE token) and upstream generation (reasoning + visible output) cannot exceed `max_output_tokens`, charging every missing-usage request for `request_byte_count` uncached input tokens (`0%` cache hit) plus `100%` of its configured `max_output_tokens` establishes a genuine request-limit upper bound $U$.
  4. **Opposite-Endpoint Cost-Ratio Bounds:** For any treatment/control pair with intervals $[L_{\text{treat}}, U_{\text{treat}}]$ and $[L_{\text{ctrl}}, U_{\text{ctrl}}]$, dividing upper endpoints does not bound their ratio; the valid worst-case ratio interval is **`[L_treat / U_ctrl, U_treat / L_ctrl]`**.

## 2. Related Work

**Draft-blind verification and self-repair limits.** **Huang et al. [15]** showed that ungrounded intrinsic self-critique rarely improves reasoning, while **Dhuliawala et al. (*Chain-of-Verification*, CoVe [17])** separated verification planning from **draft-blind** answering to reduce confirmation bias. Execution-guided repair methods—*Self-Debugging* (**X. Chen et al. [18]**), *Reflexion* (**Shinn et al. [19]**), *CodeT* (**B. Chen et al. [12]**), and *MBR-EXEC* (**Shi et al. [11]**)—leverage test feedback, but **Olausson et al. [20]** demonstrated that self-repair gains are often modest or absent once cost is accounted for because imperfect feedback induces regressions on unverified inputs.

**Differential testing, MC/DC vs. mutation heuristics, and same-benchmark work.** **McKeeman [16]** formalized differential testing across implementations, and **Chilenski and Miller [14]** defined unique-cause **Modified Condition/Decision Coverage (MC/DC)** over explicit Boolean predicates, complementing **Design by Contract (Meyer [13])**. Our `ACK-MCDC` input generator (`Section 3.2`) performs *bounded heuristic mutation and record crossover* rather than certifying Boolean condition-level MC/DC [14]. On Harness-Bench, prior studies established `Batch-Index` (`Mode 50` [4]), turn-collapsing cost shifts [6], probe screening and rollback (`ESWV-Lean` [5], `ESWV-Risk` [7]), clause-union bounds [8], score-neutral check-gated Pass-2 commits (`agent_harness_1` v4 [3]), and two-trajectory `Symmetric-BWA` [10, 9]. Inheriting those ideas directly, we contribute the explicit sub-clause diff attribution of score-neutral/regressing differential commits (`Section 3.3`) and the `HTTP/1.1 -> HTTP/1.0` streaming termination diagnosis (`Section 4`).

## 3. In-Episode Counter-Kernel Differential Repair (`ACK-MCDC`)

### 3.1 Preliminary Synthetic Single-Function Sanity Check

Prior to benchmark runs, we executed a small `20`-trial synthetic sanity check (`code/run_c9_mo6ab_eval.py`, `code/run_c9_mo6ab_stress.py`; `data/c9_mo6b_3clause_results.json`, `data/c9_mo6b_7clause_results.json`) testing `gpt-6-luna` (`temperature = 0.2`, `/v1/chat/completions`, `5` trials/arm) on standalone functions with `3` (`process_transfer`) and `7` (`reconcile_batch`, requiring acceptance of unordered `parts = [3, 1, 2]`) permuted precedence rules under global vs. contract-table prompts. All `20 / 20` trials passed (`95%` Clopper–Pearson `[83.16%, 100.00%]`; `$0.0378` at script constants `$0.25/$2.00` or `$0.0102` at `prices.json` rates; generated source strings were not persisted). Because task complexity, prompt wording, temperature (`0.2` vs. `1.0`), and single-file layout all differ from Harness-Bench, **`20 / 20` passes establish only absence of precedence errors on those two synthetic functions** and do not localize or bound benchmark failures.

### 3.2 `ACK-MCDC` Architecture and Non-Compliant Task Specializations

`ACK-MCDC` (`code/ack_mcdc_runtime.py`, `NANOBOT_SCPC_MODE = 58`, frozen manifest `manifests/c14_ack_mcdc_dev_manifest.json`) runs `Pass-1 Control` (`Batch-Index`) to produce draft $W_1$, snapshots $(W_1, D_0)$, and runs three stages before at most one Pass-2 repair turn:

1. **Seed-Masked Counter-Kernel ($K_B$, `1` zero-tool call, `max_output_tokens = 32,768`):** Strips `W_0` `if/elif/for` bodies while exposing signatures, literal constants, and `(Task Prompt, non-.py W_0 fixtures)` for code tasks (`code_ack_mcdc`), or exposes `(Task Prompt, W_0)` plus target file paths, CSV headers, and JSON type skeletons (zero $W_1$ cell values) to synthesize a clean-$W_0$ script in `/__sandbox_rpc__` for data tasks (`data_ack_mcdc`).
2. **Bounded Heuristic Mutation and Record-Crossover Differential Testing (`$0.00`, `0` LLM calls):** Executes $W_1$ and $K_B$ on visible `W_0` fixture calls and heuristic perturbations—donor scalar/nested crossover (`inlist`, `iso`), joint zeroing (`sync_zero`), list duplication (`dup`), list reversal (`order`), and string corruption (`str`)—selecting up to `8` disagreements via outer-argument round-robin. Unlike Chilenski–Miller MC/DC [14], this heuristic mutator does not parse Boolean predicates or hold sibling atomic conditions fixed.
3. **Candidate-Blind Oracle (`sym_blind`, `1` zero-tool call, `max_output_tokens = 6,144`) and Rollback Gate:** Adjudicates unlabeled disagreements `Q1..Qm` from `(Prompt, W_0)` alone (`code/test_ack_mcdc_unit.py`). Pass-2 runs iff $D_0$ fails or $\ge 1$ witness favors $K_B$, committing $W_2$ iff $D_0$ passes, visible `unittest` failures do not increase, and agreed seed calls remain unchanged.

**Explicit scope restriction due to development-derived specializations:** Line-level audit of `ack_mcdc_runtime.py` shows two development-derived specializations: donor-record crossover (`L472`) skips four hardcoded identifier keys (`credential_id`, `user_id`, `upload_id`, `rp_id`, matching tasks `150` and `080`), and `CODE_KB_SYSTEM_PROMPT` (`L72`) includes the parenthetical `(e.g. unordered records covering consecutive IDs vs strictly sorted records)` motivated by task `080`. These specializations conflict with Harness-Bench's prohibition on encoding missed benchmark requirements into global prompts or field lists, and **their net effect direction is untested**: skipping identifier keys shifts witness allocation across remaining fields, and prompt parentheticals can either help or contribute to over-constrained witness phrasing (as in `080 r2` below). Consequently, **all empirical results in Section 3 apply strictly to this evaluated, specialized `ACK-MCDC` implementation** and do not establish bounds for a task-compliant general method.

**Why the aggregate contrast is inconclusive while the checkpoint attribution is informative.** On all `30` attempts, `ACK-MCDC` scores `+6.27` points higher than `Control` (`57.77%` vs. `51.50%`), but the `95%` confidence interval spans `[-0.90, +13.44]` points (`+5.83` points, `[-4.40, +16.07]` among `completed` episodes) and mean wall-clock rises from `159.6`s (median `101.1`s) to `400.3`s (median `269.9`s; `Appendix Table A3`). - **Conjunctive sub-clause masking (`7 / 12` commits, e.g., `080 r1`, `015 r1–r3`, `018 r1, r3`):** On `080 r1`, Pass-2 used `set(local_by_number) != expected` (preserving unordered parts) and repaired **`3` of `4` failing invalid-input cohorts** (`case:evidence_and_storage_action_boundary`: `failed invalid cohorts [0, 1, 2, 3]` $\to$ `[3]`). Because each graded clause is a deterministic conjunction $C(W) = \bigwedge_{j=1}^{m} c_j(W)$, cohort `[3]` alone held the `0.15`-weight clause at `0` (`0.40 -> 0.40`). Likewise, on `015 r1`, Pass-2 fixed both `blocked_by` prose arrays to canonical slugs (`security_questionnaire`, `procurement_approval`), while an unflagged `owner` mismatch kept `onboarding_dependency_state` (`0.35`) `False`.
- **Witness-phrase-induced ordering regression (`1 / 12`, `080 r2`):** On `080 r2`, $K_B$'s Witness 2 exception string on `local_parts = []` read `'local part numbers must be consecutive'`. Pass-2 encoded that wording literally as `numbers != list(range(1, session["part_count"] + 1))`, breaking unordered valid inputs on `case:paginated_part_reconciliation` (`True -> False`, `-35.0` pts, offset by `+5.0` pts from creating `resume_plan.json`). Because `W_0`'s visible `unittest` tests only sorted `local_parts`, the rollback gate saw `0` failures and committed `W_2`.
- **False-alarm or incomplete oracle witnesses (`3 / 12`, `031 r1`, `031 r2`, `018 r2`):** On `031 r1` and `031 r2`, $W_1$ already scored `1.00`, yet `K_B + sym_blind` emitted `3–4` false alarms that triggered benign Pass-2 edits. On `018 r2`, Witness 1 falsely demanded deleting valid row `S1|References` (which Pass-2 rightly refused after checking `W_0`), while Pass-2 adopted Witness 4 (`S5,Claim D -> missing,Claim D`) whose incomplete rationale still left `audit_Claim_D` `False`.
- **Missing-deliverable $D_0$ rescue (`1 / 12`, `062 r2`):** After Pass-1 suffered a `tools=None` collapse (`W_1 = 0.00`), $D_0$ (`regime = skipped`, `0` oracle calls) re-entered Pass-2 with tools restored (`+63.0` pts).

## 4. Two-Hop Proxy EOF Framing and Terminal-Event-Aware Streaming Guard

### 4.1 Why Upstream `HTTP/1.1` `IncompleteRead` Becomes Downstream Clean `HTTP/1.0` EOF

Inspecting why `Pass-1 Control` suffered three `0.00`-score runs (`r1 062`, `r1 080`, `r3 154`) and `ACK-MCDC` entered Pass-2 empty on `r2 062` revealed an interaction between **two-hop proxy framing** (`baseline/upstream/tools/model_budget_proxy.py`) and **stock `Nanobot 0.3.0`'s SSE consumer** (`code/parsing_c14_unguarded.py`):

1. **Proxy transport boundary (`HTTP/1.1` chunked $\to$ `HTTP/1.0` `Connection: close`):** Inside Harness-Bench, the container's `AsyncOpenAI`/`httpx` client connects to `ModelBudgetProxy` rather than directly to the upstream provider. `ModelBudgetProxy.Handler` sets **`protocol_version = "HTTP/1.0"`** (`L271`), strips `Transfer-Encoding` and `Content-Length` headers (`L435–437`), sends `Connection: close` (`L439`), and forwards `64 KiB` reads (`chunk = response.read(64 * 1024)`, `L441`). When the upstream `HTTP/1.1` chunked stream drops mid-reasoning without a terminating `0\r\n\r\n` chunk, `ModelBudgetProxy` catches `http.client.IncompleteRead` (`L454`) and closes the client socket (`self.close_connection = True`, `L483`). Crucially, in **`HTTP/1.0` without `Content-Length`, TCP FIN defines normal end-of-message**! Thus `httpx` inside the container raises **zero transport exception**; `response.aiter_lines()` / `stream.__aiter__()` simply yields any previously flushed `64 KiB` chunks (or zero lines if `< 64 KiB` arrived before the drop) and exits the `async for` loop cleanly. (By contrast, on a direct `HTTP/1.1` chunked connection without an `HTTP/1.0` proxy, `httpx` raises `RemoteProtocolError` directly out of the stream iterator into `OpenAICompatProvider._handle_error()`.)
2. **Unguarded `finish_reason = "stop"` $\to$ `tools=None` collapse:** In `code/parsing_c14_unguarded.py`, `consume_sse_with_reasoning()` and `consume_sdk_stream()` initialize `finish_reason = "stop"` before iterating. On clean `HTTP/1.0` EOF prior to any output text or `response.output_item.done` tool call, the parser returns `(content="", tool_calls=[], finish_reason="stop", usage={})`. `AgentRunner._run_core()` (`code/nanobot/agent/runner.py:L554–578`) counts two consecutive blank `finish_reason != "error"` turns (`_MAX_EMPTY_RETRIES = 2`) and invokes `_request_finalization_retry()` $\to$ `_request_no_tools(..., tools=None)` (`L1578–1646`). On Study 1 Control `062 r1` (`data/sse_protocol_trace_sequences.json`), `tx 1` (`IncompleteRead(58801 bytes read)`, `response_byte_count = 0`) and `tx 2` (`IncompleteRead(35568 bytes read)`, `response_byte_count = 0`) triggered `tx 3` with **`tools_count = 0`** and `"Please provide your response to the user based on the conversation above."`, forcing a tool-less prose apology (`score = 0.00`).

### 4.2 Empirical Collapse Reduction, `response.incomplete` Defect in Study 2's Frozen Guard, and Corrected Parser

In Study 2, both arms deployed a frozen parser (`code/parsing_c15_frozen.py`, matching `manifests/c15_inline_verify_dev_manifest.json`) that tracked `saw_completed` on `{"response.completed", "response.done"}` and raised `ConnectionError` on premature EOF so `OpenAICompatProvider._handle_error()` $\to$ `LLMProvider._run_with_retry()` retried with backoff while preserving all tool schemas. Across `120` prospective attempts (`Figure 2A`), `IncompleteRead` episode incidence was identical (`13 / 60` in Study 1 vs. `13 / 60` in Study 2), while `tools=None` finalization collapses fell from **`6 / 60` (`6 / 13` `IncompleteRead` episodes) to `0 / 60` (`0 / 13`; two-sided Fisher exact $p = 0.0274$ over `60` attempts, $p = 0.0149$ among `IncompleteRead` episodes)**.

**Protocol completeness defect in `parsing_c15_frozen.py` and verified fix (`code/nanobot/providers/openai_responses/parsing.py`).** Peer-review probing against the OpenAI Responses specification (`ResponseIncompleteEvent` [21]) identified an important defect in `parsing_c15_frozen.py`: besides `response.completed`, the Responses protocol defines legitimate terminal events **`response.incomplete`** (e.g., `status = "incomplete"`, `incomplete_details.reason = "max_output_tokens"` or `"content_filter"`, carrying billable `usage`) and **`response.failed` / `response.cancelled` / `error`**. Because `parsing_c15_frozen.py` checked only `response.completed` / `response.done`, a valid `response.incomplete` event raised `ConnectionError` in both `consume_sse_with_reasoning()` and `consume_sdk_stream()`—discarding returned `usage`, bypassing `map_finish_reason("incomplete") == "length"`, and risking transient-connection retries on budget-exhausted turns! Auditing all `1,161` live solver traces confirms **`0 / 1,161` live responses emitted `response.incomplete` or `response.failed`** (`data/missing_usage_cost_reconciliation.json`), so Study 2's empirical counts were unaffected. However, sound general deployment requires our corrected parser (`code/nanobot/providers/openai_responses/parsing.py`), which:
1. Treats `response.completed`, `response.done`, and `response.incomplete` [21] as valid terminal events, resolving `incomplete` to `finish_reason = "length"` (or `"content_filter"`) so `AgentRunner` (`runner.py:L586`) triggers length-recovery continuation while retaining `usage` (including `cached_tokens` and `reasoning_tokens`) and blocking incomplete tool execution (`should_execute_tools == False`).
2. Treats `response.failed`, `response.cancelled`, and `error` as explicit terminal errors (`finish_reason = "error"`, `tool_calls = []`, retained `usage`, non-transient classification).
3. Reserves `ConnectionError("SSE stream closed before terminal response event")` strictly for streams ending without any terminal event (including uncommitted partial tool-argument deltas).

All five cases—`response.incomplete` in SSE/SDK/`AgentRunner`, `response.failed`, partial tool deltas, direct iterator `RemoteProtocolError` propagation, and a live two-hop loopback socket test reproducing `ModelBudgetProxy`'s `64 KiB` `IncompleteRead(35568 bytes read)` $\to$ `response_byte_count = 0` and `HTTP/1.0` clean EOF—are verified in `code/test_mode59_and_sse_unit.py` (`7 / 7` tests passing in Tier 5).

## 5. Zero-LLM Inline Structural and Naming Heuristics (`Inline-Verify`)

### 5.1 Architecture, `90`-Workspace Live Audit, and Heuristic False-Positive Modes

Eliminating auxiliary LLM verifier calls (`$0.00` oracle spend), `Inline-Verify` (`code/nanobot/agent/inline_verify_runtime.py`, `NANOBOT_SCPC_MODE = 59`, frozen manifest `manifests/c15_inline_verify_dev_manifest.json`) attaches two Python hooks to SSE-guarded `Pass-1 Control`: **Hook 1 (`append_mode59_inline_receipt`)**, which returns byte-identical tool output (`0` extra tokens) when clean or appends a `1–3` line `[Inline Deterministic Check]` receipt when $D_0$ fires after `write_file`/`edit_file`/`apply_patch`/`exec`; and **Hook 2 (`run_mode59_prestop_verification` + `finalize_mode59_verification`)**, which fast-exits at `1.000x` Pass-1 cost when clean or permits one Pass-2 turn committed iff `len(w2_d0_fails) < len(w1_d0_fails)` with non-increasing `W_0` `unittest` failures and zero newly triggered checks (including `_REPLICA_MODE59_PY` `case_id` JSON staleness checks).

Live execution of `code/audit_d0_and_staleness.py` across all `90` archived Pass-1 `development` workspaces (`data/archived_90_workspaces_d0_bundle.json`, `Figure 2B`, Tier 3) yields **`0 / 17` (`0.0%`, `95% CI [0.00%, 19.51%]`) observed false alarms on `100%`-gold workspaces** and **`17 / 73` (`23.29%`, `95% CI [14.19%, 34.65%]`) sub-gold triggers**. Under these `10`-task `development` experiments, neither our specialized counter-kernel differential repair (`ACK-MCDC`) nor zero-LLM inline structural/naming checks (`Inline-Verify`) demonstrates a statistically reliable score improvement, and wide confidence intervals alongside missing-usage billing intervals leave frontier dominance open rather than refuted. What the paired checkpoints and proxy traces *do* establish concretely are two actionable harness diagnostics: (1) under conjunctive multi-cohort grading, partial formatting or sub-cohort fixes yield zero score change whenever sibling semantic sub-clauses remain false—while noisy counter-kernel exception strings can actively break unverified behaviors (`080 r2`); and (2) when an `HTTP/1.0` `Connection: close` proxy sits downstream of an `HTTP/1.1` chunked Responses stream, stream consumers must distinguish recognized terminal events (`response.completed`, `response.incomplete` [21], `response.failed`) from premature clean EOF to prevent silent empty-turn `tools=None` collapses without misclassifying budget-exhausted responses as transport errors.
