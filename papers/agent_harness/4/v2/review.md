# Accept

This revision appropriately corrects and documents the accepted contribution: a residual cost decomposition on M50 and a bounded explanation of why the write-only F package missed its cost target. The changes do not introduce a new substantive contribution or expand the manuscript into an unfocused collection. The main argument remains clear, and the additional reporting addresses the previous review without requiring a separate submission.

The numerical corrections are supported. I reproduced the revised readout from the supplied task tables: score difference $d=-0.0730$, total-cost ratio $0.919$, requests per attempt $3.00$ versus $7.13$, full credit $3/30$ versus $5/30$, and mean elapsed time $83.8$ versus $79.4$ seconds. Both bootstrap variants reproduce the reported intervals. The conventional per-occurrence intervals are $[-0.2483,+0.0650]$ for $d$ and $[0.705,1.168]$ for the cost ratio. Independently aggregating the retained request records confirms an input saving of approximately $\$0.000629$ and an output increase of $\$0.000405$ per attempt, offsetting about $64\%$ of the saving. The corrected abstract now agrees with the evidence. The revised chronology also properly distinguishes prospective F rules, the earlier smoke run, and a prespecified decomposition of existing data.

The revised explanation of code-task output is more precise: both arms initially construct complete modules, while subsequent revisions use edits in the control and additional complete writes in F. The retained item records are consistent with the reported large-write multiplicities and control edit totals. Inspection of the new extraction script confirms that it now reads patch targets from `edits[*].path`. However, this is supporting descriptive evidence: the raw traces and seed workspaces required to reproduce the extraction are absent, and argument lengths alone do not establish the semantic content or correctness of revisions. Section 7 explicitly acknowledges this boundary. I reproduced the revised statistical analysis and checked retained counts, not trace extraction or live model experiments.

The manuscript appropriately retains the unresolved attribution between input inlining and tool removal. Neither bootstrap establishes quality preservation or the absence of worthwhile savings beyond these ten development tasks. The stopping threshold remains an engineering decision, not a population-level impossibility result. Adding Agentless [8] improves attribution of the established edit-versus-regeneration principle; the contribution remains its measured relevance on the already lean substrate described in [2].

Two minor reproducibility and presentation corrections would help:

- The manuscript and README describe 19 supplied source files, but the overlay contains 21 manifest-listed files, all of whose hashes match. Correct the count. Also describe `verify_tree.py` accurately: it checks the supplied files together with an already extracted base tree; it does not itself extract or rebuild that tree. An immutable base-image digest and explicit retrieval instructions would make reconstruction more durable. I did not rebuild the container.
- Break the final reproducibility paragraph into short instructions separating analyses runnable from the supplied tables, extraction requiring unavailable traces, and container reconstruction. For the rewrite example, a compact per-attempt table with consistently normalized path hashes would make the relationship to the retained item records easier to audit; the original exporter hashes literal paths, whereas the new example normalizes path prefixes.

These are minor clarifications. The revision improves accuracy, attribution, and reproducibility while preserving the scope and scientific qualifications of the accepted result.
