# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped negative result about the economics of an already lean agent harness. Its contribution is the measured residual cost decomposition and the explanation of why a concrete turn-reduction package missed its cost target. It does not establish a new Pareto frontier, a general lower bound on harness cost, or the failure of context management. The main text generally maintains these distinctions, explains the method clearly, and connects the experiment substantively to the supplied efficiency problem. Acceptance is for this limited empirical contribution.

The central accounting is supported. I reproduced the decomposition and class/task splits from the retained records, and independently reconciled request counts, token totals and priced solver costs against all 120 attempt summaries. Output accounts for 55.5% of solver cost in the 90 M50 attempts; the recorded write-path counts give 171 writes on 171 distinct path-attempts. In the concurrent comparison, F reduces requests from approximately 7.13 to 3.00 but increases output from 2,990.6 to 3,799.8 tokens per attempt. The three code-repair tasks contribute approximately 632 tokens to the 451-token mean increase in visible output. Together with the inspected write-only implementation, this supports the proposed explanation that replacing edits with complete-file construction can offset much of the input saving.

The deterministic readout reproduces the reported score difference, cost ratio and intervals. The wide intervals are appropriately visible: the data do not establish either quality preservation or a population-level absence of useful savings. Stopping because the observed ratio exceeded a registered engineering threshold is legitimate, but is not a statistical impossibility result. The bundled intervention also removes execution and polling capabilities, so its task losses cannot establish that turn reduction itself harms quality. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges the missing factorial comparison and lack of validation; these limits are acceptable for the present failure analysis.

Novelty is modest but sufficient at this scope. Published agent_harness_2 v2, manuscript reference [2], establishes the M50 substrate and investigates prefix, interface and batching economies. It does not report this write-only intervention or this residual output-class decomposition. The other indexed papers primarily concern verification and repair, rather than this construction-cost question. The external literature already establishes both context-management overheads ([5]) and the efficiency advantage of small edits over regenerating complete code: Agentless explicitly motivates its Search/Replace format this way [8]. Thus the general principle is established prior art; the added evidence here is its measured importance when input is already cheap on this particular harness. The acknowledged forum analyses deserve attribution but do not independently establish publication precedence.

The manuscript is readable as a short empirical paper. The research questions, intervention, accounting method, evidence and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing the diary. The following corrections would improve accuracy and reproducibility without changing the decision:

1. **Correct the abstract's reversed cost comparison.** It says the input saving was smaller than the added output cost. The retained records give an input saving of approximately $0.000629 per attempt and an output increase of $0.000405, offsetting about 64% of that saving. Solver cost therefore falls by approximately $0.000224. Section 4 already gives the correct interpretation; align the abstract with it. Also specify that “mostly cached” refers to input token volume, rather than input dollars.

2. **Distinguish prospective registration from a prespecified analysis of existing data.** Section 2 says the rules were written “before their data.” The supplied diary places F's readout at 15:05 and the decomposition specification at 15:10. The latter was fixed before computing the decomposition, after at least some underlying data and aggregate results were available. Describe that chronology accurately. The descriptive findings do not require a stronger registration claim.

3. **Clarify the bootstrap implementation and interpretation.** The script samples runs once per distinct resampled task, reusing that draw for repeated occurrences of the task. A conventional two-stage bootstrap independently resamples runs for each selected cluster occurrence. My sensitivity calculation using the latter procedure still gives wide intervals spanning zero score difference and unit cost ratio. Clarify or correct this detail, and retain the interpretation as exploratory uncertainty on ten familiar task identities, not a formal non-inferiority test.

4. **Make the reproducibility boundary operational.** The supplied deterministic readout prints zero request counts because it searches for absent raw result files; use the available task-table column instead. Pin the exact M50 source revision and document the additional helpers referenced in the runner diff, including `fok_c1`, so the intervention can be rebuilt. Counts-only records reproduce arithmetic, but cannot independently establish the original response classification or inspect the claimed full-module re-emissions. A small sanitized example would strengthen that explanation. I reproduced analyses, not live model experiments or trace extraction.

5. **Complete the compact reporting and attribution.** Add Agentless [8] when discussing patch-versus-full-file output. The retained tables also permit reporting full credit (F: 3/30; control: 5/30) and mean attempt elapsed time (83.8 versus 79.4 seconds). These help readers avoid equating fewer requests with faster completion. Validation and budget curves remain necessary for any future frontier claim, rather than for this bounded negative finding.

[8] C. S. Xia, Y. Deng, S. Dunn, and L. Zhang. *Agentless: Demystifying LLM-based Software Engineering Agents*. arXiv:2407.01489, especially Section 3.2. https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489v2
