# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted contribution: a useful, local comparison of $K=2$ against $K=8$ for exact pipelined decoding at long context. The additional experiments correct the autoregressive baseline and qualify the existing cost explanation. They are appropriate supporting evidence for this paper, rather than a separate substantive contribution. The main text remains focused, with detailed diagnostics in the supplement.

The baseline correction is scientifically important. At 32 KV splits, the retained records support median $K2/K8$ ratios of $1.136$ [1.035, 1.196] at 8K and $1.212$ [1.130, 1.248] at 16K. The corresponding median $K2/AR$ advantages are $1.67\times$ and $1.69\times$, substantially below the original 8-split comparisons. I ran the inspected standard-library reproduction script and independently checked the new pooled ratios, median ratios, eligibility counts and output-hash agreement. Tables 4 and 4b reproduce. Both stack arms match the 32-split AR hashes on all 36 requests; the two AR configurations match on only 15. The manuscript appropriately explains that changing splits changes the numerical reference, so the AR32/AR8 comparison does not isolate kernel speed on identical output sequences. These checks reproduce analysis of retained records, not GPU inference or the underlying token streams.

The revised mechanism discussion improves on the accepted version. The conditional per-row estimates are now correctly identified as total-cycle increments, and the equal-cell comparison is restricted to narrow frontiers. The retained profiler tables support the reported attention shares: stage 1 accounts for approximately 99% of the GPU-time difference in the sampled 16K windows, with essentially unchanged GEMM time. This supports localization of that difference to attention in the profiled case. The layout experiments provide useful supporting diagnostics, while the explicit statement that the underlying mechanism remains unestablished is necessary. The added citations [6] and [7] appropriately identify the previously published scheduler and numerical configuration; these are inherited components, not new contributions here.

The development-screen corrections, replacement of “parity” by “no detected difference,” and expanded implementation lineage address the previous review constructively. A few remaining corrections and qualifications would improve precision without changing acceptance:

1. **Correct the warm/cold microbenchmark summary in Section 5.** At 8K, the retained measurements imply an incremental cost over 24 layers of $0.132$ ms per row for the warm case and approximately $0.062$ ms for the cold case. “At most 0.06 ms” applies only approximately to the cold result, not both. Report both values. Scope the profiler conclusion to the sampled 16K request and replay windows; it does not independently establish a kernel decomposition for every prompt or for 8K.

2. **Disambiguate the statistics.** After Table 4b, “pooled and median ratios agree at 8K and 16K” is accurate for $K2/K8$, but not generally for stack/AR: at 8K the latter is $2.13\times$ pooled versus $1.67\times$ median. Name the ratio explicitly. Also qualify the abstract and Section 7 statement that the 2K gain is not significant as referring to the original 8-split experiment; the 32-split interval excludes one.

3. **Describe the follow-up as reuse of the confirmation records.** Table 4's “fresh confirmation” caption should distinguish the original held-out status from this subsequent robustness check on the same 12 records. All three 32-split arms have only one run, so their bootstrap intervals describe variation across records conditional on those timings, not run-to-run uncertainty. The existing repeated 8-split evidence supports the central result, but does not supply timing replication for the new configuration.

4. **Consolidate the presentation around the corrected baseline.** Prefer a compact comparison of the 8- and 32-split results in the main text, with historical prediction records retained in the supplement. Update supplementary summaries that still say “parity” or present superseded mechanism interpretations without an immediate qualification. The metadata change summary should also remove “no new experiments,” since this revision includes new measurements.
