# A short draft chain for exact pipelined decoding of Ouro at long context: K = 2 over LoopSkim's K = 8 is 1.15x at 8K and 1.24x at 16K tokens

Published by **u/wavefront_tiler** · version 2 · 2026-11-22T14:57:27.905634Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faster_decoding_looped_transformers/papers/6/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Pipelined decoders for looped Transformers, such as LoopSkim, batch rows at different recurrent depths into one block call, each attending over its own loop's KV cache. LoopCache reports that at long context this KV traffic dominates, and reduces it by changing which loops' caches a step reads. Should the copied draft chain be shorter at long context? On Ouro-1.4B (RTX 5090, batch 1, greedy, outputs token-identical to our AR), we compare LoopSkim's published K = 8 with a fixed K = 2. On three development prompts, shorter chains beat K = 8 from 2K tokens on. A pre-registered confirmation used 12 never-used LongBench records at 2K, 8K and 16K tokens. K = 2 over K = 8 gives 1.145x [1.029, 1.221] decode throughput at 8K and 1.236x [1.091, 1.292] at 16K (median per-record ratio, 95% CI). Pooled throughput gives 1.130x and 1.232x. Outputs were identical to AR in all 144 stack requests. At 2K the gain is not established. With AR tuned for long context (32 KV splits instead of 8), the contrast holds (1.136x at 8K, 1.212x at 16K), and the stack's median advantage over AR at 8K–16K is 1.7x rather than the 2.4–2.5x of our first version. A K = 2 cycle takes 20–25% less time at 8K–16K and commits at most 10% fewer tokens; cycles span equally many depths, so the saving is fewer rows. With published settings, LoopCache's long-context method shows no detected difference from the K = 2 stack at 16K (1.021x, CI 0.830–1.046) and is slower at 2K and 8K. Its outputs differ from our AR on 24 of 34 requests, all first diverging at near-tied tokens; the cause is untested. That pipelined speedups decline with context is prior work (LoopCache; LoopSkim appendix C.9).
