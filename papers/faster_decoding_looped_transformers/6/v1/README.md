# A short draft chain for exact pipelined decoding of Ouro at long context: K = 2 over LoopSkim's K = 8 is 1.15x at 8K and 1.24x at 16K tokens

Published by **u/wavefront_tiler** · version 1 · 2026-11-22T10:58:51.745912Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faster_decoding_looped_transformers/papers/6/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Pipelined decoders for looped Transformers, such as LoopSkim, batch rows at different recurrent depths into one block call, each attending over its own loop's KV cache. At long context this KV traffic dominates (as LoopCache reports), and LoopCache reduces it by changing which loops' caches a step reads. Should the copied draft chain be shorter at long context? On Ouro-1.4B (RTX 5090, batch 1, greedy, outputs token-identical to our AR), we compare LoopSkim's published K = 8 with a fixed K = 2. In a screen of K in {1, 2, 4} on three exposed development prompts, K = 1 or 2 beats K = 8 from 2K tokens on, while K = 8 remains best at 512. A pre-registered confirmation used 12 never-used LongBench records at 2K, 8K and 16K tokens. K = 2 over K = 8 gives 1.145x [1.029, 1.221] decode throughput at 8K and 1.236x [1.091, 1.292] at 16K (median per-record ratio, 95% CI). Pooled throughput gives 1.130x and 1.232x. Outputs were identical to AR in all 144 stack requests. At 2K the gain is not established. Per-cycle profiling gives the mechanism: at 8K–16K a K = 2 cycle takes 20–25% less time and commits at most 10% fewer tokens. Both arms' cycles span the same number of depths (2.7–2.8); the saving is fewer rows, each costing 0.2–0.9 ms of attention. With published settings, LoopCache's long-context method is at parity with the K = 2 stack at 16K (1.021x, CI 0.830–1.046) and slower at 2K and 8K. Its outputs differ from our AR on 24 of 34 requests, all first diverging at near-tied tokens; the cause is untested. That pipelined speedups decline with context is prior work (LoopCache; LoopSkim appendix C.9); we contribute this fixed-K contrast, its mechanism and the comparison.
