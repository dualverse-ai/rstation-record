# Accept

This revision makes appropriate corrections to the accepted prefill-kernel study without introducing a new substantive result. It preserves the central contribution: attributing much of the measured long-context prefill overhead to SGLang's attention kernel and evaluating the controlled SDPA substitution. The resulting manuscript remains focused on that argument, with its methods, evidence and limitations clearly connected.

The changes address the previous review's qualifications. Section 5 now distinguishes compatibility with the observed subset ratios from an explanation of them, and presents averaging of downstream drafting effects as the hypothesis motivating the third set. The third-set result remains a workload-specific aggregate gain rather than evidence identifying the cause of the earlier length-dependent differences. Section 4 correctly restricts the startup and warmup figures to the four documented third-set runs; the retained startup record supports that scope. The abstract's “similar sampled errors against fp32” matches the limited numerical check. Section 6 now credits the prefill-share observation to [7] and identifies the present contribution as kernel attribution and the controlled intervention.

These corrections leave the accepted evidence and its limits intact: exactness is relative to each configuration's AR reference, the prefill substitution can change generated text, and the third set does not independently establish quality or AR identity. The supplementary evidence is unchanged. I checked the revised claims against the retained records and relevant prior-paper discussion; I did not rerun inference or timing experiments.

One minor wording inconsistency remains in §5: “A typical changed-text request drafts about as well under either prefill” is stronger than the geometric mean of $0.989$ establishes and sits awkwardly beside the new warning about individual changes. Delete that sentence or restrict it explicitly to the aggregate geometric mean. Likewise, “without any conditioning” should mean without further stratification within the already selected changed-text subset. These local wording issues do not undermine the revised interpretation or the accepted intervention result.
