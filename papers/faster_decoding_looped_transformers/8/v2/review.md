# Accept

This revision improves the interpretation and reproducibility of the accepted prefill-kernel study while preserving its contribution. Replacing “compute floor” with an explicitly empirical reference budget, limiting the numerical-error claim to sampled positions, and stating the scope of bootstrap uncertainty remove potentially misleading interpretations. The manuscript remains organized around the bottleneck, intervention, and complete-request consequences. The added backend measurement and task-family breakdown are limited supporting evidence for that argument, not a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission.

The third-set qualifications are particularly useful. The revision now identifies the change in workload composition, reports family-specific uncertainty, and states that quality and AR identity were not re-evaluated on that set. I independently recomputed the new breakdown from the retained records: code gives $1.074\times$ [1.051, 1.099] over 53 retained requests, and QA gives $1.082\times$ [0.984, 1.240] over 12. The reported mean output lengths also match: 243.66 versus 247.30 tokens for code and 256 for both QA configurations. These support the narrower workload-specific interpretation and show that shorter generations do not explain this aggregate gain. These checks concern retained measurements; I did not rerun GPU inference, backend timing, prompt reconstruction, or quality scoring. The new reconstruction and scoring scripts were inspected rather than executed.

The backend trace supports identifying PyTorch's FlashAttention kernel in the tested environment. The cuDNN timing is appropriately described as an operator measurement without live-engine validation. Attribution to the earlier prefill-share observation in [7] is warranted by that paper's §5, and the added discussion of [9]–[11] better locates the contribution within established serving and attention work.

Three small clarifications remain advisable:

1. **Finish qualifying §5's causal language.** The slope identity correctly explains why the regression cannot identify a treatment effect. However, “This, together with the near-unity mean, explains the opposite-signed subset ratios” and “the expected downstream drafting effect is near zero” still exceed what the descriptive evidence establishes. Present the latter as the historical hypothesis motivating the third set, and describe the baseline mixture as compatible with, rather than explanatory of, the subset results. A geometric mean near one also does not establish negligible changes for individual requests. The section's explicit acknowledgement of unresolved length interactions and its final workload-specific conclusion already supply the appropriate interpretation; this remaining wording does not undermine the accepted intervention result.

2. **Scope startup costs to the documented runs.** The new record reports four third-set runs. Replace “in every run” in §4 with that scope unless startup records for the other experiments are supplied. This does not affect the steady-state comparisons.

3. **Make summary wording consistent with the corrections.** In the metadata abstract, replace “equal fp32 error” with similar sampled errors against the fp32 reference, matching Table 2. In §6, revise “The prefill share and its kernel attribution are what we add” to credit the prefill-share observation to [7] consistently with §1. The contribution here is the attribution and controlled prefill intervention.

These are local precision issues. The changes preserve the supported scientific result and improve its stated limits without making the paper an unfocused expansion.
