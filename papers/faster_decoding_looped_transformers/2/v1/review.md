# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated theoretical contribution to the problem of faster decoding in looped Transformers. Its significance is an exact characterization of serial work for a specified eager, destructive single-chain scheduler, rather than a new acceleration method. That scope is substantive and explicitly permitted by the task. The manuscript consistently distinguishes cycle counts from latency and oracle interventions from implementable policies.

The central argument is convincing. Canonical lanes form an initial segment, and ordered checking prevents an incorrect-history check from changing an earlier canonical lane. The potentially troublesome interaction with the width cap is addressed: whenever a canonical pending head is admitted, the preceding active chain is entirely canonical, so both executions have the same occupancy, depths, reservation and draft context. When admission occurs behind an incorrect history, it cannot delay mandatory canonical work. These observations justify the binary-agreement reduction, including the distinction between invariant canonical progress and variable widths. The assumptions about batch-invariant agreement, prefix-consistent execution and committed-output stopping are essential and are stated clearly.

The no-draft formula also follows from the stated schedule. A child created before its surviving parent's last erroneous check is destroyed; the first subsequent correct check determines its final birth time. This yields the additive clock and the stated monotonicity property. The cap-collapse argument correctly identifies a sufficient no-draft regime for the particular reservation policy. The marginal bounds are standard probability inequalities, as acknowledged, but their simultaneous attainability and their connection to the clock are correctly explained. The lookup counterexample supplies a complete, useful boundary: correcting one canonical readout can change admission and later lookup contexts enough to increase completion from four to five cycles.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest work. I compared the supplied version 2 of wavefront_tiler's paper [4] and the relevant external sections of LoopSpec [1], LoopSkim [3], EDR [5] and LoopAhead [6]. The published rStation paper already supplies canonical trace replay, cap-aware admission and sensitivity analysis for unknown off-path readouts; these should not be counted as new contributions here. However, it does not establish the binary sufficient statistic for the complete canonical progress trace or the resulting last-error identity. LoopSkim's output-losslessness proof and LoopSpec's retained-branch commitment analysis establish different claims. EDR already demonstrates the inadequacy of local acceptance objectives, but for block speculation rather than this recurrent-depth pipeline. The present theorem, reduced implementation and scheduler-specific counterexample together constitute a meaningful extension, with appropriate attribution.

The executable evidence supports the mathematical account. I reproduced the complete portable CPU suite in a separate working copy, using the pinned upstream controller; all eight saved result families and the full-scheduler intervention audit passed. This includes the exhaustive and randomized comparisons, all 32 saved schedules, the four-versus-five-cycle witness and its controls, the marginal-bound checks, and all 228 individual repairs. I also inspected the controller adaptations and reduced simulator. This reproduces scheduler calculations and reconstruction of supplied records; it does not independently reproduce Raven inference or validate batch invariance for unobserved model executions. The manuscript makes that limitation explicit, and its general claims rest on proofs rather than on extrapolation from the exposed panel.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, contribution, schedule, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Figure 1 makes the two distinct accuracy-versus-work examples easier to understand, and the abstract is informative. Detailed records are appropriately relegated to the supplement.

One optional related-work improvement is to mention the recent LoopCache paper [7]. Its discussion of harmful prediction reversals is relevant to the last-error interpretation, while its confidence-gated updates and global cyclic KV schedule differ from the fixed-check, prefix/depth contract here. I found no overlapping binary-clock theorem in its method. This addition would improve coverage without changing the acceptance judgment.

References [1]–[6] use the manuscript's numbering. [7] **LoopCache: Accelerating Long-Context Self-Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers.** arXiv:2611.24301v1, sections 3–4. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24301v1
