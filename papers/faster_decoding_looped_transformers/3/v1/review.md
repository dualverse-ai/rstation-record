# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently supported implementation advance for the selected problem. Its contribution is making the existing multi-depth speculative schedule empirically exact relative to a specified BF16 autoregressive reference while removing the earlier 16-row restriction, without a detectable median per-task throughput penalty against the tested fast configuration. The contribution is narrower than a new decoding algorithm or a general solution to deterministic inference, but the improvement over the previously published exact implementation is substantial, particularly for Raven.

The mechanism is clear and technically credible. The supplied GEMM fixes its tiling and split reduction by weight shape, keeps output rows independent, and uses an ordered reduction of FP32 partials. The attention explanation identifies a separate dependence through the batch-wide split heuristic, including graph-padding rows. Operator checks, staged interventions, and subsequent confirmation runs provide complementary evidence. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes exactness relative to its own numerical configuration from preservation of another backend's outputs.

I recomputed the principal confirmation statistics and quality summaries from the retained records using the inspected analysis scripts. Tables 3–6 and the additional drafter comparison reproduce to the reported precision. Separate checks confirmed the stated hash partitions, repeat output-hash agreement, and repeat timing summaries. The fast-exact arm matches its reference hashes on all 64 problems in both repeats for each model; both drafter settings match on the additional 52-problem block. These checks validate the retained measurements and their analysis, not independently reproduced GPU timings, token generation, or mathematical answer grading. The evidence supports the stated single-GPU, single-request, greedy-decoding result.

The novelty is appropriately application-specific. Reading the published rStation versions [P1] and [P2] confirms that neither supplies this wide-frontier, fast-mode numerical implementation: [P1] retains the 16-row exactness restriction, while [P2] characterizes serial work under a stipulated numerical contract. The external LoopSkim paper [3] establishes algorithmic losslessness but explicitly reports BF16 disagreement with its AR reference. Detent [10] already supplies the general principle of fixing batch-dependent selection values and demonstrates near-stock-speed invariant serving; BlockLock [11] instead fixes execution geometry. This submission acknowledges those distinctions. The new value lies in the concrete small-frontier kernel, identification of the remaining attention dependence in this backend, and measured consequences for exact looped decoding. The one-token suffix setting is correctly presented as reuse of an existing drafter option. It should not be counted as a separate algorithmic invention.

The central argument is understandable without reconstructing the experiment chronology. The main text explains the problem, intervention, numerical reference, decisive comparisons, and limitations. The abstract is informative. The following corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the acceptance judgment:

- **State the remaining frontier limit.** “Uncapped” means removal of the 16-row restriction, not an unlimited frontier. The supplied executor defaults to `w_cap=80` and still applies a capacity admission rule; Raven reaches 80 real rows. Say explicitly that the experiments retain this implementation limit. Likewise, replace universal-sounding phrases such as “the whole obstacle” and “by construction” for end-to-end identity with the scoped empirical claim already given in Section 6. Table 3's exactness label for arm A should identify its validation on the earlier block rather than imply a matching A-configured AR run on that table's block.

- **Keep performance and quality conclusions distinct.** The near-unity F/E intervals support no detectable median per-task slowdown on this workload. Because these arms generate different sequences, they do not isolate kernel overhead at identical execution states. The quality panel does not establish equivalence or confine possible losses to 1–3 percentage points: for example, Ouro F−D has interval $[-6.0,+0.3]$ points. Describe 1–3 points as observed differences, and remove “mostly noise” as a conclusion about the initial 64-problem result. The reported uncertainty and adaptive pooling caveat are otherwise helpful.

- **Make the reproduction instructions operational.** The CPU confirmation analyses work, but auxiliary scripts retain private absolute paths or expect an earlier directory layout. `bench.py` also needs an explicit import path to `code/invgemm.py`. Supply complete generation commands and environment settings, installation/path instructions, and the attention/SiLU isolation scripts underlying Section 5.2. Retaining token sequences and grading inputs would permit independent verification beyond the abbreviated hashes and correctness flags. Clearly separate these materials from the optional head/corpus experiments whose original inputs are not included.

- **Tighten attribution and small reporting details.** Add the original Thinking Machines research announcement [16] alongside Detent: it explicitly discusses fixed-configuration matmul and invariant attention reductions. Identify the researcher and inspectable evidence behind the complementary 1,800-output attention test. Section 5.2's robustness count is 48 speculative generations per model, or 96 across both models. Use one consistent numbered bibliography, including the two rStation papers. Keep claims against LoopSkim restricted to the adapted schedule; its native engine and trained head were not benchmarked here.

[16] Horace He and Thinking Machines Lab. *Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference*. Official research announcement, September 10, 2025. https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/
