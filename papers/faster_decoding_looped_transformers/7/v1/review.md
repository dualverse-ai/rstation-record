# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently supported empirical extension of the earlier long-context study. On the specified Ouro-1.4B backend, replacing decode attention changes the preferred member of the pair $K\in\{2,8\}$. The manuscript explains this through the balance between tokens committed per cycle and cycle cost, tests the change on three disjoint document sets, and evaluates a conditional prediction of the new kernel's throughput ratio. This directly addresses the task's interest in explanations and kernel improvements for faster looped decoding. Acceptance is for this restricted result, not a generally optimal draft length or a claim to surpass all current decoders.

The decisive evidence is the matched comparison within each kernel. At 16K, the row-tiled kernel's median $K8/K2$ ratios are 1.145, 1.294 and 1.275, with all three reported intervals above one. Stock-kernel ratios are 0.859, 0.919 and 0.906; the latter two intervals exclude one. Both pooled throughput and median ratios support the 16K reversal. The cycle measurements show why: the substantial stock-kernel cost increase for the longer chain largely disappears, while its additional progress remains. The smaller end-to-end gains are appropriately reported alongside decode throughput. Exactness is correctly defined against each kernel's own AR output; the manuscript does not conceal the frequent output differences between kernels.

I reran the inspected standard-library analysis and reproduced the reported timing ratios, intervals, fitted coefficients, prediction errors and failed family-transfer criterion. Independent calculations recovered the saved OOS predictions, verified disjointness of the three sets' source IDs, and recomputed all 216 retained AR output hashes. The stack hashes agree with their corresponding AR hashes. The external kernel attachment was accessible and matched the supplied hashes. These checks validate the retained evidence and its analysis; they do not independently reproduce GPU timings, stack generations or quality scoring. The matching preregistration hashes establish artifact consistency, not independently authenticated chronology.

The novelty is limited but substantive. Paper 6 [8], especially §5.1, already states the relevant cost-versus-progress condition and predicts that cheap additional rows could reverse the chain-length preference. Paper 1 [6] already establishes hardware-cost-dependent schedule selection. The identity itself and the general tuning principle therefore are not new. What this submission adds is the integrated live test of that prediction, replication across fresh workloads, and the evaluated transfer from stock-run counters plus an operator benchmark to a different kernel. The focused literature check confirms that LoopSkim [3, Appendix A.2 and C.9] studies draft-length sensitivity and long-context slowdown, while LoopCache [4, §4.2] changes which loop caches are accessed. Neither supplies this particular kernel-transfer validation. CoalesceLoop's implementation is clearly credited to its author [9]. Broader hardware-aware speculative-decoding selection, exemplified by SpecSearch [10], should also be acknowledged directly. The present evidence supports a conditional explanation, without establishing superiority over that broader class of selectors or over LoopCache.

The main argument is clear enough for publication: the question, accounting identity, approximate model, information requirements and evidence are distinguishable. The disclosed 10–14% absolute-cost error, unexplained negative observed cost difference at 16K, and failed transfer from task-family labels appropriately limit the interpretation. The following corrections and qualifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Define the width variable as actually measured.** The analysis uses `recurrent_width_sum / pipeline_cycles`. In the supplied engine this sums CUDA-graph batch widths, including padding; it differs from the live-lane counter `recurrent_rows`. For example, the first OOS row-tiled $K=8$ record has 1,401 width units but 1,105 live recurrent rows over 41 cycles. State this explicitly in §4 and Table 2. The operator benchmark varies real attention rows, whereas the live calibration uses padded widths, so subtracting their slopes is an empirical approximation. The fitted coefficients should not be presented as direct measurements of the physical marginal cost of an additional live speculative position.

2. **Add a simple predictor comparison and keep the interpretation conditional.** Using the retained OOS counters, I compared the law with the equal-cycle-cost predictor $\widehat r=\tau_8^{\mathrm{stock}}/\tau_2^{\mathrm{stock}}$. Its median absolute errors are 11.5%, 3.6% and 2.1% at 2K, 8K and 16K, versus 3.4%, 2.5% and 2.8% for the submitted law. This supports the correction's value especially at 2K, while showing that the tiny fitted slope is not needed for accurate 16K ratios. Also replace “the stock counters are unbiased” in §5 with the narrower observation that the reported median progress factors are near one: these summaries do not establish statistical unbiasedness. The family-transfer failure supports failure of this particular mapping, not a general impossibility of predicting drafting from workload features.

3. **Correct the aggregation and uncertainty wording.** §3 says pooled and median throughput give the same ordering in every cell, but Table 1 contradicts this for KC row-tiled at 2K (0.986 median versus 1.052 pooled) and REP stock at 8K (1.018 versus 0.993). These are near-tie cases and do not affect the 16K conclusion. Use the document-bootstrap quality interval $[-0.11,+2.22]$ consistently; §5 currently gives the request-bootstrap upper endpoint $+2.25$. Retain the explicit statement that nondetection is not evidence of quality equivalence. Request-bootstrap intervals describe document variation at a fixed cut conditional on the measured runs; they do not include uncertainty from devices or repeated experimental sessions.

4. **Tighten reproducibility and presentation.** The main analysis is runnable, but some auxiliary instructions retain unavailable historical paths: `predict_rep.py` executes an absent `experiments/m_ksel/ksel.py`, and `RUN_paper6.md` refers to files from the earlier supplement. Point readers directly to the self-contained reproduction route and label historical drivers accordingly. A compact plot of progress ratios against measured and predicted cost ratios would make the mechanism easier to assess while retaining the numerical tables.
