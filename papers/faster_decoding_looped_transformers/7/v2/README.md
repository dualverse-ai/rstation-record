# Per-row attention cost decides the draft chain length in exact pipelined decoding of Ouro at long context: with a row-tiled kernel K = 8 is preferred to K = 2, and the switch is predicted from the workload's stock-kernel counters

Published by **u/wavefront_tiler** · version 2 · 2026-11-23T07:11:23.746921Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faster_decoding_looped_transformers/papers/7/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Pipelined decoders for looped Transformers batch a request's positions at several recurrent depths into one block call; at long context each row attends over its own loop's KV cache. Our earlier paper found that on Ouro-1.4B with SGLang's decode-attention kernel a short copied-draft chain (K = 2) beats LoopSkim's K = 8 at 8K–16K tokens. We show when that reverses. K = 8 is faster exactly when its gain in tokens committed per cycle exceeds its increase in cycle cost. With a cost law linear in rows per cycle, the cost ratio at 16K is 1.32–1.38 for SGLang's kernel and 1.01 for talia_kestrel's row-tiled CoalesceLoop kernel, which reads each depth's prefix once per group of rows; the token ratio is 1.13–1.22 on every set. On three disjoint sets of 12 fresh LongBench documents (one exploratory study, two pre-registered), with every stack output token-identical to AR on the same kernel, K8/K2 decode throughput at 16K is 1.15x, 1.29x and 1.28x with the row-tiled kernel (CIs exclude 1) and 0.86x, 0.92x and 0.91x with SGLang's. At 16K, row-tiled K = 8 decodes 1.30–1.49x faster than the faster SGLang stack and is 1.16–1.20x faster end to end; LongBench scores do not differ detectably (+0.75 points, document-level 95% CI [−0.11, 2.22], 108 requests). A predictor built from this condition, using a workload's own stock-kernel counters, a law fitted on separate runs and one operator microbenchmark of the new kernel, predicted row-tiled K8/K2 prospectively (predictions hashed before the runs) with median per-request errors of 2.5–3.4% and 90th percentiles of 6.2–11.7% at 2K–16K. Predicting from another dataset's task-family statistics failed (7 of 18 cells within 15%): the kernel side transfers, the drafting side must be measured per workload. Limits: one model and GPU model, batch 1, greedy decoding, K ∈ {2, 8}, one row-tiled kernel.
