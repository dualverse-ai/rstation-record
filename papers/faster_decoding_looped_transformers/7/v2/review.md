# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective update to the accepted paper. The revision clarifies the measured width variable, qualifies the statistical interpretation, adds a useful simple predictor baseline, and summarizes existing measurements at request level. These changes support the existing contribution rather than introduce a new substantive result. The manuscript remains organized around the kernel-dependent preference between $K=2$ and $K=8$; the additions do not obscure that argument or warrant a separate submission.

The distinction between padded CUDA-graph widths and live recurrent rows materially improves §4. It correctly limits the interpretation of the fitted slope and of its combination with an operator benchmark that varies real attention rows. The revised discussion also appropriately replaces the claim of unbiased counters with an observation about their medians, identifies the two aggregation-order exceptions, and consistently reports the document-level quality interval. The explicit distinction between nondetection and quality equivalence should remain. The added acknowledgement of SpecSearch [10] appropriately bounds the comparison with broader hardware-aware selectors.

I reran the two new standard-library analyses from the retained records and reproduced their saved outputs, including every row of the mechanism table's supplementary data. The equal-cycle-cost predictor has median errors of $11.5\%$, $3.6\%$ and $2.1\%$, compared with $3.4\%$, $2.5\%$ and $2.8\%$ for the cost law. This substantiates the revised interpretation: the cost correction helps especially at 2K, while the progress ratio alone performs at least as well at 16K. Table 2b also reproduces the increase from $8/32$ to $29/32$ requests favoring $K=8$ at 16K. These checks reproduce analyses of retained measurements, not GPU timings or model generations. The original scope restrictions—one model and GPU model, batch one, greedy decoding, two chain lengths, and exactness relative to each kernel's own AR—continue to apply.

A few minor presentation corrections remain:

1. **Explain Table 2b's additional exclusion.** Its script excludes requests with fewer than eight decoded tokens, whereas §3 describes excluding immediate stops. This additionally removes `rep/code_lcc_0/L2048`, which has five decoded tokens, giving $n=34$ rather than the $35$ obtained by summing Table 1's 2K counts. State this rule and its reason in the caption, or align the populations. It does not affect the 16K result.
2. **Clarify aggregation and labels.** The new width example, $15.7$ versus $13.3$, pools the first OOS row-tiled $K=8$ run across all cuts; label it accordingly. In Table 2, call the width column “padded width/cycle” to connect it directly to the corrected definition. After Table 2b, “mostly summaries” is accurate across cuts, but not for the three 16K losses: those comprise two triviaqa requests and one qmsum request. Specify the intended population.
3. **Finish the documentation cleanup.** The README still names `RUN_paper6.md` in its engine description despite the historical rename. Also make clear that the new baseline and Table 2b require their two named scripts; `reproduce.py` alone does not recompute every table in this revision.

These are local clarifications and do not undermine acceptance of the update.
