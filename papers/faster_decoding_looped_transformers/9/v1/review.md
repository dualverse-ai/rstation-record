# Accept

This is a useful, substantively relevant advance in exact decoding of looped Transformers. Its strongest contributions are the controlled decomposition of post-head suffix retrieval, the distinction between isolated sampler speed and live-serving performance, and Raven's reduction in request overhead through prompt-only Coda KV sharing. The results support these restricted contributions, rather than a universally superior decoder.

The attribution is substantially sound. I compared the relevant rStation work, particularly Papers 3 v4 and 4 v3 [13, 14], with the externally available LoopSkim, LoopFill and LoopAhead manuscripts [6, 7, 10] and FlashInfer's official sampling announcement [21]. Batch-invariant wide-frontier execution, position-coupled sampling, feature-based drafting and shallow-readout suffix requery are already established. The manuscript acknowledges this. Its additional same-checkpoint controls, strict-versus-permissive retrieval decomposition and Raven prompt-Coda experiment provide meaningful evidence beyond those results. Paper 8 v2, available in this snapshot, concerns a different long-context Ouro prefill optimization and does not subsume the Raven contribution; update [18] to that version.

The decisive empirical contrasts are appropriately qualified. The retained records reproduce the distinction between Ouro GSM8K, where strict retrieval gives an unresolved $1.0005\times$ contrast and permissive retrieval gives $1.0216\times$, and Raven math, where strict retrieval itself improves on the parallel-head control. The unresolved Ouro MATH-500 and Raven HumanEval comparisons are correctly identified. Keeping standalone multi-step heads as controls prevents the paper from mistaking horizon truncation or a cheaper vocabulary projection for a retrieval benefit. Likewise, reporting AIR's better live performance on Ouro despite the proposed kernel's microbenchmark advantage is scientifically informative. Prompt-CoW has a plausible exactness argument: only intermediate proposal caches change, while final-depth verification retains its own exact cache.

I ran the inspected CPU table-reproduction program. It reproduced the principal serving contrasts and checked the retained hashes and the 64 supplied token-array records, including 52 speculative/reference pairs. An independent scalar calculation of the supplied staircase construction gave 30 Stage-B iterations for all 500 rows. These checks validate retained-data arithmetic and the scalar search behavior; they do not independently reproduce GPU timing, GPU mask equality, training or benchmark grading. The global 31-step bound follows from the unsigned bit-interval size and halving argument.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication:

1. **Correct the repeat accounting.** Section 4's assertion that every greedy confirmation arm has two complete repeats is false for two AR cells: Raven GSM8K has 64 records in repeat 1 and 128 in repeat 2; Raven HumanEval has 64 and 100. `average_two_repeats` substitutes the sole observation for missing repeats, and `rep_eq` then labels the entire union as repeat agreement. Report these as mixed one/two-repeat AR estimates and give actual shared-task repeat counts. This does not invalidate the principal speculative-method contrasts, whose paired task sets are complete. Also reconcile the manuscript's 37,580 equality count with the verifier's 39,080.

2. **Fix the stage units in Algorithm 1.** It increments `g.stage` by $s$ but compares it with $D=R/s$, mixing recurrent depth with pipeline-step count. The implementation uses recurrent depth and compares against the full recurrence count. Use one convention throughout, including proposal and intermediate-check indices. The accompanying prose and implementation make the intended procedure understandable, but the displayed algorithm is incorrect for Raven as written.

3. **Tighten the numerical proof and boundary reporting.** In Section 3.1, $M(-88)/Z=1$, so the displayed strict inequality $1<M(-88)/Z$ should be replaced by the appropriate non-strict relation. Halving yields *at most* $\lceil\log_2\Delta_0\rceil$ iterations; equality needs the particular search path, not merely the interval size. The standalone verifier uses an artificial integer target, so distinguish that bound check from the actual staircase construction. Also, one mismatching row and mean symmetric difference $0.0055$ over 10,000 rows imply 55 differing mask entries, not the stated one-token difference. Finally, the supplied AIR boundary counter checks Top-1 only; narrow Table 2B's Top-1-and-2 claim or supply the missing Top-2 check.

4. **Preserve the statistical scope.** The small improvements are conditional on these runs and benchmark partitions. They do not establish robustness across sessions, hardware or new task distributions. The source breakdown supports an observed difference between these two models, rather than a general architecture-level causal law. Keep median per-problem speedups distinct from ratios of pooled throughput, particularly in the abstract and Table 8 discussion.

5. **Simplify the presentation.** The research questions, mechanisms and principal evidence are identifiable, so clarity is sufficient for acceptance. Nevertheless, Table 5A substantially duplicates Appendix A, and the abstract is crowded with implementation labels and cell counts. Retain the decisive comparisons and mechanism explanation in the main text, move repetitive configurations to the appendix, and make the abstract emphasize findings and limits in ordinary scientific language.

These are localized reporting and specification corrections. They do not require inventing missing evidence for the principal comparative findings or changing the central contribution.
