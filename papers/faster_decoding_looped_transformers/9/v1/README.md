# Position-Coupled Nucleus Verification, Post-Head Suffix Requery Decomposition, and Prompt-CoW Coda Acceleration for Looped Transformers

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faster_decoding_looped_transformers/papers/9/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Exact wide-frontier ($W \leq 80$) decoding for looped Transformers leaves open how sorting-free, position-coupled nucleus verification ($T>0, \text{top-}p<1$) performs across microbenchmarks and live serving, what drives low-data target-state drafting when post-head suffix requery is separated from strict two-token bridging, and how to cut deep-recurrence Coda setup cost without losing exactness. Evaluating Ouro-1.4B ($R=4$) and Raven-TinyLlama ($R=32$) on HumanEval+, MBPP+, GSM8K, and MATH-500 (two-repeat per-problem confirmations, conditional on measured runs; 100% bitwise output equality within our invariant contract at $B=1, L\leq 2\text{K}$), we report three findings. First, under CUDA-graphed execution, our zero-workspace 1-CTA negative-float32 bit-lattice kernel (10 float steps plus at most 31 ULP steps; 30 on staircases; 500/500 tie and staircase mask matches) beats FlashInfer AIR in kernel microbenchmarks on both models; in interleaved live serving, AIR leads Ouro while bit-lattice ties or edges AIR on Raven. Second, single-factor ablations isolate host indexing, rollout reuse, and shallow siblings, while slicing 1-step PrismHead to 8,192 tokens beats full vocabulary in all 8 cells (without excluding multi-step MLP OOV). Post-head requeries split into $w\geq 2$ verbatim anchors (12.3%–51.9%), $w=1$ matches (37.5%–52.4%), and rollouts (0.0%–4.0%): strict $w\geq 2$ suffices on Raven math whereas $w=1$ fallback drives Ouro; standalone 5-step MLP ($w\leq 1$) leads Ouro code, PrismHead-8k + Requery resolves 3/4 math cells (Ouro MATH-500 unresolved), and Bridge-Miss MLP resolves 3/4 Raven cells (HumanEval+ unresolved). Third, O(1) slot reset plus Prompt-CoW Coda lifts Raven client throughput by $1.056\times$–$1.137\times$.
