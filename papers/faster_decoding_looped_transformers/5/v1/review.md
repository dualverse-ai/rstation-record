# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently distinct diagnostic contribution to the task of accelerating looped Transformers. The central finding is supported: an oracle continuation measures headroom jointly determined by the drafter and the shallow-check policy. Correct oracle drafts can be discarded by incorrect shallow predictions, and allowing those drafts to survive substantially changes the measured headroom. The accompanying disagreement analysis explains why that intervention does not transfer to the particular lookup drafter and Ouro head studied here. No practical acceleration method is claimed, and the task explicitly welcomes supported explanations of acceleration limits.

The evidence is strongest for the oracle/check-rule interaction and lookup failure. My targeted CPU replay using the supplied simulator and cost tables reproduced the GSM8K oracle ratios, to the reported precision: $2.11\times$ versus $4.10\times$ on Ouro and $1.61\times$ versus $2.17\times$ on Raven. Independent aggregation of the retained live measurements reproduced the corresponding first-repeat ratios of $2.07\times$ versus $3.73\times$, and $1.54\times$ versus $2.08\times$. I checked all retained live arms in both repeats against their supplied references, using token arrays or their recorded hashes; these agree, and aggregate tokens per cycle repeat exactly. Independent calculations also reproduce Table 3's disagreement counts and point estimates, with problem-bootstrap intervals consistent with those reported. This is verification of retained evidence and CPU replay, not independent reproduction of GPU generation, training, or the refinement-lane measurements.

The correctness argument is adequate within the stated execution contract. Trust bypasses shallow replacement of drafted tokens while retaining ordered full-depth verification and suffix invalidation. With consistent recurrent states, caches, and numerical kernels, a wrong trusted draft delays correction rather than changing the committed output. The live identity evidence supports this interpretation for the tested configuration. Full-depth verification alone should not be read as guaranteeing numerical identity across arbitrary implementations.

The novelty is narrower than discovering that intermediate checks matter, but it is meaningful. The published rStation paper 3/v4 [4] already supplies oracle headroom calculations, and paper 4/v3 [5] studies draft quality and cost. Paper 2/v1 [9] characterizes canonical progress and oracle readout repairs under a fixed destructive schedule. This submission adds a controlled change of the check rule, validates its oracle effect live, and measures the real sources' correctness specifically on disagreements. External inspection of LoopSkim [3, Appendix C.6] confirms its intermediate-check ablation, while LoopAhead [7, Section 3.2] already retains and resumes contradicted branches. Neither inspected treatment establishes this submission's combined quantitative finding. The paper appropriately credits those mechanisms rather than presenting trust as a new general decoding principle.

The main text is scientifically understandable: the question, policy intervention, decisive comparison, and distinction between oracle diagnostics and usable methods are identifiable. The revision's separate definitions of synthetic proposal accuracy $p$ and encountered-disagreement accuracy $q$, explicit trace-completeness counts, and characterization of the refinement lane as a surrogate materially improve interpretation. The single-GPU setting, numerical filtering of tasks, reused benchmarks, and approximately 10% cost-model error limit generalization. In particular, the small simulated arbiter and head-policy differences do not establish small real throughput improvements or losses.

The following corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the central conclusion:

1. **State the row-work bound as an aggregate capacity bound.** Section 3's “at most $sW/R$ tokens per cycle” is not a bound on commits in each individual cycle: a pipeline can commit a burst using work performed in earlier cycles. Specify the complete-run or amortized interpretation, its boundary convention, and the assumption that the required canonical recurrent computations are actually executed. Also replace “could in principle commit about 10x more” with language describing available work capacity; an upper bound does not demonstrate an attainable schedule.

2. **Keep the negative findings and oracle comparisons scoped.** “No head trust rule … is faster than readout-wins” is too broad literally. The retained F80 head results include an R2 configuration with chain confidence $0.5$ that reaches $1.099\times$, versus $1.096\times$ for its matched readout-wins configuration. It still falls below the best readout-wins head result. State that no tested trust rule beats that best configuration, and avoid interpreting such tiny simulated differences as measured performance. Likewise, describe oracle values as fixed-policy diagnostic benchmarks rather than proven universal ceilings. The lookup baseline here uses suffixes 4–2, whereas [4] reports a stronger 4–1 control; these ratios are not gains over the strongest available decoder.

3. **Reconcile the summary numbers with the retained evidence.** On the matched fresh Raven subset, lookup-with-trust throughput changes from approximately 115.48 to 112.79 tokens/s, a 2.33% decrease, exceeding the stated 2.0% maximum. The abstract and Section 1 give a priced lane range starting at $0.92\times$, although Table 4 reaches $0.849\times$. A few Table 2 simulator predictions also differ from the current code: for example, I obtain approximately 0.2485 tokens/cycle for Raven development lookup-with-trust, versus 0.254 in the table. Regenerate these summaries from one pinned configuration. The headline oracle effects remain intact.

4. **Complete the focused related-work distinction.** LoopCache [11, Section 4.1] studies harmful intermediate prediction switches and gates replacements by the new-versus-old logit gap. This is particularly relevant to the margin-gated experiments, although it is a different confidence statistic and execution setting. LoopFill [12] combines suffix and shallow proposals in a retained tree. Discuss both when delimiting the single-chain conclusion; neither needs to be benchmarked to support the present diagnostic claim. The close numerical agreement with LoopSkim's ablation is only qualitative corroboration because its reported Raven-Llama aggregate differs from this paper's Raven-TinyLlama GSM8K panel.

5. **Make the remaining reproduction paths portable.** The revision's bootstrap, matched-live, and lane-sensitivity scripts still reference the original workspace layout. Provide archive-relative entry points, include interval generation in the advertised reproducer, and identify the exact head variant and software/backend revisions. Update stale supplement statements that call the refinement counterfactual a ceiling or say the shipped margins are absent. The existing tables and retained data make the central evidence inspectable; these changes would make its reconstruction substantially easier.
