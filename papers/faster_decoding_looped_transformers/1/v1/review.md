# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, appropriately scoped empirical result for faster decoding in looped Transformers: in the tested BF16 serving configuration, restricting the speculative frontier to the small-matrix kernel regime preserves the deterministic autoregressive outputs, and this restriction changes which admission rule and schedule are effective. The contribution is directly relevant to the supplied problem. Its significance is the demonstrated interaction between numerical execution, frontier capacity and scheduling, rather than a new speculative verification algorithm or a generally superior optimizer.

The principal evidence supports acceptance. I recomputed the confirmatory comparisons from the retained per-task records: the cap-aware schedules achieve median speedups of $1.051\times$ on Ouro and $1.053\times$ on Raven over the capped published-setting adaptation of LoopSkim. The matched admission control supports the different explanations on the two models: Ouro benefits mainly from admitting partial draft chains, whereas Raven benefits from changing the schedule. Pooled throughput relative to deterministic AR is approximately $3.915\times$ and $7.241\times$, respectively. Independently matching the retained hashes against the corresponding AR records reproduces all 2,336 agreements in Table 2a, with recorded maximum widths of 12 or 16. These checks verify the submitted records and statistical calculations; I did not reproduce GPU inference or independently compare the original token sequences.

The novelty is limited but sufficient for this empirical systems contribution. LoopSkim [3], particularly Appendices A.2 and C.10–C.11, already supplies multi-depth checks, configuration tuning, RTX 5090 evaluation and output-identity evidence in FP32. Detent [10] explicitly identifies kernel selection as a source of batch dependence; BlockLock [11] and Vosti [12] further establish the importance of a specified numerical execution contract. SpecSearch [8] already motivates hardware-aware speculative configuration. The present paper appropriately credits these ideas. Its additional result is the measured BF16 frontier restriction and its consequences for draft admission in recurrent pipelines. It should not be read as the first exact pipelined decoder, a general invariance theorem, or evidence of superiority over the original LoopSkim engine or its trained-head variant. The manuscript generally maintains these distinctions.

The scientific argument is clearly communicated. The main text explains the schedule, numerical mechanism, controlled comparison and limitations; the exploratory history is separated from the confirmatory results. Disclosing failed thresholds and acknowledging that simple tuning finds similar fast-mode configurations substantially improves the interpretation. The abstract is informative. The one-GPU, batch-one, greedy-decoding scope and the empirical nature of exactness are acceptable limitations for this contribution.

Several corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the supported central result:

- **Separate median agreement from exact replay.** Section 5.1 says steps per committed token “match exactly,” followed by a median ratio of 1.000. The retained records show unequal cycle counts even for identical-output tasks: 11/23 in the Ouro GSM8K $K24$ comparison and 12/23 in the Raven HumanEval comparison. The latter ratios span approximately 0.901–1.101. State that the *median* is one, and report the dispersion. Likewise, medians near one do not establish statistical unbiasedness. Describe the seven off-path models as sensitivity tests unless their claimed global bounding properties are proved.
- **Correct the tuning-control account.** The retained sweep contains 20 runs, rather than the reported 16. Its pooled HumanEval timings are approximately 1.261 ms/token for Ouro $K16$ and 1.290 for $K20$: $K16$ is about 2.3% faster, not 2.7% slower. Reconcile the run count and compute accounting. Preserve the qualification that the sweep-selected $K16$ was not evaluated on the untouched confirmation block; similar development choices do not demonstrate equal held-out performance.
- **Make the evidence qualifications explicit.** The 2,336 generations include repeated schedules and repeated runs on shared problems, not 2,336 independent prompts. Report unique problem counts alongside this total. Task-bootstrap intervals describe variation across tasks conditional on the measured repeats; add a compact account of between-repeat variation and retain the limitation that AR was timed once. Fast-mode quality evidence comes from an earlier panel and does not establish non-inferiority for the confirmatory arms.
- **Complete the reproducibility cross-references.** The retained validation file has five comparisons and omits the Ouro GSM8K CopyLoop row of Table 1. Supply that row's underlying residuals. Narrow the claim that the analysis script recomputes “every main-text ratio” to its actual coverage, and provide a compact end-to-end timing summary rather than only retaining those measurements in the data. A plot of call cost against frontier width would also make the central mechanism easier to see.
