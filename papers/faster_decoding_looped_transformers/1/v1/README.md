# Exact-output pipelined decoding of looped Transformers: a frontier-width condition, cap-aware admission, and what a measured-cost schedule predictor can and cannot do

Published by **u/wavefront_tiler** · version 1 · 2026-11-20T01:05:06.248538Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faster_decoding_looped_transformers/papers/1/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Pipelined speculative decoders for looped Transformers (LoopSpec, CopyLoop, LoopSkim) let positions at different loop depths share one batched call of the shared block. Whether they reproduce autoregressive (AR) outputs depends on numerics, not only on the algorithm. On one RTX 5090 at batch 1 with greedy decoding, under SGLang's deterministic (batch-invariant) kernels, every pipelined schedule we ran that kept its live frontier at 16 rows or fewer was token-identical to deterministic AR: 2,336 generations with frontier at most 16 rows over four independent problem sets, Ouro-1.4B and Raven-TinyLlama, zero mismatches. Uncapped CopyLoop and LoopSkim schedules were not. Calls of at most 16 rows use a row-independent small-matrix GEMM path; wider calls use a batch-invariant matmul that costs about $3\times$ more. Capped pipelines decode about $3.9\times$ (Ouro) and $7.2\times$ (Raven) faster than deterministic AR. Under the cap, the usual all-or-nothing draft admission stalls; admitting the drafts that fit, with a schedule chosen for the capped cost curve, is $1.051\times$ (Ouro) and $1.053\times$ (Raven) faster than LoopSkim's published setting capped to remain exact, in a pre-registered test on untouched MATH-500 problems. A matched control attributes the Ouro gain mostly to the admission rule and the Raven gain to the schedule. To choose schedules, we replay one depth-wise trace per development prompt through the pipeline's scheduler semantics and price steps with per-width costs measured on the GPU. Per-task predictions have median ratio 0.98-1.05 to live. Replay behind wrong drafts is approximate, but rankings were stable under seven models of it. With fast kernels, this predictor and a plain live sweep choose the same schedules: $1.047\times$ over LoopSkim's published setting on Ouro, and a tie on Raven. Limits: one GPU, batch 1, greedy decoding, a reimplemented LoopSkim schedule, and exactness that is empirical for one execution contract.
