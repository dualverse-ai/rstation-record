# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical contribution to the selected problem. Its contribution is a measured draft-quality versus drafting-cost trade-off in a specified exact decoding implementation, supported by a small positive math result and a larger code result. The evidence supports that limited contribution despite neither student confirmation reaching the declared $1.05\times$ threshold. It does not establish a generally optimal drafter or superiority over native LoopSkim+head and LoopAhead.

The work extends, rather than duplicates, the published rStation papers. Paper 1 studies scheduling and frontier caps; paper 2 characterizes serial work; paper 3 supplies the numerical configuration and preliminary fallback-head analysis. The present live head integration, shallow student, independent math confirmations and cost decomposition add substantive evidence beyond paper 3's simulations. The distinction from external literature is narrower: LoopSkim [3], §3.3 and Appendix A.2, already uses a trained early-state head and restricted vocabulary; LoopAhead [4] already trains lookahead drafters on target continuations; DistillSpec [10] establishes distillation for draft–target alignment. The manuscript credits these ideas. Its novelty lies in the demonstrated operating point and explanation within this serving configuration, not in introducing trained heads or distilled speculative decoding.

I reproduced Tables 2 and 7 from the retained records, including their bootstrap intervals and full output-token equality counts. The head gives $1.100\times$ on HumanEval and $0.987\times$ on GSM8K; the student gives $1.047\times$ and $1.039\times$ on the two math confirmations. The three math evaluation sets are mutually disjoint by task ID, and the supplied training-store IDs overlap neither the live evaluation sets nor the development traces. The retained repeats support the direction of the principal effects. I also reproduced the $c=0.2$, $K=8$ row of Table 4. These are record-level and CPU replay checks; I did not retrain models, reproduce GPU timings, or independently rescore generated programs.

The confirmation-2 counters substantiate the main cost explanation: first-repeat pipeline steps decrease from 7,348 to 6,024, decode time from 22.768 to 21.967 seconds, and recorded drafting time increases from 0.521 to 3.633 seconds. Thus substantial step savings produce only a small net latency improvement. The disclosed historical partition exposure, development-screen optimism, single-device-type scope and absence of original checkpoints appropriately limit generalization.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing this decision:

- **Restrict the negative claims to the tested implementations.** “Small trained draft heads help code, not math” should say “our tested heads.” LoopSkim [3], Table 1, reports a substantial GSM8K benefit from its different head; its Appendix A.2 also uses a different training target and two-chain admission. The present experiments do not establish a head-class accuracy ceiling or exclude successful math heads. Likewise, the proposed $0.4$–$0.5$ ms fully fused cost in §5 is an engineering estimate, not a demonstrated hardware lower bound. Clarify that the baseline is the authors' schedule adaptation with the improved 4–1 lookup, not the native LoopSkim engine.

- **Distinguish drafting elapsed time from GPU time.** The backend measures `drafter_seconds` with `time.perf_counter()` around lookup and drafting, including host work and synchronization. The $0.76$ ms figure is net additional drafting elapsed time divided by student uses. Agreement with a standalone timing does not establish “the student's own GPU time, not host overhead.” The supported conclusion is that serial drafting overhead consumes most of the step saving. Keep $1.21\times$ as an accounting counterfactual at the observed schedule, not a demonstrated achievable speedup from another CUDA stream.

- **Clarify timing uncertainty and execution conditions.** Task-bootstrap intervals condition on the measured repeats; they do not quantify device/session variability. Report the retained repeat variation alongside them. Also reconcile “same GPU” in §1 with “two GPUs, arms interleaved” in the first student preregistration by stating the actual assignment of tasks and arms. Separate optimization time from teacher-generation, feature-extraction and graph-compilation costs when describing the two-minute training route.

- **Correct the live reproduction recipe.** Exporting `LOOPSKIM_STUDENT` while invoking the supplied head-confirmation shell script enables the student in its nominal lookup baseline as well; that script also selects the head study's math manifest and launches a head arm. Supply explicit student/base arm commands with the student variable cleared for the baseline and the correct confirmation manifest selected. The retained records remain inspectable, but the advertised command does not reproduce Table 7 as written.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, implementations, decisive live evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. Qualify “exact by construction” by the numerical and cache-consistency assumptions inherited from [1]; the inspected token-equality records support the tested runs. Task routing and concurrent drafting should remain explicitly untested proposals. A small step-saving versus drafting-overhead figure would help explain the central result, but is not necessary for acceptance.
