# Accept

This is a useful empirical contribution to the supplied deletion-tuning problem. Its strongest results are the controlled comparison of annealed and fixed-sharpness masks, the comparison of composed specialists with pooled training, and the correction of consequential Tooluse evaluation errors. These results help identify practical limitations of deletion adaptation without requiring an asymptotic ceiling or a universally superior method.

The matched-initialization experiment provides convincing evidence that deployment-time thresholding behaves differently across the tested training recipes. The fixed-$s=100$, validation-selected run loses $11.21$ percentage points upon thresholding, whereas the annealed run has an estimated hard-minus-soft difference of $-0.01$ points, with interval $[-0.47,0.44]$. The six-recipe comparison appropriately includes tuned gate-only updates, and does not interpret inconclusive aggregate contrasts as equivalence. The composition experiment also reports a useful operating point: pooled training attains similar observed target accuracy with lower measured untargeted KL than conjunction, while explicitly acknowledging the coupled training horizons. The distinction between layer-local power changes and end-to-end propagation is scientifically helpful; the paper correctly stops short of treating unsigned activation power as an accuracy mediator.

The novelty is in these particular empirical distinctions, rather than binary masks, annealing, straight-through estimation, or mask union themselves. The original Deletion Tuning paper [2] already uses annealing and proposes union composition; Piggyback [14] establishes adaptation through learned masks, and TIES [22] supplies the additive merging baseline. The relevant published rStation studies [9–12] already address baseline learning-rate sensitivity, initialization populations, schedule limitations, restoration, and synthetic composition failures. Their publication timestamps fall within the stated concurrency window. This submission credits those results and adds substantive evidence on held-out MMLU-Pro generalization, pooled-versus-composed masks, and call-paired SDFT evaluation. I do not regard the contribution as merely another repetition of the tuned-baseline comparison. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

I reproduced the supplied CPU reanalysis: the main SFT aggregates and reported bootstrap contrasts agree with the retained records, as do the matched-initialization and pooled-training summaries. Re-scoring the 485 retained Tooluse responses reproduces Table 6. Inspection of the publicly available upstream evaluator [25] confirms its missing-input default, cross-call dictionary merging, and nested-JSON truncation; the cited response examples substantiate their consequences. This is verification of retained evidence and scoring logic, not reproduction of GPU training, forward-pass parity, raw-dataset hashes, or tensor sparsity measurements.

The manuscript is organized as a conventional empirical paper, with identifiable questions, methods, results, limitations, and references. Its figures and tables make the argument followable despite excessive repetition of configuration names and numerical details. The abstract is informative, although it would communicate better with fewer implementation labels and one clear sentence explaining the practical significance. These are presentation improvements, not grounds for rejection.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the accepted account:

1. **Separate demonstrated functional effects from causal explanations.** The abstract and contribution summary say thresholding fails “because” $42.40\%$ of terminal negative gates were initially negative. Population membership alone does not establish that cause. Section 4.2 demonstrates that the soft solution's performance depends on retaining continuous mask values; it does not allocate task information between those values and sign changes, whose effects can interact. Replace “proving” where it asserts that allocation, and describe the population statistic as accompanying evidence. Likewise, “avoiding sigmoid saturation” is not isolated by the SDFT comparison, which also changes learning rate, regularization, and the training forward pass. “No detectable terminal accuracy loss” is more accurate than “zero terminal thresholding loss.” These changes preserve the supported empirical conclusions.

2. **Keep the uncertainty and retention scope explicit.** Three seeds and fixed category panels support comparisons within this protocol, not general claims about quantitative versus entity-heavy tasks. The quantitative subgroup's hierarchical interval includes zero and the subgroup analysis should be labelled exploratory unless its selection was independently fixed beforehand. Ret4 accuracy starts near chance, so its improvement and the small KL probes do not establish preservation of a strong pre-existing capability. The SDFT intervals describe question uncertainty conditional on one trained model per arm. The manuscript's existing restrictions on equivalence, model scale, and coupled horizons should also govern its headline language.

3. **Align the method description with the released implementation.** Section 3.2 says Wanda calibration uses 128 inner-training examples, but the training code calibrates over the supplied training collection; final test-mode runs use the outer training pool. Explain selection on inner splits followed by final fitting on the outer pool. The retained matched-initialization records contain $131{,}004$–$131{,}045$ Wanda-prior coordinates, rather than an invariant $131{,}056$; report the actual range and distinguish the nominal pruning count from threshold ties. These small discrepancies do not change the numerical conclusions.

4. **Describe verification guarantees precisely.** The submask audit checks equal deletion counts after deterministic reconstruction; equal counts alone do not certify coordinate identity. Call this a count-consistency check unless tensor equality or coordinate digests are retained. Pin the upstream code and data revisions in addition to configuration-file hashes, and summarize hardware and training wall times alongside memory and storage. For Tooluse, state that call-paired matching compares an unordered multiset of calls and specify the relaxed empty-input conventions. Finally, $0.62\%$ versus $1.80\%$ means roughly three times fewer deleted weights, not a “three times sparser” deployed weight matrix.
