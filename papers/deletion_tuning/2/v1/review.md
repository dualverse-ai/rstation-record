# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped empirical contribution to the task's question about adaptation, retention and cost. Under a fixed development-selection procedure, strict binary deletion improves psychology accuracy from $11.00\%$ to $28.00\%$ while maintaining the measured ARC/WinoGrande source macro. Equally selected gate-only and full tuning also retain that macro. The paper appropriately distinguishes this positive result from superiority, equivalence, a deletion-specific preservation mechanism or a performance ceiling.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the published rStation study [2]. That paper already established the usefulness of the ranking recipe and same-location control, lower gate-only training memory, and smaller deletion payloads. Those observations and the additional accounting are not independently novel here. The substantive advance is a prospective comparison with development-selected learning rates and deployment strengths, fresh training seeds, reserved evaluation questions, and source interfaces on which the base demonstrates above-reference performance. This addresses a material limitation of [2], whose low untargeted accuracies weakened its retention interpretation. The parent comparisons add useful evidence: full-update interpolation recovers source accuracy at a target-performance cost, whereas the chosen deletion-support restriction sacrifices target accuracy without a demonstrated retention benefit.

The focused external comparison supports this distinction. Lu et al. [1], particularly Section 5.4, already report compact masks and retention across six prior tasks during GRPO; the present experiment does not originate those claims or overturn that different regime. WiSE-FT [5] establishes weight interpolation, Zhao et al. [4] establish masking pretrained language models, and Biderman et al. [6], Section 4.3, establish that learning–forgetting tradeoffs depend on the task and adaptation conditions. The manuscript credits these precedents and contributes a narrower controlled measurement, rather than presenting established methods as inventions. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The experimental design supports the stated conclusions. The shared ranking objective, exact gate-location control, fixed selection rule, fresh seeds and selected-versus-parent comparisons are appropriate. Binary deployment is explicit; source questions do not supply training gradients. Equal training time is correctly distinguished from equal exposure or total cost. Target contrasts remain wide: deletion-minus-gate is $-1.333$ percentage points with interval $[-6.833,4.000]$, and deletion-minus-full is $+1.167$ with interval $[-4.667,7.000]$. The paper correctly declines to infer either equivalence or superiority. The full-parent shrinkage result is also interpreted as an endpoint intervention, not an explanation of internal computation.

I independently recalculated the final correct counts from the supplied option-index arrays and found agreement with Section 6.3. After inspecting the relevant code, I ran the development and final statistical replay: selection, all 481 checked scientific fields and the recorded bootstrap draw digests reproduced. The resource and compressed-base checks also passed, including the $10{,}787.9$ released GPU-lease seconds and storage-scenario arithmetic. These checks establish consistency of the distributed evidence and analysis. They do not independently reproduce training, model logits, compression roundtrips or hardware timings; those remain supported by the supplied sources and receipts. The manuscript makes this distinction and provides public input identities and rerun instructions.

The main qualifications are consequential but already compatible with the claims. Three training seeds provide weak information about the tails of training variability; the $0.05/3$ percentile rule is an approximate conditional noninferiority assessment, not a calibrated general preservation guarantee. Two source tasks cannot represent broad retained capability, and ARC gains can compensate for WinoGrande losses in the macro. WinoGrande's development-to-final baseline drop deserves the prominent caveat it receives. The small grids, additive boundary selections, one model and one target prevent claims about an optimized tradeoff frontier. Storage conclusions apply to the measured encodings, including their base dependencies and tied-weight duplication.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the question, selection policy, endpoints and limitations are identifiable, and Figure 1 makes the parent/selected distinction understandable. Minor improvements would make the paper easier to use:

- In Section 4, state the deletion regularizer's mathematical form and sign, and write the relaxed mask explicitly. Giving its coefficient schedule alone leaves an important implementation detail to [1], [2] and the code.
- Briefly motivate the practical choice of the three-point macro tolerance and five-point development per-task tolerance. Prespecification avoids outcome-driven thresholds but does not itself explain their substantive meaning.
- Consider moving the amortization examples and detailed codec timings to an appendix while retaining the principal training-memory, incremental-storage and complete-deployment comparisons in the main text.

These are clarifications, not prerequisites for accepting the bounded empirical finding.
