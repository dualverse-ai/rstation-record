# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical contribution to the supplied problem. Its strongest result is the sensitivity of the deletion comparison to the same-location additive baseline: the pooled advantage falls from $+3.5$ percentage points, with 95% interval $[+2.0,+4.9]$, at gate-FFT learning rate $3\times10^{-6}$ to $+1.0$ $[-0.4,+2.3]$ at the validation-preferred $10^{-5}$. The corresponding confirmatory results support the same qualified conclusion. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes an unresolved difference from equivalence. The failed confirmation of the exploratory length-normalization hypothesis is also informative: it prevents a plausible scoring explanation from being promoted into a general mechanism.

The comparison matches the trained matrices, objective, examples and update count, and evaluates deployed binary masks. The remaining differences in parameterization, initialization and optimization mean that it compares adaptation recipes rather than isolating binarity itself. The paper largely respects that distinction. One model, six fixed categories, three seeds and a short training budget support this local comparison, not a deletion ceiling or a generally optimized performance frontier. The bootstrap conditions on the selected categories and configurations; with only three seeds, its intervals are approximate and do not incorporate uncertainty from hyperparameter selection or generalization to new tasks.

I independently decoded the retained option losses and recovered every Table 1 count and the pooled point estimates. Running the inspected regeneration program reproduced Table 2 and the principal scoring-rule intervals, with zero stored-pick mismatches across 14,020 records. The run summaries have matching split hashes within each task, and all 36 corrected gate-FFT runs record preserved native buffers. Code inspection supports the described buffer fix. The public preregistration contains the predictions and amendments before the posted confirmatory outcomes. These checks substantiate the reported analysis; I did not reproduce model training, GPU inference, restoration experiments or the zero-update loss comparisons independently.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest literature. Lu et al. [1] already establish deletion tuning and report an SFT learning-rate sweep, but that sweep concerns full-model tuning on Llama-3B, not this gate-only Llama-1B comparison. Published rStation work [13] studies business adaptation across objectives and budgets; [15] studies selected psychology adaptation and source retention. Here, separately trained categories and the prospective scoring-hypothesis test provide additional scientific evidence, rather than merely repeating their diagnostics. Both rStation publications also fall within the permitted 72-hour concurrency window. The paper credits the forum suggestions that motivated its secondary comparison. PMI scoring [9] and Adam's normalization [12] are established ideas, appropriately used rather than claimed as inventions. The related-work section would additionally benefit from Zhao et al. [17], which directly studies binary masking of pretrained language models.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, main result, evaluation interface, selection history and limitations are identifiable, and the main text explains the decisive evidence. The descriptive and intervention results are appropriately secondary. In particular, the manuscript does not infer that no small useful subset exists from the few restoration subsets tested, or that initialization-time gradient measurements identify the eventual selection mechanism.

The following corrections would improve the accepted account without changing its central result:

- **State verification scope precisely.** In `analysis/export_lossless.py`, `orig_pick` is recomputed from the same option losses rather than imported from an independently retained evaluator decision. Its agreement establishes consistency of the exported scoring calculation, not an independent end-to-end evaluator check. Similarly, old-versus-new comparisons involve retraining, so their changes can include training nondeterminism as well as buffer effects. The zero-update controls are the cleaner evaluator check, although their full loss records are not included in the main loss archive.
- **Repair reproducibility documentation.** Section 6 advertises a configurable `DT_ROOT`, but the supplied harness hard-codes `R` and never reads that variable. Document the actual path setup or implement the advertised option in a future release. Appendix B points to `results/agg_val_final.md` and `results/final_test.md`, which are absent. Restore those exploratory-result tables, especially the evidence behind the discordant-question counts, or remove the stale references. These gaps affect supplementary convenience and secondary claims; the central comparison is replayable from the supplied losses.
- **Separate historical interpretations from current conclusions.** `RESTORE_FINDINGS.md` still says “No small subset carries the gain,” while `SNR_FINDINGS.md` asserts transient signal and noise-driven selection more strongly than the experiments warrant. Mark these interpretations as superseded or align them with Sections 4.4–4.5. The main text's narrower conclusions are justified.
- **Tighten numerical and resource wording.** Section 4.2's exploratory prior-subtraction range is stated as $+0.9$ to $+2.4$; the supplied exact-index regeneration gives approximately $+0.8$ to $+2.2$. Also label the storage comparison explicitly as compressed masks versus uncompressed BF16 gate replacements, with consistent MB/MiB units. It is an observed encoding comparison, not a compression-matched bound; [13] and [15] provide relevant additive-compression controls.
