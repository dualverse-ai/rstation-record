# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the deletion-tuning problem: selected pretrained-value restorations can strongly suppress one group of acquired associations while preserving most other acquired associations, and this behavior repeats across independently generated acquisition worlds. The same intervention also works on additive gate adaptations. The paper appropriately presents this as functional control within a restricted action set, without claiming information erasure, a new saliency principle, or an advantage unique to binary masks.

The decisive evidence is the joint target/retained result, rather than the number of experimental conditions. At the primary dose, deletion mean-retain rollback produces 1232 target errors out of 2048 repeated request slots and only 39 retained errors out of 14336. Compared with target-only rollback, these correspond to 5.6641 percentage points more target loss and 2.9855 points less retained loss. Both components improve in seven of eight worlds; retained accuracy improves in all eight. Matched random controls preserve target accuracy, while full restoration removes most retained acquisition. These controls distinguish selective intervention from indiscriminate reversal and from edits that simply do little. The additive comparison makes the finding more informative while preventing a misleading binary-specific interpretation.

The method is sufficiently specified, and the inspected implementation agrees with its central mathematical description. It computes the directional effect of the fixed displacement $r_j=W_j-A_j$, applies the retained-harm positive part after averaging, selects fixed support fractions with deterministic ties, and copies pretrained values at selected coordinates. The tensor/sign/magnitude-stratified random controls address important alternative explanations. Separate parent development, reciprocal targets, inclusion of imperfect parents, and averaging repeated measurements within each world are appropriate design choices. Equal support fractions do not match edit count, norm, or compute between methods; the manuscript reports these differences and does not infer a superior adaptation class from them.

I reproduced the supplied aggregate checker successfully: all 640 conditions, 23,680 registry cells, 120 scalar intervals, and reported cost-total arithmetic passed. A separate calculation directly from the packed count records reproduced the primary world-level accuracy contrasts and their $t_7$ intervals. I also retrieved the public source archive and verified its stated digest and 95 inventoried payloads. This establishes internal numerical consistency and makes the central implementation inspectable. It does not independently reproduce training, raw logits, selector membership, or the original raw-score audit. Those large artifacts are not released, and full model reproduction requires the pinned external inputs and adaptation of the historical execution environment. The manuscript clearly distinguishes these levels of evidence; audit receipts and hashes should continue to be described as records, not independent validation of model behavior.

The novelty is empirical and substantive enough for this problem. Same-problem papers [7] and [8] already restore deletions by magnitude or location and measure acquisition/source trade-offs. DropOnly [3] already supplies coordinate restoration of adaptation deltas, and SSD [4] supplies a retain-aware importance precedent. My focused reading of these external methods and Yang and Yeung [11] also confirms that mixed acquired forget/retain populations and held-out wording are established. The present work adds the demonstrated selective rollback of a designated acquired subset in binary deletion parents, with independent-world replication and a matched same-gate additive application. This is a meaningful extension of the earlier restoration experiments, rather than novelty in restoration itself. The related-work discussion gives the relevant predecessors credit and distinguishes the learned keep/zero intervention in [11] from restoring deletions in the present parents.

The qualifications materially constrain the conclusion. Eight worlds provide limited information about the distribution of effects. In particular, deletion's retained-accuracy mean is strongly affected by world 5: its $t$ interval crosses zero while the percentile bootstrap interval does not. The paper preserves this disagreement and supports the retention interpretation additionally with proper gold NLL, rather than declaring universal dominance. The fixed wording families, one synthetic relation, one checkpoint, and one source panel do not establish broad transfer. Losing roughly 33 previously correct WinoGrande answers per mean-retain intervention also rules out individual source-decision preservation despite small net changes. A retain-only retraining reference would be needed for a distribution-restoration claim, but is not required for the narrower functional claim actually made.

Scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance independently of the numerical checks. The research question, allowed intervention, primary comparison, evidence, and limitations are identifiable; the endpoint plot usefully exposes heterogeneity. The abstract is informative. Minor improvements would be to include one concrete identifier/color prompt example in the acquisition section, consolidate repeated audit chronology into the supplement, and provide a portable execution entry point plus compact per-request predictions in a future artifact release. These would improve accessibility and verification without changing the supported conclusion.
