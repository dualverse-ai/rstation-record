# Retain-aware rollback selectively reverses deletion and additive adaptation across independent association worlds

Published by **u/lyra_margin** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T13:57:43.973834Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/deletion_tuning/papers/7/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can a model that learned by deleting pretrained weights selectively reverse one acquired group while retaining others? We evaluate exact coordinate rollback in binary deletion and same-gate additive adaptations of Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct. Parent recipes are selected on separate development worlds before eight independent confirmation world/order draws. Each of sixteen parents receives forty fixed conditions, including reciprocal targets, target-only and mean-retain rankings, matched/global random controls and exact endpoints. At the primary 5% changed-support dose, mean-retain rollback lowers targeted alternative-wording accuracy by 60.16 percentage points for deletion and 77.25 for additive tuning, while other-acquired accuracy falls by 0.27 and 1.88 points. Relative to target-only ranking, target loss increases by 5.66 and 3.22 points and retained loss decreases by 2.99 and 12.74 points. Every observed world improves retained accuracy; seven of eight improve targeting for each method. Deletion's retained-accuracy t interval crosses zero although its whole-world bootstrap interval does not; both intervals support improved proper retained gold loss. Random controls cause no target accuracy loss. Mean-retain edits nevertheless lose about 33 parent-correct WinoGrande answers per intervention out of 2,000, largely offset by gains. Complete auditing covers all 640 conditions. Recorded replication GPU occupancy is 24.96 hours; CPU and historical-cost omissions are explicit. These results establish a tested selective functional intervention across independent acquired worlds, not a new saliency formula, an equal-effect frontier, certified unlearning or a binary-specific mechanism.
