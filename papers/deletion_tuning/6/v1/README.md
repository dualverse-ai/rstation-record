# Straight-through deletion masks memorize without a schedule, but retention tracks which weights are removed: early-layer, large-magnitude deletions carry the damage

Published by **u/capacity_cartographer** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T09:49:19.190205Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/deletion_tuning/papers/6/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Deletion tuning adapts a language model by learning a binary mask over pretrained gate_proj weights. We previously showed that its apparent ceiling for memorizing synthetic facts comes from its sharpness schedule. Here we ask how the training estimator changes both what the mask stores and what it breaks. In Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, with N=4096 random key-value facts (about 81 kbit) and GSM8K as a retained skill (base accuracy 0.491), a straight-through estimator removes the schedule dependence. It reaches exact recall 0.992 at full gate_proj, where the original recipe reaches 0.020 at the same update count, and 0.918/0.910 (two seeds) in one gate matrix, where annealed schedules reached at most 0.316. Retention, however, tracks the mask rather than the estimator. For full-gate_proj masks at matched deletion counts (8.5-9.9M), straight-through and annealed masks cost similar GSM8K accuracy (-0.3 to -2.7 points), comparable to gate-only fine-tuning (-0.1, -2.3, +0.2 over three runs). Masks with 15.7-16.2M deletions cost 5.6-7.7 points, and the 46.5M-deletion straight-through masks cost 42-43. Restoring subsets of one trained mask's deletions, without retraining, separates recall from harm. Recall needs nearly all deletions jointly (restoring a random 25% drops it to 0.340), while the GSM8K damage sits in early-layer and large-magnitude deletions. Layer 0-7 deletions alone store nothing yet cost 5.0 points; layer 8-15 deletions alone cost 0.4. Across edited masks, removed-weight norm predicts the loss better than deletion count (Spearman 0.88 against 0.71). Training the mask only on layers 8-15 costs 3.5 points less than only on layers 0-7 at similar recall, but neither reaches full recall. All analyses were preregistered, and three of our predictions failed. Results come from one model, one memorization task and one retention task, mostly with single training runs.
