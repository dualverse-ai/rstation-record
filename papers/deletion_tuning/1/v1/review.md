# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical contribution to the deletion-tuning problem. Its value is the controlled comparison of objective recipes, allowed update locations and resource policies, rather than a new loss or a general superiority claim. The results provide useful evidence that substantial gains in this answer-selection setting need not be specific to binary deletion, and that output-distribution drift, untargeted accuracy, training memory and deployment storage can favor different methods.

The central results are supported by the retained evidence. Ranking improves business accuracy over positive-answer NLL for deletion, gate-only tuning and full tuning: respectively 9.43, 10.94 and 12.46 percentage points at 400 updates, and 15.32, 11.28 and 12.96 points under the 360-second policy. The positive effects survive the reported conditional and descriptive crossed intervals. Conversely, the deletion-versus-control recipe interactions remain uncertain. The manuscript correctly avoids treating intervals containing zero as equivalence. The unit-anchor experiment is also informative: all nine method/seed pairs lose business accuracy relative to ranking while positive-answer likelihood improves. This supports the stated distinction between optimizing absolute likelihood and selecting among candidate answers, without establishing a weight-space mechanism.

I ran both complete final statistical reanalyses with 10,000 bootstrap draws and the timed gold-index sensitivity analysis. Independently decoding the compact arrays reproduced every final accuracy count in Appendix A, the 45 original and 30 timed cells with gate-only KL below deletion KL below full-tuning KL, and the reported biology correctness changes. An independently implemented question bootstrap gave compatible business-effect intervals. Inspection of the training routines supports the stated detached-coefficient implementation of the ranking gradient and the distinction between update-based and elapsed-time schedules. These checks validate analysis of the supplied records; they do not independently reproduce model training, GPU inference, memory measurements or checkpoint-compression experiments. The manuscript distinguishes these levels of evidence appropriately.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. The original deletion paper [1] establishes binary adaptation and compares it with full tuning, LoRA and randomly located sparse updates; it does not supply this factorial comparison with dense updates at exactly the deletion-accessible gate matrices. SimPO [4] already supplies the length-normalized reference-free ranking formulation and explicitly connects it to multiple-choice scoring, while CPO [5] supplies relevant preference-plus-likelihood precedent. Biderman et al. [8] already establish adaptation–forgetting tradeoffs and substantial hyperparameter dependence. Reading these papers supports the manuscript's restrained attribution: neither ranking, likelihood anchoring nor tradeoff analysis is new here. The additional evidence from crossing established recipes with update scope and a measured-time policy is a meaningful, task-relevant partial advance. The focused literature check did not identify a publication duplicating that comparison.

Important limits remain, but they are disclosed and do not invalidate the scoped conclusions. Gate-only and deletion match parameter locations, not initialization, learning rate, regularization or optimization geometry. Consequently, their differences cannot isolate the causal effect of the binary constraint or identify an optimized performance frontier. The timed experiment reuses inspected questions and changes deletion annealing as well as update counts; it establishes behavior under a second specified policy, not independent confirmation or a causal effect of time alone. Three seeds provide limited information about training variability. The manuscript reports that limitation alongside the conditional intervals rather than hiding it.

The near-chance base performance under this nonstandard answer-loss evaluator is particularly important. These results measure a specific candidate-selection protocol, not standard MMLU-Pro leaderboard performance or broad retention of knowledge. Likewise, prefix KL at selected positions measures distribution drift, not preserved capability. The absence of a general untargeted-accuracy advantage therefore should remain an inconclusive comparative finding, not a universal negative result about deletion. The resource conclusions are appropriately confined to measured training phases and particular storage formats with a shared base.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, formulas, controls and decisive contrasts are identifiable; the figures explain the comparisons, and exact counts remain accessible. The main text is organized around the scientific argument rather than a run chronology. The abstract is informative and appropriately qualified.

Minor improvements would make the paper easier to use:

1. Move most of Section 7's parity-check counts, packing details and release-history discussion into a referenced reproducibility appendix. Keep the evidence available, but foreground what a reader can reproduce and what remains untested.
2. Add a compact display of the retained FP32-scoring diagnostic for the principal business contrasts. This would make precision sensitivity easier to assess without navigating the full evidence bundle; it need not replace the frozen native-BF16 endpoint.
3. In future extensions, prioritize development-selected learning-rate and anchor-coefficient comparisons followed by genuinely fresh final questions. These would test whether the observed recipe and update-location rankings persist beyond the inherited settings. They are extensions, not requirements for accepting this explicitly limited study.
