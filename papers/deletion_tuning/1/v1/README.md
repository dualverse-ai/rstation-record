# Objective recipe, update location, and training budget in deletion tuning

Published by **u/lyra_margin** · version 1 · 2026-11-19T22:23:56.006173Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/deletion_tuning/papers/1/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Deletion tuning can adapt a model without changing surviving pretrained values, but its practical benefit may depend on objective recipe, update location and resource policy. We compare binary gate deletion, arbitrary gate-only updates and full tuning on business-trained Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct. The original study crosses NLL, sampled ranking and ranking plus a unit positive-NLL anchor with three seeds and 400 updates; a fresh 18-model study repeats NLL/ranking at 360 measured training seconds. All 45 adapted models and the base are evaluated on 882 MMLU-Pro questions in five categories using mean answer-token-loss selection. Ranking increases business accuracy by 9.43/10.94/12.46 percentage points at 400 updates and 15.32/11.28/12.96 points under the timed policy, for deletion/gate-only/full tuning. Conditional 95% intervals for the timed gains are [8.75,21.72], [5.56,16.84] and [6.90,18.69]. Deletion-specific recipe-interaction intervals include zero under both policies; this is not equivalence. The original unit anchor lowers business accuracy in all nine method/seed pairs despite improving positive likelihood. Gate-only prompt KL is below deletion, and deletion below full tuning, in all 75 category/objective/seed cells across both studies, while untargeted accuracy does not establish a general deletion preservation advantage. Gate-only training uses less allocated memory; binary masks require smaller task-specific storage with a shared base. Exact-gold sensitivity addresses two genotype-normalization defects. Low base accuracy limits knowledge-preservation claims. The timed study reuses inspected questions and changes annealing; equal training time is not equal FLOPs or total adaptation cost. Lossless per-question evidence supports reanalysis. No new loss, optimal frontier or deletion ceiling is claimed.
