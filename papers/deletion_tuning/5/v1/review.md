# Accept

The manuscript makes a useful, sufficiently distinct empirical contribution: an apparent memorization failure of deletion tuning can disappear when the sharpness schedule changes, without increasing the number of optimizer updates. This directly addresses the task's question of distinguishing representational restrictions from optimization limitations. The contribution is a bounded result about one model and synthetic memorization, not a determination of deletion's ultimate capacity or a generally superior adaptation method.

The decisive evidence is Table 2. At $N=4096$, the retained records give $5/256$ correct generations with $T=A=8192$, versus $253/256$ with the same $T$ and $A=16384$. Extending training to $T=16384$ while retaining $A=8192$ gives only $12/256$, whereas extending both gives $255/256$. The fixed-update comparison isolates the schedule under the reported settings, and evaluation actually deploys binary masks. The analogous recovery at one layer and $N=1024$ strengthens the practical conclusion that an unsuccessful inherited schedule should not be mistaken for a binary representational ceiling. The larger one-layer gap is interesting precisely because the manuscript explicitly leaves optimization versus capacity unresolved.

The composition experiments add a relevant counterexample to the expectation that preserving weight values or earlier deletions automatically preserves an adaptation. Both independently trained masks recall their own banks, but their union fails; interference also occurs with disjoint layer supports. These observations support failure of the tested union and sequential-training procedures. They do not establish impossibility of modular deletion memories or contradict task-conditioned mask retrieval in SupSup [6].

The novelty is adequate. The published rStation studies [12]–[14] concern MMLU-Pro comparisons, objective and resource policies, or selected source retention. The additional published subspace study [17] concerns candidate-support and projection-allocation trade-offs. None supplies this synthetic memorization schedule intervention. External checks of Deletion Tuning [1], MoRA [7], and the LoRA-memory study [8] confirm relevant antecedents: annealed masks and proposed mask unions, random-association memorization probes, and adapter capacity/composition analysis. The manuscript credits these ideas; its new contribution is the deletion-specific empirical separation of schedule failure from apparent capacity failure, rather than invention of memorization probes or sigmoid annealing. Add [17] to the related-work discussion, and avoid describing the informal reports [15,16] as published comparisons.

The main argument is clearly communicated. Methods, principal comparisons, limitations, and references are identifiable, and the chronological research notebook is appropriately kept in the supplement. The abstract is informative. The following qualifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Keep sampled recall distinct from exhaustive storage claims.** Exact generation is evaluated on 256 facts, including when $N=8192$. Thus “full recall” means $256/256$ on the sampled queries, not verified recovery of all 8,192 associations. Report counts and uncertainty, and use “near-perfect sampled training-fact recall” where appropriate. For orientation, a binomial Wilson interval for $256/256$ is approximately $[0.985,1]$; this does not include variation over training seeds, independently generated datasets, or adaptive configuration selection. The large schedule effect is convincing within this experiment, but the few-point differences against gate-FFT do not establish superiority or statistical equivalence. The two training seeds also share the same generated facts.

2. **Separate the ternary observation from its mechanistic interpretation.** Inspection of `train_facts.py` shows that ternary training uses two logits per entry, symmetric positive/negative initialization without the Wanda prior, and no deletion kept-norm reward. Matching learning rate and sharpness schedule therefore does not isolate amplification as the cause of improvement. Approximately equal numbers of zeroed and doubled entries do not show that half of the useful computation requires amplification. Retain the measured improvement, but describe the statements in Sections 4.4 and 5 about indirect amplification “costing capacity” as hypotheses. A controlled attribution would require matching initialization and regularization and separating parameterization effects from the expanded deployed value set.

3. **Maintain the same scope in conclusions as in limitations.** The saturation explanation is plausible, and the manuscript commendably acknowledges that logit-gradient trajectories were not measured. Likewise, the disjoint-layer experiment establishes that coordinate overlap is unnecessary for interference; it does not establish that *any* upstream change breaks a downstream mask. The noise intervention at $N=1024$ under a successful recipe establishes robustness there, but cannot by itself rule out noise-related failure at $N=4096$ under the collapsing recipe. These are interpretive wording qualifications, not objections to the reported interventions.

4. **Make reproduction and baseline details easier to audit.** Replace hard-coded private workspace paths with documented arguments, identify the exact pilot-source revision, and explain how the supplied task code provides the imports omitted from the author supplement. Saving per-fact generations, losses, and checkpoint identifiers would permit independent rescoring without retraining. Document that the TIES implementation estimates a per-matrix trimming threshold from a strided sample, rather than computing an exact global top-20% threshold. Also describe the continual gate-FFT run as restarting from a reconstructed, rounded saved delta with a fresh optimizer, rather than implying uninterrupted training.

My verification comprised source inspection, comparison of the retained run arguments and numerical results with the central tables, regeneration of all five seed-0 fact sets with matching SHA-256 hashes, and byte-for-byte regeneration of both supplied banks. I did not reproduce GPU training or independently regenerate model predictions. Within that verification limit, the retained evidence supports the central empirical finding. Broader retention measurements, additional independent fact draws, and direct gradient telemetry would extend the work, but are not necessary to publish this scoped advance.
