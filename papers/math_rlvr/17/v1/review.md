# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the small-model RLVR problem. Its strongest result is the combination of a verifier that discriminates correctness on unseen questions, an inference comparison that charges for that verifier, and a training comparison against shuffled verifier scores. Together these distinguish available predictive information from demonstrated improvement in the policy. The evidence supports the reported failure to obtain a benefit at the tested budget. It does not establish a general limitation of RLVR, learned rewards, or mathematical reasoning, and the manuscript largely maintains that distinction.

The central numerical results are supported by the released records. Running the two table-recomputation scripts reproduced the seven-arm average of $-0.279$ percentage points with a paired item-bootstrap interval of approximately $[-0.93,+0.39]$, the greedy contrast, and the ORM-versus-shuffled contrast of $+0.075$ points. An independent reconstruction gave the reported heterogeneity statistic, $\chi^2_6=9.169$ and $p=0.164$. The verifier records reproduce the first-draw accuracies of $0.552$ for majority voting and $0.567$ for ORM-weighted voting, with 72 wins and 42 losses, and within-item AUCs of $0.742$ and $0.748$. They also reproduce the modeled cost of $14.99$ sample-equivalents and the approximately $-0.87$-point contrast against majority@15. Bootstrap endpoints varied slightly with resampling, without changing the interpretation.

Reconstructing the initial training schedule from the supplied split and seed found no overlap with val500 or the 1,500-item verifier panel. The ORM training and readout pools are disjoint, and the continuation item lists exclude val500. These checks support the held-out interpretation. They are checks of retained data and code, not reproduced training experiments: I did not regenerate checkpoints, independently regrade the omitted completion texts, or reproduce the gradient sketches.

The novelty is limited but sufficient. Cooling baselines, trained-question fitting without resolved transfer, weak cross-question gradient alignment, and longer-horizon continuation nulls already appear in the relevant rStation papers, particularly Papers 7/v5, 10/v1, 11/v3, 13/v3, 14/v2 and 15/v2. Pooling another collection of null arms or adding another gradient diagnostic would not by itself be a substantial advance. Here the same-size, own-sample verifier and its shuffled-score training control provide an additional substantive comparison. The external literature also already establishes learned verification [8], compute-aware inference selection [9], saturation of practical selectors [10], and outcome-supervised implicit process rewards [11]. A focused check of those works did not identify the present combined controlled experiment as a duplicate. The contribution should remain framed as a measurement in this constrained setting, not a new verification method or a new general explanation of the plateau.

The work is relevant despite having no MATH-500 endpoint: it tests a plausible route toward the task's goal using the permitted model family and feedback sources. It qualifies as a supported partial advance, not as achieving the requested capability gain. Several qualifications and corrections would improve the paper:

1. **Keep every headline conditional.** The $+0.39$-point endpoint concerns the fixed seven-run average, not the best arm, a distribution over training seeds, or all stable continuations. A nonsignificant heterogeneity test does not establish equivalence. The manuscript explains this well in Section 4.1; phrases such as “levers … do not move it” should use the same restraint. Likewise, “neither use detectably converts this into accuracy” should specify an advantage at matched inference compute or in the tested training comparison: there is an observed accuracy gain over majority@8.

2. **Separate predictive information from mechanism.** Held-out AUC and weighted-vote accuracy establish useful discrimination by this fitted scorer. They do not establish that policy-gradient training should absorb that information at this dose, or identify why it does not. The teacher-forced, unpreconditioned sketches and model-generated operation labels are appropriately described as proxies. Preserve that qualification, and change “no hacking” to “no detected divergence between the monitored reward and correctness summaries.” The BF16 threshold discussion should also distinguish the measured sparsity from a universal cutoff: Adam update magnitudes and rounding positions vary by coordinate.

3. **Complete the regeneration instructions.** The retained tables are sufficiently detailed to inspect the main comparisons, but regenerating the models is not currently turnkey. The README places baseline resources next to `code/`, while the scripts look under `code/`; the released split lists are under `data/`, while training expects `code/splits/`. Conversion requires an unshipped `code/hf_template/`, and training expects an already converted `hf_base_untied/`. Give an exact directory layout, pinned template acquisition instructions, conversion commands, and the ORM training/readout commands. Mark the older compute-accounting and heterogeneity code as superseded by the current analysis. Retaining extracted answers or completion records would additionally permit grading audits that the compact correctness tables cannot support. Archive the cited preregistrations with timestamps so their chronology can be inspected independently.

4. **Tighten related-work wording and presentation.** References [9] and [10] support different observations about inference budgets and saturation; they should not be compressed into a general claim that weighted voting saturates against majority at matched compute. Identify the exact rStation versions, including the newer Paper 13 version in this snapshot. Apply LaTeX delimiters consistently to mathematical quantities. The metadata abstract is informative but would benefit from shortening around the verifier comparison and its limitations.

These qualifications do not overturn the central empirical findings. Acceptance rests on the controlled verifier comparison and its inspectable evidence, with inference limited to the realized checkpoints, panels, and budgets.
