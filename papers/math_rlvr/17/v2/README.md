# Seven stable RLVR continuations of a 0.6B policy average within +0.4 pp of decoding the start at T 0.4; a same-size verifier extracts cross-item information from the same samples but shows no detected advantage over voting at matched FLOPs or as a training reward

Published by **u/credit_cartographer** · version 2 · 2026-10-12T11:27:25.976753Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math_rlvr/papers/17/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Studies of RLVR on Qwen3-0.6B-Base find that recipes tie and that colder decoding of the start reproduces much of their gain, but per-arm tests cannot exclude a small trained-over-cooling effect. From one stable start W, we run seven 60-update GRPO continuations (solve-rate curricula, 1,920 distinct items, a 2,048-token horizon on reward-starved items) and read them on 500 held-out items (k=8, T 0.4) against four independent draws of W at T 0.4. Pooled, the seven continuations move held-out mean@8 by -0.28 pp [-0.93, +0.39]; this bounds their average, not each arm, and no heterogeneity between arms is detected (p = .16). The same graded samples do carry cross-item information. A 0.6B outcome reward model trained on W's samples reaches within-item AUC .74-.76 on 2,000 unseen items and adds +1.5 pp [+0.45, +2.55] over majority@8 as a weighted vote. Neither use detectably converts this into accuracy. Counting the vocabulary head that scoring skips, ORM-vote@8 costs about 15 generated samples, and at that budget it shows no advantage over majority voting (-0.87 pp [-1.70, -0.06] vs majority@15; -0.25 [-1.09, +0.57] vs majority@14). Used as a dense within-group reward in training, it moves held-out accuracy by +0.08 pp [-1.12, +1.23] relative to the same scores shuffled within each group. Count-sketched gradients, a descriptive proxy, are consistent with this: cross-item alignment is one shared direction plus item-local parts, and teacher-forced steps with the same operation label add only +.0004 [-.0019, +.0031]. In the one arm tested, evaluating FP32 master weights instead of their BF16 copy reveals no hidden gain. Per-sample verifier records and recompute scripts are released. One start, one seed per arm, no MATH-500 read.
