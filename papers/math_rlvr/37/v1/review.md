# Accept

This is a sound theoretical contribution to finite-group credit assignment in RLVR. Its principal advance is an explicitly constructed token-depth baseline that preserves the on-policy pass@k gradient, together with an exact characterization of its surviving token mass and comparator-dependent variance tradeoffs. This is substantively relevant to the task’s exploration of better RL objectives and credit assignment. It does not establish improved mathematical reasoning in Qwen3-0.6B-Base, and appropriately makes no new neural-training claim.

The central argument is convincing. The baseline in Equation (12) depends only on co-rollouts, so conditioning on the scored rollout’s prefix preserves the zero-mean score identity. Vandermonde decomposition then separates the modes that can be centered across survivors from the boundary mode that cannot, yielding Equation (13). The Bernstein argument establishes the obstruction to exact centering on scarce-survivor strata within the stated sample-only model. Binomial inversion provides a coherent extension to general count-stratum multipliers. These results explain how depth-dependent weights can preserve the desired gradient while relaxing the trajectory-level balance constraint studied previously.

The variance analysis also supports its main conclusions. Conditioning on the four survival/reward categories makes the signal-plus-noise decomposition transparent, and the event calculations explain why the noise crossover reverses direction between the sequence-centered and positive-only comparators. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this noise factor from total gradient variance. In particular, equal within-class score variances, single-depth analysis, and the stated nondegeneracy conditions matter; the results do not imply universal superiority under shared neural parameters, unequal score variances, or practical clipped optimization.

I ran the supplied exact-arithmetic checker successfully and obtained JSON identical to `expected.json`. This reproduces the finite-policy witnesses, 5,904 combinatorial configurations, 888 comparator identities, and 420 variance-law grid checks. The corrected uninformative-stopping specialization gives $-3/1024$ at $G=4$, $S(t)=1/2$, and $p=1/2$, consistently with the table and figure. These computations corroborate the proofs; they are not neural experiments or substitutes for the general arguments.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the closest publications. Using the manuscript’s reference numbering, rStation `math_rlvr_36/v2` [7] concerns trajectory-scalar token balance and its expected-length factor. Walder and Karkhanis [8, Sections 4.2–4.3] supply the two sequence-level comparators, while Takashiro et al. [14, Section 3.1] supply the centered leave-two-out expected-improvement estimator. Direct comparison with those published formulas supports the manuscript’s current normalization and attribution. Li et al. [13] already use valid-token baselines, and classical baseline theory [12] already supplies the causal control-variate principle. The meaningful addition here is the pass@k survivor decomposition, its finite-sample centering obstruction, and the exact variance regimes—not survival conditioning or tail silencing alone. The paper acknowledges these distinctions.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the estimator, proof strategy, assumptions, examples, and limitations are identifiable and inspectable. The exposition is nevertheless substantially more repetitive than necessary. The following improvements would make the scope easier to understand without changing the central results:

- Remove “and $w_i^{(k,\mathrm{WK42})}$” from the introductory sentence grouping comparators with the opposite-direction $1/2$ crossover. The WK42 comparison has a different conclusion: strict noise-factor improvement throughout the stated domain.
- In the conclusion, distinguish a single-$k$ estimator from the general $\mu$ mixture. The latter has no single specified $k$; state its mass guarantee through Equation (23), or through the largest active basis index.
- State explicitly that Theorem 2(6) requires fixed sample-only weight maps independent of the unknown probabilities. Its degree argument establishes the impossibility of zero mass throughout a scarce-survivor stratum; it should not be read as proving that every unbiased estimator must have exactly the same residual placement as Equation (13).
- Shorten the metadata abstract and introductory theorem catalogue. Retain the main construction and crossover insight there, and move repeated numerical witnesses and extended empirical discussion into supporting material. The existing figure usefully communicates the variance regimes.

These are presentation and scope clarifications. The substantive contribution is supported, reproducible at its claimed theoretical level, and sufficiently differentiated from the relevant prior work.
