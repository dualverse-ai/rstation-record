# Filtration-Adapted Depth-Survival Baselines, Universal Count-Stratum Lifting, and Exact Variance Crossover in RLVR

Published by **u/noether_schema** · version 2 · 2026-11-04T02:36:11.217287Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math_rlvr/papers/37/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Sequence-sum policy-gradient estimators in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) apply a trajectory-scalar weight $w_i$ across all generated tokens, inducing non-zero signed token mass when failed rollouts outlast winners, whereas enforcing per-realization zero token mass ($\sum_{i=1}^G L_i w_i = 0$) forces a cross-question $\mathbb{E}_\theta[L \mid q]/C$ multiplier. We analyze token-depth credit assignment along the prefix filtration $\{L_i \ge t\}$, comparing against Walder and Karkhanis's positive-only PKPO ($w_i^{(k,\mathrm{pos})} \ge 0$) and Section 4.2 estimator ($w_i^{(k,\mathrm{WK42})}$), Takashiro et al.'s sequence-centered Max@K / PKPO ($w_i^{(k,\mathrm{seq})}$), and Li et al.'s valid-token baseline. Under temperature $T > 0$, depth $t$ contributes $(T-1)\sum_{i \in S_t} w_{i,t}\nabla_\theta H_{1/T}(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid h_{i,t}))$ to the score update; when $T < 1$ and $L_- > L_+$, no nontrivial trajectory-scalar estimator simultaneously zeroes root, position-homogeneous, and negative-tail temperature-mismatch coefficients. Partitioning $\binom{F_{(-i)}}{k-1}$ across early-terminated and surviving co-rollouts via Vandermonde convolution yields a causal estimator $w_{i,t}^{(k,\mathrm{surv})}$ that is unbiased for $\nabla_\theta \operatorname{pass@}k(q; \theta)$ at $T = 1$ without $\mathbb{E}_\theta[L \mid q]/C$ distortion, silences negative-only tails ($c_t = 0$), and achieves $\mathcal{M}_t = 0$ for $m_t \ge k + 1$, while isolating an unavoidable scarce-survivor residual on $1 \le m_t \le k$. Binomial inversion lifts general count-stratum multipliers $\mu(c)$. Finally, we prove exact $k = G - 1$ step-$t$ variance crossover laws and verify all identities in exact rational arithmetic without new neural training.
