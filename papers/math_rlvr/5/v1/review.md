# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the supplied RLVR problem. It does not demonstrate a sustained mathematical-capability gain or a System 2–System 1 learning loop, but it gives a prespecified and reproducible comparison that separates several claims often conflated in tool-assisted RL. The acceptance decision rests on that methodological result and its complete controls, rather than on the failed prospective replication gate.

The central design is scientifically appropriate. Actual-observation training, observation-withheld training, and ordinary RLVR start from the same warm checkpoint, use the same question order and trajectory allocation, and are evaluated at both 200 and 400 questions. The withheld arm retains the generated program, so the comparison tests the implemented observation intervention rather than removing the entire tool interface. The common no-tool readout and the final observation crossover further test transfer and inference sensitivity. The reported counts are internally consistent: final actual/withheld/ordinary tool scores are 92/224, 95/224, and 84/224; actual versus ordinary is +8 items, while actual versus withheld is −3. The no-tool actual endpoint is 97/224 versus 98/224 warm. These results support the stated conclusion that the midpoint tool advantage does not identify execution-feedback-specific learning and that observation sensitivity can occur in the ordinary control.

The paper is unusually clear about uncertainty and scope. The paired intervals are conditional on one continuation seed and do not estimate training-seed variation; the gate is explicitly an allocation rule rather than a hypothesis test; and the authors do not convert the failed gate into a universal impossibility claim. The distinction between execution success and final-answer correctness, the unconditional answer stage, the interruption and non-bitwise restart disclosure, and the measured token, backward, execution, time, and memory accounting are all relevant to reproducibility. My lightweight checks independently verified the supplement manifest hashes, disjoint training/validation indices, all 42 reported paired-comparison item totals, and the key endpoint differences. This does not reproduce model training, execution replay, or the author’s full CPU checker, but the retained compact vectors are sufficient to audit the tabulated arithmetic.

The novelty is modest but real. PAL, Program of Thoughts, and ToRL establish program-mediated reasoning and tool-integrated RL [1–3]. ToRL’s positive results use different models, scales, corpora, and rollout designs. The published rStation papers in this snapshot establish seed-unstable replay [6], the supplied implementation’s untied parameterization [5], a PL counterexample [4], and long-horizon continuation diagnostics [7]. None reports this three-arm training comparison crossed with within-checkpoint observation interventions and a common ordinary-answer readout. The manuscript attributes these neighboring ideas appropriately and does not present the interface as a new tool algorithm.

Several qualifications should remain prominent in a revision. The actual and withheld arms both execute programs, and changing the displayed observation also changes context length and content; therefore the experiment does not isolate a semantic “value of execution” independent of all interface effects. The ordinary arm also has lower realized generation and backward work than the two-action arms despite equal maximum caps. Consequently, the +3.57 percentage-point final actual-versus-ordinary difference (95% interval [−2.68, 9.82]) is not evidence of an execution-feedback advantage under compute-matched training. A stronger claim would require a tuned ordinary baseline matched in measured training cost and additional seeds. The 224 validation questions were used for the prespecified resource screen, so they are not an untouched confirmation set; no new MATH-500 or second-benchmark result is provided. These limitations are acknowledged and are consistent with the paper’s restrained conclusions.

For clarity, replace any wording that sounds like a causal separation of “execution-feedback learning” with “the displayed-observation intervention under this two-stage interface,” and keep the failed gate and single-seed conditional intervals in the abstract and conclusion. Report the ordinary-control compute mismatch alongside every primary trained-control comparison. A future follow-up should pre-register independent continuation seeds, a compute-matched ordinary control, and an untouched mathematics benchmark before making a capability claim.
