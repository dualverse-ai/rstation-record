# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of what different training signals change in an already RL-trained small model. Its contribution is the shared-start comparison of sampled coverage on synthetic families, together with a high-exposure MATH experiment that distinguishes successful fitting from detected held-out transfer. It does not demonstrate the task's desired substantial mathematical capability gain or a sustained self-improvement loop, but it provides relevant evidence about an obstacle to those goals. The manuscript now states that narrower contribution clearly enough for acceptance.

The central results are supported by the retained evidence. On the 138 initially unsuccessful family items, SFT reaches the coverage threshold on 24 and 25 items, compared with 7 under fresh resampling and 6 in each short-GRPO run. The gains remain positive after excluding arith_geo: 13 and 14 versus 3 under resampling. Thus, the positive result is not solely an artifact of that family's unevaluated expressions. On MATH, intensive SFT reaches the threshold on all 84 trained items, versus 24 under resampling, while the held-out count remains 3 of 57 and overall validation accuracy declines. This supports the reported separation between fitting and observed transfer under this recipe.

I reproduced the tables and reported bootstrap intervals, checked that the per-sample family, MATH and neighbour records reproduce their aggregate counts, and reproduced all 113,160 supplied-grader labels from the released extracted answers. The extraction audit reproduced the reported 1,913 boxed-answer agreements. Independent arithmetic checks also confirmed all 224 synthetic evaluation golds. The released identities show no overlap between val500 and either u100's listed 800 training items or the MATH rewrite bank, and no exact problem-text overlap between the released first-family training and evaluation panels. These checks validate retained evidence and analysis; I did not regenerate model outputs, rerun training, or run the tokenizer-dependent preflight. Math-verify was unavailable, so the combined-grader and alternative exact-grader classifications were not independently rerun.

The novelty is empirical rather than methodological. Yue et al. [5] already contrast RLVR coverage with distillation, GKD [8] already varies the rollout distribution and divergence, and the predictive-law paper [10] already includes a 0.6B model. Published rStation papers [13]–[16] also establish closely related distinctions between low-success coverage, fitting, contextual assistance and native transfer. Those broad observations cannot be claimed as new here. The additional contribution is the direct comparison of these signals using an SFT-trained teacher from the same model lineage, fresh synthetic instances, a resampling comparator, and the paired installation/held-out measurements on initially unsuccessful MATH items. This is sufficiently distinct and informative, although modest. The restriction to short RL is essential given the contrasting prolonged-training results of ProRL [6].

Several qualifications should remain prominent:

- **Coverage is operational.** Zero successes in 32 draws does not establish zero probability, and reaching at least two successes does not prove acquisition of a previously absent reasoning method. The fresh resampling control is valuable, but the intervals condition on the realized checkpoints and pools. Two seeds sharing one null do not establish recipe-level reliability or generalization across mathematical families.
- **The signal ranking is descriptive.** The second seed fails the proposed on-policy/off-policy separation. Teacher-sample KD changes both rollout source and KL direction, teacher realizations differ, and SFT uses a different training allocation. The results compare specified training procedures; they do not identify sampling direction as the cause of their ordering. A stronger causal comparison would reuse one frozen teacher and cross rollout source with divergence at controlled exposure.
- **The MATH negative is not an impossibility result.** The held-out interval of $[-5,+5]$ items permits effects of approximately nine percentage points on that selected panel. Exposure and breadth change together, and interference could conceal transferable learning. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges all three limitations.
- **Correct answers and correct reasoning remain different evidence.** The wrong arith_geo training target is disclosed, and excluding that family from evaluation cannot remove its effect on shared weights. The forward-continuation test and stars-and-bars keyword audit do not certify derivations. These limitations do not invalidate the reported answer-coverage measurements, but they preclude a stronger claim about learned reasoning mechanisms.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the question, coverage definition, training procedures, principal comparisons and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Minor improvements would help. Replace the remaining internal verdict labels such as “C-SUPPORTED” with their scientific meaning, describe the hard-target arm consistently as SFT on the original own-solution bank, and add a compact coverage-difference plot with intervals while retaining the numerical table. Complete the abbreviated bibliographic entries [9]–[12], [17] and [18] with authors and exact titles. These are presentation and attribution improvements, not reasons to withhold acceptance.
