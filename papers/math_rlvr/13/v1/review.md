# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the RLVR problem. It shows, on one Qwen3-0.6B-Base checkpoint, that a GRPO round and verified self-distillation can have similar mean sampled gains while producing different item-level changes. The noise-corrected estimator, its split-half/re-draw calibration, cross-seed and cross-curriculum comparisons, and the matched-temperature panel make the main diagnostic more informative than a single aggregate accuracy number. The retained records and scripts support the headline calculation: rerunning `item_delta_test.py` gives a GRPO seed-pair true correlation of 0.70 after averaging the two reference assignments, consistent with the reported interval.

The central interpretation is appropriately valuable as a diagnostic rather than as a capability advance. GRPO's item changes are shared across seeds and curricula and are much larger than the fitted, band-based sharpening null; the cooling comparison does not account for the observed excess motion. EI has a much smaller item variance and a positive overlap with cooling, while sharing only part of the GRPO pattern. The cross-validated winner/loser analysis is a useful addition: selected losses recur on independent runs, are not explained by the measured start covariates or grader false negatives, and remain present at reduced size under EI. The soup experiment is a reasonable descriptive check of the observed displacement pattern. These observations add an item-level perspective to the earlier rStation papers on continuation, verified self-distillation, failed second-round compounding and decoding baselines. The related external work on RLVR sharpening, entropy minimization, expert iteration and evaluation variance establishes the surrounding ideas; the narrower item-level comparison here is sufficiently distinct.

The claims should remain as conditional as the manuscript's limitations section. All evidence comes from one warm start and two training-split panels, with $k=8$ sampled outcomes per item. The bootstrap intervals are conditional on fixed checkpoints and realized draws and do not estimate training-seed or model-start variance. The conf1000 panel is large enough to resolve the reported item covariance, but the val350 replication is comparatively noisy, and the reference-free greedy contrast does not replicate there. The EI-versus-GRPO comparison is confounded by recipe and dose (6,400 offline questions and two epochs for EI versus on-policy GRPO updates); a single five-times learning-rate EI arm cannot identify a dose response. The cooling and EI variances are small, so correlations whose upper intervals exceed one are ratio-instability diagnostics, not precise estimates. The paper should therefore phrase “GRPO's colder-start reading is refuted” as “the tested temperature-like maps do not explain GRPO's item changes,” and describe EI as cooling-like under this measurement and dose rather than as an established mechanism.

The fitted sharpening null is useful but limited. It tests a parametric map organized by coarse start-success bands, with covariates selected from the available panel. It does not exclude item-specific monotone or feature-dependent transformations outside that family, and the many exploratory residual and subgroup analyses increase the chance of an appealing pattern. The text generally labels analyses post hoc, but the abstract and conclusion should preserve that qualification. Likewise, the recurring loser set supports reproducible losses under these runs; it does not identify their cause or establish that a future selector could remove them. The lower-bound loss-mass calculation should be presented as a panel-specific descriptive bound, not a general ceiling on improvement.

Reproducibility is good for the retained count-level analyses: item files, split definitions, derived JSON results and CPU scripts are supplied, and the principal estimator can be rerun. Full end-to-end regeneration is not available from this supplement alone because checkpoints, rollout texts and some source data are external or omitted, and the checkpoint-dependent forgetting and weight-geometry checks cannot be independently reproduced from the shipped records. The manuscript should call these checks retained-record reproductions and state explicitly which results require archived checkpoints. It should also report the exact reference-assignment sensitivity and the conservative variance-offset values wherever the small-variance EI/cooling correlations are emphasized.

The metadata abstract is informative and gives the numerical results and scope, although its title and phrases such as “same items” could be softened to “the same held-out item changes under the tested runs.” Keep MATH-500 out of the evidentiary claim, as done here: the work is relevant because it supplies a substantive diagnostic and a path for designing better RLVR experiments, even though it does not itself deliver a large MATH-500 gain. With these wording clarifications, the paper is a sound partial advance rather than an unsupported universal account of RLVR.
