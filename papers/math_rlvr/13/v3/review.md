# Accept

The update adds a useful, bounded extension to the accepted item-level study. The two-seed F3k experiment tests a concrete follow-up to the fitted-set comparison in [3], while retaining the accepted contribution. It differs substantively from the reselected second round in [4]: F3k restarts from the common warm checkpoint and expands the fitted set. The new evidence supports reporting an unresolved incremental sampled gain, substantial overlap with F1k's item changes, and persistent relative underperformance on previously selected losers. It does not establish a general coverage limit or an improvement on MATH-500.

CPU reproduction from the retained records recovered the primary difference of $+0.55$ percentage points, the item-only interval $[-0.36,1.44]$, the adjusted interval $[-0.82,1.92]$, and the frozen rule's FLAT verdict. I also reproduced the pooled variance term, the 3,240-question set and training order, the reported fifth-exposure rewards, the cross-arm correlation $0.7001$ with interval $[0.5939,0.8124]$, and the new loser analyses. These are retained-record reproductions, not independent training or generation experiments. The similarity analyses were inspected in code but not rerun. The readout and builder hashes match those recorded in the supplied notebook; that agreement does not independently authenticate the notebook's chronology.

The following qualifications should govern the new section and its headline wording:

- **FLAT is a decision-rule label, not an equivalence finding.** The interval permits an improvement approaching two percentage points, including much of the approximately $1$–$2$ point margin discussed in §9. Replace “does not add,” “their joint increase did not help,” and “rules out the three cheap routes” with “did not demonstrate an additional gain under the tested intervention.” The reported upper bound already makes this narrower interpretation available. The likelihood diagnostic in §6.2 also does not directly test or exclude a rehearsal intervention.
- **Preserve the distinction between relative and absolute losses.** F3k's selected-loser band residual is $-0.689$ counts, with interval $[-1.087,-0.303]$, but its raw change is $-0.230$ with interval $[-0.638,0.171]$. The binned negative-mass interval also includes zero. Thus “the losers stay” should consistently mean that previously selected losers remain below their band expectation. The correlation near $0.70$ establishes substantial shared structure, not an unchanged item pattern or equivalence of the two interventions.
- **Describe the uncertainty adjustment as approximate.** The $0.278\,\mathrm{pp}^2$ term pools sparse replicates across F1k, Lmt and EI, with four nominal degrees of freedom. Treating it as known in a normal interval neither propagates its estimation uncertainty nor establishes that F3k has the same training variance. This is a useful sensitivity adjustment, not a fully calibrated estimate of uncertainty across training seeds. Update §11's blanket statement about item-only intervals to acknowledge this exception and its assumptions.

Two reproducibility clarifications would improve the extension. First, the new trainer unconditionally imports `trunc_w`, but that module is absent from the supplement. Supply the exact runnable trainer used for these runs and its dependencies; the successful count-level reproductions do not establish that training can currently be regenerated. Second, disclose that F3k uses independently shuffled epochs whereas F1k cycles its fixed order. Along with coverage and update count, ordering changes, so attribution to coverage alone remains unavailable. Fifth-exposure online reward supports comparable observed fitting, but “fit is not the limit” should not imply optimization saturation or a common final-checkpoint training-set evaluation.

These are scope and reproducibility clarifications to a supported empirical extension. The reported numbers, explicit uncertainty, untriggered update-matched control, and disclosed prediction misses provide a sound basis for acceptance under the bounded interpretation above.
