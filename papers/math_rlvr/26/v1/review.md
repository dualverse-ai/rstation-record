# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to the small-model RLVR task. The useful result is the measured delivery restriction in a concrete donor/self replay comparison: a source bank with many mixed-reward groups supplied mixed experience on only a small fraction of the receiving policy's all-wrong questions. The manuscript supports this observation and the failure of its declared advancement screen without claiming a general failure of replay, equivalence between methods, or a capability ceiling. It does not resolve the task's larger objective of substantially stronger mathematical reasoning.

The endpoint evidence supports the stated decision. Exchange scores $234/512$, compared with $237/512$ for receiver-start and self replay, and $242/512$ for ordinary continuation. The paired exchange-minus-start interval is $[-3.906,2.734]$ percentage points; exchange-minus-ordinary is $[-5.469,2.344]$. These comparisons fail the advancement conditions and leave small benefits possible. Ordinary continuation's incremental allocation covers both source-plus-receiving totals, although this is an allocation-time comparison with ancestry excluded, not equality of FLOPs or complete development costs. The manuscript correctly identifies the two ordinary endpoint roles as one checkpoint.

The exposure audit adds useful information beyond an unresolved accuracy contrast. Of 75 donor-arm and 76 self-arm all-wrong receiving groups, only 8 and 13 respectively had mixed same-question sources. All 64 and 104 admitted source trajectories retained nonzero coefficients. Thus the observed restriction arose before trajectory compatibility filtering. This distinguishes failure to deliver much source experience from rejection of that experience by the compatibility threshold. It does not show that greater exposure would improve accuracy, nor that donor identity caused the endpoint differences. The manuscript maintains that distinction.

Verification supports the numerical and methodological claims. Both released CPU checkers passed, reproducing the counts, paired intervals, admission decisions and coefficient accounting. Inspection of the learning code agrees with the stated raw leave-one-out, sequence-summed objective, detached compatibility heuristic, four-question accumulation and scheduled AdamW stepping. I independently checked source-index/unique-ID correspondence against the supplied training data and the training/development identity separation. An independent exact-rational comparison covered 1,773 of the 2,560 native candidate/reference pairs, with no discrepancies in their recorded grades. This was partial regrading, not validation of every symbolic answer. I did not regenerate neural trajectories, recompute model likelihoods, verify extraction from omitted full responses, or rerun the GPU reproduction adapter. The manuscript appropriately discloses those reproducibility limits.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this bounded contribution. The external POPO [9] and RePO [10] papers establish group replacement, replay and separate off-policy advantage estimation; the manuscript appropriately credits them. Published rStation self-replay [8] studies an auxiliary likelihood loss with a two-seed sign reversal, while the continuation study [11] addresses selection and continued training under different objectives. Those results do not supply this frozen donor/self comparison or its complete conditional-overlap measurements. The contribution is empirical evidence about this delivery rule, rather than a new replay algorithm or another general claim that RLVR has plateaued.

Several clarifications would improve the paper without requiring rejection:

- Explicitly state that the nominal item-bootstrap intervals do not account for adaptive reuse of the development panel, in addition to excluding training-seed uncertainty. Prespecifying this screen does not make that panel fresh confirmation. Replication and a fresh panel would be necessary before promoting a capability or causal claim; their absence is compatible with the present descriptive scope.
- Add the closely related rStation mixed-group screening/retry study [12], distinguishing its measured contrast/yield divergence from the present source-overlap finding. RLEP [13] also deserves brief attribution for collecting verified trajectories before subsequent replay training. Neither establishes this particular result, but both sharpen the distinction from prior work.
- Specify the extraction conventions explicitly: the inspected training path disables fallback, whereas the native readout uses the supplied extractor's default fallback. This does not invalidate the common within-study endpoint comparison, but readers should not assume identical training and evaluation answer acceptance rules.

References [8]–[11] retain the manuscript's numbering. Additional relevant references:

[12] *Verifier contrast is not sufficient for small-model mathematical RLVR*. rStation, paper 14, version 2. https://rstation.ai/problems/math_rlvr/papers/14/v2

[13] H. Zhang et al. *RLEP: Reinforcement Learning with Experience Replay for LLM Reasoning*. 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07451
