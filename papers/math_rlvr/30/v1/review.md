# Accept

This is a careful and useful study of a practically important failure mode in retrofitting recurrent depth onto a pretrained small transformer. The central conclusions are supported by an appropriate progression of controls: naive recurrence catastrophically perturbs the pretrained Coda-entry state; a small LayerScale-style residual gate keeps the perturbation near the pretrained manifold; and the apparent advantage of the gated model over a 50-step single-depth baseline disappears against a compute-matched 65-step single-depth control. The held-out evaluation is separated from the `Val210` selection panel, the main comparisons use three seeds, and the reported paired intervals correctly make clear that the small residual gains are uncertain.

The work is novel in its combination of a production-path BF16/cache audit, an on-policy gradient decomposition, frozen-gate and bypass controls, and a deployed truncation-triggered routing comparison. It distinguishes these contributions from recurrent-depth pretraining, relaxed recursive transformers, LayerScale/ReZero, and the same-problem studies on inserted-state recovery and compute matching. The negative result is scientifically meaningful: it narrows the circumstances in which recurrence should be credited with a capability gain, while identifying deterministic `EscFB` routing as a distinct operational property rather than a superior accuracy method.

The empirical record is unusually inspectable. The split manifests, training logs, compact per-item candidate records, production diagnostic JSON, and standalone regrading/checking code make the stated counts and answer-grading procedure reproducible in principle. The supplementary implementation exposes the exact recurrence, gate, cache-slot, bypass, GRPO, and fallback-routing definitions. The paper also reports both long-horizon and compact-prefix results, external GSM8K/AMC items, MATH-500, generated-token counts, wall time, and inference layer-token proxies.

Several qualifications should be retained or sharpened in revision:

- The gradient decomposition is one on-policy step from Seed 42. It establishes the reported decomposition for that step, but the language “proves” or “refutes” a mechanism should be narrowed to an observed local audit unless the authors add steps or seeds.
- The 65-step comparison is close in measured wall time and generated-token-adjusted work, but the headline `1,820` versus `1,800` “layer-steps” omits token-dependent training work. Report the corresponding training layer-token/FLOP totals directly and describe the control as approximate compute matching. The extra 15 training questions are also a dose change that should be acknowledged alongside the compute match.
- The three-seed $t$ intervals have $df=2$ and are necessarily weakly powered. The manuscript generally handles this correctly; it should avoid interpreting the compact `Fast@512` differences as established superiority. In particular, the $R_{\text{eval}}=1$ GRD versus SD-65 long-horizon difference is compatible with zero.
- “Zero regressions” for `EscFB` is an observed property of these finite panels and seeds, not a guarantee. The paper mostly states this limitation; keep that qualification adjacent to the headline result and emphasize the measured batch-padding sensitivity between online and offline execution.

With those wording and accounting clarifications, the paper meets the task’s requirements for a substantive partial advance, clear attribution, relevant baselines, limitations, and reproducible evidence. Its strongest claim is a bounded mechanistic and empirical diagnosis of recurrent-depth retrofits, not a general improvement over ordinary RLVR.
