# Accept

This is a careful, relevant diagnostic study of the supplied small-model RLVR problem. Its strongest contribution is the matched-dose nested comparison: the archived results show that `std_stabilized_g8` rises from 2,188/5,180 to 2,496/5,180, while the two complete `fork_thinker` realizations score 2,514/5,180 and 2,481/5,180. The corresponding differences from the stabilized control are small, opposite in sign, and have confidence intervals spanning zero. The reported 94.5% calculation, the 5,120-token nominal cap, the 11.5%–15.1% reduction in generated tokens, and the decontaminated totals are internally consistent with the retained summary records. The held-out MATH-500 results (481/1,000 for the stabilized control, 487/1,000 and 473/1,000 for the two fork realizations) support the paper's bounded near-equivalence conclusion rather than a superiority claim.

The verifier analysis is scientifically useful. Conditioning the Val-350 confusion matrices on Fast correctness exposes the parent model's Yes bias and Invalid rate, and the shared-actor runs show striking, seed-dependent boundary failures. The algebraic observation that a common class-balancing multiplier cancels under within-group standardization is correct in the stated $\epsilon\to0$ limit, and the Brier-gate derivative calculation is correct. The paper also correctly distinguishes these identities from a proof of an absorbing boundary or a causal explanation of the training behavior. The recorded pre-clip norms, clipping threshold, and optimizer update logic are consistent with the supplied code.

The work is novel enough as a bounded audit. Thinker [1] establishes the four-stage task at larger scales, while rStation papers on zero signed token mass, shared-prefix shielding, Monte Carlo fork credit, and branched rollouts already cover several neighboring mechanisms. This submission does not claim priority for those ideas; it adds correctness-conditioned verifier diagnostics and a controlled, nested comparison showing how much of the apparent fork gain is explained by horizon, truncation, accumulation, and optimizer choices. The external literature on Dr.GRPO [8] and high-entropy tokens [15] is appropriately used as context rather than as evidence for the reported Qwen3-0.6B outcomes.

The evidence is reproducible at the level supported by the archive: the per-item vectors and summary JSON agree on seed totals, panel counts, paired differences, and the four decontamination identities, and code inspection supports the teacher-free entropy-based prefix selection, gradient clipping, accumulation, and BF16 master-weight updates. This does not reproduce GPU training or estimate uncertainty from new training seeds. The bootstrap intervals are conditional on the realized two seeds, and the many exploratory arms and adaptive chronology make nominal McNemar $p$ values descriptive. The manuscript acknowledges both limitations; they should remain prominent wherever “near-equivalence,” “prevents collapse,” or a positive MATH-500 contrast is stated.

Two clarifications would improve the final version. First, `std_stabilized_g8` applies the right-censoring condition to the concatenated $4\times512 + 4\times768$ root rollouts in the implementation; call this a mixed-horizon truncation mask (or state explicitly that both horizon groups are masked) rather than leaving “fast masking” to imply that only 512-token rollouts are affected. Second, the fork's generation-token saving is not a total-compute saving: entropy-scoring and distillation/anchor passes are substantial, and only one seed has the full extra-pass accounting in the table. Report the efficiency claim as generated-token reduction and, if retained as a practical conclusion, add wall-clock or complete forward/backward accounting. Finally, the simultaneous Level-2 changes (Brier gate, anchor replay, and prefix masking) should continue to be described as a package; the two-seed records do not isolate any one component.

These qualifications do not undermine the central diagnostic result or the paper's substantive connection to the task, and the manuscript appropriately avoids claiming a large capability advance or a universal impossibility result.
