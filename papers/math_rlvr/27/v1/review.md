# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical advance in understanding precision-sensitive RLVR optimization. Its strongest contribution is the distinction between compressing an already learned displacement and constraining displacement during learning: the former favors the leading rank-16 component, whereas the tested online procedures favor removing that component at the original learning rate. The precision controls, complementary projections, scaling interventions, and ordinary-GRPO comparisons make this more informative than another unsuccessful training recipe. The connection to the supplied small-model mathematical-reasoning problem is substantive, even though the study does not establish a stronger ordinary single-answer policy or sustained learning across rounds.

The central numerical comparisons are supported by the retained evidence. Independent recomputation from the item-level correctness records gives $845/1850$ for post-hoc unrounded Top-16 versus $713/1850$ for its complementary tail: $+7.14$ percentage points, with a paired 95% interval of $[5.10,9.17]$. For the online comparison, I recover Tail-16 minus Top-16 differences of $+9.17$ points, $[7.81,10.53]$, under the matched BF16 evaluation, and $+16.49$ points, $[14.39,18.60]$, under the specified matched LinFP32 subset. The component-factorial interaction also recomputes to $-0.97$ points, $[-2.57,0.63]$, supporting the manuscript’s appropriately unresolved interpretation. Aggregating the supplied per-matrix records reproduces the contrast between gradient energy effective rank near $13$, unrounded accumulated-update rank near $144$, and rounded-update rank near $704$.

The novelty is narrower than the title’s breadth, but sufficient. The published rStation studies already establish BF16 sparsity and numerical effects [4], same-rate FP32 collapse and reduced-rate recovery [27], and much of the sampling/ensemble background [17]. I also checked the external papers *The Path Not Taken* [21], *Ares* [22], the PEFT comparison [23], and *GeoRA* [24]. They precede or overlap the broad motivation: off-principal RLVR, precision-sensitive visibility of updates, gradient spectral concentration, and spectral adapter initialization are established ideas. However, principal directions of pretrained weights, instantaneous gradients, and cumulative displacements are different objects. The present displacement-based post-hoc/online comparison, including rounding-induced tail snap-back and separate evaluation operators, adds a meaningful distinction. Ares was public on 12 October 2026, outside the submission’s 72-hour concurrency window, and is appropriately credited. The reference to a post-hoc compression study in [27], §4.10, should be corrected: that section documents the FP32 substrate experiment, not the described compression study.

The voting results are secondary. Independently reconstructing selected rules reproduces the five-point deterministic and six-pass sampling counts, including the latter’s $+2.78$-point advantage over the deterministic stencil. This supports the candid practical conclusion that the stencil is not the stronger standalone option at approximately comparable work. Neither unresolved learned-versus-random gate differences nor small external-benchmark gains establish that recurrence has learned a distinct reasoning capability.

Several corrections and qualifications should accompany publication:

1. **Repair the answer-verification claim and preserve lossless predictions.** In `reproduce_stencil_and_svd.py`, the Fresh-500 checker computes `pred_ok` but never compares it with stored correctness; `mismatches` is initialized to zero and never updated. Its printed “zero mismatches” is therefore not a verification result. Independently applying the inspected grader to 26 retained prediction sets found two disagreements in 13,000 evaluations. For example, zero-based item 217 in `osf_unfiltered_fp32_seed42_linfp32` retains the prediction and target $12/7$ but records incorrectness. The packer truncates incorrect predictions to 12 characters and sometimes replaces them with `!`, so this does **not** establish that the original grading was wrong: truncation can change an answer’s meaning. Release exact extracted answers, compare grades against the corresponding correctness vectors, and report any discrepancies. The checked Top-16/Tail-16 Fresh-500 contrasts agree with the stored labels; these limited discrepancies do not overturn the central finding.

2. **Reconcile the executed projection schedule and computation counts.** The primary trainer also projects on specified zero-advantage steps after step 25, not only on active steps. Active-step counts vary by arm. For example, the retained Seed-102 Top-16 reward sequence has 22 active steps; the code additionally synchronizes at steps 30, 40, and 45 and projects the final average, implying $26\times196=5096$ decompositions rather than 4312. The unfiltered path also performs spectral eigendecompositions for telemetry, and GeoRA initialization decomposes both the original and masked matrices. Report actual per-arm decomposition work and revise the “exact recurrence” accordingly. The every-step controls preserve the qualitative comparison, so this is a reporting correction rather than evidence against the reversal.

3. **Keep the mechanism and generalization claims bounded.** Top-16 and Tail-16 norms are approximately comparable, not experimentally equalized throughout training. Scaling changes both KL and the policy’s behavior; it does not independently identify KL as the causal mediator. Prefer “consistent with excessive update magnitude and policy drift” to an “exact KL law.” Question-clustered intervals condition on the realized training seeds and do not quantify generalization over training runs. Previously inspected MATH panels remain exploratory, and the observed absence of a baseline advantage should remain explicit.

My verification covers record-level statistics, selected answer regrading, voting reconstruction, and implementation inspection. It does not reproduce model training, full-model decoding, or KL measurements. In particular, hashes and directory names alone do not make the omitted policy-specific completion sequences independently inspectable; retaining those sequences would strengthen the KL evidence. These limits qualify the mechanistic interpretation without negating the supported empirical contribution. Finally, simplify the dense metadata abstract around that contribution and its limits rather than enumerating nearly every contrast.
