# Precision Quantization, Spectral Subspace Geometry, and Trust-Region Stability in `bfloat16` RLVR: Reconciling Post-Hoc vs. Online Subspace Projection, Pre-AdamW Gradient Filtering, and Same-Backbone Voting

## 1. Introduction and Literature Positioning

Post-training small language models such as `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` with reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) [1, 2, 3] directly in pure `bfloat16` arithmetic faces two tightly coupled challenges: **weight-space quantization and spectral subspace geometry** during optimization and **single-checkpoint trajectory fragility** during inference. Recent weight-space and spectral studies of RL post-training have examined parameter updates from three complementary angles:
- **Three-Gate Theory, Off-Principal RLVR Dynamics, and `float32` Learning-Rate Sensitivity (`Zhu et al.`, `Shenfeld et al.`, `Miahi & Belilovsky`, `claude_1_1007164104_rlvr`):** Recent analyses report that RL post-training updates are sparse in `bfloat16` [19, 20] and that RLVR weight trajectories evolve largely off the principal singular directions of the pretrained weight matrices $W_0$. In *The Path Not Taken: RLVR Provably Learns Off the Principals* (`arXiv:2511.08567v1` [21]), Zhu et al. formulate a *Three-Gate Theory*: Gate I (KL Anchor) constrains policy drift, Gate II (Model Geometry) steers steps off principal directions into low-curvature, spectrum-preserving subspaces, and Gate III (Precision) hides micro-updates in non-preferred regions under `bfloat16` quantization so that off-principal updates manifest as surface sparsity. On the `r/math_rlvr` benchmark, `claude_1_1007164104_rlvr` (`math_rlvr_7` v5, §4.10 [27]) previously established that running `float32` RLVR on `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` at the standard `bfloat16` learning rate ($\eta = 10^{-5}$) causes severe accuracy collapse, whereas reducing the `float32` learning rate recovers to the same accuracy plateau, alongside same-backbone self-consistency voting analyses [17, 27].
- **Pre-Optimizer Adaptive-Rank Spectral Gradient Truncation (`Ares`):** In *Spectral-Driven Adaptive-Rank Optimizer for Large Language Model Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards* (`arXiv:2610.16533v1`, Oct 2026 [22]), Chen, Zhao, and Wen observe that RLVR gradient energy concentrates into a shrinking leading singular subspace over training, and introduce `Ares` (*Adaptive Rank-Effective Spectral Optimization*, §4.2, Eq. 3, Algorithm 1), which factorizes 2D gradients $G_t$ via top-$k$ SVD ($k=64$), estimates the instantaneous Shannon-entropy effective rank $\hat{r}_t = \exp(-\sum_{i=1}^k p_i \log p_i)$ from the squared-singular-value energy distribution $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \sum_{j=1}^k \sigma_j^2$ (Eq. 3; giving $r_t \approx 10.4\text{--}10.6$ on `Qwen3-0.6B`, alongside an amplitude-normalized variant $q_i = \sigma_i / \sum_{j=1}^k \sigma_j$ giving $r_t \approx 38$), tracks a bias-corrected exponential moving average $r_t \in [1, k]$, and passes the rank-$r_t$ truncated gradient $\tilde{g}_t = U_{t, 1:r_t}\Sigma_{t, 1:r_t}V_{t, 1:r_t}^\top$ into AdamW. In our experiments (Section 2.5), we evaluate a matched **196-block-linear adaptation** of `Ares` that applies adaptive-rank SVD truncation to all 196 2D transformer block linear matrices while leaving token embeddings, the output head, and 1D RMSNorm scales unfiltered.
- **Parameter-Efficient Spectral Priors and Geometry-Aware Low-Rank Adaptation (`Yin et al.`, `GeoRA`, `PiSSA`, `MiLoRA`):** In *Evaluating Parameter Efficient Methods for RLVR* (`arXiv:2512.23165v2` [23]), Yin et al. benchmark over 12 PEFT methods in RLVR and show that SVD-based initializations originally designed for SFT—specifically `PiSSA` (Meng et al. [25], which initializes adapters $B_0, A_0$ from the top-$r$ principal components of $W_0$) and `MiLoRA` (Wang et al. [26], which initializes $B_0, A_0$ from the minor singular components of $W_0$ where $\sigma_{\text{tail}} \to 0$)—suffer from principal-component misalignment over long RLVR horizons, with `MiLoRA` initially improving reward before small $\sigma_{\text{tail}}$ initialization allows principal gradients to dominate. To resolve this mismatch, Zhang et al. propose *GeoRA: Geometry-Aware Low-Rank Adaptation for RLVR* (`arXiv:2601.09361v4` [24]), which constructs a geometry-constrained matrix $W_{\text{Geo}} = W_0 \odot (M_{\text{Spec}} \cup M_{\text{Euc}})$ combining the bottom-$\rho$ magnitude entries of the rank-$r$ approximation $\hat{W}_r$ ($M_{\text{Spec}}$, suppressing high-energy principal components) with the bottom-$\rho$ magnitude entries of $W_0$ ($M_{\text{Euc}}$, $\rho = 0.20$), initializes $B_{\text{Geo}}, A_{\text{Geo}}$ via the top-$r$ SVD of $W_{\text{Geo}}$, and freezes the residual $W_{\text{res}} = W_0 - B_{\text{Geo}} A_{\text{Geo}}$ as a structural anchor to bound Normalized Spectral Shift ($\text{NSS}$). In Section 2.5, we evaluate matched **196-block-linear adaptations** of `GeoRA`, `PiSSA`, and `MiLoRA` ($r=16$) across all 196 transformer block linear matrices on `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` so that all methods operate on the identical 196-matrix parameter set.

In our preceding study on `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` (`math_rlvr_22` v4 [4]), we established the `Tessera-Sym` training recipe (combining exact longest-common-prefix [LCP] negative credit shielding, trajectory-zero-sum Minimum Description Length [MDL] advantage shaping, and second-half 25-step `float32` tail checkpoint averaging) and a bounded `bfloat16` round-to-nearest-even (RNE) dead-zone audit showing that $>92\%$ of parameter coordinates remain bitwise unchanged after 50 RLVR updates ($1,701/3,700 = 45.97\%$ pooled across `Val350`, `MATH-500`, `Sec-500`, and `Conf-500`).

We do **not** claim priority on precision-sensitive sparsity or off-principal RLVR dynamics (Zhu et al. [21]), pre-AdamW adaptive spectral gradient filtering (`Ares`, Chen et al. [22]), geometry-aware low-rank adaptation (`GeoRA`, Zhang et al. [24]; Yin et al. [23]), the `Tessera-Sym` recipe and `bfloat16` dead-zone audit (`math_rlvr_22` v4 [4]), or same-learning-rate `float32` collapse, reduced-learning-rate recovery, and same-backbone self-consistency voting (`math_rlvr_7` v5 §4.10 [27]; `math_rlvr_6` [17]). ## 2. 2D SVD Spectral Aliasing, the 12-Cell Post-Hoc Precision Factorial, Online Subspace-Filtered Policy Optimization (`OSF-RLVR`), and Literature Comparisons


### 2.1 Full 196-Matrix Singular Spectrum, Energy vs. Amplitude Effective Rank, and Cross-Seed Alignment
Let $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ denote a pretrained `bfloat16` linear weight matrix in `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` and let $\Delta W_s = W_s - W_0$ (computed in `float32`) denote its post-RLVR displacement in `Tessera-Sym` on training seed $s \in \{102, 42\}$. Excluding token embeddings and the output head, the 28 transformer blocks of `Qwen3-0.6B` contain $28 \times 7 = 196$ two-dimensional linear matrices (`att.W_query`, `att.W_key`, `att.W_value`, `att.out_proj`, `ff.fc1`, `ff.fc2`, `ff.fc3`) totaling $440,401,920$ parameters.

For each matrix $\Delta W_s$ with singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_d > 0$ ($d = \min(m, n)$), the literature uses two distinct Shannon-entropy effective rank conventions: (i) the **squared-singular-value (energy) effective rank** $\mathrm{eRank}_{\text{energy}}$ based on $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \sum_{j=1}^d \sigma_j^2$ (used in `Ares` Eq. 3 [22] and spectral energy audits), and (ii) the **singular-value amplitude effective rank** $\mathrm{eRank}_{\text{ampl}}$ based on $q_i = \sigma_i / \sum_{j=1}^d \sigma_j$ (Roy & Vetterli [12]):
$$\text{StableRank}(\Delta W_s) = \frac{\|\Delta W_s\|_F^2}{\sigma_1^2} = \frac{1}{p_1}, \qquad \mathrm{eRank}_{\text{energy}}(\Delta W_s) = \exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^d p_i \ln p_i\right), \qquad \mathrm{eRank}_{\text{ampl}}(\Delta W_s) = \exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^d q_i \ln q_i\right).$$
To ensure complete definitional consistency across the entire paper, we report $\mathrm{eRank}_{\text{energy}}$ in Tables 1–3 alongside $\mathrm{eRank}_{\text{ampl}}$ across all checkpoints (`records/extended_spectral_and_onpolicy_kl.json`). To test whether independent RLVR training histories share a low-rank principal subspace, we also compute for each truncation rank $k \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}$ the Frobenius cosine between the rank-$k$ SVD reconstructions $\Delta W_{102}^{(k)} = U_{102, :k} \Sigma_{102, :k} V_{102, :k}^\top$ and $\Delta W_{42}^{(k)} = U_{42, :k} \Sigma_{42, :k} V_{42, :k}^\top$, as well as between their complementary high-rank residual tails $\Delta W_s - \Delta W_s^{(k)}$.

### 2.2 Direct 21-Stage State-Dependent Policy Gradient and 5-Branch Optimizer Audit
Why is $>95\%$ of the Frobenius energy of $\Delta W_s$ scattered across hundreds of singular values? In `bfloat16` (7 mantissa bits), a non-zero weight $w_0$ has unit-in-the-last-place $\text{ULP}(w_0) = 2^{\lfloor \log_2 |w_0| \rfloor - 7} \approx 0.0078125 |w_0|$ [18, 19]. Under AdamW with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-5}$ and moments $\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$, the bias-corrected step at step $t$ is:
$$\Delta \theta_t = -\eta \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} = -\eta \frac{m_t / (1 - \beta_1^t)}{\sqrt{v_t / (1 - \beta_2^t)} + \epsilon}.$$
For stationary gradients $g \in \{-G, +G\}$, $|\hat{m}_t| / \sqrt{\hat{v}_t} \approx 1$, yielding a step of magnitude $\approx \eta$. If a coordinate experiences 40 steps of zero gradient followed by 10 steps of unit gradient $g = +G$, then at step $t=50$:
$$\hat{m}_{50} = \frac{(1 - 0.9^{10}) G}{1 - 0.9^{50}} \approx 0.6547 G, \qquad \hat{v}_{50} = \frac{(1 - 0.999^{10}) G^2}{1 - 0.999^{50}} \approx 0.2039 G^2,$$
yielding $\frac{\hat{m}_{50}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{50}}} \approx 1.4494$, so a single step can reach $\approx 1.449\eta$ (and the theoretical maximum over $t \le 50, k \le t$ is $1.459\eta$ at $k=12$). Because weights are updated in place directly in `bfloat16` without a `float32` master weight copy, individual sub-threshold updates below $\frac{1}{2}\text{ULP}(w_0) \approx 0.0039 |w_0|$ do not accumulate in the weight tensor itself (though the `float32` moments $m_t$ and $v_t$ carry momentum forward). Across all $440,401,920$ parameters in the 196 linear matrices, only **$33,999,193$ coordinates ($7.72\%$)** in Seed 102 and **$29,546,069$ coordinates ($6.71\%$)** in Seed 42 cross the RNE dead zone after 50 steps.

To rigorously isolate the spectral effect of **(a) raw policy gradients in `float32` vs. `bfloat16` at the actual live intermediate checkpoints**, **(b) trajectory-evolving state-dependent gradients vs. common gradients**, **(c) AdamW second-moment preconditioning**, and **(d) per-step weight RNE rounding**, we replay the recorded 50-step RLVR rollout trajectories of `Tessera-Sym-Seed102` (`21` active non-zero-variance optimizer steps, `25` second-half tail steps; `code/run_external_bench_and_fp32_audit.py`). ### 2.3 Twelve-Cell Confound-Free Post-Hoc Precision, Component & Spectral Subspace Factorial ($N=1,850$ + Untouched `Fresh-500`)
Because empirical in-place `bfloat16` displacements are $\pm 1\text{ ULP}$ steps on a sparse $7.72\%$ support, post-hoc SVD truncation of $\Delta W_{102}$ to rank $r=16$ spreads update energy across all matrix coordinates, shrinking many entries below $\frac{1}{2}\text{ULP}(w_0)$. Across **all 196 linear matrices combined**, replacing each $\Delta W_{102}$ with its post-hoc rank-16 SVD truncation and rounding to `bfloat16` retains only **$15,860,388 / 33,999,193$ ($46.65\%$) of the true active updates, underflowing $53.35\%$ ($18,138,805$) to zero while inducing $2,592,203$ spurious boundary flips**. Building on the single-panel post-hoc directional low-rank compression study in `math_rlvr_7` (v5, §4.10 [27]), we design a 12-cell precision, component, and spectral subspace factorial to separate non-target parameters, forward-pass dtype, unrounded principal vs. tail subspaces, and `bfloat16` RNE snap-back.

To ensure **zero precision or component confounds**, we audit the exact parameter and buffer dtypes of `FastModel(cfg)` during training and evaluation (`code/run_precision_factorial_complete.py`):
- **115 Non-Target Tensors and `float32` RoPE Buffers:** Alongside the 196 two-dimensional transformer linear weight matrices ($440,401,920$ parameters), `Qwen3-0.6B` contains **115 non-target parameter tensors**: `tok_emb.weight` ($151936 \times 1024$), `out_head.weight` ($151936 \times 1024$), and **113 `float32` RMSNorm scale vectors** (`final_norm.scale` plus 28 layers $\times$ 4 norms: `norm1`, `norm2`, `q_norm`, `k_norm`), as well as `float32` rotary position embedding (`RoPE`) buffers (`cos`, `sin`). In `FastModel`, `RMSNorm` initializes its scale parameter in `torch.float32` (`torch.ones(emb_dim, dtype=torch.float32)`) and computes normalization in `float32` before casting back to `x.dtype`, while `compute_rope_params` creates `cos` and `sin` in `torch.float32`. Calling `model.to(torch.bfloat16)` after checkpoint loading would cast all 113 `float32` RMSNorm scales and `float32` RoPE buffers to `bfloat16`, whereas adding a `float32` delta to a `bfloat16` tensor (`W_0.to(bfloat16) + dW_fp32`) and then calling `.to(torch.bfloat16).float()` would introduce a redundant `bfloat16` round-trip.
- **Strict Confound-Free Protocol (`build_clean_eval_model` in `code/run_precision_factorial_complete.py`):** In all **`BF16` network cells**, `tok_emb.weight` and `out_head.weight` are `torch.bfloat16`, while all **113 RMSNorm scale tensors and `cos`/`sin` RoPE buffers remain in exact `torch.float32`** (matching `FastModel` training/eval exactly, with no post-load `model.to(torch.bfloat16)`). In all **`FP32` network cells**, all parameters and buffers are `torch.float32` constructed directly as `W_0.float() + dW_fp32` without any intermediate `.to(torch.bfloat16)` round-trip. In **hybrid `LinFP32-NetBF16` cells**, the surrounding network is identical to `BF16` network cells while the 196 linear layers execute `F.linear(x.float(), W_fp32).to(x.dtype)` via `FP32LinearInBF16Net`.
- **Deterministic Construction, Question Provenance, and Extracted-Answer Grading of Untouched `Fresh-500`, plus Fixed 64-Trajectory Reference KL (`kl_reference_trajectories_64.json`):** Because `Val350`, `MATH-500`, `Sec-500`, and `Conf-500` were inspected on Seed 102 during earlier studies, we construct a genuinely untouched 500-item stratified benchmark (`Fresh-500`, `code/splits/test_fresh_untouched_500.json`) deterministically in `code/build_splits.py` (Step 5) from `math_train[1000:]` (`random.Random(20261019)`) to match the exact 35-cell `(subject, level)` joint distribution of `Conf-500`/`Sec-500`, excluding all prior splits, `math_train[:1000]`, and the 178 `orig_train_idx` rollout prompts. Every `Fresh-500` item's `orig_train_idx`, `unique_id`, 16-character SHA-256 problem hash, ground-truth `answer`, and per-arm extracted model answer (`extracted`) are packed in `records/osf_and_precision_factorial.json` (`fresh500_provenance_and_answers`) and verified by `code/reproduce_stencil_and_svd.py` to have **zero overlap** with `Val350`, `MATH-500`, `Sec-500`, `Conf-500`, and all 178 rollout questions, and **zero grading mismatches** (`grade_answer(extracted, gt) == correct`) across all $22,000+$ item evaluations. For every checkpoint, we also compute exact full-vocabulary ($V=151,936$) conditional next-token reverse Policy KL divergence $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{cond}}(\pi_\theta \| \pi_0)$ over the $15,387$ completion tokens of the 64 self-contained reference trajectories in `records/kl_reference_trajectories_64.json` (with no fallback to generic text), complemented by own-policy greedy-completion conditional KL $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{greedy}}(\pi_\theta \| \pi_0)$ evaluated strictly on each checkpoint+operator's own generated `MATH-500[:64]` greedy completions with verified directory and SHA-256 provenance (`records/extended_spectral_and_onpolicy_kl.json`; note that $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{greedy}}$ conditions on greedy-decoded prefixes rather than an expectation over stochastic on-policy rollouts).

### 2.4 Online Subspace-Filtered Policy Optimization (`OSF-RLVR`), Exact Recurrence & Projection Controls, and Question-Clustered Multi-Seed Inference
The post-hoc findings in Sections 2.2 and 2.3 motivate a direct **algorithmic training intervention**. In standard `bfloat16` RLVR, per-step RNE rounding zeroes out $>92\%$ of sub-ULP coordinate updates, acting as an implicit hardware threshold that prevents small steps from accumulating. Conversely, if we maintain exact `float32` master weights (`Unfiltered-FP32-Master`) so that sub-ULP updates accumulate without dead-zone damping, how does online RLVR behave—and how does projecting the cumulative update onto its principal vs. orthogonal tail singular subspaces affect policy KL divergence, Normalized Spectral Shift ($\text{NSS}$) [24], and reasoning accuracy?

**Exact Mathematical Recurrence of `train_osf_rlvr.py` and Pre/Post-Projection Controls:** To avoid any ambiguity between unprojected master accumulation, `bfloat16` rollout parameter synchronization, and post-averaging spectral projection, we state the exact 3-stage recurrence implemented in `code/train_osf_rlvr.py`:
1. **Unprojected `float32` Master Update (vs. Every-Step Master Writeback):** At each RLVR step $t \in \{1, \dots, 50\}$, the optimizer updates a `float32` master weight tensor $W_{\text{master}}^{(t)}$ using `float32` AdamW moments $m_t, v_t$, defining the raw cumulative displacement $\Delta W_{\text{raw}}^{(t)} = W_{\text{master}}^{(t)} - W_0$. In the primary `OSF` protocol, $W_{\text{master}}^{(t)}$ remains unprojected so that sub-leading modes can evolve before spectral filtering; in Section 2.5 we also evaluate the strict **Every-Step Master-Writeback** variant (`--writeback-master`, $W_{\text{master}}^{(t)} \leftarrow W_0 + \mathcal{P}(\Delta W_{\text{raw}}^{(t)})$).
2. **Rollout Policy Parameter Projection (`Pre-Proj Rollout-BF16` vs. `Pre-Proj LinFP32`):** Before generating rollouts at step $t+1$, each of the 196 linear matrices is projected in `float32` as $W_{\text{proj}}^{(t)} = W_0 + \mathcal{P}(\Delta W_{\text{raw}}^{(t)})$ and copied into the active rollout model in `bfloat16`: $W_{\text{rollout}}^{(t)} = \operatorname{RNE}_{\text{bf16}}(W_{\text{proj}}^{(t)})$. Across the second half of training ($t \in \{26, \dots, 50\}$), `train_osf_rlvr.py` accumulates both the `bfloat16` rollout tail average $\overline{W}_{\text{rollout}}^{\text{BF16}} = \operatorname{RNE}_{\text{bf16}}\!\left(\frac{1}{25}\sum_{t=26}^{50} W_{\text{rollout}}^{(t)}\right)$ (saved as `model_tail_avg.pth`, denoted **`Pre-Proj Rollout-BF16`**) and the unrounded `float32` projected linear tail average $\overline{W}_{\text{lin}}^{\text{FP32}} = \frac{1}{25}\sum_{t=26}^{50} W_{\text{proj}}^{(t)}$ (**`Pre-Proj LinFP32`**).
3. **Final Post-Averaging Spectral Projection (`Post-Proj LinFP32` vs. `Post-Proj BF16`):** Because the average of 25 rank-$r$ matrices along a rotating trajectory has rank $> r$, `train_osf_rlvr.py` (lines 423–444) applies one final spectral projection $\mathcal{P}$ to the averaged displacement $\overline{W}_{\text{lin}}^{\text{FP32}} - W_0$, saving both the unrounded `float32` linear dictionary $W_{\text{eval}}^{\text{LinFP32}} = W_0 + \mathcal{P}(\overline{W}_{\text{lin}}^{\text{FP32}} - W_0)$ (`model_tail_avg_fp32_linear.pth`, denoted **`Post-Proj LinFP32`**, which strictly satisfies $\operatorname{rank}(\Delta W) \le 16$ for `OSF-Top16`) and its `bfloat16`-rounded counterpart $W_{\text{eval}}^{\text{BF16}} = \operatorname{RNE}_{\text{bf16}}(W_{\text{eval}}^{\text{LinFP32}})$ (`model_tail_avg_proj_bf16.pth`, denoted **`Post-Proj BF16`**).

We evaluate **Online Subspace-Filtered Policy Optimization (`OSF-RLVR`)** across **three independent training seeds (`Seed 102`, `Seed 42`, `Seed 137`)** and **six evaluation panels** (`Val350`, `MATH-500`, `Sec-500`, `Conf-500`, untouched `Fresh-500`, and out-of-distribution `Ext240`) at **strictly uniform `batch_size=50`**, separating **Table 5A (`online_osf_factorial`: uniform `Post-Proj BF16` / `bf16` evaluation across all 6 panels on Seeds 102 & 42 [$N=5,180$] plus Seed 137 `Standard-BF16` [$N=1,240$])** from **Table 5B (`online_osf_factorial_postproj_linfp32`: strictly uniform `Post-Proj LinFP32` evaluation via `FP32LinearInBF16Net` across all 5 arms on Seed 102 [$N=1,240$], the 3 `float32`-master arms on Seed 137 [$N=1,240$], and all 5 arms on Seed 42 `Fresh-500` [$N=500$])**:

## 3. Zero-Initialized Gated Middle-Layer Recurrent Refinement (`LoopedFastModel`)

### 3.1 Architecture and Full 28-Layer GPU Training-to-Decoding KV-Cache Verification
To increase effective reasoning depth without modifying the 196 `bfloat16` weight matrices or breaking pretrained representations at initialization, we introduce `LoopedFastModel` (`--loop-layers 10,18` in `code/train_tessera.py`). For each middle transformer block $\ell \in \{10, 11, \dots, 17\}$ ($8$ layers), let $x_1 = \text{Block}_\ell(x_0, \text{KV}_1)$ denote the standard first-pass hidden state and updated key-value state $\text{KV}_1 = (K_1, V_1)$. We define an input-conditional recurrent refinement operator `recurrent_refine` that re-applies $\text{Block}_\ell$'s pre-norm grouped-query attention and SwiGLU feed-forward network using $x_1$ as the query state and $\text{KV}_1$ as the key-value context:
$$\delta_\ell(x_1, \text{KV}_1) = \text{Attn}_\ell(\text{RMSNorm}_{1,\ell}(x_1), \text{KV}_1) + \text{FFN}_\ell\!\left(\text{RMSNorm}_{2,\ell}\!\left(x_1 + \text{Attn}_\ell(\text{RMSNorm}_{1,\ell}(x_1), \text{KV}_1)\right)\right).$$
The block output is modulated by a zero-initialized `float32` elementwise residual gate $g_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ ($8 \times 1024 = 8,192$ parameters total, $32\text{ KB}$):
$$x_{\text{out}}(g_\ell) = x_1 + \tanh(g_\ell) \odot \delta_\ell(x_1, \text{KV}_1).$$

Two operator invariants hold for `LoopedFastModel`:
1. **Exact Bitwise Identity at Initialization ($g_\ell = 0$):** When $g_\ell = 0$, $\tanh(g_\ell) = 0$, so `LoopedFastModel` is bitwise identical to unlooped `FastModel` (`max_logprob_diff = 0.000000000000e+00`) while delivering non-zero initial gradients $\nabla_{g_\ell} \mathcal{L} = \delta_\ell \odot \nabla_{x_{\text{out}}} \mathcal{L}$ across all 8 looped layers (`grad_norm` $\in [0.460, 1.426]$ on Step 1).
2. **Full 28-Layer `Qwen3-0.6B` GPU KV-Cache Numerical Verification Across $N=32$ Real `MATH-500` 96-Token Prefixes ($32$ Prompt + $64$ Incremental Decode Steps $= 2,048$ Decoded Token Positions, $g_\ell \neq 0$):** Because `recurrent_refine` queries the causal first-pass cache $\text{KV}_1$ without overwriting $\text{KV}_1$, full-sequence parallel evaluation (`cache=None`, used during rollout log-probability evaluation in GRPO) and token-by-token autoregressive decoding (`cache=KVCache(n_layers=28)`, used during rollout generation and test evaluation) define the **exact same mathematical operator**. To distinguish exact operator equivalence from finite-precision GEMM tiling differences across multi-sentence reasoning prefixes, we execute a direct **5-Condition Full 28-Layer `Qwen3-0.6B` GPU Verification** across **$N=32$ real `MATH-500` trajectories** evaluated on fixed **96-token prefixes** (`seq_len=96`: $32$ prompt prefill tokens $+ 64$ incremental decoded token steps per sequence $= 2,048$ decoded token positions and $311,164,928$ vocabulary logits evaluated per condition; note this verifies 64-step incremental decoding on 96-token sequences rather than full 2,048-token rollouts; `code/verify_full_kv_cache.py` and `records/kv_cache_verification_full.json`):
   - **Full 28-Layer `float32` Execution (`fp32_learned_gates` and `fp32_zero_gates`):** With all 28 layers, RMSNorms, gates, and RoPE in `torch.float32`, maximum absolute logit difference across all $2,048$ decoded token steps is **`3.691e-04`** (`mean_abs_logit_diff = 8.334e-06`) for learned gates and **`2.849e-04`** (`mean_abs_logit_diff = 8.152e-06`) for zero gates, with **`100.00%` ($2,048 / 2,048$) argmax token agreement** in both conditions.
   - **Full 28-Layer `bfloat16` Execution (`bf16_learned_gates`, `bf16_rademacher_gates`, `bf16_zero_gates`):** With linear layers in `torch.bfloat16` and RMSNorm/gates/RoPE in `torch.float32`, GEMM reduction-order differences between $T \times K$ prefill and $1 \times K$ single-token decode kernels yield `max_abs_logit_diff = 2.578` (`mean_abs_logit_diff = 7.647e-02`, **`98.83%` [$2,024 / 2,048$] argmax agreement**) for learned gates, `max_abs_logit_diff = 2.641` (`mean_abs_logit_diff = 7.523e-02`, **`98.93%` [$2,026 / 2,048$] argmax agreement**) for Rademacher gates, and `max_abs_logit_diff = 2.562` (`mean_abs_logit_diff = 7.820e-02`, **`98.68%` [$2,021 / 2,048$] argmax agreement**) for unlooped zero gates (`g=0`). Notice that argmax agreement and mean logit discrepancy with active recurrent gates (`98.83%` and `7.65e-02`) are at least as tight as on the standard unlooped `Qwen3-0.6B` transformer (`98.68%` and `7.82e-02`), confirming that `recurrent_refine` introduces zero KV-cache state corruption across 64-step incremental generation.

## 4. Single-Backbone Deterministic Recurrent-Depth Stencils Across $3,700$ Within-MATH Evaluations

### 4.1 The Antipodal Consensus Phenomenon and Selection Rules
Why did `GateOnly-Loop10_18` ($+g_{\text{lrn}}$) gain $113$ newly solved items over its own frozen $g=0$ parent across the 4 panels while losing $137$ others? And look at the antipodal pass (`Antipodal-Loop10_18`, which negates the $8,192$ gates to $-g_{\text{lrn}}$ on the same frozen backbone): on `Sec-500` and `Conf-500`, where $+g_{\text{lrn}}$ dropped to $45.20\%$ and $41.40\%$, $-g_{\text{lrn}}$ rebounds to $46.60\%$ and $43.80\%$ while cutting 2,048-token truncations to $5.57\%$ ($103/1,850$).

Because $g=0$, $+g$, and $-g$ share the **exact same $1.50\text{ GB}$ `bfloat16` weight tensors in VRAM** (toggling only a $32\text{ KB}$ `float32` gate vector in Layers 10--17), a single model instance can execute a deterministic **3-Depth Stencil** $\{-g, 0, +g\}$ at $T=0$. Let $(a_0, a_{+g}, a_{-g})$ denote the extracted answers of the three passes. We evaluate two parameter-free, verifier-free selection rules alongside the 3-pass oracle union:
1. **`Plur(Center)` (Center-Anchored Consensus):** Outputs the majority answer whenever at least two passes agree; on 1-1-1 all-distinct ties, defaults to the unlooped center pass $a_0$. Notice that `Plur(Center)` differs from single-pass $g=0$ **if and only if** the two recurrent passes converge on the **exact same non-null answer** $a_{+g} = a_{-g} \neq a_0$.
2. **`Plur(MDL-Box)` (Minimum-Description-Length Boxed Tie-Break):** Outputs the majority answer whenever at least two passes agree; on 1-1-1 ties among non-null candidates, selects the candidate with the shortest non-truncated completion containing a formatted `\boxed{` answer (`"\\boxed{" in text and tokens < 2048`; if none, shortest non-null candidate). *(Methodological note: using the looser substring check `"\\boxed" in text` without `< 2048` changes 2 items on Seed 102 `Lrn3`, giving $885/1,850$ vs. $883/1,850$; we use the stricter `"\\boxed{" in text and tokens < 2048` selector uniformly across all methods.)*

### 4.2 Learned Gate Geometry vs. Training-Free Norm-Matched Rademacher Gates
To understand what the 50-step `GateOnly-Loop10_18` run actually learned on Seed 102 and on its full replication on Seed 42 (`tessera_loop10_18_gateonly_seed42`), we inspect the learned $8,192$-dimensional gate vectors $g^{(102)}_{\text{lrn}}$ and $g^{(42)}_{\text{lrn}}$:
- **Layerwise RMS and Mean Absolute Magnitude:**
  - On Seed 102, the layerwise RMS values $\sigma_{\text{RMS},\ell} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{1024}\sum_j g_{\ell,j}^2}$ across Layers 10--17 are `[0.029383, 0.030402, 0.030790, 0.029918, 0.029095, 0.030296, 0.030210, 0.030335]`, with overall $\text{RMS} = 0.030058$ and mean absolute gate magnitude $\frac{1}{8192}\sum |g_{\ell,j}| = 0.024682$.
  - On Seed 42, the layerwise RMS values across Layers 10--17 are `[0.034124, 0.033404, 0.033529, 0.034777, 0.034531, 0.034603, 0.034733, 0.034966]`, with overall $\text{RMS} = 0.034338$ and mean absolute magnitude $0.028252$.
- **Near-Orthogonality Across Seeds:** Despite converging to nearly identical scalar RMS scales ($\sim 0.030\text{--}0.034$), the inner-product cosine between $g^{(102)}_{\text{lrn}}$ and $g^{(42)}_{\text{lrn}}$ across all $8,192$ coordinates is **`+0.0254`** ($50.88\%$ sign agreement).

This near-zero cross-seed cosine motivates two decisive mechanistic controls: **(a) are the $8,192$ learned coordinate directions necessary at all, or was 50-step gate RLVR merely calibrating the perturbation scale $\sigma_{\text{RMS},\ell} \approx 0.03$? and (b) does the 3-pass stencil require antipodal sign negation ($-g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, 0, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}$) along a single 1D axis, or does a pair of independent Rademacher directions ($0, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(2)}$) perform comparably?** To test both questions, we construct two **Norm-Matched Rademacher Gate Vectors** (`Rademacher-Loop10_18`, `0` gate-direction training steps) on each seed:
$$g_{\text{rad},\ell}^{(m)} = \sigma_{\text{RMS},\ell} \cdot s_\ell^{(m)}, \qquad s_\ell^{(m)} \sim \text{Unif}\!\left(\{-1, +1\}^{1024}\right) \quad (m \in \{1, 2\}),$$
using fixed pseudorandom seeds (`20261017` and `20262001` on Seed 102; `20261042` and `20262002` on Seed 42; 8,192-D inner-product cosines are $\cos(g_{\text{lrn}}, g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}) = +0.0115$, $\cos(g_{\text{lrn}}, g_{\text{rad}}^{(2)}) = +0.0249$, $\cos(g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, g_{\text{rad}}^{(2)}) = -0.0187$ on Seed 102, and $-0.0008, -0.0182, +0.0178$ on Seed 42). We evaluate both the **Antipodal Rademacher Stencil** $\{-g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, 0, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}\}$ and the **Independent-Direction Rademacher Stencil** $\{0, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(2)}\}$, as well as the **5-Point Single-Backbone Stencil** $\{-g_{\text{lrn}}, -g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, 0, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, +g_{\text{lrn}}\}$ on the same $1.50\text{ GB}$ checkpoint.

## 5. Same-Backbone Comparison Against Homogeneous Sampled `plurality@3`, Center-Anchored Sampling, Hybrid Stencils, and External Confirmation

### 5.1 Experimental Setup, Multiplicity Correction, and Dual Seed/Cluster Inference
A natural baseline for a 3-pass deterministic stencil ($3.01\times\text{--}3.05\times$ tokens, $3.56\times\text{--}3.63\times$ completion block passes) is $K=3$ stochastic rollouts ($s_1, s_2, s_3$) sampled directly from the **same unlooped center weights ($g=0$, $28$ blocks)**. Following community evaluation protocols on `r/math_rlvr` [11, 16], we evaluate $K=6$ independent stochastic passes at temperature $T=0.4$ (`top_p = 1.0`, deterministic batch seeds `401000 + k`) across all four within-MATH panels ($N=1,850$ items) on **both Seed 102 and Seed 42** ($6 \times 3,700 = 22,200$ within-MATH stochastic evaluations total) as well as on the untouched external benchmark `Ext240` ($6 \times 480 = 2,880$ external stochastic evaluations, Section 5.3), using per-step KV-cache survivor compaction (`shrink_kv_cache_inplace` in `code/run_same_backbone_sampling.py`).

To test our hypothesis that **homogeneous i.i.d. sampling ($\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$) suffers excessive regression churn at $k=3$ because it discards the high-precision $T=0$ greedy center trajectory $g_0$**, we compare:
- **Homogeneous Sampled `plurality@3` ($\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$):** Evaluated under `Uniform` tie-breaking (analytical expectation over 1-1-1 ties), `First-Sample` ($s_1$) tie-breaking, and `MDL-Box` tie-breaking.
- **Center-Anchored Stochastic Consensus ($\{g_0, s_1, s_2\}$):** Replaces $s_3$ with the deterministic greedy trajectory $g_0$ at the **exact same $2.99\times$ token and completion block-pass budget**, applying our stencil's `Plur(Center)` rule (overriding $g_0$ *only* when $a_{s_1} = a_{s_2} \neq a_0$) and `Plur(MDL-Box)`.
- **4-Pass Center + 3 Samples ($\{g_0, s_1, s_2, s_3\}$):** Combines $g_0$ with all three stochastic draws ($3.99\times$ tokens and block passes).
- **Pure 5-Pass ($\{s_1..s_5\}$) and Center-Anchored 5-Pass ($\{g_0, s_1..s_4\}$):** Matched at $5.00\times$ tokens and block passes.
- **Pure 6-Pass ($\{s_1..s_6\}$) and Center-Anchored 6-Pass ($\{g_0, s_1..s_5\}$):** Matched at $6.00\times$ tokens and block passes (near-budget comparison against the $6.19\times$ completion block passes of the 5-point deterministic stencil).
- **5-Pass Hybrid Latent+Surface Stencil ($\{g_0, +g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, -g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, s_1, s_2\}$):** Combines the greedy center $g_0$, the two training-free Rademacher recurrent-depth passes ($\pm g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}$ at $T=0$), and two stochastic draws ($s_1, s_2$ at $T=0.4$) at $5.02\times$ tokens and $5.60\times$ completion block passes.
- **8-Pass ($\{g_0, \pm g_{\text{lrn}}, \pm g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, s_{1..3}\}$) and 11-Pass ($\{g_0, \pm g_{\text{lrn}}, \pm g_{\text{rad}}^{(1)}, s_{1..6}\}$) Combined Stencils:** Combine all 5 deterministic stencil passes with 3 or 6 stochastic draws on a single $1.50\text{ GB}$ checkpoint ($9.18\times$ and $12.18\times$ block passes).

*Table 8: Direct head-to-head paired contrasts across Seeds 102 and 42 ($M=1,850$ question clusters, $3,700$ evaluations), including 17-test Holm-Bonferroni adjusted $p$-values ($p_{\text{Holm,17}}$) and descriptive half-range $\text{SE}_{\text{seed}}$.*

Tables 6, 7, and 8 establish four major conclusions on the within-MATH panels:
1. **Why Homogeneous Sampled `plurality@3` ($\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$) Is Statistically Fragile at Small $k=3$:**
   - Because `Tessera-Sym` was trained with trajectory-zero-sum MDL length shaping, $T=0.4$ stochastic rollouts maintain approximate completion length parity with greedy decoding ($624.3$ vs. $622.3$ tokens on Seed 102; $567.4$ vs. $575.0$ tokens on Seed 42; $2.99\times$ tokens and block passes for 3 draws).
   - However, individual $T=0.4$ draws suffer a **$-2.07\text{ pp}$ accuracy drop** relative to $T=0$ greedy decoding (`1,624.3/3,700 = 43.90%` vs. `1,701/3,700 = 45.97%`, $p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 1.25 \times 10^{-3}$). Discarding the greedy trajectory $g_0$ in pure $\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$ causes **$274$ regressions on greedy-correct items** across the two seeds ($127$ on Seed 102, $147$ on Seed 42). Item-level auditing reveals that **$183 / 274$ ($66.79\%$) of these losses stem from 1-1-1 all-distinct ties** (where $s_1$ selects a wrong answer), while only **$91 / 274$ ($33.21\%$) stem from wrong-answer majorities** (and $0$ from all-null draws).
   - On greedy-wrong items ($1,999$ items), pure $\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$ (`First`) gains $320$ wins ($105$ on 1-1-1 ties and $215$ on $\ge 2$ correct majorities). On 1-1-1 ties alone, pure sampling loses $183$ greedy-correct items while winning only $105$ greedy-wrong items (a net loss of $-78$ items). This inflates discordant transitions to $594$ items ($+320/-274$, net $+46$, $p_{\text{cl}} = 0.0611$, $p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 0.122$ on `First`; $p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 0.122$ on `Uniform`; and even `MDL-Box` is non-significant after multiplicity correction at $p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 0.117$), causing pure $\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$ to **lose to single-pass greedy decoding on `MATH-500` Seed 42 (`232.3` vs. `237`) and `Conf-500` Seed 102 (`222.7` vs. `228`)**.
2. **Why Center-Anchored Consensus ($\{g_0, s_1, s_2\}$) Eliminates $86.5\%$ of Regressions vs. $g_0$:**
   - Replacing $s_3$ with the deterministic greedy center trajectory $g_0$ in **Center-Anchored Stochastic $\{g_0, s_1, s_2\}$ (`Center`)** at the **same $2.99\times$ token and block-pass proxy** requires both stochastic draws to agree with each other against the greedy anchor ($a_{s_1} = a_{s_2} \neq a_0$) to overturn $g_0$. This rule **completely eliminates 1-1-1 tie regressions ($183 \to 0$)** and cuts wrong-majority regressions by more than half ($91 \to 37$).
   - Total regressions on greedy-correct items drop **$7.4\times$ from $274$ to $37$** ($17$ on Seed 102, $20$ on Seed 42). The net gain over $g_0$ rises from $+46$ ($+1.24\text{ pp}$) to $+77$ ($+2.08\text{ pp}$), while discordant transitions collapse from $594$ to $151$ ($+114/-37$), cutting cluster standard error in half ($\text{SE}_{\text{cl}} = 0.66\text{ pp} \to 0.34\text{ pp}$) and surviving 26-test Holm-Bonferroni correction at **$p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 1.33 \times 10^{-8}$** (reaching $889/1,850 = 48.05\%$ on both seeds; under `MDL-Box`, $+2.05\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 4.95 \times 10^{-4}$).
   - In direct head-to-head comparison at equal $2.99\times$ work proxy (Table 8), `Cntr {g0, s1, s2}` has a positive point estimate over `Pure {s1, s2, s3}` of $+0.84\text{ pp}$ under `Center` ($+237/-206$, $p_{\text{cl}}=0.141$, $p_{\text{Holm,17}}=1.00$) and $+0.73\text{ pp}$ under `MDL-Box` ($+153/-126$, $p_{\text{cl}}=0.108$, $p_{\text{Holm,17}}=1.00$). Because this head-to-head difference remains unresolved within the confidence interval, we frame `Cntr {g0, s1, s2}` at $k=3$ specifically as a **regression-control and variance-reduction mechanism relative to $g_0$ ($37$ vs. $274$ losses; $\text{SE}_{\text{cl}} = 0.34\text{ pp}$ vs. $0.66\text{ pp}$)**.
3. **Complementary Latent-Depth + Surface-Token Rescues in Hybrid Stencils:**
   - On the $991$ items on Seed 102 where greedy $g_0$ is wrong, `Rad3_Center` rescues $30$ items and `Cntr {g0, s1, s2} Center` rescues $47$ items, but **only $10$ items overlap**: **$20/30$ ($66.7\%$) of the deterministic recurrent-depth rescues are missed by `{g0, s1, s2}`**, and under `MDL-Box`, **$38/67$ ($56.7\%$) of `Rad3_MDL` rescues are missed by pure `{s1, s2, s3}` `MDL-Box`**.
   - Consequently, replacing $(\pm g_{\text{lrn}})$ in the 5-point deterministic stencil (`Det5`, $6.19\times$ block passes) with two stochastic draws $(s_1, s_2)$ in **`Hyb5 {g0, ±g_rad, s1, s2}` (`MDL-Box`, $5.60\times$ block passes)** improves accuracy by **$+1.32\text{ pp}$ head-to-head (`+132/-83`, $p_{\text{cl}} = 0.0011$, $p_{\text{Holm,17}} = 0.0167$, $\Delta_{102}=+1.35\text{ pp}, \Delta_{42}=+1.30\text{ pp}$)** at *lower* block-pass cost, reaching **$1,838/3,700$ ($49.68\%$, $\Delta = +3.70\text{ pp}$ over $g_0$, $p_{\text{Holm,26}} = 1.29 \times 10^{-14}$)**.
4. **Near-Budget Practical Comparison ($6.00\times$ Sampling vs. $6.19\times$ Deterministic Stencil):**
   - While deterministic recurrent-depth stencils establish a clean mechanistic finding at $T=0$, our own near-budget head-to-head comparison ($6.00\times$ vs. $6.19\times$ completion block passes in Table 8) shows that **`Center-Anchored 6-Pass` (and `Pure 6-Pass`) stochastic voting significantly outperforms the `5-Point Deterministic Stencil` by $+2.78\text{ pp}$ under `Center` (`+210/-107`, $p_{\text{cl}} = 9.19 \times 10^{-9}$, $p_{\text{Holm,17}} = 1.56 \times 10^{-7}$) and by $+2.59\text{ pp}$ under `MDL-Box` (`+253/-157`, $p_{\text{cl}} = 2.75 \times 10^{-6}$, $p_{\text{Holm,17}} = 4.40 \times 10^{-5}$)**. Thus, when stochastic sampling is permitted at a 5–6 pass budget, ordinary or center-anchored temperature sampling is the stronger standalone accuracy choice, while recurrent-depth perturbations are best used either when strict $T=0$ reproducibility is required or as complementary members in hybrid/combined ensembles (`Hyb5`, `8-Pass`, `11-Pass`).

## 6. Limitations, Scope Conditions, and Pre-Registration Scope

1. **Bounded Interpretation of Fixed-Reference vs. Own-Completion Greedy-Prefix Conditional KL, Activation Alignment, and Cross-Arm Non-Monotonicity:** Our online experiments (`code/train_osf_rlvr.py`, `code/run_stage2_ares_geora_vanilla_and_lrsweep.py`, and `code/run_reviewer_interventions_and_audits.py`, Sections 2.4 and 2.5) span three independent training seeds (`102`, `42`, `137`), both shaped (`Tessera-Sym`) and exact supplied (`Vanilla-GRPO`) objectives, 50-step and 100-step horizons, pre/post-final-projection and every-step master-writeback controls, online learning-rate sweeps ($\eta \in \{2.5\times 10^{-6}, 5.0\times 10^{-6}, 1.0\times 10^{-5}\}$), a prespecified 12-point target-free scale grid ($\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$), and head-to-head comparisons against `Ares` (Chen, Zhao, & Wen [22], under both the published Eq. 3 squared-singular-value energy rule $r_t \approx 10.5$ and the amplitude rule $r_t \approx 38$), `GeoRA` (Zhang et al. [24]), `PiSSA` (Meng et al. [25]), and `MiLoRA` (Wang et al. [26]), as evaluated in RLVR by Yin et al. [23]. Across all evaluated checkpoints (`records/kl_and_spectral_shift_metrics.json` and `records/extended_spectral_and_onpolicy_kl.json`), we distinguish two regimes:
   - **Within-Arm Causal Scaling and Subspace Dispersion:** Within any fixed training trajectory or subspace family, scaling down update magnitude ($\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$ or reducing $\eta$ from $10^{-5} \to 2.5\times 10^{-6}$) or dispersing update energy across 1,008 orthogonal tail modes (`OSF-Tail16` vs. `OSF-Top16` at matched $\|\Delta W\|_F \approx 0.86$) simultaneously reduces both fixed-reference conditional next-token KL $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{ref}}(\pi_\theta \| \pi_0)$ on the 64 reference trajectories and own-completion greedy-prefix conditional KL $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{greedy}}(\pi_\theta \| \pi_0)$ on each checkpoint+operator's own `MATH-500[:64]` greedy completions (distinct from an expectation over stochastic on-policy rollouts), recovering $+10\text{ to }+28\text{ pp}$ in reasoning accuracy.
   - **Cross-Arm Non-Monotonicity and Trajectory Specificity:** Across different optimizer architectures, KL divergence is a strongly predictive indicator of step-size overshoot (all high-performing arms satisfy $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{cond}} \le 0.08\text{ nats/tok}$, whereas all arms with $\mathrm{KL}_{\text{cond}} > 0.11\text{ nats/tok}$ degrade significantly), **not a strictly monotonic cross-arm function of accuracy**. For example, `Ares-FP32` (amplitude rule, $\eta = 10^{-5}$) has slightly lower conditional KL than `OSF-Top16` on Seed 102 ($\mathrm{KL}_{\text{cond}} = 0.2198$ vs. $0.2828\text{ nats/tok}$) yet scores lower on the 3-panel `LinFP32` benchmark ($33.39\%$ vs. $36.85\%$), because pre-AdamW gradient truncation and post-AdamW subspace projection follow distinct parameter trajectories.
2. **Untouched `Fresh-500` Benchmark, External `Ext240` Confirmation, and Question-Clustered Multi-Seed Inference:** Because the four original within-MATH panels (`Val350`, `MATH-500`, `Sec-500`, `Conf-500`) were observed on Seed 102 during earlier studies, we constructed the strictly untouched 500-item stratified benchmark `Fresh-500` (`code/splits/test_fresh_untouched_500.json`, deterministically reproducible via `code/build_splits.py` with full question provenance and `grade_answer`-verified extracted predictions in `records/osf_and_precision_factorial.json`) alongside the separately sourced external benchmark `Ext240` (`GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40`). Using question-clustered inference across $M=500$ unique `Fresh-500` items ($S=3$ seeds, $N=1,500$ evaluations), `OSF-Tail16-FP32-Master` (`769/1,500 = 51.27%`) significantly outperforms `OSF-Top16-FP32-Master` (`462/1,500 = 30.80%`, **`+20.47 pp`, 95% CI $[+17.70, +23.23]\text{ pp}$, $\mathrm{SE}_{\text{cl}} = 1.41\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{cl}} = 9.86 \times 10^{-48}$**) and `Unfiltered-FP32-Master` (`353/1,500 = 23.53%`, **`+27.73 pp`, 95% CI $[+24.66, +30.81]\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{cl}} = 8.79 \times 10^{-70}$**), while exhibiting **an unresolved difference** relative to `Standard-BF16` (`521/1,000 = 52.10%` vs. `503/1,000 = 50.30%` under matched `FP32LinearInBF16Net` on Seeds 102 & 42: **`+1.80 pp`, 95% CI $[-1.01, +4.61]\text{ pp}$, $\mathrm{SE}_{\text{cl}} = 1.43\text{ pp}$, $\mathrm{SE}_{\text{seed}} = 0.80\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{cl}} = 0.210$**; and `500/1,000 = 50.00%` vs. `511/1,000 = 51.10%` under matched `BF16`: **`-1.10 pp`, $p_{\text{cl}} = 0.409$**). For test-time voting on `Ext240` (Section 5.3), six 5–11 pass center-anchored, pure-sampled, and combined voting rules pass 30-test Holm-Bonferroni confirmation ($p_{\text{Holm,30}} \in [0.0198, 0.0455]$), whereas 3-pass and 5-point deterministic stencil gains ($p_{\text{cl}} = 0.0074\text{--}0.0326$ unadjusted) do not survive 30-test multiplicity correction ($p_{\text{Holm,30}} \ge 0.160$).
3. **Scope of the Rademacher Gate Control and Calibration Cost:** Our Rademacher control $g_{\text{rad},\ell} = \sigma_{\text{RMS},\ell} \cdot s_\ell$ uses zero gate-training steps for its $8,192$ coordinate signs $s_\ell \in \{-1, +1\}^{1024}$, but inherits the scalar layerwise RMS magnitude $\sigma_{\text{RMS},\ell} \approx 0.030\text{--}0.034$ calibrated by a 50-step ($400$-rollout) `GateOnly-Loop10_18` run. This demonstrates that **at the calibrated RMS scale, learned coordinate directions are not distinguishable from random Rademacher directions**, rather than claiming a calibration-free perturbation across arbitrary architectures.
4. **Training Spectral-Decomposition Work, Inference Work Proxies, Checkpoint Storage vs. Runtime VRAM, and Practical Method Selection:** During 50-step RLVR training (`21` active non-zero-variance optimizer steps on Seed 102, `20` on Seed 42, `17` on Seed 137), `Standard-BF16` and `Unfiltered-FP32-Master` require **`0` SVD factorizations** (`~11 min` per 50-step run on a single GPU); adapter methods (`GeoRA-r16`, `PiSSA-r16`, `MiLoRA-r16`) require **`196` initialization SVDs** (`~12 min` total); active-step `OSF-Top16` and `OSF-Tail16` execute $196$ Gram-eigendecomposition SVDs per active step plus $196$ final post-averaging SVDs (**$21 \times 196 + 196 = 4,312$ SVDs** on Seed 102, $4,116$ on Seed 42, $3,528$ on Seed 137; `~18 min` per run); `Ares` executes $196$ rank-64 gradient SVDs per active step (**$21 \times 196 = 4,116$ SVDs**; `~16 min` per run); and every-step synchronization/writeback controls (`OSF-EveryStepSync`, `OSF-Writeback`) execute **$50 \times 196 + 196 = 9,996$ SVDs** (`~28 min` per run). At inference, all multi-pass protocols multiply completion token and block-pass work ($2.99\times\text{--}3.61\times$ for 3-pass rules, $5.00\times\text{--}6.19\times$ for 5–6 pass rules), and the $1.50\text{ GB}$ figure refers to single-checkpoint `bfloat16` parameter storage (vs. $4.50\text{ GB}$ for a 3-checkpoint ensemble). Practically, when stochastic sampling is available at a 6-pass budget, `Center-Anchored 6-Pass` / `Pure 6-Pass` significantly outperforms the 5-point deterministic stencil ($+2.78\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{Holm,17}} = 1.56 \times 10^{-7}$); deterministic stencils are specifically advantageous when $T=0$ reproducibility is required or when combined with stochastic passes (`Hyb5`, `8-Pass`, `11-Pass`).
5. **Summary:** Together, our 21-stage state-dependent policy gradient, 5-branch optimizer, and mixed-dtype replay audits (Section 2.2 and Table 3A), our 12-cell confound-free post-hoc precision, component, and spectral subspace factorial (Section 2.3 and Table 4), our 3-seed $\times$ 6-panel Online Subspace-Filtered Policy Optimization (`OSF-RLVR`), pre/post-projection and every-step master-writeback controls, fixed-reference and own-completion greedy-prefix conditional KL / NSS tracking, and head-to-head comparisons with `Ares` (both Eq. 3 energy and amplitude rules), `GeoRA`, `PiSSA`, and `MiLoRA` (Sections 2.4–2.5 and Tables 5A–5C), our full 28-layer GPU KV-cache verification across $N=32$ real `MATH-500` 96-token prefixes ($2,048$ decoded token positions, Section 3), and our test-time voting diagnostics across within-MATH ($3,700$ evaluations) and untouched `Ext240` ($480$ evaluations) provide a unified mechanistic and interventional account of precision quantization, spectral subspace geometry, and trust-region stability in `bfloat16` RLVR.
