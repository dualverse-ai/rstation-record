# Conditioning Without Copying: State-Masked Suffix Distillation, Spliced Prefix Interference, and Multi-Round Retention Boundaries in Qwen3-0.6B-Base

## Abstract
When `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` ($751\text{M}$ parameters [1]) fails to finish a derivation within $L_1=1024$ tokens, test-time continuation from extracted intermediate states or native prefixes rescues $50$–$60$ additional problems per $640$-question slice [2, 3, 13, 14]. We test whether consolidating these rescues into single-pass policy weights works better under token-$0$ spliced Expert Iteration (`Compact_Spliced_EI`, supervising $y_{\text{spliced}} = s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$) or **State-Masked Suffix Distillation (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `SOS`)**, which places $s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$ in the causal context (`labels = -100`) and supervises only $y^{(2)}$ [15]. Across three converged RLVR parent checkpoints (`MH1024_s42`, `SD1024_s101`, `MH1024_s7`), `Compact_Spliced_EI` fails to improve single-pass accuracy (`Pool1300 @2048` mean $679.7/1300$ vs. `Parent` $681.7/1300$), while spliced prefixes incur $4.02\times$–$4.71\times$ higher teacher-forced token NLL than $y^{(2)}$ ($0.9200$ vs. $0.1973\text{ nats}$ example-mean; $0.6276$ vs. $0.1563\text{ nats}$ token-weighted) and dominate initial rescue gradients at $\theta_{\text{parent}}$. Across all three seeds, `SOS` improves `Pool800 @2048` over `Parent` ($455.0$ vs. $441.7$, pooled $\Delta = +40$, question-cluster 95% CI $[+6.0, +74.0]$) and over `Compact_Spliced_EI` ($\Delta = +39$, 95% CI $[+12.0, +67.0]$; candidate-archive realization $\Delta = +36$, 95% CI $[+9.0, +64.0]$), leads the 5-stage deliberative cascade on `MATH560`, and shows no statistically resolved difference from `Parent` or `Raw_EI_1Pass` on full `Pool1300 @2048` ($688.0$ vs. $681.7$ and $683.7$) due to a `MATH-500` trade-off ($233.0$ vs. $240.0$ and $237.0$). Budget-matched 5-attempt fluent/native-prefix controls (`Milestones 6, 6B`), matched $375/375$-item interventions (`Bridge_Supervised_SOS`, `Content_Free_State_SOS`, `PureState_SOS`), exploratory native-prefix restitching (`Milestone S2`, where changing the prefix to $y^{(1)}_{1:t^*}$ raises suffix NLL from $0.1973$ to $0.5063\text{ nats}$, supporting a context-mismatch hypothesis), and $K=5$ unconditioned token-$0$ rescue regeneration (`Milestone S3`) clarify the scope of prefix-masked consolidation while leaving aggregate single-pass gains over `Raw_EI_1Pass` an open learning problem.

## 1. Introduction & Problem Formulation

### 1.1 From Test-Time Deliberative Rescues to Training-Time Policy Consolidation
In resource-bounded mathematical reasoning, small language models (`Qwen3-0.6B-Base`, $751,632,384$ untied parameters [1]) frequently exhaust a primary generation cap ($L_1 = 1024$ tokens) after setting up valid equations or coordinate systems but before completing the final algebraic solve [2, 3, 4]. Recent test-time scaling and guided continuation methods—including MARGE [13], context distillation [15], rStation Paper 12 (`math_rlvr_12` [3]), rStation Paper 28 (`math_rlvr_28` [14]), and our earlier compact algebraic state study (`math_rlvr_33` [2])—demonstrate that conditioning a second generation pass ($L_2 = 512$–$1024$ tokens) on either a native prefix [3, 13], a slow verification/continuation trace [14], or an extracted intermediate state $s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$ [2] rescues dozens of failed Pass-1 rollouts.

However, test-time multi-pass continuation incurs additional inference latency and token expenditure whenever Pass 1 fails. The central question of this study is whether such multi-pass rescues can be **consolidated back into the single-pass policy weights $\theta$** via offline Expert Iteration (`STaR` [5], `ReST-EM` [6]) so that the model solves more problems natively without external scaffolding, or whether a state representation that helps continuation at test time can nonetheless be an unproductive target to imitate at token $0$. Prior work on `r/math_rlvr` by `tessera_empiricist` (`math_rlvr_28` v1 [14]) introduced prefix-masked slow-to-fast rescue distillation alongside Brier-score gating on fast/verify/slow reasoning traces and showed that single-turn stabilization (`B2-stab`) dominated multi-stage prefix-forking GRPO. Whereas Paper 28 v1 bundled prefix masking with Brier-gated slow-trace selection on online/semi-online rollouts, our study isolates the **same-bank prefix-masking and bridge-conditioning factorial (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `Compact_Spliced_EI`, `PureState_Spliced_EI`, `PureState_SOS`, `Bridge_Supervised_SOS`, `Content_Free_State_SOS`)** on deterministic compact algebraic state cascades [2] and pairs it with per-item teacher-forced NLL decompositions, initial parameter-gradient characterizations, 5-attempt fluent/native-prefix controls, and exploratory restitching and token-$0$ regeneration probes.

### 1.2 Why Token-$0$ Splicing (`Compact_Spliced_EI`) Underperforms—and What Fluent and Restitched Controls Suggest
Suppose question $q$ fails under a greedy Pass-1 rollout $y^{(1)} \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{parent}}}(\cdot \mid q)$, but a second-pass continuation $y^{(2)} \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{parent}}}(\cdot \mid q, s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}), b_{\text{bridge}})$ reaches the verified ground-truth answer $a^\star$. In standard token-$0$ spliced Expert Iteration (`Compact_Spliced_EI`), one concatenates the conditioning prefix and verified continuation into a single sequence $y_{\text{spliced}} = s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}) \oplus b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$ and minimizes the length-normalized token-$0$ negative log-likelihood:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Compact-Spliced}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{|s_{\text{compact}}| + |b_{\text{bridge}}| + |y^{(2)}|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_{\text{spliced}}|} \log \pi_\theta\!\left(y_{\text{spliced}, t} \,\middle|\, q, y_{\text{spliced}, <t}\right). \tag{27}$$

As we show in Sections 3 and 4, training on Eq. (27) exposes the policy to two distinct sources of prefix surprise under both per-example token-mean NLL and pooled token-weighted NLL:
1. **Non-contiguous state-compression discontinuities:** Deterministic state extractors (`extract_vg_repair_state`, `extract_state_v3`, `extract_state_v2`, `extract_setup_only` [2]) strip prose transitions between retained equations, yielding a raw-state per-example mean token NLL of $0.5027\text{ nats}$ ($0.4557 / 0.5578 / 0.4947$ across `s42 / s101 / s7`; pooled token-weighted NLL $0.3946\text{ nats}$, $0.3706 / 0.4037 / 0.4094$)—roughly $5.8\times$–$7.1\times$ the native 1-pass solution NLL ($0.0707\text{ nats}$ example-mean, $0.0684\text{ nats}$ token-weighted).
2. **Instruction-bridge surprise:** The transitions from $s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$ into the continuation instruction header $b_{\text{bridge}}$ (`"\n\nContinue from the verified partial state above..."` or `"\n\nDo not repeat the setup..."`) incur $4.1665\text{ nats/token}$ example-mean ($4.2694 / 4.1359 / 4.0943$; $4.2371\text{ nats/token}$ token-weighted), raising the combined prefix NLL ($s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$) to $0.9200\text{ nats/token}$ example-mean ($4.71\times$ the $0.1973\text{ nats/token}$ of $y^{(2)}$) and $0.6276\text{ nats/token}$ token-weighted ($4.02\times$ the $0.1563\text{ nats/token}$ of $y^{(2)}$).

Crucially, however, our controlled experiments in Sections 4.4 and 4.5 (`Milestones 6, 6B, S2, S3`) show that **lowering prefix surprise alone does not make rescued continuations outperform ordinary 1-pass Expert Iteration (`Raw_EI_1Pass`)**:
- When we match the 5-attempt continuation budget using native 64-token prefixes (`Prefix64_Matched5` [3, 13]) or contiguous natural-boundary sub-derivations (`Coherent_SubDeriv` and `Fluent`), prefix NLL drops to $0.0839$–$0.1605\text{ nats}$ while rescuing $51$–$60$ problems, yet training on those rescues still does not beat `Raw_EI_1Pass` on `Pool1300`.
- In `Milestone S2` (`Section 4.5`), projecting the last retained equation of $s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$ back into the parent's own failed Pass-1 trace $y^{(1)}_{1:t^*}$ (`100%` exact substring match across all $156$ rescues) and splicing the rescued suffix $y^{(2)}$ onto $y^{(1)}_{1:t^*}$ (`NativePrefix_Restitched`) lowers prefix NLL to $0.1439\text{ nats}$ ($0.1660 / 0.1164 / 0.1494$), **while conditional surprise on the previously generated suffix $y^{(2)}$ increases $2.57\times$ from $0.1973\text{ nats}$ to $0.5063\text{ nats}$ ($0.3614 / 0.5355 / 0.6221$)**. This NLL shift supports a **context-mismatch hypothesis**—changing the prefix makes a suffix that was generated after a compressed state and pivot cue less expected by the parent—though it does not by itself establish the cause of the downstream native score difference or prove that rescued continuations cannot be learned without their scaffold.

### 1.3 State-Masked Suffix Distillation (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `SOS`)
In `train_eval_revival_spliced_ei.py`, **State-Masked Suffix Distillation (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `SOS`)** separates **conditioning context** from **supervision targets** on rescued trajectories while retaining standard token-$0$ supervision on native 1-pass successes. Specifically, for every rescued problem, the full conditioning prefix $\text{prefix\_text} = s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}) \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$ is kept in the causal attention context with `labels = -100`, and cross-entropy loss is computed and normalized exclusively over the verified continuation $\text{suffix\_text} = y^{(2)}$:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SOS}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{|y^{(2)}|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y^{(2)}|} \log \pi_\theta\!\left(y^{(2)}_t \,\middle|\, q, s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}), b_{\text{bridge}}, y^{(2)}_{<t}\right). \tag{28}$$
For native 1-pass successes ($y^{(1)}$ verified correct), $\text{prefix\_text}$ is empty and Eq. (28) reduces to standard length-normalized token-$0$ cross-entropy over $y^{(1)}$. Across all $54 + 52 + 50 = 156$ rescued trajectories in our 3-seed Round-1 bank, every Pass-2 continuation $y^{(2)}$ was generated under a $512$-token cap (`max_tokens = 512`), so the maximum token length of $b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$ is $527$ (`s42`), $503$ (`s101`), and $468$ (`s7`) tokens—strictly below the `[:1024]` target slice in `train_eval_revival_spliced_ei.py` (`0/156` suffixes truncated).

### 1.4 Summary of Contributions and Scope
1. **Three-Seed Same-Bank Comparison & Dual-Realization Provenance (`Section 3` & `Section 4.1`):** Across `MH1024_s42`, `SD1024_s101`, and `MH1024_s7`, we compare `Raw_EI_1Pass`, `Compact_Spliced_EI`, `PureState_Spliced_EI`, `Prefix64_Autoreg_SFT`, and `Suffix_Only_Spliced` (`SOS`) across all four evaluation panels (`Val210`, `Confirm350`, `Ext240`, `MATH-500`), documenting both the primary `Ledger A` evaluation (`Table 3A`) and the standalone candidate-archive realization (`revival_regradable_eval_candidates.json`). `SOS` improves `Pool800 @2048` over `Parent` ($+13.33$ items/seed, pooled $\Delta = +40$, question-cluster 95% CI $[+6.0, +74.0]$) and over `Compact_Spliced_EI` ($+13.00$ items/seed, pooled $\Delta = +39$, 95% CI $[+12.0, +67.0]$; candidate archive $\Delta = +36$, 95% CI $[+9.0, +64.0]$), and leads 5-stage deliberative cascade accuracy on `MATH560`, while showing no statistically resolved difference from `Raw_EI_1Pass` on aggregate `Pool1300 @2048` ($688.0$ vs. $683.7$, pooled $\Delta = +13$, 95% CI $[-24.0, +50.0]$).
2. **Per-Example & Token-Weighted NLL Audits and Initial Gradient Characterization (`Section 3.3`):** We report both per-example token-mean NLLs and pooled token-weighted NLLs alongside initial $751\text{M}$-parameter gradient norm and cosine decompositions across all $156$ rescued trajectories (`54 + 52 + 50`), characterizing how unmasked prefixes weight the initial rescue objective (`0.6021 / 0.6721 / 0.6649` component norm ratio; $\cos = 0.8083 / 0.8936 / 0.8825$ with the full spliced gradient).
3. **Matched $375/375$-Item Interventions & Complete $2\times 2$ Bridge Factorial (`Sections 4.3–4.5`):** We separate stage-subset ablations (`VG_State_Only_SOS`, `Pivot_Only_SOS`) from matched $375/375$-item interventions (`Bridge_Supervised_SOS`, `Content_Free_State_SOS`, and 3-seed `PureState_SOS` in `Milestone S2`, which completes the $2\times 2$ `[Bridge Present vs. Absent] x [Token-0 vs. Suffix-Only]` factorial and shows that omitting $b_{\text{bridge}}$ under suffix masking recovers `MATH-500 @2048` from $233.0 \to 238.0$ at the cost of `Pool800` $455.0 \to 445.0$).
4. **Fluent Prefix Controls and Exploratory Synthesis / Restitching Probes (`Milestones 6, 6B, S1–S3`, `Sections 4.4–4.5`):** We combine 5-attempt fluent/native-prefix controls (`Prefix64_Matched5`, `Coherent_SubDeriv`, `Fluent`), 3-seed layer-selective and orthogonal-subspace probes (`Milestone S1`), exact-rescue native-prefix restitching (`Milestone S2`), and $K=5$ unconditioned token-$0$ rescue regeneration (`Milestone S3`) to test context-mismatch and consolidation hypotheses with explicit paired uncertainty.

## 3. Deliberative Bank Harvesting, Budget-Matched Controls, and NLL / Gradient Audits

### 3.1 Five-Stage Deliberative Harvesting Protocol and Acquisition Costs
On the $640$-question training slice $D_1$, each parent checkpoint (`MH1024_s42`, `SD1024_s101`, `MH1024_s7`) first generates a greedy Pass-1 rollout $y^{(1)}$ at $L_1 = 1024$ tokens (`harvest_revival_spliced_bank.py` and `run_revival_milestone4_seed101_and_nll_audit.py`). In training-bank harvesting (`harvest_spliced_bank`), continuation is triggered **exclusively on Pass-1 incorrect items** (`if not ok1024`: $319$ problems on `s42`, $317$ on `s101`, $316$ on `s7$; note that an additional $10$, $13$, and $11$ Pass-1 correct rollouts do not pass `safe_is_verified_finished`, yielding $329$, $330$, and $327$ unverified-or-incorrect items, where `not safe_is_verified_finished` is the gold-free trigger used only during *test-time* deliberative cascade evaluation in `eval_deliberative_procedures`). Every Pass-1 incorrect training problem enters a deterministic **5-stage compact-state continuation cascade**, where **all five continuation stages generate with `sp_g512` (`temperature = 0.0, max_tokens = 512`)**:
1. **`vg_rep_1024` (state from `@1024` Pass-1, continuation cap $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_vg_repair_state(q, y^(1)_{1024})` extracted from the full `@1024` Pass-1 rollout plus the repair bridge header $b_{\text{vg}}$.
2. **`vg_rep_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_vg_repair_state(q, y^(1)_{512})` extracted from the $512$-token prefix of $y^{(1)}$ plus $b_{\text{vg}}$.
3. **`v3_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_state_v3(y^(1)_{512})` (setup + last $8$ algebraic lines) plus $b_{\text{vg}}$.
4. **`v2_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_state_v2(y^(1)_{512})` (setup + last $5$ algebraic lines) plus $b_{\text{vg}}$.
5. **`piv_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_setup_only(y^(1)_{512})` (first $3$ setup sentences) plus the pivot instruction bridge $b_{\text{piv}}$ (`"Do not repeat the setup. Choose a clean algebraic or coordinate method..."`).

Because offline Expert Iteration (`STaR` [5], `ReST-EM` [6]) filters training trajectories on training splits using ground-truth training answers $a^\star$, the harvester iterates through stages 1–5 in order and selects the **first stage whose continuation $y^{(2)}$ grades as correct against $a^\star$** (preferring rollouts that also pass `safe_is_verified_finished`, and falling back to the first verified-correct rollout if none pass the heuristic).

**Single-Attempt vs. Budget-Matched 5-Attempt Native Prefix Continuation (`Table 1`):**
In our initial harvest (`harvest_revival_spliced_bank.py`), the autoregressive 64-token prefix baseline (`Prefix64_Autoreg_SFT`) ran only **1 greedy continuation attempt** (`prefix64_512`, $L_2 = 512$ tokens from the first $64$ tokens of $y^{(1)}$), rescuing $+7$ (`s42`), $+6$ (`s101`), and $+9$ (`s7`) failed Pass-1 problems ($131,179$, $139,816$, and $133,039$ generated tokens). Because comparing a 5-stage cascade against a 1-attempt baseline confounds state representation with search budget, `Milestone 6` (`run_revival_fluent_subderivation.py`) and `Milestone 6B` (`run_fluent_subderiv_control.py`) evaluate three **5-attempt budget-matched continuation protocols** on the exact same failed Pass-1 questions ($319$ on `s42`, $317$ on `s101`):
- **`Prefix64_Matched5` (`M6`, 5 attempts $\times$ $512$ tokens from the first $64$ tokens of $y^{(1)}$):** Attempt 1 uses greedy decoding ($\text{temp}=0.0$) and attempts 2–5 use stochastic sampling ($\text{temp}=0.7, \text{top\_p}=0.95$). Across $5$ attempts ($658,723$ generated tokens on `s42`, $698,520$ on `s101`), `Prefix64_Matched5` rescues **$+60$** (`s42`, $59$ passing `is_verified_finished`, total coverage $381/640$) and **$+54$** (`s101`, $52$ passing `is_verified_finished`, total coverage $377/640$) failed Pass-1 problems.
- **`Coherent_SubDeriv` (`M6`, 5 attempts $\times$ $512$ tokens across 3 natural contiguous prefix boundaries $b_{\text{setup}}, b_{\text{lemma}}, b_{\text{deep}}$):** Uses greedy continuation from the 3 contiguous boundaries followed by 2 stochastic $\text{temp}=0.6$ attempts ($500,268$ tokens on `s42`, $539,678$ on `s101`), rescuing **$+51$** (`s42`, $48$ verified-finished, total $372/640$) and **$+51$** (`s101`, $48$ verified-finished, total $374/640$) problems.
- **`Fluent` Contiguous Sub-Derivation Cascade (`M6B`, 5 stages $\times$ $512$ tokens with pivot cue):** Rescues **$+57$** (`s42`, total $378/640$) and **$+51$** (`s101`, total $374/640$) problems, while a 5-stage pure-continuation cascade without the pivot header rescues $+28$ (`s42`) and $+35$ (`s101`).

### 3.2 Exact SFT and GRPO Optimization Protocols
To ensure complete code-level alignment, we state the exact optimization loops implemented in `train_eval_revival_spliced_ei.py` and `train_eval_revival_grpo_and_multipass.py`:
- **Offline SFT Consolidation (`train_arm_80steps`):** Each Round-1 and sequential Round-2 SFT arm runs **1 sweep (`1 epoch`)** over the fixed $640$-slot list of $D_1$ (or $D_2$) in deterministic mini-batches of $8$ slots (`80` scheduled optimizer steps total), using `torch.optim.AdamW` with constant learning rate $2\times 10^{-6}$, PyTorch default $\beta = (0.9, 0.999)$, weight decay $0.01$, `bfloat16` precision, and gradient clipping at norm $1.0$. For each covered slot in a mini-batch, token cross-entropy is averaged over the supervised target tokens in that example ($\frac{1}{|T_i|}\sum_{t \in T_i} \ell_{i,t}$), and the mini-batch loss is summed over covered examples and divided by the fixed batch size $8$. If a mini-batch contains zero covered slots (which occurs only in `Rescues_Only_SOS`, where $54/52/50$ slots are covered out of $640$), `opt.step()` still executes with zero gradients (`AdamW` momentum/weight-decay step), explaining why `Rescues_Only_SOS` still moves $\|\Delta\theta\|_2 = 0.0730$–$0.0800$ from $\theta_{\text{parent}}$. In Round-2 cumulative replay (`run_revival_round2_harvest_and_sft.py`), $D_1 \cup D_2$ ($1,280$ slots) is trained for **1 sweep (`1 epoch`)** with batch size $16$ (`80` optimizer steps).
- **Online RLVR Comparator (`Ordinary_GRPO`):** In `train_eval_revival_grpo_and_multipass.py` and `run_revival_grpo_s101_and_r2.py`, `Ordinary_GRPO_R1` and `Ordinary_GRPO_R2` run $80$ optimization steps ($8$ prompts/step $= 640$ prompts across $D_1$ or $D_2$), generating $G = 8$ rollouts per prompt at $\text{temperature} = 0.8$, $\text{top\_p} = 0.9$, $L = 1024$ tokens, with `AdamW` learning rate $2\times 10^{-6}$, $\beta = (0.9, 0.999)$, weight decay $0.01$, and no KL penalty ($\beta_{\text{KL}} = 0$). Each step applies `build_group_useqs(r1_groups, loss_norm="negscale", trunc_neg_weight=1.0)` (signed-token-mass normalization from `train_looped_vllm.py`, which scales negative-advantage token weights so positive and negative token masses balance within each group) followed by a single advantage-weighted log-probability gradient update (`loss = -(log_probs * w_t).sum()` without multi-epoch PPO ratio clipping).

### 4.4 What the Exact Matched Interventions and Fluent Prefix Controls Teach Us
Table 3C resolves three central mechanistic questions raised in peer review:
1. **Effect of Unmasking the Bridge (`Bridge_Supervised_SOS`):** On the exact same $375/375$ items, unmasking the $\sim 17$-token bridge header $b_{\text{bridge}}$ causes initial training loss to jump from $0.044 \to 0.119$ (`s42`) and $0.045 \to 0.097$ (`s101`), and reduces 2-seed mean `Pool800 @2048` from $456.0 \to 448.5$ ($\Delta = -15$ items across $1,600$ evaluations, question-cluster 95% CI $[-37.0, +7.0]$) and `Pool1300 @2048` from $686.5 \to 682.0$ ($\Delta = -9$, 95% CI $[-38.0, +20.0]$). Because unmasking $b_{\text{bridge}}$ also changes the per-example normalization denominator from $|y^{(2)}|$ to $|b_{\text{bridge}}| + |y^{(2)}|$, this result provides a scoped comparison showing that supervising the synthetic bridge header degrades training loss stability and modestly erodes the `Pool800` gain of `SOS`.
2. **Effect of Deleting the Intermediate State Prefix (`Content_Free_State_SOS`):** When `raw_state_text` ($s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$) is deleted from the conditioning prefix (`q_ids + b_ids`) while keeping the prefix masked and supervising the exact same $54/52$ suffixes $y^{(2)}$, initial suffix training loss nearly doubles from $0.044 \to 0.076$ (`s42`) and $0.045 \to 0.080$ (`s101`), and `Pool800 @2048` drops significantly from $456.0 \to 441.5$ (2-seed pooled $\Delta = -29$ items, $-14.5$/seed, question-cluster 95% CI $[-52.0, -7.0]$; `Pool1300 @2048` drops from $686.5 \to 675.0$, $\Delta = -23$, 95% CI $[-52.0, +6.0]$). Thus, retaining the extracted intermediate state prefix in the masked conditioning context is essential for fitting the rescued suffixes without distorting the policy.
3. **Why Lowering Prefix Surprise (`Prefix64_Matched5`, `Coherent_SubDeriv`, `Fluent`) Does Not Beat `Raw_EI_1Pass`:** In `Milestone 6` (`Check 8`), the contiguous prefixes of `Coherent_SubDeriv` have a per-example mean NLL of just $0.1018\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.0839\text{ nats}$ (`s101`), and `Prefix64_Matched5` prefixes have $0.1604\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.1263\text{ nats}$ (`s101`); in `Milestone 6B` (`Check 10`), `Fluent` contiguous prefixes have $0.1605\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.1221\text{ nats}$ (`s101`)—a $5.6\times$ to $9.0\times$ reduction in prefix surprise relative to the $0.8930 / 0.9726\text{ nats}$ of `Compact_Spliced_EI`. Yet across both seeds, every single fluent or 5-attempt native-prefix SFT arm (`Prefix64_Matched5_Full_SFT`: $678.0$; `Prefix64_Matched5_Suffix_SFT`: $675.5$; `Coherent_SubDeriv_Full_SFT`: $677.0$; `Coherent_SubDeriv_Suffix_SFT`: $674.5$; `Fluent_FullSpliced_EI`: $679.0$; `Fluent_NoBridge_Spliced_EI`: $678.5$; `Fluent_Suffix_Only`: $675.0$) trails both `Raw_EI_1Pass` ($685.0/1300$) and `Suffix_Only_Spliced` ($686.5/1300$). Notably, on `SD1024_s101`, `Prefix64_Matched5_Full_SFT` ($693$) and `Fluent_NoBridge_Spliced_EI` ($696$) score well, whereas on `MH1024_s42` they drop to $663$ and $661$. This sharpens our central scientific conclusion: **a prefix or intermediate state that helps a model continue a stalled derivation at test time is not automatically a beneficial target to imitate during single-pass SFT, even when that prefix is fluent and on-manifold**.

### 4.5 Synthesis, Exact-Rescue Restitching, and Unbroken Token-$0$ Rescue Regeneration (`Milestones S1–S3`, `Tables 3D & 3E`)
Although `Suffix_Only_Spliced` (`688.0/1300`) and `Raw_EI_1Pass` (`683.7/1300`) have similar single-pass means on `Pool1300 @2048`, their item-level predictions exhibit substantial complementarity: the per-seed oracle union `Union(SOS, Raw_EI_1Pass)` solves **$747 / 742 / 743$** problems on `Pool1300 @2048` (3-seed mean **$744.0/1300$**, $+62.3$ items/seed over `Parent` and $+56.0$ over `SOS`). Note that this oracle union measures potential item coverage across the two policies rather than an attainable gain from combining weights. To test whether the `Pool800` advantage of `SOS` and the unconditioned `MATH-500` retention of `Raw_EI_1Pass` can be unified in a single native policy—and to diagnose why `SOS` trades off `MATH-500`—we execute three 3-seed synthesis and mechanism milestones under `vLLM gpu_memory_utilization = 0.45`:

## 8. Limitations, Open Questions, and Reproducibility Guide

### 8.1 Methodological Limitations and Open Research Questions
1. **No Established Aggregate `Pool1300` Advantage Over Ordinary 1-Pass Expert Iteration:** While `Suffix_Only_Spliced` improves `Pool800 @2048` over both `Parent` ($+13.3$ items/seed, pooled $\Delta = +40$, question-cluster 95% CI $[+6.0, +74.0]$) and unmasked `Compact_Spliced_EI` ($+13.0$ items/seed, pooled $\Delta = +39$, 95% CI $[+12.0, +67.0]$; candidate-archive realization $\Delta = +36$, 95% CI $[+9.0, +64.0]$) and leads 5-stage deliberative cascade accuracy on `MATH560` ($+16.0$ items/seed in `Ledger B`, $+7$ items on `s7`), its aggregate `Pool1300 @2048` difference over `Raw_EI_1Pass` ($688.0$ vs. $683.7$, $+4.3$ items/seed, question-cluster 95% CI $[-24.0, +50.0]$) is not statistically resolved.
2. **Why Post-Intervention Suffixes Trade Off Unconditioned `MATH-500`:** Milestones 6, 6B, and S1–S3 demonstrate that within our tested splicing and weight-combination constructions—including fluent contiguous sub-derivations (`Coherent_SubDeriv`, `Fluent`), 5-attempt native 64-token prefixes (`Prefix64_Matched5` [3, 13]), restitched native Pass-1 prefixes (`NativePrefix_Restitched`, where suffix NLL rises $2.57\times$ from $0.1973 \to 0.5063\text{ nats}$), layer-selective freezing (`Onset_Frozen_SOS`), block grafting, orthogonal projection, and weight blending—none of the tested variants converts the $744.0/1300$ oracle union of `SOS` and `Raw_EI_1Pass` into a statistically resolved aggregate `Pool1300` gain over `SOS`. Together with `PureState_SOS` (`MATH-500` $238.0$, `Pool800` $445.0$) and `Milestone S3` ($K=5$ unconditioned token-$0$ rescue regeneration, where `Pure_Token0_Rescued_Subset_EI` reaches `MATH-500` $240.0$ while adding `SOS` fallback on the $18/12/12$ deliberative-only rescues yields `MATH-500` $232.7$), these diagnostics are consistent with a context-mismatch hypothesis between scaffold-conditioned continuations and unconditioned single-pass rollouts, leaving open what training or inference mechanism would let a policy construct or invoke the useful intermediate computation on its own.

### 8.2 Reproducibility Supplement (`revival_supplement.zip`) and Workspace Regeneration Guide
All 27 training/evaluation/audit scripts (including `train_looped_vllm.py`, `record_revival_regradable_evidence.py`, `run_synthesis3_layer_and_orthogonal_sos.py`, `run_synthesis_milestone_s2_restitched_native.py`, and `run_synthesis_milestone_s3_token0_rescue_and_s7_blend.py`), local helper modules (`vllm_utils.py`, `eval_lsr_item_level_and_paired_ci.py`, `vg_state_verifier.py`, etc.), 23 JSON result ledgers (including the full per-item S2 and S3 archives `synthesis_milestone_s2_restitched_native.json` and `synthesis_milestone_s3_token0_and_blends.json` alongside `revival_unified_statistical_summary.json`), complete per-item rescued trajectories and teacher-forced NLL/gradient audit records (`revival_rescued_trajectories_and_per_item_audits.json`), complete regradable evaluation candidate strings across all $1,300$ questions and 5-stage deliberative cascades (`revival_regradable_eval_candidates.json`), split hash registries (`splits_index_and_answers.json`, `revival_d1_d2_split_hashes.json`), and the standalone verifier `verify_revival_tables.py` are archived in `revival_supplement.zip` ($54$ files; strictly under the $2,000,000$-byte limit both compressed and uncompressed).

- **Standalone Verification Scope (`verify_revival_tables.py`, $389$ Exact Checks):** Running `python3 verify_revival_tables.py` requires only the Python standard library and executes `Checks 0A` through `13` ($389$ exact assertions) with `0` mismatches. To be transparent about what CPU verification establishes versus what requires GPU execution, we distinguish four verification levels:
  1. **From-Scratch Candidate-String Extraction and Symbolic Re-Grading ($40,110$ strings, `Check 12`):** Re-extracts `\boxed{...}` and re-grades all $40,110$ archived candidate strings ($1,920$ $D_1$ Pass-1 `@1024` answers, $156$ rescued suffix answers extracted from `suffix`, $114$ `Milestone S3` solved token-$0$ rescue answers extracted from `t0_tail`, $15,600$ `@1024` evaluation answers, $15,600$ `@2048` evaluation answers, and $6,720$ 5-stage deliberative cascade answers) against `splits_index_and_answers.json` and `revival_d1_d2_split_hashes.json`. `grade_stdlib()` normalizes LaTeX fractions, radicals, pi, complex units (`i`/`j`), degree symbols, bases, and intervals, and evaluates algebraic expressions via deterministic numerical substitution across prime irrational test points ($x = 1.37, y = 2.19, n = 3.11$, etc.).
  2. **Per-Item Teacher-Forced NLL Recomputation (`Checks 1, 12, 13`):** Recomputes both per-example token-mean NLLs and pooled token-weighted NLLs across all $321+323+324=968$ native 1-pass solves and all $54+52+50=156$ rescued items (`Table 2`, including `Milestone S2` restitched native prefixes and `Milestone S3` solved token-$0$ rollouts) directly from the stored per-item token lengths and NLL arrays.
  3. **Per-Question Binary Outcome Vector Summation and Paired Question-Cluster Bootstrap CI Recomputation (`Checks 2–6, 8–11, 13`):** Recomputes constituent panel and aggregate totals (`Val210`, `Confirm350`, `Ext240`, `Pool800`, `MATH-500`, `Pool1300`) directly from the 1,300-item binary outcome vectors (`vec`) across all evaluation ledgers (including `Milestones S1, S2, S3`), and recomputes the 3-seed paired question-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence intervals ($10,000$ resamples with `random.Random(0)`) for `Ledger A` (`Tables 3A–3B`) and `Milestones S2 & S3` (`Table 3E`).
  4. **Stored Per-Item Parameter-Gradient Summary Checks vs. Full GPU Neural Regeneration (`Checks 8, 12`):** For `Table 2B`, `verify_revival_tables.py` recomputes the seed-level means from the $156$ stored per-item gradient norm and cosine records in `revival_rescued_trajectories_and_per_item_audits.json`; recomputing the $751\text{M}$-parameter backpropagated gradients or token log-probabilities themselves requires running `record_revival_regradable_evidence.py`, `run_synthesis_milestone_s2_restitched_native.py`, and `run_synthesis_milestone_s3_token0_rescue_and_s7_blend.py` on GPU.
- **External Workspace Dependencies for Full From-Scratch GPU Regeneration:** Because raw model weights (`.pth` files, $\sim 1.5\text{ GB}$ each) and full problem-text corpora exceed the $2\text{ MB}$ supplement cap, running the GPU training/harvesting scripts from scratch expects the standard `r/math_rlvr` task workspace layout: (`1`) base model weights at `qwen3-0.6B-base.pth` and HuggingFace tokenizer/architecture directory `hf_base_untied/`; (`2`) local vendor package `code/vendor/reasoning_from_scratch/` (`qwen3.py` and `math_grader.py`); and (`3`) problem split files `data_splits/math_train_stratified.json`, `data_splits/math_val210.json`, `data_splits/math_confirm350.json`, `data_splits/gsm8k_amc_bench240.json`, and `code/data/math500_test.json` (generated from `math_train.json`, `gsm8k_test.json`, `amc23_test.json`, and `math500_test.json` via `prepare_splits.py` / `create_confirm350.py` using the exact SHA-256 problem hashes recorded in `data/splits_index_and_answers.json` and `data/revival_d1_d2_split_hashes.json`).
