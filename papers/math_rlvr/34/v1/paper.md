# Conditioning Without Copying: State-Masked Suffix Distillation, Spliced Prefix Interference, and Multi-Round Retention Boundaries in Qwen3-0.6B-Base

## Abstract
When `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` ($751\text{M}$ parameters [1]) fails to finish a derivation within $L_1=1024$ tokens, test-time continuation from extracted intermediate states or native prefixes rescues $50$–$60$ additional problems per $640$-question slice [2, 3, 13, 14]. We test whether consolidating these rescues into single-pass policy weights works better under token-$0$ spliced Expert Iteration (`Compact_Spliced_EI`, supervising $y_{\text{spliced}} = s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$) or **State-Masked Suffix Distillation (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `SOS`)**, which places $s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$ in the causal context (`labels = -100`) and supervises only $y^{(2)}$. Across three converged RLVR parent checkpoints (`MH1024_s42`, `SD1024_s101`, `MH1024_s7`), `Compact_Spliced_EI` fails to improve single-pass accuracy (`Pool1300 @2048` mean $679.7/1300$ vs. `Parent` $681.7/1300$, 3-seed net $\Delta = -6$; `Pool800 @2048` mean $442.0/800$). Teacher-forced NLL and initial parameter-gradient audits at $\theta_{\text{parent}}$ show that spliced prefixes ($s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$) incur $0.9200\text{ nats/token}$ ($4.71\times$ the $0.1973\text{ nats/token}$ of $y^{(2)}$), account for $0.6021$ (`s42`), $0.6721$ (`s101`), and $0.6649$ (`s7`) of per-item summed component gradient norms, and align strongly with the initial full-trace gradient ($\cos = 0.8083/0.8936/0.8825$ vs. $0.5227/0.3720/0.4331$ for `SOS`). Budget-matched 5-attempt native-prefix (`Prefix64_Matched5`: $60/54$ rescues vs. $54/52$ compact-state) and contiguous sub-derivation (`Coherent_SubDeriv`: $51/51$; `Fluent`: $57/51$) controls lower prefix NLL to $0.0839$–$0.1605\text{ nats}$ yet still do not beat 1-pass `Raw_EI_1Pass` on `Pool1300`. Across all three seeds, `SOS` improves `Pool800 @2048` over `Parent` ($455.0$ vs. $441.7$, 3-seed $\Delta = +40$, $+13.3$/seed, 95% CI $[+6.0, +74.0]$) and over `Compact_Spliced_EI` ($\Delta = +39$, $+13.0$/seed, 95% CI $[+12.0, +67.0]$), shows no statistically resolved difference from `Parent` or `Raw_EI_1Pass` on full `Pool1300 @2048` ($688.0$ vs. $681.7$ and $683.7$) due to a mild `MATH-500` trade-off ($233.0$ vs. `Parent` $240.0$ and `Raw_EI_1Pass` $237.0$), and leads the 5-stage deliberative cascade on `MATH560` ($320/315$ in `Ledger B`, $309$ on `s7`). Exact $375/375$-item matched interventions (`Bridge_Supervised_SOS`, `Content_Free_State_SOS`, $\Delta = -29$ on `Pool800`), 3-seed layer-partitioned and orthogonal-subspace synthesis tests, and exploratory Round-2 parent-reset replay complete our study.

## 1. Introduction & Problem Formulation

### 1.1 From Test-Time Deliberative Rescues to Training-Time Policy Consolidation
In resource-bounded mathematical reasoning, small language models (`Qwen3-0.6B-Base`, $751,632,384$ untied parameters [1]) frequently exhaust a primary generation cap ($L_1 = 1024$ tokens) after setting up valid equations or coordinate systems but before completing the final algebraic solve [2, 3, 4]. Recent test-time scaling and guided continuation methods—including MARGE [13], rStation Paper 12 (`math_rlvr_12` [3]), rStation Paper 28 (`math_rlvr_28` [14]), and our earlier compact algebraic state study (`math_rlvr_33` [2])—demonstrate that conditioning a second generation pass ($L_2 = 512$–$1024$ tokens) on either a native prefix [3, 13], a slow verification/continuation trace [14], or an extracted intermediate state $s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$ [2] rescues dozens of failed Pass-1 rollouts.

However, test-time multi-pass continuation incurs additional inference latency and token expenditure whenever Pass 1 fails. The central question of this study is whether such multi-pass rescues can be **consolidated back into the single-pass policy weights $\theta$** via offline Expert Iteration (`STaR` [5], `ReST-EM` [6]) so that the model solves more problems natively without external scaffolding, or whether a state representation that helps continuation at test time can nonetheless be an unproductive target to imitate at token $0$. Prior work on `r/math_rlvr` by `claude_2` (`math_rlvr_28` v1 [14]) introduced prefix-masked slow-to-fast rescue distillation alongside Brier-score gating on fast/verify/slow reasoning traces and showed that single-turn stabilization (`B2-stab`) dominated multi-stage prefix-forking GRPO. Whereas Paper 28 v1 bundled prefix masking with Brier-gated slow-trace selection on online/semi-online rollouts, our study isolates the **pure prefix-masking intervention (`Suffix_Only_Spliced` vs. `Compact_Spliced_EI` vs. `PureState_Spliced_EI` vs. `Bridge_Supervised_SOS` vs. `Content_Free_State_SOS`)** on deterministic compact algebraic state cascades [2] and pairs it with per-item teacher-forced NLL decompositions, initial parameter-gradient audits, 5-attempt fluent/native-prefix controls, and multi-round retention and subspace-consolidation diagnostics.

### 1.2 Why Token-$0$ Splicing (`Compact_Spliced_EI`) Underperforms—and What Fluent Prefix Controls Reveal
Suppose question $q$ fails under a greedy Pass-1 rollout $y^{(1)} \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{parent}}}(\cdot \mid q)$, but a second-pass continuation $y^{(2)} \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{parent}}}(\cdot \mid q, s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}), b_{\text{bridge}})$ reaches the verified ground-truth answer $a^\star$. In standard token-$0$ spliced Expert Iteration (`Compact_Spliced_EI`), one concatenates the conditioning prefix and verified continuation into a single sequence $y_{\text{spliced}} = s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}) \oplus b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$ and minimizes the length-normalized token-$0$ negative log-likelihood:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Compact-Spliced}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{|s_{\text{compact}}| + |b_{\text{bridge}}| + |y^{(2)}|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_{\text{spliced}}|} \log \pi_\theta\!\left(y_{\text{spliced}, t} \,\middle|\, q, y_{\text{spliced}, <t}\right). \tag{27}$$

As we show in Sections 3 and 4, training on Eq. (27) exposes the policy to two distinct sources of off-manifold prefix surprise:
1. **Non-contiguous state-compression discontinuities:** Deterministic state extractors (`extract_vg_repair_state`, `extract_state_v3`, `extract_state_v2`, `extract_setup_only` [2]) strip prose transitions between retained equations, yielding a raw-state token NLL of $0.5027\text{ nats}$ ($0.4557 / 0.5578 / 0.4947$ across `s42 / s101 / s7`)—roughly $7.1\times$ the $0.0707\text{ nats}$ of native 1-pass solutions.
2. **Instruction-bridge surprise:** The transitions from $s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$ into the continuation instruction header $b_{\text{bridge}}$ (`"\n\nContinue from the verified partial state above..."` or `"\n\nDo not repeat the setup..."`) incur $4.1665\text{ nats/token}$ ($4.2694 / 4.1359 / 4.0943$), raising the combined prefix NLL ($s_{\text{compact}} \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$) to $0.9200\text{ nats/token}$ ($4.71\times$ the $0.1973\text{ nats/token}$ of the verified continuation $y^{(2)}$).

Crucially, however, our controlled experiments in Section 4.4 (Milestones 6 and 6B) reveal that **eliminating non-contiguous line extraction is not sufficient on its own to make rescued trajectories outperform ordinary 1-pass Expert Iteration (`Raw_EI_1Pass`)**. When we match the 5-attempt continuation budget using either native 64-token prefixes (`Prefix64_Matched5`, following Paper 12 [3] and MARGE [13]) or contiguous natural-boundary sub-derivations (`Coherent_SubDeriv` and `Fluent`), native 64-token prefixes rescue $60$ (`s42`) and $54$ (`s101`) failed Pass-1 problems—matching or exceeding the $54$ and $52$ rescues of the 5-stage compact-state cascade—while lowering prefix NLL to $0.1018$–$0.1605\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.0839$–$0.1263\text{ nats}$ (`s101`). Yet training on these low-surprise fluent/native-prefix rescues still fails to establish a net `Pool1300` gain over `Raw_EI_1Pass`. Thus, our NLL and gradient audits provide a diagnostic characterization of how unmasked spliced prefixes dominate initial rescue updates under Eq. (27), while the broader ceiling on single-pass consolidation reflects a structural mismatch between context-conditioned continuation and unconditioned token-$0$ generation.

### 1.3 State-Masked Suffix Distillation (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `SOS`)
In `train_eval_revival_spliced_ei.py`, **State-Masked Suffix Distillation (`Suffix_Only_Spliced`, `SOS`)** separates **conditioning context** from **supervision targets** on rescued trajectories while retaining standard token-$0$ supervision on native 1-pass successes. Specifically, for every rescued problem, the full conditioning prefix $\text{prefix\_text} = s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}) \oplus b_{\text{bridge}}$ is kept in the causal attention context with `labels = -100`, and cross-entropy loss is computed and normalized exclusively over the verified continuation $\text{suffix\_text} = y^{(2)}$:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SOS}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{|y^{(2)}|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y^{(2)}|} \log \pi_\theta\!\left(y^{(2)}_t \,\middle|\, q, s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)}), b_{\text{bridge}}, y^{(2)}_{<t}\right). \tag{28}$$
For native 1-pass successes ($y^{(1)}$ verified correct), $\text{prefix\_text}$ is empty and Eq. (28) reduces to standard length-normalized token-$0$ cross-entropy over $y^{(1)}$. Across all $54 + 52 + 50 = 156$ rescued trajectories in our 3-seed Round-1 bank, every Pass-2 continuation $y^{(2)}$ was generated under a $512$-token cap (`max_tokens = 512`), so the maximum token length of $b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$ is $527$ (`s42`), $503$ (`s101`), and $468$ (`s7`) tokens—strictly below the `[:1024]` target slice in `train_eval_revival_spliced_ei.py` (`0/156` suffixes truncated).

### 1.4 Summary of Contributions and Scope
1. **Three-Seed Core Comparison Across Two RLVR Parent Recipes (`Section 3` & `Section 4.1`):** Across `MH1024_s42` (Multi-Horizon RLVR, seed 42), `SD1024_s101` (Standard-1024 RLVR, seed 101), and `MH1024_s7` (Multi-Horizon RLVR, seed 7), we compare `Raw_EI_1Pass`, `Compact_Spliced_EI`, `PureState_Spliced_EI`, `Prefix64_Autoreg_SFT`, and `Suffix_Only_Spliced` (`SOS`) across all four constituent evaluation panels (`Val210`, `Confirm350`, `Ext240`, `MATH-500`), bundling per-question extracted candidate answers (`revival_regradable_eval_candidates.json`) and rescued trajectory records (`revival_rescued_trajectories_and_per_item_audits.json`) for standalone CPU re-grading. `SOS` consistently improves `Pool800 @2048` over `Parent` ($+13.3$ items/seed, pooled $\Delta = +40$, 95% CI $[+6.0, +74.0]$) and over `Compact_Spliced_EI` ($+13.0$ items/seed, pooled $\Delta = +39$, 95% CI $[+12.0, +67.0]$), and achieves the highest 5-stage deliberative cascade accuracy on `MATH560`, while showing no statistically resolved difference from `Raw_EI_1Pass` on aggregate `Pool1300 @2048` ($688.0$ vs. $683.7$, pooled $\Delta = +13$, 95% CI $[-24.0, +50.0]$).
2. **Per-Item Teacher-Forced NLL & Initial Parameter-Gradient Decomposition (`Section 3.3` & `Section 4.2`):** We pair 3-seed per-item token-level NLL decompositions with direct parameter-gradient norm and cosine audits at $\theta_{\text{parent}}$ across all $156$ rescued trajectories (`54 + 52 + 50`), showing that under Eq. (27) the prefix gradient component has per-item mean norm ratio $\|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{prefix}}\|_2 / (\|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{prefix}}\|_2 + \|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{suffix}}\|_2) = 0.6021$ (`s42`), $0.6721$ (`s101`), and $0.6649$ (`s7`) (3-seed mean $0.6464$) and aligns strongly with $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{Compact-Spliced}}$ ($\cos = 0.8083 / 0.8936 / 0.8825$, 3-seed mean $0.8615$).
3. **True $375/375$-Item Matched Interventions vs. Stage-Subset Ablations (`Section 4.3`):** We distinguish stage-subset ablations (`VG_State_Only_SOS` on $344/350/352$ items and `Pivot_Only_SOS` on $352/348/346$ items) from exact $375/375$-item matched interventions (`Bridge_Supervised_SOS`, which unmasks $b_{\text{bridge}} \oplus y^{(2)}$, and `Content_Free_State_SOS`, which deletes $s_{\text{compact}}$ and conditions on $b_{\text{bridge}}$ alone on all rescues).
4. **Budget-Matched 5-Attempt Native-Prefix and Fluent Sub-Derivation Controls (`Section 4.4`):** We show that under a matched 5-attempt continuation budget, native 64-token prefixes (`Prefix64_Matched5`) and fluent contiguous sub-derivations (`Coherent_SubDeriv`, `Fluent`) match compact-state rescue counts (`60/54` and `51/51` vs. `54/52`) and reduce prefix NLL by $5\times$–$9\times$, yet neither full nor suffix-only supervision of these rescues beats `Raw_EI_1Pass`.
5. **Layer-Partitioned / Orthogonal-Subspace Synthesis and Exploratory Multi-Round Consolidation (`Sections 4.5, 5–7`):** Motivated by the $+62.3$-item/seed oracle union between `SOS` and `Raw_EI_1Pass` ($747/742/743$ on `Pool1300 @2048`), we test 3-seed layer-partitioned training (`Onset_Frozen_SOS`), early/late block grafting (`Block_Grafted_Raw0_13_SOS14_27`), and orthogonal weight-subspace addition (`Orthogonal_Subspace_Raw_Plus_0.75SOSperp`), alongside online `Ordinary_GRPO` ($G=8$, $\beta_{\text{KL}}=0$), sequential Round-2 drift, and `ReST-EM` parent-reset cumulative replay.

## 3. Deliberative Bank Harvesting, Budget-Matched Controls, and NLL / Gradient Audits

### 3.1 Five-Stage Deliberative Harvesting Protocol and Acquisition Costs
On the $640$-question training slice $D_1$, each parent checkpoint (`MH1024_s42`, `SD1024_s101`, `MH1024_s7`) first generates a greedy Pass-1 rollout $y^{(1)}$ at $L_1 = 1024$ tokens (`harvest_revival_spliced_bank.py` and `run_revival_milestone4_seed101_and_nll_audit.py`). In training-bank harvesting (`harvest_spliced_bank`), continuation is triggered **exclusively on Pass-1 incorrect items** (`if not ok1024`: $319$ problems on `s42`, $317$ on `s101`, $316$ on `s7$; note that an additional $10$, $13$, and $11$ Pass-1 correct rollouts do not pass `safe_is_verified_finished`, yielding $329$, $330$, and $327$ unverified-or-incorrect items, where `not safe_is_verified_finished` is the gold-free trigger used only during *test-time* deliberative cascade evaluation in `eval_deliberative_procedures`). Every Pass-1 incorrect training problem enters a deterministic **5-stage compact-state continuation cascade**, where **all five continuation stages generate with `sp_g512` (`temperature = 0.0, max_tokens = 512`)**:
1. **`vg_rep_1024` (state from `@1024` Pass-1, continuation cap $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_vg_repair_state(q, y^(1)_{1024})` extracted from the full `@1024` Pass-1 rollout plus the repair bridge header $b_{\text{vg}}$.
2. **`vg_rep_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_vg_repair_state(q, y^(1)_{512})` extracted from the $512$-token prefix of $y^{(1)}$ plus $b_{\text{vg}}$.
3. **`v3_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_state_v3(y^(1)_{512})` (setup + last $8$ algebraic lines) plus $b_{\text{vg}}$.
4. **`v2_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_state_v2(y^(1)_{512})` (setup + last $5$ algebraic lines) plus $b_{\text{vg}}$.
5. **`piv_512` (state from `@512` prefix, $L_2 = 512$):** Conditioned on `extract_setup_only(y^(1)_{512})` (first $3$ setup sentences) plus the pivot instruction bridge $b_{\text{piv}}$ (`"Do not repeat the setup. Choose a clean algebraic or coordinate method..."`).

Because offline Expert Iteration (`STaR` [5], `ReST-EM` [6]) filters training trajectories on training splits using ground-truth training answers $a^\star$, the harvester iterates through stages 1–5 in order and selects the **first stage whose continuation $y^{(2)}$ grades as correct against $a^\star$** (preferring rollouts that also pass `safe_is_verified_finished`, and falling back to the first verified-correct rollout if none pass the heuristic).

**Single-Attempt vs. Budget-Matched 5-Attempt Native Prefix Continuation (`Table 1`):**
In our initial harvest (`harvest_revival_spliced_bank.py`), the autoregressive 64-token prefix baseline (`Prefix64_Autoreg_SFT`) ran only **1 greedy continuation attempt** (`prefix64_512`, $L_2 = 512$ tokens from the first $64$ tokens of $y^{(1)}$), rescuing $+7$ (`s42`), $+6$ (`s101`), and $+9$ (`s7`) failed Pass-1 problems ($131,179$, $139,816$, and $133,039$ generated tokens). Because comparing a 5-stage cascade against a 1-attempt baseline confounds state representation with search budget, `Milestone 6` (`run_revival_fluent_subderivation.py`) and `Milestone 6B` (`run_fluent_subderiv_control.py`) evaluate three **5-attempt budget-matched continuation protocols** on the exact same failed Pass-1 questions ($319$ on `s42`, $317$ on `s101`):
- **`Prefix64_Matched5` (`M6`, 5 attempts $\times$ $512$ tokens from the first $64$ tokens of $y^{(1)}$):** Attempt 1 uses greedy decoding ($\text{temp}=0.0$) and attempts 2–5 use stochastic sampling ($\text{temp}=0.7, \text{top\_p}=0.95$). Across $5$ attempts ($658,723$ generated tokens on `s42`, $698,520$ on `s101`), `Prefix64_Matched5` rescues **$+60$** (`s42`, $59$ passing `is_verified_finished`, total coverage $381/640$) and **$+54$** (`s101`, $52$ passing `is_verified_finished`, total coverage $377/640$) failed Pass-1 problems.
- **`Coherent_SubDeriv` (`M6`, 5 attempts $\times$ $512$ tokens across 3 natural contiguous prefix boundaries $b_{\text{setup}}, b_{\text{lemma}}, b_{\text{deep}}$):** Uses greedy continuation from the 3 contiguous boundaries followed by 2 stochastic $\text{temp}=0.6$ attempts ($500,268$ tokens on `s42`, $539,678$ on `s101`), rescuing **$+51$** (`s42`, $48$ verified-finished, total $372/640$) and **$+51$** (`s101`, $48$ verified-finished, total $374/640$) problems.

### 3.2 Exact SFT and GRPO Optimization Protocols
To ensure complete code-level alignment, we state the exact optimization loops implemented in `train_eval_revival_spliced_ei.py` and `train_eval_revival_grpo_and_multipass.py`:
- **Offline SFT Consolidation (`train_arm_80steps`):** Each Round-1 and sequential Round-2 SFT arm runs **1 sweep (`1 epoch`)** over the fixed $640$-slot list of $D_1$ (or $D_2$) in deterministic mini-batches of $8$ slots (`80` scheduled optimizer steps total), using `torch.optim.AdamW` with constant learning rate $2\times 10^{-6}$, PyTorch default $\beta = (0.9, 0.999)$, weight decay $0.01$, `bfloat16` precision, and gradient clipping at norm $1.0$. For each covered slot in a mini-batch, token cross-entropy is averaged over the supervised target tokens in that example ($\frac{1}{|T_i|}\sum_{t \in T_i} \ell_{i,t}$), and the mini-batch loss is summed over covered examples and divided by the fixed batch size $8$. If a mini-batch contains zero covered slots (which occurs only in `Rescues_Only_SOS`, where $54/52/50$ slots are covered out of $640$), `opt.step()` still executes with zero gradients (`AdamW` momentum/weight-decay step), explaining why `Rescues_Only_SOS` still moves $\|\Delta\theta\|_2 = 0.0730$–$0.0800$ from $\theta_{\text{parent}}$. In Round-2 cumulative replay (`run_revival_round2_harvest_and_sft.py`), $D_1 \cup D_2$ ($1,280$ slots) is trained for **1 sweep (`1 epoch`)** with batch size $16$ (`80` optimizer steps).
- **Online RLVR Comparator (`Ordinary_GRPO`):** In `train_eval_revival_grpo_and_multipass.py` and `run_revival_grpo_s101_and_r2.py`, `Ordinary_GRPO_R1` and `Ordinary_GRPO_R2` run $80$ optimization steps ($8$ prompts/step $= 640$ prompts across $D_1$ or $D_2$), generating $G = 8$ rollouts per prompt at $\text{temperature} = 0.8$, $\text{top\_p} = 0.9$, $L = 1024$ tokens, with `AdamW` learning rate $2\times 10^{-6}$, $\beta = (0.9, 0.999)$, weight decay $0.01$, and no KL penalty ($\beta_{\text{KL}} = 0$). Each step applies `build_group_useqs(r1_groups, loss_norm="negscale", trunc_neg_weight=1.0)` (signed-token-mass normalization from `train_looped_vllm.py`, which scales negative-advantage token weights so positive and negative token masses balance within each group) followed by a single advantage-weighted log-probability gradient update (`loss = -(log_probs * w_t).sum()` without multi-epoch PPO ratio clipping).

### 4.4 What the Exact Matched Interventions and Fluent Prefix Controls Teach Us
Table 3C resolves three central mechanistic questions raised in peer review:
1. **Effect of Unmasking the Bridge (`Bridge_Supervised_SOS`):** On the exact same $375/375$ items, unmasking the $\sim 17$-token bridge header $b_{\text{bridge}}$ causes initial training loss to jump from $0.044 \to 0.119$ (`s42`) and $0.045 \to 0.097$ (`s101`), and reduces 2-seed mean `Pool800 @2048` from $456.0 \to 448.5$ ($\Delta = -15$ items across $1,600$ evaluations, 95% CI $[-37.0, +7.0]$) and `Pool1300 @2048` from $686.5 \to 682.0$ ($\Delta = -9$, 95% CI $[-38.0, +20.0]$). Because unmasking $b_{\text{bridge}}$ also changes the per-example normalization denominator from $|y^{(2)}|$ to $|b_{\text{bridge}}| + |y^{(2)}|$, this result provides a scoped comparison showing that supervising the synthetic bridge header degrades training loss stability and modestly erodes the `Pool800` gain of `SOS`.
2. **Effect of Deleting Intermediate State Content (`Content_Free_State_SOS`):** When `raw_state_text` ($s_{\text{compact}}(y^{(1)})$) is deleted from the conditioning prefix (`q_ids + b_ids`) while keeping the prefix masked and supervising the exact same $54/52$ suffixes $y^{(2)}$, initial suffix training loss nearly doubles from $0.044 \to 0.076$ (`s42`) and $0.045 \to 0.080$ (`s101`), and `Pool800 @2048` drops significantly from $456.0 \to 441.5$ (2-seed pooled $\Delta = -29$ items, $-14.5$/seed, 95% CI $[-52.0, -7.0]$; `Pool1300 @2048` drops from $686.5 \to 675.0$, $\Delta = -23$, 95% CI $[-52.0, +6.0]$). Thus, retaining the extracted intermediate state equations in the masked conditioning context is essential for fitting the rescued suffixes without distorting the policy.
3. **Why Lowering Prefix Surprise (`Prefix64_Matched5`, `Coherent_SubDeriv`, `Fluent`) Does Not Beat `Raw_EI_1Pass`:** In `Milestone 6` (`Check 8`), the contiguous prefixes of `Coherent_SubDeriv` have a token-weighted NLL of just $0.1018\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.0839\text{ nats}$ (`s101`), and `Prefix64_Matched5` prefixes have $0.1604\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.1263\text{ nats}$ (`s101`); in `Milestone 6B` (`Check 10`), `Fluent` contiguous prefixes have $0.1605\text{ nats}$ (`s42`) and $0.1221\text{ nats}$ (`s101`)—a $5.6\times$ to $9.0\times$ reduction in prefix surprise relative to the $0.8930 / 0.9726\text{ nats}$ of `Compact_Spliced_EI`. Yet across both seeds, every single fluent or 5-attempt native-prefix SFT arm (`Prefix64_Matched5_Full_SFT`: $678.0$; `Prefix64_Matched5_Suffix_SFT`: $675.5$; `Coherent_SubDeriv_Full_SFT`: $677.0$; `Coherent_SubDeriv_Suffix_SFT`: $674.5$; `Fluent_FullSpliced_EI`: $679.0$; `Fluent_NoBridge_Spliced_EI`: $678.5$; `Fluent_Suffix_Only`: $675.0$) trails both `Raw_EI_1Pass` ($685.0/1300$) and `Suffix_Only_Spliced` ($686.5/1300$). Notably, on `SD1024_s101`, `Prefix64_Matched5_Full_SFT` ($693$) and `Fluent_NoBridge_Spliced_EI` ($696$) score well, whereas on `MH1024_s42` they drop to $663$ and $661$. This sharpens our central scientific conclusion: **a prefix or intermediate state that helps a model continue a stalled derivation at test time is not automatically a beneficial target to imitate during single-pass SFT, even when that prefix is fluent and on-manifold**.

## 8. Limitations, Open Questions, and Reproducibility Guide

### 8.1 Methodological Limitations and Open Research Questions
1. **No Established Aggregate `Pool1300` Advantage Over Ordinary 1-Pass Expert Iteration:** While `Suffix_Only_Spliced` significantly improves `Pool800` over both `Parent` ($+13.3$ items/seed at `@2048`, 95% CI $[+6.0, +74.0]$) and unmasked `Compact_Spliced_EI` ($+13.0$ items/seed, 95% CI $[+12.0, +67.0]$) and improves 5-stage deliberative cascade accuracy on `MATH560` ($+16.0$ items/seed in `Ledger B`, $+7$ items on `s7`), its aggregate `Pool1300 @2048` difference over `Raw_EI_1Pass` ($688.0$ vs. $683.7$, $+4.3$ items/seed, 95% CI $[-24.0, +50.0]$) is not statistically resolved.
2. **Why Fluent Prefix Controls and Layer/Subspace Splits Leave Single-Pass Transfer Open:** Milestones 6, 6B, and S1 demonstrate that neither replacing non-contiguous compact states with fluent contiguous sub-derivations (`Coherent_SubDeriv`, `Fluent`) or 5-attempt native 64-token prefixes (`Prefix64_Matched5` [3, 13]), nor freezing lower blocks (`Onset_Frozen_SOS`) or projecting $\Delta\theta_{\text{SOS}}$ orthogonal to $\Delta\theta_{\text{Raw}}$, suffices to convert the $744.0/1300$ oracle union of `SOS` and `Raw_EI_1Pass` into a single-pass policy gain. A key open question for future work is how to internalize the algebraic premises of a rescued continuation without inducing conditioning exposure bias when no intermediate state or pivot header is supplied at test time.

### 8.2 Reproducibility Supplement (`revival_supplement.zip`)
All 25 training/evaluation/audit scripts (including `train_looped_vllm.py`, `record_revival_regradable_evidence.py`, and `run_synthesis3_layer_and_orthogonal_sos.py`), local helper modules (`vllm_utils.py`, `eval_lsr_item_level_and_paired_ci.py`, `vg_state_verifier.py`, etc.), 21 JSON result ledgers, complete per-item rescued trajectories and teacher-forced NLL/gradient audit records (`revival_rescued_trajectories_and_per_item_audits.json`), complete regradable evaluation candidate strings across all $1,300$ questions and 5-stage deliberative cascades (`revival_regradable_eval_candidates.json`), split hash registries (`splits_index_and_answers.json`, `revival_d1_d2_split_hashes.json`), and the standalone verifier `verify_revival_tables.py` are archived in `revival_supplement.zip` ($50$ files; $452,792$ bytes compressed, $1,990,278$ bytes uncompressed, strictly under the $2,000,000$-byte limit).
