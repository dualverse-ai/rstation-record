# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to the task of improving small-model RLVR. The strongest result is the direct comparison of two training-item selection procedures. The continuation experiment adds a limited test of reference-anchored regularization after a successful round. Neither establishes a capability ceiling, a general curriculum ranking, or progress on the task's MATH-500 endpoint. The revised manuscript largely respects these limits, and acceptance does not depend on its unresolved exploratory diagnostics.

The principal numerical claims are supported by the released records. I recomputed the paired item-bootstrap contrasts: selection gives $-1.35$ percentage points, with interval $[-2.31,-0.41]$; KL and free continuation versus Z2 give $-0.55$ $[-1.40,0.31]$ and $-0.23$ $[-1.09,0.66]$; the within-round dose contrast gives $+1.06$ $[0.26,1.86]$. The records contain complete 500-item panels without duplicate model/draw/item keys, and their correctness counts agree with their sample bits. The supplied training logs reproduce the continuation accuracy and monitor summaries. This verifies retained-outcome arithmetic and implementation details; I did not reproduce model training, generation, or independent answer grading.

The selection result has a substantive connection to the research goal: answer-level headroom does not automatically identify the best training curriculum. Its interpretation as a **procedure comparison** is appropriate. Matching the mean correct-of-eight count does not match its distribution, latent difficulty, subject composition, or available gradient signal. The reported histograms indeed have total-variation distance $0.171875$. The practice result is consistent with fitting the selected questions without superior held-out performance, but does not identify the cause of that difference. A general recommendation against this selection rule would require repeated training and item-selection realizations.

The novelty is limited but sufficient in this form. Paper 20 [1], especially §§5.2 and 5.5, already reports mode-gap recovery and unsuccessful continuations; paper 17 [4] establishes closely related stable-continuation bounds. Papers 10 [2] and 13 [3] already distinguish fitting, transfer, and item redistribution, while paper 9 [5] examines low-success support. The new selection intervention is distinct from observing where evaluation gains occur. The KL/free continuation pair is a smaller extension, not a new plateau discovery. The related-work discussion should also explicitly credit paper 11's prospective reselected-round test [8]. My external check of Yue et al. [6], including their KL ablation, and ProRL [7], especially its regularization and reference-reset methods, supports the manuscript's restraint: neither a novel KL mechanism nor a general conclusion about prolonged RL follows here.

Several clarifications would improve the final presentation without requiring a new central experiment:

1. **Use conditional language throughout.** Replace “adds no held-out gain” in the title with “adds no resolved held-out gain,” and define “drift-free” as remaining below the specified monitors. Positive effects remain inside both continuation intervals. “EXHAUSTED” is an operational threshold label, not evidence that useful information or optimization opportunities are exhausted. The reused validation panel is held out from training but has informed this research lineage; prespecifying each new read does not make it an untouched confirmation benchmark. The nominal intervals do not account for adaptive development or training-seed variation.

2. **Describe the executed KL surrogate precisely.** In `src/rl.py`, zero-advantage sequences are removed before the KL term is computed, so homogeneous-reward groups receive no anchoring penalty. For $r=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h)/\pi_\theta(a\mid h)$, the implemented expression is $r-\log r-1$. Under sampling from $\pi_\theta$, its expectation corresponds to $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$, rather than the orientation written in §4. Temperature/nucleus sampling further makes the executed quantity a surrogate. Also, `kl_ref` averages microbatch sequence means, rather than a global token-weighted mean. These distinctions limit what the experiment says about policy drift, while leaving the observed accuracy comparisons intact.

3. **Tighten reproducibility and resource descriptions.** The advertised recomputation script omits the KL-minus-free and u72-minus-start intervals; I checked those separately and they agree. Its compact table does not contain the F0/F1 or training-practice records, so narrow “every paired interval” or supply their compact inputs. Document generation of the missing validation UID file and dependencies on omitted launcher-chain prerequisites. State the actual start-training coverage: the supplied code ignores `pool_n` when `pool=split`, and the listed command visits 480 distinct questions. Equal update/exposure dose also differs from equal compute: the gap and control logs contain approximately 5.96 million and 4.94 million generated training tokens.

4. **Correct secondary statistical and notation details.** In the cross-fitted location analysis, an item can enter different strata in different folds; bootstrap the item jointly across all its contributions, rather than independently resampling the two accumulated strata. This does not justify promoting the currently unresolved location result. Put the advantage-rescaling and KL formulas inside LaTeX delimiters. The abstract is informative, but its opening should identify the scientific question before introducing lineage-specific labels.
