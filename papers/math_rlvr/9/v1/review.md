# Accept

This is a careful, useful bounded negative result for the supplied task. The central empirical result is supported: across five prespecified routes on the 148 validation items selected as 0/8 by the control, no arm clears the frozen +0.03 pass@8 criterion, and the four information contrasts pool to about +0.008 pass@8 with a wide interval (approximately $[-0.030,0.046]$). The fresh-pool design avoids comparing against the pool used to select the tail, and the second SFT seed usefully shows that the apparent +12-item MIX result does not replicate (−3 items). The retained ledger data and readout code reproduce the reported val, MATH-500 descriptive, pooled, exchangeability, and payoff quantities within rounding. The implementation inspection also supports the claimed mechanisms: reference SFT changes the response format, sub-goal credit is coupled to EOS termination, and the self-check arm transfers check form much more readily than correction ability.

The scope is appropriately limited to one 0.6B model, one control, one form of each intervention, and modest seeds. The paper is candid that the single-contrast intervals do not exclude effects around 0.08 pass@8 and that the post hoc analyses are not universal impossibility proofs. This qualification is essential. The work adds a focused ledger and payoff analysis to the related plateau and self-distillation studies [2,3], rather than claiming that all off-policy guidance, retrieval, or self-correction methods fail. The comparison with LUFFY and related guidance work is relevant, and the distinction that those methods use different guidance/training regimes is clear. The abstract is self-contained and reports the question, interventions, quantitative outcomes, and limits.

Several qualifications should be made explicit in revision. First, “do not move” and “could not have paid” in the title and abstract read more broadly than the evidence. The payoff calculation is conditional on the observed $f(k)$ curve and on moved items behaving like items that natively have count $k$; an intervention could preferentially move unusually valuable tail items. Rephrase this as “did not move under these five implementations” and “would have yielded little under the observed conditional curve.” Likewise, the +0.03 rule is a preregistered decision threshold, not evidence that effects below it are absent.

Second, the pooled four-contrast estimate is a descriptive synthesis of heterogeneous interventions. Equal weighting and an item bootstrap are reasonable for the stated ledger summary, but the shared items, common random-number streams, and different training/evaluation changes mean it should not be presented as a single common treatment effect. The CRN-preserving null is a useful sensitivity analysis; report its exact intervals alongside the main pooled interval and avoid treating exchangeability $P$ values as proof that all format-preserving arms share one distribution.

Third, the novelty boundary with paper 7 should be sharper. That paper already contains a self-repair/checking SFT arm and a related difficulty-profile analysis. State precisely that P1 differs in its control-generated after-answer data, target-bin ledger, and preregistered replication, and identify which conclusions are genuinely new. The same distinction should be made for the expert-iteration comparisons with paper 6.

Fourth, the reference-SFT comparison has a large exposure and format confound: R3000 trains roughly 1.36M completion tokens versus roughly 2.92M for EI, while MIX is separately exposure-matched only through a repeated-own-data control. The manuscript acknowledges this, but the conclusion should say that plain human-reference SFT was not competitive under this recipe, not that human information itself is ineffective. Retrieval also changes topic similarity as well as method similarity; the reported negative result therefore applies to the specified TF-IDF example construction.

Finally, retain the reproducibility details that make this negative result valuable: the exact batch-64 evaluation rule, the known batch dependence of greedy decoding, the seed floors, and the fact that MATH-500 endpoint results are descriptive after the preregistered gates. With those wording and attribution changes, the experiments provide a credible, inspectable partial answer to the open-ended RLVR problem and a useful guide toward testing transfer on the middle-probability band.
