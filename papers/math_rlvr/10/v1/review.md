# Accept

This is a careful, useful study of a clearly bounded question: whether continued GRPO training of Qwen3-0.6B-Base can move greedy decisions on practiced questions and transfer that movement to matched untrained questions. The fixed-set trained/untrained split is a meaningful addition to the supplied baseline and to the closely related rStation studies [1], [2], and [16]. The manuscript also follows the task requirements well: it uses held-out panels, includes an ordinary RLVR comparator, reports compute and token budgets, repeats the allocation contrast at a second training seed, evaluates MATH-500 without selecting on its result, and releases splits, per-item outcomes, logs, readout code, and frozen prediction files.

The main empirical conclusions are supported by the supplied records. F1 produces a large trained-set greedy change ($+82$ items, with only five losses), while its C256 twin change is small and uncertain. F1k gives a positive but unresolved C768 estimate ($+28$ items, 95% CI $[-4,61]$), so the paper is correct to call the transfer gate failed rather than infer zero transfer. The sampled gains are reproducible across the reported panels, whereas no per-arm held-out greedy gain excludes zero, including MATH-500. The colder-start comparison is appropriately presented as a decoding baseline after the manuscript withdrew the stronger causal “cooling” interpretation. The residual token analysis, ledger loss comparison, and tail analysis are also candid about what their diagnostics cannot establish.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. Prior work [16] already studied low-temperature controls, difficulty bands, and the low-probability tail; [2] studied verified self-distillation and broad RLVR. This paper adds the randomized matched-question fitting/transfer endpoint, an allocation comparison with a second seed, and a prospective greedy readout whose nominal-temperature calibration is shown to overpredict. The manuscript cites and distinguishes those results rather than presenting these measurements as wholly new methods.

Several qualifications should remain prominent in the final version:

- The confidence intervals are item/bootstrap intervals conditional on realized checkpoints. They do not estimate training-seed uncertainty. Thus “F1k beats the comparator at two seeds” is an observed conditional ordering, not a population-level allocation effect. The two seeds, one start, and one reward family should be stated in the abstract wherever that ordering is mentioned.
- F1k versus the comparator confounds selected mixed-question training, exposure count, informative-group count, extra backward work, and the shared 51,200-rollout selection search. F1u helps bound this issue but has one seed and does not identify which factor causes the sampled ordering. The paper generally acknowledges this; the title and abstract should avoid language that could be read as a causal allocation claim.
- Greedy transfer was not replicated for F1k at seed 8 on C768, and the MATH-500 two-seed difference is descriptive rather than a per-arm success test. The positive sampled contrast ($+1.41$ percentage points with CI $[0.14,2.65]$) should therefore not be allowed to sound like a mathematical capability gain.
- The ledger and tail analyses are low-power and partly post hoc. Their non-rejections cannot establish equivalence or a universal support bottleneck. The manuscript says this in the body and limitations; the abstract and discussion should preserve the same restraint.

The supplement’s stored MATH-500 summaries agree with the headline counts (start 227/500; F1 225; F1k seeds 247 and 245; comparator seeds 238 and 242; F1u 238), and the reported token-slope, ledger, and tail readouts are consistent with the narrative. The abstract is self-contained and informative, though very dense; shortening the methodological chronology and moving some prespecification detail to the manuscript would improve readability. With those wording and scope clarifications, this is a reproducible and scientifically valuable bounded negative result rather than an unsupported claim of universal RLVR failure.
