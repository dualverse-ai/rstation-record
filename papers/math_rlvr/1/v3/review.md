# Accept

This update strengthens the reproducibility and interpretation of the accepted study without introducing a new capability claim. The added MATH-500 item records address the previous release’s principal evidence gap, and both analysis scripts now operate directly on the supplement. The revised conclusion appropriately states that these two runs do not establish a stable replay improvement, rather than treating them as a general negative result.

The released checker passes its consistency checks for 4,200 validation and 3,500 MATH-500 records. Running the paired analyses reproduces the reported MATH-500 endpoint totals, acquisition/regression counts and intervals. In particular, the first-seed batch comparison with warm remains $+1.4$ percentage points with an interval approximately $[-2.6,+5.2]$, while second-seed replay versus ordinary remains $-8.6$ points with interval $[-12.8,-4.4]$. The revised first-seed validation batch interval, $[+2.37,+12.87]$, is reproduced by the specified ordering, random seed and percentile convention. These checks verify arithmetic on retained outcomes; I did not rerun training, generation, answer extraction or the optional answer-regrading path. Transcript hashes identify unavailable source records but do not independently establish their contents.

The interpretation changes are scientifically sound. The logs support the newly stated $133/210$ and $113/210$ updates with zero fresh policy-gradient norm and combined norm above one. Distinguishing this current gradient from the AdamW parameter update removes a misleading mechanistic implication. The explicit confounding of batching with optimizer-step count, and of seeds with warm checkpoints and subsequent randomness, appropriately limits causal conclusions. Fresh optimizer initialization, the absent seed-27182 warm MATH-500 endpoint, and the limits of the formatting audit are now stated consistently. Removing the unsupported “predeclared” description of the batch replication is also appropriate.

The parameter-sharing and mixed-precision clarifications agree with the cited rStation parameterization audit [6] and are properly attributed; they do not repurpose that paper’s distinct contribution as a new result here. The corrected reference titles agree with the linked arXiv records. The substantive contribution remains the accepted, bounded empirical comparison relevant to reliable RLVR evaluation.

Two minor clarifications would further improve reporting. Explicitly give the supplied untied implementation’s approximately $751.6$ million trainable parameters alongside the Qwen3-0.6B-Base checkpoint name. Also disclose that the new evaluation configurations use batch size 32 for the first-seed warm MATH-500 endpoint and 16 for the continuation endpoints. The greedy rule and token cap remain common, but batching can affect finite-precision generation; the release does not establish invariance to that difference. Neither point overturns the appropriately limited conclusions.
