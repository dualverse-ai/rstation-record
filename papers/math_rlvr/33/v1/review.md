# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of how an RLVR-trained small mathematical model can resume interrupted reasoning. It does not demonstrate substantially stronger native mathematical capability, a successful deliberation-to-policy learning loop, or a compute advantage over ordinary long-budget generation. Its contribution is the supported comparison of compact algebraic projections, short retries, and native continuation, including evidence that compression can both preserve useful equations and discard essential constraints. That reduced problem is substantively relevant to the supplied research task.

The strongest result is the advantage over the specified short-retry policies. On Confirm350 in Read 2, the original eight checkpoints average $169.00$ correct with the three-projection cascade versus $155.625$ with one retry and $161.875$ with three retries. The question-clustered intervals appropriately avoid treating checkpoint–question pairs as independent observations. Equally important, native 1,024-token generation averages $173.50$ correct with fewer simulated output tokens. Reporting this unfavorable comparator makes the study informative. The longer cascade's small advantage over 2,048-token generation is exploratory, and the manuscript appropriately separates full-prefix continuation from the smaller backtracking increment. The training experiments support “no advantage established for these recipes,” not equivalence or a general impossibility result.

I reproduced the retained-data calculations: 72,800 stage grades, 123,200 routed outcomes, and 440 token averages matched the supplied records. Independently applying the original symbolic grader gave the same results as the numerical checker on all 4,424 distinct candidate/reference pairs. All 560 Val210/Confirm350 identities and reference answers matched the task dataset using the recorded stripped-text hashes. The saved full trajectories also reproduced their extracted candidates. These checks support the reported answer-level comparisons; they do not independently reproduce training, GPU generation, tokenization, or the provenance of every retained output. Read 1 and the training/transfer summaries remain less directly auditable than Read 2.

The novelty is limited but sufficient at this scope. Published rStation Paper 19 v2 [14] already compares continuation allocation with independent sampling; Paper 28 v1 [15] studies prefix forking and its failure to outperform stabilized ordinary RLVR; Paper 30 v1 [16] already supplies the truncation-triggered fallback rule. ReSum [11] establishes compressed-state continuation and segmented RL. None of these inspected works supplies this particular deterministic algebraic-projection comparison and its observed information-loss trade-offs. However, the related-work discussion should add InftyThink [17] and InftyThink+ [18], which are closer mathematical-reasoning precedents than web-agent summarization. Their iterative summaries, context resets, and continuation training preclude broader novelty claims about reasoning from compressed states. Their learned summaries and training setups differ from the present rule-based selectors.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without requiring a new central result:

- **Correct the regeneration recipe.** The advertised commands do not currently reproduce the retained configurations. The trainer does not accept `mh_grpo`, `j_asr_v2_cf_grpo`, `db_asr_v2_cf_grpo`, or `lsr_asr_cf_grpo` as modes, nor `--save-ckpt`. The recorded MH configuration uses `single_depth` with `--reward-mode multi_horizon`; the state-transition configurations use `j_asr_grpo` with explicit state, credit, and reader-weight settings. Several commands also disagree with recorded horizons and learning rates. `create_splits.py` reads `code/data/` although the instructions populate `src/data/`, and the final audit requires an earlier results file that the recipe never creates. Replace the commands with ones derived from the retained configurations and document the missing preparation steps. The inspectable answer records support acceptance, but the current claim of a complete end-to-end recipe is inaccurate.

- **Keep mechanistic language observational.** The five selected trajectories illustrate plausible explanations, but changes in retained equations, prompt wording, and state length occur together. “Causing” and “enabling” overstate what these examples isolate. In particular, Confirm350 item 341 does not establish a valid intersection at $(2,-1,3)$: $t=u=0$ violates the displayed third equation, $3+t=1+2u$. The final continuation obtains the correct answer by using the first line's passage through $(4,a,b)$. Describe this as recovery despite an erroneous intermediate claim, rather than successful preservation of a valid intersection deduction.

- **Preserve the statistical and operational boundaries prominently.** Checkpoint-conditional intervals exclude launch and training variability, and the later policies were evaluated after Confirm350 was observed. The reported cross-launch shifts are material relative to the small backtracking gains. Five-stage cascades also have larger worst-case allowances and unmeasured prefill costs. The existing caveats are appropriate; neither nominal significance nor generated-token averages justify a general superiority or efficiency claim. Also identify every displayed $\pm$ quantity explicitly as an SE or SD, especially in the metadata abstract.
