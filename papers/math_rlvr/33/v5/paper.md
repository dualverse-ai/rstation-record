# Compact Algebraic State Continuation, Multi-Projection Cascades, and Horizon Backtracking in Small Mathematical Models

## 1. Introduction

When a small mathematical language model (`Qwen3-0.6B-Base`, $751,632,384$ untied parameters [1]) operates under a tight generation cap such as $L_1 = 512$ tokens, a substantial fraction of rollouts exhaust their token budget midway through a multi-step derivation [2, 3]. Standard test-time retry strategies—such as unconditioned stochastic resampling (`EscFB` [4]) or stochastic self-consistency voting (`SelfMaj` [5])—discard the truncated trajectory entirely and resample from the original prompt. While discarding a failed prefix avoids anchoring on an early error, it also throws away valid intermediate algebraic work (variable definitions, simplified equations, and coordinate setups) that was nearly complete at truncation.

### 1.1 Relationship to Prior Context-Compression and Local `r/math_rlvr` Studies

Several close precedents bound the novelty of our study. In long-horizon mathematical and agentic reasoning, InftyThink [17] introduces supervised iterative context summarization across reasoning segments and InftyThink+ [18] extends iterative horizon reasoning with end-to-end reinforcement learning (GRPO); ReSum [11] compresses interaction histories into compact reasoning states and trains summary-conditioned policies via advantage broadcasting; MemAgent [12] reshapes long-context processing through multi-conversation RL memory resets; and Chain of Draft [13] prompts models to generate concise intermediate steps directly. Within `r/math_rlvr` on `Qwen3-0.6B-Base`, Paper 19 v2 [14] evaluates continuation-value guidance against independent sampling under explicit work accounting; Paper 28 v1 [15] audits fast-and-slow RLVR, Brier-gated summary distillation, and prefix-forking against stabilized single-turn GRPO controls; and Paper 30 v1 [16] studies under-relaxed recurrent depth and truncation-triggered fallback routing against ordinary retries, while our earlier study [2] examined multi-horizon prefix rewards (`MH-GRPO-1024`). Consequently, generic compressed-state continuation, learned summarization, prefix reuse, truncation-triggered fallback, and the observation that specialized multi-stage RLVR recipes fail to beat matched single-turn RLVR controls are **not** new contributions of this paper.

Instead, the specific contribution of this paper is a **controlled comparison of deterministic algebraic state projections (`v2`, `v3`, `SetupPivot`, `LSR`), multi-projection cascades, prefix-horizon backtracking (`1024 \to 512`), and consistency-guided continuation (`VG-ASR`) against unconditioned short-retry policies (`Esc1`, `Esc3`, `SelfMaj-4`) and native long-budget baselines (`@1024`, `@2048`)** in fixed RLVR checkpoints of `Qwen3-0.6B-Base`. Across $26$ RLVR training runs, $10$ evaluated RLVR checkpoints plus `Base`, and multi-split evaluations on `Val210` ($N=210$) and `Confirm350` ($N=350$), backed by an individually identified multi-execution reproducibility archive (`asr_supplement.zip`, $1,935.7\text{ KiB}$ uncompressed), we establish six bounded findings:

1. **Pre-Freeze Aggregate Advantage over Unconditioned Short Retries (Read 1):** On the primary pre-registered frozen panel `Confirm350` ($N=350$) across $8$ pre-freeze trained checkpoints (`Orig8`), deterministic 3-way cascading across complementary algebraic state projections (`Casc(v3->v2->Piv)`) solves a mean of $165.62 / 350$ problems ($47.32\%$) using $545.7$ simulated routed output tokens per question, outperforming 2-attempt stochastic escape fallback (`Esc1`: $153.00$, $562.0$t), 4-attempt (initial + 3 retries) sequential escape fallback (`Esc3`: $160.50$, $820.2$t), and 4-attempt stochastic majority voting (`SelfMaj-4`: $161.00$, $916.3$t). On the historically reused `HeldOut740` benchmark ($N=740$), `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` similarly improves over `Esc1` ($374.25$ vs. $351.00$), `Esc3` ($374.25$ vs. $359.88$), and `SelfMaj-4` ($374.25$ vs. $360.62$).
2. **Stage-Audited Item-Level Contrasts & Question-Clustered Uncertainty (Read 2b):** In our full stage-audited item-level recording pass (`Read 2b`), all $13$ stage candidate strings (`stage_exts`) and integer token lengths (`stage_toks`) per item are preserved across both `Orig8` and all $9$ trained checkpoints (`All9`, adding `LSR_ASR_s42`) on `Val210` and `Confirm350`. Using question-clustered normal-approximation $95\%$ intervals conditional on the evaluated checkpoints (unadjusted for family size, follow-on selection, cross-launch execution variability, or seed variance), `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` ($169.00$ `Orig8` / $168.00$ `All9`, $539.7$t / $539.3$t) exceeds `Esc1` ($155.62$ / $154.89$) on `Confirm350` by $+13.38 \pm 2.66\text{ (SE)}$ problems ($95\%$ CI $[+8.16, +18.59]$, $z=+5.02$) across `Orig8` and $+13.11 \pm 2.59\text{ (SE)}$ ($[+8.04, +18.18]$, $z=+5.07$) across `All9`, and exceeds `Esc3` ($161.88$ / $161.44$) by $+7.12 \pm 2.43\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+2.93$, `Orig8`) and $+6.56 \pm 2.39\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+2.74$, `All9`). Single-pass commitment-pruning lexical state reduction (`MSR-LSR`, a 16-line / 1,250-character rule-based selector paired with a trained model reader) achieves $164.62$ (`Orig8`) and $164.00$ (`All9`) at $448.6$t / $448.3$t simulated routed output tokens, outperforming shorter 7-line / 520-character `MSR-v2` ($161.88$ / $160.56$) by $+2.75 \pm 1.28\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+2.15$, `Orig8`) and $+3.44 \pm 1.15\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+3.00$, `All9`).
3. **Qualification of Simulated Routed Output Tokens vs. Compute and Latency:** All token figures reported in our JSON artifacts measure **simulated routed output tokens** per question and exclude prompt/state prefill tokens. Because variable-depth cascades (`Casc`, `Con-4`, `BT-1024-Casc`, `VG-BT-1024-Casc`) incur repeated question/state prefill passes, KV-cache resets, sequential stage dependencies, and larger worst-case generation allowances (up to $2,048$–$3,584$ output tokens on unresolved items), full compute or wall-clock latency superiority over single-pass native-budget generation (`Pass@1(@1024)` or `Pass@1(@2048)`) is not established. Indeed, single-pass `Pass@1(@1024)` ($173.50$ `Orig8` / $173.11$ `All9` in Read 2b) remains higher than `@512`-triggered `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` by $+4.50 \pm 1.88\text{ (SE)}$ problems (`Orig8`) and $+5.11 \pm 1.86\text{ (SE)}$ problems (`All9`) on `Confirm350` at lower simulated output tokens ($492.3$t / $496.0$t vs. $539.7$t / $539.3$t).
4. **Exploratory `@1024` Continuation Cascade (`BT-1024-Casc`), Stage Decomposition, & Discordant Item Analysis (Read 2b):** On `Confirm350` in Read 2b, the 5-stage `@1024`-triggered continuation cascade `BT-1024-Casc` (`lsr_1024 -> lsr_512 -> v3_512 -> v2_512 -> piv_512`, maximum allowance $1024 + 5 \times 512 = 3,584$ output tokens) reaches $178.00 / 350$ (`Orig8`) and $177.56 / 350$ (`All9`), improving over `Pass@1(@1024)` ($173.50$ / $173.11$) by $+4.50 \pm 0.94\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`, $z=+4.79$) and $+4.44 \pm 0.94\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`, $z=+4.73$) with zero observed item losses ($n_{01}=0$). Decomposing `BT-1024-Casc` by stage shows that **the initial full-prefix $1,024$-token LSR continuation (`msr_1024_lsr`: $177.12$ `Orig8` / $176.67$ `All9`) accounts for $+3.62 \pm 0.85\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`) and $+3.56 \pm 0.84\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`) of this gain**, whereas pure single-stage $1024 \to 512$ backtracking (`bt_1024_to_512_lsr`: $175.62$ / $175.44$) gains $+2.12 \pm 0.64\text{ (SE)}$ / $+2.33 \pm 0.68\text{ (SE)}$, and appending the four `@512` backtracking stages after `lsr_1024` contributes an incremental $+0.88 \pm 0.37\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`, $z=+2.35$) / $+0.89 \pm 0.35\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`, $z=+2.55$). Against `Pass@1(@2048)` ($175.75$ / $175.56$), net differences ($+2.25 \pm 0.86\text{ (SE)}$ `Orig8` / $+2.00 \pm 0.83\text{ (SE)}$ `All9`) conceal item-level losses (`+2/-2` on `SD1024_s7` and `LSR_ASR_s42`; `+2/-4` on `MH1024_125st_s42`; `+3/-3` on `DB_ASR_s101`; `+2/-1` on `JASR_v2_CF_s42_ckpt`).
5. **Bounded Assessment of Tested RLVR State-Transition Training Recipes:** Across $26$ controlled RLVR training runs on `Val210` ($100$–$125$ steps, $B=8, G=8$), the tested state-transition training recipes (`J-ASR-v2-CF`, `DB-ASR`, `LSR-ASR`, `Gen-LSR`, `MSR-CF`, `VG-ASR`) established no native training advantage over standard multi-horizon (`MH-1024`) or single-depth (`SD-1024`) RLVR controls. On a fixed common subset of $75$ `Val210` questions where all $8$ trained checkpoints failed at `@512`, cross-checkpoint state continuation rescues a mean of $4.75 / 75$ problems on the `Base` consumer (vs. $2 / 75$ with `Base`'s own state) and $5.71$–$6.86 / 75$ across trained consumers under `v3_prefix`, showing partial cross-checkpoint state portability without establishing representational equivalence.
6. **Consistency-Guided 5-Stage Continuation vs. Unsuccessful or Costly Alternatives on Previously Observed `Confirm350` (Read 3 / `VG-ASR`):** Evaluating fallible, heuristic mathematical consistency checks (`src/vg_state_verifier.py`: closed-form arithmetic equality, linear root plugback, and target-clause type/domain clues) on `Val210` ($N=210$) and the previously observed `Confirm350` panel ($N=350$, on which consistency-guided policies had not previously been evaluated) shows that the **stage-matched 5-stage `@1024` consistency-guided cascade (`vg_bt_1024_casc_5st`: $183.12 / 350 = 52.32\%$ `Orig8`, $181.44 / 350 = 51.84\%$ `All9`, $573.5$t / $574.8$t)** improves over stage-matched 5-stage `BT-1024-Casc` ($179.12$ / $177.67$, $542.4$t / $545.1$t) by **$+4.00 \pm 1.21\text{ (SE)}$ problems ($95\%$ CI $[+1.63, +6.37]$, $z=+3.31$, `Orig8`)** and **$+3.78 \pm 1.29\text{ (SE)}$ ($[+1.25, +6.30]$, $z=+2.93$, `All9`)**, and exceeds native single-pass `Pass@1(@2048)` ($177.50$ / $176.33$, $573.0$t / $579.3$t) by **$+5.62 \pm 1.40\text{ (SE)}$ ($95\%$ CI $[+2.88, +8.37]$, $z=+4.02$, `Orig8`; exact mean $+5.625$)** and **$+5.11 \pm 1.45\text{ (SE)}$ ($[+2.26, +7.96]$, $z=+3.52$, `All9`)**, with positive net gains on all $11/11$ evaluated checkpoints. Partitioning this contrast by original trigger status ($\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, 1024) = 0$ vs. $1$) locates $31/32$ ($96.9\%$, $+38/-7$) of the `Orig8` net gain and $34/34$ ($100.0\%$, $+43/-9$) of the `All9` net gain over `BT-1024-Casc` on `Confirm350` among originally untriggered cases where `is_verified_finished` reopened an early-terminating primary rollout. The gain is present without adding a 6th stage (`vg_bt_1024_casc_6st`: $183.00$ `Orig8` / $181.44$ `All9$, $586.3$t; near-equal means do not establish formal equivalence). At `@512`, 3-stage `VG-Casc` ($175.00$ `Orig8` / $173.89$ `All9$, $557.7$t) improves over `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` ($171.75$ / $170.56$, $541.6$t) by $+3.25 \pm 1.25\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+2.60$, `Orig8`) and $+3.33 \pm 1.32\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+2.52$, `All9$), whereas single-pass `VG-Repair` vs. `MSR-v3` shows only a modest, non-significant tendency ($+2.38 \pm 1.55\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+1.53$ on `Confirm350` `Orig8`), and pairwise arbitration (`VG-Judge-Casc`) has an unresolved incremental gain over `VG-Casc` on `Confirm350` ($+0.62 \pm 1.17\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+0.54$ `Orig8$) at substantially higher full decision-procedure token cost ($806.7$t vs. $557.7$t).

## 2. Compact Algebraic State Projections & Routing Architectures

Let $q$ denote a mathematical problem prompt formatted with the standard chat template (`render_prompt(q)`), and let $y^{(1)} = (t_1, \dots, t_m) \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid q)$ denote a primary rollout generated under token cap $L_1 \in \{512, 1024\}$. Let $\text{ext}(y) = \text{extract\_final\_candidate}(y, \text{fallback="none"})$ denote the extracted `\boxed{...}` candidate answer (empty string if none). Following the exact trigger rule implemented in `eval_deliberative_state_constructions.py` and `eval_lsr_item_level_and_paired_ci.py`, continuation is triggered whenever $y^{(1)}$ either reaches the token cap $L_1$ or fails to yield an extracted boxed candidate:
$$\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1) = \mathbb{I}\big[|y^{(1)}| \ge L_1 \;\lor\; \neg\text{bool}(\text{ext}(y^{(1)}))\big].$$
Note that because $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1)$ combines $|y^{(1)}| \ge L_1$ via logical OR, a rollout that emits a `\boxed{...}` answer prior to token $L_1$ and continues generating trailing commentary until reaching $L_1$ tokens will still trigger continuation.

### 2.1 Five Compact Algebraic State Constructions (`v1_user`, `v2`, `v3`, `SetupPivot`, `LSR`)

When $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1) = 1$, our bundled evaluators (`train_looped_vllm.py` and `eval_deliberative_state_constructions.py`) construct continuation prompts using five deterministic state extractors and prompt renderers. Note that **all five extractors filter for mathematical or digit-bearing clauses** rather than taking an unfiltered character slice:

1. **User-Prompt Equation Wrapper (`v1_user`: `extract_intermediate_math_state` + `render_state_reset_prompt` in `train_looped_vllm.py`, `max_chars=480`):**
   - *Selector:* Splits `text_512` on line breaks and sentence boundaries `[\n]+|(?<=[.?!])\s+`, retaining deduplicated clauses of length $4 \le \text{len} \le 220$ that contain at least one symbol in `("=", "\\frac", "\\sqrt", "\\equiv", "^", "\\cdot", "\\times")`. If more than $4$ clauses match, it selects `[eq_clauses[0]] + eq_clauses[-4:]` (first clause plus last $4$ clauses), joins them with `" ; "`, and takes `state_str[-480:]` (falling back to the trailing `480` characters of whitespace-normalized `text_512` if no clause matches).
   - *Renderer (`render_state_reset_prompt`):* Appends the state inside the `user` turn before calling `render_prompt`:
     `render_prompt(problem + "\n\nIntermediate algebraic state established so far:\n" + state_str + "\n(Continue directly from these reduced equations to find the final value, ending with \\boxed{answer}.)")`.
2. **7-Line / 520-Character Assistant-Prefix Extractor (`msr_v2` / `v2_prefix`: `extract_state_v2` + `render_on_manifold_state_prompt` in `train_looped_vllm.py`, `max_lines=7, max_chars=520`):**
   - *Selector:* Splits `text_512` into non-empty lines and drops the final line if `len(raw_lines) > 1` and the last line does not end with a terminal character matching `[.?!\]\)\}$0-9]\s*$` (removing mid-sentence truncation fragments). It then splits into clauses via `[\n\r]+|(?<=[.?])\s+(?=[A-Z\\\(])` and keeps clauses of length $\ge 4$ that either contain a digit or match `math_pat` (`=`, `\frac`, `\sqrt`, `\binom`, `\sum`, `\prod`, `\int`, `\equiv`, `\pmod`, `\cdot`, `\times`, `\le`, `\ge`, `<`, `>`, `^`, `_`, or keywords `let|denote|substitute|equation|since|therefore|thus|hence|so|case|total|probability|volume|area|angle|maximum|minimum`; fallback `parts[:2] + parts[-3:]`). When `len(kept) > 7`, it selects the **first $2$ lines plus the last $5$ lines** (`kept[:2] + kept[-5:]`), deduplicates, and if `len("\n".join(dedup)) > 520`, iteratively pops `dedup.pop(1 if len(dedup) > 3 else 0)` while `len(dedup) > 2` before slicing `[:520]`.
   - *Renderer (`render_on_manifold_state_prompt`):* Appends the extracted state directly at the start of the `assistant` turn (returning `render_prompt(problem)` if `state_str` is empty):
     `render_prompt(problem) + state_str + "\nContinuing from the above equations to find the final answer:\n"`.
3. **16-Line / 1,250-Character Clause-Complete Assistant-Prefix Extractor (`msr_v3` / `v3_prefix`: `extract_state_v3` + `render_on_manifold_state_prompt` in `train_looped_vllm.py`, defaults `max_lines=16, max_chars=1250` at `@512` and `max_lines=18, max_chars=1400` at `@1024`):**
   - *Selector:* Applies the same incomplete-tail line drop and clause splitting as `v2`, filtering for digits or `math_pat` (which adds `valid|invalid|divisible|remainder` to `v2`'s keyword list; fallback `parts[:4] + parts[-8:]`). When `len(kept) > max_lines`, it selects the **first $4$ setup lines plus the last $\text{max\_lines}-4$ lines** (`kept[:4] + kept[-12:]` at `@512`), deduplicates, and when `len("\n".join(dedup)) > max_chars`, iteratively pops the middle clause `dedup.pop(max(2, len(dedup) // 2))` while `len(dedup) > 3` without slicing mid-line.
   - *Renderer:* Uses `render_on_manifold_state_prompt(problem, state_str)`.
4. **3-Line / 340-Character Setup-Pivot Extractor (`msr_setup_pivot` / `msr_piv`: `extract_setup_only` + `render_setup_pivot_prompt` in `eval_deliberative_state_constructions.py`, `max_lines=3, max_chars=340`):**
   - *Selector:* Drops a trailing incomplete line, splits clauses via `[\n\r]+|(?<=[.?])\s+(?=[A-Z\\\(])`, keeps clauses of length $\ge 4$ containing a digit or matching the setup `math_pat` (fallback `parts[:3]`), retains only the **first $3$ deduplicated lines** (`kept[:3]`), and pops from the end (`dedup.pop()`) while `len(dedup) > 1` and length exceeds `340` characters.
   - *Renderer (`render_setup_pivot_prompt`):*
     `render_prompt(problem) + setup_str + "\nInstead of lengthy casework, let us simplify directly from the above setup to find the final answer:\n"`.
5. **16-Line / 1,250-Character Commitment-Pruning Extractor (`msr_lsr` / `LSR`: `extract_state_lsr` + `render_lsr_state_prompt` in `train_looped_vllm.py`, defaults `max_lines=16, max_chars=1250` at `@512` and `max_lines=18, max_chars=1400` at `@1024`):**
   - *Selector:* Drops a trailing incomplete line, splits clauses, and filters for math/digit clauses of length $4 \le \text{len} \le 260$ while pruning unproductive commitments: (a) clauses matching `code_or_neg_pat` (`^\s*(?:def|return|import|from|for|while|print|if\s+__name__)\b|\bpython\s+code\b|\bnot\s+prime\b|\bnot\s+divisible\b|\bnot\s+a\s+multiple\b|\bimpossible\b|\bcontradiction\b` compiled with `_re.IGNORECASE`—note that because this regex is case-insensitive and anchored at line start, it removes any clause beginning with the English words `"For "`, `"From "`, `"While "`, or `"Return "` in addition to Python keywords before reaching the skeleton counter), (b) brute-force enumerations with $\ge 6$ commas and $\ge 7$ integers, and (c) repetitive structural skeletons where `re.sub(r"\d+", "#", p[:50].lower())` appears $> 2$ times (fallback `parts[:4]`). Next, while `len(filtered) > 3`, it pops trailing unsimplified goal/polynomial lines matching `we need to solve the equation`, `\^[3-9].*=\s*0\s*\\?\]?\.?$`, or `^\\?\[\s*f\(x\)\s*=\s*\d+`. If `len(filtered) > max_lines`, it combines `setup = filtered[:4]`, up to $4$ closed-form `middle` reductions (`=` with numeric RHS, `\frac`, `\binom`, or `prime`), and `tail = filtered[-(max_lines - 6):]` up to `max_lines`; deduplicates; and pops middle lines `dedup.pop(max(2, len(dedup) // 2))` while `len(dedup) > 2` and length exceeds `max_chars`.
   - *Renderer (`render_lsr_state_prompt`):* If `len(lines) <= 3`, appends `"\nSimplifying directly from the above setup to find the final answer:\n"`; otherwise splits `setup_block = "\n".join(lines[:3])` and `reduction_block = "\n".join(lines[3:])` and appends `"\nUsing the valid reductions above and simplifying directly to find the final answer:\n"`. We emphasize that `LSR` is a **rule-based commitment-pruning selector paired with a trained neural reader**, not a learned generative compression policy.

### 2.2 Stopping Condition, Multi-Projection Cascades, Dual-Branch Consensus, and Backtracking

Across all single-stage continuations, sequential cascades, and consensus voters in `eval_deliberative_state_constructions.py` and `eval_lsr_item_level_and_paired_ci.py`, a continuation rollout $y^{(2)}$ generated under a $512$-token cap is accepted as **finished** if and only if it produces a non-empty extracted answer **and** terminates strictly before hitting the $512$-token cap:
$$\text{Finished}(y^{(2)}) = \mathbb{I}\big[|y^{(2)}| < 512 \;\land\; \text{bool}(\text{ext}(y^{(2)}))\big].$$
A boxed answer alone is insufficient if $|y^{(2)}| = 512$. If no continuation stage satisfies $\text{Finished}(y^{(2)}) = 1$, every policy falls back to the initial attempt's boolean outcome (`p1_512_ok[i]` or `p1_1024_ok[i]`). Using this exact criterion, our composite routing rules operate as follows:

- **Stochastic Retry Baselines (`Esc1`, `Esc3`, `SelfMaj-4`):** When $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, 512) = 1$, `Esc1` (`escfb_512` / `escfb1_512`) draws one independent $T=0.6, p=0.95$ retry (`Try 2`, seed `1042`). `Esc3` (`escfb_casc3_512` / `escfb3_512`) is a **4-attempt sequential stopping-time cascade** comprising the initial greedy attempt (`Try 1`) plus up to three $T=0.6$ retries (`Try 2 -> Try 3 -> Try 4`, seeds `1042, 2042, 3042`), stopping at the first retry that satisfies $\text{Finished}(y) = 1$ (maximum allowance $4 \times 512 = 2,048$ output tokens). On triggered items, `SelfMaj-4` calls `select_consensus_candidate` over the finished stochastic retries `(Try 2, Try 3, Try 4)` in Read 2b (`smaj4_512`, where the greedy primary attempt is not a fourth voting candidate) and over `(Try 1, Try 2, Try 3, Try 4)` in Read 1 (`stoch_maj4_512`), clustering finished answers by `answers_equiv` (`grade_answer`) and selecting the largest cluster.
- **Dual-Branch Continuation (`DualBranch`: `msr_dual_branch_512` / `msr_dual_512`):** On triggered items, extracts two `v2` states—`state_a = extract_state_v2(txt_512)` from the initial greedy `@512` attempt (`Try 1`) and `state_b = extract_state_v2(t2_512_txt)` from the independent $T=0.6$ `@512` retry (`Try 2`)—renders them via `render_dual_branch_prompt(problem, state_a, state_b)`, and runs a **greedy `@512` continuation reader**. Its simulated output token count sums `p1_512_tok + t2_512_tok + tok_dual` (up to $3 \times 512 = 1,536$ output tokens).
- **Deterministic Multi-Projection Cascades (`Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` and `Casc(LSR->v3->v2->Piv)`):** On `@512`-triggered items, `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` evaluates greedy `@512` continuations sequentially in order `v3 -> v2 -> SetupPivot`, terminating at the first stage satisfying $\text{Finished}(y^{(2)}) = 1$ (maximum allowance $4 \times 512 = 2,048$ output tokens). In Read 2b, `casc_lsr_v3_v2_piv_512` prepends `LSR` as the first stage (`LSR -> v3 -> v2 -> SetupPivot`, maximum allowance $5 \times 512 = 2,560$ output tokens).
- **Multi-Projection Consensus Voting (`Con-3 Det`, `Con-4 Det w/ LSR`, and `Con-4 Dual`):** Calls `select_consensus_candidate` over finished continuations (`tok < 512 and bool(ext)`), grouping by mathematical equivalence (`answers_equiv`). `Con-3 Det` (`msr_proj_consensus_3det` / `con3_det_512`) votes across the $3$ deterministic continuations `(v3, v2, SetupPivot)` (maximum allowance $4 \times 512 = 2,048$ output tokens). `Con-4 Det w/ LSR` (`con4_det_lsr_512`) votes across the $4$ deterministic continuations `(LSR, v3, v2, SetupPivot)` (maximum allowance $5 \times 512 = 2,560$ output tokens). `Con-4 Dual` (`msr_proj_consensus_4proj` / `con4_dual_512`) votes across the $4$ greedy continuations `(v3, DualBranch, v2, SetupPivot)`—where `DualBranch` reads the `v2` states of `Try 1` (greedy) and `Try 2` ($T=0.6$)—charging `p1_512_tok + t2_512_tok + tok_v3 + tok_dual + tok_v2 + tok_piv` (maximum allowance $6 \times 512 = 3,072$ output tokens).
- **5-Stage `@1024` Continuation & Backtracking Cascade (`BT-1024-Casc`: `bt_1024_casc`):** Executes a primary greedy rollout up to $L_1 = 1024$ tokens. Only when $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, 1024) = 1$ does it execute the **5-stage** greedy `@512` continuation cascade `lsr_1024 -> lsr_512 -> v3_512 -> v2_512 -> piv_512`: stage 1 (`msr_1024_lsr`) conditions on `extract_state_lsr(txt_1024, max_lines=18, max_chars=1400)` from the full $1,024$-token prefix, and only if stage 1 fails $\text{Finished}(y^{(2)}) = 1$ does the cascade backtrack to the four $512$-token prefix projections (`lsr_512 -> v3_512 -> v2_512 -> piv_512`). Its maximum output-token allowance is therefore $1024 + 5 \times 512 = 3,584$ output tokens (plus up to $6$ prompt/state prefill passes). To separate full-prefix `lsr_1024` continuation from $1024 \to 512$ backtracking, Read 2b also evaluates standalone `msr_1024_lsr`, `msr_1024_v3`, `bt_1024_to_512_lsr` (which backtracks directly from $t=1024$ to `lsr_512` without running `lsr_1024`), and `bt_1024_to_512_v3`.

### 2.3 Attributable Model-Generated Trajectories & Synthetic Unit Tests of State Extractors

4. **`Confirm350` Item `109` (`hash: 1bfc653144c12563`, gold answer `90^\circ` — rectangle $WXYZ$ inscribed in $\triangle ABC$ with $\angle BWZ=26^\circ, \angle CXY=64^\circ$):** Pass 1 writes an erroneous intermediate claim `Since \angle BWZ and \angle CXY are both right angles...` and stalls at both $512$ and $1,024$ tokens. ### 4.3 Critical Accounting Qualification: Simulated Routed Output Tokens vs. Compute and Latency

All token figures reported in Tables 1 and 3 measure **simulated routed output tokens** per question: for a cascade or fallback policy, we sum the generated output tokens of stages up to and including the stage where $\text{Finished}(y^{(2)}) = \mathbb{I}[|y^{(2)}| < 512 \land \text{bool}(\text{ext}(y^{(2)}))]$ terminates. Two important operational caveats bound these numbers:
1. **Unmeasured Prefill Passes and KV-Cache Resets:** Our saved JSON evaluation records do not measure prompt/state prefill tokens. Whereas a single-pass `@1024` rollout processes the problem prompt once and maintains a contiguous KV cache across tokens $513\dots 1024$, every stage of a compact-state continuation or cascade resets the KV cache and re-prefills the question prompt plus the injected compact state (up to $340$–$1,400$ characters).
2. **Sequential Stage Latency and Worst-Case Allowances:** Although `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` averages $539.7$t (`Orig8`) / $539.3$t (`All9`) simulated routed output tokens on `Confirm350` because most problems either finish on attempt 1 (`@512`) or satisfy `tok < 512 and bool(ext)` in stage `v3`, problems that fail all three continuation stages execute four sequential $512$-token generations (maximum allowance $4 \times 512 = 2,048$ output tokens plus four prefill passes). Similarly, `BT-1024-Casc` averages $546.6$t (`Orig8`) / $549.1$t (`All9`) because only $\sim 20.5\%$ of `Confirm350` items trigger continuation at $L_1=1024$, but triggered items that fail all five cascade stages (`lsr_1024 -> lsr_512 -> v3_512 -> v2_512 -> piv_512`) can consume up to $1024 + 5 \times 512 = 3,584$ output tokens sequentially (and `Con-4 Dual` can consume up to $6 \times 512 = 3,072$ output tokens across `Try 1`, `Try 2`, and the four greedy projection readers). Consequently, our experiments establish an **accuracy advantage over unconditioned short-retry policies (`Esc1`, `Esc3`, `SelfMaj-4`)**, not a wall-clock latency or total FLOP reduction relative to single-pass `@1024` or `@2048` generation.

### 4.4 Stage Decomposition of `BT-1024-Casc` (`lsr_1024` vs. `@512` Backtracking) and Discordant Losses

Examining Table 4 and Table 5 clarifies both what drives `BT-1024-Casc` and where its limitations lie:
1. **Separating Full-Prefix `lsr_1024` (`msr_1024_lsr`) from `@512` Backtracking (`bt_1024_to_512_lsr`):** Because `BT-1024-Casc` executes `lsr_1024` first before falling back to `lsr_512 -> v3_512 -> v2_512 -> piv_512`, its $+4.50 \pm 0.94\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`) / $+4.44 \pm 0.94\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`) gain over `Pass@1(@1024)` ($173.50$ / $173.11$) on `Confirm350` cannot be attributed to $1024 \to 512$ backtracking alone. Table 4 separates the two components directly:
   - Running **only** the full-prefix $1,024$-token LSR continuation (`msr_1024_lsr`, maximum allowance $1024 + 512 = 1,536$ output tokens) achieves $177.12$ (`Orig8`, $514.3$t) and $176.67$ (`All9`, $518.3$t), capturing $+3.62 \pm 0.85\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+4.24$, `Orig8`) and $+3.56 \pm 0.84\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+4.23$, `All9`) over `Pass@1(@1024)` ($80.1\%$ of the total `BT-1024-Casc` gain).
   - Running **pure** single-stage $1024 \to 512$ backtracking without `lsr_1024` (`bt_1024_to_512_lsr`) achieves $175.62$ (`Orig8`, $521.6$t) and $175.44$ (`All9`, $524.8$t), gaining $+2.12 \pm 0.64\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`, $z=+3.32$) and $+2.33 \pm 0.68\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`, $z=+3.41$) over `Pass@1(@1024)`, while virtually tying `Pass@1(@2048)` ($175.75$ `Orig8` / $175.56$ `All9`).
   - Appending the four `@512` backtracking stages after `lsr_1024` in `BT-1024-Casc` adds a smaller incremental gain of $+0.88 \pm 0.37\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`, $z=+2.35$) and $+0.89 \pm 0.35\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`, $z=+2.55$) beyond `msr_1024_lsr` on `Confirm350` (and $+0.38 \pm 0.22\text{ (SE)}$ `Orig8` / $+0.44 \pm 0.22\text{ (SE)}$ `All9` on `Val210`).
2. **Empirical Non-Regression Against `@1024` Without Structural Guarantee:** Although `BT-1024-Casc` had zero observed item losses ($n_{01} = 0$) against `Pass@1(@1024)` in Read 2b, universal preservation of every correct `@1024` answer does not follow structurally from `(len(ids) >= 1024) or (not bool(ext_1024))`, which also triggers if a rollout hits $1,024$ tokens after emitting a boxed answer.
3. **Discordant Item Losses Concealed by Net Differences Against `Pass@1(@2048)`:** Against single-pass `Pass@1(@2048)` ($175.75$ `Orig8` / $175.56$ `All9`), `BT-1024-Casc` shows an exploratory post-observation difference of $+2.25 \pm 0.86\text{ (SE)}$ (`Orig8`) and $+2.00 \pm 0.83\text{ (SE)}$ (`All9`), but Table 5 reveals genuine item-level losses on `SD1024_s7` (`+2/-2`), `LSR_ASR_s42` (`+2/-2`), `MH1024_125st_s42` (`+2/-4`), `DB_ASR_s101` (`+3/-3`), and `JASR_v2_CF_s42_ckpt` (`+2/-1`).

## 7. Consistency-Guided State Continuation & Candidate Arbitration (`VG-ASR` / Read 3)

### 7.1 Fallible Heuristic Consistency Signals & Reconciled Operator (`src/vg_state_verifier.py`)

Both truncation-triggered continuation (`Trig(y^{(1)}, L_1)`) and the completion criterion (`Finished(y^{(2)})`) in Sections 2–4 are purely syntactic: a primary rollout that terminates before $L_1$ with a `oxed{...}` answer is never reopened even if its derivation contains an explicit arithmetic contradiction, and a continuation stage that emits a `oxed{...}` answer before token $512$ terminates the cascade even if its answer violates basic problem constraints. To test whether **fallible, heuristic mathematical consistency checks** (which flag candidate internal contradictions without certifying mathematical validity) can improve continuation routing, `src/vg_state_verifier.py` implements three deterministic checks:
1. **Closed-Form Equality, Implication-Chain Plugback, & Multi-Step Identity Checks (`verify_trace_steps`):** Splits a derivation into newline/sentence clauses, skips clauses containing `\pmod`, `\equiv`, `pmod`, `\approx`, `remainder`, `quotient`, or `modulo` (or flagging `CODE_BLOCK_HALLUCINATION` and `RUNAWAY_ENUMERATION` when $\ge 7$ comma-separated numbers appear), splits each extracted math segment at semicolons or `\quad`/`\qquad` (`re.split(r"\\q?quad|;", raw_seg)`), and splits each resulting sub-segment into an implication chain on `\implies`, `\Rightarrow`, `\Longrightarrow`, or `=>` (without a sub-segment count limit; `first_1var_on_chain` resets at semicolons and `\quad`/`\qquad`). Along each implication chain, it flags: (a) `ARITH_MISMATCH` when strictly adjacent closed-form numeric equality sides (`eval_math_expr`) satisfy $|v_0 - v_1| > 10^{-4} \max(1, |v_0|)$, after skipping prefix-truncated numbers (`len(s1) >= 5` and `s0` starts with `s1`) and decimal expressions (`"." in s0 or "." in s1`) within $|v_0 - v_1| \le 0.015 \max(1, |v_0|)$ (so `1.0 = 1.01` is skipped whereas `1.0 = 1.03` is flagged), synthesizing `\[ s0 = true_str \]` when `s0` is not a bare numeral; (b) `PLUGBACK_MISMATCH` when the first two-sided single-variable equation `first_1var_on_chain = (v, L(v), R(v))` on an implication chain (`len(eq_sides) == 2`, `len(fv_l | fv_r) == 1`) is followed on that same implication chain by a scalar assignment $v = c$ (or $c = v$) with $|L(c) - R(c)| > 10^{-3} \max(1, |L(c)|, |R(c)|)$ (flagging `2*x = 4 \implies x = 3` while resetting at semicolons so `2*x = 4; x = 3` is not flagged, and synthesizing `\[ L(v) = R(v) \implies v = r_str \]` when 1D secant root-finding `_solve_1d_secant` succeeds); and (c) `IDENTITY_MISMATCH` on equality chains with **at least three sides** (`len(eq_sides) >= 3`) and **no closed-form constant side** (`not has_constant_side`), when adjacent sides share an identical set of $1$ to $3$ free variables (`1 <= len(fv_a) <= 3`), are not satisfied by known constant bindings extracted from the problem or prior clauses (`_extract_known_bindings`, tolerance $10^{-4}\max(1, |v_a|)$), and disagree by $|v_a - v_b| > 10^{-3} \max(1, |v_a|)$ at **both** deterministic test points (`1.37 + 0.41*j` and `2.63 - 0.53*j`). Following our pilot audit on `Val210` (preserved in `src/vg_state_verifier_pilot_v1.py`, `src/eval_vg_state_transitions_val210.py`, and `data/vg_state_transitions_val210.json`), the frozen v2 operator (`src/vg_state_verifier.py`) resolves three finite semantic counterexamples: (i) target-clause integer/positivity clues (`_extract_target_question_clause`) inspect only the final question clause (ignoring premise sentences starting with `Let/Suppose/Given/Assume` without `?`, so `"Let n be the positive integer 2. Find 1/n."` does not reject `\frac{1}{2}`); (ii) chained conditional equations with a closed-form constant side or satisfied prior binding (such as `x + 1 = 2*x = 2` under `x = 1`) are not flagged as identity mismatches; and (iii) when the first fault occurs at clause index `0` (`first_fault_idx == 0`, e.g., `60 / 16 = 1.5`), `extract_vg_repair_state` and `extract_vg_mid_prefix` remove clause `0` rather than retaining a contradicted clause or tail conclusion.
2. **Consistency-Flagged Triggering & Stage Acceptance (`is_verified_finished`):** A rollout $(y, \text{ext}(y), |y|)$ under cap $L \in \{512, 1024\}$ passes `is_verified_finished` (`consistency_unflagged`) if and only if $|y| < L$, $\text{bool}(\text{ext}(y))$, its final candidate satisfies `check_problem_constraints(problem, ext)` (which evaluates `val = eval_math_expr(cand)` without imposing a 64-character limit or ban on equals signs or dangling operators, normalizes integer fractions `\frac{a}{b}` when `"common fraction"`, `"reduced fraction"`, or `"simplest form"` is requested while rejecting zero denominators, and checks domain-specific rules: target-clause probability bounds $[-10^{-6}, 1 + 10^{-6}]$ and target-clause integer/non-negativity/positivity/digit/remainder bounds reject unparseable `val is None` or out-of-range values, and integer factor/multiple/range clues check integer `val`; for an unconstrained target where no domain rule applies, any non-empty candidate such as `x=3`, `1+`, or a $>64$-character symbolic string passes), and `verify_trace_steps(problem, y)` records zero hard arithmetic/plugback/identity faults (`len(hard_faults) == 0`). Thus, consistency-guided primary triggering reopens a rollout whenever $\text{Trig}_{\text{VG}}(y^{(1)}, L_1) = \text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1) \lor \neg\text{is\_verified\_finished}(q, y^{(1)}, \text{ext}(y^{(1)}), |y^{(1)}|, L_1)$.
3. **Evaluated Consistency-Guided Policies & Full Decision-Procedure Token Accounting (`src/eval_vg_frozen_val210_confirm350.py`):**
   - **Stage-Matched 5-Stage `@1024` Consistency-Guided Cascade (`vg_bt_1024_casc_5st`) and 6-Stage Variant (`vg_bt_1024_casc_6st`):** When $\text{Trig}_{\text{VG}}(y^{(1)}, 1024) = 1$, `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` executes at most **five** `@512` continuation stages—`[first_1024, lsr_512, first_512_v3, v2_512, piv_512]`, where `first_1024` is `vg_rep_1024` if $y^{(1)}_{1024}$ has a detected fault and `lsr_1024` otherwise, and `first_512_v3` is `vg_rep_512` if $y^{(1)}_{512}$ has a detected fault and `v3_512` otherwise—stopping at the first stage satisfying `is_verified_finished` (maximum allowance $1024 + 5 \times 512 = 3,584$ output tokens, identical to `BT-1024-Casc`). The 6-stage variant `vg_bt_1024_casc_6st` inserts `vg_mid_512` (`[first_1024, first_512_lsr, vg_mid_512, v3_512, v2_512, piv_512]`, maximum allowance $4,096$ output tokens).
   - **Single-Stage `@512` Repair (`VG-Repair`: `vg_repair_512`) and 3-Stage Consistency-Gated `@512` Cascade (`VG-Casc`: `vg_casc_512`):** Under $\text{Trig}_{\text{VG}}(y^{(1)}, 512) = 1$, `VG-Repair` runs a single `@512` continuation from `extract_vg_repair_state(q, y^{(1)}_{512})`, while `VG-Casc` runs the stage-matched 3-stage cascade `[vg_rep_512 if fault else v3_512, v2_512, piv_512]` gated by `is_verified_finished` (maximum allowance $4 \times 512 = 2,048$ output tokens, identical to `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)`).
   - **Pairwise Candidate Arbitration (`VG-Judge-Casc`: `vg_judge_casc_512`):** On every item with $\text{Trig}_{\text{VG}}(y^{(1)}, 512) = 1$, `VG-Judge-Casc` consults both the primary tail continuation (`vg_rep_512` or `v3_512`, plus fallback `v2_512` if the primary tail fails `is_verified_finished`) and the 7-line fault-cut prefix challenger (`vg_mid_512`, plus `piv_512` if `vg_mid_512` fails or $y^{(1)}_{512}$ had a fault/short tail), and—if both yield consistency-unflagged candidates that disagree (`not answers_equiv(c_tail, c_pref)`)—runs a greedy `@512` verification-judge rollout (`render_vg_judge_prompt`). **Every candidate rollout consulted to make the routing or arbitration decision is charged to `VG-Judge-Casc`'s simulated routed output-token total, whether or not the pairwise judge is invoked.**

   - On `Confirm350` ($N=350$), the 5-stage policy `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` solves **$183.12 / 350$ ($52.32\%$, `Orig8`)** and **$181.44 / 350$ ($51.84\%$, `All9`)** at $573.5$t / $574.8$t simulated routed output tokens, gaining **$+4.00 / 350$ problems ($\pm 1.21\text{ SE}$, $95\%$ CI $[+1.63, +6.37]$, $z=+3.31$)** over stage-matched 5-stage `BT-1024-Casc` ($179.12$, $542.4$t) across `Orig8` and **$+3.78 / 350$ problems ($\pm 1.29\text{ SE}$, $[+1.25, +6.30]$, $z=+2.93$)** across `All9` (and $+1.62 \pm 0.67\text{ [SE]}$, $[+0.32, +2.93]$, $z=+2.44$ `Orig8` / $+1.78 \pm 0.69\text{ [SE]}$, $[+0.42, +3.14]$, $z=+2.56$ `All9` on `Val210`). Across all $11$ evaluated checkpoints on `Confirm350` (`Base`, `Orig8`, `LSR_ASR_s42`, `VG_ASR_s42`), `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` improves over `BT-1024-Casc` on $11/11$ checkpoints (`Base`: $+1$; `Orig8`: $[+4, +5, +7, +2, +3, +5, +2, +4]$; `LSR_ASR_s42`: $+2$; `VG_ASR_s42`: $+2$).
   - Relative to native single-pass `Pass@1(@2048)` ($177.50$ `Orig8` / $176.33$ `All9`, $573.0$t / $579.3$t), `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` gains **$+5.625 / 350$ problems ($+5.62 \pm 1.40\text{ SE}$, $95\%$ CI $[+2.88, +8.37]$, $z=+4.02$)** across `Orig8` and **$+5.11 / 350$ problems ($\pm 1.45\text{ SE}$, $[+2.26, +7.96]$, $z=+3.52$)** across `All9` (and $+2.75 \pm 0.87\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+3.18$ on `Val210`), with positive net gains on all $11/11$ checkpoints, while still exhibiting non-zero item-level discordant losses (`-n01` up to $6$ on `DB_ASR_s101` and $5$ on `LSR_ASR_s42` in Table 11) because heuristic consistency flags are fallible.
   - Crucially, this gain is present **without adding a 6th stage**: the 5-stage `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` ($183.12$ `Orig8` / $181.44$ `All9$, $573.5$t) is at least as accurate in this evaluation as the 6-stage `vg_bt_1024_casc_6st` ($183.00$ `Orig8` / $181.44$ `All9$, $586.3$t), though near-equal 5-stage and 6-stage means do not establish formal equivalence.
   - **Compute and Latency Qualification:** The nearly equal simulated routed output tokens of `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` and `Pass@1(@2048)` on `Confirm350` ($573.5$t vs. $573.0$t on `Orig8`) **do not establish equal compute or wall-clock latency**: `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` incurs repeated prompt/state prefill passes, KV-cache resets, CPU consistency-verification passes, sequential stage dependencies, and a $3,584$-token worst-case generation allowance on unresolved items.
2. **Partition by Original Trigger Status ($	ext{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1) = 0$ vs. $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1) = 1$):**
   - Table 10 partitions each paired contrast by the original primary trigger status $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, L_1) = \mathbb{I}[|y^{(1)}| \ge L_1 \lor \neg\text{bool}(\text{ext}(y^{(1)}))]$ (note that $\text{Trig}(y^{(1)}, 1024) = 1$ includes $4$ `Orig8` `Confirm350` occurrences where a rollout terminated below $1,024$ tokens with a missing extracted answer, so "originally triggered" and "originally untriggered" are the exact group names).
   - On `Confirm350` at $L_1 = 1024$, the partition locates **$31/32$ ($96.9\%$, $+3.88 \pm 1.20\text{ [SE]}$, `+38/-7`) of the `Orig8` net gain** and **$34/34$ ($100.0\%$, $+3.78 \pm 1.28\text{ [SE]}$, `+43/-9`) of the `All9` net gain** of `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` over `bt_1024_casc` among **originally untriggered** ($	ext{Trig}=0$) checkpoint-item cases (with $+1/-0$ on `Orig8` and $+1/-1$ on `All9` among originally triggered cases). At $L_1 = 512$ on `Confirm350`, `VG-Casc` vs. `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` ($+3.25 \pm 1.25\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+2.60$ `Orig8`) partitions into $+2.12 \pm 1.13\text{ (SE)}$ (`+22/-5`) on originally untriggered cases and $+1.12 \pm 0.54\text{ (SE)}$ ($z=+2.07$, `+14/-5`) on originally triggered cases.
   - **Interpretive Boundary of the Partition:** Reopening via $\text{Trig}_{\text{VG}}$ is a *necessary condition* for originally untriggered ($	ext{Trig}=0$) cases to change, **but prefix fault cutting, continuation generation, and stage consistency gating also operate once an item is reopened**. Consequently, this partition locates where the gains occur across the original trigger groups rather than isolating the individual causal contributions of reopening, repair, and stage gating.
3. **Keeping Unsuccessful or Costly Alternatives Visible (`VG-Repair` and `VG-Judge-Casc`):**
   - **Single-Pass `VG-Repair` vs. `MSR-v3` (`@512`):** Without cascade fallback, replacing `v3_512` unconditionally with `vg_repair_512` yields only a modest, non-significant positive tendency ($+1.75 \pm 0.90\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+1.95$ on `Val210` `Orig8`; $+2.38 \pm 1.55\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+1.53$ on `Confirm350` `Orig8`), because on originally triggered items ($	ext{Trig}=1$) wins (`+23` on `Val210`, `+37` on `Confirm350` `Orig8`) are largely offset by losses (`-16` on `Val210`, `-34` on `Confirm350` `Orig8`) from unconditional non-fault selector differences and early fault cuts without a fallback stage. By contrast, `VG-Casc`—which invokes `vg_rep_512` only when `p1_512_has_fault` is true (`v3_512` otherwise) and falls back to `v2_512 -> piv_512` under `is_verified_finished`—rescues $15/16$ ($93.8\%$) of standalone `VG-Repair`'s originally triggered losses on `Val210` and $30/34$ ($88.2\%$) on `Confirm350`, yielding $+14/-5$ ($+1.12 \pm 0.54\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+2.07$) on originally triggered `Confirm350` items.
   - **Pairwise Candidate Arbitration (`VG-Judge-Casc` vs. `VG-Casc` at `@512`):** In our initial `Val210` pilot (`vg_state_transitions_val210.json`), charging only the winning branch's tokens made `VG-Judge-Casc` appear inexpensive; once full decision-procedure token accounting is enforced in Read 3 (charging both the primary/fallback tail stages and the prefix challenger stages consulted on every triggered item), `VG-Judge-Casc` consumes **$744.8$t (`Val210`) and $806.7$t (`Confirm350`)** simulated routed output tokens. While `VG-Judge-Casc` improves over 3-stage `VG-Casc` on `Val210` ($118.88$ vs. $117.00$, $+1.88 \pm 0.69\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+2.74$ `Orig8`), on `Confirm350` its incremental gain over `VG-Casc` is unresolved ($175.62$ vs. $175.00$, **$+0.62 \pm 1.17\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+0.54$ `Orig8`**; $+0.89 \pm 1.07\text{ [SE]}$, $z=+0.83$ `All9$) at $+249.0$t higher simulated token cost ($806.7$t vs. $557.7$t), and it merely ties single-pass `Pass@1(@1024)` ($175.62$ vs. $176.00$, $\Delta = -0.38 \pm 2.39\text{ [SE]}$, $489.5$t).

## 8. Discussion, Limitations, and Reproducibility Package

### 8.1 Summary of Limitations & Scope Conditions

1. **Simulated Routed Output Tokens vs. End-to-End Serving Cost:** Our JSON records measure simulated routed output tokens per question and omit prompt/state prefill tokens, KV-cache resets, CPU consistency checking, and sequential stage latency. Single-pass `Pass@1(@1024)` remains simpler and more accurate than `@512`-triggered `Casc(v3->v2->Piv)` ($173.50$ vs. $169.00$ in Read 2b; $176.00$ vs. $171.75$ in Read 3), and equal simulated output tokens between `vg_bt_1024_casc_5st` and `Pass@1(@2048)` ($573.5$t vs. $573.0$t) do not establish equal compute or latency.
2. **Conditional Intervals, Launch Variation, and Reused-Panel Qualification:** All confidence intervals in Tables 4 and 10 are question-clustered normal-approximation $95\%$ intervals conditional on the evaluated checkpoints (`Orig8` and `All9`), without adjustment for family size, cross-launch execution variability (Section 3.2), or training-seed variance. Moreover, both Read 2b (`MSR-LSR`, `BT-1024-Casc`) and Read 3 (`VG-ASR` policies) were evaluated on `Confirm350` after `Confirm350` had been observed in Read 1 (and Read 2), and are reported strictly as follow-on evaluations on a previously observed panel. The causal reasons for individual item losses (`-n01`) and any native RLVR training advantage from state-transition rewards remain open.
3. **Artifact Granularity & Reproducibility Scope Across Read 1, Read 2a, Read 2b, and Read 3:**
   - **Read 1 (`read1_*.json`)** retains recorded 64-hex-character checkpoint `sha256` identifier strings and aggregate split metrics (omitting unused `joint_*` matrices and multi-GB `.pt` weight files to stay within the $< 2,000,000$-byte archive cap), and **Read 2a (`read2a_initial_item_level_val210_confirm350.json`)** retains 22-policy item-level outcomes (`vecs`) and simulated token averages (`metrics`).
   - **Read 2b (`read2_lsr_item_level_val210_confirm350.json`)** retains recorded checkpoint `sha256` identifier strings and all $13$ primitive stage extracted candidate strings (`q_cands` + `stage_exts`) and integer stage token lengths (`stage_toks`) per item on `Val210` and `Confirm350`, enabling `verify_all_tables.py` to re-grade all $72,800$ stage slots and reconstruct all $123,200$ truncation-triggered policy outcomes and $440$ token averages from `(stage_exts, stage_toks)` alone.
   - **Read 3 (`vg_frozen_val210_confirm350.json`, plus preserved `Val210` pilot `vg_state_transitions_val210.json`)** retains recorded 64-hex-character checkpoint `sha256` identifier strings across all $11$ checkpoints, all $12$ primitive stage extracted candidate arrays (`q_cands` + `stage_exts`, $73,920$ stage slots), `judge_results`, compact per-policy selected stage codes (`route_stage`), `3` representative `reopened_examples`, stored policy outcome vectors (`vecs`), simulated routed output-token averages (`metrics`), original primary trigger bitstrings (`trig_512`, `trig_1024`, `noext_lt1024`), `fault_counts`, `judge_count`, and `contrasts`. This enables `verify_all_tables.py` to independently re-grade all $73,920$ Read 3 primitive stage candidates (and $5,584$ unique nonempty `(pred, gt)` SymPy pairs), reconstruct and re-grade all $104,720$ Read 3 item-level policy outcomes ($374$ policy totals) from the recorded selected stage codes (`route_stage`), and cross-check the $3$ `reopened_examples` (their identifiers, reference answers, original-trigger bits, and stage candidates) against the retained records. Because full Pass-1/Pass-2 derivation texts (`txt`), intermediate stage token arrays, and `judge_decision_meta` for non-example items are omitted to respect the $< 2,000,000$-byte archive limit, selected-answer re-grading on CPU does **not** regenerate consistency-dependent routing decisions, full multi-stage routing histories, or simulated token costs (which requires running `src/eval_vg_frozen_val210_confirm350.py` on GPU).
