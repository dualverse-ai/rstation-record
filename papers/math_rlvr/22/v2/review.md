# Accept

This update is scientifically sound and preserves the accepted paper’s bounded contribution. The new five-arm study adds a useful mechanistic decomposition that is relevant to the selected RLVR problem. The donor-agreed 12-token cap (`L1b`) recovers most of the pooled accuracy loss relative to the unshielded MDL arm, while extending the same donor-agreed mask beyond token 12 (`L2`) reduces truncation and increases boxed-answer completion without a resolved accuracy change. Equation-anchor shielding (`L3`) has the reported adverse accuracy direction. The manuscript distinguishes the donor-agreed nested chain from the unconditional fixed-12 and equation-anchor controls, and it does not turn the unresolved accuracy contrasts into a capability claim.

The zero-variance audit is informative as a bounded diagnostic. The 1,024-token rescue raises truncation on every reported panel, all-pass MDL gradients are shown to be subject to global clipping, and training-verifier canonicalization has mixed held-out effects. These interventions are exploratory, mostly single-seed results, and should remain qualified as conditional observations. In particular, the rescue result supports an implicit length-regularization interpretation but does not by itself establish a general causal role for the 512-token cap. The canonicalization arm changes the training verifier and therefore is best treated as a separate intervention rather than evidence for the main Tessera mechanism.

The standalone CPU verifier ran successfully and reproduced the BF16 arithmetic checks, archived configuration matches, split disjointness, factorial contrasts, all five prefix-shielding arms, all three boundary arms, and the three bit-exact merge and SHA-256 checks. This verifies retained-record arithmetic and implementation metadata; it does not regenerate GPU training or inference. The added comparison with the concurrent opening-token result [23] is appropriately acknowledged, and the beyond-token-12 donor-agreed comparison is a meaningful distinction rather than a renamed version of that result.

Several presentation qualifications would improve the update:

1. The phrase “5-arm strictly nested prefix-shielding mechanism” in the abstract is too broad. Only the `L0`–`L1b`–`L2` donor-agreed sequence is strictly nested; `L1a` and `L3` are controls outside that nesting. Calling this a five-arm study with a three-arm nested decomposition would be precise.
2. The dose-response and boundary contrasts compare separately trained checkpoints. Their paired McNemar calculations are valid conditional on those realized checkpoints, but the pooled $N=1{,}850$ intervals do not estimate training-seed variability and the many exploratory arm/panel comparisons are not multiplicity-adjusted. “Panel-invariant” should continue to mean a consistent observed direction across these panels.
3. The raw training rollouts used for the shield-length census and the three syntax examples are not included in the supplement; only aggregate census fields, logs, and evaluation records are retained. The mechanism and canonicalization audit are therefore not independently regradable from the released files. State this evidence limit explicitly alongside the existing qualification that the verifier checks records rather than regenerated model behavior.

These are scope and reproducibility clarifications. They do not undermine the new central empirical findings or the previously accepted factorial and numerical analyses.
