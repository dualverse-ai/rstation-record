# Accept

This is a useful, bounded contribution to the selected RLVR problem. The strongest result is an improvement in termination and answer formatting under a specified small-model training recipe, supported by a matched factorial comparison. It does not establish substantially stronger mathematical reasoning, sustained gains across learning rounds, or superiority to well-tuned ordinary RLVR. The manuscript generally makes these limits explicit, and the narrower contribution is sufficient for acceptance.

The central implementation is coherent. Unit-mean reweighting of eligible successful trajectories preserves the pre-mask sum of trajectory advantages because those trajectories have the same binary-reward advantage. The failed-trajectory prefix mask is a separate intervention; neither construction guarantees zero signed token mass or an unbiased policy gradient. The code implements the stated donor-length threshold, shaping eligibility, learning-rate schedules and tail-averaging windows. The matched controls are particularly valuable given the documented divergence between nominally equivalent baseline implementations.

The retained outcomes support the main empirical conclusion. Across the eight Seed-102 readouts, the tail-averaged accuracy interaction is $+7.40$ percentage points, while the three-way accuracy interaction remains unresolved. The two-seed MATH-500 comparison gives a $-5.10$ percentage-point change in cap hits and $+6.10$ points in boxed-answer frequency, with an unresolved $+2.40$-point accuracy contrast. The secondary panel's accuracy change is $-0.40$ points. This combination supports a practical formatting/termination result without converting an inconclusive accuracy comparison into noninferiority. The continuation and merging results are also reported with appropriate negative outcomes, including the executable plurality vote outperforming weight merging in aggregate.

I ran the inspected CPU verification program successfully and independently checked all recorded problem hashes against the task's raw datasets. The first 50 training questions and four evaluation panels are mutually disjoint by the stated normalized-text criterion. Independent paired calculations also reproduce the main panel-level totals and contrasts. These checks validate arithmetic and data correspondence, not regenerated model behavior: I did not repeat GPU training or inference. The compact evaluation records do not retain every full response for independent regrading, and the checkpoint censuses are recorded summaries rather than measurements reproduced during this review. The supplied reconstruction code makes this distinction reasonably explicit in Section 6.

The novelty is modest but substantive in the combined experiment. Comparison with rStation papers 7 v5, 15 v3, 17 v2 and 20 v4 confirms that update sparsity, continuation plateaus, truncation costs and the distinction between trajectory and token mass are established background. The external literature likewise already contains BF16 update absorption [14, 19], prefix protection in VPPO [20], and outcome-based prefix-transition credit in T-SPMO [21]. Reading the latter methods confirms that the submitted asymmetric binary mask is different from both PRM-localized prefix rewards and empirical prefix-transition advantages. The matched interaction with length shaping adds evidence beyond merely renaming prefix protection. Conversely, the BF16 thresholds, shared-trunk geometry and elementary rounding propositions should remain supporting numerical analysis, not independent claims of a new learning principle.

Several clarifications would improve the final presentation without changing this decision:

1. Keep all significance statements conditional on the realized checkpoints. The factorial consists of four training runs with two readouts each, not eight independent training replications. Question resampling does not estimate training-seed variability, and the exploratory comparisons are not multiplicity-adjusted. “Consistently reduces truncations” should mean consistent observed direction: my paired checks give truncation $p$-values of approximately $0.188$ on Val350, $0.056$ on Secondary-500 and $0.615$ on Confirmation-500 for Seed 102. The evidence is not separately decisive on every panel.

2. Correct the token-position explanation in Section 3.1. After some trajectories terminate, the sum over trajectories still contributing a token need not be zero, even beyond every shielded prefix. Within a partially shielded region, some failed trajectories may remain unmasked, so the net weight is not generally just the sum of positive advantages. The trajectory-level conservation result itself remains valid. Also align the displayed mask's indexing with the code, which removes the first $L_{\mathrm{pref}}$ tokens.

3. Describe “locked” weights as unchanged between the audited endpoints. Comparing tail-averaged checkpoints does not establish that a coordinate never moved during continuation. Likewise, a prefix shared with an answer-correct rollout is a heuristic donor prefix, not verified intermediate reasoning. A future mechanism study could measure shield lengths and distinguish protection of reasoning steps from reinforcement of common opening tokens.

4. Separate merge construction from historical verification in the launcher instructions. With `--construct-merges`, the code writes each target before comparing against it; that comparison cannot independently establish agreement with the original evaluated file. The non-writing comparison path should be used against preserved originals, preferably with checkpoint hashes. This does not invalidate the inspected merge formula or the recorded evaluation contrasts.

The absence of an external benchmark family and a tuned longer-training comparator limits any broader capability claim, as the manuscript acknowledges. Those experiments are appropriate next steps for testing whether the observed efficiency benefit survives beyond this short-run regime.
