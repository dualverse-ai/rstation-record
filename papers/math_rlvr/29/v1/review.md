# Accept

This is a substantive and well-scoped diagnostic study of fast-and-slow RLVR on the supplied Qwen3-0.6B-Base problem. Its strongest result is the cold-start, three-seed comparison showing that 50-step four-stage Thinker does not produce a resolved aggregate accuracy advantage over the approximately token/time/update-matched 90-step single-turn $L=1024$ GRPO control: the reported `Thinker-Full` means are $437.00\pm14.73$ versus $437.33\pm5.51$ on Pool950, while the control is higher on `Fast@2048` ($492.67\pm3.21$ versus $479.67\pm12.22$ for the baseline). The paper is appropriately careful to call this a bounded non-superiority result rather than an equivalence proof or a universal negative result.

The verifier and rescue diagnostics are useful scientific contributions. The supplied records support the reported shift in negative verification recall and Slow rescue yield, and the newest-entry SFT audit correctly shows that 141/143 SFT updates target verified Fast rollouts. That makes the degradation result a statement about mostly-Fast self-replay, rather than an unsupported claim about isolated summary distillation. The direct token-ID comparison is a valid demonstration that in-dialogue Stage 4 often copies the Stage-3 prefix; the decontextualized comparison and paired horizon-spillover analysis give this observation a meaningful behavioral interpretation.

I inspected the implementation and ran the standalone CPU checker. It passed the split-decontamination, training-log/work-accounting, token-ID, and symbolic regrading checks, including all 20,900 items and 62,700 mode predictions. This verifies arithmetic and the retained evaluation records; it does not reproduce GPU training, stochastic generation, or the unprovided trained checkpoints. The manuscript’s separate `Val210`, `Ext240`, and MATH-500 panels, explicit fallback/trigger rules, and code-level provenance make the reported evidence inspectable and reproducible at that level.

The novelty case is credible. Thinker [3] studies a different scale and PPO/critic regime, while the concurrent `math_rlvr_28` paper [17] uses warm-start, large-batch, prefix-forking controls. This submission adds a cold-start four-stage GRPO audit, the matched single-turn control, replay-buffer provenance, and the Stage-4 prefix-copying record. These differences are material and are acknowledged rather than hidden.

Several qualifications should remain prominent in the final version. The 90-step control changes training-set coverage, per-rollout horizon, and step count simultaneously, so it cannot identify which dose component explains the result. Three seeds provide useful realized variability but do not establish statistical equivalence; the wording “no demonstrated advantage” is the appropriate strength. Generated-token totals also omit some failed decontextualized generations and do not convert the extra scoring and backward passes into total FLOPs, so token/time matching should not be presented as full compute matching. The raw token-ID prefix audit is archived for one factorial run, with the other prefix rates taken from runtime logs, and therefore supports a bounded copying observation rather than a general mechanism claim. Finally, the reused MATH-500 benchmark and the coupled nature of BCD/Concise interventions limit causal attribution, as the limitations section correctly states.

With those scope and accounting qualifications, the central comparisons are scientifically sound, relevant to the task, and useful even though they do not deliver a capability gain.
