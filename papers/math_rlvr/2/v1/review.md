# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped contribution to the task’s experimental foundation. A 26.10% discrepancy in unique trainable parameters materially affects comparisons at a common model scale. Establishing that discrepancy in the supplied implementation, and distinguishing restoration of sharing from an unchanged baseline, is useful even without a reasoning-accuracy improvement. Acceptance applies to this implementation audit and bounded counterfactual, not to achievement of the task’s sustained-capability goal.

The central argument is correct. Equal embedding and output-head values preserve the initial forward function but do not establish parameter identity. The supplied constructors allocate separate Parameters, and ordinary loading copies values into them. I independently confirmed the reported counts of 751,632,384 untied and 596,049,920 tied scalars using the full architecture on the meta device. The submitted baseline and vendored model sources match the task supplement. The identity $\nabla_W L(W,W)=\nabla_E L+\nabla_H L$, the associated clipping cross-term, and the distinction between shared and separate Adam moments correctly explain why tying changes optimization. The small CPU contract passed its logits, gradient, optimizer, serialization, restoration, and unequal-array rejection checks.

The retained evidence supports the reported empirical comparison. Running the supplied checker against the task dataset reproduced re-extraction and grading of all 896 validation completions, grading of the 1,600 compact training predictions, and the first-eight-response agreement within each pair. Independent aggregation also confirmed the endpoints and transitions: 98 to 109 with 25 gained/14 lost, and 108 to 99 with 19 gained/28 lost. The paired item intervals reproduce the reported values and include zero. The seven-subject validation allocation is balanced and excludes the 50 training indices.

The interpretation is appropriately restrained. Opposite realized signs do not establish statistical equivalence, a seed-population effect, or general ineffectiveness of tying. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges these limits and the different homogeneous-group execution paths across seed blocks. Within each block, the inspected source supports the intended matching. Neither MATH-500 evaluation nor long-horizon controls are necessary to establish the architectural discrepancy; they would be necessary for the stronger capability claims the manuscript expressly avoids.

The novelty is specific rather than algorithmic. Press and Wolf [3] already derive the combined embedding update, and Inan, Khosravi and Socher [4] establish theoretical and empirical motivations for tying. Inspection of those publications and the official Qwen configuration [2] supports the manuscript’s attribution and distinction. The published same-problem self-replay study [6] investigates a different intervention and does not establish this loader discrepancy or its matched counterfactual. The implementation finding supplies the substantive contribution; generic alias checks and a two-seed sign reversal would not, by themselves, constitute a new method.

Verification has practical limits: I did not rerun GPU training or independently inspect the large base and trained checkpoints. Their exact equality/divergence statistics, full-model startup checks, and timing/memory measurements remain recorded observations supported by inspectable audit code. The public records do not permit re-extraction of later training responses, as the manuscript correctly discloses. These limits do not undermine the independently checkable parameterization result or the released validation comparisons.

Two small corrections would improve precision:

- Replace “All weights and AdamW moments use BF16” with the actual mixed storage description. The supplied implementation retains 65,536 RMSNorm scale parameters in FP32, with corresponding FP32 AdamW moments; the large embedding and projection matrices use BF16. This does not change the parameter counts or the matched comparison.
- State explicitly that validation extraction uses the last-number/full-text fallback when no boxed answer is available, whereas training rewards require a boxed extraction. The code applies this consistently across arms, but the distinction matters when reproducing the reported accuracy.
