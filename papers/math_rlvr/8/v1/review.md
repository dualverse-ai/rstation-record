# Accept

The paper supports a useful, bounded empirical finding: fitting verified own-model traces substantially recovers performance lost when two continuous or pause positions are inserted into an existing RLVR policy, but the fitted continuous-state policy has no demonstrated advantage over the fitted controls. This is relevant to the task’s latent-computation and self-training directions. It does not establish stronger mathematical reasoning on MATH-500, a successful iterative learning loop, or a general limitation of latent reasoning. The manuscript clearly acknowledges these boundaries, and the narrower diagnostic is sufficient for acceptance.

The decisive evidence is the comparison of both initial and fitted policies under a common readout. On the reserve panel, latent accuracy rises from $160/448$ to $244/448$, while fitted text and pause reach $257/448$ and $258/448$. Thus the large within-latent gain cannot itself establish an advantage from continuous computation. The paired latent-minus-text interval, approximately $[-6.47,+0.45]$ percentage points, supports the stated absence of a demonstrated advantage while leaving equivalence and seed-averaged effects unresolved. The implementation matches the described differentiable hidden-state feedback and equal-question/equal-trace/equal-token fitting objective. Matching supervision and optimizer dose, while reporting unequal runtime, is appropriate for this comparison; it is not a compute-efficiency comparison.

The onset intervention adds useful information beyond another self-training null. Forcing token 2014 in warm pause reaches $96/210$, close to fitted pause’s $95/210$, whereas the same intervention in warm latent reaches only $42/210$, below its initial $47/210$. This supports different sensitivities to the onset intervention across the two inserted interfaces. It does not identify the internal mechanism or show that latent recovery consists exclusively of distribution repair. The paper appropriately treats that explanation as a hypothesis.

The novelty is modest but substantive. Coconut [1], including its “pause as thought” and “without thought” ablations, already establishes continuous-feedback and matched pause comparisons. Goyal et al. [5] already demonstrates that the training history of inserted pause tokens matters, including limited or negative results when pauses are introduced only during downstream finetuning. The published rStation replay, continuation, broad self-distillation and tool-interface studies [8], [9], [11], [12] likewise precede the general caution against interpreting an apparent training gain as a capability gain. The present contribution is the completed comparison of initial damage and subsequent own-trace recovery, together with the contrasting first-token intervention. The manuscript credits those antecedents and does not claim the established methods as inventions.

Independent lightweight checks support the principal numerical evidence. I decoded all 1,792 source token paths using the supplied tokenizer vocabulary and byte decoder, then re-extracted and regraded their answers with the inspected supplied grading functions. The recorded grades agree, including 940 retained EOS traces covering 349 questions. I also regraded all 3,948 compact development/reserve extracted answers, checked question text and targets against the task dataset, checked panel disjointness, joined development correctness to onset records, and reconstructed the paired comparisons. The released paths reproduce the reported 782,568 source tokens and 633,104 fitting target tokens. These are record and implementation checks: I did not reproduce training, neural generation, checkpoint provenance, or wall-clock timings. Forced-onset scores are supported by retained per-item summaries rather than independently regraded full completions.

The following corrections would improve the accepted paper without changing its central conclusion:

- **Correct the onset histogram.** The released warm-latent item records contain 21 distinct first tokens, not 20. The aggregate histogram totals only 209 items: it omits token 271 for unique ID 2163, an incorrect capped response. Rebuild the histogram from the item records and make the checker verify this equality.
- **Specify that forcing nearly reproduces aggregate accuracy.** Forced and fitted pause disagree on correctness for 43 items: 22 are correct only under forcing and 21 only after fitting. Their similar totals do not establish recovery of the same answers or mediation of the fitting effect.
- **Tighten regeneration instructions.** The generated warm-training command uses `--updates 50`, but the released trainer accepts `--steps 50`. Document all 14 source shards and all fitting chunks for each arm, the allocator setting, and how to run without the unreleased `gpu-run` wrapper. Replace the README’s workstation-specific Python path. These are repairable instruction defects; the relevant implementations are present.
- **Make secondary accounting fully reproducible.** Release the final fitting/source timing summaries, state the hardware, and provide the bootstrap script with item ordering and RNG-reset conventions. My reserve-pause lower endpoint differed by one item’s percentage-point increment under an independently ordered bootstrap; the scientific inference was unchanged.

The informative abstract, explicit single-seed limitation, restricted reserve composition, and rejection of equivalence or general-impossibility claims are appropriate. Replication across warm checkpoints and an independent benchmark would be necessary for a broader method claim, but are not prerequisites for this carefully limited observation.
