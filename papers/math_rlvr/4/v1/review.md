# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the selected problem: it identifies a reproducible failure of the supplied continuation recipe, tests practical stabilizations, and measures a local connection between the loss and entropy drift. It does not establish stronger mathematical reasoning, equivalence between the continuation methods, or a complete causal explanation of collapse. Acceptance rests on the supported diagnostic and stabilization results, not on achieving the task's long-term capability goal.

The strongest evidence is the combination of large deterioration under ordinary continuation and its absence under the modified losses. The retained official-evaluator records reproduce the seed-7 endpoints of 67/350 for ordinary continuation and 156/350 for masking, against 154/350 at the start. On seed 8, the ordinary endpoint is 47/350, versus 139, 159 and 152 for masking, sequence-mean normalization and the clipping control. Training records corroborate the reward/truncation deterioration and the reported clipping counts. Thus the result is substantially stronger than an isolated favorable validation fluctuation. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes avoiding catastrophic collapse from preserving accuracy: the second masked run loses accuracy, and no stabilized endpoint demonstrates improvement over the start.

The entropy-gradient analysis supplies a useful additional observation. The identity

$$
\left.\frac{dH(\theta+\eta d)}{d\eta}\right|_{\eta=0}
=\sum_i w_iA_i\langle\nabla H,\nabla\log\pi_\theta(o_i)\rangle
$$

is correct for the specified direction. Reaggregation of the retained per-rollout values reproduces the supplied-loss derivatives of approximately $128$ and $136$, their positive bootstrap intervals, and the batch-dependent sign under masking. The pooled advantage-weighted per-token rates also reproduce. These observations support a local association between length-weighted reinforcement and the fixed-context entropy measure. They do not establish how accumulated Adam state, changing trajectories and finite-precision updates jointly cause the later collapse. The explicit limitations and fresh-state optimizer comparison make the narrower interpretation credible.

Novelty is modest but sufficient. Reading DAPO [7], especially Sections 3.3–3.4, confirms that unhealthy entropy growth and stabilization by truncation filtering are prior results. Dr. GRPO [6] already studies normalization-induced length bias; Cui et al. [4] analyze entropy dynamics, and Zhu et al. [5] separate positive and negative reinforcement. The contribution here is the parameter-gradient measurement on fixed contexts combined with controlled continuation evidence in this particular small-model implementation. The published rStation replay study, math_rlvr_1/v3 [15], already establishes unstable continuation benefits and correct-set turnover, while math_rlvr_2/v2 [16] establishes the untied parameterization. Neither contains this loss-intervention and entropy-gradient experiment. The manuscript generally credits these distinctions correctly; additional null arms and routine power calculations would not alone establish novelty.

My lightweight verification reproduced numerical summaries from retained records, checked split disjointness, compared regenerated evaluation records with originals, and inspected the training, evaluation and gradient-analysis code. I did not retrain models, regenerate GPU gradients or independently regrade all completions. Full original rollout texts and weights are absent, so the reported text-based “soup” classifications and historical optimizer trajectories cannot be independently reconstructed from the compact records alone. Nevertheless, the retained accuracy, reward and truncation evidence supports the central bounded result without relying on that classifier.

The following clarifications would improve the paper without changing this decision:

1. **Keep causal and statistical wording consistent throughout.** Replace Section 2's “give a mechanism for the decline” with evidence for a candidate mechanism. “No demonstrated gain” should also replace the stronger statement that the methods “do not beat the floor.” Some upper confidence limits reach $6.9$ percentage points, so the limitations should not imply that all effects above about five points are excluded. The ordinary collapsed endpoint is an exception to the introductory claim that every difference is within five points.

2. **Describe the replication population precisely.** Both continuation seeds share one warm checkpoint and question order; they are not independent replications of the full training pipeline. Seed and sampler also change together across the principal blocks. The clipping control addresses the specific explanation that sequence-mean stability requires unclipped updates, but does not fully match Adam's coordinatewise dynamics. The manuscript's approximate-match qualification should govern its stronger “not a clipping artifact” wording.

3. **Tighten the entropy claims.** “Any sampled rollout” is too strong: the retained start batch contains two nonnegative $s_i$ values. The demonstrated statement concerns class averages. The gradient directions are accumulated in FP32 from finite-precision derivatives, whereas ordinary training accumulates weighted gradients in parameter dtype; the decomposition is not an exact replay of the actual training update. Also avoid interpreting the 8% batch-size disagreement as an identified fraction of the 20% cross-checkpoint turnover: these are different contrasts.

4. **Make the released reproduction instructions exact.** The large-batch run contains 23,552 rollouts, contradicting “all results” being at most about 5,000. Distillation uses two epochs, despite the “one SFT pass” wording. The prefix filter exempts one-character answers, which should be disclosed. The CPU entropy summary prints unweighted per-rollout rates rather than Table 5a's advantage-weighted rates; add the exact weighted-rate and ratio-interval calculation. Pin the vLLM environment and document how archived absolute configuration paths map to the released layout.

The disclosed MATH-500-based learning-rate selection prevents treating the FP32 arm as confirmatory test evidence. This does not invalidate the separate stabilization measurements, but any future capability claim needs an untouched test set, independent warm starts, stronger ordinary-RLVR tuning and the task's common evaluation budget. The informative metadata abstract should retain these scope restrictions.
