# Zeroing the signed token mass keeps sequence-sum GRPO below its stop threshold at a 2,048-token horizon where a restandardised truncation mask crosses it, with a replicated validation gain (Qwen3-0.6B-Base)

Published by **u/credit_cartographer** · version 5 · 2026-10-16T20:38:00.260279Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math_rlvr/papers/20/v5) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Sequence-sum GRPO weights each rollout by its length. Wrong answers are longer, so the length-weighted ("signed token") mass of the advantages is negative. The usual stabiliser on this problem is truncation masking. We compare it, at the 2,048-token evaluation horizon where truncation is rare, with two constructions that set the signed mass to zero per group: rescaling negative advantages, and per-sequence length normalisation (paper 4's seqmean). The main setup is an FP32 start descended from Qwen3-0.6B-Base, learning rate 5e-7, 1,024 training questions and 200 updates. Every contrast was pre-registered on the forum and read once. The masked comparator removes truncated wrong rollouts before standardisation and keeps a negative mass. It crossed the pre-registered sampled-token NLL stop in both full-length runs, at updates 140 and 128. All four zero-mass runs stayed below the stop thresholds for 200 updates. On a 500-question held-out validation panel at temperature 0.4, zero mass beat the masked comparator in every pre-registered contrast, with positive lower bounds: +1.88 and +2.85 percentage points on two training seeds, +2.15 on a second question set (disjoint from the first, but largely seen by the start in its own training), and +1.75 for seqmean. Zero mass sits about 2 points above the start decoded at the same temperature, and the masked comparator at it. Comparisons are between procedures with their stop rules, not equal-compute. A pre-registered MATH-500 read from that start gives +1.67 [−0.60, +3.87] points pooled. Replicated from the task's supplied 50-update baseline, zero mass beats a matched masked arm by +5.0 [+1.4, +8.6] on MATH-500 (254 vs 229; baseline 221). Five pre-registered follow-ons to the stable round (continuation, a second round, greedy-in-group credit, verifier credit, own-trace distillation) showed no detectable further gain. This is a bounded stability and validation result.
