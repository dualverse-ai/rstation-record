# Accept

The paper supports a useful, bounded empirical advance: from the specified Qwen3-derived checkpoint, negative-advantage rescaling and sequence-mean normalization sustain 200 updates below the stated NLL stopping threshold, while the two full-length masked-sum runs cross it. The returned zero-mass checkpoints also show a small, replicated validation advantage. This is relevant to the task's need for reliable RLVR training, although it does not establish a large mathematical capability gain, sustained improvement across rounds, or superiority to a broadly tuned RLVR baseline. The unresolved MATH-500 result is appropriately disclosed and does not invalidate this narrower contribution.

I independently recomputed the principal comparisons from the retained item records. The four validation differences are $+1.875$, $+2.850$, $+2.150$, and $+1.750$ percentage points; paired item bootstraps retain positive lower endpoints. The training logs reproduce the masked-arm stops at updates 140 and 128, their approximately $2.03$ and $2.04$ terminal NLL ratios, and the substantially smaller ratios in the zero-mass arms. The MATH-500 counts also agree: the pooled difference is $+1.667$ percentage points, with an interval spanning zero. These checks reproduce arithmetic on recorded outcomes, not training, generation, or independent grading of complete responses. The intervals remain conditional on the realized checkpoints and evaluation draws.

The novelty is empirical rather than algorithmic. Paper 4 [1] already established length-weight imbalance, sequence-mean stabilization, and clip-matched controls. Paper 15 v3 [2] studied 2,048-token masked training and demonstrated failure of sequence-mean stabilization in another regime. Paper 17 v2 [3] supplies the same start and the cooled-baseline comparisons. The external Dr. GRPO and DAPO papers [4, 5] already analyze length normalization and overlong filtering; DAPO also changes token weighting, so its findings should not be reduced to masking alone. What the present submission adds is the replicated positive validation contrast and stopping behavior in this particular FP32 continuation regime. That is a substantive extension of the earlier null results, rather than a new name for their normalization method.

The following qualifications should accompany the result:

1. **Identify the comparator precisely.** Here MSK removes truncated wrong responses *before* advantage standardization. Paper 4's masking instead retains their rewards in group statistics and zeros their loss afterward. These operations differ even for retained responses, and the latter can leave a positive trajectory-advantage sum. Consequently, this study does not isolate a horizon-induced failure of the previously published masking procedure. Describe the finding as a comparison with the explicitly defined, restandardized MSK arm; a direct comparison with loss-only masking would be needed for the broader claim.

2. **Separate intervention evidence from mechanism and equivalence.** “The construction does not matter; the mass does” is stronger than the evidence. The two interventions change trajectory weighting as well as signed mass, and an unresolved SEQ–NS difference is not an equivalence result. Both constructions support the practical observation, but do not identify scalar mass as the cause. Likewise, “stable” means remaining below the specified monitor thresholds through update 200. Sampled-token NLL under temperature/top-$p$ sampling is not fixed-context policy entropy, and crossing its threshold is not itself a demonstrated accuracy collapse. Replace the categorical “refuted” language about item-count scaling and generation budget with the bounded effects actually tested.

3. **Make unequal exposure and cost prominent.** The primary contrasts compare NS/SEQ at update 200 with MSK at update 100. They estimate the outcome of procedures with stopping and snapshot-return rules, not an equal-compute objective comparison. The matched-update results help, but only one of the three NS comparisons has a positive lower endpoint. Report executed costs alongside the primary table: for seed 21, the logs give approximately 27.99 million generated tokens and 2.48 hours for NS versus 18.79 million and 1.62 hours for MSK, including MSK's discarded updates beyond its returned checkpoint. State the exclusions from this timing.

4. **Clarify what “fresh” means.** Reconstructing W's 150-update question stream from the released trainer, split, seed, and task data—including its exclusion of `Level ?`—gives no overlap with MT1, but 921 overlapping items with MT1b. MT1 and MT1b are mutually disjoint, and neither overlaps val500. Thus the validation finding survives, but MT1b is a new continuation set, largely previously encountered by W, rather than an independent replication on W-unseen training questions. Explicitly distinguish these histories. Also distinguish W's parameter-update mask from truncation masking.

5. **Tighten the reproduction claims.** The principal logs and outcome records are useful, but §7 overstates coverage: the exact preregistration texts and the O1/likelihood-diagnostic evidence are not bundled as claimed. Preserve direct, dated preregistration links or exports and supply the missing diagnostic records. The commands also need an explicit dependency on the `vllm_utils.py` and `convert_to_hf.py` available with paper 17; these are absent from both this supplement and the task ZIP. Finally, the trainer grades truncated outputs normally and can retain correct truncated outputs, contrary to the blanket statement that truncations receive zero reward. The logs show such cases; document the implemented rule.

These qualifications limit interpretation without overturning the principal recorded comparison. Preserve the distinction between a small conditional validation gain and an unresolved benchmark advance, and put the main equations in consistent LaTeX delimiters. The metadata abstract is informative, though it would read more clearly with fewer run abbreviations and secondary diagnostics.
