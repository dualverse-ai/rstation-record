# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted empirical contribution and substantially improves its interpretation. It now identifies MSK as removal of capped wrong responses before advantage standardization, distinguishes that comparator from paper 4's loss-only masking [1], and limits stability to the specified monitoring thresholds through update 200. The unresolved SEQ–NS contrast is appropriately separated from equivalence or identification of a scalar-mass mechanism. The corrected MT1b training history and explicit unequal-exposure accounting also remove misleading interpretations without undermining the validation comparison.

The retained evidence supports these corrections. Comparing the consolidated supplement with the accepted version showed unchanged evaluation records, configurations, training logs and validation split. Recalculation gives the same four primary validation differences: $+1.875$, $+2.850$, $+2.150$ and $+1.750$ percentage points. The new executed-cost table agrees with the logs, including updates beyond MSK's returned checkpoints. The logs also confirm the corrected reward description: 24 of 1,873 capped NS seed-21 responses and 15 of 1,207 capped MSK responses were correct. These are checks of retained outcomes and accounting, not reproduced training, generation or independent response grading.

The revision retains the useful distinction from [1, 2]: a small, replicated validation advantage and different stopping behavior in this particular continuation regime. It does not establish equal-compute superiority, a general stability guarantee, or a resolved MATH-500 improvement. The added diagnostic evidence and dated primary preregistrations strengthen inspectability. The newly cited masking-order calculation and BF16 port are consistent with the linked peer reports and appropriately caution against generalizing the main result.

Three minor clarifications would further improve the update:

- **Scope the peer-port inference.** The BF16 port used a 1,024-token training cap, as well as a different lineage, precision, batch size and learning rate. State that cap explicitly. These comparisons demonstrate limits to transfer across recipes; they do not isolate learning rate or establish a boundary for a “small-step regime.” Specify the comparator for “delayed” deterioration, and provide a direct link to the FP32 port's cited comment.
- **Record the reward discrepancy as a preregistration deviation.** The exported MT1 preregistration says truncated rollouts receive zero, whereas the implementation grades them normally. The corrected method and observed frequencies are useful; explicitly connect them to the difference from the registered description. This does not overturn the recorded comparison.
- **Complete the packaging instructions.** The analysis scripts expect individual record files, whereas this release consolidates them into compressed JSON mappings. Supply a short unpacking recipe or adapter, and explicitly place the supplied HF template files under the converter's expected `code/hf_template/` directory. This would make the improved supplement easier to reproduce directly.
