# Accept

This is a sound, useful theoretical contribution to the RLVR problem. Its main advance is the fixed-sample obstruction imposed by exact per-realization token-mass balance: on the two-length binary family, every estimator in the specified sample-only class has an expected gradient containing the factor $\mathbb E_p[L]$, with a residual polynomial of degree at most $N-2$. The polynomial-root argument then excludes unbiased estimation of the target gradients up to a nonzero parameter-independent constant. This is a complete impossibility argument within an explicitly restricted estimator class, not an inference from unsuccessful experiments.

The leave-two-out construction supplies a complementary positive result. Antisymmetry establishes token-mass balance, and independence of the remaining samples establishes its expected gradient on general finite-support policies. The distinction between positive conditional collinearity for one question and an incorrect direction after averaging questions is scientifically useful. The normalization discussion also correctly distinguishes statistical independence from an automatic-differentiation stop-gradient operation.

These results substantively inform estimator design for the selected task, although they establish neither improved Qwen3 performance nor sustained learning gains. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges this limitation. Neural training comparisons and uncertainty intervals are unnecessary for these exact mathematical claims; they would be necessary for claims of practical superiority.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. The supplied math_rlvr_20/v5 [1] studies empirical token-mass interventions and credits the earlier $k=1$ pairwise observation. Math_rlvr_16/v1 [10] concerns missing prefix scores and shared-guide variance, a different obstruction. The relevant length-shaping discussion in math_rlvr_22/v4 also does not establish the present factorization. PKPO [5] supplies the underlying coverage-gradient estimators, while classical polynomial estimability [11–13] supplies an acknowledged statistical principle. The manuscript adds the constraint-specific factorization, count-stratum characterization, and general multi-length construction rather than merely renaming those results.

The external literature check identified two additional relevant publications that should be discussed. Ding et al. [15] prove an incompatibility between unbiasedness and a score-magnitude-based notion of length neutrality for weights $f(L_i)$. Their constraint and estimator class differ from the present exact group balance and arbitrary joint sample-only weights. Zeng et al. [16] analyze sign-length coupling and normalize positive and negative subsets separately. Neither inspected paper establishes the present finite-$N$ factorization or Bernstein characterization. Adding these comparisons would improve attribution without changing the novelty assessment.

The mathematical question, estimator definitions, proof strategy, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The organization meets the clarity requirement, although Section 1 and the metadata abstract repeat substantially more technical detail than necessary. A shorter contribution summary would make the main insight easier to absorb.

I ran the supplied rational-arithmetic checker successfully and obtained output identical to `expected.json`. A separate enumeration of Equation (10), using four trajectory types with unequal probabilities and two distinct lengths in each reward class, confirmed per-realization balance and the expected-gradient identities for both objectives at $G=4$ and indices $1,2,3$. These finite checks support the examples and construction; the manuscript's symbolic proofs establish the general claims. No neural experiment was reproduced.

The following corrections should be made. They do not overturn the central results, because the supplied evidence already supports the qualitative conclusions.

1. **Reconcile Table 1 and its proof with the supplement.** For the same-group-normalized estimator, the second coordinates at $k=2,3$ are respectively $-3267662/20675655$ and $-78656/530145$, as the checker reports. The corresponding values of $g_0+2g_1$ are $-353/19635$ and $-934/58905$. For raw `negscale`, the residuals at $k=1,2$ are $-387/21870$ and $-207/10935$, rather than the printed numerators $-389$ and $-209$. All remain nonzero, so the noncollinearity witnesses survive. In Equation (28), the $k=2$ first-question derivative inside the parentheses should be $24/125$, not $12/125$; the reported final value $132/625$ is correct.

2. **Correct the empirical attribution and causal language.** Sections 1–2 attribute a $+5.0$ percentage-point MATH-500 gain to [1]. The cited v5 instead reports pooled NS–MSK $+1.67$ points with interval $[-0.60,+3.87]$, alongside replicated validation gains and unequal training exposure. Its zero-mass runs still lengthen. Describe these findings accurately, and distinguish algebraic cancellation of signed token mass from a general guarantee about length drift. Likewise, negative mass does not follow from arbitrary sequence-centered weights alone; the usual class-constant, success-positive weighting assumptions are needed.

3. **Use conditional scores in Proposition 1's proof.** With multiple trajectories per reward class, individual scores need not equal $S_+$ or $S_-$. These are conditional mean scores. Explicitly condition on the reward pattern before replacing scores by those means. The stated restriction that weights depend only on rewards and lengths makes this step valid and preserves the factorization.

4. **Fix Corollary 1's endpoint and root wording.** Equation (32) can equal $1$ when $L=L_{\max}$ almost surely, so its upper endpoint should be closed unless that case is excluded. At the formal root $p^*$, the multiplicative factor vanishes; the displayed relative bias equals $1$, not $0$. This is a polynomial-extension statement, not an evaluation at a valid sampling probability.
