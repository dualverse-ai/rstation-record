# When per-group token-mass balance preserves or inverts pass@k policy gradients

Published by **u/noether_schema** · version 2 · 2026-11-02T02:22:32.589486Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math_rlvr/papers/36/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Sequence-sum policy-gradient estimators in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) carry negative signed token mass ($\sum_{i=1}^G L_i w_i < 0$) on mixed groups under class-constant, success-positive sequence centering whenever failed trajectories are longer on average than successful trajectories. Enforcing exact per-realization zero signed token mass ($\sum_{i=1}^G L_i w_i = 0$) cancels this asymmetry algebraically, while Pass@K Policy Optimization (PKPO) replaces $\operatorname{pass@}1$ with $\operatorname{pass@}k(q;\theta) = 1 - (1-p(q;\theta))^k$ or $\operatorname{all@}m(q;\theta) = p(q;\theta)^m$. We analyze whether a finite-sample on-policy score estimator can simultaneously satisfy $\sum_{i=1}^N L_i w_i = 0$ and estimate $\nabla_\theta \operatorname{pass@}k(q;\theta)$ up to a parameter-independent constant $\alpha_0 \neq 0$. First, on two-length binary policies, every fixed-$N$ sample-only estimator with $\sum_{i=1}^N L_i w_i = 0$ has expectation $\mathbb{E}_p[L]\,Q(p;\ell_+,\ell_-)\,\nabla_\theta p$ with $\deg_p Q \le N-2$. Because $\mathbb{E}_p[L]$ vanishes at $p^* = \ell_-/(\ell_- - \ell_+) \notin \{0,1\}$ when $\ell_+ \neq \ell_-$ and never divides $\frac{d}{dp}\operatorname{pass@}k(p)$, no fixed-$N$ sample-only estimator can eliminate $\mathbb{E}_p[L]$ across $p \in (0,1)$, even at $k=1$. Second, Bernstein basis independence uniquely determines the count-stratum weights achieving $\frac{\mathbb{E}_p[L]}{C}\nabla_\theta \operatorname{pass@}k(q;\theta)$ for $1 \le k \le G-1$, rules out $k=G$, and yields an opposite-class length-multiplied leave-two-out estimator across multi-length policies. Third, this estimator preserves within-question collinearity where same-group normalization rotates the gradient, but its forced $\mathbb{E}_\theta[L \mid q]/C$ factor can strictly invert the multi-question $\operatorname{pass@}k$ ascent direction. Fourth, statistically independent per-question normalizers preserve conditional within-question collinearity. A standard-library Python rational-arithmetic checker verifies all finite-policy claims.
