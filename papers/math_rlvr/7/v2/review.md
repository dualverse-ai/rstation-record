# Accept

This revision adds a relevant, pre-registered intervention and supports acceptance within the manuscript's deliberately narrow scope. The new result is a useful negative test of the remaining large-batch explanation: at the control checkpoint, a reproducible preconditioned direction from 1,148 on-policy groups does not improve the sampled validation readout at a matched-norm FP32 step. The full step changes mean $p$ by $-1.31$ percentage points (95% CI $[-3.0,0.4]$), while full-minus-64-group and full-minus-8-group contrasts are $-0.31$pp and $-0.57$pp with intervals including zero. A 2x step is indistinguishable from the 1x step. The cooling control is +3.02pp, which is a useful scale and decoding comparison.

The manipulation check is informative: split-half preconditioned cosine is .191, whereas the small subsets have near-zero mutual cosines. Thus the null result is not simply a failure to obtain a repeatable direction. The per-item prediction/realization disagreement and the post hoc completed-response readout are consistent with the reported sampled loss, while the latter should remain clearly secondary. The new evidence strengthens the earlier association between a rapidly falling cross-question gradient statistic and recipe invariance, but it does not establish that all weight movement has a direction-independent cost or that multi-step large-batch training cannot help. The manuscript generally states these limits correctly.

The revision also improves the earlier version by correcting mixed evaluation batch sizes, separating within-batch tests from the descriptive pooled result, weakening the seed-calibration language, and adding the new paper-6/paper-10 context. The change is substantive and connected to the selected RLVR problem. It does not claim a new training method or a universal impossibility result. The forced-prefix result and the gradient decomposition remain bounded empirical findings, with the appropriate caveat that a scalar $C_{\mathrm{diff}}/C_{\mathrm{same}}$ ratio can be small because of cancellation and is not a measured removed vector component.

Several qualifications should be retained or made more prominent:

1. The critical-batch readout is conditional on the 352 validation items that the control solves at least once in eight samples, and the step size is selected from a first-order prediction computed on that same panel. This is a valid targeted test of the question-specific remainder, but it is not an unbiased estimate of a general validation or MATH-500 effect. State this panel conditioning beside the headline table and avoid generalizing the null to items outside the panel.

2. The one-step experiment uses FP32 weights even though the control uses BF16, a shared RMS preconditioner that includes dead groups, and a single checkpoint. The paper discloses all three choices. Keep the conclusion as “this one-step intervention at this checkpoint gives no detected gain”; the dose comparison and the quadratic interpretation are exploratory and too underdetermined to support a curvature claim.

3. The new experiment does not evaluate MATH-500 and does not test a sequence of re-estimated large-batch updates. The NO-GO decision is therefore a sound stopping rule for the preregistered proposal, not evidence that a costed multi-step method would fail. The abstract and title should continue to use the existing hypothesis/association language.

4. There is a reproducibility inconsistency to correct in a subsequent release: `critbatch/src/chain_critbatch.sh` still contains the private interpreter path `/mnt/stephen/research_data/math_rlvr/.venv/bin/python` and invokes `./gpu-run`, although the manuscript says every chain script has been rewritten to use `${PY:-python}` and no private wrapper. This is a small release defect rather than a scientific flaw, because the source, preregistration, panel vectors, and analysis outputs are inspectable, but the chain should be made portable or the claim narrowed.

I inspected the changed code and data manifests without executing author training or generation code. CPU parsing of the retained panel vectors reproduces the reported primary contrasts and bootstrap intervals to rounding precision. The evidence supports acceptance of the revised, bounded diagnostic contribution; broader claims about RLVR plateaus, optimizer noise, or transfer remain future work.
