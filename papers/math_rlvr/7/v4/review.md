# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds useful, bounded follow-ups. The strongest addition is the matched forced-onset training comparison: it tests a practical consequence of the earlier onset hypothesis while explicitly acknowledging that it does not isolate gradient alignment as a mediator. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes failure to resolve a gain from equivalence or exhausted reasoning capacity. The Section 4.14 wording correction also addresses the previous review.

Independent recomputation from the new correctness vectors reproduces all ten reported paired comparisons, including their bootstrap intervals and exact McNemar probabilities. Masking improves validation accuracy over the unmasked continuation by $7.0$ percentage points, with interval $[3.0,11.2]$, but neither masked seed resolves an improvement over the original control. Forced-onset training versus its matched control gives $-0.8$ points, $[-4.8,3.0]$. The soup's MATH-500 difference is $+1.2$ points, $[-2.6,5.0]$. Token counts also reproduce the horizon decomposition: among 447 items completed by both checkpoints, 39 improve and 51 regress. These checks reproduce statistics from retained evidence; they do not independently reproduce training, generation, or answer grading.

Masking and checkpoint averaging are established techniques. Papers [33]–[34] already document stabilization without a resolved held-out gain, and [24] supplies the soup method. The new experiments are useful extensions within this accepted study, especially the forced-onset comparison, rather than new stabilization or averaging methods. Direct attribution to DAPO for overlong filtering would improve Section 4.15.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany the update:

1. **The coverage comparator is not established as a “truth-0” calibration.** The supplied `onmask.sh` and `longmask.sh` both use seed 24, the same training split, and eight questions per update. In `rl.py`, these settings determine the same shuffled question order: the masked 120-update run's questions should be a subset of the 300-update run's questions. This contradicts the stated different question stream. Release the actual question identifiers if the executed schedules differed; otherwise describe this as an overlapping-exposure comparison and withdraw its interpretation as a known-null calibration. The reported nonpositive similarity gradient can remain a descriptive observation, but this comparator does not validate it.

2. **Reconcile the horizon gate with the retained plan.** `prereg_longmask.md` originally conditions MATH-500 evaluation on both validation score at least 261 and monotonic improvement across snapshots. Its result notes launch the update-120 evaluation before the later, declining snapshots are available. Section 4.15 instead describes a score-only gate. Disclose this deviation and distinguish the descriptive MATH-500 result from compliance with the original decision rule. It does not alter the reported null comparison.

3. **Complete the reproducibility instructions for the additions.** The new launchers contain private working directories, interpreter paths, and a GPU wrapper. Provide portable equivalents and a conversion from the compressed vectors to the JSONL format expected by `paired.py`. The coverage script needs unshipped rollout identifiers, and `list(set(tr))` makes its supposedly seeded untrained draw depend on Python hash ordering. Preserve the actual identifiers and similarity scores, and use a stable candidate ordering. Archive compact training summaries supporting the new trajectory claims; the released vectors establish endpoint comparisons, not those trajectories.

These limitations do not overturn the supported endpoint results. Their intervals remain conditional on the observed checkpoints, mostly single training seeds, and a repeatedly used validation panel. The update supports the stated bounded negative findings, not a general limit on RLVR.
