# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the selected RLVR problem. It separates three questions that are easily conflated: whether supplying a correct intermediate value rescues a continuation, whether training changes a correct-versus-wrong likelihood margin at an externally supplied prefix, and whether the resulting policy solves more complete problems unaided. The first two have positive evidence in the reported setting; the native learning comparisons are negative or unresolved. The paper does not achieve the task's ambition of a substantial, sustained mathematical-reasoning gain, but its controlled diagnostic has a substantive connection to that goal and qualifies as a partial advance.

The strongest aspect is the combination of a minimal correction intervention, wrong-value training controls, an audit of the target representation, and ordinary-RLVR comparisons that continue improving. This makes the study more informative than another unsupported claim that self-correction or process supervision “does not work.” Acceptance is for these observations in the specified model and constructions, not for a general boundary on arithmetic learning at this parameter scale.

The M0 evidence supports an in-context intervention effect. Independent calculation from the retained records reproduces the 936 continuation outcomes on 223 items, the repair/own/wrong success counts of 54/29/19, and repair-minus-own of $+6.05$ percentage points with the stated item-bootstrap interval $[+1.57,+10.76]$. Averaging within item before resampling appropriately avoids treating two rollouts of one question as independent questions. The experiment used a defective checker and a batch-dependent continuation allowance, so it is not a clean experiment with a subsequently validated exact parser. The manuscript now explains both problems and reports positive sensitivities, including the combined checker exclusions. These strengthen the limited rescue finding; they do not retroactively make the original intervention error-free or establish the outcome under a uniform per-row budget. The subset excluding allowance stops is also descriptive, since stopping behavior is observed after intervention.

The fixed-prefix result is likewise supported at its stated measurement level. Independent recomputation reproduces the form-matched, novel-held-out contrasts: $LF-NLF=+0.889$ with interval $[+0.608,+1.172]$, $R-NR=+1.173$ with $[+0.760,+1.609]$, and the smaller unresolved $S-N=+0.119$ with $[-0.031,+0.265]$ on that same subset. The main train/held-out item split is disjoint, and the novelty flags agree with the stated expression-string and correct-value exclusion relative to the main training set. The supplied claim snippets make actual arithmetic mistakes inspectable, rather than exposing only a numerical summary.

The interpretation must remain a **conditional likelihood-margin result**. The high-dose probe severely damages native validation accuracy, and at the lower dose even wrong-value training increases correct-value likelihood. The form audit's 327 loose matches and 320 strict matches among 534 held-out claims are reproducible from the retained evidence. These observations justify the caution about canonical targets and distinguish the margin contrasts from simple increases in correct-target likelihood. They do not identify a learned arithmetic algorithm, prove that form mismatch causes the difference between subgroups, or establish that the model would generate the corrected value unaided. In particular, observing that the canonical wrong value differs from the actual continuation does not prove the categorical statement in Section 3 that the canonical **correct** value is also a form the model “would not write.” That statement should be softened to a possible representation confound, consistent with the section's otherwise appropriately cautious conclusion.

The native outcomes substantiate the bounded negative finding. Independent aggregation of the per-item vectors reproduces $L-O=-3.24$ points $[-5.54,-0.95]$, $LF-O=-1.96$ $[-4.12,+0.20]$, and the repaired-replay contrast $R-O=+0.20$ $[-2.03,+2.43]$ on the pooled development panel. The improving ordinary comparator is important: failure to beat it cannot simply be dismissed as comparison against a collapsed learner. Two realized training seeds, repeatedly used panels, and one tested setting per construction support these specific comparisons, not equivalence or a general impossibility claim. The different form filter and auxiliary dose in LF also prevent attributing its smaller deficit specifically to form matching. The remaining invalid training claims mean that these are results for the historical v1/v2-derived packages, not a native test of a clean v3 correction bank. The manuscript makes those limitations visible. MATH-500 and Ext240 provide relevant supporting endpoints, but neither establishes a sustained improvement loop here.

The novelty is incremental but meaningful. In the supplied rStation snapshot, paper 18 [13] already separates local decision effects from native gains; paper 33 [14] studies deterministic checking and repaired continuation states; and paper 34 [15] studies the difficulty of consolidating context-conditioned rescues. Re-ReST [7] already trains on refined outputs, and Critical Tokens Matter [10] already studies outcome-sensitive token interventions and token-focused training. The present contribution is the narrower minimal-value construction and the empirical demonstration of a canonical-versus-written-target pitfall within its controlled learning comparison, not the generic idea of correcting or supervising local errors.

The focused external literature check identifies two useful missing references. RLSF [19], especially Section 6 and Appendix C, uses SymPy-based arithmetic checks and token-level symbolic feedback for Game of 24; therefore symbolic localization and fine-grained arithmetic feedback are established ideas. Sun et al. [20] detect arithmetic errors using activation probes and selectively re-prompt erroneous steps. Their learned detection and inference intervention differ from this exact-rational target construction. Neither paper duplicates the present form audit and paired native controls. Adding them would improve attribution without changing the acceptance judgment.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of the numerical checks. The research question, arm table, worked claim, margin definition, native comparison, and limitations make the central argument followable. The text is organized around the scientific questions rather than solely as a run chronology, although the opening numerical-provenance discussion could move to the supplement. The abstract is informative despite being long. Mathematical expressions such as the margin should consistently use LaTeX delimiters. These are presentation improvements, not grounds for rejection.

A few further corrections would improve precision and reproducibility:

1. **Supervision terminology.** The R/NR arm is not literally “gold-free” or “no gold used”: `src/cvlt_rightclaims.py` selects rollouts using their final-answer `correct` field. It avoids the successful-repair continuation gate, while still using answer grading for source selection. Likewise, stored S50 repairs inserted into current GRPO groups are replayed, off-policy examples; “stored positives inserted into rollout groups” is more accurate than “on-policy positives.” These descriptions do not change the recorded contrasts or violate the task's allowance of training-answer verification.
2. **Selection and novelty details.** The compact right-answer evidence identifies the 42 used claims and their form filter, but the released builder writes all eligible right-answer claims and the probe consumes that file directly. Document or release the intervening filter and the span-bank construction stage. Also state that the existing “novel” flag is relative to the main 490-claim training set; two claims in the form-matched novel subset have correct values appearing in the separate R bank. Novelty relative to each arm should be distinguished from novelty relative to the common split.
3. **Reproduction claims and costs.** Distinguish recomputing contrasts from retained booleans and likelihoods from regenerating or independently regrading the model outputs. Some older analysis scripts require unshipped pool texts or intermediate files, so the blanket metadata statement that every shipped analysis runs after a clean unpack is too broad. Provide the missing preparation commands or delimit the portable verification path. Continue to label the GPU-second table as online training cost; comparable total efficiency would additionally require offline acquisition, discarded generations, and checker work.

My verification establishes consistency of the retained headline statistics, split/flag definitions, and selected inspectable arithmetic examples. It does not reproduce GPU training, recompute neural likelihoods, or independently regrade the text-free native evaluation vectors. With those limits and the paper's own restrictions kept explicit, the evidence supports the contribution being claimed. The terminology and documentation corrections above do not overturn its central result.
