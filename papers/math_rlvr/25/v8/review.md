# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded study of adaptive inference and online counterfactual fork training. The new results support descriptive comparisons between complete training packages; they do not establish a stronger single-policy RLVR method or isolate the causal benefit of rescue gating. The manuscript generally maintains that distinction. The specific experiments extend the acknowledged fork-credit, prefix-shielding, and high-entropy-token work [7], [17], [21] without establishing priority for those underlying ideas.

Independent reconstruction from the retained records reproduces the tie-gated result of $514/950$ selected-answer successes, including $247/500$ on MATH-500; the $3,302$ branch count; all $920$ step-gating decisions and $329$ active groups; the new per-seed accuracy totals; and the $350/500$ six-model oracle union. The stratified rescue-gated and pair-balanced variants retain positive prefix credit on their respective $10$ and $9$ post-warmup $(0,1)$ groups. Recomputing the executed sequence coefficients gives mean signed token masses of $+15.598$ and $-55.647$, consistent with the revised explanation. The earlier evaluation records remain intact after decoding the new compression format.

The update also addresses the previous review's aggregation ambiguity: it distinguishes the hybrid MDL totals of $496/950$ under voting over all nonempty answers and $497/950$ under completion-restricted voting. This makes the earlier one-item discrepancy understandable.

Several qualifications should accompany the extension:

- **Interpretation and uncertainty.** Arm 15's $+1.20$ percentage-point MATH-500 difference from Arm 12 remains unresolved under seed-and-question resampling. Removing $(1,0)$ training pairs occurs by construction, while continuation weighting and hinge strength also change. It should not be described as demonstrated prevention of harmful learning. Likewise, the inference gain is conditional on the explored checkpoint and reused panels; its nominal interval does not account for adaptive method selection. Extend the chronology disclosure to Arms 14–19 before extending the “untouched confirmatory” designation to their external-benchmark results. The six-model union is oracle coverage, not selectable accuracy.

- **Fork and tie definitions.** Of the $452$ added FG3/FG4 slots, only $143+98=241$ have margin at most $0.5$; $211$ use above-threshold fallback positions. State this alongside the new result. Of the $137$ cases called “resolved,” $15$ still have tied maximal answer frequencies and require the specified tie-breaker. Thus $137/226$ measures acquisition of repeated answers, not unique-plurality resolution. The online generator also has a minimum-margin fallback and early-EOS handling absent from its displayed first-threshold formula. Describe those cases explicitly; retrospective minimum-margin selection does not inherit the earlier stopped-tree marginal guarantee.

- **Verification scope.** The coefficient reconstruction checks the sequence-loss weights and signed token mass, not the hinge contribution, actual gradients, optimizer trajectory, or model generation. Some new atlas claims are checked only against stored summaries. Add executable reconstruction of the new voting intervals and subgroup claims where underlying records permit it. My checks reproduce retained-record arithmetic and inspect implementation; they do not reproduce training or inference.

- **Small reporting corrections.** The Arm 14 discussion assigns $A_{\mathrm{pref}}=-1.0604$ too broadly: among its $13$ activated all-four-root-passing groups, $11$ have seven rewarded leaves and two have six, which yield a different coefficient. The Act18 configuration records `steps=18` and omits the stopping-mode flag needed to explain its $53/57/60$ visited questions; reconcile it with the reproduction command. Finally, summarize the new runs' retained timing/token costs and the extra inference work: active-update counts and branch counts alone do not establish comparable compute.

These clarifications do not overturn the supported descriptive findings or the accepted contribution.
