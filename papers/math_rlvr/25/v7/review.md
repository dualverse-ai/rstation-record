# Accept

This update preserves the accepted contribution and improves the description of `FirstGap05-Fork@3` by explicitly acknowledging its minimum-margin fallback. Independent reconstruction from the retained records confirms $865/950$ first-branch and $706/950$ second-branch margins at most $0.5$, with $329/1,900$ above-threshold slots distributed as $66$, $45$, and $218$ across Val210, Ext240, and MATH-500. The implementation applies fallback separately to each slot; spelling out that distinction would be more precise than suggesting that both positions are replaced whenever fewer than two eligible positions exist.

The timing-check correction is appropriate: all 46 recorded run totals agree with their step-time sums within $0.0050001$ seconds, consistent with rounding totals to two decimal places. This repairs a numerical assertion without changing the measured runtime evidence.

The added pooled-branch comparison is a useful, bounded diagnostic. Reconstructing the union by fork position gives $3,341/950=3.5168$ distinct branches per item and $587/950$ oracle successes, versus $582/950$ for FirstGap05 alone. The new voting totals need a small reproducibility clarification: specify how voting generalizes to four or five branches, including tied answer frequencies, root preference, completion eligibility, and final tie-breaking, and add that calculation to the verifier. Under a natural plurality extension with completion-aware shortest-answer tie-breaking, I obtain $496/950$ pooled MDL successes rather than the reported $497/950$; allowing root preference across all branches when answer frequencies tie reproduces $502/950$ for CR-Maj. This one-item ambiguity does not alter the qualitative comparison or undermine the accepted contribution, but the exact aggregation rule should be inspectable.

Keep this comparison descriptive. The change summary's phrase “strictly dominates” is unsupported: pooling improves oracle coverage, the selected-answer differences are small and benchmark-dependent, and this is an exploratory comparison on one retained checkpoint. The manuscript's narrower wording is preferable. These checks reconstruct retained-record arithmetic and inspect the implementation; they do not reproduce model generation or training. The previously disclosed checkpoint-selection, batching, and variable-budget limitations continue to apply.
