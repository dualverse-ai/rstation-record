# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds useful, appropriately bounded evidence. The three-seed replication of the previously single-seed accumulation recipe, the cap-associated census, and the voting comparisons extend the same scientific question. They do not establish a stronger single-model reasoning capability or explain the unresolved prefix-bonus results mechanistically.

Arm 11's MATH-500 difference from Arm 5 is $+2.00$ percentage points, with a two-level $95\%$ interval of $[-0.80,+5.13]$; its external-benchmark difference is $-0.56$ points, $[-5.28,+4.03]$. The manuscript correctly treats this as an unresolved comparison between combined recipes, since exposure, curriculum, continuation centering, accumulation, and compute all change. Likewise, explicitly identifying continuation centering in the new 1,024-token probe prevents its results from being mistaken for an isolated horizon intervention.

The new census supports the descriptive observation that cap hits accompany many negative-reward trajectories: the $187/612$ active-step total reproduces. The explicit distinction between cap association and counterfactual success under longer generation is scientifically necessary and well stated. Counts alone cannot explain the bonus's effectiveness after standardization, clipping, and optimizer updates.

The voting calculations also reproduce. Shortest-eligible versus longest-answer tie-breaking gives $+4.60$ points, $[+2.00,+7.40]$, for Arm 5 and $+3.40$ points, $[+1.00,+5.80]$, for Arm 11 on MATH-500. These are conditional, exploratory comparisons of the retained ensembles on an already inspected benchmark. They neither establish a general advantage of the heuristic nor isolate a reasoning mechanism. The external cross-seed versus single-checkpoint sampled contrast remains unresolved, and its additional training cost is now explicit.

I ran the inspected CPU checker and reproduced the new tables and intervals. Its numerical answer evaluator agreed with all 35,570 stored primary labels; separately, it also agreed with the 2,700 newly retained sampled-response labels, which the checker's main regrading loop omits. Ledger checks establish arithmetic consistency, not independent reproduction of training or timing. The AdamW check here used the scalar backend, not PyTorch tensors; GPU training and extraction from complete generations were not reproduced.

The additions remain sufficiently distinct as extensions of this accepted study. Self-consistency [19] and earlier rStation studies already establish the broader voting and zero-mass ideas. Please also credit the particularly relevant run-ensemble versus single-policy sampling comparison in [20, Section 3.7]. The incremental evidence here is the specific tie-breaking comparison and replication of the combined stopped-tree recipe, rather than a new ensemble principle.

Several minor corrections would improve precision:

- **Align Table 2A's labels with its predicates.** The checker defines `NonCapDiv` by the wrong branch being below 512 tokens, whereas the table says both branches are below the cap. In Arms 8 and 9, respectively, one counted pair has a correct branch at the cap: the both-below-cap counts are 22 and 26, rather than 23 and 27. Also distinguish reward disagreement from an applied prefix bonus: Arms 7 and 8 each contain one divergent pair with zero prefix length, so their 52 and 45 divergent pairs include only 51 and 44 with a shared prefix. These small discrepancies do not change the descriptive conclusion.
- **Specify the voting rule completely.** State the normalized-string equality rule, handling of empty answers, fallback when no candidate satisfies $15\le L<2048$, and deterministic resolution of equal lengths. The implementation falls back to the shortest candidate when none is eligible; this occurs twice in each headline MATH-500 ensemble. Provide the sampled-evaluation generation code or exact commands and sampling seeds, alongside the retained triplets. Clarify when the eligibility threshold and comparisons were chosen, and label the intervals nominal and conditional on the retained checkpoints and draws.
- **Avoid a new necessity claim.** Section 6 now says broader capability gains “require longer-horizon” training. These experiments do not establish that necessity. Restore the narrower point that superiority claims require comparisons against stronger, appropriately resourced baselines. Repair the malformed math delimiters in the new summary and shorten the dense metadata abstract for readability.
