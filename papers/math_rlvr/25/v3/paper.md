## 1. Problem Formulation and Summary of Contributions

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) [1, 2, 3] enables a pretrained base language model (`Qwen3-0.6B-Base`, $P = 751,632,384$ untied trainable parameters [4]) to rise from $14.80\%$ ($74/500$) greedy accuracy on `MATH-500` and $12.50\%$ ($30/240$) on an independent external benchmark (`GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40`) to $44.40\%$–$48.80\%$ on `MATH-500` and $57.92\%$–$61.25\%$ on `GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40` after 50 single-question updates on `math_train.json[:50]`. In the standard single-question GRPO loop, the policy samples $G=8$ trajectories $y^{(1)}, \dots, y^{(G)} \sim \tilde{\pi}_\theta(\cdot \mid x)$ per question $x$ (under the 512-token-capped `temp=0.8, top_p=0.9` rollout kernel $\tilde{\pi}_\theta$), computes standardized binary rewards $R_i \in \{0,1\}$, and updates parameters via sequence-summed token log-probabilities:

$$
A_i = \frac{R(y^{(i)}, a^*) - \bar{R}}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}}, \qquad \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^G A_i \sum_{t=1}^{|y^{(i)}|} \log \pi_\theta\left(y^{(i)}_t \;\middle|\; x, y^{(i)}_{<t}\right).
$$

Within this small-budget regime, we study four bounded diagnostic questions: (i) how omitting redundant zero-advantage backward passes on the $52\%$–$66\%$ of questions with homogeneous rewards ($\sigma_R = 0 \implies A_i = 0$) while executing explicit $O(P)$ zero-gradient `AdamW` steps compares against both full-backward training and step skipping (`--skip-zero-var`), with and without a 30-character pre-boxed derivation gate (`text.rfind("\\boxed") >= 30`); (ii) in two-stage ($K=4 \times B=2$) branched rollouts [13, 14, 16], how the distributional properties of early-EOS branch handling distinguish a genuinely $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable stopped tree from outcome-dependent root-restart and midpoint-split samplers; (iii) whether adding a sibling-disagreement prefix bonus ($\beta = 0.25$) yields any detectable benefit over TreeRL's descendant-mean prefix credit ($\beta = 0.0$) under matched branching rules, alongside a descriptive census of 512-token cap-associated active steps and reward-divergent pairs and a matched 3-seed single-factor 1,024-token horizon comparison (`Arm 12` vs. `Arm 5`); and (iv) how three-seed 100-question combined gradient accumulation (`Arm 11`) and cross-seed vs. single-seed test-time voting [19, 20] (`MDL-Maj@3` vs. `MaxLen-Maj@3`) behave across benchmarks when accounting for training and inference costs and conditional uncertainty.

We report a twelve-arm, three-seed (`seeds = 42, 101, 7`) experimental matrix plus six single-seed (`seed=101`) diagnostic ablations across **43 primary training runs (2,352 steps and 9,408 rollout pairs with complete per-step and per-pair token, prefill, padded-prefill, untrimmed batch-max generation, backward, timing, and branch-event ledgers in `training_records.json`, plus 12 matched 50-step external-evaluation retraining runs = 600 steps)** and **119 primary evaluations (38,420 graded item responses) plus 2,700 sampled-$k=3$ responses (41,120 independently regraded item responses total in `per_item_grades.json`)**: **43 `MATH-500` evaluations (21,500 grades)**, **44 `math_val210` evaluations (9,240 grades)**, and **32 evaluations on an untouched 240-item external mathematics benchmark (`GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40`, 7,680 grades)**. Because `MATH-500` was inspected on `seed=101` during initial gate and warmup selection (Section 5.1), we treat `GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40` ($N=240$) as the untouched confirmatory benchmark and `MATH-500` ($N=500$) and `math_val210` ($N=210$) as broader in-domain diagnostic readouts. All aggregate metrics, independent regrading of all 41,120 extracted answers (`PASS 1`), direct finite-policy execution of `generate_bifurcated` and fixed-trajectory AdamW equivalence (`PASS 2`), per-step/per-pair integer token conservation laws (`PASS 3`), and **4,000-resample two-level (seed $\times$ question) and paired-item bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals** are verified in $\sim 2\text{s}$ on CPU by `verify_supplement.py`:

1. **$O(P)$ Zero-Gradient AdamW Stepping vs. Step Skipping and Execution-Recipe Controls (Section 3.2 & Section 5.2):**
   In the supplied baseline (`Arm 1`, `code/run_baseline.py`), homogeneous groups ($\sigma_R = 0$) execute 8 forward-and-backward passes multiplied by $0.0$ ($135.7\text{k}$ output backward tokens, $169.4\text{k}$ prompt+output backward tokens; $815.9\text{ TFLOPs}$ output-only / $1018.6\text{ TFLOPs}$ unpadded full-sequence / $1069.5\text{ TFLOPs}$ padded full-sequence analytical dense-work proxies) before calling `optimizer.step()`. Allocating explicit zero gradients (`param.grad = torch.zeros_like(param)`) and calling `optimizer.step()` costs $O(P)$ elementwise parameter operations and zero transformer backward FLOPs while preserving the 50-step AdamW momentum ($m_t \leftarrow \beta_1 m_{t-1}$), second-moment ($v_t \leftarrow \beta_2 v_{t-1}$), bias-correction, and decoupled weight-decay updates that step skipping (`--skip-zero-var` alone) omits.
   - **Fixed-Trajectory Bitwise Equivalence vs. Descriptive Live Execution-Recipe Comparison (`Arm 2` vs. `Arm 1`):** On any fixed sequence of active and homogeneous steps sharing a common active-step loss function, `param.grad = torch.zeros_like(param); optimizer.step()` produces **bitwise identical parameters $\theta_t$ and AdamW buffers $(m_t, v_t)$ (`max_abs_diff = 0.0`)** to executing $0.0$-weighted backward passes, whereas skipping `optimizer.step()` diverges (`PASS 2`). In our live GPU runs, `Arm 1` (`code/run_baseline.py`) sums token cross-entropies before multiplying by scalar advantage (`-adv[i] * logprob(seq) / 8`), whereas `Arms 2–12` (`code/run_experiment.py`) multiply each token's NLL by advantage before summing (`compute_weighted_logprob`). In `bfloat16`/`float32`, multiplying before summing vs. summing before multiplying introduces a 1-ULP active-step rounding difference (e.g., `grad_norm = 808.9206` vs. `808.9137` on Step 6 of `seed=42`) that diverges subsequent rollout trajectories [4]; accordingly, live `Arm 1` vs. `Arm 2` differences are reported descriptively as comparisons between two execution recipes rather than isolated timing or accuracy tests of homogeneous-step backward elimination alone. Under the identical ungated reward (`--min-pre-boxed-chars 0`), `Arm 2` cuts output backward tokens by **$56.8\%$** ($58.6\text{k}$ vs. $135.7\text{k}$), analytical output-token dense FLOPs by **$43.2\%$** ($463.3$ vs. $815.9\text{ TFLOPs}$), unpadded full-sequence dense FLOPs by **$43.9\%$** ($571.3$ vs. $1018.6\text{ TFLOPs}$), and padded full-sequence dense FLOPs by **$41.3\%$** ($627.4$ vs. $1069.5\text{ TFLOPs}$), with an observed same-host mean wall-clock time of $438.1\text{s}$ vs. $454.1\text{s}$ (`-3.5%`, $\sim 16.0\text{s}$ across the 50-step loop, as 512-token autoregressive decoding accounts for $\sim 88\%$–$90\%$ of step time; $530.0\text{s}$ historical `Arm 1` mean). Across three seeds, `Arm 2` scores $60.83\% \pm 0.42\%$ on `Ext240` (`+1.67 pp` vs. `Arm 1`, two-level $95\%$ CI $[-2.22, +5.69]\text{ pp}$), $44.67\% \pm 0.90\%$ on `MATH-500` (`-1.40 pp`, $[-4.60, +1.80]\text{ pp}$), and $54.44\% \pm 1.92\%$ on `math_val210`.
   - **Gated Zero-Gradient Stepping (`Arm 4`, `ra_flat`) vs. Gated Step Skipping (`Arm 3`, `gate_flat`):** When paired with the 30-character pre-boxed derivation gate, `ra_flat` ($45.53\% \pm 1.03\%$ on `MATH-500`, $59.31\% \pm 1.68\%$ on `Ext240`, $489.0 / 600.1 / 654.8\text{ TFLOPs}$, $433.0\text{s}$) vs. `gate_flat` ($43.07\% \pm 6.54\%$ on `MATH-500`, $58.47\% \pm 4.49\%$ on `Ext240`) yields per-seed differences of `[-1.40, -1.40, +10.20] pp` on `MATH-500` (mean $+2.47\text{ pp}$, two-level $95\%$ CI $[-3.47, +9.60]\text{ pp}$). Relative to `Arm 1`, both `Arm 2` ($34.3 \pm 1.5$) and `Arm 4` ($33.7 \pm 11.0$, **$-3.80\text{ pp}$**, per-seed **`[-2.40, -5.20, -3.80] pp`**, two-level $95\%$ CI **$[-6.60, -1.00]\text{ pp}$**) exhibit fewer 2,048-token test cap hits (`AllCap2048`) than `Baseline Flat GRPO` ($52.7 \pm 5.5$), which we interpret descriptively as an execution-recipe difference under `bfloat16` trajectory sensitivity (`Arm 4` vs. `Arm 2` is $-0.13\text{ pp}$, $[-3.20, +2.80]\text{ pp}$).

2. **Exact Distributional Analysis of Stopped-Tree vs. Outcome-Dependent Branching Samplers (Section 4.2 & Section 4.3):**
   In two-stage ($K=4 \times B=2$) rollouts where Stage 1 targets the first newline boundary $\tau_{\text{causal}} \in [96, 160]$, how early-EOS trajectories ($T_{\text{EOS}} \le \tau_{\text{causal}}$) are handled determines whether the sampler preserves the executed on-policy rollout distribution $\tilde{\pi}_\theta(\cdot \mid x)$ [13, 16]:
   - **True $\mathcal{F}_t$-Stopped-Tree Sampler (`--strict-causal-tau --early-eos-mode terminal_dup`, `Arms 5, 6, 11, 12`):** Stopping at $\tau_k = \min(\tau_{\text{causal}}, T_{\text{EOS}})$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable on the natural token filtration. When $T_{\text{EOS}} \le \tau_{\text{causal}}$, the stopped prefix $u^{(k)} = y_{1:T_{\text{EOS}}}$ is already an absorbing terminal state, so its $B=2$ descendants have degenerate zero-length continuations ($v^{(k,1)} = v^{(k,2)} = \epsilon$). By the chain rule of probability, **every leaf $Y_{k,b}$ has the exact marginal $\Pr(Y_{k,b} = y) = \tilde{\pi}_\theta(y \mid x)$ of the capped sampling policy**.
   - **Outcome-Dependent Root-Restart Sampler (`--strict-causal-tau --early-eos-mode root_resample`, `Arms 7` and `8`):** Retrospectively setting $\tau_k = 0$ after observing EOS in $p^{(k)}_{1:\tau_{\text{causal}}}$ is not $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable because $\{\tau_k = 0\}$ depends on future tokens up to $T_{\text{EOS}}$. Retaining the early-EOS sequence as Branch 1 while drawing Branch 2 freshly from the root biases the sibling distribution: for any policy that emits immediate EOS with probability $p \in (0,1)$ and otherwise traverses the branching boundary, the analytical chain-rule derivation establishes $\Pr(Y_1 = \text{EOS}) = p$ whereas $\Pr(Y_2 = \text{EOS}) = p^2$ (checked at $p=1/2$ by executing `code/run_experiment.py:generate_bifurcated` on a 4-outcome finite policy in `PASS 2`, yielding `(0.50, 0.25)` vs. `(0.50, 0.50)`).
   - **Prefix-Sum Qualification on Shared-Prefix Subsets (Proposition 2):** On any fixed realized tree, $\beta = 0$ descendant-mean credit equals leaf-summed GRPO on every shared prefix (Proposition 1). However, when only a subset $S = \{k : \tau_k > 0\} \subsetneq \{1,\dots,K\}$ of pairs share a non-empty prefix (as in `Root-Restart`), the sum of applied prefix coefficients at $\beta = 0$ is $B\sum_{k \in S}(\hat{V}_k - \bar{R})/(\sigma_R + 10^{-4})$, which need not vanish (e.g., $-1.5/(\sigma_R+10^{-4})$ with one $(0,0)$ shared pair and three $(1,1)$ root-restarted pairs); only the bonus increment $\Delta_\beta = B\beta\sum_{k \in S}(M_k - \hat{V}_k)/(\sigma_R + 10^{-4}) \ge 0$ is nonnegative for every $S$.

3. **Matched Three-Seed Bonus Comparisons ($\beta = 0.25$ vs. $\beta = 0.0$), Descriptive Cap-Associated Census, and Single-Factor 1,024-Token Horizon Comparison (`Arm 12` vs. `Arm 5`, Section 4.4 & Section 5.3):**
   - Across `seeds = 42, 101, 7`, **no accuracy benefit from $\beta = 0.25$ over $\beta = 0.0$ is detected within two-level $95\%$ confidence intervals** under either the $\mathcal{F}_t$-stopped tree (`Arm 6` vs. `Arm 5`: $-0.97\text{ pp}$ on `Ext240`, $[-5.42, +3.47]\text{ pp}$; $+0.73\text{ pp}$ on `MATH-500`, $[-3.00, +4.67]\text{ pp}$) or the root-restart sampler (`Arm 8` vs. `Arm 7`: $+0.69\text{ pp}$ on `Ext240`, $[-3.06, +4.72]\text{ pp}$; $+0.07\text{ pp}$ on `MATH-500`, $[-2.80, +3.00]\text{ pp}$), whereas one-sided prefix clamping (`--clamped-prefix`, Proposition 3) degrades `seed=101` accuracy by **$-14.80\text{ pp}$** ($33.00\%$, $95\%$ CI $[-19.00, -10.40]\text{ pp}$).
   - **Descriptive Census of Cap-Associated Active Steps and Divergent Pairs (`Table 2A` & Section 4.4):** Auditing all 612 active steps across `Arms 1–10` in `training_records.json` shows that **`187 / 612 (30.6%)` of active steps** (`37.5% = 24/64` in `Arm 1`, `29.3% = 17/58` in `Arm 5`, `37.1% = 23/62` in `Arm 6`) have every $R=0$ leaf at the 512-token cap ($L_{k,b} = 512$), and across $200$ tree pairs per 50-step run only $15.0$–$17.3$ pairs/run have $R_{k,1} \ne R_{k,2}$ (`45, 51, 52, 45` total across 3 seeds in `Arms 5–8`, of which `45, 51, 51, 44` have a shared prefix $\tau_k > 0$), with **`48.9\%$–$66.7\%$ (`30/45` in `Arm 5`, `30/51` in `Arm 6`, `32/52` in `Arm 7`, `22/45` in `Arm 8`) having the $R=0$ branch at $L = 512$ (`CapR0Div`)** while $15, 21, 20, 23$ pairs (`15, 21, 20, 22` with both branches below 512 tokens) have $L_{R=0} < 512$. Because $M_k - \hat{V}_k = 0.5$ fires identically on all shared-prefix divergent pairs and observing $R=0, L=512$ does not establish counterfactual success under longer generation, these counts are reported strictly as a descriptive census of limited, cap-associated reward disagreement.
   - **Single-Factor 3-Seed 1,024-Token Horizon Comparison (`Arm 12` vs. `Arm 5`):** Holding `--split-tokens 160` and `zero_cont_token_mass=False` identical to `Arm 5` (`strict_mst_beta0_50`) and changing only `--tokens 512 -> 1024` across `seeds = 42, 101, 7` (`Arm 12`, `cap1024_matched`) cuts training rollout cap hits from $293/1,200$ ($24.4\%$) to $70/1,200$ ($5.8\%$), cuts `CapAssocActive` steps from $17/58$ ($29.3\%$) to $3/54$ ($5.6\%$), and increases non-cap divergent pairs (`Both < Cap`) from $15/45$ ($5.0\text{/run}$) to $45/50$ ($15.0\text{/run}$). Across three seeds, `Arm 12` scores $46.47\% \pm 0.81\%$ on `MATH-500` ($+2.27\text{ pp}$ vs. `Arm 5`, per-seed `[+2.40, +2.80, +1.60] pp`, pooled $95\%$ CI $[+0.13, +4.47]\text{ pp}$, two-level $95\%$ CI $[-0.73, +5.13]\text{ pp}$), $55.71\% \pm 2.52\%$ on `Val210` ($+0.48\text{ pp}$, $[-5.40, +6.03]\text{ pp}$), and $61.11\% \pm 3.37\%$ on `Ext240` ($+0.83\text{ pp}$, $[-4.17, +5.83]\text{ pp}$) at $502.2\text{ OutTFLOPs}$ ($649.8\text{s}$).

## 3. Diagnostic Analysis of Baseline Mechanics

### 3.1 The Bimodal Token-0 Distribution and Early Short-Guess Reinforcement

Evaluating pretrained `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` greedily on `MATH-500` (`base_math500`, $14.80\% = 74/500$) and `GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40` (`base_ext240`, $12.50\% = 30/240$: $13.50\%$ on `GSM8K-200`, $7.50\%$ on `AMC23-40`) reveals a bimodal length distribution (`PASS 1`):
- **Short Mode ($|y| < 30$ tokens):** $426/500$ `MATH-500` responses ($85.2\%$) emit $<30$ tokens (`6–29` tokens, typically immediate `\boxed{...}<|endoftext|>`) at **$11.03\%$ accuracy ($47/426$)**.
- **Multi-Step Mode ($|y| \ge 30$ tokens):** $74/500$ `MATH-500` responses ($14.8\%$) generate $\ge 30$ tokens at **$36.49\%$ accuracy ($27/74$)**. After 50 RLVR updates (`baseline_s42` and `baseline_s101`), $100.0\%$ ($219/219$ and $221/221$) of correct boxed responses contain $\ge 30$ characters before `\boxed{}`.

On Step 2 of `seed=101` (`math_train.json[1]`, answer `98`), all 8 rollouts are 7–8 token responses. When `ablate_skip_zerovar_seed101` rewards the 7-token response `' \boxed{98}<|endoftext|>'` ($R=1$) while skipping subsequent homogeneous steps, the policy reinforces token-0 `\boxed{}` emission (`early_10_mean_tokens = 32.0`, 22 all-zero groups, $78,773$ total tokens; `math_val210` drops to $51.90\%$). Similarly, an exploratory easy-first + length-mean run (`ctrl_clean50_s101`) stayed at $21.9$ mean tokens over the first 10 steps and scored $41.43\%$ ($87/210$) on `math_val210` (reported only as a confounded exploratory observation since it varied both curriculum ordering and length reduction).

### 3.2 Homogeneous-Group Optimizer Stepping in AdamW ($O(P)$ Parameter Work vs. Backward Pass FLOPs) and Active-Step Floating-Point Associativity

In the 50-question baseline runs, $54.0\%$ (`seed=42`: 12 all-0, 15 all-1), $56.0\%$ (`seed=101`: 10 all-0, 18 all-1), and $62.0\%$ (`seed=7`: 13 all-0, 18 all-1) of rollout groups have homogeneous rewards ($\sigma_R = 0 \implies A_i = 0$). Under PyTorch `AdamW` ($\eta = 10^{-5}, \beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999, \epsilon = 10^{-8}, \lambda = 0.01$), a step $t$ with zero gradient $g_t = \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_t = 0$ updates all $P$ parameters:

$$
\theta_t \leftarrow (1 - \eta \lambda)\theta_{t-1} - \eta \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon}, \qquad m_t = \beta_1 m_{t-1}, \quad v_t = \beta_2 v_{t-1}, \quad \hat{m}_t = \frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t}, \quad \hat{v}_t = \frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}.
$$

Skipping `optimizer.step()` on homogeneous groups (`--skip-zero-var` alone, `Arm 3`) eliminates the 8 redundant backward passes but also omits $m_t, v_t$ decay, bias-correction increments, and weight decay on those steps. Setting `param.grad = torch.zeros_like(param)` and calling `optimizer.step()` (`--skip-zero-var --zero-var-momentum-step`, `Arms 2` and `4`) omits the $6 P D_{\text{bwd}}$ transformer backward pass while executing the exact AdamW parameter-state update in **$O(P)$ elementwise memory/arithmetic operations** ($\sim 6$–$8\text{ GFLOPs}$ per step for $P = 751.63\text{M}$, negligible compared to $\sim 16\text{ TFLOPs}$ per 8-trajectory backward pass).

**Distinguishing Fixed-Trajectory Bitwise Equivalence from Active-Step Loss Associativity (`logprob` vs. `compute_weighted_logprob`):**
On any fixed sequence of active and homogeneous steps using a **common** active-step loss function, replacing $0.0$-weighted backward passes on homogeneous steps with `param.grad = torch.zeros_like(param); optimizer.step()` produces **bitwise identical parameters $\theta_t$ and AdamW state buffers $(m_t, v_t)$ (`max_abs_diff = 0.0`)**, whereas skipping `optimizer.step()` diverges (`PASS 2` in `verify_supplement.py`, verified both via the standard-library scalar AdamW recurrence and, when PyTorch is present, via `torch.nn.Module` + `torch.optim.AdamW` tensor checks). When comparing `Arm 2` (`code/run_experiment.py`) against `Arm 1` (`code/run_baseline.py`) in live `bfloat16` training, a second implementation detail enters on *active* steps: `code/run_baseline.py` computes `-adv[i].detach() * logprob(model, seq, len(prompt)) / 8` (summing token cross-entropies first via `F.cross_entropy(..., reduction='sum')` and then multiplying by scalar $A_i$), whereas `compute_weighted_logprob` in `code/run_experiment.py` computes `-(weights * token_nll).sum() / 8` (multiplying each token's negative log-likelihood by $A_i$ before summing). Because floating-point addition is non-associative with scalar multiplication ($A_i \sum_t \ell_t$ vs. $\sum_t (A_i \ell_t)$), active steps can differ by 1 ULP (producing `grad_norm = 808.9206` vs. `808.9137` on Step 6 of `seed=42`), which under `bfloat16` rollout sensitivity [4] alters sampled trajectories from Step 8 onward. We therefore establish fixed-trajectory zero-gradient equivalence via `PASS 2` and treat live `Arm 2` vs. `Arm 1` trajectory, accuracy, and timing differences descriptively as execution-recipe comparisons.

### 3.3 Surface-Token Divergence in Mixed Groups

Across the 96 failed ($R=0$) rollouts in the 23 active groups of `baseline_s42` and the 82 failed rollouts in the 22 active groups of `baseline_s101`, $43.75\%$ ($42/96$) and $50.00\%$ ($41/82$) share $\ge 1$ regex-extracted equation in their first 160 tokens with $R=1$ siblings, while mean exact surface-token longest common prefix (LCP) is $11.9$ and $19.3$ tokens. Regex equation matching is only a surface proxy; the LCP statistic reflects that independent root draws rarely share identical token prefixes beyond the opening sentence, motivating two-stage prefix sharing [13, 14, 16].

## 4. Method, Sampler Distributions, Estimators, and Cap-Associated Census

### 4.1 Pre-Boxed Derivation Gate (`rfind`)

To suppress rewards on bare $<30$-character `\boxed{}` guesses during training while keeping test-time evaluation unmodified (`Arm 3`–`Arm 12`), we define:

$$
R_{\text{train}}(y, a^*) = \mathbb{I}\Big[\text{grade}(\text{extract}(y), a^*) = 1 \;\wedge\; \text{rfind}(y, \texttt{"\textbackslash boxed"}) \ge L_{\text{gate}}\Big], \qquad L_{\text{gate}} = 30.
$$

### 4.2 Two-Stage Branched Rollouts: Exact Distributional Comparison of Three Samplers

After 3 active warmup steps (`--bifurcate-after-active 3`), Stage 1 generates $K=4$ root sequences $p^{(1)}, \dots, p^{(4)}$ up to $T_{\max} = 160$ tokens under the executed sampling kernel $\tilde{\pi}_\theta$ (`temp=0.8, top_p=0.9`, total trajectory cap `max_tokens = 512` in `Arms 5–11` and `1,024` in `Arm 12`; note that because rollout sampling uses temperature and top-$p$ truncation while the policy-gradient loss scores raw model log-probabilities $\log \pi_\theta$, standard flat rollouts already sample from $\tilde{\pi}_\theta$, and the question below is whether branching preserves or distorts the leaf marginals under $\tilde{\pi}_\theta$). Define the causal newline stopping time on the natural token filtration $\{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t \ge 1}$ ($T_{\min} = 96$):

$$
\tau_{\text{causal}}(p^{(k)}) = \min\Big(T_{\max},\; \inf\big\{t \ge T_{\min} : \text{token } p^{(k)}_t \text{ contains } \texttt{'\textbackslash n'}\big\}\Big), \qquad T_{\text{EOS}}(p^{(k)}) = \inf\big\{t \ge 1 : p^{(k)}_t = \text{\texttt{EOS}}\big\}.
$$

We compare three concrete branch-construction rules (verified analytically below, checked at $p = 1/2$ by direct execution of `code/run_experiment.py:generate_bifurcated` on a 4-outcome finite policy in `PASS 2`, and verified across all 2,352 steps by per-pair ledger reconstruction in `PASS 3` of `verify_supplement.py`):

1. **True $\mathcal{F}_t$-Measurable Stopped-Tree Sampler (`--strict-causal-tau --early-eos-mode terminal_dup`, `Arms 5, 6, 11, 12`):**
   - **Stopping Rule:** Each prefix stops at $\tau_k = \min\big(\tau_{\text{causal}}(p^{(k)}),\, T_{\text{EOS}}(p^{(k)})\big)$. Since both $\tau_{\text{causal}}$ and $T_{\text{EOS}}$ are stopping times on $\{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t \ge 1}$, their minimum $\tau_k$ is a strictly $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable stopping time [16].
   - **Continuation Rule:** When $T_{\text{EOS}}(p^{(k)}) > \tau_{\text{causal}}(p^{(k)})$ (`causal_non_eos_pairs`), both branches inherit $u^{(k)} = p^{(k)}_{1:\tau_k}$ (`prefix_len` $= \tau_k \in [96, 160]$) and draw independent continuations $v^{(k,1)}, v^{(k,2)} \sim \tilde{\pi}_\theta(\cdot \mid x, u^{(k)})$ up to $\text{max\_tokens} - \tau_k$ tokens. When $T_{\text{EOS}}(p^{(k)}) \le \tau_{\text{causal}}(p^{(k)})$ (`terminal_dup_pairs`), $u^{(k)} = p^{(k)}_{1:T_{\text{EOS}}}$ is already an absorbing terminal leaf ending in `EOS`; its two descendants have degenerate zero-length continuations ($v^{(k,1)} = v^{(k,2)} = \epsilon$, `prefix_len` $= T_{\text{EOS}}$, `stage2_gen_tokens` $= 0$).
   - **Exact Sampling Marginal Property:** For any complete trajectory $y = (y_1, \dots, y_L)$ under the capped kernel $\tilde{\pi}_\theta$, if $L \le \tau_{\text{causal}}(y)$ then $\Pr(Y_{k,b} = y) = \tilde{\pi}_\theta(y \mid x) \cdot 1 = \tilde{\pi}_\theta(y \mid x)$; if $L > \tau_{\text{causal}}(y)$ then by the chain rule $\Pr(Y_{k,b} = y) = \tilde{\pi}_\theta(y_{1:\tau_k} \mid x)\,\tilde{\pi}_\theta(y_{\tau_k+1:L} \mid x, y_{1:\tau_k}) = \tilde{\pi}_\theta(y \mid x)$ for both $b \in \{1,2\}$. Executing `generate_bifurcated` directly on a 4-outcome finite policy where $\tilde{\pi}_\theta$ emits immediate `EOS` with probability $p = 1/2$ and otherwise traverses $\tau_{\text{causal}}$ (`PASS 2`) confirms **$\Pr(Y_{k,1} = \text{EOS}) = \Pr(Y_{k,2} = \text{EOS}) = 0.50$** with `prefix_lengths = [1, 1, 1, 1, 49, 49, 49, 49]`.
   - **Executed Pair Breakdown (`PASS 3`):** Across `seeds = 42, 101, 7`, `(flat_warmup_pairs, causal_non_eos_pairs, terminal_dup_pairs)` are `(24, 163, 13)`, `(20, 177, 3)`, and `(20, 179, 1)` for `Arm 5` ($\beta=0.0$); `(24, 164, 12)`, `(20, 178, 2)`, and `(20, 177, 3)` for `Arm 6` ($\beta=0.25$); `(48, 351, 1)`, `(32, 366, 2)`, and `(32, 362, 6)` across the $400$ pairs/run of 100-question `Arm 11`; and `(28, 168, 4)`, `(24, 174, 2)`, and `(20, 178, 2)` for 1,024-token `Arm 12`, with zero conservation violations.

2. **Outcome-Dependent Root-Restart Sampler (`--strict-causal-tau --early-eos-mode root_resample`, `Arms 7` and `8`):**
   - **Rule & Non-Causality at $t=0$:** When $T_{\text{EOS}}(p^{(k)}) > \tau_{\text{causal}}(p^{(k)})$ (`causal_non_eos_pairs`), both branches share $u^{(k)} = p^{(k)}_{1:\tau_{\text{causal}}}$. However, when `EOS` is observed at $T_{\text{EOS}}(p^{(k)}) \le \tau_{\text{causal}}(p^{(k)})$ (`tau_zero_pairs`), the procedure retrospectively sets $\tau_k = 0$ (`prefix_len = 0`), keeps $p^{(k)}_{1:T_{\text{EOS}}}$ as Branch 1, and draws Branch 2 freshly from the root prompt $x$. Because the event $\{\tau_k = 0\} = \{T_{\text{EOS}} \le \tau_{\text{causal}}\}$ depends on tokens generated up to $T_{\text{EOS}}$, it is **not measurable at time $0$** ($\{\tau_k = 0\} \notin \mathcal{F}_0$).
   - **Biased Sibling Marginals (Analytical Proof & `PASS 2` Implementation Check):** Analytically, for any policy that emits early `EOS` before $\tau_{\text{causal}}$ with probability $p \in (0,1)$, Branch 1 emits early `EOS` with probability $\Pr(Y_1 = \text{EOS}) = p$, whereas Branch 2 emits early `EOS` only if Stage 1 emits early `EOS` (triggering $\tau_k = 0$, probability $p$) *and* the independent restarted root draw also emits early `EOS` (probability $p$):
     $$
     \Pr(Y_1 = \mathrm{EOS}) = p, \qquad \Pr(Y_2 = \mathrm{EOS}) = p^2 \qquad \big(\text{at } p = \tfrac{1}{2}:\; \Pr(Y_1 = \mathrm{EOS}) = 0.50,\; \Pr(Y_2 = \mathrm{EOS}) = 0.25\big),
     $$
     which is checked on the 4-outcome finite policy ($p=1/2$) in `PASS 2` (`(0.50, 0.25)` with `prefix_lengths = [0, 0, 0, 0, 49, 49, 49, 49]`).
   - **Executed Pair Breakdown (`PASS 3`):** Across `seeds = 42, 101, 7`, `(flat_warmup_pairs, causal_non_eos_pairs, tau_zero_pairs)` are `(24, 160, 16)`, `(20, 177, 3)`, and `(20, 179, 1)` for `Arm 7` ($\beta=0.0$) and `(24, 169, 7)`, `(20, 178, 2)`, and `(20, 178, 2)` for `Arm 8` ($\beta=0.25$), summing to $200/200$ pairs per run.

3. **Outcome-Dependent Midpoint-Fallback Sampler (`Arms 9` and `10`):**
   Without `--strict-causal-tau`, early-EOS sequences of length $\ge 32$ keep the completed trajectory as Branch 1 and split Branch 2 at an interior newline via `find_midpoint_boundary` (`early_eos_gt104_pairs` $= 3, 5, 6$ and `midpoint_fallback_pairs` $= 12, 4, 2$ in `Arm 9`), which depends on terminal length at `EOS`.

### 4.3 Estimator Analysis: Fixed-Tree Summation at $\beta=0$ and Subset Qualification for $\beta > 0$

For any pair $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ with $\tau_k > 0$, let $\hat{V}_k = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B R_{k,b}$ ($B=2$) be the descendant mean (TreeRL [13]), $M_k = \max_{b} R_{k,b}$ (the max-backup value studied in OPTS-TTPO [16]), and $\bar{R} = \frac{1}{KB}\sum_{k,b} R_{k,b}$. For $t \le \tau_k$ and $t > \tau_k$, the engine assigns:

$$
A_{\text{prefix}}(k) = \frac{\tilde{V}_k - \bar{R}}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}} \quad \big(\tilde{V}_k = \hat{V}_k + \beta(M_k - \hat{V}_k) = (1-\beta)\hat{V}_k + \beta M_k\big), \qquad A_{\text{cont}}(k, b) = \frac{(1 - \alpha)(R_{k,b} - \bar{R}) + \alpha (R_{k,b} - \hat{V}_k)}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}} \quad (\alpha = 0.5),
$$

while pairs with $\tau_k = 0$ (`prefix_len = 0`) receive unmodified leaf credit $(R_{k,b} - \bar{R})/(\sigma_R + 10^{-4})$ on all tokens.

1. **Proposition 1 (Fixed-Tree Shared-Prefix Summation Identity at $\beta = 0$):**
   For any pair $k$ with $\tau_k > 0$, because $u^{(k)} = y^{(k,b)}_{1:\tau_k}$ is shared across $b \in \{1, \dots, B\}$, summing the $\beta = 0$ prefix gradient across its $B$ leaves yields:
   $$
   \sum_{b=1}^B A_{\text{prefix}}^{\beta=0}(k) \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(u^{(k)} \mid x) = \frac{B(\hat{V}_k - \bar{R})}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(u^{(k)} \mid x) = \left(\sum_{b=1}^B \frac{R_{k,b} - \bar{R}}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}}\right) \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(u^{(k)} \mid x),
   $$
   matching the summed prefix gradient of leaf-level GRPO on that realized tree.

2. **Proposition 2 (Conditional Expectation and Subset Qualification on Shared-Prefix Pairs $S = \{k : \tau_k > 0\}$):**
   Under i.i.d. Bernoulli($q$) continuations from $u^{(k)}$, $\mathbb{E}[\tilde{V}_k \mid u^{(k)}] = q + \beta q(1-q)$. Across a batch of $K$ pairs where $S = \{k \in \{1,\dots,K\} : \tau_k > 0\}$ is the set of pairs with a non-empty shared prefix, the sum of prefix coefficients actually applied to shared prefixes is:
   $$
   \sum_{k \in S} \sum_{b=1}^B A_{\text{prefix}}(k) = \frac{B \sum_{k \in S} (\hat{V}_k - \bar{R}) + B \beta \sum_{k \in S} (M_k - \hat{V}_k)}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}}.
   $$
   When all $K$ pairs have $\tau_k > 0$ ($S = \{1,\dots,K\}$, as holds on every post-warmup step of `Stopped-Tree` where $\tau_k = \min(\tau_{\text{causal}}, T_{\text{EOS}}) \ge 1$), the first term $B\sum_{k=1}^K (\hat{V}_k - \bar{R}) = 0$ vanishes identically. However, when some pairs have $\tau_k = 0$ ($S \subsetneq \{1,\dots,K\}$, as in `Root-Restart` when early EOS occurs), $B\sum_{k \in S}(\hat{V}_k - \bar{R}) = -B\sum_{k \notin S}(\hat{V}_k - \bar{R})$ need not vanish: for example (`PASS 2`), with $K=4$ pairs where one shared pair $S=\{1\}$ has rewards $(0,0)$ ($\hat{V}_1 = 0$) and three root-restarted pairs $\{2,3,4\}$ have $(1,1)$ ($\bar{R} = 0.75$), the numerator at $\beta=0$ on $S$ is $2(0 - 0.75) = -1.5 \ne 0$. What holds for *every* subset $S \subseteq \{1,\dots,K\}$ is that the **bonus increment** from adding $\beta > 0$ on shared prefixes is nonnegative:
   $$
   \Delta_\beta \sum_{k \in S} \sum_{b=1}^B A_{\text{prefix}}(k) = \frac{B \beta \sum_{k \in S} (M_k - \hat{V}_k)}{\sigma_R + 10^{-4}} \ge 0.
   $$

3. **Proposition 3 (Failure of One-Sided Prefix Clamping When $\bar{R} > 0.5$):**
   In `ablate_selective_s101` (`--clamped-prefix`), on a divergent pair ($\hat{V}_k = 0.5, M_k = 1$), keeping $(1-\bar{R})/(\sigma_R+10^{-4}) > 0$ on the winning branch while clamping the losing branch to $\max(0, (0.625-\bar{R})/(\sigma_R+10^{-4})) \ge 0$ gives a strictly positive net prefix weight for all $\bar{R} < 1$, positively reinforcing below-average prefixes whenever $\bar{R} \in \{0.625, 0.75, 0.875\}$.

### 5.5 Test-Time Voting ($k=3$) with Complete Rule Specification, Uncertainty, and Cost Accounting (`Table 4`)

Building on self-consistency majority voting [19] and rStation comparisons of multi-run ensembles vs. single-policy sampling ([20], Section 3.7; see also [12, 17, 18]), we evaluate $k=3$ test-time aggregation across two post-hoc diagnostic axes (`Table 4` in `verify_supplement.py`): (i) **Cross-Seed Greedy ($T=0, k=3$ across `seeds = 42, 101, 7`)** vs. **Single-Seed Sampled ($T=0.6, \text{top-}p=0.9, k=3$ from a single checkpoint using `evaluate_sampled_k3` with `torch.manual_seed(1000 + seed)`)** on `Arm 11`, and (ii) **Minimum-Description-Length tie-breaking (`MDL-Maj@3`)** vs. **`MaxLen-Maj@3`** and **`Rand-Maj@3`**.

**Complete Specification of the $k=3$ Voting and Tie-Breaking Rules (`_eval_k3_triplets` in `verify_supplement.py`):**
For each test question with $k=3$ candidate responses $(c_i, L_i, \text{ext}_i)_{i \in \{0,1,2\}}$ (`seed=42, 101, 7` in Cross-Seed Greedy; draws $0, 1, 2$ in Single-Seed Sampled):
1. **String Normalization and Empty-Answer Handling:** Each extracted string is normalized via $e_i = \text{normalize\_text}(\text{ext}_i)$. An empty normalized string ($e_i = \texttt{""}$, occurring when a rollout hits the 2,048-token cap without emitting a closed `\boxed{...}`) **never matches another candidate to form a majority**: if $e_0 \ne \texttt{""}$ and $(e_0 = e_1 \lor e_0 = e_2)$, all three rules (`Rand-Maj@3`, `MaxLen-Maj@3`, `MDL-Maj@3`) select candidate $0$; else if $e_1 \ne \texttt{""}$ and $e_1 = e_2$, all three rules select candidate $1$.
2. **Tie-Breaking When No Non-Empty Majority Exists (`1-1-1` Ties):**
   - **`Rand-Maj@3`:** Scores the exact uniform expectation $\frac{1}{3}(c_0 + c_1 + c_2)$.
   - **`MaxLen-Maj@3`:** Selects the longest candidate with a non-empty extracted answer (or longest overall if all $e_i = \texttt{""}$), breaking equal-length ties deterministically by the lowest index $i \in \{0,1,2\}$ via Python's `max(range(3), key=lambda i: (1 if e_i else 0, L_i))`.
   - **`MDL-Maj@3`:** Defines a candidate $i$ as **eligible** if $e_i \ne \texttt{""}$ and $15 \le L_i < 2048$ (a post-hoc eligibility window chosen after inspecting the base model's $<30$-token short-guess mode and 2,048-token cap hits). Among eligible candidates, `MDL-Maj@3` selects the shortest completion $L_i$; **if no candidate is eligible (`fb` count in Table 4), it falls back to the shortest candidate overall $\arg\min_i L_i$**, breaking equal-length ties deterministically in favor of the lowest index $i \in \{0,1,2\}$ via `min(range(3), key=lambda i: (0 if (e_i and 15 <= L_i < 2048) else 1, L_i))`. This fallback occurs `fb = 0` times in all four single-seed sampled runs, `fb = 1` time on `Val210` and `Ext240` (`Arm 11`), `fb = 2` times on `MATH-500` in `Arms 5, 7, 9, 11`, `fb = 4` times in `Arm 12`, and `fb = 8` times in `Arm 1`.

## 6. Limitations and Conclusions

1. **Scope of the Diagnostic Study:** All single-checkpoint 50-question and 100-question arms (`Arms 1–12`) cluster within $58.47\%$–$61.11\%$ on the untouched external `GSM8K-200 + AMC23-40` benchmark and $43.07\%$–$47.00\%$ on `MATH-500`, with seed-aware two-level confidence intervals spanning zero across well-controlled update rules. Broader capability superiority claims would require multi-seed comparisons against stronger, appropriately resourced baselines, such as longer-horizon (e.g., 2,048-token [9, 10]) or larger-exposure [20] RLVR training.
2. **Methodological Conclusions:** Within this `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` regime, (i) replacing redundant zero-advantage backward passes on homogeneous groups with explicit $O(P)$ zero-gradient AdamW updates (`Arms 2` and `4`) is bitwise equivalent to $0.0$-weighted backward passes on any fixed trajectory sharing a common active-step loss (`PASS 2`) and reduces analytical output-token dense FLOPs by $40.1\%$–$43.2\%$ ($3.5\%$ observed same-host wall-clock reduction); (ii) retrospective root resampling at $\tau_k = 0$ after early EOS is not $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable and biases sibling marginals ($\Pr(Y_1=\text{EOS})=p, \Pr(Y_2=\text{EOS})=p^2$), whereas a stopped tree $\tau_k = \min(\tau_{\text{causal}}, T_{\text{EOS}})$ with degenerate terminal continuations preserves exact sampling marginals $\Pr(Y_{k,b}=y)=\tilde{\pi}_\theta(y\mid x)$; (iii) under both samplers (`Arms 5–6` and `Arms 7–8`), the sibling-disagreement prefix bonus $\beta = 0.25$ yields no detected benefit over $\beta = 0.0$ within two-level $95\%$ CIs, while our descriptive census (`Table 2A`) documents that $30.6\%$ ($187/612$) of active steps and $48.9\%$–$66.7\%$ of sibling-divergent pairs at 512 tokens have their $R=0$ rollouts at the cap, and that expanding `--tokens` from $512$ to $1,024$ (`Arm 12` vs. `Arm 5`) reduces cap-associated active steps from $29.3\%$ to $5.6\%$ (`+2.27 pp` on `MATH-500`, two-level $95\%$ CI $[-0.73, +5.13]\text{ pp}$); and (iv) 3-seed 100-question combined gradient accumulation (`Arm 11`) reaches $46.20\% \pm 0.35\%$ single-model `MATH-500` accuracy (two-level $95\%$ CI $[-0.80, +5.13]\text{ pp}$ vs. `Arm 5`), while 3-seed greedy `MDL-Maj@3` tie-breaking improves over `MaxLen-Maj@3` on `MATH-500` in `Arm 5` ($+4.60\text{ pp}$, nominal $95\%$ CI $[+2.00, +7.40]\text{ pp}$) and `Arm 11` ($+3.40\text{ pp}$, $[+1.00, +5.80]\text{ pp}$).
