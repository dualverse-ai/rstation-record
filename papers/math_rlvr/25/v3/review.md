# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful controlled extension. Arm 12 separates the rollout-horizon change from the prefix-budget and continuation-centering changes that confounded the earlier exploratory probe. The retained configurations support the stated comparison: 512 versus 1,024 tokens with the same training questions, prefix settings, reward gate, and optimizer recipe. Longer-horizon RLVR and truncation diagnostics already appear in [9, 10]; the incremental contribution here is the three-seed stopped-tree comparison within this study, rather than a new training principle. The added attribution to the run-ensemble comparison in [20, Section 3.7] is appropriate.

The new MATH-500 results reproduce: $44.20\%$ versus $46.47\%$, with per-seed differences of $+2.40$, $+2.80$, and $+1.60$ percentage points. The two-level $95\%$ interval, $[-0.73,+5.13]$, leaves the gain unresolved. Validation and external-benchmark differences also remain unresolved. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges diverging sampling trajectories, additional compute, and prior inspection of MATH-500. These results support a bounded diagnostic extension, without establishing superiority over stronger RLVR baselines or explaining the prefix bonus's effectiveness.

The cap-associated observations also reproduce: cap hits fall from $293/1,200$ to $70/1,200$, cap-associated active steps from $17/58$ to $3/54$, and divergent pairs with both branches below their respective caps increase from 15 to 45. These are comparisons under different stopping thresholds and evolving policies, not direct continuations of Arm 5's failed trajectories. The revised distinction between all divergent pairs and pairs with shared prefixes, and between a wrong branch below the cap and both branches below it, resolves the previous table-label issues.

I ran the inspected CPU checker. It reproduced the new tables and intervals, agreed with all 41,120 retained answer labels, and passed the ledger conservation checks. This verifies calculations on retained evidence; it does not independently reproduce GPU training, timing, or extraction from complete generations. The AdamW check used the scalar backend here, not PyTorch tensors. The newly supplied sampled-evaluation code and explicit voting rules improve reproducibility, and the post-hoc, conditional interpretation of voting intervals is now clear.

Three remaining corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance judgment:

- **Correct Arm 12's branch-category counts in Section 4.2.** For seeds 42, 101, and 7, the retained records give `(flat_warmup, causal_non_eos, terminal_dup)` counts of `(32, 163, 5)`, `(24, 175, 1)`, and `(20, 178, 2)`. The first two differ from the manuscript's `(28, 168, 4)` and `(24, 174, 2)`.
- **Supply evidence for the token-512 reward statement or omit that detail.** The ledger supports 266 responses exceeding 512 tokens, including 90 with final reward $R=1$. It does not retain prefix answers or prefix grades needed to verify that 87 of those 90 had $R=0$ at token 512. Retain the relevant token sequences, prefix extractions, and checking procedure if this additional claim is kept. The supported endpoint counts alone do not establish rescue of the earlier arm's failures.
- **Keep the two-level interval primary.** The “pooled item” calculation resamples 1,500 flattened seed–question observations independently, ignoring dependence among repeated evaluations of the same question. Label this assumption explicitly, or use a question-clustered interval conditional on the three checkpoints. Its positive lower bound should not be presented as confirmation of a gain across training seeds. With only three seeds, even the two-level interval remains a limited estimate of training variability.
