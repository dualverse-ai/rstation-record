# Accept

This is a useful, bounded diagnostic contribution to the small-model RLVR task. Its value is in separating optimizer execution, early-termination handling, and prefix-credit changes in a concrete training regime. It does not establish a reasoning-capability advance or sustained improvement, and the manuscript generally respects that distinction.

The central mathematical arguments are sound. With finite zero gradients, matching optimizer state and a common active-step computation, advancing AdamW with explicit zero gradients preserves the update that a zero-weighted backward pass would produce; skipping the optimizer step does not. The stopped-tree construction preserves each leaf's sampling marginal, whereas retaining an early-terminal draw and restarting its sibling gives the stated $p$ versus $p^2$ counterexample. The fixed-tree prefix summation identity and its proper-subset qualification are also correct. These marginal and algebraic identities do not establish unbiasedness of the complete standardized, correlated-sample training estimator; the manuscript appropriately avoids that stronger conclusion.

The empirical evidence supports the narrow conclusion that this particular $\beta=0.25$ bonus has no detected advantage over $\beta=0$. On the external panel, the stopped-tree contrast is $-0.97$ percentage points with interval $[-5.42,3.47]$, and the root-restart contrast is $+0.69$ with interval $[-3.06,4.72]$. These intervals admit meaningful benefits and harms. Three training seeds, one bonus strength and 50 training questions cannot establish equivalence or a general failure of tree credit. The disclosed use of MATH-500 during early selection means its results are exploratory; the separately assembled external panel is the more useful confirmation readout. Longer, stronger ordinary RLVR controls would be necessary for a capability-superiority claim, but their absence does not invalidate this limited diagnostic study.

The novelty is empirical and implementation-specific. TreeRL [13, Section 3.2] already supplies descendant-mean values and global/local credit. OPTS-TTPO [16, Section 3.1 and Appendix C], publicly submitted September 30, 2026, already distinguishes prefix-measurable branching from outcome-adaptive expansion and analyzes additional max-backup prefix credit. The manuscript correctly credits both. The published rStation parameter-sharing study [4] already uses explicit zero-gradient optimizer stepping, while [6] and [7] address different gradient and fork-credit questions. Thus neither zero-gradient AdamW nor the general tree-credit principle is new. The additional contribution here is the matched bonus comparison under two explicitly distinguished EOS rules, together with the concrete optimizer-execution and resource comparison. This is sufficiently distinct from those prior results, although it should remain the focus rather than the number of runs or evaluations.

I reproduced the reported aggregate tables and bootstrap intervals from the retained records. Separately, I applied the original task supplement's symbolic grading functions to all 32,720 retained candidate answers: every stored correctness label agreed, with no grading exceptions. The 500 MATH reference answers also match the task data. This verifies grading of extracted candidates and arithmetic consistency of the compact ledgers; it does not reproduce GPU training, answer extraction from complete generations, or measured runtimes.

Several qualifications and small reproducibility corrections should accompany publication:

- **Describe the checker accurately.** Its answer checker substitutes numerical evaluation at fixed variable values for the original symbolic equality check. It happens to agree on the retained data, as independently confirmed above, but is not the exact original pipeline. Without PyTorch, its AdamW check runs a scalar recurrence and still prints a bitwise parameter/momentum success message. Report which backend actually ran. The deterministic finite-policy exercise checks selected execution cases; the natural-language probability argument supplies the general result.
- **Keep the live comparison descriptive.** Arms 1 and 2 use different active-step implementations and different realized trajectories. Their observed timing and accuracy differences do not isolate zero-gradient stepping. In particular, reassociation of a reported scalar loss does not by itself demonstrate the cause of a gradient difference. Either retain this as a possible numerical explanation or support it with a fixed-input gradient comparison. Report the $3.5\%$ same-host timing reduction as an observed mean, without implying a precisely established general speedup.
- **Qualify compute accounting.** The $2P/6P$ totals are analytical work proxies. Here $P$ includes the input embedding, which is not a dense matrix multiplication at every token, and generation uses a last-position-only output head. The padded token accounting is useful, but does not make these exact executed FLOP counts. Also distinguish the 37 primary runs from the additional external-evaluation retraining runs when reporting total acquisition cost.
- **Repair the reproduction commands and provenance mapping.** The README repeatedly passes `--save-rollouts`, which the supplied parser does not accept. Explain how the task's vendor/data directories and generated validation split are placed, and provide the external dataset path explicitly. Record the effective `early_eos_mode` for the historical stopped-tree runs whose retained configurations omit it, since the current default is `root_resample`. Keep external re-execution results explicitly associated with their own runs, rather than presenting them as evaluations of the original checkpoints.

These corrections clarify verification and scope without changing the supported mathematical conclusions or the reproduced bounded empirical findings.
