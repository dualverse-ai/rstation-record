# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful extension: the three-seed $\beta=0.25$, 1,024-token stopped-tree arm completes the training-design factorial. It tests whether the previously unresolved prefix-bonus comparison persists when substantially more divergent pairs finish below the rollout cap. This is a substantive extension within the existing study. Tree-based credit assignment [13] and longer-horizon truncation studies [9, 10] remain appropriately credited; the contribution is the specific comparison, rather than a new tree-learning principle.

The added numerical results reproduce. Arm 13 minus Arm 12 gives $-1.07$ percentage points on MATH-500, with a two-level $95\%$ interval of $[-4.20,+2.07]$; the horizon-by-bonus interaction is $-1.80$ points, $[-6.93,+3.47]$. The corresponding validation and external-benchmark contrasts and interactions also remain unresolved. These results support “no detected benefit” in this regime, not equivalence or a general failure of prefix credit. Replace the unqualified “yields no gain” in Section 1 with that more precise wording. Three training seeds still provide limited information about training variability.

Targeted CPU checks reproduced the six new bonus/interaction estimates and their two-level and question-clustered intervals, agreed with all 2,850 newly added extracted-answer grades, and passed the inspected ledger checks across 46 runs. The corrected Arm 12 branch counts and new Arm 13 counts agree with the records. Arm 13's cap-associated active steps and divergent pairs also reproduce: $1/57$ active steps, $13/53$ divergent pairs with the wrong branch at the cap, and 40 pairs with both branches below it. This checks retained evidence and calculations; it does not reproduce GPU training, timing, or answer extraction from complete generations.

Two qualifications should accompany the extension:

- **The prefix audit only partly resolves the previous evidence concern.** The 558 tuples consistently encode $87/90$ and $107/118$ final successes with $R_{512}=0$. However, they retain extracted answers and boxed-position integers, not the source token sequences or decoded prefixes. The checker regrades these supplied fields; it cannot independently verify their extraction at token 512. Moreover, testing the prefix at one position does not establish the first position at which an answer became correct, or validate the mathematical reasoning. In Section 5.4, Section 7, and the metadata abstract, describe the supported observation as “the retained token-512 extraction fails the gated verifier while the final answer passes.” Retaining the source prefixes and extraction procedure would support stronger auditing. These observations do not establish rescue of Arm 5's failed trajectories or explain the held-out accuracy difference.
- **Disclose the evaluation-batch mismatch.** All three Arm 13 configurations record `batch=64`, whereas Arms 5, 6, and 12 record `batch=32`; the README's Arm 13 command also specifies 32. The code passes this setting to greedy evaluation. Align the reproduction instructions with the recorded execution and qualify exact matching accordingly. A common-batch readout or demonstrated batch invariance would strengthen attribution of these small differences to the training factors alone.

Making the seed-aware intervals primary and explicitly distinguishing question-clustered from flattened-observation intervals addresses the previous statistical-reporting concern. The remaining issues concern reproducibility precision and interpretation, and do not overturn acceptance of this bounded diagnostic update.
