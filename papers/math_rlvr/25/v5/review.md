# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution, addresses the prior review’s main qualifications, and adds a useful conditional study of cross-seed prefix transfer and native token alternatives. The distinction between donor-prefix splicing and sequential donor-token overrides is now explicit. The comparison with length-matched identity controls adds evidence beyond teacher-forced agreement alone. Related work on donor overrides [5], near-tie branching [7], and high-entropy tokens [21] is appropriately credited; the contribution is this specific diagnostic comparison, rather than a new forking-token principle.

Targeted CPU checks agreed with all 4,222 newly supplied extracted-answer grades and reproduced Tables 5A and 5B, including their reported paired intervals. The records reconstruct $15,141/15,636$ teacher-forced agreements and $422/495$ top-2 containment at mismatches. Among the 86 prompt-only failures, the two native branches recover 42 items versus 11 for the identity-control union. These are informative conditional observations. The unconditional voting contrasts remain unresolved: $+2.86$, $+1.25$, and $-0.80$ percentage points on Val210, Ext240, and MATH-500, respectively, with every reported interval spanning zero. Oracle coverage is not an implementable answer-selection gain, and neither result establishes improved native reasoning through training.

The earlier qualifications are handled constructively. The token-512 claim now concerns retained verifier outputs rather than the first moment of mathematical correctness. Extraction checks pass for the 35 retained full boxed prefixes; the other 523 records permit only tail/index consistency checks. The evaluation-batch mismatch is disclosed, and the 950-item audit reproduces 45 grade changes with a net difference of $-3/950$. As the manuscript acknowledges, that one-checkpoint audit does not remove the mismatch in the factorial comparison.

The following qualifications should accompany the new section:

- **Describe the implemented continuation budget precisely.** In `run_batched_prefixes`, each batch receives `2048 - max_pref` continuation steps. A row with prefix length $K_i$ therefore has an effective total limit of $2048-\max_j K_j+K_i$, which can be below 2,048. For example, the MATH-500 discordant-item Rank1 record for item 389 has $K_i=23$ and length 2,007, exactly that batch’s effective limit. Consequently, the voting rule’s test $L<2048$ does not certify completion for these new branches. Describe Table 5B as the implemented variable-budget procedure, and avoid calling all eligible responses completed or non-truncated. Retain EOS/budget-exhaustion flags for a completion-aware analysis. This qualification does not undo the Table 5A identity comparison, whose paired conditions share prefix lengths and budgets, but it limits interpretation of the unconditional voting result.
- **Specify the actual fork-selection rule.** The code searches zero-based positions $4\le t<64$ and selects the second position subject to separation of at least two tokens from the first. It is not necessarily the unrestricted second-smallest margin. Identity controls restore the recorded native token, whereas alternatives insert the unpadded teacher-forced top-2 token. Given the documented numerical sensitivity, record the original token too before describing every intervention as a verified top-1-to-top-2 flip. Also disclose that the donor conditions reuse an earlier cut when fewer than the requested number of mismatches exist.
- **Keep verification and inference limits explicit.** The CPU checks reconstruct statistics and regrade supplied candidate strings; they do not reproduce model logits, generation, or extraction from complete new responses. The rescue percentages and any item-bootstrap uncertainty are conditional on this outcome-selected checkpoint pair and item set. Full inference-cost accounting, including root acquisition and margin-scoring passes, would be needed for an efficiency claim.

These qualifications narrow implementation and interpretation without overturning the supported conditional comparison or introducing a stronger capability claim.
