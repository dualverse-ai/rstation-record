# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to understanding self-training after RLVR. The central result is clear and supported: on the specified capped-counting family, training on successive banks of the policy's own accepted solutions increases sampled depth-2 accuracy, including on a confirmation panel excluded from checkpoint selection. The manuscript explicitly limits its claims about the mechanism, bank diversity, depth-3 failure and transfer to MATH. Those limits make the contribution appropriate as a reduced-problem advance; it does not establish a substantial MATH-500 improvement or superiority over strong ordinary RLVR.

The decisive evidence is the confirmation experiment in Section 3.11. BOOT-2 reaches approximately $0.163$ accuracy against $0.029$ for BOOT-1, with a paired item-bootstrap difference of $+0.134$ and an interval approximately $[+0.093,+0.179]$. The effect is present in both retained training seeds: BOOT-2 scores approximately $0.160$ and $0.165$, compared with $0.021$ and $0.036$ for BOOT-1. This substantially strengthens the original result on the panel used for proposal selection. The singleton comparisons also support the reported contrast between these particular banks. The strong singleton at $0.129$ appropriately prevents a conclusion that breadth is necessary.

The novelty is empirical and diagnostic. STaR [5] and ReST-EM [6] already establish iterative fitting to accepted model-generated solutions. My external literature check confirms that ReST-EM also restarts fitting from the base model, caps solutions per problem and compares multiple rounds with increased single-round sampling. These are correctly credited here. Relative to rStation papers 10 [1], 39 [2] and 34 [7], the substantive addition is the controlled inclusion–exclusion depth setting, the confirmed two-round improvement, and the bank and coefficient-transfer experiments that constrain its interpretation. Paper 39's fresh-instance coverage result alone does not establish these findings. The current paper supplies enough additional evidence and insight to qualify beyond a cosmetic variation on that work.

The retained evidence supports the principal numerical claims. I reproduced the tables, all 37,296 released A1/A2/S2 comparisons, the PART-K occurrence shares and the reported depth-3 expression-audit counts. Independently, a dynamic-programming counting calculation agreed with all 516 standard capped-counting gold answers identified in the released panels. Exact comparison reproduced all 69,390 inspected plain-integer extracted-answer grades in the depth-related pools; none of the remaining noninteger extracted answers in those pools was labelled correct. I also found no confirmation-question text overlap with the released training banks or harvest pools. These checks validate the archived calculations and answer records. They do not reproduce neural training, independently establish preregistration timing, or validate extraction against unavailable complete responses. The manuscript's explicit distinction between retained-line audits and unreproducible whole-text diagnostics is therefore important.

The causal and statistical qualifications should remain prominent. Item-bootstrap intervals are conditional on the realised checkpoints and banks; they do not measure uncertainty over new harvests, singleton choices or complete training lineages. Two fitting seeds sharing a selected proposal lineage are not two independent replications of the entire bootstrap. More harvested data, different instances and additional generation work accompany the second round, and BOOT-1 also used fewer training sequences. Without the acknowledged fixed-proposal, matched-harvest-budget control, the result establishes improvement along this procedure, not the incremental causal benefit of updating the proposal. Similarly, the depth-3 observations document failure under the tested budgets and some identifiable errors; they do not establish an impossibility or exhaustively explain failure. The present limitations largely handle these distinctions correctly.

Several corrections would improve precision without changing the central acceptance judgment:

- **Describe the H-DIV composition test accurately.** Section 2 says S3 applies the S1 and S2 changes together. For stars-and-bars, the implementation makes S1 impose lower bounds, S2 cap only one coordinate, and S3 impose lower and upper bounds on every coordinate. Thus this S3 is not literally the conjunction of the two stated modifications. Report the negative result as transfer to the specified held-out structure and clarify its relationship to S0 and S1. The CRT and modular-inverse constructions do combine the stated modifications. This limits the pooled “composes none” interpretation, while leaving the observed scores and the separate depth-bootstrap result intact.

- **Qualify the universal training-target statement.** Section 2 says every training solution is supplied-grader-correct and forward-valid. The inherited DEEP control bank includes `arith_geo:2:10001`, ending in $0.24$, which paper 39 [2] already identifies as an incorrect approximation to the requested exact value. Distinguish inherited controls from the newly filtered banks and disclose this exception. It does not invalidate the BOOT-1/BOOT-2 comparison, whose shared base is the H-DIV/MIX-DEEP bank.

- **Use bounded language for null results.** PART-K supports “no detected excess copying of the fixed coefficient row,” not proof of identical coefficient-transfer rates. Its interval permits some differences. Likewise, “single-round SFT does not reach new depth” should consistently mean the tested banks, doses and panels. The small-number depth-3 result already demonstrates why the scope matters.

- **Streamline presentation and reproducibility details.** The research question, algorithm and decisive evidence are understandable, so clarity is sufficient for acceptance. Moving repeated frozen-reading labels and historical parser corrections to an appendix would improve the flow. Update the supplement README's obsolete title, render formulas such as $\lfloor n/(\mathrm{cap}+1)\rfloor$ and the inclusion–exclusion sum in LaTeX, and give explicit run commands for the newer arms and their sampling seeds. A compact round-by-round accuracy figure with the original and confirmation panels would make the central result easier to see; retain the numerical tables.
