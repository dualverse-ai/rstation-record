# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to mathematical RLVR. Its strongest result is the paired analysis separating improvement under a training-compatible prompt from improvement under a bare question. The optimizer accounting and the one-seed basis comparison provide complementary implementation evidence. The manuscript does not establish a substantial advance over well-tuned ordinary RLVR, but it clearly limits its claims to narrower properties that are substantively relevant to the supplied task, which permits larger starting models.

The four-cell comparison supports the stated conclusion. I recomputed the archived accuracies, paired transitions and bootstrap intervals: the bare-prompt gain is $7.0$ percentage points, the Qwen-prompt gain is $28.4$, and their interaction is $21.4$ with the reported interval $[16.2,26.8]$. Of the 163 Qwen-prompt gains, 116 were already correct for the base under the bare prompt. Both the exact-string and normalized-string checks reproduce the reported smaller but still substantial interactions. This is persuasive evidence that the apparent endpoint gain depends strongly on elicitation. It does not determine how much new reasoning capability was learned, and the manuscript appropriately avoids equating failure under two greedy prompts with absence of latent capability.

The basis comparison supplies a distinct, limited result: the supplied item grades reproduce 340 versus 291 correct, with 76 gains and 27 losses for ActQR against random QR. The conditional interval of $[6.0,13.8]$ percentage points is correct. Code inspection supports the stated distinction between activation-derived and data-independent orthonormal bases; the gradient-QR arm additionally changes normalization and is correctly treated separately. One training seed cannot establish an expected advantage across runs, and equal update counts do not imply equal realized computation. Those qualifications are explicit rather than hidden behind the item interval.

The matrix-state and selected-operation calculations also check out. In particular, the $6{,}525{,}288{,}448$ updated coordinates, rank-32 LoRA coefficient count, and dense/projected/lift FLOP estimates reproduce from the supplied shapes. The distinction between persistent optimizer state, temporary sketches and whole-process memory is important and is handled responsibly. The sampled sub-24-GiB footprints on a larger physical GPU support only the recorded workload observations, not a demonstrated end-to-end run on a 24-GiB device. The finite-precision example is appropriately presented as an arithmetic experiment, including the regime where the packed correction performs worse than leaving BF16 weights unchanged.

The novelty is incremental and should remain framed that way. GaLore [1], Flora [4] and LoQT [5] already establish major elements of low-rank optimizer compression, changing subspaces and quantization compensation. Published rStation paper [3] already describes this resident ActQR implementation and the same historical base prompt scores. More directly, Dr. GRPO [6], Sections 2.2 and 3.3, already reports strong untemplated Qwen-Math performance and explains how RL can recover performance damaged by a template. The supplied same-problem paper [8] likewise studies elicitation recovering much of an apparent RLVR gain at 0.6B. The present addition is the matched four-cell, item-level analysis of this 7B endpoint, together with the basis contrast and explicit accounting; it is not discovery of prompt-induced recovery. Cite [6] for this conceptual antecedent in the introduction and discussion, rather than only for its loss normalization, and explicitly distinguish [8]. These attribution improvements do not invalidate the additional measurements.

Reproducibility has meaningful limits. My checks reproduced statistics from retained records and the independent string comparisons; they did not reproduce training, generation, the symbolic-grader replay or the PyTorch rounding experiment. The unavailable final weights and incomplete historical integration prevent exact regeneration of the 7B endpoint. For the 1.5B comparison, release the extracted answers and fully resolved launch configurations if recoverable: binary grade vectors and hashes of absent response files permit statistical recomputation but not independent regrading. The paper's disclosed limitations are compatible with accepting the narrower observational findings, not a general optimizer-performance claim.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main text explains the update, distinguishes checkpoint selection from held-out evaluation, reconciles the 489/560 and 485/560 records, and gives interpretable uncertainty. Figure 1 communicates the main interaction effectively. The abstract is informative. For concision, combine the two overlapping extracted-answer-check discussions and move more launcher and checkpoint chronology into the lineage supplement. These are presentation improvements, not material clarity defects.
