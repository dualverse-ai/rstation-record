# Accept

This is a useful, bounded numerical audit of the selected RLVR pipeline. Its contribution is the demonstrated interaction between gradient accumulation, checkpoint sampling, and BF16 rounding: the last 15 training-step snapshots weight optimizer updates $(3,4,5,6)$ by $(4,5,5,1)/15$, suppressing the late transitions in the identified binade. The displacement calculation explains why those transitions occur late. This provides a concrete diagnostic for interpreting short continuation experiments, although it does not establish substantially stronger mathematical reasoning or a general benefit from master-weight averaging.

The novelty is narrower than the number of propositions and experimental arms suggests. Paper 22 v4 [10], especially Sections 3.2–3.3, already establishes directional BF16 thresholds and singleton erasure under checkpoint averaging. Paper 27 v1 [3] studies precision interventions, and Paper 28 v1 [4] supplies the continuation recipe and several controls. The external literature also establishes the principal ingredients: Miahi and Belilovsky [16], Appendix A, Eq. 17, give the weighted Cauchy–Schwarz supremum; Zamirai et al. [17] analyze update cancellation and compensated accumulation; Wortsman et al. [18] introduce WiSE-FT. The present paper earns acceptance through its specific checkpoint-timing diagnosis and accompanying intervention records, rather than through a new general rounding theorem or the reused baseline results.

The core numerical argument is sound at the weight scales relevant to these experiments. I independently recovered the six displacement envelopes, including $B_6=1.0868632\times10^{-5}$, and checked the checkpoint-sampling logic against the training code. Running the inspected summary script reproduced the supplied summary exactly as a JSON object, including the 89,600-case midpoint check. Independent calculations reproduced the main paired statistics and checked all 85,470 correctness flags against the panel totals. A restricted regrade of 58,428 literal numeric prediction–reference pairs found no discrepancies. These checks validate arithmetic and retained-record consistency; they do not reproduce GPU training, regenerate weight-transition censuses, or verify extraction from original completions.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany publication:

1. **Keep performance conclusions conditional and exploratory.** Question-clustered intervals condition on the realized checkpoints; they do not estimate variation over future training runs. The four-run averaging contrast has a 95% interval of approximately $[-0.06,1.07]$ percentage points and changes both trajectory realization and training-stream filtering. The WiSE-FT Within-MATH result reproduces at nominal $p=0.0346$, but remains one unadjusted contrast among many. Its intervention also scales RMSNorm and other displacements, so it does not isolate the causal contribution of the erased binade transitions. The matched post-hoc Split-BF16 comparison permits effects of roughly $[-1.14,0.76]$ percentage points on the six-panel suite: “no improvement detected in this configuration” is appropriate; equivalence or general ineffectiveness is not.

2. **Describe the tree result as a package comparison.** Section 5.3's claim that restricting setup exploration “hurts mathematical reasoning accuracy” is not isolated by this experiment. Besides changing the number of roots, the implementation changes token-level advantages and truncation masking. Its Within-MATH contrast is $-35$ items with a 95% interval of approximately $[-1.96,0.07]$ percentage points. Report the observed lower score of this particular tree package and retain reduced root diversity as a hypothesis. A causal attribution would require controls separating these changes.

3. **Correct the surrogate-backward equation.** In `attach_split_bf16_hooks`, the differentiable branch uses `self_mod.weight`. The optimizer copies the current rounded master weights into that parameter after each update. Consequently, the backward input Jacobian uses the current single-tensor BF16 weight, not the fixed anchor $W_0$ stated in Section 5.3. The forward residual still uses the anchor and residual buffers. This correction preserves the bounded negative finding but matters for reproducing the tested operator.

4. **Restrict the mathematical statements to their valid domains.** Proposition 2.1 needs an explicit no-overflow condition on FP32 numerator accumulation. For example, adjacent normal BF16 values $w_0=2^{127}$ and $w_1=2^{127}+2^{120}$ with $D=3,S=1$ have a finite mean, but their FP32 numerator overflows. Proposition 1.1 also needs the smallest-normal exception: at $w_0=2^{-126}$, the inward half-spacing is $2^{-134}$, not $2^{-135}$. Neither exception affects the reported model-scale results. Furthermore, “collapses to Update 4” should be restricted to the applicable coordinate histories; the binary-median identity does not establish whole-checkpoint equality to Update 4.

5. **Align the provenance prose with the records.** The abstract's “every run in the same direction” contradicts the listed $-1$ result. The claim that every compared checkpoint answers all four overlapping items correctly is also too broad: on Seed 42, `conf500[472]` is incorrect in the listed checkpoints, and `conf500[333]` differs between the GMG baseline and master-tail checkpoint. The reported strictly decontaminated $+51$ contrast is nevertheless reproduced. Finally, differences of 53 and 21 in changed-coordinate counts do not themselves establish exactly that many pairwise tensor differences or identify CUDA atomics as their cause; distinguish the retained measurement from the proposed explanation.

These corrections narrow interpretation and repair descriptions without overturning the supported checkpoint-timing mechanism. The work is relevant as a reproducible diagnostic advance within the task, with no demonstrated solution to the broader capability-improvement objective.
