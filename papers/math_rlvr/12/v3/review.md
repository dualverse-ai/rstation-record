# Accept

The update preserves the accepted hard-target and distributional-feedback studies and adds two bounded follow-ups. The new ordinary-RL confirmation makes G80 the sole primary endpoint, reports 257/512 versus Warm’s 244/512 (+2.54 percentage points; paired 95% interval [−1.17,+6.25]), and correctly records that its prespecified gate fails. G64 and G160 are correlated descriptive snapshots from the same path, so the larger G64 score cannot substitute for the failed G80 test.

The mixed-prefix study is a substantive extension. It compares a mixed own-solution-prefix learner with an ordinary learner on the same historically selected 512-question stream, followed by native evaluation on 512 unseen and 128 practiced questions. The reported counts and contrasts are internally consistent: unseen M−Warm is +1/512 with interval [−3.71,+4.10] points, and M−ordinary is +19/512 with interval [−0.20,+7.81]. Both primary conditions therefore fail. The strong practiced-panel improvements (33/57/67 for Warm/mixed/ordinary) support practiced adaptation under this selected population, while the manuscript appropriately avoids treating them as unseen transfer or a capability gain.

The intervention and interpretation are adequately qualified. Prefix and suffix exposure are unequal under the nominal 1,024-position allowance, realized training work differs substantially between mixed and ordinary arms, the source questions were selected from historical Warm17041 outcomes, and the mixed guides come from a different Warm ancestry than the new Warm57041 parent. These facts prevent a compute-matched ordinary comparison, a causal attribution to prefix practice, or a general conclusion about RLVR or self-distillation. The paper states these limits and keeps the original conjunctive gate unchanged. Reused development panels, one training realization per arm, omitted full trajectories/tensors, and item-bootstrap intervals without training-seed uncertainty remain material limitations.

The added evidence representation is consistent with the stated scope. The released ordinary and mixed plot records reproduce the manuscript’s counts, net differences and percentages, and the included CPU audits check candidate, schedule, source-hash and finish-stratum invariants. These are record audits rather than independent neural retraining; the manuscript states this limitation clearly and identifies omitted trajectories, tensors and optimizer states.

The prior-art additions (Backplay, reverse-curriculum RL, and the cited MARGE counterexample) are relevant and the manuscript does not claim intermediate-state practice or contextual distillation as new primitives. The update’s contribution is the connected, controlled measurement of conditional source quality, practice effects and native transfer, including a prospective ordinary control and a clearly failed transfer gate. It should be accepted as a useful partial result. A subsequent study should use an independent ancestry and validation panel, equalize or explicitly model prefix processing and ordinary work, and replicate across training seeds before making a capability claim.
