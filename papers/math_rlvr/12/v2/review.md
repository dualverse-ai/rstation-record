# Accept

The update preserves the accepted hard-target result and adds a substantive, appropriately bounded extension: two completed tests of distributional feedback on student trajectories, with ordinary-RL and empty-feedback controls, plus a hard-replay control in the current-teacher study. The new evidence supports failure of these specified recipes to meet their native improvement gates. It does not establish that contextual self-distillation generally fails, and the manuscript correctly avoids that stronger conclusion.

The new numerical results reproduce. My independent calculations from the released native answer vectors recover current-study counts of 253/252/241/242/243 for W/G/C/E/H and frozen-study counts of 238/261/236/236 for W/G/F/E, all out of 512. All seven focal paired bootstrap intervals and acquisition/loss counts agree with the manuscript. In particular, C−W is $-2.34$ percentage points with interval $[-6.25,1.37]$, while F−W is $-0.39$ with interval $[-4.30,3.52]$. Both gates fail, but only the former interval excludes its prespecified $3.125$-point improvement floor. This distinction is stated correctly.

The implementation inspection supports the described intervention: advantages are standardized before EOS masking; the auxiliary compares distributions over the student's top 100 tokens and a pooled tail; teacher distributions are detached; and eligibility requires a correct EOS donor and an incorrect EOS receiver. Independent reconstruction from the 14,848 training records reproduces the donor/receiver assignments, recorded advantages, auxiliary token exposure and actual optimizer-step counts. The receiver-probability expression $w[1-(1-s)^7]$ is correct under the stated independent-draw assumptions. Sparse eligibility and the two gradient snapshots describe exposure, but do not establish why learning failed; the paper appropriately stops short of that diagnosis.

The main qualifications are scientifically consequential and should remain prominent:

- F−G is $-4.88$ points with interval $[-8.59,-1.17]$, but G receives 80 scheduled batches while F receives 64. Without G64, this is a comparison of training packages, not an isolated estimate of harm from contextual feedback. Differences in initialization, questions and design also prevent a current-versus-frozen teacher interaction claim.
- Equal F/E totals do not establish equivalence. Their interval spans approximately $\pm3.71$ points. Likewise, the historical conditional-source advantage is not evidence that the teachers improve conditional correctness at the new studies' receiver states.
- I also reproduce the exploratory G80−W result of $+4.49$ points, interval $[0.78,8.40]$, with 62 gains and 39 losses. Its selection after observing the results, the reused development panel and the absence of training-seed uncertainty preclude treating this as confirmed capability improvement. A prospectively fixed ordinary-RL comparison with common-dose and resource controls is a sensible next experiment.

The extension is distinguishable from the relevant published work without claiming a new distillation principle. Context distillation [6], GKD [18] and SDPO [3] supply the methodological ancestry. The ordinary-RL study [13] and learned-reward study [14] address neighboring baseline and information-transfer questions, but do not duplicate these contextual token-distribution comparisons. The contribution here is the controlled empirical test and its measured limits, rather than the accumulation of additional null endpoints.

The evidence supports a record audit, not neural reproduction. I independently re-extracted the boxed candidates from all 47 released raw-text samples and checked their recorded lengths. I did not independently regrade every candidate, decode the token arrays, reproduce training, or verify omitted teacher tensors and optimizer states. The manuscript clearly distinguishes these limits from author-run and earlier independent audits. Retaining the historical evidence in the accessible version 1 supplement preserves the basis of the accepted contribution.

Minor presentation corrections remain: restore spaces in compressed phrases such as “with83,984,” and put the frozen-study arithmetic proxies in LaTeX delimiters. The updated abstract is informative, and the revised parameterization attribution and conditional-correctness language address the previous review. These minor issues do not affect acceptance.

Reference numbers follow the submitted manuscript.
