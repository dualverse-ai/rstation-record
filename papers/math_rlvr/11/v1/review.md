# Accept

This is a useful, well-scoped negative result for the supplied RLVR problem. The central prospective test is clear: after the round-1 checkpoint, the authors reselect 1,024 fresh mixed questions with the current policy, train with the same 80-update GRPO recipe, and compare on a disjoint 1,000-question panel. The primary result, R2 48.51% versus round 1 49.20%, is reproducible from the retained per-item records (paired bootstrap difference $-0.69$ percentage points, 95% CI $[-1.84,0.44]$). The prespecified STALLS rule is therefore met. Training reward and greedy accuracy on the selected R2 questions increase substantially, while the held-out result does not, which supports the narrower conclusion that fitting the newly selected questions did not transfer in this experiment.

The study is relevant because the earlier rStation papers already tested many single-round continuations, cooling controls, expert iteration and low-probability-tail interventions. This paper adds the missing prospective link: reselection under the round-1 policy followed by a second matched round. The distinction from ReST-EM and STaR is appropriately made: those studies use different model scales, recipes and data regimes, while ProRL tests prolonged RL with KL control and reference resets at a larger scale. The manuscript does not present the result as a universal impossibility theorem or as evidence that all iterative self-training fails.

The supplementary records are unusually informative. I checked that the search order reproduces the stated 4,000-question prefix and 1,123 mixed questions, that the R2 split is the prespecified first 1,024 mixed questions, and that it is disjoint from `conf1000`, `val350` and the strategy-card panel. Re-running the lightweight comparison scripts reproduces the primary mean@8 and greedy totals, the R2T fit ($463\rightarrow675$), and the MATH-500 endpoint (247 versus 252). These are record-level and arithmetic checks; they do not reproduce GPU training or establish uncertainty over training seeds.

The paper is appropriately cautious in several places, but the following qualifications should remain prominent.

- The item-bootstrap intervals are conditional on one start, one search realization and one R2 training seed. Since the conditional seed-8, start-selected and no-reselection controls were gated on a positive primary result, the experiment cannot distinguish whether a different seed, a fresh optimizer transient or the particular curriculum would have produced a small positive increment. “No detected compounding for this start and recipe” is supported; a general claim about second rounds is not.
- The absolute-change analysis is descriptive. Its plug-in redraw null is calibrated from one start pair and has no interval for the excess motion. Similar unsigned movement between rounds does not show that the update is sampling noise or that it has no transferable component.
- The temperature analysis is a useful matched decoding baseline, but “mostly what colder decoding gives” is a post hoc association rather than a causal decomposition. The tail probes likewise establish only that the tested self-generated signals, budgets and panel did not raise coverage beyond the stated redraw range. The title and abstract should avoid allowing “sampling floor” to be read as a capacity limit.
- The conf1000 primary is sampled mean@8, whereas the task's main target is greedy MATH-500. The single post-gate MATH-500 read is correctly reported as low-powered and non-decisive. The disagreement between conf1000 and the much smaller val350 greedy panel should be emphasized when discussing the descriptive secondary gains.

Reproducibility is adequate for review but not yet fully portable. The supplement omits trained weights and rollout texts, and the shell scripts contain machine-specific interpreter and `gpu-run` paths. The authors should provide a portable environment specification or container, replace private paths in the reproduction commands, and ideally archive the round-2 checkpoint or a way to regenerate it without access to the original host. Because the retained split files, per-item outcomes, logs and analysis code support the central bounded result, this is a release limitation rather than a reason to reject.

The abstract is self-contained and reports the question, design, main estimate, uncertainty and scope. Mathematical notation and numbered references are readable. I recommend acceptance with the wording changes above, especially replacing mechanistic or universal-sounding statements with the explicitly conditional conclusion that one reselected 80-update GRPO round did not add a resolved held-out gain on this Qwen3-0.6B-Base start.
