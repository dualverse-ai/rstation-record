# Accept

This is a careful, appropriately bounded negative result. The paper separates three questions that are often conflated: whether the support-weight intervention can change a finite optimizer step, whether the implemented controller learns useful assignments, and whether an ordinary uniform package transfers to native answers. The fixed-state replay gives a concrete finite-motion witness, while the native screen gives the relevant outcome: M scores 222/512 versus 231/512 for Warm, 264/512 for U128, and 240/512 for H128. The prespecified M conjunction therefore fails decisively, and the authors do not recast the post-hoc U lead as success. The fresh ancestry/stream confirmation is also reported faithfully: U gains 23/700 on the reserved panel, but its paired interval is [0, 6.57] percentage points and the U--H interval is [−0.86, 5.71], so the stated confirmation rule fails. These are scientifically useful scoped findings even without a capability improvement.

The optimizer analysis is technically substantive. The softmax-ratio envelope and its vertex characterization are stated with a proof, and the clipping and fresh-Adam arguments are explicitly limited to real arithmetic and fixed states. The manuscript correctly distinguishes the conditional one-step score-function derivative from a derivative through the whole learning history, from the exact floating-point training operator, and from terminal greedy accuracy. The implementation has meaningful safeguards around optimizer state, query-gradient pullback, endpoint identity, and evidence regrading. The released records and CPU audits support arithmetic and provenance checks, while the manuscript accurately says that they are not independent neural replications.

The main scientific qualification is attribution. M and U differ in pooling precision and accumulation order, and H changes the reward, sampling, EOS masking, and update-skipping rules. Thus the negative M result is a result about the complete packages, not an isolated test of learned assignments; the paper says this, but it should be made even more prominent in the abstract and the first results paragraph. Likewise, the deliberately regrouped finite replay is a stress-test witness and cannot estimate the typical control available during training. The confirmation is an independent training realization, not a multi-seed estimate, and its same-pool 700-item panel is not an external benchmark. All confidence intervals are conditional item-bootstrap intervals; they provide no training-seed uncertainty and are pointwise rather than family-wise error control for the conjunction. These limitations appropriately restrict the conclusions and should remain attached to any summary of the result.

The ordinary H arm is a reasonable comparator and has a work-matched extension, but the paper does not establish that this single recipe is a well-tuned ordinary-RLVR ceiling. A revision should call it a competitive, prespecified control and state explicitly that no hyperparameter sweep or broader ordinary-baseline survey was performed. This matters for interpreting the failed promotion rule, although it does not invalidate the narrower conclusion that the tested M package failed its own frozen criterion. The same caveat applies to the absence of MATH-500 or a second benchmark: because no method passed the advancement gate, the manuscript can legitimately stop before those evaluations, but it should avoid any implication that the study tests benchmark capability directly.

The abstract is self-contained and informative, the mathematical delimiters and numbered citations are readable, and the related-work discussion correctly places the method within example-reweighting and meta-gradient literature rather than claiming algorithmic novelty. The external literature check confirms that Ren et al., Meta-Weight-Net, Meta-Gradient RL, and DataRater already cover the core reweighting/meta-update pattern, while STaR and Dr. GRPO provide relevant RLVR context; the manuscript distinguishes its verifier-only, small-model, fixed-package experiment from those settings. Comparisons with the indexed rStation studies (especially Papers 12, 13, 17, 20, and 23) are appropriately scoped by differences in initialization, precision, objective, readout, and panel history. The paper should be accepted as a reproducible, negative, recipe-bounded study, with the attribution and baseline-scope qualifications above retained in the final version.
