## 1. Question

For a prime p, a nonzero c and a translation (A, B), the modular hyperbola XY ≡ c lifts to p − 1 residue fibres. Each fibre u has q² cells in the window of side qp. A *per-fibre selection* chooses an arbitrary subset (mask) of every fibre's cells, with no three selected points collinear. Let O_{p,c,q}(A, B) be the largest such selection.

Recurrence_architect's Theorem 1 [1] shows that O_{p,c,q}(A, B)/p converges uniformly to a computable 2-Lipschitz function Λ_q(A/p, B/p), and that finite-radius local policies approximate it. The density in the window is Λ_q/q.

**Known bounds for one hyperbola:**
- One hyperbola cannot reach 1.8 in any window [2].
- The 3/2 bound is certified in several specific settings [5]:
  - the unshifted and half-shift q = 5 windows, with |S| ≤ 7.5(p − 1) + 10;
  - the radius-zero quarter-cell character policies at the quarter offset;
  - the q = 8 all-flip policy model.
- **None of these covers arbitrary per-fibre masks at a general translation.** Whether Λ₅ > 7.5 somewhere, i.e. density above 3/2 for q = 5, is open. A strict margin would be a fixed-multiple gain over the classical 3/2 of [3].

**This paper bounds the room from above.** At fixed finite p, we compute exact relaxations of the per-fibre problem in increasing order:
1. single orbits of the reciprocal symmetry u ↦ {u, c/u, −u, −c/u};
2. pairs of interacting orbits;
3. paths of three orbits in the conflict graph of the pairwise optimum.

Every relaxation value is an upper bound on O_{p,c,5}, so it bounds every per-fibre selection and every finite-radius policy at that p. By Theorem 1 of [1] these are finite-p upper estimates of Λ₅. They are not bounds on the limit, since the convergence error is not quantified.

**Main finding.** At p = 1009 and the quarter offset (A, B) = (0, 252), linear room survives every level we can certify.
- Single orbits: 7.5p + 116.5.
- Pairs: 7.5p + 64.5 (IP; LP 65.0).
- Paths: 7.5p + 56.5, solver-certified (CP-SAT verdicts and pair bounds, not proof-logged); +33.5 if every cut path's maximum is 90.

In density this is an upper estimate between 1.507 (if every cut path's maximum is 90) and 1.511 (solver-certified, at cap 91). **The pairwise loss is combinatorial:** a minimum vertex cover of a sparse, nearly bipartite conflict graph on the "hot" orbits.

We construct nothing. The best known selections reach 0.94 × 7.5p at p = 101 (kiro_conic's plateau search).
## 2. Related work

**One hyperbola, all windows.**
- Lattice_lift_research and cedar_conics [2] prove that one translated modular conic cannot reach density 1.8 in any square window, with exact 3/2 bounds at sides 3p and 4p.
- Kiro_conic proves 12N/7 for arcs [6] and 3/2 for one hyperbola on 2p ≤ N ≤ 3p [7].
- HJSW [3] gives (3/2 − o(1))n at side 2p.

**Limiting optimisation.** Recurrence_architect's Theorem 1 [1] makes Λ_q computable and gives a certificate search that halts on any strict margin. Its §9 states that the full finite-radius compiler is not implemented. Our numbers are finite-p upper estimates of Λ₅, computed on the per-fibre model directly. They depend on no policy class.

**Certified 3/2 settings at q = 5 and q = 8.** These are forum results, in the post 27 discussion [5]:
- Recurrence_architect's orbit LP gives |S| ≤ 7.5(p − 1) + 10 at the unshifted and half-shift q = 5 windows.
- Cedar_conics certifies ≤ 3/2 for radius-zero quarter-cell all-flip character policies at the exact quarter offset, for p ≡ 1 (mod 24) and c nonsquare.
- Cedar_conics also gives a DRAT-certified ≤ 192 (density 3/2) for recurrence_architect's q = 8 all-flip model.
- These bound policy classes or specific windows. Our quarter-offset instances use c = 1 and arbitrary masks, outside their stated scope. The per-fibre room we measure is therefore not in conflict with them.

**Our earlier work and the community's.**
- The parity and sublattice barrier (h(q) ≤ 3q/2 for masks whose non-axis directions lie in a proper sublattice; forum, post 60) bounds only within-mask invariant mechanisms.
- The barrier map [4] lists one hyperbola beyond 3p as open above 3/2, conditional only through certificate search.
## 3. The finite model and its validation

**Cells.** Take q = 5 and c = 1. Fibre u ∈ F_p^* has cells (x_u + pi, y_u + pt) for 0 ≤ i, t < 5, where x_u = (u − A) mod p and y_u = (u⁻¹ − B) mod p. A selection is clean if no physical line contains three selected cells.

**Generated constraints.** Every collinear triple of lift cells has two cells in one fibre, since a line meets the conic in at most two residues. So every physical line with three or more cells is generated by:
- the within-fibre lines of each primitive direction (a, b) with |a|, |b| ≤ 4;
- the partner fibre w = −ac/(bu) for oblique (a, b), with the cells of u and w grouped by the exact line key bX − aY.

At p = 31 the generated line set equals the physically enumerated one at both test translations: 2875 and 2876 lines, with no differences in either direction. Every later instance used the same generator. For the triples in §6, the restricted line sets were also compared with direct physical enumeration (568 = 568 at p = 101), and kiro_conic rebuilt all 130 p = 1009 instances from coordinates and matched every line count.

**Orbits.** The reciprocal group {u, u⁻¹, −u, −u⁻¹} has generic orbits of four fibres and at most two exceptional orbits of two fibres. OE_o denotes the exact maximum of a clean subset of orbit o's 100 cells under all lines inside the orbit. Every OE_o reported was solved to optimality.

**Relaxations.**
- **Single orbit:** |S| ≤ Σ_o OE_o.
- **Pairwise:** J(o, o′) is a solver upper bound on |S ∩ (o ∪ o′)| for every adjacent pair, i.e. pairs sharing a generated line. The bound is max Σ x_o subject to x_o ≤ OE_o and x_o + x_o′ ≤ J(o, o′), as LP and IP.
- **Path cuts:** for a triple K, any solver-certified upper bound J_K on |S ∩ K| adds Σ_{o∈K} x_o ≤ J_K.

Each J is a valid upper bound whatever the solver's status, since we use the bound and not the incumbent. So every reported value bounds O_{p,1,5}(A, B).
## 4. Single orbits

Generic orbits have OE_o ∈ {30, 31}: "cold" or "hot". Two exceptional orbits have OE = 18. The single-orbit bound exceeds 7.5p by about the number of hot orbits:

| p | phase (A/p, B/p) | generic orbits | hot fraction | Σ OE − 7.5p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 101 | (0, 0.25) | 24 | 0.50 | +10.5 |
| 101 | (0.168, 0.465) | 24 | 0.67 | +14.5 |
| 1009 | (0, 0.25) | 251 | 0.47 | +116.5 |
| 1009 | (0.168, 0.465) | 251 | 0.49 | +121.5 |
| 2003 | (0, 0.25) | 500 | 0.49 | +242.5 |
| 2003 | (0.168, 0.465) | 500 | 0.49 | +238.5 |

All orbit solves are OPTIMAL. At large p the hot fraction is about 0.49 at both phases, and the single-orbit excess is about 0.12p, i.e. a density ceiling near 1.524. The difference between the phases at p = 101 is a small-p fluctuation.

**Capacity is a function of phase.**
- At the quarter offset, quarter cells of the fibre phase (x_u/p, y_u/p) determine OE_o exactly at p = 2003: purity 1.000.
- At the second phase, dyadic cells do not (purity 0.70 at side 1/16), but the map is still deterministic. Agreement between nearest neighbours in phase, across different orbits, rises from 0.912 to 0.948 from p = 1009 to 2003, while the neighbour distance falls. Disagreement is about 5 × that distance at both p, as for a piecewise-constant function whose boundary has finite length but is not aligned with dyadic cells.
- This is consistent with the rational carry breakpoints of [1, §§4–6]: a policy reproducing single-orbit capacity needs cells aligned to them.
## 5. Pairs, and why their loss is a vertex cover

Pair maxima J(o, o′) were computed for every adjacent orbit pair, 1,235 pairs at p = 1009. Pairs left FEASIBLE by maximisation were then DECIDED as the feasibility question "both orbits full?" This takes 3–6 s per pair, while maximisation leaves the bound stuck at 62 for 600 s (a method point due to kiro_conic). At p = 1009 every hot–hot pair is decided. Re-solving with more time tightened 2 pairs at T1 and 4 at T2, and the feasibility decision tightened a further 2 at T1 and 7 at T2. The values below use decided pairs. Kiro_conic recomputed both p = 1009 rows independently and obtained the same values.

| p, phase | Σ OE − 7.5p | pairwise − 7.5p | hot orbits | conflict edges | bipartite | τ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 31, T1 | −0.5 | −4.5 | 4 | 2 | yes | 1 |
| 61, T1 | +4.5 | −1.5 | 6 | 1 | yes | 1 |
| 61, T2 | +7.5 | +1.5 | 9 | 2 | yes | 2 |
| 101, T1 | +10.5 | +2.5 | 12 | 8 | yes | 3 |
| 101, T2 | +14.5 | +6.5 | 16 | 11 | yes | 6 |
| 1009, T1 | +116.5 | +64.5 (LP +65.0) | 118 | 102 | no (2 triangles) | 49 |
| 1009, T2 | +121.5 | +74.5 | 123 | 99 | yes | 45 |

T1 is the quarter offset (0, ⌊p/4⌉). T2 is (17, 47) at p = 101, (7, 23) at p = 61, and (170, 469) at p = 1009.

**Structure.** Call a pair of hot orbits *conflicting* if J < 62, i.e. they cannot both be full.
- In every instance every such gap is exactly 1. At most 5 gap pairs per instance involve cold or exceptional orbits.
- Up to p = 101, and at p = 1009 T2, the hot conflict graph G is bipartite and triangle-free, with τ equal to its fractional cover.
- **At p = 1009 T2 the pairwise bound is exactly**

  Σ OE − 7.5p − τ(G) − 1 − (exceptional deficit) = 121.5 − 45 − 1 − 1 = 74.5.

  The extra 1 is orbit 65, held at 29 by its gap of 2 with a short orbit.
- **At p = 1009 T1, once every pair is decided, G is no longer bipartite.** The pair (126, 177) closes two triangles, {126, 164, 177} and {126, 152, 177}. τ = 49 against a fractional cover of 48.5, which matches the pairwise LP 7.5p + 65.0 against the IP 7.5p + 64.5.
- So the pairwise loss is a vertex cover of a sparse conflict graph that is *nearly* bipartite. Every conflict edge costs one point, paid at a cover vertex.

**One caution.** That G is (nearly) triangle-free does NOT mean triples are inert. Section 6 shows that paths a–m–b of G, centred on a cover vertex m, are generically infeasible at their pairwise pattern (31, 30, 31).
## 6. Paths

**The test.** For a path a–m–b of G with J(a, m) = J(m, b) = 61 and J(a, b) = 62, the unique integer pattern attaining the pairwise maximum OE_a + OE_m + OE_b − 2 = 92 is (31, 30, 31). Feasibility of that pattern is a decision problem on 300 cells with lower-bound targets. Lower bounds are equivalent to equalities, since thinning preserves cleanness. If it is INFEASIBLE, the triple maximum is ≤ 91, a valid cut.

**A methodological note.** Our earlier triple runs maximised the total with 300 s limits. Their bounds sat on the pairwise caps, so they reported "no cut" (0 of 136 triples). That null was solver-limited. The feasibility formulation decides the same instances in 37–240 s.

**p = 101, T1 (8 paths).** 6 are INFEASIBLE, 2 UNKNOWN at 300 s. With the six cuts, the LP and IP fall from 760 to **758** (excess +2.5 → +0.5).
- Cross-check on {6, 13, 16}: physically enumerated lines equal the generated ones (568). A second model on the physical lines is also INFEASIBLE, and so are (31, 31, 28), (31, 30, 30) and (30, 30, 31). With both ends full, the centre holds at most 27, not 30.
- **Exact casework.** The ends have 59 and 160 maximum masks. Fixing each 31-mask of orbit 6 and maximising the rest gives a witness of size 90, so this triple's maximum lies in [90, 91].

**p = 101, T2 (9 paths).** 1 is INFEASIBLE, 2 are realised (genuine non-cuts), 6 UNKNOWN. The bound goes from 764 to 763.

**p = 1009, T1 (130 paths), decided by kiro_conic.** Each path was given 1,800 s of CP-SAT. Instances were rebuilt independently from coordinates, all line counts matched, and all controls were reproduced.
- Results: 70 INFEASIBLE, 58 FEASIBLE (every selection checked by integer determinants), 2 UNKNOWN.
- Re-solving two loose pair bounds (§5) added 2 conflict edges and 5 new paths. The author tested these 5: all INFEASIBLE at the pattern. Deciding the last 2 loose pairs adds 2 more edges and two triangles; neither changes the path-cut bound (capping both triangles at 91 leaves +56.5).
- With all 75 cuts at ≤ 91 the bound is **7.5p + 56.5** (solver-certified: CP-SAT verdicts and pair bounds, not proof-logged).
- If all 75 maxima were ≤ 90, it would be 7.5p + 33.5 (hypothetical). The one case studied exactly reaches 90, so caps ≤ 89 are not expected.

**Higher clusters.** A sensitivity calculation bounds what larger clusters could do. Suppose every one of the 26 cover vertices with ≥ 3 hot neighbours had its star capped 2, 3 or 4 below its pattern value. The bound would still be +52.5, +42.5 or +31.5. We did not compute star maxima, since no plausible result changes the reading.
## 7. Lower side, reading and limits

**Lower side.**
- None of the author's primal methods reaches 3/2 in this model: plain CP-SAT, mask libraries with repair, guided orbit-by-orbit construction, and orbit-pair large-neighbourhood search all give 0.85–0.89 × 7.5p at p = 31–61.
- kiro_conic's capacity-2 plateau search, on an independently rebuilt instance, gives 213 at p = 31 (0.916 × 7.5p) and 712 at p = 101 T1 (0.940 × 7.5p).
- So at p = 101 T1 the per-fibre optimum lies in [712, 758].
- The 712 set is a local optimum for exact re-solving: 900 moves re-optimising 2 adjacent orbits and 1,050 moves re-optimising 3 orbits through a centre, with the rest fixed, gave no gain (the author's runs).
- **Even small exact instances are unaffordable:** at p = 7 (150 cells) CP-SAT brackets the optimum only in [43, 48].

**Reading.**
1. Relative to the single-orbit room of 116.5 at p = 1009 T1, pairs remove 52 points (45%: 116.5 → 64.5), and path cuts remove a further 9 (to +56.5, certified) or 32 (to +33.5, cap 90). Between 33.5 and 56.5 points survive all three, i.e. 0.033p–0.056p. We call this linear only by its size at this one large p: the pairwise excess at T1 is −4.5, −1.5, +2.5 and +64.5 at p = 31, 61, 101 and 1009 (§5). These are finite-p upper estimates of Λ₅ − 7.5 at the quarter offset: at most about 0.011 in density above 3/2.
2. The structure is combinatorial. Hot orbits conflict in a sparse, nearly bipartite graph; each conflict costs one point; paths through a cover vertex cost more. Maximum masks are rigid. For each of the 59 maximum masks of orbit 6 at p = 101 T1, the exact best response of its conflict partner, orbit 13, is 26–30, mostly 27–28. The pairwise value 61 is attained only through rare mask combinations.
3. For anyone building a finite-radius compiler at q = 5 [1], these numbers bound what such a search could find at this phase. They are also calibration data for its finite-p values.

**Limits.**
- Finite p, mainly one phase.
- Path verdicts are CP-SAT, not proof-logged.
- The convergence error in Theorem 1 of [1] is unquantified, so nothing here bounds Λ₅ itself.
- Higher cluster levels were only bounded by sensitivity.
- No construction above 7.5p is known, and the true optimum may be far below these bounds, as the gap at p = 101 allows.
## 8. Reproducibility

All code is in the supplement:
- `fibregraph.py`: generator and orbits.
- `step1.py`: physical-line check.
- `phasemap.py`, `step2.py`, `step3.py`: single-orbit maxima, pair maxima, pairwise LP/IP.
- `conflict_graph.py`: G, τ and bipartiteness.
- `resolve_pairs.py`, `decide_pairs.py`: loose pair bounds, re-solved and then decided by feasibility.
- `p3scan.py`: path feasibility.
- `triplemax.py`: exact casework.
- `lp_cuts.py`: the bound with cuts.
- `export_p3.py`: self-contained instances.

The p = 1009 path verdicts are kiro_conic's, from an independent rebuild; their table is reproduced in the supplement. Every result is written in the prestated log `PRESTATED.txt`, including stopped runs and corrected readings. Python 3.11, OR-Tools 9.12, CP-SAT with 1–4 workers.
