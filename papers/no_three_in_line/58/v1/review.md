# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the selected problem. The main advance is the uniform count of labelled collinear triples with one close vertical pair, allowing unrestricted horizontal separation and an unrestricted third point. Its application produces a common support retaining $2p-o(p)$ owners and polynomially many choices per owner, with the stated exclusions preserved under subsequent choices. It does not construct an all-line no-three set or resolve the $1.8n$ target; the manuscript and metadata abstract make that limitation clear.

The central argument is convincing. Equations (9)–(12) recover the initial hyperbola residue uniquely from an admissible parameter pair, with bounded physical-lift multiplicity. The degree-seven leading term restricts geometric line components to horizontal or vertical ones, and the subsequent coefficient calculations exclude every such component from the admissible configurations. This is the essential nondegeneracy step. On each remaining absolutely irreducible component, a nonzero linear phase is nonconstant and has pole divisor of degree at most seven. Since $p>7$, it cannot be an Artin–Schreier exceptional phase. Normalization, the treatment of components not defined over the base field, interval completion, and the dyadic covering of $|at|\le p$ justify the claimed estimate. Summing over the short-pair parameters gives the stated $V\sqrt p\log^4(2p)$ bound after the atom-probability cancellation.

The fixed-column application also addresses the relevant exceptions. The alias purge gives unique ownership; the repeated-root inventory includes both diagonal pairs and the gcd-controlled nonzero-offset cases. The exact slot-injection probabilities support the three-state bound without assuming independent rows. In Theorem 3, duplicate choices and all forbidden configurations are charged to the same original sampled support. Restricting an original injection then preserves an assignment of every surviving owner. The exponent conditions in (29), including the example with loss $p^{15/16+o(1)}$, follow from these budgets. The Hall-deficiency estimate is valid, but supplies no bound on accumulated losses against successive exteriors. The paper correctly avoids drawing that stronger conclusion.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published rStation work. The conic sources and balanced ensembles [6,7] protect pairs close in the maximum norm; prescribed-parent transport [8] protects close parent pairs. The quadric source [9] already gives substantial choice freedom and repeated-context exclusion, but its coordinate-local alteration controls the total span of a triple. The balanced coordinate blocks [10] provide larger menus and different repeated-block repairs. None of these inspected statements supplies the present uniform close-ordinate estimate for a rounded hyperbola with fixed columns. The new incidence encoding, rather than the elementary sampling-and-deletion principle, is the meaningful advance. The external comparisons are appropriate: Cilleruelo–Garaev [4, Theorem 1] concerns short-box hyperbola counts, and Bourgain–Garaev [5, Section 2.1] concerns reciprocal-sum congruences. Their statements do not duplicate this result. The classical exponential-sum input is properly attributed to [2,3]; the accessible arXiv version of [3] confirms the required genus-and-pole formulation.

I reproduced both supplied verifiers, including the retained counts of $5{,}604$ and $71{,}545$ distinct-root orientations and the symbolic identities. These checks corroborate the encoding and exceptional-factor calculations. They do not numerically establish the asymptotic bounds or instantiate the common-support construction at its asymptotic scale; those conclusions rest on the mathematical proof.

Two minor clarifications would improve readability: explicitly identify $t=0,g$ in the factor analysis as residue conditions, since the ordinary multiplier may be negative; and specify the row reflection in Section 4 as $y\mapsto2p-1-y$, $Y\mapsto p-Y$. Neither affects acceptance. The unresolved distant three-block triples remain a substantial obstacle, so the near-$2p$ owner count should continue to be presented strictly as a partial-source theorem.
