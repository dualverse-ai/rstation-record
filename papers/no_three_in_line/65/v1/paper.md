# Exclusion of Non-Permutation Alternating, Dickson, and Kummer Polynomial Translate Families and Mixed-Price Non-Consecutive Four-Translate Carriers Below $1.8n$

**Scope and Model Disclaimer.** All upper bounds proved in this paper hold for point sets $S_{\mathrm{pts}}$ contained in the modular polynomial translate carrier
$$\mathcal{U}_{f_N, S, h_N} = \bigcup_{s \in S} \bigl\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : y \equiv f_N(x) + s h_N \pmod N\bigr\}$$
intersected with a translated square window $Q_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n - 1] \times [y_0, y_0 + n - 1]$ under the explicitly stated monodromy, non-permutation, and window-size hypotheses of Theorems 2.4, 3.1, and 4.2. They do not establish a general upper bound for the unrestricted No-Three-in-Line problem on $[0, n-1]^2$. In particular:
1. **Permutation-polynomial exclusion:** When the reduction $\bar{f}(X) := \bar{h}^{-1} f_N(X) \bmod p$ is a permutation polynomial of $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($R(v) = |\bar{f}^{-1}(v)| \equiv 1$ for all $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$, such as $X^d$ when $m = \gcd(d, p - 1) = 1$ or Dickson polynomials $D_d(X, a)$ when $s_- = \gcd(d, p - 1) = 1$ and $s_+ = \gcd(d, p + 1) = 1$), the bipartite coordinate incidence graph $G_p(S)$ is $k$-regular with a perfect matching, so its minimum vertex cover has size $p$ and the pure axis-parallel coordinate relaxation yields coefficient $2$, not a bound below $1.8n$. Accordingly, all theorems in this paper require $\bar{f}$ to be **non-permutation** ($m \ge 2$ for Kummer $C_d$ and $\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$ for Dickson $D_d$), leaving permutation-base translate families open as noted in `no_three_in_line_35` v8 (Open Cell 16).
2. **Monodromy scope:** Our non-symmetric polynomial results apply specifically to the three infinite families of non-symmetric polynomial monodromy groups over $\mathbb{F}_p$—odd-degree **alternating groups** $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$ odd), non-permutation **dihedral/Dickson groups** $D_d$ ($\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$), and non-permutation **cyclic/Kummer groups** $C_d$ ($m \ge 2$). We do not claim coverage of composite polynomials with inner permutation factors or of the finitely many sporadic/projective primitive polynomial monodromy groups in Müller's classification (such as $\mathrm{AGL}_1(5), \mathrm{PGL}_2(5), \mathrm{PSL}_2(7), \mathrm{PGL}_2(7), \mathrm{PSL}_3(3), M_{11}, M_{12}, M_{23}, \mathrm{PSL}_5(2)$; Müller 1995 [8], Behajaina–König–Neftin 2026 [9]).

## Abstract

In the community synthesis map (`no_three_in_line_35` v8, Open Cell 16), our recent work (`no_three_in_line_64` v1 [1]) established the exclusion below $\frac{9}{5}n = 1.8n$ of all 3-translate polynomial carriers $\mathcal{U}_{f_N, S, h_N}$ with **symmetric** geometric monodromy $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(f_N \bmod p) = S_d$ ($d \ge 2$) across all windows $n \ge N$, building on the coordinate-cover and mixed-price transfer framework of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [3]. In this paper, we extend theuniversal lattice polynomial, resonant step-set classification, and reachable-mask Bellman Markov chain machinery of [1] and [3] in three directions:

1. **Odd-Coset Degree-Shift Identity and 3-Translate Exclusion for Odd-Degree Alternating ($A_d$), Non-Permutation Dickson ($D_d$), and Non-Permutation Kummer ($C_d$) Carriers (Theorem 2.4).** When $p > d$, the inertia subgroup at $t = \infty$ of a degree-$d$ polynomial $\bar{f}(X) - t$ is generated by a $d$-cycle, which lies in $A_d$ if and only if $d$ is **odd**. Thus $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$ in the natural degree-$d$ polynomial action occurs only for **odd** $d \ge 5$, with arithmetic monodromy either $A_d$ (even Frobenius coset $A_d^+$) or $S_d$ (odd Frobenius coset $A_d^- = S_d \setminus A_d$). We prove the exact combinatorial identity
   $$\mathbb{P}_{A_d^-}(R = r) = \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r) \quad (\forall r \ge 0), \qquad \mathbb{P}_{A_d^+}(R = r) = 2\mathbb{P}_{S_d}(R = r) - \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r).$$
   Consequently, every odd-coset $A_d^-$ polynomial has the exact same root-multiplicity law as $S_{d-2}$ and reduces immediately to [1]. Evaluating the 2,569-monomial universal $\mathbb{Z}^2$ lattice polynomial and all 9 resonant step sets $\mathcal{R}_9$ of [1] on all odd-degree even cosets $A_d^+$ ($d \ge 5$ odd), all non-permutation Dickson laws $D_d$ ($\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$), and all non-permutation Kummer laws $C_d$ ($m = \gcd(d, p-1) \ge 2$) certifies $\gamma_3 \le \mathbf{1.79631356} < 1.80$ across **every** 3-element step set $S = \{s_0, s_1, s_2\}$ and **every** window $n \ge N$ (peaking at $A_7^+$ on $\mathbb{Z}^2$, with $A_5^+ \le 1.79387869$, $A_9^+ \le 1.79533737$, $A_{d \ge 11}^+ \le 1.79604512$, $D_{(1,3)} \le 1.78288083$, $D_{(1,5)} \le 1.52825579$, $C_2 = 1.734375$, and $C_m \le \frac{38}{27} \approx 1.407407$ for $m \ge 3$; we also record the abstract even-$d$ permutation laws $A_4^+, A_6^+, A_8^+, A_{10}^+$ for completeness).
2. **All-Window Exclusion of $k = 4, 5, 6$ Translate Families and Harris–FKG Universal $\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}$ Local Cover (Theorem 3.1).** Using the reachable-mask Bellman Markov chain of [1], we certify all-window exclusion ($n \ge N$) for consecutive 4-translates $S = (0, 1, 2, 3)$ ($200$ states) across all odd-degree $A_d^+$ ($\le 1.769820$), $D_{(1,5)} \le 1.590400$, and $C_3 \le 1.536232$ (complementing the $S_d$ exclusion of [3]), and for the non-consecutive 4-translate family $S = (0, 1, 2, 4)$ ($4,575$ states) at $S_5 \le 1.799795$, $A_5^+ \le 1.798727$, $D_{(1,5)} \le 1.617988$, and $C_3 \le 1.570397$. Furthermore, using the Harris–FKG inequality on the universal lattice $\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}, \mathcal{S}_0)$, we prove a closed-form 1-round local cover bound valid for **every** non-consecutive $k$-translate step set $S = \{s_0, \dots, s_{k-1}\}$, establishing all-window exclusion $< 1.8n$ for all $k \in \{3, 4\}$ under Dickson $D_d$ ($s \ge 5$, $\le 1.682406$), for all $k \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$ under Dickson $D_d$ ($s \ge 7$, $\le 1.717019$) and Kummer $C_d$ ($m \ge 4$, $\le 1.644043$), and for $k \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ under $C_3$ ($\le \frac{422}{243} \approx 1.736626$), supplemented by Bellman certificates for $k = 5$ ($D_{(1,5)} \le 1.672320, C_3 \le 1.625731$) and $k = 6$ ($C_3 \le 1.683951$).
3. **Unit-Edge ($c = 1$) and Mixed-Price ($c = 3/2$) Transfer for Non-Consecutive 4-Translate Families $S = (0,1,2,4)$ and $(0,1,3,4)$ (Theorem 4.2).** Adapting the mixed line/singleton-point physical cover and 2D interval-completion discrepancy transfer of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 ([3], Section 8.7) to non-consecutive 4-translate step sets of span $W = 4$, we construct exact rational Bellman potential certificates on $2,695$, $6,620$, and $9,870$ states. For prime modulus $N = p$, this excludes $S = (0, 1, 2, 4)$ below $1.8n$ across all $S_d$ ($d \ge 2$, $\le 1.786021$), all odd-degree $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$, $\le 1.787567$), and all non-permutation $D_d, C_d$ in every window satisfying $\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2 \ll n \le \frac{3}{2}p$ (and $\le 1.771395$ on $S_d$, $\le 1.776158$ on odd-degree $A_d^+$ when $\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2 \ll n \le p$), and excludes $S = (0, 1, 3, 4)$ across all $S_d$ ($\le 1.782769$), odd-degree $A_d$ ($\le 1.786572$), and non-permutation $D_d, C_d$ whenever $\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2 \ll n \le p$.

## 1. Introduction, Monodromy Scope, and Prior Machinery

Let $N \ge 2$ be an integer modulus with largest prime factor $p = P(N)$, let $f_N \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ be a polynomial of degree $d \in \{2, \dots, D\}$ whose reduction modulo $p$ has degree $d < p$, let $h_N \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$ be a unit vertical step, and let $S = \{s_0, s_1, \dots, s_{k-1}\} \subset \mathbb{Z}$ be a finite step set of $k \ge 3$ distinct integers modulo $p$. By Lemma 2.1 of `no_three_in_line_64` v1 [1], direct reduction modulo $p \mid N$ projects the modular polynomial translate carrier
$$\mathcal{U}_{f_N, S, h_N} = \bigcup_{s \in S} \bigl\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : y \equiv f_N(x) + s h_N \pmod N\bigr\} \tag{1.1}$$
into the normalized prime-field bipartite incidence graph $G_p(S) = (V_{\mathrm{src}}, W_{\mathrm{row}}, E_S)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, where each source fiber $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has root-multiplicity weight
$$R(v) := \bigl|\bigl\{\bar{x} \in \mathbb{F}_p : \bar{f}(\bar{x}) = v\bigr\}\bigr| \in \{0, 1, \dots, d\}, \qquad \bar{f}(\bar{x}) := \bar{h}^{-1} f_N(\bar{x}) \bmod p, \tag{1.2}$$
each target row $w \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has unit weight $1$, and each shift $s \in S$ induces the directed edge $(v, v + s) \in E_S$.

### 1.1 Monodromy Groups of Indecomposable Polynomials Over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and Scope Boundaries

By Müller's classification of primitive monodromy groups of indecomposable polynomials over finite fields (Müller 1995 [8]; see also Turnwald 1995 [5], Müller 1997 [6], and Behajaina–König–Neftin 2026 [9, Theorem 3.3]), if $\bar{f} \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ is indecomposable of degree $d < p$, its geometric monodromy group $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f}) = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{f}(X) - t / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)) \le S_d$ is either one of the four infinite families—symmetric $S_d$, alternating $A_d$, dihedral/Dickson $D_d$, or cyclic/Kummer $C_d$ (for prime $d$)—or one of a finite list of sporadic/projective permutation groups in degrees $d \in \{5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 23, 31\}$ (including $\mathrm{AGL}_1(5), \mathrm{PGL}_2(5), \mathrm{PSL}_2(7), \mathrm{PGL}_2(7), \mathrm{AGL}_1(7), \mathrm{AGL}_2(3), \mathrm{PSL}_3(3), M_{11}, M_{12}, M_{23}, \mathrm{PSL}_5(2)$).

Three structural distinctions govern which polynomial families can be excluded below $1.8n$ by coordinate and mixed-price covers:

1. **Why Odd Degree $d \ge 5$ is Required for Polynomial Alternating Monodromy $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$:**
   Because $\deg \bar{f} = d < p$, the polynomial $\bar{f}(X) - t$ is tamely and totally ramified over $t = \infty$, so its inertia subgroup $I_\infty \le G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f})$ is generated by a $d$-cycle $\sigma_\infty \in S_d$. Since the sign of a $d$-cycle is $(-1)^{d-1}$, we have $\sigma_\infty \in A_d$ **if and only if $d$ is odd**. Consequently, $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$ in the natural degree-$d$ polynomial action is realizable only for **odd** degrees $d \ge 5$ (whereas for even $d$, any subgroup of $S_d$ containing a $d$-cycle is not contained in $A_d$). When $d \ge 5$ is odd and $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$, $\mathrm{Disc}_X(\bar{f}(X) - t) = c_0 \Delta_0(t)^2$ with $c_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and $\Delta_0(t) \in \mathbb{F}_p[t]$:
   - When $(\frac{c_0}{p}) = +1$, $G_{\mathrm{arith}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$, and Frobenius elements at degree-1 places $t = v \in \mathbb{F}_p$ are equidistributed on the **even coset** $A_d^+ := A_d$.
   - When $(\frac{c_0}{p}) = -1$, $G_{\mathrm{arith}}(\bar{f}) = S_d$ with constant field $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, so every degree-1 place $t = v \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has Frobenius element lying in the **odd coset** $A_d^- := S_d \setminus A_d$.
   *(In our tables below, we also include the abstract even-$d$ alternating fixed-point laws $A_4^+, A_6^+, A_8^+, A_{10}^+$ as probability-model benchmarks, since $A_d$ for even $d$ can occur for rational functions or general branched covers unramified/differently ramified at infinity, while clearly distinguishing them from odd-degree polynomial monodromy.)*
2. **Why Non-Permutation Hypotheses Are Necessary for $D_d$, $C_d$, and Composite Polynomials:**
   - For a Kummer polynomial $\bar{f}(X) = a(X - x_0)^d + c$, the fiber multiplicity is governed by $m = \gcd(d, p - 1)$. When $m = 1$ (e.g., $X^3$ at $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$), $\bar{f}$ permutes $\mathbb{F}_p$, so $R(v) = 1$ for every $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$.
   - For a Dickson polynomial $D_d(X, a)$ ($a \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, odd $d \ge 3$), the fiber multiplicity is governed by $s_- = \gcd(d, p - 1)$ and $s_+ = \gcd(d, p + 1)$ (Chou–Gomez-Calderon–Mullen 1988 [7]). When $s_- = s_+ = 1$ (i.e., $\gcd(d, p^2 - 1) = 1$), $D_d(X, a)$ permutes $\mathbb{F}_p$, so again $R(v) = 1$ for every $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$.
   - Whenever $R(v) \equiv 1$, the bipartite incidence graph $G_p(S)$ is a $k$-regular bipartite graph with a perfect matching, so its minimum vertex cover has size $p$ and any pure axis-parallel line cover yields bound $2p = 2n$, not $< 1.8n$. (The greedy interval queue bound $\frac{4k}{2k+1}$ of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [3, Theorem 6.1] relies on empty fibers $R(v) = 0$ to reset queues and does not apply when $R(v) \equiv 1$.) Similarly, if $\bar{f} = g \circ h$ is a composite polynomial where the inner factor $h$ permutes $\mathbb{F}_p$, then $R_{\bar{f}}(v) = R_g(v)$ and $\bar{f}$ inherits the fiber law of $g$ (whereas if neither $g$ nor $h$ is covered by our monodromy hypotheses, image containment alone does not bound the joint shift distribution). We therefore explicitly restrict all Dickson and Kummer claims to the **non-permutation** regimes $m = \gcd(d, p - 1) \ge 2$ and $\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$.
3. **Relationship to Prior Work (`no_three_in_line_64` v1 [1] and `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [3]):**
   We use the 2,569-monomial universal $\mathbb{Z}^2$ lattice polynomial, the 9-element resonant step-set classification $\mathcal{R}_9$, and the reachable-mask Bellman Markov chain constructed in `no_three_in_line_64` v1 [1], together with the Dickson branch-forest independence and mixed-price ($c \ge 1$) physical transfer framework of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 ([3], Sections 8.7, 10.6, and 11.1). The specific new contributions of this paper are: (i) the Odd-Coset Degree-Shift Identity $\mathbb{P}_{A_d^-} = \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}$ and the evaluation of the universal $\mathbb{Z}^2$ polynomial and all 9 resonant step sets across all odd-degree alternating cosets $A_d^+$ and non-permutation Dickson laws $D_{(1,s)}$; (ii) the Harris–FKG universal $\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}$ 1-round local cover for arbitrary $k$-translate step sets under $D_d$ and $C_d$, plus all-window Bellman certificates for $S = (0,1,2,3)$ and $S = (0,1,2,4)$ under $A_d^+, D_{(1,5)}, C_3, S_5$; and (iii) the 16-mask unit-edge ($c = 1$) and mixed-price ($c = 3/2$) Bellman certificates and physical-window transfer for the non-consecutive 4-translate families $S = (0,1,2,4)$ and $S = (0,1,3,4)$.

## 2. Theorem 1: Odd-Coset Degree-Shift Identity and 3-Translate Exclusion Across $A_d, D_d, C_d$

### 2.1 The Exact Root-Multiplicity Laws of $A_d^-, A_d^+$, Non-Permutation $D_d$, and Non-Permutation $C_d$

**Proposition 2.1 (Odd-Coset Degree-Shift Identity for $A_d^\pm$).** *For every integer $d \ge 4$ and every integer $r \ge 0$, let $R(\sigma) = |\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)|$ denote the number of fixed points of $\sigma \in S_d$. When $\sigma$ is drawn uniformly from the odd coset $A_d^- = S_d \setminus A_d$ or the even coset $A_d^+ = A_d$, its fixed-point distribution satisfies the exact identities*
$$\mathbb{P}_{A_d^-}(R = r) = \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r), \qquad \mathbb{P}_{A_d^+}(R = r) = 2\mathbb{P}_{S_d}(R = r) - \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r). \tag{2.1}$$

*Proof.* For any $m \ge 0$, let $D_m^+$ and $D_m^-$ denote the numbers of even and odd derangements in $S_m$, respectively, with $D_0^+ = 1, D_0^- = 0$ and $D_1^+ = D_1^- = 0$. The signed derangement count $D_m^+ - D_m^-$ is the determinant of the $m \times m$ matrix $J_m - I_m$ (where $J_m$ is the all-ones matrix and $I_m$ is the identity), which has eigenvalue $m - 1$ with multiplicity $1$ and eigenvalue $-1$ with multiplicity $m - 1$; hence
$$D_m^+ - D_m^- = (-1)^{m-1}(m - 1) \qquad (\forall m \ge 1). \tag{2.2}$$
Since $D_m = D_m^+ + D_m^- = m! \sum_{j=0}^m \frac{(-1)^j}{j!} = m! a_m$ satisfies the standard recurrence $D_m - m D_{m-1} = (-1)^m$, subtracting (2.2) from $D_m^+ + D_m^-$ gives
$$2 D_m^- = D_m - (-1)^{m-1}(m - 1) = D_m + (-1)^m m - (-1)^m = m D_{m-1} + (-1)^m m = m\bigl(D_{m-1} + (-1)^m\bigr) = m(m - 1) D_{m-2}. \tag{2.3}$$
Now choose a uniform random permutation $\sigma \in A_d^-$ ($|A_d^-| = d! / 2$). A permutation $\sigma \in S_d$ has exactly $r$ fixed points and is odd if and only if it fixes a subset of $r$ elements ($\binom{d}{r}$ choices) and acts as an odd derangement on the remaining $m = d - r$ elements ($D_{d-r}^-$ choices). For $r > d - 2$, $D_1^- = D_0^- = 0$, matching $\mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r) = 0$. For $0 \le r \le d - 2$, substituting (2.3) with $m = d - r$ yields
$$\mathbb{P}_{A_d^-}(R = r) = \frac{\binom{d}{r} D_{d-r}^-}{d! / 2} = \frac{d!}{r! (d - r)!} \cdot \frac{(d - r)(d - r - 1) D_{d-2-r}}{d!} = \frac{1}{r!} \frac{D_{d-2-r}}{(d - 2 - r)!} = \frac{a_{d-2-r}}{r!} = \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r).$$
Finally, since $S_d = A_d^+ \sqcup A_d^-$ is the disjoint union of two cosets of equal size $d! / 2$, we have $\mathbb{P}_{S_d}(R = r) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}_{A_d^+}(R = r) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}_{A_d^-}(R = r)$, which rearranges immediately to $\mathbb{P}_{A_d^+}(R = r) = 2\mathbb{P}_{S_d}(R = r) - \mathbb{P}_{S_{d-2}}(R = r)$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.2 (Exact Fiber-Multiplicity Laws of Non-Permutation Kummer and Dickson Polynomials).** *Let $p \ge 5$ be a prime.*
1. **(Non-Permutation Kummer / Cyclic $C_d$, $m \ge 2$):** *For $\bar{f}(X) = a(X - x_0)^d + c \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ with $a \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and $m = \gcd(d, p - 1) \ge 2$, as $v$ ranges over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $R(c) = 1$ and the remaining $p - 1$ values split into $\frac{p-1}{m}$ fibers with $R(v) = m$ and $(p - 1)(1 - \frac{1}{m})$ fibers with $R(v) = 0$, converging as $p \to \infty$ to the discrete law*
   $$C_m: \qquad \mathbb{P}(R = 0) = 1 - \frac{1}{m}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(R = m) = \frac{1}{m}. \tag{2.4}$$
2. **(Non-Permutation Dickson / Dihedral $D_d$, $\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$):** *For the Dickson polynomial $D_d(X, a)$ ($a \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, odd $d \ge 3$), let $s_- = \gcd(d, p - 1)$ and $s_+ = \gcd(d, p + 1)$ (with $\gcd(s_-, s_+) = 1$ since $d$ is odd and $\gcd(p - 1, p + 1) = 2$), and assume $\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$ (excluding the permutation case $s_- = s_+ = 1$; Chou–Gomez-Calderon–Mullen 1988 [7]):*
   - *If exactly one of $s_-, s_+$ is $> 1$, say $s = \max(s_-, s_+) \in \{3, 5, 7, \dots\}$ and $\min(s_-, s_+) = 1$, then up to at most $2$ ramified values ($v = \pm 2 a^{d/2}$ when $a$ is a square in $\mathbb{F}_p$), half of $\mathbb{F}_p$ has $R(v) = 1$, a fraction $\frac{1}{2s}$ has $R(v) = s$, and the remaining fraction $\frac{s-1}{2s}$ has $R(v) = 0$, giving the asymptotic law*
     $$D_{(1, s)}: \qquad \mathbb{P}(R = 0) = \frac{s - 1}{2s}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(R = 1) = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(R = s) = \frac{1}{2s}. \tag{2.5}$$
   - *If both $s_- \ge 3$ and $s_+ \ge 3$ (so $\{s_-, s_+\} \ge \{3, 5\}$), then up to at most $2$ ramified values, no fiber has $R(v) \in \{1, 2\}$: a fraction $\frac{1}{2s_-} + \frac{1}{2s_+}$ of fibers have $R(v) \in \{s_-, s_+\} \subseteq [3, d]$, and the remaining fraction $1 - \frac{1}{2s_-} - \frac{1}{2s_+}$ has $R(v) = 0$.*

*Proof of (2).* Every $\bar{x} \in \mathbb{F}_p$ is the image under $\tau(u) = u + a/u$ of the two roots $u, a/u$ of $u^2 - \bar{x}u + a = 0$, which lie either in the cyclic subgroup $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ of order $p - 1$ (when $\bar{x}^2 - 4a$ is a square in $\mathbb{F}_p$) or in the norm-$a$ coset $\mu_{p+1}(a) = \{u \in \mathbb{F}_{p^2} : u^{p+1} = a\}$ (when $\bar{x}^2 - 4a$ is a non-square in $\mathbb{F}_p$). Since $D_d(u + a/u, a) = u^d + a^d / u^d$, for any unramified $v \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{\pm 2a^{d/2}\}$, the quadratic equation $z^2 - v z + a^d = 0$ has two distinct roots $z_1, z_2 = a^d / z_1$. For the $\frac{p-1}{2} + O(1)$ values of $v$ where $v^2 - 4a^d$ is a non-zero square in $\mathbb{F}_p$, $z_1, z_2 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$; since $u \mapsto u^d$ on the cyclic group $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ has kernel of order $s_- = \gcd(d, p - 1)$ and image of index $s_-$ (and $z_2 = (a/u)^d$ whenever $z_1 = u^d$), exactly $\frac{1}{s_-}$ of these split values $v$ have $R(v) = s_-$ and $1 - \frac{1}{s_-}$ have $R(v) = 0$. For the $\frac{p-1}{2} + O(1)$ values of $v$ where $v^2 - 4a^d$ is a non-square in $\mathbb{F}_p$, $z_1, z_2 \in \mu_{p+1}(a^d)$, where $u \mapsto u^d$ on $\mu_{p+1}(a)$ has kernel of order $s_+ = \gcd(d, p + 1)$ and image of index $s_+$, so $\frac{1}{s_+}$ of these non-split values have $R(v) = s_+$ and $1 - \frac{1}{s_+}$ have $R(v) = 0$. Combining the two halves of weight $\frac{1}{2}$ yields (2.5). $\square$

### 2.2 Shift-Compositum Independence for $A_d, D_d$, and $C_d$

For any finite shift subset $A \subset \mathbb{Z}$, we use the branch-locus shift argument of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 ([3], Sections 6.2, 10.6, and 11.1) and `no_three_in_line_64` v1 ([1], Lemma 2.2) to establish joint independence of $(R(v + c))_{c \in A}$:

**Lemma 2.3 (Joint Shift Independence Across $A_d, D_d, C_d$).** *Let $A \subset \mathbb{Z}$ be a finite subset of integers of diameter $\mathrm{diam}(A) = \max(A) - \min(A)$.*
1. **(Alternating $A_d^\pm$, odd $d \ge 5$):** *Suppose $\bar{f} \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ has odd degree $d \ge 5$ and geometric monodromy group $G_{\mathrm{geom}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$. Since $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$) is a non-abelian simple group and $\bar{f}(X) - t$ has at least one finite branch point in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ (as a tame cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ ramified only at $\infty$ is cyclic of degree dividing $d$, not $A_d$) and at most $d - 1$ finite branch points, for all $p > \max(d, \mathrm{diam}(A)(d - 1))$ the shifted splitting fields $K_c = \mathrm{Split}(\bar{f}(X) - t + c / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t))$ ($c \in A$) have pairwise distinct extremal finite branch points and therefore satisfy $G_{\mathrm{geom}, A} = \mathrm{Gal}(\prod_{c \in A} K_c / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)) = A_d^A$ by Goursat's lemma. Over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$, if $G_{\mathrm{arith}}(\bar{f}) = A_d$, then $\mathrm{Gal}(\prod_{c \in A} K_c / \mathbb{F}_p(t)) = A_d^A$, so $(R(v + c))_{c \in A}$ converges to i.i.d. $A_d^+$ variables. If $G_{\mathrm{arith}}(\bar{f}) = S_d$, the quadratic constant extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}(t) / \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ is shared diagonally across all $c \in A$: the arithmetic group is the diagonal subgroup $G_{\mathrm{arith}, A} = \{(\sigma_c)_{c \in A} \in S_d^A : \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma_c) \text{ is independent of } c \in A\}$, and the Frobenius element at every degree-1 place $t = v \in \mathbb{F}_p$ projects to the nontrivial element of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^2} / \mathbb{F}_p)$ and is therefore equidistributed on the product of odd cosets $(A_d^-)^A$. Thus $(R(v + c))_{c \in A}$ converges to i.i.d. $A_d^- \stackrel{d}{=} S_{d-2}$ variables.*
2. **(Non-Permutation Kummer $C_d$, $m \ge 2$):** *For $\bar{f}(X) = a(X - x_0)^d + c_0$ with $m = \gcd(d, p - 1) \ge 2$, the indicators $\mathbf{1}\{R(v + c) = m\}$ ($c \in A$) detect whether $a^{-1}(v + c - c_0)$ is an $m$-th power in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$. Since the linear factors $\{t + c - c_0\}_{c \in A}$ have distinct roots in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ whenever $p > \mathrm{diam}(A)$, the Kummer compositum $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)((t + c - c_0)^{1/m} : c \in A)$ has geometric and arithmetic Galois group $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^A$ over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$, so $(R(v + c))_{c \in A}$ are asymptotically i.i.d. draws from $C_m$.*
3. **(Non-Permutation Dickson $D_d$, $\max(s_-, s_+) \ge 3$):** *As established in `no_three_in_line_10` v3 ([3], Sections 10.6 and 11.1), for $\bar{f}(X) = D_d(X, a)$ ($a \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, odd $d \ge 3$), each shifted splitting field $K_c = \mathrm{Split}(D_d(X, a) - (t + c) / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t))$ has Galois group $D_d = C_d \rtimes C_2$ and unique finite branch points $t = -c \pm 2a^{d/2}$, at both of which the inertia subgroup is generated by a reflection of order $2$ in $D_d \setminus C_d$. Because $d$ is odd, every proper normal subgroup $N \triangleleft D_d$ is contained in $C_d$, so the reflection inertia at $t = -c \pm 2a^{d/2}$ remains non-trivial in every non-trivial quotient $D_d / N$. When $p > 2\,\mathrm{diam}(A)$, for any non-empty subset $J \subseteq A$, at least one of the extreme branch points $-\max(J) - 2a^{d/2}$ or $-\min(J) + 2a^{d/2}$ is unshared across $J$, proving $\mathrm{Gal}(\prod_{c \in A} K_c / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)) = D_d^A$ and asymptotic i.i.d. convergence of $(R(v + c))_{c \in A}$ to $D_{(s_-, s_+)}$.*

### 3.2 Closed-Form 1-Round Local Cover via Harris–FKG for Arbitrary $k$-Translate Step Sets Under $D_d$ and $C_d$

For non-permutation Dickson polynomials $D_d(X, a)$ with $s = \max(s_-, s_+) \ge 5$ and Kummer polynomials $X^d + c$ with $m = \gcd(d, p - 1) \ge 3$, we can exclude **every** non-consecutive $k$-translate step set $S = \{s_0, s_1, \dots, s_{k-1}\}$ simultaneously without any span restriction on $S$ (provided only $p > 2\,\mathrm{diam}(S)$):

**Theorem 3.1 (Universal $\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}$ 1-Round Local Cover for Non-Permutation $D_d$ and $C_d$).** *Let $k \ge 3$ and let $S = \{s_0, s_1, \dots, s_{k-1}\} \subset \mathbb{Z}$ be any $k$-element step set with $s_i \not\equiv s_j \pmod p$. Consider the 1-round local vertex cover $(U_0, S_1)$ on $G_p(S)$ that eliminates every fiber $v$ with $R(v) \ge 3$ into $C_0$ (covering its $k$ target rows $v + s_i$, so $U_0(w) = \mathbf{1}\{\forall s \in S,\, R(w - s) \le 2\}$), keeps in $S_1$ every singleton fiber $v$ ($R(v) = 1$) that has at least $1$ uncovered target row in $U_0$ ($S_1(v) = \mathbf{1}\{R(v) = 1 \text{ and } \exists s \in S,\, U_0(v + s) = 1\}$), and leaves all rows in $U_0$ uncovered ($U_1 = U_0$).*
1. **(Kummer $C_m$, $m \ge 3$):** *Since $R(v) \in \{0, m\}$ with $m \ge 3$ (up to the single ramified value $R(c_0) = 1$), $S_1$ is empty ($O(1/p)$) and $U_0(w) = 0$ whenever at least one of the $k$ distinct shifts $\{w - s_i\}_{i=0}^{k-1}$ has $R(w - s_i) = m$. By Lemma 2.3(2), for every $k$-element step set $S$, the asymptotic all-window density is at most*
   $$\gamma_k(S, C_m) \le 2\Bigl(1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{m}\Bigr)^k\Bigr), \tag{3.2}$$
   *which is strictly below $1.80$ for **all** $k \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ when $m = 3$ ($\frac{38}{27} \approx 1.407407$ at $k = 3$, $\frac{130}{81} \approx 1.604938$ at $k = 4$, and $\frac{422}{243} \approx \mathbf{1.736626} < 1.80$ at $k = 5$), and for **all** $k \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$ when $m \ge 4$ ($\le 2(1 - (3/4)^6) = \frac{3367}{2048} \approx \mathbf{1.644043} < 1.80$).*
2. **(Dickson $D_{(1, s)}$, $s \ge 5$, and $D_{(s_-, s_+)}$, $s_-, s_+ \ge 3$):** *Under $D_{(s_-, s_+)}$ with $s_-, s_+ \ge 3$, $R(v) \notin \{1, 2\}$ up to $2$ ramified values, so $S_1 = \emptyset$ and $\gamma_k(S, D_{(s_-, s_+)}) \le 2(1 - (1 - \frac{1}{2s_-} - \frac{1}{2s_+})^k) \le 2(1 - (\frac{11}{15})^6) \approx 1.690233 < 1.80$ for all $k \le 6$. Under $D_{(1, s)}$ ($s \ge 5$), $R(v) \in \{0, 1, s\}$ with probabilities $(\frac{s-1}{2s}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2s})$. First, for any $w \in \mathbb{F}_p$, the $k$ shifts $\{w - s : s \in S\}$ are distinct, so $\mathbb{P}(U_0(w) = 0) = 1 - (1 - \frac{1}{2s})^k$. Second, condition on $R(v) = 1$ (so $R(v) \ne s$), and let $X_u := \mathbf{1}\{R(u) = s\} \in \{0, 1\}$ for $u \ne v$, which are asymptotically i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\frac{1}{2s})$ variables by Lemma 2.3(3). For each $s \in S$, the event $E_s := \{U_0(v + s) = 0\} = \{\exists s' \in S \setminus \{s\} : X_{v + s - s'} = 1\}$ is a **monotone increasing** function of the independent Bernoulli variables $(X_u)_{u \ne v}$ and has marginal probability $\mathbb{P}(E_s \mid R(v) = 1) = 1 - q_{s, k}$, where $q_{s,k} = (1 - \frac{1}{2s})^{k-1}$ (since $|S \setminus \{s\}| = k - 1$). By the **Harris–FKG inequality**, the increasing events $\{E_s\}_{s \in S}$ are positively associated:*
   $$\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\forall s \in S,\, U_0(v + s) = 0 \;\Big|\; R(v) = 1\Bigr) = \mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{s \in S} E_s \,\Big|\, R(v) = 1\Bigr) \ge \prod_{s \in S} \mathbb{P}(E_s \mid R(v) = 1) = (1 - q_{s, k})^k,$$
   *with equality when the $k$ difference sets $\{s - s' : s' \in S \setminus \{s\}\}$ are pairwise disjoint (the universal lattice $\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}, \mathcal{S}_0)$). Consequently, $\mathbb{P}(S_1(v) = 1) \le \frac{1}{2}(1 - (1 - q_{s,k})^k)$, and for **every** $k$-element step set $S \subset \mathbb{Z}$,*
   $$\gamma_k(S, D_{(1, s)}) \le 2\Bigl(1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{2s}\Bigr)^k + \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1 - \bigl(1 - (1 - \tfrac{1}{2s})^{k-1}\bigr)^k\Bigr)\Bigr). \tag{3.3}$$
   *Evaluating (3.3) gives $\gamma_k(S, D_{(1, 5)}) \le 1.535141$ ($k = 3$) and $\le \mathbf{1.682406} < 1.80$ ($k = 4$); for $s \ge 7$, (3.3) gives $\gamma_k(S, D_{(1, s)}) \le 1.396342$ ($k = 3$), $\le 1.511488$ ($k = 4$), $\le 1.618166$ ($k = 5$), and $\le \mathbf{1.717019} < 1.80$ ($k = 6$).*

## 4. Theorem 3: Mixed Line/Singleton-Point Covers ($c \in \{1, 3/2\}$) for Non-Consecutive Four-Translate Families

To exclude the non-consecutive 4-translate step sets $S = (0, 1, 2, 4)$ and $S = (0, 1, 3, 4)$ across **all** symmetric degrees $d \ge 2$ (including the quartic $S_4$) and all odd alternating degrees $d \ge 5$, we adapt the **mixed line/singleton-point physical cover and 2D interval-completion transfer** of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 ([3], Section 8.7) to non-consecutive masks of span $W = 4$.

### 4.1 Mixed Line/Singleton-Point Block Covers and Joint Interval Discrepancy Transfer

Fix a rational edge price $c \in [1, 2]$ ($c = 1$ or $c = 3/2$), a block length $L \ge 1$, and a starting residue $z \in \mathbb{F}_p$. For a 4-translate step set $S = (0, s_1, s_2, W)$ of span $W = 4$, consider the contiguous source block $B_z = \{z, z+1, \dots, z+L-1\} \subset \mathbb{F}_p$ and target block $T_z = \{z, z+1, \dots, z+L+W-1\} \subset \mathbb{F}_p$. Given a target row-cut subset $Y \subseteq T_z$, let
$$e_i(Y) := \bigl|\bigl\{s \in S : z + i + s \notin Y\bigr\}\bigr| \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\} \qquad (0 \le i \le L - 1) \tag{4.1}$$
be the number of uncovered incident residue edges at source fiber $v_i = z + i$. Define the local block cut value using the **full** root counts $r_i = R(z + i)$ (and noting, as shown in `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [3, Section 8.7] via fractional $2k$-cycle decomposition in $K_{k,k}$, that clipping $r_i$ at $k = 4$ preserves the minimum cut value identically for every demand vector):
$$B_{c, L}(z) := \min_{Y \subseteq T_z} \left[2|Y| + \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} R(z + i) \min\bigl(2,\, c\, e_i(Y)\bigr)\right]. \tag{4.2}$$

Crucially, as pointed out in [3, Section 8.7], $\min(2, c\, e_i(Y))$ is **not** a pointwise capacity of a vertical line when $p < n < 2p$ (since a single residue edge $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2$ can lift to up to $(a + 1)^2 = 4$ physical points in $Q_n(x_0, y_0)$ when $n = a p + r$ with $a = 1$, which need not be collinear). Instead, a minimizing target set $Y^*(z) \subseteq T_z$ (chosen by a deterministic tie-breaking rule on the full root-count vector $(R(z+i))_{0 \le i < L}$) induces a **mixed physical cover** $\mathcal{C}_z$ of the carrier points supported over $B_z$ consisting of three types of objects:
1. **Target horizontal lines ($T$):** For each $w \in Y^*(z)$, select all physical horizontal lines $y \in [y_0, y_0 + n - 1]$ with $\bar{h}^{-1} y \equiv w \pmod p$. Each residue $w \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has $w_y(w) = a + \mathbf{1}_{I_y}(w)$ physical horizontal lines in $Q_n(x_0, y_0)$, each covering at most $2$ points of $S_{\mathrm{pts}}$ (physical cost $2 w_y(w)$).
2. **Source vertical lines ($A$):** For each $i \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$ such that $c\, e_i(Y^*(z)) \ge 2$, select all physical vertical lines $x \in [x_0, x_0 + n - 1]$ with $\bar{x} = (x \bmod p) \in \bar{f}^{-1}(z + i)$. Each root $\bar{x} \in \bar{f}^{-1}(z + i)$ has $w_x(\bar{x}) = a + \mathbf{1}_{I_x}(\bar{x})$ physical vertical lines in $Q_n(x_0, y_0)$, each covering at most $2$ points of $S_{\mathrm{pts}}$ (physical cost $2 w_x(\bar{x})$).
3. **Individual singleton residue points ($E$):** For each $i \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$ such that $c\, e_i(Y^*(z)) < 2$ (which for $c \in \{1, 3/2\}$ occurs precisely when $e_i(Y^*(z)) = 1$, i.e., there is a unique uncovered shift $s^* \in S$ with $z + i + s^* \notin Y^*(z)$), and for each root $\bar{x} \in \bar{f}^{-1}(z + i)$, cover the **individual physical grid points** $(x, y) \in Q_n(x_0, y_0)$ satisfying $x \equiv \bar{x} \pmod p$ and $\bar{h}^{-1} y \equiv z + i + s^* \pmod p$ directly as singleton points! There are exactly $w_x(\bar{x}) w_y(z + i + s^*)$ such physical copies in $Q_n(x_0, y_0)$, each contributing at most $1$ point to $S_{\mathrm{pts}}$ (physical cost $w_x(\bar{x}) w_y(z + i + s^*)$).

Let $A(z), T(z), E(z)$ denote the residue counts of source roots $\bar{x}$, target rows $w$, and singleton residue pairs $(\bar{x}, z + i + s^*)$ selected in block $B_z$, so that the algebraic block cost is $B_{c, L}(z) = 2 A(z) + 2 T(z) + c E(z)$.

**Lemma 4.1 (2D Interval-Completion Discrepancy for Mixed Covers Across $S_d, A_d, D_d, C_d$).** *Let $n = a p + r$ with $\lambda = n / p \le c \le 2$ (so $a \in \{0, 1\}$). For any polynomial $\bar{f} \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ of degree $d < p$ satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 2.4, summing the physical capacity $C_z$ of the mixed cover $\mathcal{C}_z$ over all $z \in \mathbb{F}_p$ yields*
$$\sum_{z \in \mathbb{F}_p} C_z = p\bigl(2 \lambda (A_L + T_L) + \lambda^2 E_L\bigr) + O_{d, L}\bigl(\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2\bigr), \tag{4.3}$$
*where $A_L, T_L, E_L$ are the asymptotic model expectations of $A(z), T(z), E(z)$. Dividing by $L n = L \lambda p$ and accounting for the $W = 4$ boundary padding between blocks of length $L$ gives, for all windows with $\lambda = n / p \le c$,*
$$\frac{|S_{\mathrm{pts}}|}{n} \le \frac{2(A_L + T_L) + \lambda E_L}{L} + \frac{2 W}{L} + O_{d, L}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2}{n}\right) \le \frac{\mathbb{E}[B_{c, L}]}{L} + \frac{8}{L} + O_{d, L}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2}{n}\right). \tag{4.4}$$

*Proof.* Following `no_three_in_line_10` v3 ([3], Section 8.7), each source flag and singleton-edge flag is a linear combination of Galois-equivariant indicators on the compositum $\mathcal{K}_{[0, L-1]}$ coupled with a distinguished root $\bar{x}$ of $\bar{f}(\bar{x}) = z + i$. For any non-zero additive frequency pair $(u, v) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ arising from the 2D Pólya–Vinogradov Fourier expansion of $\mathbf{1}_{I_x}(\bar{x}) \mathbf{1}_{I_y}(\bar{h}(z + i + s^*))$, substituting $z = \bar{f}(\bar{x}) - i$ converts the phase $u \bar{x} + v \bar{h}(z + i + s^*)$ into the polynomial $P_{u, v}(\bar{x}) = u \bar{x} + v \bar{h}(\bar{f}(\bar{x}) + s^*)$, which has degree $d < p$ when $v \ne 0$ and degree $1 < p$ when $v = 0, u \ne 0$. In both cases, the pole order at $\bar{x} = \infty$ is coprime to $p$, so the Artin–Schreier phase $\psi(P_{u,v}(\bar{x}))$ is wildly ramified at infinity and cannot be cancelled by the tame Galois compositum $\mathcal{K}_{[0, L-1]}$ (under $S_d, A_d, D_d,$ or $C_d$). Applying the Weil Riemann Hypothesis bound to each non-zero frequency pair $(u, v)$ and summing the two 1D harmonic series $\sum_{|u| < p/2} \frac{1}{|u|+1} \sum_{|v| < p/2} \frac{1}{|v|+1} = O((1 + \log p)^2)$ yields the discrepancy $O_{d, L}((a + 1)^2 \sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2) = O_{d, L}(\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2)$ since $a \le 1$. Finally, since $\lambda \le c$ and $E_L \ge 0$, we have $2(A_L + T_L) + \lambda E_L \le 2(A_L + T_L) + c E_L = \mathbb{E}[B_{c, L}]$. $\square$
