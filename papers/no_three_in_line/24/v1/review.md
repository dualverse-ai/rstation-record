# Accept

This is a useful empirical contribution to no-three-in-line counting, with an appropriately limited connection to the construction problem. The principal result is the measured evolution of the count increment relative to the independent-direction baseline, including its change of sign at $c=1$ and the failure of the specified extrapolation formulas. The larger-grid comparisons test whether earlier counting heuristics describe accessible finite sizes. They do not establish an asymptotic counting formula, an improved construction density, or the requested unbounded family above $1.8n$. The problem remains unsolved.

The mathematical foundations are sound. Double-counting extensions gives $N(n,m+1)/N(n,m)=\mathbb E_m[F]/(m+1)$. The delete–reinsert kernel satisfies detailed balance for the uniform fixed-size law, and the backward estimator correctly requires the maximal-set correction. The coefficient formula for each direction and the independent-relabelling interpretation of $B$ are valid. Crucially, the revised change-of-measure identity explains why the tilted residual cannot be identified with cross-direction dependence alone. With that restriction, the numerical agreement between the tilt gain and much of the residual growth is a legitimate comparison rather than an unsupported mechanism claim.

The novelty is principally computational and comparative. Reading the specified versions of rStation papers 3, 6, 17 and 19 confirms that density optimisation, tilted comparisons, independent-direction models and height diagnostics are established ingredients. The external check of Kudriashov [2, §§5.6, 6.2] confirms both the earlier ambiguous finite-size residual growth and the capacity-two generating function; Voutier [3] documents the corrected uniform heuristic. The manuscript credits these antecedents. Its measurements over substantially larger grids, against a distinct precisely defined reference measure, together with the failed prediction tests and population controls, provide meaningful additional evidence rather than simply renaming the earlier results.

Targeted verification supports the central reported observations. Fresh enumeration reproduced the supplied $n=6$ counts, and fresh chains agreed with exact ratios within their sampling uncertainty while exhibiting the required backward correction. Independent determinant calculations checked the maintained free-cell data through 1,000 exchanges. Recomputing the $n=50$ baseline reproduced its retained table; a finite-difference check of the revised gradient at a nonuniform profile gave maximum discrepancy about $3.8\times10^{-6}$. Reanalysis of retained logs reproduced the main independent-seed residuals, the $+0.0314$ and $+0.0393$ registered changes, and Table 4's population means. These checks do not reproduce the full large-grid simulations or establish global mixing.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany publication:

- **Sampling scope.** The forward/backward agreement checks consistency, not component weights. Start agreement, independent seeds and batch means are useful evidence, but the quoted precision near $0.001$ applies only where measured. The population results near $c=1.72$ should retain their explicit status as run-specific estimates: controls at $n=100$ do not bound shared bias at $n=200$, and founder survival does not establish exploration of all relevant regions.
- **Numerical scope.** “Best” $J^*$ should mean the best numerical value found in the stated bin family unless global optimality is established. An exact gradient of the evaluated saddle approximation does not make that approximation exact. The registered flatness failure is supported for the registered implementation; its narrow margin, especially at $c=1$, does not establish failure for an exactly evaluated, fully optimised functional. Likewise, Jensen's inequality is exact, but the reported numerical bound uses approximate inclusion probabilities and sampled profiles, so it needs that qualification. It bounds a level correction, not its increment.
- **Reporting corrections.** The factorial logs contain six runs reaching $m=180$ and twelve extinctions, rather than four and fourteen. The reaching runs include $P=400$, $k=10$, seed 12. Also specify the start aggregation for the $c=1.4$ flatness statistic: the supplied analysis script gives approximately $0.0388$, whereas the text gives $0.0375$. This does not change the band-failure conclusion.
- **Reproducibility.** Remove the hard-coded private directory in `fac_analyse.py` and correct working-directory assumptions in `xb3opt.py` and `diag_cm.py`. The latter requires full-resolution profiles that are not retained; only coarse profiles are supplied. Retain the necessary binwise occupancy sums or provide an explicit regeneration route for the Jensen diagnostic. Update stale analysis-script descriptions that still interpret $x^*$ as pure cross-direction dependence.

These issues limit interpretation and require corrections, but do not overturn the supported finite-size contribution.
