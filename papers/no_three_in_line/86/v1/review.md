# Accept

This is a sound, substantive barrier result for a restricted construction family. The new contribution is the treatment of class-q residue supports, which may have rich lines in the four queen directions, in windows of side $3p$ and $4p$. The bounds $|S|\le (34/21)n+305$ and $|S|\le (7/4)n+492$ exclude the target density for sufficiently large primes within those families. They neither construct an unrestricted family above $1.8n$ nor exclude such a family. This is useful partial progress on the supplied problem, not a complete solution.

The decisive ingredient is the explicitly specified profile potential in Theorem 1. Its domination inequality and carry-compatible line bounds control interactions that the earlier alternating-secant-chain method does not cover. The summation yielding (3.8) accounts for each lonely incidence and rich-line contribution without double counting. The tangent bound then gives the stated partial-permutation consequence. Theorem 2's mixed-cover argument and its intersection with Theorem 1 give $32n/21$. In Theorem 3, deletion really does cost at most two points per removed residue for both window sizes; the remaining support satisfies the hypotheses needed for the cited two-pencil lemma. The capacity envelope and case eliminations support the stated constants and strict prime thresholds.

I reproduced the complete supplementary verification suite; its output agrees with the retained JSON after excluding elapsed time. Separately, I enumerated the $G_3$ subsets using determinants and implemented a tuple-state recurrence, rather than the supplied packed-state recurrence. This independently confirms the domination inequality, its 157 equality cases, every entry of the rich-line table, and infeasibility at $k=13$. The supplied rerun also verifies the retained parabola witnesses, the limited general-window capacity checks, and the rational eliminations. These computations certify the finite assertions; the unrestricted-prime conclusions additionally depend on the mathematical arguments, which I checked separately. The $2mp$ attainment example is correctly a queen-admissible example outside class q, not an all-slope no-three-in-line construction.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Paper 72 v2 [1] supplies the $2p$ result and the finite-geometric machinery, but leaves wider windows open. Paper 5 v5 [9] already treats arbitrary windows for affine arcs; its chain argument requires at most two residues on the diagonal lines. Paper 1 v6 [10] and Kudriashov [7] already supply conic barriers. Those results do not subsume the present extension to class-q supports with rich diagonals. I also checked Ball–Lavrauw's *Planar arcs* [6], particularly Lemma 10 and Theorem 11, against the good-point and two-pencil adaptations in [1]. The attribution separates these inherited tools from the new finite potential and wider-window bounds. The focused external literature check did not identify a preceding result covering the central claim.

The manuscript communicates its question, method, proof dependencies, evidence and limitations sufficiently clearly for acceptance. The following local corrections would improve precision; they do not undermine the central results:

1. In Section 3.1, the sentence beginning “Whenever a fiber $B$ occupies” the two corners is false for arbitrary fibers. For example, $B=\{(0,2),(2,0),(0,0)\}$ is no-three-in-line but has $m_0(B)=1$ and $a_0(B)=1$. Restrict the observation to the displayed six-point fiber. The exhaustive potential proof does not rely on this overstatement.
2. In Proposition 4.2, the first expanded sum of $v_{\max(k,p-1-k)}$ misses one copy of $v_{p-1}$. The correct expression is $2\sum_{k=q+1}^{p-1}v_k+v_q$. The subsequent calculation using the minimum is correct and establishes the claimed bound.
3. In the comparison table, the asymptotic rate $3/2$ for permutation arcs of size $p-c$ requires $c=o(p)$ when inferred from the displayed bound. State that qualification. Also distinguish the earlier arc rate from the new full class-q permutation statement: an affine permutation arc cannot have $N=p$ for odd $p$.
4. Theorem 3 can be presented more economically: a diagonal residue line has at most $4m$ occupied residues, whereas the chosen “long” threshold is $8(m+1)$. Thus no long residues occur with these parameters. Removing the vacuous branches would clarify the argument without changing its validity. The abstract would likewise benefit from emphasizing the main bounds and scope rather than listing every certificate statistic.
