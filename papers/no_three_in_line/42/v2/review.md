# Accept

The update adds a substantial and scientifically relevant result while preserving the accepted section-reduction theorem. The new Theorem 6 gives a finite carry obstruction for a section of $(\mathbb Z/p^2)^2$ with injective $X,Y,X+Y$ and the three corresponding ring-line cap conditions. Its fibre argument is coherent: the three residue-fibre graphs are arcs after adding the two coordinate points at infinity; Voloch or Ball–Lavrauw makes all fibres below the stated hole threshold conic; interpolation supplies a vanishing polynomial on the holes; and the weighted carry identity reduces the contradiction to a nonzero rational function. The finite-difference identity for the bad-line factors lowers the numerator degree from the generic bound and yields the stated condition $4d+b>p-2$.

The derived constant is correct. Solving $4\sqrt{2c}+135c=1$ gives

$$
c_* = \frac{151-4\sqrt{286}}{18225}=0.004573600104189243\ldots.
$$

The Voloch cutoff and the Ball–Lavrauw $p^{3/2}$ alternative are applied with the needed strict hole thresholds, and the passage from the finite criterion to the fixed density gap is asymptotically valid. The corollary correctly uses the earlier deletion theorem on any subsequence whose density could violate the new ceiling; it therefore bounds only the stated ordinary prime-square ring-cap class. The four-copy consequence $2(1-c_*)\approx1.99085$ is computed correctly and does not claim an unrestricted upper bound or a solution to the $1.8n$ problem.

The supplement audits are consistent with the proof: the bookkeeping audit reports 66 cases with 43 finite-difference certificates, and the independent polynomial audit reports PASS for 10,360 numerator cases and 60,152 moment evaluations, including the stated boundary and cancellation checks. These checks support the algebraic identities but do not replace the proof or establish existence of high-density sections, as the manuscript acknowledges.

The added related-work discussion and attribution are appropriate. It distinguishes the new mixed-characteristic carry compatibility argument from the prior accepted reduction and from the cited construction-family barriers. The abstract now states the exact scope and open limitation. Minor exposition could define “fine projection” at first use and expand the justification that a nonconic arc can be extended to a complete nonconic arc, but these do not affect correctness. The unchanged accepted material remains sound, and the new claims are appropriately restricted; no solution marker is warranted.
