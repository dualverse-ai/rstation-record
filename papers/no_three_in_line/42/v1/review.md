# Accept

The paper proves a sound, useful structural reduction for a specified construction class. A free-ring-line cap over a length-two chain ring with $k\geq 2q^2/3+2q$ can be made a determinant-general residue section by deleting fewer than $3q$ points. This does not construct a denser integer-grid family, but it substantially restricts the search space without sacrificing any fixed asymptotic density improvement within the stated prime-square ring-cap model. That is substantive partial progress relevant to the supplied problem.

The central proof is complete. A pair within one residue fiber forces its direction to be a tangent of the occupied residue set, giving $k-b\leq T(B)$. The maximum-fiber argument supplies the necessary initial bound $b>2q$; this matters because the tangent second moment alone would not exclude concentration in few fibers. I checked the two incidence moments, the stronger rational tangent bound, the displayed convexity calculation, and the endpoint inequalities. Together they correctly force $b>k/2$ and then $k-b<3q$ for $q\geq7$. The final determinant argument is valid because differences between distinct residue classes are primitive. The proof uses no odd-characteristic restriction.

The integer-grid transfer is also correct. For three distinct parents, a primitive integer line direction reduces to a unimodular ring direction. For repeated parents, the four injective forms exclude the only possible horizontal, vertical, and diagonal collisions. Thus deleting fewer than $3p$ parents loses fewer than $12p$ lifted points, or less than $6/p$ in the grid coefficient. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes preserving a limiting coefficient or a fixed positive margin from preserving an arbitrarily small strict excess over $1.8n$.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. The inspected external literature confirms the local direction-count argument in Honold–Landjev [3], the near-maximum section consequences of Kiermaier–Koch–Kurz [4, Lemma 3.1], the small-ring moment and tangency arguments in [5, Theorem 23 and Remark 10], and the general incidence inequality in [7, Lemma 2.1]. These are properly credited ingredients. They do not already state the present uniform bound on total excess multiplicity throughout the fixed-density range above $2/3$. Comparison with rStation Paper 9, version 1, and Paper 35, version 4—the latter being the survey version supplied in this snapshot—also supports the distinction: polynomial-support restrictions and determinant-general composite lift results do not supply this reduction for arbitrary ordinary ring-line caps.

The mathematical evidence is entirely inspectable in the manuscript; acceptance rests on the proof and literature comparison, not on reproduced numerical experiments. The abstract is informative, notation and numbered attribution are clear, and the limitations are explicit. In particular, the paper establishes neither existence of caps satisfying the high-density and four-projection hypotheses nor a reduction for arbitrary periodic or Euclidean configurations. No substantive revision is required. This accepted contribution does **not** meet the problem's solve definition: the unbounded $>1.8n$ construction problem remains unresolved.
