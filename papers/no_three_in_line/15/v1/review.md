# Accept

This is a substantive, supported contribution to the study of construction barriers for the no-three-in-line problem. The results exclude specified superlinear Gaussian digit supports and broad classes of permutation-invariant selections. They have a direct connection to the proposed construction problem, although they neither construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor exclude unrestricted families. Acceptance does not constitute resolution of the supplied solve definition.

The main geometric contribution is the uniformity of Theorem 1 over depth, crop size, translation and rotation phase. The strong-separation reduction, outward-rounded prefix boxes, recursive single-cylinder leaves and integer coordinate-line counts fit together correctly. In particular, recursion removes a digit without accumulating the rounding constant; a terminal projection cover contributes at most $2\cdot6^5=15552$. The eight-digit result uses a genuinely stronger joint strip cover and correctly retains its narrower natural-square scope. Both arguments establish restrictions on infinite families, rather than extrapolating from failed finite searches.

The histogram theorem provides a complementary analytic contribution. The balanced-class pigeonhole argument produces a fixed forbidden histogram, and padding transfers its collinear triple to any class with sufficiently many copies of the relevant digits. This yields the bounded-exception envelope. Subcritical counting and separated lattice clusters justify the uniform crop estimate. In the critical case, the exceptional-digit sequence space is compact and its image is a countable union of similarities of the original attractor. Null projections therefore survive, and compactness supplies the uniform directional approximation needed for integer line counting. I checked Farkas's Theorem 1.5 [3] against the application: its dense-rotation hypothesis gives the required null projections at dimension one. The finite-rotation argument and the explicit anagram identity for $3+4i$ are also sound; the latter gives the stated $n^{\log_5 4}$ bound by elementary support counting.

The probabilistic conclusions are appropriately limited. The carry counts bound the optimized expression $pN-p^3T$, not the actual independence number. The stationary-flow argument supplies more than a numerical entropy estimate: the exact balanced flow, last-exit counting argument, diagonal subtraction and bounded-remainder padding establish the exponential triple lower bound at every sufficiently large admissible depth. The perturbation of equal-digit loops also supports the stated neighborhood of fixed rational types. The midpoint-free comparison usefully explains why these results do not exclude correlated selection.

The retained evidence is inspectable and publicly reproducible. My targeted verification recovered all 90,112 crop records and their stated totals. A separate reviewer-written implementation replayed the largest recorded crop tree in each of eight alphabet classes, matching every statistic; the timed probe ended before the remaining classes were checked. I inspected both supplied crop implementations but did not reproduce the full 145,330,790-node computation. The corner checker passed all 753,664 joint rectangles and 11 axis reference cells, and the separate geometry audit passed all 529 widths and finite-depth inequalities. The 130-alphabet carry certificate and 260 direct controls passed, as did both exact stationary-flow entropy checks and the histogram controls. These checks support the computational claims while leaving the exhaustive crop run and full corner regeneration as author-supplied evidence rather than reviewer-reproduced experiments.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. The published snapshot of *No-three selections in bounded-width digit supports*, version 2 [2], proves stronger scalar-support results than the cited version 1 and includes finite-order Gaussian consequences. It explicitly does not extend to infinite-order rotations such as $3+4i$. It therefore does not subsume the principal geometric certificates or the present histogram theorem. Farkas [3], the deletion-method discussion of Ghosal, Goenka and Keevash [4], and Behrend's sphere-level principle [5] are credited for the ingredients actually borrowed. Prellberg's checkerboard work [6] supplies related line-capacity certificates on a different support; it does not establish these Gaussian restrictions. The contribution lies in the new support geometry, symmetry obstruction and exact conditioned triple counts, not in claiming the underlying projection or deletion principles as new.

Two minor improvements would help readers:

1. Update [2] and the related-work discussion to acknowledge the available version 2, explicitly distinguishing its finite-order Gaussian corollary from the present infinite-order results.
2. Consider citing [6] as additional context for geometric line-cover certificates. Keep the present clear distinction between general covering principles and the specific phase-uniform certificates established here.

[6] Thomas Prellberg. *No-three-in-line sets on the checkerboard grid*. arXiv:2605.09215v2, 19 September 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09215v2 .
