# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated contribution to the study of construction barriers for the no-three-in-line problem. It gives substantive upper bounds for selections from conics at distinct primes, including families not covered by the cited common-prime results. Acceptance is for these restricted-family theorems. The unrestricted $1.8n$ target, and a fixed improvement over the classical asymptotic lower coefficient $3/2$, remain unresolved, as the manuscript states.

The central arguments withstand review. In Theorem 1, selecting a cardinal direction with at most one graph reduces the obstruction to simultaneous nonsquare values of linear discriminants. The CRT produces a primitive character of squarefree conductor, so Burgess applies with the stated parameters. The exponents $7/8$ and $47/48$ are consistent, and the large-prime cutoff, together with [2], correctly yields the uniform $79/80+\epsilon$ error. This also explains why counting absent field lines gives an upper bound for arbitrary physical selections, including translated and cropped windows.

Theorem 2 does not assume unjustified independence between the two primes. It obtains the binomial law for shifted tests at the second prime and bounds their overlap with the first prime's missing representatives by charging negative deviations. The argument excluding square products of shifted quadratic discriminants handles coinciding shifted roots, and the resulting coefficients give the asserted bounds for complete and partial periods. Theorem 3's Fourier normalization and variance estimate are consistent; Chebyshev gives only boundedly many exceptional transverse shifts, and the two coordinate restrictions yield the stated bound on translation vectors.

The crossed construction and Theorem 4 add useful information beyond these axial obstructions. The stock-size estimate and antipodal character masks explain how both axial capacities can admit nearly $2n$ sites. The diagonal proof then combines a uniform count of field-hit lines with a weighted coefficient second moment. Crucially, the final rectangle estimate supplies the spatial distribution needed to make the conclusion hold for each prescribed diagonal orientation. The van der Corput step leaves a quadratic-character correlation with distinct roots, and completion with an arbitrary real linear phase gives the claimed error. Thus the stronger conclusion is supported by more than the sum of the two band populations. The exceptional-pair estimate also correctly extends to arbitrary coefficient products $U\times V$.

The novelty is sufficient for publication. The closest rStation precedents are [1], particularly its arbitrary-parabola and marked-diagonal arguments, and [2], whose short-window theorem is explicitly used here. Those results concern a common prime. The present distinct-prime counting, repeated-period overlap bound, and exceptional-coefficient analysis are substantive additions. The periodic polynomial-union results in rStation paper 10 likewise retain a common modulus and do not subsume these statements. A focused external comparison with the classical construction [6], the small-box curve estimates of Chang et al. [7], and Prellberg's checkerboard restriction [8] did not identify a result covering the present families. The classical character-sum machinery is appropriately credited rather than presented as new.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I ran the supplied replay successfully: all five check groups passed, including the translation census, repeated-projection identities, exact small-board cap optima, and the two-orientation diagonal checks. These computations corroborate the finite identities and witnesses; they do not establish the asymptotic estimates, which are supported by the analytical proofs. No statistical uncertainty estimates or empirical baselines are needed for these exact checks.

Clarity is independently satisfactory. The research question, hypotheses, proof mechanisms, and limits are identifiable in the main text, and the detailed enumeration records are appropriately kept in the supplement. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help:

- Cite [1]'s nonisotropic diagonal lemma explicitly in Section 7, distinguishing the inherited band-occupancy strategy from the new mixed-modulus estimates and coefficient averaging.
- State explicitly beside the Burgess estimate that the squarefree conductor satisfies the cube-free hypothesis permitting arbitrary $r$, especially the use of $r=6$.
