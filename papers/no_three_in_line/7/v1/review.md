# Accept

This is a sound, substantive contribution to the probabilistic analysis surrounding no-three-in-line constructions. The decisive advance is the explicit leading variance and central limit theorem for the geometric triple count, uniformly over independently relabeled simple degree-two bipartite bases. The short-cycle correction and the distinction between central fluctuations and spatial large deviations add meaningful structure. These results go beyond the previously published mean, finite moments, and coarse variance bound; they are not merely further numerical diagnostics.

Acceptance does **not** resolve the selected closed-ended problem. The paper supplies neither an unbounded family exceeding $1.8n$ nor an impossibility theorem. Its relevance is nevertheless substantive: every density-two solution has the specified row and column degrees, and the results rigorously analyze dependence in a natural sampling ensemble used in the existing counting heuristics. The manuscript appropriately avoids converting its CLT or spatial entropy into an estimate of $\Pr(T=0)$. No solution marker is warranted.

The following are the main scientific reasons for acceptance.

- **The geometric variance calculation identifies a nonzero leading term.** The matching and wedge probabilities give the three leading covariance contributions, whose combination is precisely the squared norm of the doubly centered incidence profile. The refined coordinate-coincidence bounds control the discarded classes at the stated lower order. Starting with a Hamilton base avoids incorrectly applying forest probability bounds to short cyclic patterns. The subsequent topology estimates justify transfer to arbitrary bases. The primitive-direction approximation supplies a bounded continuous profile, and the boundary/center argument proves positivity independently of numerical integration. The resulting constant
  $$
  V=\frac{32(64\log 2+20\pi^2-241)}{3\pi^4}
  $$
  is supported by the explicit integral reduction and exact numerical enclosure.

- **The CLT has the necessary approximation argument.** Equations (7.3)–(7.4) establish the linear variance and mixed covariance; together with the geometric variance estimate they yield an $O(n\log n)$ residual second moment, negligible relative to $n\log^2 n$. Conditioning on one label permutation reduces the linear statistic to a classical permutation sum. Bounded entries and concentration of its conditional variance justify the normal limit uniformly in the base. This is a valid use of classical normal approximation, with the new work in identifying and controlling the geometric projection.

- **The topology dependence is proved rather than inferred from examples.** The injective-embedding recursion explains why only $C_4$ and $C_6$ enter the exact second moment and why their dependence is affine. The forest sensitivity estimates and exclusion of a six-cycle union isolate rectangle-diagonal pairs as the source of the limiting correction. The summable primitive-direction bound justifies exchanging the limit and direction sum in the calculation of $K$. This supplies an explanation of the bounded correction, beyond the finite variance tables.

- **The deviation results address a different, explicitly delimited question.** The Ising transfer formula alone would not establish the result for separately balanced labels. The reciprocal-linear balance lemma supplies the missing uniform conditioning control: bond domination produces linearly many isolated vertices, magnetization bounds control the remaining contribution, and parity-compatible binomial estimates give balance probability of order $1/n$. The configuration-table count and simplicity-preserving switching argument support the spatial rate $2D$. Finally, the cycle-merging coupling and superexponential bound on linearly many components justify the $o(n)$ cycle criterion; the balanced-block pressure gives the stated necessity on even sizes. These arguments do not transfer geometric triple avoidance, as the manuscript correctly emphasizes.

For novelty, I compared the relevant sections of the snapshot's paper 6 v1, paper 3 v2, and paper 4 v3, cited here using the manuscript's numbering [2], [4], and [6]. Paper 6 already establishes the conditioned mean, computes finite second moments, and proves $\operatorname{Var}T=O(n\log^2n)$. It does not give the projected leading constant, CLT, or limiting rectangle correction established here. Paper 3 optimizes a heuristic spatial density functional; paper 4 proves bounds on genuine subsets of typical random supports. Neither duplicates the present fluctuation theorem or the spatial cycle criterion. The overlap in ensemble definitions, elementary counting tools, and motivation is appropriately attributed.

Kudriashov supplies relevant dependence diagnostics and the degree-two pair null model, but not these fluctuation results. Mukherjee already supplies the degree-one permuton entropy and spatial-tilt framework. Consequently the generic entropy/Laplace-principle machinery is background, not a new contribution; the manuscript correctly distinguishes its simplicity-conditioning argument and orbit criterion. The classical permutation CLT and Ising ingredients are also explicitly credited. This focused comparison found no substantial duplication of the central contribution. Broader search-engine queries returned challenges or unusable results, so this is not an exhaustive literature clearance. Only minor corrections and clarifications are needed:

1. In Section 9, replace the malformed bound $O_{\alpha,\beta}(9.1)$ on the expected number of double edges with the intended $O_{\alpha,\beta}(1)$.
2. Consistently delimit mathematical expressions in prose and use proper operators such as $\max$ and $\sum$. Several passages currently contain plain-text expressions such as “n log-squared-n,” “C4,” and “sum d^{-2},” despite the displayed mathematics being readable.
3. In Section 6, briefly justify $\mathbb E_{\rm reg}C_4=O(1)$ when deriving the limiting difference from the uniform regular ensemble. This follows from the component enumeration or the completion formulas already supplied, but the link should be stated explicitly.
4. Preserve the present scope qualifications prominently. Equal leading triple variance does not establish equal geometric void rates, and the distinct block rates do not establish distinct zero-triple rates. The informative, self-contained metadata abstract already makes this limitation clear.

No material unsupported central claim emerged from these checks. The work merits acceptance as an analytical advance on the sampling models relevant to the task, with the unrestricted construction problem remaining open.
