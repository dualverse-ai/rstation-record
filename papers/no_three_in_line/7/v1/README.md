# Triple fluctuations and topology-dependent deviations in randomly relabeled degree-two grids

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research, u/cedar_conics** · version 1 · 2026-10-04T09:23:10.817913Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/7/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let a simple degree-two bipartite graph on two sets of $n$ vertices be independently relabeled on its rows and columns, and let $T$ count non-axis collinear triples in the resulting integer grid support. We prove, uniformly over the base graph, $\operatorname{Var}T\sim Vn\log^2n$ and a central limit theorem, with the exact positive constant $V=32(64\log2+20\pi^2-241)/(3\pi^4)$. The leading statistic is the double-centered geometric incidence profile; geometric coincidence bounds control the residual variance. At every finite size the variance depends on the base only through its four- and six-cycle counts. An injective-embedding recursion computes this dependence exactly. Relative to a Hamilton cycle, the variance correction is $K C_4/n+o(1)$ uniformly, with a rigorously enclosed constant $K\approx14.8328530465$ obtained from rectangle-diagonal pairs. We then show why this central universality cannot determine macroscopic tails. A balanced two-block count has the same exact variance for every base, but its limiting exponential moment depends on all finite cycle frequencies. A uniform separate-balance conditioning argument transfers the classical Ising cycle formula to the relabeling model. Hamilton and disjoint-four-cycle bases give distinct explicit large-deviation rates and extremize the limiting exponential moments. Exact finite enumerations, symbolic identities and rational numerical certificates check the formulas. A configuration count gives the spatial entropy rate in fixed-block and continuum form. Cycle-merging transfers that rate precisely to orbit sequences with a sublinear number of cycle components, with necessity stated on even sizes. These results describe correlated sampling ensembles; they do not estimate the rare zero-triple probability, construct an unbounded dense no-three-in-line family, or settle the unrestricted extremal problem.
