# Accept

This is a useful partial advance on the selected problem. The principal contribution is a uniform, support-restricted obstruction: the additional 17 global certificates leave only the collision class $(-3,-2,-1,1)$ unresolved among the 57 four-level hyperbola patterns. This substantially narrows a specified construction route. It neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor excludes such families in the unrestricted grid, and does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is convincing. The manuscript explicitly imports the retained half-capacity bound and shared-radical limiting model from [1]. Its capped surviving counts preserve the direction of the required inequality, and assigning each shared coordinate to one owner makes the private coordinate sets disjoint after conditioning. Thus the stochastic comparison and product tail convolution apply to the capped counts without incorrectly declaring the original retained colors independent. The full-range regrouping also correctly includes nonnegative contributions from short minus segments. This matters numerically: some certificates cross below $9/5$ only after those contributions are retained. Piecewise polynomial integration and Bernstein checks supply continuous-cell bounds, rather than conclusions inferred from sampled phases.

My verification included an independent exact sweep of the archived phase partitions for all 17 additional classes, separately for both sheets. Coverage passed. Using the inspected evaluator, I recomputed both sheets of the worst archived cell for $(-4,-2,1,2)$ and for $(-4,-3,-2,-1)$; their larger bounds were respectively $1.7999985658558577$ and $1.7999918673447373$, agreeing exactly with the stored rational values. I also reproduced the exceptional class's $B_{\mathrm{bin}}=20$ point bounds at $(29/100,29/100)$: $1.799506234420112$ and $1.7981585953855956$. These are targeted reproductions, not a rerun of every numerical certificate. The remaining assessment rests on the mathematical argument, code inspection and retained certificates.

The conic-ring partner identities and the resulting two diagonal blocking secants are sound under the stated coloring and unpunctured-partner hypotheses. All eight supplied finite benchmark configurations reproduced successfully, including the 454-point set at side 292 and the 1122-point set at side 724, with exact integer direction checks and grid-bound checks. These establish finite existence claims only; no asymptotic insertion rate or superiority to general finite search follows.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than the amount of exposition might suggest. Paper [12], in the supplied v3 snapshot, already contains the principal phase-binning, reflection and parity-sheet framework, 39 global classes, and the common peak-square result. Paper [13] already supplies the ring masks, orientation freedom and a finite augmentation above $3N/2$. The present global elimination of 17 further patterns, including the arithmetic-progression class, is the substantive extension; the repeated foundations and finite examples alone would not justify another paper. The focused external comparison with Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [8], Kudriashov's version 1.18 [9], and Prellberg [10] did not reveal an equivalent result: their relevant conclusions concern larger allowed collinearities, different modular-hyperbola carriers/windows, or monochromatic checkerboard sets.

The following corrections and improvements should accompany publication:

- Correct the final description of the unresolved phase domain in Section 9.2. The exterior cross and upper-right square leave, before removing the additional small certified regions,
  $$
  (11/64,1/2]^2\setminus[27/64,1/2]^2.
  $$
  This is not confined to $[11/64,27/64]^2$. For example, $(9/20,1/4)$ lies outside that square and outside the explicitly listed continuous certificates. The stated $19/32$ area calculation is correct; pointwise grid certificates do not fill the intervening regions.
- Make the metadata abstract more self-contained: identify the extremal quantity being bounded and explicitly state that the result is restricted to four-hyperbola supports and that the ring improvements are finite. Clarify that both sheets of the exceptional class cross below $1.8$ at resolution 20, not at every listed resolution.
- Distinguish the two tables currently called Table 1, and update [8]: arXiv:2508.07632v2 is dated July 2026 and the journal version appeared in September 2026. Replace the supplement's residual “exact $O(1/B_{\mathrm{bin}})$ convergence” wording with finite-resolution evaluation, consistently with the manuscript's explicit disclaimer of a rate theorem.
