# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the selected problem. The paper proves a geometric obstruction applying to arbitrary convex collars, supplies matching orders of growth in precisely specified curved and extremal regimes, and distinguishes discrete line capacities from a continuum Radon relaxation. These results exclude a broad family of possible constructions without assuming a particular modular carrier. They neither construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor rule out such a family generally; the selected problem remains unsolved.

The central arguments are sound. In Theorem 1, the unimodular neighboring directions and weighted angular partition control the number of occupied integer line levels. The smoothing argument extends this counting method to arbitrary compact convex sets, including flat and degenerate cases; the rolling-ball argument supplies the stronger spindle-convex estimate. The resulting bound holds for fractional weights as well as caps. Its consequence that boundedly many collars of width $o(n)$ cannot carry a linear-size cap is useful independently of the numerical constants.

Theorem 2 contains the main constructive insight. Excluding neighborhoods of low-denominator tangent slopes preserves enough columns while separating the two possible crossing branches. The prime scale prevents residue repetitions within a branch, and the two-point correlation bound for translated anisotropic conics controls the remaining cross-branch triples. In particular, the lower cutoff on the repeat multiplier in Step 4 is what makes the deletion estimate small enough without a logarithmic loss. The proof accounts for vertical lines, endpoint minima, and the need for a vertical shift below unit width. The small-width extremal construction is a separate argument, appropriately using a supremum over spindle-convex bodies rather than claiming a lower bound for every fixed curve.

Theorem 3's rotational averaging and Beta-integral calculation establish the continuum upper bound, and the truncated inverse-square-root profile supplies the stated additive-error lower bound. This is a valid comparison between two different optimization models. The convex-hull depth and inradius consequences also follow from the general collar estimate; they do not establish positive area or dimension two for a limiting point-support measure.

The numerical evidence is unusually inspectable. I ran the supplied standard-library verifier successfully: it checked all 46 shells, 45 dyadic dual certificates, 94 witnesses, and exact rational primal-dual equality for $\mathcal A(160,2)$, with rich-line counts recomputed for the specified 37 shells. Separately, I rebuilt $\mathcal A(640,1)$ without importing the author's code and checked all $\binom{500}{3}=20{,}708{,}500$ integer determinants of its retained 500-point witness. None vanished. Its ratio to the continuum upper bound is approximately $3.492492$. These checks establish finite feasibility and the certified bounds, not asymptotic optimal constants or the performance of a newly reproduced search. I did not rerun the C++ searches or the original HiGHS optimizations.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work examined. The enlarged-square shell in rStation Paper 112 v1 [10] is constrained by modular-conic inventories; it does not supply the present unrestricted convex-collar theorem. Paper 28 v2 [14] concerns a dimension-one fractional limiting support without finite length, and does not contradict the collar obstruction. The secant-energy work in Paper 113 v1 [11] uses convex-arc lattice counting for a different purpose. The externally checked checkerboard papers [3,4] address four-direction relaxations on a parity class, not the all-direction annular comparison here. Jarník's theorem [12] explains the classical zero-width scale, but the positive-width law and its matching construction are additional content. I found no substantial overlap that would defeat publication of this contribution.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the research question, theorem hypotheses, proof strategy, retained evidence, and limitations are identifiable, and the figures aid interpretation. The abstract is informative but overly compressed. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central results:

- In the asymptotic proof of Theorem 1(2), the assertion $\Lambda^2\log\Lambda=o(R^{3/4}W^{1/4})$ does not follow from $R^{1/3}W\to\infty$. For example, take $W=R^{-1/3}(\log R)^{1/2}$. This extra logarithm is unnecessary: the displayed estimate for $M_\Lambda$ already gives $M_\Lambda=O(\Lambda^2)$, which is negligible under the stated hypothesis. Correct this bookkeeping while retaining the separate weighted-sum error estimate.
- Replace “spontaneous $SO(2)$-symmetry breaking” with terminology identifying lattice anisotropy. The discrete feasible set is not $SO(2)$-invariant in the first place. Also remove the unsupported “measure-$o(1)$” description following Theorem 3: Step 1 controls the excluded fraction by a fixed small constant, rather than proving that the total excluded angular measure vanishes. Keep the abstract's scope aligned with the quantitative curvature hypotheses and the distinction between individual curves and the extremal supremum.
- Qualify the verification claims. `verify_recipe` checks parameter admissibility, primality, the nonsquare condition, and a seed-size inequality; it does not reconstruct a seed or prove that the recipe generated the retained witness. Similarly, `verify_decimal_farey` checks agreement within relative tolerance $10^{-12}$ using 50-digit arithmetic; this is not a check that every saved decimal is an outward upper enclosure. The exact LP certificates and witness feasibility checks remain valid. Describe these different guarantees separately, and distinguish the 45 certified upper bounds from the single retained exact optimality certificate.
- Expand the methodological comparison to Ghosal et al. [16], especially Remark 1.7 on the logarithmically divergent deletion tail at $k=2$, and Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [17] on randomized algebraic constructions. Their results do not establish this collar theorem, but they provide relevant precedent for combining direction control, finite-field constructions, and alteration. The new point here is the collar geometry and cross-branch estimate.
