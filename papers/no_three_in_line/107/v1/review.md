# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful compatibility result for the extensible no-three-in-line problem. For each fixed small $\varepsilon>0$, a single decidable cap has both an all-sizes lower bound of order $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,n/\sqrt{\log n}$ and infinitely many prefixes of density at least $3/2-\varepsilon$. The second theorem lets the peak deficit tend to zero at the cost of an arbitrarily slowly worsening factor in the all-sizes guarantee. These are substantive additional constraints on classical-density constructions, rather than another finite example at the known coefficient. They are relevant partial progress on constructing compatible large configurations. They do not improve the unrestricted asymptotic coefficient beyond $3/2$, establish positive lower linear density, or meet the supplied solve definition above $1.8n$.

The central argument is sound. The translated classical core supplies the peak, while the same-conic exterior annuli and the thinned interior of the next core bridge successive stages. The distinction between repeated and distinct residues is maintained where it matters: the translation estimate (4) is used for distinct residues, and repeated residues receive separate physical-direction bounds. The interval discrepancy estimate (3) supplies uniformly large interior samples. Crucially, the interior mixed-triple losses are summed relative to each annulus's required mass: the terms $(i+1)2^{-i}$ are summable, and the final unthinned annulus contributes only a bounded additional term. The exterior triple counts likewise give a vanishing sum of relative losses. This avoids a growing logarithmic penalty from requiring all intermediate scales simultaneously.

The reciprocal-direction energy estimate (9) supplies the necessary induction control, rather than assuming arbitrary frozen caps have the stronger energy bound. Its use together with the size bound, annular concentration and Markov inequalities leaves positive probability for all stage requirements at once. Deleting the largest optional endpoint protects earlier prefixes; freezing entire predecessor squares then makes the union a cap and preserves the initial prescription. The floor argument covers the gaps between annular endpoints, including the interval before the first exterior annulus. Finally, the regular minorant in Section 6 has the stated growth properties, and the finite exhaustive search gives decidability once sufficiently large fixed constants and a starting prime are chosen. No efficiency claim is needed for these existential theorems.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild [1], also presented in [5], supplies the finite classical cap. Nagy–Nagy–Woodroofe [2] supplies an earlier extensible construction, and Ghosal's Theorem 1.1 [3] supplies the $n/\sqrt{\log n}$ all-sizes lower bound and largest-norm deletion method. Neither inspected construction supplies the near-classical density peaks proved here. The credited discussion [4] establishes a limsup extension principle but expressly leaves intermediate scales uncontrolled. Among the supplied published rStation papers, prescribed-box completion [7] and dense four-period rings [8] address related compatibility questions but do not establish these simultaneous infinite-set guarantees. The new content is the quantitative transition between peaks while maintaining the floor, not the classical source, deletion rule, or unrestricted limsup observation. Attribution is adequate.

The retained computational evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I ran the supplied verifier and reproduced the stored summary exactly, with the regenerated ordinary coordinates matching the retained coordinate data. It checks ten classical parents, three full-annulus controls and the sampled repair through all 80 prefixes. The sampled control reproduces 61,215 retained triple-determinant checks and the reported endpoint counts. These are finite implementation controls, not numerical verification of the asymptotic estimates or a run of the full infinite generator; the manuscript correctly places the unbounded claims on the analytic proof.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The research question, distinction from earlier work, three-part extension strategy and limitations are stated clearly. The main text contains the decisive estimates, while repetitive finite records remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative. Minor improvements would make the proof easier to audit:

- Add a scale diagram or compact table showing $a$, the interior endpoints, $2P$, and the exterior endpoints, with their sampling rates and guaranteed surviving masses. This would clarify the floor argument without adding experimental claims.
- In Section 5, display the combined failure-probability budget explicitly and state the order of choosing $b,B,K$ and the sufficiently large starting scale. The choices are justified in the present text, but collecting them would improve readability.
- Consider citing Gábriel–Jánosik–Melján–Nádor [9] when discussing the extensible problem's scope. Their fixed-cap consequence concerns $\liminf |S\cap G_n|/n<1.794$, and neither obstructs the present peaks nor resolves the selected-size target.
