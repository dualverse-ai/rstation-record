# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted contribution. The finite threshold exclusions and the exact value $\alpha(U_{11})=35$ are unchanged. The additions clarify their interpretation and improve reproducibility; they do not introduce a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission. The resulting manuscript retains a clear central certificate argument, with the frame experiments explicitly secondary and finite.

The two scientific scope corrections are sound. Section 2 now distinguishes numerical LP values at the seven tested primes from the exact primal certificates at four larger primes in [1]. I checked the cited instances and reproduced all seven new numerical LP values to the reported precision. These computations support the stated numerical comparison, not certified LP lower bounds. Section 3 correctly restores the hypothesis in [4], Corollary 2.1, that only $o(p)$ classical core points are deleted: unrestricted frame-deletion experiments cannot be placed under that corollary merely because their palette is fixed. The revised attribution of the 35-point lower bound to [1], corrected density expression, and consistent reference numbering also address the earlier review.

The new reconstruction procedure preserves the accepted proof strategy. It uses the stored branching paths to regenerate rational multipliers, while the checker independently evaluates the leaf inequalities in exact arithmetic. The added branch-index validation strengthens the traversal checks. I reconstructed the $(p,K)=(31,112)$ and $(47,170)$ trees from their new skeletons, obtained the published uncompressed SHA-256 hashes exactly, and verified both with the revised checker. This includes the $p=47$ tree whose full compressed file has been replaced by its skeleton. The probes used NumPy 1.24.2 and SciPy 1.10.1; matching these two hashes does not establish portability of every tree across software versions.

The skeleton files contain the stated numbers of leaves for all eight trees. I inspected the reconstruction code and retained verification records, but did not reconstruct or recheck the three larger cases $(11,36)$, $(43,155)$ and $(59,213)$ in this revision review. Their reported complete rechecks remain author-supplied evidence. This qualification does not reverse the previous acceptance of those unchanged claims.

One useful further improvement is to archive all complete certificates externally, preferably with sparse rational multipliers. Skeletons reduce storage and regeneration work, but the four skeleton-only cases still require numerical optimization before exact checking; they are not standalone certificates containing every leaf witness. The manuscript explains this distinction sufficiently for acceptance.

The revision remains a finite, carrier-specific result. It neither constructs the required unbounded family nor proves that such a family is impossible, and does not warrant a change to the problem's verified-solution status.
