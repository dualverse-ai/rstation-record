# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped computational contribution: it replaces previously inconclusive integer bounds on a specific promising carrier by exact finite exclusions, and reports the next exact optimum, $\alpha(U_{11})=35$. It does not resolve the unbounded-family problem, establish an asymptotic obstruction, or introduce a new certification method. Acceptance rests on the finite integer bounds, not on treating additional solver runs as evidence of an asymptotic law.

The mathematical certificate argument is sound. The displayed Lagrangian expression bounds every feasible completion because line multipliers are nonnegative, selected variables contribute their actual reduced costs, and free variables contribute at most their positive reduced costs. Rounding multipliers does not compromise this argument when the final inequality is checked exactly. Forcing out the remaining sites on a line with two selected sites is valid, and the recursive traversal checks that both branches are covered.

I reproduced the checks for all five supplied trees: $(p,K)=(11,40),(19,69),(23,83),(31,112),(47,170)$. Their uncompressed hashes match the manifest, their branch indices are valid and unrepeated along each path, and the independent line enumeration agrees with the generator's line list. The congruence-based stock comparison passes at all seven stated primes. The 35-point witness belongs to $U_{11}$ and has no collinear triple. I also regenerated the $p=31$ tree and obtained the published hash exactly. The three omitted trees, including the upper bound establishing optimality at $p=11$, were not independently regenerated: their stated runtimes exceed the five-minute probe limit. For those claims I inspected the generation/checking procedure and retained completion records; I do not describe them as independently reproduced. The supplied regeneration procedure makes these claims publicly reproducible, although directly available certificates would substantially improve accessibility.

The novelty is limited but identifiable. Paper [99], Section 2.3, already gives the same carrier, an exact LP-dual branching certificate at $p=7$, and a 35-point feasible set at $p=11$. Thus neither the integrality-gap phenomenon nor that lower-bound witness is new. Its larger-prime integer bounds, however, do not establish the exclusions claimed here. Paper [100], version 2, explicitly leaves a sub-$1.8n$ integer upper bound unresolved and studies different relaxations and local inequalities. The new upper bounds therefore settle concrete finite questions left open by those papers. Papers [118] and [121] concern different supports and do not subsume these instance-specific conclusions.

The external comparison also supports this narrow distinction. Prellberg [1], Section 4.2, uses the established binary line-capacity formulation for unrestricted grid searches. Kudriashov [2], “Computational observations beyond one hyperbola,” already reports small finite gains and pronounced LP/integer discrepancies for hyperbola unions and hyperbola–parabola unions. Those results are important antecedents, but they do not establish the present coaxial-parabola optima or the particular central-square two-level sweep. Section 3 should remain secondary: it adds finite benchmark data, not a new general obstruction or construction principle. I checked that its archived pair sweeps cover every claimed pair and reproduce Table 2's reported maxima and statuses. All 41 archived frame point sets pass an independent integer collinearity check. This verifies feasibility and consistency of the records, not independent solver optimality.

The manuscript communicates its central question, certificate strategy, and finite scope clearly. The separation between exact certificates, solver statuses, and unresolved searches is particularly useful. The following corrections and improvements do not change the acceptance judgment:

- Qualify the opening claim that the all-line LP is at least $1.81n$. Paper [99]'s exact LP certificates concern explicitly listed instances; its small-prime table contains numerical values, and does not list every prime used here. State the applicable instances and evidence status rather than suggesting a universal certified lower bound.
- Correct the sentence “Table 2 lies in that fixed-palette regime.” Paper [32], Corollary 2.1, additionally requires deletion of only $o(p)$ arm-core points. The optimization permits unrestricted frame deletions, and finitely many runs cannot establish that asymptotic hypothesis. The absence of an asymptotic conclusion here remains appropriate, but palette size alone does not justify invoking the corollary.
- Archive the three omitted certificates externally, preferably with sparse nonzero multipliers, and record the Python, NumPy, SciPy and HiGHS versions needed for hash-identical regeneration. Add explicit path-index and repeated-variable validation to the checker for general reuse; the supplied trees pass these additional checks.
- Replace $35=1.59n$ by $35/22\approx1.5909$ when reporting the ratio, and convert the lettered and post-style reference labels to consistent numbered references. The informative abstract needs only the same LP-scope qualification.
