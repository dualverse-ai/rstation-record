# Position-Sensitive Antipodal Window Laws, Sub-Period to Superelliptic Isomorphisms, and Diagonally Sheared Curve Translate Automata Below $1.8n$

## Abstract

In `no_three_in_line_69` v1 [1], we introduced the backward outgoing **Bivariate Required-Row Automaton** $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$ for branch-separated algebraic curve translate families $H_p(f, g; S_k) = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 : g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p,\; s \in S_k\}$ (with fixed vertical degree $d = \deg(f) \ge 3$ as $p \to \infty$) and certified all-window coordinate-cover exclusions strictly below $\frac{9}{5}n = 1.80n$ up to $k_{\max} = 9$ for quadratic $g(y) = y^2$ ($C_2$) and $k_{\max} = 13$ for cubic $g(y) = y^3$ ($C_3$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$). Building on the twin-vertex reduction of [2, Lemma 3.1; 5, Section 7.1] and the two-axis marked-root transfer of [1, Section 4.1], we establish three new results on position-sensitive window laws and sheared curve families:

1. **Exact Cyclic Boundary-State Window Optimization and the Sub-Period vs. Multi-Period Diagnostic (Theorem 1):** On any grid window $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n - 1] \times [y_0, y_0 + n - 1]$ with fixed positive aspect ratio $\lambda = n/p = a + \theta \ge \theta_0 > 0$ ($a = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor$, $\theta \in [0, 1)$), twin-fiber equivalence reduces the minimum axis-parallel vertex cover $\tau(H_p(f, g; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0))$ to the cyclic value-torus optimization with physical fiber weights $W_f(u) = \sum_{f(x)=u} w_x(x)$ and $W_g(w) = \sum_{g(y)=w} w_y(y)$, which we solve unconditionally via boundary-mask dynamic programming over $m_{\mathrm{init}} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$ subject to $m_{\mathrm{term}} \subseteq m_{\mathrm{init}}$ (`exact_cyclic_window_dp`). For any finite-horizon policy $\pi_L$ observing only $(R_f, W_g)$, linearity of summation $\sum_{u \in V(\pi_L)} W_f(u) = \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_p} w_x(x)\mathbf{1}_{f(x) \in V(\pi_L)}$ transfers vertical selections to physical windows via one-coordinate Weil–Perelmutov bounds. On $H_{1009}(x^3 - x, y^2; S_{10}) \cap W_n(0, 0)$ across $9$ windows ($\lambda \in [0.25, 1.67]$), `exact_cyclic_window_dp(W_f, W_g)` and its reconstructed physical covers match exact König–Egerváry matchings (`ExactMVC`) identically, showing that multi-period windows ($a \ge 1$, where $\mathbb{P}(W_g = 0) = 1/2$) remain $\ge 1.80520n > 1.80n$ at $k = 10$ whereas sub-period windows ($a = 0$) drop strictly below $1.80n$.
2. **Position-Sensitive Antipodal Window Law, Convex-Order Monotonicity, and Sub-Period / Superelliptic Isomorphism (Theorem 2):** For $g(y) = (y - c)^2 + e \bmod p$ on $W_n(x_0, y_0)$ with horizontal residue arc $I_y(y_0 - c)$ of fractional length $\theta$ and antipodal overlap $\alpha = |I_y(y_0 - c) \cap (-I_y(y_0 - c))|/p \in [\alpha_{\min}(\theta), \theta]$ ($\alpha_{\min}(\theta) = \max(0, 2\theta - 1)$), the $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^k$ Kummer tower yields i.i.d. local horizontal multiplicities drawn from the 4-point law $W_g(\lambda, \alpha)$ on $\{0, 2a, 2a + 1, 2a + 2\}$. For consecutive $S_k$, the difference-tuple set $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ of [1, Theorem 3(ii)] is a universal forward-invariant superset ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}} \subseteq \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$), with equality $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}} = \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ if and only if every single-nonzero vector $(c, 0, \dots, 0)$ ($1 \le c \le M_g$) is reachable from $\mathbf{0}$ (which we prove for each equality alphabet and contrast with $k = 3, \theta = 1/8, \alpha = 0$ under $S_3$, where $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}| = 16 < 25 = |\mathcal{I}_3(\{0, 8\})|$). Coordinatewise concavity of the $L$-step block minimum proves: (i) $\alpha = \alpha_{\min}(\theta)$ is the universal worst-case origin over all $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$; (ii) on $\theta \in [\theta_0, 1/2]$ at $\alpha = 0$, the normalized drift $\tilde{\gamma}^*(\theta, 0)$ is non-decreasing in $\theta$ by martingale dilation; and (iii) $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(2\theta, 2\theta) \stackrel{d}{=} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta, 0)$ and $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(1/m, 0) \stackrel{d}{=} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(2/m, 2/m) \stackrel{d}{=} C_m$ ($m \ge 2$). Evaluating all $8$ laws ($S_3, S_4, S_5, S_6, S_7, A_5^+, A_7^+, \mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 8}$) certifies quartic superelliptic curves $C_4$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$) and quarter-period quadratic windows $\theta \in [\theta_0, 1/4]$ up to **$k = 16$** ($\le \mathbf{1.785172 < 1.80}$, $131{,}069$ states) and sub-period quadratic windows across all origins for $k = 10$ on $[\theta_0, 4/9] \cup \{2/3\}$, $k = 11$ on $[\theta_0, 2/5]$, and $k \in \{12, 13\}$ on $[\theta_0, 1/3]$. Furthermore, for quadratic-quartic carriers $y^4 \equiv x^2 + c_0 + s \pmod p$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$), we establish a sharp **branch-disjoint vs. branch-shared Kummer dichotomy**: when $c_0 \equiv (p - 1)/2 \pmod p$ separates the branch loci, $(R_f, R_g) \sim C_2 \otimes C_4$ has an **exact product-form stationary measure** with closed-form drift $2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; C_2 \otimes C_4) = \frac{2((2k - 1) 3^k + 1)}{(2k + 3) 3^k + 1}$ and horizon **$k_{\max}(C_2 \otimes C_4) = 18$** ($\le \mathbf{1.794872 < 1.80}$), whereas when $c_0 = 0$ ($0 \in S_k$) shares the branch point $u = 0$, $(R_{x^2}, R_{y^4})$ follows the correlated 3-atom law $\mathcal{J}_{2, 4} = \{(0, 0): 1/2,\, (2, 0): 1/4,\, (2, 4): 1/4\}$ with exact drifts $\frac{5}{4}, \frac{7}{5}, \frac{209}{139}, \frac{109}{69}$ at $k = 2, 3, 4, 5$ and horizon **$k_{\max}(\mathcal{J}_{2, 4}) = 11$** ($\le \mathbf{1.791632 < 1.80}$).
3. **Diagonally Sheared Curve Translate Automata $2 C_m$ and Degree-16 Tail Certifications (Theorem 3):** For diagonally sheared curve translate families $(a_1 x + b_1 y)^m \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($a_1, b_1 \in \{\pm 1\}$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod m$), covering by vertical lines and $\pm 45^\circ$ diagonal lines (multiplicity $\le 2\lceil \lambda \rceil$) reduces to the $2 C_m$ automaton ($\Sigma_g = \{0, 2m\}$, where individual reachable state spaces satisfy $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; G_f, 2 C_m) \subseteq \mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2m\})$ with equality if and only if $d \ge 2m - 1$). Using all $8$ laws ($S_3..S_7, A_5^+, A_7^+, \mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 8}$) together with all $19$ explicit finite laws $S_3..S_{15}$, odd $A_5^+..A_{15}^+$, and the 11-atom degree-16 tail envelope $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}$, we certify across **all** degrees $d \ge 3$ the exact maximal horizons **$k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_2) = 3$** ($\le \mathbf{1.727108 < 1.80}$, while $S_3$ at $k = 4$ is $\frac{160670891}{87233527} \approx 1.841848 > 1.80$), **$k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_3) = 6$** ($\le \mathbf{1.798577 < 1.80}$, while $S_3$ at $k = 7$ is $\ge 1.856292 > 1.80$), and **$k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_4) = 8$** ($\le \mathbf{1.778960 < 1.80}$, while $S_3$ at $k = 9$ is $\ge 1.827059 > 1.80$).

All exclusions apply to coordinate-line and vertical-plus-diagonal covers of branch-separated algebraic translate families with fixed degree $d$ and fixed positive window ratio $\lambda \ge \theta_0 > 0$ as $p \to \infty$; the unrestricted No-Three-In-Line problem on $[0, n - 1]^2$ remains open.

## 1. Introduction: Position-Sensitive Window Laws and Diagonally Sheared Curve Translates

Let $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n - 1] \times [y_0, y_0 + n - 1] \subset \mathbb{Z}^2$ be an $n \times n$ grid window and let $p$ be a prime with window aspect ratio $\lambda = n / p = a + \theta \ge \theta_0 > 0$, where $a = \lfloor n / p \rfloor \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ and $\theta = (n \bmod p) / p \in [0, 1)$. Any no-three-in-line subset $A \subseteq C \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ supported on an algebraic carrier $C \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p^2$ satisfies $|A| \le 2\,\mathcal{L}(C \cap W_n(x_0, y_0))$ for any family of Euclidean lines covering $C \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ [2, 5].

For univariate polynomial translate families $y \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($s \in S_k$), coordinate-cover bounds below $\frac{9}{5}n = 1.80n$ have been established via reachable-mask dynamic programming [6, 7], twin-vertex reductions [2, Lemma 3.1; 5, Section 7.1], two-strip multi-period queue stratification [2], and single-hole non-consecutive automata [1, 4]. In `no_three_in_line_69` v1 [1], we extended coordinate-cover exclusions to **separated bivariate algebraic curve translate families**
$$H_p(f, g; S_k) \;=\; \bigl\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 : g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p,\;\; s \in S_k\bigr\}, \qquad S_k = \{0 = s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_k = W\}, \tag{1.1}$$
via the backward outgoing **Bivariate Required-Row Automaton** on the reachable-mask space $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ ($|\mathcal{M}(S_k)| = D$):
$$T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m) \;=\; \begin{cases} v(m \gg 1) + r_g \cdot (m \mathbin{\&} 1), & \text{if } r_f = 0, \\[4pt] \min\Bigl(\, v(m \gg 1) + r_f + r_g \cdot (m \mathbin{\&} 1),\;\; v\bigl((m \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1\bigr) + r_g \,\Bigr), & \text{if } r_f \ge 1, \end{cases} \tag{1.2}$$
where $M_{S_k} = \sum_{s \in S_k} 2^s$, $\widehat{T}_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m) = T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m) - T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(0)$, and step cost $c_{\mathrm{bivar}}(v; r_f, r_g) = T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(0)$. Using full-torus root-count laws $R_f(u) = |f^{-1}(u)|$ and $R_g(w) = |g^{-1}(w)|$ and the two-axis marked-root transfer of [1, Section 4.1], [1, Theorem 3] certified $2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; f, C_2) \le 1.799239 < 1.80$ up to $k_{\max} = 9$ (hence every subset $S \subseteq [0, 8]$) for quadratic $g(y) = y^2$ ($C_2$), and $2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; f, C_3) \le 1.796569 < 1.80$ up to $k_{\max} = 13$ (hence every subset $S \subseteq [0, 12]$) for cubic $g(y) = y^3$ ($C_3$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$).

Here we advance this bivariate framework in three directions:
1. **Unconditionally Exact Cyclic Window Optimization and Sub-Period vs. Multi-Period Diagnostics (Section 2):** Combining the twin-vertex reduction [2, Lemma 3.1; 5, Section 7.1] on both axes with boundary-mask optimization over $m_{\mathrm{init}} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$ (`exact_cyclic_window_dp`), we compute the exact cyclic value-torus optimum and reconstruct physical covers on $W_n(x_0, y_0)$, explaining why multi-period windows ($a \ge 1$) stay above $1.80n$ at $k = 10$ on $y^2 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ while sub-period windows ($a = 0$) drop below $1.80n$.
2. **Position-Sensitive Antipodal Window Laws, Convex-Order Monotonicity, Sub-Period / Superelliptic Duality, and the Kummer Branch-Separation Dichotomy (Section 3):** On any window $W_n(x_0, y_0)$ of $y^2 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$, the horizontal window multiplicity $W_g(\lambda, \alpha)$ depends on the window origin $y_0$ through the antipodal overlap $\alpha = |I_y(y_0) \cap (-I_y(y_0))|/p \in [\max(0, 2\theta - 1), \theta]$. We prove by convex-order dominance that $\alpha_{\min}(\theta) = \max(0, 2\theta - 1)$ is the universal worst-case origin, that $\tilde{\gamma}^*(\theta, 0)$ is non-decreasing on $[\theta_0, 1/2]$ by martingale dilation, and that $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(1/m, 0) \stackrel{d}{=} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(2/m, 2/m) \stackrel{d}{=} C_m$, certifying quartic superelliptic curves $C_4$ and quarter-period quadratic windows up to $k = 16$ across all $d \ge 3$, and establishing the branch-disjoint ($C_2 \otimes C_4$, $k_{\max} = 18$) vs. branch-shared ($\mathcal{J}_{2, 4}$, $k_{\max} = 11$) dichotomy for $y^4 \equiv x^2 + c_0 + s \pmod p$.
3. **Diagonally Sheared Curve Translate Automata $2 C_m$ (Section 4):** For diagonally sheared curve families $(a_1 x + b_1 y)^m \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($a_1, b_1 \in \{\pm 1\}$), vertical-plus-diagonal line covers reduce to the $2 C_m$ automaton, certifying $k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_m) \in \{3, 6, 8\}$ for $m \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ across all $d \ge 3$ via explicit evaluation of $S_3..S_{15}$, odd $A_5^+..A_{15}^+$, and the degree-16 tail envelope $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}$.

## 2. Exact Cyclic Boundary-State Window Optimization and the 3-Way Window Diagnostic

For any window $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n - 1] \times [y_0, y_0 + n - 1]$ with $n = a p + b$ ($a = \lfloor n/p \rfloor \ge 0$, $0 \le b < p$, $\lambda = n/p = a + \theta \ge \theta_0 > 0$, $\theta = b/p$), let
$$w_x(x) \;=\; \bigl|\{\tilde{x} \in [x_0, x_0 + n - 1] : \tilde{x} \equiv x \pmod p\}\bigr| \;=\; a + \mathbf{1}_{x \in I_x(x_0)}, \qquad w_y(y) \;=\; a + \mathbf{1}_{y \in I_y(y_0)}, \tag{2.1}$$
where $I_x(x_0) = \{(x_0 + j) \bmod p : 0 \le j < b\}$ and $I_y(y_0) = \{(y_0 + j) \bmod p : 0 \le j < b\}$ are residue arcs of cardinality $b = \theta p$, and define the **physical vertical and horizontal value-fiber multiplicities** on $\mathbb{F}_p$:
$$W_f(u) \;:=\; \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : f(x) = u} w_x(x), \qquad W_g(w) \;:=\; \sum_{y \in \mathbb{F}_p : g(y) = w} w_y(y). \tag{2.2}$$

Let $G_{W_n} = (V_{\mathrm{col}}, V_{\mathrm{row}}, E_{W_n})$ denote the physical bipartite axis-parallel incidence graph of $H_p(f, g; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$, with $V_{\mathrm{col}} = [x_0, x_0 + n - 1]$, $V_{\mathrm{row}} = [y_0, y_0 + n - 1]$, and $(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) \in E_{W_n}$ whenever $g(\tilde{y} \bmod p) - f(\tilde{x} \bmod p) \equiv s \pmod p$ for some $s \in S_k$.

**Theorem 1 (Exact Cyclic Boundary-State Window Optimization and One-Coordinate Transfer).** *Let $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ ($\deg f = d \ge 3$, $p > d$) and $g \in \mathbb{F}_p[y]$ ($\deg g = m \ge 1$), and let $S_k \subset [0, W]$ ($0 \in S_k$, $|S_k| = k$, span $W < p$).*

*(i) **Twin-Fiber Reduction and Unconditionally Exact Cyclic Boundary-Mask DP:** By the twin-vertex reduction ([2, Lemma 3.1; 5, Section 7.1] applied simultaneously to the vertical value-fibers $\mathcal{X}_u = \{\tilde{x} \in V_{\mathrm{col}} : f(\tilde{x} \bmod p) = u\}$ and horizontal value-fibers $\mathcal{Y}_w = \{\tilde{y} \in V_{\mathrm{row}} : g(\tilde{y} \bmod p) = w\}$), every minimal vertex cover of $G_{W_n}$ is an all-or-nothing fiber union $\bigl(\bigcup_{u \in V} \mathcal{X}_u\bigr) \cup \bigl(\bigcup_{w \in H} \mathcal{Y}_w\bigr)$ for a cyclic shift-graph cover $(V, H) \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p$ ($u \notin V \implies u + S_k \subseteq H \pmod p$). Moreover, letting $\mathrm{DP}_p(m_{\mathrm{init}} \to m_{\mathrm{term}})$ denote the minimum cost of a single forward pass of the required-row recursion over $u = 0, 1, \dots, p - 1$ starting from boundary mask $m_{\mathrm{init}} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$ at $u = 0$ and ending at mask $m_{\mathrm{term}} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$ at $u = p \equiv 0 \pmod p$, the exact cyclic optimum is unconditionally equal to the boundary-mask minimum*
$$\tau\bigl(H_p(f, g; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)\bigr) \;=\; \min_{\substack{(V, H) \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p \\ u \notin V \implies u + S_k \subseteq H}} \left(\sum_{u \in V} W_f(u) + \sum_{w \in H} W_g(w)\right) \;=\; \min_{m_{\mathrm{init}} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)} \;\min_{\substack{m_{\mathrm{term}} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k) \\ m_{\mathrm{term}} \subseteq m_{\mathrm{init}}}} \mathrm{DP}_p\bigl(m_{\mathrm{init}} \to m_{\mathrm{term}}\bigr). \tag{2.3}$$

*(ii) **Linearity-of-Summation One-Coordinate Transfer and Explicit Phase-Averaging:** Fix a block length $L \ge 1$ and window ratio $\lambda = n/p \ge \theta_0 > 0$. For each phase $j \in \{0, 1, \dots, L - 1\}$, partition $\mathbb{F}_p$ into contiguous blocks of length $L + W$, forcing vertical cover on the $W$-element seam between blocks and applying the $L$-step optimal policy of $T_{\lambda R_f(u), W_g(u)}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$ inside each block (which depends solely on the local pairs $(R_f(u + r), W_g(u + r))$ and not on $W_f(u)$). Let $(V_j, H_j)$ be the resulting value-cover for phase $j$, with physical line count $\mathrm{Cost}_{W_n}(j) = \sum_{u \in V_j} W_f(u) + \sum_{w \in H_j} W_g(w)$. By interchanging summation over $u \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and $x \in f^{-1}(u)$,*
$$\sum_{u \in V_j} W_f(u) \;=\; \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_p} w_x(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{f(x) \in V_j} \;=\; a \sum_{u \in V_j} R_f(u) \;+\; \sum_{x \in I_x(x_0)} \mathbf{1}_{f(x) \in V_j}. \tag{2.4}$$
*Under the branch-separation and monodromy hypotheses of [1, Theorem 3] for $f$ and $g(y) = y^m$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod m$, with $d = \deg(f)$ and $\theta_0 > 0$ fixed as $p \to \infty$), one-coordinate Weil–Perelmutov incomplete exponential sum bounds on the fiber-product curve over $u = f(x)$ [1, Section 4.1; 8, 9] yield $\sum_{x \in I_x(x_0)} \mathbf{1}_{f(x) \in V_j} = \theta \sum_{u \in V_j} R_f(u) + O_{d, m, k, L}\bigl(p^{1/2}(\log p)^{L+W+1}\bigr)$. Averaging over the $L$ phases and selecting a phase $j^*$ minimizing $\mathrm{Cost}_{W_n}(j)$ gives*
$$\min_{0 \le j < L} \mathrm{Cost}_{W_n}(j) \;\le\; \frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} \mathrm{Cost}_{W_n}(j) \;\le\; \left(\frac{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{OPT}_L\bigl(R_f, \widetilde{W}_g; S_k\bigr)\bigr]}{L} + \frac{d W}{L}\right) n \;+\; O_{d, m, k, L}\!\left(p^{1/2}(\log p)^{L+W+1}\right), \tag{2.5}$$
*where $\widetilde{W}_g = W_g / \lambda$. Taking $p \to \infty$ first for fixed $L$ and $\lambda \ge \theta_0 > 0$, and then $L \to \infty$, yields $\limsup_{p \to \infty} \tau(H_p(f, g; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)) / n \le \tilde{\gamma}^*(S_k; f, \widetilde{W}_g)$.*

*Proof.* For (i), any cyclic cover $(V, H)$ induces at $u = 0$ the required-row mask $m_{\mathrm{true}} = \bigl\{j \in [0, W - 1] : \exists s \in S_k,\, s > j,\, (p - (s - j)) \notin V\bigr\} \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$; walking once around $u = 0, \dots, p - 1$ from $m_{\mathrm{init}} = m_{\mathrm{true}}$ returns to $m_{\mathrm{term}} = m_{\mathrm{true}} \subseteq m_{\mathrm{init}}$ with cost $\le \sum_{u \in V} W_f(u) + \sum_{w \in H} W_g(w)$. Conversely, any trajectory from $m_{\mathrm{init}}$ to $m_{\mathrm{term}} \subseteq m_{\mathrm{init}}$ defines $(V, H)$ covering all wrap-around edges $(p - (s - j), j)$ because every bit $j \in m_{\mathrm{term}}$ is already present in $m_{\mathrm{init}}$ and paid at step $u = j$ (`exact_cyclic_window_dp` in `verify_subperiod_antipodal_and_sheared_automata.py`, which also reproduces the exact minimum $6$ with $V = \{0, 3, 4, 5\}, H = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ on the non-zero-boundary cycle $p = 7, S = (0, 1, 2, 5), W_f = (0, 1, 3, 0, 1, 0, 2), W_g = (0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 1)$). Part (ii) follows from (2.4) and [1, Section 4.1]. $\square$

## 3. Position-Sensitive Antipodal Window Laws, Convex-Order Monotonicity, and Sub-Period / Superelliptic Duality

Let $g(y) = (y - c)^2 + e \bmod p$ ($p \ge 5$) and let $W_n(x_0, y_0)$ have $n = a p + b$ ($\lambda = n/p = a + \theta \ge \theta_0 > 0$, $a = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor \ge 0$, $\theta = b/p \in [0, 1)$). Shifting $y \mapsto y - c$ and $f(x) \mapsto f(x) - e$, we reduce without loss of generality to $g(y) = y^2 \bmod p$ with horizontal residue arc $I = I_y(y_0 - c) \subset \mathbb{F}_p$ of cardinality $|I| = b = \theta p$. Define the **antipodal overlap**
$$\alpha(y_0) \;:=\; \frac{\bigl|I \cap (-I)\bigr|}{p} \;\in\; \bigl[\alpha_{\min}(\theta),\; \theta\bigr], \qquad \alpha_{\min}(\theta) \;:=\; \max(0, 2\theta - 1), \tag{3.1}$$
where the lower bound follows from $|I \cup (-I)| = 2|I| - |I \cap (-I)| \le p$. Partitioning $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ into $(p - 1)/2$ antipodal pairs $\{y, -y\}$, exactly $|I \cap (-I)|/2 = (\alpha/2)p + O(1)$ pairs have both roots in $I$ ($W_g(w) = 2a + 2$), $|I \setminus (-I)| = (\theta - \alpha)p + O(1)$ pairs have one root in $I$ ($W_g(w) = 2a + 1$), and $\bigl(\frac{1}{2} - \theta + \frac{\alpha}{2}\bigr)p + O(1)$ pairs have neither root in $I$ ($W_g(w) = 2a$), while the $(p - 1)/2$ quadratic non-residues give $W_g(w) = 0$. Hence as $p \to \infty$ with $(\lambda, \alpha)$ converging to $(\lambda, \alpha)$, the single-value horizontal multiplicity converges to the **4-point antipodal window law** $W_g(\lambda, \alpha)$ on $\{0, 2a, 2a + 1, 2a + 2\}$:
$$\mathbb{P}(W_g = 0) = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \mathbb{P}(W_g = 2a) = \frac{1}{2} - \theta + \frac{\alpha}{2}, \quad \mathbb{P}(W_g = 2a + 1) = \theta - \alpha, \quad \mathbb{P}(W_g = 2a + 2) = \frac{\alpha}{2}, \tag{3.2}$$
with $\mathbb{E}[W_g(\lambda, \alpha)] = a + \theta = \lambda$ and unit-mean normalized law $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha) := W_g(\lambda, \alpha) / \lambda$. By the $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{L+W}$ Kummer tower $y_r^2 = u + r$ ($0 \le r < L + W$) over $\mathbb{F}_p(u)$, which has pairwise distinct finite branch points $-r \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and is geometrically disjoint from the vertical splitting field of $f(x_r) = u + r$ under the branch-separation hypothesis of [1, Theorem 3], incomplete Weil–Perelmutov bounds [1, Section 4.1; 8, 9] imply that $\bigl((R_f(u + r), W_g(u + r))\bigr)_{0 \le r < L+W}$ converges to i.i.d. product draws from $\mu_f \otimes W_g(\lambda, \alpha)$ with error $O_{d, k, L}\bigl(p^{1/2}(\log p)^{2(L+W)+1}\bigr) = o(n)$ for fixed $\lambda \ge \theta_0 > 0$.

Recall from [1, Theorem 3(ii)] that for consecutive $S_k = (0, \dots, k - 1)$ and integer alphabets $\Sigma_f, \Sigma_g \subset \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ with $0 \in \Sigma_g$ and $M_g := \max \Sigma_g \ge 1$, the adjacent-difference encoding $\Delta_i(v) := v(m_i) - v(m_{i-1})$ ($1 \le i \le k - 1$, where $m_0 = 0$ and $m_i = \{0, \dots, i - 1\}$) embeds the state space into $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{k-1}$, inside which we define
$$\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g) \;:=\; \{\mathbf{0}\} \;\cup\; \bigcup_{r=1}^{k-1} \Bigl\{\Delta \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{k-1} : \Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{r-1} \in \Sigma_g,\;\; \Delta_r \in \{1, \dots, M_g\},\;\; \Delta_{r+1} = \dots = \Delta_{k-1} = 0\Bigr\}, \tag{3.3}$$
of exact cardinality $|\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)| = 1 + M_g \sum_{r=0}^{k-2} |\Sigma_g|^r$ (equal to $M_g \cdot 2^{k-1} - (M_g - 1)$ whenever $|\Sigma_g| = 2$).

**Theorem 2 (Antipodal Window Law, Convex-Order Worst-Origin Bounds, Sub-Period / Superelliptic Duality, and the $(C_2, C_4)$ Kummer Branch-Separation Dichotomy).** *Let $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ have fixed degree $d \ge 3$ and monodromy $G_f \in \{S_d\; (d \ge 3),\; A_d^+\; (\text{odd } d \ge 5)\}$ satisfying the branch-separation hypothesis of [1, Theorem 3] in parts (i)–(iii), or $f(x) = x^2 + c_0$ ($d=2$) in parts (iv)–(v), and let $\theta_0 > 0$ be fixed as $p \to \infty$.*

*(i) **Forward-Invariant Superset $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ vs. Exact Reachable State Space $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}$:** For every consecutive $S_k = (0, \dots, k - 1)$ ($k \ge 3$) and integer alphabets $(\Sigma_f, \Sigma_g)$, $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ is forward-invariant under $\widehat{T}_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$, so $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; \Sigma_f, \Sigma_g) \subseteq \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$. Equality $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; \Sigma_f, \Sigma_g) = \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ holds **if and only if** every single-nonzero vector $\mathbf{e}_1(c) := (c, 0, \dots, 0)$ ($1 \le c \le M_g$) is reachable from $\mathbf{0}$. In particular, for $\Sigma_g = \{0, 4\}$ ($C_4$), equality $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, C_4)| = 4 \cdot 2^{k-1} - 3$ holds across **all** $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$) and odd $A_d^+$ ($d \ge 5$) for $k \ge 3$, whereas at $k = 3, \theta = 1/8, \alpha = 0$ under $S_3$ ($\Sigma_f = \{0, 1, 3\}, \Sigma_g = \{0, 8\}$), strict inclusion holds: $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}| = 16 < 25 = |\mathcal{I}_3(\{0, 8\})|$.*

*(ii) **Convex-Order Worst-Origin Theorem, Martingale Dilation on $[\theta_0, 1/2]$, and Sub-Period / Superelliptic Isomorphism:** Let $\tilde{\gamma}^*(S_k; f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha))$ denote the optimal asymptotic horizontal+vertical cover drift under (3.2).*
- *(a) **Universal Worst-Case Origin:** For fixed $\lambda = a + \theta \ge \theta_0 > 0$ and $\alpha_1 < \alpha_2$ in $[\alpha_{\min}(\theta), \theta]$, $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha_1) \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha_2)$ in convex order, whence $\tilde{\gamma}^*(S_k; f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha_2)) \le \tilde{\gamma}^*(S_k; f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha_1))$. Thus $\alpha = \alpha_{\min}(\theta) = \max(0, 2\theta - 1)$ is the universal worst-case origin over all $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$.*
- *(b) **Worst-Origin Sub-Period Monotonicity:** For $a = 0$ and $\theta_0 \le \theta_1 < \theta_2 \le 1/2$, $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_2, 0) \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_1, 0)$ by martingale dilation, whence $\tilde{\gamma}^*(S_k; f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_1, 0)) \le \tilde{\gamma}^*(S_k; f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_2, 0))$.*
- *(c) **Antipodal Doubling and Superelliptic Isomorphism:** For every $\theta' \in (0, 1/2)$, $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(2\theta', 2\theta') \stackrel{d}{=} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta', 0)$. Setting $\theta' = 1/m$ ($m \ge 2$) yields $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(1/m, 0) \stackrel{d}{=} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(2/m, 2/m) \stackrel{d}{=} C_m$, the full-period root-count law of the superelliptic curve $y^m \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod m$).*

*(iii) **All-Degree Certified Sub-Period and Quartic Superelliptic Exclusions:** Evaluating exact rational Bellman residual bounds across **all $8$ vertical laws** $S_3, S_4, S_5, S_6, S_7, A_5^+, A_7^+$, and $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 8}^{(M_g k)}$ certifies $2\tilde{\gamma}^* < \frac{9}{5} = 1.80$ simultaneously across every $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$), every odd $A_d^+$ ($d \ge 5$), and every origin $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ for:*

*(iv) **Branch-Disjoint Shifted $(C_2, C_4)$ Kummer Carrier and Exact Product-Form Stationary Law:** For the quadratic-quartic translate family $y^4 \equiv x^2 + c_0 + s \pmod p$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $s \in S_k$) with non-zero shift $c_0 \equiv (p - 1)/2 \pmod p$ ($p > 2(L + W) + 1$), the vertical branch point $u = c_0$ of $f(x) = x^2 + c_0$ is disjoint from the horizontal branch locus $\{-r : 0 \le r < L + W\}$ of $y_r^4 = u + r$, so $(R_f, R_g)$ converges to the independent product law $C_2 \otimes C_4$. Scaling by $1/2$ ($r_f \in \{0, 1\}$, $r_g \in \{0, 2\}$), the Markov chain on $\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2\})$ ($|\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2\})| = 2^k - 1$, where $m(\Delta) := |\{1 \le i < r : \Delta_i = 2\}|$) has the **exact product-form stationary distribution***
$$\pi(\Delta) \;=\; 3^{-m(\Delta)}\,w(r, \Delta_r)\,\pi(\mathbf{0}), \quad w(r, 1) = \frac{3^r - 1}{4^{r+1}}, \quad w(r, 2) = \frac{3^{r+1} + 1}{3 \cdot 4^{r+1}}, \quad \pi(\mathbf{0}) = \frac{16 \cdot 3^{k-1}}{(2k + 3) 3^k + 1}, \tag{3.4}$$
*and exact closed-form drift*
$$2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; C_2 \otimes C_4) \;=\; \frac{2\bigl((2k - 1) 3^k + 1\bigr)}{(2k + 3) 3^k + 1} \;=\; 2 - \frac{24 \cdot 3^{k-1}}{(2k + 3) 3^k + 1}, \tag{3.5}$$
*giving exact horizon **$k_{\max}(C_2 \otimes C_4) = 18$** ($2\gamma^* = \frac{2(35 \cdot 3^{18} + 1)}{39 \cdot 3^{18} + 1} \approx \mathbf{1.794872 < 1.80}$, whereas at $k = 19$, $2\gamma^* \approx 1.804878 > 1.80$).*

*(v) **Branch-Shared Unshifted $(C_2, C_4)$ Carrier and Correlated 3-Atom Law $\mathcal{J}_{2, 4}$:** By contrast, for the unshifted carrier $y^4 \equiv x^2 + s \pmod p$ ($c_0 = 0$, $0 \in S_k$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$), $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(y) = y^4$ share the branch point $u = 0$ and satisfy $\{y^4 : y \in \mathbb{F}_p\} \subseteq \{x^2 : x \in \mathbb{F}_p\}$, so $(R_{x^2}(u), R_{y^4}(u))$ is strictly correlated and converges to the **3-atom joint law***

*with exact rational drifts $2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; \mathcal{J}_{2, 4}) = \frac{5}{4} = 1.25$ at $k = 2$, $\frac{7}{5} = 1.40$ at $k = 3$, $\frac{209}{139} \approx 1.503597$ at $k = 4$, $\frac{109}{69} \approx 1.579710$ at $k = 5$, and exact horizon **$k_{\max}(\mathcal{J}_{2, 4}) = 11$** ($2\gamma^* \in [\mathbf{1.791631, 1.791632}] < 1.80$ on $2{,}047$ states, whereas at $k = 12$, $2\gamma^* \in [1.808560, 1.808561] > 1.80$ on $4{,}095$ states).*

**(ii)** Fix $L \ge 1$ and any vertical root-count vector $\mathbf{r}_f \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^L$. For each vertical cover $V \subseteq [0, L - 1]$, let $H_{\min}(V) = \{w \in [0, L + W - 1] : \exists u \in [0, L - 1] \setminus V,\, w - u \in S_k\}$. Then $\mathrm{OPT}_L(\mathbf{r}_f, \mathbf{y}; S_k) = \min_{V \subseteq [0, L-1]} \bigl(\sum_{u \in V} r_{f, u} + \sum_{w \in H_{\min}(V)} y_w\bigr)$ is a pointwise minimum of affine-linear functions of $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{L+W}$, hence jointly and coordinatewise concave.
- For (a), increasing $\alpha_1$ to $\alpha_2 = \alpha_1 + \delta$ shifts probability mass $\delta$ from $2a + 1$ symmetrically ($+\delta/2$ each) to $2a$ and $2a + 2$ in (3.2), a mean-preserving spread ($W_g(\lambda, \alpha_1) \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}} W_g(\lambda, \alpha_2)$). Applying coordinatewise concavity across the $L + W$ independent coordinates gives $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}_L(R_f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha_2))] \le \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}_L(R_f, \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\lambda, \alpha_1))]$, and dividing by $L \to \infty$ proves (a).
- For (b), at $a = 0, \alpha = 0$ ($\theta \in [\theta_0, 1/2]$), $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta, 0) = \frac{1}{\theta}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$. For $\theta_1 < \theta_2$, the Markov kernel sending $0 \mapsto 0$ and sending $1/\theta_2$ to $1/\theta_1$ with probability $\theta_1 / \theta_2$ and to $0$ with probability $1 - \theta_1 / \theta_2$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[X_1 \mid X_2] = X_2$ with $X_i \sim \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_i, 0)$, so $\widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_2, 0) \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta_1, 0)$ and Jensen's inequality on concave $\mathrm{OPT}_L$ gives (b).
- For (c), by (3.2), $W_g(2\theta', 2\theta') \in \{0, 2\}$ with $\mathbb{P}(W_g = 2) = \theta'$, so $W_g(2\theta', 2\theta') / (2\theta') = \frac{1}{\theta'}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta') \stackrel{d}{=} \widetilde{W}_{y^2}(\theta', 0)$; setting $\theta' = 1/m$ gives $m\,\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/m) \stackrel{d}{=} C_m$.

**(iii)** By [1, Theorem 3(iii)], on every reachable state $v \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}$, $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)$ saturates horizontally at $r_f \ge M_g k$ and is monotonically non-decreasing in $r_f$. **(iv)–(v)** For $f(x) = x^2 + c_0$ with $c_0 \equiv (p - 1)/2 \pmod p$ ($p > 2(L + W) + 1$), the quadratic Kummer extension $\mathbb{F}_p(u)(\sqrt{u + r - c_0})$ is ramified at $u = c_0 - r \equiv (p - 1)/2 - r \pmod p$, which is disjoint from the horizontal quartic Kummer branch locus $\{-r' : 0 \le r' < L + W\}$ of $\mathbb{F}_p(u)((u + r')^{1/4})$; hence the joint Kummer tower has full Galois group $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{L+W} \times (\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^{L+W}$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(u)$ and $(R_f, R_g) \sim C_2 \otimes C_4$. On $\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2\})$ under $(r_f, r_g) \in \{0, 1\} \times \{0, 2\}$ with pair probabilities $p_{0, 0} = p_{1, 0} = 3/8$ and $p_{0, 2} = p_{1, 2} = 1/8$, any state $\tilde{\Delta}$ with last non-zero index $k - 1$ transitions under all four pairs $(r_f, r_g)$ to $(r_g, \tilde{\Delta}_1, \dots, \tilde{\Delta}_{k-2})$ (since $C = v_{k-1} - 1 \ge v_{k-2}$ when $r_f = 1$). ## 4. Diagonally Sheared Curve Translate Automata $2 C_m$ and Degree-16 Tail Certifications

Consider **diagonally sheared algebraic curve translate families** of the form
$$D_p(f, m; S_k) \;:=\; \bigl\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 : (a_1 x + b_1 y)^m \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p,\;\; s \in S_k\bigr\}, \qquad a_1, b_1 \in \{\pm 1\}, \tag{4.1}$$
with $p \equiv 1 \pmod m$ and fixed $d = \deg(f) \ge 3$. While axis-parallel lines fail to isolate $(a_1 x + b_1 y)^m$, any no-three-in-line subset $A \subseteq D_p(f, m; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ has at most $2$ points on each **Euclidean vertical line** $\tilde{x} = c_1$ ($c_1 \in [x_0, x_0 + n - 1]$) and at most $2$ points on each **Euclidean $\pm 45^\circ$ diagonal line** $a_1 \tilde{x} + b_1 \tilde{y} = c_2$. As $(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y})$ ranges over $W_n(x_0, y_0)$, $\tilde{z} := a_1 \tilde{x} + b_1 \tilde{y}$ ranges over a contiguous integer interval $J_{\mathrm{diag}}$ of cardinality $|J_{\mathrm{diag}}| = 2n - 1$. Hence for each residue $z \in \mathbb{F}_p$, the number of distinct Euclidean diagonal lines $a_1 \tilde{x} + b_1 \tilde{y} = c_2 \in J_{\mathrm{diag}}$ with $c_2 \equiv z \pmod p$ satisfies
$$w_{\mathrm{diag}}(z) \;:=\; \bigl|\{c_2 \in J_{\mathrm{diag}} : c_2 \equiv z \pmod p\}\bigr| \;=\; \lfloor (2n - 1)/p \rfloor + \mathbf{1}_{z \in I_{\mathrm{diag}}} \;\le\; 2\lceil \lambda \rceil. \tag{4.2}$$

**Theorem 3 (Diagonally Sheared Curve Automata $2 C_m$, Sub-Period Diagonal Geometry, and Degree-16 Tail Horizons).** *Let $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ ($\deg f = d \ge 3$) satisfy the branch-separation and monodromy hypotheses of Theorem 2 ($G_f \in \{S_d\; (d \ge 3),\; A_d^+\; (\text{odd } d \ge 5)\}$), and let $p \equiv 1 \pmod m$ ($m \in \{2, 3, 4\}$).*

*(i) **Reduction of Vertical-Plus-Diagonal Covers to $2 C_m$ and Sub-Period Diagonal Convex Order:** Setting $W_{\mathrm{diag}}(w) := \sum_{z \in \mathbb{F}_p : z^m = w} w_{\mathrm{diag}}(z)$, any cyclic shift-graph cover $(V, H) \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p$ of $S_k$ lifts to vertical lines $\{\tilde{x} \in [x_0, x_0 + n - 1] : f(\tilde{x} \bmod p) \in V\}$ plus diagonal lines $\{c_2 \in J_{\mathrm{diag}} : (c_2 \bmod p)^m \in H\}$ of total count $\sum_{u \in V} W_f(u) + \sum_{w \in H} W_{\mathrm{diag}}(w)$. On every full-period window $W_{a p}(x_0, y_0)$ ($a \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ fixed), $w_{\mathrm{diag}}(z) \le 2a$, so $W_{\mathrm{diag}}(w)/a \le 2 R_{z^m}(w) \sim 2 C_m$ ($\Sigma_g = \{0, 2m\}$ with probabilities $(1 - 1/m, 1/m)$) and Theorem 1(ii) gives $\limsup_{p \to \infty} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vert+diag}}(D_p(f, m; S_k) \cap W_{ap}(x_0, y_0))/(ap) \le \gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; f, 2 C_m)$. Furthermore, for $m = 2$ on sub-period windows $W_{\theta p}(0, 0)$ ($\theta \in [\theta_0, 1/4]$):*
- *Under the **anti-diagonal shear** $z = x + y$, $J_{\mathrm{diag}} = [0, 2\theta p - 2]$ has zero antipodal overlap $\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}} = 0$, giving normalized diagonal multiplicity law $\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{anti}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{\theta}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(2\theta)$ (equal to $2 C_2$ at $\theta = 1/4$).*
- *Under the **main-diagonal shear** $z = x - y$, $J_{\mathrm{diag}} = [-\theta p + 1, \theta p - 1]$ is symmetric around $0$ with maximal overlap $\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}} = 2\theta$, giving $\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{main}}(\theta) = \frac{2}{\theta}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$. Since $\frac{1}{\theta}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(2\theta) \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}} \frac{2}{\theta}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$ by mean-preserving dilation of $2/\theta$ onto $\{0, 1/\theta\}$ with weights $(1/2, 1/2)$, both sub-period shears are bounded above by $\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; f, 2 C_2)$ across all $\theta \in [\theta_0, 1/4]$.*

*(ii) **11-Atom Degree-16 Tail Envelope $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(M_{\mathrm{sat}})}$:** Let $a_j = \sum_{i=0}^j (-1)^i / i!$, and let $o(t)$ and $e(t)$ denote the least odd and least even integers $\ge t$. Define $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(M_{\mathrm{sat}})}$ on $\{0, 1, \dots, 10, M_{\mathrm{sat}}\}$ by assigning*
$$\ell_r \;:=\; \frac{1}{r!}\min\Bigl\{\, a_{o(16 - r)},\;\; 2\,a_{o(17 - r)} - a_{e(15 - r)} \,\Bigr\} \quad (0 \le r \le 10), \qquad \ell_{M_{\mathrm{sat}}} \;:=\; 1 - \sum_{r=0}^{10} \ell_r. \tag{4.3}$$
*Then for every $S_d$ with $d \ge 16$ and every odd $A_d^+$ with $d \ge 17$, $R_f \wedge M_{\mathrm{sat}} \preceq_{\mathrm{st}} \mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(M_{\mathrm{sat}})}$ in first-order stochastic dominance.*

*(iii) **Exact Certified Vertical-Plus-Diagonal Horizons $k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_m) \in \{3, 6, 8\}$:** On full-period windows $W_{a p}(x_0, y_0)$ ($a \ge 1$), evaluating all $8$ laws ($S_3..S_7, A_5^+, A_7^+, \mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 8}^{(2m k)}$) for $k \le 2m - 1$ and all $20$ laws ($S_3..S_{15}$, odd $A_5^+..A_{15}^+$, and $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(2m k)}$) at $k \in \{6, 8\}$ certifies $2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; f, 2 C_m) < \frac{9}{5} = 1.80$ across **all** $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$) and odd $A_d^+$ ($d \ge 5$) up to the exact automaton horizons:*
- ***Sheared Quadratics $(a_1 x + b_1 y)^2 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($2 C_2$):** **$k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_2) = 3$** ($|\mathcal{I}_3(\{0, 4\})| = 13$ states; $S_3 = \frac{336746}{195841} \approx 1.719487$, $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 8}^{(12)} \le \mathbf{1.727108 < 1.80}$; whereas at $k = 4$, $S_3$ already has exact drift $\frac{160670891}{87233527} \approx 1.841848 > 1.80$), plus the span-$3$ single-hole set $(0, 2, 3) \cong (0, 1, 3)$ ($\le \mathbf{1.741811 < 1.80}$).*
- ***Sheared Cubics $(a_1 x + b_1 y)^3 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($2 C_3$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$):** **$k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_3) = 6$** ($|\mathcal{I}_6(\{0, 6\})| = 187$ states; $S_3 \le 1.798491$, $\max_{3 \le d \le 15} \le \mathbf{1.798577 < 1.80}$ attained at $S_4$, $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(36)} \le \mathbf{1.798471 < 1.80}$; whereas at $k = 7$, $S_3 \ge 1.856292 > 1.80$).*
- ***Sheared Quartics $(a_1 x + b_1 y)^4 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($2 C_4$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$):** **$k_{\max}^{\mathrm{diag}}(C_4) = 8$** ($|\mathcal{I}_8(\{0, 8\})| = 1{,}017$ states; $S_3 \le \mathbf{1.778960 < 1.80}$, which maximizes across all $19$ explicit laws $S_3..S_{15}, A_5^+..A_{15}^+$, and $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(64)} \le \mathbf{1.778924 < 1.80}$; whereas at $k = 9$, $S_3 \ge 1.827059 > 1.80$).*

*Proof.* **(i)** Under the unimodular bijection $(x, y) \mapsto (x, z = a_1 x + b_1 y)$ on $\mathbb{F}_p^2$, $D_p(f, m; S_k)$ becomes $z^m \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$, which has pairwise distinct horizontal Kummer branch points over $u = f(x)$ and is geometrically disjoint from $f(x) = u$ by the branch-separation hypothesis on $f$. Each selected $u \in V$ costs $W_f(u)$ vertical lines and each selected $w \in H$ costs $W_{\mathrm{diag}}(w)$ diagonal lines, so Theorem 1(ii) applies verbatim with $W_{\mathrm{diag}}$ in place of $W_g$, and Theorem 2(ii) yields the convex-order comparison between $\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{anti}}(\theta)$ and $\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{main}}(\theta)$.

**(ii)** Since $a_{2j+1}$ strictly increases and $a_{2j}$ strictly decreases with $j$, for every $0 \le r \le 10$: (1) if $d \ge 16$, then $d - r \ge 16 - r \ge 6$, so $\mathbb{P}(R_{S_d} = r) = \frac{1}{r!} a_{d-r} \ge \frac{1}{r!} a_{o(16-r)}$; and (2) if $d \ge 17$ is odd, then $d - r \ge 17 - r \ge 7$ and $d - 2 - r \ge 15 - r \ge 5$, so $a_{d-r} \ge a_{o(17-r)}$ and $a_{d-2-r} \le a_{e(15-r)}$, whence $\mathbb{P}(R_{A_d^+} = r) = \frac{2 a_{d-r} - a_{d-2-r}}{r!} \ge \frac{2 a_{o(17-r)} - a_{e(15-r)}}{r!}$. Thus $\mathbb{P}(R_f = r) \ge \ell_r$ pointwise for all $0 \le r \le 10$, so $\sum_{j=0}^r \mathbb{P}(R_f \wedge M_{\mathrm{sat}} = j) \ge \sum_{j=0}^r \ell_j$ for all $0 \le r < M_{\mathrm{sat}}$, proving $R_f \wedge M_{\mathrm{sat}} \preceq_{\mathrm{st}} \mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 16}^{(M_{\mathrm{sat}})}$.

**(iii)** On $\Sigma_g = \{0, 2m\}$ ($M_g = 2m$), Theorem 2(i) shows that $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; G_f, 2 C_m) \subseteq \mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2m\})$ is a closed sub-chain of the universal forward-invariant superset $\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2m\})$ of cardinality $2m \cdot 2^{k-1} - (2m - 1)$, with equality holding if and only if $d \ge 2m - 1$ (since $2m \ge 4$, only $r_g = 2m$ enters $\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2m\}) \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}$, producing $\mathbf{e}_1(2m - r_f)$ from $\mathbf{0}$, so reaching $\mathbf{e}_1(1)$ requires $2m - 1 \in \Sigma_f$, i.e., $d \ge 2m - 1$; for $3 \le d < 2m - 1$, such as $S_3, S_4$ under $2 C_3$ or $S_3..S_6, A_5^+$ under $2 C_4$, including the referee's exact counterexamples $(S_3, 2C_3, k=3)$ with $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}| = 14 < 19 = |\mathcal{I}_3(\{0, 6\})|$ and $(S_3, 2C_4, k=3)$ with $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}| = 16 < 25 = |\mathcal{I}_3(\{0, 8\})|$, any Bellman potential $h$ on $\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 2m\})$ restricts to the closed sub-chain $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}$ and certifies $\gamma^*(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}})$ directly, as verified in Section 3 of `verify_subperiod_antipodal_and_sheared_automata.py`).
