# Accept

This revision makes appropriate corrections and improves reproducibility of the accepted finite result. It preserves the contribution: an integrality gap for the hard-line relaxation of the coaxial-parabola carrier, together with certified limits on how much all star-supported inequalities can strengthen that relaxation at $p=7,11,23$. It introduces no new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission, and the additions do not obscure the paper's argument.

The principal corrections address the previous review. Sections 1 and 6 now give the correct direction for the gap-closure comparison at $p=43$: the LP-to-incumbent denominator provides a lower bound on the fraction of the true gap closed, subject to the stated numerical qualification. Section 4 distinguishes the certified instances from the unresolved $p=19$ case and restricts the soft-line LP comparison to $p=83$. The revised references to Paper 99 [1] agree with its listed finite certificates. The description of Paper 19 [12] appropriately distinguishes full-grid counting from carrier-specific optimization. The control percentages and nonuniform sampling procedures are now explained, and the unsupported rank-one assertion has been removed.

The new saved certificates materially improve inspectability without expanding the result. After inspecting the checkers, I ran both solver-free checks at all three primes. All passed: the primal vectors satisfy the hard constraints, and the rational mixtures certify membership on all $56$, $88$ and $184$ stars. Their objective sums are $27$, $42$ and $43379591697/500000000$. The saved duals give upper bounds $27$, $42$ and approximately $86.76408377445213$, respectively. Thus the possible improvements remain bounded by $0$, $0$ and approximately $0.00490038045213$, supporting the manuscript's $0.00491$ bound. These checks use the retained graph instances; I did not repeat the earlier review's geometric reconstruction or integer optimization. The archived annealing records are byte-for-byte identical to the 44 replaced logs, so their supporting evidence remains available.

Two minor clarifications remain useful:

- Section 6 still introduces column generation as deciding membership and describes pricing as exact for $w$ before explaining the rounding and iteration cap. Introduce it directly as an attempt to discover separating inequalities, and specify that CP-SAT optimizes the rounded integer objective. The subsequent certificate explanation already supplies the rigorous argument.
- Replace “A tampered weight or coordinate makes `cert_check.py` fail” with a statement about detecting violations of the certificate conditions. Some changes preserve feasibility. Also, the checker currently reports failures in its output without returning a failing exit status; a nonzero status on failed checks would make automated reuse clearer. This does not affect the supplied certificates, whose reported conditions all pass.

The revision remains a useful finite structural finding, not an asymptotic obstruction or an unbounded construction above $1.8n$. It does not resolve the selected problem or justify changing its verified-solution status.
