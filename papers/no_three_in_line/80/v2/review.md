# Accept

This is an appropriate presentation revision of the accepted paper. The changes preserve its contribution: the finite classification of 124 labelled maximal 11-point configurations on $G_6$ with $\beta=1$ and $\delta=2$, explaining why a cheap local repair need not yield a path to a maximum at the same cardinality floor. The theorem statements, proof arguments, numerical tables and original computational evidence are unchanged. The additions improve the explanation of this contribution without introducing a separate substantive result or turning the paper into an unfocused collection.

The new census description in Section 6 agrees with the inspected `fresh_grid.cpp`: increasing-order insertion enumerates each safe set once, line occupancies determine legal extensions, and descending-threshold unions recover the components that reach a maximum. Including states with no larger coface is necessary and is now explicit. One minor clarification would help: distinguish the full set of legal insertion sites used to test maximality from the increasing-index subset used to recurse. The implementation makes this distinction correctly; exhaustion of the recursion choices alone would not establish maximality.

The new comparison in Section 5 accurately describes the graph studied in paper [17], whose Section 3.3 restricts exchanges to sets contained in a maximum. The distinction between $J_h[T_h]$ and $J_h$, including the threshold shift to $H_{h-1}$, explains why that earlier connectivity result does not duplicate the present maximum-free component classification. Section 8 appropriately identifies the complete-bipartite clearance mechanism as an antecedent from [3], while explaining the different endpoint sizes and why allowing anchor deletions does not invalidate the existing restricted-carrier argument.

The figures communicate the four component types and the C1 geometry clearly while retaining exact coordinates. In a small independent determinant-based check, I reconstructed the safe states from the supplied figure coordinates, confirmed their maximality on the full $G_6$, and verified every labelled graph edge and state mask: the four graphs have respectively 3, 3, 5 and 6 states and 3, 3, 6 and 9 edges. The displayed images agree with these data. Their captions correctly distinguish an illustration of local geometry from the complete boundary certificate needed to establish a barrier.

Section 11 now clearly separates the earlier fresh implementation from the acceptance reviewer's replay and accurately retains the limits of the larger-board verification. I inspected the census implementation and checked the new figure data; I did not rerun the full census, SAT/DRAT computations or larger-board path searches for this revision. The existing qualifications concerning omitted traces and sampled paths remain applicable. The manuscript remains a finite reconfiguration contribution and does not meet the problem's solve definition of an unbounded no-three-in-line family above $1.8n$ or a proof of impossibility.
