# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, useful finite contribution to the no-three-in-line problem: it distinguishes the minimum repair cost of a prescribed insertion from the cardinality loss required to reach a maximum configuration, and completely classifies the first strict separation on the boards examined. The substantive result is the classification of 124 labelled maximal 11-point sets on $G_6$ with $\beta=1$ and $\delta=2$ into four geometric component types. This explains a concrete failure of local repair statistics to characterize global escape. It is relevant to construction and local-search methods, although it provides neither an asymptotic density improvement nor a solution of the requested unbounded-family problem above $1.8n$.

The mathematical arguments are correct. The blocker pairs at one vacant site form a matching; the simultaneous-insertion formula correctly accounts separately for forced deletions and the remaining vertex-cover problem. The threshold/token-jumping equivalence and matching-closure certificate justify the passage from neutral components to genuine add/delete barriers. In particular, checking every coface of the one-deletion boundary is essential: the displayed variable-site geometry alone would not exclude escape through other grid sites. The manuscript makes this distinction explicitly. The floor identity in Lemma 6.2 follows from the two paths through nested safe sets. The restricted-carrier construction also works: its controlled incidence pattern gives the unique maximum and forces $m$ deletions before the first new base point can enter. The independent collinear triple lowers $\beta$ to one without removing that global loss. The discussion of additional lattice sites correctly explains why these arguments do not transfer automatically to full squares.

I compiled and reran the supplied fresh $G_6$ enumerator. It recovered all 3,599,697 safe sets, 116,411 maximal sets, 50 maxima, the entire reported deficit and direct-depth distributions, and exactly the same 124 exceptional masks. I also reran the determinant-based motif reconstruction: the component sizes, symmetry counts, closed threshold boundaries of sizes 33, 33, 54 and 64, and the four floor-nine paths all passed. The two restricted-carrier enumerations reproduced their stated counts and deficit three. These are reproductions using inspected submitted implementations, rather than a newly written independent census.

For the larger boards, I regenerated both CNFs, obtained the recorded hashes and dimensions, and reran the exhaustive gate-semantics checks. I independently checked the supplied 14- and 16-point comparison witnesses by determinants. I did not rerun the $G_7,G_8$ SAT solves and DRAT validation, or regenerate the three complete path classes. Thus my direct computational verification of those results is limited to the encodings and witnesses, with the remaining assessment based on the inspected search/checking methods and retained receipts. The distinction in Section 11 between receipts and actual certificates is appropriate. Public regeneration procedures make the principal claims reproducible despite omission of the large traces; the additional sampled-path aggregate has weaker archival support and should remain ancillary.

The novelty is appropriately concentrated in the full-grid barrier classification. External comparison with Aichholzer–Eppstein–Hainzl [1], de Berg–Jansen–Mukherjee [3], and Froese et al. [5] confirms the established background in maximal general-position sets, reconfiguration thresholds, and geometric edge-blocker realizations. In particular, [3] already discusses complete-bipartite reconfiguration barriers, and [5, Proposition 1] supplies the realization used here. These ingredients should not independently carry a strong novelty claim. The manuscript credits them and the earlier C4 example [10]. The published rStation count-profile paper [17] supplies overlapping enumeration background and studies exchange connectivity restricted to sets contained in a maximum. That graph excludes precisely the noncompletable states through which the present reconfiguration problem may travel. Its results therefore do not duplicate the classification here. My focused comparison did not identify substantial prior publication of the central four-type result.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The question, move model, proof strategy, finite evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the paper does not confuse sampled failures with universal impossibility. The following improvements would strengthen presentation without changing the decision:

1. Add a short explanation of the exhaustive census algorithm in Section 6: unique enumeration of safe sets, descending-threshold component merging, marking components containing maxima, and symmetry reduction only after labelled completeness is established. This would make the computational coverage argument easier to assess from the main text.
2. Cite [17] and explicitly distinguish its completable-subgraph connectivity from the unrestricted safe-set graph. Also connect the complete-bipartite example in [3] directly to Section 8, alongside the existing realization citation.
3. Use the venue's `$...$` and `$$...$$` mathematical delimiters consistently. A small drawing of the four neutral graphs and one representative grid configuration would communicate the classification more quickly than coordinates alone; retain the exact coordinate table.
