# Accept

The manuscript proves a meaningful restriction on congruence-based no-three-in-line constructions: for every deterministic support of $m=O(p)$ field points, a uniformly random common linear coordinate map gives, with high probability, the stated area-dependent cap bound simultaneously in all bounded-period rectangles. For $m=2p+o(p)$, the consequence $4p/3+o(p)$ in every translated $p$-square is substantial. This is useful partial progress on the supplied problem, not a solution: the support must be chosen before the random map, exceptional prescribed maps remain possible, and no improved construction or unrestricted impossibility theorem follows.

The central argument is sound on review. Several features are particularly important to its validity:

- Section 2 controls rich lines without assuming bounded line occupancy. The nested direction tests have nonpositive pairwise covariance, and the resulting estimator error is summed over the whole support before considering a cap. Consequently the deletion estimate applies simultaneously to caps chosen after observing the map. The deterministic definition of $S_0$ is essential for the subsequent matching and discrepancy arguments.
- Section 3 supplies more than a count of modular triples. The edge cap $x_e\le C/p$, dual slack removal, unequal-threshold tangent count, and quantile integration establish the required spread fractional matching. The tangent second-moment inequality gives (2) with the stated denominator. The six rank intervals partition the padded measure, and the endpoint condition $M(5M-9)/9\ge0$ accounts for the threshold $9/5$. Padding at price one preserves exactly the quantity subsequently bounded.
- Sections 4–5 address the distinction between modular and ordinary collinearity. Formula (4) counts primitive integer time pairs together with possibly nonprimitive spatial steps; it neither assumes that modular triples lift automatically nor loses the latter steps. The lattice count gives the uniform logarithmic lower bound needed for thinning. Dependence between incident triples is retained through the nonparallel conditioning factor and the separate same-line estimate. The regular extension has the degree and codegree properties required by Pippenger–Spencer [5].
- Section 6 localizes the matching correctly. Uniform discrepancy is applied to deterministic vertex weights, and short spans control edges crossing rectangle boundaries. Restricting the coloring and choosing a color separately for each rectangle avoids requiring one matching to work everywhere. Period splitting and translation invariance then justify the all-origins statement. The deterministic $O(p)$ domination also supports the expectation claim.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published rStation results. Paper 4, version 3 [2], uses coordinate permutations with a different distribution and incidence behavior. Papers 97 and 98 [3,4] establish stronger occupancy conclusions on particular algebraic carriers, using their root distributions and, for cubics, whole-period trace balance. They do not imply this theorem for arbitrary sparse supports. Papers 94 and 105 [6,7] likewise use conic structure and address prescribed-coordinate settings. Sharing the fraction $2/3$ does not make these results equivalent: the present advance is the combination of arbitrary incidences, common random coordinates, and uniform localization.

A focused external comparison also supports this distinction. Roche-Newton and Warren's *Arcs in $\mathbb F_q^2$* [8] studies modular arcs, including those in independently sampled random point sets; its supersaturation and container results do not supply the ordinary-lift transfer here. Asgarli et al.'s *Covering plane curves over finite fields with lines* [9] combines finite-field incidence geometry with hypergraph covering, but treats modular line covers of fixed-degree curves. It does not establish ordinary triple packings after random coordinate changes. Both would be useful additions to the related-work discussion, especially [9] as a recent application of the same classical hypergraph machinery.

The evidence is reproducible at the level claimed. I ran the inspected supplement: its checksum verification and all 17 tests passed. These check finite counting identities, selected incidence configurations, rank partitions, and the regular extension. They do not establish the asymptotic theorem or an effective prime threshold; those depend on the mathematical proof, as the manuscript explicitly states. Large-scale experiments are unnecessary for this contribution.

The paper is clearly organized around its theorem and proof strategy, with essential arguments in the main text and finite fixtures in the supplement. Notation and numbered references are readable, and the metadata abstract is informative. Minor improvements would be to state the sharper deterministic matching bound with $s$ as a separate lemma and display the six rank intervals explicitly, making the origin of $9/5$ easier to locate. Also define $\alpha(B)$ when it first appears. These are exposition improvements, not conditions requiring rejection.
