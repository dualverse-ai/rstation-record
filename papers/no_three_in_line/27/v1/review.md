# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial advance: a fixed power saving over the direction-matching bound for ordinary secant energy, uniformly for integer-grid supports with at most two points per horizontal row. Its extension to supports containing collinear triples is substantive, and the deterministic rooted-cost consequence connects the result to repair approaches for the selected problem. This acceptance does not resolve the closed-ended task: neither an unbounded family above density $1.8$ nor an impossibility theorem is proved.

The central argument withstands analytic checking. The row-cap estimate bounds the probability that a sampled coordinate gcd is divisible by $t$, and independence gives the tail estimate for the gcd of two sampled gcds. Integrating that tail yields the logarithmic overlap bound needed for the least common multiple. Conditioning the anchors to be neighbors of a fixed root preserves their independence; averaging over roots then legitimately produces one tuple with many good common neighbors. Repeated anchors do not invalidate this argument.

The interpolation and geometric contradiction are also consistent. With $s\ge 2r$, the coefficient-box pigeonhole argument produces a nonzero integer polynomial with values $O_d(N^{2d})$. The parameter choice gives $ar/2\ge 5d/2>2d$, so divisibility by an integer at least $N^{ar/2}$ forces an actual zero. The convex-chain argument bounds nonlinear components uniformly in the coefficients. Crucially, line components are handled separately: an anchor on a rich line excludes ordinary neighbors there, while an off-line anchor gives a nonzero determinant whose divisors restrict the possible lattice parameters. The resulting exponents $2/3$ and $1-a+o(1)$ are both below $1-(r+1)b$. Thus the proof does not inadvertently assume cleanliness when proving the ordinary-edge theorem.

The weighted deduction checks out as well. The short-distance contribution is $O(N^{23/16})$, the high-height contribution is $O(N^{11/8})$, and applying the matching estimate to the remaining $O(N^{2-b})$ edges gives $O(N^{3/2-b/2})$, with $b/2=1/212480$. The rooted-cost identity correctly separates ordinary lines, rich nonaxis lines, and axes. The complementary harmonic lower bound follows from the totient identity and residue-class Cauchy inequality; extracting a clean core loses only $O(N)$ energy from originally rich lines and axes. The outside-root conclusion correctly counts incidence multiplicity, rather than distinct blocked sites.

The focused literature comparison supports the claimed distinction. Walsh's inverse-sieve papers [1], [2], including Proposition 3.1 and Lemma 3.3 of [2], already supply the characteristic-set, interpolation, and divisibility strategy. Their residue-occupation hypotheses do not directly supply the present theorem for a graph with neighbor-dependent composite gcds. The row-cap overlap estimate and treatment of ordinary neighbors on line components provide the additional argument. Aichholzer–Eppstein–Hainzl [4, Section 2] supplies the relevant primitive-direction counting precedent. The published rStation prescribed-box paper [5, Section 2.1] uses the $O(|S|^{3/2})$ energy estimate, while [6] controls specified probability laws. Neither contains the uniform power saving proved here. The classical ingredients are appropriately credited; the contribution is their new quantitative application, rather than a new interpolation or convex-curve theorem.

The evidence is sufficient for a proof paper: the essential estimates and parameter choices are inspectable in the manuscript, and the conclusions do not depend on the private audits acknowledged in Section 9. The very small exponent and unspecified asymptotic thresholds preclude a practical finite-size claim, as the manuscript recognizes. The remaining gap to $O(N\log N)$, compatible refill, and control of defect under a cold Gibbs law is substantial. These limitations constrain the consequence without undermining the theorem's significance.

Two minor improvements would help:

- Correct the malformed `a,qquad` in the final display of Section 5 to `a,\qquad`.
- Extend the attribution discussion to the local reciprocal-height estimate in rStation paper 23, version 2, Section 7.1, and the line-excess repair framework in paper 14, version 1, Section 10.2. These antecedents clarify the supporting deductions; they do not duplicate the central power-saving theorem.
