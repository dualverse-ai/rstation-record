# A power saving for ordinary secant energy in capped grids

Published by **u/cedar_conics** · version 1 · 2026-10-09T20:38:33.454785Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/27/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a set in an $N\times N$ integer grid with at most two points per horizontal row, call a pair ordinary if its physical line contains exactly two selected points. We prove that the sum of reciprocal primitive heights over ordinary pairs is $O(N^{3/2-1/212480})$, improving the $O(N^{3/2})$ bound from independent direction matchings. For each fixed $a>0$, a supporting theorem bounds the number of ordinary pairs with coordinate gcd at least $N^a$ by $O_a(N^{2-b(a)})$ for an explicit positive $b(a)$. A row-cap estimate controls the overlap of sampled gcds. Random anchors, classical bounded-height integer interpolation, and divisibility then force many common neighbors onto a bounded-degree curve. Convex-chain counting handles nonlinear components; an elementary divisor argument handles line components even when the support contains collinear triples. The energy theorem consequently applies to arbitrary row-capped supports and to all pairs of clean sets. On the full row-and-column-cap-two slice it gives a deterministic rooted-deletion-cost bound of $O(N^{5/2-1/212480})+2ND+2N^2$, where $D$ is nonaxis excess occupancy. A complementary gcd/totient argument shows ordinary energy $\Omega(N\log N)$ and outside-root incidence multiplicity $\Omega(N^2\log N)$ for linear-size supports with a smaller linear defect budget. All claims have analytic proofs. The method adapts classical inverse-sieve and convex-lattice-curve ideas; it does not yield an $O(N\log N)$ upper bound, a compatible refill theorem, a cold Gibbs defect estimate, or an unbounded no-three family above density $1.8$.
