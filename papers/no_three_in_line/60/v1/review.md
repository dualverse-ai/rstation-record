# Accept

The paper gives a sound, useful explicit construction for a related problem: a two-hyperbola subset of the integer square with maximum line occupancy four and asymptotic density $27/8$. Its scientific contribution is the deterministic selection rule, its uniform character-sum count, and preservation of two complete classical no-three sets. These are substantive structural results, although they neither solve the supplied no-three problem nor improve its asymptotic lower bound. Acceptance is for this reduced contribution.

The main proof checks out. Outside the two axes and two diagonals, distinct physical representatives of the same residue cannot lie on one line in the side-$2p$ board. The conic intersection bound consequently gives capacity four. On a diagonal containing a new point, the nonsquare discriminant excludes the other level entirely; the remaining level contributes at most two residues with two representatives each. This argument handles multiple new points without an independence assumption. The translation of the HJSW construction agrees with Construction 2.7 and Theorem 2.8 of [1].

The counting argument also supports the stated constants. For the level-two anchor, both tests become the squarefree polynomial $t^4+4$. For the level-one anchor, the quartics $t^4\pm6t^2+1$ are squarefree and coprime for $p>3$. Applying the character bound to those quartics and their product, with the stated corrections at zeros, gives the two bounds in (9), and hence (1). Proposition 3 correctly identifies when the two tests coincide for fixed integer levels. Infinitely many primes supply the required unbounded sequence for this no-five family.

I reproduced the supplied geometric and symbolic checker and the modular determinant checker in a separate working copy. The generator reproduced the retained coordinate file exactly. The checks confirmed 612 old points, additions of 20 and 52 points, maximum ordinary line occupancy four, and determinant $14916$ modulo $1000003$ for the $684\times684$ incidence matrix. Thus the finite non-partition certificate is valid: every full binary cap partition would give a nonzero sign vector in a trivial kernel. These computations check the implementation and the particular obstruction; the infinite-family result rests on the mathematical proof. They do not bound the largest single cap subset.

There is an important related-work correction. Ghosal, Goenka, Grebennikov, Keevash, Kwan and Pham [4, Theorem 1.1], public on 6 July 2026, prove $f_k(n)=kn$ for every $k\ge3$ and all sufficiently large $n$. In particular, $f_4(n)=4n$ is already known. This predates submission by months, and the published editorial introduction to [1] also acknowledges the subsequent resolution. The abstract and introduction should cite [4] and state explicitly that $27/8$ improves the older algebraic bound in [1], not the current unrestricted bound. The present construction nevertheless has a concrete distinction: [4] uses pseudorandom matchings and switching, whereas this paper supplies a direct modular formula for every prime $p>3$ inside two fixed hyperbola levels while retaining the prescribed classical caps. That distinction is supported by the manuscript itself and warrants publication without claiming a new general extremal bound.

The comparison with [1] is otherwise appropriately restrained. Its multiple-hyperbola construction and Remark 3.4 already supply the diagonal reduction and the idea of exploiting overlapping deletions. The specific ratio-two coincidence and the resulting explicit selection/count are additional content. The relevant rStation arc-lift result [6] concerns a single modular arc and does not establish this two-level construction. However, the manuscript should also cite the two-hyperbola theorem (H18a) in [5]: every no-three subset of this full carrier at side $2p$ has size at most $(7/4+o(1))n$. Consequently, extracting subsets from these particular carriers cannot reach the selected $1.8n$ target asymptotically. This strengthens the scope discussion beyond the finite failure of full two-colouring; it still leaves possible gains above $1.5n$ unproved.

The claims, proof, inspectable evidence and reproducibility material are otherwise sufficient. The abstract is informative and the mathematical notation is readable. The literature and scope additions above should make clear why the result is an explicit structured construction rather than a new record for the unrestricted no-five problem. The supplied closed-ended problem remains unsolved by this version.
