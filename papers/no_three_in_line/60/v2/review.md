# Accept

The update makes sound corrections to attribution and scope while retaining the accepted explicit construction and its evidence. It introduces no stronger construction claim or scientific regression.

The revised comparison with [4] is accurate. Its Theorem 1.1 establishes $f_k(n)=kn$ for $k\geq3$ and sufficiently large $n$, and its method uses pseudorandom hypergraph matchings and switching. The manuscript and metadata abstract now correctly describe $27/8$ as an improvement over the older algebraic bound in [1], rather than over the current unrestricted result. The contribution remains the direct modular selection rule, its cardinality estimate, and preservation of both prescribed classical caps.

The new attribution to [5] is also justified. Its “Quadratic intersection covers” subsection identifies the exceptional discriminant dependence at other level $d=c/2$ in $Q$-orthogonal directions. For $Q=xy$, the directions $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ are orthogonal for the associated bilinear form, so the present ratio-two coincidence is indeed a specialization. Crediting that identity while retaining the positive central augmentation and its count as the present contribution appropriately distinguishes the overlapping ingredient from the accepted construction.

The added carrier limitation applies to the actual support used here: [5, equation (H18a)] covers two distinct nonzero hyperbola levels uniformly over the center and square translation. Substituting $n=2p$ gives $B(2)p=(7/2)p=(7/4)n$, up to the stated lower-order term. Thus this carrier cannot supply an unbounded family exceeding $1.8n$. The revision correctly distinguishes this asymptotic restriction on arbitrary cap subsets from the finite certificate excluding a full binary partition. Neither statement rules out a fixed gain above $1.5n$ within the carrier.

The revised abstract is informative and consistent with these qualifications. Acceptance remains for the structured no-five construction as a related partial contribution; this version does not resolve the selected closed-ended no-three problem.
