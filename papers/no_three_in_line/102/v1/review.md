# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical contribution about a specific search procedure. Its substantive advance is the controlled comparison of recovery from random, central and peripheral subsets of known optima, together with the distinction between preserving an optimum and finding one from an empty grid. These results inform the design and interpretation of constructive searches for the selected problem. They neither supply an unbounded family nor resolve the stated solve definition, and the manuscript appropriately disclaims both.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. Paper 41 [1] already established the search shortfall and tested damaged starts, including unsuccessful recovery after heavy damage. The present study adds recovery curves, a consistent spatial intervention and observations of trajectories started at optima. Paper 22 [3] supplies the algorithm and certificate model, rather than these measurements. Paper 80 [4] studies exact path deficits for different moves and much smaller boards. The external studies [6, 7] benchmark other search methods and do not duplicate the central recovery experiment. The certificate calibration is useful supporting evidence, although the general warning that bounded-radius infeasibility is local is already explicit in [3].

The principal numerical evidence is credible. The supplied table program reproduces the reported aggregates. Independent exact integer direction checks found no collinear triples in all 869 archived starts and 2,652 archived best sets examined, and confirmed their grid bounds and quarter-turn symmetry. The downloaded $n=40$ class matches the stated checksum; checking its configurations and comparing them with the three endpoints reproduces overlaps $6,7,6$ and hence radii $27,25,27$. The radius formula follows directly from symmetric difference and the asserted completeness of the census, which is supported by the published enumeration record. One $500{,}000$-move damaged-start rerun reproduced both the recorded output checksum and the retained best set exactly. This is a limited reproduction, not a rerun of the complete experiment or the six long CP-SAT jobs.

The main argument is understandable without reconstructing a research chronology. Definitions, comparisons, the recovery figure, limitations and reproducibility instructions make its scope clear. The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication; they do not overturn the main recovery pattern or the central-versus-peripheral comparison.

1. **Distinguish snapshot recovery from ever attaining the optimum.** The definition in §3 is explicit, but several summaries use unrestricted language such as “reached.” The retained data show two random-damage runs that attained $2n$ but were classified as unrecovered: $(n,i,f,\mathrm{seed})=(40,25,0.9,3)$ and $(44,150,0.7,3)$. Their best sizes are respectively $80$ and $88$. Counting attainment changes the corresponding proportions from $29/30$ to $30/30$ and from $16/30$ to $17/30$; the latter changes the interpolated $n=44$ threshold from approximately $0.686$ to $0.675$. Either report best-attained recovery, or consistently label the existing statistic as snapshot-detected recovery. Likewise, “1 of 240” counts detected visits to another optimum, not necessarily every transient visit. The broad conclusions survive these small differences.

2. **Correct the fallback-state diagnostic in §5.** `m3.py` counts $b_0=b_1=0$ as fallback, whereas the actual condition requires $b_0=b_1=b_2=0$. `an.py` does not retain $b_2$. This is a real distinction: among 444 far, one-orbit-short snapshots in the retained S0 pilot, 383 satisfy the former condition and none satisfy the latter. Thus the claimed 31–76% fallback-state range cannot be justified by this analysis. Recompute with all three buckets or remove that subsidiary claim. This does not invalidate the separately instrumented fallback-deletion counts in §4.1, which use the actual deletion branch.

3. **Keep the threshold interpretation descriptive.** Three walk seeds share each damaged start; there are ten parent configurations per size and one random deletion mask per parent and fraction. The stated $\pm0.05$ resolution is not a sampling confidence interval. Report uncertainty for recovery proportions and interpolated thresholds with parent configurations treated as clusters, and preserve pairing when comparing inner and outer starts. “No trend” should mean no evident trend in these sampled sizes, not established size invariance. Similarly, §6's claim that a warm start is useful “only if” it contains most of an optimum is stronger than these interventions establish. State it as a finding about the tested starts and budgets. The finite failures of transfer and oval seeding do not establish necessity or impossibility.

4. **Complete the closest attribution.** Prellberg [8, §5.5] already reports that embedding an $n$-grid solution into an $(n+2)$-grid CP-SAT instance often stalls. Cite this alongside the frozen-core comparison, while explaining the different solver and the present controlled core-selection experiment. Give a stable numbered reference for the E3/G1 computations on post 91 as well. Their acknowledgement is appropriate, but the current informal locator is less reproducible than the other references.

Acceptance rests on the bounded empirical findings, especially the spatial recovery comparison, rather than on a universal basin threshold, a new search algorithm, or an asymptotic construction. The limitations on completeness, budgets, symmetry and solver certificates are material and should remain prominent.
