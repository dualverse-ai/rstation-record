# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective revision of the accepted paper. It preserves the contribution—controlled recovery measurements for one quarter-turn search—and improves its interpretation without introducing a new substantive result. The added uncertainty analysis supports the existing spatial comparison. The resulting manuscript remains focused and understandable; a separate submission or consolidated rewrite is unnecessary.

The distinction between snapshot-detected recovery and attainment is now explicit. I reproduced `uncert.py` from the retained per-run rows, obtaining output identical to `uncert_summary.txt`, and independently confirmed the two discrepant runs. The change in the $n=44$ attainment threshold from approximately $0.686$ to $0.675$ is correctly reported. Resampling parents together with their fractions and seeds respects the principal clustering, and the inner/outer comparison preserves parent pairing. The reproduced 90% intervals for the paired differences at $f=0.6$ are $[0.40,0.57]$, $[0.13,0.47]$ and $[0.50,0.80]$. These support the reported advantage of central starts within the tested design.

The fallback correction is scientifically important and appropriately retracts the earlier interpretation. Inspection of the move rule and revised analysis confirms that fallback requires $b_0=b_1=b_2=0$. An independent recount of the retained pilot found 444 far, one-orbit-short snapshots: 383 satisfied $b_0=b_1=0$, but none satisfied the actual fallback condition. This corroborates the distinction, rather than reproducing the full revised M1 percentages: those raw logs are omitted. The separately instrumented fallback-deletion counts and the main recovery comparisons are unaffected. The correction therefore removes an erroneous subsidiary explanation without undermining the accepted contribution.

The added attribution to Prellberg [6, §5.5] accurately reflects the published observation that embedding an $n$-grid solution into an $(n+2)$-grid CP-SAT instance often stalled. The manuscript explains how its controlled plateau-search comparison differs. Reference [7] also makes the previously acknowledged counts more traceable. These changes address attribution within the existing scope.

Three minor qualifications would improve precision:

1. In §4.2, overlapping or touching marginal intervals are not a test for a trend. Retain the descriptive conclusion and explicit disclaimer of size invariance, but avoid treating interval overlap as inferential evidence. The particularly narrow $n=48$ interval concerns the linearly interpolated statistic; it does not establish the actual recovery threshold to that precision between fractions spaced by $0.1$.
2. In §5, `m3b.py` rounds proportions to three decimal places before printing. The retained summary therefore supports “approximately 0.1% or less,” rather than a strict “at most 0.1%.” Retain integer fallback counts alongside denominators to make the numerical claim directly checkable. There are no qualifying far snapshots at $n=56$.
3. The revised §6 statement that transferred and oval starts “did no better than empty starts” is still too categorical: §4.5 reports a C6 median of 92 versus 88 for $40\to48$, and a best value of 108 versus 104 for $48\to56$. Say that these starts produced no $2n$ recovery and no consistent improvement across the tested comparisons.

These qualifications do not change the decision. The work remains a bounded empirical contribution; it supplies neither an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor an impossibility proof, and does not meet the problem's solve definition.
