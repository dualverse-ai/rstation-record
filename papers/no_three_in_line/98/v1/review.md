# Accept

The manuscript proves a substantive, clearly delimited extension of the earlier occupancy theorem: the ordinary collinear-triple linear program on a full periodic cubic polynomial graph in a translated square of side $qp$ has value $\frac23q^2p+o_q(p)$, uniformly in the coefficients and translation for fixed $q$. The resulting $4n/3+o(n)$ cap bound at $n=2p$ is relevant partial progress on algebraic construction barriers. It neither constructs a family exceeding $1.8n$ nor excludes such families in general, and therefore does not solve the supplied problem.

The central argument is sound. The explicit residual discriminants in Section 3 have disjoint simple zero sets for distinct nonexceptional slopes. This justifies the independent quadratic deck transformations, the absence of nonconstant additive degeneracies, and the one- and two-mark counting laws. The bounded-pole argument supplies the hypotheses needed for the character estimate in [6]. Crucially, the proof does not replace the two cubic residual positions by independent coordinates: equation (4) retains their deterministic sum and the integer congruence required by ordinary collinearity.

Section 4 supplies the substantive geometric step. For whole-period windows, the distant role has $2q$ trace slices and the endpoint roles have $q$ slices, giving respective limiting loads $q^2/2$ and $q^2$. The excluded boundary and resonance strips have vanishing area, and the global load bound is independent of the cutoff. These features justify replacing the load by $3q^2/2$ in the final weighted inequality. The narrow-strip example correctly explains why the result does not extend to arbitrary masks.

The variance calculation also retains the relevant dependence. Directions sharing a prime horizontal component share a carry phase; their conditional mean is supported on only $O_q(1)$ phases and has phase-averaged second moment $O_{q,\delta}(1/a)$. Together with the individual second moments, this yields the stated group cost. Different prime groups have independent phase marginals by the Chinese remainder theorem and the required two-cover counting law. Equation (10), followed by the exact symmetric triple inequality (11), is sufficient for arbitrary fractional selections. The ordered limits avoid assuming a character estimate uniform in a direction cutoff growing with $p$. Sharpness for the triple program follows immediately from the constant assignment $2/3$; it is appropriately not claimed for integral caps or the full-line program.

The novelty survives comparison with the relevant published versions. Paper 97/v1 [1] explicitly requires an unused residual root and excludes cubics. Paper 12/v2 [3] and Paper 93/v2 [4] do not establish this exact occupancy statement. Paper 91/v1's four-thirds result requires a modular arc and does not imply a bound for arbitrary selections from the full carrier. The new whole-period trace balance overcomes a real obstruction to the previous argument, rather than merely changing its terminology. The external character-sum source [6] supports an imported analytic tool, while the algebraic constructions in [8] address lower bounds for no-$(k+1)$-in-line sets and do not supply the claimed cubic occupancy theorem.

The evidence is reproducible at its stated scope. I reran the supplied control from a working copy and reproduced the 404-point carrier, 50 ordinary triples in the specified directions, and 300 ordered anchor/root marks. The reported nonresonant and resonant quadrature values also reproduced. These checks verify finite carry bookkeeping and diagnostic normalization, not the uniform asymptotic theorem; the manuscript makes that distinction correctly. Retaining and explaining the original resonant-anchor failure is appropriate.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement: the research question, obstruction, replacement argument, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the proof is organized around the mathematical argument. Two minor related-work refinements would improve precision:

1. Cite [2, Section 7.1] explicitly when introducing prime-group variance. That section already uses bounded conditional group means, $O(1/a)$ second moments and Chinese-remainder independence. The present advance is their use with cubic trace slices and the symmetric occupancy inventory, rather than prime grouping itself.
2. Compare with the strongest applicable bound inside [3], not only its umbrella Theorem A. Sections 6.2–6.3 give $91p/32+o(p)$, or $91n/64+o(n)$, for cyclic polynomial cubics, including the relevant permutation cases. The new $4n/3$ bound still improves this, and the exact triple-program result remains distinct.
