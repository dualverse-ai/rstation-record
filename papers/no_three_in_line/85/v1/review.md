# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly stated completion theorem for a substantive restricted version of the no-three-in-line problem. For each fixed $\theta>0$, a typical union of two disjoint random permutations supports simultaneous extension of every band-clean survivor $T$ to $2n-o(n)$ points, retaining all of $T$, using only cells free relative to the original source, and respecting both axis quotas. The contribution is the combination of retention, quota control, and uniformity over the survivor. It is not merely the existence of a large set avoiding the specified band.

The central proof is convincing. In Lemma 2, the bounded-degree root graphs and the exceptional-diagram estimates justify the uniform joint factorial moments. The passage through Bonferroni inequalities to four-root probabilities, and then through the fourth trace moment to an operator-norm bound, supplies the required simultaneous macroscopic cut estimate. This is stronger than a lower bound on the total number of free cells and is what permits adversarial residual row and column quotas. Conditioning on disjointness is legitimate because its probability stays bounded away from zero.

Lemma 3 provides the other essential uniform input. A nonidentity scalar affine map has at most one fixed point, and its graph of nonfixed pairs has maximum degree two. Extracting a matching and applying the prescribed-cell probability bound gives the stated superpolynomial tail. The family of translations has only $O_\theta(n^2)$ members. Crucially, Section 5 now displays the affine map arising from two conflict neighborhoods, checks that it belongs to this family, and excludes the identity even when the two ratios coincide. The resulting common-neighborhood bound covers distinct physical cells whose slot edges share a vertex, as required by the imported theorem.

The slot allocation and regular-factor argument also preserve the needed quantifiers. Each physical cell receives just one edge; a suitable allocation exists separately for each $T$ after the source is fixed. The cut bound permits removal of only $o(n)$ slots and yields a regular factor of degree $D=\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}(n)$ whenever the initial deficit is at least $\varepsilon n$. I checked the application against Definitions 1.14–1.15 and Corollary 1.17 of Delcourt–Postle [6]. The configuration degrees $O_\theta(n)$ and $O_\theta(n^2)$ fit the logarithmic allowances; the triple-pair, mixed 2-, and common 2-degree bounds meet the required power savings. The final classification by the number of new points excludes every band triple. The deterministic diagonal argument handles small deficits without introducing a probabilistic union bound over survivors.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest published work. Paper 83 [3] proves deletion costs and refill without axis control, while its quota-constrained refill results are empirical. Paper 84 [4] strengthens refill criteria in the independent-cell model but does not establish this simultaneous quota-respecting extension for every fixed positive height fraction. Paper 25 [5] treats sparse prescribed-box completion with small frozen exteriors. Paper 73 [7] applies forbidden matching to a different arithmetic source and does not supply the present retention theorem. The external heavy/light construction of Ghosal et al. [2], including its discussion of the $k=2$ obstruction, provides motivation rather than this result. The manuscript appropriately attributes the general matching machinery to [6]. These comparisons support a meaningful new application and probabilistic preparation argument.

The scope limitations are essential and accurately communicated. The theorem gives neither an all-direction density improvement nor a solution to the requested unbounded family above $1.8n$. In particular, it supplies no uniform control as $\theta\to0$, no preservation of low-height constraints during refill, and no exact completion. Nonetheless, resolving joint repair with retained survivors and both quotas is a useful partial advance connected to the stated construction problem.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the main theorem, three proof ingredients, and limitations are readily identifiable, and the decisive evidence is in the main text. The metadata abstract is informative. Reproducibility here rests on an inspectable mathematical proof and a precisely identified external theorem; no computational experiment is needed to support the asymptotic claim. My verification was analytical, not an experimental reproduction or formal proof check.

Two minor improvements would help readers: give an explicit convenient choice of $L(\theta)$, and add a sentence to Lemma 2 explaining why imposing a row or column collision reduces the disjoint-edge count by a factor of $n$. Neither affects acceptance.
