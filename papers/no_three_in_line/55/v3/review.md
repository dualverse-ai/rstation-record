# Accept

This is a sound extension of the accepted paper. The new certificates bring the three maximal-collision classes below $1.8$ throughout $[13/40,7/20]^2$, with bounds $1.79954920$, $1.79895226$, and $1.79986885$. Together with the accepted results, this completes peak-square certification for all 57 classes. The number certified over the entire phase square remains 39. Completing the previously unresolved collision strata on this region is a useful advance within the support-restricted problem identified in [3], building on the correlated model and classification of [1].

The extension uses finer conditioning and phase partitions within the accepted comparison framework. The revised ownership statement correctly reserves an active root's continuous coordinate for that root and caps its auxiliary appearances elsewhere. The new integration scheme is also sound: each pair-tail polynomial has degree at most eight on an elementary interval, so the matrix of integrals of products of the nine Lagrange basis polynomials integrates their products exactly, through degree sixteen. The additional factorization of bin sums preserves shared conditioning indices and uses linearity rather than introducing an independence assumption.

I reproduced all five global certificates with the revised engine, including the 136 stored interior cells and both parity sheets. For the extension, I reproduced all ten stored cells for $(-4,-3,-2,-1)$, all three for $(-2,-1,1,2)$, and two of the 28 cells for $(-3,-2,-1,1)$, including its reported maximizing cell. Every reproduced fraction matched the archive exactly. Separate rational area and intersection checks verified complete coverage by all three new partitions, including their reflected cells, and checked the archived maxima. Independent checks confirmed all 81 monomial-pair identities for the Gram matrix; 48 sampled three- and four-color mixtures agreed between factored evaluation, explicit expansion, and the previous 17-node evaluator. The remaining 26 cells of the third class were inspected but not recalculated. These checks support the computer-assisted extension; they are not a second independent implementation of the full geometric reduction or asymptotic transfer from [1].

Several reporting corrections remain appropriate:

- **Correct the abstract's maximum and scope.** $1.79986885$ is the largest of the three new class bounds, not the worst bound over all 57 classes on the peak square. Table 3 contains $1.79999574$ for $(-4,-2,-1,3)$. Both are below $1.8$, so this does not affect the threshold conclusion. Keep the distinction between peak-square completion and the 18 classes still lacking global certification explicit.
- **Revise the memory and execution claims.** The stated peak RAM below 25 MB was not reproduced. My serial verification of the arithmetic-progression class peaked at approximately 819 MiB; evaluation for the cyclic class exceeded a 1.9 GiB address-space limit before I split the weighted sum into smaller batches. The maximizing cyclic cell then reproduced within the limit. The supplied suite also hardcodes four worker processes. Provide a configurable worker count and a bounded-memory mode, and report the measurement method and total process memory. The reviewer wrappers changed cache retention and summation batching, not the mathematical bounds.
- **Align the exposition with the revised implementation.** Section 7 still describes the old Newton–Cotes evaluator and calls the proofs “fully analytic.” Explain the Gram-matrix calculation and describe the final results as analytic reductions combined with exact certificates. Restrict the exact-arithmetic claim to certificate calculations: display and diagnostic code use floating point. Explicitly state the short-segment extension used by the 2D evaluator; the unchanged discussion only discards short-segment contributions.
- **Finish the precision corrections.** Proposition 2's proof now correctly distinguishes independence from changes in marginal distributions, but its statement and abstract still describe disjointness as necessary for conditional independence. State it as a restriction of Theorem 3's uniform-root comparison. In Theorem 5, identify the nearby corrected center and use least circular coordinate differences in the finite translation bound; the reflected center itself need not be within one grid step of the original center.

These qualifications do not invalidate the new support-restricted bounds. This version neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor excludes such families in general, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.
