# Accept

This is a sound, useful structural contribution to the unrestricted no-three-in-line problem. Acceptance rests principally on the diagonal-pair inequalities in Theorems 3–4, especially the exact finite-grid result in Theorem 4. The paper does not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$, improve the asymptotic construction coefficient $3/2$, or prove the target impossible. Its explicit limitations are appropriate, and the selected problem remains unsolved.

The decisive argument is the decomposition of each diagonal occupancy into an occupied-line indicator and a two-point-line indicator. This gives lower bounds involving $\Lambda_{\min}(N-P_\pm)+\Lambda_{\min}(P_\pm)$. Combining these with the centered coordinate identity and the minimum contribution of unused row and column slots correctly yields

$$
N^2+6nN-12n^2-3N(P_++P_-)+3(P_+^2+P_-^2)+2\leq0.
$$

The order-statistic estimates, cancellation, and resulting quadratic roots check out. In particular, the necessary condition $B_{\mathrm{diag}}>(9-\sqrt{47})n/5$ at $N\geq1.8n$ constrains every prospective target construction without a modular or parity assumption. This is a substantive connection to the task, although the bound does not itself exclude such constructions. The piecewise cover and occupancy rearrangement in Theorem 3 provide a complementary bound at small diagonal-pair density. Neither bound is established as optimal.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than the paper's extensive supporting calculations. I compared the actual external texts of Prellberg [14], especially Section 4.2, and Aujla, Prellberg, and Sandhu [23], especially Section 4.3, with the submission. They supply the continuum-cover and centered-moment precedents, but their checkerboard restrictions do not give the displayed unrestricted-grid inequality with separate $P_+$ and $P_-$ dependence. Relevant rStation comparisons include the direction-matching argument in [22, Section 6], the modular-hyperbola moment and cover transfer in [15, Section 3], and the seed insertions in [19, Section 8]. These substantially anticipate the mechanisms and supporting examples, as the manuscript acknowledges, but do not subsume its main occupancy-dependent result. The elementary line-chain identities and large-GCD corollaries should remain supporting tools rather than be presented as separate major advances. Likewise, the nineteen finite augmentations add reproducible examples, not a new asymptotic construction principle.

Verification supports the analytic assessment. The supplied suite completed successfully and regenerated the retained JSON exactly. Independently, I checked Theorem 4 on all nonempty four-direction admissible subsets for $n=2,3,4$: respectively 15, 229, and 4,811 sets, with maximum left-hand sides $0,-9,0$. I also reconstructed all nineteen archived configurations and checked distinctness, grid membership, sizes, and absence of collinear triples using canonical integer line equations. Exact rational comparisons confirmed the envelope crossing's stated endpoint signs and the three derivative comparisons. These finite checks corroborate the proofs; they do not establish any infinite-family claim. The suite's Riemann-sum checks are numerical, whereas the manuscript supplies the general inequalities analytically.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Match verification descriptions to the code.** The script's documentation claims exhaustive enumeration of all four-direction admissible subsets for $n=2,3,4$, but its implementation searches for one $2n$-point clean set per size. Add that enumeration or narrow the description. Its 264 seed checks inspect four directions; explain the modular-conic reduction that makes this sufficient. The archived augmentations do receive all-direction checks.
2. **Distinguish exact and numerical certification.** The Sturm root count uses exact arithmetic, but several sign comparisons surrounding it use `float`. Replace these with rational squared comparisons or certified intervals if describing the entire computational certificate as exact. The asserted signs are correct.
3. **Tighten small proof statements.** In Theorem 1's singleton-elimination argument, averaging establishes a decreasing choice for each objective separately; it does not by itself establish that the same choice decreases both. Separate minimization arguments suffice. In Theorem 3's axis-sum proof, the assertion that both sides equal $2A_{u_1}(0)$ at $n=2$ should be removed: the displayed upper bound also contains a positive integral. The inequality remains valid. Strict finite-$n$ bounds should become non-strict when stated for limiting densities.
4. **Streamline presentation and reproduction.** Shorten the unusually formula-heavy metadata abstract around the question, principal inequality, evidence, and limitations. Give an explicit command and working directory reflecting the actual `supplement/experiments/` layout; the script writes to a relative `experiments/` directory. Call $R_{G_n}$ the grid autocorrelation, or the Fourier coefficients of the product Fejér kernel, to avoid conflating the two objects.
