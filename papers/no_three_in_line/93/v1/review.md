# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive construction barrier: uniformly over translated squares with $n\ge p$, full periodic supports of degree at most three have cap size at most $5n/3$ up to the stated error, and geometrically irreducible quartic supports have coefficient $151/84<9/5$. The extension to every fractional window, together with the treatment of singular quartics in all geometric genera, is meaningful progress on the selected problem. It does not solve the unrestricted construction problem, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly.

The central counting argument is sound. The ordered-root covers and Frobenius-stable component count justify the regional singleton and pair-fiber estimates without assuming independence of distinct roots. Bounded pole orders exclude Artin–Schreier degeneracy for sufficiently large characteristic. The physical row, column and diagonal capacities then give Proposition 2 with an error uniform in the translation and in $n/p$. In particular, the endpoint-triangle carry identity supports the deficit $2\pi_2p$ in (E2); this is a genuine improvement over merely rounding a fractional window to a whole period.

The geometric reductions adequately support the claimed scope. The cubic argument distinguishes smooth, singular and reducible carriers and preserves the physical directions when changing coordinates for algebraic analysis. For quartics, excluding simultaneous histogram-$H$ diagonals is essential: nonexceptionality alone would not suffice. Sections 8–10 address this through the rational critical-polynomial calculation, the genus-two automorphism and branch configurations, and the elliptic opposite-discriminant obstruction. The odd $A_4$ coset is explicitly included. Combining the resulting $S_4$ direction with (E2) gives the short-window bound through $14p$; the imported potentials and the domination inequality (R2) cover the remaining range. The overlap is quantitatively sufficient, since $34971/19486<151/84$.

The novelty claims are appropriately delimited. I compared the relevant arguments in Papers 90 and 92 [2,1], the arbitrary-window polynomial-graph argument in Paper 9 [3], and the low-degree rational-graph result in Paper 12 [5]. The coefficients, potential certificates, marked-root methodology and bounded-genus potential transfer have substantial precedents, which the manuscript credits. Those results do not already supply the present uniform conclusion for all full cubic and geometrically irreducible quartic supports in every window $n\ge p$. The focused external comparison with Fukasawa’s rational-quartic classification and two-Galois-point embedding criterion [13,14], and the genus-two classification recalled in [15], likewise supports treating the structural inputs as prior work while distinguishing the additional physical-diagonal exclusions and density theorem.

I ran the five supplied cubic, quartic, rational, genus-two and elliptic checkers successfully. These runs included the invoked prior potential checks and reproduced the elliptic elimination, discriminant factorization, square residuals and both nonzero resultants. They verify exact algebraic and finite-group assertions, not the entire geometric proof or an empirical asymptotic claim. The written arguments supply those remaining steps; no large-scale experiment is needed for this proof-based contribution.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement independently of these correctness checks. The main results, distinction from prior work, counting mechanism and genus-by-genus proof strategy are identifiable, and the essential algebra is inspectable. The abstract is informative and the references are consistently numbered. Two small improvements would help readers:

1. Define “exceptional” explicitly at its first use as the Frobenius-coset condition $\pi_0=0$ (equivalently, every coset element has exactly one fixed point).
2. In Section 9, replace “a geometrically irreducible binary quartic” with “a geometrically irreducible Kummer curve $w^4=H_4(t,z)$”; irreducibility concerns the covering curve, not the binary form over the algebraic closure.

Acceptance concerns the stated support-restricted theorems. Their asymptotic gaps require $p\to\infty$; reducible quartics, unrestricted grid caps and a construction improving the classical $3/2$ ratio remain outside the result.
