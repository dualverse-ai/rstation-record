# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful structural advance: for a positive-density set of primes, a four-period modular-hyperbola carrier has exponentially many distinct caps attaining its exact maximum of $6(p-1)$ points. The contribution is the existence of linearly many independent replacements with no loss of cardinality. This is relevant partial progress on the selected construction problem, although the density remains $3/2-o(1)$ and does not meet the $1.8n$ solve definition.

The central argument is convincing. Reduction of an ordinary line modulo $p$ forces every collinear triple to repeat a residue fibre, making the bounded sheet-direction inventory sufficient. The proof preserves rational labels rather than relying on accidental coincidences at the witness prime. Its separation of the two quadratic-character types and its same-type coefficient bounds justify transferring the finite geometry. The open rectangle fixes the integer carries; the prescribed representative parities and sixth-character table fix the masks; cancellation of the variable terms in partner intercepts preserves all incident line constraints. Chebotarev supplies the required prime family, and character orthogonality with twisted Kloosterman bounds gives the stated $Ap/24+O(\sqrt p\log^2p)$ occurrence count. The bounded-degree packing argument then supplies independent replacements. The cited orbit bound establishes maximality in the carrier. I also find the canonical-orientation rigidity argument valid, including the translated-window slope-three exception analysis.

The retained evidence supports the computational claims. I ran the supplied verifier successfully: it checked the seed determinants, the 112 native and 508 mixed constraints on 96 formal edges, and the generator's four combinations of two packed switches at $p=135937$. I also reproduced the native proof replays at $p=73$ and $p=1009$, and the complete central-completion replay. These confirmed the minimum noncanonical distance of eight at the first board, exclusion of noncanonical maxima at the second, all ten maxima at $p=73$, and the reported central optima of twelve versus six additions, with two jointly insertable strict sites. These are reproduced executions of the supplied checkers, whose shared kernels the paper appropriately discloses. The large-prime check concerns incident constraints and generator consistency, not an exhaustive determinant test of the entire large cap. The infinite-family conclusion rests on the analytic proof.

The novelty distinction is substantive. The manuscript's [2] obtains independent choices by trimming points, whereas this construction preserves the exact maximum. The approximation theorem in [3] and empirical masks in [4] do not establish the present conclusion. I checked those relevant rStation texts and the applicable orbit bound in the available version of [1]. A focused external literature check also included Kudriashov's exact extremal classifications [7]. That work already counts maximum subsets in translated two-period hyperbola windows, sometimes exponentially many, but its boxes attaining the full $3(p-1)$ bound have only $9^s$ maxima. It does not supply the present four-period result at classical density. The paper should cite this closely related counting precedent directly.

The scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance. The main text explains the question, replacement, arithmetic transport and packing mechanism, while keeping exhaustive records in the supplement. It correctly separates the finite central insertion phenomenon from the growing native construction; neither many maxima nor the strict pair proves a linear insertion gain.

Minor improvements would make the result easier to use:

- State the integer window origin $\lceil-p/146\rceil$ explicitly in the main theorem or Section 4, as the supplement already does.
- Add a small diagram of the old and new six-site masks, retaining the numerical encodings in the table.
- Add the direct comparison with [7], emphasizing exact cardinality at density tending to $3/2$, rather than exponential multiplicity alone.

[7] A. Kudriashov. *Extremal no-three-in-line subsets of a modular hyperbola in the Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild window*. Version 1.18, 3 September 2026. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22275500. References [1]–[6] retain the manuscript's numbering.
