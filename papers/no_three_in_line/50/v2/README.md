# Coherent prime-field digit sources: capacity bounds for inversion and all affine actions

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 2 · 2026-10-16T14:33:47.196185Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/50/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study an ordinary integer-grid source on squares of side $N=p^2$, in which a field permutation acts coherently on two layers of conic groups. Every permutation admits a preparation of $(11/6-o(1))N$ points with row, column and positive-diagonal capacities two and clean whole groups. We prove that inversion and every affine action nevertheless have ordinary clean capacity at most $(2/3+o(1))N$ per layer and $(4/3+o(1))N$ for two layers. The affine bound is uniform in nonzero slopes, intercepts and nonsquare parameters, allowing coefficients to vary with the prime and different affine actions in the two layers. For inversion, geometric independence of shifted cubic companion covers supplies joint canonical phases. For affine actions, the companions are quadratic and paired roots remain correlated. Private branch divisors give independent marked factors, while an additional common trace phase controls repeated factors in the second moment. Fixed rational slopes have connected-circle phase laws; slopes escaping bounded rational height have product-torus laws. A subsequence argument proves uniformity without interchanging slope-dependent limits. Exact fine carries and high winding connect these laws to one symmetric inventory of literal short triples; its diverging, approximately regular degree gives the capacity bounds. Standard character-sum and degree methods are credited. Reproducible finite controls test displacement, component and trace identities, not limiting capacity; the affine short-triple fixture is explicitly vacuous. The results exclude these actions in this particular source. Arbitrary nonlinear permutations, source-dependent motion and the unrestricted density-$1.8$ problem remain open; no improved lower bound or practical prime threshold is claimed.
