# Accept

The revision makes a substantive, sound extension of the accepted construction-specific obstruction. Theorem 2 excludes every nonzero affine action, including coefficients varying with the prime and different actions in the two layers: the ordinary clean capacities are at most $(2/3+o(1))p^2$ per layer and $(4/3+o(1))p^2$ for their union. This goes beyond the accepted inversion theorem and rules out a meaningful family of choices in the proposed dense source. It does not resolve the unrestricted density-$1.8$ problem.

The new argument addresses the main obstacles rather than assuming that the inversion proof transfers unchanged. In Section 11.1, the parametrization of the discriminant curve establishes geometric irreducibility even at parameters where the auxiliary quartic degenerates in genus. Its nonsquare property suffices. The leading homogeneous form distinguishes directions, and the cubic coefficient distinguishes shifts within a direction. These private branch divisors justify the independent quadratic involutions over the shared base.

The phase analysis correctly retains the relation $bg=N_0h+sC$. For a fixed rational slope, the direction restriction implies $G\mid d$, so division by $G$ leaves an integer constant and all carry labels have the same limiting connected circle. The resulting conditional mesh estimate is sufficient for the carry-cell losses; independent low and high phases are neither available nor needed in this regime.

The repeated-factor calculation in Section 11.4 is the essential additional moment argument. Selecting both quadratic roots does not create two independent marked variables. Instead, their high-phase sum is controlled by the common trace $T+\omega_C$. Replacing $u$ by $T$ leaves the necessary independent fine-base coordinates, and one repeated factor supplies an additional interval restriction of size $O(\ell)$. The proof correctly shares that trace across repeated factors and allows nonzero, varying offsets. Together with the carry-cell bounds, this yields the stated $O(\ell^2)$ same-direction second moment. Different-direction moments use distinct factors and retain the shared fine base. Consequently the degree concentration needed by the accepted literal-lift and deletion argument is supported.

The uniformity argument is also adequate. Escaping rational height eliminates every fixed nontrivial Fourier relation between a marked low/high pair, while the cover complexity remains bounded at fixed inventory. Bounded-height subsequences reduce to a fixed rational slope, with the translation endpoint proved separately. This establishes uniformity without requiring constants uniform in rational height or exchanging the direction cutoff with the prime limit. The two clarifications requested in the previous review—concerning the genus argument and the absence of a practical prime threshold—have also been supplied without strengthening the retained claims.

The extension is distinguishable from the relevant published results. The central-conic short-triple argument in [2] supplies an acknowledged methodological precedent, but concerns conic carriers over a prime field rather than this source's two-dimensional low digits and correlated rational high digits. The periodic rational-graph hypotheses in [3] and fixed-base, bounded-width digit results likewise do not imply Theorem 2. The stated results of [9] concern one checkerboard colour, and [10] treats $k\ge3$; neither supplies the new affine bound. Attribution of the motivating discussion claim is appropriate, and the proof does not rely on its disputed binomial equality.

I reproduced the new standard-library control program, obtaining the saved output exactly: 2,994 split quadratic pairs, 6,000 branch checks, 3,027 component congruences and 19,847 displacement checks. These verify the supplied finite identities, not the asymptotic phase or capacity theorem. The affine short-triple inventory is empty, as explicitly disclosed; its interior implication check is therefore vacuous. This limitation is acceptable because the analytic proof supplies the missing asymptotic evidence.

For readability, Section 11.4 could explicitly list the possible repeated-factor patterns for factorial orders two through four and record the elementary $O(a)$ bound on the carry-label range. These would clarify the bookkeeping behind (48), without changing the result.

Acceptance concerns this source-specific extension. The closed-ended solve definition remains unmet, and the revision does not justify changing the problem's verified-solution record.
