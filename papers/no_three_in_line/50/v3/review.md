# Accept

The update makes a substantive, sound extension of the accepted source-specific obstruction. Theorem 3 establishes the $2/3$ single-layer and $4/3$ two-layer capacity bounds for the reciprocal-offset source with its relocated carry. Theorem 4 then excludes every binary row mask in that source at coefficient $16/9<1.8$, uniformly in the mask, field gap and other parameters. Allowing an unrestricted mask is a meaningful enlargement of the excluded family, rather than a cosmetic variation of the previously accepted affine result.

The new geometric argument supplies the additional ingredients needed for Theorem 3. The nonsquare rational function in (59) proves geometric irreducibility even at the genus-zero specializations. Excluding (56) removes at most two projective directions and prevents the extra vertical discriminant component at $w=0$. The pole restrictions and degree-14 leading form support the extension of the private-branch argument over the shared base. Crucially, Section 12.2 replaces the old low-degree phase argument with the appropriate two-pole comparison: (65) can be constant on all three companion roots only through a scalar multiple of the transverse map, and the two residues force (66), hence vanishing of the marked coefficients. The restriction to at most two marked roots per cubic is retained where the moment argument needs it.

The physical transfer is also justified. Moving the carry changes ordinary collinearity, so the accepted source's lifting formula would not suffice by itself. Equations (67)–(69) retain both the fine carry and high winding for the new coordinates. The revised carry cells, row-capacity bound and removal of at most two exceptional low columns provide the inputs needed by the accepted short-triple degree argument. Fixed-inventory uniformity and the bounded number of excluded directions support the stated prime-dependent parameter range without requiring a growing-inventory character-sum estimate.

Theorem 4's treatment of arbitrary masks is particularly useful. The rational Weil bound and Parseval control the convolution uniformly for every binary function, including the separate case $\eta=0$. This establishes the pair law (75) without a complexity assumption on the mask. Both column charges use only pair correlations; no four-bit independence is assumed. Combining them with the majority constant-source bound gives the claimed $16/9$ coefficient. The exceptional gaps $0,1,-1$ are handled separately, and the $2E$ edit allowance and resulting $1/90$ necessary edit fraction follow directly from the number of points introduced at each changed base.

Proposition 5 is a correct application of the prime-field case of [11, Theorem 1.1], whose statement I checked against the cited author-hosted paper. The common missing projection value permits extension to a full graph, after which the direction bound forces affinity. Including $d=0$ is essential to this argument, as the manuscript explicitly explains. The result is appropriately presented as exact rigidity, with no unsupported near-matching stability claim. The new results are also distinct from [12]: that paper treats specified affine actions and fixed integer offsets in the original source, rather than the reciprocal source and arbitrary binary row masks considered here. The standard direction, character-sum and degree-counting ingredients are credited.

I reproduced all four new control programs, obtaining the saved JSON outputs exactly. The two carry controls verify 30,696 and 30,424 displacement identities, respectively; their 36 and 56 formal short triples include 33 and 39 literal triples, with the remaining triples confined to the boundary. The binary control verifies 10,580 column-load and pointwise-inequality cases, and the symbolic control reproduces the cubic, critical discriminant, leading homogeneous form and pole restrictions. These computations verify finite algebra and lifting identities, not geometric irreducibility or asymptotic capacities. The manuscript correctly places the latter burden on its analytic proof.

Acceptance concerns this enlarged construction-specific obstruction. General row offsets, arbitrary base-dependent motion and the unrestricted density-$1.8$ problem remain open. No improved lower bound or practical prime threshold follows, and the closed-ended solve definition remains unmet; this update does not warrant changing the verified-solution record.
