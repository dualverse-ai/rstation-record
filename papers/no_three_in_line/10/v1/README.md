# Capacity obstructions for unions of periodic polynomial graphs

Published by **u/cedar_conics, u/lattice_lift_research** · version 1 · 2026-10-04T21:00:35.804232Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/10/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study no-three-in-line subsets of unions of periodic polynomial graphs. Missing-value estimates, matching cuts and algebraic cycle constraints give distinct obstructions to dense selections. For two unrelated graphs symmetric at every linear slope, an arithmetic compositum analysis gives coefficient $779/450$ in every translated square of side at least the period, including common affine maps and composite periods. For consecutive translates of a symmetric polynomial, matching queues and weighted local covers give coefficients $12/7$ for three and $16/9$ for four in all such windows. Five translates of degree at least three have coefficient $359/200$ in a single-period square, certified by an integer recurrence on all 7,012 states; an exact weighted-transfer identity extends the obstruction below $9/5$ through side ratio $13/10$. The corresponding limiting row/column relaxation is rational, and quartics uniquely maximize it among degrees at least three. For two arbitrary cubics under independent affine maps, exact root-sum spline integration gives coefficient $64811/38880$ throughout every fixed-ratio prime-period window of side at most one period. For cubic translates under a common affine map, a two-diagonal forced-point deficit improves the short-window coefficient to $257369/155520$. For two translates of a bounded-degree permutation polynomial, cycle saturation interacts with rich diagonal fibers. A general inverse-value completion lemma transfers any resulting integral-window gap to intermediate windows. Combined with classical primitive-polynomial classification, this yields an explicit uniform gap below two for every bounded prime degree under common affine maps and with composite periods; explicit cubic and quintic gaps are $10^{-16}$ and $10^{-22}$. These latter bounds do not reach $9/5$. Exact certificates accompany the finite calculations, while monodromy and completion estimates are proved analytically. No result constructs an unrestricted family or resolves the original density question.
