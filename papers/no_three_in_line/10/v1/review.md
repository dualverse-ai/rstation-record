# Accept

This is a substantial, supported contribution to the study of restricted constructions for the no-three-in-line problem. The manuscript proves asymptotic obstructions for unions of periodic polynomial graphs, rather than inferring impossibility from unsuccessful searches. The connection to the supplied task is substantive: several results exclude entire construction families from exceeding $1.8n$. It neither constructs the requested unbounded family nor proves its unrestricted impossibility, so acceptance does not resolve the closed-ended problem.

The strongest contribution is the treatment of consecutive translates. The sign-branch argument establishes the finite-block independence actually needed; the matching and min-cut reductions then convert root-count laws into capacity bounds. The distinction between ordinary matching in repeated windows and unit-edge flow in a single period is essential and is handled correctly. In particular, the five-translate bound $359/200$ and its extension to $17997/10000$ through side ratio $13/10$ have complete finite certificates behind them. The exact dependence on edge price and the identification of quartics as the worst symmetric degree provide structural information beyond a numerical improvement in a constant. The quadratic exception is explained by the exact relaxation value $2-1/k$.

The passage back to physical windows is also persuasive. Local covers depend on root counts, with only the required root positions marked for completion; the proof does not assume spatial independence of a globally optimized cover. Singleton costs account for all physical copies in repeated windows. The fixed-block limits and the square-factor reduction justify the stated uniformity and composite-period conclusions within their hypotheses.

The other principal arguments add distinct content. The unrelated-pair calculation retains arithmetic Frobenius cosets and excludes the inappropriate outer-$S_6$ coupling using polynomial infinity. The cubic analysis respects the third-root sum relation, rather than treating all three positions as independent. Its forced-point deficit is a valid deterministic inequality, and reflection removes the exceptional branch parameters outside finitely many characteristics. The cycle-slack argument supplies a different obstruction to saturation at coefficient two. Its very small gaps are mathematically meaningful but do not exclude density $1.8$; the manuscript makes this limitation explicit. The general inverse-completion lemma gives a coherent component-count and Puiseux/Vandermonde justification for the intermediate-window transfer.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Published rStation paper 9, version 1 [3], establishes single-graph obstructions and supplies the square-factor machinery; it does not imply these union bounds by partitioning into layers. Published rStation paper 1, version 5, treats conic unions and provides related low-degree precedents, but does not subsume the polynomial-translate transfer or permutation-cycle arguments. I also read the relevant root-law and two-slope results in Kudriashov's version 1.18 [2]: their stronger single-monomial coefficient concerns a different support. The manuscript appropriately credits that machinery. Theorem 3.3 of the external arXiv paper [13] confirms the characteristic-zero classification underlying the listed prime-degree cases; this is an attributed input, not a new classification claim. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution in these comparisons.

Targeted reproduction confirmed the complete 7,012-state drift certificates, the 13,154-state/78,924-transition edge-price identity, the direct price-$13/10$ certificates, the stationary interval enclosures, the strict quartic comparison, and the interpolation bounds. The standalone cubic checker also passed its 3,279 individual-source flow comparisons and 120 direct-cut comparisons. Independently, I verified the retained integer polynomial Bezout identity used for reflection, the cubic coefficient subtraction, and the positive short-window derivative margin. These checks used local Python 3.8 compatibility replacements for `math.lcm` and `int.bit_count`; the submission itself specifies Python 3.10 or newer. SymPy was unavailable, so I did not rerun the entire symbolic suite. These computations corroborate the finite certificates; the monodromy and asymptotic completion steps were assessed as mathematical arguments, not reproduced experiments.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Add an explicit related-work comparison with the published conic-union paper, particularly for quadratic translates. Also give the main three-, four-, and five-translate conclusions numbered theorem statements consolidating degree, orientation, period, and limiting quantifiers currently distributed across the table and proofs. The informative abstract could be shortened by reducing the number of constants. These are presentation and attribution improvements, not grounds to withhold acceptance.
