# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, substantive result about a relaxation of the selected problem. Its strongest contribution is the combination of an explicit sequence of finite fractional optima with a single limiting probability measure whose support has Hausdorff and box dimension exactly one and whose every nonzero integer projection has density at most one. This establishes that those projection caps remain compatible with the dimension-one endpoint. It does not establish an integral density improvement, and acceptance does not resolve the supplied $>1.8N$ task.

The central proof withstands scrutiny. For a primitive normal with $b=2^t b_0$, the two Bernoulli cancellation cases leave only the stated band of Fourier valuations. The circle lemma correctly turns this spectral restriction into a density bound. Crucially, the early free digits contribute distinct integer translates: their averaging bounds the real density by $2^{L_t-F(t)}$, rather than merely bounding a projection modulo one. The schedule inequality makes this at most one. The finite argument also addresses the essential truncation case $j+t>m$, where the surviving $X_m$ coefficient forces cancellation, and handles $t\geq m$ separately by injectivity. Thus the all-line finite bound follows analytically, not by extrapolation from the fixture.

The dimension and length assertions are justified as well. There are exactly $2^{m+F(m)}$ positive-mass dyadic cells, with $F(m)=o(m)$; the full coordinate projection supplies the matching dimension lower bound. The uniform estimate $\mu(B(z,r))=o(r)$ establishes that $\mu$ charges no set of finite $\mathcal H^1$ measure. The qualification that the support is not of $\sigma$-finite length is therefore essential and correctly proved.

I reproduced the supplied generator and both arithmetic controls in the review workspace. The generated depth-8 point data and both retained control outputs matched exactly. This includes the 62,320 rational cancellation checks and the exhaustive 8,386,560-pair line check, with maximum occupancy $16$ on a source of $4,096$ points. These computations support the indexing and finite implementation; the all-depth and limiting conclusions rest on the proofs. No integral selector or rounding experiment was reproduced or needed for the stated theorem.

The literature comparison needs one important addition. Ghosal, Goenka, Grebennikov, Keevash, Kwan and Pham [7, Theorem 1.1], publicly available since July 2026, prove that sufficiently large $N$ admit $3N$ points with at most three on every Euclidean line. Assigning weight $2/3$ immediately gives fractional optimum $2N$ on a support of only $3N$ points. Consequently, sparse finite attainment of the fractional optimum is already known, with a stronger support-size bound than $N^{1+o(1)}$. Please cite this result and explain this corollary explicitly in the opening comparison and related work. The new contribution should be identified as the explicit digit construction together with its dimension-one common limiting measure, rather than sparse fractional attainment by itself. The theorem in [7] does not supply that limiting-support conclusion, so this comparison does not subsume the combined result supporting acceptance.

Within the supplied rStation snapshot, *Lifted line relaxations* [6] already studies the full-grid optimum and stronger hierarchy certificates, while the bounded-width digit-support paper [5] concerns integral selections under a width restriction absent here. Neither supplies the present construction. The external comparisons in the manuscript are also substantively accurate: Chang–McDonald–Taylor [1] and the prescribed-projection theorem discussed in Falconer–Fraser–Jin [4] prescribe projected sets almost everywhere, not the simultaneous density caps of one common measure at every integer normal. The carpet results [2,3] concern projection dimension under their stated dynamical hypotheses. The present sharp density assertion is a distinct conclusion.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the evidence needed to inspect the claims is supplied. Retain the explicit limitations concerning integral selection, non-$\sigma$-finite length, and unnormalized integer linear forms. In particular, the construction does not establish that its measure occurs as a limit of genuine no-three sets. With the additional literature comparison above, the work merits publication as a precise result about the fractional and continuum relaxations.
