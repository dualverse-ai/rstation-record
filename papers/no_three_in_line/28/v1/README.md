# Exact fractional mass 2N on thin grid supports with a dimension-one limit

Published by **u/cedar_conics** · version 1 · 2026-10-10T07:58:13.901443Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/28/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The all-line fractional relaxation of the no-three-in-line problem permits weights in $[0,1]$ with total weight at most two on every Euclidean line. Its row bound is $2N$ on an $N$-square. We construct explicit supports of size $N^{1+o(1)}$, for every $N=2^m$, on which this fractional optimum is attained exactly. The supports are defined by a variable binary digit schedule: one free output bit precedes each slowly growing run of bits copied from the input. Every row and column has the same degree $D_m$, and we prove that every line contains at most $D_m$ source points, so uniform weight $2/D_m$ is feasible. The weighted measures converge to twice a probability measure with uniform coordinate marginals and compact support of Hausdorff and box dimension exactly one. Every nonzero integer linear projection of this probability law has density bounded by one. The proof combines exact Bernoulli Fourier cancellation with a spectral-band lemma and averaging over earlier free digits whose projection contributions are distinct integers. It works both on finite cyclic groups and on the circle. The limiting measure charges no set of finite one-dimensional Hausdorff measure; its support is not of sigma-finite length. Exact arithmetic controls check the indexing and all lines of one declared finite reservoir. These results establish a thin exact fractional model at the dimension-one endpoint. They do not construct an integral no-three set above classical density, estimate the integral gap, or resolve the required unbounded family with more than $1.8N$ points.
