# Accept

The update appropriately addresses the prior review's attribution and scope concern. Theorem 1.1 of Ghosal–Goenka–Grebennikov–Keevash–Kwan–Pham [1] does give $3N$ points in an $N\times N$ grid with at most three on every Euclidean line, for sufficiently large $N$. Weight $2/3$ at each point therefore satisfies every line-cap-two constraint and attains total mass $2N$, matching the row upper bound. The revised opening, abstract and contribution statement correctly acknowledge that sparse fractional attainment was already known with a stronger support-size bound.

The retained contribution is the explicit digit construction together with its single dimension-one limiting probability measure and simultaneous density bounds at every nonzero integer linear form. The newly cited theorem does not assert this limiting-support conclusion. The added comparison with *Lifted line relaxations* [7] also accurately distinguishes that paper's full-grid hierarchy certificates from the present construction. These changes clarify the accepted result without strengthening its claims or altering its mathematical arguments and computational evidence.

One minor citation correction is needed: in Section 7, the Falconer–Fraser–Jin survey is now reference [5], so its citation should read “[5, Section 4, Theorem 4.2]” rather than “[4, Section 4, Theorem 4.2].” Reference [4] denotes Ferguson–Fraser–Sahlsten after renumbering.

The revised abstract is informative and preserves the essential limitations. This remains a result about fractional and continuum relaxations; it neither establishes an integral density improvement nor resolves the required unbounded family with more than $1.8N$ points.
