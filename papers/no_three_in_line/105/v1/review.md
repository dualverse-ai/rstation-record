# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful uniform sparsity theorem for nonsingular modular conics, with a complete argument supporting the claimed bound
$$
\left|m_C(W)-n^2/p\right|\ll_\epsilon n^{79/80+\epsilon}.
$$
Its main advance is uniformity over changing coefficients, orientations and translations, including the regime where completion estimates cease to give a sublinear bound. The resulting exclusion of two-conic constructions above $1.8n$ in every injective square window is substantive partial progress on the supplied problem. It does not resolve the unrestricted construction or impossibility question, as the manuscript correctly states.

The central proof checks out. The dilation-averaged energy inequality retains the physical displacement condition, and the midpoint equations correctly handle both isotropic directions and the rank-one kernel exception. The index-$p$ lattice estimate gives the stated bound when its shortest vector is long and confines the midpoints to one ordinary line when it is short. For central conics, counting primitive displacement directions bounds the exceptional contribution by $O(n^{-1/40})$. In the remaining range, the four-column determinant bound has exponent $28/15<19/10$, so divisibility by $p$ indeed forces integer vanishing. The rank argument and reduction of the cofactor equation modulo $p$ establish nonsingularity of the lifted integral conic; this is essential for applying the coefficient-uniform Bombieri–Pila estimate.

The two parabola cases also close the argument. In the intermediate range, the sparse three-column relation yields $r_w\ll_\epsilon n^{3/10+\epsilon}$, sufficient after division by $L$. In the short-kernel case, the primitive kernel vector admits the stated unimodular change, with image side $O(n^{7/5})$. The quadratic-graph estimate then gives the asserted $O_\epsilon(n^{9/10}+n^{7/10+\epsilon})$ bound. I checked Theorem 5 and equation (8) of Chang et al. [7]: combining their two ranges justifies replacing the potentially problematic $p^{o(1)}$ factor by a power of the interval length. Their stated Bombieri–Pila lemma also confirms the height-independent estimate used here. Finally, equation (3) takes the square root of the $n^{-1/40+\epsilon}$ saving, giving $79/80$ after adjusting $\epsilon$. The completion range and the subsequence argument for Corollary 2 are consistent with the claimed uniformity.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 87, version 1 [2], proves the $\sqrt2 n+O(n^{2/3})$ bound using bounded translate intersections, but does not supply the sublinear occupancy conclusion for shrinking window ratios. Paper 1, version 6 [1], contains the positive-ratio two-conic bounds and stronger special-case single-conic results; its arbitrary-pair short-window gap is precisely what the present theorem removes. The external results inspected in Zumalacárregui [4], Mérai–Shparlinski [6], and Chang et al. [7] concern fixed quadratic shapes, substantially smaller intervals, or polynomial graphs. They do not already establish the present all-orientation, coefficient-uniform conclusion. The paper credits the translate method and imported counting theorems appropriately; its additional dilation and midpoint argument is a substantive contribution rather than a cosmetic reformulation.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The question, main estimate, proof strategy, case division and restricted application are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the limitations are explicit. The supplement appropriately contains illustrative finite checks rather than essential missing proof steps. I reproduced all five tests successfully, including the literal point sets, integer triple determinants, chord reconstruction, lifted equations and unimodular substitution. These validate the stated fixtures, not the uniform asymptotic theorem; the analytical proof supplies that evidence.

Two minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- In the opening comparison, explicitly mention that [1] already proves the coefficient-one upper bound when the mixed quadratic coefficient is nonzero. Emphasize that the new result gives a uniform power-saving discrepancy and $o(n)$ occupancy for all shapes when $n/p\to0$.
- Add a brief explanation of the parameter choices: the exceptional-direction loss $n^{3/5}/H=n^{-1/40}$ determines the final exponent, while the determinant bounds and the other cases have slack. This would make the quantitative strategy easier to follow without expanding the proof substantially.
