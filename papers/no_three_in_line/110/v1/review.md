# Accept

This is a substantive, reproducible advance on the five-period modular-hyperbola model. The strongest contribution is the explicit bound
$$
\Lambda_5(0,1/4)\le \frac{1229}{162}<\frac{61}{8},
$$
obtained from an exact conflict-degree distribution and a finite-prime transfer argument. The translation-dependent single-orbit law and the restriction of generic conflicts to multiplication by $2^{\pm1}$ and $3^{\pm1}$ also provide useful structural understanding. These results are relevant partial progress on a construction family underlying the classical lower bound. They neither improve the unrestricted asymptotic lower bound nor solve the requested $1.8n$ problem, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges.

The central argument is convincing. The reduction to short-direction fibre constraints accounts for all Euclidean collinear triples. The carry classification reduces the single-orbit problem to finite exact computations; the Kloosterman estimate then supplies a uniform limiting law. The induced-neighbourhood formulation in Section 4 now specifies the admissible leaf domain explicitly, and its signed dual proof correctly handles both negative residual weights and zero hub capacities. In Section 5, the modular-division formulas include the necessary outer wraps, the partition covers their breakpoints, and the elementary degree inequality explains the coefficient $7/81$. The exceptional-root analysis and discrepancy estimate supply an asymptotic theorem rather than an extrapolation from the benchmark primes.

I inspected the implementations and reproduced the universal enumeration and the full continuum computation, including all $82{,}944$ cells, the degree histogram, signature balance, and both continuum certificates. I also regenerated the star and pair tables for all five benchmarks through $p=101$ and checked them against the rational certificates, reproducing $O_{101,1,5}(0,25)\le756$. The Python checks passed for all ten supplied dual certificates, all omitted leaf subsets, seventeen pair witnesses, and fourteen primal sets. I did not regenerate the larger finite star and pair tables through $p=1009$; their review rests on the retained tables, exact certificate checks, and inspection of the regeneration algorithm. In particular, the default Python run checks several saved enumeration results rather than independently deriving every local maximum. Section 7's distinction between certificate checking and regeneration should remain explicit wherever verification is summarized.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the relevant snapshot papers. Paper 46 [11] already introduced the hot-orbit conflict graph and finite pair/path relaxations; those ingredients are appropriately credited here. The analytic phase law, generic conflict classification, induced-neighbourhood certificates, and rigorous quarter-shift limiting bound go beyond its results. Papers 32 [10] and 48 [15] provide limiting optimization and arithmetic policy transfer, but do not supply this bound. The external comparison with Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [2] confirms the classical construction and its scope. Published checkerboard continuum-dual work [16] is also relevant methodological background: direct dual transfer is not new as a general principle, although the present modular-hyperbola certificate and exceptional-orbit control are distinct.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement: the research question, four contributions, proof dependencies, and limitations are identifiable, and the figures explain the phase landscape and certificate improvement. The introduction and theorem summaries could be shortened by moving repeated enumeration counts to the reproducibility section, but this is not an obstacle to following the science.

Please make the following localized corrections:

- In Lemma 2.2(1), the displayed minus-slope carry formulas have the opposite sign from the definition in Section 2.1. With the stated convention, $\Delta^-_{0\to1}=\phi^-(v_0)-\phi^-(v_1)$, and similarly for $2\to3$. For example, $p=101,c=1,A=25,B=0,u=3$ gives carry $+1$, whereas the printed formula gives $-1$. The parity classification is unaffected, and the coordinate-based implementation uses the correct convention.
- Remove or precisely restrict the equality assertion at the end of Theorem 1(3). It cannot hold as stated for the diagonal branch: when $A=B$, both minus-slope carries vanish, so every four-fibre cycle has capacity at most $30$. With $n_2=2$, this gives $\sum_o\mathrm{OE}_o\le(15/2)p-3/2$. The regenerated $p=13,c=1$ controls likewise give diagonal maximum $96$, versus antidiagonal maximum $97$. The stated universal upper inequality remains valid; its sharpness should be supported by a correctly specified antidiagonal example rather than the present “whenever” clause.
- Add the stronger previously reported $712$-point witness at $p=101$ from [11] to the lower-bound discussion. The present LNS witnesses validate feasibility but do not establish the magnitude or mechanism of the remaining optimization gap.
