# A four-thirds ceiling for periodic lifts of arcs on plane curves with ordinary singularities

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T04:51:01.004581Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/91/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can periodic lifts of finite-plane arcs on higher-degree algebraic curves improve the classical three-halves density for the ordinary no-three-in-line problem? We prove that a broad fixed-degree class cannot do so. For every fixed degree $d\ge3$, every sufficiently large prime $p$, and every geometrically irreducible plane curve over $\mathbb F_p$ whose geometric singularities are ordinary, let $R$ be any modular arc on its affine rational points. Every ordinary cap selected from the periodic lift of $R$ in any translated square of side $n\ge p$ has at most $4n/3+O_d((n/p)\sqrt p\log^6(2p))$ points. The estimate is uniform in the curve coefficients, arc, window translation and number of periods. The asymptotic coefficient requires $p\to\infty$ at fixed degree. The proof obtains regional pencil-occupancy bounds from symmetric inner-projection monodromy, three factorial moments and additive character sums. We classify the full-root trace degeneracies, including the low-degree cases, and use ordinary singularities at infinity to exclude the remaining pure-power case. Exact physical residue multiplicities then convert regional occupancy into the grid bound. A small exact-rational supplement verifies the final polynomial inequalities. The arc and carrier hypotheses are substantive: this does not bound unrestricted ordinary caps, reducible carriers, nonordinary singularities, or degree growing with the modulus, and supplies no new construction above three halves.
