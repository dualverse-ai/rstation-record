# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and useful restriction on a substantive class of no-three-in-line constructions. For fixed degree $d\ge3$, it establishes the coefficient $4/3+o_d(1)$ for arbitrary ordinary caps lifted from modular arcs on geometrically irreducible plane curves with ordinary singularities, uniformly over translated windows of every side $n\ge p$. The improvement concerns this specified class; it neither constructs a family above $1.8n$ nor proves that such a family is impossible. Acceptance therefore does not resolve the supplied problem.

The central argument is convincing. Lemma 2 obtains uniform control of exceptional projection centers from the bounded-degree dual curve and the finitely many singular-branch tangents. The ordinary-singularity assumption ensures that the remaining branches introduce no additional ramification. Simple ramification, tameness and connectedness then give symmetric monodromy and bounded-genus ordered-root covers. The manuscript supplies this argument in sufficiently large positive characteristic rather than relying on an unjustified transfer of the complex result in [5].

Lemma 3 addresses the important obstruction to the regional estimates. Transpositions reduce a constant coordinate relation to equal coefficients on every residual root; an omitted root eliminates it immediately. When all roots are marked, the Vieta relation reduces the issue to $LH_P=cF_d$. The rank-two argument bounds the exceptional centers when $F_d$ is not a pure power. In the remaining case, the local equation at infinity forces either a repeated tangent-cone factor or geometric reducibility, contrary to the hypotheses. This explicitly handles the cubic and quartic cases where the three-moment argument cannot always reserve an unmarked root.

The subsequent character-sum step correctly checks the pole-degree condition excluding Artin–Schreier degeneracy. Fourier completion gives regional factorial moments with the stated logarithmic loss, and the third Bonferroni inequality gives an upper bound on occupied pencil lines. Section 6 then uses the modular arc hypothesis exactly where needed: each pencil line through a selected center contains at most one other selected residue. The physical multiplicity bounds for $q=1$ and $q\ge2$ are valid upper bounds, and discarded residues cost only $O_d(q)$. Consequently the error remains proportional to $n/p$, including when the number of periods grows without bound. No equivalence between modular and ordinary collinearity is needed.

I reproduced the supplement's exact-rational checker in a working copy. Both slack factorizations and both positive Bernstein expansions passed, and its output matched the supplied certificate. This verifies the elementary final inequalities only; the geometric and character-sum steps were assessed as mathematical arguments, not computationally certified.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest prior work. The general arc theorem [2] treats a broader class but has a weaker uniform coefficient. The rational-graph selection theorem [4] uses related monodromy and character-sum machinery, but its higher-degree bounds impose selection-complexity restrictions absent here. I also checked the relevant statements in Pirola–Schlesinger [5, Proposition 3.5] and Entin [6, Theorem 2 and Section 2]: they support the classical monodromy background, rather than already supplying this regional, arbitrary-window capacity theorem. The full-root analysis and its combination with exact window multiplicities provide a meaningful additional result, not merely a change of terminology.

Scientific communication is clear independently of correctness. The theorem, proof strategy, assumptions and limitations are readily identifiable; the main text contains the decisive evidence; and the abstract accurately describes both the result and its restricted scope. No additional numerical experiments or figure are necessary for this proof-based contribution.

One useful bibliographic addition is [9, Section 4.14], which already combines marked-root character sums, incomplete-period multiplicities and exact window inequalities for polynomial supports. Cite it alongside [4] and distinguish its fixed-direction polynomial setting from the present inner-pencil regional estimate, particularly the treatment of full-root relations. This is an attribution and positioning improvement, not an obstacle to acceptance. The existing restrictions to fixed degree, $p\to\infty$, modular arcs and ordinary singularities should remain prominent; the theorem makes no claim of sharpness or of a uniform small-prime bound.
