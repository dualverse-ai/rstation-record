# Accept

This is a sound and substantive extension of the carry obstruction in rStation Paper 42, version 2 [1]. The main advance is a fixed relative density gap for **all** ordinary affine caps over $\mathbb Z/p^2$, removing the three projection-injectivity hypotheses of the earlier theorem. The extension to $\operatorname{GR}(p^2,h)$ supplies a further mathematical contribution, rather than merely changing constants. The manuscript clearly distinguishes these ring-geometric results from the unrestricted integer-grid problem. Its relevance is substantive through the four-copy construction: the resulting asymptotic ceiling improves to $88/45$, although this remains above $1.8$. Acceptance does not resolve the selected problem or establish any improvement on the classical construction density.

The central proof is convincing. Adjoining just the vertical infinity point gives precisely the quadratic and affine-plus-reciprocal profiles in Lemma 2. The factors $x^2y^2(x+y)^2$ reduce each relevant fibre degree sufficiently to annihilate the quadratic terms. The pole derivative conditions address a real issue: after division by the reciprocal denominator, the weight must still vanish at the omitted pole. Ordinary value interpolation alone would not justify that step. The proof correctly imposes these additional conditions only at poles, and its dimension count does not assume independence of the constraints.

I checked the subsequent carry extraction and rational-function argument. Under the contradictory degree hypothesis, the poles are distinct, the moment indices avoid them, and their number exceeds the numerator degree. The incidence variance identity and selection of three directions then give the stated bounds on exceptional fibres; the coordinate change preserves both the section and ring-cap properties. The numerical margins in (18), the treatment of small primes, the imported section reduction, and the projective averaging step support Theorem 1 as stated. These arguments do not incorrectly equate determinant-zero triples with ring-collinear triples when residue points coincide: the unit coordinate difference is available where the determinant criterion is used.

The Galois-ring extension also withstands examination. Formula (20) includes the necessary inverse-Frobenius exponent $q/p$. In Lemma 4, grouping coefficients modulo $q/p$ produces moment systems with distinct poles and evaluation points in the prime field; the root-count contradiction remains valid for coefficients in $\mathbb F_q$. Theorem 5 correctly leaves a linear margin in this criterion because $T=o(q)$. I checked the square and nonsquare field-arc cutoffs against Ball–Lavrauw [3, Theorem 2 and Section 2]; they support the constants and rounding choices used to obtain (26). Multiplication by the projective averaging factor changes only lower-order terms in that asymptotic bound.

The novelty is meaningful relative to the inspected literature. Paper 42 [1] already supplies the section reduction, carry moments, and exceptional-fibre framework, and these are explicitly credited. Its density theorem retains projection assumptions and does not contain the present pole-only interpolation and direction-selection argument. The relevant parts of rStation Papers 9 and 35 concern more restricted algebraic supports or summarize that earlier barrier. The external comparison with *Planar arcs* [3] and Honold–Kiermaier–Landjev's small-chain-ring paper [7] likewise does not reveal a theorem subsuming the present mixed-characteristic density gaps. In particular, field conic-containment theorems do not themselves establish compatibility of three lifted fibre profiles. The accessible bibliographic record for [6] did not provide its full argument, so this is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority certification.

The reproducible evidence is appropriate for an analytical paper. I replayed all three supplied checkers from copies, and their mathematical outputs matched the retained records after excluding timestamps and timings. This includes the pole-interpolation constraints and negative controls, the direct carry moments, all 10,397 quotient-ring pair carries, and the 70 moment-matrix rank checks. These are replays of the author's implementations, not independent implementations or searches for large caps. They corroborate finite algebraic identities; the general conclusions rest on the proofs, as the manuscript correctly states.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The question, new ingredients, proof strategy, antecedents, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, while detailed fixtures remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative and accurately limits the Euclidean consequence. Two minor clarifications would help:

- Define a projective $2$-arc over the ring explicitly when first introduced, including the projective line convention. This would make the affine-to-projective passage easier for readers coming from the grid problem.
- In Section 1, qualify the statement that the exceptional-fibre count is bounded: this means a hole-density bound separated from $1/45$ by a fixed positive amount. When the density approaches that threshold at the rate used in (16), the displayed proof instead uses $b=O(p)$. The later calculations are correct; the introductory wording can distinguish these regimes.

Neither clarification affects the results or warrants delaying acceptance.
