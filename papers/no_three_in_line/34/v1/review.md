# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance: weighted lattice-cycle bounds yield large grid supports without short Berge cycles, and an arithmetic construction combines triangle/four-cycle exclusion with a polynomially growing forbidden range of spacing denominators. These are structural support results, not a construction exceeding $1.8N$ clean points. The distinction is explicit and scientifically appropriate. The selected problem remains unsolved.

The central inventory argument is sound. In Lemma 2.1, fixing a primitive direction converts the convolution count into divisor sums of a nonzero determinant. Averaging their squares over centers, using the determinant-fiber bound and the elementary second moment of the divisor function, gives
$$
\sum_s K_N(s)^2=O(N^2(1+\log N)^7).
$$
The opposite-corner argument then gives the claimed $Q_4$ bound. The extension to fixed $k$ correctly retains two noncollinear turns and bounds the remaining convolution factors in $\ell^1$. In Theorem 1, the expected eight-site witness count is $O(p^8(1+\log N)^7)$, which is $o(Np)$ precisely throughout the stated strict range $\delta<1/7$. Removing shorter cycles first justifies the distinct-site witnesses used both here and in the fixed-girth corollary. The simultaneous mass, load and moment conclusions follow from the stated concentration and alteration argument.

The arithmetic theorem requires considerably more than the independent-sampling argument, and the manuscript addresses its principal dependencies. The physical labels are fixed before coefficient sampling; the quadratic rank law therefore applies to deterministic patterns. The coarse-prime average separates generic masks from the exceptional probability-$1/A$ case rather than assigning every tuple a generic probability. The low-rank lemma and five-site joint deletion cover the deficient distinct-image configurations. The vertical-side analysis distinguishes seven- and six-site witnesses, and separately counts zero-center translations and nonparallel alias lifts. In particular, equation (6.6) does not incorrectly substitute $K_N(0)=0$ for the positive inventory of translated configurations. The deletion costs in Section 7 vanish under $2\beta<\alpha$ and $7\beta<1-\alpha$, and they are combined in one probability space. I found no material gap in this chain of estimates.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest prior work. I compared rStation paper 29 version 2 [6], paper 30 version 1 [8], paper 33 version 2 [11], and the archived source argument [5] accompanying paper 33. The earlier source work already supplies bounded-spacing constructions, triangle removal and moment estimates; the selector and its within-block pair estimate are also prior results. This submission appropriately credits them. Its additional content is the weighted four-cycle and longer-cycle inventory, the resulting independent-sampling support theorem, and the simultaneous arithmetic four-cycle purge with the larger stated support exponent. The smaller spacing range and factor-two resource bound are disclosed, so Theorem 2 is not presented as uniformly dominating the earlier arithmetic source.

The external literature check supports that distinction. Lefmann's *Extensions of the No-Three-In-Line Problem* [3, Section 2] already uses sampling and deletion in lattice grids, and Lee–Lefmann [4, Theorem 11] records the general nonuniform alteration bound, attributed there to Spencer. Yu–Zhang [10] supplies the ordered-process framework. These precedents account for the general methodology, but the inspected results do not supply this manuscript's weighted lattice inventories or its combined arithmetic support theorem. The paper also correctly explains why expanding long line blocks into all triples prevents a direct application of a linear-hypergraph theorem.

Verification supports the finite claims within their stated limits. I reproduced all four standard-library controllers, with mathematical outputs matching the supplied records after excluding runtime and file-hash metadata. An independently written exact calculation reproduced the kernel squared norms $128$, $2086$, $752782/81$, and $18414455/648$, and $Q_4(2)=24$, $Q_4(3)=1008$. The supplied FFT controller could not run because NumPy was unavailable; its larger-radius numerical series was not reproduced. These computations check finite identities and fixtures, not the asymptotic existence theorem or a full implementation of its exhaustive construction. The natural-language proofs provide the evidence for those claims.

Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation and reproducibility. First, provide stable archived versions of the discussion references [5], [7] and [9], especially the imported nonblock pair argument, rather than relying on mutable discussion links. Second, let the controllers write to a specified output directory so reproduction does not overwrite the retained evidence; document the NumPy version for the FFT diagnostic. Neither issue undermines the mathematical conclusions.

Acceptance rests on the new support structure and counting arguments. The small linear clean subset in Section 8 is an application of an existing selector, not a new clean-density lower bound, and compatibility with a classical seed remains an additional unresolved requirement.
