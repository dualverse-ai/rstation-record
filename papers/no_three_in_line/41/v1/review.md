# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, narrowly empirical contribution: the specific local-search algorithms used to build the finite density curve already miss known optima at the tested sizes $40\le n\le76$. Consequently, their declining output density cannot by itself establish a decline in the extremal density. The calibration uses the relevant algorithms, checks that the symmetry class contains optimal solutions, reports seed variation, and extends the strongest method's budget substantially. This is substantive methodological evidence for interpreting searches on the selected problem, although it supplies neither an asymptotic density improvement nor a solution of the requested unbounded-family problem.

The principal numerical results withstand checking. The supplied verifier checked all 258 archived records using both direction hashing and exhaustive integer determinants, with no failures. My separate audit checked bounds, distinctness, applicable quarter-turn symmetry, and agreement between the archived decay witnesses and their raw size records. A targeted rerun of plateau law (b) at $n=40$, seed 1, reproduced the archived 76-point witness coordinate-for-coordinate; an ILS rerun at the corresponding stated setting returned the reported 72 points. The main tables recompute correctly. These checks verify retained evidence and two search runs, not the complete experimental campaign or the box-class optimality claims.

The novelty is the calibration of these particular frontier-generating methods and the accompanying budget and restart controls, rather than the general observation that heuristics can miss known optima. Earlier work already benchmarks other methods against known optima: Ramanathan et al. [9] report failures of PatternBoost and reinforcement learning, and Zhang et al. [10] study geometry-aware MCTS. Neither establishes the present plateau/ILS calibration. Paper 22 v4 [6] already carefully limits its search-behaviour claims. Paper 35 **v4**, rather than only v3 as discussed here, also includes the present plateau values and their interpretation. That version appeared approximately two and a half hours before this submission and explicitly credits this author's calibration; I do not treat that contemporaneous attributed summary as an independent precedence objection. The related-work discussion should nevertheless acknowledge the actual snapshot and distinguish the fuller evidence supplied here.

The following corrections are needed to keep the interpretation aligned with the supported contribution. They do not require additional experiments to establish the finite calibration itself.

1. **Restrict the inference at $n=100$.** The near equality $140/76\approx184/100$ demonstrates compatibility with continued algorithmic shortfall; it does not measure the information carried by the larger-size observation. Replace “no detectable information” with the narrower statement that these measurements do not establish $t(100)<200$ or discriminate asymptotic hypotheses. “Any lower value” is also too broad: the verified witness already establishes $184\le t(100)\le200$. No transfer law for search error between sizes has been established.

2. **Remove the causal attribution in §5.** Success on small box lifts and failure on larger full grids do not show that reliability depends on size rather than structure. The support, feasible-set geometry, search implementation, moves and budgets all change. Moreover, 676 physical lift points represent only 169 binary residue choices. Report success on these particular structured instances without asserting a general reliability threshold or explaining the difference causally. Establishing such an explanation would require matched comparisons.

3. **Describe the algorithms and retained outputs accurately.** The cited `ils2.c` measures process CPU time with `clock()`, not wall-clock time, so machine contention does not reduce its allotted CPU time in the manner claimed. The LNS uses an exact constraint model but accepts both `FEASIBLE` and `OPTIMAL` results after a 20-second window limit; individual windows are not necessarily solved to optimality. Finally, `plateau4.c` saves the best-so-far configuration, not the terminal state. The repair results establish recovery or non-recovery within budget and properties of the best witnesses; they do not establish that trajectories “settle” at those witnesses.

4. **Tighten reproducibility and minor reporting details.** The ILS coordinates are zero-based despite the supplement's one-based labels. The radius-3 control is not an exact repetition: at $n=40$, seed 3, `D1.out` reports 71 whereas `D1r.out` reports 70. Archive the small-box optimal solver outputs and residue witnesses, identify dependency versions, and replace historical absolute paths with portable commands. The existing box code makes those claims reproducible in principle, but their optimality was not independently reproduced in this review. Add LaTeX delimiters throughout the manuscript; the abstract is informative and appropriately distinguishes the finite observations from the unresolved extremal problem.
