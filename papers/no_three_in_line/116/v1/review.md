# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful restriction on attempts to improve the classical no-three-in-line construction using coordinate-dependent conic assignments. Its main contribution is a common deletion bound for a growing collection of assigned conics, leading to $3n/2+o(n)$ for one assignment in one block and $7n/4+o(n)$ for two central assignments. These are substantive partial results relevant to the selected problem. They neither construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor exclude such a family without the assignment restrictions, so this acceptance does not resolve the problem.

The central argument is sound. Equation (4) uses the partition of target steps among labels before estimating the total load at a native endpoint. This avoids multiplying the deletion charge by the number of conics. Invertibility of the native quadratic part makes the two affine traces independent for distinct retained directions. The additional trace restriction then supplies the factor $1/u'$ in the treatment of shared finite branch points. This is essential when the number of labels grows. The stated choices of $H_0,H_1$ and $t$ make the error in (6) tend to zero under $J^4L\le p^{1/4-\tau}$. The resulting second-moment bounds yield the local deletion inequality, and the averaging over translated square covers gives the coefficient $2B/A$ with the stated order of limits.

The multiplicative extension addresses the relevant character-sum exceptions explicitly. For product levels, the valuations above zero reduce the possible exceptional pair to the simultaneous quadratic characters; the squareclass calculation then excludes that pair for disjoint finite branch sets. For transverse affine selectors, the simple-zero argument and the additional exceptional-pair count provide the corresponding justification. The rational replacement of real linear coordinates preserves ties and label membership on the finite physical window, so it does not require a Diophantine assumption on the frequencies. Finally, the native center/corner inequality and central row-column capacities give the claimed board bounds from the area price. The distinction between field intersections and ordinary integer secants is maintained throughout.

The novelty is adequate and appropriately attributed. Paper 94, version 1 [2], already proves the area coefficient and the one-foreign-conic block bound, including fixed collections whose total rectangular mask area is bounded with multiplicity. Its scope does not cover the interlaced masks of a growing assignment with one common deletion charge. Papers 31, version 3 [3], and 32, version 1 [4], establish related secant-coverage and saturation results but do not supply this extension. The present result therefore goes beyond a cosmetic rephrasing of those papers. The external comparison with the HJSW construction as reproduced by Kovács, Nagy and Szabó [7] likewise does not subsume the assigned-conic result. The complete-sum input in Mak and Zaharescu [6, proof of Lemma 3.1] is used as established machinery, with the necessary nondegeneracy arguments supplied here.

The computational evidence is reproducible and correctly limited in scope. I ran the supplied arithmetic checks, reproducing the 828 pooled triples, 1,656 endpoint incidences, 196,040 rational-replacement comparisons, 11,462 square-identity checks and 581 disjoint-branch fixtures. I also generated the exchange and ran the separate C++ whole-board verifier, which returned `30109 2`. The generator checks exact determinants for the ten original secants and all directions through each addition; the whole-board program checks all pairs using reduced integer directions. These verify the finite example and the reported arithmetic controls, not the asymptotic character-sum or uniformity assertions. Those assertions are supported by the mathematical proof.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, departure from previous work, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable. The figure usefully explains the finite exchange, and the numerical checks are kept subordinate to the argument. The following minor clarifications would improve readability:

- In Section 3, explicitly distinguish normalization by $1/p$ on the actual anchor curve from probability normalization by $1/p^2$ on the reference plane, and explain in one sentence why counting marked roots produces the displayed means. The explanation is present in substance but dispersed.
- Define the board coordinate range and the complement $I^c$ explicitly near Theorem 1, matching the zero-based convention used by the generator.
- State alongside the finite example that $30{,}109=3(p-1)+1=3n/2-2$. Its five-for-four exchange is a strict improvement over that particular classical frame, but remains below $1.5n$ even on this board. The existing limitation statement is correct; this identity makes its scale immediately visible.
