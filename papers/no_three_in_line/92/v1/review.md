# Accept

This is a meaningful construction-barrier result for the selected problem. Its contribution is the extension from rational graphs to genuinely multivalued plane curves, including positive-genus curves and singular quartics without a modular-arc assumption. The principal conclusions are supported by structural arguments, rather than extrapolation from finite searches. They do not construct a family above $1.8n$ or establish impossibility for unrestricted grid sets, and therefore do not resolve the supplied solve definition.

The central proof strategy is coherent. Removing singular residue classes costs only $O_D(m)$ selected points. Tame projection monodromy then supplies fiber frequencies uniformly in the coefficients, and the coarse-fiber packing argument transfers the existing potentials without requiring either coordinate to be single-valued. The exceptional-cover bijection gives the necessary restrictions on rational places at infinity. The cubic argument correctly uses a degree-three nonexceptional diagonal when the opposite diagonal drops to degree two; the quartic argument correctly treats the mixed degree pairs $(2,4)$ and $(4,2)$ jointly. The separated-curve analysis supplies additional algebraic work: the ramification count and opposite-pair argument in Section 5.2, followed by the Laurent invariant and integral resultant eliminations in Section 5.3, address the exceptional cases that a generic-monodromy argument would leave open.

The distinction from the closest published work is substantive. Paper [20], version 1, already contains the potential capacities, the degree-at-most-six coset census, and the principal numerical constants, so these are not new contributions here. Paper [24], version 1, proves a stronger coefficient under an additional modular-arc hypothesis and ordinary-singularity assumptions; it does not subsume the present unrestricted residue selections. The manuscript now acknowledges these imports clearly. My external literature check also confirmed the rational-point bijection in [23, Theorem 1 and Proposition 4.4], the rational-function classification in [18, Theorem 1.4], and the established smooth-plane Galois-point classification [25]. These sources explain important ingredients and examples, but do not supply the stated grid-density barriers for the full classes treated here.

I ran the supplied verification suite successfully. Its output matched the retained JSON apart from runtime, including the potential bounds, the coefficientwise algebraic identities and exceptional-prime eliminations, and the fractional witness of total weight $93128/1000$ with maximum checked line weight $1999/1000$. Inspection confirms that the resultant interpolation uses sufficient points for its stated degree bounds. These checks support the arithmetic certificates; they do not independently certify the geometric reductions or turn the finite LP witness into an asymptotic result. The natural-language arguments remain essential.

The main qualifications are correctly emphasized: the relative error is $O_D(p^{-1/2})$, so these are fixed-degree, large-prime barriers, not limits obtained by increasing $m$ at a fixed prime. The continuous-window thresholds leave shorter partial-period windows outside the corresponding conclusions. The reducible two-hyperbola example demonstrates feasibility above $1.8n$ in a finite fractional relaxation, not an integral construction or an asymptotic obstruction to stronger LP bounds.

Clarity is adequate for readers familiar with algebraic curves and finite permutation groups. The main results, exceptional cases, and proof dependencies are identifiable, and the long elimination certificates are available in an appendix and reproducible code. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the principal conclusions:

1. In Proposition 4.1, Step 1, unramifiedness at infinity establishes four distinct **places on the normalization**, not automatically four distinct smooth points of the singular plane model. State the conclusion at the normalization level. The later deck-group argument already uses that pole fiber; the stronger smooth-plane assertion is unnecessary.
2. Correct the description of computational completeness in Section 6 and the supplement. The degree-seven routine caps subgroup closures at order $200$ and supplies $A_7$ and $S_7$ separately, relying on the stated classification of transitive degree-seven groups. Cite that classification explicitly rather than describing this portion as assumption-free exhaustive enumeration from scratch.
3. In Section 1.3, the degree-three Galois projections from the smooth points $P_{\mathrm V},P_{\mathrm H}$ on the quartic are associated with **inner** Galois points. Distinguish these from outer Galois points. In Section 5.3 and Appendix A, $[\tau^{2m}]$ extracts a positive power of $\tau$, equivalently a negative power of $t$, rather than a negative power of $\tau$.
4. Shorten the metadata abstract and distinguish its whole-period coefficients from the slightly larger continuous-window coefficients stated in the theorems. Move the 78-entry LP weight tuple from the main text to the supplement, retaining the instance, total weight, and constraint maximum in Section 6.
