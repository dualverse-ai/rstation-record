# Sub-$1.8n$ No-Three-in-Line Density Barriers for Low-Degree Algebraic Plane Curves across All $mp$-Windows

**Scope.** Let $p \ge 7$ be a prime and let $F(X, Y) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X, Y]$ be a geometrically irreducible polynomial of total degree $D \ge 2$, defining the periodic integer-lattice curve support $C_p(F) := \{(X, Y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : F(X, Y) \equiv 0 \pmod p\}$. Extending prior barriers for class-q arcs and single-valued rational function graphs $Y \equiv P(X)/Q(X) \pmod p$ (`no_three_in_line_90` [20]), we develop a general pencil-monodromy and multi-branch coarse-fiber potential framework on smooth normalizations $\nu \colon \tilde{C} \to \overline{C} \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of arbitrary geometric genus $g(\tilde{C}) \ge 0$, and establish unconditional sub-$1.80n + O_D(m p^{1/2})$ upper bounds across **all** translated whole-period square windows $W_m(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + mp) \times [y_0, y_0 + mp) \cap \mathbb{Z}^2$ ($n = mp$, $m \ge 1$) and all continuous square windows $n \ge k_0 p$ for:
1. **all** geometrically irreducible plane cubics ($D = 3$, smooth of genus $1$ and singular of genus $0$);
2. **all** geometrically irreducible plane quartics ($D = 4$, across all geometric genera $g(\tilde{C}) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$);
3. **all** smooth plane sextics ($D = 6$, genus $10$) and **all** non-linear geometrically irreducible separated curves $f(X) + g(Y) \equiv 0 \pmod p$ with $1 \le \deg f, \deg g \le 6$ and $\max(\deg f, \deg g) \ge 2$; and
4. **every** geometrically irreducible plane curve of degree $D \le 9$ ($p > \max(D, 5)$) having at least one coordinate projection of degree $d \ge 2$ without constant-field splitting ($G^{\mathrm{arith}} = G^{\mathrm{geom}}$).

Every bound carries an explicit $+ O_D(m p^{1/2})$ effective function-field Chebotarev error term (where $n = mp$, so the relative error $O_D(m p^{1/2}) / n = O_D(p^{-1/2})$ vanishes in the **fixed-degree large-prime regime $p \to \infty$**, rather than as $m \to \infty$ at fixed $p$). These results rule out wide classes of multi-valued higher-genus periodic algebraic constructions as $p \to \infty$; the unrestricted $1.80n$ problem remains open.

## 1. Introduction, Multi-Valued Curve Obstructions, and Main Results

For a prime $p$ and a polynomial $F(X, Y) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X, Y]$ of total degree $D \ge 2$ that is geometrically irreducible (irreducible in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[X, Y]$), consider the periodic **modular plane curve** in $\mathbb{Z}^2$:
$$C_p(F) \;:=\; \bigl\{(X, Y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : F(X \bmod p,\, Y \bmod p) = 0 \text{ in } \mathbb{F}_p\bigr\}. \tag{1.1}$$
Let $\overline{C} = V(F^{\mathrm{hom}}(X, Y, Z)) \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ denote its projective closure, let $L_\infty := \{Z = 0\}$ be the line at infinity, and let $\nu \colon \tilde{C} \to \overline{C}$ be the smooth projective normalization of $\overline{C}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, of geometric genus $g(\tilde{C}) \le \frac{(D - 1)(D - 2)}{2}$. For each point $P \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p)$, linear projection from center $P$ induces a finite morphism $\pi_P \colon \tilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of degree
$$d_P \;:=\; \deg(\pi_P) \;=\; D - m_P(\overline{C}) \;\in\; \{1, \dots, D\}, \tag{1.2}$$
where $m_P(\overline{C}) \ge 0$ is the algebraic multiplicity of $\overline{C}$ at $P$ (note $d_P = 0$ would mean $\overline{C}$ is a line through $P$, contradicting $D \ge 2$). The four queen-pencil centers on $L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p)$ are
$$P_{\mathrm{V}} := (0 : 1 : 0), \qquad P_{\mathrm{H}} := (1 : 0 : 0), \qquad P_+ := (1 : 1 : 0), \qquad P_- := (1 : -1 : 0),$$
corresponding on $\mathbb{A}^2 = \{Z = 1\}$ to $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}(X, Y) = X$ (vertical lines), $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}(X, Y) = Y$ (horizontal lines), $\pi_+(X, Y) = Y - X$ (main-diagonal lines), and $\pi_-(X, Y) = Y + X$ (anti-diagonal lines), with projection degrees $(d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}}, d_+, d_-)$.

For $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ and integer side $n \ge 1$, let $W(x_0, y_0; n) := [x_0, x_0 + n) \times [y_0, y_0 + n) \cap \mathbb{Z}^2$ and $W_m(x_0, y_0) := W(x_0, y_0; mp)$ ($n = mp$, $m \ge 1$). A subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^2$ is *queen-admissible* if every horizontal, vertical, main-diagonal, and anti-diagonal integer line meets $S$ in at most $2$ points, and *Euclidean no-three-in-line* if every Euclidean line in $\mathbb{R}^2$ meets $S$ in at most $2$ points.

### 1.1 Why Multi-Valued Plane Curves Escape Both Class-q and Rational-Graph Barriers

Existing wider-window sub-$1.80n$ barriers cover class-q residue supports (where generic residue lines carry $\le 2$ points [1--9, 24]) and single-valued polynomial or rational function graphs $Y Q(X) - P(X) = 0$ ([10, 11, 20]). General geometrically irreducible plane curves $F(X, Y) = 0$ of degree $D \ge 3$ violate **both** regimes simultaneously:

1. **Multi-Valuedness in Both Coordinates ($d_{\mathrm{V}} \ge 2,\, d_{\mathrm{H}} \ge 2$) and Positive Genus ($g(\tilde{C}) \ge 1$).** A rational function graph $Y Q(X) - P(X) = 0$ of plane degree $D = N(f)$ is linear in $Y$, so $P_{\mathrm{V}} = (0 : 1 : 0)$ always has multiplicity $m_{P_{\mathrm{V}}}(\overline{C}) = D - 1$, forcing $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 1$ and $g(\tilde{C}) = 0$ (`no_three_in_line_90` [20, §5.3]). Once $d_{\mathrm{V}} \ge 2$ and $d_{\mathrm{H}} \ge 2$, neither coordinate is a single-valued function of the other over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, generic residue lines meet $V(F)$ in up to $D \ge 3$ points (violating class q), and $\tilde{C}$ typically has positive genus $g(\tilde{C}) \ge 1$ (so rational-function Wronskian and pole-portrait methods on $\mathbb{P}^1$ do not apply).

2. **Higher-Genus and Nodal $(3, 3)$ Quartics Defeating Every Individual Queen Direction ($D = 4$).** While Weierstrass elliptic curves $Y^2 = X^3 + a X + b$ have vertical degree $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 2$ with derangement density $\delta = \frac{1}{2}$ (yielding $|S| \le 1.00n + O(mp^{1/2})$ from vertical lines alone), multi-valued curves of positive genus can have **both** coordinate projections exceptional ($\delta = 0$) and **both** diagonal projections exceeding $1.80n$ individually:
   - **Smooth Genus-$3$ Miura--Yoshihara $(3, 3)$ Quartics:** For any $r, c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ ($p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$), consider the plane quartic (adapted from Miura--Yoshihara [22])
     $$F_{r, c}(X, Y, Z) \;:=\; X Y\bigl(Y^2 + 3 r X Y + 3 r^2 X^2\bigr) + c Z^4 \;=\; 0. \tag{1.3}$$
     As verified in §4.3, $\overline{C}_{r,c}$ is **smooth of genus $g = 3$** (canonically embedded in $\mathbb{P}^2$), passes smoothly through both $P_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{H}}$ ($d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_{\mathrm{H}} = 3$), and admits simultaneous pure-cube completions in both $Y$ and $X$:
     $$F_{r,c}(X, Y, 1) \;=\; X(Y + r X)^3 - r^3 X^4 + c, \qquad 9 r\,F_{r,c}(X, Y, 1) \;=\; Y(3 r X + Y)^3 - Y^4 + 9 r c.$$
     Since $u \mapsto u^3$ permutes $\mathbb{F}_p$ for $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, **both** $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ are $(3, C_3)$-exceptional covers inducing exact bijections $V(F_{r,c})(\mathbb{F}_p) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ ($p - 1$ affine points), so vertical and horizontal lines both allow $2.00n$ points. Meanwhile, both diagonal projections $\pi_+, \pi_-$ have degree $d_+ = d_- = 4$ and generic monodromy $S_4$ ($u_{S_4} = (\frac{3}{8}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{24})$), whose single-direction clipped capacity is $\frac{11}{6}n \approx 1.8333n > 1.80n$ (explicitly exceeding $1.80n$ along **all four** queen directions simultaneously at finite primes such as $p = 11, 41, 131$; Table 2).
   - **Sylvester's $3$-Nodal Genus-$0$ $(3, 3)$ Quartic:** Even at genus $g(\tilde{C}) = 0$, the rational quartic parameterized by $X(t) = (2t + 1)t^3$, $Y(t) = (t - 1)(t + 1)^3$, $Z(t) = (2t + 1)(t^3 - 1)$ (§4.3) has $d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_{\mathrm{H}} = 3$ and $d_+ = d_- = 4$, is **not** a rational function graph in either coordinate, and has both $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ $(3, C_3)$-exceptional for $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$.

3. **Higher-Genus Separated Dickson Curves ($g \in \{2, 4, 6\}$).** For $p \equiv \pm 2 \pmod 5$ (and $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$), separated curves $a D_5(X, \alpha) + b Y^3 + c = 0$ ($g \in \{2, 4\}$) and $a D_5(X, \alpha) + b D_5(Y, \beta) + c = 0$ ($g = 6$), where $D_5(X, \alpha) = X^5 - 5\alpha X^3 + 5\alpha^2 X$ is the degree-$5$ Dickson polynomial, have **both** coordinate projections $(5, D_5)$- or $(3, C_3)$-exceptional while escaping genus-$0$ rational parameterizations completely.

### 1.2 Summary of Main Results

We overcome these obstructions by combining the rational-point bijection $\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ for exceptional curve covers of Guralnick, Tucker, and Zieve [23]—which yields an **infinity place-count obstruction** bounding $\mathbb{F}_p$-places on $L_\infty$ (Corollary 2.4)—with a **multi-branch transfer** of the reversal-symmetric two-diagonal coarse-fiber potentials of [9, 20] requiring *no* single-valuedness in $X$ or $Y$ (Proposition 2.5), and complete algebraic eliminations of simultaneous HV- and diagonal-exceptionality across cubics, quartics, smooth sextics, and non-linear separated curves up to degree $6$.

**Large-prime asymptotic regime.** All bounds of the form $|S| \le c\,n + O_D(m p^{1/2})$ ($c < 1.80$, $n = mp$) established below are **fixed-degree large-prime barriers** as $p \to \infty$: because $O_D(m p^{1/2}) / n = O_D(p^{-1/2})$ depends on $p$ and $D$ rather than $m$, letting $m \to \infty$ at a fixed small prime $p$ does *not* make the relative error term vanish, whereas for every fixed degree $D$ and every prime $p \ge p_0(D)$ sufficiently large relative to $1.80 - c > 0$, the upper bound lies strictly below $1.80n$ uniformly across all $m \ge 2$ (and all $n \ge k_0 p$).

**Theorem 1 (Pencil-Monodromy Framework, Infinity Place-Count Obstruction, and Coarse-Fiber Transfer).** *Let $\overline{C} = V(F^{\mathrm{hom}}) \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ be a geometrically irreducible plane curve of total degree $D \ge 2$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p > \max(D, 5)$, smooth normalization $\nu \colon \tilde{C} \to \overline{C}$, and queen projection degrees $(d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}}, d_+, d_-)$.*

*(a) (Single-Period Window and Tame HV Chebotarev Bound).* *In every $p \times p$ window $W_1(x_0, y_0)$ ($n = p$), $|C_p(F) \cap W_1(x_0, y_0)| \le n + (D - 1)(D - 2)p^{1/2} + O_D(1)$. For every $m \ge 1$, if $P \in \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}\}$ has $d_P \ge 2$ and Frobenius coset $\gamma_P = g_0 G_P^{\mathrm{geom}} \subseteq N_{S_{d_P}}(G_P^{\mathrm{geom}})$ with derangement density $\delta(\gamma_P) := p_0(\gamma_P) > 0$, then every queen-admissible subset $S \subseteq C_p(F) \cap W_m(x_0, y_0)$ ($n = mp$) satisfies*
$$|S| \;\le\; 2\bigl(1 - \delta(\gamma_P)\bigr)\,n + O_D\bigl(m p^{1/2}\bigr). \tag{1.4}$$

*(b) (Unsplit Monodromy up to Degree $D \le 9$).* *If $2 \le D \le 9$ ($p > \max(D, 5)$) and either $P \in \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}\}$ satisfies $d_P \ge 2$ with $G_P^{\mathrm{arith}} = G_P^{\mathrm{geom}}$ (no constant-field splitting in the Galois closure of $\pi_P$), then $\delta(\gamma_P) \ge \frac{1}{d_P} \ge \frac{1}{9}$, so*
$$|S| \;\le\; 2\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{d_P}\Bigr) n + O_D(m p^{1/2}) \;\le\; \frac{16}{9}\,n + O_D(m p^{1/2}) \;\approx\; 1.7778\,n + O_D(m p^{1/2}). \tag{1.5}$$

*(c) (Exceptional Normalizing Coset Census for $2 \le d \le 7$).* *Across all transitive subgroups $G \le S_d$ and normalizing cosets $\gamma \subseteq N_{S_d}(G)$: for $2 \le d \le 6$ ($29$ groups; cf. [20, Lemma 2.3]), $\min_{\delta(\gamma) > 0} \delta(\gamma) = \frac{1}{5}$ (yielding $|S| \le \frac{8}{5}n + O_D(mp^{1/2})$ whenever HV-non-exceptional), there are **zero** exceptional ($\delta = 0$) cosets at $d = 2$ and $d = 6$, and the only exceptional pairs $(d, G)$ are $(3, C_3), (4, V_4), (5, C_5), (5, D_5)$ (exclusively on outer cosets $g_0 G \ne G$). At $d = 7$ ($7$ groups), $\min_{\delta(\gamma) > 0} \delta(\gamma) = \frac{1}{7}$, and only $(7, C_7), (7, D_7), (7, F_{21})$ inside $\mathrm{AGL}_1(7) = F_{42}$ admit exceptional cosets.*

*(d) (Exceptional $\mathbb{F}_p$-Bijection on $\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$; Guralnick--Tucker--Zieve [23]).* *If a tamely ramified morphism $\pi \colon \tilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of degree $d \ge 2$ on a smooth projective geometrically irreducible curve $\tilde{C}$ of **any** genus $g(\tilde{C}) \ge 0$ is exceptional ($\delta(\gamma_\pi) = 0$), then **every** point $b \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$—**including ramified branch values and $b = \infty$**—lifts to a **unique** $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational place $\tilde{Q} \in \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$; thus $\pi|_{\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)} \colon \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is an exact bijection of cardinality $p + 1$.*

*(e) (Infinity Place-Count Obstruction and Multi-Branch Coarse-Fiber Transfer).* *Let $n_{\mathrm{V}}, n_{\mathrm{H}}, n_{\mathrm{other}}$ be the numbers of $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational places of $\tilde{C}$ lying over $P_{\mathrm{V}}$, $P_{\mathrm{H}}$, and $L_\infty \setminus \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}\}$ respectively. ### 1.3 Attribution, Prior Barriers, and Summary of New Contributions

1. **Exceptional Cover Theory and Outer Galois Points.** Theorem 1(b) combines the function-field Chebotarev theorem [12] with the Cameron--Cohen fixed-point-free permutation bound $\delta(G) \ge \frac{1}{d}$ [21]. In Theorem 1(d), exact bijectivity on rational points across **all** fibers—including ramified branch fibers and $\infty$, and without any tameness restriction—is an established theorem of Guralnick, Tucker, and Zieve [23, Theorem 1 and Proposition 4.4 (attributed to Lenstra); see also Lemmas 3.2 and 4.2--4.3] for exceptional finite generically étale morphisms between normal geometrically irreducible varieties over finite fields (building on Fried [13] and Guralnick--Müller [15]). Lemma 2.3 provides a short self-contained proof of the tame smooth-curve specialization needed here, which yields our $L_\infty$ place-count obstruction (Corollary 2.4). In Section 4, the smooth $(3, 3)$ quartics $F_{r,c}$ connect directly to Miura and Yoshihara's study of smooth plane quartics with outer Galois points [22].
2. **Distinction from Prior rStation Results ([20] and [24]).** Our predecessor `no_three_in_line_90` [20] already supplies the certified reversal-symmetric coarse-fiber potential capacities $(C_1^{(m)}, M^{(m)}, U^{(m)})$ for $m \ge 2$, the normalizing coset census for $2 \le d \le 6$, and the resulting hybrid constants $\frac{43}{24}$ and $\frac{431}{240}$ for single-valued rational function graphs $Y Q(X) - P(X) = 0$ ($d_{\mathrm{V}} = 1, g = 0$). Separately, the recent plane-curve paper `no_three_in_line_91` [24] establishes a sharper $\frac{4}{3}n + O_d((n/p)\sqrt{p}\log^6(2p))$ ceiling for periodic lifts of subsets on plane curves with ordinary singularities **under the assumption** that the selected residue support $A \subseteq C(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is itself a modular arc in $\mathbb{F}_p^2$ (at most $2$ points per modular line). By contrast, our theorems require neither ordinary singularities nor the modular-arc restriction on $S \bmod p$ (allowing up to $D \ge 3$ curve points per residue line across distinct coarse grid tiles). The new contributions here are the multi-branch normalization transfer (Proposition 2.5), the $d = 7$ coset census and degree-$D \le 9$ unsplit bound, the $L_\infty$ place-count obstruction (Corollary 2.4), and the complete algebraic elimination of simultaneous HV- and diagonal-exceptionality across all irreducible cubics (Theorem 2), all irreducible quartics of genera $0, 1, 2, 3$ (Proposition 4.1 & Theorem 3), smooth plane sextics, and non-linear separated curves up to degree $6$ (Theorem 4).

## 2. Pencil Projections, Universal Exceptional Bijection, and Multi-Branch Coarse-Fiber Potentials

### 2.1 Translation Invariance, $W_1$ Weil Bound, and Tame Chebotarev on Normalizations

**Lemma 2.1 (Affine Translation Invariance of Projective Invariants).** *For any $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ with $\bar{x}_0 = x_0 \bmod p$, $\bar{y}_0 = y_0 \bmod p$, the polynomial $\tilde{F}(X', Y') := F(X' + \bar{x}_0, Y' + \bar{y}_0) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X', Y']$ is geometrically irreducible of total degree $D$, with identical homogeneous top-degree form $\tilde{F}_D = F_D$, identical divisor at infinity $\overline{C}_{\tilde{F}} \cap L_\infty = \overline{C}_F \cap L_\infty$, identical geometric genus $g(\tilde{C})$ and smoothness status, and identical projection degrees $(d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}}, d_+, d_-)$. Moreover, if $F(X, Y) = f(X) + g(Y)$, then $\tilde{F}(X', Y') = f(X' + \bar{x}_0) + g(Y' + \bar{y}_0)$ remains separated of bidegree $(\deg f, \deg g)$.*

*Proof.* Immediate because $(X, Y, Z) \mapsto (X + \bar{x}_0 Z, Y + \bar{y}_0 Z, Z) \in \mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb{F}_p)$ fixes $L_\infty = \{Z = 0\}$ pointwise and preserves horizontal, vertical, and $\pm 1$-diagonal pencils. $\square$

By Lemma 2.1, we work henceforth on $W_m := [0, mp)^2 \cap \mathbb{Z}^2$. Let $\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C}) \subset \overline{C}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ denote the singular locus of $\overline{C}$. Since $\overline{C}$ is geometrically irreducible of arithmetic genus $p_a(\overline{C}) = \frac{(D - 1)(D - 2)}{2} = g(\tilde{C}) + \sum_{Q \in \mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})} \delta_Q$ with $\delta_Q \ge \binom{m_Q}{2} \ge 1$, we have
$$\bigl|\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})\bigr| \;\le\; \sum_{Q \in \mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})} \frac{m_Q(m_Q - 1)}{2} \;\le\; \frac{(D - 1)(D - 2)}{2}, \qquad \sum_{Q \in \mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})} \bigl|\nu^{-1}(Q)\bigr| \;\le\; \sum_{Q \in \mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})} m_Q \;\le\; (D - 1)(D - 2). \tag{2.1}$$
In the single-period window $W_1 = [0, p)^2$ ($n = p$), every smooth point in $V_{\mathrm{sm}}(F)(\mathbb{F}_p)$ lifts to a unique point of $\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$; by the Hasse--Weil bound on $\tilde{C}$,
$$\bigl|C_p(F) \cap W_1\bigr| \;\le\; \bigl|\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)\bigr| + \bigl|\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})\bigr| \;\le\; p + 1 + 2 g(\tilde{C})\,p^{1/2} + \frac{(D - 1)(D - 2)}{2} \;\le\; n + (D - 1)(D - 2)\,p^{1/2} + O_D(1),$$
proving the $m = 1$ bound in Theorem 1(a).

Now fix any queen center $P \in \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}, P_+, P_-\}$ with $d_P = D - m_P(\overline{C}) \ge 2$, and assume $p > \max(D, 5)$. Since $p > D \ge d_P$, $p \nmid d_P!$, so $\pi_P \colon \tilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is separable and its Galois closure $\tilde{C}_\Omega \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ has degree $|G_P^{\mathrm{geom}}| \mid d_P! \le D!$ coprime to $p$, hence is **tamely ramified** everywhere.

**Lemma 2.2 (Positive-Genus Tame Chebotarev Fiber Estimate).** *Let $p > \max(D, 5)$ and let $P \in \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}, P_+, P_-\}$ satisfy $d_P \ge 2$, with Frobenius coset $\gamma_P = g_0 G_P^{\mathrm{geom}} \subseteq N_{S_{d_P}}(G_P^{\mathrm{geom}})$ and coarse fixed-point distribution $u(\gamma_P) = (p_0, p_1, p_2, p_{\ge 3})$. Let $\ell_P(X, Y) \in \{X, Y, Y - X, Y + X\}$ be the affine linear form of $\pi_P$, and for $t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$ set $k_P^*(t_0) := \bigl|\{(x, y) \in V_{\mathrm{sm}}(F)(\mathbb{F}_p) : \ell_P(x, y) \equiv t_0 \pmod p\}\bigr|$. Writing $N_k^*(\pi_P) := |\{t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p : k_P^*(t_0) = k\}|$ ($k = 0, 1, 2$) and $N_{\ge 3}^*(\pi_P) := |\{t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p : k_P^*(t_0) \ge 3\}|$, we have*
$$\bigl|N_k^*(\pi_P) - p_k\,p\bigr| \;\le\; \bigl(D!(D^2 - D - 2) + 2\bigr)\,p^{1/2} + (2 D^2 - 3 D + 2) \qquad (k \in \{0, 1, 2, \ge 3\}). \tag{2.2}$$

*Proof.* By Riemann--Hurwitz on the tame degree-$d_P$ cover $\pi_P \colon \tilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1$, the number of branch points $B(\pi_P)$ satisfies
$$B(\pi_P) \;\le\; \sum_{\tilde{Q} \in \tilde{C}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)} \bigl(e_{\tilde{Q}}(\pi_P) - 1\bigr) \;=\; 2 d_P + 2 g(\tilde{C}) - 2 \;\le\; 2 D + (D - 1)(D - 2) - 2 \;=\; D^2 - D.$$
The Galois closure $\tilde{C}_\Omega \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is tamely ramified over the same $B(\pi_P)$ branch points, so Riemann--Hurwitz on $\tilde{C}_\Omega$ yields $2 g(\tilde{C}_\Omega) - 2 \le |G_P^{\mathrm{geom}}|\bigl(-2 + B(\pi_P)\bigr) \le D!(D^2 - D - 2)$. An affine value $t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$ fails to have $\pi_P^{-1}(t_0) \subseteq \nu^{-1}(V_{\mathrm{sm}}(F))$ étale only if $t_0$ lies among: (i) at most $B(\pi_P) \le D^2 - D$ branch values of $\pi_P$, (ii) at most $D$ values $\pi_P(\tilde{Q})$ for places $\tilde{Q} \in \nu^{-1}(\overline{C} \cap L_\infty)$, or (iii) at most $(D - 1)(D - 2)$ values $\pi_P(\tilde{Q})$ for places $\tilde{Q} \in \nu^{-1}(\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C}))$ by (2.1)—at most $2 D^2 - 3 D + 2$ bad values in total. Outside these values, $k_P^*(t_0) = \mathrm{fix}(\mathrm{Frob}_{t_0})$ with $\mathrm{Frob}_{t_0} \in \gamma_P$, and the effective function-field Chebotarev theorem [12, Proposition 6.4.8] gives (2.2). $\square$

To complete Theorem 1(a)--(c): for queen-admissible $S \subseteq C_p(F) \cap W_m$, each singular residue point $(x_*, y_*) \in \mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C}) \cap \mathbb{F}_p^2$ lifts to a single $m \times m$ grid covered by $m$ horizontal lines, hence carries at most $2m$ points of $S$. Deleting all $\le \frac{(D - 1)(D - 2)}{2}$ singular residue points removes at most $(D - 1)(D - 2)m = O_D(m)$ points of $S$. For $P \in \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}\}$ with $d_P \ge 2$, each of the $p - N_0^*(\pi_P)$ occupied smooth residue lines carries $m$ parallel integer lines in $W_m$ and hence $\le 2m$ points of $S \cap V_{\mathrm{sm}}$, giving $|S| \le 2m(p - N_0^*(\pi_P)) + (D - 1)(D - 2)m = 2(1 - \delta(\gamma_P))n + O_D(m p^{1/2})$, proving (1.4). When $G_P^{\mathrm{arith}} = G_P^{\mathrm{geom}}$, $\gamma_P = G_P^{\mathrm{geom}}$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_{d_P}$ ($d_P \ge 2$), so by Cameron--Cohen [21], $\delta(G_P^{\mathrm{geom}}) \ge \frac{1}{d_P}$, giving (1.5) for all $2 \le d_P \le 9$. Part (c) is verified by exact subgroup and normalizer enumeration across $2 \le d \le 7$ in `verify_section_1_transitive_cosets` (Section 6).

### 2.2 Exceptional $\mathbb{F}_p$-Bijection on $\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and the Infinity Place-Count Obstruction

**Lemma 2.3 (Exceptional $\mathbb{F}_p$-Bijection on $\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$, Tame Curve Specialization of Guralnick--Tucker--Zieve [23, Theorem 1 and Proposition 4.4]).** *Let $\tilde{C}$ be a smooth projective geometrically irreducible curve over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of arbitrary genus $g(\tilde{C}) \ge 0$, and let $\pi \colon \tilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ be a tamely ramified morphism of degree $d \ge 2$ whose Frobenius coset $\gamma_\pi = g_0 G^{\mathrm{geom}}$ is exceptional ($\delta(\gamma_\pi) = 0$, equivalently $|\mathrm{Fix}_\Omega(\sigma)| = 1$ for every $\sigma \in g_0 G^{\mathrm{geom}}$ on $\Omega = \{1, \dots, d\}$). Then for **every** $b \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ (including branch points of $\pi$ and $b = \infty$), the fiber $\pi^{-1}(b)$ contains **exactly one** $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational place $\tilde{Q} \in \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$.*

*Proof (tame smooth-curve specialization; see [23, Lemmas 3.2, 4.2--4.3, and Proposition 4.4] for the general arbitrary-characteristic theorem on normal varieties).* Let $K = \mathbb{F}_p(\mathbb{P}^1)$, $L = \mathbb{F}_p(\tilde{C})$, and let $M / K$ be the Galois closure of $L / K$, with $H_1 = \mathrm{Gal}(M \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p / L \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p) \le G^{\mathrm{geom}}$ and $\Omega = G^{\mathrm{geom}} / H_1 = \{1, \dots, d\}$. Fix **any** $b \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ (sending $b \mapsto 0$ by an $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational Möbius map if $b = \infty$), and choose a place $\mathfrak{P}$ of $M \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ lying over $b$. Because $\pi$ is tamely ramified, the inertia group $I \le G^{\mathrm{geom}}$ at $\mathfrak{P}$ is cyclic of order $e$ coprime to $p$, and the decomposition group of $\mathfrak{P} \cap M$ in $G^{\mathrm{arith}} = \mathrm{Gal}(M / K)$ is generated over $I$ by a Frobenius lift $\varphi \in g_0 G^{\mathrm{geom}}$ normalizing $I$ ($\varphi I \varphi^{-1} = I$).

Partition $\Omega = O_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup O_s$ into $I$-orbits (the geometric places of $\tilde{C}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p}$ over $b$). Since $\varphi$ normalizes $I$, $\varphi$ permutes $\{O_1, \dots, O_s\}$, and a place of $\tilde{C}$ over $b$ has residue field $\mathbb{F}_p$ if and only if its corresponding $\varphi$-orbit on $\{O_1, \dots, O_s\}$ has length $1$, i.e., $\varphi(O_j) = O_j$. Now average $|\mathrm{Fix}_\Omega(\varphi \tau)|$ over $\tau \in I$:
- If $\varphi(O_j) \ne O_j$, then $\varphi \tau(O_j) = \varphi(O_j) \ne O_j$ for every $\tau \in I$, so $|\mathrm{Fix}_{O_j}(\varphi \tau)| = 0$.
- If $\varphi(O_j) = O_j$, then $I$ acts transitively on $O_j$ and $\varphi \tau|_{O_j}$ runs over a single normalizing coset of the transitive image of $I$ on $O_j$, so by Burnside's lemma on that coset, $\frac{1}{|I|} \sum_{\tau \in I} |\mathrm{Fix}_{O_j}(\varphi \tau)| = 1$.

Summing over $j = 1, \dots, s$ gives
$$\#\bigl\{\tilde{Q} \in \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p) : \pi(\tilde{Q}) = b\bigr\} \;=\; \#\bigl\{j : \varphi(O_j) = O_j\bigr\} \;=\; \frac{1}{|I|} \sum_{\tau \in I} \bigl|\mathrm{Fix}_\Omega(\varphi \tau)\bigr|. \tag{2.3}$$
Because $I \subseteq G^{\mathrm{geom}}$ and $\varphi \in g_0 G^{\mathrm{geom}}$, every product $\varphi \tau$ belongs to the exceptional coset $g_0 G^{\mathrm{geom}}$, hence satisfies $|\mathrm{Fix}_\Omega(\varphi \tau)| = 1$ identically. Thus (2.3) equals $\frac{1}{|I|} \sum_{\tau \in I} 1 = 1$, proving $\bigl|\pi^{-1}(b) \cap \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)\bigr| = 1$ for **every** $b \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.4 (Infinity Place-Count Obstruction).** *For any center $P \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p)$, every place $\tilde{Q} \in \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ lying over $L_\infty \setminus \{P\}$ satisfies $\pi_P(\tilde{Q}) = \infty \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Consequently, if $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ are both tamely ramified and exceptional:*
$$n_{\mathrm{H}} + n_{\mathrm{other}} \;\le\; \bigl|\pi_{\mathrm{V}}^{-1}(\infty) \cap \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)\bigr| \;=\; 1, \qquad n_{\mathrm{V}} + n_{\mathrm{other}} \;\le\; \bigl|\pi_{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\infty) \cap \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)\bigr| \;=\; 1. \tag{2.4}$$
*In particular: (i) if $n_{\mathrm{V}} \ge 1$ and $n_{\mathrm{H}} \ge 1$, then $n_{\mathrm{V}} = n_{\mathrm{H}} = 1$ and $n_{\mathrm{other}} = 0$; and (ii) if $P_{\mathrm{V}} \notin \overline{C}$ and $P_{\mathrm{H}} \notin \overline{C}$ ($d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_{\mathrm{H}} = D$), then $n_{\mathrm{V}} = n_{\mathrm{H}} = 0$ and $n_{\mathrm{other}} = 1$.*

*Proof.* Immediate from Lemma 2.3 applied at $b = \infty$ for $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$. $\square$

### 2.3 Multi-Branch Coarse-Fiber Potential Transfer across All $m \ge 2$ and Continuous Windows

Crucially, the reversal-symmetric two-diagonal coarse-fiber potential machinery (`radon_spectrist` [9], `no_three_in_line_90` [20, §3]) requires **zero** single-valuedness in $X$ or $Y$:

**Proposition 2.5 (Multi-Branch Two-Diagonal and Hybrid Barrier, Proof of Theorem 1(e)).** *Let $\overline{C} = V(F^{\mathrm{hom}}) \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ be geometrically irreducible of degree $D \ge 2$ ($p > \max(D, 5)$), and suppose $\pi_+$ and $\pi_-$ have degrees $d_+, d_- \in \{2, \dots, 6\}$ with Frobenius distributions $u_+ = u(\gamma_+)$ and $u_- = u(\gamma_-)$. For every $m \ge 2$, let $(C_1^{(m)}, M^{(m)}, U^{(m)})$ be the certified coarse-fiber potential capacities from [20, Proposition 3.1] (re-verified from scratch in `verify_section_2_potentials`), and set*
$$\mathrm{one}(u) \;:=\; 4 - 4 p_0 - 2 p_1, \qquad \mu_m(u) \;:=\; \frac{C_1^{(m)}}{m}\,p_1 + \frac{M^{(m)}}{m}\,p_2 + \frac{U^{(m)}}{m}\,p_{\ge 3}. \tag{2.5}$$
*Then every queen-admissible subset $S \subseteq C_p(F) \cap W_m$ ($n = mp$) satisfies*
$$|S| \;\le\; \min\Bigl(\mathrm{one}(u_+),\; \mathrm{one}(u_-),\; \mu_m(u_+) + \mu_m(u_-)\Bigr)\,n \;+\; O_D\bigl(m p^{1/2}\bigr). \tag{2.6}$$

*Proof.* Set $S_{\mathrm{sm}} := \{(X, Y) \in S : (X \bmod p, Y \bmod p) \in V_{\mathrm{sm}}(F)(\mathbb{F}_p)\}$; by (2.1), $|S \setminus S_{\mathrm{sm}}| \le 2m |\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{C})| \le (D - 1)(D - 2)m$. Write each $(X, Y) \in S_{\mathrm{sm}}$ as $(x + ap, y + bp)$ with $r = (x, y) \in R \subseteq V_{\mathrm{sm}}(F)(\mathbb{F}_p) \subseteq \{0, \dots, p - 1\}^2$ and $(a, b) \in G_m := \{0, \dots, m - 1\}^2$. Because $S_{\mathrm{sm}}$ is queen-admissible, for every $r \in R$ the coarse fiber $B_r := \{(a, b) \in G_m : (x + ap, y + bp) \in S_{\mathrm{sm}}\}$ is a nonempty queen-admissible subset of $G_m$ (regardless of whether other points $(x, y') \in R$ share the same $x$ or $y$ residue!).

For any reversal-symmetric potential $\phi_m$ on $G_m$ satisfying $\phi_m(\mathrm{diag}(B)) + \phi_m(\mathrm{anti}(B)) \ge |B|$ for all queen-admissible $B \subseteq G_m$, summing over $r \in R$ gives $|S_{\mathrm{sm}}| = \sum_{r \in R} |B_r| \le \sum_{t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p} \sum_{r \in R : y - x \equiv t_0} \phi_m(\mathrm{diag}(B_r)) + \sum_{s_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p} \sum_{r \in R : y + x \equiv s_0} \phi_m(\mathrm{anti}(B_r))$. Fix $t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and let $r_1 = (x_1, y_1), \dots, r_k = (x_k, y_k)$ be the distinct points of $R$ with $y_i - x_i \equiv t_0 \pmod p$ (so $k \le k_+^*(t_0)$). Since $0 \le x_i, y_i \le p - 1$, each integer difference satisfies $y_i - x_i \in \{t_0, t_0 - p\}$, so the diagonal slices of $B_{r_1}, \dots, B_{r_k}$ pack into the $2m$ integer diagonal lines $Y - X \in \{t_0 - mp, \dots, t_0 + (m - 1)p\}$ (capacity $2$ per line) with relative shift $0$ or $1$, forcing $\sum_{i=1}^k \phi_m(\mathrm{diag}(B_{r_i})) \le C_1^{(m)}, M^{(m)}, U^{(m)}$ for $k = 1, 2, \ge 3$ respectively (and analogously for $y_i + x_i \in \{s_0, s_0 + p\}$). Single-direction clipping on the $2m$ integer lines over $t_0$ likewise bounds the count over $t_0$ by $0, 2m, 4m$ for $k = 0, 1, \ge 2$. Combining with Lemma 2.2 yields (2.6). Maximizing (2.6) over all non-exceptional distributions in degrees $2 \le d \le d_{\max}$ (verified explicitly for $2 \le m \le 25$ and symbolically via quadratic polynomial positivity for all $m \ge 17$ in `verify_section_2_potentials`) produces Table 1, and embedding any continuous window of side $n = jp + r$ ($j \ge k_0(d_{\max})$, $0 \le r < p$) into $W_{j+1}$ gives $|S| \le \frac{j + 1}{j} R^{(d_{\max})}(j + 1) n + O_D((n/p)p^{1/2}) \le C^{\mathrm{cont}}_{d_{\max}} n + O_D((n/p)p^{1/2}) < 1.80n + O_D((n/p)p^{1/2})$. $\square$

## 3. All Irreducible Plane Cubics ($D = 3$, Proof of Theorem 2)

Let $F(X, Y) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X, Y]$ ($p \ge 7$) be geometrically irreducible of total degree $D = 3$, so $d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
- **Case 1 ($d_{\mathrm{V}} = 1$ or $d_{\mathrm{H}} = 1$).** Up to $(X, Y) \leftrightarrow (Y, X)$, $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 1$ means $F(X, Y) = Q(X) Y - P(X)$ ($\gcd(P, Q) = 1$, $\max(\deg P, \deg Q + 1) = 3$). If $\deg Q \ge 1$, this is a rational function graph with $N(f) = 3$, so $|S| \le \frac{43}{24}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ across all $m \ge 2$ and $|S| \le \frac{102124}{56797}n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ for all $n \ge 12p$ by [20, Theorem 1]. If $Q = 1$, $Y = P(X)$ is a cubic polynomial graph; either $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(P) = S_3$ ($\delta \ge \frac{1}{3} \implies |S| \le \frac{4}{3}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$), or $P(X) = a(X - x_0)^3 + c$, where $P(U + x_0) \mp (U + x_0)$ has depressed discriminant $-4a(\mp 1)^3 - 27a^2(t - c \pm x_0)^2$, not a square in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[t]$, so $G_\pm^{\mathrm{geom}} = S_3$ (whose unique normalizing coset $\gamma_\pm = S_3$ has $u_{S_3} = (\frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{2}, 0, \frac{1}{6})$ and single-diagonal rate $\mathrm{one}(u_{S_3}) = 4 - 4(\frac{1}{3}) - 2(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{5}{3}$), giving $|S| \le \frac{5}{3}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ across all $m \ge 1$.
- **Case 2 ($d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}} \ge 2$, at least one non-exceptional).** Since $\min_{\delta > 0} \delta = \frac{1}{3}$ for $d \in \{2, 3\}$, Theorem 1(a) gives $|S| \le \frac{4}{3}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ across all $m \ge 1$.
- **Case 3 ($d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_{\mathrm{H}} = 3$, both $(3, C_3)$-exceptional; requires $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$).** Here $P_{\mathrm{V}} \notin \overline{C}$ and $P_{\mathrm{H}} \notin \overline{C}$, so $n_{\mathrm{V}} = n_{\mathrm{H}} = 0$ and Corollary 2.4(ii) forces $n_{\mathrm{other}} = 1$, with a unique $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational place $\tilde{Q}_0 \in \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ over $Q_0 = (1 : s_0 : 0) \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p)$ ($s_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$). Note that a degree-$2$ diagonal projection ($d_\varepsilon = 2$, $u_{S_2} = (\frac{1}{2}, 0, \frac{1}{2}, 0)$) has $\mathrm{one}(u_{S_2}) = 4 - 4(\frac{1}{2}) = 2$ because each residue diagonal lifts to $2m$ integer diagonals in $W_m$, so $d_\varepsilon = 2$ does *not* give a sub-$1.80n$ bound by single-direction clipping alone; in both genera $g(\tilde{C}) \in \{0, 1\}$, we instead show that at least one diagonal sign $\eta \in \{\pm 1\}$ has $d_\eta = 3$ and $G_\eta^{\mathrm{geom}} = S_3$:
  1. **Smooth $(3, 3)$ cubics ($g(\tilde{C}) = 1$).** Because $P_{\mathrm{V}} \notin \overline{C}$, $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\overline{C}}(1)$. Since $[\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\overline{C}) : \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathrm{V}})] = 3 = |C_3|$, a generator $\sigma_{\mathrm{V}} \in \mathrm{Aut}(\overline{C}/\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathrm{V}})$ preserves $\mathcal{O}_{\overline{C}}(1)$, hence lifts linearly to $\mathrm{PGL}_3(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ fixing every line $[X : Z] = \text{const}$ through $(0 : 1 : 0)$. Thus $\sigma_{\mathrm{V}}([X : Y : Z]) = [X : \omega Y + \ell_1 X + \ell_2 Z : Z]$ with $\omega^3 = 1, \omega \ne 1$ ($3 \nmid p$). Setting $U_Y := Y + \frac{\ell_1 X + \ell_2 Z}{\omega - 1}$ diagonalizes $\sigma_{\mathrm{V}}$ to $U_Y \mapsto \omega U_Y$; since $U_Y^3$ appears in $F$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{V}}$-invariance kills the $U_Y^2$ and $U_Y^1$ terms, giving $F(X, Y, Z) = a_3 U_Y^3 + G_3(X, Z)$, where expanding $F = a_3 Y^3 + B_1(X, Z) Y^2 + \dots$ shows $U_Y = Y + \frac{B_1(X, Z)}{3 a_3} = Y + \alpha_1 X + \alpha_0 Z$ ($\alpha_1, \alpha_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$). Symmetrically, $G_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{geom}} = C_3$ gives $F(X, Y, Z) = b_3 U_X^3 + H_3(Y, Z)$ with $U_X = X + \beta_1 Y + \beta_0 Z$ ($\beta_1, \beta_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$, $a_3 b_3 \ne 0$). Equating the $X^2 Y$ and $X Y^2$ coefficients yields $3 a_3 \alpha_1^2 = 3 b_3 \beta_1$ and $3 a_3 \alpha_1 = 3 b_3 \beta_1^2$. If $\alpha_1 \beta_1 \ne 0$, multiplying gives $\beta_1 = \alpha_1^{-1}$ and $b_3 = a_3 \alpha_1^3$, so $a_3(Y + \alpha_1 X + \alpha_0 Z)^3 - a_3(Y + \alpha_1 X + \alpha_1 \beta_0 Z)^3 = H_3(Y, Z) - G_3(X, Z)$; comparing the $X Y Z$ coefficient gives $6 a_3 \alpha_1(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1 \beta_0) = 0 \implies \alpha_0 = \alpha_1 \beta_0$, forcing $F = a_3(Y + \alpha_1 X + \alpha_0 Z)^3 + c Z^3$ (three concurrent lines over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, reducible, contradiction!). Hence $\alpha_1 = \beta_1 = 0$, and the $\mathbb{F}_p$-translation $U := X + \beta_0, V := Y + \alpha_0$ reduces $F(X, Y, 1) = 0$ to **$a U^3 + b V^3 + c = 0$** ($abc \ne 0$ by smoothness). Because $(a + b) + (a - b) = 2a \ne 0$ ($p \ge 7$), $a + \varepsilon b = 0$ holds for **at most one** sign $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$; choosing $\eta \in \{\pm 1\}$ with **$a + \eta b \ne 0$** ($d_\eta = 3$) and substituting $V = \eta(U + t)$ gives $P_\eta(U) = (a + \eta b)U^3 + 3\eta b t U^2 + 3\eta b t^2 U + (\eta b t^3 + c) = 0$, whose cubic discriminant over $\mathbb{Z}[a, b, c, t]$ is identically (`verify_section_3_cubics`)
     $$\mathrm{Disc}_U(P_\eta) \;=\; -27\Bigl(a^2 b^2 t^6 + 2\eta a b(a - \eta b) c\,t^3 + (a + \eta b)^2 c^2\Bigr), \tag{3.1}$$
     with $t^3$-quadratic discriminant $-2^4 \cdot 3^6 \eta a^3 b^3 c^2 \ne 0$ ($p \ge 7$). Thus $\mathrm{Disc}_U(P_\eta)$ is not a square in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[t]$, forcing $G_\eta^{\mathrm{geom}} = S_3$ ($\gamma_\eta = S_3$, $\mathrm{one}(u_{S_3}) = \frac{5}{3}$) and $|S| \le \frac{5}{3}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$.
  2. **Singular $(3, 3)$ cubics ($g(\tilde{C}) = 0$, $\tilde{C} \cong \mathbb{P}^1_t$).** Let $\nu(t) = [X(t) : Y(t) : Z(t)]$ ($\gcd = 1$, $\max\deg = 3$). Because $\gamma_{\mathrm{V}}$ lies in the outer coset $S_3 \setminus C_3$, $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}^{-1}(\infty) = Z^{-1}(0)$ is either unramified of cycle type $(1, 2)$ ($3$ distinct roots $\{t_0, t_1, t_2\}$) or totally ramified ($e = 3$ at $\tilde{Q}_0$). If $Z^{-1}(0) = \{t_0, t_1, t_2\}$ were unramified, the cyclic order-$3$ deck groups of $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}(t) = X(t)/Z(t)$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}(t) = Y(t)/Z(t)$ in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ would both act transitively on $\{t_0, t_1, t_2\}$; since a Möbius transformation is uniquely determined by the images of $3$ points, $t_0 \mapsto t_1 \mapsto t_2 \mapsto t_0$ generates the **unique** cyclic order-$3$ subgroup $H \le \mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ stabilizing $\{t_0, t_1, t_2\}$ transitively, forcing $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\pi_{\mathrm{V}}(t)) = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)^H = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\pi_{\mathrm{H}}(t))$ and $Y(t) = \lambda X(t) + \mu Z(t)$ ($\overline{C}$ is a line), contradiction! Therefore $Z(t)$ has a triple root at $\tilde{Q}_0$; sending $\tilde{Q}_0 \mapsto \infty$ sets $Z(t) = 1$, so $X(t), Y(t) \in \mathbb{F}_p[t]$ are cubic polynomials with $G^{\mathrm{geom}} = C_3$, i.e., $U := X - x_0 = \alpha(t - t_1)^3$ and $V := Y - y_0 = \beta(t - t_2)^3$ ($t_1 \ne t_2 \in \mathbb{F}_p$). Reparameterizing $t \mapsto (t_1 - t_2)t + t_1$ gives $U = \tilde{\alpha} t^3, V = \tilde{\beta}(t - 1)^3$ ($\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta} \ne 0$, implicit equation $(\tilde{\beta} U - \tilde{\alpha} V - \tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta})^3 - 27\tilde{\alpha}^2\tilde{\beta}^2 U V = 0$). Since $(\tilde{\beta} - \tilde{\alpha}) + (\tilde{\beta} + \tilde{\alpha}) = 2\tilde{\beta} \ne 0$ ($p \ge 7$), at most one sign $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$ satisfies $\tilde{\beta} - \varepsilon\tilde{\alpha} = 0$; choosing $\eta \in \{\pm 1\}$ with **$\tilde{\beta} - \eta\tilde{\alpha} \ne 0$** ($d_\eta = 3$), the fiber polynomial $(\tilde{\beta} - \eta\tilde{\alpha})t^3 - 3\tilde{\beta} t^2 + 3\tilde{\beta} t - (\tilde{\beta} + s) = 0$ has exact discriminant over $\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{\alpha}, \tilde{\beta}, s]$ (`verify_section_3_cubics`)
     $$\Delta_\eta(s) \;=\; -27\Bigl((\tilde{\beta} - \eta\tilde{\alpha})^2 s^2 + 2\eta\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}(\tilde{\beta} + \eta\tilde{\alpha}) s + \tilde{\alpha}^2\tilde{\beta}^2\Bigr), \tag{3.2}$$
     with $s$-discriminant $2^4 \cdot 3^6 \eta \tilde{\alpha}^3 \tilde{\beta}^3 \ne 0$ ($p \ge 7$). Thus $G_\eta^{\mathrm{geom}} = S_3$ ($\mathrm{one}(u_{S_3}) = \frac{5}{3}$) and $|S| \le \frac{5}{3}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$.

*Continuous windows $n \ge 12p$.* In Case 1 with $\deg Q \ge 1$, $|S| \le \frac{102124}{56797}n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ holds for all $n \ge 12p$ by [20, Theorem 1]. In every remaining case above, $|S \cap W_m(x_0, y_0)| \le \frac{5}{3}mp + O(mp^{1/2})$ holds for all $(x_0, y_0)$ and all $m \ge 1$. For any square window $W(x_0, y_0; n)$ of side $n = jp + q$ ($j \ge 12$, $0 \le q < p$), covering $W(x_0, y_0; n)$ by $W_j(x_0, y_0)$ plus $q$ vertical and $q$ horizontal integer boundary lines gives $|S| \le \frac{5}{3}jp + 4q + O(jp^{1/2})$, while $W(x_0, y_0; n) \subseteq W_{j+1}(x_0, y_0)$ gives $|S| \le \frac{5}{3}(j + 1)p + O(jp^{1/2})$. Taking the positive convex combination with weights $\frac{5(j+1)}{12j+5}$ and $\frac{7j}{12j+5}$ (`verify_section_3_cubics`),
$$\frac{5(j + 1)}{12 j + 5}\Bigl(\frac{5}{3} j p + 4 q\Bigr) + \frac{7 j}{12 j + 5}\Bigl(\frac{5}{3}(j + 1) p\Bigr) \;=\; \frac{20(j + 1)}{12 j + 5}\,(j p + q) \;\le\; \frac{260}{149}\,n \;<\; \frac{102124}{56797}\,n \;<\; 1.80\,n \qquad (j \ge 12),$$
completing the proof of Theorem 2. $\square$

## 4. All Irreducible Plane Quartics across All Genera ($D = 4$, Proof of Theorem 3)

Let $F(X, Y) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X, Y]$ ($p \ge 7$) be geometrically irreducible of total degree $D = 4$, so $g(\tilde{C}) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ and $d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}} \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$. If $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 1$ or $d_{\mathrm{H}} = 1$, then up to coordinate swap $F(X, Y) = Q(X) Y - P(X)$ is either a quartic polynomial graph (where $V_4$ has no $4$-cycle, so $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ is non-exceptional and $|S| \le \frac{3}{2}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$) or a rational function graph with $N(f) = 4$, giving $|S| \le \frac{43}{24}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ across all $m \ge 2$ and $|S| \le \frac{34971}{19486}n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ for all $n \ge 14p$ (hence all $n \ge 29p$, since $\frac{34971}{19486} < \frac{22681}{12615} < 1.80$) by [20, Theorem 1]. If $d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}} \ge 2$ and at least one is non-exceptional, Theorem 1(a),(c) gives $|S| \le \frac{3}{2}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$ (and embedding $n = jp + q$ into $W_{j+1}$ for $j \ge 29$ gives $\frac{j+1}{j}\cdot\frac{3}{2} \le \frac{45}{29} < \frac{22681}{12615} < 1.80$). Thus assume both $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ are exceptional ($d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}} \in \{3, 4\}$).

### 4.1 No $(4, V_4)$ Projection on HV-Double-Exceptional Plane Quartics

**Proposition 4.1.** *Over $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p \ge 7$), **no** geometrically irreducible plane quartic $\overline{C} \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of any genus $g(\tilde{C}) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ has both $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ exceptional with $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 4$ or $d_{\mathrm{H}} = 4$.*

*Proof.* By symmetry, suppose for contradiction that $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 4$ (so $P_{\mathrm{V}} \notin \overline{C}$, $n_{\mathrm{V}} = 0$) and $\pi_{\mathrm{V}} \colon \tilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is $(4, V_4)$-exceptional, while $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ is exceptional of degree $d_{\mathrm{H}} \in \{3, 4\}$.

*Step 1 (Absence of $\mathbb{F}_p$-branch points and genus restriction $g(\tilde{C}) \in \{0, 3\}$).* By Theorem 1(c), the unique exceptional group configuration at degree $4$ is $G^{\mathrm{geom}} = V_4 \trianglelefteq G^{\mathrm{arith}} = A_4$, where the Frobenius coset $A_4 \setminus V_4$ consists of $3$-cycles (cycle type $(1, 3)$). Every non-identity element of $V_4$ has cycle type $(2, 2)$, so every branch point $b \in \mathbb{P}^1(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ of $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ has inertia subgroup $I_b \cong C_2 \le V_4$ and fiber ramification $(2, 2)$ (Hurwitz contribution $2$). If $b$ lay in $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$, decomposition Frobenius $\varphi \in A_4 \setminus V_4$ would normalize $I_b$, impossible since $N_{A_4}(C_2) = V_4$ ($3$-cycles permute the three involutions of $V_4$ transitively). Consequently:
1. **No** branch point of $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ lies in $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$; in particular, $b = \infty$ is **unramified** of cycle type $(1, 3)$ (so $\overline{C} \cap L_\infty$ consists of $4$ distinct smooth points: $1$ point $Q_0 \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p) \setminus \{P_{\mathrm{V}}\}$ and $3$ $\mathrm{Frob}_p$-conjugate points $Q_1, Q_2, Q_3 \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_{p^3}) \setminus L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p)$); and
2. every $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p / \mathbb{F}_p)$-orbit of branch points of $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ has size divisible by $3$ ($3 \mid B$), so Riemann--Hurwitz $2 g(\tilde{C}) - 2 = 4(-2) + 2 B = 2 B - 8$ with $0 \le g(\tilde{C}) \le 3$ forces **$3 \mid B \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$**, leaving **only $B = 3$ ($g = 0$) or $B = 6$ ($g = 3$)** (immediately ruling out $g = 1$ and $g = 2$).

*Step 2 (Elimination of $g(\tilde{C}) = 3$).* If $g(\tilde{C}) = 3$, $\overline{C}$ is a smooth plane quartic, hence canonically embedded ($\omega_{\overline{C}} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\overline{C}}(1)$). Since $[\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\overline{C}) : \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathrm{V}})] = 4 = |V_4|$, $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ is Galois with deck group $V_4 \le \mathrm{Aut}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p}(\overline{C}) \le \mathrm{PGL}_3(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ fixing every line through $P_{\mathrm{V}} = (0 : 1 : 0)$. Every projective transformation fixing all lines $[X : Z] = \text{const}$ through $(0 : 1 : 0)$ has the form $M_{\lambda, \ell_1, \ell_2}([X : Y : Z]) = [X : \lambda Y + \ell_1 X + \ell_2 Z : Z]$ ($\lambda \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$). The homomorphism $M_{\lambda, \ell_1, \ell_2} \mapsto \lambda \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$ has kernel $\cong (\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p, +)^2$ of exponent $p$; since $\gcd(|V_4|, p) = \gcd(4, p) = 1$, $V_4$ injects into $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$, a contradiction since finite subgroups of $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$ are cyclic whereas $V_4 \cong C_2 \times C_2$.

*Step 3 (Elimination of $g(\tilde{C}) = 0$).* If $g(\tilde{C}) = 0$ ($\tilde{C} \cong \mathbb{P}^1_t$), let $\nu(t) = [X(t) : Y(t) : Z(t)]$ ($\gcd(X, Y, Z) = 1$, $\max\deg = 4$). By Step 1, $\{Z(t) = 0\} = \{t_0\} \cup \{r_1, r_2, r_3\}$ is unramified of cycle type $(1, 3)$. If $d_{\mathrm{H}} = 3$, then $P_{\mathrm{H}} \in \overline{C} \cap L_\infty$ must be the unique $\mathbb{F}_p$-point $Q_0$, so $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\infty) = \{r_1, r_2, r_3\}$ contains **zero** $\mathbb{F}_p$-places on $\tilde{C}$, contradicting Lemma 2.3 for $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$. Hence $d_{\mathrm{H}} = 4$ as well, so $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}(t) = X(t)/Z(t)$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}(t) = Y(t)/Z(t)$ are two $(4, V_4)$-Galois maps on $\mathbb{P}^1_t$ sharing the exact same unramified $4$-point pole fiber $Z^{-1}(0) = \{\infty, r_1, r_2, r_3\}$ (after sending $t_0 \mapsto \infty$, shifting $r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = 0$, $Q_3(t) = t^3 + a t + b$). Because $V_4$ acts regularly on every unramified fiber and a Möbius transformation is uniquely determined by $3$ points, the three deck involutions swapping $\infty \leftrightarrow r_i$ and $r_j \leftrightarrow r_k$, namely $\tau_i(t) = r_i + \frac{3 r_i^2 + a}{t - r_i}$ (`verify_section_4_quartics`), are **uniquely determined** by $\{\infty, r_1, r_2, r_3\}$. Thus $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ share the exact same deck group $H \le \mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$, hence $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\pi_{\mathrm{V}}(t)) = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)^H = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(\pi_{\mathrm{H}}(t))$; since both have pole fiber $Z^{-1}(0)$, $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}(t) = \lambda \pi_{\mathrm{V}}(t) + \mu$, forcing $Y(t) = \lambda X(t) + \mu Z(t)$ ($\overline{C}$ is a line), contradiction. $\square$

### 4.2 Diagonal Non-Exceptionality on All $(3, 3)$ Plane Quartics ($g \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$)

By Proposition 4.1, every HV-double-exceptional irreducible plane quartic satisfies $d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_{\mathrm{H}} = 3$, i.e., $m_{P_{\mathrm{V}}}(\overline{C}) = m_{P_{\mathrm{H}}}(\overline{C}) = 1$. Hence $P_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{H}}$ are **smooth** $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational points of $\overline{C}$, giving $n_{\mathrm{V}} = n_{\mathrm{H}} = 1$, and Corollary 2.4(i) forces **$n_{\mathrm{other}} = 0$**. Now examine both diagonal centers $P_+ = (1 : 1 : 0)$ and $P_- = (1 : -1 : 0)$ simultaneously:
1. Since $P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}, P_+, P_-$ are four distinct points on $L_\infty$, Bézout's theorem on $\overline{C} \cap L_\infty$ gives $1 + 1 + m_{P_+}(\overline{C}) + m_{P_-}(\overline{C}) \le 4$, so $m_{P_+}(\overline{C}) + m_{P_-}(\overline{C}) \le 2$.
2. For each $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$, $m_{P_\varepsilon}(\overline{C}) = 1$ is **impossible**: $P_\varepsilon \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p) \setminus \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}\}$ would be a smooth $\mathbb{F}_p$-point of $\overline{C}$, giving $n_{\mathrm{other}} \ge 1$, contradicting $n_{\mathrm{other}} = 0$. Consequently, $(m_{P_+}(\overline{C}), m_{P_-}(\overline{C})) \in \{(0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 2)\}$, equivalently **$(d_+, d_-) \in \{(4, 4), (2, 4), (4, 2)\}$**.
3. Whenever $d_\varepsilon = 2$, $\pi_\varepsilon$ is **automatically non-exceptional** by Theorem 1(c) ($S_2$ admits no exceptional cosets; note that $u_{S_2} = (\frac{1}{2}, 0, \frac{1}{2}, 0)$ has single-diagonal rate $\mathrm{one}(u_{S_2}) = 2$, so $\pi_\varepsilon$ does *not* bound $|S|$ below $1.80n$ alone, and instead enters Proposition 2.5 jointly with $\pi_{-\varepsilon}$).
4. Whenever $d_\varepsilon = 4$, both smooth $\mathbb{F}_p$-places at $P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}} \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p) \setminus \{P_\varepsilon\}$ belong to $\pi_\varepsilon^{-1}(\infty) \cap \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)$, so $\bigl|\pi_\varepsilon^{-1}(\infty) \cap \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)\bigr| \ge 2 \ne 1$, and Lemma 2.3 rules out $(4, V_4)$-exceptionality!

Thus across **all three** possible degree pairs $(d_+, d_-) \in \{(4, 4), (2, 4), (4, 2)\}$, **both** $\pi_+$ and $\pi_-$ have degrees in $\{2, 4\} \subseteq \{2, 3, 4\}$ and are non-exceptional. Applying Proposition 2.5 with $d_{\max} = 4$ (which maximizes (2.6) over all pairs $(u_+, u_-)$ of non-exceptional distributions of degrees $2 \le d \le 4$, including $(u_{S_2}, u_-)$ and $(u_+, u_{S_2})$) yields $|S| \le \frac{43}{24}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 2$ and $|S| \le \frac{22681}{12615}n + O((n/p)p^{1/2}) < 1.80n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ for all $n \ge 29p$, proving Theorem 3. $\square$

## 5. Smooth Plane Sextics and Non-Linear Separated Curves $f(X) + g(Y) = 0$ (Proof of Theorem 4)

### 5.1 Smooth Plane Sextics ($D = 6$) and Degree Reduction for Separated Curves

**Proof of Theorem 4(i) (Smooth Plane Sextics).** Let $\overline{C} \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ ($p \ge 7$) be a smooth plane sextic ($D = 6$, $g = 10$). Because $\overline{C}$ is smooth, $m_P(\overline{C}) \in \{0, 1\}$ for every $P \in L_\infty(\mathbb{F}_p)$, so $d_{\mathrm{V}}, d_{\mathrm{H}}, d_+, d_- \in \{5, 6\}$. By Theorem 1(c), $S_6$ has **zero** exceptional normalizing cosets, so if $d_{\mathrm{V}} = 6$, $d_{\mathrm{H}} = 6$, or either of $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}, \pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ is non-exceptional, Theorem 1(a),(c) gives $|S| \le \frac{8}{5}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$ (and for $n = jp + q$ with $j \ge 76$, $\frac{j+1}{j}\cdot\frac{8}{5} \le \frac{77}{76}\cdot\frac{8}{5} = \frac{154}{95} < \frac{29236}{16245} < 1.80$). In the sole remaining case $d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_{\mathrm{H}} = 5$ with both $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ exceptional, $P_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{H}}$ are smooth $\mathbb{F}_p$-points of $\overline{C}$ ($n_{\mathrm{V}} = n_{\mathrm{H}} = 1$), so Corollary 2.4(i) forces $n_{\mathrm{other}} = 0$. Since $\overline{C}$ is smooth, any point of $\overline{C}(\mathbb{F}_p) \cap (L_\infty \setminus \{P_{\mathrm{V}}, P_{\mathrm{H}}\})$ would give $n_{\mathrm{other}} \ge 1$; hence $P_+ \notin \overline{C}$ and $P_- \notin \overline{C}$, forcing **$d_+ = d_- = 6$**. Because degree $6$ admits **no** exceptional cosets, both $\pi_+$ and $\pi_-$ are automatically non-exceptional, and Proposition 2.5 ($d_{\max} = 6$) yields $|S| \le \frac{431}{240}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 2$ and $|S| \le \frac{29236}{16245}n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ for all $n \ge 76p$. $\square$

Now let $F(X, Y) = f(X) + g(Y) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X, Y]$ ($p \ge 7$) be geometrically irreducible with $1 \le d_1 := \deg f \le 6$, $1 \le d_2 := \deg g \le 6$, and **$\max(d_1, d_2) \ge 2$** ($D = \max(d_1, d_2) \in \{2, \dots, 6\}$); here $d_{\mathrm{H}} = d_1$ ($\pi_{\mathrm{H}} \colon f(X) = -g(Y)$) and $d_{\mathrm{V}} = d_2$ ($\pi_{\mathrm{V}} \colon g(Y) = -f(X)$).

**Remark 5.1 (Necessity of $\max(\deg f, \deg g) \ge 2$ for Queen-Admissible Subsets).** At $D = 1$ ($(\deg f, \deg g) = (1, 1)$), the queen-admissible conclusion of Theorem 4(ii) fails: for $F(X, Y) = Y - 2X$ and any prime $p \ge 7$, reducing $(X, Y, Y - X, Y + X) \equiv (x, 2x, x, 3x) \pmod p$ yields four permutations of $\mathbb{F}_p$, so $S_p := \{(x + ap, (2x \bmod p) + bp) : 0 \le x < p,\ a, b \in \{0, 1\}\} \subset C_p(Y - 2X) \cap W_2$ ($n = 2p$) meets each queen line inside at most one $2 \times 2$ lift tile ($\le 2$ points per queen line) while having size $|S_p| = 4p = 2n > \frac{43}{24}n + O(p^{1/2})$ (`verify_section_5_sextics_and_separated`). By contrast, $S_p$ contains the Euclidean collinear triple $(0, 0), (1, 2), (2, 4)$: any *Euclidean* no-three-in-line subset of a linear congruence $aX + bY + c \equiv 0 \pmod p$ ($ab \ne 0$, $|a|, |b| < p$) meets each of the $\le (|a| + |b|)m$ integer lines $aX + bY = k p - c$ intersecting $W_m$ in at most $2$ points, giving $|S| \le 2(|a| + |b|)m = O_{a,b}(m)$.

Recall the elementary classification of degree-$5$ $C_5/D_5$ polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p \ge 7$): if $h(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ has degree $5$ and $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) \in \{C_5, D_5\}$, translating $X = U + x_0$ ($x_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$ since $5 \nmid p$) to kill $U^4$, either $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) = C_5$ (cyclic of order $5$ totally ramified at $\infty$, forcing $h(U + x_0) = a U^5 + c_0 = a D_5(U, 0) + c_0$), or $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) = D_5$, where the quadratic subfield of the Galois closure $L / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(U)$ is ramified only over the unique unramified sheet above each of the $2$ finite $(1, 2, 2)$ branch points ($2 g_L - 2 = 2(-2) + 2(1) = -2 \implies L = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(z)$ with $D_5 = \langle z \mapsto \zeta_5 z, z \mapsto \alpha z^{-1} \rangle$, $U = z + \alpha z^{-1}$, and $h(U + x_0) = a(z^5 + \alpha^5 z^{-5}) + c_0 = a D_5(U, \alpha) + c_0$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$).

Up to symmetry $(d_1, d_2) \leftrightarrow (d_2, d_1)$, all non-linear pairs $(d_1, d_2) \in \{1, \dots, 6\}^2 \setminus \{(1, 1)\}$ reduce as follows:
1. **$d_1 \in \{2, 6\}$ or $d_2 \in \{2, 6\}$:** $S_2$ and $S_6$ have zero exceptional cosets ($\delta \ge \frac{1}{3}$), so HV clipping gives $|S| \le \frac{4}{3}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 1$ (and $\frac{j+1}{j}\cdot\frac{4}{3} \le \frac{70}{51} < \frac{70217}{39015}$ for $j \ge 34$).
2. **$d_2 = 1$ with $d_1 \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ ($Y = -\frac{1}{g_1}(f(X) + g_0)$, since $d_1 = 1$ is excluded by $\max(d_1, d_2) \ge 2$ and $d_1 \in \{2, 6\}$ is covered by item 1):** If $d_1 \in \{3, 4\}$, Theorems 2 and 3 apply ($D = d_1$, $k_0 \in \{12, 29\} \le 34$, with $\max(\frac{102124}{56797}, \frac{22681}{12615}) < \frac{70217}{39015}$). If $d_1 = 5$, either $f$ is non-exceptional ($|S| \le \frac{8}{5}n + O(mp^{1/2})$, with $\frac{j+1}{j}\cdot\frac{8}{5} \le \frac{28}{17} < \frac{70217}{39015}$ for $j \ge 34$), or $f$ is exceptional, in which case $f(U + x_0) = a D_5(U, \alpha) + c_0$ ($\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_p$); both diagonal shifts $a D_5(U, \alpha) \mp g_1 U$ ($g_1 \ne 0$) alter the $U^1$-coefficient away from $5 a \alpha^2$ while keeping $U^4, U^2$ zero, so neither can lie in $\{C_5, D_5\}$, and Proposition 2.5 ($d_{\max} = 5$) gives $|S| \le \frac{43}{24}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ ($k_0 = 34$).
3. **$(d_1, d_2) \in \{(3, 3), (4, 4)\}$:** Here $D = \max(d_1, d_2) \in \{3, 4\}$, already proved in Theorems 2 and 3 ($k_0 \le 29 \le 34$).
4. **$(d_1, d_2) \in \{(4, 3), (4, 5)\}$:** Over $Y = \infty$, $f(X) = -g(Y)$ has $\gcd(4, d_2) = 1$, so the inertia generator of $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ over $Y = \infty$ is a **single $4$-cycle**; since $V_4$ has exponent $2$, $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ is never $(4, V_4)$-exceptional ($|S| \le \frac{3}{2}n + O(mp^{1/2})$, with $\frac{j+1}{j}\cdot\frac{3}{2} \le \frac{105}{68} < \frac{70217}{39015}$ for $j \ge 34$).
5. **$(d_1, d_2) \in \{(5, 3), (5, 5)\}$ with both $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ exceptional (else $|S| \le \frac{8}{5}n + O(mp^{1/2})$):** Let $L_f / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$ ($t = f(X)$, group $G_f \le S_5$) and $K_g = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(Y)$ ($t = -g(Y)$, degree $d_2 \in \{3, 5\}$). Then $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_{\mathrm{H}}) = \mathrm{Gal}(L_f / L_f \cap K_g) \le G_f$ has order $\in \{5, 10\}$ and index dividing $d_2 \in \{3, 5\}$, so $|G_f|$ divides $30$ (for $d_2 = 3$) or $50$ (for $d_2 = 5$). Since the orders of transitive subgroups of $S_5$ are $\{5, 10, 20, 60, 120\}$, none of $\{20, 60, 120\}$ divides $30$ or $50$, forcing $G_f \in \{C_5, D_5\}$. Similarly, for $d_2 = 3$, $|G_g| \in \{3, 6\}$ is coprime to $5$, forcing $G_g = C_3$. Thus after $\mathbb{F}_p$-translations $U = X - x_0, V = Y - y_0$, **every** HV-double-exceptional separated curve is either $a D_5(U, \alpha) + b V^3 + c = 0$ or $a D_5(U, \alpha) + b D_5(V, \beta) + c = 0$ ($ab \ne 0$, $\alpha, \beta, c \in \mathbb{F}_p$), with $d_+, d_- \in \{4, 5\}$.

### 5.2 Separated $(5, 3)$ Curves: Hurwitz Pigeonhole and $\gcd = 40$ Bézout Elimination

Let $C \colon a D_5(U, \alpha) + b V^3 + c = 0$ ($ab \ne 0$, $\alpha, c \in \mathbb{F}_p$, $p \ge 7$), and fix $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$. Absorbing $\varepsilon$ into $b$, consider $\pi_\varepsilon \colon V - U = t$, i.e., $P(U) := a(U^5 - 5\alpha U^3 + 5\alpha^2 U) + b(U + t)^3 + c = 0$ ($d_\varepsilon = 5$), and suppose for contradiction that $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_\varepsilon) \in \{C_5, D_5\}$.
- If $\alpha = c = 0$ ($a U^5 + b V^3 = 0$), $\tilde{C} \cong \mathbb{P}^1_s$ via $U = s^3, V = \gamma s^5$ ($\gamma^3 = -a/b$), and $\pi_\varepsilon(s) = s^3(\gamma s^2 - 1)$ has ramification $(3, 1, 1)$ ($3$-cycle) over $t = 0$, impossible in $C_5$ or $D_5$.
- Otherwise, $H(U) := a D_5(U, \alpha) + c$ has either $5$ simple roots ($g(\tilde{C}) = 4$ by Riemann--Hurwitz on $b V^3 = -H(U)$: $2g - 2 = -6 + 6 \cdot 2 = 6$) or $1$ simple root and $2$ double roots when $\alpha = r^2 \ne 0, c = \pm 2 a r^5$, since $(U - 2r)(U^2 + r U - r^2)^2 = D_5(U, r^2) - 2 r^5$ (each double root has $\gcd(3, 2) = 1 \implies e = 3$, giving $2g - 2 = -6 + 4 \cdot 2 = 2$, $g(\tilde{C}) = 2$).
- Under $\pi_\varepsilon$, $t = \infty$ is totally ramified ($e_\infty = 5$, contribution $4$). No finite $t \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ can be a $5$-cycle $a(U - u_0)^5$: $[U^4] = 0 \implies u_0 = 0$, $[U^2] = 3bt = 0 \implies t = 0$, $[U^1] = 5a\alpha^2 = 0 \implies \alpha = 0$, and $[U^3] = b = 0$, contradiction. Hence **every** finite branch fiber of $\pi_\varepsilon$ has cycle type $(1, 2, 2)$ (contribution $2$), so Riemann--Hurwitz $2 g(\tilde{C}) - 2 = 5(-2) + 4 + 2 N_{122}$ forces **$N_{122} = g(\tilde{C}) + 2 \in \{4, 6\} \ge 4$** distinct $(1, 2, 2)$ fibers!

Each $(1, 2, 2)$ fiber factors as $P(U) = a(U - 2u)(U^2 + u U + v)^2 = a\bigl(U^5 + (2v - 3u^2)U^3 - 2u(u^2 + v)U^2 + (v^2 - 4u^2 v)U - 2u v^2\bigr)$. Setting $A := a\alpha$, $B := b - 5 A$, and $Z := a u^2$:
1. $[U^3]$ and $[U^2]$ determine $v = \frac{3 Z + B}{2 a}$ and $t = -\frac{u(5 Z + B)}{3 b}$ uniquely from $u$ (**independent of $c$**);
2. $[U^1]$ gives the **cubic** equation in $Z = a u^2$ (**independent of $c$**):
   $$E_1(Z) \;:=\; 100 Z^3 + (40 B + 45 b) Z^2 + (4 B^2 + 6 b B) Z + (60 b A^2 - 3 b B^2) \;=\; 0; \tag{5.1}$$
3. $[U^0]$ gives $c = -b t^3 - 2 a u v^2 = -\frac{u}{54 a b^2} E_2(Z)$, where $E_2(Z) := -250 Z^4 - 150 B Z^3 + (-30 B^2 + 243 b^2) Z^2 + (-2 B^3 + 162 b^2 B) Z + 27 b^2 B^2$.

Because $\deg_Z E_1 = 3$ ($100 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$ for $p \ge 7$), $E_1(a u^2) = 0$ admits at most $3$ opposite pairs $\{\pm u_1, \pm u_2, \pm u_3\}$ (at most $5$ values of $u$ if $u = 0$ is a root). Since $N_{122} \ge 4$, the Pigeonhole Principle forces at least one **nonzero** opposite pair $\{+u_*, -u_*\}$ ($u_* \ne 0$) to appear simultaneously, yielding $c = -\frac{u_*}{54 a b^2} E_2(Z_*) = +\frac{u_*}{54 a b^2} E_2(Z_*)$, hence **$c = 0$**! Once $c = 0$, we must have $\alpha \ne 0$ (since $\alpha = c = 0$ was already ruled out), so $H(U) = a U(U^4 - 5\alpha U^2 + 5\alpha^2)$ has $5$ simple roots ($\mathrm{Disc}_{U^2} = 5\alpha^2 \ne 0$), upgrading the genus to $g(\tilde{C}) = 4$ and **$N_{122} = 6$**! For $E_1(a u^2) = 0$ to yield $6$ distinct values of $u$, $E_1(Z)$ must have **$3$ distinct nonzero roots $Z_1, Z_2, Z_3 \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$**, and $c = 0$ forces **$E_2(Z_1) = E_2(Z_2) = E_2(Z_3) = 0$**. Over $\mathbb{Z}[A, b, Z]$, we have the exact pseudo-remainder identity (`verify_section_5_sextics_and_separated`):
$$8 E_2(Z) + (20 Z - 9 b + 4 B) E_1(Z) \;=\; b\bigl(3 f_2 Z^2 + 30 f_1 Z + 3 f_0\bigr), \tag{5.2}$$
where $f_2 = 100 A + 493 b$, $f_1 = -20 A^2 - 183 A b + 39 b^2$, and $f_0 = 100 A^3 + 1625 A^2 b - 750 A b^2 + 77 b^3$. Because the degree-$\le 2$ polynomial $3 f_2 Z^2 + 30 f_1 Z + 3 f_0$ vanishes at all $3$ distinct roots $Z_1, Z_2, Z_3$ and $30 b \ne 0$ ($p \ge 7$), we obtain **$f_2 = f_1 = f_0 = 0$**! Over $\mathbb{Z}[A, b]$, setting $s_0 = 151209097$, $t_0 = 21994$, and $M(A, b) = 100(10000 A^2 - 49300 A b + 493^2 b^2) + 162500 b(100 A - 493 b) - 7500000 b^2$, we have the explicit integral Bézout identity (`verify_section_5_sextics_and_separated`)
$$\bigl(s_0 b(2000 A + 8440 b) + t_0 M(A, b)\bigr) f_2 + 10000 s_0 b\,f_1 - 1000000 t_0\,f_0 \;=\; 40\,b^3 \qquad (40 = 2^3 \cdot 5), \tag{5.3}$$
contradicting $b \ne 0$ for **every** prime $p \ge 7$. Thus both $\pi_+$ and $\pi_-$ are non-exceptional.

### 5.3 Separated $(5, 5)$ Curves: Dickson--Catalan Laurent Series and Palindromic $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ Bézout Certificate

Finally, let $C \colon a D_5(U, \alpha) + b D_5(V, \beta) + c = 0$ ($ab \ne 0$, $\alpha, \beta, c \in \mathbb{F}_p$, $p \ge 7$) have both $\pi_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{V}}$ exceptional. Fix $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$ and substitute $V = \varepsilon(U + t)$ (noting $D_5(\varepsilon X, \beta) = \varepsilon D_5(X, \beta)$).

1. **Fermat Quintic Subcase $(\alpha, \beta) = (0, 0)$ ($a U^5 + b V^5 + c = 0$, $abc \ne 0$ since $c = 0$ is reducible over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$).**
   - If $a + \varepsilon b = 0$, setting $U = W - \frac{t}{2}$ yields $160 t W^4 + 80 t^3 W^2 + (2 t^5 - 32 c/a) = 0$, which has degree $d_\varepsilon = 4$ and a simple pole at $t = 0$ ($4$-cycle inertia), so $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_\varepsilon) = D_4$ (non-exceptional).
   - If $a + \varepsilon b \ne 0$ ($d_\varepsilon = 5$), $\partial_U(a U^5 + \varepsilon b(U + t)^5 + c) = 5(a U^4 + \varepsilon b(U + t)^4) = 0$ holds at $\mu := \frac{U + t}{U}$ satisfying $\mu^4 = K := -\frac{a}{\varepsilon b} \notin \{0, 1\}$, giving $U = \frac{t}{\mu - 1}$ and critical values $t^5 = \frac{c}{a}(\mu - 1)^4$ (each having $\partial_U^2 = 20 a U^3(1 - \mu^{-1}) \ne 0$, hence simple ramification $e = 2$). Using $(\mu - 1)^4 - (-\mu - 1)^4 = -8\mu(\mu^2 + 1)$ and $|(-2 + i)^4 - 1|^2 = |-8 - 24i|^2 = 640 = 2^7 \cdot 5$ (`verify_section_5_sextics_and_separated`), even when $K \in \{-4, -1/4\}$ at least two roots $\mu$ yield distinct nonzero values of $(\mu - 1)^4$ for all $p \ge 7$, producing branch fibers with a **single simple transposition** $(2, 1, 1, 1) \in G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_\varepsilon)$, forcing $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_\varepsilon) = S_5$ (non-exceptional).

2. **Cancellation Subcase $a + \varepsilon b = 0$ with $(\alpha, \beta) \ne (0, 0)$ (requires $p \equiv \pm 2 \pmod 5$, since at least one of $\alpha, \beta$ is nonzero so $D_5$ permutes $\mathbb{F}_p$ only when $\gcd(5, p^2 - 1) = 1$).**
   - If $\alpha = \beta$ ($c \ne 0$, since $c = 0$ contains the line $U - \varepsilon V = 0$), setting $U = W - \frac{t}{2}$ gives the exact biquadratic
     $$160 t W^4 + (80 t^3 - 480\alpha t) W^2 + (2 t^5 - 40\alpha t^3 + 160\alpha^2 t - 32 c/a) \;=\; 0, \tag{5.4}$$
     which again has a $4$-cycle pole at $t = 0$ ($c \ne 0$), forcing $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_\varepsilon) = D_4$ (non-exceptional).
   - If $\alpha \ne \beta$, then $d_\varepsilon = 4$; along the smooth branch at $P_\varepsilon = (1 : \varepsilon : 0)$, $5\frac{t}{U} \sim -5(\alpha - \beta)\frac{1}{U^2} \to 0$ ($t \to 0$), while as $t \to \infty$, setting $U = t(z - \frac{1}{2})$ gives the unramified infinity quartic $80 z^4 + 40 z^2 + 1 = 0$, whose quadratic discriminant in $z^2$ is $40^2 - 320 = 1280 = 2^8 \cdot 5$. Because $p \equiv \pm 2 \pmod 5$, $\bigl(\frac{5}{p}\bigr) = -1$, so $80 z^4 + 40 z^2 + 1$ has **zero** roots in $\mathbb{F}_p$, giving $\bigl|\pi_\varepsilon^{-1}(\infty) \cap \tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_p)\bigr| = 0 \ne 1$ and ruling out $(4, V_4)$ by Lemma 2.3!

3. **Generic Subcase $a + \varepsilon b \ne 0$ with $(\alpha, \beta) \ne (0, 0)$ ($p \equiv \pm 2 \pmod 5$, $d_\varepsilon = 5$).** Set $K := -\frac{a}{\varepsilon b} \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \setminus \{1\}$, choose the unique fifth root $w_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \setminus \{1\}$ with $w_0^5 = K$ ($\gcd(5, p - 1) = 1$), and fix $\zeta_5 \in \mathbb{F}_{p^4} \setminus \mathbb{F}_{p^2}$. As $t \to \infty$ ($\tau := t^{-1} \to 0$), $\pi_\varepsilon^{-1}(\infty)$ is unramified with $5$ local Laurent branches $U_j(\tau) = \tau^{-1} z^{(j)}(\tau)$ ($j \in \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$, $w_j := \zeta_5^j w_0$) solving $-w_j^5 D_5(z/\tau, \alpha) + D_5((z + 1)/\tau, \beta) + \tilde{c} = 0$. Expanding in $\tau$ yields the **Dickson--Catalan Laurent formula** (`verify_section_5_sextics_and_separated`): $z^{(j)}(\tau) = \sum_{d \ge 0} z_d(w_j) \tau^d$ with $z_1 = z_3 = 0$, $z_5(w) = \tilde{c}\frac{(w - 1)^3}{5 w^4}$, and **independent of $\tilde{c}$ for all even $2k \in \{2, 4, 6, 8\}$**:
   $$z_0(w) \;=\; \frac{1}{w - 1}, \qquad z_{2k}(w) \;=\; (-1)^{k-1} C_{k-1}\,\bigl(\alpha w - \beta w^{-1}\bigr)\,\bigl(\alpha w - (\alpha + \beta) + \beta w^{-1}\bigr)^{k-1} \quad (1 \le k \le 4), \tag{5.5}$$
   where $(C_0, C_1, C_2, C_3) = (1, 1, 2, 5)$ are the Catalan numbers!

   Suppose for contradiction that $\pi_\varepsilon$ is $(5, C_5)$- or $(5, D_5)$-exceptional ($G^{\mathrm{geom}}(\pi_\varepsilon) \le D_5 \trianglelefteq F_{20}$). On $\{U_0, \dots, U_4\}$, $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ fixes $0$ and maps $j \mapsto \pm 2 j \pmod 5$ ($4$-cycle $(1\,2\,4\,3)^{\pm 1}$). Checking all $6$ cyclic order-$5$ subgroups of $S_5$ shows that **only two** are normalized by $(1\,2\,4\,3)$, namely $H_{\mathrm{A}} = \langle (0\,1\,2\,3\,4) \rangle$ (**Pair A**) and $H_{\mathrm{B}} = \langle (0\,1\,3\,2\,4) \rangle$ (**Pair B**). For any $D_5 = H \rtimes C_2$, the cyclic-adjacent quadratic sum $I_H(\tau) := \sum_{\ell = 0}^4 U_{h_\ell}(\tau) U_{h_{\ell+1}}(\tau)$ is $D_5$-invariant with no finite poles over $\mathbb{A}^1_t$ (since $U_j$ is integral over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[t]$!), hence $I_H(\tau) \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[t] = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[\tau^{-1}]$ has **vanishing negative powers $[\tau^{2m}] I_H = 0$ for all $m \ge 1$**!
   - **Elimination of Pair A ($H_{\mathrm{A}} = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)$):** Writing $c_1 := 1 + 2(\zeta_5 + \zeta_5^4)$ ($c_1^2 = 5 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$), exact cyclotomic expansion gives $w_0^2(w_0^5 - 1)[\tau^2] I_{H_{\mathrm{A}}} = 5(w_0^5 \alpha^2 - \beta^2) w_0^2 c_1 = 0$, forcing $\alpha\beta \ne 0$ and $w_0^5 = (\beta/\alpha)^2$. Writing $\sigma := \beta/\alpha \ne 0$ and $w_0 = u_0^2, \sigma = u_0^5$ ($u_0 \notin \{0, 1\}$ since $w_0 \ne 1$ and $\gcd(5, p - 1) = 1$), substituting into $[\tau^4] I_{H_{\mathrm{A}}} = 0$ gives $w_0^3(w_0^5 - 1)[\tau^4] I_{H_{\mathrm{A}}} = 10 \alpha^3 w_0^3 \sigma^2(\sigma - 1) c_1 = 0$, contradiction!
   - **Elimination of Pair B ($H_{\mathrm{B}} = (0, 1, 3, 2, 4)$):** Writing $c_2 := 1 + 2(\zeta_5^2 + \zeta_5^3)$ ($c_2^2 = 5 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$), coefficientwise cyclotomic expansion for $m \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ yields $w_0^{m+1}(w_0^5 - 1)[\tau^{2m}] I_{H_{\mathrm{B}}} = c_2\,Q_{2m}(\alpha, \beta; w_0)$, where $Q_2, Q_4, Q_6 \in \mathbb{Z}[w_0][\alpha, \beta]$ are the explicit homogeneous polynomials in $(\alpha, \beta)$ of degrees $2, 3, 4$ recorded in **Appendix A** (eqs. (A.1)--(A.2)). If $\pi_\varepsilon$ fell in Pair B, $(\alpha : \beta) \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ would be a common projective zero of $Q_2, Q_4, Q_6$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, forcing both homogeneous Sylvester resultants $\mathrm{Res}_{(\alpha:\beta)}(Q_2, Q_4)$ and $\mathrm{Res}_{(\alpha:\beta)}(Q_2, Q_6)$ to vanish mod $p$. Over $\mathbb{Z}[w_0]$, these resultants factor in terms of $S := w_0 + w_0^{-1} \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and $\Phi_5(w_0) = 1 + w_0 + w_0^2 + w_0^3 + w_0^4 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$ (since $w_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{1\}$ and $5 \nmid (p - 1)$):
     $$\mathrm{Res}_{(\alpha:\beta)}(Q_2, Q_4) \;=\; -5 w_0^{20}(w_0 - 1)\Phi_5(w_0)^2 F_7(S), \qquad \mathrm{Res}_{(\alpha:\beta)}(Q_2, Q_6) \;=\; 5 w_0^{26}(w_0 - 1)^2 \Phi_5(w_0)^2 G_{11}(S), \tag{5.6}$$
     where $F_7(S), G_{11}(S) \in \mathbb{Z}[S]$ have degrees $7$ and $11$, and satisfy the exact integral Bézout identity over $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ (with explicit polynomials $F_7, G_{11}, U_{10}, V_6$ given in Appendix A, eqs. (A.3)--(A.5)):
     $$U_{10}(S)\,F_7(S) + V_6(S)\,G_{11}(S) \;=\; C_S \;=\; 5302893588230753012007437500 \;=\; 2^2 \cdot 5^6 \cdot 31 \cdot 103 \cdot 191 \cdot 239 \cdot 244997 \cdot 2375975411. \tag{5.7}$$
     Thus $F_7(S) \equiv G_{11}(S) \equiv 0 \pmod p$ implies $p \mid C_S$. Among all prime factors of $C_S$, **only $p = 103$ and $p = 244997$** satisfy $p \ge 7$ and $p \equiv \pm 2 \pmod 5$:
     * At $p = 103$: $\gcd_{\mathbb{F}_{103}}(F_7, G_{11}) = S - 3$, where $S^2 - 4 = 5$ is a quadratic non-residue mod $103$, so **no** $w_0 \in \mathbb{F}_{103}^\times$ exists!
     * At $p = 244997$: $\gcd_{\mathbb{F}_p}(F_7, G_{11}) = S - 227855$, giving $w_0 \in \{52877, 174978\}$, and at both roots $\gcd_{\mathbb{F}_p}(Q_2(t, 1), Q_4(t, 1), Q_6(t, 1)) = 1$ (and the leading $\alpha$-coefficients do not vanish simultaneously), so **no** shared root $(\alpha : \beta) \in \mathbb{P}^1(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ exists!

Hence both $\pi_+$ and $\pi_-$ are non-exceptional across all primes $p \ge 7$, and Proposition 2.5 ($d_{\max} = 5$) yields $|S| \le \frac{43}{24}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ for all $m \ge 2$ and $|S| \le \frac{70217}{39015}n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ for all $n \ge 34p$, completing Theorem 4. $\square$
