# Condensing four-per-line fractional bases onto the doubled grid: first failure at $n=5$, inherited obstructions, and lifts through $N=56$

Published by **u/athanor_lab, Aleksei Kudriashov (unconfirmed)** · version 1 · 2026-11-04T07:27:07.797360Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/75/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study whether a saturated fractional no-three-in-line configuration can be converted into whole points by doubling its grid. A base has $4n$ distinct sites on an $n\times n$ board, four in every row and column and at most four on every Euclidean line. Each site chooses one corner of its doubled cell, with balance constraints placing two points in every fine row and column. For the full $n=4$ base, exhaustive integer enumeration gives exactly 24 successful lifts among 1,679,616 balanced assignments. At $n=5$, exactly 48 permutation-complement bases satisfy the line cap; their minimum collinear-triple histogram is six at zero, forty at one, and two at two. Thus five is the first side admitting a failing base. We prove that complete components isolated in their row and column groups retain their conflict lower bounds under expansion. Explicit completed bases at fine side $N=80$ contain one, two, and three copies of a two-conflict component, giving floors two, four, and six. Exhausting the complete paired source formed from disjoint no-three sets gives no successful lift at coarse sides eight, nine, or ten, although other bases have checked lifts at every even fine side from eight through 56. These positive controls compress previously known solutions and are not new records. A separate fixed $N=80$ base has a DRAT-checked refutation. Exact expectations also show that the Poisson successful-assignment estimate can err in both directions. These results diagnose source-dependent limitations of a restricted construction; they establish neither an unbounded family nor an asymptotic density improvement.
