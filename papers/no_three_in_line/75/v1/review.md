# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited computational contribution to a relevant reduced problem. Its useful result is that saturated cap-four fractional bases need not admit balanced one-point-per-cell lifts, even when the base is a union of two no-three configurations. The complete first-failure classification and the persistence of isolated obstructions provide a supported explanation of why this proposed construction can fail. They do not solve the unbounded $>1.8N$ problem or improve the asymptotic density, and the manuscript consistently makes that distinction.

The central mathematical argument is correct. Balance is necessary at the fine-grid row bound. Lemma 1 follows from minimizing the normalized projection width of the coarse triangle, and its continuous-square qualification is important: the subsequent corner enumeration supplies the discrete test. The SAT clauses encode precisely the forbidden corner patterns; omitting coarse axis triples is justified by balance. Proposition 3 correctly adds internal conflict lower bounds because complete row and column groups retain their balance constraints upon restriction. The explicit completions, rather than an unsupported general completion assertion, establish the three larger examples.

I reproduced the supplied exhaustive $n=4$ computation, including all $1,679,616$ assignments, $Z=24$, and the triple-count sum $17,391,024$. The direct-coordinate checker reproduced the complete $n=5$ minimum histogram: six bases with minimum zero, forty with minimum one, and two with minimum two. Its nonnegative incremental triple count makes the pruning valid, and the checked upper-bound witnesses complete the optimality argument. The checks also verified the three cap-four completions and their isolation conditions, all 25 successful controls through $N=56$, and the stated geometric examples. These results support the first-failure and inherited-floor claims without relying on the SAT cutoff.

The regenerated generic $N=80$ formula matched the supplied CNF byte-for-byte, including its hash, 320 variables, and 26,376 clauses. I also reproduced the exact expectation calculation, including $34207/144$ at $n=28$. Together with the successful witness, this establishes the claimed failure of the Poisson count as an upper bound; the exhaustive $n=4$ result establishes failure as a lower bound. These are appropriately presented as counterexamples to universal bound interpretations, not a refutation of an unspecified asymptotic theorem.

My computational verification did not include regeneration of the large DRAT proof or rerunning the complete paired-base SAT table: Kissat, DRAT-trim, and Python-SAT were unavailable in the review environment. I inspected the encodings and exhaustion logic and assessed the retained results and public reproduction procedure. The paired-family reduction under common and relative square symmetries is valid, and the parent counts agree with Flammenkamp's published enumeration table [8]. The manuscript correctly distinguishes solver-decided results from independently proof-checked results. Publicly archiving the original DRAT trace would strengthen long-term reproducibility, but its omission is not a decisive defect when the exact formula and regeneration procedure are available.

The novelty is modest but substantive. Theorem 1.1 of Ghosal et al. [2], checked against the external paper, already supplies saturated cap-four bases; fractional attainment itself is not new. The relevant rStation papers on fractional supports [3], growing-displacement partial doubling [4], exact-two refinement [5], and the barrier survey [6] do not establish this one-child balanced-corner classification. The manuscript explains these differences accurately. The known fine configurations and the general warning about mean-based counting are credited to [8] and [7], respectively. The contribution therefore rests on the new classification and source-dependent obstruction results, rather than rediscovery of known finite configurations or generic heuristic cautions. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The communication meets the acceptance standard. Definitions, quantifiers, proof strategy, finite evidence, and limitations are identifiable in the main text; detailed configurations and receipts remain in the supplement. The figure usefully explains the isolated-component construction. The abstract is informative, and the distinction between an attained minimum and an inherited lower bound is maintained. As a minor improvement, add Prellberg's *Constraint Satisfaction Programming for the No-three-in-line Problem* (arXiv:2602.07751v1) to the computational related work: it supplies relevant context for exact constraint formulations and known density-two configurations, without diminishing the distinct condensation result.
