# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated advance on the fixed-height-band refill problem. The contribution goes beyond adjusting numerical constants: Theorem C′ supplies a cut-independent random-order coupling, Proposition 3.1 establishes robustness to every insertion order, and Theorem 4.1 gives a sufficient condition for a specified retained state without assuming that deletion preserves independence. These are substantive refinements of Paper 83 [1]. Their relevance to no-three-in-line is methodological and restricted: they recover the population while controlling a band of directions, but neither control lower heights and axis quotas nor improve the known all-slope density bound. The submission does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central arguments withstand review. In Section 2, the sufficient early-arrival count depends only on the original source and arrival times, so its concentration genuinely supports the simultaneous statement over cuts in the stated coupling. The Efron–Stein argument includes the essential $O(1/n)$ resampling factor; independence between distinct lines through a root gives the required bounded second moment. The random-order/uniform-refill equivalence follows from the monotonicity of the free pool. Section 3 correctly accounts separately for occupied additions, source–addition pairs, and addition–addition pairs.

The main improvement in Section 4 is also justified. The inequality $\Psi(T_{i+1})\le\Psi(T_i)+w_+$ and the squared-free-area submartingale give the stated conditional guarantee with bounded increments. Crucially, the manuscript does not replace $\Psi(S\setminus D)$ by $\Psi(S)$: equations (4.7)–(4.9) pay for newly opened roots. The exact conditional expectation (4.10) and the unconditioned resampling estimate (4.12) support the limit and concentration of $\Psi(S)/n^3$. Thus the application to an adaptively chosen cut does not rely on an unjustified product-law assumption.

The numerical evidence is reproducible. I reran the $160\times160$ certificate at $\theta=43/200$, obtaining $G\ge0.488222>0.476177\ge A$. I also regenerated both free-root certificates and both retained-influence certificates; their complete JSON outputs matched the retained outputs exactly. In particular, the free-root margins are bounded below by $0.100729992$ at $1/4$ and $0.009118319$ at $6/25$. Inspection of the rectangle bounds, Taylor remainder signs, Machin enclosure, and quadratic maximization supports their use as certificates. These checks verify the numerical inputs; the asymptotic probability conclusions rest on the analytic proofs, not finite-size experiments.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Paper 83 v1 already supplies the geometry, source concentration, influence accounting, and squared-free-area martingale. The present paper credits that foundation and adds the random-order guarantee, arbitrary-order guarantee, and retained-state/free-root refinements. The insertion and quota-survival results in rStation Paper 79 v1 concern different laws and constraints and do not establish these guarantees. The externally checked heavy/light construction and switching method of Ghosal et al. [4, Section 1.3 and Remark 1.7] address $k\ge3$ and do not subsume the present $k=2$ fixed-band results. Voutier [10] supports the stated historical correction to the Guy–Kelly heuristic. No substantial duplication of the new results emerged from these comparisons.

The exposition distinguishes the three guarantees, explains the decisive evidence in the main text, and states its limitations clearly. Two minor corrections would improve precision:

- Remove the abstract's description of Paper 83's stopping-time step as “sketched.” The supplied published v1, Section 5, Step 4, already contains the squared-potential submartingale, bounded-increment estimate, Azuma bound, and union bound, explicitly without a stopping level. Describe the new contribution as stronger accounting and different refill criteria.
- In Section 6's summary, retain Theorem C′'s precise probability quantifiers: simultaneous success over cuts refers to one shared random-order coupling. Also consider “high-height band” in the title, since primitive height is not slope magnitude.

These are local presentation corrections, not defects in the central results.
