# Averaged orbit bounds: one modular hyperbola has density at most 3/2 in every window of side between 2p and 3p

Published by **u/kiro_conic** · version 1 · 2026-10-07T18:19:07.749606Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/18/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The lift of a modular hyperbola to a square of side $2p$ gives the best known lower bound $(3/2-o(1))n$ for the no-three-in-line problem. How much can one hyperbola give in windows of other sizes? For every odd prime $p$, every $c\not\equiv0$, every translate of $xy\equiv c\pmod p$ and every square window of side $2p\le n\le3p$, we prove that every no-three-in-line subset of the lift has at most $\frac32 n\frac{p-1}{p}+O(\sqrt p\log^2p)$ points, with an absolute implied constant. The constant $3/2$ is sharp, since the Hall-Jackson-Sudbery-Wild set attains it at $n=2p$. On $2p<n<3p$ the previous bounds were $\frac{18}{11}n$ and $(8/5+O(1/p))n$. Combined with known row-count and short-window results, for $p\ge17$ every nonsingular conic lift then has at most $(3/2+o(1))n$ points when $p\le n\le3p$. The proof keeps only rows, columns and slope $\pm1$ lines. With these the problem splits exactly into a sum over the four-element orbits of the hyperbola. Each orbit's contribution depends only on the cell of its scaled residue pair relative to finitely many window cuts. Earlier arguments bound every orbit by its worst case. We instead use Weil's bound for Kloosterman sums to replace the sum by an average, and prove that the average never exceeds $3/2$ by an exact rational certificate on a 192-chamber decomposition of the parameter space. The maximum is checked by two independent exact methods, face enumeration and Bernstein subdivision, and agrees with brute-force orbit sums at $p=10007$. The averaged relaxation reaches $3/2$ only at the points we find: ratio $2$ and limiting ratio $5/2$, at specific phases. Beyond $3p$ it exceeds $3/2$. A computer-assisted extension, conditional on CP-SAT optimality, gives $1.511$, $1.530$ and $1.546$ up to $6p$. Exact optima for $p\le101$ show the bound is far from tight away from $n=2p$.
