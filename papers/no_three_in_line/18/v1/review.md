# Accept

Theorem 1 is a sound and meaningful construction-family restriction: uniformly over the hyperbola, translation and window position, it establishes the asymptotically sharp coefficient $3/2$ throughout $2p\le n\le3p$. This improves the previously available bounds in the interior of that interval and explains a limitation of the construction responsible for the classical lower bound. It is substantive partial progress relevant to the supplied problem, although it neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor rules out such families in general. The problem should remain unsolved.

The central reduction is convincing. The four retained line families cannot connect different hyperbola orbits, so their relaxation separates exactly. The cut partition captures changes in lift multiplicities and integer offsets; the exceptional coincidence set contributes only the stated exceptional residue classes. Interval completion and the Kloosterman bound then replace the orbit sum by the torus average with a uniform $O(\sqrt p\log^2p)$ error. The bounded number of cells, boundary residues and exceptional orbits makes this uniformity legitimate. The final replacement of $3n/2$ by $3n(p-1)/(2p)$ costs only $O(1)$ in this range. Corollary 6 follows from this result and the cited row-support and short-window bounds.

The computational proof is inspectable. The main per-orbit optimizer exhaustively branches over point selections with exact capacity bookkeeping. Chamber polynomials and face maximization use rational arithmetic, and the treatment of singular stationary systems is valid when applied through independent defining constraints for each face. Bernstein subdivision supplies a second maximization method, although it shares the chamber polynomials and orbit evaluator with the first method. Thus these are independent checks of maximization, not two fully independent implementations of the entire theorem.

My bounded checks independently reconstructed the 23-plane arrangement by polyhedron clipping, obtaining 192 chambers, and reproduced the triangulation totals of 2,496 simplices and exact volume 1. All 15,552 cell representatives avoided the exceptional coincidence set. Direct evaluation reproduced the means $12$, $15$ and $3194/225$ at $(L,f_x,f_y)=(2,0,1/2)$, $(5/2,1/4,3/4)$ and $(12/5,7/36,25/36)$, respectively. All 63 retained point sets passed independent checks of cardinality, range, hyperbola membership and collinearity using exact normalized directions. Further orbit optimization and a selected chamber maximization reached the probe limit; I did not reproduce the complete maximization runs or the CP-SAT optimality claims. Acceptance rests on the inspected proof and exhaustive algorithms together with the retained evidence, not a claimed full experimental rerun. The explicit conditional status of Theorem 1′ should remain.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. I examined the relevant arguments in the supplied versions of [3]–[5], Kudriashov’s external paper [2], and Shparlinski’s distribution results [6], and checked the scope of the checkerboard result [7]. Kudriashov provides the two-period orbit theory and finite larger-window computations; [3] supplies the envelope and integer-period bounds; [4] gives the broader but weaker arc restriction. Equidistribution itself is classical. The new contribution is its combination with the exact orbit optimization and phase-uniform chamber certificate, yielding $3/2$ throughout the interval. I found no earlier result in this focused comparison that subsumes it. The finite optimum table is supporting evidence, not a separate novelty claim.

The following clarifications would improve the paper without changing the accepted central result:

1. **Equality locus.** The abstract says the listed phases are the only equality points. `zero_set.py` does not establish this: `max_on_chamber` updates its stored maximizer only for a strictly larger value, so a tied nonvertex maximizer need not be reported. Finding a maximizing vertex and excluding zero edges between zero vertices does not exclude all other zeros. Either certify the full zero locus on every face or restrict this assertion to the equality points actually certified. Proposition 5 and sharpness at $L=2$ do not require uniqueness.
2. **Finite optima.** Several retained runs at $p=31,53$ have status FEASIBLE with unequal primal and dual bounds, including larger windows summarized in Section 6. Distinguish these lower bounds from proven optima and report the corresponding upper bounds where interpreting tightness. “Only $n=2p$ is extremal” should be confined to what the theorem and certified experiments establish; the tested phases do not exhaust all phases.
3. **Cut formula.** Correct the affine-form list in Section 5.1 to match Lemma 3 and `cert_planes.py`: with $t_1=L-2$, it is $\{\pm f_x,\pm(t_1-f_x),\pm f_y,\pm(t_1-f_y)\}$. The implemented list is correct. Also replace the statement that a stronger argument “must use” particular additional slopes by the supported conclusion that the four-direction relaxation alone cannot prove a uniform $3/2$ bound in the wider range.

These are secondary claim and notation corrections. They do not undermine the uniform averaged bound, its substantive distinction from prior work, or its relevance as a restriction on a natural construction strategy.
