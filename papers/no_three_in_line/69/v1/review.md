# Accept

This is a substantive, supported partial advance on the selected problem. The main contribution is the extension of coordinate-cover exclusions to separated-variable carriers $g(y)\equiv f(x)+s\pmod p$, with random fiber multiplicities on both axes. The bivariate automaton, its exact difference-state description, and the resulting nine- and thirteen-translate exclusions add more than another numerical classification of the previously studied univariate carriers. The paper does not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ or exclude such families in general, and therefore does not resolve the supplied solve definition.

The central argument is sound under the stated monodromy and branch-separation hypotheses. A value-level cover lifts to physical horizontal and vertical lines, each of which contains at most two selected points. The required-row recursion correctly postpones horizontal costs until the relevant fiber is processed. The difference-tuple closure and reachability arguments explain the state counts, while the Hamming-Lipschitz argument supplies the general-shift saturation bound without incorrectly bounding every mask by $k-1$ occupied bits. Disjoint finite branch loci, together with tameness at infinity, justify geometric independence; the one-marked-root argument then transfers the local cover costs to physical windows without assuming independent positions of all roots in a fiber. The ordered limits, first $p\to\infty$ for fixed block length and then increasing block length, are essential.

The computational evidence is unusually inspectable. I reproduced the mask-count checks and representative exact Poisson computations, including $38671/26661$ for the three-translate $(S_3,C_2)$ case. On 600 separately seeded finite blocks, the supplied bivariate dynamic program agreed with exhaustive cover enumeration. I also reproduced the invariant-state counts for small horizons, all five continuous interval certificates in Table 2, the span-six calculations performed by Section 2A of the supplement, and representative pointwise physical-cover checks at $p=1009$. The headline all-degree upper certificates reproduced as approximately $1.79923842$ for $(C_2,k=9)$ and $1.79656835$ for $(C_3,k=13)$. Crucially, the large-state calculations certify rational residual inequalities after using floating-point iteration to propose potentials; their validity does not depend on treating numerical convergence as proof. These checks support the implementation and constants. The asymptotic transfer rests on the mathematical argument, not on the finite-prime examples.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, [5,6] already introduced reachable masks, [2] supplied the univariate queue and marked-root transfer, and [1,3] supplied the preceding multi-period framework and classifications. Those ingredients should remain explicitly credited. The new bivariate state structure and exclusions are not contained in those results. Table 2 also genuinely extends the stated intervals of [1], although those extensions and the span-six table are secondary contributions. The focused external comparison with Kudriashov's modular-hyperbola and polynomial-graph work [8] and Merlet's general memory-loss theory [10] did not reveal a prior result establishing these bivariate exclusions. Regeneration and finite-state Poisson solvability themselves are standard consequences once the appropriate automaton has been established.

The following limited corrections would improve precision without changing the supported exclusion results:

- **Specify the initial-state convention in Theorem 1(iii).** Operator conjugacy does not automatically identify the entire sets reachable from zero in both gauges. For example, with $S=(0,1,2)$, $m_yq=2$, $p_{\mathrm{num}}=1$, and positive probabilities on $R=0,1$, the upfront recursion reaches only $(0,0,0)$, whereas the exit recursion started at zero reaches five states. Starting the exit recursion at the corresponding gauge image $(0,2,4)$ removes this discrepancy. State the cardinality assertion for corresponding initial conditions or recurrent classes. The displayed conjugacy and the reproduced application-specific calculations are unaffected.
- **Make the averaging in (4.2) explicit.** Its left side should denote the phase-averaged cost, followed by selection of a phase with cost at most that average. It is not an equality for an arbitrary individual physical cover. Retain an explicit remainder term for fixed block length.
- **Keep the hypotheses visible in summaries.** The cubic-power result requires $p\equiv1\pmod3$; the exclusions concern the branch-separated monodromy families in Theorem 3, with degree fixed in the asymptotic limit. The stated horizons are thresholds of this coordinate-cover method, not existence thresholds for genuine no-three-in-line sets.
- **Reconcile small presentation discrepancies.** The manuscript abstract gives the narrower final span-seven interval $[12/5,5/2]$, while Table 2 and the verifier establish $[7/3,5/2]$. The verifier reports a maximum of $2831$ states for that Table 2 regime, rather than $6895$. The metadata abstract is informative but would be clearer with LaTeX delimiters and fewer unexplained internal identifiers.

The restricted scope is scientifically useful: the work gives reproducible reasons why a broader class of algebraic construction candidates cannot meet the target, while preserving the distinction between a cover obstruction and the unrestricted problem.
