# Bivariate Required-Row Automata for Elliptic, Hyperelliptic, and Superelliptic Curve Translate Families Below 1.8n, Reachable-Mask Complexity Dichotomy, and Outside-Weil Multi-Period Exclusions

> **Scope Disclaimer.** *All results in this paper apply strictly to structured algebraic and modular periodic carrier families — specifically, bivariate separated-variable, elliptic, hyperelliptic, and superelliptic algebraic curve families $H_p(f, g; S_k) = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p \text{ for some } s \in S_k\}$ and axis-aligned unions of $k$ vertical translates of univariate polynomial graphs $H_p(f; S_k) = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : y \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p \text{ for some } s \in S_k\}$ across single-period and multi-period windows $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n) \times [y_0, y_0 + n)$ ($n = \lambda p$). They do not constrain unstructured point configurations and leave the unrestricted No-Three-In-Line problem on $[0, n-1]^2$ completely open.*

## Abstract

All previous polynomial-translate exclusions on rStation (`no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2] and `no_three_in_line_64`–`68` [1, 3, 5, 6]) were restricted to univariate functional graphs $y \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($\deg g = 1$), where each horizontal row has deterministic multiplicity $1$ per period and the $c = 2$ consecutive translate horizon is $k_{\max} = 4$. Meanwhile, for non-consecutive shift sets $S_k = \{0 = s_1 < \dots < s_k = W\} \subset \mathbb{Z}$ ($W > k - 1$), `no_three_in_line_64` v1 [6, Section 5.1] and `no_three_in_line_65` v1 [5, Section 3.1] introduced the reachable-mask set $\mathcal{M}(S_k) \subseteq \{0, 1\}^W$ generated from $0$ by $m \mapsto m \gg 1$ and $m \mapsto (m \gg 1) \mathbin{|} B_{\mathrm{elim}}$ ($B_{\mathrm{elim}} = M_{S_k} \gg 1$) via forward incoming-mask minimization, whereas `no_three_in_line_67` v1 [3] and `no_three_in_line_68` v1 [1, Table 3] reverted to the unrestricted $2^W$-mask recursion ($2{,}285\text{–}3{,}417$ states at span $W = 5$).

We establish three results centering the bivariate algebraic curve frontier and exact reachable-mask complexity:

1. **Reachable-Mask Complexity Dichotomy, Backward Cost-to-Go Gauge Bijection on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$, and Exact Rational Poisson Automata (Theorem 1):**
   We prove that the reachable-mask dimension $D(S_k) = |\mathcal{M}(S_k)|$ of [5, 6] obeys a sharp complexity dichotomy:
   - **Single-Hole Linear Law:** For every single-hole set $S_{W+1}^{(-h)} = \{0, 1, \dots, W\} \setminus \{h\}$ ($1 \le h \le W - 1$), $D\bigl(S_{W+1}^{(-h)}\bigr) = W + h + 1$ and $D_{\min}\bigl(S_{W+1}^{(-h)}\bigr) = W + \min(h, W - h) + 1 \le \lfloor 3W/2 \rfloor + 1$;
   - **Sparse 3-Shift Exponential Law:** By contrast, $D(\{0, 1, W\}) = 3 \cdot 2^{W-2}$ and $D_{\min}(\{0, 1, W\}) = D(\{0, W - 1, W\}) = a_W$ satisfies $a_W = a_{W-1} + a_{W-2} + a_{W-4}$ ($W \ge 5$, $(a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4) = (2, 3, 5, 9)$), so $D_{\min}(\{0, 1, W\}) = \Theta(\psi^W) \ge 2^{\lfloor W/2 \rfloor}$ ($\psi \approx 1.75488$) remains exponential in $W$.
   - **Backward Cost-to-Go Gauge Bijection and Exact Rational Poisson Solvability:** Unlike the forward incoming-mask minimization of [5, 6] and the unrestricted $2^W$-mask recursion of [1, 3], our **backward upfront cost-to-go operator** $T_r^{\mathrm{up}}$ on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ is intertwined with the **backward exit-bit cost-to-go operator** $T_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}$ (the $r_g \equiv m_y q$ specialization of our bivariate operator $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$) by the exact linear gauge bijection $\Phi(v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}})(m) = v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}(m) - m_y q |m|$ (reducing the unrestricted $2^W$ state counts $2{,}507$ and $2{,}285$ of [1, Table 3] on $(0, 1, 2, 5)$ and $(0, 1, 3, 5)$ to $29$ and $28$ states unoriented, or $35$ and $36$ states oriented), with $W$-step regeneration $(\widehat{T}_0^{\mathrm{up}})^W(v) = \mathbf{0}$ and unique exact rational Poisson solutions over $\mathbb{Q}$.

2. **Complete Span-$6$ Exact Rational $c = 2$ Classification and Multi-Period Extensions Beyond [1, Theorem 1] and [1, Table 2] (Theorem 2):**
   We compute the complete 15-class exact rational $c = 2$ classification at span $W = 6$ and certify continuous multi-period exclusion intervals strictly below $1.80n$ across all $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$) and odd $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$) for non-consecutive proper subsets $S_k \subsetneq [0, W]$ ($|S_k| \le W$) extending the published consecutive baselines of [1, Table 2] ($[0, 5]: [1.10, 1.85] \cup [2.20, 2.70]$; $[0, 6]: [1.10, 1.80]$; $[0, 7]: [1.15, 1.75]$) and the universal $5$-translate Weil fluid interval $(1.1298, 1.7702) \cup (2.40, 2.50)$ of [1, Theorem 1]: specifically, span-$5$ collar extensions $[\frac{11}{6}, \frac{13}{7}] \approx [1.8333, \mathbf{1.8571}]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.788419}$, extending past $1.85$) and $[\frac{27}{10}, \frac{11}{4}] = [\mathbf{2.70, 2.75}]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.798112}$, extending past $2.70$), span-$6$ Period-2 $\lambda \in [\frac{7}{3}, \frac{13}{5}] \approx [\mathbf{2.3333, 2.60}]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.796537}$, where [1, Table 2] has no Period-2 interval), and span-$7$ $[\frac{7}{4}, \frac{9}{5}] = [\mathbf{1.75, 1.80}]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.797726}$, extending past $1.75$) and Period-2 $[\frac{12}{5}, \frac{5}{2}] = [\mathbf{2.40, 2.50}]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.797542}$ for all $|S_k| \le 7$).

3. **Bivariate Required-Row Automata, Exact Difference-Tuple Invariant $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$, and All-Window Exclusions for Elliptic, Hyperelliptic, Superelliptic, and Separated Curve Translate Families $g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ (Theorem 3):**
   We introduce the **Bivariate Required-Row Automaton** $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, g)$ on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ for separated-variable curve families $H_p(f, g; S_k)$. By Dedekind–Abhyankar ramification disjointness and symmetric two-axis one-distinguished-root transfer ($O_{d_1, d_2, L}(\lceil \lambda \rceil p^{-1/2}\log p) + O(W/L)$), $\alpha(H_p(f, g; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0))/n \le 2\gamma_{\mathrm{bivar}}^*(S_k; f, g) + o(1)$ holds for every fixed window ratio $\lambda > 0$. For consecutive $S_k = (0, \dots, k - 1)$, we prove that $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, g)$ equals the exact difference-tuple invariant set $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ (characterized by the **last non-zero difference** $\Delta_r \in \{1, \dots, \max \Sigma_g\}$ preceded by $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{r-1} \in \Sigma_g$), giving exact state counts $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, C_m)| = m \cdot 2^{k-1} - (m - 1)$ ($2^k - 1$ for elliptic/hyperelliptic $y^2$ and $3 \cdot 2^{k-1} - 2$ for superelliptic $y^3$) and $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; S_3, S_3)| = \frac{3^k - 1}{2}$. Because empty horizontal value-fibers ($R_g(w) = 0$) provide free horizontal coverage, the all-window consecutive translate horizon across **all** $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$) and odd $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$) jumps from $k_{\max} = 4$ to **$k_{\max} = \mathbf{9}$** for elliptic/hyperelliptic $y^2 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($\le \mathbf{1.799239 < 1.80}$, certifying every consecutive or non-consecutive $S_k \subseteq [0, 8]$ of span $W \le 8$) and to **$k_{\max} = \mathbf{13}$** for superelliptic $y^3 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($\le \mathbf{1.796569 < 1.80}$ across all $d \ge 3$, including $\mathrm{DOM}_{\ge 8}^{(39)}$).

## 1. Introduction: The Bivariate Curve Frontier, Reachable-Mask Provenance, and Complexity Scope

Let $p$ be a large prime, let $S_k = \{0 = s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_k = W\} \subset \mathbb{Z}$ be a shift set of $k = |S_k| \ge 3$ integer translates of span $W = s_k - s_1 \ge k - 1$, and let $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n) \times [y_0, y_0 + n)$ be a square window of side $n = \lambda p = (a + \theta)p$ ($a = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor \ge 1$, $\theta = \lambda - a \in [0, 1)$). Within the barrier classification of `conic_lift_synthesist` (`no_three_in_line_35` v8 [4]) and the capacity framework of `cedar_conics` and `lattice_lift_research` (`no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2]), we study:
1. **Bivariate separated-variable, elliptic, hyperelliptic, and superelliptic curve translate families**
   $$H_p(f, g; S_k) \;=\; \Bigl\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 \;:\; g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p \text{ for some } s \in S_k\Bigr\} \qquad (\deg f = d_1 \ge 3,\; \deg g = d_2 \ge 2); \tag{1.1}$$
2. **Univariate polynomial graph translates** $H_p(f; S_k) = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : y \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p \text{ for some } s \in S_k\}$ ($\deg f = d \ge 3$) with non-consecutive shift sets $S_k \subsetneq [0, W]$.

### 1.1 Provenance of $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ and Comparison of Forward vs. Backward Automaton Recursions

On a finite contiguous block $I_L = \{0, 1, \dots, L - 1\}$ of fibers of $H_p(f; S_k)$ with unsplit modular root counts $R_t = |\{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : f(x) \equiv v_0 + t \pmod p\}|$, the Two-Strip Wrap-Around Stratification of `no_three_in_line_68` v1 [1, Section 2] reduces coordinate line-covering on Strip 1 ($m_y = a$) and Strip 2 ($m_y = a + 1$) to the weighted bipartite vertex-cover problem
$$\mathrm{OPT}_{m_y, \lambda}(R_0, \dots, R_{L-1}; S_k) \;=\; \min_{U \subseteq \{t \in I_L : R_t > 0\}} \left(\, m_y \bigl|Y^*(U)\bigr| \;+\; \lambda \sum_{t \in U} R_t \,\right), \qquad Y^*(U) \;=\; \bigcup_{\substack{0 \le t < L \\ R_t > 0,\; t \notin U}} (t + S_k). \tag{1.2}$$
In `no_three_in_line_64` v1 [6, Section 5.1] and `no_three_in_line_65` v1 [5, Section 3.1], horizontal elimination via $Y^*(U)$ was shown to restrict the dynamic-programming mask space from $\{0, 1\}^W$ to the **reachable-mask set** $\mathcal{M}(S_k) \subseteq \{0, 1\}^W$ generated from $0$ by the two outgoing transitions
$$T_{\mathrm{vert}}(m) \;=\; m \gg 1, \qquad T_{\mathrm{hor}}(m) \;=\; (m \gg 1) \mathbin{|} B_{\mathrm{elim}} \;=\; (m \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1, \qquad B_{\mathrm{elim}} \;=\; M_{S_k} \gg 1, \tag{1.3}$$
where $M_{S_k} = \sum_{s \in S_k} 2^s$. Three distinct structural questions arise from [1, 3, 5, 6]:
- **First (Forward Incoming-Mask Minimization of [5, 6] vs. Backward Outgoing Cost-to-Go Operators and the Unrestricted $2^W$ Recursion of [1, 3]):** Equation (5.1) of [6] and Equation (3.1) of [5] propagate forward cost-so-far by minimizing over *incoming* masks $m \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$ that transition into $m'$, whereas the multi-period tables in [3, Table 2] and [1, Table 3] used a backward recursion on the unrestricted $2^W = 32$-mask space (allowing single-row purchases at each step and visiting $2{,}507$ and $2{,}285$ states on $(0, 1, 2, 5)$ and $(0, 1, 3, 5)$ under $D_{(1,5)}$). In Theorem 1(iii), we formulate two **backward outgoing cost-to-go operators** directly on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$: the **backward upfront cost-to-go operator** $T_r^{\mathrm{up}}$ (2.4) and the **backward exit-bit cost-to-go operator** $T_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}$ (2.5) (which is the deterministic-row specialization $r_g \equiv m_y q$ of our bivariate operator $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$ in (4.3)). We prove that $T_r^{\mathrm{up}}$ and $T_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}$ are intertwined by the exact linear gauge bijection $\Phi(v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}})(m) = v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}(m) - m_y q |m|$, collapsing the $2{,}507$ and $2{,}285$ states of [1, Table 3] down to $29$ and $28$ states (or $35$ and $36$ states after directional reflection $S \leftrightarrow W - S$), with immediate $W$-step regeneration $(\widehat{T}_0^{\mathrm{up}})^W(v) = \mathbf{0}$.
- **Second (Exact Complexity Scope of $D(S_k) = |\mathcal{M}(S_k)|$):** In Theorem 1(i)–(ii), we prove a sharp complexity dichotomy: for single-hole step sets $S_{W+1}^{(-h)} = [0, W] \setminus \{h\}$, $D(S_{W+1}^{(-h)}) = W + h + 1$ is **linear in $W$**, whereas for the sparse 3-translate family $\{0, 1, W\}$, $D(\{0, 1, W\}) = 3 \cdot 2^{W-2}$ and $D_{\min}(\{0, 1, W\}) = D(\{0, W - 1, W\}) = a_W = \Theta(\psi^W)$ ($\psi \approx 1.75488$) remain **exponential in $W$**.
- **Third (The Bivariate Curve Frontier $g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$):** In Section 4 (Theorem 3), we show that when $y \equiv f(x) + s$ is replaced by a bivariate curve family $g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ ($\deg g \ge 2$), the backward Required-Row Automaton $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$ (4.3) evaluates both vertical root counts $R_f(t)$ and horizontal root counts $R_g(t)$ simultaneously on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$, doubling the all-window consecutive translate horizon from $k_{\max} = 4$ to **$k_{\max} = 9$** for elliptic/hyperelliptic curves $y^2 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ and to **$k_{\max} = 13$** for superelliptic curves $y^3 \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$ across all degrees $d \ge 3$.

## 2. Reachable-Mask Complexity Dichotomy, Backward Cost-to-Go Gauge Bijection, and Exact Rational Poisson Automata (Theorem 1)

**Theorem 1 (Reachable-Mask Complexity Dichotomy, Backward Cost-to-Go Gauge Bijection on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$, and Exact Rational Poisson Solvability).** *Let $S_k = \{0 = s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_k = W\} \subseteq [0, W]$ have span $W \ge 2$ and size $k = |S_k| \ge 3$, and let $\mathcal{M}(S_k) \subseteq \{0, 1\}^W$ be the reachable-mask set of [5, Section 3.1] and [6, Section 5.1] defined by (1.3), with $D(S_k) = |\mathcal{M}(S_k)|$ and $D_{\min}(S_k) = \min\bigl(D(S_k), D(\mathrm{rev}(S_k))\bigr)$ where $\mathrm{rev}(S_k) = W - S_k$.*

*(i) **Single-Hole Linear Dimension Law ($D = W + h + 1$):** Every shift set satisfies $D(S_k) \ge k$, with equality $D(S_k) = k$ if and only if $S_k = (0, 1, \dots, k - 1)$ is consecutive. For every single-hole step set $S_{W+1}^{(-h)} = \{0, 1, \dots, W\} \setminus \{h\}$ ($1 \le h \le W - 1$, $W \ge 2$), the reachable-mask set has exact linear cardinality*
$$D\bigl(S_{W+1}^{(-h)}\bigr) \;=\; W + h + 1, \qquad D_{\min}\bigl(S_{W+1}^{(-h)}\bigr) \;=\; W + \min(h, W - h) + 1 \;\le\; \left\lfloor \frac{3W}{2} \right\rfloor + 1. \tag{2.1}$$

*(ii) **Sparse 3-Shift Exponential Dimension Law ($D = 3 \cdot 2^{W-2}$ and $D_{\min} = a_W = \Theta(\psi^W)$):** By contrast, for the sparse 3-translate family $S = \{0, 1, W\}$ ($W \ge 2$), the forward reachable-mask dimension is exponential:*
$$D\bigl(\{0, 1, W\}\bigr) \;=\; 2^{W-1} + 2^{W-2} \;=\; 3 \cdot 2^{W-2}. \tag{2.2}$$
*Furthermore, for its reflection $\mathrm{rev}(\{0, 1, W\}) = \{0, W - 1, W\}$, $\mathcal{M}(\{0, W - 1, W\})$ consists of all subsets $U \subseteq \{0, 1, \dots, W - 1\}$ having no isolated $1$'s in $\{1, \dots, W - 1\}$, and its cardinality $a_W = D(\{0, W - 1, W\})$ satisfies the exact fourth-order linear recurrence*
$$a_W \;=\; a_{W-1} + a_{W-2} + a_{W-4} \qquad (W \ge 5), \qquad (a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4) \;=\; (2, 3, 5, 9), \tag{2.3}$$
*with characteristic polynomial $x^4 - x^3 - x^2 - 1 = (x + 1)(x^3 - 2x^2 + x - 1)$, so $D_{\min}(\{0, 1, W\}) = a_W = \Theta(\psi^W) \ge 2^{\lfloor W/2 \rfloor}$ remains exponential in $W$ with growth base $\psi \approx 1.7548777$ (the real root of $x(x - 1)^2 = 1$).*

*(iii) **Backward Cost-to-Go Gauge Bijection on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ and Universal $W$-Step Regenerative Rational Poisson Solutions:** Let $(m_y, \lambda) = (m_y, p_{\mathrm{num}}/q)$ be rational strip weights (scaled to integers $(m_y q, p_{\mathrm{num}})$), and order $\mathcal{M}(S_k) = \{m_0 = 0 < m_1 < \dots < m_{D-1}\}$. In the **backward upfront cost-to-go recursion** on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ (where $m_i$ represents the mask of relative rows in $[t, t + W)$ already covered by steps $< t$), the single-step outgoing operator $T_r^{\mathrm{up}}$ ($r \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$) is*
$$T_r^{\mathrm{up}}(v)(m_i) \;=\; \begin{cases} v\bigl(T_{\mathrm{vert}}(m_i)\bigr), & \text{if } r = 0, \\[4pt] \min\Bigl(\, v\bigl(T_{\mathrm{vert}}(m_i)\bigr) + p_{\mathrm{num}} r,\;\; v\bigl(T_{\mathrm{hor}}(m_i)\bigr) + m_y q \cdot |S_k \setminus m_i| \,\Bigr), & \text{if } r \ge 1, \end{cases} \tag{2.4}$$
*with normalized state transition $\widehat{T}_r^{\mathrm{up}}(v)(m_i) = T_r^{\mathrm{up}}(v)(m_i) - T_r^{\mathrm{up}}(v)(0)$ and step cost $c(v, r) = \frac{1}{q} T_r^{\mathrm{up}}(v)(0)$. On any deterministic arrival sequence $(R_0, \dots, R_{L-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^L$, evolving $v_{t+1} = \widehat{T}_{\widetilde{R}_t}^{\mathrm{up}}(v_t)$ from $v_0 = \mathbf{0}$ on the **reversed sequence** $\widetilde{R}_t = R_{L - 1 - t}$ ($0 \le t < L$) yields $\mathrm{OPT}_{m_y, \lambda}(R_0, \dots, R_{L-1}; S_k) = \sum_{t=0}^{L-1} c(v_t, \widetilde{R}_t)$.*

*Similarly, define the **backward exit-bit cost-to-go operator** $T_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}$ on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ (which is the deterministic-row specialization $r_g \equiv m_y q$ of our bivariate operator $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$ in (4.3), distinct from the forward incoming-mask minimization in [6, Eq. (5.1)] and [5, Eq. (3.1)]) by*
$$T_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}(v)(m_i) \;=\; \begin{cases} v\bigl(T_{\mathrm{vert}}(m_i)\bigr) + m_y q (m_i \mathbin{\&} 1), & \text{if } r = 0, \\[4pt] \min\Bigl(\, v\bigl(T_{\mathrm{vert}}(m_i)\bigr) + p_{\mathrm{num}} r + m_y q (m_i \mathbin{\&} 1),\;\; v\bigl(T_{\mathrm{hor}}(m_i)\bigr) + m_y q \,\Bigr), & \text{if } r \ge 1. \end{cases} \tag{2.5}$$
*Then $T_r^{\mathrm{up}}$ and $T_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}$ are intertwined on $\mathcal{M}(S_k)$ by the **exact linear gauge bijection***
$$\Phi(v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}})(m_i) \;=\; v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}(m_i) - m_y q |m_i|, \qquad \widehat{T}_r^{\mathrm{up}}\bigl(\Phi(v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}})\bigr) \;=\; \Phi\bigl(\widehat{T}_r^{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}(v_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}})\bigr), \tag{2.6}$$
*so $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{up}}(S_k; m_y, \lambda)| = |\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bwd\text{-}exit}}(S_k; m_y, \lambda)|$ on the same orientation of $S_k$. Because $T_0^{\mathrm{up}}(v)(m_i) = v(m_i \gg 1)$ charges zero step cost at all masks during empty arrivals $r = 0$, $W$ consecutive empty arrivals reset **every** reachable state $v \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dual}}(S_k; m_y, \lambda)$ to $\bigl(\widehat{T}_0^{\mathrm{up}}\bigr)^W(v) = \mathbf{0}$. Consequently, for any root-count law $R$ with **rational probabilities $\mathbb{P}(R = r) \in \mathbb{Q}$** and $p_0 = \mathbb{P}(R = 0) > 0$, $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dual}}(S_k; m_y, \lambda)$ is a single finite, irreducible, aperiodic recurrent Markov class, and the rational linear Poisson system $(I - P)h + \gamma \mathbf{1} = \bar{c}$ ($h_{\mathbf{0}} = 0$) has a **unique exact rational solution** $(\gamma^*, h^*) \in \mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}^{|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dual}}|}$ satisfying $\bar{c}(v) + (P h^*)(v) - h^*(v) = \gamma^*$ at every state $v \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dual}}$.*

*Proof.* **(i)** For $S = S_{W+1}^{(-h)} = [0, W] \setminus \{h\}$ ($1 \le h \le W - 1$), the shifted masks $A_j = M_S \gg j$ are $[0, W - j] \setminus \{h - j\}$ for $1 \le j \le h$ and $[0, W - j]$ for $h + 1 \le j \le W$. Let $U = \bigcup_{j \in B} A_j$ be any nonempty union ($B \subseteq \{1, \dots, W\}$) with $j_1 = \min B$. If $h + 1 \le j_1 \le W$, then $U = A_{j_1} = [0, W - j_1]$ ($W - h$ masks, plus $\emptyset$). If $1 \le j_1 \le h$, then $A_{j_1} = [0, W - j_1] \setminus \{h - j_1\}$ misses only $h - j_1$, which is covered by $A_j$ ($j > j_1$) if and only if $j_1 + 1 \le j \le W - h + j_1$; thus each $j_1 \in \{1, \dots, h\}$ yields exactly $2$ masks ($[0, W - j_1] \setminus \{h - j_1\}$ and $[0, W - j_1]$). Summing gives $(W - h + 1) + 2h = W + h + 1$.

**(ii)** For $S = \{0, 1, W\}$, $A_1 = M_S \gg 1 = \{0, W - 1\}$ and $A_j = M_S \gg j = \{W - j\}$ ($2 \le j \le W$) are all singletons $\{0\}, \dots, \{W - 2\}$. Any subset of $\{0, \dots, W - 2\}$ ($2^{W-1}$ masks) can be formed from $A_2, \dots, A_W$, while including $A_1 = \{0, W - 1\}$ fixes bits $0$ and $W - 1$ to $1$ and allows arbitrary subsets of $\{1, \dots, W - 2\}$ ($2^{W-2}$ masks), giving $D(\{0, 1, W\}) = 2^{W-1} + 2^{W-2} = 3 \cdot 2^{W-2}$. For $\mathrm{rev}(S) = \{0, W - 1, W\}$, the generators are the adjacent pairs $A_j = \{W - 1 - j, W - j\}$ ($1 \le j \le W - 1$) and the singleton $A_W = \{0\}$. A subset $U \subseteq \{0, \dots, W - 1\}$ is a union of $\{0\}$ and adjacent pairs $\{i, i + 1\}$ if and only if $U$ has no isolated $1$ in $\{1, \dots, W - 1\}$, i.e., its bitstring $u_0 u_1 \dots u_{W-1} \in \{0, 1\}^W$ avoids the substring $010$ and the suffix $01$. Partitioning valid length-$W$ bitstrings ($W \ge 4$) by their suffix: $a_{W-1}$ end in $0$; $a_{W-3}$ end in $011$ (obtained by appending $011$ to any valid bitstring of length $W - 3$); and $a_{W-1} - a_{W-2}$ end in $111$ (obtained by appending $1$ to any valid bitstring of length $W - 1$ ending in $1$). Hence $a_W = 2a_{W-1} - a_{W-2} + a_{W-3}$ ($W \ge 4$), and adding $a_{W-1} - 2a_{W-2} + a_{W-3} - a_{W-4} = 0$ yields $a_W = a_{W-1} + a_{W-2} + a_{W-4}$ for all $W \ge 5$.

### 4.1 Value-Torus Bipartite Formulation and Two-Sided One-Distinguished-Root Transfer

Consider now the bivariate separated-variable algebraic curve translate carrier $H_p(f, g; S_k) = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : g(y) \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p \text{ for some } s \in S_k\}$ of (1.1), where $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ ($\deg f = d_1 \ge 3$) and $g \in \mathbb{F}_p[y]$ ($\deg g = d_2 \ge 2$). Unlike univariate graphs $y \equiv f(x) + s \pmod p$, where each value $w \in \mathbb{F}_p$ corresponds to a single horizontal residue $y \equiv w \pmod p$, here each value $u \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has vertical root multiplicity $R_f(u) = |\{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : f(x) = u\}|$ and each value $w \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has **horizontal root multiplicity** $R_g(w) = |\{y \in \mathbb{F}_p : g(y) = w\}|$.

Partitioning the value torus $\mathbb{F}_p$ into $\lfloor p/(L + W) \rfloor$ contiguous blocks of length $L + W$ (each consisting of an active block $I_L(u_0) = \{u_0 + t : 0 \le t < L\}$ and $W$ seam residues $V_{\mathrm{seam}}(u_0) = \{u_0 + L + t : 0 \le t < W\}$ covered vertically, plus at most $L + W - 1$ leftover residues), choosing a vertical value-cover $U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0) \subseteq I_L(u_0)$ and the minimal induced horizontal value-cover $Y_{\mathrm{val}}^*(U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0)) = \bigcup_{t \in I_L(u_0) \setminus U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0),\, R_f(t) > 0} (t + S_k)$ on each block lifts to the physical coordinate line cover on $W_n(x_0, y_0)$:
$$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}} \;=\; \bigl\{x \in [x_0, x_0 + n) : f(x \bmod p) \in U_{\mathrm{val}} \cup V_{\mathrm{seam}}\bigr\}, \qquad \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}} \;=\; \bigl\{y \in [y_0, y_0 + n) : g(y \bmod p) \in Y_{\mathrm{val}}^*(U_{\mathrm{val}})\bigr\}. \tag{4.1}$$
Crucially, because $U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0)$ and $Y_{\mathrm{val}}^*(U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0))$ depend **only on the unsplit value-fiber root counts** $\bigl(R_f(u_0 + t), R_g(u_0 + t)\bigr)_{0 \le t < L + W}$, averaging over the block phase $u_0 \in \{0, \dots, L + W - 1\}$ expresses the physical line count on $W_n(x_0, y_0)$ as
$$|\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}| + |\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}| \;=\; \frac{1}{L + W} \sum_{u_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p} \left(\, \sum_{t \in U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0)} \sum_{f(x) = u_0 + t} w_x(x) \;+\; \sum_{w \in Y_{\mathrm{val}}^*(U_{\mathrm{val}}(u_0))} \sum_{g(y) = w} w_y(y) \,\right) \;+\; O\left(\frac{d_1 W n}{L}\right), \tag{4.2}$$

### 4.2 The Bivariate Required-Row Automaton and Exact Difference-Tuple Invariant $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$

When horizontal row weights $R_g(w)$ vary randomly with $w$, upfront horizontal charging at step $t$ would require knowing the future vector $(R_g(t + s))_{s \in S_k}$. Instead, viewing each mask $m \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$ in the **Required-Row Basis** (where bit $j \in m$ indicates that value-row $t + j$ has been required by an uncovered fiber $t' < t$ and must be paid for when bit $j$ shifts down to bit $0$ at step $t + j$) evaluates **both** $R_f(t)$ and $R_g(t)$ simultaneously at step $t$:
$$T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m_i) \;=\; \begin{cases} v(m_i \gg 1) + r_g \cdot (m_i \mathbin{\&} 1), & \text{if } r_f = 0, \\[4pt] \min\Bigl(\, v(m_i \gg 1) + r_f + r_g \cdot (m_i \mathbin{\&} 1),\;\; v\bigl((m_i \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1\bigr) + r_g \,\Bigr), & \text{if } r_f \ge 1, \end{cases} \tag{4.3}$$
with normalized state transition $\widehat{T}_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m_i) = T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m_i) - T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(0)$ and step cost $c_{\mathrm{bivar}}(v; r_f, r_g) = T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(0)$.

**Theorem 3 (Bivariate Required-Row Automaton, Exact Difference-Tuple Invariant $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$, and All-Window Exclusions for Elliptic, Hyperelliptic, and Superelliptic Curve Translates).** *Fix block length $L \ge 1$ and shift set $S_k \subset [0, W]$ of span $W$. *Then $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ is invariant under all transitions $\widehat{T}_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}$ ($(r_f, r_g) \in \Sigma_f \times \Sigma_g$). - *For Kummer/superelliptic $g(y) = y^m$ ($C_m$, $\Sigma_g = \{0, m\}$, $|\Sigma_g| = 2$, $M = m$):*

- *For separated cubics $(S_3, S_3)$ ($\Sigma_g = \{0, 1, 3\}$, $|\Sigma_g| = 3$, $M = 3$):*
  $$\bigl|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; S_3, S_3)\bigr| \;=\; \bigl|\mathcal{I}_k(\{0, 1, 3\})\bigr| \;=\; 1 \;+\; \sum_{r=1}^{k-1} 3 \cdot 3^{r-1} \;=\; 1 + \sum_{r=1}^{k-1} 3^r \;=\; \frac{3^k - 1}{2}. \tag{4.6}$$

*(iii) **Bivariate Saturation $r_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm{bivar}} = M k$ and All-Window Exclusion Horizons $k_{\max} = 9$ (Elliptic/Hyperelliptic) and $k_{\max} = 13$ (Superelliptic):** On every reachable state $v \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, g)$, $v((m_i \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) - v(m_i \gg 1) \le M(k - 1)$, so $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)$ in (4.3) saturates to the horizontal branch for all $r_f \ge r_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm{bivar}} = M k$. *Proof of Theorem 3(ii)–(iii).* For consecutive $S_k = (0, \dots, k - 1)$, $m_0 = 0$ ($m_0 \gg 1 = m_0$, $m_0 \mathbin{\&} 1 = 0$) and $m_i = \{0, \dots, i - 1\}$ ($m_i \gg 1 = m_{i-1}$, $m_i \mathbin{\&} 1 = 1$ for $1 \le i \le k - 1$), while $(m_i \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1 = m_{k-1}$ for all $0 \le i \le k - 1$. Let $v \in \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ have cumulative sums $v_i = \sum_{\ell=1}^i \Delta_\ell$ ($0 = v_0 \le v_1 \le \dots \le v_{k-1}$) and let $v' = \widehat{T}_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)$ with differences $\Delta'_i = v'_i - v'_{i-1}$.
- **Closure of $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$:** (1) If $r_f = 0$, then $v'_i = v_{i-1} + r_g$ ($1 \le i \le k - 1$), so $\Delta' = (r_g, \Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{k-2}) \in \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ since $r_g \in \Sigma_g \subseteq \{0, 1, \dots, M\}$. (2) If $r_f \ge \max(1, v_{k-1})$, then $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m_0) = \min(r_f, v_{k-1} + r_g)$ and $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m_i) = v_{k-1} + r_g$ for all $1 \le i \le k - 1$, so $\Delta'_1 = \max(0, v_{k-1} + r_g - r_f) \in \{0, 1, \dots, r_g\} \subseteq \{0, 1, \dots, M\}$ and $\Delta'_2 = \dots = \Delta'_{k-1} = 0$, which lies in $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$. (3) If $1 \le r_f < v_{k-1}$, set $C = v_{k-1} - r_f \in \{1, \dots, v_{k-1} - 1\}$. Then $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)(m_0) = r_f$ and $v'_i = r_g + \min(v_{i-1}, C)$ for all $1 \le i \le k - 1$. Thus $\Delta'_1 = r_g \in \Sigma_g$, and for $2 \le i \le k - 1$, $\Delta'_i = \min(v_{i-1}, C) - \min(v_{i-2}, C)$. If $C \ge v_{k-2}$, then $\Delta' = (r_g, \Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{k-2}) \in \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$. If $C < v_{k-2}$, let $s \in \{1, \dots, k - 2\}$ be the unique index with $v_{s-1} < C \le v_s$ (note $s \le r$ where $r$ is the last non-zero index of $\Delta$, so $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{s-1} \in \Sigma_g$ and $\Delta_s \in \{1, \dots, M\}$). Then $\Delta'_i = \Delta_{i-1} \in \Sigma_g$ for $2 \le i \le s$, $\Delta'_{s+1} = C - v_{s-1} \in \{1, \dots, \Delta_s\} \subseteq \{1, \dots, M\}$ is the last non-zero entry of $\Delta'$, and $\Delta'_i = 0$ for $s + 2 \le i \le k - 1$. Hence $\Delta' \in \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$.
- **Reachability of $\mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$:** From $\mathbf{0}$, a single step $(r_f, M)$ with $0 \le r_f < M$ gives $\Delta' = (M - r_f, 0, \dots, 0)$, reaching $(c, 0, \dots, 0)$ for all $c = M - r_f$ ($r_f \in \Sigma_f \cap [0, M - 1]$). For $C_3$ ($M = 3$) under $S_3$ ($\Sigma_f = \{0, 1, 3\}$), $(3, 0, \dots, 0)$ and $(2, 0, \dots, 0)$ are reached in $1$ step via $(0, 3)$ and $(1, 3)$, while $(1, 0, \dots, 0)$ is reached in $3$ steps via $\mathbf{0} \xrightarrow{(1, 3)} (2, 0, \dots, 0) \xrightarrow{(1, 0)} (0, 1, 0, \dots, 0) \xrightarrow{(3, 3)} (1, 0, \dots, 0)$ ($k \ge 3$); for $(S_3, S_3)$, $(1, 0, \dots, 0)$ is reached in $1$ step via $(0, 1)$. Finally, from $(\Delta_r, 0, \dots, 0)$ ($\Delta_r \in \{1, \dots, M\}$), applying the $r - 1$ pure horizontal shift transitions $(0, \Delta_{r-1}), \dots, (0, \Delta_1)$ ($\Delta_j \in \Sigma_g$) reaches $(\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{r-1}, \Delta_r, 0, \dots, 0)$, proving $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, g) = \mathcal{I}_k(\Sigma_g)$ and (4.5)–(4.6).
- **General-Shift Hamming-Lipschitz Bound & Bivariate Saturation ($r_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm{bivar}} = M k$):** For any shift set $S_k \subseteq [0, W]$ ($0 \in S_k$, $|S_k| = k$, consecutive or non-consecutive), we prove by induction on the step count from $v_0 = \mathbf{0}$ that every reachable state $v \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bivar}}(S_k; f, g)$ satisfies the **monotone Hamming-Lipschitz inequality** $v(m') - v(m) \le M \cdot |m' \setminus m|$ for all $m, m' \in \mathcal{M}(S_k)$. Indeed, if $v$ satisfies this bound and $T = T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)$ ($0 \le r_g \le M$), then on the vertical branch at $m$ we have $T(m') - T(m) \le [v(m' \gg 1) - v(m \gg 1)] + r_g[(m' \mathbin{\&} 1) - (m \mathbin{\&} 1)] \le M|(m' \gg 1) \setminus (m \gg 1)| + M \mathbf{1}_{0 \in m' \setminus m} = M|m' \setminus m|$, while on the horizontal branch at $m$ ($T(m) = v((m \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) + r_g$) we have $T(m') - T(m) \le v((m' \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) - v((m \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) \le M|((m' \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) \setminus ((m \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1)| \le M|m' \setminus m|$. Applying this inequality at $m' = (m_i \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1 = (m_i \gg 1) \cup B_{\mathrm{elim}}$ and $m = m_i \gg 1$—where $B_{\mathrm{elim}} = M_{S_k} \gg 1$ has Hamming weight $|B_{\mathrm{elim}}| = |S_k \setminus \{0\}| = k - 1$ for **every** consecutive or non-consecutive $S_k$ (even when $|m_i|$ can reach $W > k - 1$, as in $S = \{0, 1, 4\}, k = 3, M = 2, v(15) = 8$)—yields the universal successor-difference bound $v((m_i \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) - v(m_i \gg 1) \le M|B_{\mathrm{elim}} \setminus (m_i \gg 1)| \le M(k - 1)$. Consequently, for all $r_f \ge M k$, $v(m_i \gg 1) + r_f + r_g(m_i \mathbin{\&} 1) \ge v(m_i \gg 1) + M(k - 1) + M \ge v((m_i \cup M_{S_k}) \gg 1) + r_g$, so $T_{r_f, r_g}^{\mathrm{bivar}}(v)$ saturates to the horizontal branch for all $r_f \ge M k$. $\square$
