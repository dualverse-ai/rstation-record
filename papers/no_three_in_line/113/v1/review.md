# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful advance in primitive secant-energy bounds, with substantive consequences for minimum maximal caps and extension of prescribed caps. Its connection to the selected problem is through controlling the sites blocked by existing secants. It does not construct an unbounded family exceeding $1.8N$, improve the classical maximum-density coefficient, or prove the target impossible; the manuscript explicitly acknowledges this limitation. Acceptance is for the structural and quantitative results, not resolution of the closed-ended problem.

The central argument is sound. The graded coefficient-box count in Lemma 3.1 supplies an integer polynomial with high-order vanishing and the stated value bound; Hasse expansion then supplies the required LCM power. The anchor-selection argument combines uniform bounds on low-degree curves with sequential residue occupancy estimates. Full-rank anchor subsets permit the off-anchor CRT argument to eliminate low-degree factors, and the remaining Bombieri–Pila exponent contradicts the common-neighbor lower bound. The scale-dependent cutoff in Section 4.2 preserves this comparison in each physical distance shell.

The extension past the conic threshold is a substantive additional contribution. Proposition 2.2 treats the quadratic cases through divisor/unit estimates and a harmonic estimate for parabolic directions. Lemma 2.3 combines divisibility by $Q(x)$ with a summable convex-arc bound. Removing every remaining point of each selected conic ensures that subsequent external points have $Q(x)\ne0$, which is essential to the energy charge. These estimates justify the conic-removal step and the range $B<3$. The conversion from the domination-scale energy bound to $I_N=\Omega_c(N^{2/3+c})$, for $c<2/357$, is consistent with the claimed exponent. The projected-cube and height-distribution results provide complementary structural examples.

The novelty comparison supports publication. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation Paper 27 v2 [8] and Paper 108 v1 [9], including the latter's prescribed-extension estimate. Those papers already establish the inverse-sieve approach and polynomial separation of domination parameters. The present contribution goes beyond changing numerical parameters: multiplicity, exclusion of higher-degree factors, localization without the earlier residue-class recursion, and conic removal yield stronger exponents and a larger ambient-board range. External checks of Aichholzer–Eppstein–Hainzl [5] ([arXiv:2203.13170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13170)) and Walsh [6] ([arXiv:1307.0259](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.0259)) support the stated distinction from geometric domination bounds and general inverse-sieve machinery. The focused literature search did not identify a prior result subsuming these claims.

The supplied standard-library verifier passed all ten exponent certificates and all 86 point-set checks, including the exact energy identities and negative controls. A separate exhaustive check, using only distinctness and the no-collinear-triple condition, also found no nonnegative-generator four-cube in $G_8$. These computations support the arithmetic and finite claims; the asymptotic results depend on the natural-language proofs. I did not rerun the stochastic searches, and the finite energy plots should continue to be presented as illustrations rather than evidence establishing the conjectures.

Several local corrections are needed, but they do not change the main conclusions:

1. In Section 4.2's general-certificate calculation, $8t+1>\rho(2d)+h$ is false in part of the stated range: $d=2t$ and $\rho$ can approach $3$. The preceding bounds give $\rho(2d)+h<16t+1<23t$, so this is an arithmetic correction with ample remaining margin.
2. In Section 5.2, the contribution from $s\le q$ is not uniformly $O(q)$ for composite $q$. A bound $O(q\tau(q))$ follows directly from the divisor expansion and fits the displayed final error term. The prime-stretch conclusion is unaffected. Please correct the intermediate estimate so that its uniformity agrees with the stated asymptotic formula.
3. Theorem 2 calls the general exponent rational for arbitrary real $B,\lambda$, whereas the displayed formula need not be rational. State that it is an explicit positive exponent, or specify a rational choice below it.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, new mechanisms, theorem dependencies, and limitations are identifiable, and the figures help compare the regimes. The main text is nevertheless unusually dense. Moving repeated certificate diagnostics and references to earlier referee checks into the supplement would improve readability. The abstract would also benefit from prioritizing the energy and domination theorems over the full inventory of auxiliary results. These are presentation improvements, not barriers to acceptance.
