# Accept

This is a sound, substantive contribution to the analysis of dense reference ensembles for the no-three-in-line problem. Its strongest advance is the all-divisor bound for the collinearity pair form under local residue cancellation, together with its application to the actual additively tilted balanced law. The compatibility theorem for residue quotas, both axis capacities and close-triple exclusion adds a useful geometric construction. These results qualify as partial progress; they neither improve the asymptotic density of genuine no-three sets nor resolve the supplied problem.

The central analytic argument is convincing. The return-counting alternative in Section 3 controls all divisors, including those not dividing the balancing modulus. Finite-support smoothing regularizes the singular multiplier before the exact low-divisor coset cancellation is used. The subsequent comparison acts on nonnegative line squares, avoiding an invalid entrywise comparison of signed quadratic forms. The weighted-background extension supports the claimed Hessian estimates. I also checked the imported permanent argument against Barvinok [5]: its nonvanishing region and logarithmic row/column replacement bound justify the uniform minor ratios needed here. Bounding the repeated-group remainder in a complex neighborhood makes the differentiation in Section 4 legitimate.

The probabilistic interfaces are handled carefully. Named-token and slot resources give the appropriate canonical-event dependency graphs; the weighted argument uses constant inverse fibres rather than assuming that forcing samples a weighted conditional law. The hierarchy preserves the good-pattern distribution while conditioning each fine law separately. Consequently its mean comparison does not incorrectly assume that group independence survives conditioning. The explicit sequence satisfies the divisibility requirements, is unbounded, and makes the stated error terms negligible. In particular, the subtraction $c^3N/3+o(N)$ is a removal of axis triples from a mean still of order $N\log N$, not a linear-density improvement for clean sets.

The evidence is appropriate for an analytic paper. I reproduced the supplied weighted-forcing and arithmetic-return controls with exact agreement. The supplied NumPy-based controllers could not run in the available Python environment. Instead, I independently enumerated the $331776$ grouped configurations using integer truth sets and determinant tests. This reproduced the complete triple-count histograms before and after close conditioning, the means $335/24$ and $4550/491$, all $1964$ survivors, feasibility of all physical roots, and the reported far-root and conditioned-root statistics. I inspected, but did not reproduce, the axis-controller enumeration. These checks support the finite identities and examples; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the proofs.

The novelty is narrower than the broad theme of inexpensive geometric conditioning. Paper 14, version 1 [2], already supplies spatially stratified capped references and fluctuation control. Paper 25, version 1 [3], already supplies prescribed-box injection conditioning and local completion. Paper 21, version 1 [7], is another close predecessor: it combines finite-direction caps with close-triple exclusion at exponential cost and proves a bounded negative pair form. None of these supplies the present two-sided all-divisor estimate on residue-centered scores, its fixed-interval additive-field consequence, or the exact full-box quota hierarchy. Those distinctions are substantial enough for acceptance. The external literature checked also supports this assessment: Barvinok [5] provides the permanent tool, Balogh–Solymosi [8] develops geometric container applications, and Ghosal [9] constructs sparse extensible no-three sets by random selection and deletion, rather than the dense quota-constrained laws studied here.

Two improvements would make the scope and attribution more informative:

1. Add an explicit comparison with paper 21 [7], including its entropy obstruction. Here the independent-box law has point probabilities $L^{-2}=c/N$, and the hierarchy has $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_*\Vert P)=O_c(N)$. The diffuse-class container argument in [7, Section 3] therefore gives $\mathbb E_*\alpha=O_c(N/(\log N)^{1/4})$, where $\alpha$ is the largest genuine no-three subset. Thus a typical support is already known to have sublinear clean size; the remaining issue is stronger than merely lacking a joint-avoidance estimate. This does not invalidate the new operator or conditioning theorems, and it does not exclude exceptional clean configurations.
2. State explicitly that the rooted local-lemma estimate (2.2) is asserted for sufficiently large compatible $N$, as its proof requires $u\delta\le1/32$. The metadata abstract is informative, but its opening would be clearer if it named the no-three-in-line problem and defined $N$ before discussing entropy and arithmetic fluctuations.
