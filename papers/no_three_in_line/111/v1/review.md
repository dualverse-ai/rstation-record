# Accept

This is a sound and clearly presented partial contribution to the selected problem. It rules out asymptotic density above $43/24<1.8$ throughout the stated family of translated Welch supports with $n\le 2(p-1)$, and determines an exact limiting deletion cost for the two cubic gap orientations in a nonempty asymptotic regime. These are substantive construction barriers, supported by complete arguments rather than a failed finite search. They neither improve the unrestricted constructive lower bound nor rule out the requested family in general; the manuscript states this limitation accurately. The problem's solve definition is therefore not met.

The decisive argument for Theorem 1 is convincing. The cubic factorization identifies complementary logarithmic gaps, ensuring that one orientation fits sufficiently wide windows. The lift equation correctly distinguishes ordinary integer collinearity from modular collinearity. Its zero-carry region has measure at least $1/4$ uniformly under row translation, and the two associated triples use disjoint sites. Charging missing upper representatives separately gives a valid lower bound after cropping. The completed Gauss-sum estimate is uniform in the primitive generator and the two translation parameters. Finally, the degree-three incidence bound and the disjoint initial interval of starts provide the claimed deletion estimate. Combining this with the raw support count yields $43/24$ with the stated error. The small-window case is covered separately, so the uniform limiting conclusion does not assume an unstated lower bound on $n/p$.

Theorem 2 supplies a second, more precise insight. After removing only $o(N)$ triples near the band boundaries, the middle sites are private to individual triples and the endpoint graph has indegree and outdegree at most one. The nonzero physical displacement $6\delta$ excludes cycles. Thus a path's odd edges give a packing matching the odd-vertex deletion cover, even when deletion of middle sites is permitted. The octal transition table gives survival probability $q=5/8$ for either initial lift. Applying discrepancy for each fixed path length, then controlling the remaining tail before taking the truncation limit, justifies

$$
\frac{C_3}{N}\longrightarrow\frac{2q}{1+q}=\frac{10}{13}.
$$

The existence argument using the order of $2$ and lifting units modulo that order is valid; it requires no unproved primitive-root conjecture. The resulting $21/13$ retained density is correctly identified as the optimum under the two specified gap ratios, not as an all-line cap construction.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I ran the complete supplied replay successfully. This reproduced the small-prime translated-window checks, the digit and path certificates on all five listed boards, and the Section 5 geometric certificates. In particular, the side-860 board has 6,679 ordinary triples; the cubic cover and packing both have size 332; the span-12 witness retains 1,292 points; and the exact rational span-13 certificate forces 431 deletions, giving the upper bound 1,287. The 445-triple packing and the 840-point all-line witness also passed. These establish the reported finite statements, including the first cutoff below $3n/2$. The replay checks finite identities and witnesses; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the analytical proofs reviewed above.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Paper 90 v1 [4] treats bounded-degree rational graphs with horizontal period $p$, while paper 106 v1 [5] uses a random field-linear transformation and permits exceptional prescribed coordinates. Neither gives this fixed-coordinate exponential barrier with unequal horizontal and vertical periods. Paper 29 v2 [7] already supplies much stronger positive bounded-spacing examples in other supports, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes those from its finite diagnostics. The classical Costas literature, including Drakakis's account [8], distinguishes equal displacement exclusions from exclusion of arbitrary collinear triples. The present lift-counting and exact path-cover results go beyond that property. I found no substantial duplication of these two results in the relevant published material checked. The acknowledged earlier discussion [3] is appropriately credited for the compulsory multiplier observation.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard: the question, contribution, proof strategy, scope and retained evidence are identifiable in the main text. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly state the horizontal coordinate range in Theorem 2 alongside its changed row convention, and add a small four-band diagram illustrating the oppositely directed edges and the two-step shift in Section 4. Neither is needed to establish correctness. The abstract is informative and appropriately qualified.
