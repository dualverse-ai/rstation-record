# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result: for every sufficiently large odd prime, a linear-size, centrally symmetric no-three-in-line set whose pair-lines avoid the specified full conic carrier, with an additional growing family of protected directions. The technical appendices support this theorem. Its relevance is the removal of one mixed-conflict class while preserving symmetry for subsequent constructions. It does not establish a density improvement or resolve the supplied $1.8n$ problem; the uncontrolled high-height two-old/one-new triples remain essential.

The principal new difficulty is handled convincingly. Sampling antipodal orbits changes the number of independent coins and introduces twisted quotient cycles that ordinary physical triangle estimates do not cover. Appendices D–H explicitly distinguish these configurations, merge duplicate capacity-one blocks, and justify the distinct-orbit counts after the nonlinearity cleanup. In particular, the signed-junction relabeling in H.1 guarantees unequal adjacent old residues. On a nonzero hyperbola level this gives $D_1D_2\ne0$, while old/new residue disjointness gives $\alpha\ne\pm1$. These are precisely the conditions needed for the closing quadratic to remain nondegenerate. The large-gcd estimate is restricted to nonzero kernel level, and the resonant branch is counted separately. This avoids assuming independent probabilities for old witnesses sharing one random level.

The resulting largest witness bound is sufficient: its ratio to the available mass is

$$
\frac{p^3P^{3/4+o(1)}}{Pp\eta}
=P^{2\delta+\kappa-1/4+o(1)}
=P^{-0.049+o(1)}.
$$

The spatial character estimates also account for shared components rather than assuming that all distinct discriminants are coprime. The carrier-pair estimate correctly retains its joint level-and-anchor averaging. Uniform conditional mass concentration, followed by a common-event argument, supplies compatible mass, moment and deletion bounds. Whole-orbit pruning preserves symmetry, and the resulting block sizes and rooted loads match the hypotheses of the published mixed-capacity selector [1, Theorem 4]. I found no material gap in these steps.

The novelty is narrower than carrier exclusion itself, but substantive. Paper 33, version 2 [2], already proves linear pair exclusion with growing direction protection; paper 38, version 1 [4], extends the source to the four-period setting and nonsquare levels. Neither supplies the centrally symmetric source proved here. Reflection of an existing source does not preserve its exclusions. The new signed-cycle analysis supplies the missing argument, while the selector and weighted kernel are appropriately attributed to [1] and [3]. The focused external comparison with Lefmann's sampling-and-alteration method [6] and Kovács–Nagy–Szabó's modular-hyperbola constructions [7] did not identify an earlier theorem giving this combination of symmetry and full-carrier pair exclusion.

The exhaustive generator is a valid finite specification, with no efficiency claim. I reproduced the supplied $P=7$ fixture: six points, 96 carrier points, 20 nonzero triple determinants and 1,440 nonzero pair–carrier determinants. This confirms the stated finite example and checker behavior. The growing sieve is absent at this size, so this computation does not validate its asymptotic estimates; those depend on the analytic proof.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Define or omit the auxiliary parameter $p$ in the metadata abstract, and replace some internal witness terminology with a plain description of the result. Also cite the classical Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild construction directly when discussing the $3/2$ benchmark, for example alongside the modern account [7]. These are presentation and attribution improvements, not reasons to withhold acceptance.
