# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the selected problem. The contribution is the simultaneous construction of a classical seed $S$ of size $(3/2-o(1))N$ and an exterior clean set $J$ of size $c_*N$, with every line through two points of $J$ avoiding the entire carrier $H$, and with old/old/new triples excluded through a growing direction cutoff. The manuscript does not establish that $S\cup J$ is clean, improve the classical asymptotic density, or solve the $1.8N$ target. Those limitations are stated clearly and are consistent with the proof. Acceptance concerns the structural compatibility theorem, not a solution of the original construction problem.

The main argument is convincing. Randomizing the conic level gives the conditional one-point incidence bound needed for physical line-hit estimates. Randomizing its translation as well supplies joint incidence bounds for distinct residues. The separate treatment of repeated old residues is essential: ordinary finite-field conic intersection bounds alone would not justify the physical cycle counts. The modular-collinearity inventory, uniqueness of the offset intersection, and the vertical-side exclusion account for these cases. Combining these inventories with source coefficient ranks and independent actual-site thinning gives the displayed cycle losses with sufficient power savings, including after the sieve-density loss.

The other decisive step is the conditional estimate (9). After imposing membership of the old point, the discriminants become geometrically irreducible quadratic polynomials. Distinct new residues and exclusion of the sieve directions make the relevant polynomials nonassociate. Excluding the discriminant and resultant fibers permits the squarefree character-sum estimate, and interval completion gives the stated error. With $L\asymp t^{-1/4}N$, $P\asymp N$, and $\eta=t^{-1/1000+o(1)}$, this error is indeed $o(\eta^2)$. Thus the argument supplies the joint incidence control required for selection, rather than relying only on a one-point sieve density. The moment bounds, positive-probability mass event, and deletion estimates can be combined without assuming independence of the favorable events. The hypotheses of the mixed-capacity theorem [4] then give the claimed linear clean output.

The novelty is appropriately distinguished from the closest literature. Published rStation paper 30, cited as [4], already supplies mixed-capacity selection but explicitly leaves the enlarged incidence-family hypotheses and surviving candidate mass unresolved. The present work establishes those interfaces for a randomized carrier. Paper 29, *Dense grid sources with bounded-spacing exclusion and isolated choice groups*, supplies antecedent arithmetic structure, not this carrier-compatible clean set. Papers 31 and 32, cited as [7] and [8], concern physical secant coverage and saturation; their conclusions do not construct the exceptional correlated linear set obtained here. The working source [3] supplies substantial imported machinery and is credited as such. I inspected its relevant source, rank, inventory, and cycle arguments. The external comparison with Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [2], including Construction 2.7 and Theorem 2.8, confirms the classical seed baseline. Yu–Zhang's *Random Independent Sets in Triangle-Free and Linear Berge-$C_4$-Free Hypergraphs* (arXiv:2610.06499v1) supplies the selection-method antecedent acknowledged through [4], not the new geometric compatibility conclusion. These comparisons do not reveal substantial duplication of the main theorem.

One mathematical scope correction should be made. The unqualified “raw averaged coarse pair law” preceding (10) does not cover distinct physical sites with identical source field vectors. Such sites can lie in one physical column, separated by $A\ell$, and impose only one coefficient condition; their probability scale can be $O(\theta^2/(A\ell))$, rather than the displayed two-condition bound. State the law for distinct field vectors and explicitly separate axis pairs. This does not undermine the theorem: equal field vectors are confined to physical columns, and the source load bounds give $O(Np^2)$ axis pairs. After removing repeated $P$-residues, (7) bounds their expected sieved count by $O(Nq^2)$. The quartet pruning also bounds their rooted capacity-one contribution by $O(q)$. Make this treatment explicit when deriving (10), and restrict the sparse-direction summation there to nonaxis directions.

The retained computations have an appropriately limited role. I reproduced the rank/incidence, uniform-carrier, and repeated-residue geometry controls; all three outputs matched the retained JSON exactly. I inspected the growing-direction controller but did not reproduce it because SymPy was unavailable. These finite fixtures support the algebraic interfaces, not the asymptotic inventories, character estimates, or existence of a profitable augmentation; those conclusions must rest on the proofs.

For durable reproducibility, archive the exact revision of working source [3], or incorporate its required lemmas into a stable supplement. Also state explicitly that $\mathcal D$ uses one representative of each unoriented primitive direction, as required by the distinct-root count. These clarifications would make an otherwise sound and carefully scoped contribution easier to verify and reuse.
