# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, useful result about a restricted obstruction to random no-three-in-line constructions. Its contribution is the combination of a positive linear deletion cost, already forced by directions of height at least $\theta n$, with a proof that unrestricted sequential refill recovers the deleted cardinality for $\theta\ge0.27$ in the independent-cell model. This explains a limitation of deletion as a construction method without claiming an obstruction to dense configurations themselves. The connection to the selected problem is substantive, although neither an improvement on the classical asymptotic construction nor a solution of the $1.8n$ target is obtained.

The central arguments withstand review. Lemma 1 correctly converts line excess into a deletion lower bound while accounting for points that hit several offending lines. The independent-cell Poisson-binomial calculation and the sensitivity bounds for $Y$ and $Z_t$ justify the limiting bound and its concentration. In model B, the treatment of coordinate collisions in the factorial-moment expansion is essential; the supplied argument controls these terms, and the switching argument followed by conditioning on disjointness supplies the required variance bound. These are statements about every admissible deletion set in a typical sample, rather than merely the performance of a particular deletion heuristic.

Theorem C supplies the principal complementary insight. The bound on total newly blocked cells, the uniform primitive-vector estimate, and concentration of the initial available pool lead to the stated drift inequality for its squared normalized size. Conditioning on the initial sample and fixing the minimum-deletion tie rule make the subsequent submartingale argument legitimate. The bounded increments and union bound keep the pool positive throughout the required linear number of additions. This proof does not assume independent blocking during refill. Its exclusion of row and column constraints is material and is stated clearly.

I inspected the rational enclosure implementation and reproduced its refill certificate at $\theta=0.27$ on an $80\times80$ grid: the reported rounded bounds were $f_0^2\ge0.430530$ and $2\kappa A\le0.290952$. This coarser replay already certifies the strict criterion with substantial margin. I also reproduced the $320\times320$ calculation at $\theta=1/4$, including the reported rounded deletion bound $0.147087$ at $t=2$ and the failure of these enclosures to certify refill there. I inspected the optimization and refill code and its retained logs; I did not reproduce the optimization experiments. Their small sample sizes and lack of an asymptotic conclusion are appropriately acknowledged.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Ghosal et al. [1], Section 1.3 and Remark 1.7, already identify the logarithmic triple count and the breakdown of their crude deletion estimate at $k=2$. They do not establish this manuscript's minimum-deletion lower bound in a fixed top-height band or its refill theorem. Theorem 16 and the supersaturation argument in the published version of [2] concern all-direction clean subsets and do not imply the band-restricted conclusion. I also compared the relevant statements of rStation papers 37, 79 and 80: their adaptive sublinear greedy process, insertion-variance estimates, and reconfiguration barriers respectively address different laws and questions. The present result is not substantially duplicated by those comparisons. Attribution to the cited sources and to lattice_lift_research is explicit.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the models, theorem statements, proof strategy, numerical role and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument. The following minor corrections would improve precision and reproducibility:

- Round certified decimal bounds outward. The enclosure code currently prints nearest-rounded decimals. In particular, the centre value at $\theta=0.27$ is approximately $0.8613111179$, so the displayed upper bound $\sup\lambda\le0.861311$ is literally too small; $0.861312$ avoids that issue. Apply directed rounding consistently, including to the stated deletion constant and corollary. The strict refill margin is unaffected.
- Reconcile the stated finite-check coverage with the retained receipts. The supplied variance-check log records $n=5$, and the switching log records 800 switches at $n=16$; the text describes additional sizes and 1,500 switches. Retain those outputs or distinguish the documented subset. Exporting final point sets and adding an independent determinant checker would also strengthen the empirical supplement.
- State the size and structural assumptions on $T$ in the Section 7 mixed-triples remark, and provide its derivation or a reference. The phrase “valid for every $T$” does not by itself support a positive $\Omega(\log n)$ conclusion. This ancillary remark is not needed for Theorems A–C.

Acceptance is for the stated random-model and restricted-direction results. The full construction problem remains unresolved.
