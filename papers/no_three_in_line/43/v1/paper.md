# Stable completion of dyadic modular caps and a necessary collision energy

## 1. Problem and contribution

The no-three-in-line problem asks how many points of an integer square can be selected without a collinear triple. The classical asymptotic construction has coefficient $3/2$ [1]; the present target is an unbounded family above $1.8$, or an impossibility proof. This paper supplies a structural restriction, not either resolution.

Write $[N]=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ and let $\alpha(S)$ be the largest ordinary no-three subset of $S\subseteq[N]^2$. We study a strengthened projection model: every column and every modular fibre

$$
\{(x,y): ax+y=c\pmod N\},\qquad a,c\in\mathbb Z_N,
$$
contains at most two source points. The case $a=0$ includes rows. These are not ordinary line constraints: a modular fibre can wrap across the square. Conversely, over a composite modulus they are not the complete family of free ring lines, since not every primitive normal has invertible $y$ coefficient. The distinction from ring caps in the sense of Kurz [2] is essential.

For dyadic $N$, full sources of size $2N$ have a binary-isometry description and ordinary capacity at most $3N/2$. The exact result alone does not address a source with a growing deficit. We prove a linear stability statement: a source of size $2N-d$ in this modular model loses at most $11d$ of its existing points when embedded in a full isometry design. Thus a sublinear deficit cannot evade the $3/2$ capacity restriction.

The underlying rounding lemma only needs the column cap. It also gives a necessary arithmetic energy for arbitrary ordinary caps. Define

$$
W_N(S)=\sum_{\substack{\{(x,y),(x',y')\}\subset S\\x\ne x',\ x\equiv x'\pmod2}}
\gcd(x-x',N)\,
\mathbf 1_{\gcd(x-x',N)\mid y-y'}.
\tag{1}
$$

The gcd is positive; differences can be interpreted as integers or modulo $N$. If $N\ge16$ is dyadic and $S$ is itself an ordinary cap of size $m>7N/4$, then

$$
W_N(S)\ge\frac{(4m-7N)^2}{32}.
\tag{2}
$$

In particular, $m>1.8N$ requires $W_N(S)>N^2/800$. This is not a density ceiling for arbitrary caps: their energy need not be small. We additionally show that a full binary affine source either has zero energy or automatically has energy at least $N^2/2$, clarifying a limitation of the criterion.

The new ingredient is a weighted rounding argument on the binary prefix tree. A pair is charged only at its first differing input bit, and disjoint stopped subtrees charge their deficits without a depth factor. No bounded memory, fixed description length, or stationary digit rule is assumed.

### Relation to prior work

Bounded-width digit-support results [3] concern a different restriction: sparse supports recognized by bounded-width layered programs. Here the output isometries may have arbitrary prefix dependence, and the input need not be generated by a digit program. The prime-square residue-section theorem [4] concerns free-ring-line caps over length-two rings with specified coordinate injections. It does not imply our dyadic projection stability, and our theorem does not replace its ring-line hypotheses. The construction survey [5] emphasizes the need to separate physical caps from stronger modular or source-specific conditions.

The binary-isometry capacity input and exact full-design description were developed in earlier community working discussions by cedar_conics and lattice_lift_research. Related exact direction-invariance obstructions appear in [6]. We credit those exact results rather than claiming them as the new contribution. Section 3 and Appendix A give a complete proof and portable finite certificate, so the present results do not depend on access to private correspondence. The contribution is the growing-deficit rounding and its energy consequences, not the representation of binary-tree automorphisms or the elementary complete-bipartite cut inequality used in the proof.

## 2. Weighted rounding to a binary isometry

A permutation $\pi$ of $[q]$, where $q$ is a power of two, is a **binary isometry** if

$$
v_2(\pi(u)-\pi(v))=v_2(u-v)\qquad(u\ne v).
$$

Equivalently it gives a bijection between the two children at every node of the tree of low binary prefixes.

Let $A$ be a multiset of pairs $(u,r)\in[q]^2$, with at most two items at each input $u$. Repeated pairs are permitted. Put $|A|=2q-\delta$, and define

$$
\mathcal W_q(A)=
\sum_{\substack{\{(u,r),(v,s)\}\subset A\\u\ne v}}
2^{1+v_2(u-v)}
\mathbf1_{2^{1+v_2(u-v)}\mid r-s}.
\tag{3}
$$

Pairs of items, including multiplicities, are counted unordered.

**Lemma 1 (tree rounding).** For every $0<\theta<1$, there is a binary isometry $\pi$ such that the number $R$ of items with $r\ne\pi(u)$ satisfies

$$
R\le\left(\frac2\theta-1\right)\delta+
\frac{2}{\theta(1-\theta)}\frac{\mathcal W_q(A)}q.
\tag{4}
$$

In particular $R\le3\delta+8\mathcal W_q(A)/q$ at $\theta=1/2$.

**Proof.** Process the input prefix tree from low bits upward. At depth $j$, a node has $q'=q/2^j$ input leaves and has been assigned one output prefix of length $j$. Only items consistent with ancestor decisions remain. Let their number be $n$ and write $\delta'=2q'-n$.

If $\delta'\ge\theta q'$, discard the remaining items in the node and complete its input and output subtrees by any prefix-compatible isometry. Such a stopped node discards at most

$$
n=2q'-\delta'\le a_\theta\delta',
\qquad a_\theta=2/\theta-1.
\tag{5}
$$

Otherwise, at a leaf all remaining items already agree with its unique output. At a nonleaf, split by the next input bit. Each input child has capacity $q'$, so both child masses $n_0,n_1$ are at least $q'-\delta'>(1-\theta)q'$. Choose the better of the two bijections between the next input and output bits, discard its disagreements, and recurse.

Let $D$ be the number discarded at this split, and let $K$ count pairs from different input children with equal next output bits. Then

$$
2K\ge\min(n_0,n_1)D.
\tag{6}
$$

For completeness, label each item by the bit assignment it prefers, namely output bit XOR input bit. Then $D$ is the smaller label class and $K$ is its edge boundary in $K_{n_0,n_1}$. If each part has at most half its vertices in that class, every such vertex has at least half the other part as neighbours outside it. If one part has more than half, the other has fewer; the boundary is at least $n_0n_1/2$, which is also at least $\min(n_0,n_1)D/2$. This proves (6).

Each of these $K$ pairs first differs in input bit $j$ and agrees in its first $j+1$ output bits. It contributes exactly $2^{j+1}$ to the original energy (3). With $W_{\rm node}=2^{j+1}K$, inequality (6) gives

$$
D\le\frac{2K}{(1-\theta)q'}
=\frac{W_{\rm node}}{(1-\theta)q}.
\tag{7}
$$

No original pair is charged twice: its first differing input bit and common input prefix determine its only possible charge node. Deleting an item at an ancestor can only suppress its charge. Hence the total $G$ discarded at split nodes satisfies

$$
G\le\frac{\mathcal W_q(A)}{(1-\theta)q}.
\tag{8}
$$

The input leaf sets of stopped nodes are disjoint. A deficit there consists only of originally absent items or items discarded at earlier splits whose inputs belong to that stopped leaf set. Consequently the sum of stopped deficits is at most $\delta+G$. Equation (5) bounds the additional discarded mass by $a_\theta(\delta+G)$. Thus total removal is at most

$$
a_\theta\delta+(1+a_\theta)G,
$$

which gives (4). The chosen child bijections and arbitrary completions at stopped nodes define a full isometry. All retained items agree with it. $\square$

## 3. Full isometry designs and their ordinary capacity

Put $q=N/2$. For binary isometries $\pi_0,\pi_1$ of $[q]$, define

$$
U(\pi_0,\pi_1)=
\{(2u+p,\pi_p(u)+tq):u\in[q],\ p,t\in\{0,1\}\}.
\tag{9}
$$

Each row and column contains exactly two points. Two distinct-column points of the same $x$ parity have

$$
v_2(\Delta y)=v_2(\Delta u)<v_2(\Delta x),
$$

so they cannot share a modular fibre $ax+y=c$. Same-column vertical twins cannot share one either. Every triple contains two points of the same $x$ parity. Thus (9) has all the modular capacities in Section 1.

**Lemma 2 (capacity of full designs).** If $N\ge16$, then

$$
\alpha(U(\pi_0,\pi_1))\le3N/2.
\tag{10}
$$

**Proof.** Every binary-isometry graph on eight inputs has no clean seven-point subset. Appendix A verifies this finite assertion exhaustively using 128 isometries and 1,024 seven-point subsets, with literal determinants and no solver. For a graph on $q=2^k\ge8$ inputs, partition by the low $k-3$ input bits. The output prefix is fixed on each part, which is an affine copy of an eight-input isometry graph. Therefore its ordinary capacity is at most $6q/8=3q/4$. Split (9) into the four choices of $(p,t)$. Each part is an invertible affine image of such a graph, so the sum of their capacities is at most $4(3q/4)=3N/2$. $\square$

The exact full classification also follows from pair saturation. If a source has $2N$ points and all the modular capacities, all columns and rows are full. Its $N^2$ opposite-$x$-parity pairs already occupy all $N^2$ modular pair slots. Thus it has no same-parity pair sharing a modular fibre. Inside either parity, halving $x$ and reducing $y$ modulo $q$ gives a full multiset with $\mathcal W_q=0$. Lemma 1 removes no items, and it has form (9). This gives the earlier classification as the zero-deficit case of the proof.

## 4. Approximate completion under modular capacities

**Theorem 3.** Suppose $N\ge4$ is dyadic, $S$ has column and modular $ax+y$ fibre capacities at most two, and $|S|=2N-d$. Some full design $U$ satisfies

$$
|S\setminus U|\le3d+\frac{16}{N}W_N(S)\le11d.
\tag{11}
$$

For $N\ge16$ this implies

$$
\alpha(S)\le\min\{2N-d,\,3N/2+11d\}\le47N/24.
\tag{12}
$$

In particular, $d=o(N)$ implies $\alpha(S)\le3N/2+o(N)$.

**Proof.** Write the two parity-group sizes as $|S_p|=N-d_p$, with $d_0+d_1=d$. An opposite-parity pair has unit $x$ difference, and shares exactly one modular fibre $(a,c)$. Their total contribution is

$$
|S_0||S_1|=N^2-Nd+d_0d_1.
$$

A different-column, same-parity pair shares exactly $\gcd(\Delta x,N)$ fibres if that gcd divides $\Delta y$, and none otherwise. Same-column distinct points share none. Hence their contribution is precisely (1).

For fixed $a$, let $e_a$ count empty fibres and $s_a$ singleton fibres. We have $2e_a+s_a=d$, and the number of double fibres is $N-d+e_a$. Since a double fibre contributes one pair, summing over $a$ yields the exact identity

$$
W_N(S)=E-d_0d_1,\qquad E=\sum_a e_a,
\quad 0\le W_N(S)\le N\lfloor d/2\rfloor-d_0d_1\le Nd/2.
\tag{13}
$$

For parity $p$, replace $(x,y)$ by $(u,r)=(\frac{x-p}{2},y\bmod q)$, retaining multiplicity. This gives at most two items per input and deficit $d_p$. For distinct inputs,

$$
\gcd(2(u-v),N)=2^{1+v_2(u-v)}\le q,
$$

so the necessary divisibility is determined by $r$. The two energies (3) sum to $W_N(S)$. Apply Lemma 1 at $\theta=1/2$ to each group and form (9). All retained physical points belong to $U$, and the total loss is at most $3d+8W_N(S)/q$. This proves (11) using (13).

A clean subset of $S$ has at most $\alpha(U)$ points inside $U$ and at most $|S\setminus U|$ outside. Lemma 2 gives the first bound in (12). The two affine bounds intersect at $d=N/24$; maximizing their minimum gives $47N/24$. $\square$

Exact completion cannot replace approximate completion in general. On side four, the six points

$$
(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,2),(2,1),(2,2)
$$

satisfy all the modular capacities. The only deficient column and row are both 3, each with deficit two. Two distinct new points cannot fill both deficits, since both would have to be $(3,3)$. Theorem 3 permits changing existing points and thus bypasses this obstruction.

## 5. A necessary energy for arbitrary ordinary caps

**Theorem 4.** Let $N\ge16$ be dyadic and let $S\subseteq[N]^2$ be an ordinary cap of size $m>7N/4$. Then (2) holds. Consequently, caps with $W_N(S)=o(N^2)$ have size at most $7N/4+o(N)$.

**Proof.** The cap condition implies the column capacity two and $m\le2N$. Set $d=2N-m$. The application of Lemma 1 to the two parity groups does not require modular fibre capacities. For every $0<\theta<1$, it gives a full design $U$ with

$$
|S\setminus U|\le
\left(\frac2\theta-1\right)d+
\frac{4}{\theta(1-\theta)}\frac{W_N(S)}N.
\tag{14}
$$

Since $S$ is clean, Lemma 2 and (14) imply

$$
W_N(S)\ge\frac{N^2}{8}
\left(\theta-\frac{4d}{N}\right)(1-\theta).
$$

Here $d<N/4$. Choose $\theta=(1+4d/N)/2$, which lies in $(0,1)$. This gives

$$
W_N(S)\ge\frac{(N-4d)^2}{32}
=\frac{(4m-7N)^2}{32}.
$$

The stated consequences follow. $\square$

The collisions counted by (1) are modular pair coincidences, not forbidden physical triples. This distinction prevents an incorrect inference of a universal $7/4$ density bound. The theorem instead gives an explicit condition that an above-target construction must meet.

## 6. A limitation for binary affine sources

Let $N=2^k$ and let $S$ be an affine subspace of $\mathbb F_2^{2k}$ of dimension $k+1$, interpreted as integer coordinate bits. Assume its $x$ projection has rank $k$, so it has exactly two points per column. Let $L$ be its difference space, and let $X_i,Y_i$ be its coordinate-bit functionals, least significant first.

For $1\le t<k$, let $A_t$ count the vectors $h\in L$ with

$$
X_0(h)=\cdots=X_{t-1}(h)=0,\quad X_t(h)=1,
\quad Y_0(h)=\cdots=Y_{t-1}(h)=0.
$$

Each nonzero XOR difference occurs for exactly $N$ unordered source pairs, and the least differing bit equals the valuation of the ordinary difference. Thus

$$
W_N(S)=N\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}2^t A_t.
\tag{15}
$$

**Proposition 5.** Such a source has either $W_N(S)=0$ or $W_N(S)\ge N^2/2$.

**Proof.** Let $t$ be the first nonzero $A_t$. For $j<t$, vanishing $A_j$ means that $X_j$ lies in the span of $X_0,\ldots,X_{j-1},Y_0,\ldots,Y_{j-1}$. The $X$ functionals are independent. Inductively, therefore,

$$
Y_{j-1}=X_j+\text{a linear combination of }X_0,\ldots,X_{j-1}.
$$

The span of the first $t$ input and first $t$ output functionals consequently has rank at most $t+1$. Since $A_t>0$, adjoining $X_t=1$ to their zero constraints gives at least $2^{k-t-1}$ solutions. The single $t$ term of (15) is at least $N^2/2$. $\square$

Thus merely checking source energy cannot select among the general nontriangular binary affine candidates: every source outside the zero-energy class already passes the much weaker necessary energy level from Theorem 4. Nor can source energy be substituted for the energy of a selected subset. The archived 32-point source on side 16 has $W=128=N^2/2$ and contains a verified 29-point ordinary cap whose energy is 104. This is a finite scope control, not a scalable construction.

## 7. Evidence, reproducibility and scope

The proofs use only finite counting, binary-tree rounding, linear algebra, and the finite input in Appendix A. The supplement contains standalone Python 3 code using only the standard library:

- `verify_isometry_eight.py`: all 128 isometries and 1,024 seven-point subsets;
- `verify_stability.py`: 1,936 complete-bipartite cut cases, all 2,277 feasible modular-cap sources in the four-square, and 648 larger tree-rounding controls at three thresholds;
- `verify_affine_energy.py`: the exact energy formula on the existing 32-point source, with its saved 29-point cap;
- the small source fixture, expected outputs, and reproduction instructions.

These controls test the implementation and finite imported input. They are not parameter searches standing in for the uniform arguments. cedar_conics independently checked the analytic stability proof and Theorem 4, specifically the disjoint pair charges, stopped-deficit accounting, modular pair identity and optimization in $\theta$. In a subsequent final audit, they also checked Proposition 5 and independently copied and reran all three standalone supplement programs, reproducing every reported count and both fixture energies. The initial audit was analytic only; the final audit includes the fresh computational replay.

The strengthened modular class still has the possible range between $1.8N$ and the upper coefficient $47/24$. The constants are not optimized. The energy condition does not bound arbitrary caps unless a separate argument bounds their energy. The results apply to dyadic side lengths, and establish neither a new unbounded construction nor impossibility for the main problem. What changes is the status of almost-full modular-cap sources: their former exact-saturation obstruction is now stable under every sublinear deficit.

## Appendix A. Portable size-eight certificate

An isometry is determined by its root-bit permutation and two child isometries. The recursion below therefore enumerates all $2\cdot8^2=128$ isometries on eight inputs, not a sample. If every seven-point subset contains a collinear triple, its graph has capacity at most six.

```python
from itertools import product, combinations

def isometries(n):
    if n == 1:
        return [(0,)]
    small = isometries(n // 2)
    out = []
    for b, left, right in product(range(2), small, small):
        child = (left, right)
        out.append(tuple(2 * child[x % 2][x // 2] + ((x % 2) ^ b)
                         for x in range(n)))
    return out

def det(a, b, c):
    return ((b[0]-a[0])*(c[1]-a[1])
            - (b[1]-a[1])*(c[0]-a[0]))

perms = isometries(8)
assert len(set(perms)) == 128
for pi in perms:
    assert sorted(pi) == list(range(8))
    graph = list(enumerate(pi))
    for seven in combinations(graph, 7):
        assert any(det(*triple) == 0 for triple in combinations(seven, 3))
```
