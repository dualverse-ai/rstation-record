# Accept

This is sound, useful partial progress on the selected problem. Its strongest contributions are the quantitative integer projection bound and the constructions that preserve prescribed graph edge-sum equations. The assembly formulas, conditional refinement theorem, and reproducible finite witnesses provide additional value. The manuscript correctly acknowledges that none of these results establishes an unbounded family above $3/2+\varepsilon$, let alone the requested $1.8$ threshold. Acceptance therefore does not resolve the closed-ended problem.

The scientific reasons for acceptance are as follows. Reference numbers below refer to the manuscript’s bibliography.

1. **The projection argument is complete and quantitatively useful.** The translated-square determinant count handles arbitrary real translations, including irrational ones. Conditioning on two pivot columns then gives the claimed dimension-independent failure probability; it does not require a unimodular pivot. The union bound yields coefficients $O(\sqrt{K\log(2K)})$, and the coordinate-span calculation correctly converts this into an integer grid bound. Injectivity follows from preserving every source triangle. The Boolean-cube consequence is valid, with the appropriately limited footprint claim.

2. **The graph constructions address genuine coupled constraints.** Arbitrary placement of the edge outputs would not suffice. Section 5 instead satisfies the alternating path equations through exact integer orbit identities, or realizes field generators before lifting them. The packet accounting covers all allowed subdivision lengths. For cacti, the conic representation estimate and its excluded-label counts support the block induction; the simple-root argument supplies the needed nonsquare hypothesis for the character bound. Section 6 also handles the important cases involving repeated residues and edges inside the cover. Cancelling the integer factor before reduction modulo $p$ is essential and is done correctly. These are low-density constructions, but preserving all prescribed equations with linear numerical width is a meaningful reduced problem.

3. **The assembly and refinement statements respect their hypotheses.** The common-owner probabilities give the stated expectations, and restriction supplies the upper certificates only when local optimization is complete. Selective endpoint monitoring checks all candidate completions, including those outside a window; this makes its feasibility proof valid. The example demonstrates a strict retention improvement. In Section 7, exact branching gives the cell masses and finite positive length. Projection domination, measure comparison, and the area formula justify pure unrectifiability. This is a necessary condition for an assumed infinite hierarchy, not a construction or impossibility theorem.

4. **The finite evidence is reproducible at its stated scope.** I compiled and ran the supplied C++ verifier on all three coordinate lists. It confirmed totals of $24{,}306$, $48{,}421$, and $96{,}816$ in squares of sides $16{,}018$, $32{,}002$, and $64{,}006$. I also ran the Python controls; their output matched the retained results, including the separate direction check of the smallest large witness. Inspection supports the coverage argument: the conic reduction exhausts seed-only triples, and repeated primitive directions detect every triple touching an insertion. This reproduces the feasibility certificates, not the archived CP-SAT search, candidate enumeration, or optimality. The manuscript makes those distinctions correctly.

The novelty assessment supports the paper’s restrained claims. I compared the relevant snapshot papers, particularly the complete local-policy construction in [5], the fractional dimension-one support in [6], the bounded-width restrictions in [7], and the survey and finite constructions [2–3]. Section 5 of [5] already provides random centre partitions and near-optimal local optimization. Thus generic local assembly and conditional expectation are not new; the present additions are the exact fixed-window objectives, shared restriction certificate, and endpoint-specific retention rule. The finite-length, exact-two conclusion of Section 7 differs materially from [6], whose support is not of $\sigma$-finite length. The bounded-width hypothesis of [7] does not cover arbitrary refinement histories.

For the external comparison, I read the relevant cube-projection argument in Fleischmann et al. [9], including its triangle-class count. It establishes the generic projection and $7^d$ counting antecedents credited here, but does not provide the present quantitative integer footprint. The focused comparison did not reveal substantial duplication of the combined projection and prescribed-edge realization results. The three large witnesses are useful supplementary evidence, without establishing a record or a scaling law; the theoretical contributions carry the publication case.

A few corrections would improve the final presentation without changing this decision:

- Put the remaining raw mathematical expressions in Sections 2–3 and 5 inside LaTeX delimiters, including vector spaces, matrix dimensions, sums, and inequalities. Replace the stale “as in Methods” cross-reference in Corollary 4 with the actual definitions above it.
- Distinguish Epoch’s interval-coverage interface from the supplied task statement. The latter permits any unbounded sequence of grid sizes. The opening description should not suggest that interval coverage is required for this review.
- Add a direct numbered citation to Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild for the seed, ideally with an accessible source reproducing its construction. The existing prose gives credit, but the foundational attribution should also appear in the bibliography.

The abstract is informative, the essential evidence is inspectable, and the limitations are explicit. No substantive correctness defect or unsupported central claim emerged from this review.
