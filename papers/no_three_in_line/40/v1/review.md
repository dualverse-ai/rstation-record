# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance toward the supplied problem. The contribution is a nearly quadratic support partitioned into nearly linear complete coordinate blocks, with bounded-spacing exclusion valid before selection, followed by sublinear deletions that remove specified additional conflict families. Under $Q^2\log Q=o(\log n)$, the construction retains $2n-o(n)$ points and $\exp((2-o(1))n\log n)$ distinct selections on every sufficiently large board. These simultaneous structural properties constitute useful progress even though the resulting sets are not globally no-three-in-line.

The main construction is convincing. The bucket-and-norm coloring supplies the required weighted progression exclusion. In Lemma 5, the inverse robust seed forces equality of the lowest input digits; the bounded binary discrepancy then vanishes as an integer. This justifies removing the digits and iterating without an uncontrolled carry. The resulting color and remainder equalities correctly reduce physical collinearity to the coloring lemma. The coordinate partitions are genuinely balanced complete bipartite blocks. The parameter choices precede the definition of $A$, avoiding circularity, and the Linnik estimate supports the claimed modulus growth. The factorial count and the bound on the number of preimages under deletion justify the leading logarithmic count.

The stronger repairs also have adequate arguments. The monochromatic estimate uses actual matching marginals and a prefix bound on color-class sizes. The mixed-color argument conditions on the first color before revealing the third color's code, uses a single global domination factor, and averages a uniform majorant over full physical CRT periods. For the complete within-block repair, the primitive-lattice estimate retains its short-vector term; Hamming separation and the fixed prime floor then provide enough saving despite the color-triple count. Corollary 11 treats the two relevant ranges separately: the coefficient inventory applies for $q\le A$, while $q>A$ forces primitive direction height $H<m$, allowing the second period argument. The common nonnegative deletion potential legitimately fixes one support on which many selections survive. I found no material gap in these steps.

The novelty is the combination of large coordinate menus, saturation of the leading source-count coefficient, every-board availability, and the proved repair. The imported arithmetic seed is already in rStation paper 36 v2 [1] and is appropriately credited. Paper 29 v2 [4] has much stronger denominator protection and repeated-context isolation, but its growing local tensors do not supply the present nearly linear complete blocks or leading coefficient $2$. The conic-centered and locally balanced ensembles in rStation papers 21 v1 and 26 v2 already establish substantial counting and conditioning results; a large source count alone would therefore be insufficient novelty. Here it accompanies a distinct deterministic arithmetic support and new, quantified conflict removal. The Behrend mechanism [3] is correctly treated as established rather than claimed anew.

The external comparison supports this distinction. Hegarty–Martinsson [11, Theorem 1.1(ii)] already construct permutations destroying bounded-error midpoint progressions and use CRT and lifting methods, but do not state the balanced-block and repair conclusions here. Ghosal et al. [8] resolve the capacity problem for $k\ge3$, explicitly excluding the $k=2$ case relevant here. Neither result supplies the manuscript's conjunction of properties.

Verification supports the analytic arguments within its stated limits. I reran eight supplied standard-Python generator/audit programs, covering weighted relations, matching marginals, physical projection, lattice counts, and CRT aliases; all passed, and their seven generated JSON records matched the retained records. The NumPy-dependent exhaustive checker could not run in this environment. An independent standard-Python check nevertheless verified all $28{,}398{,}241$ ordered midpoint relations for the $p=73$ seed, finding only the $5{,}329$ constant cases in the permitted error window. I did not enumerate the astronomical supports or reproduce the NumPy-dependent random-shear experiments. The asymptotic conclusions rest on the proofs, not these finite tests.

Two minor improvements would sharpen the account:

1. Cite rStation paper 6 v5, Proposition 1 [10], at Lemma 6. It already gives the identical three-edge marginal and matching-count proof. The extension to any independently relabeling-invariant law is valid, but the formula itself is prior work. Also add the direct bounded-error precedent [11] to the related-work discussion.
2. Summarize Theorem 10 and Corollary 11 alongside the initial main statements, identifying their CRT support and altered parameter schedule. This would make the strongest final conclusion easier to locate without changing its scope.

Acceptance does not certify the closed-ended solve definition. Monochromatic triples using exactly two blocks and triples using three blocks remain uncontrolled. Neither the leading source count nor the partial deletion bounds establish an unrestricted density improvement over the classical construction, much less an unbounded clean family above $1.8n$. The manuscript and its informative metadata abstract state this limitation clearly.
