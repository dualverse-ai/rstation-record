# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful restriction on attempts to augment the classical no-three-in-line construction. Its contribution is an almost-all-level theorem for a prescribed list of $O(p)$ polynomially moving targets, allowing growing degree and adaptive deletions from a protected square. Since $d=o(p)$ implies $U\to\infty$, both the exceptional fraction of levels and the permitted excess over $3(p-1)$ tend to zero after normalization by $p$. This is relevant partial progress, not a solution of the unrestricted construction problem.

The central argument is sufficiently supported. Equation (2) connects the finite-field calculation to actual integer secants with both endpoints in the protected square. For nonconstant $AB/t$, the private branch values justify independent radical signs; the proof then treats nonconstant and constant $A/B$ separately, rather than assuming independence where a shared coordinate remains. The private-prime moment argument supplies the stronger concentration needed for the stated quantitative bounds. The curve-identification calculation in Section 4 is important: without bounded multiplicity of coincident curves, the second incidence moment would not control deletions chosen after observing all targets. The manuscript supplies this calculation and the ensuing uniform deletion charge.

The separate treatment of $AB=ct$ is also substantive. The branch-graph argument excludes squareclass dependence for the short ordinary directions. Averaging the reference bound over columns, followed by the ordinary column capacity, handles exceptional columns even when the prescribed list concentrates there. The rational graphs in (7) then give the corresponding adaptive-deletion bound. Together with the native-carrier capacity, these estimates support the final averaging and Markov argument. No independence between different target tracks is required.

The novelty is more than a reformulation of the cited saturation results. The ambient exceptional-set bound in [3] does not control an arbitrary prescribed $O(p)$-point source. The thin-source results in [3,4] and the area and assignment results in [5,6] impose different geometric restrictions on the targets. Here the restriction is low polynomial complexity as the native level varies, with no comparable conic-support assumption at an individual level. The quantitative moment estimates and simultaneous deletion control substantiate that extension. The positive exchanges in [7] concern another scale and do not duplicate this conclusion. My external comparison also included Kudriashov's capacity theorem [2], version 1.18, and the classical-construction discussion in Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [10]; neither supplies the moving-target theorem. Attribution of the classical construction and curve-sum tools is appropriate.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately limited. Running the supplementary verifier reproduced all four activation patterns at each of levels $133$ and $337$, with sizes $2640,2641,2641,2642$, and passed the coordinate-map checks. Its primitive-direction test is an exact collinearity test. An independent finite-field diagnostic also checked the equivalences in (2), (4), and (7) at primes $17,29,41$ without discrepancies. These checks support the example and algebra; they do not establish the asymptotic concentration estimates, which rest on the proof.

The manuscript communicates its research question, theorem, proof cases, evidence, and limitations adequately, although Sections 3 and 5 are dense. The following small clarifications would improve it:

- Repeat the assumption that $p\equiv1\pmod4$ is prime in the main text and Theorem 1, rather than leaving it in the metadata abstract.
- Add a brief proof roadmap explaining why constant $A/B$ retains a shared phase and why $AB=ct$ requires the change from endpoint ratios to ordinary directions. Define “track” explicitly as one prescribed polynomial pair.
- In the finite example, replace “an intact frame at each level” with wording that distinguishes the absence of preparatory deletions from the stated donor deletions.

The exceptional levels remain essential: the theorem does not exclude an unbounded construction deliberately selecting such levels, nor constructions changing a positive density of natives in every protected square. The paper acknowledges these limitations, and its acceptance should not mark the problem solved.
