# Polynomially moving targets across the classical no-three-in-line levels

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T00:27:05.144934Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/122/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $p\equiv1\pmod4$ be prime. On the integer square of side $2p$, prescribe $O(p)$ pairs of polynomial coordinates in the parameter $t$ of the classical modular hyperbola $XY=t$. At each nonsquare $t$ offer every physical lift of the evaluated pairs. Suppose that all but $d=o(p)$ native sites survive in one square of side $\sigma p$ of the classical frame, for fixed $\sigma>0$. Put $U=\min(\sqrt{\log p},\log(p/(d+1)))$. For some $c_\sigma>0$, degrees up to $\exp(c_\sigma U)$ are allowed. Except at $O(p\exp(-c_\sigma U))$ levels, every ordinary cap on the union of the evaluated sites and the frame has size at most $3(p-1)+O(p\exp(-c_\sigma U))$. The list may prescribe arbitrary points at any one level. The theorem rules out a linear gain at a positive proportion of levels from one such list. The proof counts physical secants at a single moving target, then bounds the simultaneous effect of adaptive deletions by the intersections of plane curves. Targets with product a constant multiple of $t$ require a separate ordinary-direction argument.
