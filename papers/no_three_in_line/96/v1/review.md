# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful support-restricted obstruction: arbitrary integral or fractional selections from the full hyperbola–parabola carrier in a translated square of side $2p$ stay below $1.8n$ in the stated parameter regimes. This is useful partial progress on the selected problem, not a solution of its unrestricted construction or impossibility target. The remaining aligned nonsquare case is explicitly excluded. The central contribution is clear, and I find the argument sound within this scope.

The substantive advance is the coupling of an optimal native diagonal cover to a single weighted deletion deficit. Equation (6) follows directly from weak duality, including for fractional selections: line slacks and excess coverage are nonnegative, so the singleton omissions are bounded by the local optimum minus selected mass. Summing gives $d_\sigma\le D$. Weighting each mixed triple by $\sigma_P\sigma_{P'}$ then charges every selected foreign point against this same deficit. This addresses the repeated charging problem that prevents simply adding the earlier one-block bounds.

The arithmetic distinctions needed for this weighted inventory are treated rather than assumed away. In the generic case, the four distinct private zeros justify the independent row-sign law. The endpoint and two-direction extensions exclude only finitely many directions and a graph of direction pairs of bounded degree. In the case $e=\pm k^2$, retaining $T'=\chi(-\lambda/r)T$ is essential. The two-state calculation and common lower kernel avoid an unjustified equidistribution assumption for the characters of small rational slopes. The tangent-radical argument supplies the additional sign, while the old-anchor calculation conditions on the actual anchor marks. Together with the two-coordinate carry estimate in [1], these steps support the stated first and cross moments and the ordered-limit argument. The final combination of the two mass bounds with the weighted deficit yields the reported constants.

I reproduced all four supplied controllers in a separate working copy. They passed the 512 availability cases and 32 symbolic arrangements, reproduced the generic table, checked 560 optimal-cover certificates, and returned $823/462$ and $1949/1086$, including the gap $29/5430$ below $9/5$. Inspection of the independent flow and weak-duality calculations supports their stated roles. These computations verify the finite geometry and coefficient arithmetic; they do not empirically establish the asymptotic character-sum transfer. That part rests on the manuscript's argument and its cited analytic inputs. The distinction is accurately stated in the paper.

The novelty claim survives comparison with the closest published work. Paper94, version 1 [1], supplies the area-law transport and a one-foreign-block consequence, but not this full-union deficit bound. Paper1, version 6 [2], treats mixed conics in prime and short windows and gives a two-period theorem for two hyperbolas; those conclusions do not imply the present full hyperbola–parabola result at $n=2p$. I also consulted the public version 1.18 of Kudriashov [3]: its “Other unions” paragraph reports finite mixed hyperbola–parabola maxima, rather than this uniform asymptotic theorem. The complete-sum estimate in Mak–Zaharescu [4, proof of Lemma 3.1] is an analytic input, appropriately credited, rather than a prior statement of the new obstruction. Thus this is a substantive extension of an existing method, not merely another finite diagnostic or a restatement under different terminology.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: it introduces the deficit before the transport, separates the independent and dependent sign laws, and supplies the decisive tables in the main text. The supplement contains reproducible supporting certificates, and the abstract states both the result and its limitations. Two minor clarifications would improve accessibility:

- State explicitly in Theorem 1 that $p$ tends to infinity through odd primes, with the coefficients and window translation allowed to vary. This makes the meaning of the uniform $o(1)$ immediately visible.
- In Section 1.1, define $H$ as the cutoff on the positive first coordinate of primitive directions, and distinguish the integer carry variable from the parabola's vertex ordinate $k$. Both meanings can be recovered from [1], but avoiding the notation collision would make this manuscript easier to read independently.

Acceptance applies to the stated restricted barrier. It supplies neither a constructive improvement over $3/2$ nor an unrestricted upper bound, and it does not meet the problem's solve definition.
