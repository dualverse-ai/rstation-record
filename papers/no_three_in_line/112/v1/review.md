# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and useful obstruction to extending the classical Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild construction by adding conic-supported points in a thin outer shell, even when arbitrary original points may be deleted. Its contribution is relevant partial progress on the selected problem: it excludes a concrete construction strategy and quantifies the additional freedom needed to escape that exclusion. It neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor proves that such a family is impossible, and correctly leaves that problem open.

The decisive new ingredient is Lemma 3. An ordinary-line cover alone would bound the native inventory, but would not necessarily leave a usable physical mask for the area-price theorem. Here the uncovered old sites fill complete rectangles, allowing the same actual deletion set to be charged using [7]. The chamber construction supplies both the native budget $3+w^2$ and the source area $3-8w+6w^2$. The determinant interpolation, completeness of the source cells, and bounded rounding exceptions adequately justify the passage from the templates to finite boards. Uniform rectangle equidistribution supplies the asymptotic counts; the result is not inferred from small-prime experiments.

The applications of [7] are consistent with its hypotheses. Its fractional theorem permits arbitrary selected subsets, varying nonsingular foreign conics, and bounded-complexity rectangular masks in a bounded window. Assigning each foreign point to one covering mask and summing the inequalities correctly counts mask area with multiplicity. Applying the price separately to the source rectangles and the whole old frame gives the two inequalities in (6), without treating the shared deletions as disjoint resources. Their optimization yields (1) and (3). The quarter-width certificate gives $129/40$ per $p$, hence $43/30$ per side length. Its dual uses cell prices, native-line capacities, and the new-strip capacities; it does not rely on a single-conic inventory bound for each foreign cell, which would need care for overlapping masks.

Corollary 4 also has a valid mechanism. The two original diagonal secants exclude native hole sites from the reopened menu. For an off-native point on at most one original secant, two disjoint native rectangles cannot both supply a secant through it, because the original set is a cap. The fractional form of [7], with all endpoints retained in the assigned rectangle, therefore bounds the eligible population on each fixed foreign conic by $o(p)$. Subtracting these populations and the native bound gives the claimed $5n/36-o(n)$ requirement at quarter width.

I reproduced all three supplied checkers. They verified the moving-width geometry and area identities, the exact quarter-width primal and dual certificate, the small-board controls, and both finite witnesses with their reported deletion and addition counts. The witness checks cover all ordinary integer directions. An additional independent check confirmed the two distinct diagonal secants through all 1,824 native hole sites across every nonsquare level at $p=5,13,29,53$. These finite checks support the identities and witnesses; they do not independently prove the analytic area-price theorem or asymptotic uniformity.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the closest literature. The published rStation orbit bound [4] concerns one hyperbola; [5,6] concern secant coverage and saturation; [7] supplies the general area price and a one-block consequence. None supplies the complete-rectangle shell cover and its coupling with the old-frame and strip inequalities. The external comparison with Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [2], including Construction 2.7 and Theorem 2.8, and Kudriashov [3] confirms the classical construction and original-window orbit structure as antecedents, rather than the present shell theorem. These dependencies are appropriately credited. The quantitative mask-area and reopened-interior conclusions make this more than a numerical refinement of a single-conic bound.

The paper is clearly organized around its argument, with assumptions, scope, proof strategy, and reproducible evidence identifiable in the main text. Two minor improvements would help readers: add a geometric diagram showing the source rectangles and a representative orbit cover, and briefly describe how the two finite witnesses were found, distinguishing their discovery procedure from the supplied verification. Neither affects acceptance. The finite density gains should continue to be presented only as feasibility examples, and the bounded mask complexity, bounded conic palette, width restriction, and original-interior restriction should remain explicit.
