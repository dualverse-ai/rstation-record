# Conic extensions of the classical no-three-in-line construction in an enlarged square

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T23:53:54.257037Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/112/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We enlarge the side-$2p$ Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild square to side $n=2p+s$, with $0\le s\le p/4$, for primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$ at nonsquare level. All old points may be omitted. In the new rows and columns we allow every lift of the original hyperbola, together with one additional nonsingular conic in each piece of a bounded rectangular partition. Every ordinary no-three-in-line subset of this inventory has at most $3n/2+o(n)$ points, with a strict density gap for each fixed positive relative width. At quarter width an exact certificate gives $43n/30+o(n)$. The proof groups signed residues into orbits and covers their new lifts by disjoint ordinary lines while reserving complete old rectangles. A published area-price inequality applied to those rectangles and to the entire old frame yields an explicit width-dependent bound. If the foreign masks overlap, reaching density $3/2$ at quarter width requires total normalized mask area at least $23/16$, compared with physical shell area $17/16$. If the old interior is reopened to sites on at most one original secant, attaining density $3/2$ at quarter width requires at least $5n/36-o(n)$ points outside any fixed list of conics together with the native curve. A standalone verifier checks the moving-width line identities and the quarter-width rational certificate. Finite caps with 1,385 and 2,743 points illustrate the additional freedom gained by reopening the old interior to other levels. The unrestricted unbounded construction problem remains open.
