# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful construction barrier: for the specified native conics and rectangular masks, the common fractional deletion price is $m\le (2B/A)d+o(p)$. This controls arbitrary selections made after observing the carrier, including deletions of positive density. The exterior-block consequence and the extension from literal opposite parabolas to arbitrary quadratic-graph pairs are substantive advances. They are relevant to the selected problem because they exclude natural ways of augmenting the classical construction. They do not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$, or prove that such a family is impossible; the problem remains unsolved.

The central argument is convincing at its stated asymptotic scope. The symmetric triple inventory correctly gives target load approximately $A/2$ and endpoint load at most $B$, with the different incidence normalizations accounted for. Crucially, equation (27) charges every selected foreign point against one common deletion set. Lemma 6 retains both integer carry coordinates, and its dyadic gcd estimate is summable. The circle extension supplies the corresponding Gaussian-ideal calculation, including the even-norm correction. These steps support the claimed coefficient rather than merely showing that most foreign points are blocked when few native points are deleted.

The arithmetic transfer addresses the main dependence issues. The tangent geometry gives nontrivial quadratic covers outside boundedly many directions; squareclass collisions are retained through a bounded-degree covariance estimate, so the angular distribution is not lost by thinning. The root-flip argument and bounded pole orders justify the complete character-sum input. For hyperbolas and circles, the ordered limits in $p,H,\delta$ are adequate. The parabola argument separately retains the deterministic sum phase, averages it jointly with the direction through (26e)–(26g), and supplies a quantitative transfer for the growing medium-prime family. Thus it does not rely on a fixed-family estimate where a growing-family estimate is needed. The final Cauchy–Schwarz step applies to arbitrary fractional selections. The disjoint native charge in Corollary 2 and the two-role summation in Corollary 3 then give the stated bounds.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Paper [7] gives an $\varepsilon p+C_\varepsilon d$ saturation bound, rather than the explicit area price. The supplied version 3 of paper [6] additionally proves rectangle-local aligned saturation and treats an explicit transverse conic, but still does not supply the present coefficient for arbitrary nonsingular foreign conics. Paper [3], version 6, already establishes the opposite-parabola $4p/3$ result and much of the cover/carry framework; the submission appropriately identifies that special case as prior work. The new weighted-mask accumulation and broader graph-pair conclusion are distinguishable from it. Paper [16]'s bounded-height obstruction is compatible with the limits used here. External comparison with Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [2] and Kudriashov [5] confirms that their classical construction, single-carrier certificates, and finite union experiments do not establish this area law. The cited complete-sum input in Mak–Zaharescu [12, proof of Lemma 3.1, equation (3.1)] is also consistent with the use made of it.

I reproduced all four supplied Python controls in separate working copies. They passed with the reported case counts, including the weighted covariance, circle compatibility, and parabola Ramanujan checks. These are finite identity checks, not independent verification of the asymptotic estimates or coefficient uniformity. The manuscript correctly makes that distinction; acceptance rests on the analytic argument, with the controls supporting its arithmetic normalization.

Clarity is adequate for a specialist reader. The main result, proof strategy, applications, and limitations are identifiable, and the paper is organized around the argument rather than experimental chronology. The following minor improvements would make it easier to use:

- Update reference [6] and the cited-version metadata to acknowledge the available version 3, especially its Theorem 9 and shared-phase covariance argument. Explain explicitly how the present all-primitive-direction weighted estimate differs from that prime-group rectangle argument.
- Give displayed definitions of $\mathcal T_{r,\theta}$ and $\mathcal O_{r,\theta}$ in Section 7.1. Their verbal definitions are interpretable, but explicit formulas would make the normalization in (26c) easier to audit.
- A schematic of the close native endpoint pair and the four physical blocks would help readers connect the transport to Corollary 2. The existing formulas contain the necessary information, so this is an expository improvement rather than missing evidence.

The restrictions on native quadratic shapes, mask complexity, bounded physical windows, and foreign mask area are substantive and are stated clearly. No extension to general caps or a constructive improvement above $3/2$ should be inferred.
