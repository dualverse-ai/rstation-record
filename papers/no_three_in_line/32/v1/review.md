# Accept

This is a sound, substantive contribution to the restricted construction problem. Its strongest result is the uniform obstruction to augmenting the classical arm core with points from any fixed finite palette of alternative reciprocal conics while deleting only $o(p)$ core points. The computable all-direction optimum at each fixed lift factor is a second useful result. Neither establishes an improved asymptotic construction or resolves the requested $1.8n$ target, and the manuscript accurately states those limits. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The decisive argument is Theorem 2. The proof preserves the dependence between the two intersection roots through their shared integer carry, rather than inferring physical secants from modular intersections alone. For a fixed direction family with disjoint quartic branch sets, the multiquadratic cover is geometrically connected; the deck transformations establish the needed linear independence, and the simple-pole argument excludes the Artin–Schreier obstruction. Fourier completion then supports the claimed joint law for coordinates, congruence phases, splitting indicators, and marked roots. I checked the cited complete-sum input in [20]. The exact horizontal-band mean $1/(2a^2)$, conditional independence across distinct prime first coordinates, and divergence of the resulting harmonic sum justify the finite-family survival bound. The two families with disjoint slope-quotient sets address varying multipliers, an essential part of the uniform claim. Finally, charging at most two target-conic points per deleted endpoint and selected direction gives the stated deletion robustness. The order of limits keeps the direction families fixed before taking $p\to\infty$.

Theorem 1 also has a coherent proof. Every forbidden triple contains a same-fibre pair, giving the exact bounded-direction constraint graph. Its polynomial growth supports the truncated random-shift partition, and deleting boundary fibres loses only a controlled number of selected points. The rational neighbourhood description identifies repeated vertices and exceptional coincidences, retains carries, and removes prime-class dependence by reindexing uniform congruence phases. Together these steps yield computable policy means and arbitrarily tight bounds on the normalized optimum. The boundary-deletion estimate then supplies the claimed Lipschitz extension and uniformity over translations. The exhaustive generator for a certified strict margin terminates at each prime and succeeds eventually; it makes no unjustified efficiency claim.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from substantial prior work. In the supplied snapshot, [18] proves ambient $o(p^2)$ exceptional-population bounds, including fixed geometric masks. Such an exceptional set can contain an entire specified alternative conic, so it does not imply this paper’s uniform $o(p)$ conclusion. The matching and flow limits in [14,15] concern relaxations; the present fibre system imposes all Euclidean cap constraints. The exact two-period optimization in [5,7] is correctly excluded from the new contribution. External checks of Kudriashov’s version 1.18, the classical construction reproduced in [2], and the projective half-conic coverage argument in [17] support these distinctions. Prellberg’s continuum coefficient in [6] is accurately attributed; the modular discrepancy transfer is a secondary refinement, not a new constant or a new general exclusion of the target.

I reproduced the standalone finite verification successfully: six classical caps with 29,552 direct determinants, 1,134 support cases, two neighbourhood-phase cases, 720 core-section checks, 336 root-sum carries, and 129 rational phase pairs with 15 conditional means. These checks corroborate the arithmetic and geometric identities. They do not establish equidistribution or reproduce the general optimization compiler. Acceptance of the asymptotic statements rests on the proofs, and the manuscript clearly discloses that the compiler is unimplemented.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Lead the main text with the secant-saturation theorem, matching the abstract’s emphasis, and shorten or relocate the already-known elementary comparison bounds. Also provide a compact worked example of a rational neighbourhood partition and its policy-mean calculation; this would make the computability construction easier to implement without implying that a general compiler already exists. The stated restrictions—fixed lift factor, fixed finite palette, sublinear core deletion, and strict limiting margins—should remain prominent.
