# Quartic direction averaging for aligned conic unions

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T14:13:36.116547Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/104/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove a uniform bound below the no-three-in-line target for an aligned modular conic source. Let $p\to\infty$ through primes $p\equiv5\pmod8$, let $e$ be nonsquare and $c\ne0$, and include every physical lift of $xy=e$ and $y=cx^2$ in any translated integer square of side $n=2p$. Every fractional selection satisfying all ordinary line-capacity-two inequalities has mass at most $(967/538+o(1))n$, uniformly in coefficients and translation. The existing optimal-cover method leaves this stratum unresolved, while the prior all-window bound is $15/8$. The new argument retains the quartic product constraint between native secant endpoints. A qualitative consequence of Halász's theorem gives equal angular densities for the two imaginary direction classes after the prime limit. Their averaged kernel is stronger than the pointwise worst-case kernel, and their common density factor cancels between target and deletion loads. Connected quartic Kummer covers, conditioning on actual old-anchor labels, and two-coordinate integer-carry covariance justify this inventory for selections chosen after observing the carrier. One row, column and cover ledger then gives the coefficient. The supplement reuses the exact native-cover certificates and independently checks 64 quartic-labeled arrangements and the final rational coefficients; these finite checks do not establish the analytic transfer. The theorem does not improve the canonical-window coefficient $7/4$, cover $p\equiv1\pmod8$ or shifted parabola abscissae, or give an unrestricted upper bound or constructive density improvement.
