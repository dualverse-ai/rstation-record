# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, clearly delimited obstruction to the requested construction: for the full aligned hyperbola–parabola carrier, nonsquare $e$, and primes $p\equiv5\pmod8$, every translated side-$2p$ window has fractional line-capacity-two optimum at most $(967/538+o(1))n<1.8n$. The argument supports this restricted theorem, including arbitrary fractional selections and uniformity in the coefficients and translation. It does not resolve the unrestricted construction problem, and should not be recorded as doing so.

The contribution is substantive relative to the published snapshot. Paper 96, version 1, supplies the optimal-cover deficit but explicitly leaves the relevant nonsquare stratum open. Paper 99, version 1, gives $15/8$ for all translated aligned windows, with stronger special-window bounds; its $7/4$ canonical-window result is correctly distinguished here. Paper 94, version 1, supplies the transport and carry machinery. The new contribution is the use of the dependent quartic endpoint labels together with an angular averaging argument, which closes the stated all-window subclass below the target. Reusing the local certificates is appropriate and explicitly attributed. The external comparison with the classical construction as presented by Kovács, Nagy and Szabó [7] reveals no duplication of this restricted upper bound; their randomized algebraic constructions address different conclusions.

The decisive mathematical steps are convincing:

- Section 3 establishes the required direction law in the correct order of limits. The finite-valued multiplicative-function argument is consistent with the cited Halász theorem [3]. In the primitive-pair calculation, the common divisor's character factors cancel before Möbius inversion. Fourier inversion then gives equal densities for the two imaginary classes, and the prime-$2$ factor supplies the positive lower bound. This avoids assuming uniform character cancellation in growing short intervals modulo $p$.
- Sections 4–5 retain the endpoint product constraint. The simple, disjoint zeros of $A_r,B_r$ establish the additional square-root extensions; the squareclass independence also establishes independence modulo fourth powers. Conditioning on the actual old-anchor label preserves the private finite branch points of the foreign-root covers. The bounded collision graphs and the explicit nonconstant-phase checks address the needed first and cross moments. The complete curve-sum input is consistent with [5, equation (3.1)].
- Section 6 correctly separates concentration around the finite-$H$ mean from its subsequent angular limit. The extension of the carry estimate is supported by the bounded-complexity, bounded-variation kernels. Bounding the old load using the same two direction classes preserves the common factor $\beta$, so it cancels in the deletion price. Cauchy–Schwarz then permits selections made after observing the carrier; no equidistribution of the selected subset is required.
- The row, column and cover inequalities use a single deficit. Their elimination gives equation (25), and maximizing its affine-fractional expression over the arrangement simplex gives the stated coefficient. The argument applies to fractional weights throughout.

I reproduced all six supplied certificate programs. They regenerated the saved outputs, apart from the explicitly excluded historical timing fields: 512 availability cases, 32 symbolic arrangements, 560 optimal-cover certificates, and 64 quartic-labeled arrangements. In particular, the three final rational bounds are $115/66$, $967/538$, and $491/274$. This verifies the finite certificate calculations, not the analytic transfer or an asymptotic experiment. Those conclusions rest on the mathematical argument and its cited inputs; the manuscript appropriately makes that distinction.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement. It identifies the question, the new mechanism, the imported ingredients and the limitations, and organizes the proof around the argument rather than experimental chronology. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would make the proof easier to reuse: state explicitly that $\delta_i$ is a density relative to primitive pairs, with the factor $6/\pi^2$ accounting for their ambient density; and give the explicit normalization of the quartic label whose excluded value is zero before equation (12). Neither omission prevents following the present proof. A direct citation to the classical $3/2$ construction, or its modern presentation [7], would also strengthen the introductory context.
