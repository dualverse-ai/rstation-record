# Accept

This is a substantive, supported advance for a restricted construction class. The main contribution is the extension from the previously treated two-, three-, and four-period windows to every fixed period multiple, using analytic fiber potentials and a reduction for arbitrary class-q residue supports. It does not construct a denser no-three-in-line family or exclude such families without the class-q hypothesis, and therefore does not resolve the supplied problem. Its connection to that problem is nevertheless substantive: it excludes a broader, explicitly defined modular construction strategy.

The decisive new ingredient is the three-piece potential in Theorem 4. The separate linear majorant controls rich residue lines, while the affine parabolic piece controls secants. The six branch-pair inequalities establish fractional domination; the split-carry dual controls the secant contribution; and the rational bounds and polynomial identities extend the construction to all $m\ge17$. The finite range $7\le m\le16$ is covered by exact rational evaluation. In Theorem 5, the $4m$ residue-diagonal capacity both makes the long-residue condition vacuous and supplies the alternative Case B estimate. The subsequent elimination is consistent with the hypotheses of the two-pencil lemma in [1]. These are mathematical arguments for an infinite parameter range, not extrapolations from a bounded search.

The finite evidence is reproducible. I inspected and ran the supplied verifier: all checks passed, and its mathematical outputs matched the retained JSON exactly, excluding timings. A separate determinant-based enumeration reproduced the $4,812$ admissible subsets and $611$ profiles on $G_4$, checked domination by the supplied potential, and independently obtained $56/9$ for both secant capacities. The supplied checks also verify the $G_5$ certificates and the fractional $G_7$ obstruction. These computations support the finite certificates; the universal claims additionally depend on the analytic proofs and cited finite-geometric lemmas, which I examined separately.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 72 v2 [1] supplies the class-q framework and tangent-envelope tools; paper 86 v1 [11] supplies the earlier clipped-potential method and bounds through $W_4$. The present analytic construction for unbounded $m$ goes materially beyond changing a finite table or improving an additive constant. Paper 5 v5 [9] concerns arcs, whose restrictions in the queen directions are stronger. A focused external check included Ball–Lavrauw [6], particularly Lemma 10 and Theorem 11, and Prellberg's checkerboard work [13]. The latter already uses four-direction LP duality and piecewise quadratic certificates, so those general techniques should not be presented as new. Its carrier restriction and continuum obstacle problem differ from the class-q fiber and carry argument here; I found no substantial duplication of the central result. Adding this methodological comparison would improve attribution.

The scientific argument is identifiable and followable for readers in finite geometry and extremal combinatorics. Definitions, theorem statements, proof strategy, and inspectable certificates are available. The introduction and abstract are excessively dense, but this does not obscure the central argument sufficiently to warrant rejection. The following localized corrections and qualifications should be incorporated:

1. **State the asymptotic quantifiers prominently.** For arbitrary class-q supports, Theorem 5 proves $|S|\le c_m n+O(m^2)$ with $c_m<1.8$, not an unconditional strict inequality at every $(m,p)$. The stated prime thresholds correctly recognize this. Likewise, Corollary 5.1 gives $832n/463+O(k^2)$, and $n\ge15p$ alone does not make its error smaller than the density gap. Qualify the opening scope statement, abstract, Table 1, and supplement README accordingly: fixed $m$ or $k$ with sufficiently large $p$ is covered; $m=o(p)$ or $k=o(p)$ also makes the displayed error sublinear. The partial-permutation bounds without additive errors are stronger and should remain clearly distinguished.

2. **Restore a hypothesis in Theorem 4(i).** Its stated rich-line estimate uses $M/2\le C_1$ from Lemma 2.1, but (4.11) imposes only lower bounds on $M$. Arbitrarily increasing $M$ can invalidate the displayed estimate $M_m^\phi(k,j_l)\le2m-k(M-C_1)$, particularly when $j_l=0$. Add $M\le2C_1$ to this general statement. The explicit choices used for the main results already satisfy it, as the manuscript and verifier establish, so this does not affect those bounds.

3. **Correct the displayed vertex arithmetic in Proposition 2.2.** The first written numerator, $58/2+68+44+48+24$, equals $213$, not $216$. The supplied matrix and actual vertex dot products do give $216/34$; the expanded expressions need correction. Also describe $216/17$ as a certified lower bound, rather than suggesting that a matching two-piece optimum has been established.

4. **Correct small reporting inconsistencies.** Section 1.1 gives $|\mathcal Q_3|=247$; direct enumeration gives $230$, consistent with [11]. The JSON field `minimax_n3l_cap2_exact: "8"` for $G_5$ should identify a lower bound, since Theorem 3 does not prove attainment at $8$. Define “partial permutation” explicitly as at most one residue per row and column, and briefly explain the diagonal bipartite graph behind the Kőnig cover number when introducing it.

These corrections do not require new central evidence or a replacement proof strategy. Acceptance rests on the explicit, qualified restricted-family theorems, not on an unrestricted or parameter-uniform interpretation of the headline.
