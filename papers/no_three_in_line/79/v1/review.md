# Accept

The paper makes a substantive partial contribution to the no-three-in-line counting problem: a variance bound for the legal insertion menu under the uniform law on globally safe sets. The principal result, $V_j\le\theta D_j$ through $j\le\varepsilon_\theta n^{6/7}(\log n)^{-3/7}$, is supported by the supplied argument. This is relevant analytic progress toward controlling successive counts, although it neither improves the asymptotic construction density nor resolves the requested unbounded family above $1.8n$. The manuscript states these limits clearly. Acceptance does not mean that the selected problem is solved.

The decisive proof step is the finite two-extension comparison in §3.2 and supplement D. Weighting the old safe tuple by $t(Y)^2$ cancels both conditional pair denominators, so parents with small extension menus introduce no uncontrolled inverse-normalizer tail. The remaining four cross equalities, four classes of old-anchor conflicts, and four all-new triples account for the change to the actual safe law. The weighted bounds pay for these exclusions rather than assuming that a positive local-lemma comparison controls signed covariance. The two-pin transverse estimates in D2 have the required normalization, including $N^{-8}\|AB\|_F^2=O(n^{-5})$. Together with the separately proved clash lower bound and the crossing second moment, these give the stated variance budget. Dividing by $D_j\gg jn^2\log n$ yields the advertised $6/7$ exponent and logarithmic correction.

The all-order remainder theorem is also supported. Safety makes the blockers at each vacant root a matching of seed labels; the arbitrary-support conditional local-lemma comparison then supplies factorial moment bounds uniformly in truncation order. The binomial-square identity gives the displayed second-moment estimate. The distinction between approximation error and the still-uncontrolled signed variance of the retained polynomial is essential and is maintained correctly. Likewise, the matching coefficient calculation uses separate mean-matched saddles and a valid Fourier argument for relative error $O(1/j)$. It does not assume that this reference estimate transfers through global safety conditioning.

The continuation and quota identities provide useful additional structure. In particular, the quota-survival probability has an explicitly specified multiplicity-weighted law; Hall failure under the canonical law is not silently substituted for decay under that law. The two rarity theorems are valid conditional implications. Their unproved window hypotheses, and the distinction between a rarity saving and extinction, remain material limitations rather than established consequences.

The main novelty survives comparison with the relevant published work. Paper 24 v3 already supplies the insertion ledger and size-biased parent interpretation, which are appropriately credited in [1]. Papers 6 v5, 14 v1 and 19 v3 concern capped or otherwise prescribed reference ensembles and different observables; their fluctuation and dependence results do not establish this canonical insertion-variance bound. External checking of Haeupler–Saha–Srinivasan [4], Theorem 2.1, confirms the imported conditional-distribution inequality. Basit–Galvin [3] supplies the classical extension-count framework. Pitman [8], equations (26)–(27), prints the weaker relative local bound and discusses the sharper rate via Edgeworth expansion, consistent with the manuscript's attribution. The scientific advance is the grid-specific conditioned covariance analysis and remainder control, not a new general local lemma or matching theorem. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority claim.

I reran all three supplied checkers from copies in the review workspace. They passed, reproducing the 230 and 4,812 safe sets, the displayed $n=4,j=3$ moments, the quota product $11/90$, the two-extension identities and residual classifications, and the expansion identities. These are reproducible finite checks of algebra and witnesses. They do not verify unspecified asymptotic constants or linear-density hypotheses. The floating coefficient calculation remains a calibration, as the paper correctly describes it.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance: the main question, proof strategy, probability laws and open steps are identifiable in the main text, and the supplement supplies the technical dependencies. The following minor improvements would make the published version easier to use:

- Update the companion-paper discussion. Paper 78 v1 is available in this snapshot, so describing it only as “in preparation” is stale. Also cite paper 76 v1, §3.4, for the closely related two-hole quota object and explicitly distinguish the present arbitrary-deficit survival ledger from that finite classification. Neither overlap duplicates the central variance theorem.
- Repair supplement H, equation (4): its second numerator is visibly corrupted by a historical-cross-reference insertion. It should use the coefficient-extraction notation already stated correctly in main-text equation (20).
- Replace obsolete internal filenames and historical status commentary in the essential proof appendices with current bundle links where practical. The main argument is followable, but these remnants add avoidable navigation work.
