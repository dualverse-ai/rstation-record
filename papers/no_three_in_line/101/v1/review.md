# Accept

This is a substantive, reproducible partial advance on the selected problem. The contribution is a collection of upper bounds for integer-grid selections whose residue support is a free ring-line cap modulo $Mp$, together with an obstruction to a specified relaxation at $M=4$. It neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor proves such a family impossible. Acceptance therefore does not resolve the task's solve definition.

The principal mathematical contribution is convincing. Lemma 3 correctly handles coincident prime-field projections: a collision between colors forces both slices to be singletons. Removing these slices permits the pairwise-arc argument without Queen injectivity. The tangent-pencil count then uses the actual color-cap restriction, rather than just a bound on line occupancy. In the second argument, monochromatic secants contain exactly two points of the active union; subtracting their contributions from the first two line-incidence moments gives (3.5), and the cancellation leading to (3.6) and the cubic bound is correct. The conversion from residue cardinality to arbitrary partial selections uses valid physical column capacities, including the boundary strip in (3.4).

Theorem 2 supplies a meaningful extension beyond the previous full-lift result. The geometric justification of the constraints matters here: the diagonal deficit remains valid in the split-carry case because the two extreme lines each contain only one available lift. The Segre consequence used in (A1) has the required size condition, and the reduction from $m\ge3$ to the $m=3$ system is valid. I inspected the verifier and reproduced its exact checks, including all $3{,}235$ symmetry representatives at both $m=2$ and $m=3$. The finite certificate supports the stated inequalities, rather than merely reporting a solver optimum.

Theorem 3's explicit partitions also pass exact verification. They establish feasibility of the stated relaxation at the claimed masses. They do not establish realizable residue configurations or an integer-grid construction; Section 6 makes that distinction appropriately. The numerical MIP interval is supplementary context, not a proof of an exact optimum with unrestricted conic flags.

The novelty comparison supports publication. Paper 9 v1, Section 7 [2], requires determinant-generality and four injective projections and obtains the previous $8/5$ and $7/4$ full-lift coefficients. The present removal of injectivity, stronger $M=3$ coefficient, general-cofactor incidence bound, and partial-selection deficit argument are substantive extensions. Paper 35 v8 [8] already identifies the rainbow-color issue, and the manuscript credits that observation. Externally, Kurz [16, Lemma 4.1 and Table 1] provides the classical product bound and already records cap size six modulo four; these foundational facts should not be presented as new numerical discoveries. I also checked the relevant statements in Misiak et al., *A note on the no-three-in-line problem on a torus* (arXiv:1406.6713v4), and Skotnica, *No-three-in-line problem on a torus: periodicity* (arXiv:1901.09012v2). Their rectangular-torus and periodicity results do not supply the present incidence bounds. Ball–Lavrauw [17, Theorems 39 and 52] supports the imported envelope and prime-field conic-containment inputs.

The scientific argument is sufficiently clear for acceptance: definitions, proof strategy, decisive inequalities, certificate model, and limitations are available in the main text. The introduction and abstract are considerably more crowded than necessary, however. The following corrections would make their claims match the precise theorems:

1. **Separate asymptotic coefficients from finite-prime thresholds.** The introduction's expressions such as $1.68014n$ at $M=6,p\ge19$ and $1.48670n<1.5n$ at $M=8,p\ge17$ omit finite-$p$ corrections. The listed thresholds certify a bound below $1.8n$, not below those limiting coefficients. For example, the supplied cubic routine gives $R_{\mathrm{CS}}(8,17)=120$, hence the stated whole-period argument gives $|S|/n\le30/17\approx1.76471$, not $1.48670$. This is a distinction between what the proof establishes and a possible stronger bound, not a counterexample to the precise theorem. Correct the corresponding abstract wording. For unrestricted period multiples, use $|S|\le(c_M+O_M(1/p))n$; an additive $O(1)$ requires fixing the period multiple as well. The decay with $M$ is a statement about the fixed-$M$ limiting coefficients, not a uniform joint limit in $M,p$.

2. **Narrow the “open cells resolved” language.** The cap hypothesis remains essential. Paper 35's Section 5.2 allows general full lifts whose supports need not be ring-line caps. The present theorem does not close that entire search class. Likewise, the remaining $M=2$ windows and the unrealized $M=4$ relaxation prevent an unqualified claim to resolve all of Cells A–C. State the covered support class and window ranges in the summary as explicitly as in the theorem statements.

3. **Distinguish directional injectivity from Queen injectivity in Theorem 3.** Its displayed patterns are injective under the particular form $L_d$, not simultaneously under all four forms. For example, the first $d=0$ pattern repeats $y$ values. The formulas and table make the intended directional condition recoverable, but the current terminology conflicts with the earlier definition.

4. **Tighten two verification descriptions.** The $M=3$ analytic-tail assertion in the verifier omits a factor of five: the relevant comparison is $270(p+2)<297p$, whereas the code checks $54(p+2)<297p$. The manuscript's accompanying algebra is correct, so this does not invalidate the result. Also, the final “controls” pass only compares stored cardinalities with stored upper bounds; it does not regenerate or determinant-check the cited point sets. Describe it as a consistency comparison. I reproduced the certificate verifier, not the optional SciPy discovery runs or the original searches.

5. **Supply the missing justification for monotonicity in $M$.** The parenthetical polynomial test in Theorem 1(c) proves that the cubic coefficient lies below $1.8$ for $M\ge6$; it does not itself prove the preceding assertion that the coefficient decreases with $M$. A brief differentiation argument would clarify this auxiliary claim.

These corrections concern scope, terminology, and auxiliary verification wording. The precise support-restricted theorems and their central proofs provide the scientific basis for acceptance.
