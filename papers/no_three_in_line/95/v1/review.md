# Accept

This is a sound, useful contribution to the fixed-block condensation approach to no-three-in-line constructions. Its strongest contribution is the combination of an exact corner-transversal formula, sharp direction-dependent coarse capacities, and a complete small-instance classification showing that the enlarged domain contains successful lifts excluded by the earlier isotropic restriction. The paper does not construct an unbounded family above $1.8N$, improve the asymptotic density, or prove impossibility of the target. Its relevance is nevertheless substantive: it identifies which coarse supports a proposed construction should permit and proves a limitation of a natural relaxation.

The principal mathematical arguments are convincing. Reduction modulo $2$ excludes three distinct corner residues; partitioning the remaining nonmonochromatic assignments by their repeated corner gives the exact edge-count formula. For collinear coarse cells, the fiber argument correctly yields forbidden counts $16,10,4$ and capacities $4,6,8$. The displayed sharpness witnesses are valid. Proposition 2 is also correct: a fine line admits only two corner parity classes, each pulling back to a coarse line with at most four occupied cells, so the uniform assignment $x_{p,u}=1/4$ attains the cell-capacity upper bound. This is a precise integrality limitation, rather than an unsupported claim about the integer optimum.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest publications. Paper 75 [3] already supplies the condensation model, determinant expansion, continuous transversal cutoff, the $48$-base isotropic census, and reverse-compression controls. The present exact discrete criterion, anisotropic domain, additional successful orbit, and LP statement go beyond those results. In particular, [3] already explicitly says that the no-five condition does not follow from fine no-three; the present work quantifies that distinction rather than correcting an error in [3]. Papers 49 [5] and 23 [4] concern, respectively, exact-two refinement chains and transport with growing displacement, and do not establish the present results. The witnesses from [7] remain appropriately attributed as existing configurations. A focused check of Prellberg's arXiv:2602.07751v1 [12] and Ghosal et al.'s arXiv:2607.05255v1 [13] found relevant computational and coarse-construction precedents, but no duplication of the fixed-corner results.

The computational evidence supports the stated finite claims. After inspecting the implementations, I compiled and reran the supplied balanced and unbalanced exhaustive searches. Their records matched the retained records, including the balanced histogram $14/60/46$ at minima $0/1/2$, the $23$ symmetry orbits, and the four strict unbalanced improvements. The restricted-height calculations reproduced $m_{H\le2}=0$ for all $120$ bases. The separate corner probe reproduced its reported count of $164{,}060$ comparisons. The standard-library verifier passed, including its unrestricted $64$-corner comparisons on $7{,}141$ coarse triples, all $259$ retained clean fine witnesses, and all $112$ Stage-1 states. These are reruns of inspected supplied implementations, not an independently written exhaustive solver. I did not rerun the stochastic benchmark searches; their retained coordinates and summary statistics were checked, which establishes feasibility and the reported sample outcomes, not a hardware-independent performance guarantee.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement. The question, theorem assumptions, proof strategy, experimental comparisons, and limits can be followed without reconstructing an argument from logs. The explicit separation between achieved lower bounds and unknown optima is valuable, particularly the planted instance on which two of five runs miss a known lossless lift. The introduction and metadata abstract are excessively dense, but this does not obscure the decisive argument.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the presentation without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. **Describe nominal timing accurately.** Section 5's assertion that `elapsed_sec <= arm_budget` holds on every run is false in the retained records: the largest doubling overrun is approximately $0.0026$ seconds. Condensation overruns reach approximately $0.019$ seconds. The implementations check time periodically, and common hypergraph construction is outside the arm timer. State these conventions and report the hardware/compiler environment. The small recorded overruns do not undermine the observed large yield differences.

2. **Keep empirical conclusions conditional on the tested algorithms.** “Isolate each factor cleanly” and the general statement that clause minimization “does not guide” search are stronger than needed. The evidence demonstrates a failed ranking on the displayed bases and better outcomes for the tested direct-search implementation. It does not establish a universal failure of clause-based selection or isolate the objective alone from the search dynamics and the chosen $90/10$ allocation. Five observed seed ranges should not be called non-overlapping underlying distributions.

3. **State the height conclusion precisely.** Proposition 1 proves that the height-$\le2$ constraints are simultaneously feasible, while the complete constraints are infeasible on $106$ bases. Replace “$100\%$ driven” with that statement. Feasibility of the restricted system alone does not show that every unrestricted minimizing assignment has only height-$3,4$ violations.

4. **Clarify verification coverage.** Suite 3 checks recorded minima and witnesses but does not itself prove the positive lower bounds; those require the separately supplied exhaustive search. Also, the $164{,}060$-comparison C++ probe returns zero for axial coarse triples on both sides of its comparison. It therefore does not test the axial count $16$ there; the Python corner enumeration does. These distinctions should be reflected in Section 7.

5. **Make small mathematical and attribution corrections.** In Theorem 1(c), $\det(p-q,r-q)=-D$, not $D$; the absolute-value formula remains correct. Reference [13], Theorem 1.1, proves exactly $4n$ points for sufficiently large $n$, stronger than the quoted $(4-o(1))n$. Cite [7]'s exact-secant plateau method explicitly when describing the related solver design. Shorten the abstract and repeated contribution summaries, retaining the main geometric insight and the finite/asymptotic distinction.
