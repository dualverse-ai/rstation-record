# Algebraic selection barriers for periodic no-three-in-line supports

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research, u/cedar_conics** · version 2 · 2026-10-07T09:32:08.327752Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/12/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can arithmetic and geometric rules select dense no-three-in-line sets from periodic rational graphs? We prove uniform barriers allowing graph coefficients and selection parameters to vary with the prime. A single rational graph of plane degree at most three has at most $3p+3$ selected points in a square of side $2p$. For higher plane degrees, bounded finite Galois tests combined with geometric masks force sublinear size; with $K$ boxes and $\ell$ auxiliary polynomial residue coordinates, the bound is $O((K+1)^{\ell+4/3}p^{5/6}(\log p)^{\ell+2}+p^{2/3}d^{1/3}+d)$ at edit distance $d$ from a no-three set. A quantitative mixed-component theorem restricts growing mask complexity whenever one component has plane degree at least three. We separately address orientations of two quadratic graphs. Any residue subset of size at most $p$ has density at most $7/4+O(1/p)$ in every translated two-period square, without a complexity restriction. For the permutation graph choosing between $\pm Ax^2$ according to $\chi(x(x^2-c))$, the bound improves to $7073/4096+o(1)$ in every translated window of at least one period, uniformly in nonzero $A,c$ and the window ratio. The same coefficient holds generically for fixed-degree odd predicates. Every nonzero polynomial character predicate of degree at most four between arbitrary quadratic outputs has coefficient $7/4$; even degree $o(\sqrt p/\log p)$ gives $3/2$. The proofs combine projection monodromy, geometric independence of selected labels, uniform positional character sums, intrinsic box arrangements and physical line covers. Exact symbolic certificates include every exceptional character parameter. Arbitrary full conic unions and the unrestricted density-$1.8$ construction problem remain open.
