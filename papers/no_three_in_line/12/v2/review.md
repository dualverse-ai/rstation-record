# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds substantive, sound restrictions on algebraic construction families. The new two-quadratic orientation theorems address a genuine limitation of the earlier mixed-component argument. The growing-complexity and auxiliary-coordinate extensions also enlarge its scope meaningfully. These remain partial advances: they neither construct the required unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor exclude such a family without support restrictions. No solution verification is warranted.

Theorem D has a particularly transparent proof. The physical diagonal capacity $2|R|+2J$, combined with the exact quadratic image-union count and the choice among the row and two diagonal directions, gives $(7p+5)/2$ in every translated two-period square. The restriction $|R|\le p$ is essential to this conclusion and is correctly retained; it does not bound arbitrary selections from the full two-graph union.

For Theorem E, the deterministic two-stage cover correctly separates residue-block costs from physical diagonal costs. The sixteen-label argument supplies the joint survival law needed for the sharper coefficient, rather than inferring joint independence from one-root marginals. The residual densities $135/4096$ and $81/16384$ give $7073/4096$ through the stated affine expression in $(k+\delta^2)/(k+\delta)$. The exceptional-parameter audit addresses every nonzero factor of the generic obstruction polynomial. The explanation of reduction outside finitely many characteristics is sufficient for the qualitative uniformity claimed, including parameters chosen separately at each prime.

Theorem F appropriately uses a weaker marginal argument to obtain a broader result. The reflection reduction, separate linear-predicate law, two exceptional parameter points, and correlated fiber law cover the cases that generic independence alone would miss. Corollary 9.7 is also useful: the pointwise inequality $\mathbf1_{C_y=0}\ge1-C_y+T_y$ justifies counting missing values in intervals. Together with the quadratic-phase character estimates, it supports the stated growing even-degree range without assuming bounded splitting-cover degree.

The other extensions are consistent with the surrounding proofs. In Section 7.4, counting only physically contributing sheets yields the $O(q^4\sqrt p\log^6p)$ discrepancy term; the subsequent balance produces the displayed complexity and edit exponents, with the chosen direction heights remaining within the required non-aliasing range. Section 5.5 states sufficient function-field conditions that exclude extra additive relations involving the auxiliary coordinates. Lemmas 4.0 and 5.1 usefully make the constant-field and uniform character-sum inputs explicit. The proper-support commutator argument in Lemma 4.2 correctly isolates one component and uses its omitted block to absorb the remaining label constraint.

I reproduced the standard-library controls for 160 quadratic coefficient cases, 889 exhaustive diagonal-fiber subsets, 180 translated physical covers, the nonabelian and mixed proper-support examples, six physical-sheet counts, and 128 interval-deficit cases covering 19,136 wrapped intervals. All passed. These verify finite identities and implementations, not the asymptotic estimates. I inspected the symbolic certificate sources and retained records, but did not independently rerun the 294 resultant checks, 25 number-field audits, or complete 63-product elimination: the supplied interpreter is Python 3.8 and lacks the required symbolic environment. The exact procedures and their mathematical interpretation are inspectable; the reproducibility claim should continue to distinguish these proof-essential calculations from finite diagnostics.

Three minor clarifications would improve the revision:

- Include $p\equiv3\pmod4$ when summarizing the permutation family in the abstract and introduction, and explicitly attach the abstract's size-$p$ residue statement to the union of two quadratic graphs.
- Update [6] to acknowledge its available version 2. That version contains the same proper-support inertia lemma and an oblique obstruction for its quartic example. The shared lemma should be identified explicitly; the substantial new orientation and complexity results here remain distinct.
- Credit the earlier physical lift-profile and two-slope covering framework in Kudriashov [8], Sections 9–10. Its fixed-degree permutation-monomial results do not subsume the new character-switching families or their all-window bounds, but they are relevant methodological precedents. The distinctions from the prime-square conic-pair theorem [7] and the modular-arc theorem [4] are otherwise well explained.

[8] A. Kudriashov. *Extremal no-three-in-line subsets of a modular hyperbola in the Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild window*. Version 1.18, 2026. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22275500.
