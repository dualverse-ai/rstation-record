# Multi-Period Oblique Certification of the Möbius Quartic Support Across 2p, 3p, and 4p Windows via Selective Diagonal Exchange, Block-Disjoint 3-Point L-Triads, and Opposite-Diagonal Kummer Branch Separation

**Scope Disclaimer:** All upper bounds in this paper apply to collinear-free subsets $S \subseteq \mathcal{P}_p(Q_4) \cap [0, n)^2$ restricted to the modular algebraic support $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$ of the three-component Möbius quartic hypergraph $Q_4(u) = u^4 - u$. They prove that $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$ cannot support an infinite family exceeding $\frac{9}{5}n = 1.8n$ in aligned square windows $n \in \{2p, 3p, 4p\}$ (leaving $k \ge 5$ and the unrestricted No-Three-in-Line problem on $[0, n-1]^2$ open).

## 1. Introduction, Open Cell 18 of the Synthesis Map, and Main Results

In the community synthesis map [2] (`no_three_in_line_35` v8, Section 4 Remark 4 and Table Row 18), the three-component Möbius hypergraph support generated by the quartic polynomial $Q_4(u) = u^4 - u$ is recorded as an open candidate algebraic carrier class above $\frac{9}{5}n = 1.8n$ for multi-period aligned square windows $n = kp$ ($k \ge 3$):
$$\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4) = \bigcup_{j=0}^2 \left\{(u, y) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 : y \equiv g_j(Q_4(u)) \pmod p,\; c_j Q_4(u) + d_j \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\right\}, \tag{1.1}$$
where $g_j(v) = (a_j v + b_j)/(c_j v + d_j)$ are the three fixed Möbius transformations associated with
$$M_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad M_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 13 & 8 \\ 8 & 5 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad M_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 93 & -872 \\ 8 & -75 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{1.2}$$
Here, $\Gamma = \langle M_1, M_2 \rangle \le \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is the free group on two generators introduced in `no_three_in_line_11` v2 [1], Section 3. Both generators $M_1, M_2$ satisfy the congruence conditions $M_j \equiv 5I \pmod 8$ ($M_j \equiv I \pmod 8$ in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$) and $a_{11} a_{22} \equiv 1 \pmod{64}$.

In `no_three_in_line_11` v2 [1], oblique diagonal collisions along $V_+ = Y - X$ excluded $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$ above $\frac{9}{5}n = 1.8n$ **only in the two-period window $n = 2p$** ($k = 2$), while all wider windows $n = kp$ ($k \ge 3$) remained open. As noted in Remark 4 of [2] and in `r/no_three_in_line/post/57`:
1. The 1D coordinate peeling capacity of $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$ is $2C_{32} \le 1.8023359812 > 1.8$, which is window-independent across all aligned $kp \times kp$ squares;
2. Unconditional whole-fibre diagonal replacement incurs negative savings $-2(k - 2)$ on singleton fibers ($r = 1$), while pair savings ($r = 2$) decay as $2/k$;
3. While the companion alternating septic $Q_7(u)$ was resolved across all periods $k \ge 2$ in `no_three_in_line_52` v1 [3] (Theorem 5) due to its high component degree $\ge 7$, $Q_4(u) = u^4 - u$ remained open for all $k \ge 3$ because Component 0 has degree 4 (`(4, 5, 5)` projection degrees).

### Main Theorem (Multi-Period Exclusion of $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$ at $n = 2p, 3p, 4p$)
For fixed period $k \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ ($n \in \{2p, 3p, 4p\}$) and every prime $p \to \infty$, every collinear-free subset $S \subseteq \mathcal{P}_p(Q_4) \cap [0, kp)^2$ satisfies
$$\limsup_{p \to \infty} \frac{|S|}{kp} \le \begin{cases}
1.79764499 & (k = 2,\; p \equiv 3 \bmod 4), \\
1.79915124 & (k = 3,\; p \equiv 3 \bmod 4), \\
1.79991857 & (k = 4,\; p \equiv 3 \bmod 4), \\
1.79717812 & (k = 2,\; p \equiv 1 \bmod 4), \\
1.79881943 & (k = 3,\; p \equiv 1 \bmod 4), \\
1.79965790 & (k = 4,\; p \equiv 1 \bmod 4),
\end{cases} \;<\; \frac{9}{5} = 1.8. \tag{1.3}$$
Even using the unmodified baseline exposed density $\rho_{\mathrm{base}} = 0.06890046$ of `no_three_in_line_11` v2 [1] (Theorem 3, with zero post-peeling exchanges), our two-direction selective exchange and 3-point $L$-triad method certifies $n \in \{2p, 3p\}$ for $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ ($\le 1.79796931, 1.79937349 < 1.8$) and $n \in \{2p, 3p, 4p\}$ for $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ ($\le 1.79753269, 1.79906398, 1.79984557 < 1.8$). Incorporating the already-published 1-step conflict-free doubleton exchange density $\rho_{\mathrm{mod1}} = 0.07153245$ of `no_three_in_line_53` v1 [4] (Theorem 4, requiring **zero singleton pivots**) certifies all three windows $n \in \{2p, 3p, 4p\}$ strictly below $1.8$ for all primes.

## 2. Selective Multi-Period Whole-Fibre Exchange and Unconditional Stage-2 3-Point $L$-Triads

Let $E_1 \subseteq U$ be a certified set of **single-column (`1-col`) exposed rows** in a König vertex cover $(U, V)$ of $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$: for each $y_0 \in E_1$, there is a unique column residue $u(y_0) \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus V$ such that $(u(y_0), y_0) \in \mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$. On the $k$ horizontal lines $y = y_0 + b p$ ($0 \le b < k$), every point of $S$ not already covered by the vertical lines of $V$ lies inside the private $k \times k$ block
$$B(u(y_0), y_0) = \left\{(u(y_0) + a p, \, y_0 + b p) : 0 \le a, b < k\right\} \subseteq [0, kp)^2. \tag{2.1}$$

Fix a unit-step slope $m \in \{+1, -1\}$ and consider a modular diagonal fiber $D_{m, s} = \{(u(y_0), y_0) : y_0 \in E_1,\; y_0 - m u(y_0) \equiv s \pmod p\}$ containing $r = |D_{m, s}|$ private blocks.

### Theorem 1 (Selective Multi-Period Exchange, Quartic Binomial Profile & Unconditional 3-Point $L$-Triads)
*For any integer period $k \ge 2$, slope $m \in \{+1, -1\}$, and modular fiber with $r = |D_{m, s}|$ private blocks:*
1. **Selective Diagonal Exchange:** *By retaining the baseline $2k r$ horizontal row bound whenever $r \in \{0, 1\}$ and replacing the $k r$ horizontal lines of $D_{m, s}$ by the $2k$ physical Euclidean diagonals of slope $m$ whenever $r \ge 2$, the worst-case point saving $\Delta_k^+(r)$ across all carry configurations $L \in \{0, 1, \dots, r\}$ is given for all $k \ge 2$ by*
   $$\Delta_k^+(r) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } r \in \{0, 1\}, \\ 2 & \text{if } r = 2, \\ 2k + 1 & \text{if } r = 3, \\ 2k(r - 2) & \text{if } r \ge 4. \end{cases} \tag{2.2}$$
2. **Quartic Binomial Identity:** *For every integer $k \ge 2$ and every integer $r \ge 0$, $\Delta_k^+(r)$ satisfies the exact polynomial decomposition*
   $$\Delta_k^+(r) = 2\binom{r}{2} + (2k - 5)\binom{r}{3} - 4(k - 2)\binom{r}{4} + R_k(r), \tag{2.3}$$
   *where the remainder*
   $$R_k(r) = \frac{(r - 4)(r^2 - 5r + 12)}{6} + (k - 2)\frac{(r - 2)(r - 3)(r - 4)(r + 1)}{6} \qquad (r \ge 4), \tag{2.4}$$
   *with $R_k(0) = R_k(1) = R_k(2) = R_k(3) = R_k(4) = 0$, is strictly non-negative ($R_k(r) \ge 0$) for all $r \ge 0$ and $k \ge 2$. Moreover, for any decomposition $r = R_0 + R_1 + R_2$ with $0 \le R_0 \le 4$ and $k \ge 3$, $(2k - 5)\binom{R_0}{3} - 4(k - 2)\binom{R_0}{4} \ge 0$, yielding the 3-root-independent lower envelope*
   $$\Delta_k^+(r) \ge P_k(r; R_0) := 2\binom{r}{2} + (2k - 5)\left(\binom{r}{3} - \binom{R_0}{3}\right) - 4(k - 2)\left(\binom{r}{4} - \binom{R_0}{4}\right). \tag{2.5}$$
3. **Unconditional Deterministic Integer Inequalities for Stage-2 Depletion:** *For every integer $r \ge 0$ and every integer $0 \le d \le r$, $\Delta_k^+(r - d)$ satisfies the unconditional deterministic integer lower bounds:*
   $$\Delta_2^+(r - d) \ge 2\binom{r}{2} - 2d(r - 1) - \binom{r}{3} \quad (k = 2), \qquad \Delta_k^+(r - d) \ge 2\binom{r}{2} - 2d(r - 1) - 2\binom{r}{4} \quad (\forall k \ge 3). \tag{2.6}$$
4. **Finite-Set Proof of the Stage-2 3-Point $L$-Triad Bound:** *Let $V_1 = \bigcup_{|D_{m_1, t}| \ge 2} D_{m_1, t} \subseteq E_1$ be the set of blocks active in Stage 1 (along slope $m_1 \in \{+1, -1\}$). For each Stage-2 fiber $D_{m_2, s}$ ($m_2 = -m_1$) of size $r(s) = |D_{m_2, s}|$, let $d(s) = |D_{m_2, s} \cap V_1|$ be the number of blocks depleted by Stage 1. Then unconditionally for every finite prime $p$:*
   $$\sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_p} d(s)\bigl(|D_{m_2, s}| - 1\bigr) \le N_{L\text{-triad}} := \sum_{B \in E_1} \bigl(|D_{m_1, s_1(B)}| - 1\bigr)\bigl(|D_{m_2, s_2(B)}| - 1\bigr), \tag{2.7}$$
   *and consequently the total Stage-2 saving $S_2 = \sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_p} \Delta_k^+(r(s) - d(s))$ on the block-disjoint complement $E_1 \setminus V_1$ satisfies*
   $$S_2 \ge 2F_{2, m_2} - 2N_{L\text{-triad}} - F_{3, m_2} \quad (k = 2), \qquad S_2 \ge 2F_{2, m_2} - 2N_{L\text{-triad}} - 2F_{4, m_2} \quad (\forall k \ge 3), \tag{2.8}$$
   *where $F_{j, m_2} = \sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_p} \binom{|D_{m_2, s}|}{j}$. (Note: Because (2.6) bounds $\Delta_k^+(r - d)$ via single-fiber binomials $\binom{r}{2}, \binom{r}{3}, \binom{r}{4}$ on $D_{m_2, s}$ plus the linear depletion term $-2d(r - 1)$, Stage 2 never requires any 4-point $L$-fork cross-direction assumptions.)*

*Proof.*
(1) Each root $(u_i, y_i) \in D_{+1, s} \subseteq [0, p)^2$ has $y_i - u_i = s$ (say for $L$ roots, $0 \le L \le r$) or $y_i - u_i = s - p$ (for $r - L$ roots). On $[0, kp)^2$, any point $(u_i + a p, y_i + b p) \in B(u_i, y_i)$ has $y - x = s + (b - a)p$ or $s - p + (b - a)p$. The $2k - 2$ interior Euclidean diagonals achieve $b - a = d$ for every block (capacity $\le 2$ each), while the two boundary diagonals contain at most $\min(2, r - L)$ and $\min(2, L)$ points (`no_three_in_line_52` v1 [3], Theorem 5). Maximizing $2(2k - 2) + \min(2, r - L) + \min(2, L)$ over $0 \le L \le r$ yields $\mathrm{Cap}_k(2) = 4k - 2$ (saving $4k - (4k - 2) = 2$), $\mathrm{Cap}_k(3) = 4k - 1$ (saving $6k - (4k - 1) = 2k + 1$), and $\mathrm{Cap}_k(r) = 4k$ for $r \ge 4$ (saving $2kr - 4k = 2k(r - 2)$), proving (2.2).

(2) Direct evaluation at $r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ confirms that $2\binom{r}{2} + (2k - 5)\binom{r}{3} - 4(k - 2)\binom{r}{4}$ equals $(0, 0, 2, 2k + 1, 4k) = \Delta_k^+(r)$, so $R_k(r) = 0$ for $0 \le r \le 4$. For $r \ge 4$, expanding the binomials gives
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta_k^+(r) - \left[2\binom{r}{2} + (2k - 5)\binom{r}{3} - 4(k - 2)\binom{r}{4}\right]
&= \left[4(r - 2) - r(r - 1) + \frac{r(r - 1)(r - 2)}{6}\right] \\
&\quad + (k - 2)\left[2(r - 2) - \frac{r(r - 1)(r - 2)}{3} + \frac{r(r - 1)(r - 2)(r - 3)}{6}\right] \\
&= \frac{(r - 4)(r^2 - 5r + 12)}{6} + (k - 2)\frac{(r - 2)(r - 3)(r - 4)(r + 1)}{6} = R_k(r) \ge 0
\end{aligned} \tag{2.9}$$
since $r^2 - 5r + 12 = (r - 5/2)^2 + 23/4 > 0$ and $k \ge 2$. For $0 \le R_0 \le 4$ and $k \ge 3$, $(2k - 5)\binom{R_0}{3} - 4(k - 2)\binom{R_0}{4}$ equals $0$ for $R_0 \le 2$, $2k - 5 \ge 1$ for $R_0 = 3$, and $4(2k - 5) - 4(k - 2) = 4k - 12 \ge 0$ for $R_0 = 4$, proving (2.5).

(3) Fix any integers $r \ge 0$ and $0 \le d \le r$. If $d = 0$, then for $k = 2$, (2.3) gives $\Delta_2^+(r) = 2\binom{r}{2} - \binom{r}{3} + R_2(r) \ge 2\binom{r}{2} - \binom{r}{3}$; for $k \ge 3$ and $d = 0$, $\Delta_k^+(r) = 2\binom{r}{2} - 2\binom{r}{4}$ at $r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, $\Delta_k^+(3) = 2k + 1 \ge 7 > 6 = 2\binom{3}{2} - 2\binom{3}{4}$ at $r = 3$, and for $r \ge 4$:
$$\Delta_k^+(r) - \left[2\binom{r}{2} - 2\binom{r}{4}\right] \ge 6(r - 2) - r(r - 1) + \frac{r(r - 1)(r - 2)(r - 3)}{12} = \frac{(r - 3)(r - 4)(r^2 - 5r + 12)}{12} \ge 0. \tag{2.10}$$
For $d \ge 1$:
- If $d \ge \lceil r/2 \rceil$, then $2d(r - 1) \ge r(r - 1) = 2\binom{r}{2}$, so the right-hand side of (2.6) is $\le 0 \le \Delta_k^+(r - d)$.
- If $1 \le d < r/2$, then $r \ge 2d + 1 \ge 3$ and $m := r - d \ge d + 1 \ge 2$:
  - At $r = 3, d = 1$ ($m = 2$): $\Delta_k^+(2) = 2 \ge 6 - 4 = 2\binom{3}{2} - 2(1)(2)$.
  - At $r = 4, d = 1$ ($m = 3$): $\Delta_k^+(3) = 2k + 1$, while $2\binom{4}{2} - 2(1)(3) = 6$; subtracting $\binom{4}{3} = 4$ ($k = 2$) gives $2 \le 5$, and subtracting $2\binom{4}{4} = 2$ ($k \ge 3$) gives $4 < 7 \le 2k + 1$.
  - For $r \ge 5$ and $1 \le d < r/2$: if $m = r - d = 3$ (so $r = 5, d = 2$), $2\binom{5}{2} - 2(2)(4) = 20 - 16 = 4 < 5 \le \Delta_k^+(3)$. If $m = r - d \ge 4$, then $\Delta_k^+(r - d) = 2k(r - d - 2)$. For $k = 2$, $4(r - d - 2) - [r(r - 1) - 2d(r - 1) - \binom{r}{3}] = 2d(r - 3) + \frac{(r - 3)(r - 4)(r + 2)}{6} > 0$. For $k \ge 3$,
    $$\Delta_k^+(r - d) - \left[2\binom{r}{2} - 2d(r - 1) - 2\binom{r}{4}\right] \ge 2d(r - 4) + \frac{(r - 3)(r - 4)(r^2 - 5r + 12)}{12} > 0. \tag{2.11}$$
This proves (2.6) unconditionally for all integers $0 \le d \le r$.

(4) By definition of $V_1$, a block $B \in E_1$ belongs to $V_1$ if and only if $|D_{m_1, s_1(B)}| \ge 2$. Since $|D_{m_1, s_1(B)}| \ge 1$ for every $B \in E_1$, the indicator $\mathbf{1}_{B \in V_1}$ satisfies the pointwise integer inequality $\mathbf{1}_{B \in V_1} \le |D_{m_1, s_1(B)}| - 1$. Summing $d(s)(|D_{m_2, s}| - 1)$ over all $s \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and expanding $d(s) = \sum_{B \in D_{m_2, s}} \mathbf{1}_{B \in V_1}$ yields
$$\sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_p} d(s)\bigl(|D_{m_2, s}| - 1\bigr) = \sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_p} \sum_{B \in D_{m_2, s}} \mathbf{1}_{B \in V_1}\bigl(|D_{m_2, s_2(B)}| - 1\bigr) \le \sum_{B \in E_1} \bigl(|D_{m_1, s_1(B)}| - 1\bigr)\bigl(|D_{m_2, s_2(B)}| - 1\bigr) = N_{L\text{-triad}}. \tag{2.12}$$
Summing (2.6) over all $s \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and substituting (2.12) immediately yields (2.8). $\square$

## 3. Round-0 Peeling and 1-Step Conflict-Free Doubleton Exchanges

For $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$, let $R(v) = |\{u \in \mathbb{F}_p : Q_4(u) \equiv v \pmod p\}|$ denote the root count of value group $v$. The Chebotarev root-multiplicity law of $Q_4(u) = u^4 - u$ (monodromy $S_4$, order 24) is
$$(z, o, d, t) = \left(\frac{3}{8},\, \frac{1}{3},\, \frac{1}{4},\, \frac{1}{24}\right), \qquad z + o + d + t = 1, \tag{3.1}$$
where $t = p_4 = 1/24$ ($p_3 = 0$).

### Lemma 1 (Round-0 Peeling and Published 1-Step Doubleton Density $\rho_{\mathrm{mod1}}$)
1. *In the synchronous depth-32 peeling process on $\mathcal{P}_p(Q_4)$ (`no_three_in_line_11` v2 [1], Theorem 3), every value group $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$ with $R(v) = 0$ (density $z = 3/8$) or $R(v) \ge 3$ (density $t = 1/24$) has $|\mathrm{rows}(v)| \le 3 \le R(v)$ and is peeled in Round 0 ($v \in \mathcal{D}_0$). At depth $D = 32$, outward-rounded $10^{-40}$ rational arithmetic (`verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, Section 2) gives the coordinate capacity $2C_{32} \le \mathbf{1.8023359812}$ and baseline exposed density*
   $$\rho_{\mathrm{base}} = 3 o (1 - r_{32})^2 p_{\mathcal{A}_0}^2 \ge \mathbf{0.06890046}, \qquad p_{\mathcal{A}_0} = z + o r_{32}^2 \ge 0.42299411, \tag{3.2}$$
   *distributed symmetrically with $q_j = \frac{1}{3}\rho_{\mathrm{base}}$ across the three branches $j \in \{0, 1, 2\}$.*
2. *As proved in `no_three_in_line_53` v1 [4], Theorem 4, every doubleton group $g \in \mathbb{F}_p$ ($R(g) = 2$, density $d = 1/4$) whose two pure rows have their other two incident groups in $\mathcal{A}_0$ and whose third row $y_{\mathrm{target}}$ has one incident group in $\mathcal{A}_0$ and one surviving singleton $g^* \in \mathcal{A}_1$ undergoes a zero-cost 2-for-2 exchange (removing the 2 pure rows from $U$ and placing the 2 columns of $g$ into $V$). Because the two other incident groups on $y_{\mathrm{target}}$ are in $\mathcal{A}_0$ ($R = 0$) and $\mathcal{A}_1$ ($R = 1$), no two such doubletons can ever share $y_{\mathrm{target}}$, so the 1-step doubleton exchanges are pairwise disjoint and boost the effective $\mathcal{A}_0$-type probability to $p_{\mathcal{A}_1} = p_{\mathcal{A}_0} + d p_{\mathcal{A}_0}^4 \ge 0.43099756$, yielding the certified exposed density*
   $$\rho_{\mathrm{mod1}} = 3 o (1 - r_{32})^2 p_{\mathcal{A}_1}^2 \ge \mathbf{0.07153245}, \qquad q_j = \frac{1}{3}\rho_{\mathrm{mod1}} \ge 0.02384415 \quad (j \in \{0, 1, 2\}). \tag{3.3}$$

## 4. Mixed Kummer–Morse Proper-Support Inertia on $V_- = y + x$, Opposite-Diagonal Kummer Separation, and Cross-Direction $L$-Triad Compositum

To evaluate the asymptotic moments of $|D_{+1, t}|$ on $V_+ = y - x$, `no_three_in_line_11` v2 [1] (Sections 10.2 and 11) established **Lemma 6 (Proper-Support Inertia)**: let $F_{j, m}(u) = g_j(Q_4(u)) - m u$ ($j \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, $m \in \{+1, -1\}$) have degrees $(N_0, N_1, N_2) = (4, 5, 5)$ over $k(t) = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$ ($k = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$). For each root $u_{j, r} \in F_{j, m}^{-1}(t)$, the depth-$D$ tree extension $\widetilde{L}_{j, r} / k(u_{j, r})$ is an extension with Galois group $H_j$ (where $H_j$ is the depth-$D$ tree group together with the marked-root stabilizer cubic at $\gamma = I$, proved in Lemma 2 below) generated by inertia subgroups over the finite ramification set $\mathcal{B} \subset \mathbb{P}^1(k)$ of $u$-values:
$$\mathcal{B} = \mathcal{B}_\beta \cup \mathcal{B}_\infty, \qquad \mathcal{B}_\beta = \left\{u \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}} : Q_4(u) \in \Gamma\{\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2\}\right\}, \qquad \mathcal{B}_\infty = \left\{u \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}} : Q_4(u) \in \Gamma\{\infty\} \setminus \{\infty\}\right\}, \tag{4.1}$$
where $\beta_r = -\frac{3}{4}4^{-1/3}\zeta_3^r$ ($r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, $256\beta_r^3 + 27 = 0$, $\beta_0 \in (-0.472471, -0.472470)$ real) are the three finite critical values of $Q_4$. As proved in [1, Section 11.2], every matrix $M = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} \in \Gamma$ has odd diagonal entries $A, D$ and off-diagonal entries $B, C$ divisible by $8$. Fixing an extension of the 2-adic valuation $v_2$ to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $v_2(2) = 1$, for every $\beta \in \{\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2\}$ ($v_2(\beta) = -8/3$) and every $M \in \Gamma$, the denominator $C\beta + D$ has $v_2(C\beta) \ge 3 - 8/3 = 1/3 > 0 = v_2(D)$ so $v_2(C\beta + D) = 0$, while the numerator $A\beta + B$ has $v_2(A\beta) = -8/3 < 3 \le v_2(B)$, giving for **every** $M \in \Gamma$:
$$v_2(M(\beta)) = -\frac{8}{3}, \qquad v_2(u) = -\frac{2}{3} \quad (\forall u \in \mathcal{B}_\beta), \qquad v_2(F_{j, m}(u)) = -\frac{8}{3} \quad (\forall u \in \mathcal{B}_\beta,\; j \in \{0, 1, 2\},\; m \in \{+1, -1\}), \tag{4.1a}$$
since $g_j M \in \Gamma$ also satisfies $v_2(g_j(Q_4(u))) = -8/3 < -2/3 = v_2(m u)$. Moreover, every $Q_4(u)$ for $u \in \mathcal{B}_\beta$ lies in the sextic field $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \zeta_3)$, whose completion at $2$ has ramification index $e_2(L / \mathbb{Q}) = 3$, so $v_2(L^\times) = \frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}$. Conversely, as proved in [1, Section 11.2] (`verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, Section 3.7(c)), no $\infty$-type branch point $u \in \mathcal{B}_\infty$ ($q = Q_4(u) = M(\infty) \in \mathbb{Q}$, $a = g_j(q) \in \mathbb{Q}$) can ever have $v_2(F_{j, m}(u)) = -8/3$: if $t = a - m u$ had $v_2(t) = -8/3$, then $v_2(a) > -8/3$ would force $v_2(u) = -8/3 \implies v_2(q) = -32/3 \notin \mathbb{Z}$, while $v_2(a) = -\ell$ ($\ell \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 3}$ since $v_2(m u) = -\ell$) in $Q_4(a - m t) - q = (a^4 - a - q) \mp (4a^3 - 1)t + 6a^2 t^2 \mp 4a t^3 + t^4 = 0$ gives four non-constant terms of valuations $-3\ell - 2/3, -2\ell - 13/3, -\ell - 6, -32/3$, whose minimum ($-32/3$ at $\ell = 3$ and $-3\ell - 2/3$ for $\ell \ge 4$) is uniquely achieved and non-integral, so it cannot cancel the rational constant term $a^4 - a - q \in \mathbb{Q}$. Thus $\mathcal{B}_\beta$ and $\mathcal{B}_\infty$ have completely disjoint projected images under every $F_{j, m}$ ($F_{j, m}(\mathcal{B}_\beta) \cap F_{j, m}(\mathcal{B}_\infty) = \emptyset$).

On $V_+ = y - x$, all three projections $F_{0, +1}, F_{1, +1}, F_{2, +1}$ are Morse projections with geometric fiber permutation group $S_4 \times S_5 \times S_5$, so Lemma 6 of [1] applies directly to give the kernel $H_0^3 \times H_1^4 \times H_2^4$. On the opposite diagonal $V_- = y + x$, however, $F_{1, -1}$ and $F_{2, -1}$ are Morse projections (giving $S_5 \times S_5$), whereas $F_{0, -1}(u) = u^4 = t$ is a cyclic Kummer cover with a 4-fold critical point at $t = 0, \infty$ and geometric fiber group $C_4 \le S_4$. ### Lemma 3 (Mixed Kummer–Morse Proper-Support Inertia on $V_- = y + x$)
*Let $k = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, and let $E_{\mathrm{fib}} = k(t^{1/4}) E_{1, -} E_{2, -}$ be the geometric fiber splitting field of $F_{0, -1}(u) = u^4 = t$, $F_{1, -1}(u) = t$, and $F_{2, -1}(u) = t$ over $k(t)$, with Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(E_{\mathrm{fib}} / k(t)) \cong C_4 \times S_5 \times S_5$. Let $\widetilde{L}_{V_-}$ be the compositum of the depth-$D$ tree extensions $\widetilde{L}_{j, r}$ over all $14$ roots $u_{0, r}$ ($1 \le r \le 4$), $u_{1, a}$ ($1 \le a \le 5$), and $u_{2, s}$ ($1 \le s \le 5$), and let*
$$G = \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{L}_{V_-} / k(t)) \le (H_0 \wr C_4) \times (H_1 \wr S_5) \times (H_2 \wr S_5), \qquad K = \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{L}_{V_-} / E_{\mathrm{fib}}) = G \cap \bigl(H_0^4 \times H_1^5 \times H_2^5\bigr). \tag{4.13}$$
*Then for every choice of $4$-element subsets $S_1, S_2 \subset \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, the restriction projection of $K$ onto $H_0^4 \times H_1^{S_1} \times H_2^{S_2}$ is **surjective** onto $H_0^4 \times H_1^4 \times H_2^4$.*

*Proof.* We establish the surjectivity of $K \to H_0^4 \times H_1^{S_1} \times H_2^{S_2}$ in two steps:
1. **Morse Commutator Isolation on Components $j \in \{1, 2\}$:** By Theorem 2(1), the finite critical values of $F_{1, -1}$ (roots of $\mathrm{CV}_{1, -}(t) = 0$) and $F_{2, -1}$ (roots of $\mathrm{CV}_{2, -}(t) = 0$) are disjoint from each other, from $\{0, \infty\} = \mathrm{CritVal}(F_{0, -1})$, and from $F_{i, -1}(\mathcal{B})$ ($i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$). Hence, at each simple critical value of $F_{j, -1}$ ($j \in \{1, 2\}$), $k(t^{1/4})$ and $E_{3-j, -}$ as well as all tree extensions on Component 0 and Component $3-j$ are unramified; the inertia generator in $G$ is a labelled transposition $((1, \dots, 1), (a\ c))$ supported purely on Component $j$ (acting trivially on Component 0 and Component $3-j$). As in [1, Proof of Lemma 6], gauging a spanning tree of simple critical transpositions on Component $j \in \{1, 2\}$ produces a pure copy of $S_5$ in $G$ acting on the 5 blocks of Component $j$ and acting as the identity on Component 0 and Component $3-j$. Now take any test-branch place $t_0 = F_{j, -1}(b)$ ($b \in \mathcal{B}, j \in \{1, 2\}$) and any inertia element $g \in K \le H_0^4 \times H_1^5 \times H_2^5$ above $t_0$. By Theorem 2(2), $F_{j, -1}^{-1}(t_0) \not\subseteq \mathcal{B}$, so at least one root $u_{j, c} \in F_{j, -1}^{-1}(t_0)$ lies outside $\mathcal{B}$ and is unramified in $\widetilde{L}_{j, c}$, meaning the $c$-th coordinate of $g$ on Component $j$ is $1 \in H_j$. Taking the commutator $[g, \tau_{a, c}] \in K$ with the pure transposition $\tau_{a, c} = (a\ c) \in S_5$ of Component $j$ cancels **every** coordinate on Component 0 and Component $3-j$ as well as all coordinates $d \notin \{a, c\}$ on Component $j$, yielding an element in $K$ supported purely in $\{1\}^4 \times H_1^5 \times \{1\}^5$ (for $j = 1$) or $\{1\}^4 \times \{1\}^5 \times H_2^5$ (for $j = 2$) of the form $(h^{-1})^{(a)} h^{(c)}$. Conjugating by the pure $S_5$ action on Component $j$ and ranging over all inertia generators $h \in H_j$ over $b \in \mathcal{B}$ (which generate $H_j$ since $\pi_1^{\mathrm{tame}}(\mathbb{A}^1_k) = \{1\}$) generates a normal subgroup $\{1\}^4 \times N_1 \times N_2 \le K$ that projects surjectively onto $\{1\}^4 \times H_1^{S_1} \times H_2^{S_2} \cong \{1\}^4 \times H_1^4 \times H_2^4$.
2. **Single-Root Kummer Isolation on Component $j = 0$:** Since $\{1\}^4 \times H_1^{S_1} \times H_2^{S_2}$ is already contained in the image of $K$, it suffices to prove that the projection $\pi_0(K) \le H_0^4$ of $K$ onto Component 0 equals the full 4-fold product $H_0^4$. Pass to the rational parameter field $k(u_0)$ where $u_0 = t^{1/4}$ ($t = u_0^4$). Over $k(u_0)$, the four roots of $F_{0, -1}(u) = t$ are $u_{0, r} = i^{r-1}u_0$ ($r = 1, 2, 3, 4$, with $i \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ a fixed primitive fourth root of unity). Fix any $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ and any finite branch point $b \in \mathcal{B}$, and consider the specialization $u_0 \mapsto i^{-(r-1)}b$ (lying above $t_0 = b^4 \in k^\times$). By Theorem 2(1), $t_0 = b^4 \notin \{0, \infty\}$ and $\mathrm{CV}_{1, -}(t_0) \ne 0$, $\mathrm{CV}_{2, -}(t_0) \ne 0$, so $E_{\mathrm{fib}} / k(u_0)$ is unramified at $u_0 = i^{-(r-1)}b$; hence the inertia group of $\widetilde{L}_{V_-} / k(u_0)$ at $u_0 = i^{-(r-1)}b$ is contained in $K = \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{L}_{V_-} / E_{\mathrm{fib}})$. At $u_0 = i^{-(r-1)}b$, the $r$-th root on Component 0 is $u_{0, r} = b \in \mathcal{B}$, while the other three roots on Component 0 are $\{-b, ib, -ib\}$. By Theorem 2(3), $|\{b, -b, ib, -ib\} \cap \mathcal{B}| = 1$, so none of the other three roots $u_{0, s}$ ($s \ne r$) lies in $\mathcal{B}$! Consequently, $\widetilde{L}_{0, s}$ ($s \ne r$) is unramified at $u_0 = i^{-(r-1)}b$, and the projection to $\pi_0(K) \le H_0^4$ of the inertia subgroup at $u_0 = i^{-(r-1)}b$ is supported purely in the $r$-th factor $\{1\}^{r-1} \times H_0 \times \{1\}^{4-r}$. Because the finite inertia subgroups over $b \in \mathcal{B}$ normally generate $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{L}_{0, r} / k(u_0)) = H_0$ ($\pi_1^{\mathrm{tame}}(\mathbb{A}^1_k) = \{1\}$) and $\pi_0(K) \trianglelefteq \mathrm{Gal}(\prod_{r=1}^4 \widetilde{L}_{0, r} / k(u_0)) \le H_0^4$ projects surjectively onto each factor $H_0$, $\pi_0(K)$ contains $\{1\}^{r-1} \times H_0 \times \{1\}^{4-r}$ for every $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, proving $\pi_0(K) = H_0^4$. $\square$

### Lemma 4 (Cross-Direction $L$-Triad Compositum Over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(u_0)$ and Exact $N_{L\text{-triad}}$ Expectation)
*For each branch triple $(j_0, j_1, j_2) \in \{0, 1, 2\}^3$, let $u_0$ be a generic root on branch $j_0$, let $t_+ = F_{j_0, +1}(u_0)$ and $t_- = F_{j_0, -1}(u_0)$, and let*
$$\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j}^m(u_0) = \begin{cases} \mathrm{prim}(\mathrm{CV}_{j, m}(F_{j_0, m}(u_0))) & (j \ne j_0), \\ \mathrm{prim}(\mathrm{CV}_{j_0, m}(F_{j_0, m}(u_0)) / W_{j_0, m, \mathrm{rad}}(u_0)^2) & (j = j_0) \end{cases} \tag{4.14}$$
*be the primitive residual pair-discriminant polynomials of the $M_j^m := N_j - \delta_{j_0, j} \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ non-corner roots $u_{1, a} \ne u_0$ of $F_{j_1, +1}(u_1) = t_+$ ($m = +1$, degrees `[[6,32,32],[15,24,40],[15,40,24]]`) and $u_{2, s} \ne u_0$ of $F_{j_2, -1}(u_2) = t_-$ ($m = -1$, degrees `[[0,32,32],[5,24,40],[5,40,24]]`). Then:*
1. **Residual Pair-Discriminant Coprimality:** *Across all 27 triples $(j_0, j_1, j_2) \in \{0, 1, 2\}^3$, $\gcd(\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j_1}^+(u_0), \widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j_2}^-(u_0)) = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_{65537}[u_0]$ and hence in $\mathbb{Q}[u_0]$ (`verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, Section 3.2).*
2. **Cross-Direction Critical-to-Branch Separation ($F_{j_0, -m}(a) \notin F_{j', -m}(\mathcal{B})$ at All Residual Critical Points):** *All 17 non-constant residual pair-discriminant polynomials $\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j}^m(u_0)$ ($m \in \{+1, -1\}$, $(m, j_0, j) \ne (-1, 0, 0)$) are irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[u_0]$ of degree $d \in \{5, 6, 15, 24, 32, 40\}$, and for every root $a \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ of $\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j}^m(a) = 0$, the opposite-direction value $t_{-m} = F_{j_0, -m}(a)$ generates the full degree-$d$ field $\mathbb{Q}(a)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ ($[\mathbb{Q}(F_{j_0, -m}(a)) : \mathbb{Q}] = d$, certified in Section 3.7(d) of `verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`). Consequently, for every branch $j' \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and every $b \in \mathcal{B}$, $F_{j_0, -m}(a) \ne F_{j', -m}(b)$: at every residual critical point $u_0 = a$ of $E_{j_0, j}^m / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(u_0)$, all tree extensions $\widetilde{L}_{j_0}(u_0)$ and $\widetilde{L}_{j'}(u')$ ($u' \in F_{j', -m}^{-1}(F_{j_0, -m}(u_0))$) on the opposite direction are unramified!*
3. **Full 3-Fold Product $H_{j_0} \times H_{j_1} \times H_{j_2}$ Over $E_{\mathrm{tri}}$:** *Over the residual fiber splitting field $E_{\mathrm{tri}} = E_{j_0, j_1}^+ E_{j_0, j_2}^- / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(u_0)$, for every choice of residual $+1$-neighbor $u_{1, a} \ne u_0$ on branch $j_1$ and residual $-1$-neighbor $u_{2, s} \ne u_0$ on branch $j_2$, the joint Galois group $K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ of $\widetilde{L}_{j_0}(u_0) \widetilde{L}_{j_1}(u_{1, a}) \widetilde{L}_{j_2}(u_{2, s})$ over $E_{\mathrm{tri}}$ surjects onto the full 3-fold product $H_{j_0} \times H_{j_1} \times H_{j_2}$.*
4. **Exact $N_{L\text{-triad}}$ Expectation:** *Consequently, as $p \to \infty$, the asymptotic expectation of $N_{L\text{-triad}}/p$ is*
   $$\frac{N_{L\text{-triad}}}{p} = \begin{cases} 27\left(\frac{\rho}{3}\right)^3 + O(p^{-1/2}) = \rho^3 + O(p^{-1/2}) = 2 f_{2, +}\rho + O(p^{-1/2}) & (p \equiv 3 \bmod 4), \\ (24 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 3)\left(\frac{\rho}{3}\right)^3 + O(p^{-1/2}) = \frac{11}{9}\rho^3 + O(p^{-1/2}) = 2 f_{2, -}\rho + O(p^{-1/2}) & (p \equiv 1 \bmod 4), \end{cases} \tag{4.15}$$
   *where $f_{2, +} = F_{2, +}/p = \frac{1}{2}\rho^2$ and $f_{2, -} = F_{2, -}/p = \frac{11}{18}\rho^2$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$).*

*Proof.*
(1) Verified by exact polynomial arithmetic in $\mathbb{F}_{65537}[u_0]$ in `verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, Section 3.2.

(2) In Section 3.7(d) of `verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, for each of the 17 non-constant triples $(m, j_0, j)$ with degree $d = \deg(\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j}^m) \in \{5, 6, 15, 24, 32, 40\}$, we exhibit an explicit witness prime $p_{\mathrm{wit}}$ ($p_{\mathrm{wit}} \in \{3, 5, 7, 13, 17, 31, 41, 61, 67, 131, 257, 389\}$) such that:
- $\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j}^m(u_0)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{F}_{p_{\mathrm{wit}}}[u_0]$ by Rabin's Frobenius test (and hence irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[u_0]$ of degree $d$); and
- In the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p_{\mathrm{wit}}}[u_0]/(\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j}^m(u_0)) \cong \mathbb{F}_{p_{\mathrm{wit}}^d}$, the opposite-direction element $t_{-m} = F_{j_0, -m}(u_0)$ satisfies $t_{-m}^{p_{\mathrm{wit}}^{d/\ell}} \ne t_{-m}$ for every prime divisor $\ell \mid d$. Since reduction modulo $p_{\mathrm{wit}}$ cannot increase the degree of a generated subfield, $F_{j_0, -m}(a)$ lies in no proper subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(a)$, proving $[\mathbb{Q}(F_{j_0, -m}(a)) : \mathbb{Q}] = d$.

Now suppose for contradiction that $F_{j_0, -m}(a) = F_{j', -m}(b)$ for some $j' \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $b \in \mathcal{B}$. Since $F_{j', -m}(b) \in \mathbb{Q}(b)$, the field degree $d = [\mathbb{Q}(F_{j_0, -m}(a)) : \mathbb{Q}]$ must divide $[\mathbb{Q}(b) : \mathbb{Q}]$. However, every $b \in \mathcal{B}_\infty$ has $[\mathbb{Q}(b) : \mathbb{Q}] \mid 4$, and every $b \in \mathcal{B}_\beta$ has $[\mathbb{Q}(b) : \mathbb{Q}] \mid 12$. For all 16 of the 17 triples with $(m, j_0, j) \ne (+1, 0, 0)$, $d \in \{5, 15, 24, 32, 40\}$ does not divide $12$ ($12 \bmod d \ne 0$), so $F_{j_0, -m}(a) = F_{j', -m}(b)$ is impossible! For the single remaining triple $(m, j_0, j) = (+1, 0, 0)$, $\widetilde{\Delta}_{0, 0}^+(a) = 8a^6 + 20a^3 + 27 = 0$ has $d = 6$, which does not divide $4$ (ruling out $b \in \mathcal{B}_\infty$), and has $v_2(a) = -1/2 \implies v_2(F_{0, -1}(a)) = v_2(a^4) = -2 \ne -8/3$, whereas every $b \in \mathcal{B}_\beta$ has $v_2(F_{j', -1}(b)) = -8/3$ by (4.1a) (ruling out $b \in \mathcal{B}_\beta$). Thus $F_{j_0, -m}(a) \notin F_{j', -m}(\mathcal{B})$ holds unconditionally for all 17 triples!

(3) Work over $k(u_0)$ ($k = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$). Let $\widetilde{L}_{\mathrm{tri}}$ be the compositum over $E_{\mathrm{tri}} = E_{j_0, j_1}^+ E_{j_0, j_2}^-$ of the corner tree $\widetilde{L}_{j_0}(u_0)$, all $M_{j_1}^+ = N_{j_1} - \delta_{j_0, j_1} \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ residual $+1$-trees $\widetilde{L}_{j_1}(u_{1, a})$ ($u_{1, a} \ne u_0$), and all $M_{j_2}^- = N_{j_2} - \delta_{j_0, j_2}$ residual $-1$-trees $\widetilde{L}_{j_2}(u_{2, s})$ ($u_{2, s} \ne u_0$), so that
$$G_{\mathrm{tri}} = \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{L}_{\mathrm{tri}} / k(u_0)) \le H_{j_0} \times \bigl(H_{j_1} \wr S_{M_{j_1}^+}\bigr) \times \bigl(H_{j_2} \wr G_2\bigr), \qquad K_{\mathrm{tri}} = G_{\mathrm{tri}} \cap \bigl(H_{j_0} \times H_{j_1}^{M_{j_1}^+} \times H_{j_2}^{M_{j_2}^-}\bigr), \tag{4.16}$$
where $G_2 = S_{M_{j_2}^-}$ when $j_2 \in \{1, 2\}$, $G_2 = C_4$ when $j_0 \in \{1, 2\}, j_2 = 0$, and $G_2 = \{1\}$ when $j_0 = j_2 = 0$ (since $u_{2, s} \in \{-u_0, iu_0, -iu_0\} \subset k(u_0)$).
- **Step 3a (Isolation of $\{1\} \times H_{j_1} \times \{1\}$ on Block 1 via Morse Commutators):** Because $F_{j_1, +1}$ is a Morse projection, $E_{j_0, j_1}^+ / k(u_0)$ is generated by simple critical transpositions at the roots of $\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j_1}^+(u_0) = 0$. At each root $u_0 = a$ of $\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j_1}^+(a) = 0$, Theorem 2(1) ($a \notin \mathcal{B}$ and $F_{j_0, +1}(a) \notin F_{j_1, +1}(\mathcal{B})$), Part (1) ($\widetilde{\Delta}_{j_0, j_2}^-(a) \ne 0$), and Part (2) ($F_{j_0, -1}(a) \notin F_{j_2, -1}(\mathcal{B})$) prove that $\widetilde{L}_{j_0}(u_0)$, $E_{j_0, j_2}^-$, and **all** residual $-1$-trees $\widetilde{L}_{j_2}(u_{2, s})$ are unramified at $u_0 = a$! Hence the inertia generator at $u_0 = a$ is a labelled transposition in $\{1\} \times (H_{j_1} \wr S_{M_{j_1}^+}) \times \{1\}$ acting trivially on $\widetilde{L}_{j_0}(u_0)$ and on Block 2. Gauging a spanning tree of simple transpositions on Block 1 produces a pure copy of $\{1\} \times S_{M_{j_1}^+} \times \{1\} \le G_{\mathrm{tri}}$. Now specialize $u_0$ to any place where a residual $+1$-root $u_{1, a} \in \mathcal{B}$ is ramified. By the Strong Fiber-Ramification Bound (Theorem 2(2), $|F_{j_1, +1}^{-1}(t_+) \cap \mathcal{B}| \le N_{j_1} - 2$), even after removing $u_0$ when $j_1 = j_0$, at most $N_{j_1} - 2 = M_{j_1}^+ - 1$ of the $M_{j_1}^+$ residual roots lie in $\mathcal{B}$, so **at least one residual $+1$-root $u_{1, c} \ne u_0$ lies outside $\mathcal{B}$ and is unramified**! Taking the commutator $[g, \tau_{a, c}] \in K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ of any inertia element $g \in K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ with the pure transposition $\tau_{a, c} = (a\ c) \in S_{M_{j_1}^+}$ cancels the coordinate on $H_{j_0}$ and all coordinates on Block 2 (`verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, Section 3.7(e)), generating a normal subgroup $\{1\} \times N_1^+ \times \{1\} \le K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ whose projection to $(u_0, u_{1, a}, u_{2, s})$ is $\{1\} \times H_{j_1} \times \{1\}$.
- **Step 3b (Isolation of $\{1\} \times \{1\} \times H_{j_2}$ on Block 2 and $H_{j_0} \times \{1\} \times \{1\}$ on the Corner):** Quotienting by $\{1\} \times N_1^+ \times \{1\}$ (which is already contained in $K_{\mathrm{tri}}$) reduces the problem to proving that the projection $\pi_{0, 2}(K_{\mathrm{tri}}) \le H_{j_0} \times H_{j_2}^{M_{j_2}^-}$ of $K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ onto $(u_0, \{u_{2, s}\})$ surjects onto $H_{j_0} \times H_{j_2}$ for any residual $-1$-root $u_{2, s}$. Notice that $(u_0, \{u_{2, s}\})$ lies entirely on a single fiber $t_- = F_{j_0, -1}(u_0) = F_{j_2, -1}(u_{2, s})$ of the negative direction $V_- = y + x$! By Lemma 3, the geometric Galois kernel $K_{V_-} = \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{L}_{V_-} / E_{\mathrm{fib}})$ over $k(t_-)$ surjects onto $H_0^4 \times H_1^{S_1} \times H_2^{S_2}$ for any 4-element subsets $S_1, S_2 \subset \{1, \dots, 5\}$. Since $E_{\mathrm{tri}} / k(t_-)$ is a finite base extension unramified at all test-branch places $t_- \in F_{j, -1}(\mathcal{B})$ (by Theorem 2(1) and Part (2)), the inertia subgroups of $K_{V_-}$ at $t_- \in F_{j, -1}(\mathcal{B})$ lie in $K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ and generate the exact same geometric image $H_0^4 \times H_1^{S_1} \times H_2^{S_2}$, which restricts surjectively onto $H_{j_0} \times H_{j_2}$ on any distinct pair of roots $(u_0, u_{2, s})$ ($u_{2, s} \ne u_0$) on $V_-$. Combining Step 3a and Step 3b proves that $K_{\mathrm{tri}}$ surjects onto $H_{j_0} \times H_{j_1} \times H_{j_2}$.

(4) Expanding $N_{L\text{-triad}} = \sum_{B \in E_1} (|D_{+1, s_+(B)}| - 1)(|D_{-1, s_-(B)}| - 1)$ over the branch $j_0 \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ of the corner block $B = (u_0, g_{j_0}(Q_4(u_0)))$, the branch $j_1 \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ of the residual $+1$-neighbor $u_1 \ne u_0$, and the branch $j_2 \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ of the residual $-1$-neighbor $u_2 \ne u_0$ gives $27$ branch triples $(j_0, j_1, j_2) \in \{0, 1, 2\}^3$. By Part (3) and the Chebotarev density theorem [5], for each triple $(j_0, j_1, j_2)$ the joint exposure probability of $(u_0, u_1, u_2)$ is $q_{j_0} q_{j_1} q_{j_2} = (\rho/3)^3 + O(p^{-1/2})$. For every pair $(j_0, j_1)$ on $V_+$ and every pair $(j_0, j_2) \ne (0, 0)$ on $V_-$, the average number of rational residual roots $u \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{u_0\}$ of $F_{j, m}(u) = F_{j_0, m}(u_0)$ as $u_0$ varies over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is $1 + O(p^{-1})$. For the pair $(j_0, j_2) = (0, 0)$ on $V_-$ ($u_2^4 = u_0^4$, $u_2 \ne u_0$), each $u_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ has $1$ residual root $u_2 = -u_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ when $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and $3$ residual roots $u_2 \in \{-u_0, iu_0, -iu_0\} \subset \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ when $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$. Summing over all 27 triples (`verify_multiperiod_mobius_q4.py`, Section 3.7(e)) gives $27(\rho/3)^3 = \rho^3 = 2 f_{2, +}\rho$ for $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $(24 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 3)(\rho/3)^3 = \frac{11}{9}\rho^3 = 2 f_{2, -}\rho$ for $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, proving (4.15). $\square$

## 5. Exact Moment Envelopes and Multi-Period Capacity Bounds at $n = 2p, 3p, 4p$

By Lemma 3, Lemma 4, and `no_three_in_line_11` v2 [1] (Theorem 9 and Section 10.2), on $V_+ = y - x$ all three branches have independent exposure up to order $(3, 4, 4)$, giving the asymptotic moments $f_{2, +} = F_{2, +}/p = \frac{1}{2}\rho^2 + o(1)$, $f_{3, +} = F_{3, +}/p = \frac{1}{6}\rho^3 + o(1)$, and $f_{4, +} = F_{4, +}/p \le \frac{q_0^3}{24} + \frac{\rho^4 - q_0^4}{24} + o(1) = \frac{1}{648}\rho^3 + \frac{80}{81}\frac{\rho^4}{24} + o(1)$ (where $\frac{q_0^3}{24} = \frac{1}{648}\rho^3$ bounds $\mathbb{E}[\binom{R_{0, +}}{4}]$ via the deterministic integer inequality $\binom{R_{0, +}}{4} \le \frac{1}{4}\binom{R_{0, +}}{3}$ for $R_{0, +} \le 4$, and $\frac{\rho^4 - q_0^4}{24}$ is the exact expectation of the remaining 4-tuple terms where at most 3 roots lie on Component 0).

On $V_- = y + x$, Lemma 3 establishes that the geometric Galois kernel $K$ surjects onto the full 4-fold product $H_0^4 \times H_1^4 \times H_2^4$, so any shared arithmetic constant-field quotient fixes the Frobenius coset on each factor independently ([1, Sections 10.1 and 13.1]):
1. **Case 1: Primes $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.** Since $-1 \notin (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$, $u \mapsto u^4$ on $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ is 2-to-1 onto $(\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$, where $u^4 - t = (u - u_1)(u + u_1)(u^2 + u_1^2)$ has 2 rational roots $\{u_1, -u_1\}$ on the $1/2$ of fibers $t \in (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$ and 0 rational roots on $t \notin (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$. Hence $R_{0, -}(t) \sim \frac{1}{2}\delta_0 + \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Bin}(2, q_0)$ independently of $R_{1, -}, R_{2, -}$, so $\binom{R_{0, -}}{3} = \binom{R_{0, -}}{4} = 0$ identically, and:
   $$f_{2, -} = \frac{1}{2}q_0^2 + q_0(2q_{12}) + \frac{1}{2}(2q_{12})^2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho^2, \quad f_{3, -} = \frac{13}{81}\rho^3, \quad f_{4, -} = \frac{1}{27}\rho^4 \qquad (p \equiv 3 \bmod 4). \tag{5.1}$$
   Applying Stage 1 on $V_+$ via (2.3) and Stage 2 on $V_-$ via (2.8) with $2 N_{L\text{-triad}}/p = 4 f_{2, +}\rho = 2\rho^3$ (Lemma 4) and $\rho_{\mathrm{mod1}} = 0.07153245$ (`no_three_in_line_53` v1 [4], Theorem 4) yields for $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ (upward-rounded to $10^{-8}$):
