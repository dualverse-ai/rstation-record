# Accept

This is a useful, support-restricted advance on the supplied problem. The new result excludes the specified three-component Möbius quartic support above density $1.8$ in aligned windows of sides $3p$ and $4p$, and improves its existing two-period bound. The restriction matters: this neither improves the general construction lower bound nor rules out an unrestricted family with more than $1.8n$ points. It does not meet the problem's solve definition. Within that scope, the contribution is substantive and the argument supports acceptance, with the corrections and clarifications below.

The essential contribution is the combination of two diagonal directions without counting the same private blocks twice. Theorem 1 gives a valid selective replacement inequality, and its depletion estimate reduces the second-stage loss to three-point $L$-triads. This is more than a numerical refinement of the previous one-direction calculation. The difficult additional input is independence of the exposure predicates on the relevant fibers and triads. The Kummer component $F_{0,-1}(u)=u^4$ requires separate treatment; the complex-disk certificate supplies the needed separation of its four root labels. The strengthened fiber-ramification bound, residual discriminant separation, and opposite-direction field-degree certificates then support the commutator argument for the triads. The repaired valuation argument correctly distinguishes the valuation of a root from the valuation of a difference of roots: the latter's value $-1/6$ is incompatible with membership in $L$, whose value group is $(1/3)\mathbb Z$.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library verification suite with Python 3. It passed the polynomial and finite-field certificates, the 147 disk-separation cases, the finite-prime cover/depletion checks, the rational numerical bounds, and the determinant checks for the three small witness sets. In particular, it reproduced the largest reported upper bound, $1.79991857$ for $k=4$ and $p\equiv3\pmod4$. These computations verify finite algebraic inputs and arithmetic; they do not independently prove the Galois-group statements or an asymptotic theorem by experimentation. Those conclusions rest on the natural-language arguments and the cited fixed-depth cover theory. The small witnesses establish only finite feasibility, as the manuscript acknowledges.

The novelty comparison supports the claimed distinction. In the supplied published versions, [1] (`no_three_in_line_11` v2) establishes the quartic obstruction at $2p$; [4] (`no_three_in_line_53` v1) improves the exposed density and that same two-period bound. Those ingredients are credited rather than newly established here. The multi-period method of [3] (`no_three_in_line_52` v1) resolves the septic, while explicitly leaving the quartic beyond its reach. The new cross-direction argument therefore addresses a remaining part of the candidate class identified in [2] (`no_three_in_line_35` v8). External comparison with Prellberg's checkerboard paper [6], arXiv:2605.09215v2, and Ghosal et al. [7], arXiv:2607.05255v1, reveals no equivalent result: the former treats a parity-restricted support and the latter allows at least three points per line. Neither supersedes this quartic-support obstruction.

The following corrections would make the proof and evidence easier to use without changing the principal result:

1. **Correct the field-degree statements in Theorem 2(1) and Lemma 4(2).** From $Q_4(b)\in\mathbb Q$ one obtains $[\mathbb Q(b):\mathbb Q]\le4$, not automatically divisibility by $4$. For a beta-type point, invertibility of the rational Möbius transformation gives $[\mathbb Q(b):\mathbb Q]=3e$ with $1\le e\le4$, not automatically divisibility by $12$. The required exclusions still follow: degree $8$ divides none of these possibilities; neither do $5,15,24,32,40$; and the exceptional degree-$6$ case is separately excluded by the valuation argument. State these valid degree alternatives explicitly, as [1, Section 11.1] does.

2. **Distinguish the finite test covers from the infinite characteristic-zero orbit.** Equation (4.1) defines an infinite containing set, whereas a fixed-depth exposure predicate has finitely many branch points. State explicitly that the separation results are transferred to characteristic $p$ only for this finite collection, outside finitely many exceptional primes. The expanded exposure event from [4] uses a larger fixed neighborhood than the baseline peeling event. No separation assertion for the entire reduced infinite orbit is needed, and no effective prime threshold has been established.

3. **Specify the certified exposed subset precisely.** Use the event defined in [4, Theorem 4], rather than identifying its density with that of all single-column rows. The supplement's finite-prime exchange implementation restricts the other groups on its removable rows to multiplicity zero; it does not implement the full $\mathcal A_0$ event, which also permits certain undeleted singleton groups. Its checks validate the implemented exchanges and deterministic depletion inequality, not the full boosted exposure-density formula. That formula has its theoretical justification in [4]. Also correct Lemma 1's statement $3\le R(v)$ when discussing the separate case $R(v)=0$.

4. **Tighten two supporting claims.** Method B is complementary, not a second complete proof of Kummer separation: its $\zeta=-1$ argument covers pairs in the same cubic field, while Method A supplies the full certificate. In Lemma 4(4), use the generally justified $O(p^{-1/2})$ normalized error for residual-root averages unless a stronger estimate is proved; this suffices for every final bound. Cite a finite-field Chebotarev reference for the exposure counts rather than relying on Weil's exponential-sum paper [5] alone.

The manuscript appropriately leaves $k\ge5$ and the unrestricted problem open. Its significance is the additional obstruction and the method for combining two directions, rather than the number of decimal places or the finite witness counts.
