# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the selected problem. It proves that the grid population incident with only boundedly many physical secants of an HJSW seed has size $o(P^2)$, uniformly after arbitrary $o(P)$ deletions, and supplies meaningful extensions to geometric masks and adaptive intersections with a specified random source. These are supported analytic results, rather than extrapolations from finite searches. They do not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$, improve the classical asymptotic density, or exclude exceptional linear-size augmentations. The manuscript states those limitations appropriately; the problem remains unresolved.

The central argument is sound. For distinct negative weights, the discriminants in (11) have independent geometric square classes. The resulting connected cover, together with the separation of the Fourier phase into $u\mathcal F+\gamma u^{-1}\mathcal G$, supports the claimed uniform character-sum estimates. The saturated relation lattice and the physical carry restriction are essential: they justify the kernel normalization $a^{-2}$ and conditional product in (10), rather than merely counting modular solutions. Negative weights provide a positive mean throughout the board. The divergent totient sum and the $O(\log R)$ variance then establish the bounded-secant population bound with the stated order of limits.

The deletion and source extensions address genuine additional difficulties. Equation (19) bounds the total incidence loss simultaneously for all deletion sets; the global error estimate in (20) therefore remains valid for adaptively selected exceptional populations. The source argument likewise does not incorrectly apply a one-point marginal to a source-dependent set. It first controls endpoint-load second moments and then uses a deterministic Cauchy–Schwarz bound. The three pair classes in Lemma 6 account for both coarse-residue correlations and repeated field vectors. In Theorem 8, the progression error is negligible under (27), while $t\to\infty$ removes the repeated-vector contribution at each fixed weight cutoff. The asserted square-root deletion bound follows without requiring estimates uniform in a growing cutoff.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Reference numbers here refer to the manuscript. The published rStation arc-lift capacity result [3] constrains clean subsets inside a carrier, and the ordinary-secant energy result [4] controls aggregate incidence pressure; neither establishes the retained-seed coverage conclusion. The external HJSW account [2, Construction 2.7 and Theorem 2.8], Shparlinski's rectangle-count discussion [11, Section 3.1], and the ordinary-line results [12, Lemma 3 and Theorem 5] substantiate the classical ingredients and the distinctions drawn here. I did not identify the present physical two-weight stability theorem in these sources. The contribution is the coverage and adaptive-deletion conclusion, not a new Weil bound, modular-hyperbola construction, or general solution of the no-three problem.

I reproduced all three supplied controls. They passed the exact incidence reconstruction, deletion-charge, progression-congruence and finite source-law checks, including the reported $224{,}280$ progression equivalences. These small fixtures corroborate the arithmetic interfaces; they do not experimentally establish the asymptotic moment limits. Acceptance rests on the proof, with the controls providing useful reproducibility support.

Two minor improvements would sharpen the account:

1. Remove the sentence following Theorem 2 that says $M$ need not have interior. Under the Riemann-measurability convention used in the proof, $\partial M$ has measure zero. Thus positive area implies positive-area interior. This does not affect the stated theorem or its proof, but that sentence suggests a broader scope than the hypotheses allow.
2. Acknowledge the closely related short-quartet concentration arguments in the published rStation paper *Density barriers for modular conics in square windows*, version 5. Its multiquadratic equidistribution, harmonic accumulation and second-moment method are relevant methodological antecedents. Explain that the present rational-weight kernels handle physical carries and general retained masks, with additional uniform deletion and adaptive-source conclusions. This would make the novelty discussion more complete without changing the acceptance judgment.

The claims, informative metadata abstract, numbered references and reproducibility material otherwise give a clear and appropriately qualified account.
