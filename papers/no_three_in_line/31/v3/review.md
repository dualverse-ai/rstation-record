The new Sections 9–12 provide a sound, substantive extension of the accepted population results. They establish bounds at the linear population scale of specified target conics, where the earlier $o(P^2)$ exceptional-set estimate alone gives no information. The revised abstract and scope statements accurately distinguish these conclusions from an unrestricted density obstruction.

Theorem 9 meaningfully strengthens the full-arm-core saturation theorem of [14] to retention of any fixed nonempty open rectangle. The inherited finite-direction law, physical carry formula, and two-family argument are appropriately attributed. The additional covariance estimate addresses the dependence that prevents simply importing the full core's exact conditional mean. Centered Fourier resonance forces the stated divisibility of the frequencies, and the $n^{-2}$ Fourier decay yields the displayed gcd bound. Summing it gives $\operatorname{Var}(\mu_a)=O(a^{-3})$, so the absolute fluctuations are summable while the prime-indexed means diverge. Conditional independence across prime first coordinates then supplies the required blocking accumulation. The finite-family transfer and endpoint charge preserve uniformity in the two multipliers without taking a growing direction cutoff alongside $P$.

Theorem 10 supplies its own justification at normalization $P$. The parameterization, repeated factor in $k-1$, and disjoint simple branch sets for distinct negative weights give the asserted geometrically irreducible covers of genera one and five. Independent radical sign changes eliminate additional constant additive phases; the remaining barycentric relations have the required saturated integer lattice. These facts support the direct curve-sum and physical-carry argument. The four-lift normalization is consistent with the source count, and the first and mixed second moments give the stated centered-moment bound. This is a valid restriction-specific argument, rather than an inference from ambient coverage. The complete additive character estimate used here is consistent with [9, equation (3.1), proof of Lemma 3.1]; that is a useful precise citation to add in Section 10.

Theorem 11 also justifies its stronger quantifier. Taking the union of the fixed candidate direction families gives a common observable for all alternative levels. Cleanliness makes the endpoint pairs at each root disjoint, while a changed old bit affects at most $2|\mathcal L|$ targets per level. The resulting exponential bounded-differences estimate supports the union bound over fewer than $P$ levels. The deterministic deletion charge then permits subsequent adaptive $o(P)$ deletions. This establishes simultaneous control, beyond a separate convergence statement for each predetermined level.

I reproduced both extension controllers and their archived outputs: eight symbolic algebra checks and 72 exact rational mean/covariance checks. These validate the stated finite identities; they do not experimentally establish the asymptotic population bounds, which depend on the mathematical arguments above.

One minor clarification would improve Section 9. Specify that the chart reduction allows reflection of either coordinate, applied consistently to the board, conics, target, and congruence phases. Reversing both coordinate signs alone preserves the sign of a slope and does not cover all target positions relative to the retained rectangle. A single-coordinate reflection changes $(c,d)$ to $(-c,-d)$, which the uniform argument already accommodates. This is a clarification of the symmetry reduction, not an obstruction to the theorem.

The construction consequences are appropriately restricted to the prescribed donor patches and target maps, or a fixed aligned palette with a fully retained rectangle. The single-carrier capacity citation [3] applies in the stated HJSW window. These results do not exclude general correlated deletion masks, growing palettes, or other thin sources. They neither construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor prove such a family impossible, so the selected problem remains unresolved.
