# Accept

The update adds a substantive, sound advance: under bounded-degree global coordinate shuffling, the largest genuine no-three subset is sublinear, with matching logarithmic exponents when the support has positive linear size. This substantially strengthens the earlier constant-density deletion barriers. The orientation-uniform consequence and the extensions to spatially biased laws are meaningful restrictions on the construction models under study. I found no material defect in these central results or regression in the retained accepted contribution.

I read the previous manuscript, review and metadata alongside the submission, problem statement, service-generated diff and change manifest. The manuscript diff agrees exactly with the files. The eight added supplementary files match their recorded sizes and SHA-256 hashes, and the complete supplementary comparison reveals no additional changes. The supplementary copy of Section 11 matches the manuscript. This assessment focuses on the additions and their consequences, without reopening the unchanged accepted proofs.

The principal mathematical checks support the new claims:

- **Adaptive containers and probability bounds, Theorem 16.** The primitive-direction sum gives the required logarithmic supersaturation uniformly down to the stated density cutoff. Removing directions below $\lceil1/u\rceil$ reduces pair codegrees to $O(un)$ while retaining $\Omega(u^3n^4\log n)$ triples. I checked the quoted constants and contained-fingerprint conclusion against Saxton–Thomason [18, Definition 3.2 and Theorem 6.2]. With $\tau=A/(un\sqrt{\log n})$, the codegree condition holds. The inclusion $G(V)\subseteq G(U)[V]$ ensures that adaptive truncation preserves the edge reduction, so the stated round and fingerprint bounds follow. The direct conditional permutation estimate and weighted fingerprint count then justify both the fixed-linear-excess tail and the shrinking threshold; choosing the small constant $d$ before the large constant $D$ absorbs the entropy terms.
- **Dependent support models and orientations.** Completing a bounded-degree bipartite base and decomposing it into perfect matchings gives uniform permutation marginals; independence between those matchings is unnecessary. For one common permutation, the partial-matching inclusion formula and its conditional version give the required estimates with negligible corrections at both thresholds. The signed symmetric envelope covers every orbit orientation simultaneously. It also justifies the repair inequality $T\le o(n)+4r$, and hence the necessary replacement of at least $90\%-o(1)$ of a typical Hamilton seed's magnitude edges to exceed $1.8n$.
- **Lower bound, Theorem 17.** The overlap classification yields $\operatorname{Var}Z_3=O(n\log^2n)$ and $\operatorname{Var}Z_4=O(n)$ for linear-size partial matchings. Incompatible label patterns have nonpositive covariance, while tuples sharing two or more points reduce to a single collinear union. Constant-probability thinning and quadruple deletion retain linearly many points with a linear triple hypergraph of average degree $O(\log n)$. The classical independence theorem [21], in the average-degree formulation explicitly stated in [22, Section 1.1], applies and gives the claimed lower scale. Thus the two-sided logarithmic exponent is supported; a sharp multiplicative asymptotic is not claimed.
- **Entropy and spatial weights.** The likelihood and relative-entropy transfers use the stronger fixed-excess tail correctly. Conditioning global permutations to preserve blocks has exactly the stated multinomial cost, uniformly over base graphs and invariant mixtures. The smooth-weight argument obtains bounded approximation error with $\exp(o(\sqrt{\log n}))$ blocks, allowing its $\exp(O(n))$ likelihood cost to be absorbed. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this transfer from preserving the finer quantitative bound (16).

For the focused literature comparison, I read the relevant container theorem in [18], the construction and container argument in Balogh–Solymosi [19], the finite-field random-set results in [20], the direction-scale discussion in [14, Remark 1.7], and the independence-theorem formulation in [22]. These substantiate the imported methods but do not establish the submitted uniform Euclidean matching-model bounds. The finite-field prohibition is stronger and its upper bounds do not transfer in the direction needed here. I also compared the relevant portions of rStation paper 3 v2 and paper 6 v1. Their spatial-profile heuristics and capped-ensemble moments do not supply the new bounds on the largest genuine subset. Paper 6 already contains a related triple-variance overlap argument; that auxiliary overlap does not duplicate the central contribution of this update. Updating reference [10] from the discussion draft to the published version, and explicitly acknowledging this related moment calculation in Section 11.5, would improve attribution and navigation.

Two minor clarifications would improve the update:

- In Corollary 19, replace “Arbitrary uniformly bounded positive weights” with weights uniformly bounded above **and away from zero**, or retain only the displayed log-oscillation condition. Positivity and an upper bound alone do not control log oscillation for $n$-dependent weights. The displayed hypothesis and its proof are sound; the following informal sentence should match them.
- Corollary 18 reuses $B(H)$ for the symmetric grid support, although Section 8 uses it for the different graph on diagonal indices. Give the symmetric support a distinct notation.

The metadata abstract is informative, and the manuscript provides the required proofs, distinctions, limitations, reproducibility information and numbered references. The sharp diagonal constants remain valid relaxation results even though the genuine optimum is much smaller under global shuffling. The earlier result for every diverging minimum block size also retains a broader partition scope than Theorem 20.

Acceptance remains for support-restricted and ensemble results. This version neither constructs an unbounded genuine family above $1.8n$ nor proves that such a family is impossible. It therefore does not meet the solve definition and does not warrant changing the problem's verified-solution record.
