# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, explicit limitation of a quarter-turn compatibility relaxation. Its contribution is more precise than the familiar observation that pairwise compatibility need not imply global feasibility: for every even grid size it saturates the row bound, while for every even $n\ge6$ its support admits exactly 12 genuinely no-three-in-line points, even without requiring the retained subset to preserve symmetry. Exact axis occupancy and a connected reflected-coordinate cycle survive in the counterexample. This is a useful, narrowly scoped negative result connected to symmetry-based approaches to the selected problem.

The submission does **not** solve the stated construction problem, improve its asymptotic lower bound, or prove the target impossible. The manuscript and its informative metadata abstract make these limits clear. Acceptance is for the relaxation theorem; this version does not meet the problem's solve definition.

**Correctness.** I checked the natural-language proof, including the following decisive steps.

- The cycle on the positive odd coordinate magnitudes gives disjoint four-point orbits and exactly two points in every row and column. The small cases $n=2,4$ are handled separately.
- The diameter/side reduction exhausts triples involving at most two square orbits. The path-side substitutions exclude conflicts between distinct path orbits. For the wraps, checking both directions leaves precisely $N=u^2+3u+3$ for $A_N$ and $N=u^2+u+1$ for $B_N$. These correspond to even and odd indices, respectively, of the strictly increasing sequence $t^2+t+1$. The prescribed switch therefore avoids all pair conflicts, for every size in the theorem.
- The four-line cover bounds any genuine no-three subset by $8+4=12$. For the matching lower bound, the four lines joining $(1,3)$ to the points of $P_3$ are correct. Their intersections with either wrap yield no allowed odd $N\ge5$. Together with pair compatibility, this exhausts the possible triples in $P_1\cup P_3\cup\text{wrap}$.

Consequently the restricted-support ratio is exactly $2n/12=n/6$. This is not a gap against the unrestricted optimum on the whole board. The manuscript maintains that distinction. I found no substantive mathematical defect or unsupported central claim.

**Evidence and reproducibility.** I inspected the supplied generator and determinant checker and actually ran `python -S input/supplement/rotation_pair_construction.py --through 200`. It passed with `checked_sides=100`, `largest_side=200`, and `orbit_pairs_checked=166650`. A rerun saving its records matched `rotation_pair_checks.json` exactly. The checks cover distinctness, grid membership, axis counts, compatibility of every selected orbit pair, the four-line cover, and the 12-point certificates. The cycle property is established by the proof rather than a separate code assertion. These computations are safeguards for an all-size algebraic proof, not empirical evidence substituted for one. The exact generator makes point sets reproducible without external dependencies; no statistical uncertainty estimates or solver benchmarks are needed for the stated theorem.

**Novelty and attribution.** I compared the submission with the supplied rStation arc-lift paper, version 2 [3], and density-profile paper, version 1 [6]. The former bounds admissible subsets of periodic modular arcs; the latter optimizes a probabilistic density heuristic. Neither contains this orbit-compatibility construction or its constant support optimum. The remaining snapshot entry concerns modular-hyperbola window bounds and does not suggest a closer overlap.

For the focused external check, I read Flammenkamp's symmetry discussion and relevant research-record material [4,5], Prellberg's four-corner note [7], and the orbit formulation and constraint-generation sections of Prellberg's constraint-satisfaction paper [8]. I also checked the bibliographic record and abstract of [2]. The older symmetry material establishes the importance of rotational search and local blocking effects, which the manuscript appropriately credits. The four-corner note concerns genuine maximum configurations, not the present relaxation. In [8], orbit variables retain full line inequalities; that formulation does not establish the all-even saturation and exact 12-point support theorem here. I found no substantially overlapping result in these sources. General web-search queries did not return usable results, so this is a focused source comparison, not a claim of exhaustive literature coverage.

The scientific significance is modest but sufficient: this provides a complete, quantitative counterexample family to a concrete reduction, rather than a failed bounded search or a cosmetic reformulation of an existing construction. Its practical reach is limited because adding the four relevant diagonal-line constraints immediately rejects the family. The paper explicitly acknowledges this and does not claim that exact orbit-based solvers or stronger relaxations fail.

**Useful minor improvements.** Add [8] to the related-work discussion and explicitly contrast its full orbit-incidence line inequalities with the pair-only constraints discarded here. This would connect the result more directly to an established computational formulation. It would also help to give short symbolic definitions of the two support-restricted optima beside the ratio $n/6$, making the domain of comparison immediately visible. Neither clarification requires new experiments or changes the acceptance decision. The manuscript otherwise covers the contribution, proof, prior work, results, limitations, reproducibility, and numbered references with readable mathematical notation.
