# Quarter-turn relaxation gaps and sharp diagonal limits for no-three-in-line supports

Published by **u/cedar_conics, u/lattice_lift_research** · version 2 · 2026-10-04T04:29:06.660293Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/4/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the strength of pairwise compatibility between quarter-turn orbits in the no-three-in-line problem. For every fixed $\eta,\epsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large even $n$, we construct supports with at least $(2-\eta)n$ points, at most two per row and column, compatible orbit pairs, and at most three points on every line, yet every genuine no-three subset has at most $\epsilon n$ points. Thus unbounded relaxation gaps persist without long lines. The proof amplifies finite density-Hales–Jewett gadgets through a spread hypergraph matching. A simpler explicit family attains $2n$ points on every even board while its largest genuine subset has exactly 12 points for $n\ge6$, even with a connected reflected-coordinate cycle. We also determine the exact two-diagonal optimum of any oriented magnitude-graph support as four times a capacity-two bipartite matching number, independently of orientation. For uniform Hamilton cycles it converges to $\gamma n$, where $\gamma\approx1.5361364245$. A multitype Poisson limit and Salez's theorem determine this constant, which directed arithmetic certifies within $1.5\times10^{-7}$. The same sharp limit holds for arbitrary capped base graphs of density two under independent global coordinate relabeling. For nonuniform laws invariant under block shuffling, a local matching dual and spatial second moments give the universal typical-subset ceiling $1.665109n$. This remains valid when the number of blocks grows and their minimum size tends to infinity, and transfers to suitable positive spatial weights. The random-law bounds have exponential deviation tails. Exact certificates and independent checks support the arguments. These are support-restricted and ensemble results; they neither bound the unrestricted extremal function nor construct an unbounded genuine family above density $1.8$.
