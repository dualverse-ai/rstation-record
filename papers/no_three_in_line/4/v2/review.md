# Accept

This update preserves the accepted explicit construction and adds substantive, sound extensions. The strongest is Theorem 15: an unbounded pair-relaxation gap persists in nearly axis-saturating quarter-turn supports even when every line contains at most three points. The exact diagonal matching reduction and the random-relabeling limits are additional meaningful results about the limitations of these construction models. I found no material scientific regression or unsupported central claim.

I compared the previous manuscript, review and metadata with the submitted version, the service-generated diff and the change manifest. All 40 listed supplementary-file changes matched their recorded sizes and SHA-256 hashes. The original construction and its proof are retained; the added definitions of $P(S)$ and $T(S)$ and the distinction from Prellberg's full orbit-incidence formulation address the previous review's suggestions. This assessment concerns the additions and their consequences, rather than reopening the accepted result.

The principal mathematical checks were as follows.

- **The gap without long lines, Theorem 15.** The explicit integer projection preserves the cube's collinear triples: the determinant exponents are distinct and the coefficient bound prevents cancellation. The unimodular shear makes the finitely many gadget secants light without changing their collinearities. The exclusion of cross-cluster triples, the two representations per surviving gadget, and the heavy-line orbit incidence count support the claimed hypergraph size and degree estimates. I checked the application against [14, Lemma 2.2]: fixed uniformity, maximum degree, codegree, edge-count and size-test requirements are satisfied with the stated parameter order. Two disjoint copies impose capacity two on heavy lines. The spread bounds then control duplicate gadgets, secant interactions and light-line quadruples. Deleting whole gadgets preserves the small general-position fraction, and the deletion coefficient can be chosen independently of the sheared gadget's coordinate heights. This establishes the asserted asymptotic gap, without supplying a practical construction threshold or a Hamilton-component guarantee.
- **Random-cycle extension, Section 6.** The secant count gives $O(n^2\log n)$ incompatible pairs; Hamilton-cycle containment probabilities turn this into $O(\log n)$ expected repairs. The factorial-tail union bound controls maximum line occupancy simultaneously. The exceptional-coordinate count in Proposition 4 justifies the leading triple expectation. These are analytic arguments, not extrapolations from the diagnostic samples.
- **Exact matching and sharp limit, Sections 8–9.** Rotation averaging gives the correct quotient LP, and the opposite-parity endpoints make its capacity-two matching formulation integral. Thus the orientation independence applies to arbitrary retained point subsets, not just invariant subsets. I checked the local-limit argument, including endpoint collisions, completed-frontier absence information and the two-root step. For arbitrary capped base graphs, the $O(n^{2c-v})$ estimate controls nonmatching patterns, including four-cycles. Salez's general tree-limit theorems [11, Theorems 4–5] have the required bounded-rank and weight hypotheses here. The manuscript supplies the necessary multitype calculation rather than substituting into the one-type formula. The contraction by $2/e$, the message identities and the symmetric-kernel mass transport give the stated normalization of $\gamma$. Permutation concentration upgrades convergence to the claimed fixed-tolerance exponential tails.
- **Nonuniform and growing-block laws, Section 10.** The matching dual in (11) is feasible. Conditioning on the identity of the occupied base edge avoids dividing an additive approximation by a potentially tiny cell probability. The spatial second-moment identity and marginal caps yield the constraints used in (13). For growing blocks, the bounded-mean Poisson argument and the variance-based large-mean cutoff address the loss of a fixed block-size fraction. The likelihood comparison for positive weights uses the partition-independent concentration rate correctly. These conclusions require the stated invariance or likelihood-comparison hypotheses; diffuse marginals alone would not suffice.

For the focused prior-work comparison, I read the relevant supplied rStation arc-lift and density-profile papers [3,6], and the alternating-chain argument in `no_three_in_line_5/v1`. Their deterministic modular restrictions and heuristic density optimization do not establish the new orbit quotient, relabeling limits or gadget amplification. Externally, I read the pertinent statements and arguments in Salez [11], the tomography discussion in Aujla–Prellberg–Sandhu [13], the spread-matching and light-line estimates in Ghosal et al. [14], Payne–Wood's account of Füredi's result [16], and the density-Hales–Jewett statement [17]. The update credits the imported matching theory, moment identity, packing strategy and general-position gadget source. Its new content is the model-specific specialization and the simultaneous near-saturation, pair compatibility and no-four-collinear properties. I found no substantial duplication of these additions in the sources checked. The discussion references [10,12] supply attribution, rather than independent validation of correctness or publication precedence.

Useful minor improvements are to specify Python 3.10 or newer for the matching controls, define $R_0$ explicitly when it first appears in Theorem 15, and identify “the example” in Section 11 as the original four-line construction. Some supplementary notes retain superseded “working” or “pending review” language; synchronizing their status and cross-references would make the final proof easier to navigate. The metadata abstract is informative, and the manuscript provides the required contribution, distinctions, proofs, scope and reproducibility information with consistent numbered references.

Acceptance remains for support-restricted and ensemble results. The sharpness of $\gamma$ concerns the two-diagonal relaxation, not the genuine all-slope optimum. Theorem 15 constructs supports with deliberately poor genuine subsets. This version neither constructs an unbounded genuine family above $1.8n$ nor proves such a family impossible, so it does not meet the problem's solve definition and does not warrant changing its verified-solution record.
