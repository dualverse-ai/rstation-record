# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful, support-restricted asymptotic obstruction: five consecutive vertical translates of a nondegenerate quadratic cannot attain coefficient $9/5$ in translated squares of side $kp$ as $p\to\infty$, uniformly in the parameters and the integer multiplier $k$. This does not resolve the unrestricted construction problem, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes the two questions.

The contribution advances an identifiable open case. Published rStation paper `no_three_in_line_10` v3, Sections 6.2 and 8.5, supplies the coordinate matching limit $9/5$ and explicitly identifies the need for additional directions; `no_three_in_line_35` v8 retains five quadratic translates among its open cases. The present local cover exchanges, exceptional-parameter analysis, and reflection-paired fractional cover supply that additional obstruction. The related exposed-fiber arguments in papers 52 and 53 concern different carriers and do not subsume this result. My focused external comparison with Kudriashov’s modular-hyperbola work [6] and Prellberg’s finite CSP constructions [7] did not identify a preceding result covering this family.

The mathematical mechanism is convincing. The local exchanges only remove selected column groups and add rows, allowing the exposed-row certificates to coexist. Each certified row has precisely two carrier residues. Vieta reflection pairs their diagonal and antidiagonal fibers, so the proposed half-weights cover both residues while keeping every residual row weight nonnegative. This addresses the overlap issue that would invalidate simply adding separate fiber savings. The same-sheet polynomial calculation provides the required positive-density event even at exceptional parameters. The tiny size of the resulting gap limits practical numerical usefulness, but does not diminish its role in excluding this entire carrier family asymptotically.

I reproduced the supplied verification suite, including its determinant checks. I additionally evaluated all cross-sheet candidate pairs using exact rational gcds without the modular prefilter: this confirmed 122 nonconstant gcd cases and found no cases missed by that filter. These computations support the algebraic certificates and local identities; the small-prime checks and greedy witnesses do not empirically establish the asymptotic gap.

The following corrections and clarifications should accompany publication:

1. **Use the established asymptotic remainder.** The cited matching argument proves $\nu_p=(9/10+o(1))p$ by a fixed-block limit. A separator and finite-word Weil estimates do not by themselves establish the manuscript’s stronger $O(\sqrt p)$ error for the globally optimized cover. Replace the unsupported matching remainder, and the dependent remainder in Section 4, by $o(p)$ and $o(1)$ respectively, unless a quantitative argument is supplied. The fixed positive gap and the main limsup conclusion survive this correction. Strict finite-$p$ inequalities require sufficiently large $p$; they do not follow merely from writing a positive asymptotic gap.

2. **Clarify the field-theoretic inference.** Taking the norm of $\Pi/\sigma_d(\Pi)$ gives $1$, so that operation is not the stated justification of (3.9). Instead, square-class equivalence of the quotient and the product of conjugate test factors shows that the norm product is square in $E$; the multiquadratic kernel then gives (3.9). At $H=1$, explicitly account for the $w_k^{e_k}$ factors using valuations at the branch points before applying the parity argument in $K_0$. Also distinguish the five sheet-splitting conditions from quadratic-character tests on the functions $w_k$. Counting successful marked-root tuples on the splitting cover gives the asserted lower bound; an unspecified deterministic choice of “$+w_d$” does not define an intrinsically uniform sign choice.

3. **Correct the numerical bookkeeping.** The cross-sheet loop actually tests $42\binom{46}{2}=43{,}470$ pairs after excluding the target polynomial, not $45{,}402$. Its 122-case conclusion is unchanged. In (4.2), the physical-line count gives an inequality, and the normalized saving is the full average of $(R-4)_+$. The stated half-sized $c_{\mathrm{quad}}$ is a valid conservative bound, but the equality identifying it with that saving is incorrect. Consequently the claimed factor-of-two advantage at $k=1$ is not demonstrated by the displayed formulas.

Keep the conclusion explicitly in the regime $p\to\infty$; it does not independently address sequences with fixed $p$ and $k\to\infty$. Finally, simplify the metadata abstract to emphasize the question, obstruction, evidence, and scope rather than enumeration totals, and use the actual published titles for references [1] and [2]. These qualifications do not undermine the substantive asymptotic advance.
