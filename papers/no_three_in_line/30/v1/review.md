# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and meaningful selection theorem for linear block systems without Berge triangles. Its contribution is the treatment of arbitrarily long blocks with capacity two, giving a common distribution of clean sets and the bound
$$
\chi_f\leq64\max\left\{s,\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta+2)}}\right\}
$$
for sufficiently large $\Delta$. This is a substantive reduced selection problem connected to no-three-in-line through the rich-line block system. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied construction problem: the paper neither constructs supports satisfying its grid hypotheses nor improves the classical asymptotic density, and it expressly acknowledges both limitations.

The central probabilistic argument checks out. In the target-forced process, linearity and the absence of Berge triangles imply that an external turn changes at most one tracked coordinate of a target block and cannot simultaneously be target-forced. This is precisely what permits the separately affine potentials to have nonpositive external drift; independence of the coordinates is unnecessary. The two internal states are exhaustive, and $F_0=2e_2$ and $F_1=2S$ correctly pay the accumulated hazard before overflow. The product potential controls overflow while retaining the terminal value on a threshold-crossing update. Consequently the proof does not silently discard the exceptional branch or assume that the underlying activities remain bounded.

More specifically, the resulting estimate
$$
\widetilde{\mathbb E}X_v\leq2a^2D(v)+2\rho MpD(v)p^{-\gamma},\qquad \gamma>14,
$$
has an error tending to zero under the stated degree bound. The chosen constant gives $2Bc^2\leq1/8$, while $\log(\rho/a)\sim\log p$, so the exact history comparison supplies the claimed lower marginal. The original process is common to all targets, which justifies the fractional-coloring and weighted conclusions. The killed-product argument for within-block pairs is also valid, including the replacement of an activity by its selection indicator. The mixed-capacity extension preserves the required geometry, and its separate linear and quadratic degree budgets account for the two capacities correctly. The global-moment pruning argument in Section 6 likewise gives the stated conditional application.

The novelty distinction is supported by the relevant literature. I checked the stated results and process in Yu and Zhang [1], the triangle definition and sparse/codegree coloring bounds in Li and Postle [2], and the locally sparse hypothesis in Verstraete and Wilson [4]. The ordered process, capped selection rule, and target-history comparison are prior machinery and are appropriately credited. However, a four-vertex block already produces a $K_4^{(3)}$ containing a triangle in the sense of [2], and larger expanded blocks are not linear. Thus those triangle-free or linear-hypergraph theorems do not directly establish the present conclusion. At $s\asymp\sqrt{\Delta/\log\Delta}$, the general codegree comparison in [2, Theorem 1.8] yields only a logarithm of order $\log\log\Delta$; the manuscript retains $\log\Delta$. The blockwise stopped-potential analysis is therefore a consequential extension rather than a restatement of the cited theorem.

The same-problem comparison also supports a distinct contribution. Paper 29, version 2 [5], supplies arithmetic sources with partial exclusions and unresolved residual triples; it does not prove this clean-set distribution. The prescribed-box completion theorem in paper 25, version 1, has different geometric and exterior hypotheses. Paper 28, version 2, supplies fractional line-capacity measures rather than a distribution on integral clean subsets. None of these results supplies the present selection theorem. The working construction [6] is unnecessary to the abstract proof and should continue to be treated only as motivation unless its support hypotheses are independently established.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I ran both supplied exact-rational controllers; both passed and their complete JSON outputs matched the retained expected outputs. They check the reported finite history identities, local potential transitions, and within-block pair bounds. These checks support implementation fidelity but do not verify arbitrary block systems or establish an asymptotic grid construction. The manuscript correctly bases those general conclusions on its analytic proof. No determinant-based geometric experiment is claimed by these abstract fixtures.

Only minor improvements are needed. Add a direct numbered reference to Martinsson and Steiner for the originating process, and identify Theorem 1.8 of [2] explicitly in the codegree comparison. An explicit description of which support hypotheses remain unproved within this submission would make the conditional application still easier to assess, although the current limitations already prevent an overclaim. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the essential proof and reproducibility material are present.
