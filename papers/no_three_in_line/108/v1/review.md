# Accept

The manuscript establishes meaningful advances on geometric domination and maximal no-three-in-line sets. The cardinality-dependent secant estimate is the main contribution: it yields a polynomial separation between unrestricted domination and domination in general position, and permits arbitrary prescribed caps slightly beyond the two-thirds exponent in classical-density extensions. The separate construction of maximal caps with a uniform linear deficit is also substantive. These results concern the ordinary integer grid and are relevant to the selected problem through maximality and extension barriers. They do not construct an unbounded family above $1.8N$, exclude such a family, or improve the unrestricted constructive density beyond $3/2$; the manuscript states these limits correctly.

The central proof is convincing. Selecting one representative pair per determined line preserves the distinct-direction property needed for the energy estimate, including for supports with collinear subsets. The two-stage treatment of heavy residue classes avoids circularity: Case I excludes heavy classes by the occupancy bound, and its uniform estimate applies to the rescaled classes in Case II. The divisor-count charge in (2) has a strict exponent margin below $3/2-1/200000$. After removal, the conditional overlap estimate produces the required large least common multiple. The interpolation bound is smaller than that least common multiple, and both the nonlinear-component bound and the separate divisor argument for line components contradict the number of good common neighbors. I checked the stated exponent inequalities and the identity $c=2/899997$. The domination and prescribed-extension consequences follow with the stated hypotheses; in particular, the mixed-triple calculation treats repeated residues separately rather than assuming injectivity modulo the prime.

The other two arguments also support their claims. The rational-approximation construction supplies two distinct selected lattice points on a line through every target, while its cross-shaped support has the advertised size. In Section 6, the split hyperbola construction accounts for the possible repeated-residue triples and bounds the union of repairs. The two block-incidence inequalities then imply

$$
2\Delta_1+\Delta_2\ge 2K-\alpha N-O(1),
$$

so the smaller completion has the claimed deficit. Padding from $4p$ to arbitrary sufficiently large sides costs only $o(N)$. The resulting upper coefficient is approximately $1.97005224$, consistent with the stated $1.971$ bound.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Aichholzer, Eppstein and Hainzl [1], including Section 6 of the publicly available paper, give the earlier bounds and ask both questions addressed here. Paper 27, version 2 [2], supplies the interpolation strategy and a board-side energy saving, but explicitly does not improve the domination exponent in this sparse regime. The heavy-class rescaling and cardinality-dependent conclusion are a substantive extension. Paper 107, version 1 [7], obtains extensible classical-density subsequences using additional energy control on its frozen sets; it does not give the present arbitrary-seed guarantee. The external checkerboard papers [8,9] establish the constant and its finite-grid transfer, and [8] already gives the doubled-cap obstruction. Those ingredients are credited; the split seed and two-completion argument add the uniform bound for minimum maximal caps. The focused literature check did not identify a publication already establishing these combined advances.

The supplied computational evidence is reproducible. I ran both verifiers successfully: the domination checks cover every target at $N=101,128$, and the larger case checks its arithmetic count and 337 sampled targets. The split verifier confirms the four completed caps, all 6,732 omitted-site secant witnesses, and the larger seeds at $p=31,101$. These are finite checks of the constructions, not experimental verification of the asymptotic energy theorem; that theorem rests on the mathematical argument.

The paper communicates its contribution and proof strategy clearly, distinguishes minimum maximal size from maximum achievable density, and keeps detailed finite records in the supplement. Minor improvements would help: explicitly include $S\subseteq G_N$ in Theorem 2; cite the precise version and Theorem 5.3 of [9] when invoking continuum-to-discrete transfer; and clarify that the covering generator currently rejects unfitted small boards whereas the mathematical construction uses the whole board there. A diagram of the two block partitions in Section 6 would aid readers, but is not necessary for understanding or validity. None of these points requires changing the scientific conclusions.
