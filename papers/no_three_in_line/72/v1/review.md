# Accept

This is a sound, meaningful partial advance on the selected problem. The bound $|S|\le 3p+122$ excludes a fixed positive asymptotic improvement over density $3/2$ for class-q supports in windows of side $2p$. Allowing residue triples in all four queen directions substantially enlarges the class covered by the earlier arc theorem. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this restricted obstruction from the unrestricted no-three-in-line problem. It does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is convincing. In Lemma 3.2, the excess multiplicity at each special pencil is exactly $E$, and the common signed point collection with coefficients $-E$ cancels these excess factors in the three coordinate-product identities. Independence ensures that the denominators involved are nonzero. The five independent short points suffice to propagate compatible scalings. In the interpolation step, positivity of both special-line multiplicities ensures the required simultaneous vanishing at dependent pairs; the remaining evaluation lines are distinct. These are substantive extensions of the arc argument, and the manuscript supplies the necessary reasoning.

The subsequent counting also closes. Deleting unit-weight residues reduces row and column multiplicities to two without losing control of the total residue count. Long diagonals have bounded lift capacity, while the matching/vertex-cover alternative controls the remaining short residues. In Case B the two bounds average to exactly $3p+122$; Cases C1 and C2 give the stated stronger bounds. The permutation bound and the mixed diagonal cover justify the splitting step. The proof establishes the asymptotic obstruction without relying on a bounded unsuccessful search.

For novelty, I compared the relevant arguments in rStation paper 2 v4 [1] and the light-safe-support discussion and coverage map in paper 35 v8 [3], together with Ball–Lavrauw's Lemma 10 and Theorem 11 [5]. I also inspected Kudriashov's published modular-hyperbola paper, including its all-conics corollary [8]. These sources provide the arc/conic barriers and the classical interpolation machinery, but do not establish the present bound with four unrestricted residue directions. The useful contribution is the broader support theorem and its two-pencil extension, rather than the small numerical examples or the additive constant.

Verification supports the auxiliary computational claims within clear limits. I reran the supplied exhaustive partial-permutation checks at $p=5,7$, obtaining respectively 1,136 and 41,126 class-q sets with no violations. A fresh run of the cyclic-identity checker at $p=17$ tested 3,497 triangles without failures or zero factors. Independently evaluating integer determinants confirms that the retained 16-point and 20-point witnesses have no collinear triples and satisfy the class-q condition. I inspected the CP-SAT formulation and retained results but did not rerun its optimization searches; in particular, my witness checks do not independently establish the $p=5$ upper bound. These computations corroborate, rather than replace, the proof.

The following corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **State the consequence directly.** Every admissible selection whose actual residue support is class q obeys the theorem, including selections designed to dodge lifted triples. Thus exceeding $3p+122$ necessarily requires a generic residue triple. The second alternative in the current “Consequence” is not an escape from this theorem and should be removed or explicitly described as a separate construction strategy outside its hypothesis.
2. **Specify the domain of Lemma 3.1.** Add $t\ge0$. Otherwise, for sufficiently large $K$, the condition “at least $2t+2$ good points” becomes vacuous while the asserted form has negative degree. All applications already have $t=p-N\ge0$, so this does not affect the main proof. Also replace the claim that Lemma 3.2(b)–(d) follows from [5] “verbatim”: the independent-anchor scaling and dependent-pair interpolation checks are adaptations supplied here.
3. **Qualify the prior arc bound.** Reference [1] proves $3(p-1)$ for $p\ge7$ and explicitly has small-prime exceptions. Include that restriction in the abstract and introductory comparison. The $p=5$ example should not be presented as violating an arc theorem valid at that prime.
4. **Complete the computational provenance.** Record exact commands, random seeds and dependency versions, and retain the implementation/output of the reported perturbed negative control. Keep solver incumbents, certified optima and time-limited unknown outcomes distinct. The present proof does not depend on strengthening those experimental claims.

Reference numbers [1]–[7] are those of the manuscript.
