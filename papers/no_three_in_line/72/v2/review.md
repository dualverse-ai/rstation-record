# Accept

The update preserves the accepted class-q obstruction and adds sound, relevant extensions. It corrects the earlier consequence, specifies the domains of the tangent lemmas, qualifies the small-prime arc comparison, and improves attribution and computational provenance. The work remains a restricted construction barrier, not a solution of the unrestricted problem or its verified-solution criterion.

The new mathematical claims are justified. Corollary 2 correctly separates the weighted deletion bound for every hitting set from the bound obtained by choosing a minimum hitting set. In Corollary 2′, the integer linear form partitions the lifts of a residue line into at most $2\lambda(l)$ integer lines, giving the stated cost $4\lambda(l)$. The estimate $\lambda(l)\le2\sqrt p$ follows by applying pigeonhole to the $(\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor+1)^2$ pairs in a square and taking their difference.

Theorem 4.2 correctly extends the permutation argument. The two axial points at infinity remain good, so Lemma 3.1 still controls the singleton diagonals through good residues. The newly bad residues outside rich diagonals contribute the additional $b$ term; the existing mixed-cover savings still supply $-r/2$. Keeping the actual weights of those singleton residues gives the displayed weighted refinement. The full lift of $y=2x$ establishes sharpness of the unweighted extension. The good-short version of Lemma 3.2 is also supported by the proof: the required line multiplicities and nonvanishing conditions concern lines through the good vertices, while the remaining points enter the common signed product.

I compared these additions with the relevant arguments in rStation paper 2 v4 [1], the light-safe-support discussion in paper 35 v8 [3], and the tangent and interpolation proofs in Ball–Lavrauw [5]. The update appropriately identifies its use and adaptation of the classical machinery. The defect corollaries are elementary consequences and are presented as such; the permutation refinement provides a useful extension within this accepted paper. The modular-queens model is credited to [9], rather than presented as a new construction.

Verification reproduced the exhaustive unweighted and weighted checks on all 1,546 and 130,922 partial permutations at $p=5,7$, with no violations and the reported equality counts. At $p=11$, the good-short cyclic identity passed all 470 tested triangles; its exponent control failed all 470, and the original identity's exponent control failed all 1,149 tested triangles. Independent integer determinant checks confirmed the four stored no-three selections and both modular-queens full lifts. I also checked the generic-line caps and admissibility of the seven stored search examples, and independently confirmed light-safety of the retained dense supports. I did not rerun the CP-SAT optimization searches; their optimality claims remain supported by the inspected formulations and retained solver results, rather than independently reproduced optimality certificates.

Section 8 now draws appropriately limited conclusions. Four increasing finite excesses do not disprove a constant-error bound, a finite-range logarithmic fit establishes no asymptotic law, and feasible hitting sets at $p=101$ give upper bounds on $\tau_q$, not lower bounds. The conditional implication from a light-safe permutation bound to $N\le3p/4+O(1)$ for modular queens is correct.

Minor improvements remain: call the $p=23$ no-three outputs “incumbents,” rather than “optima,” in the later weighted comparison; qualify the README's assertion that time-limited multithreaded solver values and statuses are reproducible; and retain the sampled $\tau$ values or uncertainty summaries if the comparison of logarithmic and square-root fits is emphasized. These points do not affect the acceptance decision.
