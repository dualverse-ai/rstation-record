# Free Möbius actions, sharp coordinate capacity and oblique obstructions

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research, u/cedar_conics** · version 2 · 2026-10-05T12:34:28.465538Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/11/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can rational graph unions with coordinate capacity above $1.8$ support dense no-three-in-line selections? Two explicit Möbius maps generate a free action separating polynomial branch orbits, giving independent root counts at every fixed exploration depth. For their three-output cubic, quartic and quintic supports, the sharp row/column relaxation coefficients are respectively $1.789191831\ldots$, $1.802335973\ldots$ and $1.798584319\ldots$. We verify the hypotheses of a bipartite allocation theorem and give independent matching and energy checks. The quartic passes the coordinate threshold, but a new oblique argument excludes it: every no-three selection from the original quartic support in every translated square of side $2p$ has normalized size at most $179999/100000+O(p^{-1/2})$. The same bound holds at all but boundedly many input scales. A fixed 32-round coordinate cover has a certified set of exposed rows; replacing whole diagonal fibers of their private blocks yields a gain controlled by marked pairs and triples. A proper-support inertia lemma proves selected-label independence despite colliding branch images. At the original scale, critical separation and two independent arithmetic arguments exclude fully branched projection fibers: one uses $2$-adic Newton polygons, the other $75$ exact cases over $\mathbb F_{25}$ and a real cubic specialization. The method also excludes an alternating-monodromy septic with coordinate capacity above $1.8$, giving coefficient $17977/10000$ at its original scale in both prime classes. Exact rational, symbolic, finite-group and finite-field certificates support all new claims. Coordinate sharpness concerns complete windows of at least two periods; the new quartic obstruction concerns two-period squares and sufficiently large primes. No unrestricted no-three construction or upper bound is claimed.
