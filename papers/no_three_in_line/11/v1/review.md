# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive result about a restricted construction family. Its contribution is the explicit free Möbius action with separated polynomial branch orbits, the resulting arithmetic local limit at every fixed depth, and the sharp coordinate capacities obtained from that limit. The quartic capacity exceeds $1.8$, whereas the cubic, quintic, and specified higher-degree class fail that coordinate test. These results meaningfully distinguish candidate periodic supports. They neither construct the requested unbounded no-three-in-line family nor prove its impossibility, so acceptance does not resolve the supplied problem.

The central proof withstands scrutiny. The strict ping-pong inclusions establish freeness and hyperbolicity; the exterior-domain argument handles the cubic, while algebraic degree and preservation of the real axis and half-planes separate the quartic and quintic branch orbits. Disjoint full branch loci justify joint geometric linear disjointness, and the full symmetric permutation groups eliminate an additional arithmetic constant-field obstruction. The argument correctly fixes the neighborhood depth before excluding bad primes and taking the finite-field limit. It does not assume an infinite free action on a finite field.

The grouped deletion is a valid finite vertex-cover operation. Its recurrence accounts for both columns of a doubleton fiber. The interval-completion argument treats membership in the final physical line cover, including the marked source coordinate, rather than assuming independent positions for roots of one fiber. This supports the stated uniformity over translated windows with $N\ge p$. The subsequent order of limits justifies the limiting upper constants without claiming the fixed-depth error rate at those constants.

I checked the allocation result against Leconte–Lelarge–Massoulié, arXiv:1207.1659v1, Theorem 1 [7]. The compressed graphs have the required bounded marked degrees and capacities, consistent edge laws, and the stated bipartite tree limit. The theorem applies to locally convergent deterministic graph sequences as used here. Its objective specializes to (A3), with normalization $\nu(G_{j,p})/p$. The scalar uniqueness arguments remove the possible ambiguity between recursive solutions. The cited matching formula [6] also agrees with the independent cubic reduction. Finally, the complete-window identity $2k\nu(G_{j,p})$ follows from the repeated vertex cover and two disjoint perfect matchings in each matched block for $k\ge2$.

The evidence is reproducible. All twelve supplied programs completed and exactly reproduced their retained JSON outputs, including the rational bounds, branch-image controls, and 864 physical-window counts. I additionally computed maximum residue matchings independently for all three polynomials at $p=1009$ and $10007$, checking each optimum against an equal-size vertex cover. At $p=10007$, the matching numbers were respectively $8930$, $9030$, and $9007$. These finite checks support the implementation and normalization; they do not establish convergence or an effective prime threshold. The asymptotic conclusions rest on the proofs and imported matching theorem.

The novelty is sufficient. The closest supplied predecessor, *Capacity obstructions for unions of periodic polynomial graphs*, version 2 [2], already establishes root-count independence, physical-window transfer, and matching capacities for consecutive polynomial translates. Its ordered-neighborhood queue argument does not supply the free-action tree model or the branch-orbit theorem proved here. The supplied single-polynomial paper, *Polynomial obstructions and sharp prime-square capacities for periodic grid supports*, likewise does not subsume this rational three-output union. The external matching papers [6]–[8] supply the general analytic machinery, which is appropriately attributed. I also examined the root-count, root-position, and two-slope results in Kudriashov's Section 9 [9]; those precedents concern polynomial supports and do not establish the present arbitrary-depth Möbius-orbit construction. The contribution is consequently more than a numerical variant of an existing cover calculation.

Two minor improvements would strengthen exposition and attribution:

- Cite the relevant theorem or proposition in [4] for finite-field Chebotarev and specialization, and add a direct citation to the earlier root-count and marked-root work [9]. The general references are adequate for correctness but make these dependencies harder to trace.
- In Section 5, explicitly describe the directed cavity event with its parent row held uncovered. This would make the relation between synchronous finite deletion and (13) easier to verify.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation and numbered references are readable, and the limitations are appropriately explicit. In particular, the quartic result must retain its qualification as a coordinate relaxation: exclusion of oblique collinear triples remains unproved.
