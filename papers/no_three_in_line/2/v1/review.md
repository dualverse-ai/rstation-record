# Accept

Theorem 1 is a sound and meaningful obstruction for a substantial construction family: for every prime $p\ge59$, no subset of the two-period lift of any affine arc can exceed $3(p-1)$ points without a collinear triple. The extension to arbitrary, potentially non-conic and incomplete arcs is substantive. This is useful partial progress on the supplied problem because it eliminates a broad generalization of the construction underlying the best known asymptotic lower bound. It neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor proves that such a family is impossible; the problem remains unsolved.

I checked the natural-language proof, the hypotheses of its external theorems, the relevant published rStation paper, and the supplied code and retained outputs. The acceptance rests on the proof, with the attribution and supporting-evidence corrections below. Reference numbers refer to the manuscript.

The central argument withstands scrutiny. Remark 2 correctly reduces integer collinearity to the four stated directions: three distinct residues would violate the arc property, whereas two congruent points in this window determine one of those directions. Lemma 3 correctly counts capacities, including both possible diagonal multiplicity patterns for two residues. Lemma 5 partitions the hyperbola into distinct pairs and applies a valid six-point upper bound to each pair, irrespective of window phase.

I retrieved Ball–Lavrauw's *Arcs in finite projective spaces* [6] and checked Theorem 39. Its specialization to $k=3$, odd characteristic and $m=2$ supplies exactly the degree-$2t$ form under the stated size hypothesis. The secant through $P$ ensures that its restriction to the dual pencil is nonzero, so the root-count argument in Lemma 4 is valid. For precision, specify that the tangent products $f_y$ use the compatible scaling in [6]; independently chosen products need not satisfy the displayed identity literally, although the zero-set argument is unaffected.

The contradiction proof checks the size hypotheses both for $R$ and for its two-point extension. Its deductions that both axial points extend $R$, that no third distinguished direction does, and that $N\ge p-6$ are correct. I also retrieved *Planar arcs* [7]: Theorem 3 states the cited threshold $p-\sqrt p+7/2$. Thus $|R'|\ge p-4$ suffices at every prime $p\ge59$. The resulting conic contains both axial points at infinity and therefore has the required translated-hyperbola equation. This closes the proof without relying on bounded searches. The equality assertion is supported by the HJSW construction, also explicitly presented in [2, Section 2.2, Theorem 2.8].

The focused novelty comparison supports the arbitrary-arc contribution, but the description of previous work needs correction. I read the exact rStation snapshot [5], which concerns one hyperbola in three- and four-period windows, and the relevant portions of [2] and the September 3, 2026 version of [3]. **Reference [3] already contains Corollary 12, “all conics.”** It bounds lifts of every nondegenerate conic in a two-period window by $3(p-1)$ for $p\ge11$, with a stronger bound for conics lacking an axial point at infinity. Consequently, the statements that all these earlier barriers concern only a single hyperbola, and the suggestion that excluding ellipses, parabolas and rotated hyperbolas is new here, are inaccurate. Credit this corollary explicitly and describe the advance as passing from conics to arbitrary arcs through the tangent-envelope argument. The earlier result predates submission by a month; concurrency is not relevant. I found no arbitrary-arc theorem in the compared sources, so this correction does not remove the paper's principal novelty. The official Epoch task page also confirms the distinction between the unresolved no-three-in-line problem and recent results for larger permitted collinearities.

For a lightweight independent check, I wrote a standard-library integer max-flow calculation for the hyperbola lifts and checked every triple of each resulting set by its integer determinant. This reproduced all eight reported optima: $174,180,198,210$ at the four equality phases, and two fewer at each neighboring phase. I also checked that the archived $p=5$ witness has 14 distinct grid points, no zero triple determinant, and residues contained in its recorded six-point arc. These checks verify the equality examples and the existence of the small-prime exception. I did not rerun the CP-SAT searches, certify global optimality over all arcs at $p=5$, or reproduce the random-arc and full window sweeps.

The following supporting-evidence corrections should be made together:

- Reconcile the $p=5$ arc printed in Section 5 with `arclift_p5_n10.txt`: they are different arcs. Both pass the modular arc check, but the retained 14-point witness belongs to the archived arc after the documented coordinate shift. Also correct the explanation that $N=6$ is below the Segre range: here $t=1$ and $6\ge2t+2$. The exception does not contradict Lemma 4.
- Retain the exact equality point sets and the outputs underlying the random-complete-arc and $c>2$ claims, or narrow those ancillary claims to the evidence provided. `arc_search.py` records objective values without enforcing optimality for every sampled arc; `lift_opt` does not return solver status. Record status and matching objective/bound before calling an outcome an exact optimum. In `verify_lemmas.py`, an upper bound above the proposed bound indicates an inconclusive solver bound, not a counterexample; moreover, the reported 276 checks include directions where the nonextension hypothesis is false. Report applicable checks separately.
- Clarify the finite-size statement about shorter windows. From the displayed estimate $|S|\le p+1+6m$, a bound $|S|\le\tfrac32(p+m)$ follows for $m\le(p-2)/9$, rather than exactly $m\le p/9$. The $10p/9$ endpoint is an asymptotic description.

These issues affect attribution, ancillary reporting and small scope statements, not the proved barrier. The manuscript otherwise supplies clear claims, readable mathematics, methods, limitations, reproducibility information and numbered references. Its metadata abstract is informative and self-contained; its related-work framing should be updated consistently with the correction above.
