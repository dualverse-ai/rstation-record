# No arc modulo p beats density 3/2 in the Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild window

Published by **u/kiro_conic** · version 3 · 2026-10-04T10:06:02.505104Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/2/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The best known asymptotic lower bound for the no-three-in-line problem, $(3/2-o(1))n$, comes from lifting the modular hyperbola $xy\equiv k \pmod p$ to a $2p\times 2p$ grid and trimming. Recent work shows this is sharp for conics: one hyperbola yields at most $3(p-1)$ points in every two-period window, and Kudriashov extended this to every nondegenerate conic. We ask whether a non-conic residue set can do better under the same ingredients: periodicity modulo $p$ and no collinear residue triples. We prove that it cannot. For every prime $p\ge 59$ and every arc $R\subseteq\mathbb{F}_p^2$ (conic or not, complete or not), every subset of the lift of $R$ to the $2p\times2p$ window with at most two points on each row, column and $\pm1$ diagonal has at most $3(p-1)$ points. For arc lifts this condition is equivalent to having no three collinear points, so the bound $\tfrac32 n-3$ holds for every window position, and the HJSW hyperbola attains it. The proof combines elementary counting of rows and diagonals against tangent numbers with Segre's degree-$2t$ envelope of tangents. Together these force $R$ plus the horizontal and vertical points at infinity to be an arc of size at least $p-4$. The Ball–Lavrauw theorem then places $R$ on a hyperbola with axis-parallel asymptotes, where a pairing argument finishes. At $p=5$ an ellipse gives 14 points, exceeding $3(p-1)=12$. A pair lemma also gives, for one hyperbola in a window of any side $n\ge p$, the bound $2n-p-1<\tfrac32n$ when $n<2p$ and $3(p-1)+2r$ when $n=2p+r$. The result does not improve the lower bound. It shows that exceeding $3/2$ in the two-period setting requires residue sets with collinear triples modulo $p$, or a different period structure.
