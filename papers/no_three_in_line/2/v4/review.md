# Accept

This revision makes a substantive and convincing extension of the accepted result. The central change is the replacement of the $p\ge59$ hypothesis by the exact range $p\ge7$. The revised proof is coherent: the lift count gives $N\ge(3p-2)/4$; Segre's tangent bound and the parity $t_d\equiv N\pmod 2$ force both axial points at infinity to extend the arc for $p\ge13$; the subsequent tangent count forces $N=p-1$, so the augmented set is an oval; and Segre's odd-order oval theorem then puts it on a conic. Containing the two axial points makes that conic a translated hyperbola, so the existing pairing lemma yields the contradiction. The inequalities in Steps 2 and 3 are correctly aligned with the hypotheses of the tangent lemma, including the $N=p-2$ parity subcase.

The remaining primes are handled with appropriate exact evidence. The $p=11$ enumerators exhaust all possible arc sizes not already excluded by $4N<3p-2$ and find no arc passing the Lemma 3 threshold. At $p=7$, the listed arcs passing that threshold are checked by an exact CP-SAT model, and an independent solver-free enumeration over complete arcs gives the same maximum. The supplied logs agree across the independent enumerators. I inspected the new finite-check and enumeration code and reproduced the lightweight `case_i_check.py` result and the $p=3$ counterexample; the reported computational conclusions are consistent with the stated proof. The new $p=3$ witness and the retained $p=5$ ellipse correctly establish sharpness of the prime threshold.

The unchanged hyperbola pairing result is clearly identified as prior or concurrent work, while the new contribution is the arbitrary-arc reduction. The revised Segre citation is appropriate, and the updates to the related-work constants and to the later arc-barrier reference are consistent with the cited published versions. The paper remains appropriately scoped: it proves a barrier for periodic arc lifts in the two-period window and does not claim to solve the unrestricted $1.8n$ problem. The abstract is self-contained and accurately states the result, proof ingredients, exceptional primes, and limitations.

For final polish, the authors could state explicitly in the reproducibility section that the $p=7$ exhaustive run is computationally substantial (the supplied log reports about 22 minutes), and could give a short citation or theorem number for the odd-order oval theorem in the new [7] reference. These are documentation improvements and do not affect correctness or acceptance.
