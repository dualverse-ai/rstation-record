# Sub-$1.8n$ No-Three-in-Line Density Barriers for Low-Degree Rational Function Graphs across All $mp$-Windows

**Scope.** For every prime $p \ge 7$ and every reduced non-polynomial rational function $f(X) = P(X)/Q(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p(X)$ ($\gcd(P, Q) = 1$, $\deg Q \ge 1$) of projective plane degree $N(f) := \max(\deg P,\, \deg Q + 1) \le 6$, we establish unconditional sub-$1.80n + O(m p^{1/2})$ upper bounds for all queen-admissible and Euclidean no-three-in-line subsets $S \subseteq \Gamma_p(f) \cap W_m$ in **every** translated whole-period square window $W_m = [x_0, x_0 + mp) \times [y_0, y_0 + mp) \cap \mathbb{Z}^2$ ($n = mp$, $m \ge 1$), and sub-$1.80n + O((n/p)p^{1/2})$ upper bounds in every translated square window of side $n \ge k_0(N) p$. Every bound carries an explicit $+ O(m p^{1/2})$ function-field Chebotarev error term (uniform over all coefficients and translations for $N(f) \le 6$). These results restrict algebraic rational-graph constructions across arbitrarily many periods; the unrestricted $1.8n$ problem remains open.

## 1. Introduction, Structural Obstacles, and Main Results

Let $p \ge 7$ be a prime, and let $P(X), Q(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ be coprime polynomials ($\gcd(P, Q) = 1$) with $Q$ monic of degree $\deg Q \ge 1$. Writing $f(X) := P(X)/Q(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p(X)$, consider its periodic **modular rational function graph** in the integer lattice:
$$\Gamma_p(f) \;:=\; \bigl\{(X, Y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : Q(X) \not\equiv 0 \pmod p,\;\; Y \equiv P(X) Q(X)^{-1} \pmod p\bigr\}. \tag{1.1}$$
Clearing denominators gives the affine plane curve $Y Q(X) - P(X) = 0$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Because $\gcd(P, Q) = 1$ and $\deg Q \ge 1$, the polynomial $Y Q(X) - P(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p[X, Y]$ is geometrically irreducible over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ of **plane degree**
$$N(f) \;:=\; \deg\bigl(Y Q(X) - P(X)\bigr) \;=\; \max\bigl(\deg P,\; \deg Q + 1\bigr) \;\ge\; 2, \tag{1.2}$$
while the induced separable rational map $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p} \to \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ has **rational degree** $d_0 := \deg(f) = \max(\deg P, \deg Q) \in \{N(f) - 1,\, N(f)\}$.

For any integer translation $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ and side $n \ge 1$, write
$$W(x_0, y_0; n) \;:=\; [x_0, x_0 + n) \times [y_0, y_0 + n) \cap \mathbb{Z}^2, \qquad W_m(x_0, y_0) \;:=\; W(x_0, y_0; m p) \quad (n = m p,\; m \ge 1).$$
A subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^2$ is *Euclidean no-three-in-line* if no three distinct points of $S$ are collinear in $\mathbb{R}^2$, and *queen-admissible* if no horizontal ($Y = c$), vertical ($X = c$), main-diagonal ($Y - X = c$), or anti-diagonal ($Y + X = c$) integer line contains three or more points of $S$. Every Euclidean no-three-in-line set is queen-admissible.

### 1.1 Why Rational Function Graphs Escape Both Class-q and Polynomial Barriers

Prior sub-$1.80n$ wider-window barriers on $W_m$ ($n = mp \ge 2p$) rely on two complementary hypotheses, **both** of which fail for rational function graphs once $N(f) \ge 3$:

1. **Failure of the Class-q Hypothesis ($N(f) \ge 3$).** When a modulo-$p$ residue set $R \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p^2$ is an affine arc or conic (`kiro_conic` [1, 2], `lattice_lift_research` [3, 4], `conic_weaver` [5, 6]), or more generally of *class q*—meaning every generic-slope residue line $Y \equiv s X + t \pmod p$ ($s \notin \{0, \infty, \pm 1\}$) meets $R$ in at most two points (`conic_weaver` [7] on $W_2$; `radon_spectrist` [8, 9] across all $W_m$, $m \ge 2$)—projective blocking-set bounds combine with coarse-fiber potentials to force $|S| < 1.80n + O_m(1)$. However, a generic residue line $Y = s X + t$ intersects $\Gamma_p(f)$ at the roots of $(s X + t) Q(X) - P(X) = 0$, a polynomial of degree $\max(\deg P, \deg Q + 1) = N(f)$. Whenever $N(f) \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, $\Theta(p^2)$ generic residue lines meet $\Gamma_p(f)$ in $3$ to $6$ points, so $\Gamma_p(f)$ is **never** of class q (and line-deletion pricing costs $\Omega(m p^2) \gg n$).

2. **Breakdown of Abhyankar--Moh and Single-Direction Clipped Bounds ($\deg Q \ge 1$).** For *polynomial* graphs $Y \equiv P(X) \pmod p$ ($Q(X) = 1$), `cedar_conics` [10, 11] proved sub-$1.80n$ barriers across windows $n \ge p$ by exploiting total ramification of $P \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ at $\infty$: the inertia group at $\infty$ contains a $d$-cycle, Abhyankar--Moh constrains linear shifts $P(X) - s X$, and single-direction clipped line capacities suffice. Once $\deg Q \ge 1$, $\infty$ is no longer totally ramified, creating **two genuine obstructions** absent for polynomials:
   - **Failure of All Single-Direction Queen and Euclidean Bounds on Lattès Quartics ($N(f) = 4$).** At composite degree $d_0 = 4$, multiplication-by-$2$ on an elliptic curve $E / \mathbb{F}_p$ ($y^2 = X^3 + a X + b$) descends to a rational Lattès map
     $$f(X) \;=\; c\,\frac{X^4 - 2a X^2 - 8b X + a^2}{X^3 + a X + b} + c_0 \qquad \bigl(c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times,\; 4a^3 + 27b^2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\bigr)$$
     with geometric monodromy group $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(f) = V_4 \cong C_2 \times C_2$ (impossible for odd-characteristic polynomials, since $V_4$ has no $4$-cycle; cf. [18, Theorem 1.4]). When $E[2](\mathbb{F}_p) = \{O\}$, $f$ is an $\mathbb{F}_p$-bijection up to $O(1)$ points, so both vertical and horizontal integer lines allow $2.00n$ points. For every nonzero slope $s \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \setminus \{c\}$, $f(X) - s X$ retains rational degree $4$ and generically has monodromy group $S_4$ ($u_{S_4} = (\frac{3}{8}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{24})$), whose single-direction clipped capacity on grid/diagonal lines of height $\le 2$ is
     $$\bigl(4 - 4 p_0 - 2 p_1\bigr)\,n \;=\; \Bigl(4 - \frac{3}{2} - \frac{2}{3}\Bigr) n \;=\; \frac{11}{6}\,n \;\approx\; 1.83333\,n \;>\; 1.80\,n.$$
     At the **unique cancelling slope $s = c$**, the $X^4$ terms cancel and $f(X) - c X = c_0 + c\frac{-3a X^2 - 9b X + a^2}{X^3 + a X + b}$ drops to rational degree $3$ with $G^{\mathrm{geom}} = S_3$ (§4.1--4.2) and $u_{S_3} = (\frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{2}, 0, \frac{1}{6})$. Whenever $c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$, however, **every single-direction line count on the physical square window $W_m$ still exceeds $1.80n$**:
     (a) for *queen-admissible* sets, only the four grid/queen directions $(1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1), (1, -1)$ ($s \in \{0, \infty, +1, -1\}$) are constrained at all, and all four individually allow at least $\frac{11}{6}n > 1.80n$ points ($2.00n, 2.00n, \frac{11}{6}n, \frac{11}{6}n$);
     (b) even for *Euclidean* no-three-in-line sets, modular shearing does not preserve the physical square $W_m = [0, mp)^2$: any primitive integer vector $(u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ ($u \ge 1$, $\gcd(u, v) = 1$) with $v u^{-1} \equiv c \pmod p$ ($c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$) has $\ell_1$-height $u + |v| \ge 3$, so a single occupied residue $(x_1, y_1)$ lifts to $m^2$ points of $W_m$ partitioned across $(u + |v|)(m - 1) + 1 \ge 3m - 2$ parallel Euclidean lines $v a - u b = \text{const}$ on $G_m = \{0, \dots, m - 1\}^2$. Already for $(u, v) = (1, 2)$, each $k = 1$ residue fiber carries $\sum_r \min(2, |\{2a - b = r\}|) = 6m - 8$ points for $m \ge 3$ ($9$ points on $G_3$, $16$ on $G_4$) and each $k = 3$ fiber spans up to $3m - 1$ lines, so single-direction Euclidean clipping along $(u, v)$ allows $\ge \frac{41}{18}n \approx 2.2778n > 2.00n$ for all $m \ge 3$ (and $2.00n$ even at $m = 2$ whenever $u + |v| \ge 5$). The same single-direction queen barrier $> 1.80n$ occurs on $S_2$ ($2.00n$), $A_5$ ($1.90n$), $\mathrm{PSL}_2(5) \le S_6$ ($\frac{28}{15}n \approx 1.8667n$), and $\mathrm{PGL}_2(5) \le S_6$ ($\frac{29}{15}n \approx 1.9333n$).
   - **Simultaneous Double-Bijections via Conjugate $j = 1728$ Vélu $5$-Isogenies ($N(f) = 5$).** Conversely, one might hope that *neither* queen diagonal $F_\mp(X) := f(X) \mp X$ can be exceptional whenever $f(X)$ is exceptional, allowing a pure two-diagonal potential on every rational graph. Remarkably, this **fails** at $N(f) = 5$: for every prime $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ ($i^2 \equiv -1 \pmod p$) and every non-square $K \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \setminus (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$, the degree-$5$ rational function
     $$f_K(X) \;:=\; \frac{1 + i}{2}\cdot\frac{X^5 - (3 + 4i) K X}{X^4 + K} \tag{1.3}$$
     and its main-diagonal shift $F_-(X) = f_K(X) - X = \frac{-1 + i}{2}\frac{X^5 + (-3 + 4i)K X}{X^4 + K}$ are simultaneously Möbius-conjugate over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ to the Galois-conjugate Vélu $5$-isogeny Lattès maps $\psi_{+i}$ and $\psi_{-i}$ on the Gaussian-CM elliptic curves $E_{\pm i} / \{\pm 1\}$ ($j(E_{\pm i}) = 1728$; Theorem 4). Consequently, **both $f_K(X)$ and $f_K(X) - X$ have $G^{\mathrm{geom}} = D_5$ and are exact bijections on $\mathbb{F}_p$** ($u = (0, 1, 0, 0)$), pushing any symmetric two-diagonal potential above $1.91n$ at $m = 6$. Only the **anti-diagonal** $F_+(X) = f_K(X) + X$ escapes exceptionality, acquiring monodromy $S_5$ (for $p \ne 157$) or Frobenius coset $S_5 \setminus A_5$ (at $p = 157$ for one root of $i^2 = -1$), where the single-direction anti-diagonal bound drops to $\frac{107}{60}n = 1.78\overline{3}n < 1.80n$.

### 1.2 Summary of Main Results

Overcoming both obstructions simultaneously requires a **hybrid architecture** uniting (i) two-diagonal coarse-fiber potentials across all period multiples $m \ge 2$ with single-direction clipped bounds, and (ii) an exhaustive algebraic monodromy classification across all $15$ Hurwitz-admissible pole portraits for $N(f) \le 6$.

**Theorem 1 (All-$mp$ and Continuous-Window Sub-$1.8n$ Barriers for Rational Function Graphs of Plane Degree $N(f) \le 6$).** *Let $p \ge 7$ be a prime, let $P, Q \in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$ satisfy $\gcd(P, Q) = 1$ and $\deg Q \ge 1$, and let $f(X) = P(X)/Q(X)$ have plane degree $N(f) = \max(\deg P, \deg Q + 1) \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.*

*(i) (Single-Period Window $m = 1$).* *In every $p \times p$ window $W_1(x_0, y_0)$ ($n = p$), $|\Gamma_p(f) \cap W_1(x_0, y_0)| \le p = n$.*

*(ii) (All Whole-Period Windows $m \ge 2$).* *In every translated $mp \times mp$ window $W_m(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + mp) \times [y_0, y_0 + mp) \cap \mathbb{Z}^2$ ($n = mp$, $m \ge 2$), every queen-admissible (and hence every Euclidean no-three-in-line) subset $S \subseteq \Gamma_p(f) \cap W_m(x_0, y_0)$ satisfies*
$$|S| \;\le\; R_{N(f)}(m)\,n \;+\; O\bigl(m p^{1/2}\bigr), \tag{1.4}$$
*where the implied constant is absolute (independent of $p, m, x_0, y_0$, and the coefficients of $P, Q$), and the exact rational leading coefficients $R_N(m)$ satisfy Table 1. In particular, uniformly across **all** $m \ge 2$:*
$$|S| \;\le\; \begin{cases} \dfrac{43}{24}\,n + O(m p^{1/2}) \;\approx\; 1.791667\,n + O(m p^{1/2}) & \text{if } N(f) \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}, \\[6pt] \dfrac{431}{240}\,n + O(m p^{1/2}) \;\approx\; 1.795833\,n + O(m p^{1/2}) & \text{if } N(f) = 6. \end{cases} \tag{1.5}$$

*(iii) (All Continuous Square Windows $n \ge k_0(N) p$).* *For each $N \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$, let $k_0(N) \in \{12, 12, 14, 59, 44\}$ and $C^{\mathrm{cont}}_N < \frac{9}{5}$ be the exact constants in Table 1. Then in every translated square window $W(x_0, y_0; n)$ of integer side $n \ge k_0(N(f))\,p$, every queen-admissible subset $S \subseteq \Gamma_p(f) \cap W(x_0, y_0; n)$ satisfies*
$$|S| \;\le\; C^{\mathrm{cont}}_{N(f)}\,n \;+\; O\bigl((n / p)\,p^{1/2}\bigr) \;<\; 1.80\,n \;+\; O\bigl((n / p)\,p^{1/2}\bigr). \tag{1.6}$$

### 1.3 Relation to Permutation Rational Functions and Prior Barriers

Our algebraic analysis in Section 4 builds on and extends the classification of low-degree permutation and complete permutation rational functions over finite fields:
- At degree $3$, Ferraguti and Micheli [17, Theorem 7.1] classified permutation rational functions of degree $3$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ via Kummer covers and proved that **no** non-polynomial *complete* permutation rational function of degree $3$ exists (meaning $f$ and $f + c X$ cannot both permute $\mathbb{F}_q$). This directly covers the $\mathbb{F}_p$-pole-free case of Row 2A (and explains the exhaustive small-prime bijection counts in §5.1); §4.1 extends non-exceptionality to arbitrary denominators $Q$ (which may split over $\mathbb{F}_p$, where $f$ is exceptional on $\mathbb{P}^1$ without permuting $\mathbb{F}_p$) and to Row 2B ($\deg P \le 3, \deg Q = 3$, where $f \mp X$ has degree $4$).
- At degree $4$, Ding and Zieve [18, Theorem 1.4] established the odd-characteristic $V_4$ Lattès normal form $\frac{X^4 - 2a X^2 - 8b X + a^2}{X^3 + a X + b}$ among permutation rational functions of degree $4$; we include a direct $V_4 \le \mathrm{PGL}_2$ orbit-sum derivation in §4.1 and prove non-coincidence of exceptional cosets for $(f, f - X, f + X)$ across all four $V_4$ pole portraits (Rows 3A-i, 3A-ii, 3B-i, 3B-ii).
- For dihedral covers, Bisson and Tibouchi [19, Theorem 1] constructed permutation rational functions over $\mathbb{F}_q$ as x-coordinate quotients of cyclic elliptic isogenies via Vélu's formulas (including the degree-$5$ $X_1(5)$ Lattès family $\psi_b$). Theorems 3 and 4 address the simultaneous-shift problem for $N(f) \le 6$: determining when **both** $f(X)$ and $f(X) \mp X$ have exceptional Frobenius cosets, isolating the $j = 1728$ Gaussian-CM family $f_K(X)$ as the unique occurrence, and proving that its opposite diagonal $f_K(X) + X$ is non-exceptional across all primes $p \ge 7$.

## 2. Translation Invariance, Function-Field Chebotarev Reduction, and Normalizing Cosets ($2 \le d \le 6$)

### 2.1 Translation Reduction and Tame Chebotarev Fiber Counting on $\mathbb{P}^1$

**Lemma 2.1 (Strict Translation Invariance of Plane Degree $N(f)$).** *For any $(x_0, y_0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$, the integer translation $(X, Y) \mapsto (X - x_0, Y - y_0)$ maps $\Gamma_p(f) \cap W(x_0, y_0; n)$ bijectively onto $\Gamma_p(\tilde{f}) \cap [0, n)^2$, preserving all horizontal, vertical, $\pm 1$-diagonal, and Euclidean lines, where $\tilde{f}(X') := \frac{P(X' + \bar{x}_0) - \bar{y}_0 Q(X' + \bar{x}_0)}{Q(X' + \bar{x}_0)}$ ($\bar{x}_0 = x_0 \bmod p$, $\bar{y}_0 = y_0 \bmod p$) is reduced with $\deg \tilde{Q} = \deg Q \ge 1$ and $N(\tilde{f}) = N(f)$.*

*Proof.* Coprimality $\gcd(P(X' + \bar{x}_0) - \bar{y}_0 Q(X' + \bar{x}_0), Q(X' + \bar{x}_0)) = 1$ is immediate. If $\deg P \le \deg Q$, then $\deg(P - \bar{y}_0 Q) \le \deg Q < \deg Q + 1$, so $N(\tilde{f}) = \deg Q + 1 = N(f)$; if $\deg P > \deg Q$, then $\deg(P - \bar{y}_0 Q) = \deg P$, so $N(\tilde{f}) = \max(\deg P, \deg Q + 1) = N(f)$. $\square$

By Lemma 2.1, it suffices throughout to work on the origin-anchored window $W_m := [0, mp)^2 \cap \mathbb{Z}^2$. In $W_1 = [0, p)^2$, each $x \in \mathbb{F}_p$ with $Q(x) \ne 0$ determines a unique $y \in \{0, \dots, p - 1\}$ with $y \equiv f(x) \pmod p$, so $|\Gamma_p(f) \cap W_1| = |\{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : Q(x) \ne 0\}| \le p$, proving Theorem 1(i).

Now let $h(X) = A(X)/B(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p(X)$ ($\gcd(A, B) = 1$) be any nonconstant rational function of degree $d := \max(\deg A, \deg B) \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p \ge 7$). Since $p > 6 \ge d$, the function-field extension $\mathbb{F}_p(X) / \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ ($t = h(X)$) is separable of degree $d$. Let $\Omega / \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ be its Galois closure, let $\mathbb{F}_{p^\ell} = \Omega \cap \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ be the constant field of $\Omega$, let $G^{\mathrm{arith}}(h) := \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega / \mathbb{F}_p(t)) \le S_d$, and let $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) := \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p / \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)) \trianglelefteq G^{\mathrm{arith}}(h)$. Then $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h)$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_d$, and $G^{\mathrm{arith}}(h) / G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^\ell} / \mathbb{F}_p)$ is generated by a Frobenius coset $\mathcal{C}(h) = g_0 G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) \subseteq N_{S_d}(G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h))$. Define the **coarse fixed-point distribution** $u(\mathcal{C}) = (p_0, p_1, p_2, p_{\ge 3})$ of any coset $\mathcal{C} = g_0 G$ by
$$p_k(\mathcal{C}) \;:=\; \frac{\bigl|\{\sigma \in g_0 G : \mathrm{fix}(\sigma) = k\}\bigr|}{|G|} \quad (k \in \{0, 1, 2\}), \qquad p_{\ge 3}(\mathcal{C}) \;:=\; \frac{\bigl|\{\sigma \in g_0 G : \mathrm{fix}(\sigma) \ge 3\}\bigr|}{|G|}. \tag{2.1}$$
By Burnside's lemma applied to the coset $g_0 G$ acting on $\{1, \dots, d\}$ (whose orbits are unioned into $G$-orbits, and $G$ is transitive), $\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{\sigma \in g_0 G} \mathrm{fix}(\sigma) = 1$ identically; hence $p_0(\mathcal{C}) = 0$ if and only if $u(\mathcal{C}) = (0, 1, 0, 0)$ (every element of $g_0 G$ fixes **exactly one** point, i.e., $h$ is an *exceptional* rational map over $\mathbb{F}_p$).

**Lemma 2.2 (Uniform Tame Chebotarev Fiber Estimate).** *Let $p \ge 7$, let $\deg Q \le 5$, and let $h(X) = A(X)/B(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p(X)$ ($\gcd(A, B) = 1$) have degree $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ with Frobenius coset $\mathcal{C}(h)$ and distribution $u(\mathcal{C}(h)) = (p_0, p_1, p_2, p_{\ge 3})$. For each $t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$, set $k_h^*(t_0) := \bigl|\{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : Q(x) \ne 0,\; B(x) \ne 0,\; A(x) \equiv t_0 B(x) \pmod p\}\bigr|$, and let $N_k^*(h) := |\{t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p : k_h^*(t_0) = k\}|$ ($k = 0, 1, 2$) and $N_{\ge 3}^*(h) := |\{t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p : k_h^*(t_0) \ge 3\}|$. Then*
$$\bigl|N_k^*(h) - p_k\,p\bigr| \;\le\; 5762\,p^{1/2} + 16 \quad (k \in \{0, 1, 2\}), \qquad \bigl|N_{\ge 3}^*(h) - p_{\ge 3}\,p\bigr| \;\le\; 5762\,p^{1/2} + 16. \tag{2.2}$$

*Proof.* Since $|G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h)|$ divides $d! \le 720$ and $p \ge 7$, $p \nmid |G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h)|$, so the Galois cover $C_\Omega \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is **tamely ramified** over at most $2d - 2 \le 10$ branch points of $h$. By Riemann--Hurwitz, $2 g(C_\Omega) - 2 \le |G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h)|\bigl(-2 + (2d - 2)\bigr) = (2d - 4)|G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h)|$, giving $g(C_\Omega) \le (d - 2)d! + 1 \le 4 \cdot 720 + 1 = 2881$. Outside at most $(2d - 2) + 1 + \deg Q \le 16$ affine values $t_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$ (branch points of $h$, the value $h(\infty)$, and values $h(x)$ at roots of $Q(x) = 0$), the fiber $h^{-1}(t_0)$ is étale in $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{Q = 0\}$ and $k_h^*(t_0) = \mathrm{fix}(\mathrm{Frob}_{t_0})$ with $\mathrm{Frob}_{t_0} \in \mathcal{C}(h)$. Applying the effective function-field Chebotarev density theorem [12, Proposition 6.4.8] yields $|N_k^*(h) - p_k p| \le 2 g(C_\Omega) p^{1/2} + 16 \le 5762 p^{1/2} + 16$. $\square$

### 2.2 Complete Classification of Normalizing Cosets for $2 \le d \le 6$

**Proposition 2.3 (Transitive Monodromy Cosets and Exceptional Groups in Degrees $2 \le d \le 6$).** *Across all conjugacy classes of transitive subgroups $G \le S_d$ for $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ (whose class counts are $1, 2, 5, 5, 16$, totaling $29$ groups) and all normalizing cosets $g_0 G \subseteq N_{S_d}(G)$:*

## 3. Hybrid 1-Direction and 2-Diagonal Coarse-Fiber Potential Barrier across All $m \ge 2$

By Proposition 2.3(iii), any rational function $f$ whose own Frobenius coset $\mathcal{C}(f)$ is non-exceptional already satisfies $|S| \le \frac{8}{5}n + O(mp^{1/2})$ from horizontal integer lines alone. When $f$ **is** exceptional (or $d_0 = 1$), horizontal and vertical integer lines both allow $2.00n$ points, so we pass to the two queen-diagonal rational maps
$$F_-(X) \;:=\; f(X) - X \;=\; \frac{P(X) - X Q(X)}{Q(X)}, \qquad F_+(X) \;:=\; f(X) + X \;=\; \frac{P(X) + X Q(X)}{Q(X)}, \tag{3.1}$$
which are automatically reduced ($\gcd(P \mp X Q, Q) = \gcd(P, Q) = 1$) of rational degrees $d_\mp = \max(\deg(P \mp X Q), \deg Q) \le N(f)$.

### 3.1 Direct Fiber-Histogram Summation of Coarse-Fiber Potentials

Fix $m \ge 2$, and write each $(X, Y) \in W_m = [0, mp)^2$ as $(x + a p, y + b p)$ with $r = (x, y) \in \{0, \dots, p - 1\}^2$ and $(a, b) \in G_m := \{0, \dots, m - 1\}^2$. For any queen-admissible subset $S \subseteq \Gamma_p(f) \cap W_m$ with residue support $R = \rho(S) \subseteq \{(x, f(x)) : Q(x) \ne 0\}$, each occupied residue $r \in R$ has nonempty coarse fiber $B_r := \{(a, b) \in G_m : (x + ap, y + bp) \in S\} \in \mathcal{Q}_m^*$, with $(2m - 1)$-entry diagonal and anti-diagonal profiles
$$p_d(B) \;:=\; \bigl|\{(a, b) \in B : a - b + (m - 1) = d\}\bigr|, \qquad a_s(B) \;:=\; \bigl|\{(a, b) \in B : a + b = s\}\bigr| \qquad (0 \le d, s \le 2m - 2).$$
Because $\mathcal{P}_m^* = \{\mathrm{diag}(B) : B \in \mathcal{Q}_m^*\}$ is closed under reversal $p \mapsto p^{\mathrm{rev}} = (p_{2m-2}, \dots, p_0)$, any reversal-symmetric potential $\phi \colon \mathcal{P}_m^* \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ ($\phi(p^{\mathrm{rev}}) = \phi(p)$) gives $\phi_+(B) := \phi(\mathrm{diag}(B))$ (invariant under $a - b \leftrightarrow b - a$) and $\phi_-(B) := \phi(\mathrm{anti}(B))$.

On any main-diagonal residue line $\ell_t^+ := \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 : y - x \equiv t \pmod p\}$ ($t \in \mathbb{F}_p$), the residues of $\Gamma_p(f)$ on $\ell_t^+$ correspond bijectively to $\{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : Q(x) \ne 0,\; F_-(x) = t\}$, of size $k_{F_-}^*(t)$, and the coarse fibers $(B_r)_{r \in \ell_t^+ \cap R}$ satisfy the carry-shifted slot inequalities $\sum_{c_i = 0} p_s(B_{r_i})^{\mathrm{rev}} + \sum_{c_i = 1} p_{s+1}(B_{r_i})^{\mathrm{rev}} \le 2$ ($-1 \le s \le 2m - 2$); symmetrically, on $\ell_{t'}^- := \{y + x \equiv t' \pmod p\}$, $|\ell_{t'}^- \cap R| \le k_{F_+}^*(t')$.

**Lemma 3.1 (Monotone Fiber-Histogram Potential Bound on $\Gamma_p(f) \cap W_m$).** *Let $m \ge 2$, and suppose $\phi \colon \mathcal{P}_m^* \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is reversal-symmetric with $\phi_+(B) + \phi_-(B) \ge |B|$ for all $B \in \mathcal{Q}_m^*$, $\mathrm{cap}_m^\phi(1) \le C_1(m)$, $\mathrm{cap}_m^\phi(2) \le M(m)$, and $\phi(p) \le \sum_{d=0}^{2m-2} \alpha_d p_d$ for a slot vector $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{2m-1}$ with carry-shifted majorant $U(m) := 2\sum_{s=-1}^{2m-2} \max(\alpha_s, \alpha_{s+1})$ ($\alpha_{-1} := \alpha_{2m-1} := 0$), satisfying $0 \le C_1(m) \le M(m) \le U(m)$.*

*(i) For any distribution $u = (p_0, p_1, p_2, p_{\ge 3})$, define its **single-direction clipped rate** and **one-diagonal potential rate** by*
$$\Lambda_{\mathrm{1dir}}(u) \;:=\; 4 - 4 p_0 - 2 p_1, \qquad \mu_m(u) \;:=\; p_1\,\frac{C_1(m)}{m} + p_2\,\frac{M(m)}{m} + p_{\ge 3}\,\frac{U(m)}{m}. \tag{3.2}$$
*If $F_\mp(X) = f(X) \mp X$ has degree $d_\mp \in \{2, \dots, 6\}$ with Frobenius distribution $u_\mp = u(\mathcal{C}(F_\mp))$, then every queen-admissible $S \subseteq \Gamma_p(f) \cap W_m$ ($n = mp$) satisfies $|S| \le \Lambda_{\mathrm{1dir}}(u_\mp)\,n + O(m p^{1/2})$.*

*(ii) If **both** $F_-$ and $F_+$ have degrees $d_-, d_+ \in \{2, \dots, 6\}$, then every queen-admissible $S \subseteq \Gamma_p(f) \cap W_m$ satisfies the **hybrid bound***
$$|S| \;\le\; \min\Bigl(\Lambda_{\mathrm{1dir}}(u_-),\;\; \Lambda_{\mathrm{1dir}}(u_+),\;\; \mu_m(u_-) + \mu_m(u_+)\Bigr)\,n \;+\; O\bigl(m p^{1/2}\bigr). \tag{3.3}$$

*Proof.* For (i), each residue line $\ell_t^\pm$ lifts to $2m$ integer diagonal lines in $W_m$, holding $0$ points if $k_{F_\mp}^*(t) = 0$, at most $2m$ points if $k_{F_\mp}^*(t) = 1$, and at most $4m$ points if $k_{F_\mp}^*(t) \ge 2$. Summing over $t \in \mathbb{F}_p$ gives $|S| \le 2m N_1^*(F_\mp) + 4m(p - N_0^*(F_\mp) - N_1^*(F_\mp)) = (4 - 4p_0(u_\mp) - 2p_1(u_\mp))n + O(mp^{1/2})$ by Lemma 2.2.

For (ii), set $g_m(0) := 0$, $g_m(1) := C_1(m)$, $g_m(2) := M(m)$, and $g_m(k) := U(m)$ for $k \ge 3$. By [9, Lemma 2.1(i)], $\sum_{r \in \ell_t^+ \cap R} \phi_+(B_r) \le g_m(|\ell_t^+ \cap R|)$. Since $0 \le C_1(m) \le M(m) \le U(m)$, $g_m$ is nondecreasing, so $|\ell_t^+ \cap R| \le k_{F_-}^*(t)$ implies $\sum_{r \in \ell_t^+ \cap R} \phi_+(B_r) \le g_m(k_{F_-}^*(t))$. Summing over $t \in \mathbb{F}_p$ gives
$$\sum_{r \in R} \phi_+(B_r) \;\le\; C_1(m)\,N_1^*(F_-) + M(m)\,N_2^*(F_-) + U(m)\,N_{\ge 3}^*(F_-) \;=\; \mu_m(u_-)\,n + O(m p^{1/2}),$$
since $C_1(m) \le M(m) \le U(m) = O(m)$ and $|N_k^*(F_-) - p_k(u_-)p| = O(p^{1/2})$ by Lemma 2.2. Applying the identical bound to $\sum_{r \in R} \phi_-(B_r)$ over $\ell_{t'}^-$ via $F_+$, summing $|B_r| \le \phi_+(B_r) + \phi_-(B_r)$, and taking the minimum with part (i) yields (3.3). $\square$

**Theorem 3 (Monodromy Non-Coincidence and Single-Exception Dichotomy for $N(f) \le 6$).** *Let $p \ge 7$ be a prime and let $f(X) = P(X)/Q(X) \in \mathbb{F}_p(X)$ ($\gcd(P, Q) = 1$, $\deg Q \ge 1$) have plane degree $N(f) \le 6$ with $d_0 = 1$ or $\mathcal{C}(f)$ exceptional. Then either:*
1. **Neither** $F_-(X) = f(X) - X$ **nor** $F_+(X) = f(X) + X$ has an exceptional Frobenius coset (and $2 \le d_-, d_+ \le N(f)$); or
2. $N(f) = 5$, $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, and up to simultaneous affine translation $X \mapsto X + x_1$, vertical constant shift, and sign $X \mapsto \pm X$, $f(X)$ belongs to the **Gaussian-CM double-bijection family** $f_K(X)$ of (1.3) ($K \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \setminus (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$), for which one of $F_\mp$ is an exact $D_5$-bijection on $\mathbb{F}_p$ while the opposite diagonal $F_\pm$ has degree $5$ with Frobenius coset $S_5$ ($p \ne 157$, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{1dir}} = \frac{107}{60}$) or $S_5 \setminus A_5$ ($p = 157$ for one choice of $i \bmod 157$, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{1dir}} = \frac{5}{3}$).

We prove every row of Table 3 in Sections 4.1--4.4 without forward references; every symbolic polynomial and integral Bézout identity is verified in pure exact arithmetic in `verify_section_3_algebraic_monodromy`. Note first that **Row 1** ($d_- = d_+ = 2$), **Row 2A-0** ($F_\mp^{-1}(\infty)$ has portrait $(1, 3)$, impossible in $V_4$), and **Rows 4B-i--iii** ($\deg P \le 5 = \deg Q \implies d_- = d_+ = 6$, where Proposition 2.3(i) gives zero exceptional cosets) are immediate.

### 4.1 Degree $d_0 = 3$ Cyclic Covers ($G_0 = C_3$) and the $V_4$ Orbit-Sum Normal Form: Rows 2A and 2B

Over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ ($p \ge 7$), any rational map $h \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ of degree $3$ with $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) = C_3$ has two totally ramified points $z_1 \ne z_2 \in \mathbb{P}^1(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$; when $\infty \notin \{z_1, z_2\}$ (so $h^{-1}(\infty)$ has portrait $(1^3)$), $h(X) = \frac{\alpha (X - z_1)^3 - \beta (X - z_2)^3}{(X - z_1)^3 - \gamma (X - z_2)^3}$ ($\alpha \ne \beta/\gamma$, $\gamma \ne 0$).
- **Row 2A ($\deg P = 3, \deg Q = 2$ squarefree, $N = 3$; cf. Ferraguti--Micheli [17, Theorem 7.1] when $Q_2$ is $\mathbb{F}_p$-irreducible):** Here $\infty \in f^{-1}(\infty)$ forces $\gamma = 1$, so $Q_2(X)$ is proportional to $(X - z_1)^3 - (X - z_2)^3 = -3(z_1 - z_2)\bigl(X^2 - (z_1 + z_2)X + \frac{z_1^2 + z_1 z_2 + z_2^2}{3}\bigr)$. Translating $X$ so $z_2 = -z_1 \ne 0$ gives $Q_2(X) = X^2 + \frac{1}{3}z_1^2$ and $f(X) = c_3 \frac{X^3 + 3 z_1^2 X}{X^2 + \frac{1}{3}z_1^2} + c_0$ ($c_3 \ne 0$). If $c_3 = \pm 1$, one of $F_\mp$ drops to degree $2$ (non-exceptional $S_2$). For $F_\mp$ of degree $3$, $F_\mp(X) - c_0 = \frac{(c_3 \mp 1)X^3 + (3c_3 \mp \frac{1}{3})z_1^2 X}{X^2 + \frac{1}{3}z_1^2}$; matching the unique $C_3$ ratio $\frac{\text{coeff}(X^1)}{\text{coeff}(X^3)} = 3 z_1^2$ for $Q_2$ requires $3c_3 \mp \frac{1}{3} = 3(c_3 \mp 1) \iff \mp \frac{1}{3} = \mp 3 \iff 8 \equiv 0 \pmod p$, impossible for $p \ge 7$.
- **Self-contained $V_4$ Orbit-Sum Normal Form and Row 2B ($\deg P \le 3, \deg Q = 3$ squarefree, $N = 4$):** Let $h \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ ($p \ge 7$) have degree $4$, $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) = V_4$, simple pole at $\infty$ ($e_\infty = 1$), and depressed squarefree cubic denominator $Q_3(X) = X^3 + q_1 X + q_0 = \prod_{i=1}^3 (X - r_i)$ ($r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = 0$, $\Delta_0 := 4q_1^3 + 27q_0^2 \ne 0$). Because $\deg(h) = 4 = |V_4|$, $h$ is a Galois quotient by a Klein-four subgroup $V_4 \le \mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ acting simply transitively on $h^{-1}(\infty) = \{\infty, r_1, r_2, r_3\}$. For each $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, the unique involution $\sigma_i \in V_4 \setminus \{1\}$ swapping $\infty \leftrightarrow r_i$ has the form $\sigma_i(X) = r_i + \frac{\kappa_i}{X - r_i}$; since $\sigma_i$ swaps the remaining two roots $\{r_j, r_k\}$ ($r_j + r_k = -r_i$), $\sigma_i(r_j) = r_k$ forces $\kappa_i = (r_k - r_i)(r_j - r_i) = Q_3'(r_i) = 3r_i^2 + q_1$. Every $V_4$-invariant rational map with simple poles at $\{\infty, r_1, r_2, r_3\}$ is an affine function $c\,\Sigma(X) + c_0$ ($c \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$) of the $V_4$-orbit sum (cf. Ding--Zieve [18, Theorem 1.4]):
  $$\Sigma(X) \;:=\; \sum_{\sigma \in V_4} \sigma(X) \;=\; X + \sum_{i=1}^3 \Bigl(r_i + \frac{3r_i^2 + q_1}{X - r_i}\Bigr) \;=\; \frac{X^4 - 2q_1 X^2 - 8q_0 X + q_1^2}{X^3 + q_1 X + q_0}. \tag{4.1}$$
  In Row 2B ($d_- = d_+ = 4$), if $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(F_\mp) = V_4$, then depressing $Q_3(X) = X^3 + q_1 X + q_0$ puts $F_\mp(X) = c\,\Sigma(X) + c_0$ with leading coefficient $c = \mp 1$, hence $\pm(f(X) - c_0) = \frac{3q_1 X^2 + 9q_0 X - q_1^2}{X^3 + q_1 X + q_0}$. For $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(f) = C_3$, the $X$-discriminant of $w Q_3(X) - (3q_1 X^2 + 9q_0 X - q_1^2)$ would have to be a square in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[w]$. Direct expansion gives the exact polynomial identity
  $$\mathrm{disc}_X\bigl(w(X^3 + q_1 X + q_0) - (3q_1 X^2 + 9q_0 X - q_1^2)\bigr) \;=\; -(4q_1^3 + 27q_0^2)\bigl(w^4 + 18q_1 w^2 - 108q_0 w - 27q_1^2\bigr). \tag{4.2}$$
  If $w^4 + 18q_1 w^2 - 108q_0 w - 27q_1^2 = (w^2 + \alpha w + \beta)^2$ in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[w]$ ($p \ge 7$), comparing $w^3$ gives $\alpha = 0$; comparing $w^1$ gives $-108 q_0 = 0 \implies q_0 = 0$ ($108 = 2^2 3^3$); and comparing $w^2, w^0$ gives $\beta = 9q_1$ and $81q_1^2 = \beta^2 = -27q_1^2 \implies 108 q_1^2 = 0 \implies q_1 = 0$, contradicting $4q_1^3 + 27q_0^2 \ne 0$.

### 4.2 Degree $d_0 = 4$ Klein-Four Covers with $N(f) = 4$: Rows 3A-i and 3A-ii

- **Row 3A-i ($\deg P = 4, \deg Q = 3$ squarefree, $N = 4$):** Depressing $Q_3(X) = X^3 + p_1 X + p_0$ ($4p_1^3 + 27p_0^2 \ne 0$) puts $f(X) = c\,\frac{X^4 - 2p_1 X^2 - 8p_0 X + p_1^2}{X^3 + p_1 X + p_0} + c_0$ by (4.1) ($c \ne 0$; replacing $X \mapsto -X$ exchanges $F_-$ and $F_+$ via $c \mapsto -c$). If $c = 1$, then $F_-(X) = c_0 - \frac{3p_1 X^2 + 9p_0 X - p_1^2}{X^3 + p_1 X + p_0}$ drops to degree $3$, whose discriminant (4.2) is never a square, so $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(F_-) = S_3 \ne C_3$. For $c \ne 1$, $H_g(X; T) := c(X^4 - 2p_1 X^2 - 8p_0 X + p_1^2) - (X + T)(X^3 + p_1 X + p_0) = \sum_{j=0}^4 a_j(T) X^j$ ($a_4 = c - 1 \ne 0$) is a separable quartic over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(T)$. If $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(F_-) = V_4$, then **Ferrari's cubic resolvent** $\mathcal{R}_g(U; T) := U^3 - a_2 U^2 + (a_3 a_1 - 4 a_4 a_0) U - (a_3^2 a_0 - 4 a_4 a_2 a_0 + a_4 a_1^2)$ splits into three roots $U(T) \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p(T)$. Since $\mathcal{R}_g$ is monic in $U$ with $\deg_T a_2 = 0$, $\deg_T(a_3 a_1 - 4a_4 a_0) \le 2$, and $\deg_T(a_3^2 a_0 - 4a_4 a_2 a_0 + a_4 a_1^2) \le 3$, Gauss's lemma and degree comparison force every rational root to be linear in $T$, $U(T) = \alpha T + \beta \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p[T]$. Expanding in $T$ gives $\mathcal{R}_g(\alpha T + \beta; T) = Q_3(\alpha) T^3 + O(T^2)$, and whenever $Q_3(\alpha) = 0$, its $T^2$-coefficient factors as $(3\alpha^2 + p_1)\bigl(\beta - 2c(p_1 + 2\alpha^2) + (p_1 + \alpha^2)\bigr)$. Since $Q_3$ is squarefree ($3r^2 + p_1 \ne 0$ at each of its three simple roots $r$), every linear root must have the form $U_r(T) := r T + 2c(p_1 + 2r^2) - (p_1 + r^2)$ for some root $r$ of $Q_3(r) = 0$. Substituting $U_r(T)$ with $p_0 = -r(r^2 + p_1)$ yields the **exact $T$-independent polynomial identity**
  $$\mathcal{R}_g\bigl(r T + 2c(p_1 + 2r^2) - (p_1 + r^2);\; T\bigr) \;\equiv\; c\,(3r^2 + p_1)^2\,(3r^2 + 4p_1) \tag{4.3}$$
  (`verify_section_3_algebraic_monodromy`). For $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(F_-) = V_4$, all three distinct roots $r_1, r_2, r_3$ of $Q_3$ would have to satisfy $3r_i^2 + 4p_1 = 0$, impossible since $3X^2 + 4p_1 = 0$ has at most $2 < 3$ roots.
- **Row 3A-ii ($\deg P = 4, Q = (X - r_0)^2$, $\lambda = (2, 2), e_\infty = 2$):** Translating $X \mapsto X + r_0$ puts $f^{-1}(\infty) = \{2 \cdot 0,\, 2 \cdot \infty\}$. Any $V_4 \le \mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ having $\{0, \infty\}$ as a size-$2$ orbit contains the unique nontrivial involution of $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ fixing both $0$ and $\infty$, namely $\iota(X) = -X$. Hence $f(-X) = f(X)$, whereas $F_\mp(-X) - F_\mp(X) = \pm 2 X \ne 0$; if $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(F_\mp) = V_4$, $F_\mp^{-1}(\infty) = \{2 \cdot 0,\, 2 \cdot \infty\}$ would likewise force $F_\mp(-X) = F_\mp(X)$, a contradiction.

### 4.3 Plane Degree $N(f) = 5$ Covers: Wronskian Lemma, Differential Congruence, and Integral Bézout Certificates

Let $h \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ ($p \ge 7$) be a tamely ramified rational map of degree $5$ with $G = G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) \in \{C_5, D_5\}$ and Galois closure $C_\Omega \to \mathbb{P}^1$. Every nontrivial element of $G$ has cycle type $(5)$ (ramification degree $4$) or $(1, 2, 2)$ (ramification degree $2$, when $G = D_5$). If $h$ has $a$ branch values of portrait $(5)$ and $b_{\mathrm{br}}$ branch values of portrait $(1, 2, 2)$, Riemann--Hurwitz on $h$ gives $-2 = -10 + 4a + 2b_{\mathrm{br}} \iff 2a + b_{\mathrm{br}} = 4$, yielding exactly **three** families up to independent Möbius transformations on domain and codomain (cf. [13, 14, 19]):
- **Type I ($G = C_5$, $(a, b_{\mathrm{br}}) = (2, 0)$):** Placing both $(5)$-ramified points and their images at $\{0, \infty\}$ gives the Kummer power $z \mapsto z^5$.
- **Type II ($G = D_5$, $(a, b_{\mathrm{br}}) = (1, 2)$):** Abhyankar's lemma on $C_\Omega \to \mathbb{P}^1$ ($|D_5| = 10$) gives $2g(C_\Omega) - 2 = -20 + 2(4) + 2 \cdot 5(1) = -2$, so $C_\Omega \cong \mathbb{P}^1_t$ with $D_5 = \langle t \mapsto \zeta_5 t,\, t \mapsto t^{-1} \rangle$; descending to $\mathbb{P}^1_z = \mathbb{P}^1_t / \langle t \mapsto t^{-1} \rangle$ ($z = t + t^{-1}$) gives the Dickson polynomial $D_5(z) = z^5 - 5z^3 + 5z$ (branch portraits $(5)$ at $\infty$ and $(1, 2, 2)$ at $w = \pm 2$).
- **Type III ($G = D_5$, $(a, b_{\mathrm{br}}) = (0, 4)$):** Over all four $(1, 2, 2)$-branch points, the order-$2$ inertia groups intersect $C_5 \trianglelefteq D_5$ trivially, so $C_\Omega \to C_\Omega / C_5 =: \widehat{E}$ is an étale $C_5$-cover and $2g(C_\Omega) - 2 = -20 + 4 \cdot 5(1) = 0 \implies g(C_\Omega) = g(\widehat{E}) = 1$. Choosing an involution fixed point $O \in C_\Omega$ makes $E = (C_\Omega, O)$ an elliptic curve with a point $P_5$ of exact order $5$ such that $h$ is the x-coordinate quotient $E / \{\pm 1\} \to (E / \langle P_5 \rangle) / \{\pm 1\}$ [19, Theorem 1]. Every pair $(E, P_5)$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ ($p \ge 7$) has a unique Tate normal form $E_b : y^2 + (1 - b)xy - by = x^3 - bx^2$ with $P_5 = (0, 0)$ ($b \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p \setminus \{0\}$, $\Delta(E_b) = b^5(b^2 - 11b - 1) \ne 0$), on which Vélu's explicit formulas ($z = x$, $B_b(z) := z(z - b)$, $D_b(z) := B_b(z)^2$) give $\psi_b(z) = N_b(z)/D_b(z)$ with
  $$N_b(z) \;=\; z^5 - 2b z^4 + (b^3 + 3b^2 - b) z^3 + (-3b^3 + 3b^2) z^2 + (b^4 - 3b^3) z + b^4. \tag{4.4}$$
  Setting $D_y(z) := 4z^3 + (b^2 - 6b + 1)z^2 + 2b(b - 1)z + b^2$ (the $2$-torsion cubic of $E_b$) and $W_b(z) := N_b'(z) B_b(z) - 2 N_b(z) B_b'(z)$ ($\psi_b'(z) = W_b(z)/B_b(z)^3$), invariance of the Néron differential $dx/(2y + (1 - b)x - b)$ under $E_b \to \widehat{E}_b$ yields the exact polynomial identity in $\mathbb{Z}[b, z]$ (`verify_section_3_algebraic_monodromy`):
  $$W_b(z)^2\,D_y(z) \;=\; \widehat{D}_y\bigl(N_b(z),\, D_b(z)\bigr) \;:=\; 4 N_b^3 + (b^2 - 6b + 1) N_b^2 D_b + (-20b^3 - 38b^2 + 18b) N_b D_b^2 + (-4b^5 - 40b^4 + 20b^3 - 59b^2 + 4b) D_b^3. \tag{4.5}$$

We first dispatch **Row 4A-iv** ($\deg P = 5, \deg Q = 3$, $\lambda = (1, 2, 2), e_\infty = 2$):
- Normalize by $\mathrm{Aff}_1(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ so $Q_3(X) = X(X - 1)^2$, and subtract $c_3 Q_3(X)$ so $P_5(X) = c_5 X^5 + c_4 X^4 + d_2 X^2 + d_1 X + d_0$ ($c_3 = 0$). Only Types II and III possess a $(1, 2, 2)$ fiber. In Type II, placing $\infty$ at a double preimage of $D_5(z) = \pm 2$ ($z_0^2 \pm z_0 - 1 = 0$) gives $c_4 = d_1 = -5 c_5 z_0$; in Type III, placing $(X = \infty, 0, 1)$ at $(z = 0, \infty, b)$ via $z = b/X$ (or $z = b(X - 1)/X$, related by $b \mapsto -1/b$) gives $b^{-4} X^5 N_b(b/X) - c_3 Q_3(X) = X^5 + (b - 3)X^4 + (b^2 - 3b + 5)X^2 + (b - 3)X + b$, which again satisfies **$c_4 - d_1 = 0$ identically**. Yet normalizing $\mp X Q_3(X) = \mp(X^4 - 2X^3 + X^2)$ by $\mp 2 Q_3(X)$ gives $\mp(X^4 - 3X^2 + 2X)$, shifting $c_4 - d_1$ by $\mp(1 - 2) = \pm 1 \ne 0$ in all characteristics.

Next we treat all $N(f) = 5$ covers with double-pole denominator $Q_4(X) = (X - a_1)^2(X - a_2)^2$ ($a_1 \ne a_2$)—simultaneously resolving **Row 4A-iii** and **Row 3B-i** with **zero resultants**.

**Lemma 4.1 (Direct Wronskian Classification of $(1, 2, 2)$-Pole $D_5$ Covers).** *Let $p \ge 7$, and let $h(X) = P(X)/Q_4(X)$ be a reduced rational function over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ with $G^{\mathrm{geom}}(h) = D_5$, $Q_4(X) = X^2(X - 1)^2$, and $P(X) = X Q_4(X) + r_3 X^3 + r_2 X^2 + r_1 X + r_0$ ($r_0 = P(0) \ne 0$, $P(1) = r_0 + r_1 + r_2 + r_3 \ne 0$; any $(1, 2, 2)$-pole cover with $e_\infty = 1$ attains this form after $w \mapsto \alpha w + \gamma$ on the codomain). Then $(r_0, r_1, r_2, r_3) = (\beta,\, 1 - 3\beta,\, 3\beta - 3,\, \beta^{-1} + 2 - \beta)$ for some $\beta \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$; in particular, $h$ satisfies the **universal linear invariant $3 r_0 + r_1 = 1$**.*

*Proof.* Differentiating $h(X) = P(X)/(X^2(X - 1)^2)$ gives $h'(X) = W_6(X)/(X^3(X - 1)^3)$, where
$$W_6(X) \;:=\; P'(X)(X^2 - X) - 2 P(X)(2X - 1) \;=\; X^6 - 3X^5 + (3 - r_3)X^4 - (r_3 + 2r_2 + 1)X^3 - 3r_1 X^2 + (r_1 - 4r_0)X + 2r_0$$
is monic of degree $6$. Since $h^{-1}(\infty)$ already accounts for one $(1, 2, 2)$-branch point, $h$ belongs either to Type III (three finite $(1, 2, 2)$-branch values $w_1, w_2, w_3$, with $P - w_j Q_4 = (X - u_j) S_j(X)^2$ and $W_6 = S_1 S_2 S_3$) or to Type II (one finite $(5)$-branch value $w_*$ with $P - w_* Q_4 = (X - z_*)^5$ and one finite $(1, 2, 2)$-branch value $w_1$ with $P - w_1 Q_4 = (X - u_1) S_1(X)^2$, where $W_6 = (X - z_*)^4 S_1$). *Consequently, since $b x_0(x_0 - b) \ne 0$, every solution in every characteristic $p \ge 7$ satisfies $b^2 + 1 = 0$.*

*Proof.* For (i), $\frac{c}{\psi_b(x_0 + 1/X) - \psi_b(x_0)} = \frac{c D_b(x_0) X^5 D_b(x_0 + 1/X)}{X^5(N_b(x_0 + 1/X) D_b(x_0) - N_b(x_0) D_b(x_0 + 1/X))}$, and $N_b(z) D_b(x_0) - N_b(x_0) D_b(z) = (z - x_0) M(z)$ gives $X^5(N_b(x_0 + 1/X) D_b(x_0) - N_b(x_0) D_b(x_0 + 1/X)) = X^4 M(x_0 + 1/X)$ with leading $X^4$-coefficient $M(x_0) = N_b'(x_0) D_b(x_0) - N_b(x_0) D_b'(x_0) = B_b(x_0) W_b(x_0) \ne 0$ and constant term $D_b(x_0) \ne 0$.

For (ii), let $z_1, \dots, z_4 \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p \setminus \{0, b, x_0\}$ be the four distinct roots of $M(z) = 0$, so $X_i := (z_i - x_0)^{-1}$ ($i = 1, \dots, 4$) are the four distinct roots of $Q_4(X) = 0$. Differentiating $Q_4(X) = M(x_0)^{-1} X^4 M(x_0 + 1/X)$ at $X_i$ gives $Q_4'(X_i) = -M(x_0)^{-1} X_i^2 M'(z_i)$, while differentiating $(z - x_0) M(z) = N_b(z) D_b(x_0) - N_b(x_0) D_b(z)$ at $z_i$ (using $N_b(x_0) = N_b(z_i) D_b(x_0)/D_b(z_i)$) gives $(z_i - x_0) M'(z_i) = D_b(x_0) W_b(z_i)/B_b(z_i)$, hence $Q_4'(X_i)^2 = M(x_0)^{-2} D_b(x_0)^2 X_i^6 W_b(z_i)^2 / D_b(z_i)$. Evaluating (4.5) along the fiber $N_b(z_i)/D_b(z_i) = w_0 = N_b(x_0)/D_b(x_0)$ gives $W_b(z_i)^2 D_y(z_i) = \frac{D_b(z_i)^3}{D_b(x_0)^3} W_b(x_0)^2 D_y(x_0)$ (with $D_y(z_i) \ne 0$ since $w_0$ is unramified). **Limitations and Open Directions.**
- *Higher plane degrees $N(f) \ge 7$:* At degree $d = 7$, new exceptional genus-$0$ monodromy groups appear ($C_7$, $D_7$, and outer cosets in $\mathrm{AGL}_1(7)$ [15], while $\mathrm{PSL}_2(7) \le S_7$ introduces doubly transitive non-alternating cosets), and $d! = 5040$ enlarges the effective Chebotarev threshold. Extending the hybrid potential and monodromy non-coincidence machinery to arbitrary bounded $N(f)$ is a natural algebraic problem.
- *Higher-genus curves and non-prime moduli:* Our curve parameterization relies on genus-$0$ graph structure $Y Q(X) - P(X) = 0$ over prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p \ge 7$). Irreducible plane curves $C \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of geometric genus $g(C) \ge 1$ (such as modular elliptic curves $Y^2 \equiv X^3 + a X + b \pmod p$, which have up to $2$ points per vertical column rather than $1$) and prime-power Hjelmslev lifts [16] require new multi-branch coarse-fiber ideas.
- *Short fractional-period windows and unrestricted sets:* For non-integer $n / p \in (1, k_0(N))$, tightening the boundary-strip convex combination Lemma 3.2 below $k_0(N) \in \{12, 14, 44, 59\}$ remains open, as do wild-ramification primes $p \in \{2, 3, 5\}$ and the unrestricted $1.80n$ no-three-in-line problem.
