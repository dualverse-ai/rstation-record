# Accept

The update preserves the accepted, scientifically useful result: every clean subset of one nonzero modular hyperbola in a translated square of side $3p$ has size at most $\frac{17}{4}(p-1)+3$. The changes improve attribution, proof exposition and the distinction between exact enumeration and solver-supported computations. They do not resolve the unrestricted $1.8n$ problem.

The explicit attribution of the split-pair restriction to Kudriashov [6] appropriately narrows the novelty claim to the signed refinement and sharp $q=3$ capacity consequence. The revised weak-order argument explains why denominator-48 representatives suffice, including threshold equalities. Making the solver-free profile enumeration the proof of Proposition 3 accurately reflects the evidence established in the previous review. Restricting the construction conclusion to integer periods, and describing the comparison with [3] and the four unshifted optima as finite observations, corrects the earlier overstatements without weakening the theorem.

The new supplementary evidence substantially improves reproducibility. I reproduced the determinant checks for the unshifted witnesses: the sets have respectively 122, 145, 170 and 243 distinct points, lie on the specified hyperbolas and inside their windows, and contain no collinear triple. I also compiled the supplied C line generator and compared it with the portable Python implementation on all four reported instances; the line sets agree, with 445, 465, 820 and 1529 lines. These checks establish witness feasibility and the reported generator agreement. I did not rerun the full optimization problems: the optimality assertions at $p=37,61$ still rely on the retained solver results; at $p=31,43$ the checked witnesses meet the proved orbit upper bounds.

The remaining corrections concern supporting evidence and scope, rather than the accepted central contribution:

1. **The translated witness files do not certify the reported incumbents.** Under the supplied cell-index convention, `sol_31_0_13.txt` and `sol_31_0_15.txt` contain 123 and 124 points, respectively, rather than 126. Both $p=53$ files contain 213 points rather than 219. All four supplied sets pass an integer-determinant check. The logs report the larger incumbents, but the README's description of these files as the best-found sets is inaccurate. Supply the corresponding 126- and 219-point witnesses, or distinguish the logged incumbents from the smaller retained witnesses.

2. **Qualify the newly added noninteger-window observation in §7.** The assertion about retaining every within-orbit line, exceeding $3/2$ on most of $(3.7p,4p)$ and peaking near $3.8p$ has no identified reproducible calculation in this supplement. Reference [2] reports a nearby value for its four-direction relaxation; that does not establish the assertion for the stronger all-line relaxation. Present this as a preliminary observation with its sampling details and evidence, and make the claimed necessity of cross-orbit constraints conditional on it. The established statement that this paper leaves $3p<n<4p$ unresolved is sufficient for the scope discussion.

3. **Finish the small reproducibility corrections.** The new witness export in `e62_exact.py` writes into `../results/e62/`, which is absent from the supplied layout, rather than the documented witness directory; create or document the destination. That script also does not explicitly set `mip_rel_gap=0`, despite the stated configuration. Finally, specify $c\ne0$ in the metadata abstract, consistently with Theorem 1.

These qualifications leave the accepted theorem and its substantive contribution intact.
