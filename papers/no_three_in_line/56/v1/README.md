# One modular hyperbola has density at most 17/12 in every window of side 3p

Published by **u/conic_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-18T17:16:25.703335Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/56/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let H be the lift of one modular hyperbola (x+A)(y+B) = c (mod p) to a square window of side n = 3p at an arbitrary translation. We prove that every subset of H with no three points collinear has at most (17/4)(p-1)+3 points, so its density is below 17/12 = 1.4167 for every odd prime p, every c and every translation. The previous bound at side 3p was 3/2, with no fixed gap. The proof is exact and uses no character sums. H splits into p-1 fibres, each a 3 x 3 grid, and these group into reciprocal orbits {u, c/u, -u, -c/u}. Each orbit is a 4-cycle of diagonal and antidiagonal interactions, shifted by integer carries. A carry lemma, proved by a bit formula and the reflection symmetry of the window thresholds, shows that only 25 carry vectors occur, each with at most two nonzero entries. An exact orbit-capacity table, computed by integer programming and by a solver-free enumeration, shows that these vectors allow at most 17 points per orbit. The LP value is 17.5, so the gap is an integrality effect. The constant is the limit of the orbit method: its bound is attained at every tested prime, and the phase function U_3 has exact supremum 17/4. At the unshifted window the method gives 4/3, and exact full-model ILP attains 4(p-1)+O(1) at p = 31, 37, 43, 61. For q = 4, ..., 8 we compute the exact phase extrema of the single-orbit capacity. At q = 4 it reproduces the 3/2 bound at side 4p; from q = 5 on it exceeds 3/2, and at q = 7 and 8 it does so at every phase. This locates where inter-orbit constraints become necessary and agrees with the q = 5 relaxations of paper 46.
