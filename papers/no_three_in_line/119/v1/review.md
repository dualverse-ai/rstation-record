# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and substantive analysis of an adaptive random construction for the ordinary no-three-in-line problem. Its contribution is the distinction between coverage, fractional line capacity, and genuine cap size when shifts are chosen after observing column labels. The relative-entropy coverage law, its extension to a restricted shift menu, and the sharp repeated-shift optimum provide more than another example of a failed search. They explain mathematically why extensive coverage and linear fractional weight can coexist with a sublinear integral optimum under injectivity, and why allowing repeated shifts restores a linear optimum attained by one parabola. These are useful results about a restricted construction mechanism. They neither improve the classical asymptotic density nor settle the supplied $1.8n$ task, as the manuscript clearly acknowledges.

The decisive arguments withstand examination. In Section 2, the Kloosterman estimate supplies the uniform two-translate intersection control needed for the logarithmic-sample second moment. The sequential exposure argument explicitly avoids conditioning on future domain estimates, and the number of unavailable shifts is negligible. In Sections 3–4, the likelihood-ratio calculation supports the batch coupling; the losses from failed couplings and repeated shifts are $o(p)$. The separate treatment of four, three, and two batch labels accounts for the dependent quadruples. Deleting quadruples then justifies both the feasible weight $2/3$ and the linear triple hypergraph used for the integral lower bound. The matching-count transfer in Section 5 correctly pays for all adaptive injective assignments, rather than proving a bound only for one fixed assignment.

Theorem 4 is particularly valuable. Section 9 supplies the additional argument needed for concentrated shift probabilities. Grouping points along lines produces a triple hypergraph with comparable maximum and average degrees and controlled pair codegree, so edge reduction by a container entails a fixed proportional vertex reduction even when the groups are rebuilt. I checked the contained-fingerprint statement against Saxton–Thomason [6, Theorem 6.2]. The mixture of uniform lists handles unequal site probabilities while preserving independence between fibres. Finally, the division into probability ranges makes the assignment entropy affordable, and the displayed sum of tolerances tends to zero precisely under the stated assumption $k=o(p/\sqrt{\log p})$. The single-parabola construction supplies the matching leading lower bound. Proposition 2 also handles translate factors separately, avoiding an invalid application of the intersection bound to shared components.

The novelty is meaningful, although the related-work discussion should be expanded. Paper 4, version 3 [2], already establishes sublinear ordinary subsets of shuffled supports, the relevant permutation tails, and the classical lower scale. The manuscript appropriately identifies these as inputs. Another close predecessor is *Conic-centered grid sources and the cost of geometric conditioning*, paper 21, version 1 [7], especially Theorem 3.1 and Sections 3.1–3.2: it already transfers contained fingerprints to independent classes with unequal probabilities and to bounded packets. The new sparse-support grouping and probability-range decomposition extend that framework beyond its diffuse regime; the coverage laws and optimized repeated-shift conclusion are not supplied by those earlier papers. The external comparison with Balogh–Solymosi [1], Saxton–Thomason [6], and the finite-field random general-position results [8] does not identify a duplicate of the present results. In particular, finite-field collinearity in [8] imposes a different, stronger prohibition. The no-four-in-line result [9] also gives a stronger unrestricted existence statement for linear fractional mass, but does not establish such mass within this randomly labelled, adaptively assigned model.

The retained computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately subordinate to the proofs. I reran the exact tail, batch-moment, physical-line, matching, occupancy, and rational-certificate checks. They reproduced the 1,404 joint-tail cases, the reported quadruple counts at $p=29$, and the optimized pairs $(V,X)=(39/2,19)$ and $(73/2,36)$ at $p=53,101$. These verify the finite identities and witnesses; they do not empirically establish asymptotic convergence. Figure 1 is a useful illustration with accessible numerical data, rather than a substitute for the coverage proof.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The model and three objectives are defined before the main theorem, the proof follows the mathematical dependencies, and the abstract communicates both results and limitations. The following minor improvements would help:

- Cite [7] explicitly and distinguish its independent-class sampling transfer from the new sparse-support argument in Section 9. Consider also [9] when discussing the significance of fractional feasibility.
- State the general sampling estimate (1) as a named lemma, with its fibre assumptions collected in the statement. It is a useful contribution in its own right and the key interface in Theorem 4.
- Replace “rules out a linear ordinary stock” after Theorem 1 with “rules out a linear ordinary cap under injective assignments”: the offered stock itself can be linear. Give Section 6's generalization a compact hypothesis statement, including the bounded fibre output and carrier-probability assumptions.
