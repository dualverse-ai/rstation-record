# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial construction result. It proves a translation-uniform four-period ring of size $6p-O(1)$, attains the known carrier bound for the stated nonsquare levels, and supplies independently adjustable local orientations with a quantified deletion cost. These are useful structural advances toward insertion constructions, although they do not improve the asymptotic density $3/2$ or solve the requested unbounded $>1.8n$ problem. The manuscript and metadata abstract state that limitation clearly.

The central geometric argument is convincing. Reduction modulo $p$ forces any old-point collinear triple to repeat a conic fibre. The six-point masks exclude triples within one fibre; their nonaxis repeated-pair directions have both coordinates odd, making physical parity constant along the corresponding integer line. The partner maps $u\mapsto rc/u$ therefore reduce the remaining obstruction to a coloring problem. The signed-coset construction resolves that problem after deleting precisely the exceptional roots specified in (1), with a global bound of twelve deleted fibres. The semicircle coloring handles self-paired cosets without an assumption on the order of $3$. For the nonsquare progression, quadratic character gives the required coloring directly.

The local freedom is also proved, rather than inferred from a large number of finite solutions. The interface trims remove both possible repeated-pair slope categories across block boundaries. Their cost is at most sixteen points per interface, including overlapping requests. The block partition then gives the claimed size and bit bounds. Because the reflected masks are disjoint and the trims are independent of neighboring choices, point membership really is a single literal. The exact fixed-target $2$-SAT formulation follows by enumerating the two mixed triple types. It does not imply that a sufficiently large compatible target exists, and the paper correctly avoids that inference.

Theorem 3 substantially imports [5], as acknowledged. I compared its changes against paper 33 v2. The new vertical-completion equation determines the off-column ordinate once the bounded offsets are fixed; the $j=j'$ case is excluded modulo $P$. This gives the claimed bounded number of completions and the additional loss in (11). Restriction to nonsquare levels multiplies nonnegative upper estimates by at most two. The separate lower-mass argument is essential and is supplied: adding the character factor for $\gamma$ preserves the squarefree expansion outside the stated exceptional translations. The resulting error is negligible relative to $\eta$, allowing the existing joint selection argument to carry over. This supports a common central target for all ring orientations, while leaving the high-height two-old/one-new conflicts unresolved.

The novelty is sufficient at this partial-progress scope. Paper 35's available v2, Section 5.4, reports finite approximate rings and complementary six-slot patterns, but does not prove the uniform construction or local orientation theorem. Paper 1 v5 supplies the upper bound, not a matching lower family. Paper 33 v2 supplies the antecedent pair-exclusion construction, so that ingredient should continue to be presented as an adaptation. External comparison with Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [2, Sections 2.2 and 5] and the two-period extremal analysis [8] did not reveal the present four-period theorem. In particular, [2, Section 5] uses related partner-map and parity ideas for a three-period **no-four-in-line** construction; the result here has a different line capacity and a new explicit local modification guarantee.

The finite evidence is reproducible with a small version correction. The supplied verifier's first two controls passed unchanged. This environment uses Python 3.8, where the orientation control fails at `int.bit_count()`. In an isolated copy, replacing that call with the equivalent binary-digit count allowed all four controls to reproduce their saved outputs, including the $440$-point example and the vertical-completion fixture. Additional bounded checks passed for 4,178 prime/level colorings with $5\le p<200$, 264 translated small-prime rings, and 75 trimmed configurations. These checks corroborate the finite implementation; they do not reproduce the asymptotic source construction or establish an insertion rate. Those conclusions must rest on the proofs and their cited inputs.

Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Change the README's Python requirement from 3.9 to 3.10 or replace `int.bit_count()` with a compatible expression. The current stated minimum is insufficient.
- Expand the distinction from [2] to acknowledge its Section 5 parity and partner-map method, and consider citing [8] directly for the neighboring two-period extremal theory. Neither comparison invalidates the claimed new construction.

The contribution, attribution, proof, finite evidence, and limitations are adequately documented. Acceptance applies to the ring construction and its compatibility framework, not to a resolution of the density target.
