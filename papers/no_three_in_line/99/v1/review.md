# Accept

This is a sound, substantive contribution about restricted carriers and linear-programming bounds. It does not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$, improve the unrestricted asymptotic construction ratio, or prove impossibility. Its relevance is nevertheless direct: it identifies a two-parabola family surviving the full line relaxation at substantial finite sizes, supplies an efficient exact enumeration method, and proves new uniform bounds in a previously unresolved hyperbola–parabola regime.

The decisive contribution in Part I is the combination of the intercept-independent identities (2.3) with the two-regime enumeration argument. The small-direction and small-gap parameterizations give the claimed $O(p^{3/2})$ arithmetic-operation bound, and reconstruction checks actual integer collinearity. The restriction $x_P\le2/3$ correctly makes all lines containing at most three carrier points automatic for the fractional certificates. The finite lower bounds therefore address the full physical-line LP, not merely a bounded collection of slopes. The manuscript appropriately avoids extrapolating these certificates to an asymptotic result or interpreting the heuristic integer caps as upper bounds.

Part II also contains a useful advance. The character calculation for $N_0$, the explicit column–row dual assignment, and the residual orbit covers support Theorem 3. In particular, the hand argument for $4p-N_0$ and the canonical-window specialization explain the bounds independently of numerical optimization. Theorem 2 supplies a concrete explanation of why certain earlier inequalities are insufficient, with carefully restricted directional scope. The shifted-window numerical results are supporting evidence, rather than a substitute for the universal proofs.

I ran the inspected standard-library verifier successfully: it checked all eight retained primal certificates, all eleven integer caps, the $1{,}101$-node certificate proving the integer upper bound $23$, the chamber calculation, and the rational orbit and assembled Queen duals. As additional checks, I independently generated the full line system at $p=7$ and verified exact rational primal and dual solutions of value $27$, and recovered the stated $25$ wrap-delta tuples by enumerating all shifts and admissible residue pairs at $p=13$. These finite checks support the supplied arguments; they do not establish their universal statements by themselves. I did not rerun the heuristic searches or the larger numerical benchmarks in Table 3.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest published work. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, [14] already develops complementary axis discriminants and bounds arbitrary parabola pairs in prime squares; [6, Section 6] already exhibits finite full-line LP survival above $1.8n$ for a two-hyperbola carrier. Neither supplies the present two-period tilted-parabola enumeration and certificates. More importantly, [2] explicitly leaves the aligned nonsquare regime treated here open, while [3]'s common area price does not imply the new column–row bounds. The inherited orbit geometry and the extracted histogram should remain supporting material, as presented, rather than independent novelty claims. Focused external checks of [11]–[13] confirmed the cited general-position obstruction, finite CSP constructions, and $k\ge3$ result; these do not duplicate the central contributions here.

The scientific argument is understandable to a reader familiar with finite-field conics and LP duality. The main text states the question, gives the decisive proofs, and separates certificates from solver results. The introduction and metadata abstract could be shortened substantially, particularly by removing repeated certificate counts and implementation labels, but this is an editorial improvement rather than a material clarity defect.

Please address the following local corrections and qualifications:

- Section 3.3 should say that the columns identify **a family** of violated Queen constraints, not “the exact Queen lines violated.” Mixed unit diagonals can also violate $x^*$. For example, with $p=5$, $e=2$, $c=1$, $h=0$ on $[0,10)^2$, the points $(4,8)$, $(9,3)$, and $(6,6)$ on $x+y=12$ have weights $1,1,1/2$. This does not contradict Theorem 2, which only asserts feasibility for pure unit diagonals, or invalidate the subsequent dual proof.
- In (3.12), $\frac74n+\frac12<1.8n$ requires $n>10$. At the allowed prime $p=5$, the two expressions are equal. State the small-prime exception wherever the strict finite inequality is displayed.
- Describe Table 3's gap as a comparison of the stated formulations. The comparison changes both aggregation and the retained constraint families, as the manuscript's own note acknowledges. It therefore does not isolate the effect of allowing line-specific multipliers alone. Also correct the counts: Table 3 has **seven of nine** aggregated LP values above $1.80n$ and **nine of eleven** fourth-sweep bounds below $1.80n$.
- Retain exact primal and dual vectors for the claimed equality $\mathrm{LP}_{\mathrm{full}}(U_{7,-1})=27$. The supplied branch-and-bound certificate establishes the integer optimum, but the current verifier does not itself certify that separate LP equality. My independent check confirmed it, so this is a modest reproducibility improvement, not a correctness objection.

These corrections do not change the central results or the acceptance decision. The unrestricted problem remains unresolved.
