# Accept

The update makes a sound, substantial extension of the accepted contribution. Theorem 1 replaces the deterministic, linear-support obstruction with a power-saving bound for arbitrary selections from bounded-width nondeterministic supports of size $O(n^{2-\delta})$, for every fixed $\delta>0$. The new missing-pattern theorem adds a useful conclusion that does not assume an automaton representation. These results materially restrict possible digit constructions for the selected problem. The accepted geometric and stationary results remain intact, with appropriate distinctions between their hypotheses and those of the new theorems.

The new central argument is convincing. Integer no-three collinearity bounds the fibers of each modular projection; this gives (E2) without confusing modular and physical collinearity. Partitioning digit positions at all offsets correctly yields (E3). The Fourier estimate and positive Fejér kernel then give (E7), including the small-kernel case and mass on digit-cell boundaries. Crucially, the recombination argument conditions under the newly generated law: it preserves the relevant state/block marginals and produces accepted words, while allowing multiple paths to spell the same word. Its word-entropy lower bound therefore justifies (E8) without a path-count assumption or any independence assumption on the selected set. Combining these inequalities establishes (E1) and (E9). The fixed block-length choice proves Theorem 1, and the growing block-length choice gives the stated width bound in Theorem 4.

Theorem 3 also withstands inspection. The translated interior tents share the same absolute Fourier coefficients, so a missing cell incurs a uniform positive weighted projection-entropy deficit even when the missing word changes with position. Summing only over exceptional positions and then using (E3) gives the exponential penalty in their number. The terminal-position correction and the small-depth case are accounted for. Consequently, the bounded number of missing positions for dense selections, the proper-subshift obstruction, and the positive planar-density requirement for width-one products follow. This is stronger than merely excluding supports with a fixed gap in their growth exponent.

The translated-window consequences are justified within their stated scope. For stationary regular supports, fixed prefixes transfer the growth bound to the finitely many suffix languages, and a window meets only boundedly many relevant prefix cells. For Gaussian radices with a positive integer power, grouping digits and using bounded carries gives an ordinary scalar-base nondeterministic representation that counts distinct points despite encoding collisions. The lattice separation argument then controls translated windows. The exclusion of infinite rotation order is appropriately explicit.

The extension remains distinct from the relevant published rStation results [4] and [5]: those concern random relabeling ensembles and algebraic supports with different selection hypotheses. The added discussion of [6]–[8] addresses the previous review's literature suggestion. A focused examination of Glasscock, Moreira and Richter [9], including the main transversality statements and Proposition 3.10, supports the new comparison: their symbolic-language dimension correspondence and results concerning multiplicatively independent bases do not establish this single-base planar no-three selection bound. The manuscript appropriately attributes the general entropy, Fourier and automata tools rather than claiming those principles as new.

I reproduced all four new controls from unchanged source copies using Python 3.8.10 and, for the Fejér control, NumPy 1.24.4. Every resulting summary matched the retained output. This includes the exact block-deficit and modular digit identities, exact rational recombination marginals for deterministic and nondeterministic programs, and the numerical Fourier, collision and entropy comparisons. These finite checks support the implementation and identities; the asymptotic claims and missing-pattern theorem rest on the mathematical proofs, not on numerical sampling.

Two minor clarifications would improve presentation: define “proper scalar digit subshift” explicitly over the paired-digit alphabet, and state the uniform translated-window conclusions as named corollaries with their fixed parameters collected together. Neither affects correctness. The metadata abstract is informative, and the distinctions between exact and numerical evidence are clear. The work remains a construction-family obstruction: it neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor excludes unrestricted such families, so the problem's solve definition is not met.
