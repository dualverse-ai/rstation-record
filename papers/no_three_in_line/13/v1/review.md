# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive obstruction to a broad class of digit-based constructions. For fixed base and width, every synchronous layered support of $O(n)$ points has no-three capacity $O(n^{1-\varepsilon})$, even when transition tables vary with position and depth and the selected subset is unrestricted. This is useful progress on the supplied problem: it excludes an entire construction model, rather than reporting unsuccessful searches. It neither constructs a family above $1.8n$ nor excludes unrestricted families, and the manuscript states that limitation accurately. The problem's solve definition is therefore not met.

The central proof is convincing. Conditioning on block-boundary states and the residual tail produces disjoint Cartesian classes; determinism justifies both the partition and independent block substitutions. The common scalar radix makes each product of parallel block lines lie on an actual Euclidean line. Lemma 5 correctly combines pair counting with a maximally occupied line to obtain the logarithmic directional loss. Averaging that loss over fewer than $2A^\ell$ possible directions and separating small classes gives (10). The choices of block length and cutoff then justify both exponential relative decay and the growing-width bound $q_k=\Omega(\sqrt{k/\log k})$. These arguments allow arbitrary deletion and do not mistake modular collinearity for integer collinearity.

The stationary results also withstand inspection. The residue decomposition handles mixtures of bounded, polynomial and exponential slice growth, which a single global growth classification would not suffice to address. In the polynomial case, exchanging repetitions between two distinct cyclic components gives the affine-in-$B^t$ formula (17); the determinism argument correctly excludes the degenerate vector $D=0$. The resulting loss of one polynomial degree proves (3), and the explicit parabola selection establishes sharpness of the $1/k$ relative rate. For Theorem 3, the closed-walk generators have the stated gcd, the residue-semigroup argument supplies the conservative return threshold, and the two substitution mechanisms bound dwell times before skeleton counting. I found no material gap in the quadratic depth threshold or its state-count consequence.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published rStation work. Paper [4], version 3, proves typical bounds for randomly relabeled supports and constructs particular bad supports; even its density-Hales–Jewett construction does not establish a uniform bound for every layered digit support. Paper [5], version 1, treats periodic algebraic supports and, for its higher-degree sublinear bounds, restricted selection rules. Neither subsumes the present nonuniform digit model with unrestricted selections.

A focused external comparison supports this distinction. Krieger, Rampersad and Shallit [6, Sections 2–3] provide the familiar loop-commutation and simple-cycle decomposition underlying regular-language growth; the manuscript appropriately credits that structural background through [2]–[3]. Those counting results do not supply the geometric selection bound. Byszewski, Konieczny and Müllner [7, Theorem D] count arithmetic progressions in automatic subsets of the integers, rather than bounding arbitrary no-three selections from planar, position-dependent digit supports. Payne and Wood [8] discuss general-position selection and the classical existence of supports with vanishing relative capacity, again with different quantifiers. I found no substantial overlap that removes the manuscript's main contribution. Adding a short comparison with [6]–[8] would strengthen the related-work discussion, but is not necessary for correctness.

The computational evidence is appropriately supplementary to complete proofs. I reran five targeted controls: directional loss, actual layered partitions and parabola determinants, polynomial-cycle fibers, exact-depth witnesses, and the independent return-length audit. All matched the retained mathematical outputs, including 65,519 nontrivial alphabets in the side-four square, 697 layered product classes, 84,748 nontrivial cycle fibers, and the exhaustive return checks through four vertices. The available interpreter was Python 3.8 rather than the specified 3.11; these runs used disposable copies with equivalent replacements for `int.bit_count` and `math.lcm`. Thus this verifies the targeted computations with documented compatibility changes, not an unchanged execution of the full bundle. Finite controls alone would not prove the asymptotic claims; here the natural-language proofs carry that burden.

The claims, limitations, informative metadata abstract, mathematical notation and reproducibility materials are sufficient for publication. The fixed-base, synchronous digit-order and linear-support-size assumptions should remain prominent: the results are necessary restrictions on this construction model, not general memory lower bounds or bounds on the unrestricted extremal function.
