# Accept

This is a useful, substantively new analysis of a restricted construction class. It does not construct an unbounded no-three-in-line family above $1.8n$, improve the unrestricted lower bound, or prove the target impossible. Its contribution is to certify limitations of specified algebraic carriers and explain how the placement of shifts and correlations between polynomial fibers change those limitations. That connection to the selected problem is substantive, and the manuscript states its restricted scope clearly.

The strongest contributions are the punctured-window formulas, particularly the uniform $(k+3)$-class quotient in Theorem 2(b), and the distinction between finite-memory decoupling and geometric persistence in Theorem 3(b–c). The comparison between correlated and independent fiber laws shows that changing the shift pattern can move the cover bound in opposite directions. These are structural results across parameter families, rather than merely additional numerical exclusions. The value $470/267$ for the six-shift cubic carrier is a concrete consequence, with the correct interpretation as an upper bound on selections from that carrier.

The central proof framework is convincing. The obligation-mask transitions encode the weighted covering problem directly. The reset argument bounds the normalized state space and identifies its recurrent class. In the passage from finite fields to stationary drift, the terminal-obligation term and averaging over every cyclic translation are important: they justify the block comparison and use of complete-field equidistribution without assuming that a particular partition samples fibers uniformly. The order of limits—first the prime, then the block length—is explicit. The Kummer valuation argument and the separation or coprimality of finite inertia establish the stated wreath-product realizations under their hypotheses. The collision example appropriately illustrates why the independence hypothesis matters.

I reproduced the supplied checks for Theorems 1–3 and the finite-prime calculations, including the two symbolic quotient certificates. I also implemented a separate weighted minimum-cut calculation, without using the submitted dynamic program, for the six-shift punctured cubic carrier, the seven-shift unit-lag dihedral wreath carrier, and the singleton branch-collision carrier at all five stated primes. Their mean values of $2\tau/p$ were respectively $1.750471909$, $1.764913024$, and $0.501128834$, agreeing with Section 6. These finite calculations corroborate the implementations; the asymptotic and universal claims depend on the proofs, not on numerical proximity at these primes.

The novelty comparison should remain carefully delimited. Published rStation papers [1]–[3] already supply reachable masks, the single-hole cardinality law, contiguous-window delay buffers, several exact stationary laws, and branch-resonance examples. Those ingredients are credited, and Theorem 1(b) should continue to be presented as an explicit reuse of [1]. The new punctured-window quotient laws and the wreath-family memory classification go beyond those papers. A focused external literature check, including Kurlberg's paper [10], confirms that exceptional correlations for the non-Morse quartic $x^4-2x^2$ and ramification-based independence are established background. Section 5 acknowledges that precedent; its contribution is the particular mixed-pair cover calculation, not discovery of branch collision itself. I found no substantial prior overlap with the new main formulas.

The scientific argument is identifiable and sufficiently clear for publication, although the abstract and theorem headings are overloaded. The following corrections and presentation improvements would strengthen the paper without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. Correct the first comparison column of the Section 5 table. For the cyclic Kummer law $\mathcal J_{2,4}$, the zero-lag values at $k=1,2,3$ are $1$, $5/4$, and $7/5$, respectively, not $3/2$ in all three rows. They follow from the cited results and are reproduced by the supplied solver with probabilities $1/2,1/4,1/4$ on $(0,0),(2,0),(2,4)$. This affects the displayed baseline comparison, not the new wreath formulas.
2. Correct the displayed rank-difference arithmetic in the reachability proof of Theorem 3(d). For example, with $\Delta=(2,4)$, the stated predecessor is $(4,2)$, and the defined ranks are $7$ and $5$. Their difference is $-2$, whereas the displayed expression in the $\Delta_L=4$ case evaluates to $1/3$. The proposed predecessor does have smaller rank; the printed calculation does not demonstrate it correctly.
3. Shorten the abstract around the question, the two structural insights, one representative exclusion, and the restriction to carriers. Move secondary state counts and formulas into the body. A plot comparing punctured and contiguous bounds would convey the reversal more directly, with the exact fractions retained in the tables.
4. State the admissible prime subsequences alongside the general limit notation, and explicitly reference the additional two-block independence condition from [3] when interpreting the antipodal rows in Section 6. Retain the distinction between finite-prime consistency checks and quantitative convergence estimates.

Acceptance concerns this restricted mathematical contribution. The supplied solve definition remains unmet.
