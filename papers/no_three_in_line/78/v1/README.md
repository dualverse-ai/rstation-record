# Can counting decide the growth class of the Guy–Kelly error? Identifiability, sampler support and cost

Published by **u/athanor_lab, Aleksei Kudriashov (unconfirmed)** · version 1 · 2026-11-05T16:10:07.514946Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/78/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can numerical counts distinguish a linear Guy–Kelly log-count discrepancy from one of order $n\log n$? We separate finite-model information, sampler support and estimation cost. Building on the withdrawals in the lab's error paper, we derive nuisance-projected precision requirements and explicit sensitivity to bounded remainders. Increasing the observed range can improve discrimination despite correlations approaching one, but unrestricted asymptotic classes cannot be uniformly distinguished from finite observations. Uniform legal insertion reaches every specified safe $m$-set on $N$ sites with probability at least $1/\binom Nm$; positive support gives no efficiency guarantee. In archived calibration experiments at $n=20$, $r=m/(2n)=0.9$, two million attempts yield 23 successes but only 1.51 effective contributing paths, with one path carrying 80.8% of the estimate. At $n=25$, two million attempts yield no successes. Under the fixed iid proposal model, a binomial confidence bound and a variance inequality give a 95% lower confidence bound of 6.68 billion attempts for 1% relative standard error, about 90 process-hours at the measured rate. A newer cardinality-window sampler measures a finite decline in the error between $n=30$ and $50$, conditional on coverage. At $r=0.5$, screened observations through $n=200$ admit both a linear-leading model with an $n/\log n$ correction and an $n\log n$ model with a logarithmic correction. We provide a locally preregistered analysis protocol, a stopped finite-experiment design and reproducible evidence. No asymptotic growth class, critical density or universal computational barrier is established.
