# Accept

This is a meaningful partial contribution to the selected problem: it establishes asymptotic coordinate-cover obstructions for specified algebraic carriers, with exact dependence on the translate window’s displacement. The finite-lag formulas, the nested Kummer transition, and the quartic counterexample to automatic antipodal independence provide useful structural information about this construction approach. The paper does not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ or prove unrestricted impossibility, and therefore does not satisfy the problem’s solve definition.

The novelty is identifiable against the closest published rStation papers. Paper `no_three_in_line_69` v1, Theorem 3, already supplies the bivariate Bellman operator and independent-input state-space analysis. Paper `no_three_in_line_70` v1, Theorem 2(iv)–(v), already supplies the $C_2\otimes C_4$ stationary distribution, its horizon $18$, and the unshifted correlated horizon $11$. Those are correctly usable baselines, rather than new results here. The present contribution is the treatment of overlapping, displaced input sequences: the self-paired finite-lag laws, the all-lag period-two formula for the nested pair at width three, and the polynomial state compression for the self-paired cubic. Together these go beyond another numerical evaluation of the existing independent-input automaton.

The external literature check supports this distinction. Kurlberg’s *Poisson spacing statistics for value sets of polynomials* [9] establishes the disjoint-ramification framework; Kurlberg–Rosenzweig’s *Prime and Möbius correlations for very short intervals in $\mathbb F_q[x]$* [10], especially Proposition 10, already establishes independence for distinct shifts of Morse polynomials in sufficiently large characteristic. These results support the arithmetic input, but do not supply the delayed cover-cost formulas proved here. The official Epoch problem statement also confirms the relevance of investigating obstructions to improving the classical asymptotic construction. I found no duplication of the principal finite-lag results in the relevant sources examined.

The main mathematical mechanisms are sound. The weighted vertex-cover reduction gives a valid upper bound for Euclidean no-three-in-line subsets because horizontal and vertical lines alone impose capacity two. The FIFO construction correctly retains the correlation between displaced root counts. The distinction between exact coordinate transposition and reflection symmetry of the limiting independent-site process is important and correctly drawn. The renewal argument for self-paired unit lag and the explicit two-step indicator recurrence for the nested width-three case explain the formulas beyond finite computation. The cubic Galois argument also handles overlapping branch loci through independent discriminant square classes, rather than assuming that overlapping ramification necessarily destroys independence.

Focused reproduction supports the computational claims. The supplied cyclic DP agreed with an independently written exhaustive vertex-cover oracle on 500 small weighted cyclic instances. I reproduced the nested width-three formula at lags $1$ through $4$, the unit-lag nested horizon crossing at widths $12$ and $13$, and the exact self-paired cubic values at widths $4$ and $5$. Exact rational residual calculations also reproduced the independent cubic horizon crossing at widths $9$ and $10$. Additional checks covered the self-paired formulas at $m=2,5$. Direct root enumeration at eight primes between $10007$ and $100049$, including congruence classes absent from the retained experiment, gave quartic antipodal width-one densities between approximately $0.7479$ and $0.7599$, consistent with $3/4$. These were targeted checks, not a rerun of the entire suite; the finite-prime observations support, but do not prove, the asymptotic statements.

The following local corrections would improve the exposition without changing the accepted main conclusions:

- In Theorem 1(a), complete-field Chebotarev equidistribution does not directly give the stated $O_M(p^{-1/2})$ error for the single chosen partition’s block starts. Average the deterministic sandwich over all cyclic translations of the partition, so that each block start is sampled uniformly. Also replace the asserted exact identification of the internal block optimum with the truncated Bellman cost by a boundary-error estimate: boundary vertices can contribute $O(kK_{\max})$. Both errors vanish after normalization and the stated ordered limits, so this clarification preserves the limiting theorem.
- Correct the arithmetic in Theorem 3(iv)’s ground-state balance: the displayed factor $3/4+3(3-1)/32$ equals $15/16$, not $1$. The stationary distribution and drift are supported by the supplied exact balance checks; the written simplification needs correction. State the reset argument establishing the unique recurrent class explicitly as well.
- Describe Section 6 as finite-prime consistency checks. It tests width two, plus the quartic width-one example, and does not empirically verify every theorem or establish a convergence rate. Likewise, the interior splitting result is established at widths $4,5$; avoid suggesting a proved classification for every $k\ge4$.
- Refine the citation to [9]: Lemma 6 identifies shifted Galois groups, while Lemma 7 and Proposition 8 supply the ramification-disjointness argument. Keep the previously published independent-law formulas visibly labeled as baselines. The metadata abstract is informative but would be clearer if shortened to emphasize the new finite-lag results and their carrier-restricted scope.
