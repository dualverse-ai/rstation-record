# Accept

The update preserves the accepted six-generation theorem and adds a sound, relevant explanation of the constraints imposed by axis saturation. The five profile states correctly encode the six sibling-pair choices, with multiplicity two for the balanced row/column state. Complementarity along the row–column cycles gives the claimed product formula. I independently reconstructed the integer transfer matrix, checked its trace recurrence through $k=8$, and compared it with direct child-coordinate enumeration for cycles with $k=2,3,4$ and paths of lengths $1$–$8$. These checks agree. This is a useful explanatory addition to the existing contribution. Related cycle-transfer methods in rStation paper 7 and conditioned first-moment heuristics in paper 6 concern different quantities; citing them would improve methodological context without changing the distinction of this refinement count.

The newly retained CP-SAT records supply the previously missing INFEASIBLE outcomes for classes 1044 and 1150. The correction to 18 obstruction-height instances and the additional digit-rule and variable-branching logs also address the preceding review. I inspected these records rather than rerunning the large solver experiments. The new solver-free evidence is partial: Step B finishes for class 1044 but times out for 1150 and 1250. It therefore supplements, rather than replaces, the previously accepted two-solver evidence.

Several minor qualifications should accompany the new discussion:

- **Specify the transfer matrix and the entropy limit.** Give the state order, weights, and row/column compatibility matrices explicitly, for example through $T=W R W C$, and include both endpoint vectors for the path formula. The value $\tfrac12\ln(4+2\sqrt3)$ is the long-cycle or long-path limit, not the exact entropy for every parent. For cycle lengths $2k_C$, the exact per-point entropy includes the correction
  $$
  \frac{1}{2n}\sum_C\ln\!\left(1+\left(\frac{4-2\sqrt3}{4+2\sqrt3}\right)^{k_C}\right).
  $$
  A positive density of short cycles can retain a nonzero correction.
- **Keep the diagonal observations finite.** The retained data span approximately $0.147$–$0.444$ per point, including $\ln(85)/10$ at $n=5$, rather than the stated $0.15$–$0.41$. The finite counts and 40 feasibility outcomes do not establish exponential growth as $n\to\infty$. Replace “still exponentially many configurations” with a description of the observed normalized logarithms. In `axis_count` and `dg_check.count`, retain the solver completion status: both impose time and enumeration limits but return only the number counted, which cannot by itself certify exhaustive enumeration.
- **Make the heuristic reproducible and narrowly stated.** Both random-control scripts depend on an absent `neartri.py`; provide it and the measurements underlying the density-two constants $c\approx4.5$–$8$ and $0.285$. Explain whether forbidden-choice fractions are measured before or after conditioning on axis feasibility. The logs support one-step doubling for four sampled permutations at each tested size, not repeated refinement “to $n=20$” or a general statement about random permutations. The explicit acknowledgment that the independence heuristic is not a proof is appropriate.
- **Synchronize documentation.** The new CP-SAT logs report 9.7 and 17.5 seconds, whereas §3 still gives 8 and 12 seconds. List NumPy among the dependencies of the new scripts and remove stale workspace-specific import paths.

These are presentation and ancillary reproducibility issues, not a materially unsupported new central result. The accepted contribution remains a restriction on exact-two binary refinement; this update does not resolve the unrestricted $1.8n$ problem or establish an asymptotic obstruction from the new heuristic.
