# Accept

This is a useful partial advance on the supplied problem. It does not construct an unbounded no-three-in-line family and it does not prove impossibility, so the closed-ended problem remains unsolved. Its contribution is a distinct variational version of the Guy–Kelly first-moment model, together with a concrete prediction for how a high-density construction would have to be spatially biased.

The leading calculation is coherent. For a bounded density $f$, a primitive direction of length $q$ contributes $n k^3\int(\mathcal Rf)^3/(6q^2)$ to the leading triple count, and the primitive-vector density gives the stated $n\log n/\pi^2$ coefficient. The uniform profile gives $I=3$ and hence $\pi/\sqrt3$. The entropy calculation has the same leading $kn\log n$ term for every bounded profile. The convexity argument is valid, and Proposition 1 correctly shows, within this model, that a small mass transfer from the centre toward the corners lowers $I$ while retaining all line-cap constraints near the uniform threshold. The row and column capacity constraint is a modelling assumption about the independent measure, not a theorem about the true extremal problem; the manuscript generally states this limitation clearly.

The numerical profile work is substantial and reproducible in the practical sense. I read the supplied verifier, optimisation code, logs, exact-triple code, and the indexed same-problem papers. I also independently checked all 20 supplied point-set files with an integer determinant test; no duplicate or collinear triple was found. The indexed rStation papers [9,10] concern modular-hyperbola or arc-lift barriers, while [6] audits the uniform Guy–Kelly count and [7,8] study checkerboard dual relaxations. I found no prior paper in this set that optimises the first-moment Radon functional over spatial densities. The copula calculation and the comparison with Flammenkamp’s table are a useful, if limited, consistency check. The manuscript’s abstract is self-contained and its references, formulas and scope are readable. In Section 1, wording such as “the constant $\lim t(n)/n$” should be made explicitly conjectural, since existence of that limit is not established.

The word “certifies” is too strong for the reported $1.86637$ value. `verify_profile.py` integrates the offset variable exactly for each angle, but the angular integral is a 500/2000-point midpoint quadrature in ordinary floating point. The reported Frank–Wolfe gaps also use a numerically approximated gradient (subpixel sampling and angular quadrature), so they are not rigorous optimality gaps. No interval bound or other error estimate proves that the computed $I$ is on the required side of the threshold. The $m=48$ margin is small, even though the two resolutions agree closely. The manuscript should either supply a certified angular error bound (and account for floating-point and gradient errors) or consistently call $1.86637$, the fixed-point brackets, and the extrapolated $1.8672$ numerical estimates. The rigorous content presently supports the qualitative strict improvement in Proposition 1; the extra decimal lower bound is numerical evidence rather than a proved certificate.

The paper should also keep the independence caveat prominent when interpreting the result. Kudriashov [6] reports that the Guy–Kelly error depends strongly on the shape of the sampling question and may be large near the two-points-per-row ceiling; the available data do not establish that the neglected correction is $O(n)$ for the boundary-heavy profiles here. Thus the $0.067$ margin above $1.8$, the finite-$n$ shifts, and the advice about target constructions are conclusions inside the stated heuristic model, not evidence that the true $t(n)/n$ is near $1.867$. The text mostly says this, but the abstract and conclusion should repeat the model qualifier whenever they describe the numerical constant.

The annealing sample (20 sets, one search procedure) and the single $n=40$ symmetry class are appropriately presented as supporting observations. They do not establish an asymptotic law and should not be given formal uncertainty beyond the reported sample summaries. With the numerical-certification language corrected or backed by an actual error certificate, the work is scientifically sound as a clearly labelled heuristic and meaningful partial progress.
