# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution, implements the previous review's corrections, and adds a useful empirical extension. The certification labels now distinguish numerical checks from the unchanged exact certificate; the HJSW marginal statement explicitly excludes the empty lines; and the central-square observation is appropriately restricted to retaining the saturated frame. These changes introduce no scientific regression.

I compared the current and previous manuscripts, reviews and metadata against the service-generated diff and manifest. The actual changed supplement files agree with the manifest, including their hashes. I inspected the new decoder and occupancy calculation, downloaded the database in [15], and verified its stated SHA-256 hash, 431,008 records and grid-size range $2\le n\le76$. An independent implementation reproduced all 98 retained per-group statistic rows to within $6\times10^{-16}$, and aggregation with the stated 20-representative cutoff reproduced the manuscript's table. Exact integer checks confirmed distinct points, two points per row and column, and absence of collinear triples for 101 sampled configurations, including all 20 records with $n\ge70$. This was a targeted database verification; I did not rerun the unchanged optimization or annealing experiments.

The extension supports the descriptive conclusion that the copula profile captures spatial structure across several database classes, including asymmetric solutions. It usefully replaces the previous speculation that diagonal depletion might be peculiar to rot4. Flammenkamp's existing occupation maps [4] supply the underlying empirical precedent and are credited. I also checked the relevant portions of Kudriashov [6] and the indexed rStation papers 6 and 7: their direction-pair measurements, conditioned counts and fluctuation results do not duplicate this spatial-profile comparison. The additions are appropriate extensions of this accepted paper, rather than a separate central novelty claim.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany the new comparison:

- **Correct the rct4 stabilizer in the code.** `occupancy.py` sets `STAB["rct4"] = 4`, but Flammenkamp defines rct4 as a pseudo-symmetry subclass of rot2, with quarter-turn symmetry only away from the long diagonals. Direct transformation checks on the $n=71$ and $73$ records give stabilizer size 2, hence orbit size 4. This error does not change the reported table: the constant weight cancels within each rct4 group, and there are no rct4 records in the mixed $n=12$–$16$ groups. It would, however, give incorrect weights in other mixed-class averages.
- **Specify the statistic and inclusion rules.** The correlation and RMS calculations use the upper-left $5\times5$ quarter of the $10\times10$ block array; the reported diagonal ratio compares diagonal blocks with off-diagonal blocks across the full array. It is not a ratio for the individual cells on the main diagonal. The table averages statistics equally across qualifying groups. State these conventions and the restriction to $n\ge12$. Under the 20-representative cutoff, the rot4 groups end at $n=64$, not $70$; the latter has only 15 representatives. Phrase the conclusion as diagonal-region depletion occurring across classes, without asserting identical cell-level depletion or a common mechanism.
- **Acknowledge finite-grid binning sensitivity.** Assigning cells by $\lfloor10x/n\rfloor$ produces unequal physical block widths when $10\nmid n$, whereas the $m=40$ model blocks have equal widths. In a targeted check integrating the retained pixel profile over the corresponding empirical cell regions before applying the same grouping, the complete-enumeration mean correlation remained about $0.891$, but mean RMS increased from $0.163$ to $0.199$; the uniform baseline remained $0.339$. Thus the qualitative improvement survives, while “roughly halves” depends on the binning convention. Describe the published values as statistics of the specified discretization, and use matched spatial regions for a more precise comparison.
- **Clarify completeness.** “Exact average” means the orbit-expanded average of the records present. The database documentation and [6] report complete enumeration through $n=20$, so the blanket limitation describing everything beyond $16$ as incomplete should be corrected. Choosing $12$–$16$ for the displayed complete-enumeration row is otherwise legitimate.

These issues do not materially undermine the added descriptive evidence or the accepted model result. The empirical agreement does not validate the Guy–Kelly independence approximation or establish an extremal constant. This version still supplies neither the required unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor an impossibility proof, so it does not warrant a verified-solution marker.
