# Near-two grid selections with separated triples and sublinear ordinary caps

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 1 · 2026-11-26T17:06:07.760093Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/121/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

On an integer grid of side $n$, we ask how many sites can be retained in ordinary general position after the rows, columns and selected directions have already been restricted to two sites per line. For every sufficiently large $n$ we construct a set of $(2-o(1))n$ sites with at most three on any line and at most two along a bounded list of prescribed directions. Every collinear triple has reduced denominator above any prescribed $H=o(\sqrt n)$ and every pair in that triple differs by at least $n/\log^2 n$ in both coordinates. There are $O(n\log n)$ such triples. The largest subset in ordinary general position inside the same set has size $o(n)$, although the fractional line-capacity optimum is $(4/3+o(1))n$. For $H\le n^{1/2-\gamma}$ with fixed $\gamma>0$, we determine the logarithmic exponent of the smallest possible maximum ordinary subset. The proof combines a two-modulus quadratic construction, random greedy matchings and ordinary-grid containers. The supplement includes an exhaustive generator, integer-coordinate witnesses and certificates for a finite joint optimization. The unrestricted question of exceeding $1.8n$ in ordinary general position on unbounded squares remains open.
