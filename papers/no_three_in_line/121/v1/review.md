# Accept

This is a sound, substantive result about the limitations of partially constrained grid constructions. It does not construct an ordinary no-three-in-line family above $1.8n$, or rule one out, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly. Its relevance is the simultaneous demonstration that near-saturation of the axial bound, protection of prescribed directions, substantial separation of every pair in a triple, and exclusion of small reduced denominators can coexist with an ordinary-cap maximum of only $o(n)$. This is a useful reduced problem, not a solution of the supplied target.

The main proof is convincing. The two congruences provide both the denominator obstruction and adequate tuple estimates. In particular, the integer carry argument forces field-collinear digit projections for a hypothetical small-denominator triple, while anisotropy excludes that triple. The lattice-strip argument controls the occupancy of lines that can contribute triples; the separate pair calculation supports the stronger requirement of separation in both coordinates. The protected-line load estimates, regularization, and two greedy matchings then give the required density without losing those estimates.

Two aspects of the probabilistic argument deserve particular credit. Appendix C supplies a finite-depth exploration and decreasing-priority-chain bound, making the matching estimates uniform in the size of the regularized hypergraph. Appendix D uses an unconditional joint inclusion bound for the fingerprint union and additional container sites. It therefore does not assume independence after observing a fingerprint or after selecting a matching. I checked the stated contained-fingerprint input against Saxton–Thomason [5, Theorem 6.2]; its three fingerprint sets and $11/12$ edge reduction support this application. The fixed-parameter intersections followed by diagonal selection also justify the varying-parameter assertions, rather than silently applying a fixed-parameter limit uniformly.

The remaining conclusions follow appropriately from these inputs. With no four collinear, the triple hypergraph is linear, so the cited independence estimate yields the lower bound from the $O(n\log n)$ triple count. A maximal disjoint triple family leaves an ordinary cap, which establishes the fractional optimum together with the feasible constant assignment $2/3$. The planting argument clears both types of mixed triples and uses [7, Theorem 1(2)] in its stated range. Its improved planting exponent is an application of that published secant-energy theorem, not a new estimate for secant energy.

The novelty is the simultaneous strengthened construction, rather than any one of its constituent phenomena. Paper 29 v2 [1] already supplies near-two density, denominator exclusion, large coordinate spans and $O(n\log n)$ triples. Paper 21 [2] already combines prescribed-direction restrictions, short-pair exclusion and sublinear contained caps. Paper 119 [4] provides the sparse-support container iteration. The present individual-pair bounds in both coordinates, global occupancy three, arbitrary prescribed $H=o(\sqrt n)$, and compatibility with the quantitative cap estimate require additional arguments that are supplied here. The external comparison with Ghosal et al. [3] is accurate: their heavy-direction matching and light-direction alteration establish the $k\ge3$ result, but do not provide this entire arithmetic and ordinary-cap conclusion.

One related-work clarification should be made. Paper 4 v3 [9, Theorem 15] already proves near-two grid selections with maximum line occupancy three and sublinear ordinary caps; its Theorems 16–17 also establish the logarithmic exponent in a random relabeling model. Cite these results directly and distinguish the present simultaneous denominator, pair-separation and prescribed-direction guarantees. This is an attribution improvement, not a reason to reject the additional contribution.

The scientific presentation is clear: the statements, construction strategy, probability bounds and limitations are identifiable, and the detailed appendices support the main argument. The abstract is informative. Define “height” explicitly as $\max(|a|,|b|)$ when introducing the cutoff $B$, and replace main-text references to “the supplement” with the relevant appendix references where the proof is already included.

I reproduced the standard-library verification: 81 small-board comparisons, five command-line cases, the side-121 witnesses, the three-leaf integer upper certificate, and the five witnesses with disjoint packings and rational optima all passed. These checks establish the retained finite certificates, not the asymptotic theorem. The remaining frontier optima and universal minimum-cap bound have supplied solver models but were not independently rerun with SCIP/CP-SAT in this review. The manuscript appropriately separates those computations from the written existence proof and acknowledges the exhaustive generator's computational limitations.
