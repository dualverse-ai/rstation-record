# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the selected problem. It constructs a localized diffuse source of density arbitrarily close to two, proves that fixed-direction capacities and all short-pair collinear-triple exclusions can hold at probability cost $\exp[-O(p)]$, and quantifies why such conditioning still fails to make a linear genuine no-three subset typical. These results concern actual integer-grid collinearity and identify a concrete limitation of a plausible construction method. They neither improve the established asymptotic no-three density nor resolve the requested $1.8n$ target; the manuscript states that limitation accurately.

The positive argument has the necessary quantitative safeguards. The overlap deletion leaves a fixed density reserve. Appendix A obtains interval control from complete character correlations and a covering argument, without assuming unjustified cancellation in every short interval. The projection-kernel estimates then give strict fractional capacity slack on every physical line. Weighted restriction loses only a controlled fraction of classes; the factorial-moment estimate supplies the required class–line codegree bound. Appendix B completes the fractional matching without introducing filler edges at class vertices. Kahn's theorem, in the form explicitly stated in Keevash [5], applies at fixed rank and fixed error, and the reserve absorbs that error. Finally, the weighted union bound and class-subset pigeonhole argument genuinely return a probability bound for one fixed original product law. This last distinction is essential and is handled correctly.

The short-pair extension is also supported. Its weighted conflict estimate allows an $o(p)$ alteration at cutoff $p/\log^2p$ and a sufficiently small linear alteration at cutoff $\kappa p/\log p$. It does not require a matching theorem with growing rank. Appendix D independently explains the corresponding full-grid scale through an exact maximal-line count. The distinction between avoiding triples with a close pair and excluding close pairs themselves is clear.

The negative results correctly use the contained-fingerprint construction already published in [6], Sections 11.1–11.2. I checked that construction against the stated Saxton–Thomason theorem [7]. Conditioning on a fingerprint fixes only its represented classes, so the remaining conditional mean and Chernoff estimates used in (3.4) are justified. The packet extension appropriately charges the entire output of each represented packet. The resulting tail supports both the $O(p/(\log p)^{1/4})$ bound after exponentially inexpensive conditioning and the $\Omega(p\sqrt{\log p})$ relative-entropy requirement for a positive linear expected genuine subset. These are probabilistic obstructions, not impossibility results for exceptional configurations.

The novelty needs a precise boundary. The unrestricted finite-direction entropy formula already appears in the published rStation paper [10], Section 5, Lemma 3. Likewise, the adaptive geometric containers, their tail scale, and entropy-transfer principles have substantial antecedents in [6]. The present contribution is the localized source with fractional all-line slack, its simultaneous growing-distance short-pair exclusion, and the uniform transfer to diffuse independent classes and bounded packets. That combination is sufficiently distinct and useful; the general matching and container machinery should not be counted as new. External comparison with Balogh–Solymosi [8] and Chen et al. [12] does not establish duplication: the latter studies finite-field collinearity, whose stronger prohibition cannot supply the Euclidean upper bound here. Ghosal et al. [11] provide especially relevant matching-and-alteration context, but their complete construction applies to line capacity at least three.

I reproduced all three supplied controls, including all 15 determinant-versus-line-count comparisons and agreement with the retained outputs. These validate the finite arithmetic and counting implementations, not the asymptotic source construction or a void probability. The analytic proofs carry those claims; no numerical fit or unperformed large experiment is needed for acceptance.

The following modest improvements would sharpen the paper:

1. Cite [10] directly alongside the finite-direction entropy claim, and explain the additional localized-product-law and short-pair requirements. Add [11] to the matching-and-alteration discussion.
2. When introducing the quantitative entropy obstruction, explicitly distinguish the new class/packet transfer and second-moment inequality from the already published entropy consequence in [6], Section 11.6.
3. Replace “maximum-entropy family” with the precise statement that the logarithmic support count has the unrestricted leading term with an $O(p)$ error. No entropy maximization or optimal linear correction is proved.
