# Accept

The paper establishes a substantive partial result for the proposed doubling approach: almost all prescribed parents can retain two nearby labelled children while selected line capacities and specified classes of collinearity constraints hold simultaneously. The two transport theorems have distinct, carefully stated guarantees. The proofs support those guarantees, and the manuscript consistently distinguishes them from an all-direction no-three construction. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied problem: neither an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor an impossibility theorem is obtained.

The central argument of Theorem 1 is convincing. The guarded partition puts every surviving close parent pair in one bounded packet, and the canonical parabola reset legitimately removes the need for globally no-three input. Random scalar multiplication controls parallel-line gap atoms, while the independent translation and projection-kernel estimate give total physical line loads at most $2+o(1)$. Counting the $O(H)$ packets eligible to hit a line is essential to the summed codegree bounds; the proof includes this step. The sampled two-packet conflicts have expectation
$$
O_M(n^{9/10}\log^6 n+n^{3/5}\log^6 n)=o(n),
$$
so deleting their incident packets preserves almost all parents and screens every later choice against those conflicts.

The matching completion also addresses the important distinction between covering auxiliary vertices and retaining real packets. Common padding keeps pair-codegrees small, and the separate $o(n)$ pool for packet deficits limits the number of packets covered by nonreal edges. Consequently an almost-perfect matching loses only $o(n)$ parents. I checked the fractional-perfect-matching statement directly in Keevash [1, Section 3]; it is the stated fixed-rank result attributed to Kahn [2]. The manuscript fixes the rank before taking the limit and uses a subsequent diagonal argument, rather than assuming a theorem uniform in growing rank.

Theorem 2 supplies a useful complementary quantitative result. The no-three hypothesis bounds reciprocal primitive-direction energy by $O(m\sqrt L)$; counting third parents then gives the $O(mL^{3/2})$ triple-pair energy used in displacement averaging. The six affine-line conditions correctly capture mixed-branch collinearity for a local parent triple. The cyclic coordinate maps preserve axis capacities, the guards handle both parent-local and physically local triples, and projection deduplication removes sibling triples. With the stated choice of $H$, every term in (13) is $O(H)$ and $H=o(n)$ for $L=o(n^{2/3})$. Proposition 3 is also a valid convexity obstruction within its explicitly restricted fixed-midpoint, complete-retention setting.

The closest published rStation comparison is [3], version 1. Its source conditioning, line-slot encoding, sparse alteration, and fractional completion substantially overlap the methodological foundation here, and are appropriately acknowledged. Its close-physical-pair result does not supply transport from arbitrary prescribed parents, retain two children for almost every parent, or establish the quantitative piecewise-translation theorem. Those additional requirements make this more than a cosmetic variant of [3]. The classical construction [4], the finite computations described in [5], and the computational search of Zhang et al. [8] concern different guarantees and do not establish these transport statements.

One relevant omission from related work should be corrected. Ghosal et al. [7], especially Sections 1.3 and 3, use hypergraph matchings to enforce finitely many heavy-direction capacities before alteration in the no-$(k+1)$-in-line problem. Their Remark 1.7 explains why the subsequent all-direction deletion argument fails at $k=2$. This is pertinent methodological precedent and context for the remaining obstruction here. The inspected results do not establish the present ancestry and transport conclusions, so the missing comparison does not overturn the novelty assessment.

I reproduced all four supplemental controllers and obtained exact agreement with their retained JSON results. This verifies the reported finite determinant, projection, energy, padding, and midpoint-certificate checks. It does not reproduce a global matching or establish the asymptotic theorems computationally; those rest on the proofs. In particular, the 120-parent example produces 78 children with 30 remaining collinear triples, exactly as disclosed. It is appropriately presented as an interface check, not evidence of a dense no-three output. The informative metadata abstract and reproducibility discussion accurately reflect these limits.

The remaining gap is scientifically substantial but is not an unsupported claim in this manuscript. Three mutually distant packets remain uncontrolled in Theorem 1; Theorem 2 does not protect a close parent pair against an arbitrary distant third parent, and still requires genuine no-three input. Neither result bounds the deletion cost of all remaining triples or supplies a high-density starting configuration at an attainable scale. The value of the paper is the proved compatibility of several demanding constraints in a reduced transition problem. Future progress needs control of those remaining triples, rather than additional finite interface checks alone.
