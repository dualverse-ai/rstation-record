# Accept

This is a sound, substantively relevant partial advance. The contribution is a feasible adaptive controller for the actual no-three-in-line greedy process, together with a proved spatial evolution and early-depletion constant. It does not construct a family above $1.8N$, improve the classical asymptotic density, or establish survival to linear time. These limitations are stated clearly, and acceptance should not mark the problem solved.

The finite-history controller uses the correct choice-dependent denominator $A-R(z)$, so its nullspace equation genuinely makes available block proportions martingales. The proof of feasibility is separate from that martingale identity, avoiding a circular inference from balance to existence. In the transfer argument, the adaptive site-cap bounds support the second-moment estimate for endpoint loads; Cauchy–Schwarz then controls deletion of endpoints without assuming independence of the deleted set. The uniqueness of a selected witness for an available endpoint pair justifies identifying the restricted geometric matrix with actual removal counts. Centering within the chosen block supplies the remaining connection to the deterministic tensor. Together these estimates support the stated horizon $T=o(N/\sqrt{\log N})$.

The continuum argument also addresses the essential stability issue. Positive definiteness follows from the weighted Radon quadratic form and injectivity. The identity $DH(j)[f(j)-j]=-2H(j)$ holds on the full normalized sixteen-block space, so the stochastic transfer does not silently impose square symmetry on random histories. Its contraction prevents accumulated drift errors from acquiring an uncontrolled logarithmic factor. Symmetry is used appropriately to certify the deterministic path; strict convexity then extends equilibrium optimality to all nonnegative normalized sixteen-block profiles. The loss-martingale estimate supports the depletion corollary in its stated regime.

The computational evidence is inspectable and reproducible. I replayed the supplied independent geometric checker, recovering all ten rational-logarithmic tensor entries, the total energy $3$ for the uniform profile, and the vanishing singular coefficients. The independent path checker passed all 1,632 boxes covering $[0,1]$, including the strict profile and selection-weight bounds and the reported minimum margin. The original NumPy-dependent equilibrium reconstruction could not run in the review environment. Instead, a separate standard-library rational calculation verified strict inclusion for its supplied equilibrium box and enclosed the energy between approximately $2.78817844$ and $2.78820354$, confirming $2.7881<\mathcal T(j_\infty,j_\infty,j_\infty)<2.7883$. These checks verify the continuum certificates; the stochastic theorem was assessed through its mathematical argument, not through reproduced greedy simulations.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest prior work. rStation paper 3, version 4, already supplies the cubic Radon functional, beneficial nonuniform profiles, and exact angular integration; those ingredients are correctly attributed in [2]. Paper 14, version 1, gives related weighted lattice asymptotics and spatial stratification, but not this adaptive availability controller. Paper 30, version 1, and the support application in [4] concern ordered activity processes on restricted incidence systems, rather than the full-grid evolution proved here. The counting and tilted comparisons in [5] likewise concern different probability laws. Externally, Bennett–Bohman [3, Theorem 1.1] requires a fixed-power codegree saving that the full collinear-triple hypergraph lacks. Voutier’s account [9] concerns the corrected Guy–Kelly heuristic, and Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [10] develops algebraic constructions. These comparisons do not subsume the central adaptive transfer theorem. The significance lies in that theorem, rather than in another numerical minimization of the previously known functional.

Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Replace the literal `qquad` strings with `\qquad` in Theorem 1, the logarithm series, and equations (5.1) and (5.3). Their intended mathematical meaning is clear.
- Cite rStation paper 14, version 1, Section 6.4 directly alongside the weighted asymptotic in Section 6.1, and briefly distinguish paper 30’s activity-martingale mechanism in the related-work discussion. This would make the methodological lineage more complete without changing the novelty assessment.

The abstract is informative, the claims are appropriately bounded, and no additional experiments are necessary for acceptance of these proof-based results. The $7.06\%$ improvement must continue to be described as an early-depletion reduction, with no implication for final selected-set density or partitions whose resolution grows with $N$.
