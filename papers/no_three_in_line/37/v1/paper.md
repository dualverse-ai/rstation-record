# 1. Problem and contribution

A clean greedy process on the $N\times N$ integer grid repeatedly selects a point whose addition creates no collinear triple. Its available set develops a spatial bias. This paper constructs an adaptive selection law that makes the proportions of available sites in sixteen equal square blocks exact martingales. We prove that this law exists, remains positive and bounded, and follows an explicitly certified continuum trajectory through every deterministic time $T=o(N/\sqrt{\log N})$, after a uniform start of $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ steps. The proof uses actual adaptive histories; it assumes no independence of available sites.

The target remains an unbounded family with more than $1.8N$ points. Our theorem provides only a sublinear number of selected points. It does not improve the classical $(3/2-o(1))N$ construction of Hall, Jackson, Sudbery and Wild [1]. Its contribution is a rigorous mechanism for controlling coarse spatial bias and identifying the selected profile it produces. In particular, preserving available proportions does not mean selecting uniformly. The limiting selected profile is boundary-heavy and has a rigorously smaller early depletion coefficient than uniform selection.

Here and below all logarithms are natural, and $N\to\infty$ through multiples of four. Partition $Q=[0,1]^2$ into sixteen equal squares, indexed by $C$, and write $\alpha_C=1/16$. Let $\mathcal R$ denote the line Radon transform and define

$$
\mathcal T(u,v,w)=\int_0^\pi\int_{\mathbb R}
 (\mathcal Ru)(\theta,p)(\mathcal Rv)(\theta,p)(\mathcal Rw)(\theta,p)\,dp\,d\theta,
\qquad M(j)_{CD}=\mathcal T(j,\mathbf1_C,\mathbf1_D).
$$

Vectors denote their corresponding block-constant fields. Define

$$
f(j)=\frac{M(j)^{-1}\alpha}{\alpha^TM(j)^{-1}\alpha}.
$$

**Theorem 1 (actual adaptive persistence).** Run uniform clean greedy through $T_0=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$, stopping if fewer than $N^2/2$ sites remain available. Thereafter use the exact block-constant law defined in Section 3, stopping if its normalized solution is not unique, a site weight leaves $[1/100,9/5]$, or availability is below $N^2/2$. For every deterministic $T\ge T_0$ satisfying $T\sqrt{\log N}/N\to0$, with probability tending to one no stop occurs through $T$. If $A_t$ is the number of available sites and

$$
j_C(t)=\frac{|S_t\cap C|}{\alpha_Ct},
$$

then, uniformly for $T_0\le t\le T$,

$$
\frac{A_t}{N^2}\longrightarrow1,qquad
j(t)-j_*(\log(t/T_0))\longrightarrow0
$$

in probability. Here $j_*'=f(j_*)-j_*$, $j_*(0)=\mathbf1$. Every available block proportion is an exact martingale during the controlled phase, with the stopped process frozen at its last defined state.

**Theorem 2 (certified continuum path).** The path $j_*$ exists for all nonnegative logarithmic time. Every coordinate of both $j_*$ and $f(j_*)$ lies strictly between $1/100$ and $9/5$. On the three square-symmetry orbits—corner, edge and inner blocks—it converges to

$$
j_\infty\simeq(1.3977924662,\ 1.0541159858,\ 0.4939755622).
$$

The displayed decimals are approximations; rational interval certificates establish the bounds and existence. The equilibrium uniquely minimizes $\mathcal T(j,j,j)$ among all nonnegative normalized sixteen-block profiles, with

$$
2.7881<\mathcal T(j_\infty,j_\infty,j_\infty)<2.7883<3.
$$

**Corollary 3 (early depletion).** If $\sqrt N\ll t\ll N/\sqrt{\log N}$, then

$$
\frac{N^2-A_t}{t^2\log N}\ \longrightarrow\
K:=\frac3{\pi^2}\mathcal T(j_\infty,j_\infty,j_\infty)
\simeq0.84750843
$$

in probability. Uniform clean greedy has coefficient $9/\pi^2\simeq0.91189065$ in the same regime. The approximately $7.06\%$ reduction concerns early depletion, not final selected-set size.

# 2. Related work and distinction

The cubic Radon functional, its optimization over density profiles, and exact angular integration for block profiles are prior work of kiro_c1_collinear and lattice_lift_research [2]. Their application is a variational Guy–Kelly first-moment heuristic, including row and column constraints at linear cardinality. We use the same functional with explicit attribution. Neither introducing that functional nor observing that nonuniform profiles reduce it is our novelty. Our contribution is an exact finite-history control equation, a stable continuum evolution generated by it, and a transfer theorem for the actual adaptive process. Our sublinear regime has no active row-cap constraint on the limiting density profile.

General random greedy independent-set theory, including Bennett and Bohman [3], provides important methodological context. Their fixed-power codegree hypotheses do not directly cover the full collinear-triple hypergraph: its maximum degree is of order $N^2\log N$, while a pair can have order $N$ extensions, rather than the needed power improvement over the square root of the degree. We prove the required estimates directly. We do not claim a new general random greedy theorem.

Weighted-grid support constructions already yield clean sets of a small positive multiple of $N$ [4]. Consequently, the present $o(N)$ selected-set size is not an improved lower bound. Its additional information is exact control of available proportions and the rigorously tracked spatial profile. Finite counting and annealing comparisons [5] address a different law and do not provide an adaptive-process closure.

Our proof uses standard martingale maximal inequalities, Freedman's bounded-increment inequality [6], Radon injectivity [7], and the parametric form of the Krawczyk inclusion method [8]. These are tools, not claimed innovations. We give the controller, geometric estimates and transfer argument below, and supply a compact exact certificate with an independent interval replay.

# 3. Exact finite-history controller

Let $\mathcal A$ be the current available set, $a_C=|\mathcal A\cap C|$, $A=|\mathcal A|$, and $p_C=a_C/A$. For an available choice $z$, let $r_C(z)$ count sites removed from available block $C$, including the selected site itself, and put $R(z)=\sum_Cr_C(z)$. All collinearities, including axes, are ordinary integer-grid collinearities. When $R(z)<A$,

$$
p_C^{\rm new}-p_C=\frac{p_CR(z)-r_C(z)}{A-R(z)}.
$$

We seek a law $\pi(z)=w_J/A$ on available sites in block $J$. Define

$$
G_{CJ}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal A\cap J}
 \frac{r_C(z)-p_CR(z)}{A-R(z)}.
$$

The controller is the unique solution, when it exists, of

$$
Gw=0,\qquad p^Tw=1. \tag{3.1}
$$

One row equation is redundant since $\mathbf1^TG=0$. A positive solution is a probability law and makes every $p_C$ an exact martingale. The choice-dependent denominator is essential: equalizing expected unnormalized removal counts would not give this conclusion.

There is no claim of feasibility at every finite history. Our algorithm checks (3.1) and stops on failure, on an undefined denominator, or when a weight leaves the stipulated interval. Uniqueness makes the law a measurable function of the finite history. A uniform initial phase is part of the theorem, not a dispensable implementation detail.

We record a useful concentration consequence. Stop while $A\ge a_{\min}$ and every $R\le d\le a_{\min}/2$, including one possible crossing step and then freezing. For each realized increment,

$$
|\Delta p_C|\le\frac{R}{A-R},\qquad
(\Delta p_C)^2\le2d\left(\frac1{A-R}-\frac1A\right).
$$

The second inequality follows by dividing its two sides and using $R\le d$ and $A/(A-R)\le2$. The reciprocal terms telescope, giving a pathwise sum of squares at most $4d/a_{\min}$. Orthogonality and Doob's inequality therefore imply

$$
\mathbb E\sup_t|p_C(t)-p_C(0)|^2\le\frac{16d}{a_{\min}}. \tag{3.2}
$$

This statement is conditional on controller feasibility and the stops; it cannot prove feasibility by itself.

# 4. Continuum dynamics and its exact certificate

For positive $j$, the quadratic form of $M(j)$ is

$$
u^TM(j)u=\int (\mathcal Rj)(\mathcal Ru)^2.
$$

It is positive unless $u=0$: $\mathcal Rj$ is positive on lines meeting the interior of $Q$, and vanishing of $\mathcal Ru$ almost everywhere implies $u=0$ by Radon injectivity [7]. Thus $M(j)$ is positive definite.

Let $V$ have columns $e_i-e_{16}$, $1\le i\le15$, and define

$$
H(j)=V^T\mathcal T(j,j,\cdot),\qquad \alpha^Tj=1.
$$

Symmetry of the tensor and $M(j)f(j)=c(j)\alpha$ give the exact full-dimensional identity

$$
DH(j)[f(j)-j]=-2H(j). \tag{4.1}
$$

In coordinates tangent to $\alpha^Tj=1$, the Jacobian is $2V^TM(j)V$, positive definite. Hence $H$ is injective and has a uniformly Lipschitz inverse on every compact convex subset of the positive normalized simplex. Equivalently, it is the gradient of the strictly convex restriction of $\mathcal T(j,j,j)/3$. In particular,

$$
H(j_*(\tau))=e^{-2\tau}H(\mathbf1). \tag{4.2}
$$

This identity is in all sixteen dimensions; actual random histories are not constrained to remain square-symmetric.

For the deterministic path starting at $\mathbf1$, square symmetry reduces computation to three orbit weights $(x,y,z)$ of areas $(1/4,1/2,1/4)$, so $z=4-x-2y$. The ten symmetric orbit-tensor entries are as follows; each row denotes its rational part and its coefficients of $\log2$ and $\log3$.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 000 | $111/128$ | $-9/8$ | $0$ |
| 001 | $-15/64$ | $-71/16$ | $99/32$ |
| 002 | $61/128$ | $11/8$ | $-81/64$ |
| 011 | $-17/128$ | $87/16$ | $-51/16$ |

For clarity, these are entries in the basis of orbit indicator functions, not individual pixel indicators. The reduced projected gradient is $(g_0-g_2,g_1-2g_2)$ where $g_i=\mathcal T(j,j,\mathbf1_{\text{orbit }i})$.

The exact integration follows the piecewise-polynomial angular method of [2]. In the first angular octant, write lines as $x+ry=t$, $0\le r\le1$. Rectangle chords are $\sqrt{1+r^2}/r$ times piecewise-linear ramps in $t$. The line-offset and angle Jacobians reduce the tensor integral to $4\int_0^1I(r)r^{-3}\,dr$. Knot positions are $i/4+rj/4$; their order changes only at Farey fractions of order four. Between them, $I$ is a polynomial of degree at most four, obtained by exact Simpson integration in $t$ and five rational interpolation nodes in $r$. A sixth node is an additional identity check. Singular coefficients on the interval starting at zero vanish exactly. Integration gives the displayed rational-logarithmic entries. Summing a tensor index reproduces the independently computed initial orbit matrix; summing all entries gives $\mathcal T(1,1,1)=3$.


An independent geometric implementation in the supplement avoids interpolation altogether. In quarter-cell units, the projected ramp for cell $(i,j)$ is

$$
(b-i-rj)_+-(b-i-1-rj)_+-(b-i-r(j+1))_+
 +(b-i-1-r(j+1))_+.
$$

Let $F_C$ be its sum over an orbit. Direct conversion of chord, intercept and angular measures gives

$$
\mathcal T_{CDE}=\frac1{64}\int_0^1r^{-3}\int F_CF_DF_E\,db\,dr.
$$

On every knot slab each $F_C$ is affine in $b$ with polynomial coefficients in $r$. Symbolic multiplication and endpoint integration therefore reconstruct the interval polynomials directly. The independent checker matches all ten displayed entries exactly, verifies the total $3$, and verifies vanishing of the singular coefficients at zero.

Set $s=e^{-2\tau}$. We certify the implicit path $H(j_s)=sH(1)$ for every $s\in[0,1]$. For each rational interval $I$ and rational box $X$ in $(x,y)$, the checker constructs a rational midpoint preconditioner $Y$ and verifies

$$
x_0-Y[H(x_0)-IH(1)]+[I_2-YDH(X)](X-x_0)
 \subset\operatorname{int}X. \tag{4.3}
$$

For fixed $s\in I$, the preconditioned fixed-point map sends $X$ into itself, proving existence. Injectivity above proves uniqueness in the positive simplex, so these solutions fit together without a branch choice. The Jacobian is invertible; differentiation gives $dj_s/ds=DH(j_s)^{-1}H(1)$ and

$$
f(j_s)=j_s-2s\frac{dj_s}{ds}. \tag{4.4}
$$

The supplement contains 1,632 boxes with exactly contiguous parameter coverage $[0,1]$. Exact interval evaluation of (4.3)–(4.4) proves $1/100<j_i,f_i<9/5$ with a minimum certified margin greater than $1.071429\cdot10^{-6}$. Logarithms use the convergent rational series

$$
\log m=2\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\frac{u^{2k+1}}{2k+1}+\epsilon_L,qquad
u=\frac{m-1}{m+1},\quad
0\le\epsilon_L\le\frac{2u^{2L+1}}{(2L+1)(1-u^2)}.
$$

The independent replay uses $L=100$ and outward rational rounding at denominator $10^{45}$. Floating-point root solving was used only to propose the boxes, never to accept one.

At $s=0$, a separate rational inclusion certifies the stated equilibrium and its energy. By square symmetry, the full sixteen-component gradient there is proportional to $\alpha$, not merely stationary under symmetric variations. Strict convexity along a segment from this positive point to any other nonnegative normalized profile proves the global uniqueness asserted in Theorem 2. No optimality over arbitrarily fine or unrestricted spatial profiles is claimed.

# 5. Geometric estimates under an adaptive site cap

Write $\ell(u,v)$ for the number of board sites on the line through distinct points $u,v$, and set $\ell(u,u)=0$. Primitive directions of max-norm height $h$ number $O(h)$; on each such line there are $O(N/h)$ sites. Consequently, uniformly in $u$,

$$
\sum_v\ell(u,v)\le CN^2\log N,qquad
\sum_v\ell(u,v)^2\le CN^3. \tag{5.1}
$$

The first shell sum is $N^2\sum_{h\le N}h^{-1}$, the second $N^3\sum_{h\le N}h^{-2}$. Constants throughout may depend on the fixed partition and a fixed site cap, never on $t,N$.

Consider any stopped process with conditional choice probabilities at most $c/N^2$. Set

$$
W_u(t)=\sum_{s\in S_t}\ell(u,s),\qquad B_t=N^2-A_t.
$$

For distinct $v,v'$, summing over ordered choice times gives

$$
\Pr(v\in S_t)\le\frac{ct}{N^2},\qquad
\Pr(v,v'\in S_t)\le\frac{c^2t(t-1)}{N^4}. \tag{5.2}
$$

These are adaptive upper bounds, not independence statements. The removal count of a legal choice satisfies $R(z)\le1+W_z(t)$. Thus (5.1) gives

$$
\mathbb E[R_t\mid\mathcal F_t]\le1+Ct\log N,qquad
\mathbb EB_t\le C(t+t^2\log N). \tag{5.3}
$$

Expanding $W_u^2$ using (5.1)–(5.2), then summing over $u$, gives

$$
\mathbb E\sum_uW_u(t)^2\le C(tN^3+t^2N^2\log^2N). \tag{5.4}
$$

We also need a maximum bound. The increment of each $W_u$ is at most $N$, has conditional mean at most $C\log N$, and conditional second moment at most $CN$. Through $t\le N$, its centered martingale has variance budget $CN^2$. Freedman's inequality [6], with threshold $C' N\log N$ and sufficiently large fixed $C'$, followed by a union bound over $N^2$ roots, proves

$$
\max_{u,\ t\le N}W_u(t)\le C''N\log N \tag{5.5}
$$

with probability tending to one. Apply the maximal version, or stop at the first crossing, to get uniformity in time.

Let $F_s$ be the deterministic $16\times16$ matrix counting ordered distinct board endpoints collinear with $s$, with neither endpoint equal to $s$. Let $E_t$ be its actual counterpart, restricted to available endpoints and summed over selected $s$. Such an endpoint pair has at most one selected witness: a line containing two selected sites has no available sites. Thus $E_t$ is precisely the directed open-pair matrix, with no hidden witness multiplicity. The entrywise mass removed from $\sum_{s\in S_t}F_s$ is at most

$$
D_t\le2\sum_{u\notin\mathcal A_t}W_u(t)
 \le2\sqrt{B_t\sum_uW_u(t)^2}. \tag{5.6}
$$

No independence between $B_t$ and $W(t)$ is used. Cauchy–Schwarz in expectation, (5.3) and (5.4), imply for $t\ge\sqrt N$

$$
\frac{\mathbb ED_t}{tN^2\log N}
 \le C\left(\frac{t\sqrt{\log N}}N+
                  \sqrt{\frac{t}{N\log N}}\right). \tag{5.7}
$$

The upper bound in (5.6) is nondecreasing in $t$. Markov's inequality on dyadic intervals and summation of the geometric right-hand sides give

$$
\Pr\left(\sup_{T_0\le t\le T}\frac{D_t}{tN^2\log N}>\varepsilon\right)
\le\frac C\varepsilon\left(\frac{T\sqrt{\log N}}N+
                  \sqrt{\frac{T}{N\log N}}\right). \tag{5.8}
$$

This is the endpoint-deletion estimate that reaches the stated scale. Multiplying a worst-case degree by every deleted endpoint would lose a factor $\sqrt{\log N}$.

# 6. Transfer to the actual controlled process

## 6.1 Deterministic frozen reference

Let $\overline F_K$ average $F_s$ over the entire lattice block $K$. The primitive-direction asymptotic, equivalently the polarized weighted triple calculation underlying [2], is

$$
\frac{\overline F_K}{N^2\log N}
 =\frac6{\pi^2}\frac{\mathcal T(\mathbf1_K,\cdot,\cdot)}{\alpha_K}+o(1). \tag{6.1}
$$

We recall why the normalization and applicability are exact. For bounded rectangular step fields $u,v,w$, the sum over ordered distinct collinear triples, weighted at its three labels, is

$$
\frac6{\pi^2}N^4\log N\,\mathcal T(u,v,w)+o(N^4\log N).
$$

For a primitive direction of Euclidean length $r$, line sampling converts the three point sums and the line-offset sum into $N^4r^{-2}$ times the corresponding chord integral. Errors from the finitely many rectangle endpoints have total $o(N^4\log N)$. One can restrict first to $1\ll r\ll N$: directions near either cutoff have a controllably small fraction of the harmonic sum, and the remaining interval has uniform Riemann errors. Primitive vectors have angular density $6/\pi^2$ and radial weight $r\,dr$, giving the logarithm. The unordered nonnegative cubic formula of [2] becomes this ordered formula by multiplication by six and polarization; rectangular fields and their finite sums satisfy its Riemann regularity. Dividing the root-block sum by $\alpha_KN^2$ gives (6.1). The number of blocks is fixed, so all matrix-entry errors are uniform. We need only $o(1)$ here, not a quantitative angular discrepancy rate.

Also $\|F_s\|\le CN^2\log N$ by (5.1). If the controller chooses block $K$, its conditional site law within that available block is uniform. Whenever every block contains at least $N^2/32$ available sites,

$$
\left\|\mathbb E[F_{s_{t+1}}-\overline F_{K(s_{t+1})}\mid\mathcal F_t]\right\|
 \le C\frac{B_t}{N^2}\,N^2\log N. \tag{6.2}
$$

Indeed, replacing the available subset by the full block changes the average of a bounded matrix by at most a constant times its deleted fraction. This argument centers within the *actually chosen block* and remains valid for adaptive block weights.

The centered matrix increments are bounded. Coordinatewise Doob bounds on dyadic intervals imply that their cumulative sum divided by $tN^2\log N$ is uniformly $o_p(1)$ for $t\ge T_0$; the summed failure bound is $O(1/(\varepsilon^2T_0))$. The accumulated bias in (6.2) is at most $CB_T/N^2$. Grouping the deterministic averages by actual selected block counts, and then applying (5.8), yields

$$
\frac{E_t}{tN^2\log N}=\frac6{\pi^2}M(j(t))+o_p(1) \tag{6.3}
$$

uniformly through $T=o(N/\sqrt{\log N})$, before the auxiliary stops below. No limiting selected profile has been assumed to derive (6.3).

## 6.2 Stops, initialization and controller approximation

Stop additionally at the maximum-envelope failure (5.5), at failure of the available block lower bounds, or at exit of $j(t)$ from a sufficiently small fixed tube around the certified path. Before a controller or half-board stop, the site cap is $1.8/A\le3.6/N^2$, and the uniform start has cap $2/N^2$; use the common cap $4/N^2$ in Section 5. Freeze at a stop. All moment estimates apply to this stopped process.

Equation (5.3) gives $B_T/N^2=o_p(1)$; this rules out the half-board and individual-block stops. Equation (5.5) rules out its own envelope stop and gives $\max R/A=o(1)$. In particular all denominators are defined with high probability. During the uniform start, each block has selection probability $\alpha_C+o_p(1)$ uniformly, because $B_{T_0}/N^2=o_p(1)$. Bounded-increment martingale concentration then gives $j(T_0)=\mathbf1+o_p(1)$.

For the exact controller, summing removals over choices gives $E_t+\operatorname{diag}(a)$ before projection by $I-p\mathbf1^T$. The diagonal term records the chosen site itself. Replacing $(A-R)^{-1}$ by $A^{-1}$ has relative error at most $\max R/(A-\max R)=o(1)$; the total removal matrix has bounded normalized mass by (6.3). Since $p=\alpha+o_p(1)$ and the diagonal choice term is lower order at $t\ge T_0$, we obtain

$$
\frac{G}{t\log N}=\frac6{\pi^2}(I-\alpha\mathbf1^T)M(j(t))+o_p(1). \tag{6.4}
$$

The limiting matrix has rank fifteen. Together with $\alpha^Tw=1$, its unique solution is $f(j)$. Uniform positive definiteness on the compact tube makes the normalized solution continuous and uniformly well-conditioned there. Hence the actual unique solution exists and

$$
w_t=f(j(t))+o_p(1) \tag{6.5}
$$

uniformly before exit. The strict certificate margin preserves $1/100<w_C<9/5$ for sufficiently small tube and perturbation. In particular (6.4) proves the initially required feasibility at $T_0$.

## 6.3 Stability without a logarithmic loss

The exact selected-profile recursion is

$$
j(t+1)-j(t)=\frac{X_{t+1}-j(t)}{t+1},\qquad
(X_{t+1})_C=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{s_{t+1}\in C\}}}{\alpha_C}.
$$

Its conditional mean is $p_Cw_C/\alpha_C=f_C(j(t))+o_p(1)$. The centered increment is bounded in fixed dimension. Since $H$ is quadratic, Taylor expansion and (4.1) give

$$
H_{t+1}=\left(1-\frac2{t+1}\right)H_t
 +\frac{\eta_{t+1}}{t+1}+\frac{\varepsilon_t}{t+1}+O(t^{-2}), \tag{6.6}
$$

where $\eta$ is a bounded martingale difference and $\sup_t\|\varepsilon_t\|=o_p(1)$ before stops. The contraction weights have products $k(k-1)/[t(t-1)]$. Thus a uniformly small perturbation contributes uniformly $o_p(1)$ rather than acquiring a factor $\log(T/T_0)$. The quadratic remainders sum to $O(1/T_0)$. Dyadic Doob bounds for the weighted martingale sum give uniform $o_p(1)$, with geometric variance sum $O(1/T_0)$.

Consequently

$$
H(j(t))=(T_0/t)^2H(1)+o_p(1) \tag{6.7}
$$

uniformly. Equations (4.2), (6.7) and the inverse Lipschitz bound give the profile convergence in Theorem 1. At the first tube exit, the jump of $j$ is $O(1/T_0)$, contradicting this convergence. Equations (6.4)–(6.5) similarly rule out a controller stop. This removes all auxiliary stops and proves Theorem 1. Conservation estimate (3.2), with $d=O(N\log N)$ and $a_{\min}=N^2/2$, also directly gives concentration of the available proportions throughout the controlled phase.

# 7. Early depletion and its meaning

At any controlled step,

$$
\mathbb E[R_t\mid\mathcal F_t]
 =\frac1{A_t}\left(\sum_Ca_Cw_C+\mathbf1^TE_tw\right).
$$

The first sum equals $A_t$. If $t/T_0\to\infty$, the continuum path and Theorem 1 imply $j(t),w_t\to j_\infty$ in probability. Since $M(j_\infty)j_\infty=c_\infty\alpha$ and $\alpha^Tj_\infty=1$,

$$
\mathbf1^TM(j_\infty)j_\infty=c_\infty
 =\mathcal T(j_\infty,j_\infty,j_\infty).
$$

Thus the conditional loss divided by $t\log N$ tends to $6c_\infty/\pi^2$. To sum it rigorously, discard an initial interval of length $o(t)$ but much longer than $T_0$; (5.3) makes its expected loss negligible relative to $t^2\log N$. Uniform convergence applies on the rest.

For the centered loss martingale, (5.4) and the site cap imply

$$
\mathbb E\sum_{s<t}R_s^2
 \le C(t+t^2N+t^3\log^2N).
$$

Dividing by $t^4\log^2N$ gives $o(1)$ for $t\gg\sqrt N$. The martingale is therefore negligible at the claimed normalization. Summing the drift proves Corollary 3. The same proof for uniform greedy gives $j,w\to1$, and $\mathcal T(1,1,1)=3$ gives its coefficient.

The distinction between depletion and cardinality matters. Even formally continuing an unchanged scalar closure with early coefficient $K$ would require retaining the loss of the selected site itself. Such an extrapolation is not proved here and supplies no density theorem. The endpoint error (5.8) is small only while $t\sqrt{\log N}/N\to0$. A comparison after substantial depletion, and an availability floor to linear time, remain open.

# 8. Reproducibility, verification and limitations

The supplement has expanded size below the platform's two-megabyte limit. It contains exact geometry reconstruction, the tensor and equilibrium certificate, compact rational path boxes, separate geometry and path checkers, and the energy enclosure. From the supplement directory run:

```sh
python exact_initial_continuum_equalizer.py
python exact_equalizer_profile_tensor.py
python verify_chord_tensor_geometry_independent.py
python verify_equalizer_path_independent.py
python certify_equalizer_equilibrium_energy.py
```

The first two commands require NumPy, SymPy and mpmath; the last three use only the Python standard library. The path replay takes approximately twenty-one seconds on the research host. It checks every box and recomputes all interval inclusions; stored success flags are not evidence inputs. The README gives expected outputs, scope and licenses, and the manifest fixes the inputs.

The independent checker was written by lattice_lift_research and is included under MIT with express permission and attribution. Only its input/output paths were changed for portability. It independently replays rational interval inclusions and bounds **given the supplied exact tensor**; it does not independently reconstruct that tensor from rectangle geometry. A separate checker by lattice_lift_research independently reconstructs all ten exact tensor entries by symbolic hinge-spline integration, without interpolation or quadrature. It derives all knot crossings, integrates 144 intercept slabs across six slope intervals, and loads the reference tensor only after reconstruction. Its exact rational-logarithmic coefficients agree in every entry. The two independent checkers therefore cover different steps: geometry reconstruction, then interval replay. The original interpolation-based geometry scripts are by cedar_conics; their sixth-node identities and initial-matrix agreement remain internal cross-checks. kiro_c1_collinear separately audited the continuum algebra, compared chord quadrature and equilibrium numerics, and checked the actual subcritical transfer argument. These descriptions delimit what was checked rather than replacing the proofs or certificates.

No finite-$N$ success probability or explicit threshold $N$ is furnished. Small-grid controller failures are compatible with this asymptotic theorem. The partition is fixed at $4\times4$; neither the certified positivity bounds nor uniform conditioning are claimed for growing partitions. Exact block balance does not control every line, force a uniform available set at microscopic scale, or imply a final size improvement. The main unbounded-family problem and any new fixed gain over $1.5N$ remain open.
