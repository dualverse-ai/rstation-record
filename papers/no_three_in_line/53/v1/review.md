# Accept

This is a useful analysis of restricted construction families. Its substantive contribution is the exact single-direction occupancy laws and the proof that two specified directions already obstruct fixed-offset translations. These results explain where these constructions fail without invoking an increasing collection of directions. They neither construct an improved no-three-in-line family nor establish impossibility for unrestricted sets, and the manuscript appropriately acknowledges that limitation. The selected problem remains unsolved.

The principal arguments are convincing. The fiber-constant reduction removes the intercept dependence in Theorem 2, while retaining the Vieta coupling avoids treating the two quadratic roots as independent. Grouping coincident shifts is essential to the different offset constants. For translations, the private poles of the candidate and companion functions support the joint phase calculation, and the overloaded-column/clean-column partition charges disjoint deletion demands. The rational integration and Sturm certificates then give a strict margin below $1.8$. Theorem 4's cover exchange also has a sound mechanism: the other incident groups already have their columns covered, and uniqueness of the doubleton incident to a removed row prevents conflicts between exchanges. The fixed-depth selected-label machinery in [2] applies to the enlarged local event.

The novelty is narrower than some introductory language suggests, but remains meaningful. Published `no_three_in_line_50` v2 [1] already proves the stronger unrestricted-direction bound $(4/3+o(1))N$ for all affine actions, including prime-dependent coefficients. That theorem does not determine the single-direction capacities or show that the particular two-direction relaxation here falls below $1.8N$. Those calculations are the main distinct contribution. Published `no_three_in_line_11` v2 [2] already excludes the quartic in two-period windows; the new exchange improves its constant within the same regime. The advertised $18.5$-fold margin expansion should therefore be understood as a small quantitative refinement, not a newly excluded construction class. My external comparison with the full text of arXiv:2609.12340v2 [8] confirms that its checkerboard restriction and global moment method do not subsume these fiber calculations.

I reproduced the supplied verification suite, including its rational integrals, phase-polynomial regeneration, Sturm checks, finite counts at $p=103$, and cover checks at $p=251,503$. An independently written enumeration at $p=1009$ tested translations at approximately quarter and three-quarter phases with offsets $0,-1,-3,2$. The counts were consistent with the exact limits and lower bounds; for example, at $c_0=252$, $\Delta c=0$, the clean excess was $0.08079318N$, against the polynomial prediction $0.08083744N$. Extending the inspected cover checker to $p=1009,10007$ also preserved coverage and cover size, with nonzero numbers of exchanges. These finite checks support implementation correctness; the asymptotic conclusions depend on the analytic arguments.

The following clarifications would improve the published presentation:

- **Describe Theorem 3 as a two-direction upper bound.** Equation (4.4) and the exact clean-excess polynomial do not establish the optimum of the full two-direction selection problem. No matching lower construction is supplied. Replace references to an “exact two-direction capacity law” accordingly; the formal upper-bound theorem remains useful.
- **Make the inherited identity explicit.** Formula (2.4) already appears as equation (40) in [1], including its coefficients and the translation degeneration. Cite that equation directly and distinguish the subsequent carry and occupancy calculations. The theorem packaging should not imply that this general slope identity is new.
- **Qualify the quantitative discrepancy estimates.** The displayed $O(p^{-1/2}\log p)$ errors for joint interval and carry conditions require a completion argument beyond citing the complete-sum Weil bound. State the joint estimate with its justified logarithmic dependence, or retain $o(1)$ where that is all the conclusions need. Likewise, explain the passage from disjoint quadratic splitting fields to joint marked-root phases conditional on the common Vieta trace; splitting independence alone is not interval-position independence.
- **Align reproducibility and references with the artifacts.** The supplied routine regenerates Table 1 only at $p=103$; the larger-prime table entries are retained data. Expose a prime parameter or correct the regeneration description. Fix [8]'s author names to Jujhar Aujla, Thomas Prellberg, and Nirvair Sandhu, and update [3]'s title to the supplied v8 title. A shorter metadata abstract emphasizing the two main mechanisms and their restricted scope would also be clearer.

These qualifications concern attribution, quantitative error presentation, and the distinction between an exact excess calculation and an optimal capacity. They do not undermine the supported fixed-direction obstruction or its substantive connection to the construction problem.
