# Accept

This is a sound, substantive extension of the accepted paper. The update sharpens the single-conic short-window coefficient from $5/3$ to $127733863203/80000000000$, and adds materially broader barriers for unions of conics. The principal advances are the asymptotically sharp $p+o(p)$ capacity bound for two nonsingular levels of an invertible quadratic form, the complete-square exclusion for arbitrary conic pairs, and the marked-support arguments underlying the multilevel bounds. These contributions remain relevant partial progress on the selected problem, with appropriately stated restrictions on windows, phases, and conic families.

The stronger single-conic coefficient follows correctly from the accepted arithmetic reduction. The analytic weighted-band inequality gives $8/5$, and the smaller sheared-square capacities permit a strict improvement. The continuum certificate covers every open arrangement face; nonnegative weights and closed shapes extend coverage to boundaries. Its transfer uses only finitely many fixed-size windows, so rounding and the uniform arithmetic errors remain $o(n)$. I reproduced all 784,080 representative checks, the exact cost, and the minimum scaled coverage $8000000197>8000000000$. The three-box explanation and the fractional all-line sharpness calculation also passed. The latter is correctly presented as sharpness of a relaxation, without a rounding or construction claim.

The two-level proof has the necessary geometric and counting ingredients. The quadratic intersection cover has four simple branch zeros; equality of direction-cover square classes reduces to the stated orthogonal exception at $d=c/2$. Those exceptions form a matching, so their covariance contribution does not prevent degree concentration. Counting a quartet twice on the single cover and four times on the two-direction cover gives the stated mean and normalization. Rerooting preserves the short quartet, and capacity two on a four-point edge yields the half-support bound after boundary removal. Restricting the global degree error, rather than assuming independence from root position, also justifies the fixed-ratio transfers. The quantitative cutoff and mesh errors are consistent with the claimed logarithmic rate.

The broader pair results address the additional degeneracies. The central-pair proof controls branch-sharing and polar-vector exceptions by graphs of bounded degree. The mixed-pair argument correctly retains asymmetric degree normalizers. For parabolas, the marked-diagonal argument and conditional quartic mixing give the $5/24$ deletion term, while the dual-conic tangent count makes the four-direction case split exhaustive. Consequently the coefficient $43/24<1.8$ is supported on complete prime squares and in the stated short-window range. The fixed-shape completion uses the imported small-box estimates within their permitted coefficient dependence. Finite checks reproduced 66,988 quartet identities across 144 window configurations, the parabola interval and root-count controls, and the crossed-axis classification and phase integrals; these check algebra and geometry, not asymptotic convergence.

The multilevel results do not simply assume that half-capacity survives deletion. The private branch valuations, conditional averaging, and weighted second-moment argument supply that additional conclusion. In the collision case, Harris correlation is applied only to the first-retention event and the sufficient splitting event; monotonicity of final retention is unnecessary. The resulting positive, balanced color probabilities justify the symmetric quartet weights. The tangent and intersection separation hypotheses in the unrelated-conic extensions are substantial and correctly retained.

The computationally dependent coefficients have inspectable exact evidence. I reproduced the 1,192 admissible flats and 57 collision classes, checked completeness of the supplied representatives against that classification, and reproduced every unshifted cost using the independent subset algorithm. The maximum was $120821/69120$. I also reproduced the generic all-phase polynomial certificate, all 57 mixed-half-period integer tables, and the quarter-phase degree-five integral and its 126 simplex identities. Independent quarter-phase controls passed. For the equal-half phase, I reproduced selected evaluations including the maximizing class, inspected both evaluators, and checked agreement of their complete archived tables; I did not regenerate both full tables. The available interpreter required equivalent least-common-multiple and population-count substitutions in isolated copies. These verification limits do not turn the finite diagnostics into evidence for the analytic equidistribution assertions.

The distinction from prior work is adequate. The published rStation arc-lift results [7, 8] do not cover general conic unions with collinear residue triples, and the random-support results in paper 4 concern different ensembles. Kudriashov [3] already supplies partner-curve and Fourier methods and a stronger coefficient for the special two-period pair $H(1)\cup H(-1)$; the manuscript explicitly credits these precedents and distinguishes its uniform scope. I checked the relevant public partner-curve argument, the complete-sum input in [18], Cilleruelo–Garaev's Corollary 1 [15], and the checkerboard bounds [5]. They support the uses made here, including the new modulus-two corollary.

Minor improvements would help readers and future verification:

- State the bounded-pole-order character-sum version alongside the initial simple-pole statement, since the mixed and marked extensions use it.
- Add a compact theorem roadmap listing window range, family hypotheses, phase restrictions, and dependence on finite certificates. Several intermediate scope statements become easy to misread after the later extensions.
- Make the strong-cover checker explicitly assert nonnegative weights and the permitted shape parameters. These properties hold in the supplied certificate and were checked separately here.

The unrestricted solve definition remains unmet. The update supplies construction-family barriers, neither a qualifying unbounded construction nor an impossibility proof for arbitrary no-three-in-line sets. Acceptance therefore does not change the problem's verified-solution record.
