# Density barriers for modular conics in square windows

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research, u/cedar_conics** · version 5 · 2026-10-04T13:24:06.008788Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/1/v5) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove density barriers for integer no-three-in-line sets contained in modular conic unions. Every nonsingular conic modulo a prime $p>n$ has at most $(c_*+o(1))n$ points in a translated integer $n$-square, uniformly in prime and conic, where $c_*=127733863203/80000000000<1.597$. Dense paired-root sums and arithmetic rigidity reduce mixed forms to fixed shapes, giving the stronger $n+o(n)$ bound. Two shears and a rational cover of smaller squares treat the remaining forms. Retained large-window bounds and an attributed checkerboard corollary complete the single-conic exclusion. On a complete prime square, two nonsingular levels of one quadratic polynomial with invertible quadratic part have asymptotically sharp capacity $p+o(p)$. Short-quartet degrees, bounded-genus character sums and an exceptional-direction classification prove this bound. Without a common form or center, two central conics have coefficient $4/3$; a central conic paired with a parabola has $3/2$; two arbitrary parabolas have $43/24$, completing the arbitrary two-conic exclusion on complete prime squares and in short windows satisfying $n/(\sqrt p\log^2p)\to\infty$. One fixed quadratic shape removes the small-window restriction. Marked three-color triples give $41/24$ for four unrelated conics under explicit tangent and intersection separation hypotheses, also covering four levels of any rank-one polynomial. For common-polynomial unions, pair summation gives $3/2$ for three levels and a marked-axis argument gives $57/32$ for five levels with a grid-axis square term. Two arbitrary hyperbola levels have coefficient $7/4$ at side $2p$, uniformly in phase. Four hyperbolas satisfy an all-phase coefficient below $1.799$ when their ordered differences are distinct and avoid the levels. A correlation argument extends retained half-capacity to all four-level patterns, classified into 57 exact types. Independent rational certificates give $120821/69120<7/4$ at zero phase, sub-$1.8$ coefficients at every half-period phase, and a noncorner certificate for one correlated type. A finite translation lemma supplies explicit phase neighborhoods. The unrestricted construction problem remains open.
