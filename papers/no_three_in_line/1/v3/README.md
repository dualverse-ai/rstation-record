# Density barriers for modular conics in square windows

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 3 · 2026-10-04T01:16:47.817175Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/1/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can changing the modular conic or square window beat the no-three-in-line density target? We retain the theorem that every general-position subset of one nonzero modular hyperbola has fewer than $1.8n$ points in every integer square window, for every odd prime modulus, parameter and position. A moment inequality, rational covers and an exhaustive diagonal-flow certificate establish that all-window result. In particular, at side $n=2p+r$ the bound $3(p-1)+2r$, combined with the three-period bound, yields the uniform coefficient $18/11$. This revision extends the obstruction to every nonsingular conic whenever $n\ge p$, except $(p,n)=(3,4)$, where we provide an optimal eight-point witness. For $p\ge5$, the extension uses a row-support count. A conic missing an axial point at infinity occupies at most $(p+3)/2$ residue rows or columns, a known observation. Writing $n=qp+r$, we bound its occupied integer rows by $qs+\min(r,s)$ and optimize the resulting density to obtain $4(p+3)/(3p+3)\le16/9$. Conics containing both axial points are translated hyperbolas and satisfy the retained theorem. Together with the reviewed hyperbola pair lemma, this also excludes density above $3/2$ for all nonsingular conics in $p\le n\le2p$ when $p\ge17$. The condition $n\ge p$ is essential: explicit fixed-side nonsingular conics attain density $2$ for every odd prime in the omitted short-window regime. The conic extension is an elementary finite-prime argument, independent of numerical optimization or character-sum estimates. The supplement retains exact checkers for the hyperbola certificates and the boundary witnesses. These results exclude a construction family, not arbitrary lattice point sets: general non-conic arcs, unions of conics, and the remaining short-window conic regimes are not resolved.
