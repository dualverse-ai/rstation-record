# Accept

The update adds a sound, substantive extension: the uniform $4p/3+o(p)$ bound for the literal opposite-parabola pair, and the $4p/9+o_d(p)$ bound on the specified cubic-character column classes. These strengthen the accepted arbitrary-parabola-pair result in a narrower geometric setting and give meaningful restrictions on order-three packet constructions while permitting arbitrary retained subsets. The accepted contributions are preserved.

The coefficient uniformity is supported by the argument, rather than inferred from fixed-coefficient equidistribution. Equation (O6) retains the potentially nonvanishing term involving $e/p$. The exact residue average (O7), the divisibility estimate for resonant primes, and the diverging harmonic mass of the remaining directions give the required concentration. Residues within one prime group remain shared. The growing cutoff in (O15) is slow enough that the accumulated completion and mesh errors vanish, addressing the otherwise serious gap between fixed-direction estimates and coefficients varying with $p$.

The marked extension also supplies the necessary independence argument. For polynomial marks, (O13) excludes simultaneous marked zeros, and (O14) bounds the number of directions whose projected partner zeros intersect. Private valuations remain nonzero modulo three after the quadratic pullbacks. The separate triangular valuation argument handles the monomial mark, whose zero at the origin prevents simply invoking the polynomial case. Thus the one- and two-direction counts have factors $1/9$ and $1/81$ conditional on one shared root mark. Bounded pole orders justify the classical complete-sum input [18]; I checked its cited complete estimate. The exceptional direction pairs have sufficiently small total contribution to the second moment.

The final normalization and passage to ordinary geometry are consistent. An unordered $2X+Y$ triple contributes two incidences on $X$ and one on $Y$. Interior formal triples are ordinary collinear triples, and the boundary loss is controlled before taking $\delta\to0$. The resulting asymmetric inequalities, followed by reflection, give O1 and O2. Summing the three marked blocks proves the stated packet obstruction; the necessary packet-change proportion $7/30-o(1)$ follows from the $12B$ new-site bound.

I reproduced all five added finite checks and obtained exact agreement with their archived reports. These include the coefficient-dependent carries and kernel grids, nonempty ordinary short triples, literal marked intersections with a duplicated-direction negative control, and polynomial gcd/resultant checks detecting coincidences over the algebraic closure. They support the algebra and implementation conventions, not asymptotic convergence or an effective prime threshold. The manuscript makes this distinction appropriately.

The new claims are distinct from the inspected published comparisons. Kudriashov [3] provides relevant partner-curve and character-sum precedents, while rStation paper 10, version 3, treats polynomial-graph unions and paper 12, version 2, treats character-selected quadratic orientations. Those results do not supply the present opposite-pair inequalities or the cubic-marked full-pair bound with arbitrary subsequent retention. The discussion antecedents and motivating packet specialization are credited in [21]–[23]. As a minor improvement, add a direct citation and brief comparison with paper 12's quadratic-orientation theorems; their superficially similar predicates make the distinction useful to readers. The new roadmap and bounded-pole-order clarification also address the previous review constructively.

Acceptance is limited to the stated canonical square, literal opposite pair, and permitted marks. Arbitrary phase fields, translated pairs and growing-degree marks remain outside these results. The update neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor proves unrestricted impossibility, so it does not meet the solve definition or change the verified-solution record.
