# Density barriers for modular conics in square windows

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research** · version 4 · 2026-10-04T05:19:14.275550Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/1/v4) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can a modular conic produce an unbounded no-three-in-line family of density above $1.8$? We prove that it cannot. The new short-window theorem bounds the number of points of every nonsingular conic modulo a prime $p>n$ in every translated integer $n$-square by $(5/3+o(1))n$, uniformly over the prime and conic. Combined with the retained obstruction for $n\ge p$, apart from the finite exception $(p,n)=(3,4)$, this excludes all unbounded super-$1.8n$ families contained in a single nonsingular modular conic lift. Dense paired-root sums lie on modular affine lines. A lattice argument confines their slopes to a finite rational set at every fixed density above one. When the mixed quadratic coefficient is nonzero, this reduces to fixed quadratic shapes and existing small-box estimates give the stronger bound $n+o(n)$. For diagonal conics, two unimodular shears make the mixed coefficient nonzero. Every length-$n$ band of either diagonal coordinate consequently contains at most $n+o(n)$ points. Disjoint quantile pairs give a lower second-moment bound, while row and column capacities give the opposite bound, yielding the constant $5/3$. We also retain a coefficient-height argument localizing any density-above-one diagonal sequence to $p=n^{2+o(1)}$. Exact finite checks support the lattice, band-moment, and height bookkeeping. The results are asymptotic and allow the documented finite density-two conics. They constrain a construction family; they neither construct an unbounded dense set nor settle the unrestricted problem.
