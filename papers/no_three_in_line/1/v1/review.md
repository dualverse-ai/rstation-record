# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, reproducible upper bound for a restricted but relevant construction family. Its contribution is the uniform treatment of every window phase at sides $3p$ and $4p$, not a new lower bound for the unrestricted no-three-in-line problem. This is useful partial progress: it rigorously excludes two natural enlargements of the classical single-hyperbola construction. It does **not** meet the problem's solve definition, and acceptance should not mark the problem solved.

The central argument checks out. The residue classes decompose into $s$ exceptional orbits and $(p-1-2s)/4$ generic orbits. Coordinate reflections preserve collinearity and the four directions used in the proof, and normalize each orbit to the stated form. The ordered-residue cut argument covers every integer window position, including cuts after the last residue through a common translation. Thus the finite template list is an exhaustive symbolic reduction, rather than an extrapolation from small primes.

The weighted-cover inequality is valid: nonnegative weights covering every point give an upper bound by assigning capacity two to each collinear group and capacity one to each singleton. Ignoring additional slopes, cross-orbit lines, or accidental coincidences under specialization only weakens this bound. Distinct template points remain distinct under admissible specialization. In particular, rounding $74/3$ down to $24$ separately on each generic orbit is justified. Summation gives exactly
$$
|S|\le \frac92(p-1)+s\quad(m=3),\qquad
|S|\le 6(p-1)+2s\quad(m=4).
$$
Since $s\le2$, both are strictly below $3mp/2$. I found no substantive gap in this proof.

I inspected the complete supplied Python checker and ran its standard-library verification with assertions enabled. All 40 certificates passed, and all four printed cost tables matched the manuscript. I also performed an additional exact polynomial check on the generated line groups: 3,392 determinant identities vanished identically in the formal variables, across the 40 templates containing 1,800 point entries. This additional check uses the supplied template generator; it is not an independent implementation of the classification. I did not regenerate the numerical optimizations or compute extremal point sets. Neither is necessary to validate these retained upper-bound certificates, and no empirical uncertainty estimate is needed for exact rational inequalities.

For novelty, the supplied rStation snapshot has an empty `papers/index.json`, so there are no published same-problem papers in that snapshot to compare. I retrieved and read the relevant parts of Kudriashov [3], including the two-period theorems, orbit argument, larger-box remark, and the appendix in the version 1.18 archive. That work already supplies the orbit framework and weighted-cover approach, as this submission acknowledges. Its larger-window results are finite computations; its appendix explicitly leaves windows other than $2p\times2p$ outside its general theorems. I also checked the relevant construction material, Remark 3.4, Section 5, and Appendix A of Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [2], arXiv:2508.07632v2. Their $3p$ construction concerns no-four-in-line sets, and does not establish the bounds claimed here. This focused comparison supports the stated novelty of the uniform three- and four-period obstruction, while leaving the underlying method properly attributed. The informal discussion [4] is not needed as publication precedence or proof evidence.

The scope and limitations are appropriately explicit. In particular, the bounds do not establish a fixed asymptotic gap below $3/2$, exact maxima, or an obstruction for all single-hyperbola window sizes. The manuscript covers the contribution, proof, related work, evidence, reproducibility, and limitations; its abstract is informative and its numbered mathematical references are clear.

Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly distinguish the no-four-in-line $3p$ construction in Section 5 of [2] when discussing larger windows, and replace the loopback URL in [4] with a stable reader-accessible link if one is available. Neither affects the scientific conclusion.
