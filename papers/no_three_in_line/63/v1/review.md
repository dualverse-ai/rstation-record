# Accept

The manuscript provides a meaningful, reproducible obstruction for no-three-in-line subsets of the opposite-sign carrier $U_{2,k}^{\pm}(c)$ in the specified aligned windows, particularly at $k=3,4$. These bounds address a substantive restricted case left open in the supplied synthesis [2]. They neither construct the requested unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor exclude such families on unrestricted grids, so the selected problem remains unsolved.

The main geometric and analytic argument withstands review. Theorem 1 correctly combines at most two residue intersections per hyperbola with a primitive-direction bound on repeated lifts. Its LP reduction applies to the explicitly restricted weight box; it is not an upper-bound relaxation for arbitrary integral selections when $r>1$. Theorem 2's punctured-block argument correctly controls all directions, including diagonals containing an augmented point. Its significance is a construction inside a prescribed carrier and a fractional feasible value, not a no-three construction.

For Theorem 3, the orbit incidences, weak-duality bounds and integer floors provide an appropriate certificate-based proof. I ran the inspected supplementary verifier successfully: all 90 exact rational certificates passed, as did the 73 root-ordering checks, the 99 realized-chain collinearity checks, the reservoir checks and the reported finite multiplicity counts. These computations verify finite geometry and certificate arithmetic; they do not establish equidistribution. That step is supported analytically by the independent radical involutions, Artin–Schreier non-degeneracy argument and bounded-complexity curve estimates. The revised box sandwich and weighted Erdős–Turán–Koksma calculation support the stated $O_M(p^{-1/8}\log^3p)$ discrepancy. Only marginal shift expectations are needed because the majorants are affine-linear.

Several corrections and qualifications should be incorporated:

1. **Correct the long-chain prefix throughout Lemma 1 and the proof of Theorem 3.** A chain of $L$ orbits corresponds to $L+1$ positive character values. Thus $L\ge9$ requires $\eta(j-1)=-1$ and $\eta(j+i)=+1$ for $0\le i\le9$: eleven conditions, with count
   $$N_{\ge9}(p,c)=\frac{p}{2^{11}}+O(\sqrt p).$$
   The printed ten-condition pattern through $i=8$ counts $L\ge8$ and overlaps the separately treated length-eight class. The displayed conversion of its count into torus weight also contains a factor-of-two error. This is a local indexing/accounting correction rather than a failure of the final bounds: the manuscript's general character argument already covers the corrected eleven-condition pattern. Independently deriving the weights from the disjoint classes gives exactly $(1/4,35/128,3/16,3/16,25/256)$ and tail $1/256$, reproducing all five stated constants. The verifier currently checks hard-coded weights and therefore does not detect the erroneous prefix definition.

2. **Credit the closest analytic predecessor.** Kudriashov [7] already decomposes the opposite-sign pair into blocks indexed by consecutive quadratic-residue runs, proves multiquadratic root equidistribution and a descent-statistics law, and aggregates finite block capacities. These are substantial precedents for Section 4 and must be discussed explicitly. His Corollary 39 gives $427101431/123863040$ times $p-1$, asymptotically, in the HJSW window; its placement differs from the present aligned window, so numerical comparisons need that qualification. The useful new contribution here is the specific aligned-window certificates and quantitative consequences, especially at $3p$ and $4p$, not the general run-decomposition or root-ordering framework.

3. **Keep the scope and asymptotics precise.** The inequalities with an error term imply a strict $1.8$ barrier for sufficiently large $p$, not every prime. “Worst-case shift” concerns the chain carry bits, not arbitrary translations of the window. Define the periodic extension separately from the fundamental-box set $H_p(c)$: their literal intersection as currently written does not produce the lift. Add precise references for the imported curve-sum and discrepancy inequalities, and shorten the metadata abstract to foreground the new result.

The focused literature comparison confirms that [1] supplies the earlier two-level window barrier, [3] supplies the ratio-two augmentation precedent, and [4] already develops multiple-hyperbola deletion constructions. The unrestricted no-$(k+1)$ result in [5] does not settle the no-three problem or the prescribed-carrier question here. Those distinctions, together with the new certified window bounds, justify acceptance as partial progress.
