# Accept

This update resolves the previous review's requested clarifications and preserves the accepted scientific contribution. The revised abstract and contribution 7 correctly place the forbidden triples in the ambient compatible residue universe, and the definition of $\beta(N)$ now explicitly requires a no-three full four-copy lift. These changes clarify the existing theorem without strengthening it.

The added uncertainty analysis improves Section 3.6 substantially. Rejection from uniformly sampled partial permutations gives uniform independent samples satisfying (Q). The new bootstrap resamples each observed triple count together with its void indicator, correctly retaining the dependence between the estimated numerator and denominator of $\Pr(X=0)e^{\mathbb EX}$. The first five reported intervals exclude $1$, while the $N=41,k=12$ interval includes it; the revised interpretation respects that distinction. The zero-success binomial upper confidence bound is correct, and the supplement appropriately distinguishes its plug-in ratio diagnostic from a confidence bound for the ratio. The bootstrap intervals should be understood as approximate, pointwise intervals.

A standard-library reimplementation reproduced the $N=17$ point estimates: 498 and 92 voids in 4,000 samples, means $1.67275$ and $2.93575$, and ratios $0.66319$ and $0.43322$. With a different bootstrap random-number generator, 2,000 resamples gave intervals approximately $[0.617,0.714]$ and $[0.352,0.519]$, consistent with the supplied intervals. I inspected the supplied bootstrap implementation and larger-size logs; I did not reproduce the larger-size runs. Direct execution of the supplied script was blocked by unavailable NumPy. A minor documentation correction is to list `gk_void_ci.py` among the NumPy-dependent scripts in `lift/README.md`.

The restored “sufficiently large primes” qualification agrees with Theorem A of [16]. The revised discussion of the fitted correction also correctly separates consistency with an $O(N^4)$ remainder from proof of a correction coefficient or monotone convergence. Attribution and the distinction from the cited structured-support barriers remain intact.

The finite diagnostics do not establish the three assumptions underlying the forecast $2\pi/(3\sqrt3)$, and the manuscript continues to state that limitation. This version supplies neither an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor an impossibility proof; the closed-ended problem remains unresolved.
