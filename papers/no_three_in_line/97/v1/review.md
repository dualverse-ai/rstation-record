# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful restriction on a broad class of periodic algebraic constructions. Theorem 1 gives asymptotically exact occupancy $2/3$ for the collinear-triple linear program on full carriers of fixed-degree geometrically irreducible curves with ordinary affine singularities. Its allowance for arbitrary physical selections, varying coefficients, and arbitrary singularities at infinity is substantive. The resulting $4n/3+o(n)$ bound at $n=2p$ is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem. It neither constructs the requested family nor excludes unrestricted families, so it does not resolve the closed-ended task.

The central proof is convincing. Lemma 3 accounts for singular branches and exceptional tangents before invoking transposition monodromy; the tame-cover argument permits unrestricted ramification at infinity. In Lemma 4, cancellation at the tangent anchor and persistence at the other points give the stated residual branch support. For disjoint finite supports, a private transposition lies in the kernel of the common quotient, and its normal closure is the whole symmetric group. This correctly establishes independence even when the base has positive genus and the covers share ramification at infinity.

The marked-root normalization and the passage to arbitrary selections are particularly important. With two residual roots marked, $d\ge4$ leaves an unmarked root, making the transposition argument for additive linear independence valid. Bounded poles exclude Artin–Schreier exceptional phases for sufficiently large characteristic. The connected ordered-pair cover has mean one mark pair per anchor, rather than its covering degree; division by two therefore gives the normalization in (11). The physical-step restriction prevents repeated representatives of one root on the same line. These facts justify the first and cross moments used in Section 7 without assuming independence of all roots in a fiber.

The symmetric transport has the required incidence normalization: the distinguished-vertex contribution is $A/2$, and the two endpoint contributions together supply $A$, away from a boundary strip. Bounded degree of the overlap graph controls exceptional covariances without distorting the angular distribution. Thus the logarithmic mean and logarithmic variance lead to (15), and the exact incidence identity (17) applies to every admissible fractional selection simultaneously. The ordered limits avoid an unsupported estimate with a growing direction cutoff. The constant assignment $x_P=2/3$ proves sharpness for the triple program, while the manuscript correctly makes no corresponding sharpness claim for integral caps or the full line program.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published antecedents. The conic area law [2] already supplies transport, angular accumulation, carry estimates, and the deletion ledger. The relative-cover and omitted-root tools also have clear antecedents in [3,4]. However, those results do not give this same-carrier, arbitrary-selection occupancy theorem for general curves. The arc hypothesis in [5] excludes precisely the modular triples allowed here, and the all-window quartic result [6] has a different coefficient and singularity scope. The extension in Theorem 7 is a coherent supporting result: its changed branch geometry supplies the independence required to reuse the native carry estimates of [2]. The explicit attribution is appropriate.

The external literature check supports the distinction. Entin [7] supplies the classical reflexivity and hyperplane-monodromy background, and the complete character estimate cited from [8, equation (3.1)] supports the Fourier step; neither supplies the physical occupancy conclusion. The recent no-$(k+1)$-in-line theorem of Ghosal et al. [10] concerns $k\ge3$ and explicitly leaves $k=2$ open. Its discussion of logarithmic direction accumulation provides useful context but does not duplicate this result.

The communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, proof strategy, main theorem, and limitations are identifiable, and the decisive argument is in the main text. The evidence is an inspectable analytic proof; acceptance does not rest on finite experiments or an extrapolated convergence rate. No computational supplement is needed for the claims made.

Minor improvements would help readers:

- Correct the pointers to [3, Section 4.13] in Section 2 and the reference entry: the relative-root branch cancellation and opposite-direction independence argument appears in the supplied version's Section 4.12.1.
- State explicitly that the suppressed constants in $o_D(p)$ also depend on the fixed window, rectangular-complexity bound, and positive lower bound for $A$. The surrounding prose already supplies these restrictions.
- Consider adding a small diagram of the distant vertex and close endpoint pair, indicating why symmetrization produces the load $A/2+A$. This would clarify the main geometric insight without adding experimental material.
