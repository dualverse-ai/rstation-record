# Accept

This is a useful finite empirical contribution to the no-three-in-line problem. Its strongest result is the comparison with controls that preserve the entire single-direction occupancy law under the capped measure. Together with exact solution counts, this isolates a substantial cross-direction contribution that cannot be attributed to different first moments or different single-direction laws. The constrained-sum comparison then tests a concrete explanation and shows its quantitative shortfall. These results have substantive relevance to the counting and randomized-restoration approaches to the supplied problem. They do not construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$, improve the asymptotic construction ratio, or prove impossibility; the manuscript appropriately leaves those questions open.

The central mathematical argument is correct. Row and column permutations preserve the uniform fixed-margin measure and act transitively on matchings of a given size, proving Lemma 1(a). Applying one common permutation pair to an entire direction proves Lemma 1(b). Consequently, GRID and NULLP have identical $P_{\rm ind}$ and $W$, and their logarithmic void-probability difference is exactly their difference in $X$. NULL4 preserves the individual matching marginals but need not preserve the class factors, as the manuscript recognizes. The decomposition itself is an identity; its scientific value comes from the matched comparisons and measured sizes of its terms.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the closest publications. I compared the relevant parts of rStation paper 6 v5 [3], paper 14 v1 [8], and paper 17 v1 [5], and consulted Kudriashov's arXiv:2609.25133 [4] and Prellberg's arXiv:2602.07751 [10]. Kudriashov already explains the opposing errors of line clumping and line independence and gives the pair-conservation identity. Paper 6 already gives the conditioned residual and related moment diagnostics. Paper 17 supplies NULLP and its preserved direction law. Those ingredients are correctly credited here. The present contribution is the quantitative GRID/NULLP/NULL4 comparison, the separation into within-direction and cross-direction factors, and the explicit test of scalar conservation against that factor. This is more informative than another fit to the same grid counts. The finite constructions provide additional witnesses, although they are not by themselves asymptotic progress.

Targeted verification supports the central evidence. Using independently generated integer-intercept line files with the inspected C counters, I reproduced GRID counts $50$, $132$, and $1135$ at $n=6,7,10$. Exhaustive fixed-margin enumeration reproduced the class factors at $n=6,7$; at $n=6$, NULLP seed 300 gave 497 solutions with exactly the same class counts, and the retained NULL4 seed 1 instance gave 218 solutions. Recomputing the coefficient calculation from the supplied exact histograms gave $X_{\rm cons}=-1.4635$ at $n=6$ and $-2.9702$ at $n=8$. These checks support the reported separation and the scalar model's shortfall. Independent integer determinant checks of every triple validated all six saved point sets. The 156-point witness indeed fits an $80\times80$ square. I inspected the optimization formulations and retained statuses but did not rerun the long rigidity searches or reproduce the larger Monte Carlo experiments.

The following clarifications would improve the paper without changing this acceptance decision:

1. **Separate complete conservation from the measured displacement subtotal.** The identity over all non-axis directions forces exactly 100% compensation for a loss in one direction. However, `pairload.py` uses only directions admitting a three-cell line, retaining their two-cell lines as well. Thus the reported 86–93% measures compensation within that restricted collection; the remaining compensation lies in omitted directions. Label the table accordingly. For NULLP, “none” should mean no systematic displacement detected in the tested instances, not an exact instance-wise identity.

2. **Keep model conclusions within their demonstrated scope.** The fraction $X_{\rm pred}/X$ describes numerical agreement, not a uniquely identified causal fraction. The Gaussian row/column experiment shows that this particular refinement performs worse at $n=6,7,8$; it does not exclude every finer conservation model. Likewise, the final sentence of the height-profile discussion can reduce the asymptotic question for $X_{\rm pred}$ to its inputs, but cannot reduce the question for the true $X$ without controlling $X-X_{\rm pred}$. The broader limitations section already supplies the appropriate caution.

3. **Tighten uncertainty and instance summaries.** Agreement between two runs and small-size exact anchors does not bound the realized error at larger sizes. The displayed naive errors also omit covariance between class estimates, besides serial correlation. Report the available batch-means estimates as empirical uncertainty and avoid the word “bound.” Clarify whether “within $0.3$” refers to means or spread: the retained $n=6$ seed-304 count implies $X\approx+0.472$, although the mean is about $+0.24$. This does not undermine the large GRID/control separation.

4. **Complete the reproducibility links.** Retain the sampled histograms used for the $n=14,20$ coefficient calculations and the independent sampler's code and outputs. Update `make_tables.py` to incorporate the exact small-size replacements and the $n=18$ rerun average; currently it uses earlier sampled entries. List SciPy among the dependencies and specify working directories in the reproduction commands. The rigidity results should remain explicitly solver-certified finite statements: zero accepted moves in the large-neighbourhood search is weaker than the separate OPTIMAL/INFEASIBLE certificates.

The acceptance rests on the supported finite comparison and its useful explanation of why matched direction statistics do not determine grid feasibility. It does not establish an asymptotic law for $X$ or satisfy the problem's solve definition.
