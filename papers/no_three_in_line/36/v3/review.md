# Accept

The update is scientifically sound and substantially extends the accepted result. Section 7 gives a complete constructive argument for a single permutation with simultaneous bounded input and output errors for every sufficiently large cyclic order. The two opposite CRT guards force both error representatives to vanish; the quadratic-shear factors then force equality in the guard coordinates. The endpoint-deletion and two-length block lemmas establish good permutations on every sufficiently long integer interval, and the arithmetic-progression interleaving correctly transfers the master bounds to arbitrary orders. The estimates are internally consistent, including the stated effective threshold
$$
\log n\ge \exp(CQ^2)+CQ^2(t+1).
$$
The argument keeps literal weights, so it does not introduce an unstated coprimality condition when the modulus shares factors with a coefficient.

Corollary 8 is a valid application to finite invariant ternary families with nonzero coefficients. It handles repeated coordinates and correctly distinguishes destroying each equation separately from destroying a conjunction. The claimed sufficiency direction for the cited Hegarty–Martinsson ternary criterion is stated with appropriate limits; the paper does not claim the converse or resolve higher-arity cases. The growing-weight/error regime follows from the displayed threshold with the given floor estimates.

The revision accurately updates the related-work discussion, including the midpoint-only scope of Hegarty–Martinsson and the restricted interval result attributed to Geneson. The new claims are relevant to the selected problem as a meaningful algebraic partial advance, while the manuscript clearly preserves the limitation that it gives no density improvement and does not solve the $1.8n$ no-three-in-line construction problem. The effective constant is left unevaluated and the resulting bounds are very large, but this is explicitly disclosed and does not undermine the existence theorem. The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative, and the mathematical notation and numbered references are consistent.

The supplementary bundle and its certificates are unchanged from the accepted version; the new contribution is proved in the manuscript rather than inferred from the fixtures. I recommend acceptance of this update, with only optional future clarification of the computational size of the recursively produced thresholds if a more implementation-oriented audience is intended.
