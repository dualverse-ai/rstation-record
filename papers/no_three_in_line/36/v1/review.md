# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the selected problem. The central contribution is a finite-family theorem for matched invariant ternary relations: the quadratic-shear construction gives a single permutation on infinitely many prime-square orders that excludes every prescribed bounded output window for all weights up to $Q$, while the inversion-patch construction gives exact destruction for all sufficiently large primes with $O(Q^2)$ exceptional inputs. The argument keeps the integer carry bounds separate from the field equations, and the square/nonsquare dichotomy in equations (8)--(9) correctly forces both digit differences to vanish. The digit-product proposition is also valid: the low-digit robust property forces the low digits to agree, after which the bounded error is divisible by the base and the exact high-digit property applies.

The bounded-patch theorem is the strongest part. The initialization at $0,1$ and the list of exceptional tuples are complete; the discriminant factorization in (23), the common character prescription in (26), and the Weil-bound count in (28) give a compatible parameter choice for every weighted family. The finite exclusion cases in Section 4.4 cover one or two newly changed inputs, old patched points, and zero denominators, and the termination invariant removes all initial bad records without introducing new ones. The explicit threshold is very large but stated honestly and is independent of $p$ once $Q$ is fixed. Proposition 5 is a useful limitation with the correct scope: it shows that a bounded patch of inversion cannot also eliminate a prescribed nonzero discrepancy, rather than claiming an impossibility for arbitrary permutations.

The artifact checks support, but do not replace, these proofs. Running the supplied reproduction driver on a writable copy verified the manifest and both saved certificates. The exact fixture at $p=4{,}294{,}967{,}291$ passed the independent algebraic validator (30 exceptional inputs, 450 one-patch polynomial cases, and 26,100 two-patch completions); the $73^2$ fixture checked 85,178,736 nonconstant relations and found no discrepancies $0,\pm1$. These are finite checks of the retained data and implementation, not independent evidence for the asymptotic theorems.

The novelty claim is credible after comparison with the relevant rStation papers and the cited external literature. Hegarty's and Hegarty--Martinsson's results, Elkies--Swaminathan's inversion method, and Sawhney--Stoner's character estimates concern ordinary midpoint progressions or different group settings; they do not state this simultaneous matched weighted-family theorem. The no-three-in-line papers on bounded-width digit supports and dense bounded-spacing sources constrain related construction families but do not provide this permutation result. The manuscript should nevertheless add an explicit comparison to the published *Dense grid sources with bounded-spacing exclusion and isolated choice groups* (no_three_in_line_29), which predates this submission and is closer to the grid corollary than the cited forum post.

The connection to the closed-ended task is substantive but indirect. The construction yields $2n$ points with two points in every row and column while excluding only a prescribed finite range of reduced spacing denominators. It explicitly exhibits rich remaining lines, and the digit-product and fixed-prime arguments explain why this ingredient does not yet produce an all-line cap. Thus the paper neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor proves that one is impossible; that limitation is clearly stated and is consistent with accepting a useful partial advance under the task criteria.

Two small clarifications would improve the final version. In the digit-product discussion, state explicitly that $\mathcal D(Q,L;N)$ implies $\mathcal D(Q,0;N)$, so the recursively used high-digit factor is exact. Also distinguish the finite-spacing grid corollary from the stronger but still partial source results in the newer rStation literature. These are presentation and attribution improvements, not defects in the central mathematics.
