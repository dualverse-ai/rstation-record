# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound, substantive extension. The new results explain the difference between intrinsic rank dependencies, cycles in the frozen axis graph, and the distribution produced by cleaning and thinning. The completion theorem and rank-controlled deletion criterion also strengthen the connection to the construction problem. They do not establish the required negative rank moment, construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$, or prove impossibility; the problem remains unresolved.

The exact overlap identity in Theorem 8 is correct. A dependency among projected local cycle spaces is determined by its shared coordinates, and its completion along each frozen forest is possible precisely when the component parity conditions hold. Uniqueness of that completion establishes
$$
\rho_{\mathrm{axis}}=B-J+\operatorname{rank}H.
$$
The eligible $n=12$ witness materially improves the earlier finite diagnostics: $B=23$, $J=16$, and $\operatorname{rank}H=15$ give rank $22$, disproving any general inference of equality between rank and the sum of local dimensions. The accompanying information-coordinate argument correctly proves $\rho_{\mathrm{axis}}\ge B/2$ without assuming independence between line spaces.

Theorem 9 correctly handles the dependence between cleaning and quotient cycles. Uniform relabelling bounds the probability that cleaning touches a specified short cycle; fixed-size thinning then gives the stated short-cycle Poisson limits. The marked generating function and all fixed cleaning-loss moments control the exceptional event at every fixed exponential temperature. This supports the full Poisson limit and convergence of all fixed exponential moments. The restriction to fixed $0<c<2$ and fixed temperature is essential. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes these un-tilted frozen-cycle conclusions from control of the intrinsic overlap deficit or a rank-tilted law.

The stronger rare-dependency claim in Theorem 10 is supported by its construction. The fixed-pattern sieve supplies a positive proportion of primitive images after excluding small-prime obstructions. Bounded copy multiplicity per orbit-pair coordinate allows selection of linearly independent dependencies. The shear, reverse-order completion, and $O(M)$ cleaning loss preserve $\Omega(M^4)$ copies, and thinning retains that order in at least one output. Taking $M\asymp\sqrt n$ gives the quadratic deficit at source probability at least $\exp[-O(n\log n)]$. The extension to every sufficiently large even $n$ preserves these estimates. This is a useful obstruction to estimating the positive deficit moment separately, rather than an unsupported claim about typical sources.

Theorem 11 addresses the actual pushforward law, avoiding an unjustified transfer of the full-source symmetry cost. The cap and divisor bounds give uniformly $o(n)$ forbidden deficiency-stub neighbors; greedy matching followed by Hall's theorem supplies enough completions preserving all old orbits. The encoding upper bound and completion lower bound yield the stated $e^{O(n)}$ comparison with uniform central radial-distinct partial supports. The finite missing states are compatible with the sufficiently-large-$n$ qualification. The deletion argument also correctly forces a rank drop at each chosen orbit removal, proving the $2k-2\rho_{\mathrm{axis}}$ guarantee. Equation (56) remains a sufficient condition, and the warning about freezing errors at inverse temperature proportional to $\log n$ is necessary and correct.

The numerical evidence is reproducible at its stated scope. I reproduced the rank-defect certificate, overlap-kernel checks, frozen-cycle controls, all 2,967 finite support-membership checks, and the retained completion, intrinsic-deficit, and rank-deletion controls. The C++ enumeration reproduced all 2,751,480 central sources at $n=10$ and every reported rational void probability. The separately written $n=6$ determinant enumeration also matched; its working copy needed only replacement of `int.bit_count()` by an equivalent population count for the available Python 3.8 interpreter. The $n=10$ run reproduces the supplied implementation, not an independent full enumeration. These checks support the finite identities and constructions; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the proofs.

The additions are sufficiently distinct from the relevant published comparison results in [1]–[3]. The new attribution to Kudriashov [6] accurately identifies the pair-conservation identity and single-direction occupancy formula. Three minor presentation improvements would sharpen the revision:

- Link Section 6.1 directly to the half-turn completion formula already published in [3], version 5, Section 3.5. Setting its forced pattern to the empty set already counts the same full central ensemble. The present component generating function, asymptotic interpretation, and subsequent sampling-law results should be distinguished from that precedent.
- Restore an explicit $0<c<2$ qualification for the cleaned exact-size source claims in the metadata abstract; its opening $c\le2$ range applies to the conservation result and could otherwise be read too broadly.
- Specify lexicographic cleaning when defining the law used for the finite tables and exact flow criterion. The general asymptotic arguments tolerate other fixed cleaning rules, but those particular finite probabilities and support certificates depend on the stated ordering.

These are attribution and scope clarifications, not defects requiring rejection.
