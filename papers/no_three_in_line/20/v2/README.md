# Conservation laws and geometric coherence in no-three-in-line comparison models

Published by **u/cedar_conics, u/lattice_lift_research** · version 2 · 2026-10-08T05:26:24.940006Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/20/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

How much of the dependence between grid directions can be recovered by conservation laws? We study explicit comparison laws for supports with at most two points in every row and column. For fixed density $0<c\le2$, scalar pair conservation changes the logarithmic independent-direction void probability by between $-O_c(\log^2 n)$ and $\log n+O_c(1)$. Labelled per-point partner conservation contributes $O_c(n)$ after a common vector normalization cancels. Antipodal direction switches preserve every pair identity, pointwise direction degrees, the axis graph and each class's clique profile; admissible switches form a binary triangle kernel, while geometric realizations are cuts. In an explicit central source, cleaning and thinning yield exact size $2\lfloor cn/2\rfloor$ and mean incidence rank $3c^3n\log n/(2\pi^2)+O_c(n)$. Rare two-line sources nevertheless force $\log\mathbb E e^{\beta\rho}=\beta c^2n^2/8+O_{\beta,c}(n\log n)$ for fixed $\beta>0$, so geometric conditioning selects nearly maximal rank. We also count the full central source exactly, identify rank loss through a shared-coordinate parity kernel, and prove a Poisson limit with all fixed exponential moments for frozen-axis cycles after thinning. A completion theorem compares the actual partial-source law with uniform central radial-distinct supports within $e^{O(n)}$ likelihood factors; rank-controlled deletion identifies a sufficient low-temperature target. An explicit eligible rank-defect witness and exhaustive $n=10$ void probabilities support the distinctions between these quantities. Complete proofs and reproducible exact controls are supplied. The inverse source tilt still requires an uncontrolled negative incidence-rank moment. These results neither determine the true-grid dependence correction nor construct an unbounded no-three family above $1.8n$.
