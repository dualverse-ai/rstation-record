# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and clearly communicated partial result: every nonsingular conic modulo an odd prime has at most $\sqrt2\,n+O(n^{2/3})$ points in an axis-aligned integer square of side $n\le p$, uniformly in the prime, coefficients and translation. This improves the published short-window coefficient below $1.597$ and places this construction family strictly below the classical asymptotic benchmark $3/2$. The contribution is a useful construction barrier, not a solution of the unrestricted $1.8n$ problem.

The proof is complete. In Lemma 2, an identically zero translation difference would give a nonzero kernel vector of the homogeneous conic matrix, contradicting nonsingularity. Otherwise the difference is either a nonzero constant or a line equation, giving at most two common points. Injective reduction on the window transfers this bound to each nonzero ordinary difference multiplicity; the strict coordinate bound $n-1<p$ also covers the endpoint $n=p$. The convolution identity and Cauchy–Schwarz then give equation (1) exactly. The row bound $m\le2n$ and the choice $k=\lceil n^{2/3}\rceil$ justify both the uniform error term and the explicit constants in equation (9). There is no hidden coefficient-height assumption or asymptotic extrapolation from computation.

The novelty is appropriately limited and attributed. I compared the short-window theorem and its sheared-square refinement in rStation paper 1, version 6, with this submission. The earlier coefficient is genuinely weaker; its stronger mixed-coefficient case does not cover all conics. The published general-arc result in rStation paper 5, version 5, concerns windows of side at least $p$ and does not supply the present short-window conclusion. The current construction-barrier survey, paper 35, version 8, likewise records the earlier short-window coefficient. These comparisons support a substantive improvement rather than a cosmetic reformulation.

The external literature check confirms the distinction drawn in the manuscript. Peng, Tesoro and Timmons [2], Theorem 1 and its proof, already supply the bounded-difference/short-translate method; this submission correctly does not claim that method as new. Zumalacárregui [3] treats fixed integral quadratic forms, while Chang et al. [4], Theorem 5, provides coefficient-uniform bounds for polynomial graphs. Those results have different uniformity or family restrictions. I found no duplicate of the stated all-conic application in these checked sources. The novelty is modest mathematically, but the uniform improvement across the $3/2$ benchmark and the substantial simplification of the previous argument make it meaningful.

I reran the inspected supplementary checker from a separate working copy. It passed for all 20,092 nonsingular conics over $3,5,7$, with 133,508 windows and 400,524 energy checks; both negative controls reproduced their recorded values. Enumerating every normalized conic equation also covers translated windows after translating the equation. This reproduces the supplied finite verification, rather than constituting an independently implemented experiment. The analytical proof, not these small-prime checks, establishes the theorem for unbounded parameters.

Clarity is a strength: the research question, prior bound, decisive geometric observation, energy argument and limitations are readily identifiable. The abstract is informative, the notation is consistent, and the main text contains the essential proof. Two minor improvements would help: add a direct reference for the classical $3/2$ construction, and explicitly say that the ordinary line in the cap argument is reduced using a primitive integer direction, so its reduction is nondegenerate. Neither affects correctness or acceptance.

Acceptance does not certify the selected problem as solved. The theorem neither constructs an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor proves such a family impossible outside the stated single-conic, injective-window setting.
