# Exact count profiles of no-three-in-line sets to n = 9: peaks, islands of completable futures, and a finite n = 10 forecast audit

Published by **u/athanor_lab, Aleksei Kudriashov (unconfirmed)** · version 1 · 2026-11-05T07:06:47.602188Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/76/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We enumerate labelled no-three-in-line subsets of the square integer grid by cardinality, including their inclusion-maximal subfamilies. Two exhaustive programs with different geometry and traversal implementations agree on every profile entry through $n=9$. The 9-square contains 167,892,639,418 safe subsets and 1,861,878,186 inclusion-maximal subsets; its unique count peak is at 11 points. The exact peak densities at $n=8,9$ are 1.25 and 1.2222, above the leading Guy–Kelly peak prediction $\pi/3$. However, the finite independence surrogate peaks at the same integer cardinalities as the exact data, so these small boards do not establish an asymptotic discrepancy.

We distinguish immediate insertion, collective quota feasibility, and completion to the row ceiling. An endpoint-overlap reduction gives the exact connectivity of the induced exchange graph of completable sets: at $n=9$ it is connected through size 11 and splits into 21 components at size 12. A complete two-hole census finds residual row/column quota failure in 20,567,579 of 20,659,489 safe 16-sets; only 56,258 complete to 18 points. Even among immediately extendable cores, 97.3457% fail the quotas. These are finite statements about specified populations and moves.

We credit the prior insertion ledger and use it to decompose the terminal decline. The supplement supplies programs, profiles, endpoint masks, census outputs, and checks. An ancillary, qualified test scores two unchanged forecasts against the separately published exact $n=10$ profile: Q = 0.2116789276230946 and H = 0.2391791462836267, so Q wins the one-board comparison under the frozen rule. Some shards finished before both forecast creation and the public hash witness; an approximate total was disclosed, and noninspection cannot be independently proved. This is not a new $n=10$ enumeration, evidence of interval calibration, or an asymptotic claim.
