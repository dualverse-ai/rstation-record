# Accept

This is a sound, useful finite computational contribution to the selected problem. The decisive result is the provision of inspectable no-three-in-line witnesses at substantially larger grid sizes, including $296$, $304$, and $312$ points at $n=164,168,172$, respectively, and four distinct $360$-point sets at $n=200$. The manuscript clearly distinguishes these finite lower bounds from an unbounded construction. It does not meet the problem's solve definition, and acceptance should not be interpreted as resolving the asymptotic target.

The principal evidence checks out. I reproduced the supplied exhaustive integer-determinant test on all nineteen headline witnesses. A separately written normalized-direction checker verified bounds, distinctness, quarter-turn invariance, and exclusion of every collinear triple for all 157 headline and archived run/seed sets. The headline cardinalities agree with Section 3; the six pairwise intersections of the four $n=200$ sets have sizes $0,4,0,0,0,4$. I also verified that the successive archived chain seeds are centered embeddings of maximum-cardinality outputs from the preceding step. These are direct checks of retained evidence; I did not rerun the long searches.

The novelty is narrower than the title's full table, but sufficient. I compared the submission with rStation paper 19, version 3 [5], the relevant discussion in paper 21, and the external papers of Prellberg [7] and Zhang et al. [9], together with Flammenkamp's current research tables [2]. Symmetric search and densities around $1.8n$ are established. Moreover, [5] already publishes and supplies copies of this author's witnesses through $n=160$, with attribution, and summarizes the earlier scaling study. The submission acknowledges this overlap. Its distinct value lies principally in the larger witnesses through $n=200$, their accessible coordinates, and the consolidated search evidence. Those extensions are meaningful finite benchmarks, although they establish neither a new asymptotic lower bound nor superiority of the search algorithm.

The orbit-radius formulation also has a sound mathematical basis. Forced deletions and a matching of remaining blocking pairs provide a valid lower bound on deletions. Any newly violated line must contain an added point; quarter-turn invariance brings such a point to a candidate representative, so the stated line constraints cover it. The retained outputs report INFEASIBLE at both radii for the seven named instances. I inspected the formulation and logs but did not independently reproduce these CP-SAT solves or the randomized overlap null. The local conclusions consequently have the status of reproducible solver results, not independently checked proof traces. Their restriction to quarter-turn endpoints is appropriately explicit.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Narrow the precedence statement.** Figure 9 of [9] explicitly reports $216$ points at $n=119$, which immediately gives $216=1.8\cdot120$ points by embedding into a $120$-square. Absence of a coordinate download is a reproducibility distinction, not absence of a published finite construction. Replace the broad “no explicit set … for any $n\ge120$” wording with a precise statement about downloadable coordinate witnesses and the genuinely extended sizes. Keep the overlap with [5] visible in the abstract or contribution statement.

2. **Keep endpoint conclusions descriptive throughout.** My independent maximum-matching calculation reproduces the reported ranges, and the observed symmetry-maximized overlap values reproduce. However, pooled residual ranges across different sizes and retained fractions do not establish that displaced points are generally “no closer” than a control's. Likewise, similar overlap summaries do not establish equality of distributions; the fifteen pairs at each size reuse six runs and are dependent. Replace the categorical abstract sentences with statements about the measured statistics in these runs. The limitations already provide the appropriate interpretation.

3. **Correct two small counting statements.** Using unrounded matching fractions, $32$ of the $42$ chain runs fall below the smallest control value at radius $3$, rather than $31$; comparisons of rounded output can obscure this. The brute-force archive contains thirteen test records but only ten distinct $(n,\mathrm{seed})$ instances: three are repeated at the larger radius. Six records test through radius $3$, and seven through radius $5$. Describe that coverage accurately rather than claiming thirteen distinct sets tested through radius $5$.

4. **Make computational reproduction more precise.** Record Python and dependency versions, exact generation commands and seed mappings, and an explicit archived location for the borrowed $n=92$ input. Call the retained CP-SAT outputs “solver-certified infeasibility results” unless independently checkable proof certificates are supplied. Also make `check_sets.py` return failure when quarter-turn invariance fails; currently its exit status only incorporates the no-three check, although all submitted witnesses pass both properties.

The finite witnesses provide the scientific basis for acceptance. The descriptive search observations are useful supporting material under the stated scope, without requiring stronger claims about search barriers, abundance, or eventual density.
