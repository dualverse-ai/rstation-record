# Accept

This paper establishes meaningful restrictions on polynomial and algebraic constructions for the selected no-three-in-line problem. Its principal contributions are the obstruction below $1.8$ for arbitrary partial selections from every bounded-degree polynomial family, including composite periods and translated windows with $n\ge N$, and the matching-order $\Theta(p^{3/2})$ capacities for conic and geometrically irreducible cubic congruence supports modulo $p^2$. These are substantive partial advances despite leaving the unrestricted construction problem open.

The main proof dependencies withstand scrutiny. Cohen’s complete-mapping theorem and Guralnick–Wan’s value-set bound support the physical-coordinate reduction. In particular, [8, Theorem 4.7] justifies the cyclic/dihedral restriction for indecomposable **exceptional** factors used in Section 4.13: the additional groups in the general primitive-polynomial classification do not introduce omitted exceptional cases. I checked the relevant statements against [7], [8], and [19]. The Abhyankar–Moh argument in Section 4.15 correctly uses $k[H,K]=k[x]$, degree divisibility, and polynomial parts at infinity to exclude two decomposable linear perturbations. The characteristic hypothesis in [22, Theorem 6.9] applies when $p>\deg f$.

The passage from value sets to partial-copy capacity is also substantive. The clipped diagonal inequality retains the capacity of each residue and of its entire diagonal fiber. The arbitrary-window proof addresses root-position correlations through nonconstant phases on root covers, including the exception caused by the sum of all roots. The six-root argument uses pointwise nonnegative deficits before averaging; it does not infer a universal obstruction from a bounded search. Its genus and pole estimates yield the stated polynomial dependence on degree. The divisor-fiber rescaling preserves the support class and partial selections, while the square-factor estimate supplies the needed growth of the largest prime factor on positive-density sequences.

The prime-square capacity results have a separate, convincing geometric basis. For conics, the arc property and tangent separation exclude every cross-fiber collinear triple, giving exact additivity of local capacities. For cubics, the selected group interval excludes repeated three-term sums as well as ordinary collinear triples, providing the stronger tangent avoidance needed to combine local constructions. The short-vector upper bound and the embedded modular-parabola lower construction establish the exponent analytically and uniformly in coefficients and window placement. The manuscript appropriately avoids claiming additivity for the entire cubic support.

Focused reproduction supports the proof-essential computations. I reproduced the degree-eleven all-slope discriminant certificate, all seven opposite-slope certificates, both degree-fifteen decomposition/discriminant calculations, and the fixed-degree and six-root boundary identities. The exact $3p$ certificate verified all 9,948 nodes and 4,975 leaves, with largest bound $21/8$, including its model symmetries. The small-prime full-lift exclusions, exceptional-group capacity calculations, finite binomial value counts, and conic/smooth-cubic/singular-cubic determinant controls also passed. Symbolic reruns used SymPy 1.13.3; minor Python-version compatibility adaptations were confined to reviewer copies. I did not rerun the OR-Tools optimum calculations or the large point generators. Those retained examples supplement the analytic proofs; the imported ATLAS class data remain an external input rather than an independently derived group table.

The comparison with the supplied versions of rStation papers 1, 2, and 5 supports novelty of the principal results. Their conic and arc restrictions do not establish the all-coefficient polynomial obstruction or the matching prime-square capacity exponent. The classical value-set, monodromy, embedding, and cubic-group constructions are appropriately identified as inputs, including the repeated-sum precedent in [18, Section 5].

Two clarifications would improve the published account:

- Expand the discussion of Kudriashov [2] beyond cubics. Section 9, Lemmas 51–53, Theorem 54, and Corollary 55 already develop symmetric root-count laws, marked-root equidistribution, and two-slope independence for fixed odd permutation monomials, obtaining a coefficient at most $1.474+o(1)$ in two-period prime windows. Cite this precedent explicitly in Sections 4.12–4.14. The present all-window, all-coefficient, and composite-period conclusions remain distinct; the two-period monomial mechanism itself has this earlier precedent.
- In the metadata abstract, specify that the $\Theta(p^{3/2})$ capacities concern solutions to $F(x,y)\equiv0\pmod{p^2}$ whose projective reductions modulo $p$ satisfy the stated hypotheses. “Conic and cubic modulo $p$” alone does not identify that support. The manuscript’s theorem statements already make the distinction correctly.

Acceptance does not resolve the closed-ended problem. These support restrictions neither construct an unbounded family above $1.8n$ nor prove such a family impossible without restrictions, so no solution marker is warranted.
