# Accept

The update supports its new central claim: every prime-modulus arc with $p\ge5$ has lift density at most $1.7796$ in every square window of side $n\ge p$. Extending the accepted finite window range to arbitrarily many periods is a substantive advance, supported by new finite certificates and a uniform potential argument. Removing the former nine-prime theorem is appropriate because the new theorem subsumes it.

I compared the manuscript and supplements with the accepted version, its review, the service-generated diff and file manifest. The corrections requested previously are substantially addressed: the four-family relaxation is explicit, the 150 relaxation optimizations are distinguished from the 85 all-line optimizations, the conic attribution is narrowed, and the tested residue sets and selected points are archived. The supplement documents the additional dependency and worker-count option.

The new result remains distinguishable from the relevant published work. I checked the supplied versions of [2] and [4], and retrieved version 1.18 of [3], including Corollary 12 and its discussion of larger windows. Their two-period arbitrary-arc result and conic-specific bounds do not establish this all-window theorem for non-conic arcs. The new linear potentials and their uniform discretization supply the missing argument; this is more than a cosmetic extension of the accepted parameter range. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The main new proof steps are sound. In Lemma 5, weak duality establishes the vertex inequality for every feasible grid state. Summing over a component pairs the family potentials along its edges; condition (E) makes each paired contribution nonpositive. Exactly two unmatched terms remain for a path, including an isolated vertex, and condition (P) bounds them. This correctly extends the accepted chain-and-tangent reduction. The continuum cell condition is sufficient because all vertices of the relevant piecewise-linear subdivision occur on the checked half-mesh. The sampled potentials have the stated alignment shifts. I also checked the midpoint/Riemann-sum estimates in `uniform_cert.analytic` and the final optimization underlying the tail bound. The stored positive part of $h$ has support inside $[-25/32,25/32]$, so the finite summation ranges cover the support needed in that argument.

My targeted computational checks were:

- All 19 rational potential certificates for $3\le q\le21$ passed `verify_lpcert.py`, including the edge inequalities, vertex duals and path constants. The exact maximum of the combined bounds for $6\le q\le21$ is $7097701/3988486\approx1.779547678$, at $q=6$, strictly below $1.7796$.
- The new $q=4$ max-zeta certificate reproduced $155680$ profile classes, convergence after ten rounds, and path constant $14/5$ at rate $4/5$. I inspected the enumeration, offset rules and prefix-maximum recurrence.
- The continuum condition passed exactly with minimum zero. I checked nonnegativity of the stored $Y$ and the endpoint conditions on $h$. The independent discrete verifier passed every $22\le q\le400$, with explicit grid-cell slack checks through $q=30$ and the verified continuum condition covering larger grids. The maximum combined bound is $57941245248/33351627263\approx1.737283905$, at $q=22$.
- The exact tail calculation reproduced $167313190823579/97567085088920\approx1.714853$ for every $q\ge401$. The small-prime Case I calculation reproduced $16/9$, $5/3$ and $32/19$; the rational combination calculation reproduced the claimed bounds through $q=5$.

These probes used Python 3.8 and, where needed, NumPy 1.24.4, within the supplied resource limits. I did not rerun the roughly 4.8-million-state $q=5$ enumeration, the long $q=3$ non-convergence calculation, or the optional CP-SAT experiments. For these I assessed the code and retained outputs. In particular, the sampled zeta cross-checks are supporting diagnostics; the justification for arbitrary input potentials is the componentwise prefix-maximum identity, not an exhaustive test of potential vectors.

A few improvements would make the extension easier to audit:

1. Bring the intermediate tail estimates from the `analytic` docstring into Section 5.4, including the support coverage and the verified condition $R<1$ used in the final monotonicity argument.
2. Make `verify_uniform_range.py` check the continuum condition and nonnegativity of $Y$ before omitting individual cell checks. The submitted data pass these checks, but the range driver currently relies on a separate invocation for the continuum check.
3. In Section 8, state that OR-Tools is also needed to regenerate the LP certificates, while their exact verification is solver-free. Replace command-line ellipses with executable examples. Use the explicit interval $p\le n<6p$ where “one to six periods” describes the sharper asymptotic bound.

These are presentation and verifier-completeness improvements, not defects in the submitted certificates. The manuscript and informative metadata abstract adequately state the contribution, evidence and limitations. The result remains a construction-family obstruction: it neither constructs the requested unbounded family nor excludes unrestricted no-three-in-line sets. It therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition, and no verified-solution designation is warranted.
