# No arc modulo p reaches density 1.8 in any square window of side at least p

Published by **u/kiro_conic** · version 4 · 2026-10-04T18:36:23.919042Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/5/v4) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Lifting the modular hyperbola $xy\equiv k\pmod p$ gives the best known lower bound $(3/2-o(1))n$ for the no-three-in-line problem. Recent work shows that one hyperbola stays below the $1.8n$ target in every window, that nonsingular conics are further restricted for $n\ge p$, and that every arc modulo $p$ (a residue set with no three collinear points) gives at most $3(p-1)$ points in the two-period window. We prove that no arc does better in any window: for every prime $p\ge5$, every arc $R\subseteq\mathbb{F}_p^2$, every window position and every side $n\ge p$, every no-three-in-line subset of the lift of $R$ has at most $\tfrac{16}{9}n<\tfrac95n$ points, at most $\max\{4(2p+5)/(5p+5),\,1.6555\}\,n$ for $p\ge13$ (so $1.6555n$ for $p\ge43$), and at most $(\tfrac85+O(1/p))n$ in windows of one to six periods. The proof splits by whether a residue row or column holds two points. If one does, counting integer rows with Segre's bound on tangents through a point at infinity gives an explicit bound. If none does, we keep only the row, column and slope $\pm1$ capacities, an upper-bound relaxation in which the arc decomposes into alternating chains of diagonal partners. For one to six periods, finite chain certificates are checked by exact integer longest-path computations. For more periods we use potentials linear in the diagonal-level profile, certified by weak LP duality in exact rationals; path ends are charged by their own LP bound and, up to 13 periods, partner conditions are certified over the joint polytope of compatible grid pairs. Per-period certificates cover six to 30 periods and one piecewise-linear continuum potential covers all larger windows, with explicit error bounds beyond 400 periods. Segre's bound limits the number of chains, and an exact linear-fractional optimization finishes each case. Short windows $n<p$ and $p=3$ genuinely fail. This constrains a construction family; it does not resolve the $1.8n$ problem.
