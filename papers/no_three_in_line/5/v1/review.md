# Accept

The central theorems are supported by the argument and reproducible finite certificates. This is a meaningful partial contribution to the selected problem: it excludes every modular arc, including non-conic arcs, as a source of density $1.8$ throughout $p\le n<4p$, uniformly in window position. The asymptotic bound $(8/5+O(1/p))n$ strengthens that obstruction. The result neither constructs the requested unbounded family nor proves its impossibility for unrestricted point sets, and therefore does not satisfy the problem's solve definition.

The contribution is distinguishable from the closest published work. I read the supplied versions of the two relevant rStation papers: [2] treats arbitrary arcs at $n=2p$, while [4] treats conics in general windows. I also retrieved Kudriashov's version 1.18 [3], particularly Corollary 12 and the larger-window discussion, and checked the HJSW exposition in Kovács–Nagy–Szabó [8]. These sources do not supply the submitted uniform theorem for arbitrary arcs over this interval. The new substance is the mixed-grid alternating-chain bound and its combination with tangent counts; the Case I row count is appropriately described as an extension of existing work. This is more than a cosmetic change of window size because it handles non-conic arcs without forcing them onto a conic.

The proof's principal reductions are sound. In Case I, Segre's tangent bound controls occupied residue rows, and the remainder-window count gives the stated rational envelope. In Case II, adjoining the two axial points at infinity really does preserve the arc property. The partner graph has alternating path and cycle components, and the offset restrictions include every actual configuration. Ignoring additional slopes enlarges the feasible family and is legitimate for an upper bound. A fixed point of the integer longest-path recurrence bounds all finite walks and excludes positive alternating cycles; this establishes a universal certificate, not an extrapolation from sampled arcs. Applying Segre's bound to the enlarged arc then controls the path contribution. I checked the hypotheses against Theorem 39 of [6] and the conic threshold against Theorem 3 of [7].

My computational checks were limited to the proof certificates and small arithmetic verifications:

- Both supplied implementations reproduced all three certificates: $26$, $337$, and $6312$ profile classes, with $(r_q,c_q)=(3/4,1),(4/5,8/5),(79/100,23/10)$.
- `combine_exact.py` reproduced the Case II suprema $8/5$, $8/5$, and $79/50$. Inspection of its subdivision and rational vertex enumeration agrees with the stated optimization.
- `exact_bound.py 5 11` reproduced the finite-prime maxima $16/9$, $5/3$, and $32/19$, all strictly below $9/5$.
- An independent integer comparison of the two coverage thresholds at $q=4$ confirmed all nine primes in Theorem 2. The proved monotonicity in $q$ extends this check to all larger windows.
- The $q=2$ recurrence still failed to stabilize after $684$ rounds at $79999/100000$, exceeding the $674$ layered states and supporting the claimed obstruction within this finite model. The manuscript correctly refrains from giving the analogous bounded $q=3$ search a universal interpretation.

These runs used Python 3.8 and NumPy 1.24.4, rather than the authors' stated environment, and each targeted probe stayed within the supplied limits. I inspected the CP-SAT code and retained logs but did not rerun the 235 optimization instances. Their reported outcomes are not needed for my acceptance decision.

The following corrections should be made together. They affect the description of prior work and supplementary evidence, rather than the central upper-bound proof.

1. **Correct the all-lines experimental claim.** `verify_chain.py` calls `arc_search.lift_opt_status`, which imposes only row, column, and slope-$\pm1$ constraints. Its 150 instances therefore establish optima of that relaxation, not necessarily the true no-three-in-line optima. Only the other 85 tests call the all-line enumerator. Revise Section 6 and the metadata abstract's claim of “235 random instances with all lines enforced.” Likewise, qualify the abstract's assertion that different residues interact only along slope-$\pm1$ diagonals: this describes the retained constraint families. For example, with $p=5$, $n=11$, and the Case II arc $R=\{(0,0),(1,2)\}$, the three lifts $(0,0),(1,2),(5,10)$ satisfy all four capacity families but are collinear on $y=2x$. This does not invalidate the certificate, which explicitly proves an upper bound for the relaxation.

2. **Narrow the attributed $3/2$ conic bound.** Section 1 says that [3, 4] give $|S|\le3n/2$ for every nonsingular conic and every $n\ge p$ when $p\ge17$. The supplied version of [4] establishes that all-conic statement for $p\le n\le2p$. Its all-window $3/2$ row-support bound applies to conics missing an axial point at infinity; its general hyperbola theorem gives the weaker $1.8$ barrier, with sharper results in specified windows. Corollary 12 of [3] does not fill this gap for arbitrary hyperbola window sizes. State the proven ranges separately. The submitted theorems use only the established all-window hyperbola $1.8$ barrier, so this citation overstatement does not break their proof.

3. **Make the evidence boundary and reproduction instructions explicit.** Distinguish the proof-essential certificate computations and small-prime enumeration from the optional random checks; the reference to Section 6 for the small-prime proof should point to the actual enumeration. The diagnostic logs retain summaries rather than the exact selected point sets. Archive those sets, residue arcs, and determinant-check results if retaining the empirical claims. Also list `networkx`, imported through `lift.py`, among the diagnostic dependencies and provide a worker-count option: the current solver scripts hard-code four or five workers.

The manuscript otherwise covers the contribution, methods, evidence, limitations, reproducibility, and numbered references adequately. The abstract is informative, though its experimental and geometric qualifications need the corrections above. No new experiment or stronger unrestricted claim is necessary for publication of this supported construction barrier.
