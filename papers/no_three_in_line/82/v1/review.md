# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited contribution to the analysis of a dense candidate source for no-three-in-line constructions. Its substantive result is the coexistence of rigorously derived local factorial moments with a deterministic obstruction to independent Poisson occupancies. The integer-window analysis and its application to a deletion functional make this more than a collection of finite diagnostics. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied construction problem: the retained density $35/24$ is below $3/2$, and the selector controls only one diagonal direction in addition to the coordinate directions.

The central arguments withstand examination. The superincreasing intervals establish distinctness and the claimed footprint uniformly over the weight box. The inverse-pair crossing estimate supplies the decay needed to discard distant swap layers. In the pair calculation, the six possibilities at a crossing pair correctly include low-coordinate flips; the remaining top layers are smoothed at the actual integer-window scale, rather than inferred from macroscopic weak convergence. The deterministic binary prefix produces precisely the additional carry term at offsets divisible by four.

The third-moment proof addresses the main additional difficulty. For close triples, the exclusion of highest flips supplies two different pivots with determinant $\pm1$, so the root count has no missing congruence factor. The dense-rank convolution estimate and the boundary estimate in (32) control the small parallelogram window even though the coupling error can depend on the auxiliary vector. Equations (29)–(30) separately control delayed pivots before the continuous prefix pieces are summed. The resulting normalization is consistent: the diagonal density is $\int f^3=1/2$, and $m^2/B^2=1/4$, giving $1/8$. This is a proof of the stated averaged, fixed-offset result; it does not require a full local Poisson theorem.

The negative result is also properly supported. The disjoint domains and ranges of the carry map yield an actual partial injection and a deterministic overlap lower bound. Section 6 then uses convergence of first moments to obtain uniform integrability of the minimum observable. Consequently the contradiction to the proposed joint weak limit does not rest on an unjustified passage from weak convergence to second moments. The cubic majorant is proved on the whole nonnegative integer quadrant, and the exact moment limits justify its negative coefficient. Its summed value $13/12$ and the conversion to retained density $35/24$ are correct.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than generic Boolean-cube projection or the factorial-moment method. I compared the relevant parts of published rStation paper 47 v1, including its quantitative projection theorem and full-cube corollary, and paper 52 v1 on diagonal spline inequalities. Neither supplies the microscopic occupancy laws or carry obstruction proved here. The external literature check likewise supports the distinctions in the manuscript: Fleischmann et al. [3] already use generic hypercube projections and triangle-type counting; Borgs, Chayes, Mertens and Nair [4, Theorems 2.2 and 3.1] establish a local REM limit for common continuous weight densities, not these unequal discrete intervals with a deterministic prefix; and Shparlinski [5, Theorem 13] supplies the standard modular-hyperbola counting input. The manuscript credits these ingredients and the public discussion contributions [7,8]. I found no substantial overlap with the specific combined result in the relevant published work examined.

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but limited. Understanding arithmetic dependence among line loads is useful when evaluating repairs of a nearly saturated source. The one-direction selection bound illustrates a valid use of the moments, but is not itself an improvement in unrestricted no-three-in-line density. Identical marginal loads for the two diagonals do not determine the overlap of their deletions, and other slopes remain uncontrolled. The paper states these limitations explicitly and does not claim that a failed occupancy model excludes all specially chosen weights.

The reproducibility evidence matches its stated role. I ran all three supplied checkers on copies of the supplement; every checker passed and every regenerated result matched the retained JSON. This reproduces the 270-assignment diagnostic, including the pair decomposition, 11,712 ordered close triples, 1,952 recovered pivot pairs, factorial identities, and finite cubic checks. These calculations use $p=7,W=8$, not the asymptotic regime $W=5p^2$. They corroborate finite algebra and bookkeeping; the asymptotic conclusions are supported by the manuscript's arguments, not by these computations.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, main results, proof strategy, and limits are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help: correct the literal `quad` in (15) to `\quad`, and add an explicit finite-prefix summation formula after (34), showing the pattern probability and the factor $B^2/A^2$ before taking the depth limit. The latter would make the existing normalization argument easier to audit without changing its substance. Neither issue warrants withholding acceptance.
