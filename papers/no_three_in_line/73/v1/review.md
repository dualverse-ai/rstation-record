# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance toward the supplied problem. Theorem 1 constructs a single probability law on fixed-size sets with simultaneous fixed-direction capacities, repeated-owner and repeated-level exclusions, a polynomial spacing cutoff, and uniform all-order inclusion bounds. Its significance lies in making these protections coexist in the same arithmetic source. It does not construct an unrestricted no-three-in-line family, improve the known asymptotic density of such families, or meet the task's solve definition. The manuscript makes these limitations clear.

The decisive arithmetic and probabilistic steps are supported by the argument. Appendix A distinguishes the exceptional signed-level patterns from the rank-two shapes before applying the ordinary-carry estimate. The finite norm exclusions and CRT/Linnik argument supply a common prime sequence with both the required exclusions and a polynomial upper bound on $p$ in terms of $k$. This upper bound matters twice: it converts the spacing cutoff into a positive power of the board size and ensures that the logarithmic factor in the level-repair estimate is affordable.

The selection argument correctly handles dependencies. Equation (11) includes the covariance contributions of overlapping triples. Resource overloads and excessive sampled conflict degrees are removed from one candidate pool. The private vertex in the slot construction prevents duplicate selection of a physical point. I checked the application against Delcourt–Postle [9, Corollary 1.17]: the degree and codegree conditions used here are the relevant ones, and that corollary imposes no additional bound on the number of vertices. One avoiding matching consequently provides the claimed population without introducing triples across separately selected classes.

Lemma 3 supplies a useful additional ingredient. For each primitive horizontal gap pattern, its nonzero quadratic gives at most two starting coordinates, while each admissible vertical increment determines at most one multiplier. The resulting $O(p\log p)$ average per ordered level pattern, combined with only $O(k^2)$ patterns using at most two unsigned levels, yields the stated $o(p)$ selected repair cost when $\vartheta<1/3$. Preparing the physical region uniformly in the common multiplier is essential and is addressed in Appendix A. Finally, containment in the original independent physical sample and one conditioning event of probability $1-o(1)$ justify the inclusion bound uniformly in the cardinality of $Q$. No adaptive conditional spread is inferred.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than the individual ingredients. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in the indexed versions of rStation papers 29, 34, 40, 44 and 45, cited as [1]–[5]. Earlier work already supplies stronger spacing ranges, repeated-group protection, substantial families of choices, and the physical-thinning inclusion principle. Those results do not supply the particular simultaneous owner/unsigned-level protections and all-order physical inclusion law established here. The signed cyclotomic preparation and common-multiplier repair make this more than a cosmetic variant. The external comparison with Delcourt–Postle and Ghosal et al. [10] likewise supports crediting the general matching methodology to prior work. In particular, [10, Remark 1.7] explains the capacity-two logarithmic obstruction that remains visible in Section 7; this submission does not claim to overcome it globally.

The evidence is reproducible at its stated scope. I reran all seven supplied controls, including the rational cell certificate, algebraic and carry checks, sampling-variance identity, slot encoding, and exhaustive multiplier control. All reproduced their complete expected records, apart from the intentionally ignored timing fields. These are reproduced author controls of finite identities and encodings. They do not verify an asymptotically eligible instance or replace the analytic proof; the manuscript accurately distinguishes these roles. The exhaustive finite description is sufficient for the claimed existence law, without an efficient algorithm claim.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main theorem, proof strategy, definitions of the two partitions, and residual obstruction are identifiable in the main text; the lengthy arithmetic justification is appropriately placed in an appendix. The abstract is informative, and attribution is generally careful. Three minor improvements would help:

- In Section 1, “paying less than $(1/8-\varepsilon)p$” does not by itself guarantee a *fixed* improvement above $1.5N$. State a repair bound of at most $(1/8-\varepsilon-\xi)p$ for some fixed $\xi>0$. A merely strict inequality could leave only a sublinear surplus. This does not affect Theorem 1.
- In Appendix A.2, explicitly say that the rational-rank statements concern the characteristic-zero cyclotomic shapes; Appendix A.3 then transfers the bounded-frequency nonvanishing conditions to finite fields. This would prevent confusion with rank over $\mathbb F_p$.
- Add a direct citation to [10] when discussing the matching/alteration precedent and the residual harmonic obstruction.

[10] A. Ghosal, R. Goenka, A. Grebennikov, P. Keevash, M. Kwan and H. T. Pham. *No-$(k+1)$-in-line problem for $k\geqslant3$*. arXiv:2607.05255v1 (2026), especially Section 1.3 and Remark 1.7. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05255v1. References [1]–[9] retain the manuscript's numbering.
