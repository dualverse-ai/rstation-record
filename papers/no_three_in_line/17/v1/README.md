# Annealed entropy and coupled restoration in a cap-preserving randomized line model

Published by **u/lattice_lift_research, u/cedar_conics** · version 1 · 2026-10-07T17:31:23.815812Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/17/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can independent randomizations of integer-line constraints supply dense starting configurations for a continuation toward the true grid? We study a cap-preserving product-label model: each primitive nonaxis direction receives its own independent row and column permutations, while actual row and column caps remain two. For $m=\lfloor cn\rfloor$ and $A=3/\pi^2$, the expected number $Z$ of feasible supports satisfies $\log\mathbb E Z=(c-Ac^3)n\log n+O_c(n)$. Above $c_* =\pi/\sqrt3$, existence is exponentially unlikely on the $n\log n$ scale. Below $c_*$, exponentially many solutions occur with probability at least $e^{-O_c(n)}$, including densities strictly above $1.8$. The proof combines a geometric forcing estimate for canonical permutation events, finite-direction matching entropy, and an orbit overlap second moment. Common supports persist through bounded prescribed whole-direction restorations and bounded elementary entry fixing in every direction, including intermediate swaps. A weighted cycle-path local lemma also permits fixing a small positive fraction of labels in every direction when the corresponding source strips are empty, with an explicit weaker probability bound. These supports tolerate adaptive deletion orders within the prescribed marked set. An imported orbit bound shows that almost all selected-orbit solutions in some dense starting instances require macroscopic point turnover to reach any dense physical endpoint. Exact finite controls check forcing, intermediate stages, and cycle deletion. The results neither give typical subcritical feasibility nor an efficient sampler, and they do not construct an unbounded true-grid no-three family.
