# Accept

The revision makes two sound, substantive extensions to the accepted randomized-model result. Theorem 6 now establishes matching annealed entropy bounds and an $e^{-O(n)}$ subcritical abundance probability for all-subset restoration of empty marked strips. Theorem 7 permits restoration of a small positive fraction of occupied points, with an explicitly weaker probability bound. These changes advance the connection to geometric restoration without claiming a construction for the unrestricted no-three-in-line problem.

The new upper bound in Section 9.4 addresses the obstacle left by the accepted version: the full path-event family can contain nested events. Logarithmic truncation retains the required leading mass, and removing events that force another geometric triple loses only the stated polynomial-in-path-length fraction divided by $n$. The pinned-prefix/no-entry argument supports the free-tail estimates. Together with the geometric exclusion of alternative fully pinned triples, these estimates control newly created events uniformly enough for conditional transfer. The error in (S6) is summable over directions with total logarithmic cost $O(n)$. Applying the same argument to the union of two supports' triple families supplies the overlap coefficient $A(1-\rho)^{-6}j^3/n^2$ in (S8), which is the essential improvement over simply discarding the restored stages. The resulting subcritical range and probability bound follow from the stated overlap calculation.

The occupied-label extension also accounts for its additional costs. Its mixed deleted-coordinate/path representation includes cases where a deleted source is an internal path vertex. The resource estimates use both coordinate caps and the seed's exclusion of collinear triples, particularly in the four- and five-pin cases. Crucially, the scalar functional (O4) bounds the additional event mass uniformly over every residual support: averaging over affine images of the parabola therefore selects one seed before the residual orbit is chosen. The field inclusion probabilities and lattice counts give the claimed $O(p\log n)$ aggregate mass. The short-direction argument explicitly pays for fixing the marked labels pointwise, while the candidate entropy loses $p\log n$. These costs are retained in (O1)–(O2), and the convex overlap exponent supports the stated second-moment bound.

The attribution changes satisfactorily address the previous review. Comparison with [8, Section 6] confirms the provenance of the capped matching occupancy and leading geometric mean. The fluctuation and stratification results in [9–10] concern different laws and do not duplicate these restoration theorems. Lu–Székely [5, Theorems 4–5] already supplies the general sparse-canonical-event enumeration framework; the contribution here is the geometric verification and its restoration consequences. One minor clarification would help: Theorem 5 of [5] allows its event-size bound to grow with the ambient size. Thus logarithmic truncation alone is not the distinction; emphasize the clean-family construction, controlled compatible overlaps, and uniform error estimates in the product permutation space.

I reproduced all five newly supplied finite controls, with exact agreement with the retained outputs: the pinned-path identities, truncated clean/unclean event masses, occupied-source deletion states, affine-conic averages, and marked secant identities. These checks support the specified finite interfaces. They do not establish the asymptotic resource constants or independently verify the full scalar-functional bound; those conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments.

The scope remains appropriately limited. Theorem 6 requires empty marked strips and safety for every deleted-subset pair, not merely one prescribed order. Theorem 7 supplies no numerical restoration fraction, efficient sampler, typical-feasibility conclusion, or iteration to full restoration. Neither extension constructs an unbounded genuine integer-grid family above density $1.8$, nor proves such a family impossible. The selected problem therefore remains unresolved by this version.
