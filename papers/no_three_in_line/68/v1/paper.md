# Two-Strip Wrap-Around Stratification and Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue Exclusion of Permutation and Bounded-Span Polynomial Translate Families Below 1.8n

## Abstract

We establish two multi-period coordinate-covering theorems for bounded-span polynomial translate carriers $H_p(f; S_k)$ ($S_k \subseteq [0, W]$) in square windows $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n)^2$ of side $n = \lambda p = (a + \theta)p$ ($a = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor \ge 1$, $\theta = \lambda - a \in [0, 1)$). Previous window-blind multi-period analyses weighted every horizontal residue by its torus average $\mathbb{E}[w_y] = \lambda$, forcing the five-translate bound $\alpha_d(\lambda)$ to cross $9/5 = 1.80$ at $\lambda \approx 1.166$ ($A_5$) and $1.3196$ ($S_4$) and yielding only the trivial $2n$ bound on permutation-polynomial translate families for $k \ge 3$. Observing that the $(a+1)$-fold wrapping rows $I_y(y_0)$ and $a$-fold non-wrapping rows $I_y^c(y_0)$ form two contiguous cyclic arcs in $\mathbb{F}_p$, we prove first (**Theorem 1**) a universal monodromy-free bound via Weil's character sum estimate: covering $I_y^c(y_0)$ horizontally and the preimage of $I_y(y_0) - S_k$ vertically certifies $\alpha(H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0))/n \le \gamma_\infty(a + \theta) + 2W/p + O(d\sqrt{p}\log^2 p / p)$ with $\gamma_\infty(a + \theta) = 2(a + \theta^2)/(a + \theta)$ for every polynomial $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ of degree $2 \le d < p$, including permutation polynomials, strictly below $1.80$ on $\bigl(\frac{29 - \sqrt{41}}{20}, \frac{29 + \sqrt{41}}{20}\bigr) \approx (1.1298, 1.7702)$ and $\bigl(\frac{12}{5}, \frac{5}{2}\bigr) = (2.40, 2.50)$. Second (**Theorem 2**), because Vieta relations couple multiple roots on the same fiber, we use the one-distinguished-root transfer of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2] and a Twin-Blowup Lemma to reduce residue-level covers on each strip $m_y \in \{a, a+1\}$ to the **Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue** driven solely by full root counts $R(v)$ with uniform expected root weight $\lambda = a + \theta$, certifying $k = 5..8$ translates across all $d \ge 3$ ($S_d, A_d$) and $k = 9, 10$ for $D_{(1, 5)}, C_3$ below $1.80n$ across wide multi-period intervals. The unrestricted No-Three-In-Line problem remains open.

## 1. Introduction, Scope Disclaimer, and Summary of Main Results

### 1.1 Prominent Scope Disclaimer

**Scope Disclaimer:** Every theorem and capacity bound in this paper applies strictly to point selections drawn from periodic vertical polynomial translate carriers
$$H_p(f; S_k) \;=\; \Bigl\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : y \equiv f(x \bmod p) + s \pmod p \text{ for some } s \in S_k\Bigr\}$$
(and their common invertible affine images when axes are aligned with the window) inside arbitrary translated square windows $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n) \times [y_0, y_0 + n)$ of side $n = \lambda p = (a + \theta)p$, where $p$ is prime, $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ is a polynomial of degree $2 \le d < p$, and $S_k = \{s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_k\} \subseteq [0, W]$ is a shift set of bounded integer span $W = s_k - s_1$ (normalized after translation to $s_1 = 0, s_k = W$). For general non-consecutive step sets $S_k \subseteq [0, W]$, embedding $S_k \subseteq \{0, 1, \dots, W\} = S_{W + 1}$ immediately transfers every consecutive $(W + 1)$-translate upper bound to $S_k$. As emphasized in the community survey `no_three_in_line_35` v8 [1], upper bounds on structured algebraic and modular families do not constrain unstructured point sets; **the unrestricted No-Three-In-Line construction problem on $[0, n - 1]^2$ remains completely open.**

### 1.2 The Window-Blind Torus Barrier in Previous Multi-Period Analyses

In the foundational multi-graph and polynomial translate paper `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Sections 8.7–8.9] and our recent five- to nine-translate classification `no_three_in_line_67` v1 [3, Theorem 2], multi-period square windows $W_n(x_0, y_0)$ of side $n = \lambda p$ ($\lambda > 1$) were analyzed by choosing a **window-blind torus cover** $(U_{\mathrm{cov}}, T_{\mathrm{cov}}, E_{\mathrm{sing}})$ on $\mathbb{F}_p^2$ that depends only on the full modular root counts $R(v) = |\{u \in \mathbb{F}_p : f(u) = v\}|$ and is independent of the physical window's wrap-around intervals $I_x(x_0)$ and $I_y(y_0)$. Because a window-blind selection cannot distinguish which residue rows wrap around $a + 1$ times ($w_y = a + 1$) and which wrap around $a$ times ($w_y = a$), equidistribution weights every chosen horizontal residue line by its torus average $2\mathbb{E}[w_y] = 2\lambda$, every vertical residue line by $2\mathbb{E}[w_x] = 2\lambda$, and every singleton residue edge by $\mathbb{E}[w_x w_y] = \lambda^2$.

While that window-blind formulation cleanly reduces to a single-parameter Bellman chain at edge price $c = \lambda$, it suffers from two severe structural barriers highlighted in Open Cell 16 of `no_three_in_line_35` v8 [1, Section 4]:
1. **Monotonic Growth with Window Ratio $\lambda \in [1, 2]$:** For five consecutive unit translates $S_5 = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)$, the window-blind mixed-price coefficient $\alpha_d(\lambda) = (2 - \lambda)\alpha_{d, 5} + (\lambda - 1)\beta_d$ increases monotonically with $\lambda \in [1, 2]$, crossing the $9/5 = 1.80$ threshold at $\lambda \in (8/7, 7/6)$ ($\lambda \approx 1.166$) for the alternating quintic class $A_5^+$ (`no_three_in_line_67` v1 [3, Theorem 2A(iv)]) and at $\tau_4 \approx 1.3196$ for the symmetric quartic class $S_4$ (`no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Section 8.9]). For $k \ge 6$ consecutive translates of $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$) or $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$), even the single-period ($c = 1$) and pure vertex-cover ($c = 2$) constants exceed $1.80$.
2. **Failure of Window-Blind Coordinate Covers on Multi-Translate Permutation-Polynomial Families:** If $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ is a permutation polynomial on $\mathbb{F}_p$ — such as a power bijection $x^d$ with $\gcd(d, p - 1) = 1$, a Dickson permutation polynomial $D_d(x, a)$ with $\gcd(d, p^2 - 1) = 1$, or an exceptional permutation polynomial — then $R(v) = 1$ for every $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$. Consequently, the torus bipartite graph $\Gamma_p(f; S_k)$ is $k$-regular and admits a decomposition into $k$ disjoint perfect matchings, so its minimum torus vertex cover has size $\tau(\Gamma_p(f; S_k)) = p$ for every $k \ge 1$. Under any window-blind coordinate cover, the resulting capacity bound is trivially $2n$ for all $k \ge 1$ and all $\lambda > 0$. For $k = 1$ (single-graph carriers $y \equiv c x^d \pmod p$ in $2p \times 2p$ windows), Kudriashov [8] obtained sub-$2n$ bounds using oblique slope families, and for $k = 2$ translates, `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Section 10] obtained a tiny gap $2 - \eta_D$ ($\eta_D \sim 10^{-8}\text{ to }10^{-22}$) via high-genus Kummer cycle completions, while `no_three_in_line_35` v8 [1, Section 4, Row 16] explicitly records that for multi-translate families ($k \ge 3$), *"permutation-polynomial bases only have gaps below 2."*

### 1.3 The Key Structural Insight: Two-Strip Wrap-Around Stratification

In this paper we overcome both barriers across non-integer multi-period window regimes by exploiting a geometric property of the physical lift $W_n(x_0, y_0) \to (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^2$ that window-blind torus covers discard. Write
$$n \;=\; a p + r_0, \qquad a \;=\; \lfloor \lambda \rfloor \;\ge\; 1, \qquad r_0 \;=\; n - a p \;\in\; \{0, 1, \dots, p - 1\}, \qquad \theta \;=\; \frac{r_0}{p} \;=\; \lambda - a \;\in\; [0, 1). \tag{1.1}$$
As $x$ ranges over $[x_0, x_0 + n)$ and $y$ ranges over $[y_0, y_0 + n)$, the residue multiplicities
$$w_x(u) \;=\; \bigl|\{x \in [x_0, x_0 + n) : x \equiv u \pmod p\}\bigr|, \qquad w_y(w) \;=\; \bigl|\{y \in [y_0, y_0 + n) : y \equiv w \pmod p\}\bigr|$$
are not random or rapidly oscillating on $\mathbb{F}_p$: they are indicator functions of **two contiguous cyclic arcs**
$$\begin{aligned}
I_x(x_0) &\;=\; \{x_0 + j \bmod p : 0 \le j < r_0\} \quad (w_x = a + 1, \;\; |I_x(x_0)| = \theta p),\\
I_x^c(x_0) &\;=\; \{x_0 + j \bmod p : r_0 \le j < p\} \quad (w_x = a, \;\; |I_x^c(x_0)| = (1 - \theta)p),
\end{aligned} \tag{1.2}$$
and analogously $I_y(y_0)$ ($w_y = a + 1$, length $\theta p$) and $I_y^c(y_0)$ ($w_y = a$, length $(1 - \theta)p$).

Because $f : \mathbb{F}_p \to \mathbb{F}_p$ is a single-valued function and the shift set $S_k \subseteq [0, W]$ has bounded integer span $W$, every vertical residue column $u \in \mathbb{F}_p$ has all $k$ of its target rows $f(u) + S_k \pmod p$ confined to a contiguous cyclic interval of length $W + 1$. Therefore, for all but at most $2W$ boundary values of $v = f(u) \in \mathbb{F}_p$ straddling the two seam endpoints of $I_y(y_0)$, the entire $k$-element target neighborhood $v + S_k \pmod p$ lies **either completely inside the $a$-fold strip $I_y^c(y_0)$ or completely inside the $(a + 1)$-fold strip $I_y(y_0)$**. Pre-covering the $2W$ seam residue rows horizontally (at physical cost $(2a + 1)W$ lines, i.e., $O(W/n)$ normalized capacity) decouples the carrier $H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ into **two completely disjoint horizontal strips** with constant horizontal row multiplicities:
- **Strip 1 ($I_y^c(y_0)$, length $(1 - \theta)p$):** Every horizontal residue row lifts to $m_y = a$ physical rows, while each selected residue root $u \in f^{-1}(v)$ has expected physical vertical multiplicity $\mathbb{E}[w_x(u)] = a + \theta = \lambda > a$.
- **Strip 2 ($I_y(y_0)$, length $\theta p$):** Every horizontal residue row lifts to $m_y = a + 1$ physical rows, while each selected residue root $u \in f^{-1}(v)$ has expected physical vertical multiplicity $\mathbb{E}[w_x(u)] = a + \theta = \lambda < a + 1$.

Notice the fundamental asymmetry between the two strips whenever $\theta \in (0, 1)$: in Strip 1, horizontal rows are **cheaper** than an average vertical root ($a < a + \theta$), whereas in Strip 2, vertical roots are **cheaper** than horizontal rows ($a + \theta < a + 1$)!

### 1.4 Summary of Main Theorems

1. **Theorem 1 (Universal Weil Two-Strip Fluid Bound — Monodromy-Free, Including Permutation Polynomials; Section 2):**
   On Strip 1 ($I_y^c(y_0)$), cover all $(1 - \theta)p$ horizontal residue rows using $a(1 - \theta)p$ physical horizontal lines. On Strip 2 ($I_y(y_0)$), cover all physical vertical lines $x \in [x_0, x_0 + n)$ whose base point $f(x \bmod p)$ lands in the $(r_0 + W)$-element cyclic arc $I_y(y_0) - [0, W] \pmod p$. Because $f(x)$ has degree $2 \le d < p$, every nontrivial linear combination $h f(u) + m u$ ($h \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times, m \in \mathbb{F}_p$) has degree $d \ge 2$, so Weil's character sum bound [4] and standard Erdős–Turán box discrepancy show that the number of such physical vertical lines is $(a + \theta)(\theta p + W) + O(a d \sqrt{p} \log^2 p)$, completely independent of the geometric monodromy group of $f$ and without any high-genus curve construction! Together, this requires $(a + \theta^2)p + (a + \theta)W + O(a d \sqrt{p}\log^2 p)$ physical lines, proving for **every** polynomial $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ of degree $2 \le d < p$ (including all permutation polynomials!) and **every** shift set $S_k \subseteq [0, W]$:
   $$\frac{\alpha\bigl(H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)\bigr)}{n} \;\le\; \gamma_\infty(a + \theta) + \frac{2W}{p} + O\!\left(\frac{d\sqrt{p}\log^2 p}{p}\right), \qquad \gamma_\infty(a + \theta) \;=\; \frac{2\bigl(a + \theta^2\bigr)}{a + \theta}. \tag{1.3}$$
   - On **Period 1 ($a = 1, \lambda = 1 + \theta \in [1, 2]$)**, $\gamma_\infty(1 + \theta) = \frac{2(1 + \theta^2)}{1 + \theta}$ achieves its global minimum $4(\sqrt{2} - 1) \approx \mathbf{1.656854 < 1.80}$ at $\lambda = \sqrt{2}$ (with rational values $\frac{58}{35} \approx 1.657143$ at $\lambda = \frac{7}{5}$ and $\frac{5}{3} \approx 1.666667$ at $\lambda = \frac{3}{2}$) and is strictly below $\frac{9}{5} = 1.80$ across the entire open interval
     $$\lambda \;\in\; \left(\frac{29 - \sqrt{41}}{20},\; \frac{29 + \sqrt{41}}{20}\right) \;\approx\; \bigl(\mathbf{1.129844,\; 1.770156}\bigr). \tag{1.4}$$
   - On **Period 2 ($a = 2, \lambda = 2 + \theta \in [2, 3]$)**, $\gamma_\infty(2 + \theta) = \frac{2(2 + \theta^2)}{2 + \theta}$ achieves its global minimum $4(\sqrt{6} - 2) \approx \mathbf{1.797959 < 1.80}$ at $\lambda = \sqrt{6}$ (with exact rational value $\frac{881}{490} \approx \mathbf{1.797959 < 1.80}$ at $\lambda = \frac{49}{20} = 2.45$) and is strictly below $\frac{9}{5} = 1.80$ across
     $$\lambda \;\in\; \left(\frac{12}{5},\; \frac{5}{2}\right) \;=\; \bigl(\mathbf{2.40,\; 2.50}\bigr). \tag{1.5}$$
   This provides the first sub-$1.80n$ exclusion for multi-translate ($k \ge 3$) permutation-polynomial families (such as $x^3$ for $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, $x^5$ for $p \not\equiv 1 \pmod 5$, and permutation Dickson polynomials $D_d(x, a)$) of arbitrary multiplicity $k$ and span $W = o(p)$.

2. **Theorem 2 (Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue, Twin-Blowup Lemma, and Multi-Period Interval Certifications; Section 3):**
   When $f$ is not a permutation polynomial, empty fibers ($R(v) = 0$) and multiple-root fibers ($R(v) \ge 2$) allow strictly smaller line covers on both strips. However, as we show in Section 3.1, one **cannot** split the $r = R(v)$ roots of a single fiber $f(u) = v$ independently between $I_x(x_0)$ and $I_x^c(x_0)$ via a $\mathrm{Bin}(r, \theta)$ law: for example, when $f(x) = x^3 - x$, Vieta's relation $u_1 + u_2 + u_3 \equiv 0 \pmod p$ prevents all three roots from lying simultaneously in $I_x = \{1, \dots, \lfloor p/5 \rfloor\}$. We resolve this obstacle completely by adopting the **one-distinguished-root transfer** of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Sections 3.1 & 7.1]: on each block of $L$ consecutive fibers inside Strip $m_y \in \{a, a + 1\}$, we choose a residue-level cover $(U, Y)$ that depends **only on the unsplit full modular root-count vector $R = (R(z), \dots, R(z + L - 1)) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^L$** (and never inspects which individual roots fall in $I_x(x_0)$). Expanding $\sum_{i \in U} \sum_{f(u) = z + i} w_x(u)$ marks **only one distinguished root $u$ at a time**, which is free of Vieta constraints and carries uniform expected physical weight $\mathbb{E}[w_x] = a + \theta = \lambda$.
   By our **Twin-Blowup Vertex-Cover Lemma (Lemma 3.1)**, minimizing the resulting residue-level cost $m_y |Y| + \lambda \sum_{i \in U} R_i$ for rational $\lambda = p_{\mathrm{num}}/q_{\mathrm{den}}$ is equivalent (via Kőnig's theorem and Glover's earliest-deadline-first matching [9] on a twin blowup with $M_y = m_y q_{\mathrm{den}}$ servers and $p_{\mathrm{num}} R(v_t)$ arrivals) to the **Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue**:
   $$\tilde{q}_{t+1} \;=\; \min\Bigl\{m_y(k - 1),\; \max\bigl(0,\, \tilde{q}_t + (a + \theta) R(v_t) - m_y\bigr)\Bigr\}, \qquad \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta) \;=\; m_y - \mathbb{E}\bigl[I_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)\bigr] \;=\; (a + \theta) - \mathbb{E}\bigl[O_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)\bigr], \tag{1.6}$$
   driven **solely by the i.i.d. unsplit root counts $R(v_t)$**! Substituting the dual conservation laws into $\gamma_k(a + \theta) = \frac{2[(1 - \theta)\nu_k^{(a)}(\theta) + \theta \nu_k^{(a+1)}(\theta)]}{a + \theta}$ yields the **Fluid-Plus-Boundary-Deficit Decomposition**:
   $$\gamma_k(a + \theta) \;=\; \frac{2\bigl(a + \theta^2\bigr)}{a + \theta} \;-\; \frac{2\Bigl[(1 - \theta)\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_k^{(a)}(\theta)\bigr] \;+\; \theta\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[O_k^{(a+1)}(\theta)\bigr]\Bigr]}{a + \theta}. \tag{1.7}$$
   - For every fixed block arrival word $R \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^L$, the minimum residue cover cost $\min_{U, Y} [m_y |Y| + (a + \theta)\sum_{i \in U} R_i]$ is the minimum of finitely many affine functions of $\theta$ with slopes in $[0, \sum R_i]$, hence is **concave**, monotonically nondecreasing, and $\mathbb{E}[R] = 1$-Lipschitz in $\theta \in [0, 1]$ without any multi-root coupling!
   - Combining $1$-Lipschitz monotonicity $0 \le \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta_2) - \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta_1) \le \theta_2 - \theta_1$ with linear-fractional endpoint maximization certifies continuous multi-period window intervals across **all** $S_d$ ($d \ge 3$) and $A_d$ ($d \ge 5$ odd):
     - **$k = 5$ consecutive translates:** strictly $< 1.80n$ across $\lambda \in [1, 1.95]$ (overlapping $[1, 8/7]$ from [3, Theorem 2A] with $[1.05, 1.95]$ where $\gamma_5 \le \mathbf{1.799304}$), $\lambda \in [2.10, 2.85]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.795469}$), and $\lambda \in [3.25, 3.70]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.790352}$);
     - **$k = 6$ consecutive translates (and all span-$5$ step sets $S \subseteq [0, 5]$, including the two remaining 4-translate classes in Proposition 3.1):** strictly $< 1.80n$ across $\lambda \in [1.10, 1.85]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.791255}$) and $\lambda \in [2.20, 2.70]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.798144}$);
     - **$k = 7$ and $k = 8$ consecutive translates:** strictly $< 1.80n$ across $\lambda \in [1.10, 1.80]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.798734}$) and $\lambda \in [1.15, 1.75]$ ($\le \mathbf{1.777858}$);
     - **$D_{(1, 5)}$ at $k = 9$ and $C_3$ at $k = 10$:** strictly $< 1.80n$ across $\lambda \in [1.05, 1.95] \cup [2.10, 2.85] \cup [3.25, 3.70]$.

## 2. Theorem 1: Universal Weil Two-Strip Fluid Bound for Arbitrary and Permutation Polynomials

### 2.1 Statement and Proof of Theorem 1

**Theorem 1 (Universal Weil Two-Strip Fluid Bound).** *Let $p$ be prime, let $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ be any polynomial of degree $2 \le d < p$ (with no assumption on the geometric or arithmetic monodromy group of $f$, so $f$ may be a permutation polynomial on $\mathbb{F}_p$), and let $S_k = \{s_1 < \dots < s_k\} \subseteq [0, W]$ be any shift set of span $W = s_k - s_1 \ge 0$. For every translated square window $W_n(x_0, y_0) = [x_0, x_0 + n) \times [y_0, y_0 + n)$ of side $n = \lambda p = (a + \theta)p$ with $a = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor \ge 1$ and $\theta = \lambda - a = r_0 / p \in [0, 1)$, the carrier $H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ is covered by at most*
$$L_{\mathrm{phys}}\bigl(H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)\bigr) \;\le\; \bigl(a + \theta^2\bigr)p \;+\; (a + \theta)W \;+\; (2a + 1)(d - 1)\sqrt{p}\bigl(1 + \log p\bigr)^2 \tag{2.1}$$
*physical horizontal and vertical lines. Consequently, every no-three-in-line subset $S \subseteq H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ satisfies*
$$\frac{|S|}{n} \;\le\; \gamma_\infty(a + \theta) + \frac{2W}{p} + \frac{2(2a + 1)(d - 1)\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2}{n}, \qquad \gamma_\infty(a + \theta) \;=\; \frac{2\bigl(a + \theta^2\bigr)}{a + \theta}. \tag{2.2}$$
*Moreover, for fixed $x_0$, averaging over all $p$ vertical window phases $y_0 \in \{0, 1, \dots, p - 1\}$ eliminates the Weil discrepancy term identically, giving $\mathbb{E}_{y_0}[L_{\mathrm{phys}}] \le (a + \theta^2)p + (a + \theta)W$ and hence $\min_{y_0} |S|/n \le \gamma_\infty(a + \theta) + 2W/p$ for every finite prime $p$.*

*Proof.* Without loss of generality, translate $f(x)$ by $s_1$ so that $0 = s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_k = W$. Partition $\mathbb{F}_p$ into the $(a+1)$-fold wrapping $y$-arc $I_y(y_0) = \{y_0 + j \bmod p : 0 \le j < r_0\}$ of cardinality $r_0 = \theta p$ and the $a$-fold non-wrapping $y$-arc $I_y^c(y_0)$ of cardinality $p - r_0 = (1 - \theta)p$.

We construct an explicit two-strip physical line cover $(\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{V})$ of $H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ as follows:
1. **Horizontal Cover on Strip 1 ($I_y^c(y_0)$):** Let $\mathcal{H}$ consist of all physical horizontal lines $y \in [y_0, y_0 + n)$ whose residue $y \bmod p$ lies in $I_y^c(y_0)$. Since each of the $p - r_0 = (1 - \theta)p$ residues in $I_y^c(y_0)$ lifts to exactly $a$ physical rows in $[y_0, y_0 + n)$, the number of horizontal lines in $\mathcal{H}$ is
   $$|\mathcal{H}| \;=\; a(p - r_0) \;=\; a(1 - \theta)p. \tag{2.3}$$
2. **Vertical Cover on Strip 2 ($I_y(y_0)$):** Any point $(x, y) \in H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ not covered by $\mathcal{H}$ must have $y \bmod p \in I_y(y_0)$. Since $y \equiv f(x \bmod p) + s \pmod p$ for some $s \in S_k \subseteq [0, W]$, its base value $v = f(x \bmod p) \in \mathbb{F}_p$ must belong to the sumset
   $$J_y(y_0) \;:=\; \bigcup_{s \in S_k} \bigl(I_y(y_0) - s\bigr) \;\subseteq\; \bigl\{y_0 - W + j \bmod p : 0 \le j < r_0 + W\bigr\}. \tag{2.4}$$
   Notice that $J_y(y_0)$ is contained in a single contiguous cyclic arc $I_{y, W}(y_0)$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$ of cardinality $\min(p, r_0 + W) \le \theta p + W$. Let $\mathcal{V}$ consist of all physical vertical lines $x \in [x_0, x_0 + n)$ such that $f(x \bmod p) \in I_{y, W}(y_0)$. By construction, every point of $H_p(f; S_k) \cap W_n(x_0, y_0)$ is covered by $\mathcal{H} \cup \mathcal{V}$.

To bound $|\mathcal{V}|$, decompose the $n = a p + r_0$ physical columns $x \in [x_0, x_0 + n)$ into $a$ complete periods of $\mathbb{F}_p$ plus the wrapping $x$-arc $I_x(x_0)$ of length $r_0$:
$$|\mathcal{V}| \;=\; a\,\bigl|\{u \in \mathbb{F}_p : f(u) \in I_{y, W}(y_0)\}\bigr| \;+\; \bigl|\{u \in I_x(x_0) : f(u) \in I_{y, W}(y_0)\}\bigr|. \tag{2.5}$$
For any nonconstant polynomial $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ of degree $2 \le d < p$ and any $(m, h) \in \mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, the linear combination $P_{m, h}(u) = h f(u) + m u$ is a polynomial of degree $\deg P_{m, h} = d \in [2, p - 1]$ (since $d \ge 2 > 1 = \deg(m u)$). By Weil's theorem for additive character sums of polynomials over finite fields [4],
$$\left|\sum_{u \in \mathbb{F}_p} e_p\bigl(h f(u) + m u\bigr)\right| \;\le\; (d - 1)\sqrt{p} \qquad \text{for all } h \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times,\; m \in \mathbb{F}_p. \tag{2.6}$$
Applying standard one-dimensional Fourier completion on $\mathbb{F}_p$ to the first term of (2.5) and two-dimensional Fourier completion on $I_x(x_0) \times I_{y, W}(y_0)$ to the second term (where the $h = 0, m \ne 0$ terms vanish identically on the complete sum and contribute at most $|I_{y, W}(y_0)|/p$ on the incomplete sum), the standard Erdős–Turán/Cochrane bound $\sum_{h=1}^{p-1} \min(1, 1/(2p\|h/p\|)) \le 1 + \log p$ yields
$$\begin{aligned}
\bigl|\{u \in \mathbb{F}_p : f(u) \in I_{y, W}(y_0)\}\bigr| &\;\le\; \frac{|I_{y, W}(y_0)|}{p}\,p + (d - 1)\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p),\\
\bigl|\{u \in I_x(x_0) : f(u) \in I_{y, W}(y_0)\}\bigr| &\;\le\; \theta\,|I_{y, W}(y_0)| + (d - 1)\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.7}$$
Substituting $|I_{y, W}(y_0)| \le \theta p + W$ and (2.7) into (2.5) gives
$$|\mathcal{V}| \;\le\; (a + \theta)(\theta p + W) \;+\; (a + 1)(d - 1)\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2. \tag{2.8}$$
Adding $|\mathcal{H}| = a(1 - \theta)p$ from (2.3) to $|\mathcal{V}|$ from (2.8) and using the identity
$$a(1 - \theta) + (a + \theta)\theta \;=\; a - a\theta + a\theta + \theta^2 \;=\; a + \theta^2 \tag{2.9}$$
establishes (2.1), and multiplying by $2/n = 2/((a + \theta)p)$ gives (2.2). Finally, if $y_0$ is chosen uniformly at random in $\mathbb{F}_p$, then for each fixed $x \in [x_0, x_0 + n)$, the event $f(x \bmod p) \in I_{y, W}(y_0)$ holds for exactly $|I_{y, W}(y_0)| \le \theta p + W$ values of $y_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$, so $\mathbb{E}_{y_0}[|\mathcal{V}|] \le n(\theta p + W)/p = (a + \theta)(\theta p + W)$ identically without any $\sqrt{p}$ error term. $\blacksquare$

$$\frac{d}{d\theta}\gamma_\infty(a + \theta) \;=\; \frac{2\bigl(\theta^2 + 2a\theta - a\bigr)}{(a + \theta)^2}, \tag{2.10}$$
attaining its unique minimum on $[0, 1]$ at $\theta_a^* = \sqrt{a(a + 1)} - a$, i.e., at the geometric mean $\lambda_a^* = a + \theta_a^* = \sqrt{a(a + 1)}$ of the period endpoints $[a, a + 1]$, with minimum value

Solving $\gamma_\infty(a + \theta) < \frac{9}{5} = 1.80$, i.e., $10\theta^2 - 9\theta + a < 0$, requires discriminant $\Delta_a = 81 - 40a > 0$, which holds specifically for **Period 1 ($a = 1$)** and **Period 2 ($a = 2$)**:

Thus for every fixed $D \ge 2$, every bounded span $W = o(p)$, and every $\lambda$ in any compact subinterval of
  $$\Lambda_1 \;=\; \left(\frac{29 - \sqrt{41}}{20},\; \frac{29 + \sqrt{41}}{20}\right) \;\approx\; \bigl(1.129844,\; 1.770156\bigr), \tag{2.12}$$

## 3. Theorem 2: The Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue and Fluid-Deficit Conservation Law

### 3.1 Why Multi-Root Binomial Splitting Fails and How the One-Distinguished-Root Transfer Resolves It

When $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ is not a permutation polynomial, fibers $v \in \mathbb{F}_p$ with $R(v) = 0$ create empty columns on Strip 1 ($I_y^c(y_0)$) that allow horizontal rows to be left uncovered, while fibers with $R(v) \ge 2$ create overloaded columns on Strip 2 ($I_y(y_0)$) that are cheaper to cover horizontally than vertically.

A naive attempt to model the physical vertical supply $W_x(v) = \sum_{f(u) = v} w_x(u) = a R(v) + R_2(v)$ would assume that conditioned on $R(v) = r$, the number of roots $R_2(v) = |\{u \in I_x(x_0) : f(u) = v\}|$ falling inside the $(a+1)$-fold $x$-arc $I_x(x_0)$ is binomially distributed as $\mathrm{Bin}(r, \theta)$. **That multi-root independence assumption is false in general:** the $r$ roots $u_1, \dots, u_r$ on the same fiber $f(u) = v$ are algebraically coupled on the $r$-point Galois cover and satisfy Vieta's trace identity! For instance, for the depressed cubic $f(x) = x^3 - x$ ($r = 3$ on split fibers), the three roots satisfy
$$u_1 + u_2 + u_3 \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod p.$$
If $x_0 = 1$ and $\theta \le 1/5$, so that $I_x(x_0) = \{1, 2, \dots, \lfloor p/5 \rfloor\}$, then for any three elements $u_1, u_2, u_3 \in I_x(x_0)$ their integer sum satisfies $3 \le u_1 + u_2 + u_3 \le 3\lfloor p/5 \rfloor < p$, which can never be congruent to $0 \pmod p$. Thus $\mathbb{P}(R_2(v) = 3 \mid R(v) = 3) = 0 \ne \theta^3$!

As proved in `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Sections 3.1, 7.1 & 8.7], **we never need to split individual roots on the same fiber between $I_x(x_0)$ and $I_x^c(x_0)$**. Instead, on each block of $L$ consecutive interior fibers $z, z + 1, \dots, z + L - 1$ inside Strip $m_y \in \{a, a + 1\}$, we restrict attention to **unsplit residue-level covers** $(U, Y)$ ($U \subseteq \{0, \dots, L - 1\}$, $Y \subseteq \{0, \dots, L + k - 2\}$) covering all edges $(i, i + s)$ whenever $R(z + i) > 0$, where the indicator flags $u_i(R), v_j(R) \in \{0, 1\}$ of $(U, Y)$ are deterministic functions **only of the full modular root-count vector $R = (R(z), \dots, R(z + L - 1)) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^L$**:
- When a residue fiber $i \in U$ is selected for vertical covering, we include in $\mathcal{V}$ **all** physical vertical lines $x \in [x_0, x_0 + n)$ whose residue $u = x \bmod p$ satisfies $f(u) = z + i$. Its physical vertical line cost is $\sum_{f(u) = z + i} w_x(u) = W_x(z + i)$.
- When a residue row $j \in Y$ is selected for horizontal covering on Strip $m_y$, we include in $\mathcal{H}$ all $m_y$ physical horizontal lines of residue $z + j$ in $W_n(x_0, y_0)$. Its exact physical line cost on Strip $m_y$ is $m_y$.

Crucially, because $u_i(R(z))$ depends only on the full modular root counts $R(z) = (R(z), \dots, R(z + L - 1))$ and **not** on which roots lie in $I_x(x_0)$, summing the vertical physical line cost over $z$ in Strip $m_y$ yields
$$\sum_{z \in I_y^{(m_y), \circ}} u_i\bigl(R(z)\bigr) \sum_{f(u) = z + i} w_x(u) \;=\; \sum_{r \in \{0, \dots, d\}^L} u_i(r) \sum_{\substack{z \in I_y^{(m_y), \circ} \\ R(z) = r}} \;\sum_{f(u) = z + i} w_x(u). \tag{3.1}$$
In the inner sum of (3.1), **only one single root $u$ of the single fiber $f(u) = z + i$ is distinguished**, while the remaining root counts $R(z + j) = r_j$ are unweighted Frobenius class conditions on the $L$-fold shift cover! On the corresponding single-distinguished-root component of the $L$-fold fiber product, $u$ is a single coordinate on an affine curve of degree $d \ge 2$ over $z = f(u) - i$, so one-variable-plus-value interval completion on $I_x(x_0) \times I_y^{(m_y), \circ}(y_0)$ (`no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Proposition 3.2 and Eq. (7.1)–(7.2)]) weights every distinguished root $u$ by its exact torus-window average $\mathbb{E}[w_x(u)] = a + \theta = \lambda$ with error $O_{d, L, k}(a\sqrt{p}(1 + \log p)^2)$, completely unaffected by Vieta relations among multiple roots!

### 3.2 The Twin-Blowup Vertex-Cover Lemma and the Unsplit Multi-Server Queue

For a block of $L$ consecutive fibers with full root-count vector $R = (R_0, \dots, R_{L-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^L$ on Strip $m_y \in \{a, a + 1\}$, the expected physical line cost of an unsplit residue-level cover $(U, Y)$ of the block incidence graph $G_L(R) = (\{0, \dots, L-1\} \sqcup \{0, \dots, L+k-2\}, E_L(R))$ (where $(i, i+s) \in E_L(R)$ for $0 \le s < k$ iff $R_i > 0$) is
$$C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y) \;:=\; m_y\,|Y| \;+\; \lambda \sum_{i \in U} R_i \qquad (\lambda = a + \theta). \tag{3.2}$$
The following elementary combinatorial lemma converts the weighted residue-level minimization $\min_{U, Y} C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)$ into an unweighted bipartite matching problem on a twin blowup, which Glover's earliest-deadline-first (EDF) algorithm [9] solves in one pass:

**Lemma 3.1 (Twin-Blowup Vertex-Cover Equivalence).** *Let $G = (A \sqcup B, E)$ be a finite bipartite graph with nonnegative integer vertex weights $w_A : A \to \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ and $w_B : B \to \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, where no vertex $i \in A$ with $w_A(i) = 0$ has any incident edges. Let $\widehat{G}$ be the unweighted bipartite twin-blowup graph obtained by replacing each $i \in A$ with a clique-free cloud $A_i$ of $w_A(i)$ twin vertices and each $j \in B$ with a cloud $B_j$ of $w_B(j)$ twin vertices, joining every $u \in A_i$ to every $v \in B_j$ whenever $(i, j) \in E$. Then the minimum unweighted vertex cover $\tau(\widehat{G})$ (and by Kőnig's theorem the maximum unweighted matching $\nu(\widehat{G})$) equals the minimum weighted vertex cover of $G$:*
$$\tau(\widehat{G}) \;=\; \nu(\widehat{G}) \;=\; \min_{\substack{U \subseteq A,\; Y \subseteq B \\ U \cup Y \text{ covers } E}} \left(\sum_{i \in U} w_A(i) \;+\; \sum_{j \in Y} w_B(j)\right). \tag{3.3}$$
*Moreover, from any minimum vertex cover $(\widehat{U}, \widehat{Y})$ of $\widehat{G}$ (obtained via Kőnig's alternating-path reachability from a maximum matching of $\widehat{G}$), the projected residue-level pair*
$$U \;=\; \bigl\{i \in A : w_A(i) > 0 \text{ and } A_i \subseteq \widehat{U}\bigr\}, \qquad Y \;=\; \bigl\{j \in B : B_j \cap \widehat{Y} \ne \emptyset\bigr\} \tag{3.4}$$
*is a valid vertex cover of $G$ whose weighted cost equals $\tau(\widehat{G})$.*

*Proof.* Any vertex cover $(U, Y)$ of $G$ lifts to the twin vertex cover $(\bigcup_{i \in U} A_i, \bigcup_{j \in Y} B_j)$ of $\widehat{G}$ of size $\sum_{i \in U} w_A(i) + \sum_{j \in Y} w_B(j)$, so $\tau(\widehat{G})$ is at most the right-hand side of (3.3). Conversely, let $(\widehat{U}, \widehat{Y})$ be any vertex cover of $\widehat{G}$ of size $\tau(\widehat{G})$ and define $(U, Y)$ by (3.4). For every edge $(i, j) \in E$, since $w_A(i) \ge 1$ and $w_B(j) \ge 1$, if $i \notin U$ then there is at least one twin $u_0 \in A_i \setminus \widehat{U}$. Since $u_0$ is adjacent in $\widehat{G}$ to every twin $v \in B_j$, all $w_B(j)$ twins in $B_j$ must belong to $\widehat{Y}$ to cover the edges $\{u_0\} \times B_j$; hence $B_j \subseteq \widehat{Y}$ and $j \in Y$. Thus $(U, Y)$ covers $E$, and since $A_i \subseteq \widehat{U}$ for $i \in U$ and $B_j \subseteq \widehat{Y}$ for $j \in Y \setminus \{j : i \in U \text{ for all } (i, j) \in E\}$, we have $\sum_{i \in U} w_A(i) + \sum_{j \in Y} w_B(j) \le |\widehat{U}| + |\widehat{Y}| = \tau(\widehat{G})$. $\blacksquare$

Now follow the exact hypotheses and ordered-limit framework of `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Sections 6.1, 7.1 & 8.7]: we assume that for each fixed block length $L \ge 1$, the $L$-fold shift cover of $f$ satisfies the $L$-fold shift-independence condition of [2, Section 6.1] (verified unconditionally for generic degree-$d$ polynomials and in [2, 3, 5] for $C_3, D_{(1, 5)}, S_d, A_d$), so that as $p \to \infty$ for fixed $L$, the full modular root-count vector $(R(z), \dots, R(z + L - 1))$ converges in distribution to $L$ i.i.d. copies of $R(v)$ with $\mathbb{E}[R(v)] = 1$. We take the ordered limit $p \to \infty$ first for fixed block length $L$, and then $L \to \infty$.

**Theorem 2 (Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue and Fluid-Deficit Conservation Law).** *Let $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ of degree $3 \le d \le D$ satisfy the $L$-fold shift-independence hypothesis with asymptotic root-count distribution $p_r = \mathbb{P}(R(v) = r)$ ($r \in \{0, 1, \dots, d\}$, $\sum_r r p_r = 1$), and let $S_k = (0, 1, \dots, k - 1)$ ($k \ge 2$). For any window $W_n(x_0, y_0)$ of side $n = \lambda p = (a + \theta)p$ ($a = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor \ge 1, \theta \in [0, 1)$):*

*(i) **Reduction to the Unsplit One-Distinguished-Root Multi-Server Queue:** On each strip $m_y \in \{a, a + 1\}$, the $L \to \infty$ limit of the minimum expected residue-level block cover cost $\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}[\min_{U, Y} C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)]$ equals the stationary service rate $\nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)$ of the continuous-state (or rational-grid for $\lambda = p_{\mathrm{num}}/q_{\mathrm{den}} \in \mathbb{Q}$) **Unsplit Multi-Server Queue** on $[0, m_y(k - 1)]$ driven **solely by i.i.d. unsplit root counts $R(v_t) \sim (p_r)$**:*
$$\tilde{q}_{t+1} \;=\; \min\Bigl\{m_y(k - 1),\; \max\bigl(0,\, \tilde{q}_t + (a + \theta)R(v_t) - m_y\bigr)\Bigr\}. \tag{3.5}$$
*Equivalently, for rational $\lambda = a + \theta = p_{\mathrm{num}}/q_{\mathrm{den}}$, multiplying (3.5) by $q_{\mathrm{den}}$ gives the exact finite-state Markov chain on $\{0, 1, \dots, M_y(k - 1)\}$ ($M_y = m_y q_{\mathrm{den}}$) with integer transition*
$$q_{t+1} \;=\; \min\Bigl\{M_y(k - 1),\; \max\bigl(0,\, q_t + p_{\mathrm{num}} R(v_t) - M_y\bigr)\Bigr\}, \qquad \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{1}{q_{\mathrm{den}}}\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\min\bigl(M_y,\, q_t + p_{\mathrm{num}} R(v_t)\bigr)\Bigr]. \tag{3.6}$$

*(ii) **Dual Conservation Identity and Fluid-Plus-Boundary-Deficit Decomposition:** Let $I_k^{(m_y)}(\theta) = (m_y - \tilde{q}_t - \lambda R(v_t))_+$ and $O_k^{(m_y)}(\theta) = (\tilde{q}_t + \lambda R(v_t) - m_y k)_+$ denote the stationary per-step server idleness and buffer overflow of (3.5). Then $\nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)$ satisfies the **Dual Conservation Identity***
$$\nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta) \;=\; m_y - \mathbb{E}\bigl[I_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)\bigr] \;=\; (a + \theta) - \mathbb{E}\bigl[O_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)\bigr], \tag{3.7}$$
*and the normalized two-strip line cover capacity $\gamma_k(a + \theta) := \frac{2[(1 - \theta)\nu_k^{(a)}(\theta) + \theta \nu_k^{(a+1)}(\theta)]}{a + \theta}$ satisfies the **Fluid-Plus-Boundary-Deficit Decomposition***
$$\gamma_k(a + \theta) \;=\; \frac{2\bigl(a + \theta^2\bigr)}{a + \theta} \;-\; \frac{2\Bigl[(1 - \theta)\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_k^{(a)}(\theta)\bigr] \;+\; \theta\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[O_k^{(a+1)}(\theta)\bigr]\Bigr]}{a + \theta}. \tag{3.8}$$

*(iii) **Concavity and $1$-Lipschitz Monotonicity in $\theta$ (Without Multi-Root Coupling):** For every root-count law $R(v)$ with $\mathbb{E}[R] = 1$ and every $m_y \ge 1$, the map $\theta \mapsto \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)$ is concave, monotonically nondecreasing, and $1$-Lipschitz on $[0, 1]$:*
$$0 \;\le\; \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta_2) - \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta_1) \;\le\; \theta_2 - \theta_1 \qquad (0 \le \theta_1 \le \theta_2 \le 1). \tag{3.9}$$
*Consequently, on any subinterval $[\theta_1, \theta_2] \subseteq [0, 1)$, $\gamma_k(a + \theta)$ is bounded above uniformly by the maximum at the two endpoints $\theta \in \{\theta_1, \theta_2\}$ of the linear-fractional upper envelope*
$$\gamma_k(a + \theta) \;\le\; \max_{\theta \in \{\theta_1, \theta_2\}} \frac{2\Bigl[(1 - \theta)\,\nu_k^{(a)}(\theta_2) \;+\; \theta\,\nu_k^{(a+1)}(\theta_2)\Bigr]}{a + \theta} \qquad \text{for all } \theta \in [\theta_1, \theta_2]. \tag{3.10}$$

*Proof.* **(i)–(ii)** Fix $m_y \in \{a, a + 1\}$ and partition the interior of Strip $m_y$ (after pre-covering the $2(k-1)$ seam residue rows straddling the two endpoints of $I_y(y_0)$ at physical cost $(2a+1)(k-1)$) into blocks of $L$ consecutive fibers, separated by $k - 1$ pre-covered boundary residue rows between adjacent blocks (contributing $m_y(k - 1)/(L + k - 1) = O(k/L)$ per residue row). On each $L$-block, let $(U^*(R), Y^*(R))$ be a deterministic residue-level cover minimizing $C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)$ in (3.2) for each $R \in \{0, \dots, d\}^L$. By the one-distinguished-root expansion (3.1) and `no_three_in_line_10` v3 [2, Section 7.1, Eq. (7.1)–(7.2)], as $p \to \infty$ for fixed $L$, the normalized physical line cost on Strip $m_y$ converges to $\frac{1}{L + k - 1}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\min_{U, Y} C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)] + m_y(k - 1)\bigr)$.

For rational $\lambda = p_{\mathrm{num}}/q_{\mathrm{den}}$, multiply $C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)$ by $q_{\mathrm{den}}$ so that fiber $t$ has integer weight $w_A(t) = p_{\mathrm{num}} R_t$ and row $j$ has integer weight $w_B(j) = m_y q_{\mathrm{den}} = M_y$. By Lemma 3.1, $q_{\mathrm{den}} \min_{U, Y} C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)$ equals the maximum matching $\nu(\widehat{G}_L(R))$ of the convex interval bipartite twin-blowup graph $\widehat{G}_L(R)$ in which step $t \in \{0, \dots, L - 1\}$ contributes $p_{\mathrm{num}} R_t$ identical vertical twins with row interval $[t, t + k - 1]$ and each row $r \in \{0, \dots, L + k - 2\}$ contributes $M_y$ horizontal server twins. By Glover's theorem [9], $\nu(\widehat{G}_L(R))$ is achieved by the greedy Earliest-Deadline-First (EDF) algorithm scanning rows $r = 0, \dots, L + k - 2$. At step $t$, $p_{\mathrm{num}} R_t$ new vertical twins with deadline $t + k - 1$ join the active pool $q_t$, row $t$ matches $\min(M_y, q_t + p_{\mathrm{num}} R_t)$ of them, and at most $M_y(k - 1)$ unmatched twins can ever be served by the remaining $k - 1$ rows $t + 1, \dots, t + k - 1$ before expiring, yielding the exact queue recursion (3.6) and its $q_{\mathrm{den}}$-rescaled form (3.5). Letting $L \to \infty$ gives $\lim_{L \to \infty} \frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}[\min_{U, Y} C_{m_y, \lambda}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)] = \nu_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)$ (with boundary-drain contribution $O(k/L) \to 0$).

Writing (3.5) pathwise via $\min(B, \max(0, x)) = x + (-x)_+ - (x - B)_+$ with $B = m_y(k - 1)$ and $x = \tilde{q}_t + \lambda R(v_t) - m_y$ gives
$$\tilde{q}_{t+1} \;=\; \tilde{q}_t + \lambda R(v_t) - m_y + I_k^{(m_y)}(t) - O_k^{(m_y)}(t). \tag{3.11}$$
Taking stationary expectations ($\mathbb{E}[\tilde{q}_{t+1}] = \mathbb{E}[\tilde{q}_t]$ and $\mathbb{E}[\lambda R(v_t)] = \lambda \mathbb{E}[R] = a + \theta$) yields $(a + \theta) - m_y + \mathbb{E}[I_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)] - \mathbb{E}[O_k^{(m_y)}(\theta)] = 0$, proving (3.7) and (3.8).

**(iii)** Fix any block length $L \ge 1$ and any realization $R = (R_0, \dots, R_{L-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^L$. For each fixed vertex cover $(U, Y)$ of $G_L(R)$, the cost $C_{m_y, a+\theta}^{(L)}(R; U, Y) = m_y |Y| + (a + \theta)\sum_{i \in U} R_i$ is an affine function of $\theta \in [0, 1]$ with slope $\sum_{i \in U} R_i \in [0, \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} R_i]$. The pointwise minimum $f_L(\theta; R) := \min_{U, Y} C_{m_y, a+\theta}^{(L)}(R; U, Y)$ of finitely many affine functions with slopes in $[0, \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} R_i]$ is **concave**, monotonically nondecreasing, and $(\sum_{i=0}^{L-1} R_i)$-Lipschitz in $\theta \in [0, 1]$:
$$0 \;\le\; f_L(\theta_2; R) - f_L(\theta_1; R) \;\le\; (\theta_2 - \theta_1)\sum_{i=0}^{L-1} R_i \qquad (0 \le \theta_1 \le \theta_2 \le 1). \tag{3.12}$$
Taking expectations over $R$, dividing by $L$ (using $\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=0}^{L-1} R_i] = \mathbb{E}[R] = 1$), and letting $L \to \infty$ preserves concavity, monotonicity, and $1$-Lipschitz continuity, establishing (3.9) and the linear-fractional envelope bound (3.10) without any multi-root coupling! $\blacksquare$

### 4.1 The Integer-Period ($\theta = 0$) Collapse and a Certified Finite-Prime All-Lines LP Obstruction at $p = 47$

As proved in Theorem 2(i), at exact integer periods $\lambda = a \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 2}$ (where $\theta = 0$), Strip 2 ($I_y(y_0)$) has length $\theta p = 0$, every horizontal and vertical residue has uniform multiplicity $a$, and the Unsplit Multi-Server Queue collapses to $a$ times the single-server $c = 2$ queue: $\gamma_k(a) = 2\nu_k^{(1)}(1) = 2\beta_k$.
