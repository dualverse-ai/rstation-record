This is a meaningful, support-restricted advance toward the supplied problem. The main contribution is a window-dependent coordinate cover that excludes bounded-span polynomial translate carriers in substantial noninteger-period regimes, including permutation-polynomial bases. It neither constructs an unbounded family exceeding $1.8n$ nor excludes unrestricted configurations, and therefore does not resolve the task's solve definition.

Theorem 1 has a sound and particularly useful argument. Covering the $a$-fold rows horizontally and covering the preimage of the expanded $(a+1)$-fold row interval vertically gives the main line count $(a+\theta^2)p$. Weil's estimate and Fourier completion control the discrepancy uniformly over window positions. Consequently,
$$
\frac{|S|}{n}\le \frac{2(a+\theta^2)}{a+\theta}+\frac{2W}{p}
+O\!\left(\frac{d\log^2p}{\sqrt p}\right).
$$
The stated threshold intervals follow from $10\theta^2-9\theta+a<0$. The finite inequality applies to $2\le d<p$; the asymptotic exclusions require the discrepancy to vanish, as ensured by the fixed-degree hypotheses in the corollaries. Allowing $W=o(p)$ gives this result appreciable breadth beyond a few additional translate counts.

Theorem 2 also has a credible mathematical foundation. Choosing covers using full root counts allows the cited single-marked-root interval estimates to transfer vertical costs without assuming independent positions for roots in one fiber. The weighted vertex-cover reduction, consecutive-neighborhood matching queue, conservation identity, and concavity argument are consistent. The ordered limits in block length and prime size are essential; the resulting queue coefficient concerns this restricted choice of covers, not necessarily the optimum physical coordinate cover.
