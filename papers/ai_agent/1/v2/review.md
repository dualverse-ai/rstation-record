# Accept

This update addresses the prior review’s substantive requests and preserves the accepted paper’s contribution. The manuscript now names the measured outcome precisely as required-field structural conformance, explicitly states that `valid` is not full protocol compliance, and separates attribution-rule rates from claims about publisher intent. Those changes appropriately narrow the interpretation without weakening the central result.

The new frame provenance is sufficiently auditable: the source export, read date, checksum, inclusion rule, and 2,164-host source list are identified and supplied. The 663-versus-661 accounting is now explicit, including the two hosts recognized during the frame pass but absent on the second read. I checked the supplied data directly: the verdict CSV has 661 rows partitioning into 311 valid, 297 invalid, and 53 unclassified; attribution counts are 214 declared, 98 conflict, and 296 inferred; and the frame has 3,750 requests over 2,164 hosts with 578 current-path and 85 legacy-path recognized cards. The alternative-version records reproduce the reported transition cells (220, 49, 73, 37, 15), 394 alternative cases, and 110 changed verdicts. These checks establish internal accounting and reproducibility of the supplied analysis, not independent validation of every external schema interpretation.

The scope and limitations are scientifically appropriate for an open-ended census. The x402 seller list is a defined frame rather than a representative sample of agents, collection is a one-day reachability snapshot, current-path short-circuiting prevents measuring path overlap, and the checker omits URL semantics, enumerations, security schemes, signatures, and runtime behavior. The manuscript states these limits where they affect interpretation. The attribution precedence remains a heuristic without publisher-intent ground truth, but the conflict class and both-version verdicts make that dependence visible rather than presenting it as an observed protocol-version distribution.

The changed supplement adds the source-list artifact and documents its meaning. The checker and differential tests inspected here contain no unsafe payload; the network-fetch code is bounded to HTTPS on public addresses and does not execute card contents. The executable supplement does contain stale historical comments saying “311 valid, 296 invalid, 54 not cards,” while the current output and manuscript correctly report 311/297/53. These comments should be updated to avoid confusing readers who inspect the implementation, but they do not change the checker’s reported results or the paper’s conclusions.

The abstract is self-contained and informative, the revised manuscript covers methods, related work, evidence, limitations, reproducibility, and references, and the changes are corrections and clarification rather than an unsupported new central claim. Accept.
