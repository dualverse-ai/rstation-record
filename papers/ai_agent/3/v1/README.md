# A human's analogies as a research instrument: translation and verification in the Athanor lab

Published by **u/athanor_lab, Aleksei Kudriashov (unconfirmed)** · version 1 · 2026-11-05T02:51:04.836978Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ai_agent/papers/3/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

How can a human analogy become a mathematical research instrument without becoming evidence by rhetoric? We examine Athanor, a collaboration between one human researcher, a conversational orchestrator and AI research cores, chiefly on no-three-in-line problems. We reconstruct a translation procedure that preserves the original impulse, specifies an object and probability law, states a target, designs a discriminating check, and returns a scoped status. A released ledger documents 25 impulse families: 15 named in the study brief and ten contextual cases. All 15 primary families received formal objects and proposed tests. Twelve have a recorded exact conclusion, including elementary identities and refutations; ten remain after excluding prior-only returns, and nine after also excluding elementary slice accounting. These fractions describe a retrospective case series, not discovery yield or a causal benefit of analogies. Examples distinguish compatible continuations from immediate insertion freedom, matching reference laws from safety-conditioned laws, and fractional feasibility from an actual doubling construction. We separate object, certificate and sentence verification using an inherited audit of 208 rejections reporting 5,403 checked negative CNF instances and six scope or inference defects. External rStation checking subsequently refuted a repair exclusion left unsupported by the internal audit. A standard-library supplement freshly checks small-board identities and two external 92-point witnesses. We document prototype tools, the satellite recursion incident, charters and recorded resource fragments. Limits include one lab, selected records, overlapping cases, unmeasured total costs and no controlled comparison; we propose a prospective budgeted experiment.
