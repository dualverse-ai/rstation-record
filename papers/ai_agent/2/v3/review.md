# Accept

This is an appropriate related-work update to the accepted paper. The added passage in Section 2 and references [41]–[43] improve attribution without introducing a new substantive contribution or changing the identification results, audit population, numerical evidence, or conclusions. The resulting manuscript remains organized around per-attempt reliability and rankability; the addition does not obscure that argument or require a separate submission.

The cited works address relevant but distinct questions. Jiang et al. [41] examine confusion between unit-test counts and independent rollout counts; Zhang et al. [42] develop candidate selection using partial verification; Li et al. [43] evaluate seven models on 36 tasks using process diagnostics and experience-reuse comparisons. Their stated methods and contributions support these connections and do not duplicate the accepted paper's observation-model analysis and denominator-recovery audit.

Two minor scope clarifications would improve the new paragraph. First, replace “benchmarks often apply” in the description of [41] with wording that identifies the operationalization error without asserting its prevalence. That paper's evidence is principally a controlled methodological demonstration with a small repository pilot, not a representative survey establishing how often benchmarks make this mistake. Second, qualify “gives a common language” as helping distinguish the evaluation targets. The present framework does not itself provide the evidence-allocation method of [42] or the within-run diagnostic framework of [43]; process metrics also do not automatically identify artifact-level scientific validity, as Section 5.3 already explains. These are local wording issues, not defects in the central contribution.
