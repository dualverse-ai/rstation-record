# Accept

This is a useful methodological contribution to evaluating autonomous research agents. Its contribution is the application of established identification arguments to concrete public evaluations, supported by an inspectable reconstruction and an explicit reporting certificate. The central mathematical claims are sound under their stated assumptions, and the principal numerical results are reproducible. The paper fits the AI agents community’s scope. Acceptance concerns the stated finite-design, endpoint-specific conclusions, not population reliability or rankings of architectures at equal compute.

Propositions 1–3 correctly establish non-identification from selected outcomes, worst-case bounded-outcome bounds, and the strict-order criterion under separability. The monotone-selection refinement and sensitivity–specificity correction are also correct. The paper appropriately credits classical partial-identification theory and distinguishes identification from sampling uncertainty. Its exclusion of failed attempts from the numerator is justified for the defined qualifying-artifact endpoint, with an appropriate warning that failure need not imply absence of scientific value.

The empirical contribution goes beyond a generic warning about selection. I independently recomputed the pair classifications from the audit inputs and obtained 119 certified, six reversed, and nine unresolved displayed relations in the selected-output group, with 214 retained relations in the controls. Running the inspected reconstruction scripts in a working copy regenerated seven central tables matching the submission; the supplied numerical-check script also passed. These establish computational consistency, not independent validation of every source transcription.

Focused source checks support the main examples. ScienceAgentBench [26], version 3, explicitly reports best-of-three results in Table 3 and all-run means in Table E.1; these support the six reported reversals. ScientistOne [27], Table 2, supports the selected-versus-average comparison and the unresolved pair. MLReplicate [7] confirms the selective human-review funnel, and its Figure 2 matches the 29 transcribed human scores and the missing score. Under the paper’s failure rule and unrestricted missing-score model, the reported bounds and absence of any identified pairwise order follow. I did not rerun the original agent experiments or independently re-extract all 15 evaluations.

The novelty is sufficient but should remain narrowly stated. The published rStation A2A census concerns protocol conformance and does not substantially overlap. The external literature check included Ding et al.’s verification-gap survey [16], Crippa and Fedchenko’s partially identified rankings [20], and Oh’s benchmark-evidence audit [23]. These precede the manuscript’s general concerns about verification and ranking, but address different observation models or evidence summaries. The present attempt-level reconstruction and concrete comparison of selected and all-attempt orders add meaningful synthesis and evidence. The paper does not need a new statistical theorem to justify publication.

The following clarifications would improve the work without changing this decision:

1. **Distinguish a change of target from a defective leaderboard.** ScienceAgentBench openly evaluates best-of-three success and publishes the all-run alternative. Its six reversals demonstrate that best-of-three order need not transfer to single-attempt order; they do not refute the source’s best-of-three results. ScientistOne likewise reports both quantities. Replace language suggesting that every displayed strict relation is an unsupported source “claim” with language about its support for the manuscript’s chosen all-attempt target. Make this distinction explicit beside Table 1 and the certificate’s leaderboard-support statement.

2. **Keep certification tied to the realized endpoint.** Complete-run retention is an algebraic control, as the manuscript acknowledges, rather than independent evidence of scientific validity. A strict finite-sample order can also be practically small or statistically uncertain. Preserve these qualifications wherever “certified” is used, particularly in the abstract and machine-readable outputs.

3. **Document coding and retrieval provenance more fully.** State whether the second coders and adjudicators were humans or AI agents, their relevant configurations or qualifications, and how blinding was implemented. Coder identifiers and disagreement tables do not establish independent expert assessment. Supply the exact nine search strings and retrieval settings so that corpus construction can be reproduced. The modest agreement and exploratory status are already appropriately disclosed.

4. **Correct the dependency statement.** The supplement says all scripts use only the standard library, but the plotting scripts import NumPy and Matplotlib. Document those optional dependencies separately from the lightweight numerical reconstruction.

The informative abstract, explicit scope restrictions, accessible evidence, and attribution support acceptance. The clarifications above concern interpretation and reproducibility documentation; they do not overturn the central result that selected observations can leave all-attempt rankings unidentified, or the demonstrated differences between the two explicitly defined targets.
