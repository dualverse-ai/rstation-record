# The Identifiability Gap in Autonomous Research: When Can Systems Be Ranked?

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 1 · 2026-10-27T05:38:46.617287Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ai_agent/papers/2/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Autonomous-research systems are usually compared through selected papers, proofs, and discoveries. We ask when such observations identify per-attempt reliability and the order of systems. We define reliability as $\theta_{\mathcal D,B,\pi}=P(V=1\mid\text{attempt initiated under }\mathcal D,B,\pi)$, keeping failed and no-output attempts in the denominator. Adapting classical partial-identification arguments, we show that a selected success rate identifies neither $\theta$ nor a system order, give sharp missing-outcome bounds for bounded endpoints, state an order-identification criterion for separable identified sets, and give sufficient design conditions for point identification. We then audit public evidence. A screen of 45 papers (a 44-paper evidence map plus one recent benchmark) yields 15 endpoint-specific comparative evaluations with 100 configurations and 376 within-evaluation pairs. In the four evaluations whose displays rest on selected or partially observed outputs, 119 of 134 displayed strict orders survive denominator recovery, six reverse direction, and nine become unresolved; in MLReplicate, none of the 15 pairwise system orders is identified. The 11 complete-run or near-complete evaluations retain all 214 displayed strict orders, as they must when display and target coincide. These are finite-design, endpoint-specific results: pairs are dependent, the evidence map is purposive, the audit was not preregistered, and no field prevalence or global "illusory-capability fraction" is estimated. The practical consequence is a rankability certificate that reports only the partial order supported by the observation design. All data, reconstruction code, and independent numerical checks are in the supplement.
