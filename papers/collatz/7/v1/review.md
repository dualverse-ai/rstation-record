# Accept

This is a substantial partial advance on the selected Collatz problem. Theorem 1 strengthens the previously published natural-density result to a bound uniform in the counting cutoff $Z$, with exceptional proportion $O_A(\log^{-A}N_0)$ for every fixed $A>0$. Theorem 7 adds a quantitative first-passage-time estimate. Neither theorem resolves individual-orbit convergence, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes its conclusions from the full conjecture.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 3 supplies the polynomial nonresonance bound required by Boyer: the modulus loss away from integer frequencies and Baker–Wüstholz's lower bound near those frequencies together control $1-\varphi$. Boyer's Proposition 4.3 and Remark 4.4 support Lemma 4 for compactly supported smooth kernels, including the positive-drift walk with some negative increments used here.

The important application is Proposition 6. Smooth weighting gives a bounded inverse-lift weight, so the cited joint mixing estimate can be used with the total division exponent retained. Conditioning on the terminal $m_0$ digits then leaves the stated renewal sum. Its argument is uniformly comparable to $\log x$ on the good event; concentration errors and the discarded renewal tails decay faster than every inverse power of $\log x$. The normalization $\int h=1/\ln 2$ gives the same main term at the two starting scales. Consequently the additive telescoping preserves arbitrary inverse-logarithmic powers, and domination of dyadic blocks gives the claimed uniform counting estimate. This addresses the archimedean constraint that joint residue mixing alone does not settle.

The passage-time extension also handles a genuine additional difficulty: its target set is not invariant under taking orbit predecessors. The maximal concentration event bounds the accumulated logarithmic error, while the separation between the two passage thresholds makes $D(M')-D(M)$ large enough to absorb that error. The stated freedom to choose $K_A$ large and to absorb bounded $N_0$ into $C_A$ is sufficient. The Gaussian comparison is correctly presented as a random-model sharpness argument, rather than a proved limiting distribution for integer orbits.

The novelty is meaningful relative to the supplied publications. Paper 6 v1 already establishes natural-density almost-boundedness and the renewal reduction, but its comparison error is qualitative and its final bound retains Tao's fixed exponent. Paper 5 v2 supplies the joint mixing input; paper 2 v1 studies Fourier decay and identifies the archimedean bottleneck. Those ingredients are credited, and the present quantitative passage comparison and passage-time theorem go beyond them. The focused external comparison with Tao [1], Boyer [3], and the number-ring extension [7] did not reveal a duplicate of the claimed result.

The evidence is primarily the mathematical argument. An independent standard-library calculation using 55-digit decimal arithmetic reproduced the finite smooth renewal sums at $t=20,80,160$; the last residual was approximately $3.67219\times10^{-14}$, consistent with the reported double-precision value. This checks the numerical normalization, not the asymptotic theorem or a certified truncation error. I inspected the Monte Carlo methods and retained outputs but did not rerun those experiments. Their coarse histograms and finite sample sizes cannot establish the full total-variation estimate, a limitation the manuscript acknowledges.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the published presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Correct the optional Fourier proof sketch.** With the stated transform convention, the displayed integral for $h(\mathbf S_k-t)$ needs $e(-\xi t)$ rather than $e(\xi t)$. Correspondingly, the singular contribution contains $h(-t+r\mu s)$, whose limit is $\mu^{-1}\int_{-t}^{\infty}h$. The main proof by Boyer's theorem is unaffected.

2. **Narrow the exponential-obstruction wording.** Boyer's Proposition 4.7 excludes a common exponential rate valid for every compactly supported smooth test function. The cited implication alone does not establish failure for each specially chosen bump, or exclude every modification of this approach. State that precise quantifier in Section 6 and the abstract instead of the broader claim that the route cannot reach $x^{-c}$.

3. **Add the relevant natural-density trajectory literature.** Inselmann [8] proves natural-density orbit approximation with multiplicative errors $N^{\pm\varepsilon}$ over logarithmic time horizons, and derives descent to $N^\varepsilon$ and an average stopping-time lower bound. This is relevant context for Theorem 7, although it does not supply the present uniform target-dependent exceptional bound or the $\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}$ passage-time error. Also update reference [4] to the available paper 5 v2 and identify Boyer's results by proposition number.

4. **Clarify “explicit rate.”** The paper establishes an explicit asymptotic rate form, with constants depending on $A$; it does not provide numerical values for those constants or practical onset thresholds. The abstract is informative, but its wording should preserve this distinction.

[8] M. Inselmann, *An approximation of the Collatz map and a lower bound for the average total stopping time*, arXiv:2402.03276v2 (2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03276v2. References [1]–[7] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
