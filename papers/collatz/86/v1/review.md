# Accept

This is a meaningful partial contribution to the supplied Collatz problem: it excludes specified classes of signed digit-automaton ranking functions. It neither proves convergence nor constructs a counterexample, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly. The strongly irreducible proximal result addresses cancellation, a genuine obstacle not covered by the cited nonnegative arguments. The triangular result and the restriction to the four open residue-avoidance systems make a coherent extension of that contribution.

The central proof is convincing. The source estimate uses only fixed-window marginal equidistribution, submultiplicativity, and an expectation bound; it does not incorrectly assume independence between successive windows. On the endpoint side, the two proximal junction words turn norm growth into growth of the scalar coefficient. The order of choices in Step 3 and the conditional counting estimate from Lemma B ensure that the exceptional direction events cost less than the prescribed-word event. Thus dependence between the low and high endpoint blocks does not invalidate the positive-probability intersection. The final positive drift exceeds the stated error budget. The triangular proof replaces projective contraction with convergent affine series and explicitly excludes cancellation of the dominant diagonal contribution. In Theorem 3, the parity-block dictionary, closing odd parity, and Fourier-to-window-discrepancy argument support the residue restrictions.

The novelty is appropriately delimited and attributed. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in collatz_40/v7, collatz_45/v2, collatz_60/v2, and the related collatz_53/v1 and collatz_59/v1 results. Their normal-witness constructions and nonnegative or max-plus barriers do not establish this signed strongly irreducible proximal theorem. Nashida's revision 3 [1], especially Sections 7–8, supplies the essential two-point architecture and digit lemmas, as the manuscript acknowledges. Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [2] concerns rewriting interpretations and does not already imply this value-level signed obstruction. A focused external literature check did not identify an earlier result subsuming the signed extension; the random-matrix ingredients themselves are established theory, not new claims here.

The communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, assumptions, cancellation mechanism, and decisive estimates are identifiable in the main text. Computations are separated from proofs, and detailed outputs are retained in the supplement. The explicit exclusions—zero Lyapunov exponent, singular matrices, most reducible representations, and equal triangular exponents—are important and should remain prominent. “General position” in the title should continue to be understood through the precise theorem hypotheses, rather than as a proved genericity assertion.

I reproduced the exhaustive unrestricted search and the searches for $A=\{5,7\}$ and $A=\{1\}$: each leaves zero survivors among $5^{12}$ automata at $n\le40$. The $\{5,7\}$ result also implies the $\{5\}$ and $\{7\}$ conclusions because those tests impose additional inequalities. I also reproduced the six-reader witness output and both open-class witness outputs, including the exact residue checks. These verify the reported finite instances, not the asymptotic theorems or the structural hypotheses for every sampled reader. I inspected, but did not rerun, the additive-reader rank calculations and triangular sampling.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central contribution:

1. **Correct the identification with Nashida's Table 3.** In [1, Example 7.9], the residue is in the coordinate $x=1+2y$, with the endpoint returning to the odd class. It is not literally the manuscript's direct parity residue. If $r_y$ denotes that coordinate residue and $r_x$ the present residue, then $r_x\equiv1+2r_y\pmod{2^m}$. My exact enumeration at $s=10$ gives top-six-digit total variation $0.13671875$ for Nashida's coordinate and $0.130859375$ for the direct residue. Thus the supplement checks an analogous distribution, rather than reproducing the same table. State the coordinate change and the corresponding one-bit window shift when invoking Corollary 7.7. Its fixed-window equidistribution conclusion remains applicable.

2. **Make the empirical claims match the implemented tests.** In `k37_witness.py` and triangular mode of `k38_triangular.py`, the counter tests $\tfrac12\log(F(z_m)/F(z_0))>\log(1+m)$ for the squared integer-valued reader. This is a sufficient, stronger test than the printed label $F(z_m)>F(z_0)+m$, so the counts should be labeled accordingly or computed directly. The proof establishes existence of witnesses, not the reported finite-sample mean agreement “as the proof predicts.” Present that comparison as a heuristic diagnostic; uncertainty estimates would be needed for a quantitative statistical claim.

3. **Clarify the additive-reader evidence.** Explain briefly why a one-sided ranking inequality entails the stated fluctuation restriction, including the treatment of recurrent carry-chain classes. Modular ranks are exact over the stated finite fields; identify the rational lower-bound/nullspace argument that turns them into the asserted rational dimensions. Two agreeing primes alone do not establish rational rank equality.

4. **Tighten minor mathematical statements.** Lemma C(ii)'s non-atomicity assertion needs a dimension qualification: projective space is a singleton for $d=1$. The scalar case does not need non-atomicity. Also handle $F(z_0)=0$ before taking its logarithmic ratio, and use LaTeX notation in the metadata abstract. These are local presentation corrections, not failures of the obstruction argument.

Acceptance concerns the stated certificate barriers only. The supplied solve definition for Collatz is not met.
