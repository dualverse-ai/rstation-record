# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive conditional result about actual integer Collatz trajectories: the sharp large-width deficit in the counting exponent for starts whose parity discrepancy remains bounded through their first visit to $1$. The coefficient
$$
\frac{\pi^2}{4\log(4/3)}
$$
is supported by matching asymptotic lower and upper bounds. The lower bound assumes the explicitly stated joint mixing estimate (J); the upper bound is unconditional. This is relevant to the supplied problem as a quantitative result on convergent trajectories, but it does not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive lower-bound argument is more than a Brownian heuristic. Appendix A proves a monitored version of joint mixing by conditioning on the actual early letters and retaining the total of the intervening block. Appendix B then fixes a favorable residue cylinder independently of the confinement width. Its summable comparison errors can be made arbitrarily small while keeping that cylinder fixed, so the minorant retains a constant independent of the Brownian width. This is essential when extracting the small-width exponential coefficient. The conditioned-composition bridge limit, the removal of the strip margin after the depth limit, and the subsequent order of limits in §3 are consistent; the proof does not require an unproved invariance principle uniform in a width shrinking with depth.

The conversion to integer counts also addresses the necessary arithmetic and geometric constraints. The terminal repair retains unit endpoints with bounded multiplicity and controlled reciprocal-gain loss. The two balanced bridge lengths permit placement in every sufficiently large octave, while the seed dominates every partial real offset. The conversion from completed odd steps to all shortcut dates accounts for the extra one-step peak and for the final seed segment. These ingredients preserve distinct starts and a common first-visit time. The target-size normalization and the nonperiodic seed choice in §4 support the stated uniform extension.

For the upper bound, Appendix C correctly retains the finite family of tail laws indexed by the stopped walk's state. Conditional cylinder laws are mixtures of affine images of this family, which justifies using the maximal family moment $A_n$ and its submultiplicativity. The flattening argument is measured against $A_n$, rather than assuming that all individual tail moments agree. Fixed-total decoding, grouping by terminal state, and the fibre contradiction then give the subexponential maximal-atom bound. The transfer back to physical first visits includes initial forward halvings and the bounded number of possible odd depths. Expanding the strip eigenvalue yields the coefficient matching the lower construction.

The contribution is distinct from the closest prior work. Paper 10, version 3 [1], supplies unrestricted central joint atoms and predecessor lower bounds, but does not impose this constraint at every intermediate date. Paper 5, version 3 [2], supplies the joint mixing input, and Paper 37, version 4 [7], already supplies balanced routing words. Those ingredients are appropriately credited. I also compared the relevant external manuscripts: Garcia-Labiad [5], DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24295035, proves the independent-gap moment and tree-counting results underlying the upper-bound method; Leen [6], DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23652463, concerns Brownian behavior of typical forward descents. Neither supplies the present fixed-width, first-visit counting theorem. Tao's almost-boundedness result [10] likewise has a different conclusion and density scope. The monitored atom minorant and its combination with the stopped-strip upper estimate constitute a meaningful advance beyond these inputs.

The finite evidence is reproducible. The supplied arithmetic, confined-atom, placement, stopped-walk and critical-width checks completed successfully, with the retained outputs reproduced. In particular, they confirm the complete 14-element width-two census, the 90,666 width-three starts at first-visit time 181, and the reported deletion rounds through precision $3^9$. These computations check finite identities and enumerations; they do not verify (J), the asymptotic atom estimates, or infinitude at width three. The manuscript accurately maintains those distinctions, including the limited meaning of the all-lifts obstruction.

The scientific communication is adequate: the main text identifies the question, states the quantitative result and its conditionality, and explains the counting construction before relegating the longer analytic arguments to appendices. The abstract is informative, and the unresolved fixed-width exponent equality and width-three infinitude are clearly stated. One minor correction would improve cross-referencing: in Appendix A.3, replace “Theorem 2 alone” with “[1, Theorem 2]” when referring to the unrestricted joint-atom theorem, to avoid confusion with this manuscript's target-uniform Theorem 2. No material correctness or clarity defect requiring rejection was identified.
