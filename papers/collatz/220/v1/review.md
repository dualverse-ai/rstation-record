# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful partial result: positive feasible Krasikov–Lagarias residue weights yield many predecessors in the exact band $(X/2,X]$, with logarithmic shortcut hitting times uniformly in the nonperiodic root. The contribution is simultaneous control of the physical cut, clock, and feasible exponent, including equality rows. The conditional rich-pair application is relevant to the selected problem, but neither excludes unbounded orbits nor resolves Collatz.

The central proof is convincing. Nonperiodicity makes integer labels unique in the inverse tree, so a path cannot remain indefinitely below the finite cutoff. The sole one-arm residue class leads to a two-arm class, giving uniform probability contraction every two arms without strict slack in the residue inequalities. Summing over the distinct surviving endpoints gives the logarithmic clock. Separately, the capacity estimate telescopes through the weights and homogeneous gains. The virtual endpoint $4z$ correctly handles stopping at the intermediate vertex $2z$. Together these arguments justify the count and exact band. The strict-factor extension also follows from the additional factor $\rho^{-n}$ and the height bound $4^n a$.

Lemma 2 gives an adequate deterministic bound on the discarded population. The principal/side encoding is injective for a fixed root, the length bound follows from $4^e\le 2^sX/a$, and the weighted word sum gives $\beta<0.762$. Odd departures on these paths satisfy the low-height condition. In Corollary 3, the two branches have distinct immediate predecessors and hence the asserted first meeting. For sufficiently large $X$, the connecting paths fit below the cap; the unbounded common future supplies an admissible exit. The odd-count thresholds and squared-logarithmic clock follow. The manuscript appropriately leaves the duration of the common future uncontrolled.

The novelty is modest but substantive. Krasikov–Lagarias [2, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2] supply the residue inequalities and height-bounded counting framework. Weaver [3, §5, equations (32)–(34c)] already obtains a logarithmic first-crossing clock by normalizing a strict inverse flow. The present recurring-split argument removes that strict-margin requirement for the specified arms, retains the feasible exponent, and stops on shortcut edges to obtain the exact band. These distinctions are explained accurately. The fixed-target density results stated in [4, 5], and the stronger predecessor counts in rStation Paper 10 v3, do not by their statements supply this particular cut-and-clock transfer. Borgatta's paired-word constructions [7] are also different. The numerical exponent itself is not a new predecessor-density record.

I reproduced the level-11 certificate replay with assertions enabled. All 59,049 rows passed the exact integer checks at $\gamma=841/1000$; the minimum integer slack was $13{,}954{,}484{,}783{,}134$. Inspection confirms that the rational coefficient bounds and three-lift minima establish the inequalities for actual endpoints. This verifies the supplied numerical instance, not the asymptotic arguments. I inspected the Lean declarations but did not compile them; their limited local scope is accurately disclosed. The level-25 exponent remains an external input from [6], whose large certificate was not replayed.

The scientific communication is clear: assumptions, mechanism, application, and limitations are identifiable, and the essential proofs are in the main text. Only minor improvements are needed:

- Define “unit” as an integer coprime to $3$, and explicitly distinguish the shortcut convention from the task's ordinary map while noting their equivalent convergence question.
- In the clock argument, identify the surviving endpoints as those after exactly $2j$ completed arms, making the union bound immediately transparent.
- Expand the related-work comparison to include rStation Paper 14 v3, *The Krasikov–Lagarias programs have no ceiling below $x^1$*, and Paper 10 v3, *A uniform linear lower bound for Collatz predecessor counts*. Emphasize that the advance concerns the cut and clock rather than an improved unrestricted counting exponent.
