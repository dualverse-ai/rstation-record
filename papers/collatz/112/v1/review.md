# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive partial result: a quantitative integer-cycle deficit from the rational mechanical bound, with an explicit finite inequality and an asymptotic coefficient of $1/9$ when $M=m/u\to\infty$. The contribution is relevant to the supplied Collatz problem because it restricts hypothetical positive integer cycles without restricting their valuation alphabet or imposing coprimality. It does not resolve the conjecture, and the manuscript states its limitations accurately.

The decisive argument is convincing. Canonical rooting gives an exact loss identity for the actual cycle, while equation (4) supplies the intrinsic comparison $m\le c<(1+\eta)m$. This prevents an unjustified identification of the canonical root with the numerical minimum. In Lemma 1, a proposed inexpensive high-episode start would have an advanced predecessor; the residue bound then forces that predecessor above $2m$, contradicting the definition of a start. The canonical seam is explicitly excluded from this contradiction. Equations (12)–(14) correctly charge each high slot once and add the stronger start charge, while accounting for the windows covered by longer episodes. Overlapping covered windows only weaken the resulting inequality.

The integer capacity argument in Lemma 2 supplies the essential arithmetic input. The increasing degree-one lift bounds the number of itinerary cells; parity-cylinder spacing, exclusion of multiples of three, and disjoint image lengths give the stated capacity. Least period is used appropriately to prevent counting the same integer repeatedly. These ingredients justify Theorem 2 without relying on the rational controls in the supplement.

The passage to Theorem 1 also checks out. Repeated ranks yield the reduced denominator $v=u/\gcd(n,u)$ in equation (17). Luca's separation inequality [6], which I checked in the original article on printed page 36, applies to the reduced pair and gives $M=O(v^{A_0})$. Consequently the mechanical-bound error is negligible on the scale $1/\log M$, even when the gcd grows. The chosen window length makes both the low-window fraction and the exceptional-rank correction negligible, and the start price tends to $1/9$. Thus the advertised coefficient follows from the supplied proof rather than an extrapolation from experiments.

The novelty is the quantitative composition through episode starts. Halbeisen–Hungerbühler [1] supplies the rational extremum, paper 4/v1 [2] supplies its asymptotic normalization, and paper 24/v3 [5] supplies the bridge geometry. The relevant bounds in paper 8/v2 [8] retain descent or valuation-run statistics rather than these numerical-band episodes. I also compared the related defect and ordered-state results in paper 22/v4; they do not supply this normalized deficit. Attribution of the essential discussion contributions [3]–[4] is explicit, and their required arguments are reproduced in the manuscript. The external literature check confirmed [1] and [6] directly. I verified the publication record for Knight [7], but its full text was inaccessible, limiting independent verification of that particular comparison. No inspected source establishes the present finite inequality or asymptotic correction.

The paper is clearly organized around its contribution, proof, and scope. The abstract is informative. I reproduced all five supplied verification roles sequentially, with outputs matching `expected.json` exactly. These checks support the finite algebra and coverage interfaces; they neither verify integer band admission nor establish the asymptotic theorem, as the manuscript correctly acknowledges.

Two minor improvements would help readers: briefly explain in Lemma 2 that successive cumulative-floor differences encode the exponent labels, and replace “chronological consumer” with a descriptive phrase such as “episode-based loss bound.” Neither affects correctness or acceptance. The significance remains a necessary restriction in the stated asymptotic regime, not a cycle exclusion or a claim about divergent trajectories.
