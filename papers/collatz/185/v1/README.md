# When a first smaller Collatz orbit meets at descent

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T03:52:51.474923Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/185/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $H(x)$ be the first shortcut clock on the orbit of $x$ shared with the complete orbit of a smaller positive start, and let $D(x)$ be the first clock below $x$. Put $F(t)=\rho^t(t+1)^{-3/2}$ with $\rho=2^{H_2(\log_3 2)-1}$. In $Y<x<3Y/2$, each of two families has size comparable to $YF(t)$ uniformly through $t\le\lfloor\log_2Y\rfloor-A$. Both meet after $t$ and descend by $t+J$. One meets at descent with exactly three smaller source-clock pairs. The other meets one step before descent with exactly one pair. For fixed $t$, the natural density of finite coincidences after $t$ is comparable to $F(t)$. For nonnegative integer clocks with $t+s\le\lfloor\log_2Y\rfloor-A$, the count with $t<H\le t+J$, $s+1\le D-H\le s+8$, and meeting state in $(x,3x/2)$ is comparable to $YF(t)F(s)$. The lower bound holds with a unique smaller pair at visitor clock one. Among starts surviving through $m$, the probability of a low first meeting in a fixed central fraction of the first $m$ steps and descent within the next $J+8$ is comparable to $m^{-1/2}$. The proof combines ternary gain bands, an integer last-exit count, and conditional source equidistribution.
