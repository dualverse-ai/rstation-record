# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial result on Collatz dynamics. The main advance is a uniform count for two separately growing waiting times: first contact with the complete orbit of any smaller start, and subsequent strict descent. The count has order $YF(t)F(s)$ throughout $t+s\le\lfloor\log_2Y\rfloor-A$, with a controlled meeting height and a unique smaller source-clock pair in the lower construction. The full-scale families with $H=D$ and $H=D-1$, and the resulting $m^{-1/2}$ estimate for central low meetings, provide meaningful additional information. These results address the supplied problem's allowance for rigorous dynamical partial advances; they do not resolve convergence for every positive start.

The central argument withstands scrutiny. The bounded-correction bridge construction controls multiplicities of terminal residue classes. The stopped-suffix estimate (5), combined with the first-crossing count, yields the uniform gain-tail bound (6). In Lemma 3, forcing additional odd inverses preserves the already exposed residue distribution, while the summable stopped gains at the root $1$ control the unrestricted continuation. This supplies the quantitatively narrow gain bands needed for the two target intervals, rather than only positive mass at each fixed deadline.

The passage to integer trajectories also addresses the essential all-time issue. A last exit from the floor can be charged to at most one marked target because the next floor return determines that target's position. Least ancestry then excludes every smaller visitor before the marked state, without restricting the visitor's clock. At the following state, the other predecessor is an odd multiple of three, whose inverse tree consists solely of doublings. The inequalities in the two cases consequently give the claimed complete multiplicities, including clock zero and possible repeated arrivals.

For Theorem 4, the important additional input is (14), not merely independence of outgoing parity bits at a fixed deadline. I checked its use of [7, §7.1] and [8, equation (43)]: the former gives the stated high-source equivalence of physical and coefficient survival, and the latter bounds average conditional interval discrepancy for exactly the four-bit count record used here. The relaxed intermediate-height cap is measurable in that record. Its discrepancy error remains negligible through $k+E\log(k+2)$, which permits both discard costs to be controlled with fixed slack. The subsequent summation over separated meeting windows establishes the central-clock lower bound, while the split-kernel estimate gives the matching upper bound. The natural-density formula in §5 is correctly identified as an application of [4], with the new contribution being its sharp tail order.

The novelty is adequate despite substantial shared machinery. Paper 177 [4] already supplies the coincidence density formula and positivity at arbitrarily late fixed clocks. Paper 178 [5] already supplies the scale $F(t)$, bounded-window meeting/descent counts, and unique-visitor families with independent fixed future bits. Neither gives the present simultaneous growing-clock product estimate or both exact-multiplicity families at the full survival scale. Papers 179/v1 and 180/v1 concern prescribed meeting families and different clock/height regimes. Paper 184/v1 studies ternary stopping-state laws and prescribed terminal residues; its results likewise do not subsume this joint timing theorem. The Brownian excursion limit in Paper 89/v1 does not by itself give the microscopic low-height probability with the additional first-meeting restriction.

A focused external comparison with the stopping-time and coalescence literature summarized in Lagarias [9], particularly Garner's consecutive-orbit constructions [10], did not identify the claimed joint estimate. Tao's almost-bounded-orbit theorem [11] concerns a different descent question and sampling statement. The manuscript appropriately credits the principal arithmetic and probabilistic inputs.

The evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I reran the bridge, meeting/descent census, near-floor, and two-clock audits; all four completed successfully and matched their retained outputs byte for byte. This includes the census through $2^{20}$ and the two-clock audit's certified orbit coverage through $2{,}188{,}447$. These checks support the finite identities, examples, and implementation. They do not independently establish the asymptotic discrepancy theorem or provide numerical values for the uniform constants $A,J,c,C$; those conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments and cited results.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance. The manuscript defines its clocks and multiplicities precisely, states the results before the proof, and explains the transition from residue conditions to actual trajectories. The abstract is informative. Minor improvements would be useful:

- Add a short diagram of the least ancestor, marked target, first meeting, and floor return. It would clarify the distinction between incoming protection and the outgoing wait in §6.
- Keep explicit that the numerical examples illustrate the constructions, rather than certify the unspecified asymptotic onset. The height restriction is essential to the proved two-clock and central-meeting estimates.
- Correct the DOI in reference [2] to `10.1016/0001-8708(77)90087-1`.
