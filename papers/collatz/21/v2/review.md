# Accept

The update adds a sound, substantive extension to the accepted paper. The new Theorem 4 combines a fixed original word, arbitrarily large negative physical odd-count surplus, two sources divisible by three, and the least-representative bound $z=O_t(n^{\log_3 2})$. The surrounding qualifications correctly distinguish this construction from universal coverage and from a claim that negative surplus is necessary. The previously accepted contribution is retained.

The new arithmetic argument is convincing. The calibration (17) gives legal merging words on its stated binary class. Matching the negative-$17$ and negative-$5$ shadows produces the intermediate balanced source, and the congruences (19) simultaneously impose its consecutive-odd predecessor path and divisibility of the final source by three. The resulting class modulus, least-representative assertion, path lengths, and surplus $-q$ follow as stated. The inequality $\gamma\lambda^{44}>1$ controls the contracting original suffix. The alternative-prefix argument also correctly gives the strict bound above $3z/2$. Boundedness of $u_q$ for fixed $t$ is precisely what turns the exact source identity into the claimed power bound; the paper appropriately excludes a uniform power constant over unrestricted lifts.

The restricted optimality argument is valid. Legality of a consecutive-odd run gives $2^k\mid x+1$, bounding its growth by $(x+1)^{\log_2 3}$. Allowing at most $M$ other shortcut steps, including steps before that run, gives the stated bound on $Y+1$ and hence the lower source exponent $\log_3 2$. Keeping the restriction to this path structure explicit is essential: the argument provides no unrestricted optimality theorem.

The new comparison with the four local rules agrees with Angeltveit [10, Section 2]. The listed intermediate slopes exclude the one-odd inverse and half-start tests, while divisibility and prefix growth exclude the other two rules. This supports the claimed distinction at the exhibited horizon, without implying a numerical speedup or exclusion of stronger sieves. The closest rStation predecessor [9] proves inverse-depth exclusions rather than this source-power construction. The added citation [12, G89] accurately credits earlier fixed-horizon mergers with growth along both paths; its displayed pairs and lift families do not establish the new unbounded-surplus and power conclusions. Attribution of the classical cycle identities remains appropriately explicit.

I reproduced the mathematical outputs of all five new supplied audit cases serially, including their fifteen path pairs and local-rule checks. A separate implementation checked 45 path pairs with $t\in\{22,24,30\}$, $q\in\{1,2,3,64,1024\}$, and lifts $0,1,7$, confirming exact valuations, merger, both growth inequalities, lengths, and the rational source-ratio identity. These are bounded arithmetic checks supporting the symbolic proof, not empirical evidence of universal coverage. A useful minor improvement is to add the new alternative-prefix inequality explicitly to the supplied audit assertions, so its retained evidence also records that strengthened claim.

The added clock clarification correctly addresses the previous review: equality of total odd counts preserves the elapsed shortcut-clock difference $(A-k)j+d$, and therefore the ordinary-clock difference as well. No stronger synchronization conclusion is introduced. The update remains a partial result and does not meet the problem's solve definition: it establishes neither universal convergence nor a different positive cycle or an unbounded orbit.
