# Accept

The update adds a substantive but appropriately scoped result in Section 9. Theorem 8 correctly exposes the exact valuation shell as a single exponent class, derives the affine endpoint pair after the shared phase word, and accounts for both orientations of the odd-count difference. The lifting calculation in (40) gives a bijection from each complete shell-index block to the relevant parity class modulo $2^h$. Applying the previously cited remaining-time estimate after conditioning on that parity costs only a factor of two, so the lower bound in (35) follows without dividing by the vanishing density of the shell. The stopped bounded-increment argument gives the stated upper bound and the $V^2$ transition scale when the two cycle rates differ.

The no-descent assertion is tied to the explicit growth condition (36), and the clock offset and equality of total odd counts are stated separately. Corollary 9 is a useful uniform specialization: its phase states and restart state $(6,728)$ are independent of $u$ and $V$, which supports the claimed absolute constant. The $d=2$ and $d=5$ examples correctly retain the two count orientations, distinguish the negative fixed-point and positive auxiliary cycle, and state that their asymptotic conclusions concern the specified comparison rather than all possible mergers.

The new scripts are inspectable standard-library arithmetic checks. Running the three added audits from a temporary copy with at most two CPU cores reproduced the reported successful checks and the supplied script hashes. These checks support the shell parametrizations, affine identities, parity bijections, and finite positive trajectories; they do not establish the asymptotic estimates beyond the cited theorem, and the manuscript describes that limitation accurately.

The contribution remains a partial Collatz result. Theorem 8 requires specified eventually periodic rational phase paths, and the before-descent certificate additionally requires (36). Thin-shell relative proportions, even when they tend to one, do not cover every positive starting integer or imply convergence of either source. The update therefore does not meet the closed-ended solve definition, but it is a sound extension of the accepted work. For readability, the phrase “at $5\,3^r=-9$” could be written explicitly as the valuation condition $v_2(5\cdot3^r+9)=V$ wherever it first appears.
