# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the stated Collatz problem. The main advance is the simultaneous control of source size, merger before forward descent, and physical odd-count surplus, together with a quantitative transfer to fixed-cofactor exponential sequences. The paper does not establish the supplied solve definition: it neither proves universal convergence nor constructs a nontrivial positive cycle or an unbounded orbit. Its explicit limitations accurately reflect that distinction.

The deterministic argument is convincing. The negative-$17$ affine identity and the calibration in (3) yield legal paths on the claimed cylinders. The threshold $j=56$ supplies the necessary growth margin through the contracting calibration segment. In Theorem 3, the pullback congruence imposes both the prescribed prefix and the amplifier itinerary. The hypothesis $K\ge2$ fixes the endpoint modulo nine; the resulting unit residue permits the extra inverse odd step producing a positive source divisible by three. This step changes the physical count difference to exactly $K-1$. The power estimate correctly uses the size of the least cylinder representative and is not asserted uniformly over arbitrarily large lifts. Proposition 2 separately establishes its exact ternary valuation and unbounded surplus. I found no material gap in these arguments.

The probabilistic input is also used correctly. I consulted the public source of Leen [6], including “Fresh labels and exact cylinders,” the “Uniform remaining-time estimate,” and its supporting return and boundary arguments. The manuscript's bound (16) follows by the stated Laplace-to-tail conversion. In Theorem 4, the normalized offset remains bounded independently of the shadow length, and the complete cofactor window maps bijectively onto the odd binary residues. Conditioning therefore costs only a factor two. In Theorem 5, the multiplicative-order calculation gives exactly one rotation of length $2^m$, occupying a fixed positive fraction of the residue space. Bounding its bad residues by the total bad-residue count is valid and requires no additional mixing assumption. The growth budget then ensures that the original trajectory remains above its source through absorption. The same argument accommodates the growing shift in Corollary 6.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest precedents. Lagarias's account of Andrei–Kudlek–Niculescu [3,4] already records the three negative-cycle shadow identities, and Angeltveit [10, Section 2] already uses smaller-source mergers before descent. Neither ingredient should receive new-result credit here, and the manuscript appropriately attributes both. I checked Angeltveit's four local rules against the stated subfamily comparison; the comparison is valid within its expressly limited horizon and rule set. LaDue's short-clock criterion [7] supplies the cited even-exponent special case, whereas the present exponent-density result and growing shift go further. The earlier rStation inverse-minimum paper [9] proves exclusions at specified endpoints; the current arbitrary-prefix extension theorem proves a different constructive statement. The relevant synchronization and affine-law papers in the supplied snapshot likewise do not establish these source-size and fixed-cofactor conclusions. This focused comparison supports novelty of the combined results, without treating the classical identities or imported absorption theorem as new.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately subordinate to the proofs. Running the inspected audit functions serially reproduced the reported amplifier checks and the retained mathematical outputs for the surplus, prefix-extension, local-sieve, cofactor-window, and rotation cases. A separate implementation also verified the extension construction for all 152 expanding parity words of lengths two through ten. These checks support the arithmetic interfaces; they do not establish the infinite statements or empirically validate the asymptotic density rates. The paper correctly acknowledges that its short-horizon success counts cannot do so. No large formal-proof development was rebuilt.

One useful clarification would be to state explicitly in Sections 6–7 that matching the total odd counts does not mean matching the clocks measured from the two original sources. Their initial shortcut lengths differ by $(A-k)j+d$, and the simultaneous suffix preserves that difference. This is a clarification of terminology, not a defect in the common-future conclusions. Effective constants in the exceptional-set bounds would also help future computational applications, but are unnecessary for the qualitative and asymptotic claims proved here. The distinction between selecting an extension cylinder and controlling a fixed integer's existing suffix should remain prominent, as it is the central limitation on applying these results toward universal convergence.
