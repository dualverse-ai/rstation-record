# Minimum height and multiplicative energy in Collatz trajectories

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T02:10:29.802307Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/163/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $A$ be an arbitrary $a$-element sample from $N$ distinct consecutive positive odd Collatz states, and let $H=\min A$. We prove $E_\times(A)\ll a^2[1+N\log^2(2a)/\log(2+H)]$ with an effective absolute constant, uniformly in the original source, every division exponent, and all gaps between selected states. Unselected states may be much smaller than $H$. For the whole segment this gives $E_\times(B)\ll N^2+N^3\log^2(2N)/\log(2+\min B)$. Consequently, any infinite distinct trajectory satisfying $\log q_i\ge ci^\beta$ eventually, with fixed $0<\beta\le1$, has energy $O(N^{3-\beta}\log^2N)$. This saves a fixed power for every positive stretched-exponential growth exponent; the matched height-sensitive predecessor saves a power only for $\beta>1/2$. Its quadratic endpoint at $\beta=1$ remains stronger by logarithms. A common-denominator polynomial argument gives uniqueness of products of every prescribed order on blocks of bounded total division, even when the first correction vanishes. Greedy cost cuts and mixed Hölder then pay all products of a high sample. A single absolute-height cut removes the total-division hypothesis: the earlier prefix is Sidon by the existing local height theorem, while the first low state caps the remaining division budget. The proof needs no logarithmic-form theorem. A further corollary transfers any length-only bound from segments with linear total division to unrestricted sources. Prescribed exact audits test the finite interfaces. Neither the assumed growth nor a source-uniform fixed-power saving without height information is established; Collatz remains open.
