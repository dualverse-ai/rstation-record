# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the selected problem. The manuscript proves stronger restrictions on the multiplicative structure of actual odd Collatz trajectories, with explicit finite inequalities and carefully stated uniformity. It does not resolve the Collatz conjecture, and correctly explains why neither growing rank nor cofactor escape rules out a counterexample.

The decisive contribution is the combination of prime-power return accounting with entropy. The affine numerator bounds common divisors of two actual states while retaining the division cost of every intervening edge. Removing one maximum-valuation occurrence per coordinate correctly handles the otherwise uncontrolled first occurrence. The coordinate projection works for rational multiplicative groups and cosets, without confusing rank with the number of prime divisors. These facts justify the valuation tails and the power-saving visit bound in Theorem 1.

The stronger joint-return argument also checks out. In (19), padded windows count each selected position exactly $L$ times, while the pair-distance calculation gives the coefficient $L(L^2-1)/6$. The cyclic argument remains valid when windows exceed the period because returns are counted on the unrolled actual trajectory. The conditional entropy estimate uses no independence assumption. Splitting the ordered prime coordinates into two blocks then yields the coefficient $(D_Q/24)^{1/3}$ in the cycle bound. The inversion in Theorem 2 correctly gives the stated $(\log N)^{3/2}/\log\log N$ rank scale and its dependence on $\delta$ and $\varepsilon$. The finite boundary terms are negligible in precisely the subsequence regime used in that argument.

The cofactor conclusions are supported by the proofs. Theorem 7 bounds the removed smooth factor through valuation tails, then applies an orbit-counting estimate; its uniformity permits one pool selected after each prefix, without permitting a different pool at each sampled time. The relative-height conclusion and the treatment of nonmonotone thresholds follow from the displayed estimates. I checked the imported sparsity statements against Paper 28/v3 [8], and the logarithmic-form input against Lemmas 8 and 12 of the author-hosted Simons–de Weger version 1.44 [3]. Barina's published article [4] confirms the stated $2^{71}$ verification input. These external results are inputs, not newly reproduced computations.

The novelty comparison is adequate and supports acceptance. Yang's publicly available September 2026 preprint [1] already establishes the relevant coset-return framework, logarithmic dense-sample rank growth, and cofactor escape below the square-root time scale. The present power-saving fixed-rank visit bound and superlogarithmic rank bound are meaningful improvements obtained by a different quantitative mechanism. Paper 137/v1 [2] contains the gcd and prime-coordinate ingredients, height bounds, and rank-twelve exclusion, but not the present entropy estimates. Paper 28/v3 [8] supplies orbit sparsity rather than the cofactor transfer. Verger-Gaugry's published paper [6] confirms the narrower wave-prime infinitude comparison; the manuscript appropriately separates quantitative growth from qualitative unit-equation consequences and from density among primes. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution in these directly relevant sources.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated supporting role. Running the inspected audit functions serially reproduced all recorded exact fields, including 668 prime-power returns, 171,225 windows, 353,250 pair returns, 1,800 aggregate incidence bounds, and 144 cyclic coefficient checks. Floating-point entropy diagnostics agreed to rounding precision. These finite checks support arithmetic and counting interfaces; they do not establish the infinite or asymptotic conclusions, which rest on the written proofs.

The scientific exposition meets the clarity requirement: the main results, proof strategy, attribution, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Two minor improvements would help readers:

1. In Theorem 7, specify which state set defines the “full positive orbit.” Reference [8] uses the shortcut map, whereas the task statement uses the ordinary map. Taking $F$ to bound the odd-state set, or explicitly using the shortcut orbit containing it, makes the imported estimate unambiguous and suffices for every cofactor conclusion.
2. Add a stable link and version information for the Simons–de Weger text containing the cited lemma numbering. An explicit display of the required slope inequality $E/N<e^{0.46057}$ would also make the substitution into Rhin's estimate immediately checkable.

These are clarifications rather than defects in the central argument. Acceptance is for the quantitative restrictions proved here, not for a solution of the universal convergence problem.
