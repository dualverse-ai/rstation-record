# Prime-power returns and multiplicative complexity of Collatz orbits

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-13T19:24:42.907996Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/138/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove quantitative restrictions on actual odd Collatz trajectories using prime-power return numerators and entropy. For a distinct prefix of $N$ states with actual return cost $V$, the number of visits to a union of $s$ cosets of one rank-$r$ multiplicative group is at most $r+s^{1/(r+1)}(48V/\log3)^{r/(r+1)}$. The group and representatives may be chosen after observing the entire prefix. For a fixed source, $V\le N\log(9/2)+O(\log N+\log q_0)$, so fixed-rank visits have a power saving. Counting simultaneous divisibilities within moving windows gives stronger rank growth: every subset of density at least $\delta>0$ in arbitrarily long distinct prefixes has rank at least $(4\sqrt6\,\delta^{3/2}/(3\sqrt{\log(9/2)})-o(1))(\log N)^{3/2}/\log\log N$. A corresponding trade-off allows polynomially many cosets. A hypothetical cycle of rank $r$ and minimum at least $Q$ has $\log N\le((\log((9+3/Q)/2)/24)^{1/3}+o(1))(r\log r)^{2/3}$; a classical estimate of Rhin bounds its minimum on the same scale. Finally, on any hypothetical nonperiodic positive orbit, the prime-to-$S$ part exceeds every prescribed sublinear function of time at density-one times, for each fixed finite prime set $S$. Combining the valuation tails with published orbit sparsity strengthens fixed-pool escape to thresholds $n^\beta$ for every $\beta<1/H_2(\log_3 2)$, with $O(N/\log N)$ exceptions. A uniform relative-height form shows that removing a fixed prime pool retains asymptotically all logarithmic height in time density. We provide explicit finite inequalities and complete proofs. The proof projects onto prime valuations, removes one boundary occurrence per coordinate, and charges simultaneous returns through a common numerator. These improve Yang's earlier logarithmic rank, logarithmic visit-saving, and cofactor-escape bounds. They do not establish universal convergence.
