# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem: exclusions governed by the number of repeated blocks, uniformly over their internal parameters. The distinction matters because a bounded number of these blocks can contain arbitrarily many ascents and classical circuits. The mixed $\{1,2\}$ exclusion, the uniform-$r$ exclusion including its infinite tail, and the unrestricted-valuation result are supported by the arguments and certificates supplied. None establishes universal convergence, excludes every nontrivial cycle, or treats divergent trajectories; the manuscript states these limits appropriately.

The central proof is sound on review. In Section 4, the modulo-$6$ coordinates remain valid when $v_s=0$, the centered identities give the required divisibilities, and the bound on $r_su_s$ avoids dependence on the climb length. The backward recurrence retains the negative endpoint term with the correct sign. Lemma 3.2 and the maximization over arc partitions then apply to actual cyclic block entries. The certificate's monotone envelopes cover smaller block counts, while its remaining cases explicitly check each count; checking only the largest count with a nonmonotone bound would not have sufficed.

Section 5 supplies an actual argument for all large $r$. The minimum-entry divisibility yields a lower bound linear in $r$ for $\ln Q_1$, and the exact ratio monotonicity supports the stated uniform linear envelope for $K_{\mathrm{odd}}$. Both continued-fraction and Rhin regimes have endpoint inequalities and derivative bounds extending the exclusions throughout their infinite domains. Section 6 also handles the potentially troublesome contracting units: the fixed-point estimate and integrality force decrease, locate the cycle minimum at a block boundary, and allow the forward propagation argument to cover arbitrary terminal valuations and descent words. The bounded computations are not being used as evidence for a universal impossibility claim without an analytic tail.

I inspected the interval arithmetic and certificate implementation and reran Suite A, all five mixed-polymer floor certificates, both analytical-tail certificates, the unrestricted-valuation certificates, and every finite-$r$ check for $1\le r\le42$ at the $2^{74}$ floor. These completed successfully. The recomputation obtained 2,600 continued-fraction terms, 1,462 contiguous denominator ranges, the reported tail margins, and the reported unrestricted constants. This is reproduction of the submitted implementation, not an independently implemented certificate engine. I did not rerun the four additional finite-$r$ benchmark grids or the previously published large searches establishing the external floors. Those dependencies should remain explicit; the self-contained replay and the analytical tails provide separate, weaker or more restricted conclusions.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. The periodic-stretch estimates in [8] retain template-length dependence; [9] permits general dictionaries but retains a dictionary budget; and [11,12] provide stronger exclusions for the fixed dimer specialization. The present removal of internal-length dependence for the specified block grammar is a meaningful extension, particularly with unrestricted valuations. The classical literature checked, including Hercher [4] and the Simons–de Weger treatment of Rhin's estimate [3], does not subsume that extension. However, several comparisons and citations need correction:

1. **Separate classical circuits from Syracuse ascent runs.** Section 1 and Table 1 incorrectly identify the classical circuit count with $\sum_s u_s$. In the submitted decomposition, the classical shortcut-map circuit count is
   $$
   m=\#\{i:e_i\ge2\}=\sum_s(u_s+v_s),
   $$
   where $v_s$ is the length of the pure-descent word. The number of maximal nonempty valuation-$1$ runs is $\sum_su_s$, which is the parameter used in the run exclusions of [7]. For example, $1^r2\,2^v$ has one such ascent run but $v+1$ classical circuits. Correct the terminology and mapping throughout the comparison. This does not change the new block definitions or their proofs, and the separation from classical bounds survives.

2. **State the strongest cited dictionary theorem accurately.** Table 1 says [9] degrades exponentially in the dictionary budget $B$. Its Theorem 9 instead gives an absolute effective constant $K_*$ and
   $$
   t+1\le \exp\!\left(K_*\frac{k+1}{\log(k+2)}\right)
   [\log H(a/b)+B+1].
   $$
   Thus the displayed dependence on $B$ is linear, with subexponential dependence on the piece count. The appropriate distinction is that this estimate still charges unbounded template complexity and does not itself give the present explicit exclusions uniformly in the internal parameters. Also, a dictionary counts distinct templates, so $B\ge\sum_s(r_s+d_s)$ is not generally valid when a template recurs in different blocks.

3. **Update the verification reference and classical comparison.** The published source for Barina's $2^{71}$ verification is *Improved verification limit for the convergence of the Collatz conjecture* (2025), DOI [10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0), rather than the 2021 article currently listed as [6]. The supplied snapshot also contains `collatz_20` v1, whose Theorems 3–4 exclude classical $m=94,95$ cycles using that floor. Consequently, the discussion treating $m\ge94$ as the remaining classical range is outdated. These corrections do not alter the new polymer thresholds.

4. **Scope the methodological language precisely.** Section 2's claim to bypass linear forms on $3^{r+1}-2^{r+d}$ “completely” should refer specifically to the expanding-block propagation estimate. Proposition 6.2 uses continued fractions and Rhin to control the contracting fixed point. Likewise, the all-$r$ theorem uses a published least-element floor in its finite-$r$ portion; only the stated large-$r$ tails dispense with an external floor.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the block grammar, three principal results, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations are identifiable, and the proof sections develop the argument rather than recount experiments. The numerical summary and figure help distinguish the scopes. The abstract would be more effective with fewer implementation constants and benchmark floors, but it is informative. The corrections above improve attribution and interpretation without requiring reconstruction of the central argument or additional evidence for its principal conclusions.
