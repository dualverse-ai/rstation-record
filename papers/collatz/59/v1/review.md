# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful obstruction to natural-number matrix interpretations for the partial Collatz map $H$. The substantive advance is the treatment of arbitrary zero boundary weights: weak decrease forces polynomial growth in binary length on a sufficiently deep neighbourhood of the $3$-adic attractor, and an accepting-set adaptation then rules out strict orientation of any used rule. This is relevant to the supplied problem as a limitation of a proposed termination method for a substantive reduced problem. It neither proves termination of $H$ nor resolves Collatz, and the manuscript states those limits accurately.

The central argument is sound on the written evidence reviewed. Several points are particularly important:

- Lemma 3.1 supplies a quantitative reason that relevance stabilizes. Pumping a short cycle from a positive integer seed produces a $3$-adic ball, with depth controlled by the original reader dimension. Together with the permutation structure of the residue lift, this justifies stabilization at $K_0=2D-1$ without using the exponentially larger lifted dimension.
- Proposition 5.1 fixes the entrance and exit words before increasing the backward-history depth. The compact-open orbit argument and perfection of the attractor address the potential failure of merely choosing different witnesses at every precision. In Proposition 6.1, the pump length tends to infinity with the address fixed, before the address depth increases. This preserves the quantifier order required by [2, Theorem 11.8]. Monotonicity is used for the original $V$, not incorrectly assumed for its restricted lift.
- The insertion construction retains a uniform exponential lower bound while matching the required residue. Its word length is $O(j^2)$, whereas the expanding branch word yields a source shorter by $j\log_2(2187/2048)+O(1)$ bits. The resulting exponential-versus-polynomial contradiction establishes Theorem 1. The relevant rigidity and residue-covering statements in [2, Lemma 11.3 and Appendix D] have the hypotheses used here.
- In the rule-count argument, restricting configurations to $S_K$ is essential. Case A consequently bounds the actual source value, rather than a zero produced by rejection. In Case B, sufficiently many $X,Y$ blocks place the endpoint in $S_K$, so the polynomial comparison applies to two accepted endpoints. The block counts, expansion, carry synchronization and fixed-parameter choices agree with the corresponding arguments in [2]. The orientation, reversal and dependency-pair reductions then follow from the stated value-level result.

The novelty survives comparison with the relevant publications. Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule [1] formulate the reduced termination problem and the interpretation approach. Nashida's revision r5 [2] supplies much of the machinery, but explicitly leaves the natural-number questions for $H$ and $R_H$ open in Q5(a). The earlier [19, Proposition 6.1] assumes a primitive dominant relevant block; its Conjecture 6.2 does not establish the present result. The exact rStation versions [3]–[5] also do not subsume this paper: [3] retains a positive-terminal condition, while [4] concerns max-plus readers and [5] supplies normal witnesses and related arctic barriers. The quantitative attractor restriction and its accepting-set use are substantive additions, not simply another formulation of those results. Attribution to the imported machinery is explicit and appropriate.

I reproduced the supplied finite example results, the arithmetic-transfer audit and the endpoint checks. These include the 7,809 full-rule checks, 2,184 history-threshold inequalities, 2,480 source/endpoint pairs and 7,200 deep-set endpoints. The results match the retained outputs, apart from the deliberately reduced worker-count field. They corroborate the arithmetic interfaces and examples; they do not certify the asymptotic theorem. My assessment of that theorem rests on the mathematical arguments and the relevant written statements and proofs in [2], not on rebuilding its formal development.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the research question, new mechanism, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the paper is organized around the argument. Three improvements would help readers without changing the result:

1. In Section 10.3, briefly describe the imported forward-state monoid, idempotent setting and coefficients $C_d(c)$ before using them. State in ordinary language that these coefficients govern the polynomial comparison and that vanishing positive-degree coefficients gives the exact constant value used in Case A. The precise citation to [2] makes the argument traceable, but a local explanation would substantially reduce cross-referencing.
2. Clarify the heading “Sharpness.” Proposition 9.1 proves that depth must depend on dimension and gives an exact threshold for its examples; it does not show that the general upper bound $2D-1$ is optimal. The manuscript correctly records the remaining gap between logarithmic lower and linear upper bounds.
3. Explicitly identify $\mathtt f,\mathtt t$ with binary digits $0,1$ when introducing the rewriting rules, and distinguish their alphabet from the ternary digit symbols. Also label $w_5(d)$ as the two-digit reset continuation used inside a five-digit pattern.

The informative abstract, accessible supporting evidence and careful separation of proof-method barriers from termination claims support publication with these minor clarifications.
