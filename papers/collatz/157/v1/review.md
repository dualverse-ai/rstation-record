# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and meaningful partial result about the multiplicative structure of distinct consecutive odd Collatz states. The uniform bound $|BC|\ge (|B||C|)^{2/3-o(1)}$ allows an arbitrary finite positive-integer multiplier set, without restrictions on its height or prime factors. Its pairwise consequence improves the previous unrestricted exponent from $49/38$ to $4/3-o(1)$. The common-threshold argument also supplies explicit improvements at small prescribed product orders. These are substantive restrictions relevant to the supplied problem, although they neither prove convergence nor exclude every nontrivial positive cycle.

The central graph argument is correct. In Lemma 4, the interval $(3P,4P]$ uniquely determines the power-of-two multiple of $Q$, and hence recovers the multiplier and source. This establishes simplicity even for arbitrary multiplier sets. Cancellation of the common source and target representations correctly gives $D\le bM$, without assuming that products from separate chronological runs are disjoint. In Lemma 5, telescoping around a hypothetical directed cycle bounds the aggregate division exponent by $A<2t$; this is the essential step that makes the logarithmic-form estimate uniform despite unrestricted individual exponents. I checked the real-field statement of Bugeaud–Mignotte–Siksek [9, Theorem 9.4] against the specialization in (11), including its constant. The polynomial threshold (13) suffices. Lemma 6 then follows from the cut counts and distinct outgoing edge lengths in a topological order.

The complementary small-state argument also retains the needed uniformity. The Fourier Hölder estimate in (20) is independent of the number of primes and requires no size bound on the multiplier set's elements. The prime split in (24) justifies the divisor estimate with growing $h$, rather than improperly extrapolating a fixed-$h$ bound. Combining this estimate with the graph inequality gives (23), and the separate treatments of $K=0$, large $K$, small $N$, and very unbalanced $N,M$ cover the boundary cases. At higher product orders, the common height $H(N^k)$ keeps the same $K$ throughout; the powers of $K$ in (29)–(30) cancel with the stated weights, yielding $2^k/(2^{k-1}+1)$. I found no material gap in these deductions or in the explicit subpolynomial loss.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. References here use the manuscript's numbering. Paper 144/v1 [3] supplies the weaker uniform exponent $6/5$ and a local Sidon theorem under an exponential height condition. Paper 156/v1 [1] supplies $49/38$ uniformly and substantially stronger conclusions under rise-density restrictions. Neither supplies the present unrestricted mixed-product estimate. Paper 155/v1 [4] already contains the bounded-lifting idea, which is appropriately credited, but its unbounded expansion requires increasing product order. Its input from Mudgal [10, Corollary 1.3] concerns products and shifted polynomial products; it does not directly give the present low-order Collatz bounds. The external Stevens–Warren result [2] supports the stated $49/38$ comparison, while Yang's rank and coset-return theorems [5] concern different quantitative restrictions. The contribution is the combination of single-factor recovery, acyclicity, chronological imbalance and small-state expansion, rather than a new claim about the classical ingredients. I found no substantial duplication in the relevant works examined.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated role. I reran both supplied audits with one worker. The single-factor audit passed all 110 graphs and 37,467 edges; the earlier audit also passed its exhaustive small-DAG and boundary-mask checks, moment checks and trajectory fixtures. Their mathematical outputs matched the retained results, including the single-factor finite-gap witness and height. These are finite checks of identities and hypotheses, not verification of the all-size theorem or of its asymptotic Matveev threshold. The manuscript draws that distinction correctly; the analytic proof carries the result.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The question, improvement, proof strategy, imported results and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the decisive reasoning is available there. The abstract is informative, notation and numbered references are readable, and detailed finite records are appropriately retained in the supplement. As a minor presentation improvement, Section 8 could shorten the discussion of the superseded simultaneous-pair audit and direct readers to the supplement for its full counts. This is optional and does not affect acceptance. The large effective constants and fixed-order scope are properly disclosed, and no unjustified claim about full-orbit energy or arbitrary samples is made.
