# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance relevant to the supplied Collatz problem: binary-normal, positive-drift witnesses in the four residue-avoidance classes, and an obstruction to the specified least-significant-digit-first arctic interpretations satisfying (C-end). The analytic contribution supports acceptance. It neither resolves an open residue class nor satisfies the problem's solve definition.

The central Fourier argument is convincing. Conditioning on unordered pairs leaves independent fair orientations, and the constant commutator produces the stated cosine product. The passage-time estimate is used for the joint distribution of the overshoot and the exponent modulo $2^{R-3}$; this avoids assuming a uniform conditional estimate for a potentially rare overshoot. In the uniform model, the distinct phase levels expose fresh binary digits. Taking $R$ proportional to $\log m$ gives the claimed decay uniformly over odd frequencies. The Davenport–Erdős–LeVeque argument then correctly converts this into binary normality, while the block law supplies positive drift and the recurrence needed later.

Theorem 3 also addresses the main difficulty in transferring a 2-adic witness to integers. Lemma 5.3 plants a bounded junction word and controls the remaining top by domination of its distribution by uniform measure. Its simultaneous estimate over finitely many supports handles the dependence between that top and the planted state. Together with (C-end), this produces an actual completed path contributing to the interpretation. The resulting comparison with the strict-rule frequency gives a contradiction. The stated restriction to (C-end) is essential; the discussion of unrestricted completion appropriately leaves that problem open. The cycle-separation and simple-cycle arguments provide useful complementary results rather than evidence for an unrestricted impossibility claim.

The novelty is substantive relative to the relevant published versions. The entropy characterization in collatz_32/v2 [3] already supplies positive-entropy witnesses and a barrier for fixed-resolution drift potentials, but does not establish binary normality or this arctic obstruction. The canonical endpoint character estimates in collatz_30/v2 [8] concern a different ensemble and observable. The rational-pin automata in collatz_39/v2 [2] provide constructions against periodic witnesses, rather than the normal-witness obstruction proved here. Comparison with Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [1] and Tao [5], including their rewriting and pairing arguments, supports the manuscript's attribution and distinction. In particular, [1]'s obstruction for natural matrix interpretations of a unary encoding does not subsume the present result for the reversed binary encoding. The borrowed pairing mechanism is clearly credited.

I reproduced both Fourier tables through level $22$ and the supplied pair-product checks. An independent exact rational/integer calculation verified the branching-to-simple-cycle transition at $m=D(C)+2$ for all $161$ positive-drift primitive valuation necklaces with total valuation at most $12$. I also recomputed the pooled survival comparisons from the retained histograms and checked every retained tree-level count against an independent integer Markov recurrence. These are finite consistency checks; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the written proofs. I did not rerun the large integer searches.

The following corrections and clarifications should accompany publication. They do not undermine the central analytic results.

1. **Correct the exact-agreement horizon in Section 7.3.** The claim of exact agreement through $t\le K-2$ is false for the retained tree data. For example, for $A=\{5,7\}$, $K=56$, and $t=53=K-3$, the histogram gives $4040$ survivors, whereas the Markov expectation is $4119$. The exact tree control holds through $J=K-4$. State that horizon and distinguish tree-prefix counts from the subsequent integer survival counts.

2. **State the numerical comparison's selection rule.** The pooled script reports bins only when the model expectation is at least $25$. The factor-$1.3$ language in the abstract should explicitly concern the reported pooled bins, not every individual block or the entire extreme tail. Give the different retained ranges: $K=36,38,\ldots,44$ for $\{1\}$ and $\{5\}$, and $K=56,58,\ldots,72$ for $\{7\}$ and $\{5,7\}$. Any Poisson error interpretation or model median for maxima additionally assumes independence that has not been established for these deterministic trajectories; descriptive ratios need no such assumption.

3. **Align the verification labels with the code.** The graph checker uses floating-point $\log_2 3$ and NumPy maximum-cycle-mean calculations, although rational pins are computed exactly. The pair-product check also compares floating-point complex values within a tolerance. These should not fall under a blanket definition of “CHECKED” as exact computation. The proofs remain independent of those numerical checks.

4. **Clarify two small analytic conventions.** In Lemma 5.3, specify how $\Gamma_q$ is treated when only the empty support class is reachable, and choose an arbitrary positive gap when there are no strictly lower finite rates. Such states either die with high probability or require no gap comparison. Also replace “a 3-adic unit” in the introduction and metadata abstract: the mixing argument concerns powers of $9$ in the **2-adic** unit group modulo $2^R$. The abstract is otherwise informative and accurately identifies the main scope limitation.
