# Accept

This update satisfactorily addresses the previous review’s corrections without broadening the accepted mathematical claims. The normal-witness results and the obstruction to arctic interpretations remain subject to their stated hypotheses, including (C-end). The revision does not resolve an open residue class or the supplied Collatz problem.

The revised Section 7.3 correctly distinguishes residue-tree prefix counts from integer survival counts, replaces the erroneous exact-agreement horizon with $t\le K-4$, and specifies the pooled block ranges and reporting threshold. Restricting the empirical conclusion to reported bins with model expectation at least $25$, and explicitly treating the ratios as descriptive, removes the previous overstatement about the extreme tail and avoids an unsupported independence assumption.

I independently recomputed the model using integer counts and compared it with the retained histograms. All retained tree-prefix counts agree with that recurrence. The example for $A=\{5,7\}$, $K=56$, $t=53$ gives exactly $4040$ survivors against expectation $4119$. The pooled reporting horizons are indeed $t-K=60,75,21,16$ for the four classes in the stated order. Combining the reported class comparisons gives extrema approximately $0.88246$ and $1.21220$; the full-problem comparison gives approximately $0.92737$ and $1.28332$, with the last qualifying bin at $t=340$. These support the revised factor-$1.3$ statement. This verification recomputes comparisons from retained data; it does not reproduce the large integer searches.

The added convention in Lemma 5.3 is sound. When the only reachable closed support class is the empty class, absorption has a geometric tail under uniform letters. Consequently, with high probability such a junction state cannot support the independent length-$l$ continuation used in Step 1. Assigning it rate $-\infty$ and taking an arbitrary positive gap when no smaller finite rate exists resolves the degenerate cases without changing the argument. The corrected description of powers of $9$ in the $2$-adic unit group also matches the existing proof. Distinguishing floating-point pair-product and graph-drift checks from exact rational-pin calculations appropriately clarifies the evidence.

One minor wording improvement remains: describe the intervals $[0.88,1.21]$ and $[0.93,1.28]$ as **approximately** those ranges, since their displayed upper endpoints are rounded down from the observed maxima. This does not affect the scientific conclusion or acceptance.
