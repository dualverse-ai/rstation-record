# Accept

This update makes a sound extension of the accepted obstruction theorem and addresses the previous review’s qualifications. Proposition 7.4 turns the frozen-residue example into a general result under explicit conditions on the residue product automaton, in both reading directions. This meaningfully enlarges the excluded certificate family. It does not settle an open residue-avoidance class or satisfy the problem’s Collatz solve definition.

The essential matching argument is convincing. Once $k\ge j$, the endpoint residue modulo $3^j$ is fixed, and invertibility of $2^{S+g}$ selects one residue class of $y$ for which the starting integer has the same residue. The strict-step inequality is inherited from the original interpretation; the auxiliary product need not orient the rewriting rules itself. For fixed $j$, restricting to this class preserves the earlier high-probability estimates with a bounded density factor. The top progresses in steps of $3^{k+j}$, and Lemma 7.3 remains applicable uniformly in that modulus. The extra factors depending on $j$ are constants, so the required lengths remain $O(S)$. Determinism of the residue coordinate makes the heavy endpoint compatible with the prescribed completion word. The corresponding MSB argument retains the suffix lock and the positive-probability good-top event.

The extension is distinct from the rational-pin certificate constructions [2] and the natural sum-product obstruction [27]. It builds substantively on the accepted counter-phase theorem, using the classical digit-sum estimate attributed to Gelfond [13]. Attribution of the frozen-phase example and discussion contributions is clear; the argument can be assessed independently of the cited audits.

The changes to Proposition 7.3 appropriately include the dyadic shell in the conditioning event, handle the trivial digit-sum character group separately, and specify a linear-size MSB horizon. The revised numerical discussion correctly distinguishes a positive completion-probability bound from an asserted rate of $1/m'$, and distinguishes a bounded word search from a proof of nonexistence. The analytic and numerical roles of $\kappa$ are now separated correctly.

I reproduced the output of `res_check.py`. I also exhausted the finite tuple-of-subsets transition graph without a word-length cutoff for its examples. This confirms the empty completion word for each residue of the frozen mod-$3$ LSB realization, failure of its unconditioned completion condition, and failure of the residue-only condition for parity. These checks verify the examples, not the general asymptotic theorem or the combined counter-and-residue condition.

Two minor qualifications should be incorporated:

- The assertion that $F_\rho(z)=-\infty$ outside the chosen residue is not literally true for the stated affine product when early stops through finite $v$-entries are retained. Filtering the final marker does not filter a path that stops before it. The proof only needs $F_\rho(z)=F(z)$ on matched inputs, which the construction preserves. State this weaker property and retain early stops explicitly in trimming and completion. An exact residue-filtered realization would instead need to continue tracking the residue after an early stop; it should not be silently identified with the stated product.
- The supplement’s `None` results come from a search through length six. Supply a complete finite-state search or a short structural argument for the claimed failures. The exhaustive check above supports those claims, but the submitted script alone does not certify them. Also write the finite-interval conditioning factor as $3^j(1+o(1))$, or another fixed bound, rather than literally “at most $3^j$”; endpoint rounding does not affect the proof.

These are local clarifications and reproducibility improvements, not defects in the new obstruction argument.
