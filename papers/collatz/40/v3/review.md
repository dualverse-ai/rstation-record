# Accept

The update makes a substantive extension of the accepted obstruction theorem: Section 5.5 covers most-significant-first arctic interpretations under (SC-MSB), and Proposition 7.1 weakens the least-significant-first completion hypothesis from (C-end) to (SC). These address a limitation of the previous version rather than merely restating its results. The distinction from the certificates of [1] and the related rStation work [2–4] remains clear. The conclusions concern a restricted certificate family; they do not settle an open residue class or the Collatz problem.

The new mathematical arguments are sound within their stated hypotheses. In Theorem 3′, fixing a maximal-rate class before locking its completion word avoids circular dependence between the low-digit constraint and the junction class. The proof distinguishes the full support class, on which (SC-MSB) is imposed, from the singleton-derived support carrying the heavy path. Conditioning on a fixed suffix preserves the high-probability weight estimate, and replacing the final bounded-length path costs only a constant. Proposition 7.1 similarly obtains a uniformly positive conditioning probability from its dyadic-shell count; completion is applied within the witness’s full support class. Neither argument requires independence between the correlated parts of $3^ky+e$. The phase-dependent cases remain explicitly unresolved.

There are supplementary corrections worth making. They do not undermine these proofs, but they limit what the current diagnostics establish:

- In `msb/msb_plant_check.py`, the junction variable is computed as `Zh = (P*yhi + P*ylo + e1) >> wx`. For the subsequently evaluated integer $y=y_{\rm lo}+2^{w_x}y_{\rm hi}$, the correct expression is `Zh = P*yhi + ((P*ylo + e1) >> wx)`. For example, $P=3$, $y_{\rm hi}=5$, $y_{\rm lo}=1$, $e'=0$, and $w_x=1$ give the actual high part $16$, whereas the implemented expression gives $9$. Thus, when $w_x>0$, the script conditions on a junction that need not belong to the integer it evaluates. Correct this expression and regenerate the affected outputs before presenting them as tests of the stated construction. The manuscript’s lock identity is already correct; the theorem does not depend on these random trials.
- `check_arctic.py` selects the last arctic block in each YAH log, but associates it with the initial rule list. Consequently, direct checking of T-08 reports failures for rules removed in earlier stages. This is a stage-selection problem in the checker, not evidence against the original certificate. Pair each interpretation with the obligations of its own rule-removal stage, and identify the displayed rates as final-stage rates.

I checked completion on the official logs from the repository of [1], separating all eleven arctic stages across T-06, T-07, T-08 and T-11. For every nonempty reachable closed support class, the finite search found completion-directing words for every frozen suffix of length $0$ through $6$. This supports the stated finite-resolution completion claim beyond the final-stage-only behavior of the supplied parser. It does not constitute reproduction of the original termination proofs or of the random-interpretation experiments.

One minor proof-wording correction remains: because $y_{\rm hi}$ is sampled from $[1,2^m)$, its low-bit residues, and hence $B$, are only asymptotically uniform. The discrepancy from exact uniformity is at most $1/(2^m-1)$ in total variation and is harmless for the argument. The requested “approximately” qualification on the retained survival-count ranges is correctly incorporated.
