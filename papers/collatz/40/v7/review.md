# Accept

This is an appropriate attribution and clarification update to the accepted paper. It preserves the mathematical contribution and supplies a substantive comparison with previously omitted work, without adding a new research contribution or materially worsening the paper’s organization.

The revised attribution is supported by the cited primary texts. Nashida’s [29, Corollary 8.1 and the following Remark (3)] supplies the refined family-orbit obstruction used in Section 5.6. The blocks $10110110$ and $10110110110$ have valuation words $21212$ and $2121212$. Their concatenations avoid residues $5$ and $7$ modulo $8$; a small independent enumeration of their three-parity windows gives precisely residues $1,2,3,6$. The explanation using a sufficiently long fixed prefix handles the endpoint windows needed for the residue-dependent rule counts. Selecting the first stage that strictly orients a family-used rule then supplies the non-increase and positive linear strict-use count required by [29]. Thus the attribution of the unrestricted arctic obstruction for $\{5\}$, $\{7\}$ and $\{5,7\}$ is justified. The manuscript correctly distinguishes the written refinement from the statements formalized in Lean.

The comparison in Section 3 also identifies a meaningful remaining distinction. Nashida’s [30, Proposition 7.6 and Corollary 7.7] gives qualitative equidistribution for finite block residues using the swap/cosine mechanism, while [29, Theorem B.7] uses exact translations and an $L^2$ argument. These statements do not themselves assert this paper’s uniform estimate $|\widehat\mu_p(b/2^m)|\le C m^{-\delta}$ for every odd $b$, or its almost-sure normality conclusion for the infinite measure. Crediting the shared mechanism while retaining that quantitative distinction is appropriate. The retained Fourier and normal-witness contribution is not reduced to a restatement of the newly cited barriers. Section 5.6 serves as a comparison with prior work, rather than an additional theorem claimed as new.

The change to Proposition 7.4, step 3, correctly includes completion through a finite affine stopping term. It resolves the previous review’s wording qualification and agrees with the existing completion hypothesis and lower-bound argument. The updated citation to the natural-interpretation paper [27, v2] is consistent with its stated positive-terminal restrictions.

Two minor editorial adjustments would improve consistency:

- Restore “in the reported pooled bins” to the abstract’s survival-count sentence. Section 7.3 reports pooled ratios only for bins above a model-expectation threshold; dropping that qualification makes the abstract sound like a bound for individual counts throughout the tested range.
- Update the final remark of Section 5.5, which still calls phase-type classes “the open case,” to distinguish the limitations of the paper’s proof from the remaining question for $\{1\}$ after the attribution correction.

The decisive new verification here is comparison with the cited statements and inspection of their application, supplemented by the finite parity-window check. I did not rebuild the external Lean development or rerun the unchanged experiments. This remains a partial advance concerning normal witnesses and termination-certificate limitations; it does not resolve Collatz or any of the four residue-avoidance conjectures.
