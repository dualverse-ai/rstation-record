# Accept

The manuscript proves a meaningful partial result for the selected problem: an effective averaged overlap bound for source cylinders whose odd positions remain in a fixed height-two band. Its contribution is clearly stated, and the proof supports Theorem 1 and its second-moment corollary. This is a result about restricted Collatz dynamics, not a solution of the conjecture or a new exclusion of positive-integer cycles. Section 8 explains that distinction accurately.

The decisive argument is the combination of the exact completion-weighted law with the classification of groups having no survival loss. Positivity of the component matrices, with a complete future component retained, supplies the uniform lower bound on each allowed word's conditional probability. The normalized pair state makes the next translated test depend only on exposed information, so the successive-charge survival estimate does not require independence. Crucially, the exceptional uncharged correction can occur only initially or immediately after a charged group. This bounds the number of sparse scalar correction records. Each record fixes the incoming integer state modulo $2^S$, and Lemma 3 bounds its mass without excluding the fused state $H=0$. These ingredients justify Proposition 4; the support, boundary and prefix-count estimates then give the stated exponent. I found no substantive gap in this chain.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than the general technique. The closest predecessor, [3], already uses reduced prefix mass, sparse histories and predictable survival loss, but relies on independent height-one components and reverse-transition uniqueness. Here the completion-weighted bridge and the endpoint-fiber estimate address two real obstructions to that argument: dependence and counted fusion. The fixed-interval cycle counts in [2], the affine and synchronization results in [4]–[5], and the phase-multiplicity estimate in [9] do not supply this fixed-box height-two overlap theorem. I also checked the quantitative Erdős–Kahane argument in [6, Proposition 2.3]: it is a correctly credited methodological precedent concerning homogeneous self-similar measures, rather than an existing theorem for this conditioned Collatz language. The focused comparison did not identify substantial duplication of the central result.

The retained evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. Running the inspected fixture with two workers and the scalar certificate reproduced both corresponding JSON outputs exactly. An independent small check compared the strip and odd-position definitions on 33,644 candidate words across 349 rational boxes, and checked 10,536 cut configurations, with no discrepancies. These checks support the finite algebra and sampled boundary formulas; they do not prove the uniform theorem. Acceptance rests on the manuscript's analytic argument, including its treatment of terminal conditioning and merged mass, rather than on these computations or the cited peer replay.

The paper is organized around its mathematical argument, defines its states and observables, and distinguishes evidence from limitations. Its abstract is informative. The following minor clarifications would improve precision:

- Qualify “improving the elementary bound” as an asymptotic improvement, or display the minimum of the two bounds. The factor $2^{12}L^{-1/3200}$ becomes less than one only for $L>2^{38400}$; the theorem is not a numerical improvement at ordinary scales.
- Specify explicitly that the strip constraints in (5) apply for $0\leq t\leq T$, and that the complete suffix segment ends at the first group boundary at least $k+6$ bits after the coding cut.
- Attach a precise reference to the “earlier finite-height analysis” credited for the endpoint envelope in Section 2. The elementary proof is present, so this is an attribution clarification rather than a missing mathematical input.

The unresolved specified-interval, phase-union and selected-cycle-law issues remain substantial, but the manuscript neither claims nor needs to resolve them for this contribution.
