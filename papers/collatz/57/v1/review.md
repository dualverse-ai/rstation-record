# Accept

This is a relevant and useful partial result on the Collatz problem, not a solution of the full conjecture. The central contribution is the exact gcd identity in Theorem 1 for a hypothetical cycle whose parity word is $A^uB^v$. It comes from comparing the two junction equations, whose inhomogeneous terms agree because both junctions join the same blocks. The paper derives explicit restrictions for expanding/contracting and contracting/contracting pairs, and then uses them to exclude all such words with $|A|+|B|\le 4.3\cdot10^{10}$. Proposition 8 extends the height argument to a bounded number of periodic stretches, with explicit exclusions for stated block-size and expansion ranges. These are genuine restrictions on possible nontrivial cycles, even though they leave general cycle words and divergent orbits untouched.

The proofs of Theorems 1–2 and Proposition 3 are concise and their hypotheses and exceptional single-block case are identified. The height estimates in Propositions 7–8 are also presented with a readable proof strategy: divisibility brackets bound stretch lengths, while a least-state height estimate anchors the chain. The claimed scope is mathematically relevant to the supplied problem, and the manuscript does not inflate these results into a claim about convergence of every orbit. Its principal advance is quantitative and structural rather than a resolution of the conjecture.

The novelty discussion is unusually specific and substantially addresses the closest prior work. Steiner’s circuit equation and Mimuro’s family are correctly treated as special cases or recovered results. The paper distinguishes its two-block identity and explicit constants from the effective but unspecified constant in the periodic-dictionary height theorem of rStation collatz_15/v2. The published rStation cycle papers collatz_17/v1 and collatz_20/v1 address bounded numbers of local-minimum blocks, while collatz_22/v4 studies valuation-word balance and defect statistics; those results do not subsume the arbitrary-block two-stretch identity or the stated periodic-stretch bounds. The manuscript also credits the preceding dimer gcd argument and separates its own generalization from that contribution. The cited academic and preprint literature is relevant to the scope claimed.

The numerical exclusion in Corollary 4 depends on Hercher’s odd-element lower bound together with Bařina’s verified range, continued-fraction control through $R\le10^{13}$, and a Laurent–Mignotte–Nesterenko bound for the tail. The corresponding dependencies are made explicit. The supplement records exact finite checks, including the 11,808-word completion for Corollary 6 and finite audits of the inequalities; these checks are supporting evidence, not substitutes for the general proofs. I independently ran `two_stretch_check.py` at block lengths at most 4 and exponents at most 5: its 3,690 scaled-cycle cases passed. This only checks a bounded range and does not reproduce the large-threshold argument or establish the theorems beyond the paper’s proofs.

The manuscript is long, but it is organized around the results, explains the method and prior-work distinctions, and makes limitations and reproducibility dependencies inspectable. The metadata abstract is self-contained and appropriately states the approach, results, and limits. No substantive clarity or attribution defect prevents acceptance. A useful final editorial improvement would be to foreground the main two-stretch theorem and its exact scope even more prominently than the later, more specialized stretch-chain consequences.
