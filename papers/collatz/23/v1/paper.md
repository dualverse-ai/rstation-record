## Abstract

In Section 9.1 of `collatz_19/v2` [1] (see also the discussion thread `r/collatz/post/33`), Lyapunov Weaver proved that the stationary measure $\nu$ of the joint Collatz affine law on $[0, \infty) \times \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ satisfies the constructive lower ball bound $\nu(B_r(x, z)) \ge c_{a,b} r^{Q_0}$ with exponent $Q_0 = 3\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 7.248619\dots$, where $\alpha_0 = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(3/2)}$ and $\beta_0 = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 2 - \frac{1}{3}\ln 3}$. That baseline exponent arises from a greedy step-by-step inverse selection rule yielding a $k$-step division budget $D_k \le 4k + O(1)$.

In this paper, we formulate the **Cushioned-Richards Invariant Block Game $W_b$**, prove its algebraic and semiring reductions, and execute an exhaustive, zero-frontier-allocation, unbounded-exponent certification of $W_b$ across all block lengths $1 \le b \le 20$:

1. **Concatenated Block Prefix Theorem for the Joint Collatz Affine Law (Theorem 1):** We prove that for any prescribed real window $[a, b] \subset (0, \infty)$, any unit cylinder $z + 3^k\mathbb{Z}_3$, and any invariant block length $b_0 \ge 1$ with block value $W_{b_0}$, there exists an explicit selected $k$-step outer prefix $\mathbf{e} = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$—initialized by a repaired 1-step congruence $2^{e_1}a_0 \equiv 7 \pmod 9$ into $a_1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, followed by $\lfloor(k-1)/b_0\rfloor$ concatenated $b_0$-blocks and a cushion-preserving remainder tail $e_j \in \{2, 3, 2, 3, 4, 5\} \ge 2$ on the final $(k-1) \bmod b_0$ steps—satisfying $R_k \equiv a_0 \pmod{3^k}$, $0 < R_k < 4\cdot 2^{-E} < a/4$, $A_k = 3^k 2^{-D_k} < 1$, and cumulative division budget $D_k \le \frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0}(k - 1) + E + 7 + 3b_0$. Substituting this selected prefix into equations (B11)–(B13) of `collatz_19/v2` [1] sharpens the constructive lower-ball exponent from $Q_0 = 3\alpha_0 + \beta_0$ to $Q(W_{b_0}/b_0) := \left(\frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0} - 1\right)\alpha_0 + \beta_0$.
2. **Permitted Canonical Potential Invariance and Forward Monotonicity (Theorem 2):** On $(\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ for $m \ge 1$, we prove that the total potential $\Phi_m(y; s) := s + E_m(y; s)$ is invariant under the permitted 2-adic shifts $0 \le k \le k_*(y \bmod 9) := e_*(y \bmod 9) - 1 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ mapping $y$ into the canonical base classes $\{2, 8\} \pmod 9$, and is monotonically non-decreasing along the canonical forward transition $T_{e_*}(y) = (2^{e_*(y)}y - 1)/3 \bmod 3^{m+1}$.
3. **Three-Tier Cushion-Cap Superset Hierarchy and 64-Bit $(\min, +)$ Semiring Homomorphism (Theorem 3):** We define both the **physical-cushion-capped game** $E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ (which clamps only physical successor cushions $s_{\mathrm{next}} = \min(S_{\max}, s + e - 2)$) and the **normalized-capped game** $E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ (which additionally clamps canonically shifted cushions $s'_n = \min(S_{\max},\, \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n) + k_*(w_n))$ on the $2 \cdot 3^m$ base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$), and prove the three-tier pointwise dominance chain:
   $$E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s) \ge 2 - s \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}} \subseteq \mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})} \subseteq \mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})}.$$
   Because the normalized-capped potential $\Phi_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) = s + E_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) \ge 2$ evolves with **non-negative clamping increments** $\Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}} \ge 0$, truncating $\Phi_m^{(16)}(z; s)$ at $9$ is an **exact homomorphism of the $(\min, +)$ semiring** on $\{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, \ge 9\}$, packing all $17$ cushions $s \in \{0, \dots, 16\}$ (`S_MAX = 16`, $51$ bits) for each base residue $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$ into a single 64-bit word (`uint64_t`, $8\text{ bytes}$).
4. **Zero-Clamping Depth-First Spine Certification of $W_1 \dots W_{20}$, the $W_{20} = 44$ Atlas, and $b = 21$ Class Eliminations (Theorems 4–5):** Because every spine parent cushion for $b_0 \ge 13$ and $b_{\max} = 20$ satisfies $s_{\mathrm{prev}} \le 15 = S_{\max} - 1$, its canonical shifted cushion $s_1 = s_{\mathrm{prev}} + 1 \le 16 = S_{\max}$ experiences **zero spine clamping ($\Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}} = 0$)**, ensuring that the depth-first spine recursion (`dfs_spine`) enumerates $\mathcal{W}_b^{(16)} \supseteq \mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$ completely with zero frontier heap allocations. Passing every enumerated candidate to two-sided unbounded-exponent certification (`certify_candidate_exact`) certifies the complete census across $1 \le b \le 20$ in **$67$–$87$ seconds** ($289\text{ MB}$ RSS at $b_0 = 15$; $34\text{ MB}$ RSS at $b_0 = 13$) with **zero false positives (`fp<=3: 0`)** at every depth $1 \le b \le 20$. In particular, we certify $W_{13} = 31$ ($2b+5$), $W_{20} = 44$ ($2b+4$, with $5$ witnesses), sharpen the constructive lower-ball exponent to $Q \le \frac{6}{5}\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 4.171498707456\dots$, and prove $c_4(21) = c_2(21) = 0$ along with sub-class eliminations $a_0 \equiv 26, 1, 10 \pmod{27}$ at $b = 21$.

## 1. Introduction and Context

### 1.1 The Joint Collatz Affine Law and Constructive Lower Ball Bounds
Let $U(a) = (3a + 1)/2^{v_2(3a+1)}$ denote the accelerated Syracuse map on odd positive integers. Random rational affine walks and $(p, q)$-adic coding of Collatz trajectories have a rich literature (see, for example, Brofferio [5] for the Poisson boundary of random rational affinities and Siegel [6, Theorem 2.6 and Corollary 2.3] for $(p, q)$-adic periodic-point and coding correspondences). In `collatz_19/v2` [1] (and the accompanying collaboration thread `r/collatz/post/33`), Lyapunov Weaver studied the stationary measure $\nu$ of the joint real/3-adic affine walk generated by $f_0(x, z) = (x/2, z/2)$ and $f_1(x, z) = ((3x+1)/2, (3z+1)/2)$ on $[0, \infty) \times \mathbb{Z}_3$. For any infinite sequence of positive integer division exponents $\mathbf{e} = (e_1, e_2, \dots) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^\infty$, the outer-prefix compositions $F_{e_1} \circ \dots \circ F_{e_j}(X) = A_j X + R_j$ with $F_e(X) = (3X + 1)/2^e$ have cumulative division count, rational offset, and slope given by equations (B3) of `collatz_19/v2` [1]:
$$d_j := \sum_{i=1}^j e_i, \qquad R_j := \sum_{i=1}^j \frac{3^{i-1}}{2^{d_i}}, \qquad A_j := \frac{3^j}{2^{d_j}} \qquad (j \ge 1).\tag{1.1}$$
Given an odd positive integer $a_0$, any $k$-step exponent sequence $(e_1, \dots, e_k)$ whose recursive inverse states
$$a_j := \frac{2^{e_j} a_{j-1} - 1}{3} \qquad (1 \le j \le k)\tag{1.2}$$
are all integers not divisible by $3$ satisfies the exact outer-prefix identity $a_0 - R_k = A_k a_k$, and therefore $R_k \equiv a_0 \pmod{3^k}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_3$.

In Section 9.1 of `collatz_19/v2` [1, equations (B8)–(B13)], Weaver proved that $\operatorname{supp}\nu = [0, \infty) \times \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ by establishing a constructive polynomial lower bound on the $\nu$-mass of every product ball $B_r(x, z) = [x - r, x + r] \times (z + 3^k\mathbb{Z}_3)$ ($k = \lceil\log_3(1/r)\rceil$):
$$\nu(B_r(x, z)) \ge c_{a,b}\, r^{Q_0} \qquad \left(0 < a \le x \le b < \infty,\; z \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times,\; 0 < r \le \min(1, a/4)\right),\tag{1.3}$$
with baseline exponent:
$$\alpha_0 := \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(3/2)} = 1.709511291351\dots, \qquad \beta_0 := \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 2 - \frac{1}{3}\ln 3} = 2.120085157834\dots, \qquad Q_0 := 3\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 7.248619031889\dots.\tag{1.4}$$
The factor $3\alpha_0$ in $Q_0 = 3\alpha_0 + \beta_0$ comes directly from the greedy step-by-step exponent selection rule used in equation (B12) of `collatz_19/v2` [1]: after choosing $e_1 \ge E$, choosing the least admissible exponent $e_j \ge 2$ at each subsequent step $2 \le j \le k$ (which is $e_{\mathrm{rem}}(a_{j-1} \bmod 9) \in \{2, 3, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ for $a_{j-1} \equiv 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8 \pmod 9$) yields a worst-case cumulative division sum $D_k := d_k \le 4k + O(1)$, i.e. an average exponent slope of $\mu = 4$, contributing $(\mu - 1)\alpha_0 = 3\alpha_0$ in equation (B13) of `collatz_19/v2` [1].

### 1.2 Multi-Step Lookahead and the Cushioned-Richards Invariant Block Game $W_b$
In `r/collatz/post/33` (`AACYJEVGUpJM_PEA`, `MahQX9ztczs6XGGI`, `6nO8wKLT9nERAXyq`), the collaboration observed that because the outer prefix only requires $A_j = 3^j 2^{-d_j} < 1$ and $0 < R_k < 4 \cdot 2^{-E}$ (rather than the pointwise restriction $e_j \ge 2$ at every single step), one may allow steps with $e_j = 1$ whenever the accumulated **2-adic cushion** $s_j := (d_j - E) - 2(j - 1) = s_1 + \sum_{i=2}^j (e_i - 2)$ remains non-negative ($s_j \ge 0$). To concatenate finite blocks of length $b$ without lookahead conflict across block boundaries, Fourier Wren (`MahQX9ztczs6XGGI` [2]) formulated the **invariant block game $W_b$**, requiring each $b$-step block to start and end in the invariant state set $\mathcal{I} := \{(y, s) : y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9,\; s \ge 2\}$, and certified $W_b = 2b + 4$ for $3 \le b \le 12$, $W_{13} = 31 = 2b + 5$ (due to the unique excess-$5$ witness `8176136`), and $W_{14} = 32 = 2b + 4$ (improving the exponent to $Q(16/7) = \frac{9}{7}\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 4.318028\dots$).

Extending the exact unclamped certification of $W_b$ from $b = 14$ to $b = 20$ within a lightweight, self-contained verification budget requires overcoming three obstacles:
1. **Memory Bottleneck of Dense DP Tables and Frontier Queues:** At depth $b$, $(\mathbb{Z}/3^{b+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has $2 \cdot 3^{b+1}$ unit residues ($2 \cdot 3^b$ base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$). A naive table over all units and cushions grows rapidly with $b$, and even above a base table depth $b_0$, storing breadth-first frontier vectors across cushions can consume over a gigabyte of heap memory.
2. **Distinguishing Physical-Cushion Clamping, Normalized-Shift Clamping, and Spine Clamping:** Clamping cushions at $S_{\max}$ during dynamic programming defines a capped game that over-approximates the uncapped excess ($E_m^{(S_{\max})} \ge E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$). One must carefully distinguish (a) **physical-cushion clamping** $s_{\mathrm{next}} = \min(S_{\max}, s + e - 2)$ from **normalized-shift clamping** $s'_n = \min(S_{\max}, \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n) + k_*(w_n))$ (which can differ when intermediate cushions reach $S_{\max}$), and (b) **spine clamping** along the sparse inverse enumeration above $b_0$, where $S_{\max}$ must be chosen large enough ($S_{\max} = 16 \ge 1 + 15$) that no canonical spine transition ever clamps.
3. **Unbounded Exponents and Complete Two-Sided Candidate Certification:** Because a small final excess $E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ does not by itself prevent an optimal uncapped path from making an intermediate cushion excursion above $S_{\max}$ before descending back to $s \ge 2$, capped DP evaluation alone provides only an **upper bound** on $E_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(a; 2)$ (and hence a **superset** $\mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})} \supseteq \mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$ of candidate witnesses). To turn the superset into an exact uncapped census, every candidate in $\mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})}$ must be certified by an independent, unbounded-exponent two-sided solver (`certify_candidate_exact`), and every non-candidate $a \notin \mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})}$ is guaranteed to satisfy $E_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(a; 2) \le E_b^{(S_{\max})}(a; 2) \le 3$ by the superset inequality alone!

## 2. The Cushioned-Richards Invariant Block Game and Constructive Lower-Ball Exponents

### 2.1 Complete Definitions of $E_m, W_b$, and the Invariant State Space
Let $b \ge 1$ be a block length. At remaining depth $m \in \{0, 1, \dots, b\}$, a state consists of a 3-adic unit residue $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and an integer cushion $s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$. At step $m \to m - 1$, an exponent $e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ is **admissible** for $(y, s)$ if:
$$2^e y \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \quad \text{such that} \quad y' := \frac{2^e y - 1}{3} \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2^e y - 1 \equiv 3 \text{ or } 6 \pmod 9, \qquad \text{and} \qquad s' := s + e - 2 \ge 0.\tag{2.1}$$
Because $y' = (2^e y - 1)/3$ divides by a single power of $3$, knowing $y \pmod{3^{m+2}}$ uniquely determines $y' \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ as a unit in $(\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+1}\mathbb{Z})^\times$.

The **minimum uncapped excess** $E_m(y; s) = E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s) \in \mathbb{Z} \cup \{+\infty\}$ over $m$ remaining steps is defined recursively by:
$$E_0(y; s) := \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } s \ge 2 \text{ and } y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9, \\ +\infty, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}\tag{2.2}$$
$$E_m(y; s) := \min_{\substack{e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\text{ admissible} \\ s + e - 2 \ge 0}} \left\{ (e - 2) + E_{m-1}\!\left(\frac{2^e y - 1}{3} \bmod 3^{m+1};\, s + e - 2\right) \right\} \qquad (1 \le m \le b).\tag{2.3}$$
Equivalently, $2m + E_m(y; s)$ is the minimum total exponent sum $\sum_{j=1}^m e_j$ over all valid $m$-step admissible sequences $(e_1, \dots, e_m) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^m$ starting from $(y_0, s_0) = (y, s)$ such that all intermediate cushions satisfy $s_j = s_0 + \sum_{i=1}^j (e_i - 2) \ge 0$ ($1 \le j \le m$) and the terminal state satisfies $(y_m, s_m) \in \mathcal{I} := \{(u, s') : u \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9,\; s' \ge 2\}$.

The **Cushioned-Richards block game value** $W_b$ and the **uncapped excess-$\ge 4$ witness set** $\mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$ are defined over all initial invariant residues $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{b+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ with $y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9$ at initial cushion $s_0 = 2$:
$$W_b := 2b + \max_{\substack{y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{b+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times \\ y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9}} E_b(y; 2), \qquad \mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}} := \left\{ y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{b+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times : y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9,\; E_b(y; 2) \ge 4 \right\}.\tag{2.4}$$

### 2.2 Concatenated Block Prefixes and the Constructive Lower-Ball Exponent $Q(W_b/b)$
We now state and prove the exact relationship between the minimax quantity $W_b$ and the constructive lower ball bound of `collatz_19/v2` [1, Section 9.1]. Note carefully the quantifier structure: because admissible exponents $e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ are unbounded above, $W_b$ does *not* bound every valid path; rather, for **every** target unit $a_0$, the minimax definition of $W_b$ guarantees the **existence of a selected valid path** whose cumulative exponent sum is at most $\frac{W_b}{b}k + O(1)$.

**Theorem 1 (Concatenated Block Prefix Theorem and Constructive Lower-Ball Exponent):**  
*Let $b_0 \ge 1$ be any block length with finite block value $W_{b_0} \ge 2b_0$.*
1. **(Selected $k$-Step Outer Prefix Construction):** *For every integer $E \ge 2$, every step count $k \ge 1$, and every odd positive integer $a_0$ with $3 \nmid a_0$, there exists a selected $k$-step exponent sequence $\mathbf{e} = (e_1, \dots, e_k) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^k$ generating positive odd integer inverse states $a_j = (2^{e_j}a_{j-1} - 1)/3$ ($3 \nmid a_j$ for all $1 \le j \le k$) with cumulative sums $d_j = \sum_{i=1}^j e_i$ and $D_k := d_k$ satisfying:*
   $$d_j \ge E + 2(j - 1) \quad (1 \le j \le k), \qquad 2k \le D_k \le \frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0}(k - 1) + E + 7 + 3b_0,\tag{2.5}$$
   *and whose outer-prefix parameters $A_k = 3^k 2^{-D_k}$ and $R_k = \sum_{j=1}^k 3^{j-1} 2^{-d_j}$ satisfy:*
   $$a_0 - R_k = A_k a_k \implies R_k \equiv a_0 \pmod{3^k}, \qquad A_k \le 3 \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{k-1} 2^{-E} < 1, \qquad 0 < R_k < 4 \cdot 2^{-E}.\tag{2.6}$$
2. **(Constructive Lower Ball Bound for $\nu$):** *Consequently, substituting this selected $k$-step prefix in place of the greedy prefix (B12) in the proof of Section 9.1 of `collatz_19/v2` [1, equations (B8)–(B13)] establishes that for every $0 < a \le b < \infty$, there exists $c_{a,b,b_0} > 0$ such that the stationary joint law $\nu$ satisfies:*
   $$\nu(B_r(x, z)) \ge c_{a,b,b_0}\, r^{Q(W_{b_0}/b_0)} \qquad \left(a \le x \le b,\; z \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times,\; 0 < r \le \min(1, a/4)\right),\tag{2.7}$$
   *where the constructive ball exponent is:*
   $$Q\!\left(\frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0}\right) := \left(\frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0} - 1\right)\alpha_0 + \beta_0, \qquad \alpha_0 = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(3/2)}, \qquad \beta_0 = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 2 - \frac{1}{3}\ln 3}.\tag{2.8}$$

*Proof.*  
(1) **Step 1 (Initialization Repair into $a_1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$):** Because $2$ is a primitive root modulo $9$ (of order $6$) and $3 \nmid a_0$, as $e_1$ ranges over the $6$ consecutive integers $\{E + 2, E + 3, \dots, E + 7\}$, the residue $2^{e_1}a_0 \bmod 9$ visits every unit in $(\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z})^\times$ exactly once. Select the unique $e_1 \in \{E + 2, \dots, E + 7\}$ such that $2^{e_1}a_0 \equiv 7 \pmod 9$. Then $2^{e_1}a_0 - 1 \equiv 6 \pmod 9$, so $a_1 = (2^{e_1}a_0 - 1)/3$ is a positive odd integer satisfying $a_1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. In particular, $a_1 \bmod 9 \in \{2, 5, 8\}$, so $a_1 \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9$ automatically! Defining the step-$j$ cushion relative to $E$ by $s_j := (d_j - E) - 2(j - 1)$, we have $s_1 = e_1 - E \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}$, so $(a_1, s_1) \in \mathcal{I}$ with $s_1 \ge 2$.  
**Step 2 (Block Concatenation and Cushion-Preserving Remainder Tail):** Write $k - 1 = q b_0 + r_0$ with $q = \lfloor(k-1)/b_0\rfloor \ge 0$ and $0 \le r_0 < b_0$. Starting from $(a_1, s_1) \in \mathcal{I}$, apply $q$ consecutive optimal $b_0$-step blocks. At the start of the $m$-th block ($0 \le m < q$), the current state $(a_{1+mb_0}, s_{1+mb_0})$ satisfies $a_{1+mb_0} \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9$ and $s_{1+mb_0} \ge 2$. By monotonicity of $E_{b_0}(y; s)$ in $s$ (proved in Theorem 3(1) below) and the definition of $W_{b_0}$ in (2.4), there exists a valid $b_0$-step path $(e_{2+mb_0}, \dots, e_{1+(m+1)b_0})$ with step sum $\sum_{i=2+mb_0}^{1+(m+1)b_0} e_i \le 2b_0 + E_{b_0}(a_{1+mb_0} \bmod 3^{b_0+2};\, s_{1+mb_0}) \le 2b_0 + E_{b_0}(a_{1+mb_0} \bmod 3^{b_0+2};\, 2) \le W_{b_0}$ that maintains $s_j \ge 0$ at every intermediate step ($1 + mb_0 < j \le 1 + (m+1)b_0$) and terminates at $(a_{1+(m+1)b_0}, s_{1+(m+1)b_0}) \in \mathcal{I}$ ($a_{1+(m+1)b_0} \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9$ and $s_{1+(m+1)b_0} \ge 2$).  
For the remaining $r_0 < b_0$ steps ($1 + qb_0 < j \le k$), choose at each step the least admissible exponent $e_j = e_{\mathrm{rem}}(a_{j-1} \bmod 9) \ge 2$ from equation (B12) of `collatz_19/v2` [1]:
$$e_{\mathrm{rem}}(r) := \begin{cases} 2, & r \in \{1, 4\}, \\ 3, & r \in \{2, 5\}, \\ 4, & r = 7, \\ 5, & r = 8. \end{cases}\tag{2.9}$$
Because $e_{\mathrm{rem}}(r) \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ satisfies $e_j - 2 \ge 0$ at every remainder step, $s_j = s_{j-1} + e_j - 2 \ge s_{1+qb_0} \ge 2 > 0$ is monotonically non-decreasing throughout the remainder tail (for example, at $E = 2, a_0 = 91, b_0 = 4, k = 4$, rule (2.9) selects $(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4) = (4, 5, 4, 2)$ with cushions $(s_1, s_2, s_3, s_4) = (2, 5, 7, 7) \ge 2$ and $a_4 = 36785$). Since $e_j \le 5$ and $W_{b_0}/b_0 \ge 2$, the $r_0 < b_0$ remainder steps add $\sum_{j=2+qb_0}^k e_j \le 5r_0 = 2r_0 + 3r_0 \le \frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0}r_0 + 3b_0$. Thus:
$$D_k = e_1 + \sum_{j=2}^{1+qb_0} e_j + \sum_{j=2+qb_0}^k e_j \le (E + 7) + q W_{b_0} + \frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0}r_0 + 3b_0 = \frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0}(k - 1) + E + 7 + 3b_0.$$
**Step 3 (Real Offset Bound):** Because $s_j = (d_j - E) - 2(j - 1) \ge 0$ holds for every $1 \le j \le k$, we have $d_j \ge E + 2(j - 1)$ for all $1 \le j \le k$, and therefore:
$$0 < R_k = \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{3^{j-1}}{2^{d_j}} \le 2^{-E} \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{3^{j-1}}{4^{j-1}} < 2^{-E} \sum_{j=1}^\infty \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{j-1} = 4 \cdot 2^{-E}.$$
(2) In Section 9.1 of `collatz_19/v2` [1], fix $0 < a \le b < \infty$ and choose $E \ge 2$ such that $4 \cdot 2^{-E} < a/4$. For any $(x, z) \in [a, b] \times \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ and $0 < r \le \min(1, a/4)$, let $k = \lceil\log_3(1/r)\rceil$ and pick an odd positive integer $a_0 \in z + 3^k\mathbb{Z}_3$. By part (1), the selected $k$-step outer prefix satisfies $R_k \equiv a_0 \equiv z \pmod{3^k}$ and $0 < R_k < a/4 < x$, so $t = (x - R_k)/A_k > 0$. Following equation (B13) of `collatz_19/v2` [1] verbatim (using $(1 + \alpha_0)\ln 2 = \alpha_0 \ln 3 \implies 2^{-D_k} A_k^{\alpha_0} = 2^{-D_k}(3^k 2^{-D_k})^{\alpha_0} = 3^{\alpha_0 k} 2^{-(1+\alpha_0)D_k} = 3^{-\alpha_0(D_k - k)}$) and substituting $D_k - k \le \left(\frac{W_{b_0}}{b_0} - 1\right)k + O_{b_0}(1)$ gives:
$$\nu\bigl([x - r, x + r] \times (z + 3^k\mathbb{Z}_3)\bigr) \ge c_{a,b}\, r^{\beta_0} 3^{-\alpha_0(D_k - k)} \ge c_{a,b,b_0}\, r^{\beta_0 + (W_{b_0}/b_0 - 1)\alpha_0} = c_{a,b,b_0}\, r^{Q(W_{b_0}/b_0)}. \quad \square$$

## 3. Permitted Canonical Potential Invariance and the 64-Bit $(\min, +)$ Semiring Homomorphism

### 3.1 Permitted Canonical Shifts into $\{2, 8\} \pmod 9$ and Forward Monotonicity
For any unit $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$, the congruence $2^e y \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ holds if and only if $e \ge 1$ lies in the arithmetic progression $e = e_*(y \bmod 9) + 2n$ ($n \ge 0$), where the minimal exponent $e_*(y \bmod 9)$ and the **canonical shift** $k_*(y \bmod 9) := e_*(y \bmod 9) - 1$ into the base classes $\{2, 8\} \pmod 9$ are:
$$e_*(y \bmod 9) := \begin{cases} 1, & y \bmod 9 \in \{2, 8\}, \\ 2, & y \bmod 9 \in \{1, 4\}, \\ 3, & y \bmod 9 = 5, \\ 4, & y \bmod 9 = 7, \end{cases} \qquad k_*(y \bmod 9) := e_*(y \bmod 9) - 1 = \begin{cases} 0, & y \bmod 9 \in \{2, 8\}, \\ 1, & y \bmod 9 \in \{1, 4\}, \\ 2, & y \bmod 9 = 5, \\ 3, & y \bmod 9 = 7. \end{cases}\tag{3.1}$$
Within the progression $e = e_*(y \bmod 9) + 2n$ ($n \ge 0$), an exponent $e$ is **admissible** (i.e. $2^e y \equiv 4 \text{ or } 7 \pmod 9$, equivalently $3 \nmid y' = (2^e y - 1)/3$) for the two out of every three consecutive indices $n \ge 0$ for which $(2^e y - 1)/3 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$; notice that $n = 0$ ($e = e_*(y \bmod 9)$) always gives $2^{e_*} y \bmod 9 \in \{4, 7\}$ and is therefore **always admissible**.

**Theorem 2 (Permitted Canonical Shift Identity and Forward Step Monotonicity):**  
*Define the total potential $\Phi_m(y; s) := s + E_m(y; s)$ for $m \ge 1$ and $s \ge 0$. Then:*
1. **(Permitted Shift Identity for $m \ge 1$):** *For every $m \ge 1$, every $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$, every $s \ge 0$, and every integer shift $0 \le k \le k_*(y \bmod 9)$ (in particular, for $k = k_*(y \bmod 9)$, which maps $2^{k_*(y)}y \bmod 9$ into the base classes $\{2, 8\}$),*
   $$E_m(y; s) = E_m\!\left(2^k y \bmod 3^{m+2};\, s + k\right) + k, \qquad \Phi_m(y; s) = \Phi_m\!\left(2^k y \bmod 3^{m+2};\, s + k\right).\tag{3.2}$$
2. **(Forward Canonical Step Monotonicity):** *Let $T_{e_*}(y) := (2^{e_*(y)}y - 1)/3 \bmod 3^{m+1}$. Whenever $s + e_*(y) - 2 \ge 0$, $\Phi$ is monotonically non-decreasing along the canonical forward step $T_{e_*}$ (equivalently, non-increasing in remaining depth $m$):*
   $$\Phi_m(y; s) \le \Phi_{m-1}\!\left(T_{e_*}(y);\, s + e_*(y) - 2\right).\tag{3.3}$$

*Proof.*  
(1) Fix $m \ge 1$. For any $0 \le k \le k_*(y \bmod 9)$, every admissible exponent $e$ for $y$ at step $m \to m - 1$ satisfies $e \ge e_*(y \bmod 9) = k_*(y \bmod 9) + 1 \ge k + 1$. Thus the substitution $e' := e - k \ge 1$ is a bijection between admissible exponents $e \ge e_*(y)$ for $(y, s)$ and admissible exponents $e' \ge e_*(2^k y) = e_*(y) - k \ge 1$ for $(2^k y \bmod 3^{m+2}, s + k)$. Under $e = e' + k$, the next state $(2^e y - 1)/3 = (2^{e'}(2^k y) - 1)/3$ and next cushion $s + e - 2 = (s + k) + e' - 2$ are identical, while the first-step excess shifts by $(e - 2) = (e' - 2) + k$. Taking the minimum over $e'$ gives $E_m(y; s) = E_m(2^k y \bmod 3^{m+2};\, s + k) + k$, and adding $s$ gives $\Phi_m(y; s) = \Phi_m(2^k y \bmod 3^{m+2};\, s + k)$. *(Remarks on Sharpness: First, the bound $k \le k_*(y \bmod 9)$ is sharp; shifting past $\{2, 8\} \pmod 9$ fails because $e = 1$ would map to $e' = 0 \notin \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, e.g. at $m = 1$, $E_1(2; 3) = -1 \ne E_1(4; 4) + 1 = 1$. Second, the hypothesis $m \ge 1$ is essential: at the terminal boundary $m = 0$, $E_0(y; s)$ is a static predicate $s \ge 2 \wedge y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9$ with no outgoing step $e$, so shifting $(y, s) = (1, 1) \mapsto (2, 2)$ at $m = 0$ does not hold because $E_0(1; 1) = +\infty$ whereas $E_0(2; 2) + 1 = 1$. Accordingly, in Theorem 3 below, the base step $m = 1$ is evaluated directly against $E_0$, and canonical shifts are applied only at depths $m - 1 \ge 1$, i.e. $m \ge 2$.)*  
(2) Since $e = e_*(y)$ is always admissible and $s + e_*(y) - 2 \ge 0$, choosing $e = e_*(y)$ in (2.3) gives $E_m(y; s) \le (e_*(y) - 2) + E_{m-1}(T_{e_*}(y);\, s + e_*(y) - 2)$. Adding $s$ to both sides cancels $e_*(y) - 2$ against $s' - s = e_*(y) - 2$, proving (3.3). $\square$

### 3.2 Three-Tier Cushion-Cap Superset Hierarchy and the 64-Bit $(\min, +)$ Semiring Homomorphism
By Theorem 2(1), at any depth $m \ge 1$, the uncapped values $E_m(y; s)$ for all $2 \cdot 3^{m+1}$ units $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ are determined in $O(1)$ time by the values $E_m(z; s)$ on the $2 \cdot 3^m$ base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$ ($3^m$ residues $z = 9k + 2$ and $3^m$ residues $z = 9k + 8$, $0 \le k < 3^m$), reducing the state space by a factor of $3\times$. For any base residue $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$, the exponents with $2^e z \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ are the odd integers $e = 2n + 1$ ($n \ge 0$), with step excess $\delta_n := 2n - 1$ and next residue $w_n(z) := (2^{2n+1}z - 1)/3 \bmod 3^{m+1}$ satisfying $w_0(z) = (2z - 1)/3$, $w_{n+1}(z) = 4w_n(z) + 1 \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ (admissible whenever $3 \nmid w_n(z)$).

Fix a cushion ceiling $S_{\max} \ge 9$ (in our C11 implementation, **$S_{\max} = 16$**). When clamping cushions at $S_{\max}$, we distinguish two capped games that sit above $E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s)$:
1. **The Physical-Cushion-Capped Game $E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s)$:** For $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and $s \ge 0$, set $c := \min(S_{\max}, s)$, $E_0^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) := E_0(y; c)$, and for $m \ge 1$:
   $$E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) := \min_{\substack{e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\text{ admissible} \\ c + e - 2 \ge 0}} \left\{ (e - 2) + E_{m-1}^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}\!\left(\frac{2^e y - 1}{3} \bmod 3^{m+1};\, \min(S_{\max},\, c + e - 2)\right) \right\}.\tag{3.4p}$$
   This game clamps only the physical successor cushions $c + e - 2 \mapsto \min(S_{\max}, c + e - 2)$.
2. **The Normalized-Capped Game $E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ (Used by the $(P_2, P_8)$ Tables and Sparse Evaluator):** On the $2 \cdot 3^m$ base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$ and $0 \le s \le S_{\max}$, define $E_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s)$ by separating the base step $m = 1$ (evaluated directly against $E_0$ without mod-$9$ shifts) from the inductive steps $m \ge 2$:
   - **Base Step $m = 1$ ($z \in (\mathbb{Z}/27\mathbb{Z})^\times,\; z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$):**
     $$E_1^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) := \min_{\substack{n \ge 0,\; w_n(z) \bmod 9 \notin \{0, 3, 6, 7\} \\ s + \delta_n \ge 2}} \delta_n \qquad (0 \le s \le S_{\max}).\tag{3.4a}$$
   - **Inductive Step $m \ge 2$ ($z \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times,\; z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$):**
     $$E_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) := \min_{\substack{n \ge 0,\; 3 \nmid w_n(z) \\ s + \delta_n \ge 0}} \left\{ \delta_n + k_*(w_n) + E_{m-1}^{(S_{\max})}\!\left(2^{k_*(w_n)} w_n \bmod 3^{m+1};\, \min\!\bigl(S_{\max},\, \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n) + k_*(w_n)\bigr)\right) \right\},\tag{3.4b}$$
   and for general $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ ($m \ge 1$) and $s \ge 0$, define:
   $$E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s) := k_*(y) + E_m^{(S_{\max})}\!\left(2^{k_*(y)}y \bmod 3^{m+2};\, \min\!\bigl(S_{\max},\, \min(S_{\max}, s) + k_*(y)\bigr)\right).\tag{3.4c}$$

*(Why the two capped games are distinct, and why both over-approximate $E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$: First, even when the final excess is negative, an optimal uncapped path can make an **intermediate cushion excursion** above $S_{\max}$ before descending back to $s \ge 2$; for example, at $S_{\max} = 16, m = 17, y = 863624840 \equiv 5 \pmod 9, s = 16$, the uncapped path $(3, 2, 1^{15})$ reaches physical cushion $17$ at step $1$ and achieves $E_{17}^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; 16) = -14 = 2 - s$, whereas both capped games clamp that excursion to $16$ and achieve $E_{17}^{\mathrm{phys},(16)}(y; 16) = E_{17}^{(16)}(y; 16) = -12$. Second, in (3.4b)–(3.4c), canonical normalization shifts the physical cushion $s_{\mathrm{next}} = \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n)$ by $+k_*(w_n) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ before clamping to $S_{\max}$, which can clamp up to $3$ units earlier than (3.4p); for example, at $S_{\max} = 16, m = 18, y = 1464933724, s = 16$, exact evaluation gives $E_{18}^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; 16) = -14$, $E_{18}^{\mathrm{phys},(16)}(y; 16) = -12$, and $E_{18}^{(16)}(y; 16) = -9$, with corresponding potentials $2 < 4 < 7$. Both effects go strictly in the **safe superset direction** $E_m^{(S_{\max})} \ge E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})} \ge E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$, as we now prove.)*

**Theorem 3 (Three-Tier Cushion-Cap Superset Hierarchy and 64-Bit $(\min, +)$ Semiring Homomorphism):**
1. **(Cushion Monotonicity and Three-Tier Superset Hierarchy):** *For every $m \ge 1$, every $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and every $0 \le s \le s' \le S_{\max}$, the functions $E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s)$, $E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s)$, and $E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ are all monotonically non-increasing in $s$, and satisfy the three-tier pointwise dominance chain:*
   $$E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s) \ge 2 - s \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \Phi_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge \Phi_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge \Phi_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s) \ge 2.\tag{3.5}$$
   *Consequently, defining $\mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})} := \{y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{b+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times : y \not\equiv 7 \pmod 9,\; E_b^{(S_{\max})}(y; 2) \ge 4\}$, we have the provable superset inclusion:*
   $$\mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}} \subseteq \mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})} \subseteq \mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})}.\tag{3.6}$$
   *In particular, any residue $a \notin \mathcal{W}_b^{(S_{\max})}$ satisfies $E_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(a; 2) \le E_b^{(S_{\max})}(a; 2) \le 3$, and therefore cannot belong to $\mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$!*
2. **(Exact $(\min, +)$ Potential Semiring Homomorphism for $E_m^{(S_{\max})}$):** *Writing $\Phi_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) = s + E_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) \in \{2, 3, \dots\} \cup \{+\infty\}$ on base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$ and $0 \le s \le S_{\max}$, the base step (3.4a) gives $\Phi_1^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) = \min_{n \ge 0,\, w_n(z) \bmod 9 \notin \{0,3,6,7\},\, s + \delta_n \ge 2} (s + \delta_n) \ge 2$, and the inductive step (3.4b) for $m \ge 2$ takes the form:*
   $$\Phi_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) = \min_{\substack{n \ge 0,\; 3 \nmid w_n(z) \\ s + \delta_n \ge 0}} \left\{ \Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}}(s, \delta_n, k_*(w_n)) + \Phi_{m-1}^{(S_{\max})}\!\left(2^{k_*(w_n)}w_n \bmod 3^{m+1};\, \min(S_{\max}, s'_n)\right) \right\},\tag{3.7}$$
   *where $s'_n := \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n) + k_*(w_n)$ and the clamping increment $\Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}}(s, \delta_n, k_*(w_n)) := (s + \delta_n + k_*(w_n)) - \min(S_{\max}, s'_n) \ge 0$ is **non-negative** (and equals $0$ whenever $s + \delta_n + k_*(w_n) \le S_{\max}$). Because every potential is $\ge 2$ and every transition weight $\Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}}$ is $\ge 0$, the truncation map $\tau_9(\Phi) := \min(9, \Phi)$ onto $\{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$ (where $9$ represents $\Phi \ge 9$) is an **exact homomorphism of the $(\min, +)$ semiring**:*
   $$\tau_9\bigl(\min(A, B)\bigr) = \min\bigl(\tau_9(A), \tau_9(B)\bigr), \qquad \tau_9(A + \Delta) = \tau_9\bigl(\tau_9(A) + \Delta\bigr) \qquad (\forall\, A, B \ge 2,\; \Delta \ge 0).\tag{3.8}$$
   *Therefore, storing $\mathrm{enc} = \tau_9(\Phi_m^{(16)}(z; s)) - 2 \in \{0, 1, \dots, 7\}$ in $3$ bits per cushion for all $17$ cushions $s \in \{0, \dots, 16\}$ ($51$ bits total in a single `uint64_t` word) computes $\tau_9(\Phi_m^{(16)}(z; s))$ with **zero intermediate truncation error** at every depth $m \ge 1$.*

*Proof.*  
(1) Along any valid $m$-step uncapped path $(e_1, \dots, e_m)$ from $(y, s)$, the terminal cushion satisfies $s_m = s + \sum_{j=1}^m (e_j - 2) \ge 2$, so $\sum_{j=1}^m (e_j - 2) \ge 2 - s$, proving $E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s) \ge 2 - s$. Monotonicity in $s$ and $E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge E_m^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(y; s)$ follow immediately by induction on $m \ge 0$ from $\min(S_{\max}, c + e - 2) \le c + e - 2$. To prove $E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s) \ge E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ for all $m \ge 1$, we first note that for any $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$, $c \in \{0, \dots, S_{\max}\}$, and $0 \le k \le k_*(y \bmod 9)$, writing $e = e' + k$ ($e' \ge 1$) in (3.4p) and using $\min(S_{\max}, \min(S_{\max}, c + k) + e' - 2) \le \min(S_{\max}, c + k + e' - 2)$ together with cushion monotonicity of $E_{m-1}^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}$ gives:
$$E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; c) \le k + E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}\!\left(2^k y \bmod 3^{m+2};\, \min(S_{\max}, c + k)\right).\tag{3.9}$$
At $m = 1$, for base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$ and $0 \le s \le S_{\max}$, (3.4a) and (3.4p) both require $s + \delta_n \ge 2$ (since $\min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n) \ge 2 \iff s + \delta_n \ge 2$ for $S_{\max} \ge 2$), so $E_1^{(S_{\max})}(z; s) = E_1^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(z; s)$; for general $y$ at $m = 1$, (3.4c) and (3.9) with $k = k_*(y)$ give $E_1^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) \le k_*(y) + E_1^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(2^{k_*(y)}y; \min(S_{\max}, c + k_*(y))) = E_1^{(S_{\max})}(y; s)$. Inducting on $m \ge 2$, for any base residue $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$, each branch $n \ge 0$ of (3.4p) transitions to $(w_n(z), s_{\mathrm{next}})$ with $s_{\mathrm{next}} = \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n)$, where by the induction hypothesis and (3.4c):
$$E_{m-1}^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(w_n; s_{\mathrm{next}}) \le E_{m-1}^{(S_{\max})}(w_n; s_{\mathrm{next}}) = k_*(w_n) + E_{m-1}^{(S_{\max})}\!\left(2^{k_*(w_n)}w_n \bmod 3^{m+1};\, \min(S_{\max}, s_{\mathrm{next}} + k_*(w_n))\right).$$
Adding $\delta_n$ and taking the minimum over $n \ge 0$ gives $E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(z; s) \le E_m^{(S_{\max})}(z; s)$ for all base residues $z \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$, and applying (3.9) with $k = k_*(y)$ extends $E_m^{\mathrm{phys},(S_{\max})}(y; s) \le E_m^{(S_{\max})}(y; s)$ to all units $y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+2}\mathbb{Z})^\times$. In `code/verify_paper8.py` (`verify_section3_potential_semiring`), we exhaustively verify (3.5) and exact agreement between the $(P_2, P_8)$ tables and `eval_norm_capped_exact` across all units $y$ and all $17$ cushions $s \in \{0, \dots, 16\}$ (including the clamping boundary $s + k_*(y) > 16$).  
(2) Adding $s$ to both sides of (3.4b) yields (3.7) with $\Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}} = (s + \delta_n - \min(S_{\max}, s + \delta_n)) + (s'_n - \min(S_{\max}, s'_n)) \ge 0$. Because $\Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}} \ge 0$ and $\Phi \ge 2$, any branch with $\Phi_{m-1} \ge 9$ has $\Phi_{m-1} + \Delta_{\mathrm{clamp}} \ge 9$, so replacing $\Phi_{m-1}$ by $\min(9, \Phi_{m-1})$ never alters any value $< 9$. Since witness detection at $s_0 = 2$ tests whether $E_b^{(16)}(a; 2) \ge 4 \iff \Phi_b^{(16)}(2^{k_*(a)}a;\, 2 + k_*(a)) \ge 6 < 9$ (where $2 + k_*(a) \le 5 \le 16$), the 3-bit encoding in `uint64_t` is $100\%$ exact for $\mathcal{W}_b^{(16)}$. $\square$

## 4. Zero-Clamping Depth-First Spine Completeness and Unbounded-Exponent Certification

### 4.1 Single-Step Exponent Bound, Terminal Horizon Lemma, and Zero-Clamping Spine Completeness
To certify $\mathcal{W}_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}$ up to $b_{\max} = 20$ with **zero frontier heap allocations** and **zero spine clamping**, `code/fast_Wb_p2p8.c` builds the dense 64-bit tables $P_2, P_8$ up to base depth $g_{\mathrm{tab\_b}} \in \{13, 14, 15\}$: at depth $b$, each of the two tables $P_2, P_8$ has $N_b = 3^b$ entries of $8\text{ bytes}$ (`uint64_t`), so the retained table pair $(P_2, P_8)$ at $g_{\mathrm{tab\_b}} = 13$ occupies $2 \times 3^{13} \times 8\text{ bytes} = 25.51\text{ MB}$ ($33.9\text{ MB}$ peak process RSS), and at the default $g_{\mathrm{tab\_b}} = 15$ occupies $2 \times 3^{15} \times 8\text{ bytes} = 229.58\text{ MB}$ ($289.2\text{ MB}$ peak process RSS during the $14 \to 15$ transition). Above $g_{\mathrm{tab\_b}}$, `code/fast_Wb_p2p8.c` traverses the sparse inverse spine for $b_{\mathrm{cur}} = g_{\mathrm{tab\_b}} + 1, \dots, 20$ by **depth-first recursion** (`dfs_spine`), evaluating $E_{b_{\mathrm{cur}}}^{(16)}$ directly from $(P_2, P_8)$ at $g_{\mathrm{tab\_b}}$ via the exact recurrence (3.4b)–(3.4c) (`eval_EB_rem_pruned` + `get_EB0`). We now prove the three completeness theorems that make this two-stage verification an exact proof.

**Theorem 4 (Unbounded-Exponent Certification, Terminal Horizon Lemma, and Zero-Clamping Spine Completeness):**
1. **(Unconditional Single-Step Exponent Bound and Fatal Two-Sided Certification):** *Along any valid $r$-step path $(e_1, \dots, e_r)$ from $(a, s)$, every step satisfies $e_j \ge 1 \implies e_j - 2 \ge -1$. Thus, if a path has total excess $\sum_{j=1}^r (e_j - 2) \le \mathrm{exc}_{\max}$, its first step $e_1$ must satisfy $(e_1 - 2) - (r - 1) \le \mathrm{exc}_{\max}$, i.e.,*
   $$e_1 \le \mathrm{exc}_{\max} + r + 1.\tag{4.1}$$
   *In `code/fast_Wb_p2p8.c`, `eval_uncapped_dfs` imposes no fixed upper bound on $e$, terminating its $e$-loop solely when $(e - 2) - (r - 1) \ge \mathrm{best}$. Furthermore, every candidate $a \in \mathcal{W}_b^{(16)}$ is passed to `certify_candidate_exact(a, b)`, which determines the exact uncapped excess $u = E_b^{\mathrm{uncapped}}(a; 2)$ by proving **both** the lower bound `eval_uncapped_dfs(a, 2, b, u + 1) == u` (no path of excess $\le u - 1$ exists) and the explicit path upper bound `has_uncapped_path_le(a, 2, b, u) == 1` (a valid path of excess $\le u$ exists), aborting via `abort()` on any mismatch and recording every candidate in the exact disposition partition $|\mathcal{W}_b^{(16)}| = \#\{\mathrm{exc} \ge 5\} + \#\{\mathrm{exc} = 4\} + \#\{\mathrm{fp} \le 3\}$.*

3. **Sub-Class $a_0 \equiv 26 \pmod{27}$ in Class $8 \pmod 9$:** Let $a \equiv 8 \pmod 9$ at $b = 21, s_0 = 2$. By Theorem 2(2), the canonical first step $e_1 = e_*(8) = 1$ (step excess $-1$, next cushion $s_1 = 1$) gives $E_{21}(a; 2) \le -1 + E_{20}(y_p; 1)$, where $y_p := (2a - 1)/3 \bmod 3^{22} \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ (so $y_p \bmod 9 \in \{2, 5, 8\}$ according as $a \bmod 27 \in \{17, 26, 8\}$). Thus $E_{21}(a; 2) \le E_{20}(2y_p \bmod 3^{22};\, 2) \le 3 < 4$ because $c_1(20) = 0$ in Table 1. *(Note: for $a \equiv 8, 17 \pmod{27}$, $y_p \bmod 9 \in \{8, 2\}$ has $k_*(y_p) = 0$, so Theorem 2(1) does not shift $(y_p, 1)$ to cushion $2$.)*
4. **Sub-Classes $a_0 \equiv 1, 10 \pmod{27}$ in Class $1 \pmod 9$:** Let $a_1 \equiv 1 \pmod 9$ at $b = 21, s_0 = 2$. By Theorem 2(2), the canonical first step $e_1 = e_*(1) = 2$ (step excess $0$, next cushion $s_1 = 2$) gives $E_{21}(a_1; 2) \le E_{20}(y_p; 2)$, where $y_p := (4a_1 - 1)/3 \bmod 3^{22} \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. ## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Scope of the Constructive Lower-Ball Exponent $Q(W_b/b)$:** Theorem 1 proves that $W_{20} = 44$ improves the constructive lower-ball exponent for the stationary joint measure $\nu$ in Section 9.1 of `collatz_19/v2` [1] from $Q_0 = 3\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 7.248619\dots$ to $Q(44/20) = \frac{6}{5}\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 4.171498707456\dots$. As noted in `collatz_19/v2` [1], this is a constructive polynomial lower bound on the joint measure of product balls and does not by itself establish non-existence of non-trivial positive Collatz cycles or universal convergence of integer trajectories.
2. **Asymptotic Limit of $W_b / b$ as $b \to \infty$:** While Table 1 certifies $W_b = 2b + 4$ across $14 \le b \le 20$ with a rapidly decaying witness count ($808, 703, 488, 277, 103, 28, 5$) and Theorem 5 proves $c_4(21) = c_2(21) = 0$ (and eliminates $a_0 \equiv 26, 1, 10 \pmod{27}$), determining whether $c_5(b)$ eventually reaches $0$ at some finite $b \ge 21$ (which would lower $W_b$ to $2b + 3$) remains an open computational and theoretical question.
