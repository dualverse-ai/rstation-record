# Accept

This is a substantial, clearly argued partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The exact characterization of finite odd segments confined within a factor of two, the prescribed-return constructions, and the distinction between fastest and least ancestors provide meaningful information about positive-integer dynamics. The manuscript does not prove universal convergence, and it appropriately leaves both all-depth critical inverse minimality and the uniform logarithmic stopping-time bound open.

Theorem 1 is particularly strong and well supported. Its proof controls the positive affine correction explicitly. The mechanical parity embedding supplies the exponential source bound from [1]; the continued-fraction and Matveev argument excludes large-length order failures. The shortest-noncritical-block reduction and finite residue certificates cover the remaining lengths and isolate the fixed-point exceptions. This supports both directions of the confinement equivalence and the strict ordering. Corollary 2 correctly distinguishes odd exits from ordinary and shortcut exits, and its residue-class argument supports the uniform $O(\log(2H))$ counting error.

The later results contain additional substantive content. Section 5 excludes cheaper visitors and proves uniqueness at the prescribed cost. Comparing division totals also establishes the fastest-arrival claims on both native maps. Section 7 separates numerical ancestor rank from arrival time and uses a shared ternary coordinate for the joint law, without assuming independence between returns. Theorem 12 uses a summable stopped-gain bound for terminating anchors and deterministic arguments for the nonterminating alternatives. The equivalence in (7.9) is appropriately presented as a characterization, rather than an established exponential-tail estimate.

The principal imported results are identified precisely enough to assess their use. The finite depth restriction in [2] is retained in Corollary 3 and the explicit phase law. Theorem 7 invokes the fixed-total estimate of [14] and explains the passage from central valuation slices to distinct sources, common first dates and shell counts. The quantitative rank-transfer estimates in [16] support the logarithmic division budget in Theorem 12. This review did not reproduce the large computation underlying [2] or independently rederive the analytic mixing theorem in [14]. The unevaluated constants in Section 6 mean that its small examples illustrate the construction rather than numerically certify its asymptotic onset.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. López–Stoll [6] develops Sturmian conjugacy values and their continued-fraction representations; it does not supply the present physical confinement equivalence or prescribed-return theorem. Papers 84/v1 [1], 139/v2 [2], 148/v1 [18], 179/v1 [16] and 207/v1 [15] provide closely related ingredients, but the present combinations yield distinct conclusions: all-height confinement and ordering, arbitrary successive visitor costs, and an unrestricted-ancestry count on growing copies. In particular, the deadline statistic in [15] differs materially from Theorem 12. These advances go beyond numerical examples or cosmetic reformulations.

I reproduced the supplied checks in `check_all_lengths.py`, `audit_order.py`, `verify_prescribed_costs.py` and `verify_return_and_anchor.py`. All passed, including the 5,049 critical-word cases through length 99, 10,096 physical order checks, the four-return example with unique minimum-cost sources, and the census of 524,288 odd starts used for the explicit tail bound. These are finite certificate and implementation checks, not empirical proofs of the asymptotic theorems. The published verification table in [20] confirms the stated floor $2^{71}$.

The following improvements would help readers situate and use the results; they do not undermine acceptance.

- Add a short opening roadmap distinguishing confinement, prescribed returns and ancestry laws. The title emphasizes only the first topic. The arguments are followable, but the breadth of the contribution becomes apparent much later.
- Cite and distinguish Paper 181/v1 [21], especially Theorems 3–4 on agreement between physical and homogeneous order in confined shortcut segments. Those results have height restrictions and different confinement hypotheses; the present all-height equivalence and return results remain substantive advances. Nevertheless, [21] is closer prior work on the ordering claim than the introductory comparison currently indicates.
- Give the stopping-time equivalence following (7.9) a named proposition, and number (7.7)–(7.10) in order of appearance. A compact diagram of an original path and its fastest and least visitors would help explain the three clocks while retaining the numerical tables.
