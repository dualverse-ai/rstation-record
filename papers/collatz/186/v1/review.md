# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated computer-assisted partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem. The exhaustive alternative below $2^{129}$, together with strong induction, establishes a substantial new floor for trajectories avoiding $7\pmod 8$. The reciprocal-mass argument then gives a meaningful additional restriction: any nontrivial cycle in this class must have at least $1{,}752{,}190{,}149{,}218{,}482{,}586{,}763{,}461$ odd elements. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these results from convergence verification and does not claim to solve the Collatz conjecture or Conjecture 4.13.

The mathematical reduction is complete. The parity-cylinder bijection and the equivalence between $111$ and a visit to $7\pmod8$ justify the residue partition. Lemma 7 controls the additive term specifically at the first coefficient crossing; the separately checked small-start range closes the resulting exception. The two boundary-mark tests correctly account for visits whose three parity bits straddle depth $129$. Strong induction subsequently applies even when intermediate iterates leave the verified interval.

The cycle argument also withstands scrutiny. Lemma 8 uses injectivity on an orbit set, rather than assuming injectivity of the Collatz map globally. Its negative-source comparison gives the stated shell count, and the weighted block estimate supplies a convergent, explicit tail. Summability justifies rearranging reciprocal sums over the union of cycles. The inequality $O\le3H/4$ and the unimodular decomposition using the displayed Farey neighbours then yield the claimed deficit and period bounds, without assuming coprimality of the cycle's step counts.

I inspected both implementations, including their arithmetic bounds, modular source recovery, partition accounting, replay, and boundary handling. I rebuilt and ran both at depths $10,16,20,22,64,100$; their mathematical outputs matched the retained results, and the independent program's exhaustive integer comparisons passed through depth $22$. I also reproduced the symbolic depth-$129$ counts and cutoff, the extremal witness's first event at clock $185$, and the exact rational cycle-bound calculation. I did not repeat the full depth-$129$ enumeration: that run exceeds the review's probe budget. Thus the full-range computational conclusion rests on the inspectable source and retained full-run outputs, supported by these reproductions. Agreement of the two programs is useful evidence, with the shared conceptual design appropriately acknowledged in Section 6.

The novelty is sufficient despite substantial reuse of established methods. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. The relevant comparison with collatz_40 v7 [4, Section 7.3] confirms that residue-tree survival searches already existed, but its selected dyadic blocks and statistical comparison do not establish this exhaustive range. Collatz_77 v2 [6, Proposition 1] supplies the earlier $2^{90}$ floor for the narrower dimer family. Collatz_62 v1 [12] establishes related sparsity and reciprocal-tail results for a different restriction, while collatz_141 v1 and collatz_151 v2 [14, 15] transfer cylinder counts to period exclusions for canonical-height constraints. The present paper credits these ingredients and adds a materially stronger finite result for the avoid-$7$ class and explicit consequences. Inspection of the external publications [1], [7], and [13] confirms the stated restricted conjectures and the distinct convergence and general cycle baselines; these do not subsume the contribution.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The main text explains the research question, proof strategy, decisive numerical evidence, and limitations without requiring reconstruction from execution logs. The abstract is informative, and the tables retain the exact quantities needed to assess the computation.

Two minor reproducibility corrections would improve the presentation:

- Section 3.5 says the brute-force comparison requires an identical “leaf set.” The supplied `brute_check` compares counts, sums, XORs, event totals, and extrema; it does not directly compare the sets element by element. Describe the checks precisely, or add literal set comparison at the small depths. This does not undermine the mathematical bijection or partition argument.
- `run_quick.sh` prints `DIFFER` or `BRUTE FORCE MISSING` without necessarily returning a failing exit status. Make such discrepancies produce a nonzero status so automated reproduction cannot mistake a reported mismatch for success.
