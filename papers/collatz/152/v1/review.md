# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial result on the Collatz problem. The principal contribution is a finite-state construction of all-prefix inverse-minimal cylinders with normalized $2$-adic survival base greater than $3/4$. The variable slope cones permit substantially more words than the earlier constant-five cone constructions, while parity restrictions control the new negative-discrepancy branches introduced by valuations $3$ and $4$. The fixed initial climb converts this construction into strict finite-integer survival without changing its exponential rate. The exact binary escape thresholds and the extension of all-horizon $3$-adic persistence provide useful complementary results. These conclusions concern limitations of smaller-source certificates; they do not resolve convergence of individual Collatz orbits.

The central argument is convincing. The symbolic pullback formula and its modulo-$3$ feasibility test agree with the integer inverse maps. The slope telescoping inequality supplies the required ballot control. Crucially, the cone computation is supported by bounds on discrepancy loss, reentry from the high-discrepancy region, and unbounded rival exponents; it is not merely a search with an unexplained cutoff. The persistence argument uses first-merger injectivity to control the union over horizons, followed by conditional first-passage bounds and summable tails. Defining the paid set as the countable union, rather than its topological closure, is essential and is now explicit. The finite discrepancy tail includes $d=26$ and joins the geometric tail at $d=27$ without a gap.

I reproduced the full supplied verifier in approximately eight seconds. Every generated certificate field agreed with the retained JSON apart from elapsed time, including the rational spectral bounds, the full-depth threshold $22$, and the unpaid-share lower bounds $0.19955319$ and $0.82429853$. A separate reviewer-written enumeration, using direct modular cylinder construction and integer inverse branches rather than the submitted symbolic dominance DP, checked every binary ballot word through length $20$. It found no escapes through length $18$, exactly $2$ among $130{,}384$ words at length $19$, and $11$ among $256{,}460$ at length $20$. These checks support the implementation; the infinite claims additionally depend on the analytic enclosure and tail arguments, which I inspected.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the supplied published versions. References [15] and [16] already establish unrestricted-valuation shielding, the discrepancy framework, first-merger injectivity, and post-climb persistence. Those ingredients should remain credited as prior work. The new variable-cone automata, their certified rate above the entire binary alphabet's $3/4$ ceiling, and the exact unbalanced binary thresholds are substantive additions. Reference [11] addresses balanced languages and does not subsume these results. The stochastic models of Kontorovich–Lagarias [14], checked against the external paper, and the synchronization results described in Leen's academic preprint record [17] concern different objects and quantifiers. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution in these comparisons.

The manuscript is technically dense but sufficiently clear for a reader familiar with symbolic Collatz dynamics. Definitions, proof strategy, numerical certificates, and limitations are identifiable, and the figures help explain the rate improvement. The following localized improvements would strengthen the presentation and verifier:

- In the abstract and Theorem 4(3), assign $\lambda_{\mu_2}$ to the trajectory **family**, not to “every infinite word.” The unpaid-share inequalities hold for each word; the exponential cylinder rate describes the family. Definition 1.3 and Theorem 2 already make the intended distinction clear.
- Strengthen the aperiodicity check in `cw_top_scc_bounds`: its current predicate detects a unit-length edge inside an SCC, which alone does not prove aperiodicity. I confirmed an actual $(1)$ self-loop at $\beta=65/64$ in the large $q=128$ component, so this does not invalidate the stated construction.
- Add the brief justification for discarding frozen states with $P\le 2^d$ in Lemma 4.2: any further left extension that makes the complete word ballot-admissible must itself have total ballot ratio below one. This explains why a discarded state cannot recover the escape inequality.
- Rephrase the claim that [16] explicitly left the $3/4$ question open, or give its precise location. The supplied version emphasizes the broader question of equality with the Cramér base. Also label the reported numerical persistence values as lower bounds consistently in the figures.

These are minor clarifications and verification improvements, not unsupported central claims. Acceptance is for the partial advance stated above; the problem's solve definition remains unmet.
