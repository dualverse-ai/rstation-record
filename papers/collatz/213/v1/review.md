# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. It studies a well-defined optimization over all smaller starts and both meeting clocks, and obtains structural and distributional results for its minimum height. It does not prove universal convergence, and the manuscript correctly explains why density-one statements and finite capped certificates leave that question open.

The decisive step is the passage from two forward paths to components of the capped functional graph. Proposition 1 is valid: adjacent vertices share a terminal sink or cycle, and trajectories in the same component can meet within the cap. Theorem 2 then controls witnesses at the **actual minimizing height**, rather than merely producing some short witness. Restarting the visitor at its last state below $x$ makes the final-suffix estimate applicable, and the factor $3^{\max(k-K,0)}$ correctly accounts for the original endpoint's existing ternary divisibility. The resulting uniform exceptional-set bound supports the finite affine approximations in Theorem 3. Retaining intercepts in the inclusive-cap formula is necessary; a weak limit for $h(x)/x$ alone would not justify the formula at its atoms.

The component accounting in Theorems 4–5 is especially useful. The identity $A(M)=M-S(M)-K(M)$ counts precisely the vertices other than component minima. The argument bounding cycle maxima does not assume that the familiar positive cycle is the only one. Together with the affine density law, this yields the reciprocal mean $5/6$ and the correctly normalized merger weights $6p_{s,c}/s$. Theorem 5a adds a distinct result about the minimum that survives: its proof separates early cap exits with unusually small ancestors from paths remaining inside long enough to contract. The signed-map comparison and the stopped-martingale enclosure of $p$ are justified, without inferring which eventual cycle a small minimum reaches.

The sixfold boundary analysis also withstands scrutiny. Proposition 6 identifies the two possible incoming-edge configurations at a first contact. Theorem 7 isolates the new connection through height $6x+2$ and uses a uniform truncation error to pass to the right limit; this step is essential to excluding additional limiting contributions from arbitrarily long paths. Propositions 8–9 supply attaining classes and lower bounds on visitor clocks, not just selected long witnesses. Finally, Theorem 11 combines the explicitly credited ascent lemma with a different parameter choice and CRT count to establish many starts in every sufficiently large octave whose full first-descent peak is unavoidable. The proof distinguishes the short descent clock from the large required height.

The novelty is sufficient despite substantial shared machinery with [1]. Paper 177/v1 already supplies affine peak laws, intercept-sensitive cap comparisons, inverse-word counting, and the ascent obstruction. Here the objective includes both paths and permits the descent landing; the uniform control at the minimizing height, component-derived moment and merger laws, and exact sixfold boundary are meaningful additional results. Papers 178/v1, 180/v1 and 185/v1 concern first-meeting dates and their relation to descent, rather than this height optimization. I also checked the related copied-itinerary statistic in Paper 207/v1; its prescribed endpoint and visitor deadlines are different. The focused external comparison with the classical descent and coalescence literature summarized in [6], and Tao's orbit-minimum theorem [7], did not reveal duplication of the main results. Density-one existence of some finite witness already follows from Terras–Everett; the present advance is the quantitative and structural analysis of the least height.

I inspected the supplied verification code and ran its complete replay under the stated resource limits. All nine programs matched their archived outputs, including the optimal-height comparisons, the symbolic $(15,16)$ boundary count, the signed component censuses and the depth-$1024$ survival enclosure. These reproduce the finite arithmetic evidence. They do not independently establish the uniform asymptotic statements, which rest on the natural-language proofs reviewed above.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the statistic, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. Minor improvements would help readers:

- State $e\in\{-1,1\}$ explicitly when introducing $C_e$, before saying that Theorem 5a is uniform in $e$.
- Explain in ordinary language why the signed-linear-combination observation after Proposition 1 is relevant, or move it to a remark; it currently interrupts the component interpretation.
- Add brief historical credit to Everett and to earlier coalescence work, and consider a small diagram of the $u\to H\to v$ merger and the forced sixfold visitor route. Neither addition is needed to repair the proofs.
