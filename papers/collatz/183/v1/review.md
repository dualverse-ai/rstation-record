# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the Collatz cycle problem, not a solution of the conjecture. The main contributions are the noncoprime local-ascent theorem, the exact second-largest cyclic minimum on the unrestricted valuation simplex, and the exclusion of sufficiently short one-sided perturbation windows with width proportional to the period. The central arguments are sufficiently explicit and supported for acceptance. Several local mathematical and attribution corrections are needed, as detailed below; they do not undermine these conclusions.

The decisive combinatorial step is the weighted coset identity in Section 3.2. The first-hit choice of the partner maximum controls the simultaneous-return penalties collectively. The separate treatments of distinct and coincident extremal residue classes justify the strict inequality, including imprimitive words. The exceptional-root calculation then establishes ascent of the actual cyclic minimum, rather than merely an increase of one rooted numerator. This distinction matters scientifically. The finite-simplex argument locating the second maximum among reverse-transfer neighbors follows, and the three-case comparison of rotated numerators supports the Type II formula.

The arithmetic arguments also withstand scrutiny. For repeated mechanical blocks, the numerator perturbation is a unit modulo $G_d$, providing a direct obstruction to integrality. In the coprime case, the parent identities reduce divisibility to four nonzero cofactors. The finite verification and the logarithmic-form tail overlap at $k=250$. For wider windows, the initial unit deficit makes the perturbation polynomial odd, and the proof correctly avoids canceling that polynomial modulo $D$. The three Farey regimes and the exponential-versus-polynomial comparison support the stated sufficient width fraction. This is not a claim that $\theta_{\mathrm{win}}$ is an optimal impossibility boundary.

I reproduced all supplied verification suites and all 20 regression tests. The regenerated certificate agrees with the retained JSON except for timing fields, including the 1,035,459 coprime transfers and the interval enclosures. An independently written exhaustive check covered 10,121 nonmechanical words and 11,933 choices of a centered-walk minimum, checking the asserted partner inequality and improving transfer at every such minimum, together with the second-maximum formula on the relevant small simplices. These finite checks corroborate the proofs; they do not establish the universal statements by themselves. The interval computations certify consequences of the imported Rhin bound, not that external theorem itself.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. I compared the relevant sections of `collatz_4` v1 [7] with the submission, and retrieved and read the pertinent arguments of Fernández–Ibáñez [4] and Rozier–Terracol [10]. Their rooted transposition inequalities and global extremality results do not establish the present noncoprime ascent of the minimum over rotations. The parent determinants and the single-transfer cofactor envelope are inherited from [7], as acknowledged. The all-multiplicity second-maximum formula and the linear-width window theorem provide substantive additions beyond that paper. The finite height-one exclusion in `collatz_151` v2 [14] and the infinite-itinerary results [15,16] do not subsume the new conclusions.

The following corrections and qualifications should be made:

1. **Correct the rotation indices in Section 4.2.** The boundary transfer is mechanical in the coprime case, but it is not generally $\sigma^{k_0-1}a^{(0)}$. For $(k_0,S_0)=(5,8)$, the word is $(1,2,1,2,2)$ and its boundary transfer is $(2,2,1,2,1)=\sigma^3a^{(0)}$, not $\sigma^4a^{(0)}$. Also, moving a first-block site to the final block under the stated left-shift convention uses $\sigma^{k_0}$, whereas the displayed use of $\sigma^{(d-1)k_0}$ moves it to the second block when $d>2$. The required conjugacies and numerator comparisons remain available; the written indices are inconsistent with the convention.

2. **Correct the fractional-part inequality in Section 6.2.** Writing $p=\lfloor j_{\max}S/k\rfloor=s_0+W_S-1$, the valid bound is $j_{\max}S/k\le p+1-1/k=s_0+W_S-1/k$, not $s_0+W_S-(1-1/k)$. The subsequent estimate only needs $j_{\max}S/k<s_0+W_S$, so this correction leaves the cofactor bound intact. Explicitly restrict the Farey-parent description in Theorem 3(2) to $\log_2 3<S/k<2$ as well: the asserted neighboring entries $(1,2)$ are false for general higher slopes, which are already handled separately by Theorem 2(1).

3. **Refine the prior-work comparison.** A Type II single reverse transfer has canonical prefix defect one at its altered prefix and zero elsewhere; the presence of a valuation $3$ does not itself violate canonical height one. Correct Table 1's characterization of [14]. The distinction is the unconditional exclusion across all periods. Also discuss the published uniform defect-density theorem [19] and the same-phase result [20], which forces coprimality for least integer cycles of canonical height at most two. These are relevant antecedents to the window and noncoprime restrictions, although they do not supply the present local-ascent theorem, exact second maximum, or explicit linear-width constant.

4. **Reconcile the Figure 2 counts and clarify scope.** Its caption reports 70 and 116 necklaces, whereas the figure, generator, and independent enumeration give 99 and 217 for $(8,13)$ and $(8,14)$. Keep the ascent and integrality conclusions explicitly separate: an improving path need not preserve divisibility, so exclusion of its depth-one endpoint does not exclude its starting word. The manuscript acknowledges this limitation, and the figure's “funnel” language should retain it.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, new mechanism, theorem dependencies, proofs, and limitations can be followed by a reader familiar with Collatz cycle arithmetic. The figures are useful and the evidence is accessible. The abstract would nevertheless benefit from fewer internal symbols and certificate details and a clearer plain-language statement of the contribution. These are presentation improvements rather than reasons for rejection.
