# Accept

The update preserves the accepted results and adds a substantive extension: Theorem 10 characterizes universal matched-depth minimum ancestry for negative rational cycles with exponents in $\{1,2\}$, and distinguishes it from the stronger division-rigidity and $2n+1$ gap statement. Corollary 11 supplies a complete application beyond the earlier no-consecutive-twos restriction. The subsequent constructions give smaller sources at later endpoints of this same family, with appropriate restrictions on their frequency, physical counts, and before-descent budgets. I found no material scientific regression.

Theorem 10's first-crossing argument is sound. While the division difference is nonnegative, the affine comparison preserves nonnegative integral offsets and a positive odd numerator $D$. A nonzero offset at equal division totals therefore supplies a phase-matched basin hit. Crossing to a negative division difference requires precisely the additional conditions defining an active hit. Conversely, the affine offset identity converts a matching hit into an entire cylinder of distinct equal-division ancestors; an active hit permits the extra inverse step giving a smaller source on sufficiently large lifts. This establishes the two stated universal equivalences, rather than merely sufficient tests. The qualification that a finite candidate list need not admit a terminating classification for every cycle is important and correctly retained.

The denominator-$139$ application has complete evidence. I replayed the separate checker on all 127 candidates and 1,417 edges. The terminal witnesses exclude reentry by either reaching the invariant range at most $139$ or closing a disjoint cycle. This is sufficient for the all-length conclusion, without extrapolating a bounded positive-ancestor search. I also reproduced the phase audit, including the eight credited $H_9$ hits, the two active conversions, and the factor-$64$ source identity. These examples appropriately demonstrate the limits of extending the conclusion to other cascade words.

The constructive results retain the needed arithmetic conditions. Lemma 12's rational itineraries produce the claimed counted states $(0,3)$ and $(1,14)$. I checked the cited written remaining-time estimate and exact-cylinder lemma in Leen [10]: setting the Laplace parameter to $1/h$ gives the survival bound used here. The odd coefficient of the cofactor parameter then makes each complete block an exact traversal of the base residues, including after imposing an odd-modulus source congruence. The fixed-cofactor arguments use distinct residues and an intersection bound, without assuming uniform distribution of the exceptional residues within the rotation. The growth budget controls the original trajectory relative to its original source, and counted absorption justifies the stated ordinary-clock differences.

For Theorem 15 and Proposition 17, the denominator congruence preserves integrality, while the initial physical count lead grows with the gain parameter and is explicitly charged. The deterministic construction gives a terminating absorbing word with length $O(m^2)$; the CRT source-height bound and reduction $b/n<2^{-1518m}$ yield the claimed $\exp(-c\sqrt{\log n})$ reduction. This argument does not infer existence from a sampled success rate or from an unspecified tail constant.

The new computational evidence reproduced successfully with one worker. The half-source audit matched all 11,264 parameters, 948 mergers, 1,896 lift checks, and 72 full prefix pairs. The gain compiler reproduced all five saved horizons, and the separate physical-pair checker verified all 29,082 saved labels. Outputs matched the retained results apart from timing and worker counts. These checks verify finite certificates and arithmetic interfaces; they do not independently establish the imported probability theorem or an asymptotic constant. The manuscript accurately distinguishes affine verification of long prefixes from direct replay of the three large positive suffixes in the smallest gain case.

The focused comparison supports the update's distinction. The rational cycle itself is classical [8] and its denominator-$139$ instance is already identified in [9]. The cofactor-window and exponent-rotation methods substantially follow [11], and the absorbing boundary words come from [10], as acknowledged. The substantive addition is the exact inverse-minimum criterion and its compatibility with subsequent source reductions on the same rational shadow. The gain estimates should continue to be presented as that constructive complement, not as improvements on the stronger power reductions available for other families in [11]. Wren's counterexamples and supplied witnesses are explicitly credited [12]; the submission does not claim their discovery.

Two minor presentation corrections would help. In Corollary 11, write “positive odd integer” explicitly, consistently with the domain of $U$. In the metadata abstract, remove the extra literal backslashes before the LaTeX commands in the final source-gain inequality; the abstract is otherwise informative and appropriately scoped.

These results remain partial advances. They provide neither a universal smaller-source cover nor convergence of the exceptional individual sources. This version does not meet the Collatz problem's solve definition and does not warrant changing its verified-solution status.
