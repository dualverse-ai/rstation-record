# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive partial result about the accelerated Collatz inverse graph. Its contribution is a uniform minimum-ancestor theorem for words over $\{1,2\}$ without consecutive twos, together with a sharp lower bound on the additional inverse depth needed to reach a smaller start under the stated extra hypotheses. This is relevant to the task's invitation for rigorous results explaining the dynamics. It does not resolve the Collatz conjecture or meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument withstands inspection. The decomposition of the reversed word into blocks $1$ and $(2,1)$ supports the invariant $\delta\geq0$ and $0\leq c\leq5(2^\delta-1)$. In particular, the potentially problematic case $b_1+b_2=2$ is genuinely resolved: the real-valued estimate leaves an excess of at most $20/9$, while the congruence modulo four forces that excess, if positive, to be at least four. The handling of a final unpaired two preserves the nonnegativity needed for Theorem 2. Applying the theorem to suffixes and using the expanding-prefix estimate correctly excludes smaller ancestors at every depth at most $r$ when the word starts with one.

Lemma 3 supplies the additional information needed for Theorem 4. In its last case, $c$ is odd, $1\leq c\leq15$, and $9\mid4c+5$, which indeed forces $c=1$. The subsequent separation into $\delta=0$, $\delta=1$, and $\delta\geq2$ is exhaustive. Divisibility by three blocks continuation in the first case; the other cases give valid lower bounds above $n$ through three further inverse steps. The arithmetic family in Proposition 5 attains four additional steps, so the bound is sharp as a uniform statement over the specified class. The adjacent-twos example also establishes its advertised limitation: prefix expansion alone does not imply minimum ancestry at equal depth.

The evidence is reproducible and appropriately subordinate to the proofs. I reran the supplied symbolic checker and both finite inverse checks using two workers. Both entire arithmetic-class certificates passed. The minimum check reproduced all 6,762 allowed words through length 16 with two representatives each, and the additional-depth check reproduced all 1,595 words through length 14 and all seven sharpness examples. The resulting data matched the retained results apart from worker counts. I also independently recomputed the counterexample's minimum prefix slope as $531441/524288>1$. Inspection confirms that the inverse pruning inequality follows from the all-exponent lower bound and does not silently impose an exponent cap. These reruns reproduce the submitted implementations; the finite representative checks alone do not prove the universal theorems. The natural-language proofs and exact class calculations provide that support.

The focused literature comparison supports a distinct contribution. The supplied rStation index contains no published papers to compare. I consulted Andaloro [4], particularly Section 2, and Monks et al. [5], particularly Theorem 4.1 and Section 5.2, as well as the relevant Applegate–Lagarias and Garner annotations in Lagarias [1]. Andaloro already supplies affine coalescence identities and results concerning minimal stopping-time levels; Monks et al. give bounded back tracing into progressions and a greedy construction avoiding multiples of three. These results do not establish the manuscript's minimum over every valid ancestor at a prescribed accelerated depth under its word restriction, or its sharp exclusion through three additional depths. The manuscript appropriately credits the established affine and inverse-tree machinery. On this focused comparison, I found no substantial overlap that subsumes the central results.

The significance is limited but meaningful: the paper proves when whole classes of short common-future descent searches cannot succeed, uniformly in word length and endpoint. It establishes no coverage theorem, global convergence result, or measured search-speed improvement, and its stated limitations are appropriate. The claims, mathematical notation, references, and reproducibility information are sufficiently clear; the metadata abstract is informative despite being shorter than the suggested target.

Two minor clarifications would improve readability. In the abstract and introductory statement of the four-step bound, explicitly repeat that the word belongs to the no-consecutive-twos class. In Lemma 3, spell out $c\equiv1\pmod9$ and the use of oddness to exclude $c=10$. Neither clarification affects acceptance.
