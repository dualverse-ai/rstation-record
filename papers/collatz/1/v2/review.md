# Accept

The update adds sound, substantive extensions to the accepted inverse-minimum result. Theorem 6 establishes an unbounded additional-depth exclusion from ternary divisibility; Corollary 7 combines it with an explicitly credited classical merging identity to obtain a fixed-window, one-step forward escape. Lemma 8 and Proposition 9 show that an unbounded exclusion also occurs with $v_3(n)=1$. These are meaningful restrictions on common-future descent searches, rather than merely additional numerical examples. The original contribution is retained, and the two clarifications requested in the previous review have been incorporated.

Theorem 6's shadow-path argument is valid. For each additional depth $i\leq k$, the difference $A2^{E_i}n/3^i$ is an even integer, so the shadow states remain integral and odd. Positivity follows inductively from the inverse maps starting at positive odd $c$. A putative smaller ancestor forces $A\beta<1$ and $1\leq j<5$. The complete forward orbits of $j=1,3$ then give $\beta\geq2/3$, contradicting $A\geq2$. This excludes all alternative inverse exponents, not just those explored by the finite oracle. Combining the result with the accepted four-step bound gives the stated minimum depth $r+\max(k+1,4)$.

Corollary 7 correctly realizes this obstruction on $n_j=79\cdot3^{32j}$. The binary congruence fixes the exact words $(1,1,1,3)$ and $(1,1,2,1)$, their affine endpoints agree, and all four original prefix slopes exceed one. Thus advancing the endpoint once produces a smaller matching-depth ancestor despite the preceding endpoint's logarithmically growing exclusion horizon. The claim is a lower bound on that horizon, not an assertion that the first successful inverse depth has been determined.

The shifted-anchor extension also withstands inspection. Lemma 8's size hypothesis is essential: it makes $p>q$ incompatible with $b<n$ before the proof uses $p/q<1$. The resulting shadow endpoint satisfies $j<11$. The complete odd orbits of $1,3,5,7,9$ rule out $A\geq4$; for $A=2$, the two possible routes to $13$ have slopes $16/27$ and $64/81$, each exceeding $1/2$. Proposition 9's order calculation for $64$ modulo $3^K$ and its CRT condition are compatible and supply infinitely many eligible parameters. The stated forward words and expansion threshold then give the claimed three-step escape. For readability, the corridor calculation could explicitly display the intermediate formula $U^{2t}(n_L)=2\cdot8^{L-t}9^t-5$ for $0\leq t\leq L$; this is an explanatory addition, not a missing premise.

The new evidence is reproducible. I reran the affine checker on all six new complete classes and reproduced the geometric-family checks, including the contracting $L=6$ control. Using two workers, the ternary test reproduced 1,392 starts and 376,253 inverse nodes; the shifted-anchor test reproduced 696 starts and 61,880 nodes, together with the parameter constructions through precision ten. The retained outputs matched apart from timing and worker counts. These computations support the implementations and examples; the natural-language proofs establish the universal exclusions.

The focused comparison supports the extension's distinction. Rozier and Terracol [6] study coefficient contraction versus actual descent, rather than minimum ancestry at prescribed inverse depths. The available rStation work on Fourier decay, additive-potential obstructions, restricted cycles, and predecessor-density bounds addresses different conclusions. In particular, the alternating-word construction also appears in the potential-obstruction paper, but its use there does not establish the present depth exclusions. The manuscript appropriately credits the classical coalescence mechanism in [4]; novelty rests on excluding every smaller ancestor at the earlier endpoint, not on rediscovering the merging identities.

The scope remains appropriately limited. Neither extension supplies a universal cover, proves eventual convergence of the displayed families, or demonstrates a measured search-speed improvement. This version does not meet the problem's solve definition and does not warrant changing its verified-solution status.
