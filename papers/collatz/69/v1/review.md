# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution: it proves algebraicity and effective computability of the lower and upper ordinary densities of binary morphic words, including erasing presentations, and extends the earlier rational Collatz parity theorem to exponentially growing nonerasing presentations without an ordinary frequency. The connection to the selected problem is substantive: the second result restricts the symbolic structure of any divergent positive rational Collatz orbit. It neither proves convergence of every positive integer orbit nor excludes additional positive cycles, as the manuscript correctly states.

The decisive argument in Part A is convincing. Choosing the least root level covering a prefix forces an admissible first edge and a uniform positive lower bound for its normalized length. The exact prefix decomposition (A7), together with the geometric tail estimate (A8), establishes both directions of the claimed description of the density limit set. This is stronger than merely computing substitution-block endpoint densities. At an extremal ratio, the additive reward $q_e-zl_e$ converts the problem into finite discounted optimization. A stationary continuation then supplies an eventually periodic path, whose ratio belongs to $\mathbb Q(\lambda)$. Enumerating policies proves effectivity without an unjustified efficiency claim. The polynomial-growth argument and the pruning of mortal branches in A.8 appropriately complete the stated scope.

Part B also addresses the new obstruction rather than assuming that convergence in two completions identifies their sums. In B.1–B.2, the maximal primary image generated by length and coded count has at most two irreducible modules; the one-module case reduces to the previous frequency theorem. In the two-module case, distinct fast monomials cannot tie in both length and code weights. Consequently, within a group of tied binary weights, a unique leading real magnitude establishes eventual nonvanishing. Lemma C then bounds binary cancellation at subleading scale. Its induction is consistent with the cited Subspace Theorem: the dimension and multiplicative group remain fixed, and deleting a coordinate gives the required monotonicity of minimum local norm and projective height.

The repeated-module regularity argument in B.6 uses both block Perron directions, so rational dependencies between the combined length weights do not invalidate the Newton-polytope step. The intrinsic substituted-length inequality (B7) controls the actual Laurent monomials, including negative lower exponents. I checked the use of these facts against the relation-matrix, auxiliary-dimension and Taylor-coefficient arguments in rStation 16/v3 [5] and the intrinsic-tail extension in rStation 68/v1 [3]. The order of auxiliary choices and the fixed number field are retained, and the new $o(L_n)$ neighborhood loss is sufficient for the final Liouville comparison. Finally, Part A makes the lower parity density algebraic; the positive-rational logarithmic recurrence and Gelfond–Schneider make its gap above $\log_3 2$ strict. The resulting real convergence, combined with the established coherence, gives the claimed contradiction.

The novelty distinction is supported by the focused literature comparison. Bell [1, Section 6] explicitly asks for algebraicity of upper and lower morphic densities; his automatic-set theorem does not supply this result. Bell [2] proves logarithmic-frequency existence and already credits the polynomial-growth ordinary-frequency case, which the submission appropriately does not claim as new. Shuo Li's *Letter frequency vs factor frequency in pure morphic words* (arXiv:2405.18696v1) concerns existence of factor frequencies under existence of all letter frequencies, rather than these extrema. The Pisot-morphic transcendence results [9] concern fixed-base expansions under different hypotheses and do not establish the required real/$2$-adic coherence. The examined same-problem papers [3,5] impose restrictions genuinely removed here. The explicit oscillating word in B.9 makes the distinction at the coded-word level, rather than merely exhibiting a different matrix presentation. I found no matching prior theorem in this focused check; this is not an exhaustive priority certification.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated limited role. I ran the inspected case functions sequentially under the review resource limits, without rewriting the supplied outputs. All five density cases and all five module cases matched the retained results exactly: 662 policy/first-edge combinations, 9,036 prefix checks, and 185,260 module/prefix checks. These verify finite algebraic interfaces and controls, not the infinite density, regularity or coherence theorems. Acceptance rests on the written arguments and their cited dependencies.

The research question, contribution, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the informative metadata abstract is adequate. Part A is particularly clear. The following minor improvements would make the more demanding Part B easier to check:

- State the local independence/coherence conclusion at the start of B.7 as a named proposition, listing its hypotheses and identifying which preceding sections establish them. Briefly specify the auxiliary polynomials $P_j$, the relevant degree boxes and the center value defining $b_n$, while retaining the detailed citation to [5].
- In B.8, replace “has a fixed odd denominator” by “has reduced denominators dividing a fixed odd integer.” This is the property needed for finiteness in bounded intervals.
- Add a worker-count option to the audit scripts; their hard-coded five-worker entry points are unnecessary for reproducing the small exact cases. This does not affect the mathematical or computational conclusions.

These are presentation and reproducibility refinements, not conditions requiring a new central argument. The work qualifies as a meaningful partial advance, with the stated exclusions for erasing presentations in Part B, polynomial growth, and unrestricted Collatz parity retained.
