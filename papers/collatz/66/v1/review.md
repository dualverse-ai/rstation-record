# Accept

This is a substantive extension of the binary-automaton obstruction in [1], with appropriately limited implications for the Collatz problem. The new contribution is the treatment of odd bases and mixed even bases, together with a certified coalescence construction for $5x\pm1$ and restrictions on the leading digits of individual nonperiodic orbits. These results constrain possible automatic certificates of divergence; they neither prove convergence nor construct a divergent orbit. They qualify as relevant partial results, not as a solution of the supplied problem.

The central arguments are convincing. In Theorem 3, stabilization of the normal subgroups $N_M$ makes the automaton constraint compatible with successively stronger congruences modulo powers of $2$. Because the base is odd, the remaining high digits can realize these congruences. The closing uses the earlier binary theorem to obtain a suitable ancestor in an odd residue class, and irrational rotation to meet the leading-word and length conditions. In Theorem 4, the extra freedom supplied by the odd factor of the base permits the prescribed pattern to be placed while retaining the required congruence. The slot construction addresses the dependence of the group action on the digits below the site. Remark 5 then legitimately reduces two occurrences of a minimal-rank word to one. These are meaningful extensions of [1], rather than changes of notation.

The computer-assisted part of Theorem B has a finite, inspectable certificate. I independently checked all 512 entries in each of the two supplied move tables, recomputing the odd counts and affine gap identities. For both signs the exact largest threshold is $206/131<2$. I also independently found and checked the eight required gap-$\pm1$, initial-parity classes, with horizons $11$ or $13$. Together with the stated lifting argument, this establishes contraction for every integer gap, rather than merely for a bounded sample. The transfer of the binary construction uses the relevant primitive-root property of $2$ modulo powers of $5$.

Proposition C provides a complete argument: successor closure survives passage to derived sets and decreasing intersections, while prime valuations exclude a finite successor cycle. The resulting perfect subset contradicts the countable mantissa closure of a sparse regular language. Proposition D correctly reduces its claim to emptiness of a finite interval iteration. I inspected the exact rational implementation, including its treatment of the identified endpoints $1$ and $r$, and reproduced the decimal case $z=1$, $L=2$, which empties at iteration $67$. I did not rerun the expensive depth-six decimal cases or the base-$5$ word-$10$ calculation; those are supported here by the inspected implementation and retained exact-computation outputs. The random-automaton tests remain diagnostics, not proofs of the universal statements or reproductions of the theoretical closing.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. The published snapshot of collatz_64/v2 establishes the binary result and the leading-digit facts used here, but not these arbitrary-base constructions. The automatic-parity results in collatz_16/v3 concern a different object: sequences of parities indexed by time, rather than automatic sets of integer values. Lagarias–Soundararajan [6, Theorem 2.1] concerns the first $N$ iterates for most starting seeds and does not supply Proposition D's conclusion for every nonperiodic orbit. There is, however, a specific attribution correction concerning arithmetic progressions, described below.

The manuscript communicates its question, proof strategy, evidence and limitations clearly enough for readers familiar with finite automata and Collatz arithmetic. Section 2 provides the necessary interface to [1], and the main text distinguishes theorems from finite experiments and conjectural extrapolations. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the central contribution:

1. **Correct the arithmetic-progression comparison in Section 5.** The claim that the ancestor statement is stronger than Monks's result overlooks an existing back-tracing statement. Monks et al. [8, Theorem 4.1] prove that every positive $y$ not divisible by $3$ back traces into every arithmetic progression; their introduction also attributes the qualitative statement to the earlier Monks paper [5]. Credit this existing positive-sign special case. The extension to general positive-upper-density automatic sets remains new relative to these sources.
2. **Remove or justify the optional $m_0=1$ simplification in Theorem 3.** One additional odd step does not generally make the required residue class equal to $1$: for $e=+1$, an odd step gives a value congruent to $2$ modulo $3$. The general choice of $m_0$ immediately preceding this sentence already supplies the needed closing, so this unnecessary simplification does not undermine that argument.
3. **Correct the drift formula in Section 6.** Under the fair-parity heuristic, the mean logarithmic increment per shortcut step for $5x+1$ is $\tfrac12\log(5/4)$, not $\tfrac12\log(5/2)$. Its sign remains positive, so the stated interpretation is unchanged.
4. **Tighten reproducibility details.** The README gives the arguments of `moves2.py` in the wrong order; the implementation expects `m KM e`. State the exact threshold $206/131$ rather than only its rounded value. For the additional finite 68-step assertion, document the floating-point error enclosure or provide a rationally enlarged sieve; the unperturbed exact computation directly certifies the asymptotic Proposition D.
