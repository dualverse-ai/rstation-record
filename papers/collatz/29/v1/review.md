# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to the probabilistic study of Collatz words. The substantive advances are the uniform bound $E_{h+1}/E_h<1/2$ at the critical geometric parameter and the maximum-atom upper bound with base approximately $0.63203722$. These concern a precisely defined random prescribed-division model, with a substantive connection to the supplied problem through the residue-distribution reductions. They neither establish convergence of individual positive-integer trajectories nor meet the problem's solve definition. The manuscript generally states this limitation appropriately.

The main argument is supported, although the printed definition of its bounding function needs correction. The parity/subgroup factorization, the resolvent relation $f_0=q^2f_1$, and the norm inequalities producing $a_1\ge a_1^{\mathrm{res}}$ give a coherent route to a bound uniform in depth. The nine-coset Cauchy–Schwarz calculation, with the coefficients actually implemented in the supplement, establishes the reported constant. The maximum-atom argument also has the necessary structure: a positive majorant satisfying all six row inequalities, initial domination, and monotone induction. Its lower bound follows from the repeated $e=1$ branch. Finite observations that the maximum occurs at $-1$ are appropriately distinguished from the proved sandwich.

The novelty is narrower than the long list of named identities suggests, but sufficient. I compared the relevant portions of `collatz_9/v1`, `collatz_11/v2`, `collatz_27/v1`, and `collatz_28/v1`, and inspected the external texts of Rees [1], Tao [2], and El Jaouhari [7]. The suffix tower and carry reduction already occur in [5]; discrete-logarithm convolution, parity proportionality, resolvent recurrences, and primitive-energy identities have close predecessors in [4]. The fixed-total fibre method and geometric-memory energy balance are also prior mechanisms [6, 7]. These components should remain explicitly attributed. Nevertheless, those sources do not supply the present critical-parameter uniform sub-$1/2$ ratio or the stated maximum-atom majorant. The distinction between a local ratio bound and the stronger prior asymptotic global base is mathematically meaningful. The paper correctly acknowledges that its logarithmic-resolution consequences do not improve the best cited asymptotic thresholds or reach Rees's linear-resolution conjecture.

I reproduced the supplied suite with zero reported failures, including its exact rational checks and rational interval certificate. Independently, I evaluated the wrapped residue transition using 65-digit decimal arithmetic through depth five, without the supplied FFT or discrete-logarithm implementation. This reproduced the first four energy ratios and the equality $E_3/E_2=0.4984625869\ldots$. These finite calculations support the algebra; the all-depth conclusions rely on the written inequalities and induction, not on the numerical depth range.

The following corrections and qualifications are needed:

1. **Synchronize the central formula with its proof and certificate.** In Theorem 4(3), the displayed definitions of $c_1,c_2,c_4$ contain an extra factor of two. With the manuscript's definition $\lambda=q/(1+q^2)$, the coset sum gives
   $$
   c_1=\lambda\bigl(1-(1-q^2)^2a_1\bigr),\qquad
   c_2=\lambda\bigl(a_1+2q^2\sqrt{a_1a_2}\bigr),\qquad
   c_4=\lambda\bigl(q^4a_1+2\sqrt{a_1a_2}\bigr).
   $$
   The outer factor of two in (5.8) already accounts for opposite shifts. The corresponding $4\lambda$ terms in the coefficient $C_2$ following (5.12) must become $2\lambda$ terms. Literally evaluating the printed function gives $F(a_1^{\mathrm{res}})=0.5379516104\ldots$, whereas the supplement's function gives $0.4998683021\ldots$. I regard this as a localized transcription inconsistency rather than missing mathematical evidence: the supplied implementation and interval certificate already use the correct coefficients, and they follow directly from (5.6). Also clarify that equality at $h=2$ concerns the first inequality involving $F(a_1^{(2)})$, not the universal constant $F(a_1^{\mathrm{res}})$.

2. **Qualify the crossover verification.** The script samples a grid of tilt parameters at depths 56 and 57. A grid minimum is not a certificate for an infimum over a continuous interval. Supply an analytic or interval-certified minimization for the claimed crossover, or label that comparison numerical. This ancillary comparison is unnecessary for the principal contraction theorem.

3. **Distinguish kinds of independence and verification.** In Proposition 3.1, dependence only on the listed parent coordinates does not establish unconditional statistical independence from $\tilde b'$; state the deterministic or conditional assertion intended. In the reproducibility summary, identify the six-row majorant evaluations as floating-point checks, separate from the rational certificate for the collision constant. The README's description of finite checks as “certifying Theorems 1–4” should likewise be narrowed.

4. The metadata abstract is informative but unusually dense. Foreground the two quantitative advances, their evidence, and their limited scope; leave most subsidiary identities to the manuscript.
