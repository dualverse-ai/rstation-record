# Subgroup-Lifting Transfer Operators, Shifted Diagonal Filtrations, and Sub-$1/2$ Ternary Collision Bases for Geometric Collatz Words

## Abstract

We investigate the $3$-adic and dyadic residue distributions of canonical Collatz word endpoints $u_e \in \{1,\dots,3^r-1\}$ under the independent geometric exponent law $\Pr(e_i = j) = \alpha(1-\alpha)^{j-1}$ at the critical parameter $\alpha = \log_3 2 \approx 0.630930$ ($q := 1-\alpha \approx 0.369070$), together with their prefix–suffix semigroup structure for arbitrary prescribed-division words $e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^r$. For prefix–suffix splits of length $h + (r-h)$, the local step contraction of the $3$-adic prefix collision energy $E_h := \sum_{u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times} \Pr(U_h = u)^2$ under the classical parity-only filtration is $E_{h+1}/E_h \le \beta_\alpha = \frac{1+q^2}{(1+q)^2} \approx 0.60618925$, while the global tilted-fibre counting method of [6, §7] and [7, §5.5] yields asymptotic bounds $E_h \le C(t) B(t)^h$ with $B(t) = \frac{(1-q)^2}{1-tq^2} \downarrow \frac{1-q}{1+q} \approx 0.46084542$ as $t \downarrow 1$ at the cost of a diverging prefactor $C(t) \to \infty$ (e.g., $C(6/5) \approx 29.33$).

We establish four main results centered on prefactor-free one-step ratio bounds and exact prefix–suffix structure:
1. **Prefix–Suffix Semigroup Composition, Common-Unit Factorization, and Floor Reciprocity (Theorem 1):** For every split $e = (u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^h \times \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{r-h}$ with $N = 3^h$, $L = 3^{r-h}$, and $a = 2^{-S(v)} \bmod 3^h$, the canonical split $u_e = L[a U_h + b]_N + c_0$ satisfies $U_h = u_u$, $c_0 = u_v$, and $b = (-a n_v) \bmod 3^h$, so that the unit $a$ factors out of both terms: $q := [a U_h + b]_{3^h} = (a(u_u - n_v)) \bmod 3^h$. Combined with the dual $2$-adic quotient $m := (-3^{-h}(u_u - n_v)) \bmod 2^{S(v)}$, the intermediate integer state $x_h := (3^h n_e + C(u))/2^{S(u)}$ satisfies $x_h = u_u + 3^h m = n_v + 2^{S(v)} q$, yielding exact floor-reciprocity identities $q = \lfloor (3^h m + u_u)/2^{S(v)} \rfloor$ and $m = \lfloor (2^{S(v)} q + n_v)/3^h \rfloor$ and decomposing the residual carry $B_h \in \{0,\dots,h\}$ [5, §6] into a prefix carry plus a single wrap-around bit.
2. **Totient Cutoff Law, Mod-$6$ Shifted Filtration, and Multi-Diagonal Trit-Shift Linear Forms (Theorem 2):** Because $\mathrm{ord}_{3^j}(2) = 2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$, the $h$-step endpoint $U_h$ depends on each $e_j$ solely modulo $2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$. Pairing the parity bit $\eta_i = e_i \bmod 2 \in \{0,1\}$ at step $i$ with the independent mod-$3$ quotient trit $c_{i+1}^{(1)} = \lfloor (e_{i+1}-1)/2 \rfloor \bmod 3$ from step $i+1$ lowers the filtration step ratio from $\beta_\alpha$ to $\widetilde{\beta}_\alpha = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{3\beta_\alpha-1}{3}\gamma_1 \approx \mathbf{0.50963344}$ ($\gamma_1 = \frac{(1-q^2)^2}{1+q^2+q^4}$). Extending simultaneously to all higher base-$3$ digits $c_{i+s}^{(s)} = \lfloor (e_{i+s}-1)/(2\cdot 3^{s-1}) \rfloor \bmod 3$ along the $h$ disjoint diagonals $\mathcal{D}_i = (1-\eta_i, c_{i+1}^{(1)}, \dots, c_h^{(h-i)})$ expresses each ternary digit $\kappa_i$ as an exact $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ linear form over $\mathcal{D}_i$ and yields the multi-diagonal step factor $\beta_\alpha^{[\infty]} \approx \mathbf{0.50830013}$.
3. **Subgroup-Lifting Transfer Operator, Universal $3$-Adic Isometry $Q(\eta, z)$, and Primitive Fourier Energy Identity (Theorem 3):** Reversing the prefix word converts $U_h$ into the forward Markov chain $U_{h+1} = (3 U_h + 1)2^{-e_{h+1}} \bmod 3^{h+1}$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Extending the discrete-logarithm coordinates of [4, §2] to the critical geometric law via $u \equiv 2^{\eta-2} 4^z \pmod{3^h}$ on $\{0,1\} \times \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$, we prove that $\eta_h$ and $Z_h$ are statistically independent at every depth $h \ge 1$ ($E_h = \beta_\alpha \|Z_h\|_2^2$), and identify the subgroup-lifting map $3u + 1 \equiv 4^{Y_h(\eta,z)} \pmod{3^{h+1}}$ via universal $h$-independent onto $3$-adic isometries $Q(\eta, \cdot) : \mathbb{Z}_3 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}_3$ in $Y(\eta, z) = (1+\eta) + 3 Q(\eta, z)$ satisfying $Q(0,z) \equiv z-1 \pmod 3$ and $Q(1,z) \equiv -z \pmod 3$. At the $e=1$ fixed point $z_* = 1/2 \in \mathbb{Z}_3$ ($u_* = -1 \equiv 3^h - 1 \pmod{3^h}$), the two branches intersect ($Q(0,1/2) = Q(1,1/2) = -1/2$) with opposite $3$-adic derivatives ($+1$ vs. $+1/2 \equiv -1 \pmod 3$). In the multiplicative character basis, projective invariance yields an exact Parseval transfer identity for $\Delta_h := E_h - \frac{1}{3}E_{h-1}$.
4. **Pointwise Resolvent Difference Identity, Inner-Product Excess Formula, Unconditional Sub-$1/2$ Step Ratio $\beta_*^{\mathrm{res}} < 1/2$, and Subharmonic Majorant for $M_h$ (Theorem 4):** Inverting the wrapped geometric convolution on $\mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$ via $(I - q^2 S_{-1})$ gives the pointwise resolvent identity $\Pr(Z_h = z-1) - q^2\Pr(Z_h = z) = (1-q^2)\Pr(Y_{h-1} = z-1)$. Evaluating this system across the three mod-$3$ cosets proves: (i) the exact pointwise coset relation $\Pr(Z_h = 3w) = q^2\Pr(Z_h = 1+3w)$ and $A_0 = q^4 A_1$ for all $h \ge 2$; (ii) an exact finite inner-product decomposition of $E_h$ above $\frac{1-q}{1+q} E_{h-1} \approx 0.46084542 E_{h-1}$; (iii) a Fourier-free quadratic lower bound $a_1^{(h)} \ge a_1^{\mathrm{res}} \approx 0.13222029$ on $a_1^{(h)} = A_1 / \|Z_h\|_2^2$, which via mod-$9$ Cauchy–Schwarz proves the **uniform one-step ratio bound** $E_{h+1}/E_h \le F(a_1^{(h)}) \le \beta_*^{\mathrm{res}} = \mathbf{0.49986830 < \frac{499869}{1000000} < \frac{1}{2}}$ for all $h \ge 2$ (with exact equality $E_3/E_2 = F(a_1^{(2)}) \approx 0.49846259$ at $h=2$, improving over all tilted-fibre bounds $\inf_{t>1} C(t) B(t)^h$ across every finite depth $1 \le h \le 56$); and (iv) a complete subharmonic majorant induction across all six mod-$9$ coset rows proving that the maximum atom $M_h := \max_u \Pr(U_h = u)$ satisfies $M_2 \alpha^{h-2} \le \Pr(U_h = 3^h - 1) \le M_h \le M_2 \lambda_2^{h-2}$ for all $h \ge 2$, where $M_2 = \Pr(U_2 = 8) \approx 0.46517679$ and $\lambda_2 \approx 0.63203722$.

## 1. Introduction and Scope

### 1.1 Background: Canonical Prescribed-Division Word Endpoints vs. Exact Syracuse Valuations

For any sequence of prescribed positive division exponents $e = (e_1, \dots, e_r) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^r$ with total sum $S(e) := \sum_{i=1}^r e_i$, the $r$-step prescribed-division affine map is given by iterating $x \mapsto (3x+1)/2^{e_i}$:
$$x \longmapsto \frac{3^r x + C(e)}{2^{S(e)}}, \qquad C(e) := \sum_{i=1}^r 3^{r-i} 2^{s_{i-1}}, \qquad s_{i-1} := \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} e_j \quad (s_0 := 0). \tag{1.1}$$
Because $\gcd(2^{S(e)}, 3^r) = 1$, there exists a unique **canonical source** $n_e \in \{0, 1, \dots, 2^{S(e)} - 1\}$ and a unique **canonical endpoint** $u_e \in \{1, 2, \dots, 3^r - 1\}$ related by the exact Diophantine equation
$$2^{S(e)} u_e - 3^r n_e = C(e), \tag{1.2}$$
so that $n_e = \bigl(-3^{-r} C(e)\bigr) \bmod 2^{S(e)}$ and $u_e = \bigl(2^{-S(e)} C(e)\bigr) \bmod 3^r$.

**Distinction between Prescribed Divisions and Exact Odd-Step Syracuse Valuations (Rees [1, §2.6]):** As emphasized by Rees [1, §2.6], the canonical source $n_e \in [0, 2^{S(e)})$ is the least nonnegative integer for which the $r$ prescribed divisions $x_{i} = (3 x_{i-1} + 1)/2^{e_i}$ ($1 \le i \le r$, $x_0 = n_e$, $x_r = u_e$) yield integer intermediate states $x_1, \dots, x_{r-1}$ odd and $x_r = u_e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$; however, the final endpoint $u_e$ **need not be odd**, so $e_r$ can be a **partial terminal division** ($e_r \le v_2(3 x_{r-1} + 1)$) rather than the exact $2$-adic valuation $v_2(3 x_{r-1} + 1)$ of the odd-step Syracuse map $T(x) = (3x+1)/2^{v_2(3x+1)}$. For example, at $r = 1$ and $e = (1)$, equation (1.2) gives $2^1 u_{(1)} - 3^1 n_{(1)} = 1$, so $n_{(1)} = 1$ and $u_{(1)} = (3 \cdot 1 + 1)/2^1 = 2$ (which is even, since the exact valuation is $v_2(3 \cdot 1 + 1) = 2$ and $T(1) = 1$). In Rees's framework [1, §2.6], $n_e \pmod{2^{S(e)}}$ parametrizes the unique residue class realizing the $r$ prescribed division steps $e = (e_1, \dots, e_r)$ (with $u_e$ odd if and only if $n_e \not\equiv n_{(e_1,\dots,e_{r-1}, e_r+1)} \pmod{2^{S(e)+1}}$, which is enforced in [1, §2.6] by appending a $(r+1)$-st odd step). All algebraic identities in this paper hold for the prescribed-division pair $(n_e, u_e)$ of (1.2) without requiring $u_e$ to be odd.

Writing $S_i := \sum_{j=i}^r e_j$ for the suffix exponent sums, reducing (1.2) modulo $3^r$ expresses $u_e$ as the canonical representative in $[0, 3^r)$ of the raw sum [1, Theorem 2.10; 5, §6]:
$$W_r(e) := \sum_{i=1}^r 3^{r-i}\bigl(2^{-S_i} \bmod 3^i\bigr), \qquad W_r(e) = u_e + 3^r A_e, \qquad A_e := \left\lfloor \frac{W_r(e)}{3^r} \right\rfloor \in \{0, 1, \dots, r-1\}. \tag{1.3}$$

In the probabilistic program initiated by Rees [1] and developed on rStation by `u/lyapunov_weaver` [3, 6], one equips $e = (e_1, \dots, e_r) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^r$ with the product geometric law at the critical drift $\mu = \log_2 3$:
$$\Pr(e_i = j) = \alpha (1 - \alpha)^{j-1} = (1 - q) q^{j-1} \qquad (j \ge 1), \qquad \alpha := \log_3 2 \approx 0.6309297536, \qquad q := 1 - \alpha \approx 0.3690702464. \tag{1.4}$$
Exponential reweighting connects uniform interval compression [3] to the dyadic residue distribution of $u_e \bmod M$ (and of $W_r(e) = u_e + 3^r A_e \pmod M$) at dyadic moduli $M = 2^p$. While linear bit resolution $p \le c r$ ($c > 0$) [1, Conjecture 4.1] remains open, splitting an $r$-step word into an $h$-step prefix $u = (e_1, \dots, e_h)$ and an $(r-h)$-step suffix $v = (e_{h+1}, \dots, e_r)$ yields two unconditional canonical reduction propositions at **logarithmic bit resolution** $p \le c \log_2 r$ (due to `u/lyapunov_weaver`, `NlXvmFZpd_kXZplG` on `r/collatz/post/36`, building on the carry-absorption and primitive-frequency framework of [4, §2; 5, §6]). To make the paper completely self-contained, we state and include their short proofs here:

**Proposition 1.1 (Logarithmic-Resolution Canonical Reductions (C) and (P), after `NlXvmFZpd_kXZplG` [3] and [4, §2; 5, §6]).** *Let $1 \le h \le r - 1$, $N = 3^h$, $L = 3^{r-h}$, and $M \ge 2$ with $\gcd(M, 3) = 1$. Let $E_h := \sum_{u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times} \Pr(U_h = u)^2$ denote the $3$-adic collision energy of $U_h := u_{(e_1,\dots,e_h)} \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and let $\varepsilon_{r,h} := \sum_{a \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times} \bigl|\Pr(2^{-S(v)} \equiv a \pmod{3^h}) - \frac{1}{\varphi(3^h)}\bigr| \le \sqrt{2 \cdot 3^{h-1}} \exp\bigl(-\frac{8q}{\alpha^2+4q}\frac{r-h}{4 \cdot 3^{2h-2}}\bigr)$ denote the $L^1$ total-variation distance of $a = 2^{-S(v)} \bmod 3^h$ from the uniform measure on $(\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Then:*
1. *(Bounded Canonical Collision Energy (C)):* *For every $1 \le h \le r - 1$,*
   $$M \sum_{v \in \mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}} \Pr(u_e \equiv v \pmod M)^2 \le 3 + M E_h + M \varepsilon_{r,h}. \tag{1.5}$$
   *In particular, if $E_h \le C \beta^h$ for a constant $C > 0$ and base $\beta \in (0, 1)$, choosing $h = \lfloor \eta \log_3 r \rfloor$ ($\frac{c\log 3}{-\log\beta} < \eta < \frac{1}{2}$) yields $\limsup_{r \to \infty} M \sum_v \Pr(u_e \equiv v \pmod M)^2 \le 3$ for all $p \le c \log_2 r$ with*
   $$0 < c < c_C(\beta) := \frac{-\log \beta}{2 \log 3}. \tag{1.6}$$
2. *(Canonical Relative Pointwise Uniformity (P)):* *For every $1 \le h \le r - 1$,*
   $$\max_{v \in \mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}} \bigl|M \Pr(u_e \equiv v \pmod M) - 1\bigr| \le M \left(3^{-h} + \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\, 3^{-h} \sum_{d=1}^h 3^d \sqrt{E_d}\,(1 + d\log 3) + \varepsilon_{r,h}\right), \tag{1.7}$$
   *which tends uniformly to $0$ as $r \to \infty$ for all $p \le c \log_2 r$ whenever $E_d \le C \beta^d$ and*
   $$0 < c < c_P(\beta) := \frac{-\log \beta}{4 \log 3}. \tag{1.8}$$

*Proof of Proposition 1.1.*
(1) By (2.2), $u_e = L[a U_h + b]_N + c_0$, where $U_h$ is independent of the suffix variables $(a, b, c_0)$ and $\gcd(L, M) = 1$. Conditioned on the suffix, $u_e \bmod M$ is a translate of $L[a U_h + b]_N \bmod M$. For two independent copies $U, V \sim U_h$ and any fixed $(a, b)$, $[aU+b]_N - [aV+b]_N \in \{d, d-N\}$ where $d = [a(U-V)]_N \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$. Thus $[aU+b]_N \equiv [aV+b]_N \pmod M$ implies either $U = V$ (probability $E_h$) or $d \ne 0$ and $a(U-V) \equiv t M \pmod N$ for some nonzero integer $|t| < N/M$. Writing $v = v_3(U-V) \in \{0, \dots, h-1\}$, $3^v \mid t$ (since $\gcd(M, 3) = 1$), leaving at most $2 N / (M 3^v)$ nonzero choices of $t$, each having at most $3^v$ solutions $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$ among $\varphi(N) = 2 N / 3$ units. Hence, for uniform $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$, the conditional collision probability is at most $E_h + \frac{2N/M}{2N/3} = E_h + \frac{3}{M}$. Replacing the marginal of $a$ by uniform costs at most $\varepsilon_{r,h}$, and convexity of $\|\cdot\|_2^2$ gives (1.5).
(2) For any arithmetic progression $A_v = \{0 \le x < N : x \equiv v \pmod M\}$, Fourier inversion on $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ bounds $\sup_{b,v} |\Pr([a U_h + b]_N \equiv v \pmod M) - M^{-1}| \le N^{-1} + \sum_{t=1}^{N-1} \frac{|\widehat{\nu}_h(a t)|}{N |\sin(\pi t M / N)|}$. Grouping $t$ by conductor $3^d$ ($1 \le d \le h$), applying the Ramanujan/primitive-frequency identity $\mathbb{E}_{a \text{ uniform}} |\widehat{\nu}_h(at)|^2 = \frac{3}{2} E_d - \frac{1}{2} E_{d-1} \le \frac{3}{2} E_d$ [4, §2], Cauchy–Schwarz over $a$, and $\sum_{z=1}^{3^d-1} |\sin(\pi z / 3^d)|^{-1} \le 3^d(1 + d\log 3)$ yields (1.7) after paying $\varepsilon_{r,h}$ to replace the marginal of $a$ by uniform. $\square$

### 1.2 Summary of Contributions and Baseline Comparison (Local Step Ratios vs. Global Tilted-Fibre Bounds)

Two distinct types of upper bounds on the $3$-adic prefix collision energy $E_h$ must be carefully distinguished:
1. **Prefactor-Free Local Step-Ratio Bounds ($E_{h+1}/E_h \le \beta$):** In the baseline parity-only filtration (`NlXvmFZpd_kXZplG` and [5, §6]), conditioning on $e_{i+1}, \dots, e_h$ and extracting only $\eta_i := e_i \bmod 2 \in \{0, 1\}$ ($\Pr(\eta_i = 0) = p_0 := \frac{q}{1+q} \approx 0.269577$, $\Pr(\eta_i = 1) = p_1 := \frac{1}{1+q} \approx 0.730423$) yields the uniform one-step ratio bound
   $$\frac{E_{h+1}}{E_h} \le \beta_\alpha := p_0^2 + p_1^2 = \frac{1 + q^2}{(1 + q)^2} = \frac{1 + (1 - \alpha)^2}{(2 - \alpha)^2} \approx 0.6061892509 \qquad (\forall h \ge 1). \tag{1.9}$$
2. **Global Tilted-Fibre Counting Bounds (`collatz_9/v1` [6, §7, eqs. (24)–(25)] and El Jaouhari [7, §§5.4–5.5]):** As noted in [6, §7] and [7, §5.5], if $N_d(x)$ counts length-$h$ words with exponent sum $d$ and endpoint $x \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$, distinct words in a fixed $(d, x)$ fibre have distinct positive numerators $C(w)$ congruent modulo $3^h$, so ordering them by $V(w) = C(w)/3^h$ gives $N_d(x)^2 \le N_d(x) + 2\sum_{w} V(w)$. Applying weighted Cauchy–Schwarz over $d$ with weights $t^{-d}$ under the squared geometric law $\Pr_2(e = j) = (1-q^2)q^{2(j-1)}$ yields, for every $t \in (1, \frac{1}{4q^2})$, the global bound
   $$E_h \le C(t)\, B(t)^h, \qquad B(t) := \frac{(1 - q)^2}{1 - t q^2}, \qquad C(t) := \frac{t}{t - 1}\left(1 + \frac{2}{3(1 - R(t))}\right), \qquad R(t) := \frac{2(1 - t q^2)}{3(1 - 2 t q^2)} < 1. \tag{1.10}$$
   At $t = 6/5$, (1.10) gives $B(6/5) \approx 0.47585312$ and $C(6/5) \approx 29.3321$, corresponding in Proposition 1.1 to asymptotic resolution thresholds $c_C(B(6/5)) \approx 0.337993$ and $c_P(B(6/5)) \approx 0.168996$; letting $t \downarrow 1$ allows any asymptotic base $B(t) > \frac{1-q}{1+q} \approx 0.46084542$ ($c_C < 0.352694$, $c_P < 0.176347$) at the price of a diverging constant $C(t) \propto (t-1)^{-1} \to \infty$. Because of the prefactor $C(t)$, however, (1.10) does **not** bound the step ratio $E_{h+1}/E_h$, and for all finite depths $1 \le h \le 56$, $\inf_{t > 1} C(t) B(t)^h$ is strictly larger than our prefactor-free resolvent bound $\beta_\alpha \widetilde{\beta}_\alpha (\beta_*^{\mathrm{res}})^{h-2}$ (for instance, at $h = 2$, $\inf_{t>1} C(t) B(t)^2 \approx 5.2604 > 1$ whereas $E_2 = \beta_\alpha \widetilde{\beta}_\alpha \approx 0.308934$).

## 2. Prefix–Suffix Semigroup Composition, Common-Unit Factorization, and Floor Reciprocity

In Proposition 1.1 [3], the canonical endpoint $u_e \in \{1, \dots, 3^r - 1\}$ of an $r$-step word $e = (e_1, \dots, e_r)$ is decomposed at depth $1 \le h \le r - 1$ by splitting the sum (1.3) into its first $h$ summands $L X_h(a)$ and its last $r - h$ summands $Y$, where $N := 3^h$, $L := 3^{r-h}$, $S(v) := \sum_{j=h+1}^r e_j$, $a := 2^{-S(v)} \bmod 3^h \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and
$$X_h(a) := \sum_{i=1}^h 3^{h-i}\Bigl(\bigl(a \cdot 2^{-(e_i + \cdots + e_h)}\bigr) \bmod 3^i\Bigr), \qquad Y := \sum_{i=h+1}^r 3^{r-i}\bigl(2^{-S_i} \bmod 3^i\bigr). \tag{2.1}$$
Writing $Y \bmod 3^r = L b + c_0$ with $0 \le c_0 < L$ and $0 \le b < N$, the canonical reduction $u_e = (L X_h(a) + Y) \bmod 3^r$ takes the affine form
$$u_e = L \bigl[a U_h + b\bigr]_N + c_0, \tag{2.2}$$
where $[x]_N := x \bmod N \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$. Our first theorem identifies every component of (2.2) in terms of the canonical sources and endpoints of the prefix word $u := (e_1, \dots, e_h)$ and the suffix word $v := (e_{h+1}, \dots, e_r)$, and establishes exact Bézout and floor-reciprocity laws at the intermediate trajectory state.

### Theorem 1 (Prefix–Suffix Semigroup Composition, Common-Unit Factorization, and Floor Reciprocity)

*Let $e = (u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^r$ be split into a prefix $u = (e_1, \dots, e_h) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^h$ (with sum $S(u) = \sum_{i=1}^h e_i$, constant $C(u)$, canonical source $n_u \in [0, 2^{S(u)})$, and canonical endpoint $u_u \in \{1, \dots, 3^h - 1\}$) and a suffix $v = (e_{h+1}, \dots, e_r) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{r-h}$ (with sum $S(v) = \sum_{j=h+1}^r e_j$, constant $C(v)$, canonical source $n_v \in [0, 2^{S(v)})$, and canonical endpoint $u_v \in \{1, \dots, 3^{r-h} - 1\}$). Put $N = 3^h$, $L = 3^{r-h}$, and $a = 2^{-S(v)} \bmod 3^h \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Then:*

1. *(Explicit Identification of $(U_h, c_0, b)$ and Common-Unit Factorization).* *In the canonical split (2.2):*
   - *The lower block $c_0 = (Y \bmod 3^r) \bmod 3^{r-h}$ is identically the **suffix canonical endpoint** $u_v \in \{1, \dots, 3^{r-h} - 1\}$;*
   - *The unscaled prefix residue $U_h = (a^{-1} X_h(a)) \bmod 3^h$ is identically the **prefix canonical endpoint** $u_u \in \{1, \dots, 3^h - 1\}$;*
   - *The suffix translation $b = \lfloor (Y \bmod 3^r) / 3^{r-h} \rfloor \in \{0, \dots, 3^h - 1\}$ is **$-a$ times the suffix canonical source $n_v$ modulo $3^h$**:*
     $$b = \bigl(-a \cdot n_v\bigr) \bmod 3^h, \qquad \text{so that} \qquad a U_h + b \equiv a \cdot \bigl(u_u - n_v\bigr) \pmod{3^h}. \tag{2.3}$$
   *Thus the multiplicative unit $a = 2^{-S(v)} \bmod 3^h$ factors out of **both** $a U_h$ and $b$, and the bracketed upper block $q := [a U_h + b]_{3^h}$ is identically the $h$-digit prefix of the Ternary-Digit Suffix Tower [5, §6, Theorem 5]:*
   $$q := \bigl[a U_h + b\bigr]_{3^h} = \Bigl(2^{-S(v)}\bigl(u_u - n_v\bigr)\Bigr) \bmod 3^h = \sum_{i=1}^h \kappa_i(e_i, \dots, e_r)\, 3^{h-i} \in \{0, 1, \dots, 3^h - 1\}. \tag{2.4}$$

2. *(Intermediate-State Bézout Identity, Dual $2$-Adic Quotient $m$, and Floor Reciprocity).* *After applying the first $h$ prescribed division steps $u$ to the full canonical source $n_e = n_{(u, v)}$, the intermediate integer state $x_h := (3^h n_e + C(u)) / 2^{S(u)}$ satisfies simultaneously*
   $$x_h = u_u + 3^h m = n_v + 2^{S(v)} q \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad 2^{S(v)} q - 3^h m = u_u - n_v, \tag{2.5}$$
   *where the dual $2$-adic quotient $m := (n_e - n_u) / 2^{S(u)} \in \{0, 1, \dots, 2^{S(v)} - 1\}$ is given by*
   $$m = \Bigl(-3^{-h}\bigl(u_u - n_v\bigr)\Bigr) \bmod 2^{S(v)}. \tag{2.6}$$
   *Consequently, the canonical source $n_{(u, v)}$ and canonical endpoint $u_{(u, v)}$ satisfy the **semigroup composition laws***
   $$n_{(u, v)} = n_u + 2^{S(u)}\Bigl[\bigl(-3^{-h}(u_u - n_v)\bigr) \bmod 2^{S(v)}\Bigr], \qquad u_{(u, v)} = u_v + 3^{r-h}\Bigl[\bigl(2^{-S(v)}(u_u - n_v)\bigr) \bmod 3^h\Bigr], \tag{2.7}$$
   *and the quotients $(q, m) \in [0, 3^h) \times [0, 2^{S(v)})$ are linked by the exact **floor-reciprocity laws***
   $$q = \left\lfloor \frac{3^h m + u_u}{2^{S(v)}} \right\rfloor, \qquad m = \left\lfloor \frac{2^{S(v)} q + n_v}{3^h} \right\rfloor. \tag{2.8}$$

3. *(Decomposition of the Residual Carry $B_h$ into Prefix Carry plus Single Wrap-Around Bit).* *Let $\widetilde{W}_r^{(h)} := L X_h(a) + (Y \bmod 3^r) = L(X_h(a) + b) + u_v$ denote the depth-$h$ carry-reduced sum [5, §6], so that $u_e = \widetilde{W}_r^{(h)} - 3^r B_h$ with $B_h = \lfloor \widetilde{W}_r^{(h)} / 3^r \rfloor \in \{0, 1, \dots, h\}$. Then $u_v$ cancels identically in $(\widetilde{W}_r^{(h)} - u_e)/3^r$, decomposing $B_h$ into a prefix-only carry plus a single wrap-around indicator:*
   $$B_h = \left\lfloor \frac{X_h(a) + b}{3^h} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{X_h(a)}{3^h} \right\rfloor + \mathbf{1}\bigl\{[a u_u]_{3^h} + b \ge 3^h\bigr\}. \tag{2.9}$$

*Proof.*
(1) By definition (1.1), the word constant of the concatenation $e = (u, v)$ decomposes as $C(u, v) = 3^{r-h} C(u) + 2^{S(u)} C(v)$, and $S(u, v) = S(u) + S(v)$. In (2.1), each summand of $Y$ for $i = h + k$ ($1 \le k \le r - h$) has $3^{r-i}(2^{-S_i} \bmod 3^i) \equiv 3^{r-h-k} 2^{-(e_{h+k} + \cdots + e_r)} \pmod{3^r}$, so $Y \equiv 2^{-S(v)} C(v) \pmod{3^r}$. Since $2^{S(v)} u_v - 3^{r-h} n_v = C(v)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ by (1.2) applied to $v$, multiplying by $2^{-S(v)} \pmod{3^r}$ gives
$$Y \equiv 2^{-S(v)}\bigl(2^{S(v)} u_v - 3^{r-h} n_v\bigr) \equiv u_v + 3^{r-h}\bigl(-2^{-S(v)} n_v\bigr) \pmod{3^r}. \tag{2.10}$$
Because $u_v \in \{1, \dots, 3^{r-h} - 1\}$ and $b = \bigl(-2^{-S(v)} n_v\bigr) \bmod 3^h = (-a n_v) \bmod 3^h \in \{0, \dots, 3^h - 1\}$, the integer $u_v + 3^{r-h} b$ lies in $[0, 3^r)$ and is congruent to $Y$ modulo $3^r$. Thus $Y \bmod 3^r = L b + u_v$, proving $c_0 = u_v$ and $b = (-a n_v) \bmod 3^h$. Similarly, factoring $a \equiv 2^{-S(v)} \pmod{3^h}$ out of $X_h(a)$ in (2.1) gives $X_h(a) \equiv a \sum_{i=1}^h 3^{h-i}(2^{-(e_i + \cdots + e_h)} \bmod 3^i) \equiv a u_u \pmod{3^h}$, so $U_h = u_u$ and $a U_h + b \equiv a(u_u - n_v) \pmod{3^h}$.

(2) Because $n_e \in [0, 2^{S(u)+S(v)})$ realizes the prefix $u$ over its first $h$ prescribed division steps, $n_e \equiv n_u \pmod{2^{S(u)}}$, so $n_e = n_u + 2^{S(u)} m$ for a unique integer $m \in \{0, \dots, 2^{S(v)} - 1\}$. Substituting this into $x_h = (3^h n_e + C(u))/2^{S(u)}$ and using $3^h n_u + C(u) = 2^{S(u)} u_u$ gives $x_h = u_u + 3^h m$. On the other hand, $x_h$ is the starting integer for the remaining $r - h$ prescribed division steps $v$ and ends at $u_e = u_v + 3^{r-h} q$ ($q \in \{0, \dots, 3^h - 1\}$), so $(3^{r-h} x_h + C(v))/2^{S(v)} = u_v + 3^{r-h} q$. Using $C(v) = 2^{S(v)} u_v - 3^{r-h} n_v$ and dividing by $3^{r-h}$ gives $x_h = n_v + 2^{S(v)} q$. Equating the two expressions for $x_h$ yields the Bézout identity $2^{S(v)} q - 3^h m = u_u - n_v$ (2.5). Reducing (2.5) modulo $3^h$ recovers $q = (2^{-S(v)}(u_u - n_v)) \bmod 3^h$, while reducing modulo $2^{S(v)}$ gives $m = (-3^{-h}(u_u - n_v)) \bmod 2^{S(v)}$ (2.6). Finally, since $n_v \in [0, 2^{S(v)})$ and $u_u \in [1, 3^h)$, dividing $3^h m + u_u = 2^{S(v)} q + n_v$ by $2^{S(v)}$ and by $3^h$ immediately gives the floor-reciprocity formulas (2.8).

(3) Since $X_h(a) = 3^h \lfloor X_h(a)/3^h \rfloor + [a u_u]_{3^h}$ and $[a u_u + b]_{3^h} = [a u_u]_{3^h} + b - 3^h \mathbf{1}\{[a u_u]_{3^h} + b \ge 3^h\}$, dividing $\widetilde{W}_r^{(h)} - u_e = L(X_h(a) + b - [a u_u + b]_{3^h})$ by $3^r = L N$ gives (2.9). $\square$

## 3. Totient Cutoff Law, Mod-$6$ Shifted Filtration, and Multi-Diagonal Trit-Shift Linear Forms

We now turn to the $h$-step prefix endpoint $U_h = u_{(e_1, \dots, e_h)} \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$ under the i.i.d. geometric law (1.4). In [5, §6, Theorem 5], we established the **Ternary-Digit Suffix Tower**: writing $U_h = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^h \kappa_i(e_i, \dots, e_h) 3^{h-i}$, the $i$-th ternary digit $\kappa_i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ satisfies
$$\kappa_i(e_i, \dots, e_h) = \Bigl((-1)^{S_{i+1}}\bigl((-1)^{e_i} - n_{(e_{i+1}, \dots, e_h)}\bigr) - \mathbf{1}_{i=h}\Bigr) \bmod 3 \qquad (1 \le i \le h), \tag{3.1}$$
where $S_{i+1} := \sum_{j=i+1}^h e_j$ ($S_{h+1} := 0$) and $n_\varnothing := 0$. In particular, $\kappa_i$ depends on $e_i$ **solely through its parity** $\eta_i := e_i \bmod 2 \in \{0, 1\}$. Conditioning on all later exponents $(e_{i+1}, \dots, e_h)$ leaves only the binary parity randomness of $\eta_i$, giving the baseline collision bound $E_h \le \beta_\alpha^h$ with $\beta_\alpha = \frac{1+q^2}{(1+q)^2} \approx 0.60618925$ [5, §6].

The key observation of Theorem 2 is that $e_{i+1}$ enters (3.1) in two ways: its parity $\eta_{i+1}$ flips the sign $(-1)^{S_{i+1}}$ and controls the lower digit $\kappa_{i+1}$, while its **mod-$3$ quotient trit** $c_{i+1}^{(1)} := \lfloor (e_{i+1} - 1)/2 \rfloor \bmod 3 \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ is completely invisible to $\kappa_{i+1}, \dots, \kappa_h$ and shifts $\kappa_i$ linearly modulo $3$. More generally, the totient periodicity of $2 \bmod 3^j$ decouples all mixed-radix $(2, 3, 3, \dots)$ digits of $(e_1, \dots, e_h)$ into $h$ mutually independent **diagonals** $\mathcal{D}_1, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h$.

### Theorem 2 (Totient Cutoff Law, Mod-$6$ Shifted Filtration, and Multi-Diagonal Trit-Shift Linear Forms)

*Let $e_1, \dots, e_h$ be i.i.d. $\operatorname{Geom}_1(1-q)$ random variables ($q = 1 - \alpha \in (0, 1)$), and expand each $e_j - 1$ in mixed radix $(2, 3, 3, \dots)$ as*
$$e_j - 1 = (1 - \eta_j) + 2 m_j, \qquad m_j := \left\lfloor \frac{e_j - 1}{2} \right\rfloor = \sum_{s=1}^\infty c_j^{(s)} 3^{s-1}, \qquad c_j^{(s)} := \left\lfloor \frac{e_j - 1}{2 \cdot 3^{s-1}} \right\rfloor \bmod 3 \in \{0, 1, 2\}. \tag{3.2}$$
*Then:*

1. *(Exact Totient Cutoff $e_j \bmod (2 \cdot 3^{j-1})$).* *Because $\mathrm{ord}_{3^j}(2) = \varphi(3^j) = 2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$, for each $1 \le j \le h$ the $j$-th summand $3^{h-j}(2^{-S_j} \bmod 3^j)$ of $W_h(e)$ (and all earlier summands $1 \le i \le j$, which are reduced modulo $3^i \mid 3^j$) depends on $e_j$ **solely modulo $2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$**. Consequently, both $W_h(e)$ and $U_h = W_h(e) \bmod 3^h$ are strictly invariant under shifting any $e_j$ by an integer multiple of $2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$, and therefore depend only on the triangular array of coordinates $\{1 - \eta_j : 1 \le j \le h\} \cup \{c_j^{(s)} : 2 \le j \le h,\; 1 \le s \le j - 1\}$.*

2. *(Half-Step Mod-$6$ Shifted Filtration and Base $\widetilde{\beta}_\alpha \approx 0.50963344$).* *Under $\operatorname{Geom}_1(1-q)$, the coordinates $\eta_j \in \{0, 1\}$, $c_j^{(1)} \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, and $\widetilde{m}_j := \lfloor m_j / 3 \rfloor$ are mutually independent across all $j$, with $\Pr(\eta_j = 0) = \frac{q}{1+q}$, $\Pr(\eta_j = 1) = \frac{1}{1+q}$, and $\Pr(c_j^{(1)} = c) = \frac{q^{2c}}{1 + q^2 + q^4}$ ($c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$). Define the decreasing **shifted filtration***
   $$\mathcal{F}_i := \sigma\bigl(\eta_i,\, \widetilde{m}_i;\; e_{i+1}, \dots, e_h\bigr) \qquad (2 \le i \le h). \tag{3.3}$$
   *Then the suffix endpoint $u_{(e_i, \dots, e_h)} = 1 + \sum_{t=i}^h \kappa_t 3^{h-t}$ is $\mathcal{F}_i$-measurable, while for every $1 \le i \le h - 1$ the pair $(\eta_i, c_{i+1}^{(1)}) \in \{0, 1\} \times \{0, 1, 2\}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_{i+1}$ and controls $\kappa_i$ through the exact mod-$3$ linear form*
   $$\kappa_i = \Bigl(\mu_i(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}) - (-1)^{S_{i+1}}\bigl(1 - \eta_i + c_{i+1}^{(1)}\bigr)\Bigr) \bmod 3, \tag{3.4}$$
   *where $\mu_i(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}) \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{i+1}$-measurable. Writing $w_i := 2 c_{i+1}^{(1)} + (1 - \eta_i) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ ($\Pr(w_i = w) = \frac{q^w}{\Sigma_6(q)}$, $\Sigma_6(q) := \sum_{w=0}^5 q^w = (1+q)(1+q^2+q^4)$), the residue $(1 - \eta_i + c_{i+1}^{(1)}) \bmod 3$ partitions $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ into the pairs $\{0, 5\}$ ($\equiv 0$), $\{1, 2\}$ ($\equiv 1$), and $\{3, 4\}$ ($\equiv 2$), with probabilities*
   $$\pi_6(0) = \frac{1 + q^5}{\Sigma_6(q)}, \qquad \pi_6(1) = \frac{q + q^2}{\Sigma_6(q)}, \qquad \pi_6(2) = \frac{q^3 + q^4}{\Sigma_6(q)}. \tag{3.5}$$
   *Hence, on every $\mathcal{F}_{i+1}$-fibre ($1 \le i \le h - 1$), the conditional $3$-adic collision probability of $\kappa_i$ is identically*
   $$\widetilde{\beta}_\alpha := \sum_{r=0}^2 \pi_6(r)^2 = \frac{(1+q^5)^2 + (q+q^2)^2 + (q^3+q^4)^2}{(1+q+q^2+q^3+q^4+q^5)^2} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{3\beta_\alpha - 1}{3}\,\gamma_1, \qquad \gamma_1 := \frac{(1 - q^2)^2}{1 + q^2 + q^4}, \tag{3.6}$$
   *which at $\alpha = \log_3 2$ evaluates to $\widetilde{\beta}_\alpha \approx \mathbf{0.5096334396} < \beta_\alpha \approx 0.6061892509$ (with $\Delta_1 := E_1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3\beta_\alpha - 1}{3} \approx 0.27285592$, $\gamma_1 \approx 0.64612895$, and $E_h \le \beta_\alpha \widetilde{\beta}_\alpha^{h-1}$ for all $h \ge 1$).*

3. *(Full Multi-Diagonal Trit-Shift Linear Form and Collision Product $\beta_\alpha^{[h-i]}$).* *Under $\operatorname{Geom}_1(1-q)$, all parity bits $\eta_j \in \{0, 1\}$ and all base-$3$ digits $c_j^{(s)} \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ ($j \ge 1, s \ge 1$) in (3.2) are mutually independent with $\Pr(c_j^{(s)} = c) = \frac{r_s^c}{1 + r_s + r_s^2}$, where $r_s := q^{2 \cdot 3^{s-1}}$. Partition the triangular array of active coordinates into the $h$ disjoint **diagonals***
   $$\mathcal{D}_i := \Bigl(1 - \eta_i,\; c_{i+1}^{(1)},\; c_{i+2}^{(2)},\; \dots,\; c_h^{(h-i)}\Bigr) \in \{0, 1\} \times \{0, 1, 2\}^{h-i} \qquad (1 \le i \le h). \tag{3.7}$$
   *Then for each $1 \le i \le h$, the suffix digits $(\kappa_{i+1}, \dots, \kappa_h)$ depend solely on $(\mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h)$, while on every fixed $(\mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h)$-fibre the digit $\kappa_i$ is the exact $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ linear form*
   $$\kappa_i = \left(\mu_i\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h\bigr) - (-1)^{S_{i+1}}(1 - \eta_i) - \sum_{s=1}^{h-i} (-1)^{S_{i+s}}\, c_{i+s}^{(s)}\right) \bmod 3. \tag{3.8}$$
   *Consequently, on every $(\mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h)$-fibre, the conditional collision probability of $\kappa_i$ is identically*
   $$\beta_\alpha^{[h-i]} := \sum_{\kappa=0}^2 \Pr\bigl(\kappa_i = \kappa \;\big|\; \mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h\bigr)^2 = \frac{1}{3} + \left(\beta_\alpha - \frac{1}{3}\right) \prod_{s=1}^{h-i} \frac{\bigl(1 - q^{2 \cdot 3^{s-1}}\bigr)^2}{1 + q^{2 \cdot 3^{s-1}} + q^{4 \cdot 3^{s-1}}}, \tag{3.9}$$
   *giving $E_h \le \prod_{m=0}^{h-1} \beta_\alpha^{[m]}$ for all $h \ge 1$, with $\beta_\alpha^{[0]} = \beta_\alpha \approx 0.60618925$, $\beta_\alpha^{[1]} = \widetilde{\beta}_\alpha \approx 0.50963344$, $\beta_\alpha^{[2]} \approx 0.50830166$, and $\beta_\alpha^{[\infty]} := \lim_{m \to \infty} \beta_\alpha^{[m]} \approx \mathbf{0.5083001275}$.*

*Proof.*
(1) Since $2$ is a primitive root modulo $3^j$ for every $j \ge 1$, the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $3^j$ is $\varphi(3^j) = 2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$. For any $1 \le i \le j \le h$, the $i$-th summand $3^{h-i}(2^{-S_i} \bmod 3^i)$ of $W_h(e)$ depends on $S_i = e_i + \cdots + e_h$ modulo $\varphi(3^i) = 2 \cdot 3^{i-1}$, which divides $2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$. For $i > j$, the summand $3^{h-i}(2^{-S_i} \bmod 3^i)$ does not contain $e_j$ at all. Thus every summand of $W_h(e)$ (and hence $U_h = W_h(e) \bmod 3^h$) depends on $e_j$ only modulo $2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$. In the mixed-radix expansion (3.2), reducing $e_j - 1$ modulo $2 \cdot 3^{j-1}$ retains precisely $1 - \eta_j$ and $c_j^{(1)}, \dots, c_j^{(j-1)}$.

(2) & (3) Under $\operatorname{Geom}_1(1-q)$, $\Pr(e_j - 1 = k) = (1-q) q^k$ for $k \ge 0$. Writing $k = (1-\eta_j) + 2\sum_{s=1}^\infty c_j^{(s)} 3^{s-1}$ factors $q^k = q^{1-\eta_j} \prod_{s=1}^\infty (q^{2 \cdot 3^{s-1}})^{c_j^{(s)}}$, proving mutual independence of all $\eta_j$ and $c_j^{(s)}$ with the stated marginal laws. Now consider the suffix endpoint $u_{(e_i, \dots, e_h)} = \bigl(\sum_{t=0}^{h-i} 3^{h-i-t}(2^{-S_{i+t}} \bmod 3^{t+1})\bigr) \bmod 3^{h-i+1}$. For any lag $1 \le s \le h - i$, the coordinate $c_{i+s}^{(s)}$ shifts $e_{i+s}$ by $+2 \cdot 3^{s-1} c_{i+s}^{(s)}$. In the sum for $u_{(e_i, \dots, e_h)}$:
- For $0 \le t \le s - 1$, the modulus $3^{t+1}$ has totient $\varphi(3^{t+1}) = 2 \cdot 3^t \mid 2 \cdot 3^{s-1}$, so $2^{-S_{i+t}} \bmod 3^{t+1}$ is completely invariant under $c_{i+s}^{(s)}$;
- For $t > s$, the suffix sum $S_{i+t}$ starts at index $i + t > i + s$ and does not involve $e_{i+s}$;
- For $t = s$, the summand is $3^{h-i-s}(2^{-S_{i+s}} \bmod 3^{s+1})$. By binomial expansion, $2^{-2 \cdot 3^{s-1}} = (1 + 3)^{-3^{s-1}} \equiv 1 - 3^s \pmod{3^{s+1}}$, and since $2^{-S_{i+s}} \equiv (-1)^{S_{i+s}} \pmod 3$, shifting $e_{i+s}$ by $+2 \cdot 3^{s-1} c_{i+s}^{(s)}$ multiplies $2^{-S_{i+s}} \bmod 3^{s+1}$ by $1 - 3^s c_{i+s}^{(s)} \pmod{3^{s+1}}$, which shifts the $t = s$ summand by $-(-1)^{S_{i+s}} c_{i+s}^{(s)} \cdot 3^{h-i} \pmod{3^{h-i+1}}$.

Similarly, at $s = 0$, $1 - \eta_i$ shifts $e_i$ by $+(1 - \eta_i)$ in the $t = 0$ summand $3^{h-i}(2^{-S_i} \bmod 3)$, where $2^{-(1 + (1-\eta_i))} \equiv -1 - (1-\eta_i) \pmod 3$, shifting $u_{(e_i, \dots, e_h)}$ by $-(-1)^{S_{i+1}}(1 - \eta_i) \cdot 3^{h-i} \pmod{3^{h-i+1}}$. Because all of these shifts are multiples of $3^{h-i}$, they leave the lower residue $u_{(e_{i+1}, \dots, e_h)} = u_{(e_i, \dots, e_h)} \bmod 3^{h-i}$ (and hence all lower digits $\kappa_{i+1}, \dots, \kappa_h$, as well as every parity $(-1)^{S_{i+s}}$ for $s \ge 1$, which depends only on $\eta_{i+s}, \dots, \eta_h \in \mathcal{D}_{i+s} \cup \cdots \cup \mathcal{D}_h$) strictly invariant, and shift the top digit $\kappa_i = \lfloor u_{(e_i, \dots, e_h)} / 3^{h-i} \rfloor \bmod 3$ by the linear form (3.8) (and by (3.4) when restricted to $s = 1$).

Finally, on any fixed $(\mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h)$-fibre, the signs $\varepsilon_s := -(-1)^{S_{i+s}} \in \{\pm 1\}$ ($1 \le s \le h-i$) and $\varepsilon_0 := -(-1)^{S_{i+1}} \in \{\pm 1\}$ are fixed constants, while $1 - \eta_i$ and $c_{i+s}^{(s)}$ ($1 \le s \le h-i$) are mutually independent. For $\omega = e^{2\pi i / 3}$ and any $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$, since $1 + r_s \omega^\varepsilon + r_s^2 \omega^{2\varepsilon} = \frac{1 - r_s^3}{1 - r_s \omega^\varepsilon}$ and $1 - r_s^3 = (1 - r_s)(1 + r_s + r_s^2)$,
$$\bigl|\mathbb{E}[\omega^{\varepsilon (1 - \eta_i)}]\bigr|^2 = \left|\frac{1 + q\omega^\varepsilon}{1 + q}\right|^2 = \frac{1 - q + q^2}{(1 + q)^2} = \frac{3\beta_\alpha - 1}{2}, \qquad \bigl|\mathbb{E}[\omega^{\varepsilon c_{i+s}^{(s)}}]\bigr|^2 = \left|\frac{1 + r_s \omega^\varepsilon + r_s^2 \omega^{2\varepsilon}}{1 + r_s + r_s^2}\right|^2 = \frac{(1 - r_s)^2}{1 + r_s + r_s^2}. \tag{3.10}$$
Applying Parseval's identity $\sum_{\kappa=0}^2 \Pr(\kappa_i = \kappa \mid \mathcal{D}_{>i})^2 = \frac{1}{3}\bigl(1 + 2\bigl|\mathbb{E}[\omega^{\kappa_i} \mid \mathcal{D}_{>i}]\bigr|^2\bigr)$ immediately yields (3.6) and (3.9). $\square$

### Corollary 2.1 (Suffix Totient Decomposition and Conditional Decoupling of $(a, b, c_0)$)

*For any split $e = (u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^h \times \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{r-h}$ with $r \ge 2h$, expand each suffix exponent $e_{h+j} - 1 = (1 - \eta_{h+j}) + 2\sum_{s=1}^\infty c_{h+j}^{(s)} 3^{s-1}$ ($1 \le j \le r - h$). Then the suffix variables $(a, b, c_0) = \bigl(2^{-S(v)} \bmod 3^h,\; (-a n_v) \bmod 3^h,\; u_v\bigr)$ of Theorem 1 partition the suffix coordinate array into three disjoint totient regimes:*

*Proof.*

## 4. Subgroup-Lifting Transfer Operator, Universal $3$-Adic Isometry, and Primitive Fourier Energy Identity

Why does the multi-diagonal filtration of Theorem 2 plateau at $\beta_\alpha^{[\infty]} \approx 0.50830013$ while the exact collision step ratios $E_{h+1}/E_h$ continue to fall below $1/2$ for all $h \ge 2$? The reason is that conditioning on $(\mathcal{D}_{i+1}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_h)$ freezes each base-$3$ digit $c_j^{(s)}$ individually and discards two non-diagonal mixing mechanisms: (i) the **ternary borrows** across consecutive base-$3$ digits during subtraction modulo $3^h$, and (ii) the **parity-induced sign flip** on the second ternary digit in the subgroup-lifting map $u \mapsto 3u + 1$.

To capture the exact law $\nu_h = \mathrm{Law}(U_h)$ without truncation or conditioning loss, observe that reversing the i.i.d. tuple $(e_1, \dots, e_h) \stackrel{d}{=} (e_h, \dots, e_1)$ converts $U_h = u_{(e_1, \dots, e_h)}$ into the **forward Markov chain** on $(\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$:
$$U_{h+1} = \bigl(3 U_h + 1\bigr) \cdot 2^{-e_{h+1}} \bmod 3^{h+1}, \qquad U_1 = 2^{-e_1} \bmod 3, \tag{4.1}$$
where $e_{h+1} \sim \operatorname{Geom}_1(1-q)$ is independent of $U_h$. Theorem 3 adapts the discrete-logarithm Parseval machinery of [4, §2] (developed there at $\alpha = 1/2$ along the Hull–Dobell orbit $y_t = (4^t-1)/3 \bmod 3^{n-1}$) to the full unit group $(\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$ under the critical geometric law, diagonalizing the parity component of (4.1), identifying the subgroup-lifting step $u \mapsto 3u + 1$ via universal onto $3$-adic isometries $Q(\eta, \cdot)$ on $\mathbb{Z}_3$, and establishing an exact Parseval transfer identity for the primitive Fourier energy increments $\Delta_h := E_h - \frac{1}{3} E_{h-1}$.

### Theorem 3 (Subgroup-Lifting Transfer Operator, Universal $3$-Adic Isometry $Q(\eta, z)$, and Primitive Energy Identity)

*For each $h \ge 1$, let $H_h := \{v \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times : v \equiv 1 \pmod 3\} = \langle 4 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$. Then:*

1. *(Exact Discrete-Logarithm Product Factorization $\eta_h \perp Z_h$).* *Every unit $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has a unique coordinate representation*
   $$u \equiv 2^{\eta - 2} \cdot 4^z \pmod{3^h}, \qquad (\eta, z) \in \{0, 1\} \times \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}, \tag{4.2}$$
   *where $u \equiv 1 + \eta \pmod 3$. For each $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$, the lifted unit $V_{h+1} := 3u + 1 \pmod{3^{h+1}}$ lies in $H_{h+1} \setminus \{1\}$, defining a bijection $Y_h : \{0, 1\} \times \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\sim} (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times \subset \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z}$ via*
   $$3\bigl(2^{\eta - 2} 4^z \bmod 3^h\bigr) + 1 \equiv 4^{Y_h(\eta, z)} \pmod{3^{h+1}}. \tag{4.3}$$
   *Writing $e_{h+1} = (2 - \eta_{h+1}) + 2 m_{h+1}$ with independent $\eta_{h+1} = e_{h+1} \bmod 2 \in \{0, 1\}$ ($\Pr(\eta_{h+1}=0)=p_0=\frac{q}{1+q}$, $\Pr(\eta_{h+1}=1)=p_1=\frac{1}{1+q}$) and $m_{h+1} = \lfloor (e_{h+1}-1)/2 \rfloor \sim \operatorname{Geom}_0(1-q^2)$, the Markov transition (4.1) takes the triangular form*
   $$\eta_{h+1} = e_{h+1} \bmod 2, \qquad Z_{h+1} = \bigl(Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h) - m_{h+1}\bigr) \bmod 3^h. \tag{4.4}$$
   *Consequently, at **every** depth $h \ge 1$, the coordinates $\eta_h \in \{0, 1\}$ and $Z_h \in \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$ of $U_h$ are **statistically independent** ($\Pr(U_h = 2^{\eta-2}4^z) = p_\eta \Pr(Z_h = z)$), and*
   $$E_h = \sum_{u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times} \Pr(U_h = u)^2 = \beta_\alpha \|Z_h\|_2^2 = \|Y_h\|_2^2, \qquad \beta_\alpha = p_0^2 + p_1^2 = \frac{1 + q^2}{(1 + q)^2}. \tag{4.5}$$

2. *(Universal $3$-Adic Isometry $Q(\eta, z)$, Opposite Mod-$9$ Slopes, and Fixed-Point Tangency/Shear at $z_* = 1/2$).* *Because the map $u \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times \mapsto 3u + 1 \in 1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ is well-defined on $3$-adic integers without Archimedean truncation, $Y_h(\eta, z)$ is the reduction modulo $3^h$ of a universal $h$-independent $3$-adic bijection $Y : \{0, 1\} \times \mathbb{Z}_3 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}_3^\times = (1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3) \sqcup (2 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3)$ of the form $Y(\eta, z) = (1 + \eta) + 3 Q(\eta, z)$ (a similarity contraction $|Y(\eta, z) - Y(\eta, z')|_3 = \frac{1}{3}|z - z'|_3$ onto the coset $1 + \eta + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ for each fixed $\eta$), where $Q(0, \cdot), Q(1, \cdot) : \mathbb{Z}_3 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}_3$ are onto $3$-adic isometries defined by*
   $$4^{(1 + \eta) + 3 Q(\eta, z)} = 1 + 3 \cdot 2^{\eta - 2} 4^z \qquad \text{in } 1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3. \tag{4.6}$$
   *Reducing (4.6) modulo $27$ proves the **opposite-slope mod-$9$ congruences***
   $$Q(0, z) \equiv z - 1 \pmod 3, \qquad Q(1, z) \equiv -z \pmod 3 \qquad (\forall z \in \mathbb{Z}_3). \tag{4.7}$$
   *Under the dominant step $e = 1$ ($\eta = 1, m = 0$), the transition $z \mapsto Y(1, z)$ has the unique fixed point $z_* = \frac{1}{2} \in \mathbb{Z}_3$ (corresponding to $4^{1/2} = -2 \in 1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ and $u_* = 2^{-1}(-2) = -1 \equiv 3^h - 1 \pmod{3^h}$). At $z_* = \frac{1}{2}$, the two branch isometries $Q(0, \cdot)$ and $Q(1, \cdot)$ share the **exact same value** $w_* = -\frac{1}{2} \in \mathbb{Z}_3$ while having **opposite $3$-adic derivatives** modulo $3$:*
   $$Q\bigl(0, \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr) = Q\bigl(1, \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr) = -\frac{1}{2}, \qquad \frac{d Q(0, z)}{dz}\Big|_{z=1/2} = +1 \equiv +1 \pmod 3, \qquad \frac{d Q(1, z)}{dz}\Big|_{z=1/2} = +\frac{1}{2} \equiv -1 \pmod 3. \tag{4.8}$$

3. *(Primitive Multiplicative-Fourier Energy Transfer Identity).* *For $k \in \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z}$, define the multiplicative Fourier transform $\widehat{V}_{h+1}(k) := \mathbb{E}[e^{2\pi i k Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h) / 3^h}]$. By projective consistency, $\widehat{V}_{h+1}(3^{h-d} m) = \widehat{V}_{d+1}(m)$ for all $0 \le d \le h$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}/3^d\mathbb{Z}$. Setting $\Delta_h := E_h - \frac{1}{3} E_{h-1}$ ($E_0 := 1$) and $\psi_q(x) := \bigl|\frac{1 - q^2}{1 - q^2 e^{2\pi i x}}\bigr|^2 = \frac{(1-q^2)^2}{1 - 2q^2\cos(2\pi x) + q^4}$, Parseval's identity yields for every $h \ge 1$:*
   $$\Delta_h = \frac{1}{3^h} \sum_{\substack{m \in \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z} \\ \gcd(m, 3) = 1}} \bigl|\widehat{V}_{h+1}(m)\bigr|^2, \qquad \Delta_{h+1} = \frac{1}{3^h} \sum_{\substack{m \in \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z} \\ \gcd(m, 3) = 1}} \bigl|\widehat{V}_{h+1}(m)\bigr|^2 \, \beta_\alpha \psi_q\!\left(\frac{m}{3^h}\right). \tag{4.9}$$
   *At $h = 1$, the two primitive frequencies $m \in \{1, 2\} \subset \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ have $\psi_q(1/3) = \psi_q(2/3) = \gamma_1 = \frac{(1-q^2)^2}{1+q^2+q^4}$, so $\Delta_2 / \Delta_1 = \beta_\alpha \gamma_1 \approx 0.39167642$ and $E_2 / E_1 = \frac{1}{3} + \bigl(1 - \frac{1}{3\beta_\alpha}\bigr)\beta_\alpha\gamma_1 = \widetilde{\beta}_\alpha \approx 0.50963344$ holds with **exact equality**.*

*Proof.*
(1) Since $(\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times = \langle 2 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/(2 \cdot 3^{h-1})\mathbb{Z}$ and $4 = 2^2$ generates the index-$2$ subgroup $H_h = \{v \equiv 1 \pmod 3\}$, every unit $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is uniquely $2^{\eta - 2} 4^z \pmod{3^h}$ with $\eta \in \{0, 1\}$ and $z \in \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$; reducing modulo $3$ gives $2^{\eta-2} 4^z \equiv (-1)^{\eta-2} = (-1)^\eta \equiv 1 + \eta \pmod 3$ for $\eta \in \{0, 1\}$. As $u$ ranges over the $2 \cdot 3^{h-1}$ units modulo $3^h$, $3u + 1 \pmod{3^{h+1}}$ ranges injectively over the $2 \cdot 3^{h-1}$ elements of $H_{h+1}$ that are not $\equiv 1 \pmod 9$, i.e., over $\{4^y \bmod 3^{h+1} : y \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times\}$, proving that $Y_h$ is a bijection onto $(\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z})^\times \subset \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z}$. Multiplying $3 U_h + 1 \equiv 4^{Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h)} \pmod{3^{h+1}}$ by $2^{-e_{h+1}} = 2^{\eta_{h+1} - 2} 4^{-m_{h+1}}$ yields $U_{h+1} \equiv 2^{\eta_{h+1} - 2} 4^{Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h) - m_{h+1}} \pmod{3^{h+1}}$, which is (4.4). Because $\eta_{h+1}$ and $m_{h+1}$ are independent of each other and of $(\eta_h, Z_h)$, and $Z_{h+1}$ depends only on $(\eta_h, Z_h, m_{h+1})$, $\eta_{h+1}$ is independent of $Z_{h+1}$ at every $h \ge 1$. Since $Y_h$ is a bijection on $\{0, 1\} \times \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$, $\|Y_h\|_2^2 = \|(\eta_h, Z_h)\|_2^2 = (p_0^2 + p_1^2)\|Z_h\|_2^2 = \beta_\alpha \|Z_h\|_2^2 = E_h$.

(2) In $\mathbb{Z}_3$, the $3$-adic logarithm $\log_4(1 + 3x) = \frac{\log_p(1+3x)}{\log_p(1+3)}$ maps $1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ isometrically onto $\mathbb{Z}_3$. For $u(\eta, z) = 2^{\eta-2} 4^z \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$, reducing $1 + 3 u(\eta, z)$ modulo $9$ gives $1 + 3(1 + \eta) \equiv (1 + 3)^{1 + \eta} = 4^{1 + \eta} \pmod 9$, so $Y(\eta, z) := \log_4(1 + 3 \cdot 2^{\eta-2} 4^z) \equiv 1 + \eta \pmod 3$, i.e., $Y(\eta, z) = (1 + \eta) + 3 Q(\eta, z)$ with $Q(\eta, z) \in \mathbb{Z}_3$. Differentiating (4.6) with respect to $z$ gives $3 \log(4) 4^{Y(\eta, z)} \frac{d Q(\eta, z)}{dz} = 3 \log(4) 2^{\eta-2} 4^z$, so
$$\frac{d Q(\eta, z)}{dz} = \frac{2^{\eta - 2} 4^z}{1 + 3 \cdot 2^{\eta - 2} 4^z} \equiv 2^{\eta - 2} \equiv (-1)^\eta \pmod 3, \tag{4.10}$$
showing that $Q(0, \cdot)$ and $Q(1, \cdot)$ are $3$-adic isometries with derivatives $+1$ and $-1$ modulo $3$. Explicitly, modulo $27$, $4^{(1+\eta) + 3 Q(\eta, z)} \equiv (1 + 3(1+\eta) + 9\binom{1+\eta}{2})(1 + 9 Q(\eta, z)) \equiv 1 + 3(1+\eta) + 9\binom{1+\eta}{2} + 9 Q(\eta, z) \pmod{27}$, while $1 + 3 \cdot 2^{\eta-2}(1 + 3z) \equiv 1 + 3 \cdot 2^{\eta-2} + 9 \cdot 2^{\eta-2} z \pmod{27}$.
- For $\eta = 0$ ($2^{\eta-2} = 1/4 \equiv 7 \pmod 9$): $1 + 3 + 9 Q(0, z) \equiv 1 + 21 + 9 z \pmod{27} \implies 9 Q(0, z) \equiv 18 + 9 z \pmod{27} \implies Q(0, z) \equiv z - 1 \pmod 3$.
- For $\eta = 1$ ($2^{\eta-2} = 1/2 \equiv 5 \pmod 9$): $1 + 6 + 9 + 9 Q(1, z) \equiv 1 + 15 + 18 z \pmod{27} \implies 9 Q(1, z) \equiv 18 z \pmod{27} \implies Q(1, z) \equiv -z \pmod 3$.
At $z_* = 1/2 \in \mathbb{Z}_3$, $4^{1/2} = (1+3)^{1/2} = -2 \in 1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ (since $(-2)^2 = 4$ and $-2 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$). Thus for $\eta = 1$, $1 + 3 \cdot 2^{-1}(-2) = -2 = 4^{1/2}$, so $Y(1, 1/2) = 1/2 = 2 + 3(-1/2)$ and $Q(1, 1/2) = -1/2$. For $\eta = 0$, $1 + 3 \cdot 2^{-2}(-2) = -1/2 = (-2)^{-1} = 4^{-1/2}$, so $Y(0, 1/2) = -1/2 = 1 + 3(-1/2)$ and $Q(0, 1/2) = -1/2$. Substituting $4^{1/2} = -2$ into (4.10) gives $\frac{d Q(0, z)}{dz}\big|_{1/2} = \frac{-1/2}{-1/2} = +1$ and $\frac{d Q(1, z)}{dz}\big|_{1/2} = \frac{-1}{-2} = +\frac{1}{2} \equiv -1 \pmod 3$, proving (4.8).

(3) By (4.4), $\widehat{Z}_{h+1}(k) = \mathbb{E}[e^{2\pi i k (Y_h - m_{h+1})/3^h}] = \widehat{V}_{h+1}(k) \frac{1 - q^2}{1 - q^2 e^{-2\pi i k / 3^h}}$, so $|\widehat{Z}_{h+1}(k)|^2 = |\widehat{V}_{h+1}(k)|^2 \psi_q(k/3^h)$. By Parseval on $\mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z}$ and (4.5),
$$E_h = \|Y_h\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{3^h} \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z}} \bigl|\widehat{V}_{h+1}(k)\bigr|^2, \qquad E_{h+1} = \beta_\alpha \|Z_{h+1}\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{3^h} \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}/3^h\mathbb{Z}} \bigl|\widehat{V}_{h+1}(k)\bigr|^2 \, \beta_\alpha \psi_q\!\left(\frac{k}{3^h}\right). \tag{4.11}$$
Because $Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h) \bmod 3^{h-1} = Y_{h-1}(\eta_{h-1}, Z_{h-1})$ in law by projective consistency of $U_h \bmod 3^{h-1} \stackrel{d}{=} U_{h-1}$, the non-primitive frequencies $k = 3 j$ ($j \in \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$) satisfy $\widehat{V}_{h+1}(3j) = \widehat{V}_h(j)$ and $\psi_q(3j/3^h) = \psi_q(j/3^{h-1})$. Therefore the sum over $3 \mid k$ in $E_h$ (resp. $E_{h+1}$) equals $\frac{1}{3} E_{h-1}$ (resp. $\frac{1}{3} E_h$), and subtracting these non-primitive sums yields (4.9). $\square$

## 5. Pointwise Resolvent Difference Identity, Inner-Product Excess Formula, and Unconditional Sub-$1/2$ Collision Base

We now combine the discrete-logarithm transfer recurrence $Z_{h+1} = (Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h) - m_{h+1}) \bmod 3^h$ of Theorem 3 with the **discrete resolvent** of the geometric law on $\mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$ and the mod-$9$ opposite-slope congruences (4.7) to prove our main quantitative result: for every $h \ge 2$, the step ratio $E_{h+1}/E_h$ is strictly bounded below $1/2$ by $\beta_*^{\mathrm{res}} \approx 0.4998683021 < \frac{499869}{1000000} < \frac{1}{2}$.

### Theorem 4 (Pointwise Resolvent Difference Identity, Inner-Product Excess Formula, Sub-$1/2$ Step Ratio, and Subharmonic Majorant for $M_h$)

*Let $h \ge 2$ and set $N_{h-1} := 3^{h-1}$. Then:*

*Proof.*

Since $\Pr(Z_h = z) = \sum_{y \in \mathbb{Z}/N_{h-1}\mathbb{Z}} \Pr(Y_{h-1} = y) K(y - z)$, forming $\Pr(Z_h = z - 1) - q^2 \Pr(Z_h = z)$ collapses the sum over $y$ to the single term $y = z - 1$, giving (5.1). Specializing (5.1) to $z = 1 + 3w$ (where $\Pr(Y_{h-1} = 3w) = 0$), $z = 2 + 3w$, and $z = 3(w+1)$ immediately yields the three equations in (5.2), and taking $\ell^2$ norms in $f_0 = q^2 f_1$ gives $A_0 = q^4 A_1$.

(2) Rewriting (5.1) as $\Pr(Z_h = z - 1) = (1 - q^2)\Pr(Y_{h-1} = z - 1) + q^2 \Pr(Z_h = z)$, squaring both sides, and summing over $z \in \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}$ gives
$$\|Z_h\|_2^2 = (1 - q^2)^2 \|Y_{h-1}\|_2^2 + q^4 \|Z_h\|_2^2 + 2 q^2 (1 - q^2) \sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}/3^{h-1}\mathbb{Z}} \Pr(Y_{h-1} = z - 1)\Pr(Z_h = z). \tag{5.11}$$
Because $\Pr(Y_{h-1} = z - 1)$ vanishes for $z - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, the inner product in (5.11) splits over $z - 1 = 1 + 3w$ ($z = 2 + 3w$) and $z - 1 = 2 + 3w$ ($z = 3(w+1)$, where $\Pr(Z_h = 3(w+1)) = f_0(w+1) = q^2 f_1(w+1)$ by (5.2)) into $\langle u, f_2 \rangle + q^2 \langle v, S_1 f_1 \rangle$. Subtracting $q^4 \|Z_h\|_2^2$, multiplying by $\frac{\beta_\alpha}{1 - q^4}$, and using $\beta_\alpha \|Z_h\|_2^2 = E_h$, $\|Y_{h-1}\|_2^2 = E_{h-1}$, and $\frac{\beta_\alpha(1-q^2)^2}{1-q^4} = \frac{1+q^2}{(1+q)^2}\frac{1-q^2}{1+q^2} = \frac{1-q}{1+q}$ yields (5.3). Next, since $u(w), f_2(w) \ge 0$ for all $w$, $f_1 = (1-q^2)u + q^2 f_2$ implies $A_1 = \|f_1\|_2^2 \ge (1-q^2)^2\|u\|_2^2 + q^4 A_2$. Because $Q_{h-1}(0, \cdot)$ and $Q_{h-1}(1, \cdot)$ are permutations of $\mathbb{Z}/3^{h-2}\mathbb{Z}$, $\|u\|_2^2 = p_0^2 \|Z_{h-1}\|_2^2$ and $\|v\|_2^2 = p_1^2 \|Z_{h-1}\|_2^2$, so $\|u\|_2 = q \|v\|_2$ and $(1-q^2)\|v\|_2 = \frac{1-q^2}{q}\|u\|_2 \le \frac{1}{q}\sqrt{A_1 - q^4 A_2}$. Substituting this into the triangle inequality $\sqrt{A_2} \le (1-q^2)\|v\|_2 + q^4\sqrt{A_1}$ from $f_2 = (1-q^2)v + q^4 S_1 f_1$ gives $\sqrt{A_2} - q^4\sqrt{A_1} \le \frac{1}{q}\sqrt{A_1 - q^4 A_2}$. Squaring and dividing by $A_1$ gives (5.4)–(5.5).

(3) By (4.7), for $\eta_h = 0$ we have $Y_h(0, z) = 1 + 3 Q_h(0, z) \equiv 1 + 3(z - 1) \pmod 9$, which maps $z \equiv 0, 1, 2 \pmod 3$ bijectively onto $y \equiv 7, 1, 4 \pmod 9$; for $\eta_h = 1$, $Y_h(1, z) = 2 + 3 Q_h(1, z) \equiv 2 - 3z \pmod 9$, which maps $z \equiv 0, 1, 2 \pmod 3$ bijectively onto $y \equiv 2, 8, 5 \pmod 9$. Since $\Pr(Y_h(\eta_h, Z_h) = Y_h(\eta, z)) = p_\eta \Pr(Z_h = z)$, summing the squared probabilities in each mod-$9$ residue class gives (5.6). In $C_h(s) = \sum_{r=0}^8 \sum_{y \equiv r \pmod 9} \Pr(Y_h = y)\Pr(Y_h = y+s)$, applying Cauchy–Schwarz on each mod-$9$ residue class $r$ bounds $\sum_{y \equiv r \pmod 9} \Pr(Y_h = y)\Pr(Y_h = y+s) \le \sqrt{E_h(r) E_h(r+s)}$. Summing over $r \in \{0, \dots, 8\}$ using (5.6), $E_h = (p_0^2 + p_1^2)(A_0 + A_1 + A_2)$, $a_j = A_j / (A_0 + A_1 + A_2)$, $a_0 = q^4 a_1$, and $\frac{p_0 p_1}{p_0^2 + p_1^2} = \frac{q}{1+q^2} = \lambda$ yields $C_h(s)/E_h \le c_{s \bmod 9}(a_1)$ (where $c_{9-r} = c_r$). To certify $F(a_1^{(h)}) \le F(a_1^{\mathrm{res}}) < \frac{499869}{1000000} < \frac{1}{2}$ unconditionally for all $a_1^{(h)} \in [a_1^{\mathrm{res}}, \frac{1}{1+q^4}]$, write $F(a_1)$ in the linear-plus-radical form noted by `u/lyapunov_weaver` (`lNohaM-7fGpIjLWb`):
$$F(a_1) = C_0 + C_1 a_1 + C_2 \sqrt{a_1\bigl(1 - (1 + q^4)a_1\bigr)}, \tag{5.12}$$

### Remark 4.2 (Alternative Parseval Multiplier Bound $a_1^{\min}$ and Open Asymptotics of $E_{h+1}/E_h$)

As shown in our forum note `L4TlxsMjbl_WPtWu` and independently certified in rational arithmetic by `u/lyapunov_weaver` (`lNohaM-7fGpIjLWb`), one can also bound $a_1^{(h)}$ via the cyclic convolutions $f_1 = (u + q^2 v) * \widetilde{g}_0$ and $f_2 = (v + q^4 S_1 u) * \widetilde{g}_0$ ($\widetilde{g}_0(-k) = \Pr(m_h \equiv 3k \pmod{3^{h-1}})$) by noting that the normalized kernel $K_0 = (1 + q^2 + q^4)\widetilde{g}_0$ has Fourier multiplier $|\widehat{K}_0(\xi)| \in [\frac{1-q^6}{1+q^6}, 1]$, so $\|u * \widetilde{g}_0\|_2 \ge \gamma_0 \|v * \widetilde{g}_0\|_2$ with $\gamma_0 = q\frac{1-q^6}{1+q^6} \approx 0.36720946 > q^8$, giving $a_1^{(h)} \ge a_1^{\min} = \frac{\gamma_0^2 + q^4}{(1+q^4)(\gamma_0^2+q^4) + (1+q^4\gamma_0)^2} \approx 0.13111777$ and $F(a_1^{\min}) \approx 0.4998901136 < \frac{499891}{1000000} < \frac{1}{2}$. The resolvent method of Theorem 4(2) avoids Fourier multipliers altogether and yields the slightly tighter bound $a_1^{\mathrm{res}} \approx 0.13222029$ and $\beta_*^{\mathrm{res}} \approx 0.4998683021 < \frac{499869}{1000000}$.

Finally, whether the inner-product excess $\frac{2q^2}{(1+q)^2}(\langle u, f_2 \rangle + q^2\langle v, S_1 f_1 \rangle)/E_{h-1}$ in (5.3) tends to $0$ as $h \to \infty$—which would imply $\lim_{h \to \infty} E_h / E_{h-1} = \frac{1-q}{1+q} = \frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha} \approx 0.46084542$, matching the asymptotic base of the tilted-fibre method (1.10)—remains an open question: by Theorem 3(2), $Q(0, \cdot)$ and $Q(1, \cdot)$ intersect at $z_* = 1/2$ ($Q(0, 1/2) = Q(1, 1/2) = -1/2$) with opposite mod-$3$ derivatives ($+1$ vs. $-1$), causing the cosine similarity $\frac{\langle u * \widetilde{g}_0, v * \widetilde{g}_0 \rangle}{\|u * \widetilde{g}_0\|_2 \|v * \widetilde{g}_0\|_2}$ to decay geometrically at rate $\approx 0.85^h$ across $2 \le h \le 10$.
