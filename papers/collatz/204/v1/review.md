# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, clearly delimited obstruction to using a prescribed smaller Collatz partner as a substitute for direct descent. Its contribution is the explicit separation for $m=(27n+7)/32$, together with a finite criterion explaining when a meeting persists under dyadic lifting. This is a useful partial result about certificate searches within the problem's scope. It neither proves convergence of all positive orbits nor establishes a counterexample.

The decisive argument in Theorem 2 is complete. The eight-step affine prefix proves $D(n)=8$ throughout the stated residue class. Independently, both reference orbits remain below $2^{20}$, while the lifted perturbations are divisible by $2^{20}$ throughout the prescribed horizon. A lifted meeting therefore requires equality of the reference states and equality of the perturbation slopes. The latter forces $i=j+5$ and $A_i-B_j=3$. The first reference equality on that clock line instead has odd-count difference $15$, which persists thereafter. This excludes every pair of clocks in the finite budget, rather than merely excluding the reference meeting times. The construction's dependence on the deadline is stated correctly; it does not produce one pair that never meets.

Proposition 1 correctly reduces uniform finite coverage to an affine identity: without an identical pair of affine functions, finitely many clock pairs exclude all but finitely many lift parameters. The comparison propositions also have substantive arguments. In Proposition 3, divisibility by $3^H$ supplies the integrality needed for the inverse-word shadow argument after the forward climb has been undone. In Proposition 4, the positive residual in the telescoping identity bounds the total number of clocks with insufficient slope; the finite union over the negative reference starts then supplies a common good window. Neither argument assumes convergence of negative trajectories. The restart calculation in Section 5 supports the stated $16/31$ density for this particular certification rule, with the geometric remainder controlling arbitrarily many restarts.

I reproduced the supplied standalone verifier using `python3 -I -B input/supplement/verify.py`. All five groups of checks passed, including the reference states and odd counts, delayed lifts through horizon 1000, inverse-tree controls through horizon 25, consecutive-start excursion fixtures, restart examples, and the 64 theta classes. These computations corroborate finite certificates; the unrestricted conclusions depend on the written proofs. I did not reproduce the exploratory census campaigns, and the paper appropriately does not use their residual percentages as universal evidence.

The novelty is narrower than a new theory of Collatz coalescence, but sufficient for acceptance. The parity-affine machinery is classical and credited to [2,3]. The published versions of [4–6] concern optimization over all smaller visitors; their results do not establish this fixed-partner separation with constant descent time. Paper [7] gives stronger orbit-prefix exclusions in a different displacement regime, without the present fixed affine ratio and neighborhood-of-a-coalescing-seed conclusion. The inverse-minimality results in [8] and supplied-pair bounds in [12] likewise address different quantifiers. The negative-prefix transfer in [10] is acknowledged; Proposition 4 adds the reciprocal-slope argument needed for a simultaneous high excursion.

The external literature check confirms that Ansari [1] supplies the recursive-sufficiency framework, rather than the new delay construction. Leen's *Late synchronization and the geometry of Collatz families* [14] gives late-synchronization laws and failure of uniform relative tails over exponential offset envelopes. Those results concern synchronization and offset populations, rather than this deterministic exclusion of all unequal-clock meetings near a fixed reference pair. I found no substantial duplication of the central theorem in these comparisons.

The main text communicates the question, mechanism, evidence, and limitations clearly. The abstract is informative, and the chronological research record is appropriately relegated to the supplement. Three minor improvements would sharpen presentation and attribution:

- Add the external synchronization comparison above to the related-work discussion. Also acknowledge the older paired-parity and role-exchange constructions of Garner, documented in Lagarias's bibliography [15], when introducing the restart mechanism.
- State explicitly that $16/31$ is a natural density relative to the parameter $k\ge0$ and measures success of the specified rule, not the density of all pairs that eventually coalesce.
- Consider moving the moving-sum and theta diagnostics to an appendix. They are correctly qualified, but contribute less to the central separation theorem than Sections 2–5.
