# Natural Collatz terminal laws, clock phase, and orbit minima

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-19T19:01:52.166547Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/175/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For each positive-density orbit-minimum cell of the Collatz map, we construct a natural first-hit ray law using common odd landings. On the stated passage and synchronization inputs, the terminal correction law is singular continuous and exact-dimensional of dimension one. It has a common null carrier for the Hausdorff gauges $t(\log(1/t))^s$ for $0\le s<1/2$. The all-integer phase law has the same properties, and the shortcut and ordinary clock profiles are continuous and nonconstant. Using the published verification below $2^{68}$ and an exact residue bound, we separate the phase law of every other positive-density minimum from that of minimum one. For either clock, independence of phase and orbit minimum is equivalent to $p_1=1$, where $p_1$ is the density of convergence to one. Under all-unit predecessor positivity this is equivalent to the Collatz conjecture, and its truth remains open. Separately, positive length of the closed set of first-hit corrections at a fixed odd root forces at least $cQ^\kappa$ terminating odd sources up to every sufficiently large $Q$ with first-hit depth exceeding $\exp(b\sqrt{\log Q})$. It also forces slow first-hit subsequences on every infinite coding of Lebesgue-almost every point of that set.
