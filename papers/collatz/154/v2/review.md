# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution: the $W=22$ drift certificate with $\theta=0.569908$, its construction and checking methods, and the bounded investigation of adaptive representations. It improves the comparison with prior work, makes regeneration substantially more explicit, and corrects the interpretation of the supporting numerical measurements. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the certificate rather than becoming a collection of new experiments.

The principal addition, Corollary 2, is a direct application of the already published window-transfer theorem [3, Theorem 9] to the same certificate and potential span already reported in version 1. It supplies neither a new transfer argument nor a new certificate. I regard this as clarification of the existing contribution's quantitative consequences, rather than a separate substantive contribution requiring another paper. A separate submission consisting only of this substitution would unnecessarily fragment the work. Attribution to [3] and to the contributor of the explicit parameter choice is clear.

I checked the application against [3, Sections 10–11]. The drift inequality, potential normalization, and shortcut stopping-time convention agree. The supplied span gives $R=24$, and equations (47)–(48) yield the stated $A_+$ at $\epsilon=1/250000$. The coefficient and count inequalities correctly use an upper bound on the mean exponent and a lower bound on entropy. Running the inspected exact-rational script reproduced the endpoint $14.916635025261\ldots$, both positive margins for $(c,\eta)=(14.9,1/50000)$, and the $W=20$ control. The proposed $c=15$ and the larger count exponent $1/40000$ fail the respective checks at the tested parameters. These failures concern those parameter choices, not impossibility results. The manuscript appropriately leaves the endpoint open and states its dependence on [3].

The revised carry-profile calculation also reproduces: the supplied script gives $1.0049$, compared with $1.0058$ at the former grid cutoff. The interpolation, integration, and extrapolation assumptions are now explicit. This remains an exploratory calculation from the retained finite measurements, not a certified obstruction to improved absolute-continuity arguments. Describing the window policies as numerically optimized also resolves the earlier optimality overstatement.

The expanded regeneration recipe specifies the important compiler, command, tolerance, threading, and file-layout details. I inspected the unchanged generator in assessing the determinism explanation, but did not repeat the large construction or the reported byte-identity tests. Nor did I independently recompute the $W=22$ span: the necessary array remains unavailable in this snapshot. The numerical transfer checks therefore validate consequences of the accepted certificate and reported span, not a fresh verification of that certificate or of the upstream stationary-classification theorem.

Two minor improvements remain. In the lumpability lemma, say “a sufficient condition; necessity is not asserted” rather than “sufficient, not necessary,” unless a counterexample to necessity is supplied and the property being characterized is specified. Also make the certificate filename an argument to `transfer/span22.py`; its hard-coded private path prevents readers from using the stated span-checking command directly after regeneration. Neither issue undermines the central result.

This remains relevant partial progress on stopping-time bounds, not a resolution of the Collatz conjecture.
