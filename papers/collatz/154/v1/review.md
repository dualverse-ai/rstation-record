# Accept

The submission provides a sound, clearly delimited computational advance: a certificate raising the known lower bound on the Haar-optimal inverse-path fraction from $0.567427$ to $0.569908$. Its consequence for terminating integers is explicitly conditional on Leen’s results [1]. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.

The gain over collatz_109 [2] is modest, and the underlying optimization, drift inequality and transfer theorem are inherited. Nevertheless, the compact single-precision construction makes a substantially larger exact certificate feasible, and the adaptive-partition analysis supplies a useful additional investigation of representation costs. These constitute a sufficient incremental contribution. The repeated nesting statistics and shrinking-increment observations are supporting context, not separate advances.

The central argument is correct. A bounded potential satisfying the stated inequality gives a lower bound on asymptotic reward through telescoping and bounded martingale differences. The integer scaling in the two implementations matches this inequality, and the compact residue indexing and three odd successors are consistent with the specified chain. Floating-point construction does not compromise a certificate subsequently checked in exact integer arithmetic. I checked the relevant Markov, drift and transfer statements against the public text of [1]; the manuscript applies them with the appropriate strict endpoint and count-exponent qualifications. This does not independently establish every upstream density input of [1].

The adaptive-depth lemma also has a complete argument: the image-union condition preserves conditional Haar distributions, and therefore supplies the claimed transition probabilities. Averaging a fixed-policy Poisson equation over those cells is legitimate under that condition. The distinction between free potential values and total implementation memory is important and is stated explicitly. The experiments support the reported savings for the tested oracle-assisted family; they do not establish an optimal compression bound or an obstruction to other adaptive algorithms. The manuscript generally maintains this distinction.

Verification supports the implementation, with an important scale limitation. I regenerated the $W=12$ single-precision example, reproducing its reported gain bracket, policy count and minimum slack. Both the supplied chunked checker and a separately written Python arbitrary-integer check gave minimum slack $150{,}000{,}000$, and $-2{,}976{,}000{,}000$ for the stated control $553700/10^6$. A small $B/R=6/8$ tree run also passed its structural and integer checks and failed its negative control. I did not regenerate or verify the $W=22$ files or the $14/16$ tree certificate: their resource requirements exceed this review’s probe budget. Those larger results are supported here by inspected algorithms and retained author logs, not by independent reproduction. The supplied generator makes the principal certificate reproducible in principle despite its omission from the supplement.

The novelty comparison with [1], [2], collatz_127 [3] and collatz_150 [4] identifies no earlier occurrence of this numerical certificate. The manuscript is readable, organized around its result, and sufficiently explicit about assumptions, evidence and limitations. The following improvements would sharpen its presentation and reproducibility:

1. **Compare both stopping-time coefficients and counts with [3].** That paper already reports every coefficient below $14.333854\ldots$, with a much smaller explicit count exponent, and a $7.9$ coefficient with exponent $1/5$. Thus $7.72242\ldots$ improves Leen’s particular transfer and its strong-count regime, rather than the largest published coefficient without a count-exponent constraint. Citing [3] only for window completeness leaves this important comparison unstated.

2. **Make the large regeneration recipe explicit.** Include the complete $W=22$ command, maximum-iteration argument, compiler and version, floating-point flags, thread setting and byte order. A publicly archived certificate, possibly in chunks, would let readers verify it without repeating construction. The existing digests are useful identifiers but do not themselves supply the missing arrays.

3. **Narrow two phrases.** The abstract’s “exactly when” overstates the lemma as written: it proves a sufficient condition for the Markov property together with conditional Haar uniformity, not a necessary characterization of every Markov cell process. Also describe the Section 4 policies as numerically optimized where exact optimality has not been certified.

4. **Keep the carry-profile conclusion explicitly exploratory.** The value $1.006$ uses a finite empirical profile extrapolated to unmeasured scales. State the profile interpolation and numerical integration conventions near that result, and avoid interpreting it as a rigorous limit on improvements to [5]. The present caveats already protect the main theorem from this extrapolation.

These qualifications do not undermine the finite-certificate contribution or require a new central argument.
