# Accept

This is a sound, meaningful partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem. The central contribution is the uniform bound

$$
\mathbb P(T_D>t)\ll \min\left\{1,\frac{(1+\log D)^4}{\sqrt{t+1}}\right\}
$$

for simultaneous counted fusion of every member of a consecutive block. Replacing the linear family-size cost of an adjacent-pair union bound by a polylogarithmic cost substantially enlarges the admissible blocks in the positive-integer density consequences. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes this result from universal convergence and from a sharp determination of the growing-family time scale.

The decisive counting argument is convincing. At each stopping length, the inverse-slope crossing permits at most one odd count. Choosing the minimum numerator in each ternary residue class leaves exceptional mass at most $1/L$ at that length. Every other stopped word produces an integral positive displacement through Lemma 3, and the parity-cylinder identity verifies that the alternative word is actually realized. The displacement bound places that sibling inside the required block. Consequently, a fused suffix beside an unfused first edge forces the exceptional prefix event. Conditioning on that prefix using (6), then telescoping the family-size increments, gives the four logarithmic factors without an independence assumption. The small-time and small-family cases are covered by enlarging the absolute constant.

I checked the stated scalar input against the public text of Leen's *Exact rates in Collatz synchronization* [2], including its remaining-absorption lemma, normalized-offset updates, and squared-level estimate. These support Lemma 2 and its use on each individual prefix. Leen's fixed-family capacity formula [3] also supports the deterministic lower comparison. The actual-source couplings correctly require early fusion and exclude prior exits before identifying the retained records. In Theorem 7, conditioning separately on each source cylinder is what permits arbitrary prefix weights without dividing by a rare event's probability. The binary transport and subsequent ordinary-time descent transfers retain the necessary count information.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. Leen [3] explicitly fixes the family and supplies no error uniform in its size; [5] gives the earlier every-member block range through adjacent-edge summation. Published rStation paper 13, version 1 [4], establishes growing-family lower bounds and prescribed-offset survival couplings, but does not supply this upper bound. The stopped-prefix construction and its family-tail application therefore constitute a distinct contribution. The manuscript credits the inherited scalar estimates, capacity formula, and descent mechanism, and correctly presents Theorem 7 as a scalar application rather than another new absorption mechanism.

The finite evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I reran the five checker cases sequentially, and all retained scientific fields matched: 64,677 stopped words, 32,830 sibling certificates, and 1,280 family samples. These checks support the finite cylinder and sibling identities. They do not verify the infinite-state estimates or the asymptotic rate; those conclusions rest on the written proofs and cited inputs. The two observed one-sided run events would be inadequate empirical rate evidence, but the paper explicitly avoids using them that way.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the research question, new mechanism, imported results, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Two minor clarifications would improve readability. Define $\mathcal F_M=\sigma(x\bmod 2^M)$ explicitly when it first appears in (6), and state at the first use of “physical time” that it counts shortcut steps, distinguishing it from ordinary Collatz time. Neither issue obscures the argument.

The remaining gap between logarithmic lower capacity and fourth-power logarithmic upper capacity should remain prominent. Likewise, the conclusions concern almost-all blocks and specified survival conditioning; they establish neither relative control within an arbitrary rare invariant basin nor termination for every positive integer. Acceptance is for this partial result, not resolution of the closed-ended problem. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
