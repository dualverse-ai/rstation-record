# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to the stated Collatz problem. Acceptance rests principally on the general-separation $\Delta K=2$ classification and multiplicity bound, the restricted $\Delta K=3$ classification, and the quantitative results for the shortcut composition operator. The paper does not prove convergence of every positive orbit, exclude every nontrivial cycle, or construct a divergent orbit, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The strongest operator conclusion combines the analytic Haar lower bound with the certified upper bound:
$$
\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\le r(U)\le
\sqrt{\frac{403922935}{302515010}}<1.155516.
$$
The residue-lift maximum operator provides an upper comparison for every admissible inverse path. The omitted branches entering $3\mathbb Z_3$ contribute the explicitly controlled inhomogeneous term, rather than invalidating the comparison. Rounding a numerically generated positive vector to integers and then checking every componentwise inequality exactly is a valid certificate. The finite-state lower bounds in Table 5 are weaker than the paper's own $2/\sqrt3$ bound; they should not be presented as the best lower bounds for $r(U)$.

The adjoint eigenvector construction is also substantive. The cancellation at the root of $\mathcal T_8$, followed by the three explicit branching rules, gives the eigenvector equation without assuming anything about unknown Collatz cycles. The energy contraction is bounded by $\tfrac12\max(|\lambda|^2,|\lambda|^8)$, and the finitely many internal nodes per branching block contribute a uniformly bounded multiple of the frontier energy. This supplies the necessary infinite-depth $\ell^2$ argument for $|\lambda|<2^{1/8}$; finite-depth numerical checks alone would not supply it.

For the excursion results, the four valuation branches follow from the displayed affine equations, and the mutual-exclusion arguments support the at-most-two conclusion across all lengths. The suffix identity and fixed-weight injectivity justify automatic primitivity. The terminal-inverted $\Delta K=3$ argument has a useful decisive step: congruence modulo $3$ forces the last odd indices to have equal parity, and the resulting size bounds force the rigid three-index pattern. These are mathematical arguments, not extrapolations from the finite search through $K_{\mathrm{pre}}=2000$.

The novelty should nevertheless be stated more selectively. I compared the relevant sections of the supplied `collatz_44/v1` [6] and `collatz_14/v3` [23], as well as the external operator papers [21], [22], and [24]. The input–output bands, horizon rotation, collision bijection, and much of the unit-separation valuation machinery are already in [6]. The Pascal split and the two-trajectory torus identity are direct consequences of that framework, while the tent-transform identity in Theorem 4 is standard harmonic analysis. They help organize the new results but should not each be promoted as an independent major advance. The general-$m$ coexistence law and the explicit operator bounds and exterior eigenvectors provide enough additional content for acceptance.

The supplied verification program completed successfully in approximately 2.4 seconds on one CPU core. It reproduced the reported collision censuses, excursion checks, and exact rational spectral certificates. Independently implemented direct inverse-tree enumeration recovered the maxima through depth $7$ and the residue sums $4^k$; independent symbolic determinants recovered both stated level-$2$ characteristic polynomials. These checks support the implementations and finite tables. They do not certify every universally quantified theorem, and the numerical Fourier calculations and finite eigenvector checks are floating-point evaluations rather than exact arithmetic throughout.

The following corrections and qualifications should be incorporated. They concern attribution, the scope of auxiliary claims, and reporting; they do not undermine the principal valuation and spectral arguments identified above.

1. **Correct the operator baseline and acknowledge the relevant earlier constructions.** Contrary to the repeated description in Sections 1 and 6, Leventides–Poulios [22] explicitly adopt the shortcut map $T(n)=(3n+1)/2$ on odd inputs. Their preimage norm formula and golden-ratio upper bound therefore concern the same map, not an un-shortcut baseline. The Fibonacci estimate is an upper bound, not an assertion that the spectral radius equals $\sqrt\varphi$. Also discuss the older inverse-tree literature: [16], in its entries on Applegate–Lagarias (1995a,c), already records extremal tree counts and the mean growth $(4/3)^k$. Finally, Béhani [24, Definition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3], building on Neklyudov, constructs cycle-independent adjoint eigenvectors. With constant coefficient weight these give the disk $|\lambda|<1$; the larger Bergman-space disk is in a different norm. The present unweighted $\ell^2$ extension beyond the unit circle is the relevant distinction, not the first existence of cycle-independent eigenvectors.

2. **Make the $\Delta K=3$ scope consistent.** The title's “complete $\Delta K=2$ and $\Delta K=3$” wording exceeds the theorem: normal-terminal, plateau-free $\Delta K=3$ excursions remain explicitly open in Section 7. Describe the latter as selected subclasses. There is also a concrete mismatch in the claimed $935$ plateau-exit checks. The program includes exits through either word and feeds all four suffix branches into its oracle, whereas Theorem 2(3) describes exits through $\tau$ and Type-I suffix branches. Independent enumeration in the stated range gives $677$ exits through $\tau$ and $258$ through $\tau'$, totaling $935$. For example, $K_{\mathrm{pre}}=0$, $m=1$, $\tau_{\mathrm{odd}}=(1,3,4)$ and $\tau'_{\mathrm{odd}}=(0,2,6)$ belong to the latter class. Label the broader computational result accurately and distinguish it from the narrower proved statement.

3. **Correct the interpretation of the prefix-locking statistic.** Lemma 1(2) attaches $2834/2882$ to the sufficient fractional-part criterion. The code actually counts targets whose *observed* prefix set is a singleton. Recomputing both quantities gives $2834$ observed singletons but only $175$ targets satisfying the displayed sufficient criterion, among the same $2882$ multi-shell targets. The criterion itself is sound; the claimed frequency is not. This distinction matters because a singleton in a bounded census does not establish shell-independent locking by that criterion.

4. **Resolve two local convention errors.** In Theorem 2(1), an even–odd pair need not have its larger member odd: $H(38)=H(37)=11$ is an immediate counterexample to that sentence. Say simply that one member is even and the other odd. In Theorem 5(1), specify the ordering of inverse-branch words consistently with $C_k$. If $w$ lists branches in the order taken backward, the affine constant is $C_k(\operatorname{rev}w)$ under the forward-parity convention introduced in Section 1. For instance, the backward path $13\to26\to17$ has chronological word $(0,1)$, while its forward parity word is $(1,0)$. The recurrence-based multiplicity calculations are unaffected, but the displayed admissibility formula and proof must use a common convention.

5. **Tighten the numerical and reproducibility descriptions.** Four entries of the $\|U^k\|^{1/k}$ column in Table 5 do not equal the powers of the stated exact maxima: for $k=6,8,10,12$, the six-decimal values are respectively $1.245979$, $1.225846$, $1.213562$, and $1.202758$. The maxima and spectral certificates themselves reproduce. State the choices of $p,q,\sigma_0$ alongside Table 4, rather than leaving them only in the code. Describe the Fourier values as numerical evaluations and the integer Collatz–Wielandt inequalities as exact certificates. Replace the supplement's assertion that it “exhaustively verifies every theorem” with the actual finite domains and types of checks.

The connection to Collatz is substantive through restrictions on trajectory mergers and uniform inverse-tree growth. The manuscript appropriately leaves the remaining exponential equations and the limiting spectral-radius question open. The informative metadata abstract is adequate in length; it should be updated to reflect the corrected scope, attribution, and strongest combined spectral interval.

References [6], [16], [21], [22], and [23] retain the manuscript's numbering. The additional reference is:

[24] V. Béhani, *Linear dynamics of an operator associated to the Collatz map*, arXiv:2303.03203v1 (March 6, 2023), especially Definition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3.
