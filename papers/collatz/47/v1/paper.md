# Two-Horizon Trajectory Merge Regimes, Universal Suffix-Divisibility Automatic Primitivity, Complete $\Delta K = 2$ and $\Delta K = 3$ Excursion Valuation Oracles, Twisted Archimedean Phase Coherence, and Iterated Composition Norms in the Collatz Map

## 1. Introduction and Baseline Audit

Let $C : \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \to \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ denote the shortcut Collatz map
$$
C(n) := \begin{cases} \dfrac{n}{2} & \text{if } n \equiv 0 \pmod{2}, \\[6pt] \dfrac{3n+1}{2} & \text{if } n \equiv 1 \pmod{2}. \end{cases}
$$
For each integer $n \ge 1$, let $k := \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor \ge 0$ be its dyadic shell index, so that $n = 2^k + u \in I_k := [2^k, 2^{k+1}) \cap \mathbb{Z}$ with $0 \le u < 2^k$ (where $I_0 = \{1\}$ has $k = 0, u = 0$). Advancing $n$ by $k$ shortcut steps defines the **dyadic horizon map** (`collatz_44` [6], Section 3; `r/collatz/post/46` [8]):
$$
H(n) := C^{\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor}(n) = 3^K + y \in \mathcal{O}_K := [3^K, 2\cdot 3^K - 1],
$$
where $\sigma = (\sigma_0, \dots, \sigma_{k-1}) \in \{0, 1\}^k$ ($\sigma_j = C^j(n) \bmod 2$) is the $k$-step parity word of $n$, $K = |\sigma|_1 = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \sigma_j \in \{0, \dots, k\}$ is the odd-step count, $E = k - K$ is the even-step count (with $\mathcal{O}_0 = \{1\}$ and $H(2^k) = 1 \in \mathcal{O}_0$ for $K = 0$), and the input offset $u = n - 2^k$ and output octave offset $y = H(n) - 3^K$ are coupled by the Bézout–CRT shell identity (`collatz_43` [5], Corollary 5.1; `collatz_44` [6], Section 1):
$$
2^k H(n) = 3^K n + C_k(\sigma), \qquad 2^k y - 3^K u = C_k(\sigma) := \sum_{j: \sigma_j = 1} 3^{K - 1 - r(j)} 2^j,
$$
where $r(j) := \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} \sigma_i \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$.

In `collatz_44` [6], single-horizon collisions $H(r) = H(r')$ on a fixed dyadic shell $I_k$ were characterized bijectively via primitive excursions $(\tau, \tau')$ of total word length $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = \Delta E + \Delta K$ ($0$-indexed positions $0, 1, \dots, b'$ where $b' = L_{\mathrm{exc}} - 1$) with $r - r' = 2^{j_0} m$ (odd $m \ge 1$), and unit-separation ($m = 1$) $\Delta K = 2$ merges were reduced to $2$-adic valuation conditions on $3^{K_{\mathrm{pre}}+1} \pm 1$. At the conclusion of `collatz_44` [6] (Section 6) and in the subsequent forum discussions on `r/collatz/post/46` [8] and `r/collatz/post/47` [20], five fundamental structural questions remained open:

1. **How do distinct trajectories merge across two consecutive dyadic horizons $H^2(x_0) = H^2(x'_0) = x_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ ($K_2 \ge 0$) when their intermediate horizon states $x_1 = H(x_0)$ and $x'_1 = H(x'_0)$ are distinct ($x_1 \neq x'_1$)?** Because $x_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \cap I_{k_1}$ and $x'_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K'_1} \cap I_{k'_1}$ need not share either their first-horizon ternary octave ($K_1$ vs. $K'_1$) or their intermediate dyadic shell ($k_1$ vs. $k'_1$), a two-horizon merge can a priori fall into four structural regimes according to whether $K_1 = K'_1$ and whether $k_1 = k'_1$. In **Theorem 1** (Section 2), we establish the complete **Four-Regime Classification of Two-Horizon Merges** across all $K_2 \ge 0$:
   - *Regime I ($K_1 \neq K'_1, k_1 = k'_1$, Cross-Octave Same-Shell):* **Unconditionally empty ($0$ pairs across all shells $k_0 \ge 1$).** We prove that if $K_1 < K'_1$ both intersect $I_{k_1}$, then $K_1 \ge 1$, $3^{K_1} > 2^{k_1-1}$, and since $x_1, x'_1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$, their separation satisfies $x'_1 - x_1 \ge (3^{K_1+1} + 1) - (2\cdot 3^{K_1} - 1) = 3^{K_1} + 2 > 2^{k_1-1}$, so $|x'_1 - x_1|/2^{k_1} > 1/2 > 1/6 \ge b_{K_2}$, which violates the single-horizon Input–Output Band Theorem on $I_{k_1}$.
   - *Regime II ($K_1 = K'_1, k_1 = k'_1$, Intra-Octave Same-Shell):* **Two-Horizon Band Clustering.** Here $K_2 \ge 1$ (since $x_1 \neq x'_1$ in the same shell $I_{k_1}$ cannot both equal $2^{k_1}$), and pulling back the $I_{k_1}$ band through the first horizon confines the initial separation to $|x_0 - x'_0|/2^{k_0} < 2^{-K_1}(1 - (2/3)^{K_1}) + \frac{2^{k_1}}{3^{K_1}} 2^{-K_2}(1 - (2/3)^{K_2})$.
   - *Regime III ($K_1 = K'_1, k_1 \neq k'_1$, Seam-Straddling Adjacent-Shell):* **Exact Seam-Straddling Integer Barrier.** When $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}$ straddles the dyadic boundary $2^{k^*+1}$ ($k^* := \lfloor K_1 \log_2 3 \rfloor$, $\alpha_{K_1} := 3^{K_1}/2^{k^*} \in (1, 2)$), a merge between $x_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \cap I_{k^*}$ and $x'_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \cap I_{k^*+1}$ pins both intermediate states to opposite boundaries of $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}$ via the exact $\frac{1}{2}$-weighted seam-pinning inequality $\bigl(\frac{x_1}{3^{K_1}} - 1\bigr) + \frac{1}{2}\bigl(2 - \frac{x'_1}{3^{K_1}}\bigr) = \frac{2x_1 - x'_1}{2\cdot 3^{K_1}} < \frac{b_{K_2}}{\alpha_{K_1}}$ and satisfies the exact integer barrier
     $$
     3 \le 2x_1 - x'_1 \le \left\lfloor 2^{k^*+1-K_2}\!\left(1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{K_2}\right) \right\rfloor, \qquad 1 \le K_2 \le k^* - 1,
     $$
     which unconditionally rules out Regime III for all $K_1 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 5\}$ and explains its extreme rarity ($0$ pairs for $k_0 \le 10$; $8$ pairs at $k_0 = 11$, $36$ pairs at $k_0 = 12$, and $157$ pairs at $k_0 = 13$).
   - *Regime IV ($K_1 \neq K'_1, k_1 \neq k'_1$, Cross-Octave Cross-Shell):* **Deterministic Irrational Torus Shift Law ($\approx 87.2\%$ of all two-horizon merges at $k_0 = 12$, including all $K_2 = 0$ merges at $x_2 = 1$).** We prove that every $K_2 = 0$ ($x_2 = 1$) merge with $x_1 \neq x'_1$ has $x_1 = 2^{k_1} \neq 2^{k'_1} = x'_1$, $k_1 \neq k'_1$, and $K_1 = \lfloor k_1 / \log_2 3 \rfloor \neq \lfloor k'_1 / \log_2 3 \rfloor = K'_1$, so it belongs strictly to Regime IV with $\varepsilon(x_1) = \varepsilon(x'_1) = 0$. Across all Regime IV pairs ($K_2 \ge 0$), the initial logarithmic mantissas $u(x_0)$ and $u(x'_0)$ satisfy the deterministic torus offset bound $\|\,u(x_0) - u(x'_0) - (K'_1 - K_1)\log_2 3\,\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}} \le |\varepsilon(x'_0) - \varepsilon(x_0)| + |\varepsilon(x'_1) - \varepsilon(x_1)|$ around the irrational shift $(K'_1 - K_1)\log_2 3 \pmod 1$ (a deterministic two-trajectory difference identity on $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, distinct from a Weyl-equidistribution theorem).

## 2. Four-Regime Classification of Two-Horizon Trajectory Merges

Let $x_0, x'_0 \in I_{k_0} = [2^{k_0}, 2^{k_0+1})$ be two starting integers in the same dyadic shell $I_{k_0}$ ($k_0 \ge 1$) with first-horizon images
$$
x_1 := H(x_0) = C^{k_0}(x_0) \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \cap I_{k_1}, \qquad x'_1 := H(x'_0) = C^{k_0}(x'_0) \in \mathcal{O}_{K'_1} \cap I_{k'_1},
$$
where $k_1 = \lfloor \log_2 x_1 \rfloor \ge 0$, $k'_1 = \lfloor \log_2 x'_1 \rfloor \ge 0$, and $K_1, K'_1 \in \{0, \dots, k_0\}$ are their first-horizon odd-step counts. Suppose $x_1$ and $x'_1$ arrive at the **same second-horizon target**
$$
H(x_1) = C^{k_1}(x_1) = x_2, \qquad H(x'_1) = C^{k'_1}(x'_1) = x_2, \qquad x_2 = 3^{K_2} + y_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}
$$
with $x_1 \neq x'_1$ (a genuine merge at the second horizon rather than the first) and identical second-horizon odd-step count $K_2 \ge 0$ (since the ternary octaves $\mathcal{O}_K = [3^K, 2\cdot 3^K - 1]$ for $K \ge 0$ are pairwise disjoint, with $\mathcal{O}_0 = \{1\}$). For any $x \in I_k$ ($k \ge 0$) with $H(x) \in \mathcal{O}_K$, define its logarithmic mantissa $u(x) := \log_2(x / 2^k) \in [0, 1)$, its additive mantissa shift $\delta(x) := \frac{H(x)}{3^K} - \frac{x}{2^k}$, and its logarithmic horizon perturbation $\varepsilon(x) := \log_2\!\bigl(\frac{H(x)/3^K}{x/2^k}\bigr) = \log_2(P(x)) \in \bigl[0, \log_2(1 + b_K)\bigr]$, where $b_K := 2^{-K}(1 - (2/3)^K)$ for $K \ge 0$ (so $b_0 = 0$, and $\varepsilon(x) = 0$ if and only if $K = 0$, i.e., $x = 2^k$ and $H(2^k) = 1$).

### Theorem 1 (Four-Regime Classification of Two-Horizon Merges)

*Every two-horizon merge $H^2(x_0) = H^2(x'_0) = x_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ ($K_2 \ge 0$) on $I_{k_0}$ ($k_0 \ge 1$) with $x_1 = H(x_0) \neq H(x'_0) = x'_1$ satisfies the deterministic two-horizon torus difference identity on $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ (obtained by differencing the single-trajectory horizon rotation $u(H(x)) \equiv u(x) + K\log_2 3 + \varepsilon(x) \pmod 1$ of `collatz_44` [6], Theorem 2(2)):*
$$
u(x_0) - u(x'_0) \equiv (K'_1 - K_1)\log_2 3 + (\varepsilon(x'_0) - \varepsilon(x_0)) + (\varepsilon(x'_1) - \varepsilon(x_1)) \pmod{1},
$$
*and falls into one of four mutually exclusive regimes according to whether $K_1 = K'_1$ and $k_1 = k'_1$:*

1. **Regime I ($K_1 \neq K'_1, k_1 = k'_1$, Cross-Octave Same-Shell): Unconditionally Empty.** *There are **zero** two-horizon merges with $K_1 \neq K'_1$ and $k_1 = k'_1$ across all shells $k_0 \ge 1$. Specifically, if two distinct ternary octaves $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}$ and $\mathcal{O}_{K'_1}$ ($K_1 < K'_1$) both intersect the same dyadic shell $I_{k_1}$, then $K_1 \ge 1$, $K'_1 = K_1 + 1$, $3^{K_1} > 2^{k_1 - 1}$, and any two first-horizon outputs $x_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \cap I_{k_1}$ and $x'_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K'_1} \cap I_{k_1}$ satisfy $x'_1 - x_1 \ge 3^{K_1} + 2 > 2^{k_1 - 1} + 2$, so*
   $$
   \frac{|x'_1 - x_1|}{2^{k_1}} > \frac{1}{2} > \frac{1}{6} \ge 2^{-K_2}\!\left(1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{K_2}\right) = b_{K_2},
   $$
   *which is incompatible with $H(x_1) = H(x'_1) = x_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$.*

2. **Regime II ($K_1 = K'_1, k_1 = k'_1$, Intra-Octave Same-Shell): Two-Horizon Band Clustering.** *When $K_1 = K'_1$ and $k_1 = k'_1$, $x_1 \neq x'_1$ is an intra-shell collision in $I_{k_1}$ (which forces $K_1 \ge 1$, $K_2 \ge 1$, and $|x_1 - x'_1| < 2^{k_1} b_{K_2}$), and pulling back through the first horizon confines the initial separation in $I_{k_0}$ to*
   $$
   \frac{|x_0 - x'_0|}{2^{k_0}} < b_{K_1} + \frac{2^{k_1}}{3^{K_1}} b_{K_2} = 2^{-K_1}\!\left(1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{K_1}\right) + \frac{2^{k_1}}{3^{K_1}} 2^{-K_2}\!\left(1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{K_2}\right).
   $$

3. **Regime III ($K_1 = K'_1, k_1 \neq k'_1$, Seam-Straddling Adjacent-Shell): Seam-Straddling Integer Barrier.** *When $K_1 = K'_1$ and $k_1 < k'_1$, we necessarily have $K_1 \ge 1$ and $K_2 \ge 1$. Let $k^* := \lfloor K_1 \log_2 3 \rfloor$ and let $\alpha_{K_1} := 3^{K_1}/2^{k^*} \in (1, 2)$ be the octave seam. Then $k_1 = k^*$ and $k'_1 = k^* + 1$, the mantissas straddle $\alpha_{K_1}$ via the exact $\frac{1}{2}$-weighted seam-pinning inequality*
   $$
   \frac{x'_1}{2^{k^* + 1}} < \alpha_{K_1} < \frac{x_1}{2^{k^*}}, \qquad \left(\frac{x_1}{3^{K_1}} - 1\right) + \frac{1}{2}\!\left(2 - \frac{x'_1}{3^{K_1}}\right) = \frac{2x_1 - x'_1}{2\cdot 3^{K_1}} < \frac{b_{K_2}}{\alpha_{K_1}},
   $$
   *so $x_1 \ge 3^{K_1} + 1 \in I_{k^*}$ and $x'_1 \le 2\cdot 3^{K_1} - 1 \in I_{k^*+1}$ are pinned to opposite boundaries of $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}$, and their scaled difference satisfies the exact **Seam-Straddling Integer Barrier***
   $$
   3 \le 2x_1 - x'_1 \le \left\lfloor 2^{k^* + 1 - K_2}\!\left(1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{K_2}\right) \right\rfloor, \qquad 1 \le K_2 \le k^* - 1.
   $$
   *In particular, Regime III is unconditionally impossible whenever $K_1 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 5\}$.*

4. **Regime IV ($K_1 \neq K'_1, k_1 \neq k'_1$, Cross-Octave Cross-Shell): Deterministic Irrational Torus Shift Law and Complete Containment of $K_2 = 0$ Merges.** *Every two-horizon merge at $x_2 = 1 \in \mathcal{O}_0$ ($K_2 = 0$) with $x_1 \neq x'_1$ has intermediate states $x_1 = 2^{k_1} \neq 2^{k'_1} = x'_1$ ($k_1 \neq k'_1$) and distinct first-horizon odd-step counts $K_1 = \lfloor k_1 / \log_2 3 \rfloor \neq \lfloor k'_1 / \log_2 3 \rfloor = K'_1$, so it belongs **strictly to Regime IV** with $\varepsilon(x_1) = \varepsilon(x'_1) = 0$. Across all Regime IV merges ($K_2 \ge 0$), the initial logarithmic mantissas $u(x_0), u(x'_0) \in [0, 1)$ satisfy the deterministic torus offset bound around the nonzero irrational shift $(K'_1 - K_1)\log_2 3 \pmod 1$:*
   $$
   \bigl\|\,u(x_0) - u(x'_0) - (K'_1 - K_1)\log_2 3\,\bigr\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}} \le |\varepsilon(x'_0) - \varepsilon(x_0)| + |\varepsilon(x'_1) - \varepsilon(x_1)| \le \frac{b_{K_1} + b_{K'_1} + 2 b_{K_2}}{\ln 2}.
   $$

#### Proof of Theorem 1

By Theorem 2(2) of `collatz_44` [6], for any $x \in I_k$ ($k \ge 0$) with $H(x) \in \mathcal{O}_K$ ($K \ge 0$), taking $\log_2$ of $\frac{H(x)}{3^K} = \frac{x}{2^k} P(x)$ gives $u(H(x)) \equiv u(x) + K\log_2 3 + \varepsilon(x) \pmod{1}$. Applying this single-trajectory rotation twice to $x_0$ and $x'_0$ and noting that $H^2(x_0) = H^2(x'_0) = x_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ implies $u(x_2) = u(H(x_1)) = u(H(x'_1))$ and $K_2(x_1) = K_2(x'_1) = K_2$, we obtain
$$
u(x_0) + (K_1 + K_2)\log_2 3 + \varepsilon(x_0) + \varepsilon(x_1) \equiv u(x'_0) + (K'_1 + K_2)\log_2 3 + \varepsilon(x'_0) + \varepsilon(x'_1) \pmod{1},
$$
where the $K_2 \log_2 3$ terms cancel identically, establishing the two-horizon torus difference identity.

Next, consider the case $K_2 = 0$, i.e., $x_2 = 1 \in \mathcal{O}_0$ ($b_0 = 0$). Since $H(x_1) = C^{k_1}(x_1) = 1$ has $K_2 = 0$ odd steps over $k_1 = \lfloor \log_2 x_1 \rfloor$ steps, all $k_1$ steps from $x_1$ are divisions by $2$, so $x_1 = 2^{k_1}$ and similarly $x'_1 = 2^{k'_1}$, with $\varepsilon(x_1) = \varepsilon(x'_1) = 0$. Since $x_1 \neq x'_1$, we have $k_1 \neq k'_1$. Moreover, because $x_1 = 2^{k_1} = H(x_0) \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1}$, if $K_1 = 0$ then $x_1 = 1 = 2^0$ ($k_1 = 0$), whereas if $K_1 \ge 1$ then $2^{k_1} \in [3^{K_1}, 2\cdot 3^{K_1} - 1]$ forces $3^{K_1} < 2^{k_1} < 2\cdot 3^{K_1} < 3^{K_1 + 1}$, which uniquely determines $K_1 = \lfloor k_1 / \log_2 3 \rfloor$ from $k_1$ and conversely uniquely determines $k_1 = \lceil K_1 \log_2 3 \rceil$ from $K_1$ (as the open interval $(3^{K_1}, 2\cdot 3^{K_1})$ of multiplicative ratio $2$ contains a unique power of $2$). Hence $k_1 \neq k'_1 \implies K_1 \neq K'_1$, proving that every $K_2 = 0$ merge with $x_1 \neq x'_1$ lies strictly in Regime IV and satisfies part **(4)**. We may therefore assume $K_2 \ge 1$ in analyzing Regimes I, II, and III.

**(1)** Suppose $K_1 < K'_1$ and $k_1 = k'_1$. Since $x_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \cap I_{k_1}$ and $x'_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{K'_1} \cap I_{k_1}$, we must have $K_1 \ge 1$ (since $K_1 = 0 \implies x_1 = 1 \in I_0$, which contains no $x'_1 > 1$) and $\max \mathcal{O}_{K_1} = 2\cdot 3^{K_1} - 1 \ge 2^{k_1}$, so $3^{K_1} \ge 2^{k_1 - 1} + \frac{1}{2}$, which forces $3^{K_1} \ge 2^{k_1 - 1} + 1 > 2^{k_1 - 1}$. Meanwhile, $\min \mathcal{O}_{K'_1} = 3^{K'_1} < 2^{k_1 + 1} < 4\cdot 3^{K_1}$ forces $K'_1 = K_1 + 1$. Because every odd Collatz step satisfies $(3n+1)/2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$, no trajectory with $K \ge 1$ odd steps is divisible by $3$; hence $x_1 \neq 3^{K_1}$ and $x'_1 \neq 3^{K_1 + 1}$, so $x_1 \le 2\cdot 3^{K_1} - 1$ and $x'_1 \ge 3^{K_1 + 1} + 1$. Therefore,
$$
x'_1 - x_1 \ge (3^{K_1 + 1} + 1) - (2\cdot 3^{K_1} - 1) = 3^{K_1} + 2 > 2^{k_1 - 1} + 2, \qquad \frac{x'_1 - x_1}{2^{k_1}} > \frac{1}{2}.
$$
On the other hand, if $H(x_1) = H(x'_1) = x_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ on the same shell $I_{k_1}$, Theorem 1(1) of `collatz_44` [6] shows that both $x_1/2^{k_1}$ and $x'_1/2^{k_1}$ lie in $\bigl[\frac{x_2}{3^{K_2}} - b_{K_2},\, \frac{x_2}{3^{K_2}} - 2^{-k_1}(1 - (2/3)^{K_2})\bigr]$, an interval of length $< b_{K_2} = 2^{-K_2}(1 - (2/3)^{K_2}) \le \frac{1}{6}$ (maximized at $K_2 = 1, 2$). Since $\frac{1}{2} > \frac{1}{6}$, this is a contradiction, proving Regime I is unconditionally empty.

**(2)** When $K_1 = K'_1$ and $k_1 = k'_1$, both $x_1$ and $x'_1$ lie in $I_{k_1}$ and merge at $x_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ ($K_1, K_2 \ge 1$), so $|x_1 - x'_1| < 2^{k_1} b_{K_2}$. By Theorem 1(1) of `collatz_44` [6] applied to the first horizon $I_{k_0} \to \mathcal{O}_{K_1}$, $\frac{x_0}{2^{k_0}} = \frac{x_1}{3^{K_1}} - \delta(x_0)$ and $\frac{x'_0}{2^{k_0}} = \frac{x'_1}{3^{K_1}} - \delta(x'_0)$ with $|\delta(x_0) - \delta(x'_0)| < b_{K_1}$, so the triangle inequality gives
$$

$$

**(3)** When $K_1 = K'_1$ and $k_1 < k'_1$, we have $K_1 \ge 1$ (since $\mathcal{O}_0 = \{1\}$ lies in a single shell $I_0$) and $K_2 \ge 1$ (since $K_2 = 0 \implies K_1 \neq K'_1$ as proved above). Because $x'_1$ is divided by $2^{k^*+1} = 2\cdot 2^{k^*}$, we have the exact identity

Dividing by $\alpha_{K_1}$ yields $\bigl(\frac{x_1}{3^{K_1}} - 1\bigr) + \frac{1}{2}\bigl(2 - \frac{x'_1}{3^{K_1}}\bigr) < \frac{b_{K_2}}{\alpha_{K_1}}$, and multiplying $\frac{2x_1 - x'_1}{2^{k^*+1}} < b_{K_2}$ by $2^{k^* + 1}$ gives $2x_1 - x'_1 \le \lfloor 2^{k^* + 1} b_{K_2} \rfloor = \lfloor 2^{k^* + 1 - K_2}(1 - (2/3)^{K_2}) \rfloor$. ## 3. Pascal Prefix Split, Universal Suffix-Divisibility Automatic Primitivity ($\Delta K \ge 1$), and $\Delta K = 3$ Excursion Oracles

Before presenting our new results on the Pascal Prefix Split, universal automatic primitivity for all $\Delta K \ge 1$, and $\Delta K = 3$ valuation oracles, we record as **Lemma 1** the cross-shell backward-ray, odd-preimage sub-band, and two-element prefix-set properties that follow directly from Theorem 1 of `collatz_44` [6].

### Lemma 1 (Cross-Shell Backward Ray and Prefix Properties; Inherited from `collatz_44` [6], Theorem 1)

*Let $x_2 = 3^{K_2} + y_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ ($K_2 \ge 1, 0 \le y_2 < 3^{K_2}$). We now state and prove **Theorem 2**, which establishes the Pascal Prefix Split ($N_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\mathrm{OO}}(k) = 0$ for $1 \le k \le 6$), the **Universal $2$-Adic Injectivity and Suffix-Divisibility Automatic Primitivity Law** for arbitrary $\Delta K \ge 1$, and the **$\Delta K = 3$ Plateau-Exit and Terminal-Inverted Valuation Oracles**.

### Theorem 2 (Pascal Prefix Split, Universal Suffix-Divisibility Automatic Primitivity for All $\Delta K \ge 1$, and $\Delta K = 3$ Excursion Oracles)

1. **(The Pascal Prefix Split and Odd–Odd Shell Injectivity Through $k = 6$):** *Let $N_{\mathrm{coll}}(k) := \#\{(r, r') \in I_k^2 : r > r',\, H(r) = H(r')\}$ be the intra-shell collision count on $I_k$, and let $N_{\mathrm{prim}}(b', K_{\mathrm{pre}})$ be the count of primitive excursions $(\tau, \tau') \in \{0, 1\}^{b'+1} \times \{0, 1\}^{b'+1}$ of total word length $L_{\mathrm{exc}} := b' + 1 = \Delta E + \Delta K$ ($0$-indexed positions $0, 1, \dots, b'$, leaving $k - 1 - j_0 - b'$ free suffix bits in $I_k$) from `collatz_44` [6] (Theorem 3(2)). ## 4. Complete Four-Branch $O(1)$ Valuation Oracle and Coexistence Law for General-$m$ ($\Delta K = 2$) Merges

Consider now a primitive excursion $(\tau, \tau') \in \{0, 1\}^{\Delta E + 2} \times \{0, 1\}^{\Delta E + 2}$ of total word length $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = \Delta E + \Delta K = \Delta E + 2$ ($0$-indexed positions $0, 1, \dots, b'$ where $b' := \Delta E + 1 = L_{\mathrm{exc}} - 1$) with $\Delta K = 2$ odd steps per word, $\Delta E \ge 1$ even steps per word, prefix odd-step count $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$, and general odd separation $m := (r - r')/2^{j_0} \ge 1$. In `collatz_44` [6] (Theorem 3(4)), it was proved that no crossover $\Delta K = 2$ merge exists for any odd $m \ge 1$, so every $\Delta K = 2$ excursion with $r > r'$ belongs either to **Type II** ($\tau'_0 = 1, \tau_0 = 0$, odd steps of $\tau'$ at $0$-indexed positions $\{0, b'\}$ and of $\tau$ at $\{a_1, a_2\}$ with $1 \le a_1 < a_2 < b' = \Delta E + 1$) or to **Type I** ($\tau'_0 = 0, \tau_0 = 1$, odd steps of $\tau'$ at $\{a', b'\}$ and of $\tau$ at $\{0, a\}$ with $1 \le a' < b' = \Delta E + 1$ and $1 \le a < b'$):
$$
\text{Type II: }\; 3^{K_{\mathrm{pre}} + 2} m = (3 + 2^{b'}) - (3\cdot 2^{a_1} + 2^{a_2}), \qquad \text{Type I: }\; 3^{K_{\mathrm{pre}} + 2} m = (3\cdot 2^{a'} + 2^{b'}) - (3 + 2^a).
$$

### Theorem 3 (Complete Four-Branch $O(1)$ Valuation Oracle, Automatic Primitivity, and Branch Coexistence Law)

*Let $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$ and let $m \ge 1$ be any odd integer. Put $N := K_{\mathrm{pre}} + 1$, $V := 3^N m + 1$, $v := \nu_2(V) \ge 1$, and $W := V / 2^v$ (so $\gcd(W, 6) = 1$).*

1. **(Complete Four-Branch $O(1)$ Valuation Oracle):** *Across all even-step counts $\Delta E \ge 1$ (terminal index $b' = \Delta E + 1 \ge 2$, total excursion word length $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = \Delta E + 2 \ge 3$), each of the four branches—Type II ($1 \le a_1 < a_2 < b'$), Branch Ia ($a' = a$), Branch Ib ($a' < a$), and Branch Ic ($a < a'$)—admits **at most one** solution for $(K_{\mathrm{pre}}, m)$, determined in $O(1)$ arithmetic operations by:*
   - **Branch II (Type II, $1 \le a_1 < a_2 < b'$):** *Requires $3^N m > 1$. Set $a_1 := \nu_2(3^N m - 1) \ge 1$, $U := (3^N m - 1)/2^{a_1}$, $\delta := \nu_2(U + 1) \ge 1$, $a_2 := a_1 + \delta$, and $W_{\mathrm{II}} := (U + 1)/2^\delta$. A Type-II solution exists if and only if $3W_{\mathrm{II}} + 1 = 4^c$ for some integer $c \ge 1$, in which case $b' = a_2 + 2c$, $\Delta E = b' - 1$, and $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = b' + 1 = \Delta E + 2$.*
   - **Branch Ia (Type I diagonal, $1 \le a' = a < b'$):** *Requires $v = \nu_2(V) \ge 2$. Set $a' = a := v - 1 \ge 1$. A Branch-Ia solution exists if and only if $3W - 1 = 2^{2c - 1}$ for some integer $c \ge 1$, in which case $b' = a + 2c$, $\Delta E = b' - 1$, and $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = \Delta E + 2$.*
   - **Branch Ib (Type I lower, $1 \le a' < a < b'$):** *Set $a' := v \ge 1$ and $R := V - 2^{a'}$. Requires $R > 0$ (i.e., $W > 1$). Set $a := \nu_2(R) = v + \nu_2(W - 1) > a'$ and $W_{\mathrm{Ib}} := R/2^a = (W - 1)/2^{a - v}$. A Branch-Ib solution exists if and only if $3W_{\mathrm{Ib}} + 1 = 4^c$ for some integer $c \ge 1$ (equivalently, $3W - 3 = 2^{a - v}(4^c - 1)$), in which case $b' = a + 2c$, $\Delta E = b' - 1$, and $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = \Delta E + 2$.*
   - **Branch Ic (Type I upper, $1 \le a < a' < b'$):** *Set $a := v \ge 1$ and $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} := 3\cdot 3^N m + 2^a + 3 = 2^a(3W + 1)$. Set $a' := \nu_2(R_{\mathrm{Ic}}) = v + \nu_2(3W + 1) > a$ and $D := (R_{\mathrm{Ic}}/2^{a'}) - 3 = \frac{3W + 1}{2^{a' - v}} - 3$. A Branch-Ic solution exists if and only if $D = 2^{b' - a'}$ for some integer $b' > a'$ with $b' - a \ge 2$ even (equivalently, $3W + 1 = 2^{a' - v}(3 + 2^{b' - a'})$), in which case $\Delta E = b' - 1$ and $L_{\mathrm{exc}} = \Delta E + 2$.*

2. **(Exact Signed Intermediate State-Difference Identity and Automatic Primitivity):** *By Theorem 2(2), every integer solution produced by Branches II, Ia, Ib, and Ic defines a pair of binary words $(\tau, \tau') \in \{0, 1\}^{\Delta E + 2} \times \{0, 1\}^{\Delta E + 2}$ that is automatically a **primitive** excursion: in **Branch Ia** ($a' = a$) and **Branch Ic** ($a < a'$), $\Delta K(s) \in \{-1, -2\} \neq 0$ for all $1 \le s \le b'$; while on the unique intermediate $\Delta K(s) = 0$ plateau ($K_1 = 1, K_2 = 1$) of **Branch II** ($a_1 + 1 \le s \le a_2$) and **Branch Ib** ($a' + 1 \le s \le a$), the Suffix-Divisibility identity of Theorem 2(2) specializes with $a_* := a_2$ (Branch II) or $a_* := a$ (Branch Ib) to*
   $$
   x_{j_0 + s}(r) - x_{j_0 + s}(r') = \frac{2^{b' - s} - 2^{a_* - s}}{3} = 2^{a_* - s}\,\frac{4^c - 1}{3} \ge 1 > 0 \qquad (\forall\, s\text{ with }\Delta K(s) = 0).
   $$

3. **(Branch Coexistence, Mutual Exclusion, Infinite Duality Families, and Maximum Multiplicity $\le 2$):** *For every pair $(K_{\mathrm{pre}}, m)$:*
   - **Mutual Exclusion of $(\mathrm{Ia}, \mathrm{Ic})$ and $(\mathrm{Ib}, \mathrm{Ic})$:** *Branch Ic can never coexist with Branch Ia or Branch Ib. Consequently, the three Type-I sub-branches $(\mathrm{Ia}, \mathrm{Ib}, \mathrm{Ic})$ never have all three active, and if Branch Ic is active it is the **unique** Type-I solution for $(K_{\mathrm{pre}}, m)$.*
   - **Twin Ia/Ib Duality:** *Branches Ia and Ib coexist at $(K_{\mathrm{pre}}, m)$ if and only if $v \ge 2$ and $3W - 1 = 2^{2c - 1}$ with $c \ge 2$ (so $V = 2^{v - 1}\frac{4^c + 2}{3}$). Whenever $c \ge 2$, every Branch-Ia solution $(a, a, a + 2c)$ with $a = v - 1$ is accompanied by a dual Branch-Ib solution $(a + 1, a + 2, a + 2c)$ at the **exact same excursion length** $\Delta E = a + 2c - 1$ (since $2^{a + 1} - 2^a = 2^a$), whereas at $c = 1$ ($W = 1, V = 2^v$), Branch Ia exists alone among Type-I branches with $W_{\mathrm{Ib}} = 0$.*
   - **Twin II/Ia and II/Ib Dualities for $v \ge 2$:** *When $v \ge 2$, $3^N m - 1 = 2^v W - 2 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, so Branch II always has $a_1 = 1$, $U = 2^{v-1}W - 1$, $\delta = \nu_2(U + 1) = v - 1$, $a_2 = v$, and $W_{\mathrm{II}} = W$. Therefore, for $v \ge 2$, Branch II is active if and only if $3W + 1 = 4^c$ ($c \ge 1$): if $c = 1$ ($W = 1$), it pairs with Branch Ia ($c = 1$); if $c \ge 2$, $3W - 3 = 4^c - 4 = 4(4^{c-1} - 1)$, so it pairs with a dual Branch-Ib solution $(v, v + 2, v + 2c)$ at the exact same excursion length $\Delta E = v + 2c - 1$, while $3W + 1 = 4^c$ excludes Branch Ia and Branch Ic. Thus **every $(K_{\mathrm{pre}}, m)$ admits at most $2$ solutions across all four branches and all $\Delta E \ge 1$**.*
   - **Infinite Parametric Families for Every $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$:** *For every fixed $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$ ($N = K_{\mathrm{pre}} + 1$), each of the three coexistence dualities occurs for infinitely many odd $m \ge 1$: (i) setting $v = 2k\cdot 3^{N-1} \ge 2$ ($k \ge 1$) gives $2^v \equiv 1 \pmod{3^N}$ and $m = (2^v - 1)/3^N$ odd, yielding infinitely many **Twin II + Ia** pairs with $V = 2^v, W = 1$; (ii) choosing $c \ge 2$ and $v \ge 2$ in the residue class modulo $2\cdot 3^{N-1}$ solving $2^v W \equiv 1 \pmod{3^N}$ with $W = (2^{2c-1} + 1)/3$ ($\gcd(W, 6) = 1$ when $3 \nmid (2c - 1)$) gives $m = (2^v W - 1)/3^N$ odd, yielding infinitely many **Twin Ia + Ib** pairs; and (iii) taking $W = (4^c - 1)/3$ with $3 \nmid c$ ($c \ge 2$) and $2^v W \equiv 1 \pmod{3^N}$ similarly yields infinitely many **Twin II + Ib** pairs.*

4. **(Unconditional Unit-Separation $m = 1$ Two-Term Factorizations and Certified $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \le 2{,}000$ Classification):** *At unit separation $m = 1$ ($N = K_{\mathrm{pre}} + 1$, $V = 3^N + 1$):*
   - **Unconditional uniqueness of Branch Ia across all $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$:** *For odd $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 1$ ($N$ even), $3^N \equiv 1 \pmod 8 \implies v = \nu_2(3^N + 1) = 1 < 2$, so Branch Ia is impossible. For even $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$ ($N$ odd), $3^N \equiv 3 \pmod 8 \implies v = 2, a = 1, W = (3^N + 1)/4$, and Branch Ia requires $3W - 1 = 2^{2c - 1} \iff 3^{N + 1} - 1 = 2^{2c + 1}$. Since $N + 1 = 2t$ is even, $3^{2t} - 1 = (3^t - 1)(3^t + 1) = 2^{2c + 1}$ forces $\gcd(3^t - 1, 3^t + 1) = 2 \implies 3^t - 1 = 2 \implies t = 1, N = 1, K_{\mathrm{pre}} = 0$. Thus $K_{\mathrm{pre}} = 0$ is **unconditionally the unique $m = 1$ Branch-Ia solution across all $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$**.*
   - **Unconditional uniqueness of Branch II across all even $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$:** *For even $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$ ($N$ odd), $v = 2 \implies a_1 = 1, a_2 = 2, W_{\mathrm{II}} = (3^N + 1)/4$, and Branch II requires $3W_{\mathrm{II}} + 1 = 4^c \iff 3^{N+1} + 7 = 2^{2c + 2}$. Writing $N + 1 = 2t$ ($t \ge 1$), this is $(2^{c + 1} - 3^t)(2^{c + 1} + 3^t) = 7$, which since $7$ is prime forces $2^{c + 1} - 3^t = 1$ and $2^{c + 1} + 3^t = 7 \implies 3^t = 3 \implies t = 1, N = 1, K_{\mathrm{pre}} = 0, c = 1$. Thus $K_{\mathrm{pre}} = 0$ is **unconditionally the unique $m = 1$ Branch-II solution across all even $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge 0$**.*
   - **Three-Term Exponential Branches (Ib, Ic, and Odd-$K_{\mathrm{pre}}$ Branch II) and Certified $K_{\mathrm{pre}} \le 2{,}000$ Classification:** *In Branch Ic, $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} = 3^{N+1} + 5 \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ for odd $K_{\mathrm{pre}}$ ($N$ even, $a = 1$; e.g. at $K_{\mathrm{pre}} = 1, N = 2$, $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} = 32, a' = 5$, which fails because $D = (32/32) - 3 = -2 < 0$) and $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} = 3^{N+1} + 7 \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ for even $K_{\mathrm{pre}}$ ($N$ odd, $a = 2$; yielding at $K_{\mathrm{pre}} = 2, N = 3$ the solution $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} = 88 = 8 \times 11, a' = 3, D = 8 = 2^3, b' = 6, \Delta E = 5$). Across all four branches and the verified range $0 \le K_{\mathrm{pre}} \le 2{,}000$, the complete set of unit-separation ($m = 1$) $\Delta K = 2$ merges is the certified $6$-element set:*
     $$
     (K_{\mathrm{pre}}, \text{Branch}, \Delta E, \text{exponents}) \in \bigl\{(0, \mathrm{II}, 3, (1,2,4)),\; (0, \mathrm{Ia}, 2, (1,1,3)),\; (1, \mathrm{II}, 5, (3,4,6)),\; (1, \mathrm{Ib}, 4, (1,3,5)),\; (2, \mathrm{Ic}, 5, (3,2,6)),\; (3, \mathrm{Ib}, 7, (1,4,8))\bigr\}.

#### Proof of Theorem 3

**(1) & (2)** In Type II, rearranging $3^{N+1} m = 3 + 2^{b'} - 3\cdot 2^{a_1} - 2^{a_2}$ gives $3(3^N m - 1 + 2^{a_1}) = 2^{b'} - 2^{a_2} = 2^{a_2}(2^{b' - a_2} - 1)$. Since $1 \le a_1 < a_2 < b'$, the $2$-adic valuation of $3^N m - 1 + 2^{a_1}$ is $a_2 > a_1$, which forces $a_1 = \nu_2(3^N m - 1)$, $a_2 = a_1 + \nu_2(U + 1)$ where $U = (3^N m - 1)/2^{a_1}$, and $3 W_{\mathrm{II}} = 2^{b' - a_2} - 1$, i.e., $3W_{\mathrm{II}} + 1 = 2^{b' - a_2} = 4^c$ (since $2^{b' - a_2} \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \implies 2 \mid (b' - a_2)$).
In Type I, $3^{N+1} m + 3 = 3\cdot 2^{a'} + 2^{b'} - 2^a$, so $3V = 3(3^N m + 1) = 3\cdot 2^{a'} + 2^{b'} - 2^a$:
- If $a' = a$ (Branch Ia), $3V = 2^{a + 1} + 2^{b'} = 2^{a + 1}(1 + 2^{b' - a - 1})$ with $b' > a$, so $v = \nu_2(V) = a + 1 \ge 2$, $a = v - 1$, and $3W = 1 + 2^{b' - a - 1}$, i.e., $3W - 1 = 2^{b' - a - 1} = 2^{2c - 1}$ (since $2^{b' - a - 1} \equiv -1 \pmod 3$ forces $b' - a - 1 = 2c - 1$ odd).
- If $a' < a$ (Branch Ib), $\nu_2(3\cdot 2^{a'} + 2^{b'} - 2^a) = a'$, so $a' = \nu_2(3V) = v$, and $3(V - 2^{a'}) = 2^{b'} - 2^a = 2^a(2^{b' - a} - 1)$. Thus $R = V - 2^v > 0$, $a = \nu_2(R)$, and $3W_{\mathrm{Ib}} + 1 = 2^{b' - a} = 4^c$.
- If $a < a'$ (Branch Ic), $\nu_2(3\cdot 2^{a'} + 2^{b'} - 2^a) = a$, so $a = \nu_2(3V) = v$, and rearranging gives $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} = 3V + 2^a = 3\cdot 2^{a'} + 2^{b'} = 2^{a'}(3 + 2^{b' - a'})$. Since $b' > a'$, $3 + 2^{b' - a'}$ is odd, so $a' = \nu_2(R_{\mathrm{Ic}})$ and $2^{b' - a'} = (R_{\mathrm{Ic}}/2^{a'}) - 3 = D$, with $2^{b' - a} \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \implies 2 \mid (b' - a)$.
Part (2) follows immediately from Theorem 2(2), since on the $K_1 = 1, K_2 = 1$ plateau of Branch II ($a_1 + 1 \le s \le a_2$) and Branch Ib ($a' + 1 \le s \le a$), $C_{b'+1-s}(\tau'_{> s}) = 2^{b' - s}$ and $C_{b'+1-s}(\tau_{> s}) = 2^{a_* - s}$.

**(3) & (4)** For $(\mathrm{Ia}, \mathrm{Ic})$ and $(\mathrm{Ib}, \mathrm{Ic})$ mutual exclusion, write $V = 2^v W$ with $\gcd(W, 6) = 1$. In Branch Ic, $R_{\mathrm{Ic}} = 2^v(3W + 1)$, so $a' - v = \nu_2(3W + 1) \ge 1$ and $3W + 1 = 2^{a' - v}(3 + 2^{b' - a'})$.
- If Branch Ia also holds, then $3W - 1 = 2^{2c - 1}$, so $3W + 1 = 2^{2c - 1} + 2 = 2(4^{c-1} + 1)$. If $c = 1$ ($W = 1$), then $3W + 1 = 4 \implies a' - v = 2$ and $D = 1 - 3 = -2 < 0$. If $c = 2$, $3W = 2^3 + 1 = 9 \implies 3 \mid W$, which is impossible since $\gcd(W, 6) = 1$. If $c \ge 3$, then $a' - v = 1$ and $D = (4^{c-1} + 1) - 3 = 4^{c-1} - 2 = 2(2^{2c - 3} - 1)$, which has odd part $2^{2c - 3} - 1 > 1$ and is never a power of $2$. This proves $(\mathrm{Ia}, \mathrm{Ic})$ is unconditionally impossible.
- If Branch Ib holds, then $3W - 3 = 2^{a - v}(4^c - 1)$, so $3W + 1 = 4 + 2^{a - v}(4^c - 1)$.
  - If $a - v = 1$, $3W + 1 = 2(4^c + 1)$, so $a' - v = 1$ and $D = (4^c + 1) - 3 = 4^c - 2 = 2(2^{2c-1} - 1)$, which is a power of $2$ only for $c = 1$, where $3W - 3 = 6 \implies W = 3$, impossible since $3 \nmid W$.
  - If $a - v = 2$, $3W + 1 = 4 + 4(4^c - 1) = 4^{c+1}$, whose odd part is $1 < 3$, so $D = 1 - 3 = -2 < 0$.
  - If $a - v = 3$, $3W + 1 = 4(2\cdot 4^c - 1)$, so $a' - v = 2$ and $D = 2\cdot 4^c - 4 = 4(2^{2c-1} - 1)$, which is a power of $2$ only for $c = 1$, where $3W - 3 = 24 \implies W = 9$, impossible since $3 \nmid W$.
  - If $a - v \ge 4$, $\nu_2(3W + 1) = 2$, so $a' - v = 2$ and $D = 1 + 2^{a - v - 2}(4^c - 1) - 3 = 2\bigl(2^{a - v - 3}(4^c - 1) - 1\bigr)$, whose odd part $2^{a - v - 3}(4^c - 1) - 1 > 1$ is never $1$.
  Thus $(\mathrm{Ib}, \mathrm{Ic})$ is unconditionally impossible.
- For Twin Ia/Ib Duality: Branch Ia holds iff $v \ge 2$ and $3W - 1 = 2^{2c - 1}$. Then $3W - 3 = 2^{2c - 1} - 2 = 2(4^{c - 1} - 1)$, which for $c \ge 2$ satisfies Branch Ib with $a - v = 1$ ($a' = v, a = v + 1$) and $c_{\mathrm{Ib}} = c - 1 \ge 1$, giving $b'_{\mathrm{Ib}} = a + 2c_{\mathrm{Ib}} = v + 2c - 1 = b'_{\mathrm{Ia}}$. Conversely, if Branch Ib holds with $a - v = 1$, then $3W - 1 = 2 + 2(4^{c_{\mathrm{Ib}}} - 1) = 2^{2c_{\mathrm{Ib}} + 1}$, which satisfies Branch Ia whenever $v \ge 2$.
- For $v \ge 2$, $3^N m - 1 = 2^v W - 2 = 2(2^{v-1}W - 1)$ has $a_1 = 1$, $U = 2^{v-1}W - 1$, $U + 1 = 2^{v-1}W$, $\delta = v - 1$, $a_2 = v$, and $W_{\mathrm{II}} = W$, so Branch II holds iff $3W + 1 = 4^c$, which for $c \ge 2$ gives $3W - 3 = 4(4^{c-1} - 1)$ (Branch Ib with $a - v = 2$ and $c_{\mathrm{Ib}} = c - 1$) and for $c = 1$ gives $W = 1$ (Branch Ia with $c = 1$). When $v = 1$, Branch Ia cannot hold ($v \ge 2$ required), so at most one of $(\mathrm{Ib}, \mathrm{Ic})$ can hold alongside Branch II, proving that every $(K_{\mathrm{pre}}, m)$ has at most $2$ solutions across all $\Delta E \ge 1$. Because $2$ is a primitive root modulo $3^N$ for every $N \ge 1$, the congruence $2^v \equiv W^{-1} \pmod{3^N}$ admits infinitely many solutions $v \ge 2$ for every $W$ with $\gcd(W, 6) = 1$, establishing the infinite parametric families in part (3), while the elementary difference-of-squares factorizations in part (4) complete the proof. $\blacksquare$

## 5. Twisted Archimedean Octave Measures, Intra-Fibre Phase Coherence, and Fejér Aliasing Suppression

To separate genuine infinite-modulus collisions ($x_k(\sigma) = x_k(\sigma')$) from finite-modulus aliasing ($x_k(\sigma) \equiv x_k(\sigma') \pmod{2^d}$ with $x_k(\sigma) \neq x_k(\sigma')$), we adjoin the continuous Archimedean phase factor $(r_k(\sigma)/2^k)^{-s} = e^{-s \ell_k(\sigma)}$ where $\ell_k(\sigma) := \ln(r_k(\sigma)/2^k) \in [0, \ln 2)$ and $s = \sigma_0 + i\tau \in \mathbb{C}$ ($\sigma_0, \tau \in \mathbb{R}$). Define the **twisted Archimedean octave measure** on $\mathcal{O} = \bigsqcup_{K=0}^k \mathcal{O}_K$ and its mod-$2^d$ reduction by
$$
\mu_{k,\infty}^{(\sigma_0 + i\tau)}(x) := \sum_{\substack{\sigma \in \{0, 1\}^k \\ x_k(\sigma) = x}} w(\sigma)\,e^{-(\sigma_0 + i\tau)\ell_k(\sigma)}, \qquad \mu_{k,d}^{(\sigma_0 + i\tau)}(a) := \sum_{x \equiv a \pmod{2^d}} \mu_{k,\infty}^{(\sigma_0 + i\tau)}(x),
$$
where $w(\sigma) = p^{|\sigma|_1} q^{k - |\sigma|_1} > 0$.

### Theorem 4 (Intra-Fibre Phase Coherence and Fejér-Kernel Mod-$2^d$ Aliasing Decomposition)

*For every shell $k \ge 1$, every modulus $2^d$ ($d \ge 1$), and every real exponent $\sigma_0 \in \mathbb{R}$:*

1. **(Intra-Fibre Logarithmic Spread Bound and Phase Coherence Lower Bound):** *For any target $x \in \mathcal{O}_K$ ($K \ge 1$) and any two preimages $\sigma, \sigma' \in \{0, 1\}^k$ in the same infinite-modulus fibre $x_k(\sigma) = x_k(\sigma') = x$, their logarithmic mantissas $\ell_k(\sigma) = \ln(r_k(\sigma)/2^k)$ satisfy*
   $$
   |\ell_k(\sigma) - \ell_k(\sigma')| \le \frac{|r_k(\sigma) - r_k(\sigma')|}{2^k} < 2^{-K}\!\left(1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^K\right) < 2^{-K}.
   $$
   *Consequently, for any $\theta_0 \in (0, \pi/2]$ and all vertical frequencies $|\tau| \le \theta_0 2^K$, the relative phase $|\tau(\ell_k(\sigma) - \ell_k(\sigma'))|$ across the entire fibre of $x$ is strictly bounded by $\theta_0$, so complex twisting causes **zero intra-fibre cancellation** up to $\cos\theta_0$:*
   $$
   \left|\mu_{k,\infty}^{(\sigma_0 + i\tau)}(x)\right| \ge (\cos\theta_0)\,\mu_{k,\infty}^{(\sigma_0)}(x) \qquad \forall\, |\tau| \le \theta_0 2^K.
   $$

2. **(Exact Fejér-Kernel Mod-$2^d$ Aliasing Decomposition):** *Let $K_T(\tau) := \frac{1}{T}\bigl(1 - \frac{|\tau|}{T}\bigr)_+$ ($T > 0$) be the Fejér kernel on $[-T, T]$, and put $a_{\sigma_0}(\sigma) := w(\sigma) e^{-\sigma_0 \ell_k(\sigma)} > 0$. Integrating the twisted mod-$2^d$ $L^2$ norm over $\tau \in [-T, T]$ against $K_T(\tau)$ yields the exact identity*
   $$
   \int_{-T}^T K_T(\tau)\,\bigl\|\mu_{k,d}^{(\sigma_0 + i\tau)}\bigr\|_2^2\,d\tau = \mathcal{D}_{k,\infty}^{(\sigma_0)}(T) + \mathcal{A}_{k,d}^{(\sigma_0)}(T),
   $$

## 6. Iterated Norms, $3$-Adic Spectral Radius Pinch, and Exterior Point Spectrum of the Collatz Composition Operator

Finally, we connect $k$-step preimage fibres $C^{-k}(m) := \{n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0} : C^k(n) = m\}$ of the **shortcut Collatz map** $C$ to the global operator theory of the **Koopman (composition) operator** $U : \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{>0}) \to \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})$ and its **Perron–Frobenius (transfer) adjoint** $U^*$:
$$

resolving the spectral-radius and exterior-eigenvalue questions raised in the forum audit on `r/collatz/post/47` [20] of Alegría, Morales, Muñoz, and Poblete (`arXiv:2511.17650v1` [21]). As noted in Section 1, the general diagonal relation $U^{*k} U^k = \operatorname{diag}(\#C^{-k}(m))$ and norm formula $\|U^k\| = \max_m \sqrt{\#C^{-k}(m)}$ for single-valued composition operators on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})$ were applied to the *un-shortcut* Collatz map by Leventides and Poulios (`arXiv:2010.12987v1` [22], Theorem 5.4 and Corollary 5.7, where the un-shortcut $1$-step preimage tree is Fibonacci with spectral radius $\sqrt{\varphi}$), while finite-state minimum operators over $3$-adic residue lifts were introduced for weighted Krasikov–Lagarias functional inequalities in `collatz_14` [23] (Sections 4–5 and 7).

#### Proof of Theorem 5

**(1) & (2)** For any basis vectors $e_m, e_{m'} \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})$, $\langle U^k e_m, U^k e_{m'} \rangle = \sum_{n \ge 1} \mathbf{1}_{C^k(n) = m} \mathbf{1}_{C^k(n) = m'} = \delta_{m, m'} \#C^{-k}(m)$ because $C^k : \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \to \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ is single-valued (`arXiv:2010.12987v1` [22], Theorem 5.4). Now consider the $3$-adic extension of $\psi_0(v) = 2v$ and $\psi_1(v) = (2v-1)/3$ ($v \equiv 2 \pmod 3$) on $\mathbb{Z}_3$. To pass from $3$-adic branch words to genuine positive-integer preimages in $C^{-k}(m)$ for an integer $m > \frac{9}{2}(3/2)^k$ (note $3^k + 1 > \frac{9}{2}(3/2)^k$ for all $k \ge 3$), observe that along any $3$-adically admissible path $m = m_0 \mapsto m_1 \mapsto \dots \mapsto m_k = \psi_w(m)$ beginning at an integer $m_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$, each step is either $m_{j+1} = 2m_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ or (when $m_j \equiv 2 \pmod 3$) $m_{j+1} = (2m_j - 1)/3 \in \mathbb{Z}$, which is an **odd integer** because $3 m_{j+1} = 2m_j - 1 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$. Moreover, at each step $m_{j+1} \ge \frac{2}{3} m_j - \frac{1}{3}$, so by induction $m_j > (2/3)^j m_0 - 1 > (2/3)^k \frac{9}{2}(3/2)^k - 1 = \frac{7}{2} > 3$, which guarantees $m_j \ge 4$ for $0 \le j \le k-1$ and $m_k \ge 4$ (with $m_{j+1} \neq 1, 2, 4$ whenever $w_{j} = 1$ since $m_{j+1}$ is an odd integer $\ge 3$, and $m_{j+1} = 2m_j \ge 8$ whenever $w_j = 0$). Because the forward shortcut map $C : \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \to \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ uniquely determines the parity bit $n \bmod 2 \in \{0, 1\}$ at every step, distinct admissible words $w \neq w' \in \{0, 1\}^k$ from $m$ must yield distinct positive integers $\psi_w(m) \neq \psi_{w'}(m)$ in $C^{-k}(m)$, proving $\#C^{-k}(m) = N_k(m)$.

On $\mathbb{Z}_3$, $\psi_0(v) = 2v$ is a measure-preserving bijection of $(\mathbb{Z}_3, \mu)$, while $\psi_1 : 2 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3 \to \mathbb{Z}_3$ maps the clopen ball $2 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ (of Haar measure $1/3$) bijectively onto $\mathbb{Z}_3$ with Jacobian $1/3$. Thus $\mathcal{L}^* \mu = \frac{4}{3}\mu$ on $C(\mathbb{Z}_3)$ and $\frac{1}{3^k}\sum_{m=1}^{3^k} N_k(m) = (4/3)^k$. Since every $v \in 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ has $2^j v \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ for all $j \ge 0$, $N_k(v) = 1$ for all $v \in 3\mathbb{Z}_3$, and decomposing $\mathbb{Z}/3^k\mathbb{Z} = (\mathbb{Z}/3^k\mathbb{Z})^\times \sqcup 3(\mathbb{Z}/3^{k-1}\mathbb{Z})$ gives $\frac{1}{2\cdot 3^{k-1}}\sum_{3 \nmid m} N_k(m) = \frac{3^k (4/3)^k - 3^{k-1}}{2\cdot 3^{k-1}} = \frac{3}{2}(4/3)^k - \frac{1}{2}$. For $v \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$, $(2v-1)/3 \in 3\mathbb{Z}_3 \iff v \equiv 5 \pmod 9$ (where $N_{k-1}((2v-1)/3) = 1$) and $(2v-1)/3 \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times \iff v \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$, establishing the closed recurrence on $\mathbb{Z}_3^\times$. Applying this recurrence two steps from $v = -1/4 \equiv 2 \pmod 9$ ($\psi_0(-1/4) = \psi_1(-1/4) = -1/2 \equiv 4 \pmod 9 \to -1$) gives $N_k(-1/4) = 2 N_{k-2}(-1)$; and applying it three steps from $v = 1/8 \equiv 8 \pmod 9$ ($\psi_0(1/8) = 1/4 \equiv 7 \pmod 9 \to 1/2 \equiv 5 \pmod 9 \to \{1, 0\}$, $\psi_1(1/8) = -1/4 \to 2 N_{k-3}(-1)$) gives $N_k(1/8) = 2 N_{k-3}(-1) + N_{k-3}(1) + 1$. Finally, choosing $m \in [3^k + 1, 2\cdot 3^k]$ with $3 \nmid m$ maximizing $N_k(m) = \#C^{-k}(m)$ gives $M_k \ge \frac{3}{2}(4/3)^k - \frac{1}{2}$, proving $r(U) \ge 2/\sqrt{3}$.

**(3)** Let $r \ge 2$, and let $\pi_r : \mathbb{Z}_3^\times \to \Omega_r = (\mathbb{Z}/3^r\mathbb{Z})^\times$ be reduction modulo $3^r$. For any $v \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$, $\pi_r(2v) = 2\pi_r(v) \bmod 3^r$, and when $v \equiv 2, 8 \pmod 9$, $\pi_r((2v-1)/3)$ belongs to the $3$-element lift set $L_r(\pi_r(v)) \subset \Omega_r$. If $U_{\mathrm{int}}, L_{\mathrm{int}} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\Omega_r}$ satisfy $Q_{\max}(T_r^{\max} U_{\mathrm{int}}) \le P_{\max} U_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $Q_{\min}(T_r^{\min} L_{\mathrm{int}}) \ge P_{\min} L_{\mathrm{int}}$, then with $\lambda_r^+ = P_{\max}/Q_{\max}$ and $\lambda_r^- = P_{\min}/Q_{\min}$, induction on $k$ via the homogeneous recurrence $N_k^{\mathrm{hom}}(v) = N_{k-1}^{\mathrm{hom}}(2v) + \mathbf{1}_{v \equiv 2,8 \pmod 9} N_{k-1}^{\mathrm{hom}}((2v-1)/3)$ yields $(\lambda_r^-)^k \frac{\min L_{\mathrm{int}}}{\max L_{\mathrm{int}}} \le N_k^{\mathrm{hom}}(v) \le N_k(v) \le (1 + k)(\lambda_r^+)^k \frac{\max U_{\mathrm{int}}}{\min U_{\mathrm{int}}}$, proving $\sqrt{\lambda_r^-} \le r(U) \le \sqrt{\lambda_r^+}$. At $r = 2$ ($\Omega_2 = \{1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8\}$), $L_2(2) = \{1, 4, 7\}$ and $L_2(8) = \{2, 5, 8\}$: the maximizing policy selects $2 \to 1, 8 \to 2$ with characteristic polynomial $x^6 - x^4 - x^3 - 1 = 0$, while the minimizing policy selects $2 \to 7, 8 \to 5$ with characteristic polynomial $x^6 - x^2 - x - 1 = 0$.

**(4)** Set $g(m) = 0$ for all $m \notin \mathcal{T}_{16} \sqcup \mathcal{T}_5$, and initialize $g(16) = 1$, $g(32) = \lambda$, and $g(5) = -1$, so $(U^* g)(8) = g(16) + g(5) = 0 = \lambda g(8)$ and $(U^* g)(16) = g(32) = \lambda g(16)$, with initial frontier $F_0 = \{32, 5\}$ consisting of integers $v \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. - If $v \equiv 2 \pmod 9$: both $u_1 := (2v-1)/3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $u_2 := 2v \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ have unique children $w_1 := 2u_1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ and $w_2 := 2u_2 = 4v \equiv 8 \pmod 9 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. - If $v \equiv 5 \pmod 9$: $(2v-1)/3 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ is a dead branch, so we set $g((2v-1)/3) = 0$, $g(2v) = \lambda g(v)$, and $g(4v) = \lambda^2 g(v)$ where $v' := 4v \equiv 2 \pmod 9$, and then apply the $v' \equiv 2 \pmod 9$ rule to obtain $u_2 = (2v'-1)/3$, $u_3 = 2v' = 8v$, $w_1 = 2(2v'-1)/3 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, and $w_2 = 2u_3 = 4v' = 16v \equiv 8 \pmod 9$ with $g(u_2) = g(u_3) = \frac{1}{2}\lambda^3 g(v)$ and $g(w_1) = g(w_2) = \frac{1}{2}\lambda^4 g(v)$.

## 7. Discussion and Open Directions

The five theorems proved here establish a unified structural framework for two-horizon trajectory merges, universal suffix-divisibility primitivity across all $\Delta K \ge 1$, general-$m$ $\Delta K = 2$ and $\Delta K = 3$ excursion valuation oracles, twisted Archimedean phase cancellation, and global shortcut composition-operator norms:
1. **Multi-Horizon Merge Hierarchy:** Theorem 1 proves that two-horizon merges $H^2(x_0) = H^2(x'_0)$ with $x_1 \neq x'_1$ across all $K_2 \ge 0$ never occur across distinct octaves inside the same intermediate shell (Regime I $= 0$), obey the $\frac{1}{2}$-weighted seam-pinning inequality and integer barrier $3 \le 2x_1 - x'_1 \le \lfloor 2^{k^*+1-K_2}(1-(2/3)^{K_2})\rfloor$ when straddling an octave seam (Regime III), and are governed in the dominant cross-octave cross-shell regime (Regime IV, which contains all $K_2 = 0$ merges at $x_2 = 1$) by the deterministic irrational torus shift $(K'_1 - K_1)\log_2 3 \pmod 1$.
2. **All-$\Delta K$ Automatic Primitivity, $\Delta K = 3$ Oracles, Twisted Transfer Bounds, and Composition Spectra:** Theorem 2 proves via $2$-adic injectivity of $C_L$ that every endpoint-distinct equal-weight solution $(\tau, \tau')$ of $C_{b'+1}(\tau') - C_{b'+1}(\tau) = 3^{K_{\mathrm{pre}} + \Delta K} m$ is automatically primitive for all $\Delta K \ge 1$ and establishes exact $O(1)$ valuation oracles for $K_1 = 1$-plateau-exit and terminal-inverted $\Delta K = 3$ excursions, while Theorem 3 gives the complete 4-branch $O(1)$ valuation oracle and multiplicity-$\le 2$ law for $\Delta K = 2$. Extending the valuation oracle to plateau-free ($\Delta K(s) \neq 0$) normal-terminal $\Delta K = 3$ excursions, combining the Fejér-twisted aliasing decomposition of Theorem 4 with the sparse Fourier character bounds of `collatz_42` [4], and determining whether the Collatz–Wielandt pinch $\sqrt{P_{\min}/Q_{\min}} \le r(U) \le \sqrt{P_{\max}/Q_{\max}}$ converges to the Haar lower bound $2/\sqrt{3} \approx 1.1547005$ as $r \to \infty$ are natural next directions.
