# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, substantive partial result on Collatz dynamics. Its strongest contribution is the exact agreement of physical and coefficient first exits from the starting band $[x,9x/2]$ for every integer $x\ge2$ through $P=217976794617$, together with the survival count and the natural densities of the exit directions. The sharp finite census and the explicit descent and motion restrictions provide additional mathematical content. These results concern restricted trajectories and finite horizons; they do not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive comparison in Section 7 is convincing. For large starts, the positive logarithmic deficit is bounded below by $D=P\log2-h\log3$, while the accumulated additive correction is smaller than $D$. The intermediate range is covered by the exhaustive prefix filter and the coefficient-gap calculation through step 164; the remaining small starts are checked directly. The argument includes the exit step and treats both boundaries, avoiding an unjustified identification of physical growth with its homogeneous coefficient. The independent choices at isolated zeros of the ceiling word give the stated residue count. The fixed-date cutoff and vanishing survival density justify passage to unrestricted natural densities, and the binary-digit argument establishes irrationality.

The other proofs also supply their essential mechanisms. The arbitrary-swap theorem combines proper-prefix expansion with a complete small-source exclusion, rather than treating a failed search as an unrestricted impossibility proof. The shifted-height identity, distinct-state argument and factor counts support the template descent bound. The low-state occupation estimates and boundary allowances support the disjoint-window conclusions. The critical pair is correctly presented as inherited from published scalar restrictions, not as a newly improved universal cycle bound.

I reproduced the complete supplied replay: the rational inequalities, both unit-test suites, all prefix exclusions, the original-band census, the auxiliary 121-letter census, the witnesses and the downstream census matched the retained outputs. In particular, this reproduces the unique exit at step 164, the two latest upward exits at step 153, and the distinct maxima of 382 and 701 for the two downstream clocks. A separately written coefficient-word recurrence verified the survival formula through 256 steps and enclosed the upward density to 25 decimal places; direct integer traces independently checked first-exit agreement for 100,000 starts. These finite checks support the proofs and computational certificates; they do not enumerate length-$P$ trajectories. Bařina's original verification and Hercher's original computation remain published external inputs. Their stated bounds and the scope of the corrigendum are consistent with the manuscript's use of them.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 177/v1, Section 6, already establishes the single-word classification for the factor-three starting band, and Terras [9] supplies the coefficient-stopping-time framework. The wider band here has an exponentially branching admissible language, an explicit two-boundary comparison and a different exit law. These are meaningful additions, not merely another numerical cutoff. The earlier source-floor, cycle-occupation and shift-mismatch papers address related mechanisms but do not supply these conclusions.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the main claims, hypotheses, proofs, retained evidence and limitations are identifiable. The following improvements would make the contribution easier to assess without changing the decision:

- Give the Section 7 first-exit result a numbered theorem and a short dependency roadmap. It is the title result but currently follows several substantial auxiliary results. The abstract would benefit from emphasizing this result and its proof strategy rather than listing nearly every numerical constant.
- Expand the related-work discussion to include Rozier and Terracol's treatment of paradoxical sequences and the coefficient stopping-time conjecture [11]. It provides particularly relevant context for the finite-horizon comparison. Also briefly distinguish the template theorem from the substitution bounds in Paper 84/v1, Section 7, and the qualitative finite-descent result in Paper 173/v1.
- State explicitly near the survivor census that convergence of this selected cohort does not establish convergence for all starts below $2^{100}$. The current conclusion about the minimum of a hypothetical nonconvergent orbit is correctly restricted.

References [1]–[10] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
