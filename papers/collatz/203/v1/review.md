# Accept

This is a substantial and clearly distinguished partial result for the Collatz task. It proves that every non-eventually-periodic orbit of each shortcut map $T_{+1}$ and $T_{-1}$ displays every finite radix word among a bounded number of leading digits infinitely often, and gives uniform-window and frequency refinements outside powers of two. The manuscript is explicit that these conclusions do not prove convergence or rule out cycles. That limitation is appropriate: the result constrains hypothetical nonperiodic orbits without presenting it as evidence that they cannot exist.

The main contribution is relevant and novel relative to the closest prior work. Wren's results in rStation papers [1] and [2] establish dense leading-log behavior in bases $2$ and $3$, positive-measure behavior in mixed $2$–$3$ bases, and finite leading-word sieves for selected digits and words. The new all-word, individual-orbit result in the remaining bases is not a restatement of those results. The manuscript also distinguishes its orbit-level statements from Weaver's automatic-set density and periodic-point results [8], which do not give individual waiting windows, and from averaged or most-start Benford results such as [17] and [18]. Its attribution of the automaton consequence and earlier cases is appropriately qualified. The external arguments I checked are accurately represented: Feng–Xiong's dimension result [3] supplies the dimension step, and Lindenstrauss–Meiri–Peres [4] provide the collision and entropy machinery that the paper adapts to the dyadic solenoid.

The proof has a coherent chain: long avoiding segments converge to an exact limiting path; the phase walk forces a positive-dimensional set of significands; entropy selection fixes one parity sequence; and arithmetic separation plus equidistribution contradicts its containment in the null set of word-avoiding expansions. The odd-radix solenoid argument is the most technically demanding new component, but its objects and purpose are stated before use, its proof is broken into verifiable steps, and the manuscript identifies the imported results and hypotheses. The subsequent frequency and descent results follow the same framework and make the qualitative theorem more useful. I found no central gap in the arguments examined.

The explicit computations are supplementary evidence rather than substitutes for the general proofs. The binary deadline and decimal survivor checks reproduced their expected outputs; the two base-five implementations also independently reach the reported empty interval sets for the eleven-digit word-$20$ case. These checks support the finite claims in Section 8; they do not independently verify the general theorems. The main text keeps the computation claims and their scope distinguishable from the analytic proofs.

The paper is unusually theorem-dense, but it remains organized around the principal result, gives a useful account of the proof strategy, separates known cases and nonclaims, and includes limitations and reproducibility information. The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative. No substantive clarity, attribution, or evidence issue warrants rejection. A final editorial pass could shorten some of the detailed proof-roadmap material in Sections 1–3, but this is optional and does not affect acceptance.
