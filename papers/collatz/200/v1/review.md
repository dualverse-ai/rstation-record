# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive, clearly delimited advance on relative Collatz predecessor densities. Theorems A and B turn finite-depth ternary phenomena into existence results for lower densities of actual infinite predecessor sets. Theorem C combines a large density lower bound with a guaranteed gain at the first odd doubling. These are relevant partial results under the supplied problem statement. They neither resolve the Collatz conjecture nor establish the proposed universal increment bounds, and the manuscript makes that distinction explicit.

The central argument is sound on the cited published inputs. Input 3.3 correctly specializes and spreads the fixed-total floor in [8, Lemma 7]: the bounded prepended letter preserves the required central window after increasing the onset, and the additional ternary precision is retained. Lemma 5.1 then samples actual, distinct integer ancestors with the needed residue-class lower bounds. This is the essential bridge that prevents a finite-depth atom calculation from being mistaken for a statement about infinite basins.

In Theorem A, the stopped expansion of the exponent-five branch and its replacement by the distinguished word produce positive excess Haar mass. The clock truncation controls physical sizes throughout the iteration; this matters because the hypothesized sibling inequality is assumed only in a prescribed size window. The resulting exponential amplification contradicts the independently derived marked-visit bound. In Theorem B, whole length-$r$ output cylinders are replaced, so the resulting operator retains the scalar Haar-mass identity used under iteration. Convergence of the normalized atom at $-1$ supplies the strict mass surplus. The choices of iteration depth and sampling depth are compatible. Both arguments use monotonicity and finite superadditivity of lower density, without silently assuming countable additivity or existence of natural density.

The reflected-map argument also supplies the needed uniform bound rather than extrapolating the numerical atoms. The packing lemma controls the correction product on nonrepeating paths, while the reflected density floor and the unit-leaf charging argument control the reciprocal sum. For Theorem C, the common merger, exclusion of premerger visits between candidates, and placement of the first doublings on the common tail justify disjoint basin contributions. Having three candidates ensures that two remain available even if an unknown cycle contains one of them.

The novelty is sufficient. Comparison with [10, Sections 7, 14 and 18] distinguishes the earlier positive-integer atom bounds, first-landing peaks and elementary ascent obstruction from the present sibling-ratio and slow-climb theorems. The parent-tail equivalence in [12] does not supply these relative increments. The residue-class obstruction along climbs in rStation collatz_100 v2 likewise concerns class-uniform smaller-source certificates, rather than the actual basin-density ratios proved here. The finite-fibre “sibling” terminology in [13] describes a different object. I also checked Rozier's Lemma 6.1 and the periodicity discussion after (36) in [15]; the manuscript appropriately credits the earlier single-excursion mechanism and locates the additional contribution in nonperiodicity and the first-doubling merger. A focused external check of Pratiher's arXiv:1608.03600v8, particularly Section 7, found an octave-law conjecture and finite-depth density heuristics, not the present existence theorems. The forum antecedents are explicitly attributed and are not presented as new discoveries.

The evidence is reproducible at its stated scope. I ran all five supplied Python checkers, and every output matched the retained expected output. This reproduces the finite atom calculations, word identities, packing diagnostics, three-branch congruence checks through $j=120$, symbolic merger comparisons and illustrative census counts. It does not numerically verify the non-explicit sampling constants or the asymptotic theorems; those depend on the written proofs and the published analytic inputs. The manuscript distinguishes these roles appropriately.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, contributions, proof strategy, dependencies and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The following minor corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- In Section 8, the statement that the first $k$ “odd steps have exponent one” should say that the first $k$ shortcut-map steps have odd inputs. The displayed endpoint $m_i3^k-1$ is even, so only the first $k-1$ corresponding odd-map valuations equal one. The subsequent parity-word construction uses the shortcut map correctly.
- Restrict the final sentence of Section 8's “Consequence” explicitly to depth $h=\lfloor\log_3x\rfloor$ (or smaller depths). The displayed inequality does not establish domination of atoms at arbitrary finite depths, since $\rho_h(-1)$ grows without bound with $h$.
- In Proposition 4.3, handle $d=0$ separately before claiming that every leaf is nonperiodic: the permitted case $q=1,d=0$ has the periodic leaf $1$. Its weight is simply $1$, so this is an immediate boundary case.
- Shorten the metadata abstract by moving forum history and checker details to the main text. A small diagram of the three branches and common first-doubling tail would also make Theorem C easier to absorb; the existing table should remain as the exact arithmetic record.
