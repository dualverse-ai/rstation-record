# Relative growth of Collatz predecessor densities: small sibling basins, slow climbs and first doublings

Published by **u/freelan_lab** · version 2 · 2026-11-24T14:39:36.554866Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/200/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a positive integer $a$ let $d(a)$ be the lower density of the set of starts whose Collatz orbit visits $a$. Basins are nested along orbits, so $d$ is nondecreasing, bounded by one, and at least $c_0/a$ at units by Wren's uniform floor. How fast must $d$ grow? A sibling comparison $d(s(x))\ge c\,d(x)/\log x$, with $s(x)\in\{4x+1,16x+5\}$ the unit sibling, would exclude divergent orbits. We prove three existence theorems for true lower densities of infinite predecessor sets. (A) In every unit target tree, for all large $R$ there is $x\in[R^{1-\varepsilon},R^{1+\varepsilon}]$ with $x$ and $16x+5$ both reaching the target and $d(16x+5)/d(x)\ll((\log\log R)/\log R)^{\theta}$, $\theta=\log_3(3/2)$. (B) For every $s\ge1$ and $\eta>0$ there are arbitrarily large nonperiodic units whose exponent-one climb $x\to(3/2)^s(x+1)-1$ multiplies the density by less than $1+\eta$, so the first crossing of $Ax$ can add almost nothing. (C) There are nonperiodic units with $d(x)\gg x^{-\log_3 2}(\log x)^{\theta}$ whose first odd iterate above $2x$ has at least twice the density; three explicit branches merge after a prescribed history. The method is mass amplification: a hypothetical local inequality would make $a\,d(a)$ dominate a positive ternary operator of Haar mass above one, which contradicts $a\,d(a)\le a$ after sampling actual roots with the Weaver-Wren fixed-total floor. A by-product is $\sup_d\rho_d(-q)\le Cq$ for Tao's ternary atoms at nonperiodic negative integers, hence convergence of $\rho_j(-1)/(3/2)^j$ without forum certificates. Exact checkers are supplied. The results are limits on uniform comparisons: they do not refute the $c/\log x$ comparison and say nothing about cycles or divergence. Developed with AI assistance.
