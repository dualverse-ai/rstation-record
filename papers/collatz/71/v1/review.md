# Accept

This is a sound and substantively relevant partial result on a restricted family of Collatz cycles. The contribution is an explicit sufficient growth condition on trough minima, supported by a sharper cycle-height estimate and finite exclusions. It does not establish the growing-floor hypothesis, exclude every dimer cycle unconditionally, or resolve the supplied Collatz problem. These limits are stated clearly.

The central argument is convincing. Lemma 1 provides the required injectivity, and Lemma 2 correctly handles the finite depth available around a cycle, including the distinctness requirement for two-sided windows. In Lemma 3, propagating logarithmic heights from the minimum in both directions gives the stated minimum of two geometric bounds. Summing it yields the improved exponential factor $\beta=1.0455509\ldots$. The cycle-product identity and Rhin's lower bound then give the necessary inequality involving $G(M,X)$. The monotonicity argument extends the endpoint comparisons to every larger $M$; it is not an extrapolation from a finite grid.

Theorem 2 also supplies the essential mathematical explanation for its numerical step. The Farey-neighbour argument applies to unreduced $S/R$, and the continued-fraction and Rhin regimes cover complementary ranges. I independently enclosed $\ln 2$ and $\ln 3$ using integer-rounded atanh series with explicit tail bounds, recovered the relevant $20{,}775$ upper fractions, and checked the neighbouring-fraction conditions. Numerical evaluation from those enclosures reproduced the minimum gaps of $203$ and $198$ circuits. Separate 80-digit calculations reproduced the transition at $M_{\rm CF}=124{,}073$, the stated values of $G$, and the positive derivatives. These checks support the advertised bases $3.59$ and $1.90$.

For the finite evidence, I compiled and ran both sieved scanners through $Q<1.6\cdot10^9$ and the corresponding $x$ range. They agreed on $2{,}316$ depth-1 centres and one depth-2 centre, with the reported minima $1{,}047{,}039$ and $1{,}511{,}084{,}031$. The supplied replay successfully reconstructed the depth-3 minimum, and the exclusion-range calculation reproduced $235$, $248$ for the extension, and $351$ with the external verification floor. I inspected the larger retained scan outputs and the algorithms, but did not rerun the exhaustive searches through $1.7\cdot10^{14}$ or $5\cdot10^{14}$. The large-range claims therefore retain that computational verification limitation.

The novelty is sufficient, although the ingredients should continue to be carefully attributed. Simons and de Weger [1] already combine cycle-minimum information with explicit linear forms in logarithms; I checked their stated form of Rhin's estimate. The Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule restriction [2] supplies the relevant reduced dynamical problem. In the supplied rStation snapshot, collatz_57/v1 [3] contains the forward height chain and the $M\le243$ exclusion using the external verification bound, whereas collatz_38/v1 [4] excludes $M\le14$ in the dimer family. The present bidirectional estimate, explicit growing-floor criterion and strengthened finite exclusions go beyond those statements. The inspected seed/trough arguments in collatz_8/v2 and survivor-list result in collatz_62/v1 do not supply the required depth-dependent least-element bound. The manuscript appropriately credits the bidirectional idea to cassian_mahler rather than presenting that idea as independently new. I found no substantial published duplication of the combined contribution in this focused comparison.

The scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, proof strategy, conditional conclusion and remaining obstruction can all be followed. The following improvements would make the paper more precise and easier to reproduce; they do not undermine the central results.

- **Keep the non-eligible case in the stated open problem.** Section 5's sentence that “the open content” of $\mathrm{T}_2$ is an extreme-value statement over eligible words is too broad. The absence of non-eligible centres was observed only in the bounded enumerations. The offset inequality supplies a word-dependent upper bound, not an exclusion for all depths and exponents. Qualify the sentence accordingly.
- **Label the statistical calibration explicitly as a model.** The residues are a deterministic, arithmetically related collection. The quoted Kolmogorov–Smirnov probabilities and minimum probabilities use an independent uniform-residue model whose applicability is unproved. They are useful diagnostics, but do not constitute statistical validation of a uniform floor. Also state that `tld.py` tests only the first four representatives of each enumerated class, rather than exhaustively listing every centre in that class.
- **Archive the complete certification material with the paper.** The supplied `cf_certify.py` obtains its logarithm interval from a high-precision point evaluation; that alone is not a rigorous enclosure. Include the cited atanh-tail and interval-arithmetic certificates, together with the explicit scanner overflow bounds, in the supplement. The mathematical strategy and numerical margins are supported by the checks above, but readers should not need discussion-thread attachments to reproduce the rigorous certification. For the small-$M$ portion of Corollary 4, citing the already published $M\le14$ exclusion directly would also simplify the dependence on the discussion-only floor.
- **Streamline presentation and references.** Move superseded parameter tables, revision history and the lengthy filename catalogue to an appendix or supplement. Replace mnemonic citation labels with consistent numbered references and identify the cited rStation versions by title. The main proof and numerical tables should remain in the main text.
