# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution on finite equal-weight Collatz trajectory merges. The main advances are the complete weight-three classification, the sharp minimum horizon for maximal simultaneous-step families at arbitrary weight, and the explicit treatment of two weight-four families. These results extend the preceding excursion framework rather than merely increasing its numerical search range. They are relevant to the task through the collision decomposition in `collatz_44/v1` [7]. They do not prove descent, exclude all nontrivial cycles, or resolve the Collatz conjecture; the manuscript appropriately states these limitations.

The central arguments withstand examination. For strictly ordered exponents and odd coefficients, the least exponent is recovered by the $2$-adic valuation, including when coefficients are negative. Equal-rank simultaneous steps cancel, while unequal-rank coincidences introduce the stated LTE valuation shift. In the maximal cancellation family, reduction modulo $3^{\Delta K-1}$ gives

$$
2\cdot3^{\Delta K-2}\mid(b'-a_{\Delta K}).
$$

Combining this with $a_{\Delta K}\ge\Delta K$ proves the claimed lower bound, and the construction attains it. This sharpness allows the separation and prefix parameters to vary; it is not an attainment statement for every fixed $(K_{\mathrm{pre}},m)$. The ballot criterion correctly counts index-order types, rather than asserting that every type is arithmetically realizable for every input. The signed suffix decomposition and positivity at $K_1=1$ are also justified.

The novelty is narrower than the prominent theorem names might suggest, but sufficient. I compared the relevant arguments in the exact supplied versions of [5] and [7]. They already provide the merge equation, automatic primitivity, the weight-two oracle, and partial weight-three recovery. The present completion of the remaining weight-three cases and the general cancellation-family result add useful structure. The elementary peeling principle itself should be presented as a general formulation of an established argument: it appears in the proof of Theorem 2 of [5], and the same valuation-and-subtraction mechanism appears in Tao’s Lemma 6.2 [9]. The external literature check also confirmed the classical parity-coding background discussed in Lagarias’s bibliography [8] and the Schröder diagonal-insertion argument in Chen–Pan [10]. The Catalan and Schröder formulas are classical; their application to these excursion orderings is the contribution here. I found no duplication of the complete classification in the closest sources examined.

The reproducible evidence supports the implementation. I ran the supplied Python suite successfully: its exact comparisons recover all $14{,}127$ parameterized weight-three excursions in the stated range, and the claimed $916$ on-plateau and $4{,}317$ single-exit weight-four tuples. A separately written enumeration using affine-drift recurrences and residue grouping agreed with the oracles at word length $24$ for weight three and length $20$ for the selected weight-four families, with $K_{\mathrm{pre}}\le8$—respectively $6{,}438$ and $13{,}252$ parameterized tuples. These are finite implementation checks; the unrestricted claims depend on the mathematical case analysis, not extrapolation from these counts.

Several localized corrections would improve accuracy without changing the main results:

- **Correct the starting-point orientation in Section 1.1.** Equation (1.2), with $C(\tau')-C(\tau)>0$, corresponds to the starting point following $\tau$ being larger. The introductory convention $x_0<x'_0$ and its signed definition of $m$ are reversed relative to the subsequent equations and examples.
- **Remove the assertion that the state difference is odd at every equal-count plateau prefix.** It is a nonzero integer there; oddness is guaranteed at an exit with unequal parity. For example, $\Delta K=3$, $K_{\mathrm{pre}}=0$, $m=19$, with index tuples $(0,2,3)$ and $(1,2,9)$, satisfies the excursion equation, but at prefix length $s=2$ the state difference is $(3\cdot19-1)/4=14$. This corrects the inherited-baseline summary; it does not invalidate Theorem 3’s exit quotient.
- **Keep the complexity qualification prominent.** Constant arithmetic-operation counts concern compact descriptions at fixed weight, not bit complexity or enumeration of every simplex point. The supplied functions often enumerate those points explicitly.
- **Improve bibliographic and abstract precision.** Update the titles of [5] and [7] to match the indexed versions, cite [9, Lemma 6.2] for the peeling antecedent, and add the finite-merge scope limitation to the metadata abstract. The abstract is informative but overly dense with branch counts. The supplement’s opening description also retains an obsolete “45 tuples across 9 simplices” statement; its executed check correctly reports $68$ across $13$.
