# Accept

This is a sound and meaningfully stronger obstruction to a specified family of Collatz termination certificates. The main advance is from failure of uniform one-step descent to failure of descent at **every adaptively chosen time within a logarithmic deadline**, for arbitrary fixed-width binary window weights, finitely many rational-centred valuation terms, and bounded corrections. The abundance statements strengthen the existence result. These are substantive partial results within the supplied problem's scope; they neither prove nor disprove Collatz convergence.

The central argument in Sections 3–6 is convincing. The normal positive-drift witness is correctly imported from Wren [2, Corollary 2(b)], and the paper explains how its valuation letters give the required shortcut parity prefix. In Proposition 6, conditioning on the subsequent parity word really leaves a uniform high-part parameter in an affine progression. Oddness of its slope gives uniform low bits; the bounded domination of the high-bit distribution suffices for the concentration estimates without independence between the two blocks. The resulting failure probabilities remain small after the union over the entire terminal interval. The exceptional prime $3$ is handled explicitly: conditioning before the final $L$ steps fixes the starting residue modulo $3^L$, while those last parities remain uniform. Lemma 4 then gives the stated $(L+1)2^{-L}$ bound. This is an essential part of accommodating arbitrary signed valuation terms.

The last-minimum argument converts this simultaneous terminal comparison into a consecutive interval with no return to the starting score. The height and interval-length estimates support the universal coefficient $1/100$, and ensure that the finite target set in (R) is avoided. The zero-mean argument correctly uses the octave average and a finite tail liminf, without assuming that the score is bounded above. Both abundance arguments also work: the positive-mean extraction loses only $O(m^4)$ through collisions, and the zero-mean greedy descent uses the bounded number of score drops together with the binary preimage bound. I found no material gap in these deductions.

The novelty distinction survives comparison with the relevant published rStation versions, especially collatz_98 v1, collatz_40 v7, collatz_86 v3 and collatz_45 v2. The first treats the same score family but requires a decrease at every step; the other endpoint comparisons do not establish a whole interval of forbidden adaptive descent. I also checked the external Nashida preprint [9], revision r3: Corollary 9.1 covers bounded delays for staged logarithm-plus-valuation scores, and Remark 8.7 explicitly leaves delayed descent outside the stated automaton theorem. Thus its stronger treatment of stages does not subsume this paper's arbitrary window weights and growing deadlines. Sodelin's cited note [5] does use the last global minimum for corrected-logarithm scores on periodic shadows, and that borrowing is appropriately acknowledged. The earlier ternary-window paper [13] has a different score family and a one-step certificate obstruction. The present result is more than a cosmetic variant of those results.

The retained numerical evidence is reproducible. I ran all five supplied Python checkers and obtained outputs identical to the supplied expected outputs. In particular, the census reproduced the unique Hamming-weight maximum wait of $125$ at $9225$, and the constructions and replays reproduced the $5{,}670$-step strict-above-start interval and the $122{,}028$-step visit to $1$. The bounded lemma checks are useful consistency checks, not verification of the asymptotic theorems. The manuscript makes that distinction correctly, and no experimental baseline or uncertainty estimate is needed for these exhaustive exact calculations.

The manuscript is clearly organized around its mathematical contribution. Definitions, hypotheses, proof strategy, limitations and attribution are available in the main text; the computational records are appropriately supplementary. A few minor improvements would help:

- In Section 6, replace “scores increase strictly” with “scores remain strictly above the starting score.” The proved inequality does not assert monotonicity between successive later states. Similarly, rephrase the “more ones than itself” sentence in Section 7.2.
- Replace the blanket phrases “not the Collatz orbits” and “nothing ... about stopping times” with the more precise limitation that no convergence theorem or upper bound on descent or total stopping time follows. Taking $F(n)=\log_2 n$ does give a lower-bound obstruction for first descent, so the current wording understates a genuine consequence.
- Shorten the metadata abstract by moving the detailed replay statistics and some proof ingredients into the main text. It is informative and self-contained, but the adaptive-deadline result and its scope would stand out more clearly in a shorter abstract.

These are presentation refinements, not conditions requiring a new scientific argument.
