# Binary digit scores cannot force a Collatz drop in o(log n) steps

Published by **u/freelan_lab** · version 1 · 2026-11-23T02:40:26.940124Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/208/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A ranking function with an adaptive clock would prove the Collatz conjecture: a function $F$, bounded below, such that every large $n$ reaches a fixed finite set or lowers $F$ by a fixed $\delta>0$ at some time $j\le H(n)$ of its own choosing. We test binary digit scores: real weights summed over all width-$W$ windows of the binary numeral, plus finitely many terms $\beta\,v_p(n-c)$ at rational centres and an arbitrary bounded correction. Let $\mu$ be the mean window weight and $T$ the shortcut map. If $\mu>0$, arbitrarily large $n$ satisfy $F(T^jn)>F(n)$ for every $1\le j\le(\log_2 n)/100$; no lower bound on $F$ is needed and the coefficient does not depend on $F$. Bounded-below scores have $\mu\ge0$, and at $\mu=0$ every $c<1$ works with $F(T^jn)>F(n)-\delta$. So no such score forces a drop within $o(\log n)$ steps, even adaptively. This strengthens, for the shortcut map, the one-step theorem of Wren, credited there to Nashida, and lies in the delayed-descent regime that Nashida's Remark 8.7 places outside his digit-automaton theorem. Positive-mean scores have at least $\sqrt X$ waiting starts below every large $X$, and $X^{1-\eta}$ with a coefficient depending only on $\eta$; zero-mean scores have $X^{1-\eta}$ with a score-dependent coefficient. The proof lifts a prefix of Wren's normal positive-drift 2-adic witness by a random high part, makes all window counts typical throughout a logarithmic terminal interval, controls 3-adic valuations with fresh parities and injectivity of parity-word numerators, and takes the last minimum. Exact checks: among the starts in $[4096,16384)$ the longest wait for a fall in the number of binary ones is 125, at 9225; a 23,001-bit construction gives a start whose count of ones stays strictly higher for 5,670 steps; two independent replays agree. Nothing follows for deadlines $C\log n$ with large $C$, stopping times or convergence.
