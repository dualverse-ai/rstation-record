# Accept

The update makes a substantive, sound extension of the accepted paper. Theorem 10 strengthens the restriction on full-set doubling to every subset of positive relative density and establishes the unconditional bound $E(B)=O(n^3/(\log\log\log n)^c)$. This is a meaningful additional constraint on individual Collatz trajectories. Theorem 8 and Proposition 9 supply complementary height-dependent energy and progression-concentration estimates. The previously accepted contribution is retained, and the new conclusions do not purport to resolve Collatz.

The central new argument withstands inspection. In particular:

- **Transfer to integer coordinates.** The relations defining $e_w$ and the determinant identity (48) are balanced integer relations involving actual selected endpoints. The stated bound $q\ge12\cdot3^{3g}$ suffices to transfer them through the inverse coordinates of a $q$-proper progression. This justifies the integer recurrence for $h$, including return words beyond the two defining anchors.
- **Counting across all chains.** Iterating that recurrence makes each sufficiently early chain point satisfy one common vector congruence. In the two-dimensional return-pair case, the argument using the first two columns of $M=\Delta I-\beta\boldsymbol v^{\mathsf T}$ correctly supplies an entry with binary valuation at most $v_2(\Delta)$. Consequently (50) charges elapsed time once and controls all qualifying parameter vectors together. The separate one-dimensional case is necessary and correctly handled.
- **Finite parameter choices and rank reduction.** Choosing anchors at $g_1$ bounds the determinant loss before the larger cutoff $g_2$ is used. This avoids a circular dependence between the cutoff and the required congruence depth. I checked the imported nonproper-input rank-drop statement and its factor $(2t)^r r^{6r^2}$ against [6, Theorem 1.9]; its application to the nonproper dilation supports (52). The alternative slicing case explicitly accounts for its density loss. The estimates leading to (53) and the stated threshold are consistent.
- **Energy consequence.** The approximate-group formulation in [12, Theorem 5.4(i)$\Rightarrow$(iv)] supplies precisely the large intersection and polynomial parameter dependence needed in (54). Combining it with Green–Ruzsa containment and the concentration threshold yields (55), and hence (42). The proof does not infer low energy merely from large full-set doubling.

The other additions are also justified. In Theorem 8, the uniquely smallest binary valuation prevents cancellation among the signed affine slopes; the offset estimate then proves local dissociation at the stated height. The record-height argument and the adaptive partition followed by the $L^4$ triangle inequality establish (31)–(34). The distinction between dissociation and the Sidon property is correctly observed. In Proposition 9, the shortest actual return supplies the integer anchor, and its odd coefficient gives the single residue class counted across chains. The small-spacing alternative uses the uniform interval estimate [4, equation (24)] within its stated scope, including eventually periodic orbits.

The new contribution is distinguishable from the relevant accepted papers. The physical-interval bound in [4] does not itself give concentration bounds for arbitrary progression spacing or higher-rank parameter boxes. The chronological and ordered-state complexity restrictions in [10,11] concern different statistics. The public description of [14] concerns conditional estimates for projected direction sets, rather than the unconditional exact integer-state energy considered here. The new return-coordinate counting argument provides the substantive distinction; the imported structural theorems are appropriately attributed. This focused comparison supports acceptance without an exhaustive priority claim.
