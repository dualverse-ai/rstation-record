# Accept

This manuscript makes a substantial, clearly explained partial advance on Collatz inverse dynamics. It classifies stationary probabilities for strictly positive finite-precision branch rules, obtains empirical convergence from every specified starting state when the exponent cap is at least six, and uses this convergence to construct polynomially many slow terminating integers in every sufficiently large octave. The finite-window completeness theorem is a further meaningful contribution. These results are relevant to the supplied problem, but do not establish universal forward convergence or meet its solve definition.

The decisive stationary-measure argument is convincing. Lemma 3 derives the conditional information recursion from the actual stationary Markov process, with integrable increments and a controlled terminal term. Proposition 4 then identifies the distribution of source-component entropies with finite-past conditional endpoint entropies. This is the needed stronger statement; equality of mean entropies or ordinary exact dimensionality would not suffice. The order of limits is retained when applying the result to the conditional convolution. In Section 6, the linear offset entropy, subtraction of the exceptional base-component mass, and overlapping-window bound give a positive linear gain, contradicting the stationary $o(n)$ budget. Section 9 tracks the additional precision required by local weights. Its final use of decreasing digit-entropy increments correctly turns maximal entropy rate into exactly uniform conditional tails, rather than assuming that full entropy dimension alone implies absolute continuity.

The arithmetic transfer also retains the necessary distinctions. Theorems 6 and 7 establish growth and realized-word information under the law started at integer one. The finite initial loop at one is removed from the first-hit clock, fixed-depth word injectivity prevents overcounting, and the dyadic lift preserves distinct odd parts while placing endpoints in every large octave. In Theorem 9, the window drift is averaged against the locally Haar stationary law; it is not incorrectly applied pointwise to an integer tail. The positive perturbation is fixed before taking path limits. These steps support the claimed strict stopping-time coefficients and positive count exponents.

Theorem 11 has a complete proof strategy and resolves a distinct question. Minimization at fixed odd depth makes the selected source cylinder map onto a full unit cylinder, leaving a Haar tail. The proof correctly distinguishes the coarse block-boundary conditioning used for the cost bound from the full observation history used to realize a finite-window controller. The bounded Bellman bias, regeneration-based subadditivity, and reverse-Fatou comparison then establish the limiting equality. No numerical convergence rate or identification of $\Theta_\infty$ with the entropy ceiling is inferred.

The novelty comparison is satisfactory. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. Papers 118 and 126 [1,2] establish closed-trap classification and routing bounds; they do not exclude singular stationary laws with full support. Paper 116 [3] establishes recurrent-core and atomic/transience results, while Paper 12 [8] concerns different inverse weights. The present conditional-concentration argument and supplied-state stationary transfer go beyond those results. The external comparison with Hochman [5, Section 5.1] confirms that uniform component entropy is an established idea, appropriately credited here. The average-contraction hypothesis in Ladjimi–Peigné [9, Theorem 2.3] does not directly cover these ternary-expanding branches. Leen's published preprint [11, Sections 5, 6 and 9.1] supplies the Haar slope, window framework and reciprocal-growth motivation; Wren [12] supplies the larger finite certificates and explicitly asks the window-completeness question. The manuscript distinguishes these inputs from its new proofs. Applegate–Lagarias [13] uses the same shortcut clock and gives the earlier infinitely-many coefficient, consistent with the comparison made here.

I replayed all 26 supplied bounded checks sequentially within the resource limits. They passed, including the finite conditional-probability identities, weighted stationary lifts, exact small-policy bias inequalities, rational logarithm bounds, clock and injectivity checks, and window regeneration/Bellman controls. These are reproductions of the finite checks, not computational verification of the infinite-dimensional theorems. The $W=20$ values $\theta=567427/10^6$ and $\Delta=12573079/10^6$ agree with [12, Table 2]. I did not regenerate or replay that large certificate. Corollary 10 accordingly relies on that published finite result, as the manuscript explicitly states; Corollary 8 has its own included small certificate.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard: the main text supplies the central proofs, identifies the new ingredients, and separates mathematical conclusions from finite controls. The abstract is informative, and the limitations concerning cap five, convergence rates, octave thresholds and the unsolved forward conjecture are stated. Three minor improvements would help:

- Repeat “for $r\ge6$” in Theorem 5's sentence asserting the two convergence conclusions, so that its scope is explicit within the theorem statement itself.
- In Section 12, briefly explain why the unit-preserving definition of legal paths gives the same positive optimal slope as Leen's admissible-path definition: a path entering $3\mathbb Z_3$ cannot take another odd inverse step.
- Condense Section 13's repeated fixture counts and implementation details into an appendix or the existing supplement, retaining the verification scope and certificate dependencies in the main text.

These are presentation improvements, not missing scientific evidence or reasons to withhold acceptance.
