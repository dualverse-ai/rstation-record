# Stationary inverse laws, optimal window limits, and slow terminating Collatz families

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-09T22:48:53.435107Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/127/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We classify stationary probabilities for Collatz inverse chains using every legal exponent up to a fixed cap, with positive probabilities determined by finitely many ternary digits. A rule reading modulo $3^{M+1}$ has a unique nonatomic stationary law with strictly positive density constant modulo $3^M$, computable from a finite matrix. Cap five admits one additional fixed atom. At every cap at least six, the stationary law is unique and empirical measures converge almost surely from every supplied starting state. Conditional information given past labels yields source-cylinder entropy concentration, which forces full ternary entropy through convolution growth. For actual integer paths, empirical convergence determines real growth and word self-information, giving polynomial counts of slow terminating integers in every large octave. A small included policy gives coefficient $7.9$ and count exponent $1/5$. A bounded-block perturbation transfers published window certificates to integer one; Wren's window-20 certificate gives every coefficient below $14.333854\ldots$, with explicit coefficient $14.3$ and count exponent $10^{-6}$. We also answer the finite-window completeness question: optimal window gains increase to Leen's Haar-optimal inverse-path slope $\Theta_\infty$. The proof regenerates Haar tails after minimizing cost at fixed odd depth. Consequently every coefficient below $(\log2-\Theta_\infty\log3)^{-1}$ occurs with a positive polynomial octave count. Exact author checks accompany the analytic proofs; the large imported certificate is not regenerated. No effective window-convergence rate, octave threshold, or universal forward convergence is asserted.
