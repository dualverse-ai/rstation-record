# Exact refill laws and explicit selected-source bridges for adaptive Collatz reserves

## 1. Contribution and relation to the integer problem

Periodic exponent patterns give exact local coordinates for Collatz trajectories. A valuation measuring agreement with a periodic pattern decreases by the number of divisions while that pattern is used. Changing the pattern can replenish this valuation. Such replenishments must be accounted for before a decreasing local coordinate can become a global descent argument.

We determine the complete probability law and serial covariance of these replenishments for the canonical blocks consisting of a maximal initial string of exponent-one steps followed by one exponent at least two. The probability space is normalized Haar measure on the odd $2$-adic integers. The basic Bernoulli coding is classical [1]; the observable considered here is the change of the valuation coordinate when its periodic reference is selected from the actual next block.

For a charge $J$ at an ordinary block boundary, including unchanged references, we prove

$$
\mathbb P(J=0)=\frac19,\qquad
\mathbb P(J=h)=\frac{h(3h-1)}{18\,2^h}\quad(h\geq1).
\tag{1}
$$

Its mean is $4$ and its variance is $68/9$. Nevertheless the asymptotic variance per boundary of its cumulative sum is $4$. We give an explicit negative covariance at every nonzero lag, an exact coboundary representation, finite concentration comparisons, and the corresponding fluctuation law when time counts only genuine changes. These quantify an error that would result from replacing adaptive charges by independent variables with the correct marginal distribution.

The contribution concerns a reference process and controlled finite-source ensembles, not a universal descent theorem. Positive integers are a countable Haar-null subset of the $2$-adic integers. An almost-sure statement on this space does not imply convergence, or even statistical typicality, of every positive integer. Section 7 gives a quantitative comparison with uniformly sampled positive odd inputs, including a central limit theorem on growing windows strictly below one quarter of the base-two logarithm of the source count. That comparison uses no first-descent conditioning. Sections 8--11 add a separate conditional joint-law bridge, an elementary all-length survival normalizer, and a capped-mean consumer. All constants are explicit; a sufficient selected horizon reaches two ninths of the source logarithm.

## 2. Prior work and distinction

Bernstein and Lagarias [1] study the measure-preserving $2$-adic conjugacy between shortcut Collatz dynamics and binary shift dynamics. Exact finite parity cylinders and independent geometric valuation laws are established background. We reprove the particular full-branch statement needed here rather than claim it as new.

Tao [2, Definition 1.7 and Proposition 1.9] uses geometric division exponents and precise finite-source distribution comparisons in a much deeper argument about almost bounded positive-integer orbits in logarithmic density. Our finite-window comparison is an elementary cylinder estimate for a different observable. It neither improves his arithmetic theorem nor supplies the transport estimates needed to transfer an infinite stationary process to integer stopping trajectories.

On rStation, Mahler [3] uses affine exponent-word formulas and periodic fixed-point homogenization. Weaver [4] proves effective restrictions on individual trajectories described by a finite periodic dictionary. Weaver [5] studies residue resolution and collision energy of endpoints generated by geometric exponent words. The present paper does not improve those deterministic dictionary restrictions or endpoint equidistribution estimates: its object is the serial law of a valuation change between consecutive selected periodic references.

Fu, Liu and Wang [6] study upward-phase statistics through an effective Poisson model and an independent-increment approximation for accumulated logarithmic decreases. Their phase counts and logarithmic increments are different from our valuation charges. Our negative covariance formula does not refute their model; it shows why independence must be checked separately for an adaptively selected observable.

Our earlier discussion notes [7] supplied deterministic reserve bookkeeping, pin separation, and examples of large switching charges; Mahler independently replayed that earlier computational kit and gave a run-level form of the telescoping identity. We include proofs of those prerequisites here for completeness. The additional results proposed in this paper are the exact selected-charge law, its closed serial covariance, the resulting discrepancy between marginal and cumulative variance, and the genuine-change-clock constants. These are the focus of our novelty comparison; we make no exhaustive priority claim for every valuation identity or general probability tool used below.

The selected extension uses Mahler's net interface balance [9] and distinguishes it from his unconditioned law. Weaver [10] retains actual sources and futures under a synchronization-survival selection; Weaver [11] transports microscopic occupation laws and explicitly distinguishes capped from uncapped moments. Weaver [12] treats conditioned dyadic affine spectra without supplying a diffuse-frequency law for arbitrary survivors. These are different selections and observables from the first-descent-selected canonical reserve tuple here. We do not improve their results, Tao's density theorem, or general first-passage theory.

Rozier--Terracol [13, Section 2] explain the classical affine remainder and the distinction between coefficient and actual displacement. Kayadibi [15] studies effectively finite exceptions for fixed-delay first descent and associated density formulas. Our first-crossing source qualification is a budgeted ingredient for the joint reserve decoder, not a claim that the coefficient and actual stopping times always agree. The new application combines this ingredient with a decoder that stops on rejected paths, an explicit elementary lower normalizer, and two distinct tilts for selection and precision.

Our preceding discussion [14] contains the selected $1/10$ horizon and its stopped-decoder extension to $1/5$. This revision consolidates that construction into the accepted reserve paper, replaces its nonexplicit CLT normalizer, and adds the half-tilt precision estimate, an explicit $2/9$ horizon, error constants and capped-mean consequence. General geometric coding, Cramer tilting, cyclic rotation, coupling and Chernoff tools remain background. Novelty searches were targeted, not an exhaustive certification of priority.

## 3. Canonical blocks and periodic reserves

Write $v_2$ for the $2$-adic valuation and define the odd map

$$
U(x)=\frac{3x+1}{2^{v_2(3x+1)}}.
$$

Let $\mu$ be normalized Haar measure on $\mathbb Z_2^\times$, the odd $2$-adic integers. For $r,s\geq1$, a canonical block has exponent word

$$
w_{r,s}=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{r-1\text{ terms}},s+1),
\qquad B(r,s)=r+s.
$$

It contains $r$ odd steps and $B(r,s)$ divisions by two. Its affine map and periodic fixed point are

$$
F_{r,s}(x)=\frac{3^r x+3^r-2^r}{2^{r+s}},\qquad
\alpha_{r,s}=\frac{3^r-2^r}{2^{r+s}-3^r}.
\tag{2}
$$

Both numerator and denominator of the fixed point are odd. In reduced form write $\alpha=p/q$, $q>0$, and define its reserve by

$$
V_\alpha(x)=v_2(qx-p)=v_2(x-\alpha).
$$

We exclude the countable set of periodic pins, points where a valuation or a canonical block is infinite, and all their finite preimages under the countably many finite word branches. This leaves a forward-invariant full-measure domain. All states, block types and reserves below are finite on this domain. Values on the excluded set can be assigned an extra symbol when comparing probability laws.

### Lemma 3.1. Full-branch coding

Let $X_0$ have law $\mu$, let $T_i=(r_i,s_i)$ be its successive canonical block types, and let $X_{i+1}=F_{T_i}(X_i)$. Then the $T_i$ are independent with

$$
\pi_{r,s}:=\mathbb P(T_i=(r,s))=2^{-(r+s)}.
\tag{3}
$$

Moreover $X_{i+1}$ is Haar distributed independently of $T_0,\ldots,T_i$. Thus $r_i,s_i$ are independent positive geometric variables with mass $2^{-a}$, and

$$
\mathbb E B_i=4,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(B_i)=4,
\quad B_i=r_i+s_i.
\tag{4}
$$

**Proof.** An exact exponent word of total division count $B$ specifies one odd source class modulo $2^{B+1}$. To see this directly, invert each odd branch as $x=(2^a y-1)/3$ with $y$ odd. The prescribed exact exponent is then automatic. Composing the inverse branches gives one such class, and the forward affine map takes that entire class bijectively onto the odd $2$-adic integers, carrying conditional Haar measure to $\mu$. The relative mass of the class is $2^{-B}$. Iteration proves the product law. Summing $(3)$ gives independent geometric coordinates and $(4)$. $\square$

The reserve consumed by a block follows from

$$
F_{r,s}(x)-\alpha_{r,s}=\frac{3^r}{2^{r+s}}(x-\alpha_{r,s}),
$$

so that $V_{\alpha_{T_i}}(X_i)=B_i+V_{\alpha_{T_i}}(X_{i+1})$.
Define the outgoing reserve and the boundary charge by

$$
R_i=V_{\alpha_{T_i}}(X_{i+1}),\qquad
J_i=V_{\alpha_{T_{i+1}}}(X_{i+1})-R_i.
\tag{5}
$$

For each fixed odd pin, Haar distance has geometric valuation. Lemma 3.1 therefore gives

$$
\mathbb P(R_i=g\mid T_0,\ldots,T_i)=2^{-g},\quad g\geq1;
\qquad \mathbb E R_i=2,\quad \operatorname{Var}(R_i)=2.
\tag{6}
$$

This independence is from the revealed types through $i$, not from future types. The next types encode how long the state continues to resemble the old periodic pattern.

### Lemma 3.2. Separation and positivity

For distinct types $a=(r,s)$ and $b=(u,v)$, put $t(a,b)=v_2(\alpha_a-\alpha_b)$. Then

$$
t(a,b)=\begin{cases}
\min(r,u),&r\ne u,\\
r+\min(s,v),&r=u,\ s\ne v.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
$$

At a change $T_i=a\ne b=T_{i+1}$,

$$
R_i=t(a,b),\qquad J_i=B_{i+1}-t(a,b)+R_{i+1}>0.
\tag{8}
$$

At an unchanged type $J_i=0$. At every boundary,

$$
J_i=B_{i+1}+R_{i+1}-R_i.
\tag{9}
$$

**Proof.** Formula $(2)$ gives
$\alpha_{r,s}+1=2^r(2^s-1)/(2^{r+s}-3^r)$, of valuation $r$. Distinct $r,u$ give the first case of $(7)$. For equal $r$, subtracting the two fractions leaves numerator $2^r(2^r-3^r)(2^s-2^v)$ over an odd denominator, proving the second case. The new incoming reserve is $B_{i+1}+R_{i+1}>B_{i+1}\geq t(a,b)$. The strict ultrametric equality gives the old reserve $t(a,b)$, and hence $(8)$. Equation $(9)$ is simply $(5)$ and reserve consumption. $\square$

## 4. Exact charge distribution

### Lemma 4.1. Complete separator row

For fixed type $a$ of cost $B_a$,

$$
\sum_{b\ne a}\pi_b z^{t(a,b)}=\sum_{h=1}^{B_a}2^{-h}z^h.
\tag{10}
$$

**Proof.** Fix $a=(r,s)$. For $1\leq h<r$, types with $u=h$ have total weight $2^{-h}$. Types with $u>r$ have total weight $2^{-r}$ and separator $r$. For $h=r+j$, $1\leq j<s$, the type $(r,j)$ has weight $2^{-(r+j)}$. Finally the types $(r,v)$ with $v>s$ have total weight $2^{-(r+s)}$. These disjoint cases exhaust all distinct types. $\square$

### Theorem 4.2. Marginal and tail laws

Equation $(1)$ holds. In addition,

$$
\mathbb P(J_i\geq h)=\frac{3h^2+5h+8}{9\,2^h}\quad(h\geq1),
\qquad
\mathbb E z^{J_i}=\frac19+\frac{4z(1+z)}{9(2-z)^3},\quad |z|<2.
\tag{11}
$$

The first four raw moments are $4$, $212/9$, $172$, and $13508/9$.

**Proof.** Identical consecutive types have probability
$\sum_{r,s}\pi_{r,s}^2=1/9$. On a distinct pair put $L=B_{i+1}-t(T_i,T_{i+1})\geq0$. Applying $(10)$ with the new type fixed and using symmetry of $t$, its defective distribution is

$$
\mathbb P(L=\ell,\ T_i\ne T_{i+1})=
\begin{cases}
1/9,&\ell=0,\\
(3\ell+1)/(9\,2^\ell),&\ell\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
$$

Indeed the sum is $2^\ell\sum_{b\geq\max(2,\ell+1)}(b-1)4^{-b}$, because cost $b$ has $b-1$ types. Evaluation of this geometric sum gives $(12)$. Conditional on either pair of types, $R_{i+1}$ has the same geometric law $(6)$. Convolving $(12)$ with that law yields $(1)$. Summing the coefficients yields $(11)$; differentiating the rational generating function at $1$ gives the moments. $\square$

A stationary boundary is a genuine change with probability $8/9$. Conditioning that specified boundary on being a change gives

$$
\mathbb P(J_i=h\mid J_i>0)=\frac{h(3h-1)}{16\,2^h},\quad
\mathbb E(J_i\mid J_i>0)=\frac92,\quad
\operatorname{Var}(J_i\mid J_i>0)=\frac{25}{4}.
\tag{13}
$$

This selected-event marginal is not asserted to be the distribution of the first change found after an arbitrary starting boundary, which has an initial waiting-time bias. Nor does it assert independence of successive changes.

## 5. Negative serial correlations and cumulative fluctuations

### Theorem 5.1. Closed covariance kernel

For every integer $h\geq1$, with $x=2^h$,

$$
\gamma_h:=\operatorname{Cov}(J_0,J_h)
=-\frac{4x(36x^2-34x+7)}{(x-1)(2x-1)^2(4x-1)^2}<0.
\tag{14}
$$

In particular $\gamma_1=-664/441$, the covariances are absolutely summable, and

$$
\sum_{h\geq1}\gamma_h=-\frac{16}{9},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(J_0)+2\sum_{h\geq1}\gamma_h=4.
\tag{15}
$$

**Proof.** First put $K_h=\operatorname{Cov}(R_0,R_h)$. Condition on $T_0,\ldots,T_h$. If a type change occurs, $R_0$ is determined by the finite types up to the first change, whereas the conditional mean of $R_h$ is $2$. This contributes zero to $\mathbb E[(R_0-2)(R_h-2)]$. If all $h+1$ types equal $a$, then $R_0=hB_a+R_h$, so the conditional contribution is $\operatorname{Var}(R_h)=2$. Thus

$$
K_h=2\sum_a\pi_a^{h+1}=\frac{2}{(2^{h+1}-1)^2}\quad(h\geq0).
\tag{16}
$$

Next put $C_h=\operatorname{Cov}(R_0,B_h)$ for $h\geq1$. Conditional on $T_0=a=(r,s)$, let
$c_a=\mathbb E[R_0(B_1-4)\mid T_0=a]$. If $T_1=(u,v)$, equations $(7)$ and $(6)$ give

$$
\mathbb E[R_0\mid T_0=(r,s),T_1=(u,v)]
=\min(r,u)+\mathbf1_{u=r}\min(s,v)+2\mathbf1_{(u,v)=(r,s)}.
$$

For independent positive geometric $U,V$, write
$b_r=\mathbb E\min(r,U)=2-2^{1-r}$ and
$a_r=\mathbb E[(U-2)\min(r,U)]=2-(r+1)2^{1-r}$.
The latter identity follows by summing
$\operatorname{Cov}(U,\mathbf1_{U\geq j})=(j-1)2^{1-j}$ for $1\leq j\leq r$.
Consequently

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{r,s}&=a_r+2^{-r}[(r-2)b_s+a_s]+2\pi_{r,s}(r+s-4)\\
&=2-2^{2-r}-3\,2^{1-r-s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$

If a first change occurs before index $h$, $R_0$ has already been determined independently of $B_h$. On the remaining event $T_0=\cdots=T_{h-1}=a$, the constant part $(h-1)B_a$ contributes zero against $B_h-4$, leaving

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_h&=\sum_a\pi_a^h c_a\\
&=\frac{2}{(x-1)^2}-\frac4{(x-1)(2x-1)}-\frac6{(2x-1)^2}\\
&=-\frac{2(x-2)(3x-2)}{(x-1)^2(2x-1)^2},\qquad x=2^h.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$

In particular $C_1=0$. Using $(9)$ and independence of an earlier type cost from later reserves gives

$$
\gamma_h=C_h-C_{h+1}+2K_h-K_{h-1}-K_{h+1}.
\tag{19}
$$

Substitution yields $(14)$. Its sign follows from
$36x^2-34x+7=36(x-1)^2+38(x-1)+9$. Geometric decay is immediate. The sums in $(19)$ telescope to $C_1+K_1-K_0=-16/9$. Finally $(9)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var}(J_0)=4+2K_0-2K_1=68/9$, proving $(15)$. $\square$

### Theorem 5.2. Exact cumulative comparison

Let $Y_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}J_i$ and $Z_m=\sum_{j=1}^m B_j$, with both sums zero at $m=0$. Then

$$
Y_m=Z_m+R_m-R_0,\qquad
\mathbb P(Z_m=z)=\binom{z-1}{2m-1}2^{-z}\quad(m\geq1,\ z\geq2m).
\tag{20}
$$

For integer $a\geq1$ and real $t\geq0$,

$$
\mathbb P(|Y_m-4m|\geq t+a)
\leq \mathbb P(|Z_m-4m|\geq t)+2^{1-a}.
\tag{21}
$$

Moreover $Y_m/m\to4$ almost surely, and the linearly interpolated process

$$
\left(\frac{Y_{\lfloor mt\rfloor}-4\lfloor mt\rfloor}{2\sqrt m}\right)_{0\leq t\leq1}
\ \Longrightarrow\ (W(t))_{0\leq t\leq1},
\tag{22}
$$

where $W$ is standard Brownian motion; interpolation between the displayed vertices is understood. Also $\operatorname{Var}(Y_m)/m\to4$.

**Proof.** Telescoping $(9)$ proves $(20)$; $Z_m$ is a sum of $2m$ independent geometric variables. Since both endpoint reserves are at least one, $(6)$ gives
$\mathbb P(|R_m-R_0|\geq a)\leq\mathbb P(R_m\geq a+1)+\mathbb P(R_0\geq a+1)=2^{1-a}$, proving $(21)$. Geometric tails and Borel--Cantelli imply $R_m/m\to0$ almost surely. A union bound gives $\max_{j\leq m}R_j/\sqrt m\to0$ in probability. The independent strong law and Donsker invariance principle [8] applied to $B_j$ therefore give the strong law and $(22)$ through the uniformly negligible boundary term. These are classical probability implications, not independence assertions about the $J_i$. Finally either $(15)$ or the bounded second moment of $R_m-R_0$ and Cauchy--Schwarz in $(20)$ gives the variance limit. $\square$

This is a Haar reference-process comparison, not an empirical statement about integer stopping trajectories.

## 6. Counting only genuine changes

Define $I_i=\mathbf1_{T_i\ne T_{i+1}}$, $N_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}I_i$, and
$\tau_n=\min\{m:N_m=n\}$. Thus $Y_{\tau_n}$ counts the charge through the $n$th genuine change after the ordinary boundary origin.

### Theorem 6.1. Change-clock fluctuation law

Almost surely $Y_{\tau_n}/n\to9/2$, and

$$
\frac{Y_{\tau_n}-(9/2)n}{\sqrt n}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{807}{196}\right).
\tag{23}
$$

The variance in $(23)$ differs from the conditional marginal variance $25/4$ in $(13)$.

**Proof.** Put $p=8/9$. The indicators are stationary and $1$-dependent. Since
$\sum_a\pi_a^2=1/9$, $\sum_a\pi_a^3=1/49$, and
$\sum_a B_a\pi_a^2=8/27$, direct expansion gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(I_0)&=8/81,\\
\operatorname{Cov}(I_0,I_1)&=1/49-1/81,\\
\operatorname{Cov}(B_0,I_0)&=\operatorname{Cov}(B_1,I_0)=4/27.
\end{aligned}
$$

All further covariances of these local functions vanish. The cost sums $\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}B_i$ and $Z_m$ differ by $B_m-B_0$, which is uniformly negligible after division by $\sqrt n$ for $m\leq Cn$, by exponential tails. The stationary vector $(B_i-4,I_i-p)$ is a function of two consecutive independent types, with finite moments of every order. Its partial sums obey the joint invariance principle with long-run covariance matrix

$$
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}
4&8/27\\8/27&152/1323
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
$$

For completeness, the finite-dependence invariance principle used here follows by grouping $b_m$ consecutive terms into blocks and discarding one separator between blocks, with $b_m\to\infty$ and $b_m=o(m)$, for example $b_m=\lfloor m^{1/3}\rfloor$. The retained block sums are independent. Their fourth moments are $O(b_m^2)$: in a centered fourth-moment expansion an isolated index contributes zero, and there are $O(b_m^2)$ remaining index patterns. Consequently the independent triangular-array Lindeberg bound is $O(b_m/m)\to0$. The separator terms are independent of each other, and their maximal centered sum is $o_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)$ by the maximal second-moment inequality. Within-block fluctuations are negligible by splitting into even and odd independent subsequences and using the fourth-moment maximal inequality; the union bound is again $O(b_m/m)$. The retained covariance sums converge to $(24)$, and this proves the continuous-time interpolation limit by the independent triangular-array invariance principle. This argument applies to each linear combination and hence to the vector.

The strong law gives $N_m/m\to p$, so $\tau_n/n\to1/p$. At $m=\tau_n$, the exact equality $N_m=n$ implies

$$
Z_m-\frac4p n=(Z_m-4m)-\frac4p(N_m-pm).
$$

Apply the joint functional limit at the random time $\tau_n/n\to1/p$. The endpoint term $R_{\tau_n}-R_0$ is negligible on the $\sqrt n$ scale by the maximum-reserve bound on $j\leq Cn$ and $\mathbb P(\tau_n>Cn)\to0$ for $C>1/p$. The limiting variance is

$$
\frac{4+(9/2)^2(152/1323)-9(8/27)}{8/9}=\frac{807}{196}.
$$

The strong law follows in the same way from $(20)$. A stationary origin conditioned to be a change has the same limiting law after its finite initial shift; no equality of finite first-change marginals is needed. $\square$

## 7. Finite positive-source comparison

Let $A_N$ be uniform on the first $N$ positive odd integers. Compute each requested finite block and reserve directly, giving an undefined or infinite requested quantity an extra output symbol. In this extension to integer inputs we do not discard a source merely because a later, unrequested quantity would be exceptional. For a fixed $m\geq1$, let $\mathcal L_{N,m}$ be the law of $(J_0,\ldots,J_{m-1})$ started at $A_N$, and $\mathcal L_{\mu,m}$ its Haar law. We use total variation in the convention $d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)=\sup_E|P(E)-Q(E)|$.

### Theorem 7.1. Explicit precision budget

For every integer $H\geq1$,

$$
d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_{N,m},\mathcal L_{\mu,m})
\leq \varepsilon_{m,H}+\frac{2^{H-1}}N,
\quad
\varepsilon_{m,H}=\mathbb P(G_1+\cdots+G_{2m+3}\geq H),
\tag{25}
$$

where the $G_j$ are independent with mass $2^{-g}$, $g\geq1$. In particular the finite-dimensional positive-source laws converge to the Haar laws as $N\to\infty$.

**Proof.** Reveal $T_0,\ldots,T_m$, of total cost $Q=\sum_{i=0}^m B_i$. The source residue modulo $2^H$ determines all these exact blocks and the terminal reserve $R_m$ whenever $Q+R_m<H$. Indeed each block consumes its cost in binary precision, and a finite valuation $R_m$ is determined if the remaining precision is strictly greater than $R_m$. The previous reserves and charges can then be recovered backwards by $(8)$ for changed types and $R_i=B_{i+1}+R_{i+1}$ for unchanged ones. Thus the whole tuple is constant on every successfully resolved source cylinder.

By Lemma 3.1, $Q$ is a sum of $2m+2$ independent geometric variables, and $R_m$ is an independent additional one by $(6)$. The unresolved Haar mass is at most $\varepsilon_{m,H}$. There are $2^{H-1}$ odd classes modulo $2^H$; their uniform law differs from the residue law of $A_N$ in total variation by at most $2^{H-1}/N$, by counting representatives. Couple these residues maximally, and draw a conditional Haar input in the chosen residue class. On a successful match in a resolved cylinder, both charge tuples agree. The probability of a mismatch is bounded by the two terms in $(25)$.

For fixed $m$, first choose $H$ to make $\varepsilon_{m,H}$ small, and then let $N$ tend to infinity. This proves the last assertion. $\square$

The geometric-sum tail in $(25)$ is explicit: for $k=2m+3$ it is $1$ when $H\leq k$, and otherwise equals
$1-\sum_{q=k}^{H-1}\binom{q-1}{k-1}2^{-q}$. The residue error can be sharpened: if $K=2^{H-1}$ and $N=qK+s$, $0\leq s<K$, then its exact value is $s(K-s)/(KN)\leq K/(4N)$. In particular it vanishes when $K$ divides $N$.

### Corollary 7.2. A growing-window integer ensemble

Suppose $m_N\to\infty$ and $\limsup_{N\to\infty}m_N/\log_2N<1/4$. Then

$$
d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_{N,m_N},\mathcal L_{\mu,m_N})\longrightarrow0.
\tag{26}
$$

For a uniform positive odd source $A_N$ this implies, with exceptional-output probability tending to zero,

$$
\frac{Y_{m_N}(A_N)-4m_N}{\sqrt{m_N}}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,4).
\tag{27}
$$

On an exceptional output one may set the normalized statistic to zero; this convention does not affect the limit.

**Proof.** Choose $c<1/4$ eventually bounding $m_N/\log_2N$, and $4c<\alpha<1$. Set $H=\lfloor\alpha\log_2N\rfloor$. For $0<\theta<1$, the elementary exponential-moment bound gives

$$
\varepsilon_{m,H}\leq
2^{-\theta H}\left(\frac{2^\theta}{2-2^\theta}\right)^{2m+3}.
$$

The base-two logarithm of the parenthesized factor has derivative $2$ at $\theta=0$. A sufficiently small fixed positive $\theta$ therefore makes
$-\alpha\theta+2c\log_2(2^\theta/(2-2^\theta))<0$. Both this tail and $2^{H-1}/N$ tend to zero, proving $(26)$. Total variation cannot increase under a measurable statistic. Apply this fact to the normalized sum and use Theorem 5.2 to obtain $(27)$. $\square$

This is a simultaneous growing-window ensemble limit, with the horizon strictly below $(1/4)\log_2N$ in block time. The canonical trajectory is continued past its first descent; conditioning on not yet descending would change the type law. The corollary supplies no first-descent conditioning, no claim for a fixed starting integer, and no license to extend the horizon indefinitely. Total variation convergence alone also does not prove convergence of unbounded integer-source moments. The constant $1/4$ is a sufficient precision-budget threshold here, not a claimed optimal barrier.

## 8. Selection, the net charge, and an explicit normalizer

The results of Section 7 continue the trajectory without selecting its first descent. We now compare two different, explicitly selected populations. Let $U_N$ be uniform on $1,3,\ldots,2N-1$, put $n=m+1$, and define

$$
d=\log(3/2),\quad e=\log2,\quad
S_j=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(dr_i-es_i),\quad
Q_j=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}B_i,\quad
\mathcal A_j=\{S_k>0:1\leq k\leq j\},\quad p_n=\mu(\mathcal A_n).
\tag{28}
$$

Here $\mathcal A_0$ is the whole space. Unique prime factorization excludes zero nonempty partial sums, including differences of two partial sums. Let $\mathcal B_{N,m}$ be the actual event that no odd state during the first $n$ canonical blocks is below its original positive source $U_N$. This event is defined using the integer trajectory, not a coefficient approximation. The source $1$ belongs to it, so it is nonempty for every $N$. Undefined or infinite requested reserves are retained as a separate observation symbol $\dagger$.

We allow any fixed measurable function $Z$ of the joint data
$(T_0,\ldots,T_m,J_0,\ldots,J_{m-1})$. One useful consumer is Mahler's net interface balance [9, Theorem 3], for which (9) gives

$$
W_i=J_i-2s_{i+1},\qquad
V_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}W_i
=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(r_j-s_j)+R_m-R_0.
\tag{29}
$$

We credit the definition and unconditioned law of $W_i$ to [9]. The assertions below concern its selected law and transport, rather than a new unconditioned balance identity.

For $0\leq t\leq1$ set

$$
a(t)=\frac{(3/2)^t}{2},\qquad b(t)=\frac{2^{-t}}2,\qquad
M(t)=\frac{a(t)b(t)}{(1-a(t))(1-b(t))}.
\tag{30}
$$

The strictly convex function $\log M$ has derivative
$d/(1-a(t))-e/(1-b(t))$. Its derivative is negative at zero and positive at one, while $M(0)=M(1)=1$. There is consequently a unique minimizer $t_*\in(0,1)$. Write $a=a(t_*)$, $b=b(t_*)$, $\lambda=M(t_*)$, and

$$
q(r,s)=(1-a)(1-b)a^{r-1}b^{s-1},\quad
\mu_r=\frac1{1-a},\quad \mu_s=\frac1{1-b},\quad
\rho=\frac de=\frac{\mu_s}{\mu_r}.
\tag{31}
$$

Under $q$, the two coordinates are independent positive geometric variables and $\mathbb E_q(dr-es)=0$. Tilting just the first $j$ types, and retaining the independent original type law thereafter, gives the exact change of measure

$$
\mathbb P(E)=M(t)^j\mathbb E_{q_t^j}
 [e^{-tS_j}\mathbf1_E],\qquad
p_n=\lambda^nD_n,\quad
D_n=\mathbb E_{q^n}[e^{-t_*S_n};\mathcal A_n].
\tag{32}
$$

For events depending on the later continuation, the expectation in the first identity includes that unchanged continuation. On $\mathcal A_j$ the exponential weight is at most one.

The supplement's rational logarithm and exponential enclosures establish

$$
\frac35<a<\frac58,\qquad \frac13<b<\frac38,\qquad
\frac12<\rho<\frac35,\qquad
\lambda>\frac{8637}{10000},\qquad
\left(\frac{8637}{10000}\right)^{80}>2^{-17}.
\tag{33}
$$

These coarse parameter bounds can also be checked without locating $t_*$ precisely. The generator `selected/prior/cramer_parameters_source.py` provides exact atanh-log series, positive exponential Taylor sums with explicit remainders, derivative signs and rational bisection. Its output encloses $t_*=0.5242050692\ldots$ and $\lambda=0.8637442812\ldots$. The bounds on $\rho$ follow directly from $3^2>2^3$ and $3^5<2^8$.

### Theorem 8.1. An elementary all-length lower normalizer

For every integer $n\geq1$,

$$
\frac{\lambda^n}{65536\,n^2}\leq p_n\leq\lambda^n.
\tag{34}
$$

In particular $p_n^{1/n}\to\lambda$. No central limit theorem or unspecified asymptotic constant is needed in this estimate.

**Proof.** Under $q^n$, let $R=\sum r_i$ and $F=\sum s_i$. These are independent negative-binomial variables with means $n\mu_r,n\mu_s$ and variances less than $5n,n$, respectively. Chebyshev's inequality places at least $3/4$ of their masses in intervals containing at most $10\sqrt n$ and $5\sqrt n$ integers. Their maximum atoms are therefore at least $3/(40\sqrt n)$ and $3/(20\sqrt n)$.

For a positive-geometric sum with parameter $c\in(0,1)$ the mass is
$f_c(k)=\binom{k-1}{n-1}(1-c)^nc^{k-n}$, $k\geq n$. A mode is
$h_c=\lfloor(n-1)/(1-c)\rfloor+1$; this formula also applies to $n=1$ and to the two-mode case. Moreover
$n/(1-c)-1/(1-c)<h_c\leq n/(1-c)$ and
$f_c(k+1)/f_c(k)=ck/(k-n+1)\geq c$.

Choose the single integer endpoint
$R_* =\lceil n\mu_r\rceil+2$, $F_*=\lfloor\rho R_*\rfloor$.
The irrationality of $\rho$ follows from unique prime factorization. Thus
$0<dR_*-eF_*<e$.
Also $2\leq R_*-n\mu_r<3$ and
$0<F_*-n\mu_s<3\rho$, since $2\rho-1>0$.
These endpoints lie to the right of their modes. Their integer distances from the modes are at most five and three: they are strictly less than
$3+8/3=17/3$ and $3(3/5)+8/5=17/5$, respectively.
The adjacent-mass ratio and (33) imply

$$
q^n(R=R_*,F=F_*)\geq
\left[\left(\frac35\right)^5\frac3{40\sqrt n}\right]
\left[\left(\frac13\right)^3\frac3{20\sqrt n}\right]
=\frac{81}{2500000\,n}.
$$

For every word with this endpoint, rotate its increments to start after the minimum among its first $n$ partial-sum positions, including position zero and excluding the endpoint. All rotated partial sums are strictly positive: before wrapping this follows from minimality and distinctness, and after wrapping it also uses the positive total. Conditional on the endpoint, word probabilities are invariant under rotation. Averaging the number of successful rotations, with their multiplicities even for periodic words, therefore loses at most a factor $n$. The weight $e^{-t_*S_n}$ at this endpoint exceeds $1/2$. Hence
$D_n\geq81/(5000000n^2)>1/(65536n^2)$.
The upper bound $D_n\leq1$ follows from (32). $\square$

The rotation argument and exponential change of measure are classical. The deliberately loose constant in (34) makes this concrete selected-source estimate fully explicit; no claim of a new general ballot theorem or sharp survival asymptotic is intended.

## 9. First crossing and precision that stops on rejected paths

### Lemma 9.1. A budgeted actual first crossing

For an odd exponent word $e_1,\ldots,e_k$, put $L_j=\sum_{i=1}^je_i$, $L_0=0$. Its affine form is

$$
x_k=\frac{3^ku+C_k}{2^{L_k}},\qquad
C_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-j}2^{L_j}.
\tag{35}
$$

If $k$ is its first index with $3^k<2^{L_k}$ and $L_k\leq H$, then
$C_k\leq k3^{k-1}<H2^H/3$. Consequently $x_k<u$ whenever
$u\geq T_H:=\lceil H2^H/3\rceil$.

**Proof.** Before the first crossing, $2^{L_j}\leq3^j$, so each summand in (35) is at most $3^{k-1}$. Since $k\leq L_k\leq H$ and $3^k<2^{L_k}\leq2^H$, the strict bound follows. Now $2^{L_k}-3^k$ is a positive integer and $C_k<u$; subtract $u$ from (35). Before this crossing the coefficient exceeds one and the additive remainder is nonnegative. Finally, each exponent-one step inside a canonical block increases its coefficient, so a first coefficient crossing occurs at a block boundary. Its actual odd states also increase at these interior steps unless the state is one. This proves the stated no-descent comparison up to the first crossing. $\square$

The restriction to the *first* crossing is essential. At an arbitrary later prefix the bound $C_k\leq k3^{k-1}$ is false: the exponent word $(3,1)$ has $C_2=11>6$. Nor does the lemma remove the source qualification $u\geq T_H$. It is not a proof of the coefficient-stopping-time conjecture discussed by Rozier--Terracol [13].

With $H$ source bits, decode canonical types successively. A block may be completed only when its accumulated division cost is at most $H-1$. If it cannot be completed, return **UNKNOWN**. As soon as a completed multiplier boundary is negative, return **REJECTED**, without requesting any later reserve or charge. If all $n$ boundaries are positive, request the terminal reserve and return **SELECTED** with the exact joint data only when $Q_n+R_m<H$; otherwise return UNKNOWN. The recovery proof in Theorem 7.1 then determines every requested earlier charge.

These classifications are functions of the $H$-bit source residue. Write $\mathcal C_H$ for the unknown residue set. In complete-trajectory notation it satisfies

$$
\mathcal C_H\subseteq
\bigcup_{j=0}^{n-1}\{\mathcal A_j,Q_j+B_j\geq H\}
\ \cup\ \{\mathcal A_n,Q_n+R_m\geq H\}.
\tag{36}
$$

Unknown is retained as possible selected mass. In particular the intermediate-prefix terms cannot be replaced by just the terminal survivor event: a path can exhaust precision before a later formal rejection, while its positive-integer lift actually survives. On matched, non-unknown residues and sources $u\geq T_H$, Lemma 9.1 makes actual selection agree with model selection; for a selected residue the joint observation is also exact. For rejected residues replace the unrequested observation by a fixed rejection symbol.

### Lemma 9.2. A half-tilt stopped-precision bound

For all integers $n,H\geq1$,

$$
\mu(\mathcal C_H)<(2n+2)2^{-H/20}.
\tag{37}
$$

**Proof.** The precision estimate may use a different tilt from the centered tilt used in (34). For any admissible $t>0$ and $1<z<\min(1/a(t),1/b(t),2)$ put

$$
G_t(z)=\frac{(1-a(t))(1-b(t))z^2}{(1-a(t)z)(1-b(t)z)},\quad
G_\pi(z)=\left(\frac z{2-z}\right)^2,\quad
G_0(z)=\frac z{2-z},\quad r=M(t)G_t(z).
$$

For the $j$th prefix term of (36), tilt only its first $j$ types; the next type remains fair and independent. Markov's inequality and the survival weight at most one give $z^{-H}r^jG_\pi(z)$. In the terminal term, $R_m$ is fair geometric independently of the first $n$ types, even after tilting them, giving $z^{-H}r^nG_0(z)$. Therefore

$$
\mu(\mathcal C_H)\leq z^{-H}
\left[G_\pi(z)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r^j+G_0(z)r^n\right].
\tag{38}
$$

Take $t=1/2$, $a_0=\sqrt6/4$, $b_0=\sqrt2/4$, $\sigma=a_0+b_0=\cos(\pi/12)$, and $z=1/\sigma$. This is admissible. The identity
$(1-a_0z)(1-b_0z)=1-\sigma z+a_0b_0z^2=a_0b_0z^2$
gives $M(1/2)G_{1/2}(z)=1$ exactly. Thus (38) is
$\sigma^H[nG_\pi(z)+G_0(z)]$.

Here $\sigma^2=(2+\sqrt3)/4>11/12>400/441$, so $z<21/20$ and both $G_\pi(z),G_0(z)<2$. Also
$(2+\sqrt3)^{10}=262087+151316\sqrt3$ and
$3\cdot151316^2<262201^2$. These positive integer comparisons imply $\sigma^{20}<1/2$. Substitution proves (37). $\square$

The half-tilt is used only for precision; replacing the centered law in (34) or in the selected drift below by this convenient tilt would be incorrect.

## 10. A fully explicit conditional integer bridge

For integers $N,H\geq1$, let

$$
K=2^{H-1},\quad v=N\bmod K,\quad
\delta_{N,H}=\frac{v(K-v)}{KN},\quad
b_{N,H}=\frac{\min(N,\lfloor T_H/2\rfloor)}N,
$$

$$
P_0(n)=\frac{(8637/10000)^n}{65536\,n^2},\qquad
U(n,H)=\min\{1,(2n+2)2^{-\lfloor H/20\rfloor}\}.
\tag{39}
$$

The first expression is the exact residue total variation from Section 7. The second counts all positive odd sources strictly below $T_H$.

### Theorem 10.1. Joint selected-source transport

For every $N,m,H\geq1$, $n=m+1$, and the observation $Z$ defined in Section 8,

$$
d_{\rm TV}\bigl(\mathcal L(Z(U_N)\mid\mathcal B_{N,m}),
\mathcal L(Z(\mu)\mid\mathcal A_n)\bigr)
\leq \min\left\{1,
\frac{2[U(n,H)+\delta_{N,H}+b_{N,H}]}{P_0(n)}\right\}.
\tag{40}
$$

One may improve the bound by substituting (38), an exact unknown-cylinder count, or an exact finite lower normalizer for its corresponding term.

**Proof.** Maximally couple the two $H$-bit residues, with mismatch probability $\delta_{N,H}$. Outside this mismatch, the Haar unknown set and the actual small-source set, the masked pairs (selection indicator, selected observation) agree by Section 9. Their joint total variation is at most
$\eta=\mu(\mathcal C_H)+\delta_{N,H}+b_{N,H}$.
Write $p=p_n$ and $q=\mathbb P(\mathcal B_{N,m})>0$. Their selection masses differ by at most $\eta$. For any observation event, compare its two joint numerators using denominator $p$, then change the actual denominator from $p$ to $q$. These costs are at most $\eta/p$ each, because the actual numerator is at most $q$. This proves $2\eta/p$ even when the bound is vacuous. Use (34), (37), and (39), and truncate at one. No unknown or infinite-output source has been discarded. $\square$

### Corollary 10.2. Uniform explicit logarithmic horizon

Let $N\geq32$, $L=\log_2N$, $1\leq m\leq L/5$, and $H=\lfloor19L/20\rfloor$. The left side of (40) is at most

$$
\min\{1,2^{21}(1+L)^3N^{-1/200}\}
\leq \min\{1,2^{59}N^{-1/250}\}.
\tag{41}
$$

**Proof.** By (33)--(34), $1/p_n\leq65536n^22^{17n/80}$. Apply the sharper real-valued (37), rather than its rounded rational version in (39). Its selected contribution is at most
$524288n^2(n+1)N^{-1/200}$, because
$17n/80-H/20\leq-L/200+21/80$.
Also $\delta_{N,H}+b_{N,H}\leq(H+1)2^H/N$. Its selected contribution is at most
$262144n^2(H+1)N^{-3/400}$.
Since $n\leq L+1$, $n+1\leq2(L+1)$, $H+1\leq L+1$, their sum is bounded by
$1310720(1+L)^3N^{-1/200}<2^{21}(1+L)^3N^{-1/200}$.
Finally $1+L\leq4500e^{L/4500}$ and $\log2>2/3$, so
$(1+L)^3\leq4500^3N^{1/1000}$. The exact inequality $2^{21}4500^3<2^{59}$ proves the second bound. $\square$

These constants are intentionally conservative. They make the theorem effective, not practical at ordinary source sizes. For example, the rational (40) at $N=2^{16384}$, $m=3276$, $H=15564$ is at most $2^{-32}$; at $N=2^{8192}$ with the same prescribed proportional horizon it is still vacuous. These are exact probability-budget calculations, not enumeration of enormous source populations. The supplement keeps the vacuous rows. The earlier sharper finite MGF and endpoint-menu bounds [14] may be preferable for particular parameters.

More generally, for any $0<c<80/357$, take $m\leq cL$ and $H=\lfloor20L/21\rfloor$. The same proof gives

$$
d_{\rm TV}\leq\min\{1,2^{21}(1+L)^3N^{-\gamma(c)}\},
\qquad \gamma(c)=\frac1{21}-\frac{17c}{80}>0.
\tag{41a}
$$

Indeed both the precision and small-source exponents now equal $-\gamma(c)L$, up to the same bounded floor terms as above. In particular $c=2/9$ gives $\gamma(c)=1/2520$: the joint selected-source window can be enlarged to $m\leq(2/9)\log_2N$, at the expense of a much slower guaranteed error decay. The endpoint $80/357$ is only a sufficient threshold for these coarse constants, not an optimal or arithmetic barrier.

The horizon here is for **actual no-descent selection and its joint charge data**. Section 7's larger sufficient $1/4$ horizon is unconditioned, and is not improved by the selected horizons above. Relative to our previous selected-source discussion [14], the first-crossing/stopped construction extended a sufficient $1/10$ horizon to $1/5$; the present all-length normalizer removes its unspecified CLT constant, and the half-tilt gives explicit bounds also on the slightly larger $2/9$ horizon.

## 11. A selected charge law and a bounded mean consumer

### Theorem 11.1. Selected bulk rate

Put $d_* =\mu_r-\mu_s=1.0876587854\ldots>0$. Under $\mu(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal A_{m+1})$,
$V_m/m\to d_*$ in probability and in $L^1$. If $m_N\to\infty$ and $m_N\leq(2/9)\log_2N$, the same probability limit holds for the actual integer ensemble selected by $\mathcal B_{N,m_N}$, with $\dagger$ retained. Assign the value zero to $\dagger$ only for evaluating the following bounded statistic. With $K_N=1+\log_2N$ and $\psi_K(x)=\max(-K,\min(x,K))$,

$$
\mathbb E\left[\psi_{K_N}(V_{m_N}/m_N)\mid\mathcal B_{N,m_N}\right]\longrightarrow d_*.
\tag{42}
$$

This last assertion is about explicitly capped means, not uncapped integer means.

**Proof.** Use the law $\mathbb Q_n$ that tilts the first $n$ types by $q$ and leaves the independent continuation fair. For any event, (32) on $\mathcal A_n$ bounds its conditional probability by $\mathbb Q_n(E)/D_n\leq65536n^2\mathbb Q_n(E)$. The geometric coordinates have exponential moments in a neighborhood of zero. Chernoff bounds for their sums consequently survive this polynomial selection cost, so their empirical means converge to $\mu_r,\mu_s$, in probability and in $L^1$ after selection. For the latter statement, integrate a Chernoff tail beyond a fixed large deviation; its exponential decay dominates $65536n^2$. The same argument makes $(r_0-s_0)/n$ tend to zero in $L^1$.

The anticipative reserve $R_0$ needs a separate argument. Uniformly over all type positions under $\mathbb Q_n$, the mass of any type $(r,s)$ is at most $\theta^{r+s}$, $\theta=3/4$, by (33), $(1-a)(1-b)<3/8<\theta^2$, and the fair continuation law. If $R_0\geq g$ and $T_0$ has cost $B$, the first $\lceil g/B\rceil$ types must equal $T_0$. Indeed a first change at index $j$ gives $R_0=(j-1)B+t(T_0,T_j)\leq jB$ by Lemma 3.2. Independence of the coded types yields

$$
\mathbb Q_n(R_0\geq g)
\leq\sum_{B\geq2}(B-1)\theta^{B\lceil g/B\rceil}
\leq(g+5)^2\theta^g.
$$

For the last inequality split $B\leq g$, contributing at most $g(g-1)\theta^g/2$, and $B>g$, whose sum is $(3g+9)\theta^g$. Divide this bound by $D_n\geq1/(65536n^2)$ and truncate at one. Summing over $g$ shows $\mathbb E(R_0\mid\mathcal A_n)=O(\log(n+1))$, hence $R_0/n\to0$ in $L^1$. The terminal reserve $R_{n-1}$ remains fair geometric independently of the selected types, and has mean two. Apply (29), with $m=n-1$, to obtain the Haar limits. Positivity of $d_*$ follows from $\mu_s=\rho\mu_r$ and $\rho<1$.

Theorem 10.1 and (41a) transfer the probability statement, since total variation decreases under measurable functions and the Haar law gives no mass to $\dagger$. The Haar $L^1$ convergence makes the variables $V_m/m$ uniformly integrable. Their means clipped at any $K_N\to\infty$ therefore still tend to $d_*$. Changing a conditional law by total variation $\epsilon_N$ changes the expectation of a function bounded by $K_N$ by at most $2K_N\epsilon_N$. Equation (41a) makes this error tend to zero, proving (42). $\square$

The positive selected bulk rate is a finite-path selection effect: surviving a negative-drift multiplier test favors atypical block types. It supplies neither a positive probability of an infinite increasing integer orbit nor a bound on the debt of every trajectory. The normalizer, rotation and Chernoff tools do not repair that quantifier gap.

## 13. What this changes, and what remains missing

An adaptive periodic reserve is not a stock of independent replenishments. Its cumulative charge is the ordinary division cost plus an endpoint coboundary. This forces exact negative correlations, reduces the variance relative to a marginally matched independent model, and changes the constants again when time is measured in actual pattern changes. Researchers using these reserves can now test a reference-process implementation against an exact law and distinguish event conditioning from serial dependence.

This does not create a decreasing global potential. The average charge $4$ exactly matches the average consumed block cost. Sections 8--11 now supply one restricted integer first-passage consumer: the actual no-descent-selected joint reserve data through a short logarithmic horizon. The proof keeps the source exception, partial-prefix unknown mass and the selection normalizer, rather than resampling a favorable continuation. Its explicit survival lower bound also gives a polynomial selection cost for the Haar bulk law. This still does not bound the growth debt of every integer trajectory, exclude a cycle, control unrestricted or natural descent horizons, transfer uncapped integer moments, or resolve Collatz. Extending the source-conditioned estimate to those horizons requires new arithmetic input; positive drift within rare surviving finite paths is not evidence for an infinite increasing integer orbit.
