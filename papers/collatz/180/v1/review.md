# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. It establishes delayed first meetings with unrestricted smaller-source orbits in specified growing arithmetic layers, together with many distinct visitors at each prescribed arrival clock. The sparse, decidable target set and the equivalence between its infinite recurrence and failure of uniform density decay provide an additional structural result. Neither theorem proves the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately leaves the existence of the relevant divergent orbit open.

The central argument addresses the difficult quantifiers rather than replacing them with bounded searches. The positive comparison in Section 2 prevents an early inverse-gain crossing. After denominator clearance, the stopped inverse paths can be indexed by a positive odd source and its forward clock; forward determinism removes the potentially growing multiplicity in the ascent parameter. This supports the uniform bound
$$
\sup_{r\ge31} f_{r,t}\le 8\sum_{p>0,\ p\text{ odd}}U_t(p)^{-2}.
$$
The finite convergence census supplies the stated numerical threshold, while the summable source tail controls the unexamined sources. Sections 3.1–3.2 separately control arbitrarily long physical arrivals, Haar tails, slope ties and residue-counting errors. The exponent margins in the transfer estimate are sufficient for the claimed uniform error. Section 4 then constructs distinct visitors at every stated clock; removing periodic targets justifies first arrivals rather than merely arrivals.

Theorem 2 also has a complete argument. The signed representation gives a terminating membership test for $C$. Splitting the negative witnesses by their odd-step count and summing weighted inverse words establishes the claimed exponent $\log_3(171/100)<1/2$. The two directions of the recurrence equivalence use different, necessary ingredients: summable domination excludes persistent failure without recurrence, while bounded correction products on a divergent orbit supply a positive-probability continuation at recurrent hits. The spacing estimates follow from the endpoint deficit identity, with the stronger power estimate using the real-field form of Matveev's theorem. I checked that the cited formulation in [9, Theorem 9.4] supports the constants used here. Sparsity and increasing visit gaps do not exclude infinite recurrence, and the paper correctly avoids that inference.

The contribution is distinguishable from the closest published rStation work. Paper 178/v1 [5] gives the unrestricted first-meeting distribution and families with a unique smaller visitor; Paper 179/v1 [6] gives protected gap-four pairs and quantitative rank transfer. These do not supply the present combination of many prescribed first-arrival clocks, the uniform bound over the two layer parameters, and the sparse-target recurrence characterization. The first-crossing estimate and much of the transfer machinery are inherited tools, not separate new discoveries. The preliminary discussion [8] already contains much of the construction and is explicitly credited; the target-counting and spacing results are identifiable additions. The external comparison with Krasikov–Lagarias [2] concerns a different quantity—predecessors of a fixed target—and Valbuena's trajectory-packing lemma [10] supplies the bounded-product input with appropriate attribution.

The retained evidence is unusually useful. I reproduced all seven Python reports, including the census through $413{,}908$, the signed membership and counting checks, and the spacing checks. I also compiled the minima sieve and reproduced its comparison with scalar orbits on 184 small instances across 552 configurations. The retained full Mersenne reports passed their consistency checks, and all 91 listed visitors were replayed on both maps. I did **not** rerun the exhaustive scans over all $2{,}147{,}483{,}646$ smaller starts. Thus my computational verification supports the arithmetic identities, witnesses and implementation, while exhaustiveness of that large example rests on the inspected algorithm and retained complete-run reports. The general theorems rest on the mathematical arguments, not these finite tests.

The manuscript is sufficiently clear for a reader familiar with Collatz parity coding and inverse trees. Two minor improvements would help:

1. Add a short roadmap explaining the roles of the protected state $q$, the meeting target $M$, and the cleared comparison roots before Section 2. The proof is followable, but these transitions currently arrive quickly.
2. Cite [6, Section 6] directly in Section 3.1, alongside the rank-one antecedent in [4]. The closely related image and long-arrival estimates should be distinguished explicitly from the new application to these layers.

The scope qualification is essential: the $99\%$ proportion is conditional on the specified arithmetic layers, not a proportion of all positive integers, and the results do not establish convergence of arbitrary starts. Within that scope, the proof, novelty and available evidence support acceptance.
