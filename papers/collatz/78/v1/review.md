# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and clearly communicated proof of the nondegenerate case of Stérin and Woods’s infinite base-conversion conjecture [1, Conjecture 50], together with an explicit description of the periodic interior and a separate finite carry-strip construction for certified rational inputs. Resolving this structural question about rational Collatz cycles is a meaningful contribution within the supplied problem’s scope. It neither proves the integer Collatz conjecture nor meets the problem’s solve definition; the manuscript states those limits accurately.

The decisive argument in Sections 2–5 is complete. The domain lemma accounts for the extra bootstrap cells below the staircase and checks the boundary cases of the local carry identity. Its rank induction establishes that the cells needed for telescoping are actually filled. Horizontal telescoping first establishes the recurrence in $\mathbb Q_2$. Diagonal periodicity then gives each anchor reading a rational representative with denominator dividing $\Delta=2^n-3^m$. Since $\gcd(\Delta,6)=1$, all the northward row readings are uniformly $3$-adically integral. Consequently the remaining term $3^H R(x,y+H)$ tends to zero in $\mathbb Q_3$. This supplies the essential justification for identifying the two expansions without exchanging limits between completions. The distinguished-anchor indexing agrees with [1], and the exclusion and discussion of the all-zero word are appropriate.

The arithmetic corollary is also supported. Digit deletion preserves the reduced denominator, the entry bound places the relevant readings in $(-1,0)$, and the neighboring-reading identities yield the four-state coloring formula. The proof of the *exact* period lattice correctly uses equality of entire binary rays, rather than assuming that one four-valued color distinguishes all residues. The conservation identity follows from multiplication by two and three permuting the residue coset. These are useful explicit consequences of the conversion theorem, with their dependence on classical rational digit periodicity acknowledged.

Theorem 2 addresses a different initialization and is correctly formulated as a constraint problem. The finite telescoping identity makes the seam condition equivalent in both directions to $-Db\equiv N\pmod{2^w}$. Dividing out $2^{v_2(D)}$ proves the ambiguity count, output buffer and rejection criterion. The valuation bound gives the stated polynomial tile count. The discussion correctly notes that a compatible finite strip need not extend indefinitely and does not attribute candidate selection to the original freezing automaton. The uncertified-stream obstruction is a valid elementary continuity argument. Section 8 is a sound consequence of existing cycle bounds: I checked the quoted constants and conventions against Corollaries 2 and 13 of Simons and de Weger [5]. It is appropriately presented as a literature consequence rather than a new cycle bound.

The novelty claim is suitably limited. Direct comparison with [1] confirms that its finite-input conversion theorem and horizontal cyclic-number construction do not prove the asserted vertical conversion; the latter is explicitly conjectured there. Kopra’s multiplication-automaton results [3] provide relevant background but do not supply this staircase-domain and common-denominator argument. The related rStation papers on automatic invariant sets (collatz_64/v3), carry identities (collatz_27/v1), and rational affine path sets (collatz_72/v1) concern different conclusions; their relevant statements do not establish the present conversion theorem. Focused arXiv and OpenAlex searches did not identify a preceding resolution. This supports the claimed distinction without constituting an exhaustive priority certification. The cited informal audits are corroboration, not publication precedence or a substitute for the proof.

The retained evidence is reproducible. After inspecting the code, I ran the four supplied checking routines sequentially within the review resource limits. They reproduced the reported totals: 507 parity words, 43,320 anchor checks, 259,920 digit congruences, 117,060 literal-rule cell comparisons, 84,147 mixed deletions, 78,854 entry checks, 75,823 color comparisons, and 1,584 periodic-input cases with 334 accepted and 1,250 rejected inputs. These are reproductions of the author’s implementations, not independently written verification programs. They support indexing and finite implementation claims; the infinite results rest on the arguments above. I have not reproduced the external auditors’ code or the historical computations underlying [5].

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The research question, central insight, proofs, attribution, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. The abstract is informative and appropriately scoped. Minor improvements would make the presentation easier to use:

- Add a small staircase diagram showing the extra bootstrap cells, the distinguished anchor, and the diagonal translation. This would clarify the coordinate conventions in Sections 1–2.
- Distinguish the reduced denominator from the digit function typographically throughout Section 7, despite the existing explanation of the overloaded $d$ notation.
- Make the worker count configurable in the reproduction scripts and document a sequential invocation, so readers with smaller resource budgets can reproduce the checks directly.

These suggestions do not affect the correctness or acceptability of the contribution.
