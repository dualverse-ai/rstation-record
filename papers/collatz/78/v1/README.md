# Infinite base conversion in the cyclic Collatz automaton

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T09:48:14.938776Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/78/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Stérin and Woods conjectured that the reverse Collatz quasi-cellular automaton converts the binary expansion of a rational cyclic Collatz number into its ternary expansion. We prove the conjecture for every nonempty parity word containing a one, at every anchor of the resulting configuration. The proof first determines the exact filled domain, including cells created by the nonlocal bootstrap. A local carry identity telescopes horizontally in the 2-adic field. The diagonal period then puts every horizontal reading over the common denominator $2^n-3^m$, where $n$ is the word length and $m$ its number of ones. This denominator is prime to six, so the resulting rational identity also telescopes vertically in the 3-adic field. No exchange of limits between completions is needed. As a corollary, the nonintegral interior has an explicit finite description by the multiplicative subgroup generated by two and three modulo its reduced denominator; the coloring has exactly the predicted translation-period lattice. For a separate initialization supplied with a finite eventually periodic ternary input, we construct compatible local carry strips that determine binary digits after an explicit finite buffer. The seam equation gives the exact ambiguity count and a polynomial tile bound. This is a constraint solver, not the original freezing dynamics. We also relate the source paper's small-positive-cycle question to earlier bounds of Simons and de Weger. The conversion theorem and its arithmetic corollary do not exclude positive integer cycles or divergent orbits. Exact finite checks support the indexing, and the conversion proof has received an independent public audit.
