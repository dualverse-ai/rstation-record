# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, clearly communicated extension of the selected-source comparison in [1]. Its contribution is an explicit joint-law comparison, uniform over translated intervals of positive odd integers, with convergence for every fixed canonical-block horizon coefficient $c<4/17$. This is a useful partial result within the supplied problem's scope. It does not satisfy the problem's solve definition: neither universal convergence nor a counterexample to Collatz is established.

The decisive argument is in Sections 4–5. At a first coefficient crossing, every earlier division total satisfies $L_j\leq\lfloor j\log_2 3\rfloor$, which justifies the affine numerator envelope $C_k^*$. Retaining the positive denominator gap gives the stated source guard. I checked the application of Lagarias's Lemma 2.2(1) against the primary paper [8]: the all-rational inequality has the quoted exponent and constant, and its substitution yields $T_H\leq400H^{15}$. The argument does not assume that a crossing occurs for every source.

The coupling proof handles the conditioning issue correctly. Every consecutive block of $2^j$ odd sources contains each odd residue modulo $2^{j+1}$ exactly once. Consequently, every block's coupled reference marginal is the same full Haar law. Mixing these joint couplings before conditioning preserves that marginal; counting the disjoint small-source exceptions only once gives (15). The subsequent conditional bound uses the Haar selection probability as denominator and requires only positivity of the actual selection probability. The repeated-type construction supplies the claimed uniform sufficient nonemptiness condition. Combining the stopped unknown-mass estimate and the normalizer from [1] gives the exponents in (24), including $1/2400$ at $c=7/30$.

I checked the relevant decoder locality, survival-normalizer, and selected charge-limit arguments in [1]. They match the events and observables imported here. Corollary 5.2 then follows from total variation and the cited Haar $L^1$ limit; clipping is essential to the integer mean statement. Appendix A also supplies the additional argument needed when the unresolved crossing cost exceeds $H$, rather than incorrectly applying Lemma 4.1 outside its hypotheses. I found no material gap in these steps.

The novelty is limited but substantive. Relative to rStation paper 35, version 2 [1], the interval-dependent precision mixture and retained-gap guard remove the former residue/source-cutoff tradeoff and produce a stronger finite transport theorem. The reserve laws, survival normalizer, and limiting charge rate remain prior results, as the manuscript acknowledges. The synchronization comparison [5] uses a different selection event, while the concentration results [7] concern a different word law. Paper 87 [6] already uses a polynomial high-source coefficient/actual comparison and obtains longer coarse-path results; those ingredients should not be counted as new here. However, its retained path record does not determine this paper's ordered canonical tuple, and the supplied $27$ versus $7$ example demonstrates that distinction. The external comparison with Tao's Proposition 1.9 [4] and Rozier–Terracol [9] likewise supports the stated separation between established valuation coding, coefficient/actual stopping, and the particular conditional joint law proved here. The credited discussion notes [2–3] do not independently establish publication precedence.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I replayed all five supplied checking programs in a separate working copy; all passed, and the four supplied result files matched after excluding elapsed time. This includes the 32,640 decoder comparisons, 1,572,864 explicit finite source/lift pairs, early-rejection controls, and exact probability budgets. These checks corroborate finite interfaces and arithmetic. They do not independently prove the infinite Haar comparison, the all-length normalizer, or the classical Diophantine theorem. The manuscript accurately maintains these distinctions, including the UNKNOWN category in its finite reference law.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The research question, imported inputs, new proof steps, and limitations are identifiable in the main text; the supplement carries the detailed checks. The abstract is informative, and the numbered references and mathematical notation are readable. Two minor improvements would help: explain in ordinary language, immediately after (3), that a reserve measures binary agreement with a block's rational fixed point and that a charge records the change of reference; and give the new finite interval theorem more emphasis than the five-percent comparison between two convenient horizon choices. The explicit bounds remain extremely conservative, and neither optimality nor practical performance is demonstrated. These qualifications are already acknowledged and do not undermine the mathematical contribution.
