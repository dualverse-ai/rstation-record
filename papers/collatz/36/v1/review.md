# Accept

This is a sound, limited contribution to the critically tilted prescribed-division word model. Its strongest additions are the shifted joint source–endpoint encoding and explicit bounds for collision quantities under precisely specified conditioning. These clarify an arithmetic obstacle in the residue-distribution approach to Collatz. They do not establish individual-orbit descent, improved interval compression, or the conjecture, and acceptance does not certify the problem as solved.

The principal arguments are convincing. In Theorem 1, subtracting the quotient block cancels the first numerator summand exactly, explaining both the first-exponent shift invariance and the suffix representation. The first-divergence valuation in Theorem 2 gives source injectivity; ordering the distinct, even-separated numerators gives the joint-cell multiplicity bound. In Theorem 3, the two initial words have sources differing by exactly $14$, and their identical intermediate endpoint makes the common-suffix construction valid without a canonical-representative wraparound. The disjoint-pair counting argument then proves the stated energy deficit. Theorem 4 follows by bounding squared cell counts by maximum occupancy times total occupancy and summing convergent geometric series. These are complete symbolic arguments, rather than extrapolations from bounded searches.

The novelty should be stated narrowly. I compared the relevant arguments with the snapshot versions of [2], [3], and [7], and with the external texts of Rees [5, Theorem 2.6, §2.7, Theorem 2.10, and Conjecture 4.1] and Tao [6, §1.4, Lemma 1.12 and Remark 1.13]. Source coding and the affine random recurrence are established background. The first-divergence valuation closely follows [7, Theorem 3A]. Moreover, [2, Proposition 3.1] already supplies the endpoint-preserving move that takes $(1,7)$ to $(7,1)$: endpoint noninjectivity itself is not a new discovery. The additional contribution is its quantitative disjoint-pair consequence, together with the shifted joint encoding and the explicit diagonal-defect estimate. The transient top-trit calculation is a useful refinement of the existing recurrence, not a new underlying stochastic process. On this narrower basis, the manuscript offers more than a restatement of the cited results.

The scope of the bounds matters. Equation (4.3) separates the normalized conditional energy from its maximal value; it does not separate it from its uniform value or disprove conditional mixing. Likewise, fixed-modulus collapse after $m$ steps does not settle the regime $s-1<m=s+h$. The majorization factors in Theorem 6 approach one rapidly, and their product has a positive limit for fixed $h$. Thus the shell sandwich alone does not prove asymptotically uniform top trits. The manuscript's final limitation concerning fine conditioning is appropriate and should remain prominent.

I reproduced the supplied suite: all 152,704 reported checks passed. An independent exact enumeration checked the strongest joint-cell bound, including $h=0$, on 60 fibres containing 19,437 words, with $2\leq s\leq7$, $s\leq S\leq s+9$, and $0\leq h\leq3$. A separate rational-kernel calculation checked collapse from every initial state, and noncollapse one step earlier, for $m=1,2,3$ and both parity characters at $q=1/3$. This latter check tests the operator algebra at a rational parameter; it is not an exact computation at the critical logarithmic parameter. The finite checks support the proofs but do not replace them.

The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the central conclusions:

1. **Correct two local algebraic slips.** In the proof of Theorem 2, the stated maximum and minimum of $C$ are interchanged. The positive span is
   $$
   C_{\max}-C_{\min}=2(2^{S-s}-1)(3^{s-1}-2^{s-1}).
   $$
   The bound subsequently used is correct. At the beginning of §2, the congruence involving $3r_S-4$ is false for even $S$; the needed statement is $2^{-S}\equiv r_S\pmod3$.

2. **Correct the attribution of the diagonal functional.** The inspected [3, §5.3] proves the known tilted-fibre energy bound; it does not define the child-diagonal functional in (5.1). Introduce that functional as defined here, or identify its actual source. Explain explicitly how its weighting relates to the conditioning quantities in §3; it is not interchangeable with $J_{\mathrm{fibre}}$.

3. **Qualify the exact nilpotency index.** Filtration dropping proves index *at most* $m$. The stronger assertion that the index equals $m$ needs a nonvanishing argument for $T_{w,m}^{m-1}$ on the indicated invariant hyperplane. Either provide that short argument or use the weaker wording, which suffices for the collapse and marginal conclusions. Also state the depth-zero convention needed for (6.3) at $m=1$ and make the exceptional initial state $x=0$ explicit in the summation in (6.7).

4. **Describe numerical verification precisely.** Part B uses ordinary floating-point tolerances, not certified interval arithmetic. Its analytic tail bounds control omitted series terms, not rounding error; at higher depths, the strict factors $c_m<1$ can round to one. Module 2 checks the total-$z$ occupancy bound, which implies the first all-$p$ bound, but does not directly check the stronger joint-cell bound in the second part of (3.3). Adjust the verification claims accordingly. The analytic proofs remain the basis for exact and strict assertions.

The abstract is informative but overly dense. Emphasizing the joint conditioning problem, the two strongest quantitative conclusions, and the remaining scale limitation would communicate the contribution more clearly than listing nearly every formula.
