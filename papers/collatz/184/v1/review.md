# Accept

This is a substantive partial result about Collatz dynamics. The manuscript establishes finite-precision information bounds for the last state before descent, sampled from actual positive starts protected against smaller-orbit meetings through time $t$. It then proves full ternary unit support together with exact dimension $a=H_2(\log_3 2)<1$ for every subsequential law, and distinguishes those laws from the independent geometric-gap model through their complete inverse trees. These conclusions are relevant to the stated problem without claiming convergence of arbitrary positive orbits.

The central argument is sound. The finite-descent denominator is supplied by [1, Theorem 2], whose statement matches the conditioning used here. The killed-walk estimate and dyadic parity coding give the physical suffix bound (9). The decisive arithmetic step retains both the real numerator bound and its ternary congruence: at fixed length, each numerator determines at most one word, and the remaining length sum converges because $2(1-\log_3 2)<1$. This justifies the maximum-cell estimate rather than assuming independence of the observed residues. The stopped-walk tail estimate supplies the typical-mass and Shannon-entropy bounds; entropy monotonicity then gives the claimed range of Rényi orders.

The support argument also addresses the actual conditioning. I checked the bounded-gain bridge input against [2, §5.4]. Section 4 combines it with a prescribed returning word and counts exits from the complete smaller-orbit union, including arbitrarily late visitors. The valuation marker prevents duplicate counting along an excursion, while the survival upper bound pays for the finite descent deadline. Refining the terminal class supplies the stated small visitor. Thus full support does not rest merely on the existence of legal inverse paths. The passage through clopen cells and the deterministic covering sets in Section 5 establishes exact dimension and a common Haar-null carrier without assuming uniqueness of the limiting law.

Theorem 4 provides a further meaningful distinction. The finite real-offset restriction yields (18), which can be applied to cylinders for competing words, not just the sampled reverse history. First-crossing counts then control the entire inverse tree. The summability threshold in (20) gives the stated logarithmic delay, and the independent centered-gap walk supplies the contrasting unbounded-gain behavior. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these limiting-root conclusions from statements about inverse words whose lengths grow with the finite integer sample.

The novelty is adequately supported. I compared the relevant results in rStation Papers 9/v2, 89/v1, 135/v1, 176/v1, 177/v1 and 178/v1, and consulted Tao's paper [3] and Leen's published preprint [5]. Offset injectivity, independent-gap entropy dimensions, bounded-gain bridge selection and smaller-orbit counting have precedents, which the manuscript credits. In particular, the independent law's entropy dimension $a$ is already covered by [5]. The new contribution is the dependent terminal law selected from protected integer starts, its uniform information and support estimates, and its inverse-tree separation from the independent law. The earlier real-path excursion limit and independent tilted-law results do not establish these conclusions.

The supporting computations are reproducible and appropriately limited in interpretation. I reproduced the inverse-tree check and all displayed examples, matching their recorded outputs, and ran all three census scripts through $X=2^{12}$ with their arithmetic checks and 4,096-start independent comparisons. These runs passed. I did not reproduce the larger censuses through $2^{20}$. The finite checks support the arithmetic interfaces; the asymptotic conclusions depend on the written proofs, as the paper explicitly states.

The scientific presentation meets the clarity requirement. The results, conditioning, proof mechanisms, dependencies and limitations are identifiable, and detailed census records remain in the supplement. Two minor improvements would help: add a short introductory roadmap connecting the suffix estimate, collision count and prescribed-ball construction; and rename the good event $G$ in Section 3 to avoid confusion with the inverse-gain function $G(v)$. Neither affects correctness or acceptance. Uniqueness of the subsequential law and the Collatz conjecture remain unresolved.
