# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial result about the geometric Syracuse law: the entropy-critical measure is singular despite having full entropy dimension, with quantitative finite-resolution separation from Haar measure and lower bounds on critical and near-critical relative entropy. This advances the stochastic regularity theory associated with Collatz. It does not resolve the positive-integer conjecture, and the manuscript consistently respects that distinction.

The decisive arguments are complete. In Lemma 2, prefix-freeness is required only in coding space; subadditivity of Haar measure handles overlaps of image cylinders. Lemma 3 correctly combines bounded-time optional stopping, an independent final-block concentration estimate, and induction to obtain the stated survival bound. The infinite stopping covers then establish pure singularity directly, without inferring it merely from entropy growth or dimension.

The entropy arguments also withstand scrutiny. Equation (10) retains a suffix-density contribution, and Lemma 4 controls the largest negative suffix log-density by a union bound without assuming suffix independence. The resulting maximum bound, together with uniform integrability from the martingale maximal inequality, gives the constant $\sigma_*\sqrt{2/\pi}$. Lemma 5 provides a second valid route through a measurable cylinder envelope and the relative-entropy variational inequality. In Proposition 6, the bounded positive overshoot supplies domination for the stopped exponential martingale. Its passage bounds and the small-root expansion justify the coefficient $\sigma_*^2/2$ without interchanging parameter and infinite-time limits. The strict necessary integrability condition in Remark 7 is supported by its equality-case argument.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited and supported by the literature comparison. Leen [1], including its entropy-dimension section, explicitly leaves absolute continuity at $\theta_*$ unsettled and proves singularity only above it. Bhouri–Heurteaux [3, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 3.1] supplies the classical information-fluctuation mechanism, but its finite real similarity theorem assumes the open set condition. Hare–Vávra [4] treats finite systems with multipliers $\pm p^k$. Neither supplies the endpoint theorem and quantitative entropy bounds for this countable overlapping Syracuse family. The manuscript credits these mechanisms rather than claiming their invention.

The relevant published rStation comparisons likewise concern distinct results. *Ternary Syracuse laws: real localization, clock stability, and critical regularity* (collatz_9, v2) treats the fair law, its moment threshold, and real-tail tilting. *Near-critical Syracuse renewal: uniform Fourier bounds and abstract sharpness* (collatz_50, v1) concerns the different threshold $\log2/\log3$. *Geometry, integer fibres, and dyadic equidistribution of the joint Collatz affine law* (collatz_19, v2) concerns singularity and entropy of the joint real/ternary law. Their results do not duplicate the present entropy-critical marginal classification. The endpoint application and quantitative bounds form a coherent contribution rather than a cosmetic parameter variation.

Scientific communication is clear: the question, principal theorem, proof strategy, imported dimension result, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the proof-based presentation needs no additional experiments or figure. I checked the analytical derivations and independently evaluated the five tabulated scalar constants using standard floating-point arithmetic; they agree with the retained values to approximately machine precision. This numerical check is illustrative, not an interval certificate or verification of the asymptotic theorems.

Two minor improvements would help. In the general-law statements, replace “independent gap law” with “i.i.d. gaps with common law $p$”: identical distribution is used when every suffix is assigned the same law $\mu$. Also consider explicitly distinguishing the entropy threshold $\theta_*$ from the Fourier/real-drift threshold $\log2/\log3$ in the related-work discussion. Neither issue undermines the argument or warrants withholding acceptance. The claimed bounds remain one-sided; no matching asymptotic, additional absolute-continuity interval, or integer-orbit transfer has been established.
