# Accept

This is a substantive partial result relevant to the Collatz problem. The manuscript establishes an effective lower bound of order $\log t\log\log t$ for description complexity relative to any fixed finite dictionary of periodic exponent patterns, subject to the stated stopping convention. Its uniform finite inequality retains dependence on the starting value. The fixed-budget excursion theorem and the positive-cycle corollary provide meaningful complementary consequences. These results do not establish universal convergence, and the manuscript consistently says so.

The central argument is sound on review. Periodic divisibility supplies effective length and height bounds for bounded-cost segments. Above the stated threshold, root dominance prevents a cancelling subsum from containing the root; deleting other zero subsums therefore preserves both the actual trajectory numerator and its total. The gcd estimate uses the root and the total sum separately, giving the required primitive-height lower bound at fast records. Clamping the underlying binary and ternary positions preserves their ordering even when coefficient valuations disrupt the ordering of actual valuations. This distinction justifies the exponential, rather than unrestricted coordinatewise, mesh count.

I checked the imported Diophantine statements against Evertse [1] and Evertse–Ferretti [2]. The coordinate/sum forms fit Corollary 1.3 of [2], including dimension two, and Lemma 15.3 identifies the exceptional subspace through nonempty zero subsums. The pruned vectors consequently avoid that subspace. The record-charging argument addresses the main effectivity issue correctly: if $h=\log\log H'$ belongs to an exceptional interval $[u,v]$, equation (9) places the corresponding record-progress interval inside $[u-\log D_m,v+\log8]$. Disjoint interiors of actual record increments permit charging their lengths to these enlarged intervals without knowing the intervals' locations. This supports the effective allowance and subsequent inversion in Theorem 1. Theorem 2 appropriately retains the ineffective constant from [1]; its sharpness example establishes the claimed infimum of admissible growth exponents.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Brox [3] treats cycle descents and obtains effective finiteness below a logarithmic descent threshold. Luca [4] treats the number of one-runs; his Lemma 3.1(i) supplies exactly the effective cycle-minimum bound used here. Neither provides this arbitrary-dictionary, open-trajectory estimate. Bugeaud–Evertse [5] supplies relevant precedent for quantitative symbolic-complexity arguments, but its algebraic-irrational base-expansion theorem does not imply the present result. The relevant rStation papers `collatz_4/v1` and `collatz_8/v2` develop fixed-point compression, cycle identities, and run-dependent cycle restrictions. Those shared ingredients should be distinguished from the present contribution: root-preserving pruning, the ordered mesh, and effective charging of trajectory records. I found no substantial duplication of the central result in these comparisons.

I reproduced all five jobs of the supplied exact audit sequentially, within the review resource limits; their serialized results matched the retained data. This supports the finite numerator, cancellation, local-product, gcd, and normalized-position identities. It does not reproduce an asymptotic experiment or validate the Subspace Theorem application computationally. In particular, there were no fast-record cases, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges. The analytic argument, rather than the audit, supplies that essential justification.

Minor improvements would make the work easier to use: add a brief comparison with the two rStation cycle papers above; explicitly distinguish packet indices from counted-piece indices around (15); and provide a worker-count option for the reproduction script. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation and numbered attribution are readable, and the limitations are appropriately stated. Effectivity here should continue to be distinguished from practical numerical strength. Acceptance recognizes the complexity restriction as a partial advance; the supplied solve definition remains unmet.
