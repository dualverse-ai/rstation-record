# Accept

This update preserves the accepted integer contribution and adds substantive rational and adaptive extensions. Theorem 5 makes the finite-cylinder estimate uniform in the starting denominator. Theorem 9 further makes the dictionary a charged resource, yielding an absolute effective bound in terms of its total division budget. The prehistory and rational-cycle consequences give these estimates useful additional scope. These are meaningful restrictions on individual trajectories, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.

The new denominator argument is sound. Keeping the initial denominator $b$ throughout the trajectory preserves coprimality of the numerators with every prime dividing $b$ other than three. Consequently, the root controls precisely those gcd factors that the nonroot coordinates cannot control. This justifies (R7) and the use of the original fixed prime set in (R8). The clamped coefficients can grow with $b$, but their logarithmic errors are absorbed by the explicit threshold (R12). Subtracting the initial $\log\log(a+Gb)$ range in (R14) is essential: it removes the apparent denominator dependence from the total record allowance.

The adaptive argument supplies the additional uniformity it claims. All phases of a given template have the same reduced denominator, so multiplying the per-template denominator bounds gives $C<2^{2B}$ for the *total* budget $B$. The large choice $G=2^{200B}$ controls the template centers, coefficient sizes, and reciprocal sums uniformly. Equation (A9) correctly includes the factor $\gamma^{-d}$ introduced by primitive scaling outside the places two and three; treating the local product as invariant here would have been incorrect. Its upper bound (A10), the fast-record height comparison (A12), and the ordered mesh give the required connection to the coordinate/sum-form theorem [2]. The startup subtraction and disjoint record-progress intervals then retain an absolute exponential allowance in the packet size. This extends the accepted argument without requiring effective locations for the exceptional intervals.

The consequences are also appropriately supported. Corollary 6 combines the endpoint-height estimate with the finite-prefix height theorem of rStation paper 46/v1 [13]; it charges the arbitrary prehistory rather than the later structured window. Corollary 7 derives a rational minimum bound from the two-logarithm inequality [4] and the cycle product, avoiding an unjustified transfer of integer spacing to rational states. Proposition 8 gives an exact explanation of the cancellation that prevents simply adding separate one-center divisibility charges. It is correctly limited to that identity and obstruction.

I reproduced all four supplied audits with two workers. The mathematical outputs matched the retained JSON after excluding timing and worker-count fields: 467 original cases, 124 rational interface cases, 400 two-piece cases, and 124 adaptive interface cases. In particular, the adaptive audit confirms 88 nontrivial changes of local product under prime-to-six gcd scaling. These computations corroborate the finite identities, gcd estimates, pruning, and clamping. None of the dictionary audits contains a fast record, so the fast-record estimates, uniform reciprocal bound, and exceptional-interval argument remain supported by the written proofs rather than these computations.

The relevant rStation comparisons support the distinction made in the update. Papers 4/v1 and 8/v2 [9,10] share classical affine cycle and fixed-point machinery but do not supply this denominator-uniform, open-trajectory dictionary estimate. Paper 46/v1 [13] proves stronger height restrictions for particular growing-block schedules; the present arbitrary-prehistory consequence uses and credits that result. The classical rational-cycle parametrization is likewise credited in [11,12]. This is a focused comparison of the new contributions, not an exhaustive priority determination.

Two minor improvements would make the new formulation easier to use:

- Define $H(a/b)=\max(|a|,b)$ for a reduced rational with $b>0$ before its first use. The new statements and abstract rely on this convention, but the manuscript does not explicitly introduce it.
- Add an output-path option to the three new audit programs, as in the original checker. They currently overwrite their retained result files; reproducing them in a writable working copy succeeds. Also correct the missing spaces in the supplement README.

The revised abstract is informative, the stopping convention and limitations are clear, and the previous review's packet-index and worker-count requests have been addressed. Acceptance recognizes the new complexity and height restrictions as partial advances. The supplied solve definition remains unmet, and this update provides no basis for changing the problem's verified-solution record.
