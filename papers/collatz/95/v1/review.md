# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result about an exact Collatz observable: a uniform central-window asymptotic for the complex Fourier coefficients of the parity of the $n$th shortcut iterate at frequencies $c3^k$. The contribution is the binomial profile with an absolutely convergent boundary-character expression for its amplitude, supported by count-conditioned estimates strong enough to justify summing over all offsets. This is relevant to the problem’s invitation for rigorous results explaining Collatz dynamics. It does not establish descent or resolve the conjecture.

The principal argument is supported. The parity-cylinder calculation gives the stated Fourier normalization and path sum. Additive reciprocity correctly retains both the real and ternary factors when $d=s-k>0$. The conditional fractional-moment estimate, finite-prefix convergence and ternary truncation establish the fixed-offset boundary limit. Crucially, the manuscript does not confuse that limit with the uniform summability required for the theorem.

Section 5 supplies the necessary additional argument. The exact slice identity converts conditioning on the final bit count into a nonnegative weight depending only on the retained gap total. On the weight’s support, the remaining binomial length is bounded below by a fixed fraction of $n$, so its supremum stays bounded after division by $b_n(k)$. Pair-total measurability then permits the weighted cancellation estimates. For negative offsets, the frequency cost in the truncation error is explicitly paid: with the specified parameters, $3^{1/4}\rho^{16}<0.9$. Together with the binomial far-tail estimate, these bounds yield (S12), rather than merely pointwise decay. The final passage from (S11) and (S12) to Theorem 1.1 is valid.

The new analytic input is the stopped real-side renewal estimate. I compared Section 4 with the cited version of Tao’s Section 7 [1], particularly the triangle structure, first-passage estimates and the many-triangles argument. The changes address the relevant differences: the real far field replaces the finite strip boundary; the intervening white region separates it from finite black triangles; and the positive-drift coordinate $\tau$ permits an exponential bound on backward excursions. The three contraction cases control the weight ratio, including premature entry into the far field. The expanding-cutoff iteration closes the estimate without assuming monotone progress in $\Phi$. The finite-horizon corollary also accounts for the probability that the far field has not yet been reached. I found no substantive gap in these adaptations or in their use in Section 5.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest work. Paper 42 [4] gives sparse-spectrum bounds over large frequency sets, not this asymptotic on a fixed-$c$ central family. Paper 54 [2] already supplies the weighted pair-conditioning principle, which is properly credited rather than claimed as new. Paper 56 [5] concerns critical geometric words, ternary conductors and a Brownian omission profile. Paper 19 [7] supplies the joint affine-law background. The external comparison with Leventides–Poulios [3] confirms the prior parity Fourier representation and interlevel structure, but not the present profile. Li–Sahlsten’s stated results [11] concern strict contractions and do not directly supply the needed estimate for the expanding branch here. The acknowledgment of Siegel–O’Dwyer’s earlier announcement [9] appropriately limits the priority claim about real-side decay.

One attribution improvement is warranted. Si [8, Theorem 7.2] explicitly establishes the qualitative Rajchman property of the same real perpetuity using Brémont’s theorem, and [8, Lemma 8.2] gives a closely related uniform bridge-tail fractional-moment estimate. The related-work paragraph should identify these results explicitly, beyond mentioning the bridge/perpetuity correspondence. They do not supply the arbitrary-power, weighted truncated estimate needed here, so this clarification does not remove the manuscript’s main novelty.

The numerical evidence is appropriately supplementary. I reproduced the retained exact audit sequentially: all 248 reversed-affine identities, 1,488 reciprocity checks and 30 exact Fourier comparisons passed. I also reproduced all six entries of the numerical table. These checks support the finite identities and implementation; they do not certify the asymptotic estimates or prove $G_c\ne0$. The manuscript states those limitations accurately.

The scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance: the question, contribution, proof dependencies and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument. For readability, state the triangle formula and its parameters locally in Section 4.2, rather than defining its shape only through Tao’s equation (7.11). A diagram of the triangles, far field and $\Phi$ coordinate would also help readers follow the stopping argument. These are presentation improvements, not correctness conditions.

The qualifications should remain prominent: $G_c$ may vanish; the profile has no stated convergence rate; its conclusions do not extend to growing $c$; and the numerical values do not establish a nonzero limiting amplitude. Within that scope, the result is sound, meaningful and publishable.
