# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The strongest results are the exact additive-energy description of two-generator affine shells and the cycle restrictions that remain uniform when the number of contracting stretches is unbounded. Neither result resolves the conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately leaves unrestricted expanding complexity and conditioned dispersion open.

The shell argument is convincing. The unique least $2$-adic valuation in the Young-diagram difference establishes injectivity; excursion swaps preserve the affine pair sum; and the same-ray reduction to rod-start multiplicities uses a valid no-carry argument modulo $3^a$. The six-mask transfer matrix is consistent with the hard-rod constraint. Appendix B supplies the important passage from a tilted total-count estimate to four individually balanced counts, followed by padding to arbitrary sequences with $m/L\to\rho$. Thus the strict growth hierarchy is supported by an asymptotic argument, rather than extrapolated from the finite tables. The off-ray examples provide a useful, precisely bounded failure of recombination exactness; the third example is correctly reported as locating the first exception in $[13,18]$.

The cycle argument also supports the stated conclusions. Contracting integer blocks strictly descend, so their intervening segments do not multiply the height envelope. For unbounded contracting complexity, the height-dependent cap on the odd-run length supplies a positive lower bound on logarithmic contraction per odd step. Combining this with the cycle-product error, upper-semiconvergent ranges, and the Rhin tail yields effective finiteness at fixed expanding-stretch count. The numerical exclusions distinguish alternating order, bounded counts in arbitrary order, and unrestricted contracting counts; these hypotheses should remain explicit whenever the bounds are quoted.

The novelty is sufficient. Paper 142 v1 [6] already supplies the fixed-point roof, descent, height factor, and bounded-polymer exclusion, and these are now credited. Paper 57 v1 [11] already distinguishes expanding factors in a height chain, but its bounds retain block-length and total-stretch resources. The present removal of a contracting-count bound is a meaningful extension. The dictionary result [12] charges different resources. The adjacent-swap results in Paper 193 v1 [3] do not supply the present shell-energy hierarchy or exact hard-rod model. The rational controls from [4] and [5] are supporting illustrations, not independent novelty. A focused external literature check found no substantial duplication of the central shell results; the classical cycle results concern different complexity parameters.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I ran the full verifier within the probe limits: its generated certificate matched the submitted certificate byte for byte. The checker passed against the actual manuscript. An independently written pair-sum and prefix-class enumeration also reproduced $61{,}514=61{,}506+8$ and $1{,}042{,}874=1{,}042{,}866+8$. These checks support the finite claims; the infinite conclusions depend on the supplied proofs. I checked the stated Rhin inequality in [17], Hercher's Corollary 29 in [9], and the published $2^{71}$ verification claim in [14]. I did not reproduce those external large computations.

The main argument is readable despite the dense theorem titles. The figures usefully distinguish asymptotic growth from finite-size behavior, and the numerical values remain accessible. Three minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Explain the mathematical content of “fermionization” and “metabelian” briefly, or simplify those labels. The explicit shell and path definitions already communicate the mechanism well.
- Expand the comparison with [11] to acknowledge its expanding-only amplification factor explicitly, then emphasize the new uniformity in block lengths and contracting complexity.
- Include the finite truncation argument behind `minimum_crt` in an appendix. The code supplies bounds using $8^t-5$ and $9^t-5$, but Section 5 should explain why enumerating an alphabet with unrestricted $t$ gives a certified global minimum.

These are exposition improvements, not defects in the central results.
