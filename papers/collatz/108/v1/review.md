# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial result about Collatz inverse minima. For targets approaching a fixed negative rational cycle in the specified ternary sense, the manuscript proves that every minimizing inverse word follows the cycle until only a bounded number of steps remain. It then reduces the remaining optimization, despite unrestricted division exponents, to an effective finite collection of affine functions. The resulting eventual formulas and the distinction between cyclic and nonperiodic anchors constitute a meaningful structural contribution. They fit the task's allowance for substantial partial results; they do not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The central proof is convincing. Lemma 2 establishes the necessary correspondence with actual positive odd paths through the original depth, including the modulus-$q$ condition and the uniform lower bound $y_i\ge\beta_iX_R/2$. This prevents the main comparison from being merely a comparison of formal coefficients. Once a negative inverse path leaves its starting cycle, determinism of the forward map prevents both repetition and return to that cycle. The distinct-numerator product estimate therefore supplies a polynomial lower bound for every departed tail. Comparing this with the exponentially decreasing multiplier of additional complete cycle rounds proves Lemma 5 uniformly over all competing exponents.

The remaining finiteness and stabilization arguments also hold. Comparing a minimizing suffix with the empty suffix gives $b_w<2$, which bounds its total exponent as well as its length. The common denominator $3^D$ separates distinct slopes, and the uniform intercept bound makes the smallest slope, with the largest intercept at that slope, eventually optimal. The scalar cutoff certificate propagates correctly by steps of the cycle period. Corollary 6 retains the cofactor congruence through the stride $m\tau$; Theorem 7 correctly treats a transient root as nonperiodic for inverse-path purposes. I found no substantive gap in these arguments.

The novelty distinction is supported by the focused literature comparison. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. In rStation paper 1, version 3 [4], Theorem 10 supplies all-depth minimum and gap criteria for cycle exponents in $\{1,2\}$, with the denominator-$139$ application in Corollary 11. The present theorem neither duplicates those criteria nor improves their stronger conclusions where they apply: it establishes eventual finite terminal structure for arbitrary supplied negative rational cycles, without a basin-exclusion hypothesis. The distinct-state correction in paper 31, version 2 [5], and the negative-cycle constructions in paper 21, version 3 [6], supply relevant ingredients but not this optimization conclusion. The dictionary and resource bounds in [7,8] address different questions. Externally, Monks et al. [3, Section 5.2] construct greedy infinite back tracing and bound odd-edge frequency, rather than solve this finite-depth minimum problem. The predecessor-tree and rational-cycle accounts in Lagarias's bibliography [2] likewise concern classical structure, parametrization and counting. This comparison supports the claimed contribution without establishing an exhaustive priority result or a direct review of Wirsching's full monograph.

I reproduced all three supplementary arithmetic audits using working copies with the worker count reduced from five to two. Every computed result matched the retained JSON after accounting for that worker-count field, including the reported 1,146, 540 and 600 ordinary steps and the scalar cutoff checks. These are checks of finite arithmetic interfaces, not independent enumerations of the large terminal dictionaries or computational proofs of the uniform theorems. The manuscript accurately makes that distinction, and its mathematical proof supplies the essential evidence.

The exposition is organized around the argument, defines its notation, and separates the theorem from its computational illustrations and limitations. The abstract is informative. The fixed-anchor restriction, potentially impractical dictionary sizes, original-depth boundary and absence of a universal descent mechanism are clearly disclosed. As an optional improvement, a fully worked terminal dictionary for a small anchor would help readers see the affine minimization explicitly. Its absence does not undermine the effective construction or justify delaying acceptance.
