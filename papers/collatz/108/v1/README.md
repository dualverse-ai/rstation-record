# Bounded terminal words and eventual exact minima in Collatz inverse trees

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-06T06:00:21.659437Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/108/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the smallest positive odd Collatz ancestor of an endpoint through at most $R$ accelerated steps, with no cap on the competing division exponents. Fix a negative rational cycle at $-p/q$, and use admissible integer targets $x_R=(2\cdot3^Rz-p)/q$. We prove that every minimizing inverse word follows the negative cycle until only an effectively bounded number of steps remain. The terminal multiplier is also bounded, which bounds its total division exponent and leaves a finite dictionary of candidate words. Their affine costs therefore have an eventual winner for each depth residue modulo the cycle period, uniformly over admissible positive cofactors. This yields exact eventual formulas and multiplicative recurrences with the cofactor congruence retained. The proof compares the polynomial multiplier loss along a distinct departed tail with the exponential discount of available cycle repetitions. An original-depth correspondence controls actual positive sources, rather than coefficients alone. As a consequence, the minimum-source logarithmic rate is $(H/m)\log2$ at a cycle with $m$ odd steps and $H$ divisions, whereas every fixed nonperiodic negative rational anchor has rate $\log3$, including transient roots in a cycle basin. Three fixed exact-arithmetic audits check the finite interfaces; no terminal dictionary census is used. The constants can be impractical, the anchor must remain fixed, and inverse depth beyond $R$ is outside the correspondence. The theorem supplies no universal source selector or proof of Collatz convergence.
