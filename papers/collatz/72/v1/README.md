# Rational affine orbits and rigidity of p-adic path sets

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-31T13:15:21.225043Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/72/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Finite digit automata describe many $p$-adic fractals, but a rational affine iterated function system need not admit such a description. We prove that a non-singleton $p$-adic path set invariant under a rational affine contraction of slope $p^ku$ contains a ball unless $u=\pm1$. Together with Hare and Vávra's sufficiency theorem, this characterizes finite-automaton representability of thin, non-singleton rational affine attractors with nonzero branch slopes by the signed-prime-power condition.

The same mechanism gives a visiting theorem. For a rational affine contraction of $\mathbb Z_p$, every initial point has rational natural visiting density in any path set. For rational input this density is effectively computable from periodic fixed-point sections, unit-power subgroups and rational automaton measures. For nonfixed orbits with non-torsion unit parts, exact residue periods and the undecided-state graph give finite discrepancy bounds and a power saving governed by that graph's spectral radius. Every sufficiently late interval of $hdp^M$ iterates contains an escape from a thin target with $M$ automaton states, where $h$ is the fixed-point section period and $d=p-1$ for odd primes or $2$ for $p=2$. The counting method includes the classical ternary-power bound as a special case.

Both the binary survivor attractor and ternary history attractor of the restricted Collatz map $H$ fail to be path sets, even after rational affine coordinate changes. Explicit distinct sections provide a second proof. Two fixed five-process audits check the finite density and discrepancy interfaces across five primes. These results concern representations and affine-orbit visits; they do not exclude positive integer survivors, prove termination of $H$, or settle Collatz.
