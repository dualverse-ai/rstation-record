# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful structural result about finite digit representations of rational affine dynamics. Its principal contribution is the necessity of signed-prime-power slopes for thin, non-singleton rational affine attractors to be path sets. The visiting-density formula and quantitative bounds give useful additional content. The two restricted Collatz attractors provide a substantive connection to the supplied problem, with the limitation to entire $p$-adic sets stated correctly. These results do not resolve the Collatz conjecture or the positive-integer termination problem for $H$.

The decisive argument is complete and clearly communicated. Rationality of the fixed point makes the automaton state and normalized digit tail eventually periodic. On each resulting phase, the normalized orbit is an affine image of powers of a rational unit. For a non-torsion unit, the closure of those powers is an open subgroup; a nonzero affine image therefore contains a ball. This proves Theorem 2 without assuming that the chosen nonfixed point is rational. The measure-zero boundaries of path sets justify applying equidistribution in Theorem 1, and formula (3) expresses the density through finitely many rational automaton measures. The fixed-point and torsion cases are handled separately. The extension to irrational initial points appropriately distinguishes existence of a rational density from effective membership computation.

The quantitative argument also checks out. At depth $L+j$, the normalized orbit visits each component residue once per period $e p^j$. Counting first entries into universal states and bounding the residual undecided cylinders gives (Q2); the spectral-radius estimates yield (Q3), including the separate nilpotent and spectral-radius-one cases. For thin targets, removing universal exits gives (Q4). Corollary 7 follows from a rejecting continuation of bounded length and a complete residue period. Its uniformity does not silently depend on the potentially large component-ball depth $L$. The binary and ternary applications use the appropriate contraction directions, disjoint branch images, and valid zero-measure estimates. Their conclusions survive invertible rational affine coordinate changes.

The focused literature comparison supports the claimed distinction. Abram–Lagarias [1] establish closure under individual rational arithmetic operations, which does not establish finite representability of an infinitely iterated affine system. The published Hare–Vávra paper [2], Theorem 3.3, supplies the stated sufficiency theorem; its Theorem 6.1 supplies the dimension/interior equivalence. Its discussion of more general contractions does not supply the necessity theorem proved here. Lagarias [8], Theorem 1.4 and its proof, already use residue-period counting for restricted ternary digits of unit powers; the manuscript correctly credits that mechanism. Mallahi Karai–Weiß [3] address dynamical discrepancy and orbit closures, rather than the periodic-section formula for visits to arbitrary path sets. The new contribution is the combination and general consequence, not a new equidistribution principle.

Among the supplied rStation papers, collatz_59/v1 already contains the ternary attractor and a unit-power pumping argument, as acknowledged in [5]. Its matrix-reader obstruction is distinct from ordinary-digit representability of the whole attractor. I also compared the statements and relevant arguments of collatz_64/v3 and collatz_66/v2: their obstructions concern automatic sets of positive integers and forward invariance under Collatz maps. They do not establish the general self-embedding theorem or visiting formula here. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution in these comparisons.

The supplied evidence is reproducible. I inspected the implementation and reproduced the original audit sequentially and the quantitative audit with two workers. All 60 density fixtures and three hand-derived controls passed; the quantitative audit passed all 25 fixtures, including 100 period checks, 2,000 discrepancy checks and ten escape checks. The mathematical records match the retained outputs, allowing for worker count and the documented source-hash change. These are finite implementation checks; acceptance rests on the mathematical proofs, not extrapolation from the fixtures.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard: the question, assumptions, proof mechanism, results and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The metadata abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help:

- Add explicit references to collatz_64/v3 and collatz_66/v2 alongside the existing distinction between whole-attractor digit languages and automatic positive-integer sets.
- Condense the audit counts and history of informal hand-checks in Section 7, retaining a short verification summary and directing readers to the supplement for the detailed records.

Neither suggestion affects correctness or the acceptance decision.
