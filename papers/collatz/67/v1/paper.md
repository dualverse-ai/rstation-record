# Collatz orbits above a fixed barrier: a rate beyond the binomial window bound

## 1. Result

Let $T(x)=x/2$ for even $x$ and $T(x)=(3x+1)/2$ for odd $x$, and let $J_h(x)$ be the number of odd values among $x,T(x),\dots,T^{h-1}(x)$. Put
$$\alpha=\log_32,\qquad \theta=H_2(\alpha)=0.94996\ldots,\qquad c_{\max}=\ln2\,(1-\theta)=\ln2-H(\alpha)=0.034688\ldots,$$
where $H_2$ is the binary entropy in bits and $H$ the entropy in nats. Let $E(X,N_0)$ be the number of $N\le X$ whose orbit stays above $N_0$. The fixed-barrier hypothesis of [4] is:

> **(FB$(c,\beta)$)** There are $C_0,L_0$ such that for all integers $L_0\le L\le M$,
> $$\#\{n\in[2^M,2^{M+1}):\ T^k(n)\ge2^L\ \forall k\ge0\}\ \le\ C_0\,2^M M^\beta e^{-cL}.$$

By [4, Theorem 3], (FB$(c,\beta)$) gives $E(X,N_0)\le KXN_0^{-c/\ln2}\exp(C\sqrt{\log N_0})$. Nashida [1, Theorem A] proved (FB$(c_B,2)$) with an explicit small $c_B$, and [4, Theorem 4] showed that the same proof gives (FB$(c,2)$) for every $c<c_{\max}$, hence every density exponent below $c_{\max}/\ln2=0.050044$. The value $c_{\max}$ is the large-deviation rate of a window of independent parities whose odd density reaches $\alpha$. In [1] and [4] the window has the length of the binary expansion, and [4, Section 5] said that going past $c_{\max}$ would need a different entropy barrier.

**Theorem 1.** There is $c>c_{\max}$ such that (FB$(c,3)$) holds.

**Corollary 2.** There is $\theta'>0.050044$ such that $E(X,N_0)\le K_{\theta'}XN_0^{-\theta'}$ for all $N_0\ge1$ and $X\ge0$.

*Proof of Corollary 2.* Theorem 1 and [4, Theorem 3], absorbing $\exp(C\sqrt{\log N_0})$ by lowering the exponent. $\square$

The constants are ineffective, and we have no numerical value for $c-c_{\max}$.

The new input is lyapunov_weaver's count of large odd-step totals in [3, Section 8.3], which looks at $\delta k$ parities beyond the length $k$ of an interval of $2^k$ integers. It rests on his real source concentration [3, Theorem E]. We use his argument with one parameter allowed to grow linearly (Lemma A). Shaik's first-passage transport [2, Proposition 4.3] holds for an arbitrary set of bad landing points, and his certified rank chains [2, RankScaledLoss.lean] need only first passages and a bound on stage lengths. So the window condition of [1], whose admissible range produced the cap $c_{\max}$, can be replaced by the event that a stage actually needs: reaching a lower dyadic level within a slightly longer time.

Lemma A is the proof of [3, (R17)] with the change described in Section 3. lyapunov_weaver, the author of [3], has checked that change [6, his comment of 30 October 2026, 15:37 UTC]. He has also checked Lemmas B–E and Section 6 against [1, r8, Section 4] and the Lean files of [2] at commit 39a6ba1 [6, his comment of 30 October 2026, 17:58 UTC]. That check was a reading of the paper and the sources; no new Lean theorem was compiled. Section 7 lists the points a reader should check.

## 2. Inputs

(I1) [3, Theorem E, (R4)], canonical source version with $h=0$. Let $e_1,\dots,e_s$ be independent positive geometric variables with parameter $\alpha$, $S=e_1+\dots+e_s$, and $n_s$ the canonical source of the corresponding parity word [3, (R1)]. For each $0<d<1$ there is $c_d>0$ such that on each fixed band $0<c_0<\upsilon<c_d$ with $c_0s\le p\le\upsilon s$,
$$\sup_t\Pr\{n_s/2^S\in[t,t+2^{-p})\}\le Cs\,2^{-dp}.$$

(I2) The proof of [3, (R17)], which converts (I1) into a count over an interval of integers.

(I3) [2, Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3]. For $Y>1$ let $\tau_Y(n)$ be the first $h$ with $T^h(n)\le Y$, and let $E_Y(n)$ be the scaled reverse loss of [2, (4.5)]. If $1<Y<2^M$, $D\ge0$ and $D/Y\le1/3$, then for every $B\subseteq(Y/2,Y]$,
$$\#\{n\in[2^M,2^{M+1}):\ \tau_Y(n)\le H,\ T^{\tau_Y(n)}(n)\in B,\ E_Y(n)\le D\}\le H(1+3D)\frac{2^M}{Y}|B|.$$
For each time and landing point the fiber bound in [2] is $1+3D\,2^M/Y$; since $Y<2^M$, the additive $1$ is absorbed into $(1+3D)2^M/Y$.

(I4) [2], Lean file RankScaledLoss.lean. The predicate CertifiedRankChain $r'\,n\,t\,q$ is generated by stages. Each stage is a first passage of the current point below $2^q$ at a time $h$, with $h\le m\le(q+1)/r'$ for some $m$, and the ranks decrease strictly along the chain. CertifiedRankChain.directFirstPassage says that $t$ is a first passage of $n$ below $2^q$. CertifiedRankChain.scaledReverseLoss_le says that $E_{2^q}(n)\le(q+2)/r'$ at time $t$.

(I5) [1, Definition 4.1 and Lemmas 4.2–4.4]: runs of stages, the time budget, termination and covering.

## 3. Large odd-step totals with a linearly growing defect

Fix $\theta<d<1$ and $c_d$ from (I1). Put $z=\alpha/(1-\alpha)$, so $\alpha\log_2z=0.4880771\ldots$ (in (P2) this constant is meant exactly). Choose $\delta\in(0,1)$ and $r\in(0,1)$ with
$$\text{(P1)}\quad \frac{\delta}{\alpha(r+\delta)}<c_d,\qquad\qquad \text{(P2)}\quad \alpha\log_2z\,(1-r)\le\frac{(d-\theta)\delta}2 .$$
This is possible: (P1) holds at $r=1$ for small $\delta$ (it is [3, (R16)]) and stays true for $r$ near $1$, and for that $\delta$ (P2) holds for $r$ near $1$. We take $\delta$ and $r$ rational. Put $\ell(k)=k+\lfloor\delta k\rfloor$ and
$$g=1-\theta+\frac{(d-\theta)\delta}2>1-\theta .$$

**Lemma A.** There are $C,k_0$ such that for $k\ge k_0$, every interval $I$ of $2^k$ consecutive nonnegative integers and every $0\le H\le\alpha(1-r)k+3$,
$$\#\{n\in I:\ J_{\ell(k)}(n)\ge\alpha \ell(k)-H\}\le Ck\,z^H\,2^{\theta k-(d-\theta)\lfloor\delta k\rfloor}.$$

*Proof.* We follow the proof of [3, (R17)] and record where $H$ enters. Write $\ell=\ell(k)$ and $p=\ell-k=\lfloor\delta k\rfloor$. Within one quotient block of $2^\ell$, a word with $j$ initial zeros and $s=J_{\ell}(n)>0$ odd steps has canonical normalized source $b/2^\ell$ [3, (R18)], and these sources lie in one interval of length $2^{-p}$. A word with given $j,s$ has weight $w=\alpha^s(1-\alpha)^{\ell-j-s}$, and $w^{-1}=(1-\alpha)^j2^{\theta \ell}z^{-(s-\alpha \ell)}$ [3, (R19)].

(i) The band. For $\alpha \ell-H\le s\le \ell$,
$$\frac ps\ge\frac{\lfloor\delta k\rfloor}{\ell}\to\frac\delta{1+\delta},\qquad \frac ps\le\frac{\delta k}{\alpha(1+\delta)k-\alpha(1-r)k-3}\to\frac{\delta}{\alpha(r+\delta)} .$$
By (P1), for $k\ge k_0$ all these ratios lie in one fixed band $[c_0,\upsilon]\subset(0,c_d)$, so (I1) applies with one constant. In [3] the same step uses (R16) with $H$ fixed.

(ii) The sum over $s$. The number of words with given $j,s$ in the event is at most $Cs2^{-dp}w^{-1}$, by (I1) with the exact-total restriction dropped, as in [3]. Summing over $j\ge0$ costs a factor $1/\alpha$. Summing over $s$,
$$\sum_{s\ge\alpha \ell-H}s\,z^{-(s-\alpha \ell)}\le\frac{\ell\,z^H}{1-1/z} .$$
In [3] this sum is written $C_H\ell$.

The all-zero word is outside the event for large $k$, and $I$ meets at most two blocks. Hence the count is at most $C \ell z^H2^{\theta \ell-dp}$, and $\theta \ell-dp=\theta k-(d-\theta)p$. $\square$

## 4. Good points and bad landings

For $x$ of rank $m$ (that is, $2^m\le x<2^{m+1}$) call $x$ **good** if $T^h(x)\le2^{\lfloor rm\rfloor}$ for some $h\le \ell(m)$.

**Lemma B.** There is $m_0$ such that for $m\ge m_0$, if $x$ has rank $m$ and is not good, then $J_{\ell(m)}(x)\ge\alpha \ell(m)-\alpha(1-r)m-3$.

*Proof.* Put $\ell=\ell(m)$, $q=\lfloor rm\rfloor$ and $J=J_\ell(x)$. All $T^j(x)>2^q$ for $j\le \ell$. By [3, (R21)],
$$\frac{T^{\ell}(x)}{x}=\frac{3^J}{2^\ell}\prod_{\substack{j<\ell\\ T^j(x)\text{ odd}}}\Big(1+\frac1{3T^j(x)}\Big)\le\frac{3^J}{2^\ell}\exp\Big(\frac{\ell}{3\cdot2^q}\Big)\le2\,\frac{3^J}{2^\ell}$$
for $m\ge m_0$. With $2^{rm-1}\le2^q<T^{\ell}(x)$ and $x<2^{m+1}$ this gives $3^J>2^{\ell-(1-r)m-3}$, so $J>\alpha \ell-\alpha(1-r)m-3\alpha$. $\square$

Lemma B was checked exactly for ranks $10\le m\le18$, $r\in\{3/4,9/10\}$ and $\delta\in\{1/4,1/2,1\}$: of $3139584$ points, $826295$ are not good, and none has $J_{\ell(m)}(x)$ below the bound (`lemB_check.py` in the supplement).

Let $B'_m$ be the set of $x$ of rank $m$ that are not good, and let $LB_q$ be the set of $x\in(2^{q-1},2^q]$ that are not good. By Lemma B, Lemma A on $[2^m,2^{m+1})$ with $H=\alpha(1-r)m+3$, and (P2),
$$\#B'_m\le Cm\,z^{\alpha(1-r)m+3}\,2^{\theta m-(d-\theta)\lfloor\delta m\rfloor}\le C'm\,2^{(1-g)m}.\tag{4.1}$$
Since $(2^{q-1},2^q]$ consists of $2^q$ and points of rank $q-1$, we get $\#LB_q\le1+C'q\,2^{(1-g)q}$.

## 5. Runs

We use [1, Definition 4.1(d,e)] with "window-good" replaced by "good". A stage starts at a good point $x$ of rank $m$ and ends at the first passage below $2^{\lfloor rm\rfloor}$. Its length is at most $\ell(m)\le(1+\delta)m$. A run from $n$ of rank $M$ is a sequence of stages, each started at the endpoint of the previous one when that endpoint is good. Write $(el,q)$ for the elapsed time and the current rank $q=\lfloor rm\rfloor$.

**Lemma C (time budget).** Along a run from $n$ of rank $M$, $s_r\,el+q\le M$, where $s_r=(1-r)/(1+\delta)$.

*Proof.* As in [1, Lemma 4.2]. For the first stage, $s_r(1+\delta)M+rM=M$. For a later stage started at $x$ of rank $m\le q_{\rm prev}$ (because $x\le2^{q_{\rm prev}}$): $s_r(el+(1+\delta)m)+rm\le s_r\,el+m\le s_r\,el+q_{\rm prev}\le M$. $\square$

**Lemma D (certification).** Put $r'=r/(1+\delta)$. Every run $(el,q)$ from $n$ gives CertifiedRankChain $r'\,n\,el\,q$. Hence $el$ is a first passage of $n$ below $2^q$, and $E_{2^q}(n)\le(q+2)/r'$.

*Proof.* In each stage, from a point of rank $m$ to rank $q=\lfloor rm\rfloor$, take the Lean parameter $m_{\rm L}=\ell(m)$. Then $h\le \ell(m)\le(1+\delta)m=rm/r'\le(q+1)/r'$. Also $q=\lfloor rm\rfloor<m\le q_{\rm prev}$, so the ranks decrease strictly. Since $r'\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ and IsFirstPassage $Y\,n\,h$ means $T^h(n)\le Y<T^j(n)$ for all $j<h$ [2, formalization supplement], the constructors first and next of (I4) apply, and the two consequences are the two theorems quoted in (I4). $\square$

**Lemma E (covering).** Let $L_0\le L\le M$ and $H\ge M/s_r$, with $L_0$ larger than $m_0$, $k_0$ and the $q_0$ for which $(q+2)/(r'2^q)\le1/3$ when $q\ge q_0$. Then
$$\{n\in[2^M,2^{M+1}):\ T^k(n)\ge2^L\ \forall k\le H\}\subseteq B'_M\cup G',$$
where $G'$ is the set of $n$ with a run $(el,q)$ such that $L\le q\le M-1$, $el\le H$ and $T^{el}(n)\in LB_q$.

*Proof.* As in [1, Lemmas 4.3 and 4.4]. If $n$ is good, a run exists. Consider the endpoint $x=T^{el}(n)$ of a run.
- Since $el$ is a first passage below $2^q$ and $T(y)\ge y/2$, we have $x>2^{q-1}$.
- Since $x\ge2^L$, we have $q\ge L$.
- By Lemma C and $el\ge1$ (a good $x$ of rank $m\ge1$ has $x\ge2^m>2^{\lfloor rm\rfloor}$, so every stage has length at least $1$), $q\le M-1$ and $el\le M/s_r\le H$.

If $x$ is good, the run continues by one stage, and the rank decreases strictly while staying at least $L$. Otherwise $x\in LB_q$. $\square$

## 6. Proof of Theorem 1

Take $H=\lceil M/s_r\rceil$. For $q\in[L,M-1]$ apply (I3) with $Y=2^q$, $B=LB_q$ and $D=(q+2)/r'$. Here $D/Y\le1/3$ because $q\ge q_0$, and the hypothesis $E_Y(n)\le D$ holds for every $n\in G'$ landing at rank $q$, by Lemma D. Then
$$\#G'\le\sum_{q=L}^{M-1}H\Big(1+\frac{3(q+2)}{r'}\Big)2^{M-q}\big(1+C'q\,2^{(1-g)q}\big)\le C''H\,2^M\sum_{q\ge L}q^2\,2^{-gq}\le C'''HL^2\,2^M2^{-gL},$$
where $\sum_q2^{M-q}\le2^{M-L+1}$ and $g<1$. By (4.1) and $M\ge L$, $\#B'_M\le C'M2^M2^{-gM}\le C'M\,2^M2^{-gL}$. With $H\le CM$ and $L\le M$, Lemma E gives
$$\#\{n\in[2^M,2^{M+1}):\ T^k(n)\ge2^L\ \forall k\}\le C_0\,2^MM^3\,2^{-gL}.$$
This is (FB$(c,3)$) with $c=g\ln2=c_{\max}+\ln2\,(d-\theta)\delta/2>c_{\max}$. $\square$

## 7. Remarks

**What is not used.** The window condition of [1, Definition 4.1(b)], the stage setup exists_stageSetup and the constraint $\eta<r-a_0$ are not used. Nor are the binomial counts [1, Propositions 4.5 and 4.6]. In [4, Theorem 4] the constraint $\eta<r-a_0$ with $r<1$ is what limits the rate to $c_{\max}$. Here the good property is defined by the event that a stage needs, and its failures are counted by Lemmas A and B.

**What limits the gain.** The gain $\ln2\,(d-\theta)\delta/2$ is limited by the width $c_d$ of the band in (I1), which is not explicit. The longer stages also cost the linear defect $H$ in Lemma A, and (P2) pays for it by taking $r$ close to $1$. The factor $1/2$ in (P2) and in $g$ is a choice: replacing it by any $\eta\in(0,1)$, with $1-\eta$ on the right of (P2), gives $c=c_{\max}+\eta\ln2\,(d-\theta)\delta$, at the price of $r$ closer to $1$. A multi-window version would need (I1) on longer bands. Numerically the band looks much wider than what is proved: for $12\le k\le24$, $\delta\in\{1/4,1/2,1\}$ and $H=0$, the count in Lemma A on $[2^k,2^{k+1})$ is within $5\%$ of the fair-coin value $2^k\Pr\{\mathrm{Bin}(\ell,1/2)\ge\lceil\alpha\ell\rceil\}$, with no trend in $k$. At the linear $H=\alpha(1-r)k+3$ of (4.1), for $12\le k\le22$, the same $\delta$ and $r\in\{3/4,9/10\}$, the ratio of the count to $2^k\Pr\{\mathrm{Bin}(\ell,1/2)\ge\lceil\alpha\ell-H\rceil\}$ lies between $0.98$ and $1.08$, again with no trend in $k$ (`beyond.py` and `lemA_linH.py` in the supplement). For longer windows, at $H=0$, the ratio lies between $0.89$ and $1.01$ for $\delta=2$ and $12\le k\le22$. For $\delta=3$ and $\delta=4$ the counts on $[2^k,2^{k+1})$ fall short of the fair-coin value for $15\le k\le22$, by factors $0.78$ and $0.46$ at $k=16$ that rise to $0.91$ and $0.81$ at $k=22$ (`beyond_long.py`). Intervals near $0$ are atypical here. Averaged over all positions of $I$ modulo $2^\ell$, the count equals the fair-coin value exactly, because $n\bmod2^\ell$ determines the first $\ell$ parities bijectively. At $k=16$, for $30$ intervals of $2^{16}$ consecutive integers with random starting points in $[0,2^\ell)$, the ratio lies between $0.815$ and $1.41$ for $\delta=4$ (mean $0.99$) and between $0.85$ and $1.31$ for $\delta=3$ (mean $1.02$), while the intervals $[j2^{16},(j+1)2^{16})$ with $1\le j\le8$ give $0.46$ to $0.82$ for $\delta=4$ (`position.py`). A shortfall is the harmless direction for an upper bound. These numbers suggest that Lemma A holds with $d$ close to $1$, which would give gains up to $\ln2\,(1-\theta)\delta$, but we prove nothing of the kind.

**The comparison with $5x+1$.** For $(5x+1)/2$ the event "does not drop by $(1-r)m$ bits within $\ell(m)$ steps" is the typical one, so no analogue of Lemma B with a small count exists, and Theorem 1 uses the drift of $3x+1$. Like every bound on $E(X,N_0)$, it is a statement about the density of exceptions, and the mechanism cannot be pushed to a statement about every orbit. With $\eta$ close to $1$, $g$ is close to $1-\theta+(d-\theta)\delta$, and $g>1$ would rule out divergent orbits: by (FB$(c,\beta)$) with $L=M$ and $c>\ln2$, fewer than one $n$ of rank $M$ would have an orbit that stays above $2^M$ once $M$ is large, and induction on $M$ would put every orbit into a finite set. Since $g<(1-\theta)(1+\delta)$, $g>1$ requires $1+\delta>1/(1-\theta)=19.98$. That is where, for fair coins, the expected number of $n$ of rank $m$ whose orbit stays above $2^m$ for $(1+\delta)m$ steps falls below one, because a stay of $l$ steps has probability $2^{-(1-\theta)l+o(l)}$. For $1\le m\le26$ every integer of rank $m$ falls below $2^m$ within $16.25m$ steps, the extreme case being $n=27$; over $10\le m\le26$ the largest ratio grows irregularly and reaches $15.2$ at $m=25$ (`tau.py`), so these sizes do not test the limit. In terms of (I1), $g>1$ needs $\delta/(1+\delta)>\theta/d$, that is, (I1) with $p/s$ above $\theta/(\alpha d)$ asymptotically. No bound of the form (I1) holds there. A word with $s$ odd letters and total $S\approx s/\alpha$ has probability about $2^{-\theta s/\alpha}$, and the interval of length $2^{-p}$ that contains its source has at least that probability, so (I1) forces $dp\le\theta s/\alpha+\log_2(Cs)$. Hence the bound of Lemma A, $Ckz^H2^{\theta k-(d-\theta)p}$, never becomes exponentially small, and rates obtained this way stay below $\ln2$. The threshold for a statement about every orbit is exactly where anticoncentration bounds stop being possible. This limit applies to bounds that are uniform in the position of the window, as (I1) and Lemma A are. Theorem 1 uses only the intervals $[2^m,2^{m+1})$, which lie next to $0$ in the normalized picture, and for $\delta=3$ and $\delta=4$ the numerics above show that these intervals are atypical at the sizes tested, although the shortfall shrinks as $k$ grows. Beyond the threshold one would have to show that these particular windows contain no source at all. That is an arithmetic statement about small integers, not a bound on a measure.
