# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance: a fixed-barrier rate strictly greater than $c_{\max}=\ln 2-H(\log_3 2)$, and consequently a natural-density exceptional-set exponent strictly greater than $1-H_2(\log_3 2)$. The improvement is qualitative and ineffective, but crossing the previously identified rate barrier is substantive. The result fits the supplied problem's explicit allowance for stronger descent and exceptional-set bounds. It neither proves convergence of every orbit nor establishes a counterexample.

The decisive new step, Lemma A, is justified by the cited source estimate. I compared it with [3, Theorem E, (R4), and Section 8.3]. The estimate used here is the **unconditioned canonical-source** bound, uniform on a fixed positive band. Dropping the exact-total restriction is legitimate; it avoids imposing the asymptotically critical total condition that would not accommodate the proposed linear defect. Condition (P1) keeps every relevant $p/s$ inside one admissible band, while summing the geometric weights introduces precisely the factor $z^H$. Condition (P2) then leaves a strictly positive improvement after paying for the defect. This is a counting argument for actual integer sources, not an assumption of independent parities along selected surviving trajectories.

The remainder of the proof correctly transports that gain. Lemma B follows from the multiplicative orbit identity while the trajectory remains above its target. The time budget and decreasing-rank argument in Lemmas C and E support the stated covering. I checked the relevant declarations in Shaik's source at commit `39a6ba1`: `CertifiedRankChain` requires a first passage and a time envelope bounded by $(q+1)/r'$, with strictly decreasing threshold ranks. It does not identify that envelope with the source rank. Thus the substitution $r'=r/(1+\delta)$ in Lemma D is admissible. The arbitrary-target transport estimate also has the stated hypotheses and applies to $LB_q$. Its combination with $\#LB_q\ll 1+q2^{(1-g)q}$ gives the summable tail $\sum_{q\ge L}q^2 2^{-gq}$ and hence the claimed $M^3 2^{-gL}$ bound. Corollary 2 follows from [4, Theorem 3] by a small reduction of the exponent.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. The supplied versions of [3] and [4] contain, respectively, the concentration theorem and fixed-defect counting estimate, and the rate-to-density transfer with rates below $c_{\max}$. Neither presents the present stronger fixed-barrier conclusion. A focused external comparison with Nashida's r8 preprint [1], Shaik's v4.0.0 preprint and cited formalization [2], and Tao's almost-boundedness theorem [5] supports this distinction. The contribution is the linear-defect extension and its use with a new stage-success event, rather than a new concentration or transport theorem. Attribution to these ingredients is clear; the informal checks cited in [6] are not needed as evidence of correctness.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, quantitative gain and limitations clearly enough for publication. The numerical material is ancillary. I reproduced the Lemma B check: $3{,}139{,}584$ parameter–source cases, $826{,}295$ failures of the good-point condition, and zero violations of the lemma. Although the script compares against floating-point logarithms, an independent integer-power comparison certified all 54 thresholds used in that enumeration. I did not reproduce the other numerical surveys or compile the Lean project. These finite checks do not establish the asymptotic concentration estimate or measure the unknown improvement in the exponent; the written argument supplies those conclusions.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more accessible:

1. Define the scaled reverse loss explicitly in Section 2 and give a short mathematical description of the chain predicate alongside its Lean name. This would make the interface in Lemmas D and E easier to read without consulting the formalization.
2. Shorten Section 7 by moving the extended numerical comparisons and audit history into an organized appendix. Retain the explanation of the gain, its ineffective constants, and the limitation of uniform concentration bounds in the main text.
3. Use LaTeX delimiters consistently in the metadata abstract and distinguish the exact benchmark $1-H_2(\log_3 2)$ from its rounded decimal. The abstract is informative, but could omit the audit history and some secondary discussion of the method's ultimate limit.

These are presentation refinements, not conditions needed to support the central theorem. No numerical value of the improved exponent or effective threshold follows from this version, and the universal Collatz problem remains open.
