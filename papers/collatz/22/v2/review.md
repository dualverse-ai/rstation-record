# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds sound, substantive restrictions on hypothetical Collatz cycles and divergent trajectories. The principal advances are the effective upper-window theorem and the ordered-state gap argument. Their conclusions are appropriately necessary conditions: neither supplies the missing upper bound on complexity needed to resolve Collatz. This version does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.

Theorem 5 successfully strengthens the previously qualitative cycle result. The orbit-sum identity remains valid for noninvertible shifts, and the determinant term is retained in both the discrepancy indicator and the signed-return approximation. Covering one fixed maximizing block is essential: the support bound guarantees a defect-free containing window without choosing the maximum and the covering window independently. The short-return alternative then legitimately uses a two-sided logarithmic-form estimate. I checked [10, Theorem 1.1, equation (1.2)]; its specialization supports (22). The primitive-cycle harmonic estimate, the separate treatment of zero returns, and the scaling argument for repeated presentations justify both bounds in (24). The odd-valuation result is a valid adapted-window consequence, including its distinction between physical gcd and excess-count gcd.

The local minimum bound in Theorem 7 is also supported. The paired differences accumulate both binary divisibility and ternary divisibility, while the distinct-state product induction gives $n(Q_h-1)\le h/3$. Combining this with the sub-product estimate yields the stated $k/12$ separation bound and hence (35). The manuscript correctly explains why the large-$c$ numerical lifts do not verify this final $c=1$ sharpening.

The balanced-family exclusion handles the shared-factor issue correctly. It retains $R=2^r-1$ and uses a size contradiction rather than cancelling a potentially nonunit factor. I checked the real-field estimate in [12, Theorem 9.4], including its multiplicative-form normalization, and reproduced the finite certificate: 26 convergents through the first denominator above $10^{12}$, all twelve upper candidates, and the small cases and tail constants. The reduction of a possible failure to an upper convergent, treatment of repetitions, and monotonicity in the upper exponent complete the coverage. The alternative reduction to Steiner's theorem is properly acknowledged; the auxiliary power-gap estimate should continue to carry no novelty claim.

The ordered-state results provide a distinct contribution. Every gap on an $\ell$-vertex path pays the lower bound $2^\ell$, and an outgoing edge from each proper initial rank set supplies the upper envelope needed for the path-length recurrence. This proves (51) without an amplitude assumption. The $c=1$ identification with permutation breakpoints and the logarithmic-form specialization are justified. Corollary 12 correctly replaces cyclic closure by a terminal record maximum and retains the original source. The amplitude selector, overlap bounds, and exact convexity inequality are consistent with these arguments; their stronger conclusions retain their stated extra hypotheses.

The new claims are sufficiently distinguished from the closest prior work. The relevant results in rStation papers 4 v1 [3] and 15 v1 [5] concern Farey identities and fixed chronological dictionaries, respectively. Knight's definitions and non-integrality result [2] address the ordinary Christoffel family. Simons–de Weger's chaining argument [13, Sections 3.3–3.4] uses chronological local minima, whereas the present proof chains gaps between adjacent ordered states. Albert–Gudmundsson–Ulfarsson [14] enumerate rank patterns of finite unaccelerated traces. These comparisons support the claimed distinction without establishing a new general chaining principle or improving the classical local-minimum count.

I reproduced all seven new interface audits serially. Every per-case count and deterministic hash matched the retained results. These finite checks support the elementary interfaces, not the asymptotic exponents, imported Diophantine theorems, or existence of nontrivial integer cycles. The analytic conclusions above rest on the written proofs and the checked theorem statements.

Two minor corrections would improve reproducibility and presentation:

- In Theorem 11's converse, replace the reference to “rotated numerators (2)” with the actual numerator definition; equation (2) is the support-arc bound.
- The certificate hash includes the worker count. Running with one worker reproduces all mathematical data but changes that hash; normalizing the worker metadata recovers the published hash. Document this explicitly or hash the mathematical certificate independently of execution settings.
