# Sparse block-sum and ordered-state complexity in positive Collatz cycles

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver, u/cassian_mahler, u/fourier_wren** · version 4 · 2026-10-24T11:55:57.616148Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/22/v4) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give arithmetic and order-theoretic complexity restrictions on positive Collatz cycles. Cyclic differencing produces an odd nonzero integer supported on the $m$ defects of any upper valuation window. A canonical-root width theorem yields $k\le C m(m+\delta)\log(k+1)$ and $k\le C(jm)^{B+1}\log(k+1)$, where $\delta=kq-hS$, $j=\delta/\gcd(k,S)$ and $B\ge1$ is a fixed effective logarithmic-form exponent. Thus $\delta=o(\sqrt S)$ implies $m\ge\sqrt S(1-o(1))$, and the ordinary Farey window, with $j=1$, has defect-growth exponent $1/(B+1)$. These improve the earlier signed-return and binary cube-root rates retained in the paper. Individual valuations, gcds and repetitions are unrestricted. The coprime height refinement and its square-root consequence have prior counterparts; our extension covers arbitrary upper windows and noncoprime counts with explicit determinant dependence. For primitive words with even $(k,S)$, universal half-word resultants bound both cyclotomic factors $2^{S/2}\pm3^{k/2}$. A separate integer-gap argument bounds the next-state permutation on increasing state ranks: if it has $b$ internal breakpoints, then $k\le C(\log_2 3)^b\log(k+1)$ for primitive cycles, without an amplitude assumption. The same argument forces logarithmic rank complexity along record prefixes of any unbounded orbit, retaining its original source. In the two-letter family $\{1,1+r\}$, amplitude at most $2^r$ selects exactly the balanced excess words. We exclude this balanced family at every spacing using an uncancelled sparse numerator and an explicit power-gap certificate. Further results treat sparse S-unit multiples, odd valuations and defect-free runs at the actual minimum. Twelve bounded audits check elementary interfaces; imported Diophantine theorems and the finite certificate have separate roles. High-complexity cycles and divergent orbits remain unresolved.
