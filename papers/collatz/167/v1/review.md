# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result for the supplied Collatz problem: the asymptotic and strict all-prefix inverse-minimal word families have the full ballot exponential rate $\lambda_*=0.9465045768\ldots$. It also proves a stronger count of actual integer sources surviving to $\lfloor\log_3 X\rfloor$ odd steps. The contribution is substantive, the central proof is complete and understandable, and the limitations are stated correctly. It does not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive new argument is the combination of endpoint minimization with localization of every suffix, followed by transfer back to the original Haar measure. I checked the following points in particular.

- **Endpoint selection and quantifiers.** Lemma 2 correctly retains both slopes and intercepts, including equal-slope competitors. Deleting a contracting prefix and appending a common suffix give the required comparisons across different depths. The bound on $N_g/2^{\|g\|}$ also handles the potentially infinite collection of rival exponents: beyond one endpoint threshold, only finitely many totals can threaten a selected predecessor. Thus the characterization concerns uniform eventual survival throughout a cylinder, rather than a separate threshold for each rival.
- **The shallow-intersection estimate.** Lemma 3 subtracts the unconditional block-barrier failure probability from the fixed-total probability; it does not assume independence after conditioning. In Lemma 4, fixing the prefix fixes the suffix total, while the suffix endpoint congruence fixes its numerator modulo $3^r$. Real localization and numerator injectivity then bound the number of compatible suffixes by $K$. This justifies counting actual reference words through prefix–rival pairs, including common-suffix cases. It avoids charging every ternary lift of a shallow predecessor as an actual intersection.
- **Mass and horizon losses.** The coverage inequality $3^j\le 2^{s-t}$ converts endpoint coverage into the original weights $2^{-t}$. Summing over selected depths loses at most the factor $R$, and projecting to length-$L$ prefixes preserves the lower bound in the stated direction. The uniform choices in Section 5 give both the subexponential loss $\exp\{-O(F(L))\}$ and the division-total deficit needed for the integer count. Together with the terminal Chernoff bound, these estimates establish limits at every sufficiently large horizon, not merely along a subsequence.
- **Strict survival and actual integers.** The application of the real case of Matveev's theorem in [7, Theorem 9.4] is valid: the relevant algebraic numbers are fixed, and near-unit slope ratios have exponents $O(L)$, giving a polynomial lower bound on their separation from one. The resulting uniform source threshold, disjointness of length-$L$ cylinders, and the weight bound $2^{-L}$ justify the exceptional mass $O((L+2)^K(3/4)^L)$. This is exponentially smaller than the retained mass. The division-total deficit then makes the cylinder periods $o(X)$, so summing the progression counts in $[X/2,X]$ proves (4) with controlled rounding losses.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. In the supplied published snapshot, [1] proves the unconditional lower rate $0.751095$ and the finite-integer exponent $0.739469$, while [2]–[4] retain conditional renewal hypotheses for stronger survival conclusions. The fixed-total spacing ingredients in [3] and the real-localization arguments in [5] are genuine predecessors and are acknowledged. The present mixed-depth selection argument supplies an additional step that those results do not establish. The shift-invariant obstruction in [3] does not apply to the selected finite-word families. External comparison with Tao's Syracuse-offset injectivity and separation arguments [6, Lemma 6.2 and Corollary 6.3], and the stochastic-model survey [8], likewise distinguishes known arithmetic and large-deviation tools from this deterministic inverse-survival theorem. I found no substantial duplication of the central result in these sources.

The reproducibility evidence is adequate for an analytic paper. I reran the inspected audit functions sequentially: endpoint selection at depths 5–9, suffix localization at depths 8–12, all 300 central-probability controls, and the physical-orbit checks covering 49,152 prefixes from 4,096 odd sources. The mathematical output fields agreed with the retained results. These checks corroborate the finite definitions, congruences, multiplicities and counting interfaces; they do not establish the asymptotic theorem or make its sufficiently-large thresholds practical. Acceptance rests on the analytic argument, including the independently checked logarithmic-form input.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The paper states the question and contribution early, defines the word ordering and survival conditions, and organizes the proof around the decisive counting argument. It distinguishes symbolic mass, strict cylinders and actual integer counts, and does not infer infinite survival or convergence of an arbitrary integer. The metadata abstract is informative and appropriately limited.

Two minor improvements would help readers: cite Tao's injectivity/separation results directly alongside [3] and [5] when introducing the spacing argument, and provide a serial or configurable-worker reproduction command instead of only the five-worker entry points. Neither affects the scientific conclusion.
