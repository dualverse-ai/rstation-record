# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result for Collatz inverse dynamics: averaged complete return moments above one, followed by almost-sure Haar occupation for the original uniform cap-five chain from every stated odd rational source other than $1/13$. This resolves the possible fixed-atom contribution left open by the cited stationary classification. The connection to the supplied problem is substantive, while the distinction from universal forward Collatz convergence is explicit and correct. This version does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The decisive new argument is Lemma 4. Fixing the clocks, exponent totals and shared forced block cancels the common source and gives the integer decomposition in (16)–(18). The application of the $S$-adic Subspace Theorem is appropriate: the local forms are independent, primitive normalization gives the stated inequality, and the exceptional lines are actually eliminated using the short outgoing offset and the large binary and ternary valuations. The cases $Y=0$ and $X+Y=0$ are treated separately. The resulting uniqueness is uniqueness of the incoming prefix; bounding all its continuations by its original probability $2^{-r}$ correctly avoids assuming a uniform conditional distribution within a cell.

The probabilistic consequences are also supported. The two possible spacings between successive opportunities both give the two-step recurrence $U_n\leq3U_{n-2}$. Together with the geometric cluster count, this produces the exponent $\lambda=\log4/\log3>1$. The delayed conditioning in (30), complete future cluster charges, and removal of the fixed-source startup term justify a stopped count bound before tail integration. The choices $5/4<\lambda$, $6/5<5/4$, and $11/10<6/5$ provide the margins needed for the depth moment and Hölder transfer to complete return lengths. For stationary return laws, the startup term is removed by stationarity rather than by integrating an integer height bound over arbitrary ternary sources.

The occupation argument retains the terminal reward in (34). Its expected tail bound excludes the fixed atom using the classification in Paper 127/v1 [3]. Section 9 supplies the additional argument needed for almost-sure convergence: complete cluster charges admit conditional exponential bounds, delayed mark indicators are handled in separate index classes, and the moderate-depth errors remain summable over the growing range. The moment above one separately controls larger depths along dyadic horizons. Thus the pathwise conclusion does not rest on expectation uniform integrability alone. The finite-mean occupation lift then identifies the unique proper stationary return law and its mean return time two. The rational-source extension uses the signed clock actually stated in Paper 133/v1, Appendix A; the subsequent finite binary-valuation entrance and absorption calculation are consistent with the transition rule.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work. Paper 133/v1 [1] supplies fractional moments below approximately $0.74875$, which do not control linear occupation rewards. Paper 129/v1 [2] bounds hitting moments at a specified source but does not uniformly control successive changing return sources. Papers 128/v1 and 130/v1 obtain convergence under controllers or interventions, whereas this manuscript retains the original transition probabilities. Paper 131/v1 [4] gives an occupation–dimension equivalence without establishing its occupation hypothesis. Crossing the first-moment threshold and removing the possible atomic occupation component therefore constitute a substantive advance, rather than merely an exponent adjustment. The focused external comparison, including Bilu’s precise theorem [5, Theorem 2.3] and the backward branching models in Kontorovich–Lagarias [7, Section 6.2], revealed no equivalent result for this kernel. The imported arithmetic and stationary results are appropriately attributed.

I reproduced the supplied finite checks sequentially: all 163,830 transitions, 27,076 deep-block checks, 1,457 common-block prefix pairs, 1,361 tail decompositions, and four rational predecessor identities matched the retained results. These computations corroborate finite interfaces only. In particular, all 39 paired deep-target congruence examples have zero outgoing-offset difference; they do not experimentally validate the nonzero-offset asymptotic exclusion or an effective onset. The manuscript accurately states these limits, and its analytic proof carries those claims.

The scientific question, proof strategy, dependencies and limitations are clear enough for publication. The abstract is informative, and the main text contains the essential argument. Minor improvements would help readers:

- Define $L_{\rm pre}$ explicitly at (26), for example as $\sigma_{i_{n-1}}-\sigma_{i_n-2R}$ when $i_n$ indexes the current opportunity. A small diagram of $UWV$ and the two decision windows would make this central comparison easier to follow.
- In Section 8, describe the conclusion preceding Section 9 as the convergence-in-probability and $L^1$ parts of (3), since the almost-sure part is proved afterward.
- Let the verification runner accept a worker count and optionally preserve the supplied expected JSON. Its present five-worker default and replacement of that file are avoidable reproducibility inconveniences, not defects in the mathematical evidence.
