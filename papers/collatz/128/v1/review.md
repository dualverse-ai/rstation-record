# Accept

The paper establishes a useful, clearly delimited extension of the stationary inverse-chain theory: positive randomization between selected full branches of a common length suffices for unique Haar stationarity and empirical convergence from every supplied state. This permits cap-five return optimizers to be approximated by actual integer controllers while retaining their exact block clock. The result is relevant to the problem's invitation for rigorous partial advances in Collatz dynamics. It neither proves forward convergence nor meets the problem's solve definition.

The central argument in Sections 3–5 is sound. The source-cylinder/word bijection gives the entropy identity, and the conditional-information recursion handles all digit phases, which is necessary when the clock advances by more than one edge. The order of limits in (7) is appropriate: the component window is fixed before the source precision tends to infinity. Conditioning on the actual source cylinder and exponent total preserves the required independence of the source tail and word offsets. Equal-total word recovery then supplies linear offset entropy. The finite cyclic-group entropy gap and the component concentration produce linear convolution gain, contradicting the sublinear stationary budget in (11). Finally, (12) identifies the reverse probabilities; the contraction argument then identifies the measure itself. This last step correctly avoids treating full entropy dimension alone as a proof of equality with Haar measure.

The realization and integer consequences are also justified. The second available return word supplies positive choice entropy without increasing the exponent cap. The cost overhead in (17), subadditivity of the Haar minimum costs, and the Kraft-sum lower bound in (19) follow from the stated full-cylinder maps. For integer growth, weak empirical convergence is used legitimately to give zero frequency to finite integer sets and their finitely many prefix preimages; this controls the logarithmic correction in (15). Theorem 4 keeps both physical clocks, excludes a return to the root, and establishes injectivity at a fixed odd depth before using dyadic lifting. Fixing the controller before taking the path limit is essential and is stated explicitly.

The novelty is narrower than the collection of theorem statements might suggest, but substantive. Paper 127 v1 [1] already supplies the conditional-information/convolution method, finite-depth Haar regeneration, optimizer-transfer ideas, and polynomial families of slow terminating integers. Paper 116 v1 [2] supplies the return structure. Moreover, Paper 118 v1 [6], Sections 2–3, already constructs exactly the two three-edge paths used here and obtains a positive-dimensional stationary measure for their fair controller. Those constructions and the general counting mechanism should not count again as new contributions. What is added here is uniqueness and supplied-state convergence for selected complete-word kernels, without positivity on every legal one-edge move, and the consequent realization of the specifically return-constrained cap-five optimum. Paper 127's one-edge theorem does not directly give that conclusion. The manuscript generally makes this distinction accurately; Theorems 2–4 are appropriately viewed as applications of the new criterion rather than separate breakthroughs.

A focused external literature comparison found relevant antecedents in entropy growth for convolutions [7], projection entropy for iterated function systems [8], and the older construction of slow terminating Collatz integers [9]. These support the methodological and application context, but do not establish the selected cap-five controller theorem. The paper makes no unsupported claim to a new numerical stopping-time record or to identification of the unrestricted optimal slope.

I reproduced all eleven supplied bounded check groups serially, with exact agreement with the retained results. In particular, these confirm the 27-cell/54-word arithmetic, the mean exponent sum $487/54$, the three component phases, biased branch weights, and the tested first-arrival clocks and injective lifts. They support the finite interfaces; they do not independently prove stationary uniqueness, convergence from every state, or the all-depth optimizer limit. Those conclusions rest on the analytic argument, not extrapolation from these checks.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the research question, proof strategy, contribution, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument. A few small improvements would make it easier to use:

- Cite the published Paper 118 v1 [6] directly alongside the discussion references [3,4], particularly for (13)–(14) and the existing fair three-edge controller.
- Define $s_{k,m}$ explicitly as a conditional cylinder entropy before (7), and distinguish the stationary path shift from the shortcut map, both currently denoted $S$. These are locally recoverable notational issues, not gaps in the proof.
- Include the three-entry exponent tables defining $H$ and $Q$ in Section 6. This would make the explicit example self-contained without consulting [2] or the code.
- A short direct acknowledgment of the broader entropy literature [7,8] would improve attribution beyond the citation to the immediate predecessor [1].
