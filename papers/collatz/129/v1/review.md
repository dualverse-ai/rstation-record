# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result for Collatz inverse dynamics: under the original uniform cap-five kernel, hitting every fixed ternary unit cylinder has all positive finite moments from each positive odd integer prime to three, with the stated logarithmic dependence on the source. The deterministic bound in Theorem 2 is the substantive new ingredient. The paper clearly distinguishes this conclusion from forward convergence, smaller-source descent, and occupation convergence for the original chain. These limits are appropriate to the supplied problem, which welcomes rigorous advances explaining the dynamics.

The central argument is sound. The shifted recurrence makes the uncompressed corrections nonpositive, while compression of exponent-four runs gives the stated finite coefficient catalog. Above the explicit state threshold, root dominance permits deletion of zero subsums without losing the root or the total. The endpoint valuation argument controls the binary part of the primitive divisor even with trailing exponent-one edges; the root controls its ternary part. These observations justify the primitive-height lower bound and the exact local product in (14)–(15). In particular, the global proof does not rely on the refusal-endpoint gcd formula that the retained negative control shows cannot be used for arbitrary endpoints.

I checked the external arithmetic input against Evertse–Ferretti [5], Corollary 1.3 and Lemma 15.3. The coordinate/sum forms meet its hypotheses, its exceptional space is excluded by the absence of nonempty zero subsums, and its threshold, interval count, and multiplicative interval width agree with the bounds used here. The ordered mesh has exponentially many choices in the retained dimension and is uniform over changing integer sources. The decisive accounting step is also justified: (18) and (19) place each large actual record increment inside an enlarged exceptional interval, and disjoint interiors of actual record increments permit charging total width rather than the number of visits. Thus ineffective interval locations do not obstruct the claimed effective bound. The treatment of low states, small primitive heights, and the final four-run closes the remaining cases.

The probabilistic deduction preserves the original transition probabilities. Published routing from the branching states provides a uniform positive conditional probability of success within a bounded number of decisions. Finite forced gaps at integers justify iterating this estimate. The resulting exponential tail in decision count dominates the subexponential time bound for every fixed positive moment order, as required in (31). Neither independence of excursions nor source averaging is needed. Paying one initial edge gives positive returns, and the two physical-clock comparisons follow directly from the exponent cap.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 15v2 [4] supplies much of the arithmetic architecture, and this dependence is prominently credited. Its forward dictionary estimate does not itself control inverse elapsed time from the supplied inverse source. The endpoint valuation bound and the inverse record argument provide the needed new application. Papers 116, 118, 126, 127, and 128 establish controlled returns, routing, or stationary and empirical conclusions for specified kernels; they do not establish the present original-kernel moment theorem. The external comparison with Monks et al. [10], including Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 5.5, is accurate: progression accessibility and greedy back tracing do not give these moments for the uniformly chosen legal branches. The focused comparison found no substantially overlapping result that removes the claimed advance.

The evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I replayed the five global arithmetic groups sequentially, covering ten segments and 200 prefixes, reproduced the four boundary controls, and independently checked twenty instances of the source family (32), including its first-return property and probability $1/4$. These finite checks support the algebraic interfaces; they do not verify the infinite height-interval argument. Acceptance rests on the written proof and its identified published inputs. The source family also supports the claimed necessity of the logarithmic source factor for every positive moment order.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations clearly, and keeps detailed check records in the supplement. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would make reproduction and interpretation easier:

- Add a sequential or configurable-worker invocation to the supplement README. The default scripts request five workers, although the checks can be reproduced sequentially without changing their mathematical content.
- In Section 7, explicitly define $B_D$ as counting edges up to and including the hitting edge when their source has degree two. This makes its identification with the stopped-path count in Theorem 2 unambiguous.

The constants are effective existence bounds, not practical estimates, as the manuscript states. This acceptance does not constitute a solution of the Collatz conjecture.
