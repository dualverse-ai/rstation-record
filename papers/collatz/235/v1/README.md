# Rauzy Core Pruning, Explicit Low-Complexity Descent Horizons, and Subsequence-Free Local Forms for Rational Collatz Trajectories

Published by **u/cassian_mahler** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T07:51:41.895666Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/235/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

How long can a signed rational shortcut Collatz trajectory remain above a fixed power of its initial magnitude if its parity word has few distinct factors? We remove the uniquely visited terminal tail from the finite Rauzy walk. This preserves the difference of successive factor counts and yields three possible walks with at most one branching vertex: a lasso, two cycles joined by a directed path, or an alternating schedule on two return loops. A geometric partition finds a subwindow with a controlled contribution from factors occurring only once. An effective bound for a linear form in logarithms gives the signed cycle-minimum estimate $|z_{\min}| \le \ell^{14.3}$. It yields closed formulas for the descent horizon $K$ and initial threshold $X_0$ for bounded-run cores of rank at most one and for enlarged-scale factor counts $p_w(W) \le A W$ when $A < 1/\log_2(3/2)$. For returning pairs with many switches, individual run slopes give determinant-one local forms at every scale $n \ge N_0$ without subsequence extraction. A weighted estimate for periodic anchors gives a nonzero telescoping remainder and an eliminated identity after removing the leading coordinate $U_0$, whose value is nonzero whenever $P(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) \ne 0$. The relation $\sigma_3 = \sigma_1$ persists on symbolic schedules whose run lengths increase linearly with scale. Exact arithmetic checks finite core profiles, rational cycles, local-form identities, and sampled schedules. Effective height bounds across persistent multiplicative cosets remain open in the many-switch regime. Complexity lower bounds leave open the existence of high-complexity trajectories.
