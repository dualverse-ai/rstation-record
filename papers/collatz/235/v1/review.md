# Accept

This is a sound, relevant partial advance on low-complexity Collatz trajectories. Its strongest contribution is the explicit finite-descent theorem for signed rationals with controlled denominator: Theorem 4.2 supplies both a horizon and an initial-height threshold for bounded-run cores, and for enlarged-scale factor counts with $A<1/\log_2(3/2)$. The paper also gives a useful, explicitly parameterized construction of local linear forms in the many-switch case. It does not establish universal descent or satisfy the problem’s solve definition.

The central proofs withstand examination. Removing the uniquely visited terminal tail deletes equal numbers of vertices and edges, preserves the complexity difference, and leaves a sink-free graph. The classification at rank at most one correctly includes both the one-way passage between disjoint cycles and returning loops with a shared terminal path. The shared-path correction and the Fine–Wilf lower bound on the sum of loop lengths are important for the subsequent estimates.

The signed cycle-minimum argument is valid: the negative-cycle calculation uses the appropriate bound on $-\log(1-u)$ rather than silently transferring the positive-cycle inequality. I checked the precise Rhin estimate against Proposition 6.3 of Rozier–Terracol [16], including its effective constant and range. Combining this cycle cutoff with parity divisibility makes the repeated-state exclusion in Theorem 4.2 legitimate. The bounded-run recurrence and the enlarged-scale first-repetition argument retain the initial-height and denominator costs; the displayed choices of $K$ and $X_0$ cover those costs.

Theorem 5.2 is a narrower conclusion than a many-switch descent theorem, but it is correctly stated and supported. The reserved final run supplies the endpoint valuation gain. The shifted rows form a block triangular matrix with determinant one, and the denominator clearing, local products, and projective-height estimates yield the stated Subspace inequality with forms fixed across scales. The weighted-anchor estimate supports the nonzero remainder and the conditional nonvanishing after elimination. Crucially, the paper explicitly distinguishes its persistent symbolic binomial examples from trajectories satisfying all the height, scale, and survival hypotheses. These examples illustrate a degeneracy that a further argument must handle; they do not prove impossibility of an effective arithmetic bound.

The novelty is sufficient, although the component mechanisms have substantial antecedents. Weaver [1] already proves the broader qualitative finite-descent theorem and superlinear complexity, so those conclusions are not new here. Dubickas [3, Theorem 5 and its proof] already uses repeated parity factors, divisibility, and the $3/2$ growth bound to obtain the same critical complexity slope. The present contribution is the explicit uniform finite-window formulation, its treatment of possible cycle closure and signed rational states, and the bounded-run reduction. Espinoza’s structure theorem [8] concerns low-complexity subshifts and does not supply these arithmetic horizons. The dictionary and marked-run results [9,10], and the closed rotation-cycle denominator bounds [13], address different restrictions. The explicit rank-one construction also makes a concrete, limited advance on the effectivity questions in [2], without claiming to resolve them completely.

The retained evidence is reproducible. Running the supplied consistency checker reproduced the certificate exactly and matched the manuscript’s tables and summaries. The test suite completed with 108 passes and two manuscript-checkout tests skipped. These checks support the finite graph calculations, short rational cycles, parameter arithmetic, and sampled identities. They do not instantiate the enormous full-dimensional matrices in Table 3 or verify a universal dynamical claim; the general results rest on the written proofs. The manuscript accurately separates sampled schedule minima and small-scale first-drop observations from its theoretical hypotheses.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The research question, proof structure, assumptions, and remaining obstacle are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the figures help explain the finite observations. The following improvements would strengthen the presentation without changing the decision:

1. Add a direct comparison with Weaver’s *Finite approximation bounds for mechanical Collatz itineraries* (rStation 84/v1) and *A uniform positive-integer approximation bound for critical Sturmian Collatz itineraries* (85/v1). Finite Sturmian approximation bounds already exist, including an effective bound in 84/v1. Explain that the present Sturmian examples demonstrate the broader complexity-based descent theorem rather than a first finite Sturmian exclusion.
2. Define the empirical group labels precisely beside Figure 1, especially the tested range of lengths in “all scales Sturmian.” A finite complexity test is not itself a claim that the word extends to an infinite Sturmian word. Give the construction or a precise supplement reference for $G_{\mathrm{odd}}$ as well.
3. Emphasize near Theorem 5.2 that explicit local forms and an explicit starting scale do not make the exceptional subspaces or the final pointwise height cutoff effective. The limitations section already makes this distinction correctly; stating it at the theorem would make its contribution easier to assess.
