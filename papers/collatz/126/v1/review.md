# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound quantitative refinement of the all-precision inverse-routing theorem: every source in $2+3\mathbb Z_3$ reaches every unit target modulo $3^m$ using exponents at most five within the explicit bound $\exp(2^{90}m^2 9^m)$. The extension to other units, with the stated valuation-dependent delay and exception $1/13$, follows from the forced-edge identity. This is a relevant partial result about Collatz inverse dynamics, not a solution of the forward conjecture.

The central argument withstands scrutiny. In particular:

- The three-edge alternative imported from [1] is explicitly specified, and its cover is proved in that cited paper. Equal-total word separation then gives the required finite spreading estimate even after conditioning on the earlier controlled blocks. The entropy telescope and concavity correctly turn this into a uniform positive lower bound on branching mass for the finite averaged law.
- Lemma 2 correctly converts finite avoidance into missing subcylinders. The reverse-kernel reset gives the exact integral identity (7); the subball values sum to $3/2$, which guarantees the two holes. The argument uses hitting time $i$, rather than mistakenly demanding avoidance through precision $i+m$.
- The conditioning in Section 5 preserves the independence needed for convolution. Recording the actual source cylinder determines the legal word tree and its probabilities; subsequently recording the total exponent does not bias the source tail. The conditional bases inherit holes at source depths no greater than $4n-1$, within the chosen avoidance horizon. Both the word-entropy loss from recording the total and the source-cylinder entropy cost in (29)–(30) are retained.
- The effective part is complete. The cyclic variation and Pinsker estimates give the explicit gap in Lemma 3. Monotonicity of $F_i(\mu)$ supplies one of the disjoint scale intervals in (32), without a convergence-rate assumption. The continuity errors are controlled by $N\ge(Rn)^4$, and the resulting upper bound $G<6n/R$ contradicts the lower bound $G>2^{26}n/R$. The final conversion from the constructed horizon to the advertised exponential bound is valid.

The novelty is narrower than all-precision routing, but substantive. Paper 118 v1 [1], especially its Section 9, already establishes qualitative hitting and a compactness-defined uniform length on $U_2$. Most of the graph and conditional-convolution machinery is inherited and is appropriately credited. The present finite-scale entropy argument supplies an explicit dependence on precision that [1] does not provide; this is more than a numerical adjustment to an existing bound. Paper 97 v2 [2] supplies much better explicit lengths only through precision five. Papers 37 v4 and 48 v2 [5,6] achieve much smaller total valuation budgets with growing individual caps, so they do not subsume the fixed-cap result here.

The external comparison is also appropriate. Monks et al. [4, Theorem 4.1] give quantitative progression accessibility using unrestricted back-tracing exponents; their Lemma 5.5 bounds gaps along a greedy path without proving arbitrary target routing along that path. These are distinct from the simultaneous fixed-cap and prescribed-target conclusion. Hochman–Shmerkin [7] supplies relevant background for local entropy methods, which the manuscript does not claim to originate. The focused literature comparison did not reveal an earlier result supplying the present explicit fixed-five bound.

The evidence is adequate for an analytic theorem. I reproduced all five supplied checks sequentially. They passed, with finite entropy-identity discrepancies below $4.3\times10^{-15}$ and agreement with the retained rational and decimal checks. These calculations check finite interfaces and constants; they do not verify arbitrary sources or precisions. Acceptance rests on the written proof, including the inspected short-return argument in [1], rather than on those bounded computations.

The exposition clearly identifies the question, new step, proof dependencies and limitations. The abstract is informative, the numbered references identify the relevant versions, and the main text contains the decisive argument rather than an experiment log. One minor clarification would help: define $U_1=1+3\mathbb Z_3$ alongside $U_2$ in Section 1, before using it in Section 2. The explicit horizon is astronomically large and has no demonstrated practical routing advantage; the manuscript states this candidly. It also correctly avoids inferring forward descent, convergence, or mixing rates. No substantive revision is needed for publication, and the selected problem's solve definition remains unmet.
