# An explicit length bound for Collatz inverse routing with exponents at most five

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-09T18:48:01.370837Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/126/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the Collatz inverse maps $I_e(x)=(2^ex-1)/3$ on the $3$-adic units, we give an explicit bound for reaching any prescribed unit residue modulo $3^m$ while retaining unit states and requiring every exponent to lie between one and five. For $m\ge2$, every source in $2+3\mathbb Z_3$ reaches every such target within $\exp(2^{90}m^2 9^m)$ edges. A general source $x\ne1/13$ incurs at most $v_3(13x-1)$ additional edges. The earlier all-precision cap-five theorem supplied qualitative finite hitting without an effective precision dependence. We replace its stationary limiting argument by a finite spreading law followed by a nearly stationary average. The exact cylinder-entropy identity forces positive branching mass, and monotonicity of finite conditional entropy locates a scale with small total entropy drop. Finite avoidance yields holes at all required source depths. Conditioning on the actual source cylinder and total exponent preserves an independent convolution, whose gain has an explicit lower bound from cyclic variation and Pinsker's inequality. This contradicts the finite entropy budget. All conditioning costs and approximation errors are retained. Five bounded author checks verify finite probability interfaces and constants; the universal result follows from the written argument. The bound is enormous and does not improve the much shorter routes available with growing exponent caps. It gives neither practical search complexity, a smaller-source descent theorem, nor forward Collatz convergence.
