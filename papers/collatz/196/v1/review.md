# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result: the sharp lower bound $P\ge 2/3$ for every ordinary negative simple Collatz segment, with equality only on $-1\to-2$. The sliding-minimum argument supplies a uniform estimate for arbitrarily long high segments, while exact finite calculations control the remaining regions. This is substantially more than a bounded search or an empirical observation. The signed count-recovery and contact statements are useful consequences. The work is relevant to the selected problem through its control of the multiplicative correction in Collatz trajectories and its independently certified positive bounds. It does not resolve universal positive convergence, and the manuscript appropriately leaves that question open.

The novelty claims are adequately delimited. I checked Nikzad, Frinkle and Bagherzadeh's cited version [1], especially Corollary 2.21 and Theorem 2.22: the positive four-thirds bound, including its application after completing a final odd ordinary edge, is indeed prior work. Several positive count consequences are prior work as well, as acknowledged here. Luo's arXiv v3 [2] states the negative residue conjectures and the one-step witness, but does not prove the global negative minimum. Leen's archived supplement [4] concerns positive first-hit residues and supplies the cited finite-record data. The relevant rStation comparisons—harmonic occupation results [11], reciprocal bounds for the restricted avoid-$7$ class [12], and reciprocal cuts of inverse trees [13]—do not establish this sharp signed-segment theorem. The negative result and its quantitative proof method provide a sufficient distinct contribution; the positive corollaries should continue to be presented as applications or alternative proofs.

The central argument is sound on inspection. The sliding-window minimum isolates starting positions whose entire lookahead has positive shifted increment. Counting those positions by the minimum affine prefix correction, rather than treating every odd departure equally, is the decisive step. Upward rounding preserves the virtual survivors, and the panel estimate uses cumulative counts with nonnegative summation-by-parts coefficients. The infinite-tail argument uses injectivity of fixed-time images along a simple list and a binomial grade bound; it does not assume random parity statistics for an individual orbit. The treatment of terminal unpaired odd edges, inclusive high floors, negative halving blocks, and the exceptional departures from magnitude $1$ closes the relevant endpoint cases. The resulting signed interval also justifies the stated ambiguity and opposite-sign contact formulas.

The computational evidence supports the numerical parts of this proof. I replayed the complete default certificate and reproduced both high bounds, the low maxima $2^{61}/(993\,3^{32})$ and $729/640$, the negative $330$-step halving cover, and both positive rational comparison margins. I also replayed the complete local-positive certificate, obtaining $P\le1.987041505875978<2$, including its $469$-step cover and outward-rounded final comparison. The separate small audit of rounded counts and terminal-window inequalities passed. These are reproductions of the supplied arithmetic computations, not a formal verification of the whole proof. I did not run the full alternative certificate in Appendix A.

For the sharp positive result, the strict delay-record property remains an imported computational premise. I checked that the record starts occur in Roosendaal's published list and that the bundled excerpt matches the downloaded Leen archive, including its stated archive digest. Recounting a record trajectory does not establish maximality over all smaller starts; the manuscript makes this distinction correctly. The negative theorem and the local positive factor-two bound do not require that external premise.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement: the theorem, proof mechanism, finite inputs, and limitations can be followed from the main text. A few modest improvements would help:

- Add a short proof roadmap showing the high, middle, and low contributions for each sign before the detailed counting argument.
- Explicitly define $B_i$ in terms of the accumulated original log cost and the potential. The displayed increment identity determines it, but an initial normalization would ease reading.
- Give a precise archive-file pointer for the strengthened delay-based estimates attributed to [4], distinguishing those retained calculations from that note's principal theorem. Keep the new negative theorem more prominent than the already published positive consequences.
