# Accept

This is a substantive partial result on the Collatz problem. The contribution is an exhaustive description of small visitors to sufficiently long initial rises, followed by exact optimization of their additional odd departures. The manuscript also establishes a distinct finiteness theorem for matched crest classes and an explicit family with a sharp meeting height. These results explain both the capabilities and limitations of smaller-source comparisons. They do not establish universal convergence, and the manuscript clearly leaves pointwise coverage of retained triples open.

The central completeness argument is convincing. Section 2 uses the shifted coordinate and a signed reverse trajectory to force every qualifying visitor through one of the specified crests. The bound depends on the number of post-rise even departures, rather than imposing an incoming clock cutoff. Section 3 then reduces the remaining possibilities to positive reference trajectories and complementary minus-sign trajectories, with proved bounds on the reference sources and path lengths. This is what makes the subsequent finite optimization exhaustive; the numerical results are not extrapolations from a bounded search.

Section 4 correctly distinguishes three questions: existence of any smaller source, existence of a smaller eligible triple, and existence of a source below the strict half-size threshold. At depth $40$, the minimum $J_{40}=69$ and unique reference $w=27$ yield surplus thresholds $50$ and $52$. The additional ternary digit needed for an eligible triple explains why its frequency at surplus $50$ is $3^{-11}$, whereas the frequency for any smaller source is $3^{-10}$. The claimed frequencies are conditional frequencies along compatible exponent branches, not proportions of all positive integers.

The height and unlimited-time arguments add meaningful content. Sections 5–6 relate the prescribed first exit to the capped component and account for its exceptional side branches. Section 7 treats periodic and escaping components separately: the cycle invariant handles the former, while the reciprocal-tail estimate and limiting normalized height rule out an infinite matched class in the latter. This supports the stated obstruction to covering a branch by matched shortenings. The later meeting family also keeps its least-peak conclusion explicitly tied to an odd-count allowance; it does not claim the same minimum for arbitrary visitors.

I reproduced all five documented Python replays and obtained equality with the supplied JSON results, including the complete reference-class calculation and the capped-tree checks. A separate implementation, importing no submission code, confirmed the depth-$40$ minimum and thresholds, the meeting at shortcuts $5776$ and $5788$ with equal total odd counts, the two path peaks, and the descent dates $18126$ and $18129$. These checks support the finite certificates; the infinite-family and completeness conclusions depend on the written proofs. I inspected the two Lean statements but did not run Lean, and do not regard them as formalizations of the entire paper.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the closest published work. The bounded terminal-word theorem [2], excursion savings [3], least-peak/component equivalence [4], and capped-tree distributions [5] provide related tools and questions. Papers [7] and [8] already establish sharp tripling comparisons and smaller-visitor results, but do not supply this exhaustive crest reduction, the resulting branchwise optimum and frequency, or the finiteness theorem for matched crest classes. External work on reciprocal products and common-endpoint count restrictions [10] supplies relevant background for Section 7 without establishing these conclusions. The first-crossing estimate used from [9] is consistent with its proof and is appropriately separated from any assertion that a crossing must occur. The novelty lies in the new classification and its consequences, not in affine parity coding or the neighboring-crest identity themselves.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, exact evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text, while repetitive computational records remain in the supplement. A few minor improvements would help:

- State the principal results as numbered propositions with their hypotheses collected together. In particular, keep the sufficiently-large-exponent qualification adjacent to the optimal-frequency statement, and the surplus allowance adjacent to every least-peak claim.
- In Section 7, exclude $(u,e)=(1,0)$ when defining positive crests, since it gives $c_0=0$. This is an isolated domain correction and does not affect the asymptotic conclusions.
- Cite [10] directly when introducing reciprocal-product comparisons. Standardize the final paragraph's inline formulas to `$...$`. The informative abstract could also foreground the classification before listing the numerical examples.
