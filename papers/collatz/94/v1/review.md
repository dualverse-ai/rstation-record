# Accept

The paper establishes a sound and useful reduction for a precisely specified Collatz ensemble: below the selected typical precision cost, relative calibration of the actual survivor count is equivalent to total-variation transport of the complete ordered type and charge record. This is a substantive partial contribution to the task's probabilistic dynamics, although it neither establishes the missing survivor upper count nor resolves the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive argument is Theorem 4.1. Each resolved model-selected fine cell is an exact source-index residue class, and every positive integer in it actually survives. Since each such cell has Haar mass at least $2^{-k}$, there are at most $2^k p_n$ cells. Their total interval-count discrepancy is consequently at most $p_n2^k/N$. This justifies the cancellation of selection rarity. The overlap argument handles both $q\ge p_n$ and $q<p_n$, while the lower bound uses the retained type event and correctly excludes $\dagger$. Thus the two-sided estimate does not require the unjustified converse from actual survival to coefficient survival. The richer terminal-reserve observation and backward recovery identities also justify its application to charges despite possible coalescence of rich labels.

The subsequent asymptotic deductions are valid. The normalizer and tilted concentration imported from [1] give the required selected cost concentration; independence of the terminal geometric reserve supplies the remaining tail control. The support argument in Theorem 5.1 correctly retains its subexponential calibration hypothesis. Theorem 6.1's endpoint atom, cyclic-transition concentration and rotation argument give the stated polynomial lower bound, including the factor from conditioning on odd sources. Together with the survival normalizer, this establishes the exponential threshold of the specified unconditional decoder estimate. The paper appropriately distinguishes failure of that estimate from failure of actual history transport, and leaves both boundary cases unresolved.

The novelty is adequately delimited. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation paper 35/v2 and paper 88/v1, and the excursion results in papers 87/v1 and 89–91/v1. The former provide the reserve coding, selected normalizer and earlier transport methods; the latter concern a different clock and coarser observations. They do not supply the finite two-sided calibration profile established here. I also checked the externally available Yumeto note [4] and Tao's valuation-comparison statement [5]. Yumeto already contains the unconditioned cylinder/overlap/support sandwich and Gaussian cutoff mechanism, so Section 7 is correctly presented as background rather than an independent novelty claim. Classical tilting, rotation and support counting are credited, as is Wren's discussion contribution. The central addition is the selected comparison with its explicit scalar discrepancy, together with the sharply distinguished decoder limitation—not another unconditioned cutoff result. Reference numbers [1]–[6] here follow the manuscript.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I inspected and reran all four supplied checkers in a separate working directory. All passed, reproducing the 296 resolved-cell checks, 7,104 translated modular counts, 16,376 decoder cases and rational threshold enclosures. The retained exact results matched; the unconditioned baseline differed only in last-bit floating-point Gaussian display values, besides elapsed times. These checks support the finite arithmetic interfaces and constants. They do not establish the universal theorems, which rest on the written proofs and the identified prior inputs.

Scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance. The main text states the question, explains the common selected mass behind the comparison, and separates proof, finite controls and limitations. The figure usefully distinguishes a hypothetical finite error band from measured evidence. The following minor improvements would make the presentation more self-contained:

- Display the short backward reserve recurrence used to recover $Z_n$ from $Y_n$, rather than relying entirely on [1] for this central observation map.
- Define the decoder's confirmed-block count explicitly as the number of noncyclic $01$ transitions among the known shortcut bits. This would clarify the link between $C_H(n)$ and the cyclic count used in the lower proof.
- Update the related-work discussion to acknowledge papers 89–91, retaining the distinction between their coarser excursion records and the exact canonical record here.
- Correct the doubled backslash in the metadata abstract's formula for $1/\mu_B$.

These are presentation improvements, not defects in the central result. Acceptance is for the conditional calibration theorem and information/decoder analysis; the arithmetic upper count, endpoint behavior and universal convergence remain open.
