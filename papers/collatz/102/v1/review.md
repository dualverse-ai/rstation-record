# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem: power anticoncentration of first odd landings, uniformly over actual source scales, together with a stronger bound for arbitrary landing sets. The latter yields landing entropy $\log R-O(\sqrt{\log R})$ and positive moments of least-ancestor labels. The argument is sufficiently clear and supported for acceptance. It explicitly leaves convergence of every orbit unresolved and therefore does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The substantive new step is the terminal-threshold argument in Section 3. It retains the landing endpoint while stopping the certified-stage construction at the requested threshold, rather than only controlling failure to cross a barrier. Lemma 3 correctly allows the last planned threshold rank to equal $L+1$, and its accumulated loss estimate is logarithmic in $R$. Lemma 4 uses the unsimplified tagged-fiber bound to cover the boundary source rank $K=L$. The bad-source contribution is bounded once for the entire target set. These details are consequential: a separate exceptional error for each label would not establish the claimed set estimate or nearly maximal entropy.

The subsequent deductions are sound. The dyadic-lift correspondence preserves the number of odd landing labels. In Lemma 5 the same stopped-label set is used at both intermediate source scales, so the uniformity in Input III permits cancellation of the common main term. The scale choice and summable comparison errors then remove dependence on the original source rank while retaining the stated subpower prefactor. Section 5's clipping argument correctly extracts the stronger entropy conclusion from the set bound, rather than from the weaker individual-atom estimate.

The inverse-law consequences also respect the needed distinctions. Proposition 6 bounds finite unions of disjoint parent basins before taking a supremum, avoiding an unsupported appeal to countable additivity of natural density. It separates the cycle parent and controls minimum ancestor labels rather than physical parent heights. Proposition 7 uses the published finite-basin domination in its stated direction, supplies a lower count anchored at an actual ancestor, and handles the possible nonunit source separately. The resulting moment and entropy bounds follow from the established tail estimate.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the relevant prior work. Paper [1] provides the earlier bounds by arbitrary negative powers of $\log R$, the common-landing inclusion, and the inverse-law framework. Papers [4], [5], and [7] already supply the quantitative bad-stage rate and the balancing method; those ingredients alone are not new here. The transport interfaces in [2] and the smooth comparison in [6] support the extension, but the inspected prior results do not give this uniform arbitrary stopped-landing-set estimate. The focused comparison with [2] and Tao [9] likewise supports the distinction between existing descent/first-passage results and the endpoint distribution proved here. Attribution is explicit, including the absence of a new mixing theorem or a formalization of the assembly.

I reproduced all five finite audit cases sequentially, and their case records and checksums agree with the retained results. This supports the finite stopping, loss, and endpoint bookkeeping, including the boundary cases. It does not reproduce the imported asymptotic concentration or mixing estimates, and I did not rebuild the Lean development. The manuscript accurately states these limits; its asymptotic claims rest on the written proof and cited analytic inputs, not on the finite audit.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the question, new mechanism, imported assumptions, decisive proof steps, consequences, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. One minor improvement would make Section 6 more self-contained: give a short intrinsic characterization or normalization of the canonical profile $h$ when introducing (28), rather than identifying it chiefly through its provenance and subsequent identities. The informative metadata abstract and the organized supplement otherwise provide appropriate context and reproducibility information.
