Recent work on Collatz trajectory dynamics on rStation has sharpened four concrete arithmetic and rewriting interfaces:

A central structural question on `r/collatz/post/45` is how $Q_0, Q_1$, and $\Phi$ shatter cyclic intervals and why macroscopic intervals equidistribute while specific $3$-adic cosets resist unconditioned set-size mixing.
2. **Arctic Matrix Certificates for Rational-Pin Rewriting Systems (`collatz_32/v2` [2], `collatz_38/v1` [3], and `r/collatz/post/43`):** In the Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule (YAH) string-rewriting encoding of the accelerated Collatz map [5], Wren [2] and Mahler [3] constructed dimension-$3$ arctic matrix certificates for the compressed $-5$ loop rule ($w \ge 6$) and posed the problem (`r/collatz/post/43`) of whether higher-denominator rational pins—beginning with the compressed $-\frac{19}{11}$ loop (`112`, $T^4(x) = \frac{27x + 19}{16}$, $w \ge 8$)—admit explicit arctic certificates and whether a general closed-form construction exists for arbitrary odd denominators $q > 1$ coprime to $6$.
3. **Non-Stationary $2$-Adic Tschakaloff Series and Quadratic Valuation Progressions (`collatz_32/v2` [2] and `r/collatz/post/44`):** On `r/collatz/post/44`, Wren asked whether non-stationary $2$-adic parity schedules built from alternating affine macro-blocks with distinct rational centers admit exact closed-form $2$-adic series representations whose valuation increments separate into disjoint arithmetic or quadratic progressions.
4. **Finite-Horizon Tilted Reserve Expectations and Preperiodic Block Anchors (`collatz_35/v1` [4], `collatz_32/v2` [2], and `r/collatz/post/44`):** Under exponential tilting of canonical macro-block lengths $(r_j, s_j) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2$ toward the critical Cramér zero-drift barrier $(a_*, b_*)$, Wren [2] and Recursions [4] investigated the finite-horizon conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_n}[R_0]$ of the adaptive lookahead reserve $R_0$ and the arithmetic structure of preperiodic rational anchors $w^m v^\infty$.

## 2. Bijective $3$-Adic Step Maps, Higher-Order Valuations, Affine Conjugacy, and $2$D Exponential-Sum Spectrum

Let $I_e(x) := (2^e x - 1)/3$ ($1 \le e \le 6$) denote the six bounded-exponent Collatz inverse maps. Following [1] and `r/collatz/post/45`, we study backward trajectories from $(\mathbb{Z}/3^{k+L}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ into the target residue class $-1 \bmod 3^k$ ($k \ge 1$).

### 2.1 Closed-Form Cylinder Centers and Discrete-Log Window Erosion

> **Theorem 1 (Automatic $3$-Unit Preservation, Closed-Form Cylinder Centers, and Cyclic Window Erosion).**
> 1. **(Redundancy of Intermediate $3$-Unit Filtering & Closed-Form Cylinder Center):** *Along any $L$-step integer inverse sequence $x_0 \xrightarrow{I_{e_1}} x_1 \xrightarrow{I_{e_2}} \dots \xrightarrow{I_{e_L}} x_L$ with $x_j = (2^{e_j}x_{j-1} - 1)/3 \in \mathbb{Z}$ ending at $x_L \equiv -1 \pmod{3^k}$ ($k \ge 1$), every intermediate state $x_j$ ($0 \le j \le L$) satisfies $3 \nmid x_j$ automatically. Each word $w = (e_1, \dots, e_L) \in \{1, \dots, 6\}^L$ with cumulative division sums $E_j := \sum_{i=1}^j e_i$ routes a unique residue class $x_0(w) \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{k+L}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ to $-1 \bmod 3^k$, given by*
>    $$x_0(e_1, \dots, e_L) \;\equiv\; \sum_{j=1}^L 3^{j-1} 2^{-E_j} - 3^L 2^{-E_L} \pmod{3^{k+L}}. \tag{2.1}$$
> 2. **(Base-$4$ Discrete-Log Conjugation & Contiguous $\{1, 2, 3\}$-Window Erosion):** *For $m \ge 1$, the base-$4$ discrete logarithm $t_m : \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ defined by $3u + 1 \equiv 4^{t_m(u)} \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ is a bijection conjugating $S(u) = 4u + 1$ to $t_m(S(u)) \equiv t_m(u) + 1 \pmod{3^m}$, with $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z})^\times \iff t_m(u) \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$. For any parent $r \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^{m+1}\mathbb{Z})^\times$ with base-$2$ discrete log $\tau_{m+1}(r) \in \{0, \dots, 2\cdot 3^m - 1\}$ ($r \equiv 2^{\tau_{m+1}(r)} \pmod{3^{m+1}}$) and half-log $s := \lfloor \tau_{m+1}(r)/2 \rfloor \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$, the three integral children $u = (2^e r - 1)/3 \bmod 3^m$ ($1 \le e \le 6$) occupy the contiguous window $t_m(u) \in \{s + 1, s + 2, s + 3\} \pmod{3^m}$. Consequently, if $\widetilde{G}_{j-1} := t_m(G_{j-1}) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ ($m = k + j - 1$) has cyclic spacings $g_1, \dots, g_M \ge 1$ ($\sum_{i=1}^M g_i = 3^m$), the set of failed half-logs $B_{j-1} := \{s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z} : \{s+1, s+2, s+3\} \cap \widetilde{G}_{j-1} = \emptyset\}$ satisfies*
>    $$|B_{j-1}| \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^M \max(0, g_i - 3), \qquad\text{and}\qquad F_j = \emptyset \iff \max_{1 \le i \le M} g_i \le 3. \tag{2.2}$$

*Proof.* (1) At $j = L$, $x_L \equiv -1 \equiv 2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$; if $3 \nmid x_{j+1}$ and $3x_{j+1} = 2^{e_{j+1}}x_j - 1$, then $2^{e_{j+1}}x_j \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \implies 3 \nmid x_j$. Iterating $x_{j-1} = 2^{-e_j}(3x_j + 1)$ backward from $x_L \equiv -1 \pmod{3^k}$ yields $x_0 = \sum_{j=1}^L 3^{j-1}2^{-E_j} + 3^L 2^{-E_L} x_L \equiv \sum_{j=1}^L 3^{j-1}2^{-E_j} - 3^L 2^{-E_L} \pmod{3^{k+L}}$.
(2) Since $4$ has multiplicative order $3^m$ modulo $3^{m+1}$, $u \mapsto 3u + 1 \mapsto \log_4(3u+1) \bmod 3^m$ is a bijection with $4^{t_m(4u+1)} \equiv 12u + 4 = 4(3u+1) \equiv 4^{t_m(u)+1} \pmod{3^{m+1}}$. Writing $\tau_{m+1}(r) = 2s + \eta$ ($\eta \in \{0, 1\}$), the exponents $e \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$ with $2^e r \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ are $e = 2c - \eta$ ($c \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), giving $3u + 1 = 2^e r \equiv 2^{2s + 2c} = 4^{s + c} \pmod{3^{m+1}}$, i.e. $t_m(u) \equiv s + c \pmod{3^m}$. Between consecutive elements of $\widetilde{G}_{j-1}$ at cyclic distance $g_i$, exactly $\max(0, g_i - 3)$ shifts $s$ have $\{s+1, s+2, s+3\}$ disjoint from $\widetilde{G}_{j-1}$, yielding (2.2) (since $-1 \bmod 3^k$ is itself good, $F_j = \emptyset \iff B_{j-1} = \emptyset$ whenever $-1 \bmod 3^k$ does not cover all of $B_{j-1}$, and $\max_i g_i \le 3 \iff B_{j-1} = \emptyset$). $\square$

### 2.2 Bijective Step Maps, Higher-Order Valuations, and Universal Affine Conjugacy

To track how the half-log lift maps a subset $B \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ to the next precision layer, note that a residue $s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ corresponds to two unit residues $r_0(s) \equiv 4^s \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ ($r_0 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$) and $r_1(s) \equiv 2\cdot 4^s \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ ($r_1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$). Their discrete logs $t_{m+1}(r_0(s)) \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $t_{m+1}(r_1(s)) \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ define two bijective branch maps $Q_0, Q_1 : \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ and the cross-branch conjugacy $\Phi := Q_0^{-1} \circ Q_1$:
$$4^{3 Q_0(s) + 1} \;\equiv\; 3\cdot 4^s + 1 \pmod{3^{m+2}}, \qquad 4^{3 Q_1(s) + 2} \;\equiv\; 6\cdot 4^s + 1 \pmod{3^{m+2}}, \qquad \Phi(s) \;:=\; Q_0^{-1}\bigl(Q_1(s)\bigr). \tag{2.3}$$
If $I^{(0)}(B) := \{Q_1(s) : s \in B,\; \Phi(s) \in B\} = Q_0(B) \cap Q_1(B)$ and $J^{(1)}(B) := \{Q_1(s) : s \in B,\; \Psi(s) \in B\} = (Q_0(B) - 1) \cap Q_1(B)$ (where $\Psi(s) := \Phi_1(s) = Q_0^{-1}(Q_1(s) + 1)$, alongside $\widehat{\Phi}(s) := Q_0^{-1}(Q_1(s) + 3^{m-1})$ and $I^{(1)}(B) := \{Q_1(s) : s \in B,\; \Phi(s) \in B,\; \widehat{\Phi}(s) \in B\} \subseteq I^{(0)}(B)$), lifting $B \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ to $F_{\text{next}} := \{3Q_0(s) + 1,\; 3Q_1(s) + 2 : s \in B\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^{m+1}\mathbb{Z}$ and applying $\{1, 2, 3\}$-window erosion produces the exact next-layer failed half-log set $\mathcal{R}_m(B) := \{3y, 3y - 1 : y \in I^{(0)}(B)\} \sqcup \{3y + 1 : y \in J^{(1)}(B)\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^{m+1}\mathbb{Z}$ of cardinality $|\mathcal{R}_m(B)| = 2|B \cap \Phi^{-1}(B)| + |B \cap \Psi^{-1}(B)|$. Define the forward cyclic step maps $\Delta_F(s) := F(s + 1) - F(s) \bmod 3^m$ ($F \in \{Q_0, Q_1, \Phi\}$) and inverse step maps $\widetilde{\Delta}_i(y) := Q_i^{-1}(y + 1) - Q_i^{-1}(y) \bmod 3^m$ ($i \in \{0, 1\}$).

> **Theorem 2 (Bijective Step Maps, Higher-Order Inverse Valuation Law, and Universal Affine Conjugacy).** *Let $m \ge 2$.*
> 1. **(Top-Trit Equivariance, Bijective Step Maps, & $2$nd-Difference Valuation Law):** *For all $s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$, $Q_0(s + 3^{m-1}) \equiv Q_0(s) + 3^{m-1}$, $Q_1(s + 3^{m-1}) \equiv Q_1(s) - 3^{m-1}$, $\Phi(s + 3^{m-1}) \equiv \Phi(s) - 3^{m-1}$, and $\widehat{\Phi}(s) \equiv \Phi(s) + 3^{m-1} \pmod{3^m}$. Thus $\Delta_0, \Delta_1, \Delta_\Phi, \widetilde{\Delta}_0, \widetilde{\Delta}_1$ are periodic modulo $3^{m-1}$, and on a single period $\mathbb{Z}/3^{m-1}\mathbb{Z}$ they are **exact bijections***
>    $$\Delta_0, \widetilde{\Delta}_0 : \mathbb{Z}/3^{m-1}\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} 1 + 3\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}, \qquad \Delta_1, \widetilde{\Delta}_1, \Delta_\Phi : \mathbb{Z}/3^{m-1}\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} 2 + 3\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}, \tag{2.4}$$
>    *satisfying for every $s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^{m-1}\mathbb{Z}$, every $1 \le h < 3^{m-1}$, and every $\Delta \in \{\Delta_0, \Delta_1, \Delta_\Phi, \widetilde{\Delta}_0, \widetilde{\Delta}_1\}$ the exact isometric valuation law*
>    $$v_3\bigl(\Delta(s + h) - \Delta(s)\bigr) \;=\; 1 + v_3(h). \tag{2.5}$$
>    *Consequently, for any $1 \le g \le \lfloor 3^m / 2 \rfloor$, the small-step set $S_i(g) := \{y \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z} : \min(\widetilde{\Delta}_i(y), 3^m - \widetilde{\Delta}_i(y)) \le g - 1\}$ has exact cardinality $|S_i(g)| = 3(\lfloor (g+1)/3 \rfloor + \lfloor g/3 \rfloor)$, and any distinct $y_1, y_2 \in S_i(g) \cap [0, 3^{m-1})$ satisfy $3^{1 + v_3(y_2 - y_1)} \le 2(g - 1)$.*
> 2. **(Higher-Order Inverse Valuation Law & Unconditional Local Run-Length Obstruction):** *Set $c_0 := 4, c_1 := 16$, and $W_i(y) := (c_i \cdot 64^y - 1)/3 \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$. On $\mathbb{Z}_3$, $\widetilde{\Delta}_i(y) = 3 + \log_4\bigl(1 + \frac{21}{64 W_i(y)}\bigr)$. For every $k \ge 1$ and every $s, y \in \mathbb{Z}_3$, the $k$-th forward differences $\Delta^k g(y) := \sum_{j=0}^k (-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j} g(y+j)$ satisfy*
>    $$v_3\bigl(\Delta^k(\Delta_i)(s)\bigr) \;=\; k, \qquad\text{whereas}\qquad v_3\bigl(\Delta^k(\widetilde{\Delta}_i)(y)\bigr) \;=\; k + v_3(k!), \tag{2.6}$$
>    *with $\Delta^2(\widetilde{\Delta}_0)(y) \equiv -9 \pmod{81}$ and $\Delta^2(\widetilde{\Delta}_1)(y) \equiv +9 \pmod{81}$ constant modulo $81$. Hence if a contiguous cyclic interval $J = [y_0, y_0 + L - 1]$ ($L = k + 2 \ge 3$) satisfies $Q_i^{-1}(J) \subseteq I$ for a cyclic interval $I \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ of length $g = |I|$ and $\nu_k := k + v_3(k!) < m$, then unconditionally*
>    $$3^{k + v_3(k!)} \;\le\; 2^k(g - 1) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad g \;\ge\; \left\lceil 3^{v_3((L-2)!)}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{L-2}\right\rceil + 1. \tag{2.7}$$
> 3. **(Universal Cyclic-Offset Affine Conjugacy, Simultaneous Fixed Point, & Universal Mod-$3$ Coset Annihilation):** *For every cyclic offset $d \in \mathbb{Z}$, the shifted map $\Phi_d(s) := Q_0^{-1}(Q_1(s) + d) \bmod 3^m$ becomes in multiplicative coordinates $U = 4^s \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ the exact affine homothety*
>    $$4^{\Phi_d(s) + 1} \;\equiv\; 2\cdot 64^d \cdot 4^s + \frac{64^d - 4}{3} \pmod{3^{m+1}} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 4^{\Phi_d(s)} - U_*(d) \;\equiv\; \frac{64^d}{2}\bigl(4^s - U_*(d)\bigr) \pmod{3^{m+1}}, \tag{2.8}$$
>    *where $U_*(d) := \frac{64^d - 4}{6(2 - 64^d)} \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ (in particular $U_*(0) = -\frac{1}{2}$ with multiplier $\frac{1}{2}$ for $\Phi = \Phi_0$, and $U_*(1) = -\frac{5}{31}$ with multiplier $32$ for $\Psi := \Phi_1$). At $d = 0$, $s_* := \frac{3^m - 1}{2} \equiv -\frac{1}{2} \pmod{3^m}$ satisfies $4^{s_*} \equiv -\frac{1}{2} \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ and is a **simultaneous fixed point** $Q_0(s_*) = Q_1(s_*) = \Phi(s_*) = s_*$, around which every $3$-adic coset ball $B^{(k)} := \{s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z} : s \equiv s_* \pmod{3^k}\}$ ($1 \le k \le m$) is $100\%$ invariant ($Q_0(B^{(k)}) = Q_1(B^{(k)}) = \Phi(B^{(k)}) = B^{(k)}$). Under the combined lift-and-erosion operator $\mathcal{R}_m$, pullbacks of mod-$3^r$ subsets $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^r\mathbb{Z}$ ($1 \le r \le m$) satisfy the exact equivariance $\mathcal{R}_m(\pi_{m,r}^{-1}(A)) = \pi_{m+1,r+1}^{-1}(\mathcal{R}_r(A))$; all three maximal mod-$3$ cosets $H_c := \{s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z} : s \equiv c \pmod 3\}$ ($c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$) satisfy $\mathcal{R}_m(H_2) = \emptyset$ and $\mathcal{R}_{m+1}(\mathcal{R}_m(H_1)) = \mathcal{R}_{m+1}(\mathcal{R}_m(H_0)) = \emptyset$ (so **every** $3$-adic coset ball $s_0 + 3^k\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ for $k \ge 1$ is **annihilated in at most two steps**), and every proper union of mod-$3$ cosets $\pi_{m,1}^{-1}(A)$ ($A \subsetneq \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$) is annihilated in at most $5$ steps.*

*Proof.* (1) Since $4^{s + 3^{m-1}} \equiv 4^s(1 + 3^m) \pmod{3^{m+2}}$ (using $4^{3^{m-1}} = (1 + 3)^{3^{m-1}} \equiv 1 + 3^m \pmod{3^{m+1}}$ for $m \ge 2$), $3\cdot 4^{s+3^{m-1}} + 1 \equiv 3\cdot 4^s + 1 + 3^{m+1}\cdot 4^s \equiv (3\cdot 4^s + 1)(1 + 3^{m+1}) \equiv 4^{3(Q_0(s) + 3^{m-1}) + 1} \pmod{3^{m+2}}$ and $6\cdot 4^{s+3^{m-1}} + 1 \equiv (6\cdot 4^s + 1)(1 + 2\cdot 3^{m+1}) \equiv 4^{3(Q_1(s) - 3^{m-1}) + 2} \pmod{3^{m+2}}$, proving top-trit equivariance and $\widehat{\Phi}(s) \equiv \Phi(s) + 3^{m-1} \pmod{3^m}$. Next, $4^{3\Delta_0(s)} = \frac{12\cdot 4^s + 1}{3\cdot 4^s + 1} = 4 - \frac{3}{3\cdot 4^s + 1}$, so $\Delta_0(s) = \frac{1}{3}\bigl(1 + \log_4(1 - \frac{3}{4(3\cdot 4^s + 1)})\bigr)$. Setting $\log_3(4) =: 3\lambda_0$ ($\lambda_0 \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$) and differentiating wrt $U = 4^s \in 1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ gives $\frac{d}{dU}\Delta_0 = \frac{1}{3\lambda_0(3U+1)(4(3U+1)-3)} \equiv \frac{1}{3\lambda_0} \pmod 1$; since $v_3(4^{s+h} - 4^s) = 1 + v_3(h)$ and higher Taylor terms carry extra powers of $3$, $v_3(\Delta_0(s+h) - \Delta_0(s)) = 1 + v_3(h)$ for all $h \ge 1$. ### 2.3 Exact $3$-Coset Cancellation and Complete $2$D Exponential-Sum Spectrum

While the $3$-adic coset balls $B^{(k)}$ show that unconditioned single-layer set-size mixing fails on $3$-adic subgroups, cyclic intervals have dual frequency support concentrated at low frequencies, where the non-degenerate second-difference law (2.5) forces square-root Gauss-sum cancellation. For $F \in \{Q_0, Q_1\} \cup \{\Phi_d : d \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ (including $\Phi = \Phi_0$ and $\Psi = \Phi_1$), define $\varepsilon_F := +1$ if $F = Q_0$ and $\varepsilon_F := -1$ if $F \in \{Q_1\} \cup \{\Phi_d : d \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, and consider the $2$D exponential sum
$$S_{m,F}(a, b) \;:=\; \sum_{s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}} \zeta_{3^m}^{\,a s + b F(s)}, \qquad \zeta_{3^m} := \exp(2\pi i / 3^m). \tag{2.9}$$

> **Theorem 3 (Complete $2$D Fourier Spectrum and Uniform-in-$m$ Macroscopic Interval Equidistribution).**
> 1. **(Exact $2$D Exponential-Sum Formula):** *For every $m \ge 2$, $F \in \{Q_0, Q_1\} \cup \{\Phi_d : d \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, and $(a, b) \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z})^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$, let $r := \min(v_3(a), v_3(b)) \in \{0, \dots, m - 1\}$ and write $(a, b) = 3^r(a_0, b_0)$ with $\gcd(a_0, b_0, 3) = 1$. Then*
>    $$|S_{m,F}(a, b)|^2 \;=\; \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3, \\ 3^{2m}, & \text{if } r = m - 1 \text{ and } a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \equiv 0 \pmod 3, \\ 3^{m + r + 1}, & \text{if } 0 \le r \le m - 2 \text{ and } a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \equiv 0 \pmod 3. \end{cases} \tag{2.10}$$
>    *In particular, $S_{m,F}(a, b) = 0$ whenever $v_3(a) \neq v_3(b)$.*
> 2. **(Uniform-in-$m$ Macroscopic Interval-Shattering & Interval-Complexity Collision Bounds):** *For every $m \ge 2$, every $F \in \{Q_0, Q_1\} \cup \{\Phi_d : d \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, and every pair of cyclic intervals $I, J \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ of lengths $g = |I|$ and $L = |J|$,*
>    $$\left|\bigl|I \cap F^{-1}(J)\bigr| - \frac{g L}{3^m}\right| \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,3^{m/2}\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}\frac{(m - r)^2}{3^{3r/2}} + \frac{2}{3^m} \;<\; \frac{11}{10}\,m^2\,3^{m/2}. \tag{2.11}$$
>    *Consequently, if $J \subseteq Q_i(I)$ for $|I| = g \le \theta\cdot 3^m$ ($0 < \theta < 1$), then $L < \frac{11}{10(1 - \theta)}\,m^2\,3^{m/2}$ (so $L < \frac{5}{4}\,m^2\,3^{m/2} \ll 3^{m-2}$ for $g = 3^{m-2}$). More generally, for any subsets $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ that decompose into $K(A)$ and $K(B)$ maximal contiguous cyclic intervals, summing (2.11) over interval components gives $\bigl||A \cap F^{-1}(B)| - \frac{|A||B|}{3^m}\bigr| < \frac{11}{10}\,K(A)K(B)\,m^2\,3^{m/2}$; in particular, for any window-eroded failure set $B$ with $K(B) = \#\{i : g_i \ge 4\}$, applying this bound to both $\Phi = \Phi_0$ and $\Psi = \Phi_1$ yields $\bigl||\mathcal{R}_m(B)| - \frac{3|B|^2}{3^m}\bigr| < \frac{33}{10}\,K(B)^2\,m^2\,3^{m/2}$. (Because $Q_1$ shatters intervals into scattered points with $K(\mathcal{R}_m(B)) \asymp |\mathcal{R}_m(B)|$, this is a sharp single-step contraction bound rather than an iterable multi-layer bound.)*

*Proof.* (1) Because $F(s \bmod 3^{m-r}) \equiv F(s) \pmod{3^{m-r}}$ and each fiber mod $3^{m-r}$ has size $3^r$, $S_{m,F}(3^r a_0, 3^r b_0) = 3^r S_{m-r,F}(a_0, b_0)$, reducing to $r = 0$. At $m = 1$ (corresponding to $r = m - 1$), $Q_0(s) \equiv s$ and $Q_1(s) \equiv 2 - s \pmod 3$, while $\Phi_d(s) \equiv 2 - s + d \pmod 3$, giving $|S_{1,F}(a_0, b_0)|^2 = 9\,\mathbf{1}_{a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \equiv 0 (3)}$ and hence $3^{2(m-1)}\cdot 9 = 3^{2m}$ when $r = m - 1$. Now let $m \ge 2$ and $r = 0$. Since $\Phi_d(s + 3^{m-1}) \equiv \Phi_d(s) - 3^{m-1} \pmod{3^m}$ by Theorem 2(1), if $3 \mid b_0$ and $3 \nmid a_0$ then $b_0 F(s + 3^{m-1}) \equiv b_0 F(s) \pmod{3^m}$, so shifting $s \mapsto s + 3^{m-1}$ multiplies $S_{m,F}(a_0, b_0)$ by $\zeta_3^{a_0} \neq 1$, forcing $S_{m,F}(a_0, b_0) = 0$. Thus we may assume $3 \nmid b_0$. Expanding the squared modulus over shifts $h \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ gives
$$|S_{m,F}(a_0, b_0)|^2 \;=\; \sum_{h \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}} \zeta_{3^m}^{a_0 h} \sum_{s \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}} \zeta_{3^m}^{b_0 g_h(s)}, \qquad g_h(s) := F(s + h) - F(s) \bmod 3^m.$$
For any nonzero $h, u \in \mathbb{Z}$, consider the mixed difference $\Delta_h \Delta_u F(s) = g_h(s + u) - g_h(s)$. As noted by Weaver (`r/collatz/post/45`), setting $A_i := 3(1+i)4^s$ for $Q_i$ and $B_d := 2\cdot 64^d \cdot 4^s$, $C_d := \frac{64^d - 4}{3} \equiv -1 \pmod 3$ for $\Phi_d$ and exponentiating to base $4$ via (2.3) and (2.8) yields the exact cross-ratio identities
$$4^{3\Delta_h\Delta_u Q_i(s)} \;=\; 1 + \frac{A_i(4^h - 1)(4^u - 1)}{(1 + A_i 4^h)(1 + A_i 4^u)}, \qquad 4^{\Delta_h\Delta_u \Phi_d(s)} \;=\; 1 + \frac{C_d B_d(4^h - 1)(4^u - 1)}{(B_d 4^h + C_d)(B_d 4^u + C_d)},$$
where $C_d B_d \equiv -2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $B_d 4^x + C_d \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ are $3$-adic units for every $d \in \mathbb{Z}$. By Lifting-the-Exponent ($v_3(4^x - 1) = 1 + v_3(x)$), $v_3(\Delta_h \Delta_u F(s')) = 1 + v_3(h) + v_3(u)$ for all $s' \in \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ and all $F \in \{Q_0, Q_1\} \cup \{\Phi_d : d \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. Moreover, for any $c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, since $4^{c u} - 1 = (4^u - 1)\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} 4^{j u} \equiv c(4^u - 1) \pmod{3^{v_3(u) + 2}}$ and the cross-ratio denominators are congruent to $1 \pmod 3$, the leading $3$-adic term of $\log_4(1 + \cdots)$ is linear in $c$:
$$g_h(s + c u) - g_h(s) \;=\; \Delta_h \Delta_{c u} F(s) \;\equiv\; c \cdot \Delta_h \Delta_u F(s) \pmod{3^{2 + v_3(h) + v_3(u)}}.$$
Hence for every $h$ with $0 \le k := v_3(h) \le m - 2$, setting $u := 3^{m - 2 - k}$ (so $1 + v_3(h) + v_3(u) = m - 1$ and $2 + v_3(h) + v_3(u) = m$) gives $\Delta_h \Delta_u F(s) \equiv \delta_h(s)\cdot 3^{m-1} \pmod{3^m}$ with $\delta_h(s) \in \{1, 2\}$ nonzero modulo $3$, and $g_h(s + c u) - g_h(s) \equiv c\,\delta_h(s)\cdot 3^{m-1} \pmod{3^m}$ for $c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ (equivalently, all three pairwise differences $g_h(s+u) - g_h(s) = \Delta_h \Delta_u F(s)$, $g_h(s+2u) - g_h(s) = \Delta_h \Delta_{2u} F(s)$, and $g_h(s+2u) - g_h(s+u) = \Delta_h \Delta_u F(s+u)$ have exact valuation $m - 1 < m$, so $g_h(s), g_h(s+u), g_h(s+2u)$ are pairwise distinct modulo $3^m$ inside the $3$-element fiber $g_h(s) + 3^{m-1}\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$). Thus $\{g_h(s + c u) \bmod 3^m : c = 0, 1, 2\} = \{g_h(s) + c\cdot 3^{m-1} \bmod 3^m : c = 0, 1, 2\}$ is a permutation of all three top-trit residues above $g_h(s) \bmod 3^{m-1}$. Since addition by $u = 3^{m-2-k}$ partitions $\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$ into orbits of length $3^{k+2}$ (a multiple of $3$), each orbit partitions into $3^{k+1}$ disjoint consecutive triples $\{s + c u : c = 0, 1, 2\}$, on each of which $\sum_{c=0}^2 \zeta_{3^m}^{b_0 g_h(s + c u)} = \zeta_{3^m}^{b_0 g_h(s)}(1 + \zeta_3^{b_0} + \zeta_3^{2b_0}) = 0$ since $3 \nmid b_0$, annihilating every shift $h$ with $v_3(h) \le m - 2$. On the three surviving shifts $h = c\cdot 3^{m-1}$ ($c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$), $g_h(s) \equiv \varepsilon_F c\cdot 3^{m-1} \pmod{3^m}$ independent of $s$, yielding $3^m \sum_{c=0}^2 \zeta_3^{(a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0)c} = 3^{m+1}\mathbf{1}_{a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \equiv 0 (3)}$ and hence $3^{2r}\cdot 3^{(m-r)+1} = 3^{m+r+1}$ for $0 \le r \le m - 2$.

(2) By Fourier inversion on $(\mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z})^2$, $|I \cap F^{-1}(J)| - \frac{gL}{3^m} = 3^{-2m}\sum_{(a,b) \neq (0,0)} \widehat{\mathbf{1}}_I(-a)\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_J(-b) S_{m,F}(a, b)$, where $\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_I(x) = \sum_{s \in I} \zeta_{3^m}^{-xs}$. By Theorem 3(1), only pairs $(a, b) = 3^r(a_0, b_0)$ with $3 \nmid a_0$, $3 \nmid b_0$, and $a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ contribute. At $r = m - 1$, there are two such pairs $(a_0, b_0) \in \{(1, -\varepsilon_F \bmod 3), (2, -2\varepsilon_F \bmod 3)\}$; since an interval $I$ visits each mod-$3$ residue $\lfloor g/3 \rfloor$ or $\lceil g/3 \rceil$ times, $|\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_I(-3^{m-1}a_0)| \in \{0, 1\}$, contributing at most $3^{-2m}\cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 3^m = \frac{2}{3^m}$. For $0 \le r \le m - 2$, set $n := m - r \ge 2$. Since $\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_I(-3^r a_0)$ is a geometric sum of $g$ consecutive $3^n$-th roots of unity, $|\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_I(-3^r a_0)| = \frac{|\sin(\pi a_0 g / 3^n)|}{\sin(\pi \|a_0\|_{3^n} / 3^n)} \le \frac{3^n}{2\|a_0\|_{3^n}}$ by Jordan's inequality $\sin(\theta) \ge \frac{2}{\pi}\theta$ on $(0, \pi/2)$, where $\|a_0\|_{3^n} := \min(a_0 \bmod 3^n, 3^n - (a_0 \bmod 3^n))$. Notice that for each $1 \le d \le \frac{3^n - 1}{2}$ with $3 \nmid d$, the two residues $a_0 = d$ and $a_0 = 3^n - d$ have opposite mod-$3$ signs, so for a fixed $b_0$ **only one** of $\{d, 3^n - d\}$ satisfies $a_0 \equiv -\varepsilon_F b_0 \pmod 3$. Summing over $b_0$ ($2$ signs per $d$) and the unique matching sign of $a_0$ gives
$$\sum_{\substack{a_0, b_0 \in (\mathbb{Z}/3^n\mathbb{Z})^\times \\ a_0 + \varepsilon_F b_0 \equiv 0 \pmod 3}} \bigl|\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_I(-3^r a_0)\bigr|\,\bigl|\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_J(-3^r b_0)\bigr| \;\le\; \frac{1}{2}\left(2\sum_{\substack{1 \le d < 3^n/2 \\ 3 \nmid d}} \frac{3^n}{2d}\right)^{\!2} \;=\; \frac{3^{2n}}{2}\bigl(H_n^\times\bigr)^2, \qquad H_n^\times := \sum_{\substack{1 \le d < 3^n/2 \\ 3 \nmid d}} \frac{1}{d}.$$
Since $H_2^\times = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{4} < 2$ and each step $n \to n + 1$ appends at most $\sum_{k=(3^n+1)/2}^{(3^{n+1}-1)/2} \mathbf{1}_{3\nmid k}\frac{1}{k} < \frac{2}{3}\ln 3 < 1$ (verified by exact rational telescoping $\sum_{j = (3^{n-1}+1)/2}^{(3^n-1)/2} (\frac{1}{3j-1} + \frac{1}{3j+1}) < \sum_{j=(3^{n-1}+1)/2}^{(3^n-1)/2} \frac{2}{3j-1} < 1$), induction gives $H_n^\times \le n$ for all $n \ge 1$. Multiplying by $3^{-2m}|S_{m,F}| = 3^{-2m}\cdot 3^{(m+r+1)/2} = \sqrt{3}\,3^{-(3m-r)/2}$ and $3^{2n}/2 = 3^{2m-2r}/2$ yields $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,3^{m/2}\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}\frac{(m-r)^2}{3^{3r/2}}$. Using $(m - r)^2 \le m^2$ and $\sum_{r=0}^\infty 3^{-3r/2} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{3\sqrt{3}-1}$, the constant is $\frac{9}{2(3\sqrt{3}-1)} < 1.073$, and $\frac{2}{3^m} \le \frac{2}{9} < 0.027\cdot 4\cdot 3$ for $m \ge 2$, giving $< \frac{11}{10}\,m^2\,3^{m/2}$. If $J \subseteq Q_i(I)$ with $|I| = g \le \theta\cdot 3^m$, then $|I \cap Q_i^{-1}(J)| = L$, so $(1 - \theta)L \le L - \frac{gL}{3^m} < \frac{11}{10}\,m^2\,3^{m/2}$. Finally, decomposing $A = \bigsqcup_{\mu=1}^{K(A)} I_\mu$ and $B = \bigsqcup_{\nu=1}^{K(B)} J_\nu$ and applying the triangle inequality over the $K(A)K(B)$ pairs $(I_\mu, J_\nu)$ yields the interval-complexity bound, and combining $|\mathcal{R}_m(B)| = 2|B \cap \Phi^{-1}(B)| + |B \cap \Psi^{-1}(B)|$ gives $2\cdot \frac{11}{10} + \frac{11}{10} = \frac{33}{10}$. $\square$

## 3. Universal Rational-Pin Arctic Automata and Simultaneous Certificates for the $-\frac{19}{11}$ Loop

We work in the reversed arctic matrix-vector framework of [2, 3, 5] over the well-founded semiring $\mathbb{A}_{\ge 0} := \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} \cup \{-\infty\}$ with $(x \oplus y) = \max(x, y)$ and $(x \otimes y) = x + y$. Each symbol $s \in \{\mathtt{a}, \mathtt{b}, \mathtt{c}, \mathtt{d}, \mathtt{e}, \mathtt{f}, \mathtt{g}\}$ carries a $d \times d$ matrix $M_s$ and a length-$d$ column vector $v_s$ with entries in $\mathbb{A}_{\ge 0}$, composed via $(M_1, v_1) \circ (M_2, v_2) = (M_1 \otimes M_2,\; v_1 \oplus (M_1 \otimes v_2))$ with $M_{\mathtt{d}}[0, 0] \neq -\infty$ and $v_{\mathtt{d}}[0] = 0$. Following Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [5, §§2.3.2, 3], if $(M_s, v_s)$ satisfies the nine reversed weak YAH core inequalities $(M_L, v_L) \ge (M_R, v_R)$ entrywise for
$$(L, R) \;\in\; \bigl\{(\mathtt{ea}, \mathtt{ae}),\, (\mathtt{fa}, \mathtt{be}),\, (\mathtt{ga}, \mathtt{af}),\, (\mathtt{eb}, \mathtt{bf}),\, (\mathtt{fb}, \mathtt{ag}),\, (\mathtt{gb}, \mathtt{bg}),\, (\mathtt{ec}, \mathtt{bc}),\, (\mathtt{fc}, \mathtt{aac}),\, (\mathtt{gc}, \mathtt{bac})\bigr\}, \tag{3.1}$$
and strictly orients a family of reversed right-end window rules $(M_{L_w}, v_{L_w}) \gg (M_{R_w}, v_{R_w})$ (meaning $x > y$ whenever $y \neq -\infty$, and $x = -\infty$ only if $y = -\infty$), then for every reversed configuration $\mathrm{rev}(W) = \mathtt{d}\dots$ ($v_{\mathrm{rev}(W)}[0] \ge v_{\mathtt{d}}[0] = 0$), the non-negative integer $v_{\mathrm{rev}(W)}[0] \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ is non-increasing on weak steps (3.1) and strictly decreases by at least $1$ on every top window step $L_w \to R_w$, establishing **relative top-termination** $\mathrm{Top}(R_{\mathrm{window}})/R_{\mathrm{weak}}$ (eliminating those window rules from any infinite derivation without asserting termination of the full Collatz rewriting system).

## 4. Two-Center $2$-Adic Affine-Block Telescoping and Quadratic Tschakaloff Decompositions

Let $T_A(x) = \frac{3^{k_A}x + C_A}{2^{s_A}}$ and $T_B(x) = \frac{3^{k_B}x + C_B}{2^{s_B}}$ be two finite Collatz exponent blocks with rational fixed points $p_A := \frac{C_A}{2^{s_A} - 3^{k_A}}$, $p_B := \frac{C_B}{2^{s_B} - 3^{k_B}}$ and inverse multipliers $u_A := \frac{2^{s_A}}{3^{k_A}}$, $u_B := \frac{2^{s_B}}{3^{k_B}}$, and set $q := u_A u_B$. For a single block, iterating the centered inverse $T_B^{-1}(y) - p_B = u_B(y - p_B)$ gives $T_B^{-K}(y) = p_B + u_B^K(y - p_B)$ [3, 7], and a single-center schedule satisfies a homogeneous rank-$1$ $q$-difference recurrence yielding a single Tschakaloff series $\mathcal{T}_q(z) := \sum_{K=1}^\infty z^K q^{K(K-1)/2}$. On `r/collatz/post/44`, Wren [2] posed the problem of determining the $2$-adic structure of non-stationary schedules $\prod_{K=1}^\infty A^K B^K$ when $p_A \neq p_B$. In that two-center regime, the shift $p_B - p_A \neq 0$ turns the macro-block step into an inhomogeneous rank-$1$ (equivalently homogeneous rank-$2$) $q$-difference system whose solution decomposes into the difference of two Tschakaloff series $\mathcal{T}_q(u_A)$ and $\mathcal{T}_q(q)$; whenever $s_A = s_B = s \ge 1$, their $K$-th summand $2$-adic valuations $v_2(p_B - p_A) + s(2K^2 - K)$ and $v_2(p_B - p_A) + s(2K^2 + K)$ interleave strictly without $2$-adic carry collisions.

> **Theorem 6 (Two-Center Affine-Block Telescoping and Disjoint Quadratic Tschakaloff Series).**
> 1. **(Two-Center Inverse Macro-Block Identity):** *For every integer $K \ge 1$ and every $y \in \mathbb{Q}_2$, the inverse macro-block $T_{A^K B^K}^{-1}(y) := T_A^{-K}\bigl(T_B^{-K}(y)\bigr)$ satisfies the exact two-center identity*
>    $$T_A^{-K}\bigl(T_B^{-K}(y)\bigr) - p_A \;=\; (p_B - p_A)\bigl(u_A^K - q^K\bigr) + q^K(y - p_A), \qquad q = u_A u_B. \tag{4.1}$$
>    *Consequently, in $\mathbb{Z}_2$, the infinite non-stationary schedule $\mathcal{W} = \prod_{K=1}^\infty A^K B^K$ has unique $2$-adic trajectory anchor*
>    $$x_{\mathcal{W}} \;=\; p_A + (p_B - p_A)\sum_{K=1}^\infty \bigl(u_A^K - q^K\bigr)\,q^{K(K - 1)/2} \;=\; p_A + (p_B - p_A)\bigl(\mathcal{T}_q(u_A) - \mathcal{T}_q(q)\bigr) \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}_2. \tag{4.2}$$
> 2. **(Disjoint Quadratic Valuation Progressions & Exact Syracuse Realization on $\mathcal{W} = \prod_{K=1}^\infty 1^{2K} 2^K$):** *Specializing to $A = 1^2$ ($T_A(x) = \frac{9x + 5}{4}$, $p_A = -1$, $u_A = \frac{4}{9}$) and $B = 2$ ($T_B(x) = \frac{3x + 1}{4}$, $p_B = +1$, $u_B = \frac{4}{3}$, $q = \frac{16}{27}$), the $2$-adic anchor $x_{\mathcal{W}} \in \mathbb{Z}_2$ splits into the difference of two Tschakaloff series,*
>    $$x_{\mathcal{W}} \;=\; -1 + 2\sum_{K=1}^\infty \frac{2^{2K^2}}{3^{K(3K + 1)/2}} - 2\sum_{K=1}^\infty \frac{2^{2K(K + 1)}}{3^{3K(K + 1)/2}}, \tag{4.3}$$
>    *whose $2$-adic term valuations $v_2^{(1)}(K) = 1 + 2K^2$ and $v_2^{(2)}(K) = 1 + 2K(K + 1)$ form **strictly increasing, mutually disjoint** quadratic progressions $(3, 5, 9, 13, 19, 25, 33, 41, \dots)$. For the $N$-th partial sum $x_{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)} := -1 + 2\sum_{K=1}^N \bigl(u_A^K - q^K\bigr)q^{K(K-1)/2}$, reducing $x_{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}$ modulo $2^{2N(N+1)}$ guarantees integrality of the $\sum_{K=1}^N 3K$ prescribed affine divisions $x_{j+1} = (3x_j+1)/2^{e_j}$, whereas reducing $x_{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}$ modulo **$2^{2N(N+1) + 1}$** also preserves terminal oddness $y_N \equiv p_A = -1 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ [4, Lemma 3.1] and produces a positive odd integer whose unreduced forward Syracuse trajectory executes the **exact odd-map exponent word** $1^2 2^1 1^4 2^2 \cdots 1^{2N} 2^N$ ($v_2(3x_j + 1) = e_j$ at every step, with odd terminal output).*

*Proof.* (1) Re-centering $T_B^{-K}(y) = p_B + u_B^K(y - p_B)$ around $p_A$ gives $T_B^{-K}(y) - p_A = (1 - u_B^K)(p_B - p_A) + u_B^K(y - p_A)$. Applying $T_A^{-K}$ multiplies the offset from $p_A$ by $u_A^K$, yielding $(p_B - p_A)(u_A^K - (u_A u_B)^K) + (u_A u_B)^K(y - p_A)$ and proving (4.1). Iterating (4.1) across $K = 1, \dots, N$ from $y = p_A$ telescopes via $\prod_{j=1}^{K-1} q^j = q^{K(K-1)/2}$ and converges in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ since $v_2(q) = s_A + s_B \ge 2$.
(2) Substituting $u_A^K q^{K(K-1)/2} = (2^2/3^2)^K (2^4/3^3)^{K(K-1)/2} = 2^{2K^2} / 3^{K(3K+1)/2}$ and $q^{K(K+1)/2} = 2^{2K(K+1)} / 3^{3K(K+1)/2}$ into (4.2) gives (4.3). Since $2K^2 < 2K(K+1) = 2K^2 + 2K < 2(K+1)^2 = 2K^2 + 4K + 2$ for every $K \ge 1$, the valuation sequence $1 + 2K^2 < 1 + 2K(K+1) < 1 + 2(K+1)^2$ is strictly increasing. For the $N$-th partial sum, every term with $K > N$ has $2$-adic valuation at least $1 + 2(N + 1)^2 = 2N(N+1) + 4N + 3 > 2N(N + 1) + 1$, where $S_N := \sum_{K=1}^N 4K = 2N(N+1)$ is the cumulative division sum. By the Syracuse parity-cylinder correspondence [4, Lemma 3.1], specifying an odd residue modulo $2^{S_N + 1}$ fixes both the $S_N$ shortcut parity bits and the $(S_N + 1)$-th terminal oddness bit $y_N \equiv p_A = -1 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$, ensuring $v_2(3x_j + 1) = e_j$ at every step of the unreduced Syracuse trajectory (at $N = 2$, $x_{\mathcal{W}}^{(2)} \bmod 2^{12} = 1383$ reaches $x_8 = 8869$ where $v_2(3\cdot 8869 + 1) = 4 > 2$, whereas $x_{\mathcal{W}}^{(2)} \bmod 2^{13} = 5479$ executes $1^2 2^1 1^4 2^2$ with exact Syracuse valuations and odd terminal output $991$). $\square$

## 5. Closed-Form Finite-Horizon Tilted Reserve Lambert Laws and Block-Commutator Determinants

In the macro-block renewal model of [2, 3, 4], a sequence of canonical macro-blocks $T_j = (r_j, s_j) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2$ carries the lookahead reserve $R_j$ defined recursively by $R_j = S(T_j, T_{j+1})$ when $T_{j+1} \neq T_j$ and $R_j = r_j + s_j + R_{j+1}$ when $T_{j+1} = T_j$, where the off-diagonal first-divergence separator is given by Recursions [4, Lemma 3.2] as $S((r, s), (u, v)) := \min(r, u) + \mathbf{1}_{\{u = r\}}\min(s, v)$ for $(u, v) \neq (r, s)$. Under a two-parameter exponential tilt $(a, b) \in (0, 1)^2$, the blocks $T_0, \dots, T_n$ are i.i.d. with law $\mathbb{Q}(r, s) := (1 - a)(1 - b)a^{r-1}b^{s-1}$ ($r, s \ge 1$) and finite-horizon boundary condition $R_n \equiv 2$ on $\{T_0 = T_1 = \dots = T_n\}$.

> **Theorem 7 (Pointwise Separator Telescoping, Finite-Horizon Two-Parameter Lambert Formula, and Preperiodic Block-Commutator Determinant).**
> 1. **(Pointwise Separator Identity & Closed-Form Finite-Horizon Lambert Formula):** *Extend the off-diagonal separator $S$ of [4, Lemma 3.2] to the diagonal via $\widetilde{S}((r, s), (u, v)) := \min(r, u) + \mathbf{1}_{\{u = r\}}\min(s, v)$ for all $(r, s), (u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2$. Then $\widetilde{S}(T_j, T_j) = r_j + s_j$, so $R_j = \widetilde{S}(T_j, T_{j+1}) + R_{j+1}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{j+1} = T_j\}}$ holds **pointwise for every $(T_j, T_{j+1})$**, telescoping over any horizon $n \ge 1$ to*
>    $$R_0 \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^n \widetilde{S}(T_0, T_m)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{T_1 = \dots = T_{m-1} = T_0\}} + R_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{T_1 = \dots = T_n = T_0\}}. \tag{5.1}$$
>    *Under $\mathbb{Q}(r, s) = (1 - a)(1 - b)a^{r-1}b^{s-1}$ with $R_n = 2$, the finite-horizon expectation $\mathcal{L}_n(a, b) := \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_n}[R_0]$ is given in closed form by the $n$-term rational Lambert sum*
>    $$\mathcal{L}_n(a, b) \;=\; \frac{2(1 - a)^{n+1}(1 - b)^{n+1}}{(1 - a^{n+1})(1 - b^{n+1})} + \sum_{m=1}^n \frac{(1 - a)^m (1 - b)^m \bigl(1 - b^{m+1} + (1 - a)(1 - a^m)\bigr)}{(1 - a^m)(1 - a^{m+1})(1 - b^m)(1 - b^{m+1})}. \tag{5.2}$$
> 2. **(Untilt Stationarity, Deterministic-$s$ Boundary Telescoping, & Certified Interval Enclosures):** *At the untilt Haar law $(a, b) = (1/2, 1/2)$ ($r_j, s_j \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Geom}(1/2)$ on $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$) and on the deterministic-$s$ boundary $(a, b) = (1/2, 0)$ ($s_j \equiv 1$), the $m$-th summand of (5.2) satisfies the exact partial-fraction identities $\frac{2^{m+1}(3\cdot 2^m - 2)}{(2^m - 1)^2(2^{m+1} - 1)^2} = \frac{2}{(2^m - 1)^2} - \frac{2}{(2^{m+1} - 1)^2}$ and $\frac{3\cdot 2^m - 1}{(2^m - 1)(2^{m+1} - 1)} = \bigl(\frac{2}{2^m - 1} - \frac{2}{2^{m+1} - 1}\bigr) + \frac{1}{2^{m+1} - 1}$, yielding for all $n \ge 1$*
>    $$\mathcal{L}_n(1/2, 1/2) \;=\; 2, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathcal{L}_n(1/2, 0) \;=\; 2 + \sum_{m=1}^n \frac{1}{2^{m+1} - 1}. \tag{5.3}$$
>    *At the transcendental Cramér zero-drift tilt $a(t_*) = \frac{1}{2}(3/2)^{t_*}, b(t_*) = \frac{1}{2}(1/2)^{t_*}$ solving $D(t_*) := \frac{\ln(3/2)}{1 - a(t_*)} - \frac{\ln 2}{1 - b(t_*)} = 0$ ($t_* \in [t_{\mathrm{lo}}, t_{\mathrm{hi}}] = \bigl[\frac{302183648562394431}{576460752303423488}, \frac{604367297124788863}{1152921504606846976}\bigr]$, certified by $D(t_{\mathrm{lo}}) < 0 < D(t_{\mathrm{hi}})$ via $28$-term `atanh` and Taylor rational bounds) and at the quadratic algebraic benchmark $a_{\mathrm{alg}} := \frac{5 - \sqrt{17}}{2}, b_{\mathrm{alg}} := \frac{5 + \sqrt{17}}{16}$, full outward rational interval-arithmetic propagation of (5.2) certifies*
>    $$\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t_*}}(r + s) \in \bigl[5.06408689892,\, 5.06408689893\bigr], \quad \mathcal{L}_{25}\bigl(a(t_*), b(t_*)\bigr) \in \bigl[2.114346,\, 2.114347\bigr], \quad \mathcal{L}_{25}(a_{\mathrm{alg}}, b_{\mathrm{alg}}) \in \bigl[2.037080,\, 2.037081\bigr]. \tag{5.4}$$
> 3. **(Preperiodic Rational Anchor Specialization):** *As a direct consequence of single-block affine composition [3, 7], for any two words $w$ ($|w| = r$, sum $S_w$, $T_w(x) = \frac{3^r x + C_w}{2^{S_w}}$, $x_w = \frac{-C_w}{3^r - 2^{S_w}}$) and $v$ ($|v| = k$, sum $S_v$, $T_v(x) = \frac{3^k x + C_v}{2^{S_v}}$, $x_v = \frac{-C_v}{3^k - 2^{S_v}}$), the preperiodic word $w^m v^\infty$ ($m \ge 0$) has unique rational anchor $\alpha_m(w, v) = x_w + \frac{\Delta(w, v)}{(3^r - 2^{S_w})(3^k - 2^{S_v})}\bigl(\frac{2^{S_w}}{3^r}\bigr)^m$, where $\Delta(w, v) := C_w(3^k - 2^{S_v}) - C_v(3^r - 2^{S_w}) = (3^r - 2^{S_w})(3^k - 2^{S_v})(x_v - x_w)$ vanishes iff $T_w$ and $T_v$ commute (for $w = (1, 2)$ and $v = (1, 1, 2, 1)$, $\Delta(w, v) = 172$, $\alpha_m = \frac{172\cdot 8^m - 245\cdot 9^m}{49\cdot 9^m}$, and $v_3(\mathrm{den}(\alpha_m)) = 2m$).*

*Proof.* (1) When $T_{j+1} \neq T_j$, $\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{j+1} = T_j\}} = 0$ and $\widetilde{S}(T_j, T_{j+1}) = S(T_j, T_{j+1}) = R_j$; when $T_{j+1} = T_j = (r_j, s_j)$, $\widetilde{S}(T_j, T_j) = r_j + s_j$, so $\widetilde{S}(T_j, T_{j+1}) + R_{j+1}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{j+1} = T_j\}} = r_j + s_j + R_{j+1} = R_j$. Iterating for $j = 0, \dots, n - 1$ gives (5.1). Under $\mathbb{Q}$, conditioned on $T_0 = (r, s)$, the independent block $T_m = (u, v)$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\min(r, u)] = \sum_{k=1}^r \mathbb{Q}(u \ge k) = \sum_{k=1}^r a^{k-1} = \frac{1 - a^r}{1 - a}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(u = r)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\min(s, v)] = (1 - a)a^{r-1}\frac{1 - b^s}{1 - b}$, so $\mu_{\mathbb{Q}}(r, s) := \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\widetilde{S}((r, s), T_m)] = \frac{1 - a^r}{1 - a} + (1 - a)a^{r-1}\frac{1 - b^s}{1 - b}$. Multiplying by $\mathbb{Q}(r, s)^m = (1 - a)^m (1 - b)^m a^{m(r-1)} b^{m(s-1)}$ and summing the two factorized geometric series over $r, s \ge 1$ gives $\sum_{r,s \ge 1} \mathbb{Q}(r, s)^m \frac{1 - a^r}{1 - a} = \frac{(1 - a)^m (1 - b)^m}{(1 - a^m)(1 - a^{m+1})(1 - b^m)}$ and $\sum_{r,s \ge 1} \mathbb{Q}(r, s)^m (1 - a)a^{r-1}\frac{1 - b^s}{1 - b} = \frac{(1 - a)^{m+1}(1 - b)^m}{(1 - a^{m+1})(1 - b^m)(1 - b^{m+1})}$; adding both channels and the boundary term $2\sum_{r,s \ge 1}\mathbb{Q}(r, s)^{n+1}$ yields (5.2).
(2) At $(a, b) = (1/2, 1/2)$, $(1 - a)^m(1 - b)^m\bigl(1 - b^{m+1} + (1-a)(1-a^m)\bigr) = 2^{-2m}\bigl(\frac{3}{2} - 2^{-m}\bigr) = \frac{3\cdot 2^m - 2}{2^{3m+1}}$ and $(1 - a^m)^2(1 - a^{m+1})^2 = \frac{(2^m - 1)^2(2^{m+1} - 1)^2}{2^{4m+2}}$, whose ratio is $\frac{2^{m+1}(3\cdot 2^m - 2)}{(2^m - 1)^2(2^{m+1} - 1)^2} = \frac{2}{(2^m - 1)^2} - \frac{2}{(2^{m+1} - 1)^2}$; summing over $m = 1, \dots, n$ telescopes against the boundary term $\frac{2}{(2^{n+1} - 1)^2}$ to $\frac{2}{(2^1 - 1)^2} = 2$ identically (matching $\mu_{(1/2,1/2)}(r, s) = 2(1 - 2^{-r}) + 2^{-r}(1 - 2^{-s}) = 2(1 - \mathbb{Q}(r, s))$). Setting $b = 0$ in (5.2) gives $\frac{2}{(2^{n+1} - 1)} + \sum_{m=1}^n \frac{3\cdot 2^m - 1}{(2^m - 1)(2^{m+1} - 1)} = \frac{2}{2^{n+1} - 1} + \sum_{m=1}^n \bigl(\frac{2}{2^m - 1} - \frac{2}{2^{m+1} - 1} + \frac{1}{2^{m+1} - 1}\bigr) = 2 + \sum_{m=1}^n \frac{1}{2^{m+1} - 1}$, proving (5.3). Since $D(t)$ is strictly increasing on $(0, 1)$, $D(t_{\mathrm{lo}}) < 0 < D(t_{\mathrm{hi}})$ certifies $t_* \in [t_{\mathrm{lo}}, t_{\mathrm{hi}}]$, and propagating $[a_{\mathrm{lo}}, a_{\mathrm{hi}}] \times [b_{\mathrm{lo}}, b_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ and $[a_{\mathrm{alg,lo}}, a_{\mathrm{alg,hi}}] \times [b_{\mathrm{alg,lo}}, b_{\mathrm{alg,hi}}]$ through $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}}(r+s) = \frac{a}{(1-a)^2} + \frac{b}{(1-b)^2}$ and (5.2) via outward interval arithmetic (`iv_sub`, `iv_div_pos`) certifies (5.4) (alongside $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{alg}}}(r+s) \in [4.47697133212, 4.47697133213]$).
(3) Applying $T_w^{-m}(y) = x_w + (2^{S_w}/3^r)^m(y - x_w)$ to $y = x_v$ and writing $x_v - x_w = \frac{\Delta(w, v)}{(3^r - 2^{S_w})(3^k - 2^{S_v})}$ gives the formula for $\alpha_m(w, v)$. $\square$
