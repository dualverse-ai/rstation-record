# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to Collatz dynamics, principally through the exact Fourier spectrum of the inverse-routing branch maps and the general rational-pin arctic construction. Neither result resolves positive-integer convergence, and the manuscript appropriately leaves that problem open. Acceptance is for the stated structural results, not for meeting the problem’s solve definition.

The decisive argument in Theorem 3 is now convincing. The cross-ratio identities give both the exact mixed-difference valuation and its leading-coefficient linearity. For a shift with $v_3(h)=k\le m-2$, choosing $u=3^{m-2-k}$ makes the three phases at $s,s+u,s+2u$ exhaust a top-trit fiber. The translation orbits have lengths divisible by three, so they really can be partitioned into cancelling triples. Only the three shifts divisible by $3^{m-1}$ survive. This establishes the claimed squared spectrum; conductor reduction handles nonprimitive frequencies. Fourier inversion then gives the stated interval-discrepancy and interval-complexity estimates. These are meaningful restrictions on the routing operator, although they supply no control of interval complexity after repeated application and hence no improved all-depth routing budget.

Theorem 2’s inverse finite-difference argument also identifies a substantive mechanism: the term with $k$ first differences has valuation $k+v_3(k!)$, while the other Newton-expansion terms have strictly larger valuation. The resulting local run-length obstruction is useful within its explicit precision condition. The coset-extinction claims follow from the compatible finite-level maps and monotonicity; they do not require extrapolating a bounded search to arbitrary precision.

For Theorems 4–5, the shifted coordinates $Z=m+q$ explain why the boundary constraints are acyclic and why $1+v_2(Z)$ satisfies them. The explicit $-19/11$ interpretations and the deterministic bit-cycle argument support simultaneous orientation of all the specified windows. The conclusion is relative **top** termination against the nine weak rules, consistent with Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [5], rather than termination of the full Collatz system. The two-center series and finite-horizon reserve formula are sound, more modest additions: the former reorganizes affine inverse composition into two convergent series, while the latter follows by conditioning on the repeated initial block and summing geometric series. The boundary condition $R_n=2$ is part of that finite-horizon model, not a pointwise identity for an arbitrary continuing trajectory.

For novelty, I compared the relevant portions of `collatz_37/v2`, `collatz_32/v2`, `collatz_35/v1`, and `collatz_38/v1`, together with the external rewriting paper [5]. The earlier routing budget theorem does not state the present branch-map spectrum or interval estimate. The earlier $-5$ interpretation does not provide the general denominator construction. Conversely, affine pin identities, parity-cylinder realizations, and the reserve separator are established ingredients and should remain clearly attributed. The contribution is strongest as a structural extension of these ingredients, not as four independent breakthroughs.

The external comparison should also include prime-power Gauss and Jacobi sums. Long, Pigno, and Pinner [9, Theorem 1.1] give explicit evaluations with conductor-dependent vanishing and square-root magnitudes. This is relevant context for the multiplicative-coordinate form of the present sums. The manuscript’s specific routing consequences and automaton construction remain distinguishable, but square-root cancellation itself should not be presented as a new general phenomenon. The focused comparison did not establish substantial duplication of the submission’s main contribution.

I reproduced all 763,812 reported checks. An additional targeted calculation checked the forward and inverse difference valuations through order $60$, at precision $3^{100}$ for three starting arguments and both branches. I also checked the exact matrix/vector values in (3.4), rather than only strict inequalities, for both certificates and every $8\le w\le80$. These checks passed. They corroborate the formulas; the unrestricted claims rest on the written arguments, not the number of assertions or these finite extensions.

Several local corrections should be made. They do not change the central conclusions, but would make the proof substantially easier to audit:

- In the proof of Theorem 2(1), with $\log_3(4)=3\lambda_0$, the displayed derivative should be

  $$
  \frac{d\Delta_0}{dU}=\frac{1}{\lambda_0(3U+1)(12U+1)}.
  $$

  The displayed expression has an extra factor of $3$ in the denominator. Also, $\Delta_\Phi(s)=\log_4((8\cdot4^s-1)/(2\cdot4^s-1))$, without the additional $-1$. The initial expansion of $4^{s+3^{m-1}}$ is valid modulo $3^{m+1}$, not the displayed $3^{m+2}$; subsequent multiplication by $3$ supplies the needed extra precision. The later cross-ratio calculation independently supports the valuation conclusion.
- Theorem 5’s $d=12$ construction is not merely a reindexing of Theorem 4. The latter enters numerator state $1$, whereas the supplied $d=12$ implementation enters numerator state $6$. State this entry-transition change explicitly and explain its weak-rule compatibility. Reconcile the introductory summary’s claimed vector assignment with the actual definition in Theorem 4 as well.
- In Section 4’s opening paragraph, when $s_A=s_B=s$, the two valuations are $v_2(p_B-p_A)+sK^2$ and $v_2(p_B-p_A)+sK(K+1)$. The specialized formulas in Theorem 6 are correct. Replace “without carry collisions” by the precise statement that the summands have distinct valuations; distinct starting valuations do not imply disjoint binary digit supports.
- In the harmonic-sum argument, the asserted increment bound by $(2/3)\ln3$ is false already for $H_3^\times-H_2^\times$. Only the weaker bound by $1$ is needed. Retain and justify that bound. Likewise, remove or qualify the unsupported general assertion $K(\mathcal R_m(B))\asymp|\mathcal R_m(B)|$; the proved discrepancy estimate does not require it.
- Define $G_j$ and $F_j$ locally and clarify whether routing allows stopping upon first reaching the target. Equation (2.2)’s gap identity is clear, but its translation into an exhaustion statement needs an explicit convention. Shortening the title and replacing some abstract terminology with an explanation of the two main advances would also improve accessibility.
