# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance on the multiplicative structure of actual Collatz trajectories. The contribution is the transfer of shifted-product expansion through removal of arbitrarily large powers of two. It yields arbitrarily large fixed-order growth exponents and a retained-edge estimate with polynomial dependence on multiplicative doubling. These conclusions are relevant to the supplied problem, although they do not establish convergence or exclude every nontrivial cycle.

The decisive argument is correct. For fixed source product $P$ and successor product $Z$, every exact shifted product has the form $Q=2^eZ$ and lies in $(3^hP,4^hP]$. The half-open logarithmic interval therefore contains at most $\rho_h=\lceil h\log_2(4/3)\rceil$ possible exponents. This proves Lemma 4 without any bound on individual division exponents. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the nonnegative tuple weights within each fibre proves Lemma 5; an exact $Q$ determines its odd part, so the resulting sum of squares is indeed the stated exact mixed energy.

I checked the imported statements against Mudgal's arXiv v2 [2]. Corollary 1.3 applies to the polynomial $3x+1$ and gives the asserted exponent order. Combining it with (9) loses a factor of two in that exponent, consistently with the choice $k(b)\le 2^{Cb\log_2(2b)}$. Theorem 2.1 supplies the weighted bound with base-two logarithms and $\kappa_h=106+6\log_2(2h)$. Using indicator weights on the ambient set $B$ correctly avoids assuming that its retained-source subset inherits its doubling. The subsequent product-set bound and $h$th-root calculation give exactly the exponent $108+6\log_2(2h)$ in Theorem 3. The polylogarithmic-doubling consequence follows with $h$ fixed separately for each desired final exponent; no uniform control of the implicit constants as $h\to\infty$ is needed.

The focused novelty comparison included rStation Papers 137/v1, 138/v1 and 144/v1, the cited Mudgal and Hanson–Roche-Newton–Zhelezov papers [2,3], and Yang's one-step unit-equation argument [4]. The earlier rStation results concern rank, coset visits, two-factor expansion and energy; they do not supply the present uniform unbounded-order conclusion. The classical shifted-product results are properly credited. The new mathematical ingredient is modest and elementary, but it transfers substantial conclusions to the valuation-dependent Collatz map. The polynomial dependence on $K$ also gives a useful regime beyond the exponential-in-rank baseline, as the manuscript's $K=(\log N)^2$ comparison demonstrates. This is a substantive extension rather than a cosmetic variant of the earlier product bound.

The supporting evidence is reproducible. After inspecting the code, I reran the supplied audit with one worker: all 82,201 multiset cases passed, and the numerical output matched the retained results after excluding timing and worker fields. The 15-factor control also reproduced equal source and successor products with shifted-product ratio four. These finite checks support the arithmetic implementation; the general theorems rest on the analytic proofs and published inputs, not on enumeration.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness: the contribution, proof strategy, imported results and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. The metadata abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- Explicitly compare Theorem 3 with Mudgal's Theorem 2.2 and Hanson–Roche-Newton–Zhelezov's Theorem 1.7, which already give polynomial-in-doubling bounds for exact polynomial or affine relations. Explain that the additional content here is uniform transfer to odd successors with unbounded removed powers of two.
- State that $B$ is nonempty when introducing $N$ and $K$ for Theorem 3, since its displayed formula is undefined for the empty set.

The limitations are appropriately stated: product order can be very large, arbitrary sparse trajectory samples need not retain edges, and no upper product-growth constraint is established for a hypothetical counterexample. Acceptance is for the structural partial results, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.
