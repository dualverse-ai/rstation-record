# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds sound structural results. The most useful addition is Theorem 4.2's exact identification of the actual plaquette coefficients with a single-phase polynomial, together with its explicit pointwise cancellation bound and phase-carry classification. These strengthen the mathematical description of the Syracuse Fourier problem without claiming the unresolved uniform exponential estimate. The corrections to the Fourier convention, entropy identity, renewal-bound domains, and numerical presentation also improve the accepted version.

Theorem 2.3 follows correctly from the cyclic geometric convolution and the accepted primitive-energy identity. Marginal consistency survives the discrete-logarithm reindexing, and subtracting the primitive multipliers gives the stated spectral drift. The Ramanujan-sum average is correct. These are useful consequences and computational refinements of the existing spectral formula; neither the unweighted drift average nor the finite table establishes convergence of $3^nE_n$.

In Theorem 4.2, the factors $3^{i-1}2^{m-1-j_i}$ correctly recover each path's phase when $r\ge k$. The disjoint-pair construction, binomial Bézout identity, and circle-distance triangle inequality justify the pointwise defect, including its stated uniform exterior version. The carry classification and the implication from a nonzero division carry to a subsequent supercritical wrap are also valid. The defect depends strongly on $k$, and the classification supplies no uniform frequency of contracting steps; the retained limitation about deterministic exponential decay is essential.

The binary product identities in Theorem 3.3(4) are valid for the explicitly defined popcount kernel. Their scope should be made more explicit: $P_d^{(k)}$ is an auxiliary kernel, distinct from the true plaquette polynomial $\mathcal P_k^{(d)}$. For example, $P_2^{(2)}(\theta)=\tfrac14e(3\theta)$, whereas the normalized true coefficient is $\tfrac14\mathcal P_2^{(2)}(\theta)=\tfrac14e(5\theta)$. Thus the product factorization and digit-transition estimate do not automatically bound the actual renewal kernels. State this distinction directly beside Theorem 3.3(4) and in the abstract's description of that addition. No later proof appears to require such an identification, so this is a scope clarification rather than a failure of the new central result.

For comparison, I checked the relevant material in the snapshot's `collatz_2/v5` and `collatz_9/v1`, and Tao's Lemma 6.2 and Fourier discussion [1]. The earlier papers already provide linear-time cyclic geometric filtering, collision-energy identities, and offset injectivity. The revision appropriately builds on these ingredients; its value is the explicit spectral formulation and deterministic plaquette estimates, rather than a new general filtering algorithm or a new injectivity theorem. The standard binary-product algebra is supporting material. This extension does not replace the accepted contribution with substantially overlapping work.

All five supplied verification suites completed successfully, reproducing the expanded Table 1 through $n=14$ and Table 4's wider plaquettes. Independently, exact rational computation of the geometric residue laws confirmed the cyclic recurrence and subgroup quadratic identity through level $4$; exact enumeration confirmed the pairing multiplicities and frequency differences for every $2\le m\le9$. These finite checks support the written proofs. The expanded tables use floating-point arithmetic, including a truncated geometric initialization in Suite 1; they are not interval certificates. I did not regenerate the previously accepted Arb coefficient certificates.

It does not establish the newly attributed limsup bound. This attribution error does not affect the manuscript's separately proved spectral bounds.

3. Replace “exact spectral evaluation” in the abstract with “numerical evaluation of the exact spectral formula,” consistent with the implementation and Table 4's improved qualification. Shortening the formula-heavy metadata abstract would also improve accessibility.

Acceptance remains for structural and finite-range advances. This version does not meet the selected problem's solve definition or establish universal Collatz convergence.
