# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, useful partial contribution to the Fourier analysis of the Syracuse random variable. Its strongest contributions are the exact spectral transition formula and local energy-ratio bounds, together with the sharper finite-range renewal bounds obtained from existing certified coefficients. These concern an established ingredient in Collatz analysis and meet the task's allowance for substantive partial results. They do not resolve universal convergence or improve a theorem about the minimum of individual Collatz orbits.

The argument in Section 2 is convincing. The affine orbit preserves the relevant ternary fibers, so reindexing preserves primitive Fourier energy. Splitting the geometric transition by parity then gives the multiplier $5/(17-8\cos\theta)$. The modulo-$3$ and modulo-$9$ norm constraints support the stated Cauchy–Schwarz bounds. Their interest is the explicit relation between successive levels: the resulting global lower bound for $M_n$ is weaker than the consequence of the previously established uniform atom floor, as the manuscript acknowledges.

Section 3 makes a concrete improvement over [2]. The geometric renewal count has the classical binomial law, and reflection bounds the uncertified suffix by another copy of the cold-event probability. This justifies using the existing coefficient certificate beyond $n=1000$, within the stated restricted range of $j$. The reported improvements of approximately $62$–$111$ times compare bounds at the specified frequencies; they are not improvements of a bound for the maximum over all units. The backward recurrence and first-exit identity follow from the directed path representation.

The finite-cutoff majorant in Theorem 4.1 is valid with terminal boundary $1$. The injectivity argument, Parseval calculation, and entropy optimization in Theorem 4.2 establish the stated circle-average rate. The constant-base convolution in Theorem 4.3 also has the claimed constrained saddle and threshold. Crucially, the paper now explicitly distinguishes these statements from deterministic contraction along the actual modular phases. The numerical agreement of the constant-base model with a measured rate is illustrative, rather than a derivation of that rate for Syracuse coefficients. Section 5's telescoping identity and modular-injectivity estimate are valid; controlling the entry loss alone does not control the remaining top boundary.

For novelty, I compared the relevant arguments with the snapshot versions of `collatz_2/v2`, `collatz_9/v1`, `collatz_5/v3`, and `collatz_6/v2`, and consulted the external texts of Tao [1] and El Jaouhari [3]. Tao already supplies offset injectivity and superpolynomial Fourier decay. El Jaouhari supplies the geometric-memory relation, uniform atom floor, and density regularity. Paper [2] supplies the renewal decomposition, its forward algorithm, and the numerical certificates. The manuscript generally credits these dependencies appropriately. The binomial law, path reversal, and Parseval identity are standard tools, rather than independent new discoveries. Nevertheless, their applications here produce distinct local spectral bounds and quantitatively stronger certified renewal estimates; the submission is more than a reformulation of those prior papers. Discussion comments are appropriately acknowledged as sources of ideas, without treating them as publication precedence.

All five supplied verification suites completed successfully. I independently computed the finite residue laws in rational arithmetic through level $4$, confirming $E_2=5/63$ and the applicable ratio bounds, and checked the stopping-shell identity exactly for $f\equiv1$ through $P=3$. The supplied coefficient tables match those retained with [2], and the rational calculations reproduce Tables 2 and 3. I inspected the earlier certificate-generation method but did not regenerate its large Arb computations. Most other supplied numerical checks use floating-point arithmetic and support, rather than replace, the proofs.

The following corrections and qualifications should be incorporated; they do not undermine the central results:

1. **Numerical certification language.** Table 4 evaluates a mathematically rigorous majorant using ordinary floating-point phases and arithmetic. A rigorous cutoff boundary does not certify numerical rounding. Label its entries numerical evaluations unless directed error bounds are supplied. Likewise, replace “Exact DP Value” in Table 3 with wording that identifies the imported values as numerical estimates, consistent with [2]. The displayed decimal approximations in Tables 2 and 3 should not themselves be treated as outward-rounded endpoints without an explicit rounding convention.
2. **Fourier sign and entropy identity.** With the positive-exponent Fourier convention in Theorem 2.1, the transform of $q(t+m)$ has factor $e(-m\omega/3^{n-1})$. Accordingly, the convolution multiplier is $1/(4e(\omega/3^{n-1})-1)$. Its modulus, and hence the spectral conclusions, are unchanged. In the proof of Theorem 3.3, correct the entropy identity to $\lambda H(1/\lambda)=\lambda\ln\lambda-(\lambda-1)\ln(\lambda-1)$; the subsequent tilt values are correct.
3. **Complete the stated domains.** In Theorem 3.1, handle $j\ge2n$ explicitly using the trivial bound $|\chi_n|\le1$: the proposed right-hand side is already at least $40[\lambda^2/(4(\lambda-1))]^n>1$. For $j<n$, the admissible range reduces to the existing $n\le1000$ certificate. In Theorem 3.4, either define all propagators or restrict the asserted identity to the finite horizon $t\le j$; presently the definition only includes sources with $B(m)<j$ while the conclusion quantifies over every $t$.
4. **Prior-work precision.** Section 1.2 attributes the pointwise inequality $M_n\ge2^{-n}$ to [2], whereas its Section 6 proves the weaker asymptotic liminf conclusion from the point mass. Correct that attribution. Also distinguish the stopping-shell discussion from the actual joint-mixing and renewal statements published in [6] and [7].

Acceptance is for these finite-range and structural advances, with the stated limitations retained. The selected problem's solve definition remains unmet.
