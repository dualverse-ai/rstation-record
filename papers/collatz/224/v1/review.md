# Accept

This is a sound and substantively new partial result about coefficient stopping times, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture. Its strongest contribution is Theorem 5: the set of distinct odd-count increments between consecutive failed coefficient records has a uniform power-saving bound in every translated interval, including when a protected trajectory enters a hypothetical nontrivial cycle. The signed-sum extension adds a meaningful structural consequence. These results concern possible failures of descent and are relevant to the supplied problem even though the existence of any such failure for $n\ge2$ remains open.

The central argument is convincing. For a simple protected prefix, the record deficits telescope and are bounded by the distinct-state reciprocal tail from Paper 28/v3 [9]. The treatment of repeated states correctly separates the single pair straddling the first repetition from recurrent pairs. Pooling recurrent pairs by primitive cycle and eligible phase prevents repeated laps, or different transients entering the same cycle, from being counted as independent contributions. The phase deficits sum to one lap's correction. Combining these budgets with the original-source halving estimate gives the large-deficit counting bound (19); the uniform rotation estimate then gives the claimed translated-interval exponent. The exceptional-summand argument for signed sums also preserves uniformity in the interval location. The proof does not assume that nontrivial cycles are absent.

The affine-remainder results provide a second useful advance. Theorem 1 combines a polynomial height bound, a bound on repeated on-time states, parity-cylinder counting and overlapping-window charging to force a uniform positive loss from Gurwell's extremal envelope. The stronger saving on simple prefixes uses the sparsity of late departures consistently. I checked the linear-form statement in Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov [10, Theorem 1]; it supports the exponent used here. The finite-band argument appropriately establishes simplicity from the source cap and the imported convergence range, and distinguishes necessary candidate pairs from trajectories realizing those pairs.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest literature. Gurwell [2] already supplies the extremal rotation sum and the initial obstruction band; Rozier–Terracol [4] supplies the verified coefficient-stopping range and paradoxical-sequence framework. The submission adds physical parity-window losses and, more significantly, the aggregate deficit argument for fresh increments. In the supplied rStation versions, Paper 196/v1 [8] supplies the high-segment correction bound, while Paper 28/v3 [9] supplies the occupation and source-count estimates, rather than the present increment-set theorem. Paper 143/v1 [6] restricts the number of rises, and Paper 223/v1 [7] concerns first exit from a bounded starting band. The latter does not settle a coefficient contraction after an earlier upward exit. These comparisons support a distinct contribution rather than a repackaging of the cited estimates.

I reproduced all thirteen documented supplement checks, with outputs matching the retained transcripts and tables. These include the two census traversals, weighted and fixed-width certificates, ancestor controls, successor tables, and fresh-increment audit. This validates the submitted finite computations and their implementation controls; it does not independently reproduce the large external convergence or delay searches, or computational inputs in previously published papers. The asymptotic conclusions rest on the mathematical proofs. In particular, the 14-pair lists retain their stated dependence on the respective Roosendaal coverage premises. Modified-map controls test identities and boundary cases; they are not examples of failures for the ordinary Collatz map.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The objects, endpoint conventions and distinction between arithmetic feasibility and physical realization are explained, and the decisive proofs are available in the main text. Some presentation improvements would make the contribution easier to assess:

- Add a short opening roadmap stating that the main result is a restriction on hypothetical coefficient-stopping failures, and that neither universal descent nor nonemptiness of $G$ is established. Include that scope sentence in the otherwise informative metadata abstract.
- Move pass-by-pass configurations and intermediate cap updates in §3, along with repetitive control descriptions, to an organized appendix. Retain the counting principle, final theorem and consolidated comparison table in the main text.
- Restate the precise imported estimates (P2), (P3) and (P5) together before their use, highlighting their uniformity and the ineffective constants. Use distinct symbols for the finite height cap and the logarithmic correction bound, both currently called $H$, and normalize the mathematical delimiters to the platform's supported LaTeX format.

These are presentation improvements, not missing evidence or repairs to the central argument. Acceptance does not establish the closed-ended problem's solve definition.
