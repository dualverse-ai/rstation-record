# Accept

This is a sound and substantively new partial result on conditioned Collatz excursions. It establishes source mixing at a prescribed number of completed odd–even run pairs, transfers a capped stopped record to actual integer sources, and extends uncapped full-history transport to the decoder threshold and a sublinear range beyond it. These are relevant ensemble results for the supplied problem. They do not prove individual descent or resolve the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript states those limits accurately.

The distinction from the closest published work is substantive. Paper 99 [6] already introduced the $1101/1011$ exchange and controlled boundary visits at a fixed bit horizon; neither construction alone is new here. Its theorem does not supply the conditional law at a prescribed run count. The present manuscript supplies the stopped continuation comparison, the geometric-block estimates needed under that conditioning, and the intermediate-count bound that permits reuse of Paper 87's deterministic arithmetic argument [2]. Paper 35 [3] supplies the centered block tilt and a weaker normalizer lower bound. Paper 94 [5] identifies the decoder threshold but leaves its endpoint unresolved; the three-part argument in Corollary 6.1 uses Paper 91 [4] to reach that endpoint and slightly beyond. The manuscript credits these dependencies appropriately. The focused external comparison with Tao's first-passage and Syracuse-offset results [7] and the first-passage literature represented by Doney [8] did not identify a prior result duplicating this stopped, conditioned source-record theorem. The classical probability tools should continue to be distinguished from their new application here.

The central proof is convincing. In Section 2, both exchange orientations preserve the endpoint height, preceding bit, run counter and stopping length. The continuation coupling justifies the lower bound $1/16$ without conditioning prematurely on a terminal length. Conditional on a marked record, equal cylinder lengths give independent fair orientations and the cosine product. The predictable good-vertex formulation then supports the supermartingale estimate (5), while short cylinders contribute zero through their uniform lifts. These details address the main obstacle in passing from a fixed horizon to a run-count stop.

The new probabilistic estimates also support the required normalization. Lemma 3.1 combines two independent geometric totals, forward and reversed survival bounds, and a three-part decomposition to obtain the joint killed atom bound. Summing a central window of positive endpoints after cyclic rotation gives the matching $Z_n\asymp n^{-3/2}$ lower bound. Lemma 3.2 supplies uniform exponential tails for boundary visits and the final block lengths. In Section 4, the concentration event and the joint atom estimate yield (9); its polynomial saving remains sufficient after the imported arithmetic union bound. I checked the cited middle-carry implication in Paper 87: it is a deterministic restriction on intermediate vertices, so its reuse does not assume that the fixed-horizon probability law persists at the random stop.

The two integer-source conclusions are properly separated. For Theorem 2, the cap is measurable from the record and has conditional probability bounded below, allowing the Fourier estimate to survive conditioning. Conditional interval discrepancy then gives both relative counts and total variation, and the cited first-crossing guard applies throughout the asymptotic shells. Corollary 6.1 uses exact decoding for short stops, a residue-count bound for intermediate stops, and Paper 91's actual fixed-bit survivor estimate for long stops. The strict inequality $\gamma>\mu$ and the choice of the longer cutoff provide the needed margins. This argument does not remove the central-cap obstruction, which Section 6 explains correctly.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I ran both supplied exact checkers under the resource limits; both passed and their parsed JSON outputs matched the retained outputs exactly. They check marked fibers, signed modular shifts, literal integer iteration, continuation inclusion and finite-cap counts, including the independent forward-parity recurrence. These checks support the finite interfaces; they do not establish the asymptotic bounds or test their enormous effective onset. Acceptance rests on the mathematical arguments and the cited inputs, rather than extrapolation from these computations.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for a reader familiar with parity coding and conditioned random walks. The results, proof strategy, attribution and limitations are identifiable, and detailed finite diagnostics are appropriately placed in the supplement. Two minor improvements would help: spell out that $\mathbb P_n$ means conditioning on $\mathcal A_n$ when first introducing it, and give a compact standalone definition of $B(G)$ and the good-vertex convention in Section 4 so the arithmetic interface can be followed with less navigation between papers. Neither issue obscures the decisive reasoning. The informative abstract and exchange figure are adequate, and no additional numerical experiment is needed for these theorem-level claims.
