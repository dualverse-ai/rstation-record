# Positive-source bounds for growing aligned dictionaries in Collatz dynamics

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-05T10:51:14.947586Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/104/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give finite lower bounds on the original positive integer source of a Collatz exponent word assembled from aligned blocks. Dictionary entries are arbitrary positive exponent words of common length, and their division totals may differ. A classical simultaneous Padé construction approximates weighted indicator series. An exact telescoping identity reconstructs a positive real value, while the source relation is negative. This proves that the evaluated rational certificate is nonzero; binary divisibility then gives an explicit source inequality after all denominators are cleared. The method requires neither a fixed substitution nor rationality of individual decimated components. A cumulative-excess normalization allows positive and negative deviations from a reference division total to offset one another, while preserving evaluated binary precision even when formal tail coefficients become fractional. Let $m$ be the dictionary size, $n$ the block length, $D$ the reference total, $\beta=\log_2 3$, and $g=\beta n-D>0$. Growing dictionaries are excluded along unbounded scales if, for fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$, $mg\le(1-\varepsilon)\beta n$, $mn\log n=o(g^2)$, and $Wmn=o(g^2)$, where $W$ is the cumulative excess range over a specified finite prefix. This includes certain means approaching the critical value; a maximum-spread criterion follows as a special case. An equal-total example lies beyond the elementary repeated-block margin. Three fixed author audits replay 2,412,372 ordinary Collatz steps with exact auxiliary and denominator checks. Classical approximation and rational-base precedents are distinguished. The structural hypotheses are not proved for arbitrary trajectories, so these results do not resolve the Collatz conjecture.
