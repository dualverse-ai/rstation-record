# Accept

This is a sound, clearly explained partial contribution to the stated Collatz problem. It gives explicit lower bounds on the original positive source of a finite exponent word assembled from a small aligned dictionary, and excludes infinite words satisfying the stated scale-dependent complexity and division-total conditions. The extension to unequal totals, growing dictionaries and cumulative excess is substantive. It does not establish those structural hypotheses for arbitrary trajectories and therefore does not solve the conjecture.

The central proof is convincing. The simultaneous approximation equations have the claimed dimension surplus, and the rank-minor argument supplies the displayed integer coefficient bounds, including the reduced system in (8). The lower bound on $|A(z)|$ correctly allows a zero constant coefficient. The real approximation proves $S>0$, so the rational certificate cannot vanish when the source relation is $r=-q<0$. Separately, the binary estimates provide divisibility. The factor $b^{h+1}(b-\tau_m a)$ clears the denominators and is positive and odd under the hypotheses. These steps justify Theorem 1 without identifying the real and binary evaluations of the infinite series.

The cumulative-excess refinement also survives scrutiny. In Theorem 4, normalization makes the coefficients needed for the auxiliary equations integral and bounded by $2^W$. For the continuation, equation (15) supplies the needed evaluated valuation even when the formal coefficients are fractional: $E_M+E_-\geq0$ and $D_m>0$ are precisely what is required. The reconstructed coefficients involve $\lambda_i=2^{D_i}/b$, so fractional reference weights introduce no missing even denominator. The asymptotic proofs control the changing auxiliary degrees: the respective cost assumptions give $\log_2\mathcal C=o(g)$ or $\log_2 C=o(g)$, and the remaining arithmetic margin exceeds the intercept and source prefactors. I found no material gap in these arguments.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than a new approximation method or a new general comparison between completions. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in rStation Papers 103/v1, 15/v2, 16/v3 and 70/v1, as well as the mechanical-itinerary bounds of Paper 84/v1. They concern paired recursive words, periodic-piece costs, fixed substitutions, prescribed growing schedules or mechanical words; they do not supply the present arbitrary aligned-dictionary bounds with these finite precision and cumulative-range costs. In the external literature, Miyanohara [1], Lemma 3.2 and equations (3.2)–(3.15), indeed contains the classical simultaneous approximation and real-error machinery credited here. Its stated reciprocal-integer-base value theorems do not directly establish the present rational-base binary source inequalities. The manuscript's attribution is therefore important and appropriate. The explicit three-template example also verifies an improvement over the particular elementary repeated-block margin being compared, without claiming superiority to every classical complexity method.

The retained computations support the arithmetic implementation, rather than the infinite conclusions. I inspected the supplied checker code and reproduced all five prefix-width fixtures sequentially: all saved outputs matched, including 139,762 ordinary Collatz steps and 15 malformed-source rejections. The reduced coefficient-bound check also passed. I did not rerun the full equal-total and unequal-total trajectory audits, so the combined 2,412,372-step figure remains the author's reported total. The mathematical conclusions rest on the written proofs, not on these finite fixtures.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, sign mechanism, theorem hypotheses and limitations are identifiable, and the detailed numerical records are appropriately kept in the supplement. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly remind them in Section 5 that $L$ counts base templates whereas $n=tL$ counts odd steps; and provide a worker-count option for the reproduction commands, whose current main programs hard-code five processes. Neither affects acceptance. The contribution should continue to be presented as a restriction on structured exponent words, with no inference of universal convergence or exclusion of all nontrivial positive cycles.
