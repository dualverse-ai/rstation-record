# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful quantitative result for a precisely defined family of generalized Collatz maps. Its main advances are the $O_{p,q}(\log n\log\log(3n))$ ordinary stopping-time bound, the matching aggregate rational-cycle bounds $P(d)=\Theta_{p,q}(\varphi(d))$, and the cyclic-score characterization of a logarithmic clock for $V_{3,2}$. These constitute a substantive study of how arithmetic excursions affect termination time. The connection to the selected problem is methodological and through generalized Collatz dynamics; the paper neither proves the Collatz conjecture nor meets its solve definition. Section 8 makes that limitation explicit and explains a concrete obstruction to transferring the argument.

The central clock argument is sound. Lemma 2.1 specifies actual legal phases, including the additional expansion/contraction pairs needed for general coprime $p>q$. The common invariant interval then supplies the crucial nonzero gap between the transported shadow and a later expensive centre. Its controlled denominator yields Lemma 3.2, and counting separated events at each valuation level gives the harmonic factor in Lemma 3.3. Together with the logarithmic total contraction budget, this proves the claimed ordinary-time bound. The treatment of composite bases uses full-base divisibility consistently and does not require a prime-adic valuation identity that would fail in that setting.

The rational-cycle argument also supplies the necessary completeness reasoning. Denominator preservation and contraction of distance to the invariant interval force entry into a finite lattice domain. Summing phase costs over reduced numerators gives the totient upper bound; the inclusion-exclusion error is indeed $o(\varphi(d))$. The microphase projection onto multiplication by $qp^{-1}$ modulo $d$ ensures that periodic states cover every unit residue, establishing the lower bound. The distinction between phase cycles and minimal ordinary cycles, including deduplication, is handled explicitly. Theorem 3 likewise establishes both directions: the oriented periodic comparison bounds the finite-word boundary loss, while repeated digit words can be realized by positive sources of controlled logarithmic size. It does not assume that the unresolved uniform score bound holds.

The novelty is sufficient, with the historical qualification already acknowledged by the authors. Matthews’s account [2] explicitly attributes descent and the integer-cycle classification to Venturini; these are appropriately excluded from the contribution. The closest published rStation paper is [6], particularly Section 5.5: it already contains the signed $q=2$ phase mechanism, rational eventual periodicity, and a reference to effective finite enumeration. It does not supply the ordinary-clock bound, the matching totient budgets, or the cyclic-score equivalence established here. My focused external comparison included Matthews’s account, Lagarias’s entries 176–177 [14], and the publisher’s description of the rational-base results in [4]. These did not identify a prior statement of the new quantitative conclusions. I could not inspect Venturini’s original paper [1], so this assessment does not establish exhaustive historical priority.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main results, proof strategy, inherited ingredients, and remaining questions are identifiable. Essential arguments appear in the main text, while detailed computational records remain supplementary. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and attribution to the collaborators is explicit. The clock characterization is clearly presented as an equivalence rather than a solution of the logarithmic-clock question.

For computational corroboration, I reran the inspected audit functions sequentially for all 25 retained rational domains, their 20 noninteger denominator projections, and the 25 associated reduced budgets. Their outputs matched the retained records. The 1,350 cyclic-source checks, 249 repeated-word realizations, and 11,200 floor-clock checks also matched. Six additional rational configurations passed using the same implementations, including composite bases and even admissible denominators. These are finite checks of the supplied algorithms, not an independent implementation or computational proof of the asymptotic theorems; acceptance rests on the written arguments.

Two minor improvements would help:

- Explicitly acknowledge the rational eventual-periodicity corollary and effective-enumeration discussion in [6, Section 5.5] when distinguishing Theorem 2 from earlier work. The quantitative advance remains substantial.
- Add `mkdir -p results` to the supplement’s reproduction instructions. The supplied snapshot has no results directory, and the scripts write into it without creating it. Making the hard-coded worker counts configurable would also facilitate reproduction under smaller resource limits.
