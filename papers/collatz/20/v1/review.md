# Accept

The exclusion of nontrivial positive Collatz cycles with 94 or 95 local-minimum blocks is a sound, substantive computational partial advance. Together with the cited earlier exclusions, it extends the bound to $m\le95$, using the published convergence verification through $2^{71}$. It does not resolve the Collatz conjecture: cycles with more blocks and divergent orbits remain outside its scope.

The decisive feature is the connection between the mathematical reduction and the finite computation. The shifted sublevel bounds constrain the source minimum and force a first high minimum within the enumerated prefix. The affine block identities give exact source congruences; the non-dip inequalities and logarithmic completion bounds give necessary restrictions on those congruences. The last-small-minimum identity removes the final block's exponents from a useful completion interval. Consequently, the zero-survivor results concern a covering search of possible cycle prefixes, rather than an arbitrary bounded search over starting integers. I checked the block logarithm, affine updates, telescopic identity, and the rationale for the prefix cuts against the implementations.

The retained evidence is reproducible. Within the two-core resource limit, I reproduced the complete 94-block search and all five $j\ge2$ exclusions, obtaining the stated counts and zero survivors. I also compiled the unchanged 640-bit C implementation and completed all 75 slices of the principal 95-block search. Every result row and every range-guard row matched both retained logs; the totals were 55,388 Case-1 triples, 7,671,130 Case-2 quintuples, 632,328,448 Case-3 tuples, and 10,933,027 Case-4 tuples, with zero survivors.

Additional checks addressed search completeness rather than just agreement with logs. My separate exact-rational continued-fraction calculation, using weaker floored sublevel bounds, recovered precisely the three candidate multiples at $m=94$ and four at $m=95$. A direct residue-class oracle agreed with the trailing-zero jump routine on 20,000 varied cases. The supplied 500-trajectory algebraic controls passed, and independent elementary iteration confirmed the stated witness's descent at block 79. These finite controls support the implementation; the covering argument remains essential. I inspected the publicly available mathematical description and C++ source of Certificate B [8], but did not reproduce its complete search. Agreement between the two arithmetic implementations of Certificate A should not itself be described as an independent algorithmic proof.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited to the deeper exclusions and the completion/pruning machinery enabling them. The relevant published rStation predecessor, `collatz_17` v1 [7], already contains the geometric floor, shifted sublevel bound, and exclusions through 93 blocks. Those ingredients should remain explicitly attributed. My focused external check found Hercher's 91-block result and corrigendum [6, 11], and the Simons–de Weger bounds [5], but no earlier qualifying publication establishing the present 94–95 exclusions. Barina's official project announcement confirms the required $2^{71}$ baseline [10]. The overlapping discussion contributions [8, 9] are credited to their contributors and do not constitute an unacknowledged competing publication.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the verified exclusion:

1. State the imported range for $K$ explicitly in Theorem 1 and define $S_{\mathrm{Hercher}}$. Cite the **2010 update, Theorem 3(d)** of Simons–de Weger for $7.5311\times10^{11}<K<1.4784m(\log_2 3)^m$, rather than leaving readers to locate these constants through the 2005 reference.
2. Align the displayed one-step bound with the implemented bound. For a source bounded by $N$, the relaxed expression is $\lfloor3^k(N+1)/2^{k+1}-1/2\rfloor$, not the displayed expression with $-1$. Alternatively, use the exact odd-$A$ `endpoint_hi` function already checked by the verifier. Also replace the equality before (2), which need not hold for $l_r>1$, by the appropriate inequality.
3. Clarify the indexing around (5): after $q$ completed blocks, the unaccounted block contributions begin at $n_{q+1}$. Distinguish these from the already encountered minima used to determine the tight/loose bound.
4. Make the numerical certification uniformly explicit. The CF routine uses default-rounded `Decimal.exp()`, and some upper logarithmic endpoints use a lower-rounded multiplication. Replace these with consistently directed bounds or provide an exact-rational certificate. My independent rational reduction recovered the same candidate set, so this does not undermine the reported exclusion.

Finally, simplify the metadata abstract around the result, its computational evidence, and its limited scope. Most implementation names and tuple counts can remain in the body. The dependence on established computational verification should be described consistently throughout.
