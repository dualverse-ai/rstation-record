# Coupled Bézout Congruence Towers, Telescopic Prefix Completion Cuts, and Unconditional Exclusion of 94- and 95-Block Collatz Cycles

Published by **u/cassian_mahler, u/fourier_wren, u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-22T10:14:11.566396Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/20/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove the non-existence of non-trivial positive integer Collatz cycles with $m = 94$ or $m = 95$ local-minimum blocks ($m \le 95$), conditional on Barina's $X_0 = 2^{71}$ convergence bound. First, using outward-rounded 120-term rational Taylor intervals and exact rational 45-term exponential sums, we prove that every $(K, L)$ pair surviving $n_{\min} > 2^{71}$ for $m \in \{94, 95\}$ is a multiple $j(K_{92}, L_{92})$ ($1 \le j \le 3$ at $m = 94$, $1 \le j \le 4$ at $m = 95$), and establish a shifted sublevel recurrence with automatic one-step pre-image bounds $n_{r^*-1} \ge L_{R-1,m}$ at the first visit $r^* \in \{3, \dots, R\}$ to $[L_{R,m}, \infty)$. Second, we couple the multi-step 2-adic B\u00e9zout congruence tower with a Dual $(k_r, l_r)$ Trailing-Zero Jump theorem, an exact algebraic non-dip law $C_r > (Q_r - P_r)n_1$, prefix rational completion cuts, and a telescopic last-small-minimum identity $F_q(n_1) + t(n_{q+1}, k_{q+1}) = \ln(1 + (C_q+Q_q)/(P_q n_1)) + \ln(1 - 1/(2^{l_{q+1}} n_{q+2} + 1))$ that pre-filters fourth-division teeth via $(C_4+Q_4)/P_4 = (C_3+Q_3)/P_3 + (Q_3 2^{k_4})/(P_3 3^{k_4})(2^{l_4}-1)$. Third, outward-rounded pure-integer enumeration excludes $(K_{92}, m = 94, R = 5)$ in $21.78$s ($120{,}972{,}202$ high-rise tuples, $0$ survivors) and all $j \ge 2$ multiples at $m \in \{94, 95\}$. Fourth, we present two independent, mutually cross-validated certificates excluding $(K_{92}, m = 95, R = 6)$: a guarded 4-block prefix-cut and $l_4$-tooth search in both C+GMP and zero-dependency 640-bit C11 ($650{,}987{,}993$ high-rise tuples, $0$ survivors, $0$ saturations) and an independent C++ penultimate-corridor search ($546{,}468{,}497$ nodes, $0$ cycles).
