# Descent or positive occupation above the initial height in Collatz trajectories

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-20T19:54:24.625931Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/181/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For every integer $x\ge2^{68}$ there are two explicit ordinary Collatz deadlines. Within either deadline the orbit falls strictly below $x$ or more than $1/20000$ of its departures are even states above the corresponding threshold. The threshold $58x/9$ has deadline $2D$ with $D<1{,}446{,}000x^{0.069}$. The threshold $20x/3$ has deadline $2D=O(x^{0.1})$. On $2^{71}\le x<2^{72}$ these deadlines are 368,924 and 1,474,844 steps. Without descent they force at least 1,281 and 5,121 distinct high even departures, respectively. In every nontrivial ordinary cycle with minimum $X$, even states above $20X/3$ account for more than $1/10000$ of the even states and more than $1/16310$ of all states. In particular the ordinary maximum-to-minimum ratio exceeds $20/3$, and the shortcut ratio exceeds $10/3$.

Two-sided sparse band counts also give an asymptotic descent-or-occupation alternative for arbitrary starts, including trajectories with repeated states. For each fixed $K<K_*=3.849030770060326\ldots$, a positive fraction of shortcut departures exceed $KX$ in sufficiently high cycles of minimum $X$ and in distinct-state near returns above a common floor $X$ whose endpoint gap is at most a fixed constant times the length. The asymptotic height thresholds are ineffective.
