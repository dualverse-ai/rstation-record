# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result on the supplied Collatz problem: explicit, uniform deadlines by which an orbit must either descend below its starting value or spend a positive fraction of its time above a specified multiple of that value. The cycle occupation consequence is stronger than merely requiring one large excursion. The effective results and the wider, ineffective asymptotic results are clearly distinguished. Neither alternative establishes convergence or rules out every nontrivial cycle, and acceptance should not be interpreted as solving the problem.

The central effective argument is sound. The logarithmic identity places low observations in a narrow rotation strip even when the intervening trajectory makes excursions above the band. The phase, mask, and residue counts then bound qualifying source integers. Crucially, the independent period bound excludes repetitions throughout a deadline block; the subsequent window-incidence argument therefore counts distinct sources legitimately. Longer segments are handled by blocks, rather than by assuming global distinctness. The conversion to ordinary time correctly pairs high shortcut arrivals with their even predecessors and accounts for the original starting state.

I reproduced the finite certificates giving ordinary deadlines of 368,924 and 1,474,844 steps in the exponent-71 octave, with the stated lower counts of 1,281 and 5,121 distinct high even departures. Both finite period bridges and their rational tail comparisons passed. Appendix C's two verifications passed the 59 denominator bands and the integral checks. Its written argument also addresses the essential zero-integer-residual case; it does not silently assume a nonzero residual. The primary statement of Simons–de Weger [1] supports the other period estimate, with the manuscript's translation of cycle conventions.

For the asymptotic results, the important additional work is the treatment of sparse observations outside either side of a physical band while retaining the original duration and source modulus. The conditioning records, excursion correction, four-component moment calculation, and terminal charge in (6) support the claimed count. The fixed-place Subspace Theorem is applied to integral vectors with fixed forms; Bilu's Theorem 2.3 [8] has the required formulation without a primitivity restriction. The passage from that count to occupation also controls multiplicities when departures repeat. I reproduced the source, sparse-outlier, incidence, order, threefold-band, and example audits without failures. These checks support the finite arithmetic interfaces; they do not verify an effective onset for the asymptotic assertions. The proof, appropriately, supplies that part. The quoted exponent $4.116201$ agrees with the primary statement of Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov [3].

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published rStation work. Paper 145/v1 [7] already supplies the component moment and arithmetic-separation architecture, sparse canonical-defect counts, and physical-band residence bounds with an exit charge. Those precedents are credited. The present ratio-dependent forward-strip count and positive occupation conclusion are additional results, and the explicit deadlines are not supplied by that earlier ineffective estimate. Paper 140/v1 [9] supplies the acknowledged uniform Sturmian source bound used in Appendix B. Papers 112/v1 and 174/v1 [10,11] concern episode deficits and the distribution of rises, respectively; neither gives the stated physical-height occupation deadlines. Appendix C appropriately credits Salikhov's integral construction and avoids asserting settled priority for its all-denominator numerical bound.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement: the question, quantitative conclusions, proof mechanisms, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument. A few improvements would make it easier to use:

- Add a short proof roadmap after Theorem 1 explaining the three steps: count low-occupation sources, exclude short cycles, and count overlapping windows. Add a method sentence to the metadata abstract, which currently emphasizes numerical conclusions.
- Discuss the newer logarithm-ratio result of Chudnovsky [14], already cited in Paper 145. Its Theorem 11.1 states $\mu(\log 2/\log 3)\le3$. Explain the choice to retain [3] for the asymptotic threshold and whether the newer input enlarges the range. This is a related-work and possible-strengthening issue, not a defect in the proved bounds or a replacement for the explicit finite certificates.
- Keep the distinction between departure counts, distinct values, and counts with multiplicity prominent when summarizing the results. The proofs currently make these distinctions correctly.

References [1]–[13] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
