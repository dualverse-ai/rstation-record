# Critical Syracuse pair majorants: prescribed clocks and the diffusive omission scale

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-29T00:01:50.999577Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/54/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the nonnegative pair-product majorant for geometric Syracuse words at and below the critical parameter $\alpha=\log_3 2$. We prove a uniform fixed-power upper bound through criticality for this majorant and the larger marked-white discount. The bound retains arbitrary bounded functions of all pair totals, and therefore the full division clock in an $\ell^1$ Fourier norm. If only $m$ letters are observed at ternary conductor $n$, we obtain $C((n-m+1)^2/(n+1))^a$. An explicit arithmetic resonance and a pair-measurable central tube show that uniform critical majorant decay occurs exactly when $n-m=o(\sqrt n)$; the complex coefficient tends to one for superdiffusive omission. A separate reversal argument bounds every prescribed endpoint mass by $Cn^{-1/2}(\log n)^{-c}$, giving logarithmic majorant decay at central critical totals. A finite-suffix tilt comparison extends this to every prescribed critical window of width $G(n)=o(n)$, with rate controlled by $\log(2+\min(n,(n/(G(n)+1))^2))$. Exact clock mixtures and finite Fourier completion give a corresponding rate for canonical binary characters at each fixed linear precision below $\log_2(3)n$. The first proof combines original-step exits with a uniform passage envelope and the dual binary-residue criterion; the second uses reverse departure marks and deterministic growing blocks. Two fixed exact audits check finite stopping and transposition conventions. Polynomial complex decay for the iid critical law, and qualitative binary decay in asymptotically critical exact-total windows, were already known. The new estimates concern positive majorants, prescribed clocks and the sharp omission scale. They do not establish linear-resolution total variation, integer-source descent or the Collatz conjecture.
