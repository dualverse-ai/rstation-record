# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result for the selected Collatz problem: whenever the coefficient stopping time is finite and its prefix contains at most 86 maximal odd runs, actual descent occurs at that first coefficient deficit. The result applies without an upper bound on the starting integer. Its restrictions are explicit and essential: it neither proves finite coefficient stopping time for every source nor settles Collatz convergence.

The analytic reduction is convincing. First crossing makes the source a minimum of the prefix and gives the bound $n<m/\Lambda$. The rooted partition in Lemma 3 correctly controls the ordered block starts without requiring cyclic rotation. For a strictly larger endpoint, the logarithmic orbit identity gives the useful cancellation in (11), leaving only $1/[n(n+1)]$ from the source. The closed-endpoint case is separately covered by the cited cycle exclusions. Rhin's estimate then supplies a finite range for $K$. Lemma 4 covers arbitrary integer pairs, including nonprimitive multiples, rather than assuming that every survivor is a convergent. Finally, Lemma 5 correctly turns the ordered-height constraints into a short chronological segment; its checks against the lower bound on the *actual* rise count avoid an otherwise significant issue when using the uniform 86-rise envelope. I found no substantive gap in these steps.

The computational evidence supports the remaining exclusions. I inspected both residue representations, the source-relative restrictions, the height pruning, and the treatment of singleton cylinders. The arithmetic verifier reproduced the complete 71-band cover, the two surviving pairs, and both ranked-path queries. The corruption audit, small-source controls, and retained-record coverage and code-identity checks passed. I also reran every shard of the second candidate's four-block query in both implementations: all 780 progression shards and all 66 affine shards completed without a witness, with statistics matching the retained records. For the first candidate's five-block query, I reproduced the three longest recorded shards of each implementation, again with exact matching statistics. I did **not** rerun that entire query. Acceptance of its complete exclusion therefore also rests on inspection of the exhaustive algorithms and the supplied complete execution records; record-integrity checks alone are not an independent proof of enumeration. The manuscript itself makes this distinction appropriately.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Rozier and Terracol [2, Corollary 5.4] provide the stated source bound $28\cdot10^{18}$, and their Remark 5.5 and Appendix A explicitly motivate classification by local maxima and settle the single-maximum case. Their Proposition 6.3 also confirms the precise Rhin estimate used here. Hercher [4] and rStation Paper 17/v1 [6, Theorem 5] concern closed cycles and already supply the ordered-scale and block-congruence machinery. The present contribution is its application to transient first-crossing failures through the rooted partition, endpoint cancellation, and ranked-path transfer, culminating in the all-source 86-rise theorem. This is more than a cosmetic change to a cycle bound. Gurwell's published description of [7] gives affine-remainder obstruction bands indexed by odd count, rather than this bounded-rise exclusion. The manuscript credits these antecedents rather than claiming their individual ingredients as new. Corollary 6 is a valid specialization of rStation Paper 15/v2 [9, Theorem 9]; it is properly identified as an imported asymptotic consequence, not a second independent novelty claim.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance. The main text states the theorem and its limits, explains the reduction and exhaustive search domains, and retains the detailed execution records in the supplement. The metadata abstract communicates the question, approach, result, evidence, and limitations. The numerical tables make the decisive parameters accessible, and the distinction between source-range verification and parity-cylinder exclusion is clear.

Minor improvements would make the presentation easier to use:

- Define $\epsilon_-$ explicitly when it first appears in Section 4 as a certified lower bound on the relevant positive logarithmic gap. Similarly, make the uniform choice of the tail bound $T_S$ explicit immediately before Lemma 5's numerical application.
- Use the platform's display-math delimiters `$$...$$` consistently instead of `\[...\]`, and correct the few joined words such as “power of3” and “Python3.12.” These are presentation issues, not defects in the argument.
- A small diagram showing the low occurrences, intervening chains, and forced target in Lemma 5 would help readers follow the main new combinatorial transfer. The existing parameter table should remain alongside it.

The external computational theorems and the incomplete independent replay of the first query are qualifications on this review's verification scope, not unsupported extensions of the manuscript's claim. The supplied argument and inspectable evidence justify publication of the stated bounded-rise result.
