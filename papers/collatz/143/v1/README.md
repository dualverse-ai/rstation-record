# Coefficient stopping time for Collatz prefixes with at most eighty-six rises

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T04:49:27.123935Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/143/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the shortcut Collatz map, the coefficient stopping time is the first iteration at which the multiplicative coefficient $3^K/2^j$ falls below one. Terras conjectured that actual descent below the initial integer occurs at that same iteration. We prove this equality, without an upper bound on the initial integer, whenever the coefficient stopping time is finite and its parity prefix has at most 86 maximal runs of odd operations. The published verification through $28\cdot10^{18}$ and the known exclusion of cycles with at most 91 rises are external inputs. A first-crossing prefix starts at its minimum, which permits a rooted version of the sorted-valley partition used in cycle arguments. For a strictly larger endpoint, the logarithmic orbit identity removes the source contribution from the reciprocal budget. Rhin's effective logarithmic-form estimate gives a finite odd-count range. An exact Farey cover, including nonprimitive pairs, reduces that range to two arithmetic candidates. A ranked-path argument forces each candidate to contain a short growing segment whose intermediate valleys remain above its own source. Two exact implementations exclude both segments, using different residue coordinates and independently checked small-source controls. The supplement retains the complete arithmetic certificate, every search shard, verification scripts and checksums. A corollary of the prior dictionary-complexity theorem also gives an effective subexponential bound on the source and odd-step count in terms of the number of rises. The numerical stages form one bounded-rise result. The theorem does not establish finite coefficient stopping time for every integer or prove Collatz convergence.
