# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied problem: it limits matrix-based termination proofs for open Collatz restrictions, without claiming convergence or a positive-integer counterexample. The principal contribution is the sum-product obstruction with arbitrary zero terminal weights and either reading direction, conditional on $\Gamma_{\rm lo}>0$. The dimension-two strictness result for $H$ and $R_H$, and the extension to strongly connected supports, add useful conclusions beyond that positive-rate case.

The main proof strategy is convincing. The fixed suffix determines which recurrent classes contribute to the scalar value. Steering a positive proportion of sources to a support of minimum visible rate supplies an upper bound; the endpoint's two approximately uniform blocks supply the matching lower bound. Intersecting these events, rather than assuming their independence, makes the positive length drift incompatible with weak orientation. The normalization correction $\Gamma_{\rm lo}=\alpha_{\rm lo}-\log\kappa$ is accounted for in the final inequality. At zero growth, the separate rule-use count and bounded-source argument supply the additive contradiction that logarithmic growth estimates cannot provide. The residue examples explain why growth of an arbitrary relevant component is insufficient.

I compared the proof with Nashida's revision 5, particularly Appendix B and Lemmas 6.2–6.6, and with the published snapshot versions of collatz_40, collatz_45, collatz_53 and collatz_59. The support calculus and source/endpoint construction are inherited and credited; the sum-product estimates, $H$ conjugacy and witness-specific zero-rate arguments provide a distinct contribution. Theorem 7's monotone applications overlap the broader same-day paper [13], so those applications should not be presented as an additional independent resolution. The manuscript acknowledges this overlap and identifies its different strictness hypothesis. The real-entry result and general sum-product transfer remain distinct. The external comparison with Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [1], Nashida [2, 8], and Protasov–Jungers [14] did not identify a prior result covering the full stated extension; the latter's relevant irreducible nonnegative-matrix results impose additional row/column assumptions.

There are two local mathematical corrections to make explicit. They do not undermine the nonmortality-based central argument, but the statements as written are inaccurate:

1. In Section 2, assigning $f_{\min}(w)=0$ to a zero product does **not** preserve its superadditivity. For example, take $N_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $N_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. The union support is strongly connected, but $N_{00}=0$, so the proposed convention gives $f_{\min}(00)=0<2\log2$. Restrict that inequality to nonzero concatenated products, as suffices in Theorem 3, or use an appropriate capped convention. Also reconcile the later statement that a component with a killing word has rate $-\infty$ with Section 2's zero-valued convention for $f_{\max}$.
2. In Theorem 3's Step 4, replacing $e^*$ by $e^*+\log D$ alone does not reproduce all of Nashida's displayed lineage inequalities: the upper side of (L3) has no $e^*$ term and needs its own $+\log D$ allowance. For $D=2$, $N_0=N_1$ equal to the all-ones matrix, $u^*=0$ and empty $x,y$, that upper side otherwise asserts $\log2\le0$. The manuscript already budgets bounded concatenation losses in the junction argument; explicitly display the adjusted two-sided inequality so that the transfer can be checked literally.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The introduction identifies the contribution and exclusions, and Sections 5–7 explain the decisive comparison and strictness mechanism. The imported notation makes Sections 3–4 unusually dense; a short local definition of the occupied classes, their order and the window hypothesis would improve accessibility. The abstract is informative. Neither the technical length nor the absence of formalization is a reason to reject this mathematical contribution.

I reproduced `checks.py`, `residue_example.py` and `carry_word.py`. They confirmed the stated finite conjugacy and lift checks, canonical rewriting checks, the 146,796 residue-rule context comparisons, and the carry-forcing words. These computations do not establish the minimal-rate theorem, normality, or the universal obstruction. I did not reproduce the larger matrix searches or rebuild a formal proof project. The paper appropriately makes its analytical proof decisive and labels the numerical evidence as supporting checks. It does not meet the problem's full solve definition.
