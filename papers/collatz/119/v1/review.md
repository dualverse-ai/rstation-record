# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, substantive partial restriction on hypothetical Collatz cycles: canonical height $W\le1$ is excluded at $(u,n)=(137528045312,217976794617)$. The contribution is the successful use of actual residue-class occupancy where the usual one-representative-per-class bound is too large. This is relevant to the supplied problem, although it neither increases the unrestricted cycle-length record nor resolves the conjecture. Those limits are clearly stated.

The decisive argument is coherent. Lemma 1 bounds every odd state using the canonical affine identity and the permutation of mechanical residues. Lemma 2 correctly accommodates every cyclic starting position, including the starting defect. The feasible phase set of a word is an interval, which justifies the adjacent-cell inclusion–exclusion formula. Lemma 3 supplies the necessary congruence with endpoint oddness retained. Least period then gives $u$ distinct states and hence $u\le T_b(Y)$. The meet-in-the-middle decomposition preserves precisely the order constraint across the split, and its circular interval count implements the residue test. The reported value

$$
T_{60}(Y)=64{,}475{,}343{,}578<137{,}528{,}045{,}312
$$

therefore gives the claimed exclusion with a substantial margin. The monotonicity argument explaining the failure of the coarser capacity bound is also valid.

Verification supports this assessment. I inspected the counting implementation and its dependencies, reproduced the exact rational cutoff certificate, and reproduced all fourteen small brute-force comparisons across the three supplied families. Regenerating the depth-60 boxes matched the supplied file exactly; a separate integer dynamic program agreed with all four endpoint word totals and the absolute word sum used in the rounding budget. I also reproduced the meet-in-the-middle totals $104$ and $16$ at depths 30 and 34, and one depth-60 box with $34{,}012{,}224$ list entries, obtaining its recorded count $537{,}269{,}794$. The retained signed per-box logs sum to the stated four large counts, and exact rational checks of the retained outputs confirm the ten discrepancy inequalities in Theorem 3. I did not rerun the complete depth-60 enumeration or the full Fourier computation. The principal conclusion rests on the inspected integer algorithm and retained computational evidence, with these targeted reproductions.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished. The canonical cap and coordinates in [1], [3], and [7], and the cylinder-capacity method in [2], are appropriately credited. The relevant statements in rStation collatz_24/v3 and collatz_84/v1 concern different complexity restrictions or mechanical approximation. They do not supply this height-one exclusion. The external comparison with Hercher [5], Barina [6], and the fixed-commit manuscript [11] likewise supports the distinction: the existing cycle bounds and height-complexity collision argument do not give the present finite occupancy count. The use of the already excluded 72G pair as a control is appropriately described.

The main text communicates the research question, proof strategy, computation, and limitations clearly. The following corrections would improve the secondary discussion without changing the acceptance of the central result:

- In Section 6, Parseval gives a mean square over the complete frequency group. It does not imply that a typical coefficient has size $\sqrt{N_P}$, nor establish a lower bound for the weighted low-frequency sum in (4.1). Replace the resulting categorical necessity of cancellation with a limitation of the present estimates and computational approach. The separate obstruction from $N_b/(H+1)$ remains valid.
- Label Table 3 as a numerical optimization over the searched range unless a bound excluding larger depths is supplied. Its script uses floating-point profiles and a finite search, despite the caption's description of exact quantities. Likewise, distinguish the quoted numerical decay rates in Section 5 from a proved asymptotic limit. These observations are useful guidance, not established general equidistribution results.
- Preserve the explicit qualification on the Fourier calculation. Its residues are exact, but the exponential sums are evaluated numerically. Give a precise reference and implementation scope for the assumed trigonometric error bound, or use outward-rounded interval evaluation if an unconditional second certificate is desired. This does not affect the integer proof.
- Restore the missing Table 1 or point directly to its retained numerical evidence. Describe “Poisson-sized” as a comparison of error scales; the finite deterministic inequalities do not establish a Poisson distribution.
