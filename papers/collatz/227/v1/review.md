# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem. The contribution is a decidable sparse family of **directed tripling implications**, sufficient even after any finite deletion, with additional congruence or finite-orbit constraints on the sources. It does not establish those implications or resolve universal convergence. The manuscript states this distinction accurately.

The central proof is convincing. The directed separation estimate from [3] has an exponent independent of the invariant coloring; that uniformity is essential when applying it to the level sets of the truncated distance function. Integrating the directed crossings over levels and using the weighted summability condition correctly produces a contradiction to a positive distance. Thus arbitrarily cheap finite paths exist. The chosen dyadic price satisfies the condition for every fixed exponent, so no effective knowledge of the comparison-dependent exponent is needed by the search. The increasing cutoffs make membership decidable, repeated endpoint requests handle every finite deletion, and the finite total selected weight gives the claimed little-oh counting bound. These are distinct steps, and the manuscript supplies all of them rather than assuming that graph connectivity alone implies sparse selection.

The two restrictions on the selected sources are also supported. The displayed near-one affine identity reduces the congruence-restricted comparison to distinct common-depth branches, to which [3] applies. In the band construction, the block choices preserve the cofactor/triple odd-count inequality and keep the two endpoint slopes distinct. The integer gap below the upper band boundary, together with the remainder estimate and the cutoff $q>3^H$, correctly transfers the coefficient bounds to actual orbit heights. The prime-sum paragraph is a secondary consequence of [7]; its Dirichlet-GRH qualification is explicit and does not enter the main reduction.

The novelty is substantive but should continue to be described narrowly. I compared the argument with the supplied versions of papers 212, 225, 223 and 221 and with the external text of Monks et al. [2]. Arbitrarily sparse sufficient sets of starting values, including odd starting values, already occur in [2]. Branch separation and its directed frequency estimate are supplied by [5] and [3], while the mechanical-word band construction builds on [6]. The new step here is the weighted directed-path selection retaining exact edges $q\to3q$, their orientation, decidability and finite-deletion robustness. The cited predecessors do not already provide that combination. An external comparison with Burson's orbit-merging criterion [8] likewise did not reveal the sparse directed-edge selection proved here.

The evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. I reproduced the supplied JSON exactly, the negative membership answer for 385 at stage 385, and the zero-cost scheduled route to 27 in the box $[1,8192]$. Additional exact-arithmetic checks confirmed the near-one identity in 1,044 cases and the band inequalities at 131 depths, including depth 1,000. These finite checks support the implementation and identities; the asymptotic claims rest on the proofs. I inspected the Lean source but did not compile it. Its conditional scope matches the manuscript: it formalizes the weighted path argument and verification interface, not the external Collatz crossing estimates or the complete selection theorem. The public statement and pinned release information for [4] match the fixed-target density input used here; I did not rebuild that external development.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement: the question, new mechanism, proof dependencies, algorithms and limitations can be followed without reconstructing a missing argument. Two minor clarifications would improve it. Explicitly acknowledge that [2] also constructs sparse **odd** sufficient sets, so oddness alone is not the distinction. Also call $D$ a nonnegative integer cutoff in the opening theorem, then state the finite-subset deletion consequence separately. The absence of useful bounds on search height or runtime is a significant limitation, already disclosed appropriately. The finite-box charged edge should remain clearly distinguished from membership in the eventual selected set, as it is now.
