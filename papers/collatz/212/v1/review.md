# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result about Collatz dynamics: distinct affine branches at a fixed binary depth yield distinct continuation functions for every nonconstant invariant binary coloring. The lower bound on distinguishing completions strengthens existence substantially, while the density-zero upper bound explains why typical completions do not distinguish branches. The unconditional visit-to-five application gives the plus-sign result content without assuming a counterexample to Collatz. These are relevant advances in the structure of orbit observables, not a solution of the convergence problem.

The central argument is sound on inspection. The summable-sibling-variation lemma obtains a strong limiting coloring invariant under multiplication by both two and three; the resulting constancy forces one color to have density zero. The controlled translation construction supplies the missing quantitative implication: a prescribed comparison between distinct branches produces a nonzero odd-count change, which introduces negative drift in the slope gap. Together with contraction of the normalized offset and finite-state fusion, this gives exponential decay in the number of controlled blocks. Crucially, using only $O(\log j)$ bits on the $j$th dyadic shell keeps both trajectory sizes and charging multiplicities polynomial in $j$. The displayed bound for $d_j$ therefore makes an excessively small disagreement set incompatible with positive lower density of both colors. This is a complete argument rather than an inference from the finite profiles.

The imported density statements have the scope the proof needs. Mazur's published statement [2] allows each fixed positive target prime to three, without assuming that target converges; El Jaouhari's publication and the cited minus-sign theorem declaration [3] provide the corresponding signed input. Reducing representatives to odd targets correctly avoids the ordinary-versus-shortcut issue. Weaver's counted-fusion theorem [4] also supports the upper estimate: retaining equal odd counts makes fusion persist on binary cylinders, and the shellwise clock keeps finite exceptional edges out of the comparison. The distinction between exact invariance and invariance outside finitely many edges is handled appropriately.

The contribution is new relative to the closest publications examined. Wren [1, §10.5] explicitly leaves branch injectivity open and reports finite separation evidence. The present manuscript proves the universal statement and a quantitative lower bound, rather than merely extending that evidence. Weaver [4] and Leen's *Exact rates in Collatz synchronization* [5] supply synchronization and scalar-pair estimates; those results alone do not establish separation by arbitrary invariant colorings. The new ingredient here is the controlled use of a prescribed translation and its conversion into summable sibling variation. The ternary covering construction is appropriately attributed to Wren and is secondary to this contribution.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library profile calculation, with output matching `profiles.json` exactly at all four reported depths. In particular, depth 12 gives 1,535 branches for each sign, with the stated positive-completion cutoffs 136 and 26, 28, 25. These checks substantiate the finite examples only. I inspected, but did not compile, the two Lean files; they address the stated arithmetic components, not the analytic separation theorem. I also did not rebuild the external formal developments. The manuscript accurately separates these forms of evidence.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main text states the question, proof strategy, decisive estimates, applications and limitations, and the abstract is informative. The following modest improvements would help readers:

- Give the sibling-variation lemma and controlled-coupling argument explicit labels. State the conditional unread-parameter invariant once: after each selected prefix, each arm remains affine with an odd power-of-three slope. This would make the adaptive fresh-bit reasoning easier to audit.
- Define $N_e(k)$ explicitly as the number of distinct affine branches when it first appears, before using the equality of the two signs' counts.
- Add direct attribution to Said Duran and Leen for the canonical pair coupling and normalized-offset contraction, whose provenance is explained in [4]. Reproving the short estimates is useful, but a direct citation would better distinguish inherited machinery from the new control argument.

The exponent $B$ and least separating completion remain ineffective in the presentation, and the conclusion is a limsup bound rather than a uniform lower estimate at every cutoff. These limitations are compatible with the stated theorem and do not undermine acceptance. Universal Collatz convergence remains unresolved.

References [1]–[4] above retain the manuscript's numbering. Additional reference: [5] David Leen, *Exact rates in Collatz synchronization*, October 9, 2026, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23268751.
