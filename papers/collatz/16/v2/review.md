# Accept

Theorem 1d is a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper. It removes the irreducible-characteristic-polynomial and integer-Perron-root restrictions from the primitive fixed-word conclusion, while retaining the precise limitations on coding and finite modifications. Sections 15–17 supply the additional argument needed for reducible incidence matrices with noninteger Perron roots. I find that argument sufficient for the stated eventual-periodicity theorem.

The new proof has three consequential components:

- **Relative formal regularity.** In Lemma 7, the integral value group of the iterated Laurent embedding makes the fractional monomial cosets linearly independent over the algebraic subextension. This establishes preservation of degree under monomial pullback, and hence the equality in (R1), rather than merely an inclusion. The reduced branch-support identity (R2) then gives the Newton-polytope width equation. Rational independence of the Perron coordinates forces that polytope to be a point. Purity and the classification of finite étale torus covers finish the argument, since a nontrivial monomial cover would enlarge the value group. The cited hypotheses in [16] and the base-extension and Riemann-existence statements in [17] support these uses.
- **Local specialization with several fast coordinates.** Proposition 8 combines the unique least Perron weight with the smaller height scale of the slow parameters. The resulting denominator and neighborhood losses are $o(\lambda^n)$. Crucially, (G7) absorbs the nonunit slow contribution uniformly over all omitted fast degrees, because $\gamma<1$ and $\mu<\lambda$. The auxiliary gain $q^{1/r}D$ then exceeds the linear height cost after the coefficient field is fixed. The order of choices—field, auxiliary degrees, then iteration—is retained correctly.
- **Applicability to every primitive presentation.** The lattice subdivision produces a positive integral fast basis, and Perron asymptotics make its coordinate matrix positive after a power. The strict Perron gap also justifies preservation of irreducibility under that power. Equations (P1)–(P6) give the required exact representation and sublinear slow support. Equation (P7) establishes eventual contraction without assuming that every initial coordinate contracts. Finally, the algebraic primitive frequency cannot equal the transcendental critical density $\log 2/\log 3$. Under a hypothetical nonperiodic positive rational realization, the strict gap permits the real/$2$-adic comparison and its contradiction. The finite-shift argument correctly avoids requiring positivity of the reconstructed predecessor.

This is a meaningful advance relative to the accepted version, rather than simply another example family. The displayed three-letter presentation is outside both earlier matrix hypotheses; the manuscript appropriately does not infer that its coded word has no alternative presentation. The published periodic-dictionary result [15] concerns a different restriction and does not supply the new primitive-word conclusion. The relative regularity argument is also distinguished from the previously cited formal regularity input [10]; the paper does not claim priority for general $p$-adic Mahler theory.

The revised discussion of López–Stoll [14] addresses the earlier review's important attribution request. Their Theorem 1 states a broader density claim, but equations (15)–(16) and the concluding argument of Section 6 do not establish the asserted transfer from real irrationality to $2$-adic irrationality. Identifying that missing implication, without claiming to disprove their conclusion, is appropriate. The added credit to Fourier Wren [18] also usefully distinguishes the elementary rational-point complexity bound from the algebraic-irrational content attributed to the Subspace criterion.

The accepted scope remains coded primitive fixed words and their stated finite modifications, alongside the earlier automatic result. It does not cover arbitrary words in a substitution subshift, all morphic words, or arbitrary integer parity sequences, and it does not exclude other positive cycles. The Collatz solve definition is not met; this acceptance does not establish a verified solution.
