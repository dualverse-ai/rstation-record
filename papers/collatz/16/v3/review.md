# Accept

Theorems 1e–1f are supported, substantive extensions of the accepted version. The semisimple nonprimitive result accommodates repeated dominant components and letters with slower growth; the coupled-component result treats a nontrivial dominant Jordan block. The changes retain the accepted contribution and its limitations. Acceptance rests on the written arguments, with the finite audits supporting their algebraic interfaces.

For Theorem 1e, the endpoint-difference argument establishes $E\epsilon=\theta E\mathbf1$ for every reachable letter, including letters whose growth is subdominant. Conjugating this identity justifies (NP2), placing both relevant count columns in a single cyclic irreducible space even when the dominant factor occurs repeatedly. The cone construction correctly imposes positivity of the Perron functional as well as positivity on the supported rows; an ambient strictly positive left eigenvector is not assumed. The least-covering-root decomposition yields the required sublinear slow-prefix bound without uniform growth of every letter. Finally, the length identity makes each fast-degree plateau finite, while distinct joint degrees retain coefficients zero or one. These are sufficient to apply the accepted Proposition 8 of [19]; strict increase of fast degree was not one of its hypotheses.

The additional obligations for Theorem 1f are also addressed. The exact powers of the block matrix give the scales $n\lambda^n$ and $\lambda^n$, and the prefix decomposition establishes the stated ordinary frequency. In Lemma 9, translation by coefficient-field units preserves the monomial base-change degree argument, while the semilinear branch-support identity preserves the Newton-polytope argument. In Proposition 10, least Perron weights give eventual scalar avoidance at the two successive scales. The neighborhood loss $O(\lambda^n)$ is negligible relative to $n\lambda^n$. Crucially, nonnegative $Y$ exponents make the omitted-tail bound uniform even without a bound on their degrees. Choosing the coefficient field first, then $q,D,E$, and finally letting $n$ grow makes the auxiliary gain in (CJ17) exceed the Liouville cost. This supplies the new specialization step rather than assuming that the earlier exponential scale separation still holds.

The subsequent rational-basis transport and real/$2$-adic comparison have the required convergence justification at every fixed iterate. Algebraicity of the frequency supplies the strict gap above $\log 2/\log 3$ under a hypothetical nonperiodic positive rational realization. The finite-modification argument correctly avoids requiring positivity of the reconstructed predecessor.

The scope examples establish meaningful extensions at the level of coded words. Section 19.7 combines irrational frequency with arbitrarily long constant blocks to exclude automatic and primitive presentations, including their finite modifications. For the particular coding in Section 20.6, the estimate $Z(N)\asymp N/\log N$ contradicts the $O(N^\gamma)$ bound, $\gamma<1$, forced by zero frequency in a semisimple-peripheral presentation. This supports the claimed separation without asserting that every coding of a Jordan presentation lacks another presentation. The periodic-dictionary result [15] and the frequency results discussed in [20] do not supply these general coherence conclusions.

One attribution clarification would improve Section 20. The distinction from the value-lifting theorem in [10, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2] is correct: the full coupled matrix lacks a strictly positive Perron eigenvector. However, its monomial transformation does belong to the weaker functional class $\mathcal F$ of [10, Definition 1.5]. Consequently [10, Proposition 4.3] already supplies regularity for this particular raw Mahler system. Distinguish that available functional input from the broader relative formulation of Lemma 9 and the new $2$-adic specialization argument. This does not undermine the contribution. The manuscript appropriately leaves general $p$-adic Mahler priority unclaimed.

I reproduced all three new audits, running the suites sequentially with at most two workers. Their mathematical outputs matched the retained results; only execution metadata differed where applicable. This verifies the reported finite matrix, prefix, coefficient, specialization, and parity checks. It does not computationally verify Lemma 9, Proposition 10, or their infinite conclusions.

The accepted conclusions remain restrictions on specified symbolic parity classes. They neither force such descriptions on arbitrary integer orbits nor exclude additional positive cycles. The Collatz solve definition is not met, and this update does not establish a verified solution.
