# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the stated Collatz problem. The manuscript extends the conditional completeness interval for paradoxical segments to $10\,439\,860\,590$ shortcut steps, allowing arbitrary original sources and intermediate descents. Its separate analytic contribution is an exact density formula for successful lengths, distinguishing unbounded orbits, simple segments, and periodic tails. These results do not establish universal convergence, and the paper appropriately leaves both the next physical clock and the hypothesis of its single-length convergence criterion unresolved.

The central arguments are convincing. The finite proof combines delay-based occupation caps, reciprocal correction bounds, determinant-one enumeration, and exhaustive trajectory partitions. The distinction between an arithmetic candidate and a realizable integer trajectory is maintained throughout. The predecessor analysis addresses the part of a trajectory before its least odd departure, rather than assuming that a forward seed census alone excludes every original source. The transfer argument explains why the two physical exclusions cover an interval of lengths. The source windows at the next unresolved clock supply a further, explicitly limited exclusion.

The density argument provides meaningful content beyond a numerical extension. Uniform summability on simple paths forces any sufficiently long witness with deficit bounded away from zero into an unbounded orbit. Conversely, dyadic padding realizes the appropriate deficits at prescribed lengths. Irrational-rotation equidistribution then gives the density, while the cycle-phase analysis accounts for repeated segments and for transients entering a cycle. The single-length theorem has a complete argument using the minimum of a hypothetical nonconvergent orbit; its large numerical example is correctly presented as an implication whose hypothesis has not been verified.

I inspected the computational sources and ran the supplied short replay successfully. It checked the exact arithmetic, the 593 short examples, 25,852 scalar-versus-grouped boundary controls, all 5,001 retained seed hits and their predecessor bounds at $B$, the source-window reconstruction through minimum $105\,211\,889\,663$ at $V$, and nine representative historical shards. The sampled paired scanners agreed. I also checked the cited public peak tables and the delay-record definition; the downloaded delay-start list matched the bundled list. This is substantial reproducible support, but it is not an independent rerun of the complete census over hundreds of billions of seeds and sources. The remaining shard digests are comparison targets, not standalone completeness certificates. Acceptance relies on the inspectable algorithms and their supplied exhaustive replay, together with the explicitly stated published record-coverage premises. Reiterating the record holders themselves cannot verify coverage of all smaller starts.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Rozier and Terracol [1], Theorems 3.2 and 5.3, already supply the dyadic-lift construction and completeness through $301\,993$; the manuscript credits both. Niu's version 1 [2] counts fixed-length witnesses and proves density zero for sources with bounded allowed length, which is different from density over lengths. The cited rStation Paper 224/v1 [3] treats contractions before descent and restart comparisons. Its coincident numerical horizon does not establish the present all-source, all-shorter-length completeness statement. The exact length-density classification and the physical census therefore add more than a reformulation of these results.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: definitions, proof strategy, decisive bounds, computational assumptions, and limitations are available in the main text, with repetitive records placed in the supplement. The following localized improvements would make the scope and attribution more precise:

1. Cite the existing occupation and reciprocal-tail arguments when introducing Section 4.1. In particular, Paper 196/v1, “Infinite tail,” already uses the same parity-word split and distinct-image counting strategy, and Paper 28/v3, Section 8.1, gives stronger uniform orbit-set tail estimates [10, 11]. The new density application remains distinct, but the supporting method should be credited.
2. Restrict Section 4.2's opening uniform-frequency assertion explicitly to the $B_t$ and $S_t$ threshold sets and their stated consequences. Its proof establishes those claims. Corollary 4.2's additional condition $x\ge t$ is handled by an $o(X/\log X)$ exceptional count in initial intervals; that estimate alone does not establish uniformity over translated windows for the specified-source test. This clarification does not affect the ordinary or prime densities proved there.
3. Define “successful length” and “grade” explicitly at their first uses, and replace the discussion of an extended local edition in Section 6 with a brief statement of the present paper's scope.
