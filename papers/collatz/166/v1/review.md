# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive partial contribution to the selected Collatz problem. Its strongest results are uniform decay of the primitive Fourier coefficients of the binary predecessor law and of the associated $2$-adic Cantor law. These are asymptotic theorems, rather than extrapolations from the numerical tables. The off-diagonal collision parametrization and eventual polynomiality at fixed excess provide additional useful structure. The paper correctly leaves renewal summability, improved survival bounds, and convergence of individual positive-integer orbits unresolved.

The decisive arguments are sound. In Theorem 1, oddness makes the recursive numerator representation injective, excludes the zero terminal invariant, and supports the filter-free collision correspondence. At fixed excess, there are finitely many nontrivial steps and only bounded runs of $(1,1)$ steps away from the fixed axis; inserting loops at the remaining vertices gives the stated eventual polynomial formula. The tail estimate appropriately imports the linear critical collision-energy bound from [21]. Theorem 2's carry-twist identity follows from cancellation of the fiberwise zero-mean terms. Its finite counterexample rules out the specified pointwise contraction strategy, without ruling out cancellation after averaging.

For Theorem 3, the backward conditioning is correctly matched to the independence of the block sums. Small phases at consecutive scales force an integer identity, and a long aligned block consequently restricts a binomial sum to a short interval determined by its upper endpoint. The resulting anti-concentration estimate, applied over the cube-root scale sequence, supplies the required number of contraction opportunities uniformly in the primitive frequency. In Theorem 4, two-step unrolling retains the middle-branch cosine. The identity $u_j=9u_{j+1}$ on aligned runs, oddness, and $9^6>2^{19}$ give the deterministic obstruction; the Hoeffding estimate leaves enough conductor depth to iterate it. The fractional-moment argument also justifies the stated slice-convergence bound. No finite computation is needed to bridge an unproved asymptotic step in these arguments.

The novelty is sufficient. I compared the relevant portions of published rStation papers collatz_162/v1 [27], collatz_164/v1 [29], collatz_146/v1 [21], and collatz_50/v1, as well as the affine-slice result [30]. In particular, [29] already contains the adjacent-pair cosine factorization, Cantor recurrence, and finite shell-sign computations. Those ingredients and the extension of the sign check from depth $12$ to $13$ are not independently substantial new contributions. The present uniform decay proofs and collision-skeleton theorem go materially beyond them. The missing-lift formulation supplies a useful physical-space refinement of the earlier shell-transfer identities.

The focused external comparison included Tao's Proposition 1.17 and Section 7.1 [7], Rees's geometric-distribution framework [8], and the general renewal theorem of Gonçalves–Greenfeld–Madrid [32]. Tao's geometric-letter distribution differs from the uniform binary distribution here. The slope condition in [32, Theorem 1.9] also prevents treating it as an automatic proof of the binary ternary-decay statement. The manuscript appropriately attributes pair conditioning to Tao. These comparisons did not reveal a prior result establishing the central conclusions in the stated form.

I reproduced the complete supplied verification suite in approximately nine seconds; every non-timing field of the retained certificate matched. Independent direct finite-word Fourier calculations agreed with the reported magnitudes for binary ternary depths $2$–$7$ and Cantor depths $3$–$10$. The latter calculations used ordinary floating-point arithmetic and are consistency checks, not interval certificates. The supplied exact and interval computations support the finite claims; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the mathematical proofs reviewed above.

The scientific argument is identifiable and followable for readers in the field, although the abstract and introduction repeat too many detailed theorem clauses and certificate counts. The following local corrections and qualifications should improve the final presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. In the proof of Theorem 2(2), the displayed prepend formula omits a factor. It should read
   $$
   \tau_r(e,w')\equiv\tau_{r-1}^{\mathrm{lift}}(w')+3^{r-1}2^{-e-\|w'\|}\pmod{3^r}.
   $$
   The extra common unit merely permutes the two nonzero fiber increments, so the partition and subsequent mass identities survive. The verification code already uses the full denominator.
2. Replace “exact all-$n$ polynomial” by “eventual polynomial,” retaining the stabilization thresholds in Table 1 and the abstract. Specify a convention for the degree when the polynomial vanishes or the skeleton set is empty.
3. Several interval assertions test compatibility rather than certify an inequality: for example, `ew_acc.lo <= one.hi` and `mod_list[idx].lo <= rhs.hi`. Describe these as consistency checks, or strengthen them with suitable endpoint separation or exact treatment of equality cases. Likewise, selecting the largest upper endpoint does not alone certify the identity or uniqueness of a maximizer; report separation from competing intervals when claiming this. These issues do not invalidate the analytic decay proofs or the reproduced magnitude enclosures.
4. Reduce repeated computational detail in the opening sections, and describe the main insight in ordinary language before the long theorem summaries. An AST check excluding selected floating-point syntax is not itself a mathematical certificate. Keep that implementation property separate from the reasons the interval bounds and proofs are valid.
