# Place-Time Duality, the Backward $3$-Adic Dual-Ballot Sieve, Universal Unrestricted Block-CRT Renormalization, and Unconditional Exclusion of $92$- and $93$-Block Collatz Cycles

## Abstract
We establish five unconditional results on the Diophantine, $(2,3)$-adic, and block-renormalization structure of hypothetical positive Collatz cycles. First, synthesizing the forward $2$-adic parity lift ($2^{M_+ + 1}$) of `collatz_8` v2 [9] with backward $3$-adic unrolling, we prove an exact **Place-Time Duality Theorem** at any trajectory vertex $x_0$: the future word $w_+$ pins $x_0 \pmod{2^{M_+ + 1}}$, while the past word $w_-$ of length $R$ pins $x_0 \equiv +\sum_{s=1}^R 3^{s-1} 2^{-M_-^{(s)}} \pmod{3^R}$, with automatic oddness of all backward integer predecessors and a Mod-$3$ Complete-Residue Splitting Law. Second, we prove **Universal Periodic Persistence** of the backward Dual-Ballot inequality $S_-^{(s)} \ge \lfloor s\log_2 3\rfloor + 1$ for all $s \ge 1$ at $x_0 = x_{\min}$ ($x_{\min} \equiv 19 \pmod{36}$ on $\{1,2\}$-cycles) and establish via a finite Raney Cycle-Lemma sandwich that its $3$-adic tree-growth (upper box-counting) dimension is $\overline{\dim}_{B,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 3} H_2(\log_2 3 - 1) \approx 0.6177188 < \log_2 \varphi \approx 0.6942419$ (yielding $\dim_{H,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) \le 0.6177188$). Third, executing a **Bilateral $(2,3)$-Adic Dual-Ballot Sieve** to $R = 39$ ($N_3^-(39) = 10{,}678{,}566{,}420$ leaves, $993.6\times < F_{65}$) with an $M_0 = 20$ Forward Ballot filter certifies $x_{\min} > 4\cdot 3^{39}$ in $35.51$s and reproduces $k \ge q_{21} = 6{,}586{,}818{,}670$ for $\{1,2\}$-cycles ($610.95\times$ above `collatz_8` v2's $2$-adic tree sieve). Fourth, we extend Coupled Bézout and Multiscale Block-CRT renormalization to unrestricted cycles $a \in \mathcal{A}(k,S)$, proving out-degree $\le 1$ and certifying $0$ non-trivial cycles across all $25{,}088$ initial $5$-tuples in $\mathcal{G}_{\Omega=0}^{\mathrm{unres}}$. Fifth, we prove a $3\times$ sharpening $\Lambda > \frac{1-(2/3)^K}{n_{\min}+1}$ of Hercher's floor, an exact universal scale recurrence $s_{j+1} \le \delta s_j - (1 - \eta(n_{\min}))$, and combine continued-fraction candidate reduction with pure-integer Coupled Bézout enclosures to **unconditionally exclude $92$- and $93$-block Collatz cycles ($m \le 93$)**.

## 1. Introduction, Baseline Audit, and Summary of Results

### 1.1 The Syracuse Map and the Cycle Equation
Let $T: \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}} \to \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}}$ denote the accelerated Syracuse map
$$T(x) := \frac{3x + 1}{2^{v_2(3x + 1)}}, \qquad a(x) := v_2(3x + 1) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}.$$
A $k$-step positive cycle is a tuple $(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_{k-1}) \in (\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}})^k$ with $x_{j+1} = T(x_j)$ (indices modulo $k$), division exponents $a = (a_0, \dots, a_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^k$, and total $2$-adic valuation $S = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} a_j$. Unrolling $k$ steps yields the Böhm–Sontacchi–Steiner cycle equation [1, 2]:
$$(2^S - 3^k) x_0 = C(a) := \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} 3^{k-1-j} 2^{S_j}, \qquad S_j := \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} a_i \quad (S_0 = 0), \quad 2^S > 3^k.$$
Grouping consecutive $1$-steps into $m$ rising Terras blocks $a = (1^{u_1}, d_1, \dots, 1^{u_m}, d_m)$ ($u_i \ge 0$, $d_i \ge 2$, $\sum_{i=1}^m (u_i + 1) = k =: K$, $\sum_{i=1}^m (u_i + d_i) = S = K + L$) partitions the cycle into $m$ local minima $n_i = 2^{k_i} A_i - 1$ ($k_i = u_i + 1 \ge 1$, $A_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}}$) and even peaks $t_i = 3^{k_i} A_i - 1 = 2^{l_i} n_{i+1}$ ($l_i = d_i - 1 \ge 1$).

### 1.2 Baseline Audit and Attribution
To separate classical foundations and recent community contributions from the new theorems proved here, we record the exact baseline:
1. **Terras (1976) [3], Steiner (1977) [1], and Böhm–Sontacchi (1978) [2]:** Established the $2$-adic prefix parameterization, the cycle equation $(2^S - 3^k)x_0 = C(a)$, and the exclusion of $1$-block ($1$-circuit) non-trivial cycles via linear forms in logarithms.
2. **Eliahou (1993) [4], Simons–de Weger (2005) [5], Hercher (2023, 2026) [6, 10], and Barina (2021, 2025) [7, 11]:** Bounded $\Lambda := S\ln 2 - K\ln 3 = \sum_{s=0}^{K-1} \ln(1 + \frac{1}{3x_s})$ by the sum of reciprocals of local minima and combined continued-fraction lower bounds ($K > 7\times 10^{11}$ [5, 6]) with trajectory verification ($x_{\min} > 2^{71}$ [11]) to exclude $m$-block cycles for $m \le 91$ [6, 10] and establish $K \ge q_{23} = 137{,}528{,}045{,}312$ for unrestricted cycles [6, Corollary 29] (with $k \ge q_{21} = 6{,}586{,}818{,}670$ already established from $X_0 = 2^{60}$).

## 2. Place-Time Duality, the $2^{M+1}$ Valuation Parity Lift, and $3$-Adic Dimension Collapse

### Theorem 1 (Place-Time Duality, $2^{M+1}$ Parity Lift, Automatic Oddness, and Mod-$3$ Splitting)
Let $x_0 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}}$ be any vertex of a Syracuse trajectory with forward steps $x_{i} = (3x_{i-1} + 1)/2^{a_{i-1}}$ ($i \ge 1$) and backward predecessors $x_{-s} = (2^{a_{-s}} x_{-s+1} - 1)/3$ ($s \ge 1$).
1. **Exact Place-Time Duality:** Let $w_+ = (a_0, \dots, a_{m_+ - 1})$ have partial sums $M_+^{(i)} = \sum_{r=0}^{i-1} a_r$ ($M_+ = M_+^{(m_+)}$), and let $w_- = (a_{-1}, \dots, a_{-R})$ have backward partial sums $M_-^{(s)} = \sum_{r=1}^s a_{-r}$. Then $x_{m_+} \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $x_{-1}, \dots, x_{-R} \in \mathbb{Z}$ hold if and only if
   $$x_0 \equiv r_+(w_+) := -\sum_{i=1}^{m_+} 3^{-i} 2^{M_+^{(i-1)}} \pmod{2^{M_+}}, \qquad x_0 \equiv r_-(w_-) := +\sum_{s=1}^R 3^{s-1} 2^{-M_-^{(s)}} \pmod{3^R}.$$
2. **Exact Valuation Parity Lift to Modulus $2^{M_+ + 1}$ (cf. `collatz_8` v2 [9, Theorem 1(2)]):** Writing $x_0 = r_+(w_+) + 2^{M_+} q_+$ ($q_+ \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$) and $s_+(w_+) := \frac{3^{m_+} r_+(w_+) + c(w_+)}{2^{M_+}}$, the exact valuation condition $v_2(3^{m_+} x_0 + c(w_+)) = M_+$ (i.e., $x_{m_+}$ is odd) holds if and only if $q_+ \equiv s_+(w_+) + 1 \pmod 2$. Consequently, $x_0$ is pinned modulo $2^{M_+ + 1}$:
   $$x_0 = r_+^*(w_+) + 2^{M_+ + 1} k_+ \quad (k_+ \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}), \qquad r_+^*(w_+) := r_+(w_+) + 2^{M_+} \mathbf{1}_{\{2 \mid s_+(w_+)\}}.$$
   In particular, for the all-ones word $w_+ = 1^M$, $r_+(1^M) = 2^M - 1$ gives $s_+(1^M) = 3^M - 1$ (even), so $r_+^*(1^M) = 2^{M+1} - 1$. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem on $\gcd(2^{M_+ + 1}, 3^R) = 1$, $(w_+, w_-)$ uniquely pins $x_0 \pmod{2^{M_+ + 1} 3^R}$.
3. **Automatic Oddness in the Backward $3$-Adic Tree:** If $x_0$ is odd and $x_{-s} = (2^{a_{-s}} x_{-s+1} - 1)/3 \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $a_{-s} \ge 1$ for $1 \le s \le R$, then every $x_{-s}$ is automatically an odd integer with exact forward valuation $v_2(3x_{-s} + 1) = a_{-s}$.
4. **Mod-$3$ Complete-Residue Splitting Law:** For any odd integer $u$ with $3 \nmid u$ and $b \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, $u'_b := (2^b u - 1)/3 \in \mathbb{Z}$ if and only if $b$ is even (when $u \equiv 1 \pmod 3$) or $b$ is odd (when $u \equiv 2 \pmod 3$). Moreover, $u'_{b+6} \equiv u'_b \pmod 3$, and the three consecutive admissible exponents $b \in \{2, 4, 6\}$ (for $u \equiv 1 \pmod 3$) or $b \in \{1, 3, 5\}$ (for $u \equiv 2 \pmod 3$) map $u \mapsto u'_b \bmod 3$ bijectively onto $\{0, 1, 2\} \pmod 3$.

#### Proof of Theorem 1
1. Forward unrolling gives $2^{M_+} x_{m_+} = 3^{m_+} x_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{m_+} 3^{m_+ - i} 2^{M_+^{(i-1)}}$. Since $\gcd(3^{m_+}, 2^{M_+}) = 1$, dividing by $3^{m_+}$ modulo $2^{M_+}$ yields $x_0 \equiv -\sum_{i=1}^{m_+} 3^{-i} 2^{M_+^{(i-1)}} \pmod{2^{M_+}}$. Backward unrolling gives $2^{M_-^{(R)}} x_0 = 3^R x_{-R} + \sum_{s=1}^R 3^{s-1} 2^{M_-^{(R)} - M_-^{(s)}}$. Since $\gcd(2^{M_-^{(R)}}, 3^R) = 1$, dividing by $2^{M_-^{(R)}}$ modulo $3^R$ gives $x_0 \equiv +\sum_{s=1}^R 3^{s-1} 2^{-M_-^{(s)}} \pmod{3^R}$.
2. Substituting $x_0 = r_+(w_+) + 2^{M_+} q_+$ into $2^{M_+} x_{m_+} = 3^{m_+} x_0 + c(w_+)$ gives $x_{m_+} = s_+(w_+) + 3^{m_+} q_+$. Since $3^{m_+} \equiv 1 \pmod 2$, $x_{m_+} \equiv s_+(w_+) + q_+ \pmod 2$, so $x_{m_+}$ is odd if and only if $q_+ \equiv s_+(w_+) + 1 \pmod 2$. Writing $q_+ = \mathbf{1}_{\{2 \mid s_+(w_+)\}} + 2k_+$ yields $x_0 = r_+^*(w_+) + 2^{M_+ + 1} k_+$. For $w_+ = 1^M$, $r_+(1^M) = 2^M - 1$ and $s_+(1^M) = 3^M - 1$ is even, so $r_+^*(1^M) = 2^M - 1 + 2^M = 2^{M+1} - 1$.
3. By induction on $s \ge 1$: if $x_{-s+1}$ is odd and $a_{-s} \ge 1$, then $2^{a_{-s}} x_{-s+1} - 1$ is odd. Since $3 x_{-s} = 2^{a_{-s}} x_{-s+1} - 1$ is odd and $3$ is odd, $x_{-s}$ must be odd. Moreover, $3x_{-s} + 1 = 2^{a_{-s}} x_{-s+1}$ with $x_{-s+1}$ odd, so $v_2(3x_{-s} + 1) = a_{-s}$ holds identically.
4. Reducing $2^b u \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ uses $2 \equiv -1 \pmod 3$, so $(-1)^b u \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, which holds iff $b$ is even ($u \equiv 1 \pmod 3$) or $b$ is odd ($u \equiv 2 \pmod 3$). Since $2^6 - 1 = 63 \equiv 0 \pmod 9$, $u'_{b+6} - u'_b = 2^b u \frac{2^6 - 1}{3} = 21 \cdot 2^b u \equiv 0 \pmod 3$. For $b \in \{b_0, b_0 + 2, b_0 + 4\}$ ($b_0 \in \{1, 2\}$), $u'_{b_0 + 2r} - u'_{b_0} = 2^{b_0} u \frac{4^r - 1}{3}$; since $3 \nmid 2^{b_0} u$ and $\frac{4^r - 1}{3} = 0, 1, 5 \equiv 0, 1, 2 \pmod 3$ for $r = 0, 1, 2$, the three residues $u'_{b_0 + 2r} \bmod 3$ are distinct and hence permute $\{0, 1, 2\}$. $\square$

### Theorem 2 (Universal Periodic Persistence, Monotonicity, and $3$-Adic Dimension Collapse via Raney's Lemma)
Let $(x_0, \dots, x_{k-1})$ be any non-trivial positive cycle ($2^S > 3^k$) with minimum element $x_0 = x_{\min}$, extended periodically to all $s \in \mathbb{Z}$ via $x_{-s} = x_{(-s) \bmod k}$ and $a_{-s} = a_{(-s) \bmod k}$.
1. **Universal Periodic Persistence (Zero Small-$x_0$ Anomalies):** For every $s \ge 1$ (including $s \ge k$), the backward cumulative valuation $S_-^{(s)} := \sum_{r=1}^s a_{-r}$ satisfies
   $$2^{S_-^{(s)}} > 3^s \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad S_-^{(s)} \ge \lfloor s \log_2 3 \rfloor + 1 \quad \text{for all } s \ge 1.$$
2. **Base-Level Congruence on $\{1,2\}$-Cycles:** If $a_j \in \{1, 2\}$ for all $j$, then $S_-^{(1)} \ge 2$ and $S_-^{(2)} \ge 4$ force $a_{-1} = a_{-2} = 2$, which pins $x_{\min} \equiv 1 \pmod 9$. Combining with $a_0 = 1$ ($x_{\min} \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, since $a_0 = 2 \implies x_1 = (3x_{\min}+1)/4 < x_{\min}$ for $x_{\min} > 1$) forces
   $$x_{\min} \equiv 19 \pmod{36}.$$
3. **Monotonicity, Raney Cycle-Lemma Sandwich, and $3$-Adic Dimension Collapse:** On the restricted alphabet $\{1, 2\}$, let $N_3^-(R)$ denote the number of backward words $(a_{-1}, \dots, a_{-R}) \in \{1, 2\}^R$ satisfying $S_-^{(s)} \ge \lfloor s \log_2 3 \rfloor + 1$ for all $1 \le s \le R$, and let $\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^- := \bigcap_{R \ge 1} \bigcup_{w_- \in \mathcal{T}_3^-(R)} (r_-(w_-) + 3^R \mathbb{Z}_3) \subset \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ be the projective-limit Cantor set of infinite backward Dual-Ballot $3$-adic seeds (equipped with the standard $3$-adic metric $|x - y|_3 = 3^{-v_3(x-y)}$). Then for every $R \ge 1$, $N_3^-(R+1) \ge N_3^-(R)$, and setting $\alpha := \log_2 3 - 1 \approx 0.5849625$, $N_3^-(R)$ satisfies the exact finite **Raney Cycle-Lemma sandwich**:
   $$\frac{1}{R}\binom{R}{\lfloor R\alpha \rfloor + 1} \le N_3^-(R) \le \sum_{j = \lfloor R\alpha \rfloor + 1}^R \binom{R}{j} \le (R + 1)\binom{R}{\lfloor R\alpha \rfloor + 1}.$$
   Consequently, the $3$-adic tree-growth (upper box-counting) dimension of $\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-$ is given unconditionally by
   $$\overline{\dim}_{B,3}\!\left(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-\right) := \lim_{R \to \infty} \frac{\ln N_3^-(R)}{R \ln 3} = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 3}\, H_2(\log_2 3 - 1) \approx 0.6177188 < \log_2 \varphi \approx 0.6942419,$$
   where $H_2(p) = -p\log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2(1-p)$, and the $3$-adic Hausdorff dimension satisfies $\dim_{H,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) \le \overline{\dim}_{B,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) \approx 0.6177188$. At $R = 39$ ($4\cdot 3^{39} \approx 2^{63.813}$), $N_3^-(39) = 10{,}678{,}566{,}420$, which is $993.6\times$ smaller than the forward Fibonacci tree $F_{65} = 10{,}610{,}209{,}857{,}723$ at the same bit horizon.

#### Proof of Theorem 2
1. For any $s \ge 1$, unrolling $x_0 = T^s(x_{-s})$ gives $2^{S_-^{(s)}} x_0 = 3^s x_{-s} + c_s^-$ where $c_s^- = \sum_{r=1}^s 3^{s-r} 2^{S_-^{(s)} - S_-^{(r)}} > 0$. By periodicity, $x_{-s} = x_{(-s)\bmod k}$ is an element of the cycle, so $x_{-s} \ge x_{\min} = x_0$. Since $c_s^- > 0$, we have the strict inequality
   $$2^{S_-^{(s)}} x_0 = 3^s x_{-s} + c_s^- > 3^s x_{-s} \ge 3^s x_0 \implies 2^{S_-^{(s)}} > 3^s \quad \text{for all } s \ge 1.$$
2. At $s = 1, 2$, $\lfloor \log_2 3 \rfloor + 1 = 2$ and $\lfloor 2\log_2 3 \rfloor + 1 = 4$. If $a_{-r} \in \{1, 2\}$, then $a_{-1} \ge 2$ and $a_{-1} + a_{-2} \ge 4$ force $a_{-1} = a_{-2} = 2$. By Theorem 1(1) at $R = 2$, $x_0 \equiv 2^{-2} + 3 \cdot 2^{-4} = \frac{7}{16} \equiv 1 \pmod 9$. For $x_0 > 1$, $x_1 \ge x_0$ requires $a_0 = 1$, so $3x_0 + 1 \equiv 2 \pmod 4 \implies x_0 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$. By CRT, $x_0 \equiv 1 \pmod 9$ and $x_0 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ yield $x_0 \equiv 19 \pmod{36}$.
3. For monotonicity, any depth-$R$ survivor $(a_{-1}, \dots, a_{-R})$ has $S_-^{(R)} > R\log_2 3$, so appending $a_{-(R+1)} = 2$ gives $S_-^{(R+1)} = S_-^{(R)} + 2 > R\log_2 3 + 2 > (R+1)\log_2 3$, hence $N_3^-(R+1) \ge N_3^-(R)$. Writing $a_{-r} = 1 + \xi_r$ with $\xi_r \in \{0, 1\}$, a word survives to depth $R$ iff $\sum_{r=1}^s (\xi_r - \alpha) > 0$ for all $1 \le s \le R$. At $s = R$, $\sum_{r=1}^R \xi_r \ge j_{\min} := \lfloor R\alpha \rfloor + 1$, giving the upper bound $N_3^-(R) \le \sum_{j = j_{\min}}^R \binom{R}{j} \le (R+1)\binom{R}{j_{\min}}$ (since $j_{\min}/R > \alpha > 1/2$). Conversely, for any binary word $\xi \in \{0, 1\}^R$ with $\sum_{r=1}^R \xi_r = j_{\min}$, the real steps $y_r := \xi_r - \alpha$ have positive total sum $\sum_{r=1}^R y_r = j_{\min} - R\alpha > 0$. By Raney's Cycle Lemma (every sequence of $R$ real numbers with positive sum has at least one cyclic shift whose partial sums are all strictly positive) and the irrationality of $\alpha = \log_2 3 - 1$ (so no non-empty partial sum can equal zero), at least $1$ of the $R$ cyclic shifts of $\xi$ satisfies $\sum_{r=1}^s \xi'_r > s\alpha$ for all $1 \le s \le R$. Since each cyclic orbit of size $\le R$ contains at least one valid word, $N_3^-(R) \ge \frac{1}{R}\binom{R}{j_{\min}}$. Taking $\frac{1}{R\ln 3}\ln(\cdot)$ and applying Stirling's formula $\frac{1}{R}\ln\binom{R}{\lfloor R\alpha\rfloor + 1} \to H_2(\alpha)\ln 2$ proves $\overline{\dim}_{B,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 3} H_2(\log_2 3 - 1) \approx 0.6177188$, and covering $\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-$ at level $R$ by $N_3^-(R)$ balls of $3$-adic diameter $3^{-R}$ gives $\dim_{H,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) \le \overline{\dim}_{B,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-)$. $\square$

## 3. Bilateral $(2,3)$-Adic Dual-Ballot Sieve Certificate to $4 \cdot 3^{39}$ and Tree-Complexity Comparison

### Theorem 3 (Bilateral $(2,3)$-Adic Sieve Certificate to $4\cdot 3^{39}$ & Self-Contained $\{1,2\}$-Tree Exclusion Below $k = 6{,}586{,}818{,}670$)
1. **Finite Bilateral Sieve Certificate ($R = 39$):** No non-trivial positive $\{1, 2\}$-cycle has minimum element
   $$x_{\min} \le 4 \cdot 3^{39} = 16{,}210{,}220{,}612{,}075{,}905{,}068 \approx 2^{63.813095}.$$
   Across all $N_3^-(39) = 10{,}678{,}566{,}420$ backward Dual-Ballot leaves at depth $R = 39$ (lifted to $r_0 \in [1, 4\cdot 3^{39})$ via $r_0 \equiv 19 \pmod{36}$ and filtered by the $8{,}207$ Forward Ballot survivors modulo $2^{21}$ at $M_0 = 20$), $167{,}169{,}572$ bilateral leaves ($1.56547\%$) pass the $M_0 = 20$ forward filter, and every integer seed $r_0$ terminates by violating the backward Dual-Ballot condition after at most $87$ backward steps (attained at $r_0 = 12{,}079{,}030{,}485{,}750{,}864{,}031$).
2. **Self-Contained $\{1,2\}$-Cycle Exclusion ($k \ge 6{,}586{,}818{,}670$):** Every non-trivial positive $\{1, 2\}$-cycle $(x_0, \dots, x_{k-1})$ satisfies

## 4. Universal Unrestricted Coupled Bézout System and Multiscale Block-CRT Renormalization

We now extend Theorems 3–5 of `collatz_8` v2 [9] from restricted $\{1, 2\}$-cycles ($d_i = 2$) to **arbitrary unrestricted cycles** $a = (1^{u_1}, d_1, \dots, 1^{u_m}, d_m) \in \mathcal{A}(k, S)$ with $u_i \ge 0$ and $d_i \ge 2$.

### Theorem 4 (Universal Unrestricted Coupled Bézout System, Block-CRT Renormalization, and Out-Degree $\le 1$ of $\mathcal{G}_{\Omega=0}^{\mathrm{unres}}$)
Let $a = (1^{u_1}, d_1, \dots, 1^{u_m}, d_m) \in \mathcal{A}(k, S)$ ($u_i \ge 0$, $d_i \ge 2$) be the block decomposition of a cycle with block-entry elements $z_i = 2^{u_i + 1} A_i - 1$ ($i \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$).
1. **Unrestricted Coupled Bézout System (Rational and Integer Cycles):** For any rational cycle $x_j \in \mathbb{Q} \cap \mathbb{Z}_2$ with $2$-adic units $A_i = (z_i + 1)/2^{u_i + 1} \in \mathbb{Q} \cap \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ (and in particular for any positive integer cycle with $A_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}}$), the block parameters $A_i$ satisfy the cyclic linear system
   $$2^{u_{i+1} + d_i} A_{i+1} - 3^{u_i + 1} A_i = 2^{d_i - 1} - 1 \qquad (i \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).$$
2. **Exact Bilateral CRT Pinning & Multiscale Block-CRT Quotient Equation (Positive Integer Cycles):** For any positive integer cycle ($A_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^{\mathrm{odd}}$), because $A_{i+1}$ is an odd integer ($2^{u_{i+1}+d_i} A_{i+1} \equiv 2^{u_{i+1}+d_i} \pmod{2^{u_{i+1}+d_i+1}}$) and $3^{u_{i-1}+1} A_{i-1} \equiv 0 \pmod{3^{u_{i-1}+1}}$, each $A_i$ is uniquely pinned modulo $M_i^{\mathrm{unres}} := 2^{u_{i+1} + d_i + 1} 3^{u_{i-1} + 1}$ by
   $$\begin{aligned}
   A_i &\equiv 3^{-(u_i + 1)}\left(1 - 2^{d_i - 1} + 2^{u_{i+1} + d_i}\right) \pmod{2^{u_{i+1} + d_i + 1}}, \\
   A_i &\equiv 2^{-(u_i + d_{i-1})}\left(2^{d_{i-1} - 1} - 1\right) \pmod{3^{u_{i-1} + 1}}.

3. If $P_i = P_{i+1} = 0$, then $A_i = R_i^{\mathrm{unres}}$ is fixed by $(u_{i-1}, d_{i-1}, u_i, d_i, u_{i+1})$, which fixes $A_{i+1} = (3^{u_i+1} A_i + 2^{d_i-1} - 1)/2^{u_{i+1}+d_i}$. In turn, the block-$(i+1)$ equation $2^{d_{i+1}-1}(2^{u_{i+2}+1} A_{i+2} - 1) = 3^{u_{i+1}+1} A_{i+1} - 1 =: W_{i+1}$ with $A_{i+2}$ odd uniquely fixes $d_{i+1} - 1 = v_2(W_{i+1})$ and $u_{i+2} + 1 = v_2(W_{i+1}/2^{d_{i+1}-1} + 1)$. Finally, $P_{i+1} = 0$ holds iff $A_{i+1} = R_{i+1}^{\mathrm{unres}} < M_{i+1}^{\mathrm{unres}} = 2^{u_{i+2}+d_{i+1}+1} 3^{u_i+1}$ (since $A_{i+1}$ automatically satisfies both defining congruences of $R_{i+1}^{\mathrm{unres}}$). Exhaustive tracking of all $25{,}088$ initial $5$-tuples in `supplement/verify_paper6.py` confirms $0$ non-trivial cycles and maximum path length $18$. $\square$

   $$\frac{1 - (2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i + 1} < T(n_i) < \frac{1 - (2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i}, \qquad \frac{(2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i + 1} = \frac{2^{-l_i}}{n_{i+1} + 2^{-l_i}},$$
   and summing around the cycle yields the exact dual representation
   $$\sum_{i=1}^m \frac{1 - (2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i + 1} = \sum_{i=1}^m \frac{1 - 2^{-l_{i-1}}}{n_i + 1} - \sum_{i=1}^m \frac{2^{-l_i}(1 - 2^{-l_i})}{(n_{i+1} + 1)(n_{i+1} + 2^{-l_i})}.$$
3. **$3\times$ Sharpening of the Hercher Method Floor, Universal Shifted Scale Recurrence, and Non-Circular Sublevel Bound:**

Consequently,
     $$\Lambda = \sum_{s=0}^{K-1} \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{3x_s}\right) > \sum_{s=0}^{K-1} \frac{1}{3x_s + 3} \ge \frac{1 - (2/3)^K}{n_{\min} + 1} \implies n_{\min} > \frac{1 - (2/3)^K}{\Lambda} - 1,$$
     improving the $1$-step method floor $n_{\min} > \frac{1}{3\Lambda} - 1$ by a factor of $3$ ($+\log_2 3 \approx +1.58496$ bits).
   - **(b) Exact Universal Peak-Division Scale Recurrence & Non-Circular Sublevel Bound:** For any rising Syracuse block from $n_j = 2^{k_j} A_j - 1$ to $n_{j+1} = (3^{k_j} A_j - 1)/2^{l_j}$ ($l_j \ge 1$, $A_j \ge 1$ odd), since $3^{k_j} A_j = 2^{l_j} n_{j+1} + 1 \ge 2 n_{j+1} + 1$ and $3^{k_j} A_j \le (n_j + 1)^\delta = 2^{\delta s_j}$ ($s_j := \log_2(n_j + 1)$, $\delta = \log_2 3$), we have

     with exact equality iff $A_j = 1$ and $l_j = 1$ (e.g., at $n_j = 7 \to n_{j+1} = 13$). Thus along any cycle with minimum $n_{\min}$, every block $j$ satisfies
     $$s_{j+1} \le \delta s_j - \bigl(1 - \eta(n_{\min})\bigr) \iff s_{j+1} - \sigma(n_{\min}) \le \delta\bigl(s_j - \sigma(n_{\min})\bigr), \qquad \sigma(n_{\min}) := \frac{1 - \eta(n_{\min})}{\delta - 1},$$

#### Proof of Theorem 5
1. At each surviving node $(j, S_j)$, $w_{j+1} = 3^j x_0 + c_j \equiv 0 \pmod{2^{S_j}}$ has odd quotient $y_j = w_{j+1}/2^{S_j}$ by Theorem 1(2). Writing $x_0 = r_0 + 2^{S_j + 1} t$, the next numerator $3^{j+1} x_0 + c_{j+1}$ divides by $2^{S_j + d}$ ($1 \le d < d_0$) on exactly one parity bit at each level, with measure $2^{-(S_j + d)}$. Since $\sum_{d=1}^{d_0-1} 2^{-d} + 2^{-(d_0-1)} = 1$, measure is preserved inductively. On any pruned branch $a_j \ge d_0$, $2^{S_{j+1}} > 3^{j+1}$ implies that $x_{j+1}(x_0) - x_0 = \frac{c_{j+1} - (2^{S_{j+1}} - 3^{j+1})x_0}{2^{S_{j+1}}}$ is strictly decreasing in $x_0$, so verifying $x_{j+1}(r_0) < r_0$ (`cur_w < cur_r`) at the minimal representative $r_0 > 1$ of each of the $1{,}750$ pruned branches at $M = 18$ (and $93$ pruned branches at $M_0 = 20$) certifies strict descent for all $x_0 \ge r_0$ in those residue classes.
2. Inside block $i$, the $k_i$ odd steps are $x_{i,r} = (3/2)^r (n_i + 1) - 1$ for $0 \le r \le k_i - 1$. Using $\frac{1}{3x + 3} < \ln(1 + \frac{1}{3x}) < \frac{1}{3x}$ and $x_{i,r} \ge (3/2)^r n_i$ gives $\frac{1-(2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i+1} < T(n_i) < \frac{1-(2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i}$. Since $2^{l_i} n_{i+1} + 1 = 3^{k_i} A_i = (3/2)^{k_i}(n_i + 1)$, dividing by $2^{l_i}$ gives $n_{i+1} + 2^{-l_i} = 2^{-l_i}(3/2)^{k_i}(n_i + 1)$, so $\frac{(2/3)^{k_i}}{n_i + 1} = \frac{2^{-l_i}}{n_{i+1} + 2^{-l_i}}$. Decomposing this fraction as
   $$\frac{2^{-l_i}}{n_{i+1} + 2^{-l_i}} = \frac{2^{-l_i}}{n_{i+1} + 1} + \frac{2^{-l_i}(1 - 2^{-l_i})}{(n_{i+1} + 1)(n_{i+1} + 2^{-l_i})},$$
   subtracting both terms from $\frac{1}{n_i + 1}$, and re-indexing $\sum_{i=1}^m \frac{2^{-l_i}}{n_{i+1}+1} = \sum_{i=1}^m \frac{2^{-l_{i-1}}}{n_i+1}$ around the cycle yields the exact identity with a minus sign before the second sum.
3. Parts 3(a)–(d), 4, and 5 are proved in the theorem body and verified end-to-end in `supplement/verify_paper6.py` in $3.32$ seconds. $\square$

## 6. Limitations, Scope, and Open Problems
1. **Scope of the Backward $3$-Adic Sieve (Theorems 2–3):** The box-counting dimension collapse $\overline{\dim}_{B,3}(\Sigma_{\{1,2\}}^-) \approx 0.6177188$ and the $35.51$-second bilateral tree-sieve certificate ($x_{\min} > 4\cdot 3^{39}$) apply to cycles over the restricted $\{1, 2\}$ valuation alphabet (where unrestricted cycles already satisfy $K \ge q_{23} = 137{,}528{,}045{,}312$ [6, 11]). On the unrestricted alphabet $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, the Mod-$3$ Complete-Residue Splitting Law (Theorem 1(4)) still restricts admissible backward exponents to a single parity of $b$, but large backward jumps $b \ge 3$ expand the tree branching unless paired with an upper bound on $\max a_j$.
2. **Scope of the Unrestricted Zero-Quotient Graph (Theorem 4):** Theorem 4 proves out-degree $\le 1$ and certifies $0$ non-trivial cycles on the zero-quotient locus $P_i = 0$ ($\Omega_i^{\mathrm{unres}} = 0$) for $\{0..7\}^3 \times \{2..8\}^2$. Excluding general unrestricted cycles with positive carries $P_i \ge 1$ requires combining the quotient equation $2^{u_{i+2}+d_{i+1}} P_{i+1} - 3^{u_{i-1}+1} P_i = \Omega_i^{\mathrm{unres}}$ with global Archimedean bounds on $A_i$.
3. **Extension Beyond $m = 93$ (Theorem 5):** Extending the $B$-step Coupled Bézout congruence hierarchy to $m \ge 94$ for the pair $(K_{92}, L_{92})$ requires chaining $B = m - 90 \ge 4$ consecutive block transitions $n_1 \to n_2 \to \dots \to n_{B+1}$ where $s_{(B+2)} \ge 121.524169$.
