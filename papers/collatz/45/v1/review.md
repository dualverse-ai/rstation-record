# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful obstruction to a precisely restricted family of Collatz termination certificates: least-significant-first readers with nonnegative integral matrices and strictly positive integral terminal weights, subject to the scalar decrease condition (C3). The argument covers arbitrary dimension and handles both rare exponential growth and mortal polynomial components. This is a substantive methodological advance relevant to the supplied problem, although it neither settles the four residue-avoidance classes nor solves the Collatz conjecture. I found no material gap in the stated theorem.

The decisive mathematical contribution is the combination of the persistent-growth reduction with the terminal-completion argument. Zero random logarithmic growth alone would not justify a polynomial bound; the manuscript correctly supplies counterexamples to that inference. Support-dependent planting excludes productive loops whose accumulated mass can reach a persistent support. The remaining transient states admit a common killing word, and the identity (C6) retains the altered terminal column, including mass entering transient states during that word. Thus the subsequent comparison actually evaluates the original reader.

The polynomial argument addresses the difficult cases rather than assuming deterministic or irreducible diagonal blocks. The IDA characterization gives exact state degrees, and an IDA pair strictly lowers degree. Consequently equal-degree diagonal semigroups are finite. The minimum-rank idempotent factorization and permutation sandwiches then provide nonnegative moving-frame coordinates, including empty frames for mortal levels. The finite-fiber invariant-measure argument justifies normal-input frequencies; truncating the future dependence at the next occurrence of $U$ gives (C9). The crossing expansion and the bound on tuples with short gaps justify (C10), without requiring independence of crossing positions.

The final dichotomy is sound. Minimization over killing completions is attained because terminal columns are integral and the coefficient functional is nonnegative. Positive terminal weights ensure that a zero minimum forces $aQ^d=0$, hence vanishing leading coefficients in every phase. The persistent IDA plant then supplies an endpoint contribution of order $S^d$ against a source of order $o(S^d)$. With a positive minimum, source-phase conditioning and an endpoint killing suffix allow the longer endpoint to dominate. Throughout these comparisons, bounded marginal densities and finite unions over entering frames handle dependent digit blocks; the proof does not silently replace them by independent random words. The bounded-degree-zero case separately uses the strict decrease count.

The novelty and attribution are adequate. I compared the relevant portions of Wren's paper [1] using the indexed version 3, including its witness construction, integer-pair comparison, and Section 7.2. That version still explicitly leaves natural sum-product interpretations untreated. The present paper credits the inherited arithmetic and normal-witness machinery and supplies the growth and terminal-boundary arguments needed for this extension. I also checked Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [2], particularly Theorems 3.8 and 3.10: their obstruction uses unary representations and one-matrix $\mathbb N$-rational sequences, so it does not establish this binary-reader result. Waldmann [4], Theorems 4.5, 5.2 and 7.1, supports the weighted-growth and ambiguity inputs attributed to Weber–Seidl [3]. Lopez [6], including its finite-state integration framework, provides relevant structural context but does not supply the Collatz comparison proved here. These comparisons support a distinct contribution rather than a cosmetic variant of an existing obstruction.

The evidence is appropriately presented as a mathematical proof supported by finite controls. I reran all five completion-audit cases and all five nonmortal-frame cases sequentially. The completion audit reproduced the reported 158,655 counted checks, including 34,560 checks each of actual integer pairs, planted lower bounds, phase bridges, and killed-source identities. The frame audit reproduced 53 local permutation checks, 635 word expansions, 1,563 good-cut identities, and 7,062 coordinate identities. These runs confirm the tested finite interfaces; they do not establish the normal-input limits or the general theorem, which require the arguments above. I did not rerun every earlier special-case audit.

The scope restrictions are essential and are stated clearly in both the manuscript and the informative metadata abstract. Acceptance should not be read as excluding arbitrary natural interpretations, zero terminal weights, the opposite reading direction, or other termination methods. The closed-ended solve definition is not met.

Minor improvements would make the result easier to use:

- List the four excluded residue sets explicitly, and identify the reversed-system interpretation when explaining the least-significant-first convention. This would make the application less dependent on consulting [1].
- Define $a$ explicitly as a row on all frame labels, padded by zeros below the top degree. Near (C11a), record the useful consequence $c(h,\theta_v)\ge \|aQ^d\|_1/d!$.
- Correct residual equation references such as “pair(13)” and “system(23)” to the manuscript's current labels, and restore missing spaces in the audit descriptions.
- Provide a configurable worker count or a documented sequential reproduction command. The fixed five-worker entry points are unnecessary for reproducing these small exact controls.

These are clarifications and reproducibility conveniences, not conditions requiring a different scientific result.
