# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful quantitative improvement for positive integer Collatz sources whose initial parity words belong to the critical Sturmian language. The uniform least-source exponent increases from $6/11$ in Paper 85 [1] to $2/3$, equivalently improving the positive-integer approximation exponent from $11/6+\eta$ to $3/2+\eta$. The contribution is a substantive partial result within the supplied problem's scope. It does not establish general descent or resolve the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript clearly acknowledges this limitation.

The central argument is sound. I checked the imported suffix-basis and return-coverage statements against Paper 85, including the distinction between retained and removed first entrances. I also checked Evertse's original Theorem 2, equation (14), and its norm definition on printed pages 228 and 226. For primitive integer vectors and $S=T=\{\infty,2,3\}$, the specialization in equation (11) is correct; its coordinates need not be $S$-units. Lagarias's Lemma 2.2(1) supplies the stated irrationality bound used to obtain uniform positivity at partial cuts.

The decisive new steps are both justified:

- In Lemma 2, the next-return divisor and the suffix power $\rho$ give $2^M v\equiv1\pmod\rho$ in both orientations. Applying Evertse to $(2^M v,-1)$ and using $2^M v\le128y$ gives the claimed complete-return estimate. This preserves the original positive integer source.
- In Lemma 3, division by $Q$ is legitimate under $e\le\min(m,n)$. The coordinate $-1$ ensures primitivity, and the proof excludes every possible zero subsum. The known ternary divisor of the total sum, together with the positive-column bounds, gives equation (13) with the stated loss $2e$.
- At the next removed-entrance stage, the column scales are indeed $M$ and $M+r$. Equation (18) combines the two estimates at the same source and covers exactly the interval left uncovered by the inherited next-return estimate. The uniform positivity threshold, bounded early completion lengths, and common finite factor language justify passage to all intercepts and both boundary conventions. No bounded-partial-quotient assumption is introduced.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. Papers 84 and 85 already provide the effective exponent-two bound, transported lattices, and much of the return geometry. The present improvement comes from extracting additional ternary divisibility through two different applications of the classical inequality and using them together to eliminate the earlier interpolation loss. This is more than a cosmetic reformulation or another finite diagnostic. My focused external comparison included Ren's exact lattice theorem and scope discussion [4], De Jesús's transcendence statements and explicit distinction from integer approximation [6], and Houze's Crossref abstract [7]. These sources do not state the new uniform $2/3$ positive-integer source bound. Ren optimizes projective height over rational sources; De Jesús's cited results concern transcendence and rational approximation; Houze's abstract reports the effective exponent two at a specified boundary word. The Houze comparison remains abstract-only, so it should retain the manuscript's explicit qualification. The lattice and Diophantine antecedents are credited rather than presented as new.

I inspected the audit implementation and reran its complete fixed domain with one worker. It passed, reproducing all aggregate scientific counts: 15 pairs, 192 two-term cases, 960 three-term cases, 1,442,874 replayed steps, and 2,112 rejected corruptions, including the reported denominator and orientation coverage. This is independent reproduction of the supplied implementation, not an independently designed exhaustive test. It supports the finite arithmetic interfaces; neither it nor the retained results establishes Evertse's ineffective constant or infinite coverage. Those conclusions rest on the analytic proof, as the paper correctly states.

The scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance. The main text identifies the question, separates imported facts from the new lemmas, and explains how the lemmas produce the stronger theorem. The abstract is informative, and detailed audit records are appropriately placed in the supplement. Two small additions would improve self-containment: define $\operatorname{rough}_{2,3}(z)=|z|/(2^{v_2(z)}3^{v_3(z)})$ before equation (11), and state the initial pair $U=0,V=1$ when introducing the standard/intermediate construction. These are minor exposition issues, not gaps in the argument.

The result remains ineffective and restricted to a rigid symbolic language. It supplies no explicit crossover against the earlier effective bound, no polynomial measure for unrestricted rational approximants, and no control of arbitrary positive Collatz trajectories. These limitations are accurately represented and do not diminish the validity of the stated partial advance. Acceptance does not mark the supplied closed-ended problem as solved.
