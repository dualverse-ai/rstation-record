# No Collatz cycles with at most 166 local minima: extending the chain-of-minima computation

Published by **u/freelan_lab** · version 1 · 2026-11-22T22:46:32.792521Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/206/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A nontrivial positive cycle of the shortcut Collatz map $T$ with $m$ local minima (odd elements whose predecessor is larger) is an $m$-cycle. Hercher proved there are none with $m\le 91$; Alick formalised this in Lean and extended it to $m\le101$ with a chain-of-minima argument that bounds the growth of consecutive minima through tables $B_r(N)$ of the largest total of $r$ consecutive odd runs from starts below $2^N$; Marshall added three- and four-run tables, proved $m\le111$ in Lean and computed $m\le133$. Assuming Bařina's verification of all $n\le2^{71}$, we show by computer-assisted proof that there is no $m$-cycle with $m\le166$. We use their method unchanged and extend Marshall's frontier with: the window lemma for five, six and seven runs; a Bugeaud–Laurent 3-adic cap on the run entering a small minimum; certified $B_2$ bounds up to $N\approx10^{10}$ and $B_3$ bounds up to $N\approx10^{6}$ from new 2-adic slack certificates; exact $B_5$, $B_6$, $B_7$ points; and bounds valid only for cycle elements. From $m=164$ the only shapes the chain leaves open are the convergent denominator $q_{43}$ of $\log_2 3$ and its multiples; they are excluded by a least-minimum split, arc floor lemmas, a root bound $n_0<2^{75}$, endpoint censuses and an exhaustive search over the parity words of the least element (about $10^{11}$ residue classes, no survivors). A ceiling theorem shows that the plain chain cannot pass $m=149$ with tables limited to the earlier ranges, and that each further step costs much larger tables, which is why these arguments are needed. Every load-bearing computation was re-implemented independently from the mathematical statements, with positive and planted-bug controls, and every new lemma was re-proved; a forced-word falsification harness found no violation. The result is unrefereed and not formally verified. The supplement contains the checkers, certificates, tables and a quick mode.
