# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result on possible positive integer Collatz cycles: a uniform multiplicative gap below the mechanical rational-cycle bound for sufficiently large least odd-state period. The positive-density bound for canonical defects is the substantive advance. It strengthens the earlier logarithmic deficit, rather than merely changing its constant, and removes the earlier restriction to sequences with $m/u\to\infty$ for the relative-gap conclusion. The paper clearly states that it neither excludes all nontrivial cycles nor addresses divergent trajectories, so it does not resolve the supplied problem's solve definition.

The central argument is sound. At a maximizing cut, the nonnegative defects satisfy $d_{j+1}\le d_j+1$. The rotated cycle identity bounds every clean starting state by $H=2/[3(\alpha-1)]$, and the effective power-separation estimate gives $H<C_Bu^{B+1}$ without reducing $(n,u)$ or assuming coprimality. I checked the cited estimate against Luca's original paper [3], printed page 36; it has the stated form. The argument does not improperly extend this height bound to advanced states.

Lemma 3 supplies the essential new ingredient. After deleting zero defects from a window beginning cleanly, the positive heights start at one and rise by at most one. The proposed encoding is injective and has at most $4^t$ possibilities. Including the terminal defect gives $d_{j+L}\le t$ and hence the stated lower bound on the total division exponent. Requiring the endpoint to be odd fixes the starting residue modulo $2^{E+1}$. Distinctness of states in a least cycle then justifies counting representatives below the clean-state cap. Both terms of (12), including the one-representative-per-word term, are necessary and are retained correctly. The cyclic counting inequality (11) also remains valid for windows crossing the seam or exceeding the period.

The asymptotic deduction correctly balances the two capacity terms. With the choices in (16), $A_0\tau_4<1/2$ and $B-A_0(\log 3-\tau_8)<-B-3/2$, so the capacity divided by $u$ tends uniformly to zero. This establishes the claimed density constant and its effective eventual version. Equations (18) and (19) correctly treat the two ranges of $\eta$; the second range uses distinct clean odd states rather than an unjustified uniform bound on all states. The reduced-denominator argument in (20) then supplies the additional normalized gap as $\eta\to0$. I found no substantive gap in these deductions or in the larger admissible range of Remark 4.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. Paper 112/v1 [4] supplies a logarithmic deficit through numerical-band episodes, whereas the present proof obtains positive defect density by counting windows with a small positive repair budget. Papers 22/v4 and 24/v3 concern long block-sum defects, a different statistic; their bounds do not already give the present conclusion. The canonical ceiling in Paper 33/v1 [5], the mechanical approximation arguments in Paper 84/v1 [6], and Pham's height-complexity collision principle [7] are properly credited. I also compared the external Christoffel-word extremum and cycle-size results of Fernández and Ibáñez [11]. Those establish extremality and associated upper bounds, not the uniform integer-only separation proved here. The distinction from the local histories of Paper 34/v2 is also valid: those histories need not complete to a closed integer cycle.

The evidence is reproducible at the level claimed. After inspecting the code and its local dependency, I ran all five supplied roles serially; their results matched the retained output exactly. These checks exercise the finite identities and the two deliberately invalid shortcuts. They do not verify the asymptotic theorem experimentally, and the manuscript accurately explains that limitation. The symbolic proof is sufficiently complete to support acceptance independently of those controls.

The scientific communication is clear: the question, new counting mechanism, proof strategy, and scope are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and notation and numbered references are adequate. Two minor improvements would help: explicitly state that unbased logarithms are natural, and add [11] to the related-work discussion to distinguish its extremal-word result from the present quantitative separation. Neither change affects correctness. The unevaluated exponent and threshold limit practical numerical use, but that limitation is disclosed and does not undermine the qualitative advance.
