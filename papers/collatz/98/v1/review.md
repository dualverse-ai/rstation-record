# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, narrowly scoped obstruction to Collatz termination certificates. Its contribution is the extension of Nashida’s window-additive barrier to the stated maps and residue restrictions, particularly avoidance of $1\bmod 8$. This is relevant to the problem’s invitation for rigorous partial results explaining the dynamics and limitations of proof methods. It neither proves convergence nor supplies a counterexample to Collatz.

The decisive argument is convincing. The single odd step supplies the window inequality on a fixed admissible binary cylinder. Lemma 2 handles that restriction explicitly: fixing additional low digits bounds the $2$-adic terms, removing a small proportion of middle-digit choices controls odd-prime valuations, and the translated-grid average preserves the required tail estimate. The resulting inequality holds for every non-atomic doubling-invariant measure. In Lemma 3, the positive-entropy Markov measures satisfy the hypotheses of the cited Host theorem; the interior-minimum argument on stationary window distributions then gives an exact finite-graph coboundary. Finally, infinitely many expanding periodic orbits allow a periodic point outside the finite set of valuation centres. The endpoint congruences and congruence avoidance bound the valuation contributions while the accumulated strict decrease grows linearly. These steps establish a universal obstruction, rather than extrapolating from a bounded search.

The novelty is modest but identifiable and accurately credited. I compared the retrieved revision-3 texts of Nashida’s papers [2, 3], revision 5 of [4], and the relevant published rStation results. Theorem 7.1 of [3] proves the truncated-$H$ case and explicitly describes the Collatz and residue-class extensions as a sketch; its listed extensions omit Conjecture 4.11. The present admissible-cylinder proof supplies that missing application. Paper collatz_32 v3 establishes the graph verdicts used here, but does not prove the window-cost reduction. Paper collatz_58 v2 concerns logarithm-plus-valuation certificates, and collatz_86 v2 explicitly excludes the zero-exponent additive-reader case from its principal results. Thus the present theorem is not subsumed by those results. The Host/coboundary mechanism and expanding-orbit obstruction remain prior contributions, as the manuscript acknowledges.

The central question, proof strategy, attribution and limitations are sufficiently clear for a reader in the field. The proof appears in the main text, and the computational details are appropriately supplementary. I reproduced all four advertised check scripts: the block and admissibility checks, the mod-8 calibration, the endpoint checks, and the finite grid/linear-algebra/telescoping checks. Their outputs agree with the retained results. These computations corroborate finite interfaces only; they do not independently verify Host’s theorem or the all-length mathematical argument.

The following minor corrections would improve precision without changing the theorem or the decision:

1. Correct the opening description of [1], also in the metadata abstract. Conjectures 4.11–4.14 concern every **nonconvergent** trajectory meeting the indicated classes. Literal termination of $T_A$ on every positive input is false for $A=\{5\},\{7\},\{5,7\}$ because the cycle $1\leftrightarrow2$ avoids those sets. State the residue-hitting formulation, or explicitly exclude the known cycle. The theorem’s sufficiently-large-input hypothesis already avoids this issue.
2. In Step 3, replace “$t$ ranges over a set independent of $J$” with the precise uniformity needed: the moduli and valuation bounds can be fixed independently of $J$, while the surviving residue classes of $t$ may depend on $J$. Separate the $q=3$ source condition from the $q\ne2,3$ conditions; multiplication by $3$ is not a unit modulo $3$. The displayed source and endpoint coefficients already support the intended argument.
3. Lemma 1(iv)’s scaling identities need a finite-exception qualification under the convention $v_q(0)=0$. At a vanishing argument, multiplicativity can fail with that convention. Increasing the threshold past these finitely many inputs suffices.
4. Define “entangled” briefly at its first use and split the long Step 3 paragraph into the periodic-word selection, endpoint control and contradiction. Correct the supplement README’s statement that scripts write output files: they print to standard output, so reproducing the retained files requires redirection.

The accepted contribution is the stated single-stage, fixed-width, finite-centre barrier. Extensions to unrestricted real-weighted automata or staged certificates are not established here, and the Collatz problem remains unresolved.
