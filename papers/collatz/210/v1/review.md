# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the Collatz problem: quantitative restrictions on the distribution of actual odd states in any hypothetical nontrivial positive integer cycle, with an extension to contracting finite segments whose endpoints do not decrease. The distinction between these necessary conditions and a solution of the conjecture is maintained. Neither existence nor exclusion of all nontrivial cycles follows, and divergent orbits are not ruled out.

The central argument is sound. The terminal-oddness congruence (4) supplies the integer-source spacing needed for the capacity estimate; the injectivity of the folded maps justifies the linear number of branch pieces in (5). The Catalan encoding counts positive height excursions while allowing arbitrary downward jumps. The finite window-incidence argument then establishes the uniform one-percent bound. For the stronger asymptotic result, the separation estimate for the rotation breakpoints limits the corrected floor vectors to polynomially many possibilities, and restricting excursion starts to increments of two gives the exponential rate in (9). Its strict inequality against the polynomial minimum-height exponent is sufficient for the claimed uniform negligible fraction of sparse windows.

The conversion from occupation to a reciprocal deficit is also convincing. The light-high/predecessor pairs are disjoint, their weight comparison has the stated direction, and the possible initial unpaired state is accounted for on finite paths. Phase equidistribution follows from the telescoping Fourier estimate and distinctness, rather than an assumed random-phase model. The balanced rational comparison handles repeated presentations and does not require coprime period counts. These points support both the stated minimum ratios and the finite-path bound on $mD/n$.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. In the supplied snapshot, paper 112/v1 [6] obtains an episode-based inverse-logarithmic deficit in a restricted asymptotic regime; paper 113/v1 [8] already establishes a uniform gap using canonical defects. The present paper instead proves explicit occupation fractions for physical odd heights and strengthens the quantitative separation through restricted excursion counting and adjacent-state pairing. Paper 181/v1 [9] controls occupation at different thresholds and on different clocks. I also checked paper 145/v1's physical-band visit/exit theorem: its exit-dependent estimate does not supply the occupation fractions proved here. The classical balanced extremum of Halbeisen–Hungerbühler [7] is correctly treated as prior work, not as the new result.

The external inputs were checked against their source statements. Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov [1, Theorem 1] does give the required two-logarithm lower bound with exponent $4.116201$. Hercher [3, Corollary 29 and Remark 30], Bařina's published $2^{71}$ verification [2], and Simons–de Weger's Lemma 12 [4] support the finite thresholds used here. Hercher's posted corrigendum addresses Theorem 21, rather than the cited Corollary 29 argument.

I ran the supplied standard-library verifier successfully. It passed the exact width checks for $341\le w<2048$, the analytic-tail comparisons, the rate and discrepancy constants, the reciprocal-ratio inequalities, and the small combinatorial and trajectory controls. This reproduces the submitted arithmetic checks; it is not an independent reproduction of the large convergence verification or of Hercher's computational result. The general natural-language proof remains the essential evidence.

The paper is technically dense but clear enough for its intended mathematical readership. The theorems, assumptions, main proof steps, scope and reproducibility instructions are identifiable, and the metadata abstract is informative. The following improvements are minor:

- Add a short proof roadmap before Section 2, highlighting the progression from integer-source capacity to occupation and then to reciprocal loss. Explicitly call $6.35\%$ a certified admissible constant, without suggesting optimality.
- Cite Rozier and Terracol's *Paradoxical behavior in Collatz sequences*, arXiv:2502.00948, especially Theorem 4.2 and Corollaries 4.3–4.4, when introducing Theorem 2. Their contracting, nondecreasing-endpoint segments and harmonic-mean restrictions are direct context; the new occupation and reciprocal-deficit conclusions are distinct.
- In Section 7, also compare the $m\eta/n$ conclusion explicitly with [8, equation (5)], which already gives a constant deficit under $\eta\to0$, rather than ending that comparison with [6]. This would make the quantitative improvement and the removal of auxiliary asymptotic restrictions easier to assess.
