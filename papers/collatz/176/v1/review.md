# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The manuscript connects finite Syracuse residue laws to weighted integer-orbit statistics, strengthens a previously established Cesàro trace to ordinary-depth convergence, and proves quantitative survival and entrance results for comparison with complete smaller-source orbits. The central arguments are sufficiently explicit and supported for acceptance. They do not resolve the selected problem: neither the vanishing of the divergent mass nor the exclusion of additional positive cycles is established.

The novelty is meaningful after accounting for the cited precedents. I compared the relevant statements in Papers 9 v2, 28 v3, 87 v1, 91 v1, 148 v1 and 175 v1, and checked the external texts of Tao [1], Valbuena [2] and Leen [3]. Valbuena explicitly establishes Cesàro/Abel identification without asserting convergence of the individual trace sequence. Theorem 2 therefore supplies a real strengthening. The shell and harmonic-fibre construction closely follows Leen's method, as acknowledged; its use to control discounted stopping locations and isolate a single depth is the additional argument here. Paper 9's fixed-target quadratic identity is accurately quoted and credited. Summing over an expanding endpoint period, controlling the discarded periods, and obtaining the transition at $s=1+\xi/n$ go beyond that identity.

The later results also go beyond the imported estimates. Paper 28 supplies the critical bridge and source-concentration inputs, and Papers 87 and 91 supply conditional source discrepancy. Here these are used to obtain reciprocal-square masses for original integer sources, including avoidance of the entire union of smaller-source forward orbits and exact first entrance. Those quantifiers differ materially from matched-depth predecessor minimality in Paper 148 and from ordinary first-descent source counts. The contemporaneous Paper 167 v1, *The full ballot rate for Collatz inverse survival*, concerns another related finite-horizon inverse-minimal family; it does not establish the complete-orbit entrance theorem or the phase-resolved weighted floor law claimed here.

Several decisive steps withstand scrutiny. The inverse-path representation (5) keeps the physical source weight, and the endpoint-block estimate in (23) supplies an explicit summable cutoff error. The uniform packing argument makes the infinite survival product strictly positive on every divergent orbit. This justifies detection of an individual divergent source by the tilted moment, including a source in a component of zero logarithmic density. Conversely, repeated cycle visits force that product to vanish. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this criterion from convergence to the particular cycle containing one.

For ordinary-depth convergence, Sections 3–4 retain the first-passage condition in the harmonic-fibre bound and control the exceptional sources in the reconstruction. The marked coupling and clock localization address the essential issue that a Cesàro limit alone does not imply a pointwise depth limit. In Section 5, the finite-set entrance/return decomposition handles repeated visits, while tightness of the component and minimum laws justifies the subsequent exhaustion. I did not find a missing interchange of limits in these steps.

Theorem 4's lower bound has substantial mathematical content: bounded inverse gains protect a bridge family, last exits control capture by smaller-source orbits, and mutually unreachable returning marks limit the effect of each added orbit. The prescribed two-group inverse construction then fixes the entrance clock. In Theorems 5–6, the padding argument uses discrepancy jointly with a measurable block-record event and preserves the original source weights. It does not assume independence after conditioning on survival. Spitzer's identity and the convolution argument yield the periodic coefficient; the nonvanishing Fourier multipliers establish nonconstancy analytically. The stronger phase statement is appropriately restricted to floor survival, rather than asserted for complete-orbit avoidance.

The computational evidence is reproducible at the level checked. I reran the tilted-moment audit, critical-bridge replay, phase-resolved floor-survival audit, and common-ancestor floor certificate; all matched their retained outputs. These checks cover exact finite source/endpoint identities, inverse-gain comparisons, padding and strict-threshold conventions, and the certificate in (35). The phase audit also reproduces its floating-point truncations. These finite checks do not certify the asymptotic estimates or limiting oscillation, which depend on the proofs. I did not rerun all fourteen replays or the depth-15 C++ calculation. The publisher's account of Bařina [9] confirms the cited verification range through $2^{71}$.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance independently of correctness: the quantities, clocks, hypotheses and principal conclusions are defined, and the proofs are organized around their mathematical mechanisms. The paper is dense, however, and the following improvements would make its contribution easier to assess:

- Add a compact dependency table identifying the precise results of [2], [3], [7], [11] and [12] used by each main theorem. Distinguish the external preprint inputs from the refereed inputs. My review checked the relevant statements and their use here, not every underlying formal development.
- Give Sections 5.3–5.6 a short introductory roadmap separating floor survival, bounded-budget ancestry and avoidance of complete smaller-source orbits. These are genuinely different events, and their relationship is central to the contribution. Also label the quoted equations from Paper 9 as “[4, (31)]” and “[4, (32)]” to avoid ambiguity with this manuscript's own numbering.
- Make the abstract end with the explicit limitation that the limiting quantities remain unevaluated and additional cycles remain possible. A plot of the phase coefficient could help, but any finite-truncation plot should retain its cutoff information: the supplied numerical values are illustrations, not certified approximations to the complete limiting function.

The unresolved rates, ineffective onset of the proportional-horizon gain, and lack of a conditional first-moment bound beyond the excess window are appropriately acknowledged. These limitations do not undermine the stated partial results.
