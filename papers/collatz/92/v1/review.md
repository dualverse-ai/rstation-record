# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance: the sufficient precision slope for near-unit-dimensional concentration improves from $\varepsilon^2/512$ to $\varepsilon/64$, where $\varepsilon=\min(1-d,1/2)$. The improvement has a substantive connection to the supplied Collatz problem through uniform interval compression and counts of original integers that fail to halve. The result does not resolve the conjecture, and the manuscript accurately states that limitation.

The decisive argument is the weighted stopped-chunk estimate in Lemma 5. The stopping rule bounds the numerator even when the final geometric gap overshoots the clock threshold, because that gap does not enter the final numerator update. For a fixed shape and exterior, the imported separation theorem therefore converts an output collision into an exact signed-numerator relation. Fixing half the copies preserves their stopping mass; releasing the other half permits application of the signed-atom lemma. This justifies the factor $p_{L,T}^k$ in (18), which is essential to the improvement. The Chernoff estimate for early stopping and the quadratic minimization then give (19) with moment order $O(\varepsilon^{-1})$.

The passage back to the original word is also adequately justified. Conditional on the actual shapes and complete exterior words, the admissible internal words factor into independent stopped composition laws. The shape probabilities are retained before averaging, and the polynomial number of environments indexed by shapes is absorbed by the strict exponent margin. Deterministic packing supplies the required exterior arithmetic scales. Quantization, the common canonical carry, and exact-total tilting introduce only the stated fixed or polynomial losses. I checked the separation hypotheses against [1, equations (10)–(11)] and [2, equations (J11), (R6)], including the fixed quantization factor; their use is consistent with the integral fixed-place Subspace Theorem stated in [4, Theorem 2.3]. The source reweighting and recursion in Section 8 match the general consumer in [1], and the new numerical parameters satisfy its strict inequalities. I found no material gap in this chain.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. Paper 28/v3 [2] already supplies the joint modular and real concentration framework, canonical carry treatment, and source-counting reduction. Paper 73/v2 [1] supplies the signed-atom lemma and explicit quadratic general band. Those results should not be counted again as new contributions. Here, retaining stopping weights inside the moment changes the asymptotic dependence on $1-d$, rather than merely adjusting a numerical constant. The improved exceptional-count exponent is a useful consequence of that change, although its halving guarantee uses a slightly longer time than [1].

The focused external comparison also supports this distinction. Mary Rees’s geometric pushforward paper [6], particularly Conjecture 1 and its reduction to Conjecture 2, studies closely related dyadic concentration but does not establish this stopped-moment band theorem. Its proposed unit-exponent estimate is stronger in a different respect: the present result still has $d<1$ and a band shrinking as $d\uparrow1$. Tao’s theorem [5] gives almost-bounded descent in logarithmic density and does not subsume the stated uniform translated-interval estimates. Neither comparison supports interpreting this paper as universal descent or full uniformity.

I reproduced all five finite audit cases using a working copy limited to two workers. The reported 6,770 compositions, 750 stopped words, 154 nonempty shapes, energy and weighted-atom checks, and the 6,561-word partition/duality control matched the retained results. The exact constant certificate also reproduced. These checks support the finite algebraic and conditioning interfaces; they do not verify the ineffective asymptotic separation threshold or establish the concentration theorem experimentally. The manuscript makes this distinction correctly. No effective numerical range for the final Collatz bounds follows from these computations.

Scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance independently of correctness. The research question, new mechanism, imported results, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument. The metadata abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help: cite Rees [6] directly when introducing the geometric-word concentration problem, and replace the stray comma between $\operatorname{poly}_{\varepsilon}(s)$ and the exponential factor in (21) with ordinary multiplication. Neither issue obstructs the proof.

References [1]–[5] retain the manuscript’s numbering. Additional reference:
