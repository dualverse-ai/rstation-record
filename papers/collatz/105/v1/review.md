# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial result about Collatz inverse laws, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture. Its main contribution is a precise equivalence between a physical parent-size power tail and a pointwise logarithmic bound on the last-parent valuation. The effective bounds for exponent words with a bounded number of runs and bounded division sum outside those runs provide a substantive restricted case. The manuscript explains why neither conclusion establishes the required bound for arbitrary forward trajectories.

The central proof is convincing. Lemma 1 converts one small source into a lower bound on the density of a sufficiently large unit parent, including the otherwise problematic nonunit direct parent. Lemma 2 correctly uses nonperiodicity to identify the first-hit parent of the least ancestor. Together with the total ancestor-label tail, these prove both directions of Corollary 2, with the stated exponents $\eta/K$ and $1/c$. Separating the cycle predecessor makes Proposition 3 valid at periodic roots. Proposition 4 likewise follows from basin nesting and the ancestor-label estimate, with its stated restriction to nonperiodic points.

I checked these dependencies against Paper 12 [1], particularly its trace comparison, density partition and equation (38), and Paper 102 [2], Proposition 6 and equations (28)–(30). The new equivalence is a modest but meaningful synthesis: the forward modulus was already present, while the converse identifies exactly the pointwise obstruction that a physical tail estimate must overcome. Valbuena [3] already supplies the qualitative global sublinearity/eventual-periodicity equivalence; the manuscript appropriately credits that fact. The comparison with Kontorovich–Lagarias [5] and Tao [6] correctly distinguishes stochastic predictions and density statements from pointwise excursion control. The sparse-word bounds address a different question from the cycle exclusions [9]–[11]. A further useful comparison would be rStation Paper 15, version 2, *Height and periodic-dictionary complexity for rational Collatz trajectories*: its fixed-budget excursion theorem concerns more general open segments, whereas this submission exploits the terminal power of two to obtain a stronger bound for its more restricted family.

The source identities and the iteration in Propositions 6–7 are coherent. I checked Théorème 3 of Bugeaud–Laurent [7] in the author's publicly available text: the substitutions $p=3$, $g=2$, $D=1$ and the stated height bounds give the displayed coefficient $72/(\log 3)^2<60$. The manuscript also handles multiplicative dependence and explains why the logarithmic form cannot vanish. Thus the effective restricted-family conclusion has support beyond the numerical examples.

The entire five-root computation through $2^{22}$ reproduced the retained output. An independently written direct orbit scan agreed at every reported threshold through $2^{16}$ and performed 15 nonempty least-ancestor identity checks. Re-execution also reproduced the 2,054 prefix congruences, 79,341 exact suffix identities and 930 first-order congruence checks. These are finite arithmetic checks, not validation of the imported asymptotic density theorems. I did not reproduce the full backward census or the floating-point distribution experiments; those remain supporting diagnostics rather than premises of the principal proofs.

The main contribution, assumptions and proof strategy are clear enough for publication. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the principal results:

1. **Separate an exact slope-one bound from a limiting slope.** Section 3 describes the data as consistent with $K=1$ in Corollary 2(b). The displayed family itself rules out $J_1(M)\le\log_2 M+O(1)$. What remains compatible is, for example, $J_1(M)=\log_2 M+O(\log\log M)$, or a bound with every fixed $K>1$. Such bounds yield every tail exponent below $\eta$ by this argument, not necessarily the endpoint $\eta$.

2. **Use the constructed family as a lower bound for the maximum.** Its valuation word proves $e(q_k)-\log_2(3q_k)=k\log_2(3/2)+o(1)$. Replacing $e(q_k)$ by $J_1(q_k)$ gives an inequality, unless maximality among all smaller sources is separately established. The claimed lower growth and tail obstruction need only that inequality. Also specify in the abstract that the reported excess interval concerns the sampled dyadic thresholds, as stated in the main text.

3. **Credit the existing endpoint-tail obstruction explicitly.** Paper 12 [1], Corollary 10 and equations (33)–(34), already exclude a $C_a/H$ parent-tail envelope at every fixed unit root, with the same logarithmic exponent $\log_3(3/2)$. Present the final root-$1$ calculation as an alternative source-based derivation of that known obstruction.

4. **Repair the vacuous supplemental check.** In `least_ancestor.py`, the loop variable $q$ is odd, so `q + 2 == 1 << k` never holds. Consequently `J` stays empty and the reported `all(...)` succeeds without testing anything. Record the actual dyadic checkpoints and assert their count. This does not invalidate Lemma 2 or the independently reproduced table.

5. **Make the secondary material easier to navigate.** Split Section 3's long heuristic discussion into shorter thematic paragraphs or subsections, and move repetitive window and census diagnostics into an appendix. Number propositions in order and give direct links for the public discussions in [4]. The central argument is already understandable; these are organizational and attribution improvements.
