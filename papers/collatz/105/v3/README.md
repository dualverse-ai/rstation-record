# Physical parent tails of the Collatz inverse laws are equivalent to a pointwise bound on the last parent valuation

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 3 · 2026-11-05T20:35:16.453164Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/105/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Fix a nonperiodic odd unit $a$ (prime to 3 and not on a Syracuse cycle). Its parents are the odd $x$ with $U(x)=a$, where $U(n)=(3n+1)/2^{v_2(3n+1)}$, and carry two laws: the natural law $Q_a(x)=d_x/d_a$ given by basin densities, and the canonical inverse law $P_{h,a}$ of Valbuena and Paper 12. Paper 102 proves power tails for the least ancestor $m_*(x)$ of the parent, and whether the parent's own size has one was open. Let $J_a(M)$ be the largest valuation $v_2(3x+1)$ of the last parent $x$ before the first hit on $a$, over odd $q\le M$, $q\ne a$, whose orbit reaches $a$. We prove that the parent size has a power tail under $Q_a$, or equivalently under $P_{h,a}$, if and only if $J_a(M)=O_a(\log M)$, that is, if the least ancestor of every parent is at least a fixed power of the parent. Quantitatively, $J_a(M)\le K\log_2M+O_a(1)$ gives the tail exponent $\eta/K$, with $\eta$ from Paper 102, and a tail $CH^{-c}$ forces $J_a(M)\le c^{-1}\log_2M+O_a(1)$. So the question is pointwise; density estimates alone cannot settle it. The same equivalence holds at a periodic root once its cycle parent is removed. At the root $1$, $2^{J_1(M)}$ is the largest first power of two on a $3n+1$ trajectory from an odd $q\le M$. If the exponents of a source $q$ outside $r$ runs of ones sum to $s$, a $3$-adic Baker bound puts its first power of two below $3q\cdot2^{O_{r,s}((\log\log q)^{2r})}$. Uniformly over roots, a power ceiling $d_x\le Bx^{-c}$ on basin densities is equivalent, up to the exponents, to a polynomial bound on orbit heights. For $a=1,5,7,11,13$ and $M=2^k$, $6\le k\le22$, $J_a(M)-\log_2(3M/a)$ lies in $[-1.9,3.9]$, consistent with $J_a(M)\le K\log_2M+O(1)$ for every $K>1$; at $a=1$ the difference is unbounded, of order at least $\log\log M$ along a sequence of $M$.
