# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution: the equivalence between a physical parent-size power tail and a pointwise logarithmic bound on the last-parent valuation, together with the restricted exponent-word bounds. The changes improve its precision and organization. The additional census is limited supporting evidence for the existing discussion of the uniform-placement heuristic, appropriately placed in Appendix A and explicitly excluded from the proofs. It does not introduce a separate substantive contribution or obscure the main argument.

Section 3.1 now correctly distinguishes the impossible bound with $K=1$ and a bounded additive constant from possible bounds for every fixed $K>1$. The corresponding qualification about obtaining every tail exponent below $\eta$, without claiming the endpoint, is correct. Replacing the constructed family's asserted maximality by $J_1(q_k)\ge e(q_k)$ supplies exactly what the lower-growth and tail-obstruction arguments need. The abstract now identifies the sampled dyadic thresholds. The added attribution agrees with Paper 12 [1], Corollary 10 and equations (33)–(34): exclusion of a $C_a/H$ envelope was already established there, and the present root-$1$ calculation is properly described as an alternative derivation. The subsections, reordered propositions and relocation of diagnostics improve navigation.

The repaired least-ancestor program reproduced its retained output through $2^{20}$, including all 19 nonempty dyadic checkpoints. After inspecting the new census code, I reproduced the complete $n=15$ fixed-window output for 490,314 words and all 80 windows. Recomputing the statistical summaries from the retained $n=15,\ldots,21$ tables reproduced the reported standardized sums and correlations. I did not rerun the larger enumerations. These checks support the finite diagnostics, not an asymptotic law or a Collatz solution.

Appendix A needs minor numerical and descriptive corrections:

- The bottom window is **among the lowest**, not the lowest of all 80 at every length. At $n=15$ its count at $X=10^3$ is 8, while two controls have smaller counts; at $n=21$ it ties one control for the minimum. The aggregate script's `rank` counts controls at or below the bottom statistic; it is not a one-based rank among all 80 windows. Define this convention rather than translating its output directly into an ordinal rank.
- At $X=10^4$, the control standard deviation divided by the square root of the control mean ranges from approximately $1.28$ to $1.87$, rather than being uniformly about two. Give that range. Define the word-dependent positivity cutoff $e_{\min}$, and describe the C implementation as computing exact membership in the searched exponent windows; it does not recover every word's least admissible exponent outside those windows.

These local corrections do not change the qualitative observation of low bottom-window counts at small thresholds. Keep that observation explicitly restricted to the tested lengths and thresholds, with the substantial cross-length correlation retained. The manuscript remains a useful partial result and does not meet the problem's full solve definition.
