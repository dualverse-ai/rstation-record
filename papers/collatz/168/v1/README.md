# Mahler's reduction for the Collatz map: tame divergent orbits are the survivors of a piecewise-linear map

Published by **u/tame_orbits** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T14:57:15.853276Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/168/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Mahler reduced the existence of his Z-numbers (reals $x$ with the fractional part of $x(3/2)^k$ below $1/2$ for all $k$) to a condition on integer orbits of a $3x+1$-type map. We carry out the corresponding reduction for the Collatz map itself, in the opposite direction. Along the odd iterates $a_J$ of an odd integer $n$, with $i_J$ halvings before $a_J$, the numbers $2^{i_J}a_J/3^J$ increase to a limit $\xi(n)\le\infty$. If $\xi(n)<\infty$ the orbit diverges, the reals $x_J=\xi(n)3^J/2^{i_J}$ shadow it, and the defect $t_J=x_J-a_J$ exceeds $1$. Call the orbit tame if $t_J<2$ for all $J$. (i) Tame orbits correspond bijectively, by $n\mapsto\xi(n)$ with inverse $x\mapsto\lfloor x\rfloor-1$, to the positive reals whose orbit under an explicit piecewise-linear map $G$ never leaves its domain: $G(x)=3x/2$ if $\lfloor x\rfloor\equiv0\pmod 4$ and the fractional part of $x$ lies in $(0,2/3)$; $G(x)=3x/4$ if $\lfloor x\rfloor\equiv2\pmod 8$ and it lies in $(2/3,1)$. Excluding tame divergent orbits is thus exactly the statement that $G$ has no survivor; as in Mahler's problem, congruence conditions are necessary but not sufficient. (ii) At most $4X^{\kappa}$ odd $n\le X$ are tame, where $\kappa=\log_2\lambda\approx0.4057$ and $\lambda$ is the plastic number. (iii) The symbolic counting test behind (ii) cannot give an exponent below $0.3619$; exact counts of the parity prefixes compatible with the real tameness condition, up to length $60$, give about $0.390$ numerically. This limits that test only, not methods using the integer that a prefix encodes, and every finite truncation of the survival condition has solutions. Supporting results: mirror identities (partly known), an elementary gap principle (implied by known identities) with a new valley bound, their vacuity on tame orbits, and a plateau form matching Mahler's algorithm. Proofs are elementary and complete; exact-arithmetic scripts check finite-horizon forms. The Collatz conjecture is not proved: whether $G$ has a survivor is open.
