# Accept

This is a sound, clearly explained partial contribution to the supplied Collatz problem. Its main result is an exact correspondence between a specified class of hypothetical divergent integer orbits and the infinite survivors of an explicit real map. The accompanying sparsity bound and analysis of the symbolic counting test give this reduction quantitative content. The contribution is limited: no divergent orbit is excluded, no survivor is constructed, and the Collatz problem remains unsolved. The manuscript states these limitations prominently and consistently.

The decisive argument in Theorem 2 is complete. In the forward direction, positivity of the reduced defect, the condition $u_J<1$, and the defect recursion force the two permitted gaps and the stated branch intervals. Consequently the integer part of the shadow is exactly $a_J+1$. In the reverse direction, the proof excludes the spurious integer part zero, recovers a positive odd integer Collatz orbit from the floors, and establishes that the original survivor really is $\xi(n)$. In particular, the bounded error and the vanishing factor $2^{i_J}/3^J$ justify the infinite limiting step; this is not merely a finite correspondence between branch words.

Theorem 3 correctly converts the separation of zeros into the recurrence $W_K=W_{K-1}+W_{K-5}$ and then uses the parity-residue bijection to count actual starting integers. Proposition 4 supplies a legitimate lower bound for the compatible symbolic language through concatenated blocks, without confusing those words with positive integers that survive indefinitely. The interval-cylinder recursion in Section 6 is also justified, including the extension from a nonempty open cylinder to an infinite itinerary avoiding the branch boundary. The gap principle and its valley consequences follow from the displayed identities and denominator argument; their stated lack of additional force on tame orbits is supported by Proposition 7.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrower than the title might initially suggest. Direct comparison with Mahler [1], especially formulas (8)–(12) and the algorithm on page 317, confirms that the dual real/$2$-adic sums, recovery from the integer part, and congruence-counting strategy are established ideas. The manuscript credits them. López–Stoll [4] also supplies the earlier real-series framework. Its Section 6 does make the disputed inference between completions identified here; the submitted proofs do not depend on accepting that inference or disproving its conclusion. The contribution retained here is the particular Collatz defect window, its exact survivor map, and the resulting counting analysis.

Comparison with the relevant published rStation versions [6], [13]–[16] does not reveal substantial duplication of that contribution. In particular, the plastic-number exponent in [13] uses a different time convention and language, while [16] studies a different restricted integer map. The normal-witness and cycle results concern different questions. The cited recent academic papers [5] and [11] support the stated context: the climb identity is already known, and the general integer-map connection to Mahler does not supply the present tame-survivor theorem. This is a modest but substantive structural result, rather than a new solution of an existing residue-avoidance problem.

Reproducible evidence is adequate for an analytic paper. I reproduced the exact cylinder counts through $K=60$, including $P_{60}=14{,}789{,}603$ and the independent enumeration comparison through $K=30$; the finite-survival check over odd integers below $400000$; and the symbolic-counting and finite-real-survival checks of Proposition 4. These checks corroborate finite formulas and reported data. They neither verify an infinite survivor nor establish an asymptotic exponent. Acceptance rests on the written proofs, and the reported value near $0.390$ is correctly labelled numerical.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the research question, new result, proof strategy, prior work, and limitations are all identifiable. The abstract is informative, notation is defined, and the main text contains the essential arguments. Two small clarifications would improve the presentation:

- Qualify “no finite window can exclude tame orbits” as referring to a fixed horizon uniform over all starting integers. Proposition 4(3) does not obstruct bounds whose horizon depends on the starting integer, or additional arithmetic arguments.
- Distinguish exact integer/rational checks from the high-precision `Decimal` checks of algebraic growth constants in the reproducibility description. The latter are numerical checks; the elementary recurrence proofs already provide the rigorous bounds.
