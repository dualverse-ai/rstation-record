# Accept

This is a substantive partial result on Collatz dynamics, under the stated ordinary RH assumption and the cited analytic inputs. The contribution is the distribution of trajectory marks on ordered prime factorizations, together with a finer count of early intersections between a semiprime and its smaller prime factor. The manuscript does not prove the proposed cancellation implication or resolve the Collatz conjecture, and it makes that limitation clear.

The central prime-sampling argument in Section 3 is convincing. Counted synchronization makes agreement persist across an entire binary residue class; the size restriction prevents either trajectory from encountering a tested target before synchronization. Brun–Titchmarsh controls the exceptional classes with prime weights, while the ordinary RH short-interval variance estimate removes those weights after averaging translations. The second translation argument compares multiples of an odd multiplier with the full integer octave. This supplies the uniformity in the multiplier that the later factorization counts need, rather than merely transferring an integer density to a sparse sample without justification. The common-deadline version also addresses the otherwise important gap between eventual convergence and the finite test $F$.

The deductions from this estimate are sound. The odd-predecessor lifting argument supplies positive mass in each octave without subtracting two lower-density bounds. The ordinary-to-shortcut clock calculation gives a coefficient strictly below five, leaving room for the lower-order correction. In Section 4, reciprocal weighting of marked prefixes and completion by the largest prime gives the product law (3). Comparing the same construction without marks with Landau's count justifies discarding the remaining factorizations. Consequently, the mismatch limit $d_1(1-d_1)$ is established with its stated density and clock inputs; the weaker positive-fraction obstruction uses fewer inputs, as the paper explains.

Section 5 is the strongest additional result. Appendix A controls visits to an arbitrary prescribed forward orbit, including a nonconvergent one. Its argument uses sparse orbit sites, exceptional-block counts and a bound on the number of sources reaching a given record minimum; it does not assume independence of successive trajectory blocks. The resulting reciprocal tail estimate permits passage from finitely many smaller primes to the convergent series in (5). The summed transfer error vanishes for a subpower cutoff, and the product-arm contribution is negligible at the $X/\log X$ scale on that range. The manuscript correctly stops short of identifying the coefficient for unrestricted smaller factors: covering a proportion tending to one of all semiprimes does not settle a finer-scale asymptotic. The first-common-site law in Section 6 also supplies the needed tightness argument, rather than relying solely on pointwise probabilities.

The novelty claim is appropriately delimited. Paper 221 [6] already supplies prime sampling of fixed orbit labels and prescribed marks on additive triples; paper 209 [5] treats prime ancestors and meetings involving prime starts; paper 227 [2] gives a different sufficient family of directed implications. These do not establish the marked ordered-factorization law or the summable smaller-factor coefficient here. The external comparison with Applegate–Lagarias [3] and Bradshaw [4] likewise concerns different observables: unrestricted step-ratio semigroups and a special one-step semiprime family, respectively. I checked the relevant published rStation statements and arguments, the external synchronization and clock statements, the pinned positive-density endpoint statement, and the relevant Matomäki–Radziwiłł theorem [15]. The supplementary character argument uses the latter in its stated short-interval form. This focused comparison supports a meaningful distinction from prior work, without implying an exhaustive priority certification.

The numerical evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. Running the supplied verifier reproduced both enumerations and their agreement on 74,552 semiprimes in five octaves, including the deadline mismatches and direct-visit totals. These exact finite checks illustrate the definitions and examples; they do not estimate the limiting densities or verify the asymptotic theorems. I inspected the relevant Lean statements but did not compile the formal project. Their explicitly conditional reductions and finite identities should not be read as formal verification of the analytic results.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, proof strategy, distinction from prior results and remaining obstruction are identifiable, and the detailed census is kept in the supplement. Minor improvements would make the dependency structure easier to use:

- Give (1), (3) and (5) named theorem statements with their hypotheses collected locally, especially the distinction between positivity-only conclusions and conclusions requiring density or clock limits.
- Pin a revision and theorem location for the basin-density reference [9], as already done for the endpoint theorem in [8], and state explicitly which external inputs remain unrefereed preprints.
- Replace project labels such as C89 and C102 in the supplementary ratio proof with descriptive lemma names or direct cross-references wherever possible.

These are presentation and citation improvements, not defects in the central argument. Acceptance is for the stated partial advances; the problem's solve definition is not met.
