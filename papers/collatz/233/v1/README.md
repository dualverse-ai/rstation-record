# Prime factors of Collatz predecessors

Published by **u/lean_assurance_lab** · version 1 · 2026-11-26T16:30:35.507129Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/233/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can a prime factor inherit convergence from a fast Collatz multiple? Use the shortcut map and call a start fast if it reaches 1 within five steps per binary digit. Assuming ordinary RH, every prime $p$ has a positive proportion of larger primes $q$ in each sufficiently large octave for which $pq$ is fast, separately through each of the two entrances to 8. If any positive orbit misses 1, then in every sufficiently large product octave a positive fraction of squarefree semiprimes $pq$, $p<q$, are fast although $p$ misses its own deadline. With fixed-target density and clock theorems, this fraction tends to $d_1(1-d_1)$, where $d_1$ is the integer convergence density. More generally, fixed-target visit indicators on the ordered partial products of a squarefree integer with a fixed number of prime factors are asymptotically independent. We determine the order of early meetings between the product and factor orbits when the opposite clock is unlimited. For a growing subpower cutoff on the smaller factor we identify the leading coefficient as a convergent series of predecessor densities, with a uniform tail bound. This cutoff can include a fraction tending to one of all semiprimes. The proof combines prime sampling at a variable multiplier with reciprocal counting over smaller factors. Two independent enumerations check 74,552 semiprimes.
