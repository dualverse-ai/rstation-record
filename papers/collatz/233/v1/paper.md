# Prime factors of Collatz predecessors

## 1. Can a prime inherit convergence from one of its multiples?

Let $T(n)=n/2$ for even $n$ and $T(n)=(3n+1)/2$ for odd $n$. A positive integer is a predecessor of $v$ if its trajectory visits $v$, allowing time zero. Write $b_v$ for this indicator and $d_v$ for its integer natural density when used. Define a finite test

$$F(n)=\mathbf 1_{\{\exists\,0\le i\le5(\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1):T^in=1\}}.$$

For primes $p<q$, knowing $F(pq)=1$ gives us an actual path. What could it tell us about $p$? Taking $p=31,q=37$ gives first arrivals at 1 after 67 steps from $p$ and 39 from $pq$. Their budgets are 25 and 55. The product succeeds while the smaller prime misses its deadline.

Assume ordinary RH for the prime estimates below. Every prime $p$ has two positive-proportion families of larger primes $q$ for which $F(pq)=1$. One family enters 8 from 5 and the other from 16. This holds in **every** octave $(Y,2Y]$ with $Y\ge\max(Y_0,p^A)$, for absolute constants $A,Y_0$. If a single positive orbit misses 1, pairs with $F(p)=0$ and $F(pq)=1$ comprise a positive fraction of **all** squarefree semiprimes in every sufficiently large octave. Thus a zero lower limiting fraction of these decidable mismatches would prove convergence. Section 4 gives separately the hypotheses for the actual limit $d_1(1-d_1)$.

There is a reason finding early intersections of the two paths is difficult. Write $k(x)=\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor$, $\kappa=2\log 2/\log(4/3)$ and, for a sufficiently large absolute $C$, put

$$J(x)=\max(0,\lfloor\kappa k(x)-C\sqrt{k(x)\log(k(x)+2)}\rfloor).$$

Allow either arm to run to this early clock and the opposite arm to run forever. Only $\asymp X/\log X$ pairs meet this way in $X<pq\le2X$, out of $\sim X\log\log X/\log X$ pairs. On the range $p\le P(X)$ with $P\to\infty$ and $\log P=o(\log X)$, we obtain the leading coefficient

$$\sum_{p\ {\rm prime}}\frac{d_{T^{J(p)}p}}p.$$

It comes entirely from early arrivals on the smaller-prime arm. The contribution of primes beyond $B$ to this series is $O((1+\log B)^{-2})$. Taking $P=X^{1/\log\log X}$ covers a fraction tending to one of the semiprimes. The omitted pairs could still change the coefficient at the finer $X/\log X$ scale.

## 2. What earlier theorems already give

Monks proved sufficiency of every arithmetic progression by orbit intersection [1]. Our earlier paper 227 tests directed implications from a cofactor to its triple [2]. Here a varying prime multiplier supplies a fast product, and the implication in question runs from that product to its smaller factor. Applegate and Lagarias determine the semigroup generated by individual step ratios over all starts [3]. Bradshaw studies a family of semiprimes mapped by one step to other semiprimes satisfying an arithmetic-derivative identity [4]. His reciprocal sum converges. Neither construction samples the full forward orbits of the two starts $p,pq$.

Freelan Lab's paper 209 proves a fixed inverse-depth dichotomy for varying targets, meeting estimates for consecutive primes and an integer-to-prime comparison for the first meeting with any smaller orbit [5, Theorems A–E]. Its §7 asks about a prime ancestor for each fixed odd target prime to three. At unrestricted depth the zero-frequency estimate of our paper 221, combined with positive predecessor density, already gives many such primes under Dirichlet GRH [6, “On the primes”]. Paper 221 also gives product probabilities for marks on three summands of a prescribed sum. Our sample contains one number per ordered factorization, with marks on the nested products $p_1,p_1p_2,\ldots$. Weaver's prescribed visitors in paper 222 can first meet a chosen orbit at specified returns [17, Corollary 5]. The designated visitor here is the smaller prime divisor of the starting integer.

Bondurant-David's Theorems P and R bound the logarithmic weight of primes and of integers with all prime factors above $n^\delta$ whose orbit minimum stays above a threshold [7]. To see the difference of populations, fix $0<\delta<1/2$. Counting the larger prime by the prime number theorem gives at most
$O((X/\log X)\sum_{X^\delta<p\le\sqrt{2X}}1/p)=O_\delta(X/\log X)$ such semiprimes. Their fraction tends to zero. Also, an orbit minimum below a threshold does not specify which fixed vertex is visited. The fixed-target densities, convergence clocks, pair synchronization, and unmarked count of almost-primes used here have prior proofs [8–14]. The additional estimates are uniformity in the smaller factor, the marked factorization count and the localization of the early intersections.

## 3. Sampling prime multiples on one octave

Write $A_X(v)=X^{-1}\sum_{X<m\le2X}b_v(m)$. For odd $D\le X^{1/4}$, $Y=X/D$ and $1\le v\le X^{1/4}$, we claim

$$
\frac{\log Y}{Y}\sum_{\substack{Y<q\le2Y\\q\ \mathrm{prime}}} b_v(Dq)
=A_X(v)+O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\log D+\log\log X}{\log X}}\right). \tag{1}
$$

The same formula holds for a visit by any common deadline $L\ge\lfloor(\log_2 X)/4\rfloor$, for a joint test of finitely many such visits below $X^{1/4}$, or for the mark $T^L m\in S$ with any prescribed set $S$. In each case replace the integer mean by that of the same mark. The implicit constant is absolute. All unmarked logarithms are natural.

Leen's uniform pair estimate, in the binary-residue form of [10, §2] and [5, Theorem 4.2], leaves at most a fraction $O(\sqrt{(1+\log(1+|d|))/(1+t)})$ of initial residues modulo $2^t$ for which starts $x,x+d$ have not fused after $t$ steps with equal odd-step counts. The equal counts guarantee that fusion on a residue persists for all its representatives. At our cutoffs all trajectories start near $X$ and stay above $X^{1/4}$ until fusion, since $T^i m\ge m/2^i$. Thus the two marks agree on every successful residue, also at a prescribed common time.

First compare the log-prime-weighted mean in the parameter with its integer mean. Put $H=\lceil(\log Y)^6\rceil$ and $t=\lfloor(\log_2Y)/4\rfloor$. Slide $Dq$ to $D(q+j)$ for $1\le j\le H$. Since $D$ is odd, multiplication by $D$ permutes the binary residues of the parameter. Each failed residue has total prime log weight $O(Y/2^t)$ by Brun–Titchmarsh [12]. Averaging over $j$ therefore costs $O(\sqrt{(1+\log(DH))/\log Y})$ in the normalized mean. After the change of variable $m=q+j$, each integer mark receives the average prime log weight in $[m-H,m)$. Trimming the two ends costs $O(H\log Y/Y)$. The ordinary RH Selberg integral, $\int_Y^{2Y}|\psi(u+H)-\psi(u)-H|^2\,du\ll YH\log^2Y$, and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the normalized mean absolute error by $O(\log Y/\sqrt H)$ [13]. For integer $H$ the integrand is constant on each unit interval. Trimming or shifting by one window is covered by the endpoint cost. Proper prime powers contribute $O(Y^{-1/2}\log^2Y)$ after averaging. Replacing $\log q$ by $\log Y$ costs $O(1/\log Y)$.

Next average the integer marks at $Dm+a$, $0\le a<D$. Compare each with $Dm$ using $t=\lfloor(\log_2 X)/4\rfloor$. The parameter interval contains complete binary periods up to an error $O(2^t/Y)$ in the normalized count. The pair estimate now costs $O(\sqrt{(1+\log D)/\log X})$. The blocks of $D$ translates tile the full product octave apart from $O(D)$ endpoints. This proves (1), including the common-clock variants. In particular, its error is $o(1)$ uniformly up to any common bound $D\le X^{o(1)}$.

### A supply of fast products for every prime

Mazur's positive-density theorem in AxiomMath's explicit ordinary-clock version gives $\gg_t U$ odd $a<U$ reaching any fixed $t$ prime to three in at most $\lfloor10.46\log(a/t)\rfloor$ ordinary steps, after discarding finitely many small $a$ [8]. Take $t=2v$, $v=5$ or $16$. An ordinary visit to $2v$ followed by the halving ensures a shortcut visit to $v$, even if $2v$ itself was skipped. The subsequent path to 1 is fixed. Lift each odd $a<U$ to the unique $z=2^e a\in(U,2U]$. The odd parts distinguish these lifts, and each added ordinary step costs $1<10.46\log2$. Hence a fixed positive fraction of the octave visits $v$ and has ordinary time to 1 at most $10.46\log z+O_v(1)$.

If the first arrival takes $s$ shortcuts with $u$ odd inputs, its ordinary duration is $s+u$. Multiplying the individual step ratios gives $2^s=z\,3^u Q$, with $Q=\prod_{x\ {\rm odd\ input}}(1+1/(3x))$. All these inputs are distinct before the first 1, so $\log Q=O(\log(2+u))$ by the harmonic sum. With $\alpha=\log_2 3$ this yields

$$s=\frac{\log_2 z+\alpha(s+u)+\log_2 Q}{1+\alpha}
\le\frac{1+10.46\log3}{1+\log_2 3}\log_2 z+O(\log\log z).$$

The coefficient is less than $4.833$. Thus the lifts satisfy the common deadline $5\lfloor\log_2 U\rfloor$ for large $U$. Apply (1) with $U=pY,D=p$ to the joint mark of visiting $v$ and reaching 1 by this deadline. Taking $Y\ge\max(Y_0,p^A)$ for sufficiently large absolute $A,Y_0$ makes the error smaller than half the lower bound for each of the two fixed targets. Each branch supplies $\gg Y/\log Y$ larger primes $q$. For $p=2$ use $D=1$ on the parameter octave and prepend the halving, which enlarges the bit budget by five. The two branches are disjoint until 8. This also gives a witness $q\ll(1+p)^A$ for every $p$.

Suppose some trajectory misses 1. It contains a target $t$ prime to three, since an odd input maps to 2 modulo 3 and every positive even start reaches an odd input. The lifts at $2t$ and (1) with $D=1$ give $\gg_t Y/\log Y$ primes in that component in every large octave. All miss 1 forever. Their reciprocal weight up to $Z$ is $\gg_t\log\log Z$ by summing dyadic octaves. Set $Z=X^{1/\log\log X}$. For each such $p\le Z$ the two-branch supply applies at $Y=X/p$ uniformly. Summing gives $\gg_t X\log\log X/\log X$ pairs with $F(p)=0,F(pq)=1$. The same argument proves the pointwise equivalence

$$
\text{all positive starts reach 1}\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\bigl(F(pq)=1\ \Longrightarrow\ b_1(p)=1\bigr)\quad\text{for every prime pair }p<q. \tag{2}
$$

The use of $b_1$ on the right matters, as 31 shows. These assertions use the positive lower bound and (1); they require no limiting density or central limit theorem.

The prime-parameter estimate also has a simultaneous application. For any fixed $v$ prime to three, there are constants $M,E$ such that for every $a,b\ge1$ and cutoff $B$, there are a *single prime* $q$ and two indices $0\le i<j\le M$ for which both $q a^i b^{M-i}$ and $q a^j b^{M-j}$ are predecessors of $v$ above $B$. Their sizes are $O_v((1+B)\max(2,a,b)^E)$ and their ratio in the appropriate order is $(a/b)^{j-i}$. Indeed the lift at $2v$ supplies a fixed octave fraction for every small odd multiplier by (1). For an even multiplier strip its initial power of two. A large fixed $M$ makes the total of $M+1$ individual hit fractions exceed one, forcing two hits at the same prime. Take the prime-parameter octave above $\max(B,Y_0,\max(2,a,b)^{AM})$; the lifts give a logarithmic deadline for both hits. The supplement gives the related bounded-product theorem for ratios of predecessors and the character argument it requires [15].

## 4. Visits along the ordered partial products

Take $n$ uniformly among squarefree integers in $(X,2X]$ with exactly $k$ prime factors, for fixed $k\ge1$. Write them in increasing order $p_1<\cdots<p_k$ and set $P_j=p_1\cdots p_j$. Prescribe fixed targets $v_j$ prime to three and bits $e_j$. With the fixed-target densities of [9], we obtain

$$
\Pr\{b_{v_j}(P_j)=e_j\ (1\le j\le k)\}\longrightarrow
\prod_{j=1}^k\begin{cases}d_{v_j}& e_j=1,\\1-d_{v_j}& e_j=0.\end{cases} \tag{3}
$$

At target 1 one may use $F$ as the mark under Leen's clock law [11]. In fact among integers in $(X,2X]$, only $o(X)$ convergent starts first reach 1 after $5\lfloor\log_2X\rfloor$, since their typical first-arrival coefficient in binary rank is $\kappa<5$ and the centered clock on the square-root scale is tight on the convergent basin. Squeeze $F$ between the common-deadline hit and the eventual hit in (1). Both transferred prime means tend to $d_1$ uniformly up to subpower multipliers. This step controls the finite test on the sparse sample itself.

**Proof of (3).** Landau's unmarked count in the octave is $N_j(X)\sim X(\log\log X)^{j-1}/((j-1)!\log X)$ for $j$ distinct primes [14]. For one prime use (1) with $D=1$. Suppose the first $j=k-1$ marks occur with limiting fraction $\alpha$. Sum their octave counts dyadically, then apply partial summation to the cumulative count. The reciprocal weight of marked prefixes $D$ through $Z$ is $(\alpha+o(1))(\log\log Z)^j/j!$, by integrating $(\log\log t)^{j-1}/(t\log t)$. The error is additive also when $\alpha=0$. Prefixes divisible by 2 have one fewer free prime and contribute $O((1+\log\log Z)^{j-1})$.

With $Z=X^{1/\log\log X}$, each prime in $(X/D,2X/D]$ is larger than every factor of $D\le Z$. The factorization is unique. By (1) the number completing the final mark is $(\mu_k+o(1))X/(D\log X)$ uniformly in the odd prefixes, where $\mu_k$ is the last factor in (3). Use the prime number theorem and subtraction for a zero bit. Since $\log\log Z\sim\log\log X$, the result after summing is $(\alpha\mu_k+o(1))N_k(X)$. Omitting all marks in the same calculation already counts $(1+o(1))N_k(X)$ completions. Positivity bounds the discarded cases by $o(N_k(X))$. This proves (3).

In particular the semiprime mismatch $F(p)=0,F(pq)=1$ has limit $d_1(1-d_1)$. If any orbit misses 1, its component has positive predecessor density by §3, so $0<d_1<1$. On these fuller hypotheses a zero liminf is **equivalent** to universal convergence. There is also a nondegenerate target independent of that open question. Both 7 and 19 reach 11 without visiting one another, and the common tail from 11 reaches 1 without either. Their predecessor sets are disjoint and have positive densities. Thus $0<d_7<1$, and every fixed pattern of hits and misses at 7 along the partial products has positive limiting frequency.

## 5. Counting early intersections and identifying their coefficient

For $S$ a complete forward orbit and $R=2^r$, we use the following uniform integer bound:

$$
\#\{R\le n<2R:\ T^in\in S\text{ for some }0\le i\le J(n)\}
\ll R/(1+r)^3. \tag{4}
$$

Appendix A proves (4). It is uniform even if the prescribed orbit is nonconvergent. Fix any choice of integers $0\le j(p)\le J(p)$ and put $v_p=T^{j(p)}p$. Meeting by the smaller clock $j(p)$ is *exactly* the event that the full product orbit visits $v_p$: advance both paths together from any earlier common point. In particular a test only on the smaller arm can be counted by an eventual-visit mark.

Let $E_X(P)$ count pairs with $p\le P$, $X<pq\le2X$, for which **either** this event occurs **or** the product meets the full orbit of $p$ by $J(pq)$. Then

$$
\frac{\log X}{X}E_X(P)\longrightarrow K_j=\sum_p\frac{d_{v_p}}p
\quad\left(P\to\infty,\quad\frac{\log P}{\log X}\to0\right). \tag{5}
$$

The same limit holds if only the smaller arm is allowed the early clock. For $j(p)=0$ this gives the constant $\sum_p d_p/p$ for products visiting their smaller factor. Including all smaller factors, the union of the two early tests has order $X/\log X$ for $j=J$. The same order holds even for direct visits in either direction. We give details because total-density arguments at scale $N_2(X)$ lose the coefficient.

**The smaller arm.** For each orbit $S$, $v_p\in S$ implies $T^{J(p)}p\in S$. Weight (4) on each octave by the reciprocal of its left endpoint and sum. Uniformly for $B\ge2$,

$$
\sup_S\sum_{p>B:\ v_p\in S}1/p\ll(1+\log B)^{-2}. \tag{6}
$$

Average over integer reference orbits and pass to the limiting densities on each finite set of primes. Taking the supremum over finite sets proves the same tail bound for $\sum_{p>B}d_{v_p}/p$. The term at 2 is positive because $v_2\in\{1,2\}$ and $d_2=d_1>0$.

For fixed odd $p$, (1) gives $(d_{v_p}+o(1))X/(p\log X)$ hits. For $p=2$ remove the first halving and apply (1) to the remaining prime at scale $X/2$. For $B<p\le P\le X^{1/32}$, $v_p\le p\,2^{J(p)}\le p^6\le X^{1/4}$. Interchange the integer octave sum in (1) with $\sum_p1/p$ and apply (6) to each reference orbit. The factor $\log X/\log(X/p)$ is bounded by $32/31$. After normalization by $X/\log X$, the total transfer error is

$$
O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(2P)}{\log X}}+
\frac{(\log\log(3X))^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\log X}}\right), \tag{7}
$$

by partial summation of $\pi(t)\ll t/\log t$, which gives $\sum_{p\le P}\sqrt{\log p}/p\ll\sqrt{\log(2P)}$. For a subpower cutoff this error vanishes. Fix $B$ first, let $X$ grow and then send $B$ to infinity. This proves (5) for the smaller arm.

**The product arm.** Cover $(X,2X]$ by two adjacent dyadic octaves, with endpoints treated separately. In either octave $J(n)$ is a common clock $L$ away from the endpoint. For large octaves $L$ exceeds the fusion cutoff in (1). Apply the endpoint form of (1) with $S=\mathcal O(p)$ and the mark $T^L n\in S$. Its integer mean is $O((1+\log X)^{-3})$ by (4). Summing this mean costs $O((1+\log X)^{-3}\log\log(3X))$ in normalized units, and its summed errors satisfy (7) with absolute constant adjustments for adjoining octaves. For $p=2$ the first halving shifts the parameter clock by one; increasing $C$ relative to (4) absorbs this and the one-rank shift. The product arm alone thus contributes $o(X/\log X)$ for any subpower cutoff. This proves (5) for the union. Taking $P=X^\delta$ in the bounds also gives

$$\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{X\to\infty}
\left|\frac{\log X}{X}E_X(X^\delta)-K_j\right|=0.$$

For the unrestricted upper bound use (6)–(7) up to a fixed sufficiently small power of $X$. Above it, counting every possible $q$ costs $O(X/\log X)$ because the reciprocal prime sum from $X^\delta$ to $\sqrt{2X}$ is $O_\delta(1)$. A matching lower bound follows from the fixed prime 5 and the positive supply of products visiting 5 in §3. Direct visits in either direction are a subset of the early union and contain this lower-bound family. The order bounds need only integer-octave positivity; (5) additionally uses the fixed-target densities for its coefficient.

## 6. Where convergent pairs first meet

Every convergent start above 8 passes through 8 on the way to $4,2,1$. Stop both convergent paths at 8 and denote their first common site by $V$, in path order. For a fixed $v$ reaching 8, the probability over **all** semiprimes that both paths reach 8 from above 8 and first meet at $v$ tends to

$$
d_v^2-\sum_{Tu=v}d_u^2=
\begin{cases}2d_{2v}d_{(2v-1)/3},&v\equiv2\pmod 3,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} \tag{8}
$$

Indeed, (3) gives the probability of both visiting $v$. Subtract the disjoint events of entering through the same immediate child. A start equal to $v$, or a bounded smaller prime, contributes a vanishing fraction. Targets divisible by 3 have only their iterated doubles as predecessors and give zero mass on both samples. The immediate child basins partition the basin of $v$ up to the singleton, hence their densities sum to $d_v$. In particular the mass at 8 is $2d_5d_{16}>0$.

These masses sum to $d_1^2$. At depth $h$ before 8 the finite set $L_h=\{v:T^h v=8\}$ has disjoint predecessor basins of total density $d_1$ (only the finitely many shallower starts are missing). For each fixed $B$, all sufficiently deep $v$ have no predecessor at most $B$: each start through $B$ reaching 8 has a finite first-arrival time. Every odd predecessor of such a $v$ has the same first odd landing below or at $B$ as the forward path of $v$, for $B\ge8$. Weaver's uniform single-landing bound [16, Theorem 1, (3)] gives density $O(B^{-\eta})$ for that cell among odd starts, for an absolute $\eta>0$. Its input interval $(Y/4,Y]$ contains the octave of interest with bounded relative cost. For all integers, split off the power of two initially dividing the source: the fixed valuations have the same bound with weights $2^{-e-1}$, and the remaining valuation tail has arbitrarily small mass. This proves $\max_{v\in L_h}d_v\to0$. The limiting probability that the common path contains at least $h$ steps before 8, namely $\sum_{v\in L_h}d_v^2$, is therefore at most $d_1\max_{v\in L_h}d_v\to0$. Both the height of the first common site and the remaining time to 1 are tight conditional on convergence of both arms. The clock squeeze in §4 leaves these limits unchanged when both finite deadlines are required.

## 7. Exact finite checks and the remaining question

Two integer enumerations replay five octaves with lower endpoints $2^{10},2^{12},2^{14},2^{16},2^{18}$. One enumerates ordered prime pairs and follows both paths to their first 1. An independent pass factors every integer by its least prime factor. They agree on all 74,552 semiprimes, on mismatch totals by octave, and on both directions of direct visits. In $(2^{18},2^{19}]$, 4,369 of 54,730 pairs have $F(p)=0,F(pq)=1$. Of the 21,364 products visiting $p$, 15,116 have $p\le5$, 4,387 have $5<p\le31$, 1,271 have $31<p\le127$, and 590 have $p>127$. At the same scale all eight visit words at target 7 occur among the three-factor partial products, including 128 instances of three hits. At $p=31$, the products $31\cdot37$ and $31\cdot41$ first meet the factor's path at 20 and 8, respectively.

The supplement contains both enumerations, their data, the ratio argument and Lean sources for the pointwise reduction under its supply hypotheses, clock identities and finite examples. The asymptotics are written proofs. Formula (2) leaves a precise arithmetic question: does a fast prime multiple force its smaller prime factor eventually to reach 1? Even arbitrarily many multiples on each entrance branch at 8 leave that implication to be proved. A second question is whether polynomial-size smaller prime factors change the leading coefficient in (5).

## Appendix A. An integer estimate uniform in the entire prescribed orbit

First, any forward orbit $S$ has $O(R^\beta+\log(2R))$ distinct sites through $R$, for an absolute $\beta<1$. For $R=2^m$, the length-$m$ parity words occur once each among the $R$ residue classes, by induction on the first bit (the next affine odd coefficient is invertible modulo every power of two). If $x\le R$ has $s\le3m/5$ odd inputs, the step formula gives $T^m x\le 2\cdot3^{3m/5}$: in the numerator $3^s x+A$, each addition at date $i<m$ contributes at most $2^i3^s$. The other starts number at most $2^{mH_2(3/5)}$, with $H_2$ the binary entropy. List the distinct orbit sites up to the first repeat if there is one and discard its last $m$ positions. On the remaining list, all $m$-step images are distinct. This proves the bound with $\beta=\max((3/5)\log_2 3,H_2(3/5))<1$. Rounding the cutoff up to a power of two gives general $R$.

Here are the time and multiplicity estimates needed in (4). Let $N=2^k$, $Z=k^P$, and fix $\log_4 3<\theta<1$. Choose a large fixed $D$ and then sufficiently large fixed $P$. Starting at $x\in[N,2N)$, at each block start $m>Z$ follow the path until it first falls to $m^\theta$ or below, using at most $i=\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor$ steps. Stop altogether on reaching $Z$ or less. In the complete octave $[2^i,2^{i+1})$, at most a fraction $2i\,k^{-2D^2}$ of starts have any initial odd count at length $\ell\le i$ differing from $\ell/2$ by more than $D\sqrt{i\log k}$, by the parity bijection and the binomial tail. Every other block reaches its threshold within the cap: $T^i m\le2\cdot3^{i/2+D\sqrt{i\log k}}+(3/2)^i$, and $i\ge P\log_2 k-O(1)$. The choice of $P$ makes this less than $m^\theta$ for large $k$.

After each successful block the logarithm of the next start is at most $\theta$ times the preceding logarithm, so the total tested clock through the first bad block or the landing is $O(k)$. Each block start $m$ is a record minimum. A prefix ending there, of length $l$ with $s$ odd inputs, satisfies

$$|\log(x/m)-l\log2+s\log3|\ll k/m$$

by multiplying the exact step ratios, as in §3. For each of the $O(k)$ possible $l$ there are $O(1)$ possible $s$ because $x\in[N,2N)$ and $m>Z$. Each $(l,s)$ allows $O(1+Nk/m)$ integer starts. Charge the first failed count test to $m$. Summing $O(Nk^2/m)$ per bad $m$ over the $O(k)$ dyadic octaves gives $O(Nk^{4-2D^2})$ exceptions. Take $D$ large enough for this to be $O(N/k^4)$.

All remaining starts first enter $[1,Z]$ at $a\in(Z/2,Z]$. The odd-count errors over their successive blocks sum to $O(\sqrt{k\log k})$ by geometric decrease of the square roots of their ranks. Before landing the total log correction is $O(k/Z)$. Thus the landing clock $l$ satisfies

$$l=\kappa(k-\log_2 Z)+O(\sqrt{k\log k}).$$

Enlarging $C$ puts $J(x)$ below this clock. For each fixed $a$ the same prefix count, now at the landing, gives $O(Nk^2/Z)$ retained sources. An early visit to $S$ places the subsequent landing on $S$. There are $O(Z^\beta+\log(2Z))$ available landing sites. Take $P(1-\beta)>6$ as well as the earlier lower bound on $P$. The resulting count, including exceptions, is $O(N/k^3)$, as required. This argument treats trajectories throughout their actual first passage; no independence of successive blocks is assumed.
