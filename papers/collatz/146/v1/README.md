# Exact critical tail and linear collision energy for the ternary Syracuse law

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T16:51:21.622528Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/146/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The original ternary Syracuse law has a Haar density $\rho$ on $\mathbb Z_3$ with integrability threshold two. Previous quantitative estimates retained logarithmic losses in its tail and bounded finite-level collision energy quadratically. We prove the exact asymptotic $m_3\{\rho>t\}\sim\kappa t^{-2}$ for a positive finite constant $\kappa$. Consequently $\rho$ belongs to weak $L^2$, its moment residue satisfies $\lim_{p\uparrow2}(2-p)\int\rho^p\,dm_3=2\kappa$, and the collision energy of its reductions modulo $3^n$ is comparable to $n$.

The endpoint estimate restricts the original law by its maximum real affine multiplier. At the first crossing of a threshold, exponential tilting gives a uniform exponential moment for the reversed real offset. Fixed-total numerator spacing bounds each stopped-length contribution. The main arithmetic estimate shows that a later surviving prefix can enter an earlier stopped cylinder only by paying extra binary divisions: their probability cost dominates the number of available numerators. Exponential decay in the delay and a partition by the earliest stopped support control the entire cubic norm. Tao's fine-scale mixing supplies the fixed-cap input.

The resulting linear energy bound makes the limiting distinct-branch interaction integrable. This verifies the missing hypothesis in the previously identified density-tail renewal equation; classical implicit renewal theory then identifies $\kappa$ by a convergent interaction integral, without assuming branch independence. Two fixed exact audits check finite interfaces; all uniform bounds are analytic. The constant is not numerically evaluated, and no limit of energy divided by depth is claimed. These results do not supply pointwise integer traces or prove Collatz convergence.
