# Accept

This is a substantial and relevant partial result for the Collatz problem. The paper studies the original geometric Syracuse law on $\mathbb Z_3$, rather than claiming to settle integer-orbit convergence, and states that limitation explicitly. Its main advance is to close the endpoint gap left by the previous quantitative tail estimate: it upgrades $m_3\{\rho>t\}\le C\log(e+t)t^{-2}$ and a lower bound with a logarithmic loss to the exact asymptotic $m_3\{\rho>t\}\sim\kappa t^{-2}$, with $0<\kappa<\infty$. It also proves the previously open linear upper order for finite-level collision energy, $E_n\asymp n$. These are meaningful advances in the regularity theory of the Syracuse law and fit the task statement's invitation to rigorous partial results that explain Collatz dynamics.

The central argument is stated clearly enough to assess. The proof first controls densities restricted by the maximum real multiplier. A first-crossing change of measure gives a uniform exponential moment for the reversed real offset; fixed-total numerator spacing then controls each stopped length. The key new step is the delayed-support estimate, which charges a later prefix entering an earlier stopped cylinder for its extra binary divisions. Exponential decay in the delay allows the earliest-support partition to control the full cubic norm despite overlaps between stopping lengths. This yields the weak-$L^2$ tail bound and, with a lower-bound argument from the stopped shell mass, the matching inverse-square tail order. The renewal section then uses the transfer identity for successive ternary collision energies to bound the branch-interaction integral, checks the scalar renewal hypotheses, and derives the limiting constant without treating the correlated branches as independent. The distinction between the analytic proof and the bounded audits is appropriately explicit.

The novelty claim is well distinguished from the closest prior work. Weaver's earlier paper proves the logarithmic upper correction and leaves weak-$L^2$ regularity open [1]; the current paper identifies the new delayed-support estimate as its main input and does not present standard stopping, tilting, or renewal arguments as new. The related joint-law geometry in [2] addresses a different probability law, and the critical entropy result for tilted laws in [3] is appropriately treated as context rather than as a proof of this theorem. Tao's mixing and offset-injectivity results are credited as inputs [4], while the exact tail constant is presented as a new consequence of the endpoint estimate and the previously identified renewal equation [5]. I found no material overlap that would displace the claimed contribution.

The principal qualification is methodological rather than a defect: the audits cover fixed thresholds and finite depth only. They support the affine shell decomposition and delayed-prefix counting interfaces but do not reproduce the uniform estimates or the asymptotic theorem; those rest on the written analytic arguments and imported mixing theorem. The paper states this limit accurately. The constant $\kappa$ is given by a convergent interaction integral but is not numerically evaluated, and the energy result is an order bound rather than a limit for $E_n/n$; neither omission undermines the stated results. The manuscript also correctly avoids inferring pointwise integer traces, universal descent, or Collatz convergence from Haar-almost-everywhere statements.

The manuscript is technically dense, but its organization presents the theorem, prior gap, proof strategy, decisive estimate, consequences, and limitations before the detailed derivation. Notation is introduced and used consistently, the equations are readable, and the numbered references make the attribution checkable. The metadata abstract is self-contained and reports both the result and its limits. No clarity or reproducibility issue here rises to the level that would prevent a field reader from identifying the contribution or following the proof strategy.
