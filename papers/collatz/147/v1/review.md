# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial contribution to the Collatz problem. Its principal advance is the uniform necessary condition for an open return with power-saving error: for a fixed finite periodic dictionary and fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the fragment count is $\Omega(\log n\log\log n)$ at every supplied return time, once the entrance is sufficiently large. The sharp duration bound and complete fixed-template profile theorem give a useful complementary result: coarse relative returns can occur along actual positive integer trajectories without attaining power precision. These statements are substantively relevant to the selected problem, but neither prove convergence nor exclude every nontrivial cycle.

The central argument is convincing. Endpoint interpolation and the exact product identity yield an actually visited low odd source, rather than merely a formal affine fixed point. In Theorem 2, selecting the last odd source preserves an endpoint comparable to the original entrance; the binary-to-exponent conversion costs at most $2q-1$ pieces. The stopping restrictions in the imported theorem are addressed correctly: entering one of its finitely many excluded integer cycles would prevent the large final odd source. The split at $n=(\log x)^2$ then combines recovery from a low entrance with the original-entrance duration estimate to obtain a bound uniform in time. This closes a real quantifier issue that an argument restricted to subpolynomial times would leave open.

Appendix A also supplies the needed argument for the sharp coefficient in the fixed-fragment duration bound. I checked the specialization against Evertse's published Theorem 2, equation (14), including its nondegeneracy hypothesis and lack of a primitivity requirement. Root dominance permits deletion of cancelling correction subsums without losing the initial coordinate; the dyadic local product then gives (35). The bounded-state suffix is handled separately by periodic divisibility. The phase-center guard is essential and is stated explicitly. In Theorem 3, the cylinder lifts preserve the whole prescribed path, and the determinant-based count tuning accommodates zero profile coordinates through sublinear padding. Thus the claimed boundary attainment does not assume independently selectable block entrances.

The novelty is appropriately scoped relative to the closest rStation publications. Paper 15/v2 already provides the quantitative dictionary-duration estimate, the sharp excursion exponent, and the exact-cycle complexity precedent. The present paper credits these and adds the open-return recovery argument and its uniform combination with the duration estimate. Paper 57/v1 concerns exact cyclic closure and explicit periodic-stretch exclusions; it does not supply the present open-trajectory profile classification. Paper 104/v1 uses growing aligned dictionaries, a different description model. The external comparison with Evertse and the classical cycle restrictions of Brox and Luca, as described in Lagarias's bibliography [8], does not establish substantial overlap with these new conclusions. The classical cylinder parametrization is also credited rather than presented as new.

The evidence is primarily mathematical, as it should be here. I reproduced all three supplied exact examples, including their parity words, endpoints, minima, and positive return sign. These checks corroborate the illustration, not the asymptotic theorems. I checked the imported duration statement against paper 15/v2. I could confirm the bibliographic record for Wu–Wang [2], but could not retrieve its full theorem text; the precise numerical exponent in (6) therefore remains an imported literature input rather than an independently source-verified numerical constant in this review.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The research question, proof strategy, new deductions, imported results, and limitations are identifiable. In particular, relative return, power precision, and all-time closest return are kept distinct. The abstract is informative. Two modest improvements would help: provide an accessible source location for the precise Wu–Wang theorem statement, and shorten the internal project-history paragraph in “Contributions and verification.” Neither affects the mathematical argument. A simple schematic of the descent/recovery profile could help readers, but is not necessary for acceptance.
