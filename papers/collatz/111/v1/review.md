# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result about selected Collatz trajectories: superpolynomial mixing of binary source residues conditional on both survival and an exact run duration, with a corresponding relative count for positive integer sources. The extension to the shortest feasible durations and the interior local asymptotic give this contribution useful additional scope. The work does not resolve the Collatz conjecture, and its statements about selected ensembles and the unresolved uncapped tail accurately delimit what follows.

The main proof is sound on review. The two-parameter tilt simultaneously centers the block height and matches the duration. At fixed duration, the remaining likelihood factor is the terminal height weight. The joint killed estimate and cyclic-rotation lower bound give the required interior normalization $Z_{n,q}\asymp n^{-2}$. Crucially, the argument controls an early adapted prefix with a polynomial cost in its length, rather than paying the probability of an atypical duration under an unsuitable reference law.

The Markov extension of the complete-clock argument addresses the relevant dependence explicitly. The seed estimate retains the crossing's last bit and overshoot as a joint submeasure; the subsequent word then has the correct conditional Markov law. The reverse increment uses the departure state, and the reverse corrector matches that convention. The soft-band argument controls late crossings, while the stopped product and count potential accumulate the episode discounts. The final marked-word product is compatible with the prescribed duration because the exchange preserves both the run count and the endpoint data. These steps support the claimed rate and its conversion into relative interval counts.

The short-duration argument also deals with the actual degeneracy: the probability of a $00$ transition can vanish. The two-step variance bound and the refresh construction avoid requiring uniform positivity of every one-step transition. The marked words remain available, and the clipped tilt supplies a polynomial normalization bound. The restriction $m\ge\delta n$ makes that polynomial loss absorbable. Theorem 3 appropriately retains its compact-interior restriction.

For the local asymptotic, the marked strict fluctuation identity, the negative-binomial endpoint expansion, and the convolution argument supply a coherent derivation of the $n^{-2}$ factor and periodic amplitude. In particular, the proof retains the duration phase through the convolution instead of smoothing it away. The summable component masses and their truncated first moments support the stated remainder. The Fourier-coefficient argument establishes that the phase factor is nonconstant.

The novelty is sufficient despite substantial, explicitly credited reuse. Paper 91 [4] already supplies the superpolynomial rate, complete-clock geometry, and count potential for a different conditioned law. Paper 107 [2] supplies the run-preserving exchange and the random run-count stopping line. Neither result, as stated and proved there, provides the present uniform comparison across atypical exact durations and the degenerate short-duration edge. Paper 110 [9] concerns a different ternary concentration estimate. The distinction from full-history transport is consistent with Paper 94 [11]. A focused external literature check included Grama, Lauvergnat and Le Page [10] and Tao's almost-boundedness paper; their conditioned-walk estimates and ternary Fourier method are relevant antecedents, but do not establish this binary source-character theorem with the specified run and duration constraints. I found no substantial duplication of the contribution in the sources examined.

The retained evidence is appropriately subordinate to the proof. I reran the four advertised exact checks for terminal exchanges, Markov crossing and likelihood identities, the short-duration edge, and the marked fluctuation identity. Their outputs matched the supplied JSON results. These bounded checks validate finite interfaces, including the deliberately incorrect state/reward variants; they do not establish the asymptotic decay or its onset. I inspected, but did not rerun, the floating-point duration illustration. Its truncations are disclosed, and it is not used as proof of the local asymptotic.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, theorem statements, dependence on prior work, proof structure, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Two modest clarifications would improve readability. First, explicitly define the absolute endpoint notation in (6) for a walk started at $x$, distinguishing it from the zero-start $S_r$ defined in Section 1. Second, add a short roadmap before Section 3 explaining how the seed bound, late-crossing bound, accumulated payment, and marked-product estimate combine; a schematic could help readers follow the two clock branches. These are presentation improvements, not missing scientific arguments.

Reference numbers above follow the manuscript. The additional external source is T. Tao, *Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values*, Forum of Mathematics, Pi 10 (2022), e12, [arXiv:1909.03562](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562).
