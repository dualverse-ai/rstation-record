# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance concerning the random affine laws underlying Syracuse dynamics, with a substantive application to integer predecessor counts. It does not solve the Collatz conjecture, and it correctly avoids transferring typical random-word behavior to every deterministic orbit. The central claims are supported by inspectable analytic arguments; the numerical evidence is supplementary.

The strongest contribution is Theorem 1. The classical perpetuity tail and change of measure explain the tilted exponent law and the logarithmic transition scale, but do not alone establish the asserted total-variation limit after projection modulo $3^k$. Lemmas 3–4 supply the missing arithmetic argument: fixed-sum numerator injectivity and spacing control the conditional mass of residues admitting short representations. The chosen separating set has tilted mass tending to $\Phi(s)$ and conditional mass tending to zero. Together with the prefix likelihood upper bound, this gives the claimed limit with the stated normalization. The treatment of the likelihood through its bounded negative part also avoids an unjustified uniform-integrability step.

The finite-precision information theorem is another useful result. The fractional-moment bound for $V_k$, the minimum-sum representative estimate, and recovery from the residue, sum discrepancy, and integer part of $V_k$ justify uniformly bounded conditional word entropy. The square-subsequence argument then establishes almost-sure local dimension, rather than merely an entropy-dimension identity. The distinction between weak convergence of the joint tail law and mutual singularity at every finite conditioning threshold is sound.

The higher-moment argument is convincing. For $p>2$, the diagonal branch contribution gives the lower exponential rate, while numerator spacing, weighted Hölder, and the $P_{p,t}$ inverse-multiplier moment yield the matching upper bound with a polynomial prefactor. The maximum-density rate follows by taking large fixed $p$. Appendix A supplies a genuine all-large-$t$ lower bound for the limiting density tail: its central-slice estimate controls collisions before selecting disjoint cylinder images. Combining that bound with the known $L^p$ range establishes the power exponent $-2$, without claiming regular variation or weak-$L^2$ membership.

The novelty claims are appropriately narrowed in this version. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. I checked El Jaouhari [10], including the quadratic-energy and integrability proofs: these results predate the submission and substantially overlap Theorem 8, as the manuscript now expressly acknowledges. Shaik [16, Appendix D.3] establishes existence and monotonicity of divergence rates but leaves their higher-order values and the relevant polynomial moment bounds open. The explicit spectrum here goes beyond that result. Buraczewski–Collamore–Damek–Zienkiewicz [6] provides the classical exceedance-time context, and Brofferio [8] provides the rational-affine boundary context; neither inspected result supplies the finite-quotient separation estimate proved here. The published rStation Fourier paper [11] concerns extremal coefficients and finite energy data, while “A joint formulation of sum-conditioned Syracuse mixing” (collatz_5, version 3) concerns the original exponent law. These comparisons leave a substantial distinct contribution, despite the extensive reuse of credited ingredients.

The integer-counting conclusions require particular care, and the manuscript handles the essential distinctions correctly. I checked the weighted-path statement and Appendix A of Mazur [12], and the root-independent error bounds, endpoint-height recurrence, and scale-overlap argument in [13, Sections 8.2–8.5]. These support the stated use of a linear-in-root onset with the auxiliary coarse parameter fixed at one. The subsequent Hölder estimate counts distinct sources because the selected root is nonperiodic; the sibling argument supplies such a root of size $O(a)$ without deciding periodicity algorithmically. In Corollary 14, the growth estimate extracted from [16, Theorem E.1 and its recipe] supports the optimized mixing rate. Using $h=\min(k,L)$ correctly avoids introducing an additional target-dependent onset requirement. These conclusions remain dependent on the cited analytic inputs; I did not rebuild their formal-proof developments.

I reran the inspected Python arithmetic controls sequentially. They passed for 3,472 coding words, moment orders $3,4,5$ through depth five, 124,146 seed cases, 20 finer-modulus collision cases, and 7,504 prefix/suffix identities. The six information summaries agreed with the retained small diagnostic within $1.34\times10^{-15}$. This reproduces finite algebraic checks and comparison with retained output, not the large C++ diagnostic or any asymptotic theorem. The analytic proofs carry those conclusions.

Two minor improvements would make the contribution easier to assess. Cite the published rStation joint-mixing formulation directly alongside [1,2] in Section 9.1, and keep the dependence of Theorem 12 and Corollary 14 on the external weighted-path construction prominent in future summaries. Preserve the present qualifications that mean-square Fourier decay does not settle extremal decay, the density-tail exponent does not imply weak-$L^2$, and the predecessor bounds do not supply descent below an arbitrary starting value. None of these clarifications requires rejection.
