# Accept

The update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper. Theorems 16 and 18 establish new upper regularity estimates for real-restricted ternary laws, including quantitative stability under an exact central division total. The accepted contributions are retained, and the new statements preserve the distinction between random affine laws and individual integer orbits.

Theorem 16's proof supports both its localization claim and its improved critical-tail bound. Fixed-total injectivity gives a contribution at most $4B$ per total to the truncated prefix density; the exponential bound on large totals then yields (B4). Freezing a Lipschitz real test before applying ternary mixing controls refinement errors in the norm of measures, without an erroneous factor of $3^n$. Interpolation with the prefix supremum bound gives every finite $L^q$ moment for bounded real restrictions.

The decisive additional step is retaining the shell mass in (B9)–(B10). The frozen selector has mass $O(B^{-1})$, and its freezing error becomes small enough at depth $N_B=O(\log(eB))$. This justifies the stated shell estimate, including its dependence on $B$. Summing shells at $q=3$ gives $\|g_B\|_3^3\le CB\log^2(eB)$; balancing this against the residual mass $O(B^{-1})$ at $B=t/\log t$ proves the logarithmic correction in (B3). The subsequent $(2-p)^{-2}$ moment estimate follows by layer cake. The argument needs only a fixed mixing order for this conclusion, so it does not conceal an uncontrolled optimization over mixing constants. It correctly leaves weak-$L^2$ regularity unresolved.

The exact-clock extension is also justified. The stationary domination in (C3) supplies uniform finite-prefix moment bounds. The negative-binomial estimates in (C4) control the central conditioning likelihood and its variation. Crucially, (C6) uses the joint estimate of [17] while retaining the remaining division sum: convolution with the unread sum contracts the error, and restriction to a central total costs only $O_C(\sqrt n)$. Interpolating and summing these refinement errors, then comparing at $r=\lfloor n^\delta\rfloor$, gives the claimed $n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ rate after choosing fixed sufficiently large mixing orders. The sharp cutoff conclusion is appropriately stated as a norm bound, rather than a convergence rate for discontinuous tests. The all-one example correctly rules out uniform extension of (C2) to arbitrary totals.

Corollary 17 follows from the new tail estimate through the small-set integral bound (B13). Conditional expectation and the Haar-preserving maps in (35) transfer that bound to the finite reference densities. The normalized residue multiplicity used in (B14) is bounded by one, and the resulting inequality forces the asserted $1/[R^2\log(eR)]$ source fraction. The existing seed selection and weighted-path construction retain the previous linear onset without an additional precision threshold. This remains an application of the external integer-path input, not a consequence of congruence mixing alone.
