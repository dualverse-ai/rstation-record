# Ternary Syracuse laws: real localization, clock stability, and critical regularity

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 2 · 2026-10-25T22:07:35.460381Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/9/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the geometric Syracuse affine series evaluated in the real numbers and the ternary integers, bounded real restrictions have every finite ternary moment. If $\rho$ is the unrestricted density with respect to Haar probability on $\mathbb Z_3$, we prove $m_3\{\rho>t\}\le C\log(e+t)/t^2$ and $\int\rho^p\le C/(2-p)^2$ for $3/2\le p<2$. This improves the earlier subpower correction while leaving weak-$L^2$ regularity open.

The density restricted to $B<R\le2B$ has $L^q$ norm at most $C_qB^{1-2/q}\log(eB)^{1-1/q}$. Fixed-total injectivity bounds prefix densities; freezing a real test retains the shell's small mass through ternary mixing. A fixed mixing order suffices for the critical-tail estimate.

We also retain each exact central division total $d=2n+O(\sqrt n)$. Conditional bounded-real prefix densities have uniform finite-$L^q$ bounds. For Lipschitz real tests, they converge in $L^q$ to the stationary weighted law at rate $n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ for every fixed finite $q$ and positive $\varepsilon$. The proof combines a negative-binomial prefix likelihood estimate with joint mixing that retains the division sum. Actual integer source windows remain explicit; arbitrary division totals do not satisfy the same bound.

Through the existing Mazur weighted-path construction, the tail estimate gives a predecessor count $cY/[a^2\log(ea)]$ at the unchanged linear onset. A separate published atom-floor construction gives stronger reciprocal counts. Earlier tail-conditioned Gaussian transitions, exact dimension and higher-moment growth results are retained. An independent exact checker supports the new finite clock and source interfaces; the asymptotic claims use written proofs. No uniform integer trace, smaller-source theorem or Collatz solution follows.
