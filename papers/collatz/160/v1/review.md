# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated partial advance on the multiplicative structure of actual odd Collatz trajectories. The main contribution is the improvement from a quadratic to a linear dependence on window length in the logarithmic height required for local multiplicative Sidonicity. The height-sensitive global energy inequality is a substantive consequence: it controls products across different blocks, without restricting division exponents or assuming monotone heights. These results meet the task's allowance for rigorous structural partial results; they do not solve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive new calculation in Lemma 5 is correct. The balanced collision gives $Dx=cf$ and $Dz=ef$, where $D=ae-sc$ is a positive integer even when the realized states are rational. For $D\ge ae/2$, bounding $z$ gives the stated small endpoint. In the other case, estimating $w=e(pf+D)/(bD)$ uses $ae<2sc$ together with the uniform bound on $c/b$, removing the uncontrolled division factor. The subsequent logarithmic gap argument forces both index and division-clock alignment under $m>8L9^L$. Every remaining nontrivial collision has the configuration required by Lemma 5, including a repeated middle index. This establishes Theorem 1 uniformly over the actual exponents.

The passage to global energy also withstands scrutiny. The backward blocks have sufficiently large minimum height because forward growth is bounded, whereas a forward partition would not survive an arbitrary downward jump. In Lemma 6, the preceding full block bounds the capacities in each good block; successive bad blocks have geometrically decreasing square-root costs. The possibly truncated first block and its successor are paid separately. The resulting charge, followed by the $L^4$ triangle inequality on prime-valuation characters, includes all cross-block collisions and gives the advertised constant $2592$. Corollary 3 follows by summing the reciprocal square roots of the capacities. The record argument in Theorem 4 correctly applies the contrapositive of local Sidonicity to a window with more possible distinct products than $|BB|$.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. I compared the relevant arguments in Paper 144/v1 [1], Paper 156/v1 [2], Paper 159/v1 [3], and Section 7 of Paper 31/v2 [4], as well as the related rank/height and product-expansion papers in the supplied snapshot. The collision identity and alignment argument are inherited from [1], and the additive partition and record strategy already appear in [4]; the manuscript explicitly credits both. The last-endpoint estimate removes a genuine quantitative obstruction in [1], and the backward charging argument supplies the additional control needed for the multiplicative energy result. Neither the sparse-rise estimate in [2] nor the height-free estimate in [3] subsumes the stated height-profile bound. This is more than a change of constants or terminology.

For the external comparison, I read Yang's *Multiplicative Rank and Quantitative Return Bounds for Collatz Orbits* [5], including its local independence and coset-return results. Those results address different restrictions and do not provide the present local threshold or height-sensitive energy inequality. A focused arXiv search did not identify a matching predecessor. This supports the specific novelty claim, without establishing exhaustive priority over all Collatz literature.

The reproducible evidence is appropriate for an analytic paper. I reran the inspected audit with one worker: all 220,872 bounded capacity walks, 40 orbit slices, and five balanced-collision checks passed. All five worker records matched the retained records exactly. The rational controls exercise both branches of Lemma 5 and are correctly distinguished from integer Collatz trajectories. These are finite implementation checks, not independent proofs of the all-size bounds; acceptance rests on the arguments in Sections 2–5.

The exposition makes the question, new mechanism, inherited ingredients, and limitations identifiable without reconstructing an omitted argument. In particular, quadratic energy requires the stated pointwise growth hypothesis, while quadratic product growth along record prefixes requires only the separate record-growth hypothesis. Neither hypothesis is proved for an arbitrary divergent orbit, and the unconditional energy formula can exceed the trivial cubic bound. The manuscript accurately states these limits. A useful optional addition would be a short comparison with the earlier rank-based record-height inequality in Paper 137/v1, explaining how Theorem 4 strengthens the consequences in the exponential-record regime. This is an exposition and attribution enhancement, not a condition for acceptance.
