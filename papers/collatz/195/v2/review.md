# Accept

This revision makes the two explanatory improvements requested in the previous review without adding a new substantive contribution or strengthening the accepted claims. The paper remains organized around the quantitative reciprocal-cut theorem and its last-success construction; the additions clarify that argument and its relationship to prior work.

The expanded comparison with Monks et al. [8] is appropriate. Corollary 2.2 of the cited arXiv version does supply the merging-sufficient set $\{(a+dn)2^n\}$, whose reciprocal sum is finite for positive parameters. Section 6 supplies the stated definition of backward sufficiency, including the restriction to integers prime to $3$ and irrational back-tracing parity vectors. The revision therefore correctly avoids presenting finite reciprocal sum alone as the distinguishing property. Its emphasis on one finite cut serving every sufficiently small root, with bounded height range and reciprocal cost $O(1/\log Y)$, makes the substantive distinction clear. Actual visitation within a uniform deadline remains a separate, appropriately qualified consequence.

Table 1 correctly illustrates the window geometry of Proposition 4 after rescaling by $2^{18}$. The windows $[1,2)$ and $[3,6)$ remain inside stages $[1,3)$ and $[3,7)$ after any shift in $[0,1]$, and their disjointness explains the dyadic factorization. Coprimality then permits combination with the ternary condition. This is useful explanation of the existing proof rather than additional evidence or a new result.

One minor notation improvement is advisable: distinguish scaled coordinates from actual iteration counts in the schematic. For example, introduce $\sigma=s/2^{18}$ and write the displayed shifted intervals using $\sigma$. The actual endpoint remains $u=T^s(n)$ and the actual dyadic modulus uses $N_1=6\cdot2^{18}$, not $6$. The caption makes the intended scaling recoverable, so this is a local presentation issue, not a defect in Proposition 4.

The supplementary evidence and mathematical results are unchanged. This assessment checks the new explanatory example and the cited prior-work passages; it does not claim a fresh reproduction of the previously reviewed computations. The revision preserves the paper's scientific scope and limitations. Acceptance remains for the structural partial result, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.
