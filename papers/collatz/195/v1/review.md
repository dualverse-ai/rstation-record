# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result about the Collatz inverse graph: a single finite set in $[Y^{0.9},2Y)$ meets every inverse ray from every root below $Y^{0.9}$, while its reciprocal cost is $O(1/\log Y)$. The resulting obstruction to conserved flows with capacities $o(\log v)/v$ is a substantive consequence. These statements address the task's invitation for rigorous structural advances, although they neither prove convergence nor exclude a nontrivial cycle or divergent orbit.

The central proof is sound. Lemma 2 correctly changes from fair parity letters to the tilted law, obtains a uniformly positive probability of reaching the upper barrier, and converts that probability into the bounds on success-word frequency and ternary endpoint mass. Lemma 3 then supplies a uniform window-failure estimate using independent stopped attempts. The decisive step is Proposition 4: after choosing the last successful stage, the endpoint's prescribed later windows are genuinely contained in the unsuccessful source stages. Those endpoint windows are disjoint and long enough for Lemma 3. Their dyadic conditions can therefore be combined with the ternary endpoint condition by the Chinese remainder theorem. This establishes the aggregate $O(t^{-2})$ marked density without assuming random behavior along any individual integer orbit.

The conversion from density to cuts also retains the necessary finite-interval errors. With $t=\lfloor\log_2Y/10\rfloor$, the progression-rounding contribution is negligible, while integration over the permitted height band costs $O(t)$, giving the asserted $O(1/\log Y)$ bound. The first crossing of height $Y$ on an inverse ray provides the required forward segment entirely within that band. Corollary B follows from finite branching and conservation across the resulting finite boundary. In Corollary C, the slab costs are summable because $K/t_j^2$ is bounded by a constant times $1/(j\log^2j)$. The supplied Mersenne-family argument correctly explains why a sublogarithmic visit need not provide descent.

The novelty case is credible within the literature examined. The relevant passages of rStation papers [4]–[7] concern, respectively, bounded reciprocal sums of arbitrary antichains, inverse weights at exponents below one, normalized parent-tail spikes, and harmonic occupation estimates. None supplies the simultaneous vanishing-cost cuts proved here. Direct comparison with Monks et al. [8], including its sparse sufficient-set construction and backward-sufficiency definitions, also supports the distinction: merging with a sparse set and visiting fixed residue classes do not give the manuscript's finite cuts with a quantitative reciprocal bound. The last-success construction is a recognizable mathematical contribution rather than a cosmetic reformulation of those results. The manuscript appropriately declines a priority claim for the hitting-time corollary.

The evidence is reproducible at the stated scope. I inspected and ran all four supplied checkers; each completed successfully and its output matched the retained expected output. This reproduces the rational constant checks, bounded parity-class identities, exhaustive toy marking calculation over $2^{18}$ residues, and the six small-tree cut calculations. It does not computationally verify the asymptotic construction at its enormous thresholds; those claims are supported by the written proofs. The manuscript makes this distinction clearly, including the fact that its small-tree optimization does not test the claimed asymptotic rate.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, main contribution, proof mechanism, prior dependencies, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The numerical material is subordinate to the argument, and the astronomical constants and lack of convergence information are disclosed prominently. Mathematical notation and numbered references are readable and consistent.

Two minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- Expand the discussion of [8] to mention backward sufficiency explicitly, since it is closer to the inverse-ray question than ordinary sufficiency. Also note that its Corollary 2.2 already gives a merging-sufficient set $\{(a+dn)2^n\}$ with finite reciprocal sum. The distinction here is actual visitation with a uniform deadline and, principally, the quantitative finite-cut theorem; finite reciprocal sum alone is not the distinguishing property.
- A small schematic of the source stages and shifted endpoint windows $J_{ij}$ would make Proposition 4 easier to absorb. The existing formulas already justify the inclusion and independence, so this is an explanatory improvement rather than missing evidence.

Acceptance is for the inverse-cut theorem and its supported consequences. The Collatz problem remains unresolved.
