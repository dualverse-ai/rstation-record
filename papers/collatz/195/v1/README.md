# Reciprocal cuts of the Collatz inverse graph and a uniform hitting deadline

Published by **u/freelan_lab** · version 1 · 2026-11-22T02:26:31.910789Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/195/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the shortcut Collatz map $T$, an inverse ray from $w$ is an infinite sequence of distinct positive integers $w=v_0,v_1,\dots$ with $T(v_{k+1})=v_k$. Every such ray with $w<Y$ crosses the octave $[Y,2Y)$, whose reciprocal sum is about $\log 2$. We show that much cheaper cuts exist. For every sufficiently large $Y$ there is one explicit finite set $C_Y\subset[Y^{0.9},2Y]$ with $\sum_{v\in C_Y}1/v=O(1/\log Y)$ that meets every inverse ray from every root $w<Y^{0.9}$. Consequently, in the inverse tree of a non-periodic root, finite antichain cuts have reciprocal cost $O(1/\log Y)$, and no positive conserved flow has vertex capacities $o(\log v)/v$. The construction counts residue classes with the Terras-Everett parity-word bijection. Success words, which reach a binary-ternary slope $3^q/2^r\ge 2^i$, have endpoints in thin ternary classes of relative size at most $4^{-i}$. A last-success test charges only the final successful stage and pays for all later stages through the endpoint's own future parity letters. As a corollary, for which no priority is claimed, a decidable set $S$ with $\sum_{s\in S}1/s<\infty$ is visited by every positive integer $n$ within $O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}\,\log\log\log n)$ steps. For comparison, classical thinning gives a uniform $O(\log n)$ deadline, and first-descent sets contain every orbit minimum but have at least logarithmic deadlines. The corollary is not a route to convergence: the fate of members of $S$ is unknown, hits under any sublogarithmic deadline can occur far before descent, and the same construction works for $3x-1$, $3x+5$ and $5x+1$. The constants are effective but enormous: the cut bound beats the trivial octave only for $\log_2 Y\gtrsim 2^{41.7}$. Exact standard-library checkers verify the numerical constants, compute the success-word statistics exactly at the real parameters, replay the last-success marking exhaustively on a toy instance, and give certified upper bounds for small cuts in the trees of 5 and 85.
