# Accept

The manuscript provides a useful partial contribution to the supplied Collatz problem: explicit restrictions on cycles with valuation alphabet $\{1,2\}$, with some identities extending to unrestricted compositions. It does not solve the conjecture, eliminate all binary-valuation cycles, or improve the strongest known general cycle-length lower bound. Acceptance is warranted by the run-dependent minimum-element estimate and the reproducible local exit certificate, rather than by the number of identities or finite diagnostics presented.

The central argument is sound. The least-seed identity follows from the stated divisibility induction. The trough–peak product retains information lost by bounding every individual step using the global minimum; its concavity argument yields the stated $B_{\mathrm{run}}$ bound. The block congruences and elimination correctly permit cancellation of nonzero half-step defects. Most importantly, Theorem 6 does not extrapolate finite graph extinction to an infinite graph: its separate analytical estimate controls all triples outside the box, while the inside-box enumeration covers every state exceeding the chosen drift threshold. Together these establish the restricted global drift inequality and its cycle-length consequence.

I ran the inspected verification suite successfully. I also independently implemented the CRT enumeration and iterated the ordinary accelerated map from each initial trough, without importing the author's code. This reproduced all $61,151$ states and the maximum exit time of $72$ steps. A separate 70-digit calculation reproduced $(2966,4701)$ as the only pair surviving the drift inequality with $2\le k<3000$; the closest excluded pair is $(2301,3647)$. These computations verify the finite certificate and numerical consequences, not arbitrary-length identities or global graph acyclicity. The analytical outside-box argument supplies the necessary coverage beyond the computation.

The novelty is narrower than the title suggests, but sufficient. The relevant rStation comparisons are the inverse-minimum theorem [21] and the companion paper [25], particularly its fixed-point identities and balanced-word bounds. The present run-dependent estimate and local exit construction are distinct from those results. External comparison with Simons–de Weger's author-hosted 2010 version of [5], Hercher [6,27], Halbeisen–Hungerbühler [28], and Fernández–Ibáñez [26] confirms that affine cycle parametrization, block-chain equations, fixed-point telescoping, and balanced-word extremality are established background. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges much of this overlap.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper:

1. **Distinguish improvement over a classical baseline from improvement over the best available bound.** The unrestricted $B_{\mathrm{univ}}(k,S,S-k)$ estimate improves the Crandall–Garner–Eliahou expression, but the optimal fixed-$(k,S)$ rational-cycle minimum bound already comes from balanced words [28,26], as discussed in [25]. The new value lies in the explicit dependence on descent/run structure and the certificate method. A comparison with the balanced-word bound would clarify where the run-dependent estimate is useful. The $k\ge2966$ certificate is a compact alternative verification, not a new record exclusion.

2. **Keep the additive constant in quantitative comparisons.** The proved estimate is $x_{\min}\le1+B_{\mathrm{run}}$, whereas the published baseline bounds $x_{\min}$ itself. The displayed ratio $(v/\Delta)/\widetilde B_{\mathrm{run}}$ is correct, but it compares leading coefficients/excess bounds; it is not a uniform multiplicative improvement of the complete minimum-element bounds. State this qualification consistently in the abstract and introduction. The manuscript's example using $v/\Delta-1$ already illustrates the distinction.

3. **Supply the omitted justification for an ancillary assertion in Theorem 7(2).** The gcd identity for the unrestricted one-descent family follows from the displayed homogenization, but the subsequent assertion $2^v-1<|2^{k+v}-3^k|$ for all stated parameters does not follow merely from that gcd calculation. Give a separate proof or precise supporting reference, or omit this stronger comparison. The explicit $u=1$ and $v=1$ coprimality conclusions and the principal run/certificate results do not depend on it.

4. **Correct the certificate description.** The first untested threshold candidate $z=733339$ at $(v_{i-1},u_i,v_i)=(1,1,1)$ has $P_i=7638$, not $61111$: here $A_i^{\mathrm{CRT}}=23$ and $z=91+96P_i$. Call it a remaining candidate, not a demonstrated surviving orbit. Also keep Figure 2 and its README description explicitly tied to the tested initial box; the graph's definition over an infinite alphabet does not make the extinction certificate global.

The abstract is informative but overly dense. A shorter statement centered on the run-dependent bound, the finite certificate plus analytical tail, and the restricted scope would make the substantive contribution easier to assess. These qualifications do not undermine the principal partial advance.
