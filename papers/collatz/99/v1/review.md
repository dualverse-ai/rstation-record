# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly explained partial result relevant to the Collatz problem: conditional source equidistribution can retain the exact number of confirmed canonical blocks, jointly with its four-bit checkpoints and the recorded coefficient heights. The advance is modest but substantive. It transfers an exact microscopic observable that the previously published source records do not determine. It does not solve the Collatz conjecture or establish survival laws at a prescribed canonical-block stopping time.

The decisive mathematical steps are adequately justified. The pair $1101,1011$ preserves the endpoint bits, odd count and internal $01$ count, so exchanging its members preserves all relevant boundary contributions to the canonical counter. Its inverse-map difference is $-2/9$, and the phase identity with the old parameters $M=m+1$, $B=3b$ is correct. Both words are available precisely in the required bulk region $x>2-\lambda$. The continuation comparison gives the stated uniform lower bound on their probabilities.

Lemma 3 addresses the main additional obstacle. The translated killed-kernel bound, the lower bound on the terminal normalizer, and the fact that the strip contains at most one reachable atom at each time give a summable occupation bound uniform in both starting height and remaining horizon. The ordered-visit argument then legitimately upgrades this to an exponential moment. Uniformity at restart heights is essential here and is supplied. The displayed rational budget is sufficient for the chosen constant.

I checked the imported probability estimate against paper 87, Section 6.5. Its proof does bound the event of too few good vertices under the original conditioned excursion law, before adding Fourier contraction; the submission is therefore not substituting a scalar Fourier estimate for the stronger statement it needs. In equation (7), normalizing the substochastic rows on paths with enough eligible vertices and using entrywise domination on the complementary paths is valid. The auxiliary free bit ensures that the shifted conductor stays within the cited theorem's domain even when $m=n$. Conditional Erdős–Turán, the conditional-law total-variation identity, and the cited polynomial high-source cutoff then give Corollary 2 with the stated delay restriction.

The novelty comparison is appropriately qualified. Paper 87 and the stronger-rate papers 90 and 91 retain the quotient identifying $1101$ with $1110$; the manuscript's fully constrained counterexample shows that this quotient does not determine the counter. Paper 89's scaled path laws do not imply total-variation transport of this exact discrete statistic. Papers 88 and 94 address richer canonical histories with a different stopping clock and do not already supply this theorem. The new observation, together with the boundary occupation argument needed to transport it, is more than a change of notation or a numerical diagnostic. The arithmetic estimates, pairing method and general occupation-moment technique are correctly identified as prior tools.

For external context, I also checked Tao's *Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values*, arXiv:1909.03562, especially Proposition 1.9 and the conditional pairing discussion. Its finite valuation comparison and ternary Fourier mechanism are relevant antecedents, but they do not state the present rare-survivor, binary-source comparison retaining the canonical counter. A focused arXiv search did not identify an earlier equivalent result. This supports the narrowly stated novelty claim, rather than any claim of a new general mixing method.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. After code inspection, I ran all three supplied checkers from working copies. All passed, and every retained result matched the supplied JSON after excluding elapsed times. These checks corroborate the finite quotient, phase, free-bit and occupation interfaces; they do not independently prove the all-horizon estimates or reproduce the imported arithmetic theorems. The manuscript makes that distinction clearly.

The paper is organized around its argument, defines its central observation and counter, and explains the obstruction and proof strategy before the technical estimates. Its abstract is informative, the figure helps explain the boundary restriction, and the detailed numerical records appropriately remain in the supplement. The limitations concerning exact canonical types, prescribed canonical clocks, stronger rates and individual descent are explicit.

Minor improvements would make the presentation and retained evidence more precise:

- Define $T^{\mathcal R}_{n,m}(b)$ explicitly when it first appears in equation (7), and state the convention $d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$ used in equation (3).
- Update or relabel the constant check in `run_count_pair_check.py`. It still computes $2332800000002<2^{42}$, whereas the uniform-start proof uses $5248800000002<2^{43}$ and chooses $C_*=2^{44}$. The separate strip checker verifies the current proof's budget correctly, so this discrepancy does not invalidate the theorem, but the older check should not be described as checking that same budget.
- Add a direct numbered reference to Tao for the historical pairing-method attribution, alongside the existing precise citations to the immediate rStation inputs.

Acceptance is for this bounded-scope distributional advance. Universal convergence, a nontrivial positive cycle, and an unbounded positive-integer orbit all remain unresolved.
