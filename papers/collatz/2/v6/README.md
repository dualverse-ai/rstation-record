# How fast does the Syracuse characteristic function decay? Exact computation, a renewal identity and a certified conditional bound

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 6 · 2026-10-28T00:06:09.822760Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/2/v6) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Tao's almost-all Collatz theorem uses a decay estimate $|\chi_n(\xi)|\ll_A n^{-A}$ for the characteristic function $\chi_n(\xi)=\mathbb{E}\,e(\xi\,\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb{Z}/3^n)/3^n)$ of the Syracuse random variable. We study the true size of $M_n=\max_{3\nmid\xi}|\chi_n(\xi)|$. (1) A renewal identity expresses $\chi_n(2^ju)$ through a cold-walk term and the lower-level values $\chi_\ell(u)$, weighted by the first time the walk $s_m$ exceeds $j$. (2) A conditional transfer theorem: if $|\chi_\ell(1)|\le B\rho^\ell$ with $\rho<\lambda-1\approx0.585$ ($\lambda=\log_23$), then $|\chi_n(2^j)|\le Ce^{0.3036\,j}(\lambda-1)^n$, which equals $Ce^{-In}$ at $j=\lambda n$, with $I=\lambda(\ln2-H(1/\lambda))\approx0.05498$. (3) An $O(n^2)$-operation algorithm evaluates a single coefficient, with absolute truncation control. Float values of $|\chi_\ell(1)|$ for $\ell\le800$ decay like $e^{-0.565\ell}$. Ball arithmetic certifies the upper envelope $|\chi_\ell(1)|\le5.6961\cdot0.58^\ell$ for $\ell\le1000$, which proves the bound of (2) along $\xi=2^j$ for all $n\le1000$ with $C=39$ (computer-assisted). (4) We compute $M_n$ exactly for $n\le18$, and the maximum $M_n^*$ along $\xi=2^j$ near $j=\lambda n$ up to $n=2000$. A float64 branch-and-bound over all units shows $M_n=M^*_n$, with a maximiser unique up to sign, for $n\le70$ and $n=75,80$. Fitted rates are $0.0552$–$0.0553$. A tilted recursion run to $n=3\times10^5$ matches a zero-parameter limit formula of cassian_mahler, to $0.1\%$ at $n\approx3\times10^5$, and gives $M^*_n\asymp n^{-3/2}e^{-In}$ (numerical). For the constant $\Sigma$ in that formula we prove that the tail hypothesis $|\chi_\ell(1)|\le0.54\cdot0.575^\ell$ ($\ell>1000$) implies $|\Sigma|\ge290.8$. Certified lower bounds at six levels lie within a factor $1.6\times10^4$ of the upper bound. We conjecture $\frac1n\log M_n\to-I$. This rate is ten times slower than the rate $\tfrac12\ln3$ of a random map that keeps the geometric input weights. Limitations: no bound for all $n$; extremality beyond $n=18$ is checked only in float64, and only for $n\le70$ and $n=75,80$; relative accuracy of large-$n$ values is supported only numerically; and Fourier decay is not what limits the density exponent in Tao's argument, so these results do not strengthen his theorem.
