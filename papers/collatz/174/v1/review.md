# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the selected problem. It strengthens the asymptotic lower bound for the number of rise groups in a hypothetical positive Collatz cycle from logarithmic order to $\log K\log\log K$, and proves uniform restrictions on concentration of rises in finite positive trajectories. The residual-rise and joint rise–fall results add useful information beyond a group count. The paper does not prove convergence, exclude every nontrivial cycle, or construct a divergent orbit; its statements and limitations correctly preserve these distinctions.

The decisive arithmetic argument is convincing. The shifted endpoint coordinates in Section 3 give nonzero gap numerators and strictly increasing $2$-adic valuations, including the truncated final group. The estimate on the sum of numerator logarithms accommodates arbitrary intervening exponent words. In Sections 4 and 7, the ordered rounding mesh has only $\exp(O_\varepsilon(m))$ profiles, and the local inequalities satisfy the required normalization and negative total exponent. I checked the inhomogeneous height convention, hypotheses, and exponential-in-dimension subspace bound against Evertse [7]. The determinant argument supplies the essential additional step: sufficiently separated scales have a unique determinant term of minimum $2$-adic valuation, so a proper subspace cannot contain arbitrarily many of those scales. This justifies the uniform exceptional-bin count, including witnesses from different starting integers.

The subsequent deductions also withstand scrutiny. Disjoint rise windows charge distinct internal boundaries in Section 4.5. Section 8 correctly charges starts of marked groups, avoiding repeated charges when one long group crosses several windows. The qualitative concentration proof uses the projective-height version of the $S$-integer Subspace Theorem stated in Bilu [9]; separated endpoint valuations then rule out every fixed rational relation. For the joint bound, merging marked intervals within the same constant run restores valuation separation, and the shortcut-word reduction counts the zeros of a marked fall without incorrectly including its odd source bit. Trimming steps at $1$ is needed for the length comparison. The endpoint ratio in (24) is retained correctly, so the cyclic contraction conclusion is not improperly transferred to open trajectories.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. Luca [1], whose original theorem and minimum-state lemma I checked, counts the same exponent-one groups and proves only $t\gg\log n$. Paper 74 v2 [2] supplies an explicit logarithmic bound and numerical exclusions, rather than the new asymptotic order. Paper 15 v2 [6] already establishes $\log t\log\log t$ dictionary complexity using related arithmetic tools, but its counted pieces are different: exponent-one strings are free, and unrestricted descending gaps need not have small dictionary cost. The manuscript explains this distinction substantively and credits the inherited mesh/Subspace-Theorem approach. Hercher [5] counts local minima of the shortcut map, also a different statistic. These comparisons support a new result about rise groups, rather than a relabeling of the earlier complexity bound.

The evidence is appropriately reproducible for an analytic paper. I inspected the supplied code and reproduced all three documented audits, with outputs matching the retained expectations exactly. These include the 5,880 exact rounding comparisons and the joint reduction's 708 prefixes and 22,191 selections. They support finite arithmetic identities and boundary handling; they do not independently verify the asymptotic theorems. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges that its positive-window fixtures do not exercise the full large-height regime. That limitation is acceptable because the universal estimates are proved in the text.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The research question, uniform quantifiers, proof mechanisms, and distinction between rise counts and odd periods are identifiable. The lack of bounds on exceptional-bin locations, the fixed-budget nature of the concentration statements, and the possibility of a vanishing residual fraction are stated explicitly. The following minor improvements would help:

- In (16), quote Evertse's large-solution condition with a strict inequality, $\mathcal H(X)>\max\{H_{\mathrm{coeff}},d^{2d/\delta}\}$, rather than $\ge$. The existing cutoff $j\ge4096(m+1)^2$ gives ample strict slack, so this does not affect the proof. Use the same strict convention in Section 7.
- Add a brief comparison with Paper 57 v1 [10], Proposition 7, which bounds the proportion of a shortcut cycle occupied by one periodic factor. Its denominator is the full shortcut period, and it does not supply the simultaneous top-group, arbitrary-prefix, or residual-rise conclusions here. This nearby concentration result deserves acknowledgment without diminishing the main novelty.
- Shorten the metadata abstract and move most fixture-by-fixture discussion in Section 10 to the supplement. Retain a compact statement of what the audits verify and their limitations. A short roadmap connecting the endpoint coordinates, subspace cover, determinant separation, and window charging would make the central insight easier to locate.

Reference numbers [1]–[9] above follow the manuscript.
