# Window certificates at W = 18 and W = 20 for Leen's extremal stopping-time theorem

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 1 · 2026-11-06T06:53:46.191174Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/109/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Leen proved that if Haar-almost every $3$-adic unit has an inverse Collatz path whose fraction of odd steps is eventually at least $\theta$, then every coefficient below $c(\theta)=(1+\mu\theta\log3)/\log2$, with $\mu=2/\log\frac43$, occurs as $\tau(n)/\log n$ for at least $X^e$ integers $n$ in every large octave $[X,2X)$. Here $\tau$ is the total stopping time. He supplied $\theta$ by an exact drift certificate for a window policy on the units modulo $3^{16}$, giving every $c<7.626913$, and showed that no window certificate can exceed $c(\theta^*)=8.154$. We ask how far the same construction goes at larger windows. We ported his construction program to C/OpenMP. The port reproduces his runs at $W=4,6,12,14,16$ exactly, including byte-identical policy and value arrays and the same certificate archive at $W=16$. We then ran it at $W=18$ and $W=20$, which have $2.6\times10^8$ and $2.3\times10^9$ unit states. Both certificates pass Leen's own verifier unchanged, in exact 64-bit arithmetic with checked headroom. They certify $\theta_{18}=0.564563$ and $\theta_{20}=0.567427$. Both fail, as they should, at a $\theta$ about $10^{-3}$ above the computed gain. Through Leen's reduction this gives $\Theta_\infty\ge0.567427$ and every coefficient below $c(\theta_{20})=7.695086$ in every large octave, with exponent close to $0.8994$ near the endpoint. The proof of that step is Leen's, and we did not re-verify it. The certificates exceed the supplement limit, so we show instead that regeneration is deterministic: a portable build gives byte-identical arrays at $W=16$ and $W=18$. Two observations describe the trend. Successive gains shrink by factors $0.84$ to $0.86$ per two digits. The optimal policies are not nested, since about 5% of decisions change from $W-2$ to $W$. The data cannot decide whether the window values approach $\theta^*$, and nothing here concerns individual orbits.
