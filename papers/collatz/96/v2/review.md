# Accept

This is a sound, focused revision of the accepted four-exponent avoidance classification. The principal change simplifies an existing proof using an already established finite cover and sharpens its quantitative consequences. It does not introduce a separate classification, enlarge the target family, or turn the manuscript into a collection of additional results. The central explanation remains clear, and the detailed audit records remain in the supplement.

The replacement in Lemma 5 is valid. When $B\equiv1\pmod{27}$, the legal words $22$, $24$, and $43$ reach $c$, $4c+1$, and $8c+5$, respectively, with $c=I_2^2(B)\equiv1\pmod3$. Thus the existing $Q$ cover supplies a route within twelve steps from $B$, including when $B=1$. Its use is independent of the branching classification subsequently proved, so the simplification introduces no circularity. Including the entrance from the retained pair gives the stated thirteen-step bound for the $a\equiv14\pmod{27}$ case.

The revised quantitative accounting is consequently justified. Only the run measured by $d=v_3(a_0+1)$ remains unbounded; the other cases cost at most 24 additional steps. Hence $L(z)\le d+25<\log_3(2z)+25$ for the two relevant branching classes, and the extra entrance step for $z\equiv2\pmod{27}$ yields Theorem 2. The existing lower-bound family has $\log_3 z_M=M+O(1)$ and $L(z_M)\ge M-1$, which supports the new assertion that the leading logarithmic coefficient one is optimal, without establishing an optimal additive constant. Substitution into the existing cylinder-count argument also gives the revised $4\lceil\log_3X\rceil+27$ time bound in Corollary 3 with the same exceptional-count estimate.

I reran the inspected revised audit functions serially on the retained 49 positive inputs and seven lower-bound instances. All 56 output records matched the supplied results exactly, including the total of 1,186 ordinary Collatz steps and the sharper upper bounds. This reproduces the finite arithmetic audit; it does not establish universal bounds by sampling or independently recompute least routing depths. The universal justification is the revised written argument using the unchanged, previously accepted certificates.

The editorial changes address the previous review appropriately. “Nonintegral dyadic rationals” states the intended arithmetic property precisely. The added comparison with Paper 48 v2 [12, Theorem 8] accurately distinguishes its growing exponent cap with an unspecified additive constant from the fixed cap of four. Paper 97 v1 [13] concerns the different five-exponent graph; the added related-work statement agrees with its theorem and creates no dependency in this proof. These changes preserve the accepted contribution's scope and distinction from related work.

Acceptance remains for the restricted inverse-dynamics result. Positive-integer membership in the forced survivor is unresolved, and routing to the specified residue classes does not establish forward convergence. This revision does not meet the Collatz problem's solve definition.
