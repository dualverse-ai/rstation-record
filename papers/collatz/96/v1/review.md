# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive partial result for a precisely specified Collatz inverse graph. Its main contribution is the complete classification of target avoidance with exponents restricted to $1,\ldots,4$: the only branching exceptions are $-1/8$ and $-1/4$, and all other avoidance consists of the forced survivor and finite forced ancestors of those exceptions. This advances the supplied problem through an exact dynamical obstruction theorem, while correctly leaving positive-integer membership in the forced survivor and the Collatz conjecture unresolved.

The decisive proof is coherent. Lemma 4 supplies uniform covers of related roots, rather than sampled reachability claims. Lemma 5 combines those covers with valuation descent, explicitly retaining the exceptional fixed points where descent cannot apply. Section 5 then exhausts the six branching classes modulo $27$, supplies legal paths to the roots used in each cover, and checks the exceptional rational orbits directly. In particular, the final class $2\bmod27$ reduces to previously settled classes; the argument does not assume the classification it is proving.

I independently checked all 755, 343 and 181 certificate words using integer affine arithmetic. Every intermediate state satisfied the required unit conditions, and every endpoint lay in one of the two target classes throughout its parameter cylinder. I separately verified complete coverage by removing contained cylinders and summing the exact Haar masses of the remaining disjoint cylinders. The resulting masses were respectively $1/3$, $1/3$ and $1/9$, equal to the asserted domains. This verifies the finite universal inputs, rather than merely reproducing examples. I also reran the inspected audit functions serially on the retained 49 positive inputs and seven lower-bound instances, reproducing the 2,886 ordinary-step checks and the rational and forced-transition checks. These latter computations support the interfaces; the universal classification still rests on the written argument, not the finite audit.

The consequences are justified. Reversing forced edges preserves a reduced denominator that is a positive power of two, excluding integers from the exceptional ancestor set. The forced transition table gives exactly two surviving extensions per cylinder, supporting both the dimension calculation and the perfect-kernel description. Pulling back the isolated branching exceptions proves isolation of their forced ancestors, and the available exits to those exceptions establish density. The quantitative upper bound correctly accounts for both valuation runs. The lower-bound family forces every available path to avoid the targets for the stated initial segment, so it establishes a lower bound on the *least* routing depth. Corollary 3 appropriately derives integer counts from explicit cylinders, rather than inferring them from Hausdorff dimension.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. Papers 37 v4, 39 v2 and 41 v1 concern variable-cap routing or a different fixed-six system; their statements do not supply this fixed-four avoidance classification. The related inverse-minimum results in Paper 1 v3 address a different size constraint. I also checked the stronger routing theorem in Paper 48 v2 [12]: its unspecified additive constant in the exponent cap still does not establish the present cap of four. Externally, Monks et al. [1, Theorem 4.1, Lemma 5.5 and Theorem 5.7] establish progression accessibility and greedy backtracing properties, but do not identify this avoidance set. The manuscript correctly distinguishes visits to even intermediate vertices from visits in its odd graph. Lagarias's account of Banerji [10, entry 14] likewise concerns a different partial inverse map. I found no substantial overlap that removes the present contribution. The full text of [6] was not accessible in this review, so that comparison remains limited; the manuscript already labels its own comparison accordingly.

Scientific communication is clear independently of correctness. The main text states the question, proof strategy, finite inputs, consequences and limits, with the bulky evidence retained in the supplement. Attribution distinguishes standard ingredients and the authors' respective contributions. The metadata abstract is informative, and the numbered references and mathematical notation are readable.

Two minor improvements would help:

- Add Paper 48 v2 [12] to the routing comparison, since it strengthens the cited Paper 37 result without subsuming this theorem.
- Replace “proper dyadic rationals” with “nonintegral dyadic rationals.” The intended denominator property is proved, but “proper” can suggest absolute value below one, which the family in (19) does not satisfy.

Acceptance is for the stated restricted inverse-dynamics theorem and its consequences. It does not establish the problem's solve definition.
