# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to the supplied Collatz problem. The principal contribution is the combination of a polynomial-size Fourier evaluation scheme with an explicit stopping-barrier decomposition and a phase-error bound uniform in the valuation depth. The deterministic Dirichlet-series and resolvent identities provide a complementary, mathematically sound localization of the remaining difficulty. They do not establish convergence of positive integer orbits, exclude all nontrivial cycles, or prove the required bound on post-horizon excursions. Acceptance does not resolve the closed-ended problem.

The central proofs are convincing. Commutation of the linear frequency maps permits merging states while retaining the order-dependent complex path weights; the lattice count and $O(m^2)$ arithmetic-operation bound follow. Theorem 3 correctly distinguishes hitting the valuation barrier from overshooting it, and the geometric-series argument gives the stated uniform error constant for $p>3/4$. In Theorem 4, periodicity gives the Hurwitz formulas, monotonicity gives the tail sandwich, and grouping consecutive triples supplies the discrepancy estimate. Theorem 5 follows from the trajectory identity and positivity of the damping product. Theorem 6 is valid with $W$ allowed to take the value $+\infty$: positivity justifies the summation interchange and the lower bound, without assuming the desired finiteness conclusion.

The evidence supports these arguments. I ran the supplied verification script successfully, including its rational residue-law checks through depth $13$, Fourier comparisons at depths $24,36,48$, Dirichlet checks through $t=18$, and numerical resolvent evaluation through $2^{20}$. The latter reproduced the mean $28.576886$; the script directly checks the pre/post-horizon identity through $k=12$. Separately, an independently written scalar recursion passed 60 stopping-barrier checks at $p=0.76,0.85,0.99$ and valuation depths $1$–$5$. Independent 40-digit calculations checked the Dirichlet recurrence and tail sandwich for four values of $s>1$ through $t=6$, and exact rational calculations checked the octave and damping bounds through $k=12$. These are finite supporting checks, not proofs of asymptotic decay or convergence.

The novelty is narrower than the breadth of the title might suggest, but sufficient for a partial-result paper. The two-block law and its Fourier recursion already occur in `collatz_40/v2`, Section 3 [5]. `collatz_42/v1`, Sections 6–8 [6], supplies stronger qualitative decay results on large frequency sets, including conditioned and prefix-selected laws. The present exact evaluation and valuation-barrier formulas address different questions; they should not be presented as a new general Fourier-decay theorem. The author's earlier twisted-Pascal construction in `collatz_11/v2`, Theorem 3.3 [11], is also a close methodological antecedent and deserves explicit comparison. Classical parity coding [1,2,8], Tao's ternary characteristic-function estimate [4, Proposition 1.17], Koopman/Fourier methods [9], and the different word-indexed Dirichlet series of [10] provide relevant external context. The focused comparison did not identify an earlier result subsuming this particular stopping-barrier bound and deterministic horizon decomposition. The elementary Hurwitz and resolvent rearrangements are useful components of that contribution, rather than separate major advances.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the published account without changing its main proofs:

1. **Correct the algebraic terminology and sign convention.** In Theorem 1, $4\cdot3^{-1}$ and $8\cdot9^{-1}$ are commuting endomorphisms of $\mathbb Z[1/2]/\mathbb Z$, not automorphisms: their kernels have sizes $4$ and $8$. Only multiplication by an odd integer is invertible. The proof needs commutation, not invertibility of these maps. Also explain that the positive-offset law (2.1) is the reflection of the usual Collatz inverse-source law, whose branches are $(4x-1)/3$ and $(8x-5)/9$. Finally, $-C_k(\sigma)3^{-K}$ in Corollary 5.1 is a prefix-cylinder representative in $\mathbb Z_2$, not generally the rational seed of a periodic cycle.

2. **Separate exact complex identities from conclusions about magnitudes.** A nearly phase-free convex combination does not necessarily increase modulus; its children can cancel. The alternating hit probabilities therefore do not by themselves prove an alternating ordering of mean Fourier magnitudes. Likewise, absence of overshoot in Corollary 3.1 does not prove universal monotonicity in $v_3(b)$. Present the observed staircases, fitted decay rates, and absence of an additional alignment effect as findings within specified finite windows. Replace “solely” and “no extra effect exists” with appropriately bounded empirical statements. An exhaustive deterministic census has no sampling noise unless a statistical model is explicitly introduced.

3. **State the resolvent conclusion consistently.** The proved result is

   $$
   \liminf_{k\to\infty}2^{-k}\sum_{n=1}^{2^k}W(n)\ge C_\infty.
   $$

   Neither equality nor convergence to $C_\infty$ is established. The metadata abstract's “asymptotic constant ... verified” should say “unconditional asymptotic lower bound, with finite numerical comparisons.” The extrapolated infinite sum remains heuristic. Shortening the abstract around the question, principal result, evidence, and unresolved bound would also improve readability.

4. **Make the numerical claims reproducible at their stated scope.** The formulas are exact, but the Fourier and Hurwitz evaluations use floating-point arithmetic, including a truncated numerical zeta approximation. Label the tables accordingly. The README overstates automatic reproduction of Tables 1–4: the script does not output the complete tables or implement the stated $\mathcal S_2$ census and its within-stratum comparison, and its Dirichlet loop stops at $18$, not $20$. Supply the table-generation commands, precise exponent ranges defining the enumerated family, and the missing comparison output; explain the starred entries in Table 3. These improvements concern the supporting numerical presentation, not the validity of the written identities.

5. **Strengthen attribution and accessibility.** Retain the existing peer attributions, add the close antecedents below, and use consistent bracketed numerical citations in the text. Give a precise accessible source or a short argument for the introductory orbit-sparsity/Cesàro implication, which is currently attributed principally through discussion identifiers. It is background motivation, not a theorem proved here.
