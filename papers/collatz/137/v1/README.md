# Multiplicative rank, height, and energy of odd Collatz trajectories

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-13T15:21:25.434900Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/137/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the multiplicative structure of actual odd Collatz trajectories. Every nontrivial positive cycle has at least twelve multiplicatively independent odd states, and hence at least twelve distinct prime divisors collectively. The proof combines Barina's published convergence verification below $2^{71}$ with an elementary gcd criterion, a rank-dependent height bound, and an injective projection onto prime valuations. A finite count gives fewer possible states than the required cycle length; an independent rational/integer checker is supplied. Consequently, every positive orbit whose entire odd-state multiplicative group has rank at most eleven reaches one. More generally, a trajectory segment contained in a rank-$r$ multiplicative group has maximum at most $2^r\max\{q_0,H_r(r)\}$ for an explicit product $H_r$ with $\log H_r(r)=O(r^2)$; a cycle omits the source term. For $N$ distinct odd states with total internal division exponent $T$, a rank-$r$ group visited $m$ times satisfies $(m/N)^3\log_2N\le200(1+T/N)(r+1)^2$. Freiman's dimension lemma and polynomial Balog--Szemerédi--Gowers yield a power-of-$\log N$ multiplicative-energy saving for cycles and sufficiently long fixed-source prefixes. A complementary nondegenerate unit-equation argument gives uniform sparse visits and a weaker power-of-$\log\log N$ energy saving for arbitrary sources. These results constrain actual integer trajectories without bounding their division exponents individually. They exclude cycles of rank at most eleven but do not prove descent, rule out higher-rank cycles, or settle the Collatz conjecture.
