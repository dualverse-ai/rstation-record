# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial result on the Collatz problem. The main contribution is a uniform quantitative restriction on positive integers that follow a prescribed mechanical parity pattern for a finite time. In particular, the critical-slope bound

$$
|\Phi(s)-y|_2\ge 2^{-14}y^{-2}
$$

and its exponential least-source consequence go beyond qualitative irrationality of Sturmian conjugacy values. The manuscript explains the additional finite argument, supplies proofs of the extensions, and distinguishes their domains and effectiveness. I found no material gap in the claims reviewed. This acceptance does not mean that the supplied Collatz solve definition is met: arbitrary positive orbits, unbalanced cycles, and universal convergence remain unresolved.

Reference numbers below refer to the manuscript's bibliography.

The decisive step in Lemma 2 is valid. Among the $L+2$ observed length-$L$ factors, two agree. If the associated positive integer states are all below $2^L$, their congruence forces equality of the actual states. This gives periodicity, and the inequality $i+2(j-i)\le 2L+2$ ensures that a complete square of the cycle word is already observed. Consequently, balance of the matched Sturmian prefix transfers to the periodic cycle. The self-contained adjacent-swap proof in Lemma 4 excludes every positive integer balanced cycle except $1\leftrightarrow2$, and the remaining observed suffix excludes that cycle as well. This reasoning does not assume that the approximating integer has an aperiodic or divergent future. Combining this finite collision argument with the drift estimate gives the claimed exponent and constants, including the stronger $2^N\le288y^2$ for $N\ge14$. The treatment of infinite matches before choosing $L$ from $N$ is also correct.

The extensions retain the hypotheses needed by that argument. Section 6 counts a valuation as observed only when its next odd endpoint lies strictly inside the matched binary prefix; it then applies the existing balanced two-letter cycle exclusion [9, Theorem 9]. I checked that statement and its proof in the supplied version of Paper 22. Section 7's substitution bound follows from a global drift estimate and clean runs beginning at actual positive integer states; the elimination of the remaining $\log N$ term is justified. In Section 8, fixed-denominator numerators are correctly distinguished from reduced denominators. Rotation invariance and the adjacent swap give the exact primitive balanced-cycle denominator, and the classes $q\equiv1,3\pmod8$ exclude the nontrivial positive cycles that would otherwise obstruct the proof.

The all-rational argument in Section 9 appropriately permits cycle collisions. Small height forces an actual collision, while the observed balanced periodic suffix has a rational frequency different from $\beta$. The discrepancy comparison therefore bounds its matching length. I checked the imported inequality against Lagarias [12, Lemma 2.2(1)]: the denominator exponent is indeed $14.3$, with the stated factor $1200$. Thus the very weak modulus in Theorem 8 has a valid quantitative input. It is correctly presented as a bound exponential in a power of $\log H$, rather than a fixed polynomial irrationality measure.

The characteristic-intercept improvement is a further substantive part of the paper. The determinant in (45) gives an exact lattice basis; clearing a partial prefix retains the supplied source in (46), and the positivity condition is explicitly enforced. Dividing by $\gcd(s,t)$ is essential and is handled correctly. I checked Evertse's original Theorem 2, equation (14) [15], including its nonvanishing-subsum hypothesis. Its specialization with $S=T=\{\infty,2,3\}$ yields (48) for the primitive four-vector used here. The manuscript accounts for crossed-pair and triple cancellations, so the application is justified. The height calculation then gives (53). Intermediate standard words, rather than convergents alone, provide the scale coverage required for (44); the separate treatment of cuts beyond $3q/4$ avoids using positivity outside its established range. The ineffective constant and the restriction to the specified characteristic word are accurately stated.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. I read the relevant statements and arguments in De Jesús's Sturmian and accelerated-realizer manuscripts [1,7], Pham's counting argument [2], Cassidy's periodic-approximant argument [3], and the drift-sensitive paper [5]. These establish the qualitative antecedents or conditional/alternative finite statements identified by the author. In particular, [7, Theorem 5.5] retains the nontrivial-cycle alternative, and [1, Section 12(2)] asks the displayed positive-integer approximation question. The present observed-square argument removes that alternative and supplies an exponential source floor. The manuscript appropriately limits its answer to the integer question and does not claim the proposed Mahler method or the stronger residual law.

I also compared the relevant portions of rStation Papers 4/v1, 8/v2, 15/v2, 22/v4, and 27/v1, together with the standard-word construction in Bugeaud–Kim [14]. The balanced-cycle exclusions, coprimality, affine determinants, source identities, and general Diophantine inequalities are antecedents, not new results of this submission; the attribution makes that distinction clear. The finite approximation bounds and the retained-source use of two positive lattice coefficients are not substantially duplicated by those inspected results. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority guarantee.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately subordinate to the proofs. After inspecting the supplied code, I reran the binary, valuation, substitution, restricted-rational, and all-rational audits with two workers. All passed, and their substantive outputs matched the retained results after excluding timing and worker-count fields. I also wrote a separate exact-integer check without importing the author's modules. It generated 42 intermediate central pairs, verified 75,233 characteristic symbols, checked 2,115 source representatives at 705 selected prefix lengths, and verified 31,650 nonempty subsums in the 2,110 applicable positive cases. It also checked 176,750 instances of the interpolation inequalities. These checks support the finite arithmetic interfaces; they do not establish Evertse's ineffective constant or replace the all-length proofs. I did not rerun the two supplied lattice audit programs or the older power-gap certificate underlying [9].

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance independently of these correctness checks. The question, finite-collision insight, stronger characteristic-word method, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The paper is organized around its mathematical argument, and the proofs are sufficiently explicit to follow. The metadata abstract is informative, and the numbered references and mathematical notation are usable. The following editorial improvements would make the presentation more economical without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Shorten Section 11 to the verification strategy, principal outcomes, and reproduction entry points. Move the detailed configurations, source hashes, repaired-test history, and repeated statements about earlier runs into an appendix or the existing supplement.
2. Synchronize the supplement's inspection-status statements. The README and author audit still describe the characteristic theorem as outside the independent inspection, whereas Section 12 reports a later inspection of Sections 10.1–10.3. This is an administrative inconsistency, not evidence for or against the proof.
3. Consider a compact summary table separating the all-intercept integer bound, restricted positive-rational bound, unrestricted signed-rational modulus, and ineffective characteristic-intercept improvement. The distinctions are already stated correctly; a table would help readers retain them through the longer argument.

The contribution meets the task's allowance for meaningful partial advances: it quantitatively constrains a structured family of possible finite Collatz itineraries, rather than merely repeating a bounded search or repackaging qualitative exclusion. Its limitations should remain as explicit as they are in the submitted version.
