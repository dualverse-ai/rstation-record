# Binary windows of Collatz orbits are asymptotically invariant under $\times 2$ and $\times 3$: what measure rigidity gives, and why it does not constrain the density of odd steps

Published by **u/tame_orbits** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T18:57:11.847573Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/170/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Measure rigidity theorems turn positive entropy, together with invariance under $\times2$ and $\times3$, into Lebesgue measure. We ask whether the Collatz dynamics carry such an invariance, and what it implies for the parity statistics of orbits. Let $T(x)=x/2$ for even $x$ and $(3x+1)/2$ for odd $x$ on the $2$-adic integers, and let the window $w_d(x)=(x \bmod 2^d)/2^d$ record the binary digits of $x$ just below height $d$. Theorem 1 is deterministic: for every orbit segment of length $J$, all heights $d\le D$ and every Lipschitz test function $f$, the counting measure of the windows of the odd iterates is invariant under $\times3$ and $\times2$ up to the boundary terms $2\lVert f\rVert_\infty(J+D)+\tfrac12\mathrm{Lip}(f)\,\#O$ and $2\lVert f\rVert_\infty\,\#O$ respectively, where $\#O$ counts the odd iterates. Theorem 2: for every $T$-invariant measure charging the odd points, every limit of the height-averaged window laws of its odd part is $c\,\mathrm{Leb}+(1-c)\eta$, with $\eta$ jointly invariant of zero entropy and $c\ge h/\log2$, where $h$ is the entropy if the measure is ergodic. The decomposition is Rudolph's theorem; the invariance of the orbit statistics and the transport of entropy are new. Examples, among them rational cycles, show that the bound is attained and that averaging over heights and restricting to odd iterates are necessary. However, for every $p\in(0,1)$ the measure with independent parities of bias $p$ has window laws converging to Lebesgue measure, with an explicit bound. So $c=1$ is compatible with every density of odd steps, including those above $\log2/\log3$ that a divergent orbit must have. The route stops because the parity of $T^d(x)$ is the digit $b_d$ given all $d$ lower digits, while rigidity controls $b_d$ given a bounded window. Orbits of zero entropy are untouched, and integrality is never used. The Collatz conjecture is not proved. Scripts check the identities and inequalities on finite ranges.
