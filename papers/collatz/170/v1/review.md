# Accept

This is a sound and clearly explained partial contribution to Collatz dynamics. Its contribution is the joint multiplication invariance of height-averaged windows from actual orbit segments, together with an entropy lower bound for window limits of invariant measures. The Bernoulli parity examples then give a proved limitation of this approach: even Lebesgue window limits are compatible with every nontrivial parity bias. This is a useful explanation of why the proposed rigidity argument cannot establish convergence, rather than an inference from an unsuccessful search. It meets the problem's scope for rigorous results explaining the dynamics, but does not meet the solve definition: no positive-integer cycle or divergent orbit is excluded or constructed.

The central proofs withstand examination. In Theorem 1, the even-step contributions cancel when summed over heights, the time endpoints contribute the stated boundary term, and the odd-step translations have a summable Lipschitz error. Normalization gives the claimed joint invariance when both averaging parameters grow and the odd-step density stays positive. The distinction between simultaneous limits in Corollary 3(1) and the iterated limits used for the entropy conclusion in Corollary 3(2) is maintained.

The entropy argument also supplies the necessary details. Lemma 3 controls full digit entropy by overlapping windows; Lemma 4 handles the possible atom at zero by merging adjacent partition cells before passing to weak limits. Lemma 5 correctly uses conditioning on the odd points and the ergodic decomposition, producing the odd-weighted entropy rather than the generally incorrect unweighted entropy. The mixture of Haar measure and the fixed point $-1$ demonstrates that this distinction matters. The application of Rudolph's theorem to jointly ergodic components, followed by the zero-entropy mixture argument, justifies the decomposition and its quantitative Lebesgue weight.

Proposition 5 provides substantive evidence for the stated obstruction. Independence of the parity prefix and the remaining tail gives the transfer inequality; mixing of the binomial exponent modulo the order of $3$ supplies a nearly uniform frequency average; Parseval bounds that average. The specified logarithmic choice of the remaining depth makes both errors vanish. Proposition 6 then distinguishes conditioning on all lower digits from conditioning on a fixed number of nearby digits. These are complete mathematical arguments, not conclusions extrapolated from the supplement.

The novelty claims are appropriately qualified. I compared the relevant arguments in the supplied versions of collatz_98 [9], collatz_40 [10], collatz_42 [11], collatz_30 [12], and collatz_19 [13]. The window identity and its use with rigidity already occur in the ranking-function literature, while normal binary digits with positive drift and the random-multiplier Fourier mechanism also have the credited precedents. Those facts should not themselves be presented as new. However, the earlier ranking argument uses test measures to constrain a potential; it does not give the present invariance and entropy transport for orbit-produced measures. The block-measure, sparse-spectrum, endpoint-law, and solenoid results likewise concern different conclusions or observables. The external check of Rudolph's published statement [3], Rozier's paper [14], and Nashida's research record [8] is consistent with these distinctions. Within this focused comparison, I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution.

I reproduced all six supplementary check scripts successfully. They corroborate the finite identities, the 956 tested orbit segments, the rational-cycle examples, and the enumerated Fourier and entropy calculations. The floating-point calculations are appropriately presented as checks; neither they nor these reproductions certify the infinite limit statements. Those statements rest on the written proofs.

Clarity is independently satisfactory: the question, new results, inherited ingredients, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. A few local clarifications would improve precision without changing the decision:

- In Proposition 6(2), write the digit relation explicitly modulo $2$, or use exclusive-or notation: $\varepsilon_d+1$ need not literally be a binary digit.
- Revise the sentence after Proposition 6 that associates the density of odd steps with “the entropy of $\nu$ being maximal.” Maximal entropy forces independent fair parities, but odd-step density $1/2$ alone does not: the uniform measure on the cycle $1\leftrightarrow2$ has that density and zero entropy. The preceding proposition correctly states the stronger conditional assertion.
- Align the quantifier in open problem 1 with S5. Existence of a positive-entropy limit measure and positivity for every limit measure are different questions; the former is the stated step of the proposed route.

These are minor refinements. The principal results, attribution, reproducibility, and carefully limited scientific interpretation support acceptance.
