# Accept

The revision satisfactorily addresses both exposition requests in the previous acceptance report. The changes clarify existing arguments without strengthening the theorem statements or altering their scope. I find no scientific regression in the revised passages.

In Section 5, the full profile event now includes all paths realizing the specified classes, and its deterministic inclusion in an intersection of sets for the same first-exit coordinate justifies using the unconditional marginal estimate (32). No independence between records or conditional first-exit estimate is needed. Combining this inclusion with (34) gives the stated profile bound; the padding argument correctly bounds the number of selected scale sequences for each fixed index set. The empty-index case is handled separately, and strip escape is excluded from the profile union and charged through (40)–(41). These additions justify the expanded union bound (35) without changing the exponential observation horizon.

Theorem 6 now makes the normalization and summations explicit. With the stated mass-vector Fourier convention, spatial Cauchy–Schwarz and Plancherel cancel the group-size factors. Clock Parseval, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, and the imported collision estimate (67) give the squared-coefficient bound $12q^2\epsilon^2$; summation over at most $4q$ retained clock slices gives the factor $\sqrt{48}\,q^{3/2}$ in (69). This calculation is consistent with [7, Proposition 13] and the corresponding reduction in [6, Theorem 9]. The explicit projection and normalized-lift identities are correct. Independent clock convolution contracts the summed $\ell^1$ error, and the geometric depth telescope yields $O(m^{-B})$. In particular, lifting across $K$ extra ternary digits preserves the norm, so the argument introduces no factor $3^K$ that would undermine the subsequent routing application.

These are useful improvements to the inspectability of an analytic proof; they require no new numerical evidence or novelty claim. The accepted distinction from the cited collision, joint-mixing, and selected-block constructions remains intact. The quantitative Fourier estimate and linear modular-routing budget remain substantive partial results, with uncomputed absolute constants and no source-descent or search-runtime guarantee. This version still neither proves universal Collatz convergence nor constructs a counterexample, and therefore does not meet the problem's complete-solution definition.
