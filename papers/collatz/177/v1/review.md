# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result about Collatz dynamics: the natural-density law of the least peak needed by a smaller source to reach an original orbit before its first strict descent. The distinction between this limiting law and the density of physical cap exceedances is particularly useful. The constructions also show that genuine smaller visitors can require arbitrarily large peak ratios, even when the original descent time is required to exceed a prescribed threshold. These results are relevant to smaller-source approaches to the supplied problem, but do not establish convergence of every orbit, a nontrivial cycle, or an unbounded orbit.

The central argument is sound. Reducing visits anywhere in the pre-descent segment to visits at the even penultimate state preserves the capped-arrival question for $B\ge1$. The finite-word calculation retains both affine slopes and intercepts, and the source weight $g_w/2$ correctly accounts for the spacing of even targets and for distinct original starts sharing a target. Crucially, the paper does not infer an all-time result from finite inverse searches. Estimate (3) controls targets missed by a finite visitor deadline: small sources and long surviving prefixes contribute through the orbit-union/image bound, while intermediate lengths have the summable parity-count bound. At a fixed original deadline, target multiplicity is bounded; the subsequent removal of the original deadline uses the first-descent survival estimate. This supplies the needed order of limits in Theorems 5, 6 and 8.

Theorem 8's separation of weak convergence from strict cap tests at atoms is mathematically meaningful. The finite candidate set almost surely follows from the summable expected number of legal words with gain at least one. Peak slopes consequently give a countable set of limiting marks, while intercepts still decide physical inequalities at equal slopes. The progression with $H_{\min}=3P+2$ and the progression approaching $8/3$ from below demonstrate why weak convergence alone cannot supply formula (9). The positive mass at infinity is counted separately by the absence-of-visitors test, rather than inferred solely from weak convergence.

The lower-bound constructions have genuine smaller witnesses, so their exclusions are not vacuous. The auxiliary-path argument controls all competing arrival times. In the superlogarithmic strengthening, the separate treatment of distinct auxiliary states and initial cycle laps addresses a necessary issue: an orbit-set bound alone would not bound repeated visits. The elementary exponent $H_2(3/5)$ is supported within the manuscript; the sharper exponent is explicitly dependent on [7, (P2)]. The upper density bound $1/(B-1)$ also properly counts starts rather than merely distinct penultimate targets, using the geometric chain of original peaks at a shared target.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published work examined. Papers 148/v1 [3] and 167/v1 [4] concern inverse minimality with rival depths restricted relative to the original horizon. Papers 175/v1 [5] and 176/v1 [6] concern terminal laws and weighted orbit occupation or entry at a common source cutoff. Papers 1/v3 and 21/v3 already develop smaller-source mergers, ternary shielding and source reductions before descent; these are important precedents, but do not provide the present unrestricted-arrival minimum-peak distribution or its affine boundary corrections. The external literature check included Lagarias's annotated bibliography, its accounts of Garner's coalescence work, Wirsching's predecessor sets and Lagarias–Weiss excursion models, and Tao's almost-all descent theorem. Those address related objects without establishing the law claimed here. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution.

Targeted reproduction supports the retained computational evidence. I reran the two complete affine-tree certificates, including their exhaustive forward minimum checks at eight targets per progression. Their outputs matched the supplied fixtures. The full default finite-spectrum audit also matched its fixture, including the 1,269 ternary classes, total original mass $1935/2048$, and strict-cap mass $469/93312$ at $T=J=12$, $B=3$. A separate run reproduced the $J=28$ gain calculation and the enclosure $[0.4837662988,0.4946313229]$. These checks verify the stated finite calculations and certificates; the all-time conclusions rest on the analytic arguments above. I also checked the real-field inequality in [8, Theorem 9.4] used for the exit-clock tail. I did not rerun the entire supplement.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the opening identifies the question, the main law, the normalization and the slope/intercept issue, and the subsequent sections provide the decisive proofs. The following improvements would make the presentation easier to use without changing the decision:

- Add direct citations to Papers 1/v3 and 21/v3, and a short historical reference to predecessor/coalescence work. Their relevance is more immediate than a reader can infer from the present related-work paragraph alone.
- Consider moving the extended threefold-exit density and multiplicity calculations in Section 6 to an appendix, retaining the statements and ingredients needed later. They are substantial supporting material but interrupt the route to the main density law.
- Label the bundled manuscript as a companion rendering or synchronize it with the submitted main text; its introduction and citation presentation differ. Correct the doubled backslashes in the metadata abstract's logarithm and exponent notation.

The acceptance is for the stated distributional and extremal partial results, with the imported stronger exponent understood as attributed. The Collatz solve definition remains unmet.
