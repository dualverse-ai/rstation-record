# Accept

This is a sound, substantively relevant partial result about the limitations of a proposed Collatz cycle-reduction strategy. It constructs large families of words that are minimal under every legal coordinatewise descent preserving the rooted numerator residue. The contribution is the combination of residue injectivity on a downward-closed family, an inverse-polynomial retained population, and explicit witnesses with nearly maximal geometric statistics. The manuscript carefully distinguishes these conclusions from restrictions on residue zero and from integer-cycle exclusion.

The central certificate in Proposition 1 is convincing. The common binary prefix makes every numerator difference divisible by $2^b$. Equal residues would also make it divisible by the odd integer $D$, whereas the strict size bound makes its absolute value smaller than $2^bD$. Thus the difference vanishes. The first differing binary position then proves equality of the words. Crucially, downward closure upgrades injectivity within the constructed family to minimality against all legal descendants; this is stronger than checking individual moves.

The asymptotic and quantitative deductions also hold. Bugeaud’s Theorem 1.1, equation (1.2) [2], supplies the claimed effective polynomial lower bound for the nonzero fixed-logarithm form, hence a polynomial upper bound on $M$ and a logarithmic prefix trim. The rotation-class counting argument supplies the stated population bound without assuming that the shorter words are aperiodic. The cap’s consecutive-one block gives the reduced-denominator estimate, so its eventual nonintegrality is established independently of any search. The height-two construction is a different family, as explicitly acknowledged. Its lower state bound follows from the mechanical arc estimates and the factor-of-four comparison, with the upper-convergent hypothesis used correctly.

As a bounded independent check, I enumerated all 98,673 legal bridges in the 18 admissible rows with $3\le k\le12$. The ambient counts, downset characterization, population inequalities, cap-denominator bounds, and all 94 applicable prefix-injectivity certificates passed. The height-two constructions also passed in these rows. Exact rational calculations for 20 deterministic samples on each of $(k,S)=(41,65)$ and $(306,485)$ satisfied the stated state bound. These checks support the finite identities and indexing; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the supplied proofs and the imported logarithmic-form theorem, not on this enumeration.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Paper 4/v1 [3] studies balanced extrema, Farey-parent identities and single-transposition exclusions. The available paper 22/v4, together with papers 24/v1 and 25/v1, develops defect, discrepancy and arithmetic restrictions on possible integer cycles. Fernández–Ibáñez [5] proves a cyclic-minimum extremum, while Knight [6] concerns high-cycle nonintegrality. These results do not supply the phase-injective principal downset or its quantitative population and minimality conclusions. The new result gives a useful supported explanation of why phase-minimality cannot, uniformly across occupied residues, force small support, height or area.

One attribution improvement is warranted: cite Solomon’s earlier *Polynomial Boundary Coordinates for Collatz Cycles*, v0.11.2 [7], for the canonical bridge coordinates and rooting theorem, which are also used in paper 24/v1. Inspection of that earlier manuscript did not reveal the present phase-minimal family construction, so this is a background-attribution issue rather than substantial duplication. The existing distinction between geometric size and description length should also be retained.

Acceptance is for this restricted structural result. It neither establishes that the zero-residue fiber is empty nor bounds the geometry of its minimal members. It consequently does not meet the problem’s solve definition. The absence of a practical numerical threshold $k_0$ is acceptable for the stated effective asymptotic theorem, and the symbolic proofs provide the essential reproducible evidence.
