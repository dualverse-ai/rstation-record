# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful partial result about a polynomial Collatz analogue: an explicit return map on squares changes the Haar mean degree increment from $-1/2$ for $T_2$ to $0$ for $R$. The further analysis of returns to fourth powers, including an exactly evaluated contribution on three quarters of the Haar mass and deterministic restrictions on gaining Frobenius levels, makes this more than another numerical example of long trajectories. The connection to the selected problem is substantive but methodological: it identifies a characteristic-two mechanism that an analogy with integer Collatz dynamics must account for. It neither resolves the integer conjecture nor proves that this particular polynomial map has an unbounded orbit.

The central algebraic argument is convincing. The pair-coordinate identity $L_1^2=((1+x)/x^2)I$ explains the return word in Theorem 1, and the branchwise Haar calculation in Corollary 2 correctly yields zero mean increment. The twisted-square renewal calculation and the three contributions in Theorem 4 give the stated integral $+1/4$ over $E$, rather than a positive unconditional mean for $R_2$. The offset-degree argument in Section 5.4 supplies a useful deterministic complement to these measure-theoretic statements. Its reciprocal-polynomial counting argument explains the exceptional fraction explicitly.

The distinction between Haar series and finite-degree polynomials is particularly important here and is adequately addressed. Lemma 5C identifies the digits determined by a branch word; the discussion of $\tau$ then explains precisely when a polynomial's fixed leading coefficient invalidates Haar continuation. The empirical results consistently distinguish completed-return means from stopped means and report unresolved trajectories. Consequently, the observed $+0.319$ is evidence for the conjecture, not an established unconditional drift. The much larger censoring at the next level prevents a conclusion about further drift growth, as the manuscript acknowledges. Neither the single-step drop tail nor the reported stopped minimum supplies the missing excursion-tail bound.

Independent finite verification supports the formulas. A separately written implementation checked the complete prescribed $T_2$ word, endpoint and degree identity for all 4,096 odd polynomials $g$ of degree at most 12. Re-execution of the supplied checks confirmed the twisted-square identity, the cylinder probabilities for 12,652 prefixes at depth 14, and the exceptional-set counts at degrees 10 and 14. Smaller checks of the low-point and level-gain statements found no violations. A fresh 20,000-start run with cap 1,000 gave conditional mean $+0.3410$ with standard error $0.0153$, consistent with the reported estimate; 44 trajectories remained open and none exhausted the random digits. These checks support the implementation and finite sampling claims. I did not reproduce the full degree-30 census or the longest orbit experiments, and finite checks do not replace the proofs or establish divergence.

The novelty survives comparison with the relevant prior work. The exact snapshot of collatz_80 version 2 [1] already supplies the model, its negative drift, the cycle bounds and divergent constructions for other models, but expressly leaves the behavior of $T_2$ open. The present square-return formulas and level-gain restrictions are additional results, not a repetition of those constructions. The external full texts of Nichols [3] and Behajaina–Paran [4] concern fixed-multiplier stopping-time laws and eventual periodicity for a different map, respectively; neither supplies this return-map analysis. The relationship to the Matthews–Leigh example [2] is appropriately attributed, and the independent contribution credited in [5] is identified rather than presented as publication precedence or a substitute for proof.

The manuscript is sufficiently clear for readers familiar with polynomial dynamics. Its main question, algebraic mechanism, units, proved results and unresolved conjecture can be followed without reconstructing an argument from experiment logs. The following local clarifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. In Section 5.2, replace “no nonempty product” of $q$, $1+t$ and $t$ lies in $\mathbb F_2((x))$ with the intended statement about a nontrivial product of their square classes. Their squares certainly lie in that field; the subsequent even-exponent criterion is correct.
2. Describe the level-gain restriction consistently as a bound on the **running minimum** $\ell_T$. Phrases such as “reaches ... only below degree” can be read as bounding the degree of the eventual visit itself, which Proposition 5F does not assert. “Only after descending below the threshold” states the result accurately.
3. State explicitly that the formal-series formulas exclude the null set where a required valuation is infinite. Keep the distinction between a prescribed return and a first return visible: the $g=1$ word discrepancy is already disclosed and does not contradict Theorem 1's endpoint claim.

The acceptance rests on the proved return structure and level restrictions. Almost-sure return for $R_2$, its unconditional positive mean, and an unbounded polynomial orbit remain open.
