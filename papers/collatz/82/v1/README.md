# Squares renormalize a polynomial Collatz map of drift -1/2 to a map of drift 0

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T22:42:56.804884Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/82/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

$T_2$ is the polynomial Collatz map on odd $f\in\mathbb F_2[x]$ given by $f\mapsto((1+x+x^2)f+1)/x$ when the coefficient of $x$ is 0, and $f\mapsto((1+x)f+1)/x^v$ otherwise. It preserves Haar measure and has mean degree change $-1/2$ per step, and neither divergent construction of the companion paper covers it. We prove that the orbit of a square $g^2$ reaches $R(g)^2$ for an explicit map $R$ that preserves Haar measure and has mean degree change exactly 0. On a set of Haar measure $3/4$ the first return $R_2$ of $R$ to squares is explicit, with integrated degree change exactly $+1/4$. Above degree 3, an $R$-orbit from a square can return to the squares only at times fixed by a parity condition on its branch word, and must return if at such a time its degree is within 1 of its running minimum. A $T_2$ orbit can move from $2^j$-th to $2^{j+1}$-th powers only below an explicit degree, and the exceptions among polynomials of fixed degree are counted exactly. Under the exact Haar law of the branch word, the mean of $R_2$ per return, conditional on return within 16,000 $R$-steps, is $+0.319$ (standard error 0.003; 0.046% unreturned). At the next level 7% of polynomial starts remain open at the largest caps, so that mean is not determined. From fourth powers of degree 120, 9 of 16 orbits reach degree 20,000, while all 16 from typical starts and all 16 from squares end in cycles. We conjecture, but do not prove, that $T_2$ has unbounded polynomial orbits through fourth powers. If so, drift $-1/2$, Haar measure, the Mason-Stothers cycle bound and squarefree multipliers outside $\mathbb F_2[x^3]$ do not force convergence. Function-field evidence about $3x+1$ should be read on orbits that avoid $2^k$-th powers.
