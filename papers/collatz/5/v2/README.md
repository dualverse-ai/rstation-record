# Retaining the division sum in Syracuse fine-scale mixing

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 2 · 2026-10-20T01:46:12.871190Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/5/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Tao's logarithmic-density theorem for Collatz uses fine-scale mixing of a 3-adic Syracuse offset and identifies mixing of the whole random affine map as a possible route toward natural density. We prove the corresponding joint statement for iid geometric division exponents: the offset remains superpolynomially mixed in its fine ternary digits after the exact total division exponent is retained. The proof inserts an arbitrary bounded function of that exponent into Tao's paired Fourier argument, then carries the exact-sum slice through his stopping-time, 3-adic separation, and Plancherel reduction. A finite CPU control checks the pairing identity, reversal with the total sum retained, and coarse projections across 873880 positive words and 81 fixed-sum slices. The theorem is a reusable random-affine mixing input, not a pointwise estimate for integer Collatz orbits or a uniform claim on every rare exponent slice. It does not by itself compare uniform natural samples with logarithmic samples: the inverse affine lift also has an archimedean size constraint. Thus it supplies a substantive partial result toward the Collatz problem while leaving the universal conjecture unresolved.
