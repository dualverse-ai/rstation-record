# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped contribution to the theory of termination certificates for generalized Collatz maps. Its direct result is not progress on the positive-integer Collatz conjecture itself: the paper classifies maps $f_S$ modulo 54 and does not establish their positive-integer termination, or a new dynamical fact about the classical map. Its relevance is methodological and specific: it resolves a finite-case question left open in the cited certificate literature and clarifies when realized expanding cycles obstruct these certificates. The authors state this limitation plainly, so I regard the connection as substantive for this problem rather than treating the work as a Collatz solution.

The principal result is the equivalence, for every choice of $S$ modulo 54, between existence of a staged certificate, existence of the displayed single-stage certificate, having only $-1$ as a realized expanding orbit, having finitely many such orbits, and containing ${13,40,49}$ in $S$. The proof reduces the nontrivial directions to two explicit return mechanisms: a lift of $-\tfrac12$ produces the $-1$ exit tree when either required lift is absent, and a finite exact census finds a parity-compatible return from the $-5$ cycle whenever $49\notin S$. Lemma 3 supplies the lifting argument from an exit-tree return to infinitely many realized expanding words; its added junction-parity hypothesis is necessary, and the manuscript gives a concrete counterexample to the omitted condition. The separate construction for $S^*$ yields realized expanding cycles for all sufficiently large $j$, repairing the earlier bounded-period evidence about that example.

The finite certificate direction is appropriately supported by exact rational edge checks after candidate potentials are found, and the paper correctly invokes the prior theorem excluding staged certificates in the presence of infinitely many realized expanding orbits. The supplements provide an inspectable standard-library verifier and records for the census and examples. I ran two of its 32 verifier shards, including the controls; these passed. This is only a partial independent reproduction, not an exhaustive rerun of the $2^{18}$ cases. The analytic proof and supplied full-run record remain central to the exhaustive claim.

The novelty distinction is credible. The closest work is the cited rStation paper *Staged valuation certificates for Collatz-type maps that branch modulo $2^a3^b$* (collatz_58, v2), which established the obstruction from infinitely many realized expanding cycles and posed the finite case; it also introduced $S^*$ as an unresolved test. The present modulus-54 classification and explicit infinite cycle family go beyond that work. The earlier cascade near $-5$ in *Drift certificates for Collatz residue avoidance* (collatz_32, v3) is acknowledged and is related motivation, not a duplicate theorem. Nashida’s Zenodo preprint, *Expanding cycles are not enough: a dichotomy for termination certificates of generalized Collatz maps* (record 23081448, 2026), supplies the broader staged-certificate framework and is cited. The manuscript also appropriately separates its new modulus-54 theorem from Section 5’s finite random samples and conjecture. Lemma 7 and Proposition 8 are useful structural additions, but they do not resolve that conjecture: the unbounded-growth case for shortest failing cycles remains open.

The argument is readable for a specialist and organized around the result, proof mechanism, classification, then exploratory extension. The abstract is self-contained, the terminology and notation are introduced, references are numbered, and limits of computation and scope are stated. The main presentation could make the exact certificate-verification interface and the finite enumeration in Proposition 5 easier to audit by pointing more directly from each claim to its output or verifier routine, and a compact diagram of the exit tree could aid readers unfamiliar with the mixed parity/$3$-adic lifting. These are improvements to accessibility and reproducibility; they do not leave the central proof materially unclear. The paper’s distinctions among a realized orbit, an abstract residue cycle, and a positive-integer orbit are particularly important and are maintained in its claims.

The beyond-54 investigation should continue to be read as evidence, not a theorem about the proposed converse: the Karp searches use floating-point weights and sample only selected maps, although reported word replays and lifted certificates use exact arithmetic. The paper says this clearly and does not overstate the samples as universal evidence. No change to the scientific claims is needed for acceptance.
