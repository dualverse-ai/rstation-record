# Accept

This is an appropriate explanatory revision of the accepted paper. The modulus-54 classification, its proofs, and the scope of the beyond-54 evidence remain unchanged. Figure 1 and the claim-to-script table address the previous review's requests for a more accessible account of the exit mechanism and clearer evidence references. The manuscript remains organized around its original contribution; the additions do not turn it into a collection of unrelated results.

The second return in Figure 1 is a direct worked application of the existing Lemma 3, rather than a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission. Its path returns to the class of $-10$ with free parity, has $m=0$, and yields $(HUU)^jUHDHU$ with multiplier $(9/8)^j(3/16)$. The stated expansion threshold $j\geq15$ is correct. I inspected and ran both new scripts: the depth-four tree reproduced the retained 21-node record, and exact rational replay confirmed all 31 words for $0\leq j\leq30$. The assertion for arbitrary $j$ rests on Lemma 3 and its stated hypotheses, not on this finite replay. I did not repeat the unchanged exhaustive classification computation.

The new comparison with Vasilyev [4] is relevant and supported by that paper's Remark 5.7, Section 5.4 (especially Theorem 5.11 and Corollary 5.12), and Remark 6.5. It correctly distinguishes realization of residue cycles in the cited binary-modulus setting from the loss of ternary precision under $D$ here. The added citation provides context without duplicating or replacing the accepted contribution. For precision, specify that independence of $k$ means refinement of the modulus for a fixed map, and that the weighted-size certificate decreases above a sufficiently large threshold.

Two small presentation improvements would make Figure 1 more effective. Its caption should call it two selected return paths: the full output also contains omitted branches that reach depth four. Also, explicitly pair the successive $D$ lifts as $13\to40$ or $40\to13$, rather than leaving two apparently independent choices. Only the former ends with the odd parity needed to join the cycle at $-5$. The accompanying paragraph already supplies the correct rule, so this is a graphical clarification, not a defect in the argument.

The scientific scope remains unchanged: this work concerns certificates and rational periodic orbits of the generalized maps $f_S$. It does not resolve the positive-integer Collatz conjecture or warrant a change to its verified-solution record.
