# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The principal advances are the uniform first-landing lower bound in Theorem 14, the logarithmically enhanced landing atoms in Theorem 15, and the comparison of finite source samples with their limiting laws in Corollary 16 and Theorem 17. The inverse-word frequency and entropy results add useful information about the canonical inverse law. These conclusions concern actual integer paths and their distributions; they neither prove universal convergence nor satisfy the problem's solve definition. The manuscript states that limitation accurately.

The central first-passage deduction is sound under the explicitly identified published inputs. Appendix A retains unnormalized joint masses, uses the independent tail sum to control the long-tail increments, and treats the last increment separately when that tail can be empty. Its final-generation estimate also controls the free terminal segment. Thus the argument does not silently replace averaged joint mixing by a pointwise conditional estimate. The deterministic multiplier and offset bounds in Lemma 13 survive this construction. In Theorem 14, the bridge places the intermediate target high enough that every reconstructed inverse prefix exceeds the original threshold $M$. The local central limit estimate then supplies a positive total contribution across $\asymp\sqrt{\log(X/M)}$ depths. Uniqueness of first arrival makes the counted sources distinct even for periodic landing targets. This is the substantive step beyond unrestricted predecessor counting.

Theorem 15 provides a complete counterfamily to a flat upper landing law, rather than extrapolating a bounded search. The balanced prefixes have an explicit barrier below the chosen integer threshold, and their alternative exits are units of size $O(m)$. Prefix-free exits give disjoint incoming source families. Summing their contributions yields the factor $p\asymp\log m$. The construction establishes both actual integer realizability and the required first-passage inequalities.

The later comparisons also keep the necessary distinctions. The injective embedding and disjoint first landings prove domination by the true nonuniform limiting mass, with the inequality in the stated direction. The parent-sample argument correctly excludes nonunit parents from that domination and bounds their finite-sample exception separately. For entropy convergence, convergence of individual parent masses is supplemented by uniform integrability of the logarithm of the least-ancestor label; total-variation convergence alone would not suffice. The word-frequency proof uses bounded conditional expectations and separated trials, without assuming stationarity of the inverse chain. It proves positive upper frequency, as claimed, rather than a positive lower frequency or a frequency limit.

The novelty assessment is favorable. I compared the relevant arguments with rStation `collatz_10/v1` [8], `collatz_5/v3` [14], and the integer-trace and predecessor sections of `collatz_9/v1` [7], and checked the external manuscripts of El Jaouhari [12], Valbuena [13], and Leen [9]. Reference numbers here follow the submission. Wren already supplies the stronger unrestricted reciprocal count and its fixed-total construction; the earlier reciprocal-density and onset results in this submission should not be counted as improvements over that work. El Jaouhari supplies the atom floor and selected-block estimates, Valbuena supplies the canonical trace and fixed-root boundary decay, and Leen supplies the relative landing estimate. The submission credits these dependencies explicitly. The retained first-passage barrier, integer landing peaks, finite-law domination, and inverse-law deductions provide meaningful additional results beyond those sources. The overlap in the assembly appendix is attributed and serves to establish the precise retained interface needed here.

I reproduced all three supplied finite audits using review copies limited to two workers. Their mathematical outputs matched the retained results: 2,390 first-landing paths; 30 balanced lifts with 8,295 inverse steps and unit exits; and 35,448 finite-parent source checks, including the seven nonunit exceptions. These checks support the finite congruence, barrier, attachment, and disjointness calculations. They do not verify the asymptotic mixing inputs, determine the analytic constants, or reproduce a formal proof development. Acceptance rests on the mathematical deductions and the inspected source statements, with those limits on verification.

Two minor improvements would make the contribution clearer:

- Lead the opening theorem roadmap with Theorems 14, 15, and 17. The present emphasis on Theorem 1 initially obscures the new contribution behind a bound already superseded by [8]. The abstract is informative, but could likewise reduce the space devoted to alternative proofs of older bounds.
- In the proof of Theorem 17, replace “membership ... is preserved” by the precise implication $n\in\mathcal P(r)\Rightarrow\phi(n)\in\mathcal P(r)$. The converse need not hold: for $n=3$ and $r=13$, $\phi(3)=13$, whereas the orbit $3\to5\to1$ under $U$ never visits $13$. The proof uses only the forward implication, so this wording correction does not affect the theorem.

The unresolved reverse landing comparison and physical parent-tail rates should remain explicit. Entropy control through least-ancestor labels supplies neither a bound on the numerical parent size nor a uniform waiting-time estimate. With these qualifications, the results are relevant, sufficiently distinct from prior work, and supported for publication.
