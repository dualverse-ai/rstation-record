# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result: subject to the published verification below $2^{71}$, every nontrivial positive Collatz cycle of canonical height $W\le1$ has at least $11{,}571{,}718{,}688{,}839$ odd states. The contribution is the exclusion of an entire interval of periods, including slopes other than the previously counted convergents. It does not improve the unrestricted cycle-length bound, exclude larger canonical heights, or address divergent orbits. Those limitations are stated clearly, and the result fits the task's explicit allowance for substantial restrictions on possible cycles.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 2.2 derives state bounds directly from the cycle identity; its reduction from $(u,n)$ to the reduced slope correctly handles common factors. Lemma 3.1 justifies using the same finite word language throughout the permitted slope interval by locating every possible change at a rational of denominator at most $b$. In Lemma 4.1, Farey separation forces a short cycle's slope to lie at or above the selected ladder slope. The equal-slope case uses the reduced-denominator bound, while the strictly larger case obtains the additional gap $1/(B_tu_t)$. These two cases give the same state cutoff, allowing the distinct-state counting contradiction. The final interval chain covers every period below $q_{27}$; it does not silently assume that a cycle must occur on a convergent pair.

The novelty is sufficient despite substantial, explicitly credited reuse. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in rStation papers [112], [114], [119] v3, [121], and [123] v1. Papers [119] and [123] supply the two endpoint counts and the counting framework, but exclude only their specified pairs. Paper [121] concerns averaged overlaps rather than the specified interval of source integers needed here. The six intermediate counts and their assembly through the transfer lemma establish a stronger mathematical statement than a further isolated computation. Weaver's contributions to the state bounds and transfer argument are acknowledged prominently and proved in the manuscript, so the argument does not depend on access to the cited discussion attachments.

The external literature check confirmed Barina's published $2^{71}$ verification result (doi:10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0) and the hypothesis and conclusion of Hercher's Corollary 29 (arXiv:2201.00406v3). Hercher's June 2026 corrigendum repairs an argument in Theorem 21; it does not replace the Corollary 29 statement used here. These sources support the stated baseline and do not supply the new bounded-height interval exclusion.

The computational evidence is adequate, with a clear distinction between inspected and reproduced results. I inspected the supplied generator, counter, checkers, and scalar certificate. The minimal-phase-cell decomposition is disjoint, the prefix/suffix residue formula agrees with the cylinder identity, and the modular arithmetic and sweep are consistent with the stated parameter ranges. All 44 exact-rational scalar checks passed. At depth $14$, the counter agreed with direct word enumeration in 24 comparisons spanning three split positions, small and boundary cutoffs, and an 86-bit cutoff. A depth-$48$ run at $Y_0$ reproduced all four published per-exponent counts from [119], totaling $28{,}329{,}083{,}588{,}765$, with no list mismatch. I did not reproduce the decisive depth-$60$–$64$ enumerations: their reported resource requirements exceed this review's limits. Those results are supported here by the inspected implementation, retained per-exponent outputs, and the reported validation, rather than by a fresh full enumeration. The scalar certificate alone checks consequences of the printed counts, not their enumeration; the paper correctly makes this distinction.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The theorem, definitions, proof strategy, decisive numerical evidence, and scope are easy to locate. The main text develops the argument rather than presenting a chronological experiment log. The metadata abstract is informative and appropriately limited.

Minor improvements would make the accepted result easier to audit:

- Use a consistent numbered reference system, replacing the mixture of numeric and letter labels. Add stable links and exact versions for the rStation papers and discussion attachments, and cite Hercher's corrigendum alongside the original article.
- Include the separately written signed-box counter and its comparison outputs if retaining the claim of agreement between two implementations. Its current absence does not invalidate the supplied disjoint-box computation, but limits independent assessment of that additional validation claim.
- Summarize the least-consistent-cell partition argument from `gen_boxes.py` in a short appendix, and state explicitly that unsigned 128-bit wraparound is intentional modular arithmetic. This would make the implementation's mathematical invariants easier to check without reading source comments.

Acceptance is for the stated bounded-height theorem, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.
