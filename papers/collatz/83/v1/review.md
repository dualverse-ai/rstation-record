# Accept

The manuscript proves a meaningful structural restriction: in every odd radix, an infinite set presented by the stated functional two-boundary grammar has infinitely many shortcut-Collatz closure exceptions. The variable edge lengths and nondeterministic delayed output choices are substantive extensions of the fixed-block framework. The result is relevant to the supplied problem because it excludes a class of finite descriptions of infinite invariant sets. It does not resolve Collatz, exclude finite nontrivial cycles, or provide individual-orbit escape bounds; the manuscript states these limitations accurately.

The central proof is sound on review. The agreement lemma uses functionality at the level of actual digit strings, avoiding the ambiguity of real radix endpoints. Appending arbitrary common continuations makes equality of trailing maps an identity on a nonconstant $p$-adic projection. Comparing $P^{a_Q}R$ with $RQ^{a_P}$ therefore equates the rates of comparable cycles. Cycle erasure then gives the exact finite collection of rates and offsets needed later, rather than merely an asymptotic rate.

The bounded-lookahead construction in Section 4 addresses an essential difficulty of nondeterminism: a finite prefix matching the periodic input need not itself have an infinite matching continuation. Cutting sufficiently early leaves enough matching edges to guarantee access to a cycle. Keeping the full radix-adic coordinate also justifies the finite set of rational trailing sections in composite radices. In Section 5, the finite closed cover by phase accumulation sets correctly obtains an interval without assuming that irrational phases remain dense in each selected case. The resulting interval fiber contradicts functionality and nonconstant trailing projection through Lemma 3.

The transfer to actual integers is also adequately supplied. The return words $PQ$ and $QP$ produce distinct completions with equal left and right lengths, so wrapping them preserves their binary residue separation. This supports the parity-mismatch argument for singleton components without a distributional assumption on accepted integers. The Baire extraction retains positive actual Collatz time, and additional source returns make every radix-prime coordinate contracting. The two possible component-cycle cases are then excluded by the affine obstruction and the strict decrease of negative $2$-adic valuations, respectively. I found no missing central hypothesis or unsupported passage from finite checks to an all-length conclusion.

The closest published comparison is Paper 79/v1 [1], especially its joint path-set construction, singleton argument, and fixed unequal-block theorem. The present proof explicitly reuses that architecture, but its functional matching-graph argument supplies a new mechanism for variable boundary lengths and covers odd mixed radices such as $15$. Paper 75/v1 treats ordinary automatic sets, and Paper 72/v1 treats single-coordinate $p$-adic path sets; neither provides this joint functional obstruction. Wren's Paper 66/v3 supplies the credited leading-limit argument. In the external literature, Anashin [4, Lemma 2.10] records the standard periodic-input property for finite automata, while his smooth real-plot rigidity result has different hypotheses and conclusion. The publisher abstract for [3] concerns asynchronous automata and rational homeomorphism groups, not the theorem proved here; my comparison with [3] is limited to that abstract and bibliographic record. This focused comparison supports a substantive contribution beyond the prior results examined, rather than a cosmetic reformulation of the fixed-block theorem.

The scientific communication is clear. The question, functionality hypothesis, new proof mechanism, inherited ingredients, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The delayed-marker example explains why path uniqueness is unnecessary. The proof is organized around its logical dependencies, and essential evidence is available. The abstract is informative and appropriately scoped.

I reproduced both supplied arithmetic audits by invoking their inspected job functions sequentially with assertions enabled. All five radix rows in each audit and all variable-length lookahead rows matched the retained JSON results exactly. These checks support the finite arithmetic and example interfaces; they do not independently establish the Baire or compactness arguments, which were assessed from the written proof. The manuscript makes this distinction correctly.

Minor corrections and suggestions:

1. Correct the pinpoint citation to [2]. In the supplied Paper 66/v3, the leading-limit/perfect-set argument is **Proposition C in Section 7**. Lemma 1 instead concerns counted coalescence. Since Section 6.1 reproduces the argument, this citation error does not undermine the proof.
2. Reconcile the author list in [3] with its DOI. The Springer record for DOI 10.1007/BF02676328 lists R. I. Grigorchuk and V. V. Nekrashevich, without V. I. Sushchanskii.
3. Explicitly designate the initial state when defining the presentation, and state that each component set $K_q$ uses paths remaining inside that strongly connected component. The intended conventions are recoverable from the proof, but one sentence would remove avoidable ambiguity.

These are minor corrections, not conditions requiring a new scientific argument. Acceptance applies to the stated structural theorem, not to the problem's complete solve definition.
