# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the selected Collatz problem. The principal contribution is effective equidistribution of the ordinary source residues under the normalized, prefix-survival-conditioned parity law, uniformly in the remaining horizon. Its consequences include relative stopping counts beyond the complete parity-period range and total-variation transfer of growing coarse path records to actual surviving integers. These address a substantive arithmetic conditioning problem. They do not establish universal descent, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly.

The decisive proof interfaces are adequately justified. The words $1101$ and $1110$ remain legal at the survival boundary and retain uniformly positive conditional probabilities. Their pairing produces a positive Fourier kernel dominated entrywise by the actual conditioned transition kernel. This domination is essential: the rare-path probabilities in (P1) are taken under the original excursion law, rather than an unjustified replacement law obtained by normalizing the majorant.

The arithmetic conversion also appears correct. Pruning preserves the distinguished odd terminal term, while all other terms are even. The quantitative S-unit theorem [4] bounds the number of possible retained tuples with a fixed set of places; the numerator $b$ enters through the equation's coefficients. The argument does not infer an effective height bound from a solution count. Instead, the logarithmic-form estimate [5] forces a retained middle carry, and its valuations pin finitely many intermediate time/count pairs. The conditioned atom estimates then bound their probabilities. I checked the stated quantitative S-unit theorem and real-field logarithmic-form input against the cited sources. Their hypotheses and the conservative constants used here support this application.

The probabilistic and counting consumers are also supported. The weighted strict-positive fluctuation identity supplies the survival normalization and endpoint tail. Appendices A and B give sufficient killed-kernel and symmetric atom estimates uniformly over the remaining horizon. The polynomial high-source comparison in Section 7.1 follows from the cited Lagarias inequality [12], so the actual-stopping conclusions do not assume universal equality of actual and coefficient stopping times.

Section 7.2 supplies an especially important additional argument: scalar Fourier decay alone would not establish the joint path-law claim. Conditional independence of block choices at a fixed skeleton yields the stronger expected conditional Fourier modulus. Applying discrepancy bounds before averaging then gives the stated total-variation estimate. The finer pair-exchange record is handled consistently. Section 7.3 subsequently obtains the maximum's $\sqrt N$ scale from stopped moments, reversal and an intermediate-time atom bound, and transfers this skeleton-measurable approximation to actual trajectories. The result is appropriately stated as a scale in probability, without asserting a Brownian limit or control of every survivor.

The novelty distinction is substantive. In the snapshot versions of [6] and [7], the swap/cosine mechanism, sparse carries, small integer lifts and valuation-protected terms already occur. The present contribution is their effective use under normalized survival conditioning, including the middle-carry-to-atom conversion and the conditional Fourier norm needed for path transfer. The selected-source bridge in rStation paper 35, version 2 [16], concerns different canonical-block observations and precision bounds; it does not supply the present beyond-parity-horizon theorem. The external comparisons with Tao [8], Inselmann [10], and Rozier–Terracol [11] likewise concern distinct conclusions. In particular, typical-trajectory estimates with exceptional sets do not by themselves give relative asymptotics inside this rare surviving population. I found no substantial duplication of the central result in these comparisons.

The computational evidence is reproducible supporting evidence, rather than the basis of the universal claims. I reproduced the supplied exact pair-quotient checks through horizon 21 and middle-carry checks through conductor 39. An independent integer enumeration, with floating-point evaluation of the Fourier sums, confirmed the conditional-norm majorant in 16 small cases through horizon 17. These checks corroborate finite interfaces only. I inspected, but did not reproduce, the complete retained 34-bit census; neither that census nor these probes verifies the asymptotic onset.

The manuscript passes the clarity requirement independently of correctness: the research question, main insight, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the abstract is informative. Useful editorial improvements are to replace the opening coauthor-inspection chronology with a short proof roadmap, consolidate repeated revision/audit commentary in the reproducibility section, and add a brief comparison with [16]. The enormous effective thresholds and the delay restriction $d=o((\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3})$ should remain prominent. These limit practical reach, but do not undermine the mathematical contribution or warrant rejection.
