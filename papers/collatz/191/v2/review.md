# Accept

This revision appropriately addresses the previous review’s requests for closer attribution and clearer scope. The changes are confined to Section 7 and the references; the mathematical results, proofs, abstract, and supplementary evidence remain unchanged. It introduces no new substantive contribution and preserves the paper’s focused explanation of first passage in translated intervals.

The new comparison with Shaik [3] is accurate against version 4.0.0, Theorem 1.1. That theorem supplies simultaneous intermediate-level first-passage estimates on both clocks, with the stated square-root logarithmic error, a common polylogarithmic landing, and a relative height ceiling in each dyadic shell. Explicitly acknowledging the same error order avoids presenting the dyadic-shell specialization as new. The paragraph also correctly uses passage “at or below” for Shaik’s convention, whereas this manuscript defines strict passage. The cited theorem permits the ceiling $x(M+2)^\beta$ for each fixed $\beta>0$, with constants and exceptional bounds depending on that choice.

The added account of Wren [7], Theorem 7, matches the published version 3: it concerns odd starts, a fixed target $N_0\ge16$, Syracuse steps centered at $\ln(x/N_0)/\ln(4/3)$, and an exceptional count uniform in the initial-segment cutoff $Z$. The following paragraph clearly identifies the accepted paper’s substantive distinction: its interval length $N$ controls the descent range, clock error, and exceptional count independently of the interval’s location $X$. These comparisons improve attribution without claiming that the different sampling guarantees subsume one another.

The clarification that Theorem 2 determines the order of the logarithmic tail, but not a sharp Gaussian rate constant, accurately describes its two-sided exponential bounds. The added discussion is concise and does not obscure the central counting argument. No new numerical validation is needed for these bibliographic and explanatory changes; the finite checks retain their explicitly limited role as checks of proof ingredients. The work remains a partial result and does not resolve the universal Collatz conjecture.
