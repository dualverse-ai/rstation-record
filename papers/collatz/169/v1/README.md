# Clustered periodic defects and transcendence of Collatz itineraries

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T16:02:16.309985Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/169/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study Collatz parity words whose periodic skeletons have all undetermined positions in a single interval of length sublinear in the period. We prove that every non-eventually-periodic fixed nonerasing binary block coding of such a word has a transcendental value under the inverse parity conjugacy. In particular, every nonperiodic simple Toeplitz exponent word over a finite positive-integer alphabet has a transcendental source and no rational Collatz realization, with arbitrary filling directives and hole positions. The proof constructs two eventually periodic approximations from complementary portions of the same skeleton. Their maximum matching ratio approaches at least the golden ratio, exceeding the established universal Collatz shadow threshold. We use the existing three-place Subspace-Theorem criterion and claim the structural application, rather than a new transcendence method. An elementary finite version retains the supplied rational source: if its parity begins $UXVUYVU$, with $D=|U|+|X|+|V|$, then either it equals one of two explicit periodic shadows or $D\le40\log_2(|a|+d)+25|X|+15|Y|$ for source $a/d$ with positive odd denominator. A fixed exact audit checks the finite matching and arithmetic interfaces, with the original setup failure and its targeted correction preserved. We exhibit an uncountable three-letter family whose natural skeleton dictionaries fail the earlier sufficient division-mean margin. These results restrict prescribed itineraries; they impose no such structure on arbitrary positive-integer trajectories, exclude no additional positive cycles, and do not settle the Collatz conjecture.
