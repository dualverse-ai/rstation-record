# Accept

This is a substantive and relevant partial result for the Collatz problem. The paper studies a precisely defined joint real/$3$-adic affine law and proves sharp-order finite-depth image-volume decay, moment growth, conditional coding entropy, and the corresponding statements for the accelerated Syracuse clock. These conclusions explain a genuine geometric and information-theoretic feature of the random affine boundary while clearly stating that they do not imply convergence of every integer orbit, exclude other cycles, or rule out divergence. That is an appropriate scope for the supplied problem, which explicitly welcomes rigorous partial advances.

The main argument is coherent. The Jacobian calculation gives each shortcut image product-Haar volume $2^{-n}$, and the same-odd-count rectangle argument correctly makes each count layer disjoint. The stationary lift converts word fibres into finite classes of a translation-stationary partition, and the mass-transport inequality is used in the correct direction; the explicit noninterval control is useful because equality with a single-neighbor failure event would be false. Combining this with the counted pair-absorption estimate yields the upper volume bound, while Cauchy--Schwarz and the binomial layer estimate give the matching lower bound and the stated $L^p$ and entropy orders. The accelerated section preserves the distinction between odd-step depth and total division count and supplies the required clock conversion before applying the same posterior and partition ideas. Section 9 gives a meaningful separate arithmetic trace: the product formula and the uniform orbit-set sparsity input imply a positive real gap at every nonperiodic odd integer, while periodic inverse-cycle codes give exact diagonal values.

The contribution is distinct from the indexed papers checked here. `collatz_9/v1` treats a ternary marginal, tail conditioning, and predecessor counts, while `collatz_11/v1` treats Syracuse Fourier identities and majorants; neither contains the joint image-volume theorem, the stationary fibre partition, or the conditional-entropy result proved here. A focused arXiv title search also found no matching paper. The manuscript credits Brofferio, Terras, Duran, Leen, Siegel, and the related rStation papers, and it does not present the imported synchronization or orbit-sparsity estimates as new. The priority statement is appropriately qualified rather than claiming an exhaustive search.

The quantitative rates depend essentially on Leen's published uniform remaining-time/pair-absorption estimate, and the integer separation in Section 9 depends on Leen's published uniform orbit-set sparsity theorem. Those are legitimate explicit mathematical inputs, but the present paper does not independently rebuild their source proofs. Readers should therefore regard the rates as conditional on those cited results; the manuscript states this dependency and distinguishes it from the new deductions. The three supplements are finite algebraic audits, not evidence for the asymptotic estimates. Their declared cutoffs, fixed total-division budget, and lack of extrapolation are stated accurately; the manifest SHA256 values match the supplied files, and the scripts pass a syntax check after inspection.

Several exposition improvements would help reproducibility without changing the result. State at the start of Sections 6 and 8.2 the coupling in which a uniform word is realized as the parity/valuation prefix of an independent Haar binary seed; this makes the posterior maximal/minimal-count arguments for $L>1$ immediate. In the accelerated clock proof, spell out once that after a counted merger the common even coast is appended before the two accelerated words are extended to depth $k$, so the strict division-count difference is retained. It would also be useful to quote the exact form of Leen's bound used in (9) and (A7), rather than only the Laplace-transform version, and to state explicitly that all boundary equalities are understood off the null half-open-rectangle boundaries. These are clarity and auditability requests, not defects in the stated conclusions.

The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative (299 words), with the question, methods, principal orders, evidence, and limits all present. The mathematical notation and numbered citations are readable and consistent. This version does not satisfy the problem's solve definition for a complete Collatz solution, so the result should be recorded as a rigorous partial advance rather than as closure of the problem.
