# Singularity, volume decay, and coding entropy of the joint Collatz affine law

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-22T09:44:31.244653Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/19/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The real and ternary evaluations of the random Collatz affine series define a stationary law on $\mathbb R\times\mathbb Q_3$. We prove that this joint law is purely singular relative to product Haar measure. At shortcut depth $n$, the union of word images of $[0,L)\times\mathbb Z_3$ has volume at most $CL/\sqrt{n+1}$, uniformly for $L\ge1$, with matching order for each fixed $L$. The same conclusion holds at accelerated Syracuse depth $k$, with $\sqrt{k}$ replacing $\sqrt{n+1}$. For the unit seed, the transported density has $p$th integral of order $(n+1)^{(p-1)/2}$ or $k^{(p-1)/2}$ for every fixed $p>1$. Conditional word entropy equals half the logarithm of the depth, up to a bounded error. The endpoint determines the word once its odd count or total division count is specified, while revealing only bounded mutual information about that count. The proof imports Leen's counted pair-absorption estimates. Measure-preserving lifts identify word representations with finite classes of stationary partitions; mass transport and an extremal posterior argument bound image volume by neighboring-lift fusion failure. The accelerated argument requires an exact conversion between shortcut merger time and odd-step depth. Binomial and negative-binomial layers provide the reverse bounds. Three reproducible exact CPU audits check these interfaces. Using a published uniform orbit-sparsity bound, we also show that an odd positive integer is an exact simultaneous real and ternary code value precisely when it is periodic. Every code at a nonperiodic ternary integer retains a uniform positive relative real gap. We identify the integer diagonal trace with actual finite-source Collatz counts, but the invariant seed probability is unavailable at a fixed integer source. The results therefore do not exclude divergent integer trajectories or additional cycles.
