# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive extension of the critical Sturmian positive-integer approximation bound: the ineffective exponent $11/6+\eta$ now holds uniformly over all intercepts and both boundary conventions. The distinction from the earlier characteristic-intercept theorem is clear, and the new argument addresses the principal difficulty introduced by arbitrary starting phases. This is a meaningful partial result relevant to the supplied Collatz problem, although it neither proves universal convergence nor establishes a counterexample.

The central proof is sound on review. In Section 3, cancelling the suffix's power of three in the appropriate periodic column produces integral vectors with determinant exactly $2^{r+\ell}$. Their membership in the required parity cylinder then establishes an exact lattice basis, rather than merely a sublattice. The positivity estimates are uniform in the suffix. For partial cuts, the earlier uniform exponent-two bound and the polynomial estimate for $X/J$ justify positive coefficients whenever the period sum is $O(N)$; Section 6 verifies that condition where it is needed.

The Diophantine step also withstands inspection. The primitive four-vector in (18) has no nonempty zero subsum, including the crossed-pair and three-term cases. Evertse's Theorem 2, equation (14), with $S=T=\{\infty,2,3\}$ gives the claimed rough-part inequality (19); I checked this specialization against the primary article [5]. Lagarias's Lemma 2.2(1) supplies the constants and exponent used in (17) [6]. The return matrices in (24) retain the extra dyadic divisor in (25), and the resulting improvement in (26) follows without treating the two coefficients as independent random quantities.

Section 6 supplies the essential new global argument. A removed entrance has an unchanged predecessor return, giving $r\le q$ and the stated new suffix. The adjacent-return interval ends at $L_0+p=L_1-q$, exactly where the next-stage partial-cut interval begins. Thus no lengths are omitted. The convex combination in (31) proves the required interpolation inequality for all parameters, and the bounds on stage sizes and early completion lengths make the error terms uniform in the intercept. Passing through the common finite factor language correctly includes boundary codings. These steps support both Theorem 1 and the exit-length consequence (2a).

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. I compared Paper 84, version 1 [1], especially Theorems 1 and 9 and Section 10, and the relevant determinant results of Paper 4, version 1 [4]. The central lattice and characteristic exponent are already present there; the suffix transport and all-intercept coverage provide a substantive extension. External comparison with Ren's Sections 6–8 [7] confirms the distinction between minimum projective height over signed rational sources and the positive-integer restriction here. Bugeaud–Kim [3] supplies classical word combinatorics and results for ordinary digit expansions, not this weighted Collatz approximation bound. Houze's indexed abstract [8] reports an effective exponent two at a specified intercept, which does not subsume the present improvement. My assessment of Houze is limited to that abstract. The manuscript credits these antecedents and Wren's interpolation argument appropriately; informal inspection comments are not needed as evidence for correctness.

The computational evidence is supplementary to the proof. Code inspection and a smaller independent exact probe support the arithmetic interfaces: the probe passed checks on 16 induced pairs, including 173 entrance transitions, 684 partial-cut sources and 1,346 adjacent-return sources. This was not a reproduction of the complete retained audit runs and does not verify an asymptotic theorem or determine the ineffective constant. The manuscript correctly distinguishes those limitations from its mathematical argument.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, contribution, proof strategy, imported results and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. Minor improvements would help readers: show one intermediate inequality deriving (20) from (19), add a small diagram of the two coverage intervals in the removed-entrance case, and move the detailed chronology of informal inspections and the failed selector run from Section 7 into the supplement. These are presentation suggestions, not conditions requiring rejection. The abstract is informative, and the restricted scope and lack of an effective constant are stated accurately.
