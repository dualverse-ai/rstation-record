# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the stated problem. The main contribution is the extension of uniform inverse-minimality results to reference words containing arbitrarily large valuations: linear shielding bounds, an explicitly concatenable family preserving minimality at every matched or shallower horizon, and a complete single-terminal-letter coalescence classification along the alternating corridor. These results clarify both the reach and the limitations of smaller-source arguments. They do not establish convergence, exclude every nontrivial cycle, or resolve the supplied solve definition; the manuscript makes those limits clear.

The central arguments are sound. The alphabet-free lower bound constructs a surviving rival by choosing between two inverse exponents whose outputs cannot both vanish modulo $3$. The upper bound uses the exclusion of the negative fixed point $-1$, eventual odd integrality, and the $5/6$ contraction to control the discrepancy uniformly in the terminal valuation. The two-stage construction then handles incoming positive-cone states that a single shield cannot absorb. Separating this absorption argument from the shallower-horizon condition is essential: the explicit $B_3$ counterexample shows why matched-depth rigidity and prefix expansion alone would not suffice.

For the computer-assisted part, Lemma 4.7 provides the necessary coverage of both infinite domains. Its incoming-tail recurrence bounds every admissible exponent tuple, with the odd-integer tightening justified when $\beta_t$ is integral. The outgoing-tail argument starts on the same parity lattice as the discrete enumeration and bounds every subsequent exponent. Together with the shallower cutoffs and Lemma 4.6, these support arbitrary concatenations, rather than only the tested examples. I inspected the implementation and reran the complete supplied verifier in a separate working copy. All four suites passed in approximately 31 seconds, and every regenerated certificate field matched the submission apart from runtime. This reproduces the supplied computation; it is not an independent implementation or a formal verification. The all-$d$ conclusions depend on the analytical proofs, not on the finite lemma audits.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. In [9], the constant-five cone, binary minimum theorem, rational-cycle criteria, and half-source mechanism are established antecedents. In [10], unrestricted *rivals* are already allowed, but the reference language remains binary. The bounded terminal dictionaries of [15] concern targets approaching a fixed negative rational cycle; they do not supply the present linear shielding bounds or closure across the proposed unrestricted-reference generators. My external comparison included the Garner and Wirsching accounts in [7] and the greedy back-tracing results of Monks et al. [16]. Those results provide relevant ancestry and coalescence background, but do not establish the present uniform extremal statements. The elementary cylinder identities and individual affine mergers should continue to be presented as supporting tools, with the shielding and closure theorems carrying the novelty claim.

The scientific argument is identifiable and follows a conventional theorem–proof organization. The manuscript is dense, but its definitions, separation examples, proof strategy, limitations, and accessible certificates make the central reasoning followable. The following improvements are minor and do not change the decision:

- Correct the description of [9, Proposition 9] in Section 5.1. In the supplied published version, the companion word is $(1,2)^{L-1}(1,4,1,2,1)$, not $(1,2)^{L-1}(1,7)$. The attribution of the $(n-5)/2$ mechanism is otherwise appropriate.
- State explicitly how the limit-tree extinction certificates extend the bounded scans used to report the permanent $W_{m,d}$ thresholds. The code computes `m_perm` from a finite profile; readers should be directed to the extinction argument that rules out later exceptions.
- Make the abstract's “through $d=15$” wording match the actual certified range. In particular, Table 1 explicitly supplies the division threshold at $d=14$ by parity equivalence, but does not report its matched-depth threshold.
- Move repeated state counts, seam counts, and the regression-test narrative from the main theorem discussion into the reproducibility section or an appendix. Retain the mathematical coverage conditions in the main text. Define the word-count growth rate explicitly, and distinguish it from a density statement about integer sources.
