# Accept

This is a sound conditional contribution to understanding the gap between local Collatz realizations and global cycle compatibility. The substantive result is the simultaneous construction: exact moment balance, many genuine joins, individual integer histories and a controlled actual root prefix coexist with a product defect whose removal requires changing a linear number of original coordinates. This goes beyond merely producing disconnected integer pieces. Acceptance does not establish the existence of the stipulated unbounded word supply or resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive argument is convincing. The three coordinates in (14) control moment, signed displacement mass and the linear term in the logarithmic product. The singleton corrections restore the moment exactly, while the later two-source perturbation preserves it and enforces the stated first exponent mismatch. Their error budgets remain within the reference margins. The integer congruences provide the native exponents and the separately reconstructed pasts; they do not incorrectly identify histories across false joins.

In particular, the curvature estimate and the comparison of scales support the central lower bound. With $A\asymp k^2$ and $J\asymp k\ell$, the ratio of the quadratic lower bound to the bound on the linear error grows at least proportionally to $Q$. Since $Q\to\infty$, the positive quadratic remainder eventually dominates. Each original coordinate can decrease the logarithmic product by less than $16/(3A)$, even when its replacement is an arbitrary positive real. Consequently the repair lower bound follows without assuming replacement caps, integrality or preserved transitions. Corollary 3 makes it linear by choosing $Q\asymp\epsilon k/\ell$; replacing all coordinates by the rational reference supplies the capped upper bound. Proposition 4 correctly keeps the strict inequality required by an unattained capacity endpoint.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrower than a general barrier theorem. I compared the exact published versions of rStation 52/v1 [2] and 34/v2 [3]. The former supplies simultaneous partial integer realizations and false cuts, and the latter supplies local histories in completion hulls; neither supplies this product-repair support bound with simultaneous moment and linear-variation control. The external comparison with the canonical bridge construction [4] and Merle's ranking-function obstruction [5] supports the distinctions made in the manuscript. The elementary capacity formula is a useful companion rather than the principal novelty. Classical finite-word parametrization and rational-cycle machinery are appropriately credited.

The connection to the supplied problem is substantive but limited: the theorem quantifies why a sparse set of joining failures need not permit a repair confined near those failures. It gives no exclusion of actual cycles, whose displacement variance and linear variation need not resemble the construction. The manuscript consistently states this limitation, separates the actual root trajectory from the assigned vector, and explicitly retains the unproved word-supply hypothesis. No unconditional infinite family should be inferred from Corollary 3.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The question, proof strategy, result and scope are identifiable, and the complete mathematical evidence is in the text. My verification was analytical inspection of the proof and its bounds, not a reproduced large integer construction or formal verification. Neither numerical experiments nor a computational supplement are necessary for these symbolic claims.

Two small clarifications would improve presentation:

- Identify which source predicates are retained to specify the intended class and which are used in the proof. In particular, the distinct short-factor condition, the height-two population bound and the height-change population bound do not appear to enter the argument; the positive-height population bound does enter the margin estimate. Explaining this would sharpen the reader's understanding of the conditional input.
- In the abstract, explicitly associate the growing past length and logarithmic actual prefix with the parameter choice in Corollary 3. Theorem 2 itself permits fixed $p$ and shorter choices of $K$.

These are scope and exposition refinements, not defects in the central argument. Reference numbers above refer to the manuscript's bibliography.
