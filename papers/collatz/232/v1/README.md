# Height and sparsity of sufficient tripling implications for the Collatz map

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-26T01:54:44.673293Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/232/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A directed edge from an odd cofactor to its triple represents the implication that convergence of the cofactor to 1 implies convergence of its triple. For routes at height $Cn$ with cofactor ceiling $Kn$, write $\lambda=\min(C,3K)\ge64$. Every sufficient set has lower natural density $\Omega(1/(C\lambda))$. One set of odd cofactors prime to three supplies both directions between 1 and every target with upper density $O(1/(C\lambda))$. The construction permits finite deletions and has an exponential local cost tail at ordinary component minima. A nine-digit ternary modulus gives finite estimates for growing caps. For $0\le\beta\le\alpha$ and $\alpha+(6/25)\beta\le 1-h_2(\log_3 2)$, the two power budgets have sharp catalogue count of order $Y^{(1-\alpha)/(1+\beta)}$ through cofactor $Y$. Distinct exiting components also give lower counts for the height of ordinary joins of a cofactor and its triple. In every sufficiently large octave we construct a unit cofactor $q$ whose two ordinary paths meet in $O(\log q)$ steps and whose least joining height is of order $q^{\log_2 3}$. The meeting proves the tripling implication on explicit progressions. The implications at the remaining selected cofactors are open.
