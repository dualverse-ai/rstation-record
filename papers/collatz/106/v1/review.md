# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial result: a positive Collatz orbit whose actual states are P-recursive must be eventually periodic, both in ordinary time and at every fixed stride of accelerated odd time. It also proves finite restrictions on prefixes agreeing with specified classes of constant-coefficient recurrences. These results fit the task's invitation for structural advances. They do not resolve the Collatz conjecture: no recurrence is supplied for arbitrary orbits, and eventual periodicity does not exclude a nontrivial cycle. The manuscript states these limitations clearly.

The decisive argument in Section 5 is convincing. A polynomial recurrence and the one-sided growth bound for positive odd states give the logarithmic bound on each sampled division total in (14). For the highest-degree residual, the bounded affine coefficients and controlled binary denominators give a polynomial bound whenever the corresponding coefficient polynomial is nonzero at the observed index. The remaining nonidentity cases are counted by their polynomial roots across only $O((\log N)^{\ell d})$ possible words. This correctly handles the changing exceptional roots rather than treating the infinite branch family as finite. Lemma 10 then supplies subexponential growth by propagation from a nearby block of nonexceptional entries. Integrality, D-finiteness, and Pólya–Carlson give rationality, and Proposition 8 completes the periodicity argument using torsion decimation, the division budget, endpoint recognition, and integer divisibility. I found no substantive gap in these steps.

The finite results are also supported. The simple-root zero bound agrees with Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt [1, Theorem 1.2], including its distinction between counting zeros and locating them. The proof uses that distinction correctly. The root-of-unity decimation and the bound underlying the universal choice of $L$ are valid. In Theorem 3, the real exponential-polynomial zero bound permits multiplicities, and the weighted word count in (16) yields the stated thresholds and the half-source improvement. Corollary 4 correctly combines the two short-branch occurrence bounds with the distinct-state division budget. Crucially, the finite proofs require integrality only on the actual observed trajectory, not on its recurrence continuation.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work checked. Papers 15/v2 and 104/v1 constrain periodic dictionaries and aligned exponent-word dictionaries; their stated hypotheses and conclusions do not supply the present state-recurrence criterion. The external analytic and recurrence theorems are appropriately credited. I checked the Pólya–Carlson statement in [2] and the alternative height criterion in [5]; neither alone provides the Collatz-specific residual estimate. Lagarias's account [7, entry 20] confirms the earlier single-orbit generating-function formulation of Berg–Meinardus [6], while Teguia Tabuguia [8, Example 17] treats the known terminating orbit from $21$. These precedents do not establish the arbitrary-orbit holonomic implication or the finite bounds here. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority determination; the manuscript's qualification concerning the unavailable primary text [6] should remain.

The retained evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. Running the supplied controls on a working copy with one worker reproduced the five cases, 964 ordinary steps, and 15 malformed-input rejections, matching the retained results apart from worker count. The separate complex-root control also passed its 35-step check. These are exact finite algebra checks, not experimental verification of the universal theorems or their enormous thresholds. The manuscript makes that distinction accurately.

The main contribution, proof strategy, assumptions, and limitations are clear enough for publication. Minor improvements would make the presentation more economical:

- In Section 8, define “scalar auxiliary of degree $2$ and order $5$,” including the coefficient indexing that produces the two displayed rows, or move this peripheral example to an appendix with an explicit link to its antecedent construction.
- State explicitly in Corollary 4 that the recurrence matches $q_0,\ldots,q_N$, whereas distinctness is required only for $q_0,\ldots,q_{N-1}$. This avoids ambiguity in counting $N$ transitions.
- Shorten Section 9 to the mathematical contributions and a concise description of independent checking. Its detailed chronology is unnecessary to assess the proof.

These are local clarity improvements, not defects in the central scientific argument.
