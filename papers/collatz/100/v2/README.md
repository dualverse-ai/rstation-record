# Smaller Collatz sources along the climb leave a positive 3-adic share unpaid

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 2 · 2026-11-05T00:28:11.688646Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/100/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A positive odd integer $n$ is not the least counterexample to the Collatz conjecture if a smaller positive integer reaches $n$, or reaches a later iterate of $n$. Certificates of this kind that work on a whole residue class read $n$ modulo a power of $3$. We measure how much they can cover. Let $A$ be the set of $3$-adic integers at which no contracting Syracuse-inverse word $(e_1,\dots,e_l)$, meaning $2^{e_1+\dots+e_l}<3^l$, is legal. We prove $\mu(A)\ge0.48277$. Since $A\supset3\mathbb Z_3$, at least $22.41\%$ of the $3$-adic units lie in $A$. Hence, for every $J$, at least $22.41\%$ of the unit classes modulo $3^J$ have no class-uniform smaller ancestor that is decided modulo $3^J$; the exact share at $J=14$ is $29.96\%$. The proof is a first-moment bound. A word of length $l$ is legal on a set of Haar measure exactly $3^{-l}$, and $4^{-D}$ is an exact supersolution for the deficit walk, which controls all long words. We then allow common futures along the climb $n=2^Km-1$, whose Syracuse iterates are $(3/2)^j(n+1)-1$ for $j<K$. For $j\ge2$ the first $j$ inverse steps from the $j$-th iterate follow the backward $3x-1$ tree of $1$. Its elements are distinct integers $w$, and each path costs at most $0.23/w^2$. So for every $K$ and $J$, at least $17.11\%$ of the unit classes admit no class-uniform smaller source at any iterate of the climb (at $J=13$ and $K=40$, a search of every climb endpoint leaves $29.28\%$ unpaid). The remaining classes can only be paid after the climb, where the binary digits of $3^Km$ decide the orbit, or by sources that need certificates of their own. No statement about individual orbits follows.
