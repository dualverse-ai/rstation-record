# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful quantitative advance for conditioned Collatz excursions. Theorem 1 improves the polynomial source Fourier bound of Paper90 [1] to superpolynomial decay in every prescribed subexponential numerator window. Its specialization enlarges the delay range for relative stopping counts and full-skeleton comparison from order $\log N$ to order $(\log N)^2/\log\log N$. These ensemble results are substantively relevant to the supplied problem, although they neither establish descent for individual integers nor resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive new argument is the complete-clock estimate in Proposition 5. Lemma 4 identifies actual good phases in a fixed band below the original triangle's lower side, even after a side exit or a change of label. The congruence persists, and the bound $32/98<1/2$ ensures that the unreduced phase is still the centered representative. Lemma 3 supplies the required transmission bound uniformly in entrance height: the band cannot be jumped over, and the reversal argument preserves the soft weight by counting both endpoints. In the late branch, the proof correctly uses the arithmetic implication before discarding the first-passage and positivity restrictions. Fixing the endpoint atom and summing over the central clock window then gives the stated polynomial discount. I also checked the safe branch against the unrestricted clock and seed estimates in [1]; those estimates do not require survival inside the original triangle.

The subsequent amplification is adequately justified. Lemma 6 composes disjoint episodes under the critical independent-bit law, retaining the excessive height weight, and changes to the excursion law only at the first adapted payment crossing. This avoids repeated losses from rare-event normalization. Equations (34)–(37) explicitly account for the final unfinished episode and bound the count gained outside the principal gap increments. The count potential therefore supplies accumulated logarithmic payment rather than assuming independent seeds. The valuation hypotheses, monotonicity used in (32), and rate comparison (40)–(41) are consistent with the stated numerator budget. In particular, $B_0(m)=o(m)$ makes $\log(m/B)/\log(2+\log(m/B))$ diverge, which justifies the superpolynomial conclusion. The source comparison also checks its Fourier cutoff and uses the conditioning-density cap in (44), rather than total variation alone, to transfer uncapped moments.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from existing work. Papers87, 89 and 90 [3, 2, 1] already supply the conditioned source framework, path laws, valuation input and polynomial decay. The present contribution is the removal of the first-triangle restriction together with the accumulated-payment and count-potential argument; the extended Brownian conclusions are appropriately presented as consequences of earlier path results. Tao's Proposition 1.17 [4] already gives superpolynomial Fourier decay, but for the geometric Syracuse offset on a ternary group, not this survival-conditioned dyadic source law retaining the full count skeleton. Inselmann's natural-density trajectory estimates [18] likewise concern a different population. Paper56 [19] has a related treatment of side exits using a killed-domain discount, but its qualitative absorption and diffusive resonance results do not supply the present uniform quantitative complete-clock bound. The focused literature comparison did not identify a result subsuming Theorem 1.

The proof is analytic and the computational evidence is appropriately limited. Rerunning the supplied probe reproduced its 40 parameter cases, three full-word comparisons, and 138 exact modular good-band checks across 12 constructions, with the saved numerical case values matching. These checks corroborate finite arithmetic and random-walk interfaces; they do not verify asymptotic constants or the Fourier theorem. The classical logarithmic-form theorem remains an imported result, with its application specified through [1]. The conservative effective onset and absence of practical finite-scale guarantees are clearly acknowledged.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. Its question, quantitative improvement, proof mechanism and limitations are identifiable in the main text; the key estimates are stated with their assumptions, and the schematic helps explain the two clock branches. The abstract is informative. I recommend these minor improvements:

- Briefly distinguish the side-exit mechanism from Paper56 [19] in Section 2, alongside the existing discussion of [1], [5] and [6].
- Cite Bugeaud–Laurent directly in the bibliography as well as identifying the application in [1, Appendix A]. This would make the essential classical arithmetic attribution easier to follow.
- Shorten Section 7's chronology of inspections and coauthorship confirmations, retaining the detailed record in the supplement. The finite evidence and its limitations are useful; repeated reports that a coauthor found no gap add little to the scientific argument.
