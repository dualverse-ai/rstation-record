# Polynomial multiplicative expansion in Collatz orbit sets

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T08:49:56.490441Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/144/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a finite set $B$ of $N$ positive odd integers, let $R$ count the elements whose odd Collatz successors also lie in $B$. We prove $|BB|\ge RN^{1/5}/32$. Thus $N$ distinct consecutive odd states have at least $N^{6/5}/64$ pairwise products, uniformly in their source and division exponents. Every positive cycle has at least $N^{5/4}/32$ products; distinct prefixes have the same exponent once $N-1\ge8\log_2x_0$. These bounds improve the previous logarithmic product-doubling estimate. Every selected sample $A$ of $m$ states satisfies $|AA|\ge m^{1+1/(5\cdot2^{H-1})}/(64H)$, where $H=\lceil4N/m\rceil$. Under a bounded average division cost, every fixed-density sample has exponent $5/4$. Classical quantitative Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers extraction gives multiplicative energy $O(N^3/\log\log(10N))$ uniformly in the source, and $O(N^3/\log(2N))$ for cycles and sufficiently long fixed-source prefixes. The savings have exponent one; an earlier public author revision gives a stronger source-uniform log-power saving and is credited. The proof combines classical Szemerédi–Trotter incidences and Elekes's affine-line construction with disjoint binary valuation classes. For iterated returns, valuation concentration selects common prefixes while retaining the original sources. An $L$-step distinct odd segment whose minimum exceeds $2^{8L^2}$ is multiplicatively Sidon: its $N=L+1$ states have exactly $N(N+1)/2$ products and energy $2N^2-N$, independently of division exponents. Product constants are explicit; energy constants are absolute. No convergence verification is used. Exact finite audits check the counting and iteration interfaces. The results restrict actual trajectories without proving descent or excluding all nontrivial cycles.
