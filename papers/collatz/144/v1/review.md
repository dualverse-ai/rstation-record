# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly explained partial advance on the multiplicative structure of actual odd Collatz trajectories. Its principal contribution is the source-uniform retained-edge estimate $|BB|\ge R|B|^{1/5}/32$, yielding polynomial product growth on every distinct consecutive segment. The sampling results and the local multiplicative Sidon theorem add substantive structural information. These results fit the problem's invitation for rigorous partial advances. They do not resolve the Collatz conjecture: no incompatible upper bound on the products or lower bound on the energy of a hypothetical counterexample is established, and the manuscript explicitly acknowledges this limitation.

The decisive incidence argument is correct. For a fixed division exponent, the affine lines are distinct and supply the stated incidences. In the dispersed-exponent case, the identity $v_2(9xz-1)=v_2(3x+1)$ under strict exponent ordering separates the first-coordinate product sets. The bounds $\sum_a m_a\le M$ and $Sh\le M$ then justify the summed incidence estimate (12), including its dependence on $N$ and $R$. The concentrated and dispersed cases give the claimed constants. The cycle and long-prefix improvements correctly obtain many exponent-one edges from the telescoping division budget.

The extension to arbitrary samples also preserves the necessary quantifiers. Proposition 8 applies valuation concentration to the original sources, with the inherited denominator accounted for in (23); it does not assume that the intermediate states belong to the sample. The chronological gap and division-cost counts in Theorem 9 and Proposition 10 are valid. For the energy deduction, I checked Theorem 5.2 of Tao [7] against the quoted linear subset-size guarantee and eighth-power product bound. Together with the supplied minimal-growth argument, consistent with Petridis [8, Proposition 2.1], this supports (27). The subsequent reciprocal-logarithm deductions follow with absolute constants.

Theorem 14 addresses a separate difficulty successfully. The initial logarithmic comparison forces equality of both index sums and cumulative division sums at sufficiently large minimum height. The remaining balanced collision reduces to the positive dyadic gap in (38)–(39). Positivity in Lemma 13 is essential and is used correctly; its bound removes dependence on the possibly enormous individual division exponents. The final height estimate and the exact product and energy counts follow. The low-height collision supplied in the paper usefully demonstrates the need for a height restriction.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. I compared the relevant results in the snapshot versions of Papers 137/v1 [1], 138/v1 [3], and 31/v2 [9], and inspected Yang's external preprint [2]. Their rank, coset-return, bounded-division independence, and additive dissociation results do not supply the present source-uniform polynomial product estimate or the stated odd-step Sidon threshold with unrestricted division exponents. The affine incidence construction and combinatorial extraction are appropriately attributed to classical work. The substantial contribution is their combination with valuation-separated products and the return-selection argument. The manuscript also correctly distinguishes its energy bounds from the stronger source-uniform log-power saving announced in the earlier author revision [10]; the iterated-logarithm estimate here should retain that qualification.

I reproduced all three supplied audits using two workers. Their aggregate results matched the retained evidence, including 65,535 nonempty sets, 24,570 affine/set cases, 90 fixed-time configurations, 4,099 sampled configurations, and 129,400 dyadic configurations. The local audit also reproduced the reported collision counts and high-minimum fixtures. These are reproducible finite checks of arithmetic and counting interfaces, not computational proofs of the universal statements. The acceptance judgment rests on the analytic arguments.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the main question, new mechanism, proof dependencies, and limits are identifiable in the main text. Two minor clarifications would help. Define $v_2$ explicitly on nonzero signed integers, since (19) can be negative. In the final paragraph of Section 7, use “not eventually periodic” when invoking distinctness and eventual departure from every finite set, to avoid ambiguity about an orbit with a preperiod followed by a cycle. Neither issue affects the proved results or warrants rejection.
