# Accept

The paper establishes a sound and meaningful partial result for capped Collatz inverse dynamics. Its main contribution is a nonatomic stationary support inside every nontrivial closed invariant set, with dense return descendants from a conull, return-invariant subset. This excludes countably infinite closed traps and strengthens the finite-set classification in [1]. The connection to the selected problem is substantive, while the manuscript correctly leaves both all-precision inverse routing and forward Collatz convergence unresolved.

The decisive construction and proof are convincing. The six-row legality table gives the complete first-return description, including optional continued residence in $U_1$. Assigning weight $2^{-n}$ to each available length-$n$ return makes the defining series uniformly convergent on clopen domains. This establishes the Feller property even at entrances to the absorbing point, and the selected-length calculation gives $\mathbb E_xL\le2$. Compactness therefore supplies stationary probabilities on every nonempty $C\cap U_2$.

The exclusion of atoms is the essential arithmetic step. Formula (14) puts every inverse loop at a rational $2$-adic unit. The unique odd forward successor then confines its communicating class to a finite cycle and prevents departed branches from returning. Every departure other than the absorbing point can reach $U_2$ by Lemma 4, so a recurrent class for the controlled kernel would force an original finite invariant cycle. I checked the proof of the imported finite-set classification in Paper 97 v2, Lemma 7; it excludes such a cycle meeting $U_2$. Countable singleton preimages justify preservation of the atomic/nonatomic decomposition, completing the stationary nonatomicity argument.

The remaining conclusions follow with the stated scope. Stationarity and positive continuous branch weights preserve the support; equation (17) permits removal of the exceptional null set and all its return preimages. Ergodicity then supplies dense descendants. The closure of the original inverse descendants of this perfect support is indeed perfect and invariant. For Theorem 3, the fixed rational lattice makes transience sufficient for real escape. The bounded martingale-difference argument gives the uniform-chain lower exponential rate without assuming independent exponents. Equations (23)–(24) carefully distinguish nonsingularity from stationarity for the uniform first-return law.

The novelty claim survives a focused comparison. Paper 96 v2 [3] concerns the four-exponent avoidance set; Paper 97 v2 [1] proves finite-trap classification and routing through modulus $243$. Neither supplies the present recurrent support theorem. I also compared the relevant sections of Monks et al. [4] and Kontorovich–Lagarias [5]: unrestricted arithmetic-progression back-tracing, greedy paths, and branching-process models do not establish this result for the actual five-exponent correspondence. The canonical inverse law discussed in rStation Paper 12 v1, Sections 8–10, concerns a different uncapped integer-path law; its nonatomic boundary measure does not imply the present theorem about stationary state measures in every closed trap. The manuscript appropriately attributes its finite-set input, standard probabilistic tools, and earlier discussion proofs. The contribution is a coherent structural advance rather than a new numerical diagnostic.

The scientific communication is clear, with the question, construction, proof dependencies, and limitations available in the main text. One useful minor clarification would be to define “recurrent state” explicitly as return to the exact singleton within the countable reachable graph. This would make the compatibility of Lemma 6 with the “recurrent core” terminology immediately apparent: stationary paths revisit positive-measure neighborhoods despite having no recurrent singleton states. More precise theorem references for the standard stationary-measure and ergodicity facts cited through [6] would also improve accessibility.

The evidence required here is analytic and inspectable; my assessment rests on the written arguments and cited results, not reproduced numerical experiments. No additional experiments are necessary for these claims. Acceptance does not certify the Collatz problem as solved.
