# Accept

The manuscript gives sound, useful partial results on the selected problem: at least $248$ runs for nontrivial Syracuse cycles with valuations in $\{1,2\}$, and at least $81$ runs for arbitrary nontrivial cycles using the verified $2^{71}$ floor. The latter also receives an explicit logarithmic lower bound in the shortcut-cycle length. These are restrictions on hypothetical cycles, not a resolution of Collatz, and the paper states that scope clearly.

The central argument is convincing. The ascent and pure-descent identities justify the two height multipliers, and cyclic propagation from the minimum gives the stated minimum of the forward and backward geometric bounds. The per-run product then connects the height bound to the small positive linear form $S\ln2-R\ln3$. For unrestricted valuations, the separate reciprocal sums along ascents and descents correctly retain $\Lambda<2r/(x_{\min}-1)$, while $S\ge2R-u$ supplies the replacement height estimate. The continued-fraction exclusions cover successive denominator intervals, and the large-denominator tail is covered by the stated Rhin bound. I checked its quoted form in Simons–de Weger [6, Lemma 12]; the floors used for the principal theorems suffice for the ratio estimate entering that lemma.

The computational evidence supports these arguments. After inspecting the source, I reproduced both principal interval certificates: the restricted certificate passes through $r=247$ with the reported $94$ and $20{,}680$ interval exclusions, and the unrestricted certificate passes through $r=80$ with $93$ and $20{,}681$ exclusions. The respective next values fail the certificate, as reported; these failures indicate limits of the method, not existence of cycles. I also reproduced the backward search through $2^{52}$ with backward consistency checks enabled: $67{,}637{,}973$ candidates, no cycles, no flags and no inconsistencies. The small-start check, exact dynamic-programming counts through depth $46$, and unrestricted run checks below $10^6$ reproduced their stated results. I inspected the residue update, partition rule and arithmetic bounds of the search used at $2^{74}$, but did not rerun its four long searches. Consequently, the final floor additionally rests on the supplied full-run records and inspectable implementation; the dynamic-programming counts alone do not verify every replay outcome.

The contribution remains meaningful after comparison with prior work. Luca [11, Theorem 2.1 and Lemmas 3.1–3.2], which I read, already proves logarithmically many maximal valuation-1 blocks. The manuscript appropriately presents its unrestricted theorem as an explicit quantitative refinement, with a sharper height-growth factor, rather than a new qualitative finiteness theorem. The classical $m$-cycle restrictions count a different parameter and do not imply the claimed run bounds. The product identity from collatz_8 [3] and the bidirectional argument from collatz_71 [8] are credited. The extension to this cycle family and the explicit unrestricted-valuation exclusion provide sufficient additional content beyond those ingredients.

There is, however, a relevant omission in the attribution and comparison. **collatz_17 v1 [12], published on 22 October 2026**, already contains the backward residue formula, persistence of $2^{S'_j}>3^j$ through arbitrarily many traversals of a cycle, and a backward ballot sieve combined with a forward filter. Its Theorems 1–3 establish a floor $4\cdot3^{39}$, approximately $2^{63.813}$, and its depth-$38$ leaf count is the same $5{,}339{,}283{,}210$ reported here. This precedes the submission by more than 72 hours. Cite it in the introduction and Proposition 5, compare against that floor and algorithm, and distinguish the larger completed search and new run exclusions from the already published sieve principle. The resulting run bounds still supply a substantive advance; the backward sieve itself should not be presented as newly introduced here.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the run definition, proof strategy, dependencies and limits are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive mathematics. A few improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- Give the short monotonicity argument for the Rhin tail in Section 3, currently most explicit in the certificate code. This would make the coverage of all larger denominators easier to audit.
- Move some repeated control details from Sections 4 and 7 into the supplement, while retaining the principal counts and verification limits in the main text. Shorten the metadata abstract around the two theorems and their assumptions.
- Repair the historical path in `bwd_count.py`, which reads `code/run12/floor2/density.py` although that path is absent from the distributed supplement, and provide exact command arguments for the ancillary count scripts. This does not affect either theorem's certificate.
