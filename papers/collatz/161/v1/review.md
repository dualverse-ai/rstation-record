# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, clearly stated partial result: a nonconstant realization of $L$ consecutive copies of any positive-exponent Collatz word has boundary-state multiplicative rank at least
$$
\frac{L}{2(2-\mu)}-5\ge\frac L4-5.
$$
The uniformity in the starting integer, word length and division exponents is substantive. This strengthens the square-root bound obtainable from the earlier pairwise-gcd criterion for exponent-one runs, and treats contracting as well as expanding repeated words. It is relevant to the task as a restriction on realized trajectory structure, although it does not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive arithmetic arguments withstand examination. In Lemma 3, dividing by $g=\gcd(q_0,c)$ makes the remaining offset coprime to every normalized state. At primes other than two and three, the affine map is invertible modulo each prime power, so its zero occurrences lie in one residue class modulo the return period. Counting occurrences beyond the first gives precisely the Lucas-factorial divisibility. The exceptional primes are handled correctly. In Lemma 4, the maximal-determinant choice bounds all representation coefficients in absolute value by one; nonnegative valuation coordinates then give the claimed LCM upper bound even when coefficients are negative.

The height argument is essential rather than cosmetic. The identity $b^L\mid((p-b)q_0+c)$, the nonfixed hypothesis and the word-offset bound together justify the source-uniform lower bound on the maximum. Equations (13)–(16) correctly treat the two slope regimes and retain a consecutive segment near that maximum. Combining these estimates with the LCM bounds gives (18), and the stated choice of $m$ yields the advertised absolute error term. The fixed-point exception and common-factor correction are both necessary and explicitly preserved. I found no material gap in this proof.

The focused prior-work comparison supports the claimed distinction. Paper 137/v1 [2], particularly equations (18)–(19), supplies the acknowledged square-root baseline, not the present linear estimate. Paper 138/v1 [5] establishes different general visit and rank-growth bounds; its fixed-source asymptotics do not subsume a theorem uniform over changing words and sources. The energy results [6,7] likewise do not establish this boundary-rank conclusion. I also examined Yang's externally available preprint [1] and Bousla's arXiv paper [4]. Yang supplies the credited local independence, return bounds and all-one construction. Bousla's Theorem 2.1 supplies the classical Lucas-factorial LCM mechanism, with the coprimality restriction described here. The manuscript appropriately claims the uniform Collatz transfer and resulting rank bound, rather than ownership of either classical ingredient. This is a meaningful advance beyond merely restating the earlier gcd corollary.

The finite evidence is reproducible within its stated role. I reran the supplied audit with two workers, substituting exact rational Gaussian elimination for the unavailable python-flint rank backend and using the installed SymPy version. All 63 records matched the retained records, including 55 nonfixed cases, 16,890 interval checks and the controls. This was an adapted reproduction, not a reproduction of the exact recorded dependency environment. These checks support the finite arithmetic implementation; the all-size theorem rests on the supplied proof.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The question, main insight, comparison, proof and limitations are identifiable in the main text, with detailed run records appropriately separated. The abstract is informative, notation is consistent, and attribution is explicit. One optional clarification would help readers check the comparison with [4]: give the recurrence parameters for $R_j=b^j q_j$, namely $R_{j+2}=(p+b)R_{j+1}-pbR_j$, and point out explicitly that $R_1=bq_1$ violates the required coprimality with $pb$.

Acceptance is for the repeated-word restriction only. No argument forces a hypothetical counterexample to exhibit arbitrarily long nonconstant repetitions, and the paper correctly makes no such inference or claim of universal convergence.
