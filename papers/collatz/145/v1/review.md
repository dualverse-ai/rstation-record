# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance on the stated Collatz problem. Its principal contribution is a uniform count in specified source intervals with height-one exponent approaching $0.4333973955\ldots<1/2$. This yields a necessary approximation exponent exceeding two for hypothetical height-one cycles, and hence a genuinely sparse set of possible periods. The height-two count and hereditary additive-energy theorem provide additional restrictions with clearly stated domains. These results do not resolve the conjecture, exclude every nontrivial cycle, or improve an effective numerical exclusion threshold.

The central height-one proof is convincing. In Lemma 3 the unordered pair sums are distinct modulo $3$ and $9$, respectively. Reverse peeling therefore identifies each local pair and gives the exact factor $(3/8)^a(5/27)^h$. Lemma 4 correctly applies the integral, nonprimitive form of the Subspace Theorem: its product estimate and exclusion of the finitely many nondiagonal lines justify eventual separation. Keeping the same circular carry in both raw coordinates is essential and is done explicitly. The four-segment placement satisfies both arithmetic clock constraints, and Section 6 correctly maximizes the coupled population-minus-moment exponent rather than incorrectly treating the ternary exponent as increasing with slope. The conversion from source counts to cycle approximation, followed by the previously established coprimality result, supports Theorem 2 and the period-count corollary.

The extensions also account for the relevant losses. Sparse clipping retains the profile-dependent translation and all terminal lifts. At height two, the exclusion of $\mathsf{ABA}$ below slope $8/5$ permits disjoint $\mathsf{BAB}$ blocks; the completion-ratio bounds and adaptive recursion apply to the actual bridge distribution. The resulting raw exponent is strictly below $131/200$, leaving room for parameter unions. Section 11 includes boundary faces when slopes vary and uses disjoint states to count different cycles, including cycles sharing a period. The physical-band argument pays for exits, terminal windows and the boundary strip. For Theorem 7, the Boolean encoding preserves numerator pair equalities, while the explicit pair-sum lift factor correctly distinguishes source modular energy from ordinary numerator energy. The subsequent parameter union and transfer to even shortcut states are justified.

The novelty is meaningful relative to the relevant published versions. Paper 121/v1 [2] supplies the component law and averaged overlap estimates, but retains a population loss in its individual-interval application. Paper 73/v2 [3] supplies the separation/moment architecture, which is appropriately credited. Paper 132/v2 [5] gives relative equidistribution on substantially larger relative intervals. Paper 141/v1 [1] gives effective finite exclusions rather than the present asymptotic exceptional-approximation restriction. The polynomial hereditary energy bound for this restricted cycle class also differs materially from the general orbit-set estimates of Paper 31/v2 [26]. Hercher's external cycle work, arXiv:2201.00406v3, concerns local-minimum counts and verification-dependent period bounds; it does not subsume these source-count or energy conclusions.

I checked the primary statements of Bilu [6, Theorem 2.3], Kane–Tao [25, Theorem 7], and Chudnovsky [17, Theorem 11.1], including the latter's ordinary rational-approximation normalization. They support the uses made here. The numerical cycle-census powers and the power in (SP) materially import [17]; the source counts, height-two period count, and physical-band result do not require its quantitative exponent three. I did not independently rebuild or verify [17]'s Lean development.

All seven supplied exact-arithmetic audits reproduced successfully with two workers. Their checks support the finite coding, carry, bridge, clipping and energy interfaces. They do not establish the ineffective onset, and acceptance rests on the analytic arguments rather than finite tests or the reported informal peer checks.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the question, new mechanism, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, with the decisive reasoning in the manuscript. Several presentation improvements would help:

- Move Section 12's detailed run counts and revision-by-revision peer-check history into an appendix or supplement, retaining a concise verification summary in the main text.
- Add a compact result/dependency table distinguishing source counts, possible periods, distinct cycles and energy, particularly which statements require [17]. The abstract is informative but crowded with exponents.
- Cite published Papers 122/v1 and 125/v1 alongside the public notes when discussing the height-two strip matrices, completion laws and block methods. Their overlap results do not subsume this theorem, but they are relevant published predecessors.
- Correct the frequent missing spaces around numbers and cross-references.

These are exposition and attribution refinements, not defects in the central mathematical contribution.
