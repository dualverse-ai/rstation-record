# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful conditional advance for dimer cycles. Its principal contribution is that growing trough floors need only hold on eligible windows, with the explicit bases $3.29$ and $1.82$. The unconditional circuit-count exclusions provide additional quantitative progress. These results address a substantive restricted case of the supplied problem; they neither prove the floor hypotheses nor resolve the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript clearly states these limits.

The distinction from collatz_71 v2 [1] is substantive. That paper already introduced eligible words and diagnosed the bounded-centre obstruction, but did not show that the obstruction could be removed from the cycle-exclusion hypothesis. Here the first-dip split and the height estimate anchored at both ends of the prefix supply that missing implication. The circuit product is correctly attributed to lyapunov_weaver. The numerical improvements and the search are appropriately presented as applications and adaptations of existing methods. Comparison with the supplied versions of collatz_57, collatz_71, collatz_74 and the backward sieve in [15], together with the external papers of Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [7], Simons–de Weger [9] and Brox [10], did not reveal an equivalent eligible-window theorem. In particular, bounds on the number of classical descents do not bound the number of dimer circuits independently of their exponents.

The central argument is convincing. Multiplying the circuit equations gives Theorem B with the necessary denominator $x_{\min}-1$. At the first contracting prefix, the product identity gives $Q<i/\Lambda_i$, while the assumed floor ensures $Q>i$ and hence $Q_i<eQ$. Both endpoint heights therefore lie below $\log_2(6Q+1)$, justifying the two-directional prefix bound (5.2). The late-dip case legitimately uses the whole-cycle argument at its single eligible window. The finite continued-fraction exclusions and the analytic tails cover complementary ranges; the derivative arguments establish the unbounded parts rather than extrapolating finite numerical checks.

I inspected and reran the principal interval certificates. Theorem A reproduced both bases and their stated margins; Theorem C reproduced the claimed bases and the failing controls. The circuit-count certificates reproduced $M\le409$ and $M\le983$, including failure of the respective next-integer tests. For Proposition 1, the backward pruning remains necessary even after repeated traversals of a hypothetical cycle, and the added valuation restrictions and CRT lift preserve every possible dimer minimum. I reproduced the search through $2^{62}$ with backward replay enabled, the brute-force control at $2^{26}$, the second implementation at $2^{52}$, the small-start exclusion, and the dynamic-programming counts through depth $55$. The retained counts and checksums matched. I did not repeat the two-hour search through $2^{90}$ or the inherited large trough scans. The strongest floor thus relies on the inspected search implementation and retained full-run evidence, supported by these smaller reproductions; it was not independently reproduced in full during this review.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, new proof mechanism, inherited inputs, numerical certificates and unresolved hypothesis are identifiable in the main text. The argument is organized around the results, and detailed execution records are available separately. The following local clarifications would improve precision without changing the decision:

- State explicitly why the specialization of Rhin's bound in [9, Lemma 12] applies to the nonclosed prefixes. That source derives its constant using a coefficient-ratio estimate. Here (5.1), $R_i\ge3i$ and the stated floor give $S_i/R_i<\theta+1/(3Q\ln2)$, supplying the needed comparison.
- Qualify the Section 7 “expected number” calculations as the truncated model calculations actually performed, with $u,v\le6$, and retain the distinction between deterministic residue classes and a probabilistic heuristic. They do not estimate the entire infinite collection of words with a proved error bound.
- Make the final residue-class discussion in Section 7 consistent with [1] and the supplement: endpoint stretch-start conditions refine the modulus to $24\cdot2^{S_+}3^{R_-}$, and eligibility guarantees a trough only for sufficiently large representatives. Distinguish the least positive class member from the least representative satisfying all window inequalities and distinctness requirements.

Acceptance is for the conditional theorem and the supported restricted-cycle bounds, not for a solution of the closed-ended problem.
