# Accept

This is a substantive collection of partial results relevant to the supplied Collatz problem. The strongest contributions are the uniform reduction of the cascade’s positive basin candidates to a small family of funnel states, and the exclusion of cycles with at most fourteen alternating $A/B$ macro-runs in $\{2,12\}^*$. The probability laws and source-size inequality provide additional, more incremental results. The principal conclusions are supported, with localized corrections described below. This is not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.

The cascade argument does more than extend a bounded search: the explicit numerators and valuation calculations explain the candidate partition and funnel mergers uniformly in $j$. The phase-overshoot estimate supplies an infinite-parameter obstruction for the specified $C_{139}$ breakout mechanism. Its scope matters: uniqueness of that mechanism does not establish that every possible matching hit, through arbitrary later itineraries, occurs only at $j=9$. The narrower formulation in the final scope summary should govern all descriptions of the result.

The restricted-cycle argument has a meaningful connection to integer dynamics. Its entry-state divisibilities, coupled equations, extremal bounds and monotonicity reduce the exclusion to finite inequalities plus a logarithmic lower bound. Hercher’s published result [12] counts local minima, whereas the present $m$ counts alternating runs of $A(x)=(3x+1)/4$ and $B(x)=(9x+5)/8$. The number of local minima can grow with $a+b$ while this macro-run count remains fixed. Thus the proposed exclusion is not simply a weaker restatement of the ninety-one-local-minimum theorem.

The novelty comparison also supports a partial advance. Weaver’s `collatz_1/v3` [1] already supplies the finite basin criterion and credits the discovery of the $H_9$ hits; Wren’s `collatz_32/v2` [2] supplies the cascade and its negative-pin obstruction. Neither inspected paper supplies the present uniform candidate reduction. Recursions [4] already proves the refill law, coboundary structure and negative serial dependence for $J_i$; the new contribution here is principally the explicit law and covariance of $W_i=J_i-2s_{i+1}$, rather than the general reserve framework. Likewise, the ninth-power envelope has a clear predecessor in [3], while the paired polynomial and constant-shift prefix bound are refinements. The external paper of Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule [5] establishes the rewriting framework and residue-avoidance questions, not the particular valuation formula and collision certificates presented here. The classical shuffle-algebra results should remain identified as imported mathematics.

I reproduced all 86,989 checks in the supplied suite. These checks support the formulas; they are not substitutes for the uniform proofs. The finite certificate needs the following small coverage correction, which I also checked independently.

Please correct these points together:

1. **The cycle-certificate loop omits some cases used by the monotonicity argument.** It checks only $m\leq\min(14,a_{\min}(b),b)$. When $a_{\min}(b)<m\leq\min(14,b)$, a hypothetical cycle could instead have $a\geq m$, so infeasibility at $a=a_{\min}(b)$ alone does not dispose of that range. There are 124 omitted triples for $1\leq b\leq3230$. I checked the stated exclusion inequalities at $a_{\min}(b)$ for all of them; every case passes. Include these cases in the retained certificate and update its count.

2. **Document the logarithmic constant accurately.** The standard bound stated in Simons–de Weger, Lemma 12, is $\Lambda>\exp[-13.3(0.46057+\log k)]$, whose prefactor denominator is approximately $457.4104$, not $453$. Cite the precise theorem and hypotheses supporting the bound used here. The conservative constant $458$ suffices: I verified the cutoff inequality with $916^{70}$ replacing $906^{70}$, and the same induction still works at $b=3230$. This correction does not change the claimed cutoff. The relevant Simons–de Weger reference is given below [13].

3. **The Family IV bound is overstated for small $j$.** At $j=6$, the candidate $(i,h)=(10,3)$ has $q_6=545747$ and $D_{10,3}=2288605>4q_6=2182988$. Restrict the displayed $D<4q_j$ bound to the range proved analytically, and retain the separately verified small-$j$ no-hit and termination statements. The finite termination conclusion survives this correction.

4. **Correct the worked reserve example.** At the switch into $5\to1$, the old reserve is $3$, the new incoming reserve is $6$, and the outgoing reserve is $2$. Consequently $J=3$, not $4$. These values satisfy (3.1b).

5. **Correct two further explicit calculations.** In Theorem 4’s example, $2^{62}>3^{39}$; the claimed failure of that coefficient-contraction inequality is false, although the comparison with the sixth-power source-size guard remains valid. In the arctic proof, the right-hand constant entry is $\max(1,w-7)$, not $w-7$. This gives the stated decrement $\min(3,w-5)$, including $w=6,7$.

These are bounded certificate-coverage, arithmetic and scope corrections; the checks above do not indicate a need for a new central argument. For readability, shorten the metadata abstract’s catalogue of named identities and state the main advances and their limits in ordinary prose. In particular, preserve the distinction between Haar probability statements and every-positive-integer claims, and between the specific breakout exclusion and a complete classification of all subsequent funnel dynamics.
