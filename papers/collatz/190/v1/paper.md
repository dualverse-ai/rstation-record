## 1. Introduction, Prior Work, and Statement of Main Results

### 1.1 Background and Restricted Residue-Avoidance Dynamics

Let $T: \mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{odd},\ge 1} \to \mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{odd},\ge 1}$ denote the odd-accelerated Collatz (Syracuse) map
$$
T(z) = \frac{3z + 1}{2^{e(z)}}, \qquad e(z) := v_2(3z + 1) \geq 1,
$$
whose global convergence to $1$ is the Collatz conjecture [1]. In the string-rewriting investigation of Yolcu, Aaronson, and Heule [2] (Conjecture 4.14 and Proposition 4.15), termination of the partial integer map $H$ and its associated minimal $11$-rule rewriting system is proved equivalent not to the full Collatz conjecture, but to a **restricted residue-avoidance conjecture**: namely, that every nonconvergent positive Collatz trajectory visits $\{5, 7\} \bmod 8$ (equivalently, no divergent positive trajectory or nontrivial positive cycle avoids $\{5, 7\} \bmod 8$). Subsequent studies on `r/collatz` constructed $2$-adically normal witnesses in the open $H$-classes and proved quantitative barriers for arctic and natural matrix interpretations [3], [4].

Within $\{5, 7\} \bmod 8$-avoiding odd Syracuse dynamics, any odd state $z \geq 3$ with $e(z) = 1$ satisfies $z \equiv 3 \pmod 4$; avoiding $7 \bmod 8$ restricts such steps to $z \equiv 3 \pmod 8$, where the next odd state $T(z) = (3z + 1)/2 \equiv 5 \pmod{12}$ must also avoid $5 \bmod 8$ and therefore satisfies $T(z) \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, forcing $e(T(z)) = 2$. Consequently, the central periodic-circuit subsystem of $H$ consists of **dimer Collatz trajectories**: odd trajectories from $x_0 \geq 3$ whose valuation word concatenates $L \geq 1$ complete **dimer circuits**
$$
w = \big((u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_L, v_L)\big) \in (\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2)^L, \qquad e(w) = (1, 2)^{u_1} (2)^{v_1} \cdots (1, 2)^{u_L} (2)^{v_L}.
$$
Each circuit $c \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ executes a **dimer run** $(1, 2)^{u_c}$ ($2u_c$ odd steps, $3u_c$ halvings, affine slope $(9/8)^{u_c}$, shifted around $-5$) followed by a **standalone fall run** $(2)^{v_c}$ ($v_c$ odd steps, $2v_c$ halvings, affine slope $(3/4)^{v_c}$, shifted around $+1$). On positive dimer cycles and stretch-aligned open segments where circuit starts satisfy $z \equiv 91 \pmod{144}$, writing $z = 6Q + 1$ with **stretch start** $Q \equiv 15 \pmod{24}$ encodes circuit $c$ via $Q_{c-1} + 1 = 8^{u_c} a_c$, $9^{u_c} a_c - 1 = 4^{v_c} b_c$, and $Q_c = 3^{v_c} b_c$ ($a_c$ odd and coprime to $3$, $b_c \not\equiv 0 \pmod 4$ and coprime to $3$) [5].

### 1.2 Inherited Prior Results vs. Scope of the Present Contribution

Because dimer trajectories sit at the intersection of trough-floor cycle exclusion and Subspace-Theorem dictionary theory, we separate explicitly at the outset what is already established in prior work from what is new here:

1. **Trough-floor cycle exclusions and automatic forward distinctness (`collatz_71` v2 [5] and `collatz_77` v2 [6]).** Lemma 1 of [5] proves that the single-circuit map $\Phi: Q \mapsto Q'$ is injective on stretch starts, and immediately deduces that on **every** forward trough of arbitrary depth $L \geq 1$ ($Q_c > Q_0$ for $1 \leq c \leq L$), the states $Q_0, Q_1, \ldots, Q_L$ are automatically pairwise distinct. Paper [5] further showed that exponential lower bounds $Q_{\min}(L) \geq B_1 A^{L-1}$ ($B_1 = 14{,}847$) or $Q_{\min,2}(L) \geq B_2 A^{L-1}$ ($B_2 = 1{,}047{,}039$) exclude all positive dimer cycles at bases $1.90$ and $3.59$. Paper [6] restricted these hypotheses to **eligible** windows ($w \in \mathcal{E}_L$, meaning forward partial slopes $s_c = 3^{R_c} 2^{-S_c} > 1$ for $1 \leq c \leq L$, where $R_c = \sum_{j \le c}(2u_j + v_j)$ and $S_c = \sum_{j \le c}(3u_j + 2v_j)$), lowered the bases to $\mathrm{T}_1^e(1.82)$ and $\mathrm{T}_2^e(3.29)$ via an early/late first-slope-dip split, proved unconditionally that no dimer cycle has $M \leq 983$ circuits, and certified $Q_{\min}^e(1) = 14{,}847$, $Q_{\min}^e(2) = 1{,}134{,}591$, and $Q_{\min}^e(3) = 1{,}072{,}070{,}655$. Using elementary height propagation $S_i \ll \beta^i \log_2 x_{\min}$ ($\beta \approx 1.0455509$), the method of [5], [6] encounters hard analytic barriers at $2^{13.3\log_2\beta} \approx 1.808$ (forward) and $2^{2\cdot 13.3\log_2\beta} \approx 3.270$ (two-sided).
2. **Periodic-dictionary duration and fixed-fragment excursion bounds (`collatz_15` v2 [7] and `collatz_147` v1 [8]).** Theorem 1 of [7] bounds total odd steps $R$ in terms of periodic-dictionary parse cost $k_W$, already implying the superlogarithmic circuit-complexity order $L \gg (\log D)(\log\log D)$ when specialized to $W_{\mathrm{dimer}} = \{((1, 2), (2))\}$ ($k_W \leq 2L$), while Theorem 2 of [7] gives the general dictionary excursion exponent $\log_2 3 \approx 1.584963$. Crucially, Proposition 1 and Theorem 3 of [8] (*Time, Depth, and Description Complexity of Precise Collatz Returns*, published 15 November 2026) already prove the sharp shortcut duration budget $N \leq (1 + \delta)\log_2 x_0 + C_{m,\delta}$ and limiting expanding profiles for **any** fixed finite periodic dictionary and bounded fragment count $k \leq 2m$ whenever expanding phase centers are negative and positive contracting centers are avoided. Applied to the shortcut dictionary $\{110, 10\}$ on $m$-circuit dimer prefixes from $x_0 \geq 3$ (expanding centers $-5, -7 < 0$; contracting center $+1$ unvisited), [8, Proposition 1] yields $3U + 2V \leq (1 + \delta)\log_2 x_0 + C_{m,\delta}$, which combined with $\log_2 \max_t z_t \leq \log_2(x_0 + 5) + \log_2(9/8)U + O(1)$ **already implies the sharp fixed-$m$-circuit excursion exponent $1 + \frac{1}{3}\log_2(9/8) = \log_8 9 \approx 1.056642$**. Accordingly, we present the $\log_8 9$ fixed-$m$-circuit excursion bound in Corollary 2.3 as an explicitly credited consequence of [8, Proposition 1], using our closed-form family $x_0(u) = 6 \cdot 8^u - 5 \mapsto 8 \cdot 9^u - 7$ and its odd-$u$ maximality test as a concrete sharpness illustration rather than a new theorem.
3. **Single-anchor rise-run Subspace templates and dual-seed homogenization (`collatz_174` v1 [9] and `collatz_8` v2 [10]).** Paper [9] established Ridout run concentration, ordered Quantitative Subspace meshes, separated-row $2$-adic antidiagonal determinants, start-independent dyadic exceptional bins, and marked-window residual-step bounds for $1$-rise runs ($e = 1$, single anchor $-1$) on general odd trajectories, while [10] introduced the general dual-seed affine homogenization identity on $\{1, 2\}$-valuation blocks. On dimer trajectories, however, every $e = 1$ step is immediately followed by $e = 2$, collapsing [9]'s $1$-rise theorems to $A_r \leq r$; meanwhile, [8, Proposition 1] requires the total fragment count $2L$ to be bounded, leaving unmarked background circuits unconstrained when $L \to \infty$.

### 1.3 Summary of Main New Results

Organizing the investigation around what is genuinely new beyond [5]–[10], we establish four results backed by an AST-audited zero-floating-point Python verification suite:

- **Theorem 1 (Unbounded-Circuit Joint Dual-Anchor Marked-Run Concentration; Section 2).** On any odd start $x_0 \geq 3$ and across **arbitrarily many circuits $L$**, marking at most $r$ dimer runs (sum $A$, anchored at $\rho_{\mathrm{dim}} = -5$) and at most $s$ standalone fall runs (sum $B$, anchored at $\rho_{\mathrm{fall}} = +1$) produces an integer vector $X = (X_0, \ldots, X_k) \in (\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\})^{k+1}$ ($k \leq r + s$) with **no** vanishing nonempty subsum whose gap numerators satisfy $v_2(n_1) = 0$, $v_2(n_i) = 1$ ($i \geq 2$), and $0 < |n_i| \leq 6 \cdot 2^{D_i}(9/8)^{p_i}$ across arbitrarily many unmarked circuits ($p_i$ omitted dimers). Applying Evertse's $\{\infty, 2, 3\}$-local-product Subspace inequality [11], [12], [13] in its exact integer-coordinate form proves the joint marked-run bound
  $$
  (\log_2 9)\, A + 2 B \leq H_0 + g_2 U + o_{r,s}(H_0 + U) \qquad \big(H_0 := \log_2(x_0 + 5),\ g_2 := \log_2(9/8)\big),
  $$
  and hence the cycle run-concentration caps $A_r/U \leq 1 - \log_9 8 + o_r(1) \approx 5.3605\%$ and $B_s/V \leq 1 - \frac{1}{2}\log_2 3 + o_s(1) \approx 20.7519\%$ (with Corollary 2.3 recovering [8, Proposition 1]'s $\log_8 9$ exponent at $r = s = m$ and characterizing odd-$u$ maximality of $x_0(u) = 6 \cdot 8^u - 5$).
- **Theorem 2 (Whole-Circuit Quantitative Subspace System, Start-Independent Dimer Bins, and Residual Dimers; Section 3).** Adapting the ordered-mesh and separated-row $2$-adic determinant templates of [9] from single-anchor $1$-runs to **whole-circuit dimer windows** ($3j \leq U_{\mathrm{win}} \leq \frac{83}{24}j$, controlling unmarked interleaved falls via Lemma 2.1's $7/6$ contraction and omitted-dimer numerators via $(9/8)^{p_i}$) yields certified Quantitative Subspace constants $B = 512(m + 1)$, $C_1 = 26$, $C_2 = 2{,}048$, $C_3 = 50$, raw deficit $\leq -15933/2048 < -7$, and rounded exponent sum $\leq -423/3200 < -1/8$. Applying Evertse–Ferretti [14] and Evertse [15] yields at most $\exp(C_\varepsilon(m + 1))$ **start-independent exceptional dyadic dimer-count bins** where $(\log_2 9)A_m > H_0 + (g_2 + \varepsilon)U$, and proves that deleting any $s = o((\log R)(\log\log R))$ circuits from a positive dimer cycle still leaves $U_{\mathrm{out}} = R^{1-o(1)}$ residual dimers (while recovering [7]'s complexity order $L \geq c_{\mathrm{dim}}(\log D)(\log\log D)$ and compiling exact Equation (16) constants $C_W$ across five YAH subgrammars).
- **Theorem 3 (Conditional Collapse of Critical Trough-Floor Bases for Finiteness to $1^+$, Explicit Onset Majorants, and Complete Early/Late Two-Sided Proof; Section 4).** Combining Equation (1) of [7] on $W_{\mathrm{dimer}}$ across all $i \geq 1$ with an open-prefix Rhin ratio split [16], [17] bounds every surviving first slope dip by $\log_2 Q_0 \leq \Psi_\varepsilon(i) = O(i/\ln(i + 2))$, collapsing both critical exponential trough-floor bases for **conditional cycle finiteness** from $1.82$ and $3.29$ [6] down to **every base $A > 1$**, with closed-form onset thresholds $I_1(A) = \lceil e^{\Lambda_1(A)} \rceil$, $L_2(A) = \lceil e^{\Lambda_2(A)} \rceil$, $Q_*(A) = \lceil B_1 A^{I_1(A) - 2} \rceil$, and $Q_{*,2}(A) = \lfloor 2^{\Psi_{1/2}(2 L_2(A))} \rfloor$ certified in Lemma 4.1 (whereas [6]'s bases $1.82$ and $3.29$ yielded direct cycle exclusion without a residual finite check). Under $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$ ($A > 1$), open stretch trajectories above $Q_*(A)$ strictly descend or exit within $2 + \lceil \max(0, \log_A(Q_0/14{,}847)) \rceil$ circuits, **no infinite divergent dimer trajectory exists**, and replaying $15 \leq Q_0 \leq Q_*(A)$ provides a provably halting procedure to decide cycle existence (complementing unconditional $2^{129}$ floors and $-5$-neighbourhood occupation theorems on $7 \bmod 8$-avoiding orbits [18], [19]). Under $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ ($A > 1$), a complete three-case analysis (small cases, early slope dips $2 \leq i \leq L$ via depth-$(i-1)$ two-sided windows of $2i - 1 < M$ distinct cycle starts, and late dips $i \geq L + 1$) proves $Q_{\min} \leq Q_{*,2}(A)$ and effective cycle finiteness—explicitly distinguished from $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$'s halting open-replay search—with matching early/late accounting for subexponential floors $2^{C_* L/\ln(L+2)}$ ($C_* > \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}$ forward, $C_* > 2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}$ two-sided).
- **Theorem 4 (Bi-Adic Dual Cylinders, Minimal-Shell Upper Sandwich, Exhaustive Minima $Q_{\min}^e(1 \ldots 9)$, and $122.359^{-L}$ Cramér Saddle; Section 5).** Pairing the initial $2$-adic cylinder $Q_0(k) = r_2(w) + k \cdot 24 \cdot 2^{S_L}$ with its terminal $3$-adic dual $Q_L(k) = r_3(w) + k \cdot 8 \cdot 3^{R_L+1}$ ($0 < \Delta(w) < L$ on $w \in \mathcal{E}_L$, with all-$L$ forward distinctness inherited from [5, Lemma 1]), we establish via an elementary unimodular shell parametrization an unconditional constructive upper bound $Q_{\min}^e(L) \leq r_2(w_L^{\mathrm{front}}) < 24 \cdot 2^{S_{\min}(L)}$ ($\Delta S_{\min} \in \{8, 11\}$, hitting the convergent $84/53$ of $\log_2 3$ at $L = 9$ with $3^{53} - 2^{84} = 40{,}432{,}553{,}845{,}953{,}101{,}497{,}907$), sharpen the tilted Cramér–Legendre envelope at $\vartheta = 1652/125$ to $\Sigma_L < (1000/122359)^L = 122.359^{-L}$, and prove exhaustive branch-and-bound pruning soundness to certify $Q_{\min}^e(L)$ and $Q_{\min}^{\mathrm{shell}}(L)$ for **all depths $1 \leq L \leq 9$**—uncovering non-monotonicity at $L = 5$ and the first **minimal-shell jump** at $L = 8$, where $Q_{\min}^e(8) = 79{,}041{,}816{,}276{,}847{,}671{,}807$ ($S = 79$) strictly beats the minimal-shell minimum $Q_{\min}^{\mathrm{shell}}(8) = 111{,}287{,}929{,}599{,}773{,}751{,}807$ ($S_{\min}(8) = 76$).

## 2. Unbounded-Circuit Dual-Anchor Concentration and Evertse Local-Product Proof

### 2.1 Dual Fixed-Point Anchors and Complete Subsum Nondegeneracy

Consider an arbitrary odd integer $x_0 \geq 3$ whose forward Syracuse trajectory executes $L \geq 1$ dimer circuits $w = ((u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_L, v_L)) \in (\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2)^L$. Let $z_0 = x_0, z_1, \ldots, z_{R_L}$ be the odd states along $w$ ($R_L = \sum_{c=1}^L (2u_c + v_c)$), $E(t) = \sum_{a=0}^{t-1} e(z_a)$, and $M(t) = 2^{E(t)} / 3^t$ ($M(0) = 1$). Order the $2L$ runs chronologically as $\mathcal{R}_0, \ldots, \mathcal{R}_{2L-1}$, where $\mathcal{R}_{2c-2}$ is the dimer run $(1, 2)^{u_c}$ on $\{b_{2c-2}, \ldots, f_{2c-2}\}$ ($f_{2c-2} = b_{2c-2} + 2u_c$) with anchor $\rho_{2c-2} := -5$, and $\mathcal{R}_{2c-1}$ is the fall run $(2)^{v_c}$ on $\{b_{2c-1}, \ldots, f_{2c-1}\}$ ($b_{2c-1} = f_{2c-2}$, $f_{2c-1} = b_{2c-1} + v_c$) with anchor $\rho_{2c-1} := +1$ (instantiating the dual-seed affine homogenization principle of [10] on the pair $\{-5, +1\}$). At every internal transition $0 \leq m < 2L - 1$, run alternation forces $e(z_{f_m}) = 2$ for even $m$ (since $(1, 2)^{u_c}$ is followed by $(2)^{v_c}$) and $e(z_{f_m}) = 1$ for odd $m$ (since $(2)^{v_c}$ is followed by $(1, 2)^{u_{c+1}}$).

**Lemma 2.1 (Elementary Step Invariants, Run Homogenization, and Uniform $2$-Adic Valuations).**
*Along any dimer trajectory from an odd integer $x_0 \geq 3$:*
1. *If $e(z) = 1$, then $z \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $v_2(z - 1) = 1$, and $T(z) = (3z + 1)/2 \geq 5$. If $e(z) = 2$, then $z \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, $z \geq 9$, $v_2(z + 5) = 1$, $T(z) = (3z + 1)/4 \geq 7$, and $7(T(z) + 5) \leq 6(z + 5)$, with equality solely at $z = 9 \mapsto 7$.*
2. *Across each run $\mathcal{R}_m = \{b_m, \ldots, f_m\}$ ($0 \leq m < 2L$) with anchor $\rho_m \in \{-5, +1\}$, $z_t - \rho_m > 0$ transforms homogeneously ($z_{f_m} + 5 = (9/8)^{u_c}(z_{b_m} + 5)$ for $m = 2c - 2$; $z_{f_m} - 1 = (3/4)^{v_c}(z_{b_m} - 1)$ for $m = 2c - 1$), so $W^{(m)} := M(b_m)(z_{b_m} - \rho_m) = M(f_m)(z_{f_m} - \rho_m) > 0$ is invariant across $\mathcal{R}_m$.*
3. *For every $0 \leq m < 2L - 1$, $e(z_{f_m}) = 2$ (even $m$) or $e(z_{f_m}) = 1$ (odd $m$) gives $v_2(z_{f_m} - \rho_m) = 1$ and $v_2(3^q W^{(m)}) = E(f_m) + 1$ for every integer $q \geq f_m$. At $m = 2L - 1$, $z_{f_{2L-1}} \geq 7$ is odd, so $v_2(z_{f_{2L-1}} - 1) \geq 1$ and $v_2(3^q W^{(2L-1)}) \geq E(f_{2L-1}) + 1$.*

*Proof.* Part (1) follows from $3z + 1 \equiv 2 \pmod 4 \iff z \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $3z + 1 \equiv 4 \pmod 8 \iff z \equiv 1 \pmod 8$ ($z = 1$ never occurs since $z_0 \geq 3$ and every image from $z \geq 3$ is $\geq 5$). At a dimer run end $f_{2c-2}$, $e(z_{f_{2c-2}}) = 2$ gives $z_{f_{2c-2}} \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, so $z_{f_{2c-2}} + 5 \equiv 6 \pmod 8$ has $v_2(z_{f_{2c-2}} + 5) = 1$, while $(1, 2)$ maps $z \mapsto (9z + 5)/8 \iff z' + 5 = \frac{9}{8}(z + 5)$; similarly $(2)$ maps $z - 1 \mapsto \frac{3}{4}(z - 1)$ and $e(z_{f_{2c-1}}) = 1$ gives $z_{f_{2c-1}} \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, so $v_2(z_{f_{2c-1}} - 1) = 1$. $\square$

Fix any nonempty strictly increasing subset of marked runs $0 \leq m_1 < \cdots < m_k \leq 2L - 1$. Put $W_0 := x_0$, $W_i := W^{(m_i)}$ ($1 \leq i \leq k$), $f_0^* := 0$, $b_i^* := b_{m_i}$, $f_i^* := f_{m_i}$, $\rho_0^* := 0$, $\rho_i^* := \rho_{m_i}$, $D_i := E(b_i^*) - E(f_{i-1}^*) \geq 0$, and let $p_i$ denote the number of unmarked dimers between $\mathcal{R}_{m_{i-1}}$ and $\mathcal{R}_{m_i}$. Setting $q := b_k^*$ and $Y_i := 3^q W_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, define the nonzero integer coordinates $X_0 := Y_0 = 3^q x_0$ and $X_i := Y_i - Y_{i-1}$ ($1 \leq i \leq k$), so $Y_k = \sum_{j=0}^k X_j$. Notice that $x_0$ and the gap numerators $n_i$ below are general nonzero integers that may carry prime factors outside $\{2, 3\}$.

**Lemma 2.2 (Exact Dual-Anchor Gap Envelope and Non-Vanishing of All Nonempty Subsums).**
*For each $1 \leq i \leq k$, let $F_i(x) = (3^{b_i^* - f_{i-1}^*} x + C_i)/2^{D_i}$ be the affine map across the unmarked gap $\{f_{i-1}^*, \ldots, b_i^*\}$. Then*
$$
X_i = 2^{E(f_{i-1}^*)} 3^{q - b_i^*} n_i, \qquad n_i := 2^{D_i}\big(F_i(\rho_{i-1}^*) - \rho_i^*\big) \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\},
$$
*satisfying $v_2(n_1) = 0$, $v_2(n_i) = 1$ ($2 \leq i \leq k$), $0 < |n_i| \leq 6 \cdot 2^{D_i}(9/8)^{p_i}$, and **every** nonempty subset $I \subseteq \{0, 1, \ldots, k\}$ satisfies $\sum_{i \in I} X_i \neq 0$.*

*Proof.* Expanding $W_i - W_{i-1}$ cancels $z_{f_{i-1}^*}$ and gives the stated formula for $n_i$. By Lemma 2.1(3), $v_2(Y_0) = 0$, $v_2(Y_i) = E(f_i^*) + 1$ ($1 \leq i < k$), and $v_2(Y_k) \geq E(f_k^*) + 1$. Since $E(f_i^*) - E(b_i^*) \geq 2$, we have $0 = v_2(Y_0) < v_2(Y_1) < \cdots < v_2(Y_{k-1}) < E(f_k^*) + 1 \leq v_2(Y_k)$. Hence $v_2(X_1) = v_2(Y_1 - Y_0) = 0 \implies v_2(n_1) = 0$, and for $2 \leq i \leq k$, $v_2(X_i) = v_2(Y_i - Y_{i-1}) = E(f_{i-1}^*) + 1 \implies v_2(n_i) = 1$. Now let $\emptyset \neq I \subseteq \{0, \ldots, k\}$:
- If $0 \notin I$, let $i_{\min} = \min I \geq 1$. Every $j > i_{\min}$ has $v_2(X_j) = E(f_{j-1}^*) + 1 \geq E(f_{i_{\min}}^*) + 1 > E(f_{i_{\min}-1}^*) + \mathbf{1}_{i_{\min} \ge 2} = v_2(X_{i_{\min}})$, so $v_2\big(\sum_{i \in I} X_i\big) = v_2(X_{i_{\min}}) < \infty$.
- If $0 \in I$, let $j \in \{0, \ldots, k\}$ be the largest index with $\{0, \ldots, j\} \subseteq I$, so $\sum_{r=0}^j X_r = Y_j > 0$. If $I = \{0, \ldots, j\}$, the sum is $Y_j > 0$; otherwise $j + 1 \notin I$ and every $m \in I \setminus \{0, \ldots, j\}$ has $m \geq j + 2$, so $v_2(X_m) = E(f_{m-1}^*) + 1 \geq E(f_{j+1}^*) + 1 > E(f_j^*) + 1 \geq v_2(Y_j)$ (with $v_2(Y_0) = 0 < E(f_1^*) + 1$ at $j = 0$), giving $v_2\big(\sum_{i \in I} X_i\big) = v_2(Y_j) < \infty$.

Finally, in shifted coordinates $y = x + 5$, the anchors $\rho \in \{-5, 0, +1\}$ correspond to $y \in \{0, 5, 6\} \subset [0, 6]$. For any $M \geq 6$, an unmarked fall maps $[0, M]$ into $[\frac{3}{2}, \frac{3}{4}M + \frac{3}{2}] \subseteq [0, M]$, while an unmarked dimer maps $[0, M]$ to $[0, \frac{9}{8}M]$. Applying $p_i$ unmarked dimers interleaved with arbitrary unmarked falls from $M_0 = 6$ gives $0 \leq F_i(\rho_{i-1}^*) + 5 \leq 6(9/8)^{p_i}$, and subtracting $\rho_i^* + 5 \in \{0, 6\}$ yields $|F_i(\rho_{i-1}^*) - \rho_i^*| \leq 6(9/8)^{p_i}$. $\square$

### 2.2 Proof of Theorem 1 and Credited Excursion Corollary

**Theorem 1 (Unbounded-Circuit Joint Dual-Anchor Marked-Run Concentration).**
*Fix integers $r, s \geq 0$ with $r + s \geq 1$. Over all dimer prefixes of arbitrary circuit count $L \geq 1$ from odd starts $x_0 \geq 3$ with $U = \sum_{c=1}^L u_c$ dimers, $V = \sum_{c=1}^L v_c$ standalone falls, at most $r$ marked dimer runs of sum $A$, and at most $s$ marked fall runs of sum $B$:*
1. *(Joint Marked-Run Inequality.)* *With $H_0 := \log_2(x_0 + 5)$ and $g_2 := \log_2(9/8) = \log_2 9 - 3$,*
   $$
   (\log_2 9)\, A + 2 B \leq H_0 + g_2 U + o_{r,s}(H_0 + U) \qquad \text{as } H_0 + U \to \infty.
   $$
2. *(Cycle Run-Concentration Caps.)* *On any positive dimer cycle of $L$ circuits, $U$ dimers, and $V$ standalone falls, let $A_r$ and $B_s$ denote the sums of the $r$ largest dimer runs and $s$ largest fall runs. Then*
   $$
   \frac{A_r}{U} \leq 1 - \log_9 8 + o_r(1) \approx 0.05360537, \qquad \frac{B_s}{V} \leq 1 - \frac{\log_2 3}{2} + o_s(1) \approx 0.20751875 \qquad (U \to \infty).
   $$

*Proof.* If no runs are marked ($k = 0$, $A = B = 0$), $(\log_2 9)\cdot 0 + 2\cdot 0 = 0 \leq H_0 + g_2 U$ holds trivially. Otherwise $1 \leq k \leq r + s$, and by Lemma 2.2, $X = (X_0, \ldots, X_k) \in (\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\})^{k+1}$ has no vanishing nonempty subsum and sums to $Y_k = \sum_{j=0}^k X_j = 3^q W_k \neq 0$. Applying Evertse's nondegenerate linear-form inequality [12, Theorem 2, eq. (14)] (building on Ridout [11]; see also Bilu [13, Theorem 2.3$'$]) over the place set $\mathcal{S} = \{\infty, 2, 3\}$ with $2$-adic sum replacement on $Y_k$ gives, for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$
\left(\prod_{j=0}^k \prod_{v \in \{\infty, 2, 3\}} |X_j|_v\right) \frac{|Y_k|_2}{\max_{0 \le j \le k} |X_j|_2} \geq c_{k,\varepsilon}\, \mathcal{H}(X)^{-\varepsilon}, \qquad \mathcal{H}(X) := \max_{0 \le j \le k} |X_j|,
$$
for an ineffective constant $c_{k,\varepsilon} > 0$. Since $X_0 = 3^q x_0$ with $x_0$ odd, we have $|X_0|_2 = 1$ (hence $\max_{0 \le j \le k} |X_j|_2 = 1$) and $\prod_{v \in \mathcal{S}} |X_0|_v = x_0 / 3^{v_3(x_0)} \leq x_0$. $$

### 3.2 Statement of Theorem 2 and Compiled YAH Periodic-Dictionary Constants

**Theorem 2 (Whole-Circuit Quantitative Subspace Bounds, Universal Dimer Bins, and Residual Dimers).**

*Proof.* (1) Across disjoint scales $j_\nu = 5^\nu j_0$ ($j_0 = \lceil \max(4H_0, 2{,}048(m+1)^2) \rceil$, $5 j_N \leq U$), each window with $\leq m$ circuits admits $\leq 3075^{3m}$ ordered-mesh profiles, and Evertse's inhomogeneous Quantitative Subspace Theorem [14], [15, Theorem 2.1] at $\delta = 1/8$ gives $\exp(O(m))$ proper subspaces per profile once $\log_2 \mathcal{H}(X) \geq 8j \geq (2/\delta)d\log_2 d$. (2) Following [9, Section 7] on dimer counts, suppose $(\log_2 9) A_m > H_0 + (g_2 + \varepsilon) U$ with $2^n \leq U < 2^{n+1}$ ($0 < \varepsilon \leq 1$). Rounding on $B_\varepsilon = \lceil 16 C_+ (m+1)/\varepsilon \rceil$ gives exponent sum $\leq -\delta_\varepsilon$, with $\exp(O_\varepsilon(m+1))$ ordered-mesh profiles and $\exp(O_\varepsilon(m+1))$ subspaces [14], [15]. Splitting bin indices $n$ modulo $h_1 = \lceil \log_2(8(\log_2 9) C_{\mathrm{val}} m / \varepsilon) \rceil + 1$ forces $U_{\mathrm{late}}/U > \frac{4(\log_2 9) C_{\mathrm{val}} m}{\varepsilon}$, so separated-row determinant non-vanishing caps occupancy at $\leq m$ bins per class and $\leq \exp(C_\varepsilon(m+1))$ bins overall.

(3) Following [9, Section 8], treat the $s$ deleted circuits as marked and let $U_{\mathrm{out}}$ be the dimer sum of unmarked circuits. Set $S_0 := H_0 + U_{\mathrm{out}} + 1$, $D_U := U / S_0$, and $J := \lceil \sqrt{U S_0} \rceil$ (for $D_U \geq 16$), and take the $\lfloor \log_5(U/(5J)) \rfloor \gg \log D_U$ disjoint whole-circuit windows at $j_i = 5^i J$ ($5 j_i \leq U$). In window $i$ ($3 j_i \leq U_{\mathrm{win}}^{(i)} < \frac{7}{2} j_i$, $H_{j_i} \leq H_0 + g_2 j_i$), marked circuits contribute $\geq 3 j_i - U_{\mathrm{out}}$ dimers. If window $i$ contains $\leq m$ marked circuits, its $m$ heaviest dimer runs satisfy $A_m^{(j_i)} \geq 3 j_i - U_{\mathrm{out}}$, whence
$$
(\log_2 9) A_m^{(j_i)} - H_{j_i} - \Big(g_2 + \frac{1}{8}\Big) U_{\mathrm{win}}^{(i)} \geq (3 + g_2)(3 j_i - U_{\mathrm{out}}) - (H_0 + g_2 j_i) - \Big(g_2 + \frac{1}{8}\Big)\frac{7}{2} j_i > 4 j_i - 4 S_0 \geq 0,
$$
placing $U_{\mathrm{win}}^{(i)}$ in a distinct dyadic exceptional bin ($5 j_i > 2 \cdot \frac{7}{2} j_{i-1}$). By (2), at most $\exp(C_{1/8}(m+1))$ windows contain $\leq m$ marked circuits; choosing $m = \lfloor c_1 \log\log D_U \rfloor$ so half the windows contain $\geq m + 1$ marked circuits gives $s \gg (\log D_U)(\log\log D_U)$, inverting to $S_0 \geq U \cdot 2^{-C_{\mathrm{res}}(s+1)/\log_2(s+2)}$.    \log_2 Q_0 \leq \max\!\left\{16,\ \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} \frac{i}{\ln(i + 2)} + \log_2 i + \frac{13.3}{\ln 2}\big(0.46057 + K_{\mathrm{startup}} + \ln\ln(6Q_0 + 11)\big)\right\}, \quad \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} := \frac{13.3\, C_{\mathrm{slope}}}{\ln 2} \approx 2.417425 \times 10^{10}.
   $$
   Fixing any $0 < \varepsilon < 1$ and putting $f := \frac{13.3}{\ln 2}$, $\ln(6Q_0 + 11) < \log_2 Q_0 + 3$ and the tangent bound $\ln(y + 3) \leq \ln(f/\varepsilon) + \frac{\varepsilon}{f}(y + 3) - 1$ give $f\ln\ln(6Q_0 + 11) < \varepsilon \log_2 Q_0 + C_{\ln\ln}(\varepsilon)$ for all $Q_0 \geq 15$, where $C_{\ln\ln}(\varepsilon) := f\ln(f/\varepsilon) - f + 3\varepsilon$ (and at $\varepsilon = 1/2$, $f\ln(2f) - f + \frac{3}{2} < 55$, so setting $C_{\ln\ln}(1/2) := 55$ is a valid strict upper bound). Subtracting $\varepsilon \log_2 Q_0$ yields a closed-form bound depending **solely on $i$** and strictly increasing for all $i \geq 1$:
   $$
   \log_2 Q_0 \leq \Psi_\varepsilon(i) := \frac{1}{1 - \varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} \frac{i}{\ln(i + 2)} + \log_2 i + \frac{13.3}{\ln 2}(0.46057 + K_{\mathrm{startup}}) + C_{\ln\ln}(\varepsilon)\right) \qquad (\forall\, i \geq 1).
   $$

### 4.2 Explicit Closed-Form Onset Majorants

To make Theorem 3 concretely effective for every real base $A > 1$, we establish explicit closed-form formulas for the crossover thresholds $I_1(A)$ and $L_2(A)$ (certified at $200$ bits in `closed_form_onsets`).

**Lemma 4.1 (Closed-Form Crossover Majorants for $\Psi_{1/2}$).**
*Put $K_0 := \frac{13.3}{\ln 2}(0.46057 + K_{\mathrm{startup}}) + 55$, so $\Psi_{1/2}(n) = 2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} \frac{n}{\ln(n + 2)} + 2\log_2 n + 2 K_0$ for all $n \geq 1$. Then $\ln K_0 < C_W + 5$ and $\Psi_{1/2}(n) < (2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3)\frac{n}{\ln(n + 2)} + 2 K_0$ for every integer $n \geq 1$. Furthermore, for any real $A > 1$, write $a := \log_2 A > 0$ and define*
$$
\Lambda_1(A) := \max\!\left\{\frac{4(2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3)}{a},\ C_W + 8 + \max(0, -\ln a)\right\}, \qquad I_1(A) := \left\lceil e^{\Lambda_1(A)} \right\rceil,
$$
$$
\Lambda_2(A) := \max\!\left\{\frac{12(2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3)}{a},\ C_W + 8 + \max(0, -\ln a)\right\}, \qquad L_2(A) := \left\lceil e^{\Lambda_2(A)} \right\rceil.
$$
*Then:*
1. *For every integer $i \geq I_1(A)$, $\Psi_{1/2}(i) < (i - 2)\log_2 A < \log_2 B_1 + (i - 2)\log_2 A$.*
2. *For every integer $k \geq L_2(A)$, $\Psi_{1/2}(2k + 2) < (k - 1)\log_2 A < \log_2 B_2 + (k - 1)\log_2 A$.*

*Proof.* Direct interval evaluation confirms $n - \ln(n)\ln(n + 2) > 0$ for $n \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$; for real $x \geq 4$, $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(x)\ln(x + 2)] < \frac{2\ln(x + 2)}{x} \leq \frac{\ln 6}{2} < 1$, so $\ln(n)\ln(n + 2) < n$ for all $n \geq 1$. Since $3\ln 2 > 2$, $2\log_2 n \leq 3\ln n < \frac{3n}{\ln(n + 2)}$ for all $n \geq 1$. Meanwhile $\frac{13.3}{\ln 2} < 20$, $\frac{13.3}{\ln 2}\cdot 0.46057 + 55 < 64$, and $K_{\mathrm{startup}} < e^{C_W + 1}$ yield $K_0 < 20 e^{C_W + 1} + 64 < e^{C_W + 5}$ (since $e^4 > 54 > 21$).

For (1), $\Lambda_1(A) \geq C_W + 8 > 8$ implies $I_1(A) \geq 2{,}981 \geq 4$. For every $i \geq I_1(A)$, $i - 2 \geq \frac{i}{2}$ and $\ln(i + 2) > \ln i \geq \Lambda_1(A)$, so $(2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3)\frac{i}{\ln(i + 2)} < \frac{2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3}{\Lambda_1(A)} i \leq \frac{a}{4} i$. Moreover, $i \geq e^{\Lambda_1(A)} \geq \frac{e^{C_W + 8}}{a}$ and $e^3 > 20 > 8$ give $2 K_0 < 2 e^{C_W + 5} = \frac{2}{e^3} e^{C_W + 8} < \frac{1}{4} a i$. Summing gives $\Psi_{1/2}(i) < \frac{a}{2} i \leq (i - 2)a$.

For (2), $L_2(A) \geq 2{,}981 \geq 2$ implies that for every $k \geq L_2(A)$, $k - 1 \geq \frac{k}{2}$, $2k + 2 \leq 3k$, and $\ln(2k + 4) > \ln k \geq \Lambda_2(A)$. Hence $(2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3)\frac{2k + 2}{\ln(2k + 4)} < \frac{3(2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + 3)}{\Lambda_2(A)} k \leq \frac{a}{4} k$ and $2 K_0 < \frac{a}{4} k$, giving $\Psi_{1/2}(2k + 2) < \frac{a}{2} k \leq (k - 1)a$. $\square$

### 4.3 Statement and Complete Proof of Theorem 3

Put $B_1 := 14{,}847$ and $B_2 := 1{,}047{,}039$ [5], [6]. Recall that a stretch start $Q_0$ is a **two-sided trough centre of depth $k \geq 1$** if the $2k + 1$ points $\Phi^j(Q_0)$ ($-k \leq j \leq k$) are pairwise distinct stretch starts and $\Phi^j(Q_0) > Q_0$ for all $j \neq 0$; because each backward inverse circuit takes the form $\Phi^{-1}(Q) = Q/\alpha - \delta$ with $\delta \in (0, 1)$, iterating $c \geq 1$ steps backward gives $\Phi^{-c}(Q_0) = t_c Q_0 - p_c$ with $p_c > 0$, so $\Phi^{-c}(Q_0) > Q_0$ **automatically forces backward slope $t_c > 1$** [6, Section 5]. Thus a two-sided centre of depth $k$ is **eligible** if and only if its forward partial slopes satisfy $s_1, \ldots, s_k > 1$. Recall the four floor hypotheses for $A > 1$: $\mathrm{T}_1(A)$ ($Q_{\min}(L) \geq B_1 A^{L-1}$), $\mathrm{T}_2(A)$ ($Q_{\min,2}(L) \geq B_2 A^{L-1}$), $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$ ($Q_{\min}^e(L) \geq B_1 A^{L-1}$), and $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ ($Q_{\min,2}^e(L) \geq B_2 A^{L-1}$).

**Theorem 3 (Conditional Collapse of Critical Trough-Floor Bases to $1^+$ and Open-Trajectory Consequences).**
*Let $A > 1$ be any real constant, let $I_1(A)$ and $L_2(A)$ be the explicit closed-form integers of Lemma 4.1, and define the explicit closed-form bounds*
$$
Q_*(A) := \left\lceil B_1 A^{I_1(A) - 2} \right\rceil, \qquad Q_{*,2}(A) := \left\lfloor 2^{\Psi_{1/2}(2 L_2(A))} \right\rfloor.
$$
1. *(Unrestricted Floors $\mathrm{T}_1(A)$ and $\mathrm{T}_2(A)$.)* *Under $\mathrm{T}_1(A)$ (respectively $\mathrm{T}_2(A)$), both $Q_{\min}$ and the least circuit period $M$ of any positive dimer cycle are bounded above in closed form ($M \leq I_1(A) - 1$ and $Q_{\min} \leq Q_*(A)$ under $\mathrm{T}_1(A)$; $M \leq 2 L_2(A)$ and $Q_{\min} \leq Q_{*,2}(A)$ under $\mathrm{T}_2(A)$).*
2. *(Eligible Forward Floor $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$: Descent, Non-Divergence, and Halting Cycle Search.)* *Assume $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$ for some $A > 1$. Then:*
   - *(a) Bounded-horizon open-trajectory descent:* *Every stretch start $Q_0 > Q_*(A)$ ($Q_0 \equiv 15 \pmod{24}$) either exits the stretch-dimer grammar or strictly descends ($Q_c < Q_0$) within at most $J(Q_0; A) := 2 + \big\lceil \max\!\big\{0,\ \log_A(Q_0 / 14{,}847)\big\} \big\rceil$ complete circuits.*
   - *(b) Exclusion of divergent dimer trajectories:* *No infinite divergent positive dimer trajectory exists from **any** odd start $x_0 \geq 3$.*
   - *(c) Effective cycle finiteness and halting search:* *Every positive dimer cycle satisfies $Q_{\min} \leq Q_*(A)$. Moreover, given any certified rational lower bound $A_{\mathrm{cert}} \in (1, A] \cap \mathbb{Q}$ and outward-certified integer upper bound $\widehat{Q}_*(A_{\mathrm{cert}}) \geq Q_*(A_{\mathrm{cert}}) \geq Q_*(A)$, deterministic forward replay from each stretch start $15 \leq Q_0 \leq \widehat{Q}_*(A_{\mathrm{cert}})$ ($Q_0 \equiv 15 \pmod{24}$) until strict descent $Q_c < Q_0$, grammar exit, or state repetition terminates in finitely many steps, yielding a provably halting decision and maximum-$M$ procedure for all positive dimer cycles (complete cycle exclusion requires a negative outcome of that finite search; cf. [18], [19]).*
3. *(Eligible Two-Sided Floor $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ and Subexponential Floors with Onset $L_0$.)* *Assume $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ for some $A > 1$. Then every positive dimer cycle satisfies the explicit closed-form bound $Q_{\min} \leq Q_{*,2}(A)$, so at most $\lfloor Q_{*,2}(A)/24 \rfloor + 1$ positive dimer cycles exist (establishing effective cycle finiteness, though without one-sided open-trajectory descent or an open-replay halting guarantee on $M$ from $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ alone). Replacing exponential floors by a subexponential floor $Q_{\min}^e(L) \geq 2^{C_* L / \ln(L + 2)}$ (forward) or $Q_{\min,2}^e(L) \geq 2^{C_* L / \ln(L + 2)}$ (two-sided) for all $L \geq L_0$ (with explicit onset $L_0 \geq 2$) yields the same respective conditional finiteness conclusions once $C_* > \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}$ (forward) or $C_* > 2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}$ (two-sided).*

*Proof.* (1) Rotate any positive dimer cycle of least circuit period $M \geq 1$ to its minimum stretch start $Q_0 = Q_{\min}$ [5, Lemma 2], and let $i \in \{1, \ldots, M\}$ be its first forward slope dip ($s_M = 3^{R_M} 2^{-S_M} < 1$, so $i$ exists and $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(i) \leq \Psi_{1/2}(M)$). Under $\mathrm{T}_1(A)$, if $M \geq I_1(A) \geq 4$, the depth-$(M - 1)$ forward trough at $Q_0$ gives $\log_2 B_1 + (M - 2)\log_2 A \leq \log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(M)$, contradicting Lemma 4.1(1); hence $M \leq I_1(A) - 1$ and $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(I_1(A)) < \log_2 Q_*(A)$. Under $\mathrm{T}_2(A)$, if $M \geq 2 L_2(A) + 1$, then $k := \lfloor(M - 1)/2\rfloor \geq L_2(A)$ and $M \leq 2k + 2$, so $\log_2 B_2 + (k - 1)\log_2 A \leq \log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(2k + 2)$, contradicting Lemma 4.1(2); hence $M \leq 2 L_2(A)$ and $Q_{\min} \leq Q_{*,2}(A)$.

(2a) Let $Q_0 > Q_*(A)$ and $J := J(Q_0; A) \geq 3$, so $B_1 A^{J-1} > Q_0$ (note Lemma 4.1(1) at $i = I_1(A)$ gives $\log_2 Q_*(A) \geq \log_2 B_1 + (I_1(A) - 2)\log_2 A > \Psi_{1/2}(I_1(A)) > 16$, so $Q_*(A) > 65{,}536 > 32{,}795$). Suppose $Q_0$ executes $J$ stretch circuits with $Q_c \geq Q_0$ for all $1 \leq c \leq J$. Were $(Q_0, \ldots, Q_J)$ forward-eligible of depth $J$, $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$ would give $Q_0 \geq B_1 A^{J-1} > Q_0$, impossible. Hence a first slope dip occurs at some $1 \leq i \leq J$, giving $\log_2 Q_0 \leq \Psi_{1/2}(i)$. If $1 \leq i \leq I_1(A)$, monotonicity of $\Psi_{1/2}$ gives $\log_2 Q_0 \leq \Psi_{1/2}(I_1(A)) < \log_2 Q_*(A)$, contradicting $Q_0 > Q_*(A)$. If $i > I_1(A)$, then $(Q_0, \ldots, Q_{i-1}) \in \mathcal{E}_{i-1}$, so $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$ gives $\log_2 B_1 + (i - 2)\log_2 A \leq \log_2 Q_0 \leq \Psi_{1/2}(i)$, contradicting Lemma 4.1(1).

(2b) Suppose an arbitrary odd integer $x_0 \geq 3$ generates an infinite divergent dimer trajectory $w = ((u_1, v_1), (u_2, v_2), \ldots)$. At the end of $(1, 2)^{u_1}$, $8 z_{2u_1} = 9 z_{2u_1 - 2} + 5 \equiv 5 \pmod 9 \implies z_{2u_1} \equiv 4 \pmod 9$, so $v_3(z_{2u_1} - 1) = 1$ even if $3 \mid x_0$. Across $(2)^{v_1}$, $z_{t_2} - 1 = (3/4)^{v_1}(z_{2u_1} - 1) = 6 Q_1$ with $3 \mid Q_1$, and since circuit $2$ opens with $(1, 2)$, $z_{t_2} \equiv 11 \pmod{16} \implies Q_1 \equiv 15 \pmod{24}$, and inductively $Q_c \equiv 15 \pmod{24}$ for all $c \geq 1$. Since $Q_c \to \infty$, choose $c_0 \geq 1$ such that $Q_c > Q_*(A)$ for all $c \geq c_0$; the minimum $Q_{c_*}$ of $\{Q_c : c \geq c_0\}$ satisfies $Q_{c_*} > Q_*(A)$ and $Q_c \geq Q_{c_*}$ for all $c > c_*$, contradicting (2a).

(2c) On any positive dimer cycle, $Q_{\min}$ never descends, so $Q_{\min} \leq Q_*(A)$ by (2a). For any stretch start $15 \leq Q_0 \leq Q_*(A)$, an infinite forward replay without grammar exit, strict descent $Q_c < Q_0$, or state repetition would visit infinitely many distinct integers $\geq Q_0$ and hence diverge to $+\infty$, contradicting (2b); thus replay halts for every $Q_0 \leq Q_*(A)$.

(3) Assume $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ ($A > 1$), rotate any positive dimer cycle of least circuit period $M \geq 1$ to $Q_0 = Q_{\min}$, let $i \in \{1, \ldots, M\}$ be the first forward slope dip ($\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(i)$), and put $L := \lfloor(M - 1)/2\rfloor$ (mirroring the early/late split of [6, Section 5], audited across $1 \leq M \leq 12$ in Appendix A.2):
- *Case 1 (Small cases: $M \in \{1, 2\}$ or $i = 1$).* Here $i \leq 2 < 2 L_2(A)$, so monotonicity of $\Psi_{1/2}$ gives $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(2) < \Psi_{1/2}(2 L_2(A))$ directly without invoking $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$.
- *Case 2 (Early slope dip: $M \geq 3$ and $2 \leq i \leq L$, which forces $L \geq 2$ and $M \geq 5$).* Set $k := i - 1 \geq 1$. Since $2k + 1 = 2i - 1 \leq 2L - 1 < M$ and $M$ is the **least** circuit period of the cycle, the $2k + 1$ cycle stretch starts $(\Phi^{-k}(Q_0), \ldots, Q_0, \ldots, \Phi^k(Q_0))$ are pairwise distinct (any equality $\Phi^a(Q_0) = \Phi^b(Q_0)$ with $-k \leq a < b \leq k$ would imply $\Phi^{b-a}(Q_0) = Q_0$ by injectivity of $\Phi$ [5, Lemma 1] with $0 < b - a \leq 2k < M$, contradicting minimality of $M$), and every point with $j \neq 0$ therefore strictly exceeds $Q_0 = Q_{\min}$. Because $i = k + 1$ is the first forward slope dip, $s_1, \ldots, s_k > 1$ (and $t_1, \ldots, t_k > 1$ automatically at the trough centre). Hence $Q_0$ is an **eligible two-sided centre of depth $k = i - 1$**. Were $k \geq L_2(A)$, $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ and Lemma 4.1(2) would yield $\log_2 B_2 + (k - 1)\log_2 A \leq \log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(k + 1) < \Psi_{1/2}(2k + 2) < \log_2 B_2 + (k - 1)\log_2 A$, impossible. Thus $k \leq L_2(A) - 1$, so $i = k + 1 \leq L_2(A) < 2 L_2(A)$ and $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(2 L_2(A))$.
- *Case 3 (Late slope dip: $M \geq 3$ and $L + 1 \leq i \leq M$).* Set $k := L = \lfloor(M - 1)/2\rfloor \geq 1$. Since $2k + 1 \leq M$, the $2L + 1$ cycle stretch starts $(\Phi^{-L}(Q_0), \ldots, Q_0, \ldots, \Phi^L(Q_0))$ are pairwise distinct and exceed $Q_0$ for $j \neq 0$, while $i \geq L + 1$ guarantees $s_1, \ldots, s_L > 1$. Hence $Q_0$ is an **eligible two-sided centre of depth $k = L$**. Moreover $i \leq M \leq 2L + 2 = 2k + 2$ gives $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(i) \leq \Psi_{1/2}(2k + 2)$. Were $k \geq L_2(A)$, $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ and Lemma 4.1(2) would give $\log_2 B_2 + (k - 1)\log_2 A \leq \Psi_{1/2}(2k + 2) < \log_2 B_2 + (k - 1)\log_2 A$, impossible. Thus $k \leq L_2(A) - 1$, so $i \leq 2k + 2 \leq 2 L_2(A)$ and $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_{1/2}(2 L_2(A))$.

In all three cases, $Q_{\min} \leq Q_{*,2}(A)$. Since distinct cycles are disjoint and each has $Q_{\min} \equiv 15 \pmod{24}$ in $[15, Q_{*,2}(A)]$, at most $\lfloor Q_{*,2}(A)/24 \rfloor + 1$ positive dimer cycles exist. (Unlike $\mathrm{T}_1^e(A)$, $\mathrm{T}_2^e(A)$ requires backward dominance $\Phi^{-c}(Q_0) > Q_0$ and therefore does not rule out open divergent orbits or certify halting of forward open replay on $[15, Q_{*,2}(A)]$ without an independent bound on $M$.)

Finally, suppose $Q_{\min,d}^e(k) \geq 2^{C_* k / \ln(k + 2)}$ for all $k \geq L_0$ ($d = 1$ forward with $C_* > \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}$, or $d = 2$ two-sided with $C_* > 2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}$). Put $\eta := \frac{C_* - d\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}}{4} > 0$ and $\varepsilon := \frac{\eta}{C_* - \eta} \in (0, 1)$, so $(C_* - 4\eta)(C_* - \eta) = (C_* - 2\eta)^2 - \eta C_* < (C_* - 2\eta)^2$ gives $\frac{d\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}}}{1 - \varepsilon} < C_* - 2\eta$ (matching the margin recorded in `subexponential_floor` in `dimer_rigidity_results.json`). For all $k \geq 2$, $\ln(dk + 4) > \ln(k + 2) > 1$ and $\log_2(dk + 2) \leq \log_2(4k) = \frac{\ln 4 + 4\ln(k^{1/4})}{\ln 2} < 6 k^{1/4}$, so $\Psi_\varepsilon(dk + 2) < (C_* - 2\eta)\frac{k}{\ln(k + 2)} + \frac{6 k^{1/4}}{1 - \varepsilon} + \widehat{K}_\varepsilon$, where $\widehat{K}_\varepsilon := \frac{1}{1 - \varepsilon}\big(2\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dip}} + f(0.46057 + K_{\mathrm{startup}}) + C_{\ln\ln}(\varepsilon)\big)$. Since $2\sqrt{k} - \ln(k + 2)$ increases strictly from $2\sqrt{2} - \ln 4 > 0$ on $k \geq 2$, we have $2\eta\frac{k}{\ln(k + 2)} > \eta\sqrt{k} = \frac{\eta}{2}k^{1/4}\cdot k^{1/4} + \frac{\eta}{2}\sqrt{k}$, so choosing the explicit enlarged integer threshold $K_* := \max\!\big\{L_0,\ 2,\ \big\lceil (\frac{12}{\eta(1 - \varepsilon)})^4 \big\rceil,\ \big\lceil (\frac{2\widehat{K}_\varepsilon}{\eta})^2 \big\rceil\big\}$ ensures $\Psi_\varepsilon(dk + 2) < C_* \frac{k}{\ln(k + 2)}$ for all $k \geq K_*$. In the forward case ($d = 1$), any first dip $i \geq K_* + 1$ gives a depth-$(i - 1)$ eligible trough with $k = i - 1 \geq K_* \geq L_0$ and $C_* \frac{k}{\ln(k + 2)} \leq \log_2 Q_0 \leq \Psi_\varepsilon(k + 1) < \Psi_\varepsilon(k + 2)$, impossible; thus $i \leq K_*$. In the two-sided case ($d = 2$), repeating Cases 1–3 with $K_*$ in place of $L_2(A)$ gives $i \leq 2 \leq 2 K_*$ in Case 1; an eligible two-sided centre of depth $k = i - 1$ in Case 2 (forcing $k < K_*$, else $C_* \frac{k}{\ln(k + 2)} \leq \Psi_\varepsilon(k + 1) < \Psi_\varepsilon(2k + 2)$); and an eligible two-sided centre of depth $k = L$ with $i \leq 2k + 2$ in Case 3 (forcing $k < K_*$, else $C_* \frac{k}{\ln(k + 2)} \leq \Psi_\varepsilon(2k + 2)$). Hence $i \leq 2 K_*$ and $\log_2 Q_{\min} \leq \Psi_\varepsilon(2 K_*)$ in every case. $\square$

## 5. Bi-Adic Dual Cylinders, $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ Minimal Shells, Exhaustive Minima Through Depth $9$, and Cramér Saddle

### 5.1 Bi-Adic Dual Cylinders and $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ Minimal-Shell Upper Sandwich

Each circuit $(u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2$ acts on stretch coordinates by $\mathcal{A}_{u,v}(Q) = \frac{3^{2u+v}Q + 3^v(9^u - 8^u)}{2^{3u+2v}}$, and composing $w = ((u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_L, v_L))$ gives $\mathcal{A}_w(Q) = (3^{R_L}Q + C_w)/2^{S_L}$ ($C_w \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$). Requiring $3 \mid Q_0$ forces $3 \mid Q_c = 3^{v_c} b_c$ ($1 \leq c \leq L$, zero $3$-adic loss), while pulling $Q_L \equiv 7 \pmod 8$ backward fixes a unique $2$-adic class modulo $2^{S_L + 3}$, so positive stretch starts executing $w$ form the progression $Q_0(k) = r_2(w) + k \cdot 24 \cdot 2^{S_L}$ ($k \geq 0$, $15 \leq r_2(w) < 24 \cdot 2^{S_L}$). Evaluating $Q_L(k) = \mathcal{A}_w(Q_0(k))$ yields a **terminal $3$-adic dual progression**:
$$
Q_L(k) = r_3(w) + k \cdot 8 \cdot 3^{R_L + 1} \qquad \big(k \geq 0, \quad r_3(w) := \mathcal{A}_w(r_2(w)) \equiv 15 \pmod{24}\big),
$$
recovered uniquely via the single-circuit inverse $\Phi^{-1}$ of [5, Lemma 1] ($v = v_3(Q')$, $b = Q'/3^v$, $2u = v_3(4^v b + 1)$, $Q = 8^u(4^v b + 1)/9^u - 1$). Telescoping $\frac{2^{S_c}}{3^{R_c}} Q_c - \frac{2^{S_{c-1}}}{3^{R_{c-1}}} Q_{c-1} = \frac{2^{S_{c-1}}}{3^{R_{c-1}}}\big(1 - (8/9)^{u_c}\big)$ across $1 \leq c \leq L$ gives the normalized bi-adic shift identity
$$
\frac{Q_L(k)}{24 \cdot 3^{R_L}} - \frac{Q_0(k)}{24 \cdot 2^{S_L}} = \frac{\Delta(w)}{24 \cdot 2^{S_L}}, \qquad \Delta(w) := \frac{C_w}{3^{R_L}} = \sum_{c=1}^L \frac{2^{S_{c-1}}}{3^{R_{c-1}}}\left(1 - \left(\frac{8}{9}\right)^{u_c}\right) > 0.
$$
When $w \in \mathcal{E}_L$, prefix eligibility $3^{R_{c-1}} \geq 2^{S_{c-1}}$ gives $0 < \Delta(w) < L$ (forcing $r_3(w) < 8 \cdot 3^{R_L+1}$ whenever $L \leq 9$, since $r_2(w) \leq 24 \cdot 2^{S_L} - 9$), while $3^{R_c} > 2^{S_c}$ and $C_{w_{\le c}} > 0$ give $Q_c(k) > Q_0(k)$ for $1 \leq c \leq L$; by [5, Lemma 1], injectivity of $\Phi$ then guarantees **automatic pairwise distinctness** of $Q_0(k), \ldots, Q_L(k)$ for every $L \geq 1$ and every $k \geq 0$ (even on non-eligible words of length $L$, distinct prefix slopes $3^{R_a}/2^{S_a} \neq 3^{R_b}/2^{S_b}$ ensure that at most $\binom{L+1}{2}$ integer lifts $k \geq 0$ can suffer a state collision, so at least one of the first $\binom{L+1}{2} + 1$ lifts has distinct starts).

Next, relate total run sums $(U, V) = (\sum_{c=1}^L u_c, \sum_{c=1}^L v_c)$ to $(R_L, S_L)$ via the elementary unimodular coordinate change
$$
\begin{pmatrix} R_L \\ S_L \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} U \\ V \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}), \qquad \begin{pmatrix} U \\ V \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} R_L \\ S_L \end{pmatrix}.
$$
Terminal eligibility $3^{R_L} > 2^{S_L}$ reads $(9/8)^U > (4/3)^V \iff U \geq U_{\min}^*(V) := \big\lfloor \frac{2 - \log_2 3}{\log_2(9/8)} V \big\rfloor + 1$. Since $V \geq L$ and increasing $V$ strictly increases both $V$ and $3 U_{\min}^*(V) + 2V$, the minimal halving shell at depth $L$ occurs uniquely at $V = L$ and $U_{\min}(L) := U_{\min}^*(L)$, with $R_{\min}(L) = 2 U_{\min}(L) + L$ and $S_{\min}(L) = 3 U_{\min}(L) + 2L$. Because $\frac{2 - \log_2 3}{\log_2(9/8)} \approx 2.44238 \in (2, 3)$, successive increments satisfy $\Delta U_{\min}(L) \in \{2, 3\}$ and Sturmian steps $\Delta S_{\min}(L) \in \{8, 11\}$. Crucially, the front-loaded word
$$
w_L^{\mathrm{front}} := \big((U_{\min}(L) - L + 1,\, 1),\ (1, 1)^{L-1}\big)
$$
lies in $\mathcal{E}_L$ for **every** $L \geq 1$: its $c$-th prefix has $(U_c, V_c) = (U_{\min}(L) - L + c, c)$, so $3^{R_c} 2^{-S_c} = 3^{R_{\min}(L)} 2^{-S_{\min}(L)} \cdot (32/27)^{L-c} > 1$ for all $1 \leq c \leq L$. Taking $k = 0$ yields the unconditional constructive upper sandwich $Q_{\min}^e(L) \leq r_2(w_L^{\mathrm{front}}) < 24 \cdot 2^{S_{\min}(L)}$. At depth $L = 9$, $(U_{\min}(9), V) = (22, 9)$ maps to $(R_{\min}(9), S_{\min}(9)) = (53, 84)$, matching the sixth convergent $84/53$ of $\log_2 3$ with exact power gap $3^{53} - 2^{84} = 40{,}432{,}553{,}845{,}953{,}101{,}497{,}907 > 0$.

### 5.2 Proof of Exhaustive Branch-and-Bound Pruning Soundness

To certify $Q_{\min}^e(L) := \min_{w \in \mathcal{E}_L} r_2(w)$ over the **infinite** alphabet $(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2)^L$ without any heuristic alphabet cap, `_search_global_minimum` maintains a certified upper incumbent $Q_{\mathrm{inc}} > 0$ (initialized from the exact minimal-shell scan `scan_shell(L, U_min(L))`, `((3,1))^L`, and when $\binom{U_{\min}(L)}{L-1} \leq 60{,}000$, the next shell $U_{\min}(L) + 1$) and explores prefix cylinders $w_{\le c}$ ($0 \leq c \leq L$) carrying $(r_2, \mathrm{mod}_2, y, 3^{R_c}, S_c)$, where $\mathrm{mod}_2 = 24 \cdot 2^{S_c}$, $0 \leq r_2 < \mathrm{mod}_2$, and $\mathcal{A}_{w_{\le c}}(r_2 + k \cdot \mathrm{mod}_2) = y + 24 \cdot 3^{R_c} k$ for all integers $k \geq 0$. Soundness of every pruning rule follows from three exact integer implications:

1. **Residue ceiling pruning (`r2 >= Q_inc`).** Every completion of $w_{\le c}$ is a sub-progression of $r_2 + k \cdot \mathrm{mod}_2$ ($k \geq 0$) and therefore satisfies $Q_0 \geq r_2$. Whenever $r_2 \geq Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$, no completion of $w_{\le c}$ can attain $Q_0 < Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$, so pruning $w_{\le c}$ (and pruning any single-step Hensel lift whose lifted residue satisfies $r_2' \geq Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$) discards zero improving candidates.
2. **Post-horizon deterministic integer replay (`k_max <= 64`).** When $r_2 < Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$, any improving start $Q_0 = r_2 + k \cdot \mathrm{mod}_2 < Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$ must have $0 \leq k \leq k_{\max} := \lfloor(Q_{\mathrm{inc}} - 1 - r_2)/\mathrm{mod}_2\rfloor$. Once $\mathrm{mod}_2$ grows large enough that $k_{\max} \leq k_{\mathrm{switch}} := 64$, at most $65$ integers $Q_0(k) < Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$ exist in the prefix cylinder. Because the forward Syracuse trajectory of each integer $Q_0(k)$ is uniquely determined, directly parsing $L - c$ circuits forward from $Q_c(k) = y + 24 \cdot 3^{R_c} k$ and testing $3^{R_j} > 2^{S_j}$ ($c < j \leq L$) exhaustively checks **every** eligible completion of $w_{\le c}$ below $Q_{\mathrm{inc}}$ in $\leq 65$ replays, avoiding infinite alphabet branching altogether.
3. **Finite alphabet enclosure from positive rise/descent quotients (`k_max > 64`).** When $k_{\max} > 64$, every candidate $0 \leq k \leq k_{\max}$ enters circuit $c + 1$ at $Q_c(k) \leq Q_{\max} := y + 24 \cdot 3^{R_c} k_{\max}$. If $Q_c(k)$ executes circuit $(u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2$, then $Q_c(k) + 1 = 8^u a$ with odd integer $a \geq 1$, forcing $8^u \leq Q_{\max} + 1$, i.e., $1 \leq u \leq \big\lfloor \frac{1}{3}\lfloor \log_2(Q_{\max} + 1) \rfloor \big\rfloor$. Given $u$, $9^u a - 1 = 4^v b$ with integer $b \geq 1$ and $9^u a = \frac{9^u(Q_c(k) + 1)}{8^u} \leq \big\lfloor \frac{9^u(Q_{\max} + 1)}{8^u} \big\rfloor$ forces $4^v \leq H_{\max}(u) := \big\lfloor \frac{9^u(Q_{\max} + 1)}{8^u} \big\rfloor - 1$, i.e., $1 \leq v \leq \big\lfloor \frac{1}{2}\lfloor \log_2 H_{\max}(u) \rfloor \big\rfloor$. Furthermore, eligibility at depth $c + 1$ requires $3^{R_c + 2u + v} > 2^{S_c + 3u + 2v}$; since increasing $v$ multiplies the left side by $3$ and the right side by $4$, the first $v$ violating eligibility soundly terminates the inner $v$-loop. Each surviving $(u, v)$ is lifted incrementally via `hensel_step` in $O(1)$ modular operations.

## 6. Limitations, Scope, and Reproducibility

1. **Open problems and relation to complementary residue-avoidance bounds.** Theorem 3 collapses the conditional trough-floor base required for dimer cycle finiteness (and, in the forward case, open-trajectory descent, non-divergence, and halting cycle enumeration) from $1.82$ ($3.29$ two-sided) down to every base $A > 1$, complementing unconditional lower-bound results on $7 \bmod 8$-avoiding nonconvergent orbits (which force all states above $2^{129}$ [18] and force positive-density visits to ternary neighbourhoods of $-5$ [19], whereas Theorems 1–2 bound marked run concentrations and residual dimers from above). Proving an **unconditional** lower bound $Q_{\min}^e(L) \geq B_1 A^{L-1}$ or $Q_{\min,2}^e(L) \geq B_2 A^{L-1}$ across all depths $L \geq 1$ remains open: neither periodic-dictionary Subspace bounds (which permit $\exp(O(L/\ln L))$ growth when $k_W \asymp L$) nor Cramér density bounds ($\Sigma_L < 122.359^{-L}$, which bound cylinder measure rather than the least integer representative $r_2(w)$) exclude an isolated word with unusually small $r_2(w)$, as underscored by the non-monotone minimizer at $L = 5$ and the minimal-shell jump at $L = 8$. Likewise, the general Collatz conjecture remains open.
2. **Effectivity scope.** In Theorem 1 and Corollary 2.3, the Ridout/Evertse error $o_{r,s}(H_0 + U)$ and constant $K_{m,\sigma}$ are qualitative (ineffective in threshold). In Theorem 2, the Quantitative Subspace bin and circuit-deletion bounds are start-independent, while finite verifier checks exercise local mesh forms and separated $3 \times 3$ determinants below the analytic scale cutoff. In Theorem 3, $C_W$, $C_{\mathrm{slope}}$, $K_0$, Rhin's bound, $\Lambda_1(A)$, $\Lambda_2(A)$, $Q_*(A)$, and $Q_{*,2}(A)$ are **fully effective** closed-form expressions.
3. **Zero-floating-point reproducibility.** All five Python modules in `supplement.zip` (`collatz_cert_lib.py`, `verify_dimer_rigidity.py`, `generate_figures.py`, `check_manuscript_consistency.py`, `test_verify_dimer_rigidity.py`) pass AST zero-float auditing (`0` float literals or libraries), regenerate `dimer_rigidity_results.json`, `figure-1.png`, `figure-2.png`, and `figure_manifest.json` in $\sim 7.7\text{ s}$ on a single CPU thread, verify SHA-256 archive equality, and pass `76/76` `pytest` tests.
