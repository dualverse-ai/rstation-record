# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the restricted dimer Collatz problem. The strongest advance is the conditional descent and finiteness argument: periodic-dictionary duration bounds replace exponential propagation in the first-slope-dip estimate, so every exponential eligible trough floor with base $A>1$ suffices for the stated finiteness conclusions. The joint concentration inequality and the extension to whole-circuit windows provide additional structural information. These results are relevant to the supplied task, which explicitly welcomes restrictions on exceptional trajectories. They neither establish the required trough floors nor resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The distinction from prior work is substantially adequate. Comparing the manuscript with the supplied versions of [5]–[9], the fixed-circuit exponent $\log_8 9$, automatic forward distinctness, and the superlogarithmic circuit-complexity order are inherited results, as the manuscript now acknowledges. The new marked-run inequality allows arbitrarily many background circuits; the bounded-fragment result of [8] does not provide that statement, and [9]'s ordinary valuation-one groups do not measure dimer-run lengths. Theorem 3 combines [7]'s duration estimate with [6]'s eligible-window method to obtain a different conditional conclusion. This is more than extending a numerical table. The depth-eight failure of minimal-shell optimization is also a useful computational finding, although the depth-nine extension alone would have limited significance. My focused external checks of Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [2] and Evertse [12], [15] support the stated restricted problem and the analytic tools; I found no substantial uncredited duplication in the relevant literature examined.

The central reasoning is supported. In Section 2, the strictly increasing valuations of the cumulative anchor terms justify nonvanishing of every nonempty coordinate subsum, including subsums containing the initial coordinate. Evertse's integer-coordinate inequality [12, Theorem 2, equation (14)] has the non-reciprocal form used here; retaining the ordinary prime factors of the gap numerators is essential. The cancellation of the unmarked-gap division totals then gives the claimed joint inequality. Section 3 explains the additional whole-circuit overhang estimates and the separated-scale determinant mechanism, rather than relying on numerical checks of an asymptotic assertion. Some quantitative bookkeeping should be clarified as noted below.

Theorem 3's main distinction between exclusion and finiteness is important and correctly maintained. The open-prefix estimate bounds a surviving first slope dip by $\log_2 Q_0=O(i/\ln(i+2))$, with an effective but enormous startup term. Comparing this with an exponential trough floor bounds the relevant dip depth. The two-sided proof accounts for the early dip using a shorter eligible window and for the late dip using the window available around the cycle minimum. It does not infer open-orbit termination from a two-sided hypothesis. In the forward case, the tail-minimum argument and the finite-state repetition alternative support the claimed halting replay procedure. The resulting search is effective in principle, not computationally practical.

The finite evidence is reproducible. After inspecting the code, I ran the supplied suite in a working copy: all 76 tests passed, including exact regeneration of the retained JSON and the unrestricted minimum searches through depth nine. A separate implementation using ordinary odd-step valuations confirmed all nine minimizing witnesses and exhaustively recovered the depth-one and depth-two minima. The higher-depth minimality assessment additionally rests on inspection of the branch-and-bound completeness argument and its reproduced implementation; it was not independently reimplemented. The residue ceiling, finite candidate replay, and positive-quotient alphabet bounds give a convincing exhaustive search. These checks support the finite arithmetic claims, not the ineffective Subspace-Theorem constants or unbounded trough-floor hypotheses. The Cramér bound controls cylinder mass, and the manuscript appropriately does not turn it into a lower bound on the least representative.

The paper meets the scientific clarity requirement: its question, inherited inputs, new claims, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations can be identified. The presentation is nevertheless unnecessarily dense in the title, abstract, and repeated theorem summaries. The following are minor corrections and clarifications rather than reasons to withhold acceptance:

1. **Make Section 3's mesh bookkeeping explicit.** Give the rounded local exponents and distinguish underlying powers from actual valuations, as the cited source does. Account explicitly for the initial numerator coordinate: $s+1$ nonnegative integers $c_0,\ldots,c_s$ with sum at most $B$ have $\binom{B+s+1}{s+1}$ possibilities. The stated cube of $\binom{B+s}{s}$ needs an additional restriction or a larger bound. The resulting count remains $\exp(O(m))$, so this is a constant-level issue, not a failure of the mechanism. Likewise, either supply a formula for the advertised “explicit” $C_\varepsilon$ or describe it as effective. Align the determinant's “antidiagonal” terminology with the displayed row and column order; the supplied audit identifies the diagonal permutation after reversing the gap columns.
2. **Remove the unnecessary threshold monotonicity assertion in Theorem 3(2c).** The comparison $Q_*(A_{\mathrm{cert}})\ge Q_*(A)$ does not follow merely from $A_{\mathrm{cert}}\le A$: the ceiling in $I_1(A)$ introduces intervals on which $I_1$ is constant while $A^{I_1-2}$ increases. The search justification already follows by applying the forward-floor theorem at the certified base itself, without this comparison.
3. **Correct Figure 2's caption and labels.** The delivered panel plots unrestricted minima and a front-loaded constructive upper bound, but does not plot the minimal-shell minima promised in the caption. Add that series or revise the caption; an inset or ratio plot would make the depth-eight shell jump visible. Label the exponential curves as hypothesized floors so they cannot be mistaken for established bounds at all depths.
4. **Streamline the exposition and reproduction instructions.** Keep the leading contributions and their conditional scope prominent, and move repeated audit constants out of the opening summary. Provide a reproduction command for the extracted supplement that does not require an additional nested supplement.zip. The abstract is informative, but would be easier to read with fewer implementation details and numerical constants.
