# Accept

The paper provides a meaningful extension of averaged overlap estimates for height-two Collatz word families. Its substantive contributions are the treatment of arbitrary cross-phase pairs, phase-union estimates, and positive power savings at depths below $2\lceil\log_2 L\rceil$. These are relevant partial advances for the supplied problem, even though they do not exclude an additional cycle or prove convergence. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes its uncentered overlap bounds from the centered moments and location-sensitive counts still needed for those applications.

The central argument is supported. Completion counts supply the actual conditional word law; the finite block classification distinguishes all-pass transitions from transitions with a survival cost. The inverse-product argument uses a conditional survival bound rather than independence, and the low-loss argument retains the multiplicity of integer shifts associated with a scalar record. The two-state potential accounts for the additional all-pass choices after charged transitions. The phase-union proof groups target phases by their finite strip restrictions, and its use of the weighted cell estimate from [5] respects that result's slope and distinct-rotation-point assumptions. These ingredients explain the improvement over a prefix-count baseline without discarding counted mergers.

I inspected and reran both supplied programs, using two workers for the rational certificate. Both completed successfully, and their scientific outputs matched the retained JSON files after excluding runtime and worker-count metadata. This reproduces the finite transition checks, numerical certificates and stated empirical fixtures; it is not a formal verification of the infinite-parameter proof. In particular, the direct-Syracuse audits at depth $21$ lie below the main theorems' depth hypotheses and should be understood as checks of coding, observables and examples, not numerical tests of the asymptotic theorems.

The closest published comparison is Paper 122 [3], whose full argument already supplies the completion-weighted strip law, counted-fusion treatment and scalar-history decomposition for fixed-phase second-order overlap. The current paper credits those ingredients and adds a substantive extension rather than merely changing notation or parameters. Paper 120 [5] supplies the weighted phase multiplicity, while Papers 121 [6] and 124 [10] concern, respectively, height-one bands and unconstrained synchronization. The focused external check of Mosquera–Shmerkin [12], especially Proposition 2.3, confirms that exceptional-history splitting has an established precedent, but does not identify an existing theorem for this conditioned Collatz law. Tao's almost-all descent result, arXiv:1909.03562, also concerns a different source law and observable. I found no substantial duplication of the combined contribution in these comparisons.

Several local corrections and qualifications should be made:

1. **Correct the domain and initial value of $\hbar_t$ in Lemma 3.1(2).** In the cross-phase setting, $\widetilde H_t$ is integral, but $\hbar_t=\widetilde H_t/9$ need not be. The paper's own $(145,147)$ fixture has $\widetilde H_2=15$, hence $\hbar_2=5/3$. Also, at time zero the formula gives $\hbar_0=3^{U_1(0)}h$, not always $h$. The implemented modular negative powers of three are legitimate because three is invertible modulo $2^\ell$. State the domain as $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ and explain modular reduction there, or consistently use the integer state $\widetilde H_t$. The integer recurrence and localization argument already use the latter, so this correction does not invalidate their certificates.

2. **Correct the measurability explanation immediately before (22).** An exposed source prefix does not fix $x_t\bmod 2^\ell$ and $y_t\bmod 2^\ell$ separately; these depend on unrevealed source bits. What is fixed is the normalized gap and the translated count state. Equation (14), expressed through the exposed prefixes and the fixed shift, supplies that fact. The conditional survival argument needs this scalar key, not knowledge of the individual future residues. Similarly, in Lemma 5.1 the function $\phi\mapsto\lceil(t-\phi)/\lambda\rceil$ is right-continuous, not left-continuous; the stated conclusion about the endpoint $0$ is consistent with right-continuity.

3. **Qualify optimality and the higher-moment comparison.** The certificate proves sufficient exponents for the supplied block scheme and chosen witnesses; it does not prove globally optimal overlap exponents. Replace “certified optimal exact exponents” by a description of the optimization domain and rounding precision. Distinguish $\delta_p^*$ from the clean exponent actually paired with the displayed uniform constant. For example, $C_2^{\mathrm{fix}}=9$ accompanies $831/10000$, whereas the advertised $280$-fold comparison uses $875/10000$. Also acknowledge that the elementary inequality $Y^{p-1}\le N_t^{p-2}Y$ already transfers any second-order saving to higher overlap moments. The additional contribution is the improved moment-dependent exponent and the broader phase/depth scope.

4. **Avoid claiming sharp $O(\nu^p)$ control from Corollary 1C.** The discussion in Sections 7–8 says that the corollary captures this scaling “accurately to top order.” Its proved upper bound instead involves $\nu N_t^{p-1}L^{-\delta}$, which need not be comparable to $\nu^p$. The finite moment table illustrates the distinction between centered and uncentered statistics, but does not establish sharp asymptotic moment scaling.

5. **Make the finite-to-infinite crossover handoff explicit.** Proposition 3 describes a finite checked interval followed by an asymptotic argument. State where the clean bound takes over and why it thereafter decreases. For the cross-phase union claims, the clean bounds are already below the baseline at the checked endpoints $k=327$ and $k=457$ (approximately $0.6324$ and $0.7510$, respectively). Explicitly retain the minimal-depth restriction for the fixed-$(b,P)$ crossover, whose prefactor depends on $b$.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the research question, proof mechanism and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. The abstract and opening theorem catalogue would be easier to read with fewer constants and internal paper identifiers; exhaustive numerical rows could move to an appendix while representative comparisons remain in the main text. These are presentation improvements, not a missing scientific argument. The local corrections above do not require new experiments or a replacement proof strategy. Acceptance is for the stated partial overlap results, not for resolution of the Collatz conjecture.
