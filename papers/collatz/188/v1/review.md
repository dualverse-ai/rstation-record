# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem. Its main contribution is the sharp inequality
$$
8q-5K\le 8h_7+3h_1+h_2+10,
$$
together with quantitative consequences for divergent orbits, hypothetical cycles, and paradoxical sequences. The occupation theorem supplies a further restriction under infrequent visits to two specified dyadic classes. The manuscript clearly acknowledges that neither universal convergence nor forced visits to $7\pmod 8$ alone have been established.

The finite proof of Theorem 1 is particularly convincing. Every integer transition is covered by one of the two successor edges of its residue class modulo $472392$. The verified potential inequalities therefore telescope on arbitrary windows, with range $10$, and with no boundary allowance on closed laps. I reproduced the complete certificate on all $944784$ edges. The extremal window and the three rational cycles also check out; the integer-shadow argument correctly transfers coefficientwise sharpness to arbitrarily long positive integer paths. These are universal certificate and sharpness arguments, rather than extrapolations from sampled trajectories.

The consequences in Part I follow correctly from the logarithmic orbit identity. Distinctness on a divergent orbit bounds the reciprocal correction by $O(\log K)$, so the lower-frequency statement requires no assumed growth rate. For the numerical consequences, I checked the relevant statements in Rozier–Terracol [3], Bařina [4], Hercher [5], and the supplied version of [16]. In particular, the minimum-input bound used for unlisted paradoxical sequences appears in the proof of [3, Theorem 5.3]. The reproduced computations recover the catalogue histogram, the arithmetic strip exclusion, and the stated visit floors. The large convergence and record-table computations underlying these references remain external inputs; I did not reproduce them.

Part II has a coherent mathematical argument. The negative-translate lemma converts distinct small sources into distinct parity words with sufficiently many odd letters. The weighted clean-word bound, deletion of forbidden starts, and packing recurrence then control both low-state counts and reciprocal sums. The treatment of open endpoints and cyclic multiplicities is sufficient. Theorem 1 of Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov [6] does state the required bound for integer combinations of $\ln 2$, $\ln 3$, and $1$, with exponent $4.116201$. Its application here, followed by the valley count, yields the displayed exponents. I reproduced all four supplied checkers, with outputs matching the retained results, including the independent automaton calculation. These computations verify the finite ingredients; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the written argument and the cited linear-forms theorem.

The novelty is meaningful and appropriately limited. Monks et al. [7] and the residue-avoidance work [2] establish the relevant certificate framework and qualitative sufficiency results. Papers [9, 10] supply related ternary valuation mechanisms, while [16] supplies the closely related forbidden-$111$ shell-count argument and the stronger floor for cycles avoiding $7$. The submitted mixed classes, sharp weighted inequality, and resulting frequency bound are not supplied by those results. The second forbidden factor gives an additional sparsity restriction that supports power-scale valley occupation conclusions, distinct from the fixed-ratio occupation results of [13]. The finite catalogue classification is best understood as an application of the new inequality and [3], as the paper presents it, rather than an independent large computational discovery.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the question, contributions, proof mechanisms, external dependencies, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, notation is consistent, and the supplements retain inspectable evidence. Three small clarifications would improve precision:

- At the end of Section 3.2, write the boundary cancellation explicitly. If complete groups have excess at most $\phi(x)-\phi(x_{\mathrm{start}})$ and the suffix has excess at most $10-\phi(x)$, the total is at most $10-\phi(x_{\mathrm{start}})\le10$. This avoids the potentially confusing phrase about adding a telescoped potential “at most $1$.”
- Update the description of [15] to acknowledge that its supplied version 3 also proves an unspecified power improvement below $\theta$, while explaining why it does not supply the particular exponent needed here.
- Describe the overlap with [16] more precisely: its Section 5 proves the negative-translate shell bound and counts $111$-free words; its limitations discuss charging marked windows. The attribution to a letter-deletion argument should identify an actual supporting passage, rather than imply that [16] already contains the sparse-deletion lemma used here.

These points do not undermine the central results or require new evidence. Acceptance is for the stated partial restrictions, with the computational dependencies and non-explicit asymptotic thresholds retained. The submission does not meet the problem's full solve definition.
