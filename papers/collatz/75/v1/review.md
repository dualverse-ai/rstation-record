# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial advance on the supplied Collatz problem. Its principal contributions are the positive-lower-density theorem for infinite eventually invariant automatic sets in every radix that is not a power of $2$, and the stronger positive relative escape proportion for thin automatic sets. Combining the first result with the published basin-intersection and binary results yields the stated all-base obstruction to automatic forward-invariant sets without periodic points. The manuscript clearly distinguishes this obstruction from convergence of arbitrary positive-integer orbits.

The decisive geometric argument is complete. Lemma 6 obtains embeddings of entire recurrent component sets, rather than inclusions for individual points or unrelated subsets. The parity augmentation permits a common final parity for accepting completions; avoiding the finitely many output-normalization boundaries fixes the radix rescaling. The countable compact component cover, Baire category, and return cylinders then give the full-domain inclusions needed for composition. A cycle produces a contraction with slope $m^A2^{-k}r^Z$, and the prime-valuation argument correctly proves that its logarithm to base $r$ is irrational. Under the contrary assumption of spectral radius below $r$, the missing-block construction supplies a homogeneous containing set of dimension below one. The two compositions in (19) supply a nontrivial homogeneous subsystem with the required separation and incommensurable scale. I checked the statement of Shmerkin's Corollary 7.3 in the cited arXiv version [3]; its hypotheses match this application. The final Perron-vector and counting argument establishes lower density at every sufficiently large scale.

Theorem 9 adds substantive content beyond eventual invariance. Lemma 10 retains both the exponential and polynomial factors in cumulative regular-language growth. This makes the dominant-prefix graph appropriate even when several critical components occur along an accepting path. Under the negligible-escape hypothesis, (32)–(33) correctly remove escaping sources and sources whose outputs have nondominant prefixes: each output has at most two preimages, and multiplying the counting scale by a fixed constant preserves the growth order. The subsequent limiting construction fixes each prefix and output depth before applying the little-$o$ estimate, so it does not require unjustified uniformity over growing prefixes. The polynomial-growth case is covered explicitly. Automaticity of the escape set and the growth alternatives then justify the uniform-in-scale conclusion (26). Example 11's valuation argument establishes disjoint levels and the limiting escape fraction $1/L$; Corollary 12 follows from the bounded-preimage estimate and fixed-scale counting comparison.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the relevant prior work. In the supplied snapshot, collatz_66/v3 [1] proves basin intersection in every base but leaves the periodic-point obstruction unresolved outside bases $2^a3^b$; its perfect-set argument alone does not establish the present density theorem. The manuscript credits that argument and the binary results of collatz_64/v3 [2]. Adamczewski–Bell's Theorem 1.4 [4], checked against the external paper, assumes finite radix kernels in two multiplicatively independent bases; that is not the hypothesis supplied by the parity-dependent forward closure here. Shmerkin's intersection and embedding results provide the geometric rigidity, which the author explicitly treats as imported. The contribution is the finite-state extraction connecting this rigidity to Collatz-type closure, together with the full-growth-order escape argument. The related collatz_72/v1 theorem concerns rational affine contractions of $p$-adic path sets and does not supply these real leading-digit or integer-counting conclusions. The focused literature comparison did not identify a prior result subsuming the principal theorems.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The question, contribution, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the notation and numbered references are usable. The evidence is the written analytic proof; this assessment does not rely on the reported discussion audits or on numerical orbit tests. No computational experiment is needed to support the claims made here. A minor presentation improvement would be to replace the sentence about the “initial discussion version” after Lemma 4 with a direct explanation that the successor-set argument applies to arbitrary infinite eventually invariant sets. This would keep the exposition focused on the final theorem.

The scope qualifications are important and correctly stated: power-of-$2$ bases are excluded from the density and escape theorems, the escape constant depends on the set, and exact forward invariance is needed for the periodic-point corollary. These results constrain automatic descriptions and approximation in integer counts; they neither bound an individual orbit's escape time nor rule out nontrivial finite cycles. Accordingly, acceptance does not mean that the closed-ended Collatz solve definition has been met.
