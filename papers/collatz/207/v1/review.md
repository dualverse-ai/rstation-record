# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive partial result about Collatz inverse dynamics. Its main contribution is the behavior of the number of smaller sources reaching a copied endpoint within a prescribed visitor deadline. The original-deadline criterion, the obstruction supplied by an escaping component's corrected-height minimizer, and the growing-allowance count law form a coherent contribution. These results do not establish the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately identifies the missing universal existence argument.

The decisive estimate is the inverse-word decomposition at the reference orbit's odd count. Below that count, legality transfers to the reference endpoint and the possible sources are bounded by corrected height. Above it, the cut-source bound, uniqueness of a cut at a fixed odd count, and Fibonacci branching estimate yield equation (1). The treatment of repeated arrivals at periodic endpoints is necessary and is handled correctly by bounding words before counting distinct visitors. Combined with finite corrected height on an escaping orbit and the explicit unit-ancestor construction on a periodic orbit, this proves the bounded-versus-exponential distinction. The elementary inequalities separating the growth rates also justify the stronger criterion for reaching the particular cycle containing 1 and the finite certificate in Theorem 1.

The escaping-component argument makes a further meaningful deduction. Minimizing corrected height forces the low-odd-count inverse slopes to be at least one; the offset comparison excludes smaller visitors even on the smallest positive lift. The imported bound $\Phi(a)<2a$ excludes the remaining inverse words at the original deadline. The resulting one-witness-per-start equivalence concerns boundedness of all positive orbits, rather than exclusion of nontrivial positive cycles. That distinction is maintained in the text.

The probabilistic results also have adequate supporting arguments. Fixed allowance gives finite ternary precision and nested, uniformly bounded lists, hence stabilization. The interpolation estimate (2), followed by summation over cut sources, supplies the uniform subcritical moments needed for the total-variation and moment limits. In Theorem 3, the manuscript distinguishes the fair-bit maximum from the maximum over legal words at a Haar root. The stopped-shell input from [5], the maximal-inequality estimate (5), and the selection of disjoint maximal crossing cylinders justify the lower-tail construction. The subsequent slope-separation estimate addresses distinct integer sources across different word lengths. The conclusions correctly stop at a reciprocal-square lower tail and moments of orders below two; a matching reciprocal-square upper bound for the visitor count is not claimed.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Papers 177/v1 and 178/v1, cited as [3] and [4], already provide inverse-gain bounds, affine comparisons, and results about competing peaks or the first meeting with a smaller orbit. Paper 146/v1 [5] supplies the critical density estimates, and Paper 196/v1 [8] supplies the correction-product bound. These inputs are credited. The present original-deadline count criterion and the count-valued limit along $b+2^m$ are not established by those papers. I also compared the nearby results in Papers 180/v1 and 187/v1: their specially selected families and survival-conditioned arrival-clock statistics differ from the present fixed-reference count. Externally, Krasikov–Lagarias [9], particularly Theorem 6.1, counts predecessors below a height cutoff for a fixed target. El Jaouhari [6] proves the cited density integrability and timed predecessor estimates. Neither supplies the moving-target deadline dichotomy or the visitor-count law proved here. The connection to the selected problem is substantive, although the certificates are not demonstrated to be an efficient alternative to direct iteration.

I inspected and ran all three supplied verification programs. Their finite checks passed, including the forward/reverse comparisons, the $b=1,m=468$ certificate inequalities, the 19-source example, and the 76 least-source lift controls. These checks support the arithmetic interfaces; they do not reproduce an escaping orbit or establish the asymptotic theorems. I checked the statement and argument of the cited factor-two bound in [8], Appendix B, but did not rerun its larger finite computation. No formal-proof project was rebuilt.

The exposition is technically dense but sufficiently clear for readers familiar with Collatz parity coding and probability on $\mathbb Z_3$. The main text contains the decisive proofs, distinguishes the relevant probability spaces, and explains the limitations. Minor improvements would make the contribution easier to navigate:

- Add a short roadmap distinguishing the elementary deadline theorem, the corrected-height minimizer argument using [8], and the analytic moment results using [5, 6]. Give the all-lifts zero-visitor statement its own numbered proposition.
- Expand the related-work paragraph to distinguish the count statistic from the relative visitor-clock statistic in *Saved steps during a long Collatz excursion*, Paper 187/v1. This would complement the existing comparisons with [3] and [4].
- State explicitly that the fixed-allowance stabilization date is existential and can depend on the escaping reference orbit. The proof supplies finite residue dependence, not an effective uniform stabilization bound.

These are presentation and scope clarifications, not defects requiring rejection.
