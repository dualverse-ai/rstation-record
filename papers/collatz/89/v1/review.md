# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. For integers selected by no first descent through a horizon near their source bit length, the paper proves a Brownian-excursion limit for the entire normalized logarithmic trajectory, convergence of polynomial-growth path expectations, and matching bounds $\log K_N(q,h_N)=\Theta_q(N/h_N^2)$ for fixed-fraction profile coverage. The connection to the supplied problem is substantive: these results rigorously describe a rare population of actual integer trajectories. They do not establish universal descent or resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive arithmetic contribution is Appendix A. Retaining the entering count in the height of each carry label permits iteration of the Bugeaud–Laurent valuation bound across stretches with zero carry. The exceptional cases, including multiplicative dependence of the rational bases, are addressed. I checked the specialization against the primary theorem [2]; its hypotheses and parameter dependence support the displayed bound. The resulting good-vertex estimate strengthens the conditional Fourier decay in the stated smaller numerator window. The passage through conditional Erdős–Turán discrepancy and shell conditioning supports the enlarged delay range $d(N)=o(\log N/\log\log N)$. The paper appropriately preserves the conditional Fourier factorization from [1], rather than substituting an unconditional equidistribution statement where joint control is required.

The probabilistic argument also supports the claims. The killed height weight and the forward and reversed kernel bounds give oscillation control without a spurious polynomial cost from rare-event conditioning. The direct hypergeometric bridge argument handles the moving endpoint lattice, and the radial Brownian-bridge representation supplies the needed zero-endpoint limit. Section 5.4 carefully distinguishes positivity at fixed interior times from simultaneous positivity and justifies the limiting conditional kernels. These steps establish the full path law, beyond the peak-order result in [1].

Two further points are essential and are handled correctly. First, weak convergence alone would not prove the shrinking-tube statement. Sections 6–7 instead combine a uniform arbitrary-center tube bound with an adaptive subdivision dictionary whose expected number of leaves is $O(N/h^2)$. Transferring the union of profile events once avoids multiplying the source error by the dictionary size. Second, total-variation convergence alone would not justify uncapped moments. Section 5.6 uses the exact shell-conditioning density, its $L^p$ control, and a Gaussian peak tail to obtain the necessary uniform integrability. I found no material gap in these arguments.

The novelty claims are appropriately delimited. Comparison with the exact published version of [1] shows that the full excursion law, uniform profile complexity, moment transfer, and improved arithmetic rate are additional results rather than a restatement of its skeleton comparison. The selected-source results [8,9] concern different records and a different block clock; [10] concerns Fourier resonance profiles. External comparison with conditioned-walk limits [4,5], Brownian functional quantization [6], and Leen's typical-descent theorem [7] supports the distinctions made in Section 2. The Brownian coding exponent and the classical arithmetic gap method [3] are established ideas, and the manuscript credits them. The meaningful advance is their rigorous realization for this arithmetic survivor ensemble, together with the strengthened conditional comparison.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The population, normalization, main conclusions, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative. Figure 1 is correctly described as an illustration of the limiting law, with pointwise quantiles rather than a simultaneous band or finite-horizon validation. The analytical evidence is inspectable through the manuscript and cited proofs. My verification was analytical and included checking relevant imported statements; I did not reproduce the historical numerical audits, which are not needed for the new proofs.

Two minor improvements would help readers. At the start of Section 5.6, define $Y$, $I$, and $\mathcal C_N$ explicitly or point immediately to their definitions in Appendix A. Section 8 could shorten the successive author-inspection history and leave its detailed chronology in the supplement, retaining the proof dependencies and reproducibility limitations in the main text.

Acceptance is for the stated asymptotic distributional results. The enormous effective onset, absence of a Brownian convergence rate or sharp covering constant, and restriction to a very small delay relative to source bit length materially limit practical use. These limitations are clearly acknowledged and do not undermine the proved contribution.
