# Conditional clocks and source couplings for Collatz synchronization

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-21T13:27:03.237029Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/13/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We extend published Collatz synchronization results with quantitative estimates for conditioned clocks, couplings that retain actual inputs, and lower bounds for growing families. For an interior excursion of the counted shortcut pair chain, conditional on exactly $m$ mixed steps, fixed physical-clock deviations from $2m$ have probability at most $C_{\epsilon,\gamma}e^{-c_{\epsilon,\gamma}m^\gamma}$ for every $\gamma<1$, uniformly in the integer offset. A selected-occupation entropy inequality supplies the initial quantitative estimate and an explicit excursion-tail error; a short-block argument gives the stronger fixed-deviation bound.

For pairs $(x,3^{h+1}x+c)$ and $(x,3^{h+1}x+c')$, under survival through $N\ge ah^2$ mixed steps or its nonempty complement, we couple the actual sources and complete futures with error $C_a\sqrt{\log(2+r)/r}$, where $r=h^2/[1+\log(1+|c-c'|)]$. Exact binary transport gives finite positive-integer comparisons with a stretched-exponential clock remainder. The coupling retains physical first-exit records and every fixed source mark; a signed residue consequence uses one common functional of these records across offsets.

For simultaneous counted fusion of $D$ consecutive Haar starts, an adaptive common-future construction gives a Brownian hitting-time lower comparison on the $(\log D)^2$ scale and logarithmic growth of the known fixed-family tail capacity. A finite CRT argument also strengthens aggregate growing-residue independence to individually normalized residue tests and arbitrary nonnegative periodic weights.

Leen's square-root tails, exact leading constant and uniform pair estimate are credited as prior results. Seven bounded checkers support the finite interfaces. No matching family upper rate, relative rare-basin comparison, or universal convergence is proved.
