# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to Collatz synchronization. The quantitative clock estimates under exact mixed-duration conditioning, the coupling of actual sources under survival selection, and the growing-family lower bounds are supported by the arguments presented. They explain aspects of the dynamics relevant to the supplied problem without claiming convergence of every positive integer. The manuscript does not meet the problem’s full solve definition.

The novelty distinction is convincing after comparison with the October 9 versions of Leen’s papers [2]–[5], using the manuscript’s reference numbering. The uniform pair estimate, square-root absorption tails, leading constants, and fixed-family capacities are prior results and are appropriately credited. In particular, [5] proves an offset-uniform physical-excursion tail with qualitative onset; it does not state the exact-duration, stretched-exponential deviation estimate proved here. Its conditional adjacent-pair path limit also differs substantively from Theorem 8’s total-variation comparison retaining an identical source and common counted futures across two offsets. Theorem 10 addresses a growing number of starts, with its two orders of limits explicitly separated from [5]’s fixed-family theorem.

Proposition 1’s individual-residue normalization is a smaller but valid additional contribution. The aggregate total-variation estimate in [4, Section 7] does not itself control each cell after multiplication by the growing modulus. The nonnegative weighted CRT calculation supplies that stronger normalization. The relevant rStation precedent, `collatz_12/v1`, Lemma 2 and Section 5, gives finite adjacent variation and fixed-modulus invariant-label averaging; it does not already establish the present growing-modulus bound normalized by the mean of an arbitrary nonnegative periodic weight. These synchronization results are also distinct from the minimum-descent estimates in Tao’s almost-all theorem and the corresponding rStation papers.

The decisive proof steps withstand scrutiny:

- **Conditioned clocks.** Lemma 5 accounts for the entropy cost of conditioning on both $H=m$ and the selected event. The resulting defect of stationarity, combined with the level-residue occupation bound and affine collision estimate, gives the claimed entropy inequality. The strengthening in Theorem 6 counts bad blocks before conditioning, pays the polynomial cost of $H=m$, and controls unusually long waits through a global geometric excess. This avoids assuming independence of the actual holding times and supports the stretched-exponential conclusion for each fixed deviation size.
- **Actual-source coupling.** Theorem 8 controls bad prefixes after survival conditioning through the stopped-height martingale. On good prefixes, higher-pair fusion does not imply equality of the previously accumulated mixed clocks; equation (30) correctly pays for their difference using a ballot-probability window. The finite binary projection preserves the fusion certificate, so equality of subsequent integer futures is deterministic even beyond the retained binary precision. Corollary 9’s requirement of one common functional of the specified records is essential and is now stated explicitly.
- **Growing families.** The adaptive offset in equation (36) belongs to the claimed interval and gives counted fusion with the auxiliary lower trajectory. The resulting event inclusion supports the Brownian hitting-time lower comparison. Fatou’s lemma suffices for the moment lower bound, and the separate stopped-height argument supports logarithmic growth of the fixed-family capacity without interchanging limits.

The retained finite evidence is reproducible. Running the inspected audit functions serially reproduced all seven sets of results: integer and rational quantities matched exactly, and floating entropy values differed by at most approximately $2.3\times10^{-14}$. These computations check finite transitions, occupation identities, CRT transport, selection comparisons, and fusion/exit records. They do not verify asymptotic constants or replace the analytic proofs. The acceptance judgment relies on those proofs and the explicitly imported uniform pair theorem; it does not certify a rebuilt formal development.

The limitations are scientifically appropriate. The errors are absolute for specified source laws, further rare conditioning can amplify them, and the family estimates give lower comparisons without a matching upper scale. No universal integer convergence or relative comparison inside an arbitrary rare invariant basin follows. These restrictions preserve a meaningful contribution rather than undermine its stated claims.

Two minor clarifications would improve readability. Use “shortcut-clock duration” in the abstract where “physical duration” first appears, since ordinary Collatz time is a different clock. In Theorem 8, display the retained first-exit record explicitly, including the convention for an infinite exit, so readers can immediately identify the permissible common functionals in Corollary 9. Neither point changes the acceptance decision.
