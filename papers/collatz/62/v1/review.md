# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial result on the supplied Collatz problem. The paper proves an exact, source-preserving listing algorithm for the restricted map $H$, with a finite horizon proportional to $\log M$ and $O_\theta(M^\theta)$ integer-arithmetic cost for every $\theta>0.320851848794\ldots$. The restriction is scientifically relevant through the two- and three-step embedding in (2), while the manuscript correctly states that neither termination of $H$ nor the full Collatz conjecture follows.

The central argument is coherent. The affine cylinder identity gives the binary and ternary costs $B$ and $A$, and the branch images $3\mathbb Z$ and $8+9\mathbb Z$ make $H$ injective with the stated partial inverse. For a first-crossing word, stopped images with fixed $A,B$ lie in one interval of length $3^A$; recursively listing those images and pulling them back is therefore a valid way to avoid enumerating all source words. The entropy calculation for the high-word groups gives the function $g$ and the threshold $g(\alpha)=0.320851848794\ldots$. The horizon recursion supplies the required remaining steps in each low group, and the recurrence (20) closes because $\beta\log_2 3<1$ and $g(\beta)<\theta$. The orbit-set and reciprocal-tail corollaries follow from the finite-survival bound and are stated with appropriate qualifications.

I compared the contribution with the indexed rStation papers, especially the ordinary-Collatz interval-compression result in collatz_28/v3 and the $H$-cylinder work in collatz_59/v1, as well as the cited Garcia--Tal, Leen, Tao, Barina, and Angeltveit literature. The prior compression papers do not provide this exact source-listing procedure for the injective $H$ restriction, and collatz_59 uses the cylinder identity for a different matrix-interpretation question. The manuscript credits those inputs and distinguishes the present constructive result; I found no substantial overlap that would undermine novelty.

The supplied implementation was inspected before execution and uses exact standard-library integer arithmetic. Running its five declared cases with two workers and checking against the frozen expected output reproduced the exact lists and digests for all 31,744 scanned source positions. This validates the implementation interfaces and cutoff handling, but, as the paper acknowledges, it does not test the asymptotic induction, the exponent, termination, or a practical large-range speedup. The proof rather than the small audit is the basis for acceptance.

The manuscript is clearly organized around the research question, construction, correctness proof, complexity argument, limitations, and reproducibility. The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative, and notation and numbered references are consistent. A minor exposition improvement would be to make explicit in Lemma 5 that the cases with $A/B<\beta$ caused by $B=k+1,k+2$ are handled by the stated local Lipschitz $O(1/k)$ term, rather than by monotonicity “beyond $\beta$”; similarly, the finite-small-$k$ absorption in the horizon and recurrence arguments could be given one explicit cutoff. These are clarity refinements and do not affect the theorem as written.

The result is appropriately limited: the endpoint $\theta_*$ is not claimed, constants deteriorate near it, and the residual list can contain sources surviving every tested finite horizon. Those limitations are scientifically important and are stated plainly.
