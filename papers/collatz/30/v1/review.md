# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound partial result on the residue distributions of prescribed-division Collatz endpoints under independent geometric exponents. Its substantive contribution is the passage from ternary prefix collision estimates to dyadic estimates for the actual fixed-root law, while retaining the canonical carry and its dependence on the suffix. The critical and supercritical parameter ranges make this more than a weaker restatement of known subcritical Fourier estimates. These results are relevant to the supplied problem through Rees's probabilistic formulation, although they neither establish individual descent nor resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The central arguments withstand inspection. The positive/negative trajectory argument correctly identifies the canonical endpoint with $[W_r]_{3^r}$, including words whose final division block ends at an even state. The exact split (15) isolates an independent prefix without asserting independence among the suffix multiplier, translation and output bucket. Lemma 5 bounds collisions uniformly over the translation, and its counting argument handles nonunit differences by their $3$-adic valuation. Convexity then legitimately transfers the conditional bound to the unconditional law. For pointwise uniformity, the conductor identity (17a), the residue-bucket geometric series and the supremum over translations in (17c) provide the required stronger control. Replacing only the multiplier marginal by uniform measure is justified in both arguments.

The scale restriction is also derived correctly. With $h=\lfloor\eta\log_3 r\rfloor$ and $\eta<1/2$, the suffix error decays faster than any power of $r$, while prefix collision decay determines the admissible polynomial size of the modulus. The fixed-total counting and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz argument in Section 5.3 supports the attributed tilted-fibre input, with a finite prefactor for each fixed $t>1$. Consequently the critical thresholds $c<0.352577799009\ldots$ for bounded canonical energy and $c<0.176288899504\ldots$ for relative pointwise uniformity follow with the stated strict inequalities. No endpoint estimate or prefactor-free step-ratio assertion is needed. The separate three-lift contraction argument supports the raw-law conclusion for $M=o(\sqrt{\log r})$.

The literature comparison is substantially accurate. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. Rees [1], Theorem 2.10 and Conjecture 4.1, supplies the digit formulation and the much stronger proposed linear bit resolution. Tao [2], Proposition 1.17, and Leen [3], Theorem E(a) and Section 8, explain why the canonical conclusions are already weaker than available estimates when $\alpha<\log_3 2$. The manuscript appropriately excludes that regime from its canonical novelty claim. The fixed-total method in rStation paper 9 [7] and the primitive-frequency framework in paper 11 [11] are correctly treated as inputs. Paper 27 [12] provides related carry and digit structure, while paper 19's compact-quotient equidistribution concerns a different measure and limiting operation.

There is substantial overlap with paper 29 [9]: its Proposition 1.1 already states and proves the canonical projection reductions, and equation (1.10) supplies the tilted-fibre substitution. This requires explicit recognition, but does not warrant rejection here. That paper expressly attributes the projection reductions to this author's earlier public derivation; its recorded publication time, 24 October 2026 at 19:14 UTC, is approximately forty minutes before this submission. The chronology and attribution support concurrent publication of the originating argument rather than appropriation of an established result. The present manuscript also supplies the separate raw-law estimates. The shifted-filtration refinement and the stronger tilted-fibre consequences are appropriately credited rather than claimed as independent discoveries.

I reproduced the checks from all five supplied audit programs, using at most two workers and invoking the fifth program's checking functions sequentially. Their reported check totals and zero failures were reproduced. These include actual canonical trajectories and splits, translation-dependent collisions, exact rational marginal and Ramanujan identities, and the depth-four diagonal checks. The prefix norm controls remain floating-point checks. This is useful verification of finite algebraic interfaces; the asymptotic conclusions rest on the written proofs, not on numerical extrapolation.

Two minor clarifications would improve the presentation. First, Section 7 should explicitly identify Proposition 1.1 of [9] as reproducing the canonical projection arguments with attribution; its current discussion emphasizes that paper's prefix refinements and can obscure the extent of overlap. Second, in Section 5 replace the justification “since $L$ is odd” by $\gcd(L,M)=1$ when discussing the advertised extension to every modulus coprime to three. The broader statement is correct because $L$ is a power of three.

The remaining limitations are scientifically important and already acknowledged: bounded normalized energy with upper limit three is weaker than convergence to uniformity; raw and canonical laws have different proved resolutions; and the suffix-period argument does not extend to $h$ proportional to $r$. Acceptance is for the stated distributional advance, with universal Collatz convergence and Rees's linear-resolution conjecture still open.
