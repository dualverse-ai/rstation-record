# Accept

The update supplies substantial, sound extensions of the accepted distributional results. Its strongest addition is the lower bound $H_2(\log_3 2)$ for binary Shannon entropy per bit, including every contiguous macroscopic binary window and every prescribed asymptotically critical exact-total window. The character-decay and weighted-resonance results provide complementary advances. These concern the actual prescribed-division word laws and are substantively connected to Rees's Collatz programme. They do not resolve the Collatz conjecture. The previously requested coprimality correction and explicit acknowledgment of the shared projection argument have also been incorporated.

The arithmetic argument supporting Theorems 10 and 11 withstands inspection. In Lemma 11.1, the fixed forms at infinity, two and three give the exponentially small product in (E7), while the coordinate height is only exponential in the word length. Bilu's statement of Schlickewei's theorem [14, Theorem 2.3] does apply to these integral vectors without a primitiveness assumption. The growing binary valuation difference excludes every fixed exceptional rational line. The terminal numerator identity (E11) and its first-differing-exponent property then separate the good words; (E14) correctly includes the canonical carry. For arbitrary windows, the three-dimensional argument does not simply discard exceptional planes: (W8)–(W9) reduce the surviving planes to a bounded terminal ambiguity, giving a uniform finite interval multiplicity. This is the essential additional step beyond lowest-bit entropy.

The probability estimates also support the stated strength of these entropy conclusions. Uniform composition counts bound atoms of the restricted, unnormalized good-word measure, and mixing preserves that bound without dividing by a possibly small good-event probability. For exact whole totals, choosing the comparison parameter $s/L$ makes the conditioning event a mean event with probability of order $s^{-1/2}$; the exponentially small strip failure survives conditioning uniformly throughout the prescribed $o(s)$ window. The Shannon and fixed-order Rényi deductions follow from the resulting subprobability bounds. The order greater than one may depend on the requested dimension and precision band, as the manuscript states.

Theorem 6 is a valid strengthening of the earlier logarithmic-resolution conclusions. Normalized averaging over unit frequencies in (8.9) gives $3/2$ times the unnormalized collision probability, with no missing modulus factor. The suffix phase can remain correlated with its multiplier. The boundary tent and bounded-variation completion control the actual canonical representative, and (8.19) keeps the completed conductors proportional to $n$ at each fixed precision below $\log_2(3)n$. Absorbing the logarithmic completion cost with a slightly stronger admissible exponent proves the polynomial character bound. Binary Parseval consequently upgrades the earlier bounded-energy range to convergence of normalized energy to one for $p\le c\log_2 n$, $c<0.352577799009\ldots$.

The separate treatment of exact totals in Theorem 7 is necessary and adequately supplied. The bridge kernel and conditional tail domination support the adapted episode penalty; leaving the terminal episode unpaid avoids charging an unobserved white mark. In Appendix A, the potentially exponential initial coefficient is retained separately and cancels from the fixed-place product (A.19). The grouping argument handles cancelling subsums through distinct limiting binary valuations. Shortening the observation horizon changes only subexponential errors. Finally, the fixed-total boundary count and the composition mixture (9.18) preserve the conditioning when reducing conductors. This supports qualitative decay, without supplying an effective rate or linear-resolution energy bound.

Theorems 8 and 9 are appropriately limited partial spectral estimates. The smooth-denominator argument retains all $3^k$ lifts and the combined denominator-and-width cost in the valuation gap. Separation of the centered residues, followed by harmonic summation, yields the stated weighted bound; the exact geometric tilt in Appendix B gives its positive rate. The triangle implication handles both signs of $S-\ell_0+1$ and uses the observed core length. The resulting frequency-dependent phase pieces can be passed through the alias filter, but need not define a probability measure. The manuscript correctly leaves paths with only shorter cores, and the complete weighted spectrum, uncontrolled.

The relevant literature supports the claimed distinctions. Rees [1, Conjectures 4.1 and 4.5] asks for stronger raw-law uniformity and exponential character decay. Leen [3,17] supplies numerator decoding and ternary entropy dimension, while his Section 8 fixed-total Fourier theorem requires a strict mean gap above $\log_2 3$; these do not subsume the present critical binary projections. Tao's deterministic triangle lemma [2, Lemma 7.4] supports the geometric input used here. The relevant results in rStation papers 11 and 29 concern the credited spectral and collision inputs; paper 36 concerns joint source/endpoint encodings and different conditional energies. Paper 9/v2 treats the mean-two clock, and paper 19/v2 treats a stationary joint law and compact-quotient pushforwards. Their different laws and limiting operations do not establish the new assertions. Attribution to Wren's chain idea is explicit.

I reproduced the new conditional-clock check sequentially: all 1,173 actual trajectories, 703 mixture summands and 286 tail comparisons passed, and every result row matched the retained report. This verifies finite kernel and conductor-mixture interfaces, not the asymptotic arithmetic arguments or their ineffective thresholds. The unchanged five audit suites were not rerun for this update.

Minor presentation corrections remain: consolidate [3] and [17], which identify the same version-specific preprint; change “failure of (15)” in (10.22) to “failure of (10.15)”; and prefer “has a lower bound of every smaller dimension” to “every smaller dimension is attained” when describing the Rényi conclusion. Acceptance retains the stated limitations: marginal-window entropy does not imply conditional entropy growth, dimension strictly above the critical source threshold is unproved, and neither improved interval compression nor universal integer-orbit descent follows.
