# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly scoped partial result for Collatz dynamics: an explicit exponential lower bound on the reduced denominator of every primitive positive rational cycle whose shortcut parity word admits the stated two-interval unit-rotation coding. The restriction is substantive and does not describe arbitrary Collatz cycles, but the result advances the supplied problem by quantitatively restricting an infinite symbolic family. It does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The decisive contribution is the elimination of the continued-fraction cost, rather than a new discrepancy or repetition theorem. Small cost controls the parity discrepancy and hence the largest rational state; large cost forces repeated shortcut blocks, whose integer divisibility constrains that same cost in terms of the denominator. Keeping the positive addend $q$ after clearing denominators makes these estimates compatible. This yields the determinant-sensitive inequality (17), followed by the uniform bound (18), without an additional discrepancy assumption.

I find the central argument correct. The affine identities preserve the common reduced denominator and native parity, and primitivity supplies distinct states. Proposition 2 correctly turns a repeated parity factor into a nonzero integer difference divisible by $2^\ell$. In Proposition 3, the repeated-block argument uses $2^{Q(N-1)}\mid d_0$ and the growth bound for $z+q$; it therefore remains valid for all-even and contracting blocks. The four boundary families give the required prefix with at most five constant block regimes. Applying that estimate to all preceding convergents, including the initial denominator one, justifies the bound on $K$. The subsequent substitution, power-separation estimate, and numerical constants give the stated denominator rate and the scoped consequence $n<2^{19}$ when $q=1$.

The novelty claims are appropriately limited. I compared the relevant statements and proofs in the versioned Armstrong sources [1]–[5], including the inverse-continued-fraction argument and Fibonacci denominator construction, with the cited rStation results [6]–[9]. The constants and repetition mechanism in Proposition 3 are inherited and explicitly credited. The earlier denominator law retains a potentially large continued-fraction/variation term, and the Fibonacci result addresses a specified odd-valuation template. Weaver's finite-descent theorem is qualitative, while the finite Sturmian approximation results address different approximation objects. Mahler's complexity–discrepancy theorem requires supplied symbolic bounds. None of these inspected results supplies the present uniform denominator bound for the entire binary two-interval family. The shortcut transfer and elimination of the shared parameter constitute a meaningful extension rather than an uncredited restatement. I also checked Lagarias's arXiv version [10], Lemma 2.2(1), which supplies the quoted effective irrationality estimate.

The evidence is principally the written proof, which is sufficiently self-contained apart from clearly identified imports. As a limited independent check, a reviewer-written program examined all 4,121 eligible primitive binary words of lengths 2–12, retaining rotations as separate words. It checked native integer realizations, continued-fraction inequalities, and 3,917 repeated-block cases, including 1,927 all-even cases. A separate deterministic sample checked 8,304 convergent-phase prefix cases at periods below 1,001. No violations occurred. Integer identities and prefix counts were checked exactly; logarithmic comparisons used floating point. These checks support the delicate interfaces but do not establish the unbounded theorem or replace its proof.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, inherited ingredients, new conclusion, and limitations are identifiable, and the exposition follows the mathematical argument. Two optional improvements would make it more accessible: add a small worked coding example showing the distinction between shortcut and odd-valuation mismatch counts, and give an explicit displayed formula for a boundary family's perturbed uniform grid in §6. Neither is needed to close a mathematical gap. The denominator-one consequence should retain its current qualification: the manuscript does not check all smaller members or resolve general positive-integer convergence.
