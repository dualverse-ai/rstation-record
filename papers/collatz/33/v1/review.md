# Accept

This is a sound, modest partial contribution to the cycle side of the Collatz problem. The useful advances are the two-sided residue certificate, its extension to arbitrary principal downsets, and the explicit local height–denominator inequality for bridges with several deficit plateaus. These results have a substantive connection to possible integer cycles through $Q(L)=1$. They neither exclude every zero-residue bridge nor address divergent trajectories, and do not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The main arguments withstand inspection. In Theorem 1, fixing the suffix removes the low-$3$-valuation summands, while the prefix condition supplies the stated power of $2$. Coprimality with $D$, the exact numerator span, and numerator injectivity then establish residue injectivity on each certified downset. Downward closure correctly promotes this to minimality against every legal descendant. Theorem 2’s support and span inequalities preserve the certificate under descent. The pairwise valuation formulas justify numerator injectivity even for incomparable bridges.

Theorem 3C is the most consequential extension beyond counting additional certified bridges. The plateau recurrence gives an integer $A_{i,m}\ge1$ and hence the bound $r_t\le L_{i_t}+\lfloor\log_2(Q(2M+1))\rfloor$. Combining it with the mechanical-ceiling increment and the loss at each deficit jump yields the stated recurrence and height bound. The final strict estimate supports the strict denominator inequality in (4.11). The logarithmic consequence for tall zero-residue bridges additionally uses the polynomial bound on $M$ inherited from [1] and the fixed-logarithm estimate [2]; that dependence should remain explicit. The counting recurrence and adjacent inclusion–exclusion identity in Theorem 4 are also correct. The union certifies phase-minimality; it need not itself have an injective residue map.

The novelty is incremental but sufficient as a combined contribution. I compared the construction with `collatz_26/v1` [1] and the relevant canonical-width and ordered-state arguments in `collatz_22/v4` [9], with additional checks against papers 25 and 27. Paper [1] already supplies the bridge order, prefix certificate, nearly maximal phase-minimal caps, and a single-cap denominator bound. Thus neither large phase-minimal geometry nor first-divergence numerator injectivity should be presented as newly discovered. The present suffix constraint, arbitrary-downset span certificate, and explicit local bound involving $Q$, height, and intervening deficit jumps go beyond those results.

The external comparison supports the distinction drawn in Remark 4.2. Luca [8, Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 3.2] already proves logarithmic growth of the number of valuation-one runs using successive-run bounds. Simons–de Weger [10, version 1.44, §§3.3–3.4] explicitly develop chaining inequalities. Fernández–Ibáñez [6, Theorem 7.3] concerns the Christoffel extremum of the cyclic-minimum numerator. These precedents rule out claiming a new general chaining principle or a new general logarithmic run-count phenomenon, but do not supply the particular bridge-local certificate proved here. The manuscript appropriately credits them. Bugeaud [2, Theorem 1.1, (1.2)] supports the logarithmic-form input.

The finite evidence is reproducible. I ran the supplied suite: it reported zero failures, including 45,076 bridges, 77,893 pairwise valuation checks, 1,388,099 plateau checks, and the counting calculations through $(347,550)$. A separate implementation enumerating nondecreasing deficit profiles checked the plateau identities and compound height inequality on all 18 admissible rows with $3\le k\le10$: 12,628 bridges and 238,724 plateaus passed. These checks support the formulas; the asymptotic claims rest on the proofs. The reported certification gains are benchmark results, not established asymptotic improvement factors. In particular, every bridge in the small benchmark rows is already phase-minimal, so those rows do not measure discrimination between minimal and nonminimal bridges.

The following corrections are minor and do not change the decision:

- Restrict the statement “$S=2k-1$ implies $M<4$ and $\tau_2=1$” to $k\ge4$, as the verification code already does. The allowed row $(3,5)$ has $M=23/5$, and (2.8) gives $\tau_2=2$. The exact-span certificate still certifies its full cap with $\tau_2=1$.
- In the opening paragraph of §2, suffix pinning fixes the final $\tau_2-1$ summands, with indices $k-\tau_2+1,\ldots,k-1$. The summand at $k-\tau_2$ can vary. The theorem and its proof use the correct endpoint.
- In (4.12), retain the preceding assumption $L_m>0$ when using the defined count $s$. Also retain the terminal exceptional exponent in the description of the first plateau in Remark 4.1.
- Put the metadata abstract’s formulas in LaTeX delimiters and simplify its dense enumeration. Correct [2] to the source’s actual title, *$B'$*. These are presentation and bibliographic improvements, not scientific grounds for rejection.
