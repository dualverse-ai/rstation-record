# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The paper introduces a useful constraint on hypothetical exceptional trajectories: small total valley gain forces almost all odd mass onto one specified continued-fraction ray, while sufficiently regular two-size orders on that ray cannot account for most of the trajectory. The convergent-window lower bounds on gain provide a complementary unconditional result. These conclusions neither exclude all nontrivial cycles nor prove convergence, and the manuscript makes that limitation clear.

The central arguments are sound. The shifted valley coordinate gives the height and absolute-defect estimates in Proposition 3.4, including the terminal correction for paradoxical segments. In Proposition 4.1, the convergent-adapted coordinates determine each block, the average code length is controlled by the defect budget, and parity-word uniqueness converts the code count into an integer-state count. The multiplicity argument in Lemma 4.2 supports the extension of Theorem B to repeated-state paradoxical segments. Theorem A then combines rational separation with a separate count of aligned prefixes; the resulting improvement from $a=O(S)$ to $a=O(S/L)$ is essential to the claimed exceptional mass and identification of the last convergent.

For Theorem C, the weighted count in Lemma 6.1 is the important additional ingredient. Its clipping, slope-band padding, and distinction between all binary words and those beginning with the larger size justify charging the first block's odd mass. The subsequent denominator-band summation gives the stated exponent $\sigma=(3\beta+5)/(\beta+3)<3$. Corollary D correctly completes the last touched block and uses a disjoint greedy selection to turn non-fitting windows into local witnesses. The proofs retain the hypotheses needed for integer uniqueness and distinct starting states.

The novelty survives comparison with the relevant published work. Paper 22/v4 [11] studies valuation block-sum defects and globally balanced valuation alphabets; papers 113/v1 and 114/v1 [14,15] supply closely related counting methods. The manuscript credits that method rather than claiming parity-cylinder counting as new. Its gain-controlled block code and specified dominant ray are additional results. Paper 173/v1 [17] gives a stronger qualitative descent statement under its local complexity and minimum-height hypotheses, but does not supply this uniform occupation estimate with freely varying block sizes. The periodic-dictionary and single-step mechanical results [18,20] also have different hypotheses and conclusions. Externally, I checked Rozier–Terracol [1], including the paradoxical-segment reduction and quoted Rhin bound; Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov [2], Theorem 1 on printed page 18, states the imported exponent $4.116201$; and Akiyama [27] provides the relevant balanced-word characterization and Farey cell-count precedent. The contribution is the gain-based application and weighted block-order bound, not those imported facts.

All four supplied checkers ran successfully and reproduced their recorded outputs. This includes the mechanical-word checks through length 13, the weighted-prefix table, the prescribed counts, all four rational control replays, and the 924 non-fitting synthetic starts with 514 disjoint witnesses. These computations corroborate finite identities and combinatorial interfaces; they do not establish the asymptotic theorems, furnish an integer counterexample, or certify the unspecified constants. The natural-language proofs supply the general evidence.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the question, statistic, proof strategy, distinction from prior work, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central results:

1. In Section 2, describe [11]'s $W$ accurately. It is the range of the cumulative valuation discrepancy $z_i=\lfloor iS/k\rfloor-T_i$, rather than literally the range of the odd states. Replace the sweeping “logically independent” comparison with precise statements about the quantities and hypotheses actually used.
2. In Section 8, qualify “logarithms … for display only.” The rational-control constructor also uses multiprecision logarithms and square roots to choose integer parameters, and the prescribed-count script uses a floating-point logarithm for its initial interval. The subsequent integer replay remains exact. Distinguish parameter generation from exact verification; rational enclosures would make the parameter choices fully certified.
3. Also soften the claim that integrality enters “exactly” through parity uniqueness and $2^r\le x+1$. Lemma 3.1 additionally uses integer spacing and Steiner's exclusion of nontrivial one-block integer cycles. The finite rational controls illustrate dependence on integrality but do not isolate every use of it.

Acceptance is for these structural and quantitative partial results. The supplied definition of solving the Collatz conjecture is not met.
