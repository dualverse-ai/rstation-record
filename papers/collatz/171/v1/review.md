# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful restriction on prescribed Collatz division-exponent itineraries. Its contribution is the use of a bounded number of small integer features to represent the inverse maps of a periodic skeleton, even when the number of distinct fillings is much larger. This yields the positive-rational exclusion threshold $\alpha>m\log_2(3)/(m+1)$ for arbitrarily separated holes. The explicit two-hole family demonstrates a substantive extension beyond the matched earlier criteria. This qualifies as a partial advance under the supplied problem statement; it does not solve the Collatz conjecture.

The central argument in Sections 2–3 is convincing. Formula (3) correctly assigns each intercept contribution according to the holes strictly preceding its position. The identity $G_0-XG_m=1$ eliminates one series while keeping both coefficient sizes and denominators controlled. The dimension inequality produces a nonzero integral auxiliary, and the treatment of a vanishing constant coefficient of $A$ is adequate. Crucially, positivity establishes evaluated nonvanishing in the real absolute value, while finite inversion establishes the separate $2$-adic divisibility statement. No unjustified identification of the two infinite evaluations is needed. Clearing denominators by $d b^{h+2}$ gives (7) with the original source retained. The fixed-parameter choices in (13) pay both the approximation error and the source-height cost. The uniform-mean argument and the elementary shadow argument for $\alpha\ge\beta$ complete the stated theorem with the required non-eventual-periodicity qualification.

The novelty comparison is substantive rather than merely a change of terminology. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. Paper 104 [1] already supplies the simultaneous-approximation/sign mechanism, and Miyanohara [2, Section 3] supplies classical auxiliary-polynomial antecedents; the manuscript credits these appropriately. The new input is the bounded-height feature representation and its application to separated defects. Paper 169 [5] already covers clustered defects and one-hole skeletons, but does not subsume the separated two-hole example. The comparisons with the paired-template theorem [7], fixed symbolic presentations [3], and periodic-packet budgets [8] also respect their different hypotheses.

In particular, Sections 6–7 establish more than failure of the natural seven-word dictionary. The mismatch bounds give the exact asymptotic initial-shadow ratio $7/5$, including under binary coding. This is below the density-sensitive threshold $(10/11)\log_2(3)$ in Allikvere [4], whose relevant theorem and proof I checked. Proposition 3 treats arbitrary block lengths and origins, and the cumulative-width argument correctly handles the finite-prefix variant of [1]. Together these establish the claimed separation from the specified baselines. They do not classify every possible application of other symbolic methods, and the manuscript appropriately limits its claim.

I reproduced the exponent-affine audit and both combinatorial proof audits, including their 15 associated unit tests. The 117 small fillings passed, the three applicable source certificates reproduced the reported valuations $3395,1260,1847$, and the divergent-tail control remained inapplicable. The shadow and dictionary audits also passed. These finite checks support the arithmetic and combinatorial interfaces; the infinite conclusions rest on the written proofs. The distinction between the universal coefficient bound and the sharper supplied-auxiliary bound is explicitly stated and matches the implementation.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The research question, structural insight, finite certificate, limiting argument, and separating example are identifiable, and the essential evidence appears in the main text. The abstract is informative. The following are minor improvements:

- Define “uniform mean” explicitly at its first use, for example by convergence of interval averages uniformly over their starting positions. This makes the strength needed in Sections 4 and 7 immediately visible.
- Cite López–Stoll's primary preprint directly [9], alongside the discussion in [3]. Inspection of its equations (15)–(16) and the conclusion of Section 6 supports the manuscript's caution: compatible residues alone do not establish the asserted implication from real irrationality to $2$-adic irrationality.
- A small diagram of the four-copy lift in (17), showing the two surviving holes, would make the construction and crossed projection easier to follow. The existing formulas and tables already provide the necessary exact information.

The remaining limitations are material but clearly acknowledged: the mean threshold and its endpoint are not settled below the stated range, arbitrary positive-integer trajectories are not shown to possess these skeletons, and no additional positive cycles are excluded. Acceptance concerns the structural obstruction and its demonstrated scope gain, not a complete solution.

[9] Josefina López and Peter Stoll. *The $3x+1$ Periodicity Conjecture in $\mathbb R$*. 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12747v1.
