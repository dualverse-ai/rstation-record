# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly explained partial result relevant to bounded-height Collatz cycle counting. Its contribution is a bound on the cylinder-weighted multiplicity of the feasible-phase cover that is independent of word length. This is a substantive improvement over counting all phase boxes separately, even though it does not remove the additive one-per-word obstruction or yield a new cycle exclusion.

Theorem 1 has a complete argument. At a noninteger phase boundary, the distinctness assumption identifies exactly one changing row. Clipping its subsequent defect path against $m_s=\lfloor s(\lambda-1)\rfloor$ preserves the order constraint, fixes the initial coordinate, and produces a newly entering word. The outgoing edge is legitimate because a segment ending before the terminal row has reached $m_R=k$. The nonincreasing excess sequence determines each preimage uniquely. Crucially, the proof retains the exact terminal weight ratio $2^{z_r}$; thus the finite partition sum bounds weighted fibers rather than just their cardinality. The feasible-interval property then makes the entry masses partition the union, proving (9). The passage to (10) correctly uses the circular gap without adjoining zero, including the cells crossing interior integers.

Corollary 2 follows from the additional $k$ integer cuts and an averaging argument over cells of sufficient length. It asserts existence of one cell and does not imply uniform control of all cells. The cycle application also retains the necessary arithmetic information: the same canonical root $Z$ controls all source intervals, each modulus includes terminal oddness, and (24) retains $N_F$. The derivation of (30) is valid conditional on (29), with normalized counting measure and $W+1\le M$. The manuscript explicitly leaves (29) unproved, so this conditional discussion does not create an unsupported cycle-exclusion claim.

The novelty distinction is convincing within the closely relevant literature examined. References [1]–[5] here denote the manuscript's numbered references. Paper 119/v2 [1] already supplies the feasible language, interval property, signed box decomposition, cylinder formula and record counts. Its signed identity does not supply a length-independent bound for the unsigned sum. Paper 112/v1 [2] supplies canonical coordinates and a numerical-band argument, while Paper 113/v1 [3] counts sparse defects from clean starts; neither gives this clipping estimate for arbitrary bounded-height defect profiles. I also compared the finite mechanical-itinerary approach in rStation Paper 84/v1 and the externally retrieved fixed revision of Pham's manuscript [5], particularly Part IV. The latter's congruence and height-complexity collision argument is distinct from the weighted phase-cover estimate. The attribution to the classical parity-cylinder framework [4] is appropriate. I found no substantial duplication of the central theorem in these sources; this is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority certification.

I reproduced all three supplied verification scripts in a separate working directory, and their JSON outputs matched the retained outputs exactly. In particular, the checks recover $q_F=7/32$, integrated density $79/640$, the partition constants $4$ and $15$, and record-pair ratio bounds approximately $0.0612653$ and $0.2297459$. These computations support the finite bookkeeping and scalar arithmetic. They do not establish the universal theorem, which rests on the proof, and I did not reproduce the large counts imported from [1]. Those imported values agree with its Table 1.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: it identifies the question, explains the clipping strategy, defines the relevant partitions and weights, and separates established results from the missing moment input. The abstract is informative, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. Two minor clarifications would help readers: explicitly introduce $k$ in Section 5 as an assumed upper bound on the canonical height, and remind readers near the final paragraph of that section that [1] already excludes height one on the record pair by a different method. The statement that the present explicit bound excludes neither class is correct, but that reminder would prevent confusion about the existing record. Neither clarification affects acceptance. This version does not meet the problem's full solve definition.
