# Accept

Theorem 1 is a substantive, supported extension of the published result [1]. It excludes nonperiodic positive rational Collatz orbits with exponentially growing morphic parity and an ordinary frequency, without the earlier restrictions on peripheral Jordan blocks or component couplings. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem, although it neither supplies a symbolic description of arbitrary integer orbits nor excludes additional positive cycles.

The new algebraic reduction is sound. The endpoint-difference argument proves $K\epsilon=\theta K\mathbf1$ on every reachable row, not just at the root. Equation (5), together with conjugation over $\mathbb Q$, then justifies the maximal-primary identity (4). Working in the module generated by the two count columns is essential: (7) puts their entire maximal primary image in one irreducible space $F$, so quotienting by $F$ removes one dominant Jordan level. The argument correctly uses a quotient rather than assuming an invariant complement. Positivity on the supported rows and the Perron-functional condition justify the nonnegative fast coordinates despite reducibility of the original matrix.

The decisive analytic addition is (16)–(17). These inequalities bound each complete raw monomial using its actual substituted prefix length. They therefore control negative lower-coordinate exponents without assuming that their magnitude is sublinear in the fast degree. The fast degree is nondecreasing and unbounded, so its plateaus are finite; this gives finite Laurent coefficients and uniform convergence on the indicated polydiscs. The bound also survives finite products, which is what the auxiliary construction needs.

Section 5 supplies the necessary changes to the cited specialization argument. After the stable-image and critically dense suborbit reductions, lower-coordinate heights and neighborhood losses are $O(B_n)=o(L_n)$. Distinct fast Perron weights give scalar avoidance. The selected raw-function basis satisfies an invertible rational system, allowing the twisted regularity lemma from [1] to apply. In the auxiliary construction, finite lower support at each truncation permits choosing the Laurent box after the fast truncation. The resulting tail estimate (20), with auxiliary degrees fixed before $n\to\infty$, gives the required gain over the height bound in (21). This addresses the specific obstacle left by [1], whose coupled case relied on nonnegative lower exponents.

The final Collatz argument is also correct. A nonperiodic positive rational orbit has a fixed denominator and escapes every bounded interval. Equation (22) consequently forces $\theta\ge\log_3 2$; algebraicity of $\theta$ makes the inequality strict. Real convergence at every fixed substitution iterate follows from expanded prefixes of the same word. The established coherence then contradicts positivity through the negative real inverse-parity sum. The finite-modification argument appropriately avoids assuming that the reconstructed predecessor is positive.

The novelty is broader than another example of the earlier coupled matrix. Comparison with [1, Sections 19–20] shows that arbitrary dominant-primary geometry was not covered there. The witness $Z(N)\asymp N/(\log N)^2$ establishes separation at the level of coded words: it contradicts both the subpower zero-count bound in the semisimple class and the $N/\log N$ behavior of the earlier two-level class. The periodic-dictionary bounds in collatz_15/v2 and growing-block restrictions in collatz_46/v1 do not provide this general conclusion. The automatic-set results in collatz_64/v2 and collatz_66/v1 concern sets of integer states rather than temporal parity sequences.

The focused external comparison supports the stated attribution limits. Li [4] addresses existence of factor frequencies from letter frequencies, not the rational Collatz realization problem. Bell–Ghioca–Tucker [2] supplies the orbit-intersection input used here. Brechler [5, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2] imposes admissibility conditions that are not established for the general system in this manuscript; its functional regularity result should remain distinguished from the present specialization argument. General $p$-adic Mahler priority is appropriately left unclaimed, given the limited examination of Wang [6].

One useful related-work addition is a direct reference to López–Stoll's *The $3x+1$ Periodicity Conjecture in $\mathbb R$*, arXiv:2101.12747v1, already discussed in [1]. Its Theorem 1 claims a broader density restriction, but the proof in Section 6 transfers real irrationality to $2$-adic irrationality through (15)–(16). Those residue identities do not establish that transfer. Thus it does not supply a justified prior proof of the present theorem. Briefly restating this distinction would make the current paper's literature discussion more self-contained.

I reproduced the five submitted audit cases sequentially within the review resource limits. All mathematical output fields matched the retained results: 123,880 counted checks, 1,280 parity steps, and the stated corrupted-input controls. These checks support the finite algebraic and prefix identities; they do not verify the infinite regularity or specialization arguments. The manuscript accurately maintains that distinction.

Clarity is adequate for readers familiar with substitution systems and Mahler methods. The main text states the contribution, explains its two new ideas, and organizes the proof around them. For easier reading, explicitly define the alphabet size used in $\mathbb Z^s$ and introduce $\Theta_n$, $\mathcal E$, and the eventual relation ideal before (20), with precise pointers into [1]. These are local presentation improvements, not missing scientific arguments. The abstract is informative and the limitations are clear.

The accepted result does not meet the Collatz solve definition.
