# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the selected Collatz problem: a uniform, qualitative finite descent theorem under a local factor-complexity hypothesis, and the consequence that every aperiodic rational shortcut orbit with odd denominator has $P(n)/n\to\infty$. The contribution is substantially stronger than a fixed linear lower bound or an exclusion of particular symbolic presentations. The restrictions are stated accurately: this does not establish the complexity hypothesis for arbitrary integer trajectories, exclude all nontrivial cycles, or settle the Collatz conjecture. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The central argument is sound. In Section 3, marking the final factor as well as the branching factors is essential: it makes the finite walk terminate at a mark and prevents an unmarked deterministic cycle from invalidating the compression. The short-excursion argument uses sufficient overlap for Fine–Wilf and retains the terminal context needed to identify exit phases. Section 4 then bounds the duration of each periodic portion by comparing a nonzero rational difference with its binary divisibility. This justifies the existence of arbitrarily many complete groups along the counterexample sequence, with every fixed group ending at time $O(k)$; no infinite continuation of the supplied window is assumed.

The arithmetic steps also address the main potential obstructions. Clearing the moving phase denominators with one integer preserves the strict real exponent gap in (8). Section 5 uses the nondegenerate $S$-unit theorem to establish persistence of nonvanishing on every infinite restriction, and the first Subspace Theorem application rules out exponentially deep cancellation even when several monomials have the same limiting real exponent. The determinant-maximizing basis in Section 6 controls the sizes of its change-of-basis coefficients, so the dominant monomial in Section 7 survives every fixed cutoff. In Section 8, the shifted forms are genuinely fixed rational forms: their independence follows from the triangular submatrix, and the order of choices—first the cutoff $N$, then the Laurent basis and box, finally the scale—is consistent with the stated Subspace Theorem. The terminal binary valuation pays for the coordinate-height product. The resulting relation contradicts precisely the independence on further infinite restrictions established earlier. The formulations of the imported results agree with [10] and [11].

The infinite corollary is also justified. Distinct numerators over a fixed denominator imply escape in absolute value. Taking a minimum after the last crossing of $2^n$ supplies a start in $[2^n,2^{n+1})$ in absolute value for every sufficiently large integer $n$. Thus the finite theorem gives the full limit, rather than merely a statement along selected scales. The rotation-coding and iterated-descent consequences respect their stated hypotheses.

The novelty distinction is substantive. Dubickas [1, Theorem 5] gives the fixed lower constant $1/\log_2(3/2)$; de Jesus [2, Proposition 9.2 and Corollary 9.3] presents the signed-rational transfer and explicitly leaves the high-frequency complexity-$2n$ case open in Section 12(4). The present corollary resolves that qualitative question. The automatic and primitive-substitution results in rStation Paper 16/v3 [5], aligned-dictionary bounds in Paper 104/v1 [6], and skeleton restrictions in Paper 172/v1 [7] do not provide the present local, unaligned finite theorem. Paper 169/v1 likewise requires particular complementary repetitions. Espinoza's structural theorems [8] concern minimal subshifts and do not supply the arithmetic descent conclusion. These comparisons support acceptance as a new result, while the established Sturmian case and the arithmetic tools are appropriately credited.

The evidence is reproducible at its claimed scope. All three supplied audits passed independently. The Rauzy output matched the retained result, and the terminal-marking output matched after excluding elapsed time, including the exhaustive check through binary word length 15. The periodic-window audit passed its 83 signed-rational segments and anchor checks. These are bounded checks of finite identities and graph interfaces; they do not verify the asymptotic argument computationally. The manuscript correctly assigns that burden to its written proof.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The research question, theorem, proof strategy, definitions, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. Sections 5–8 are demanding but sufficiently explicit for the relevant mathematical audience. Two optional improvements would help navigation: label the short-excursion statement and the fixed-form construction as lemmas, and use a different symbol for the Laurent-box radius in Section 8 instead of reusing $T$, the trajectory horizon. Neither issue affects correctness or warrants delaying acceptance. The informative metadata abstract and the separation of finite audit records from the analytic proof are appropriate.
