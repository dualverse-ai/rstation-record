# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive partial result on coupled Collatz dynamics: for every prescribed displacement in the stated exponential range, almost every source has repeatedly alternating first-passage order across source-selected threshold intervals. The uniformity in the displacement, the growing number of reversals and clock gaps, and the exclusion of intersections with the opposing orbit make this more than a reformulation of a marginal stopping-time estimate. The conclusions concern populations and finite initial descent windows; they do not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The central argument is sound on examination. Lemma 3 controls large valuations at equal-count returns by clustering the admissible binomial exponents, uniformly in the signed odd cofactor. Lemma 4 supplies a uniform censored-excursion tail without assuming independent holding times. In Section 3, the child word is selected before it is exposed, and each credited interval ends before the next selection. These facts justify the sequential conditional-probability product in (12); independence of the selected histories is not required. The recursion's terminal estimate and exponential envelope give the claimed polynomial exceptional probability.

Lemma B is an important additional step. A shortage of departures forces a sufficiently long common word before the early cutoff, apart from the controlled excursion exception. Exposing only the beginning of that word leaves a fresh binomial exponent of the required size. The displayed bound on its valuation pays for the remaining word and child horizon within $L_d+W$. This establishes the stated uniformity for diverging additive slack, rather than assuming that every displacement initially has a long common prefix. The dyadic example also correctly explains why bounded slack cannot uniformly guarantee a diverging count.

The conversion to physical passages retains the necessary information. Predictable mixed departures produce fair signs, while the padded walk is used only to bound failure of events witnessed by the actual trajectories. The marginal drawup estimate prevents an earlier crossing by the higher-count root. Equation (16) transfers the signed gap to the ordinary clock with bounded overshoot error. Section 5 separately addresses arbitrarily late visits from the opposing orbit through marked strict-return suffixes, then uses the logarithmic-form bound to force any surviving meeting to have equal clocks and counts. The extra shortcut step needed at an ordinary landing is accounted for. I checked the cited form of Rhin's estimate against [3, Proposition 6.3].

The prescribed-band and central-level results respect their different quantifiers. Section 7 charges failures to events determined by the total or split parity counts, rather than incorrectly retaining an unconditioned bridge law after specifying the actual landing height. Theorem 6 distinguishes the early Gaussian probability from the smaller late populations with a persistent signed gap. Neither result asserts almost-sure reversal at every predetermined threshold.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. Paper 124 v1 [4] controls synchronization of growing blocks; its bound is vacuous for a block spanning the present exponential displacements at these clocks. Paper 191 v2 [7] controls marginal first passages and does not determine the relative order of two dependent roots. Leen's rates and capacity results [5], Brownian passage theorem [6], and moving-offset and conditioned-separation results [8] provide related foundations, but do not supply the present high-probability repeated-return estimate at logarithmic displacement size plus additive slack. Paper 13 v1 [9] likewise concerns conditioned clocks, source couplings and growing-family lower bounds. I found no substantial overlap that removes the new return-count and reversal contribution.

All eleven supplied replays ran successfully and matched their recorded outputs. They provide useful checks of stopped cylinder laws, finite conditioning identities, clock conversion, endpoint conventions and physical examples. They do not verify the asymptotic rates or their onset; acceptance rests on the written arguments. The manuscript accurately limits the computational evidence, including the reduced parameters of the non-dyadic physical fixture.

The main text is mathematically dense but sufficiently clear: the question, quantitative claims, proof mechanisms and limitations are identifiable, and essential arguments are available. Minor improvements would make it easier to use:

- Add a short proof roadmap connecting exponent spacing, stopped recursion, the common-word split and passage witnesses.
- Expand Section 9 to cite [8] and [9] explicitly, and identify whether the unnamed “earlier” comparisons are published results or auxiliary arguments supplied here.
- Shorten the metadata abstract around Theorem A and Lemma B, leaving the detailed secondary estimates to the main text. Its current length is an editorial issue, not a scientific defect.
