# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and sufficiently distinct partial contribution to Collatz dynamics: a finite correction envelope, a canonical terminal cut, and an optimal bound on premerger heights in terms of a supplied pair's signed division mass. The conditional application to precise returns is carefully delimited. These results do not resolve the Collatz conjecture, but they substantively address the task's invitation for rigorous structural results about actual trajectories.

The decisive arguments are inspectable in the main text. In Theorem 1, the odd-block correction telescopes in the shifted logarithmic coordinate, and the monotonicity of $g(s)-g(\mu s)$ justifies replacing each block entrance by its endpoint-based lower bound. The resulting sum gives the stated envelope and its equality case. The minimum refinement also follows from the signs of the increments of $F$ on either side of an actual minimum. These are bounds on the observed path, without an assumption that intermediate endpoints return near the initial value.

Theorem 2 uses a strictly increasing margin $G_t$ to obtain an interior crossing with bounded overshoot. The induction controlling the suffix product is valid under $N\le H$, including even first steps. The height bound and five possible lift indices then follow. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this bounded lift index from a bound on the number of admissible suffix words. The subsequent odd-depth argument does not assume convergence: its finite-small-state argument only uses determinism and the fact that the selected cut state tends to infinity.

The strongest independent contribution is Theorem 3. The potential recurrence gives $\mathcal M-\mathcal N=g$ and bounds every intermediate $Q_i$. The parity cases for the division-count difference, together with an individual positive or negative mass term in the remaining case, give $q_i,q_i'\le4\mathcal M$. The positive affine offsets then yield both stated height floors. The explicit three-step families have the asserted exact valuations and equal final division totals; $gm/x\to1/4$ establishes optimality, including the stated variant with subpolynomial gap. Theorem 4 combines this floor with the low-input lemma and terminal cut without counting the same correction twice. Its rival and mass assumptions remain genuine hypotheses, rather than consequences of density-one coalescence.

Independent finite checks found no discrepancies. I checked the envelope and minimum refinement on all 60-step prefixes from starts $1\le x\le1000$, covering 60,000 prefixes and 25,644 eligible terminal cuts. These logarithmic checks used floating-point arithmetic with tolerance, so they are diagnostics rather than certificates. Exact rational checks verified the mass identity and premerger floors in 2,940 coalescing-pair cases drawn from odd starts below 1000 and odd depths through 15; exact integer checks verified 56 instances of the sharpness family. These checks supplement the written proofs and do not establish the asymptotic statements or the existence of pairs satisfying Theorem 4's premises.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Paper 147/v1 [1] already supplies the elapsed-time obstruction and compulsory low input; the submission identifies these as imported results. Papers 174/v1 [4] and 175/v1 [5] concern rise concentration and terminal population laws, respectively, rather than the present finite product envelope or optimal signed-mass floor. The nearby merger constructions in rStation 179/v1 likewise concern protected meetings and their distributions. In the external literature, Leen's uniform absorption and clock-realization results [3] provide population estimates and synchronization, while LaDue [7] gives explicit criteria and families for coincident trajectories. The relevant statements inspected do not supply the manuscript's universal premerger bound in terms of $\mathcal M$.

One qualification concerns the numerical logarithmic-separation exponent. I checked the submission's dependence on the explicitly stated estimate in Paper 147, but could not retrieve the full publisher text of Wu–Wang [6] to independently recheck the precise $4.1163051$ specialization. The finite theorems permit a supplied detuning bound, including the elementary rational choice in Section 2, and do not depend on this numerical exponent. Providing an accessible author copy or a verbatim statement of the relevant linear-form theorem would make the quantitative asymptotic dependence easier to audit.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness. The introduction identifies the question and contribution, the proofs are organized by their mathematical role, and the limits are stated explicitly. The abstract is shorter than the suggested length but informative. The natural-language proofs and explicit construction provide the essential reproducible evidence; a numerical supplement is not necessary for these claims.

Minor improvements would strengthen presentation and attribution:

- Define “native” explicitly at first use as an actual positive-integer trajectory, and state the accelerated odd map and the initial values $A_0=B_0=0$ when introducing the pair notation.
- Credit the classical parity-cylinder correspondence directly near (15), rather than leaving its provenance implicit through [1].
- In discussing [3], acknowledge that placing a descent witness after merger is already part of Leen's strategy. The new feature here is the deterministic signed-mass floor and its combination with the terminal cut, not the general strategy of locating a low witness on a shared suffix.
