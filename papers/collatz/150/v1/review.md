# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial contribution to the Collatz problem. It quantifies the source concentration and finite-window memory needed to approach the known inverse entropy ceiling, and proves a logarithmic lower correction for the actual ternary-source minimum inverse cost. These are substantive restrictions on inverse dynamics, although they neither determine the optimal limiting slope nor establish forward convergence.

The central argument is convincing. The prefix-free stopping cover in Section 2 bounds unions of cylinders at their original source, without requiring disjoint cylinders or independent arithmetic branches. For stationary paths, source-residue recovery and entropy-rate subadditivity give $H(Q_n)\ge n\log3$, hence the relative-entropy budget in (19). Comparing this budget with a source concentration bound and a critical-walk endpoint event establishes the reciprocal-square source-entropy obstruction. The finite-window stationary lift and the conversion from mean gap to reward then justify (2). The stronger conclusion (3) correctly combines the forward entropy-dimension input with the explicit comparison (31)–(32); it does not confuse forward and reverse relative entropy.

The two further probability arguments also support their stated conclusions. Section 4.1 controls the area below the global maximum using the stationary potential and its nonnegative losses. The fixed-time invariance-principle limit, followed separately by the large-time spectral limit, yields the stated Airy constant. Sections 6.1–6.2 handle the unbounded positive overshoot of the reversed walk through its conditional expected overshoot. The curved first-crossing boundary supplies a fixed endpoint interval and a logarithmic reversed survival barrier, producing the summable convolution needed for (7). The expectation and almost-sure consequences follow from this tail estimate. Corollary 4 retains the actual reversed past of the positive orbit, and its passage to weak residue limits is justified by the clopen finite-depth sets.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the relevant published work. Leen’s [1] gives the first-moment ceiling and window certificates; paper 127/v1 [2] identifies window completeness without an approximation rate. Paper 135/v1 [4] already supplies critical singularity, stopping covers, and entropy-growth bounds for the independently coded Syracuse measure. The present contribution concerns arbitrary stationary legal selections and their finite-window realizations, rather than merely repeating those independent-code results. Papers 87/v1 and 89/v1 [9,10] establish related killed-walk estimates and forward survivor path laws, but do not supply these inverse selection bounds. External checks confirmed the critical full entropy-dimension statement in [3, Theorem C], the classical $3/2$ correction in [8, Theorem 3], and the double-meander normalization in [11, Section 23]. The manuscript appropriately credits these mechanisms. Its one-sided arithmetic-source estimate is a distinct application, not a new general branching-random-walk or Brownian-area theorem.

I reproduced all five advertised finite checks in working copies with concurrency reduced to two workers. All assertions passed; exact mathematical outputs agreed with the retained results, and floating entropy readouts agreed within $10^{-12}$. These checks support the finite source, clock, entropy, and event interfaces. They do not verify the infinite asymptotic statements computationally; those rest on the written proofs and the identified published input.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance: the main results, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. Three small clarifications would improve accessibility:

- Explicitly say at the outset that “physical steps” use the shortcut Collatz clock. An odd-to-odd gap costs $g$ shortcut steps but $g+1$ steps for the unaccelerated map in the task statement. Section 5 makes the intended convention recoverable, so this does not invalidate the results.
- Define $A_w$ and $C_w$ when (11) is introduced, and describe $F_w$ explicitly as recovering the source from the terminal state. Giving the defining series for the critical Syracuse measure would also make Section 5.1 more self-contained.
- Rename the absolute constant $c_E$ to avoid suggesting dependence on $E$.

The bounds are necessary obstructions, with no matching construction or sharpness claim. In particular, (3) does not establish that $\gamma_W$ approaches $\theta_*$. These qualifications are already respected in the manuscript. Acceptance does not mean that the supplied Collatz solve definition has been met.
