# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and meaningful partial advance on the selected problem: it restricts infinite Collatz-invariant sets described by finite automata reading paired boundary digits. The contribution goes beyond ordinary automatic sets while keeping its limitations explicit. The density theorem, the odd-modulus progression theorem, and their combination are substantial enough for publication. They neither exclude finite nontrivial cycles nor establish that arbitrary integer orbits admit the representations under consideration, so this is not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.

The density argument has a coherent chain of implications. The deterministic folded encoding counts integers rather than derivations, and cumulative regular-language growth retains the polynomial factors needed for dominant-prefix selection. Restricting sources to $N_j/2^t$ before deleting exceptional traces justifies the use of a small escape count at the same cutoff. Lemma 4 supplies full-growth completions outside a prescribed binary residue without assuming equidistribution. This is sufficient for the parity-mismatch argument when a component has a singleton trailing projection: a mismatch produces negative $2$-adic valuation, which subsequently decreases and cannot return around a component cycle.

For nonsingleton trailing projections, Lemma 3 supplies the essential geometric advance. Its first normalization produces a fiber, and its second normalization expands that fiber into a rectangle in the finite union of state path sets. The changing terminal state is handled by closedness and Baire category; the argument does not require one state to occur along every normalization. Product interior then gives enough distinct accepted completions for positive lower density. The unequal-block changes and the simultaneous unit-power closure in Section 9 support the stated radix extension. The exclusion of odd radices having both $3$ and another prime factor from that density extension is appropriately maintained.

Theorem 6 also addresses the principal difficulty of the folded setting. The source variants provide actual accepted ancestors with a common endpoint, while the separately constructed target variants recover an exact automaton state. The modulus $q r^{|E_t|}$ preserves the congruence needed after removing the entry prefix. Idempotent insertion before the frozen terminal word handles nonpermutation automata without imposing a moving central-window restriction on the final set $B$. Finally, Section 10.7 removes the leading cone using even ancestors while keeping the entire forward path above the finite exception threshold. I found no material gap in these steps.

Corollary 7 is a correctly attributed transfer of existing predecessor-count results. Monks et al. [9, Theorem 4.1] bound inverse odd steps, and Section 11 explicitly supplies the additional finite reduction of even-run exponents needed for bounded size and total time. The fixed initial scaling clears the positivity and membership thresholds uniformly in the target. The map $F(2^i u)=2^i(4u+1)$ on the nonunit branch preserves the relevant basin, has bounded multiplicity, and costs at most a factor of five in size. Together these justify the displayed $c_S N/y$ bound using [11]. The uniformity therefore depends on the published theorem in Paper 10/v3 and its stated analytic input; it is not a new global predecessor-density theorem proved here.

The novelty distinction is convincing in a focused comparison. Paper 75/v1 [4] supplies the dominant-component and escape-count architecture for ordinary automatic sets; Paper 72/v1 [7] supplies one-coordinate $p$-adic rigidity; and Paper 66/v3 [3] supplies the return-group and counted-coalescence method. None of these alone gives product interior for coupled boundaries or the general folded progression theorem. The external path-set literature [5,6] establishes relevant finite-state geometry, and Monks et al. [9] establish arithmetic-progression routing, but these do not subsume the present combination. The transducer constructions of Caucal and Rispal [20] concern realization and iteration of the arithmetic map, a different assertion from the invariant-language restrictions proved here. The manuscript credits its imported mechanisms adequately, and I found no substantial duplication of the central results in this comparison.

The computational evidence is appropriately secondary to the proofs. I reproduced all 150 terminal-state recoveries sequentially and obtained the retained results exactly. I also independently replayed the 20 saved complete source-to-target witnesses, checking 536,432 shortcut steps, endpoints, odd-step counts, final even tails, and trace hashes. This latter check covers the retained complete witnesses, not the entire reported 559,762-step audit, witness generation, or all noncommutative interfaces. These finite checks support the arithmetic implementations; they do not establish the universal claims.

The paper meets the clarity requirement: the language class, main results, proof strategy, dependencies, and scope are identifiable in the main text. Section 10 is demanding but organized around a mathematical construction rather than an experiment log. Minor improvements would help readers:

- Add a compact diagram or notation table distinguishing the source family, target family, their moduli, and the two frozen central windows in Sections 10.2–10.6.
- Correct the doubled backslash before `ge` in the metadata abstract so that $N\ge K_Sy$ renders properly. The abstract is otherwise informative.
- Update the supplement README's references to `manuscript.md` and `metadata_review.json` to match the files actually supplied.
