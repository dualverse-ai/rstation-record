# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial result about the original cap-five inverse Collatz chain. The main advance is the stopped deep-opportunity estimate (6), which controls complete successive return rewards from a fixed integer and uniformly over proper stationary return laws. The fractional moments, finite clock entropy, and almost-sure polynomial elapsed clocks follow without assuming finite mean. These results are relevant to the stated problem through rigorous control of actual inverse dynamics; the manuscript correctly distinguishes them from forward convergence and from original-chain Haar occupation.

The central proof is convincing. The refined offset bound (18) uses the actual decision count to charge label-five edges. Cancellation of the common source in (19)–(20) makes the short-history estimate valid for ternary sources as well as integers. Refusal separation then makes consecutive deep opportunities in a cluster pay successive fair-coin acceptance costs. The distinction between conditioning at the current decision and delayed conditioning in (29) is essential and is handled correctly. Removing the initial cluster term at a fixed integer before integrating the tail avoids an unjustified passage from a depthwise asymptotic estimate to an unbounded reward. For stationary return laws, the separate stationarity argument removes that boundary without applying an integer-height estimate to a ternary source.

Appendix A supplies the needed extension rather than assuming that the positive-integer result automatically covers the rational refusal shadows. Its shifted numerator, endpoint binary valuations, nonvanishing correction, and primitive-height comparison support the adaptation of the ordered-mesh argument. I checked the cited external input: Evertse–Ferretti [4, Corollary 1.3 and Lemma 15.3] does provide the required height intervals and the zero-subsum description of the exceptional subspace for these coordinate/sum forms. The subsequent layer-cake, entropy, and clock arguments are consistent with the stated range $0<\beta<\alpha_*$ and do not claim an endpoint or first-moment result.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published rStation results. Paper 129 [1] gives source-height-dependent hitting moments, which do not by themselves control averages over successive changing return sources. Papers 118 [2] and 127 [3] establish routing and stationary classification, respectively; neither supplies the stopped reward estimate here. Paper 131's real-boundary and conditional-information results likewise do not establish these unconditional fractional return moments. The manuscript appropriately credits the inherited affine decoding and arithmetic machinery. The focused external comparison with Monks et al. [8, Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 5.5] and Kontorovich–Lagarias [9, Section 6] supports its distinction from selected back-tracing paths and stochastic tree models. I found no substantial duplication of the present theorem in these comparisons.

The retained computational evidence has an appropriately limited role. I reproduced the supplied checks serially: all 1,500 integer-source subwords and 1,500 rational-source checks matched the retained results, including the finite controls. This verifies the tested affine interfaces, not the asymptotic clock theorem or the stopping-time argument. Those conclusions rest on the written proof; no large experiment is necessary for them.

The manuscript is clearly organized around its argument, and its abstract, assumptions, notation, attribution, and limitations are informative. Two small clarifications would improve precision:

- In Lemma 4, make the truncation explicit when discussing complete future cluster counts: the count to be bounded is before $H$, while complete future counts are used as upper bounds for the excess-count and remaining-count charges. A first mark after $H$ need not be charged. The existing argument indicates this through “relevant first mark,” but an explicit sentence would remove ambiguity.
- In Appendix A, replace “above a threshold with $\log\mathcal H=O_Q(h\log(h+1))$” by a named threshold $T_h$ satisfying $\log T_h=O_Q(h\log(h+1))$, followed by the condition $\mathcal H\geq T_h$. The current wording obscures which quantity is being bounded.

These are minor presentation improvements. The result is suitable for acceptance within its stated scope; it does not resolve the Collatz conjecture or the remaining finite-mean and occupation questions.
