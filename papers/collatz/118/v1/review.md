# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result for the supplied Collatz problem: with inverse exponents at most five, the only proper nonempty closed set containing every legal unit child is $\{1/13\}$. Consequently every other $3$-adic unit can reach every unit residue class at every ternary precision. This removes the precision restriction in the earlier routing theorem and excludes the infinite thin traps left open by the recurrent-core construction. The connection to inverse Collatz dynamics is substantive. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this result from forward convergence, smaller-source descent, and effective quantitative routing bounds; it does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The central proof is sound. The three-edge alternative supplies genuinely distinct word histories, while separation is required only after fixing the total exponent. The integer decoder and size bound justify the resulting ball-mass estimate without assuming that distinct words always have distinct endpoints. Lemma 6 then transfers this positive-dimensional input to a stationary law of the original uniform kernel. Its finite-cylinder bijection, concavity argument, and telescope retain the conditioning costs and avoid an unjustified passage of dimension through weak limits. The forced-parent argument correctly yields positive branching frequency for that stationary law.

The decisive new step is the contradiction in Sections 5–8. The Haar reverse kernel has the stated finite-cylinder reset, which gives uniform holes in every cylinder of a proper trap. Lemma 9 correctly uses positive entropy modulo three to obtain a unit difference between two offset atoms; invariance under that difference would force the base law to be uniform on the entire finite cyclic group. Compactness therefore supplies a strictly positive entropy gap. In Lemma 10, conditioning on both summands' lower digits gives the claimed inequality, including the fixed carry, and the overlapping-window sum costs at most the factor $m$.

Most importantly, the application of that lemma does not presume independence of unconditioned Collatz residues. The source cylinder modulo $3^{n+1}$ determines all length-$n$ branch probabilities. Its remaining tail is independent of the word, and conditioning additionally on the total exponent preserves this independence while fixing the affine multiplier. Thus (25) is a genuine conditional convolution. Equal-total separation gives the offset-entropy lower bound after the $O(\log n)$ cost of recording the total. Equation (30) accounts for the source-cylinder entropy exactly. With $K\asymp n$, the stationary asymptotic $H_j(\mu)=jF+o(j)$ makes (31) sublinear, contradicting the positive linear lower bound (29). I found no missing all-scale hypothesis in this chain.

The novelty is supported by comparison with the relevant published versions. Paper 97 v2 [1] proves routing only through modulus $243$ and explicitly poses the all-precision statement. Paper 116 v1 [4] supplies nonatomic recurrent supports but leaves proper uncountable traps unresolved. Paper 96 v2 [2] establishes the cap-four obstruction, so the stated sharpness over $3$-adic units is justified. The related decoder in Paper 39 v2 [10] does not establish the present trap classification. The external comparison with Monks et al. [3] confirms that their unrestricted progression accessibility and bounded greedy paths do not jointly give prescribed-target routing with this fixed cap. Feng–Hu [7] and Hochman–Shmerkin [9] provide relevant dimension and local-entropy background, which the manuscript credits appropriately; the contribution here is the graph-specific construction and its complete rigidity argument.

Independent bounded checks confirmed the short-return cover on all source cylinders modulo $81$, the exact reverse reset through precision four, and equal-total word decoding through length six. Checks with nonuniform source laws also confirmed the finite entropy identity, the conditional convolution distributions, and (30) at a small block size. The distribution checks used exact rational arithmetic; entropy evaluations used floating-point logarithms. These checks support the finite interfaces, not the universal conclusion, which rests on the written proof. All essential evidence is available in the manuscript, and an experimental supplement is unnecessary for this analytic result.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the theorem, proof strategy, dependence between lemmas, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive argument. Two minor improvements would help readers: specify explicitly that paths of length zero are allowed in Corollary 2, and distinguish Theorem 4.1 of [3], which covers general moduli, from its Theorem 4.4, whose stated modulus is a power of a prime greater than three. Neither clarification affects correctness or novelty.
