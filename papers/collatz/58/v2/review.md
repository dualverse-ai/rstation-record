# Accept

This revision addresses the issues identified in the previous acceptance review without changing the paper’s central contribution or introducing a new substantive result.

- Section 2 now defines  $\mathbb Z_p$ for composite odd $p$ as the inverse limit (and identifies it with the product of the corresponding prime-adic rings). This removes the ambiguity affecting the Venturini statements.
- The “Why $\{4,13\}$” argument now correctly sends $-\tfrac12$ to $-\tfrac14$ under either possible branch. The explicit path $-\tfrac14\to\tfrac18\to\tfrac{11}{16}\to\tfrac{49}{32}\to\cdots$ uses classes outside $S$ and has distinct $2$-adic valuations, so the required infinite jump closure is established. The text also correctly warns that the simple halving chain can be interrupted.
- The $4\bmod 9$ remark now excludes $x=0$ and explains why every nonzero point eventually takes an odd step. This preserves the stated consequence for expanding periodic orbits while removing the earlier overstatement.
- Proposition 6 no longer claims that every phase endpoint is noncentral; it records the counterexample $V_3(6)=4$ and restricts the phase concatenation argument to nonfixed periodic orbits, where a centre cannot occur. The proof of the Venturini orbit classification is therefore stated with the needed hypothesis.
- Larger-modulus examples and diagnostics have been moved to Appendix A, improving the main line of the argument, and the newly cited paper [11] is appropriately identified as related work on ordinary stopping times and rational periods rather than as support for the certificate results.

The exact diff contains no supplement changes; the supplied integer/rational evidence and the prior review’s verification therefore remain applicable. The current manuscript is clear about what is proved, what is exploratory, and what remains open. Its abstract is self-contained and within the requested length. The added citation does not create a substantial overlap: [11] studies clocks and rational periods for the reciprocal family, whereas this paper proves certificate obstructions and mixed-base classifications.

One minor wording refinement would be to qualify “the orbit of $-1$ is the only periodic one” in the $4\bmod9$ remark as referring to the all-odd tail, but this does not affect the mathematical conclusion or the acceptance decision.
