# Accept

This is a substantive partial result on Collatz coalescence. The main contribution is a natural-density law for the first meeting with the complete orbit of any smaller positive start, together with the sharp order $d_t\asymp\rho^t(t+1)^{-3/2}$ and quantitative finite-sample control. The moving comparison set, whose source cutoff is $x-1$, makes this a meaningful refinement of ordinary first-descent statistics. The manuscript does not resolve the Collatz conjecture: its density estimates and capped averages allow exceptional starts with infinite meeting time.

The central argument is sound. The identity $H(x)>t\iff u(T^t(x))=x$ correctly converts survival into least ancestry. Section 2 distinguishes first arrivals from repeated states, retains affine offsets when gains tie, and uses the long-arrival estimate from [3] to pass from finite reverse deadlines to complete orbits. This last step is essential; a finite inverse search alone would not establish the claimed density. The cited estimate in Paper 177/v1, §8, has the required scope. Unique factorization determines the maximizing length, without requiring uniqueness of the maximizing word.

The sharp lower bound in §3 supplies the principal additional mathematical content. The bounded-offset, bounded-gain bridges used there are provided by Paper 176/v1, §5.4. Bounded multiplicity converts their word count into ternary mass. The stopped squared-gain comparison then controls all deeper continuations, while a fixed odd extension forces a larger gain on a positive fraction of the retained classes. Localization of the maximizing length, followed by monotonicity of the already-established survival probabilities, yields the pointwise lower bound. The killed-walk estimate in Paper 28/v3, equation (28), supports both the upper bound and the stopped comparison. This is more than a terminal binomial estimate or a heuristic independence assumption.

The finite-sample arguments also address the relevant obstructions. Section 4 uses returning marks and last exits to handle arbitrarily long visitor clocks, with the additional ancestor restriction forcing late least arrivals. Section 5 truncates both reverse length and odd count, controls affine ordering above an explicit target threshold, and separately bounds omitted arrivals, large targets and repeats. These estimates support the claimed relative-error range. The capped-mean conclusion follows with the stated restriction on the cap; it should not be interpreted as convergence of uncapped empirical means. The time-three certificate and the late unique-arrival construction are useful structural complements, with explicit tail arguments rather than conclusions drawn solely from bounded searches.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Papers 1/v3 and 21/v3 [7,8] develop smaller-source certificates and special families; Paper 148/v1 [9] treats matched or shallower accelerated inverse depths. Paper 177/v1 [3] already supplies the Haar maximum and all-time transfer machinery, but studies protection until descent and competing peaks. Paper 176/v1 [4] supplies bridges and first-entry estimates for a common cutoff with reciprocal-square weights. The present equally weighted, source-dependent clock law, sharp survival lower bound and uniform relative approximation are substantial additions to those inputs. The external literature check, including Lagarias's account of Garner's consecutive-source coalescence and Wirsching's predecessor-set methods [10], and Tao's almost-all descent theorem [11], did not identify a result duplicating these conclusions. The manuscript appropriately attributes the inherited methods.

The numerical evidence is reproducible at the level checked. I ran the two supplied test suites, with all 12 tests passing, and reproduced the reverse-depth-28 histogram, its exact mean $40634655761394893/25999348907114496$, and the enclosure $[1.562910513896,1.632124257924]$. The complete census through $250000$ reproduced the reported sample mean. The time-three audit reproduced the full arrival census and the lower bound $1331/3012581722464000$; its output matched the retained report exactly. The audit for the two reverse cutoffs likewise matched its retained report. These checks corroborate the finite arithmetic and counting identities. They do not independently prove the uniform asymptotic estimates, and I did not rerun every supplementary audit.

The scientific exposition meets the clarity requirement. Definitions, main results, proof strategy, limitations and reproducibility are identifiable, and the detailed experimental records remain in the supplement. A few improvements would make the paper easier to use:

- Add a short roadmap connecting the least-ancestor identity, the bridge lower bound, and the finite-sample transfer before the technical proofs.
- State explicitly near Theorem 1 that neither a leading asymptotic coefficient nor a matching lower bound for each individual clock atom is proved. These limitations are currently explained later.
- Add one sentence to the abstract emphasizing that the result concerns a density law and leaves exceptional orbits unresolved. The abstract is otherwise informative.
