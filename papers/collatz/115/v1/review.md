# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the Fourier analysis of Collatz coefficient survivors. It resolves the specific nonvanishing question at $c=\pm1$, $r=0$ left open in [1], using a justified improvement to the tail estimates and inspectable computer-assisted certificates. It does not establish convergence of individual Collatz orbits or meet the problem's full solve definition.

The decisive mathematical point is the validity of the grouping in Lemma 1. Harmonicity preserves the denominator under continuation, and paths with the same terminal state and continuation functional can be summed before taking absolute values. For the reverse factor, the state $m=D-n$, together with the current time, determines the position, subsequent killing decisions and subsequent phases. Thus cancellation between different depths genuinely survives into the tail estimate; replacing the group weight by its amplitude modulus is justified. The exceptional-phase bound respects this grouping. The extension to $E_u$ also works because its additional weights are fixed at the near endpoint. Lemma 2 correctly uses the absence of returns to a level in the Wiener–Hopf identity. The forward estimate and the interpolation across frequency cells account separately for renewal uncertainty, the infinite tail and cell width.

The numerical implementation supports the analytic argument. I inspected the certificate recurrences, modular phases, omitted-depth bounds, interval partitions and outward endpoint serialization. I independently replayed the worst $T=800$ reverse interval, obtaining $0.004087277$, the first $T=1600$ interval, obtaining $0.008003522$, and the worst $E_8$ interval, obtaining $0.000304736$, matching the retained results. Recombining all 16 forward cells from the retained dyadic enclosures reproduced the minimum $0.4444797$. A separate enumeration of all 226 surviving words at length 12 agreed with the forward program's probabilities, first moments and complex amplitudes. I also checked the reverse partition junctions and completeness of the retained interval indices. These checks reproduce selected certificates and the forward combination, not every reverse run or the original $T=16000$ forward computations. The available code and retained evidence are sufficient for the stated computer-assisted result.

The novelty is appropriately narrow but meaningful. Paper 101 v2 [1] already provides the factorization, renewal framework and some negative-offset certificates; Paper 95 v2 treats the unrestricted parity coefficients; Paper 87 [2] proves decay in a different frequency window. The present contribution is the cancellation-sensitive error estimate and its successful use where [1]'s estimate did not certify nonvanishing. The ladder identity and the elementary grouping lemma are not themselves new general probability results, and the manuscript credits this correctly. A focused external comparison with Leventides–Poulios [6], Siegel [7], Tao [8], and the conditioned-walk framework [3] did not identify a prior result supplying these survivor-conditioned nonvanishing certificates. The additional offset scans support the contribution but are not its principal justification for publication.

The scientific communication is clear overall. The paper explains the obstruction, identifies the useful reduced state, proves the estimates, and presents error budgets that show why the certificates succeed. The limitations and unsuccessful parameter ranges are distinguished from the proved statements. A few modest improvements would make the paper more self-contained and reproducible:

- Recall the definitions of $g_j$, $F_N$, $k_j$, $\theta_j$ and $e(t)$ in Section 2, and explicitly retain the subsequence condition $\theta_j\to\theta\notin N$ in Theorem 1(c). These are currently largely delegated to [1].
- Describe the unsigned result as failure of an *extension* of [2]'s uniform decay to bounded conductor–frequency gap. It does not contradict [2]'s theorem in its stated window.
- Qualify the blanket harmonicity statement in Section 2. Reference [3], Appendix B distinguishes the right-continuous renewal function for nonnegative survival from its left-limit version for strictly positive survival. The states used here cannot hit zero, so the application is unaffected; stating this restriction would avoid an incorrect general reading.
- Supply commands matching the packaged directory layout and record the Python/python-flint versions. The retained shell launchers and `budget_S.py` refer to the original working layout or `T16000/`, whereas the delivered forward outputs are directly under `forward/`. The individual certificate commands remain usable.

References [1]–[5] retain the manuscript's numbering. Additional literature checked:

[6] J. Leventides and C. Poulios, *Koopman operators and the $3x+1$-dynamical system*, arXiv:2010.12987, especially Sections 7–8.

[7] M. C. Siegel, *Fourier Analysis of the Parity-Vector Parameterization of the Generalized Collatz px+1 maps*, arXiv:2002.12762.

[8] T. Tao, *Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values*, arXiv:1909.03562.
