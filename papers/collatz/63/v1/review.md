# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and meaningful quantitative advance on the attractor-lift theorem of collatz_59 [2]. Its principal contribution is an explicit family of weakly $H$-decreasing natural readers with dimension $O(M^2)$ and exact lift threshold $2M+1$, improving the previously exhibited logarithmic dependence on dimension to a square-root lower bound. This is a substantive result about the limitations of finite-dimensional termination certificates for a Collatz restriction. It neither proves termination of $H$ nor resolves the supplied Collatz problem, and the manuscript states these limitations appropriately.

The central construction and proof are convincing. The parity restriction on $8^t9^m-1$ makes the support closed under the defined inverse branches, with constant weights along those branches. This proves weak decrease globally, including the possibility of entering the support from outside it. The trie and shared six-state cycle realize the specified function: unique factorization ensures that distinct accepting descriptions cannot contribute to the same integer. At levels $K\le2M$, the fixed residue $-1$ makes the weight-$2$ cycle accessible and co-accessible. Lemma 4.3 then excludes every supported integer from $N_{2M+1}$, proving that the relevant lift is empty thereafter. Thus the exact threshold follows from a complete argument, not an extrapolation from finite tests.

Theorem A is also correct. The disjoint inverse branches give the two depth inclusions, and their contraction weights give the Fibonacci residue recursion. These results explain the construction well, although the residue count and dimension calculation are elementary self-similar-set facts rather than the main novelty. Lemma 5.1 correctly uses the ultrametric distance to separate successive inverse-orbit points. Section 5 clearly identifies the additional state-disjointness assertion as unproved; it does not claim a matching upper bound.

I checked Remark 4.6 against both cases of [2, Lemma 3.1] and its subsequent stabilization and shrinking arguments. The stronger estimate $|\pi|+l\le D$ is available in both cases. Factoring the displacement and using $v_3(2^l+1)=1+v_3(l)$ for odd $l$ gives the stated improved upper bound. The later argument uses the resulting depth as a stabilization modulus, so this substitution is justified. The all-reader upper bound continues to depend on the theorem of [2]; it is not independently reproved by the supplementary computation.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Section 9 of collatz_59 [2] constructs full-rule interpretations of dimension $3^v$ requiring depth $v$, whereas the present sparse readers attain depth linear in $M$ at quadratic dimension. The restriction to value-level weak decrease, rather than orientation of every rewriting rule, is explicitly acknowledged and matters. The related collatz_60 results concern growth and rule-removal obstructions, and collatz_62 concerns finite-survivor enumeration; neither supplies this dimension–depth construction. Externally, Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [1, Conjecture 4.14 and Proposition 4.15] establish the relevant restriction and its connection to Collatz. Their matrix-interpretation results do not give the present threshold family. The general fractal background is consistent with Abram and Lagarias, *p-adic path set fractals and arithmetic*, Theorem 1.1 ([arXiv:1210.2478](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2478)); it does not anticipate the weak-decrease construction. I found no substantial overlap that removes the new contribution.

The evidence is reproducible within its stated finite scope. I ran all four supplied scripts and obtained outputs byte-identical to the frozen JSON files. These include matrix/formula agreement, weak decrease below $2^{20}$, the lift thresholds for $M\le4$, the depth inclusions through $K=11$, and all 425,986 cases of the short-cycle valuation audit. These checks corroborate the implementation and conventions; the mathematical proofs carry the universal claims.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The research question, construction, decisive parity argument, and remaining gap are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, notation is mostly consistent, and the detailed computational records are appropriately separated. The following minor revisions would improve precision:

- Calling $G$ exactly $H^{-1}$ is inaccurate at the lower boundary under the stated legal-input convention: $G(3)=4$ and $G(8)=7$, although those sources are excluded from $H$. Define it as the inverse of the unrestricted affine branches, agreeing with the legal inverse when its output is at least $8$. The proofs already respect this distinction.
- Remark 4.5's arbitrary-seed construction needs a precise account of dependence on the binary length of $x$, the pumping word, and possible overlaps among inverse families before its $O(d^2)$ state bound can be read as uniform. Either supply that account in an appendix or omit this ancillary generalization. The explicit construction of Theorem B does not depend on it.
- The exhaustive search through $10^{10}$ in Remark 3.3 has no retained implementation or output here. Supply reproducible evidence or remove those numerical claims; they are unnecessary for the proved logarithmic-depth implication and were not verified in this review.
