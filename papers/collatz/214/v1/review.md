# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. Its principal advance is the occupation bound for specified interval-exchange and episturmian piece covers, with constants independent of the valley-block size ratios. The weighted stopping argument, the improved bounds for perturbed mechanical windows, and the restricted cycle exclusions supply complementary results. The manuscript does not resolve the conjecture: it neither bounds total valley gain for arbitrary counterexamples nor establishes the required symbolic complexity and discrepancy hypotheses for arbitrary cycles.

The decisive arguments are adequately supplied. In Theorem 2.2, the right-extension excess propagates through left extensions, with positive bilateral multiplicity accounting for new contributions. Along each propagated contribution, the first-letter weights telescope to less than the stopping threshold. This justifies the weighted bound without assuming comparable letter sizes. The interval-partition argument handles coincident cuts, and the reverse-permutation example establishes the claimed sharpness. In Theorem 2.3, arranging the suspension floors consecutively makes internal floor transitions continuous translations. Counting observable and remaining map cuts then gives a binary complexity bound independent of column heights. Combining this with the explicit state cap and parity-cylinder injectivity establishes Theorem 2.5; the separate treatment of piece ends and incomplete blocks is important and is present.

For Theorem 3.2, the padding argument accounts for the loss caused by the allowed flips. Summing slope bands before block sizes gives the stated logarithmic improvement, while the construction in part 2 makes the large-block size, two subsequent large-block positions, and interior flips recoverable from the window. Thus the lower bound establishes the exponent for head-flipped windows, rather than merely reporting finite search growth. The remaining logarithmic gap for the unrestricted catalog is acknowledged. I checked the cited Diophantine inputs against Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov [2], Theorem 1, and Rozier–Terracol [4], Proposition 6.3: they support the respective exponents and the distinction between an unspecified onset and an effective bound.

Theorem 4.1 correctly addresses a possible proper power in the binary encoding of a primitive superblock word. Prefix-free decoding makes the superblock start residues distinct even in that case. The height estimate, the two complexity inequalities, and the derivative argument closing the numerical tail support the finite ceilings. These exclusions apply under the table's stated complexity and discrepancy assumptions; dictionary membership alone is insufficient. The use of the older period threshold is conservative and sufficient for the comparison.

The novelty is meaningful relative to the closest papers in the supplied snapshot. Paper 192 [1], particularly Section 6, supplies the unperturbed weighted-window framework and states the one-flip extension without proof. The present work supplies the general fixed-$t$ argument, a logarithmic improvement, and matching head-flip growth. More substantially, its occupation bound removes the superlinear refitting term for specified covers and handles arbitrarily skewed block sizes. Paper 84 [11] treats finite approximation at the parity or valuation level; paper 173 [7] gives qualitative descent from local complexity; and paper 15 [8] charges periodic-dictionary complexity. Their statements do not subsume the uniform piece-cover result here. The comparison with paper 201 [22] also appropriately distinguishes bounded expanding-stretch counts from the present schedule hypotheses.

I reproduced the retained certificate through the supplied computational functions, including the 33 window catalogs, the golden intercept sweep, and all 53 cycle-ceiling cases. The algebra checks and manuscript-table consistency checks passed. This verifies the retained finite evidence and numerical endpoints; the all-parameter conclusions depend on the written proofs and cited results, not on those computations alone. The evidence is sufficiently accessible and reproducible for this purpose.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, three main contributions, proof mechanisms, and limitations are identifiable, and the figures help connect the combinatorial bounds with their applications. The following improvements would strengthen the presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- Add the closely related neutral-set literature, especially Dolce and Perrin [25], Proposition 3 and the interval-exchange extension-graph discussion. The conservation of right-extension excess in the neutral case is established background. Explain the additional weighted stopping threshold and positive-bilateral-multiplicity correction here.
- Shorten Section 1.3 by leaving most numerical constants and exact exponent fractions to the theorem statements. Move the exhaustive catalog in Section 3.3 to an appendix or supplement, retaining representative rows and Figure 2 in the main text.
- Keep the distinction between dictionary encoding and cyclic complexity explicit wherever the 56 golden schedules are mentioned. The parsing and gain checks establish the former and the relevant gain envelope; they should not be read as automatically establishing every complexity regime in the table.
