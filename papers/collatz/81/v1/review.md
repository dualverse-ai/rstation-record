# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly communicated quantitative advance for counted Collatz pairs. The main contribution is the uniform constructive bound $h=O(s+\log(2+|D|))$. Its application upgrades the previously established denominator-139 construction from a subpower source reduction to a fixed power reduction, while retaining a growing protected prefix and equality of physical odd counts. The uniform octave count is a useful further consequence. These are substantive partial results within the supplied problem's scope, although they neither establish descent for arbitrary sources nor resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive argument in Section 4 is complete. At level $k$, the chosen labels reach an odd offset in at most $v_2(3^k-1)$ shared steps, with at most one shared label 1, and then lower the level. Summing these costs gives the stated exact expression $S(s)$. The magnitude $M=|D|+3^k$ increases by at most $3/2$ at that shared label and contracts by at least $1/2$ at the down-step. This controls the boundary gap without a separate large cost at every level. The level-zero blocks then give the claimed logarithmic completion bound, including the terminal gap-one case. Negative and zero offsets, and the equal-count orientation change, are handled consistently.

The applications also follow. The binary-cylinder argument retains an odd affine coefficient through base changes, so the CRT conditions select actual positive trajectories. The rational-period identities and the slope budget justify the before-descent property independently of the suffix labels. The bounds on $h$, source height, and progression spacing support both power exponents and the octave count. The protected-prefix assertion appropriately imports the versioned inverse-minimum theorem [3], with its positive-odd-source domain made explicit.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation Paper 1, version 3, and Paper 21, version 3, and the external papers [1] and [2]. Said Duran's Proposition 3.3 supplies a quadratic word bound from level one. Leen already supplies the valuation threshold, exact cylinders, and boundary words. The present contribution is their deterministic combination with the magnitude budget to obtain a general linear bound. Paper 1's Proposition 17 supplies the same rational-shadow and CRT framework but only quadratic height accounting. Paper 21 supplies stronger numerical power exponents with different count guarantees. The manuscript credits these ingredients and explains the additional conjunction of properties rather than claiming priority for power reductions generally.

Verification supports the arithmetic implementation. I reproduced the generic audit: 2,429 states, 4,858 physical-pair replays, and 592,896 integer steps. A separate direct calculation checked the rational period, its prefix slopes, and the numerical inequalities used in the source-height bounds. An independent replay of the retained $m=1$ witness confirmed the source conditions, binary residue, all 1,905 suffix labels, stored meeting value, equal physical odd counts, and growth above the original source. I did not reproduce the full five-worker audit or its long initial-prefix replays; the universal conclusions rest on the written proofs and the cited inverse-minimum result.

The scientific question, proof strategy, evidence, attribution, and limitations are clear. Two minor wording changes would improve precision:

- After Section 7, replace “retain their exact ratio” with “retain identity (9) and the bound $b/n<2^{-1518m}$.” The ratio itself varies with the lift because of the additive constant in (9).
- In Section 3, qualify the exact-cylinder statement as applying to label histories through absorption, or specify how labels are continued after absorption. The transition table otherwise stops at absorption.

Neither clarification affects the proofs or the acceptance decision.
