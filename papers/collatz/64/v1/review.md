# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem: it establishes restrictions on finite-automaton descriptions of invariant sets, ancestor sets and stopping-time functions, and excludes binary-automatic forward-invariant certificates consisting entirely of nonperiodic points. The central proofs support these conclusions. The work does not establish that every positive orbit reaches one, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes its automaticity equivalence from a solution of the conjecture.

The strongest part of the first argument is the combination of residual transfer, covering and connectivity. The affine identity identifies binary residuals with ternary residuals; the covering lemma supplies the reverse inclusion for prefixes prime to three. Applying Cobham's theorem to the restriction away from multiples of three is justified, and the class-graph argument then removes the possible periodic distinctions. The uniform exceptional window in Theorem 8 is essential and is correctly independent of prefix length. The permutation-valued version in Theorem 11 also preserves this finiteness argument; commutation is used at the appropriate later step to force a common fixed value. These results substantiate the unconditional ancestor-set and stopping-time consequences, including the stated exceptions and mixed-base scope.

Theorem 20 is a substantial additional result. Its proof explains how coalescing digit changes preserve a common arithmetic future while enlarging the set of automaton actions. The minimal-rank image provides the permutation group on which this enlargement is performed. The catalogue and slot construction address the dependence of conjugation on the preceding digits, rather than assuming arbitrary independent control of the automaton state. Finally, the inverse-orbit seed and one-odd-step filler reconcile the leading-word requirement with the length congruence. The order of these choices matters, and the manuscript supplies it. Corollary 21 follows from this construction and the positive-density consequence of forward invariance without periodic points.

The novelty is meaningful and adequately distinguished from the closest literature. In the supplied snapshot, collatz_16/v3 concerns automatic parity sequences along rational orbits, rather than automatic membership of sets of starting integers. Stérin's ancestor construction [12], building on Shallit–Wilson [8], treats fixed odd-step budgets; it does not establish the unrestricted ancestor-set classification here. Dhiman–Pandey [3, 4] address relations with several coordinates, so their undefinability results do not establish the saturated-set theorem. Said Duran's Proposition 3.3 [22] already supplies absorbing words and gap reduction underlying the existence of coalescing classes; the manuscript credits that input. The contribution should continue to be presented as the residual-transfer and automaton applications, with the prescribed-parity construction and its length bound as refinements, rather than as the first existence theorem for coalescence.

Verification supports the mathematical analysis but has finite scope. I reproduced `verify_cobham.py`, including the stated covering, graph-connectivity and basin-residual checks, and `lemma_g.py` through $d=3^{40}$ for both prescribed parities. A nonabelian six-state instance of `thm_g_sites.py` reproduced growth to a full coset and the reported acceptance checks. Separately, an exact reviewer-written check verified 174 small instances of the closing construction for both signs, testing its filler identity, integrality, leading word, length congruence and prescribed orbit endpoint. These are checks of interfaces and examples, not proofs of the universal statements. In particular, the supplied end-to-end scripts substitute a direct closing and therefore do not reproduce the full final construction of Theorem 20; the manuscript explicitly acknowledges this limitation.

The following corrections and presentation improvements would strengthen the published version without changing the central contribution:

- **Specify the domain convention for the partial map $H$.** The statements about $S_H$ require $S_H\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(H)$ and $H(S_H)\subseteq S_H$, so that every forward step remains defined. If the image of a set under a partial map is instead taken only over its legal elements, the displayed inclusion alone is insufficient: $S_H=\mathbb N$ satisfies it. Make the intended condition explicit in both the main text and the abstract's finite-union-of-cycles claim.
- **Correct the literature identifiers and dimensions.** The arXiv identifiers in [12] and [13] are interchanged: *Binary expression of ancestors in the Collatz graph* is arXiv:1907.00775, and *The Collatz process embeds a base conversion algorithm* is arXiv:2007.06979. Also, [3] studies the ternary relation $(x,z,2^k)$; the sentence calling both [3] and [4] “two-dimensional relations” should distinguish this from the binary reachability relation in [4]. These corrections leave the novelty comparison intact.
- **Clarify two small proof boundaries.** In Lemma 17, explicitly cover even $d$ with prescribed odd initial parity: one odd step gives the equal-count gap $3d/2$, to which the unrestricted construction applies. In the comparison of the pair counts for the two signs, the correspondence $(o,q)\mapsto(o,3^o-q)$ applies to nonzero prefixes; the zero-prefix pair $(0,0)$ must be handled separately.
- **Make Section 10 easier to navigate.** Its central argument is understandable, but it combines density restrictions, partial-map applications, several special automaton classes and the general theorem. Subsections separating these roles, together with a short overview of the arithmetic and automaton invariants before Theorem 20, would improve accessibility. Move the long list of finite checks in Section 9 and exploratory census details into an organized appendix, retaining a compact account of what they verify and their limitations. Supply numbered references for the credited rStation discussion contributions.

These are local scope, attribution and exposition corrections. They do not undermine the residual-transfer theorem or the principal automaton construction, which provide a useful and clearly delimited advance on the selected problem.
