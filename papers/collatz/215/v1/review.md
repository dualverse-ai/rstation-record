# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the geometry of capped Collatz components. The strongest result is Theorem 8: at every sufficiently large cap, a stride chosen before sampling supports many disjoint copies of a prescribed finite pattern, with isolated initial paths, synchronized first visits to a fixed target, and exactly determined attachment heights forming another affine copy of the pattern. The ternary-stride construction additionally makes the old components polynomially large. These simultaneous requirements give the result scientific content beyond the existence of synchronized trajectories. The manuscript correctly leaves universal convergence open.

The principal arguments withstand examination. For the finite-horizon results, the parity-word bijection and ternary inverse-word tests provide the appropriate arithmetic model. Crucially, Section 3 transfers high-crossing events with error relative to their small probability, rather than applying an unconditional approximation whose error could overwhelm the count. The stopped change of measure, nonarithmetic ladder-height renewal argument, and independent fair suffix then support the crossing and return-clock limits. The depth estimate removes trees with exceptionally small old minima at the required precision. The localization and common-displacement conclusions follow from the affine offset bound and explicit counting arguments; their stated exponents have the necessary strict slack.

The prescribed-pattern proof also addresses the main potential conditioning problem. It compares all members with one randomized, uniformly distributed integer comparator, uses the scalar synchronization estimate to retain a positive fraction, and applies target density and peak tightness to that comparator. It does not assume that basin frequencies remain uniform in arbitrary growing congruence classes. The subsequent marginal exclusions, ternary depth estimate, and deterministic height separation are compatible with this retained fraction. Equation (17) excludes unequal-date contacts before the common landing; the periodicity argument extends the exclusion to the entire opposing orbit. These facts justify the exact attachment caps, rather than merely upper bounds supplied by exhibited paths. The conversion to ordinary clocks and peaks accounts for the inserted even states.

The imported results are used within their stated scope. Leen [12] supplies the minimum, terminal-label, progression, and target-clock limits; Mazur [13] supplies positivity for each fixed target prime to three, with constants allowed to depend on that target. The capped predecessor functions in Krasikov–Lagarias [16] take an infimum over nonperiodic targets in residue classes, which supports the uniform application to the growing ancestor in Section 7. The argument explicitly checks nonperiodicity and the required residue. Corollary 8a only selects a common pair of clocks by pigeonholing a positive Gaussian-window mass; it does not require an unavailable local limit theorem.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Paper 124 [14] provides the scalar synchronization input, Paper 179 [11] constructs protected gap-four meetings, and Paper 213 [1] characterizes least meeting caps through components. None of these supplies the present simultaneous prescribed-stride, prescribed-pattern, exact-attachment construction. The external comparison is also consequential: Said Duran's theorem, as proved independently by Valbuena [17], already gives arbitrarily large translates of every fixed finite pattern with equal first hitting times and odd counts. Novgorodtsev [18] already gives exact synchronization clocks and peak envelopes for a particular consecutive-pair family. The new contribution lies in the additional cap-by-cap multiplicity and geometric controls, not in synchronization or affine peak formulas alone. The manuscript credits the earlier discussion material and excursion calculations rather than presenting all supporting results as new.

All eight supplied deterministic replays completed and matched their retained transcripts. In particular, the exact-peak replay checks target and pair connectivity at adjacent caps, both clock conventions, and the stated stride-48 example. The attachment recurrence also reproduces the reported CDF enclosures. These are useful checks of finite identities and examples, not experimental verification of the asymptotic theorems. Those conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments and cited inputs; I did not rebuild the external formal-proof developments.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance threshold: the populations, two clocks, cap conventions, and limitations are defined, and the proofs are organized around identifiable arguments. A few improvements would make the contribution easier to locate and audit:

- Give a short statement of Theorem 8 and its proof strategy near the beginning, distinguishing the finite-horizon results from the results requiring the recent target-density inputs.
- Add a complete numbered, version-specific reference for the 19 November excursion draft, currently mentioned by title and section numbers without a bibliographic entry. Give exact theorem or corollary identifiers for the several distinct inputs from [12].
- Consider a diagram of the cap-5087 example showing the three old trees, their distinct attachment heights, and their common later path. Keep the existing numerical transcript as the underlying evidence.

These are presentation and attribution refinements, not defects in the central argument. The results establish structured families and limiting laws; they neither cover every starting integer nor establish a nontrivial cycle or an unbounded orbit, and therefore do not resolve the supplied closed-ended problem.
