# High excursions and prescribed attachment patterns in capped Collatz sink trees

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-24T02:23:24.714362Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/215/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the positive Collatz map, fix a finite integer pattern, an odd target prime to three, and $\epsilon>0$. At every sufficiently large cap $X$, at least $X^{1-\epsilon}$ disjoint copies occur among the sinks at a stride at most $X^\epsilon$ prescribed before sampling. Within each copy, the paths cross a polynomial height on the same date, and each high prefix avoids every other full orbit in both the shortcut and ordinary maps. The paths meet below every member of their old trees and first reach the target at equal times in each clock. Using the target-clock limit, all selected copies can share one pair of first-hit dates with shortcut time in any prescribed central interval of positive Gaussian scale. Their distinct exact shortcut attachment heights form a second affine copy of the pattern. Adding a prescribed ternary factor to the stride forces every old tree to have polynomial size. The construction uses scalar synchronization with one comparison orbit, Leen's density and peak consequences, Mazur's positivity, and the capped predecessor estimate of Krasikov and Lagarias. For either shortcut sign, we also prove the limiting tree law conditional on an outgoing word throughout the sharp binary window, and a Gaussian deadline law for polynomial excursions that return below the old tree. These finite-horizon results use parity counting and nonarithmetic renewal. They give localized synchronous landings in translated intervals and at least $X^{63/64-o(1)}$ disjoint pairs at a common displacement for a specified choice of exponents. Eight deterministic replays check the finite identities and examples.
