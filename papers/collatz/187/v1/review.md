# Accept

This is a substantive partial result about Collatz dynamics. It identifies the distribution of the largest clock advantage available from any smaller positive source during a long surviving excursion. The unique interior law, its exact positive support and point-mass bounds, and the different terminal scaling form a coherent contribution. These results concern conditioned populations and do not resolve the universal Collatz conjecture.

The central argument is convincing. Section 2 establishes an exact arithmetic interface: for sufficiently large sources, the logarithmic-form separation makes gain comparison decisive despite the affine corrections. The truncation of visitors with excessive odd grade is essential and correctly addresses why the finite lookup still minimizes over unrestricted smaller sources. Consequently, later arrivals and missing visitors are distinguished from positive savings without silently restricting the definition of $J_i$.

The probabilistic estimates retain the conditioning horizon where it matters. In Section 4, joining the reversed competitor to the actual remaining path controls all competitor grades together and produces the factor involving $m-j$. This supports the additional $(m-i+1)^{-1/2}$ contribution near the deadline. The visible one-step arrivals and their second-moment estimate supply matching lower bounds; the claimed mean orders follow by summing the tails. These are mathematical bounds, rather than extrapolations from the finite examples.

Section 6 supplies the crucial argument beyond tightness. The renewal calculation retains the actual endpoint factor before cancellation, avoiding an unjustified phase-independent endpoint limit. The finite-offset reduction then controls both aligned paths under conditioned-walk laws. Predictability of disagreement and the odd-bit bias exclude infinitely many disagreements; non-atomicity excludes accidental equality with unequal grades. The resulting finite certificates of physical coalescence justify localization and total-variation convergence even when $m-i=o(m)$. Appendix A gives a compatible quantitative refinement. The separate construction for the saving-two atom also addresses the exceptional value that the one-step construction does not cover.

The terminal conclusions are appropriately qualified. The intermediate-arrival estimate excludes mass away from zero and one after scaling by $m$, while bounded-clock visitors and protected sources establish nondegeneracy. The generic-phase formula retains both height and ternary residue. The manuscript explicitly leaves resonant phases and variation of the mixture weight open; it does not claim an unconditional terminal limit that its proof fails to establish.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. The source-transfer and killed-walk machinery in rStation 87/v1, 91/v1 and 101/v2 is credited as input [2–4]. Papers 178/v1 and 185/v1 concern the first meeting and its relation to descent [6,7]; 184/v1 concerns the pre-descent ternary state under additional first-meeting conditioning [8]. Those results do not supply the fastest-arrival saving law proved here. I also compared the stated results and relevant arguments in Leen's external papers on marked arrival laws and late synchronization [9,10]. Their observables involve minima or fixed families of shifted starts, with different conditioning; they do not establish the present minimization over all smaller sources under singleton survival. A short comparison with this synchronization literature would improve the attribution context, particularly around the aligned-path argument, but I found no substantial duplication. The explicitly cited logarithmic separation in [1] agrees with Lagarias's Lemma 2.2.

Reproducible evidence is adequate for a proof-based paper. I reran seven supplied audits: finite-prefix minima, exact saving values, the exceptional two-step atom, quantitative aligned agreement, point-mass constructions, joining to the remaining walk, and terminal interval selection. All matched their retained outputs. These checks include independent forward/inverse comparisons and exact interval-translation identities. They support the finite arithmetic interfaces; they do not computationally verify the asymptotic renewal, localization or phase-limit arguments. Acceptance rests on those written arguments as well.

The manuscript is technically dense but scientifically clear: the observable, theorem, arithmetic reduction, probability estimates and limitations are identifiable and developed in a logical order. Minor improvements would help navigation. Define $\alpha$ before its first use in theorem item 2; explicitly restrict the simultaneous earliest-clock construction in item 5 to $h\ge5$, as in its proof; and add a brief proof roadmap explaining how counting competitors, interval transfer and aligned coalescence fit together. A small alignment diagram would be useful but is not necessary for correctness. The abstract is informative, and the numerical records are appropriately kept in the supplement.
