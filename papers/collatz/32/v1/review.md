# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial contribution to Collatz residue avoidance. Its main value is the characterization of a specified certificate method and the resulting restrictions on hypothetical nonconvergent trajectories. It neither resolves an open mod-8 class nor solves the Collatz conjecture, and acceptance does not imply either conclusion.

Theorem A now supplies the essential finite-convergence hypothesis. The rational edge inequalities imply descent above the stated threshold, while (V) excludes trajectories that enter the remaining finite range. The separate treatment of simple components is valid: indefinite repetition of their parity word forces the unique rational pin by the expanding forward action in the $2$-adic metric. Thus neither a spurious rational cycle nor an unverified small positive cycle is silently discarded.

Theorem B is also convincing. Inverse branches contract whenever an even step occurs, and every infinite graph walk has infinitely many such steps; this justifies the residue-walk coding. On a finite strongly connected component, invariant edge frequencies are circulations, so their maximum drift is the maximum cycle mean. In a non-simple component with a positive-mean cycle, the two distinct first-return loops supply an ergodic measure with positive entropy and positive drift. Conversely, negative maximum cycle mean excludes every nonnegative-drift measure on that component. These arguments establish the claimed equivalence and resolution invariance of (P), without extrapolating from the finite experiments. The monotonicity argument in Theorem F then legitimately extends its exact failure witnesses to all larger depths.

The computational evidence is substantial and reproducible. I reran the exact certifier over the reported mod-8, mod-16 and mod-32 scopes, reproduced the closure-basis reduction, and reran the cascade calculations through $g=14$. A separate standard-library implementation using integer cycle weights and an independent component calculation reproduced all certified counts: $246$ for the nonempty proper mod-8 sets, $2/46/364$ for mod-16 singletons/pairs/triples, and $0/17/538$ for mod 32. The submitted checks verified convergence below $335$. Independent rational calculations also checked the cascade formulas through $j=30$ and recovered $h_+(\{5,7\})\approx0.1967294104$. These finite checks support the implementations; the unrestricted statements depend on the written proofs. I did not reproduce every ancillary numerical entropy or control experiment.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest prior work. Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [1] already provide the mod-8 forcing results and the two-block avoidance reduction. Chang [2] discusses survivor entropy, maximum-cycle-mean obstructions and periodic exclusion. The published rStation paper [3] already gives a stronger obstruction to unrestricted additive potentials, using positive shadows of the negative-$5$ cycle. Those ingredients should not be counted as new here. The general entropy criterion for arbitrary avoiding sets, its application to this certificate architecture, and the additional exact forcing statements nevertheless form a meaningful contribution beyond those results. The manuscript appropriately treats “closure-new” as relative to one deduction rule, rather than as proof of publication novelty.

The following clarifications would improve the final presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- Restrict “any proof of an open class must use positivity beyond every finite $2$-adic scale” to the certificate architecture actually analyzed. Failure of (P) at every resolution does not exclude arbitrary arithmetic arguments, adaptive return methods, or other uses of finite modular information. The proved conclusion is already useful without this broader claim.
- Expand the attribution around resolution invariance. Chang [2, Remark C.15] discusses invariance under refinement for a particular cylinder-averaged construction; distinguish that precedent from the general exact statement here. Also cite Laarhoven–de Weger [10] for the established connection between dyadic Collatz graphs, de Bruijn graphs and parity conjugacy. Neither source, in the relevant passages checked, supplies the full criterion proved here.
- Qualify the assertion that `k1_certify.py` “decides each” input. Its finite convergent list and the rounding of $\delta$ to multiples of $10^{-7}$ do not implement an unrestricted adaptive procedure for arbitrarily small negative margins. This does not undermine the reported certificates, whose edge inequalities are checked exactly. State its verified scope, or make precision refinement genuinely adaptive. Retaining complete rational potentials would also make certificate reuse easier.
- Set $X_0=1$ explicitly when there are no non-simple components. In the sign lemma, replace “can never be excluded by (Z)” with the precise statement that negative pins never violate (Z): a negative pin in a simple component is indeed excluded as a positive-integer trajectory. Define the shortcut map $T$ before reporting entropy per $T$-step, and remove the abstract's corrupted “hBcstep” unit.
