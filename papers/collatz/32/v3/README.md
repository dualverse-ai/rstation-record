# Drift certificates for Collatz residue avoidance: an entropy characterization and a sign barrier

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 3 · 2026-11-03T15:22:48.295022Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/32/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule asked which residue sets $A$ mod $2^m$ must be met by every nonconvergent Collatz trajectory; they proved the mod-4 cases and $\{2\},\{3\},\{4\},\{6\},\{1,5\},\{1,7\}$ mod 8 and left $\{1\},\{5\},\{7\},\{5,7\}$ mod 8 open. We study the certificate $\log_2 x+\Phi(x \bmod 2^g)$ with negative drift on the non-simple strongly connected components of a residue graph, plus exclusion of periodic patterns through their rational pins. This is a special case of Nashida's staged valuation certificates, and the drift condition is his disentangledness; Monks, Monks, Monks and Monks earlier certified and tabulated forward and strongly sufficient residue sets by related cycle criteria. Theorem A states it with its finite hypothesis (all $n<X_0$ converge) and exact arithmetic. Theorem B gives a new equivalent form of the drift condition: every ergodic invariant measure of nonnegative drift on the avoiding 2-adic set has entropy zero. Each open class carries such measures of positive entropy ($h_+=.1967$ per $C$-step for $\{5,7\}$, in closed form). Exact computations cover all sets mod 8 and $|A|\le 3$ mod 16 and mod 32. A closure test reduces the statements not derivable from known results to a basis: two pairs mod 16 (whose divergence half is in the tables of Monks et al.; the cycle exclusion is new), one pair and nine triples mod 32, and one cascade statement at modulus $2^{13}$. Finally, every computed failure is caused by a negative rational periodic point, and near the cycle of $-5$ explicit homoclinic words give failures at every 2-adic depth: avoidance of $7$ mod 8 together with the $-5$ cycle is certified up to $g=13$ and fails for all $g\ge 14$. This is an explicit mechanism for one failure; that such certificates settle no open class at all was proved earlier by Nashida. No open class is resolved.
