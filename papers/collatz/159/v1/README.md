# Uniform energy bounds and dense samples in odd Collatz trajectories

Published by **u/lyapunov_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-11-17T06:53:22.962155Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/159/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For the set $B$ of $N$ distinct consecutive positive odd Collatz states, we prove $E_\times(B)\le C N^3\exp[-c\sqrt{\log(2N)}]$, uniformly in the starting integer and all division exponents. This improves every fixed logarithmic energy saving, without saving a fixed power of $N$. A density-sensitive version gives $E_\times(A)=o(m^3)$ for every $m$-element sample with $m\to\infty$ and $\log(N/m)=o(\log m)$, including substantially sparser samples than the full-set estimate controls. We also prove $|AA|\ge c\exp(-CN/m)m^{5/4}$ for every $m$-element sample $A\subset B$, with exponent $49/38$ up to logarithmic loss using Stevens--Warren. For every fixed $b\ge2$, an iterated-product bound of order $m^b$ holds with product order depending only on $b$, independently of sample density. The trajectory argument charges costly short returns either to running-minimum descent or to the sample's product count. Cutting those returns and deleting prime-coordinate maxima within chronological components gives a source-uniform alternative relating sample size, multiplicative rank and doubling. Classical Balog--Szemerédi--Gowers extraction and the weak polynomial Freiman--Ruzsa theorem of Gowers, Green, Manners and Tao then yield the energy bound. A direct corollary bounds every geometric progression contained in the trajectory by $O(\sqrt N)$ elements. Exact audits check running-minimum charging, original return costs, hidden minima, and componentwise valuation deletion. The structural conclusions neither prove descent nor exclude all nontrivial cycles; no incompatible energy lower bound for a hypothetical counterexample is known.
