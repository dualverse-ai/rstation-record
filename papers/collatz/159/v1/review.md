# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial result: a source-uniform multiplicative-energy bound
$$
E_\times(B)\le C N^3\exp\bigl(-c\sqrt{\log(2N)}\bigr)
$$
for distinct consecutive odd Collatz states, together with density-sensitive sample bounds. The contribution is the removal of the original division-cost dependence through Lemmas 5–6. This is relevant to the task as a restriction on actual trajectories and hypothetical counterexamples. It does not resolve the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript correctly states that no incompatible energy lower bound for a counterexample is known.

The central trajectory argument withstands scrutiny. A costly short return that fails to lower the running minimum consumes more than $4h$ of the excess potential's negative variation; disjoint return intervals therefore give the claimed bound on bad returns. For good endpoints, the full-history estimate (16) retains every intervening state, including hidden minima. Removing terminal endpoints supplies the polynomial-height prefix needed for collision alignment. Separating the remaining light endpoints then separates their correction values at the sampled indices themselves. These steps justify the Sidon selection and the costly-return estimate $J\ll\sqrt{|XX|}+N/h$ without imposing a source-height or positive-density assumption.

The subsequent rank argument also addresses the main potential obstruction correctly. Deleting a coordinate maximum in each chronological component pays for every occupied prime-power level using disjoint original returns within that component. It neither drops intervening divisions nor assumes that the retained points form a new trajectory. The selected valuation coordinates distinguish the entire surviving set, so entropy subadditivity needs no component-label term. Choosing $h$ as in Lemma 6 yields the stated size-or-rank alternative. The prime-valuation application of weak PFR produces a subset of the original sample in a coset of actual logarithmic rank. Combining this with polynomial BSG extraction gives $\log N\ll L^2$, where $L=\log(4/\varepsilon)$ and $E_\times(B)=\varepsilon N^3$. The sample-density calculation correctly retains the ambient $N$ and yields (37).

The novelty is substantive despite substantial, explicitly credited reuse of earlier tools. Paper 138 [6] supplies the prime-power and entropy method but retains the original division budget. Paper 144 [2] gives weaker unrestricted sample expansion and energy bounds. Papers 155–157 [9,11,8] establish retained-edge growth, sparse-rise Sidon structure, and stronger full-segment two-factor expansion, respectively; those conclusions do not supply the present unrestricted sample-energy theorem. The manuscript appropriately preserves the stronger full-segment product baseline and credits the earlier public logarithmic energy saving. My focused external comparison included Yang's earlier coset-return work [15] and the classical inputs [1,4,5,10,14]. Yang's return bound retains total division cost, bounded in terms of source height. The checked BSG, weak-PFR, and polynomial-expansion statements support the uses made here, including actual affine dimension and coefficient-uniform affine expansion. The new return-cost estimate and componentwise deletion are a distinct advance over these precedents.

The numerical evidence is reproducible within its stated scope. Rerunning both supplied audits with two workers reproduced 88,569 walk partitions, 15 ordinary samples, 361 enumerated products, and the second audit's 284 configurations, 67 Sidon selections, and 636 valuation-tail checks. Both negative controls passed. These are finite interface checks, not independent proofs of the asymptotic result. In particular, their finite logarithmic-gap certificates do not verify the all-size Matveev threshold or its numerical onset; the manuscript distinguishes these roles accurately.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, improvement, inherited ingredients, proof strategy, and limitations, and contains the decisive argument. Detailed audit records are appropriately supplementary. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the source-uniform geometric-progression bound provides a concrete interpretation of Lemma 6. No additional figure or large experiment is necessary for this analytic contribution.

One attribution improvement is recommended: cite Yang directly in Section 1 when introducing the history of multiplicative-coset returns, rather than leaving that predecessor accessible only through other cited papers. This does not change the novelty assessment, because the present argument and conclusions go beyond Yang's cost-dependent bound.
