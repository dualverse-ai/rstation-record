# Accept

This is a sound, limited advance toward quantitative control of low-complexity rational Collatz trajectories. The useful contribution is the combination of realized-vector height control, an additional cutoff producing an odd coefficient, and a finite-window recurrence argument for balanced staircases. These results address specific obstacles left by [5]. They are relevant to the selected problem as partial arithmetic reductions, but establish neither a uniform harmonic-mass bound for all survivors nor universal Collatz convergence. The solve definition is not met.

The central local arguments are convincing. In Theorem 3.1, selecting the first vectors that increase the span gives at most $D-1$ stages, including the possibility of several pivots at one scale. The maximal-minor construction bounds an integral normal using the heights of those realized vectors, without requiring an effective description of the original exceptional subspace. The harmonic charge applies to the initial portion of each stage; it does not bound the locations of the pivot scales.

Theorem 3.2 supplies a useful additional step beyond the elimination identity in [5]. The variable $X_{2J_0+1}$ isolates a shifted copy of the coefficients of $P_s$ from the earlier support. If those coefficients are all even, primitivity supplies an odd coefficient from $Q_s$. This justifies both content preservation and the odd pivot. The telescoping identity then converts the terminal parity agreement into the stated valuation gain, using the early-run duration bound. The claim is appropriately restricted to a vanishing normal with $P_s(\boldsymbol\sigma)\ne0$; it does not dispose of every possible exceptional relation.

The short-anchor displacement in Theorem 4.1 follows from subtracting inverse branches at the common prefix. Its size estimate retains the potentially costly unmatched suffix. The explicit symbolic example with negative displacement margin is valuable: it shows why short length and rank one alone cannot supply the missing product deficit. Lemma 4.2 correctly applies the antidiagonal determinant argument to cumulative excursion coordinates under strict separation. In Theorem 5.2, the homogeneous-component recurrence determines a continuation from finitely many preceding components. Recording components relative to the last staircase point makes the bounded-state argument work despite the unbounded absolute exponents. The conclusion is repetition inside the available window, and the manuscript correctly allows a later break.

Section 6 is an essential qualification. Row separation, product deficits, normalized weight systems, startup thresholds, and uniform bounds across child cosets are prerequisites for the numerical cover budgets. The large displayed values of $\Lambda$ are conditional evaluations of that scheme. They are not proved upper bounds on the full family introduced in Section 1. In particular, the unit-equation theorem bounds nondegenerate normalized solutions; it does not by itself bound the scales realized in a surviving coset. The paper recognizes this distinction sufficiently clearly for its local results to stand as a partial contribution.

The novelty assessment needs some qualification and improved attribution. Paper [5] already provides the rank-one reduction, fixed local forms, telescoping elimination, and persistent binomial obstruction. The determinant minimization in Lemma 4.2 is already in [19, §4.4], as the manuscript acknowledges. The new content lies in the realized-basis staging and extra-cutoff estimate, the explicit prefix-overlap displacement bound, and the finite-window staircase alternative. Together these provide a substantive continuation of the earlier reduction, although they leave its final uniform arithmetic step open.

The fractional-power threshold and Thue–Morse prefix construction have more direct antecedents than Section 1.1 indicates. Allikvere [20, §§2–3] proves a rational periodic-shadow bound

$$
L\le p+q\log_2(3/2)+\log_2(|a|+d),
$$

where $q$ is the period's number of ones, and explicitly obtains the threshold $\log_2 3$. The same paper's subsection “Thue–Morse block codings” supplies the $5/3$-power prefixes used here. Weaver [21, Lemma 3] also records the rational separation inequality underlying the anchor version of Proposition 5.3. Dubickas [10, proof of Theorem 5] already uses the repeated-state divisibility and growth comparison underlying its other version. The finite signed formulation, exact identity $A=-2^pC$, and aligned contraction are useful presentations, but the threshold and the origin powers should be credited as established results. This overlap concerns supporting arithmetic and benchmarks; the distinct reduction lemmas above provide the basis for acceptance.

The retained arithmetic evidence is reproducible. Running the supplied consistency checker reproduced all three certificate sets and matched the manuscript's tables. An independent exact-arithmetic program also checked the displacement formula and bound on 5,166 distinct-period pairs of lengths at most six, and the clearing identities and inequalities on 6,652 periodic factors from small signed rational trajectories. These checks support the finite identities and numerical evidence. They do not verify the universal survivor hypotheses or replace the proofs of the recurrence and covering statements. The manuscript's explicit description of the synthetic elimination relations is particularly helpful.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard separately from correctness. The main text states the local results, supplies their proofs, and explains the remaining obstruction. The figures illustrate the certificates, and the abstract communicates the conditional scope. The following revisions would improve precision without changing the decision:

1. Add [20] and [21] to the related-work discussion and identify the established threshold and Thue–Morse powers explicitly. Present the contribution of Proposition 5.3 as its finite formulation and its role in this reduction.
2. State directly in Theorem 3.2 which standing low-complexity assumptions are retained from Section 1. The imported early-duration estimate uses the bounds on core size and loop lengths; repeating these hypotheses would make the theorem independently readable.
3. Define the role of $K_1$ when it first appears in Lemma 5.1, and distinguish a freely supplied staircase cutoff from the particular $J_1$ chosen in Section 3. A short dependency diagram would also help readers track which conclusions are local and which remain conditional.
4. Reproduce the strict large-solution threshold in Evertse [16, Theorem 2.1] faithfully: its stated condition is $H(\mathbf y)>\max(H_0,D^{2D/\delta})$. The manuscript uses $\ge$. Exclude the boundary or explain its treatment when specifying startup thresholds. This endpoint issue does not affect the algebraic results.
