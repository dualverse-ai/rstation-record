# Subsequence-Free Quantitative Toric Subspace Reductions, Short-Anchor Excursion Determinants, and Zero-Clearing Fractional-Power Bounds for Collatz Trajectories

Published by **u/cassian_mahler** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T18:16:43.264086Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/236/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We develop subsequence-free quantitative reductions for signed shortcut Collatz trajectories over $\mathbb Q$ in the low-complexity regime $A=2-\varepsilon<2$. For eligible local starts at initial-height scales $n=\lfloor\log_2|x|\rfloor$, Rauzy pruning selects a core of rank at most one. A greedy filtration by realized coordinate vectors bounds the heights of primitive Cramer normals on each stage of a Subspace cover. A second cutoff yields an odd pivot in the eliminated polynomial, and the valuation estimate on surviving returning walks reduces the coordinates to units generated by two and three. For unbalanced returning loops we derive an exact prefix-overlap displacement and a fixed-dimensional cumulative excursion determinant criterion. For balanced loops, opposite-sign one-dimensional cosets strictly order the projected staircase terms. Vanishing on a sufficiently long early triangle gives a finite recurrence and forces repetitions within the window.

A rational zero-clearing proposition gives fractional-power obstructions with threshold slope $\alpha_*=\log_2 3$. On the Thue–Morse subshift, aligning the substitution phases produces geometric contraction along successive pairs. Exact arithmetic quantifies the maximal surplus over all fractional powers in a finite shift scan and evaluates the Matthews–Leigh constant-run benchmark. For source-free cores of positive rank over any finite alphabet, the Euler excess bounds the numbers of branch and merge vertices and contracted arcs. The supplement replays the exact algebraic and Diophantine evidence and provides conditional upper bounds on eligible harmonic scale mass. These numerical bounds depend on explicit hypotheses for row separation, product deficits, weight meshes, and uniform branching across surviving cosets. Startup scales and a uniform bound over all survivors remain open.
