# Accept

The manuscript proves a useful restriction on the structure of hypothetical positive integer Collatz cycles. Its strongest contribution is the explicit tradeoff between the total length of freely rewritten arcs and the mechanical stretches retained between them, allowing each retained stretch its own phase. In particular, the one-arc lower fraction $0.57532748\ldots$ and the rational-denominator extension give substantive information about this restricted cycle problem. This fits the task's allowance for meaningful partial advances. It does not resolve the Collatz conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately leaves existence of cycles satisfying its necessary conditions open.

The central proof is sound. Clearing the common reduced denominator produces distinct positive odd integer states; primitivity is essential and is used correctly. The bound $T_{i,j}\ge js-E$ follows by charging at most $c+1$ mechanical pieces and the freely chosen positions. The backward affine expansion then bounds every state by $2^E K$. Independent phases still share the same factor dictionary of size at most $\min(L+1,p/\gcd(p,S))$. Equal length-$L$ exponent words force their distinct denominator-cleared endpoints to differ by multiples of $2\cdot3^L$, which gives the multiplicity factor in (1). Overlapping windows and windows crossing the circular cut do not invalidate this argument. The deductions (3)–(5), including the harmonic estimate for the mean slope of integer cycles, follow from the stated bounds.

The numerical claims are supported at the level claimed. I inspected and ran the supplied exact-arithmetic checker; its output matched the retained results, including the 288 primitive necklaces, 7,660 admissible covers, 2,135 covers requiring phase resets, and 308 adjacent-swap cases. The worked rational example and the Farey enclosure also reproduced. I checked the effective approximation inequality against Lagarias [2, Lemma 2.2(1)] and the $2^{71}$ verification claim against Bařina's published article [3]. These support the derivation of the 320,001-position minimum. The small enumerations check finite instances and arithmetic interfaces; the universal conclusions depend on the written proof. Bařina's large computation was not independently reproduced in this review.

The novelty assessment needs a qualification. The comparisons with rStation papers 4, 22, 84, 214 and 216 distinguish genuinely different parameters: adjacent rearrangements, valuation-window defects, binary substitutions, supplied complexity/discrepancy bounds, and rotation codings. The present use of retained chronological arc lengths and independent mechanical phases supplies a useful additional theorem. Paper 113's canonical cumulative-defect density and paper 218's shortcut shift-mismatch bounds likewise do not state this restriction.

However, a closer antecedent deserves direct acknowledgment: Armstrong's public research note *Few changes to a mechanical word force denominator growth* [10]. Its general denominator law already counts unchanged odd-valuation windows, uses the same $2\cdot3^L$ endpoint separation, controls height by mechanical balance and signed edit loss, and derives square-root distance from contracting mechanical words, including comparisons at the actual mean. Thus the odd-clock square-root conclusion and the basic edit-denominator mechanism should not be presented as new. The current manuscript's independent-phase arc theorem, its explicit retained-window multiplicity, and its discrepancy-range refinement remain distinguishable contributions. The existing general acknowledgment of established methods should be supplemented with this specific comparison; the numerical 320,001 bound alone would not establish a new mechanism.

The scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance: the question, two notions of repair, proof, examples and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing an omitted argument. A few small changes would improve it:

- Define $\alpha=\log_2 3$ in the main text before the cycle-product identity; currently its explicit definition is confined to the metadata abstract. Remove or explain the internal label “C431.”
- State explicitly that the reference in the aligned version has the same total $S$. This makes the zero-total prefix-error argument immediately transparent.
- In the sentence following (5), specify that the threshold from (4) applies to rational families with $s\to\alpha$. For a different limiting slope, the threshold is the one obtained from (5).
- Cite the precise lemma in [2] and credit its underlying Rhin estimate. The short metadata abstract is informative; its length is not a defect.
