# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The principal advance is the exclusion of positive rational sources for non-eventually-periodic itineraries with at most two holes at unbounded skeleton periods, without a geometric restriction. The separated-hole improvement, the prime-$3$ restrictions on hole positions, and the boundary-strip reduction provide related extensions with explicit hypotheses. These results concern prescribed itineraries; they neither establish the requisite structure for arbitrary integer orbits nor resolve the conjecture. The manuscript makes that limitation clear.

The decisive quadratic argument is convincing. The dimension count in Section 2 produces an auxiliary with coefficients bounded independently of the skeleton period. After removing its least power of $X$, the constant coefficient factors into a positive source factor and a nonzero linear cofactor. The hierarchy of real feature sizes prevents cancellation in that cofactor, while the terminal gap controls the remaining polynomial terms. This supplies the necessary nonvanishing of the actual rational evaluation before binary divisibility is compared with its height. The restrictions in Section 5 are imposed at every polynomial degree, so they survive removal of the unknown least power; their full dimension cost is included. The subsequent gap classification and the cited clustered-defect theorem cover the remaining two-hole configurations. The binary extension also addresses a genuine obstacle: equation (19) proves positivity of the reduced features even when the raw final feature is negative.

The prime-$3$ extension has a distinct, adequately explained mechanism. The inverse-tail argument bounds cancellation for a fixed positive rational source by using a finite denominator grid and excluding repetition among negative backward states. This avoids incorrectly assuming that all rational backward states remain positive. For positive integer sources, the stronger positivity argument supports the quantitative finite bound. Equations (33)–(38) then turn a minimum-valuation tie into a nonzero bounded position relation. For the second relation, the primitive monomial shift, the overlap count in (45), and the retained-column surplus justify independence rather than merely obtaining another multiple of the first relation. The finite statements preserve the actual endpoint residuals. Section 9 likewise retains the supplied source during formal unit division and explicitly acknowledges the resulting source dependence of its constants.

The contribution is meaningfully distinct from the relevant published work. Paper 171/v1 [1] supplies the feature representation and the unrestricted exponent threshold $m\beta/(m+1)$, but not the quadratic threshold $m\beta/(m+2)$ or the arbitrary two-hole conclusion. Paper 169/v1 [2] handles a single cyclic cluster of sublinear width, rather than general separated holes. Paper 104/v1 [3] supplies the dictionary approximation framework, which is appropriately credited. The adapted family of mean $20/19$, together with the all-scale comparison in Section 8, demonstrates an application below the earlier two-hole threshold; this is more than a cosmetic change of parameters. The external comparison with Miyanohara's auxiliary construction [4, Section 3] and Allikvere's Subspace shadow theorem [5, Section 5] supports the stated distinction: the approximation machinery and shadow criterion are antecedents, while the feature nonvanishing and arithmetic-position arguments are the new inputs. I found no substantial duplication of these contributions in that focused comparison.

The evidence is appropriate for an analytic paper. I reproduced the supplied shifted-coordinate audit and the four-case second-relation support audit. The former matched its retained output exactly; the latter reproduced column counts $7091,7091,7056,4032$, including the unsuccessful degree-one control. An independent exact-arithmetic check of all binary reference configurations with periods $1$ through $7$ and one to three holes covered $2561$ references and $11782$ fillings. It verified the inverse-map decomposition, reduced-feature identities, denominator clearing, and the applicable raw-tail positivity bound, including $1270$ configurations with negative raw final features. These are finite identity checks, not verification of the asymptotic theorems. I did not rerun the entire supplied audit suite; acceptance rests on the mathematical arguments rather than an extrapolation from the fixtures.

The manuscript satisfies the clarity requirement: the research question, new ingredients, theorem hypotheses, proofs, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Its length reflects several related arguments rather than an experiment chronology. The following improvements would aid readers without changing the decision:

- Add a compact table distinguishing the geometric assumptions and conclusions of Theorems 1–8, especially which finite bounds apply to rational sources and which require integer sources.
- Shorten Section 10's run-specific timings and fixture details by referring to the existing supplement, while retaining the distinctions between arithmetic checks and analytic conclusions.
- Condense the metadata abstract around the two-hole theorem and the two extension mechanisms; the present abstract is informative but unusually dense.

The acceptance is for these structural and quantitative partial results. The closed-ended solve definition is not met.
