# Shift mismatches of Collatz cycle parity words on the shortcut clock

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 3 · 2026-11-24T22:12:44.120777Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/218/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We bound the denominator $q$ of a rational Collatz cycle from below by the mismatch count of any shift of its shortcut parity word. This extends paper 216, which treats unit shifts with at most four mismatches, to every shift and every mismatch count. Let $w$ be a primitive nonconstant parity word of period $n$ with $2^n>3^u$, and let a shift $h$ have $s$ mismatches, $g=\gcd(h,n)$ and $M=n/g\ge s+1$. We prove a lower bound for $\log_2q$ linear in $g\lfloor(M-1)/s\rfloor$, with error terms in $\log n$, the continued fraction of $(h/g)^{-1}$ mod $M$, and the cycle minimum. For $k$-interval unit codings this gives $\log_2q\ge c_kn-O_k(\log n)$ with $c_k=1/(2k(1+8k\alpha H_{2k}))$, where $\alpha=\log_23$ and $H_{2k}=\sum_{i\le2k}\alpha^i$. For integer cycles it gives thresholds $n^*(s)$, certified by interval arithmetic for even $s\le200$: if $n\ge n^*(s)$, every shift with $M\ge s+1$ has more than $s$ mismatches. With the Hercher-Bařina cycle-length bound, the parity word of a nontrivial cycle of positive integers has at least $\min(24,M(h))$ mismatches at every shift $h$, and at least $\min(110,M(h))$ if $n>2^{100}$. The proof combines factor and Denjoy-Koksma height bounds in the residue classes mod $g$ with a shortcut-clock transfer of Armstrong's repetition argument. The forced count grows like $\log_\alpha n$ at unit shifts, and at all shifts for $n$ up to $1.7\cdot10^{49}$. At unit shifts with small inverse step this is not new: at $h=\pm1$ the count is $2m$, with $m$ the number of local minima, a unit shift with inverse step $\bar a$ has at least $2m/\bar a$ mismatches, and the $m$-cycle bounds of Hercher, paper 206 and Simons-de Weger give more. The new content is at unit shifts with $\bar a\ge16$ (resp. $\ge4$ for the bound 110), at nonunit shifts, and the rational-cycle bound. Nothing here excludes a cycle.
