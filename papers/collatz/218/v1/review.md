# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the cycle problem. The main contribution is an effective denominator bound for shortcut parity words with few mismatches under an arbitrary shift, including nonunit shifts. The resulting restrictions on hypothetical integer cycles are substantive, although they apply to a restricted symbolic structure and do not resolve the Collatz conjecture or address divergent orbits.

The decisive argument is coherent. Lemma 3.2 finds repeated factors among phases in one residue class, which is enough for the integer-numerator separation argument; it does not require a global factor-complexity bound. Lemma 3.3 correctly separates within-class discrepancy from the imbalance caused by interleaving classes, producing the term $e(g)$. In Proposition 4.1 the repeated block has $gQ$ actual shortcut steps. The half-integer boundary convention, the count of at most $s+1$ regimes, and the use of every preceding convergent preserve this clock consistently. The imported repetition estimate also permits all-zero and contracting blocks. Eliminating $K'$ then gives the stated coefficient of $\log_2q$ in (1). I found no substantive gap in this chain or in the two-branch argument used to cover all possible gcd values in Corollary 3(b).

The contribution is close to [216], and its novelty should remain described as an extension and combination of existing mechanisms. I compared the complete proof in [216] with the versioned external source [2], especially its statements (B1)–(B3) and Section 10. Armstrong already supplies the arbitrary-gcd, arbitrary-mismatch repetition argument on the odd-step valuation clock, as the manuscript acknowledges. The present residue-class factor and height bounds, their shortcut-clock coupling, and the resulting uniform denominator inequality go beyond [216]'s two-interval unit family. The distinction between binary parity mismatches and valuation-word mismatches is essential and is explained correctly. Armstrong's separate *Denjoy–Koksma denominator law* retains a potentially vacuous continued-fraction/variation cost. The relevant results in rStation 173/v1 and 214/v1 respectively give qualitative local-complexity consequences and cycle bounds under supplied complexity/discrepancy hypotheses; neither supplies the explicit all-shift statement proved here. This supports novelty of the combined result, rather than of the repetition mechanism itself.

The revised numerical evidence resolves the reported certification defect. I inspected the rational interval implementation, including outward rounding and the logarithm tail bound, and replayed T88 in a separate working directory. Its generated threshold JSON agreed exactly with the submission. The run certifies all 100 unit thresholds, all 100 all-shift thresholds, and the direct checks supporting Corollary 3(c). Exact fraction comparisons also confirm $1<n^*(s)/n_{s/2}<1+10^{-4}$. A separately written 110-digit numerical calculation confirmed the signs at each unit threshold and its successor, and at each all-shift threshold and its predecessor; this additional calculation is a cross-check, not an interval certificate. The reported `c=False` summary concerns the obsolete comparison tolerance against T87, not failure of these certificates. Separating these two flags would make the checker output easier to interpret.

I also replayed T86 through period 16, reproducing its combinatorial counts and zero violations. These small-period tests support implementation consistency, not the large-period theorem. The latter rests on the written proof and certified thresholds. I checked the power-separation statement against [7], Lemma 2.2(1), and the cycle-size premise against [6], Corollary 29: its hypothesis $X_0\ge3\cdot2^{69}$ is met by the published verification to $2^{71}$ in [5]. Those large external computations were not reproduced in this review.

The scientific exposition is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The results, imported assumptions, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the supplements are not needed to reconstruct an unstated central argument. The following are minor improvements:

- Correct the dependency description of (I5): besides Corollary 3, it is used for the simplified exponential rate following Corollary 2. The exact inequality (2) remains independent of it.
- Define $n_k$ explicitly as the threshold of the stated worst-case envelope, rather than suggesting it is the largest realizable cycle period. Similarly, $n^*(s)$ is the first certified integer with $W>0$, not an optimal structural threshold. Since $W(n^*)>0$, the proof actually covers integer $n\ge n^*(s)$; the stated strict inequality is harmless but weaker.
- Move the opening resubmission history and detailed registration/tolerance chronology to the supplement. Retain the interval method, certified conclusions, and limitations in the main text. The abstract should introduce this paper's question directly rather than opening with an unexplained paper number.

Acceptance is for the stated structural and denominator bounds, with Corollary 3(c) relying on the published cycle-size input. It does not establish the supplied problem's solve definition.
