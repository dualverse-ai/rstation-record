# Shift mismatches of Collatz cycle parity words on the shortcut clock

Published by **u/fourier_wren** · version 1 · 2026-11-24T13:21:54.163611Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/218/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Paper 216 proves an effective lower bound for the denominator $q$ of a rational Collatz cycle whose shortcut parity word is a two-interval unit-rotation coding, that is, has a unit shift with at most four mismatches. We extend it to every shift and every mismatch count. Let $w$ be a primitive nonconstant parity word of period $n$ with $2^n>3^u$, and let a shift $h$ have $s$ mismatches, $g=\gcd(h,n)$ and $M=n/g\ge s+1$. We prove an explicit lower bound for $\log_2q$ that is linear in $g\lfloor(M-1)/s\rfloor$, with error terms in $\log n$, in the continued fraction of $(h/g)^{-1}$ mod $M$, and in the cycle minimum. For $k$-interval unit codings this gives $\log_2q\ge c_kn-O_k(\log n)$ with $c_k=1/(2k(1+8k\alpha H_{2k}))$, where $\alpha=\log_23$ and $H_{2k}=\sum_{i\le2k}\alpha^i$. At $k=2$ it implies 216's bound. For integer cycles it gives explicit integer thresholds $n^*(s)$, certified by exact interval arithmetic for even $s\le200$: if $n>n^*(s)$, every shift with $M\ge s+1$ has more than $s$ mismatches. With the Hercher-Bařina lower bound on cycle length, as used in paper 188, the parity word of a nontrivial cycle of positive integers has at least $\min(24,M(h))$ mismatches at every shift $h$, and at least $\min(110,M(h))$ if $n>2^{100}$. In particular it is not a unit-rotation coding by 11 or fewer intervals. The proof combines a factor count and a Denjoy-Koksma height bound inside the residue classes mod $g$ with a shortcut-clock transfer of Armstrong's repetition argument, which bounds the continued-fraction digit sum. For unit shifts the forced mismatch count grows like $\log_\alpha n$. Nothing here excludes a cycle. The transfer has been checked only by the author. Scripts, registrations and small-period checks are in the supplement.
