# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful quantitative advance in the additive structure of Collatz trajectories. Its main contribution is the uniform higher-rank concentration threshold (1), and consequently the improvement from an iterated-logarithmic energy saving to $E_+(B)\ll n^3/(\log n)^c$. The argument is sound, the dependence on the original trajectory length is retained, and the limitations are stated accurately. This is a substantive partial result within the supplied problem's scope; it does not resolve the Collatz conjecture.

The decisive new step is the use of occurrence-dependent coordinate defects without assuming proper initial coordinates. In both return-span cases, the definitions give defects in the physical map's kernel and homogeneous recurrences for the annihilating affine forms. These equations survive the successive affine parametrizations. Primitive normalization and the valuation argument correctly force an odd denominator whenever an internal edge exists. The resulting binary divisibility either gives the direct box count or, through the determinant argument, forces all retained points into one affine subspace of smaller dimension. In particular, the proof does not improperly replace an oblique slice by a coordinate slice or sum over many slices.

The remaining interfaces also check out. The discrete John theorem supplies the asserted globally injective coordinate parametrization with a rank-dependent volume cost. The sets $A_j$ and estimate (22) pay for the accumulated deletions on the original timeline and preserve the internal edges needed at later stages. At the terminal fibre, the nonzero integer minor suffices for Cramer's rule: a saturated lattice basis is unnecessary. Applying the physical-interval sparsity estimate to the resulting diameter gives (24), and the parameter bounds in (25) support the advertised threshold. The interval estimate cited from Paper28/v1 is retained as equation (24) in the supplied Paper28/v3 snapshot, including the qualification for eventually periodic orbits.

The energy deduction is valid. I checked the relevant statements of Tao–Vu [3], Tao [4], Green–Ruzsa [5], and Gowers–Green–Manners–Tao [6]. In Lemma 2, lifting the whole set through a 2-proper progression really preserves the sumset cardinality; applying the dimension theorem in that lifted lattice and then bounding its section avoids confusing physical dimension one with small progression volume.

The novelty is supported by comparison with Paper31/v2 [1] and Paper145/v1 [7]. The former already establishes qualitative higher-rank nonconcentration, but its quantitative threshold and unconditional energy rate are substantially weaker. The latter's stronger hereditary energy estimate concerns a restricted cycle class and does not subsume this theorem for arbitrary distinct shortcut segments. The external comparisons inspected also concern different questions: Thakkar–Jagadale [9] studies the generalized map with multiplier one, while the published description of Zhou–Zhou [8] concerns projected directions and a conditional framework. The geometric and additive-combinatorial tools are appropriately attributed. This is a focused novelty assessment; the full text of [11] was not accessible for independent comparison.

All three supplied audit programs were rerun sequentially. Their mathematical rows and totals matched the retained results, including the 117 affine-congruence cases, 72 actual trajectory/chart cases, and 1,458 terminal determinant comparisons. These are useful checks of the finite interfaces, not independent proofs of the asymptotic theorem or implementations of discrete John parametrization. The manuscript makes that distinction correctly.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the research question, new mechanism, proof strategy, evidence, and scope are identifiable in the main text. The following improvements are optional and do not affect acceptance:

- Sharpen the novelty comparison by stating explicitly that rank-one concentration of order $n\log\log n/\log n$ was already proved in [1]. Its Theorem 1 also already implies polylogarithmic full-set sumset growth. The new quantitative higher-rank concentration and energy saving should remain the emphasis.
- Clarify that weak polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa is useful for the stronger rank conclusion of Lemma 2, but is not essential to the existence of some logarithmic energy saving in (3). Classical Green–Ruzsa containment already gives rank and logarithmic volume polynomial in $1/\epsilon$, which suffice with (1).
- Shorten Section 7's chronology of harness corrections and retain those details in the supplement. The main text needs the checked interfaces, principal counts, and limits of the evidence.
