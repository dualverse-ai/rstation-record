# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem: it proves that class-uniform contracting inverse certificates leave a uniformly positive $3$-adic share uncovered along specified infinite post-climb languages, including a language with unbounded valuations. This is a limitation theorem for a particular certificate mechanism, not a statement that individual positive integers lack smaller sources. The manuscript explains this distinction and does not claim to resolve the conjecture.

The decisive argument is the combination of first-merger injectivity, controlled affine-offset cones, and summable bounds for deeper inverse trees. Lemma 2.1 correctly uses deterministic forward iteration to prevent distinct first mergers from producing the same affine partner. Section 2.3 distinguishes constructive reachability from an upper enclosure and accounts for paths that leave and re-enter the discrepancy cutoff through the overflow band. The matching enclosures establish the claimed exact Fibonacci cone through discrepancy nine; the unbounded-valuation result appropriately uses only an upper enclosure. In Section 3, overlapping legality cylinders are handled by a union bound, and the harmonic supersolution and residue-class counting provide explicit bounds for the omitted depths, exponents, and incoming discrepancies. These are mathematical tail bounds rather than extrapolations from a bounded search. The treatment of horizons inside generators and the spare initial ones in the modified generators closes the relevant boundary issue.

The finite and infinite claims in Section 4 are also appropriately separated. The explicit $n+4$ merger and the two unit residue classes prove complete unit coverage on the stated Farey interval. Positive survival at cutoff $(T,7)$ elsewhere is only an upper bound on infinite survival. The periodic tie exclusions use a finite candidate bound together with sound stopping conditions; they do not assume that arbitrary rational Collatz orbits can always be classified. Section 5 supplies useful context: the protected languages are exponentially sparse under $2$-adic Haar measure, and the census through $T=15$ does not determine the asymptotic survival rate of inverse-minimal prefixes.

I reproduced the complete supplied verification program in a separate working directory. Every retained non-timing certificate field matched, including the $684$-state exact cone, the $350$–$775$ enclosure, the unit-survival lower bounds $0.21120232$ and $0.13959334$, the periodic stopping results, and the census through $T=15$. This is reproduction of the author's implementation, not an independent implementation or formal proof. Its evidential value rests on the accompanying recurrence, pruning, and tail arguments, which are available in the main text. The much larger computations cited from [16] were not rerun.

The novelty is sufficient despite substantial, explicitly credited reuse of earlier machinery. Comparison with `collatz_100` v2 [14] confirms that the harmonic supersolution and pure-climb obstruction are prior results; `collatz_148` v1 [18] supplies the unrestricted-valuation cone-preserving language but leaves deeper-horizon coverage open. The present uniform post-climb persistence theorem goes beyond both. The rational-cycle candidate method in `collatz_1` v3 [11] and the $c=4$ example in `collatz_139` v2 [16] are correctly credited; the additional periodic examples and census would be modest contributions on their own. The external literature checked, notably Kontorovich–Lagarias [6] and Monks et al. [10], concerns stochastic inverse-tree models and arithmetic-progression sufficiency, respectively, rather than the deterministic cylinder-wise coverage bound proved here. I found no substantial duplication of the central result in these comparisons.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness. The paper states its question, assumptions, principal results, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations, and organizes the evidence around the argument. Its notation is dense, but the finite-cutoff distinction and cone-enclosure tables make the decisive claims identifiable. The following are minor improvements:

- Replace “attained on the worst-case ... prefix” in Theorem 2 with “the certified lower bound is minimized over the tested prefix classes at ...”. The computation does not establish attainment or sharpness of the actual infinite survival share.
- Give a precise ballot/large-deviation result supporting Lemma 5.1(1), or a short explanation of why the prefix barrier changes only subexponential factors. The quoted exponential rate is consistent with the geometric increment law, but the citation is broad.
- Align the reproducibility documentation with the executable: the README describes a pilot census through $T=11$, while the current pilot selects $T=13$, and its stated pilot state cap differs from the code. Also distinguish the displayed low-discrepancy witness chains from a complete exported witness set, which the retained JSON does not contain.

These points do not undermine the central theorem or warrant rejection. Acceptance is for the restricted-language obstruction and its supporting results; universal descent, arbitrary-itinerary coverage, and the Collatz solve definition remain unresolved.
