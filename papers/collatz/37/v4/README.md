# Almost linear valuation budgets for pointwise modular routing in Collatz inverse trees

For every $\varepsilon>0$, every positive odd integer $x$ prime to three can reach every unit class modulo $3^k$ by legal Collatz inverse steps with total valuation at most $C_\varepsilon(k+1)^{1+\varepsilon}$ and maximum individual valuation at most $(3/2)(1+\varepsilon)\log_2(k+1)+C_\varepsilon$. The constants are independent of the size of $x$, and all intermediate states remain positive odd units. A word of common length $b=O_\varepsilon(k^{1+\varepsilon})$ and total exactly $2b$ suffices.

The main analytic step observes a geometric Syracuse word at ternary depth exceeding its length by a sublinear number of digits. We retain Tao's full conductor triangle geometry while stopping the marked renewal discount product early. An induction with a base proportional to the omitted strip gives arbitrary-power primitive decay, including bounded factors of the total valuation. A previously established finer-modulus collision bound then gives joint fine-scale mixing at the extra depth.

The final-tail comparison in the selected fixed-total assembly has enough arithmetic depth margin to use this estimate. Disjoint total slices control cap rejection after central normalization, preserving bounded real offsets and a logarithmic cap. A bounded initial exponent spreads the floor to all unit beta types. Prescribing the fine beta residue reconstructs the required route from the actual supplied integer.

Companion polynomial bounds retain an exact $3/2$ leading coefficient in the individual cap, with source-size dependence in the total. Their patch and Haar audits are preserved. The new direct argument uses written renewal and joint-mixing inputs; elementary selected geometry and fixed-total assembly estimates are reproduced. Constants remain uncomputed, route cost is not search runtime, and neither original-source descent nor Collatz convergence follows.
