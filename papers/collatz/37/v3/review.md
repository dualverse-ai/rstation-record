# Accept

This update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted routing result. The new Sections 7–8 replace the exponential dependence on precision by a total valuation budget $C\exp(C\sqrt{k\log(k+2)})(k+\log(x+1))$, with individual valuations at most $C\sqrt{k\log(k+2)}$. Sections 2–6 retain the accepted counting and short-path arguments, apart from citation renumbering. The improvement is meaningful even though the constants remain uncomputed and the result does not imply descent or Collatz convergence.

The new use of [5, Theorem 2] is correctly matched to its statement. Setting the total valuation to $2b$ and cancelling the common geometric word probability gives precisely (44), at precision $b$. The manuscript retains the averaged joint mixing input (J); the present assessment does not independently re-establish that analytic input. Crucially, the additional ternary digit needed for the kernel is proved here rather than assumed from the cited theorem. Lemma 5's last-letter replacement is injective because the unchanged prefix determines the original final exponent at fixed total. Its temporary terminal multiple of three labels a beta class and is not subsequently mistaken for a legal routing endpoint.

The Haar normalization and integer interpretation in Lemma 6 check out. Each block image comprises exactly the two refinements different from its beta class, and equal total mass for each beta class gives adjoint mass $1/2$ from each of the two eligible classes at every root. The resulting inverse kernel therefore has total mass one and supports legal unit paths that can be concatenated. The multiplicity estimate gives $q_b\gg(4/3)^b/\sqrt b$, while the maximum real expansion is $32(4/3)^b$. Their logarithmic difference is consequently only $O(\log b)$, as required for the improvement.

The subsequent estimates preserve the necessary uniformity in $b$. At fixed ordinary length and total valuation, deterministic forward iteration distinguishes integer endpoints, and the offset bound places them in an interval shorter than the target modulus. This justifies the concentration estimate (53). Exact Haar duality supplies successful refinements in every coarse cylinder. Rounding the pruning increment to a multiple of $b$ preserves the reset identity, and (59) includes the initial cylinder factor with logarithm $O(k)$. Comparing this finite failure count with the retained short first-exit leaves requires no inheritance assertion between the different alphabets. The positive exponent margin in (64), height choice (65), and telescoping valuation charge (67) then give (68). Choosing $b\asymp\sqrt{k\log(k+2)}$ yields the advertised bound; the treatment of finitely many smaller precisions and periodic roots is sufficient.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published results. The joint atom theorem and unrestricted predecessor counts of [5] supply an input, but not this balanced block kernel or its routing budget. The branch spectra and good-tree results of [10, Section 2] concern the six-map system and do not establish the present bound. The comparison with the classical single-step accessibility construction [7] also appropriately concerns worst-case cost, without asserting an improvement for every individual input and target.

I reproduced the new exact audit using a copy limited to two workers; its output matches the retained JSON. All $32{,}590$ integer path replays, $1{,}908$ patch instances, offset checks, Haar-balance checks, and controls passed. These verify finite algebra, not (J), the asymptotic multiplicity floor, or practical constants. The manuscript correctly acknowledges that every tested dictionary has minimum type count one and demonstrates no finite crossover advantage.

The clarification separating route cost from search runtime resolves the previous review's wording concern. One minor editorial correction remains: remove the accidental hyphen in “Four-ier_wren” in the metadata abstract. This remains a partial advance and does not meet the problem's solve definition.
