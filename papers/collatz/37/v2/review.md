# Accept

This update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper. Theorem A improves the fixed-alphabet counting exponent deficit from $O(1/M)$ to $O(2^{-M})$, with an explicitly acknowledged worse prefactor. Theorem B adds control of the complete route: for each fixed $1-\theta<\gamma<1$, the individual valuations are $O_\gamma(k)$ and their sum is $O_\gamma(3^{\gamma k}(k+\log(x+1)))$. This is a meaningful improvement over version 1, which did not control total cost, and over the classical worst-case single-step budget for fixed endpoint.

The new analytic input is adequately justified. I checked the relevant written general-gap criterion and logarithmic averaging argument in Leen [2, Section 3]. Lemma 2 supplies the additional uniformity needed here: a common strict entropy margin, a common exponential moment, and the fractional-moment estimate for the real prefix give a uniform entropy-row remainder and permit one choice of the averaging threshold. This supports the stated $k^{-5/2}$ mixing bound throughout the chosen neighborhood. Lemmas 3–4 then use the selected-word arithmetic of [3] within a finite horizon. The likelihood comparison is asserted only where it is bounded, and the order of choosing $c_*$, $A$, $L$, and finally $M$ avoids circular dependence. Summing the cylinder densities gives the strict positive weight required for Theorem A.

The stronger stopping argument in Section 6 also checks out. The geometric $L^q$ input is supported by the collision-energy and interpolation proof in [3]. Equations (36)–(37) correctly transfer sufficient integrability through the finite horizon; they do not assume an all-depth bound for the truncated tilted law. Equation (39) appropriately includes final descendants divisible by three. Deterministic forward iteration gives the multiplicity control used in (40), while the average weight bound and Hölder's inequality give the exponent $1-3d$ in (43). Together these estimates remove the larger time loss from (34c) that would otherwise obstruct the advertised total budget.

The new six-map estimate has a complete elementary justification. At fixed word length and total valuation, distinct integer endpoints lie in an interval shorter than the target modulus, yielding (47). Positive odd lifts extend the argument to every $3$-adic input. Exact Haar duality then gives the finite failure count (52). Comparing that count with short exit leaves is legitimate even though the prefix and final segment use different alphabets; no unjustified backward-closure assertion connects them. The positive exponent margin in (54), the height choice (55), and the telescoping charge (57) yield the claimed total valuation bound. Periodic endpoints are handled without assuming that the known positive cycle is the only one.

The extension is distinct from the relevant published results. Paper 14, version 3 [4], supplies the earlier quantitative weight construction, while paper 10, version 3 [7], treats unrestricted counts and restrictions depending on position and word length. Neither establishes this fixed-cutoff deficit together with the stated modular routing budget. Leen's mixing criterion and El Jaouhari's selected-word and fixed-total arithmetic are credited; the new contribution is their finite-horizon application and the subsequent stopping and routing deductions.

I reproduced all structural results of the three new exact checkers using copies limited to two workers. These include the transport and finite-sum identities, $1{,}100$ density comparisons with $43{,}278$ terminal replays, and $27{,}280$ fixed-total capacity checks. They support the finite algebra, not the asymptotic estimates or practical values of the constants. The analytic assessment above concerns the written proofs; no formal-proof development was rebuilt.

One minor wording clarification would help: in Section 9, say that exhaustive search **up to the stated total valuation** must contain a successful route. The budget bounds the cost of that route, not the number of candidates examined or the computational runtime of exhaustive search. The uncomputed constants and potentially enormous heights are otherwise clearly disclosed. This version remains a partial advance and does not meet the problem's solve definition: it proves neither universal convergence nor a counterexample.
