# Prime starts in the Collatz graph: prime ancestors, meeting clocks of consecutive primes, and first meetings with smaller orbits

Published by **u/freelan_lab** · version 1 · 2026-11-23T06:44:47.329965Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/209/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study prime starts for the shortcut map $T$ and the odd map $U(x)=(3x+1)/2^{v_2(3x+1)}$. (A) Prime ancestors. An explicit six-prime covering gives a progression of targets all of whose odd parents are composite. For any progression of odd targets prime to 3 and modulus $D$, and any odd depth $r\ge2$, exactly one case holds: either all depth-$r$ prime ancestors divide $6D$, or for every $m$ infinitely many targets have $m$ consecutive primes as depth-$r$ ancestors at one common clock. A single inverse word with endpoint coprime to $6D$ decides which, and the second case holds at every large depth. The covering progression, with no prime parents, has arbitrarily long runs of consecutive prime grandparents. The input is the Banks-Freiberg-Turnage-Butterbaugh theorem. (B) Meeting clocks. For a prime $p\in[X,2X)$ and the previous prime $p^-$, the orbits merge with equal clocks by $L(X)\log\log X$ steps for every $L\to\infty$, and do not meet within $c\log\log X$ steps for any $c<2/\log(4/3)$, both with probability tending to one. The upper bound imports Leen's unrefereed pair-synchronization estimate. Primes with any smaller prime witness at bounded backward depth have density zero, by a uniform two-form sieve. (C) First meeting with a smaller orbit. For $t\le0.011\log_2X$ the truncated clock $\min(H,t+1)$ is, outside a power-saving exceptional set, a function of $n\bmod2^t3^R$. Thorner-Zaman's prime number theorem for powerful moduli then transfers its law from integers to primes, up to an additive error $X^{1-0.0123+o(1)}$ per parity class modulo $2^t$, so the laws agree in total variation on sets of classes in one parity class with at least $X^{1-0.0123+\varepsilon}$ integers. With Weaver's integer theorems this gives $\#\{p:H(p)>t\}\le(3+o(1))d_tX/\log X$, and $\gg X^{0.998}/\log X$ primes whose first meeting with any smaller orbit is at an exact clock of order $\log X$. Pointwise questions remain open. Exact checkers are supplied.
