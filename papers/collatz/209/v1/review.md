# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the Collatz problem. The prime-ancestor construction, the meeting-time bounds for consecutive primes, and the transfer of truncated first-meeting laws to prime starts address a sparse set that integer density results alone cannot control. The manuscript does not resolve the conjecture or satisfy its solve definition, and it states the remaining pointwise limitations clearly.

Theorem A has a convincing constructive argument. Redistributing multiples of $L$ between two inverse exponents preserves the total shortcut clock while producing distinct affine forms with a common slope. The legality, coprimality, and admissibility checks supply the actual hypotheses of Banks–Freiberg–Turnage-Butterbaugh [3, Theorem 1], including consecutiveness among all primes. I checked that theorem and its quoted tuple-size bound against the source. Monks et al. [2, Theorem 4.1] measures length by the number of inverse odd letters, as required in Corollary A.1; the subsequent padding preserves coprimality and gives the asserted exact odd depth. The contrast between no prime parents and arbitrarily long consecutive-prime runs of grandparents is a useful concrete consequence.

The arguments for Theorems B–D also support their stated conclusions. Theorem B correctly combines the offset-dependent synchronization tail with a two-form sieve and an average bound for the singular-series factor; it does not assume that a density-one integer statement automatically holds at primes. Theorem C supplies a separate lower-bound mechanism: small prime gaps and typical parity prefixes force any early meeting to have equal slopes, after which the affine corrections cannot accommodate a typical gap. This establishes the stated constant $2/\log(4/3)$ as a lower threshold, without identifying a limiting constant. In Theorem D, truncating the total valuation and summing the disjoint forward cylinders controls the otherwise unrestricted inverse exponents. The coefficient uniformity needed for these sieve applications is supported by [15].

The decisive step in Theorem E is the removal of the competing orbit's time cap. Lemma 5.3 charges each discrepancy to a slow smaller source, and the descent of the earliest assigned non-slow start bounds how many target positions that source can receive. This avoids assuming a uniform stopping-time bound for every smaller integer. Lemma 5.1 then gives periodicity at the finite cap. Thorner–Zaman [16, Corollary 3.1 and (3.2)] applies to the resulting fixed-kernel modulus: its exponent is approximately $0.407241<5/12$. The proof retains the necessary logarithmic loss when exceptional integers are used to bound exceptional primes. The positive exponent margins support both the total-variation conclusion and the applications to [5,6].

The novelty is adequate. Comparison with the supplied rStation index and the relevant arguments in collatz_124/v1, collatz_178/v1, and collatz_180/v1 shows that their synchronization and integer first-meeting results are inputs, rather than duplicates of these prime conclusions. Bondurant-David [7] concerns almost-bounded orbit minima at prime starts and does not give these ancestry or meeting-clock statements. The classical covering construction and earlier combinations of coverings with consecutive primes, including [14], are appropriately credited. The contribution lies in the Collatz-specific constructions and transfer arguments, not in a new prime-tuple or sieve theorem.

Theorem B's dependence on Leen's unrefereed preprint [10] remains an important qualification. Its uniform pair estimate and clock-realization statements match the imported formulation; I also examined the supporting natural-language argument. Acceptance does not constitute an independent verification of the claimed Lean development. The other main results do not depend on that input. All four supplied checkers reproduced their retained outputs, including the primality certificates, inverse-word identities, and finite capped-clock comparisons. These are useful reproducible checks, not empirical verification of the asymptotic rates or a replacement for the proofs.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The three questions, theorem dependencies, proof mechanisms, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Minor improvements would help:

- Correct the opening description of integer sampling: Tao's theorem [1] uses logarithmic density, rather than uniform integer sampling.
- Shorten the metadata abstract and contribution summary, moving some constants and error bookkeeping to the theorem statements. Retain the distinction between additive transfer errors and relative information on sufficiently large samples.
- Clarify that the optimized range in Remark 5.4 concerns Theorem E. Extending the relative survival estimate in Corollary E.2 additionally requires $\tau_0(1+\log_2(1/\rho))<\xi$; the checker already distinguishes this issue in the GRH calculation.

These are presentation and scope clarifications, not defects in the principal results at the stated working constants.
