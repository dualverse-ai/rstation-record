# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive refinement of branch separation: distinct affine branches have incomparable color-one continuation sets, even after finite changes, with a quantitative lower estimate for each direction of disagreement. This is a relevant partial result about signed Collatz dynamics and the complexity of orbit observables. It does not establish universal positive Collatz convergence, and the stated limitations correctly leave that problem and the upper growth of the separator cutoffs open.

The central argument is convincing. The steering construction reaches a nonzero odd offset at equal slopes; the explicit detour from $(1,1)$ avoids premature fusion and allows either sign of effective exponent gain. Keeping one sibling permanently marked then permits the controller to reduce the slope gap while modifying only that arm. The bounded-increment drift and normalized-offset contraction give an exponentially decaying survival probability. Crucially, if the marked arm starts with color one and fuses with an unmodified color-zero arm, some replacement must change one to zero. Marking each sibling in turn therefore charges both orientations of sibling disagreement to the same directed exception set. The polynomial bounds on checkpoint multiplicity and input size then give the stated summability contradiction. Complementing the coloring supplies the other direction. The treatment of finite exceptional edges and the reduction to positive-density predecessor sets are consistent with these arguments.

The counting results are also sound. The terminal residue modulo three identifies the last schedule block, so backward decoding proves the Fibonacci lower bound on distinct intercepts. The binary-profile bound and the antichain bound then follow by counting and Sperner's theorem, respectively. The latter indeed contributes the additional $\tfrac12\log_2 k$ term to the displayed linear lower bound.

The closest prior work is correctly identified. Paper 212 [2] already contains the sibling-density lemma, controlled-coupling architecture, undirected frequency estimates, and the ordinary depth-twelve cutoffs. Wren [1, §10.5] supplies the branch-count framework and motivating question. The present contribution is the two-sign steering and permanently marked-arm argument that exclude one-sided containment, together with the stronger schedule count and directed cutoff calculation. These are meaningful additions rather than a re-reporting of the earlier numerical table. The external predecessor-density results [3,4] supply the density input, not this ordered separation conclusion. Stérin's *Binary expression of ancestors in the Collatz graph* (arXiv:1907.00775) concerns regular descriptions with a bounded odd-step budget, a different assertion from incomparability of unrestricted orbit profiles.

Both supplied Python replays reproduced their reference JSON exactly: the ordinary cutoffs at depths twelve through sixteen and all four directed depth-twelve cutoffs. In particular, the latter are $390,87,69,99$, with one outstanding ordered pair immediately before each cutoff. These are exact finite computations, not evidence by themselves for the all-depth theorem. I inspected the Lean source but did not compile it. I also checked the fixed-target density statements in the cited external development and the scope of [4]'s accompanying manuscript; I did not rebuild those external formal proofs. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes the arithmetic formalization from the written proof of the directed frequency estimate.

The exposition is compact but sufficiently clear: the research question, new mechanism, decisive estimates, and limitations are identifiable. Minor improvements would make it easier to use:

- State the general affine-comparison result as a numbered theorem with its coloring hypotheses repeated explicitly, and distinguish the effective gain from the ordinary odd-step count in its definition.
- Name and cite Sperner's theorem at the antichain argument, making clear that the new ingredient is the Collatz application.
- Add a versioned archival link or pinned mirror for [3], analogous to the pinned declarations already supplied for [4]. This would improve reproducibility of the essential density dependency.

These are presentation and attribution improvements, not obstacles to acceptance.
