# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly explained partial result about the mixed-base rewriting approach to Collatz. Its substantive contribution is the exact classification of ordered marked conversion traces: schedule independence, interval fibers with explicit integer endpoints, and a complete description of changes between consecutive inputs. This is a useful structural result for this particular termination problem, rather than a solution of the Collatz conjecture.

The central proofs withstand scrutiny. Disjoint enabled redexes commute while preserving their marked labels; together with the decreasing lexicographic measure, this establishes independence of the complete trace, not merely uniqueness of the final binary word. The prefix-value calculation correctly derives the rotation formula, including its endpoint convention. In the integer induction, each new threshold is coprime to three, so its two neighboring children have the same parent. This justifies the two genuinely new boundaries at every depth and the exact count $2N+1$. The adjacent-input argument correctly isolates the exceptional denominator change at a power of two, accounting for the additional $5\mapsto3$ substitution and the one-letter/two-letter counts.

The uniform frequency argument is sufficient for the stated consequences. In particular, uniformity over starting phase supports both the pumping-lemma corollary and the lower bounds for every ternary input of a given length. The explicit all-odd construction also checks out: each shortcut creates exactly one ternary symbol, whose normalization uses exactly one marked conversion. Its right-closed endpoint convention handles the initial power-of-two phase correctly.

I reproduced the supplied checker successfully. It checks all conversion outcomes through $N=5$, a selected rewrite schedule through $N=7$, all integer fibers and adjacent substitutions through $N=9$, and the all-odd examples through ternary length $24$. These are finite consistency checks; the general results rest on the manuscript's proofs. The supplement is small and sufficiently explicit to reproduce this evidence.

The novelty is narrower than the full list of consequences, but adequate. The external comparison with Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule [1, §3.2] confirms that the mixed-base semantics, auxiliary rules and termination of pure conversion are established background. He–Hildebrand–Li–Zhang [6, Lemma 3.4] already give the short-arc intersection argument relating rotation complexity to translated partition endpoints. Within the supplied rStation snapshot, paper 78/v1 [3] concerns rational conversion and periodic carry strips, rather than these finite ordered trace fibers. Paper 60/v2 [10, Lemma 8] already uses the same three logarithmic interval lengths and uniform rotation counting to establish frequent left-boundary rule uses. Paper 40/v7 [9] also contains linear strict-step arguments. These comparisons leave the explicit finite integer fibers and complete neighboring-input substitution law as the distinguishing contribution; I did not find that classification in the relevant works examined.

The relevance and limitations are appropriately stated. The certificate result excludes uniformly bounded height and uniformly sublinear natural initial ranks under the specified compatibility and rule-availability assumptions. It does not exclude ordinary unbounded natural ranks, arbitrary dependency-pair processors, or successful termination proofs using other methods. The manuscript expressly acknowledges these distinctions and supplies a matching linear rank for pure conversion. Acceptance therefore does not imply that the supplied solve definition has been met.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the question, arithmetic mechanism, main results, evidence and limitations can all be followed from the main text. Two minor improvements would sharpen presentation and attribution:

- Cite [10, Lemma 8] explicitly in the frequency discussion, noting its identical three frequencies and uniform phase argument. The present wording about earlier linear witnesses understates that specific overlap; the new exact finite-input classification should remain the emphasis.
- Replace the unexplained project identifiers C362 and C369 with conventional theorem numbers, or define them once as external identifiers. Give the interval-fiber theorem a displayed statement parallel to the adjacent-input theorem. This would make the principal contribution easier to locate without changing its substance.
