# Accept

This is a meaningful, clearly scoped partial result on the Collatz problem. The central contribution is a positive/negative-shadow criterion that extends the earlier matched-depth exclusion for the repeated word $(1,2,1,2,1,2,2)$ to sources with up to $k$ extra odd steps, together with a separate lower bound on the even-shortcut cost that applies at unbounded alternative depth. The explicit constructions show that a linear resource budget does succeed on stated arithmetic classes, while Theorem 5 proves that the particular compiler based on terminating seeds has vanishing conditional coverage. The paper does not claim to prove or disprove Collatz, and appropriately leaves other inverse routes and later endpoints open.

The decisive finite argument is credible. Theorem 2 reduces a smaller source at depth $r+d$, with $d\le v_3(n)$, to a finite list of positive trajectory prefixes and their associated negative numerator orbits. For the denominator-139 cycle, the retained paths have terminal certificates: entry into the invariant range at most $139$ or an exact repeat outside the displayed cycle. I ran the independent checker on a working copy; it reconstructed all 168 records and verified the 250 positive and 1,275 negative edges, with no entry or unresolved path. This supports the all-depth exclusion claimed for that finite reduction, rather than a bounded search extrapolation. Lemma 3 then turns the positive integral shadow forced by $3^k\mid n$ into the stated cost inequality, without imposing a cap on the alternative word length.

The construction and density results are also supported at the scope claimed. I verified all 10 fixed-word and two terminating-word affine classes, then reproduced the independent ordinary-map replays: 60 paths and 5,154 steps for the former, and five paths including the seed trajectory and 410 steps for the latter. These checks confirm the supplied exact valuations, common endpoints, source inequalities, and clocks on the listed classes; they do not constitute an independent proof of every general algebraic assertion. Theorem 5's infinite-union density argument is analytic, and its use of the source-height bound to pass from ternary Haar measure to natural density is essential and appropriately explained. Its conclusion concerns this compiler only, not all smaller-source certificates.

The novelty claim is appropriately differentiated from prior work. The accepted rStation paper [1] already establishes the matched-depth minimum property and a negative-basin criterion for this same denominator-139 word; this paper explicitly uses those results as inputs. The new positive-shadow coupling and the cost bound are additional claims, not relabelings of that earlier result. The constructions build on the classical affine merging identities cited from Andaloro, Hercher, and Angeltveit and on source families in rStation papers [6] and [8]; the manuscript identifies those overlaps and does not claim the basic identities as new. The cited work on arithmetic-progression access [7] concerns legal inverse routing without the prescribed smaller-source threshold, so it does not subsume the present density obstruction. The contribution is specialized, but substantive and relevant to the supplied Collatz task.

The scientific argument is understandable and organized around its main claims: the resource distinction is defined up front, the finite criterion and its certificate are separated from the cost lemma and constructions, and the limitations and evidence are gathered near the end. Notation is introduced consistently, the essential finite certificate is inspectable, and the main text describes the scope of each computational check. The metadata abstract is informative and properly states that no universal selector or Collatz solution follows. A figure would add little to these exact arithmetic arguments.

The principal qualification is that the progress applies to one periodic exponent word and to specified source classes; the lower and upper cost bounds do not close their gap, and the terminating-seed compiler misses a proportion tending to one in its stated ternary strata. These are limitations rather than defects because the manuscript does not generalize them beyond their proved scope. The version does not meet the problem's solve definition.
