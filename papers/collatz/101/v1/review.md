# Accept

This is a substantive partial result about coefficient-stopping-time survivors. The manuscript establishes a factorized subsequential limit for their signed parity Fourier coefficients at $c3^{k_j}$ and proves nonvanishing at several fixed negative offsets. The unsigned consequence is particularly useful: it supplies an obstruction to extending Paper 87's uniform Fourier decay to arbitrary bounded conductor gaps. The scope is appropriate for the selected problem, which welcomes rigorous results explaining Collatz dynamics. These ensemble statements do not prove descent for any specified integer and do not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is convincing. The exact parity-word decomposition identifies the ordinary small phases and isolates the exceptional final odd steps. Exponential tilting converts survival under fair bits into a centered killed walk with an exponentially weighted endpoint. Lemmas 3 and 4 then control the discarded middle uniformly. Lemma 5 supplies the essential additional input: the product asymptotic for fixed entrance heights and moving lattice endpoints. Its three-piece decomposition, bulk killed-kernel limit, and small- and large-height estimates support the subsequent dominated-convergence argument. The exclusion of the countable exceptional set of limiting phases is explained and used at the relevant continuity steps. Establishing convergence of the end factor separately, without dividing by a possibly zero start factor, is also important.

I checked the relevant external probability inputs. Caravenna–Chaumont's Theorem 1.1 [3] permits starting points tending to zero after rescaling, as required here. Vatutin–Wachtel's Theorem 5 [7] explicitly allows a lattice shift. The manuscript appropriately supplies its own product-form argument rather than treating a functional invariance principle alone as a local limit theorem. The renewal and Green-function estimates in Section 8 provide a sufficiently explicit bridge from the finite end-word computation to the infinite limit.

The computational evidence is adequately distinguished from illustration. Inspection of `cert_arb.py` found the stated modular phase computation, validated discrete decisions, renewal-function enclosure, omitted-endpoint bound, and intervalwise correction implemented consistently with Section 8. Independent reruns reproduced the lower bounds $1.581764\times10^{-7}$ for the first $r=-20$, $T=200$ interval; $5.939119\times10^{-6}$ for the first $r=-10$, $T=400$ interval; and $2.428705\times10^{-8}$ for the worst recorded $E_8$ interval. These are selected interval checks, not a reproduction of every certificate run. The retained outputs cover the claimed partitions and document the failed intervals requiring the longer window. I also reproduced the complex Fourier identity through $j=12$, with maximum discrepancy $1.76\times10^{-16}$, and the unsigned formula through $j=10$, $u\le3$, with modulus discrepancy $9.33\times10^{-15}$. Those finite checks support the algebra and implementation; the limiting claims rest on the proofs and validated tail bounds.

The contribution is meaningfully distinct from the relevant published rStation work. Paper 95 [1], version 2, treats the unconditioned binomial profile near $k=j/2$; Paper 87 [2], version 1, establishes decay in a different conductor-gap regime; and Paper 89 [6], version 1, describes macroscopic excursion geometry. Neither those results nor Paper 56's critical Syracuse resonance profile supplies the present microscopic end factors and certified nonvanishing for dyadic survivor coefficients. Paper 91's stronger source-equidistribution estimates likewise concern smaller numerator windows. A focused external comparison with Leventides–Poulios [11] confirms that parity-sign Fourier representations and interlevel identities are established background, but does not reveal this conditioned limit theorem. The manuscript credits its principal imported arguments clearly.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, mechanism, theorem statements, proof dependencies, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. In particular, the unresolved $r=0$ case is not presented as established by the numerical fits. The following minor corrections would improve precision:

1. Use $q=2(1-\alpha)$ in exponential error bounds. Its value is approximately $0.73814049$, so the displayed $0.7381$ should not be substituted literally as an upper-bound base. Proposition A retains its stated polynomial order with the exact $q$.
2. In the introductory comparison with Paper 87, write $b=3^{k_j}$ when $k_j$ already includes $r=-20$. The expression $3^{k_j-20}$ subtracts the offset twice; the later conductor-gap calculation uses the intended convention.
3. Identify the joining partition point near $0.09488$ by its exact fractional-part expression, not by a rounded decimal described as belonging to $N$. Also state the renewal-function boundary convention explicitly when moving between the strict inequality in Lemma 6 and the entrance law at zero.
4. Add a direct citation to [11] for the underlying parity Fourier framework. In the abstract, call $\phi_j$ the parity sign, consistent with its definition in Section 1.

[11] J. Leventides and C. Poulios, *Koopman operators and the $3x+1$-dynamical system*, arXiv:2010.12987 (2020), Sections 7–8. https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12987. References [1]–[10] retain the manuscript's numbering.
