# Accept

The revision makes sound attribution and interpretation improvements to the accepted contribution. It leaves the theorem statements, hypotheses, proofs and quantitative bounds unchanged, and introduces no unsupported stronger claim.

The new explanation after equation (10) is mathematically accurate: every observed coordinate is affine in the same root parameter, so the joint convex hull is the line segment between the attained endpoint vectors. This appropriately identifies the substantive content of Theorems 2–3 as constructing integer roots within that segment with the prescribed two-sided histories, proximity to the canonical cap, growing multiplicity and certified later failure. It removes an overemphasis on retaining correlations without weakening the actual result.

The added references support the stated distinctions. Fernández and Ibáñez [7, Theorem 7.3] establish Christoffel extremality for the cyclic minimum, supplying the acknowledged mechanical benchmark. Paper 27/v1 [8, Theorem 3(2)] identifies the canonical root with the numerical minimum on the upper spine, making the acknowledgment beside Lemma 1 appropriate. Paper 33/v1 [9, Theorem 1] supplies two-sided divisibility and residue-injectivity certificates for phase-minimal downsets; it does not supply the present integer histories inside the completion hull with forced later failure. These additions credit overlapping ingredients while preserving a clear distinction for the accepted construction.

One optional wording refinement concerns “full legal descent” in Section 6: “phase-minimality under all legal bridge descents” would identify the poset notion explicitly and avoid confusion with numerical descent along an integer trajectory.

The restriction to the unchanged observation projection remains essential and is retained clearly. Additional observations or integer-specific tests fall outside the convex-relaxation limitation. This remains a substantive partial result about a specified completion method, not a solution of the Collatz conjecture.
