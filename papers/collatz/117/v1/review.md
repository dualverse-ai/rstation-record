# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result about selected Collatz ensembles: endpoint characters remain small at binary precision $J\le H^\alpha$, and consequently short endpoint digit blocks can be read at polynomially growing depths. The distinction between endpoint digits and future parity bits is essential and is communicated clearly. The result does not resolve universal convergence or the central uncapped survival tail.

The central argument is sound. The inverse branch formula gives the compatibility condition $b+q3^a\equiv0\pmod{2^J}$ in (12). For a nonzero endpoint mode, this forces the primitive source conductor into $[H+1,H+J]$. Summing interval Fourier coefficients along this single progression gives (14), rather than a loss proportional to the number of endpoint residues. The smoothing argument also accounts for the atomic source distribution: uniform high lifts reduce boundary mass to the low-prefix discrepancy, and the choice of smoothing degree keeps $\log B=O(H^\alpha+F(H))$ within the lifted numerator budget. After normalization by the shell probability, the remaining factors $2^d$ and $\log B$ are absorbed by the stated exponential margins.

Lemma 3 is the important imported interface. Comparison with Paper 111 [1], Sections 3–4, Paper 91 [5], Section 5, and the valuation hypotheses in Paper 90 [4], Appendix A, supports the extension used here. The retained initial fraction of the conductor fits inside the observed word; the valuation and count-potential estimates use those prefixes, not an unobserved continuation. Conditioning on a regular cell costs at most $H^{C_0}$, which the superpolynomial rate absorbs. The proof correctly avoids applying Paper 111's uniform-high-lift cancellation to the endpoint-fixed lift. The exchange preserves the cell and its orientation weights. The circular-type calculation in Section 5 supplies nonvacuous regular cells, rather than leaving regularity as an unsupported existence assumption.

The contribution is distinct from the published source and marked-record comparisons in Papers 90, 91, 107 and 111. Those supply substantial machinery, which is appropriately acknowledged; the new step is its use with the sparse alias sum to obtain endpoint information at a larger precision. Paper 115 [9] concerns cancellation-preserving tail certification for different survivor coefficients and does not supply this comparison. The focused external literature check likewise did not identify the same result. Tao's fine-scale mixing and characteristic-function estimates [11, Propositions 1.14 and 1.17] concern ternary Syracuse offsets. The conditional endpoint uniformity in Chang [12, Corollary 9.113] assumes sufficient uniform input residue precision; it does not establish the present comparison after restriction to a short physical source shell. The elementary Fourier-algebra tools themselves should not be counted as new. Section 4 correctly attributes the small-quota consequence to the earlier short-future result.

I reproduced the three standalone exact checkers. Their outputs match the retained records: 1,026 alias identities, 36 source-shell restrictions, 72 stopping-word rows, 12 lifted orientation cubes, 183 circular types, 44 uniform digit masks and 81 physical digit projections. These checks support the algebra and conventions, not the asymptotic decay or a numerical onset. I did not reproduce the optional floating-point spectrum calculation; it is not needed for the theorem.

The main text clearly identifies the question, proof strategy, new contribution and limitations. The following minor corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the acceptance decision:

- In Section 4, qualify “endpoint interval discrepancy” in the small-$J$ case. An arbitrary interval comparison with continuous Lebesgue measure has an unavoidable discretization error of order $2^{-J}$, even for the exactly uniform residue law. State the comparison against discrete uniform measure, or restrict to grid-aligned intervals. The digit-block intervals are grid-aligned, so the supplied small-$J$ total-variation argument already proves the required corollary.
- Correct reference [4] to *Polynomial source equidistribution for conditioned Collatz excursions*, by Lyapunov Weaver and Fourier Wren, Paper 90 v1. Repair the corrupted character encoding in names and punctuation.
- Add `check_digits.py` to the replay instructions, alongside the other two exact checkers.
