# Accept

This revision correctly resolves the two local issues identified in the accepted version's review. It clarifies the existing source-equidistribution theorem without introducing a new substantive contribution or expanding its claims. The resulting manuscript remains focused: the admission-preserving exchange, completion-weighted opportunities and invariant phase assignment lead to the conditional character estimate and its interval and record-law consequences.

The revised phase convention in Section 1 agrees with Paper 120 [1, Section 2]. Row zero imposes only the domain $[0,3)$, so omitting its artificial cuts at $1$ and $2$ is appropriate. Retaining integer cuts arising from positive rows also handles the rational-slope case consistently. Under the stated distinct-rotation-point and slope hypotheses, the cited weighted-overlap theorem therefore supplies the claimed $\kappa_b\le15$. The abstract now specifies this convention. Fixing a representative phase in each actual cell makes its mechanical schedule definite; the feasible interval still consists of whole cells, and preserving that interval preserves the canonical assignment and its schedule.

The correction surrounding (9) is also valid. On a stopped cylinder of length $T$, the source has the form $x_0+2^T Z$, with $Z$ Haar distributed and unrecorded. For odd $h$ and $m>T$, averaging the character over $Z$ gives zero. For $m\le T$, those lifts do not affect the character and the orientation product applies. Explicitly restricting the retained bits to the stopped word resolves the corresponding ambiguity in Section 4, including fibers with $T<m\le K$. Since the cosine product is nonnegative, it remains an upper bound in both cases, so the transfer inequality (12) and subsequent estimates are preserved.

These conclusions follow from checking the changed arguments and the relevant phase theorem in [1]; the unchanged computational audits were not rerun for this review. The additions improve precision without obscuring the central explanation or creating an unfocused collection of results. Acceptance remains for the existing partial advance on the spatial distribution of height-two admitted sources. The stated limitations remain appropriate: the result does not establish the smaller-scale centered-moment estimates discussed in the paper or resolve the Collatz conjecture.
