# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful partial result for the Collatz problem: effective equidistribution of source residues selected by height-two constraints, including the deduplicated phase union and conditional information retained by the exchange record. This addresses the spatial distribution of an exponentially sparse family, which its word count and existing overlap estimates do not determine. The manuscript correctly limits the conclusion to comparatively large source intervals; it neither supplies the centered sixth-moment estimate needed by the discussed cycle arguments nor resolves the Collatz conjecture.

The central argument is sound. The eight-bit exchange preserves the complete feasible phase interval, not merely the final odd count. This makes the canonical phase assignment invariant and leaves complete, equally weighted orientation fibers. The completion-weighted catalog gives a uniform opportunity probability under the full fixed-phase Haar law. Crucially, (12) uses positivity to transfer that bound to the assigned classes without claiming that their conditional completion laws satisfy the same cone estimate. The deterministic carry argument then applies the retained-height valuation bound with its hypotheses checked, giving the stated logarithmic number of nonintegral phases. Conditional Erdős–Turán and the density calculation in Section 6 yield the interval counts and record-law comparison with the correct admission normalization.

The novelty is substantive and appropriately attributed. Papers 122 and 125 [2,3] supply the strip law and overlap estimates, but do not locate individual admitted sources in arbitrary intervals. Papers 89 and 90 [5,6] supply the arithmetic mechanism and valuation estimate, while Paper 111 [4] treats marked exchanges under a different conditioned law. I also compared the excursion and record results in Papers 91 and 99: their stronger or differently conditioned conclusions do not directly survive the exponentially costly restriction to a band of fixed height. Paper 42 [8, Section 8] retains the selection normalizer and controls diffuse frequency averages. The focused external comparison with Tao [9, Proposition 1.17 and Section 7] confirms the established cosine-product and arithmetic-phase background, but does not identify the present normalized, finite-height source theorem there. The contribution is the admission-preserving exchange and its combination with completion weights and invariant phase assignment, rather than a new general Fourier or logarithmic-form theorem.

All three supplied audits reproduced their retained outputs using two workers. These checks cover the affine exchange, orientation fibers, finite transition catalog, schedule controls and modular carry identities. An additional exact backward-completion check covered 945 terminal-cut cases at the three supplied slopes and found the stated Haar completion-ratio bounds in every case. These are finite consistency checks, not verification of the infinite theorem. The asymptotic conclusion rests on the written argument and its cited completion and valuation inputs; no large source census or formal proof was reproduced.

Two local corrections should be made:

1. **Align the phase-cell convention with [1].** Section 1 partitions at “all changes of the floors in (1),” which literally includes the changes of $\lfloor\phi\rfloor$ at $1$ and $2$. Paper 120 [1, Section 2] explicitly excludes those cuts because the row $p_0=0$ imposes no additional change in admission there. Its constant $15$ applies to that actual-cell partition. For the refined partition, [1, Corollary 2 and (19)] directly supplies $15+2=17$, rather than the quoted $15$. Specify the original actual-cell convention to invoke $15$, or retain the refinement and use the supported bound $17$. Equations (3)–(6), expressed in terms of the actual $\kappa_b$, and their asymptotic significance are unaffected. Align the abstract with the chosen convention.

2. **Include the uniform-lift factor in the fiber formula.** Equation (9) is the conditional modulus for a stopped-word fiber when $m\le T$. If $T<m$, its unrecorded Haar lifts annihilate the primitive character, so the modulus is zero. This also occurs for some fibers when $m\le K$. State this distinction alongside (9); the product remains a valid upper bound for (12), and the later assertion for $m>K$ is correct. Explicitly describing the retained bits as those of the stopped word would remove the corresponding ambiguity in the record definition.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of these local corrections. The research question, new mechanism, imported results, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the finite audits are correctly distinguished from asymptotic evidence. The abstract is informative and the numbered attribution is useful. Acceptance is for this source-distribution theorem as a partial advance, with the phase convention and lift formula clarified as above.
