# Accept

The update makes sound corrections to the accepted counterexample and resolves the previous review’s substantive qualifications. It preserves the explicit fields and unconditional exact $3$-rank evidence while improving the interpretation of isogeny data, the scope of the conclusion, and attribution. No new central contribution or stronger class-group claim is introduced.

The distinction between an individual curve’s isogeny-degree row and the union of the class matrix is essential and is now correctly stated. I checked Cremona’s published data [10]: in Cremona order, the row for $54a2$ is $(3,1,9)$, confirming a degree-$9$ isogeny from that particular curve. The row for $27a1$ is $(1,3,3,9)$, and the degree-$27$ entries connect $27a2$ and $27a4$. Direct computation from the published models also confirms $j=-12288000$ for those two curves and $j=0$ for $27a1$ and $27a3$. The revised ancillary discussion therefore fixes the earlier conflation without affecting the counterexample.

Inspection of the new `--row` implementation shows that it extracts the first row of the PARI matrix. The official `ellisomat` documentation confirms that its first curve is isomorphic to the input curve, so this is the appropriate source row. PARI’s ordering need not match Cremona’s ordering. As a minor reproducibility improvement, state this explicitly alongside the displayed tuples, or print the destination labels with the degrees. I could not reproduce the new PARI commands because `gp` is unavailable here; the database checks verify the relevant degrees, but not the exact reported PARI column order.

The weakened abstract and limitations correctly distinguish exact $r_3=2$ from identifying the connecting images of the same-$Y$ points. The former disproves the stated implication; it does not establish that these points generate the class-group $3$-torsion or that their fibres are unramified. The class-group certificates and enumeration code are unchanged, and the previous review independently verified their decisive counts. I have not repeated that enumeration in this update review.

Identifying the rejected precursor, correcting the Gillibert–Levin survey citation and Mestre date, and adding Kenku for composite isogeny degrees appropriately address the attribution concerns. The contribution remains the concrete counterexample, with the same substantive connection to construction mechanisms and the same distinction from the broader specialization issues discussed in [3], [5], and [6]. It neither reaches a target rank nor claims a new general construction.
