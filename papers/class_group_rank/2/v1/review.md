# Accept

This is a useful, bounded contribution to the construction problem. Its main achievement is an arithmetic analysis of a specific four-slot design: it distinguishes local insolubility, a nonzero valuation defect on every fibre, and genuine unresolved Mordell–Weil questions. Reaching none of the numerical targets is not a reason to reject this work. The task explicitly welcomes construction mechanisms and substantive partial advances, and the worked obstructions explain why promising configurations fail.

The central arguments are convincing within their stated scope. Lemma 1 follows directly from conjugation of valuations. Proposition 2 identifies the relevant nonzero defect through Tate uniformisation, and Proposition 3 correctly counts the elliptic factors and computes the base genus. Algorithm L now covers the region near infinity without a search cap and exhausts the remaining residue discs; its treatment of unresolved large-prime searches does not turn search failure into an impossibility claim. The Mordell–Weil gate includes the identity and correctly distinguishes a rank-zero torsor with points from an empty one. The manuscript also appropriately leaves simultaneous avoidance of leaks, untested primes, and the open bases unresolved.

I independently checked the worked obstruction for $s_0=-28/5$ using exact rational arithmetic. At $19$, the admissible residue classes are precisely $0,2,5,7,13$; the nonintegral region and branch-point residues are excluded. On these classes $M(u)$ is a unit square. For slots 1 and 3, the displayed transformations give integral minimal models with $v_{19}(\Delta)=5$, unit $c_4$, and split multiplicative reduction. Every allowed point reduces to the singular point $(0,0)$, hence lies outside the identity component. This independently supports Proposition 10 without relying on the dense sample or the general tree implementation.

I also recomputed the union of the retained cubic-field identifiers: 6274 visits give 5718 identifiers, with multiplicities $5170,540,8$. Aggregating the retained twist records reproduces all of Table 3, including 7577 distinct pairs and 181 unresolved ranks. The corrected near-infinity regression witness checks exactly. These are targeted checks and reaggregations, not a reproduction of the full PARI scans, class-group computations, or all ten obstruction certificates; those were assessed through their arguments, code and retained outputs.

The novelty is sufficient when attributed as the manuscript now attributes it. Reading Howe–Leprévost–Poonen [4, §3.6] confirms that the modular twist, intermediate genus-one curve and degree-three isogeny are prior work. Gillibert–Levin [1] supplies the specialisation framework, and Bartz–Levin–Thamminana [10] supplies a related genus-two construction for rank two. The new content is the arithmetic analysis of the assembled multi-slot bases and its explicit exclusions, rather than the underlying parametrisation. The published rStation paper [12, version 4] concerns Mestre–Nagao scores and representation statistics; it does not duplicate this contribution. The auxiliary rank measurements alone would be a weaker novelty claim, but they are not carrying the acceptance decision.

Several corrections and clarifications would improve the final presentation without changing this decision:

1. **Correct the sampling description in §8.** The collision-selected run also uses $s=6$ and $s=-4$, beyond the eight parameters listed in the text. In fact, the unique envelope exception in the retained data is $(s,d)=(6,-26159)$, with rank $3$ and $r_5=0$. State the two parameter sets separately and identify this exception explicitly. The reported aggregate table itself is reproducible.
2. **Correct the frequency labels in §7.1.** The values $0.224$ and $0.232$ come from `rates(14)` and `rates(-11/4)`, respectively. That routine loops over pairs of slots, hence quintic models in both cases; these are not a quintic-versus-sextic comparison. The corresponding twist-inclusive frequencies need the same relabelling. This does not affect the separate 940-model torsion exclusion.
3. **State local solubility explicitly in Lemma 8(2).** The identification of the covering class with a Selmer class requires $B$ to be everywhere locally soluble. The census supplies this hypothesis, but the standalone lemma should do so too.
4. **Make the certification boundary uniform.** The smooth scans use BPSW-tested cofactors, whereas successful configurations receive proven-prime rechecks. For unconditional certification of every discarded configuration, also prove primality of each modulus used to certify its first local obstruction, and retain that obstruction. Separately, preserve the existing GRH qualification on statistical class-group results and the sampling qualification on the Cohen–Lenstra probabilities.

Acceptance is for the finite census, obstruction mechanisms and explicit open cases. It does not establish an infinite family of fields of $5$-rank four, a universal rank envelope, or a solution of any target pair.
