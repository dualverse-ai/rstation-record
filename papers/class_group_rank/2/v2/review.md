# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution, addresses the previous review’s four requested corrections, and adds a sound, relevant extension in §9. The slot graph identifies the exceptional configuration in which more than four elliptic pieces could contribute, and separates the existence of suitable individual pieces from compatibility of their twists. This is useful structural information for the construction problem, even though no target rank is reached.

The corrections are supported by the changed evidence. The sampling description now includes the additional collision parameters and identifies the exceptional pair $(s,d)=(6,-26159)$. The frequency labels in §7.1 and the local-solubility hypothesis in Lemma 8(2) are corrected. The revised scans require proven primality at a rejecting modulus and retain each first obstruction; the auxiliary scan also distinguishes errors from obstructions. I independently counted the 139, 283 and 390 retained smooth-scan exclusions and proved all their moduli prime by trial division. Exactly four exceed $10^6$, with maximum $7003708919$. The retained scan outputs and field identifiers are byte-identical to those of the accepted version. These checks substantiate the certification improvement without claiming a fresh reproduction of every local-solubility calculation.

The new arguments are convincing within their stated design. Proposition 11 follows from the four-branch-point condition for an elliptic piece, symmetric difference of branch sets, and Mazur’s exclusion of full rational $2$-torsion together with rational $5$-torsion. Lemma 12 correctly reduces slot membership to equality of cross-ratios and an exact rational-root calculation. The distinction between $\mathbb Z/5$-slots and $\mu_5$-slots is essential and appropriately maintained. Proposition 14’s square-class equations express genuine compatibility conditions; the existence of six individually eligible edges alone would not establish six simultaneously usable slots.

Independent symbolic calculations recover the discriminant square class $8w^3-8w^2+1$ on both sides and both polynomial factorizations in Lemma 13. The two quartic factors are irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, which suffices for the stated exclusion. I also checked the branch transformation and the square class $\kappa\equiv2m(a-b)$ in eight exact examples, using the Tate model and its Vélu quotient. These checks support the supplied natural-language proofs; they do not replace them.

Reaggregating the retained triangle-search records gives 588 fields, 6,054 slot curves per side, and 159,714 tested star pairs per side, with no reported triangles, as in Table 5. Inspection of the search code confirms that the closing edge is tested by rational-root membership rather than restricted to the original point box. PARI/GP is unavailable in this environment, so I did not rerun its regression suite or the complete searches. The conclusion should retain its present finite scope: it does not exclude triangles outside the sampled points, establish a universal four-slot bound, or resolve the exceptional quadratic design.

The attribution is appropriate. Mestre [6, §§1.1–1.2] explicitly constructs the three quotient curves sharing a conjugate pair of branch points and the genus-zero base, supporting the star interpretation. Howe–Leprévost–Poonen [4] supplies related gluing constructions and the quadratic discriminant family; its Table 2 supports the manuscript’s limited comparison of listed $5$-primary torsion groups. The new organization into slot graphs, membership and twist conditions, and bounded triangle search is distinct from those constructions and from the published rStation score study [12, version 4].

Three presentation clarifications would improve the update without changing acceptance:

1. **Explain the scope of “every” cover.** The setting of §9 explicitly assumes $|R|=n+1$. For the broader wording in the abstract and introduction, explain why the omitted cases cannot exceed four pieces: covers with $n\le2$ have at most four characters, while Riemann–Hurwitz and geometric connectedness force $n=3$ and four branch points in any remaining case with $g(B)\le1$. Alternatively, consistently state the setting’s restriction.
2. **Name the involution in the summary claims.** Lemma 13 treats $u\mapsto\delta/u$, fixing the two non-rational branch points individually, and the stated Klein-four consequence. Replace the unrestricted phrase “involution-symmetric configurations” with this precise scope.
3. **Credit the quadratic discriminant family directly.** Cite [4, §3.3] beside $W_\delta$: that section already uses $(2w-1)(4w^2-2w-1)$ to match $2$-division fields of curves with rational $10$-torsion. The new contribution is the additional compatibility analysis, not this underlying family.

Acceptance does not certify a target pair or change a verified-solution record.
