# Accept

The update improves the accepted study and adds a useful, appropriately limited investigation of Jacobian points. The revised representation parameterization, observational interpretation of the cubic results, and distinction between nominal and multiplicity-adjusted probabilities address the previous review's substantive qualifications. The added degree-2 divisor comparison directly examines a limitation of the earlier curve-point search. Its contribution is this empirical comparison and its explicit witnesses; the broader descent and class-group connections remain part of the background attributed to [10], [11] and [14].

The positive results are sound. I independently checked the rational identities defining all six retained divisors in five fields, including $q\mid f-L^2$ and the norm identity for their images under $y-\sqrt h$. Rerunning the inspected exact class checker recovered all six reported nonprincipal classes of order 5, including the inverse pair in one field. The torsion argument is valid: reduction excludes primes other than 2 and 5, irreducibility excludes rational 2-torsion, and the argument using $1-\zeta_5$ excludes rational 5-torsion because the imaginary quadratic field cannot lie in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$. These are genuine positive-rank witnesses, without certifying the full class-group ranks used to classify the samples.

The retained records reproduce the Table 7 totals, Wilson intervals, and one-sided Fisher probability $p=0.1281$ for $2/30$ versus $3/200$. I also recovered the A5 selection from the retained rank-and-score table and recomputed the residual-tail expectations and nominal probabilities from the newly supplied aggregates. These support the restrained conclusion that the discovered divisors provide no statistically persuasive explanation of the weak residual association. They do not establish equal divisor frequencies, exclude points outside the searched family, or bound Jacobian rank from above. I did not regenerate the million-field sample or independently reconstruct its population quantiles.

One concrete implementation correction is needed. In `divsearch2.c`, `MODS` includes 65, but `issq` has rows of length 64 and each `tab[j]` allocation has only 64 bytes. Initialization and lookup reach index 64; a bounds-sanitized probe reports this immediately. Size both tables and their initialization consistently for the largest modulus. In a separate review copy with these sizes corrected, the largest integral-box search reproduced both retained divisors for $D=6426813556$. This spot check and the exact witness checks support the positive results, but do not certify exhaustive negative results for all 530 fields. The complete bounded search should be rerun with corrected storage, retaining the comparison with the submitted output. I regard this as a localized reproducibility defect rather than grounds to discard the verified witnesses and the qualified empirical extension.

Two scope clarifications would improve the presentation. Describe Table 7 as the union of the searches: the denominator-2 box alone finds four positive fields, while the integral boxes find five. Also phrase the abstract's “so they do not explain it” as “the divisors found do not explain it.” The small counts and bounded search support that narrower statement; they leave the source, and even the statistical robustness, of the proposed $\ell=5$ residual unresolved.
