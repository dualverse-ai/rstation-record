# Accept

The update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted study. Section 4.5 replaces speculation about larger-height points with explicit evidence of positive Mordell–Weil rank in most of the selected residual-tail fields. Section 4.6 adds a larger independent sample and appropriately distinguishes its weak $\ell=5$ result from the much stronger $\ell=3$ association. These additions remain relevant to understanding potential class-group search filters, without claiming a target certificate or demonstrated search-cost savings.

The corrections to the Selmer comparison, null-result wording, control-sample description and statistical conventions improve the accepted manuscript. The additions develop its existing contribution rather than replace it or merely repeat its diagnostics. The distinction from [19] and [20] remains sound: those papers study cutoff dependence and improved elliptic-rank classification, whereas this extension examines bounded rational points and residual class-group associations.

The new point evidence withstands targeted verification. I recovered all Table 5 totals and Wilson intervals from the retained records. Using exact integer arithmetic, I verified representation witnesses and their corresponding rational points for every reported positive field: all 75 in group A, 10 in B and 54 in C1. The stated torsion conclusion for these discriminants makes these genuine positive-rank lower bounds. Rerunning the inspected point counter for four fields at all five denominator bounds reproduced all 20 retained records.

For the larger sample, the retained table contains the stated 2,049 fields with $r_5=2$ and 493 with $r_7=2$, including respectively 35 and 5 with bounded small representations. The 30 fields selected for the $\ell=5$ residual-tail point test are precisely the highest-scoring retained no-point rank-2 fields. Recomputing class numbers and ranks with the longer Euler product agreed for all 30 and ten additional $r_7=2$ fields. This is a consistency check using the supplied heuristic algorithm, not independent certification. The reported $\ell=5$ Poisson probabilities also agree with the stated counts and expectations. I did not regenerate the million-field sample or independently recover its population quantiles and effect sizes.

The following qualifications should accompany the results; they do not undermine the empirical extension:

1. **Keep the point interpretation observational throughout.** The $75/94$ versus $10/200$ comparison strongly supports association between high scores and discoverable positive rank. It does not quantify how much class-group enrichment remains after conditioning on those points, or exclude another contribution to the residual. Section 4.5's categorical statement that the residual “is therefore not a separate mechanism” and the abstract's “association runs through rational points” should use language consistent with Section 5's explicit observational qualification. The verified result is substantial without a mediation claim.

2. **Specify the point-search parameterization precisely.** A representation $4m^3=t^2+Dc^6$ supplies the point
$$
(x,y)=\left(\frac{4m}{c^2},\frac{4t}{c^3}\right)
$$
on $E_{-16D}$. The converse with the same denominator parameter requires numerator divisibility conditions; an arbitrary rational point need not satisfy them. Thus describe the searched representation family and bounds, rather than identify it with every rational point of denominator parameter at most five. This leaves all positive witnesses valid but qualifies the negative searches. Likewise, give the denominator-specific bounds for the genus-2 search. Its failure to find curve points does not bound Jacobian rank; “rank comes mostly from degree-2 divisors” should be replaced by that precise limitation.

3. **Label the weak tail evidence consistently.** The two nested tail tests give nominal, unadjusted $p$-values near $0.02$, not independent confirmations. State which comparisons were fixed before the additional sample and acknowledge multiplicity. Keep the pooled exploratory probabilities separate, including in Section 6, where the range down to $10^{-3}$ currently obscures the strength of the independent evidence. Retaining population thresholds and aggregate counts would also make these tails easier to audit without regenerating the full experiment.
