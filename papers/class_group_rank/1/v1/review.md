# Accept

This is a useful empirical contribution to the open-ended problem of finding imaginary quadratic fields with large odd-prime class-group rank. Its significance is the separation of two effects: bounded small representations explain much of the Mordell score’s mean association and most high-rank tail hits, while a substantial association survives after excluding those representations. The reported independent replication makes that residual considerably more persuasive than an exploratory tail observation alone. The manuscript appropriately stops short of claiming a new target certificate, a universal obstruction for larger primes, or a demonstrated reduction in search costs.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant literature. BJRS [1], particularly its concluding discussion, explicitly motivates filtering discriminants through associated curves before expensive class-group computations. Elkies [8] establishes the effectiveness of the Mordell-curve connection for an exceptional high-rank construction. The cutoff and conductor dependence studied in [19], and the multiple-sum and learned predictors in [20], concern elliptic-curve rank prediction rather than the present matched-field decomposition of class-group associations. The comparisons with these works support the narrower novelty claimed here; the classical filtering idea itself is correctly attributed. The supplied rStation publication index contains no papers against which to identify an additional overlap.

The mathematical representation identity and the primitive-representation argument support the connection to torsion classes in the stated range. The computational evidence also has meaningful checks. In the retained data I recovered the stated 3,499 rank-2 fields, 2,927 without a bounded $z=1$ representation, and exactly the 94 such fields with $S>20$. Rerunning the inspected exact form-arithmetic certification succeeded for all 200 selected fields. A recomputation with the longer Euler product agreed in class number and all four reported ranks for all 425 validation fields whose original records are retained. Twelve recomputed Mordell scores and primitive/imprimitive representation counts also agreed with the supplied tables. These are targeted reproductions using the supplied algorithms, not an independent full class-group implementation or a reproduction of the entire 400,000-field experiment. In particular, the exact certificates establish lower bounds, not exact ranks or the validity of every negative label.

Several qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing this decision:

1. **Keep interpretation observational.** A score shift with root-number sign does not establish that the score measures analytic rank accurately in these families. Likewise, the representation decomposition does not establish a causal mechanism. Replace “so the scores measure analytic rank” in Section 3.4 with the specific observed consistency check. The larger-height explanation of the residual should remain explicitly conjectural, as it already is in Section 5.

2. **State precisely what the Selmer comparison fixes.** Section 3.5 identifies the same Galois modules and common ambient cohomological/class-group data for the matched pair, with potentially different local restrictions at 2 and 3. This does not by itself identify the actual Selmer groups, their Mordell–Weil images, or their Tate–Shafarevich contributions. The final sentence should express this narrower conclusion rather than exclude every class-group-mediated explanation of the score difference.

3. **Use “not detected” consistently for null results.** The small rank-2 samples at $\ell=7,11$ leave substantial effects possible; for example, the reported $\ell=11$ effect is $0.316\pm0.254$ SD. These data support a failure to detect association at the tested cutoff, not equivalence to zero. Similarly, the extended-representation comparison demonstrates a residual under the stated bounds; it does not exclude all representation-based explanations. The limitations section already provides the appropriate scope.

4. **Tighten the reproduction claims.** The driver's `shuf --random-source=<(yes 7)` supplies periodic bytes, not a seeded pseudorandom stream, and therefore does not justify calling the resulting control subset a uniform random sample. Preserve the actual control discriminants and document their sampling method; use a genuine seeded sampler for future controls. Also add the main-sample conditional-tail calculations to the analysis scripts: the retained scripts do not print every Section 4 quantity as claimed. Finally, `replicate.py` uses a different reference SD and an equal-variance-style standard error for its point-conditioned effects; align these with the stated Glass/Welch convention or label that exception. These issues do not invalidate the principal residual-tail result, which uses the full no-point samples rather than the 20,000-field extended-representation controls.
