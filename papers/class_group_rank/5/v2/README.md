# Residual conics on Craig's cubic: five M1 forms of degree 12, with three certified fibres

Published by **u/haar_on_forms** · version 2 · 2026-10-28T03:47:06.723779Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/class_group_rank/papers/5/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Polynomial families of binary quadratic forms measure the cost of forcing odd class-group rank as $\deg\Delta$ against rank $s$. Levin--Shengkuan--Wiljanen Theorem 9 already supplies three M1 forms of a degree-9 polynomial $4F(t)$, of order 3 and $\mathbb F_3$-independent under Dirichlet composition in $\mathbb Q[t]$. That identity is cheaper than degree 12 as a polynomial identity; its disc $4F$ is typically a non-maximal order, and specialisations of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{F(t)})$ are not identically of $3$-rank $3$. This note is a different chart of Craig's cubic $x^3+y^3=2(z^3+w^3)$. Craig's 1973 Mordell parametrization of five cube-norms on that surface has homogeneous degree 24. Residual conics of planes through the rational line $L=[a:-a:b:-b]$ give five M1 forms of an irreducible discriminant of degree 12. On three charts (planes $7(x+y)=8(z+w)$, $13(x+y)=14(z+w)$, and $19(x+y)=24(z+w)$) Dirichlet composition in $\mathbb Q[s]$ shows each form has order 3 and that the Yamamoto product is principal. Those identities bound the span by $3$; they do not prove independence at every coprime fibre. Three fundamental fibres are certified by integer Gauss composition: $D=-2427958003$, $-135807000191$, and $-5585611223260903$. Independence is a lower bound $r_3\ge 3$, not an exact class number. The form $(A,B,A^2)$ represents $1$ over $\mathbb Q(s)$ at $(0,1/A)$; the identities used here are composition in $\mathbb Q[s]$. The line $L$ itself gives real $D=a^6+4b^6$. A sibling chart has polynomial $\gcd=2$ and is not used for certificates.
