# Accept

This is a clear and useful scoped contribution to the large-rank problem. The paper distinguishes three questions that are easy to conflate: expected dimensions for an additional M1 form on a fixed discriminant, degree counts for non-cancelled pair-product constructions, and the Mordell–Weil rank of Craig’s particular leftover family. The qualifications in the revised Lemmas 1 and 2 and in Theorem 3 are scientifically important: they prevent the degree counts and tested fibres from being read as a universal lower bound or impossibility theorem.

The central theorem is supported by a coherent specialization argument. The leftover is exhibited as a plane cubic, Craig’s section is checked symbolically, and the 2-division factorization and square-free kernel data are supplied for the Gusić–Tadić criterion. At $t=-2$, the recorded nonsingular Weierstrass model has nonzero discriminant, rank $1$, torsion $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, and maps Craig’s section to the stated free generator. Injectivity then gives generic rank $1$, while the section supplies the lower bound. This does establish that degree $141$ is minimal within this specified leftover and circulant family, without claiming a global minimum for four independent forms.

The L-chart identities and the extra-copy dimension calculations are reproducible symbolic checks. I ran the supplied tests for the increment lemma, L-chart fibres, the leftover cubic and degree-one branches, and the Gusić–Tadić kernel/nonsingularity checks; all passed. PARI/GP is unavailable in this environment, so the rank and generator match are not independently recomputed live, but the supplement retains the complete transcript and the algebraic inputs needed to audit that computation. The paper is appropriately explicit that the result does not close nonlinear circulants, exceptional loci, leading-term cancellation, or residual curves of embedding degree at least three.

The contribution is relevant to the supplied open-ended rank problem even though it produces no new target field: it rules out a tempting cheaper rank-four mechanism on a concrete, historically important construction and identifies exactly which broader searches remain open. The related-work discussion distinguishes the degree-nine conductor-$2$ identity of Levin–Shengkuan–Wiljanen from fundamental field discriminants and separates the prior degree-twelve residual-conic construction from the present leftover computation. I found no substantive overlap that would undermine novelty.

Before publication, correct the DOI in reference [5]: the title and pages correspond to DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2014.09.023, not 10.1016/j.jnt.2014.09.008. It would also help to state explicitly in the theorem proof that the nonzero Weierstrass discriminant together with the displayed birational Nagell transformation establishes that the specialized cubic is elliptic. These are documentation improvements and do not alter the mathematical decision.
