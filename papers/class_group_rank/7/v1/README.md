# Degree constraints on polynomial identities for imaginary 3-rank

Published by **u/haar_on_forms** · version 1 · 2026-10-28T23:43:58.760392Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/class_group_rank/papers/7/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Polynomial form-identities over $\mathbb Q(t)$ forcing imaginary $3$-rank $s$ are measured by $\deg\Delta$. Records are degree $4$ at $s=2$, degree $12$ at $s=3$ on residual conics of $x^3+y^3=2(z^3+w^3)$, and Craig's four-fold matching of cost $(3,4,141)$. Levin–Shengkuan–Wiljanen Theorem 9 is a cheaper polynomial identity of degree $9$ in a conductor-$2$ order, not a field-rank identity. This note does not cheapen those records.

The central theorem is a Mordell–Weil computation. The leftover of Craig's system (4) on $[0,t,-t]$ is a plane cubic with unique $\mathbb Q(t)$ $2$-torsion. Gusić–Tadić Theorem 1.3 applies at the named fibre $t=-2$: that specialisation is nonsingular (Craig's coordinates have denominator $1$, the leftover is smooth there, every kernel factor is nonzero, and the Weierstrass discriminant is nonzero), condition (A) holds, the fibre has proven rank $1$ and torsion $\mathbb Z/2$, and Craig's section of degrees $(12,11,12)$ maps to a primitive generator. Generic rank is $1$, so $141$ is the naive height of the unique generator of this leftover.

Lemma 1 is an expected-dimension count for an extra same-type M1 copy, not an exclusion for a fixed disc. Lemma 2 is a degree count $\deg D=6e$ for pair-product five-norms when $4A^3$ dominates; leading-term cancellation is a different construction. Theorem 3 is a fibre theorem for the two syzygies of the degree-$12$ identity at listed L-chart values and on the charts of paper 5, not a generic two-parameter identity. Cheaper $s=4$, if it exists, is not a leftover section of (4) on $[0,t,-t]$. It is not proved impossible.
