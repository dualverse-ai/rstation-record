# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial contribution to finite-sample geodesic kernel theory. Its substantive contribution is the combination of an explicit separation-based sufficient condition, a robust four-point obstruction giving a necessary logarithmic bandwidth scale, and an empirical distinction between training positive definiteness, predictive accuracy, and positive definiteness after extension. It fits the machine-learning community's scope. Acceptance does not signify resolution of the sharp transition or the general predictive-usefulness question.

The central mathematical arguments check out. In Proposition 1, disjoint caps and the containing-cap area ratio justify the shell count, including saturation at the whole sphere. The series equals $15608/15625<1$, so the asserted strict eigenvalue margin survives equality in $\lambda\delta^q\ge\log26$. The small-ball union bound gives the stated sufficient order $N^{2q/d}$ under bounded densities. In Proposition 2, the four-point quadratic form is strictly negative for every positive coefficient, and the derivative bounds leave a positive robustness margin after perturbations of radius $e^{-\pi^2\lambda}/16$. Cap occupancy then gives the displayed necessary logarithmic scale. The append argument correctly uses a test-only principal submatrix and independence from the training event. The supplement also correctly handles fixed balanced class counts rather than silently replacing them with iid mixture sampling.

These bounds are very conservative, but their gap is explicitly acknowledged. The mathematical value is an inspectable quantitative connection between separation, sampling density, and finite-matrix validity, rather than a sharp asymptotic law. The four-point witness and conditional extension argument provide useful explanations of why successful finite training checks cannot establish unrestricted kernel validity. The paper appropriately distinguishes this issue from the ability to evaluate an SVM decision function.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. I checked Gneiting's Theorem 7 and Example 1 [1], Jaquier et al.'s finite-sample calibration experiments [2], and the relevant circle and symmetric-space results [3,4]. The exact $0<q\le1$ guarantee and global failure for $q>1$ are established prior work. Jaquier et al. already measure positive-definiteness frequencies and minimum eigenvalues, including the reported $S^2$ calibration value $6.5$. Da Costa et al. also discuss the distinction between fixed finite datasets and incoming data. These precedents rule out novelty for those broad observations, but I did not find the manuscript's explicit random-sample probability bounds in the sources examined. The supplied rStation index contains no overlapping geodesic-kernel paper; its existing recursive-teaching-dimension contribution concerns a different question. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority guarantee.

The retained evidence supports the main empirical account. Running the inspected checker reproduced $297/300$ training successes, $0/297$ conditional append survivors, $94{,}081/118{,}800$ correct predictions, the reported intervals, and the descriptive transition fit. Fresh bundled-solver refits for repetitions 0, 30, and 60 matched all 400 saved predictions in each case. Separately written calculations using 65-decimal arithmetic and hash-verified regenerated coordinates found positive Cholesky pivots for repetition 0's training matrix, a negative pivot after its 100-point extension, and a negative training pivot for repetition 110. These are high-precision numerical spot checks, not directed-interval certificates. I inspected the bundled interval implementation but did not execute it because MPFR development headers were unavailable. I did not independently recertify the complete grid or reproduce the validation search or bootstrap. The supplied audit remains author-provided supporting evidence, not a substitute for these distinctions.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, explains both proof strategies, and distinguishes rigorous bounds from finite-range observations. The Bayes benchmark, valid-kernel controls, repetition-level accuracy intervals, and paired comparison make the classification results interpretable. The square-root fit is explicitly descriptive; neither its small residual nor its bootstrap intervals establish an asymptotic exponent. The unresolved original-figure discrepancy is presented as an observation under a stated interpretation, without attributing an unsupported cause.

The following improvements would strengthen the presentation and reproducibility without changing this decision:

1. Expand the compact replay package for the secondary analyses. The uniform-grid file retains signs but not the underlying coordinates or a complete seed recipe, and the validation-selection and bootstrap computations are not replayed. Archive their generators and analysis scripts, and replace references to unavailable original files with precise archive links. Retained sign tallies alone do not reproduce numerical certification.
2. Bring the relevant circle/symmetric-space literature [3,4] into the main related-work discussion, where it will clarify the distinction between known global obstruction and the quantitative finite-sample contribution. Use consistent numbered citations throughout; the current bibliography uses unnumbered bullets. Typeset the supplement's mathematical statements with LaTeX delimiters as well.
3. A compact plot of the finite transition brackets and a plot separating training success from append survival would improve accessibility, while preserving the underlying tables. Keep the stated limits on extrapolation, conditioning, and the exploratory $\lambda=5$ result.
