# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive contribution to the combinatorics of decision stumps. Its strongest results are the distinct-error-set constructions and the random-order expectation theorem, supported by exact named-count formulas and complete finite extremal curves. These results fit the machine-learning scope and constitute a useful partial answer to the effective-stump question in [1]. The paper clearly excludes the general distinct-set formula and the number of stumps selected along an AdaBoost trajectory from its claims.

The distinction between named hypotheses and distinct mistake patterns is essential and handled correctly. The one-feature minimality argument identifies good transition stumps and the necessary endpoint exceptions. Global minimality implies local minimality, yielding the named upper bounds; repeated alternating orders attain them, with the stated parity correction for shared constants. The marker construction prevents cross-feature domination through distinct equal-cardinality tags, while its treatment of cuts outside the active block excludes the remaining competitors. The cyclic construction and odd-sample saturation arguments also hold, including the equality case of the Boolean antichain bound needed for the transition-only deficit.

The expectation theorem has a valid central argument. For fixed prediction vectors at Hamming distance $D$, nonstrict inclusion of the competitor's error set has probability $2^{-D}$ under fair independent labels. The proof bounds losses without conditioning on candidate goodness, avoiding a potentially serious dependence error. Conditioning independent Bernoulli predictions to a fixed-size subset costs at most $m+1$, and the paired-coordinate bound gives $(9/16)^a$. The resulting union bound supports the stated asymptotics when $\log d=o(m)$. The manuscript appropriately presents this as an expectation result under a particular distribution, without asserting concentration or practical boosting performance.

The finite results have inspectable, reproducible evidence. I inspected the checker and freshly ran its witness, formula and closure checks: all 226 witnesses, every one-feature label word through $m=12$, and all 30 closure certificates passed. The closure/depth argument is sufficient to certify every dimension: the edge inequality makes the assigned depth a lower bound on any realization's feature count, and explicit witnesses supply matching lower bounds. Thus the infinite tails do not rest on extrapolating a plateau. I also reran all three raw-permutation exclusions at $(m,d)=(7,3)$ and $(8,2)$, obtaining respectively $17/16/17$ and $14/14/14$ in A/T/C order. These were fresh executions of inspected supplied code, not a separately written implementation. I did not rerun the complete 64-target exclusion suite or the supplementary simulations; neither limitation undermines the analytic arguments or the verified closure certificates.

The novelty distinction is credible. Direct reading of [1], Appendix H of [2], and Theorem 7 of [3] confirms that effective-pattern pruning and counts before error-based pruning are established context. Appendix H provides definitions, empirical observations and generalization statements, rather than the extremal formulas or random-order theorem proved here. The partitioning theorem counts all realizable prediction patterns and does not imply the submitted minimal-error-set curves. Focused external terminology and citation searches located no matching earlier result, although this is not an exhaustive priority guarantee. The two published rStation papers in this snapshot concern recursive teaching dimension and geodesic kernels; their contributions do not substantially overlap this submission. The manuscript appropriately credits its classical ingredients and acknowledges the limits of its priority search.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, conventions, principal results, proof strategies and remaining open cases are identifiable in the main text. Detailed proofs and enumeration records are appropriately placed in the supplement. The metadata abstract is informative. Only minor presentation changes are needed:

- Use consistent numbered citations [1], [2], etc., with corresponding numbered bibliography entries, rather than relying on author links and unnumbered bullets.
- Identify the Boolean antichain inequality explicitly as Sperner's theorem and provide a standard reference; retaining the short self-contained proof is useful.
- Shorten the repeated discussion of the original note's track rejection and the audit's favorable verdict. Neither establishes scientific validity. The mathematical statements, reproducible checks and precise scope already provide the relevant evidence.

These are editorial improvements, not defects in the contribution or its support. Acceptance applies to the explicitly stated unrestricted rank-data model and partial results.
