# Reciprocal phases of fractional Euler coefficients at log-squared smoothness

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-12T06:10:18.152259Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general/papers/4/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study the distribution modulo one of real reciprocal phases $T/n$ on smooth integers with canonical fractional Euler weights. Let $\alpha(p^r)=(w_p)_r/r!$, let $\nu=\alpha^{-1}$ for Dirichlet convolution, and assume the weights equal one up to $y_0$, vanish above $Y$, and satisfy $y_0\sim Y\sim\kappa(\log N)^2$ with $\kappa>1/(4e^2)$. For $T\asymp N^{3/2}$, we prove a discrepancy bound for each of $\alpha,\nu,|\nu|$, uniformly over subintervals of $[N,2N]$, half-open arcs, arbitrary Mellin twists, and completely multiplicative twists of modulus at most one. Relative to the original positive block mass, or the inverse coefficients' total mass, the bound saves $\exp(-(B_\kappa/2+o(1))\log N/\log\log N)$, where $B_\kappa=(1+\log(2\sqrt\kappa))/2$. In particular, the positive weighted count has its predicted asymptotic in every arc of length at least $\exp(-c\log N/\log\log N)$ for fixed $c<B_\kappa/2$. The proof combines a classical first-derivative Gram estimate with an exact-ratio collision bound whose multiplicities involve only short divisors. A unique stopping-prime factorization and one shared beta variable transfer the estimate to signed inverse coefficients while charging the square norms to one original counting mass. Exact cap separation and an elementary positive Fourier majorant retain all endpoints and approximation errors. The classical saddle-point theorem supplies the critical density surplus. Exact rational CPU checks verify the coefficient and collision identities. The result does not cover the level range $T\asymp N$, estimate an uncentered signed mean, or imply a zero-free region or RH.
