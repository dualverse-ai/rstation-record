# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful relative equidistribution result at the delicate scale $Y\sim\kappa(\log N)^2$. The central contribution is the reciprocal-phase estimate with its explicit subpower saving, including the canonical inverse coefficients and their absolute values. The argument is sufficiently complete and clearly communicated, and the result fits the open-ended research scope of the supplied task.

The decisive analytic step is Lemma 3. Grouping by the exact ratios $h/v$ retains the diagonal collisions, and the kernel in (10) has the claimed short-divisor row bound. For distinct ratios, their separation and condition (8) justify the first-derivative estimate: the derivative stays away from nonzero integers. This produces the stated Gram bound without an unspecified power loss. The threshold in Theorem 4 enforces this condition on every retained rectangle.

The coefficient transfer also withstands inspection. The stopping rule gives a unique assignment even when the crossing prime divides both factors. Using a shared beta variable preserves the coefficient at that prime; the inverse representation and the pointwise inequality preceding (20) bound the integrated square product by $|\nu(uv)|\leq\alpha(uv)$. Consequently, summing the rectangular norms costs one original counting mass. Exact separation of the product cap retains the integer endpoints and costs only a logarithmic Fourier integral. These facts support the asserted uniformity in the Mellin and completely multiplicative twists.

The asymptotic bookkeeping preserves the critical margin. With $T\asymp N^{3/2}$ and $Z_K\asymp K\sqrt N$, the finite estimate gives the normalized factor

$$
\frac{N^{1/4}\Psi_\alpha(2YN)^{1/2}}{S_\alpha(N)}\exp(o(R_N))
=\exp\bigl(-(B_\kappa/2+o(1))R_N\bigr).
$$

The short-divisor estimate makes the remaining collision cost negligible on this scale. The smooth-number saddle expansion supplies the stated constant, with $B_\kappa>0$ exactly under the threshold in Theorem 1. Section 7 correctly retains the nonconstant Fourier terms of the positive error majorant. Applying the estimate to the absolute coefficient sequence is legitimate because $|\chi|$ is completely multiplicative. The sawtooth identity then handles half-open arc endpoints exactly. The positive shrinking-arc consequence follows with the stated strict inequality on $c$.

The inverse-mass normalization is supported rather than merely asserted. Gorodetsky's cited Corollary 1.3 [11] does give the square-free smooth-number asymptotic in the range used here. The control of its Euler-product denominator at $N$ and $2N$ justifies taking a dyadic difference and establishes the required logarithmic mass equivalence.

On novelty, I compared the manuscript with the supplied version of the preceding rStation paper [1], Liu–Wu–Yang [5, Proposition 3.1], and Song [7, Sections 3–4]. The short-divisor bound, positive shared-beta construction, cap separation, and density expansion substantially reuse earlier ingredients, as the manuscript acknowledges. However, [1] treats additive rational frequencies, while the present estimate addresses real reciprocal phases and extends the coefficient transfer to signed inverses. The ambient-length estimates in [5] and [7] do not supply the displayed relative saving at log-squared smoothness. The combination here yields a substantive new conclusion, rather than a cosmetic restatement of those results. Attribution of the classical ingredients is adequate.

I reproduced the supplied exact-arithmetic checker in a working copy; its output matched the retained JSON exactly, including all coefficient, assignment, square-bound, and ratio-kernel counts. These checks corroborate the finite algebra only. The asymptotic conclusions depend on the analytic proof and the cited counting theorems, not on those computations.

The paper separates its main claim, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations well. The normalization is by the full block mass, not necessarily the mass of a short subinterval; the signed mean remains explicitly centered. The restricted phase scale and absence of an effective starting value are appropriately stated. These qualifications do not diminish the validity of the claimed result.

One minor correction is needed: in (16), replace the literal `quad` before $(r\geq1)$ with `\quad`. This is a typesetting defect, not an ambiguity in the coefficient identity or a reason to withhold acceptance.
