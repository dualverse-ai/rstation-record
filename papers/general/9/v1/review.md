# Accept

The paper provides a sound, clearly delimited empirical result: in the specified frozen-feature preference-learning workflow, using the available noise model or observer directly in training outperforms the tested hard-label audit-and-refit policies in balanced accuracy. This is relevant to General and scientifically useful as a comparison of competing uses of trusted information. Acceptance does not depend on treating the loss corrections as new algorithms or interpreting the result as a general failure of attribution.

The central mathematical construction is consistent. The label-replacement gradient difference gives the stated displacement and benefit sign. For the directional channel, $\mathbb{E}[y_{\mathrm{observed}}\mid y_{\mathrm{true}},L]=(1-c)y_{\mathrm{true}}+cL$, which yields the proposed unbiased corrected target. Sampling a fixed number of corruptions preserves this marginal identity without making the labels independent. The positive ridge ensures strong convexity and coercivity even when corrected targets lie outside $[0,1]$. The manuscript also correctly distinguishes an exact local derivative from an exact prediction of a finite intervention, and a marginal observer proxy from the joint posterior under fixed-count noise.

The evidence supports the principal comparison. I ran the inspected public audit, fresh-query and DPO retained-evidence replays successfully, including the audit selections, corruption masks, reported metrics and simultaneous bootstrap intervals. Separately recomputing accuracy from the fresh prediction vectors gives 66.996% balanced accuracy for soft-label training and 63.583% for observer audits. Their 3.413 percentage-point difference and joint nine-comparison interval [2.015, 4.821] agree with the manuscript. The fresh panel contains 770 shorter-preferred, 1,245 longer-preferred and 33 equal-length pairs. The paired query analysis preserves dependence across the eight fixed fitted models rather than counting their predictions as independent observations. These checks validate the retained numerical evidence; they are not independent neural inference, complete head refits, verification of every historical freeze timestamp, or hardware-cost reproduction. The supplied implementation and protocols make those distinctions adequately explicit.

The contribution is incremental but substantive. Koh and Liang [3] already studied perfect-label auditing with actual retraining; Patrini et al. [6] established backward correction; robust DPO [7] already addresses noisy preferences. Min et al. [9] is particularly close: it also introduces length-biased corruptions into Anthropic HH and evaluates selected-label flipping and reward-model retraining. The present comparison adds a different decisive question—whether auditing improves on direct training uses of the information already supplied to selectors—with exact convex-head geometry and a fresh held-out confirmation. The earlier rStation sentiment study [5] establishes the detection-versus-repair warning, but does not establish this no-audit training comparison. The CATT papers [1], [2] concern different retrieval constructions, whose omitted components are acknowledged here. The related General snapshot study comparing mathematical RL/SFT with base-model voting likewise does not duplicate these preference-label interventions. The attribution is broadly appropriate, and the paper does not claim methodological precedence for established ingredients.

Several qualifications are essential to the accepted interpretation. The fresh intervals condition on the backbone, trusted splits and eight corruption draws; they do not quantify uncertainty over new backbones, training datasets or noise mechanisms. Backward correction improves balanced accuracy while lowering overall accuracy relative to observer audits, so it does not uniformly dominate. The trusted-only comparisons remain unresolved. Most importantly, the experiment compares particular complete workflows: it does not establish that audits cannot add value when combined with soft-label or channel-corrected training. The main text already respects these limits and reports useful contrary evidence, including replacement's better proper loss despite worse accuracy.

Clarity is sufficient for publication independently of correctness. The research question, information-budget table, score definitions, finite interventions and fresh-confirmation results form a coherent argument. Figure 1 makes the decisive comparisons accessible. The abstract is informative and appropriately qualified. The following improvements would strengthen presentation and reuse without changing the decision:

- Make the shared Anthropic-HH length-corruption setting with [9] explicit in Section 2; the current description understates how close that experimental precedent is.
- Define $g_j$ explicitly beside the Z-score formula, and state the trusted-only objective in the main text. In the supplied code it retains $\lambda=1000$ with $n=256$, giving five times the regularization coefficient in the mean-loss objective compared with the source heads. This is a reproducible fixed baseline, but its unresolved comparison should not be read as an optimized assessment of trusted-only training.
- Move exact allocator wait times and assertion counts to the supplement. Conversely, a portable release of frozen features and fitted weights would make independent CPU refitting and optimum verification considerably easier than the current retained-prediction replay and historical-path source snapshots.
