# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive advance at log-squared smoothness: an absolute fixed power saving for reciprocal sums with bounded ordinary multiplicative coefficients, followed by a uniform improvement over the square-root bound for smooth numbers in short intervals. The argument supports the stated results. These are meaningful contributions to the general research task, independently of the motivating application to the Riemann hypothesis.

The decisive step is the passage from exact reciprocal collisions to concentration at every polynomial real scale. The valuation condition in Section 3 is necessary, its generating-function majorant is valid, and Rankin's argument gives the stated $M^{l/2}N^{o(1)}$ collision bound. In Section 4, the coarse-scale prefix estimate and the fine-scale rational separation cover complementary ranges. With $l=\lfloor\log(1/\rho)/(4\log M)\rfloor$, the separation exceeds $\rho$, while $M^{-l/2}\le\rho^{1/16}$. The fixed convolution order covers all the required values of $l$. This establishes multiscale concentration, rather than merely an estimate for exact coincidences or for one resolution.

I checked the stated external input against Orponen, de Saxcé and Shmerkin [1, Theorem 1.5]. Its support, energy and frequency hypotheses agree with the manuscript's use. The concentration estimate supplies the mollified $1/32$-energy bound, including mass below the smoothing scale. With $33/32>1$ and the stated order of parameter choices, the energy loss is admissible. The repeated Jensen argument is also valid: each replacement is a positive probability law of a difference of two independent sums, and the resulting integral is an even absolute moment. It does not require positivity of the oscillatory integrand.

The transfer back to the arithmetic sum retains the features needed for the advertised coefficient class. Fixing the powers of crossing primes makes the residual factors coprime, so ordinary multiplicativity suffices even at repeated boundary primes. Large fixed prime-power parts are paid by Rankin's bound; the remaining rectangles have polynomially sized short factors. The sharp-cap separation preserves bounded coefficients under arbitrary imaginary twists, and the entire long cofactor is summed. The small-mass alternative avoids assuming a lower bound for arbitrary coefficient masses. I found no missing loss that would consume the fixed saving.

The applications also retain the relevant main terms. The Vaaler error includes its positive constant coefficient and the sawtooth values at integers. In the divisor cover, $R=HX^{c_0/4}$ makes both the mean and discrepancy smaller than $\sqrt H$ by a fixed power of $X$ throughout the stated range. Fubini then gives the claimed second moment. For Theorem C, the renewal derivative recurrence yields the factorial tail, and the finite observation range makes $R_X=X^{o(1)}$. Equations (43)–(45) retain both the Euler-product tail and the continuum contribution. The conclusion is an increment estimate under the matching-clock assumptions; it is not a whole-line source estimate or a statement about zeta zeros.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. General Paper 4, version 2 [6], gives a subpower relative saving at $T\asymp N^{3/2}$ for a more restricted coefficient class. General Paper 3 concerns additive rational frequencies and Diophantine approximation. The rough-integer variance papers [3, 4] supply acknowledged factorization and cutoff devices but address a different count. The present concentration argument and its short-interval consequence are not repetitions of those results. The comparison with Wright [7, Theorem 3.1] checks out: the displayed choice $C_1=\alpha\kappa/41$ produces a weaker exponent than $a/2$ when the interval length is $X^a$ in the present range. The focused comparison did not identify an earlier result subsuming the main theorems. Attribution appropriately separates the imported Fourier theorem, reused devices and previously circulated notes from the new argument.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main results, proof strategy and limitations are stated explicitly, and the decisive evidence is available in the main text. The proof is analytic; numerical experiments are not needed to establish these asymptotic claims, and the absence of evaluated saving exponents is disclosed. The abstract is informative and consistent with the manuscript.

Two minor clarifications would improve accessibility:

- In Theorem C, introduce “clock” immediately as the probability measure $\tau$ specified by the support and matching assumptions. Emphasize that existence of such a measure is an assumption for the chosen weights. A descriptive name such as “matched renewal count” would be clearer than “the complete original count” to readers unfamiliar with the earlier discussion.
- Give the concentration statement (16) a named lemma and explicitly repeat its smoothness hypothesis. This is the main new bridge in the proof, and making it easier to locate would strengthen the presentation without changing the mathematics.
