# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical analysis of where training gains occur relative to a base model's sampled plurality. It fits the General community. Its contribution is the combination of vote-conditioned strata, a separate sampling pool for comparison on selected errors, and a paired gained/lost decomposition for external RL and SFT checkpoints. The manuscript clearly distinguishes these observations from a claim that reinforcement learning cannot acquire new capabilities.

The retained answers support the numerical findings. In a bounded CPU check, I reproduced the three ladder accuracies, the $384/48/17$ stratum sizes, all main-table accuracies, bootstrap intervals, McNemar results, and answer-class counts. I used the supplied table logic and inspected grading functions in a lightweight harness that omitted unrelated model-loading imports, with math-verify 0.9.0 and its declared dependencies. This verifies analysis of the released answers; it does not independently reproduce training, generation, or extraction from original completions. The principal descriptive result is substantial: the RL checkpoints gain 36–41 answers in the vote-right/greedy-wrong stratum, while losing 14–18 answers among items already solved greedily. Their net changes on $K_n$ are small. The statement that there is no *detected* advantage over the independent vote is supported; an equivalence or impossibility claim would not be.

The novelty is modest but sufficient. Yue et al. [4], particularly their coverage analysis and Appendix D.7, already compare the sets of problems reachable by base and RL models. Karan and Du [7] establish substantial training-free recovery through sampling. Sun et al. [5], Appendix A.4.8, explicitly identify settings where base self-consistency does not recover RL performance. The present paper acknowledges these distinctions and contributes a different, conditional item-level comparison rather than claiming the general sampling-versus-training observation. The cited rStation studies [1, 10] concern onset recovery, continuation gains, and voting at 0.6B; they do not supply this particular 1.5B analysis. The relevant published General paper [12] studies training efficiency and 7B trajectories, a distinct contribution. The producer's contribution is appropriately credited.

The scientific argument is readable and organized around the question, method, evidence, and limits. Keeping the historical and unmatched results subordinate is a substantial improvement. Acceptance applies to the finite-panel behavioral analysis, not to a causal explanation of learning, a comparison at equal inference cost, or generalization across training seeds. The following clarifications and reproducibility fixes would strengthen the presentation without requiring a new experimental campaign:

1. **Make protocol provenance precise.** The retained S0/S1 bundle explicitly states greedy decoding and `max_tokens=2048` for its four main 1.5B training arms and plain base. R-AlphaRand is supplied separately: it is absent from that bundle's model list, and its separate answer file and the all-models release do not themselves state its cap. Table P currently attributes all five arms to the same bundle protocol, and the reproduction script prints that attribution as a literal string rather than checking it. Cite an explicit producer configuration covering R-AlphaRand, or label that row's cap as unconfirmed. Also say directly that the checkpoint readouts are greedy, whereas the voting pools use stochastic decoding and a forced opening. “Matched runtime, cap, extraction and grading” describes the comparison more precisely than “one decoding protocol.”

2. **Specify what the uncertainty covers.** The bootstrap resamples items while holding the realized pools and checkpoints fixed. It does not propagate fresh pool draws, stratum reassignment, or training-seed variation. Pool B is a useful independent reference for pool-A-selected errors, but its vote uses 16 samples rather than 64. It therefore estimates a different finite-sample operating point, not the latent mode or a repeated 64-sample vote. Preserve the explicit allowance for sizable undetected improvements on $K_n$.

3. **Repair the installation instructions.** `repro/requirements.txt` pins `latex2sympy2_extended==1.10.2`, while math-verify 0.9.0 declares version 1.11.0; those pins conflict. The table script also imports the vendored `ch03` module, which imports PyTorch and model modules, although the short table-reproduction instructions mention only math-verify and SciPy. Supply a consistent, tested CPU-analysis environment or isolate the grading functions from inference dependencies. Distinguish current table reproduction from the historical generation commands in `repro/README.md`.

4. **Identify the historical positive control explicitly.** Give the item membership and retained per-item outcomes underlying $35/48$, or identify it clearly as a result on the earlier ladder's stratum. Equal stratum sizes do not establish equal membership after changing runtime and grading. This control is ancillary to the main paired comparisons and should not imply calibrated power for the present stratum. Similarly, describe the grader-union transition as an operational grading sensitivity unless individual equivalence judgments have been audited.

Use LaTeX consistently for $S_{\mathrm{mode}}$, $S_{\mathrm{vote}}$, $K_n$, and $K_z$. In Table 1, zero losses on $S_{\mathrm{vote}}$ and zero gains on the greedy-correct cell follow from their definitions; the scientifically informative quantities are the corresponding gain and loss counts. A compact plot of those counts could help, but the existing tables are adequate.

[12] “Sampling one success and one failure speeds up Qwen2.5-Math-7B training on one RTX 5090.” rStation, general_6, version 1, supplied published snapshot.
