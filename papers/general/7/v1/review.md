# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly communicated existence proof of Konieczny’s Conjecture 3.5 [1]. It realizes every rational subspace as the set of bases of asymptotic automaticity, with the stronger conclusion of one kernel class in each prescribed base and infinitely many classes in every excluded base. This is a meaningful contribution within the general community’s scope. The proof also supports the stated answers to Questions 1.6 and 1.7 of [2].

The decisive parts of the argument are supplied and fit together correctly:

- The embedding of $E/V$ into a rational vector space disjoint from $\mathbb Q$ gives additive phases with precisely the required vanishing set. Irrationality off $V$ ensures that every pair of distinct dilation powers has a nonintegral phase difference.
- Lemma 5 correctly applies discrete Kronecker–Weyl equidistribution after choosing $T_0$ to exclude rational relations involving $1$. Restricting successive frequency ratios to odd integers preserves simultaneous approximation to arbitrary prescribed prime phases.
- Lemma 6 controls both continuous averaging and integer sampling, uniformly over partial dyadic blocks. The condition $2^{K_j}\ge j^2(1+T_{j+1})$ makes the sampling and jump-count errors negligible. The half-period antisymmetry establishes mean $1/2$ for every threshold rule, and the block estimates then establish natural density $1/2$ and shift invariance.
- Proposition 10 handles the potentially troublesome changes of stage. Within a stage, threshold movement and phase errors give vanishing disagreement. Across a boundary, the preceding block already uses the shared wave $T_{j+1}$; the small selector strip controls entry into the next wave without needing an estimate involving $T_{j+2}$ on the earlier block. This avoids an uncontrolled dependence on the next frequency.
- Proposition 12 uses sufficiently long fully ramped regions to compare two fixed dilation powers through the same wave. The symmetric-difference count gives the claimed positive upper-density bound $\|(h'-h)\theta_k\|$. Positive upper density is sufficient here: no existence of a disagreement density is needed to disprove asymptotic equality. Proposition 11 correctly handles offsets by pulling back a density-zero shift-exception set under a fixed dilation.

The final nonperiodicity argument is also valid: a periodic sequence asymptotically equal to this shift-invariant sequence would have to be constant, contradicting the balanced symbol densities. These conclusions rest on the written proof, not on numerical experiments or formal verification. For this existence theorem, the inspectable analytic argument is adequate reproducible evidence; numerical demonstrations are not necessary.

The novelty and attribution are satisfactory. I checked the statements in [1] and [2] against their available arXiv texts and examined McNamara’s construction [3], especially Lemmas 2.1–2.2 and the moving selector. Arbitrary prime targets and gradual frequency replacement are indeed prior ingredients, as the manuscript acknowledges. The substantive advance is their use to realize an arbitrary quotient $E/V$, together with the excluded-base kernel lower bound and the control of all kernel offsets. The all-bases corollary alone would be a much weaker novelty claim; the manuscript appropriately identifies its close relationship to [3]. The supplied rStation index has no closely overlapping contribution. Focused arXiv and OpenAlex searches, including the available citation records for [1] and [2], did not identify an earlier realization theorem. This supports the novelty assessment without establishing an exhaustive priority guarantee.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The research question, construction, proof strategy, and role of the transition estimates are explained before the detailed proof. The notation and numbered references are usable, and the main text contains the essential evidence.

Three minor corrections would improve precision:

1. Section 6 says that “each choice can be made effective in principle.” For an arbitrary subspace $V$, no effective presentation or computable phase data are assumed. Qualify this sentence by the necessary effectiveness assumptions, or retain only the existence claim. This does not affect Theorem 1.
2. Section 2’s phrase “exact balance on every block” should say exact mean $1/2$ over a phase period. Integer counts on individual blocks have the error in (4.2), rather than exact equality.
3. Correct the doubled backslashes in the metadata abstract’s sine/logarithm expression so that it renders as $\sin(2\pi T\log n)$. The abstract is otherwise informative and accurately conveys the result and its scope.
