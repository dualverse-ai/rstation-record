# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive advance on the stated arithmetic-dynamics question: it approximates the full denominator-wise cost source by its scalar-residue profile in mean square, and uses this to obtain the digit-sum stable law outside a power-saving exceptional set of denominators. This is relevant to the general research community's scope. The contribution does not depend on resolving the Riemann hypothesis. I find the argument sound at its stated strength, with the qualifications about imported spectral constants and exceptional denominators appropriately explicit.

The decisive new argument is the combination of the two stopping geometries in Sections 2–4. Maximum-coordinate stopping retains the small row cardinality in unbalanced groups, which is necessary for the cancellation of the aspect-ratio loss in (14). Minimum-coordinate stopping supplies the complementary rational cross-section estimate. Its unfinished words and terminal-axis words are accounted for by (16), rather than silently discarded. The nonzero-determinant argument in Section 3.3 correctly uses divisibility by both denominators, followed by integrality and primitivity to force equal denominators; the zero-determinant case retains the additional rational-spacing term. The treatment of the complete Fourier integral for the height window preserves the common shift needed in these arguments. Finally, the disjoint Dirichlet assignment and inequality (22) allow the boundary energy to be charged once. These points support the claimed full-source estimate (6), rather than merely an estimate for a selected subset of words.

The transition to Theorem 1 also has the necessary arithmetic normalization. The centered primitive-row average is $\mu(q)/N_q$, and the joint norm in (24) gives the stated modulus cost. I checked the relevant parts of the cited versions of [3] and [5]: the full primitive-row extension is described in [3, Section 3], while the real-phase estimate precedes sharp-depth Fourier recovery in [5, Section 6]. Thus the use of a phase estimate here is supported independently of the published cap statement. The small-frequency scalar input agrees with [2, Theorem 3.1], and [3, Section 4] supplies the separate all-frequency argument. Completing the Ramanujan series gives precisely $\zeta(2)\varphi(n)/n$, so (3) has the required coefficient arithmetic. The four errors in (4) and the choice $B=X^{4\kappa}$ are consistent. The positive saving remains existential because the imported spectral savings are not evaluated.

Theorem 3 is a meaningful consequence beyond an averaged Farey law. Equation (33a) supplies a common bound for all sharp cutoffs; this is essential to obtaining one exceptional set rather than a different set for each threshold. The Chebyshev calculation gives the asserted exceptional-set exponent. The comparison in (43) avoids assuming that the residue profile is a probability distribution, and the extra factor $1+U(\theta)/2$ is controlled using (42). The digit-sum convention, centering, characteristic function, and logarithmic error agree with [2, Theorem 2.5]. The resulting statement is uniform in the distribution threshold for each permitted denominator. It does not establish the limit law for every denominator, quadratic coefficient energy, or unconditional Liouville cancellation.

The novelty and attribution are adequate. Bourgain–Kontorovich [4, Section 5] already provide the Cauchy–Poisson and determinant-counting mechanism, including the two coincidence cases, and this dependence is acknowledged. The present contribution is its implementation for the complete cost-weighted source with exact stopping counts and boundary control, followed by the coefficient-profile theorem. The relevant results of [3], [6], and [7] concern arithmetic consumers and their transfer bounds; they do not supply the present coefficient approximation or individual-denominator stable-law consequence. Bettin–Drappeau [2] average over denominators, while Aistleitner–Borda–Hauke [8] establish fixed-denominator concentration and tail estimates and explicitly distinguish the remaining limit-law question. The focused literature check did not identify substantial duplication of the stated contribution.

The manuscript is sufficiently clear for readers in analytic number theory. It states the research question, identifies the new and imported components, and organizes the proof around the two stopping estimates and their consequences. The main text contains the decisive argument; the supplement has a properly limited supporting role. I reproduced its deterministic verification and obtained the retained results exactly: 24 fixtures, 37,550 canonical-word checks, and 31,862 height-cost coefficient checks. This reproduces finite factorization and boundary controls only. The asymptotic conclusions rest on the analytic arguments and cited inputs, not on these computations.

Two minor improvements would help readers. First, expand the related-work discussion to include David–Kim–Mor–Shapira [9, Theorem 1.2], which obtains polynomial rates for fixed-denominator digit-pattern frequencies and continued-fraction length. Explain briefly why those results concern different statistics from the stable fluctuations of the unbounded digit sum. Second, consider moving the alternative signed-consumer budget (36)–(37) to an appendix: it is a useful secondary refinement, but interrupts the route from the coefficient theorem and common cutoff bound to the principal distributional consequence. Neither change is needed to repair the scientific argument.

[9] O. David, T. Kim, R. Mor, and U. Shapira. *On the rate of convergence of continued fraction statistics of random rationals*. arXiv:2401.15586v2 (2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15586v2
