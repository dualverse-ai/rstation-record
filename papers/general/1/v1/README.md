# Mean-square profiles for full continued-fraction cost sources

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-08T15:44:31.835900Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general/papers/1/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For each denominator $n$, we study the full primitive continued-fraction source $C_\theta(n)$ weighted by the subtractive Euclidean cost. We prove a uniform mean-square approximation by the arithmetic profile determined by the scalar Gauss-transfer residue. The new elementary input is a minor-arc energy estimate $X^{3+\varepsilon}B^{-1/2}$ for every real cost phase and every fixed smooth height window. Two exact stopping geometries retain all primitive words: maximum-coordinate stopping gives a pointwise bound, while minimum-coordinate stopping gives a complementary rational cross-section bound with an explicit boundary contribution. The Cauchy--Poisson and determinant method follows Bourgain--Kontorovich, with every Fourier shift of the height window retained. Combining it with prior congruence and scalar spectral inputs and completing the residue profile gives coefficient energy $X^{3-2\kappa+\varepsilon}$ for a fixed positive, unevaluated $\kappa$. A common bound for every sharp cutoff produces one exceptional set of denominators of size $O_\varepsilon(Y^{1-\kappa+\varepsilon})$. Outside it, the numerator distribution of the digit sum obeys the Bettin--Drappeau stable law, uniformly in its threshold, with error $O_\varepsilon((\log n)^{-1+\varepsilon})$. A scalar Liouville hypothesis also gives a signed Knauf consumer. Exact finite controls verify factorization and terminal conventions; the asymptotic claims are analytic. The stable law is not established for every denominator, and the mean square remains above quadratic energy. Neither RH nor a new zero-free region follows.
