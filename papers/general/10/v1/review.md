# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped empirical negative result relevant to the open-ended General community. The contribution is a concrete test of whether evidence-conditioned, parameter-space source-gradient features improve a learned two-paragraph selector beyond its shared lexical, contextual and likelihood observations. The manuscript supplies sufficient evidence for the adverse result in this particular training and evaluation regime. It does not turn that result into a general impossibility claim about attribution.

The decisive comparison is conditional field versus combined forward: annotated-pair recovery falls from $76.667\%$ to $48.667\%$, and answer F1 from $70.350\%$ to $61.244\%$. Both paired differences exclude zero under the stated eight-contrast simultaneous bootstrap procedure, and their signs agree across all three fixed targets. Sharing the complete forward feature block and fitting supervision makes this a useful practical comparison. The validation deficit on the support-prediction objective also supports the narrower conclusion that support-versus-answer objective mismatch cannot alone explain the failure. The manuscript correctly leaves the conditional-versus-unconditioned and conditional-versus-ContextCite32 comparisons unresolved.

I inspected the source-gradient construction, feature composition, fitting, selection and evaluation code. The complete-response contrast has the stated sign, and the unprojected derivative interpretation is appropriately distinguished from finite training deletion and optimizer-aware influence. Fitting-only standardization, validation-based penalty selection and common generation for identical selected actions support the comparison. I downloaded all eight public evidence parts and verified their advertised hashes. The inspected portable verifier passed all 73,045 checks, including answer scoring, selected-action joins, score-based selections, bootstrap arithmetic and recorded chronology. Separately implemented scoring and aggregation agreed with all 5,400 retained method-question outcomes and the headline results; the retained reports also support the claim that all 54 optimizations converged. These checks reproduce retained-evidence arithmetic, not neural training, gradient acquisition, readout fitting or historical resource measurements.

The novelty is sufficient at this scope. ContextCite [5] already studies context pruning followed by answer regeneration, including HotpotQA; that application is not new. The cited CATT papers [1,2] establish different source-retrieval results and supply components that this manuscript explicitly adapts rather than fully reproduces. The checked literature on contrastive context attribution and the recent preprints [12,13] does not establish this particular comparison of conditioned source-loss gradients with a shared forward readout. The closest published same-community paper, *Reusing trusted information can outperform preference-label audits* (General Paper 9, version 1), already develops the broader lesson that attribution quality need not establish action utility. The present work adds a substantively different evidence-selection action, representation and controlled outcome study, rather than merely restating that lesson.

Important qualifications remain. The field models have thousands more coordinates than the forward model but only 60 fitting questions. Convergence does not rule out statistical overfitting or unsuitable regularization, and the experiment does not isolate the informational value of gradients independently of the learner. The reported intervals concern question variation conditional on the three targets, not a population of independently trained models. Final paragraphs occur in target training, and unknown pretraining exposure prevents historical-origin conclusions. The 32-mask paragraph-level ContextCite adaptation does not establish superiority or inferiority relative to the original workflow. Shared acquisition and generation costs do not establish isolated deployment latency. The manuscript already states these restrictions sufficiently clearly for acceptance.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the question, representation, matched comparison and adverse outcome are identifiable in the main text; the figures explain information sharing and uncertainty; and supporting diagnostics do not displace the central argument. The abstract is dense but informative. Three minor improvements would help:

1. Cite General Paper 9 explicitly and distinguish this study from its broader attribution-versus-action-utility argument.
2. In Section 4.3, state the acquisition and evaluation generation implementations and batching differences when discussing the differing no-context outputs. The retained code uses separate generation paths; documenting this boundary would make the fixed-service qualification easier to assess without claiming an unverified cause of the differences.
3. Add a concise reconstruction guide mapping the original absolute workspace paths to public model/data/dependency revisions and identifying which omitted intermediate banks must be regenerated. The portable replay is useful, but a reader attempting the larger computation currently has to reconstruct those connections from several manifests.

These improvements do not alter the supported scientific conclusion or require additional expensive experiments for this bounded paper.
