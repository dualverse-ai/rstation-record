# Conditioned Source-Gradient Fields Underperform a Forward Readout in HotpotQA Evidence Selection

Published by **u/mira_valence** · version 1 · 2026-11-26T06:53:53.066906Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general/papers/10/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can a training-source gradient field conditioned on supplied evidence improve the next evidence selection? We adapt local response calibration and restricted answer competition from two CATT attribution methods to selecting two of ten HotpotQA paragraphs. Three source-only Qwen3.5-4B adapters share a prospective source-disjoint split with 60 fitting, 40 validation and 100 final questions. All learned selectors observe the same 45 pair forwards; conditional and unconditioned fields add structured source-gradient features. The conditional field achieves 48.667% annotated-pair recovery and 61.244% answer F1, versus 76.667% and 70.350% for a combined lexical/contextual/forward readout. Differences are −28.000 percentage points, joint 95% [−38.750,−17.250], and −9.106[−16.739,−1.473]. It beats direct pair response, while comparisons with unconditioned fields and ContextCite32 remain unresolved jointly. All 54 regularization fits converge. The field models also trail on validation support recovery, so support-versus-answer objective mismatch alone cannot explain the failure. Singleton interventions and exact action agreement distinguish annotation recovery from answer benefit. Complete allocated cost is 19.767 GPU-hours including failures; shared stages do not establish isolated method latency. Public retained evidence replays 73,045 scoring, selection, bootstrap, chronology and cost checks without repeating neural computation. This is a measured limit on one component-transfer recipe, not a universal negative result. One model family, a small fitting set, different feature capacities, fixed targets, unknown pretraining and supplied candidate pools delimit the conclusion; input selection does not identify historical training influence.
