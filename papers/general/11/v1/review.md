# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to context selection. The central result is that adding a fixed relevance judge and its generated-claim interface improves an outcome-trained forward selector on a prospective, source-disjoint HotpotQA panel. It does not establish a superior practical workflow. The manuscript communicates that distinction clearly, retains stronger alternatives, and provides enough inspectable evidence to support its bounded conclusion. The work fits the open-ended General problem.

The designated comparison is reasonably controlled: both selectors use the same 32 fitting and 16 validation questions, all 45 pair utilities per question, the same ridge-selection procedure, and unchanged answer readers. On the 128-question panel, reader 4701 improves from 61.801% to 71.233% answer F1, a difference of $9.432$ percentage points with paired-question 95% interval $[3.113,15.923]$. Source exclusions, fitting-only standardization, fixed action tie breaking, and shared generation for identical actions help make this comparison interpretable. The other two readers preserve the direction, but appropriately remain fixed-reader transfer observations.

The augmentation buys additional information, including a full-context generated claim and 64 upstream annotated facts. Consequently, the primary comparison identifies the benefit of the specified observation bundle within this learning procedure, rather than an isolated causal effect of relevance judgment. The shared-claim lexical control is useful: the remaining $6.745$-point difference, interval $[0.311,13.183]$, supports an increment beyond this particular lexical representation. Its supplementary status and limited interpretation are stated correctly.

The practical qualification is substantive. Old support-forward selection reaches 73.960% F1, full-context answering 72.240%, and the late ContextCite comparator 72.047%. The unresolved contrasts do not demonstrate equivalence, but they also supply no evidence that augmentation improves on these workflows. In particular, a claim-dependent selector has already purchased a full-context answer before performing additional selection and generation. The paper correctly separates this dependency from measured component timings and avoids claiming a deployment speedup. The direct-text follow-up likewise supports only a failed investment gate for one declared recipe: its selected checkpoint is the unchanged relevance initializer, on exposed validation data. This is a useful qualification of the main result, not evidence against utility learning generally.

The novelty is empirical rather than algorithmic. I compared the manuscript with the published general_10/v1 study [9], the source-relevance paper [4], and the original RECOMP [5] and ContextCite [8] publications. RECOMP already learns extraction from downstream outcomes and reports Hotpot quality/transfer limitations; ContextCite already regenerates answers after attribution-based pruning. The submission credits these precedents. Relative to [9], the new outcome supervision, transferred external relevance observations, and prospective panel whose paragraphs were absent from reader fine-tuning address a distinct question. Relative to [4], the endpoint changes from annotation recovery to actual selected-context answer utility. The combined controlled result, claim-access comparison, and practical boundary constitute a meaningful advance; neither the ranking objective nor the broad support-versus-utility distinction is new.

The retained evidence supports the reported arithmetic. After inspecting the relevant code, I reproduced the main score/action/answer/interval replay, the claim-control and full-context comparisons, and the direct-text checkpoint-selection calculations. A separate calculation recovered both main ridge penalties and fitted weights and checked ContextCite selections and answer F1 from retained coefficients and raw predictions. I did not independently refit ContextCite's LASSO or reproduce neural training, inference, tokenization, or hardware timings. Thus these checks establish consistency of the released observations and analysis, not independent replication of the neural measurements.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the research question, observation differences, development rule, prospective result, and practical limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative and the figure makes the comparator results accessible. A few nonblocking improvements would help:

- Define FTRACE and CATT briefly at first use, so readers need not consult the earlier series to understand their roles.
- In Section 4, replace “selections match all 16 validation pair oracles” with “selected actions attain each question's maximum observed pair F1,” if that is the intended statement; utility ties need not identify a unique oracle pair.
- Move some optimizer settings and replay-count detail to a compact appendix. Keep the information-budget table and the scope of each verification in the main text.

The conclusions should remain conditional on the packed, balanced question population, the small adaptively developed training set, one model family, supplied candidate supports, and string-based answer scoring. The manuscript already acknowledges these restrictions; broader superiority or historical training-influence claims would require different evidence.
