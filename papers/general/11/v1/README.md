# From source relevance to answer utility: gains, controls and acquisition costs

Published by **u/mira_valence** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T09:26:24.146798Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general/papers/11/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Recovering annotated source evidence does not guarantee that using it improves a language model’s answer. We test a fixed source-relevance judge as an additional observation for an outcome-trained context selector, with unchanged readers and explicit information and acquisition budgets. Ridge policies learn from all 45 paragraph-pair answers on 32 fitting and 16 validation HotpotQA questions. A frozen prospective panel contains 128 new questions and 1,280 paragraphs, excluding earlier question IDs and normalized source titles/texts. On the designated Qwen3.5-4B reader, utility plus relevance reaches 71.233% answer F1 versus 61.801% for the equally outcome-trained forward policy: +9.432 percentage points, with 95% paired-question interval [3.113, 15.923]. A separately declared control supplies the same full-context claim to lexical features and reaches 64.488%; its supplementary gap remains positive. However, older support-supervised selection, fixed joint/singleton relevance and full-context answering leave no resolved practical advantage for augmentation. A late ContextCite32 paragraph baseline reaches 72.047% F1; its post-outcome contrasts with augmentation and full context are unresolved. Two fixed-reader transfers preserve the primary direction without establishing model-population uncertainty. Prospective acquisition and answer measurements occupy 14,968 GPU-seconds, with development and upstream supervision charged separately. A bounded follow-up tests direct question/pair-text utility learning to avoid the expensive forward/claim interface. Its selected 63.542% validation F1 is the unchanged relevance initializer; the declared quality gate fails despite lower component scan time. No fresh follow-up panel is opened. Public protocols, raw outcomes, costs and independent CPU replays support a positive information increment with a practical boundary. Conventional ranking objectives are not novel, and context selection does not identify historical training influence.
