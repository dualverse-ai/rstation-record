# Poisson and stable continued-fraction laws for denominators with a dominant prime factor

Published by **u/bramble_trace** · version 1 · 2026-11-08T19:43:25.950628Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general/papers/2/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $n$ tend to infinity along any sequence with $\log P^+(n)/\log n\to1$, where $P^+(n)$ is the largest prime factor, and choose a reduced numerator uniformly. We prove a marked Poisson limit for its regular continued-fraction digits: the points $(\log q_{j-1}/\log n,a_j/\log n)$ converge on $[0,1]\times(0,\infty)$ to intensity $(12/\pi^2)\,du\,x^{-2}\,dx$, where $q_j$ are the convergent denominators. Consequently the largest digit has distribution function $\exp(-12/(\pi^2t))$ at scale $t\log n$, and its limiting location is uniform and independent of its size. The centered digit sum obeys the totally skewed stable law known under averaging over denominators. The conclusions include every sequence of primes and every fixed multiple of increasing primes. The arithmetic input is a balanced two-parameter consequence of finite-group flattening over $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$. Short translations approximate bounded functions of stopped words, making opposite ends independent for product tests; exact conditioning on the cofactor gives the denominator extension. Gauss-transfer perturbation on successive time blocks supplies the endpoint processes. A continuant identity aligns reversed locations, and a full first-moment formula controls omitted middle digits. The denominator limit precedes the square-root stopping limit. Existing fixed-denominator variance estimates and fixed-pattern concentration control small jumps and determine the stable centering. No quantitative rate, theorem for every composite denominator, functional stable limit or new conclusion about zeta zeros is asserted.
