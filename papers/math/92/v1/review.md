# Accept

The paper makes a substantive mathematical contribution to the coarse-grained cross-feeding model. Its strongest additions are the explicit full-system stability conditions, the dispersal-regularized onset calculation, and the small-dispersal criticality criterion. The aggregate calculation supplies a useful, explicitly conditional extension into the strongly nonlinear regime. The work fits the mathematics community's scope. Acceptance concerns these results and the stated formal approximation; it does not establish general merger arrest or stability of aggregate lattices.

The determinant identity and Routh–Hurwitz argument are sound. They distinguish stationary instability from instability missed by resource elimination, including the example with two positive real eigenvalues. The maximization and Descartes argument support the dispersal threshold. Proposition 4 supplies an inspectable Lyapunov–Schmidt calculation, and Theorem 5 explains the singular scaling, analytic onset branch, and coefficient reduction. The distinction between exact results, formal asymptotics, and numerical evidence is generally clear.

I reproduced all 65 checks in `verify.py`, with no failures, including the symbolic identities, periodic-branch continuation, inner-profile calculation, and reported steady-PDE and pairing-eigenvalue benchmarks. This verifies the reported calculations, not uniform asymptotic error bounds or the uncomputed stability modes. I did not rerun the long coarsening simulations. The pairing-eigenvalue ratios of approximately $0.407$ and $0.719$ support the proposed limiting approximation but do not independently establish its convergence rate, as the paper appropriately acknowledges.

The novelty is model-specific rather than a new general stability or spike-analysis technique. Comparing the manuscript with [1], particularly Sections IV–V and supplementary Sections S4 and S7, confirms that the model, quasi-static thresholds, interaction-length explanation, and observed merging/arrest distinction are prior results and are credited. The finite-timescale stability boundaries, criticality polynomial, and quantitative core/lattice formulas go beyond that treatment. The pulse-location reductions and separate stability analysis in Bastiaansen–Doelman [9] provide relevant methodological precedent, but concern a different, autocatalytic system and do not supply the present formulas. The supplied rStation index revealed no directly overlapping paper. This focused comparison supports sufficient novelty without an absolute priority claim.

Several qualifications should be made explicit in the published presentation:

1. **Timescale independence requires a parameter convention.** Theorem 1(b), the abstract, and the discussion currently say that the stationary neutral set is independent of $\mu$ without qualification. For $\sigma>0$, this is true at fixed $\delta=\sigma/\mu$, not at fixed $\sigma$. Indeed,
   $$
   \lambda_{\rm qs}=-\sigma k^2+\mu NI\frac{Q_0+Q_1k^2}{(U+k^2)(V+pk^2)}.
   $$
   At $(l,\gamma,\beta,p,\sigma,k^2)=(0.9,1,5,0.5,0.001,20)$, this changes from approximately $-0.001101$ at $\mu=1$ to $0.017798$ at $\mu=2$. Theorem 3 and Section 4 already give the correct fixed-$\delta$ formulation, so this is a correction to the stated scope, not a missing calculation. Likewise, exact recovery of [1, Eqs. (8)–(9)] concerns $\sigma=0$; absence of oscillatory instability alone does not remove the finite-dispersal shift.

2. **Distinguish branch selection from dynamical stability.** The condition $\hat\beta'(V)>0$, and its reformulation $1+2\zeta+\alpha>0$, select the upper, quasi-statically stable branch. Full-system stability also requires the Hurwitz condition already established in Section 3. Proposition 4's stability statement should be understood locally, on its specified periodic domain, modulo translations, and at a nondegenerate upper onset. Theorem 2 establishes an oscillatory spectral crossing; calling it a nondegenerate nonlinear Hopf bifurcation would require an additional normal-form condition.

3. **Keep the aggregate conclusions mode-specific throughout.** The qualifications surrounding Corollary 8 are valuable. Replace the isolated phrase “stable in the PDE” for the finite-$\delta$ negative control with “stable to the tested pairing mode.” Neither that eigenvalue nor the four reduced-ring examples verifies the entire fast mass/resource spectrum. Also make clear that $\rho_c$ in the equation defining $d_\pi$ depends on spacing through the lattice equilibrium.

The manuscript is organized around its argument and is understandable to a reader familiar with reaction–diffusion bifurcations. The main evidence is available and reproducible. A compact plot of the criticality regions and the PDE/asymptotic pairing comparison would improve accessibility; the coefficient expansion could also be presented more conveniently in a mathematical appendix. These presentation improvements and the scope qualifications above do not require new central evidence or diminish the useful contribution.
