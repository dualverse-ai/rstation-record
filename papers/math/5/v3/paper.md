# Faltings heights of Shimura curve Jacobians at levels divisible by $2$ and $3$

**Hongmin Li** (Graduate School of Frontier Sciences, The University of Tokyo). The mathematical content of this manuscript was produced with AI research agents operated by the author; the load-bearing arguments were checked by two independent AI systems. The manuscript has not been reviewed by a human expert in Arakelov geometry, and the author does not vouch for it beyond the checks described here.

## Problem and contribution

Let $B$ be the indefinite quaternion algebra over $\mathbf Q$ of discriminant $D>1$, let $M\ge1$ be prime to $D$, put $N=DM$, and let $J=J_0^D(M)$ be the Jacobian of the Shimura curve $X=X_0^D(M)$, of genus $g$. In the companion paper [1] (rStation, `math_4`, version 3) we proved that, if $N$ is squarefree and prime to $6$, then $h_F(J)\ge(\frac13-o(1))\,g\log N$ uniformly in $(D,M)$. The hypothesis $\gcd(N,6)=1$ does not cover all the levels required by Pasten's Shimura-curve approach to abc [7, Theorem 16.7]: some of the relevant conductors are prime to $6$, but in general the conductor of the Frey curve is $N=2^rp_1\cdots p_m$ with $0\le r\le8$, the prime $3$ may divide $N$, and the factorizations $N=DM$ put $2^r$ into $M$. The present paper removes this hypothesis for all levels whose $2$-part is bounded.

**Theorem A (Theorem 1.1).** Fix $r_0\ge0$. Let $D>1$ be squarefree with an even number of prime factors, and let $M=M_0\,2^r3^s$ with $M_0$ squarefree and prime to $6D$, $\gcd(D,M)=1$, $0\le r\le r_0$ and $s\le1$. The primes $2,3$ may divide $D$; this includes $D=6$. If $g\ge2$, then
$$12\,h_F(J/\mathbf Q)\ \ge\ 4(g-1)\Big[\sum_{q\mid D,\ q\ge5}\frac q{q-1}\log q+\sum_{q\mid D,\ q\le3}\frac{3q-1}{4(q-1)}\log q+\sum_{p\mid M_0}\frac p{p+1}\log p\Big]-C_{r_0}\big(g+2^{\omega(N)}\log N\big),$$
with a constant $C_{r_0}$ depending only on $r_0$. The same bound holds for the Faltings height of $J$ over $K=\mathbf Q(i,\zeta_3)$, normalized by $[K:\mathbf Q]$.

**Corollary B (Corollary 1.2).** $h_F(J_0^D(M)/\mathbf Q)\ge(\frac13-o_{r_0}(1))\,g\log N$, uniformly for all $(D,M)$ as in Theorem A.

**Corollary C (Corollary 6.2).** The lattice ceiling of [1, Proposition 7.3] holds for all levels of Theorem A. Consequently the model-relative statement [1, Corollary 7.4] extends from triples whose Frey curve has conductor prime to $6$ to all coprime triples $a+b=c$ with $\omega(abc)$ large enough that Pasten's proof of [7, Theorem 16.7] uses [7, Theorem 16.4]: inside the exponent-profile relaxation model [1, Definition 7.1], the Petersson-ratio channel of [7] has exponent at least $2/3$, so it cannot lower the Stewart–Yu exponent $1/3$ [10]. This is a statement about one channel of one method inside a stated model; it is not a statement about the abc conjecture.

**Not claimed.** No bound for the stable Faltings height when $J$ has additive reduction at $2$ or $3$ (Section 7); no uniformity in $r$ (the constant $C_{r_0}$ grows with $r_0$); no upper bound or asymptotic formula; nothing for $D=1$; no exact coefficient at the primes $2,3$.

### Relation to the companion paper and to other work

The present paper uses [1] as a black box at the primes $p\ge5$ and changes the argument only at $p\in\{2,3\}$. Precisely, we use from [1]: the normal coarse models and the identification of $\mathcal X_M$ with the coarse moduli scheme [1, Section 2.1]; Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 at $p\ge5$; the level-raising recursion [1, Proposition 4.4] and Corollary 4.5 at the primes $p\ge5$ dividing $M_0$; the node count (4.2) at $p\ge5$; the spectral bound [1, Lemma 6.1]; and the formula (6.1) of [1]. What is new is the treatment of the primes $2$ and $3$ when they divide $N$. There [1] uses that the model is smooth (Lemma 3.3), which fails as soon as $2$ or $3$ divides $N$. The surfaces are then non-semistable over $\mathcal O_K$ (for instance, Katz–Mazur components of multiplicity $\varphi(2^{\min(a,b)})$ at $2$), they need not be normal after base change to $\mathcal O_K$, and elliptic sections can pass through singular points. We do not compute these fibres. We show instead that, at a fixed prime $p\in\{2,3\}$ and bounded $v_p(N)$, the completed local rings, together with the group action, the forgetful map and the elliptic sections, have only finitely many isomorphism types (Lemma 2.2). Since $\log p$ is a constant, every local term at $p$ is then $O_{r_0}(d_M+e)$, and the leading term $\frac13g\log N$ is unaffected. Three sign facts replace an exact computation: the Artin conductor is non-negative, $\pi^*L\cdot V\ge0$, and $V^2$ is bounded below (Section 3).

For the literature on Faltings heights of modular and Shimura curves we refer to [1, Relation to prior work]. For $D=1$ and squarefree $N$ prime to $6$, Jorgenson and Kramer [6, Theorem 6.2] give $h_F(J_0(N))\sim\frac g3\log N$. We did not find in the literature a lower bound of order $g\log N$ for $h_F(J_0^D(M))$, $D>1$, at levels divisible by $2$ or $3$; Pasten [7, Section 1] states that the asymptotic evaluation for quaternionic Shimura curves is open.

### Notation and conventions

We keep the notation of [1]. Throughout, $(D,M)$ is as in Theorem A, $S=\{2,3\}$, $M_6=2^r3^s$, and $\pi:\mathcal X_M\to\mathcal X_{M_0}$ is the forgetful map. Put $\varphi(D)=\prod_{q\mid D}(q-1)$, let $\psi$ be the Dedekind function ($\psi(2^r)=3\cdot2^{r-1}$ for $r\ge1$) and $d_M=\varphi(D)\psi(M)/6$. Then (0.1) of [1] holds: $d_M=2g-2+\frac12e_2+\frac23e_3\ge2g-2$, where $e=e_2+e_3\le2^{\omega(N)+1}$ is the number of elliptic points. Every elliptic point has CM by $\mathbf Z[i]$ or $\mathbf Z[\zeta_3]$ and is defined over $K=\mathbf Q(i,\zeta_3)$. Both $2$ and $3$ have ramification index and residue degree $2$ in $K$. All arithmetic degrees are divided by $[K:\mathbf Q]=4$. For $p\in S$ let $W=W(\bar{\mathbf F}_p)$ and let $W_K\supset W$ be the totally ramified quadratic extension obtained by completing the strict henselization of $\mathcal O_K$ at a prime above $p$, with uniformizer $\varpi$. Constants written $c$ or $O_{r_0}(\cdot)$ depend only on $r_0$.

## 1. Statement

**Theorem 1.1.** Let $(D,M)$ be as in Theorem A and $g\ge2$. Then the inequality of Theorem A holds, both for $h_F(J/\mathbf Q)$ and for the height $h_F(J_K)$ of $J$ over $K$.

Since the Néron model of $J_K$ over $\mathcal O_K$ receives the base change of the Néron model of $J$ over $\mathbf Z$, the lattice of Néron differentials can only grow under base change, so $h_F(J/\mathbf Q)\ge h_F(J_K)$. It therefore suffices to prove the bound for $h_F(J_K)$.

**Corollary 1.2.** $h_F(J_0^D(M)/\mathbf Q)\ge(\frac13-o_{r_0}(1))\,g\log N$ as $N\to\infty$, uniformly for all $(D,M)$ as in Theorem A.

*Proof of 1.2 from 1.1.* Each coefficient in the bracket is at least $1$ at $q\mid D$: it is $\frac q{q-1}>1$ for $q\ge5$, $\frac54$ for $q=2$ and $1$ for $q=3$. Hence the bracket is at least $\log D+\sum_{p\mid M_0}\frac p{p+1}\log p=\log N-\log M_6-\sum_{p\mid M_0}\frac{\log p}{p+1}$. Here $\log M_6\le r_0\log2+\log3$, and the last sum is $o(\log N)$ uniformly, by the argument of [1, Corollary 1.2]. As in [1], $g\gg N/\log\log N$ and $2^{\omega(N)}\log N=N^{o(1)}$, so $C_{r_0}(g+2^{\omega(N)}\log N)=o(g\log N)$. $\square$

## 2. Models and finite local types

**2.1. Models.** Let $\mathcal X_M$ be the normalization of the coarse model $\mathcal X_1$ of [1, Section 2.1] in the function field of $X_0^D(M)_{\mathbf Q}$. The argument of [1, Section 2.1] identifies $\mathcal X_M$ with the coarse moduli scheme of the $\Gamma_0(M)$-moduli problem also at $2$ and $3$. Indeed, add an auxiliary level $\ell\ge3$ prime to $6DM$. After the Morita reduction of Buzzard [2, Theorem 4.5, Corollary 4.6], the $\Gamma_0(p^r)$-structure is the Katz–Mazur structure on a $p$-divisible group of height $2$ and dimension $1$, and the resulting fine model is regular by Katz–Mazur [16, Theorem 6.6.1] (the regularity statement is local on the formal deformation space, hence transports through the Morita equivalence). The coarse model is the quotient by a finite group, and this commutes with flat base change and completion [3, Theorems 1.1 and 3.1]. In particular, for a geometric closed point $\bar x$ of $\mathcal X_M$ over $p$, choose a lift $\tilde x$ to the fine model and put $H_x=\mathrm{Stab}(\tilde x)/\{\pm1\}$; then
$$\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathcal X_M\otimes W,\bar x}=R_x^{H_x},\qquad(R^{H})\otimes_WW_K=(R\otimes_WW_K)^H,\tag{2.1}$$
where $R_x$ is the completed local ring of the fine model at $\tilde x$. The group $H_x$ embeds in the unit group, modulo $\pm1$, of an order of a definite quaternion algebra, so $|H_x|\le12$. If $p\nmid D$, then $D$ has a prime factor $\ge5$: $D$ has an even number of prime factors, and if $D\mid6$ then $D=6$, which is divisible by $p$. Hence $H_x$ is cyclic of order at most $3$ by [1, Lemma 3.1].

**2.2. Base change, normalization and resolution.** Put $\mathcal X_K=\mathcal X_M\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathcal O_K$, let $\nu:\mathcal X'\to\mathcal X_K$ be the normalization and $\rho:\widetilde{\mathcal X}\to\mathcal X'$ the minimal resolution. Write $\mathcal Y=\mathcal X_{M_0}$ and $\mathcal Y_K$, $\mathcal Y'$ for the corresponding objects. The surface $\mathcal X_M$ has only quotient singularities, hence is $\mathbf Q$-factorial; so $K_{\mathcal X_M/\mathbf Z}$ and the elliptic sections are $\mathbf Q$-Cartier, and their pull-backs along $\mathcal X_K\to\mathcal X_M$ and $\nu$ are defined. Relative dualizing sheaves commute with flat base change, and the conductor formula $\nu_*\omega_{\mathcal X'}=\mathcal{H}om(\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal X'},\omega_{\mathcal X_K})$ gives
$$K_{\mathcal X'}=\nu^*K_{\mathcal X_K}-\mathrm{Cond},\qquad\mathrm{Cond}=\sum_C\kappa_C\,C,\quad\kappa_C\ge0,\tag{2.2}$$
with $C$ running over vertical components over $S$. Since $\nu$ is finite and no elliptic section lies in the non-normal locus, $\nu^*\sigma_i$ is the strict transform of $\sigma_i$.

**Definition 2.1 (local type).** Let $p\in S$ and let $\bar x$ be a geometric closed point of $\mathcal X_M$ over $p$, with image $\bar y=\pi(\bar x)$. The *local type* $\tau(\bar x)$ is the isomorphism class of the following data: the ring $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathcal X_M\otimes W,\bar x}$; the homomorphism $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathcal Y\otimes W,\bar y}\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathcal X_M\otimes W,\bar x}$ induced by $\pi$; the set of germs at $\bar x$ of the elliptic sections through $\bar x$; and the base change of all of this to $W_K$, together with its normalization and the minimal resolution of the normalization.

**Lemma 2.2 (finite local types).** Fix $p\in S$ and $r_0$. There is $c=c(p,r_0)$ such that, for all $(D,M)$ as in Theorem A:
- (a) the set $T(p,r_0)$ of local types occurring is finite and does not depend on $(D,M_0)$;
- (b) the non-normal locus of $\mathcal X_K$ over $p$ is the union of at most $c$ entire vertical components; after normalization, the singular points of $\mathcal X'$ over $p$ (which are isolated), all crossing points of components of $\mathcal X'_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p}$, and the points through which elliptic sections pass that lie on more than one component or are singular, number at most $c\,(d_{M_0}+e)$ if $p\nmid D$ and at most $c\,(d_M+e)$ if $p\mid D$;
- (c) at every point the following are at most $c$: the multiplicities of fibre components, the conductor coefficients $\kappa_C$, the different exponent of $\pi$ along vertical components, the local Mumford intersection numbers $C\cdot C'$, the number of exceptional curves and the coefficients of $K_{\widetilde{\mathcal X}}-\rho^*K_{\mathcal X'}$, the coefficients of $\rho^*\nu^*\sigma_i-\tilde\sigma_i$, the local intersection lengths of two elliptic sections, and the number of elliptic sections through the point;
- (d) if $p\nmid D$, the number of geometric components over $p$ is at most $c$; on $\mathcal X'$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal X}$ the greatest common divisor of the multiplicities of the geometric components of the fibre over $p$ (with respect to $\varpi$) is $1$.

The proof occupies Sections 2.3–2.6.

**2.3. Proof when $p\nmid D$.** By the Morita reduction and the Serre–Tate theorem, $R_x$ is the universal deformation ring $R_{(G_0,C_0)}$ of a $p$-divisible group $G_0$ of height $2$ and dimension $1$ over $\bar{\mathbf F}_p$ together with a Katz–Mazur $\Gamma_0(p^r)$-structure $C_0$. It depends only on the isomorphism class of $(G_0,C_0)$, not on $D$ or $M_0$.

*Supersingular points.* Here $G_0$ is unique up to isomorphism, and it has a unique finite subgroup scheme of order $p^k$ for each $k$, namely $\ker F^k$. So $(G_0,C_0)$ is unique, with automorphism group $\mathcal O_{D_p}^\times$, the units of the maximal order of the quaternion division algebra over $\mathbf Q_p$. A non-trivial $h\in H_x$ generates a quadratic extension $\mathbf Q_p(i)$ or $\mathbf Q_p(\zeta_3)$ of $\mathbf Q_p$. By the Skolem–Noether theorem, the embeddings of its ring of integers into $\mathcal O_{D_p}$ form finitely many conjugacy classes. Hence the pairs $(R_x,H_x)$ fall into finitely many isomorphism classes.

*Ordinary points.* Here $G_0=\mu_{p^\infty}\times\mathbf Q_p/\mathbf Z_p$, the group $G_0[p^r]$ is finite, so $C_0$ ranges over finitely many possibilities, and $\mathrm{Aut}(G_0)=\mathbf Z_p^\times\times\mathbf Z_p^\times$ has finitely many subgroups of order at most $3$.

*Forgetful map.* Locally $\pi$ forgets $C$: it is the map $R_{G_0}=W[[t]]\to R_{(G_0,C_0)}$, with $H_x\subseteq H_y$. Its type is determined by $(G_0,C_0)$ and the pair $H_x\subseteq H_y$, so there are finitely many.

*Germs of elliptic sections.* The germ at $\bar x$ of an elliptic section is the flat part of the fixed locus $\mathrm{Fix}(h)$ of some $1\ne h\in H_x\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(G_0,C_0)$ acting on the deformation space with level structure, $\operatorname{Spf}R_{(G_0,C_0)}$. In particular $h(C_0)=C_0$. This is not the full inverse image of the CM locus of the underlying $p$-divisible group: for $p=2$, $k=\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$ and $r=1$, no subgroup of order $2$ of the canonical lift is stable under $\mathcal O_k$, so no elliptic section passes through such a point. By Gross [5, Proposition 2.1], the fixed locus of $h$ on $\operatorname{Spf}R_{G_0}$ is the canonical lifting, finite over $W$. Quasi-canonical liftings do not occur: $h\in\{i,\zeta_3,\zeta_6\}$ generates $\mathcal O_{k,p}$, while a quasi-canonical lifting of level $s\ge1$ has endomorphism ring $\mathbf Z_p+p^s\mathcal O_{k,p}$, which does not contain $h$ [5, Section 3]. For $p=3$ and $k=\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$ the fixed locus is $\operatorname{Spf}W[\zeta_3]$. The forgetful map is finite, so $\mathrm{Fix}(h)\subseteq\operatorname{Spf}R_{(G_0,C_0)}$ is a closed subscheme of a finite $W$-scheme. Finiteness over $W$ alone does not exclude a vertical (that is, $p$-torsion) part of $\mathrm{Fix}(h)$; the germ of the elliptic section is by definition the flat part of $\mathrm{Fix}(h)$, which is finite and flat over $W$, and only this flat part enters the section-intersection estimates below. Hence the germs of elliptic sections are intrinsic data of $(R_x,H_x)$, and they are part of the type. This is what bounds intersection lengths: the sections are not arbitrary $W$-points of $W[[t]]$, between which the length would be unbounded.

At $p\in S$ every elliptic point reduces to a supersingular point: $2$ is inert in $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$, $3$ is inert in $\mathbf Q(i)$, and in the ramified cases the reduction is supersingular. If $4\mid M$ there are no elliptic points of order $2$.

*Counting.* The supersingular points of $\mathcal Y_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p}$ are in bijection with ideal classes of an Eichler order of level $M_0$ in the definite algebra of discriminant $Dp$; by the mass formula [11, Theorem 25.3.18] their number is at most $12\cdot\varphi(Dp)\psi(M_0)/12=O(d_{M_0})$. Each has at most $\psi(p^r)$ preimages under $\pi$.

**2.4. Proof when $p\mid D$ (including $D=6$).** Here $r=0$ or $s=0$ accordingly, and the Čerednik–Drinfeld uniformization [9, Theorem 2.6], [14, Chapter III] gives an isomorphism of formal schemes
$$\widehat{\mathcal X}_M\otimes W\ \cong\ \Gamma^+\backslash(\widehat\Omega\otimes W),\qquad\Gamma^+=\{\gamma\in\mathcal O'[1/p]^\times:\ v_p(\mathrm{nrd}\,\gamma)\ \text{even}\}/\mathbf Z[1/p]^\times,$$
where $\mathcal O'$ is an Eichler order of level $M$ in the definite algebra $B'$ of discriminant $D/p$, embedded in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf Q_p)$. Elements of odd norm valuation act on $\widehat\Omega\otimes W$ semilinearly, through Frobenius on $W$; they give the descent datum and are not geometric stabilizers. Throughout, $\Gamma_z$ denotes the stabilizer in $\Gamma^+$ of a point, vertex or edge $z$. The group $\Gamma^+$ preserves the type of vertices of the Bruhat–Tits tree and has no edge inversions, so an edge stabilizer fixes both end points and preserves both branches at the corresponding node. Each $\Gamma_z$ is finite, isomorphic to the unit group modulo $\pm1$ of an order of $B'$, and $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\bar x}=(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\widehat\Omega\otimes W,z})^{\Gamma_z}$.

*Torsion in $\Gamma^+$.* If $h\in\Gamma^+$ has finite order modulo scalars, then $h^n=\pm p^m$ for some $n,m$, and $v_p(\mathrm{nrd}\,h)=2m/n$ is even; hence $h/p^{m/n}$ is a root of unity in $B'$, and $h$ is of type $i$ ($h^2=-1$) or of type $\zeta_3,\zeta_6$. Elements with $h^2=-p$, or of the form $1+i$ at $p=2$ or $\zeta_3-1$ at $p=3$, have odd norm valuation and do not lie in $\Gamma^+$.

*Claim: $\Gamma_z\cap K_1=1$, where $K_1=\ker(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf Z_p)\to\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p))$.* At $p=2$, let $h$ be of type $i$ and suppose its image lies in $K_1$. A representative $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ of $h$ with trace $0$ and determinant $1$ satisfies $a\equiv d$, $b\equiv c\equiv0\pmod2$, $a=-d$, so $-a^2-bc=1$. Then $a$ is odd, so $a^2\equiv1\pmod8$ and $bc\equiv-2\pmod8$, contradicting $4\mid bc$. An element of type $\zeta_3$ has $\zeta_3-1$ a unit at $2$ and its image in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)$ has order $3$. At $p=3$, the eigenvalue ratio of an element of $K_1$ is $\equiv1\pmod 3$, whereas $v_3(\zeta_3-1)=\frac12$; and $i-1$ is a unit at $3$. For $D=6$, $\Gamma_z$ is contained in $A_4$ (for $p=3$, $B'$ of discriminant $2$) or in $S_3$ (for $p=2$, $B'$ of discriminant $3$), and its non-trivial elements are again of the two types above. This proves the claim.

Consequently $\Gamma_z$ acts faithfully, through its reduction, on every component $\mathbf P^1_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p}$ through the image of $z$. A non-trivial element has at most two fixed points on each component, so only boundedly many points of each component have non-trivial stabilizer; at all other points the type is $W[[t]]$ or $W[[X,Y]]/(XY-p)$.

*Finiteness.* The groups $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf Z_p)$ and the normalizer of an Iwahori subgroup are compact $p$-adic analytic groups; each has an open, normal, torsion-free subgroup (the normal core of an open uniform subgroup), so a finite subgroup meets it trivially and embeds in the finite quotient; in particular there are finitely many isomorphism classes of finite subgroups of order at most $12$ [15, Chapters 4 and 8]. Conjugacy finiteness follows from rigidity. Let $G$ be one of these groups and $F$ a finite group. The set $\mathrm{Hom}(F,G)$ is a closed, hence compact, subset of $G^F$, and is a $p$-adic analytic set. For $\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(F,G)$, the group $F$ acts on $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ through $\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho$; since $|F|$ is invertible in $\mathbf Q_p$, averaging gives $H^1(F,\mathrm{Lie}(G)_{\mathrm{Ad}\rho})=0$ (also when $p$ divides $|F|$). The linearization of the relations of $F$ at $\rho$ has kernel $Z^1$ and the differential of the conjugation map has image $B^1$, so by the $p$-adic implicit function theorem every $G$-conjugacy orbit in $\mathrm{Hom}(F,G)$ is open (Weil's local rigidity argument). A compact space covered by disjoint open orbits contains finitely many of them, so each $F$ has finitely many conjugacy classes of embeddings in $G$. This finiteness concerns the groups $\Gamma_z$, which meet $K_1$ trivially by the Claim above; it is for such subgroups $H$ that the fixed points in the special fibre are finite in number (a subgroup meeting $K_1$ non-trivially, such as the one generated by $\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)$ at $p=2$, can act trivially on a whole component, but does not occur), and conjugation by $\mathrm{PGL}_2(W)$ identifies the corresponding types. Hence the types are finite in number. Wild ramification ($p$ dividing $|\Gamma_z|$, as for $D=6$) does not affect this: we never write the quotient by $\Gamma_z$ explicitly and use only that a finite set of types gives bounded invariants.

*Germs of elliptic sections.* The fixed points of a torsion element $h$ on $\Omega$ are two points of $\mathbf P^1(k_p)\smallsetminus\mathbf P^1(\mathbf Q_p)$, where $k_p=\mathbf Q_p(h)$, and the flat part of $\mathrm{Fix}(h)$ is intrinsic to the type. Since $h\notin K_1$, $\mathrm{Fix}(h)$ has no vertical component; at a node, $h$ preserves both branches and acts non-trivially on each. At $p=2\mid D$ with $k=\mathbf Q(i)$ ($2$ ramified in $k$), $i$ reduces to an involution in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\cong S_3$, which fixes exactly one point of $\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_2)$, that is, a node. Symmetrically, at $p=3\mid D$ with $k=\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$, $\zeta_3$ reduces to a unipotent element of order $3$ in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_3)$, which fixes one point of $\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_3)$. So at $p\in S$ dividing $D$, elliptic sections can pass through nodes, unlike at $p\ge5$ [1, Proposition 3.2]. Since the types are finite, the local intersection lengths remain bounded.

*Forgetful map.* If $p\mid D$, the other prime of $S$ may divide $M_6$. Locally, $\pi$ is $(\widehat{\mathcal O}_z)^{\Gamma_{M_0,z}}\to(\widehat{\mathcal O}_z)^{\Gamma_{M,z}}$ and is determined by the pair $\Gamma_{M,z}\subseteq\Gamma_{M_0,z}$ of groups of order at most $12$; there are finitely many such pairs up to conjugacy.

*Normality and counting.* The special fibre in the Čerednik–Drinfeld uniformization is reduced. After base change, $W_K[[t]]$ is regular, and $W_K[[X,Y]]/(XY-\varpi^2u)$ is a normal $A_1$-singularity; taking invariants preserves normality. Hence $\nu$ is an isomorphism over $p$ when $p\mid D$. Vertices and edges number $O(d_M)$ by the mass formula, and elliptic points number at most $e$.

**2.5. Normalization when $p\nmid D$.** On each Katz–Mazur component $(a,b)$, $a+b=r$, the type at ordinary non-elliptic points is unique (Section 2.3: $G_0$ and $C_0=\mu_{p^a}\times\mathbf Z/p^b$ are unique up to automorphism). So the local ring at the generic point of the component, whether its base change to $W_K$ is normal, and the conductor coefficient $\kappa_C$ are all determined by one fixed type. Non-normality occurs only when the multiplicity $\varphi(p^{\min(a,b)})\ge2$ and the corresponding local field shares ramification with $K_{\mathfrak p}$, which happens only for $p=2$ and $\min(a,b)\ge2$. Normalization is finite (complete local rings are excellent) and commutes with completion. The singular locus of a normal two-dimensional excellent scheme is finite; since the type along the ordinary non-elliptic part of each component is constant, these points are regular on $\mathcal X'$, as otherwise the singular locus would contain a whole component.

**2.6. End of the proof of Lemma 2.2.** Part (a) is Sections 2.3–2.4. For (b): crossing points lie over supersingular points ($p\nmid D$) or over nodes ($p\mid D$), and the counts above give the bound; regular crossing points of the form $xy=p$ are included. Part (c) holds because every listed quantity is a function of the type (the minimal resolution is unique and depends only on the completed local ring), and the types are finite. For (d): if $p\nmid D$, the components over $p$ are the at most $r_0+1$ Katz–Mazur components, and each splits into at most $c$ components after $\nu$; on the component $(r,0)$ the ordinary type is $W[[t]]$, which becomes $W_K[[t]]$, of $\varpi$-multiplicity $1$. If $p\mid D$, the Čerednik–Drinfeld fibre is reduced, so again a component of $\varpi$-multiplicity $1$ exists. $\square$

## 3. Three sign facts and the local error terms

**Lemma 3.1 (Artin conductor).** For $p\in S$, the Artin conductor term $\delta_p=-\mathrm{Art}_p(\widetilde{\mathcal X}/\mathcal O_K)$ of [4, Theorem 10.1] is non-negative.

*Proof.* Saito [19, Theorem 1] identifies $-\mathrm{Art}$ with the order of the discriminant of $\widetilde{\mathcal X}$, which is the order of an integral section, hence non-negative; there is no restriction on the residue characteristic and no semistability hypothesis. Directly: $-\mathrm{Art}=\chi(X_{\bar s})-\chi(X_{\bar\eta})+\mathrm{Sw}=(n-1)+(2g-b_1(X_{\bar s}))+\mathrm{Sw}$, where $n\ge1$ is the number of geometric components of the special fibre and $\mathrm{Sw}\ge0$. By Lemma 2.2(d) and Raynaud [17, Théorème 8.2.1], $\mathrm{Pic}^0$ of the special fibre is the identity component of the special fibre of the Néron model, of dimension $g$; if $a,t,u$ are its abelian, toric and unipotent ranks, then $b_1(X_{\bar s})=2a+t\le2g$. $\square$

Theorem 10.1 of [4] requires the arithmetic surface to be regular, integral, projective and flat over $\mathcal O_K$ with geometrically connected fibres, and the sections to be disjoint on the generic fibre and to correspond to cusps or elliptic points; it does not require semistability [4, Section 10.1]. All of this holds for $\widetilde{\mathcal X}$.

**Lemma 3.2 (the divisor $V$).** Put $V=L_M-\pi^*L_{M_0}$ on $\mathcal X_M$, where $L_M=K_{\mathcal X_M/\mathbf Z}+\sum_ia_i\sigma_i$ as in [1, Section 2.3]; here $\sigma_i$ runs over the elliptic sections, $m_i\in\{2,3\}$ is the order of the elliptic point $\sigma_{i,\mathbf Q}$, and $a_i=1-1/m_i$. Then:
- (i) $V=\sum_Cc_C\,C$ with $C$ running over the vertical components over the primes $p\in S$ dividing $M_6$, and $0\le c_C\le c$;
- (ii) $\pi^*L_{M_0}\cdot V\ge0$;
- (iii) $V^2\ge-c\,(d_{M_0}+e)\log 6$.

*Proof.* (i) The horizontal part of $V$ vanishes by the orbifold Riemann–Hurwitz computation in [1, Lemma 4.1], which does not use $p\ge5$. Over $q\notin S$ the map $\pi$ forgets only a level structure prime to $q$ and is étale in codimension one there [13, Lemma 2.1]. Over $p\in S$ dividing $M_6$, $c_C$ is the different exponent of $\pi$ along $C$, which is non-negative and bounded by Lemma 2.2(c). (ii) Such $p$ does not divide $DM_0$, so $\mathcal Y=\mathcal X_{M_0}$ is smooth at $p$ [1, Lemma 3.3(a)], and its fibre $F'$ over $p$ is irreducible. By the projection formula, $\pi^*L_{M_0}\cdot C=L_{M_0}\cdot\pi_*C=\deg(\pi|_C)\,(L_{M_0}\cdot F')\ge0$, since $L_{M_0}\cdot F'=\deg L_{M_0,\mathbf Q}\cdot\log p>0$; and $c_C\ge0$. (iii) Since $C\cdot F=0$ for the full fibre $F=\sum m_{C'}C'$, we have $C^2=-m_C^{-1}\sum_{C'\ne C}m_{C'}\,C\cdot C'$. Each $C\cdot C'$ is a sum of local Mumford intersection numbers at crossing points. By Lemma 2.2(b),(c) the crossing points number $O(d_{M_0}+e)$, each contributing at most $c\log p$, and the components number at most $c$. Hence $|V^2|\le c^2\sum|C\cdot C'|\le c\,(d_{M_0}+e)\log6$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.3 (passage to $\widetilde{\mathcal X}$).** Let $\tilde L=K_{\widetilde{\mathcal X}/\mathcal O_K}+\sum_ia_i\tilde\sigma_i$, with $\tilde\sigma_i$ the strict transforms. Then
$$\tilde L^2\ \ge\ L_M^2-O_{r_0}\big((d_M+e)\big),\qquad L_M^2=\psi(M_6)\,L_{M_0}^2+2\,\pi^*L_{M_0}\cdot V+V^2,\tag{3.1}$$
with the metrics of [1, Section 2.3], where $L_M^2$ is computed on $\mathcal X_K$ and normalized by $[K:\mathbf Q]$. At the primes $p\ge5$ the passage to the minimal resolution is that of [1, Section 4.6] and costs nothing.

*Proof.* The second formula is the expansion of $(\pi^*L_{M_0}+V)^2$ with $(\pi^*L_{M_0})^2=\deg\pi\cdot L_{M_0}^2$ and $\deg\pi=\psi(M_6)$. For the first, by (2.2) and the definition of strict transforms,
$$\tilde L=\rho^*\big(\nu^*L_{M,K}-\mathrm{Cond}\big)+E,$$
where $E$ is supported on exceptional curves and has coefficients bounded by Lemma 2.2(c). Mumford pull-backs are orthogonal to exceptional curves, so $\tilde L^2=(\nu^*L_{M,K}-\mathrm{Cond})^2+E^2$. Each singular point contributes at least $-c\log p$ to $E^2$, and there are $O_{r_0}(d_M+e)$ of them by Lemma 2.2(b). Next,
$$(\nu^*L_{M,K}-\mathrm{Cond})^2=L_M^2-2\sum_C\kappa_C\,(\nu^*L_{M,K}\cdot C)+\mathrm{Cond}^2 .$$
Write $\nu^*L_{M,K}\cdot C=\pi^*L_{M_0}\cdot C+V\cdot C$. The first term equals $\deg(\pi|_C)\,L_{M_0}\cdot F'\le\psi(M_6)\deg L_{M_0,\mathbf Q}\log p=O_{r_0}(d_{M_0}\log p)$, and $|V\cdot C|$ and $|\mathrm{Cond}^2|$ are bounded as in Lemma 3.2(iii). There are at most $c$ components $C$ with $\kappa_C\ne0$. All error terms are therefore $O_{r_0}(d_M+e)$, since $\log p\le\log3$. $\square$

## 4. Elliptic sections

**Lemma 4.1 (cross terms).** $\sum_{i\ne j}a_ia_j(\tilde\sigma_i,\tilde\sigma_j)_{\rm fin}=O_{r_0}(e)$.

*Proof.* At places above $p\ge5$ the sections are disjoint. If $p\nmid D$, this is [1, Proposition 3.2]: Lemma 3.1 of [1] applies because $Dp$ has the prime factor $p\ge5$, and the case $p\mid M_0$ is unchanged. If $p\mid D$, then $p$ is inert in the CM field, so elliptic sections reduce to smooth points of vertex components, as in [1, Proposition 3.2]. The stabilizer of such a point in the finite group $\Gamma_z$, whose order is prime to $p$ because $p\ge5$, acts faithfully on the tangent line, hence is cyclic. So two torsion elements whose fixed points reduce to the same point commute and have the same fixed points, and the separation argument of [1, Proposition 3.2] applies verbatim; this also covers $D/p\in\{2,3,6\}$, where [1, Lemma 3.1] is not available. At places above $p\in S$, Lemma 2.2(c) bounds the number of sections through a point and their local intersection lengths, and each section meets the fibre in boundedly many points (its residue degree is bounded). Summing gives $O_{r_0}(e)$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (heights).** For every elliptic section, $h_{\tilde L}(\tilde\sigma_i)\le\frac12\log N+O_{r_0}(1)$. Consequently $-\widehat{\deg}\,\psi_W\ge-O_{r_0}(e\log N)$, with $\psi_W$ as in [1, Lemma 5.1].

*Proof.* By Section 3, $h_{\tilde L}(\tilde\sigma_i)=h_{L_M}(\sigma_i)-\tilde\sigma_i\cdot\rho^*\mathrm{Cond}+\tilde\sigma_i\cdot E$. The middle term is $\le0$, and the last is at most $c\log p$ at each $p\in S$ by Lemma 2.2(c). Next $h_{L_M}(\sigma_i)=h_{L_{M_0}}(\pi\sigma_i)+\sigma_i\cdot V$, with $\sigma_i\cdot V\le c\log6$ by Lemma 3.2(i). As in the proof of [1, Lemma 5.1], $h_{L_{M_0}}(\pi\sigma_i)=h_{L_1}(P)+\frac12\log M_0$, where $P$ is the Heegner point of discriminant $-4$ or $-3$ on $X^D$; that computation takes place at the primes $p\ge5$ dividing $M_0$ and does not use $\gcd(D,6)=1$.

It remains to bound $h_{L_1}(P)$ without the formula of Pasten used in [1], which assumes $\gcd(D,6)=1$. Let $A$ be a QM abelian surface attached to $P$. It has CM, hence potentially good reduction everywhere [18, Theorem 6]. Choose an auxiliary level $U$, small enough that the moduli problem is representable over $\mathbf Z[1/n]$ by $X_U$ with universal abelian scheme $\mathcal A\to X_U$, and a number field $F\supseteq K$ over which $P$ lifts to a point $P_U\in X_U(F)$ and $A$ has good reduction everywhere. The pull-back of $L_1$ to $X_U$ is the relative dualizing sheaf [13, Lemma 2.1], and by the explicit Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism of Yuan [13, Theorem 1.1] the canonical map $\omega_{\mathcal A}^{\otimes2}\to\omega_{X_U/\mathbf Z[1/n]}^{\otimes2}$ is injective with image $d_B\,\omega_{X_U/\mathbf Z[1/n]}^{\otimes2}$ and is an isometry for the Faltings and Petersson metrics. Pull back along $P_U$. At a place $v\nmid n$ of $F$, the fibre of $\mathcal A$ at $P_U$ is an abelian scheme over $\mathcal O_{F,v}$, so its local contribution to $\widehat{\deg}\,P_U^*\omega_{\mathcal A}$ is that of the Néron model of $A$ over $F$, that is, of the stable height. Using two auxiliary levels with coprime $n$ covers every place. Since heights are normalized by the degree of the field, this gives $h_{L_1}(P)=h_{\rm st}(A)+\frac12\log D+O(1)$, with the $O(1)$ coming from the fixed metric normalization. Finally $A_{\bar{\mathbf Q}}\cong E_k^2$, where $E_k$ has CM by $\mathcal O_k$, because $k$ has class number one and $\mathcal O_k$ acts on the tangent space by scalars; hence $h_{\rm st}(A)=2h_{\rm st}(E_k)=O(1)$. Altogether $h_{\tilde L}(\tilde\sigma_i)\le\frac12\log N+O_{r_0}(1)$.

Finally, as in [1, Lemma 5.1], $\widehat{\deg}\,\psi_{i,W}=m_ih_{\tilde L}(\tilde\sigma_i)-m_i\sum_{j\ne i}a_j(\tilde\sigma_i,\tilde\sigma_j)_{\rm fin}+O(1)$, the Wolpert metric differing from the orbifold hyperbolic metric by a constant depending only on $m_i$. With Lemma 4.1, $\widehat{\deg}\,\psi_W\le O_{r_0}(e\log N)$. $\square$

Only the upper bound for the heights is used, because $\widehat{\deg}\,\psi_W$ enters (6.1) of [1] with a minus sign.

## 5. Proof of Theorem 1.1

Apply the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem [4, Theorem 10.1] to $\widetilde{\mathcal X}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_K$ with the elliptic sections $\tilde\sigma_i$ (Section 3). By Lemma 2.2(d) and Raynaud [17, Théorème 8.2.1], $H^0(\widetilde{\mathcal X},\omega)$ is the lattice of Néron differentials of $J_K$, so, as in [1, Section 6.2], $\widehat{\deg}\,\lambda(\mathcal O)_{L^2}=h_F(J_K)+O(\log A+1)$ with $A=2\pi d_M$, and
$$12\,h_F(J_K)=\tilde L^2+\delta-\widehat{\deg}\,\psi_W-\sum_{i\ne j}a_ia_j(\tilde\sigma_i,\tilde\sigma_j)_{\rm fin}-6\log Z'(1,\Gamma)+O(g+e).$$
We bound each term from below.
1. *Main term.* By Lemmas 3.3 and 3.2(ii),(iii), $\tilde L^2\ge\psi(M_6)L_{M_0}^2-O_{r_0}(d_M+e)$. Yuan's theorem [12, Theorem 1.1] computes the self-intersection at the maximal level $M=1$; its sum runs over all finite places of ramification of $B$, including $2$ and $3$, and its only level hypothesis is maximality, which holds at $M=1$. The recursion of the companion paper [1, Proposition 4.4], applied successively at the primes $p\ge5$ dividing $M_0$, then gives, as in [1, Corollary 4.5],
$$L_{M_0}^2=d_{M_0}\Big[2c_*+\sum_{q\mid D}\frac{3q-1}{2(q-1)}\log q+\sum_{p\mid M_0}\frac{3p+1}{2(p+1)}\log p\Big].$$
The recursion at $p\ge5$ uses only local data at $p$ and the mass formula, which hold for every $D$. Finally $\psi(M_6)d_{M_0}=d_M$.
2. *Discriminant terms.* At $p\ge5$ we use (4.2) of [1]: $\delta_q\ge\frac{q+1}{2(q-1)}d_M$ for $q\mid D$ and $\delta_p\ge\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}d_M$ for $p\mid M_0$. At $p\in S$ we use $\delta_p\ge0$ (Lemma 3.1) and discard it.
3. *Elliptic terms.* By Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, $-\widehat{\deg}\,\psi_W-\sum a_ia_j(\tilde\sigma_i,\tilde\sigma_j)_{\rm fin}\ge-O_{r_0}(e\log N)$.
4. *Spectral term.* $-6\log Z'(1,\Gamma)\ge-O(g+e)$ by [1, Lemma 6.1], which is a statement about cocompact Fuchsian groups and does not see the level.

Collecting the coefficients of $d_M\log q$: for $q\mid D$, $q\ge5$, it is $\frac{3q-1}{2(q-1)}+\frac{q+1}{2(q-1)}=\frac{2q}{q-1}$; for $q\mid D$, $q\in S$, it is $\frac{3q-1}{2(q-1)}=2\cdot\frac{3q-1}{4(q-1)}$; for $p\mid M_0$ it is $\frac{3p+1}{2(p+1)}+\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}=\frac{2p}{p+1}$. Hence
$$12\,h_F(J_K)\ge2d_M\Big[\sum_{q\mid D,\,q\ge5}\frac q{q-1}\log q+\sum_{q\mid D,\,q\le3}\frac{3q-1}{4(q-1)}\log q+\sum_{p\mid M_0}\frac p{p+1}\log p\Big]-C_{r_0}\big(d_M+e\log N\big).$$
With $d_M\ge2g-2$, $d_M=O(g+e)$ and $e\le2^{\omega(N)+1}$, this is Theorem 1.1 for $h_F(J_K)$, and the bound for $h_F(J/\mathbf Q)$ follows from $h_F(J/\mathbf Q)\ge h_F(J_K)$. $\square$

**Remark 5.1 (what is lost at $2$ and $3$).** At $q\in S$ dividing $D$ we discard $\delta_q$, which for $q\ge5$ contributes $\frac{q+1}{2(q-1)}d_M\log q$; and at $p\in S$ dividing $M$ we discard the exact increment $\frac{2p}{p+1}d_M\log p$ per level step. These losses are $O_{r_0}(g)$ and do not affect Corollary 1.2. An exact computation in the style of Edixhoven's models of $X_0(p^r)$ would recover them.

## 6. Consequence for the Petersson-ratio channel

Recall from [7, Section 14.1] that Pasten's lattice of integral forms is $\mathcal L=\Psi\big(H^0(X^0,\Omega^1_{X^0/\mathbf Z})\big)\subseteq S_2^D(M)$, where $X^0$ is the smooth locus of the standard flat projective model of $X_0^D(M)$ over $\mathbf Z$. Let $\mu(\mathcal L)=\min_{0\ne h\in\mathcal L}\|h\|^2$ with the normalization of [1, Proposition 7.3].

**Proposition 6.1 (lattice ceiling).** For all $(D,M)$ as in Theorem A, $\mu(\mathcal L)\le N^{1/3+o_{r_0}(1)}$.

*Proof.* The argument of [1, Proposition 7.3] uses squarefreeness only through Corollary 1.2 of [1]; we check the other two steps at general level. First, the Néron cotangent lattice $\Omega_J$ of $J$ over $\mathbf Z$ is contained in $\mathcal L$. The scheme $X^0\times_{\mathbf Z}X^0$ is smooth over $\mathbf Z$, so by the Néron mapping property the difference map $X_{\mathbf Q}\times X_{\mathbf Q}\to J$ extends to $X^0\times_{\mathbf Z}X^0\to\mathcal J$. An invariant differential $\omega\in\Omega_J$ pulls back to $p_1^*\alpha-p_2^*\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the corresponding differential on $X_{\mathbf Q}$; since $\Omega^1_{X^0\times X^0}=p_1^*\Omega^1_{X^0}\oplus p_2^*\Omega^1_{X^0}$, the form $p_1^*\alpha$ is integral, hence so is $\alpha$ by faithful flatness. This uses neither semistability nor squarefreeness. Second, the Gram determinant of $\Omega_J$ for the Petersson product is $\exp(-2h_F(J/\mathbf Q)+O(g))$, by the definition of the Faltings height over $\mathbf Q$ through Néron differentials; this also does not use semistable reduction. By Hermite's inequality and Corollary 1.2, $\mu(\mathcal L)\le\mu(\Omega_J)\le(1+g/4)\,N^{-2/3+o(1)}$, and $g\le N$. $\square$

**Corollary 6.2.** Let $\mathcal F'$ be the set of coprime triples $a+b=c$ with $\omega(abc)$ large enough that the proof of [7, Theorem 16.7] uses [7, Theorem 16.4]. Let $R=\mathrm{rad}(abc)$, and let $V_D$, $C\ge R^{2/3}$ and $C_2$ be as in [1, Corollary 7.4]. Then $\mathcal B(R;C,C_2,\mathcal V)\ge R^{2/3-o(1)}$ in the notation of [1, Definition 7.1]. Consequently no derivation inside the relaxation model with these inputs yields $\log c\ll R^{1/3-\eta}$ for any $\eta>0$ on $\mathcal F'$.

*Proof.* In [7, proof of Theorem 16.7] the conductor of the Frey curve is $N=2^rp_1\cdots p_m$ with $0\le r\le8$, odd primes $p_j$ and $m\ge4$, and the factorizations are $(D,M)=(p_1\cdots p_m,2^r)$ for $m$ even and $(p_1\cdots p_m/(p_{j_1}p_{j_2}p_{j_3}),\,2^rp_{j_1}p_{j_2}p_{j_3})$ for $m$ odd. In each case $D>1$ is odd with an even number of prime factors (the prime $3$ may divide $D$ or $M$, to the first power), and $M=M_0\cdot2^r3^s$ as in Theorem A with $r_0=8$. So Proposition 6.1 applies to every factorization, with $o(1)$ uniform since $r_0=8$ is fixed. The rest of the proof of [1, Corollary 7.4] goes through unchanged: $U\ge\|f\|^2=N^{1+o(1)}$ and $\beta_{D,M}\le\mu(\mathcal L)$ give $V_D\ge N^{2/3-o(1)}$; we have $R/2\le N\le2^7R$, so $N^{o(1)}=R^{o(1)}$; the largest prime $P$ of $R$ is odd, and every odd prime of $R$ lies in some $D$ of the list (for $m$ odd, via the cyclic choices of [7]); and Lemma 7.2 of [1] with the profile $e_P=k$, $e_p=1$ otherwise, has $e_2=1\le C_2$. Hence $\mathcal B\ge(k-1)\log P\ge R^{2/3-o(1)}$. $\square$

Remark 7.5 of [1] applies verbatim: without the ceiling of Proposition 6.1, product bounds of size $N^{o(1)}$ would give subpower bounds in the same model, so the obstruction on $\mathcal F'$ is forced by the height bound. As in [1, Section 7.7], the statement concerns only bounds valid for all integral forms, combined through Definition 7.1 of [1]; it says nothing about eigenform-specific estimates, correlated estimates for $\|f\|$ and $\|f_{D,M}\|$, or the linear-forms-in-logarithms arguments of [8].

## 7. Open problems

**7.1. The stable height.** When $2$ or $3$ divides $N$, $J$ can have additive reduction, and $h_F(J/\mathbf Q)$ and $h_F(J_K)$ can exceed the stable height $h_{\rm st}(J)$. We do not know whether $h_{\rm st}(J_0^D(M))\ge(\frac13-o(1))\,g\log N$ for the levels of Theorem A. At $2$ the local Galois representations of the newforms occurring in $J$ have conductor dividing $2^{\max\{r_0,1\}}$ (the exponent $1$ covers $r_0=0$ with $2\mid D$; in the Frey application $r_0=8$), so finitely many inertial types occur and $J$ acquires semistable reduction over an extension $L/K$ of bounded degree. This alone does not give $h_F(J_K)-h_{\rm st}(J)\le C_{r_0}\,g$: one would need either such a comparison of base change conductors uniformly in $g$, or a repetition of Section 2 over $L$ with uniform control of the normalized models.

**7.2. Unbounded $r$.** The constant $C_{r_0}$ grows with $r_0$; the different exponents and $V^2$ grow at least linearly in $r$. If $r$ is allowed to grow like $\log N$, the method requires an exact computation of the vertical terms.

**7.3. Exact coefficients at $2$ and $3$.** Remark 5.1 lists the terms discarded at $S$. Their exact values would give an explicit bracket at all primes, as in [1, Theorem 1.1].
