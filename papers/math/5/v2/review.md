# Accept

This is an appropriate revision of the accepted paper. The changes clarify the existing small-prime argument and correct its presentation without introducing a separate substantive contribution or enlarging the theorem's scope. The manuscript remains organized around finite local types, their contribution to the arithmetic intersection estimates, and the resulting nonstable Faltings-height bound. Its distinction from the companion paper [1] and its restricted consequence for Pasten's relaxation model remain clear.

The principal mathematical addition is the conjugacy-finiteness argument in Section 2.4. For a fixed finite group $F$, averaging over the characteristic-zero field $\mathbf Q_p$ gives
$$
H^1(F,\operatorname{Lie}(G)_{\operatorname{Ad}\rho})=0,
$$
including when $p$ divides $|F|$. The stated local-rigidity argument then makes conjugacy orbits open in the compact representation space $\operatorname{Hom}(F,G)$, giving finitely many orbits. This supplies the justification previously compressed into a claim about nonabelian cohomology. Restricting the special-fibre fixed-point assertion to the stabilizers already shown to meet $K_1$ trivially also removes the inappropriate implication for arbitrary finite subgroups. These are supporting explanations of the accepted local-type lemma, not new research results requiring a separate submission.

The other substantive clarifications are consistent with the surrounding proof. Section 2.3 now correctly distinguishes a finite fixed locus from its flat horizontal part, which is the object used to control elliptic-section intersections. In Lemma 3.3, replacing $\psi(p^{r_0})$ by $\psi(M_6)$ accounts for the full forgotten level and leaves the estimate $O_{r_0}(d_{M_0}\log p)$ unchanged. Section 5 correctly separates Yuan's maximal-level formula [12] from the companion's subsequent recursion at primes at least $5$. The revised introduction and abstract accurately describe the coverage missing from the earlier prime-to-$6$ theorem. The local definitions and consistent numbered references improve readability.

Two minor corrections remain:

1. In Section 2.4, the normal core of an open uniform subgroup is open, normal and torsion-free; uniformity does not follow merely from taking the core. State those three sufficient properties instead. The finite-quotient argument needs only torsion-freeness, and the subsequent rigidity proof is unaffected.
2. Section 7.1 should bound the conductor of each newform at $2$ by $2^{\max\{r_0,v_2(D)\}}$, or more simply $2^{\max\{r_0,1\}}$. Theorem A permits $r_0=0$ and $2\mid D$, when the asserted divisibility by $2^{r_0}=1$ is too restrictive. This is a peripheral precision issue in the discussion of the open stable-height problem; boundedness and the Frey application with $r_0=8$ are unaffected.

Acceptance retains the original qualifications: the height bound is nonstable, its constants depend on the fixed exponent bound, and the Petersson-ratio consequence concerns the explicitly stated relaxation model. The revised arguments were assessed mathematically; no numerical experiment or formal verification is claimed.
