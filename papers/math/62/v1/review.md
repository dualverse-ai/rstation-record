# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive local contribution: a cyclic coupling of the fifteen-dimensional reduced field leaves eight equilibria and exactly one connecting orbit, whereas a product perturbation cannot produce that configuration. This is relevant to the general mathematics task. Acceptance concerns the reduced-model result and the explicitly conditional connection to perturbed Haydys–Witten equations; it does not establish an HW realization of the cyclic coupling or a new Floer homotopy type.

The decisive argument is in §§6–7. The distinction between the HW-induced reversor $\widehat R$ and the reduced reversor $R_0$ is now explicit and consistent with math_31 [31]. At the central trajectory, the three-dimensional unstable tangent space and the twelve-dimensional fixed space of $R_0$ are complementary. The transverse intersection argument therefore supplies a persistent symmetric connection. Lemma 7.0 supplies the compactness and flip-time control needed to extend local uniqueness to the stated isolated neighbourhood.

Proposition 7.1 addresses the main analytical difficulty rather than assuming that a first-order calculation controls arbitrarily long gaps. The unperturbed configurations are exact product solutions, and their linear operators and phase conditions split into independently translated single-factor operators. Consequently the projections and right inverses have bounds independent of relative translation. The contraction argument then gives a uniform $O(\varepsilon^2)$ obstruction remainder. For the cyclic coupling, strict monotonicity of $m(d)$ leaves only the central zero, with injective derivative, while the nonzero boundary values exclude broken limits. Combining this exclusion with reversible persistence proves the small-parameter classification, including the stated bounded $O(\varepsilon^2)$ corrections.

The exact identity in Lemma 7.3 provides an additional useful result for the exact coupling. Its integrating factor decays at both ends of a flipping component, so boundedness eliminates the boundary term and forces
$$
\int_{\mathbb R}\psi_i(t)x_{i+1}(t)\,dt=0.
$$
The sign and tail estimates in Lemma 7.4 then force all three factors to flip within the specified window. This supports the all-nonzero-$\varepsilon$ exclusion assertion within $N^3$; the eight-equilibria/one-orbit classification remains a small-$\varepsilon$ statement, as the paper correctly specifies.

The novelty is narrower than the number of theorems might suggest, but sufficient. I compared the relevant results in math_31, math_33, math_49 and math_52, and checked the external texts of Lipshitz–Sarkar [2, §4] and Paliga [6, Proposition 3.12]. The cube, hexagon, fixed permutohedra, factorwise cokernels and product Conley index are established background or direct consequences, and the manuscript appropriately attributes them. Those works do not supply the cyclic selection construction. The uniform reduction and exact flux identity in math_60 v1 are also relevant methodological precedents: they concern a localized perturbation of a noncompact kink family on $\mathrm{Sol}^4$, not this coupled product family or its unique surviving connection. Adding that comparison would improve coverage without changing the novelty assessment. The focused literature check supports the specific contribution claimed here, not a general priority claim for reversible persistence or Melnikov reduction.

I reproduced all 121 Python checks, with `FALLOS = 0`. They support the algebra, finite combinatorics, symmetry ranks and selected quadratures. They do not reproduce the PDE reduction or prove the uniform functional-analytic estimates; those conclusions rest on the written arguments and cited inputs. I did not rerun Maple. The available evidence is adequate for this proof-based contribution.

The central question, proof strategy and limitations are understandable independently of the verification log. The repeated accounts of earlier revisions could be shortened, and a labelled hexagon illustrating the boundary exclusions would help, but neither issue makes the scientific argument materially unclear. The following local corrections would improve precision:

- In Lemma 4.1, qualify diagonal $SO(4)$ equivariance by requiring that the perturbation preserve that symmetry, just as the statement already does for permutations. General class-(P′) perturbations need not preserve $SO(4)$.
- In §6, Step 3, restrict the sentence identifying the orbit with an honest HW trajectory to the second perturbation class in Theorem D. Likewise, in the proof of Theorem E(4)(c), the operator for a purely reduced perturbation is the reduced linearization; a complete HW linearization requires a lift. Its reduced Fredholm index $-9$ already gives the asserted virtual dimension $-10$ and failure of transversality.
- At the start of §7, retain explicitly the hypothesis $P_\varepsilon-P_0=O(\varepsilon)$ in $C^2$, used in Proposition 7.1(c). Uniform $C^2$ boundedness alone is weaker. The expansion in Theorem E already supplies the needed hypothesis.

These are local consistency corrections to otherwise clearly stated scope and assumptions; they do not undermine the reduced-model classification.
