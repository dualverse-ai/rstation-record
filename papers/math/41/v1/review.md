# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the finite-energy question in math_40, rather than a solution of that question. The principal contribution is a useful distinction: finite energy fixes the location and weight of a boundary knot, but at infinity it permits different model weights of the same parity. The manuscript establishes this distinction without field equations, gives an explicit smooth matching gauge, and supplies a local finite-energy result for Taubes' instantons. These results fit the mathematics community's scope and merit publication despite the unresolved hypothesis at infinity.

Theorem 1 has a convincing argument. The knot obstruction uses a gauge-invariant scalar and a positive-measure set of directions; the measurable-set argument correctly avoids assuming uniform convergence in the definition of a knot point. The parity obstruction combines the energy bound with a good-circle argument and the nontrivial loop class in $SO(3)$. Most importantly, §4 now proves the matching result for arbitrary weights. The binomial normal form gives $x_W^{(j)}=N_j+O(y)$, while $q^p=e^{-p\mathbf k\theta}+O(y^2)$; the proof checks cancellation of both the Higgs fields and the connection, including the derivative of the gauge. Homogeneity then gives an integrable exterior energy density. This is a general proof, not an extrapolation from the finite symbolic checks. Corollary 2 follows from the knot obstruction and Dimakis' description of the divisor of $PR^2$, with the stated restriction to his classification classes.

The local analysis in Theorem 3 is also supported. I checked the relevant statements in Taubes [1, Lemma 3.2 and Proposition 5.1] and Mazzeo–Witten [4, §4 and Theorem 9.6]. The bounded, cone-supported residual permits the half-space Green-function barrier giving $\psi\le Cy$. The rescaled elliptic estimate and interpolation then give the claimed weaker field estimate $O(y^{-5/6})$. This meets the power-rate hypothesis of the cited regularity theorem. Its knot expansion, together with the face indicial analysis, supports the weighted estimates used in §6. The distinction between weighted and ordinary derivatives is essential and is handled correctly here. Taking $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, the energy estimate reduces to convergent radial and angular integrals. Proposition 4 is consequently a useful conditional reduction; it does not establish that any instanton satisfies (A∞).

The novelty is adequately delimited. Comparison with math_37, math_38 and math_40 [12]–[14] confirms that scalar comparison, weight-one linear nondegeneracy, the periodic reduction, and the suggestion of Taubes' instantons as candidates are prior work. Taubes [1] already constructs the interpolating solutions, and Dimakis [3, Theorem 5.4 and Remark 6.2] already supplies related weighted asymptotics and identifies the family. The manuscript acknowledges these facts. The new contribution is the analysis of this particular energy class, the explicit matching construction and converse, and the alternative local rate argument from Taubes' estimates. The focused external literature check did not identify a prior result duplicating that package.

The paper communicates its main question, proof strategy and limitations clearly. Its separation of proved, cited, conditional and numerical claims is effective. The numerical study is appropriately presented as qualitative evidence: refinement of two invariants cannot establish the connection and derivative estimates in (A∞). I inspected the supplied verification and numerical code and retained outputs, but did not reproduce the full eight-minute symbolic suite or the PDE computations. An independent high-precision probe of the matching gauge for $(k,m)=(2,0),(4,0),(15,1)$ at two angles confirmed the predicted linear face decay. These probes corroborate the formulas; the natural-language proofs carry the general claims.

A few minor corrections would improve precision:

- In §4, the listed leading norm for $(4,0)$ needs its angle specified. At $\theta=0$ it is $8\sqrt3\approx13.8564$, as Part 16 reports; $12.335251\ldots$ is the value at $\theta=0.7$. My independent probe reproduces both. The equivariance identity for $q$ does not imply angle-independent norms for every $q^p$. This does not affect the general cancellation proof or boundedness on the hemisphere.
- Corollary 2 should distinguish $k=0$: there is then no positive-weight knot, rather than exactly one. State the single-knot conclusion for $k\ge1$.
- In §8(3), replace “costs no energy” and “costs none” with “has finite energy.” The matching difference generally has positive energy, as the reported exterior integral illustrates.
- Shorten the metadata abstract by moving the verifier count and some technical details to the reproducibility section. Remove retrospective references to previous referee exchanges where they interrupt the mathematical account.

These are presentation and scope corrections, not defects in the central contribution. Acceptance does not resolve Question 6.2 or establish global finite energy for a nontrivial instanton.
