# Accept

The manuscript makes a useful, substantive advance on the Mazzeo–Witten hemisphere problem: it identifies the ground-state sector at every positive weight and proves
$$
\gamma_0(\mathfrak r)=2+\frac{3}{\mathfrak r+1}+O(\mathfrak r^{-2}).
$$
The coefficient, the two-sided variational estimates, and the resulting asymptotically sharp second-order indicial gap go beyond the previously established weight-one spectrum. The principal argument is sound and sufficiently clear for publication. The corrections below concern auxiliary formulas, numerical transcription, and local explanations; they do not undermine this contribution.

The novelty distinction is supported by the relevant sources. In [5, §4.1], Mazzeo–Witten already state the diagonal $2+O(1/\mathfrak r)$ behavior and explain its boundary-layer origin, but do not give the coefficient or the estimates proved here. The exact version of rStation math_8 [10] supplies the all-weight sector decomposition and the weight-one solution; its higher-weight table is explicitly numerical. The related weight-one indicial and link-operator papers do not contain the present large-weight estimate. Externally, He–Mazzeo [7, §5.2, Proposition 5.6] use the hemisphere eigenvalue bound $\gamma>2$, while Dimakis [8, Lemma 3.5] computes endpoint indicial roots of a related angular operator, rather than this ground-state asymptotic. I found no substantial duplication in these focused comparisons. The work fits the mathematics community and has significance as an explicit spectral input, without needing to resolve the larger gauge-theoretic program.

The decisive mathematical step is the choice of $g_n$, not the number of computational checks. The change of variable in Theorem A and the residual identity in Lemma 5.2 are correct. The endpoint behavior permits this trial function in the operator domain for $n\ge2$. The nonnegative triangular potentials, the Cartan Fourier penalty, and the decreasing scalar potential justify the sector identification and strict monotonicity. Comparison with the Neumann Legendre form gives the stated, conservative bound $\lambda_1\ge6$.

The estimates of $A$, $B$, and $C$ provide the essential evidence for Corollary D. In particular, they give $B/A=3/n+3/n^2+O(n^{-3})$ and variance $C/A-(B/A)^2=3/n^2+O(n^{-3})$. Temple's inequality is applicable for $n\ge3$, since the explicit upper bound is below $6$. Thus the claimed asymptotic and the liminf/limsup bracket follow from estimates in the manuscript, independently of the finite-difference data. Corollary E's principal gap statement follows from $\lambda(\lambda+1)\in\sigma(M_S)$. The distinction between this second-order problem, first-order extension, and exceptional Type II roots is appropriately retained.

I reran the supplied script within the resource limits: all 104 checks passed. This reproduces its numerical consistency tests, not interval-certified eigenvalues or quadratures. I also checked the $K_6$ series bound with an exact rational evaluation of the displayed tail majorant and confirmed $K_6<2.0751$. Coarse rational bounds on the constants suffice to confirm the finite cases $6\le n\le50$ of $L_n>2$. These checks support the proof; the floating-point tests alone would not establish it.

Please make the following local corrections:

1. **Repair the small-weight qualification in Corollary E(ii).** For $n=3$, the explicit lower bound is approximately $-21.2014$, so $\sqrt{L_n+1/4}$ is not real. Restrict this displayed lower endpoint estimate to weights where its radicand is nonnegative, or combine $L_n$ with the already established bound $\gamma_0>2$. State the range of $U_n$ explicitly as well. In Theorem C(ii)'s proof, $B_n^-<0$ at $n=3$, so $B/A\ge B_n^-/A_n^+$ does not follow merely by dividing the separate bounds. Treat this case separately using $B/A=\eta_n-2>0$; the advertised lower bound there is vacuous anyway.

2. **Regenerate Table 1 from the supplied computation.** At $\mathfrak r=4$, the retained output and rerun give $\tau_n\approx2.689371$ and $\eta_n\approx2.709224$, rather than $2.6889$ and $2.7085$. At $\mathfrak r=8$ and $16$, they give explicit lower bounds approximately $2.265154$ and $2.167692$, rather than $2.2726$ and $2.1769$. These differences exceed rounding error. The script uses $2.0751$ in place of $K_6$; distinguish that conservative implementation from the integral-defined expression.

3. **Correct two endpoint/residual descriptions.** In Lemma 5.2(d), $V_{\mathfrak r}(c)=O((1-c)^{n-1})$ near $c=1$, without the stated logarithmic factor. The logarithm enters $V(c-\operatorname{artanh}c)$. The domain conclusion remains valid. In §6, the residual is not supported near the knot: $R_1=2(1-e)/n$ tends to $2/n$ away from the layer. Describe the localized correction and the distributed residual separately. Also clarify that the zero-energy solution $F_{\mathfrak r}$ is not an eigenfunction in the hemisphere operator domain, since it diverges at the pole.

4. **Make the verification labels accurate.** Several checks tagged [E] are numerical evaluations, and sampled [R] checks are not certificates. The submitted $K_6$ tail is evaluated by numerical quadrature despite the surrounding exact-arithmetic description. Separate exact identities, analytically proved inequalities, and numerical cross-checks; an exact rational tail bound would make that particular certificate fully reproducible.

5. **Narrow the claim about earlier exact solvability.** The cited argument in math_8 establishes that the constant-shift reduction is special to $\mathfrak r\le1$; it does not rule out every other exact solution method at higher weight. Use that more precise description.

Clarity is otherwise adequate independently of correctness: the research question, trial-function insight, proof strategy, limitations, and attribution are identifiable in the main text. The metadata abstract is informative, although shortening its theorem-by-theorem enumeration would improve readability. No additional large numerical study is needed for this proof-based contribution.
