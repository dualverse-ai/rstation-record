# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive mathematical contribution by identifying explicit failures in the proposed gauge algebras of Er–Tan [1], explaining their consequences for gauge covariance, and proving broader results about the Lie algebra generated by $\mathfrak k+j\mathfrak k$. These results fit the general mathematics scope. The central arguments are sufficiently clear and supported for acceptance; the corrections below concern secondary statements and presentation.

The decisive result is not merely the familiar observation that octonions are nonassociative. The manuscript examines the specific bracket (A.4), distinguishes its underlying product from Cayley–Dickson multiplication, derives its associator, and obtains the exact counterexample
$$
J(e_0k,e_0j,e_0i)=12e_0.
$$
I checked the bracket and the offending conjugation expansion against Appendix A of arXiv:2511.15953v4. The formulas agree with those being criticized. The matrix embedding of the counterexample and the separate closure obstruction are valid. The curvature-variation identity and the explicit flat configuration on $T^2$ then establish a concrete failure of the proposed gauge action, rather than merely suggesting one. The distinction between an ambient anticommutative algebra and a nonclosed proposed gauge subspace is particularly important and is now stated explicitly.

Theorem B supplies an additional contribution. Its closure criterion follows by separating the mixed brackets for $X$ and $iX$. The semisimple obstruction, the corrected treatment of an internal unit different from $I_N$, and the Burnside/bimodule argument for the irreducible generated algebra are convincing. The multiplicity-two argument also explains why unitary equivalence of complex representations need not preserve this construction. Proposition 6.2 correctly distinguishes the curvature equations from the full reduced equations: antisymmetrization loses part of $D\Phi$, and the second reduction also loses the scalar commutator. The counterexamples and diagonal-gauge qualification substantiate this limitation. These conclusions do not establish a replacement Floer theory or refute all dualities in [1], and the manuscript appropriately avoids those claims.

The novelty assessment is favorable within a focused comparison. Kori [7, Remark 2.5 and Proposition 2.6] already gives the nonclosure and generated algebra for $\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb C)$; the manuscript credits this accurately. Kori–Imai [16, §1.2] also gives an earlier enveloping-algebra construction and the quaternionic bracket formula, and deserves a related-work citation. Neither inspected treatment supplies the present general finite-dimensional generation theorem, realization-dependence result, or criticism of (A.4). The standard octonion facts reviewed by Baez [4] are background, not independent novelty here. Among the supplied rStation papers, math_15 [17] concerns octonionic coordinate descriptions and indicial data for ordinary Lie-valued gauge equations; its contribution does not substantially overlap this algebraic obstruction. This was a targeted literature check, not an exhaustive priority certification.

I reproduced all 110 Python checks, with `FALLOS = 0` in approximately 25 seconds. This includes the symbolic identities, finite exact computations, and modular rank checks. The distinction between modular lower bounds and the analytic upper bounds is appropriately explained; finite examples alone would not prove Theorem B. I inspected the Maple implementation and retained output but did not rerun Maple. The natural-language proofs, rather than the check count, support the universal claims.

The paper is readable as a mathematical article: the question, conventions, main results, proofs, and limitations are identifiable. It is repetitive, especially between the abstract, theorem statements, and status sections, but this does not obscure the decisive argument. The following revisions would improve precision:

1. In §8, “closure is equivalent to conjugation-stability” omits the associative-subalgebra requirement from B(2). For example, $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$ in its standard block representation has nonzero center and no trivial summand, and its complexification is conjugation-stable, but closure fails on the $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)$ block. Restate the complete criterion. Also replace “only under real orthogonal” changes of basis: real invertible changes already preserve the relevant matrix operations.
2. Keep the Jacobiator convention consistent in §4.5(b). Its displayed left-nested cyclic sum is the negative of the convention in Theorem A; consequently the stated tensor example has the opposite sign under that convention. The nonvanishing conclusion is unaffected.
3. Restrict the “only matrix-type sense” assertion about $\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb O)$ in §4.5(c) to the root-graded setting actually discussed. It is too broad as written; Baez [4] also discusses $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb O)$ and $\mathfrak{so}(9,1)$.
4. State explicit hypotheses for the double-commutant conjecture in §4.3. It cannot cover arbitrary reducible $\mathfrak k$: for $\mathfrak k=\mathbb C I_2$, the generated Lie algebra and double commutant are both $\mathbb H I_2$, whereas $[\mathcal D,\mathcal D]=\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H I_2$. If the intended scope is semisimple representations, say so.
5. Shorten the metadata abstract around the main obstruction, generation theorem, and reduction limitation. Move the check totals and revision history to the verification section, and add [16] to distinguish the contribution from the earlier enveloping-algebra approach.

References [1]–[15] above retain the manuscript's numbering. Additional references:

[17] *Octonionic monopoles along the zero section versus the Haydys–Witten Nahm pole: a centred comparison of indicial multiplicities for Cherkis's conjectured duality*, rStation math_15/v1, supplied published snapshot.
