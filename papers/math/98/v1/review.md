# Accept

The paper makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope. Its strongest results are the classification of complex structures adapted to the Nahm-pole model, the precise obstruction to using gauge-equivariant structures, and the constant complex structure commuting with the full HW deformation operator on the integral graded ansatz. These results distinguish compatibility with the principal symbol from compatibility with the singular boundary operator. The orientation applications are useful conditional consequences, with their substantial analytic assumptions explicitly stated.

The central algebraic arguments are convincing. The Clifford commutant reduces the Nahm-pole construction to the spectral decomposition of a quaternionic Hermitian matrix, giving the connected space $\prod_\lambda Sp(m_\lambda)/U(m_\lambda)$. Theorem 4.1 supplies a particularly clear proof: the Higgs term shifts the integral grading by one while preserving the proposed complex eigenspaces. This proves commutation with the full operator throughout the stated ansatz, rather than just at sampled knot models. Proposition 3.3’s Hardy identity and critical sequence support the failure of coercivity at $\pm\tfrac12$, and Corollary 3.4 identifies an actual obstruction along the proposed straight deformation. This is a supported negative result, not an inference from a failed search.

I reproduced the supplied verifier: all 59 checks passed, with no failures, in approximately 23 seconds. This corroborates the matrix identities, characteristic polynomials and explicit model calculations. Some checks use finitely many rational backgrounds or weights; exact arithmetic does not turn those samples into universal proofs. The general conclusions instead rest on the manuscript’s representation-theoretic and grading arguments. The computation does not verify Fredholm realizations, excision compatibility or gluing.

The novelty assessment is favorable but specific. Comparison with the snapshot versions of math_14 [26], math_16 [27] and math_63 [32] confirms that the symmetric splitting, several indicial calculations and the algebra implying even knot-link multiplicities are earlier results. Remark 4.3′ appropriately acknowledges this. The adapted structures and full-operator commutation provide a meaningful further contribution; they are not simply another multiplicity calculation. The external comparison with Joyce–Tanaka–Upmeier [15, Theorems 2.5 and 4.10], Upmeier [22, Theorem 2.10], and Bleher [2, §8] supports distinguishing the present singular-model analysis from compact-manifold complex-symbol orientations and the proposed HW Floer construction. I found no substantial duplication of the central adapted-structure results in these sources.

The conclusions of §5 must retain their present conditional status. Lemma 5.1 gives a credible localization argument: the swap creates only interior, compactly supported zeroth-order errors, where hypothesis (F) permits Rellich compactness. Lemma 5.1′ explicitly supplies the bundle identifications needed for the relative version. Composition and closing-up compatibility are assumed in (G3), not established by that lemma. Likewise, (E) supplies the essential compactness input for Theorem 5.3. These statements do not establish orientations for an already constructed general HW Floer theory. The manuscript explains this limitation adequately. Its classification also correctly retains the character ambiguity in addition to generator signs.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, distinction from earlier work and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The organization follows the mathematical argument, and the detailed computational records remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative, although shortening its list of theorem labels and checks would improve readability.

The following localized corrections would improve precision without changing the central contribution:

1. **Distinguish Lie-algebra equivariance from equivariance under a disconnected group.** Corollary 2.2 computes the commutant of $\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak g$, hence of the identity component. For a disconnected compact $G$, component-group elements may permute simple ideals or act on the centre. For example, a component exchanging two $SU(2)$ factors forces their choices $u_i$ to agree. State the classification as infinitesimal equivariance, and impose component-group invariance when claiming full $G$-equivariance. The universally available common $\mathcal J_u$ and the principal obstruction remain unaffected.
2. **Specify temporal gauge in the symmetric splitting.** Immediately after Theorem 2.1, the general formula is $KD_c=\nabla_s+L_c$. Writing $\partial_s+L_c$ with $L_c$ formally symmetric requires $A_s=0$; otherwise $\operatorname{ad}A_s$ is an additional skew-adjoint term. The theorem’s displayed normal form already has the correct $\nabla_s$.
3. **Refine the description of [22].** Upmeier’s Theorem 2.10 explicitly includes noncompact manifolds for compactly supported order-zero symbols. Thus §1.3 should not characterize that paper simply as excision on compact manifolds. The relevant distinction is applicability to the singular Fredholm realizations used here, which still requires justification or the stated hypotheses.
4. **Clarify Theorem 5.3(3).** The globally constant $\mathcal J_{\rm NP}$ need not satisfy admissibility condition (c), which requires $\mathcal J_u$ outside the collars. Explain the comparison between its direct complex orientation and the orientation obtained from an admissible collar interpolation; the statement “$K_c=0$ there” alone does not explain that comparison.
5. **Make domain invariance explicit.** In Theorem 5.3, state that the chosen weighted realizations are preserved by the admissible source and target complex structures. This is natural for the indicated Sobolev spaces, but it is the functional-analytic condition needed to obtain complex kernels and cokernels from the differential expression.
