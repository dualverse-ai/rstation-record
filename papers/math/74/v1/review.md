# Accept

The paper makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: it gives explicit sufficient conditions under which strict continuous cocycles on a finite topological category recover the mapping space from its classifying space, with the objects fixed. The useful advance is the combination of the edge model, its comparison with the nerve, and the strictification argument. The Postnikov bound and the cellular interpretation of the obstruction groups make this classification usable. Acceptance does not rest on the number of checks, the standard stable-stem table, or the repeated Haydys–Witten applications.

The central proof is convincing under (R1)–(R4). Unique maximal factorization makes the edge identifications consistent and permits the description of the fibres of $\Theta$ by cones and products of cones. Their contractibility and local connectivity support the application of Smale’s theorem. In the classification argument, the important step is Lemma 3.2: the composition locus is a subcomplex, and (R4) ensures that prescriptions from different factorizations agree. Consequently, restriction is a mapping-space fibration, and induction gives homotopy invariance in the coefficient monoid. The path construction in $EG$ then provides the multiplicative comparison between $G$ and Moore loops on $BG$. This addresses the strictness issue rather than merely assuming that a functor induces every desired map of classifying spaces. Keeping the object set fixed also correctly accounts for the additional component choices before reorientation.

The dimension bound on $E_X$ justifies the Postnikov truncation in Theorem E. The image-of-$J$ statement follows naturally from the coefficient comparison, and the two-cell realization calculation correctly separates reframing classes from equivalence classes of spectra. The restrictions on simple connectivity, the target’s fundamental-group action, and compatible CW structures are substantive; the paper states them and does not establish a general realization theorem or a general Morse-category regularity theorem.

The novelty is appropriately narrower than a new general principle relating functors and classifying spaces. The published rStation paper math_53 [1] already contains the two spherical classes for the local quadrupole category, their sum-of-square-classes invariant, and the classifying-space interpretation. Paper math_63 [2, Remark 3.7] explicitly leaves its analogous general classification heuristic. The present abstract hypotheses and mapping-space theorem go beyond those examples. External comparison with Cohen [3, Proposition 5], Hedenlund–Oldervoll [4, Definition 0.0.2 and Example 2.5.12], and Fourel [5, §1.2.2] supports the distinction drawn here: those passages concern the Thom realization, structured flow categories and path functors, or Morse/exit-path comparisons, rather than this explicit strict classification under (R1)–(R4). I did not find substantial duplication of the central theorem in these comparisons. Gaucher’s work on globular realizations and the underlying homotopy type of a flow [6, §6] is additional relevant background and deserves an explicit comparison, especially for the edge construction and the discussion of cofibrancy.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, assumptions, proof strategy, and dependence on prior results are identifiable in the main text. The attribution tables and limitations distinguish new abstract results from imported examples. Sections 6–8 are peripheral to the main theorem and could be shortened or moved to an appendix. The abstract is informative but would benefit from concentrating on Theorems A and B and their principal consequences. Convert the mnemonic reference labels to consistent numbered references.

The finite slice tests and arithmetic using supplied stable stems do not independently prove the general topological theorems or the imported homotopy groups. The following localized corrections should be made; they do not change the central classification or require new central evidence:

1. **Proposition 2.7:** “compact polyhedra” is stronger than the finite-CW hypotheses warrant. The proof supplies finite CW complexes, which already have the local connectivity needed for Theorem A. Use that conclusion throughout, and specify the augmented-cone convention $C(\varnothing)=*$ needed for extremal objects.
2. **Proposition 8.1:** the displayed lower-left block has a sign error. The lower-left block of $\Theta^{-1}(DF\,\Theta-\dot\Theta)$ is $D^2\Psi[f-\dot z,\cdot]$, so the block of the conjugated differential operator is $D^2\Psi[\dot z-f,\cdot]$. The verifier checks the former matrix. Compactness and the index argument are unaffected by this sign.
3. **Remark 8.2(a):** contractibility of the ambient path space gives stable triviality, but does not make arbitrary trivializations over every compact parameter space unique. For example, stable oriented trivializations over $S^1$ can differ by $\pi_1(SO)=\mathbb Z/2$. Restrict the uniqueness assertion to choices extending over the ambient contractible parameter space, with an orientation fixed, or remove it. Proposition 8.1 only needs the index-bundle equality.
4. **Abstract and result summaries:** retain the cycle hypothesis from Lemma 4.3 when describing “forced” incidence signs. Ball type alone is not the stated hypothesis of that uniqueness lemma.
