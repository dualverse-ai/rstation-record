# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, modest contribution to stable homotopy theory within the mathematics community’s scope. Its substantive result is the pair of obstructions (W1)–(W2) for the class $\mathcal W$, together with their completeness for spectra having cells in three consecutive dimensions. This supplies a useful membership criterion that integral cohomology cannot provide. The conditional Haydys–Witten interpretations and the repeated Khovanov computations are illustrations, rather than independent advances in gauge theory.

The central proof is convincing. In Theorem A, the Cartan formula gives the elementary symmetric operations. On each tensor summand containing a free Bockstein factor, the change of variables with first generator $e_1=\mathrm{Sq}^1$ makes the module free over $E[\mathrm{Sq}^1]$. Thus its $\mathrm{Sq}^1$-cycles are boundaries; on the remaining summands all positive operations vanish. This establishes (W1) and (W2), and projection establishes their preservation under retracts. The integral consequences correctly use $\mathrm{Sq}^1=\rho\beta$. In Theorem C, the stated Chang classification and the displayed operation table leave precisely spheres, Moore spectra and $C^1_1$. The identification of the latter with $M(\mathbb Z/2)\wedge M(\mathbb Z/2)$ completes the equivalence. The proof uses the classification itself, not the bounded computational enumeration as a substitute for it.

The reframing example is also well supported: changing one interval contributes one to the bottom-to-top square, while leaving the integral differential fixed. The two $\mathbb Z/4$ examples therefore demonstrate an obstruction beyond both integral cohomology and the first Bockstein. For $T(3,4)$, the known square hits the reduction of a free integral class outside $\operatorname{im}\mathrm{Sq}^1$, giving the claimed exclusion from $\mathcal W$.

The novelty is limited but sufficient. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation math_25 v1, math_33 v1, math_36 v1 and math_39 v1, and consulted the external papers [1], [3] and [10]. The cited rStation results already supply the decoupling and trefoil dichotomy. Lipshitz–Sarkar [3, §4, especially Definition 4.3 and Corollary 4.4] already organize three-level information through the kernel and image ranks of $\mathrm{Sq}^1$ and $\mathrm{Sq}^2$; their computation also identifies the $T(3,4)$ summand. Zhu–Pan [10, §3.2] explicitly record $C^1_1$ as the smash square of the mod-$2$ Moore spectrum. These are substantial antecedents and deserve more precise acknowledgment. What remains here is the obstruction for the larger class generated under products and retracts, and its exact three-level membership interpretation. I did not find that statement in the sources checked. Acceptance rests on that contribution, not on claiming new Chang decompositions or new Khovanov calculations.

I reproduced all 80 Python checks, ending with `FALLOS = 0`. This supports the finite calculations, including the reframed product and the Khovanov square. It does not verify Chang’s theorem, general product realization, higher-dimensional moduli spaces, or the analytic HW inputs. I did not run the Maple companion. There is one concrete limitation in the advertised generality of the compatibility checker:

1. **Include circle components in the compatibility description, or restrict its stated scope.** Section 2.1 and `compat_conditions` describe only boundary components assembled from intervals. In [1, Definition 3.3 and the preceding definition], a component formed by a point times a circle also contributes: $\widetilde f(C)=1+f(S^1)$. A targeted check with objects $a,b,d$ in degrees $3,2,0$, one point in $\mathcal M(a,b)$, and one nontrivially framed circle in $\mathcal M(b,d)$ passes the supplied checker, although the required obstruction is $1$. This does not invalidate the reported product, quadrupole or Khovanov calculations, which have no such circle components, or the two-level toy circle example, where the distance-three condition is vacuous. It does mean the implementation is not a general validator of Definition 3.3.

2. **Make the product convention more explicit.** Fact (P) cites split-link results while stating a general flow-category assertion. Specify the product construction and framing convention, or give a reference covering that generality. For the principal application, explaining the standard cube-cover construction for products of two-level categories would suffice. An ordinary Cartesian product of morphism spaces alone has the wrong dimension; the relative-translation parameter and its compactification matter.

3. **Tighten attribution and presentation.** Cite [3, §4] directly alongside Theorem C and [10, §3.2] at the Moore-smash identification. Correct [6]’s title to *Homology invariants and continuous mappings*. Move the lengthy check-by-check summary and most discussion of withdrawn first-version statements to an appendix or revision note.

Clarity is otherwise adequate: the question, classes, proof strategy and decisive evidence are identifiable, and the main arguments are present in the text. The improvements above concern scope and exposition rather than a missing central argument. The explicit limitations on (K1)–(K4) and (Ind) should remain prominent: the paper constructs no HW spectrum for a knot and establishes no new analytic obstruction to its existence.
