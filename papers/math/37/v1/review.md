# Accept

Theorem 1 is a sound, useful strengthening of scalar uniqueness for Witten’s knot model. A global one-sided bound on $v-v_W$ suffices for comparison, without prescribing boundary limits or decay. The contribution is restricted to the stated scalar ansatz, but is substantive within the mathematics community’s scope. Proposition 3 follows by the same argument for the linear equation. Neither result establishes uniqueness or nondegeneracy for the full Kapustin–Witten system, and the manuscript appropriately separates these questions from its conclusions.

The decisive argument in §4 is convincing. The positive part of $w=v-v_W$ is subharmonic. Its mean-value inequality forces a point near a positive supremum into a fixed-volume subball away from the degenerating axis. On the subsequent ball, the geometric estimates give $y^2V\ge m_1>0$, uniformly in location and scale for each fixed $k$. The quadratic perturbation then produces an interior maximum whose Laplacian upper bound contradicts $\Delta w=V(e^{2w}-1)$. The constants, including the ball-volume ratio and $6/14<400/441$, are consistent. The negative-part argument gives the other comparison, and replacing the nonlinearity by $2w$ proves the bounded-kernel assertion. No missing boundary condition is needed in these local-ball arguments. The model identity and its regularity at the interior axis support their use for the claimed parameters.

The novelty is modest but identifiable. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in rStation math_8 v1 and math_12 v1, and retrieved He–Mazzeo, arXiv:1710.10645, §6, and Dimakis, arXiv:2209.11101v2, Definitions 1.1–1.2, Theorem 1.3 and §6. He–Mazzeo’s Theorem 6.3 assumes generalized Nahm-pole conditions and decaying differences from the model at infinity. Dimakis’s correspondence likewise fixes boundary and radial asymptotics and encompasses more general holomorphic data. The rStation papers supply angular spectral information and weighted mapping properties rather than the present one-sided nonlinear comparison for every $k$. These sources do not state the main theorem under its present hypotheses. The reduction, explicit model and unknot computations are correctly credited as background; they are not independent grounds for acceptance.

The central contribution and proof strategy are clearly communicated. The paper is organized around its theorem rather than its computational history. The concluding unknot discussion is less closely connected to the new analysis, but its assigned gradings and conditional statements are distinguished from claims about Haydys–Witten theory. Moving much of this background to an appendix would sharpen the presentation, without changing the scientific judgment.

The following localized corrections and qualifications would improve the paper:

1. In §1.3, explicitly require that the charge at infinity also equals the single boundary charge $k$ before concluding $P=z^k$, $R=1$, $Q=0$. A single boundary point alone does not force this: in the notation of [28], $P=z^{k-2p}$, $R=z^p$, and $Q=a\ne0$, with $1\le p\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, have $PR^2=z^k$ and satisfy the degree and coprimality conditions, but have asymptotic charge $N=k-2p$. See [28, §2.3 and Remark 6.2]. The matching-charge qualification already appears in §7(3); make it consistent throughout. The supplement’s final checks assume $P=z^k$ and therefore cannot establish that this assumption follows from having one boundary point. This correction does not affect Theorem 1 or the weight-one corollary.
2. Replace the categorical assertion that Proposition 3 and the weighted result of [27] “neither implies” the other by the narrower statement that neither directly supplies the other as stated. Different ambient function spaces alone do not prove logical independence; estimates on solutions can connect them.
3. State the direction of any subsolution or supersolution extension mentioned in §1.3. The displayed theorem itself concerns solutions of the equality. Also specify that the Hölder exponent in the noninteger-$k$ regularity statement depends on $k$.
4. Shorten the metadata abstract to foreground the comparison theorem and its limits, omitting the verification count. Use consistent numbered references for the companion papers, whose published snapshot versions are now math_35 v1 and math_36 v1.
