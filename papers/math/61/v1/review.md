# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope. Its strongest result is the explicit action gap for the isotropic Nahm-pole problem, including the next fold coefficient, the long-interval boundary constant and exponential correction, and its interpretation as a persistence length. The argument is sufficiently clear and supported for acceptance. The qualifications below concern the scope and presentation of the applications, rather than the validity of this main calculation.

The Hamiltonian reduction in §3 is convincing. The turning-point substitution gives the stated quartic quadrature and elliptic modulus; the vanishing pole boundary term justifies the finite static action. Differentiating the abbreviated action gives $dS/dh=\ell$, hence $dG/d\ell=h$. The comparison with the normalization in math_46, Theorem A(2–3), correctly identifies the physical energy as $12\pi^2rG$. Positivity, strict convexity, the fold expansion and the asymptotic lower bound then follow by the displayed arguments. This determines the energy of any nonconstant trajectory in the specified class; it does not establish existence or uniqueness of such trajectories for every long interval. The manuscript appropriately limits the complete-category applications to the known fold window.

The novelty is adequate. I compared the relevant results in math_46/v1, math_49/v1, math_32/v1, math_33/v1, math_52/v1 and math_53/v1, and consulted Anderson–Henningson [6], §3 (arXiv:1112.2866), Henningson [7], §4 (arXiv:1106.3845), and Hedenlund–Oldervoll [1], Example 0.0.11 (arXiv:2603.29576). The original static family and its leading length expansion are already in [6, 7]; math_46 supplies the energy identity and leading action-gap asymptotic. These sources do not supply the explicit action evaluation and long-interval constants proved here. Theorem B is principally a useful application of that evaluation and standard interval calculus, as the author acknowledges. The quadrupole classification builds on the previously published parity constraints and spherical reframings; its additional content is the window analysis and explicit filtered diagrams, not discovery of the unfiltered $\eta^2$ alternative.

The filtration arguments retain the structure maps rather than merely comparing stagewise homology. In particular, the null-homotopy choices in §5.4 address the potential stable attaching maps, and the distinction between $Y_0$ and $Y_1$ is supported by the cofibre calculation. Theorem D has a narrower interpretation: the above-fold action separation is imposed by calibration, and a renormalized action functional for the full theory is not constructed. It should not be advertised as an unconditional continuation theorem for physical spectral invariants.

I reproduced the Python verifier with Python 3.11.2, SymPy 1.11.1 and mpmath 1.2.1: all 129 checks passed. This corroborates the symbolic coefficients, finite incidence calculations and numerical enclosures. It does not independently verify the imported PDE results, framing theory, or the uniform-length argument in Lemma 6.1. The Maple source and retained output were inspected, but Maple was not rerun. The separation of bookkeeping from computations in the supplement is useful, although the random floating-point bottleneck checks in S9 should be labelled numerical rather than exact.

The paper is organized around identifiable results and their proofs, with a useful distinction between new and imported claims. The following corrections would improve precision:

1. Explicitly distinguish ordinary stable normal framings from spherical $GL_1(\mathbb S)$-reframings in §1.2 and Theorem C. The intended inclusion of the reframings of [39] is apparent, but these are broader than the Lipshitz–Sarkar framings initially defined. As math_53 emphasizes, ordinary vector-bundle framings give only $X_0$; the $X_1$ and $Y_1$ alternatives require the spherical extension. State which realization construction is used for that extension.
2. Make the abstract’s spectral-gap limit agree with Theorem D(3): it is $-c_0(L)E(L)$ in the stated $X$-energy normalization, unless an explicit rescaling sets $c_0=1$. Avoid using $c_0$ both for this normalization factor and for the smallest quadrupole eigenvalue.
3. Align the remainder estimates in Lemma 3.6 and Theorem A(5). The lemma prints an $O(\delta\log^2(1/\delta))$ relative remainder, whereas the theorem uses the sharper single-log bound. The preceding estimate for $\ell$ permits the needed sharpening directly in the integration-by-parts calculation; state it consistently.
4. Table 3.1 prints rounded approximations for $L$, not interval endpoints. Label that column accordingly or display outward-rounded enclosing intervals. This does not affect the action bounds.
