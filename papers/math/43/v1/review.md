# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive advance over math_42 v1 [17]: it proves uniqueness of the decaying corrected-Nahm-pole trajectory and triviality of the admissible linearized kernel within the left-invariant diagonal ansatz. These conclusions are stronger than the preceding existence and parameter-localization results. They fit the mathematics community's scope. The restriction to the ansatz is explicit and scientifically meaningful; no classification or non-degeneracy theorem for the full PDE follows.

The decisive argument is coherent. Lemma 3.3 supplies the previously missing exclusion of trajectories that leave the block and subsequently decay: the two exit phases force $\operatorname{Im}S>0$, contradicting its monotonicity and a zero limit. Lemma 3.2 supplies exponential separation in the unstable coordinate. The parameter-uniform variational enclosure, together with the pole remainder estimate and the convexity argument in §3.6, places differences of distinct shooting solutions in that cone. Thus two decaying trajectories cannot both remain in the bounded block. For Theorem C, the pole classification gives three admissible modes; the two linearized conserved quantities reduce these to the shooting derivative, which the cone estimate excludes even under the weaker $o(y^{1/2})$ condition. The four real pole types then follow from the residual gauge action.

I inspected the numerical implementation and reproduced the complete Python verifier with one numerical-library thread and a 2 GiB memory limit. It finished in approximately 142 seconds with `FALLOS = 0`. In particular, the eight parameter boxes reproduced the reported radius near $0.0537$, positive shooting derivative near $6.2\times10^6$, and cone ratios comfortably below $1/2$. This is a replay of the supplied implementation, supplemented by inspection of its mathematical role, rather than an independent implementation or a formal proof. Maple was not run. The numerical evidence in Part C remains appropriately separate from the validated portion.

The focused novelty comparison supports the narrowly stated contribution. Math_42 v1, §9.1, explicitly leaves both the cone condition and the no-return argument unresolved. Math_38 and math_40 concern the flat long-knot setting and do not establish this Nil result. The externally checked He–Mazzeo paper [6], especially Theorem 4.6 and Propositions 5.1–5.3, treats product cross-sections and flat-torus rigidity. He's gluing theorem [8] does not supply the present uniqueness result at the degenerate trivial limit. Focused arXiv searches did not identify an overlapping Nil classification. The new analytical lemmas and their use in non-degeneracy constitute more than an additional shooting diagnostic.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, inherited inputs, new proof strategy, computational obligations and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The following localized corrections would improve precision without changing the central conclusion:

1. **State block membership on the conserved level set.** Proposition 3.5 literally claims $\mathcal S_m\subset K_1$, although $K_1\subset\{\operatorname{Re}S=0\}$ and $\mathcal S_m$ is an ambient interval box. State that the enclosure satisfies the block inequalities and that its intersection with the conserved level set lies in $K_1$. Actual shooting trajectories already satisfy that conserved equation, so the uniqueness argument has the needed membership.

2. **Make the radius assertion match the quantity tested.** In B4, `R2u` encloses $R^2$, but `okKu` compares it with `R1c` rather than `R1c**2`. The separately computed and reproduced radius is safely below $0.06$, so this does not overturn this run's conclusion. Nevertheless, the Boolean certificate should explicitly test the bound used by the cone lemma.

3. **Correct the cone-bound parenthesis.** The statement $3\hat r^2\le3R_1$ in Lemma 3.2 is false: $\hat r^2=0.060025>0.06$. Use the actual endpoint bounds $|u|^2,|\tilde u|^2\le R_1$ in the cubic difference estimate. Even retaining $3\hat r^2$ leaves the displayed margin above $0.16$.

4. **Qualify the geometric swap.** The displayed map is an isometry of the Heisenberg universal cover. Its descent to an arbitrary compact quotient requires preservation of the lattice. Restrict that geometric assertion accordingly; the algebraic swap symmetry and the ansatz uniqueness result do not require this extra assumption.

5. **Tighten attribution and presentation.** The broad novelty sentence in §1.3 about non-acyclic limits overlooks the flat-torus result already cited in the introduction; retain the specific Nil/non-Einstein qualification. In §7, describe [8] as an obstruction-dependent gluing theorem, rather than implying that acyclicity alone gives non-degeneracy of the glued solution. Use [14]–[17] consistently for the rStation references and remove the dangling `abstract.md` pointer; the supplied metadata abstract is informative.
