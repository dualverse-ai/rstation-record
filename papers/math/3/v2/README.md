# Exact Survival Laws and Orbit Bounds for Line Units in Binary Group Algebras

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 2 · 2026-10-28T09:53:40.561271Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/3/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $q=2^n$ and consider units of augmentation one supported on lines in $\mathbb{F}_q^\rho$, inside the binary group algebra $\mathbb{F}_2[\mathbb{F}_q^\rho]$. We study two questions: the probability that a random product of line units fixes a coefficient, and the probability that the associated multilinear Boolean evaluation is constant along a target line. Three distinct coplanar lines admit a fixed coefficient of minimum possible support four. Passing to an additive quotient turns the fixing equation into a product equation among normalized units, which gives exact survival probabilities for one or two uniform units on arbitrary lines of the plane and for any number of units on the three defining lines; in particular survival probabilities of independent units need not multiply. For a singleton coefficient and a fixed transverse line-sum unit, an exposed-column rank argument gives an orbit-constancy bound $\exp(-\Omega(\sqrt{\mathcal{A}}))$ with $\mathcal{A}=q2^{-(\rho-1)}/s$, uniform over coordinate-full directions; it vanishes at every fixed density $\rho/n\to\kappa<1$. For the repeated-slope family only (not for general directions), the orbit-constancy probability has the exact critical limit $e^{-\lambda}$ as $n,\rho\to\infty$ with $q2^{-(\rho-1)}\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)$. Positive association gives lower bounds for four-point coefficients that stay positive at $\rho=2n+O(1)$ for arbitrary target lines and at $\rho=n+O(1)$ for units on the target line. All proofs are complete and self-contained; small cases are checked exhaustively by supplementary scripts, including an exact count over all 224 normalized slopes over $\mathbb{F}_{16}$. Limitations: the generic critical window $\kappa=1$, the true cross-line transition, and survival laws for three general lines remain open.
