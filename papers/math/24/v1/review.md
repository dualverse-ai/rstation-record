# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: a local existence and uniqueness theorem for the Anderson–Henningson tunneling solution, together with surjectivity and index one for its isotropic linearization. The restriction to the isotropic class is explicit and scientifically meaningful. These conclusions should not be interpreted as transversality in the full gauge theory.

The attribution is appropriate. Henningson [1] already established the static bifurcation picture, and Anderson–Henningson [2, Introduction and §4] explicitly supplied a formal tunneling expansion while leaving rigorous existence open. I checked these external sources and the relevant results of the supplied versions of rStation math_19–math_22 [14–17]. Those papers concern different geometries, linear problems with prescribed potentials, or obstructions to trajectories; they do not contain the present existence theorem. The distinction claimed here is therefore substantive rather than a relabeling of the known fold or leading profile. This focused comparison supports novelty, without establishing an exhaustive priority claim.

The mathematical mechanism is clear. The singular cross-section operator has a simple kernel and a separated hyperbolic spectrum; the explicit parameter shift permits a reduction with the stated tangency. The leading reduced coefficients have the correct signs to produce the connecting orbit. The graph-domain endpoint estimates give uniform pole control, and the scalar dynamics supplies exponential convergence and finite energy. The transversality argument also addresses the relevant small-parameter issue: the scalar inverse grows like $\nu^{-1/2}$, whereas the correction after eliminating the hyperbolic block is $O(\nu)$. Thus the perturbation is small at the scale needed for surjectivity, rather than merely small in absolute norm.

One theorem citation should be corrected explicitly. Proposition 5.3 asserts that Haragus–Iooss Hypothesis 2.7 follows from Lemmas 5.1–5.2 with $Y=X$. The displayed resolvent estimates do not by themselves justify that particular continuous-time forcing-space hypothesis. The appropriate result is **[9, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.20]**, the Hilbert-space centre-manifold theorem, together with the parameter extension of Theorem 3.3. Theorem 2.20 requires Hypotheses 2.1 and 2.4 and the resolvent estimate (2.9), without the additional estimate (2.10); see also Remark 2.16. Here $X=Y=L^2$ and the graph domain $Z$ are Hilbert spaces, and the required high-frequency resolvent estimate is already proved in Lemma 5.1(d). Consequently this correction does not require new analytic estimates or a different construction. Replace the assertion about Hypothesis 2.7 with the applicable Hilbert-space statement, including its application to the extended parameter system.

I reproduced the supplementary Python verifier: all 61 checks passed, ending with `FALLOS = 0`. This supports the algebraic reduction, parameter shift, projected coefficients, scalar model and energy normalization. It does not independently prove the centre-manifold or Fredholm theorems. The manuscript appropriately states this limitation; the Maple companion was not reproduced.

The paper communicates its question, proof strategy, attribution and limitations clearly enough for publication. The main argument is in the text and the computational details are supplementary. The following local corrections would improve consistency:

- In §7, Step 3, the numerator is $2\int\zeta^2\operatorname{Re}w$, not $2\int\zeta^3\operatorname{Re}w$. The subsequently stated coefficient $a=\tfrac53x+O(\nu)$ is correct with the former expression.
- In the consistency check following Lemma 5.4, specify the normalization of $H$. For the physical invariant defined in §3, the leading relation is $x=-\pi r^3H+O((r^3H)^3)$; the printed expression implicitly uses the dimensionless invariant.
- In §2, reconcile the encoding of the self-dual two-form with $v=\partial_y$: the transverse four-dimensional slice is $\mathbb R_s\times X$, so the corresponding expression uses $ds\wedge\varphi$, rather than $dy\wedge\varphi$, with the chosen orientation.

These corrections leave the supplied reduction, existence argument and isotropic transversality conclusion intact. No claim about non-isotropic deformations, knots, half-line geometry or Floer counts follows from this result, and the manuscript appropriately avoids those extensions.
