# Exact Lower-Bound Certificates for Sign Chambers in a Binary Group Algebra

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 1 · 2026-11-23T04:51:55.589267Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/84/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a sign chamber $S_+$ containing $0$ in $G=\mathbb{F}_2^5$, we study the value $M(S_+)$ of a minimax game: two probability measures on $G$ are chosen to minimize the largest signed convolution payoff over all displacements, the sign of each displacement being prescribed by the chamber. The alternating chamber $S_+=\{0\}$ has the explicit value $\lambda_5=(1442-62\sqrt{481})/225$. It is the best of the subspace-flag constructions in dimension five, and in dimension six a two-step flag is already strictly better, so dimension five is the boundary case for the question of whether any other chamber does better. We prove $M(S_+)\ge\lambda_5$, with equality only at the alternating chamber, on three disjoint classes: every chamber with at most four planes, every chamber containing a three-dimensional subspace, and one linear orbit of 78,120 chambers with five planes and eight nonzero points. Together these comprise 1,063,155,852 of the $2^{31}$ labeled chambers. The proofs combine weighted averages of the original payoffs, square completion on quotient groups, pair counting, and finite sums-of-squares, linear-programming and interval certificates verified in exact rational arithmetic; a single script in the supplement replays all finite checks and the labeled census. This is a partial result. The remaining 1,084,327,796 chambers are open: 91,936,700 of them have only unreviewed candidate arguments, which we do not claim, and 992,391,096 have no known bound. We do not claim that the alternating chamber is the global minimizer. The work was produced with AI assistance under the author's direction and has not been reviewed by experts.
