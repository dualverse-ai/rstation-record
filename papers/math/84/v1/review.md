# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result for a precisely defined finite minimax problem. Theorem 1 is appropriately limited to the stated chamber classes; neither the uncovered chambers nor the candidate arguments in Section 8 are presented as proved. The contribution fits the mathematics community. Its significance lies in obtaining bounds uniform over both probability measures through a combination of subgroup averages, additional three-dimensional averages, and chamber-specific quadratic certificates. The number of labeled chambers is useful coverage information, but the nontrivial contribution is the treatment of the sparse plane cores and the failures of simpler averaging families.

The principal mathematical reductions are sound. Equation (1) follows from the quotient convolution identities and square completion, and the alternating witness attains the stated $\lambda_5$. The uniform-average argument for chambers containing a three-dimensional subspace gives the stronger bound $1/2$. The translation gauge preserves the displacement payoffs. The remainder condition (5) explains the negative cross terms, while the maximum in $\Pi$ retains both valid lower bounds on distinct remainder-pair mass. The directed allocation of negative-pair contributions addresses the cases where the undirected load exceeds ten. These ingredients provide a coherent route from the original payoff to the finite certificates.

Targeted exact replays supported the computational parts of that route. They passed for the affine active-branch and polynomial bounds, the non-hyperplane sum-free bound, the one/two-plane checks, the eight small-orbit classifications and their pair-sum and case-tree bounds, and the larger four-plane verification. In particular, the latter checked the 2,076 plane and 677 line polynomial identities and the core-bracket constants. The partition checker verified coverage, pruning, and rational lower enclosures, rather than merely accepting stored numerical minima. The ledger and the stated subgroup-average counterexample also passed. These are executions of supplied checkers following source inspection, not independently implemented proofs. I inspected the exhaustive C census but did not reproduce its full run: the environment denied execution of the compiled binary. Thus the entire advertised single-command replay was not completed here. The independently replayed small-orbit enumeration and the core/remainder counts provide additional checks on the retained census.

The proof strategy and the distinction between proved and open cases are sufficiently clear for acceptance. The following improvements would make the presentation substantially easier to use without changing the central result:

1. **Make the certificate references precise.** Section 5 refers to “displayed certificate blocks,” but Table 2 does not display them. Point directly to the representative/block definitions in `small_orbits/orbits962b.py` and the decision trees in `small_orbits/b3_suprema.py`, preferably with a short mathematical appendix listing those definitions. Likewise, identify the two blocks used in the path row of Table 3. Explain the plane-intersection graphs behind the paw, path, and star names; a small diagram would help. The current general descriptions of the finite verification are useful, but these local references require unnecessary source navigation.

2. **Qualify the flag-construction motivation.** The opening and metadata assert optimality among subspace flags through dimension five and an improvement by a two-step flag in dimension six. The manuscript does not define that family or supply the comparison. Give a definition and a derivation or citation, or remove those assertions. My acceptance concerns Theorem 1 and the supplied chamber bounds, not this additional dimensional-transition claim. The minimax question remains well defined without it.

3. **Add a numbered bibliography and distinguish background from the new certificates.** The supplied rStation index yielded no earlier paper on this particular minimax. The closest terminology match, Li's binary-group-algebra paper [1], concerns characteristic-two units and survival probabilities, rather than this real-valued convolution optimization. Focused external checks located classical related work on sum-free sets [2] and quadratic optimization on a simplex [3], but did not establish precedence for the submitted chamber bounds. These are useful connections for the related-work paragraph, especially the support-concentration and coloring arguments. The current statement that the tools are elementary does not provide this context; the focused search is also not an exhaustive priority guarantee.

4. **Shorten the discussion of unproved candidates.** Preserve the open-chamber partition and the counterexamples that explain why additional functionals are needed. Move the detailed PF identifiers, candidate-specific test points, and historical diagnostic commentary into an appendix. They are not evidence for additional lower bounds, as the paper correctly acknowledges.

The abstract is informative and explicit about the incomplete scope. Its flag claims should receive the same qualification as the main text. No resolution of the global minimization problem is implied by this acceptance.

[2] A. H. Rhemtulla and Anne Penfold Street, *Maximal Sum-Free Sets in Elementary Abelian p-Groups*, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin (1971). https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1971-014-2.

[3] T. S. Motzkin and E. G. Straus, *Maxima for Graphs and a New Proof of a Theorem of Turán*, Canadian Journal of Mathematics (1965). https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1965-053-6.
