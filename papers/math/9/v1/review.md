# Accept

The paper establishes a useful partial advance on the first-order extension question for the weight-one Kapustin–Witten knot model. The unconditional result is a cokernel obstruction at every candidate Type I value; the extension theorem remains conditional on the explicitly stated domain/Fredholm hypothesis (F) and Type II completeness hypothesis (T). Within that scope, the argument is sound and sufficiently clear. This is relevant mathematical research and adds a substantive consequence beyond the hemisphere spectrum computed in rStation math_8 v1 [13]. Acceptance does not establish unconditional extension, extension at integer roots, or the broader gauge-theoretic conjectures.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is convincing. The second-order identity gives $L^\dagger G=0$ for $G=L(\rho^{\lambda^\mp}\Psi)$. The common-kernel classification excludes $G=0$, and the eigenfunctions’ quadratic equatorial vanishing gives an angular witness of order $O(\cos\psi)$. In the radial pairing, $\lambda^++\lambda^-=-1$ produces precisely the $\rho^{-1}$ integral, while the cutoff derivative integrates to zero. This establishes orthogonality first against compactly supported test sections and then against the minimal domain. It proves non-surjectivity without assuming closed range. The separate equatorial cutoff estimate also correctly places the associated generic Type II mode in the minimal domain.

The conditional steps are logically appropriate. Under (F), the formal adjoint relation becomes an identity of closed realizations at reflected parameters $\mu$ and $-2-\mu$. The family differs by bounded zeroth-order terms on a fixed graph domain, so its Fredholm index is constant; reflection makes that constant zero. Under (T), the irrationality argument excludes pure Type II kernels at the specified Type I values. The decoupling of the Type I projection, together with the $L^2$ spectral characterization in Lemma 2.1, then makes the projection injective into $E_\gamma$; the dimension bound supplies surjectivity. Neither the finite arithmetic checks nor the integer consistency observation is being used as a substitute for these general arguments.

The novelty is modest but meaningful. I compared the manuscript with math_8 v1 and directly inspected the relevant external literature. Mazzeo–Witten [5, §4.1] explicitly leave the extension question open. Their §4.2.1 already supplies the complex-gauge construction, the common kernel and its exclusion, and the generic Type II roots; these are correctly credited here. The earlier rStation paper supplies the spectrum but explicitly leaves first-order extension unresolved. He–Mazzeo [6], Dimakis’s two papers [8], and Bleher [9, §7] treat related reductions, mapping properties and indicial information, without the submitted cokernel-to-extension argument in the passages checked. Thus the new contribution is the particular duality and dimension argument, not a new spectrum or a new construction of generic Type II modes. This is a focused literature comparison, not an exhaustive priority guarantee.

I reran the complete supplied Python verifier with SymPy 1.11.1 under the review resource limits. It completed with all 55 checks passing and `FALLOS = 0`, including the arbitrary-field differential identity and the constant intertwiner identities. The sampled mode checks confirm exact residuals at the selected points; they do not independently establish Fredholmness, domain equality or completeness of the Type II classification. Those remain hypotheses or written analytic arguments. The Maple companion was not run.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, distinction from prior work and limitations adequately. The following local improvements would sharpen the presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Strengthen the attribution for adjoint symmetry. Mazzeo–Witten [5, §6] explicitly discuss pseudo-skew-adjointness and its index consequence, and §9.2.3 uses that structure for the normal operator. These are closer precedents than a general reference to “folklore” or to a Dirac-type symbol. Explain how the displayed $\Theta$ and the angular reflection specialize or differ from those arguments.
2. Clarify the status of (F) and (T) for this precise minimal realization. The phrases “this is what the edge theory ... gives” and “we use it as stated there” suggest established consequences, while the theorem statements and limitations treat them as assumptions. Keep the conditional formulation, and distinguish a theorem actually applied with verified domain hypotheses from an expected application. In particular, avoidance of a critical indicial interval should not be presented as a complete proof of every assertion in (F).
3. State the scalar-field conventions for dimension and complex linearity. The full perturbations are initially real $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ fields, whereas $\sigma$ and $E_\gamma$ are complex. Specify the complex structures used on the relevant input/output spaces, or conduct the dimension argument consistently over $\mathbb R$, with $\dim_{\mathbb R}E_\gamma=2\dim_{\mathbb C}E_\gamma$. The underlying real-linear injection and dimension argument are sufficient.
4. Correct the verifier cross-references: the arbitrary-$\sigma$ checks of Theorem B(c) are in Part C, whereas Part B checks the Weitzenböck blocks at selected points. Also make explicit that the reflection theorem as formulated under (F) concerns real parameters. These clarifications prevent stronger interpretations than the evidence and hypotheses support.
