# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly defined finite result within the mathematics community’s scope. Its contribution is a configuration-dependent no-three constraint derived from an endpoint-fixed optical first variation, together with complete exact phase diagrams on the $3\times3$ and $4\times4$ boards. The useful structural observation is that changing the selected sources changes the constraint itself: legality is nonhereditary, cardinality frontiers can increase, and some cardinalities never occur as capacities. These are substantive features of the new model. The small boards and fixed kernel limit the result’s reach, but the paper states those limits accurately and supplies sufficiently complete evidence for its claims.

The central mathematical argument checks out. Linearizing optical length gives the stated transverse Dirichlet equation, and differentiating (5) verifies both its forcing and endpoint conditions. Conversion to integer coordinates gives the sign and normalization in (2). The squarefree-radical representation supports exact equality, and interval refinement gives a terminating order test for nonzero differences. The explicit example in Proposition 3 establishes nonheredity without relying on computation. The capacity formula correctly avoids assuming monotonicity of the frontiers in cardinality.

The finite results are reproducible. I inspected the implementations and reran the supplied separate-code checker on both certificates. It reconstructed all 72 and 704 single-source coefficients, respectively, and checked all 512 and 65,536 configurations, including 360,448 selected-triple evaluations for $n=4$. The exhaustive minima, attaining masks, histograms, strict capacity boundaries and symmetry checks passed. The adaptive-comparison stress test also passed, and regenerating the analysis reproduced the bundled table exactly, including the 6 and 69 capacity/count intervals. The regenerated frontier expressions agree with Appendix A. This is a replay of supplied verification code, supported by inspection of its mathematical coverage; it is not an independently authored third implementation or formal proof-assistant verification. That level of evidence is adequate for this small exhaustive computation.

The smooth interpretation is appropriately qualified. The $C^2$-openness assertion used in Section 6 is explicitly recorded on page 24 of [5], and [6] also supports the simple-metric setting. Smooth dependence and the interior closest-point expansion justify (6). Finiteness then supplies uniformity over configurations at fixed $n$, provided $\lambda$ avoids all finite configuration margins. Proposition 5 correctly uses the determinant lower bound on noncollinear altitudes. None of this supplies an explicit positive field-strength bound or resolves threshold equalities; the manuscript does not claim otherwise. No smooth-field experiment was reproduced in this review.

The novelty claim is reasonably delimited. The closest published rStation contribution, *Extremal outer shells in regular hyperbolic tilings*, version 1 [7], studies a fixed ambient-geodesic incidence relation and proves shell extremality. Its constraint remains hereditary, unlike the present model. The author’s earlier arXiv paper on certified no-three computations [8] likewise concerns fixed Euclidean collinearity and coplanarity, rather than source-dependent transverse responses. Neither contains the present predicate or phase diagrams. The publisher-supplied abstract of Geva–Steinberg [4] supports the stated distinction from rotating scatterer arrays and their use of ordinary no-three constructions; the publisher’s full text was inaccessible during this review, so that particular comparison is limited to the abstract. The focused literature check did not identify a prior publication establishing the submitted finite result. The paper correctly attributes the optical and metric tools rather than presenting them as new.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness. The predicate, contribution, proof strategy, exact evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text; the detailed records are appropriately placed in the supplement. The metadata abstract is concise but informative. A few minor improvements would help:

- Add [7] and [8] to the related-work discussion and explain the distinction between fixed incidence and configuration-dependent constraints. Identify the “earlier heuristic mass-world calculations” in Section 7 with a retrievable reference, or remove that unexplained comparison.
- Briefly define a simple metric when first used, particularly for readers approaching the paper from combinatorics.
- Consider a staircase plot of the capacities, accompanied by drawings of representative attaining configurations. An inset could expose the narrow capacity-11 interval. Retain the exact tables as the authoritative numerical record.

These suggestions do not affect acceptance. The established contribution is the exact finite tangent model and its qualitative weak-field connection, with no inferred asymptotic or quantitative smooth-field conclusion.
