# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive, clearly delimited advance for the straight model knot: it extends the weight-one spectral argument to every positive integer weight and identifies the attained endpoints of the gap around the exceptional eigenvalues $\pm1$. This fits the mathematics community's scope. The uniform exceptional-sector calculation and the closed-form meridian solutions provide meaningful content beyond substituting the ground-state estimate from math_82 into an existing formula.

The central argument is sound. The Type I block identity, together with the Friedrichs-domain argument in §4.2, places every eigenvector in the proposed gap in $E_0$. The rotation identity then forces its eigenvalue to be integral. The kernel exclusion is particularly effective: the explicit bounded-pole solution has size $O(\sin^{\mathfrak r}\psi)$, whereas the Wronskian of two independent solutions has order $\sin^{\mathfrak r-1}\psi$. This excludes a second bounded solution; the remaining solution fails square integrability at the equator. Appendix A supplies enough concrete information to follow this argument. The construction $w=L_3(\rho^{\lambda_+}\psi_0)$ also justifies endpoint attainment: the pointwise identity prevents its vanishing, and the form-domain estimates supply the required angular $L^2$ control. Essential self-adjointness then identifies the maximal realization with $\bar S$.

I checked the relevant reductions in math_16 [10] and math_38 [12], and the ground-state results imported from math_82 [17]. The hypotheses needed for their use are identified and checked here. The distinction between an energy-space isomorphism and a Fredholm self-adjoint operator on $L^2$ is important and correctly retained. Likewise, the restriction of nonlinear local rigidity to $\mathbb R_t$, and the restriction of the statements outside the principal weight window to translation-invariant fields, keep the conclusions within their proofs.

For novelty, I compared the submission with math_18 [11], math_38 [12], math_82 [17], and the external texts of Mazzeo–Witten [4, §4] and Taubes [5, Part II]. Mazzeo–Witten already give the generic and exceptional indicial families, and their complex-gauge construction is closely related to the endpoint lifting used here. Taubes already proves the periodic all-weight energy isomorphism and an angular spectral exclusion. These should not be counted as new results. The manuscript substantially acknowledges this overlap. Its contribution is the uniform proof in the specified self-adjoint link realization, the exceptional-sector identities and kernel exclusion, and the resulting sharp spectral and weighted conclusions. The unresolved dictionary with Taubes' angular operator limits comparative claims, but does not undermine this independent proof. The coefficient in the large-weight asymptotic is correctly attributed to [17].

The computational evidence supports the argument, with a reproducibility qualification. Using SymPy 1.11.1, my full-script rerun reached the five-minute limit during Part C, after confirming the Type I block and general-point rotation symmetry. A targeted extraction of the inspected code also confirmed the exceptional-bundle symbol identity, the rotation/eigenvalue identity, the reduced meridian systems, their explicit solutions and the frame checks. I did not reproduce the entire 74-check run or the numerical spectral tables. The domain, Wronskian and functional-analytic arguments were assessed from the text rather than by these computations.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance: the question, proof strategy, dependencies and limitations are identifiable, and the main text explains the decisive reasoning rather than merely presenting a verification log. The following minor improvements would make the evidence and attribution more precise:

- Part G's translation-independence test evaluates one sample of $(c,\mu,\nu,\mathfrak r)$, without imposing the model relation. It is not an all-weight rank certificate. Add a short analytic justification from the displayed model, or a symbolic check on the actual profiles, and describe the existing test as a sample only.
- Distinguish exact identities from numerical and finite-sample checks consistently. In particular, the 50-digit test at $n=3.7$ should not carry an exact-verification label or itself establish a statement for all real $n$; Lemma 2.1's analytic calculation supplies that conclusion.
- Describe the computed Ritz values as numerical approximations to variational upper bounds. Floating-point quadrature and a numerical Gram-matrix cutoff do not certify upper bounds or exact multiplicities. The existing limitation that these computations are not used in the proof should remain prominent.
- In §1.3, distinguish Taubes' four-dimensional periodic energy isomorphism from the five-dimensional constant-trajectory consequence in Corollary 1(4), which requires the additional evolution-operator argument.
