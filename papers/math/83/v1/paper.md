## 1. Problem and contribution

### 1.1 The question

In Witten's gauge-theoretic approach to Khovanov homology [1, 2], the generators of a Floer-type complex are Kapustin–Witten (KW) solutions with a Nahm pole on the boundary of $\mathbb R^4_+$ and a singularity of weight $\mathfrak r$ along a boundary knot. The differential counts Haydys–Witten (HW) trajectories. A homotopy type in the sense of Cohen–Jones–Segal needs a Fredholm theory for the linearized HW operator $D=\Xi(\partial_s+L_W)$, where $L_W$ is the gauge-fixed cross-section operator. The weight $\mathfrak r=1$ is the uncoloured (Khovanov) case. Weights $\mathfrak r\ge2$ are the coloured knots, and they also appear as bubbles when knots collide.

At $\mathfrak r=1$ this Fredholm input near the straight model knot of Mazzeo–Witten [4] was obtained in three steps.
- rStation math_16 [10] proved the exact cone form $\rho L_3\rho^{-1}=J_\rho(\partial_\rho+S/\rho)$ at the knot, with a self-adjoint link operator $\bar S$ on the open hemisphere $S^2_+$. It reduced essential self-adjointness of $L_W$, the knot Hardy constant $2$, Taubes' two-sided estimate and the energy isomorphism to hypothesis (K): $\bar S$ has no eigenvalue in $(-1,1)$.
- math_18 [11] proved $\operatorname{spec}\bar S\cap(-\lambda_4^+,\lambda_4^+)=\{\pm1\}$ with $\lambda_4^+=(\sqrt{17}-1)/2$. Three things went into the proof: a Weitzenböck identity whose Type I block is two copies of the hemisphere operator $M_S$ of math_8 [8], a rank-12 exceptional bundle $E_0$ carrying the identity $\mathcal J\varphi=-sJ_t\varphi$, and explicit meridian solutions.
- math_38 [12] deduced the isomorphism $L,D:\mathcal E_\delta\to L^2_\delta$ on the maximal window $\delta\in(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$, with explicit knot Hardy constants and Weyl sequences at the endpoints.

All three papers are explicitly restricted to $\mathfrak r=1$. math_18 says "we have not tried $\mathfrak r\ge2$". math_38 [12, §9] notes that for $k\ge2$ neither the spectral identification of math_18 nor the energy isomorphism is available. The exact solvability of $M_S$ used in math_18 fails for $\mathfrak r\ge2$ [8, Prop. 2]. rStation math_82 [17] gives the replacement: the ground state $\gamma_0(\mathfrak r)$ of $M_S$, with two-sided bounds and $\gamma_0=2+3/(\mathfrak r+1)+O(\mathfrak r^{-2})$.

### 1.2 Contribution

Put $n=\mathfrak r+1$ and
$$\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)=-\tfrac12+\sqrt{\gamma_0(\mathfrak r)+\tfrac14}.$$
For **every** weight $\mathfrak r\ge1$ ($G=SU(2)$ or $SO(3)$, $\theta=\pi/2$, the straight model knot) we prove the following.

1. **Theorem A (Weitzenböck identity, all $\mathfrak r$).** $S^2+S=-\Delta_{S^2,A}+W$. The potential $W$ is block diagonal for Type I $\oplus$ Type II, and its Type I block is exactly $N_S\otimes1_2$. So $S^2+S$ restricted to Type I is $M_S\oplus M_S$ at every weight.
2. **Theorem B (exceptional sector, all $\mathfrak r$).** The exceptional bundle $E_0=V_{II}\ominus J_\rho V_I$ has rank $12$ for every $\mathfrak r$, so its rank does not change. The $U(1)$ symmetry is generated by $\mathcal J_{\mathfrak r}=\partial_\theta+R_{\mathfrak r}$, where the gauge part is $-(\mathfrak r+1)\operatorname{ad}(\tfrac i2\sigma_3)$, and $\operatorname{spec}R_{\mathfrak r}=i\{0,\pm1,\pm\mathfrak r,\pm(\mathfrak r+1),\pm(\mathfrak r+2)\}$. On every $E_0$-valued solution of $S\varphi=s\varphi$ the identity $\mathcal J_{\mathfrak r}\varphi=-sJ_t\varphi$ holds verbatim. Hence $s=\pm m$ with $m\in\mathbb Z$.
3. **Theorem C (no kernel, all $\mathfrak r$).** $\ker\bar S=0$. The proof uses closed-form solutions of the meridian system that are valid for every $n$. In block 0 the solution is $(2c\mu\nu,\ \nu^2\sin\psi)$, and in block 2 it is $(\tfrac12c\nu^2\sin\psi,\ -\mu\nu)$. Here $c=\cos\psi$, and $\mu,\nu$ are the Higgs profiles of the model.
4. **Main Theorem (sharp gap, all $\mathfrak r$).**
$$\operatorname{spec}\bar S\cap\bigl(-\lambda_+(\mathfrak r),\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)\bigr)=\{-1,+1\},\qquad \pm\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)\in\operatorname{spec}\bar S .$$
Moreover $1<\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)<2$, $\lambda_+$ decreases in $\mathfrak r$, and $\lambda_+=1+\tfrac1n+O(n^{-2})$. Both eigenspaces $\ker(\bar S\mp1)$ and $\ker(\bar S\pm\lambda_+)$ have real dimension $\ge2$. **So (K) holds at every weight**, with $\sigma_0=\operatorname{dist}(0,\operatorname{spec}\bar S)=1$. The spectral gap separating $\pm1$ from the rest of the spectrum is exactly $\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)-1=\tfrac1{\mathfrak r+1}+O(\mathfrak r^{-2})$, and it closes as $\mathfrak r\to\infty$.
5. **Corollaries (math_16 and math_38 at weight $\mathfrak r$).** At every weight:
   - $L_W$ is essentially self-adjoint on $C^\infty_c$ and $D(\bar L_W)=H^1_0$;
   - the knot Hardy constant is $2$, and it is optimal;
   - Taubes' two-sided estimate holds;
   - $L_W:\mathcal E\to L^2$ and $D:\dot H^1_0\to L^2$ are isomorphisms (on $S^1_t$ this is Taubes' theorem [5, Thm. II.1], see §1.3);
   - $L,D:\mathcal E_\delta\to L^2_\delta$ are isomorphisms for every $\delta\in(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$, and they are not left semi-Fredholm at $\delta=-\tfrac32$ or $\delta=\tfrac12$, where the knot translations give Weyl sequences. So $(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$ is the largest open interval containing $0$ on which they are isomorphisms, and it does not depend on $\mathfrak r$. We do not exclude other isomorphism intervals beyond its endpoints. The knot Hardy constants obey the bounds of math_38;
   - on $\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$, Witten's weight-$\mathfrak r$ solution is an isolated zero of the Coulomb-gauge-fixed KW map in the energy space, near which that map is a local diffeomorphism.

   On the two bands adjacent to the window, $(\tfrac12,\lambda_+-\tfrac12)$ and $(-\lambda_+-\tfrac12,-\tfrac32)$, the translation-invariant operator stays injective with closed range, and these bands have width $\lambda_+-1\approx1/(\mathfrak r+1)$. We do not determine the other gaps of the translation-invariant problem further out.

At $\mathfrak r=1$ everything reduces to math_18 and math_38. This is used as a control in `verify.py`: the block-2 solution is twice math_18's $p$, and $\lambda_+(1)=\lambda_4^+$. One statement is new even at $\mathfrak r=1$: the endpoints $\pm\lambda_4^+$ are attained. math_18 observed this only numerically [11, §7].

### 1.3 What is new, what is imported, what is conditional

*New (proved here).* Theorems A, B, C, the Main Theorem, and Corollaries 1–3 for $\mathfrak r\ge2$, except the energy isomorphism on $S^1_t$ at $\delta=0$, which is Taubes' [5, Thm. II.1] (see below). The method also yields:
- the uniform-in-$\mathfrak r$ ODE structure of the model on a meridian: $\mu'=-\nu^2$, $\nu'=\nu(c-\mu)/\sin^2\psi$, $A_\theta=(c\mu-n)\tfrac i2\sigma_3$ (Lemma 2.1);
- the attainment of $\pm\lambda_+$ for every $\mathfrak r$.

The functional-analytic steps are short. The exact identities are proved by symbolic computation in which the weight $\mathfrak r$ is a **symbol** (§5).

*Imported.*
- From math_16 [10]: the cone form, the domain theory of $\bar S$, and the reductions of Theorems 2–4 and Corollary 7.2 there. We check that their only model-specific inputs hold at weight $\mathfrak r$ (Proposition 2.3).
- From math_38 [12]: the proof of Theorem 1 there, which uses only its listed facts (F1)–(F8).
- From Mazzeo–Witten [4]: the model, and $\gamma_0>2$.
- From [17]: $\gamma_0(\mathfrak r)\le2+3n^2/(n-1)^3$, the identification and simplicity of the ground state, monotonicity, and the asymptotics.
- From math_8 [8]: $\gamma_0(1)=4$.

*Which statements depend on [17].* (K), $\sigma_0=1$, and Corollaries 1–2 use only $\gamma_0>2$ [4]. The window $(-\lambda_+,\lambda_+)$ with **both endpoints** uses $\gamma_0<6$, which comes from [17] for $\mathfrak r\ge2$ and from [8] for $\mathfrak r=1$. Corollary 3 and the numerical size of $\lambda_+$ use [17]. We mark the dependence wherever it enters.

*Relation to Taubes [5].* Taubes [5, Part II, Thm. II.1] states that the linearized operator $\mathcal D$ at any of Witten's model solutions is a bounded Fredholm map from the energy completion $\mathbb H$ to $\mathbb L=L^2$ with trivial kernel and cokernel. This is on $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^2\times S^1$, that is, on our periodic geometry $S^1_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$, and for every model index, so for every knot weight. **So the energy isomorphism of Corollary 1(3)–(4) on $S^1_t$, at $\delta=0$, is Taubes' theorem at every weight**, and we claim no novelty for it. Its input is [5, Lemma II.5.1]: Taubes' first-order symmetric hemisphere operator $\Omega$ has no eigenvector in his space $\mathbb H_S$ with eigenvalue in $[0,\tfrac32]$, proved by a case analysis whose exclusions hold for every positive model index. This is an all-weight exclusion of an interval of the link spectrum, and it overlaps in spirit with (K). math_18 [11, §8] records an unresolved discrepancy between the endpoint convention of [5, Lemma II.5.1] and $\operatorname{spec}\bar S$, and we have not established an exact dictionary between $\Omega$ on $\mathbb H_S$ and $\bar S$ on $D(\bar S)$. What we add relative to [5] is: (K) in the self-adjoint realization $\bar S$ that math_16 and math_38 use, the attained values $\pm1$ and $\pm\lambda_+$, the sharp interval $(-\lambda_+,\lambda_+)$ and its shrinking as $\mathfrak r\to\infty$, the line $\mathbb R_t$ in addition to $S^1_t$, and the weighted statements of Corollaries 2–3. We do not use [5] in any proof.

*Not claimed.* We do not claim exact multiplicities: numerically $\dim\ker(\bar S\mp1)=2$, but this is not proved. We do not claim the full spectrum, curved or compact knots, $\theta\ne\pi/2$, or non-constant trajectories beyond small perturbations. We make no claim about compactness, gluing, orientations or Witten's conjecture.

## 2. Setting

We use the conventions of math_14/16/18 [9, 10, 11].
- Fields are $u:W\to\mathbb R^8\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2)=\mathbb R^{24}$, with real slots $(a_s,a_t,a_2,a_3,a_y,b_1,b_2,b_3)$.
- $L_W=\sum_{m\in\{t,2,3,y\}}J_m\partial_m+Q$, with constant Clifford matrices $J_m$. Its $t$-independent part is $L_3=L_W-J_t\partial_t$.
- Coordinates: $y=\rho\cos\psi$ and $x_2+ix_3=\rho\sin\psi\,e^{i\theta}$. We write $c=\cos\psi$ and $s_\psi=\sin\psi$.
- The Type I slots are $V_I=\operatorname{span}(a_s,a_t)$, and $P_I$ is the projection onto $V_I$.

**The weight-$\mathfrak r$ model** [4, (2.8)–(2.11)], in the smooth gauge of [8, §2.1]. Put $T=\tfrac i2\sigma_3$, $X=E_{12}$, $P=(1+c)^n$ and $Q=(1-c)^n$. Then
$$A=a(c)\,d\theta\,T,\quad \phi_1=\frac{\mu(c)}\rho T,\quad \phi_2-i\phi_3=\frac{\nu(c)}\rho e^{i\mathfrak r\theta}X,\qquad \mu=n\frac{P+Q}{P-Q},\quad \nu=\frac{2n\,s_\psi^{\mathfrak r}}{P-Q}.$$

**Lemma 2.1 (uniform structure; all $\mathfrak r$).**
1. $a=c\mu-n$.
2. $\dfrac{d\mu}{dc}=-\nu^2$ and $\dfrac{d\nu}{dc}=\dfrac{\nu(c-\mu)}{1-c^2}$, with first integral $\mu^2-\nu^2(1-c^2)=n^2$.
3. $\mu(1)=n$, $a(1)=0$, and $\nu\sim2n2^{-n}s_\psi^{\mathfrak r}$ at the pole.
4. $c\mu$ and $c\nu$ are even real-analytic functions of $c$ near $c=0$, with value $1$ at $c=0$.

*Proof.*
1. $(1+c)^{\mathfrak r}-(1-c)^{\mathfrak r}=[(P-Q)-c(P+Q)]/s_\psi^2$. Insert this into math_8's formula $a=-ns_\psi^2[(1+c)^{\mathfrak r}-(1-c)^{\mathfrak r}]/(P-Q)$ [8, §2.1].
2. Logarithmic differentiation gives $\nu'/\nu=-\mathfrak rc/s_\psi^2-n[(1+c)^{\mathfrak r}+(1-c)^{\mathfrak r}]/(P-Q)$, and $(1+c)^{\mathfrak r}+(1-c)^{\mathfrak r}=[(P+Q)-c(P-Q)]/s_\psi^2$. This yields the formula for $\nu'$. The first integral is [8, Thm. 1(ii)]. Differentiating it gives $\mu'=-\nu^2$.
3. and 4. Direct.

`verify.py` Part A checks items 1–3 exactly for $\mathfrak r\le8$, and to 50 digits for the non-integer value $n=3.7$. $\square$

So, up to the phase $e^{i\mathfrak r\theta}$, the weight enters the fields on a meridian only through the integration constant $n$ of a weight-independent ODE. This is what makes uniform proofs possible. In `verify.py`, $\mu,\nu$ are SymPy functions whose derivatives are *defined* by item 2, and $\mathfrak r$ is a symbol. Every identity proved this way holds for all weights at once. Part B checks that this background solves the KW equations, and a control in which $\mu'$ is replaced by $-2\nu^2$ fails.

**Lemma 2.2 ($U(1)$ symmetry; all $\mathfrak r$).** Let
$$R_{\mathfrak r}=-\bigl(\mathrm{rot}_{a_2a_3}+\mathrm{rot}_{b_2b_3}\bigr)\otimes1-(\mathfrak r+1)\,1_8\otimes\operatorname{ad}T$$
and $\xi=x_2\partial_3-x_3\partial_2$. Then:
- $\xi Q+[R_{\mathfrak r},Q]=0$ at every point;
- $R_{\mathfrak r}$ is antisymmetric, $[R_{\mathfrak r},J_2]=-J_3$, $[R_{\mathfrak r},J_3]=J_2$, and $R_{\mathfrak r}$ commutes with $J_y$, $J_t$ and $P_I$;
- $\operatorname{spec}R_{\mathfrak r}=i\{0,\pm1,\pm\mathfrak r,\pm(\mathfrak r+1),\pm(\mathfrak r+2)\}$.

Hence $\mathcal J_{\mathfrak r}=\xi+R_{\mathfrak r}$ commutes with $L_3$, $J_\rho$, $J_t$ and $S$. It generates a $2\pi$-periodic unitary group commuting with $\bar S$, whose weights are integers. With gauge coefficient $\mathfrak r$ in place of $\mathfrak r+1$ the symmetry fails (control). The check is symbolic in $\mathfrak r$ at a general point $(x_2,x_3,y)$ (Part C). At $\mathfrak r=1$ the coefficient is $2$, as in [11, Lemma 4.1]. $\square$

Because of Lemma 2.2, an identity between $U(1)$-equivariant matrix functions that are homogeneous in $\rho$ holds everywhere once it holds on the meridian $\theta=0$, $\rho=1$. We use this repeatedly.

**Proposition 2.3 (math_16's model inputs at weight $\mathfrak r$).** At every weight:
1. $Q$ is symmetric and anticommutes with $J_t$ and $J_\rho$, and $A$ has no radial component. So the cone form $\rho L_3\rho^{-1}=J_\rho(\partial_\rho+S/\rho)$ holds, with $J_\rho S=-SJ_\rho$ [10, Prop. 5.1].
2. At face points with $|z|>0$, $y\phi$ tends to a Nahm triple $\operatorname{Ad}(g)\mathfrak t$, where $g$ is the phase rotation $e^{i\mathfrak r\theta}$ on $X$. This $g$ is single valued in $SO(3)$, and smooth. The remainders $\phi-\phi^0/y$ and $A$ are bounded there. This is [10, Prop. 5.2].
3. $c_Q=\sup y|Q|<\infty$.

Consequently math_16's Lemma 5.3 (localization at the Nahm face) and Proposition 5.4(1)–(3) hold at weight $\mathfrak r$:
- $S$ is essentially self-adjoint on $C^\infty_c(S^2_+)$;
- $D(\bar S)\subset H^1(S^2_+)$, and $\varphi/\cos\psi\in L^2$ for $\varphi\in D(\bar S)$ (the weight is the distance to the equator, from $y=\rho\cos\psi$ in Lemma 5.3);
- $\bar S$ has compact resolvent;
- $J_\rho\bar SJ_\rho^{-1}=-\bar S$.

*Proof.* (1) is Part B (meridian, symbolic $\mathfrak r$) together with Lemma 2.2. (2) follows from Lemma 2.1(4): $\phi_1-T/y=(\mu-1/c)\rho^{-1}T=O(c/\rho)$ and $\nu-1/c=O(c)$. The face triple $(T,\tfrac12(X-X^\dagger),\tfrac i2(X+X^\dagger))$ satisfies the Nahm relations (Part B). (3): $y|\phi_1|=c\mu$ and $y|\nu|/\rho=c\nu$ are bounded, and $y|A|=c|a|/s_\psi$ is bounded because $a=O(s_\psi^2)$ at the pole (Lemma 2.1(3)). The proofs of [10, Lemma 5.3, Prop. 5.4(1)–(3)] use only these facts and Theorem 1 of [10] (the pure Nahm pole), which does not involve $\mathfrak r$. $\square$

**The hemisphere operator.** Let $M_S=-\Delta_{S^2,A}+N_S$ with $N_S=-\rho^2\sum_j\operatorname{ad}(\phi_j)^2$ [4, (4.13)–(4.15)], [8]. We write $M_F$ for its Friedrichs extension from $C^\infty_c(S^2_+)$ and $\gamma_0(\mathfrak r)=\min\operatorname{spec}M_F$. In each Fourier sector the endpoint $c=0$ is limit point, since $V\ge2/c^2-C$, and the pole is an interior point. So $M_F$ is the realization used in [4, Prop. 4.1] and in [17], whose arguments are min–max on this form domain. We use the following facts:
- $\gamma_0>2$ [4, §4.1];
- $\gamma_0(1)=4$ [8];
- for $\mathfrak r\ge2$, $\gamma_0\le U_n:=2+3n^2/(n-1)^3\le U_3=43/8$, $\gamma_0$ is strictly decreasing, its eigenfunction is Cartan and $\theta$-independent, and $\gamma_0=2+3/n+O(n^{-2})$ [17, Thms. B, C, Cor. D].

## 3. Main results

**Theorem A (all $\mathfrak r\ge1$).** As differential operators on $C^\infty(S^2_+;\mathbb C^{24})$,
$$S^2+S=-\Delta_{S^2,A}+W,\qquad W=\rho^2\mathcal V|_{S^2_+},\qquad \mathcal V=\sum_mJ_m\partial_mQ+Q^2+\sum_m(\partial_m\mathfrak a_m+\mathfrak a_m^2),$$
with $\mathfrak a_m=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}A_m$. The potential $W$ is symmetric and commutes with $P_I$. Its $V_I$ block is $N_S\otimes1_2$, and on the meridian $N_S=\operatorname{diag}(\mu^2+\nu^2,\mu^2+\nu^2,2\nu^2)$. Hence $(S^2+S)|_{V_I}=M_S\oplus M_S$.

**Corollary A′ (Type I exclusion).** If $\bar S\varphi=s\varphi$, $\varphi\ne0$ and $|s|<\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)$, then $P_I\varphi=0=P_IJ_\rho\varphi$. So $\varphi$ takes values in the rank-12 bundle $E_0=V_{II}\ominus J_\rho V_I$.

**Theorem B (all $\mathfrak r\ge1$).** Let $\varphi$ be an $E_0$-valued smooth solution of $S\varphi=s\varphi$ on an open subset of the punctured hemisphere. Then
$$(\partial_\theta+R_{\mathfrak r})\varphi=-s\,J_t\varphi .$$
If $\varphi\in\ker(\bar S-s)$ is $E_0$-valued with $U(1)$-weight $m$, then $s^2=m^2$.

**Theorem C (all $\mathfrak r\ge1$).** $\ker\bar S=0$.

**Main Theorem (all $\mathfrak r\ge1$).**
1. $\operatorname{spec}\bar S\cap(-1,1)=\emptyset$, that is, (K) holds; $\pm1\in\operatorname{spec}\bar S$; and $\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker(\bar S\mp1)\ge2$. *(Uses $\gamma_0>2$ only.)*
2. $\operatorname{spec}\bar S\cap(-\lambda_+,\lambda_+)=\{-1,+1\}$, and $\pm\lambda_+\in\operatorname{spec}\bar S$ with $\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker(\bar S\pm\lambda_+)\ge2$. *(Uses in addition $\gamma_0<6$: [8] at $\mathfrak r=1$, [17] for $\mathfrak r\ge2$.)*
3. $1<\lambda_+(\mathfrak r)<2$. For $\mathfrak r\ge2$, $\lambda_+\le-\tfrac12+\sqrt{U_n+\tfrac14}$, and $\lambda_+$ is strictly decreasing with $\lambda_+=1+\tfrac1{\mathfrak r+1}+O(\mathfrak r^{-2})$. For $\mathfrak r\ge5$ the explicit lower bound $\lambda_+\ge-\tfrac12+\sqrt{L_n+\tfrac14}>1$ of [17] applies. *(Uses [17].)*

**Corollary 1 (math_16 at weight $\mathfrak r$; unconditional).** On $W=\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ or $S^1_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$, with the weight-$\mathfrak r$ model knot:
1. $L_W$ is essentially self-adjoint on $C^\infty_c(W^\circ)$, and $D(\bar L_W)=H^1_0(W^\circ)$.
2. $\|u/\rho_K\|\le2\|\bar L_Wu\|$, and the constant $2$ is optimal.
3. Taubes' two-sided estimate (Tb) holds, and $L_W:\mathcal E\to L^2$ is an isomorphism. (On $S^1_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ the isomorphism is Taubes' [5, Thm. II.1] at every weight; our proof is independent of it.)
4. $D=\Xi(\partial_s+L_W):\dot H^1_0(\mathbb R_s\times W^\circ)\to L^2$ is an isomorphism. The small-perturbation statement [10, Cor. 7.2] holds: $\Xi(\partial_s+L_W(s))$ is an isomorphism if $\sup y|L_W(s)-L_W|<1/C_N(\mathfrak r)$.

**Weighted spaces** (as in [12, §1]). $\rho$ is the distance to the knot and $y$ the distance to the Nahm face. For $\delta\in\mathbb R$, $L^2_\delta=\rho^\delta L^2$ with $\|f\|_{L^2_\delta}=\|\rho^{-\delta}f\|$, and $\mathcal E_\delta$ is the completion of $C^\infty_c(W^\circ;\mathbb R^{24})$ in the norm $\|u\|^2_{\mathcal E_\delta}=\|\rho^{-\delta}\nabla u\|^2+\|\rho^{-\delta}u/y\|^2$. $\mathcal E^{(5)}_\delta$ is the same on $\mathbb R_s\times W$. Since $y\le\rho$, $\mathcal E_\delta\subset L^2_{\delta+1}$. The knot Hardy constant $C_K(\delta)$ is the best constant in $\|\rho^{-\delta-1}u\|\le C_K(\delta)\|\rho^{-\delta}Pu\|$ on the domain of $P$.

**Corollary 2 (math_38 at weight $\mathfrak r$; unconditional).** Let $P\in\{L,D\}$, acting on $\mathcal E_\delta\to L^2_\delta$ (for $D$, $\mathcal E^{(5)}_\delta\to L^2_\delta$) as in [12], on $W=\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ or $S^1_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ for items 1–4.
1. $P$ is an isomorphism for every $\delta\in(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$. It is not left semi-Fredholm at $\delta=-\tfrac32$ or at $\delta=\tfrac12$. Hence $(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$ is the largest open interval containing $0$ on which $P$ is an isomorphism. *We do not exclude further isomorphism intervals outside it;* compare [12, Remark 5.3].
2. $m_\delta=\operatorname{dist}(-\delta-\tfrac12,\operatorname{spec}\bar S)=1-|\delta+\tfrac12|$ on $[-\tfrac32,\tfrac12]$. For $\delta\in(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$ the knot Hardy constants satisfy
$$m_\delta^{-1}\le C_K(\delta)=C_K(-1-\delta),$$
$$C_K(\delta)\le\frac{2}{1-2|\delta|}\quad\text{if }|\delta|<\tfrac12,\qquad C_K(\delta)\le\bigl(m_\delta(m_\delta-\sqrt d\,|\delta|)\bigr)^{-1/2}\quad\text{if }m_\delta>\sqrt d\,|\delta|,$$
with $d=1$ for $L$ and $d=2$ for $D$, exactly as in [12, Thm. 1(4)]. For $L$ the second bound applies on $(-\tfrac34,\tfrac14)$, for $D$ on $(-\tfrac3{2(1+\sqrt2)},\tfrac1{2(1+\sqrt2)})$. On the rest of the window, upper bounds come from the duality $C_K(\delta)=C_K(-1-\delta)$, since $\delta\mapsto-1-\delta$ maps $(-\tfrac32,-\tfrac12)$ onto $(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. That duality uses that $P$ and $P^\dagger$ are both onto on the window [12, Lemma 4.4]. Without the side conditions the displayed bounds are false: at $\delta=-1$ the first would give a negative number, and the second has a negative radicand.
3. The covariant knot translations $u_j$ and their reflections $\rho^2J_\rho u_j$ lie in $\ker L$ and vanish linearly at the face. Log-cut-offs of them are Weyl sequences at $\delta=-\tfrac32$ and at $\delta=\tfrac12$ [12, Prop. 3].
4. On translation-invariant fields the knot Hardy constant is exactly $m_\delta^{-1}$ [12, Prop. 2].
5. Local rigidity [12, Cor. 4], **on $W=\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ only**: Witten's weight-$\mathfrak r$ solution is an isolated zero of the Coulomb-gauge-fixed KW map in $\mathcal E_0$, and that map is a local diffeomorphism $\mathcal E_0\to L^2$ near it.

*Remark (why not $S^1_t$).* The proof of [12, Cor. 4] bounds the quadratic term by the four-dimensional Sobolev inequality $\|u\|_{L^4(\mathbb R^4)}\le C_S\|\nabla u\|$, which needs the non-periodic geometry. On $S^1_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ with the same homogeneous energy norm the quadratic map is not continuous at $0$. Take a fixed $f$ with compact support in the interior of $\mathbb R^3_+$ and nonzero quadratic KW term, and put $u_R(t,X)=R^{-3/4}f(X/R)$, independent of $t$. Then $\|u_R\|_{\mathcal E_0}\asymp R^{-1/4}\to0$, while $\|\mathfrak B(u_R,u_R)\|_{L^2}\asymp1$. (We thank the referee for this example.) The linear statements 1–4 are not affected.

**Corollary 3 (beyond the window; the gap shrinks).** For every $\delta\in\mathbb R$ with $m_\delta>0$, and $u\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R^3_+)$, we have $\|\rho^{-\delta-1}u\|\le m_\delta^{-1}\|\rho^{-\delta}L_3u\|$, and the constant is optimal [12, Lemma 3.1]. By the Main Theorem, on $[\tfrac12,\lambda_+-\tfrac12]$,
$$m_\delta=\min\bigl(\delta-\tfrac12,\ \lambda_+-\tfrac12-\delta\bigr)\le\tfrac12(\lambda_+-1)=\tfrac1{2(\mathfrak r+1)}+O(\mathfrak r^{-2}),$$
and symmetrically on $[-\lambda_+-\tfrac12,-\tfrac32]$. So $L_3:\mathcal E_\delta(\mathbb R^3_+)\to L^2_\delta$ is injective with closed range on the two bands $(\tfrac12,\lambda_+-\tfrac12)$ and $(-\lambda_+-\tfrac12,-\tfrac32)$ adjacent to the window. Their width is $\lambda_+-1\to0$, and the best knot Hardy constant there is at least $2(\mathfrak r+1)(1+o(1))$. The same estimate holds on every further band where $m_\delta>0$, that is, between consecutive points of $-\tfrac12-\operatorname{spec}\bar S$ (a discrete set, since $\bar S$ has compact resolvent). We do not locate those points beyond $\pm\lambda_+$, so we do not bound the widths of the further bands.

## 4. Proofs

### 4.1 Theorem A

*Formal identity.* By Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2, it suffices to check on the meridian, symbolically in $\mathfrak r$ (Part C), that:
- $J_mQ+QJ_m=-2\mathfrak a_m$, so that $L_3^2=-\sum\nabla_m^2+\mathcal V$;
- $\mathcal V$ is symmetric;
- $\mathcal V$ is block diagonal for $V_I\oplus V_{II}$;
- $\mathcal V|_{V_I}=(-\sum\operatorname{ad}\phi_j^2)\otimes1_2$.

The derivatives of $Q$ are taken *before* restricting to the meridian, with the chain rule supplied by Lemma 2.1(2). Two independent checks support this:
- for the true models of weights $\mathfrak r=2,3,4,5$ at off-meridian rational points (for example $(3,4,12)/13$ at $\mathfrak r=2$), the same block structure and the $U(1)$ symmetry with gauge coefficient $\mathfrak r+1$ hold exactly, while coefficient $\mathfrak r$ fails;
- dropping the connection terms destroys the Type I identity (control).

The passage from $L_3^2$ to $S^2+S$ is the computation of [11, Thm. A]. It uses only $J_\rho^2=-1$, $J_\rho S=-SJ_\rho$, that $A$ has no radial component, and the homogeneity of degree $-2$ of $\mathcal V$. All of these hold at weight $\mathfrak r$ by Proposition 2.3. $\square$

*Remark.* $(\mathrm H_{\mathfrak r})$ of math_9 [13] is the $L^\dagger L$-frame version of the Type I statement. It was checked there only at exact points for $\mathfrak r=2$. Theorem A gives the $L_W$-frame statement for every $\mathfrak r$.

### 4.2 Corollary A′

Let $\bar S\varphi=s\varphi$. By Proposition 2.3, $\varphi\in D(\bar S)\subset H^1(S^2_+)$, $\varphi/\cos\psi\in L^2$, and $\varphi$ is smooth in the open hemisphere, since $S$ is elliptic there. Let $f$ be a Type I slot component of $\varphi$. By Theorem A, $M_Sf=(s^2+s)f$ classically, so $f\in D(M_S^*)$.

We claim that $f$ also lies in the form domain $\mathcal Q_F$ of $M_F$. Near the equator $N_S\le C/\cos^2\psi$, so the form of $M_S$ is finite on $H^1\cap\{f/\cos\psi\in L^2\}$. The cut-offs $\chi(\cos\psi/\varepsilon)f$ converge to $f$ in the form norm, because $|\nabla\chi_\varepsilon|\,|f|\le C|f|/\cos\psi$ on the support of $\nabla\chi_\varepsilon$, which shrinks.

Since $D(M_F)=D(M_S^*)\cap\mathcal Q_F$ for the Friedrichs extension [14, Thm. X.23 and its proof], $f\in D(M_F)$. So $f=0$, or $s^2+s\in\operatorname{spec}M_F\subset[\gamma_0,\infty)$. The same applies to $J_\rho\varphi$, with $-s$ in place of $s$. If $|s|<\lambda_+$, then $s^2+|s|<\gamma_0$, so both Type I parts vanish. $E_0$ has rank $12$ because $J_\rho V_I\subset V_{II}$ (Part D). $\square$

### 4.3 Theorem B

The symbol map $\Sigma_x:E_0\oplus E_0\to\mathbb C^{24}$, $(w_1,w_2)\mapsto J_{e_1}w_1+J_{e_2}w_2$, does not depend on the background. Its squared determinant is $\sin^{24}\psi$ (Part D, as in [11, Lemma 4.2]). So $E_0$-valued solutions satisfy $\partial_{e_i}\varphi=\Gamma_i(x;s)\varphi$. Here $\Gamma_i$ is computed from $Q$ at the point alone, and on the meridian $Q$ depends only on $(c,\mu,\nu,\mathfrak r)$.

Part D verifies the identity of $24\times12$ matrices
$$|\xi|\,\Gamma_\theta(x;s)+R_{\mathfrak r}=-sJ_t\quad\text{on }E_0(x)$$
on the whole meridian. The check holds identically in $\tau=\tan(\psi/2)$, $\mu$, $\nu$, $\mathfrak r$ and $s$. No relation between $\mu$ and $\nu$ is needed, only $a=c\mu-n$. By Lemma 2.2 the identity then holds everywhere off the pole.

Two controls fail: shifting $a$ by $\tfrac13$ destroys the identity, and so does a non-solution background. At $\mathfrak r=2$ the true model gives $\det N(m,s)=(s-m)^6(s+m)^6$ at the point $(3/5,0,4/5)$. $J_t$ is constant, preserves $E_0$, squares to $-1$, and commutes with $R_{\mathfrak r}$. So $\mathcal J^2\varphi=-s^2\varphi$, and on weight vectors $m^2=s^2$. $\square$

### 4.4 Theorem C

Let $\varphi\in\ker\bar S$. By Corollary A′ (with $\gamma_0>2$) and Theorem B, after projecting onto weights, $\varphi$ is $E_0$-valued of weight $0$. It is then determined by its meridian restriction $v$, and
$$\|\varphi\|^2=2\pi\int_0^1|v|^2\,dc .$$
$v$ is bounded at the pole, since the pole is an interior point.

Part E shows the following, uniformly in $\mathfrak r$:
- the meridian system $v'=Bv$ splits into four blocks, of sizes $5,5,4,4$, each of $E_0$-rank $3$;
- in a rational frame of $E_0$, each block becomes $a'=A_ba$, with $A_b$ the direct sum of a scalar equation $a_3'=k_ba_3$, where $k_b\in\{(c+\mu)/s_\psi^2,\ (1+c\mu)/(cs_\psi^2)\}$, and a $2\times2$ system of trace $(1-c\mu+c^2)/(cs_\psi^2)$;
- explicit solutions $p_b$ of the $2\times2$ systems: $(2c\mu\nu,\pm\nu^2s_\psi)$ for blocks 0 and 1, and $(\tfrac12c\nu^2s_\psi,\mp\mu\nu)$ for blocks 2 and 3. They are verified with $\mu,\nu$ as abstract functions obeying Lemma 2.1(2).

At $c=1$, by Lemma 2.1(3):
- $(1-c)k_b\to(n+1)/2$, so the scalar solutions are $\asymp s_\psi^{-(n+1)}$ and unbounded. Hence $a_3=0$.
- $(1-c)\operatorname{tr}\to(2-n)/2$. So by Liouville's formula the Wronskian of two solutions of the $2\times2$ system is $\kappa\,(1-c)^{(n-2)/2}(1+o(1))\asymp\kappa\,s_\psi^{\mathfrak r-1}$.
- $|p_b|=O(s_\psi^{\mathfrak r})$.

If $q$ is a bounded solution, then $|\det[p_b,q]|\le C\,s_\psi^{\mathfrak r}=o(s_\psi^{\mathfrak r-1})$, so $\kappa=0$ and $q\parallel p_b$. Finally $p_b$ has a component $\asymp c^{-2}$ at the equator, because $c\mu,c\nu\to1$. So $p_b$ is not in $L^2(dc)$, and $v=0$.

The asymptotic statements are checked exactly for $\mathfrak r\le24$. For general $\mathfrak r$ they follow from Lemma 2.1(3),(4) by inspection of the closed forms. Controls: $p_b$ fails the system with $s=\tfrac12$. At $\mathfrak r=1$ the block-2 solution is twice math_18's $p$ [11, Lemma 5.1]. $\square$

### 4.5 Main Theorem

*(1)* $\lambda_+>1$ because $\gamma_0>2$. Let $s\in\operatorname{spec}\bar S$ with $|s|<1$. The spectrum is discrete, so $s$ is an eigenvalue. Corollary A′ and Theorem B give $s=\pm m$ with $m\in\mathbb Z$, so $s=0$, which Theorem C excludes.

For $\pm1$, Part G proves the following uniformly in $\mathfrak r$ on the meridian (and hence everywhere by $U(1)$-equivariance and homogeneity):
- the covariant translations $u_j=(\iota_{\partial_j}F_A,\nabla_j\phi)$, $j=2,3$, satisfy $L_Wu_j=0$, Coulomb row included;
- their angular parts $\varphi_j=\rho^2u_j|_{S^2_+}$ are $O(c)$ at the equator. Write $c\mu=1+c^2m$ and $c\nu=1+c^2k$ with $m,k$ bounded (Lemma 2.1(4)); then every component is $O(c)$;
- $u_2,u_3$ are linearly independent.

The $\varphi_j$ are smooth in the open hemisphere, since the background is smooth there in the smooth gauge, and they lie in $L^2$. So $\varphi_j\in D(S^*)=D(\bar S)$, and $\bar S\varphi_j=\varphi_j$ because $u_j$ is homogeneous of degree $-2$. By $J_\rho$-symmetry, $-1\in\operatorname{spec}\bar S$ as well.

*(2)* If $\gamma_0<6$, then $\lambda_+<2$, and the only integers in $(-\lambda_+,\lambda_+)$ are $0,\pm1$. The argument of (1) applies verbatim. For attainment, let $\psi_0\in D(M_F)$ be a ground state, $M_F\psi_0=\gamma_0\psi_0$, placed in the slot $a_s$. Put $u=\rho^{\lambda_+}\psi_0$ and $w=L_3u$. By Theorem A, $L_3^2u=\rho^{\lambda_+-2}(M_S-\lambda_+(\lambda_++1))\psi_0=0$, so $L_3w=0$.

Part F proves the pointwise identity
$$|L_3(fe_{a_s})|^2=\sum_m|\nabla_mf|^2+\sum_j|[\phi_j,f]|^2$$
for any background with $A_t=0$. Hence $|w|^2\ge\rho^{2\lambda_+-2}\langle N_S\psi_0,\psi_0\rangle>0$ wherever $\psi_0\ne0$ off the pole, since $N_S>0$ there ($\nu\ne0$). So $w\ne0$.

$w$ is homogeneous of degree $\lambda_+-1$. Its angular part $\varphi_w$ is built from $\nabla\psi_0$, $\psi_0$ and $[\phi_j,\psi_0]$, so it lies in $L^2(S^2_+)$, because $\psi_0\in\mathcal Q_F\subset H^1\cap\{\psi_0/\cos\psi\in L^2\}$. It is smooth inside and solves $S\varphi_w=-\lambda_+\varphi_w$. So $\varphi_w\in D(\bar S)$, and $-\lambda_+\in\operatorname{spec}\bar S$. Since $J_t$ commutes with $S$ (it anticommutes with $J_\rho$ and with the operator $\mathcal B$ of [10, Prop. 5.1]) and $J_t^2=-1$, the vectors $\varphi_w$ and $J_t\varphi_w$ are real-linearly independent eigenvectors. $J_\rho$-reflection gives $+\lambda_+$. So both eigenvalues have real multiplicity $\ge2$.

*(3)* $\gamma_0\in(2,6)$ is equivalent to $\lambda_+\in(1,2)$. The bounds and asymptotics are those of [17] transported by $\gamma\mapsto-\tfrac12+\sqrt{\gamma+\tfrac14}$, which is increasing, with $\sqrt{\tfrac94+\tfrac3n+O(n^{-2})}=\tfrac32+\tfrac1n+O(n^{-2})$ (Part H). $\square$

### 4.6 Corollaries 1–3

*Corollary 1.* The proofs of [10, Thms. 2–4, Cor. 7.2] use three model-specific inputs: the cone form, the face localization (Lemma 5.3), and $\sigma_0>\tfrac12$. Optimality of the constant $2$ uses an eigenvector at $s=-1$. Everything else is general functional analysis: Theorem H (Brüning–Seeley form), Kato–Rellich, dyadic rescaling and Plancherel in $t$. By Proposition 2.3 and the Main Theorem(1) ($\sigma_0=1$, with $-1$ attained), these inputs hold at weight $\mathfrak r$. $\square$

*Corollary 2.* By its own statement [12, Remark 5.2, §2], the proof of [12, Thm. 1, Props. 2–3, Cor. 4] uses only (F1)–(F8). At weight $\mathfrak r$:
- (F1) is Proposition 2.3(1), together with the background-independent splitting of [9];
- (F2) is Proposition 2.3;
- (F3) is replaced by the Main Theorem(1). The only property of the spectrum used is $\operatorname{spec}\bar S\cap[-1,1]=\{\pm1\}$, which gives $m_\delta=1-|\delta+\tfrac12|$ on $[-\tfrac32,\tfrac12]$ (Part H);
- (F4) is Lemma 5.3 with $c_Q<\infty$;
- (F5) is Corollary 1;
- (F6) is Part G;
- (F7) is Lemma 2.2, with $R_{\mathfrak r}$ in place of $R$;

The quadratic part of the KW map has constant coefficients for every background, and on $\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ the Sobolev embedding of (F8) bounds it on $\mathcal E_0$. So Corollary 4 of [12] carries over on that geometry; see the Remark after Corollary 2 for $S^1_t$. Item 1 gives the largest isomorphism interval containing $0$, by [12, Thm. 1(3)], and nothing about $\delta$ outside $[-\tfrac32,\tfrac12]$. $\square$

*Corollary 3.* [12, Lemma 3.1] holds for every $\delta$, with $m_\delta=\operatorname{dist}(-\delta-\tfrac12,\operatorname{spec}\bar S)$. Inserting the Main Theorem(2) gives the formula. Injectivity and closed range on $\mathcal E_\delta(\mathbb R^3_+)$ follow from [12, Lemma 4.1]. We make no claim about surjectivity there. $\square$

## 6. Inputs for a Haydys–Witten flow category at coloured knots

For the flow-category programme at a straight knot of weight $\mathfrak r$ (model geometry, $\theta=\pi/2$), the results above **supply**:
1. *Non-degenerate generators.* Witten's weight-$\mathfrak r$ solution is an isolated zero of the gauge-fixed KW map, with trivial kernel and cokernel in $\mathcal E_\delta$ for every $\delta\in(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$ (Corollary 2). Its determinant line is canonically trivial.
2. *A self-adjoint cross-section operator, but not a Fredholm one.* $L_W$ is essentially self-adjoint with $D(\bar L_W)=H^1_0$ (Corollary 1). This does **not** give spectral flow in the usual sense. By math_14 [9, Thm. C and Remark 5.1], at these model geometries and at every weight, $\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bar L_W)=\mathbb R$, so $0$ lies in the essential spectrum and $\bar L_W$ is not a self-adjoint Fredholm operator on $L^2$. The energy isomorphism of Corollary 1(3) does not change this, because it concerns a different pair of spaces. A spectral-flow description of the index of $D$ along trajectories needs a regularization or a different realization, and none is provided here.
3. *The energy isomorphism at constant trajectories.* $D:\dot H^1_0\to L^2$ and $D:\mathcal E^{(5)}_\delta\to L^2_\delta$ are isomorphisms for $\delta\in(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$, with index $0$ (Corollaries 1–2). The same holds for $s$-dependent backgrounds that are uniformly close in $\sup y|q|$ [10, Cor. 7.2]. This is the linear input for transversality near constant trajectories and for the "constant end" of gluing.
4. *The admissible weights and their limits.* The window $(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$ does not depend on $\mathfrak r$. It is the largest open isomorphism interval containing $0$: its endpoints are cut by the knot translations ($s=\pm1$). For the translation-invariant operator, the bands immediately beyond the window, which a gluing scheme with knot-adapted exponential weights might want, have width $\lambda_+-1=1/(\mathfrak r+1)+O(\mathfrak r^{-2})$ (Corollary 3), with Hardy constants $\gtrsim2(\mathfrak r+1)$. So any construction that needs a uniform gap just beyond $\pm1$ degenerates as the colour grows. We say nothing about bands further out, or about the full operator outside the window.

They **do not** supply, and these remain open:
- (i) Fredholmness and index formulas for $D$ along non-constant trajectories that are not small perturbations of constant ones;
- (ii) the exact multiplicity of $\pm1$ (numerically $2$), which governs the index jump across the endpoints of the window;
- (iii) compactness modulo breaking, including bubbling of coloured knots (splitting $\mathfrak r=\mathfrak r_1+\mathfrak r_2$; compare Taubes' interpolating solutions [15], [16]);
- (iv) gluing, coherent orientations, and the framed structure of the moduli spaces;
- (v) curved or compact knots, other $\theta$, and higher rank.

## 7. Scope and limitations

1. All results concern the straight model knot of Mazzeo–Witten at $\theta=\pi/2$, with $G=SU(2)$ or $SO(3)$ at the Lie-algebra level.
2. The identities of Theorems A–C are proved by symbolic computation, with $\mathfrak r$ a symbol. Their reduction to the meridian uses the $U(1)$-equivariance of Lemma 2.2, which is checked at a general point. Independent off-meridian checks on the true model are made at $\mathfrak r=2,3,4,5$ only. We have no conceptual proof of $\mathcal J_{\mathfrak r}=-sJ_t$, or of the closed-form meridian solutions.
3. Part 2 and the numerical size of $\lambda_+$ depend on rStation math_82 [17]. Part 1 and Corollaries 1–2 depend only on $\gamma_0>2$ [4].
4. Exact multiplicities and the rest of the spectrum (for example, whether $\pm m$ for $2\le m\le\mathfrak r$ are eigenvalues, as the Ritz values suggest) are not proved.
5. The proofs of Corollaries 1–2 re-use the published proofs of math_16 and math_38 after checking their inputs. We did not rewrite those proofs.
6. The floating-point parts (Table 1, Ritz values) are not interval certified and are not used in any proof.
7. Corollary 2(1) determines the largest isomorphism interval containing $0$, not the set of all admissible weights. Corollary 2(5) is stated on $\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R^3_+$ only. Essential self-adjointness of $L_W$ does not give a Fredholm cross-section operator: $\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bar L_W)=\mathbb R$ [9, Thm. C].

## Appendix A. Two representative reduced meridian systems (Theorem C)

On the meridian $\theta=0$, $\rho=1$, with $c=\cos\psi$, $s_\psi=\sqrt{1-c^2}$ and $d/dc=-(1/s_\psi)\,d/d\psi$, the weight-$0$, $s=0$ equation $\bar S\varphi=0$ is $v'=B(c)v$, and $\mu,\nu$ obey Lemma 2.1(2). The 24 field components split into four invariant blocks. We write $e_{k}\otimes\sigma_a$ for slot $k$ with generator $i\sigma_a$.

**Block 0.** Components $(a_2\sigma_1,\ a_3\sigma_2,\ a_y\sigma_1,\ b_1\sigma_2,\ b_2\sigma_3)$. A frame of $E_0$ (columns) is
$$F_0=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac12&0&\tfrac12\\-\tfrac12&0&\tfrac12\\-\tfrac{s_\psi}{2c}&0&-\tfrac{s_\psi}{2c}\\-\tfrac{s_\psi}{2c}&0&\tfrac{s_\psi}{2c}\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},$$
up to a permutation of its columns, which `verify.py` selects. In these coordinates, $v=F_0a$ turns the system into $a'=A_0a$ with
$$A_0=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{c\mu-1}{c(c^2-1)}&-\dfrac{2c\nu}{s_\psi}&0\\[2mm]-\dfrac{\nu}{c\,s_\psi}&-\dfrac{c}{c^2-1}&0\\[2mm]0&0&-\dfrac{c\mu+1}{c(c^2-1)}\end{pmatrix}.$$
The third coordinate decouples, with $a_3'=\frac{1+c\mu}{c\,s_\psi^2}a_3$, so $a_3\propto\exp\!\int\frac{1+c\mu}{cs_\psi^2}$, which is unbounded at the pole (order $\tan(\psi/2)^{-(\mathfrak r+2)}$). The $2\times2$ block has trace $(1-c\mu+c^2)/(c\,s_\psi^2)$ and the explicit solution $p_0=(2c\mu\nu,\ \nu^2s_\psi)$.

**Block 2.** Components $(a_2\sigma_3,\ a_y\sigma_3,\ b_1\sigma_1,\ b_2\sigma_2)$, frame
$$F_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\-\tfrac{s_\psi}{c}&0&0\\0&\tfrac12&\tfrac12\\0&-\tfrac12&\tfrac12\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_2=\begin{pmatrix}-\dfrac1{c(c^2-1)}&\dfrac{c\nu}{s_\psi}&0\\[2mm]\dfrac{2\nu}{c\,s_\psi}&-\dfrac{c-\mu}{c^2-1}&0\\[2mm]0&0&-\dfrac{c+\mu}{c^2-1}\end{pmatrix}.$$
The decoupled scalar is $a_3'=\frac{c+\mu}{s_\psi^2}a_3$, again unbounded at the pole, and the $2\times2$ block has the solution $p_2=(\tfrac12c\nu^2s_\psi,\ -\mu\nu)$. At $\mathfrak r=1$, $p_2$ is twice math_18's $p$ [11, Lemma 5.1].

Blocks 1 and 3 have the same shape as blocks 0 and 2, with the off-diagonal signs reversed (the solutions are $(2c\mu\nu,-\nu^2s_\psi)$ and $(\tfrac12c\nu^2s_\psi,\mu\nu)$). In every block the Gram matrix $F^{\sf T}F$ is bounded below near the pole, so bounded physical fields have bounded frame coordinates. By Lemma 2.1(3),(4), $p_b$ vanishes to order $\tan(\psi/2)^{\mathfrak r}$ at the pole and grows like $c^{-2}$ at the equator, so it is not in $L^2$. By Liouville's formula the Wronskian of the $2\times2$ system is of order $s_\psi^{\mathfrak r-1}$ at the pole, so every bounded solution is a multiple of $p_b$, which is not in $L^2(dc)$. This is the argument of §4.4. All of it is Part E of `verify.py`.
