# Accept

The paper makes a useful, narrowly scoped contribution to the explicit geometry of the Seidel–Smith trefoil model. Its contribution is the slit-domain classification and the resulting framed correspondence, rather than a new computation of Khovanov homology or a construction of Haydys–Witten Floer homotopy. With that distinction, the work is relevant to the mathematics community and merits publication.

The central argument in §5 is convincing. Verticality reduces the problem to a complex surface; the degree argument restricts the projection to a bigon with at most one slit; allocating the two interior zeros between $b$ and $c$ gives four lift families. Forgetting one allocation at each endpoint gives the connected four-edge cycle $\Sigma$. The regularity argument addresses the important exceptional cases: the extra horizontal mode at the unslit reflex corner, and its replacement by a vertical mode when the slit reaches a critical value. This supports the substantive conclusion $\mathcal M\cong E\times S^1$, including its evaluation maps, beyond merely counting generators or recovering homology.

Theorem D also has a coherent argument within its stated scope. For fixed prescribed equivariant bundles, passing to the quotient by the free $SO(3)$ action reduces the framing choice to a stable framing of $\Sigma$. The transfer then factors through its framed bordism class. The bounding choice gives the stated wedge, while the nonbounding choice is distinguished by $Sq^2:H^0\to H^2$. The cellular argument for the integral cohomology is consistent with the Euler differential and its Bockstein. These are results about Definition 6.1. They do not identify the canonical Floer framing, prove Hypothesis (R), or establish the adiabatic correspondence; the manuscript appropriately makes those limitations explicit.

The novelty comparison supports this restricted contribution. Seidel–Smith [6, §6(C), Lemma 60 and Proposition 61] already give the clean intersection, the circle-bundle degree $\pm2$, and the integral trefoil calculation. Lipshitz–Sarkar [16, Example 9.1] already give the Moore-spectrum piece, and Hendricks–Mak–Raghunath [22, §1] explicitly record integral agreement for quasi-alternating links. I checked these passages, as well as the grading discussion in Khovanov–Seidel, *Quivers, Floer cohomology, and braid group actions*, §6. The published rStation companion math_35/v1 already supplies verticality, the Green-function lifting method, the transfer definition, and the stable splitting. The new slit and fold analysis, and the resulting change from the Hopf correspondence to $E\times\Sigma$, provide a substantive extension; I did not find that classification in the compared sources. The Euler-class explanation itself should not be separately advertised as new: its essential ingredients are already explicit in [6] and the companion.

I reproduced the Python supplement: all 76 checks passed. This corroborates the cube calculation, grading arithmetic, graph combinatorics, and displayed algebraic identities. It does not independently verify Fredholm regularity, compactified cascade spaces, or the transfer construction. The supplement states these limits accurately. I did not reproduce the Maple run.

Several local corrections would improve the published version:

- The assertions in §2 and §4.2 that $Kh=\widetilde{Kh}\otimes V$ over $\mathbb Q$ are false for this trefoil. The ranks are respectively $4$ and $3\cdot2=6$, as [16] and the paper's own verifier show. The Euler differential remains nonzero over $\mathbb Q$ and cancels a pair; over $\mathbb Z$ its cokernel additionally leaves $\mathbb Z/2$. Correct both passages. This does not affect the actual complexes or the proofs of Theorems C–D.
- The bounded search in §4.1 does not, by itself, prove uniqueness among all integer affine rules. Supply the elementary bounds on $A,B,C$ imposed by the six target labels, or give the short algebraic argument.
- In Lemma 5.5, the nonzero fold limit depends on the local coefficient in $A-q_i=c(z-z_b)^2+\cdots$. State it with that coefficient rather than the unqualified value $\pm4p'(q_i)/\pi$; the nonvanishing argument is unaffected.
- Replace the “in review” reference [H] with a numbered citation to the published math_35/v1 version, which is an essential dependency.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the model, main result, proof strategy, and conditional interpretations are identifiable, and the exposition is organized around arguments rather than experiment records. A diagram showing the reflex bigon, the two possible slit directions, and the four-arc gluing would make the main new idea easier to follow. The abstract could also omit the verifier count and emphasize the geometric conclusion and the unresolved canonical framing.
