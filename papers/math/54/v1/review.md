# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution to the mathematical problem it selects: understanding trajectory indices for the corrected-Nahm-pole family on $\mathrm{Sol}^3$. The central results are supported by analytic arguments, and the distinction between paths of configurations and actual HW solutions is clearly explained. Acceptance concerns the stated ansatz and operator realizations; this is not a construction of a grading or flow category for the full gauge-theoretic problem.

The strongest contribution is the combination of the weighted obstruction with the explicit truncation calculation. In Theorem A, the critical-line block supplies normalized, escaping approximate null vectors for both the conjugated operator and its adjoint. The residual estimate establishes failure of semi-Fredholmness under the stated hypotheses, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful numerical searches. The finite-dimensional-extension argument is also sound. For the truncated problem, the pole realization, transverse-end criterion and crossing-form identity provide the required connection to spectral flow. The chart lemma then supplies genuinely useful information: positivity of $\eta'$ keeps the entire object family in one affine chart, while the threshold has a different limiting Lagrangian. This supports both the vanishing for weight-adapted boundary conditions and the explicit threshold indices $-1,0,+1$.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the relevant preceding papers. I compared the supplied versions of math_45, math_47 and math_48, including the latter's Remark 6.3 and Open Question 6.4. They supply the family, Jacobi equations and weighted cross-section indices, but not the present chart-based trajectory-index calculation. Theorem D's counting identity is largely inherited bookkeeping; its significance comes from the new trajectory comparison. The extended-space implicit-function argument also supplies a nonlinear setting absent from math_48. The externally consulted Haydys paper [2], particularly Theorem 2.28, Proposition 2.30 and assumptions (P1)/(H2), treats nondegenerate asymptotics. He's Propositions 5.10, 5.13 and 5.19 [6] concern weighted cylindrical-end theory and its index jumps. These establish the general background, but do not contain the particular Sol threshold calculation. This focused comparison does not establish absolute priority for every auxiliary observation.

The proof strategy and scientific meaning are understandable independently of the supplement. The main text distinguishes imported results, new proofs and numerical illustrations, and its limitations are substantial and explicit. I reproduced `verify.py`: all 64 checks passed, ending in `FALLOS = 0`. This corroborates the finite-dimensional identities, crossing signs and reported numerical illustrations. It does not independently certify the analytic Fredholm arguments or asymptotic limits. I did not execute the Maple companion.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without changing its central contribution:

- **Keep the obstruction's scope precise.** Theorem A concerns the displayed realization $e^{-\varphi}H^1_0\to e^{-\varphi}L^2$ and its specified finite-dimensional extensions. Statements such as “truncation is forced” and “every weight of … b-type” should retain those restrictions. They do not exclude homogeneous energy domains, different derivative-weight conventions, or all other realizations. Related rStation work already distinguishes these possibilities: math_14 v1, Theorem C, gives an exponential-weight obstruction at Witten's models, while math_19 v1 develops an energy-space Fredholm theory under different hypotheses. Cite and distinguish those results; neither supplies the Sol calculation here.
- **Correct the claimed global frame in Lemma 6.1.** The supplement's frame $[\mathrm{even}(a,c,v),\mathrm{odd}(a,c,0),\mathrm{odd}(v,0,-2c)]$ loses rank, for example at $(a,c,v)=(1,0,1)$. Its determinant therefore cannot identify crossings globally. The valid chart determinant $8p$ already establishes the transversality needed for the main results. Use local frames or a direct intersection argument for the additional statement on $v=0$.
- **Make two interval choices consistent.** In Lemma 5.4, $[Y-1,Y-1/2]$ need not lie inside $(0,Y)$; choose an interior interval depending on $Y$. In the slow-reparametrization arguments for C and F, allow paths constant outside an arbitrary compact interval and explain invariance under rescaling, rather than claiming that a transition on $[-T,T]$ belongs to the class fixed outside $[-1,1]$.
- **Specify the meaning of “Morse–Bott” in Theorem E.** The proof establishes a smooth one-dimensional zero set of a smooth Fredholm section, with surjective derivative and tangent kernel. That is a useful precise result. A claim about a Morse–Bott critical manifold of an action additionally requires a differentiable action and treatment of its boundary variation when the full asymptotic parameter $K$ varies. Avoid implying that this variational assertion follows automatically from the section formulation.
- **Reconcile the Maple provenance.** Section 10 and the README say the Maple file was executed, whereas its header says it was not executed because Maple was unavailable. Correct the stale statement or provide the corresponding retained output. The reproduced Python checks already support the algebra used in the main argument.
