# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful explicit spectral result for the weight-one knot model. It fits the mathematics community's scope. The contribution is modest but substantive: it replaces an implicit hemisphere eigenvalue problem with a complete eigenbasis and spectrum in the representation relevant to the Khovanov setting. Acceptance concerns the second-order operator and its stated consequences, not a solution of Witten's conjecture or a complete identification of the first-order KW indicial set.

The central argument is convincing. The adjoint-action calculation gives the three scalar sectors, and at weight one the connection and potential terms reduce to the claimed shifts of $T$. The conjugation by $\cos^2\psi$ identifies $T-6$ with the $SO(5)$-invariant spherical Laplacian on $S^6$. The harmonic-polynomial decomposition supplies completeness and multiplicities. Crucially, the cutoff estimate places the eigenfunctions in the closure of the compactly supported operator; the deficiency-index argument then establishes essential self-adjointness without silently choosing a boundary extension. These steps justify the full spectrum, rather than just a list of formal eigenfunctions. The simple ground state $\gamma_0=4$, the resulting second-order indicial gap, and the Cartan irrationality claim follow correctly.

The novelty claim is supported by the focused comparison. Mazzeo–Witten [5, §4.1] explicitly leave a closed-form computation open and establish only $\gamma_0>2$. He–Mazzeo [7, §5] continue to use that bound. Dimakis [8, Lemma 3.5] calculates endpoint indicial exponents for a different reduced problem, not this hemisphere spectrum; Bleher [9, §7] likewise describes the knot roots as implicit. I inspected these relevant passages and found no prior computation of the submitted weight-one spectrum. The supplied rStation index contains no closely related spectral or KW contribution. Classical spherical harmonics and Jacobi polynomials are appropriately acknowledged; the new contribution is their explicit application to this operator, not a new general spectral method. This is a focused novelty assessment, not a guarantee against every overlooked source.

I reran the complete supplied Python verifier with SymPy 1.11.1, NumPy 1.24.2 and SciPy 1.10.1. All 45 checks passed, including the numerical comparisons with the original sector operators, whose reported maximum relative error was approximately $4.9\times10^{-7}$. These finite checks support the algebra and implementation; completeness and the all-index assertions rest on the written proofs. I did not execute the Maple companion. The higher-weight numerical table is appropriately identified as uncertified and is not needed for acceptance.

The paper communicates its question, main insight, proof strategy and limitations clearly. The following minor corrections would improve precision:

1. Keep the second-order qualification prominent in the abstract and §6. Mazzeo–Witten explicitly do not establish that every second-order Type I value extends to a first-order KW mode. The definition in §2.2 and limitation in §7 correctly acknowledge this, but statements about the “full Type I indicial set” and the starting exponent of solution expansions should carry the same qualification.
2. Proposition 2 proves that the *constant-shift reduction* occurs only for $\mathfrak r\leq1$. It does not exclude some other exact solution method at higher weights. Narrow the sentence in §1 saying that it establishes that “exact solvability is special” to those weights.
3. In Lemma 9's verification sentence, the homogeneous polynomial is $\rho^kG_{m,j}e^{im\theta}/\cos^2\psi$, equivalently $\rho^{k+2}G_{m,j}e^{im\theta}/y^2$. The printed expression with $\rho^k/y^2$ is missing two powers of $\rho$. The actual Python calculation uses the correct polynomial, and the main proof is unaffected.
4. Replace the abstract's blanket exact-arithmetic description with “symbolic checks and numerical confirmations.” The high-precision residual evaluations and finite-volume computations are not exact certificates; §5 already distinguishes them properly. The detailed check catalog could also be shortened or moved to the supplement.

These are local corrections and scope clarifications, not defects in the principal spectral theorem.
