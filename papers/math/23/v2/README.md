# Perfect Partitions of Derangements and Even Derangements for $n \le 9$

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 2 · 2026-11-11T02:13:52.715114Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/23/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For an $n \times n$ $(0,1)$-matrix $A$ with row and column sum $k$, a perfect partition of the set $\mathcal{P}_A$ of permutation matrices dominated by $A$ is a partition of $\mathcal{P}_A$ into $k$-element subsets each summing to $A$. For the derangement matrix $L_n = J_n - I_n$ (the regular bipartite graph $L_{n,1} = K_{n,n} - I_{n,n}$), Brualdi, Chiang, and Li (2005) settled $n \le 5$ and asked in Problems 3.2 and 3.3 whether $\mathcal{P}_{L_n}$, the even derangements $\mathcal{P}_{L_n} \cap \mathcal{A}_n$, or fixed cycle-type classes admit perfect partitions for $n \ge 6$. Li, Soosiah, and Yu (2012) resolved $\mathcal{P}_{L_6}$, and Ferreri, Parameswaran, and Yu (2026) resolved $\mathcal{P}_{L_7}$ and prime $p$-cycles while leaving $n \ge 8$ open. Using a subgroup-orbit criterion (Lemma 3.1) that reduces $H$-conjugation-invariant perfect partitions to exact cover on $H$-orbits, we resolve these questions through $n = 9$: we construct $\operatorname{PGL}(2,7)$- and $\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(2,8)$-invariant perfect partitions of $L_{8,1}$ and $L_{9,1}$ and an $\operatorname{AGL}(1,7)$-invariant partition of $L_{7,1}$ (Theorem 1.1); we construct perfect partitions of $\mathcal{P}_{L_n} \cap \mathcal{A}_n$ for $6 \le n \le 9$ (Theorem 1.2); we classify pure cycle-type perfect partitions at $n = 6, 7$, constructing partitions for $(4,2)$ at $n = 6$ and $(5,2), (4,3)$ at $n = 7$ and proving impossibility for $(6), (3,3), (2,2,2)$ at $n = 6$ and $(3,2,2)$ at $n = 7$ (Theorem 1.3); and we prove that $\mathcal{P}_{L_6} \setminus \mathcal{A}_6$ has no perfect partition and $L_{6,1}$ has no $\operatorname{PGL}(2,5)$-invariant perfect partition (Proposition 1.4). All constructions and finite enumerations for $n \le 9$ are verified by standalone scripts; general orders $n \ge 10$ remain open.
