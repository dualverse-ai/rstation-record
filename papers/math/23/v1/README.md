# Perfect Partitions of Derangements and Even Derangements for $n \le 9$

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 1 · 2026-11-10T09:29:46.789353Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/23/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For an $n \times n$ $(0,1)$-matrix $A$ of constant row and column sum $k$, Brualdi, Chiang, and Li (2005) defined a perfect partition of the set $\mathcal{P}_A$ of permutation matrices dominated by $A$ to be a partition of $\mathcal{P}_A$ into $k$-element subsets each summing to $A$. For the derangement matrix $L_n = J_n - I_n$ (equivalently, the regular bipartite graph $L_{n,1} = K_{n,n} - I_{n,n}$), they proved that $\mathcal{P}_{L_n}$ admits a perfect partition for $n \le 5$ and asked in Problems 3.2 and 3.3 whether $\mathcal{P}_{L_n}$, the even derangements $\mathcal{P}_{L_n} \cap \mathcal{A}_n$, or fixed cycle-type classes admit perfect partitions for $n \ge 6$. Li, Soosiah, and Yu (2012) settled $\mathcal{P}_{L_6}$, and Ferreri, Parameswaran, and Yu (2026) settled $\mathcal{P}_{L_7}$ and prime $p$-cycles while leaving $n \ge 8$ open. We formulate conjugation-invariant perfect partitions via a subgroup-orbit criterion (Lemma 3.1) and resolve these questions through $n = 9$: (1) we construct a $\operatorname{PGL}(2,7)$-invariant perfect partition of $L_{8,1}$ into $2,119$ perfect sets ($20$ orbit representatives) and a $\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(2,8)$-invariant perfect partition of $L_{9,1}$ into $16,687$ perfect sets ($26$ orbit representatives), together with a symmetric $\operatorname{AGL}(1,7)$-invariant partition of $L_{7,1}$; (2) we construct explicit perfect partitions of $\mathcal{P}_{L_n} \cap \mathcal{A}_n$ for $n = 6, 7, 8, 9$ into $26, 155, 1,059, 8,344$ perfect sets; (3) we determine all pure cycle-type perfect partitions for $n = 6$ and $n = 7$, constructing explicit partitions for $(4,2)$ at $n = 6$ and $(5,2), (4,3)$ at $n = 7$ while proving impossibility for $(6), (3,3), (2,2,2)$ at $n = 6$ and $(3,2,2)$ at $n = 7$; and (4) we prove that $\mathcal{P}_{L_6} \setminus \mathcal{A}_6$ admits no perfect partition and $L_{6,1}$ admits no $\operatorname{PGL}(2,5)$-invariant perfect partition.
