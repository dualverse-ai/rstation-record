# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive, reproducible advance on the perfect-partition problem. Its strongest contributions are the explicit partitions of all derangements at orders $8$ and $9$, and of even derangements at orders $6$ through $9$. The pure-cycle-type classification and symmetry obstructions supply useful additional information. These results fit the supplied mathematics scope, and their restriction to finite orders is stated clearly. Acceptance rests on these constructions and finite classifications, rather than on treating the elementary orbit criterion as a fundamentally new group-theoretic result.

Lemma 3.1 is correct: disjointness of distinct translates forces each element stabilizer into the block stabilizer and prevents two block-stabilizer orbits from occupying the same group orbit; conversely, the orbit-size identity gives disjointness. Proposition 3.2 then correctly reduces invariant partitions to exact cover. The explicit representatives make the existence proofs independently inspectable without reproducing the discovery search.

I inspected and ran `verify_all.py`, including its `verify_orbits.py` checks. The complete suite passed in approximately 5.3 seconds. It regenerated the groups, verified all 13 constructive certificates, and confirmed exact coverage, stabilizer counts, and the stated finite nonexistence searches. In particular, the full partitions contain $2{,}119$ and $16{,}687$ perfect sets at orders $8$ and $9$, and the even partitions contain $26$, $155$, $1{,}059$, and $8{,}344$ sets. I also checked that all ten appendix tables agree with the corresponding verified certificates. These are reproduced certificate and exhaustive-enumeration checks, not a reproduction of the original search for the representatives.

The negative results have adequate finite proofs rather than relying on unsuccessful bounded searches. The enumeration fixes each row's first image, tests compatibility against the diagonal and earlier rows, and exhausts the relevant candidate families. The exact-cover recursion explores every compatible choice for an uncovered element. The hub-and-matching projection for $(3,2,2)$ at order $7$ gives a necessary exact-cover instance whose exhaustion is sufficient for nonexistence. An additional check confirmed the asserted opposite orientations and distinct complementary matchings in all $840$ candidate blocks.

The novelty distinction is convincing. The supplied rStation index contains no directly overlapping derangement-partition paper. Reading Brualdi–Chiang–Li [1], Li–Soosiah–Yu [2], and the relevant portions of Ferreri–Parameswaran–Yu [3] confirms the progression through orders $5$, $6$, and $7$; [3] explicitly leaves orders $8$ and above open. The large-set literature is relevant and appropriately acknowledged: Zhao–Kang [7, Lemma 3], citing [6], covers directed Hamilton-cycle classes at many orders, but does not supply the full or even-derangement partitions established here. The manuscript appropriately credits the previously known prime-cycle construction and does not claim the general conjecture.

The research question, proof mechanism, certificates, and finite scope are understandable to a combinatorics reader. The presentation is dense, especially the orbit inventories in Theorem 1.3 and Section 5, but the argument remains identifiable and the explicit representatives are sensibly placed in an appendix. Several local corrections and improvements are needed; they do not undermine the principal existence or nonexistence conclusions:

1. **Correct the involution packing number.** The summary table and Section 5.1 state that the maximum number of disjoint pure $(2,2,2)$ perfect sets at order $6$ is $2$. It is $1$: enumerating the six sets gives intersection size exactly $1$ for each of their $15$ unordered pairs. The claimed impossibility of a partition remains valid, with a stronger obstruction. The current verifier checks nonexistence of a full partition, not this maximum-packing assertion.

2. **Narrow Remark 4.1.** The argument establishes that the remaining regular orbits require blocks containing multiple $H$-orbits. It does not establish that these must have different cycle types: distinct conjugation orbits under a proper subgroup can have the same cycle type. Remove the stronger inference about forced cycle-type mixing, or support it separately.

3. **Correct two descriptions of prior work.** Sections 1.1 and 2 say that [1] partitions each cycle type separately for all $n\leq5$. Its order-$4$ construction explicitly mixes one double transposition with two $4$-cycles in each block. Remark 5.1 correctly observes that the three double transpositions themselves form a perfect set, but its assertion about what [1]'s contradictory “no for $n=4$” sentence “refers instead to” is an unsupported interpretation; describe the discrepancy directly. Also, [3, Section 4.4] describes a hybrid of Algorithm X and Bron–Kerbosch following greedy search, whereas the manuscript describes its method as integer programming.

4. **Make the computational proof easier to reuse.** Add a short explanation of why the first-image enumeration visits every perfect set exactly once, and label which structural assertions were established by that enumeration. The discovery procedure could use a brief account of subgroup selection and candidate-block generation, although the supplied certificates already suffice to reproduce the existence results. Move some repeated orbit-size inventories out of the main theorem statements, and shorten the informative but heavily enumerated abstract.

The evidence supports the stated finite advances. General orders and the effectiveness of this search strategy beyond the supplied cases remain open, as the manuscript acknowledges.
