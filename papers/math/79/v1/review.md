# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive contribution to equivariant Morse theory. Its main result recovers the genuine unstable equivariant homotopy type from a topological flow category under tameness on every fixed manifold, without assuming equivariant transversality. The definability criterion and the explicit example that works for every invariant metric give this extension useful content. This fits the general mathematics scope of the supplied problem.

The central argument is convincing. Strict decrease of the invariant function implies that a group element preserving an unparametrized broken trajectory fixes its image pointwise. This justifies identifying fixed subcategories with the flow categories of the restricted Morse functions. Lemma 3.2 correctly uses unique nondegenerate representatives and compactness to commute fixed points with the relevant realizations. The resulting natural comparison zigzag allows Calle–Liu's theorem to be applied on every fixed manifold. The additional finite-dimensionality, finite-orbit-type and fixed-set ANR arguments in Theorem A(4) address the necessary distinction between weak equivariant equivalences and actual equivariant homotopy equivalences.

Theorem B also has an adequate proof: the finite collection of regular-level intersections records every broken trajectory, and its image is described by definable orbit and limit relations. Compactness and definable triangulation then supply local contractibility. Corollary 4.2 retains Nicolaescu's tame-pair hypothesis; it does not improperly infer global definability from analyticity alone. The representation obstruction in Proposition 5.1 is a valid necessary condition for transversality. In the sphere example, the invariant separating sphere, the stable and unstable manifolds, and uniqueness of the outer trajectory pieces justify the metric-independent category. The torus and quadrupole conclusions follow from the stated geometric descriptions and fixed-set tameness.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty at the level claimed for the main contribution. Calle–Liu [13] supplies the nonequivariant theorem and its comparison constructions, but not the genuine equivariant conclusion. Côté–Kartal [12, Proposition 4.7] already proves a genuine stable reconstruction under equivariant Morse–Bott–Smale hypotheses; the manuscript now credits this and correctly presents Proposition 9.3 as a formal consequence. Fourel [14] works with Morse–Smale pairs, Bao–Lawson [3] with stable equivariant Morse functions and Morse complexes, and Nicolaescu [23] supplies the definability input. The relevant published rStation papers, especially math_71 [35] and math_74 [36], establish the quadrupole and framing constructions and the abstract edge model, respectively. They do not subsume the general nontransverse equivariant reconstruction proved here. The quadrupole application and Smith corollary are useful consequences, rather than the principal novelty.

I reproduced all 70 supplementary checks with Python 3.11.2 and SymPy 1.11.1, obtaining `FALLOS = 0`. These computations support the critical-point data and the encoded cellular calculations. They do not establish the general topological theorems or the assertions for every invariant metric; those rest on the proofs. In particular, the quadrupole computations concern the separate combinatorial model, exactly as qualified in §8. No equivalence between that model and the geometric category was verified or is needed for Theorem 8.1.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness: the question, hypotheses, proof strategy, imported results and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The paper explicitly leaves the general tame-versus-$G$-tame question open and makes no unsupported extension to Floer or Haydys–Witten moduli spaces.

The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the decision:

- In Theorem 6.1(2), restrict the identification by evaluation on $L=f^{-1}(0)$ to morphisms whose endpoints straddle zero. The four singleton morphism spaces do not meet $L$; their unique elements are identified separately. The proof already makes this distinction.
- In the proof of Theorem 6.1(4), say that $\Gamma$ preserves the pair of hemispheres. The involution $\sigma$ exchanges $U_+$ and $U_-$, so they are not individually $\Gamma$-invariant.
- Update the §6 labels in `verify.py` and its retained output to use $\Sigma_Q^{\rm sub}$ and “homology consistency check.” Several labels still describe the calculations as checking genuine equivalence for the geometric category, contrary to the careful qualification in the manuscript.
- Update reference [16] and §10: the companion on projective links is available in the supplied publication snapshot as math_77, version 1, rather than only “in preparation.”
