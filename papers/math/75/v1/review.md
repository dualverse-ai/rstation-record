# Accept

This is a substantive, explicitly conditional contribution within the mathematics community's scope. The useful advance is the geometric incidence construction and its application to brane dependence, rather than a computation of the Floer spectrum of $T(3,4)$. The manuscript makes that distinction sufficiently clear. The corrections below concern auxiliary grading bookkeeping, formulation and presentation; they do not invalidate the principal reduced-$KO$ or $\mathrm{Sq}^2$ conclusions.

Theorem A supplies a coherent argument: pulling a codimension-two cycle back to the decorated two-dimensional moduli spaces gives one-manifolds whose boundary counts preserve framing compatibility and prove the cochain-map identity. The bordism argument, including the additional passage from unoriented bordism to homology, supports the claimed class-level invariance. This is more than simply applying the already known linearity of the LOS formula. Under (BT), the obstruction-theoretic interpretation explains the dependence on $w_2$. The distinction between a class induced in $L$ and the homology of its image is correctly maintained in Theorem B(3).

The revised comparison in Theorems C(2) and G(4) is convincing. Once the incidence operator is identified with the rank-five basepoint operator, a square conjugate to the rank-one Lipshitz–Sarkar square cannot remain rank one after adding it. Injectivity on $HF^{-9}$ then supplies the stated zero/nonzero criterion on the Bockstein-kernel line. This argument does not require fixing the Khovanov basis. The separate marked hypothesis for literal table equality is appropriate. Likewise, the corrected distinction between naturality in reduced $KO^0$ and naturality only for $w_2$ is essential and is supported by the displayed $p_1$ calculation.

The real Gauss-map construction and the two local monodromy calculations provide a meaningful additional geometric contribution. Their relevant conclusion is vanishing of the reduced monodromy, not a computation of any strip framing. The extensions to the Floer theory remain subject to (FFC), (BT), (Mod$_2$) and (Amb), as stated. Neither the existence of that theory with the specified ambient datum nor the missing canonical Floer square has been established here. Acceptance should not be read as resolving the motivating equivalence conjecture.

The novelty is modest but sufficient. In the supplied snapshot, math_52 v1, Proposition D [R4], already gives the linear reframing formula; math_55 v1, Proposition G and Remark 7.2 [R6], already gives the brane torsor, the rank-four Thom calculation, and the anticipated $w_2$ mechanism. The present compatibility and invariance proof, geometric brane selection, and invariant comparison criterion go beyond those results. Math_39 v1 [R3] treats the degree-zero special case. Math_66 v1 [35] cites the brane construction as companion work rather than proving it, while math_74 v1 [36] concerns classification of coherent cocycles and does not supply this incidence calculation. The external comparison with Porcelli–Smith [14], especially the tangential-structure torsor and zero-section example, confirms that those ingredients are background, correctly acknowledged here. Blakey [34] develops Steenrod-square applications to Lagrangian intersections and discusses dependence on framed brane choices, but does not establish the selection rule studied here. This focused comparison did not identify substantial duplication of the contribution as a whole.

This supports the finite Khovanov calculations, table enumeration, automorphism computations and explicit local identities. Some checks encode known facts or test consequences of assumed geometric inputs; they do not independently prove the topology of the space of local data, analytic Floer hypotheses or general naturality. The main argument is understandable to a reader familiar with flow categories and brane structures. Hypotheses, proof strategies and limitations are available in the main text, and the abstract is informative. Repetition between the introductory theorem list, later statements and verification inventory could be reduced, but it does not make the contribution materially unclear. The following corrections would improve the published presentation:

1. **Phase-loop rank.** Theorem F(6) states the full change as $-2r[N]$, although Lemma 7.1(3) discards a nontrivial rank contribution. In its displayed model, write $A_t=\lambda D'_t=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda,1)$. Then $\det A_t=\lambda^2$, while the normal-line factor has determinant $\bar\lambda^2$. The full Gauss-map lift consequently has determinant one. With the stated real closing rotation, the constant factor contributes Maslov index $2r$, cancelling the normal factor's $-2r$. Either retain this contribution or explicitly state the formula only modulo rank. The reduced monodromy remains zero, so this does not affect the selection rule or normalization up to suspension.
2. **Abstract normalization claim.** “Unknot and unlink normalizations hold iff $w_2=0$” is too broad for equivalence of spectra. Theorem D(2) also requires $n_{ij}=0\pmod{24}$ for the three-component unlink. Specify that the $w_2$-only criterion concerns the second square, or include the additional obstruction in both abstracts.
3. **Terminology.** Definition 1.1(b) tests only $\mathrm{Sq}^1$ and $\mathrm{Sq}^2$. Call this agreement of restricted Steenrod modules, or $\mathcal A(1)$-modules, consistently, including in the abstract. It need not be agreement as modules over the entire Steenrod algebra.
4. **Cycle representatives in §3.** For several nonzero $\epsilon_i$, the factor cycles $Z_i$ intersect. Their disjoint union maps to $L$ but is not the embedded submanifold required literally by Lemma 3.1. Explain the application factor by factor, or state the corresponding version for this union; do not silently identify an embedded cycle with a mapped cycle.
5. Move the extended check-by-check inventory in §10 to the supplement and retain a concise account of what the computations establish. Add the closely related published snapshot papers [35,36] to the related-work discussion, while preserving their distinction from the present results.
