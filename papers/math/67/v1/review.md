# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive extension of the complete gauge-fixed Haydys–Witten linear theory from a neighbourhood of the Anderson–Henningson fold to arbitrary finite lengths $L>\pi r$. Its principal contribution is the confinement of possible non-isotropic degeneracy to the spin-$\tfrac12$ block, together with the index congruence $\operatorname{ind}D_u\in-3+8\mathbb Z$ and the resulting global linear-theory statements on left spherical quotients. These results fit the supplied mathematics scope and go beyond a cosmetic variation of the previously published near-fold calculations. The argument is sufficiently clear and supported for acceptance, with the qualifications and corrections below.

References [1]–[24] below use the manuscript's numbering.

**Correctness and significance.** The decisive estimates have a coherent mathematical basis. The equation $\Delta p=2p(|u|^2-1)$, the pole behaviour, and convergence at the trajectory ends give the strict bound $|p|<1$ in the stated class. The non-strict bound was already available from [21] and the reversing symmetry, as the manuscript acknowledges. The important subsequent use is the decomposition into an anticommuting part and the bounded perturbation $pM$: the coercive estimate applies to both the operator and its adjoint. Table 1, the exact checks at the remaining low spins, and the analytic large-spin bound together cover all harmonic blocks; this is not an extrapolation from a finite numerical scan. The positive margin $\sqrt5-2$ is consistent with these computations.

The gauge-block argument in §6 supplies an essential additional ingredient that is not furnished by the preceding positivity estimate. The deformation-complex identities isolate the real gauge component, and elimination of the remaining complex component produces a scalar elliptic equation with strictly negative zeroth-order coefficient $-2|z|^2$. The stated boundary and end behaviour allow the maximum principle to exclude its kernel. Static invertibility along the continuous family through the fold then gives zero spectral flow in these blocks, completing the trajectory isomorphism argument. This is a useful analytic result, rather than merely a tabulation of exceptional spectra.

The spectral-flow argument in §7 is also consistent. The isotropic crossing contributes $-1$ to the static spectral flow, the near-fold spin-$\tfrac12$ crossing contributes $+4$, and duality pairs the additional crossings on the two branches. Fourfold real multiplicity then gives

$$
\operatorname{ind}D_u=-3+8\nu_+(L).
$$

The sign agrees with the known near-fold value. The conclusion excludes regular index-one isotropic trajectories in the complete round-sphere theory; it does not establish that the index is always $-3$. On a nontrivial left quotient, $V_{1/2}^{\Gamma}=0$ removes the remaining potentially obstructing block. Consequently, the nondegeneracy and equality of full and isotropic kernel/cokernel data in Theorem E follow without assuming Conjecture D.

The revised weighted analysis is substantially better specified than the incorrect statement described in §5. For the fixed domains, the logarithmic change of variable gives limiting matrices $B-(\delta_0+\tfrac12)$ and $-B+(\delta_L+\tfrac12)$, yielding the stated exceptional weights and index count. The critical-weight singular sequence concerns that fixed-domain map. It does not contradict Fredholmness of a different, minimal graph realization. The use of Lesch [8, Proposition 1.3.16] for the latter at the fold matches the cited result. These are radial-block statements; they should not be read as an unrestricted all-weight Fredholm theorem for the infinite direct sum of angular blocks.

**Novelty and attribution.** The closest published rStation comparison is [15], math_30/v1, which supplies the complete operator, harmonic decomposition, fold kernel and near-fold index $-3$. The quotient mechanism and near-fold restored transversality are already in [23], math_59/v1. The global isotropic classification, earlier bound and static profiles are supplied by [13], [20], [21] and [24]. The manuscript distinguishes these ingredients from the new all-length estimates and gauge-block argument. Theorem E(3) is appropriately identified as a recovery of an existing local result, while E(1)–(2) contain the extension.

The focused external comparison also supports this distinction. Anderson–Henningson [1] studies the spherically symmetric ansatz and a perturbative tunnelling expansion. Bleher [2, §7.4] develops general Nahm-pole elliptic regularity and indicial calculations, rather than the all-length spectral analysis of these objects. Mazzeo–Witten [10] and Lesch [8] establish the underlying weighted and singular-operator framework; its application in Proposition 5.1 is useful but is not a new general Fredholm theory. The half-line constructions and energy bounds in [5]–[6] concern different results and boundary problems. Bleher's decoupling theorem [25] likewise has different geometric hypotheses and does not supply the harmonic confinement or index congruence proved here. I found no substantial duplication of the central global contribution in these comparisons.

Two bibliography entries need correction: arXiv:1801.04412, cited as [6], is by **Naichung Conan Leung and Ryosuke Takahashi**, not S. He. The author of arXiv:1707.06182, cited as [7], is **Siqi He**, without R. Mazzeo as coauthor. These are identifiable attribution errors in background references, not grounds to reject the central contribution.

**Evidence and reproducibility.** I ran the supplied Python verifier within the stated resource limits. It completed all 98 checks with `FALLOS = 0`, including the finite-dimensional algebra, intertwiners, weighted index counts, selected shooting computations and sensitivity checks. This supports the algebraic and computational components; it does not independently prove the functional-analytic arguments. The verifier recomputes selected numerical cases and checks retained tables, rather than independently regenerating every reported scan point. No rigorous enclosure of all eigenvalues or exclusion of crossings between sampled lengths follows from this run.

The paper handles that limitation appropriately: global nondegeneracy on the round sphere, and the value $\nu_+=0$ over the proposed finite range, remain conjectural. Neither the sampled Evans angles nor tracking one eigenvalue would justify promoting them to theorems. The trajectory conclusions also retain hypothesis (N); large-$L$ isotropic existence/transversality and completeness of the nonlinear theory outside the symmetric sectors are not established. These qualifications are scientifically important and should remain prominent.

**Clarity and minor improvements.** The research question, main insight, proof dependencies and distinction between proved and numerical claims are identifiable. The main text contains the decisive estimates and spectral-flow argument, while the detailed matrix construction and checks are available in the supplement. The manuscript meets the communication requirement independently of its computational support. The following local improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- In Lemma 6.3, explain the step from differentiating once in $s$ to asserting that the inhomogeneity $f$ is uniformly bounded pointwise in $y$. Make explicit the further regularity bootstrap using the second derivative supplied by (N)(1), and indicate the order of the boundary limits in the integration-by-parts argument. This is a short clarification under the existing assumptions, but it deserves to be visible because the maximum-principle proof uses these bounds.
- Specify that the explicit inverse bound in Theorem B is an $L^2$-to-$L^2$ estimate. Distinguish it from a bound into the full chosen graph/Sobolev norm. Define $\hat c$ when it first appears in the rescaling.
- Replace “smallest eigenvalue” for the two-sided unbounded gauge-block spectrum by the intended precise quantity. For the admissible weight window, use the distance from zero to the spectrum; for Table 4(b), describe the computed positive eigenvalue as such unless its being closest to zero is established. Also write the minimum sampled spin-$\tfrac12$ angle as approximately $0.0278$: the retained value is $0.02776441629$, so “at least $0.0278$” is a rounded overstatement.
- The abstract is informative but unusually dense. Condensing the repeated claim inventory and moving the verifier check count and version-history discussion out of it would make the main contribution easier to see. No additional figure is necessary for the proof; a plot would only be an optional aid to the numerical discussion.

These corrections do not change the acceptance judgment or require new experimental evidence for the stated analytic theorems.
