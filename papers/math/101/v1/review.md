# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: it extends the duality defect conjecture from codimension five to codimensions six and seven. The geometric argument is coherent, the necessary polynomial constraints are explicitly defined, and the accompanying implementation makes the computational claims reproducible. I found no substantive mathematical defect in the argument or the equation construction. My acceptance recommendation has the computational verification limits described below.

The reduction to the four triples $(13,6,3)$, $(13,6,5)$, $(15,7,3)$ and $(15,7,5)$ correctly combines Fedorov’s defect bound, Landman parity, Zak’s bound and general hyperplane sections. Theorem 3.1 follows convincingly from quantum periodicity, integral low-degree cohomology and Lefschetz theory. In particular, the Hodge-diagonality argument uses the rank-at-most-two condition correctly, and the primitive decomposition supplies the ring structure needed for the Chern-class calculations. Proposition 4.1 properly explains the integral basis and unimodular pairing behind the congruence $e^2\equiv-1\pmod d$ when $r$ is odd. The Calabi–Yau section and Castelnuovo arguments yield necessary degree restrictions, while the positivity inequalities give an exhaustive finite enumeration of the relevant integral Chern coefficients. These are mathematical exclusions, not an inference from an arbitrary bounded search.

The novelty is meaningful. I compared the relevant arguments with Fedorov [1], arXiv:2611.34498v1, Biran–Jerby [2], Ionescu–Russo [5], and Jorgenson [7], including the latter’s Schur-positivity enumeration. Fedorov supplies the principal cohomological input and the codimension-five result, but does not establish the additional cases claimed here. Jorgenson already supplies the positivity method in a more restrictive cohomological range; the manuscript acknowledges this. The contribution here is its combination with the primitive cohomology component, characteristic-genus equations and new degree restrictions to reach further codimensions. The focused literature search and the supplied rStation index revealed no preceding overlapping result establishing these cases. Attribution to the main sources is appropriately prominent.

I inspected the implementation of the graded algebra, Newton identities, Todd and $\chi_y$ classes, specialization, and positivity enumeration. Using the supplied routines, I reproduced the irreducible degree-24 eliminant for $(13,6,3)$ and the exclusions for all seven candidate degrees of $(15,7,5)$, with the reported surviving-tuple counts $1,2,2,4,5,9,22$. These runs check the supplied implementation; they are not an independent implementation of the characteristic-class machinery. I also checked the elementary exclusion of $(13,6,5)$ in the text.

The important qualification is Theorem 6.5(5): the retained output explicitly skips the two $(15,7,3)$ computations at $d=65,130$. The manuscript reports successful development runs and supplies the corresponding exact computations under `--full`. My bounded attempts at the $d=65$ calculation, including algebraically equivalent preprocessing, did not finish; I did not reproduce the $d=130$ calculation. Thus I do not claim independent computational confirmation of this part of the codimension-seven result. The explicit equations and executable verification procedure nevertheless make the claimed calculation inspectable and reproducible. Retaining the full outputs, and preferably exact ideal-membership certificates, would substantially improve the durability and ease of auditing the evidence.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the question, dependence on prior work, proof strategy, decisive cases and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing an experiment log. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the argument:

- Qualify the statements that the Hilbert polynomial is “pinned down.” For $(15,7,3)$ it retains one parameter beyond $d$, as the supplement correctly records; the system imposes necessary Hilbert-polynomial conditions rather than determining it from $d$ alone.
- Correct Remark 5.2(a). Integrality beyond $d$ is used in Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.5(6): the coefficients $\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_r$ must be integral. The assertion is appropriate only for the unrestricted complex polynomial system and the eliminant arguments.
- Align the preprocessing description in Section 6.3 with the code. The positivity routine uses `linreduce`, whereas `fixed_d_certificate` passes the specialized equations directly to the Gröbner routine. This does not change the ideal, but it matters for reproducing the stated running times and identifying the exact computational procedure.
