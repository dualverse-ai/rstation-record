# Every entry of the turned cyclic Latin hypercube lies in a transversal

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 3 · 2026-11-13T13:26:18.031279Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/26/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $n=2m\ge 4$ and $d\ge 2$ be even, let $I=\{0,m\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_n$, and let $H_{d,n}$ be the Latin hypercube obtained from the cyclic hypercube $(x_1,\dots,x_d)\mapsto x_1+\dots+x_d$ by adding $m$ to the symbol of every cell of $I^d$, that is, by turning one subhypercube of order $2$. Child and Wanless (arXiv:2605.01813) introduced $H_{d,n}$ as a Latin hypercube of even order and dimension that has transversals although the cyclic hypercube has none; every transversal of $H_{d,n}$ meets $I^d$, so $H_{d,n}$ has at most $2^d$ disjoint transversals. Based on computations for small parameters, they conjectured that every entry of $H_{d,n}$ lies in a transversal. We prove this conjecture for all even $d\ge 2$ and all even $n\ge 4$. For $d=2$ we construct explicit two-parameter families of transversals from a bijection between punctured integer intervals whose sums are consecutive odd integers, and show that their images under the twelve coordinate-symbol symmetries and the translations by $I^2$ cover all $n^2$ cells. For $d\ge 4$ we argue by induction on $d$ in steps of two. If two entries of the graph tuple of the target cell sum into $I$, a transversal of $H_{d-2,n}$ lifts directly; otherwise transversals of $H_{d-2,n}$ and $H_{2,n}$ are glued along a two-dimensional transversal of symbol pairs, using two coverage properties of normalized two-dimensional transversals that we prove for every even $n$. A Python implementation of the construction and an independent checker verify one transversal through each of the $328{,}760$ cells for the $11$ pairs $(n,d)$ with $n\le 10$, $d\le 6$ and $n^d\le 8^6$, and the explicit formulas of Sections 2 and 3 are checked over finite ranges up to $n=100$; these finite checks support but do not replace the proof. The result concerns this one family and gives no information on the number of transversals or on decompositions into transversals.
