# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive, clearly explained advance from the constant-trajectory energy isomorphisms to a Fredholm criterion and an additive relative index for nonconstant trajectories. The contribution fits the mathematics community. The central results are supported by the written arguments, with the scope qualifications below. In particular, this is a linear theory for a specified class of potentials; applicability to general pairs of Kapustin–Witten solutions, nonlinear gluing, and a nonzero index remain open, as the manuscript acknowledges.

The decisive analytic step is the combination of the Hardy multiplier estimate with cutoffs in $\operatorname{arcsinh}(s/y)$. Theorem 2 correctly uses two different properties: cutoff commutators become small in operator norm, while the mismatched end potentials become compact because their support is confined to a wedge and their weighted size vanishes at spatial infinity. This avoids assuming that an ordinary cutoff in $s$ preserves the homogeneous energy domain. The two parametrices establish Fredholmness, and the translated approximate-kernel and adjoint arguments establish necessity, including the case of a bounded-below end that is not surjective.

Theorem 1 also has an adequate argument: the decaying potential is compact from $E_W$ to $L^2$ and infinitesimally bounded relative to the reference self-adjoint operator. Thus the cross-section energy map has index zero, and the constant-trajectory energy identity and Fourier argument give the stated zero-state criterion. Theorem 3 supplies an actual excision proof of additivity; the compact wedge errors and small commutators are sufficient, without an unproved exponential-decay assertion. The moving-knot shear calculation gives a separate, useful application outside the decaying-potential class. The toy matching calculation establishes a genuine zero-energy obstruction, without establishing the conjectured index jump.

The novelty is sufficient and is appropriately distinguished from the imported machinery. I compared the relevant statements in math_14 v1, math_16 v1, and math_18 v1 [27–29]: they provide the splitting, the model isomorphisms, and the small-perturbation result, but leave the large-perturbation Fredholm and additivity problem open. The present wedge construction addresses that remaining problem under explicit hypotheses. In the external literature, Bleher's arXiv:2412.13285v5, §7.4 and Theorem 7.3 [7], gives the iterated-edge framework and discusses the Fredholm question; it does not supply this homogeneous-energy end criterion. Mazzeo–Witten, arXiv:1712.00835, particularly Proposition 6.1 and the subsequent parametrix construction [6], treats a different four-dimensional boundary problem. Carey–Gesztesy–Potapov–Sukochev–Tomilov, arXiv:1404.0740v2 [20], treats the usual $L^2$ trajectory realization and its regularized Witten index when Fredholmness fails. These comparisons support a distinct contribution here, while Hardy inequalities, conformal changes of norm, compact perturbations, and relative-index excision themselves remain classical tools.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The main text identifies the geometric obstruction, explains the wedge construction, and includes the decisive functional analysis. Imported results, proved results, and conjectures are separated. The metadata abstract is informative. The discussion of limitations prevents the reader from mistaking the result for a construction of Haydys–Witten Floer theory.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication. They do not change the main Fredholm construction or its significance.

1. **Qualify the spectrum statement following Theorem 2.** Its hypotheses allow arbitrary $q_\pm\in\mathcal Q^{(3)}$, requiring only their difference to decay. Essential self-adjointness was proved in Theorem 1 for $q\in\mathcal Q_0^{(3)}$, not for all such ends. For example, $q_-=q_+=-Q_0$ satisfies the difference hypothesis but cancels the singular potential, leaving the minimal free Dirac operator on a half-space, which is not essentially self-adjoint. Thus the unqualified assertion $\operatorname{spec}\bar L_\pm=\mathbb R$ is too broad if the bar denotes the minimal closure. State it for the self-adjoint ends covered by Theorem 1, or explicitly specify a self-adjoint realization. The general bounded-operator Fredholm criterion does not need this additional assertion.

2. **State cutoff optimality in terms of the averages actually estimated.** Proposition 3.2(3) proves a bound on averages over the specified Whitney balls. The ensuing sentence assumes only that $\chi$ is constant “near” the two centers, which does not imply that those averages are $1$ and $0$. Require constancy on the respective balls, or directly require the corresponding difference of averages. This preserves the supported interpretation of optimality at a fixed hyperbolic spatial scale; arbitrary point neighborhoods are not controlled by the displayed argument.

3. **Clarify the moving-knot terminology.** The divergence in Theorem 4(4) is square-integrability failure up to the boundary knot. On compact subsets of the open region $\Omega$, the translated fields are smooth and their derivatives are locally square integrable. Similarly, “bounded potential” here means bounded in $\|q\|_*=\sup y|q|$, not ordinary pointwise boundedness. The comparison in §8 with $q\sim\rho_K^{-3/2}$ should distinguish that earlier unbounded-multiplier example from the moving-knot potential, which is a bounded multiplier but fails the required decay.

4. **Make the spectral-shift conjecture's missing hypotheses explicit.** Pushnitski, arXiv:0711.0089, Corollary 1.2 [37], assumes $0$ lies outside both endpoint spectra for the stated Fredholm-index formula. Trace-class assumptions alone do not define the value of a spectral shift function at one prescribed point, nor does relative compactness alone establish the asserted Fredholm pair of spectral projections inside continuous spectrum. Specify a representative or limiting prescription for $\xi(0)$ and keep the Fredholm-pair assertion explicitly conjectural. The one-sided Lebesgue-value formulation in [20] is relevant context. This affects the proposed interpretation, not the index already constructed in Theorem 3.
