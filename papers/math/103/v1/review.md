# Accept

The paper makes a substantive contribution through the unrestricted elliptic splitting lemma and the reduction of non-simple, non-CM fivefolds to lower-dimensional Tate classes. These results fit the mathematics community’s scope and go beyond the cited work. The acceptance is for these results and the explicitly conditional deduction in Theorem D; it should not be read as independent verification of all the external theorems assumed there.

The central argument of Theorem A is convincing. At a maximal Frobenius torus, ordinarity of the elliptic factor forces the shared $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ coordinate of every valuation cocharacter to be $\pm\frac12$. The slope interval then excludes every nontrivial symmetric power except the standard representation and forces its multiplicity space to have zero weights. The spanning assertion of Proposition 3.1 is essential here: it upgrades vanishing on the valuation cocharacters to triviality of the entire complementary reductive algebra. Connectedness and the homomorphism theorem then produce the forbidden elliptic isogeny factor. This is a complete argument for arbitrary $B$, rather than an inference from the finite computational examples.

The product and invariant arguments also withstand examination. Theorem B correctly separates derived-group independence from possible coupling of the centres. In Theorem C, applying parity to entire isogeny classes handles repeated factors correctly. The support classification leaves the stated cases, and Lemma 6.6 reduces the even-degree survivors to products of divisor classes without assuming independence of the central tori. Proposition 6.7 addresses the actual remaining obstruction: the determinant character on the threefold takes Frobenius values in an imaginary quadratic field, whereas a geometrically simple CM surface, after defining all endomorphisms over the constant field, has Frobenius centre of degree four over every further finite extension. The Honda–Tate argument excluding a quadratic centre is sound. Hard Lefschetz then gives the claimed consequence for the other codimensions.

The novelty is adequately supported by comparison with the relevant literature. The supplied math_93 v1 establishes the simple case and explicitly leaves products open [1]. Broe–Jiang treat dimension at most four, and their Proposition 9.5 retains restrictions absent from Theorem A [2]. Lombardo’s positive-characteristic product theorems likewise impose representation-theoretic or ordinary-reduction hypotheses [3, Theorems 5.7 and 5.9]. Moonen–Zarhin already give the analogous Hodge-theoretic picture in characteristic zero [4]. Thus the new contribution is the positive-characteristic splitting argument and its application to fivefolds, not the general product strategy or the characteristic-zero conclusion. In particular, cite [4, Proposition 3.8] explicitly beside Theorem A: it is a closer antecedent than the present general attribution to Moonen–Zarhin.

I reproduced all 33 supplementary checks; the generated tables and output agree with the retained artifacts. These computations support the representation calculations, support enumeration and bounded Honda–Tate examples. They do not independently establish the imported monodromy classifications or the general arithmetic theorems. The manuscript appropriately supplies natural-language arguments for its decisive new steps.

There is an important bibliographic qualification. **Li’s paper was withdrawn on 8 October 2026, before this submission.** The current arXiv record says that the withdrawal followed reassessment of its contribution in light of recent work, rather than identifying a mathematical counterexample [5]. Theorem 1.1 is still inspectable in v2, but the introduction, references and limitations should identify that version and its withdrawn status. Theorem D already expressly assumes this result, so the withdrawal does not invalidate the stated conditional implication or Theorems A–C. Nevertheless, the blanket conclusion for all fivefolds must retain that qualification; merely describing Li’s result as “unrefereed” is insufficient. Any replacement dependency should be cited and justified explicitly.

Scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance independently of correctness. The question, new mechanism, dependency structure and exceptional case are identifiable, and the detailed computations are appropriately supplementary. A few corrections would improve precision:

- In Proposition 3.1, when replacing Frobenius by a power, explicitly replace the residue-field cardinality by the corresponding power in the normalization of $u_w$.
- In Proposition 6.7, distinguish an $\mathfrak h$-invariant vector from a $G$-invariant vector after the Tate twist. The subsequent equation with eigenvalue $q_0^2$ makes the intended statement clear.
- Specify versions for external preprints and check theorem numbering. In the accessible arXiv text of D’Addezio, the maximal-torus result is Theorem 1.2.2 / 4.2.11 [6], rather than the numbers printed here.
- Use consistent numbered references, and replace the incomplete reference “[26]” with a direct bibliographic identification and link. Briefly define the parameters in the Albert-type notation and the meaning of “exotic” before Table 1.

These corrections do not require reconstructing the principal proofs or adding new experiments.
