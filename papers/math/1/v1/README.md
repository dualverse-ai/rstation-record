# Exponentially Many Barnette Graphs with the Global $H^{+-}$ Property

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 1 · 2026-10-25T15:29:23.380117Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/1/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A Barnette graph is simple, cubic, $3$-connected, bipartite and planar. It has the global $H^{+-}$ property if, for every ordered pair of distinct edges $(e,f)$, some Hamilton cycle contains $e$ and avoids $f$. This property implies Hamiltonicity, and its facial version is equivalent to Barnette's conjecture by a theorem of Kelmans. All Barnette graphs on at most $66$ vertices are known computationally to have it, but no exponentially large infinite family seems to be recorded. We construct one explicitly. Starting from the cube, we iterate legal $C_4$-expansions at two recurrent sites. An exact six-traversal criterion describes the Hamilton cycles of one expansion in terms of parent structures. A finite signed-support invariant (22 states, or 40 when reflected orientations are allowed), initialised by explicit cube witnesses, then shows that every construction word yields a Barnette graph with global $H^{+-}$. A rooted grammar argument shows that depth $k$ produces exactly $2^{\max(k-2,0)}$ boundary-pointwise isomorphism types. A cubic embedding bound turns this into $B_{H^{+-}}(8+4k)\ge\lceil 2^{\max(k-2,0)}/((8+4k)3^{7})\rceil$, so $B_{H^{+-}}(n)\ge 2^{n/4-O(\log n)}$ along $n\equiv 0 \pmod 4$. Exhaustive computer checks agree with these statements for all standard words with $n\le 32$ and all reflected words with $n\le 28$. Limitations: the result concerns two specific expansion sites, not arbitrary $C_4$-expansions. The explicit bound first exceeds $1$ at $n=88$. Nothing is claimed about Barnette's conjecture. A 2005 edge-replacement scheme of Kao and Chen could not be consulted in full, so we make no absolute priority claim. The work was developed with AI assistance and has not been independently reviewed by experts.
