# Exponentially Many Barnette Graphs with the Global $H^{+-}$ Property

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 6 · 2026-11-12T00:48:14.288372Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/1/v6) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A Barnette graph is simple, cubic, $3$-connected, bipartite and planar. It has the global $H^{+-}$ property if, for every ordered pair of distinct edges $(e,f)$, some Hamilton cycle contains $e$ and avoids $f$. Barnette's conjecture has been proved (OpenAI, 2026; exposition by Z. Li, arXiv:2610.30490), and with it $P_2$-, $P_4$-Hamiltonicity, $H^-$ and the $H^{+-}$ condition for edge pairs at distance at most $1$ hold for every Barnette graph; global $H^{+-}$ for all Barnette graphs remains open. All Barnette graphs on at most $66$ vertices are known computationally to have it, and infinite families follow from tight-cut composition. We give a controlled expansion mechanism with an explicit growth rate. Starting from the cube, we iterate legal $C_4$-expansions at two recurrent sites. An exact six-traversal criterion describes the Hamilton cycles of one expansion in terms of parent structures. A finite signed-support invariant (22 states, or 40 when reflected orientations are allowed), initialised by explicit cube witnesses, then shows that every construction word yields a Barnette graph with global $H^{+-}$. A rooted grammar argument shows that depth $k$ produces exactly $2^{\max(k-2,0)}$ boundary-pointwise isomorphism types. A cubic embedding bound turns this into $B_{H^{+-}}(8+4k)\ge\lceil 2^{\max(k-2,0)}/((8+4k)3^{7})\rceil$, so $B_{H^{+-}}(n)\ge 2^{n/4-O(\log n)}$ along $n\equiv 0 \pmod 4$. Exhaustive computer checks agree with these statements for all standard words with $n\le 32$ and all reflected words with $n\le 28$. Limitations: the result concerns two specific expansion sites, not arbitrary $C_4$-expansions. The explicit bound first exceeds $1$ at $n=88$. A 2005 edge-replacement scheme of Kao and Chen could not be consulted in full, so we make no absolute priority claim.
