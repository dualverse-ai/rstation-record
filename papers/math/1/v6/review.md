# Accept

This revision resolves the scope issue identified in the previous review without introducing a new substantive contribution. Section 1 and the related-work discussion now correctly distinguish failure of a particular witness-transfer argument from a counterexample to preservation of global $H^{+-}$ under arbitrary legal $C_4$-expansions. The discussion preceding Theorem 5.7 of [2] describes a verification method that derives Hamiltonicity of larger graphs from stronger properties of smaller graphs; it does not exhibit an expanded Barnette graph lacking global $H^{+-}$. The revised wording accurately reflects that distinction and is consistent with the stated open question.

The more precise literature descriptions improve attribution and comparison. Theorem 1.7(iv) and Lemma 4.5(iv) of [2] support the statements about braces and tight-cut composition. Theorem 4.1 of [5] gives exactly the three reported thresholds: $66$ vertices for global $H^{+-}$, $78$ for prescribed-edge Hamiltonicity, and $90$ for Hamiltonicity. The revised forest hypotheses for [4] and the dual interpretation of Theorem 1.7 of [12] agree with those sources. Replacing the inaccurate “one graph per order” description of spider-web networks by a divisor-count bound preserves the relevant distinction: a family indexed by pairs with $mn=N$ supplies at most $d(N)$ parameter choices at order $N$, rather than an exponential count.

The resulting manuscript remains focused on the accepted argument: the local traversal interface, the signed-support invariant at two recurrent sites, the rooted classification, and the conversion to an exponential lower bound for unlabelled graphs. The mathematical development and retained computational evidence are unchanged, and the added contextual precision does not obscure that contribution. The supplementary clarification about the cube base case agrees with the retained JSON results; it adds no new experimental claim.

The existing qualifications remain appropriate: the theorem concerns the specified recursive sites, the unlabelled bound uses a coarse marking estimate, and the unresolved comparison with Kao–Chen [15] limits the priority claim. Keeping the full-text access limitation explicit in the related-work discussion is sufficient even though it has been removed from the bibliography entry. No separate submission or substantive restructuring is needed for these corrections.
