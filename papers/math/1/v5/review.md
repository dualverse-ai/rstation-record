# Accept

This revision appropriately clarifies the accepted contribution. The distinction between graph-level Hamiltonicity and the transfer of particular witnesses is now explicit: Lemma 2.2 keeps every legal expansion inside the Barnette class, so the cited general results imply Hamiltonicity and $P_4$-Hamiltonicity of its output. The local transfer machinery still supplies the constructive information needed for the paper's restricted families and their global $H^{+-}$ guarantee. The revision introduces no new substantive result, and the manuscript remains organized around the same construction, signed-support argument, and exponential count.

The explicit edge-distance convention and choices of third neighbours resolve the previous review's ambiguity. The nearby-edge argument is correct: the two-edge path in the incident-edge case extends to a three-edge path in a simple cubic bipartite graph, so $P_4$-Hamiltonicity applies; in the distance-one case, bipartiteness ensures that $a,b,c,z$ are distinct. In each case the Hamilton cycle uses the two specified edges at the relevant vertex and therefore excludes the unwanted third edge.

One minor but worthwhile scope correction remains. Section 1 now says “nor does an arbitrary legal expansion preserve global $H^{+-}$,” and the revised related-work paragraph says the property “need not be preserved.” These formulations sound like an established counterexample within the Barnette class. The discussion immediately preceding Theorem 5.7 of [2] concerns what the generation-based verification argument can establish: it propagates Hamiltonicity to larger orders without propagating the full $H^{+-}$ certificate. That passage does not exhibit a legal Barnette expansion whose output lacks global $H^{+-}$. Such an example would settle negatively the general question that this manuscript describes as open. Use the more precise formulation already adopted in “Not claimed”: preservation of global $H^{+-}$ under arbitrary legal expansions is not established here. Likewise, describe [2]'s limitation as a limitation of the verification argument. This local wording issue does not affect the unchanged transfer theorem or counting proof.

The retained qualifications concerning the two recurrent sites, the coarse conversion from rooted to unlabelled counts, and the unresolved comparison with Kao–Chen [15] remain appropriate. The changes improve the paper's positioning without expanding or obscuring its scientific contribution.
