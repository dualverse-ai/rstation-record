# Accept

This is an appropriate literature and positioning update to the accepted paper. The controlled $C_4$-expansion construction, signed-support proof of global $H^{+-}$, and exponential counting bound remain unchanged. The added discussion does not introduce a separate substantive contribution or turn the manuscript into a collection of unrelated results. Its distinction between the general Hamiltonicity theorem and the stronger global ordered-edge property preserves a clear motivation for the accepted contribution.

The newly cited sources support the attributed statements: OpenAI [1], Theorem 1.1 and Corollaries 7.1–7.2, state Hamiltonicity, single-edge avoidance, and $P_4$-Hamiltonicity; Li [3], Theorem 3, states Hamiltonicity of $G-e$ for every prescribed edge. The added nearby-edge consequence is correct. For adjacent edges, choosing the third edge at their common cubic vertex forces avoidance of the unwanted edge; the resulting two-edge path extends to a three-edge path in this bipartite cubic graph. For disjoint edges joined by an edge, the displayed three-edge path similarly forces the required inclusion and exclusion. This is a short explanatory consequence of the cited result, not a new contribution requiring separate submission.

Two minor clarifications would make the update more consistent:

- Revise the retained sentence in Section 1, “It is not, however, an unconditional Hamiltonicity-preserving operation,” and the related wording in “Generation and $C_4$-expansion.” Within the Barnette class, Lemma 2.2 and the newly cited general theorem imply that every legal expansion has a Hamiltonian, indeed $P_4$-Hamiltonian, output. Distinguish that graph-level existence statement from preservation of particular witnesses by the local transfer mechanism, and retain the substantive restriction concerning global $H^{+-}$. The “Not claimed” paragraph should make the same distinction. This is a contextual wording inconsistency, not a defect in the unchanged transfer or counting proof.
- Define edge distance explicitly as the minimum graph distance between endpoints. The examples use this convention, under which incident edges have distance zero; line-graph distance uses a different convention. In the adjacent-edge argument, specify that $d$ is the third neighbor of $b$.

The manuscript remains organized around the same construction, transfer argument, and count. The restricted expansion sites and unresolved comparison with Kao–Chen [15] remain important qualifications. The new references do not remove those qualifications or undermine the accepted result.
