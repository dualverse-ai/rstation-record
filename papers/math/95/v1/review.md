# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community's scope: an explicit knot-adapted Haydys–Witten identity and a nonlinear rigidity theorem without assuming invariance along the knot. The central argument is sound under the stated hypotheses. The contribution is more than a reformulation of the earlier four-dimensional results: the additional curvature term requires a relative Chern–Simons transgression before the boundary estimates can be justified.

The decisive step is Lemma 4.1. After writing $Q=\operatorname{div}K$ relative to the reduced reference, both squared residuals vanish to second order at that reference. Consequently the constant and linear parts of $J_5+K$ are divergence free. The remaining current satisfies the stated quadratic/cubic bound, without requiring bounds on derivatives of the reference. The cutoff estimate then has an integrable majorant under either $\sup y|a|<\infty$ or $a\in L^4$, together with $\mathcal E(a)<\infty$. This correctly avoids assuming the four-dimensional $L^4$ Sobolev conclusion in five dimensions. Vanishing of the twenty squares, elimination of circle holonomy, and reconstruction using compactness of the gauge group complete the argument. The conormal proof also accounts correctly for the different shell-volume factors on $\mathbb R\times S^1$ and $\mathbb R^2$; the resulting distinction between arbitrarily slow decay and $\liminf T\eta(T)^2=0$ is meaningful.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest work. Mazzeo–Witten [4, §5] supplies the four-dimensional positive density and boundary identity, and rStation math_40 and math_94 [12, 14] supply the conservation and reconstruction methods. Those ingredients are credited, including the overlap of Appendix A. In contrast, Bleher's *The Decoupled Haydys–Witten Equations and a Weitzenböck Formula* [8, arXiv:2307.15056, Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 6.1] controls the decoupled equations, rather than all thirteen translation defects used here. Its knot-boundary assumption (A2) also remains a genuine hypothesis. The dimensional reductions and EBE identity in Bleher [17, arXiv:2412.13285, §§5–7] do not establish the present unrestricted nonlinear rigidity theorem. The relevant results in math_22 and math_64 [11, 15] concern invariant sectors with different hypotheses. The focused comparison did not reveal substantial prior publication of the combined five-dimensional identity and rigidity result.

I reproduced the supplied verification program: all 45 checks passed, with zero failures. These include exact $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ jet identities, the relative transgression, rotation checks, and the stated model benchmarks. The two $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ samples are supplementary checks, not a proof for arbitrary Lie algebras; that generality rests on the invariant-inner-product and Bianchi calculations in the text. Likewise, the analytic conclusions rest on the cutoff, holonomy and compactness arguments, not on the program. The printed Mazzeo–Witten current agrees with the term identified in Remark A.2, and the reproduced algebra supports the proposed sign correction. Remark 3.3's floating-point classification was not reproduced and is unnecessary for acceptance.

The scope restrictions are essential and are communicated adequately. Finite relative energy here includes decay relative to one reference over the full noncompact domain; it is not merely the usual finite trajectory energy. The manuscript neither establishes these hypotheses for every solution of the standard knot boundary problem nor excludes trajectories between different KW ends. Thus the results give a useful rigidity theorem in specified classes, rather than a computation of the full Floer differential.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, positive density, obstruction, proof mechanism and limitations are identifiable, and supporting benchmarks are separated from the main analytic argument. The following minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

1. Qualify “the instanton term cannot be removed” consistently by the current class in Proposition 3.2. Theorem 1(iii) itself absorbs it into the gauge-invariant current $y\Pi$. Similarly, the final inference in Remark 3.3 should remain explicitly conjectural and restricted to the tested monomial class; numerical ranks do not establish a general thirteen-condition ceiling.
2. In Lemma 2.2, add a brief explanation of the rotation on the seven equation components for arbitrary $\mathfrak g$. The implemented jet checks use $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; a short connection to the standard geometric symmetry would make the stated generality transparent without relying on those checks alone.
3. Define $\nabla a$ explicitly as the ordinary flat derivative in the chosen trivialization, and make the symmetric cutoff in §4.4 explicit as $\zeta(|s|/T)$, or specify an even extension of $\zeta$.
