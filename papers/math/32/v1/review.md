# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive local contribution to the mathematics of the Haydys–Witten equations. Its main advance is the explicit gauge-invariant Higgs-mass perturbation, including the hyperbolic correction and the induced symmetry-breaking coefficient, followed by a classification of the resulting local trajectories. The finite-dimensional Morse theory is classical, but identifying a perturbation of the original equations that realizes it is meaningful. The work fits the supplied mathematics scope.

The distinction from previous work is sufficiently clear. In the supplied versions, math_30 [20] establishes the four-dimensional obstruction, and math_31 [21] establishes the unperturbed sphere, reduction and isolation results. Neither constructs the Higgs-mass perturbation or the eight-object transverse system obtained here. Math_25 [18] treats a symmetry-restricted problem and supplies the triangular reduction argument. The present correction concerning the surviving left-translation symmetry of triaxial metrics is also useful. My focused external comparison with Anderson–Henningson [2], Banyaga–Hurtubise [35], Qin [24] and Bonciocat [29] supports the manuscript's attribution: Morse–Bott perturbation, compactification and realization machinery are established ingredients; the equation-specific perturbation calculation and its consequences are the new contribution. I found no substantial duplication of that contribution in these sources.

The central argument is convincing. Orthogonality of the background forcing permits the affine hyperbolic shift. The computation of the linear term, together with equivariance, gives the negative coefficient $\kappa=-5\pi/216$. For fixed sufficiently small $\bar\nu<0$, persistence of the normally hyperbolic sphere and upper semicontinuity of the isolated invariant set localize all complete local trajectories there. The rescaled angular field is then sufficiently close to the Rayleigh flow to apply structural stability. This justifies the eight critical points, their indices and the transverse trajectory spaces. The normal stable and unstable directions explain the index shift and the reduction of full transversality to tangential transversality. The antipodal quotient correctly determines the displayed integral differential, rather than merely its ranks. The square compactification has the expected four edges and four twice-broken corners.

Above the fold, the invariant fixed set of the antipodal symmetry explains persistence of the obstructed arrow. The slice-forcing calculation has a strictly signed, convergent Melnikov integral and therefore supplies a supported mechanism for breaking that connection. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes this slice perturbation from a gauge-invariant perturbation of the original problem.

I reproduced all 43 checks in the supplied Python verifier, which finished with `FALLOS = 0`. These include the explicit shift equation, the induced coefficient and cancellation, the chain-complex calculations, and the illustrative truncated model. Independently, the direct coefficient agrees with the sphere fourth-moment identity and $\int_0^\pi\sin^6y\,dy=5\pi/16$. This verification supports the algebra; it does not reproduce nonlinear PDE solutions or establish the imported analytic theorems. I also checked the relevant statements of Qin [24] and Bonciocat [29]. The distinction between topological compactifications and a conditional framed realization is essential and is adequately recorded in Sections 5, 7 and 8.

The paper is readable as a mathematical argument: the research question, perturbation, proof strategy, dependencies and limitations can be identified without reconstructing an experiment log. The main conclusions are appropriately local, with a nonuniform smallness condition on $\varepsilon$. The following corrections and clarifications would improve the presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. In the proof of D2, the statement $W^u(k_\pm(\varepsilon))\subset\Pi_e$ is false for $k_-$: its unstable manifold has dimension four, whereas $\Pi_e$ has dimension two. Restrict the interval-action argument to the one-dimensional $W^u(k_+)$; the corresponding one-dimensional manifold at $k_-$ is $W^s(k_-)$. The later connection-breaking argument uses $W^u(k_+)$, so this erroneous extra assertion does not invalidate it.
2. The abstract's phrase “adds … exactly” should specify that the coefficient of the $\varepsilon Cw$ term is exact. Equation (1.3) retains higher-order terms, and Corollary 4.3 establishes closeness to a gradient flow, not equality with that gradient flow. Its sentence “it is the gradient flow” should refer explicitly to the leading term.
3. When applying [18, Lemma 5.3] to the complete spatial problem, spell out the applicable operator estimate. The earlier proof uses a small multiplication operator on $X$ in its restricted setting; here the available estimate $\|Q(\xi,v)\|_X\leq C\|\xi\|_Z\|v\|_Z$, together with the hyperbolic inverse into the graph domain, gives the appropriate small perturbation argument. This would make the transfer of transversality more transparent.
4. Describe the unconditional category consistently as a topological flow category with the stated regularity of its interiors. The introduction initially asks for manifolds with corners, while the proof deliberately claims only topological compactifications for the actual perturbed flow. Keep Hypothesis (CJS) attached to every assertion of the framed realization. Neither that hypothesis nor Hypothesis (T) is needed to appreciate the proved Higgs-mass result.
