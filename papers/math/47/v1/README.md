# The non-abelian left-invariant ansatz for Kapustin–Witten on Sol^3 × R_+: an exact reduction, and rigidity of corrected-Nahm-pole solutions (no non-abelian limits, no new convergent families, no flat-to-flat flow lines)

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/47/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We extend the left-invariant diagonal ansatz for the Kapustin–Witten (KW) equations ($t=1$, $SU(2)$) on $\mathrm{Sol}^3\times\mathbb R_+$ of [19] to the fixed set $\mathcal V\cong\mathbb C^5$ of a symmetry $\iota$ (an isometry composed with a constant gauge transformation). $\mathcal V$ is the smallest linear ansatz containing the diagonal one and the non-abelian triangular flat branches. For all nine left-invariant components, KW is the holomorphic gradient flow of $S=\det Z-\frac\mu2\sum_j(Z_{1j}^2-Z_{2j}^2)$ ($=-$ the Chern–Simons density) together with a Gauss law. In the sector $A_s=A_y=0$, the Haydys–Witten (HW) equations become the $\zeta$-instanton equation for $S$ with the same constraint. On $\mathcal V$ the flow is real-linear once $z_3$ is given, two charges $n_1,n_2$ are conserved, and the Gauss law reads $n_1=n_2$. The critical set is the abelian line together with four triangular lines, all with $\mathrm{CS}=0$; the triangular points are acyclic but not semisimple. *Main theorem:* a corrected-Nahm-pole KW solution in $\mathcal V$ that is bounded on $[1,\infty)$ is diagonal and converges to an abelian flat connection. The proof combines LaSalle's principle, $n_1n_2=0$ on the critical set, and a Wronskian. Non-diagonal corrected-pole solutions exist for every charge, through a sinh-Gordon-type equation, but none is bounded. Bounded left-invariant KW solutions on $\mathrm{Sol}^3\times\mathbb R$ are constant. Consequences for HW morphisms are stated only in the sector $A_s=A_y=0$ and under the local hypotheses of [19].
