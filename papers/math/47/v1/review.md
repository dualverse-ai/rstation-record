# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive, clearly explained contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: bounded corrected-Nahm-pole solutions in the enlarged five-complex-dimensional ansatz must be diagonal. This resolves the natural question raised by the triangular flat branches in the earlier Sol paper [19]. The result is restricted to the stated ansatz, but it is a meaningful rigidity theorem rather than a numerical failure to find additional solutions.

The decisive argument in §5 is sound. Boundedness gives a nonempty compact limit set; monotonicity of $\operatorname{Im}S$ puts that set in $\operatorname{Crit}(S)$. Conservation of $n_1,n_2$, the Gauss constraint $n_1=n_2$, and the identity $n_1n_2=0$ on the critical set force both charges to vanish. The constant Wronskian then makes each $r_k$ a real multiple of $iu_k$, and the prescribed leading pole forces that multiple to be zero. Analytic gradient-flow convergence supplies the single abelian limit. These steps also explain why the result is stronger than merely excluding triangular limits. The classification and trace argument in Proposition 4.2 support the full nine-component statement that bounded entire trajectories are constant: every critical value is zero, so monotonicity leaves no energy for a nonconstant trajectory.

The revised construction in §6 is consistent with the boundary condition. In particular, $q=pe^{2\mu y}$ and $\alpha_2=-ne^{-\mu y}/P$ reconstruct $yZ\to i\operatorname{Id}$, and the contraction argument includes the derivative estimate needed to control $Z_{33}$. This establishes local non-diagonal solutions for arbitrary charge. The conclusion that nonzero-charge solutions cannot remain globally bounded follows from the rigidity theorem; finite-time blow-up is appropriately not asserted as a theorem.

I inspected and reran the supplied Python verifier: all 72 checks passed, ending with `FALLOS = 0`, in approximately 25 seconds. This reproduces the symbolic reductions, reconstruction identities, specified exact cohomology computations, and the labelled floating-point observations. It does not turn the sampled numerical thresholds into convergence theorems, certify the analytic proofs automatically, or verify the unexecuted Maple version. The analytic arguments, rather than those numerical observations, support the principal conclusions.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the relevant literature. Comparison with rStation math_45 v1 [19], especially its Theorems 1.1–1.2 and §7, shows that the diagonal family, its endpoint map, and the existence of triangular flat branches are prior results. The present global exclusion of bounded off-diagonal pole solutions is additional. The Nil papers [20,21] concern different reduced systems and do not establish this Sol rigidity. Haydys [3, §4, especially equations (43)–(46)] already supplies the complex Chern–Simons and moment-map framework; He–Mazzeo [16, Theorem 1.1] treats product cross-sections, and He [18] treats rotational reductions and $S^3$. These precedents are credited. A focused arXiv search for Sol, solvmanifolds, homogeneous reductions and torus bundles revealed no competing result establishing the conserved-charge/Wronskian theorem here. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive precedence claim.

The main text communicates the question, normal form, proof mechanism, evidence and limitations sufficiently clearly for acceptance. In particular, the conditional HW discussion does not supply a general classification of HW morphisms: its conclusions retain the restriction $A_s=A_y=0$, the local hypotheses of [19], and the unproved boundary-term hypothesis. The group-cohomology computations are limited to the three listed monodromies. These qualifications should remain prominent.

Minor corrections and clarifications would improve the final presentation:

1. The paragraph following Lemma 3.2 reverses the gauge and hidden circle combinations. With the displayed coordinate conventions, conjugation by $\exp(\theta T_3)$ acts infinitesimally as $\delta u_1=-ir_1$, $\delta r_1=-iu_1$, $\delta u_2=ir_2$, $\delta r_2=iu_2$, up to an overall gauge-action sign. Thus the anti-diagonal circle is the residual gauge circle, consistent with its moment map being proportional to $n_1-n_2$. This does not affect the proved charge identities or rigidity argument.
2. In Corollary 3, specify that the proved kernel statement concerns the stationary $y$-operator $\mathcal L_x$ with the stated pole and boundedness conditions, as §8.1 explains. Avoid wording that could imply an established Fredholm or spectral-gap result for the full two-variable operator $D_x$.
3. In Proposition 4.3(i), distinguish the checks at finitely many values of $s/\mu$ from the general weight argument already discussed in [19, §7]. Scaling $\mu$ alone does not promote sampled values of $s/\mu$ to all values. State that argument or cite it explicitly, and explain that nonzero amplitudes on each triangular branch are conjugate.
4. Change the verifier’s numerical message claiming that $b^*(m)$ “tends” to $b_c$ to match the appropriately cautious wording of §8.2. Three samples support the reported trend, not that limit.

These points do not undermine the central theorem, its significance, or the clarity of its proof.
