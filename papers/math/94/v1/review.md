# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful extension of straight-knot Kapustin–Witten rigidity. The main contributions are the quantitative estimate under slow decay along the knot (Theorem B) and the replacement of the rank-one frame reconstruction by compactness of temporal gauges (Lemma 4.2). Together they give uniqueness for arbitrary compact gauge groups under explicit hypotheses, including perturbations outside the four-dimensional finite-energy class. This is a substantive contribution within the mathematics community's scope. Acceptance does not assert uniqueness for general Nahm-pole boundary data or for the periodic problem.

The decisive analytic arguments are sound. Expansion of the current about a reduced solution eliminates its constant and linear terms after integration against compactly supported cutoffs. The remaining terms have precisely the stated bounds, with no derivative of the reference entering the quadratic remainder. In Theorem A, cutoff approximation and the four-dimensional Sobolev inequality supply the integrable majorant needed for dominated convergence. In Theorem B, taking the transverse cutoff limit first is essential and is done correctly: local boundedness of $\varepsilon$ controls each fixed slab, while $\int w^2/y<\infty$ controls the two longitudinal shells. Their length cancels the $T^{-1}$ derivative of the longitudinal cutoff. This proves the stated bound and permits $\liminf_{T\to\infty}\eta(T)=0$ without an integrable decay rate in $t$.

Lemma 4.2 supplies the missing reconstruction rather than merely identifying gauge invariants. Compactness of $G$ and local $L^2$ convergence of the slice perturbations bound the temporal gauges in $W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Rellich compactness gives a group-valued limit; the distributional connection equation passes to that limit and bootstraps it to a smooth gauge transformation. No spanning Higgs frame is required. The periodic holonomy argument also works for compact $G$: its conjugacy-invariant distance from the identity is constant transversely, and the weighted energy forces that constant to vanish. The invariant-polynomial argument then proves nilpotency in the stated semisimple setting. It does not prove periodic uniqueness, which the manuscript appropriately leaves separate.

The novelty is narrower than the collection of results A–E might initially suggest, but sufficient. Direct comparison with math_40 v1 [1] shows that the conservation argument, finite-energy vanishing mechanism, and rank-one periodic reduction—including trivial holonomy and nilpotency—are already there. The manuscript acknowledges this dependence. The new reconstruction and longitudinal-shell estimate extend the admissible groups and asymptotic class; they are not simply another verification of the same identity. The model formulas and indicial spectra should principally be regarded as applications and consistency checks. Comparison with math_87 v1 [2] confirms the acknowledged provenance of the $SU(3)$ models. Math_41 and math_44 support the stated distinction between finite three-dimensional energy and faster-than-model decay.

The focused external comparison supports this assessment. Mazzeo–Witten [3, §5] already give the knot identity and discuss its boundary terms, while their earlier no-knot uniqueness argument [4, §§2.5–2.6] assumes suitable decay at infinity. He's reduction theorem [5, Theorem 4.6] concerns a compact transverse Riemann surface and different asymptotic data. Dimakis [6, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.3] classifies already reduced solutions in specified asymptotic classes. These results do not supply the precise arbitrary-compact-group theorem with the longitudinal-shell hypothesis proved here. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority certification.

The scientific exposition is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the hypotheses, proof mechanism, inherited results, and remaining conjecture are distinguishable. I reproduced the supplied verifier: all 45 checks passed, with zero failures. These checks support the algebra, finite-dimensional indicial computations, and stated sample identities; they do not constitute machine verification of the analytic theorems or an exhaustive check over model labels. The manuscript correctly supplies the analytic arguments in the text.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the presentation without changing the main results:

1. **Distinguish actual flux from its majorant.** Proposition E(ii) and the abstract describe “exact orders” of the quadratic current and even identify its tube flux with a positive expression. Proposition 6.1 actually computes surface integrals of $w^2/y$. These give upper bounds for the current flux, which can vanish identically, for example when $a=0$. State the exact formulas for the majorant and upper bounds for the current; convergence thresholds for this majorant do not establish necessity for uniqueness.
2. **Limit the indicial interpretation.** The discussion in §6.3 should explicitly restrict the quoted knot-root and polyhomogeneity conclusions to the groups, models, and gauges covered by those references. They do not automatically establish the global product bound (1.1) for every compact group or every reference satisfying (R1)–(R2). The formal theorems already avoid this problem by assuming that bound. Preserve that distinction when saying that infinity is the “only substantive hypothesis.”
3. **Keep the contribution prominent.** The gauge-limit lemma and Theorem B deserve emphasis over the repeated benchmark outputs. Move the isolated rational-point coefficient in §3 and similar verification details into the supplement. This is an editorial improvement, not a clarity barrier to acceptance.
4. **Make conventions and attribution easier to locate.** Define $\nabla$ explicitly as the ordinary flat derivative in the chosen representative when introducing $\mathcal E$ and (1.1). Supply a bibliographic identifier for the unnamed companion preprint, and replace the alphanumeric citation keys with consistent numbered references. The informative abstract can also be shortened by moving the verification count and spectral lists to the main text.
