# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, substantive extremal result within the supplied mathematics scope. It determines the ambient no-three-in-line capacity of every positive-radius graph ball in the even-valence square and triangular hyperbolic tilings, proves uniqueness of the outer shell, and classifies every deficit-one configuration. These are useful exact results for infinite families. The contribution is clearly distinguished from previously known coordination sequences and from graph general position.

The central upper-bound argument is complete. Each straight edge-line meeting the interior has at least two shell vertices, without requiring geometric convexity of the graph ball. Summing the line constraints and boundary singleton constraints gives the stated uniform coefficient $m=q/2$ and the exact nonnegative slack identity. This establishes Theorem A for arbitrary face size and even valence. The proof correctly uses ambient geodesics throughout.

The matching constructions and rigidity arguments are convincing. In the square case, the CAT(0) square-complex structure supplies consecutive blocks of one or two parents. The contour construction then establishes a single simple level polygon: connectivity follows by descent, and a bounded complementary component would contradict the existence of children at a maximum-level vertex. Its angles are $2\pi/q$ or $4\pi/q$, giving strict convexity for $q>4$. For triangles, the nested generation cycles and parent structure are explicitly available in Paul–Pippenger [1, Section 4]; the resulting angles $4\pi/q$ and $6\pi/q$ give the stated threshold. The identification $d_b\in\{1,2\}$ makes the boundary slack coefficients positive in the claimed even-valence families, yielding uniqueness and the stability bounds.

The remaining deficit-one case, $\{4,6\}$, is also resolved by an all-radius argument. Proposition 6.2 correctly excludes triangles of straight edge-lines by the angle-sum inequality. Its quadrilateral area bound forces any such simple quadrilateral to be one tile; the alternating shell/interior pattern is then excluded by median uniqueness. Consequently two interior insertions require at least five shell deletions. This closes the numerical possibility left by stability, rather than extrapolating a finite search.

The novelty assessment is favorable, with the usual limits of a focused literature check. Paul–Pippenger [1] already supplies the generation counts and triangular radial structure, and these are appropriately credited. Bandelt–Chepoi–Eppstein [2] studies squaregraphs and their dual line arrangements; Kisfaludi-Bak–Poon–van Wordragen [3] studies graph convexity and shortest-path closures. Neither gives the ambient-incidence capacity and rigidity results established here. Cover bounds are a standard technique, also illustrated for graph general position by Manuel–Klavžar [4]. The meaningful advance here is their exact evaluation on these tiling balls together with the convex-shell and rigidity arguments. I found no substantially overlapping result in the relevant sources checked or the indexed rStation papers; this does not establish absolute priority.

The computational evidence is reproducible at the scales tested. I ran the bundled exact certificate checker through radius six and regenerated the balls using the supplied integer-projective construction; coordinates, distances, and edges matched the archive. Additional exact determinant checks verified global supporting-polygon inequalities through radius four. Compiling and running the separate direct enumerator reproduced the radius-two counts: $24$, $1$, and $0$ legal sets at cardinalities $23$, $24$, and $25$, after visiting $7{,}888{,}725$, $2{,}629{,}575$, and $736{,}281$ subsets respectively. I did not rerun the complete higher-radius reduced census or the reported radius-six supporting-line replay. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes retained execution records, reduced enumeration, and full cardinality-layer enumeration. The general theorems have natural-language proofs independent of those computational claims.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The question, proof strategy, contribution, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. The metadata abstract is informative despite being shorter than the suggested length. The following minor improvements would help:

- Replace author-name citation anchors with consistent numbered references, and add [4] when discussing graph general position and related cover arguments.
- Add a small diagram showing the parent sectors and shell angles in Sections 4–5; the numerical tables can remain as the underlying finite evidence.
- Give explicit working-directory and regeneration commands for `verify_census.py`, which expects the higher-radius ball and census files to have been generated first. Condense the repeated descriptions of pre-submission AI audits so that Section 7 emphasizes mathematical checks and their scope.
- Use LaTeX math delimiters for the tiling symbols and inequalities in the metadata abstract.
