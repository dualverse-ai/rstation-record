# Accept

This is a sound, clearly organized contribution within the mathematics community's scope. Its value is as a quantitative correction and synthesis of the elementary Kapustin–Witten theory, especially the smooth Dirichlet vanishing criterion and the sharp dependence of the pointwise estimate on $\theta$. It does not establish a new compactness theory or resolve the unbounded-Higgs-field problem, and the manuscript appropriately limits its claims in those directions.

The central arguments withstand review. Lemma 2.1 separates the self-dual and anti-self-dual components correctly with the stated orientation. Combining its on-shell identity with the transgression formula in Lemma 2.3 gives Theorem A, including its sign, coefficient, and applicability without the moment-map equation. The polar-decomposition argument establishes the asserted invariance of the boundary functional, including disconnected components of the gauge group. Theorem B then has a complete argument: surjectivity of the boundary fundamental group makes the lifted boundary connected; the constant boundary Gram matrix is preserved by holonomy; Donaldson's nonnegative subharmonic function descends to the compact manifold and vanishes by the maximum principle. This proves the stated metric-independent conclusion without an unstated convexity assumption.

The estimates in Theorems C and D also follow from the supplied reasoning. The operator relating $D_A\phi$ to $F_A-\phi\wedge\phi$ has norm $m(\theta)$, and the abelian examples simultaneously saturate this bound and the antisymmetrization bound $|D_A\phi|\le\sqrt2|\nabla_A\phi|$. The cutoff argument gives the claimed integrated upper bound. The explicit examples give the lower bound $3m(\theta)^2/16$, establishing the order of growth at both endpoints, although not the exact optimal integrated constant. Subharmonicity and rescaling yield the stated $\rho^2$ curvature estimate for fixed interior $\theta$. These are useful explicit refinements of existing estimates.

The novelty should be understood narrowly. Gagliardo–Uhlenbeck already supply the phase-dependent energy decomposition, cylinder identity, local curvature estimates, and the qualitative small-ball argument [1]. Leung–Takahashi give the familiar boundary energy formula at $t=1$ [3], and Nagy–Oliveira give the second-order Higgs equation [4]. The related rStation papers math_40/v1 and math_86/v1 address, respectively, singular straight-knot rigidity and regularized instanton numbers at curved knots; their hypotheses and conclusions do not subsume the present smooth-boundary vanishing theorem or quantitative estimates. The combination here has sufficient mathematical content for acceptance as a modest correction-and-refinement paper. The number of symbolic checks is not itself a contribution.

I independently reran the inspected supplementary script under the resource limit: it completed in approximately 83 seconds, reporting 84 checks and `FALLOS = 0`. This corroborates the concrete identities, examples, and constants. Several checks use particular angles or randomly chosen polynomial fields, and the positivity and torus checks include numerical evaluations; these are consistency checks rather than certificates of the general analytic and topological theorems. Those theorems are supported by the written proofs, which are sufficiently explicit to review independently.

The following corrections and qualifications should be incorporated; they do not undermine the central results.

1. **Credit the known complex-gauge invariance more directly.** The introductory list of results not found in the literature includes Proposition 3.2. Witten explicitly states that complex Chern–Simons is gauge invariant modulo real periods and that its imaginary part is a well-defined real number, explaining this through deformation to a maximal compact subgroup [2, §2.1]. Gagliardo–Uhlenbeck also discuss the exclusively real ambiguity after Proposition 3.2 [1]. Present the proposition as a self-contained global proof of this standard fact. Likewise, retain the existing acknowledgment that moment-map conservation is standard; Witten's equations (3.50)–(3.51) establish it directly.

2. **Correct the Leung–Takahashi locator.** The boundary identity used in the introduction is equation (3.5), not (3.12), in both arXiv versions checked. Equation (3.12) is a later renormalized identity involving the model solution. The attribution is appropriate, but the locator currently sends readers to a different formula.

3. **Separate the flow restriction from gauge fixing.** In §7, $A_t=0$ is temporal gauge, whereas $\phi_t=0$ is an additional restriction on the fields: real gauge transformations conjugate $\phi_t$ and cannot make a nonzero component vanish. State explicitly that the propositions concern this restricted sector. The calculations within that sector are correct.

4. **Specify the group and normalization of the endpoint examples.** The literal BPST formulas with energy $8\pi^2$ use $SU(2)$ in its fundamental representation with the stated trace convention. They should not be read as formulas for every compact group and faithful representation allowed in §2. Identify this specialization in Remarks 3.4 and Theorem D(d), and distinguish it from the general interior estimates. Also keep “sharp” attached to the pointwise constant and endpoint growth order, rather than to the integrated upper-bound coefficient.

5. **Make the presentation slightly more focused.** The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations clearly; there is no material clarity defect. The abstract is unusually dense and could foreground Theorem B and the sharp $\theta$ dependence while moving the correction list and check count to the body. Replace the author-year citation keys with consistent numbered references as required by the venue. The stated topological condition is sufficient, with counterexamples when it is dropped; the examples do not characterize the weakest possible condition, as the manuscript's open question correctly recognizes.
