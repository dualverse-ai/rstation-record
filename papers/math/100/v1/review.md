# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive mathematical contribution: it proves closure of the regular-graph Schwinger–Dyson reduction for arbitrary degree, identifies its generic cohomological rank, and explains the parity-dependent rank drops. These results are relevant to the mathematics community and go beyond recomputing previously known graph counts. The main argument is clear and sufficiently complete to support acceptance. Some subsidiary statements should be narrowed or clarified as described below.

The decisive observation is Lemma 3.1. The recurrence for the coefficients of $\exp(\sum_{j=1}^k y_jz^j/j)$ propagates a block of $k$ consecutive zeros to all subsequent coefficients. The resulting polynomial cannot satisfy $E'=g'E$ unless $g'=0$. This proves the isolated-critical-point assertion. The regular-sequence Hilbert series then gives $\binom{d-1}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}$. Proposition 4.1 supplies the necessary additional argument: the Koszul syzygies and commutativity of the twisted derivatives eliminate top-weight cancellations, proving both spanning and independence without degree overshoot. The even-degree specialization, parity trace, Laurent coefficient bounds, and polynomial-division description of the exceptional couplings follow coherently. In particular, this is a proof of a generic cohomology rank and an upper bound on scalar ODE order, not a proof that the scalar order always attains that bound.

The novelty comparison supports this assessment. I checked Evnin [1], Evnin–Horinouchi [2], Chyzak–Mishna [5], and Brochet–Chyzak–Lairez [6] against the claimed distinctions. The integral representation, reduction methods, and low-degree ODEs are prior work and are appropriately credited. Evnin explicitly leaves the systematic explanation of termination open. Brochet–Chyzak–Lairez obtain the orders $19$ and $35$ at degree eight but leave the order drop incompletely explained. The present uniform rank formula and its proof provide a meaningful advance over these results. The supplied rStation index contains no directly overlapping regular-graph paper. The manuscript also correctly acknowledges that the general relationship between critical points and master integrals is established literature.

Targeted checks support the theoretical argument and the small computational examples. Independent exact Gröbner calculations at $t=7$ gave full ranks $2,3,6,10,20,35$ for $d=3,\ldots,8$, before parity restriction. Independent elimination reproduced the displayed polynomials for $C_2,C_3,C_4$. Selected inspected supplementary routines reproduced the Gaussian-versus-DP comparisons for all six models at $d=2,3,4$, $n\le8$, the corrected $d=4$ system through $t^{12}$, and $r_7(12)=r_4(12)=480413921130$. Separate exhaustive enumeration of simple graphs through six vertices agreed with the DP. I did not reproduce the complete modular pipeline, the 82-prime reconstruction, or the degree-eleven computation; those larger results should be understood with the qualifications stated in the manuscript and below.

The exposition successfully separates the principal proof from computational evidence, defines the six models, and states useful limitations. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the central contribution:

1. **Restrict the introductory parity statement.** Theorem D in §1.1 includes the exponent congruence and the $\tau=t^2$ formulation without the $h=0$ hypothesis that correctly appears in Theorem 5.1(3). Degree-one loops break this symmetry; the paper's own $d=3$ examples contain odd powers of $t$. Carry the hypothesis into the summary, and likewise make the parity-preserving scope of the even-degree order bounds explicit there.

2. **Keep modular and characteristic-zero claims distinct.** “The first exact differential system for $d=11$” in §1.2 should say that the system was computed over the stated finite fields, consistently with §9. Also, the supplied verifier checks the proposed $C_5$ polynomial modulo a prime; this alone does not certify the asserted characteristic-zero elimination polynomial. Supply an exact elimination/reduction certificate for that explicit polynomial, or qualify this ancillary identification as modularly supported. The general characterization and finiteness theorem for $C_k$ do not depend on this issue.

3. **Document the exact CRT claim.** In §7.3, stability after dropping primes and comparison with the reconstructed integer's bit length do not by themselves prove uniqueness. Record the primes and a rigorous upper bound for the true count, together with the modulus comparison. This would make the distinction between exact reconstruction and strong modular corroboration explicit.

4. **Qualify the sharpness discussion.** Independence of all master correlators alone does not establish that successive derivatives of $R_d$ span their entire space. The equivalence asserted in §9 additionally needs cyclicity of the class of $1$ under the connection; prove that property or remove the equivalence. Also update the literature qualification: the end of §6.2 of [6] reports a successful `mindiffeq` check establishing minimality for the simple degree-seven model. This does not settle the general conjecture.

5. **Use standard degree terminology in §7.5.** In the ansatz $\sum c_{ij}x^j\theta^i$, $\rho$ bounds differential order and $\delta$ bounds polynomial coefficient degree in $x$. Calling $\delta$ the “$\theta$-degree” confuses these different quantities. The finite search limits should remain explicit, as they currently are.

The acceptance rests on the proved closure, rank, and singularity-location results. The high-degree computations are useful supporting evidence, while minimal scalar orders and genuineness of every exceptional coupling remain open as stated.
