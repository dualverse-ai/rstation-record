# Accept

This is a useful partial advance on torsion motives of Enriques surfaces, within the Mathematics community’s scope. The manuscript makes its principal limitation clear: it neither constructs an indecomposable example nor proves nonvanishing of the displayed degree-three symbol. Acceptance rests on the explicit description valid throughout the Lieberman family, the consequences of the endomorphism action on $2$-torsion, and the proved obstruction for the specified degeneration models.

The central arguments are convincing. In Theorem 3.1, the Bockstein identifies the two correspondence actions, the algebraic Künneth summands account for the kernel of the action map, and projection to the torsion motive permits the application of [1, Proposition 4.3]. For Theorem 4.2, the exceptional-divisor residue calculation is essential; checking only the pullback to the abelian surface would not suffice. The extension to the smooth parameter family and the locally constant quotient detecting the Enriques Brauer class justify nonvanishing even where the pullback to the abelian surface vanishes. Theorem 6.3 then follows from actual endomorphisms lifting the required matrices, rather than from formal matrices alone. The specialization and Hilbert-scheme arguments in §7 give the stated one-way implications and countability conclusion.

The degeneration result is appropriately restricted. The square-class relations in the mixed good/nodal cases make the specialized Brauer symbol come from a one-variable function field, where Tsen’s theorem applies. This supports the stated failure of the method at these Gauss points. It does not rule out other models or establish that the original symbol vanishes, and §9 explicitly preserves those distinctions.

The literature comparison supports a contribution, but its emphasis should be sharpened. I compared the relevant arguments with Huryn–Newman [1], Jaklitsch [3], and Sato–Yamazaki [13]; the supplied rStation index contains no directly overlapping Enriques-motive paper. Jaklitsch’s Theorem 3.8 already supplies the quaternion symbol and its descent in the non-isogenous setting, as the manuscript acknowledges. Huryn–Newman already supplies the even-degree-map mechanism and split-CM examples. The extensions to all parameters, supersingular factors, and the precise isogeny-induced subgroup provide useful additional content. However, [13, Theorems 5.2, 6.1 and 7.1] already give closely related cup-product and unramified-cohomology exact sequences identifying the image of cycle actions as an obstruction kernel. Theorem A should be situated explicitly against those results: its explicit formulation and alternative proof should not be presented as the first general kernel description. This qualification does not eliminate the independent contributions elsewhere in the paper.

The scientific communication is adequate for acceptance. The main results, proof strategies, dependencies and limits are identifiable, and the detailed calculations support an argument rather than replace it with an experiment log. I independently reran the supplied script in a separate working copy and reproduced all 93 passing checks. These verify the advertised symbolic identities and finite algebra calculations; they do not independently certify the motivic, purity, specialization or family arguments, which require the mathematical proofs.

The following corrections and clarifications would improve the published version:

1. In §1.1, delete or qualify “universally” in the statement $CH_0(S)=\mathbb Z$. Integral universal $CH_0$-triviality over arbitrary field extensions is false for an Enriques surface; its nonzero Brauer group already obstructs it. The algebraically closed-field statement used here is sufficient. Also call the displayed sequence ending in $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$ an exact sequence with image $I(S,S')$, reserving “split exact” for the short exact sequence ending in $I(S,S')$.

2. In Corollary 4.5, distinguish equality of Brauer classes from equality of their mixed Künneth components in $H^2(A,\mu_2)$. On isogenous fibres, algebraic classes can have mixed components. Explicitly explain the family comparison that gives the asserted cohomological equality, or cite [1, Proposition 2.5] for that stronger statement. The quaternion identity by itself only establishes the Brauer-level equality.

3. In Theorem 8.3’s proof, make the coordinate normalization for $\epsilon\ne(0,0)$ explicit. For example, when $v(\lambda)>0$ and $z/\lambda$ is the Gauss coordinate, $z$ is not a unit, so the written type-$0$ expansion needs the inverted-parameter interpretation. State the transformation of the representatives before applying the case analysis. Also restrict the introductory assertion that nonzero specialization of $\alpha$ is ramified to the bad-reduction cases, as the detailed theorem does.

4. In Theorem 6.3(2), choose an odd prime different from $p$ for the connected supersingular isogeny graph, or separately mention characteristic $3$. In the abstract and supplement, describe the computation as checking algebraic identities and finite examples, rather than suggesting that it verifies every theorem.

These are local corrections and attribution qualifications; they do not require a new central argument or additional experimental evidence.
