# Accept

The paper makes a useful local advance within the mathematics community’s scope. Its strongest new contribution is an explicit gauge-invariant curvature–Higgs functional with nonzero projection onto the four-dimensional obstruction space. It thereby replaces the slice forcing of [32] with a perturbation of the gauge-covariant equations. The smooth-model construction also gives a sound realization result, with the important limitation that its corner structures are transported from a finite-dimensional Morse model. It does not establish a naturally framed infinite-dimensional HW flow category.

The main arguments are convincing. In the quadrupole model, the exact quadratic expression in projection coordinates and the block-diagonal metric restriction preserve the coordinate great spheres throughout the interpolation. Lemma 3.2 then identifies the stable and unstable manifolds and proves transversality. Structural stability and the topology of broken trajectories supply the category comparison. I checked Bonciocat [14], specifically Theorems 3.7, 4.8 and 5.9: the cited classification really does cover arbitrary compatible stable normal framings after stabilization. Thus the use of $\widetilde{KO}(S^3)=0$, followed by the grading shift, supports the stated model spectrum $\Sigma^\infty(S^1\vee S^4)$.

For the new functional, gauge invariance follows from the invariant pairing of adjoint-valued fields. The integration-by-parts calculation and the identity $\sum_i E_iN_p^i=3\langle p,q\rangle$ give the stated projection. In particular, $I_\beta=\pi/16$ and $\|V_e\|^2=9\pi^3/8$ give raw forcing $\varepsilon e/12$. The first-order linear terms are bounded $Z\to X$, and the quadratic terms are controlled by the cited four-dimensional Sobolev estimate. The positive adjoint weight and the uniform sign of the forcing along the small AH trajectory establish nonzero Melnikov splitting for fixed sufficiently small $\bar\nu>0$. The argument does not rely on extrapolating the truncated numerical experiment.

The novelty should be stated narrowly. Papers [30]–[32] already establish the obstruction, centre reduction, perturbed sphere dynamics, Conley index and slice-forcing Melnikov mechanism. Qin [10, Theorem 4.1] already supplies a regular path to a locally trivial Morse field, and [10, Theorems 6.7 and 7.5] supplies the corresponding compactification comparison and transported smooth structures. Thus the general smoothing-by-topological-equivalence principle is established literature; the present interpolation is an explicit realization preserving the relevant invariant subsets. The curvature functional and its exact obstruction projection provide a substantive additional result beyond [32]. A focused arXiv check, including the original Anderson–Henningson work [2] and the relevant Morse/CJS literature, did not identify a prior treatment of that particular perturbation and calculation.

I reproduced all 46 Python checks, obtaining `FALLOS = 0`. They support the algebraic identities, coefficients and finite-dimensional illustration. They do not independently verify the imported centre-manifold theory, PDE regularity or framing theorem. Maple was not run. The manuscript generally distinguishes these kinds of evidence appropriately, and its organization makes the research question, proof strategy and limitations clear.

Several localized corrections would improve precision without changing the main conclusions:

- Restrict the tangent-bundle conclusion of Proposition 2 explicitly to regular connecting-trajectory moduli in settings (Q) and (H). The block reduction and equality of index classes are more general, but the wording “in Theorem 3” also includes the surviving obstructed arrow of Theorem 3(5). There its index is $-3$, whereas the parametrized trajectory family has dimension $1$. Surjectivity is indispensable for identifying the index class with the tangent bundle.
- In Remark 4.3, distinguish an index-bundle identification from a chosen trivialization: linearizations alone do not select compatible framings. The assertion that all interiors are contractible applies to the quadrupole components, not the height case, whose unparametrized moduli space is $S^2$. Keep the framing conclusion explicitly attached to the model, as elsewhere in the paper.
- Restore the parameter-dependent term $(\rho(\mu)-1)\mathcal D\xi$ in §1.2, or explicitly include it in the remainder. It is present in [31] and is implicitly accommodated by the later $O(|\mu|)$ estimates. Likewise, graph tangency at the joint parameter origin should not be stated as exact tangency at every fixed nonzero parameter in §5.
- Soften Remark 4.4’s claim that smooth corner structures require suitable rational eigenvalue ratios. Fractional powers can obstruct a particular gluing parametrization; this does not establish a general necessity statement about existence of smooth structures. The stated limitation—that compatibility with the natural HW interior structure has not been proved—is sufficient.

The acceptance concerns the explicitly limited smooth-model realization and gauge-invariant local symmetry breaking. It does not extend to the unresolved transition window, global HW theory, or compatibility of the transported framings with natural analytic gluing.
