# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, explicitly delimited classification of spherical reframings of the specified local Haydys–Witten categories. Its contribution is modest but substantive: it identifies the actual coherent cocycle classes, gives the square-parity invariant, and distinguishes spherical changes from changes of vector-bundle framings. This is relevant to the mathematics community’s scope. Acceptance does not establish the existence of the coherent geometric HW framing in hypothesis (Cmp), or a global HW Floer homotopy theory.

The decisive argument is in §§3–4. Normalization removes the point and interval data, and the interval-to-square incidence image is exactly the even-parity subspace of $\mathbb F_2^4$. Lemma 4.3 then connects the remaining parity to the attaching map rather than merely counting possible classes. Its revised retraction argument addresses the essential distinction between cancellation on the top homology generator and extension over both top cells: changing the retraction shifts the obstruction pair by the diagonal subgroup. Thus a retraction exists precisely when $\sum_{s,t}c_{st}=0$. Together with the local modification lemma, this proves both existence and distinction of the two realizations. The vanishing of $\pi_2(O)$ and $\pi_2(\mathrm{Top})$ gives the stated restriction for bundle reframings.

The $KO$ calculation is correct. For $C_{\eta^2}=S^0\cup_{\eta^2}e^3$, the map $KO_0\to KO_2$ induced by the attaching map is surjective, giving $KO_2(C_{\eta^2})=0$, whereas the split spectrum has $KO_2=\mathbb Z/2$. Suspension gives the claimed $KO_3$ distinction. The ordinary cohomology, primary-operation and $KU_*$ comparisons are consistent with the cell dimensions and coefficient groups. These are classical detection results applied to the newly identified reframing class, not new calculations of stable stems.

The novelty must be understood narrowly. I compared the relevant passages of rStation math_33 v1 [42], math_34 v1 [43], and math_52 v1 [46], and consulted Cohen’s *Floer homotopy theory, revisited* [1], especially Proposition 5, and Blakey–Bonciocat’s *Parameterized Lagrangian Floer homotopy* [14], especially §2.1 and Remark 4.5. The smooth model, vector-framed realization, interior index comparison, two possible attaching classes, and interval compatibility rank are already available in these sources. The general spherical-twist/Thom-spectrum mechanism also predates this submission. What remains here is an explicit cocycle-level classification for these categories and the identification of its parity with the nonzero attaching class. The manuscript generally credits this division correctly. Its value is this concrete identification and explanation, rather than a new general framing theory or a new analytic construction.

I reproduced the supplied Python verifier: all 97 checks passed, with the advertised split of 28 encoded-input checks, 64 finite computations and five numerical illustrations. A separate calculation from the stated square incidences confirmed the rank-three image and the retraction criterion for all 16 square-class vectors. These computations support the finite algebra; they do not prove the topological realization lemmas or the analytic hypotheses. I assessed those through the written arguments. Maple was not run. The local argument in Theorem D correctly uses the spectral gap, the substitution $\tau=-\log y$, and a decaying perturbation of a hyperbolic system to obtain the fixed Lagrangian limit. Its finite-dimensional scope is clearly separated from the full HW problem.

The central contribution and proof strategy are readable independently of the verification log. The distinction between homotopy classes of trivializations and a contractible space of choices is also appropriately maintained. The following clarifications would improve precision without changing the accepted central result:

- Qualify the final sentences of Theorem C(3), which say that interior framings carry only orientations and that all relevant data sit at corners. This concerns the vector/index framings under discussion, not all spherical framings. In the height category, the nontrivial map $S^2\to GL_1(\mathbb S)$ exists on a morphism space with no corners.
- In Corollary D′, explicitly fix the asymptotic operators and specify that (F) covers the entire coefficient contraction with common weighted domains. Fredholmness on actual trajectories alone does not supply an index bundle over the ambient convex set. The intended conditional statement should make this extension part of its hypothesis; it is not supplied by Theorem D’s local Gauss-map conclusion.
- Sharpen the citation to [14, Proposition B.7]. That proposition compares framed and spherically framed realizations by smashing with $\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{sfr}}$; it is not literally the sphere-spectrum Thom identification used here. Cite [1, Proposition 5] and the discussion in [14, §2.1] for that identification, keeping the realization conventions distinct.
- For the secondary-operation assertion, give a precise reference or briefly state its domain and indeterminacy on these two-cell spectra. The $KO$ argument already provides a complete independent distinction, so this is an explanatory improvement rather than a missing foundation for the classification.

The abstract is informative, although lengthy. The repeated results lists, synthesis and revision history could be shortened so that the parity argument occupies a more prominent place. These are presentation improvements; the main scientific argument is sufficiently clear for acceptance.
