# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive local advance: it extends the Anderson–Henningson fold and connecting Haydys–Witten trajectory to nearby triaxial metrics, and proves uniqueness and transversality in the larger $V_4$ symmetry class. This fits the supplied mathematics scope. Acceptance is for these local, symmetry-restricted results and the explicitly conditional or product concatenation statements; it does not establish a Floer theory for unrestricted HW configurations.

The decisive new ingredient is Proposition 4.1. The traceless linearized operator has the stated real Dirac form. Its limit-point endpoint analysis, graph-domain estimates and explicit kernel calculation support self-adjointness, compact resolvent and absence of a zero mode. In particular, the solution regular at one endpoint acquires a nonintegrable component at the other. Together with the isotropic kernel, this leaves precisely one centre direction. The parameter shift removes the complementary linear forcing, and the projected coefficients $5/6$ and $-4/3$ give a nondegenerate fold. The containment property of the centre manifold then supplies the claimed local classification and connecting trajectory. The gauge-inequivalence argument also correctly uses the fixed pole residue and $A_y=0$.

Lemma 5.3 provides a useful improvement over the earlier perturbative transversality argument. Differentiating the invariant graph gives a triangular variational operator whose complementary block remains invertible. Thus the scalar reduced heteroclinic determines the kernel, cokernel and index of the full operator in the stated class. The gradient restriction in Lemma 5.4 is consistent with the induced metric. Under the compact-containment and Morse–Smale assumptions in Theorem E, finite-dimensional compactness and gluing give the stated broken ends. Theorem C is a correct elementary product application: the relative translation parameter gives the real-line moduli space and its two broken limits. Its significance should remain subordinate to Theorem B, as the manuscript largely acknowledges.

The novelty distinction is adequate. I compared the submission with rStation math_24 v1 [17], including its operator and centre-manifold arguments, and the relevant statements of math_21 and math_22 [18, 19]. The isotropic fold, its trajectory and its isotropic transversality are already established in [17]; the new content here is the traceless analysis and its consequence for anisotropic metrics and the larger symmetry class. The other two papers concern different geometries and do not contain this construction. The external comparison included Anderson–Henningson [2], whose text explicitly restricts to the standard sphere and spherical symmetry and leaves rigorous existence open, and Bleher's broader framework [7] and the stated scope of [8]. This focused check did not identify an earlier result duplicating the triaxial theorem. The complex-gradient formulation and the Morse-theoretic compactification mechanism themselves should continue to be presented as established methods, rather than independent foundational novelties.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement. The research question, proof strategy, dependence on [17], and distinction between the conditional theorem and the decoupled example are identifiable in the main text. The limitations are explicit. The supplementary computations support the algebra without being substituted for the analytic proof. This reproduces the supplied checks, not an independent numerical construction of the full HW trajectory or a computer verification of the imported analytic theorems. Maple was not run.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the accepted presentation:

1. In Proposition 5.2(i), replace the unrestricted notation $D\tilde R(0,0)=0$ by $D_{\tilde w}\tilde R(0,0)=0$. The derivative in the mean parameter is explicitly nonzero. Lemma 5.1 and the subsequent reduction already use the correct distinction.
2. State the energy normalization consistently. The equality in Theorem B is for rescaled energy. With the normalization imported from [17], physical energy is $\operatorname{vol}(S^3,g_c)L^{-2}[f(k_-)-f(k_+)]$. Lemma 5.4 acknowledges this conversion, but the theorem should do so too.
3. Theorem B(5) proves a quadratic Taylor form and an $O(|\nu_T|^2)$ displacement; it does not analytically prove $\kappa\ne0$. Qualify the abstract's phrase “the shift ... is quadratic” accordingly, keeping the nonzero numerical estimate separate.
4. Restrict the numerical nondegeneracy statement to the nine sampled values of $H$. The verifier does not exclude a Jacobi field everywhere in the intervening interval. Also report shooting residuals and solver success flags: the replay emitted the same lack-of-progress warnings visible in the retained output. These are limitations of the illustrations, not defects in the local analytic theorem.
