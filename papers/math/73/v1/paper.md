# Supersymmetric quantum mechanics on Morse moduli sees the image of J and beyond: isotropy-twisted framings, mod-2 Dirac indices, a three-term analytic e-invariant in every stem 4k−1, and product moduli realizing ν² and σ² with a 2|1 refinement
Claude (Anthropic), operated by Juan Ospina

**Abstract.**

Witten's deformation of a Morse function discards the higher moduli spaces of gradient lines, whose Cohen–Jones–Segal framings carry the stable homotopy type. We show that $N=1$ (Dirac) supersymmetric quantum mechanics on these moduli spaces recovers the framings of symmetric moduli spaces throughout the image of $J$, and that products of moduli spheres reach beyond it. (A) Under a transitive compact symmetry, the Morse framing is the homogeneous framing twisted by the endpoint isotropy representations. (B) Mod-$2$ Dirac indices of moduli circles and tori detect $\eta$ and $\eta^2$ and give a $KO$-refined Witten complex. (C) In stems $4k-1$ the Adams $e$-invariant of a twisted framing is $\xi(D_g)-\int\mathrm T\hat A-c_A(k)\,\mathrm{SF}$, with $c_A(k)=-B_{2k}/4k$ and $\mathrm{SF}$ the spectral flow of moduli fermions coupled to the isotropy twist. (D) This gives $e'_{\mathbb R}=(j-1)/24$ on the moduli $3$-spheres of $\mathbb{HP}^n$ and $-1/240$ on the octonionic plane. (E) For the spinor clutching over $S^{4k}$, $e'_{\mathbb R}=B_{2k}/4k$ in every stem, carried by one Killing spinor. (F) The signature analogue sees exactly the odd part of $\mathrm{im}\,J$. (G) We give closed $\eta$-invariants of all homogeneous $S^3$ and $S^7$ of positive scalar curvature. (H, I, K) Framings of moduli $S^{4k-1}\times S^{4k'-1}$ are products of slice framings, since $\pi_{4(k+k')-2}(SO)=0$; symmetric product moduli obey selection rules in restricted isotropy data; an equivariant handle construction realizes $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$, invisible to every $KO$-index, between adjacent critical points. (L) A $2|1$ three-term formula replaces $c_A(k)$ by $c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)=c_A(k)+\sum\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$. (M) It yields the Laures $f$-invariant of product moduli, $c_1c_2\cdot\frac12((E_1^2-1)/12)^2$ resp. $c_1c_2\cdot\frac12((E_4-1)/240)^2$, certified $2$-adically nonzero. Homotopy conclusions, analytic tools and von Bodecker's product formula are known; new are the moduli-space realizations, uniform evaluations, selection rules and explicit formulas. `verify.py`: 615 checks, FALLOS = 0.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 What Witten's complex discards

In "Supersymmetry and Morse theory" [Wi82] Witten considered $N=2$ supersymmetric quantum mechanics on a closed Riemannian manifold $M$, with supercharge $d_t=e^{-tf}de^{tf}$ on $\Omega^\bullet(M)$. As $t\to\infty$ the low-lying states localize at the critical points of $f$, and tunnelling along isolated gradient lines (instantons) gives the Morse differential [HS85, BZ92]. The higher moduli spaces $\mathcal M(x,z)$ of gradient lines, with $|x|-|z|\ge2$, play no role in the differential. They are compact manifolds with corners carrying coherent stable framings, and by Franks, Cohen–Jones–Segal and Abouzaid–Blumberg [Fr79, CJS95, AB24] the framed flow category determines the stable homotopy type. The simplest example is $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with its perfect Morse function. Its Witten complex $\mathbb Z,0,\mathbb Z,0,\mathbb Z$ is that of $S^2\vee S^4$; what distinguishes the two is the framing of the circle $\mathcal M(p_2,p_1)$, which represents $\eta\in\pi_1^s$.

### 1.2 The proposal

Collective-coordinate quantization of a family of instantons gives a quantum mechanics on the moduli space. In Witten's $N=2$ theory the fermion zero modes are complex and the effective theory on $\mathcal M(x,z)$ is again de Rham; its index $\chi(\mathcal M(x,z))$ vanishes on every closed odd-dimensional moduli space. We quantize the moduli spaces instead with **$N=1$ supersymmetry**: Majorana fermions, with the Dirac operator of the spin structure induced by the framing as supercharge. Its $KO$-valued index is the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro image of the framed class [ABS64, AS71]. In dimensions $1$ and $2$ the unit map $\pi^s_*\to KO_*$ is an isomorphism, so the index detects $\eta$ and $\eta^2$. In dimensions $4k-1$ it vanishes, and the framed class (which lies in $\mathrm{im}\,J$ for the moduli spaces we study) has to be read from secondary spectral data of the same operator. The paper has one axis: **local supersymmetric data at the endpoints (isotropy representations of the symmetry on the oscillator vacua) determine the framings of symmetric moduli spaces, and the $N=1$ moduli quantum mechanics reads them off — through mod-$2$ indices in stems $1,2$ and through spectral asymmetry, a local counterterm and an endpoint spectral flow in stems $4k-1$.** Beyond $\mathrm{im}\,J$ the same endpoint data still determine the framings of product moduli, and a first step towards a $2|1$ theory on the moduli spaces detects $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$, which no $KO$-index sees (§9).

### 1.3 Main results

Throughout, $\mathcal M(x,z)$ is the space of unparametrized downward gradient lines from $x$ to $z$, $V_x$ the unstable space at $x$, and $\Phi_{x,z}:T\mathcal M(x,z)\oplus\mathbb R\oplus V_z\cong V_x$ the Morse framing (§2.1). Conventions for $e$-invariants, transgressions and spectral flow are fixed in §2.4–2.5 and used uniformly; in particular $e_{\mathbb C}=\hat A[W,\varphi]\bmod\mathbb Z$, $e'_{\mathbb R}=e_{\mathbb C}/a_k$ with $a_k=2$ for $k$ odd and $1$ for $k$ even, $c_A(k)=-B_{2k}/4k$, and $\mathrm{SF}$ counts net crossings from negative to positive.

**Theorem A (isotropy-twisted framing; §3).** Let a compact Lie group $G$ act isometrically on $(M,g)$ preserving $f$, let $x,z$ be fixed critical points with $W^u(x)\pitchfork W^s(z)$ and $\mathcal M(x,z)$ closed, and let $\rho_x,\rho_z$ be the isotropy representations on $V_x,V_z$.
1. If $G$ acts transitively on $\mathcal M(x,z)$ with stabilizer $H=G_u$, then $\Phi_u$ is $H$-equivariant and $\Phi_{gu}\circ(dg\oplus1\oplus\rho_z(g))=\rho_x(g)\circ\Phi_u$.
2. If the action is free, $[\mathcal M(x,z),\Phi]$ is the class of the left-invariant framing of $G$ twisted by $g\mapsto\rho_x(g)\oplus\rho_z(g)^{-1}$. For $G=U(1)$ it is $(1+a)\eta$ with $a=\sum\mathrm{wt}(V_x)-\sum\mathrm{wt}(V_z)\bmod2$.
3. If $\mathcal M(x,z)=S(U)$ equivariantly for an orthogonal representation $U$, the Morse framing is $h\cdot(\varphi_b\oplus\Phi_u)$ with $h(gu)=\rho_x(g)\Phi_u(\rho_U\oplus\rho_z)(g)^{-1}\Phi_u^{-1}$, and the degree $W(h)$ that classifies $[h]\in\pi_{4k-1}(SO)$ is computed at one point from Lie-algebra data (Proposition 3.3).

**Theorem B ($\eta$ and $\eta^2$ from mod-$2$ Dirac indices; §4).**
1. For the standard functions on $\mathbb{RP}^n$ and $\mathbb{CP}^n$, $\mathcal M(p_j,p_{j-1})\cong S^0,S^1$ has class $1+(-1)^j$, resp. $(1+j)\eta$.
2. *(Selection rule.)* In the situation of Theorem A with $G=U(1)$, the $KO_1$-valued Witten index of the $N=1$ moduli quantum mechanics is $\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker Q\bmod2=1+a$, and $\mathrm{spec}(-iQ)=\frac{2\pi}\ell(\mathbb Z+\frac a2)$ on a moduli circle of length $\ell$. The moduli fermions are periodic exactly when the spin lifts of $\rho_x$ and $\rho_z$ have spinor weights of the same integrality.
3. *($KO$-refined Witten complex.)* For a framed flow category with all gradings even, $\delta_\eta(x,z):=\mathrm{ind}_{KO_1}$ ($|x|-|z|=2$) satisfies $\delta_\eta^2=0$, and $(\mathbb Z\langle\mathrm{Ob}\rangle\otimes KO_*,\eta\,\delta_\eta)$ is the first page $(E^1,d^1)$ of the coarse $KO$ Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence (its homology is $E^2$). If there are exactly two gradings and they are $2k$ and $2k+2$, it determines $KO\wedge|\mathcal C|$. For two even gradings further apart this fails: higher $KO$-attaching classes are invisible to $\delta_\eta$ (Remark 4.3).
4. For the Conley indices $\Sigma^m\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal O(k))$ the Witten complex is independent of $k$, while $\delta_\eta=k\bmod2$.

**Theorem C (three-term analytic $e$-invariant; §5).** Let $(Y,\Phi)$ be a closed stably framed spin $(4k-1)$-manifold with $H^{2n-1}(Y;\mathbb R)=0$ for $1\le n\le2k-1$ (e.g. a sphere), let $\Phi=h\varphi$ with $h:Y\to SO(N)$, and let $g$ be a metric with $\ker D_g=0$. Then
$$\hat A[W,\Phi]\equiv\underbrace{\xi(D_g)}_{\text{spectral asymmetry}}-\underbrace{\int_Y\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^{\varphi}\to\nabla^g)}_{\text{local counterterm}}-\underbrace{c_A(k)\,\mathrm{SF}\big(D_g\otimes\mathbb C^N;\,d\to d+h^{-1}dh\big)}_{\text{spectral flow of moduli fermions}}\pmod{a_k\mathbb Z}.\qquad(1.1)$$
In stem $3$, with $\varphi$ the left-invariant framing of $Sp(1)$, this reads $e_{\mathbb C}\equiv\xi+\frac1{24}\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^g)+\frac1{24}\mathrm{SF}$.

**Theorem D (worked stems; §6).**
1. *(Stem 3.)* If $Sp(1)$ acts freely and transitively on a closed moduli space, then for every left-invariant metric $g$ with $\ker D_g=0$ the first two terms of (1.1) (left-invariant reference) sum to $-\frac1{12}$ modulo $2\mathbb Z$, exactly on the triangle cone of positive scalar curvature; the spectral flow of $D_g\otimes V_m$ is the Dynkin index $m(m+1)(m+2)/6$, computed block by block; and $e'_{\mathbb R}=(\delta(\rho_x)-\delta(\rho_z)-1)/24$ with $\delta=\frac12\mathrm{SF}$. For $\mathbb{HP}^n$, $e'_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal M(p_j,p_{j-1}))=(j-1)/24$.
2. *(Stem 7.)* For $f=\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda X)$ on $\mathbb{OP}^2$ the gradient flow is $X\mapsto U_{e^{t\Lambda}}X/\mathrm{tr}(U_{e^{t\Lambda}}X)$, $f$ is Morse–Smale, and $\mathcal M(E_2,E_1)$ is a round $S^7=Spin(8)/Spin(7)$. Its Morse framing is the bounding framing twisted by right octonionic multiplication (via the triality section $a\mapsto(L_a,R_a,L_{\bar a}R_{\bar a})$), $\mathrm{SF}=1$, and $e=-\frac1{240}$, with the first two terms of (1.1) cancelling exactly on every homogeneous metric of positive scalar curvature.

**Theorem E (all stems $4k-1$; §7).** Let $E_k\to S^{4k}$ be the real positive spinor bundle and $F=\pi^*h-|v|^2$.
1. The moduli space between the two critical points is the round $S^{4k-1}=Spin(4k)/Spin(4k-1)$, and its Morse framing is the bounding framing twisted by Clifford multiplication $h_k(x)=c(x)c(u)^{-1}$. No section of $Spin(4k)\to S^{4k-1}$ is used.
2. At the induced metric the first two terms of (1.1) vanish, $\mathrm{SF}=a_k$, and $e'_{\mathbb R}=B_{2k}/4k$, a generator of $\mathrm{im}\,J$.
3. The spectral flow is carried by the trivial $Spin(4k)$-isotypic block: one Killing spinor coupled to its dual, with eigenvalue $(4k-1)(t-\frac12)$.
4. For arbitrary endpoint representations, $W(h)=-(M_k(\rho_N)-M_k(\rho_S))$ with $M_k(\rho)=\sum_{\mu}\mu_1\cdots\mu_{2k}$ the Pfaffian moment of the weights. For symmetric powers this is $\sum_jj^{2k-1}\binom{m-j+r-1}{r-1}$, which equals $m(m+1)(m+2)/6$ for $k=1$ and $1,16,127,688,\dots$ for $k=2$.

$\mathbb{HP}^2$ and $\mathbb{OP}^2$ are the cases $k=1,2$ of Theorem E (§7.5).

**Theorem F (signature; §8).** The $N=2$ secondary invariant $e_{\rm sig}=\eta_B-\int\mathrm TL$ satisfies $e_{\rm sig}\equiv-8e_{\mathbb C}$ on framed $3$-manifolds and $e_{\rm sig}\equiv-224e_{\mathbb C}$ on framed $7$-manifolds. On twisted spheres in every stem $4k-1$, $e_{\rm sig}=c_L(k)W(h)$ with $c_L=-2^{2k+1}(2^{2k-1}-1)c_A$, so it detects exactly the odd part of $\mathrm{im}\,J$.

**Theorem G (homogeneous $\eta$-invariants; §10).**
1. On $S^3$, the left-invariant metric $g_a$ has positive scalar curvature iff $a$ satisfies the strict triangle inequalities, and there $\eta(D_{g_a})=S(a_1^2,a_2^2,a_3^2)/(6a_1^2a_2^2a_3^2)$, $S$ Schur's polynomial; $\eta_B=-\frac{2S}{3a_1^2a_2^2a_3^2}$ for all $a$. On Berger spheres $\xi(D_T)=\frac{(T^2-1)^2}{12}-\sum_{R_{pq}<T/2}(p+q)$ for all $T>0$.
2. On $S^7$, every $Sp(2)$-invariant metric $g_x$ of positive scalar curvature has $\xi(D_{g_x})=P(e_1,e_2,e_3)/(17280e_3^2)$, and $\eta_B=Q/(135e_3^2)$ for all $x$, with explicit polynomials $P,Q$. Up to isometry, scale and orientation these are all homogeneous metrics. Special values: $\eta=\frac{11}{360}(T^2-1)^4$ (Berger), $-\frac{(\lambda^2-1)^4(4\lambda^2+3)}{2880}$ (squashed), $-\frac{76}{140625}$ (Einstein squashed).

**Beyond $\mathrm{im}\,J$ (§9).** In stems $6$ and $14$, where $KO$ and $\mathrm{im}\,J$ vanish, every invariant above is blind. Products of moduli spheres reach $\nu^2\in\pi_6^s$ and $\sigma^2\in\pi_{14}^s$, and the first step from the $1|1$ moduli quantum mechanics to a $2|1$ theory reads them. Let $x_{4k-1}=J[h_k]\in\pi^s_{4k-1}$ be the generator of Theorem E ($e'_{\mathbb R}(x_{4k-1})=B_{2k}/4k$, so $x_3=\nu$ and $x_7=\pm\sigma$). For $\mathcal M(x,z)\cong Y_1\times Y_2$ and a base point $u=(u_1,u_2)$, the **slice framing** $\Phi_1$ of $Y_1$ is the restriction of $\Phi$ to $Y_1\times\{u_2\}$, with the constant summand $T_{u_2}Y_2$ absorbed into the trivial part; likewise $\Phi_2$. $E_{2k}(q)=1-\frac{4k}{B_{2k}}\sum\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$, and $E_1,E_3$ are the Eisenstein series of $\Gamma_1(3)$ (§9.1). (We skip the letter J to avoid confusion with the $J$-homomorphism.)

**Theorem H (product rule; §9.2).** Let $Y_1,Y_2$ be closed manifolds with $\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(Y_1\wedge Y_2)=0$; for example $Y_i$ spheres of dimensions $4k_i-1$, where $\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(S^{4k_1-1}\wedge S^{4k_2-1})=\pi_{4(k_1+k_2)-2}(SO)=0$. Then for every stable framing $\Phi$ of $Y_1\times Y_2$, $[Y_1\times Y_2,\Phi]=\pm[Y_1,\Phi_1]\cdot[Y_2,\Phi_2]\in\pi^s_*$. In particular, if a closed moduli space of a Morse–Smale function is diffeomorphic to $S^{4k_1-1}\times S^{4k_2-1}$, the attaching map of the corresponding two-cell subquotient is $\pm$ the product of the classes of its slices. The hypothesis fails for $S^1\times S^{8m-1}$ and for triple products of $3$-spheres.

**Theorem I (symmetric product moduli and selection rules; §9.3).** Let $G=G_1\times G_2$ act as in Theorem A, and let $\mathcal M(x,z)=Y_1\times Y_2$ $G$-equivariantly, with $G_i$ transitive on $Y_i\cong S^{4k_i-1}$ and trivial on the other factor.
1. The slice framing $\Phi_i$ is the isotropy-twisted framing of the homogeneous $G_i$-sphere $Y_i$ with endpoint representations $\rho_x|_{G_i}$ and $\rho_z|_{G_i}\oplus\mathbf 1$. Hence $[\mathcal M(x,z),\Phi]=\pm c_1c_2\,x_{4k_1-1}x_{4k_2-1}$ with $c_i=-W(h_i)/a_{k_i}$, where $h_i$ is the slice twist relative to the bounding framing of $Y_i$ and $W(h_i)$ is computed at one point from the restricted Lie-algebra data (Proposition 3.3).
2. *(Free $Sp(1)^2$.)* If $G_1=G_2=Sp(1)$ act freely, then $c_i=\delta(\rho_x|_{G_i})-\delta(\rho_z|_{G_i})-1$, with $\delta(\rho)=\frac12\sum_{V_m\subset\rho\otimes\mathbb C}\binom{m+2}3$ as in §6.1. For the real irreducible representation with complexification $V_m\boxtimes V_n$ (resp. $2\,V_m\boxtimes V_n$ when $m+n$ is odd), $\delta(\cdot|_{G_1})=\frac{\epsilon(n+1)m(m+1)(m+2)}{12}$ with $\epsilon=1$ (resp. $2$). The class is $c_1c_2\,\nu^2$, nonzero iff both $c_i$ are odd.
3. *(Stems $6$ and $14$.)* For $k_1=k_2=1$ the class is $c_1c_2\nu^2$, for $k_1=k_2=2$ it is $c_1c_2\sigma^2$; both lie outside $\mathrm{im}\,J$ and in the kernel of every $KO$-theoretic invariant.

**Theorem K (realization; §§9.4–9.5).**
1. *(Equivariant realization.)* Let $G$ be compact, $V_x,V_z$ orthogonal representations with $\dim V_x=m$, $\dim V_z=r$, and $N\subset S(V_x)$ a closed $G$-invariant submanifold of dimension $m-r-1$ with a $G$-equivariant isomorphism $\psi:N\times V_z\to\nu(N\subset S(V_x))$ (diagonal action). Then there is a compact $G$-manifold $\mathcal H$ with boundary and a $G$-invariant Morse–Smale function $f$, maximal and regular on $\partial\mathcal H$, with exactly three critical points $p_0,z,x$ of indices $0,r,m$, all fixed by $G$, isotropy representations $\rho_z=V_z$, $\rho_x=V_x$, and $\mathcal M(x,z)=N$ with Morse framing induced by $\psi$. The flow is gradient for a $G$-invariant metric, and $\Sigma^\infty\mathcal H_+\simeq S^0\vee(S^r\cup_\alpha e^m)$ with $\alpha=\pm[N,\psi]$.
2. *(Join.)* If $(V_i,Z_i,N_i,\psi_i)$, $i=1,2$, are such data for $G_i$, then $(V_1\oplus V_2,\ Z_1\oplus Z_2\oplus\mathbb R,\ N_1\times N_2,\ \psi_1\ast\psi_2)$ are such data for $G_1\times G_2$, with $N_1\times N_2$ embedded at $|v_1|=|v_2|$, and $[N_1\times N_2]=\pm[N_1][N_2]$.
3. *(Examples.)* The join of two copies of the moduli data of $\mathcal M(p_2,p_1)\subset\mathbb{HP}^2$ gives a free $Sp(1)^2$-orbit $S^3\times S^3\subset S^{15}$ with $V_z=\mathbb R^9$ trivial, $c_1=c_2=1$ and class $\nu^2$. The join of two copies of the spinor-clutching moduli data over $S^8$ (Theorem E for $k=2$, the local model of $\mathbb{OP}^2$ near a projective line, §7.5) gives $S^7\times S^7\subset S^{31}$ with $G=Spin(8)^2$ and class $\sigma^2$. More generally, joins of $\mathbb{HP}^{j}$-moduli give $(j_1-1)(j_2-1)\nu^2$, and joins of the $\mathrm{Sym}^m$ spinor-clutching moduli of Theorem E(4) give $\nu^2$ iff $m_1\equiv m_2\equiv1\pmod 4$ and $\sigma^2$ iff $m_1,m_2\equiv1,3,5,7\pmod{16}$.
4. In these examples the Witten differential is zero and every $KO$-valued index of every closed moduli space (those not involving the minimum $p_0$) vanishes (Theorem B(3) does not apply: the gradings have different parity), while the stable homotopy type is not split.

**Theorem L ($2|1$ three-term formula; §9.6).** Let $(Y,\Phi=h\varphi)$ be as in Theorem C, $W$ a spin null-bordism, $R_n$ the Witten bundles ($\sum_nR_nq^n=\bigotimes_{n\ge1}\mathrm{Sym}_{q^n}(T_{\mathbb C}-\dim)$), $\Theta[W,\Phi](q)=\sum_nq^n\int_W\hat A\,\mathrm{ch}(R_n)(\nabla')$ the relative Witten genus, and $\Xi_g(q)=\sum_nq^n\xi(D_g\otimes R_n(TY\oplus\mathbb R))$.
1. $$\Theta[W,\Phi](q)\equiv\Xi_g(q)-\int_Y\mathrm T\Theta_q(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^g)-c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)\,\mathrm{SF}\big(D_g\otimes\mathbb C^N;d\to d+h^{-1}dh\big)\pmod{\mathbb Z[[q]]},$$
and $c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)=c_A(k)+\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$: one unit of moduli-fermion spectral flow at oscillator level $n$ weighs the divisor sum $\sigma_{2k-1}(n)$. At $q=0$ this is (1.1).
2. For $Y=S^{4k-1}$ and $W=D^{4k}$, $\Theta[D^{4k},\Phi](q)=\hat A[D^{4k},\Phi]\,E_{2k}(q)$ exactly. Modulo $\mathbb Z[[q]]$ this is the constant $e_{\mathbb C}$: a single moduli sphere carries no $2|1$ information beyond its $e$-invariant. For $k=1$ the identity $\Theta=\hat A\,E_2$ holds for every framed $3$-manifold, which gives the metric-independent relation $\Xi_g(q)\equiv\xi(D_g)E_2(q)$ mod $\mathbb Z[[q]]$, e.g. $\xi(D_g\otimes T_{\mathbb C})\equiv-21\,\xi(D_g)\pmod{\mathbb Z}$.
3. For $k\ge2$, $\Theta[D^{4k},\Phi]$ is a modular form of weight $2k$ for $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ with constant term a representative of $e_{\mathbb C}$ and integral higher coefficients: a canonical modular lift of the $e$-invariant. For $k=1$ it is only quasi-modular, and the level-$3$ combination $\frac12(3\Theta(q^3)-\Theta(q))=\hat A[D^4,\Phi]\,E_1(q)^2$ is the lift for $\Gamma_1(3)$.

**Theorem M ($f$-invariant of product moduli; §9.8).** Let $\mathcal M(x,z)\cong Y_1\times Y_2$ with $Y_i\cong S^{4k_i-1}$, slice framings $\Phi_i$, $e_i=e_{\mathbb C}(Y_i,\Phi_i)$ computed spectrally by Theorem C or Theorem L(1), and $m_1$ the lift of Theorem L(3). Then the $f$-invariant at level $3$ is
$$f[\mathcal M(x,z),\Phi]\equiv\pm(m_1-e_1)\,e_2=\pm\big(m_1(q)-m_1(0)\big)\,\Theta_2(0)\quad\text{in }\underline{\underline D}{}^{\Gamma_1(3)}_{2k_1+2k_2}\otimes\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,$$
which equals $c_1c_2\cdot\frac12\big(\frac{E_1^2-1}{12}\big)^2$ for $k_1=k_2=1$, $c_1c_2\cdot\frac12\big(\frac{E_4-1}{240}\big)^2$ for $k_1=k_2=2$, and $0$ otherwise. Here $m_1=\Theta_1$ if $k_1\ge2$, and $m_1=\frac12\big(3\Theta_1(q^3)-\Theta_1(q)\big)$ if $k_1=1$; the raw quasi-modular $\Theta_1$ must **not** be used for $k_1=1$: $(\Theta_1(q)-\Theta_1(0))\Theta_2(0)=\frac{E_2-1}{144}$ for $e_1=e_2=\frac1{12}$, which vanishes in the target since $\frac{E_2-1}{144}+\frac{E_4-1}{1440}\in\mathbb Z[[q]]$. The first two values are nonzero; we give explicit $2$-adic certificates. So the $f$-invariant, normalized by the $2|1$ partition function of the moduli spheres, detects $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$ on the moduli spaces of Theorem K.

## 2. Setting and conventions

### 2.1 Morse framings and flow categories

Let $f$ be Morse–Smale. Set $\widetilde{\mathcal M}(x,z)=W^u(x)\cap W^s(z)$ and $\mathcal M(x,z)=\widetilde{\mathcal M}(x,z)\cap f^{-1}(a)$ for a regular value $a\in(f(z),f(x))$. Transversality gives $0\to T\widetilde{\mathcal M}\to TW^u(x)|\to N_{W^s(z)}|\to0$ with $T\widetilde{\mathcal M}=T\mathcal M\oplus\mathbb R\nabla f$. Since $W^u(x)$ and $W^s(z)$ are cells, there are trivializations $TW^u(x)\cong W^u(x)\times V_x$ and $N_{W^s(z)}\cong W^s(z)\times V_z$ normalized to the identity at $x$, resp. $z$, unique up to homotopy. Splitting the sequence orthogonally gives the **Morse framing**
$$\Phi_{x,z}:T\mathcal M(x,z)\oplus\mathbb R\oplus V_z\xrightarrow{\cong}V_x .\qquad(2.1)$$
Equivalently, $\mathcal M(x,z)$ sits in the level sphere $W^u(x)\cap f^{-1}(a)\cong S^{|x|-1}$ with normal bundle $\cong V_z$; this is Franks's normally framed connecting manifold [Fr79]. The framings of [CJS95] and of the Morse flow category of [AB24] are built from the same sequences. We use two recalled facts.

**R1 (Franks [Fr79]; [CJS95, AB24]).** If $\{x,z\}$ is convex (no $y$ with $\mathcal M(x,y)\ne\emptyset\ne\mathcal M(y,z)$), then $\mathcal M(x,z)$ is closed and the corresponding subquotient of $\Sigma^\infty M_+$ (or of the realization $|\mathcal C|$ of a framed flow category) is $S^{|z|}\cup_\alpha e^{|x|}$ with $\alpha=\pm[\mathcal M(x,z),\Phi]\in\pi^s_{|x|-|z|-1}$. More generally, the connecting map between adjacent nonempty grading levels of $|\mathcal C|$ is the matrix of framed classes of the moduli spaces between them. The same holds for the Conley index of an isolated invariant set [Co78, CJS95].

**R2 ([CJS95, AB24]).** The framings are coherent: where $\partial\mathcal M(x,w)=\coprod_y\mathcal M(x,y)\times\mathcal M(y,w)$ without corners of codimension $2$, this is an identity of framed manifolds up to universal signs.

### 2.2 Low stems and the image of $J$

$\pi^s_0=\mathbb Z$, $\pi^s_1=\mathbb Z/2\langle\eta\rangle$, $\pi^s_2=\mathbb Z/2\langle\eta^2\rangle$, $\pi_3^s=\mathbb Z/24$, $\pi_7^s=\mathbb Z/240$. $KO_0=\mathbb Z$, $KO_1=KO_2=\mathbb Z/2$, $KO_{4k-1}=0$, and the unit $\pi_k^s\to KO_k$ is an isomorphism for $k\le2$. Two framings of $S^n$ differ by a map $S^n\to SO$; with $\varphi_b$ the bounding framing, $[S^n,h\varphi_b]=\pm J[h]$. In stem $4k-1$, $\mathrm{im}\,J\cong\mathbb Z/m(2k)$ with $m(2k)$ the denominator of $B_{2k}/4k$, and $e'_{\mathbb R}$ is injective on it [Ad66]. On $S^1=U(1)$ the left-invariant framing is $\varphi_b$ twisted by $g\mapsto g^{-1}$, an odd loop, so $[U(1),\mathcal L]=\eta$; it induces the periodic (non-bounding) spin structure. On $T^2$ the product framing is $\eta^2$, with spin structure $(P,P)$ of Arf invariant $1$.

### 2.3 The $N=1$ moduli quantum mechanics

For a closed spin $k$-manifold $Z$ the $Cl_k$-linear Dirac operator $Q_Z$ on real $Cl_k$-linear spinors is the supercharge of $N=1$ supersymmetric quantum mechanics [AG83]. Its kernel defines the $KO_k$-valued Witten index, equal to the image of the spin bordism class under the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro orientation [ABS64, AS71; LM89]. For $k\equiv1\pmod8$ it is $\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker Q_Z\bmod2$, and for $k\equiv2\pmod 8$ it is $\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker Q_Z^+\bmod2$, the Arf invariant for surfaces [At71]. On a framed moduli space we use the spin structure induced by $\Phi$.

### 2.4 $e$-invariants, transgressions, spectral flow

Let $(Y,\varphi)$ be a closed stably framed spin $(4k-1)$-manifold, $W$ a spin null-bordism and $\nabla'$ a connection on $TW\oplus\mathbb R^N$ equal to the framing connection $\nabla^\varphi$ near $Y$. Then $\hat A[W,\varphi]=\int_W\hat A(\nabla')$ is well defined modulo $a_k\mathbb Z$ (for $k$ odd the index of a closed spin $(8m+4)$-manifold is even), and Adams's invariants are [Ad66; Go12, (4.1), Thm. 4.6]
$$e_{\mathbb C}(Y,\varphi)=\hat A[W,\varphi]\bmod\mathbb Z,\qquad e'_{\mathbb R}(Y,\varphi)=\tfrac1{a_k}\hat A[W,\varphi]\bmod\mathbb Z.$$

**Proposition 2.1 (APS [APS75b]; [Go12, Thm. 4.6]).** For every metric $g$ on $Y$, with $D_g$ the Dirac operator for which the APS index theorem holds for the boundary orientation, and $\xi=\frac12(\eta+\dim\ker)$,
$$\hat A[W,\varphi]\equiv\xi(D_g)-\int_Y\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^g)\pmod{a_k\mathbb Z}.$$
Here $\mathrm TP(\nabla^0\to\nabla^1)$ is the transgression form, $d\,\mathrm TP=P(\nabla^1)-P(\nabla^0)$, additive along chains.

*Derivation (recalled).* For a product metric near $Y$, $\mathrm{ind}D_W=\int_W\hat A(\nabla^{g_W})-\xi(D_g)$, and Stokes gives $\int_W\hat A(\nabla^{g_W})=\int_W\hat A(\nabla')+\int_Y\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^g)$. $\square$

For $h:Y\to SO(N)$ the framing $h\varphi$ has $\nabla^{h\varphi}=\nabla^\varphi+A$, $A=h^{-1}dh$, and along $\nabla_t=\nabla^\varphi+tA$ the curvature is $F_t=(t^2-t)A^2$. Define the degree
$$W(h)=\int_Y\mathrm T\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(d\to d+A)=\frac{(i/2\pi)^{2k}}{(2k-1)!}\int_0^1(t^2-t)^{2k-1}dt\int_Y\mathrm{tr}(A^{4k-1}),\qquad(2.2)$$
evaluated on the complexification. For $k=1$ this is $\frac1{24\pi^2}\int\mathrm{tr}(A^3)$, which is also the Chern–Simons transgression $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^{h\varphi})$ of $p_1=\mathrm{ch}_2$.

**Lemma 2.2 (flat transgressions).**
1. If $P=P_1P_2$ with $P_i$ invariant polynomials of positive degree, then $\mathrm TP(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^{h\varphi})\equiv0$.
2. $\log\hat A=\sum_kc_A(k)\mathrm{ch}_{2k}$ with $c_A(k)=-B_{2k}/4k$; so $\hat A_k=c_A(k)\mathrm{ch}_{2k}+$ decomposables, $\int_Y\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^{h\varphi})=c_A(k)W(h)$, and $\hat A[W,h\varphi]-\hat A[W,\varphi]=c_A(k)W(h)$. For example $\hat A_1=-p_1/24$ and $\hat A_2=\frac{7p_1^2-4p_2}{5760}=\frac{\mathrm{ch}_4}{240}+\frac{p_1^2}{1152}$.
3. For $Y=S^{4k-1}$, $W(h)$ is up to sign $\int_{S^{4k}}\mathrm{ch}_{2k}$ of the bundle clutched by $h$; on $\pi_{4k-1}(SO)$ its image is $a_k\mathbb Z$ [Bo59], so $|W|=a_k$ characterizes generators.
4. (Odd index theorem [APS76, Ge93].) If $H^{2n-1}(Y;\mathbb R)=0$ for $1\le n\le2k-1$ and $\ker D_g=0$, then $\mathrm{SF}(D_g\otimes\mathbb C^N;d\to d+A)=-W(h)$.

*Proof.* (1) The polarization of $P_1P_2$ at $(A,F_t,\dots,F_t)$ is a sum of terms with a factor $P_j(F_t,\dots)$, a polynomial in $\mathrm{tr}(F_t^m)=(t^2-t)^m\mathrm{tr}(A^{2m})$, and $\mathrm{tr}(A^{2m})=0$ for a matrix of $1$-forms by graded cyclicity. (2) Expand $\log\frac{x/2}{\sinh(x/2)}=-\sum_k\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k)!}x^{2k}$ with $\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbb C)=\frac2{(2k)!}\sum_jx_j^{2k}$, then use (1) and Proposition 2.1. (3) Clutching, Bott integrality, and the fact that complexification $\pi_{4k-1}(SO)\to\pi_{4k-1}(U)$ is multiplication by $a_k$. (4) The odd index theorem gives $\mathrm{SF}=-\int_Y\hat A(\nabla^g)\wedge\sum_n\mathrm T\mathrm{ch}_n(h)$. Each $\mathrm T\mathrm{ch}_n(h)$ is closed ($d\,\mathrm{tr}A^{2n-1}=-\mathrm{tr}A^{2n}=0$), hence exact for $n\le2k-1$ by hypothesis, and its product with the closed forms $\hat A_j(\nabla^g)$, $j\ge1$, integrates to zero. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (round spheres).** Let $\varphi_b$ be the bounding framing of $S^{4k-1}\subset\mathbb R^{4k}$. For every $O(4k)$-invariant polynomial $P$, $\mathrm TP(\nabla^{\varphi_b}\to\nabla^{\rm round})\equiv0$ pointwise, and $\xi(D_{\rm round})=0$.

*Proof.* $\nabla^{LC}\oplus\nabla^\nu=d+B$ with $B=x\,dx^T-dx\,x^T$, and $R^*B=RBR^T$ for $R\in O(4k)$. So $\mathrm TP$ is an $SO(4k)$-invariant top form, $c\cdot\mathrm{vol}$, and invariance under a reflection, which reverses $\mathrm{vol}$, forces $c=0$. The round spectrum is $\pm(d+\frac{4k-1}2)$ on $V_d^\pm$, which have equal dimensions [Bä96b, CH96]. $\square$

### 2.5 Conventions and sign reconciliation

All signs in this paper follow from Proposition 2.1 for the boundary orientation of spheres ($(e_1,\dots,e_{n})$ at $u$ is positive iff $(u,e_1,\dots,e_n)$ is), together with $\mathrm{SF}=$ net crossings from negative to positive. The following points, checked in `verify.py` P5, make the stems consistent.

1. *Normalizations.* In stem $3$, $e_{\mathbb C}=2e'_{\mathbb R}$ and $\hat A_1=-p_1/24$, $\mathrm{ch}_2=p_1$, so $-\int\mathrm T\hat A=+\frac1{24}\mathrm{CS}$ and $-c_A(1)\mathrm{SF}=+\frac1{24}\mathrm{SF}$: the stem-$3$ form $\xi+\frac{\mathrm{CS}}{24}+\frac{\mathrm{SF}}{24}$ of (1.1) is the general form. In stem $7$, $e_{\mathbb C}=e'_{\mathbb R}$ and $-c_A(2)\mathrm{SF}=-\frac1{240}\mathrm{SF}$.
2. *Spectral flow.* $\mathrm{SF}=-W$ is the odd index theorem. In stem $3$ it is confirmed independently by an explicit block count with the APS operator (Proposition 6.3): $\mathrm{SF}(V_m)=\binom{m+2}3=-W(V_m)$.
3. *The Dirac operator.* We never fix a Clifford convention a priori. In stem $3$ the explicit operator $D^{(-)}$ with $c(E_j)=-i\sigma_j$ is the APS one (Lemma 10.2); equivalently, the complex volume element $i^2c(E_1)c(E_2)c(E_3)$ acts by $+1$. In stem $7$ and in §7, $\eta$-values are derived from Proposition 2.1 itself, so no convention enters.
4. *Comparison with the literature.* For Berger spheres we obtain $\eta=+\rho^2/6$ on $S^3$ and $+\frac{11}{360}\rho^4$ on $S^7$ ($\rho=T^2-1$), whereas Hitchin [Hi74] and Weingart's generating function [Wei20] give $-\rho^2/6$ and $+\frac{11}{360}\rho^4$. The relative sign $(-1)^k$ is not an inconsistency: the complex and real volume elements in dimension $4k-1$ differ by $i^{2k}=(-1)^k$, so a convention normalizing one of them to $+1$ agrees with ours for $k$ even and disagrees for $k$ odd. We have not checked the cited conventions; we record this only as a consistent explanation of the pattern, and our signs do not depend on it.
5. *Generators.* With these conventions the twist of the $\mathbb{HP}^2$ moduli sphere relative to $\varphi_b$ has $W=-2=-a_1$ and that of the $\mathbb{OP}^2$ moduli sphere has $W=-1=-a_2$, so $e'_{\mathbb R}=\frac1{24}=\frac{B_2}4$ and $-\frac1{240}=\frac{B_4}8$, the cases $k=1,2$ of $e'_{\mathbb R}=B_{2k}/4k$ in Theorem E. The generator $J[l_g]\in\pi_3^s$ ($l_g$ left quaternionic multiplication, $W(l_g)=-2$) has $e'_{\mathbb R}=+\frac1{24}$, and $[\mathcal M(p_j,p_{j-1})]=(j-1)J[l_g]$ in $\mathbb{HP}^n$ when the symmetry acts on $q_j$. The opposite choice of acting coordinate reverses the identification orientation and the sign; only this global orientation choice is conventional.
6. *Pfaffian sign.* $M_k(\Delta^+)-M_k(\Delta^-)=1$ and $W(h_k)=-1$ on the complex module, so the universal sign in $W=\varepsilon'(M_k(\rho_N)-M_k(\rho_S))$ is $\varepsilon'=-1$.

## 3. The isotropy-twisted framing theorem

**Lemma 3.1.** In the situation of Theorem A, the trivializations of $TW^u(x)$ and $N_{W^s(z)}$ over compact invariant neighbourhoods of $\mathcal M(x,z)$ can be chosen $G$-equivariant, with $G$ acting on $V_x,V_z$ by $\rho_x,\rho_z$.

*Proof.* Let $K=W^u(x)\cap f^{-1}([a',f(x)])$, compact and $G$-invariant. Since the flow commutes with $G$, $r_s=\varphi_{-s/(1-s)}$ ($s<1$), $r_1\equiv x$, is a $G$-equivariant deformation retraction of $K$ onto $x$ (continuity at $s=1$ is uniform convergence of the backward flow on compacta). By homotopy invariance of $G$-vector bundles over compact $G$-spaces [Se68, §1], $TW^u(x)|_K\cong K\times V_x$ equivariantly; normalize at $x$. The same argument with the forward flow applies to $N_{W^s(z)}$. Nonequivariant normalized trivializations are unique up to homotopy, so these compute the Morse framing. $\square$

**Proof of Theorem A.** (1) For $h\in H$, $h\gamma$ is a gradient line with the image of $\gamma$, so $h\gamma=\gamma(\cdot+c)$, and $c=0$ since $f(h\gamma(0))=a$. Every ingredient of (2.1) is $G$-equivariant (Lemma 3.1, invariance of $g$, $f$ and the level set), so
$$\Phi_{gu}\circ(dg\oplus1\oplus\rho_z(g))=\rho_x(g)\circ\Phi_u.\qquad(3.1)$$
(2) For $H=1$ identify $G\cong\mathcal M$ by $g\mapsto gu$ and trivialize $T\mathcal M$ by left translation. By (3.1), the Morse framing is the left-invariant framing multiplied pointwise by $\rho_x(g)\oplus\rho_z(g)^{-1}$. Stable classes are additive under pointwise products and unchanged by constant conjugation, even by orientation-reversing matrices: $c_\kappa=c_{\kappa\oplus(-1)}\circ c_{1\oplus(-1)}$, the first factor conjugation by an element of the connected $SO(N)$, the second fixing $SO(N-1)$. For $U(1)$, a real representation is a sum of rotations $R(w\theta)$ and trivial summands, whose loop has class $\sum w\bmod2$ in $\pi_1(SO)$; with $[U(1),\mathcal L]=\eta$ (§2.2) the class is $(1+a)\eta$. For finite $G$ and $\dim\mathcal M=0$, (3.1) gives $\mathrm{sign}(gu)=\det\rho_x(g)\det\rho_z(g)\,\mathrm{sign}(u)$.
(3) is Proposition 3.2. $\square$

**Proposition 3.2 (homogeneous spheres).** Suppose $\mathcal M(x,z)=S(U)$ $G$-equivariantly, and set $\rho'=\rho_U\oplus\rho_z$.
1. $h(gu)=\rho_x(g)\Phi_u\rho'(g)^{-1}\Phi_u^{-1}$ is well defined and the Morse framing is $h\cdot(\varphi_b\oplus\Phi_u)$.
2. If $s$ is a section of $G\to G/H$, then $[h]=[\rho_x\circ s]-[\rho_U\circ s]-[\rho_z\circ s]$ in $\pi_*(SO)$; in particular $[h]$ does not depend on the admissible $\Phi_u$. Without a section, replacing $\Phi_u$ by $\Phi_uq$ with $q$ commuting with $\rho'(G)$ does not change $h$.

*Proof.* (1) Well-definedness is the $H$-equivariance of $\Phi_u$; in $\mathbb R^{\dim U}$-coordinates $dg^{-1}\oplus1$ at $gu$ is $\rho_U(g)^{-1}$ composed with $\varphi_b$, and (3.1) gives the formula. (2) $h\circ s=(\rho_x\circ s)\cdot\Phi_u(\rho'\circ s)^{-1}\Phi_u^{-1}$ is a pointwise product of $\rho_x\circ s$ and a constant conjugate of $(\rho'\circ s)^{-1}$; stable classes add under pointwise products and are invariant under constant conjugation (proof of Theorem A(2)). If $q$ commutes with $\rho'(G)$, then $\Phi_uq\rho'(g)^{-1}q^{-1}\Phi_u^{-1}=\Phi_u\rho'(g)^{-1}\Phi_u^{-1}$. $\square$

Sections exist for $S^1,S^3,S^7$ only; for the spinor clutching in all stems the independence of $\Phi_u$ is Lemma 7.2.

**Proposition 3.3 (base-point formula).** Let a group act transitively and isometrically on the round $S^{4k-1}$ and let $h(g'p)=\rho_1(g')h(p)\rho_2(g')^{-1}$ for unitary representations $\rho_1,\rho_2$. Then $\mathrm{tr}((h^{-1}dh)^{4k-1})$ is an invariant form and
$$W(h)=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{2^{2k-1}(4k-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{4k-1}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{tr}\big(B_{\sigma(1)}\cdots B_{\sigma(4k-1)}\big),\qquad B_i=(h^{-1}dh)_u(e_i),\qquad(3.2)$$
for a positive orthonormal basis $(e_i)$ of $T_uS^{4k-1}$. For $h$ as in Proposition 3.2, $B_i=\Phi_u^{-1}d\rho_x(\xi_i)\Phi_u-d\rho'(\xi_i)$ for any $\xi_i\in\mathfrak g$ with $d\rho_U(\xi_i)u=e_i$. The constant is $\frac1{12}$ for $k=1$ and $-\frac1{8!}$ for $k=2$.

*Proof.* $(h^{-1}dh)_{g'p}\circ dg'=\rho_2(g')(h^{-1}dh)_p\rho_2(g')^{-1}$, so the trace form is invariant, hence constant times $\mathrm{vol}$; combine (2.2) with $\mathrm{Vol}(S^{4k-1})=2\pi^{2k}/(2k-1)!$. Lifts differing by $\mathfrak h$ give the same $B_i$ by $H$-equivariance. $\square$

For a free $Sp(1)$-action with twist $g\mapsto\rho(g)$ on $Sp(1)$, (3.2) becomes $W(\rho)=\frac1{12}\sum\epsilon_{abc}\mathrm{tr}(d\rho(X_a)d\rho(X_b)d\rho(X_c))=\frac12\mathrm{tr}(d\rho(X_3)^2)$, using $[X_a,X_b]=2\epsilon_{abc}X_c$. Hence $W(V_m)=-m(m+1)(m+2)/6$ for the irreducible unitary $V_m$, $W(l_g)=W(r_{g^{-1}})=-2$ and $W(\mathrm{conj})=-4$ on $\mathbb H$, and $W(l_{g^{-1}})=+2$ (pointwise inverse of $l_g$). The left-invariant framing of $Sp(1)$ is $\varphi_L=l_{g^{-1}}\varphi_b$.

**Remark 3.4 (the twist is essential).** Without the twist, every free $U(1)$-orbit would represent $\eta$. For $\mathbb{CP}^1=S^2$, $\Sigma^\infty S^2_+$ splits, yet $\mathcal M(p_1,p_0)$ is a free orbit; the weight $-1$ on $V_{p_1}$ gives $a=1$ and class $0$. Likewise $\mathcal M(E_1,E_0)\subset\mathbb{OP}^2$ and $\mathcal M(p_1,p_0)\subset\mathbb{HP}^1$ have trivial twist and class $0$, while an untwisted homogeneous framing would give a generator. Côté–Kartal [CK23] construct genuinely equivariant Morse–Bott flow categories; Theorem A is a different kind of statement, computing the underlying non-equivariant framed class from equivariant local data. We did not find it in the literature.

## 4. $\eta$ and $\eta^2$ from mod-$2$ Dirac indices: proof of Theorem B

**Lemma 4.1.** On $\mathbb{KP}^n$ ($\mathbb K=\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H$, $d=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathbb K$) with the standard metric, the downward flow of $f=\sum\lambda_j|q_j|^2/|q|^2$ ($\lambda_0<\dots<\lambda_n$) is $[q_j]\mapsto[e^{-\lambda_js}q_j]$ up to time rescaling. $p_j=[e_j]$ has index $dj$, with $V_{p_j}=\{q_kq_j^{-1}:k<j\}$; $W^u(p_j)=\{q_k=0\ (k>j),q_j\ne0\}$ and $W^s(p_i)=\{q_k=0\ (k<i),q_i\ne0\}$ meet transversally; and $\mathcal M(p_j,p_{j-1})\cong\mathbb K^*/\mathbb R_+=S^{d-1}$ is closed.

*Proof.* On the unit sphere of $\mathbb K^{n+1}$ the gradient of $\langle Aq,q\rangle$ is $2(Aq-\langle Aq,q\rangle q)$, horizontal for the Hopf map ($\langle Aq,qu\rangle=\mathrm{Re}(\bar u)\sum\lambda_k|q_k|^2=0$ for imaginary $u$), which is a Riemannian submersion. The rest follows from the explicit flow; no index lies strictly between $d(j-1)$ and $dj$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem B(1).* $G=S(\mathbb K)$ acting by left multiplication on $q_j$ preserves $f$ and the metric, fixes all $p_i$, and acts freely and transitively on $\mathcal M(p_j,p_{j-1})$ by $w=q_jq_{j-1}^{-1}\mapsto gw$. In the chart at $p_j$ every coordinate $q_kq_j^{-1}$ is multiplied by $g^{-1}$ on the right, and at $p_{j-1}$ only the stable coordinate $q_jq_{j-1}^{-1}$ moves; so $\rho_{p_j}=\bigoplus_{k<j}r_{g^{-1}}$ and $\rho_{p_{j-1}}$ is trivial. For $\mathbb R$, Theorem A(2) gives signs $+1$ and $(-1)^j$. For $\mathbb C$, there are $j$ weights $-1$, so $a=j$ and the class is $(1+j)\eta$. (For $\mathbb H$ see Corollary 6.7.) By R1 these are the cellular differential of $\mathbb{RP}^n$ and the attaching map detected by $Sq^2x^{j-1}=(j-1)x^j$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (spin lifts).** $\rho:U(1)\to SO(V)$ with weights $w_i$ lifts to $Spin(V)$ iff $\sum w_i$ is even; the spinor weights $\frac12\sum\pm w_i$ are all integral or all half-integral according to that parity. (The lift $\theta\mapsto\prod_k(\cos\frac{w_k\theta}2+\sin\frac{w_k\theta}2e_{2k-1}e_{2k})$ ends at $(-1)^{\sum w_k}$.)

*Proof of Theorem B(2).* By Theorem A(2) the class is $(1+a)\eta$; $\eta$ corresponds to the periodic spin structure and $0$ to the antiperiodic one. On a circle of length $\ell$ with spin structure $s$, $Q=d/d\theta$ has $\mathrm{spec}(-iQ)=\frac{2\pi}\ell(\mathbb Z+\frac s2)$ and real kernel of dimension $1-s$. Lemma 4.2 translates $a\equiv0$ into equal integrality of spinor weights. The $N=2$ index is $\chi(S^1)=0$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem B(3).* If all gradings are even and $|x|-|w|=4$, intermediate $y$ have $|y|=|x|-2$, so $\mathcal M(x,w)$ is a compact $3$-manifold with boundary $\coprod_y\mathcal M(x,y)\times\mathcal M(y,w)$ without corners, and the circles $\mathcal M(x,y)$ are closed. By R2 and multiplicativity of the $KO$-index, $\sum_y\delta_\eta(x,y)\delta_\eta(y,w)=\mathrm{ind}_{KO_2}(\partial\mathcal M(x,w))=0$, since the index of a spin boundary vanishes. Filtering $|\mathcal C|$ by grading, R1 identifies the connecting maps with the matrices $(\pm[\mathcal M(x,z)])\in M(\pi^s_1)$, and $\eta$ acts on $KO_*$ by multiplication with $\eta\in KO_1$; so $E^1=\bigoplus KO_*$ with $d^1=\eta\,\delta_\eta$. With two gradings, the attaching matrix $B\in M(\mathbb Z/2\cdot\eta)$ can be brought to $\mathrm{diag}(1^r,0)$ by self-equivalences of wedges of spheres ($SL_n(\mathbb Z)\to SL_n(\mathbb F_2)$ is onto), so $|\mathcal C|\simeq\Sigma^{2k}(\bigvee^rC_\eta\vee\bigvee S^0\vee\bigvee S^2)$, and $KO\wedge C_\eta\simeq KU$ [Wo66]. Here the two gradings are $2k$ and $2k+2$, which is the hypothesis of the last assertion. $\square$

**Remark 4.3 (the hypothesis on the two gradings is needed).** Take a two-object framed flow category in gradings $0$ and $10$ whose closed $9$-dimensional moduli space represents Adams's $\mu_9$. Its realization is $C_{\mu_9}=\mathrm{cofib}(\mu_9:S^9\to S^0)$, and $\delta_\eta=0$ as for $S^0\vee S^{10}$. But the unit sends $\mu_9$ to $\eta\beta\neq0\in KO_9$, so $\pi_9(KO\wedge C_{\mu_9})=0$ while $\pi_9(KO\wedge(S^0\vee S^{10}))=\mathbb Z/2$. A complete $KO$ description for wider gradings must include the higher $KO$-attaching classes (compare open problem 3 in §12). We thank the referee for this example.

*Example.* For $\mathbb{CP}^n$, $\delta_\eta(p_j,p_{j-1})=1+j$ and $\delta_\eta^2=(1+j)j\equiv0$, the Adem relation $Sq^2Sq^2=0$ on even classes. For $S^2\cup_{a\eta}e^4$, $KO_3$ is $\mathbb Z/2$ for $a=0$ and $0$ for $a=1$, separating $S^2\vee S^4$ from $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

*Proof of Theorem B(4).* On $X_{k,m}=\mathcal O(k)\oplus\underline{\mathbb R}^m\to\mathbb{CP}^1$ with a $U(1)$-invariant metric and connection, $F=\pi^*f_0-|y|^2-|s|^2$ satisfies $\frac{d}{d\sigma}(|y|^2+|s|^2)=4(|y|^2+|s|^2)$ along $-\nabla F$, so bounded lines lie in the zero section, an isolated invariant set with critical points of index $m+2$ and $m+4$; the Witten complex has zero differential for every $k$. With $U(1)$ acting on $z_1$ and lifted to $\mathcal O(k)$ with fibre weights $(0,-k)$, $V_x$ has weights $-1,-k$ and $V_z$ weight $0$, so $a\equiv k+1$ and the class is $k\eta$, matching $Sq^2u=w_2u$ on $\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal O(k))$. $\square$

## 5. The three-term formula: proof of Theorem C

*Proof.* By Proposition 2.1 for $\Phi=h\varphi$ and additivity of transgressions,
$$\hat A[W,\Phi]\equiv\xi(D_g)-\int\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^g)+\int\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^{h\varphi}).$$
Lemma 2.2(2) gives $c_A(k)W(h)$ for the last term and Lemma 2.2(4) replaces $W(h)$ by $-\mathrm{SF}$; at $t=1$ the operator $D_g\otimes(d+h^{-1}dh)=h^{-1}(D_g\otimes1)h$ is invertible. In stem $3$ with $\varphi=\varphi_L$, $-\int\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^L\to\nabla^g)=\frac1{24}\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^g)$ by $\hat A_1=-p_1/24$. $\square$

**Remarks 5.1.** (a) *Reference framings.* The split between the first two terms and the third depends on $\varphi$: with the bounding framing of a round sphere both first terms vanish identically (Lemma 2.3), and the whole class sits in $-c_A(k)\mathrm{SF}$; with the left-invariant framing of $Sp(1)$ the counterterm never vanishes (Remark 6.6). (b) *Where the denominators come from.* The a priori denominators of $\hat A_k$ ($24,5760,967680,\dots$) never appear: on flat gauge paths only the primitive part $c_A(k)\mathrm{ch}_{2k}$ survives (Lemma 2.2(1)), so one unit of spectral flow weighs $|B_{2k}|/4k$. (c) *Supersymmetric reading (heuristic).* $\xi$ is the fractional fermion number of the moduli vacuum, the transgression a local counterterm, and $\mathrm{SF}$ the number of moduli-fermion levels that cross zero when the fermions are coupled to the endpoint vacuum charges $\rho_x\ominus\rho_z$ and a large gauge transformation is performed — an anomaly-inflow count. We prove nothing about the collective-coordinate derivation of the moduli quantum mechanics from a single theory on $M$ (§12).

## 6. Worked stems

### 6.1 Stem 3: symmetric moduli $3$-spheres and $\mathbb{HP}^n$

$S^3=Sp(1)$ carries left-invariant fields $X_1,X_2,X_3$ with $[X_i,X_j]=2\epsilon_{ijk}X_k$. For $a\in\mathbb R^3_{>0}$, $g_a$ makes $E_i=X_i/a_i$ orthonormal; $g_{(1,1,1)}$ is the round unit sphere and $g_T=g_{(T,1,1)}$ the Berger sphere. The orientation $o$ making $(E_i)$ positive is the boundary orientation of the unit ball of $\mathbb H$, since $(x,xi,xj,xk)=l_x(1,i,j,k)$. Put $R_{pq}=\sqrt{(p-q)^2/T^2+4pq}$, and let $S(x)=\sum_ix_i(x_i-x_j)(x_i-x_k)$ be Schur's polynomial.

**Lemma 6.1.** $[E_i,E_j]=\frac{2a_k}{a_ia_j}E_k$ for $(ijk)$ cyclic, and $\mathrm{scal}(g_a)=\frac{2(a_1+a_2+a_3)(-a_1+a_2+a_3)(a_1-a_2+a_3)(a_1+a_2-a_3)}{a_1^2a_2^2a_3^2}$. So $\mathrm{scal}>0$ iff the strict triangle inequalities hold; $\mathrm{scal}(g_T)=8-2T^2$. (Koszul's formula; `verify.py` P2.S1.)

**Lemma 6.2 (Peter–Weyl blocks).** With $c(E_j)=i\sigma_j$, the operator $D^{(+)}$ acts on $V_n\otimes\mathbb C^2$ (multiplicity $n+1$ in $L^2(S^3;\mathbb C^2)$) by $D^{(+)}_n=\sum_i\frac2{a_i}J_i^{(n)}\otimes\sigma_i+\frac{a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2}{2a_1a_2a_3}$. For $g_T$ its eigenvalues are $\frac T2+\frac{n+1}T$ (twice) and $\frac T2\pm R_{pq}$ ($p+q=n+1$, $p,q\ge1$) [Hi74]. Let $D^{(-)}=-D^{(+)}$. Then $D^{(\pm)}$ is invertible for $0<T<4$, with a $2$-dimensional kernel at $T=4$.

*Proof.* Left-invariant fields act on matrix coefficients by $d\pi_n(X_j)=-2iJ_j$; the zeroth-order term $\frac14\sum\Gamma_{ij}^kc_ic_jc_k$ is the stated scalar. Quantizing along $X_1$, $K=J_1+\frac12\sigma_1$ commutes with $D_n^{(+)}$; on $K=\pm\frac{n+1}2$ one gets $\frac T2+\frac{n+1}T$, and on $K=\frac{p-q}2$ a $2\times2$ block of trace $T$ and discriminant $R_{pq}^2$. $R_{pq}^2\ge4$ with equality only at $p=q=1$. (Characteristic polynomials checked exactly, symbolic in $T$; P2.S2.) $\square$

By Lemma 10.2 below, $D_g:=D^{(-)}$ is the APS operator for $o$. So for $0<T<4$ the APS operator has, on $V_n\otimes\mathbb C^2$, exactly $n+2$ negative eigenvalues.

**Proposition 6.3 (block-by-block spectral flow).** For $0<T<4$ the spectral flow of $D_{g_T}\otimes V_m$ along $d+t\rho_m^{-1}d\rho_m$ is $\mathrm{SF}=\binom{m+2}3=-W(V_m)$. Block $n$ contributes $(n+1)(m-n)$ for $n<m$ and $0$ for $n\ge m$.

*Proof.* The coupling is left-invariant, so the family preserves Peter–Weyl blocks: on $V_n\otimes V_m\otimes\mathbb C^2$ it is $-[\sum_i\frac2{a_i}(J_i^{(n)}+tJ_i^{(m)})\sigma_i+c_0]$. At $t=0$ there are $(m+1)(n+2)$ negative eigenvalues. At $t=1$, $J^{(n)}+J^{(m)}$ is the total angular momentum on $\bigoplus_kV_k$ ($k=|n-m|,\dots,n+m$ in steps of $2$), giving $\sum_k(k+2)$ negative eigenvalues; this equals $(n+1)(m+2)$ for $n<m$ and $(m+1)(n+2)$ for $n\ge m$. Blocks with $n\gg m$ stay invertible ($\|D_t-D_0\|\le C(m)$ while the gap grows linearly). Summing with multiplicity $n+1$ gives $\sum_{n<m}(n+1)(m-n)=\binom{m+2}3$. $\square$

For a real representation $\rho$ set $\delta(\rho)=\frac12\mathrm{SF}(D_g;\rho\otimes\mathbb C)=-\frac12W(\rho)\in\mathbb Z$. Then $\delta(l_g)=\delta(r_{g^{-1}})=1$, $\delta(\mathrm{conj})=2$, $\delta=\frac{m(m+1)(m+2)}6$ on the realification of $V_m$ ($m$ odd) and $\frac{m(m+1)(m+2)}{12}$ on the real form ($m$ even).

**Proposition 6.4 (counterterm).** In the left-invariant frame, $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{g_a})=-2-\frac{2S(a_1^2,a_2^2,a_3^2)}{a_1^2a_2^2a_3^2}$; in particular $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{g_T})=-2(T^2-1)^2-2$. Also $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^{\varphi_b}\to\nabla^{\rm round})\equiv0$ pointwise.

*Proof.* $\nabla^L$ is trivial in the frame $E_i$ and $\nabla^{g_a}$ has constant connection matrices $A(E_i)_{kj}=\Gamma^k_{ij}$; substitute into $\mathrm{CS}=-\frac1{8\pi^2}\int\mathrm{tr}(A\,dA+\frac23A^3)$ with $\int e^{123}=2\pi^2a_1a_2a_3$; the numerator factors through Schur's polynomial (exact, P2.S4). The last statement is Lemma 2.3, checked also as an identically vanishing density in coordinates (P2.S5). $\square$

**Theorem 6.5 (= Theorem D(1)).** Let $Sp(1)$ act as in Theorem A freely and transitively on a closed $\mathcal M(x,z)$, oriented by $o$ through $g\mapsto gu$. For every left-invariant metric $g$ with $\ker D_g=0$,
$$e_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal M,\Phi)\equiv\xi(D_g)+\tfrac1{24}\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^g)+\tfrac1{24}\big(\mathrm{SF}(\rho_x)-\mathrm{SF}(\rho_z)\big)\pmod{2\mathbb Z},$$
the sum of the first two terms is $-\frac1{12}$ (exactly on the triangle cone, modulo $2\mathbb Z$ in general), and
$$e'_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal M,\Phi)=\frac{\delta(\rho_x)-\delta(\rho_z)-1}{24}.$$

*Proof.* By Theorem A(2), $\Phi=h\varphi_L$ with $W(h)=W(\rho_x)-W(\rho_z)$; Theorem C gives the formula. On the triangle cone, $\xi(D_{g_a})=\frac{S}{12x_1x_2x_3}$ ($x_i=a_i^2$, Theorem G(1)) and Proposition 6.4 give $\xi+\frac1{24}\mathrm{CS}=-\frac1{12}$; off the cone the sum is unchanged modulo $2\mathbb Z$ by Proposition 2.1. Independently of any $\eta$-invariant: $\varphi_L=l_{g^{-1}}\varphi_b$, $W(l_{g^{-1}})=2$, and $\xi(D_{\rm round})=0=\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^{\varphi_b}\to\nabla^{\rm round})$, so $e_{\mathbb C}(\varphi_L)=c_A(1)\cdot2=-\frac1{12}$; the two routes agree because $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{\rm round})=-2=-W(l_{g^{-1}})$. Hence $e_{\mathbb C}=\frac{-1+\delta_x-\delta_z}{12}$. $\square$

**Remark 6.6 (no purely spectral gauge).** By Schur's inequality $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{g_a})\le-2$ on the whole octant, so no left-invariant metric kills the counterterm; on the triangle cone spectral asymmetry and counterterm compensate exactly, $\frac1{24}\mathrm{CS}=-\frac1{12}-\xi$.

**Corollary 6.7 ($\mathbb{HP}^n$).** Let $Sp(1)$ act by left multiplication on the coordinates in a set $S$ with $j\in S\not\ni j-1$. Then $e'_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal M(p_j,p_{j-1}))=\frac{j-1}{24}$, independently of $S$; if $j-1\in S\not\ni j$ the value is $-\frac{j-1}{24}$ (the action $w\mapsto wg^{-1}$ reverses the identification orientation). This agrees with the Chern-character invariant $e_{\mathbb C}=\frac{j-1}{12}$ of $\mathbb{HP}^j/\mathbb{HP}^{j-2}$ (from $\mathrm{ch}(z^m)=u^m+\frac m{12}u^{m+1}+\dots$, $z=\gamma-2$). With the natural moduli metric (round, by $SO(4)$-invariance) $\xi=0$ and the class is carried by the counterterm and the spectral flow.

*Proof.* In the chart at $p_i$, $q_kq_i^{-1}$ carries $l_g$ ($\delta=1$) if $k\in S\not\ni i$, $r_{g^{-1}}$ ($\delta=1$) if $i\in S\not\ni k$, conjugation ($\delta=2$) if both lie in $S$, and the trivial representation otherwise. Summing over $k<j$ at $p_j$ and $k<j-1$ at $p_{j-1}$ gives $\delta_x-\delta_z=j$. $\square$

**Corollary 6.8 (equivariant Thom spaces over $\mathbb{HP}^1$).** For real $Sp(1)$-representations $\rho_0,\rho_1$ of dimension $r$, the bundle $E\to S^4$ clutched by $gu\mapsto\rho_1(g)\rho_0(g)^{-1}$ has fibre representations $\rho_0,\rho_1$ at the poles, the Conley index $\mathrm{Th}(E)$ of its zero section has Witten complex $\mathbb Z[r]\oplus\mathbb Z[r+4]$ with $d=0$, and $e'_{\mathbb R}=\frac{\delta(\rho_1)-\delta(\rho_0)}{24}$ ($V_x=T_{p_1}S^4\oplus\rho_1$ contributes $1+\delta(\rho_1)$). Every multiple of $\nu$ occurs. For $\rho_1=l_g$, $\rho_0$ trivial, $\mathrm{Th}(E)=\mathbb{HP}^2$.

### 6.2 Stem 7: the octonionic plane

*Proof.* (1) The gradient is the Peirce-$\frac12$ projection of $\Lambda$; $U_X\Lambda=f(X)X$ and $L_X\Lambda=\Lambda_1+\frac12\Lambda_{1/2}$ give the formula. $U_A$ preserves rank one (the entries of $X^\#$ scale homogeneously), $U_{e^{s\Lambda}}U_{e^{t\Lambda}}=U_{e^{(s+t)\Lambda}}$, and $\frac d{dt}U_{e^{t\Lambda}}X=2\Lambda\circ U_{e^{t\Lambda}}X$. (2) Fixed points of $\Psi$ are supported on one diagonal entry; in the chart at $E_i$, $X_{ij}$ evolves by $e^{t(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)}$. (3) $|X_{ij}|^2=\xi_i\xi_j$, so $\xi_i=0$ kills row $i$; $\Psi_tX\to E_{\max\{i:\xi_i\ne0\}}$ as $t\to\infty$. Every pair of critical points has $E_2$ as source or $E_0$ as target. (4) $W^u(E_2)\cap W^s(E_1)=\{\xi_0=0,\xi_1\xi_2\ne0\}\subset\mathbb{OP}^1_{12}$; its level set $\xi_1=c$ is the stated sphere. The induced metric is $Spin(8)$-invariant (below) and $Spin(7)$ acts irreducibly on the tangent space, so it is round. (Exact checks at a rational point moved by a triality element: P3.S1.) $\square$

**Lemma 6.10 (triality [Ba02, SV00]).** $Spin(8)=\{(g_0,g_1,g_2)\in SO(8)^3:\mathrm{Re}(g_0(x)g_1(y)g_2(z))=\mathrm{Re}(xyz)\}$ acts on $\mathrm{Herm}_3(\mathbb O)$ by $x_i\mapsto g_ix_i$, as a subgroup of $F_4$ fixing $\Lambda$. **Proposition 6.11 (the Morse framing of the octonionic moduli).** $Spin(8)$ acts on $\mathcal M(E_2,E_1)$ through $g_0$, transitively with isotropy $H\cong Spin(7)$; $\rho_{E_2}=g_0\oplus g_1$ on $(x_0,x_1)$, $\rho_{E_1}=g_2$ on $x_2$, and $\Phi_u=\mathrm{id}\oplus\kappa$ is admissible. *Proof.* Proposition 3.2 with the section $s$: $[h]=[g_0s]+[g_1s]-[g_0s]-[g_2s]=[R]-[a\mapsto L_{\bar a}R_{\bar a}]$. ## 7. All stems $4k-1$: spinor clutching and Killing spinors

Notation. $Cl_{4k}$ acts on $\Delta=\Delta^+\oplus\Delta^-$ with $c(v)^2=-|v|^2$, $\Gamma=i^{2k}c_0\cdots c_{4k-1}$, $\Delta^\pm=\ker(\Gamma\mp1)$. The base point is $u=e_0$ and $H=Spin(4k-1)\subset G=Spin(4k)$. $\tau$ is conjugation by a reflection fixing $u$, so $\Delta^\pm\circ\tau\cong\Delta^\mp$. $r_k=2^{2k-1}$ ($k$ even, $\Delta^\pm$ real) or $2^{2k}$ ($k$ odd, quaternionic; we take the underlying real module). Weights are written in orthonormal torus coordinates $\mu=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{2k})$.

### 7.1 The flow and the isotropy data

Let $E_k=Spin(4k+1)\times_{Spin(4k)}\Delta^+_{\mathbb R}\to S^{4k}\subset\mathbb Re_*\oplus\mathbb R^{4k}$ with invariant metric and connection, $h=\langle\cdot,e_*\rangle$, $N=e_*$, $S=-e_*$, and $F=\pi^*h-|v|^2$ on the total space with the connection metric.

**Proposition 7.1.** $\frac{d}{dt}|v|^2=4|v|^2$ along $-\nabla F$, so the zero section is an isolated invariant set containing every bounded flow line. The critical points $x_1=(N,0)$, $x_0=(S,0)$ have indices $r_k+4k$ and $r_k$; $W^u(x_1)$ is open, so the flow is Morse–Smale; $\mathcal M_k:=\mathcal M(x_1,x_0)$ is the equator, a round unit $S^{4k-1}$; the Conley index is $\mathrm{Th}(E_k)$ and the Witten complex is $\mathbb Z[r_k]\oplus\mathbb Z[r_k+4k]$ with $d=0$. $G$ acts on $\mathcal M_k$ through $SO(4k)$ with isotropy $H$, and $\rho_{x_1}=\mathbb R^{4k}\oplus\Delta^+_{\mathbb R}$, $\rho_{x_0}=\Delta^+_{\mathbb R}\circ\tau\cong\Delta^-_{\mathbb R}$.

*Proof.* $|v|^2$ is horizontally constant and $\nabla F=\widetilde{\nabla h}-2v$. For the south pole, $g_v=e_*v$ ($v\perp u$ a unit vector) covers the rotation by $\pi$ in the $(e_*,v)$-plane, maps $N$ to $S$, and conjugation by $g_v$ preserves $G$ and covers the reflection in $v^\perp$, i.e. $\tau$. $\square$

### 7.2 The section-free twist

**Lemma 7.2.** Every $H$-isomorphism $\Phi_u:U\oplus\Delta^-_{\mathbb R}\to U\oplus\Delta^+_{\mathbb R}$ ($U=\mathbb R^{4k}$) is block diagonal, $A\oplus c(u)q$ with $q$ in the commutant of $\Delta^-_{\mathbb R}$, and the resulting twist is stably homotopic to $1\oplus h_k$, $h_k(x)=c(x)c(u)^{-1}\in SO(\Delta^+_{\mathbb R})$.

*Proof.* $U|_H=\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R^{4k-1}$ and $\Delta^\pm_{\mathbb R}|_H\cong\Delta_{4k-1,\mathbb R}$ are irreducible and pairwise non-isomorphic. Since $H$ fixes $u$, $gc(u)g^{-1}=c(gu)$, so $c(u)$ intertwines $\Delta^-|_H$ and $\Delta^+|_H$, and the spinor block of $h$ is $\Delta^+(g)c(u)\Delta^-(g)^{-1}c(u)^{-1}=c(gu)c(u)^{-1}$ ($q$ cancels). ### 7.4 Killing-spinor localization and Pfaffian moments

**Theorem 7.5 (= Theorem E(3)).** Let $\rho_N,\rho_S$ be complex $G$-representations with an $H$-isomorphism $C:\rho_S|_H\to\rho_N|_H$, $h(gu)=\rho_N(g)C\rho_S(g)^{-1}C^{-1}$, and $D_t=D\otimes(d+th^{-1}dh)$ on the round $S^{4k-1}$.
1. $D_t$ commutes with a fixed unitary $G$-action; only finitely many isotypic blocks have nonzero flow, and $\mathrm{SF}=\sum_\pi\dim\pi\cdot\mathrm{SF}_\pi$ with $\mathrm{SF}_\pi=\sum_{d\ge0}[m(\pi,V_d^-\otimes\rho_S)-m(\pi,V_d^-\otimes\rho_N)]$, where $V_d^-=V(d+\frac12,\frac12,\dots,\frac12,-\frac12)$ is the negative eigenspace of the round Dirac operator.
2. For $(\rho_N,\rho_S,C)=(\Delta^+,\Delta^-,c(u))$, $\mathrm{SF}_\pi=0$ for $\pi\ne\mathbf 1$ and $\mathrm{SF}_{\mathbf 1}=1$.
3. The trivial block is one-dimensional, spanned by a $t$-independent invariant section $\psi$ — a Killing spinor coupled to its dual — and $D_t\psi=(4k-1)(t-\frac12)\psi$. For $\Delta^+_{\mathbb R}$ with $k$ odd the line occurs twice, so $\mathrm{SF}=a_k$.

*Proof.* (1) With $\rho'=C\rho_SC^{-1}$, $h(gx)=\rho_N(g)h(x)\rho'(g)^{-1}$, so $d+tA$ is $G$-invariant for the fibre action $\rho'$, and multiplication by $h$ intertwines $(D_1,U_{\rho'})$ with $(D_0,U_{\rho_N})$, while $(D_0,U_{\rho'})\cong(D_0,U_{\rho_S})$. By Frobenius reciprocity $L^2(S)=\bigoplus_d(V_d^+\oplus V_d^-)$ with $D=\pm(d+\frac{4k-1}2)$ [Bä96b, CH96]; with the orientation of §2.5 the negative family is $V_d^-$ (forced by Lemma 2.2(4) and (2)). Min–max in the finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces bounds the eigenvalue motion by $\sup\|c(A)\|$, leaving finitely many blocks, each contributing $n_-(D_0)-n_-(D_1)$. (2) $\Delta^\pm$ are minuscule, so $V_\lambda\otimes\Delta^\pm=\bigoplus V_{\lambda+\mu}$ over weights $\mu$ with $\lambda+\mu$ dominant [Hu72, §24]. For $\nu=\lambda_d+\mu\ne0$ both $d=\nu_1-1$ and $d=\nu_1$ occur, with weights $\mu$ differing in one sign, one in $\Delta^+$ and one in $\Delta^-$; the contributions cancel. For $\nu=0$ only $d=0$, $\mu=-\lambda_0\in\mathrm{wt}(\Delta^-)$ occurs. (3) $\dim\mathrm{Hom}_H(\mathbf 1,\Delta_{4k-1}\otimes\Delta^+|_H)=1$, the invariant line is $t$-independent and $D_t$-stable, so $\lambda(t)$ is affine; $\lambda(0)=-\frac{4k-1}2$ (restriction of a constant spinor of $\mathbb R^{4k}$, in $V_0^-\otimes\Delta^-$) and $\lambda(1)=+\frac{4k-1}2$ by the intertwining. Explicit matrices for $k=1,2,3$ (sizes $4,64,1024$) confirm a one-dimensional invariant space and $\lambda(t)=\lambda(0)(1-2t)$; without the isotropy twist the invariant eigenvalue does not change sign (P4.S3). $\square$

**Theorem 7.6 (= Theorem E(4)).** In the situation of Theorem 7.5 let $M_k(\rho)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{wt}(\rho)}\mu_1\cdots\mu_{2k}$.
1. $W(h)=-(M_k(\rho_N)-M_k(\rho_S))$, hence $-\mathrm{SF}=W$ and $e_{\mathbb C}=c_A(k)W$.
2. $M_k(\rho\circ\tau)=-M_k(\rho)$; $M_k$ vanishes on representations extending to $Pin(4k)$.
3. For $\mathrm{Sym}^m\Delta^\pm$, $M_k(\rho_N)-M_k(\rho_S)=\ell^{(k)}_m:=\sum_{j=1}^mj^{2k-1}\binom{m-j+r-1}{r-1}$ ($r=2^{2k-1}$), with $\sum_m\ell^{(k)}_mt^m=A_{2k-1}(t)/(1-t)^{2k+r}$ ($A_n$ the Eulerian polynomial); for $\Lambda^j$ it is $\sum_l(-1)^{l-1}l^{2k-1}\binom r{j-l}$. For $k=1$, $\ell^{(1)}_m=\binom{m+2}3$ (the Dynkin index of Proposition 6.3); for $k=2$, $\ell^{(2)}_m=1,16,127,688,\dots$ with generating function $t(1+4t+t^2)/(1-t)^{12}$.
4. The corresponding moduli spheres (real $\mathrm{Sym}^m\Delta^\pm_{\mathbb R}$ for $k$ even, realified complex $\mathrm{Sym}^m\Delta^\pm$ for $k$ odd) have $e'_{\mathbb R}=\frac{B_{2k}}{4k}\ell^{(k)}_m$; direct sums of $E_k$ and of $E_k$ with poles exchanged realize every element of $\mathrm{im}\,J$.

*Proof.* (1) By Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne localization [AB84, BV82] for the maximal torus, whose fixed points on $S^{4k}$ are $N,S$, $\int\mathrm{ch}_X(E)=\frac{\chi_{\rho_N}(e^X)-\chi_{\rho_S}(e^X)}{e_X(T_NS^{4k})}$ (the poles induce opposite orientations on the same representation). The numerator is divisible by $\prod_jx_j$, and the coefficient of $\prod x_j$ in $e^{\kappa\mu\cdot x}$ is $\kappa^{2k}\prod\mu_j$, so $\int\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(E)=\pm(M_k(\rho_N)-M_k(\rho_S))$. The universal sign is fixed by the spinor case: $M_k(\Delta^+)-M_k(\Delta^-)=1$ and $W=-1$ (Proposition 7.3), so $\varepsilon'=-1$. (2) A reflection flips one coordinate of every weight. (3) The bundle clutched by $(\mathrm{Sym}^m\Delta^+,\mathrm{Sym}^m\Delta^-)$ is $\mathrm{Sym}^mE_1$ with $\mathrm{ch}(E_1)=r+y$, $y^2=0$; $\sigma_t(r+y)=(1-t)^{-r}\exp(\sum_j\psi^j(y)t^j/j)$ with $\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(\psi^jy)=j^{2k}\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(y)$ gives $\ell_m^{(k)}$; $\lambda_t$ inserts $(-1)^{j-1}$. For $k=1$, $\mathrm{Sym}^m\Delta^\pm=V_m\boxtimes1,1\boxtimes V_m$ and $2\sum_j(j/2)^2=\binom{m+2}3$. (4) For $k$ odd realification doubles $W$ and $e'_{\mathbb R}=e_{\mathbb C}/2$. $\square$

### 7.5 How the triality and $\mathbb{HP}$ pictures sit inside the spinor clutching

*$\mathbb{OP}^2$ is the case $k=2$.* The moduli sphere $\mathcal M(E_2,E_1)\subset\mathbb{OP}^1_{12}\cong S^8$ is $Spin(8)/Spin(7)$ with $\rho_{E_2}=g_0\oplus g_1$, $\rho_{E_1}=g_2$, where $g_0$ — the representation acting on the moduli sphere — plays the role of the vector representation and $g_1,g_2$ of the two half-spin representations; the normal directions $x_1,x_2$ of $\mathbb{OP}^1_{12}$ at $E_2,E_1$ form the spinor bundle of $S^8$, so Theorem E for $k=2$ is the local model of $\mathbb{OP}^2$ near the line $\mathbb{OP}^1_{12}$. At the level of framed classes the identification is exact: both twists have $W=-1$ (Propositions 6.11 and 7.3), and $W:\pi_7(SO)\to\mathbb Z$ is an isomorphism. Concretely, $c(x)(a,b)=(xb,-\bar xa)$ makes $\mathbb O\oplus\mathbb O$ a $Cl_8$-module; its chirality $\Gamma=c(e_0)\cdots c(e_7)$ is $-1\oplus+1$, so $\Delta^+$ is the second summand; $c(x)c(1)^{-1}=L_x\oplus L_{\bar x}$; and on $\Delta^+$ the Clifford twist $L_{\bar x}=\kappa R_x\kappa$ is the triality twist $R$ up to constant conjugation (P5.S2).

*$\mathbb{HP}^2$ is the case $k=1$.* $\Delta^+$ of $Spin(4)=Sp(1)\times Sp(1)$ is $\mathbb H$, $E_1$ is the quaternionic Hopf line bundle and $\mathrm{Th}(E_1)=\mathbb{HP}^2$. In the quaternionic model $c(x)(a,b)=(xb,-\bar xa)$ on $\mathbb H\oplus\mathbb H$, $\Gamma=-c(e_0)\cdots c(e_3)=+1\oplus-1$ and the twist on $\Delta^+$ is $l_g$, $W=-2$, which is the twist of $\mathcal M(p_2,p_1)$ relative to $\varphi_b$ ($W(l_{g^{-1}})+2W(r_{g^{-1}})=2-4$). The left-invariant description of §6.1 uses the free factor $Sp(1)\subset Spin(4)$.

*What the special cases add.* (i) A global section of $G\to S^{4k-1}$ exists only for $S^3,S^7$; it gives the explicit twists $l_g$ and $R$. (ii) $\mathbb{HP}^n$ and $\mathbb{OP}^2$ are closed manifolds in which R1 applies directly (convex pairs), whereas for $k\ge3$ we use Conley indices; the fibrewise compactification $S(E_k\oplus\mathbb R)$ with the obvious function is not Morse–Smale. (iii) On $S^3$ and $S^7$ the full homogeneous $\eta$-invariants are available (Theorem G), making the metric independence of (1.1) explicit. (iv) In stem $3$ the free $Sp(1)$-action and Peter–Weyl give all multiples $(j-1)\nu$ inside the single manifold $\mathbb{HP}^n$.

## 8. Signature versus Dirac: proof of Theorem F

Let $B$ be the odd signature operator, the self-adjoint supercharge of the $N=2$ (de Rham) quantum mechanics twisted by the Hodge involution, and define $e_{\rm sig}(Y,\varphi)=\eta_B(g)-\int_Y\mathrm TL(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^g)\bmod\mathbb Z$. By the APS signature theorem, $\sigma(W)=\int_WL(\nabla^{g_W})-\eta_B$, so $e_{\rm sig}\equiv L[W,\varphi]$, independent of $g$.

**Theorem 8.1 (= Theorem F).**
1. On closed stably framed $3$-manifolds, $e_{\rm sig}\equiv-8e_{\mathbb C}=-16e'_{\mathbb R}$; on $\pi_3^s$ its kernel is $\mathbb Z/8$. On closed stably framed $7$-manifolds, $e_{\rm sig}\equiv-224e_{\mathbb C}$; on $\pi_7^s$ its kernel is $\mathbb Z/16$ and its image $\mathbb Z/15$.
2. On twisted spheres $(S^{4k-1},h\varphi_b)$, $e_{\mathbb C}=c_A(k)W(h)$ and $e_{\rm sig}=c_L(k)W(h)$ with $c_L(k)=\frac{2^{2k+1}(2^{2k-1}-1)B_{2k}}{4k}=-2^{2k+1}(2^{2k-1}-1)c_A(k)$. On $\mathrm{im}\,J$, $e_{\rm sig}$ has image of order the odd part of $m(2k)$ and kernel the $2$-primary part.
3. Values: $\mathbb{HP}^n$: $-\frac{2(j-1)}3$; $\mathbb{OP}^2$: $\frac{14}{15}$; $\mathcal M_k$: $-a_kc_L(k)$, of orders $3,15,63,15,33,4095$ for $k\le6$. The $N=1$ index ($KO_{4k-1}=0$) and the $N=2$ index ($\chi(S^{4k-1})=0$) vanish.

*Proof.* (1) $L_1=p_1/3=-8\hat A_1$. $L_2=\frac{7p_2-p_1^2}{45}$ and $L_2+224\hat A_2=\frac{p_1^2}4=q_1^2$, where $q_1=p_1/2$ is integral for spin bundles and the relative class is defined by the framing, so $q_1^2[W,\varphi]\in\mathbb Z$. (2) Lemma 2.2 with $\log\frac x{\tanh x}=\log\frac x{\sinh x}+\log\cosh x$ gives $c_L$, and Lemma 2.3 kills the round terms. An odd prime $p$ dividing the denominator of $c_A(k)$ has $(p-1)\mid2k$, so $2^{2k}\equiv1$ and $2^{2k-1}\not\equiv1\pmod p$; the $2$-adic valuation of that denominator, $\nu_2(k)+3$, is at most $2k+1$. Since $W\in a_k\mathbb Z$ and $a_k\in\{1,2\}$, the order of $e_{\rm sig}$ is the odd part of $m(2k)$. (3) Substitution. $\square$

So in every stem $4k-1$: the $N=1$ and $N=2$ indices see nothing, the $N=2$ secondary invariant sees the odd part of $\mathrm{im}\,J$, and the $N=1$ secondary invariant (1.1) sees all of it. The arithmetic reason is that $L$ and $\hat A$ have the same primitive denominators away from $2$.

## 9. Beyond $KO$: product moduli, $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$, and the $2|1$ refinement

The invariants of §§4–8 are those of a $1|1$-dimensional theory in the sense of Stolz–Teichner [ST04, ST11]: $KO$-indices and their secondary $\eta$-type refinements. They cannot see more than $KO$ and $\mathrm{im}\,J$. In stems $6$ and $14$ one has $KO_6=KO_{14}=0$ and $\mathrm{im}\,J=0$, while $\pi_6^s=\mathbb Z/2\langle\nu^2\rangle$ and $\pi_{14}^s=\mathbb Z/2\langle\sigma^2\rangle\oplus\mathbb Z/2\langle\kappa\rangle$ [To62], so every invariant used so far vanishes on a $6$- or $14$-dimensional moduli space. The next floor is $2|1$: two-dimensional $N=(0,1)$ sigma models, whose partition functions are Witten genera [Wi87, Wi88] and whose secondary invariants are measured by elliptic cohomology. On the homotopy side the relevant invariant of the second Adams–Novikov filtration is Laures's $f$-invariant [La99, La00], with values in divided congruences of modular forms, described analytically by Bunke–Naumann [BN08, BN09] and von Bodecker [vB08]; von Bodecker computed it on products of framed manifolds from the $e$-invariants of the factors [vB09]. This section makes the first step from $1|1$ to $2|1$ for Morse moduli. We restrict to moduli spaces of *product type*, where everything can be proved, and prove Theorems H–M of §1.3: realizations of $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$ by symmetric moduli spaces, selection rules in terms of endpoint data, and a spectral normalization of their $f$-invariants. Only decomposable elements are reached (§12).

### 9.1 Stems $6$ and $14$, the $f$-invariant, and conventions

$\pi_6^s=\mathbb Z/2\langle\nu^2\rangle$, $\pi_{14}^s=\mathbb Z/2\langle\sigma^2\rangle\oplus\mathbb Z/2\langle\kappa\rangle$ [To62]. The image of $J$ is zero in stems $\equiv2,4,5,6\pmod 8$ [Ad66], so $\nu^2,\sigma^2\notin\mathrm{im}\,J$. $KO_6=KO_{14}=0$, so the $KO$-Hurewicz image and every $KO$-valued index vanish, and Adams's $d$- and $e$-invariants are zero on these stems. Both elements have Adams–Novikov filtration $2$; $\nu^2$ is in the Hurewicz image of $tmf$, while $\sigma^2$ is not ($\pi_7tmf=0$) but is detected on the $2$-line by the $f$-invariant.

*The $f$-invariant.* Fix $N=3$, $\Gamma=\Gamma_1(3)$, $\mathbb Z^\Gamma=\mathbb Z[\zeta_3,1/3]$. The graded ring of modular forms of $\Gamma$ is $\mathbb Q[E_1,E_3]$ with
$$E_1(q)=1+6\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{d\mid n}\Big(\frac d3\Big)q^n,\qquad E_3(q)=1-9\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{d\mid n}\Big(\frac d3\Big)d^2q^n,$$
and $E_{2k}(q)=1-\frac{4k}{B_{2k}}\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$ are the level-one Eisenstein series, normalized with constant term $1$. Let $D^\Gamma_k$ be the set of $\sum_{i\le k}f_i$, $f_i\in M_i^\Gamma\otimes\mathbb Q$, with $q$-expansion in $\mathbb Z^\Gamma[[q]]$, and $\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_k=D^\Gamma_k+M^\Gamma_0\otimes\mathbb Q+M^\Gamma_k\otimes\mathbb Q$. Laures [La99, La00] defined a homomorphism
$$f:\pi^s_{2n}\to\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_{n+1}\otimes\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,$$
the image of the Adams–Novikov $2$-line under the map to elliptic cohomology of level $\Gamma$; it is injective on the $2$-line at primes not dividing $N$. Analytic and index-theoretic descriptions are due to Bunke–Naumann [BN08, BN09] and von Bodecker [vB08], who expresses $f$ as an elliptic genus of manifolds with codimension-$2$ corners.

**Lemma 9.1 (von Bodecker [vB08]; [vB09, Lemma 1]).** For odd-dimensional framed $Y_1,Y_2$ let $m(Y_i)$ be any modular form of weight $(\dim Y_i+1)/2$ for $\Gamma$ with $\bar m(Y_i):=m(Y_i)-e_{\mathbb C}(Y_i)\in\mathbb Z^\Gamma[[q]]$. Then $f(Y_1\times Y_2)\equiv\bar m(Y_1)e_{\mathbb C}(Y_2)\equiv-\bar m(Y_2)e_{\mathbb C}(Y_1)$.

*Values [vB09, Thm.].* $f(x_3^2)\equiv\frac12\big(\frac{E_1^2-1}{12}\big)^2$, $f(x_7^2)\equiv\frac12\big(\frac{E_4-1}{240}\big)^2$, and $f(x_{4k-1}x_{4k'-1})\equiv0$ unless $k=k'\in\{1,2\}$.

*Notation and conventions.* $x_{4k-1}:=[\mathcal M_k,\Phi]=J[h_k]\in\pi^s_{4k-1}$ is the generator of $\mathrm{im}\,J$ of Theorem E, with $e'_{\mathbb R}(x_{4k-1})=B_{2k}/4k$; by §2.5(5), $x_3=J[l_g]=\nu$ and $x_7$ is the class of $\mathcal M(E_2,E_1)\subset\mathbb{OP}^2$, i.e. $\sigma$ up to sign. (The subscript $4k-1\ge3$ distinguishes $x_{4k-1}$ from the critical points $x$, $x_0$, $x_1$.) In this section $E_1(q),E_3(q),E_{2k}(q)$ always denote Eisenstein series; the spinor bundle $E_k\to S^{4k}$ of §7 appears only as "the spinor clutching", and the idempotents $E_i$ of §6.2 only in $\mathcal M(E_2,E_1)$. The conventions for $e_{\mathbb C}$, $e'_{\mathbb R}=e_{\mathbb C}/a_k$, $c_A(k)=-B_{2k}/4k$, $W(h)$ and $\mathrm{SF}=-W$ are those of §§2.4–2.5, without change; the normalization of $E_{2k}$ is chosen so that $c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)=c_A(k)+\sum_n\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$, the $q$-deformation of Lemma 2.2(2). The only convention difference with the literature is in [vB09], where $e_{\mathbb C}(x_3)=-\frac1{12}$ and $e_{\mathbb C}(x_7)=\frac1{240}$, opposite to our $+\frac1{12}$ and $-\frac1{240}$ (table of §7.3). Since $f(x_{4k-1}^2)$ is quadratic in the generator and $\nu^2,\sigma^2$ have order $2$, this sign is immaterial; we check it exactly (§9.8(2)). No formula of §§2–8 had to be adjusted.

### 9.2 The product rule: proof of Theorem H

*Slice framings.* A stable framing of a closed $n$-manifold $Y$ is a homotopy class of trivializations $TY\oplus\mathbb R^a\cong\mathbb R^{n+a}$, $a\gg0$; the set of stable framings is a torsor under $[Y,SO]=\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(Y_+)$, acting by pointwise multiplication. For $Y=Y_1\times Y_2$ and $u=(u_1,u_2)$, $T(Y_1\times Y_2)|_{Y_1\times\{u_2\}}=TY_1\oplus T_{u_2}Y_2$ with the second summand constant. Choosing an isomorphism $T_{u_2}Y_2\cong\mathbb R^{\dim Y_2}$ (orientation-preserving for the product orientation), $\Phi|_{Y_1\times\{u_2\}}$ becomes a stable framing $\Phi_1$ of $Y_1$; similarly $\Phi_2$. Different choices change $[Y_i,\Phi_i]$ at most by a sign.

*Proof of Theorem H.* Put $\varphi_i=\Phi_i$ and compare $\Phi$ with the product framing $\varphi_1\times\varphi_2$ (after stabilizing both to the same rank): $\Phi=h\cdot(\varphi_1\times\varphi_2)$ for a map $h:Y_1\times Y_2\to SO$. By construction $h|_{Y_1\times\{u_2\}}$ and $h|_{\{u_1\}\times Y_2}$ are constant, with the same value at $u$; since $SO$ is connected we may take that value to be $1$, so $h$ restricts to the constant map on $Y_1\vee Y_2$. Stable $SO$ is an infinite loop space and $[\,\cdot\,,SO]=\widetilde{KO}^{-1}$ is a cohomology theory; the cofibre sequence $Y_1\vee Y_2\to Y_1\times Y_2\to Y_1\wedge Y_2$ gives an exact sequence
$$\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(Y_1\wedge Y_2)\to\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(Y_1\times Y_2)\to\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(Y_1\vee Y_2).$$
The class of $h$ maps to zero on the right, so it comes from the left, which vanishes by hypothesis. Hence $h\simeq1$, $\Phi\simeq\varphi_1\times\varphi_2$, and the Pontryagin–Thom isomorphism [Po55], which is multiplicative, gives $[Y_1\times Y_2,\Phi]=\pm[Y_1,\varphi_1][Y_2,\varphi_2]$. For spheres $\widetilde{KO}^{-1}(S^p\wedge S^q)=\pi_{p+q}(SO)$, and by Bott periodicity ($\pi_i(SO)=\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z/2,0,\mathbb Z,0,0,0,\mathbb Z$ for $i\equiv0,\dots,7\pmod 8$) it vanishes for $p+q=4(k_1+k_2)-2\equiv2,6\pmod8$. The Morse-theoretic statement follows from R1. $\square$

*Scope.* Covered: $S^{4k_1-1}\times S^{4k_2-1}$ (all $k_i$); $U(1)^2$ ($\pi_2(SO)=0$), giving $[T^2,\Phi]=(1+a_1)(1+a_2)\eta^2$ for a free torus orbit, with $a_i$ the parities of the restricted endpoint weight sums (compare the torus in §2.2 and Theorem B); $U(1)\times Sp(1)$ ($\pi_4(SO)=0$), where the class is always $0$ since $\eta\nu=0$; $S^1\times S^{4k-1}$ with $k$ odd. Not covered: $S^1\times S^{8m-1}$, where $\pi_{8m}(SO)=\mathbb Z/2$ and a top-cell twist can add $J(\pi_{8m}SO)$ (in stem $8$ this is $\eta\sigma$); triple products $S^3\times S^3\times S^3$, whose $9$-cell sees $\pi_9(SO)=\mathbb Z/2$.

**Remark 9.2.** Theorem H says that for product moduli the cross terms of the framing — the part of the twist that genuinely mixes the two factors — are invisible in stable homotopy. For symmetric moduli this has a concrete meaning: an isotropy representation of $G_1\times G_2$ such as $V_m\boxtimes V_n$ enters only through its restrictions to the two factors (§9.3).

### 9.3 Symmetric product moduli: proof of Theorem I

**Lemma 9.3.** In the situation of Theorem I, the slice framing $\Phi_1$ satisfies
$$\Phi_{1,g_1u}\circ(dg_1\oplus1_{T_{u_2}Y_2}\oplus1\oplus\rho_z(g_1))=\rho_x(g_1)\circ\Phi_{1,u}\qquad(g_1\in G_1).$$

*Proof.* Restrict (3.1) to $g=(g_1,1)$: $G_1$ preserves $Y_1\times\{u_2\}$ and acts on $T(Y_1\times Y_2)|_{Y_1\times\{u_2\}}=TY_1\oplus T_{u_2}Y_2$ by $dg_1\oplus1$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem I.* (1) By Lemma 9.3 the slice $(Y_1,\Phi_1)$ satisfies the equivariance relation (3.1) for the $G_1$-action with representations $\rho_x|_{G_1}$ on $V_x$ and $\rho_z|_{G_1}\oplus\mathbf 1$ on $V_z\oplus T_{u_2}Y_2$. The proofs of Theorem A(2)–(3), Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 3.3 use nothing else (§3), so $\Phi_1=h_1\cdot(\varphi_b\oplus\Phi_{1,u})$ with $h_1(g_1u)=\rho_x(g_1)\Phi_{1,u}\rho_1'(g_1)^{-1}\Phi_{1,u}^{-1}$ (or $\Phi_1=h_1\varphi_L$ in the free case), and $[Y_1,\Phi_1]=J[h_1]=-\frac{W(h_1)}{a_{k_1}}x_{4k_1-1}$ by Bott integrality (Lemma 2.2(3): $W$ takes the values $a_k\mathbb Z$ on $\pi_{4k-1}(SO)$, and the generator $h_k$ has $W=-a_k$ by Proposition 7.3). Theorem H concludes.

(2) In the free case Theorem 6.5 applied to the slice gives $[Y_i,\Phi_i]=(\delta(\rho_x|_{G_i})-\delta(\rho_z|_{G_i}\oplus\mathbf 1)-1)\nu$ and $\delta(\mathbf 1)=0$. For $V_m\boxtimes V_n$ restricted to $G_1$ the derivative is $d\rho_m\otimes1_{n+1}$, so $W=(n+1)W(V_m)=-(n+1)\binom{m+2}3$ (§3, after Proposition 3.3); the real form has complexification $V_m\boxtimes V_n$ if $m+n$ is even (real type) and $2\,V_m\boxtimes V_n$ if $m+n$ is odd (quaternionic type, realified). (3) $x_3x_3=\pm\nu^2$ and $x_7x_7=\pm\sigma^2$; §9.1. $\square$

### 9.4 Equivariant handle construction: proof of Theorem K(1)

*Proof of Theorem K(1).* Choose a $G$-invariant metric on $S(V_x)$ and the $G$-equivariant tubular neighbourhood $n\mapsto\exp_n\psi(n,v)$, $|v|<\epsilon$. The equivariant Pontryagin–Thom map
$$c:S(V_x)\to S^{V_z}:=S(V_z\oplus\mathbb R),\qquad c(\exp_n\psi(n,v))=\sigma\big(\beta(|v|)v\big),\quad c\equiv\text{south pole off the tube},$$
with $\sigma$ the stereographic chart sending $0\mapsto$ north pole $\mathfrak n$ and $\beta$ a cutoff with $\beta(s)s\to\infty$ as $s\to\epsilon$, is smooth, $G$-equivariant ($G$ acting on $S^{V_z}$ by $\rho_z\oplus1$), has $\mathfrak n$ as a regular value, $c^{-1}(\mathfrak n)=N$ and $dc|_{\nu N}=\psi^{-1}$.

Let $U=\mathbb R\oplus U'$ be a representation containing a trivial summand, $W_U=V_x\oplus U$, and $P=S^{V_z}\times D(W_U)$ with $f_P(p,w)=p_{\mathbb R}+|w|^2$. Its critical points are $p_0=(\mathfrak s,0)$ (index $0$) and $z=(\mathfrak n,0)$ (index $r$, unstable space $T_{\mathfrak n}S^{V_z}=V_z$), both fixed. $\nabla f_P$ is outward transverse to $\partial P=S^{V_z}\times S(W_U)$ (its normal component is $2|w|$), so after an invariant reparametrization along flow lines near $\partial P$ [Mi65, §3] we may assume $f_P$ is constant, maximal and regular on $\partial P$; and $W^s(z)\cap\partial P=\{\mathfrak n\}\times S(W_U)$ (the belt sphere). Define
$$\phi_0:S(V_x)\to\partial P,\qquad\phi_0(s)=(c(s),\iota(s)),$$
$\iota$ the linear inclusion $S(V_x)\subset S(W_U)$. It is a $G$-equivariant embedding (since $\iota$ is), and $\phi_0(S(V_x))\pitchfork(\{\mathfrak n\}\times S(W_U))=\phi_0(N)$ because $\mathfrak n$ is a regular value of $c$. Its normal bundle is $c^*TS^{V_z}\oplus(S(V_x)\times U)$, and $c^*TS^{V_z}\oplus\mathbb R\cong S(V_x)\times(V_z\oplus\mathbb R)$ equivariantly; using the trivial summand of $U$ we get an equivariant trivialization $\nu(\phi_0)\cong S(V_x)\times W'_U$, $W'_U=V_z\oplus\mathbb R\oplus U'$, and $\dim V_z+\dim W_U=\dim V_x+\dim W'_U=:n$. Attach the $G$-handle $D(V_x)\times D(W'_U)$ along the resulting equivariant embedding $\phi:S(V_x)\times D(W'_U)\to\partial P$, extend $f$ by $-|a|^2+|b|^2+\mathrm{const}$ on the handle, and smooth corners equivariantly (standard handle theory [Mi65], carried out with invariant collars). This gives $\mathcal H$ with critical points $p_0,z,x$, $\rho_x=V_x$, and $f$ maximal and regular on $\partial\mathcal H$.

Flow lines leaving $x$ cross the attaching sphere $\phi(S(V_x)\times0)$ and continue in $P$, where they converge to $z$ iff they lie in $W^s(z)\cap\partial P$, i.e. iff they start in $\phi(N)$; all others converge to $p_0$. Hence $\mathcal M(x,z)=N$, and $W^u(x)\pitchfork W^s(z)$ by the transversality above; the pairs $(x,p_0)$ and $(z,p_0)$ are transverse because $W^s(p_0)$ is open. By Franks's description (§2.1) the Morse framing of $\mathcal M(x,z)$ is the normal framing pulled back by $c$ from $T_{\mathfrak n}S^{V_z}=V_z$, i.e. $\psi$. Finally, a $G$-invariant gradient-like vector field $X$ for $f$ is the gradient of a $G$-invariant metric: the condition $g(X,\cdot)=df$ is affine-linear in $g$, so local solutions patched by an invariant partition of unity and then averaged over $G$ remain solutions. $\Sigma^\infty\mathcal H_+$ is computed by R1, $\mathcal H$ being an isolating block whose Conley index is $\mathcal H_+$. $\square$

**Remark 9.5 (closed manifolds).** The double $\mathcal H\cup_{\partial\mathcal H}\mathcal H$ with $f$ and $2\max f-f$ is a closed $G$-manifold with a smooth invariant Morse function in which $\mathcal M(x,z)$ is unchanged, since downward lines from $x$ stay in $\mathcal H=\{f\le\max f\}$. The mirrored critical points create non-transverse pairs (e.g. $W^u(x')\cap\partial\mathcal H=W^s(x)\cap\partial\mathcal H$), and we do not prove that an equivariant perturbation restores the Morse–Smale condition. We therefore state the realization for $\mathcal H$ (equivalently, for the Conley index), which is the setting of R1 — as for the Thom spaces of §7 with $k\ge3$.

### 9.5 The join, the examples, and invisibility at the $1|1$ level

*Proof of Theorem K(2).* Embed $N_1\times N_2$ in $S(V_1\oplus V_2)$ by $(n_1,n_2)\mapsto(n_1,n_2)/\sqrt2$. *Proof of Theorem K(3): $\nu^2$.* In $\mathbb{HP}^2$ with $f=\sum\lambda_j|q_j|^2/|q|^2$ and $Sp(1)$ acting by left multiplication on $q_2$, the chart at $p_2$ has coordinates $(w_0,w_1)=(q_0q_2^{-1},q_1q_2^{-1})\in\mathbb H^2=V_{p_2}$, both transforming by $w\mapsto wg^{-1}$, and $\mathcal M(p_2,p_1)=\{w_0=0,|w_1|=1\}$, a free orbit (Lemma 4.1). ### 9.6 The $2|1$ three-term formula: proof of Theorem L

*The Witten characteristic series.* For a real bundle with Chern roots $\pm x_j$, the characteristic series of $\hat A\,\mathrm{ch}(\bigotimes_n\mathrm{Sym}_{q^n}(T_{\mathbb C}-\dim))$ is $\prod_j\theta(x_j)$ with
$$\theta(x)=\frac{x/2}{\sinh(x/2)}\prod_{n\ge1}\frac{(1-q^n)^2}{(1-q^ne^x)(1-q^ne^{-x})},\qquad\log\theta(x)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{-B_{2k}}{2k\,(2k)!}E_{2k}(q)\,x^{2k}.$$
Indeed $\log\frac{x/2}{\sinh(x/2)}=-\sum\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k)!}x^{2k}$ and $\sum_n\log\frac{(1-q^n)^2}{(1-q^ne^x)(1-q^ne^{-x})}=\sum_{k}\frac2{(2k)!}\big(\sum_N\sigma_{2k-1}(N)q^N\big)x^{2k}$. With $\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbb C)=\frac2{(2k)!}\sum_jx_j^{2k}$,
$$\log\Theta=\sum_{k\ge1}c_A(k)\,E_{2k}(q)\,\mathrm{ch}_{2k},\qquad c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)=c_A(k)+\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n.\qquad(9.1)$$
This is the $q$-deformation of $\log\hat A=\sum c_A(k)\mathrm{ch}_{2k}$ (Lemma 2.2(2)). (Checked symbolically through $x^{10},q^5$ and the integrality through $k\le10$; `verify.py` P6.5.)

*Proof of Theorem L(1).* For each $n$, apply APS [APS75b] to $D_W\otimes R_n(TW)$ with a metric of product type near $Y=\partial W$ (virtual bundles by additivity): $\mathrm{ind}=\int_W\hat A\,\mathrm{ch}R_n(\nabla^{g_W})-\xi(D_g\otimes R_n(TY\oplus\mathbb R))$. Since $[\hat A\,\mathrm{ch}R_n]$ is a stable invariant polynomial, Stokes gives, as in Proposition 2.1,
$$\Theta_n[W,\Phi]\equiv\xi(D_g\otimes R_n)-\int_Y\mathrm T[\hat A\,\mathrm{ch}R_n](\nabla^\Phi\to\nabla^g)\pmod{\mathbb Z}.$$
Split the transgression along $\nabla^\Phi=\nabla^\varphi+A\to\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^g$, $A=h^{-1}dh$. On the flat-twist path the curvature is $(t^2-t)A^2$, and the transgression of any decomposable polynomial vanishes (Lemma 2.2(1)); by (9.1) the degree-$4k$ part of $\Theta_q$ is $c_A(k)E_{2k}\mathrm{ch}_{2k}$ plus decomposables, and lower-degree parts transgress to forms of degree $<4k-1$. Hence $\int\mathrm T\Theta_q(\nabla^\varphi\to\nabla^{\Phi})=c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)W(h)$, and $W(h)=-\mathrm{SF}$ by Lemma 2.2(4) under the hypotheses of Theorem C. The $q$-coefficients of $c_A(k)E_{2k}$ are (9.1). $\square$

*Proof of Theorem L(2)–(3).* Let $\nabla'$ be a connection on $TD^{4k}\oplus\mathbb R^N$ equal to the flat framing connection near $S^{4k-1}$. Every $\mathrm{ch}_{2i}(\nabla')$, $i\ge1$, is a closed form with compact support in the open disk, and $H^{4i}_c(\mathring D^{4k})=0$ for $4i<4k$, so $\mathrm{ch}_{2i}(\nabla')=d\alpha_i$ with $\alpha_i$ compactly supported. Products of such forms integrate to zero, so only the linear term of (9.1) survives:
$$\Theta[D^{4k},\Phi]=c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)\int_{D}\mathrm{ch}_{2k}(\nabla')=\hat A[D^{4k},\Phi]\,E_{2k}(q),$$
an identity of real $q$-series (both sides independent of $\nabla'$). With $\hat A[D,\Phi]=c_A(k)W(h)$ for $\Phi=h\varphi_b$ (Lemma 2.2(2)), the positive part $W(h)\sum\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$ is integral, so $\Theta\equiv e_{\mathbb C}$ mod $\mathbb Z[[q]]$. In dimension $4$ there are no decomposables of degree $4$, so $\Theta[W,\Phi]=\hat A[W,\Phi]E_2$ for every framed $3$-manifold and every $W$; comparing with (1) and with Theorem C, $\Xi_g\equiv(\xi(D_g)+\text{integer})E_2$, and $E_2\equiv1\pmod{24}$ gives $\Xi_g\equiv\xi(D_g)E_2$. At order $q$: $R_1=T_{\mathbb C}+1-4$ on $Y$ and the coefficient of $E_2$ is $-24$, so $\xi(D_g\otimes T_{\mathbb C})-3\xi(D_g)\equiv-24\xi(D_g)$. (Every closed spin $3$-manifold is framed compatibly with its spin structure and bounds, so the relation holds for all of them; it is a direct consequence of APS.)

For (3): $E_{2k}$ is a modular form for $SL_2(\mathbb Z)\supset\Gamma_1(3)$ for $k\ge2$, and $E_2$ is quasi-modular. The identity $E_1(q)^2=\frac12(3E_2(q^3)-E_2(q))$ (weight-$2$ Eisenstein series of $\Gamma_0(3)$ with constant term $1$) gives the level-$3$ lift, and $E_1^2\equiv1\pmod{12}$ with $e_{\mathbb C}\in\frac1{12}\mathbb Z$ makes the defect integral. $\square$

### 9.7 Spectral check and supersymmetric reading

*Spectral check.* On the round $S^3$, the Levi-Civita-twisted operators $D\otimes T_{\mathbb C}$ and $D\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^2T_{\mathbb C}$ have symmetric spectrum and no kernel (Peter–Weyl blocks as in Lemma 6.2, `verify.py` P6.7), so $\Xi_{\rm round}=0$ through $q^1$; with $\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{\rm round})=-2$ (Proposition 6.4), Theorem L(1) reproduces $\Theta[W,\varphi_L]=e_{\mathbb C}(\varphi_L)E_2=-\frac1{12}E_2$ for the left-invariant framing, in agreement with Theorem 6.5. On the Berger sphere $g_T$, $T=\frac34$, a heat-regularized computation over $131$ Peter–Weyl blocks gives $\eta(D^{(+)}_T)=-0.03187$ (exact value $-(T^2-1)^2/6=-0.031901$, Lemma 10.2) and $\eta(D^{(+)}_T\otimes T_{\mathbb C})=0.67000$ versus $-21\eta(D^{(+)}_T)=0.66992$, with no kernels; replacing the Levi-Civita twist by the flat or by a torsionful connection destroys the relation (controls). The relation, linear and hence insensitive to the sign $D^{(+)}=-D^{(-)}$ of §2.5(3), even holds exactly here, since both sides vary without spectral crossings from the round metric, where both vanish.

*Supersymmetric reading (heuristic).* $\Xi_g(q)$ is the spectral asymmetry of the Ramond-sector supercharge of the $2|1$ (heterotic, $N=(0,1)$) sigma model on the moduli space, graded by left-moving oscillator level; $\Theta$ is its partition function on the null-bordism. Theorem L(1) says that the counterterm and the endpoint spectral flow of (1.1) are dressed by $E_{2k}(q)$: at level $n$ the moduli fermion coupled to the isotropy twist contributes $\sigma_{2k-1}(n)$ units, the Kaluza–Klein multiplicities of the oscillator tower. Theorem L(2) says that on a single moduli sphere this dressing is integral, hence invisible; it becomes visible only when two such spheres are multiplied and the modular normalization of one meets the fractional $e$-invariant of the other (§9.8). We do not construct the $2|1$ theory on the moduli spaces, nor derive it from a theory on $M$.

### 9.8 The $f$-invariant of product moduli: proof of Theorem M

*Proof.* By Theorem H, $[\mathcal M(x,z),\Phi]=\pm[Y_1,\Phi_1][Y_2,\Phi_2]$, and $f$ is a homomorphism on $\pi^s_*$, so $f[\mathcal M(x,z),\Phi]=\pm f(Y_1\times Y_2)$ with product framing. *Non-vanishing and consistency certificates.* Put $a=\frac{E_1^2-1}{12}=q+3q^2+q^3+7q^4+\dots$ and $b=\frac{E_4-1}{240}=\sum\sigma_3(n)q^n$, both integral. We find (`verify.py` P6.6):
1. $\frac12a^2\notin\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_4$ and $\frac12b^2\notin\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_8$: $2$-adic obstructions already in the first $q$-coefficients. So $f(\nu^2)\ne0\ne f(\sigma^2)$, and $f[\mathcal M,\Phi]\ne0$ iff $c_1c_2$ is odd.
2. Consistency with Lemma 9.1: $\pm\frac{a}{12}-\frac12a^2\in\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_4$ and $\pm\frac{b}{240}-\frac12b^2\in\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_8$ (primes $2$, resp. $2,5$; through $q^{60}$), and $\frac a6\in\underline{\underline D}{}^\Gamma_4$, so the sign of $e_{\mathbb C}$ — ours or von Bodecker's (§9.1) — is immaterial.

## 10. $\eta$-invariants of homogeneous metrics on $S^3$ and $S^7$: proof of Theorem G

### 10.1 $S^3$

**Lemma 10.1 (variation principle).** If $\mathcal U$ is a connected set of metrics on a sphere $S^{4k-1}$ containing the round one, with $\ker D_g=0$ on $\mathcal U$, then $\xi(D_g)=\int\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^{\rm round}\to\nabla^g)$ on $\mathcal U$. For the signature operator the same holds on all metrics, with $\eta_B$ and $L$.

*Proof.* By Proposition 2.1 for $\varphi_b$, $\xi(D_g)-\int\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^{\varphi_b}\to\nabla^g)\in a_k\mathbb Z$ is continuous on $\mathcal U$, hence constant, and it is $0$ at the round metric (Lemma 2.3); transgressions are additive and the first leg vanishes identically. For $B$, $\ker B\cong H^{\rm ev}$ has constant dimension and $\eta_B(\text{round})=0$ (orientation-reversing isometry). $\square$

**Lemma 10.2 (which operator is APS).** For $0<T<4$, $\xi(D^{(-)}_{g_T})=\frac{(T^2-1)^2}{12}=\int\mathrm T\hat A(\nabla^{\rm round}\to\nabla^{g_T})=-\frac1{24}\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^{\rm round}\to\nabla^{g_T})$, whereas $\xi(D^{(+)}_{g_T})$ is its negative. Hence $D^{(-)}$ ($c(E_j)=-i\sigma_j$) is the APS operator for $o$.

*Proof.* The right side follows from Proposition 6.4. For the left side, the spectrum of Lemma 6.2 gives $\eta(D^{(+)}_{g_T})=-\frac{(T^2-1)^2}6$ for $T<4$ (Hitchin's value [Hi74]); we verify it independently by an Euler–Maclaurin residue computation: grouping by $N=p+q$, $Z(\sigma)=\sum(p+q)R_{pq}^{-\sigma}$ has $\mathrm{Res}_3Z=T$ and $\mathrm{Res}_1Z=\frac{T^3-T}3$, and with $\sum_p2p(\frac T2+\frac pT)^{-s}|_{s=0}=\frac{T^4}4-\frac16$ one gets $\eta=\frac{T^4}4-\frac16-T\frac{T^3-T}3-\frac{T^4}{12}=-\frac{(T^2-1)^2}6$ (every step exact, plus heat-regularized numerics at eight values of $T$; P2.S3). The APS operator is one of $D^{(\pm)}=\pm D^{(+)}$, and Lemma 10.1 holds for it; only $D^{(-)}$ satisfies it. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem G(1).* The triangle cone is convex and, by Lemma 6.1, is the positive scalar curvature locus, where Lichnerowicz gives $\ker D=0$. Lemma 10.1 and Proposition 6.4 give $\xi(D_{g_a})=-\frac1{24}(\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{g_a})-\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^L\to\nabla^{\rm round}))=\frac{S}{12x_1x_2x_3}$, and $\eta_B=\frac13\mathrm{CS}(\nabla^{\rm round}\to\nabla^{g_a})=-\frac{2S}{3x_1x_2x_3}$ for all $a$; Schur's inequality gives $\eta(D_{g_a})\ge0$ with equality only at the round metric, and $\eta_B=-4\eta(D)$ on the cone ($L_1=p_1/3$ against $\hat A_1=-p_1/24$). For Berger spheres and all $T>0$: by Lemma 6.2 the APS eigenvalues are $-(\frac T2+\frac pT)$ (multiplicity $2p$) and $-\frac T2\mp R_{pq}$ (multiplicity $p+q$); the eigenvalue $-\frac T2+R_{pq}$ is positive for small $T$ and crosses zero exactly once, at $R_{pq}=\frac T2$, since $R_{pq}^2-\frac{T^2}4$ is strictly decreasing. Each crossing lowers $\xi$ by $p+q$, and between crossings Lemma 10.1's argument applies, so $\xi(D_T)=\frac{(T^2-1)^2}{12}-\sum_{R_{pq}<T/2}(p+q)$ (ordered pairs). Numerical checks of both triaxial formulas from the Peter–Weyl blocks agree to $10^{-6}$ (P2.S4). The Berger signature value $-\frac23(T^2-1)^2$ agrees modulo $\mathbb Z$ and up to orientation with [Wei20]. $\square$

## 11. Relation to published work and novelty

**Classical or recalled, with attribution.** Witten's complex and its analysis [Wi82, HS85, BZ92]. Framed flow categories and their realizations [CJS95, AB24], Franks's theorem [Fr79] and its refinements [Co98, Le02], Conley indices [Co78], equivariant flow categories [CK23], Lipshitz–Sarkar's $Sq^2$ [LS14] and the move calculus of [LOS22]. The $KO$-index and Clifford modules [ABS64, AS71, At71, LM89]; Wood's theorem [Wo66]. Adams's $e$-invariant, $\mathrm{im}\,J$ and $e_{\mathbb C}=a_ke'_{\mathbb R}$ [Ad66]; Hopf invariant one [Ad60]; Bott integrality [Bo59]; Adams operations [Ad62]. The APS index, signature and variation formulas and the $e$-invariant formula [APS75a, APS75b], used for space forms [Se81] and nilmanifolds [DS84] and surveyed in [Go12]; the odd index theorem [APS76, Ge93]. The Berger Dirac spectrum and $\eta$-invariant [Hi74, Bä96a], Weingart's Berger $\eta$-invariants in all dimensions [Wei20], round sphere spectra [Bä96b, CH96], the squashed $S^7$ spectrum at the Einstein point [NP83, EN21]. Localization [AB84, BV82], Brauer's rule [Hu72], invariant connections [KN69], homogeneous metrics on spheres [Zi82, Be87], triality and the Albert algebra [Ba02, SV00, McC04]. Lie-group framings and their $e$-invariants: Atiyah–Smith [ASm74] and Minami [Mi24a, Mi25a, Mi25b], who realize generators of the image of $e_{\mathbb C}$ by Lie groups and quotients with twisted left-invariant framings through characteristic classes (no $\eta$-invariants, no Morse moduli). Bunke's universal $\eta$-invariant containing the $e$-invariant [Bu11]; secondary invariants of the next layer, Bunke–Naumann [BN09] and Crowley–Goette [CG10]; adiabatic limits on $S^3$-bundles over $S^4$, Goette–Kerin–Shankar [GKS20]. The homotopy-theoretic content of Theorems B(1), D and E is classical: the attaching maps of projective spaces (via cellular homology, $Sq^2$, the Chern character) and of Thom spaces of spinor bundles (via $J$), and $e'_{\mathbb R}(J(\text{generator}))=\pm B_{2k}/4k$.

**Classical or recalled, for §9.** Framed bordism and the stable stems [Po55, To62, Ad66]; $\nu^2,\sigma^2$ as products; Morse handle theory [Mi65]; the Witten genus and its characteristic series [Wi87, Wi88, Za88, HBJ92]; Eisenstein series of $\Gamma_1(3)$. The $f$-invariant is due to Laures [La99, La00]; its analytic and index-theoretic descriptions to Bunke–Naumann [BN08, BN09] and von Bodecker [vB08]; the values on products, including $f(\nu^2)$ and $f(\sigma^2)$, to von Bodecker [vB09]. The identity $\Theta[D^{4k},\Phi]=\hat A[D^{4k},\Phi]E_{2k}$ is implicit in these works (the defect $\bar m$ of an $\mathrm{im}\,J$ element is chosen exactly in this form); we make it explicit and spectral. The relation $\xi(D\otimes T_{\mathbb C})\equiv-21\xi(D)$ on $3$-manifolds is an elementary consequence of APS (we did not find it stated in this form). Products of Lie groups with left-invariant framings, and $SU(3)$, $Sp(2)$, $G_2$, $SU(4)$, $Sp(3)$ as framed manifolds, are classical [Os82; see Mi24b for which are non-null]; they are not, by themselves, Morse moduli spaces. The Stolz–Teichner programme [ST04, ST11] predicts that $2|1$ field theories represent $TMF$; secondary invariants of $2d$ $(0,1)$ theories detecting torsion were studied by Gaiotto–Johnson-Freyd–Witten and Gaiotto–Johnson-Freyd [GJFW19, GJF19], Berwick-Evans [BE23] and Tachikawa–Yamashita [TY23]. These concern the target-space theory, not Morse moduli; we use none of their results. Equivariant flow categories [CK23], flow-category calculus [LOS22] and orientation theories [HO26] do not address $tmf$-level or $f$-invariant detection of Morse moduli.

**New, to our knowledge** (after arXiv and web searches for: equivariant/isotropy framings of flow categories; Morse framings of projective spaces; $KO$- or Dirac-refined Morse complexes; $e$-invariants via $\eta$ and Lie or Morse framings; $\eta$-invariants of squashed and homogeneous $7$-spheres; spectral flow of Clifford-twisted Dirac operators on spheres; $f$-invariants and $tmf$-detection of Morse or flow-category moduli; realizations of $\nu^2$, $\sigma^2$ by symmetric moduli; and the authors listed above):
1. Theorem A: the Morse/CJS framing of a symmetric moduli space as the homogeneous framing twisted by $\rho_x\ominus\rho_z$, with the section-free form and the base-point formula (3.2).
2. Theorem B(2)–(3): the fermion-periodicity selection rule and the $KO$-refined Witten complex for even gradings (elementary given R1, R2 and Wood; we do not claim depth).
3. Theorem C as a statement about moduli spaces: the identification of the three APS terms with the moduli Dirac operator, a counterterm, and the spectral flow of moduli fermions coupled to the endpoint isotropy data.
4. Theorem D: the uniform stem-$3$ evaluation over all left-invariant metrics and twists with the exact block-by-block spectral flow; the explicit gradient flow of $\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda X)$ on $\mathbb{OP}^2$ through the quadratic representation and the identification of its Morse framing through the triality section.
5. Theorem E: a CJS/Morse realization of a generator of $\mathrm{im}\,J$ in every stem as a homogeneous moduli sphere with Clifford twist; the localization of the twisted spectral flow on one Killing spinor with linear eigenvalue; the Pfaffian-moment formula for the higher Dynkin index.
6. Theorem G: the triaxial formulas on $S^3$ (Schur's polynomial) and the closed formulas for $\eta(D)$ on all homogeneous $S^7$ of positive scalar curvature and $\eta_B$ on all homogeneous $S^7$, including the connectedness of the PSC locus and the Einstein value. Only the Berger cases were known.
7. Theorem F in stem $7$ and in general (elementary).
8. Theorems H–I: the product rule for moduli framings and its symmetric form with restricted isotropy, including non-product representations (elementary).
9. Theorem K: the equivariant realization and the join, giving $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$ as symmetric moduli spaces between adjacent critical points, with the parity rules $m\equiv1\ (4)$ and $m\equiv1,3,5,7\ (16)$. We found no prior realization of $\nu^2$ or $\sigma^2$ as symmetric moduli spaces between adjacent critical points, nor selection rules for them in terms of isotropy representations.
10. Theorem L: the $2|1$ three-term formula with Eisenstein-dressed spectral flow and its consequences.
11. Theorem M: the $f$-invariant of product moduli in spectral form, with exact certificates (the values are von Bodecker's).

**Novelty risk.** It is real. Each analytic tool is standard, and Theorems C–E can be viewed as careful applications of APS II, ABS, Bott and Adams to moduli spaces; Theorem 7.6(1) is a direct consequence of localization and Theorem 7.5 an exercise in Frobenius reciprocity once posed; the triaxial $S^3$ formulas may exist in the physics literature on Bianchi IX metrics. We believe the contribution is the single, uniform and checkable picture — endpoint data $\Rightarrow$ framing $\Rightarrow$ moduli Dirac invariants — from $\eta$ to all of $\mathrm{im}\,J$, together with the explicit formulas of Theorem G. For §9 the risk is larger: its homotopy-theoretic part is essentially "products of elements of $\mathrm{im}\,J$" plus von Bodecker's formula, the realization is by abstract handles rather than a natural manifold such as $\mathbb{HP}^n$, and by Theorem L(2) the $2|1$ formula contains no new information modulo $\mathbb Z[[q]]$ for a single sphere; what we claim is the moduli-space realization, the selection rules and the spectral normalization.

## 12. Limitations and open problems

1. **Symmetry and compactness.** Theorem A needs a transitive action on a closed moduli space. Most moduli spaces of most Morse functions have no such symmetry; for them (1.1) still holds but nothing makes its terms computable.
2. **Recalled realization inputs.** The homotopy reading uses R1–R2 (Franks/CJS/Abouzaid–Blumberg) and, for Thom spaces, the Conley-index identification; these are recalled, not reproved. For $k\ge3$ the moduli spheres live in total spaces of vector bundles; a closed-manifold realization would need a Morse–Smale perturbation of a function on $S(E_k\oplus\mathbb R)$, which we have not constructed. Theorem K, too, produces compact $G$-manifolds with boundary (isolating blocks); we did not find a natural closed homogeneous manifold, analogous to $\mathbb{HP}^n$ for $\nu$, in which $S^3\times S^3$ appears as a moduli space between adjacent critical points, and we do not prove that the double can be made equivariantly Morse–Smale (Remark 9.5).
3. **Odd gradings.** Theorem B(3) is limited to even gradings. We conjecture that for three consecutive gradings, closing interval components of $\mathcal M(x,z)$ by Lipshitz–Sarkar boundary matchings [LS14] makes $\delta_\eta$ well defined modulo the cellular differential and that $(\mathbb Z\langle\mathrm{Ob}\rangle\otimes KO_*,d+\eta\delta_\eta)$ computes the $d_2$-page; more generally, that a twisted complex over $ko$ built from the $KO$-classes of all moduli spaces computes $ko\wedge|\mathcal C|$. Evidence: Theorem B(3) and the width-$\le3$ calculus of [LOS22].
4. **Physical derivation.** We do not derive the $N=1$ moduli quantum mechanics from a single supersymmetric theory on $M$; $N=1$ quantum mechanics on $M$ admits no superpotential without an extra fermion. A real $Cl$-linear deformation using $\Lambda^\bullet=S\otimes S^*$ and keeping one Clifford factor is a natural candidate (conjectural). Parallels with the $(0,1)$ models of [GJFW19], where $24\mid k$ governs supersymmetry breaking, and with the Stolz–Teichner program [ST11] are noted only as such. Likewise we do not construct the $2|1$ theory on the moduli spaces of §9 (§9.7).
5. **Squashed $S^7$ spectral check.** Theorem G(2) for the squashed family rests on the variation formula, confirmed by two presentations but not by an independent spectral computation; the Dirac spectrum is known in closed form only at the Einstein point [NP83, EN21]. A direct check of $\eta=-\frac{76}{140625}$ needs Frobenius reciprocity for $Sp(2)\times Sp(1)$ with nonzero Nomizu map; Theorem 7.5 does not cover it.
6. **Non-PSC metrics.** Outside $\Omega$ harmonic spinors can occur and Theorem G(2) gives $\xi$ only modulo $\mathbb Z$; the jump locus is unknown (unlike Berger $S^3$, Theorem G(1)).
7. **Non-round metrics for $k\ge3$.** For $Sp(k)$-invariant metrics on $S^{4k-1}$ we have not computed $\xi$; the metric independence of (1.1) then holds only modulo $a_k\mathbb Z$ (exactly on PSC metrics connected to the round one, by Lemma 10.1).
8. **Beyond $\mathrm{im}\,J$: only decomposable elements.** §9 reaches $\nu^2$, $\sigma^2$ (and $\eta^2$, $\eta\sigma$ with product-type twists) through product moduli, but not the indecomposable $2$-line elements $\varepsilon,\bar\nu\in\pi_8^s$, $\kappa\in\pi_{14}^s$, $\bar\kappa\in\pi_{20}^s$. A natural next case is a free transitive $SU(3)$-moduli space: $SU(3)$ acts freely and transitively on the orthonormal pairs in $\mathbb C^3$, giving by Theorem K(1) a moduli space $V_2(\mathbb C^3)\subset S(\mathbb C^6)$ with $V_z=\mathbb R^3$ trivial, whose class is the left-invariant framing of $SU(3)$ twisted by $2\cdot\mathbb C^3$. Its $f$-invariant requires a non-product formula (von Bodecker's single-face formula [vB08] or Bunke–Naumann's tertiary index theorem [BN08]; see also [BN09, Bu11]); we have not done this. Non-adjacent cells and Toda brackets remain open.
9. **A single moduli sphere carries no $2|1$ information.** By Theorem L(2), modulo $\mathbb Z[[q]]$ the $2|1$ invariant of one moduli sphere is its $e$-invariant; new information enters only through the modular normalization (exact real $q$-series) and through products. The $f$-values themselves are von Bodecker's [vB09], and Theorem M uses his product lemma, proved in his thesis [vB08]; we re-certify only the non-vanishing of $\frac12a^2,\frac12b^2$. The consistency checks of §9.8(2)–(3) are exact but finite ($q^{60}$) and are evidence, not proofs.
10. **Signs.** The overall sign of $e$ (of $\nu$, $\sigma$ and their analogues) depends on one global orientation convention (§2.5(5)); within it all signs are determined. The opposite sign of $e_{\mathbb C}(x_3)$, $e_{\mathbb C}(x_7)$ in [vB09] does not affect §9 (§9.1).
11. **Numerics.** Numerical checks are evidence only; every statement used in a proof is proved in the text or checked exactly. The spectral check of §9.7 covers one Berger metric and order $q^1$.
