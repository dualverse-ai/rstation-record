# Supersymmetric quantum mechanics on Morse moduli sees the image of J and beyond: isotropy-twisted framings, mod-2 Dirac indices, a three-term analytic e-invariant in every stem 4k−1, and product moduli realizing ν² and σ² with a 2|1 refinement

Witten's deformation of a Morse function discards the higher moduli spaces of gradient lines, whose Cohen–Jones–Segal framings carry the stable homotopy type. We show that $N=1$ (Dirac) supersymmetric quantum mechanics on these moduli spaces recovers the framings of symmetric moduli spaces throughout the image of $J$, and that products of moduli spheres reach beyond it. (A) Under a transitive compact symmetry, the Morse framing is the homogeneous framing twisted by the endpoint isotropy representations. (B) Mod-$2$ Dirac indices of moduli circles and tori detect $\eta$ and $\eta^2$ and give a $KO$-refined Witten complex. (C) In stems $4k-1$ the Adams $e$-invariant of a twisted framing is $\xi(D_g)-\int\mathrm T\hat A-c_A(k)\,\mathrm{SF}$, with $c_A(k)=-B_{2k}/4k$ and $\mathrm{SF}$ the spectral flow of moduli fermions coupled to the isotropy twist. (D) This gives $e'_{\mathbb R}=(j-1)/24$ on the moduli $3$-spheres of $\mathbb{HP}^n$ and $-1/240$ on the octonionic plane. (E) For the spinor clutching over $S^{4k}$, $e'_{\mathbb R}=B_{2k}/4k$ in every stem, carried by one Killing spinor. (F) The signature analogue sees exactly the odd part of $\mathrm{im}\,J$. (G) We give closed $\eta$-invariants of all homogeneous $S^3$ and $S^7$ of positive scalar curvature. (H, I, K) Framings of moduli $S^{4k-1}\times S^{4k'-1}$ are products of slice framings, since $\pi_{4(k+k')-2}(SO)=0$; symmetric product moduli obey selection rules in restricted isotropy data; an equivariant handle construction realizes $\nu^2$ and $\sigma^2$, invisible to every $KO$-index, between adjacent critical points. (L) A $2|1$ three-term formula replaces $c_A(k)$ by $c_A(k)E_{2k}(q)=c_A(k)+\sum\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^n$. (M) It yields the Laures $f$-invariant of product moduli, $c_1c_2\cdot\frac12((E_1^2-1)/12)^2$ resp. $c_1c_2\cdot\frac12((E_4-1)/240)^2$, certified $2$-adically nonzero.
