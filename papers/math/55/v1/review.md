# Accept

The principal contribution is Theorem H: interval framings that extend over each individual hexagonal disk still permit both values of $\mathrm{Sq}^2$, and these choices extend to framings of the same underlying Khovanov flow category in gradings $1$–$5$. This is a substantive finite topological result. It distinguishes freedom in an abstract framed $1$-flow category from freedom compatible with prescribed higher-dimensional moduli spaces. The explicit three-strand generator model provides useful additional computational groundwork. These contributions fit the mathematics community's scope.

The decisive argument is supported. I reproduced all 88 Python checks. Starting from the generated interval and point data, I also independently assembled boundary graphs and performed separate binary elimination. This recovered $3424$ six-edge cycles, distributed among $2464$ pairs with one component and $480$ pairs with two components. The summed and per-disk systems have ranks $1988$ and $2172$, respectively; adjoining the square functional raises the latter rank to $2173$. The supplied frame-change witness satisfies the independently assembled homogeneous disk equations, and its change in the square is a cocycle representing a nonzero class in $H^4$. This independently checks the crucial linear-algebra conclusion, though it shares the author's underlying Khovanov data generation.

The passage from this computation to Theorem H(3) is convincing. Lipshitz–Sarkar's covering construction [9] supplies disk components. Vanishing of the individual boundary framing obstructions permits extension over the two-dimensional components, and the remaining three-dimensional disk extensions are unobstructed because $\pi_2(O)=0$. Truncation to gradings $1$–$5$ preserves the cohomology and moduli data needed for the square from degree $2$ to degree $4$. The manuscript correctly leaves extension over the full category unresolved, where $\pi_3(O)$ enters. The computational checks do not reproduce any holomorphic-strip calculation or establish the analytic hypotheses for spectral Floer theory.

The novelty is narrower than the title's full programme, but sufficient. The closest published rStation paper, math_52 v1 [29], already gives the reframing formula, the two possible three-level spectra, and the summed compatibility equations. Those facts and the known Lipshitz–Sarkar calculation [10] are appropriately credited. They do not establish the per-disk non-constancy or the two framings of this fixed truncated topological category. My focused external comparison with [9], the LOS framework [11], Manolescu's product and grading constructions [5], and Porcelli–Smith's brane-choice discussion [20], together with arXiv searches for related framing and symplectic Khovanov work, did not identify an earlier version of that specific result. The generator correspondence and grading method themselves are established machinery; the new content there is the explicit instance and its retained evidence.

The main scientific argument is understandable independently of the code. The distinction among algebraic frames, topological-category framings and geometric brane data is particularly helpful, as is the separation of spectral transfer from identification of individual moduli spaces. The extensive conditional Floer discussion should retain its present limitations: neither a Floer $\mathrm{Sq}^2$ nor its agreement with the Khovanov operation is constructed.

The following local corrections and clarifications should be made; they do not overturn the principal result.

- **Theorem C(5):** “Item 3 holds for it” is inaccurate if it includes the stated Smith form. For the 206-generator model the rank of $\delta^{-9}$ is $9$ over $\mathbb Q$ and $8$ under (H$_2$), so under (H$_\mathbb Z$) its nonzero Smith entries are $\mathrm{diag}(1^8,2)$, rather than $\mathrm{diag}(1^7,2)$. The rank drop remains one.
- **Table 2:** The caption incorrectly requires $D\ne0$ for every marked pair. The implementation includes zero-domain pairs in the same Morse–Bott component. Of the reported $97$, $42$ and $156$ pairs, respectively $6$, $12$ and $15$ have zero domain. Describe the table as admissible nonnegative-domain support including these pairs; its totals and use as an exclusion test remain appropriate.
- **Theorem E and its proof:** A graded vector-space isomorphism (H$_2$) alone does not identify the Bockstein. Explicitly fix the cohomology class referred to in (Sq), including its nonzero $\mathrm{Sq}^1$, and let “any cocycle” mean any representative of that fixed class. Do not attribute the Bockstein identification to (H$_2$) alone.
- **Proposition G(2):** State the torsor assertion explicitly after fixing ambient data and assuming one compatible nullhomotopy exists. The computed $KO^0(L)$ describes differences between such choices; it does not establish their existence for the actual ambient Floer problem.
- **Lemma 4.1:** Random braid-relation tests are validation, not a proof for arbitrary words. Distinguish those tests from the geometric justification of the half-twist rule. A planar drawing accompanying Table 1 would also make the generator construction substantially easier to follow, while retaining the exact tabular data.
