# Accept

The paper makes a useful, limited contribution: an explicit Chern–Simons regularization for a specified class of configurations near a planar knot, with a proof of its fractional grading and its behavior under reflection. This is relevant to the mathematics community and to the proposed Kapustin–Witten interpretation of the colored unknot. The absence of an existence theorem or a count is not a defect in this contribution: those questions, the charge bound, and the determinant signs are explicitly left open.

The central argument is convincing. The boundary decomposition into two solid tori permits a global boundary trivialization; the Nahm-face and infinity estimates remove the outer contribution; and the prescribed corner transition leaves a relative Chern–Simons term. Its evaluation uses the once-turning planar frame and gives $r/2$ modulo integers. The Higgs-field asymptotics constrain changes of admissible model gauge to a neighborhood of the stabilizer circle, which is the important ingredient in obtaining a real, gauge-independent limit rather than merely a congruence. Reflection then reverses both terms defining $P_{\rm reg}$, and the stated unsigned-count consequences follow. The coframe correction in Definition 2.2 addresses the mismatch between the transplanted straight field and the Nahm pole at the boundary face. The construction of configurations makes the result nonvacuous; it does not construct solutions.

The novelty should remain framed as this explicit regularization and its hypotheses. Witten's *Fivebranes and knots*, arXiv:1101.3216, already states $P\in\mathbb Z+j$ at the end of §3.7, develops the boundary Chern–Simons construction there, and explains the fractional grading through equation (5.62) and §5.4.2 [10]. The manuscript credits this correctly. In the supplied snapshot, math_68 v1 [9] already establishes the reflection covariance, but its obstruction uses a different reflection and rotational equivariance. Its result does not subsume the present odd-weight obstruction without equivariance. The straight-knot uniqueness and Coulomb vacuum used here are also properly attributed to [7] and [8]. The explicit planar transition calculation and the controlled regularization are sufficient incremental content, although neither the congruence itself nor the general symmetry principle is new.

The scope qualifications are important. Mazzeo–Witten, arXiv:1712.00835 [5], treats generalized Nahm-pole boundary conditions for curved knots, including transplantation in §2 and polyhomogeneity in Theorem 9.6. The present paper has not matched those expansions to its particular uniform condition (K.i), corner condition (K.ii), or (K1). Accordingly, the theorem establishes a grading for $\mathcal C(K,r)$; application to a naturally defined KW moduli space remains conditional. Likewise, the unsigned polynomial is not a computation of the signed Jones invariant, and the regularization's framing dependence should retain the explicit distinction from Witten's physical framing anomaly.

I reproduced all 192 checks in `verify.py`, obtaining `FALLOS = 0` with Python 3.11.2, SymPy 1.11.1, mpmath 1.2.1, and NumPy 1.24.2. These checks corroborate the local algebra and sampled integral evaluations. They do not independently establish the global extension argument, analytic limits, existence of solutions, or a moduli-space count; those must be judged from the proof and its assumptions.

Several local corrections and clarifications would improve the final text without changing the central result:

- In §4.2, Step 4, the explanatory identity $\operatorname{Ad}(W^{-1})\zeta=\zeta$ is false for the displayed $W$. The boundary-term cancellation itself is correct. Its relevant pairing is $\langle\operatorname{Ad}(W^{-1})t_3,\zeta\rangle=\langle t_3,\operatorname{Ad}(W)\zeta\rangle=0$, using $\operatorname{Ad}(W)\zeta=\varphi' t_1$. Correct the explanatory sentence.
- The bound $|\partial_yv|\le C/R$ following Definition 2.2 is not uniform up to the face: already at $r=1$, $-\partial_yv=1/y+y/R^2$. Restrict the accompanying $O(1)$ comparison with the straight field to angular sectors away from the face, consistently with Lemma 2.2′.
- Specify smooth polar profiles at the tube core. Vanishing of $\beta(\psi)$ to order $r$ alone does not ensure that $\beta(\psi)e^{ir\theta'}$ is smooth in Cartesian transverse coordinates. Similarly, the radial parametrization of the hemisphere extension should ensure smoothness at its core. These are local cutoff choices, but Proposition 2.5 asserts smooth configurations and should state them accurately.
- Clarify that the local frame gauges and transitions are $SO(3)$-valued throughout. The frame loop $W$ is nonliftable around the longitude even for even $r$; an interpretation requiring all these local gauges to be single-valued $SU(2)$ maps needs additional qualification.

The scientific presentation is otherwise clear: the main contribution, proof strategy, assumptions, and unproved counting inputs are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative. The lengthy verification catalogue in §5 could be shortened and moved largely to the supplement, but it does not obscure the argument enough to affect acceptance.
