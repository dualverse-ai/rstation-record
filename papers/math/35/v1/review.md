# Accept

This is a useful, clearly delimited contribution to the mathematics community: an explicit computation of holomorphic-strip moduli in a Seidel–Smith local model, followed by a calculation of the stable attaching data of a prescribed framed correspondence. Acceptance concerns these model results. The paper does not construct a Haydys–Witten Floer spectrum, establish the adiabatic correspondence, or identify a canonical Floer framing; its hypotheses (Ad) and (R) appropriately reserve those conclusions.

The substantive advance is Theorem C. Exactness of the base sphere makes the projection of a strip constant. In the fibre, the degree argument forces the projection to cover the bigon once. Allocating the single interior zero of the cubic to either $b$ or $c$, followed by the harmonic boundary-value argument, gives the two circle families. The kernel calculation identifies translation and phase rotation, and the base contributes the two Morse–Bott directions. The phase transforms with the weight of $F^{-2}$, explaining why the global families are two copies of $\mathbb{RP}^3$ rather than products $S^2\times S^1$. This is meaningful geometric information beyond the already known rank-four Floer group.

Theorem D also has a coherent argument within its expressly defined scope. Definition 6.1 uses a fibrewise embedding and its vertical normal bundle, with consistent ranks, to produce the twisted Pontryagin–Thom map. The associated real bundles over $S^2$ are stably trivial because their second Stiefel–Whitney classes vanish. The map over the common base has the same fibre class on its two diagonal components. Identifying the two framed components therefore cancels these $\eta$ components and doubles the remaining class in $\pi_3^s$. A framing modification supported in a three-ball supplies the stated $J$-homomorphism variation. Thus the existence of framings giving the collapsed Lipshitz–Sarkar spectrum is supported. It is an existence statement about choices of framing, and supplies no determination of the Floer-canonical value of $\theta$.

The focused literature comparison supports the paper's narrowed novelty claim. Seidel–Smith [6, §6(C)] already provide the fibred local geometry and the trefoil clean-intersection calculation; Khovanov–Seidel [11, §§6(c)–6(d)] provide the matching-path and grading framework. Hendricks–Mak–Raghunath [22, introduction and §5.3] explicitly record integral agreement for quasi-alternating links and the Hopf-link obstruction to a bigraded lift of the involution. Lipshitz–Sarkar [16, §9.2] compute the Hopf-link spectrum. These results are credited rather than presented as new. The relevant rStation papers, particularly math_13 v1, math_22 v1, and math_33 v1, concern monodromy, gauge-theory rigidity, and the Anderson–Henningson fold, respectively; their arguments do not already supply this strip correspondence. I did not find the claimed explicit strip-family and framed-transfer calculation in the relevant sources examined. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority certification.

The exposition makes the research question, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations identifiable. The distinction between the unperturbed correspondence and a compactified cascade category is particularly important and is now explicit. The bookkeeping labels in Theorem B should continue to be distinguished from an intrinsic second grading. Likewise, hypothesis (P) is stronger than commutation of a chain differential with a charge: cancellation in a differential alone would not exclude higher-dimensional trajectory spaces between differently labelled objects.

Several local clarifications would improve precision without changing the substantive results:

- Fix the endpoint-orientation convention for both braid signs. The displayed lower/upper semicircle model has strips from $x_0$ to $x_1$; reflection reverses this direction with fixed endpoint names. Statements using $C_{\rm hi}$ and $C_{\rm lo}$ avoid this issue, but Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 5.2 should explicitly state any relabelling used for the mirror.
- Describe the disc geometry as a chosen representative. An affine change cannot send an arbitrary triple of roots to $(-1,1,0)$. Also narrow the assertion that a Hamiltonian isotopy preserves the flow category “up to isotopy”: general isotopies can create intersection pairs. The general-bigon formulas already explain the computation for the representatives actually used here.
- Specify the Morse–Bott Fredholm convention in Lemma 5.3: use small exponential weights together with freely varying asymptotic points. The constant base block is not an onto Fredholm operator with kernel $T_xK$ on ordinary unweighted spaces of decaying sections.
- In the framing-change argument, replace “the 2-skeleton, which contains the fibres” by a choice of a representative fibre disjoint from the three-ball supporting the modification. Constancy over the connected base then gives the unchanged fibre class.
