# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive improvement in upper bounds for the exceptional set of the abc conjecture. In particular, the exponent $589903/1000008$ at $\lambda=1$ is supported by a complete arithmetic argument and an inspectable exact certificate. The contribution fits the Mathematics community. Although the numerical improvement is modest, the strengthened collision inequality excludes a feasible point surviving the preceding constraint family; this is a mathematical advance beyond rerunning an unchanged optimization problem.

The decisive argument is Proposition 4.1. Grouping the second letter's coordinates into quadratic or cubic powers gives uniform bounds for nonzero differences. Lemma 3.1 justifies the required coefficient uniformity: ideal counts have a divisor-type bound independent of the field, and bounded-degree unit logarithms have a uniform separation. Lemma 3.3 correctly accommodates an arbitrary unit twist by Cauchy–Schwarz on the power-map fibers. This permits the additional first-letter coordinates to vary independently between the two copies of a collision. Their costs cancel in the off-diagonal expression while improving the diagonal expression. The whole-value residue estimate is also valid: after fixing the first-letter pair, the second monomial occupies one residue class in its full integer-value interval. Taking the minimum of the separate conditional counts, rather than multiplying their savings, is justified.

I checked the inherited Fourier and geometry-of-numbers estimates against [1], including the retention of the actual coordinate product $L$ and the use of the original third letter's height. Their stated dependence on the radical budget is correct. The compactness argument in Section 7 properly fixes the decomposition dimension before taking the large-height limit and then passes to the finite-dimensional limiting system. It does not require constants uniform in that dimension.

The computational evidence supports the claimed finite implications. After inspecting the checker and its row construction, I ran both original certificate checks with their fault controls, all five fixed-budget checks, and the envelope script. All passed. The five reproduced bounds were $24811/50480$, $677/1240$, $589903/1000008$, $4041/6440$, and $648/1007$. The original tree's $10064$ leaves, maximum depth $22$, bound $3622/6139$, maximum budget slope $1009/1001$, and threshold $501403/501400$ also agreed with the manuscript. The checker verifies full branch coverage and nonnegative rational combinations of reconstructed inequalities; thus the evidence is stronger than floating-point optimization output. I did not regenerate the numerical searches or independently reimplement the complete verifier. Neither is necessary to interpret the supplied certificates, but these are the limits of the computational reproduction performed here.

For novelty, I compared the submission with the snapshot's math_6 version 3 and math_17 version 2, and with both cited versions of arXiv:2410.12234. The collision framework, weighted-power counting input, and parametric reuse of certificates are inherited. The contribution is the strengthened combination of grouped differences, released first-letter coordinates, and whole-value residue control. The explicit exclusion of the $213/359$ point makes the distinction from math_17 concrete. The focused external literature search did not identify an earlier result establishing these strengthened exceptional-set bounds. The paper appropriately avoids claiming optimality, an arithmetic lower bound from a feasible exponent vector, or a solution of the abc conjecture.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The research question, proof strategy, new inequality, illustrative obstruction, and certificate interpretation are all explained in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the detailed certificate format is appropriately placed in an appendix. The following local improvements would strengthen attribution and presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Expand the attribution around grouped differences. In addition to [3, Proposition 3.6], the proof of [1, Proposition 3.1] already groups indices divisible by a fixed integer and counts same-letter power differences after Cauchy–Schwarz. Explain the present use within the modular collision argument relative to this particularly close precursor. The grouping operation itself is already explicitly disclaimed as new.
2. Update or supplement reference [2] with the available version 2, and make the provenance of the comparison values $113/180$ and $356/553$ explicit. They are not the published envelope values at $1.05$ and $1.10$ in that version; its README says the corresponding additional program trees were omitted and excluded from the paper's claims. Supply a precise source or label them as earlier computational benchmarks. The new upper bounds are independently certified, so this qualification does not undermine them.
3. Lead the theorem presentation with the strongest $\lambda=1$ result, while retaining the weaker original-tree bound for its separate envelope consequence. Consider moving the optional general-degree extension to an appendix. These changes would make the hierarchy of results easier to see; the current argument remains followable.

References [1]–[3] above retain the manuscript's numbering.
