# Accept

This is an appropriate attribution and comparison update to the accepted paper. The additions in Sections 1 and 8, reference [4], and the metadata abstract acknowledge an earlier, stronger result at $\lambda=1$ while identifying where the existing budget-dependent bounds remain improvements. They do not introduce a new substantive contribution or alter the accepted proofs. The manuscript remains organized around its collision inequalities, their arithmetic justification, and the certified counting bounds.

The Zenodo record confirms the publication date of 29 August 2026. Zhang's manuscript [4, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2] states the uniform exponent $34/59$ for $0<\lambda\le1$ and the pointwise minimum quoted here. Its introduction and proof strategy also support the account in Section 8: retained valuation layers, the primitive-slope and square-full estimates, and the exposed fifth-power estimate lead to $59D-12T_m\le34$. The distinction from this paper's grouped collision differences and twisted energy estimate is clear. The revised citation to [2], version 4, retains the previously accepted comparison values.

Exact rational evaluation confirms the new numerical comparisons. At $\lambda=4/5$ and $9/10$, Zhang's quoted pointwise exponents are respectively $8/15$ and $34/59$, both larger than the corresponding bounds in Theorem 1.2. Reading the unchanged main-tree leaf bounds and budget multipliers gives the first crossing with $(23\lambda+3)/40$ at exactly $3331/2849$; the envelope is strictly below both comparison lines throughout the asserted open interval. The fixed-budget bounds at $1.05$ and $1.10$ also improve on that envelope. These checks evaluate the retained rational data; I did not rerun the unchanged certificate verifications or independently reverify Zhang's complete proof.

For reproducibility, add the crossing calculation to `parameters.py` or give its short derivation beside the comparison. The leaf line attaining the crossing is $(1995\lambda-758)/2107$, and its intersection with $(23\lambda+3)/40$ gives the stated endpoint. This is a minor documentation suggestion, not a condition of acceptance. The revised discussion appropriately prevents the bound at $\lambda=1$ from being mistaken for the strongest published exponent.
