# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive extension of the first-order knot-link spectral analysis from $SU(2)$ to $SU(3)$, including labels with one zero component. It fits the mathematics community's scope. The decisive contribution is the exclusion of the exceptional kernel in rank two, together with the algebraic identities that connect it to the full operator. The conclusion is appropriately restricted to the straight model knot; the broader operator-theoretic corollaries remain conditional on $(N_3)$.

The proof has a coherent chain of implications. The Type I Weitzenböck identity and the Friedrichs-domain argument force eigenfunctions with $|s|<\lambda_+$ into $E_0$. The angular identity then restricts their eigenvalues to integers. Section 4.4 supplies the essential additional argument excluding zero: the scalar blocks cannot be bounded at the pole; the two regular Toda zero modes form a Lagrangian subspace for the conserved symplectic form, and their independent slopes at infinity exclude a nonzero admissible solution; the remaining blocks reduce to a trace-free system whose pole-bounded solution is proportional to the displayed $p$, which is not square-integrable at the equator. These are analytic exclusions, not conclusions drawn from a bounded numerical search. The endpoint construction from an $M_S$ ground state and the $J_t,J_\rho$ symmetries then supplies the attained spectral endpoints.

The domain argument in Proposition 2.5 is concise but identifies the relevant mechanisms: the nonnegative leading inverse-square potential, Hardy control, reduction to fixed angular weights, and the limit-point threshold $|b|\ge\tfrac12$, comfortably satisfied by the stated face spectrum. Smoothness at the interior pole supplies the other endpoint condition. These steps justify the domain control used in the spectral argument. They are not consequences of the symbolic tests, and I assessed them separately.

The novelty distinction is supported by comparison with math_83 [13] and math_87 [14]. The former supplies the $SU(2)$ proof architecture; the latter supplies the $SU(3)$ second-order operator and Toda zero modes, but explicitly leaves the first-order problem open. The rank-two block analysis here is an additional mathematical argument, rather than just another parameter evaluation. The external comparison is consistent with this distinction: He–Mazzeo [9, Proposition 5.6] gives the second-order indicial formula and $\gamma_0>2$; Mazzeo–Witten [4] analyzes the knot problem for $SU(2)/SO(3)$; Bleher [19, Section 7] gives higher-rank face indicial data but states the cited knot exclusion for $SU(2)$. The focused literature check did not reveal a prior statement of the claimed $SU(3)$ first-order window.

I inspected and reran the supplied verifier. All 70 checks passed in approximately 150 seconds, including the symbolic meridian reductions, face matrix calculation, and numerical cross-checks. This reproduces the supplied computations, not an independent formal certification of the entire theorem. The floating-point Ritz calculations remain corroboration: upper approximations alone cannot exclude missing low eigenvalues. The manuscript correctly bases that exclusion on its proof.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, imported inputs, new kernel argument, and conditional consequences are identifiable in the main text; detailed computational records are kept in the supplement. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central result:

- In Table 1, identify the imported values as Cartan-sector estimates $\lambda_0$. For $(1,0)$, math_87 does not prove $\lambda_0=\gamma_0$; that identification is numerical. Thus the displayed numerical endpoint for this row also needs that qualification. The theorem itself uses the actual $\gamma_0$ and is unaffected.
- Narrow the attribution sentence in Section 1.2: the restriction to $SU(2)/SO(3)$ applies to the cited knot analysis, not to all of Mazzeo–Witten's pure Nahm-pole work [5]. Explicitly acknowledge the higher-rank face indicial calculation in Bleher [19, Proposition 7.4].
- Part G's independence test substitutes one label pair and freely chosen profile values. Describe it as a sample check, rather than an all-label certificate, and make the general nonvanishing/independence justification explicit alongside the translation construction.
- Use different symbols for the eigenvalue and $\sin\psi$ in Section 4.4. In Section 4.1, replace “non-symmetric part of $\mathcal V$” with terminology specifying the commutator/derivative contribution: $\mathcal V$ itself is symmetric. These local notation issues do not obscure the overall argument.
