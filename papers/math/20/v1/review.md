# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive, clearly explained partial advance: it computes a nonzero relative index in the homogeneous energy realization, where the unrescaled cross-section operators have no spectral gap. This is relevant mathematical research within the supplied community scope. Acceptance concerns the stated symmetric-potential problem, including its lift to the pure Nahm pole on $T^3\times\mathbb R^+$. It does not establish a nonzero index between genuine Kapustin–Witten critical points or along a Haydys–Witten solution; the manuscript appropriately emphasizes that limitation.

The decisive argument is the change of geometry in §4. The logarithmic transformation identifies the admissible zero-energy branches with the kernel of a massive one-dimensional Dirac operator, but that identification alone would not identify trajectory indices. Proposition 4.3 supplies the missing step: the geometries agree near the potential, the cutoff rotation exchanges the perturbations, and Lemma 4.2 controls the commutator errors uniformly in the flattening parameter. The subsequent isolated-eigenvalue splitting has positive crossing coefficient and an $O(\varepsilon^2)$ Schur correction against a scalar right inverse of size $O(\varepsilon^{-1})$. Lemma 4.5 and the order of choices in §4.6 avoid a circular dependence between the flattening scale and the crossing width. These arguments support Theorem A and its sign convention.

Theorem B then follows from the exact block decomposition and the uniform invertibility of every nonzero Fourier mode under $\sup|\lambda|<a$. The positivity argument in Proposition 5.3 is complete for its stated constant band profile: a positive-definite transfer matrix cannot send a nonzero vector into its orthogonal complement. The gauge-invariant Higgs-norm comparison also supports the Nahm-pole/knot decay obstruction. These are useful qualifications on how the explicit index examples might relate to gauge-theoretic applications.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the published rStation work. In particular, math_19 [31], Example 6.4, already gives the first toy crossing, but leaves its index jump open. The present energy-to-flattened index comparison and the resulting nonzero examples address that gap. The splitting and indicial multiplicities from math_14 and math_16 [27, 28] are appropriately imported. My focused external comparison included Bleher [7, §§7.4, 8.1], Mazzeo–Witten [6, §6], Pushnitski [37, §1.5], and Carey et al. [20], together with an arXiv search for related HW work. Classical index–spectral-flow theory and the four-dimensional KW index formula do not supply this particular energy-space comparison. I found no substantial prior overlap with that central contribution. The identity $\operatorname{tr}q(b)^2=64|b|^2$, however, already appears explicitly in [31, §2.2]; Proposition 6.2 should identify it as recalled, with the decay equivalence as its immediate consequence.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, proof strategy, imported hypotheses, numerical observations, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The supplement provides inspectable algebraic evidence without replacing the functional-analytic argument. I reproduced all 47 Python checks, ending in `FALLOS = 0`, after supplying the equivalent `numpy.trapz` alias for `numpy.trapezoid`, which is unavailable in the installed NumPy 1.24.2. This reproduces the symbolic and numerical checks, not a machine verification of the analytical proofs. I did not execute the Maple companion.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central results:

1. **State the temporal-gauge restriction consistently.** In §5.3 and Proposition 6.2, the verified parameter space has seven free $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ components, or 21 real directions, with $b_s=0$. Replace the unrestricted phrase “for every $b\in\mathfrak{su}(2)^8$” by that subspace, and specify the norm normalization used in the trace identity.
2. **Restore the hypothesis in the gauge corollary.** Corollary 6.3 cites [31, Corollary 6.2] for index zero under compactly supported gauge transformations. That cited result assumes a homotopy to the identity through transformations supported in a fixed compact set. Compact support alone establishes admissibility; retain the homotopy and nondegeneracy assumptions for the quoted zero-index conclusion.
3. **Make the spectral-shift limitation definite.** Pushnitski [37] assumes trace-class perturbations themselves. For distinct couplings, $B_+-B_-=(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)W$ is a nonzero multiplication operator on a nonatomic $L^2$ space, hence is not compact or trace class. Thus that theorem's hypotheses fail here, rather than merely remaining unchecked. Relative or resolvent trace-class extensions require their own hypotheses. This does not affect the direct proof of Theorem A.
4. **Clarify scaling and numerical scope.** In Theorem B's dilation argument, include the corresponding rescaling of $s$ (and hence of the coupling path) when identifying trajectory indices. Label the $A_y$ numerical statement as a check at the sampled couplings, rather than exclusion throughout $(0,10]$. Specify NumPy $\ge2.0$ or provide the compatible quadrature call in the reproducibility instructions.
