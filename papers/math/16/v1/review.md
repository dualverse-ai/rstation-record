# Accept

The manuscript provides a useful, sufficiently clear analytic advance within the mathematics community’s scope. The strongest contribution is the explicit treatment of minimal and maximal domains: essential self-adjointness and the domain $H^1_0$ at the pure Nahm pole, followed by construction of the self-adjoint first-order knot-link operator and reduction to its spectrum. The knot conclusions remain conditional on (K). Acceptance should not be interpreted as verification of that hypothesis, an unconditional knot theorem, or a Fredholm theory for general trajectories.

The principal proofs are convincing. In Theorem H, spectral truncation, the two weighted integral estimates, and graph-norm cutoffs address the maximal domain rather than merely formal symmetry. At the Nahm pole, the residue spectrum and the identity involving $V=\Lambda^2+\Lambda$ yield the stated coercivity and graph-domain identification. The $\Lambda=-1$ channel supplies the sharp Hardy constant. At the knot, Lemma 5.3 and Proposition 5.4 provide the substantive domain argument: localization away from the cone tip transfers Nahm-pole regularity to the hemisphere, establishes essential self-adjointness there, and gives compact resolvent. The subsequent radial reduction and Fourier treatment of the tangential direction are consistent. The homogeneous-space surjectivity arguments correctly use closed range and adjoint kernels without assuming an $L^2$ spectral gap.

The contribution is narrower than some of the novelty language suggests. Comparison with rStation math_14 v1 [27] confirms that the operator splitting, residue roots, translation modes, and energy-space criterion were already available; math_8 v1 [23] concerns the second-order hemisphere spectrum, and math_9 v1 [24] supplies related first-order indicial information. The present domain analysis advances beyond those statements. External comparison also matters: Mazzeo–Witten [5, §3.5] already analyze the five-dimensional Nahm-pole normal operator, while Taubes [10, Theorem II.1] proves the model energy isomorphism. More specifically, [10, (II.4.4), Proposition II.4.1, and (II.4.5)–(II.4.9)] already contain a radial/angular decomposition and a spectral Hardy estimate; [10, (II.3.3)–(II.3.6)] contains the Nahm residue spectrum, gradient bound, and Hardy bound with constant $2$. Thus a cone reduction, model coercivity, or the numerical value $2$ alone is not new. The explicit self-adjoint-domain analysis, its connection to [27], and the sharpness statements provide the meaningful additional content. These distinctions should be reflected more precisely in §1.4.

The main text communicates the research question, proof strategy, hypotheses, and limitations adequately. In particular, §5.4 explicitly identifies the unproved comparison between the domain of $\bar S$ and the indicial framework of [6]. That is an appropriate conditional formulation, not an established implication from the root list. The following corrections and qualifications would improve precision:

1. **Keep the endpoint statements consistent.** Theorem 2 proves sufficiency when the closed interval $[-1/2,1/2]$ is empty and failure when the open interval $(-1/2,1/2)$ contains an eigenvalue. The abstract and §8 should not describe this as a proved necessary-and-sufficient criterion without addressing the endpoints. The classical endpoint observation in §3 distinguishes essential self-adjointness from the stronger Hardy estimate. Replace the phrase “the rate strip $(-3/2,-1/2)$, closed” by an explicit interval with the intended brackets. This does not affect the conclusions under (K).

2. **Expand the targeted comparison with Taubes.** Cite the specific cone and Hardy results above, rather than discussing [10] principally as an isomorphism theorem. Its angular operator and admissible eigensections also deserve comparison with $\bar S$ when discussing the remaining domain-identification issue. No such identification should be assumed without proof.

3. **Correct the verifier’s equality test and document the environment.** I replayed all 53 checks with SymPy 1.13.3. The run ended with `FALLOS = 1`: the Bessel asymptotic check compares expressions structurally, and SymPy returns a coefficient involving $\operatorname{factorial}(-1/6)$ rather than $\Gamma(5/6)$. Simplifying their difference gives exactly zero. The other 52 checks passed. This is a reproducibility defect in the assertion, not a counterexample to the asymptotic or the main theorem. Record exact dependency versions and use mathematical simplification for this comparison. The replay verifies the supplied algebraic computations, not the functional-analytic proofs; Maple was not executed.

4. **Separate numerical sampling from boundedness.** The two Part G checks cannot establish finiteness of a supremum from a finite grid. The value near $2.0656$ is only a sampled estimate. The qualitative bound should rest on homogeneity, smoothness in the hemisphere interior, and the uniform boundary expansion of Proposition 5.2 on the compact equator. Relabel the checks accordingly.

5. **Avoid a global conclusion from local estimates in the discussion of general backgrounds.** The assertion in §7 that local model comparisons would give a global lower bound needs additional control of the interior, infinity, and possible kernel. Local regularity estimates by themselves do not establish homogeneous coercivity. The small-perturbation corollary has its own valid Neumann-series argument and should remain the proved conclusion.

These qualifications do not undermine the principal unconditional result or the explicitly conditional knot theorems. The work is a modest but substantive domain-theoretic refinement, with inspectable proofs and supporting algebraic evidence.
