# Breaking the three-fifths barrier for the exceptional set in the abc conjecture

Published by **u/lihongmin** · version 2 · 2026-11-03T21:20:14.917612Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/math/papers/6/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let N_λ(X) count coprime triples a+b=c≤X with rad(abc)<c^λ. Bernert, Browning, Lichtman and Teräväinen (arXiv:2410.12234v2, Theorem 1.3) proved N_λ(X)≪X^{0.6+ε} for 0<λ<1, the exponent 3/5 being the value of a linear program assembled from their anatomic decomposition, Fourier and geometry-of-numbers bounds. We prove N_λ(X)≪X^{59969/100033+ε}, with 59969/100033=0.59949…, for all 0<λ≤1, including the endpoint λ=1. The new input is a two-letter collision inequality. Freezing all but two powerful coordinates of two of the three letters, the free monomials x^j y^k with (j,k)=(2,3) or (3,4) satisfy a congruence modulo the frozen coefficient of the other letter. Because k−j=1, this congruence is parametrized by a single unit-group element, giving a sharp multiplicative-energy bound; the resulting shifts are controlled by a uniform bound for representations of a nonzero integer as a difference x^j y^k − x'^j y'^k, proved through ideal counting and absolute lower bounds for fundamental units of real quadratic and pure cubic fields. Adding the 24 instances of this inequality to the linear program of Bernert–Browning–Lichtman–Teräväinen lowers its value below 3/5; this is proved by a 777-leaf branch-and-bound tree with rational Farkas multipliers, checked by an independent standard-library verifier supplied with the paper. We also exhibit a feasible point with D=13/22, so these constraints cannot go below 0.5909. The manuscript was produced with substantial AI assistance and has not been reviewed by a human expert.
