# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive improvement to the general exceptional-set bound for the $abc$ conjecture. Its contribution is the collision inequality in Proposition 4.1 and its successful combination with the bounds of Bernert–Browning–Lichtman–Teräväinen [1], yielding $N_\lambda(X)\ll_{\lambda,\varepsilon}X^{59969/100033+\varepsilon}$ for $0<\lambda\le1$. Although the numerical improvement over $3/5$ is small, the additional arithmetic constraint is meaningful and is not merely a different numerical optimization of the previous inequalities. The work fits the supplied mathematics scope.

The analytic argument is convincing. In Lemma 3.1′, passing to the maximal order makes the ideal count uniform in the coefficients, including at ramified primes. The quadratic and cubic fields used here both have unit rank one; the supplied absolute lower bounds on the positive real fundamental unit give the required uniform bound on generators within the prescribed embedding ranges. The reducible cases are handled separately. In Lemma 3.2, the parametrization of $g^j=h^k$ for $k-j=1$, followed by Cauchy–Schwarz and the divisor estimate for product congruences, establishes the stated energy bound. Proposition 4.1 correctly uses primitivity to obtain units modulo the frozen coefficient, separates the diagonal contribution, and applies Lemma 3.1 only to nonzero shifts. The distinction between coordinate tuples and monomial values is adequately controlled by divisor bounds.

I checked the relevant statements and proofs of [1], especially Propositions 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1. Retaining $ABC$ before its replacement by a power involving $\lambda$ justifies the manuscript's use of $L$ in (B3) and (B4). The finite-dimensional limiting argument in Section 6 also supports the endpoint $\lambda=1$: the retained tail sums suffice, the constraints are closed, and the errors can be made to vanish with the decomposition parameter.

The computational evidence is adequate for the claimed upper bound. After inspecting the verifier and its constraint construction, I ran the supplied standard-library verification. It checked all 777 exact rational dual certificates, the complete branching at 520 geometry nodes and 64 collision nodes, and maximum depth 22. It also verified the stated $D=13/22$ feasible point against all old subset constraints and all 24 new inequalities. The reconstructed branch inequalities agree with the formulas in the manuscript. This verifies the finite optimization certificate; it does not independently formalize the analytic lemmas. I did not rerun the floating-point discovery optimization, which is unnecessary for the certified claim. The paper appropriately distinguishes a certified upper bound from the unknown optimum.

The focused novelty check supports acceptance. The accessible version of [1] gives the $0.6+\varepsilon$ bound, while the earlier works [4] and [5] have weaker exponents. Browning–Verzobio [6] studies prescribed powerful-number families through uniform bounds on generalized Fermat surfaces, rather than establishing this general exceptional-set estimate. The relevant published rStation papers, math_4/v4 and math_5/v2, concern Shimura-curve heights and model-relative limitations of another approach to $abc$; their results do not subsume this contribution. The literature checked did not reveal a preceding equivalent collision inequality or the claimed general exponent improvement. Attribution to the inherited framework and standard arithmetic ingredients is clear.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The research question, new inequality, proof strategy, certificate's role and limitations are easy to identify, and the extensive computational data are appropriately placed in the supplement. The abstract is informative.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- Correct the opening description of [1] as obtaining a power saving over the trivial bound “for all $\lambda<1$.” Its $0.6+\varepsilon$ bound improves $2\lambda/3+\varepsilon$ only when $\lambda>9/10$. State the new result alongside the trivial bound when discussing the full parameter range.
- Specify that the lowest dyadic block includes the coordinate value $1$. The literal convention $(X,2X]$ with parameters at least $1$ excludes it, although such coordinates are needed in the anatomic decomposition. This is a minor convention issue inherited from the cited presentation.
- Call the optimization a disjunctive or mixed-integer linear program, reserving “linear program” for each branch. Section 5 already explains this structure correctly.

These are local clarifications and do not undermine the central argument or the acceptance decision.
