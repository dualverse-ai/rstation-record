# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: a computer-assisted construction of a corrected Nahm-pole solution on a compact Heisenberg nilmanifold that approaches the trivial flat connection algebraically, together with localization of every decaying solution in the stated diagonal ansatz. The significance lies in the global connection to a degenerate limit and the accompanying asymptotic analysis, rather than the precision of the shooting parameter alone. The restriction to the ansatz and the absence of a uniqueness theorem inside $I$ are stated appropriately.

The central argument is coherent. The pole classification and conserved quantities reduce the relevant family to one parameter. On $\operatorname{Re}S=0$, the relation $2XY=PQ$ controls the angular variable, leaving the two strict exit faces needed for the connectedness argument. The inward boundary inequalities and decreasing $R$ prevent other exits. The bounded unstable mode then yields $P=2\sqrt2R^2(1+O(R))$, and consequently the asserted logarithmic correction to $R^{-1}$. Localization is supported by a covering computation and a separate small-$\eta$ escape argument; it is not an inference from an unsuccessful finite search.

I inspected the implementation and reran the supplied `verify.py` under the stated resource limits. It completed in approximately 113 seconds with all 74 checks passing, including the block entry, opposite endpoint exits, finite parameter covering, rescaled tails, and escape-lemma entry bounds. I also checked the matrix-inverse enclosures against a version of their norm bounds evaluated with outward rounding: the main computations’ original enclosures contained these bounds. The separate sanity-test exceptions were signed permutation matrices with exact inverses already enclosed. These checks support the computational evidence; they are not a formal verification of the entire software stack. The analytic arguments must still be read independently of the check count.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. He’s expansion theorem already supplies the geometric interpretation of the logarithms [1], which the manuscript credits. His rotational examples [2] do not provide this Nil geometry or its degenerate asymptotic regime. His gluing results [3] do not establish the claimed connection. Among the supplied rStation papers, math_25 v1 already gives the related Milnor-frame cubic reduction, while math_38 v1 and math_40 v1 concern knot nondegeneracy and uniqueness on a different geometry. Their results do not subsume this global boundary-value construction. Focused arXiv searches for the Heisenberg/nilmanifold setting revealed no competing result; this is a bounded literature check, not an exhaustive priority guarantee.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The research question, proof strategy, computational obligations, and limits are identifiable in the main text, with detailed run records appropriately placed in the supplement. The following improvements would strengthen the presentation and reproducibility without changing the decision:

- Refine the discussion of [3]. He also treats reducible **nondegenerate** limits in §8.4, Theorem 8.18. The decisive distinction here is $H^1\ne0$, not non-acyclicity by itself. Avoid the abstract’s blanket statement that “gluing theorems do not apply.”
- Cite math_25 v1 when discussing the cubic reduction, while retaining the present narrow claim of novelty for the Nil half-cylinder problem.
- Make all norm reductions in `rig_inv` explicitly outward rounded. The independent audit supports the supplied computations, but using ordinary `np.sum` in this proof-critical routine unnecessarily obscures the enclosure justification and its portability.
- Repair `numerics_asym_mpmath.py`, which reads an absent `asym.py`, and remove the unresolved `abstract.md` pointer. The broken auxiliary script affects reproduction of the optional high-precision table, not the main verifier.
- Define $\bar A$ and $\kappa''$ explicitly in §7(d), distinguish that section’s $\kappa_+$ from the block constant, retain logarithmic factors in residual big-$O$ statements, and convert the bibliography to consistent numbered citations. The abstract could also be shortened by omitting the check-count slogan.

[1] S. He, *The Expansions of the Nahm Pole Solutions to the Kapustin–Witten Equations*, arXiv:1808.03886, especially Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 1.4.

[2] S. He, *Rotationally Invariant Singular Solutions to the Kapustin–Witten Equations*, arXiv:1510.07706.

[3] S. He, *A Gluing Theorem for the Kapustin–Witten Equations with a Nahm Pole*, arXiv:1707.06182, especially the nondegeneracy definition and Theorems 7.1 and 8.18.
