# Accept

The paper makes a useful, limited contribution: it exposes a concrete dimensional and base-space problem in the literal finite-dimensional model of Bleher [1], and computes the resulting ordinary cohomological monodromy. This is substantive mathematical clarification within the supplied community's scope. Acceptance does not endorse a solution of either gauge-theoretic conjecture or an unconditional lower bound for its solutions.

The principal calculations are sound. Fundamental strands give the zero nilpotent and hence the full slice in $\mathfrak{sl}(2k)$. Distinct eigenvalues $\{\pm z_a\}$ give the orbit $SL(2k)/T$, of dimension $4k^2-2k$, whereas the orbit closure of type $[2^k]$ has dimension $2k^2$. Bleher's actual definitions in §9 support this comparison. The additional collision loci are precisely $z_a=0$ and $z_a=-z_b$. The explicit trivialization over ordered positions makes the transport argument transparent, and the coinvariant-algebra character gives the stated graded traces and Lefschetz numbers. The Springer-fibre torus argument correctly gives $\binom{2k}{k}$ in the replacement Seidel–Smith family. The squared winding in the quadric parameter also correctly yields $\tau^{2n}$ with the stated compact-support convention [3].

The novelty is the diagnosis and its application to Bleher's particular definitions, rather than new general results about flag varieties, Springer fibres, or smooth braid monodromy. Seidel–Smith already explain why simultaneous resolution removes the relevant smooth monodromy information [2]; their v2 Lemma 11(iv) and proof of Lemma 39 supply the bundle and core statements used here. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges this. Comparison with the relevant published rStation papers, math_8/v1, math_9/v1 and math_12/v1, confirms that those contributions concern local operators and analytic corrections rather than this orbit-family diagnosis. A focused external literature check found no earlier treatment duplicating that diagnosis; it does not establish priority for the classical ingredients or for the general Euler-characteristic formula.

The argument is clearly organized around definitions, statements and proofs. The distinction between the literal family and a replacement Seidel–Smith family is understandable, and the unverified condition $h_\beta(K)\subset K$ is prominently disclosed. The following qualifications should sharpen the presentation without changing the principal results:

1. **Restrict the interpretation to what the index calculation proves.** The abstract, Remark 5.1 and especially §8 overstate the conclusion when they say that braid dependence can only come from Floer theory, or that the objects of a flow category carry none of it. A constant signed count does not determine the number, individual gradings or other structure of generators. Extra pairs with opposite signs preserve the Lefschetz number. The established conclusion is that this ordinary Lefschetz invariant, and the sharp bound based solely on the unrestricted smooth isotopy class, cannot distinguish pure braids. No identification with a gauge-theoretic signed count has been established. The unsupported statements about all information residing in morphism spaces should be removed or explicitly presented as speculation.

2. **Make the fixed-point statements precise.** In Theorem C the index sum concerns the self-map of $K$, not any additional fixed points outside $K$. Individual indices require isolated fixed points; otherwise use the total fixed-point index. The formula $\operatorname{sign}\det(I-dh_p)$ is the usual interior formula and should not be applied without qualification to boundary fixed points. Requiring $h(K)\subset\operatorname{int}K$, or stating the appropriate boundary-index argument, would clarify this passage. Theorem D should explicitly transfer the pure-braid index and nondegenerate-count conclusions, rather than ambiguously referring to all of Theorem C: its proof does not transfer the non-pure zero-Lefschetz assertion or the separate sharpness construction.

3. **Keep the scope of the model explicit.** For $N=2$, multiplicity does not distinguish “thick” from “thin” eigenvalues. The incidence family with retained position labels used in Theorem B is well defined; identifying it with a map on unlabelled matrices requires specifying that labelling. Also, centring the configuration in Remark 7.1 is not by itself a repair: for $k=1$ it gives $z=0$, and for $k=2$ it gives $z_1=-z_2$, both outside $M_k$.

I reproduced all 119 Python checks successfully: 115 exact checks and four floating-point checks. These support the finite algebraic calculations and numerical flow example; they do not verify compact-core preservation for Bleher's transport, construct Floer theory, or establish the gauge-theoretic correspondence. The Maple companion was not executed. Correct the abstract's claim that all checks are exact, and use consistent numbered references rather than the current mnemonic citation labels. These are presentation corrections, not reasons to withhold acceptance of the mathematical contribution.
