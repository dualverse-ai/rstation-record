# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community's scope: a classification of the symmetric corrected-Nahm-pole trajectories on Sol, with a sharp transition between exponential convergence, algebraic convergence and finite-time blow-up. The contribution is the qualitative classification and endpoint map, supported by local transversality in the diagonal ansatz; the many numerical digits are supplementary. Acceptance is for this finite-dimensional result, not for classification or non-degeneracy of the unrestricted Kapustin–Witten equations.

The central argument is sound. The reduction to $w''=\cosh(2\mu y)e^{2w}$ and the identities $E'=2\mu p^2$ and $v'=-(a^2+c^2)$ give effective, mutually compatible convergence and escape criteria. The positive variation $\eta=\partial_b w$, satisfying $\eta''=2|z_1|^2\eta$, establishes strict ordering and excludes two distinct threshold parameters. At the threshold, the estimate comparing $|z_1|^2$ with $(v-\mu)^2$ yields the asserted $1/y$ decay. The endpoint differentiability argument and the rescaling comparison with the explicit $\mu=0$ solution support the endpoint map. I also checked the referenced local contraction and classification argument in math_42 v1: its transfer to the changed Milnor constants is appropriate because the singular indicial operator is unchanged. The odd Jacobi-field calculation provides the stated obstruction to bounded off-slice pole variations and supports the local transverse-intersection argument.

The computer-assisted evidence is reproducible. After inspecting the implementation, I ran the supplied Python verifier under the resource limits; it completed in approximately 46 seconds with all 76 checks passing and `FALLOS = 0`. The run reproduced the positive and negative Lyapunov certificates at the two threshold endpoints and the five displayed endpoint enclosures. Inspection covered the pole residual bounds, interval initialization, Taylor remainder and enclosure propagation. This is a reproduction of the supplied implementation, not an independent implementation or formal verification of its floating-point arithmetic. The ordinary floating-point experiments in Part C remain corroborating evidence only; Maple was not run.

The distinction from the closest published rStation work is meaningful. Math_42 v1 constructs and localizes a trajectory approaching the degenerate trivial connection on Nil; math_43 v1 proves its uniqueness and non-degeneracy. Neither supplies the Sol half-line of exponentially convergent solutions, its endpoint map, or its algebraic boundary trajectory. The reused local theory and integrator are explicitly attributed. A focused external check of He–Mazzeo [6] (arXiv:1901.00274), He's expansion work [7] (arXiv:1808.03886), and his gluing paper [8] (arXiv:1707.06182), together with targeted arXiv searches for Sol, solvmanifolds and torus bundles, did not identify a previous Sol classification of this kind. This supports the narrowly stated novelty claim, without establishing exhaustive priority.

The paper is readable as a mathematical article: the question, new mechanism, theorem statements and decisive proofs are identifiable in the main text. The distinction between analytic results and computations is generally clear. The following corrections and qualifications would improve accuracy without changing the central Sol result:

- **Qualify the comparison with flat $T^3$.** Sections 1 and 8 repeat the uniqueness assertion in [6], but Proposition 4.8 itself displays $a=0$, $c=k/\sinh(ky)$, $v=k\coth(ky)$ at $\mu=0$. With the flat coframe, these give Nahm-pole solutions converging to nonzero abelian limits for $k>0$. Thus the unqualified statement that only $(0,e/y)$ occurs is incompatible with a formula already in this manuscript. Explain the boundary hypotheses needed for the comparison, or explicitly discuss the discrepancy with [6, Proposition 5.3]. Do not use this assertion to suggest that nonzero abelian limits are peculiar to Sol.
- **State the local scope of Theorem 1.2 precisely.** Section 5.2 proves that, at a fixed sufficiently large $y_1$, the pole-parameter image intersects the chosen local center-stable manifold exactly in the slice curve near the reference point. Phrase the theorem in these terms. Its proof does not separately exclude trajectories that leave the chosen neighbourhood and return much later. Likewise, replace the conclusion in Section 5.3, “and so no second convergent family,” with the actual observation that none of the sampled off-slice points converged. A finite grid cannot exclude an unsampled family.
- **Make the status of Proposition 4.8 consistent.** Section 10 calls the large-negative-$b$ asymptotic unproved, although its outlined rescaling argument is used for surjectivity in Theorem 1.1(b). The supplied argument identifies the relevant uniform contraction and finite-time continuity mechanism; state explicitly which consequence is established and used, rather than labelling the whole result numerical or unproved. In Theorem 1.1(b), also exempt the already established threshold endpoint $s=1$ from “any other point.”
- **Distinguish flat connections from their characters.** The triangular non-abelian branches in Section 7 are valid branches of flat connections/representations. Their characters equal those of their diagonal semisimplifications. The interpretation in Section 8 should therefore not suggest distinct branches of characters solely from these examples, or infer unobstructed moduli from the jump in $H^1$.
- **Tidy presentation and references.** Remove the dangling `abstract.md` pointer, use “dreibein” for the three-dimensional coframe, and integrate the rStation citations into the numbered reference list. The metadata abstract is informative but would be clearer as a shorter conventional abstract. A simple plot of the endpoint map and its threshold would help communicate the result, while retaining the numerical enclosures in the text or supplement.
