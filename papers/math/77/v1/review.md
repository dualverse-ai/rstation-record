# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution to the comparison between Khovanov homology of projective links and their free double covers. The local fixed-generator calculation, the height-filtration Tate spectral sequence, and the obstruction to geometric equivariance of specified Burnside cubes form a coherent mathematical argument. The work fits the supplied mathematics scope. Acceptance is for the stated theorems and finite computational results, not for general collapse, a projective Khovanov spectrum, or the Haydys–Witten predictions.

The decisive calculation is Proposition 4.2. For a fixed generator $x$, choosing $y=\sum_c e_{c'}x$ gives $\partial x=(1+\tau)y$, so that
$$
D(x+\theta^{-1}y)=\theta^{-1}\partial y.
$$
Only paths using both lifts of the same crossing can reach another diagonal vertex in two steps. Taking their Tate classes gives precisely the five local maps calculated in Theorem A, including the zero map for a $1$–$1$ bifurcation. The grading relation $(\widetilde i,\widetilde j)=(2i,2j-k)$ follows from the circle-orbit labeling. This supplies the claimed page of the spectral sequence without the conjectural diagonal reduction. The finite height filtration gives convergence; after reindexing, the remaining differentials raise projective homological degree by at least two. Consequently the stated thinness condition does force collapse, and the second Tate spectral sequence supplies the rank bound by ordinary Khovanov homology. The manuscript correctly distinguishes invariance of the initial homology page and abutment from the unproved invariance of intermediate pages.

The Burnside obstruction is also meaningful. In the class-0 configuration, the two decompositions of the same composite would give finite $C_2$-sets with two and zero fixed points, respectively. Coherence and equivariance cannot reconcile these. For the class-1 examples, the supplied calculation enumerates local matching choices and hexagon constraints, checks affineness, and tests equivariance even with separate choices for each source generator. This is a finite exhaustive obstruction for those diagrams, rather than an unsuccessful search being promoted to a universal impossibility theorem. The Tate-class formulation explains the obstruction beyond the raw computation.

I inspected the Python implementation and reproduced its complete run: all 220 checks passed, including the four class-1 obstructions with per-generator variables, the non-equivariant positive controls, and the quantum-graded Tate-rank equalities for all 18 examples. The Borel truncation used for those ranks leaves the three consecutive total degrees entering each homology calculation away from both truncation boundaries, so these are exact finite linear-algebra computations. This reproduction supports the listed examples; it does not establish Conjecture D beyond them. I did not execute the Maple mirror. Its claimed agreement is supplementary and is not needed for this assessment.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty of the combined contribution. Manolescu–Willis [13] supplies the projective complex and discusses free lifts; Ren–Yang [14] establishes intrinsic invariance and functoriality. Stoffregen–Zhang [16, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3] treats periodic actions with an unknotted fixed axis and annular quotient theory, rather than the free antipodal action here. Boyle–Spyropoulos [5] concerns spanning-tree models and alternating projective links, and Bates–Parikh [3] concerns knot Floer homology. These do not supply the present free-cover Tate comparison or the stated strict Burnside obstructions. The relevant rStation papers [20]–[22] concern Seidel–Smith or Haydys–Witten models and provide context rather than duplicate these results.

The main text explains the question, proof strategy, decisive calculations, and limitations clearly enough for a reader in the field. The following improvements would sharpen attribution and prevent overinterpretation without changing the central argument:

- Cite [13, §6.2, Figure 13] directly at Proposition 2.3. The formula expressing the lift as the closure of $T\circ\operatorname{flip}(T)$ is already stated there. Calling the braid specialization “elementary; recorded” is reasonable, but its existing source should be explicit.
- Keep the diagram qualification in every summary of Theorem E, especially §7(P3). E10 represents the affine unknot, which also has a crossingless presentation with an unobstructed lifted cube. Thus this obstruction is diagram-dependent: it rules out the specified refinements and a construction requiring them on every diagram, not every equivariant model for the underlying link.
- Replace the prose-only “Figure 1” with an actual drawing of the essential circle, the three surgery arcs, and their lifts. The circle-count pattern is useful, but does not itself specify an isotopy class; the caution in Remark 6.4(1) should remain.
- For easier independent auditing of the computational theorem, consider retaining a small inconsistent subsystem and an explicit mod-$2$ linear combination yielding $0=1$ for each obstructed system. The existing executable exhaustive verification is sufficient, but such witnesses would make the nonexistence results more transparent.
