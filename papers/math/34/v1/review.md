# Accept

This is a substantive local analytic extension of the existing Anderson–Henningson fold analysis. The principal contribution is Proposition 3.2: smallness in $L^\infty_sL^4_x$, within the specified weak solution class, gives uniform control in the graph norm needed for the previously constructed centre manifold. This supplies a useful PDE interpretation of the reduced invariant set and its compactified trajectory spaces. The work is relevant to the mathematics community and sufficiently distinct from the preceding papers to merit publication.

The decisive estimate has a sound mechanism. Self-adjointness supplies the cylinder energy identity; four-dimensional slice Sobolev embedding makes the quadratic term absorbable under the critical-norm hypothesis. Applying the same estimate to time difference quotients gives the additional derivative needed for $H^1_sZ$ control and hence uniform-in-time $Z$ control. This avoids the inadequate five-dimensional $H^1$ multiplication estimate. The domain identification in Lemma 2.1 follows from the imported graph-norm estimate, the vanishing trace and the reverse Hardy estimate. Once reduction is available, isolation follows from upper semicontinuity of the reduced invariant set at the fold.

The compactness argument appropriately separates dynamical assumptions from analytic regularity. A strict Lyapunov function and finitely many hyperbolic equilibria give convergence to endpoints and control of energy on long necks. The continuous density $\|(I+D\Psi)f\|_X^2$ then transfers energy additivity to the PDE. The rotational example in Remark 3.5 correctly explains why arbitrary allowed perturbations cannot satisfy the same endpoint and energy conclusions. Constant trajectories on the unperturbed equilibrium sphere are handled separately in Theorem C. The interior bootstrap uses the additional time regularity from reduction, rather than assuming that critical five-dimensional regularity automatically improves.

The novelty is concentrated in this analytic upgrade. Comparing the supplied versions, [31] already gives the centre manifold, reduced dynamics and isolation in a graph-norm neighbourhood; [32, 33] already supply the perturbed categories and framed model. Likewise, [34, 35] precede the Clifford and half-space domain calculations. These dependencies are now credited explicitly. The focused external comparison with Anderson–Henningson [2], Taubes [8] and Bleher [26] does not reveal the same critical-slice isolation and local trajectory compactness result. Their respective subjects are the symmetric formal tunneling expansion, sequences of four-dimensional KW solutions, and decoupling/boundary expansions for HW. The use of established spatial-dynamics and finite-dimensional breaking arguments [12–14, 18–20] is appropriate; the contribution is their justified application to this singular PDE neighbourhood, not a new general compactness principle.

The scope qualifications are essential. Acceptance concerns the gauge-fixed equation in $W_{\rm loc}$, with the stated perturbation restrictions for trajectory compactness. It does not establish a Coulomb slice in the critical norm, global compactness from energy bounds, or boundary polyhomogeneity. Theorem E makes the missing gauge input explicit. The Conley index is defined through the admissible reduced flows, not through an asserted ambient PDE semiflow. These restrictions still leave a meaningful result.

The main argument is understandable independently of the computational records. I independently checked the supplied $24\times24$ matrices in exact rational arithmetic: the Clifford identities, symmetry and anticommutation identities, and indicial eigenspace multiplicities $4,8,8,4$ at $-2,-1,1,2$ all agree. This checks the matrix data, not their derivation from the nonlinear equations or the analytic estimates. I inspected the supplementary programs and retained output but did not reproduce the complete 56-check run or the Maple run. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes those checks from proofs of the estimates.

The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the central contribution:

1. Lemma 5.1(2), unlike the corrected Theorem C, still quantifies over every sequence while describing only nonconstant broken pieces. State its nonconstant-sequence hypothesis, or explicitly include a singleton constant limit; an empty union of pieces does not represent that limit.
2. In §1.2(v), specify that uniform parameter-derivative bounds are measured in the normalized parameter $\widetilde\varepsilon=K\varepsilon$. An ordinary joint $C^k$ norm in the original $\varepsilon$ coordinate need not be independent of $K$. The bounds on $z$-derivatives and the displayed $CK|\varepsilon|$ comparison should be distinguished from this notation.
3. In the proof of Theorem B, track the affine displacement $\Psi_\lambda(0)$ explicitly when asserting $\|\xi_M\|_Z,\|\eta\|_Z\le Cr$: the preceding parameter estimate immediately gives $C(r+|\lambda|)$. Add the short forcing estimate needed for the sharper prefactor, or retain the parameter term in that quantitative formulation.
4. Align the abstract and §9 with Theorem A(4): (L) holds in the listed hyperbolic settings, while (U−) and (U0) are handled directly because all complete local trajectories are constant. In the varying-parameter remark, state explicitly the full parameter convergence needed to invoke Theorem A(3).
