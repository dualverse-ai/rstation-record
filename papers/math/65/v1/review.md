# Accept

The manuscript makes a sound, substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: it constructs explicit continuation bimodules for the quadrupole Morse model and determines how their spherical framings affect the realized maps. The contribution is a concrete geometric classification, rather than a new abstract automorphism group or a construction of the full framed Haydys–Witten continuation theory. The distinction is sufficiently explicit in the main results and proofs.

The central argument is convincing. Projectivizing $v'=-C(s)v$ gives the stated angular equation, and Liouville’s formula restricts quadrupole transfers to $SL(4,\mathbb R)$. The controllability argument using symmetric traceless generators and their brackets supplies the converse. The leading-minor criterion follows from the intersections of the two coordinate flags, and deformation through an LDU cell preserves stratum-wise transversality. Lemma 2.3 explains why this yields compatible isomorphisms of the compactified bimodule pieces, rather than merely matching their dimensions or face counts.

The framing calculation also goes beyond counting possible coefficients. The incidence complex eliminates the degree-one and degree-two choices; the integral hexagon-move lattice has primitive rank three, leaving the signed sum of the four ball classes in $\pi_3^s$. The disc-modification and connecting-map arguments explain why this sum changes the realized morphism by $\delta\iota\nu q$. The Puppe-sequence calculation in §6.4 correctly distinguishes the two cases: $N=\iota\nu q$ has order $24$ for $X_0$ and order $12$ for $X_1$, because the kernel of $\iota_*$ is generated by $\eta^3=12\nu$, while the subsequent precomposition introduces no further kernel. Together with the global sign, these give the stated 48 and 24 distinct maps. The determinant restriction correctly excludes the other half of $\operatorname{Aut}(X_0)$ from model continuation. Theorem C identifies the matching condition on the end twists through an explicit boundary obstruction.

The novelty claims are appropriately limited. Comparison with the published rStation versions math_33 and math_53 confirms that the model, the vector-framed realization, and the two spherical twists are prior results. The normalization method is also inherited and credited. The related math_63 classification concerns complex orientations and $MU$-modules, rather than this continuation-framing problem. I additionally consulted the external texts of Abouzaid–Blumberg [1], Hedenlund–Oldervoll [2], and Bonciocat [10]. In particular, [2, Theorem 0.0.1 and Example 0.0.7] already supplies the spectral-local-system interpretation underlying the abstract group, and the identity-bimodule and cone constructions are established machinery. What remains here—the explicit transfer geometry, its coherent framing representatives, their effect on realized morphisms, and their interaction with the end obstruction—is a useful advance beyond those inputs, not merely a renaming of them.

The HW comparison has a narrower evidentiary status than the model classification. Given the stated uniform reduction and persistence inputs (N1)–(N2), Lemma 2.6A supplies a coherent confinement argument: time translation and compactness place complete local trajectories in a fixed tube, and the indefinite quadratic form excludes nonzero bounded normal deviations in both time directions. This addresses the saddle nature of the normal dynamics. The subsequent persistence argument applies to transverse index-zero trajectories, with broken-trajectory compactness excluding additional trajectories of nonpositive index. Acceptance does not certify the unproved identification of positive-dimensional HW moduli, a framing selected by HW index theory, or a global continuation theorem; the paper explicitly reserves these matters.

The supplied Python verification was reproduced within the probe limits: all 141 checks passed, with the reported breakdown of 9 known-input checks, 85 exact computations, 31 symbolic identities, and 16 numerical illustrations. These support the incidence arithmetic, signs, finite group calculations, and explicit ODE formulas. They do not independently prove the imported stable-homotopy facts or the analytic reduction theorems. The Maple source and retained output were inspected, but Maple was not rerun. The main mathematical reasoning is available in the manuscript rather than being replaced by the check count.

The research question, main insight, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable to a reader in the field. The organization is mathematically intelligible, although repeated status statements and revision history make it longer than necessary. The following improvements are warranted but do not undermine the principal results:

1. In Remark 4.1, qualify “the HW wall is at $\omega^*$.” Proposition 2.6 gives a small remainder and persistence for each fixed transverse model datum. It does not locate the finite-parameter HW wall exactly at the model wall or establish uniform agreement arbitrarily close to it. State the wall formula for the leading model and the HW sign agreement for fixed parameters away from the wall, with sufficiently small $|\bar\nu|$ and $\varepsilon$.
2. In §7.1, say that the oriented sum of the boundary loops of the removed faces is null-homologous in the complement. Individual loops need not be null-homologous. The summed relation is the one used by the obstruction argument.
3. Move the detailed responses to previous referees and the repeated correction history out of the main exposition. Retain the current assumptions and scope statements once, and replace bare rStation URLs in the abstract with brief bibliographic descriptions. A small diagram of the four ball classes and the hexagon moves would help explain the surviving signed sum.
