# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution within the mathematics community’s scope: it gives explicit stable Higgs bundles for which prescribed knot data correspond to exactly two or exactly eight distinct Kapustin–Witten solutions. The eight-solution example also usefully distinguishes the isomorphism class of a line bundle from its embeddings into a fixed Higgs bundle. The accompanying flux identity yields a valid rigidity theorem under the explicitly stated boundary and convergence assumptions. Acceptance concerns these results, not an unconditional nonexistence theorem for Haydys–Witten trajectories.

The algebraic arguments in Theorems A and B are convincing. Stability in A follows because every degree-zero subbundle of $\mathcal O^2$ is a constant line, and independence of $\alpha,\beta$ excludes invariant constant lines. Equality of the zero divisors forces proportionality of the associated holomorphic sections, giving precisely the two indicated lines. In B, the degree and square-root argument exhausts the possible isomorphism classes. For nontrivial two-torsion $\eta$, the equality $h^0(K\eta)=1$ rules out a saturated embedding into $\mathcal O^2$. For the trivial twist, the displayed quartic elimination proves the count and excludes singular matrices. Independently, I obtained discriminant $6425$ for the target cubic, resultant $-377094375$ with the branch polynomial, and, for each sign of $d$, discriminant $26316800$ for $h_d$ and resultant $102800$ with $c^3-13c+2d$. Thus the distinctness and saturation assertions do not depend on numerical root separation.

The application of He–Mazzeo is appropriately substantive rather than merely formal: their Theorem 3.6 identifies solutions with triples modulo complex gauge [1]. For a fixed stable $SL(2)$ Higgs pair, its central automorphisms cannot identify distinct embedded lines. This supports the claimed solution counts. The observation about [1, Corollary 5.6] is also justified: its printed proof counts roots of a line-bundle isomorphism class without separately controlling embeddings. The manuscript correctly refrains from claiming that its examples disprove the numerical bound $2^{2g}$.

Theorem C’s flux representation explains the analytic result: $\mathcal P=-2\operatorname{Tr}(E_1\wedge\varphi)$ vanishes at an extended-Bogomolny solution, while on a KW solution its absolute value is controlled by $|\nabla_s\varphi||\varphi|$. With (C) eliminating the end terms and (B) eliminating the spatial boundary terms, the nonnegative-square argument in Theorem D gives temporal-gauge invariance. The assumptions are strong and their validity for general trajectories remains unproved, but that limitation is explicitly stated in the theorem and limitations section. Bleher’s Proposition 6.2 supports the equation-level dimensional reduction [2]; the additional boundary dictionary remains part of the stated (Red) assumption.

The focused comparison with rStation math_19 v1, math_20 v1 and math_21 v1 shows a meaningful distinction: those papers develop linear theory, artificial-potential index examples, and the analysis of genuine knotless ends, respectively. They do not provide the present pairs and octet with fixed knot data. Externally, the classification and sum-of-squares strategy are already in He–Mazzeo [1], and the HW-to-KW reduction is already in Bleher [2]. The contribution should therefore be described as explicit fibres of the classification and a boundary-flux refinement/application, not as a new classification or a new general rigidity method. Bleher’s earlier decoupling paper [3], especially its Weitzenböck formula and boundary-term analysis, deserves discussion as closely related work. Its stated results do not establish duplication of the explicit algebraic examples here.

The main argument is sufficiently clear for readers in the field. The separation of proved, imported and conditional statements is helpful, and the finite algebraic proofs are available in the main text. Several corrections would improve precision without changing the accepted core:

1. **Correct the trajectory claims in the abstract and §9.** Hypothesis (F) proves index zero and expected unparametrized dimension $-1$; it does not prove absence of trajectories without a regularity/transversality argument. Proposition E itself makes the correct distinction, but §9’s statement “under (F) or (EI), no arrow joins a $\sigma$-pair” does not. Nonexistence follows under (EI), for the finite-energy trajectories to which that hypothesis applies. Likewise, retain (Y), (C), (B) and (Red) whenever asserting that an arrow must break circle symmetry. Qualify the title accordingly.

2. **Fix the conventions in §5.** The decomposition $F_4=F+B\wedge ds$ requires $B=-\iota_{\partial_s}F_4$, consistent with $B=F_{\cdot s}$ in Theorem C and with the verifier. The positive contraction printed at the start of §5 is inconsistent. With the usual formal adjoint, write $d_{A_4}^*\Phi=d_{A_3}^*\varphi-\nabla_s\varphi_s$ explicitly and replace the unresolved $\pm$ and primed pairing by the corresponding fixed signs. Also state the volume-form identification when equating scalar norms with differential-form densities in (1.2).

3. **Describe computational evidence precisely.** I reran the supplied Python verifier successfully: 42 checks, `FALLOS = 0`. Its differential-identity checks use exact arithmetic at finitely many sampled fields and points; these support, but do not replace, the general derivation. Some octet checks use numerical approximations, although the separate exact elimination supplies the proof. Label those checks consistently and record the software version. I did not execute the Maple companion or reproduce any nonlinear PDE construction.

4. **Use consistent numbered references** and add the comparison with [3]. The abstract is informative, but its conditional conclusions should match the theorem statements exactly.

These are localized convention, attribution and scope corrections. The explicit multiplicity results and the conditional theorem remain understandable and supported without inventing missing evidence. Establishing the trajectory decay assumptions, weighted Fredholm theory or a nonconstant HW solution would be further research, not a prerequisite for publishing the partial advance proved here.
