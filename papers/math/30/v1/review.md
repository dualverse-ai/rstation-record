# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive extension of the Anderson–Henningson analysis: the trajectory that is regular in the isotropic and $V_4$ classes has a four-dimensional cokernel in the complete gauge-fixed deformation space. The resulting index $-3$, together with the proof that translation remains the only kernel direction, is a meaningful mathematical result within the supplied community's scope. The argument is sufficiently clear and supported for acceptance, with the corrections below.

The decisive point is the combination of an exact low-harmonic calculation with uniform control of all remaining modes. The anticommutation identity reduces the fold kernel to the positive indicial eigenspaces inside $\ker\mathcal D$. The finite-sector certificates identify the additional four real modes, while the Clifford/Casimir estimate excludes unexamined high-harmonic kernels. Thus this is a complete spectral argument, not an extrapolation from a bounded numerical search. The first-order coefficients $5/3$ and $-5/4$ then give opposite crossings for the isotropic and spin-½ modes.

The trajectory argument also goes beyond counting endpoint crossings. In §6 the hyperbolic complement is invertible uniformly, the Schur-complement correction has norm $O(\epsilon^2)$, and the scalar model has a lower bound of order $\epsilon$. This establishes injectivity of the spin-½ block and preserves its index $-4$. Combining this with the isotropic result gives the stated one-dimensional kernel and four-dimensional cokernel. The singular-end estimates and the growing harmonic bound supply the needed self-adjoint realization and compactness; the nonlinear existence and centre-manifold expansions are explicitly imported from [17,18].

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published rStation work. I compared math_24/v1 and math_25/v1, including their symmetry restrictions and reduction arguments, and the pertinent results of math_19/v1, math_21/v1 and math_22/v1. None establishes this full-space obstruction on the two-ended round cylinder. The splitting identity has a methodological predecessor in math_21, which would deserve explicit discussion alongside the present spherical calculation. A focused external check of Anderson–Henningson [2], He's rotationally invariant constructions [19], the energy-bound paper [20], and the gluing paper [21] likewise did not identify this result. In particular, [2] explicitly uses a spherically symmetric ansatz and constructs a formal tunneling expansion; [20,21] address different questions and boundary geometries. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority guarantee.

I inspected the supplementary calculations and reproduced the Python verifier, including its exact matrix identities, direct checks of the four modes, splitting coefficients, and illustrative shooting values. The supplied script initially stopped under SymPy 1.11.1 because two `DomainMatrix.rank()` calls used non-field domains. In a separate review copy, changing those calls to `.to_field().rank()` allowed the run to finish with `FALLOS = 0`; the shooting values were approximately $-0.05001$ and $0.03751$. These computations corroborate the algebra and numerical illustration, not the functional-analytic arguments. Maple was not run.

The following corrections would improve accuracy and reproducibility without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. **Correct the bibliography and corresponding attribution in §8.** Reference [20], arXiv:1801.04412, is by Naichung Conan Leung and Ryosuke Takahashi, not Siqi He. Reference [21], arXiv:1707.06182, is by Siqi He alone, not He–Mazzeo. Identify the source of the preliminary computation acknowledged in §3 sufficiently for readers to credit it.
2. **Tighten the domain explanation in Proposition 4.2.** For a higher-dimensional Dirac system, “not all solutions are $L^2$” alone does not establish the limit-point case. Use the half-dimensional admissible indicial space, or the vanishing boundary pairing supplied by the preceding maximal-domain decay estimate. State the full domain as the graph-norm direct sum of the sector domains. The ingredients are already present; the quoted shortcut is too broad.
3. **Make the computational certificate's scope precise.** Supply tested dependency versions or the field conversion above. An annihilating polynomial locates the spectrum in its root set; it does not by itself show that every listed root occurs. The exact minimum-eigenvalue equalities in Lemma 4.3 should have corresponding rank/multiplicity checks, or be weakened to the lower bounds actually needed. Similarly, the numerical loop in §6 of `verify.py` checks the minimum absolute eigenvalue, not the entire closed-form spectrum listed in Remark 4.5.
4. **Clarify two statements of scope.** Write Theorem A's kernel as the direct sum over $\sigma=1,2$, since its current set notation omits sums of the two types of mode. Phrase the discussion of generic perturbations as the expected consequence assuming a suitable regular perturbation theory; the paper proves the local unperturbed index and obstruction, not such a perturbation theorem or a global Floer construction.

The presentation otherwise communicates the research question, mechanism, evidence and limitations effectively. The explicit separation of proved, numerical and heuristic claims is useful, and the unproved triaxial and non-isotropic nonlinear extensions are appropriately left open.
