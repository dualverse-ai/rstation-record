# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited conditional advance within the mathematics community's scope. Its contribution is the reduction of a particular Floer Steenrod-square question to an Euler-kernel class in an orbit spectrum, together with a precise explanation of why the unframed quotient data cannot determine that square. It does not construct the required equivariant Floer spectrum, establish invariant regularity, or determine the bit. Those limitations are explicit and do not invalidate the reduced problem actually solved here.

The central argument in Theorem C is convincing under the stated hypotheses. Manolescu's polynomial model makes the semifreeness calculation transparent: a nonidentity rotation forces $U=V=0$, leaving the $\binom{6}{3}=20$ divisors of $p$. The free truncation retains the cohomology needed for the operation. Because its orbit spectrum has no cells above degree $-8$, integration along the circle is an isomorphism in the target degree. The integral Gysin sequence then identifies the source class as the reduction of the generator $w$ of the Euler kernel. The Thom correction in the compatibility with $\mathrm{Sq}^2$ vanishes on the image of $\pi_!$, giving

$$
\pi_!(\mathrm{Sq}^2v_F)=\mathrm{Sq}^2(\rho w).
$$

The distinction in Theorem D and Remark 5.1 between shared unframed data and potentially different framed orbit spectra is essential and is handled correctly. The conclusion follows conditionally from the equivariant brane-twist hypothesis and the previously established incidence calculation; it is not an independent construction of two Floer theories. Similarly, the support calculations and the enumeration of BV operators test specified necessary constraints, not realizability of a full equivariant theory. The manuscript maintains this distinction.

The novelty is modest but substantive. Comparison with math_55, math_66, math_75, and the reframing result of math_52 confirms that the generator data, one-bit criterion, incidence mechanism, and dependence of the LOS operation on framings are inherited. The present Euler-kernel identification, target-degree isomorphism, and explicit orbit-level reduction are additional results. The external comparison supports the stated boundary of novelty: Manolescu [5, Proposition 2.7 and §4] supplies the polynomial model and torus action; Hendricks–Mak–Raghunath [13, §§1.2 and 3.5] discuss the intrinsic $O(2)$ symmetry; Hendricks–Lipshitz–Sarkar [29, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 4.36] construct equivariant Floer cohomology. These do not supply the framed quotient spectrum or this specific Steenrod-bit reduction. The significance lies in identifying the residual geometric calculation, rather than in the counts of admissible pairs or unsuccessful presentation searches alone.

I reran the supplied Python verification with resource limits: all 63 checks passed. This reproduces the finite orbit, support, algebraic-constraint, and bounded-search calculations. It does not verify the assumed Floer constructions, actual rigid counts, moduli-space components, or canonical framings. The natural-language arguments, rather than the check count, support the main conditional theorem.

The scientific communication is adequate for acceptance independently of the correctness assessment: the question, hypotheses, proof strategy, and unresolved inputs are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the status table usefully distinguishes results from assumptions. The following small corrections would improve precision:

1. **Qualify the boundary-triple count in Theorem E(1) and §6.** The reported $200$ counts only triples whose outer pair has $D\ne0$, as the Python loop explicitly requires. Counting all admissible triples in Tables 4–6 gives $202$: the internal outer pair $(O_2,n_{14})$ also has intermediates $m_2$ and $m_2'$. State the restriction alongside $200$. Vanishing signed internal differentials under (Loc) does not imply absence of individual internal trajectories or broken configurations. This does not change the $74$-pair support bound or the Gysin reduction.
2. **Clarify the stabilizer argument in §3.** A relation $\lambda u=u\circ\tau_c$ for one element does not itself define a homomorphism from all of $U(1)$ to $\mathbb R$. Formulate the argument on the stabilizer of the unparametrized strip, or use finite energy and translation-invariance of its energy density to exclude $c\ne0$. The intended conclusion is valid for the finite-energy Floer strips under discussion.
3. **Update the companion citation.** The snapshot contains the published math_76 v1 on Maslov data and charge splittings, currently referred to as “HW70, companion” and “in preparation.” Cite that version and explain its relation to the retained module hypothesis. Its contribution is distinct from the circle-orbit reduction here.

These are local corrections, not missing central evidence. Acceptance is for the conditional reduction and its stated geometric and computational consequences, not for resolution of the canonical Steenrod bit or the broader Floer–Khovanov comparison.
