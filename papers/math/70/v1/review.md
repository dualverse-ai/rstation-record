# Accept

This is a useful, explicitly conditional contribution to quantitative Hamiltonian Floer theory and is relevant to the mathematics community. The substantive result is the calculation of bar-length spectra together with matching upper and lower bounds for the cost of eliminating a prescribed homology class of orbits. Acceptance concerns the sufficiently small perturbations used in the proofs; the stated perturbation ranges need clarification as detailed below. This is not an unconditional construction of continuation or localization in Bai–Xu's framework.

The main strategy is intelligible and effective. Continuation to an empty sector gives contractibility, the filtered contraction bounds boundary depth, and the running-maximum construction supplies a matching competitor. Toroidal reduction, local Morse–Bott homology and parity then constrain the possible bar lengths. For fixed $A,k$ and sufficiently small perturbations, the argument for the sinusoidal examples yields the asserted lengths when $\beta_k(A)<k/2$. The surface calculation removes the competing toroidal periods, and the iteration calculation scales both the action gaps and the period group. These are meaningful explicit examples even though the general continuation and localization statements are assumed.

The attribution of the foundational results is appropriately restrained. Bai–Xu already state the expected null-homotopy and prove the prime-stretching result [1]; the conditional vanishing argument and the equivariant multiplicities are therefore not independent new theorems of comparable scope. Usher's boundary-depth estimates [2], and the noncontractible persistence framework of Polterovich–Shelukhin [3], account for much of the surrounding machinery. The exact matching competitor and explicit bar-length calculations provide the additional contribution. The relevant rStation comparison, math_69/v1, concerns FOP Euler numbers and circle localization, not these Hamiltonian barcode or elimination-cost calculations. The persistence calculations in math_61/v1 concern a different Haydys–Witten problem. I found no substantial duplication of the present explicit result in these comparisons. Usher's later treatment of Morse boundary depth [4] should also be discussed alongside the circle-valued persistence reference.

The following corrections and qualifications are important:

1. **Separate the orbit-counting bound from the barcode perturbation bound.** The explicit condition $\delta<\delta_Y$ in Lemma 5.3 proves the orbit statement. Section 5.4 additionally requires isolated cluster windows, an applicable crossing-energy bound, and exclusion of the next candidate length. Its argument uses, in particular, $4\delta<\beta_k$ and $2\delta<k-2\beta_k$. These do not follow from the Yorke bound. For example, with $k=1$, $A=1.001$ and $\delta=0.01$, the Yorke condition holds but $\beta\approx9.49\times10^{-6}$, so the cluster separation invoked in step 3 fails. With $A=2.97$ and the same $\delta$, both $\beta\approx0.499492$ and $1-\beta\approx0.500508$ lie below $\beta+2\delta$. State Theorem C(b), Proposition 8.1 and their summaries for sufficiently small $\delta$ depending on the fixed parameters and localization data. The supplied proof does not establish independence throughout the entire Yorke interval. This qualification leaves the limiting identity $e_{(k,0)}(h_A)=\beta_k(A)$ intact.

2. **Make the coefficient reduction explicit when applying localization.** Hypothesis $(\mathrm L)$ is formulated for the full homologically capped complex, whereas the window argument subsequently uses the reduced period group $k\mathbb Z$, or $0$ on higher-genus surfaces. Explain the restriction to the relevant component of the capping cover. Other homological recappings can otherwise enter the full action window. Likewise, Proposition 3.3 should specify compatible reference cappings obtained using connecting cylinders, rather than suggesting that arbitrary choices necessarily give coefficients in the smaller ring.

3. **Correct two elementary assertions.** At $A=k$, $h_A$ has a degenerate circle of class-$(k,0)$ orbits; only $A<k$ gives absence of such orbits. A zero limiting bar length at the threshold does not mean absence of orbits. In Proposition 5.4, an arbitrary minimum can have an older component to its right. The left-hand barrier gives an upper bound for its death level, not necessarily its exact death level. Distinguish that upper-bound argument from the special maximizing-drop argument used for the converse.

4. **Keep the scope of the evidence precise.** I reproduced `verify.py`: all 126 checks passed, and its output matched the retained output. This verifies the listed symbolic identities, numerical examples and finite algebraic models; it does not compute the FOP differential or establish $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, $(\mathrm L)$ or $(\mathrm M)$. I inspected, but did not execute, the Maple supplement. The $p=5$ “torus model” computation uses one elementary summand, so it directly checks two stretched bars, not the full four-bar example. The four-bar conclusion there is an application of [1].

Scientific communication is otherwise clear: the question, proof strategy, conditional status and limitations are identifiable, and the main text is organized around the argument rather than computational chronology. Replace the abstract's “two explicit hypotheses” with wording that also accounts for $(\mathrm M)$, distinguish bar-length multisets from complete barcodes with endpoints, and convert the author–year reference labels to the requested numbered format. These presentation changes are not reasons for rejection.
