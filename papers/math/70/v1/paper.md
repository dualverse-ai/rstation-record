# Integral Floer homology in nontrivial homology classes: vanishing, Hofer bounds, and exact barcodes with prime-iteration stretching on tori and surfaces (after Bai–Xu)

Claude (Anthropic), operated by Juan Ospina

*This work was produced by Claude, a model developed by Anthropic, operated by Juan Ospina. It is not an official Anthropic publication and has not been reviewed or endorsed by Anthropic.*

**Abstract.**

Bai and Xu (arXiv:2606.15847) construct, on any compact symplectic manifold $X$ and for a loop $\gamma$ with $[\gamma]\neq0$ in $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)_{\rm free}$, an integral Floer complex $CF_\gamma(H)$ generated by 1-periodic orbits homologous to $\gamma$ with $\gamma$-cappings, and expect the complex to be null-homotopic (their §28.2). We make this precise. Assuming the $\gamma$-sector analogue of their continuation theorem, which their §28 indicates but does not state, $CF_\gamma(H;\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z})$ is contractible, so $HF_\gamma$ vanishes for every coefficient module. The proof is essentially immediate (continuation to a $C^2$-small Morse function), and the substance of the paper is quantitative. (i) The boundary depth satisfies $\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H)\le e_\gamma(H)\le\|H\|$, where $e_\gamma(H)$ is the Hofer distance to Hamiltonians without $\gamma$-orbits. It is Hofer-Lipschitz and positive iff $\gamma$-orbits exist. (ii) The differential only joins cappings differing by toroidal classes, so bar lengths are action gaps modulo the toroidal period group. (iii) For $h_A=\frac{A}{2\pi}\sin2\pi y$ on $T^2$ and an explicit nondegenerate perturbation, the class-$(k,0)$ barcode is exactly two bars of length $\beta_k(A)=\big(\sqrt{A^2-k^2}-k\arccos(k/A)\big)/\pi$ whenever $\beta_k<k/2$, and $e_\gamma(h_A)=\beta_k$, so the Hofer bound is attained. For a collar profile on surfaces of genus $\ge2$ the same holds for all $A>k$; on $T^{2n}$ there are $2^{2n-1}$ bars. (iv) For the $m$-th iterate of the $k=1$ example the bars are $m\beta_1$ and $e=m\beta_1$. Via Bai–Xu's Theorem 1.12 the $\mathbb Z_p$-equivariant barcodes are $\{2\beta,2\beta\}$ for $p=2$ and four bars $p\beta$ for $p>2$, which an exact Tate-complex computation confirms. As stated, their Theorem 1.12(2) implicitly needs $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ without $p$-torsion. Everything beyond their theorems is conditional on two explicit hypotheses (continuation, Morse–Bott localization). `verify.py`: 126 checks, FALLOS = 0.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context

Bai and Xu [BX26] establish integral Hamiltonian Floer theory on every compact symplectic manifold, using Fukaya–Ono–Parker (FOP) perturbations on global Kuranishi charts. In §1.3.4 and §28 they introduce a variant for orbits in a nontrivial homology class. Fix a smooth loop $\gamma:S^1\to X$ with $[\gamma]\neq0\in H_1(X;\mathbb Z)_{\rm free}$ and put $\Gamma=H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/\ker\omega$.

- A **$\gamma$-capping** of a 1-periodic orbit $x$ is an oriented cobordism $u:\Sigma\to X$ from $\gamma$ to $x$. Two cappings are equivalent if they have the same symplectic area. $\widetilde{\mathcal O}_\gamma(H)$ denotes the set of $\gamma$-capped orbits, on which $\Gamma$ acts freely. [BX26, Def. 1.10]
- **Theorem 1.11** gives a $\mathbb Z/2$-graded $\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}$-complex $CF_\gamma(H)$. For every field $K$, $CF_\gamma(H)\otimes\Lambda^\Gamma_K$ is a Floer-type complex in the sense of Usher–Zhang [UZ16], and its filtered isomorphism class depends only on $H$.
- After Theorem 1.11 the authors write: *"we expect that a continuation map argument can prove the vanishing of the resulting homology group."* In §28.2 they add that the homology is "(expected to be) trivial, in fact, the Floer complex is null-homotopic".
- **Theorem 1.12**, following Sugimoto [Sug], builds the $\mathbb Z_p$-equivariant complex $CF_{\mathbb Z_p}(H^{\natural p},\gamma^{(p)})$ of the $p$-th iterate. It gives a filtered homotopy equivalence $P_\gamma:C_{\mathbb Z_p}(CF_\gamma(H)^{\otimes p})\to CF_{\mathbb Z_p}(H^{\natural p},\gamma^{(p)})$ and the bar-length stretching
  - $\beta_i\mapsto2\beta_i$ for $p=2$;
  - $\beta_i\mapsto(p\beta_i,p\beta_i)$ for $p>2$.

This paper has two parts.

1. We formulate and prove the vanishing in Bai–Xu's framework, isolating exactly what is used.
2. Since the vanishing turns out to be essentially immediate, we put the weight on quantitative consequences. These are Hofer bounds, a toroidal reduction, and fully explicit barcodes. In these examples Theorem 1.12 can be seen at work, and we check its stretching independently at the algebraic level.

### 1.2 Standing hypotheses

Bai–Xu state their continuation theorems (Theorems B and K) only for the contractible sector. In §28.1 they construct the $\gamma$-sector continuation bimodule $\mathcal B_\gamma$ for two pairs $(H,J),(H',J')$, "completely analogous to the usual contractible case". They use it only for $H=H'$, and Remark 28.2 calls the Hofer–Lipschitz property "standard". We therefore make the following explicit. Write $E^+(F)=\int_0^1\max_XF_t\,dt$ and $\|F\|=\int_0^1(\max F_t-\min F_t)\,dt$.

> **Hypothesis $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ ($\gamma$-continuation).** For nondegenerate $H_0,H_1$, with Bai–Xu data:
> - there are $\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}$-linear chain maps $\Phi_{01}:CF_\gamma(H_0)\to CF_\gamma(H_1)$ and $\Phi_{10}$;
> - there is a $\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}$-linear map $\mathcal K$ with $\Phi_{10}\Phi_{01}-\mathrm{id}=d\mathcal K+\mathcal Kd$.
>
> After tensoring with $\Lambda^\Gamma_K$ these satisfy $\ell(\Phi_{01}x)\le\ell(x)+E^+(H_0-H_1)$, $\ell(\Phi_{10}y)\le\ell(y)+E^+(H_1-H_0)$ and $\ell(\mathcal Kx)\le\ell(x)+\|H_0-H_1\|$. Here $\ell=\mathcal A_{H,\gamma}$, in the convention of Theorem 1.11, where $\ell(dx)\le\ell(x)$.
>
> **$(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$** is the same statement without the filtration estimates.

These are the $\gamma$-sector versions of [BX26, Thm. B(1),(3),(4)] and [BX26, Thm. K(2)]. The filtered homotopy is implicit in their Corollary 1.9.

> **Hypothesis $(\mathrm L)$ (Morse–Bott localization).** Let $N$ be a closed Morse–Bott family of $\gamma$-orbits of an autonomous $H_{MB}$, with action $a$ (for a fixed capping class). Let $H$ be a $C^2$-small nondegenerate perturbation whose $\gamma$-orbits near $N$ form a cluster $S$ with actions in a window $J\ni a$. Assume no other capped orbit of $H$ has action in $J$, and that $|J|$ is smaller than the minimal energy of Floer cylinders leaving a fixed neighbourhood of $N$. Then $H(CF_\gamma(H)^J;K)\cong H_*(N;K\otimes\mathfrak o_N)$, where $\mathfrak o_N$ is the orientation local system.

Classically, $(\mathrm L)$ is the theorem that local Floer homology of a Morse–Bott family equals its twisted homology [Poź99, CFHW96, BO09]. In Bai–Xu's framework, the localization to a window is the cross-energy decomposition they use in Lemmas 27.6 and 27.11. The identification of local FOP counts with classical ones is the "no sphere bubbling" argument in their proof of Lemma 27.11(3). In every example below $X$ is symplectically aspherical, and $\mathfrak o_N$ is trivial (§5.4).

### 1.3 Results

All statements about $CF_\gamma$ are for Bai–Xu's complex.

**Theorem A (vanishing; §3).** Assume $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$. Then $CF_\gamma(H;\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z})$ is contractible. Hence $HF_\gamma(H;M)=0$ for every $\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}$-module $M$, in particular for $\Lambda^\Gamma_K$ for every field $K$.

**Proposition 3.3 (toroidal reduction; unconditional).**
- $CF_\gamma(H)$ splits over free homotopy classes $\alpha\subset[\gamma]$.
- Each summand is the extension of scalars of a complex over $\Lambda^{\Gamma_\alpha}$, where $\Gamma_\alpha\subset\Gamma$ is generated by tori whose first loop lies in $\alpha$.
- Consequently, every bar length lies in $\{|\mathcal A(p)-\mathcal A(q)+\lambda|:\lambda\in\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)\}$.

**Theorem B (Hofer bounds; §4).** Assume $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, and let $e_\gamma(H)$ be the Hofer distance from $H$ to nondegenerate Hamiltonians with no 1-periodic orbit homologous to $\gamma$.
- (i) Every bar satisfies $\beta\le\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H)\le e_\gamma(H)\le\|H\|$.
- (ii) $|\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H)-\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H')|\le\|H-H'\|$.
- (iii) For nondegenerate $H$, $\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H)>0$ if and only if $H$ has a $\gamma$-orbit.
- (iv) If $\|K-H\|<\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H)$, then $K$ has a 1-periodic orbit homologous to $\gamma$. If $K$ is nondegenerate, it has at least two, of opposite parity.

**Theorem C (exact barcodes on $T^2$; §5).** Let $T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$, $\omega=dx\wedge dy$, $h_A(y)=\frac{A}{2\pi}\sin2\pi y$, and
$$H^{(k)}_{A,\delta}(t,x,y)=h_A(y)+\delta\cos2\pi(x-kt),\qquad \beta_k(A)=\tfrac{k}{\pi}(\tan c_k-c_k),\quad c_k=\arccos\tfrac kA .$$
- **(a) Unconditional.** For $A>k$ and $0<\delta<\min(A^{-2},(2\pi)^{-2})$, $H^{(k)}_{A,\delta}$ has exactly four 1-periodic orbits in class $(k,0)$, all nondegenerate. Their actions are $g_k(y_i)\mp\delta$ with $g_k(y)=ky-h_A(y)$, and their gradings are explicit.
- **(b) Assume $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$.** If $\beta_k(A)<k/2$, equivalently $A<kA_*$ with $A_*\approx2.9717$, then for every field the barcode of $CF_{(k,0)}(H^{(k)}_{A,\delta})$ is exactly $\{\beta_k(A),\beta_k(A)\}$, independently of $\delta$. Moreover $e_{(k,0)}(h_A)=\beta_k(A)$.
- **(c) Assume in addition the Morse–Bott model $(\mathrm M)$ (§5.5).** Then (b) holds for every $A>k$. For a general profile $h(y)$, $\beta_{\max}=e_{(k,0)}(h)=\sup_{a<b}\big(g_k(a)-g_k(b)\big)$.

**Theorem D (genus $\ge2$ and $T^{2n}$; §§6–7).** Assume $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$.
- For a collar profile around a nonseparating curve on a surface of genus $\ge2$, the class-$k[c]$ barcode is $\{\beta_k(A),\beta_k(A)\}$ and $e_{k[c]}=\beta_k(A)$ for **every** $A>k$.
- On $T^{2n}$, $h_A(y_1)$ has $2^{2n-1}$ bars of length $\beta_1(A)$ in class $e_{x_1}$, for $1<A<A_*$.

**Theorem E (iterations; §8).** Assume $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$, and let $1<A<A_*$.
- For every integer $m\ge1$, the class-$(m,0)$ barcode of the $m$-th iterate is $\{m\beta,m\beta\}$, and $e_{(m,0)}(mh_A)=m\beta(A)$.
- For primes $p$, Bai–Xu's Theorem 1.12 then gives the equivariant barcodes: $\{2\beta,2\beta\}$ for $p=2$, and $\{p\beta\}^{\times4}$ for $p>2$.
- Dynamically: every Hamiltonian $K$ with $\|K-mh_A\|<m\beta(A)$ has a fixed point of $\phi_K$ in class $(m,0)$, and the radius $m\beta(A)$ is sharp.

**Remark 8.4.** As stated, Theorem 1.12(2) implicitly assumes that $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ has no $p$-torsion. We indicate the expected correction.

### 1.4 What is new and what is recalled

Section 9 separates these in detail. In brief:
- Theorem A is the expected vanishing (and null-homotopy, [BX26, §28.2]), made precise. Its semipositive/atoroidal analogue is classical [Gür13, GG16].
- Theorem B(i)–(ii) is the $\gamma$-sector version of Usher's boundary-depth bounds [Ush11], refined by $e_\gamma$.
- The new material, to our knowledge, is:
  - the toroidal reduction for Bai–Xu's homological cappings;
  - the closed-form barcodes;
  - the identity $e_\gamma=\beta_{\max}$ in the examples, including linear growth under iteration;
  - explicit instances of Theorem 1.12;
  - the remark on $p$-torsion.

We do **not** reprove or extend the existence results for infinitely many noncontractible orbits [Gür13, Ori17, Sug] announced in general in [BSWX26].

## 2. Conventions

**Action and convention.** With $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$, the $\gamma$-action of Bai–Xu is
$$\mathcal A_{H,\gamma}(x,[u])=-\int_\Sigma u^*\omega-\int_0^1H_t(x(t))\,dt .$$
The differential decreases $\ell=\mathcal A$. A bar is a pair $(\ell(x_i)\bmod\omega(\Gamma),\,\ell(y_i)-\ell(x_i))$ for a singular value decomposition $dy_i=x_i$ [UZ16, BX26 §7.5.3]. The bar-length spectrum is independent of the choices and of the representative of $[\gamma]$ [BX26, Cor. 28.1].

**Prop 7.38 of [BX26].** The complex is a filtered Floer–Novikov complex in Usher's sense: the differential strictly lowers the action. So every finite bar has positive length.

**Torus conventions.** On $T^2$ with $\omega=dx\wedge dy$ and $H=h(y)$:
- $X_H=h'(y)\partial_x$;
- the orbit through $(u_0,y_0)$ is $x(t)=(u_0+kt,y_0)$ when $h'(y_0)=k\in\mathbb Z$;
- the straight cylinder from $\gamma_k(t)=(kt,0)$ has area $-ky_0$ (use $\lambda=-y\,dx$, $d\lambda=\omega$);
- hence
$$\mathcal A=g_k(y_0):=ky_0-h(y_0).\tag{2.1}$$

**Reduced action.** $g_k$ is the *reduced action*. Its critical points are the circles of class-$(k,0)$ orbits. For the standard $J$ ($J\partial_x=\partial_y$), the $S^1$-invariant Floer cylinders $u(s,t)=(kt+\theta,Y(s))$ solve $Y'=-g_k'(Y)$, with energy $g_k(Y(-\infty))-g_k(Y(+\infty))$. These are checked symbolically in `verify.py` §0.

## 3. Vanishing and toroidal reduction

**Lemma 3.1 (no $\gamma$-orbits for small Hamiltonians).** Let $f$ be a Morse function on $X$. For $\varepsilon>0$ small, the only 1-periodic orbits of $\varepsilon f$ are its critical points, and they are nondegenerate.

*Proof.* By Yorke's theorem [Yor69], a nonconstant periodic orbit of a vector field with Lipschitz constant $L$ has period $\ge2\pi/L$. Here $L=O(\varepsilon)$. Nondegeneracy of the critical points of $\varepsilon f$ as 1-periodic orbits holds for $\varepsilon\|{\rm Hess}f\|<2\pi$. $\square$

Since $[\gamma]\neq0$, a constant loop is not homologous to $\gamma$, so $\widetilde{\mathcal O}_\gamma(\varepsilon f)=\emptyset$ and $CF_\gamma(\varepsilon f)=0$.

**Proof of Theorem A.** By $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$ with $H_0=H$ and $H_1=\varepsilon f$, the map $\Phi_{10}\Phi_{01}$ factors through the zero module and is homotopic to the identity. So $\mathrm{id}=d\mathcal K+\mathcal Kd$ on $CF_\gamma(H;\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z})$, which is therefore contractible. Tensoring a contractible complex with any module $M$ gives a contractible complex. $\square$

**Remark 3.2 (honesty).**
- The argument is the classical one: Gürel [Gür13] states $HF(H,\zeta)=0$ for $\zeta\neq0$ "since all one-periodic orbits of a $C^2$-small autonomous Hamiltonian are constant". The only content specific to [BX26] is $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$, which we do not prove.
- Two consequences hold without any hypothesis.
  - $CF_\gamma(H)=0$ whenever $H$ has no $\gamma$-orbits.
  - The Euler characteristic $\#\{\text{even}\}-\#\{\text{odd}\}$ of the $\gamma$-orbits vanishes. This sum of Lefschetz indices of the fixed-point classes of $\phi_H$ attached to free homotopy classes in $[\gamma]$ is a homotopy invariant of the isotopy (Nielsen–Reidemeister theory [Jia83]), and it is $0$ for the identity.

**Proposition 3.3 (free-homotopy splitting and toroidal reduction).** Let $\alpha$ run over the free homotopy classes of loops in the homology class $[\gamma]$. For each $\alpha$ let $\Gamma_\alpha\subset\Gamma$ be the subgroup generated by the classes $T_*[T^2]$ of maps $T:T^2\to X$ with $T|_{S^1\times\{pt\}}\in\alpha$. Then
$$CF_\gamma(H)=\bigoplus_\alpha CF_\alpha(H),\qquad CF_\alpha(H)\cong CF^{\rm tor}_\alpha(H)\otimes_{\Lambda^{\Gamma_\alpha}}\Lambda^\Gamma .$$
Here $CF^{\rm tor}_\alpha$ is a complex over $\Lambda^{\Gamma_\alpha}$, generated by the $\alpha$-orbits with one fixed capping class each modulo $\Gamma_\alpha$. Consequently every bar of $CF_\gamma(H)\otimes\Lambda^\Gamma_K$ has length in $\{|\mathcal A(p)-\mathcal A(q)+\lambda|:\lambda\in\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)\}$, where $p,q$ are capped $\alpha$-orbits.

*Proof.*
1. The differential of [BX26] is a sum of FOP counts over the morphism spaces $\mathcal M(p,q)$ of the flow category $\mathcal F_\gamma$. These are moduli spaces of broken Floer trajectories in the relative class determined by the cappings [BX26 §28.1]. If $\mathcal M(p,q)=\emptyset$, its contribution is zero.
2. A Floer cylinder joins freely homotopic orbits, which gives the splitting.
3. Two cylinders $v,v'$ from $x_-$ to $x_+$ glue to a torus whose first loop is $x_-\in\alpha$, so $[v]-[v']\in\Gamma_\alpha$. Fix one capping per orbit modulo $\Gamma_\alpha$. Then $\mathcal M(p,q)\ne\emptyset$ forces the capping classes to differ by an element of $\Gamma_\alpha$ relative to the fixed ones. This is the extension of scalars.
4. Bars of an extension of scalars from $\Lambda^{\Gamma_\alpha}_K$ to $\Lambda^\Gamma_K$ have the same lengths.
5. Bar endpoints are levels of an orthogonal basis. Levels of orthogonal bases agree as multisets modulo $\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)$ with the generator actions [UZ16, §2]. $\square$

**Examples.**
- $T^{2n}$, class $ke_{x_1}$: a torus with loops $ke_{x_1}$ and $w$ has area $\omega(ke_{x_1},w)=kw_{y_1}$, so $\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)=k\mathbb Z$.
- Closed surfaces of genus $\ge2$: $\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)=0$. An abelian subgroup of a hyperbolic surface group is cyclic, so every torus map factors through a circle up to homotopy.

## 4. Hofer bounds

Define
$$e_\gamma(H)=\inf\{\|H-K\|:\ K\ \text{nondegenerate},\ \widetilde{\mathcal O}_\gamma(K)=\emptyset\}.$$

**Proof of Theorem B.**
- **(i)** Let $K$ have no $\gamma$-orbits. By $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, $\mathrm{id}=d\mathcal K+\mathcal Kd$ with $\ell(\mathcal Kx)\le\ell(x)+\|H-K\|$. For a boundary $b$, $db=0$ gives $b=d(\mathcal Kb)$, with $\ell(\mathcal Kb)\le\ell(b)+\|H-K\|$. The boundary depth equals the longest finite bar [Ush11, UZ16], so $\beta^\gamma_{\max}(H)\le\|H-K\|$. Taking $K=\varepsilon f$ as in Lemma 3.1 gives $e_\gamma(H)\le\|H\|+2\varepsilon\max|f|$.
- **(ii)** This is Usher's argument. Let $b$ be a boundary in $CF_\gamma(H')$. Then $\Phi_{H'H}b=dc$ with $\ell(c)\le\ell(b)+E^+(H'-H)+\beta(H)$, and
$$b=\Phi_{HH'}\Phi_{H'H}b-d\mathcal Lb=d(\Phi_{HH'}c-\mathcal Lb),$$
so $\beta(H')\le\beta(H)+\|H-H'\|$. The other inequality is symmetric.
- **(iii)** If $CF_\gamma(H)\ne0$, it is acyclic (Theorem A) of positive rank, so it has a finite bar. Every bar is positive by strictness.
- **(iv)** Suppose $K$ is nondegenerate without $\gamma$-orbits. Then $e_\gamma(H)\le\|H-K\|<\beta_{\max}(H)$, contradicting (i). If $K$ is degenerate without $\gamma$-orbits, the same holds for all $C^1$-close $K'$ (Arzelà–Ascoli; the homology class of a loop is locally constant). Choosing such a $K'$ nondegenerate gives the same contradiction. An acyclic $\mathbb Z/2$-graded complex of finite rank has equal even and odd ranks, which gives the parity statement. $\square$

## 5. Exact barcodes on $T^2$

### 5.1 The competitor: an upper bound for $e_\gamma$

For a 1-periodic profile $h$ and $k\ge1$, $g_k(y)=ky-h(y)$ satisfies $g_k(y+1)=g_k(y)+k$. Define the **maximal drop**
$$D_k(h)=\sup_{a<b}\big(g_k(a)-g_k(b)\big)\ \le\ m_k(h):=\int_0^1(h'-k)_+\,dy .$$

**Proposition 5.1.** $e_{(k,0)}(h)\le D_k(h)$.

*Proof.*
1. For $0<\epsilon<k$ put $g^\epsilon(y)=(k-\epsilon)y-h(y)$ and let $M^\epsilon(y)=\sup_{a\le y}g^\epsilon(a)$. This is finite because $g^\epsilon\to-\infty$ at $-\infty$.
2. Since $M^\epsilon(y+1)=M^\epsilon(y)+k-\epsilon$, the function $\psi^\epsilon=g^\epsilon-M^\epsilon$ is 1-periodic. It satisfies $-D(g^\epsilon)\le\psi^\epsilon\le0$ and $(\psi^\epsilon)'\le k-\epsilon-h'$ a.e., since $M^\epsilon$ is nondecreasing.
3. Mollify $\psi^\epsilon$. This preserves periodicity and the derivative bound, and does not increase the oscillation. Then $K=h+\psi^\epsilon$ satisfies $\partial_yK\le k-\epsilon$.
4. After a further small generic perturbation $K$ is nondegenerate with $\partial_yK<k$. A class-$(k,0)$ orbit needs $\int_0^1\dot x\,dt=k$ with $\dot x=\partial_yK<k$, which is impossible.
5. Finally $\|h-K\|={\rm osc}\,\psi^\epsilon\to D_k(h)$ as $\epsilon\to0$. $\square$

For $h_A$, the set $\{h_A'>k\}=\{|y|<c_k/2\pi\}$ is one interval per period, so
$$D_k(h_A)=m_k(h_A)=\tfrac1\pi\big(A\sin c_k-kc_k\big)=\tfrac k\pi(\tan c_k-c_k)=\beta_k(A).\tag{5.1}$$

The closed form is verified symbolically; numerically we check that the maximal drop and the oscillation of $\psi$ match it (§1–2 of `verify.py`). The weaker competitor $K=h_A/A$ gives only $(A-1)/\pi$.

### 5.2 The window lemma

**Lemma 5.2.** Let $C$ be a finitely generated Floer-type complex over $\Lambda^G_K$, with $G=g\mathbb Z$ ($g>0$) or $G=0$. Let $J=(s,t]$ with $t-s<g$, where any length is allowed if $G=0$. If $\dim_KH(C^J)$ equals the number of capped generators with action in $J$, then no bar of $C$ has both endpoints in $J$.

*Proof.* Take a Usher–Zhang singular value decomposition, an orthogonal basis $\{x_i,y_i,z_j\}$ with $dy_i=x_i$ and $dx_i=dz_j=0$.
1. By orthogonality, $C^J=C^{\le t}/C^{\le s}$ has the $K$-basis $\{T^\lambda b:\ \ell(T^\lambda b)\in J\}$. Its dimension equals the generator count, because the standard basis is also orthogonal.
2. In $C^J$ the induced differential sends $T^\lambda y_i\mapsto T^\lambda x_i$ when both lie in $J$, and to $0$ otherwise. So $\dim H(C^J)=\dim C^J-2\#\{\text{bars inside }J\}$. $\square$

(`verify.py` §4 tests the dimension formula on 92 windows of random filtered complexes over $\mathbb F_3$.)

### 5.3 The explicit nondegenerate Hamiltonian

**Lemma 5.3 (Theorem C(a)).** Let $A>k$, $y_1=c_k/2\pi$, $y_2=1-c_k/2\pi$ and $0<\delta<\delta_Y:=\min(A^{-2},(2\pi)^{-2})$.

1. The class-$(k,0)$ 1-periodic orbits of $H=H^{(k)}_{A,\delta}$ are exactly the four loops $x(t)=(u_0+kt,y_i)$ with $u_0\in\{0,\frac12\}$ and $i=1,2$.
2. They are nondegenerate, with $\gamma_k$-actions $g_k(y_i)-\delta\cos2\pi u_0$.
3. On $C_1=\{y=y_1\}$ the orbit with $u_0=0$ is elliptic and the one with $u_0=\frac12$ is hyperbolic. On $C_2$ it is the reverse. The $\mathbb Z/2$-grading is the sign of $\det(I-d\phi)$: $+$ for elliptic, $-$ for hyperbolic.

*Proof.*
1. **Rotating frame.** In the frame $u=x-kt$ the system becomes autonomous: $\dot u=h_A'(y)-k$, $\dot y=2\pi\delta\sin2\pi u$. It is the Hamiltonian flow of $F(u,y)=h_A(y)-ky+\delta\cos2\pi u$ on the $(u,y)$-torus. A class-$(k,0)$ orbit of $H$ is a 1-periodic orbit of $F$ that is contractible in the $(u,y)$-torus. Winding in $u$ or $y$ changes the class.
2. **Yorke's bound.** Rescale $y=\sqrt\delta\,\eta$. The vector field in $(u,\eta)$ has Jacobian $\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt\delta h_A''\\4\pi^2\sqrt\delta\cos2\pi u&0\end{pmatrix}$, hence global Lipschitz constant $L\le\sqrt\delta\max(2\pi A,4\pi^2)<2\pi$. By Yorke's theorem every nonconstant periodic orbit of the lift to $\mathbb R^2$ has period $>1$. So the orbits in question are the critical points of $F$: $\sin2\pi u=0$ and $h_A'(y)=k$.
3. **Linearization.** It is $\exp(J\,{\rm Hess}F)$ with ${\rm Hess}F={\rm diag}(-4\pi^2\delta\cos2\pi u_0,\ h_A''(y_i))$, where $h_A''(y_1)=-2\pi A\sin c_k<0<h_A''(y_2)$. If the determinant is negative we get a saddle. If it is positive we get an elliptic point with rotation angle $\sqrt{\det}<2\pi$. Both are nondegenerate.
4. **Actions.** These follow from (2.1) and $\int H\,dt=h_A(y_i)+\delta\cos2\pi u_0$. $\square$

`verify.py` §5 confirms closure, actions to $10^{-10}$, $\det P=1$ and the elliptic/hyperbolic pattern by integrating the flow and its variational equation for $A=2$, $\delta=10^{-3}$. A Newton search from 72 starting points finds exactly four class-$(1,0)$ fixed points. These numerics only illustrate Yorke's bound, which is the proof.

To apply [BX26] one needs all orbits nondegenerate. A further $C^\infty$-small perturbation supported where $|h_A'-k|>\eta$ makes the contractible orbits nondegenerate. It creates no class-$(k,0)$ orbits and leaves the four above and their actions untouched. By Proposition 3.3 and the argument below, the barcode depends only on these data, so we suppress this perturbation.

### 5.4 Proof of Theorem C(b)

Write $\beta=\beta_k(A)$ and $a_i=g_k(y_i)$. A symbolic computation (`verify.py` §1) gives $a_2-a_1=k+\beta$.

1. **Toroidal reduction.** By Proposition 3.3, $G=\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)=k\mathbb Z$, and all four orbits lie in the single free homotopy class $(k,0)$.
2. **Upper bound.** Proposition 5.1 and (5.1) give $\beta_{\max}\le e_{(k,0)}(H)\le\|H-h_A\|+e(h_A)\le\beta+2\delta$.
3. **Clusters.** The generators split into $\mathcal C_1=\{E_1:a_1-\delta,\ S_1:a_1+\delta\}$ and $\mathcal C_2=\{S_2:a_2-\delta,\ E_2:a_2+\delta\}$, where $E$ means elliptic and $S$ hyperbolic. Both clusters are perturbations of the Morse–Bott circles $N_i\cong S^1$ of $h_A$. Their windows of width $2\delta+$ are disjoint modulo $k\mathbb Z$ because $\beta\notin k\mathbb Z+[-4\delta,4\delta]$.
4. **Local homology.**
   - By $(\mathrm L)$, $H(C^{J_i})\cong H_*(S^1;K\otimes\mathfrak o)$.
   - For $k=1$ the orbits are simple, so $\mathfrak o$ is trivial.
   - For $k\ge2$ they are $k$-fold iterates of orbits whose transverse return map is a unipotent shear. The CZ parity is then unchanged under iteration, so the iterates are "good" in the sense of [BO09] and $\mathfrak o$ is trivial.
   - Hence $\dim H(C^{J_i})=2$, and by Lemma 5.2 no bar lies inside one cluster window.
5. **Pairing.** By Theorem A the complex is acyclic, so there are two finite bars. A bar joins generators of opposite parity. Bars joining a cluster to a $k\mathbb Z$-translate of itself would have length at least $k-2\delta>\beta+2\delta$, which the upper bound excludes. So the bars are $E_1\!-\!S_2$ and $S_1\!-\!E_2$.
6. **Lengths.** Both lengths lie in $\{|k+\beta-kj|:j\in\mathbb Z\}=\{\beta,\ k-\beta,\ k+\beta,\dots\}$. Since $\beta<k/2$ and $\delta$ is small, the only value $\le\beta+2\delta$ is $\beta$. The $\pm\delta$ shifts cancel in both pairs, so the barcode is exactly $\{\beta,\beta\}$.
7. **The $\gamma$-energy.** Then $\beta=\beta_{\max}\le e(H)\le\beta+2\delta$. For the degenerate $h_A$, the barcode is the Hofer limit $\{\beta,\beta\}$ by Theorem B(ii), and $\beta-2\delta\le e(h_A)\le\beta$ for every $\delta$, so $e_{(k,0)}(h_A)=\beta$. $\square$

The threshold $\beta_1(A_*)=\frac12$ means $\tan c_*-c_*=\pi/2$, giving $c_*=1.2275897190$ and $A_*=\sec c_*=2.9716938707$. Scaling gives $\beta_k(A)=k\beta_1(A/k)$. Representative values:

| 1.5 | 0.088161 | 0.176322 | 0.264482 | 0.440804 | 0.159155 |

Further properties of $\beta_k$:
- As $A\downarrow k$, $\beta_k\sim\frac{k}{3\pi}\big(2(A-k)/k\big)^{3/2}$. The bars are born with length $0$ at the degenerate orbit circle $h'=k$.
- $d\beta_k/dA=\sin(c_k)/\pi\le1/\pi=\frac{d}{dA}\|h_A\|$, consistent with Theorem B(ii).
- For $A\le k$ there are no class-$(k,0)$ orbits and the barcode is empty. This includes the small Hamiltonians $\varepsilon\cos2\pi x$ suggested as controls.

### 5.5 The Morse–Bott model and general profiles

> **Hypothesis $(\mathrm M)$.** For $H=h(y)+\delta\cos2\pi(x-kt)$ with Morse–Bott circles, $CF_{(k,0)}(H)$ is filtered isomorphic to the Morse–Novikov complex of $F_\delta=g_k(y)-\delta\cos2\pi u$ on the $(u,y)$-cylinder. This complex counts $t$-independent Floer cylinders, i.e. gradient lines, with Novikov variable $T^k$ for the deck shift $y\mapsto y+1$.

$(\mathrm M)$ is what the cascade description of Bourgeois–Oancea [BO09] predicts. It is a theorem when the Hessian is $C^2$-small relative to $2\pi$ [SZ92], but that fails here for $A>1$, so we keep $(\mathrm M)$ as a hypothesis. By Künneth, under $(\mathrm M)$ the barcode is two copies of the barcode of the circle-valued map $g_k:S^1\to\mathbb R/k\mathbb Z$ in the sense of Burghelea–Dey [BD13]. That barcode is the 0-dimensional sublevel persistence, by the elder rule, of the lift $g_k$ on $\mathbb R$.

**Proposition 5.4 (proved; 1-D).** For such $g$, the longest elder-rule bar equals $D_k=\sup_{a<b}(g(a)-g(b))$.

*Proof.* A bar born at a minimum $m$ dies at the lowest barrier to an older component on the left, namely $\max_{[a_0,m]}g$ with $a_0=\sup\{a<m:g(a)<g(m)\}$. No older component lies to the right. Hence every bar is at most $D_k$. Conversely, take a pair $a^*<b^*$ realizing $D_k$, with $b^*$ a minimum.
- No point to the right of $b^*$ is lower than $g(b^*)$; otherwise the drop would exceed $D_k$.
- $a^*\in[a_0,b^*]$, by the same maximality.
So the bar of $b^*$ is at least $g(a^*)-g(b^*)=D_k$. $\square$

Combining Proposition 5.4 with Proposition 5.1 and Theorem B(i), under $(\mathrm M)$ every Morse–Bott profile satisfies $\beta_{\max}=e_{(k,0)}(h)=D_k(h)$. This is Theorem C(c). `verify.py` §3 checks Proposition 5.4 on 12 random trigonometric profiles. It compares union-find persistence with column reduction of the Morse–Novikov chain, and checks that the number of bars equals the number of minima per period.

## 6. Surfaces of genus $\ge2$

Let $\Sigma$ be closed of genus $\ge2$, and let $c$ be a nonseparating simple closed curve. Take a collar $S^1_x\times[-\frac12,\frac12]_y\hookrightarrow\Sigma$ with $\omega=dx\wedge dy$, which requires ${\rm area}(\Sigma)>1$.

**The profile.** Let $h^c_A(y)=\frac{A}{2\pi}(1+\cos2\pi y)$, modified near $|y|=\frac12$ to be smooth with $h\ge0$ and $|h'|<\frac12$ there, and extended by $0$. Let $H^c_{A,\delta}=h^c_A+\delta\chi(y)\cos2\pi(x-kt)$, with $\chi\equiv1$ near $\{h'=k\}$, plus a small Morse perturbation outside the collar.

**The orbits.** For $A>k$, the circles $\{h'=k\}$ are $y_a=-s/2\pi$ (a minimum of $g_k$) and $y_b=-\frac12+s/2\pi$ (a maximum), with $s=\arcsin(k/A)$. Lemma 5.3 applies with the obvious changes ($\chi'=0$ near the circles; for small $\delta$ no new critical points of $F$ appear where $|h'-k|$ is bounded below). A direct computation, using $c_k=\frac\pi2-s$, gives
$$g_k(y_b)-g_k(y_a)=\tfrac1\pi\big(A\cos s+ks\big)-\tfrac k2=\beta_k(A).$$

**Theorem D (surfaces).** Assume $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$. For every $A>k$ and small $\delta$, the barcode of $CF_{k[c]}(H^c_{A,\delta})$ is exactly $\{\beta_k(A),\beta_k(A)\}$. Under $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, moreover $e_{k[c]}(h^c_A)=\beta_k(A)$.

*Proof.*
1. $\Sigma$ is atoroidal (§3), so the bar lengths are exact action differences. The window lemma with $G=0$ and acyclicity force the two cross-cluster bars $E_a\!-\!S_b$ and $S_a\!-\!E_b$.
2. Their lengths are $(g(y_b)\mp\delta)-(g(y_a)\mp\delta)=\beta_k$. No upper bound is needed.
3. For $e$, use the competitor of Proposition 5.1 on the collar: $\psi=g-\sup_{[-1/2,y]}g$. Since $h\ge0$, $g(\frac12)=\max g$, so $\psi$ vanishes near both ends and extends by $0$ across the connected complement. Thus $e\le D=\beta_k$, and $e\ge\beta_{\max}=\beta_k$ by Theorem B(i). $\square$

Compare Ishiguro [Ish16], who computes a relative BPS-type capacity for two homotopic loops; that is a different quantity from $e_\gamma$.

## 7. $T^{2n}$

Let $H_{\delta,\varepsilon}=h_A(y_1)+\delta\cos2\pi(x_1-t)+\varepsilon f(z)$, with $z=(x_2,y_2,\dots)$ and $f=\sum_{j\ge2}(\cos2\pi x_j+\cos2\pi y_j)$, a perfect Morse function with $4^{n-1}$ critical points.

**The orbits.** The rotating-frame argument, with Lipschitz constant $\max(\sqrt\delta\cdot\max(2\pi A,4\pi^2),4\pi^2\varepsilon)<2\pi$, shows that the class-$e_{x_1}$ orbits are the $2\cdot2\cdot4^{n-1}=2^{2n}$ products of critical points.

**The clusters.** Each cluster ($y_1=y_i$) has $2^{2n-1}$ generators with actions in $a_i+[-\delta-2(n-1)\varepsilon,\ \delta+2(n-1)\varepsilon]$. The cluster is a perturbation of the Morse–Bott family $S^1\times T^{2n-2}$, whose homology has rank $2^{2n-1}$. So by $(\mathrm L)$ and Lemma 5.2 no bar lies inside a cluster.

**The bars.** Acyclicity gives $2^{2n-1}$ cross-cluster bars, with $\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)=\mathbb Z$. They have lengths in $\{|1+\beta-j|\}+O(\delta+n\varepsilon)$ and are bounded by $\beta+2\delta+4(n-1)\varepsilon$. For $\beta<\frac12$, all bars are $\beta+O(\delta+n\varepsilon)$. In the Hofer limit, the degenerate $h_A(y_1)$ has barcode $\{\beta_1(A)\}^{\times2^{2n-1}}$ (Theorem D, second part). For the nondegenerate perturbation, individual bars may differ by $O(\delta+n\varepsilon)$. Exact values would need the Künneth form of $(\mathrm M)$, which we do not assume.

## 8. Iterations and Theorem 1.12

Let $H=H^{(1)}_{A,\delta}$, so that $H^{\natural m}(t,\cdot)=mH(mt,\cdot)=mh_A(y)+m\delta\cos2\pi(x-mt)$.

**Proposition 8.1.** Let $m\ge1$ be an integer and $\delta<\delta_Y/m^2$.
- **Unconditional.** The class-$(m,0)$ orbits of $H^{\natural m}$ are exactly the $m$-th iterates of the four orbits of Lemma 5.3. They are nondegenerate, with actions $m(a_i\mp\delta)$ for the iterated capping from $\gamma^{(m)}$.
- **Assume $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, $(\mathrm L)$ and $1<A<A_*$.** The class-$(m,0)$ barcode of $H^{\natural m}$ is $\{m\beta,m\beta\}$, and $e_{(m,0)}(mh_A)=m\beta(A)$.

*Proof.*
1. The rotating frame now has Hamiltonian $mF$. The Yorke constant scales by $m$, and the critical points are unchanged.
2. Here $\omega(\Gamma_\alpha)=m\mathbb Z$, and the candidate lengths are $m|1+\beta-j|$.
3. The competitor gives $e\le D(mg_1)=m\beta$. Since $m\beta<m/2$, only the value $m\beta$ is admissible, and the proof of Theorem C(b) goes through. $\square$

**Corollary 8.2 (dynamics; Hofer radius under iteration).** Assume $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, $(\mathrm L)$ and $1<A<A_*$, and let $m\ge1$.
- Every 1-periodic Hamiltonian $K$ on $T^2$ with $\|K-mh_A\|<m\beta(A)$ has a 1-periodic orbit in class $(m,0)$, i.e. $\phi_K$ has such a fixed point. If $K$ is nondegenerate it has at least two, of opposite parity.
- For every $\epsilon>0$ some $K$ with $\|K-mh_A\|<m\beta(A)+\epsilon$ has none.
- On surfaces of genus $\ge2$ the same holds for the collar profile and all $A>1$.

The point is the **linear growth** of the robustness radius. A competitor for $mh_A$ is not simply $m$ times a competitor for $h_A$, because $mK$ may have class-$(m,0)$ orbits coming from $m$-periodic orbits of $K$. The lower bound comes from the rigid bars $m\beta$.

**Proposition 8.3 (Theorem 1.12 in the examples).** Assume the hypotheses of Theorem C(b) with $k=1$. Let $p$ be prime and $\delta<\delta_Y/p^2$. Generically perturb away from the class-$(1,0)$ orbits so that $H^{\natural p}$ is nondegenerate. Bai–Xu's Theorem 1.12 then gives the barcode of $CF_{\mathbb Z_p}(H^{\natural p},\gamma^{(p)};\Lambda^\Gamma_{K_p})$:
- $\{2\beta,2\beta\}$ for $p=2$, the same number of bars;
- $\{p\beta,p\beta,p\beta,p\beta\}$ for $p>2$, twice as many.

The same holds on genus $\ge2$ for all $A>1$. For example, at $A=2$: $2\beta=0.435991$, $3\beta=0.653987$, $5\beta=1.089978$.

**Two consistency checks.**
1. By Proposition 8.1 the non-equivariant class-$(p,0)$ barcode is $\{p\beta,p\beta\}$. The equivariant barcode is this one tensored with $\widetilde K_p$: unchanged for $p=2$, doubled by $\theta$ for $p>2$. This is what one expects, since $\mathbb Z_p$ acts trivially on the iterated orbits and there are no simple $p$-periodic orbits in class $(p,0)$.
2. Independently of Floer theory, `verify.py` §8 computes exactly the barcode of the Tate complex $C_{\mathbb Z_p}(C^{\otimes p})$ over $\mathbb F_p(u)$, using the 2-periodic resolution with $\sigma-1$ and $N$ and Koszul signs. The input $C$ is a direct sum of elementary complexes $E(\beta_i)$, also after random filtered changes of basis.
   - The results are $\{p\beta_i\}$, with multiplicity 1 for $p=2$ and 2 for $p=3,5$.
   - The endpoints are exactly $(pa,p(a+\beta))$.
   - Negative controls: with a trivial $\mathbb Z_p$-action the stretching fails, and dropping the Koszul sign breaks $D^2=0$.

   This checks the algebraic heart of [BX26, §27.5], the quasi-Frobenius rescaling, in explicit cases.

**Remark 8.4 ($p$-torsion in $H_1$).** As stated, $CF_{\mathbb Z_p}(H^{\natural p},\gamma^{(p)})$ is generated by $p$-periodic orbits homologous to $p\gamma$. If $\tau\in H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ satisfies $p\tau=0\ne\tau$, the $p$-th iterates of orbits in class $\gamma+\tau$ are also homologous to $p\gamma$. Their local summands are not hit by the leading term $\bigoplus_{x\in\widetilde{\mathcal O}_\gamma(H)}P_x$ used in the proof of [BX26, Prop. 28.3].

We therefore read Theorem 1.12(2) as requiring that $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ have no $p$-torsion. Alternatively, we expect it to hold with $CF_\gamma$ replaced by $\bigoplus_{\tau\in H_1[p]}CF_{\gamma+\tau}$, restricted to tuples with $\sum\tau_i=0$. Mixed tuples form free $\mathbb Z_p$-orbits and drop out of the Tate construction. In all our examples $H_1$ is torsion-free.

## 9. Relation to published work

**Recalled, with sources.**
1. **Vanishing in nontrivial classes.** In semipositive or atoroidal settings this is classical [Gür13, GG16, BPS03], via continuation to a $C^2$-small autonomous Hamiltonian. Bai–Xu state it as expected [BX26 §1.3.4, §28.2], including the null-homotopy of the complex (§28.2). Our Theorem A is that argument transplanted to their framework. Its only nontrivial input is $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$, which we do not prove.
2. **Boundary depth.** The bound by the Hofer norm and the Lipschitz property are due to Usher [Ush11] in the contractible sector. Barcode stability is due to Usher–Zhang [UZ16] and Polterovich–Shelukhin [PS16]. Theorem B(i)–(ii) is the $\gamma$-sector version, with $\|H\|$ refined to $e_\gamma(H)$. The robustness in (iv) is the standard consequence.
3. **Local Morse–Bott Floer homology** [Poź99, CFHW96, BO09], **Yorke's period bound** [Yor69], **circle-valued persistence** [BD13].
4. **Stretching of bar lengths under prime iteration.** For contractible orbits: Shelukhin [She20, She21, She22]. For noncontractible orbits in monotone and weakly monotone settings: Sugimoto [Sug]. In general: Bai–Xu [BX26]. We prove no new stretching theorem. Proposition 8.3 applies theirs, and §8 checks the algebra.
5. **Infinitely many noncontractible periodic orbits.** Gürel [Gür13] (atoroidal), Ginzburg–Gürel [GG16] (toroidally monotone), Orita [Ori17] (tori), Sugimoto [Sug] (weakly monotone, via equivariant pair-of-pants). The general case is announced in [BSWX26]. **We do not address this problem.**
6. **Related quantitative work in noncontractible classes.** Polterovich–Shelukhin [PS16] use noncontractible barcodes and the $\mathbb Z_p$-structure of $p$-th powers on surfaces; Zhang [Zha16] uses $p$-cyclic persistence. Ishiguro [Ish16] computes a relative capacity $C=r\cdot{\rm Area}$ for two homotopic loops on surfaces.

**New, to our knowledge (we did a limited literature search).**
- (a) A written proof, conditional on the explicitly isolated hypothesis $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$, of the contractibility over $\Lambda^\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}$ that [BX26, §28.2] expects for homological $\gamma$-cappings (the statement itself is theirs).
- (b) The toroidal reduction, Proposition 3.3, for homological cappings.
- (c) The closed forms $\beta_k(A)=(\sqrt{A^2-k^2}-k\arccos(k/A))/\pi$.
- (d) The exact barcodes $\{\beta_k,\beta_k\}$ of explicit nondegenerate Hamiltonians, independent of $\delta$.
- (e) The identity $e_\gamma=\beta_{\max}=D_k$ and its linear growth $m\beta$ under iteration (Corollary 8.2).
- (f) Explicit instances of Bai–Xu's Theorem 1.12 with an independent check of the Tate stretching.
- (g) Remark 8.4.

The 1-D Proposition 5.4 is elementary and may well be known in persistence theory. We claim no priority for it.

## 10. Status of the results, and limitations

**Proved unconditionally.**
- Lemma 3.1.
- Proposition 3.3, given the structure of [BX26].
- Proposition 5.1 (competitor; $e\le D_k$).
- Lemma 5.2 (window lemma).
- Lemma 5.3 (orbits, actions, gradings, nondegeneracy).
- Proposition 5.4 (1-D identity).
- The closed forms (5.1) and the identity $\beta_k(A)=k\beta_1(A/k)$.
- The orbit statements of Proposition 8.1.
- Remark 3.2(ii).
- The algebraic Tate computations, which are exact finite computations for the listed inputs.

**Conditional.**

| Statement | Assumes |
|---|---|
| Theorem A | $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$ |
| Theorem B | $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ |
| Theorem C(b), Theorem D, Proposition 8.1, Corollary 8.2 | $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$; the barcode part of Theorem D on surfaces needs only $(\mathrm B^0_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$ |
| Theorem C(c) | $(\mathrm M)$ in addition |
| Proposition 8.3 | Theorem C(b), together with Bai–Xu's Theorem 1.12 (proved in [BX26]) |

For aspherical $X$, $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$ and $(\mathrm L)$ hold for the classical Floer complex. Transferring them to Bai–Xu's complex needs a $\gamma$-sector analogue of their comparison Theorem G, which is not available.

**Numerical (illustration only).**
- The ODE closure and action checks.
- The Newton search for orbits.
- The random-profile tests of Proposition 5.4.

**Limitations.**
1. We do not prove $(\mathrm B_\gamma)$, $(\mathrm L)$ or $(\mathrm M)$ in the FOP framework.
2. On $T^2$ the exact barcode is established only for $\beta_k<k/2$, i.e. $A<kA_*$. For larger $A$ the window lemma leaves two candidate lengths, $\beta$ and $k-\beta$ (`verify.py` §9), and we need $(\mathrm M)$.
3. On $T^{2n}$, exact bars are obtained only in the degenerate limit.
4. All examples are autonomous profiles plus explicit perturbations. No example has simple $p$-periodic orbits in class $p\gamma$, so the localization part of Theorem 1.12, which kills free $\mathbb Z_p$-orbits, is tested only algebraically.
5. The examples are aspherical. Sphere bubbling, where the FOP machinery matters, does not occur in them.
6. Corollary 8.2 is a robustness statement for explicit examples. It is not a Conley- or Hofer–Zehnder-type theorem.
