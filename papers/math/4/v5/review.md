# Accept

This is a sound, limited clarification of the accepted paper. The revision preserves the uniform Faltings-height lower bound and the explicitly restricted consequence for the Petersson-ratio method. It introduces no new substantive contribution, and the resulting manuscript remains organized around its central argument. No restructuring or separate submission is needed.

The mathematical clarifications are appropriate:

- In Corollary 1.2, the added inequality $d_M=\varphi(D)\psi(M)/6\ge\varphi(N)/6$ follows from coprimality and $\psi(M)\ge\varphi(M)$. Together with the genus formula and $2^{\omega(N)}=N^{o(1)}$, it justifies the stated uniform genus growth and the negligible relative error.
- In Proposition 3.2, the ordinary case now explicitly supplies the commutativity that the supersingular case obtains from Lemma 3.1. The inert/split distinction is consistent with CM reduction: the only CM fields here have discriminants $-4$ and $-3$, so the primes $p\ge5$ under consideration are unramified. This makes the existing canonical-lifting argument easier to follow without strengthening the proposition.
- In Section 4.6, the normalization is correct. Unramified base change preserves the geometric node count, and $\sum_{v\mid p}f_v=[K:\mathbf Q]=4$, so dividing $\sum_{v\mid p}\delta_v\log N(v)$ by $4$ gives $\delta_p\log p$. The text usefully retains the distinction between this arithmetic-surface discriminant term and the conductor exponent of the Jacobian.

The two refinements requested in the previous review have been implemented accurately. Direct comparison with [27, Theorem 1.1] confirms that $\ell$ may be any prime not dividing $N$. Equation (1.1) of [27] gives precisely $\log|\Delta|\ll\max\{1,h(E)\}$, and its Corollary 1.3 supplies $h(E)\ll N$ for the semistable curves at issue. These changes preserve the example $C\asymp R$ and the scope of Corollary 7.4. The added reference to [17, Theorem 1.4] correctly identifies the subexponential bounds discussed in Section 7.7.

The additional citation locators and bibliographic details improve traceability. The revision requires no new numerical evidence: its changes concern explicit deductions, normalization, and attribution, rather than new experimental or mathematical results.
