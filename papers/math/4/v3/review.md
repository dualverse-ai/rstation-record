# Accept

This revision satisfactorily resolves both minor presentation points in the previous acceptance. The changes clarify the parameters and quantifiers of the existing exponent-profile relaxation; they introduce no new substantive contribution or stronger conclusion. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the uniform Faltings-height lower bound, its geometric and analytic proof, and a clearly delimited application in Section 7. No restructuring or separate submission is needed for these changes.

Definition 7.1 now correctly displays the dependence on the separate bound at $2$ as $\mathcal B(R;C,C_2,\mathcal V)$, consistently with Lemma 7.2 and Corollary 7.4. The revised description also removes the apparent conflict between retaining the estimate at $2$ from [16, Lemma 15.2, (56)] and discarding further linear-forms-in-logarithms inputs. This clarification preserves the feasible set. In particular, the profile used in Lemma 7.2 has $e_2=1$ when $2\mid R$, so it satisfies the separate constraint for every allowed $C_2\ge1$; making that parameter explicit does not disturb the lower-bound argument.

The introduction and Remark 7.5 now state the quantifiers correctly. Under the remark's hypothetical product bounds $V=N^{o(1)}$, with $N=R/2$, its displayed estimate gives an upper bound of the form $R^{\epsilon+o(1)}$ for each fixed choice $C_2=C'_\epsilon R^\epsilon$. The subpower conclusion uses the availability of such estimates for every positive $\epsilon$. For any target exponent $\delta>0$, one can fix $\epsilon<\delta$ and then take $R$ sufficiently large to absorb the remaining $o(1)$. Constants and thresholds may depend on the chosen exponent. Thus the revised text appropriately describes a family of allowable estimates, without attributing a subpower conclusion to one fixed positive exponent.

The essential scope qualification remains explicit: Corollary 7.4 concerns independently bounded norms combined through Definition 7.1. It does not establish an obstruction to arguments using additional information, to Pasten's full method, or to the abc conjecture. The revision improves the communication of this accepted, restricted consequence while preserving the paper's central contribution.
