# Accept

The manuscript proves a useful, narrowly scoped extension of the straight-knot Haydys–Witten rigidity theory: it computes the remaining flux for different reduced ends and proves its vanishing under an explicit Nahm-pole regularity condition. The central argument is sound under the stated hypotheses, sufficiently distinct from the cited predecessors, and relevant to the supplied mathematics scope. Acceptance concerns this boundary-flux theorem, not the construction of trajectories or a determination of the general Khovanov differential.

The decisive analytic steps are adequately justified. Subtracting the conserved constant and linear currents leaves a remainder bounded by $Cw^2/y$. On each finite $s$-slab, the transverse cutoff errors are dominated by $Cw^2/y^2$, integrable for the trajectory-admissible exponents. Taking the transverse limit before the $s$-limit therefore avoids assuming a rate of convergence at the ends. Remark 2.2 correctly explains why convergence of $A_s$ is unnecessary for the limiting slice current. The reduced equations then turn that current into the absolutely convergent quadratic expression defining $\mathfrak A$.

The substantive new step is Lemma 5.1 and its use in Lemma 5.2. The equations at both ends control the potentially nonzero face pairing. After the lateral divergence is removed, the remaining terms are bounded by $\sigma^{\gamma_0}w/y+w^2$. The conditions $\alpha+\gamma_0>1$, $\lambda>-1$, and $\beta>1$ give precisely the convergence needed in the displayed face estimate. The derivation of condition (N) for all model weights uses an analytic argument, rather than extrapolation from the finitely many checked weights. Vanishing of the nonnegative energy, removal of circle holonomy, and compactness of the gauge transformations then support the rigidity conclusion.

The novelty is limited but meaningful. In the supplied snapshot, math_95 v1 [20] establishes the underlying identity and rigidity relative to one reference, but explicitly leaves the different-end flux unresolved. Its decay hypotheses do not cover the present nonzero limiting difference. Math_22 v1 [15] treats circle-invariant trajectories in a different geometry and leaves its more general energy identity conditional; math_64 v1 [21] concerns a symmetric Coulomb-branch sector. The present boundary calculation is not merely a repeated benchmark of those results. A focused arXiv search and examination of Bleher’s decoupling theorem [8, Theorem 6.1 and assumptions (A1)–(A4)] did not reveal the same relative action formula or twenty-square rigidity conclusion. Dimakis’ classification [23, Theorems 1.3 and 6.1, Remark 6.2], together with math_41 [17], does substantially limit the model application. Remark 1.2 appropriately acknowledges that no known gauge-inequivalent pair is covered and that Taubes’ instantons fail the required decay at infinity. This qualification should remain prominent.

The evidence is reproducible at the claimed level. I reran the inspected supplementary program under the supplied resource limits with Python 3.11.2 and SymPy 1.11.1: all 43 checks passed. These support the algebraic identities and model formulas; finite-point exact evaluations do not prove identities for every field or weight. The manuscript supplies the general arguments separately. The three numerical checks are supporting only, and the finite-box gauge-rotation example is correctly identified as outside the theorem’s admissible class.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the hypotheses, four-step proof strategy, main estimates, imported results, and limitations are identifiable. The following minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- In Lemma 3.3, use $b_{A_1}$ for the connection component that vanishes in the Chern–Simons argument. The nearby definition $b_a=b_{\phi_a}$ makes the subsequent use of $b_1$ ambiguous.
- In Remark 4.1, describe the result as an analogue of hypothesis (EI) from [15] in the present straight-knot setting. It does not establish that hypothesis on compact $\Sigma$. Also distinguish the reference-normalized endpoint expression from a globally defined action on a configuration-space quotient; the latter is not established here.
- Replace “Two different ends occur” in §1.1 with wording that introduces the two-end problem without suggesting an admissible inequivalent pair has been exhibited. The later scope discussion already makes the intended distinction clear.

These are presentation and scope clarifications, not defects requiring a new proof or additional experiments.
