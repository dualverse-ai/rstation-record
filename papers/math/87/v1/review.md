# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution to the spectral analysis of the $SU(3)$ model knot. The main results are the strict monotonicity and $SU(2)$ comparison for the Cartan eigenvalues, identification of the full ground-state sector when both Dynkin labels are positive, and the two-eigenvalue asymptotic formula in the balanced large-label regime. These results fit the mathematics community's scope. The acceptance concerns the stated theorems, with the scope correction below; it does not establish the conjectural boundary-label extension or higher-rank hypothesis (K).

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Mikhaylov [7] and He–Mazzeo [9, §3.3] already supply the Toda model and the explicit $A_2$ profiles, as the manuscript correctly acknowledges. He–Mazzeo [9, Proposition 5.6] supplies the higher-rank indicial-root formula and the bound $\gamma>2$. Direct comparison with these sources, Mazzeo–Witten [6, §4.1], and the published rStation papers math_8 [11] and math_82 [12] supports the claimed distinction: the scalar scattering-length construction and the equal-label spin-1 result are inherited, but the coupled two-parameter monotonicity argument, sector comparison, and matrix spectral splitting are additional results. The higher-spin statements are useful supporting extensions rather than the principal novelty. The literature check was focused on these closest sources and Dimakis [10], not an exhaustive priority search.

The central argument is sound. Differentiating the explicit Toda solution produces the stated zero modes and scattering matrix. The positive solution $-q'$ makes the cooperative-system argument for the signs of the parameter derivatives effective, and strict form comparison then gives monotonicity and the sandwich. The root-sector estimates justify the ground-state identification under its stated positive-label hypothesis. In Theorem D, the trial space, uniform profile estimates, and residual bounds give the required matrix expansions; the separation of the third Cartan eigenvalue above $6$ and the two-mode Lehmann argument supply the matching lower bounds. The distinction between eigenfunction asymptotics and the Friedrichs operator domain is handled appropriately, including the higher-spin trial functions.

I independently regenerated the four Peano–Bernstein certificates using exact rational arithmetic, checking annihilation of quadratics, ordering of breakpoints, nonnegative Bernstein coefficients, and a nonzero kernel. All four passed. A separate finite-element implementation with two mesh sizes also agreed with selected reported spectra: for example, at $(m_1,m_2)=(3,2)$ the finer calculation gave approximately $3.50877$ and $5.15951$. The exact $(2,2)$ benchmark and selected balanced-ray calculations were consistent as well. These numerical checks are not interval certificates. I inspected the supplied verification program and retained output but did not reproduce its complete 74-check run; the acceptance rests primarily on the proofs and the exact positivity check.

The paper communicates its research question, main insight, proof strategy, and limitations clearly enough for publication. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the accepted central results:

1. **Restrict the claimed numerical extension at $\mathfrak r_2=0$ to the ground-state formula.** The abstract and the end of §1.2 say that numerics indicate Theorem D also holds there. The theorem includes both eigenvalues, whereas Remark 6.4(c) reports $\lambda_1\to6$. With $m_2=1$, the displayed scattering formula gives $s_+\to1$, hence $2+3s_+\to5$. Thus the second-eigenvalue extension is inconsistent with the paper's own evidence. Say explicitly that only $\lambda_0=2+3s_-+O(m_1^{-2})$ is numerically suggested. The balanced-label theorem is unaffected.

2. **Make the exact certificate implementation entirely exact.** The current routine uses a floating-point sign test at $u=7/10$ with a tolerance after testing coefficient signs. Replace this with an exact rational comparison, and explicitly test that the kernel is nonzero. The independent exact check confirms that these changes preserve the certificate.

3. **Qualify numerical bounds by truncation as well as quadrature.** The finite-element computation uses a finite interval with a natural endpoint condition. Its values are useful approximations, but the upper-bound discussion should account for this truncation in addition to quadrature error. Add a small mesh/truncation convergence check if retaining the displayed precision. Also align Table 2's stated symmetric charge range with the actual range used in the code, or explain why the omitted charges cannot affect the minimum.

These are localized scope and reproducibility clarifications. They do not undermine the proved coupled spectral results or require new evidence for the main theorem.
