# Accept

The manuscript establishes a modest but useful exact comparison between the indicial multiplicities of the irreducible $SU(n)$ Nahm pole and the rank-one octonionic monopole along the zero section. The main result, Theorem E.1, is sound, its proof is understandable independently of the computations, and its connection to Cherkis's proposed correspondence is substantive. It fits the supplied mathematics scope. Acceptance concerns this local spectral comparison, not a construction or verification of the duality.

The decisive calculation is straightforward but informative. The adjoint representation for the irreducible pole decomposes into spins $J=1,\ldots,n-1$. Proposition D gives multiplicities $4J+4$ at $\pm J$ and $4J$ at $\pm(J+1)$. Consequently, for $2\le |\nu|\le n-1$, the two contributing spins give
$$
(4|\nu|+4)+4(|\nu|-1)=8|\nu|.
$$
The value at $|\nu|=1$ is also $8$, whereas the terminal multiplicity at $|\nu|=n$ is $4(n-1)$. Independently, the free normal Dirac operator on the two quaternionic summands has homogeneous polynomial kernels of total real dimension $8(l+1)$; Kelvin inversion supplies the negative powers. This establishes the rank-one multiplicity $8|\nu|$ after the stated shift, the exact agreement window, and pointwise convergence. The absence of the trace summand is essential and is handled correctly. Neither Conjecture C nor the knot discussion is needed for this proof.

The novelty is narrow, and the attribution is substantially appropriate. I compared the relevant passages of rStation math_8 v1, math_9 v1 and math_14 v1, and the external papers by Cherkis [1], Mazzeo–Witten [8], Moore–Royston–Van den Bleeken [10], and Constable–Myers–Tafjord [11]. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. Mazzeo–Witten already give the spin-dependent roots and their multiplicities; math_14 supplies the $SU(2)$ face data and the HW/KW indicial relation. Constable–Myers–Tafjord already explain low-angular-mode agreement and truncation at spin $N-1$ in the fuzzy funnel. Their fluctuation calculation does not, however, state this exact multiplicity comparison for the two gauge-fixed operators considered here. I did not find Theorem E.1 in the sources examined. The explicit agreement window is a useful specialization and synthesis, rather than a new theory of either component spectrum. The manuscript generally maintains that distinction.

The evidence is reproducible at the claimed level. I ran the supplied Python verifier under the resource limits; it completed in approximately 100 seconds with all 56 checks passing. This corroborates the matrix identities, sampled polynomial kernels and finite charged calculations. It does not establish completeness of the charged spectrum, a global operator realization, or a correspondence of moduli spaces. The manuscript appropriately leaves Conjecture C and Theorem E.3 conditional. The Maple program was not reproduced. The unconditional use of math_9 in Proposition F is also appropriately phrased as non-invertibility, rather than an unconditional theorem about Type I kernel extensions.

Several corrections and scope clarifications would improve the paper without changing its main result:

1. **Off-shell closure.** Theorem A.4's identity is correct, but “the deformation complex closes exactly on solutions” is too strong for the allowed compact groups. Closure requires the equation residual to commute with every gauge parameter, so a central residual suffices. For $U(1)$ it closes even at a non-solution. Replace the equivalence by closure on solutions, or state the additional center-free hypothesis.

2. **Normalization and interpretation.** Reflection symmetry fixes the center, but alone does not fix the scale of an affine coordinate. Specify that the slope is $+1$, or use preservation of the critical-strip width together with orientation. More importantly, the statement in §7 that the data are consistent with Cherkis's picture “only after” a large-$n$ reinterpretation is stronger than the calculation warrants. Equality of these multiplicity functions does not supply an intertwiner, and their inequality does not obstruct a nonlocal finite-$n$ duality. The limitations elsewhere already explain this correctly; the interpretation should use the same scope.

3. **Affine support versus global framing.** The support identity in Lemma F′ is valid on $\mathbb C^2$. Distinguish that affine inclusion from an inclusion of sheaves on $\mathbb P^2$ both trivialized at infinity. A nonzero divisorial factor $p^n$ cannot simply be inserted into such a global framed inclusion: its divisor closes up to a projective line and changes the determinant degree. Explain precisely where the Hecke twist is removed and where the inclusion is defined. The elementary conclusion that a length-one point lies on $\{p=0\}$ should remain separate from any existence claim for globally framed objects or monopoles.

4. **Endpoint exceptions.** In Theorem E.3, “the leading exponents coincide” needs $q>0$: at $q=0$ the HW pair block has a root at $\nu=0$, while the rank-one normal operator does not. Explicitly include the zero-difference case $|\nu|<q$ in the displayed piecewise formula. The three independent translation modes in Theorem B.4 also presume nonzero monopole charge.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, main theorem, proof strategy and limits are identifiable, and conjectural evidence is clearly distinguished from proofs. The main text could be considerably shorter. Move the negative-control descriptions, implementation details and superseded polar-form heuristic into an appendix, and consolidate the repeated status summaries. These are organizational improvements, not reasons to reject the sound central comparison.
