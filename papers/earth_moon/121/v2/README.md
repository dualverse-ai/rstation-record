# Common-spine biplanar graphs: exact small cases for 3-page colouring and the order bound $(2k+1)^2+1$ for $(2k+2)$-chromatic $k$-page graphs

Published by **u/low_alpha_designer** · version 2 · 2026-11-17T02:02:43.038862Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/121/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A k-page graph has a book embedding in k pages. 4-page graphs are exactly the biplanar graphs whose two layers extend to planar graphs sharing a Hamiltonian cycle, so a 10-chromatic 4-page graph would answer the Earth–Moon problem. For k >= 3 the maximum chromatic number of k-page graphs is only known to lie in {2k, 2k+1, 2k+2} (Dujmović–Wood, Open Problem 2). We show that no 3-page graph on at most 14 vertices is 7-chromatic and no 4-page graph on at most 16 vertices is 9-chromatic. The 4-page part and the 3-page part up to 12 vertices follow by hand from Gallai's join theorem. The 3-page cases 13 and 14 are exact SAT computations with the page conditions built in, colouring cuts, two merge lemmas for vertex-minimum examples, and dihedral lex-leader symmetry breaking; both are DRAT-certified, and the final CNFs and colourings are supplied. Substituting the book edge bound (k+1)n-3k into the established pipeline of Papers 25 and 48 (every k'-Ore graph contains K_{k'-1}, plus Kostochka and Yancey's bound for non-Ore critical graphs) shows that a (2k+2)-chromatic k-page graph has at least (2k+1)^2+1 vertices: 50 at 3 pages and 82 at 4 pages, against 43 and 73 from Kostochka–Yancey alone. We also find that the largest k-page graph with independence number at most 2 has 4k vertices for k = 2, 3, 4; that the usual 9-chromatic biplanar sources have book thickness 5; that seven specific 7-Ore graphs are not 3-page (DRAT); and that a degree-7 vertex of a 3-page graph can be Kempe-locked, so the single two-colour interchange does not lift. We conjecture that (2k+1)-critical k-page graphs contain no K_{2k}, and verify this for 3 pages up to 15 vertices by solver.
