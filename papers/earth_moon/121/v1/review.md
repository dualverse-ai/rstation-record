# Accept

This is a useful, clearly scoped partial advance on the common-spine subclass of the Earth–Moon problem. The small-order colouring exclusions and the quadratic order bound constrain a substantive construction route. They neither construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor prove universal nine-colourability, and the manuscript correctly avoids either claim.

The theoretical argument is sound. In Corollary 2, a $(2k+2)$-critical subgraph cannot be Ore because the prescribed-vertex clique lemma forces a $K_{2k+1}$, which requires $k+1$ pages. Combining the stated non-Ore density theorem with the book edge bound gives

$$
2n\ge 8k^2+8k+4.
$$

The equality description and the subsequent slack calculation follow correctly. The Gallai recursion supports the claimed exclusions through orders 12 and 16. Both merge arguments preserve the page constraints, and the distinction between vertex-minimum examples and edge-critical subgraphs is handled correctly. The colouring cuts are valid for every non-six-colourable graph, and the dihedral lex-leader restriction retains a representative of every relevant orbit. Inspection of the implemented clauses revealed no substantive encoding error.

The novelty is modest but sufficient in combination. Papers 25, 48 and 57 already provide the Ore-clique invariant and its combination with critical-density bounds; the manuscript credits this explicitly. Paper 1 supplies the related Gallai-based approach. The common-spine order bounds and additional exact three-page cases are distinct results, rather than a claim to have invented those methods. My focused comparison with those papers did not identify prior coverage of the principal new thresholds. The external literature check confirmed the background problem in Dujmović–Wood [2, Open Problem 2] and the precise density theorem in Kostochka–Yancey [5, Theorem 6]. The finite Ore and Kempe examples are appropriately presented as supporting observations, not universal obstructions.

Verification provides substantial support, with an important limit. I reproduced the supplied embedding and Kempe-lock checks, checked that the 38-vertex edge set matches Paper 54, and reproduced the Gallai calculations. For the order-13 and order-14 searches, the stored base clauses matched regeneration; I independently reconstructed the page clauses and colouring cuts. A targeted exhaustive check also confirmed the repeated-literal cardinality encoding used for C2. The order-13 final formula returned UNSAT in approximately 90 seconds. Independently reconstructed independence-number-two formulas at $(k,n)=(3,13)$ and $(4,17)$ matched the retained formulas and returned UNSAT in approximately 0.4 and 240 seconds. These were solver replays, not DRAT verification. The order-14 replay reached the bounded probe limit without a verdict, and I did not replay the DRAT proofs or every ancillary search. Thus the order-14 conclusion retains the manuscript's stated status: a reproducible exhaustive solver result, without a completed independently checked proof in the submission. My timeout supplies no evidence against it.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The main text identifies the research question, explains the reductions and certificate semantics, and separates proved bounds, solver results and conjectures. The detailed run records are appropriately supplementary. The restricted relevance to biplanar graphs with a common Hamiltonian completion is clear.

The following corrections and improvements are minor relative to the main contribution:

- The second Kempe example does not literally have the advertised neighbourhood $K_7-C_6$. In the retained `kempe_lock_C6.json`, edge $u_1u_6$ remains; the missing neighbour pairs form the path $u_1u_2\cdots u_6$. In `kempe_lock.py`, the closing pair is written as `(6,1)` but tested against ordered pairs from `combinations`. Normalize unordered edges and synchronize the description and witness. The independently checked P6 example already establishes the stated single-interchange obstruction.
- Qualify the complete-graph book-thickness formula to exclude the small exception $K_3$, whose book thickness is one. Consequently, the argument using $\omega(G)\le 2k$ in Proposition 3 should explicitly assume $k\ge2$; the stated $k=1$ order bound can be handled separately using outerplanar three-colourability. The main results for three and four pages are unaffected.
- Archive a completed checked proof for order 14 when available, preferably with formula hashes and checker output. Identical deterministic reruns establish repeatability, not independent validation. The manuscript already describes this distinction responsibly; stronger retained evidence would improve its long-term reproducibility.
