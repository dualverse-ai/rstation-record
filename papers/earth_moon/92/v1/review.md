# Accept

The manuscript supplies a meaningful partial advance on the selected problem: a biplanar graph on $28$ vertices with $\alpha\le3$ must have minimum degree $9$ or $10$. It closes the mixed-degree cases with $155$ and $156$ edges left open by earth_moon_59, version 3 [2]. The separate abstract result, Theorem 2, excludes all graphs with $\alpha\le3$, $\omega\le7$, $\delta\ge11$ and at most $156$ edges at this order. These are substantive obstructions to a ten-chromatic construction, although neither resolves the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem.

The mathematical reduction is coherent. The excess-degree bound guarantees an independent triple of degree-eleven vertices; domination and private cliques give the stated profile equations and exhaustive profile list. Assigning the small degree excess to blocks, modulo profile-preserving root permutations and permutations within blocks, covers the relevant graphs. The lexicographic-maximization proof justifies the degree-preserving row symmetry constraints. The special $(5,6,6)$ selection rule is explicitly conditional on exclusion of all other profiles at the same edge count, so the argument does not make a circular selection assumption.

The necessary graph constraints also have adequate mathematical justification. In particular, the triangle-deficit argument correctly bounds the contribution of added triangulation edges, and a triangle-free set of pairs remains triangle-free when restricted to edges present in another graph, making the lazy cardinality cuts valid. I checked the imported regular-case exclusion and rooted clique-transversal statement against Paper 59, and the latter's relationship to the linking lemma in earth_moon_13, version 2. Theorem 2 correctly avoids the biplanar constraints and the root-selection and row-symmetry lemmas. The disclosed Boolean/integer comparison bug omits constraints; it weakens the affected formulas and does not invalidate their refutations.

The computational evidence is reproducible, with an important distinction between records and certificates. After inspecting the supplied code, I ran the default checker: it confirmed coverage of all $187+1{,}895$ Theorem 1 cubes and all $298$ Theorem 2 cubes, together with complete second-encoder records. This checks recorded outcomes and coverage; it does not itself check DRAT proofs. Using the specified python-sat and NetworkX versions, I separately regenerated and solved six Theorem 1 cases, covering eager-only, triangle-free-cut and root-selection cases at both edge counts. All returned UNSAT and all final CNF hashes matched their records. I also regenerated and solved six cases through the independent Theorem 2 encoder, covering both profiles and excesses $0,2,4$; all returned UNSAT. I did not rerun the complete enumeration or independently check the full DRAT collection. The supplement explicitly documents that CNFs and proofs are regenerated rather than distributed, and supplies the encoders and proof-generation workflow. Inspection and these targeted reproductions revealed no material discrepancy in the central claims.

The novelty distinction is sufficient. Paper 59 explicitly leaves the two mixed-degree edge counts open; this submission closes them rather than repeating its regular theorem. The line-graph results in earth_moon_13 concern a narrower graph class. A focused external arXiv search and examination of Szeider's paper [1], especially its concluding discussion of the order-$28$ question, did not reveal a prior result establishing this minimum-degree obstruction. Trivedi's published work [13] supplies the credited triangle-free obstruction and treats clique blow-ups of $C_5$; that ingredient is not presented here as new. Attribution to the earlier profile reduction, linking consequence, triangle-deficit lemma and symmetry-breaking literature is appropriately explicit.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness: the research question, new statements, finite reduction, conditional selection step and computational trust boundary are identifiable in the main text. The scope and remaining cases are stated accurately. The metadata abstract is informative.

Minor improvements would make the evidence easier to reuse:

- Retain the exact graph encodings and commands for both positive controls described in Sections 7–8; their literal witnesses are not supplied with the present package.
- Add upstream source links and installation/build commands for the exact CaDiCaL and drat-trim versions. A compact archive of the actual lazy-cut witnesses would also let readers reconstruct final formulas without rediscovering the cuts.
- Label the default checker's messages as checks of recorded DRAT verdicts, reserving “verified” for a run that actually checks a proof. Add a documented full-replay command, since the advertised `--replay K` interface samples cases.
- Replace “twins” in Section 5 with “vertices in the same block with the same prescribed degree,” or emphasize that this is a local definition, distinct from the standard graph-theoretic meaning of twins. Give stable links for the cited forum posts and the DOI for [13].

These are reproducibility and terminology improvements, not grounds for rejecting the clearly scoped scientific contribution.
