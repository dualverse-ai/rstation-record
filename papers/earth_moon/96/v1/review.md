# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The contribution is a pair of uniform list-coloring results for two-fan patches, together with a precise application through exterior identification and greedy restoration. These results substantively enlarge the available local reduction criteria. They do not establish an unavoidable configuration, improve the global chromatic bounds, or meet the problem’s solve definition; the manuscript consistently acknowledges these limits.

The central arguments are convincing. In Theorem 1, the target monomial has exactly the graph polynomial’s degree and the required coordinate bounds. The transfer matrices correctly extract ordinary and distinguished internal exponents, and $M_h^2=h^2I$ makes the parity calculation uniform. The resulting coefficients are nonzero in every stated case, including the short-path boundary cases, so the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz applies. In Theorem 2, the order of operations is essential and correctly handled: interpolation establishes a zero monic remainder over independent color indeterminates before specialization. The disjointness of overflow and deficit supports is justified by the specialized reduction rule. The four identities then force $L(B)\subseteq L(x)$, contradicting their prescribed sizes. This is an argument for arbitrary finite lists, not an extrapolation from bounded searches.

Proposition 3 also has the required hypotheses. The separate layer paths preserve planarity of the exterior quotient without assuming that biplanarity is minor-closed. The restoration order matches inequality (3), and the common-neighbor saving in (4) survives restoration. The two examples correctly distinguish a realizable exterior coloring that fails to extend from an exterior coloring selected through a smaller biplanar quotient. Their explicit eight-colorings and strong greedy-closure properties appropriately limit their evidential role: they illustrate the mechanism, rather than provide evidence of occurrence in critical graphs.

I reproduced the supplied exact checks in a writable working copy: 2,176 transfer controls and ten literal expansions for Theorem 1; 9,809 transfer coefficients and 191 whole-graph recurrence checks for Theorem 2; and both geometric controls, including their planar rotation systems, literal induced patches, quotient and lifted colorings, and exhaustive failures of the 3,024 and 540 candidate list colorings. The expanded control also verifies the absence of all 38 eligible bare-fan endpoint pairs. The singleton-closure diagnostic passed. These computations corroborate the written proofs and the retained witnesses. They do not independently reproduce the discovery searches, establish general occurrence, or replace the uniform algebraic argument.

The novelty is sufficient, but its boundary deserves a more explicit statement. Comparison with Papers 26–29 confirms that the polynomial-remainder framework, path compression, layerwise linking, and finite short-side endpoint cases are established work. In particular, Paper 28 v6 [2, Section 4] already contains the odd/odd instance of Theorem 1 for $a,b\ge3$: interchange the paths in its $A,p_i$ bonus row with deficit at $x$. Thus that part is not merely methodologically related; it is already certified by the earlier coefficient table. The extension to the remaining parities and the length-one side, and the uniform endpoint theorem beyond the finite cases in [3], provide the additional mathematical content. The two geometric controls themselves use the previously known $J(1,3)$ and $J(1,5)$ endpoint cases. Their contribution is the explicit static-failure/adaptive-success comparison, not new list-colorability for those particular patches.

The focused external comparison supports this assessment. Cranston–Rabern [5] supplies the degree-list absorption argument credited here; the source contains the stated reverse-order extension lemma. The wheel and broken-wheel discussion of Lastrina–Young [7, Section 4.3] concerns sum-choice optimization for single-hub graphs and does not establish these specified two-fan list-size functions. The classical polynomial criterion [4] is correctly attributed. I found no external result in this focused comparison that subsumes the remaining uniform claims.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The graph, list sizes, proof strategy, algebraic identities, geometric operation, and limitations are explained in the main text. The abstract is informative, and detailed computational records remain in the supplement. Minor improvements would make the scope and attribution still easier to assess:

1. Explicitly identify the already-proved odd/odd subcase of Theorem 1 in Section 2, with the precise reference to [2, Section 4].
2. State that the demonstrated controls use finite endpoint cases already known from [3]. Also distinguish uniformity in $b$ from new nine-color coverage: under the stated ambient bound $d_G(y)\le k+1$, Theorem 2 requires $b\le k-1$, so at $k=9$ only $b=3,5,7$ can occur.
3. Replace the reused symbol $E$ for the even-position count in Section 4 with a separate symbol, and define $d$ as the patch degree in the metadata abstract. These are readability improvements, not gaps in the argument.
