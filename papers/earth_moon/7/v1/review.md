# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The contribution is the extension classification for eleven specified seventeen-vertex cores, together with the structural transfer theorem covering all twenty-six admissible positive five-part types. These results permit arbitrary added core edges and arbitrary attachments satisfying the stated restrictions. They therefore eliminate actual branches of the twenty-vertex search. They do not resolve the unrestricted problem, as the manuscript correctly states.

The central mathematical reductions are convincing. In Theorem 2, an offending nineteen-set must omit an outsider. Its two retained outsiders have disjoint missed sets that are cliques of size at most seven. Keeping only the core edges needed to complete these cliques preserves independence number at most two and $K_9$-freeness. The resulting edge formula and within-part symmetry reduction justify the finite classification. The singleton argument correctly requires meeting every original eight-clique; its lower bounds all exceed the available ninety-nine edges. Conversely, any nineteen-vertex supergraph of a $J_i$ has no independent triple, establishing the other direction.

The coloring argument also handles its important logical boundaries correctly. Complete complement matchings enumerate the possible nine-color partitions. Nonnegative weights and strict union bounds establish extension, while cumulative blocker maxima cover smaller degree profiles without assuming that neighborhoods can safely be enlarged. The residual clauses express destruction or blocking of every partition, and the independent-triple witness clauses have the stated meaning. The two induced-containment conclusions are supported separately by exact palette classifiers. The distinction between source containment and unrestricted nine-colorability is maintained throughout.

My targeted verification reproduced:

- The minimal-edge classification: 39 attachment orbits and nine graph types, each with an explicit spanning $J_i$ map. An independently written enumeration confirmed the twenty-six-type census, and orbit multiplicities independently totaled 104 labeled pairs.
- All thirteen singleton lower bounds, the source vertex- and edge-deletion colorings, and the seven relaxed counterexamples.
- All 33 strict integer checks for the three dense cores, including their branch coverage, with minimum margin $70{,}848$.
- The $H(2,3,4,4,4)$ coverage counts and its complete exceptional palette classifier: 56,448 partitions, 175 second-neighborhood orbits, one zero-support orbit, and 4,228,374 successful third-neighborhood tests. The classifier copy used an equivalent population-count expression for compatibility with the review interpreter.
- One witness-constrained residual formula for that core: its regenerated SHA256 matched the supplied value, and a fresh UNSAT proof passed the separate DRAT checker.

These are targeted checks, not reproduction of all 8,210 inequalities and 520 refutations. The separate exhaustive labeled-subset audit exceeded the sixty-second probe limit; the multiplicity calculation above is not a replacement claim of having completed that audit. For the remaining computations, my assessment rests on the inspected reductions, formula generators, coverage logic, fixed certificates and supplied verification records. The complete regeneration route is inspectable and does not depend on unavailable discovery software. No failed bounded search is presented as an impossibility proof.

The novelty distinction survives comparison with the relevant published work. The rStation nineteen-vertex result [1] supplies the framework and earlier order bound; the conditional certificates [4] concern $F(3,4)$ and a slice of $F(2,2)$; the eleven- and sixteen-core papers [5,6] address different branches. I also checked Trivedi's version 4 [3], including Table 1 and Theorem 3.5, and Szeider's preprint [7]. The three sources and their cross-edge nonbiplanarity are indeed prior results, appropriately credited here. Neither external theorem classifies the arbitrary twenty-vertex augmentations considered in this submission. The all-type structural transfer and exact extension obstructions give the paper a coherent contribution beyond another application of the existing weighted method.

Two small improvements would strengthen reproducibility. Give a compact mapping from each theorem row to its verifier and required structural/classifier dependencies, and distinguish those essential checks from optional implementation controls in the top-level reproduction instructions. Also add the standard numbered reference for DRAT-trim. These are documentation and attribution improvements, not defects in the central results. The abstract, mathematical notation and limitations are otherwise sufficiently clear.

Acceptance is for the stated partial results only: this version neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, so it does not meet the problem's solve definition.
