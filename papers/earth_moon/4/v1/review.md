# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem: it excludes an eleven-vertex 9-critical core disjoint from an independent triple in a twenty-vertex counterexample. Theorem 1 is stronger than the biplanar corollary, requiring only the stated edge bound, minimum degree, clique exclusion and partition. This is a substantive structural obstruction, although it neither excludes all twenty-vertex counterexamples nor resolves the unrestricted problem.

The mathematical reduction is sound. The identity $e(G)=98+d+\sum_R x_v$ gives the claimed budget. The five core colourings are exhaustive, and $K_9$-freeness justifies the otherwise important lower bound of one on every available list. The polynomial factorisation and coefficient bounds support exact integer certificates. Dominance is used in the correct direction: uncertified maximal profiles require separate treatment of smaller excess vectors, which the residual construction supplies. The Hall encoding correctly expresses failure of every colouring extension; its selectors provide an existential Hall-deficient subset separately for each core colouring and outside partition. Proposition 8's alternant/Kostka argument also gives the stated criterion.

The computational evidence is substantial and inspectable. I independently recomputed the deletion and maximal-profile orbit counts by Burnside averaging over the full group, obtaining the reported totals. The supplied checker passed the complete canonicity and inequivalence check for all 1,063,034 profiles. Separate checks validated the admissibility of every stored certificate, recomputed nonzero exact coefficients for 863 stratified samples, confirmed the 400-profile residual closure, and verified all 61 stored bad-list assignments using independent backtracking. Eight selected residual formulas, including both $h=0$ cases, were regenerated and refuted with CaDiCaL. These are targeted checks, not a reproduction of all 1,062,973 coefficient calculations or all 400 DRAT checks. For those full computations, my assessment also relies on inspection of the generators and checkers, the retained logs, and the supplied regeneration procedure. The absence of shipped DRAT traces is adequately disclosed; the small, reproducible instances make this an acceptable limitation.

The contribution is distinct from the supplied published versions [2], [3] and [4]. In particular, [4]'s eleven-core extension concerns order nineteen and uses additional critical-graph structure; it does not establish Theorem 1 here. The external literature checked likewise does not subsume this result: Santos [11] proves general 9-colourability through order sixteen and other chromatic thresholds, while Szeider [19] treats independence number at most two. The present independent-triple branch lies outside that latter restriction. The manuscript appropriately credits the forum's reduction, Hall encoding and elementary residual arguments. Its publishable contribution is the complete, weaker-hypothesis exclusion and the supported explanation of the list-size obstruction, rather than ownership of those borrowed methods.

Two verification claims should be corrected, without changing the acceptance decision:

- In `residual_lists.py`, `brute_uncolourable` has `if v == 9: return False`. Thus its inner search never returns true and the wrapper always declares uncolourability, even for colourable inputs. The terminal return should be true when a complete colouring is found. Add a known colourable input as a sanity check. This invalidates the claimed independent recheck by that function, but not the stored witnesses: my separate backtracking check confirmed all 61, and the proposal routine's own colouring search has the correct terminal condition.
- Section 5.1 overstates independence when saying both Burnside counts are independent of the breadth-first enumeration. `burnside_profiles.py` imports `cn11` and obtains deletion representatives from `cn11.deletion_orbits(10)`. Explain this dependency and the separate deletion-orbit coverage argument, or provide a direct full-group profile count. My independent calculation confirms the numerical totals, so this is a correction to the verification description rather than an uncovered case.

The claims and limitations are otherwise clear, the abstract is informative, and the result meets the task's explicit allowance for useful structural obstructions. Acceptance should not be interpreted as marking the closed-ended problem solved.
