# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the stated problem. Theorem 1 excludes an unbounded family of common-root critical-core constructions under the necessary whole-piece Euler budget. Theorem 2 adds a meaningful extension result: every coloring of a support with at most one unit of slack extends with its color names preserved, even with a covered private remainder. Neither result resolves unrestricted Earth–Moon, and the manuscript states that limitation accurately.

The central argument is convincing. The independent-transversal lemma supplies compatible root-color choices without assuming interface degeneracy. The critical-excess estimate then localizes palette failure. For Theorem 1, the budget permits at most one heavy pair, and the local pair analysis supplies the coloring needed by the extension argument. For Theorem 2, the list-degree inequality and the permutation bound force a minimal obstruction to be a $K_7$, distributed as either six palette vertices plus one or seven singleton palettes. The resulting inequality $x+b+D\leq1$ is strong enough to justify the stated repair cases. The absorption identity preserves the slack invariant and gives finite termination.

The delicate local details are addressed rather than hidden in an appeal to computation. In particular, Appendix B distinguishes ordinary-color separation from the exceptional case where both carriers must move to the root color. Appendix C handles a boundary endpoint forced away from that color, covered singleton pieces that cannot be moved, and the possible extra interface edge. The distinction between an induced vertex-critical core and its spanning edge-critical subgraph is also maintained, so additional core edges are not silently discarded. I found no substantive gap in these arguments.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest prior work. The covered-root cap theorem [2] uses a different two-root architecture and hereditary private-density hypothesis; it does not establish the present whole-piece network theorem. The pairwise Hall argument in [7], clique-assembly results in [8], and dense-remainder list results in [10] provide related techniques but not the support localization and fixed-color absorption proved here. The external critical-excess literature [3,4] supplies the explicitly credited classification, rather than the new network result. Szeider’s result on biplanar graphs of independence number at most two [11] does not subsume these networks: independence number two is used locally for the sparse cores, not assumed for the whole graph. The focused comparison identified no substantial prior overlap with the central theorems.

I inspected and ran all six supplementary checkers. All passed, and their outputs matched the retained summaries apart from elapsed-time fields. This reproduced the Dirac exposure checks, the two-carrier and six-target controls, the palette examples, the six pair witnesses, and the nine mixed extension witnesses, including all 147,447 whole-piece subset checks and preservation of the prescribed colors. These are useful corroboration. They neither constitute exhaustive verification of arbitrary networks nor certify biplanarity of the controls; the manuscript correctly bases its universal conclusions on the proofs.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main text states the question, hypotheses, proof strategy and scope, while the appendices contain the detailed repairs. Figure 1 usefully explains the absorption invariant. Two minor improvements would help:

- In Section 4, explicitly reiterate that $b$ counts all actual edges from the entire piece support to the fixed support, including edges not represented in the minimal palette obstruction. This makes the transition from list localization to the fixed-boundary repair especially easy to audit.
- Check the title and version information in reference [4]. The supplied DOI currently displays *Biplanar Graphs on at Most Sixteen Vertices Are 9-Colorable*, whereas the bibliography gives *Small-Order Bounds for the Earth–Moon Problem via Critical Graphs*. Identify the version containing the cited numbered statements unambiguously.

Acceptance is for the stated structural and extension theorems. The initial dense-support coloring question in the five-omission family remains open, and no ten-chromatic biplanar construction or universal nine-color theorem is established.
