# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The manuscript proves nine-colorability at order 21 under the simultaneous restrictions $\Delta\le12$ and $\omega\le7$. Its supporting density results also have independent value: the 82-edge bound does not require an independence-number assumption, and the 83-edge improvement controls the equality case relevant to triple deletions. The paper explicitly leaves larger orders and the other degree and clique ranges open. It therefore qualifies for publication as a structural obstruction, but does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The decisive proof steps withstand scrutiny. Supplement A accounts for nonspanning critical subgraphs, applies the low-excess classification within its strict threshold, and excludes the remaining low-vertex configurations. Sections 2–4 then eliminate the 82-edge boundary under $\alpha\le3$: disjoint triples lead to three anticomplete low cliques whose colorings extend by the matching argument, while intersecting triples permit the disjoint-seven-clique argument and the final contact count. In Section 5, the distinction between an induced deletion and its spanning critical subgraph is handled correctly. Equality is established before transferring the independence bound, avoiding the invalid inference that edge deletion preserves independence number. The resulting triple-degree bound, the bipartite neighborhood-complement reduction, the three colorings in Supplement B, and the final two-clique contradiction give the stated conclusion.

I independently filtered the referenced 5,036-entry order-15 catalogue and recovered exactly three qualifying 55-edge bases and one each with 56, 57 and 58 edges. A separate small enumeration checked every labeled edge augmentation of these bases within the 58-edge budget: all 58,998 augmentations without an eight-clique have the claimed disjoint companion for every seven-clique. This corroborates the particular catalogue consequence used here; it does not independently regenerate the maximal triangle-free catalogue or reproduce the earlier computational core-exclusion proofs. Those remain explicit published dependencies. The new arguments and local colorings are available for direct mathematical inspection.

The contribution is distinguishable from the cited same-problem work. The order bound [1], independence restriction [2], and core exclusions [3] reduce the problem but do not establish these density bounds or the final bounded-degree exclusion. The six catalogue types are appropriately credited to [7]; the new use concerns their seven-cliques under added edges. The external literature check also supports the distinction: the classical unrestricted bound at order $2r$ is $r^2-3$, while Kelly–Postle [6] gives asymptotic results for sufficiently large chromatic number and a substantially different clique regime. Those statements do not supply the finite bounds proved here. I found no substantial duplication in the relevant published results examined.

Clarity is adequate for a graph-theory readership. The research question, hypotheses, proof sequence, imported inputs and limitations are identifiable, and the abstract accurately describes the mechanism and scope. The presentation is compressed in places, but this does not conceal a missing central argument. Three minor improvements would make it easier to check:

- In Section 2's $j=5$ coloring argument, state the available-color count explicitly: an ordinary member of $W$ has seven colors before coloring $T$, at least five afterward, and at least four after the exceptional vertex is colored.
- Give the six catalogue row identifiers or graph6 strings alongside the $(s,t)$ descriptions, so the correspondence with [7] can be checked immediately.
- Separate Section 5's assertions about nonisolated vertices of $Q$, bipartiteness, and the final $4+5$ bipartition into short labeled claims. The arguments are present; clearer landmarks would ease verification.

These are presentation improvements, not conditions requiring a new scientific result.
