# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful structural restriction relevant to the Earth–Moon problem: two anticomplete Gallai components whose vertices have total degree seven can be colored jointly over a five-colorable exterior in an outerthickness-two graph, without an order bound. The consequence for the high graph of a vertex-critical eight-chromatic graph follows. This is a useful partial advance along the double-apex construction route, not a solution of the supplied problem; the manuscript states that limitation accurately.

The central argument is sound. The exact split relation accounts for interactions within each split class through an auxiliary bipartite graph. Lemma 3 correctly includes the initial-failure hypothesis needed for refinement monotonicity, and Lemma 4 uses the two spare colors to recover actual boundary columns for one component while preserving repair of the other. These steps address the principal difficulty of coordinating recolorings of one common exterior.

The subsequent closure also withstands scrutiny. Fresh pair and singleton recolorings localize the second component's defects to the intersection of the repair pairs. In the multiple-pair case, the signed-block forest argument excludes two nonadjacent rays without assuming that every primary block has a private vertex. The final single-pair and common-center constructions give actual subgraphs with chromatic number at least seven and independence number at most two. Their contradiction with [1, Theorems 1–2] uses the published theorem in its stated scope. The single-pair argument correctly permits overlap between its two cliques. I found no material unsupported step in this chain.

The novelty is sufficient. Comparison with the supplied versions of [2], especially Section 15.3 and Sections 24–29, and [3, Theorems 8.1–8.5] confirms substantial methodological ancestry: signed incidence, localization of defects, star support, and merger repairs are prior machinery. The manuscript credits these explicitly. The contribution here is the joint extension theorem for arbitrary-order five-colorable exteriors and unrestricted Gallai components, with the refinement argument and the two forbidden-subgraph conclusions supplying the additional reach. The local reductions in [6] do not already imply this result. The external comparison with Cranston–Rabern [5] and Okada–Otachi–Volk [7] likewise revealed no statement subsuming the theorem; [7] concerns maximal outerplanar-layer constructions and records the broader unresolved chromatic question.

The evidence is reproducible within its stated limits. I inspected the checkers and reran all six checks in a working copy; every receipt matched after removal of timing fields. These computations corroborate the split relations, refinement examples, finite kernels, and selected incidence forests. They do not establish the arbitrary-order theorem by enumeration, and the manuscript appropriately relies on its hand proof for that claim. I checked the statement and argument of the imported result [1], but did not regenerate its outerthickness census or unsatisfiability certificates. Thus the present verification should not be read as an independent computational recertification of [1].

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, contribution, dependencies, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Detailed computational records remain in the supplement, and Figure 1 helps explain the common-center obstruction. The abstract is informative, although dense.

Two minor corrections would improve precision:

- In Lemma 7, replace “precisely the column at $c$ violates the primary signs” with “precisely the column at $c$ violates the sign conditions in (2.1).” The proof allows a defect of $+1$ at a nonprimary block as well as $-1$ at a primary block; its subsequent argument handles both.
- Reference [5], Lemma 3.1, states the noncutvertex list-inclusion lemma. The full block-palette characterization follows by leaf elimination together with the classical degree-list theory; describe [5] as supporting that derivation rather than as directly stating the entire characterization. The manuscript already supplies the relevant derivation, so this is an attribution correction rather than a proof gap.
