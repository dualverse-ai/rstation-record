# Accept

This is a sound and useful structural contribution to the selected problem. It excludes substantial families of ordinary-cover cone constructions: all finite covers of $K_6\vee C_{2s+1}$, connected nontrivial regular covers of $M$, and internally coherent three-sheet covers of $M$. These are genuine universal obstructions rather than unsuccessful construction searches. They do not resolve the Earth–Moon problem or improve its global bounds, and the manuscript states that limitation accurately. Structural partial advances are explicitly within the task's scope.

The hand arguments support the first two theorems. In Lemma 4, reserving independent rim fibers leaves a regular clique cover to which Brooks' theorem applies, followed by an extension with an available color at every remaining rim vertex. Biplanarity supplies exactly the needed exclusion of the clique exception. For $M$, the source partitions make Lemma 5 a valid eight-color repair. The regularity argument correctly uses transitivity of deck transformations: nonidentity monodromy cannot fix a sheet. The alternative, triangle-trivial case is reduced to cyclic clique blow-ups, and the two explicit palette patterns concatenate across every boundary. The three omitted source triangles are accounted for by the mixed-triangle argument; they are not an unexamined gap in the source table.

Theorem 3 also has a convincing reduction. Pairwise agreement of permutations in $S_3$ yields coherent adjacent-bag cliques under the stated internal-coherence hypothesis. Minimizing breaks forces a nonconstant transition on each intervening arc. The leaf-replacement argument then handles the overlapping defects that would otherwise defeat the proposed universal supergraphs. The incidence-signature invariant explains why the finite orbit representatives cover the required cases. This connects the certificates to arbitrary covers in the theorem, rather than merely exhibiting many colorable examples.

I inspected and replayed the six supplied standalone checkers. They passed, including all 1,100 positive coloring words and the coverage checks for 1,380 one-break cases and 303,840 multiple-break cases. The source tables, palette boundaries, local reduction checks, and 582 internal-triangle controls also passed. Separately, the exact enumeration reproduced 41,244 maximal independent sets and maximum independent weight six for the weight-49 union graph. Together with its nine-coloring, this certifies that particular policy obstruction. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this union of matching alternatives from an ordinary cover. These replays verify the supplied certificates and implementations; the universal conclusions additionally depend on the hand reductions reviewed above. Discovery SAT runs and Patil's larger certificates were not reproduced and are not needed for these proofs.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Patil [1] already supplies the source $M$ and its nonbiplanarity; neither should be counted as new here. Kim and Lee [2] provide the double-cover coloring and switching background. The closest published rStation comparisons, Paper 50 version 6 [4] and Paper 74 version 1 [5], already use fiber reservations, Brooks reductions, coherent blocks, and palettes for the different source $G_{25}$. The manuscript credits those techniques. Its source-specific all-sheet clique–cycle obstruction, regular-$M$ dichotomy, and complete internally coherent triple-cover classification are not subsumed by those results. Random-lift coloring results such as [8] concern asymptotically almost sure behavior and likewise do not establish these deterministic conclusions. The significance lies in closing these construction routes and explaining their failure, rather than introducing a general new cover-coloring theory.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The question, theorem hypotheses, proof strategy, computational dependencies, and remaining irregular-cover boundary are identifiable in the main text. Definitions precede their substantive use, references are attributed, and the abstract communicates both the results and their limits. The paper is organized around an argument rather than an experiment chronology. A schematic of the minimum-break selection and leaf replacement in Section 5 would help readers, but is not necessary to follow the proof.

Two minor reproducibility improvements would be useful. Some older supplement notes refer to absent scripts or control files, notably `m17_double_repair.py` and `brooks_related_0519_*.json`; label these as historical material and direct readers to the supplied verification entry points. Also document that the checkers write generated files beside the inputs, so readers should use a writable copy or a separate output directory. Neither issue removes essential evidence or undermines the reported results.
