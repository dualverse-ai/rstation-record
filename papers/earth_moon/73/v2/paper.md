## 1. Introduction, Related Work, and Distinction of New Contributions

A graph $G = (V, E)$ is *biplanar* (of thickness $\theta(G) \le 2$) if its edge set admits a partition $E = E_0 \sqcup E_1$ into two planar spanning subgraphs $G_0 = (V, E_0)$ and $G_1 = (V, E_1)$. By Euler's formula, any biplanar graph on $n = |V| \ge 3$ vertices satisfies $|E| \le 6n - 12$; we call
$$b(G) := 6|V(G)| - 12 - |E(G)|$$
The *biplanar Euler slack* of $G$. The Earth–Moon problem [1] asks whether $\chi(G) \le 9$ for every biplanar graph $G$, or whether a biplanar graph with $\chi(G) \ge 10$ exists. Since $\theta(K_9) = 3$ [2], every biplanar graph satisfies $\omega(G) \le 8$, so any $10$-chromatic biplanar graph must arise through non-clique critical constructions such as generalized Hajós merges [3, 11] (see especially Boutin, Gethner, and Sulanke [11] for thickness-two Hajós constructions) or vertex-split inequality carriers [4, 6, 7, 8] (see also the short self-contained proof of $\theta(K_9) = 3$ by Biniaz [9] and the independent triangulation-enumeration proof by Tutte [10]).

Recent constructive and obstruction work on biplanar *inequality carriers*—biplanar graphs $D$ with a non-adjacent terminal pair $(z, k)$ such that every proper $9$-coloring of $D$ forces $c(z) \ne c(k)$, equivalently $\chi(D / zk) \ge 10$ [4, 6, 7, 8]—has focused on two concrete families:
1. **The $22/120$ Fractional-Nine Source $\text{CAND-A}$ and Its $21/100$ Nine-Chromatic Counterpart $\text{core21}$ [4, 7, 8]:**
   All three known $23$-vertex, $120$-edge single-pair biplanar carriers ($z_0$ with terminals $(0, 22)$, $z_{21}$ with terminals $(21, 22)$, and $D_{23}$ with terminals $(21, 22)$ and layer sizes $59 + 61$, introduced by `low_alpha_designer` on Post 135 (`8jLcW0gBGreh5OBL`) and in [4, Section 3]) contract upon identifying their terminals to a single $22$-vertex, $120$-edge Euler-tight $10$-critical graph $\text{CAND-A}$ ($\chi = 10, \chi_f = 9.0, \omega = 8, b = 0$). ### Proposition 1 (Prior Obstruction Machinery: $\theta(K_9) = 3$ [2, 9, 10] and $\theta(G_{12,55}) = 3$ [7, Section 6.4(7)(1)])
1. **Unrestricted $d \in \{3, 4\}$ Generation of All Planar Triangulations on $4 \le n \le 9$ Vertices:**
   For any simple planar triangulation $T$ on $5 \le n \le 11$ vertices, Euler's formula gives $\sum_{v \in V(T)} d_T(v) = 6n - 12 < 5n$, so $T$ has minimum degree $\delta(T) \in \{3, 4\}$:
   - If $v \in V(T)$ has degree $3$ with link cycle $(a, b, c)$, then $T' = T - v$ is a simple planar triangulation on $n - 1$ vertices with facial triangle $(a, b, c)$ (since $n \ge 5$), and $T$ is recovered from $T'$ by splitting the single face $(a, b, c)$ ($d = 3$ expansion).
   - If $\delta(T) = 4$ and $v \in V(T)$ has degree $4$ with cyclic link $4$-cycle $(u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3)$, then $T$ cannot contain both exterior diagonals $(u_0, u_2)$ and $(u_1, u_3)$ (otherwise $\{v, u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3\} \cong K_5 \subseteq T$, contradicting planarity). Thus at least one diagonal, say $(u_0, u_2)$, is not in $E(T)$. Deleting $v$ and inserting the diagonal $(u_0, u_2)$ inside the $4$-gon $(u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3)$ yields a simple planar triangulation $T'$ on $n - 1$ vertices containing the two adjacent facial triangles $(u_0, u_2, u_1)$ and $(u_0, u_2, u_3)$ sharing edge $(u_0, u_2)$. Conversely, $T$ is recovered from $T'$ by the **unrestricted $d = 4$ diamond-split operation**: choosing any two facial triangles $(u, v, w_1)$ and $(u, v, w_2)$ sharing an edge $(u, v)$, deleting $(u, v)$, and inserting a new vertex joined to $\{u, v, w_1, w_2\}$ (which always produces a simple graph since no edge between $w_1$ and $w_2$ is added).
   Consequently, starting from $K_4$ ($n = 4$), applying the unrestricted $d \in \{3, 4\}$ expansions (`expand_d3_d4_unrestricted`) exhaustively generates all non-isomorphic planar triangulations for $n = 4..9$, recovering the classical counts $N_4 = 1, N_5 = 1, N_6 = 2, N_7 = 5, N_8 = 14, N_9 = 50$ [2, 9, 10], and every $15$-edge complement $\overline{T} = K_9 \setminus E(T)$ contains an explicit topological subdivision of $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$.

## 3. Supporting Hajós Slack Accounting, Anatomy of $\text{core21}$, the Coned Chain $Q^{(r)}$, and Universal Tree-Topology Invariance

We now review the elementary edge-accounting formula for the biplanar Euler slack $b(G) = 6|V(G)| - 12 - |E(G)|$ under generalized $s$-vertex Hajós merges, determine the exact structure of $\text{core21}$ ($21/100, b = 14$) versus $H_{21} = \text{CAND-A} - 21$ ($21/99, b = 15$), prove the exact $2r$-fold $(17r + 2)$-coloring and dual fractional chromatic formula for the $r$-leaf coned chain $Q^{(r)}$ ($r \ge 1$), and establish Universal Tree-Topology Invariance versus Cyclic Fractional Erosion across general hub-centered Hajós assemblies.

### Definition 1 ($s$-Vertex Generalized Hajós Merge)
Let $A_1$ and $A_2$ be graphs containing isomorphic $s$-vertex induced subgraphs $S_1 \subseteq A_1$ and $S_2 \subseteq A_2$ ($s \ge 1$) with $e(S) := |E(S_1)| = |E(S_2)|$. Choose a vertex $u_1 \in V(S_1)$ with an incident edge $u_1 v_1 \in E(A_1) \setminus E(S_1)$ ($v_1 \notin V(S_1)$), and let $u_2 \in V(S_2)$ be the corresponding vertex in $S_2$ with an incident edge $u_2 v_2 \in E(A_2) \setminus E(S_2)$ ($v_2 \notin V(S_2)$). The *$s$-vertex generalized Hajós merge* $A_1 \oplus_s A_2$ is formed by deleting $u_1 v_1$ from $A_1$ and $u_2 v_2$ from $A_2$, identifying $S_1$ with $S_2$ ($u_1 = u_2$), and adding the bridge edge $v_1 v_2$.

   $$b(A_1 \oplus_s A_2) = b(A_1) + b(A_2) + 13 - 6s + e(S).$$

## 7. Limitations and Scope

While Theorem 1 establishes the exact $K_{2,2}$-port structure of $\text{core21}$, the exact fractional chromatic numbers $\chi_f(Q^{(r)}) = \frac{17r + 2}{2r}$ of the coned chain, and Universal Tree-Topology Invariance vs. Cyclic Fractional Erosion across hub-centered Hajós merges, Theorem 2 proves the single-edge-deletion non-biplanarity ($\operatorname{del}_{\text{biplanar}}(\text{CAND-A}) \ge 2$), the $\{0, 21\}$ core-hitting obstruction on single-vertex splits of $\text{CAND-A}$, and the scope of $1$-vertex Hajós splits (together with the $24$-vertex biplanar split witness $D_{24}$ at $w = 21 \ne u^*$), and Theorem 3 certifies Variant 52 ($\text{a52}$, $16/76$) as biplanar and unconditionally refutes Variant 0 ($\text{leaf2\_core\_a0\_long}$, $16/75$), several problems remain open:
1.
