# Accept

The update preserves the accepted results and adds a sound, substantive extension in Theorems 5–6 and Corollary 7. Within the stated order, clique, degree and edge-budget assumptions, it removes the exact-core and exact-degree restrictions for spanning cores with at least $77$ edges. The distinction between unconditional nine-colorability above that boundary and a unique inherited non-biplanar exception at the boundary is important and supported. The four $76$-edge cores with excess degrees or augmentation remain explicitly excluded. This is a useful conditional classification, not a resolution of the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem, and it does not meet the solve definition.

The general adjacent-list identity is correct. For an odd core of independence number at most two, every relevant partition consists of one singleton and pairs. Both endpoint lists are empty precisely when the singleton belongs to $A\cap B$ and every pair meets both neighborhoods. When the lists coincide in one available color, the missing class lies entirely outside $A\cup B$: it is either the singleton or one complement edge, with the singleton then in $A\cap B$. These cases are disjoint and exhaustive. Inclusion–exclusion therefore gives the stated failure count for arbitrary endpoint neighborhood sizes, including augmented cores.

The computational domain matches the stronger claims. The outside edge reduces the baseline incidence cost to $35$, leaving one budget unit over a $78$-edge core and two over the $77$-edge core. The degree profiles and augmentation transitions exhaust those possibilities. Permutations within actual true-twin classes justify the endpoint-pair reduction, while retaining literal isolated neighborhoods preserves their relative placement. The individual $K_8$ and common-neighborhood $K_7$ tests account for every possible $K_9$ involving outsiders. Both residual-search bounds are necessary conditions followed by exact containment checks; neither substitutes a bounded unsuccessful search for exhaustive coverage.

I reproduced the quick composition and augmentation-domain checks, the $52,416$ ordered-subset controls in both matching implementations, and the exception's complete $69,120$-partition check and linking certificate. I also checked that its edge-deleted reference is isomorphic to accepted class 2, comparing every mapped edge. A fresh replay of the $77$-edge branch with core degrees $(9,9,9,9)$ regenerated all $174,550$ ordered pair orbits, including $172,582$ admissible orbits, and independently checked all $1,271$ residual cases. It recovered exactly the two stated isomorphic cover records. A separate fresh color-index replay of the first added-edge branch over $(2,3,4,3,5)$ matched the entire retained mathematical record: $678,446$ pair orbits, $677,490$ admissible orbits, six residual cases and no covers.

These targeted reproductions support the inspected algorithms and retained evidence. I did not rerun the complete $80,879,442$-orbit boundary enumeration or the full $21$-branch dense extension. Acceptance of the remaining branches rests on the completeness arguments, code inspection and retained replay records, whose role should remain distinct from the computations independently reproduced in this review.

The new contribution is meaningfully distinct from the accepted exact-degree theorem and the published results [1]–[3]. Arbitrary attachments and core augmentations are not covered by Trivedi's theorem for graphs that are themselves five-cycle blow-ups. The whole extension contains an independent triple, so Szeider's independence-at-most-two theorem (arXiv:2609.28102) does not subsume it either. The manuscript properly attributes the exceptional graph to earlier work; the advance is the exhaustive classification and exclusion of other failures in this enlarged domain.
