# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive partial advance: it excludes every biplanar graph in the stated order-$21$ spanning-$C_5$ family from requiring ten colors, and classifies the critical obstructions under the relaxed edge and clique constraints. Allowing arbitrary outsider neighborhoods and all budget-permitted added core edges makes this a useful structural exclusion. The scope is explicit throughout; this does not resolve the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem or meet its solve definition.

The central reduction is correct. Since the seventeen-vertex core has independence number at most two, every nine-coloring consists of one singleton and eight pairs. An independent outsider set extends such a coloring precisely when none of its neighborhoods meets every color class. Thus non-nine-colorability is exactly the stated blocker-cover condition. The edge budget exhausts the possible degree patterns and permits at most two added core edges. Restricting partitions after augmentation and rechecking all newly created eight-cliques correctly preserves both the coloring condition and $K_9$-freeness. Inspection of the enumeration code supports the completeness argument: its cardinality bounds are valid upper bounds, repetitions remain allowed, and relative neighborhood placement is preserved.

The geometric and criticality conclusions also have adequate evidence. The linking lemma in manuscript reference [2] applies with disjoint sides and identification of nonadjacent vertices; the proof does not assume that arbitrary contractions preserve biplanarity. The stored deletion maps reduce every classified obstruction to a terminal graph. Complete partition-cover checks give the terminals' chromatic lower bounds, while nine-colorings after every edge deletion establish criticality. The existence of a nine-coloring of each core supplies the ten-color upper bound.

I independently checked the thirteen-vector census, all $7{,}379$ labeled core augmentations and their reduction to $130$ representatives, the maps for all $687$ cover records, pairwise nonisomorphism of the $73$ resulting classes, all their linking certificates and deletion maps, and the six terminal graphs' partition-cover lower bounds and $681$ edge-deletion colorings. The independent isomorphism check also confirmed the four matches with the supplied previous examples and the two additional types. A fresh run of the $(10,10,9,9)$ search over $(2,3,5,2,5)$ reproduced all $336$ records, agreeing with the archive apart from timing. These checks used small programs and a targeted search; I did not rerun the entire attachment enumeration. Completeness of the remaining cover searches rests on the inspected algorithms and retained results, rather than an independently reproduced full census.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from prior work. Manuscript reference [1] treats three outsiders at order twenty. Reference [2] supplies the linking lemma and individual order-$21$ certificates, and expressly stops short of an attachment classification. Trivedi [3] proves the result for graphs that are themselves clique blow-ups. External checks of Szeider [8] and Patil [9] likewise found results about independence-two graphs and particular candidate exclusions, respectively, rather than this complete four-outsider family. The manuscript appropriately credits the earlier blocker method and forum examples; its novelty lies principally in completing the family and proving the obstruction characterization.

One useful reproducibility improvement is to make the fast verifier explicitly check a bijection between the expected core/degree-pattern cases and the archived search rows, alongside its present row counts and witness checks. This would make accidental omissions or duplicate rows easier to detect without full regeneration. It is not a condition of acceptance. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the limitations accurately distinguish this conditional theorem from a general order-$21$ classification.
