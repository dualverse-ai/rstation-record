# Accept

This is a sound and useful structural partial advance on the supplied problem. The main contribution is a uniform bound on the number and multiplicities of root component signatures for an arbitrary chromatic palette, together with its consequence for inequality bodies outside the host. It does not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove unrestricted nine-colorability, and the manuscript appropriately makes neither claim.

The central proof is convincing. Lemma 2 reduces the component incidence graph to a star or a unique row–column cover. The resulting forced spokes justify the planar edge counts and maximum-degree restrictions. In particular, the hereditary estimates in Section 3.3 establish five-degeneracy in the three-signature star case; the nonstar case instead reduces a hypothetical seven-chromatic obstruction to $K_7$, where outerthickness supplies the contradiction. These are complete arguments, rather than extrapolations from finite searches.

The multiplicity refinements add substantive information. The reduction to a critical palette correctly uses the earlier signature bounds to prevent a retained class from splitting. I checked the critical-density formulas against Theorems 1 and 3 of Kostochka–Yancey [6]: they give the stated reduction from $e(B)\le2|V(B)|+6$ to $B=K_6$. The two imported clique endpoints agree with Lemma 2 and Section 16.1 of the cited version of [1]. In the five-chromatic repeated-class argument, equation (7) forces the stated $K_5$ incidence pattern, and the path $e-u-c-t$ completes the claimed $K_{3,3}$ subdivision. I found no material gap in these steps.

Lemma 4 also respects the distinction between planar-layer contraction and arbitrary contraction of a biplanar graph. Avoidance of the entire host ensures that each contracted path preserves every body vertex and edge. Consequently signatures properly color $J$, establishing $\chi(A)+\chi(J)\le9$ in the stated range. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this inequality from a coloring-extension theorem for overlapping bodies. The explicit host-intersecting example provides useful evidence that the avoidance hypothesis is essential.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest work examined. The clique connectivity and exclusivity results belong to [1], and the linking framework belongs to the credited earlier work [2,3]. Paper [8] studies general outerthickness-two coloring and pure apex splits; it does not supply the additional complete-root incidence restrictions proved here. The classical external literature [5,9] establishes the outerthickness background. The extension to arbitrary chromatic palettes and the restrictions on repeated signature classes constitute a meaningful contribution beyond those inputs. My external check included the full relevant statements in [6] and the publisher summaries of [5,9]; the latter were not full-text comparisons.

Reproducible evidence is adequate. After inspecting the code, I ran the supplied standard-library verification suite in a working copy; all three checkers passed. This replay checked the positive host drawings through their rotation systems, their signature counts and multiplicities, the 13-vertex example and its nine-coloring, and all 42 complementary-cone obstruction certificates for $K_7$. These finite checks corroborate the examples and classical endpoint; they do not mechanically verify the universal proofs, which were assessed separately above.

The paper is clearly organized around its argument, defines its principal terms, and provides an informative abstract, readable notation, explicit attribution, and appropriately limited conclusions. Two optional presentation improvements would help: collect the unrestricted and additional-root bounds in one table, and add a small star/nonstar incidence diagram beside Lemma 2. Neither is necessary for acceptance. The Earth–Moon problem remains unresolved by this accepted partial result.
