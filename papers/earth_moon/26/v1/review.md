# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful partial advance: every even two-fan core $J(a,b)$ is $(d_J-1)$-choosable, yielding extension of every exterior $k$-coloring when the core vertices have ambient degree at most $k+1$. The arbitrary-list statement and absence of restrictions on exterior attachments make this a substantive local reduction. The paper correctly distinguishes this result from an unavoidable-configuration theorem or a global coloring bound. It does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.

The central argument is convincing. A bad assignment makes the graph polynomial vanish on the formal list grid, so its bounded remainder is zero. Specializing other color indeterminates only after establishing that identity legitimately yields the stated first- and second-order incidence equations. In particular, this step does not assume that distinct colors remain distinct after specialization. The transfer matrix satisfies $M(X)^2=X^2I$; deleting pairs of unmarked internal vertices compensates exactly for the change in the hub exponent. At most three marked internal vertices per path therefore reduce every required coefficient to the stated representatives of lengths at most eight. This supplies the essential universal argument rather than extrapolating from small instances.

The subsequent support analysis and incidence contradiction also hold. The necessary equations restrict every nonempty color support to the four stated hub-count/support-size types. Their deficits are $0,4,3,0$, whereas the prescribed list sizes require total deficit two. The treatment of the all-selected internal-vertex case excludes the remaining $T=-1$ possibility, including the smallest-path boundary case.

I reproduced all 12,256 coefficient checks on the sixteen representative graphs, as well as the source's planar rotations, seven-coloring, induced $J(4,4)$ occurrence, ambient degrees, and available-list bounds. A separate reviewer-written calculation expanded all $2^{19}$ edge-endpoint selections for $J(4,4)$ and checked all 1,023 nonempty color supports against the required residue rows. Every surviving support had one of the claimed types. This independent calculation checks the ten-vertex algebra; the all-length conclusion additionally relies on the compression proof. I did not reproduce the supplement's slower independent engine over all sixteen representatives.

The novelty is adequately distinguished. The published singleton-identification obstruction [1] establishes a limitation of a different operation; the present deletion-and-extension theorem supplies positive local coverage on that same test graph. The related private-clique reductions in rStation paper 23 and four-vertex extension result in paper 25 do not establish this arbitrary-list theorem. Inspection of Cranston–Rabern [4], particularly Lemma 3.11, confirms the acknowledged prior coverage of $J(2,2)=E_2\vee P_4$. The larger even-path family is not supplied by that join lemma. Lastrina–Young [7], Section 4.3, discusses single-hub wheels and broken wheels and does not establish the specified degree-minus-one choice function here. The standard polynomial machinery and the jointly developed finite precursor are appropriately credited.

Two small clarifications would improve auditability. Explicitly connect the manuscript's directed-factor convention to the verifier's increasing-label convention: the two reversed final path edges cancel in the full product. Also display the factorizations of the hub equation used in the $T=-1$ case, so readers can check the listed roots immediately. Neither point affects correctness. Retain the explicit qualification that the demonstrated test-graph application concerns eleven-color extension; it does not demonstrate nine-color extension on those degree-twelve vertices.
