# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, useful obstruction for a specified construction family: arbitrary old subgraphs of the literal template, with arbitrary reembeddings, cannot produce a ten-chromatic biplanar graph by adding two independent vertices with disjoint core neighborhoods. This is a substantive partial advance under the supplied task statement. It neither constructs the requested ten-chromatic graph nor resolves unrestricted nine-colorability.

The central reduction is convincing. The positive deletion colorings force every core vertex and the marked edges into a minimal counterexample, and force exactly one apex incidence at each core vertex. The two repair certificates then force one of the four nine-chromatic kernels. Minimum degree nine, hereditary Euler bounds, and exclusion of $K_9$ are valid necessary conditions. In particular, the optional-outer-vertex encoding correctly handles the actual order: choosing $q_k$ according to whether $s\ge k+1$ admits every graph satisfying $e(H)\le102+6s$. Padding omitted vertices therefore does not invalidate the density argument. Each coloring cut excludes only graphs colored by its recorded coloring; the bank need not enumerate all colorings for an UNSAT conclusion to be rigorous.

I inspected the reconstruction and reproduced its certificate verification. All 82 positive eight-colorings and 28,170 coloring cuts passed; the reconstructed formula matched the retained CNF byte for byte, with 86,723 clauses and 29,208 variables; and the retained DRAT refutation verified. I also checked that the manuscript's adjacency table and marked-edge specification match the certificate. This replay used Python 3.8.10 and PySAT 1.8.dev17, rather than the documented versions, but reproduced the exact formula. I did not rerun the discovery search or the separately reported CaDiCaL solve. Neither is needed for the retained certificate argument.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. The triangulated-host theorem [4] requires retained triangulations; the dense-core theorem [5] requires an eighteen-vertex independence-two core and its specified root degrees; and the pure-apex result [6] concerns a different layer structure. None supplies the present arbitrary-deletion extension theorem. The external comparison with Szeider's arXiv paper [2] confirms that its global independence-number-two hypothesis does not cover this family. Gethner–Sulanke [1] supplies construction background, rather than the exclusion established here; my access to that article was limited to its publisher abstract. The seed, search framework, and earlier forum transfer result are appropriately credited. The advance is the complete exclusion with optional outer vertices, not the number of coloring cuts or the earlier bounded exploration.

One local scope correction is needed in the generalized one-apex lemma: explicitly assume $K_0\ne\varnothing$. As written, $K_0=C_0=\varnothing$ satisfies $|K_0|>5|C_0|-6$, while the deletion-coloring hypotheses are vacuous and the proof cannot choose a marked edge. This omitted hypothesis does not affect the main theorem or its application with 80 marked edges. It would also help to state explicitly that $T$ itself is an edge envelope, not a biplanar host: its 148 edges exceed the 25-vertex biplanar bound of 138. The theorem correctly concerns its biplanar subgraphs.

The literal scope, inspectable proof evidence, and acknowledged exclusions justify acceptance without treating the Earth–Moon problem as solved.
