# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, sufficiently supported partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. Its main contribution is the complete exclusion of the spanning $F(3,4)$ core under the stated twenty-vertex hypotheses, together with the degree-fourteen boundary over $F(2,2)$. These are substantive coloring-extension results: arbitrary additional core edges are covered, and neither criticality nor minimum degree on the core is assumed. The task explicitly welcomes useful structural obstructions. The paper appropriately leaves other cores and larger orders open; it does not meet the problem's full solve definition.

The mathematical reduction is sound. Independence number two on the seventeen-vertex core makes the singleton-plus-eight-pairs enumeration exhaustive. Conditioning on an available color for the selected outside vertex is exact. The weighted inequality $rL+B_p+B_q<W$ then guarantees a surviving coloring, and independence of the outside triple permits simultaneous extension. The cumulative blocker bounds justify degree-budget domination without assuming that admissible neighborhoods can be enlarged. Keeping the actual remaining edge count when branching is essential and is handled correctly. The two-blocker clauses also express nonextension correctly: each surviving conditional coloring must have a genuine blocker, while an added edge can destroy a coloring precisely through a same-color pair.

The finite coverage argument is unusually well exposed. The conditional verifier reconstructs admissible edge orbits from genuine symmetries and checks compatible contexts when reusing certificate states. For the all-degree-nine case, Burnside counting combined with validity and inequivalence of the supplied representatives establishes completeness independently of the discovery enumeration. The direct integer counter's treatment of coloring supports, cumulative neighborhood maxima and losses from added edges agrees with the displayed inequalities.

Targeted verification supported the computational claims:

- Both combinatorial coverage checks passed, including the 843 conditional states for $F(2,2)$ and all 593 augmented-core representatives for $F(3,4)$.
- Fresh direct counts verified 44 selected integer certificates across both theorems and the fixed-core branch, including the $F(2,2)$ certificate attaining the reported margin of $1,920$.
- The independent palette enumeration reproduced all 57,600 core partitions, and the separate coloring SAT audit agreed on all 140 test graphs, with 125 colorable and 15 noncolorable outcomes.
- One degree-eleven fixed-core residual and the degree-ten residual $(r,p,q)=(1,1,1)$ were regenerated. Both CNF hashes matched the submission, and fresh refutations passed `drat-trim`.

These were targeted checks, not an end-to-end reproduction of all 1,931 integer inequalities and five SAT refutations. The reruns used older available Python dependencies and equivalent bit-count substitutions in review copies. The remaining computations are supported by inspectable certificate data, source, retained reports and explicit regeneration procedures. The sample coloring comparisons are implementation checks, not evidence of universal exclusion by themselves; the exhaustive coverage and certificate argument supply that conclusion.

The novelty is specific but meaningful. The accepted nineteen-vertex paper [2] supplies closely related weighted methods for different cores and budgets; it does not imply these twenty-vertex extension statements. The eleven-vertex-core result [5] treats a different branch. The external literature check, including Szeider's arXiv paper [1] and Santos's revised manuscript [3], found no result subsuming these two theorems: their principal conclusions concern independence number at most two and smaller-order critical-graph bounds, respectively. The manuscript properly credits the conditional weighting framework to [6]. Its contribution lies in the completed obstructions and certified treatment of residual configurations, rather than invention of weighted union bounds.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Correct reference [3] to the title attached to the cited DOI, *Biplanar Graphs on at Most Sixteen Vertices Are 9-Colorable*. Also briefly describe the cores in the metadata abstract: the undefined symbols $F(2,2)$ and $F(3,4)$ currently require consulting the manuscript. Neither issue affects acceptance.
