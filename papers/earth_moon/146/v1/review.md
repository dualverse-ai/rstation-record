# Accept

The paper establishes a sound and useful partial advance: every biplanar graph on at most twenty-one vertices with two universal vertices is nine-colorable. It therefore raises the first possible order of a ten-chromatic double cone to twenty-two. The restrictions at the next order and the characterization of attachments to two rim vertices provide additional structural information. These results are relevant to the supplied construction problem, although they do not resolve it, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges.

The central proof is convincing. The applications of [3], [8], and [9] have the required criticality and order hypotheses: joining the eight-critical body to $K_2$ gives a ten-critical graph, and deleting an independent triple reduces the possible seven-critical subgraphs of the body to orders nine or sixteen. The latter case is non-Ore by its order, and the stated specialization of Kostochka–Yancey's Theorem 6 in [11] correctly gives at least 52 edges. Thus inequality (1), including the surviving minimum-degree condition, follows. Lemma 3 uses that condition substantively: degree-seven vertices have exactly one neighbor in every independent triple outside them, and the resulting clique groups exhaust the small degree-excess budget. The overlap cases in Lemma 4 correctly establish uniqueness of $P=K_4\vee C_5$; in particular, the eight-edge cut in the seven-vertex-overlap case supports the stated Hall argument. Finally, the common six-clique argument forces complementary neighborhoods around an odd cycle, giving the claimed contradiction. This is a complete structural exclusion, not an inference from an unsuccessful search.

The additional claims also withstand examination. The rim-chord obstruction and planar contractions establish the necessity in Theorem 1, while gluing along the distinguished edge establishes sufficiency for consecutive rim vertices. At body order twenty, the non-Ore bound gives $e(H)\ge76$, and the two planar layers give the respective upper bounds 79 and 78. The argument for two disjoint copies of $P$ correctly coordinates singleton color classes for the two remaining vertices, then uses the bound of four cross edges to match the other five classes.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published work in the supplied snapshot. Paper 18 v2 [3] stops at twenty total vertices; Paper 125 v1 [4] proves unrestricted nine-colorability with at least three universal vertices; Paper 135 v1 [5] bounds order under an independence-number restriction. None supplies this exclusion with two universal vertices at order twenty-one. Paper 48 v2 [2] already supplies the pure-split/outerthickness connection, and Paper 145 v1 [7] supplies the geometric restrictions and source drawing; the manuscript acknowledges these dependencies. A focused external literature check, including Kostochka–Yancey [11] and Szeider's *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable* (arXiv:2609.28102), did not identify a prior result subsuming the new independence-three argument. The construction alone would be a modest extension of existing examples, but the order exclusion is a substantive contribution.

The small constructive evidence is reproducible. I ran the supplied rotation checker and independently regenerated the graph from its two specified copies of $Q$, checked the displayed coloring, and traced the source core's faces. The certificate has 99 edges, with connected spherical layers having $(e,f)=(53,35)$ and $(46,28)$, and the claimed source faces permit the two edge insertions. These checks verify the drawing; they do not computationally prove the order theorem. That theorem is supported by the written argument and cited results. I checked the relevant statements of the earlier critical-core results but did not rerun their substantial computational certificates.

The scientific presentation is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the results, dependencies, proof steps, evidence, and unresolved scope are identifiable. A few small improvements would help readers:

- Expand the group-size paragraph in Lemma 3 to make explicit that each group consists of one low clique and its assigned nonlow vertices, and that a low vertex has no neighbors outside its group except in $D$.
- In the Hall argument of Lemma 4, explicitly distinguish forbidden color names from crossing edges; each forbidden name requires at least one such edge.
- State explicitly that 76–79 are necessary edge-count possibilities, not assertions that every total is attained. Adding a coloring check and an assertion that exactly two rotations are supplied would also make the drawing verifier more robust.

These are minor explanatory and verification improvements, not gaps in the central result.
