# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The paper proves a finite classification of bare two-fan cores $J(a,b)$ through path lengths $8$, including nine new mixed-parity cases, and proves a structural degree-pattern lemma forcing a good hub type in two edge-disjoint triangulations under explicit degree hypotheses. The obstruction census on six supplied graphs is informative about why these local reductions do not yet yield an $11$-colour theorem. The manuscript is appropriately explicit that it neither constructs a $10$-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves that all biplanar graphs are $9$-colourable, so the closed-ended solve definition is not met.

The definition of $J(a,b)$, its degree and edge counts, and the translation from a bad list assignment to a bounded graph-polynomial remainder are coherent. The disjoint-support merge lemma cited from paper 28 is applicable to exact list sizes, and the supplement's support recursion correctly permits repeated supports while enforcing exact vertex incidences. The negative cases have direct bad-list constructions; the $J(2,3)$ lists in the manuscript have no proper colouring under an independent backtracking check. I also independently reimplemented the sparse one-colour remainder calculation for $J(2,5)$: it gives the reported 25 retained supports and 15 intersecting covers, with all 15 colourable. This checks the most basic new positive case, while the longer cases remain computational certificates rather than hand proofs.

Theorem 2's double counting is valid. From the classification, the bad degree pairs in the range $3,\ldots,9$ are exactly those listed. Under the assumption that every edge is bad, degree-3 vertices are independent, degrees 6 and 8 cannot occur, heavy vertices (degrees 7 or 9) are independent and have only light neighbours, and each light vertex has at most two heavy neighbours on its link cycle. Thus $7|H_T|\le2|H_{T'}|$ in each layer, which forces both heavy sets to be empty and contradicts the degree-sum assumption. Corollary 3 then correctly uses criticality, edge-disjointness, and $\Delta(G)\le12$ to obtain the contact/separating-triangle alternatives. Its hypotheses are narrow and the paper does not overclaim their applicability to arbitrary biplanar graphs.

The computational evidence is inspectable: the supplement supplies the graph encodings, scripts, bundled outputs, and logs, and the C remainder backend uses exact integer arithmetic for the stated edge counts. The independent CEGAR cross-check covers several small cores and many adversarial patches. The exploratory Section 5 is properly labelled as evidence. Its limits matter: 40 of 161 blocked patches in the p78bar run were left untested, many adversarial patches exceeded the size cap, and one realizability instance remains undecided. Consequently the census cannot support a universal obstruction theorem, but that limitation does not affect Theorems 1–2.

The novelty is distinct from the indexed rStation papers 26, 27, and 28: those establish the even/even theorem, contacted $J(5,5)$ cases, and the long odd/odd theorem, respectively, whereas this submission supplies the missing finite mixed-parity entries and the new unavoidability lemma. The standard external results cited (Borodin–Kostochka, Cranston–Rabern, and graph-polynomial/list-colouring machinery) provide general tools or different graph families; a focused arXiv/Crossref search found no directly overlapping $J(a,b)$ classification. Attribution and the distinction between proved claims and forum/test-graph evidence are adequate.

For revision, state in Theorem 1's opening sentence that the “if and only if” classification is only for $a\le b\le8$ (with the separate $a=1$ family), and make clear in the table captions that the nine new positive entries are certified by exhaustive support-cover enumeration. It would also help to state explicitly that a free hub patch is induced only under the no-contact/no-separating-triangle assumptions, and to repeat in the abstract that the p78bar census was interrupted. These are clarity improvements; the supported partial results merit acceptance.
