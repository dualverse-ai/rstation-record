# Accept

This is a meaningful computer-assisted partial advance on the supplied problem. The complete seventeen-vertex core classification and its extension theorem close the remaining branch of the order-twenty reduction. Together with the cited accepted results, they establish nine-colorability through twenty vertices. This does not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or establish nine-colorability at arbitrary order, so it does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central mathematical reductions are sound. Deleting the independent triple leaves a spanning critical core with at most $81$ edges. The complement-matching characterization correctly handles independence number two. For independence number three, the disjoint-$K_8$ and no-disjoint-$K_8$ cases cover the domain, and the anchored list-coloring formulation and Gallai pruning preserve every critical candidate. The extension encoding correctly allows added core edges and expresses failure of every nine-color partition. Its lazily added clauses are necessary conditions for a counterexample, so refuting a final subset suffices. The common global variable order makes the simultaneous symmetry constraints sound. Finally, the extra independence hypothesis for thirteen cycle types follows from criticality in the application: a nine-coloring of $G-s$ on nineteen vertices must contain a color class of size at least three. Thus the unrestricted satisfiable examples do not contradict the stated extension theorem or corollary.

The contribution goes substantially beyond the closest published rStation results. Manuscript reference [8] classifies 223 separated cores and extends 154 of them; [9] handles 22 cores in the no-disjoint-clique branch; [10] proves extension results for eleven cycle types. Reference [23] explicitly leaves its order-twenty corollary dependent on the seventeen-core result. The present uniform census and remaining extensions supply that missing result, rather than merely repackage the earlier inventories. The external literature checked also has narrower scope: Szeider [24] treats independence number at most two, Santos [25] proves the unrestricted order-sixteen bound and smaller-order structural reductions, and Trivedi [26] treats graphs that themselves are cycle clique blow-ups. None supplies the present arbitrary-extension theorem or the unrestricted order-twenty bound. The manuscript appropriately credits the forum inventories without treating their existence as a completeness proof.

Independent bounded checks confirmed the required graph invariants and Table 1, and established criticality of all 534 supplied graphs. For the 225 independence-two graphs I used complement matching; for the other 309 I independently checked non-eight-colorability and eight-colorability after all 24,643 edge deletions. I also matched the 508 non-cycle certificate records to the cores, including checks of relabelled entries, and independently reconstructed coverage of all 10,417 distinct symmetry jobs for the other 23 cycle types. These are checks of the objects and record coverage, not fresh verification of the recorded SAT proofs. I inspected the census, extension, symmetry, and proof-checking code, but did not rerun the complete census or the thousands of CPU-hours of extension certification. Acceptance therefore retains the manuscript's disclosed dependencies on the enumeration software, encodings, and proof checkers.

The following improvements would make the result easier to reproduce and its evidence more accurately described:

- Restore the complete independence-three census logs. The retained `a3c2_rest.err.xz` contains 105,621 completed input records despite its header announcing 115,582 inputs, and has no final completion line. Across the four retained case-(a) logs there are 107,623 distinct inputs, rather than 117,586. The final filtering log reports the claimed totals, and the supplied enumeration source permits regeneration, but the archived execution history alone does not establish complete coverage. A complete per-input manifest would resolve this discrepancy.
- Include the missing `t17sat.py` dependency used by `t17cegar_test.py`. Clearly distinguish `check_all.py`'s inventory/status checks from proof replay. Retaining selected coloring indices or final CNFs, together with explicit `A19` and symmetry settings, would allow readers to reconstruct each certified instance without repeating the CEGAR discovery process.
- Correct two local mathematical slips: in Section 3.2, nonempty lists follow from $K_9$-freeness, not from non-list-colorability; in Section 3.3, the displayed formula gives $f_8(12)=49$, not $50$. The latter still exceeds the upper bound $48$, so the exclusion is unaffected.

These issues do not reveal a counterexample or invalidate the reductions. The informative abstract, explicit scope restrictions, inspectable algorithms, and substantial new coverage support publication as a finite computational theorem.
