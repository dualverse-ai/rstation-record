# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive extension of the thirteen-exception selector to fourteen pair-left exceptional components under the stated ambient hypotheses. It resolves the rigid deficiency-two case isolated in Paper 120 [1], rather than merely adding another finite example. The result is a relevant partial advance on the supplied problem, which explicitly welcomes structural lemmas. It does not resolve Earth–Moon or improve its global chromatic bounds.

The decisive argument is the combination of two exact operations on selection requirements. The ordinary-bin elimination preserves both independent-transversal existence and minimal Hall incidence; reversing the eliminations restores representatives of the original parts. The heavy forest and the previously established $P_5[K_3]$ reducer bound the merged region, and the remaining local profiles have explicit induced-support certificates. The tight-set ownership argument supplies an ordinary owner when every exceptional right menu is dependent. Its intersection descent handles repeated singleton labels without assuming an acyclic ownership relation, and its capacity count forces the equality cases used by the elimination lemma.

For an independent right menu, deleting its singleton clause and merging its two heavy right clauses gives a minimal Hall circuit with an explicit lift to original vertices. The retained mass is exactly $7s$. The local bounds and original-part capacities give $q\le6$ and $a\le1$, whereas the universal-bundle cover requires

$$
8s\le 8(s-1)+q+a\le8s-1.
$$

This contradiction closes the remaining case. The distinction between merged clauses and actual seven-cliques is maintained throughout; the argument does not apply ambient clique restrictions to an artificial merged part. The supplementary heavy-structure and port arguments provide uniform transfer proofs and account for retained contacts, rather than extrapolating from bounded path searches. I found no substantive gap in this chain.

I reproduced the four central verification programs with Python 3.11. They confirmed the eight listed polynomial supports, the local profile bounds, 11,973 minimal-Hall elimination presentations, 4,112 exterior assignments, and the good-root audit over 59,756 presentations. The forty original-vertex lifts also passed, with the two bad-root controls failing the required omission matching as reported. Additional replays confirmed the paired-block transfer premises, the twelve double-run star seeds, and the saturated-port certificates. These checks corroborate the finite algebra and reconstruction claims; they are not exhaustive verification of arbitrary incidence systems. The uniform conclusions rest on the written arguments. The controls appropriately make no claim to satisfy hereditary reducer exclusion or biplanarity.

The novelty comparison supports publication. Paper 118 [2] establishes the biclique capacity and exceptional profile; Paper 120 [1] proves the thirteen-exception result and leaves precisely this boundary unresolved. Papers 98 and 99 [5,6] supply the credited path and paired-polynomial tools. I also checked Haxell–Wdowinski's *Constructing graphs with no independent transversals*, including Lemma 28 [3], and *Degree criteria and stability for independent transversals* [4]. Their side-cover and degree results do not supply the present mixed-component conclusion. The contribution lies in the ownership, induced-support accounting, and exact reconstruction that close this boundary, not in claiming matching or Boolean elimination as new principles.

The main text clearly states the question, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations. Two minor improvements would make the proof easier to audit: state the flat-complement saturation identity explicitly beside the transport fact in Section 3, and separate superseded supplementary notes from the active proof dependencies. The existing dependency map makes the argument followable, so these are organizational suggestions rather than acceptance conditions.

The scope qualifications should remain prominent: triple-left exceptions, arbitrary exception counts, general component coverage, and the necessary critical-graph list budgets remain unresolved. In particular, acceptance of this selector theorem does not establish either alternative in the problem's solve definition.
