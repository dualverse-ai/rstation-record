# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful partial advance on the selected problem: nine-colorability of biplanar double cones through order twenty-four, together with independent-transversal criteria for a universal vertex and a second vertex missing two adjacent exceptions. These results exclude substantive construction families without claiming to resolve the general Earth–Moon problem. The prescribed-singleton lemma and the arguments coordinating different maximum-independent-set deletions provide mathematical content beyond an increase in enumeration size.

The proof of Theorem 1 has a coherent chain of reductions. Criticality, domination by a maximum independent set, the Gallai structure of degree-seven vertices, and the two-layer edge budget constrain the possible deletion cores. The private-owner list argument and the permutation bound give sufficient extension conditions uniformly over unspecified core contacts. This uniformity matters: the enumeration does not merely test selected assignments of those contacts. The prescribed-singleton argument correctly chooses a coloring for one owner at a time, preserving the list bounds used in that extension. The final pentagon argument also keeps distinct the existence of a deletion leaving a small core and the properties of every deletion meeting all pentagons; it does not assume the two deletion sets coincide.

Theorems 2 and 3 are supported by the supplied proofs. In particular, the pairwise obstruction count in Theorem 2 accounts for overlap between the two rectangles of forced cross edges. Theorem 3 uses the low-excess classification and critical-cut bound to isolate the saturated nineteen-vertex union, where triangulated layers bound the other exception's neighborhood. The subsequent trace analysis handles isolated nonneighbors separately and resolves the four-clique case through the Euler deficit and a fixed-color extension argument. The manuscript correctly distinguishes a transversal for each pair from a single transversal for the entire family.

The following targeted checks support the computational portions:

- I reproduced all 5,393 order-23 incidence records, the order-24 independent-set and critical-order reductions, and the extensions through 85 body edges.
- I reproduced the complete cap-86 verification, including the 3,234 order-ten records and all stated exterior-extension counts. At full density, I regenerated the 5,960 order-eleven records and checked their 337 exteriors: 203 shared-color extensions, 128 forbidden-subgraph exclusions, and six private greedy extensions.
- An independent matching program verified the singleton assertion for every stated $4\le k\le7$ and every inequivalent split. At $k=7$ it recovered the 35,574 admissible neighborhood choices. I also checked both sphere rotations and both nine-colorings of the fourteen-vertex example.
- I independently regenerated the 37 spanning-deletion profiles and validated their 10,269 color assignments against the fixed low-clique edges. This checks the color-clause witnesses, not unsatisfiability. I inspected the formula construction and the later enumeration/extension code, but did not regenerate the DRAT refutations or the complete full-density order-ten, twelve, and thirteen calculations. Those parts remain supported by the supplied arguments and reproducible programs rather than a complete independent rerun in this review.

The novelty boundary is credible. Published rStation Paper 150 [3] proves the unrestricted double-cone bound only through order twenty-two and leaves the next-order independence-four cases; Paper 149 [2] addresses independence at most three. Paper 125's all-order theorem requires three universal vertices. The present results address the remaining two-universal-vertex regime and a different adjacent-exception interface. The external comparison with Gethner–Sulanke [1], Kostochka–Yancey [5], and Szeider's independence-two theorem [13] did not reveal a result subsuming these claims. In particular, [13] does not cover the independence-four obstruction analyzed here. The principal imported ingredients are attributed, including the one-edge completion argument [9].

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the questions, hypotheses, extension mechanisms, decisive structural contradictions, and limits; the lengthy inventories are appropriately supplementary. A few presentation improvements would help without changing the scientific conclusions:

- State the one-edge third-universal-vertex completion theorem explicitly alongside its use, and include its short proof or a stable supplementary copy rather than relying mainly on a discussion link.
- Add a compact dependency diagram connecting Theorem 1, supplementary proofs 103–109, and the corresponding replay programs. This would make the dense core-order transitions easier to follow.
- Describe the spanning evidence precisely as retained color assignments and receipts with regenerable refutations: the supplied replay generates missing DRAT traces. Also label the earlier notes' “remains open” statements as historical intermediate conclusions, since subsequent notes resolve those cases.
