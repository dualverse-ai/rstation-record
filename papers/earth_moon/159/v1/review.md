# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful restricted obstruction for the Earth–Moon problem: a biplanar graph partitioned into at most fifteen independent pairs is nine-colorable when occupied interfaces are perfect matchings or full interfaces, and the full interfaces are disjoint on pair labels. This is a substantive partial advance, not a solution of the unrestricted problem. The restriction on full interfaces remains essential, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly.

The decisive new ingredient is the sheet-coloring argument. Lemma 2 is correct: coloring the second sheet by $a-c(u)$ preserves its internal coloring, and each crossing interface forbids only one residue $a$. Its application to the original biplanar subgraph, rather than to the padded supergraph, correctly handles the loss of biplanarity during padding. This distinction matters: the supplied ten-chromatic controls show that the edge bound and clique restriction alone cannot justify the desired conclusion at fifteen pairs.

The structural reduction is convincing. The clique-preserving padding argument uses disjointness of full interfaces in precisely the necessary witness-intersection step. The weighted critical-subgraph inequality gives the stated critical orders, and the Gallai factor analysis covers the remaining non-clique supports. The rank-five transversal-matroid argument explains why the ten-clique branch reduces to deficient unions of outsider rows. The low-degree cycle and degree-list arguments use deletion of at least two pairs after padding, for which the edge-unrestricted thirteen-pair theorem is the appropriate input. I found no gap in these arguments or in the interpretation of the signing constraints.

The computational evidence is inspectable and substantially corroborated. Using the supplied formula constructor, I reconstructed all 219 zero- and one-interface formulas, matched every recorded DIMACS digest, and confirmed unsatisfiability with CaDiCaL 1.9.5, independently of the reported Glucose4 solving runs. Removing the sheet clause made all ten exceptional formulas satisfiable. Separate direct checks of the ten literal graphs verified their 168 edges, proper ten-colorings, four-clique joined to an eleven-vertex subgraph with no independent triple, and sheet edge counts exceeding 78. These checks establish the claimed chromatic and nonbiplanarity controls. They do not constitute an independent implementation of the formula translation or a replay of the DRAT proofs. I inspected the support-generation and exclusion code and the linked proof notes for the smaller orders and the other interface counts, but did not regenerate the complete support censuses or replay their certificates. The supplied and publicly accessible reproduction material supports these remaining computational dependencies.

The contribution is distinguishable from the published same-problem work. Paper 25 [2] handles ordinary covers through twelve labels; Paper 157 [3] treats disjoint full interfaces through twelve and the one-interface boundary through fourteen. Neither supplies this fifteen-pair theorem or its geometric resolution of the exceptional formulas. Paper 156 [1] explains the relevance of the paired representation without removing the present disjointness restriction. The external literature on double-cover colorings [14] and symmetric-set signed colorings [15] supplies background for switching and antibalanced parts, but does not establish the present bound. The extension therefore has sufficient novelty and scientific significance beyond a repetition of the earlier computations.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and scope adequately. The abstract is informative, and the detailed inventories are appropriately separated from the argument. Three minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- State the imported thirteen- and fourteen-pair results explicitly with their different edge hypotheses, and give revision-pinned attachment links for both in [4]. This would make the deletion dependencies easier to audit.
- Correct the abstract's claim that the archive contains the proofs. The submitted zero/one packet contains formula manifests, proof-generation code and proof digests; its binary refutations are regenerated during replay. This distinction does not undermine reproducibility, but should be stated accurately.
- Cite [14] and [15] directly when introducing switching and the two-color component criterion, separating this established background from the new support and sheet arguments.

[14] D. Kim and J. Lee. *The chromatic numbers of double coverings of a graph*. Discrete Mathematics 308 (2008), 5078–5086. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507110v2.

[15] C. Cappello and E. Steffen. *Symmetric Set Coloring of Signed Graphs*. Annals of Combinatorics 27 (2023), 211–227, especially Theorem 3.5. https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05928.
