# Accept

This revision is a substantial and scientifically meaningful extension of the accepted result. It replaces the two previously treated sixteen-vertex families with eleven explicit core models and proves a general reduction from any sixteen-vertex 9-critical subgraph in the stated order-twenty setting to those models. The new theorem is correctly scoped: it excludes the sixteen-core branch under the necessary $K_9$-free, edge-budget, and minimum-degree hypotheses, while leaving seventeen-vertex cores and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem open.

The reduction is coherent. The $e(F)\ge75$ boundary is forced into the degree-nine outside claw and is handled by the critical-core list-coloring lemma. For $e(F)\le74$, the Gallai join-factor argument yields a universal vertex and an eight-critical fifteen-vertex graph $Q$ with at most 59 edges. The disjoint-clique bounds, independent-four-set argument, and three clique-switch closures then establish the needed independence-number-two conclusion. The complement matching characterization correctly turns vertex- and edge-criticality into a maximal triangle-free census and the eleven stated models. The weighted extension argument, actual-edge branching, predecessor containments, and the Hall treatment of the $F(1,3)$ boundary address arbitrary permitted core augmentations and outside adjacencies.

The evidence is appropriately separated by strength. The explicit extension theorem is supported by fixed integer certificates, compact verifiers, direct coloring-support counters, and literal containment maps. The supplement manifest has 85 files with matching sizes and SHA-256 hashes, and the supplied containment probes pass all listed predecessor patterns. The census reports 5,036 maximal triangle-free graphs and eleven filtered critical models, with graph maps to the eight $F(a,b)$ and three $H(\mathbf a)$ models. These checks support the stated coverage and arithmetic framework, although they do not replace the authors’ retained complete runs.

The principal qualification is correctly acknowledged: exhaustiveness of the eleven-model census imports the completeness theorem and implementation of the published Brandt–Brinkmann–Harmuth generator and nauty canonical labeling. The authors independently inspect the generated outputs and matching filter but do not formally verify that external generator. Thus the complete sixteen-core exclusion is computer-assisted under that explicit dependency; the extension results for the eleven named graphs do not depend on census completeness. The optional SAT material is properly presented as corroboration rather than as a hidden dependency.

For clarity and reproducibility, the authors should keep the census dependency prominent in the abstract and conclusion, cite the original Gallai critical-graph result directly alongside the modern account, and retain the distinction between supplied complete arithmetic audits and independently rerun spot checks. These are presentation improvements and do not undermine the result.
