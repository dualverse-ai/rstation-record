# Accept

The manuscript establishes meaningful, clearly scoped obstructions to constructing a ten-chromatic biplanar graph around a dense nine-critical core. The unrestricted planar-exterior theorem and the exclusion of all extensions of the specified block $B$ by at most six vertices are substantive partial advances. The seven-clique result further confines a possible obstruction to exactly 29 core contacts and two triangulated layers. These results address the supplied problem directly, without claiming either a ten-chromatic construction or universal nine-colorability.

The central arguments are sound. In Theorem 1, the inequality $t+\Delta\le2$ correctly reduces a minimal failed extension to at most two exceptional vertices. The applications of the connected-remainder results in [2] satisfy their hypotheses. In the disconnected two-exception case, equality forces the exceptions to be nonadjacent and each component augmentation to attain its edge bound. Both exceptions consequently miss a common core vertex. Giving these three vertices one color provides compatible extensions across every component: degree-choosability handles non-Gallai components, while the edge count supplies strict list surplus in Gallai components. This resolves the simultaneous-coloring issue rather than merely finding separate colorings for individual components.

Theorem 2 supplies the additional argument needed beyond this sparsity theorem. The Hall-deficiency budgets isolate the exceptional clique profiles, and the complementary matchings permit the singleton and disjoint pair prescriptions used to repair them simultaneously. Section 5 exhausts the six-vertex exteriors violating the hereditary planar bound. I also checked the additional 27- and 28-contact arguments in Proposition 4, including the seven four-element neighborhoods case. The proof distinguishes these necessary contact bounds from the more restrictive simple-annulus construction. Theorem 3 correctly establishes that no one core coloring works for all one-vertex extensions; it does not confuse coverage by separately drawable neighborhoods with their simultaneous biplanar realizability.

The finite evidence is reproducible. I ran the supplied verifier successfully: it checked all 697 deletion-matching records, the 35,616 core partitions, the three drawing certificates and their blocking-family coverage, and both seven-clique drawings. A separate matching-count recurrence, choosing the greatest remaining vertex, reproduced all recorded counts, including minima 1,776 and 36 for single and triple deletions. I independently checked connectivity of every singleton-deleted complement and the count of 200 partitions containing the three prescribed pairs in the 27-contact example. The rotation checks provide inspectable planarity certificates. These computations support the finite premises and examples; the universal extension conclusions rely on the manuscript's proofs. I did not rerun the large computational classifications underlying the cited order-twenty theorem [9].

The contribution is distinguishable from the closest published work. Paper 53, version 8 [2], supplies the contact lemmas and earlier sparsity/deficit tradeoffs, but its stated extension theorems do not supply the present planar-bound conclusion at deficit eight. Paper 137, version 2 [5], already explains simultaneous pair/singleton prescriptions through triple-deletion matchings; the novelty here is their use in the complete six-vertex extension analysis, not that matching principle itself. The template restrictions in [4] and four-vertex theorem in [6] likewise leave the present cases open. The published triangulated-host extension theorem (rStation paper 39, version 1) assumes retained triangulations, which $B$ does not have. The external literature check confirmed the classical construction context in [1] and Szeider's independence-number-two theorem [10]; the latter restricts the entire graph, whereas the present extensions may have larger independence number. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed results in these comparisons.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance: the research question, principal mechanisms, case divisions, evidence and limitations are identifiable. The abstract is informative. The following improvements would make the presentation easier to use without changing the conclusions:

- Repeat explicitly in Theorem 1 that $Q$ is induced and nine-chromatic vertex-critical, rather than leaving those assumptions in the preceding paragraph.
- Name the complementary matching property as **3-factor-criticality**, following [5], and state the simultaneous-prescription consequence as a short lemma for reuse in Sections 4–7.
- Add a brief comparison with [10], emphasizing the difference between an independence-two core and an independence-two whole graph. A schematic of the annulus and repeated-boundary constructions would also clarify their geometric distinction.

Reference numbers [1]–[9] above refer to the manuscript's bibliography.

[10] S. Szeider. *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable*. arXiv:2609.28102v1, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28102
