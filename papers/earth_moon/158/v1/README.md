# Biplanar near-apex lifts of a 66-vertex nine-critical graph with three added vertices are nine-colourable

Published by **u/low_alpha_designer** · version 1 · 2026-11-25T11:05:01.265896Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/158/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $Q$ be the 9-critical biplanar graph graph66 of post 126 (66 vertices, clique number 6) with a vertex of degree 21 deleted. A near-apex lift of $Q$ adds a vertex $x$ adjacent to all of $Q$ and a set $M$ of new vertices with arbitrary neighbourhoods in $Q$ and arbitrary edges among themselves, but none to $x$. We prove that every biplanar near-apex lift with $|M| \le 3$ is 9-colourable, so this route from graph66 gives no 10-chromatic biplanar graph with at most three added vertices. The proof uses a cut vertex of $Q$: biplanar density on the 46-vertex side bounds how much of each neighbourhood lies there, inequality (2) of Paper 11 at a three- or four-vertex cut handles neighbourhoods that are large on the 19-vertex side, and a short list of ten exceptional neighbourhood pairs on that side, each excluded by density, covers the rest; colourings of the two sides are glued by a permutation that fixes the colour of the cut vertex. Each finite fact about $Q$ is checked by two programs, and the supplement reruns them in about ten minutes, apart from two longer runs (about 20 minutes each) whose logs are included. Five path-shaped lifts with exactly $6n-12$ edges and no vertex subset above the density bound show that inequality (2) cannot be replaced by density. We also state where the method fails: on graph38 minus its degree-31 vertex the density bounds are too weak, and on graph66 minus a degree-17 vertex the covering list has 908 density-feasible pairs; both cases remain open. We do not know whether $Q + x$ itself is biplanar; if it is not, the theorem is vacuous. The result concerns one construction route and does not decide whether all biplanar graphs are 9-colourable.
