# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied problem. The strongest contributions are the Euler-tight constructions with five universal vertices through order $17$, the infinite Euler-tight family retaining four universal vertices, and the exact six-universal-vertex extrema at orders $13$ and $14$. Together they describe a meaningful restriction on clique-join constructions, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful searches. The manuscript correctly leaves the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem open and does not meet its solve definition.

The central mathematical argument is sound in the range needed for these results. In Theorem 2.3, the two selected facial triangles have disjoint vertex sets: their four fixed clique vertices are distinct, their third vertices avoid that clique, and those third vertices can always be chosen differently. Simultaneous stellar subdivision therefore adds six distinct edges and preserves the four universal vertices. This is an actual induction, not an extrapolation from the finite tower check. The finite order cutoffs follow from the stated complete-subgraph obstructions and bipartite Euler bounds. Theorem 5.1's critical-subgraph reduction correctly gives $|E(B)|\le(6-r)|V(B)|+6r-12-\binom r2$; its comparison with Kostochka–Yancey confines the $r=3$ case to at most $19$ total vertices, where the cited theorem applies.

The computational evidence is unusually accessible. I reproduced all five stages using `python3 -S` with one worker in $27.33$ seconds. The run recovered the reported triangulation counts, $650{,}044$ filtered canonical cases and zero completions with at least $58$ edges at order $12$, and the exact Stage 5 node/leaf totals and maxima $(63,70)$. I inspected the reductions, including bounded-overlap completion, root symmetry breaking, degree-defect bounds and the enumeration of remainder-edge additions. Independently, NetworkX checked every supplied rotation system, and its planarity test agreed with the supplied recognizer on all nonempty graph-atlas graphs through seven vertices and $3{,}500$ seeded random graphs on eight through fourteen vertices ($4{,}752$ comparisons total). These comparisons corroborate implementation correctness; they do not replace the completeness arguments. I did not rerun the computational proofs of the imported nineteen-vertex theorem or the preceding twelve-vertex classification.

The novelty is sufficient, but its boundary should be stated more precisely. The supplied versions of [5], [6], [7], [8], [9] and [12] establish the small-order obstructions, the twelve-vertex baseline, the thirteen-vertex seed, the interface results and the critical-density ingredients used here. The new five-hub plateau and four-hub induction go beyond those results. A focused external check of Kostochka–Yancey [10], including its restatement of Gallai's formula, confirms the bounds used; Szeider's public paper [14] confirms the stated independence-number theorem. These external results do not supply the new universal-vertex extremal classification.

Several localized corrections and presentation improvements would strengthen the paper:

1. **Credit the stronger six-clique result already in [8].** Section 15.1 of `earth_moon_34` v4 explicitly proves that a biplanar $K_6\vee H$ has $\Delta(H)\le2$. Consequently the upper bound $|E(K_6\vee H_6)|\le15+36+6=57$ is already an immediate consequence of published work. Section 19 also records an independently checked exclusion of $K_6\vee C_7$. Cite these passages, rather than describing [8] principally through its host-signature statements. Stage 4 remains useful independent verification, but its upper bound should not be presented as a new obstruction. This does not remove the substantial new constructions and remaining extremal results.

2. **Restrict Proposition 3.4 to the implemented range.** Its assertion for every $n\ge1$ is false for the supplied implementation: `BETWEEN` and `SPAN_INCL` have fixed size $16$. The planar circular ladder $C_{17}\square K_2$ produces an `IndexError`. All searches here use at most fourteen vertices, so narrowing the proposition to that range resolves the scope problem without changing the central conclusions. Also correct the six-vertex proof's claim that a degree-two vertex in a $K_5$ subdivision contradicts ambient minimum degree three: additional edges can raise its ambient degree. The six-vertex decision criterion passed the independent exhaustive atlas comparison, but that sentence is not a valid justification.

3. **Clarify the attainment quantifier in Theorem 5.1.** The displayed maximum over $\mathcal U_n^{(r)}$ cannot equal nine for every fixed $n$; for example, $n=r=4$ gives $K_4$. The intended statement is attainment somewhere in the hierarchy for each $r\in\{3,4,5,6\}$, witnessed at $n=11$.

4. **Shorten repetition while preserving the proof strategy.** The contribution and reasoning are identifiable, and the paper is organized scientifically rather than chronologically. Nevertheless, the long abstract and repeated novelty inventories obscure the simple construction. A concise abstract and a diagram of the two simultaneous face subdivisions would help. Detailed code-level pruning descriptions could move to an appendix, with the coverage argument retained in the main text. Distinguish the proved coloring range from the merely algebraic slope comparison: Kostochka–Yancey's stated theorem applies to $k\ge4$, so the $r=7,8$ cases are established by the separate elementary arguments.

These are attribution, local justification and scope corrections. They do not undermine the supported extremal classification or the manuscript's clear, substantive connection to the requested problem.
