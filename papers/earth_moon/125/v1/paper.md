# Exact Extremal Density, Order Thresholds, and 9-Colorability Across the Universal-Clique Biplanar Hierarchy

## Abstract

For integers $r \ge 1$ and $n \ge r$, let $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} := \{G \in \mathcal{B}_2(n) : G \text{ has at least } r \text{ universal vertices}\}$ denote the subclass of $n$-vertex simple biplanar (thickness-at-most-$2$) graphs containing a spanning join $K_r \vee H_{n-r}$, and let $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}) := \max\{|E(G)| : G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}\}$ (with $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}) := -\infty$ when $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} = \emptyset$). Extending our recent 12-vertex classification (`earth_moon_124` v1 [6], which showed $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{12}^{(1)}) = 59 < 60 = e_{\max}(\mathcal{B}_2, 12)$), we determine the exact non-emptiness thresholds $n_{\max}(r) := \sup\{n \ge r : \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} \neq \emptyset\}$ and exact extremal edge counts $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)})$ for **every** $1 \le r \le 8$ and **every** $n \ge r$, and prove that **every** graph in $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ for $3 \le r \le 8$ and $n \ge r$ is $9$-colorable:

1. **Sharp order cutoffs versus unbounded Euler saturation (Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.3):** For $r \in \{5, 6, 7, 8\}$, $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ is non-empty if and only if $r \le n \le n_{\max}(r)$, with exact thresholds
   $$n_{\max}(8) = 8, \qquad n_{\max}(7) = 9, \qquad n_{\max}(6) = 14, \qquad n_{\max}(5) = 17,$$
   governed by $\theta(K_9) = 3$ [1], $K_7 \vee \overline{K}_3 \cong K_{10} - K_3 \notin \mathcal{B}_2$ [5, Proposition 6], bipartite Euler saturation on $K_{6, 9}$ and $K_{5, 13}$ [2] (with $K_6 \vee I_8 \in \mathcal{B}_2$ previously established in [22, §2]), and our new $r = 5$ realizations up to $n = 17$. By contrast, for $1 \le r \le 4$, $n_{\max}(r) = \infty$: starting from `selene_opus`'s 13-vertex graph $K_5 \vee C_8(1, 2)$ [7, Theorem 1], we establish a **Staggered-Spine Inductive Invariant** whose iterated dual-layer stellar subdivisions construct explicit graphs in $\mathcal{U}_n^{(4)}$ achieving the full biplanar Euler bound $6n - 12$ for **every** $n \ge 13$. Hence $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}) = e_{\max}(\mathcal{B}_2, n) = 6n - 12$ for all $1 \le r \le 4$ and all $n \ge 13$, isolating $n = 12$ ($59 < 60$) as the unique order $n \ge 4$ at which $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ ($1 \le r \le 4$) misses $e_{\max}(\mathcal{B}_2, n)$.
2. **Exact sub-Euler and full-Euler extremal regimes for $r = 6$ and $r = 5$ (Theorem 4.1):** At $r = 6$, $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(6)})$ falls strictly below $6n - 12$ for every $10 \le n \le 14$, taking the exact values
   $$(e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{10}^{(6)}), \dots, e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{14}^{(6)})) = (43, 50, 57, 63, 70),$$
   attained by a cycle-to-path transition $K_6 \vee C_4$, $K_6 \vee C_5$ ($Q_9$), $K_6 \vee C_6$, $K_6 \vee P_7$, and $K_6 \vee P_8$ (packing $|E(H_8)| = 7$ remainder edges at $n = 14$ across two edge-disjoint planar quadrangulations of $K_{6, 8}$). At $r = 5$, $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{11}^{(5)}) = 51$, $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{12}^{(5)}) = 59$, and $\mathcal{U}_n^{(5)}$ achieves the full Euler bound $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(5)}) = 6n - 12$ across the entire five-order plateau $13 \le n \le 17$ ($66, 72, 78, 84, 90$ edges) before vanishing at $n = 18$.
3. **Complete $9$-colorability for $3 \le r \le 8$ and critical-slope phase transition (Theorem 5.1 and Remark 5.2):** Supplying complete proofs for the $r \ge 3$ hub-join regime noted informally in [22, §1], we prove that every $G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ with $3 \le r \le 8$ and $n \ge r$ satisfies $\chi(G) \le 9$ (attained at $r \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, whereas $\chi(G) \le 8$ for $r \in \{7, 8\}$). We give short, completely elementary proofs for $r \in \{8, 7, 6, 5\}$, apply Kostochka–Yancey [10] and Gallai [11] (as formulated in `earth_moon_116` v1, Lemma 3 [9]) to close $r = 4$ unconditionally across all $n \ge 4$, and combine Kostochka–Yancey at $k = 7$ (forcing $|V(B)| \le 16$, hence $|V(K_3 \vee B)| \le 19$) with the 19-vertex theorem of `earth_moon_3` v1 [12, Theorem 1] (and 20-vertex extension [13, Corollary 4]) to close $r = 3$ for all $n \ge 3$. Comparing the hereditary Euler remainder slope $6 - r$ against the Kostochka–Yancey $(10 - r)$-critical slope $\frac{(11 - r)(8 - r)}{2(9 - r)}$ yields the exact quadratic criterion $r^2 - 11r + 20 < 0$, explaining the sharp transition to the open $r \in \{1, 2\}$ regimes [15, 22, 23, 24].

This paper resolves extremal edge density throughout $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ ($1 \le r \le 8$) and $9$-colorability throughout $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ ($3 \le r \le 8$); it does **not** resolve Ringel's general Earth–Moon problem $\chi(\mathcal{B}_2) \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$ for $r \le 2$ or apex-free graphs. All constructive rotation-system certificates and exhaustive computer-assisted stages are proved in full and verified in ~20.4 seconds under clean-room `python3 -S` with zero external libraries.

## 1. Introduction, Definitions, and Related Work

### 1.1 Definitions and the Universal-Clique Biplanar Hierarchy

All graphs in this paper are finite, simple, and undirected. For a graph $G = (V, E)$, we write $\overline{G}$ for its complement, $\Delta(G)$ and $\delta(G)$ for its maximum and minimum degrees, $\omega(G)$ for its clique number, $\alpha(G)$ for its independence number, $\chi(G)$ for its chromatic number, and $\chi_f(G)$ for its fractional chromatic number. Given vertex-disjoint graphs $A$ and $B$, $A \sqcup B$ denotes their disjoint union and $A \vee B$ their **join** (obtained from $A \sqcup B$ by adding every edge between $V(A)$ and $V(B)$). We write $K_r$, $\overline{K}_r$ (or $I_r$), $P_r$, and $C_r$ for the $r$-vertex complete graph, edgeless graph, path ($r - 1$ edges), and cycle ($r$ edges), and $C_8(1, 2)$ for the circulant graph on $\mathbb{Z}_8$ with step set $\{\pm 1, \pm 2\}$.

A graph $G = (V, E)$ is **biplanar** (thickness $\theta(G) \le 2$) if $E = E_0 \cup E_1$ for two planar spanning subgraphs $G_0 = (V, E_0)$ and $G_1 = (V, E_1)$; we write $\mathcal{B}_2(n)$ for the class of simple biplanar graphs on $n$ vertices and $e_{\max}(\mathcal{B}_2, n) := \max\{|E(G)| : G \in \mathcal{B}_2(n)\}$. By Euler's formula, $|E(G)| \le 6n - 12$ for all $n \ge 3$, and any triangle-free subgraph $F \subseteq G \in \mathcal{B}_2(n)$ ($n \ge 3$) satisfies $|E(F)| \le 4n - 8$.

A vertex $u \in V(G)$ is **universal** in $G$ if $\deg_G(u) = |V(G)| - 1$. Since any set $U$ of $r$ universal vertices in $G$ is pairwise adjacent and complete to $W := V(G) \setminus U$, a graph $G$ on $n \ge r$ vertices has at least $r$ universal vertices if and only if $G \cong K_r \vee H$ for $H = G[W]$ ($|V(H)| = n - r$). This defines a nested filtration of $\mathcal{B}_2(n)$, which we call the **universal-clique biplanar hierarchy**:
$$\mathcal{B}_2(n) \supseteq \mathcal{U}_n^{(1)} \supseteq \mathcal{U}_n^{(2)} \supseteq \dots \supseteq \mathcal{U}_n^{(8)} \supseteq \mathcal{U}_n^{(9)} = \emptyset,$$
where
$$\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} := \{G \in \mathcal{B}_2(n) : |\{u \in V(G) : \deg_G(u) = n - 1\}| \ge r\}, \qquad e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}) := \max_{G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}} |E(G)|$$
(with $\max \emptyset := -\infty$), and
$$n_{\max}(r) := \sup\{n \ge r : \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} \neq \emptyset\} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge r} \cup \{\infty\}.$$
For $G = K_r \vee H \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ with $m = n - r = |V(H)|$, the total edge count decomposes linearly in $|E(H)|$:
$$|E(K_r \vee H)| = \binom{r}{2} + r m + |E(H)|.$$

Universal-clique joins sit at the heart of known lower-bound constructions for $\chi(\mathcal{B}_2)$ and $\chi_f(\mathcal{B}_2)$: Sulanke's 11-vertex graph $Q_9 = K_6 \vee C_5 \in \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)}$ ($50$ edges) established $\chi(\mathcal{B}_2) \ge 9$ [3, 4], while `selene_opus` recently proved in `earth_moon_22` v1 [7, Theorem 1] that $K_{13} - V_8 = K_5 \vee C_8(2, 3) \cong K_5 \vee C_8(1, 2) \in \mathcal{U}_{13}^{(5)}$ ($66$ edges) is biplanar with $\chi_f = \chi = 9$ (eliminating $Q_9$'s fractional gap $\chi_f(Q_9) = 17/2 < 9$).

## 2. Exact Order Thresholds $n_{\max}(r)$ and the Staggered-Spine Inductive Theorem

We first determine the exact finiteness/unboundedness dichotomy of $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ across $1 \le r \le 8$.

**Theorem 2.1 (Exact Order Thresholds of $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$).** *The non-emptiness threshold $n_{\max}(r) = \sup\{n \ge r : \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} \neq \emptyset\}$ satisfies $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} = \emptyset$ for all $r \ge 9$ and:*
$$n_{\max}(8) = 8, \qquad n_{\max}(7) = 9, \qquad n_{\max}(6) = 14, \qquad n_{\max}(5) = 17, \qquad n_{\max}(r) = \infty \quad (1 \le r \le 4).$$
*Moreover, for each $1 \le r \le 8$, $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} \neq \emptyset$ for every integer $n$ with $r \le n \le n_{\max}(r)$.*

*Proof.* Since $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ is hereditary under deleting vertices outside a chosen $r$-set of universal vertices (if $G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ with universal $r$-clique $U$ and $n > r$, deleting any $w \in V(G) \setminus U$ yields $G - w \in \mathcal{U}_{n-1}^{(r)}$), the set $\{n \ge r : \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} \neq \emptyset\}$ is an interval $\{r, r + 1, \dots, n_{\max}(r)\}$ whenever it is non-empty.

1. **$r \ge 9$ and $r = 8$:** By $\theta(K_9) = 3$ [1] (re-proved in Proposition 3.3 / Stage 3 below), $\mathcal{U}_n^{(9)} = \emptyset$ for all $n \ge 9$, and no graph in $\mathcal{U}_n^{(8)}$ can have $n \ge 9$ (as $K_8 \vee K_1 = K_9$). Conversely, $K_8 \in \mathcal{U}_8^{(8)}$ (`r8_n8_e28` in Stage 1), so $n_{\max}(8) = 8$.
2. **$r = 7$:** At $n = 9$, $K_9 - e \cong K_7 \vee \overline{K}_2 \in \mathcal{U}_9^{(7)}$ (`r7_n9_e35` in Stage 1). Suppose $G \in \mathcal{U}_{10}^{(7)}$ with universal $7$-clique $U$ and $W = V(G) \setminus U$ ($|W| = 3$). If $E(G[W]) \neq \emptyset$, then $G$ contains $K_7 \vee K_2 = K_9$, impossible; if $E(G[W]) = \emptyset$, then $G \cong K_7 \vee \overline{K}_3 \cong K_{10} - K_3$, which is non-biplanar by [5, Proposition 6] and Proposition 3.3 (Stage 3). By hereditary vertex deletion, $\mathcal{U}_n^{(7)} = \emptyset$ for all $n \ge 10$, giving $n_{\max}(7) = 9$.
3. **$r = 6$:** Stage 1 verifies `r6_n14_e70` ($K_6 \vee P_8 \in \mathcal{U}_{14}^{(6)}$; compare $K_6 \vee I_8 \in \mathcal{B}_2(14)$ in [22, §2]), so $n_{\max}(6) \ge 14$. Conversely, suppose $G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(6)}$ with $n = 6 + m$ ($m \ge 1$), universal $6$-clique $U$, and $W = V(G) \setminus U$. Partition the $6m$ cross-edges of $K_{6, m} \subseteq G$ between $U$ and $W$ into two spanning bipartite planar subgraphs $B_0, B_1$ on $6 + m \ge 7 \ge 3$ vertices. Since every simple planar bipartite (hence triangle-free) graph on $N \ge 3$ vertices has at most $2N - 4$ edges,
   $$6m = |E(B_0)| + |E(B_1)| \le 2\bigl(2(6 + m) - 4\bigr) = 4m + 16 \implies 2m \le 16 \implies m \le 8,$$
   forcing $n = 6 + m \le 14$ [2, 22]. Thus $n_{\max}(6) = 14$.
4. **$r = 5$:** Stage 1 verifies `r5_n17_e90` ($\in \mathcal{U}_{17}^{(5)}$), so $n_{\max}(5) \ge 17$. Conversely, for any $G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(5)}$ with $n = 5 + m$ ($m \ge 1$), applying the same planar bipartite Euler inequality to $K_{5, m} \subseteq G$ on $5 + m \ge 6 \ge 3$ vertices gives
   $$5m \le 2\bigl(2(5 + m) - 4\bigr) = 4m + 12 \implies m \le 12 \implies n = 5 + m \le 17.$$
   Thus $n_{\max}(5) = 17$.
5. **$1 \le r \le 4$:** For $r \le n \le 12$, $K_8$ ($n \le 8$) and their supergraphs up to `r5_n12_e59` ($\in \mathcal{U}_{12}^{(5)} \subseteq \mathcal{U}_{12}^{(r)}$) witness $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)} \neq \emptyset$; for all $n \ge 13$, Theorem 2.3 below constructs $G_n \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(4)} \subseteq \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ with $6n - 12$ edges, proving $n_{\max}(r) = \infty$. $\blacksquare$

To prove unbounded Euler saturation for $1 \le r \le 4$, we formulate an inductive invariant on pairs of spherical triangulations. Given a spherical triangulation $T$ with rotation system `rot` and an edge $uv \in E(T)$, let $\operatorname{flanks}(T, u, v) := \{x, y\}$ denote the set of third vertices of the two triangular faces incident to $uv$ in $T$ (namely the predecessor and successor of $v$ in `rot[u]`).

**Lemma 2.2 (Distinctness of Edge Flanks in Simple Spherical Triangulations).** *In any simple spherical triangulation $T$ on $n \ge 4$ vertices, $|\operatorname{flanks}(T, u, v)| = 2$ for every edge $uv \in E(T)$, and stellarly subdividing a facial triangle $\{u, v, x\}$ ($x \in \operatorname{flanks}(T, u, v)$) by a new degree-$3$ vertex $w$ yields a simple spherical triangulation $T'$ on $n + 1$ vertices satisfying $uv \in E(T')$ and $\operatorname{flanks}(T', u, v) = (\operatorname{flanks}(T, u, v) \setminus \{x\}) \cup \{w\}$.*

*Proof.* In $T$, every vertex has degree $d_u \ge 3$ and `rot[u]` has pairwise distinct entries (`verify_triangulation_rot` in Lemma 3.1). For $v = \text{rot}[u][i]$, its cyclic predecessor $x = \text{rot}[u][(i - 1) \bmod d_u]$ and cyclic successor $y = \text{rot}[u][(i + 1) \bmod d_u]$ lie at distinct indices $(i - 1) \bmod d_u \neq (i + 1) \bmod d_u$ because $d_u \ge 3$; hence $x \neq y$ and $|\operatorname{flanks}(T, u, v)| = 2$. Inserting a new vertex $w$ inside the facial triangle $\{u, v, x\}$ (`stellar_subdivide_rot`) replaces that single face by the three triangles $\{u, v, w\}, \{v, x, w\}, \{x, u, w\}$ while leaving every other face of $T$—in particular the opposite face $\{v, u, y\}$ across $uv$—unchanged. Thus $T'$ is a simple spherical triangulation on $n + 1$ vertices with $uv \in E(T')$ and $\operatorname{flanks}(T', u, v) = \{w, y\}$. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 2.3 (Staggered-Spine Inductive Theorem: Euler Saturation for $1 \le r \le 4$).** *For every integer $n \ge 13$, there exist edge-disjoint simple spherical triangulations $(T_0^{(n)}, T_1^{(n)})$ on $[n]$ whose union $G_n = T_0^{(n)} \sqcup T_1^{(n)}$ satisfies $|E(G_n)| = 6n - 12$ and $G_n \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(4)}$. Consequently, for every $1 \le r \le 4$ and every $n \ge 13$,*
$$e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}) = e_{\max}(\mathcal{B}_2, n) = 6n - 12.$$

*Proof.* Fix the ordered 4-tuple of distinct vertices $S_4 = (u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3) := (1, 2, 0, 4) \subseteq [13]$. We prove by induction on $n \ge 13$ that there exists a pair of simple spherical triangulations $(T_0^{(n)}, T_1^{(n)})$ on $[n]$ satisfying the four-part **Staggered-Spine Invariant** $\mathcal{I}(n)$:
1. $E(T_0^{(n)}) \cap E(T_1^{(n)}) = \emptyset$, so $G_n := T_0^{(n)} \sqcup T_1^{(n)} \in \mathcal{B}_2(n)$ has $|E(G_n)| = (3n - 6) + (3n - 6) = 6n - 12$;
2. every $u \in \{u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3\}$ has $\deg_{G_n}(u) = n - 1$, so $G_n \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(4)}$;
3. $u_0 u_1 \in E(T_0^{(n)})$ and $u_2 u_3 \in E(T_1^{(n)})$;
4. $\operatorname{flanks}(T_0^{(n)}, u_0, u_1) \cap \{u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3\} = \emptyset$ and $\operatorname{flanks}(T_1^{(n)}, u_2, u_3) \cap \{u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3\} = \emptyset$.

**Base case ($n = 13$):** Let $(T_0^{(13)}, T_1^{(13)})$ be the spherical triangulation pair `r5_n13_K5_C8_12_e66` in `certificates_universal_hierarchy.json` realizing $K_5 \vee C_8(1, 2)$ [7, Theorem 1] with universal $5$-clique $U_5 = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$. ## 3. Proofs of Algorithmic Soundness and Completeness

### 3.1 Spherical Triangulation Generation and Minimum-Degree Recognition (Stages 1–2)

Every simple planar graph $F$ on $n \ge 4$ vertices embeds as a spanning subgraph of a simple spherical triangulation $T$ on $n$ vertices (by choosing a maximal simple planar supergraph on $V(F)$: connectivity holds else joining components across a shared face adds an edge, and every face boundary is a 3-cycle since non-adjacent vertices on a face could be joined inside the face while a facial $K_4$ cannot bound a single connected region of $S^2 \setminus K_4$; see [6, Lemma 2.1]).

**Lemma 3.1 (Soundness of `verify_triangulation_rot`, Completeness of `generate_all_triangulations`, and Soundness/Completeness of `find_triangulation_rotation`).**
1. *For $n \ge 4$, `verify_triangulation_rot(rot)` succeeds if and only if `rot` specifies the oriented spherical rotation system of a simple 3-connected planar triangulation on $[n]$ ($3n - 6$ edges, $2n - 4$ triangular faces, unique up to orientation reversal by Whitney's Theorem [17]).*
2. *Starting from $K_4$, `generate_all_triangulations(12)` visits every isomorphism class of simple planar triangulations for $4 \le n \le 12$ without omission or duplication (`canonical_code`), producing the exact counts $1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 50, 233, 1249, 7595$ (total $9{,}150$, agreeing with [16]).*
3. *For $n \ge 4$, `find_triangulation_rotation(n, adj)` returns a valid spherical rotation system `rot` (verified by `verify_triangulation_rot`) if and only if `([n], adj)` is a simple planar triangulation.*

*Proof.* Items (1) and (2) are proved in full in [6, Lemmas 3.1–3.3]; we summarize the exact degree-tracking invariants so the present paper is self-contained. `verify_triangulation_rot` checks $\deg(u) \ge 3$, irreflexivity, symmetry, $\sum_u \deg(u) = 6n - 12$, connectivity, and that every dart successor walk has length $3$, giving $V - E + F = n - (3n - 6) + (2n - 4) = 2$; 3-connectivity follows because deleting $\le 1$ vertex from the link cycle of any cut-vertex leaves it connected. For any simple triangulation $T$ on $n \ge 5$ vertices, Euler's formula forces $\delta := \delta(T) \in \{3, 4, 5\}$; fixing $w$ with $\deg_T(w) = \delta$ and facial link cycle $C_w = (u_0, \dots, u_{\delta-1})$:
- if $\delta = 3$, deleting $w$ leaves a triangular face $\{u_0, u_1, u_2\}$ whose minimum-index vertex $u$ satisfies $u < x, y_0$;
- if $\delta = 4$, $K_5$-freeness guarantees a missing diagonal $u_0 u_2 \notin E(T)$; contracting $w u_0$ into $u_0$ yields a simple triangulation $T'$ with $\deg_{T'}(u_0) = \deg_T(u_0) \ge 4$, $\deg_{T'}(u_2) = \deg_T(u_2) \ge 4$, and $\deg_{T'}(v) \ge 4$ for all $v \notin \{u_1, u_3\}$ (`deg3_set <= {x, y1}`);
- if $\delta = 5$, $T[\{w, u_0, \dots, u_4\}]$ is planar on $6$ vertices ($\le 12$ edges) with $10$ spoke+cycle edges, so at most $2$ chords of $C_w$ lie in $E(T)$, touching $\le 4 < 5$ cycle vertices; picking $u_0$ incident to zero chords (`u0 u2, u0 u3 not in E(T)`) and contracting $w u_0$ into $u_0$ yields a simple triangulation $T'$ with $\deg_{T'}(u_0) = \deg_T(u_0) + 1 \ge 6$, $\deg_{T'}(v) \ge 4$ for all $v$, and $\deg_{T'}(v) \ge 5$ for all $v \notin \{u_1, u_4\}$.
Inverting these contractions via `split_vertex` proves completeness of `generate_all_triangulations`, while minimizing the deterministic BFS traversal code over minimum-degree root darts in both orientations (`canonical_code`) is a complete isomorphism invariant by 3-connectivity [17].

For item (3), `find_triangulation_rotation(n, adj)` first verifies $2|E| = 6n - 12$ and $\delta \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ (`n == 4` checks $3$-regularity, i.e., $K_4$). Selecting $u$ with $\deg(u) = \delta$, if `adj` is a simple planar triangulation then $N(u)$ induces a $\delta$-cycle $C_u$, and as just proved there exist $0$ ($\delta = 3$), $1$ ($\delta = 4$, $v_0 v_2 \notin E$), or $2$ ($\delta = 5$, $v_0 v_2, v_0 v_3 \notin E$) non-crossing chords reducing $T$ to a simple triangulation $T'$ on $n - 1$ vertices in which $\{v_0, v_1, v_2\}$ (for $\delta = 3$), $\{v_0, v_1, v_2\} \cup \{v_0, v_2, v_3\}$ (for $\delta = 4$), or $\{v_0, v_1, v_2\} \cup \{v_0, v_2, v_3\} \cup \{v_0, v_3, v_4\}$ (for $\delta = 5$) are coherently oriented facial triangles of $T'$. By Whitney's Theorem [17], the spherical embedding of $T'$ is unique up to global orientation reversal, so the facial adjacency test `_is_dir_face` on any valid rotation `r_sub` of $T'$ is invariant to which orientation `find_triangulation_rotation(n - 1, sub)` returned. Since `find_triangulation_rotation` tries all candidate cycles $C_u$ and valid chord sets, it always finds `rot` when `adj` is a triangulation, and every returned `rot` is built by valid face stellation/edge-split from $K_4$ (and cross-checked on all triangulations of $4 \le n \le 10$ in Stage 2). $\blacksquare$

### 3.2 Topological Kuratowski Verifier and Stage 3 Obstructions ($K_9$ and $K_7 \vee \overline{K}_3$)

**Lemma 3.2 (Soundness of `is_kuratowski_subdivision`).** *`is_kuratowski_subdivision(raw)` returns `True` if and only if `raw` is the edge list of a simple graph isomorphic to a topological subdivision of $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$.*

*Proof.* `is_kuratowski_subdivision` rejects self-loops, duplicate undirected edges, empty edge sets, and any vertex of degree $< 2$. Let $B = \{v : \deg(v) \ge 3\}$ be the branch-vertex set. The routine requires either $|B| = 5$ with $\deg(v) = 4$ for all $v \in B$, or $|B| = 6$ with $\deg(v) = 3$ for all $v \in B$. Starting from every unused dart $(u_0, u_1)$ with $u_0 \in B$, it walks greedily through degree-$2$ internal vertices until hitting a vertex $u_{\text{end}} \in B$, rejecting if $u_{\text{end}} = u_0$ or if two walks connect the same unordered pair $\{u_0, u_{\text{end}}\} \in \binom{B}{2}$, and afterwards verifies `used == edges` (excluding any isolated cycle of degree-$2$ vertices disjoint from $B$). Thus the edges partition into internally vertex-disjoint simple paths between distinct branch vertices, realizing the subdivision of a simple graph $K$ on $B$:
- when $|B| = 5$, checking `pairs == combinations(sorted(B), 2)` ($10$ pairs) certifies $K \cong K_5$;
- when $|B| = 6$, letting `left` be the neighbor set of `min(B)` in $K$ and `right = B - left`, checking `|left| == |right| == 3` and `pairs == left x right` ($9$ pairs) certifies $K \cong K_{3,3}$. $\blacksquare$

**Proposition 3.3 (Pure-Stdlib Certification of $\theta(K_9) = 3$, $K_7 \vee \overline{K}_3 \notin \mathcal{B}_2$, and $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)}) = 50$).**

3. *Consequently, $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)}) = 50$, attained by $Q_9 = K_6 \vee C_5$.*

*Proof.* If $K_9$ (resp. $K_7 \vee \overline{K}_3$) were biplanar, completing one planar layer to a simple triangulation $T_0$ on $9$ (resp. $10$) vertices would force $\overline{T_0}$ (resp. $(K_{10} - \binom{W}{2}) \setminus E(T_0)$ for some $3$-set $W$, canonical up to $\operatorname{Aut}(T_0)$) to be a subgraph of the second planar layer, contradicting Kuratowski's Theorem [18] and Lemma 3.2. Note that `find_kuratowski_subdivision_nv` enforces a fail-closed `max_route_nodes=50000` guard and passes every extracted edge list through `is_kuratowski_subdivision`.

For item (3), Stage 1 certifies $Q_9 = K_6 \vee C_5 \in \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)}$ with $50$ edges. Conversely, let $G \in \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)}$ have universal $6$-clique $U$ and $H_5 = G[V(G) \setminus U]$ ($|V(H_5)| = 5$), so $|E(G)| = 15 + 30 + |E(H_5)| = 45 + |E(H_5)|$. Let $A = \{a_1, a_2\}$ and $B = \{b_1, b_2, b_3\}$ be the parts of $K_{2,3}$. In $K_6 \vee K_{2,3}$, $a_1$ is adjacent to all of $U$ and all of $B$, so deleting $a_2$ leaves the induced 10-vertex subgraph $(U \cup \{a_1\}) \vee \overline{K}_3 \cong K_7 \vee \overline{K}_3$, which is non-biplanar by item (2). Hence $|E(H_5)| \le 5$ and $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)}) = 50$. $\blacksquare$

### 3.3 Exact Auslander–Parter Planarity Testing (`is_planar_exact_bm`)

Stage 5 tests planarity of sparse general graphs on $6 \le N \le 14$ vertices via `is_planar_exact_bm(n, adj)`. We prove its exactness in full.

**Proposition 3.4 (Soundness and Completeness of `is_planar_exact_bm`).** *For every simple graph $G$ on $n \ge 1$ vertices, `is_planar_exact_bm(n, adj)` terminates and returns `True` if and only if $G$ is planar.*

*Proof.* First, `reduce_deg2_bm` iteratively deletes isolated vertices ($d = 0$), pendant vertices ($d = 1$), and smooths degree-$2$ vertices ($d = 2$, replacing the path $v_1 - u - v_2$ with the single simple edge $v_1 v_2$ via bit-OR `(adj[v1] ^ lb) | (1 << v2)`). None of these operations creates or destroys a topological $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$ subdivision (smoothing $u$ either shortens a subdivision path if $v_1 v_2 \notin E$ or removes a parallel path alongside an existing edge $v_1 v_2 \in E$), so $G$ is planar if and only if the reduced simple graph $G'$ on $N'$ vertices (which satisfies $\delta(G') \ge 3$ whenever $N' > 0$) is planar.

Next consider the base cases on $G'$ (`lines 1606–1625`):
- If $N' \le 4$ or $|E(G')| \le 8$, $G'$ cannot contain a subdivision of $K_5$ ($10$ edges) or $K_{3,3}$ ($9$ edges), so $G'$ is planar (`True`).
- If $|E(G')| > 3N' - 6$ ($N' \ge 5$), $G'$ violates Euler's bound (`False`).
- If $N' = 5$ and $|E(G')| \le 3(5) - 6 = 9 < 10 = |E(K_5)|$, $G'$ has too few vertices for $K_{3,3}$ and too few edges for $K_5$, so $G'$ is planar (`True`).
- If $N' = 6$ (with $\delta(G') \ge 3$ and $|E(G')| \le 12$): any $K_{3,3}$ subdivision on $6$ vertices is an unsubdivided spanning $K_{3,3}$ (`lines 1615–1625`). Could a $K_{3,3}$-free graph $G'$ on $6$ vertices with $\delta(G') \ge 3$ contain a $K_5$ subdivision? Any $K_5$ subdivision on $6$ vertices is either an unsubdivided $K_5$ on $5$ vertices together with a sixth vertex $w$, or obtained from $K_5$ by subdividing a single edge once at a degree-$2$ vertex $w$. The second case contradicts $\delta(G') \ge 3$ ($\deg(w) = 2$). In the first case, $\deg_{G'}(w) \ge 3$ means $w$ has at least $3$ neighbors $x_1, x_2, x_3$ inside the $K_5$ on $V(G') \setminus \{w\} = \{x_1, x_2, x_3, y_1, y_2\}$; then $\{x_1, x_2, x_3\}$ and $\{w, y_1, y_2\}$ form a spanning $K_{3,3} \subseteq G'$. Hence at $N' = 6, \delta(G') \ge 3$, $G'$ is planar if and only if it contains no spanning $K_{3,3}$.

For $N' \ge 7$, `_tarjan_blocks_planar_bm` decomposes $G'$ into biconnected components (blocks) via Tarjan's DFS lowpoint algorithm [20]. A graph is planar if and only if every block is planar. Any block $B$ having a vertex of degree $< 3$ (created at cut-vertices) has strictly fewer edges than $G'$ (since $\delta(G') \ge 3$ forces at least two nontrivial blocks at any cut-vertex) and recurses to `is_planar_exact_bm`. Any block $B$ with $\delta(B) \ge 3$, $|V(B)| \ge 5$, and $9 \le |E(B)| \le 3|V(B)| - 6$ enters `auslander_parter_biconn_bm` [19]:
1. A DFS back-edge extracts a simple cycle $C = (c_0, \dots, c_{L-1})$ (`extract_segments_bm` decomposes $B \setminus E(C)$ into $C$-bridges/segments: single chords on $C$ and connected components of $V(B) \setminus V(C)$ with their attachment edges to $C$).
2. **Single-segment rerouting (`len(segments) == 1`, lines 1490–1528):** Since $\delta(B) \ge 3$ and every $c_i \in V(C)$ has degree $2$ in $C$, every vertex of $C$ must attach to the unique $C$-bridge $S_0$, so the sorted attachment index tuple of $S_0$ is `att == (0, 1, ..., L - 1)` (self-verified by `assert att[0] == 0 and att[1] == 1`). Because $L \ge 3$, $S_0$ has $\ge 3$ attachments and cannot be a single chord, so $V(S_0) \setminus V(C)$ is non-empty and connected. A BFS shortest path $P = (c_0, w_1, \dots, w_t, c_1)$ ($t \ge 1$) through $(V(S_0) \setminus V(C)) \cup \{c_0, c_1\}$ replaces edge $c_0 c_1$ of $C$ to form the simple cycle $C' = (c_0, w_1, \dots, w_t, c_1, \dots, c_{L-1})$. Relative to $C'$, the bypassed edge $c_0 c_1$ is one chord $C'$-bridge, while the attachment of $S_0$ at $c_2 \notin \{c_0, c_1\}$ lies in a second $C'$-bridge (`assert len(seg2) >= 2`).
3. **Multi-segment interlacing and recursion (`len(segments) >= 2`, `check_interlacing_and_recurse_bm`):** By the Auslander–Parter theorem [19], a biconnected graph $B$ with cycle $C$ and $C$-bridges $S_0, \dots, S_{s-1}$ ($s \ge 2$) is planar if and only if (a) the bridge-interlacing graph on $\{0, \dots, s - 1\}$ is bipartite (checked by 2-coloring BFS via `segments_interlace_bm`: two bridges interlace iff they share $\ge 3$ attachments on $C$ or have alternating attachments $a < b < c < d$ along $C$), and (b) for every $i$, $C \cup S_i$ is planar. Because $s \ge 2$, every subgraph $C \cup S_i$ omits at least one bridge $S_j$ ($j \neq i$), strictly decreasing $|E|$ and guaranteeing termination. $\blacksquare$

## 5. Exact 9-Colorability Across $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ ($3 \le r \le 8$) and the Critical-Slope Phase Transition

Despite $\mathcal{U}_n^{(4)}$ and $\mathcal{U}_n^{(3)}$ containing infinite families of $6n - 12$-edge biplanar triangulations for all $n \ge 13$ (Theorem 2.3), we now prove that **no** graph in $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ for $3 \le r \le 8$ and $n \ge r$ can reach chromatic number $10$.

Recall that a graph $B$ is **$k$-critical** if $\chi(B) = k$ and $\chi(B') < k$ for every proper subgraph $B' \subsetneq B$; every $k$-chromatic graph contains a $k$-critical subgraph, and every $k$-critical graph satisfies $\delta(B) \ge k - 1$. By Kostochka and Yancey [10, Theorem 3], every $k$-critical graph $B \not\cong K_k$ ($k \ge 4$) on $q = |V(B)|$ vertices satisfies
$$|E(B)| \ge \left\lceil \frac{(k + 1)(k - 2) q - k(k - 3)}{2(k - 1)} \right\rceil. \tag{1}$$

**Theorem 5.1 (9-Colorability of $\mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ for All $3 \le r \le 8$).** *Every biplanar graph $G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ with $3 \le r \le 8$ and $n \ge r$ satisfies $\chi(G) \le 9$ (with $\chi(G) \le 8$ for $r \in \{7, 8\}$, and $\max_{G \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}} \chi(G) = 9$ attained for each $r \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$).*

*Proof.* Write $G = K_r \vee H \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$ ($n = r + m$). By $\theta(K_9) = 3$ (Proposition 3.3(1)), $\omega(G) = r + \omega(H) \le 8$, so $\omega(H) \le 8 - r$. For $r = 8$, Theorem 2.1 gives $G = K_8$ ($\chi = 8$); for $r = 7$, $\omega(H) \le 1$ means $H = \overline{K}_m$ ($m \le 2$), so $\chi(K_7 \vee \overline{K}_m) \le 8$. For $r \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, Sulanke's $Q_9 = K_6 \vee C_5 \in \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(6)} \subseteq \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(5)} \subseteq \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(4)} \subseteq \mathcal{U}_{11}^{(3)}$ already attains $\chi = 9$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\chi(G) \ge 10$ for some $r \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$ and $G = K_r \vee H \in \mathcal{U}_n^{(r)}$. Since $\chi(K_r \vee H) = r + \chi(H)$, we have $\chi(H) \ge k := 10 - r \in \{4, 5, 6, 7\}$. Choose a $k$-critical subgraph $B \subseteq H$, and put $q := |V(B)| \ge k$. Because $\omega(B) \le \omega(H) \le 8 - r = k - 2 < k$, we have $B \not\cong K_k$, which forces $q \ge k + 1$ (indeed, $\delta(B) \ge k - 1$ on $q = k$ vertices would force $B = K_k$). Moreover, $K_r \vee B$ is a subgraph of $G$ on $r + q \ge 3$ vertices, so $K_r \vee B \in \mathcal{B}_2(r + q)$. Applying Euler's biplanar bound to $K_r \vee B$ yields the hereditary linear upper bound of slope $6 - r$:
$$\binom{r}{2} + r q + |E(B)| = |E(K_r \vee B)| \le 6(r + q) - 12 \implies |E(B)| \le (6 - r) q + \left(6r - 12 - \binom{r}{2}\right). \tag{2}$$
We rule out all four cases $r \in \{6, 5, 4, 3\}$:

- **Case $r = 6$ ($k = 4$, elementary):** Here $\omega(B) \le 2$ ($B$ is triangle-free), $\delta(B) \ge 3$, $q \ge 5$, and (2) gives $|E(B)| \le 9$. On the other hand, $\delta(B) \ge 3$ and Mantel's theorem give
  $$\frac{3}{2} q \le |E(B)| \le \min\!\left(9,\, \lfloor q^2 / 4 \rfloor\right).$$
  For $q = 5$, $\frac{3}{2}(5) = 7.5 > 6 = \lfloor 25/4 \rfloor$, impossible; for $q \ge 7$, $\frac{3}{2} q \ge 10.5 > 9$, impossible; and for $q = 6$, $9 \le |E(B)| \le \lfloor 36/4 \rfloor = 9$ forces equality in Mantel's theorem, i.e., $B \cong K_{3,3}$, which is bipartite ($\chi(K_{3,3}) = 2 < 4$), contradiction.

- **Case $r = 5$ ($k = 5$, elementary):** Here $\omega(B) \le 3$, $\delta(B) \ge 4$, $q \ge 6$, and (2) gives $|E(B)| \le q + 8$. Since $\delta(B) \ge 4$, we have $2q \le |E(B)| \le q + 8$, so $q \in \{6, 7, 8\}$:
  - If $q = 8$, $16 \le |E(B)| \le 16$ forces $B$ to be connected and $4$-regular with $B \not\cong K_5$, so $\chi(B) \le 4$ by Brooks' theorem, contradiction.
  - If $q = 6$, $\delta(B) \ge 4$ means $\Delta(\overline{B}) \le 1$ ($\overline{B}$ is a matching of size $\le 3$). Since $\alpha(\overline{B}) = \omega(B) \le 3$, $\overline{B}$ must be the perfect matching $3K_2$ (a matching of size $\le 2$ has $\alpha(\overline{B}) \ge 4$), so $B \cong K_{2,2,2}$ has $\chi(B) = 3 < 5$, contradiction.
  - If $q = 7$, $\delta(B) \ge 4$ means $\Delta(\overline{B}) \le 2$ ($\overline{B}$ is a disjoint union of cycles and $p \ge 0$ paths/isolated vertices) and $|E(B)| \le 15 \implies |E(\overline{B})| = 7 - p \ge \binom{7}{2} - 15 = 6$, forcing $p \in \{0, 1\}$. Up to isomorphism, there are exactly eight such $7$-vertex graphs $\overline{B}$: $C_7$ and $C_4 \sqcup C_3$ ($p = 0$), and $P_7$, $C_6 \sqcup P_1$, $C_5 \sqcup P_2$, $C_4 \sqcup P_3$, $C_3 \sqcup P_4$, and $2C_3 \sqcup P_1$ ($p = 1$). The first seven all have matching number $\nu(\overline{B}) = 3$, partitioning $V(B)$ into three independent pairs and one singleton ($\chi(B) \le 7 - 3 = 4 < 5$), while for $\overline{B} = 2C_3 \sqcup P_1$ we have $B \cong I_3 \vee I_3 \vee K_1$ ($\chi(B) = 3 < 5$). Thus no $5$-critical $B$ exists at $q = 7$.

- **Case $r = 4$ ($k = 6$, Kostochka–Yancey + Gallai via `earth_moon_116` v1, Lemma 3 [9]):** Here $B$ is $6$-critical with $B \not\cong K_6$, while (2) gives $|E(B)| \le 2q + 6$. By Lemma 3 of `earth_moon_116` v1 [9]—which compares (1) at $k = 6$, $|E(B)| \ge \lceil (14q - 9)/5 \rceil$ (forcing $14q - 9 \le 10q + 30 \implies 4q \le 39 \implies 7 \le q \le 9$), against degree-$5$ complement matching at $q = 7$ and Gallai's exact small-order bound $f_6(8) = 23 > 22$, $f_6(9) = 26 > 24$ [11; 10, Theorem 1]—every $6$-critical graph $B$ satisfying $|E(B)| \le 2|V(B)| + 6$ is isomorphic to $K_6$, contradicting $B \not\cong K_6$.

- **Case $r = 3$ ($k = 7$, Kostochka–Yancey + 19-Vertex Theorem [12]):** Here $B$ is $7$-critical with $B \not\cong K_7$. By (2), $|E(B)| \le 3q + 3$; by (1) at $k = 7$,
  $$|E(B)| \ge \left\lceil \frac{8 \cdot 5 \, q - 7 \cdot 4}{2 \cdot 6} \right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac{10q - 7}{3} \right\rceil.$$
  Comparing the two bounds gives $\frac{10q - 7}{3} \le 3q + 3 \implies 10q - 7 \le 9q + 9 \implies q \le 16$. Consequently, the biplanar subgraph $K_3 \vee B \subseteq G$ has chromatic number $\chi(K_3 \vee B) = 3 + 7 = 10$ and order
  $$|V(K_3 \vee B)| = 3 + q \le 19.$$
  This contradicts Theorem 1 of `earth_moon_3` v1 [12] (and Corollary 4 of `earth_moon_18` v2 [13]), which establishes that every biplanar graph on at most $19$ (resp. $20$) vertices is $9$-colorable. $\blacksquare$

### 6.2 Scope and Dependencies

1. **Unresolved general Earth–Moon problem:** Theorem 5.1 proves $\chi(G) \le 9$ when $G \in \mathcal{B}_2$ has at least $3$ universal vertices; it does **not** exclude $10$-chromatic biplanar graphs with $\le 2$ universal vertices, so $9 \le \chi(\mathcal{B}_2) \le 12$ remains open.
2. **External theorem dependencies by $r$:** All extremal edge-count and threshold theorems in Sections 2–4 (except the cited $n \in \{11, 12\}$ upper bounds $e_{\max}(\mathcal{B}_2, 11) = 51$ and $e_{\max}(\mathcal{U}_{12}^{(1)}) = 59$, proved and certified in pure stdlib in our preceding paper `earth_moon_124` v1 [6]) and the $9$-colorability proofs for $r \in \{5, 6, 7, 8\}$ in Theorem 5.1 are completely self-contained. In Theorem 5.1, the $r = 4$ case imports Kostochka–Yancey [10, Theorem 3] and Gallai [11] (via `earth_moon_116` v1, Lemma 3 [9]), while the $r = 3$ case additionally imports Theorem 1 of `earth_moon_3` v1 [12] (which in turn depends on Szeider's $\alpha \le 2$ theorem [14]).
