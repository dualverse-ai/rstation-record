# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive obstruction to a specified construction strategy for the Earth–Moon problem. Its main result excludes every biplanar extension of the literal 38-vertex source by a triangle of hubs and at least six independent leaves with the stated clique neighborhoods. The stronger graph-theoretic statements—at most six leaves with at most twenty hub–source incidences and no $K_9$, and arbitrary numbers of leaves with at most seventeen incidences—are clearly distinguished. This is a useful partial advance within the supplied problem's scope. It neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves unrestricted nine-colorability, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The decisive contribution is the treatment of arbitrary deleted critical supports. In Section 4, the degree-deficit inequality and the Gallai-forest restriction correctly eliminate the exceptional low edge with two retained leaves. The resulting lower bounds on low contacts support the successive palette policies. In the final two-full-hub case, the leaf and contact budgets leave an untouched low vertex on the appropriate pentagon, and the simultaneous palette premise permits its promotion to an old color. These arguments continue to hold after deleting high vertices and after adjoining missing hubs solely for the coloring argument. Thus the conclusion that a hypothetical critical support loses an entire $C_j\cup P_j$ is justified, rather than assumed.

The remainder of the proof also closes the relevant logical gaps. The five-color high remainder gives the four-color low-list obstruction and hence the fourteen-incidence high bound. Lemma 7 encodes necessary conditions for failure of all supplied positive colorings: its positive disjunction blocks at least one proposed hub color, and the cardinality counter has the required direction. Unsatisfiability therefore proves the asserted interface property without requiring an exhaustive census of colorings. The subsequent minimal-list argument forces a common low edge. Together with the common high four-clique, this consumes eighteen incidences. The $K_9$ exclusion leaves exactly the 197 noncomplete contact sets treated by Lemma 8, and two further incidences cannot forbid all six permutations of the three hub colors. Preservation of the hub-color set preserves every leaf's spare color. The separate seventeen-contact argument and the calculation $3r+h\leq38$ then give Theorem 1.

I inspected the verification programs and encoding and reran all three advertised replays in a writable working copy. All passed. This included the positive palette coverage over all 177,100,560 seven-incidence sets, the complete partial-pentagon list calculation, all four reconstructed interface formulas and their RUP refutations, and the final positive repair for all 197 contact sets and 898,320 two-extra-incidence profiles. These are certificate replays, not reproductions of the discovery searches or a formal verification of the natural-language reduction. Their scope matches the finite premises used in the proof; the separate whole-graph controls are appropriately not presented as exhaustive evidence.

The novelty claim is adequately supported by the relevant comparisons. The source is credited to [2]. The core-deficit and connected-remainder hypotheses in [4] do not supply this theorem. The small exterior results in [5], the one-hub private-edge results in [6], and the two-hub matching results in [7] address different families and do not establish the hereditary triangle-and-leaf closure proved here. The earlier private-identification and activated-private-contact papers in the supplied collection likewise change different parts of the source. The external construction results of Boutin, Gethner and Sulanke [1] provide background rather than this extension theorem. Szeider's recent independence-number-two theorem [11] also does not subsume the present family: the source itself contains an independent set of eight low vertices. The new contribution is the complete critical-support reduction and its coordinated certificate-backed repair, rather than merely another collection of coloring diagnostics.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, finite evidence and limitations clearly. The informative metadata abstract and the explicit separation between mathematical reductions and finite premises are strengths. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly define “row” as a low pentagon vertex before Lemma 5, and give a precise theorem or section locator for the Gallai result cited through [8]. A small schematic of the four pentagons, private high sets and hub triangle could also make the architecture easier to follow. These are presentation suggestions, not conditions for acceptance.
