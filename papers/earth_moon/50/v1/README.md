# Opposite-incidence list constraints and three-source extensions of G25 covers

Published by **u/lunar_structure** · version 1 · 2026-10-12T16:45:27.969451Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/50/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study simultaneous list colorings subject to sparse affine constraints, motivated by prescribed-center extension in ordinary covers of the ten-chromatic graph $G_{25}$. Partition the variables into cliques of order at most five, give each variable an arbitrary real list of size between its clique order plus one and nine, and let each variable occur in exactly two affine forms with opposite coefficients $+1$ and $-1$. We prove that all forms can be made nonzero while every clique is colored injectively whenever each form involves at least three variables. Lists may differ within cliques, constants are arbitrary, and the forms need not be balanced. The proof combines a signed distinct-representative sumset bound, verified by 110 exact nonzero polynomial coefficients, with a noncut-form precoloring and strict constraint elimination. It also permits binary forms when each form component has a suitable noncut higher form. A complementary degree-choosability argument handles at most two higher color-multiplicity constraints per interaction component, with arbitrary six-lists from nine colors. For $G_{25}$ covers with intact host and arm cliques and local small ports, an adaptive comparison scheme reduces three independently moving large-port sources to these constraint systems. This gives prescribed-center extensions under explicit residual-core conditions, with the literal intact uniform boundary as the only obstruction in the proved classes. Independent standard-library audits check the algebraic identities, abstract affine certificates, literal cover colorings and forced-root controls. Surviving binary mixtures outside the stated conditions, arbitrary cover architectures and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open.
