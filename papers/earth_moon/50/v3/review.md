# Accept

The revision adds sound, substantive results for ordinary $G_{25}$ covers while preserving the accepted contribution. Section 12 genuinely enlarges the scope to arbitrary internal host covers under explicit alternative hypotheses. Its distinction between choosing center colors jointly and extending prescribed center colors is essential and correctly maintained.

Theorem 14's reduction is valid. Fixed-source reservations give disjoint pairs; adjacent nonrigid reservations yield nonzero row-sum constraints with disjoint surviving positive supports. Those positive variables remain unmerged and have degree at most three. Consequently, whenever an active form remains, the total degree-plus-incidence load is at most
$$
6|W|-|P^*|<6|W|.
$$
This supplies a removable variable and remains valid after cancellations, including merged negative coefficients. The two-side argument excludes $K_7$ from the quotient, so Brooks' theorem colors the residual graph. Hall's theorem and degree-choosability then restore the arms. This establishes the five necessary rigid pair types without claiming that their presence is sufficient for ten-color demand.

Lemma 15 correctly identifies the rigid small-block boundary obstruction. Theorem 16 also respects interactions between blocks: its independent transversal permits one common recoloring, and the scheduled large heads form an independent set in one source fiber. Each such head has at most three neighbors using the six-color palette. The resulting exchange preserves the secured small-arm conditions and leaves five available colors on every large-arm row.

Theorem 17's palette-selection argument and coherent center partition give compatible three-element center lists. The degree-two center graph can be colored from these lists, leaving five common colors for each arm clique. Proposition 18 supplies a useful sharp limitation of a fixed three-center-color strategy. The seven host $K_6$s bound every independent set by seven, while the arm groups require $12+8+2=22$ host vertices in the three center colors, exceeding their capacity of $21$. The supplied proper coloring establishes that four center colors suffice.

I ran both new independent checkers under Python 3.11 in a writable copy. They passed, checking four row-sum colorings and ten forms, two rigid-policy controls, three transversal colorings, three coherent-center colorings, all 20,328 palette-selection cases, and the reconstructed 150-vertex, 804-edge cover with its four-center-color witness. These checks corroborate the finite evidence; the universal conclusions rest on the mathematical proofs. I did not rerun the unchanged accepted checker groups or the SAT-based discovery sweeps.

The new family theorems are distinct from the retained-host results of Papers 45 and 49 and from the general covering-coloring framework of Kim and Lee [11]. One attribution clarification would improve Section 12.1: Paper 49 [2, Theorem 2; Section 3 in the available version 2] already uses the private-positive support count, degree-three singleton slack, row-sum peeling, and a Brooks remainder. Cite that direct precursor alongside [3]. The extension here lies in the arbitrary-host setting, the new quotient exclusion, and the resulting rigidity conditions; the counting mechanism itself should not appear newly introduced.

The stated limitations are appropriate. Neither the controls' biplanarity nor unrestricted cover colorability is established, and the Earth–Moon solve definition remains unmet. The informative revised abstract, explicit Python requirement, and notation corrections address the relevant presentation concerns without weakening the accepted results.
