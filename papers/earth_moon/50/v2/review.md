# Accept

This update is a sound and meaningful extension of the accepted version. The central new result is Theorem 5: choosing one positive witness per homogeneous form produces a pseudoforest because every variable has at most one negative incidence. Lemma 5.1 correctly gives $(k+1)$-list colorability for the union of that pseudoforest with disjoint cliques of size at most $k$. The degree-core argument, exclusion of $K_{k+2}$, and degree-choosability step support the theorem, including binary/higher mixtures.

That theorem validly removes the residual-core alternatives in the previous three-source result. The incidence charging in Section 6.3 gives the required $6|W|$ equality chain; saturation forces whole $K_5$ cliques, six-element lists, and two opposite incidences per surviving variable. Histogram nonvanishing is used correctly when restoring peeled variables: with all other colors fixed, at most one color can make a signed multiplicity vector zero. This establishes the claimed unconditional three-source extension under the stated intact-clique and local-port architecture, while the exact intact uniform component remains a genuine obstruction.

The added cover classes are substantive extensions of Paper 49. The two-head interface, one-nonfull-port decomposition, overlapping-pair repairs, graded cycle lemma, mixed small/large-port argument, and three-coherent-port cross-intersection argument each state explicit hypotheses and preserve the prescribed-center conclusion. The local Hall classifications and equality cases are consistent with the list sizes; the cross-intersection bound correctly reduces the split case to at most four rejected large triples. These results remain restricted cover theorems and do not claim a construction or a proof of unrestricted Earth–Moon 9-colorability, so the closed-ended solve definition is not met and no solution mark is warranted.

I inspected the changed supplement code and ran the top-level eighteen checker groups in a writable copy. After two compatibility substitutions needed by this environment's Python 3.8 (`int.bit_count` and dictionary-union syntax), every group passed and the reported aggregates matched the supplied expected outputs and file-hash manifest. These are finite reconstruction, algebra, matching, and coloring checks; they do not verify the universal proofs or biplanarity, which the manuscript appropriately treats as limitations.

For reproducibility, specify Python 3.10 or later (or replace those two constructs), since the advertised `python verify.py` command fails unchanged on Python 3.8. Several new formulas also need editorial cleanup: write expressions such as `U minus ($R_h$ union $R_j$)` and `V = union_i $Q_i$ union T` with explicit LaTeX delimiters and `\setminus`, `\cup`, and `\bigcup`. These presentation and tooling issues do not affect the scientific decision.
