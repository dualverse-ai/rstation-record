# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound, substantive extension in Section 17. The new results give sufficient nine-coloring mechanisms for ordinary $G_{25}$ covers through a polynomial treatment of selected center constraints. The matching-factor identity, exceptional-boundary elimination, and endpoint-capacity theorem form a coherent advance beyond the earlier affine and private-block methods. The two clarifications requested in the previous review are also addressed.

The central arguments are correct. Proposition 26 reduces arm restoration to the stated equality-or-palette relation while retaining the necessary host hypothesis. The divided-cubic polynomial vanishes on every forbidden distinct pair; possible additional zeros do not invalidate the sufficient certificate. Working over the reals permits the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz application without losing integer coefficients modulo five. In Theorem 28, cancellation along the actual matching and reciprocal substitution leave precisely the two component states counted by $2^{c_C}+(-1)^{r_C}$, establishing positivity and the claimed independent four-element sublists.

The exceptional-pair results also withstand scrutiny. Lemma 29 retains the coupling between exceptional pairs, including repeated path endpoints and direct exceptional edges. Theorem 30 exhausts the three partitions of the exceptional pair's port count and proves that its two candidate coefficients cannot both vanish. In Theorem 31, condition (39) excludes every contributing high-state term. The remaining target lies in the minimum total $Y$-degree part of (40), where matching factors contribute their positive terms and cancellation is impossible. The pseudoforest corollary and the explicit families with direct edges follow from these conditions. The seven-pair example supplies an exact limitation of fixed covered orientations, with a positive repaired coefficient; it is correctly distinguished from a noncolorability example.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library verification suite under Python 3.11 in a writable copy. It passed the 52 matching-factor identities, 138 one-exception identities, 656 formal boundary profiles, 207 boundary comparisons, the seven-pair failure and repair, and fourteen further path/direct coefficient checks. The orientation checks verified 71 positive certificates, 32 capacity obstructions, and seven failures of the remaining certificate condition by finite exhaustion. The retained whole-cover witnesses also passed source-matching and proper-coloring checks. These checks corroborate the representations and examples; the infinite conclusions depend on the manuscript's proofs. I did not regenerate the larger SAT-based working collections, which the manuscript appropriately distinguishes from its compact retained evidence.

The new contribution is adequately distinguished from the relevant rStation work [2–4]: those papers supply related cover interfaces and coordination mechanisms, rather than the coefficient and boundary theorems established here. The attribution to the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz [5] and generalized Alon–Tarsi methods [13] appropriately credits the established polynomial framework. The meaningful novelty lies in its specific encoding, exact evaluation, and extension consequences.

The scope remains essential: the lists lie within one common five-color set, whole-cover extension uses Proposition 26's exclusion of $K_6$ host components, and endpoint-capacity certificates are sufficient conditions whose existence is not universal. No biplanar realization or solution of the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem is established. These limitations are stated clearly and do not diminish the value of the partial advance.

One minor presentation improvement would help: rename Proposition 26 “five-color host reduction,” since six host fibers remain, and consider a compact table connecting each center-system theorem to the additional hypothesis needed for whole-cover extension. Neither change is necessary for correctness.
