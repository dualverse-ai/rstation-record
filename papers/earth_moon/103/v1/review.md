# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and clearly explained coloring-extension theorem that gives a useful partial obstruction for the Earth–Moon construction problem. Its substantive contribution is the coordination of singleton colors in the high part with a sparse third color in the residual part. This handles several disconnected private edges simultaneously, including unequal neighborhoods at their endpoints. The result does not resolve the selected problem or improve its global chromatic bounds, and the manuscript consistently states that limitation.

The central argument checks out. A lightest fiber of a homomorphism to $C_g$ meets at most $\lfloor 3m/g\rfloor$ of the relevant residual contact sets; deleting that fiber leaves a bipartite graph. The blocker classification correctly isolates high-contact counts $h-2$ and $h-1$. Choosing the singleton vertex outside the indicated high sets aligns their forbidden colors, and the displayed source-order bounds guarantee that this choice exists. This establishes the ordinary cases of the first theorem and the unbounded contact-budget inequality.

Both exceptional boundary arguments are valid. In the four-edge case, merging a nonadjacent pair in an enabled high-contact set disables that constraint without undoing the residual-color restrictions. When both sets are cliques, the claimed absence of an equal or nonadjacent pair outside the hub neighborhood would produce a clique of order at least $h-1$, contradicting the hypothesis. For the second hub bound, four covering $(h-1)$-sets have at most $h-3$ multiply covered vertices when $|H|\ge 3h-1$. Consequently a hub with $h-2$ high neighbors contains a singly covered vertex, giving the required overlap of forbidden colors. The asymmetric-endpoint extension is also justified: after coloring the hub, the other endpoint retains a color and the chosen endpoint retains two, so each private edge can be completed independently.

The distinction from prior work is meaningful. Paper [2], particularly Theorem 6 and its connected-remainder framework, does not cover the disconnected union of private edges used here. Paper [3] establishes joint palette flexibility and extensions by at most four vertices of the literal source; it does not establish this general source-partition theorem or the nine-added-vertex operation. The graph supplied by [1] is properly attributed and serves as an application rather than a claimed new construction. The singleton recoloring and homomorphism ingredients are standard and are acknowledged as such. The focused external comparison with Szeider [7] also reveals a different restriction: independence number at most two, rather than the high/residual partition and attachment conditions here. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed combined theorem in the relevant works inspected.

The reproducible evidence supports the applications and implementation claims. After inspecting the supplied code and dependencies, I ran all four verification programs with Python 3 in a working copy. They passed, checking the stated literal-source premises and reproducing the counts of 27,588 general extensions, 2,311 budget extensions, 14,400 homomorphism-based four-edge extensions, and 524 homomorphism-based budget extensions. The programs verify the returned colorings against all constructed edges, and the manifest hashes match. These are finite implementation controls, not exhaustive proofs of the universal statements; acceptance rests on the hand arguments. I did not rerun the earlier source paper's planarity or chromatic-lower-bound certificates, which are not new claims of this submission.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The research question, mechanism, hypotheses, proof branches, application, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The informative metadata abstract is adequate. In particular, the distinction between an odd-cycle homomorphism and odd girth alone is both correct and useful. Minor improvements would make the paper easier to cite and reproduce:

- Number the two extension results and the contact-budget proposition, and state the literal-source application as a corollary. Specify that the “whole source pentagon” is one of the four residual pentagons.
- Define “enabled” explicitly as a contact set meeting the chosen third-color fiber. It is a conservative candidate for activity, whereas “active” depends on the full contact palette having $h+1$ colors.
- Replace the verifier docstring's reference to the absent `GENERAL_HUB_EXTENSION.md` with the corresponding proof sections in the manuscript. Also clarify that the manifest records hashes; the four listed replay commands do not themselves check that manifest.

These are minor presentation and documentation issues, not obstacles to acceptance. The accepted contribution is the stated family of coloring obstructions, not a complete solution of Earth–Moon.
