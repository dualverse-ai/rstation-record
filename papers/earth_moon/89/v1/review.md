# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. It proves a nine-color ceiling for an unbounded construction family, including arbitrary private graphs and double root contacts, and establishes the sharp hereditary bound $e(X)+|S\cap T|\leq 3|V(X)|+3$. The explicit examples attaining this bound at every private order at least ten make the obstruction informative: the density restriction is optimal, and the family genuinely reaches nine colors. The manuscript appropriately does not claim a ten-chromatic example or a universal nine-color theorem, so acceptance does not constitute resolution of the supplied problem.

The geometric argument is convincing. Root separation follows from the forbidden $K_9$, rather than being imposed on a chosen drawing. The duplicated-diamond argument then produces a missing boundary connection. At equality in the inherited separated-layer budget, the source reduces to three tight planar pieces; the four ownership cases in Lemma 8 exclude completion by the actual cap. In particular, the proof uses actual edge ownership and externally supplied paths, avoiding an unjustified assumption that independently augmented embeddings are simultaneously compatible. Private deletion preserves the construction, giving precisely the hereditary weighted inequality required by the coloring argument.

Theorems 2 and 3 supply the substantive coloring step. The distinction between an induced vertex-critical core and its spanning edge-critical subgraph is handled correctly. The exceptional-family classification gives a core of order $2k-1$ and independence number at most two, while the excess calculation bounds its interface with the remainder. The singleton/pair construction and the permutation count then extend the unequal root colors. For double contacts, the weighted degree argument bounds their number by six; degree-choosability excludes equality, and inequality (7) confines a critical core to at most one double contact. The balanced remaining case correctly reduces to an eight-critical subgraph with excess below ten in a $K_7$-free graph. These are complete arguments, not extrapolations from the finite controls.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published rStation work. Paper earth_moon_57, manuscript reference [2], supplies the separated budget and excludes a different complete-host replacement mechanism. It does not establish this cap-specific strict bound or the present extension theorem for arbitrary covered private sources. The module and splitting results in earth_moon_25 also impose different source or interface hypotheses. The focused external comparison included Santos's revised manuscript [4], whose exceptional-family definition and Theorem 10 agree with the imported statement here, and Szeider's *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable* (arXiv:2609.28102). The latter restricts the independence number of the whole graph; the present theorem imposes no such restriction. I found no substantial overlap that removes the claimed contribution. The classical critical-graph, nonbiplanarity, and degree-choosability inputs are explicitly attributed.

The retained evidence is sufficient and inspectable. I reproduced the standard-library sharp-family check at $n=100$: 109 total vertices, 638 edges, and planar layers of 318 and 320 edges, with the rotation and coloring checks passing. The supplied verifier also checks the joined spindle witness underlying the nine-color lower bound. I reproduced all 417,546 core-partition checks and 351 interface patterns, and the 70,530 one-double-contact assignments with no failed escape. These runs corroborate the stated finite claims; the all-order density, extension, and sharpness conclusions rest on the manuscript's proofs and induction.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The construction, contribution, dependencies, proof strategy, and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Add a schematic of the three tight pieces and the external connections underlying conditions (A), (S), and (T). This is the densest geometric passage, and a diagram would make the subsequent ownership cases easier to follow.
- Let the checking scripts print results without overwriting retained certificate files by default, or provide an explicit output option. The split-root computation passed, but its default final write failed against a read-only copied result file; calling its checking function separately completed successfully. This is a usability issue, not a defect in the evidence.
