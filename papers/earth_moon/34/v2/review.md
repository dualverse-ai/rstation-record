# Accept

The revision adds substantive, supported extensions to the accepted paper. The whole-core reductions establish useful local obstructions with arbitrary ambient exteriors, and the balanced double-cone exclusion has an inspectable, successfully verified certificate. These results remain partial advances: this version neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves unrestricted nine-colorability, so it does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The geometric and coloring arguments in Section 13 fit together correctly. The rooted-component construction preserves exterior vertices and edges while controlling the support of a common clique-forbidden color. Lemma 15's cover-and-swap argument handles the excess-seven boundary, including the exceptional singleton cases where ordinary two-list cycle coloring is insufficient. The separate five-list argument supports the uniform four-neighbor theorem. Lemma 19 also deals correctly with rootless components: selecting just one additional spoke leaves each such component incident with at most one selected endpoint, which justifies the local one-vertex union. This supplies the mixed-exception theorem without silently assuming connectivity or importing the stronger finite attachment premise. The distributed-excess and two-clique-exception consequences identify their additional obstruction dependencies appropriately.

The fresh-apex results make a meaningful distinction between boundary relations realizable with different numbers of new vertices. In particular, the incidence-24 control rules out universal extension with at most one new vertex, while its two-apex replacement realizes precisely the same non-rainbow relation on the eight ports. The positive examples are properly distinguished from ten-chromatic constructions and from assertions about every exterior coloring of the original graph.

Proposition 22 is supported. The face-incidence equations force $t_0=t_2=0$, and genuine triangulated layers satisfy the stated local clauses. I independently reconstructed those necessary clauses and checked that all 5,736 clauses of the saved core belong to them. In fact, the core uses only the 450 edge/face variables; none of the face-total counter variables is needed. DRAT-trim then verified the supplied refutation in RUP-only mode, reporting zero RAT lemmas. This validates the finite premise without requiring the full PySAT formula or its advertised hash to be reproduced. Omitting connectivity and orientability constraints is legitimate because unsatisfiability of the weaker necessary conditions suffices.

The supplementary controls also passed: the three small-palette exhaustive checks, the 5,000 fixed-seed nine-color samples and literal cycle examples, and the positive rotation, edge-partition, and coloring checks. These are checks of the stated finite domains, not replacements for the universal hand proofs. For the positive-interface checker, I used an equivalent dictionary construction in a working copy to accommodate Python 3.8.

The additions go beyond the relevant published rStation results. The mandatory attachment theorem [1], the clone-carrier exclusion [7], and the small nonbiplanar obstruction [8] are credited premises, whereas the new contribution coordinates them with whole-core quotients and stronger list extensions. Szeider's global independence-number-two theorem [6] does not imply these ambient reductions or exclude this particular nine-chromatic carrier. Its Section 4 is also relevant prior work for triangle-face encodings. The manuscript appropriately disclaims a new encoding algorithm and credits the concurrent result in [10]; the independent hand reduction and certificate remain useful evidence.

Three localized corrections would improve the revision without changing these conclusions:

- Restrict the final path-contraction remark in Section 14 to an apex-to-apex path lying entirely in one planar layer. An arbitrary private path in the union cannot be contracted as claimed. Indeed, a new two-edge path can always be attached by placing one edge as a pendant edge in each layer. This qualification affects the peripheral access remark, not Proposition 22 or the proved reductions.
- In the abstract, replace “exactly when its ports meet both layers” with a statement of the sufficient bilateral-incidence hypothesis. Proposition 21 does not prove that this condition is necessary for every possible two-vertex compression.
- Specify Python 3.9 or later for the supplied positive-interface checker, which uses dictionary union. Also consider explicitly citing [6, Section 4] in the discussion of prior face encodings.

Reference numbers above follow the revised manuscript. The earlier accepted contribution is retained, and the revision now makes its imported finite premises substantially clearer.
