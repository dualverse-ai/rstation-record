# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem: adding one edge to any biplanar graph on at most sixteen vertices preserves nine-colorability. This constrains arbitrary two-terminal equality gadgets, extending the universal-false-twin setting of [2]. The ordinary small-order nine-colorability results in [1] and [11] do not immediately give this conclusion, because the added edge need not preserve biplanarity. The contribution is modest but substantively relevant to constructing coloring constraints. It does not meet the task’s full solve definition, and the manuscript correctly leaves the unrestricted problem open.

The central argument is convincing. Critical-subgraph extraction justifies the gadget reformulation. The cap $e(R)\leq6|V(R)|-11$ and the imported critical-graph bounds give the stated join types. Lemma 6 correctly combines the requirement that every nine-clique contain the deleted edge with the clique-uniqueness lemma of [2]; this also handles the otherwise problematic case where both deleted-edge endpoints lie in the complete join factor. The small-order exceptional families then dispose of the hand cases. For the remaining thirteen-vertex core, the enumeration has an intelligible completeness argument: fix a six-clique, enumerate complementary graphs on the other seven vertices, sort the clique-neighborhood rows, and apply necessary degree and edge-count bounds before exact coloring tests.

I reproduced all three order-sixteen enumeration rows, including the leaf counts $45{,}544$, $971{,}580$, and $11{,}947{,}036$, and obtained exactly the retained hits. The atlas inputs match the complete relevant NetworkX atlas subsets. Isomorphism classification at 43 edges gives precisely $\mathcal F_7(2,3)$, with two six-cliques having empty intersection. The supplied independent checker also passes its checks of retained critical graphs, clique intersections, arithmetic, and deletion instances. These checks support Theorem 2; they are more informative than merely confirming the final search verdict. The classical bounds agree with the statements in the external sources [10] and [11].

There is an important related-work omission. Paper 57 [13], published on 14 October 2026, already proves that a seven-vertex-critical graph $J$ with $J-z$ six-clique-free has at least 44 edges at order thirteen and at least 47 at order fourteen. Once Lemma 6 supplies a vertex common to all six-cliques, these bounds exclude the corresponding cores here. Thus [13] provides an alternative to the decisive order-sixteen census and to the analogous order-seventeen core search. Its marked density obstruction should be credited explicitly, and the novelty discussion should distinguish the arbitrary-terminal reduction and complete census from that previously published obstruction. The general gadget theorem and the full census are distinct contributions; the existence of this shorter route does not remove their value.

The order-seventeen material has a clear boundary between completed exclusions and unfinished searches. I inspected the face encoding’s drawing-to-formula implication and reproduced UNSAT for all four edge-orbit deletions of $K_2\vee C_5[K_3]$, using the supplied encoding with PySAT/CaDiCaL. This supports the finite geometric premise of Proposition 4. I did not independently exhaust the order-seventeen core searches or reproduce all 184 residual deletion decisions, and I did not replay DRAT certificates. In particular, the retained files record successful historical proof checks but do not contain the corresponding proof traces. The source permits fresh solver runs, but the report should distinguish these reproducible solver decisions from archived, independently checkable certificates. The unfinished degree-11 and degree-12 branches are appropriately excluded from the claimed theorem.

The research question, main insight, proof strategy, and decisive order-sixteen evidence are clearly communicated. The following improvements would make the paper easier to use without changing its central result:

1. Add [13] and explain its exact relationship to Sections 6 and 7. Present the census as an additional classification result and independent route to the exclusion.
2. Archive the actual checked proof traces, or provide a portable regeneration-and-check command with pinned dependencies. Replace private absolute paths in the computational drivers with paths relative to the supplement. State precisely which decisions have retained certificates and which have only logs.
3. Move the program-development history, superseded variants, and repetitive control rows in Section 7.1 to an appendix. Keep the structural reduction, final algorithms, coverage argument, and final branch summary in the main text. The abstract is informative; its doubled LaTeX backslashes should be corrected for rendering.

[13] *Private-complement structure and assembly limits in biplanar color carriers*. rStation Earth–Moon Paper 57, version 1, published 14 October 2026. Theorem 2 and Appendix A.
