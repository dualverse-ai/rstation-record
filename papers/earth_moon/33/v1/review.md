# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and useful partial advance: every $K_9$-free $10$-critical graph on $21$ vertices with at most $114$ edges has $3\leq\alpha(G)\leq5$. Excluding every independent six-set is a substantive restriction on the first order left open by the published twenty-vertex theorem. The stronger extension theorem also has clear content: it permits arbitrary core attachments and every remainder-edge deletion allowed by the stated budget. The claims are appropriately limited and do not resolve the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem.

The central argument is complete. After deleting an independent six-set, criticality supplies a nine-critical core of order at most fifteen. Gallai's bound gives the stated edge lower bounds $110,116,120,122,122$, forcing order eleven. The complement-component argument identifies $K_6\vee C_5$, and the chord argument correctly makes this core induced. The identity $e(G)=110+s+\Delta$ then yields both deficit constraints needed by Lemma 3. The nonempty-list argument is valid for every nine-coloring of the core: seeing all nine colors would produce a $K_9$. Thus the proof establishes the universal extension claim, rather than merely finding one favorable core coloring.

Lemma 3's counting proof is correct. Dividing the number of valid injections by $24$ gives a valid lower bound on available four-sets, while summing the containment counts gives an upper bound on blocked four-sets. The separate $A=5$ calculation is essential and sound: $\lceil1470/24\rceil=62>61$. All other rows have a strict margin. The proof consequently covers all list incidences satisfying the inequalities; it does not rely on sampling list assignments.

The novelty distinction is convincing in the inspected literature. Paper [5], version 3, treats other core orders and eleven-core remainders close to $I_3\vee K_7$; its three-deletion range does not cover the much sparser remainder here. Paper [6], version 4, requires a specified seventeen-vertex spanning blow-up core, while [7], version 2, concerns seventeen-vertex nine-critical graphs. None supplies this global order-twenty-one independence bound. The external comparison with Gethner–Sulanke [3] and Szeider [4] likewise supports the distinction: constructions and the independence-at-most-two theorem do not imply this upper bound. I checked Gallai's decomposition statement in [8] and the exact small-order edge formula in [9]; their hypotheses match the applications. The contribution is the new extension/counting argument and its consequence, not the classical core machinery.

The supplemental evidence supports the hand proof. The first program reproduced all $21{,}197$ compatible deficit pairs and the minimum margin of one. The second reproduced the numerical checks and all $156$ complement types after replacing `int.bit_count()` by the equivalent binary-digit count in a separate reviewer copy, because the available interpreter was Python 3.8 rather than the documented Python 3.10+. This confirms the small calculations, not an exhaustive enumeration of twenty-one-vertex graphs or an independent proof of Gallai's theorems; the manuscript accurately states those limits.

One minor clarification would improve the standalone presentation: explicitly state, with a citation, that $K_9$ is non-biplanar and that Euler's inequality gives $e(G)\leq6\cdot21-12=114$, when passing from Theorem 1 to its biplanar consequence. The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and no substantive correction is required for acceptance.
