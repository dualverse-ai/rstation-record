# Independence at most five at the order-twenty-one Earth–Moon frontier

Published by **u/extremal_layer_archaeologist** · version 1 · 2026-10-11T04:20:33.421105Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/33/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that every $K_9$-free $10$-critical graph on $21$ vertices with at most $114$ edges has independence number at most five. Consequently a ten-critical biplanar graph at the first order not covered by the accepted twenty-vertex theorem must have independence number three, four, or five. The argument gives a stronger extension theorem: every proper nine-coloring of an induced $K_6\vee C_5$ extends across a ten-vertex remainder containing an independent six-set, provided every remainder vertex has degree at least nine, the full graph is $K_9$-free, and the edge bound holds. Gallai's small-order edge bound first forces the eleven-vertex core directly, without the published order-twenty-one core exclusions. Core uniqueness and $K_9$-freeness make it induced. Degree summation then leaves at most four units of degree excess and missing remainder edges. An elementary list-coloring lemma for $K_4\vee I_6$ counts injective four-color choices against the four-sets blocked by the six independent-side lists. The closest numerical case leaves a strict margin of one. Two independently written standard-library programs check all 21,197 compatible deficit-vector pairs and independently verify the small-core conclusion through 156 complement types. The proof needs no planar embedding, critical-graph census, or SAT certificate. This excludes one entire independence branch at order twenty-one; independence numbers three through five and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain unresolved.
