# Accept

This revision makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper: it strengthens the independence bound from $\alpha\leq5$ to $\alpha=3$ for ten-critical biplanar graphs on twenty-one vertices. The new arbitrary-palette list theorems and the completion of both surviving critical-core branches constitute a coherent advance. The independence-three case and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open, as the manuscript explicitly states.

The hand arguments supporting the stronger bound are sound. In Theorem 2, safe precoloring preserves nonempty lists and decreases the deletion-plus-excess budget by exactly $q_u$. The Small Pot reduction, common-clique-list argument, and five-list intersection classification close the terminal case, including the necessary equality argument at $a=3$. The core-edge calculation then leaves only orders eleven and twelve for an independent five-set, with valid nonempty-list arguments in both cases. Appendix A supplies the additional ingredient needed for an independent four-set: its minimal-bad-subset charging inequality, small bases, and adjacent equal-singleton classification justify Section 5. The recoloring of the eleven-core removes the sole possible exceptional pair without creating another.

The two new finite exclusions have appropriate mathematical coverage. For order seventeen, the imported census [2] and five-cycle theorem [9] leave exactly the stated augmentation and degree cases. Averaging over true-twin permutations of the actual augmented core gives the claimed neighborhood-orbit probabilities. The inequalities $4M_9<W$ and $3M_9+M_{10}<W$ are valid union bounds for one common coloring; independence of blocking events is unnecessary. The literal one-root linking witnesses meet the hypotheses of [14], and their use on retained subgraphs correctly handles additional ambient edges.

For order sixteen, choosing a minimum-order critical core justifies vertex-criticality of the induced core. The matching and simultaneous-hit arguments, together with the explicit exceptional-star recoloring, reduce the problem to the stated fifteen induced cores and 234 degree profiles. The Boolean encoding imposes necessary conditions on every hypothetical counterexample. In particular, the independent-five-set clauses are justified by the preceding hand theorem, simultaneous row and column ordering preserves an orbit representative, and the existential hit/selector encoding correctly expresses failure to color the outside star. A selected subset of proper core partitions suffices for a refutation; exhaustive partition selection is not required.

Targeted reproduction supported these conclusions:

- All three list-proof audit programs passed, including the $1{,}221{,}759$ terminal list multisets and the sharp-threshold examples.
- The boundary audit reproduced all $3{,}725$ labeled augmentations, $3{,}380{,}364$ matching-extension checks, and the three exceptional orbits with their displayed colorings.
- The order-seventeen checker reconstructed the complete $1{,}109$-case inventory. For `alpha2_nonC5_52_008_e77/degree10`, it checked $43{,}758$ literal neighborhoods and reproduced the exact normalized gap $1/24$, including the geometric exclusions.
- All 234 star formulas regenerated with their retained hashes, and all $524{,}480$ cardinality-control assignments passed. A fresh proof for `core00_F11_profile19` passed the separately compiled RUP checker and matched the retained proof hash. This also succeeded with the available Python 3.11/PySAT 1.8.dev16 environment, although the documented reference environment is newer.

These checks do not constitute a fresh replay of all 1,109 weighted inequalities or all 234 RUP refutations. The remaining results are supported by the inspected certificate data, reported completed checks, and public fixed-input regeneration pipeline. Census completeness remains an explicitly imported premise; checking the supplied census files does not reestablish it. Those files match the cited published supplement. The evidence and reproduction instructions make the computational claims inspectable without private artifacts or renewed exploratory search.

The revision is adequately distinguished from its predecessors. The triple-list framework [5,6], weighted extension method [16], census [2,15], and geometric lemma [14] receive appropriate attribution. The published five-cycle extension [9] covers a restricted core family, whereas this revision closes arbitrary surviving cores and derives the global independence restriction. Szeider’s academic result [8] concerns independence number at most two and does not imply the new upper bound. I also checked the stated Small Pot Lemma against [13, Section 3.1]; its hypotheses match the applications here. No substantial duplication of the new contribution emerged from these comparisons.

One minor presentation improvement would help: in Section 7.1, display the Kostochka–Yancey inequality used for the seven-critical fourteen-vertex factor and its rounding to forty-five edges. This is distinct from the small-order Gallai formula stated earlier. The citation is appropriate, and this omission does not undermine the reduction. The abstract is informative, the scope is clear, and no substantive correction is required for acceptance.
