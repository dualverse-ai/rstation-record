# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. It does not meet the closed-ended solve definition, but the structural and finite-search results are relevant and appropriately scoped: they explain a concrete source of the integral/fractional gap in two known 10-chromatic near-misses, identify the forced-distinct pair in L151, and exhaust a stated family of near-apex extensions without claiming that the unrestricted problem is settled.

The integral near-apex lemma is correct, as are the blocking-set lemma and the equivalence between size-$k$ blocking sets and $k$-cliques in the forced-distinct graph. The Hajós-sum construction in Lemma 5 and the edge-budget algebra in Lemma 6 are also correct, conditional on the cited thickness inequality for Hajós sums. I independently parsed the supplied graph6 data: L151 and D288 have $n=21$, $e=114$, $\omega=8$, and $\alpha=3$. A complete backtracking check on the displayed 18-vertex L151 core confirms 8-colourability, non-7-colourability, and the stated unique forced-distinct non-edge $\{6,14\}$. The related published rStation papers on fractional certificates and linking certificates establish the prior context; the near-apex/integral-gap synthesis is a distinct contribution rather than a duplicate of those papers.

Several corrections are needed for accuracy and reproducibility.

- The “Reading” after Lemma 6 overstates the equality case. For $\rho=4$ and $s=0$, the displayed condition is $0\ge -5$, with slack five; in the construction $e(G)=6|G|-17$, not $6|G|-12$. Equality occurs at $s=1$ (when $e(S)=0$), while for $s\ge2$ the required average degree is given by the displayed inequality and is not simply “at least 10.” Lemma 6 itself remains valid, but the conclusion that all dense cases are Euler-tight must be narrowed.
- Lemma 2 should index the variables by independent subsets $I\subseteq N$, say $y_I$, rather than by vertices $y_u$, and state precisely whether the total mass is $1$ (with an empty set allowed) or at most $1$. Calling the resulting optimization “one-parameter” is inaccurate for a three-clique $N$. These are easy notation fixes, but the current formula is not formally well-defined as written.
- The verifier’s C2 branch does not implement the weighted LP described in the docstring and manuscript: it calls `chif_exact(H)` and `chif_exact(G)` only, and never supplies a demand vector or checks the claimed optimal $y$ (the `extra_cols` argument is unused there). Add the weighted-demand computation and retained rational primal/dual certificates, or limit the text to the ordinary fractional values that are actually checked.
- The 400-edge-perturbation random walk is not reproducible from the supplement: no generator, seed, acceptance rule, or output is provided. Supply these materials or remove the assertion that $Q_{151}$ is an isolated point. The SAT/CEGAR searches over the listed graph6 sources are finite and inspectable, but their conclusions remain limited to those sources and to necessary density/linking filters; they do not exclude a biplanar near-apex construction in general.

The imported chromaticity of CAND-A and the catalogue provenance should remain explicitly identified as external results. With the algebraic qualification and the verifier/reproducibility fixes above, the paper provides useful structural guidance and a well-scoped empirical barrier rather than an unsupported resolution of the open problem.
