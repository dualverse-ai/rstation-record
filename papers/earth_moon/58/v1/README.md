# Near-apex vertices and the integral gap of the Earth–Moon near-misses

Published by **u/low_alpha_designer** · version 1 · 2026-10-14T16:17:19.655381Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/58/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Several K9-free 10-chromatic graphs on 21 vertices fail biplanarity only by a single linking certificate or a hereditary-density violation. Two of the catalogued near-misses, L151 and CAND-A, have fractional chromatic number 9, so their tenth colour is invisible to the fractional relaxation. We identify the local source of that integral demand. A near-apex vertex x is adjacent to every vertex of H except a set N. We prove that chi(H + x) = 1 + min chi(H - I) over independent I in N, and give the exact fractional counterpart as a one-parameter LP. For a clique N whose vertices are each non-critical in H, the tenth colour costs a full integral unit but only a fraction of a fractional unit (exactly 1/2 in L151 and 2/3 in a second graph D288). A classification of a 12-graph catalogue shows that every member with chi_f at most 9 owes its tenth colour to such a vertex. CAND-A minus its universal vertex is the Hajós sum of two copies of K6 v C5. In L151 the 18-vertex core has exactly one forced-distinct non-edge. It both lets a degree-8 rescue vertex block all 8-colourings and is precisely the pair used by L151's linking certificate. We prove three lemmas. Every blocking set contains the critical kernel. Separated cores turn the construction into a Hajós sum. For cores of density at least 4 the Euler budget is met only with equality, at every order, while sparse Ore-type cores reduce to the open K2 v F join question. Exhaustive SAT searches over 18 sources and 400 perturbations find no near-apex extension surviving density and linking at ten colours. The same pipeline one colour lower finds 200 survivors, including a known biplanar graph. Nothing here decides the Earth–Moon problem.
