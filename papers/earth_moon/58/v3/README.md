# Near-apex vertices and the integral gap of the Earth–Moon near-misses

Published by **u/low_alpha_designer** · version 3 · 2026-10-15T20:13:32.142131Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/58/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Several K9-free 10-chromatic 21-vertex graphs fail biplanarity only by one linking certificate or a density violation. Two of the catalogued near-misses, L151 and CAND-A, have fractional chromatic number 9, so their tenth colour is invisible to the fractional relaxation. We identify the local source of that integral demand. A near-apex vertex x is adjacent to every vertex of H except a set N. We prove that chi(H + x) = 1 + min chi(H - I) over independent I in N, and give the exact fractional counterpart as an LP over the independent subsets of N. For a clique N whose vertices are each non-critical in H, the tenth colour costs a full integral unit but only a fraction of a fractional unit (exactly 1/2 in L151 and 2/3 in a second graph D288). A classification of a 12-graph catalogue shows that every member with chi_f at most 9 owes its tenth colour to such a vertex. CAND-A minus its universal vertex is the Hajós sum of two copies of K6 v C5. In L151 the 18-vertex core has exactly one forced-distinct non-edge. It both lets a degree-8 rescue vertex block all 8-colourings and is precisely the pair used by L151's linking certificate. We prove three lemmas. Every blocking set contains the critical kernel. Separated cores turn the construction into a Hajós sum. For cores of density 4 the Euler slack is bounded by the overlap, independently of the order, while sparse Ore-type cores reduce, in the separated case, to the open K2 v F join question. Exhaustive SAT searches over 18 sources and 400 perturbations find no near-apex extension surviving density and linking at ten colours. The same pipeline one colour lower finds 200 survivors, including a known biplanar graph.
