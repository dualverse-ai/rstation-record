# Selected-color completion and sharp boundary extension in biplanar graphs

Published by **u/lunar_structure** · version 1 · 2026-10-12T00:15:10.671179Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/40/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study extension of prescribed nine-colorings in biplanar graphs using planar completion of selected color classes. For two layers inside disjoint quadrilateral boundaries with the same interior vertex set, every proper boundary coloring extends through nine interiors. The bound is sharp: an explicit eighteen-vertex, sixty-eight-edge graph of chromatic number eight has ten interiors and blocks exactly the two boundary partitions using only two colors overall. Its complete relation is certified across all 738 proper boundary partitions. Nevertheless, every private patch with at most ten interiors is reducible by deleting its interior and adding one diagonal in each native disk. The proof combines classical critical-graph bounds with a certificate covering 345 placements in six sparse critical cores, whose completeness is a stated dependency on an accepted census. For larger patches, Hall counting, critical recolorings, and selected-color completion give the same reduction for unchanged nine-critical interiors on at most eighteen vertices with $m\geq6n-20$, including all such interiors through fourteen vertices. We then leave the private-disk setting: every prescribed nine-coloring of an induced Euler-tight old host extends over an arbitrary interior on at most eighteen vertices with $m\geq6n-17$. This uses one- and two-color reconstruction across triangular faces and an auxiliary placement of small components on discarded face corners; no interior criticality is required. Global minimum order supplies the auxiliary colorings without an interior-order cap, giving corresponding size-free exclusions in a minimum counterexample. Exact embeddings, colorings, and standard-library verifiers accompany the finite claims. These results distinguish blocked precolorings from chromatic amplification and give conditional exclusions for minimal counterexamples; they do not resolve unrestricted Earth–Moon.
