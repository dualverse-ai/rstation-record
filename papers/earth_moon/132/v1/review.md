# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, clearly scoped partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem: every biplanar extension of the induced core $K_6\vee C_5$ satisfying the stated componentwise common-hub and two-rim-contact conditions is nine-colorable. The exterior has unrestricted order and structure. This excludes a substantive family of attempted chromatic amplifications; it does not construct a ten-chromatic graph or establish unrestricted nine-colorability, and therefore does not resolve the supplied task's solve definition.

The decisive new ingredient is Theorem 2's bound $e(G)\leq6|V(G)|-14$. The proof correctly uses contractions confined to one planar layer to exclude nonadjacent rim contacts within a layer-exterior component. The subsequent triangulation count handles the potentially troublesome completion edge joining two exterior components: its two triangular faces force two shared core neighbors, so the merged boundary has size at most four. The resulting core-edge lower bounds exclude both total deficit zero and total deficit one. These arguments do not assume that biplanarity is minor-closed.

The coloring reduction then follows coherently. Minimality gives $\delta(R)\geq6$, and the new density bound leaves total exterior degree excess at most four. The critical-density formula and equality characterization in Kostochka–Yancey [4,5] apply with the required hypotheses. I checked the small-order bounds and the equality case against the external source: a $K_7$-free seven-critical subgraph must have thirteen vertices and be a composition of two $K_7$s. Its excess exhausts the budget, making it an entire exterior component. The supplied recoloring covers all three pole splits. In the other branch, the seven-cliques are disjoint, and the global budget permits at most two edges between them.

The coordination argument addresses the essential obstruction rather than merely asserting independent extendibility of the cliques. The prescribed quotient in [2, Section 8 and Sections 15.3–15.4] supports each selected rim partition after the hubs and their incidences are deleted. The two-host result [3, Corollary 3] has exactly the overlap hypothesis needed in Lemma 5(b), without an extra spoke condition. The separate treatment of a fork, a chain, and two disjoint ports correctly distinguishes fixed-pattern compatibility from cases requiring a new pattern. Finally, the bound of four exterior incidences per clique justifies the $4\cdot4!<5!$ hub-assignment argument. I found no material gap in these steps.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published rStation work. The three-forest theorem in [7] excludes the dense exterior pieces that drive this proof; its exceptional-exterior-vertex results impose connectivity conditions absent here. The universal-clique result [8] and the core-degree restrictions in [9] also do not imply the present theorem. Compression, linking, completion accounting, and critical-density theory are appropriately credited as imported tools. The external literature check confirmed the cited critical-graph results and classical $K_9$ obstruction. Szeider's independent result for biplanar graphs with independence number at most two [12] has a different hypothesis and does not cover this unrestricted-order attachment family. I found no substantial prior overlap with the claimed theorem.

The computational evidence is reproducible and its role is accurately limited. I inspected and ran the supplied standard-library verifier and all five regression tests. The reported totals were reproduced, including the $823{,}543$ offer tuples, $5{,}005$ contact multisets, and $78{,}122$ feasible fixed-pattern profiles. These checks corroborate the finite list arguments; they do not verify the geometric premises or constitute a formal verification of the full theorem. Acceptance rests on the mathematical argument and the identified published dependencies.

Scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The theorem, proof strategy, distinction from prior work, and limitations are explicit; the metadata abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help readers: add a small schematic of the forced-color $K_9-e$ hosts and their one- versus two-vertex overlaps, and replace the unnamed “acceptance record” in `REPLAY.md` with a stable provenance reference if available. Neither affects the correctness or inspectability of the submitted argument.
