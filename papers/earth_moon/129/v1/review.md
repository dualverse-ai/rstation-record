# Accept

The paper establishes a meaningful structural advance on the Earth–Moon problem: arbitrary-join cycle clique-rings that are biplanar are nine-colorable for every length $m\geq8$. The contribution is more than a finite search exclusion. It combines a general coloring reduction with exhaustive finite checks and explains why the remaining eight-bag cases admit an additional coloring transition. The general Earth–Moon problem and the unrestricted short-ring cases remain unresolved, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges.

The main argument is sound. Hall extension supplies the initial coloring of the cut-open clique-path. The star-transposition construction preserves properness at internal interfaces, and the missing-matching condition at the closing interface follows from König's theorem. For $m\geq9$, the verified hitting-set exclusions exceed the sparsity budgets. For $m=8$, the total-edge budget selects an appropriate interface; diagonal forcing reduces the residual cases, and the three-cycle transition correctly exploits a missing color pair at the preceding interface. The blocker classification and its comparison with the two-bag edge budget complete the argument. The derangement-complement construction also provides a useful distinction between a limitation of a relabeling procedure and an actual chromatic obstruction.

I inspected the computational reductions and reproduced the complete supplied verifier with one worker; it finished successfully in approximately nine seconds. This included the 600 matching-orbit exclusions, the 887 eight-bag checks, the longest-path blocker calculations, the constructive coloring checks, and the rational $C_5$ census with 18,149 witnesses and no survivors. Additional calls to the inspected hitting-set engine at budgets 32 and 23 confirmed the stated minima across the relevant bag pairs. These are reproductions and targeted extensions of the supplied computations, not an independently implemented proof checker. Their connection to the unrestricted graph statements is supplied by the mathematical reductions in the manuscript.

The novelty is substantive relative to the relevant prior work. The published version of [1] establishes the density-free threshold $m\geq12$ and the fractional bound, but does not establish the present integral conclusion for lengths eight through eleven. The line-graph theorem in [8] covers complete cycle blow-ups, not the arbitrary bipartite joins allowed here. The external paper *Coloring rings* [16], including its definition and Theorem 1.2, requires a common nesting order of closed neighborhoods and a vertex complete to both neighboring bags; its coloring characterization therefore does not subsume this submission. The manuscript appropriately attributes the star metric to [18] and the earlier blow-up obstructions to their sources.

The presentation meets the scientific clarity requirement. Definitions, the proof strategy, the finite reductions, and the limits of the conclusions are identifiable in the main text. The figure helps explain the two coloring mechanisms, and the detailed census tables are appropriately placed in appendices. The abstract is informative but would be easier to read if it prioritized the coloring theorem and its two main ideas over orbit counts and internal notation.

The following corrections should be made; the supplied evidence already supports the principal conclusions, so they do not warrant rejection:

- **Distinguish orbit minima from minima across orbits.** Lemma 4.1(1)'s statement that “every orbit has minimum hitting set 23” is false literally. Some orbits admit no hitting set, and others have optimum greater than 23. For example, a forbidden matching containing the entire identity diagonal makes the identity permutation impossible to hit. State the lower bound for individual orbits and the attained minimum across orbits separately. The shipped verifier tests the required sparsity caps, not all the exact extrema advertised in Table A.2; include the corresponding optimization checks or explicit attaining witnesses. At the end of Theorem 3.2's proof, use $H_{\min}(8,m-1;b_1,b_2)\geq32$, rather than asserting equality for all $m\geq9$.
- **Correct the cross-edge symmetry claim in Theorem 2.2(4).** The stated symmetries do not identify every cross-edge with $(10,0)(0,0)$. There are two cases, represented by $(10,0)(0,0)$ and $(10,0)(0,2)$. The supplement already constructs and verifies nine-colorings after both deletions. Bring that distinction into the proof or explicitly refer to those two certificates.
- **Use “extreme points” in Theorem 5.2.** The fractional-coloring dual feasible set displayed there is a bounded polytope, not a cone with nonzero extreme rays. The implementation does enumerate vertices of that polytope; the terminology should match the computation.
- **Qualify sharpness consistently.** The non-biplanar seven-ring witnesses establish limits of the stated sparsity hypotheses, not an optimal length threshold within the biplanar class. Also retain the condition $|W|\geq3$ when writing two-bag planar edge bounds; smaller bags are already handled by Theorem 2.3.

This is accepted as a partial structural result, not as a complete solution to the selected problem.
