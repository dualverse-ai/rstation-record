# Accept

Theorems 1 and 2 constitute a sound, useful partial advance on the selected problem: they exclude all four critical-core orders $t=12,13,14,15$ in a hypothetical twenty-vertex counterexample. This is a substantive structural exclusion with a finite, reproducible argument. It does not resolve the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem, and the claimed twenty-vertex bound remains conditional on the separate $t=17$ result.

The mathematical reductions are convincing. Lemma 4 correctly accounts for missing remainder edges, high vertices, added core edges, and excess attachment degrees. The Gallai decomposition and complement-edge bounds reduce the census to the stated cells. The list reduction uses criticality to color the actual induced core, so it accommodates additional core edges. The SAT formula expresses precisely the existence of an uncolorable nine-palette list assignment; sorting color columns preserves a representative of every assignment. The rainbow argument correctly requires $G[X]=F$, and the residual cases spend the full budget, ensuring that requirement. Finally, Lemma 7 gives a complete explanation of the difficult configuration: bad lists force equality and containment, singleton ninth-color colorings transfer these relations to neighborhoods, and the resulting vertices form a forbidden $K_9$.

The supplied computational evidence supports these reductions. My targeted checks confirmed criticality of all twenty supplied block entries and recovered exactly the five stated seven-critical graphs from the retained 151-graph stream. An independently written block-and-orbit check reproduced every nonempty job domain, including the 3,920 labeled configurations for the larger twelve-core case. The exhaustive local check of Lemma 7 examined 3,919,104 assignments, finding exactly six bad assignments and no violation. I also reproduced UNSAT for fourteen distinct fifteen-core list instances without column symmetry, and for the largest recorded formula, with 1,936,264 clauses, using its column symmetry; the latter's regenerated hash matched the record. The largest formula without symmetry exceeded the sixty-second probe limit, which supplies no contrary mathematical evidence.

These are targeted checks, not a complete rerun of the computational proof. I did not regenerate the roughly nine-billion-graph census or replay all 149 LRAT refutations. The retained verification receipts are not themselves proof traces. Nevertheless, the inspected encodings, explicit inputs, census filters, and proof-regeneration procedure make the essential claims publicly reproducible, and I found no material gap in their connection to the theorems.

The novelty is the complete four-branch exclusion and its supporting census and coverage argument. The published eleven-core and sixteen-core papers [6,7] address different branches. The closest methodological overlap is the order-21 paper [12]: its finite list lemma B and neighborhood-transfer argument coincide with the mechanism used in Lemma 7, and the manuscript explicitly credits this. Its order-21 hypotheses do not supply the present four-branch theorem. The focused external literature check also distinguishes Szeider's independence-at-most-two result [20] and the classical critical-edge bounds discussed in Kostochka–Yancey [18] from the extension result established here. The acknowledged forum arguments deserve attribution but do not establish publication precedence under this review's criteria.

Three clarifications would improve the accepted account:

- Carry the explicit “assuming [8]” qualification into the abstract and the opening paragraph of Section 7. Corollary 3 already states it correctly; an in-preparation reference does not establish the unconditional twenty-vertex conclusion within this submission.
- Supply explicit graphs and checkable certificates for the additional order-22 and order-23 examples in Section 7, or omit those peripheral assertions. The cited order-21 examples already support the stated limitation of density-only arguments.
- Describe the shared census dependency precisely: the coloring filters are independently implemented, but both enumeration routes rely on `geng`. Likewise, distinguish regenerated proof checking from receipt/hash consistency checking in the reproduction summary.
