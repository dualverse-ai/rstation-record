# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated local advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The principal contribution is the edge-minimal obstruction $X_{19}=3K_5\vee(K_2\cup2K_1)$, together with restrictions on attachments to its four-root, root-edge-deleted subgraph. The two-root outerthickness argument and the smaller obstruction $G_{68}$ give the paper additional structural content. These results exclude concrete construction patterns that survive elementary density tests. They satisfy the task's explicit allowance for useful obstructions, while neither constructing a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proving universal nine-colorability.

The natural-language arguments are correct. Lemma 6 contracts a path only within its own planar layer, with all internal vertices outside the retained host; it does not assume that biplanarity is minor-closed. The pigeonhole and exterior-path arguments establish all three parts of Corollary 2. In Proposition 3, an edge joining opposite spoke orientations would create a forbidden exterior path in either possible layer. Connectedness therefore forces a uniform orientation, and the two universal apices make the respective restrictions to $H$ outerplanar. Corollary 5 follows from the same exterior-path principle and the published $O_{15}$ obstruction. The bipartite edge count explaining the failure of the proposed pattern at $r=5$ is also valid.

The computational claims have substantial inspectable support. I independently checked the three certificate graphs, absence of extra prescribed-layer or symmetry-invariance restrictions, every listed automorphism, the cardinality bounds, and all 219,586 stored Kuratowski subdivisions using a separate suppression-based recognizer. The subdivision counts agree with Section 2. I inspected the CNF construction, including its lexicographic encoding: choosing a maximum representative under automorphisms and layer interchange justifies imposing all the comparisons simultaneously. Rebuilding and solving each formula with CaDiCaL 1.5.3 through PySAT returned UNSAT, with the stated variable and clause counts. All 23 positive layer certificates passed NetworkX planarity, disjointness and graph-isomorphism checks, and every single-edge deletion of each claimed minimal obstruction was covered. Thus the minimality claims have positive witnesses, rather than relying on unsuccessful searches.

The limits of this reproduction should be explicit: I did not regenerate and check the standalone CaDiCaL 2.2.1 DRAT proofs, or rerun the Boyer–Myrvold backend. The SAT replays are corroboration, not an independently checked Boolean proof trace. Nevertheless, the supplement supplies the exact specifications and a concrete proof-generation-and-checking route, and the inspected mathematical encoding and independently verified necessary constraints support the conclusions. The omission of the large proof files is adequately disclosed.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. Paper 117 establishes $O_{15}$ and supplies the computational method; neither is new here. Paper 68 supplies the exterior-path lemma and a different two-shared-vertex obstruction requiring a joining edge. Paper 48's pure-apex observation explains the outerplanarity step, but does not itself force the uniform orientation obtained in Proposition 3. The root-signature bounds of Paper 116 likewise do not state the four-root obstruction proved here. The focused external comparison included Biniaz's classical $K_9$ result [3] and Yang–Kong's amalgamation bounds [119]; the latter give general inequalities for two-vertex amalgamations, not these exact exclusions or minimality results. I found no substantial overlap that removes the new contribution. The organizing comparison across root counts, its sharp positive controls, and its explicit failure at five roots make this more than an isolated parameter substitution in Paper 117.

Clarity is independently satisfactory. The main results and their connection to constructions appear before the certificate details; Section 3 explains the proof mechanisms; the tables distinguish certified negative results from explicit positive decompositions. Exploratory searches are appropriately separated from the evidence supporting the theorems. The scope and unresolved questions are clear.

Minor improvements would make the paper easier to cite and reproduce:

- Correct the version of [117]: the supplied publication index contains version 1, whose Theorem 1 already provides the required result, whereas this manuscript cites version 2. Replace the grouped placeholder bibliography entry for Papers 25, 34, 44, 48, 64 and 116 with individual, fully identified numbered references. Add the relevant amalgamation literature [119].
- In Section 2, replace “any biplanar decomposition would satisfy every clause” with “some representative of every biplanar-decomposition orbit would satisfy the formula.” The existing symmetry explanation is correct, but the final sentence should account for it.
- Make the regeneration driver fail explicitly on graph mismatch, an unsuccessful solver status, or a false verification result. It currently prints these outcomes without enforcing them as assertions. The submitted instances passed the corresponding checks during this review, so this is a robustness improvement rather than a defect in the present results.

[119] Y. Yang and X. Kong, *The thickness of amalgamations of graphs*, arXiv:1201.6483 (2012). https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.6483
