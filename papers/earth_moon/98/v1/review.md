# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantively relevant partial advance: every $12$-vertex-critical biplanar graph with maximum degree at most twelve has an independent set meeting all its eight-cliques. This removes a specific obstacle in a clique-hitting approach to coloring critical biplanar graphs. It neither constructs a graph requiring ten colors nor proves universal nine-colorability, and therefore does not resolve the supplied task. The degree restriction and the unresolved second hitting-set step are stated clearly and consistently.

The decisive argument is convincing. The criticality/list-coloring extension argument justifies excluding induced $d-1$-choosable subgraphs. The cited Cranston–Rabern classifications then restrict eight-clique intersections to shared triangles and isolated $K_9-e$ pairs. Saturation of the shared triangles supplies both the component structure and the absence of unwanted edges needed in the subsequent induced-subgraph arguments. The two graph-polynomial certificates exclude long paths and five-cycles; the other cycle lengths are handled by the stated induced path or join obstruction. These steps yield the claimed bounded path components.

The main contribution is the passage from this local structure to a global independent choice. Lemma 4 correctly applies the published linking lemma to blocking candidate sets. In particular, it uses induction under a hypothetical biplanar quotient to establish the required nonbiplanarity hypotheses; it does not assume that arbitrary vertex identifications preserve biplanarity. For odd paths, the rooted disjoint-eight-clique theorem proves the blocking property, while the explicit extension rule uses saturated triangles to avoid conflicts across components. The isolated-pair case properly identifies its additional dependence on the published nonbiplanarity of $K_7\vee E_3$. I found no material gap in this composition argument.

The supplementary evidence is adequate for the finite proof premises. I inspected and replayed the supplied exact coefficient calculations: both distinct recurrences return $-1$ for each essential graph, with the required exponent totals and degree bounds. The overlap checks, candidate-extension checks, and rooted seven-clique counterexample verification also reproduce their reported results. These are replays of supplied implementations, rather than an independently written coefficient computation. I did not rerun the earlier triangulation enumeration underlying the imported $K_7\vee E_3$ obstruction in [3]; the manuscript accurately identifies that published computational dependency.

The secondary results have useful, appropriately limited scope. Proposition 5 supplies a hand argument for the seven-clique overlap restrictions, with the finite pattern checks serving as corroboration. Corollary 6 is explicitly conditional on a perfect matching and the absence of isolated $K_8-e$ pairs. The fifteen-vertex example establishes failure of the unrestricted rooted seven-clique analogue: its planar-layer certificates, independence number two, and proper eight-coloring support the exact chromatic claim. The authors correctly explain why this example says nothing against the remaining eleven-critical hitting-set conjecture. The joint bipartite-selector formulation and its conditional coloring consequence are also justified.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Cranston–Rabern [1,2] supply essential classifications and the general reduction strategy, but their cited critical hitting-set result does not cover degree twelve. The linking operation in [3] and the rooted disjoint-clique theorem in [4] are explicitly credited. Neither those results nor the local two-fan reductions in [5] supply the present selection theorem for overlapping components. The additional equality-threshold theorem of Christofides, Edwards and King [7] still requires $\omega\geq\frac23(\Delta+1)$, whereas here $8<26/3$. Thus it does not subsume Theorem 1. The combination of the exact obstructions and blocking-set composition constitutes a coherent contribution beyond the imported ingredients.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The main text explains the research question, proof strategy, decisive evidence, and remaining obstacle; detailed checks are kept in the supplement. The abstract is informative, and Figure 1 usefully explains the local extension rule. Two minor improvements would help: define the clique-blowup notation $P_m[K_3]$ and $C_m[K_3]$ explicitly at first use, and note in Section 3 that Brooks' theorem forces $\Delta(G)=12$ before applying [1, Theorem 3] to obtain $\omega(G)=8$. Adding the equality-threshold result [7] alongside King's strict-threshold bound would also strengthen the related-work discussion. These are minor clarifications, not conditions requiring a new scientific argument.

[7] D. Christofides, K. Edwards and A. D. King. *A note on hitting maximum and maximal cliques with a stable set*. arXiv:1109.3092v2, 2012. https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.3092v2
