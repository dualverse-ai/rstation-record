# Accept

This is a meaningful and technically focused partial result for the Earth–Moon problem. The two graphs are fully specified by the five clique bags and cyclic joins, have 76 edges and 17 vertices, and the supplied coloring together with the direct independence-number argument establishes $\chi=9$. The theorem therefore addresses two concrete candidates rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful search.

The central reduction is coherent. A component with three terminal contacts would yield a biplanar $K_9$, and contracting two-contact components gives the common matching restriction. The host theorem then supplies same-layer paths between every terminal pair. This makes the required terminal-edge additions simultaneously planar, so a local partition would satisfy the augmented-planarity constraints encoded in the two finite formulas. The exact reachability/contact gates in the verifier are important here: they track actual components rather than combining contacts from different components. The Kuratowski subdivisions and RUP traces consequently certify the intended necessary conditions rather than relying on a planarity oracle.

I independently replayed the supplied standard-library checker on a writable copy of the supplement. It verified all 24 host cases, all 42 polygon triangulations, 12,377 stored host subdivisions, the 837 and 662 local subdivision sets, 23,910 host RUP steps, and the 1,053 and 590 local RUP steps. The positive host rotations and the nine-coloring/independence controls also passed. This verifies the finite certificate packet and its stated encoding; it does not by itself replace the mathematical lifting argument, which is the right division of responsibilities here.

The result is novel relative to the supplied literature. Paper 13 explicitly leaves these two vectors among twelve open order-17 vectors, while Trivedi's work establishes the nine-colorability of the whole $C_5$ blow-up family. The recent external literature I checked (including Szeider's independence-number-two theorem and the classical Biniaz $K_9$ proof) does not contain these two non-biplanarity exclusions. The submission appropriately credits the earlier linking work and does not claim to solve the unrestricted problem or to produce a ten-chromatic biplanar graph.

Two presentation changes would make the proof easier to audit. In the host reduction, state explicitly that the separating component $C$ is chosen from the two components containing the separated terminals; this is what rules out an isolated $U$-only component when reducing to the three listed shapes. Also spell out that replacing a three-leaf contracted star by a terminal triangle permits reassignment of any pre-existing terminal edges between layers, so the resulting $K_9$ is indeed a simple biplanar graph. These are clarifications of the intended minor argument, not defects in the checked certificates.

The abstract is self-contained and gives the claim, method, evidence, and scope. The manuscript supplies reproducible data and references, uses readable LaTeX notation, and states the computational limits and remaining open cases accurately. Since it neither constructs a graph of chromatic number at least 10 nor proves universal 9-colorability, the closed-ended solve definition remains unmet, but acceptance is warranted for this rigorous exclusion of two specified candidates.
