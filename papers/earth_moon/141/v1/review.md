# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The eighteen-vertex bound applies to ordinary nine-vertex-critical graphs, and its application excludes the specified two-disjoint-eight-clique configuration through 112 edges while restricting the 113-edge case. These conclusions are relevant to the supplied task, which explicitly welcomes structural obstructions. They do not resolve the unrestricted problem, establish nine-colorability at order 21 generally, or exclude the remaining 113- and 114-edge configurations.

The decisive argument for Theorem 1 is convincing. The passage from vertex criticality to a spanning minimal critical subgraph correctly handles the fact that deleting edges can increase independence number. In the 83-edge case, the nonspanning possibilities after an independent-triple deletion are all addressed: the edge inequality excludes two orders, the low-vertex block theorem excludes another, and explicit coloring extensions dispose of the fourteen- and ten-vertex cores. In particular, the replacement inequality $d_P(t)+a\le12$ removes the earlier degree restriction from the ten-vertex-core extension. The fifteen-vertex theorem [8] then applies with its actual minimum-degree, edge and clique hypotheses.

The subsequent intersecting-triple argument also closes the claimed gap. The central triple and its three pairwise nonneighbor intersections give $a_i\ge2$ and $\sum a_i\le7$. For $D\le26$, the excess calculation yields $31-D\ge112-4D$, a contradiction. Thus the second deletion has 55 or 56 edges. I checked the applicable statements in Kostochka's survey [7]: the excess threshold in Theorem 5 indeed places its spanning eight-critical subgraph in $F_8$, and Theorem 6 supplies the small-order bounds used here. The final mixed-clique argument treats both the $(2,2)$ selected-set case and the singleton-side case, including the possible extra edge. It does not assume that the induced deletion itself is edge-critical.

Theorem 2 correctly keeps degrees in the original graph while using a spanning critical subgraph for the published deletion-order result [11]. Excluding the eleven-vertex core by its five eight-cliques justifies the surviving minimum degree and the exact-one-neighbor property for degree-nine vertices. The rooted transversal theorem [5] then makes the 84-edge bound applicable through every exterior vertex. The list-coloring cases in Appendix 472, the two-triple deletion in Appendix 473, and the residual edge counts in Appendix 474 support the stated exclusions. In the final $2,2,1$ exterior case, the forced four cross edges really exhaust the four selected vertices' cross-degrees. After the prescribed pairs are removed, the nine-edge bound excludes every nontrivial Hall obstruction, while the surviving special pair excludes a full row or column. This supplies the simultaneous color extension needed there.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. Paper 138 [1] proves the 83-edge bound; paper 140 [3] and the proof 439 in paper 139 use additional degree restrictions in the related intersecting-triple argument. Removing those restrictions and obtaining the general 84-edge bound is a mathematical strengthening, with a concrete new application. The overlapping-clique reductions [4] and the rooted-transversal result [5] do not already supply Theorem 2. The external comparison also supports this distinction: Kostochka–Stiebitz [9] supplies the exceptional-family machinery rather than this restricted 84-edge conclusion; Kelly–Postle [13] addresses a sufficiently-large-chromatic-number regime; and Szeider [14] treats independence number at most two. I found no substantial duplication of the two stated theorems in this focused comparison.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations clearly enough for acceptance. The proof is inspectable without new computational enumeration. My verification was mathematical inspection of the arguments and cited theorem statements; I did not rerun the computational certificates underlying published dependencies such as [11]. Those remain inherited dependencies, rather than newly reproduced evidence.

The following minor improvements would make the presentation easier to use:

- Cite paper 139 directly when discussing the inherited intersecting-triple strategy, and identify the unrestricted fourteen-vertex extension and revised excess count as the new steps relative to that proof and [3].
- Define $e$, $\alpha$, $\omega$ and $\delta$ explicitly at their first use. State the parameter restrictions in the standard $F_8$ definition alongside its description.
- Replace the archival numbering with a short appendix concordance. Remove or clearly segregate the unused unrestricted no-eight-clique claims in Notes 469–470, whose references to missing Notes 463–467 are unnecessary for either submitted theorem. Update obsolete “pending review” and open-case statements in the retained notes. These editorial issues do not undermine the self-contained arguments used here.
