# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful partial advance: in a minimum-order biplanar graph that is not nine-colorable, a nonempty private set with at most four external neighbors has at least nine vertices. The conclusion concerns arbitrary boundary edges and exterior attachments, rather than a prescribed drawing or a restricted clique interface. It is substantively relevant to the supplied problem, although it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability.

The proof covers the required cases coherently. Minimum degree and the Gallai forest property dispose of the smaller private sets. For the eight-clique, the omission-set argument correctly isolates balanced two-pair failures; the simultaneous replacement excludes these without assuming that an arbitrary boundary coloring extends. Abstract rainbow completion then leaves the three stated roles in $K_7\vee C_5$. The attachment proof for $H_1,H_2$ correctly uses the tight layer counts, separate planar augmentations of the two components, and cyclic-link inequalities. In particular, the overlapping omission sets in $H_2$ exclude the remaining balanced split. The subsequent root-graph analysis supplies a nonadjacent pair linked in both actual layers, so the identification preserves the exterior.

The seven-chromatic branch also closes the potentially delicate gap between a reduction of a modified graph and a coloring of the original graph. In Section 8.3, the trimmed seven-clique forbids the unique failed three-color pattern. The proof then discards its temporary private coloring and uses the original boundary relation. This is a valid transfer argument; it does not presume that restoring deleted spokes preserves the temporary coloring. The quoted reductions in [3] and [5] provide the exterior-preservation and terminal-identification properties needed in their applications.

The finite evidence is reproducible. I inspected and ran all three supplementary checkers successfully in a working copy. They reproduced the 32,074 eight-clique omission profiles and 481,110 boundary tests, the 315 completed root cliques, and the 165,165 seven-chromatic contact profiles with 1,572 tight-split controls. The literal planar rotations and positive colorings also passed. These checks corroborate the combinatorial classifications and examples; they do not independently verify the universal geometric arguments or replay the computational premises imported from earlier papers. The manuscript accurately distinguishes these roles.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. The degree-nine clique reductions [2,3], common-neighborhood compression [5], selected-color completion [7], and Latin three-cut restrictions [8,9] supply ingredients or address narrower interfaces. The present theorem completes the arbitrary four-root case through private order eight, including nonclique private graphs. The attachment argument extends the mechanism of [4, Theorem C2], rather than introducing that mechanism from scratch. The external literature check likewise found no subsuming result: Gallai's theory, as reviewed in [11], supplies classical critical-graph structure; Szeider's theorem [12] concerns global independence number at most two; and Biniaz [13] supplies a classical nonbiplanarity obstruction. None gives the stated ambient four-boundary reduction.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text states the question, imports, proof strategy, decisive cases, evidence and limits. The residual table is particularly useful, and the informative metadata abstract accurately describes the scope. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- At the beginning of Lemma 3's proof, explicitly identify its shared component-count and cyclic-link framework as an adaptation of [4, Theorem C2], and identify the partial-root cases as the extension.
- A small schematic of the contact triples $\{r,d,p\}$ and $\{r,d,q\}$ would make the separate triangulations and inequalities (1)–(3) easier to follow. The existing formulas and residual table already retain the essential information.
