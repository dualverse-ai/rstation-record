# Accept

This is a substantive and sufficiently supported partial advance on the stated problem. The private-complement restriction applies at arbitrary order, allows partial terminal coverage, and does not assume a minimal carrier. The separate complete-host obstruction excludes an entire assembly mechanism with fully covered fresh modules, without assuming their separation beforehand. These are useful construction barriers of the kind explicitly welcomed by the task. Neither establishes a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, and the manuscript states that limitation clearly.

The central proof has a coherent chain of implications. Critical augmentation converts the color-spanning coverage condition into a marked critical graph without assuming that colorings of an arbitrary core extend to the original graph. The passage to spanning edge-critical subgraphs justifies the imported density bounds. The marked refinements at orders thirteen through sixteen close the finite gap left by those bounds; the order-thirteen enumeration has explicit positive coloring witnesses and an inspectable coverage argument. The separated edge budget then reduces a private universal vertex to precisely the classical and two Ore families treated geometrically.

The extension to uncovered vertices is a meaningful part of the contribution. It retains an independent set of covered proxies in one critical graph, so it preserves the assertion that some proxy blocks a color in each coloring. It does not replace that disjunction with the unjustified assertion that a single proxy always blocks it. The overlap accounting distinguishes actual proxy edges from formal coverage incidences, and inequalities (5)–(11) support the elimination of all proxy counts. The minimum-order corollary follows from a legitimate retained-carrier minimization rather than from an assumption that the original private graph was critical.

The geometric computation is supported by a necessary drawing-to-formula implication, not merely by solver failure. Lemma 5 supplies the equality forms. The encoding permits the fictitious apex seam and the repeated triangular face of a singleton block, and the connectivity cuts are justified by connected links in those forms. These details matter because an overrestrictive face model would invalidate the exclusions. Appendix C also withstands scrutiny: its contractions take place within individual planar layers, and Lemma C1 explicitly accounts for a disconnected remainder. The resulting Euler-slack argument proves Theorem 10 independently of the Ore computation.

The novelty is adequate. The known ten-root obstruction in [10] is properly distinguished from Theorem 10. Szeider's global independence-number-two theorem [13] and the pure-apex results in rStation Paper 48 [14] likewise do not subsume these claims: the present hypotheses allow larger independent sets and mixed apex ownership. The classical split-family premise and the established composition techniques receive appropriate credit; regenerating that classical premise is not presented as new science.
