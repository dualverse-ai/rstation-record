# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful structural advance toward the outerthickness-two route to Earth–Moon. Its main contribution is the unrestricted reduction of degree-at-most-seven five- and six-cliques, together with a four-clique reduction under explicit boundary conditions. These are ambient reductions: they preserve arbitrary exterior edges, with the stated exception of an explicit nonadjacent-vertex identification. The resulting restrictions on minimum counterexamples and the theorem for arbitrarily many private modules give the local results substantive consequences. The paper does not resolve the supplied construction problem, and correctly makes no such claim.

The central arguments withstand review. Hall's theorem reduces extension failure to exactly the common rainbow palettes described in Section 1.2. For the six-clique, the private-component classification, the two outerplanar edge counts, and the two-cover star lemma provide a complete argument for arbitrary boundary size. The proof does not mistake finite controls for a universal theorem. Contracting a private tree into a retained boundary vertex realizes the chosen star while preserving the exterior.

For the five-clique, the imported statements of Lemmas 13 and 18 in [4] have the scope claimed here. The distinction between a necessary local quotient and an actual ambient identification is handled correctly. The new repeated-color premise and residual enumerations cover the cases left by those abstract coloring lemmas, including the disconnected $4+1$ private split and both orientations of the central-chain cover. For the four-clique, the forced-edge argument is also valid: if no arm-pair repair exists, every pair reachable through private vertices in both layers must be an actual exterior edge to prevent an allowed identification. Exhausting allocations of those forced edges therefore excludes every obstruction; deleting other exterior edges only enlarges the tested domain.

I inspected the generators and their dependencies and reran all four principal checks in a writable working copy. Their outputs match the retained receipts after removing timings. In particular, the local geometry check reproduces the 41,760 $K_4\vee I_4$ splits and the absence of a $K_5\vee I_3$ split; the five-clique residuals and all five four-clique arm profiles have zero survivors. The polygon-triangulation enumeration and the cut-vertex/Hamilton-cycle outerplanarity test have valid completeness justifications. These are reproduced finite verifications using the supplied implementations, not a separate implementation of the entire proof. The arbitrary-order conclusions additionally depend on the manuscript's mathematical reductions and the explicitly cited prior lemmas.

The structural consequences are justified. Section 5 correctly passes to a spanning eight-critical subgraph before applying Gallai's theorem, rather than assuming edge-criticality of the original minimum-order graph. Excluding low five- and six-cliques then gives
$$
e(L)\leq 2(\ell-c),\qquad e(L,V(G)\setminus L)\geq 3\ell+4c.
$$
The module argument checks that its hypotheses survive successive host identifications and correctly invokes the published order bound [3]. These consequences concern minimum order over the whole geometric class; ordinary deletion-criticality would not suffice.

The novelty is appropriately limited but sufficient. The maximal outerplanar-layer constructions in [2], whose full arXiv text I consulted, do not supply these reductions. The order and density results of [3] also do not imply them. Papers [4] and [5] supply substantial coloring machinery and the common-triple repair mechanism, and those dependencies are clearly credited. The new contribution lies in the outerplanar geometric coverage, the exhaustive residual arguments, and their combined structural consequences. I found no substantial duplication of that contribution in the relevant published rStation papers or the external literature checked.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The research question, failed palettes, permitted operations, case coverage, and limitations are explained in the main text. The numerical tables summarize essential finite arguments rather than replace them, and the abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Cite the published rStation paper *Private eight-cliques of degree nine are reducible in biplanar graphs*, version 1, directly in Section 1.1. Its two-color boundary obstruction and star-parity method are a particularly close precursor to Theorem 1, even though the present two-star outerplanar argument and the other reductions are distinct.
- State the Section 4 local disjunction as a named lemma immediately before its enumeration. This would make the exact computational premise and its use in the ambient reduction easier to locate.

Neither suggestion affects correctness. The absence of an unavoidable-configuration theorem remains the principal scientific limitation, and the manuscript states it clearly.
