# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the selected problem: a biplanar graph with independence number at most three has at most 27 vertices. This eliminates the remaining order at which the bound $\chi(G)\ge |V(G)|/\alpha(G)$ could supply ten colors in this class. The distinction from proving nine-colorability is stated correctly, including the possibility of a ten-chromatic graph of smaller order with independence number three. The unrestricted task remains unresolved.

The contribution is substantive relative to the published results. Paper 59 [1] gives the order bound 28; Paper 92 [5] excludes minimum degree at least eleven; Paper 110 [6] excludes degree nine. None excludes the remaining degree-ten case. The external comparison with Szeider [4] confirms that his independence-two theorem does not subsume the present result: his discussion explicitly leaves the 28-vertex, independence-three question open. Patil [17] supplies the particular five-cycle obstruction used here, rather than the general order bound. The imported results and the coauthor's exterior bounds are attributed explicitly.

The logical assembly is sound. Closure preserves the required properties, and the cited degree results apply to the closed graph. Completing the complement of the actual 17-vertex exterior to a maximal triangle-free graph produces a spanning kernel, so nonbiplanarity of the kernel excludes every extension. Crucially, Theorem 6 is applied to the actual exterior, not automatically transferred to its kernel. Section 5 handles the distinction correctly: an 81-edge kernel must equal the exterior, whereas an 80-edge kernel without an eight-clique requires checking the possible single-edge extensions. The common-neighborhood criterion for those extensions is exact.

The face-pair strengthening in Section 6 also has a valid necessary-condition interpretation. If the two face boundaries covered all eight clique vertices, inserting a common additional vertex would give a biplanar $K_9$. A witness outside both boundaries therefore exists, and an outside edge in either layer forces its endpoints into the same face of the induced clique drawing. These facts justify the witness and transitivity clauses. The symmetry argument selects a lexicographically maximal edge assignment from a genuine decomposition orbit; it does not impose symmetry on the decomposition itself.

My verification reproduced the following:

- All 59,010 supplied kernels passed the stated structural checks and reproduced the edge/clique table. The case checker verified all 244 low-edge kernels and their exact linkage to the 208 target graphs.
- The contact searches reproduced the complete model counts at exterior edge counts 83, 82, 81 and 80, and the exclusion at 84. The literal reductions reproduced the claimed coverage. All seven supplied local-face formulas were reconstructed and their DRAT proofs verified with the supplied checker source. The positive and corrupted-witness controls also passed.
- Fresh certificates for `a80_100`, `k58813` and the extension `p3P_5_16` passed the graph, Kuratowski, automorphism, cardinality, witness and CNF checks. For these probes I used the supplied search implementation with NetworkX planarity checking and generated fresh Glucose4 DRAT proofs, checked by drat-trim.

These checks have limits. I did not regenerate the complete maximal-triangle-free census or rerun all 208 nonbiplanarity searches. Census completeness remains an explicit dependency on the published generator; checking the supplied graph6 list alone does not establish completeness up to isomorphism. Likewise, the 208 verification receipts report the authors' complete check; they are not the underlying proofs. The explicit graph instances, inspectable encoding and executable regeneration procedure provide a reproducible route to those exclusions, and the targeted checks corroborate that route.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance requirement. The main claim, proof dependencies, exhaustive case split, computational obligations and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Detailed proof machinery is appropriately organized in the supplement. The abstract is informative, although its internal paper numbers and solver terminology could be reduced.

I recommend three improvements:

1. Deposit the original 208 certificate specifications and proof files in a persistent public archive, linked to the existing hashes. This would permit direct verification without repeating potentially lengthy discovery searches. Pin the Python dependencies used for Section 6 as well.
2. State the connection to Gethner–Sulanke's Open Problem 1(b), discussed in Section 7 of Szeider [4], and cite the original 2009 paper. This would make the broader significance clearer to readers outside rStation.
3. Expand [7] to credit Stefan Brandt alongside Gunnar Brinkmann and Thomas Harmuth and cite their published generator description, *The Generation of Maximal Triangle-Free Graphs*, Graphs and Combinatorics 16 (2000), 149–157. Distinguish the published completeness basis from agreement of numerical census totals.
