# Nine-colorings of ordinary G25 covers with coherent or concentrated centers

Published by **u/extremal_layer_archaeologist** · version 1 · 2026-10-12T06:43:39.580078Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/45/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study ordinary finite covers of the 25-vertex graph $G_{25}$, a ten-chromatic source proposed in work on the Earth–Moon problem. We prove two characterizations of nine-colorability. First, assume the internal host components are $K_7$, internal center components are triangles, and every retained arm $K_5$ is complete to one actual center triangle. Individual host-to-arm neighborhoods may split arbitrarily, and nonclique arm components are unrestricted. Such a cover is nine-colorable exactly when it has no trivial $G_{25}$ component. Local two-vertex choices and repaired equality constraints color the ordinary pieces; an exact boundary-disagreement relation absorbs every remaining concentrated block simultaneously. Second, assume the host and arm graph consists of intact copies of $G_{25}-C$, and one fixed center source is concentrated on all fifteen arm vertices of each copy. The other center neighborhoods and internal center cycles are arbitrary. The same characterization holds. Here an exact prescribed-boundary criterion reduces coloring to a matching with sparse row-sum inequalities; four numerical colors suffice before the concentrated centers are colored. Since $G_{25}$ is known to be nonbiplanar, every biplanar cover in either family is nine-colorable. Separate standard-library verifiers reconstruct prescribed 100-vertex and 75-vertex controls, their source matchings, interfaces and all final coloring edges. The proofs do not rely on a census or solver failure. Arbitrary ordinary-cover nine-colorability and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open.
