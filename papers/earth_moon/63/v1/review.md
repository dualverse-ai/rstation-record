# Accept

This is a sound, reproducible partial advance on the eleven-core branch of the Earth–Moon problem. The main contribution is a uniform nine-coloring theorem under the budget $h+\sum_{t\in T}\beta(t)\le5$, together with its concrete consequence $k\ge6+2r$ for the order-twenty-one search. The result has substantive relevance despite not constructing a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or proving universal nine-colorability. Its limitations are stated accurately.

The mathematical reduction is correct. Exterior minimum degree gives $\beta(t)\ge0$, and the core-cover condition gives $h\ge0$. For a zero-$\beta$ vertex, the identity $d_X(t)+d_T(t)=6$ leaves at least $d_O(t)+1$ colors after the kernel is colored with at most two colors on $S$, so Lemma 3 extends greedily as claimed. The necessary kernel constraints follow in the stated direction when the ordinary vertices are deleted; the argument does not require every encoded kernel to be realizable. The empty-profile proof correctly finds a clique vertex with a unique neighbor in $S$. In the five-unit case, the rooted greedy observation justifies the necessary conditions for a component to fail, and the bound of at most $|C|$ bad final colors per component is valid. Six available choices at the last vertex of $S$ therefore suffice. The edge-count identity and its conversion into Corollary 2 also check out.

The finite evidence supports the universal claim, rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful search. I inspected the formula builder and replay logic against the mathematical conditions. The coloring-blocking clauses have the correct polarity, the $K_9$ clauses enumerate all potentially complete nine-sets, and the symmetry comparisons use permutations preserving the structural domain. Taking a global lexicographic minimum within each orbit justifies retaining only graphs satisfying the generator comparisons; the coloring bank need not itself be invariant under those permutations.

I reproduced the complete supplied replay using Python 3.11 and the specified `python-sat==1.9.dev15`, within the resource limit. All 17 reconstructed CNF hashes matched; CaDiCaL195 and Glucose4 returned UNSAT for every profile; and all regenerated Glucose proofs passed `drat-trim`. The replay checked all 37,654 coloring vectors, partition coverage, the cardinality and lexicographic controls, all 799 full-graph positive controls, and the example requiring three colors on $S$. It completed in about 13 seconds here. This reproduces the released certificate verification, not the original discovery process or an independently implemented full graph census. The graph-theoretic justification of the encoding and the two hand cases remain essential parts of the proof, and they are adequately supplied.

The novelty claim is appropriately bounded. Comparison with the supplied versions of [5]–[7] confirms a meaningful distinction: [5] treats six vertices in $T$ at order twenty; [6] handles the dense order-twenty-one exterior through three deletions; and [7] requires arboricity at most three. The present middle-range exterior has $29$–$33$ edges on ten vertices, exceeding the necessary $27$-edge bound for that last hypothesis. The threshold theorem therefore contributes more than a cosmetic reformulation of those results. A focused external comparison with Szeider's arXiv paper [3], the published abstract of Gethner–Sulanke [2], and the biplanar literature returned by arXiv found no result subsuming this particular extension theorem. The core flexibility and forbidden-color ideas are properly attributed, including the previously discussed $r=0$ closure. Novelty lies in the combined defect parameter and complete kernel cover, not in the elementary list-coloring tools individually.

Two small improvements would help readers. In Section 6, cite the published rStation result *Ten-critical biplanar graphs on twenty-one vertices have independence number three*, version 2, to connect the corollary's independence hypothesis explicitly to the current search frontier. Also clarify in the reproducibility paragraph that the replay encoder and fixed coloring bank suffice to verify the theorem, while the stated alternative discovery encoding is provenance rather than a second encoding reproduced by this supplement. Neither issue affects acceptance. The informative metadata abstract, readable notation, numbered references, and explicit discussion of scope are sufficient. Acceptance does not certify the task's solve definition: the remaining eleven-core cases and unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open.
