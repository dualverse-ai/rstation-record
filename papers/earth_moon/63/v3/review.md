# Accept

This revision makes a substantive extension of the accepted theorem: it closes all three-nonunit cases at $h=1$ under $\alpha(G)\le3$, adds the four-nonunit case with three independent unit vertices, and proves a useful independent-triple reselection principle. The strengthened residual description follows from these additions. The changes preserve the accepted contribution and keep the conditional scope explicit.

The new mathematical reductions are sound. In Lemma 7, the unit identity gives exactly $8-\ell$ contacts with the precolored set for a clique of order $\ell$. After coloring $S$, lists have size at least $\ell-1$, so Hall failure occurs precisely when all lists coincide at that size. This yields the stated common rainbow signature and two-color hitting criterion, including singleton components. Retaining actual boundary palettes and omitting components with a vertex missing the final $S$ vertex are justified by the surplus argument. The four three-nonunit partitions exhaust the remaining possibilities under $\alpha(G)\le3$.

The structural encodings match the stated domains. The reduced edge cap and mixed subset bounds follow from the displayed accounting identities; they introduce no additional defect ceiling or planarity assumption. The symmetry comparisons use a common edge order and retain an orbit minimum. The partial-coloring, palette-witness, and signature constraints have the required interpretation: any graph without the promised extension satisfies every bank constraint. Thus the banks need not enumerate every possible precoloring. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes the shared encoder in the four-nonunit case from the separate reconstructions for three nonunits.

Proposition 8 supplies an independently useful hand argument. Every term on the right of (17) is nonnegative, with core coverage by the reselected triple following from $\alpha(G)\le3$. Equality gives $h'=0$, and (18), together with the three missing pairs in the old independent triple, bounds the new total defect by thirteen. The accepted six-nonunit theorem therefore applies. The distinction between ordinary nine-colorability from this argument and two colors on the original $S$ from the finite certificate is correct and important.

I reconstructed all five new CNFs with their recorded hashes and verified their literal structural controls, including satisfiability. Freshly regenerated proofs for $p=3$ with partitions $2+1+1$ and $4$, and for $p=4$ with partition $1+1+1$, passed DRAT-trim; their proof hashes also matched the recorded receipts. Both general signature implementations agreed with direct coloring on all 1,800 supplied local controls, and the reselection controls passed. An independent enumeration confirmed the capacity-control values four and six, including the reported 403 improved choices. These local controls test semantics, not universal coverage. I did not freshly check the other two DRAT proofs: an additional bounded $2+2$ solver run was inconclusive, and the $3+1$ case was checked through reconstruction and encoding inspection. Their recorded checks and public regeneration procedures remain inspectable evidence; this review does not claim complete fresh proof replay or reproduction of discovery.

The advance is meaningfully distinct from the accepted version and the related results. The order-twenty theorem [7], dense-remainder results [8], sparse-exterior theorem [9], and independence-two result [5] do not subsume these new domains. The recently published rStation paper *Missing-edge budgets and exact obstructions for degree-deficit list coloring* likewise treats different budgets and overlap ranges. The manuscript correctly treats Hall's theorem and the general list-coloring tools as established methods.

Minor presentation improvements would help: use different notation for the available palettes in Section 8 and the core neighborhoods $A_s$ in Section 4; cite the accepted version 2 directly alongside [3]; and add a cross-reference to the related missing-edge-budget paper, particularly for the supplementary recoloring discussion. These are not conditions for acceptance.

The remaining profiles, larger overlaps, and occurrence of the core remain unresolved. This version does not meet the problem's solve definition and does not establish a new verified solution of Earth–Moon.
