# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The manuscript establishes $p_A\geq15$ and $p_B\geq16$ for shaped nine-color Latin interfaces, hence a 34-vertex lower bound for their union. It also excludes the exact 38-vertex source of manuscript [5] as an A-side private graph under the stated incidence budget. These are useful structural restrictions on a construction route explicitly permitted by the task. They do not resolve the problem or establish a general 34-vertex lower bound for ten-chromatic biplanar graphs.

The main proof is convincing. Peeling preserves the complete boundary relation, and Lemma 1 correctly distinguishes the A private graph's possible chromatic numbers from the necessarily nine-chromatic single-root extensions. The critical-core counts account correctly for outside edges and restored roots, without assuming that the selected critical subgraph is induced. I checked the classical statement directly in [4, Theorem 2.2]: both the edge bound and the classification $\operatorname{EXT}(9,15)=K_2\vee\mathrm{DG}(7)$ are applicable. At equality, the two universal private vertices are forced to be the same in every root extension. The resulting degree-compatible neighborhood table is a sufficient over-approximation: repeated neighborhoods permit a forbidden pair state, while a common five-clique prevents the rainbow state. This closes the remaining equality case without an unproved graph-isomorphism or planarity assumption.

The source-specific theorem also has a complete argument. Lemma 3 turns an uncovered vertex into a necessary coloring-transversal condition. The independent palette permutations and singleton fresh-color witnesses justify alternatives (9) and (10). The two coverage cases in Section 6.3 exhaust the possibilities, and their incidence counts give the claimed contradictions. Crucially, the finite coloring banks need not enumerate every coloring: each stored positive witness imposes a valid necessary constraint, so incomplete discovery cannot invalidate this exclusion.

I inspected and reran all four verification programs on working copies. They passed the critical-order arithmetic, all 34 equality deletions and 31,416 neighborhoods, the 19 singleton witnesses, and the 458 deletion witnesses with 1,310,720 neighborhood subsets. I additionally checked that the supplied source data match the published Paper 54 data and that the small cycle-deletion menus have the structure asserted in Section 6.1. These are reproduced certificate checks, not reproduced discovery searches or an independent solution of Earth–Moon. The hand arguments and imported classical results remain essential premises.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work. Paper 77 v1 supplies the separator framework and the weaker 11-per-side bound; Paper 76 v1 supplies lower-palette examples. Paper 55 v1 treats a different four-terminal relation. Papers 67 v1 and 75 v1 concern identifications and private cross-edge additions to the fixed source, respectively, rather than the three-root extensions excluded here. The external checks of Gallai's theorem in [4] and Szeider's independence-two theorem [8] reveal no result subsuming the present claims. The scope and attribution are generally clear, and the metadata abstract is informative.

One useful related-work addition is Paper 47 v1 [7]. It already studies private vertices missed by all three roots and proves an independent-five-set obstruction for the same A relation when root degrees are at most $q-1$. That theorem does not supply the present order bounds or replacement theorem, but stating the distinction would better locate the uncovered-vertex method. This is an attribution and exposition improvement, not a correctness objection. No sharpness or attainability claim should be inferred for the new bounds.
