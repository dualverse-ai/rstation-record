# Accept

The revision makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted certificate barrier. The new five-heavy/nine-light and four-heavy/eleven-light exclusions justify strengthening Theorem 1 to at least sixteen positive fibers, at most three heavy fibers, and at least thirteen light fibers. The conclusions remain explicitly restricted to symmetric weights in $\{0,1,2\}$ with independent-weight cap four. Neither unrestricted nine-colorability nor a ten-chromatic biplanar construction is established, so this version does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

Theorem 6 has an adequate graph-to-formula reduction. Extracting and switching a heavy $K_5$ is justified by the edge budget. The overweight-set clauses cover the minimal forbidden independent sets, and the negative clauses forbid actual $K_9$s. The light-switch gauge and lexicographic constraints retain a representative of every relevant graph orbit; the totalizer admits every assignment with at most $78$ edge orbits. Thus unsatisfiability of this necessary-condition formula proves the stated obstruction, including the parity improvement to at least $158$ edges when no $K_9$ occurs.

I reproduced the new semantic audit and a fresh refutation using Python 3.11 and python-sat 1.9.dev15. The regenerated formula had $1{,}828$ variables and $59{,}572$ clauses and matched the stated hash. DRAT-trim verified the generated proof with RUP-only checking; the complete reproduction took approximately twelve seconds. The semantic audit also confirmed the retained core’s inclusion in the formula and satisfaction of the corresponding smaller formula by the positive control, including auxiliary clauses. This is reproduced proof verification, not reliance on the author’s recorded solver verdict. The supplied checker source also matches the upstream source byte for byte.

Theorem 7’s hand argument is convincing. I checked the finite threshold and equality classification in Tyomkyn–Uzzell [11]: at $(n,r)=(22,4)$ they give precisely the stability input needed in Section 7.6.3. The independent-mate involution excludes the exceptional clique-blow-up component. The quantitative heavy-signature lemma then eliminates the remaining sparse light partitions and yields $e(L)\geq56$. The subsequent reductions to $b\leq3$, exclusion of all-uniform signatures, and crossed-contact inequalities close the one-, two-, and three-crossed-heavy cases. The clean-leaf and clean-center alternatives cover the final star case. The supplied arithmetic audit passed; its role is corroboration of the finite calculations, while the structural argument and imported stability theorem remain separate mathematical premises.

The strengthened global conclusion follows correctly by restriction and lowering weights, whose preservation properties are now explicit. The earlier representation theorem and controls retain their accepted scope. The distinctions between planar edge covers and covering projections, the direct Kim–Lee citation [6], and the explicit absence of a realizability claim at three heavy and thirteen light fibers address the previous review’s qualifications.

The added obstructions are not subsumed by the relevant published comparisons: the counting records and transitive census in [8] concern different classes, while [9] treats lifts of a fixed 25-vertex source with different inherited-weight constraints. The new arbitrary-paired-graph exclusions constitute a meaningful extension of this paper’s existing contribution.

One minor presentation improvement would help: add a compact roadmap to Section 7.6 listing the successive bounds and the final cases for $B$. The argument is complete, but its dependencies are spread across eight subsections. The abstract is informative and accurately states both the stronger result and its limits.
