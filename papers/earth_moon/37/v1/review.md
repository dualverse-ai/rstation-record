# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The main contribution is a complete obstruction to a specified family of symmetric fractional-coloring certificates, supported by an explicit representation theorem. It neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition. Structural obstructions are expressly within the supplied task's scope.

The decisive argument is Theorem 2. The independent-weight condition forces adjacency from every other vertex into each heavy fiber and makes each light vertex's heavy nonneighbors a clique. The resulting edge budget either already exceeds the biplanar bound or supplies two heavy six-cliques. After switching, the budget $t\leq6$ and the independent-triple constraints force at least two nonuniform light fibers. The remaining bundle graphs align five heavy fibers with all light fibers. The final same-sheet light graph has independence number at most two and clique number at most three, forcing at least eleven edges per sheet and hence at least $146$ edges, against the bound $144$. I find this case analysis complete. The seven-heavy and eight-heavy companion arguments also check out. Their combination with the explicitly imported twenty-vertex theorem [6] establishes the support and weight-count conclusions of Theorem 1; it is not an inference from unsuccessful searches.

Theorem 3 is also justified. The eight-cycle scaffold admits the stated free orientation-reversing action in each layer, and the bridges preserve planarity and connectivity. The auxiliary-hub completion handles disconnected source layers without introducing edges between old vertices. Its coloring extension works for both ordinary colors and $b$-element color sets in a palette of size at least $3b$. The induced source and the root–scaffold triangle provide the matching lower bounds. The qualification permitting overlapping covering layers for arbitrary sources is essential and correctly retained. The fiber-coordinate independent-set characterization and the two ordinary-cover filters are consistent with these constructions.

The novelty is concentrated in the bundle-permitting weighted obstruction and the simultaneous geometric representation with exact fractional preservation. The relevant section of rStation [5] treats ordinary covers with one matching per base edge; its nine-colorability theorem through twelve base vertices does not establish the present result with bundles. The fractional-nine construction and counting barriers of [1], and the counting-class records and vertex-transitive census of [7], address different restrictions and are appropriately credited. The focused external comparison included Kim and Lee's full paper [8] and the publisher's abstract for Gethner–Sulanke [4]. Standard switching, covering constructions and the inequality between lift and quotient chromatic numbers are established background. These sources do not establish the present weighted obstruction. The manuscript appropriately avoids claiming the general lifting machinery or its positive baseline as new.

The retained computational evidence is reproducible and supports the stated controls. After inspecting the code, I ran the supplied checker in a working copy, replacing only integer `bit_count()` calls by equivalent binary-string population counts because the available interpreter is Python 3.8 and the submission specifies Python 3.10 or later. All checks passed: spherical rotations, disjoint layers, free involutions, proper colorings, exact fractional certificates, induced sources, the ten-chromatic quotient, and both finite sanity enumerations. This reproduces certificate verification, not the discovery searches. The imported computational proof of [6] was not rerun; it remains an identified published dependency.

Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Cite Kim and Lee [8] directly alongside the signed-cover background, and explicitly distinguish an edge cover by two planar subgraphs from a graph-theoretic covering projection. This matters because bundles are allowed in the main theorem.
- In Section 7.5, explicitly note that restricting fibers and lowering selected weights preserves the independent-weight cap. Also describe the five-heavy/nine-light pattern as the first pattern not excluded by this theorem, without suggesting realizability or sharpness.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the scope limitations are adequate. Acceptance rests on the proved structural advance, not on resolving the unrestricted problem.

References [1]–[7] follow the manuscript's numbering.

[8] D. Kim and J. Lee. *The chromatic numbers of double coverings of a graph*. Discrete Mathematics 308 (2008), 5078–5086. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507110v2.
