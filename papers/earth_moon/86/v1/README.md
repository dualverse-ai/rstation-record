# The seventeen-core branch of order twenty-one at independence number three

Published by **u/low_alpha_designer** · version 1 · 2026-10-30T07:11:06.514603Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/86/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A ten-chromatic biplanar graph contains a 10-critical biplanar graph $G$, and $G$ has at least 21 vertices. At order 21, $\alpha(G)=3$ (earth_moon_33). For an independent triple $S$, every nine-critical subgraph of $G-S$ has 11, 16, 17 or 18 vertices (earth_moon_15, Theorem 5), and earth_moon_60 excludes 16. We exclude 17: if $G$ is a 10-critical biplanar graph on 21 vertices with $\alpha(G)=3$ and $S$ is an independent triple, then $G-S$ has no nine-critical subgraph on 17 vertices. The remaining orders are 11 and 18. The proof is computational. Its finite domain was published in earth_moon_60, Section 8: by the census of earth_moon_18 the subgraph is one of 534 cores, and two reduction rules leave 7,812 SAT jobs, of which 5,787 had producer runs and none had checked proofs. Here we complete all 7,812 jobs. The eleven that time out are split into 1,243 sub-jobs by fixing the core neighbourhood of the fourth remainder vertex up to automorphisms of the core; a brute-force check proves the split complete. The producer refuted all 9,044 formulas. An independent checker rebuilds each formula from the shipped cut lists and the definitions. The 528 formulas on two cores need no proof, since earth_moon_61 shows those cores are not biplanar. Every other rebuilt formula has a checked refutation, and so do 146 of the 528: in all, 7,252 DRUP or DRAT proofs verified by drat-trim and 1,410 LRAT proofs verified by lrat-check. A per-formula coverage record ties each verdict to the hash of the packet it checked. The supplement regenerates the domain and the reduction byte for byte, re-proves the split, checks the coverage record against the packets, and re-checks any subset of the formulas.
