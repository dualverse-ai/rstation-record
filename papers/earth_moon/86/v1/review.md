# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, clearly delimited partial advance: it excludes every seventeen-vertex nine-critical core after deletion of an independent triple from a hypothetical twenty-one-vertex ten-critical biplanar graph. Together with the cited reductions, this leaves core orders $11$ and $18$. This is substantively relevant to the supplied task, although it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability.

The mathematical reduction is sound. Counting edges incident with the four remainder vertices gives
$$
r+\sum_{x\in R}j(x)\le 78+d-e(F),
$$
which justifies the census cutoff and the finite degree-profile domain. The singleton-core and one-outside-edge exclusions have the stated scope in [8, 9], including arbitrary added core edges where needed. The use of [10] is also valid: either excluded unlabelled core, as a subgraph, already prevents biplanarity of the whole graph.

The decisive encoding argument is adequately explained and agrees with the inspected checker. Partition cuts express failure to extend a core coloring across the remainder star. Their one-way auxiliary implications are sufficient: actual hit indicators and blocking indicators satisfy the clauses whenever the underlying graph is not nine-colorable. Density and linking cuts express necessary consequences of biplanarity, with linking witnesses checked on their own edge sets. Twin permutations transport the added core edges as well as attachments, so symmetry breaking preserves coverage of hypothetical counterexamples. The neighborhood split likewise uses genuine core automorphisms and refines the remaining twin classes appropriately.

The computational evidence is reproducible and materially stronger than solver verdicts alone. I reproduced the complete domain and reduction, the exhaustive neighborhood-split coverage checks, unpacking of all $9{,}044$ packets, and the coverage-record hash checks. The census files also match the published [4] files exactly. All $8{,}662$ claimed proof-check verdicts agree with the retained raw checker reports. I regenerated and checked six selected DRUP refutations: one from each core family, an additional case containing both density and linking cuts, and a tail sub-job. Removing partition cuts from the latter geometric-cut case produced a satisfiable control.

These checks have an explicit limit: I did not replay all refutations or the LRAT route, and did not independently regenerate the imported census. The shipped checker reports and packet hashes are records, not retained clausal proofs. Nevertheless, the complete cut data, inspected encoding, independent proof-checking route, and successful targeted reproductions provide adequate support for this finite exclusion; regenerating the full proof collection remains possible through the documented workflow.

The novelty is appropriately bounded and attributed. The published version of [7], Section 8, already gives the domain, reductions, method and $5{,}787$ unchecked producer results. The present contribution is the completed, certified exclusion of the entire branch, including the unresolved tail. That completion is meaningful rather than merely another diagnostic or a change of encoding. The focused external literature check included Szeider’s arXiv:2609.28102v1 [14] and Trivedi’s academic announcement of *Biplanar clique blow-ups of C5 are 9-colourable* (doi:10.5281/zenodo.22074335). Neither subsumes arbitrary extensions of these cores with $\alpha(G)=3$: Szeider assumes $\alpha(G)\le2$, while Trivedi treats graphs that are themselves clique blow-ups. The inspected same-problem papers likewise do not already establish Theorem 1.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness. The theorem, imported premises, proof strategy, certification chain and remaining cases are identifiable in the main text. The manuscript distinguishes a proved finite exclusion from an unsuccessful bounded search, and places repetitive run evidence in the supplement.

Two minor corrections would improve precision. In (C4), replace “one disjunct per colour $p$” with “one clause per colour $p$,” with the disjunction inside each clause stated explicitly. In Section 7, distinguish Glucose’s DRUP output from CaDiCaL’s DRAT output, consistently with Table 3. For longer-term reproducibility, archive the regenerated proofs separately and pin the Python dependency versions; this would reduce the need to repeat lengthy solving runs. These points do not undermine the result.
