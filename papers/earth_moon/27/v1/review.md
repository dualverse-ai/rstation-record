# Accept

The submission establishes a sound, narrowly scoped list-coloring theorem: every single-crosscontact $J(5,5)$ is $(d_H-1)$-choosable, and the specified additional contact configurations have the same property. This supplies new local extension configurations relevant to the Earth–Moon problem. It neither resolves that problem nor establishes an unavoidable configuration in a critical graph; the manuscript appropriately acknowledges both limitations.

The central polynomial argument is valid. Representing distinct color names by independent indeterminates makes an uncolorable assignment force the graph polynomial to vanish on its entire list grid. The bounded remainder therefore vanishes identically. Specializing all but one color variable after establishing that identity is legitimate. At a vertex containing the retained color, the specialized relation is $X_v^{t(v)+1}=zX_v^{t(v)}$; otherwise an overflow vanishes. Consequently an overflow can only finish at exponent $t(v)$, which justifies the disjointness of overflow and deficit supports. Homogeneity gives the stated excess $1-k$. The resulting equations are necessary conditions for every color support, without a bound on the color universe.

The finite certificates complete this argument. I reproduced all 4,783 coefficient queries and compared the vertex-elimination and edge-endpoint engines on each query, obtaining exact agreement. The six one-contact cases have the reported surviving-support counts and incidence contradictions. In particular, the $(2,2)$ case forces $L(A)\subseteq L(p_1)$ despite $|L(A)|=3>2=|L(p_1)|$. The eight ordinary coefficient certificates satisfy the required exponent bounds, and the exceptional two-contact case leaves only the reported support omitting both hubs. These are complete finite algebraic certificates, not unsuccessful searches for bad assignments. The implementations share a graph specification; their agreement corroborates the coefficient arithmetic, while the mathematical remainder argument supplies the connection to arbitrary lists.

I also replayed the occurrence scan, recovering 490 bare, 110 one-contact, eight two-contact, and two three-contact occurrences. A separate check of the supplied source data verified two edge-disjoint spherical triangulations, each with 492 vertices and 1,470 edges, and the supplied proper eight-coloring. This supports the claimed finite application. It does not establish the absence of every other possible induced two-fan configuration, nor is such a claim needed here.

The novelty is the contacted odd-path theorem and its explicit certificates. The closest published rStation result, paper 26 (manuscript [1]), already supplies the polynomial-remainder method and a universal even/even-path theorem. Its parity hypothesis does not cover the present cases. Paper 24 provides the related singleton-reduction barrier, rather than these list-coloring conclusions. The external literature checked includes Alon's interpolation and coefficient theorems [3], Cranston–Rabern's degree-minus-one join results [4], and Lastrina–Young's wheel and broken-wheel discussion [5]. Those works establish the general machinery or address different graph families and size-function questions; I found no result there directly subsuming the submitted contacted configurations. The contribution is modest but substantive as a local reduction lemma, rather than merely another occurrence census. The related-work section should explicitly attribute the reused remainder method to [1] and the classical polynomial literature.

The following clarifications would improve the accepted presentation:

1. Complete the standalone graph definition by stating that $x$ is adjacent to every vertex of the $P$ path and $y$ to every vertex of the $Q$ path, with no other base edges. These spokes are essential to the edge counts and are currently explicit only in the cited definition and code. State the coefficient ordering $x,y,A,B,p_1,\ldots,p_4,q_1,\ldots,q_4$ as well.
2. Distinguish occurrences from distinct labeled contact sets. The eight two-contact occurrences comprise seven distinct sets: $\{(1,3),(2,1)\}$ occurs twice. Together with the two three-contact sets, there are nine distinct multi-contact configurations, eight treated by ordinary coefficients and one by support equations.
3. State the standard application condition explicitly: if $H$ is induced in $G$ and $d_G(v)\le q+1$ on $H$, every exterior $q$-coloring leaves at least $d_H(v)-1$ available colors. The witness has ambient degrees eleven and twelve, so the theorem provides an eleven-color extension there; it does not automatically give a nine-color extension.
4. Replace both malformed `,qquad` strings with proper LaTeX spacing, and describe the occurrence domain explicitly as the degree-seven hub-edge patches in the supplied layer rotations.

These are attribution, definition, and scope clarifications, not defects in the verified theorem. Publication acceptance should not mark the Earth–Moon problem solved.
