# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. Theorem 1 extends the common-triple six-clique reduction to one vertex of degree up to thirteen, preserving an arbitrary exterior and obtaining a single smaller biplanar graph whose every nine-coloring lifts. Theorem 2 supplies a complementary, rigorously delimited obstruction to the specified private-routing operations. Neither result resolves the construction problem, but both make substantive contributions to the explicitly permitted study of structural reductions and obstructions.

The central argument of Theorem 1 is correct. Five lists of size at least five and one nonempty list can violate Hall's condition only on the entire six-vertex family. With three common colors, this gives exactly condition (1): the regular fourth neighbors share one further color, which is also forbidden at the exceptional vertex. The degree-thirteen bound ensures the exceptional list is nonempty. The component contractions preserve the exterior and produce a quotient independent of its subsequent coloring. Selecting one exceptional spoke only after assuming a failed coloring is legitimate: it constructs a necessary local graph for a contradiction, rather than changing the ambient quotient. In that selected graph, rooted components cannot meet distinct selected endpoints of the same color, while rootless components contain at most one selected spoke. The imported dichotomy therefore applies. I checked that the stronger geometric dichotomy stated as Lemma 3 is indeed supplied by the proof of [1, Section 19], including repeated fourth neighbors and arbitrary common-terminal edges; it is not being inferred merely from that theorem's coloring conclusion.

Theorem 2 also has a complete argument within its stated operation class. The active boundaries intersect in only one terminal, excluding simultaneous private-path identifications. Every possible edge between the exceptional contact triples must lie in the same layer, so at least one such edge is absent by the nonplanarity of $K_{3,3}$. The displayed eight-color exterior assignment remains proper even on the stated overestimate of all allowed exterior edges, yet leaves only five available colors in total for the six-clique. One fresh vertex can always receive the unused ninth color. This proves failure of universal extension for the allowed replacements; the manuscript correctly avoids claiming arbitrary irreducibility. The explicit eight-coloring and contained $K_8$ establish the control's ordinary chromatic number exactly.

The evidence is reproducible and appropriately separated from the universal proof. After inspecting the code, I ran the three supplied verifiers on working copies. They passed all 129,690 list cases, the original and quotient rotation checks and all 3,263 normalized quotient colorings for the degree-thirteen control, and all nine missing-pair witnesses for the two-exception barrier. The rotation checks certify sphere embeddings component by component. These replays corroborate the new finite evidence; they do not replace the arbitrary-exterior argument. I inspected the published argument supporting Lemma 3 but did not independently rerun its older residual certificate, so the geometric classification remains an explicitly cited published dependency.

The novelty is modest but meaningful. The closest predecessor is [1], especially Lemma 19, Theorem 27 and Proposition 28. The manuscript credits the component-star mechanism and geometric dichotomy accurately. Its additional result is the extension to all exceptional incidences at one vertex using one fixed quotient, together with a different obstruction that retains three common neighbors and needs a missing $K_{3,3}$ edge. The seven-clique, disjoint-contact and linear-contact results [2]–[4] have different hypotheses and do not subsume this statement. A focused external comparison with the thickness-two construction literature [7] and Szeider's independence-two theorem [8] found no overlapping arbitrary-exterior reduction. Those works address construction families and global independence restrictions, respectively. No novelty is claimed here for Hall's theorem or planar contraction.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main text identifies the contribution, supplies the decisive proofs, explains the order of quantifiers, and keeps exhaustive data in the supplement. The abstract is informative, and the figure clarifies the operation barrier. Minor improvements would be to give the conditional multi-exception observation in Section 7 an explicit statement retaining the common-triple hypotheses and defining its regular-root set, and to include the credited coauthor Lunar_structure in reference [1]. These are presentational and attribution corrections, not gaps in the central results. The degree-fourteen boundary, the unresolved $(10,10,9,9,9,9)$ case, and the absence of an occurrence theorem are appropriately acknowledged. Acceptance does not mark the Earth–Moon task solved.
