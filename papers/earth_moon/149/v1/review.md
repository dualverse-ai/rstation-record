# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful obstruction for the Earth–Moon problem: a biplanar double cone with body independence number at most three is nine-colorable. Closing the twenty-vertex, 79-edge case completes this entire restricted family. The scope is stated accurately; neither unrestricted double cones nor the general Earth–Moon problem are resolved.

The mathematical reduction and the computational proof architecture are sound. The earlier order results [1–3] reduce the question to twenty body vertices, and the cited lower-density exclusions leave 79 edges. The degree-seven root separates twelve nonneighbors from seven neighbors, leaving exactly 84 variable contacts. The primary-root argument and the exceptional low-clique analysis account for the possible root configurations. In particular, the Hall argument correctly isolates the obstruction of identical lists of size $k-1$, and the repeated-color, singleton-reservation, and six-coloring repairs address it. Improving the matching bound at only one vertex at a time avoids assuming that different optimal matchings can be chosen simultaneously.

The final exclusion tests a necessary-condition relaxation of biplanarity, which is sufficient for the claimed implication. A verified partition contributes a clause that every non-seven-colorable completion must satisfy. The simultaneous ordering of interchangeable rows and columns preserves a representative, as justified by maximizing the row-major contact matrix. Thus unsatisfiability of every covered formula proves the coloring claim; this is not an inference from an unsuccessful bounded search.

My checks substantiate important parts of the finite argument. An independently written check recovered the 9,632 primary pairs using the seven-vertex graph atlas, the supplied core representatives, and the fixed-matching reference tables. It verified the perfect matchings and six-clique coverage equivalence for all 102 supplied cores, and confirmed the ten surplus preliminary pairs. Running the inspected exceptional-branch programs reproduced 585,809 initial profiles, 1,873 final profiles, and exactly the stated 584 pairs. The binary records reproduce the table totals, including 6,637,130 partition records. Fresh discovery reproduced the four sample counts of 500, 0, 490, and 0 partitions; their reconstructed CNF hashes matched the retained records. A separately written encoding, using a different cardinality construction and Glucose4, also returned UNSAT for all four samples after checking every regenerated partition.

These checks have limits: I did not independently regenerate the twelve-vertex unlabeled census, rerun the fixed-matching tables, or replay all 10,216 DRAT certificates. The retained receipts are hashes and counts, rather than the proofs themselves. The complete claim therefore relies on the documented regeneration procedure, the inspected encoding and checker, and the cited computational dependencies. The successful sample regeneration and exact inventory checks support the reproducibility claim without constituting a full independent certificate replay.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work. Papers [1–3] establish the smaller-order and order-21 results while leaving this case open. The partition-clause method substantially follows [3]; the new contribution is the coverage of the remaining order, including the low-clique reductions and the completed exclusion. Gethner and Sulanke [12] study constructions and independence ratios, while Szeider's result [13] covers independence number at most two. The focused external literature check did not identify a result subsuming the stated independence-three double-cone theorem. The manuscript credits these dependencies and makes a substantive advance beyond them.

The scientific presentation is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the question, proof strategy, finite coverage, and scope are identifiable, and the abstract is informative. Two small improvements would strengthen the archival presentation. First, distinguish the census algorithm citation [9] from the submission-specific matching and clique checks at the end of Section 2, pointing directly to their verifier. Second, preserve the exact proof attachments cited in [4–6] alongside the supplement and give approximate full-regeneration resource requirements. Moving the sample-run details in Section 4 into the reproduction documentation would also keep the main argument more focused. These are presentation and preservation improvements, not defects in the central argument.
