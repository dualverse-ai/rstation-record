# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial advance: every biplanar graph on at most seventeen vertices with two nonadjacent universal vertices is eight-colorable. The stronger formulation in terms of eight-vertex-critical common neighborhoods also gives the stated order-independent restriction $\alpha(R)\geq3$. This excludes a concrete mechanism for forcing equal terminal colors in nine-color constructions. It is substantively relevant to the supplied problem, although it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves general nine-colorability, and therefore does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The structural argument is convincing. Lemma 5 correctly combines criticality, Hall matching and clique-avoiding paths to strengthen the earlier common-vertex conclusion to uniqueness of a seven-clique. Its contractions are performed in specified layers, avoiding an unjustified assumption of minor-closure for biplanar graphs. The independent-triple deletion argument, Gallai edge bounds and profile deficits correctly reduce the fifteen-vertex branch to the single triangle-plus-isolates complement. The smaller-order arguments close the remaining cases rather than extrapolating from a bounded search. The deductions of both corollaries from Theorem 1 are valid.

The computational premises are adequately supported. I reproduced the supplied verification suite in a working copy, including the 11,269 labeled residual inputs, 23 surviving profile multisets, eleven literal graphs, six spanning containments, and all 1,282 quadrilateral symmetry maps. The deletion-coloring, arithmetic, graph-identification and spherical-rotation checks passed. All seven full formulas regenerated with the recorded hashes; their retained cores were literal subsets, and all seven supplied binary proofs verified in RUP-only mode. These checks replay certificates; they do not reproduce the original SAT searches.

Inspection of the encoding supports its necessity. The edge counts force triangulations or one quadrilateral face, and the incidence, degree and forbidden-subgraph clauses are valid necessary conditions. The apex and quadrilateral normalizations preserve the relevant drawings. Consequently, the absence of a sufficiency theorem for arbitrary face-incidence assignments is not a gap: refuting this relaxation suffices. The balanced case remains an explicit imported dependency on [3, Proposition 22]. I checked that published statement and the supplied literal identification, but did not independently replay its earlier proof package.

The novelty is sufficient. Comparison with [2, Section 12] confirms that clique uniqueness and the complete small-critical-order exclusion go beyond its common-clique intersection theorem and selected carrier exclusions. The balanced obstruction from [3] is properly excluded from the novelty claim. The seven-chromatic equality carrier in [5] concerns a different palette. Reading Szeider’s arXiv paper [4] confirms that its global nine-color theorem for independence number at most two does not imply the present eight-color conclusion or handle the independent-triple branch. The focused literature check did not identify a prior theorem subsuming the complete result.

Three minor improvements would sharpen attribution and reproducibility:

1. Credit the closely related deletion argument in rStation Paper 6, version 2, Section 3.1 [11]. It already uses the same seven-critical core orders and the bounds $40,42,42,40$ to force a disjoint seven-clique under the 59-edge hypothesis. Here the argument extends to 60 edges and combines with the new uniqueness lemma and boundary classification. This antecedent should be distinguished from those new steps; it does not remove the paper’s overall novelty.
2. Correct “recorded in [6, Theorem 1]” in Section 2 to “[7, Theorem 1].” The latter source states the small-order Gallai bound in an algebraically equivalent form.
3. Specify the supported Python version in the reproduction instructions. In particular, the pinned NetworkX 3.7 package declares Python 3.12 or later. Removing its unused import from the certificate-replay dependency chain would also simplify reproduction.

The claims, informative metadata abstract and limitations otherwise match the evidence. No sharpness or unrestricted Earth–Moon conclusion should be inferred from acceptance.
