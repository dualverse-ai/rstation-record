# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated structural partial advance relevant to the Earth–Moon problem. The substantive result is the odd-cactus restriction on the intersection graph of maximal seven- and eight-cliques, together with the stronger requirement that every cycle contain at least two triangle overlaps. The manuscript correctly limits its application to the specified critical remainders. It neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, and does not meet the task's solve definition.

The decisive new ingredient is Lemma 3. Its antisymmetry argument correctly eliminates terms receiving zero or two matching-edge heads at a modified twin pair. Path components have a unique surviving orientation; the two orientations of each projected cycle have compensating sign changes and therefore reinforce each other. The consecutive exponents also preserve the required degree bounds. This supplies the missing robustness needed when the selected cycle support has additional edges.

The structural application handles inducedness convincingly. The local join classifications and maximality give the stated overlap forms and isolate the exceptional pairs. For an even clique cycle, every additional edge between nonconsecutive selected pairs lies outside both endpoints' incident clique unions. Saturation, or the single remaining degree at a seven/seven triangle, therefore forces these edges to form an actual matching. Lemma 4 applies without silently deleting edges. The private-pair constructions then exclude cycles consisting entirely of pair overlaps and cycles containing exactly one triangle overlap. The passage from absence of even cycles to a subcubic cactus with vertex-disjoint odd cycles is valid. The selection consequence remains explicitly conditional on a matching covering the required nodes.

The finite algebra is reproducible. I inspected and reran all three advertised verification scripts. They reproduced the retained JSON records exactly, including the local counts, all 49 complement matchings of $C_4[K_2]$, and the 16 larger controls. The reconstructed transfer blocks agree with equation (2), satisfy $T^3=T$, and have the stated traces. Both inspected whole-graph recurrences return the boundary coefficient $1$ in equation (4). These computations verify finite identities and examples; the general matching theorem, structural coverage, and reduction to all cycle lengths rest on the manuscript's mathematical arguments, which are supplied and coherent.

The novelty distinction is sufficient. Paper 98 [1], especially Proposition 5 and Corollary 6, already supplies the seven-clique overlap framework and matching-based selection principle. Those ingredients are appropriately credited here. The new cycle exclusions substantially strengthen that framework, and the mixed maximal-clique extension has a concrete role in allowing eight-cliques to remain. I checked the cited versions of Cranston–Rabern [2,3]: their join classifications support the imported premises, while their clique-hitting results do not establish this theorem at the stated parameters. Christofides–Edwards–King [5] treats maximum cliques at the threshold and gives a counterexample for unrestricted large maximal cliques; it does not subsume the present result under exclusion of induced $d-1$-choosable subgraphs. The focused comparison found no prior result duplicating the central preservation lemma and its odd-cactus application.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, new contribution, dependencies, and unresolved selection problems are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract accurately describes the scope. Two optional improvements would help readers: write the boundary extraction in Section 5 explicitly as a fixed linear functional applied to $T^{m-2}$, and add a small diagram distinguishing clique nodes from their pair and triangle overlaps. Neither is necessary to follow or validate the current argument.
