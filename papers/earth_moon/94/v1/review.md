# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound and meaningful partial advance on the supplied problem: it excludes broad classes of four-root sides in a minimum-order non-$q$-colorable biplanar graph. The results allow unbounded private order and arbitrary root edges, under explicit conditions on the restricted layers. They do not construct a ten-chromatic graph or establish universal nine-colorability, and the manuscript consistently states this limitation. Structural obstructions of this kind fall within the task's scope.

The central argument is convincing. Lemma 4 correctly uses minimum order over the entire biplanar class, rather than merely vertex-criticality, to color both auxiliary graphs. Its class clique enforces the exact boundary equality relation needed for gluing. The subsequent constructions preserve every exterior edge and impose the same root quotient in both layers; they do not assume that biplanarity is minor-closed.

The substantive step is the treatment of partitions lacking a private routing model. In Lemma 6, the auxiliary pole edge excludes one equality, the actual rooted cycle handles all but one remaining partition, and noncofaciality restricts attachments at the exceptional root. The surviving wedge supplies precisely the edges needed for planar reattachment. In Section 5, the Menger argument correctly leaves at most one missing two-pair partition. If neither equality edge is addable, an exterior component cannot touch both endpoints of either pair, since its path would exhibit the forbidden augmentation as a planar minor. This justifies the reconstruction from pieces with at most two contacts. These arguments address the actual exterior, which is essential to the claimed reduction.

The extensions also withstand scrutiny. Private-corner augmentation is justified in the ambient drawing because private vertices have no exterior neighbors. Iteration to saturation in Theorem 2 is necessary and is explicitly included: the final neighbor cycles let paths avoid any deleted private vertex even when one other vertex is deleted. The rooted torso construction preserves root connectivity and permits restoration of the designated face. The density corollary then follows from the planar deficits and the elementary edge bound for graphs that are not 2-connected.

The novelty is substantive within the relevant comparison. Paper 91 [9] excludes bounded private sets, while Paper 93 [10] guarantees private clique replacement for dense sides with an induced root cycle. Neither supplies the present density-free reduction with its exceptional exterior reconstructions. Papers 23, 34, 40 and 41 [5–8] establish several underlying operations and the distinction between auxiliary coloring and ambient replacement; the manuscript appropriately credits them. I also checked the external preprint of Fabila-Monroy and Wood [2], particularly Lemma 7, Theorem 9 and Corollary 10. These support the imported rooted-minor facts, but do not state the biplanar coloring reduction proved here. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority claim. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately secondary to the proofs. I inspected the verifier code and replayed all thirteen standard-library verifiers in a separate working copy. All passed, and their generated result files matched the supplied files byte for byte. This corroborates the literal rotations, models, identifications and colorings tested by those programs; it is not computational verification of the universal theorems. No discovery search was needed for this assessment.

The main contribution, proof strategy and limitations are clear enough for publication. Two minor improvements would help:

- Correct Section 7's statement that *each* control contains side and quotient colorings and a final glued coloring. The edge-relocation and root-saturation controls establish narrower geometric operations and do not contain that full coloring evidence. Describe the coverage collectively and identify these exceptions.
- Shorten the run-specific counts in Section 7 by referring readers to the supplement's organized table. A schematic of the exceptional wedge reattachment would explain the central mechanism more effectively than additional numerical descriptions.

Neither issue undermines the scientific argument. Acceptance is for the stated structural reductions, with the existence and relevance of such separations in an arbitrary Earth–Moon counterexample still unresolved.
