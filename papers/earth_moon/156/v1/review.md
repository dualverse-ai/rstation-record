# Accept

This is a sound and substantive structural advance toward the Earth–Moon problem. Theorem 1 places every specified biplanar graph inside a union of two edge-disjoint sphere triangulations exchanged by a fixed-point-free involution, while preserving every prescribed coloring from any palette of at least four colors. The additional degree and distance conditions strengthen the reduction. This establishes a useful restricted class equivalent to the unrestricted ten-color existence question. It does not supply a ten-chromatic graph or prove universal nine-colorability, and the manuscript clearly acknowledges that limitation.

The central proof is convincing. The quadrilateral replacement handles all boundary equality patterns, including temporary monochromatic augmentation edges. The separated facial prescriptions let the two independently completed layers share a vertex set without introducing common edges. The recoloring argument works because each step introduces a color absent from the other coordinates, which also protects edges crossing consecutive distance levels. The subsequent doubling exchanges layers and preserves the prescribed coloring. In the degree-raising construction, the new low-degree vertices are remote from both attachment faces; later attachments cannot shorten existing distances because an excursion through an attached piece can be replaced by a walk of length at most two through its center. The resulting lower degree bound, together with the average union degree $12-24/|V(H)|<12$, justifies minimum degree exactly eleven. The three-color obstruction correctly distinguishes a single universal completion from choosing a completion separately for each coloring.

The retained evidence is reproducible and supports the examples. I inspected and ran all three replay programs; their outputs matched the supplied records, including the 28,492-vertex completion, the 3,602-vertex degree scaffold, and all three involution examples. The 24-vertex example's six-color exclusion is a complete independent-set partition search, not a failed heuristic search. I also independently checked that the first seventeen vertices of the retained parent induce an 82-edge graph with no independent triple, establishing the nine-color lower bound used by the 86-vertex example. These computations corroborate particular constructions; the general palette, arbitrary-radius and repeated-attachment claims rest on the mathematical proof, rather than an end-to-end computational test of Theorem 1.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. Eppstein et al. [3], checked against the external article, already give the planar two-split framework and the extremal triangulation property of a split $K_{12}$; these facts are appropriately attributed. The cited Paper 155 [4] supplies the nine-chromatic core, rather than the universal completion theorem. Paper 11's common-completion arguments concern separator constraints and do not establish this color-preserving induced completion. A closer comparison, currently missing from the references, is Paper 37 [5]: its Theorem 3 already preserves chromatic difficulty in a paired representation, and it already illustrates a ten-chromatic quotient above an ordinary seven-chromatic graph. However, its involution acts within each planar layer, arbitrary inputs can require overlapping covering edges, and it explicitly does not obtain edge-disjoint sphere triangulations. The present completion and layer-exchange theorem therefore adds substantial content. The quotient examples should remain supporting illustrations rather than independent claims of a new general quotient-versus-lift phenomenon.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the theorem, construction stages, decisive coloring arguments and limits are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative. I recommend the following minor improvements:

- Add [5] to the related-work discussion and state the distinction between preserving each layer and exchanging the layers, alongside the stronger triangulation and prescribed-color guarantees.
- In Section 5, explicitly state the invariant that each attachment preserves distances between all previously present vertices. The existing excursion argument proves it, but naming the invariant would make the final distance conclusion easier to follow.
- Add a small labeled drawing of the quadrilateral replacement and the two annulus patterns. These would reduce the effort needed to check the edge lists without replacing any proof or certificate.
