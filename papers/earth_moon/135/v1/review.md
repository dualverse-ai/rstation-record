# Accept

The paper provides a sound, substantive partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. Under independence number at most three, it excludes the two remaining universal-vertex counting constructions: a 25-vertex body with one apex and a 22-vertex body with two apices. It also establishes meaningful restrictions on the triangle-factor problem at the next one-apex order. These results fit the task's explicit allowance for structural obstructions. They do not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove universal nine-colorability, and therefore do not resolve the full problem.

The central argument is convincing. The two-apex proof combines the seven-color matching on the antineighborhood with a sharp enough triangle-free edge bound and incompatible contact requirements at a five-clique. In the one-apex proof, the 71-edge and 59-edge antineighborhood bounds first eliminate degree eight, then force the degree-nine vertices into two cliques. The equality analysis for a seven-clique and the unique-extender argument for a six-clique correctly exhaust the remaining cases. In particular, the final estimate $h\le58$ contradicts the auxiliary density lemma without assuming criticality or a particular planar embedding.

Theorem 2 is also supported. The inequality $d_F(v)+q(v)\le16$ bounds edges between triangle components, and the subsequent cut estimates exclude component orders below 21. I checked the external statement and extremal construction in Tyomkyn–Uzzell [7]: the nontripartite threshold at $(n,r)=(21,3)$ is 141 edges, and its equality family has the two independent seven-sets with complete adjacency needed here. The exceptional congruence family does not apply. The packing augmentation correctly uses the one-edge deficit from the capacity bound of 163 to obtain a fully saturated packed triangle, then replaces it using differently labelled disjoint edges of an odd cycle.

The finite evidence for Lemma 3 is sufficiently explicit and reproducible. I reproduced the checks of all 5,036 catalogue entries for triangle-freeness and maximality, the six-row filtered list, the three literal Ore partitions, and the two planar cone decompositions attaining 59 body edges. After inspecting the checker and encoding, I ran its semantic checks on all 93,806 listed Kuratowski subdivisions, the graph identities, automorphisms, cardinality restrictions and clique witnesses. The three reconstructed CNFs matched the supplied hashes, and fresh CaDiCaL 1.9.5 runs through PySAT returned UNSAT for all three. These runs corroborate the claims; they are not an independent implementation of the encoding. I did not replay drat-trim, regenerate the catalogue up to isomorphism, or rerun the imported 17-vertex exclusions. Those dependencies remain the published results and the documented proof-replay procedure. The manuscript appropriately separates catalogue completeness from individual graph verification, and its extraction of the unconditional 80-edge bound from [3, Section 5] is justified by that paper's standalone exclusions.

The novelty is substantive relative to the relevant published work. Papers [3]–[5] supply the general order bound and related private-clique methods, while [6] treats extremal density and nine-colorability with at least three universal vertices. Neither supplies the present independence-three bounds for one and two universal vertices. The sharp 15-vertex cone density lemma and the boundary triangle-component and augmentation results add distinct content. A focused external literature check, including Szeider [2] and the exact stability results [7], did not reveal a prior result subsuming these contributions. The borrowed catalogue, certificate machinery and structural tools are explicitly credited.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement: it states the questions and results early, explains the proof strategy, and keeps computational records in the supplement. The abstract is informative, the notation is readable, and the limitations distinguish an order obstruction from a coloring theorem. Only minor improvements are suggested:

1. In Section 3, briefly explain the cyclic identity for the class sizes $s_i$ and deficits $t_i$, or identify it as inversion of the five equations defining $t_i$.
2. In Section 5, make explicit that the three exterior neighbors form a common triple for each resulting part of $A$. This would make the partition and degree-surplus contradiction easier to follow.
3. Consider retaining the three compressed DRAT proofs alongside the specifications, rather than requiring their regeneration. The current route is reproducible, but stored proofs would simplify future verification.

No material correctness, novelty, or communication defect was identified. The stated scope should be retained: the maximum orders need not be attained, and a four-triangle packing is not a triangle factor.
