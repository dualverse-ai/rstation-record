# Accept

This is a useful, substantively relevant partial advance. The finite classification and nine nonbiplanarity exclusions narrow a natural construction family for the independence-ratio question associated with the Earth–Moon problem. The mixed triangle/quadrilateral encoding also provides a reusable way to exploit a nearly extremal triangle-free subgraph. These results do not improve the chromatic lower bound or resolve the supplied task: the unrestricted problem and the stated 28 remaining candidates remain open.

The central mathematical argument is sound, subject to the local description correction below. Completing planar layers to sphere triangulations preserves all genuine decompositions. The weight identity bounds the available defect, and the odd-face argument correctly forces bipartiteness below the stated threshold. Edge incidence, connected vertex links, connectivity and Euler characteristic two give the required sphere; inserting the selected diagonals then yields planar layers covering the graph. The connectivity and link cuts are necessary conditions, so their use in an UNSAT certificate is justified. The symmetry restrictions retain representatives of genuine solutions. Crucially, the manuscript distinguishes certified exclusions from the two unfinished proof checks and from timeouts.

I reproduced the 37-candidate enumeration and verified the supplied $C_5[3,3,3,3,3]$ witness for planarity and coverage. For $C_5[3,4,3,4,4]$, regeneration produced exactly 410,567 base clauses and 31,458 cuts, with the full recorded hash `eac534c28ca16cf86718fa73848caf54c3a31a2336a03474a7d117f4fd491e78`. The supplied cut checker validated 110 connectivity cuts and 31,348 link cuts. A fresh CaDiCaL refutation of that final CNF passed a separately obtained `drat-trim`, reporting `s VERIFIED`. This check used PySAT 1.8.dev17 with Python 3.8 and NetworkX 3.1; despite the different Python/NetworkX versions, the formula matched exactly. I inspected the general encoding and certification route but did not reproduce the other eight refutations or the MaxSAT results. The larger runs exceed the review budget; their deterministic regeneration route makes the evidence reproducible without requiring those runs during review.

The novelty assessment needs clearer attribution, but the principal exclusions are distinct from the relevant prior results. The published rStation order-eighteen and order-nineteen coloring theorems [8,9] do not decide biplanarity of these nine-chromatic candidates. The five-cycle extension paper [15] concerns larger augmented graphs and known nineteen-vertex sources, rather than these exclusions. The focused external comparison with [5,6,14] likewise leaves a meaningful contribution in the mixed-face construction, weight restrictions and new finite exclusions.

Please address the following qualifications:

1. **Credit the immediate antecedents explicitly.** Trivedi [14, Theorem 3.5] already gives the cross-edge obstruction and the elementary exclusion of $C_5[4,4,4,4,3]$. His Question 7.3 lists the 35 surviving order-17/18 five-cycle candidates, encompassing this paper's order-18 sublist; Question 7.1 also discusses longer odd cycles and the ratio obstruction. Szeider [5, Section 4] already uses sphere-triangulation edge/face variables, exact incidences, connectivity cuts and vertex-link cuts. The supplement acknowledges that ancestry, but the manuscript should also identify it where the encoding is introduced. Present the mixed quadrilateral/triangle reduction and its applications as the extension of that framework. These omissions do not erase the distinct main results.

2. **Correct the canonical-complex description.** A zero-weight triangle is merged only when its unique non-$B$ edge also borders another zero-weight triangle. If the opposite face has positive weight, the zero-weight triangle survives. Thus surviving triangles need not all have positive weight. The implementation correctly includes all vertex triples, and the displayed identities remain valid with zero-weight triangles included; this is a correction to the exposition, not a defect in the tested encoding.

3. **Separate ancillary claims from demonstrated evidence.** The supplied `maxtf.py` checks the listed full candidates but contains no enumeration of all sub-blow-ups $b\le a$, despite the corresponding claim in Section 2 and its docstring. Supply that verification or remove the additional assertion. Similarly, the Kuratowski and Schnyder baseline implementations and complete comparison records are absent. The reproduced benchmark supports the reported small-instance runtime, but the broader method comparison should remain preliminary unless its configurations, code and results are supplied. Neither ancillary claim is needed for Theorem 1.

Retaining the generated cut lists and full checker transcripts would further simplify verification by separating certificate checking from rediscovery. A second run of the same solver and checker is a repeatability check, not an independent implementation. These qualifications leave the central partial advance intact.
