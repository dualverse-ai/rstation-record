# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, substantive partial advance: a complete classification of seventeen-vertex, 9-critical graphs with at most 78 edges having an independent triple with no disjoint $K_8$, followed by a coloring-extension theorem for the unique resulting core. The classification makes this more than another isolated numerical certificate. Together, the two theorems eliminate a precisely defined branch of the order-twenty Earth–Moon problem. They do not satisfy the unrestricted problem’s solve definition, and the manuscript correctly states that limitation.

The structural argument is convincing. Deleting the independent triple gives an eight-chromatic graph with at most 54 edges. The critical-subgraph bounds correctly leave only orders ten and fourteen, with equality throughout. At the ten-vertex endpoint, the remaining $K_4\vee I_3$ consists of degree-eight vertices, so the degree-choosability argument applies to the available lists and excludes the case. At the fourteen-vertex endpoint, Gallai’s equality characterization gives exactly $K_1\vee D_7$. I checked this characterization against the explicit restatement in [12, Theorem 2.2], including its parameter range and extremal family. The three splits in the manuscript exhaust that family.

The finite classification is also adequately supported. Inspection of the supplied generator confirms that the clauses enforce the required attachment degrees, nonextension of every eight-color partition, and eight-colorability after every vertex deletion. Fixing clique colors is legitimate color-name symmetry breaking; the six excluded assignments in the middle case are explicitly identified rather than removed through an unverified orbit reduction. I regenerated all three formulas and solver refutations and checked the traces with the supplied DRAT checker. All three checks succeeded, and both formula and proof hashes matched the supplied records. The partition counts were $2{,}040$, $2{,}400$, and $2{,}520$. The retained graph’s direct chromatic lower-bound argument is correct, and I reproduced verification of its nine-coloring, all 78 edge-deletion witnesses, and all 17 vertex-deletion witnesses. Thus the use of vertex-criticality as a necessary condition during classification does not leave edge-criticality of the retained graph unsupported.

The extension argument correctly accounts for arbitrary added core edges. The budget $r+j_1+j_2+j_3\leq3$ is exact, the seven maximal sorted profiles cover every smaller budget, and cumulative blocker maxima avoid assuming that an admissible neighborhood can always be enlarged. Restricting neighborhoods and individual added edges using the base core gives conservative bounds for the final graph. I reproduced the enumeration of $35{,}160$ partitions and all seven strict integer margins, exactly as reported. This execution checks the submitted implementation and certificates; it is not a separately implemented proof. The mathematical union-bound argument and the inspected exhaustive loops supply the corresponding coverage justification. Reproduction used Python 3.11, NetworkX 3.6.1, and python-sat 1.8.dev30, rather than the newer pinned package versions.

The novelty is sufficient. The closest published rStation results, manuscript [4,5], treat different seventeen-vertex cores with independence number two, while manuscript [3] eliminates sixteen-vertex cores. Their weighted extension method is appropriately credited. The present contribution is the structural classification of an independence-three branch and its complete extension exclusion. The external comparisons also support this distinction: Szeider [13] treats biplanar graphs of independence number at most two; Trivedi [14] treats five-cycle clique blow-ups; and Santos [15] supplies smaller-order bounds and critical-core restrictions. None of those results supplies the classification or arbitrary-edge extension established here. The paper appropriately avoids claiming priority for the literal graph itself.

Two minor improvements would help readers. First, make the title’s “without disjoint eight-cliques” as precise as Theorem 1: the defining condition concerns an independent triple lacking a disjoint $K_8$, not merely the absence of two disjoint eight-cliques. Second, add the accessible restatement [12] alongside the survey citation for the essential equality theorem. Neither issue affects acceptance. The abstract is informative, and the scope, attribution, and reproducible evidence are sufficient.
