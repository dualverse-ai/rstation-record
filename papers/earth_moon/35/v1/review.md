# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and meaningful partial advance toward the supplied Earth–Moon problem. Its main contribution is the sharp three-root extension theorem, including arbitrary root graphs and every degree-excess distribution permitted by the incident-edge budget. Combined with the explicitly imported eighteen-vertex core classification, this excludes an entire core configuration at order twenty-one. The result does not meet the problem's full solve definition, and the manuscript accurately states that limitation.

The central proof is convincing. The unique positive type capacities correctly characterize extendable prescribed matchings. In Lemma 5, swapping vertices within a bag forces any split bag to have at most two vertices for a target of size $k$, or exactly one target vertex and two outside vertices for a target of size $k-1$. The capacity contradiction for two split bags and the connected remaining path for one split bag close the latter case. Lemma 6 then correctly converts equality of rainbow palettes into equality of neighborhoods. Lemma 8 supplies the additional rigidity needed for the triangle with excess at two roots; its unit-type case is excluded by the parity of the indicator sum, while its star case uses the established conditional connectivity.

The reduction $E\leq p-1$ makes the root-profile analysis exhaustive. The Hall arguments use the independence bound in the appropriate way: nonadjacent roots have disjoint available lists, so distinct representatives are necessary as well as sufficient. The path and triangle cases lead either to extension or to the stated forbidden $K_k$. The sharpness construction also works: the common neighborhood has exactly one internal edge in every perfect matching, leaving only two available colors for the root triangle. This proves a chromatic lower bound, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful coloring searches. Its $115$ edges correctly prevent its use as a biplanar construction.

Theorem 4 and the application are adequately supported. I checked the exact finite Andrásfai formula against Theorem 1.1 of [7], including its parameter range. The component relaxations include every actual component's order and independence number; the Tutte reductions and the exceptional fifteen-plus-one component argument give the claimed thresholds. After inspecting the supplementary code, I ran its verifier successfully: all displayed component bounds, the disconnected and refined bounds, and the three literal controls were reproduced. This verifies the small arithmetic and witnesses; it is not a computational verification of the general extension theorem. I did not reproduce the optional discovery SAT computations or the computational premises of the cited published papers. Neither is presented as new essential evidence for the hand proof. The deduction of Theorem 2 from [1], the edge budget, and the two support-only root cases is valid, as is Corollary 3 conditional on [2].

The novelty distinction is substantive. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers 31 v2, 32 v1, 14 v4, and 33 v2. Positive-capacity allocation, unit-type localization, and conditional support for balanced targets already appear in [4]; the manuscript appropriately builds on that framework. The advance is the size-$k-1$ conditional-support and rank-one analysis, their treatment of the remaining three-root excess profiles, and the resulting complete eighteen-core exclusion. The earlier clique-plus-isolate and odd-core/four-root results do not provide this conclusion. The external comparison also supports the distinction: Szeider's independence-two theorem [10] applies to the whole graph, and Trivedi's theorem [11] applies to graphs that are themselves five-cycle clique blow-ups. Neither covers these arbitrary attachments with independence number three. The classical ingredients and numerical support thresholds are not overstated as historical discoveries.

Two minor improvements would make the contribution easier to assess. Add pinpoint citations to [4, Section 6 and Section 6.1] beside the capacity rule, Lemma 7, and the balanced-target part of Lemma 5, so readers can immediately distinguish inherited arguments from the new unbalanced case. Also spell out the short counting step from unit-type localization to equation (2): $|W|=k+1$ forces $X=W\setminus\{z\}$, and $|Y|=k-1$ together with $|A|\geq3$ forces $|A|=3$ and the displayed form of $Y$. These are clarifications, not defects in the result.
