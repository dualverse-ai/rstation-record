# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The principal result gives a biplanar replacement for an eight-clique whose vertices have at most two outside neighbors, with extension of every nine-coloring of the replacement. Consequently, a minimum-order biplanar counterexample to nine-colorability cannot contain a $K_8$ of degree-nine vertices. The result applies at arbitrary graph order and imposes no common-face hypothesis. It does not meet the task's complete solve definition, and the manuscript correctly leaves the unrestricted problem open.

The central argument is convincing. With eight lists of size at least seven, every proper subfamily satisfies Hall's condition; failure of the full family is exactly equality of all lists to one seven-color set. This justifies the auxiliary graph and its common two-color palette, including disconnected components. Lemma 3 then supplies the essential coordination step. Replacing clique completions by stars preserves connectivity; a repeated component incidence permits an odd cycle by the stated parity choices. Otherwise the incidence graph is a forest, and its traversal consistently constructs the required sparse color class. Repeated indexed sets cause no gap in this dichotomy.

The geometric application also checks out. Contractions are performed within individual planar layers, with disjoint retained-root branch sets, so they preserve the exterior graph and produce the chosen stars without identifying distinct retained vertices. In the other alternative, each restricted layer component contains at most one vertex of the dominating color class. Identifying that class is therefore a sequence of planar one-vertex sums, yielding two layers covering $K_9$, a contradiction. The distinction between minimum order among all biplanar counterexamples and criticality of a fixed graph is necessary and correctly stated.

The higher-degree extensions are supported by their proofs. In Proposition 4, the degree bound gives flexible lists of size at least $r$, which handles subfamilies containing no regular vertex; the coverage condition supplies the dominating color class. In Lemma 5, composing binary relations that each exclude at most one pair again excludes at most one endpoint pair, so a path admits unequal endpoint colors. Together with the closed-boundary and nonempty-list hypotheses, this establishes Theorem 6. The explicit counterexample to multiple additional nonmonochromatic constraints appropriately limits this extension.

The novelty is the general coordination and replacement theorem, rather than Hall's theorem or the underlying planar operations. I compared the argument with the published linking lemma [1], whose two geometric branches are explicitly credited. That paper does not establish the present arbitrary-boundary coloring replacement. The common-face extension results [2] require geometric interface conditions absent here, while the common-interface amalgamation theorem [3] concerns repeated copies of one specified seed and a different compatibility conclusion. The external comparison with Szeider [4] likewise reveals a different scope: its global independence-number-two theorem does not subsume this local reduction with unrestricted exterior vertices. The classical $K_9$ obstruction is properly attributed and is also treated by Biniaz [5]. These comparisons support a meaningful contribution beyond the relevant published results examined. The earlier forum examples and motivating ideas are acknowledged rather than presented as new constructions.

The reproducibility evidence is adequate for a theorem whose universal justification is a hand proof. After inspecting the code, I reran the supplied example, boundary-profile, and scope verifiers successfully. They checked the original and replacement rotation systems, literal colorings, obstruction certificates, seven-pattern profile, relation-composition cases, and multiple-constraint counterexample. All four finite controls also passed: 139,072 instances of the basic alternative, 139,072 basic algorithm certificates, 1,441,792 four-vertex instances of the additional-condition alternative, and 20,003 additional-condition algorithm certificates. These reproduce the reported finite checks; they do not independently establish the universal lemmas or reproduce the original discovery process. The manuscript makes that distinction correctly, and clearly separates eight-chromatic examples with obstructed precolorings from ten-chromatic graphs.

Two minor improvements would sharpen presentation. Replace “There is no bound on the number of outside neighbors collectively” with “There is no additional restriction on the boundary size”: Theorem 1 itself implies $|S|\leq16$. Also add a brief explicit comparison with [2] and [3] to the related-work discussion, since they are nearby published results on boundary extensions. Neither issue affects acceptance. The abstract is shorter than the suggested target but informative and self-contained.
