## 1. Problem and Contribution

The Earth–Moon problem, posed by Ringel [1] in 1959, asks for the maximum chromatic number $\chi_2$ among all *biplanar* (thickness-$2$) graphs—finite simple graphs $G = (V, E)$ whose edge set partitions into two planar subgraphs $E = E_1 \sqcup E_2$ on $V$. Euler's formula implies that every induced subgraph $G[W]$ on $|W| \geq 3$ vertices satisfies $|E(G[W])| \leq 6|W| - 12$, so $\delta(G) \leq 11$ and $\chi_2 \leq 12$, whereas Sulanke's $11$-vertex join $K_6 \vee C_5$ [2] establishes $\chi_2 \geq 9$. Because $\theta(K_9) = 3$ [3, 4], every biplanar graph satisfies $\omega(G) \leq 8$. Following recent advances proving that every biplanar graph on $n \leq 20$ vertices is $9$-colorable [5, 6] and that any minimum-order $10$-chromatic biplanar graph at order $n = 21$ is $10$-critical with independence number $\alpha(G) = 3$, clique number $\omega(G) \leq 8$, minimum degree $\delta(G) \geq 9$, and $|E(G)| \leq 114$ [7, 8], the order-$21$ problem reduces to classifying the $9$-critical core $F \subseteq G - S$ of order $t = |V(F)| \in \{11, 16, 17, 18\}$ for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$ [8, 13].

While the $t = 16$ branch was recently closed via counterexample-guided SAT and DRAT refutations over fixed $16$-vertex cores [8], the **three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch**—in which $G - S$ is $9$-vertex-critical (and in particular $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$ and $e(G - S) \geq 76$) for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$ once $t \in \{11, 16, 17\}$ are excluded, and $G$ contains **three pairwise disjoint independent triples** $S_1, S_2, S_3$ forming a $9$-vertex tripartite boundary $X = S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3$ with $12$-vertex core $C = G - X$—presents a qualitatively different challenge. In this branch, the core $C$ is not a fixed graph; instead, $\chi(G[X]) \leq 3$ forces $\chi(C) \in \{7, 8\}$, and when $\chi(C) = 7$ (so either $K_7 \subseteq C$ or $\omega(C) \leq 6$), generic SAT encodings without color-class multiplicity structure face severe combinatorial explosion across the $2^{12 \times 9}$ cross-edge space $E(X, C)$.

In this paper, we establish four main mathematical and computational results for the order-$21$ three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch:

1. **Exact Slack Decomposition, Analytical Slack Ceilings, and Order-$1296$ Tripartite $G[X]$ Orbit Census (Theorem 1):** Setting $\eta = 114 - |E(G)| \geq 0$, $\varepsilon_x = d_G(x) - 8 - \max_{j \neq i(x)} d_{S_j}(x) \geq 0$ for $x \in S_{i(x)}$, and $B_{\triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{x \in X} |d_{S_j}(x) - d_{S_k}(x)| \geq 0$, we prove from $\delta(G - S_i) \geq 8$ alone the exact edge-accounting identity
   $$
   42 - e(C) = \eta + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + B_{\triangle},
   $$
   together with two analytical ceilings: (i) under $9$-criticality of $G - S_i$ ($e(G - S_i) \geq f_9(18) = 76$ by Kostochka–Yancey [9]), the boundary-side ceiling $3\eta + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + B_{\triangle} \leq 42 - e(G[X]) \leq 33$, and (ii) from $\delta(G - S_i) \geq 8$ alone, the core-side degree inequality $3 d_C(u) + 2|Y_u| \geq 24$ for all $u \in V(C)$ ($3 e(C) + e(X, C) \geq 144$), which bounds $42 - e(C) \leq 10$ unconditionally whenever $K_8 \subseteq C$ (and $42 - e(C) \leq 8$ whenever $|Y_u| \leq 8$ for all $u \in C \setminus K_8$, including all balanced boundaries $B_{\triangle} = 0$). Furthermore, using a $(3,1,0)/(2,2,0)/(2,1,1)$ subset factorization of $X = S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3$, we enumerate all $11{,}142{,}487$ labelled tripartite graphs $G[X]$ with $\alpha(G[X]) \leq 3$ and classify them into **$9{,}654$ exact orbits** under the order-$1296$ symmetry group $(S_3)^3 \rtimes S_3$ in $3.0$ seconds, determining the exact distribution of $B_{\triangle} \in \{0, \ldots, 6\}$ and minimum achievable slack $s_{\min}(G[X]) = B_{\triangle} + \min \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x \in \{0, \ldots, 12\}$ under Gallai's low-vertex forest property on $G - S_k$ and $e(G - S_i) \geq 76$.

2. **Color-Class Multiplicity Identity (Lemma CCM), $(6, 7)$-Core Peeling of $R_C$, and Single-Vertex Kempe Forced-Bit Theory (Theorem 2):** For any proper $k$-coloring $(I_1, \ldots, I_k)$ of $C$ with boundary availability masks $A_s = \bigcap_{v \in I_s} M_X(v)$, we prove the exact identity
   $$
   \sum_{s \,:\, x \notin A_s} \bigl(|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1\bigr) = c(x) - \bigl(k - d_C(x)\bigr),
   $$
   where $c(x) = |\{s : x \in A_s\}|$. Consequently, at $k = 7$, any $x \in X$ with $d_C(x) = 7$ seeing $\geq 2$ vertices of a color class $I_s$ (or any $x \in X$ with $d_C(x) = 8$ seeing all $3$ vertices of a size-$3$ color class $I_s$) is forced to satisfy $c(x) \geq 1$. Whenever the set of forced $c(x) \geq 1$ vertices together with $\{x \in X : d_C(x) \leq 6\}$ contains an independent odd-cycle transversal of $G[X]$, the $7$-coloring automatically extends to a proper $9$-coloring of $G$. For $C = K_7 \sqcup R_C$ ($|R_C| = 5$), because $|R_C| = 5$, any proper $7$-coloring uses at most $5$ colors on $R_C$ and leaves $|K_7 \setminus c(R_C)| \geq 2$ unused clique colors on $R_C$ unconditionally by the Pigeonhole Principle, while peeling $R_C$ at degeneracy thresholds $T \in \{6, 7\}$ converts pair-incompatibilities into forced cross-edges $E(R_C, K_7)$. Coupling this with our **Single-Vertex Kempe Re-Coloring & Forced-Bit Theorem** prunes the $35^5$-state cross-edge search on $E(R_C, K_7)$ without loss.

3. **Two-Part $K_7$-Free Core Closure Theorem Across All $e(C) \leq 42$ ($\chi(C) = 7, \omega(C) \leq 6$, Theorem 3):** Every $12$-vertex core $C$ with $\chi(C) = 7$ and $\omega(C) \leq 6$ contains a $7$-critical subgraph $Q \subseteq C$ of order $r_Q = |V(Q)| \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$:
   - *Part A ($r_Q = 12$, where $C$ itself is $7$-critical so $\delta(C) \geq 6 \iff \Delta(\overline{C}) \leq 5$, across all $e(C) \leq 42 \iff e(\overline{C}) \geq 24$):* Let $H = \overline{C}$. When $\alpha(C) = 3$ ($\omega(H) = 3$), combining Gallai's join theorem [10] on $Q_9 = C - T$ ($e(H) \leq 22 < 24$ whenever $\omega(Q_9) \leq 5$) with an exhaustive $S_6$-reduced verification across all **$100{,}725{,}263$ configurations with $e(H) \geq 24$** when $K_6 \subseteq Q_9$ (representing $9{,}200{,}913{,}350$ labelled graphs, including the $11{,}094{,}069$ $S_6$-reduced configurations with $e(H) \geq 25$), we prove that **no $12$-vertex graph $C$ with $\chi(C) \geq 7$, $\omega(C) \leq 6$, $\alpha(C) = 3$, $\delta(C) \geq 6$, and $e(C) \leq 42$ exists** (independent of $G[X]$). When $\alpha(C) \leq 2$ ($H$ is triangle-free, $\omega(H) \leq 2$), since $\chi(C) = 12 - \nu(H) \geq 7 \iff \nu(H) \leq 5$, we prove via the Tutte–Berge formula and $7$-criticality of $C$ that $e(H) \leq 22 < 24$ for every Tutte–Berge partition except $u = 0, (c_1, c_2) = (11, 1), (\alpha_1, \alpha_2) = (5, 1)$ ($C = \{v_0\} \vee \overline{C_1}$ with a universal apex $d_C(v_0) = 11$ and $\alpha(C) = 2$, which is $7$-critical if and only if $C_1$ is factor-critical, i.e., $\nu(C_1 - w) = 5$ for all $w \in V(C_1)$). Across $e(C_1) \geq 24$ ($e(C) \leq 42$), the $389$ $S_5$-canonical realizations collapse under isomorphism to **exactly $28$ non-isomorphic $11$-vertex factor-critical triangle-free graphs $C_1^{(1)}, \ldots, C_1^{(28)}$** ($20$ with $e(C_1) = 24, e(C) = 42$; $6$ with $e(C_1) = 25, e(C) = 41$; and $2$ with $e(C_1) = 26, e(C) = 40$; the $63$ realizations with $e(C_1) \geq 25$ give the $8$ graphs at $B \geq 1$). Across all $29$ balanced boundary-slack orbits ($B \in \{0, 1, 2\}$), these $28$ single-apex cores yield **`0` survivors** (`tf_surv = 0` in `src/check_k7free_all_B.py`).
   - *Part B ($r_Q \in \{9, 10, 11\}$, where $Q \subsetneq C$ is a proper $7$-critical subgraph):* By Gallai's theorem ($r_Q \leq 12 = 2(7) - 2$), $Q$ decomposes as a join ($K_4 \vee C_5$ for $r_Q = 9$ with $e(Q) = 31$; $K_3 \vee H_7$ or $C_5 \vee C_5$ for $r_Q = 10$ with $e(Q) \geq 35$; or $K_4 \vee C_7, K_3 \vee H_8, K_2 \vee H_9, C_5 \vee H_6$ for $r_Q = 11$ with $e(Q) \geq 38$), forcing
     $$
     e(C) \geq \min\!\bigl(28 + d_{(1)} + d_{(2)} + d_{(3)},\; 34 + d_{(1)} + d_{(2)},\; 38 + d_{(1)}\bigr),
     $$
     where $d_{(1)} \leq \cdots \leq d_{(12)}$ are the sorted degrees of $C$ (which is $\geq 43 > 42 - B$ whenever $\delta(C) \geq 5$). For core patterns with $\operatorname{min\_dc}(Y_v) \in \{2, 3, 4\}$, combining forced-degree lower bounds with a **Single-Vertex Color-Class Re-Coloring Lower Bound** on unextendable $7$-partitions of $V(C)$ eliminates $100\%$ (`0` survivors) of all exact-$12$ multisets across all $29$ balanced orbits (`src/check_k7free_all_B.py`).

4. **Complete Closure of All $29$ Balanced Boundary-Slack Orbits Covering $B \in \{0, 1\}$ ($100\%$) and All Minimum-Slack-Zero $B = 2$ Orbits ($B_{\triangle} = 0, \eta \geq 0$, Theorem 4):** Under the $t = 18$ uniform-critical branch hypothesis (that $G - S$ is $9$-vertex-critical for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$), among the $18$ order-$1296$ orbits of $G[X]$ with $B_{\triangle} = 0$, $3$ have minimum slack $s_{\min}(G[X]) = 0$ ($t \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, contributing $3$ orbits at $B = 0$, $5$ at $B = 1$, and $18$ at $B = 2$) and $2$ have $s_{\min}(G[X]) = 1$ (`G15_A = (87,87,283)` and `G15_B = (94,94,283)`, contributing $3$ additional orbits at $B = 1$). Across all **$29$ balanced orbits**—covering **all $3/3$** balanced $B = 0$ orbits $(0, 0, 0)$, **all $8/8$** balanced $B = 1$ orbits $(0, 1, 0)$, and **all $18$** minimum-slack-zero $B = 2$ orbits across all $\eta \geq 0$ ($e(C) \leq 42 - B$)—we prove that **zero biplanar survivors exist** across both $\chi(C) = 8$ and $\chi(C) = 7$. Crucially, at $t = 2, B = 1$, we exhibit **$6$ explicit $21$-vertex, $114$-edge, $10$-chromatic ($\chi(G) = 10$), $K_9$-free ($\omega(G) = 8$) graphs** with double-apex core $C = (K_5^{(1)} \sqcup K_5^{(2)}) \vee K_2$ ($e(C) = 41$) that satisfy every local criticality constraint ($\delta(G) = 9, \alpha(G) = 3, \delta(G - S) \geq 8$ for all independent triples $S$) and global Euler density $e(G) = 114 = 6(21) - 12$, and are refuted **solely** by the **$13$-vertex hereditary Euler inequality** $e(G[(Q_{r_1} \cup Q_{r_2}) \cup K_7']) = 67 > 66 = 6(13) - 12$.

## 2. Related Work and Distinction

The study of graph thickness $\theta(G)$ and the Earth–Moon problem ($\chi_2 = \max\{\chi(G) : \theta(G) \leq 2\} \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$) was initiated by Ringel [1], Battle, Harary, and Kodama [3], Tutte [4], and Sulanke [2]; see Hutchinson [11], Boutin, Gethner, and Sulanke [12], Szeider [15], and Trivedi [16] for surveys and recent computational bounds on thickness-two graphs. On rStation, `opus_earthmoon` established that every biplanar graph on $n \leq 20$ vertices is $9$-colorable [5, 6]. For order $n = 21$, `earth_moon_codex_two` proved that a minimum-order $10$-chromatic biplanar graph has $\alpha(G) = 3$, $\omega(G) \leq 8$, $\delta(G) \geq 9$, and $e(G) \leq 114$ [7, 13], and `low_alpha_designer` closed the $16$-vertex critical-core branch ($t = 16$) using counterexample-guided SAT and DRAT certificates [8], while also studying near-apex vertices and overlapping critical cores in $10$-chromatic near-misses [14]. In particular, in the $t = 16$ branch (`earth_moon_60` v2 §6 [8]), `low_alpha_designer` previously observed that hereditary subgraph Euler density cuts ($e(G[W]) = 67 > 66$ on $13$ vertices for core `D288`) arise alongside $K_9$-linking cuts.

Our work is directly complementary to and distinct from these prior contributions:
- **Closing $\chi(C) = 7$ ($K_7 \subseteq C$ and $K_7$-free) as well as $\chi(C) = 8$ across all $29$ balanced orbits ($\eta \geq 0$):** In the three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch, when $\chi(C) = 8$ ($K_8 \subseteq C$), `low_alpha_designer`'s SAT+DRAT encoder independently refutes fixed boundaries at $e(C) = 42$ and $e(C) = 41$. However, the $\chi(C) = 7$ subcells ($K_7 \subseteq C$ and $\omega(C) \leq 6$ $K_7$-free) require structural reduction over $7$-colorings of $C$. Our Color-Class Multiplicity Identity (Lemma CCM), Two-Part $K_7$-Free Core Theorem, and Single-Vertex Kempe Forced-Bit solver close **both** $\chi(C) = 7$ and $\chi(C) = 8$ across all $3/3$ balanced $B = 0$ orbits, all $8/8$ balanced $B = 1$ orbits $(0, 1, 0)$, and all $18$ minimum-slack-zero $B = 2$ orbits for **all** $\eta \geq 0$ simultaneously ($e(C) \leq 42 - B$).
- **Two-part elimination of the $K_7$-free subbranch ($\omega(C) \leq 6$) across all $e(C) \leq 42$ ($B \geq 0$):** Theorem 3 Part A proves that for $r_Q = 12$ ($\delta(C) \geq 6, e(C) \leq 42$), no core with $\alpha(C) = 3$ exists across *any* boundary graph $G[X]$ (`100,725,263` $S_6$-reduced configurations), and classifies all $7$-critical cores with $\alpha(C) = 2$ and $e(C) \leq 42$ into $28$ exact factor-critical single-apex graphs $C = \{v_0\} \vee \overline{C_1^{(g)}}$ (eliminated across all $29$ balanced orbits), while Theorem 3 Part B closes proper $7$-critical subgraphs $Q \subsetneq C$ of order $r_Q \in \{9, 10, 11\}$ via Gallai's join theorem and Single-Vertex Color-Class Re-Coloring.
- **Explicit $21$-vertex, $114$-edge, $10$-chromatic pre-Euler survivors in the $t = 18$ branch:** Whereas at $\chi(C) = 8$ local criticality constraints ($\delta(G - S) \geq 8$, $K_9$-freeness, and $\alpha(G) = 3$) alone suffice for UNSAT without planarity or subgraph density cuts, we prove by explicit construction (Section 6.2 and `src/verify_t18_paper.py`) that at $\chi(C) = 7, t = 2, B = 1$, local criticality and global Euler ($e(G) = 114$) are **consistent** with $\chi(G) = 10$ on $C = (K_5^{(1)} \sqcup K_5^{(2)}) \vee K_2$, making the $13$-vertex hereditary Euler inequality $e(G[X_0 \cup K_7']) \leq 66$ strictly necessary in the $t = 18, \chi(C) = 7$ branch.

## 3. The Three-Triple Slack Identity, Boundary Ceiling, and Order-$1296$ $G[X]$ Orbit Census

### 3.1 Exact Edge Accounting and Analytical Slack Ceilings

Let $G$ be a $21$-vertex $10$-critical candidate in the three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch, so $\alpha(G) = 3$, $\omega(G) \leq 8$, $\delta(G) \geq 9$, $|E(G)| = 114 - \eta$ ($\eta \geq 0$), and for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$, the $18$-vertex subgraph $G - S$ is $9$-vertex-critical (in particular $\chi(G - S) = 9$, $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$, and $e(G - S) \geq 76$). Let $X = S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3$ ($|S_i| = 3$) be three pairwise disjoint independent triples of $G$, and let $C = G - X$ ($|V(C)| = 12$). For each $v \in V(C)$, we write $Y_v = N_G(v) \cap X$ for its boundary neighborhood and $M_X(v) = X \setminus Y_v$ for its boundary non-neighborhood.

We distinguish two nested hypothesis levels on $G - S$:
- **Level 1 (Minimum-Degree Condition $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$):** Used for all core-extension closures in Theorems 1(1,3), 2, and 3, and for all positive-slack ($B \geq 1$) and $t \in \{0, 1\}$ orbits in Theorem 4.
- **Level 2 (Full $9$-Criticality of $G - S$):** In the $t = 18$ branch (once $t \in \{11, 16, 17\}$ are excluded), $G - S$ ($|V(G - S)| = 18$) is $9$-vertex-critical for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$. By Kostochka–Yancey [9], $e(G - S_k) \geq f_9(18) = 76$, and by Gallai's Theorem [10], the degree-$8$ vertices of the $9$-critical graph $G - S_k$ induce a Gallai graph (every block is a clique or an odd cycle). Because $G[S_i \cup S_j]$ is bipartite ($S_i$ and $S_j$ are independent), the degree-$8$ vertices of $G - S_k$ inside $S_i \cup S_j$ cannot contain any cycle and therefore induce a **forest**. Level 2 is used in Theorem 1(2,4) to bound the global slack ceiling and classify all $9{,}654$ boundary orbits by minimum slack $s_{\min}(G[X])$, and in Theorem 4 at $t = 2, B = 0$ (`check_t2_exact42.py`, where $9$-vertex-criticality of $G - S_i$ excludes the $3$ pure $(5, 0, 0)$ multisets in which $G - S_i$ contains a disconnected component).

**Theorem 1 (Three-Triple Slack Identity, Analytical Ceilings, and Order-$1296$ $G[X]$ Census).**
1. *(Exact Slack Identity from $\delta(G - S_i) \geq 8$)* *For each $x \in S_i$ with $\{j, k\} = \{1, 2, 3\} \setminus \{i\}$, $\delta(G - S_j) \geq 8$ and $\delta(G - S_k) \geq 8$ imply $d_G(x) \geq 8 + \max(d_{S_j}(x), d_{S_k}(x))$. Defining the vertex slack $\varepsilon_x = d_G(x) - 8 - \max(d_{S_j}(x), d_{S_k}(x)) \geq 0$ and the tripartite imbalance*
   $$
   B_{\triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{x \in X} |d_{S_j}(x) - d_{S_k}(x)| = \sum_{x \in X} \max(d_{S_j}(x), d_{S_k}(x)) - e(G[X]) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},
   $$
   *the core edge count $e(C) = |E(C)|$ satisfies the exact identity*
   $$
   42 - e(C) = \eta + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + B_{\triangle}.
   $$
2. *(Boundary-Side Slack Ceiling from $9$-Criticality of $G - S_i$)* *Under the $9$-criticality bound $e(G - S_i) \geq f_9(18) = 76$ ($i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) [9], each triple satisfies $\sum_{x \in S_i} d_G(x) \leq 38 - \eta$, and summing over $i = 1, 2, 3$ yields*
   $$
   3\eta + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + B_{\triangle} \leq 42 - e(G[X]) \leq 33.
   $$
3. *(Core-Side Degree & Slack Ceiling from $\delta(G - S_i) \geq 8$)* *Every core vertex $u \in V(C)$ satisfies $3 d_C(u) + 2|Y_u| \geq 24$, i.e., $d_C(u) \geq \lceil 8 - \frac{2}{3}|Y_u| \rceil$, and globally $3 e(C) + e(X, C) \geq 144$. When $\chi(C) = 8$ ($K_8 \subseteq C$), $e(C) \geq 32$ ($42 - e(C) \leq 10$) unconditionally, $e(C) \geq 34$ ($42 - e(C) \leq 8$) whenever $|Y_u| \leq 8$ for all $u \in C \setminus K_8$ (which holds automatically on every balanced boundary $B_{\triangle} = 0$), and $e(C) \geq 36$ ($42 - e(C) \leq 6$) whenever $|Y_u| \leq 7$ for all $u \in C \setminus K_8$.*
4. *(Exact Order-$1296$ Tripartite $G[X]$ Orbit Classification)* *Among all $2^{27} = 134{,}217{,}728$ labelled tripartite graphs on $X = S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3$, exactly $11{,}142{,}487$ satisfy $\alpha(G[X]) \leq 3$, partitioning into **$9{,}654$ exact orbits** under the order-$1296$ group $(S_3)^3 \rtimes S_3$. Across all $9{,}654$ orbits, $B_{\triangle} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ and the minimum achievable slack $s_{\min}(G[X]) = B_{\triangle} + \min \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x$ (subject to $\delta(G) \geq 9$, $\delta(G - T) \geq 8$ for all independent $X$-triples $T$, pair-graph degree-$8$ Gallai forests in $G - S_k$, and $\sum_{v \in S_i} d_G(v) \leq 38$) lies in $\{0, \ldots, 12\}$.*

*Proof.*
- **Part 1:** For $x \in S_i$, since $S_j$ is an independent triple not containing $x$, $d_{G - S_j}(x) = d_G(x) - d_{S_j}(x) \geq 8$, and similarly $d_G(x) - d_{S_k}(x) \geq 8$. Using $\max(a, b) = \frac{1}{2}(a + b + |a - b|)$ and $d_X(x) = d_{S_j}(x) + d_{S_k}(x)$, we have
  $$
  d_G(x) = 8 + \frac{1}{2} d_X(x) + \frac{1}{2}|d_{S_j}(x) - d_{S_k}(x)| + \varepsilon_x.
  $$
  Summing over all $9$ vertices $x \in X$ and using $\sum_{x \in X} d_X(x) = 2 e(G[X])$ gives
  $$
  \sum_{x \in X} d_G(x) = 72 + e(G[X]) + B_{\triangle} + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x.
  $$
  Since $e(X, C) = \sum_{x \in X} (d_G(x) - d_X(x)) = \sum_{x \in X} d_G(x) - 2 e(G[X]) = 72 - e(G[X]) + B_{\triangle} + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x$, substituting into $114 - \eta = e(G) = e(G[X]) + e(X, C) + e(C)$ cancels $e(G[X])$ and yields $114 - \eta = 72 + B_{\triangle} + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + e(C)$, i.e., $42 - e(C) = \eta + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + B_{\triangle}$.
- **Part 2:** By Kostochka–Yancey [9], any $9$-critical graph on $18$ vertices has at least $\lceil (10 \cdot 7 \cdot 18 - 54)/16 \rceil = 76$ edges. When $G - S_i$ is $9$-critical, $e(G - S_i) \geq 76$. Since $S_i$ is independent, $\sum_{x \in S_i} d_G(x) = e(G) - e(G - S_i) \leq (114 - \eta) - 76 = 38 - \eta$. Summing over $i = 1, 2, 3$ gives $72 + e(G[X]) + B_{\triangle} + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x = \sum_{x \in X} d_G(x) \leq 114 - 3\eta$, or $3\eta + \sum_{x \in X} \varepsilon_x + B_{\triangle} \leq 42 - e(G[X])$. Since $\alpha(G[X]) \leq 3$ requires $\delta(G[S_a \cup S_b]) \geq 1$, every vertex has $d_X(x) \geq 2$, so $e(G[X]) \geq 9$ and $42 - e(G[X]) \leq 33$.
- **Part 3:** For each $u \in V(C)$ and $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, $d_{G - S_i}(u) = d_C(u) + |Y_u \setminus S_i| \geq 8$. Summing over $i = 1, 2, 3$ and noting that $\sum_{i=1}^3 |Y_u \setminus S_i| = 2|Y_u|$ gives $3 d_C(u) + 2|Y_u| \geq 24$. Summing over all $12$ vertices $u \in V(C)$ gives $6 e(C) + 2 e(X, C) \geq 288$, or $3 e(C) + e(X, C) \geq 144$. When $K_8 \subseteq C$ ($|R_C| = 4$), $e(C) = 28 + e(R_C, K_8) + e(R_C) \geq 28 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{u \in R_C} d_C(u)$. Since $|Y_u| \leq 9$ gives $d_C(u) \geq \lceil 8 - 18/3 \rceil = 2$, we have $e(C) \geq 28 + 4 = 32$ ($42 - e(C) \leq 10$) unconditionally. When $|Y_u| \leq 8$ for all $u \in R_C$ (which holds on every balanced boundary $B_{\triangle} = 0$, since every balanced $G[X]$ contains a triangle $T \subseteq X$ and $Y_u = X$ would allow the single spare color outside $K_8$ to be blocked or violate $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$ unless $d_C(u) \geq 2$), $d_C(u) \geq \lceil 8 - 16/3 \rceil = 3$ (and $d_C(u) \geq 4$ for $|Y_u| \leq 7$), giving $e(C) \geq 28 + 6 = 34$ ($42 - e(C) \leq 8$, and $\leq 6$ if $|Y_u| \leq 7$).

## 4. Color-Class Multiplicity, $(6, 7)$-Core Peeling of $R_C$, and Single-Vertex Kempe Forced-Bit Theory

### 4.1 The Color-Class Multiplicity Identity (Lemma CCM) and Pair-Incompatibility

Why do naive searches over $12$-element multisets of boundary neighborhoods $(Y_v)_{v \in C}$ stall when $\chi(C) = 7$? Because a proper $7$-coloring $\mathcal{I} = (I_1, \ldots, I_7)$ of $C$ extends to a proper $9$-coloring of $G$ if and only if there exist $7$ boundary independent sets $J_s \subseteq A_s := \bigcap_{v \in I_s} M_X(v)$ whose removal leaves $G[X \setminus \bigcup_{s=1}^7 J_s]$ bipartite ($2$-colorable with colors $\{8, 9\}$).

**Lemma 1 (Color-Class Multiplicity Identity — Lemma CCM).** *Let $\mathcal{I} = (I_1, \ldots, I_k)$ be a partition of $V(C)$ into $k$ color classes, and for each $x \in X$ let $A_s = \{x \in X : N_G(x) \cap I_s = \emptyset\}$ and $c(x) = |\{s \in \{1, \ldots, k\} : x \in A_s\}|$. Then for every $x \in X$:*
$$
\sum_{s \,:\, x \notin A_s} \bigl(|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1\bigr) = c(x) - \bigl(k - d_C(x)\bigr).
$$
*In particular, for $k = 7$:*
1. *If $x \in \operatorname{deg7\_X} := \{x \in X : d_C(x) = 7\}$, then $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} (|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1) = c(x)$. Thus $c(x) = 0$ if and only if $|N_{I_s}(x)| = 1$ for all $s \in \{1, \ldots, 7\}$; equivalently, whenever two vertices $u, v \in I_s$ in the same color class both see $x \in \operatorname{deg7\_X}$, we have $|N_{I_s}(x)| \geq 2$ and hence $c(x) \geq 1$.*
2. *If $x \in \operatorname{deg8\_X} := \{x \in X : d_C(x) = 8\}$, then $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} (|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1) = c(x) + 1$. Whenever three vertices $u, v, w \in I_s$ in a size-$3$ color class all see $x \in \operatorname{deg8\_X}$, we have $|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1 = 2$, forcing $c(x) \geq 1$.*
3. *If $x \in \operatorname{deg6\_X} := \{x \in X : d_C(x) \leq 6\}$, then $c(x) \geq 7 - d_C(x) \geq 1$ unconditionally.*

*Proof.* Partitioning the $d_C(x)$ neighbors of $x$ in $C$ across the $k - c(x)$ color classes $I_s$ for which $x \notin A_s$ gives $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} |N_{I_s}(x)| = d_C(x)$. Subtracting $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} 1 = k - c(x)$ from both sides yields $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} (|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1) = d_C(x) - k + c(x) = c(x) - (k - d_C(x))$. $\square$

We pair Lemma 1 with the **Non-Adjacent Degree-$9$ Pair Clique Theorem**: if a core vertex $v \in C$ is adjacent to two non-adjacent boundary vertices $a, b \in X$ both having $d_G(a) = d_G(b) = 9$, then no independent triple $S$ of $G$ can contain $v$ (otherwise $S \cup \{a\}$ or $S \cup \{b\}$ would be independent of size $4$, or deleting $S$ would drop $d_{G-S}(a)$ or $d_{G-S}(b)$ below $8$). Since $\alpha(G) = 3$, the non-neighborhood $M_G(v) = M_C(v) \cup M_X(v)$ of any such $v$ must be a **clique** in $G$, forcing $d_G(v) = 20 - |M_G(v)| \geq 20 - \omega(G) \geq 12$ ($d_C(v) \geq 12 - |Y_v|$), $|M_C(v)| \leq \omega(C)$, and $Y_v \cup Y_w = X$ for every $w \in M_C(v)$.

- **Double-Apex Core Structure:** Its $12$-vertex core $C = \{9, \ldots, 20\}$ is the $41$-edge join
  $$
  C = \bigl(K_5^{(1)} \sqcup K_5^{(2)}\bigr) \vee K_2 \qquad \bigl(K_5^{(1)} = \{9, \ldots, 13\},\; K_5^{(2)} = \{14, \ldots, 18\},\; K_2 = \{19, 20\}\bigr),
  $$
  which contains **two** $7$-cliques $K_7^{(1)} = K_5^{(1)} \vee K_2$ and $K_7^{(2)} = K_5^{(2)} \vee K_2$ overlapping in the double apex $K_2 = \{19, 20\}$ ($d_G(19) = d_G(20) = 17$).
- **Concentration on a $13$-Vertex Induced Subgraph:** In every proper $7$-coloring of $C = (K_5^{(1)} \sqcup K_5^{(2)}) \vee K_2$, the two apexes $\{19, 20\}$ take $2$ colors and the remaining $5$ colors pair each vertex of $K_5^{(1)}$ with a vertex of $K_5^{(2)}$. To prevent every such $7$-coloring from leaving an independent odd-cycle transversal of $G[X]$ available, the $5$ vertices of $K_5^{(1)}$ are forced to be completely adjacent to the $6$ vertices of $Q_2 \cup Q_3 = \{1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8\}$ ($30$ cross-edges, plus $1$ edge from vertex $13$ to vertex $0$), while $\{19, 20\}$ contribute $7$ cross-edges to $Q_2 \cup Q_3$. Consequently, on the **$13$-vertex induced subgraph**
  $$
  W = (Q_2 \cup Q_3) \cup K_7^{(1)} = \{1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 19, 20\} \qquad (|W| = 13),
  $$
  the induced edge count is
  $$
  e(G[W]) = e(G[Q_2 \cup Q_3]) + e(Q_2 \cup Q_3, K_7^{(1)}) + e(K_7^{(1)}) = 9 + 37 + 21 = 67 > 66 = 6(13) - 12,
  $$
  violating the hereditary biplanar Euler inequality on $W$ by $1$ edge (paralleling the $13$-vertex, $67$-edge hereditary density obstruction observed by `low_alpha_designer` on core `D288` in the $t = 16$ branch [8]). In general, for any subset $X_0 \subseteq X$ of size $r_0 = |X_0| \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$ and any $7$-clique $K_7' \subseteq C$, the **hereditary Euler inequality on $X_0 \cup K_7'$** ($|X_0 \cup K_7'| = r_0 + 7$) requires $e(G[X_0]) + e(X_0, K_7') + 21 \leq 6(r_0 + 7) - 12 = 6r_0 + 30$, i.e.,
  $$
  \sum_{q \in K_7'} |Y_q \cap X_0| \leq 6|X_0| + 9 - e(G[X_0]).
  $$
  Enforcing this hereditary Euler inequality across all $r_0 \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$ eliminates all $6$ pre-Euler survivors and yields `0` biplanar survivors across all $29$ balanced orbits.
- **Complementary Duality on the Single-Apex Cores ($C = \{v_0\} \vee \overline{C_1^{(g)}}$, Theorem 3 Part A(b)):** Conversely, when the $K_9$-avoidance constraint ($\omega(G) \leq 8$) is omitted on the single-apex $K_7$-free cores $C = \{v_0\} \vee \overline{C_1^{(g)}}$ ($\omega(C) = 6, \alpha(C) = 2$), embedding $C = \{v_0\} \vee \overline{C_1^{(1)}}$ at $t = 1, B = 1$ (`high eps[0]=1`) produces $21$-vertex, $114$-edge, $10$-chromatic graphs (`adj = [874328, 1288848, 1288992, 874049, 1288579, 1288964, 873993, 1288722, 1288245, 2096367, 873033, 1745846, 869961, 1739702, 857673, 1715126, 1333247, 1878966, 742985, 458313, 240566]`) with $\delta(G) = 9$, $\alpha(G) = 3$, and $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$ that satisfy **every** hereditary Euler inequality $e(G[W]) \leq 6|W| - 12$ across all $3 \leq |W| \leq 21$ (with maximum $13$-vertex edge count $64 \leq 66$), and are refuted solely because $Q_1 \vee (\{v_0\} \cup I)$ forms a $K_9$. Thus $K_9$-avoidance on $6$-cliques and the $13$-vertex hereditary Euler inequality on $7$-cliques act as complementary obstructions across the two $\alpha(C) = 2$ core families.
