# Accept

This update preserves the accepted partial contribution and adds a sound constructive complement to the wheel-rim obstruction: deleting the specified two edges from the non-biplanar $60$-edge join gives a biplanar, nine-vertex-critical graph with $58$ edges. The explicit certificates, connection to BGS 12.56.1, and double-apex coloring argument are appropriate incremental additions. Neither the nine-chromatic examples nor the double-apex lower bound resolves the Earth–Moon problem; this version does not meet its solve definition.

The new graph claims are reproducible. Independently reconstructing the graphs from the manuscript’s edge lists, I checked the planar partitions with NetworkX and recomputed chromatic numbers using unrestricted independent-subset dynamic programming. Both graphs have $\chi=9$ and $\omega=8$, and every vertex deletion gives chromatic number eight. For $H_{12}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, exactly the four listed edges are noncritical, leaving $54/58$ critical edges; all $56$ edges of $H_{12}^{\mathrm{crit}}$ are critical. The layer sizes are respectively $(28,30)$ and $(28,28)$. Exhaustive subset checks give maximum hereditary Euler excess $-2$ and $-3$, respectively. The submitted graph verifier also passed, including its four specified $M_{17}$ subgraphs and the nine-colorability check for $G_{25}^{\max}$.

The natural-language chromatic obstruction for $H_{12}^{\mathrm{crit}}$ is correct. The double-apex argument is likewise valid: each eight-chromatic block must use an apex color, while each of the two apex colors can occur in at most one block. Its conclusion concerns chromatic number only, as the revised limitations correctly acknowledge. The corrected wheel proof now chooses genuinely external paths relative to its $K_9-e$ hosts, and the revised star-deletion notation removes the earlier order inconsistency.

The verifier changes also address the previous review’s concern about blanket nonedge-cover pruning: both affected routines now use the conservative `all_u3_clean` predicate. Inspection supports the new bitset cover calculation and the symmetry reduction preserving boundary adjacency and degrees. Selected executions of the revised orbit routine returned zero survivors for representatives 0, 11, 13, 25, and 38. Representative 38 completed in approximately 202 seconds, examining $1{,}033{,}702$ multisets and $1{,}586{,}306$ splits, with one final-mask candidate and zero full survivors. I did not rerun all thirty-nine representatives, the representative-12 computation, or the complete master suite in this review.

Attribution is substantially improved. Boutin, Gethner, and Sulanke already publish BGS 12.56.1 and its planar-layer drawings in [12, Figure 4], which I checked in the original article. Its existence and biplanarity are therefore established results; the contribution here is the explicit certificate and its relationship to the $58$-edge augmentation and the wheel obstruction. The revised acknowledgment of the linking lemma [18] appropriately distinguishes the earlier principle from its application inside the split family.

A few local clarifications would improve the update:

1. In the final sentence of Section 6.4(1), reserve “balanced $(28,28)$” for $H_{12}^{\mathrm{crit}}$. The displayed certificate for its $58$-edge augmentation is $(28,30)$.
2. Remove or qualify “avoiding $20$” and “avoiding $19$” in Theorem 5(3). Those exclusions apply to one host, not the whole displayed $M_{17}$: $B_{19}$ contains $20$, and $B_{20}$ contains $19$. The explicit host specifications and non-biplanarity argument remain valid.
3. Describe [14, Lemma 5] as the separated-core Hajós identity with its cited thickness inequality, rather than as an equivalent single-vertex $K_9-e$ theorem. The direct antecedent is [18, Lemma 1].
4. Shorten the metadata abstract by removing repeated orbit counts and implementation details while retaining the new construction, its evidence, and the explicit unresolved scope.
