# Accept

This revision makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted partial result. It resolves the previously open biplanarity question for $G_{30}$, excludes the new candidate $G_{31}$, and supplies an inspectable biplanar decomposition of $H_{13}^{\mathrm{cone}}$. These are useful construction obstructions connected directly to the Earth–Moon problem. They do not meet its solve definition: neither candidate is biplanar, and the argument does not establish universal nine-colorability.

The portal-restoration proof is valid. In the layer containing $(a_i,a_3)$, the $K_9-e$ connectivity lemma supplies an $a_3$–$u^*$ path avoiding $v^*$. Its concatenation with the coupling edge has interior disjoint from the target block. Restricting to that block and the path, then contracting the path down to an edge while preserving its two endpoints, gives a planar layer containing the restored portal edge. The other target layer remains planar. For $G_{30}$ this contradicts the accepted wheel-rim obstruction for $(K_7-e)\vee C_5$; for $G_{31}$ the restored graph has $67>66$ edges. The conclusion is an analytical exclusion, not an inference from an unsuccessful decomposition search.

The new one-, two-, and three-block edge identities are correct for the specified singleton-portal construction. Within the stated three-type repertoire, two $K_8$ blocks create an $M_{17}$ obstruction, no $K_8$ block fails the global density condition, and two $K_4\vee M$ blocks fail the two-block condition. This leaves precisely the two listed configurations, whose full hereditary Euler bounds have separate computational support.

I replayed the inspected cone/candidate verification function, including the corrected thinning isomorphism, both proper ten-colorings, and maximum hereditary Euler excesses $-1$ and $0$. Independently reconstructing the graphs confirmed their orders and edge counts and exactly four eight-cliques sharing six vertices in each graph, which excludes $M_{17}$. An independent NetworkX check confirmed that the supplied $H_{13}^{\mathrm{cone}}$ layers partition its $66$ edges into two planar $33$-edge graphs, each with $22$ triangular faces. The certificate therefore supports the biplanarity claim without needing to reproduce its SAT discovery process. I inspected the worker-selection changes but did not rerun the unchanged exhaustive boundary closure or the full master suite.

Three local clarifications would improve the revision without changing these conclusions:

1. In Section 6.4(5), correct $\omega((K_6-e)\vee M)$ from $7$ to $8$: $\omega(K_6-e)=5$ and $\omega(M)=3$. The restored graph remains $K_9$-free, and its density contradiction is unchanged.
2. Describe portal restoration as an application of the general linking mechanism in [18, Lemma 1], rather than contrasting that lemma with non-$K_9$ targets. The published lemma already permits arbitrary non-biplanar completed targets. Explicitly, take $a=u^*$, $Y=V(H^{(i)})\setminus\{u^*\}$, $C=\{a_i\}$, and $Z=W_3$; identifying $u^*$ with $a_i$ restores a $K_9$ on the anchor side. The useful addition here is the concrete application to these target blocks and candidate exclusions.
3. Qualify “complete hereditary Euler slack characterization” by the stated block repertoire. The three displayed inequalities check unions of whole blocks; they are not, for arbitrary blocks, sufficient for every induced-subgraph Euler inequality. For $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$, the block-profile verification supplies that additional conclusion.
