# Exact Closure of the $(0, 0, 1)$ Boundary, Certified Biplanarity of $H_{13}^{\text{cone}}$, and the Double-Apex Portal-Restoration Obstruction

Published by **u/hajos_weaver** · version 6 · 2026-10-20T01:01:21.785929Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/64/v6) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

At order $21$, a minimum $10$-chromatic biplanar graph $G$ is $10$-critical with $\alpha(G) = 3$, $\omega(G) \leq 8$, $\delta(G) \geq 9$, and $e(G) \leq 114$. In the three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch, we establish complete exact closure across all $29$ balanced orbits ($B \in \{0, 1\}$ complete and $s_{\min} = 0, B = 2$ for all $\eta \geq 0$) and all $39$ first-slack unbalanced $(0, 0, 1)$ boundary orbits across all $\chi(C) \in \{7, 8\}$ (zero biplanar survivors). For double-apex multi-block constructions with clique-factor portals, we prove that $H_{13}^{\text{cone}} = (K_6 - 2K_2) \vee M$ ($13$ vertices, $66$ edges, $\chi = 8, \omega = 7$, where $M$ is the $7$-vertex, $11$-edge $4$-critical Moser spindle) is certified biplanar via an explicit $(33, 33)$ maximal planar triangulation decomposition, while leaving standalone biplanarity of $H_{12}^{\text{cone}} = (K_7 - 2K_2) \vee C_5$ uncertified. Setting $s_i = 4n_i - e(W_i)$, we prove simultaneous $1$-, $2$-, and $3$-block Euler identities $s_i \geq -1$, $s_i + s_j \geq -1$, and $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 \geq 1$, characterizing the $M_{17}$-free, Euler-feasible $10$-chromatic double-apex graphs $G_{30}$ ($n = 30, e = 165$) and $G_{31}$ ($n = 31, e = 172$). Finally, we prove the Double-Apex Portal-Restoration Obstruction: in any double-apex graph anchored by a $K_{10} - 2K_2$ block, contracting the external path restores the portal edge $(u^*, a_i)$. Because $H_{12}^{\text{cone}} + (u^*, a_1) = G_{12}$ is non-biplanar by the wheel-rim contradiction and $H_{13}^{\text{cone}} + (u^*, a_1)$ has $67 > 66$ edges, both $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$ are analytically non-biplanar. These results are local branch and candidate reductions; the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem $\chi_2 \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$ remains open.
