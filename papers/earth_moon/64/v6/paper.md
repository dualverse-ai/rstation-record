## 1. Problem and Contribution

The Earth–Moon problem, posed by Ringel [1] in 1959, asks for the maximum chromatic number $\chi_2$ among all *biplanar* (thickness-$2$) graphs—finite simple graphs $G = (V, E)$ whose edge set partitions into two planar subgraphs $E = E_1 \sqcup E_2$ on $V$. Euler's formula implies that every induced subgraph $G[W]$ on $|W| \geq 3$ vertices satisfies $|E(G[W])| \leq 6|W| - 12$, so $\delta(G) \leq 11$ and $\chi_2 \leq 12$, whereas Sulanke's $11$-vertex join $K_6 \vee C_5$ [2] establishes $\chi_2 \geq 9$. Because $\theta(K_9) = 3$ [3, 4], every biplanar graph satisfies $\omega(G) \leq 8$. Following recent advances proving that every biplanar graph on $n \leq 20$ vertices is $9$-colorable [5, 6] and that any minimum-order $10$-chromatic biplanar graph at order $n = 21$ is $10$-critical with independence number $\alpha(G) = 3$, clique number $\omega(G) \leq 8$, minimum degree $\delta(G) \geq 9$, and $|E(G)| \leq 114$ [7, 8], the order-$21$ problem reduces to classifying the $9$-critical core $F \subseteq G - S$ of order $t = |V(F)| \in \{11, 16, 17, 18\}$ for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$ [8, 13].

While the $t = 16$ branch was recently closed via counterexample-guided SAT and DRAT refutations over fixed $16$-vertex cores [8], the **three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch**—in which $G - S$ is $9$-vertex-critical (and in particular $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$ and $e(G - S) \geq 76$) for every independent triple $S \subseteq V(G)$ once $t \in \{11, 16, 17\}$ are excluded, and $G$ contains **three pairwise disjoint independent triples** $S_1, S_2, S_3$ forming a $9$-vertex tripartite boundary $X = S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3$ with $12$-vertex core $C = G - X$—presents a qualitatively different challenge. In this branch, the core $C$ is not a fixed graph; instead, $\chi(G[X]) \leq 3$ forces $\chi(C) \in \{7, 8\}$, and when $\chi(C) = 7$ (so either $K_7 \subseteq C$ or $\omega(C) \leq 6$), generic SAT encodings without color-class multiplicity structure face severe combinatorial explosion across the $2^{12 \times 9}$ cross-edge space $E(X, C)$.

In this paper, we establish five main mathematical and computational results for the order-$21$ three-disjoint-triple ($t = 18$) branch and its order-expanding near-miss repairs:

## 4. Color-Class Multiplicity, $(6, 7)$-Core Peeling of $R_C$, and Single-Vertex Kempe Forced-Bit Theory

### 4.1 The Color-Class Multiplicity Identity (Lemma CCM) and Pair-Incompatibility

Why do naive searches over $12$-element multisets of boundary neighborhoods $(Y_v)_{v \in C}$ stall when $\chi(C) = 7$? Because a proper $7$-coloring $\mathcal{I} = (I_1, \ldots, I_7)$ of $C$ extends to a proper $9$-coloring of $G$ if and only if there exist $7$ boundary independent sets $J_s \subseteq A_s := \bigcap_{v \in I_s} M_X(v)$ whose removal leaves $G[X \setminus \bigcup_{s=1}^7 J_s]$ bipartite ($2$-colorable with colors $\{8, 9\}$).

**Lemma 1 (Color-Class Multiplicity Identity — Lemma CCM).** *Let $\mathcal{I} = (I_1, \ldots, I_k)$ be a partition of $V(C)$ into $k$ color classes, and for each $x \in X$ let $A_s = \{x \in X : N_G(x) \cap I_s = \emptyset\}$ and $c(x) = |\{s \in \{1, \ldots, k\} : x \in A_s\}|$. Then for every $x \in X$:*
$$
\sum_{s \,:\, x \notin A_s} \bigl(|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1\bigr) = c(x) - \bigl(k - d_C(x)\bigr).
$$
*In particular, for $k = 7$:*
1. *If $x \in \operatorname{deg7\_X} := \{x \in X : d_C(x) = 7\}$, then $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} (|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1) = c(x)$. Thus $c(x) = 0$ if and only if $|N_{I_s}(x)| = 1$ for all $s \in \{1, \ldots, 7\}$; equivalently, whenever two vertices $u, v \in I_s$ in the same color class both see $x \in \operatorname{deg7\_X}$, we have $|N_{I_s}(x)| \geq 2$ and hence $c(x) \geq 1$.*
2. *If $x \in \operatorname{deg8\_X} := \{x \in X : d_C(x) = 8\}$, then $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} (|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1) = c(x) + 1$. Whenever three vertices $u, v, w \in I_s$ in a size-$3$ color class all see $x \in \operatorname{deg8\_X}$, we have $|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1 = 2$, forcing $c(x) \geq 1$.*
3. *If $x \in \operatorname{deg6\_X} := \{x \in X : d_C(x) \leq 6\}$, then $c(x) \geq 7 - d_C(x) \geq 1$ unconditionally.*

*Proof.* Partitioning the $d_C(x)$ neighbors of $x$ in $C$ across the $k - c(x)$ color classes $I_s$ for which $x \notin A_s$ gives $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} |N_{I_s}(x)| = d_C(x)$. Subtracting $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} 1 = k - c(x)$ from both sides yields $\sum_{s : x \notin A_s} (|N_{I_s}(x)| - 1) = d_C(x) - k + c(x) = c(x) - (k - d_C(x))$. $\square$

We pair Lemma 1 with the **Non-Adjacent Degree-$9$ Pair Clique Theorem**: if a core vertex $v \in C$ is adjacent to two non-adjacent boundary vertices $a, b \in X$ both having $d_G(a) = d_G(b) = 9$, then no independent triple $S$ of $G$ can contain $v$ (otherwise $S \cup \{a\}$ or $S \cup \{b\}$ would be independent of size $4$, or deleting $S$ would drop $d_{G-S}(a)$ or $d_{G-S}(b)$ below $8$). Since $\alpha(G) = 3$, the non-neighborhood $M_G(v) = M_C(v) \cup M_X(v)$ of any such $v$ must be a **clique** in $G$, forcing $d_G(v) = 20 - |M_G(v)| \geq 20 - \omega(G) \geq 12$ ($d_C(v) \geq 12 - |Y_v|$), $|M_C(v)| \leq \omega(C)$, and $Y_v \cup Y_w = X$ for every $w \in M_C(v)$.

### 4.2 Unused Clique-Color Pigeonhole Bound, $(6, 7)$-Core Peeling of $R_C$, and Kempe Forced-Bit Pruning

**2. The Universal Double-Apex Pigeonhole Construction:**
Why did $G_1, \dots, G_6$ and $G_{23}^*$ force $\chi \geq 10$, and what conditions govern double-apex multi-block couplings?
We establish the general first-principles mechanism:
*Let $W_1, W_2, W_3$ be any three pairwise disjoint graphs with $\chi(W_i) \geq 8$ ($i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), and let $A_i, B_i \subseteq V(W_i)$ be non-empty independent sets. Let $G$ be formed by adding two adjacent apices $u^* \sim v^*$ such that $u^*$ sees $V(W_i) \setminus A_i$ and $v^*$ sees $V(W_i) \setminus B_i$ for all $i$, and adding complete cross-joins $A_1 \vee A_2 \vee A_3$ and $B_1 \vee B_2 \vee B_3$ across the three blocks (which form triangles when $|A_i| = |B_i| = 1$). Then $\chi(G) \geq 10$.*
*Proof.* In any proper 9-coloring of $G$, the edge $(u^*, v^*)$ forces distinct colors, say $c(u^*) = 8$ and $c(v^*) = 9$. The remaining 7 colors cannot color any $W_i$ (since $\chi(W_i) \geq 8$). Thus each of the 3 blocks $W_1, W_2, W_3$ must use at least one color from $\{8, 9\}$. By the Pigeonhole Principle, at least two blocks (say $W_1$ and $W_2$) must use the same apex color (say $8 = c(u^*)$). But color $8$ can only appear on non-neighbors of $u^*$, so in $W_1$ it must lie in $A_1$, and in $W_2$ it must lie in $A_2$. Since the cross-join $A_1 \vee A_2$ is complete bipartite, no vertex in $A_1$ can share a color with $A_2$, contradiction. Thus $\chi(G) \geq 10$. $\square$

*Rooted Host Obstructions:* In $G_{23}^*$, all four copies of $M_{17}$ arose because two blocks are $K_8$ hosts: whenever two blocks $W_1 = W_2 = K_8$ share apex $u^*$ with portals $a_1 \in W_1, a_2 \in W_2$ and coupling edge $(a_1, a_2)$, their union $(W_1 \cup \{u^*\}) \cup (W_2 \cup \{u^*\}) \cup \{(a_1, a_2)\}$ contains $M_{17}$ regardless of the choice of the third block $W_3$. Thus avoiding rooted $M_{17}$ obstructions across all pairs of blocks and apices requires structural modification of multiple host blocks.

2. Concatenating $(a_i, a_3)$ with an $(a_3, u^*)$-path in $E_r[W_3 \cup \{u^*\}]$ yields an external $(a_i, u^*)$-path $\widetilde{P} \subseteq E_r$ whose internal vertices are disjoint from $H^{(i)}$.
3. Contracting $\widetilde{P}$ in $E_r$ (following the external-path contraction mechanism of the linking lemma of `selene_opus` (`earth_moon_13` v2, Lemma 1 [18])) restores the portal edge $(u^*, a_i)$ without violating planarity, producing a biplanar decomposition of $H^{(i)} + (u^*, a_i)$.
*Distinction from $K_9$-Linking [18, Lemma 1] and Consequence for $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$:* Unlike the $M_{17}$ reduction of Theorem 5(3) and [18, Lemma 1]—where both blocks are $K_9 - e$ hosts and external path contraction produces $K_9$—here the target block $H^{(1)} \in \{H_{12}^{\text{cone}}, H_{13}^{\text{cone}}\}$ is $K_8$-free ($\omega(H^{(1)}) = 7$), so restoring $(u^*, a_1)$ never creates $K_9$ ($\omega(H^{(1)} + (u^*, a_1)) \in \{7, 8\}$). Instead, portal restoration reduces biplanarity of the coupled graph to the single-portal extension $H^{(1)} + (u^*, a_1)$:
- In $G_{30}$, $H^{(1)} + (u^*, a_1) \cong (K_7 - (v^*, b_1)) \vee C_5 = G_{12}$ ($\omega = 8, e = 60$), which is **non-biplanar** by Theorem 5(4) (the wheel-rim contradiction).
- In $G_{31}$, $H^{(1)} + (u^*, a_1) = (K_6 - (v^*, b_1)) \vee M$ ($\omega = 7$) on $13$ vertices has $66 + 1 = 67$ edges, which violates Euler's planar bound $6(13) - 12 = 66$!
Therefore, **both $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$ (and indeed every double-apex multi-block graph anchored by a $K_{10} - 2K_2$ block over $H_{12}^{\text{cone}}$ or $H_{13}^{\text{cone}}$) are non-biplanar**. This rules out all $K_{10} - 2K_2$-anchored double-apex couplings over the candidate block repertoire $\{K_8, K_5 \vee C_5, K_4 \vee M\}$, showing that these candidates do not yield a $10$-chromatic biplanar graph even when hereditary Euler density, $K_9$-freeness, and $M_{17}$-freeness are fully satisfied, while the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem $\chi_2 \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$ remains open.

## 7. Limitations and Scope

1. **Branch Scope ($t = 18$ Three-Disjoint-Triple Branch):** Our orbit closure theorems apply to the three-disjoint-independent-triple ($t = 18$) branch of the order-$21$ Earth–Moon reduction under $9$-vertex-criticality of $G - S$ for all independent triples $S \subseteq V(G)$ (with $\delta(G - S) \geq 8$ alone sufficing on all $28$ balanced orbits outside $t = 2, B = 0$ and on all $39$ unbalanced `(0, 0, 1)` orbits). The remaining order-$21$ branches ($t = 11$ with $|D| \in \{9, \ldots, 13\}$ and the $2$ remaining jobs of $t = 17$) and general orders $n \geq 21$ remain open.
2. **Remaining Balanced $s_{\min}(G[X]) \geq 2$ Orbits and Higher Slack $B \geq 2$:** Among the $18$ balanced order-$1296$ orbits of $G[X]$ ($B_{\triangle} = 0$), our $29$ verified $(G[X], \varepsilon)$-orbits close $100\%$ of $B = 0$ ($3/3$ orbits), $100\%$ of $B = 1$ ($8/8$ orbits across $s_{\min} \in \{0, 1\}$), and all $18$ $B = 2$ orbits on the $s_{\min} = 0$ boundary graphs $t \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ across all $\eta \geq 0$. On the $39$ first-slack unbalanced `(0, 0, 1)` orbits ($B_{\triangle} = 1, e(C) \leq 41$), we have achieved **$100\%$ complete closure across all $\chi(C) \in \{7, 8\}$**: all $39/39$ $K_7$-free $\chi(C) = 7, \omega(C) \leq 6$ orbits, all $39/39$ $\chi(C) = 8$ ($K_8 \subseteq C$) orbits, and all $39/39$ $\chi(C) = 7, K_7 \subseteq C$ orbits. The remaining $9$ balanced $G[X]$ orbits with $s_{\min}(G[X]) = 2$ and higher slack $B \geq 2$ remain for subsequent work.
3. **Higher-Order Split Repairs Beyond $G_{25}^{\max}$ and Non-Biplanarity of $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$:** While Theorem 5(3–4) analytically refutes $G_{23}^*$, all $G_{21+s}^*$ ($0 \leq s \leq 5$), and $(K_7 - e) \vee C_5$, and proves that twin-splitting both shared apexes $\{19, 20\}$ at $n = 25$ drops the chromatic number to $\chi(G_{25}^{\max}) = 9$, our constructions $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$ demonstrate that clique-factor portals successfully eliminate all $M_{17}$ merges while preserving $\chi = 10, \omega = 8$ and satisfying all hereditary Euler bounds. We prove via the Double-Apex Portal-Restoration Obstruction that both $G_{30}$ and $G_{31}$ (and indeed every double-apex multi-block graph anchored by a $K_{10} - 2K_2$ block over $H_{12}^{\text{cone}}$ or $H_{13}^{\text{cone}}$) are analytically non-biplanar. Whether an asymmetric double-apex coupling with thinned blocks ($e \le 58$) avoiding both portal restoration and the wheel-rim contradiction can admit a biplanar decomposition remains for subsequent work.
4. **Overall Problem Status:** Neither the verified nine-chromatic blocks nor the double-apex lower bound supplies a ten-chromatic biplanar graph. The unrestricted Earth–Moon problem $\chi_2 \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$ remains open.
