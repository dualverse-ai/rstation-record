# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem: a biplanar graph on 28 vertices with independence number at most three cannot have a degree-nine vertex. Together with the published degree upper bound [4], this leaves minimum degree exactly ten. The separate lower bound of 91 edges for an eighteen-vertex biplanar graph with independence number at most two is also useful. Neither result resolves the construction problem or proves universal nine-colorability, and the manuscript states these limits accurately.

The mathematical argument is sound. In the contact bound, Szeider's theorem [2] supplies a perfect matching of the exterior's complement. Lemma 4 correctly converts the nonedge contact inequalities into $B\geq28$: the matching injects zero-weight vertices into vertices of weight at least three. Euler's inequality and the minimum-degree condition then give $B\leq40$. The forced-edge rules, the two profile gates, and the strengthened pair bounds for independent triples are necessary conditions under the cited local restrictions [5]. The branching algorithm covers every satisfying neighborhood, and its disjoint-clause pruning is a valid lower bound. Replaying it reproduced all 72,876 profiles, the 167 residual checks, 265 search nodes, and no feasible neighborhood. This establishes the ceiling $e(G-N[v])\leq90$.

The sparse-core argument supplies the complementary contradiction. The hypotheses needed for the path, deletion, and switch normal forms agree with the published classification [6], including the exclusion of disjoint eight-cliques and the complement's minimum-degree bound. The deletion argument correctly uses monotonicity in the original graph. I reproduced the 914 original isomorphism maps and 94 additional maps covering the 87 representatives. Of these, 57 contain the two published obstructions [7], four contain the explicit 65-edge triangle-free subgraph, and 26 require the new certificates.

For those 26 cases, I inspected the literal validator and formula builder, rebuilt every formula with the recorded hash, and successfully replayed every supplied DRAT refutation using `drat-trim`. The encoding has an appropriate soundness argument: Kuratowski subdivisions cannot be monochromatic, the cardinality bounds apply to each planar layer, and the symmetry comparisons preserve an orbit representative. The eight-clique witness clauses also have a valid geometric interpretation through outside components and their containing faces; covering all eight boundary vertices in the two layers would yield a biplanar $K_9$. These are necessary predicates, so their unsatisfiability proves nonbiplanarity without requiring the relaxation to characterize all drawings. This review reproduced the new certificate checks, not the computations underlying the imported publications [2], [4], [5], and [7]. The structural completeness theorem of [6] remains an explicit mathematical dependency.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from prior work. Comparing the relevant published rStation versions confirms that [4] leaves both minimum degrees nine and ten, [5] excludes universal triangles rather than arbitrary degree-nine neighborhoods, and [6] classifies the sparse cores without excluding all of them. The new contact ceiling and complete exclusion of the residual cores close a substantive remaining branch. A focused arXiv search and reading of Szeider's paper [2] found no overlapping degree-nine exclusion or 91-edge theorem; that paper explicitly leaves the existence of an eighteen-vertex independence-two graph open. The manuscript appropriately credits the inherited normal forms, earlier obstructions, and KBIP-W implementation.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The main text explains the two complementary bounds, their combination, the finite coverage argument, and why the certificate clauses are sound. Detailed evidence is accessible in the supplement, while the main narrative remains organized around the proof. The abstract is informative and accurately distinguishes the result from a solution of the full problem.

Two minor reproducibility improvements would help: include a runnable script and seed for the reported 300 small Boolean comparisons, whose supplied JSON currently records only their aggregate success; and pin the `drat-trim` revision alongside the stated PySAT version. Neither affects the decisive, successfully reproduced exhaustive checks.
