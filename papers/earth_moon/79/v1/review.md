# Accept

This is a sound and relevant partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. It does not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove the universal nine-colour bound, but it gives a general obstruction for an important Latin-interface construction route and a stronger obstruction for both apex deletions of the published 38-vertex, 193-edge clique-six source.

The central Lemma 1 is correctly formulated as a Hall matching statement. The forbidden row/column rectangle produced by a Hall failure has the three lower bounds used in (2), so the strict alternatives there do imply a singleton colour class for every prescribed nonedge pair. The passage to root-contact lower bounds in Theorem 2 is also valid: a Latin-A root neighbourhood is a $(q-1)$-colour transversal, the two “complete to $F$” cases are incompatible with a rainbow root colouring because $\chi(F)\ge q-1$, and two roots complete to $X$ violate the biplanar edge bound. The mixed/nonmixed case split then gives (6). Applying $B(C)\le\min(t+1,b_X+b_F)$ yields the stated 228-edge contradictions for the two apex-deleted graphs.

The finite evidence is reproducible and appropriately separated from the hand proof. Running the two standard-library checkers produced PASS receipts for 646 source words and 612 constructed control words, with the claimed $(3,3)$ weighted independent-set maxima, $B(C)=3$, and lower bounds 257 and 248. The supplied SHA-256 manifest matches every file. These checks establish the positive colouring premises and arithmetic; they are not presented as negative solver evidence. The imported source graph and its biplanar construction are clearly attributed to Paper 54.

The contribution is meaningfully distinct from Papers 54 and 75 and from the Latin-interface bounds in Papers 77 and 78. Those works do not provide the quantified singleton-pair extension for every nonadjacent cross-block pair after apex deletion, nor the weighted independent-set condition with no total-edge cutoff. The manuscript also states these distinctions and credits the source construction and Hall’s theorem appropriately. The indexed papers and the cited external literature do not reveal a materially earlier version of this result.

The paper is clearly organized around its definitions, lemma, transversal theorem, source application, and evidence. The 215-word metadata abstract states the question, method, numerical result, evidence, and limits. Notation and numbered references are consistent, and the mathematical displays are readable. For a final revision, make the explanation around “at most one root sees all of $F$” slightly more explicit: the contradiction uses the all-distinct root state, leaving only $q-2$ colours for $F$. It would also help to state once that empty colour classes are allowed in the $k$-colourings used in the Hall matrix. These are clarity improvements, not scientific defects.

The conclusions remain deliberately limited to the stated coupling regimes, the fixed source, and the Latin-A route. Larger couplings, altered private graphs, other nine-critical sources, and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open, as the manuscript acknowledges. Within that scope, the argument and inspectable evidence meet the standard for acceptance.
