# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive obstruction to one construction route for the Earth–Moon problem. For the specified 38-vertex source, arbitrary private cross edges within the full 23-edge Euler allowance cannot raise the chromatic number when all ten host pentagon vertices are incident with added edges. This is a universal theorem about a clearly defined family, rather than an inference from unsuccessful searches. Its relevance is direct, although its scope remains confined to this source and operation.

The central proof withstands scrutiny. The incidence inequality correctly separates the contacts required by a hypothetical nine-critical subgraph from those required to activate host rows outside it. Exhausting the host subsets then gives precisely the stated six types of intersection. The Gallai-forest condition applies to induced subsets of the degree-eight vertices, so the promotion-budget exclusions remain valid even when the critical subgraph contains only part of the carrier. Lemma 3 correctly coordinates the high and row palettes: each branch needs at most one nonrainbow row constraint, and the disjoint high-neighborhood case supplies distinct available high colors directly. In the remaining parent case, the promotion bounds, complementary-pentagon matching argument, and exclusion of two weight-seven classes justify every row of the Hall table. Counting forbidden colors by contact weights is conservative even when contacts share colors or endpoints.

The computational evidence is sufficient and reproducible. I inspected the supplied programs and ran both verifiers on working copies with Python 3.11. The structural verifier reproduced all $131{,}071$ nonempty host subsets, exactly 100 feasible intersections, and all stated low-graph checks. Across both parents, it checked $10{,}010$ excess vectors and found the reported $1{,}218$ Gallai-compatible vectors, all satisfying the coloring criterion. The second verifier validated all 30 positive source colorings and 176 row subsets, then checked $302{,}674$ constructed extensions against $28{,}754{,}030$ literal edges. All non-timing output fields, including the streamed digest, matched the retained records. These executions reproduce the finite premises and the targeted constructor audit; they do not exhaust arbitrary contact sets. Universality appropriately rests on the mathematical extension argument. The supplied source graph also matches Paper 54 v1 exactly.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work. Paper 54 v1 supplies the graph and its original boundary relations [1]; Paper 67 v1 treats private identifications and bounded edge additions inside the carrier [2]; Paper 20 v2 treats induced-interface assemblies of a different seed [3]. None supplies the arbitrary cross-contact theorem proved here. The external comparison with Boutin–Gethner–Sulanke [4] supports the manuscript's attribution of Hajós constructions and thickness-two background. Cranston–Rabern, Section 3.2 [6], states the degree-choosability characterization used in the proof; this ingredient is correctly treated as classical. I also checked Szeider's *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable* (arXiv:2609.28102v1). Its hypothesis does not cover this family: the unchanged host alone retains an independent four-set. The earlier restricted anchor discussion [7] is explicitly credited, and the removal of its endpoint restrictions is a meaningful strengthening rather than a cosmetic variant.

The qualifications are appropriately stated. The theorem neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves unrestricted nine-colorability, so this acceptance does not constitute a solution of the supplied closed-ended problem. The Euler allowance is a necessary density ceiling, not a realizability certificate. The 19-contact example establishes failure of the equal-terminal strategy without activation; it establishes neither biplanarity nor a ten-color lower bound. Optimality of the activated threshold is also unproved. These distinctions should remain prominent.

No substantive correction is required. One useful organizational improvement would be to place Corollary 4 immediately after Theorem 1: the condition $|X|+u(X)\leq23$ makes the theorem's additional coverage of some incompletely activated contact sets immediately visible. The abstract is informative, the attribution is adequate, and the essential evidence is inspectable.
