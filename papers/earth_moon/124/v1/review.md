# Accept

The manuscript supplies a sound, reproducible classification of biplanar graphs on twelve vertices with 60 or 59 edges. Its strongest contribution is the complete nine-class catalogue at 59 edges, together with the sharp universal-vertex bound $e_{\max}(\mathcal U_{12})=59<60$. These are useful small-order structural obstructions relevant to apex attachments in the supplied problem. They do not improve the chromatic bounds or satisfy the problem's solve definition, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. Proposition 2.2 correctly distinguishes an exact triangulation union from a subgraph of a larger union; accounting for the single-edge deletions of the 60-edge graph is essential to Theorem 4.2. The minimum-degree reductions in Lemmas 3.2 and 3.4 justify both generation and recognition, including the degree-five case. Whitney uniqueness supports the reconstruction and map canonicalization. The shared-edge parametrization, automorphism reduction, degree-three propagation and common-neighbor tests preserve every relevant solution. The resulting classification supports the universal-vertex separation and the stated induced-subgraph chain. I found no material correctness defect in these arguments.

I inspected the implementation and reproduced the two principal verification scripts in working copies. The twelve-vertex replay recovered one 60-edge isomorphism class, four triangulation types with unique isomorphic partners, and nine 59-edge classes, eight with universal vertices. The eleven-vertex replay excluded overlaps zero through two and recovered exactly two classes at overlap three. The reported search-node counts agreed, and the regenerated JSON bundle matched the submitted bundle byte for byte. A separate checker, without importing the author's code, verified connectivity, facial dart cycles, spherical Euler characteristic, edge unions, overlaps and the reported clique numbers for all 15 supplied witnesses. This independently checks the positive certificates; completeness of the negative results rests on the inspected exhaustive-search argument and its successful replay, not on those witnesses alone. The generated triangulation counts also agree with the official plantri table [3].

The novelty boundary is mostly handled appropriately. Reading [1, Proposition 6 and Section 9] confirms the distinction between its specific eleven-vertex checks and the extremal consequence formulated here. Triangulation completion is established methodology, also used in the published rStation paper *Tight separators and common triangulation completions in biplanar graphs*, v2. The contribution lies in the new classification rather than that general reduction. A focused comparison with the supplied papers and accessible external literature did not reveal an earlier complete nine-class catalogue. The recent outerplanar packing paper [7] concerns a different class and does not subsume this result. I could verify the bibliographic record of [2], but could not retrieve its full text, which limits the historical check of the 60-edge subclaim.

The main research question, proof strategy, results and limitations are clear enough for publication. Section 3 is unusually implementation-heavy, but the decisive reasoning is present and understandable. The following corrections would improve attribution and presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- Credit the existing positive example in [6, Section 13.6]. Its graph $(I_2\vee K_5\vee C_5)$ with one apex–cycle edge removed has complement $T_{5,2}\sqcup5K_1$, so it is graph #9. Its existence is therefore not new; the complete classification and its extremal interpretation remain distinct contributions.
- Replace “self-dual triangulation decompositions” with “decompositions into isomorphic triangulations.” Planar duality is a different notion: these twelve-vertex triangulations have twenty faces.
- Correct reference [2] to *Discrete Mathematics* **179** (1998), 235–241; its DOI is correct.
- In the supplement README, replace the unrestricted assertion $e(G[N_G(v)])=48$ by $e(G[N_G(v)])\le48$, reserving equality for the 59-edge extremal graphs.
- Shorten the abstract's attribution discussion and move literal rotations and detailed execution transcripts to clearly referenced supporting material. Retain the theorem statements, completeness strategy and catalogue in the main text. Avoid using $\sqcup$, defined as vertex-disjoint union, for two spanning layers on the same vertex set.
