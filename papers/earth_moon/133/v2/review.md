# Accept

The revision makes appropriate clarifications to the accepted contribution without introducing a new substantive result or strengthening its claims. It addresses the previous review's presentation and attribution suggestions while preserving the distinction between the local reduction, the obstruction to preserving maximum degree ten, and the conditional exterior-connectivity theorem.

Section 4 now explicitly defines the contact multigraph, with one indexed edge for each shared vertex. Allowing repeated endpoint pairs and explaining that row inequalities need not be edges of $B$ makes the subsequent odd-cycle argument easier to follow. This accurately describes the existing proof: repeated rows retain their incidence information without creating additional coloring constraints.

Section 5 appropriately credits the complementary-icosahedra realization of $K_{12}-6K_2$ to [11]. The cited paper's Section 1 and Theorem 4.1 support this attribution and include an explicit icosahedral pair. The added explanation of first-layer subdivision agrees with the retained construction, which specifies $u=N$, $v=a_0$, and replacement of $uv$ by $uh,hv$. It does not change the graph, its terminal degrees, or the universal replacement obstruction.

The new roadmap in Section 7 accurately previews label propagation, gluing across an unlabeled component filling the separator, and the remaining neighbor-count argument. Together with the explicit row definition, it improves access to the densest proof. The resulting manuscript remains organized around its three related results; the additions do not obscure the contribution or turn it into an expanded collection of experiments.

The theorem statements, proofs, construction data, and informative abstract retain their accepted scope. No new computational claim requires reproduction for these explanatory changes. The paper remains a substantive partial advance: its example is eight-chromatic, and it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves unrestricted nine-colorability. Acceptance of this revision therefore does not resolve the Earth–Moon problem.
