# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The manuscript proves a local nine-color reduction for two eight-cliques sharing seven vertices, identifies a sharp obstruction to preserving maximum degree ten under arbitrary smaller replacements that retain the labeled exterior, and establishes a contact-connectivity restriction in a minimum counterexample within that degree class. These results address configurations and reduction methods relevant to the supplied task. They do not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove unrestricted nine-colorability, and the manuscript appropriately states that limitation.

The argument for Theorem 1 is convincing. The missing-edge obstruction identifies a unique relevant component in each layer, and the common-face argument justifies the permitted additions in the actual ambient drawings. Lemma 4 correctly distinguishes the three possible intersection sizes and uses the corresponding published clique-join restrictions. After identifying the tips, Hall failure has the stated common-six-set form. The subsequent triangle and star cases exclude that failure, including the exceptional shared vertex with three exterior neighbors. The construction does not assume bounded degrees elsewhere or incorrectly appeal to closure of biplanarity under arbitrary identifications.

Theorem 2 adds meaningful information beyond the existence of a reduction. The odd-cycle argument bounds the repair by three edges and charges all but one added incidence to deleted spokes. The lower bound addresses every smaller graph of maximum degree ten containing the labeled exterior, rather than merely showing that a particular repair fails. The saturated blocks leave precisely eighteen terminals with one spare incidence each. The matching argument and the case distinction on the at-most-eight new vertices then force intact terminal pairs to attach to an eight-clique. Applying that necessary condition to both host eight-cliques forces degree eleven at the remaining new vertex. This supports the claimed universal obstruction within the stated replacement model.

I independently checked the explicit construction using a small program written from the rotation table and indexed edge rules. The host layers have respectively fourteen and twenty-one edges, with seven and fourteen facial walks, giving Euler characteristic two in each connected layer. The two block edge sets are disjoint and their union is $K_{12}-6K_2$. The assembled graph has 117 vertices and 584 edges, with 115 vertices of degree ten and two of degree nine. The proposed eight-coloring is proper, and the host contains an eight-clique. I also checked the block colorings for equal and unequal prescribed terminal colors. These checks corroborate the construction; the assertion about all replacements rests on the mathematical argument, not on a bounded search.

Theorem 3 is supported by more than a generic appeal to Kempe chains. Section 6 first forces a common pair of contact colors in every exterior coloring, obtains at least four contacts of each color, and proves the required two-color connectivity. In Lemma 6, minimizing the number of separator vertices of one color makes the repeated-neighbor-color charge valid and yields $7\leq r+k\leq2r$, excluding $r\leq3$. Section 7 then handles the remaining possibility of contacts on opposite sides that can never share a color. Its boundary-pattern gluing and degree counts resolve the possible unlabeled row component and produce the fixed monochromatic transversal needed for Lemma 5. The resulting single-edge augmentation preserves maximum degree ten, so the final minimality argument is within the correct class. This proves the stated connectivity of surviving contacts, without asserting that the entire exterior is four-connected.

The novelty is adequate. Paper 21 [2] supplies the private-eight-clique reduction and its two-color obstruction method, but its outside-degree hypothesis does not cover these overlapping cliques. Papers 68 [3] and 34 [5] supply geometric ingredients rather than the present reduction and sharpness theorem. Paper 99 [6] permits this overlap under a different list-choosability hypothesis, while Paper 131 [9] concerns the different host $K_6\vee C_5$. I also checked the relevant proof of Cranston–Rabern [7] against the extraction described in Paper 100 [8]: that background explains the rich seven-clique motivation but does not supply the tips, the present replacement barrier, or the exterior separator theorem. I found no substantial duplication in these comparisons. The published computational exclusion of $K_7\vee3K_1$ in [4] is explicitly identified as an imported premise; I inspected its statement and reduction but did not rerun its enumeration.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The main results, their distinct minimality assumptions, proof strategy, construction data, and limitations are available in the main text. The abstract is informative. The densest passage is Section 7, but its case split and decisive inequalities can be followed without reconstructing a missing argument. The following minor improvements would make the presentation easier to use:

- Introduce the auxiliary graph of contact pairs explicitly when “rows” first appear in Section 4, stating that its edges encode inequalities and need not be edges of $B$; mention that repeated pairs may occur.
- Add a short roadmap at the start of Section 7 separating fixed labels, an unlabeled component filling the separator, and the remaining three-vertex boundary case. A small boundary schematic would help but is not essential.
- Acknowledge the known complementary-icosahedra realization of $K_{12}-6K_2$, also discussed in Paper 124 [11]. The new contribution here is its use in the universal degree-preservation obstruction, not the block's biplanarity.
