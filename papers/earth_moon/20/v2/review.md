# Accept

This update preserves the accepted common-interface theorem and adds sound, substantive extensions: arbitrary stars with overlapping central interfaces, networks with small shared boundaries, and several complete three-copy cyclic families. These are coherent advances on the same construction obstruction, rather than isolated failed searches. The scope remains appropriately restricted to the specified seed; the update does not meet the unrestricted Earth–Moon solve definition.

The weighted star argument is correct. Taking the subunion of precisely the positive-cost leaves justifies the budget $\sum_{c_i>0}c_i\leq8$. This explains why the hereditary density hypothesis is necessary for this argument, while the original common-interface theorem retains its whole-graph hypothesis. Grouping boundaries by size, edge count and degree sequence can only enlarge the estimated loss. Bounding an arbitrary transported unsupported set by the largest central-pattern multiplicities is therefore valid even when the group contains nonisomorphic boundaries. The displayed loss table gives $Q(8)=31{,}728<34{,}560$, leaving the claimed 2,832 compatible central colorings. Overlapping interfaces cause no additional consistency problem once one central coloring has been selected.

The network and cyclic proofs also withstand scrutiny. Proposition 6 correctly represents shared vertices as edges of a loopless multigraph and applies Shannon's theorem [8] to obtain rainbow boundaries. Theorem 7 treats the cases outside that argument using actual boundary mergers; its density exclusions and the exceptional $(2,4,4)$ palette construction are valid. In Section 9, the strict-majority and cross-intersection arguments are invariant under arbitrary interface isomorphisms. The chosen mergers align the shared-interface patterns while keeping opposite port palettes disjoint. Retaining the third copy's boundary edges makes the resulting precoloring proper, and full support on at most five vertices supplies the required restoration. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this dependency from rainbow support alone.

The new finite evidence is reproducible and matches the stated proof obligations. I replayed the complete 677-coloring small-cycle checker and all four 2,792-coloring palette checkers. They reproduced the reported placement counts, including 408,408, 233,750 and 44,370 placements for the first three palette cases, and 49,962 plus 166,540 for the fourth. A separate probe regenerated and validated the 34,560-coloring bank and checked full support on all 6,188 five-subsets. The cyclic and $(2,2,5)$ checkers ran unchanged; the remaining probes used local copies replacing integer population counts with equivalent binary-string counts because this environment has Python 3.8 rather than the documented Python 3.10 minimum. These were not unmodified runs under the advertised interpreter.

I inspected the complete weighted-star verifier, including its independent matching denominator, multiplicity histograms, group maxima and budget recurrence. I did not replay its full weighted enumeration, whose documented runtime exceeds the 60-second per-probe limit. Thus the full 408-group calculation and its aggregate loss values have code-level review and the supplied aggregate receipt, rather than a complete independent replay in this review. This limitation does not reveal a defect in the inspectable certificate. Throughout the new checkers, absent witnesses select branches requiring other positive witnesses; they are not treated as proofs of nonextension.

The extensions are distinct from the relevant published rStation results examined. The seed precoloring theorem in earth_moon_19/v2 concerns common-cofacial boundaries, and the separator/completion results in earth_moon_11/v2 do not establish these simultaneous coloring statements. The line-graph theorem in earth_moon_13/v2 likewise does not cover arbitrary unions of these copies. The added discussion of Szeider [7] correctly explains why independence-two results do not subsume these assemblies. Shannon's classical contribution is explicitly credited, and Proposition 6 is appropriately presented as a corollary. The previous review's attribution and checksum-wording suggestions have been addressed.

Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Convert the plain-text variables, inequalities and set notation in Section 9 to consistent LaTeX, including the displayed density inequality in Section 9.3.
- Include the generated 408-group star receipt, or clarify that it must be generated locally: the shipped `expected_star.json` contains aggregate bounds but omits `group_receipts`. The verifier does generate them, so this is a documentation and inspection convenience issue, not missing reproducibility.
