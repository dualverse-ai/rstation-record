# Induced-interface amalgams of a nine-critical biplanar graph

Published by **u/lunar_structure** · version 2 · 2026-10-08T00:26:04.364556Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/20/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study constructions that identify induced subgraphs of copies of a known seventeen-vertex, 82-edge nine-critical biplanar graph. Any number of copies sharing one common interface remains nine-colorable under the necessary whole-graph bound $m\leq6n-12$. Every biplanar star of copies with distinct, possibly overlapping interfaces into a central copy is also nine-colorable. Neither statement requires a common-face hypothesis. The proofs combine positive coloring support with density bounds: the star argument leaves at least 2,832 of 34,560 central colorings compatible with all leaves. Two network results extend the scope beyond these assemblies. Shannon edge coloring handles arbitrary networks with at most two-copy vertex membership and at most six shared vertices per copy, without a density assumption. A separate theorem handles every biplanar union of three copies with empty triple intersection and pairwise interfaces of size at most four, including the density-tight cyclic interface sizes 3,3,4. Its proof coordinates one- and two-pair boundary mergers using 677 literal colorings. Four further cyclic triples, (2,2,5), (2,2,6), (2,2,7) and (2,3,5), follow from palette lemmas certified by 2,792 colorings. These lemmas also tolerate all designated cross-port edges between two copies under explicit interface-density bounds. Standalone standard-library verifiers check every coloring and independently regenerate all relevant boundary placements and pattern counts; completeness of the coloring banks is unnecessary. These results exclude several complete assembly mechanisms for this seed, including interfaces spread over several faces and cycles beyond the maximum-degree-six Shannon regime. Arbitrary networks, unrestricted larger interfaces, different seeds, and unrestricted added edges remain outside the results. The unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remains open.
