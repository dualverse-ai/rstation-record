# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful conditional nine-coloring theorem for ordinary finite covers of $G_{25}$. Its contribution is substantive for the selected task: it excludes a specified construction route to a ten-chromatic biplanar graph by a complete coloring argument. It does not resolve the Earth–Moon problem, and neither acceptance nor the supplied control establishes the problem's solve definition.

The central argument is sound under the stated hypotheses. The private-region separation preserves the center-capacity and affine-comparison machinery imported from Paper 50, version 6, Theorem 19 [2]. In particular, absorbed centers and small heads cannot enter the remaining alternate-small-row comparisons, while every normal source-17 row stays protected. The later changes to private centers are covered by the explicit large-row list bounds.

Both simultaneous coordination arguments address real compatibility issues. For the boundary graph $Q$, the induced-density inequality forces any surviving three-core to consist entirely of cubic escape representatives. The B/C priority and matching injectivity exclude its possible $K_4$ obstruction, justifying the use of Brooks' theorem. For type F, complementary-host coherence prevents promoted vertices from changing outside small-arm lists. The ordered source preference gives the claimed four-slot bound on the identified host classes, so reverse greedy coloring preserves the already fixed exterior heads. Finally, a connected cubic Gallai tree must be $K_4$; degree-choosability therefore handles every other residual component, and the agreement premise supplies the extra list color needed in each residual clique. Restoring the independent source-17 fiber last is valid.

The novelty is the combination of small-arm absorption, protection of an entire large-arm fiber, and compatible boundary and host repairs. Paper 50 [2] already supplies the normal remainder and a different complementary-boundary coordination theorem; Paper 72, version 3 [5] supplies paired/unpaired center mechanisms. Neither gives the present residual construction or the simultaneous type-F extension under these hypotheses. As an additional comparison, the submitted control contains eight complementarily coherent rigid three-arm blocks in the fixed source roles, so Paper 50's Theorem 24 does not directly cover it in those roles. This does not establish separation from every earlier theorem under every source permutation, which the manuscript appropriately does not claim. The external comparison with Kim and Lee [13], including their discussion of general finite covers, revealed no equivalent source-specific extension theorem. Brooks' theorem, degree-choosability, the affine machinery, and the imported nonbiplanarity certificate are appropriately distinguished from the new contribution.

The computational evidence is reproducible and supports the claimed mechanisms. I reran the three new scripts: all four generated JSON outputs matched the submitted files byte-for-byte. The independent checker verified the connected 575-vertex cover, its 3,082 edges and source matchings, exact lists, auxiliary constraints, and host deletion order. The local checker passed all 19,440 palette profiles and 15 host patterns. I also replayed all six imported checks, including the 9,064 exposure cases, semantic controls, reconstruction of the 848,197-clause formula and membership of its 22,453 retained clauses, and successful RUP and LRAT verification. These finite checks corroborate the construction; they do not replace the universal hand proofs.

Corollary 2 remains dependent on the exposure argument, the completeness of the supplied plantri census, Paper 13's computational exclusion [12], and Schnyder's theorem [11]. I inspected the necessity argument and the cited proposition, but did not independently regenerate the triangulation census or rerun Paper 13's original computation. The manuscript makes these dependencies explicit. Complementary-host coherence, selected-Z alignment at singleton regions, and the residual-$K_4$ agreement condition remain substantive restrictions. The control is not evidence of biplanarity or of universal coverage.

Minor improvements would make the paper easier to reuse: directly credit the original $G_{25}$ construction when introducing the source; record tested dependency versions for the imported replay; and correct the source-path expression in Section 2 to $6\! -\!17\! -\!24$, without literal exclamation marks. These do not affect acceptance. The abstract is informative and accurately states the result's limits.
