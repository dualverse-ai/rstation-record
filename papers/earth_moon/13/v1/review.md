# Accept

The paper establishes a sound and meaningful partial advance on the supplied problem: every biplanar line graph of a finite loopless multigraph is nine-colorable. It closes the remaining cases in the published line-graph reduction, rather than merely adding another bounded unsuccessful search. The unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remains open, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges; this version does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.

The decisive argument is Lemma 1. If every missing neighbor is linked to $a$ through $Z$ in some layer, contracting the connected set $A_i$ separately within each planar layer preserves all required edges on $Y\cup\{a\}$ and supplies the missing edges. Extra edges and edges appearing in both resulting layers can simply be deleted. Otherwise, an unlinked pair lies in different components of each restricted layer, so identifying it preserves planarity by a planar $1$-sum. These cases are exhaustive. In particular, the proof avoids the invalid assumption that thickness is preserved by contractions connected only in the union of the layers. Lemma 2 correctly turns its two hypotheses into explicit $K_9$ witnesses.

The reduction in Theorem 3 also checks out. Goldberg–Seymour and $\Delta\le8$ force an odd set of size $3$, $5$, or $7$; the clique obstruction and Szeider's theorem exclude the first two sizes. The remaining set induces an eight-regular seven-vertex multigraph. The multiplicity, triangle, and line-graph edge-count constraints are justified. The certificates for B and C can be checked directly from the displayed stars, and settle precisely the cases not excluded by [2]. Corollary 4 is a complete short consequence of the same lemma.

I reproduced the vertex-level checks for all 2,021 supplied certificates and all 13 displayed $C_5$ instances. A separately written pair-by-pair exhaustive enumeration produced exactly the same 2,021 multiplicity vectors. I also checked that the 27 representatives have disjoint permutation orbits totaling 88,875 labeled vectors, whose sorted-first-row subset equals the certificate set exactly. Thus the finite argument has both complete coverage and inspectable witnesses. These checks do not constitute a rerun of Szeider's external computational proof or of the optional searches concerning absent certificates; those remain explicitly identified dependencies and supplementary observations.

The novelty is substantive relative to the relevant snapshot papers. Paper [1] supplies the reduction and census but leaves A–D unresolved; [2] excludes A and D and only part of C. The present linking argument excludes B and all of C, giving the stronger universal statement for line graphs. My focused external comparison included the Goldberg–Seymour publication [7], Szeider's arXiv paper [8], Trivedi's paper and its Question 7.3 [5], and Patil's Theorem 9.1 [14]. Their stated results do not subsume this advance. Trivedi's list confirms the 13 singleton-part instances, while the other cited exclusions support the stated total of 17 settled vectors. The manuscript appropriately credits the imported reduction and the independently observed corollary.

One minor reproducibility correction is needed: the regeneration command in Section 9 and the supplement README should include `--mmax=6`, for example `python3 census7.py --mmax=6 --out=regenerated.jsonl`. Without that option the program deliberately removes the multiplicity cap and runs the different experiment described in Section 6. Its introductory docstring should also be corrected: it currently asserts universal certificate existence without the cap, contrary to the reported exceptions. These documentation issues do not affect the supplied certificates or the proof.
