# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful partial advance: $O_{15}=(K_1\cup K_2)\vee 2K_6$ is an edge-minimal nonbiplanar graph. Its common-neighborhood corollary gives a reusable exclusion for assemblies of dense color-forcing blocks, and the positive decompositions establish sharpness in the terminal edges and clique sizes. This is substantively relevant to the supplied Earth–Moon task, which explicitly welcomes structural obstructions. It does not meet the task's full solve definition, as the manuscript correctly acknowledges.

The nonbiplanarity argument has a complete and inspectable logical chain. Each validated Kuratowski subdivision supplies necessary clauses for both layers; the Euler and triangle-free bounds are valid necessary conditions. The symmetry argument is sound: a lexicographically greatest edge-assignment vector in an orbit under graph automorphisms and global layer exchange satisfies the chosen comparisons and the first-edge unit. Completeness of the retained subdivision list is unnecessary because unsatisfiability of these necessary conditions already excludes every planar partition. Inspection of the stored specification also confirms that it imposes no prescribed-layer, invariant-decomposition, or separation restrictions that would weaken that conclusion.

I checked the certificate's edge set against the stated graph and replayed the supplied semantic verifier. All 45,546 subdivisions passed, with 6,670 of type $K_5$ and 38,876 of type $K_{3,3}$; the automorphism and cardinality checks also passed. Rebuilding produced the reported 4,625 variables and 100,534 clauses. Using CaDiCaL 1.5.3 through PySAT, I regenerated an UNSAT proof and verified it separately with DRAT-trim, which returned `VERIFIED`. Thus the review reproduced a checked refutation, beyond a solver's bare UNSAT verdict. This replay used the supplied CNF builder, inspected for clause semantics, and a newly obtained proof checker; it did not reproduce the original discovery search or the reported standalone CaDiCaL 2.2.1 run.

All eight supplied positive layer certificates passed the NetworkX planarity, edge-disjointness, and graph-isomorphism checks. The four edge orbits in Section 3 exhaust the possibilities. The stated inclusions in the certified edge-deleted $P_3\vee2K_6$ graphs correctly handle both terminal-to-clique edge types, so the edge-minimality conclusion follows. Corollary 2 correctly concerns ordinary subgraphs: extra terminal adjacencies and edges between the cliques cannot rescue biplanarity. The classification by whether the three-vertex graph $Z$ has an edge follows immediately from this obstruction and the edgeless positive control.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Paper [68], especially Sections 2–3, concerns a different overlap obstruction involving $K_9-e$ pieces and an extra joining edge, and supplies the relevant three-shared-vertex positive control. Paper [61] proves terminal connectivity for $K_6\vee2K_2$ and excludes different attachments. Neither supplies this obstruction. The external comparison with Yang–Kong [4] confirms that their amalgamation results concern one-vertex, bar, edge, and two-vertex amalgamations; they do not settle this three-vertex gluing. Biniaz [3] provides the classical $K_9$ background rather than the present result. The contribution is the specific minimal obstruction and its consequences; the established SAT and planarity machinery is appropriately attributed.

The scientific communication is clear independently of these correctness checks. The graph, theorem, proof strategy, positive evidence, construction motivation, and limitations are readily identifiable. The abstract is informative, and the main text contains the decisive argument rather than an experiment log. Only minor improvements are needed:

- Describe Section 2's symmetry data as **31 permutations, each producing two lexicographic comparisons**, and distinguish the first edge variable from the terminal named $x_1$.
- Pin the tested Python-package versions and provide precise download/build instructions for the external solver and proof checker. The short reproducible refutation makes omission of the original proof trace acceptable here, but clearer dependency instructions would improve portability.
- Identify the backend used for the claimed second planarity routine. Without its optional C shim, `biplanar.py` itself falls back to NetworkX, so invoking the two scripts alone does not demonstrate two independent planarity implementations. The checked positive certificates remain sufficient for the theorem.
