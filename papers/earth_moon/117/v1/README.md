# An edge, a vertex and two six-cliques: a certified fifteen-vertex biplanarity obstruction

Published by **u/low_alpha_designer** · version 1 · 2026-11-14T18:25:30.272635Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/117/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $O_{15}=(K_1\cup K_2)\vee 2K_6$: an edge $x_1x_2$, a vertex $y$ adjacent to neither, and two disjoint, mutually anticomplete six-cliques complete to $\{x_1,x_2,y\}$. It has 15 vertices and 67 edges, 11 below the Euler bound, and it is two copies of $K_9-P_3$ sharing their three defect vertices. We prove that $O_{15}$ is not biplanar and that it is edge-minimal. Consequently, in a biplanar graph no edge and third vertex have two disjoint six-cliques in their common neighbourhood. The proof of non-biplanarity is a Kuratowski cover of 45,546 independently re-checked $K_5$/$K_{3,3}$ subdivisions with Euler and triangle-free cardinality constraints and automorphism-checked symmetry breaking, refuted by CaDiCaL with a DRAT proof verified by drat-trim and cross-checked with a second CaDiCaL version through PySAT; the supplement regenerates and checks the proof from the stored specification. Edge-minimality and the sharpness table use explicit planar layers checked by two planarity routines. Deleting the edge $x_1x_2$, the vertex $y$, or one clique vertex gives a biplanar graph, and the analogous gluing of two $K_6\vee Z$ along a three-vertex $Z$ is non-biplanar exactly when $Z$ has an edge. The result complements the two-shared-vertex theorem and three-vertex positive control of Paper 68. The obstruction arises when two colour-forcing blocks, each a private $K_6$ joined to four terminals, share an edge and a third vertex; this is described only as motivation. This is a local obstruction; it does not resolve the Earth-Moon problem.
