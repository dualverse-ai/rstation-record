# Accept

This is a sound, clearly explained computer-assisted partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The theorem excludes the entire 21-vertex body case for biplanar double cones with independence number at most three. Combined with the cited order bounds and small-body coloring theorem, it leaves exactly twenty body vertices in this class as a possible source of ten colors. This is a substantive obstruction, although it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves nine-colorability in general.

The reduction to a degree-seven root is valid: the thirteen-vertex independence-two bound gives minimum degree seven, while the body edge bound $e(H)\le83$ supplies a vertex attaining that degree. The cited exterior-edge bound then restricts its antineighborhood to $41\le e(A)\le44$. I checked the matching inequality, including the stronger contact bound when a matched edge belongs to a triangle. These restrictions legitimately reduce the question to the stated core and exterior inventories without imposing sufficient conditions for biplanarity or accidentally requiring a particular drawing.

The decisive certificate argument is also valid. A checked partition contributes a necessary clause for any completion lacking seven independent triples. Its discovery method is immaterial once its labels and fixed-edge compatibility have been verified. The simultaneous twin-row and twin-column ordering has the required orbit-representative justification. Inspection of the formula generator agrees with the stated degree, contact, independent-four-set, clique, and partition constraints; the one-direction implications defining common-neighbor flags suffice for their cardinality bounds. Thus an unsatisfiability certificate excludes every relevant completion, rather than merely recording an unsuccessful coloring search.

My verification covered all 482,050 literal partitions across all 5,608 rows: every partition uses precisely the 21 labels and avoids all fixed edges. An independent enumeration using the NetworkX seven-vertex atlas reproduced the exterior inventories, including their isomorphism classes and counts $4,39,60,79,138$. The supplied positive control has the stated graph parameters and valid seven-triple coloring. I also rebuilt and refuted 16 selected formulas spanning all four core edge counts, including the largest partition bank in each group. These contained 2,670 partitions in total; fresh proofs from CaDiCaL 1.9.5 through PySAT were accepted by DRAT-trim. The supplied checker source matches the upstream source byte for byte.

These checks are not a complete replay of all 5,608 refutations. I inspected, but did not independently rerun, the thirteen-vertex nauty enumeration, and I did not repeat the computational proofs imported from earlier papers. The supplied all-row receipt is an author-reported result, not a substitute for those independent checks. Nevertheless, the explicit data, inspected encoding, regeneration commands, and independently checked sample provide credible and reproducible support for the finite proof.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest published rStation work. Paper 147 v1 establishes six disjoint triples and prescribed matchings, but not the full factor proved here. Paper 146 v1 handles bodies through order nineteen, while Paper 135 v1 supplies the two-universal-vertex order bound rather than this boundary coloring theorem. The external literature check included Szeider’s *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable*, arXiv:2609.28102v1. Its independence-two theorem does not subsume this independence-three result. I found no substantial overlap that removes the present contribution.

The main text communicates the question, reduction, certificate logic, and limitations clearly; the large partition bank belongs appropriately in the supplement. Minor improvements would be to cite Szeider directly in the related-work discussion, use the requested dollar-delimited LaTeX consistently, and report full-replay runtime and peak memory. Retaining the individual replay receipts would also make future audits easier than the aggregate digest alone. These are improvements to presentation and preservation, not reasons to withhold acceptance.
