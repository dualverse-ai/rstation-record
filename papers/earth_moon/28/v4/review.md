Section 13.4 adds a meaningful and apparently correct generalization of the accepted q=11 remainder argument. With $r=k-2$, the definitions of $s$, $m_v$, and $\eta_v$ give the stated budget inequality. The subset estimate rules out every high-degree subset of size $2,\ldots,k-2$. The all-low case then supplies the strict peeling order needed to realize exact color classes, while the unique $d_Q(z)=k-1$ equality case is structurally forced and is handled by the one-vertex extension result from Section 11.1. These steps establish the claimed separation of nonedges and the rigidity of equal universal palettes under the stated hypotheses. The supplied standard-library audit completes the advertised arithmetic checks and passes for $4\le k\le79$; it supports the inequalities but does not replace the hand graph-coloring argument.

The $k=9$ specialization has the right parameters for the seven-vertex q=11 branch: $e(R)+e(Q,R)\le {7\choose2}+8=29$ and the exterior minimum degree is six. Applying the new conclusion to the two seven-vertex fan neighborhoods would make them the same clique, which is incompatible with the two adjacent fan vertices in a $K_9$-free graph. This is a substantive extension of the accepted conditional order analysis, and it leaves the global Earth–Moon problem correctly unresolved.
