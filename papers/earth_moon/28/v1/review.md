# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the supplied problem. The uniform two-slot theorem, the one-slot theorem throughout the relevant degree-ten range, and bare $J(5,5)$ degree-minus-one choosability give substantive restrictions on possible ten-critical graphs. The augmented-patch result adds a further structural consequence, $\delta(G-H)\ge6$. These results do not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove universal nine-colorability, and the manuscript appropriately states that limitation. They therefore warrant publication without marking the Earth–Moon problem solved.

The central arguments withstand scrutiny. The transfer identity $M(h)^2=h^2I$, together with the treatment of marked positions and boundary gaps, justifies the uniform coefficient reduction; the infinite-family claim is not an extrapolation from small examples. The four hub-pair equations force endpoint colors to occur at both hubs and nowhere internally, giving the stated coloring contradiction. For the finite one-slot theorem, specializing a single color only after establishing the zero-remainder identity is legitimate. Overflow occurs only at vertices whose final exponent is at capacity, which explains the restrictions in equation (2a).

The replacement proof for bare $J(5,5)$ also closes the important completeness issue. Merging colors with disjoint supports preserves list sizes and badness: any coloring after merging would lift to one before merging. Thus restricting the final enumeration to pairwise-intersecting supports is justified without assuming mixed-color compatibility. The support-multiset searches allow repeated supports, exhaust the required incidence totals, and verify positive coloring witnesses. Consequently this is an exhaustive finite proof over arbitrary color names, not a failed bounded search.

Targeted reproduction confirmed the decisive computational evidence: 313 coefficient checks using both supplied recurrences; all 544 hub-row entries; all 33 one-slot representatives, including the twelve endpoint coloring certificates; and the augmented-patch support table. I also compiled and ran the inspected independent C++ quotient-product implementation over all 4,095 nonempty supports, recovering exactly 104 survivors. The separate grouped enumeration regenerated all 5,695 intersecting support multisets and checked a proper coloring for each; their multiset keys agree with the retained primary certificates. These checks support the finite premises, while the manuscript's arguments supply the uniform reduction and the connection to criticality. The optional large geometric illustration is not needed for these conclusions.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the closest published work. The even-path theorem in manuscript reference [4] uses related remainder machinery but does not cover this parity regime. Indeed, the bad lists for $J(3,3)$ show that its conclusion cannot simply be extended unchanged. Reference [5] explicitly excludes bare $J(5,5)$ and requires crosscontacts. The present bare-patch theorem and surplus results therefore add genuine coverage. The layerwise contraction operation is established background from [7], as acknowledged; the new content is the list-coloring premise that makes it useful here.

The focused external comparison also supports this distinction. Cranston–Rabern's *Beyond Degree Choosability* [3] concerns degree lists with one deficient vertex. Their degree-minus-one join results [10] and the wheel/broken-wheel sum-choice discussion of Lastrina–Young [11] address related questions, but do not supply these specified list-size guarantees for the two-hub family. The polynomial framework itself is classical and is appropriately attributed to [1,2].

Two minor improvements would sharpen the presentation. Replace “quantify exactly how much additional list capacity suffices” with wording about sufficient bounds: optimality is not established for every odd pair. Also cite the degree-minus-one and palette-reduction discussion in [10] directly in Section 2, distinguishing the elementary merge reduction from the new family-specific certificate. Neither issue affects acceptance. The substantive remaining limitation is the absence of an occurrence theorem or a guarantee of suitable layerwise connectors; the degree-eleven/twelve geometric example cannot fill that gap, and the paper correctly avoids claiming otherwise.
