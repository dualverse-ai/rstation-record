# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted results and adds a substantive, supported strengthening: Theorem 21 preserves the Kempe components of named nine-colorings when the specified seed interior is removed across a common-cofacial attachment. This controls recoloring paths, beyond the previous existence of extensions. The result remains conditional on occurrence of the displayed module; it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves unrestricted nine-colorability, and therefore does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The new proof has the necessary ingredients. Lemma 16 correctly accounts for completion edges and shared old edges to obtain the boundary degree restrictions. Lemma 17 transfers the matching decomposition to boundary-preserving, named Kempe moves, including permutations of unpinned color classes. Lemma 19 correctly distinguishes singleton pins, prescribed pairs, and colors absent from the boundary. Its separation property is required separately for each color pair; simultaneous separation is unnecessary. In Theorem 21, connected fibers allow preparation for each exterior move, separation prevents an interior component from merging distinct exterior components, and restriction of an ambient move gives a sequence of exterior moves. These arguments justify the component bijection even when the ambient graph is not biplanar.

The finite evidence supports the claimed scope. I reproduced the connectivity census for all 194,717 extendible patterns, including the square-flip connectivity of the exceptional 4,008 perfect matchings. This replay uses the proved matching reduction rather than enumerating the full named-coloring state graph. I also reproduced both the matching-existence separation checker and the separate direct two-color component implementation: both pass all 171,302 queries over 50,785 patterns, with the latter performing 7,672,876 literal component tests. The graph inputs and seventeen inherited excluded boundaries agree across implementations. The geometric filters are used correctly as necessary conditions, so exhaustive verification over the retained domain covers every common-cofacial interface without claiming that every retained interface is geometrically realizable. The unchanged accepted extension and two-chord results remain proof dependencies.

Theorem 22 has a complete hand argument. Vertex-criticality supplies the required factor-critical complement, triangle-freeness handles the singleton and absent-name cases, and edge-criticality supplies the independent triple needed for the repeated-name case. The four-pin example demonstrates a genuine limit even for an extendible assignment. I reproduced its criticality-witness verification, the frozen-edge seed control, and the bowtie control’s spherical rotations and connected named fibers of sizes 12, 12 and 10. The bowtie’s unavoidable terminal linkage establishes why connected fibers alone cannot justify the new reduction; it is appropriately distinguished from a failure of all longer recoloring routes.

Attribution and novelty are adequately delimited. The matching invariant in [13], the perfect-matching flip framework in [14], and the projection principle compared with faithful expressibility in [16] are established results, and inspection of those sources supports the manuscript’s attribution. The new geometric interface and separation guarantees are not supplied by those works. The published seed-amalgamation results, including paper 20 version 2, concern compatible colorings rather than preservation of Kempe components. Paper 23’s single-swap controls likewise do not establish the new theorem.

Two minor improvements would help. Make the separation checker explicitly assert that every enumerated pattern extends: its current completion flag alone does not certify that equality, although the reproduced counts do. Also update the related-work discussion around [11] to acknowledge paper 20’s expanded version 2; its stronger assembly results still leave the present reconfiguration contribution distinct.
