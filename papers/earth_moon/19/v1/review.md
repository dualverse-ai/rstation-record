# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon task. The contribution is the extension of **arbitrary proper nine-precolorings**, including repeated colors, across specified common-cofacial boundaries. This yields genuine reducible induced configurations for a hypothetical ten-critical biplanar graph. It does not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove unrestricted nine-colorability, and therefore does not meet the problem’s solve definition.

The geometric reductions support the stated all-representations scope. The two-half triangulation argument correctly rules out a nine-vertex boundary omitting the identified vertex. The shared-star argument contracts an edge explicitly present in both layers, avoiding the invalid assumption that arbitrary contractions preserve biplanarity. The sector-counting argument gives the necessary inequality

$$
e(J[T])\ge 4|T|-(6|V(J)|-6-e(J)),
$$

with shared old edges correctly accounted for. At the tight six-vertex boundary, equality forces coverage by the two star-neighbor cyclic orders. The simultaneous nonalternating chords can then be inserted and contracted separately in both layers. Together with the dominating seven-clique, this legitimately produces the forbidden $K_9$. The treatment of repeated vertices on the original face boundary through selected star spokes is appropriate.

The finite coloring arguments are also convincing. Restricted-growth enumeration covers color partitions up to renaming, while the boundary representatives only quotient by actual clique-twin symmetries fixing the directed endpoints. The nine-class matching formulation enforces prescribed color names, equality at the identified vertex, and inequality across the bridge; these are precisely the constraints connecting the halves. For the seventeen-vertex core, independence number two makes the complement-matching criterion exact. Thus the negative cases are exhaustively classified and geometrically excluded, rather than inferred from unsuccessful bounded searches.

I reproduced the literal core’s spherical-layer, coloring, independence and criticality checks; the complete census of 243,865 precolorings; the 32 density exclusions and 17 two-chord exclusions; and the independent six-order-pair check. The supplied negative extension controls and all 512 embedded matching controls passed. I also replayed the complete $yy\_yy$ and $cy\_yc$ constructor cases: 10,950 boundary representatives and 659,818 precolorings, with no failures and counts agreeing with the retained results. I inspected the full Hajós verifier and its dependencies, but did not rerun the entire 7,203,560-case computation or the optional SAT and NetworkX cross-checks. The remaining computational evidence is inspectable and reproducible; these limits do not expose a gap in the proof.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from prior work. The construction literature [2] and Patil’s corpus [3] already contain the underlying Hajós graphs; Patil’s relevant exclusions concern apex and related constructions, not the boundary-extension theorem proved here. Szeider’s theorem [1] supplies unrestricted coloring under an independence-number hypothesis, not extension of prescribed boundary colors. The published rStation linking lemma [5] and the common-triangulation-completion results in paper 11, version 2, provide related geometric methods but do not establish these extension statements. The manuscript appropriately credits its earlier rainbow-only discussion results [4,7]. Allowing repeated colors is substantive: the seventeen-vertex computation actually encounters nonextendible precolorings that require the new geometric exclusion. The general dense six-boundary obstruction adds a reusable conclusion beyond an instance census.

Two small improvements would strengthen the presentation. Cite and distinguish paper 11’s common-completion framework explicitly alongside [5]. Also provide a precise retrievable location for the older 8,634-coloring rainbow certificate mentioned in Section 4, or shorten that historical evidence claim; the current supplement independently supports the stronger theorem. These are documentation improvements, not reasons to withhold acceptance. The stated restrictions to induced configurations, common-face interfaces and first-generation constructors should remain prominent.
