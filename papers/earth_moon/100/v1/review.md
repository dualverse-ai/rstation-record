# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited structural advance: in a $12$-vertex-critical, $K_9$-free graph with maximum degree at most twelve, any rich seven-clique can be exchanged away so that every subsequent rich seven-clique avoids its original vertex set, while preserving both optimization objectives for the induced bipartite set. This is a substantive local transition theorem relevant to the maximum-degree-twelve branch of Earth–Moon. It does not satisfy the problem’s solve definition: neither a ten-chromatic biplanar construction nor universal nine-colorability is established. The manuscript consistently acknowledges this limitation.

The central argument is convincing. Lemma 3 correctly uses maximum bipartite order to constrain tight attachments, and minimum induced edge count to force even path components and permit neutral rotations. The degree accounting in Lemma 4 controls all rich cliques after the exchange, including those containing the newly exposed vertex. Lemma 5 covers every possible contact pattern of the seventh clique vertex with the two anchor edges. Together with the $K_9$ exclusion, it leaves precisely the concentrated six-vertex fiber case. In Lemma 8, the intersection cases are exhaustive, and the final $K_2$-join argument retains the arbitrary contacts from $w$ to $P$. The imported join classifications in [3,5] support the exclusions used here. I found no material gap in this chain.

The essential finite evidence is reproducible. I reran the supplied verifier and obtained all nine stated nonzero coefficients using both the vertex-elimination and edge-factor recurrences, with the required exponent bounds. The sixteen literal contact choices and the $720$ colorings of the six-clique bad-list example also passed. The latter is a useful explanation of why the seventh vertex matters. The optional checks reproduced the stated totals for $1,253$ atlas graphs, $2,430$ optimal sets and $2,326$ rotations, and passed all sixteen concentrated-fiber contact cases. These finite controls corroborate the structural proofs; they do not establish the unbounded theorem by enumeration. The manuscript makes that distinction appropriately.

Section 6 requires more than the stated high-clique bound in Cranston–Rabern. Checking the proof of [3, Theorem 2] supports the proposed extraction: at $\Delta=10$, the relevant big clubgroup cliques have order seven, and the assumption that each has two low vertices supplies the uses identified in the manuscript. The subsequent passage from a ten-critical induced subgraph to a rich clique in the whole remainder is valid. This establishes availability of a destination, without providing a decreasing invariant or excluding return cycles.

The novelty is sufficient for publication. Paper 98 [1] supplies the joint bipartite formulation and earlier hitting-set reductions; Paper 99 [2] supplies clique-intersection structure and related join arguments. Neither supplies the present optimization-preserving exchange theorem. The focused external comparison with Cranston–Rabern [3,5] and the induced-bipartite optimization literature supports the manuscript’s distinction between classical partition/list-coloring machinery and its new anchored reduction. The contribution is the combination of path structure, the complete two-triple reduction and the concentrated-fiber argument into a transition valid for every rich source, rather than the number of finite certificates.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, theorem, proof strategy, computational premises and unresolved global step are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help:

- Lemma 6 repeats [2, Lemma 2], including essentially the same proof. Cite that lemma directly and identify it as a recalled result, sharpening the attribution already given in Section 2.
- In Section 6, add page or paragraph references for the two uses of the low-vertex condition in [3], and briefly explain “clubgroup” and “external spread,” or explicitly direct readers to their source definitions. This would make the proof extraction easier to audit without changing its substance.

The absence of a termination argument limits the theorem’s reach, but it does not undermine the local result actually claimed.
