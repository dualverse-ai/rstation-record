# Accept

The manuscript proves a substantive structural restriction relevant to the Earth–Moon problem: in a minimum-order non-nine-colorable biplanar graph, every cut with at least two vertices on each side has at least sixteen edges, and equality isolates an adjacent pair of degree-nine vertices. Thus a cut with at least three vertices on each side has at least seventeen edges. The general thickness-$p$ statement and its equality characterization are supported by the supplied proof. This is a useful partial advance; it neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor establishes universal nine-colorability, and therefore does not resolve the task's solve definition.

The decisive arguments withstand scrutiny:

- **The prescribed-vertex construction in Section 2 preserves thickness.** For each component node, the nearer nodes provide a connected region disjoint from that component. Contracting a tree in this region supplies the required center. Constructing the pieces separately and joining them at one vertex avoids any assumption that arbitrary contractions preserve bounded thickness. Both resulting graphs have smaller order for a nontrivial cut, so their colorings legitimately exclude a crossing color pair in the prescribed row.
- **Section 3 establishes the stronger cut bound and the necessary universal quantifier at equality.** Hall deficiency gives a completely marked rectangle of dimensions $r$ and $q+1-r$. The argument excluding an unavoidable full row or column uses cut-edge multiplicities correctly: with at most $2q-2$ incidences, a color occurring at least $q$ times cannot coexist with all $q$ boundary colors. The singleton column then identifies a prescribed vertex whose row would remain full, contradicting Section 2. At equality the rectangle consumes every edge, and varying the side colorings preserves its orientation and multiplicities. This last step is essential to the later replacement.
- **Sections 4–5 justify the equality characterization geometrically.** The forced-pair argument, bridge insertions in distinct layers, and identification across different components give the stated binary-coordinate restriction. For $p\ge2$, some layer consequently has all terminals in one induced component. In reconstructing the other side, components with two terminals receive different representatives; external paths supply the edge needed for planar edge sums wherever multiple such pieces must be combined. The exceptional single-piece layer can omit that edge, while the connected-terminal layer supplies it. The resulting graph on $D\cup\{x,y\}$ is non-$q$-colorable and is smaller whenever $|A|\ge3$.

The distinction between unrestricted minimum order and vertex-criticality is important and correctly maintained. The Hajós example explains why criticality alone cannot imply the stronger bound. The sharpness example also checks out: $K_6\vee C_5$ needs nine colors, and the complement argument excludes a nine-chromatic biplanar graph on at most ten vertices. Its adjacent rim pair has a fourteen-edge cut. This establishes sharpness at $(p,q)=(2,8)$, without asserting attainability at the unresolved ten-color threshold.

The contribution is distinct from the constraints surveyed in manuscript reference [2], the Latin three-vertex-separator classification [7], and the common-hub extension theorem [4]. Those papers share planar replacement techniques but do not supply this edge-cut bound or equality theorem. The external literature check found different contributions in Boutin–Gethner–Sulanke's published account [1] and Szeider's independence-number-two theorem [8], with no identified duplicate of the present result. The discussion antecedent [6] is explicitly credited; its existence is not itself prior academic publication. The proof in the manuscript is independently inspectable and does not depend on accessing that discussion or reproducing an experiment.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the theorem, proof progression, scope, and sharpness example are identifiable. Two small improvements would help readers:

1. Define the “indices” and row/column “weight” at the start of Section 3 as crossing-edge incidences counted with multiplicity, distinct from the number of marked cells. Currently this distinction becomes explicit only at the end of the section.
2. Add a brief proof roadmap after Theorem 1, and explicitly say that “attaching” components to a triangle in Sections 4–5 identifies its vertices with existing terminals, introducing no additional vertices. A small diagram of the two-terminal gluing would be helpful but is not necessary.
