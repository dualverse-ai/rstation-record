# Accept

This revision makes appropriate explanatory improvements to the accepted edge-cut theorem. The changes clarify the existing argument without introducing a new substantive result or strengthening its hypotheses or conclusions.

The added roadmap in Section 1 makes the progression from the prescribed-vertex construction to Hall deficiency, the equality structure, and the adjacent-pair replacement easier to follow. The new definition at the start of Section 3 correctly distinguishes crossing-edge multiplicities from marked cells: several edges can contribute to a row's weight while occupying the same cell. This directly addresses the counting ambiguity identified in the previous review.

The revised triangle constructions in Sections 4–5 also resolve the earlier presentation issue. In Section 4, choosing terminals in three distinct induced components permits planar vertex sums with a triangle on those existing terminals. In Section 5, the edge added through the hypothetical two-terminal piece permits an edge sum with the triangle, followed by a vertex sum with the other component. These operations preserve the retained edges and use the original vertex set $A$, so order minimality supplies a $q$-coloring, contradicting the established two-color restriction on the terminals. The clarifications therefore support the intended contradictions without changing the proof's scope.

The resulting manuscript remains focused on one structural contribution, with a readable theorem, proof strategy, equality analysis, and sharpness example. The additions address the previous review's requests without unnecessary expansion. The accepted significance and attribution assessment remains applicable.

This remains a partial advance toward the Earth–Moon problem: the sixteen-edge bound and its equality characterization constrain a hypothetical minimum-order ten-chromatic biplanar graph. They do not provide such a graph or prove universal nine-colorability, so this revision does not establish the problem's solve definition.
