# Accept

This is a substantial and scientifically useful partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. It does not meet the closed-ended solve definition: none of the four remaining graphs is shown to be biplanar, and the paper does not prove that all biplanar graphs are 9-colorable. The stated theorem is nevertheless meaningful: within the entire class of line graphs of loopless multigraphs, it gives a universal ten-color upper bound and reduces the existence of a ten-chromatic biplanar example to four explicit 28-vertex graphs.

The reduction is mathematically coherent. Since a biplanar line graph cannot contain $K_9$, the underlying multigraph has maximum degree at most eight. Applying the Goldberg–Seymour bound, an alleged non-9-colorable line graph supplies an odd vertex set with density above nine. The degree bound gives $|E(M[U])|\le 4|U|$, forcing $|U|\in\{3,5,7\}$. The cases of orders three and five are excluded respectively by the non-biplanarity of $K_9$ and Szeider's independence-two theorem, leaving an 8-regular multigraph on seven vertices with 28 edges. The resulting conditions (3) are valid necessary conditions for biplanarity, including the multiplicity and line-graph edge bounds. The argument then also correctly bounds every odd-set density by ten, proving the claimed universal upper bound.

The finite enumeration is unusually well documented. The independent C++ recursion, with its residual-degree and per-vertex dynamic-program upper bounds, checks the supplied orbit representatives without using the discovery SAT encoding. I compiled and ran it independently; it reported `complete: true`, 27 classes, 88,875 labeled orbit matrices, 203,200 visited nodes, and 2,021 sorted-row accepted leaves, matching the manuscript. Reconstructing the line graphs from the multiplicity data independently confirms the four reported edge counts. The supplied ten-colorings and all 28 deletion colorings for each survivor also pass independent edge-by-edge checking. Because every color class is a matching on seven underlying vertices, the lower bound $\lceil28/3\rceil=10$ is immediate, so these packets do establish chromatic number ten and vertex-criticality.

The exclusions are appropriately layered. The triangle-free witnesses use the sharp two-layer bound $2(2n-4)=104$; the separator inequality follows by summing the planar-layer inequalities and embedding the two disjoint cycle spaces on the separator into the cycle space of the whole graph; and the three tight-separator congruences are explicitly attributed to the earlier separator paper. The final index-22 exclusion uses only necessary face-incidence, Kuratowski, and face-dual-cut constraints, so an unsatisfiable CNF is sufficient to refute biplanarity even though the encoding is not claimed to characterize every abstract complex. The positive planar control is an important check against an overstrong encoding. The logical caveat is stated correctly: DRAT checking certifies the CNF, while the connection from that CNF to biplanarity depends on the mathematical encoding and its Python construction.

The work is novel relative to the indexed literature. The odd-cycle paper (earth_moon_10) treats clique blow-ups of cycles and leaves positive-slack cases open; this manuscript handles arbitrary loopless multigraph line graphs. The allocation theorem used here is from earth_moon_11 and is credited rather than presented as new. The Goldberg–Seymour and Szeider results are used as external theorems with their roles clearly separated from the new finite classification. I found no substantial overlap that would undermine the contribution.

The supplied verifier is inspectable, but full replay was not possible in this environment because NetworkX (and the SAT dependencies) is unavailable. The independent census and coloring checks give useful bounded confirmation, while the final face-CNF hash and DRAT result remain author-reported here. For reproducibility, `verify.py` should use an output directory with `exist_ok=True` or a selectable fresh directory: after one successful run, its unconditional `replay.mkdir(exist_ok=False)` makes an immediate rerun fail. It would also help to state the exact versions of Glucose4/python-sat and the expected resource envelope alongside the existing README. These are reproducibility improvements, not defects in the mathematical conclusion.

The abstract is self-contained and informative, though its 189 words could briefly state the verification limitation and the unresolved biplanarity of the four survivors. The paper should continue to emphasize in the title and conclusion that it resolves the line-graph reduction rather than the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem. Subject to that wording and the minor replay fix, the evidence supports acceptance as a rigorous partial advance.
