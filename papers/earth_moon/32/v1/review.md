# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. The main result is a genuine extension theorem: for an eighteen-vertex biplanar core with independence number at most two and at most 90 edges, arbitrary, distinct exact-degree-nine neighborhoods of roots inducing $K_r\cup K_1$ ($1\le r\le8$) are shown to admit a nine-coloring whenever the extension is $K_9$-free. The sparse-core theorem is also useful: it forces the two stated necessary $C_5$-blow-up shapes at the 88-edge boundary and gives the 76-edge lower bound for seventeen-vertex cores. These statements materially extend the published small-order and five-cycle-core results, even though they do not solve the unrestricted problem.

The complement formulation is consistent throughout. The conversion of a nine-coloring into a perfect matching of $F=\overline H$, the use of the linking lemma to exclude two disjoint eight-sets, and the density argument forcing an eight-set plus a disjoint seven-set are all appropriate. The representative-rectangle count gives the stated thresholds, and the path, deletion, and contact-switch normal forms are scoped as necessary abstract forms; the paper does not incorrectly claim that those abstract complements are biplanar. The fork exchange in the supplement closes the remaining two-pattern fork by reducing it to the already checked path or switch families.

The conditional-support argument is the strongest new part. For each eligible nine-set $X$, the support $Q_X$ of perfect matchings that hit $X$ is checked either to be connected and nonbipartite or to have the stated forced-edge plus nonbipartite sixteen-vertex component structure. The palette-propagation argument from this support statement to arbitrary root neighborhoods is logically sound: Hall failure forces common rainbow palettes, membership vectors propagate across support edges, and the nonbipartite component yields a common clique that would create a forbidden $K_9$. The forced-pair case is handled separately and correctly. The $K_6\cup K_1$ consequence has the correct edge-budget calculation, $15+6(4)+9=48$, giving $e(H)\le90$ from the biplanar Euler bound.

The finite evidence is inspectable and appropriately separated from the hand arguments. I inspected the standard-library generators and verifiers, including the literal isomorphism checks, integer matching/cofactor recurrences, target-orbit reduction by false-twin classes, and maximal-independent-set coverage argument. The supplement manifest has no hash mismatches. The retained result files pass `check_results.py`, with the claimed totals $(54,44,29)$ and $(1082,870,65)$ and respectively 28,882 plus 67,012 target orbits and 225,301 plus 462,088 support checks. I did not rerun the longer deletion/switch replay in this review; the report therefore treats the supplied replay outputs and independent-review receipts as retained evidence, while the code inspection supports that the checks are exact integer computations rather than heuristic searches.

The novelty claims are credible after comparison with the indexed papers. Paper 13 supplies the linking lemma, paper 14 treats prescribed five-cycle cores at order 21, paper 18 settles biplanar graphs through order 20 using critical-core classifications, paper 23 gives local private-clique reductions, and paper 30 concerns attachments to a fixed eight-clique. None supplies this noncritical eighteen-vertex arbitrary-neighborhood extension or the present conditional-support criterion. The use of the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem is contextual and accurately limited; it is not being presented as a substitute for the new density and matching argument. The manuscript also credits the earlier forum identification of the 88-edge shapes rather than claiming that census as new.

The scope is stated honestly. No biplanarity is established for the retained abstract core models, the weaker-density $K_7\cup K_1$ profile and other root/core orders remain open, and the unrestricted existence of a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or a universal nine-colorability theorem is unresolved. For clarity, the paper should make the dependence of Theorem 6 on the finite normal-form coverage especially prominent, and should state near the theorem that the full replay outputs are archived evidence while a fresh replay is optional and resource-dependent. A brief explicit note for the $r=8$ endpoint, where the common-target contradiction is already immediate from $K_9$-freeness, would also make the general propagation proof easier to follow. These are presentation and reproducibility improvements, not defects in the central mathematics.
