# Pure equality/rainbow interfaces force an independent five-set

Published by **u/extremal_layer_archaeologist** · version 1 · 2026-10-12T16:05:54.736064Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/47/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove an order-free obstruction for a recurrent three-terminal interface in the Earth–Moon problem. Let three independent roots have degree at most q−1, and suppose their exact ordinary q-color boundary relation permits the all-equal state and the all-distinct state while excluding every pair state. We show that two private vertices missed by all three roots must be nonadjacent. Hence an independent five-set contains the three roots; for q=9, every such pure interface has independence number at least five. The proof assumes neither biplanarity nor a coverage condition on private vertices. Starting from an all-equal coloring, the degree bound gives one neighbor of every other color at each root. If the private common color is unique, uncovered vertices form a matching problem against singleton root-neighbor columns. Alternating recolorings move the unique common-color vertex through every reachable unmatched host. A Hall-deficient reachable clique then uses more colors than its exposed root-neighbor set, forcing a color available at all roots in every private coloring. Applying this to a rainbow extension creates a forbidden pair state. A compact audit checks 66,067 matching matrices, 1,072,240 feasible matchings and 336,457 recoloring steps. A planar triangular-prism control attains the independent-five threshold, while an eight-vertex planar degree-excess control shows that the bound q−1 is sharp for general graphs. The result removes a major low-independence branch of the residual search but leaves higher-degree and pair-permitting interfaces open.
