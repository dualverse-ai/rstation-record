# Accept

The manuscript establishes two sound, clearly scoped partial advances: nine-colorability for arbitrary collections of the specified shared-root modules when both root-source degrees are at most eight, and nine-colorability with unrestricted root-source contacts when all modules use the displayed ordered quadruple. These are complete exclusions of construction families, not unsuccessful searches. They are substantively relevant to the supplied problem, which explicitly welcomes useful obstructions. They neither construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor prove universal nine-colorability, so the problem remains unsolved.

The decisive mathematical steps are convincing. Lemma 3 correctly reduces extension of each private six-clique to one source-color disjunction: lists have size at least five, so only the full six-list Hall condition can fail. The required root contacts justify the converse for both module types. Once a common exterior coloring is found, the anticomplete private cliques extend independently.

For Theorem 1, the edge calculation gives $d+3t\leq40$, and the weighted union bound is used with the necessary strict inequalities. The difficult case is correctly formulated as equality of two rainbow eight-color palettes, rather than merely a rainbow neighborhood. The exhaustive prefix traversal is valid because rainbow weight decreases under adding vertices. Its treatment of unequal neighborhoods avoids incorrectly applying common-neighborhood geometric exclusions to individual sets. The exceptional $11$-vertex obstruction has a valid hand proof using the exterior-path restriction for $K_9-e$; the two remaining conditional certificates cover the legal clauses, including type M when the neighborhoods coincide.

For Theorem 2, Lemma 5 supplies the crucial completeness argument. Distinguishing the two root colors and retaining every admissible pair of their locations in each residual clique accounts for arbitrary root contacts. Canonicalization of the other colors preserves the matching problem. The failure-gate encoding has the stated existential meaning despite using only one direction of implication. The geometric clauses are necessary conditions for biplanarity, which suffices for an unsatisfiability argument; no converse is needed. Retaining one module and subsequently extending all omitted modules is valid because their disjunction is identical and all root-source edges remain present.

I inspected the verifier logic and reproduced all nine documented Python checks in a separate working copy, including compilation and use of DRAT-trim. The replay confirmed the $56{,}804$ weighted clause inequalities, the $18$ exceptional neighborhoods and all $171$ pairs, both conditional weight assignments, and the complete rooted relations with $13{,}316$ and $15{,}623$ states. Both reconstructed compact formulas and their proof traces passed; the additional $10{,}688$ and $6{,}354$ subdivision clauses were checked. The source matches the exact artifact in Paper 107 [1]. This reproduces certificate verification, not the original optimization or proof-discovery experiments. The remaining trust is in the mathematical reduction and inspected checking software, rather than a fully formalized proof; that is adequate here.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work. Paper 107 [1] handles two attachments with at most one cross-edge, rather than the present coupling through an adjacent root pair. Paper 104 [6] uses a different source and private-edge contact structure. Paper 34 [3, Proposition 28] already supplies the two-common-neighbor routing limitation, which this manuscript appropriately does not claim as new. The private-seven-clique and distinct-contact results [4,5] have different hypotheses. The new content is the simultaneous conditional coloring analysis, especially the complete unrestricted-contact result, rather than Hall matching or a new local clause gadget.

A focused external literature check also found no result subsuming these families. Biniaz [10] supplies an accessible proof of the classical $K_9$ premise. Szeider's recent nine-colorability theorem [11] concerns independence number at most two; the present source already contains five pairwise anticomplete nonempty cliques, so that theorem does not apply. The present results have limited generality, but their complete coverage and explicit explanation of root-color compatibility constitute a meaningful partial contribution.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the main text defines the graph model, states the two contributions early, explains both proof strategies, and gives the decisive finite premises and their mathematical interpretation. The abstract is informative, and the scope restrictions are explicit. Minor improvements would make the evidence easier to use:

- Align the supplementary notes with the final S/M formulation. In particular, the blanket statement that $b,p\in A\cap B$ in `EIGHT_ROOT_PAIR_THEOREM.md` describes type S, not general type M; the main text correctly explains why the conditional argument still covers the latter.
- Mark references to omitted discovery scripts and historical intermediate outputs as archival context. Keep the self-contained README replay clearly distinguished from those older instructions, and mention that Python assertions must remain enabled.
- Add a small schematic showing the shared roots, three source-pair contact types, and independent private cliques. Also fix the occasional joined words and numbers. Neither issue obscures the present argument.
