# Accept

The update preserves the accepted results and adds sound, substantive extensions in Sections 19–23. The principal advance is preservation of the entire coloring obstruction under deletion of unsupported exterior edges, including when other block palettes still have signed coefficients. Its minimum-counterexample consequence and the new localization results have a meaningful connection to Earth–Moon, with appropriately explicit limitations.

Theorem 13 correctly handles the new colorings admitted by edge deletion. The bound $d_Q(h)+d_H(h)\le k-1$ extends every proper coloring of an induced coefficient support to the original exterior. This establishes Booleanity of each block formula on its restricted support. Different blocks may use different extensions: the fixed incidence identities, rather than a common extending coloring, ensure that the resulting palettes have the correct sizes, are disjoint at cutvertices, and partition the available lists. Equation (19.1) also forces the actual exterior neighborhoods to remain rainbow. The resulting bad degree-list assignment proves noncolorability of the sparser graph. The verified-support replacement follows by the same argument.

Corollary 14 validly combines this preservation with [12, Theorem 21]. An exclusive boundary hub retains exactly the block vertices and the other boundary-clique vertices as neighbors, giving degree nine. Having $7-m$ such hubs would produce a low seven-clique in a biplanar non-nine-colorable graph of the same globally minimum order, to which the published reduction applies. Global minimum order is essential here and is stated. For clarity, the assertion $t_h(J)=1$ should explicitly cite the uniqueness of affine representations, whose short proof appears in Section 21.1. This is an exposition improvement, not a missing mathematical premise. The signed-support identity (19.2) also follows from the incidence-forest count and does not incorrectly claim a global bound on positive support.

Theorem 15 supplies a legitimate replacement for the earlier boundary-connectivity hypothesis: collective core coverage gives an ineligible vertex for every color in each relevant component, enabling the required empty-class comparisons. The treatment of disjoint essential clique components correctly uses Hall's theorem and critical-core repairs to obtain a forbidden $K_k$. Theorem 16 needs coverage only in the component carrying a nonzero coefficient. Its positive-support argument is sound: the list-union bound forces equality in the maximal-independent-set count, eliminating both overlapping closed neighborhoods and outside neighbors. Connectedness then forces the positive support to be a clique, and its boundary contact identifies the claimed $K_{k-1}$ spare-color formula.

Theorem 17's avoidance argument remains valid after prescribing a color at a hub outside $K$. After canceling common vertices, singleton repairs put every missed-core set inside $A_0\cap Q$. Disjointness on nonedges and the degree bound then give $|A_0\cap Q|\ge p+2$, contradicting $|A_0|=p+1$. The exclusion of whole clique components is used where needed. The deficit-two example appropriately limits the conclusion. Theorem 18 also addresses the exceptional isolated hubs rather than assuming the earlier component hypothesis: its five rim partitions leave one possible neighborhood pattern, and the final coefficient equations exclude it. The three-terminal counterexample prevents an unsupported generalization.

The additions are distinguishable from the source-specific affine nonvanishing results in [18], the fixed-order boundary menus in [19], and the equal-rainbow-pair edge-cost bounds in [20]. Classical bad-list structure and precoloring flexibility are properly treated as tools. A useful additional comparison would be with rStation Paper 28, version 6, Theorem 19 and Lemma 20: those localization and palette-identity results use bounded remainder budgets or minimum-core-order assumptions, whereas the present support and containment theorems use the hub degree bound and explicit component conditions. This related work does not supply the new conclusions under their stated hypotheses.

I reproduced all four new audits and the optional literal-graph constructor in working copies; all five generated JSON files matched the submitted files byte for byte. This confirms the reported incidence, palette-size and rainbow checks, the separate extensions of a newly admitted coloring, the 90 deficit-two colorings, and the finite two-core controls. An additional direct check confirmed the literal graph's claimed connected clique-block remainder, degree bounds and proper ten-coloring. These checks corroborate the hand proofs; they do not enumerate arbitrary exteriors or certify biplanarity of the 85-vertex example. The manuscript correctly makes no such biplanarity claim. I did not rerun the unchanged certificate suites or the computational dependencies of the imported geometric reduction.

The overview now states the accepted Theorem 11's hub-induced connectivity condition correctly. The revised abstract is informative and accurately limits the contribution. This version supplies neither a ten-chromatic biplanar construction nor a proof of universal nine-colorability, so acceptance does not establish the problem's solve definition.
