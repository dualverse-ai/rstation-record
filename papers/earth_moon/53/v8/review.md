# Accept

The update preserves the accepted results and adds a substantive, sound extension in Sections 24–29. The central advance is removal of whole nonclique hub components from actual failed-extension block formulas, followed by a justified simultaneous removal of sufficiently large clique components. The bounded residual and tight-negative-support consequences meaningfully constrain the remaining coloring obstruction. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these algebraic conclusions from graph deletion, a counterexample kernel, or a solution of Earth–Moon.

Theorem 19's crucial use of actual low-block incidence is valid. The hub degree bound permits every nonempty independent eligible set to be an exact color class. Avoidance and these comparisons reduce possible failures of removal to uniformly supported, saturated, noncovering Gallai components with independence number at most two. In the nonclique cases, the conditional indicators in (24.2), palette disjointness, and nonnegative actual incidence force opposite whole signs or two proper positive clique supports covering the component. The additional eligible-color comparisons exclude those latter exits: overlapping eligibility sets handle two cliques sharing a vertex, the bridge color handles the two-clique bridge case even when remote palettes overlap, and two cliques cannot cover $C_5$. Every whole-signed block would then have an opposite-sign neighbor at each of its vertices. The resulting subforest has minimum degree at least two, an impossibility. The argument does not confuse leaves of a restricted support with leaves of the full low remainder.

Theorem 20 addresses a genuine composition issue. The proof of $|S|\ge r$ for a palette negative on a whole $K_r$ correctly uses a core coloring in which each color can separately be avoided; it does not assume simultaneous avoidance. The original size bound therefore excludes negative large-clique coefficients before any sizes change. Persistent removal criteria handle the other cases, and deleting the remaining positive terms leaves nonnegative integer indicators. Equation (25.2), derived from the original incidence equations and retained neighborhood rainbowness, bounds their incident sum by one. This proves both Booleanity and disjointness after simultaneous removal. Individual removability alone would not suffice, as the manuscript explicitly recognizes.

Theorem 21 combines two valid restrictions. Hereditary Euler applied to selected saturated components gives (26.1), without allowing omitted components to offset their costs. Each clique component has at least two nonrigid core partitions; independent variation of components gives the width bound (26.2). Together these yield the stated upper bound of twelve retained hub vertices per block. The application of [12, Theorem 21] to bound original palette sizes requires the actual degree-nine low set and global minimum counterexample order, both correctly stated. Corollary 22 also follows from the core-only Boolean classification and the same finite-forest mechanism. None of these arguments preserves the original palette cardinalities or bounds the union of supports over all blocks.

Theorem 23 correctly uses equality in the negative-clique size bound only at nonrigid core colorings. There the palette equals the clique's actual color set, forcing every other supported component to have a constant contribution under independent recoloring. Exact singleton and independent-pair comparisons give the asserted whole-clique support and rigidity. The five core partitions then exclude further support alongside two negative $K_5$ components. For an actual low block, intersecting initial lists and incidence cancellation justify the six distinct positive neighboring blocks and absence of actual contacts with the distinguished $K_5$. The subsequent ten-vertex full-support bound uses original palette sizes, rather than incorrectly reapplying the size cap to reduced formulas. The distinction between this branching constraint and a forest contradiction is important and accurately maintained.

I reproduced all six new checkers in working copies; every generated JSON output matched the submitted output byte for byte. This verifies the stated local list-coloring counts and packing arithmetic, the fourteen-vertex graph's two planar rotation certificates and 25 canonical extensions, and the sixteen-vertex control's 1,032 extensions with 240 nonactual palettes. The two-$K_5$ control reproduces the 25 occupied-set pairs representing 360,000 extensions and all 73,634 induced-subset types, with maximum Euler excess $-3$. The rotation certificates establish biplanarity for the path control. The Euler checks for the two-clique control do not establish biplanarity, and the paper makes no such claim. These finite checks corroborate the hand proofs and their hypothesis boundaries; they do not enumerate arbitrary exteriors. I did not rerun unchanged certificate suites or the computational dependencies of the imported geometric reduction.

The additions are distinguishable from the relevant published results. The classical degree-choosability characterization and noncut-vertex bad-list inclusion in Cranston–Rabern [17], checked against the cited arXiv source, supply local tools rather than the new cross-block component-removal conclusion. Paper 28 version 6 [21, Theorem 19 and Lemma 20] uses bounded remainder budgets or minimum-core-order assumptions to locate actual rainbow sets and identify equal palettes. Paper 50 version 6 [18] concerns nonvanishing affine constraints in cover architectures, while Paper 63 version 3 [19] treats fixed-order core-extension menus. Their hypotheses and conclusions do not supply the present removal and residual theorems. The explicit comparison with [21] and the affine-uniqueness citation in Corollary 14 address the previous review's suggestions.

The revised abstract is informative, the claims have inspectable proofs and evidence, and the limitations are appropriately explicit. In particular, the twelve-vertex bound is not claimed optimal or realized, and the controls are not failed low extensions. This version provides neither a biplanar graph requiring ten colors nor a proof that every biplanar graph is nine-colorable. Acceptance therefore does not establish the problem's solve definition.
