# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds sound, substantive structural results in Theorems 9–12. Removing the independent-hub count restriction and deriving constraints on actual neighborhoods from universal coloring failure are meaningful advances beyond the previous one- and two-hub results. The new conclusions remain appropriately conditional on the core and exterior hypotheses.

Theorem 9's occurrence argument is valid. Universal failure forces exact low degrees, universally rainbow exterior neighborhoods, and uniform private neighborhoods within each leaf block. The disjoint missed-core sets then restrict each low vertex to at most three hubs and derive the required small core order. After excluding a private true-twin five-clique, the hereditary Euler calculation leaves only the $q=11$, type-two $K_5$ leaf. Two such leaves violate Euler whether their hub pairs coincide, overlap once, or are disjoint, with excesses $2,2,4$. The use of hereditary Euler, rather than just the whole-graph inequality of Theorem 7, is explicit and essential to these witnesses. The biplanar consequence correctly imports [16, Theorem 24] and [12, Theorem 21] under global minimum-order minimality; ordinary deletion-criticality would not justify coloring the smaller quotient.

Theorem 10 correctly handles adjacent hubs under $d_Q(h)+d_H(h)\le k-1$. The separate two-choice repair at a specified hub follows from partial-coloring extension and the nonedge-disjoint set bound; simultaneous repairs are not assumed. Comparing two colorings differing only at that hub places its actual neighborhood vector in the block-incidence column space. Singleton core colorings and the degree identity then place the all-ones vector, and every core-neighborhood vector, in that space because $q>k$. Leaf elimination establishes uniqueness and integrality. The resulting signed coordinates are explicitly distinguished from actual vertex subsets, avoiding an otherwise unjustified interpretation of the affine formulas.

The localization proof in Theorem 11 addresses the important exceptional cases. Nonempty independent eligible sets can be prescribed as exact color classes in a hub component, while an ineligible vertex permits the empty class. Essential components require additional treatment: singleton and nonadjacent-pair comparisons eliminate noncomplete components, and a persistent clique exception would force a forbidden $K_k$. The final singleton and repeated-core-class comparisons eliminate the affine constant and all coefficients outside the boundary. The application to neighboring blocks is correctly local and does not claim propagation through arbitrary all-cut blocks.

Theorem 12's component-coverage assumption supports the empty-class comparisons needed for its classification. The four possible integer constants reduce to the three stated forms; in the delicate $a=2$ case, equality in (17.4) forces whole clique components, contradicting that assumption. The omitted-hypothesis example establishes a functional distinction from every actual vertex color and every fixed eight-clique spare color, rather than merely exhibiting a different coefficient representation. Its scope is accurately limited to an abstract palette counterexample.

The comparison with the relevant published work is adequate. Classical degree-choosability and the bad-list inclusion lemma in [17] supply the block-palette machinery, but do not themselves give conservation of actual neighborhoods across all core colorings. The arbitrary-list classification in [15], the source-specific affine nonvanishing results in [18], and the fixed-order eleven-core extension results in [19] have different hypotheses and conclusions. The new results extend the accepted framework without substantially duplicating these contributions, and the imported geometric reductions are clearly attributed.

One minor scope clarification should be applied to the abstract and abbreviated summaries: Theorem 11 requires the hub-induced graph $H[T]$ to be connected and noncomplete. Saying only that the whole boundary $B=\{c\}\cup T$ is connected and noncomplete does not express that hypothesis, since $c$ is complete to $T$ by universal rainbowness. The theorem statement itself is precise. The abstract otherwise clearly conveys the question, results, evidence and limitations.

Acceptance concerns these conditional structural advances. This version neither supplies a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, and therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.
