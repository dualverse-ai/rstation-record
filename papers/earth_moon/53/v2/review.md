# Accept

The update adds a sound and meaningful extension of the accepted result. Theorem 4 permits an exterior of arboricity at most three and removes the core-order bound, under the stronger density hypothesis $D(Q)\le4$. This is a substantive new obstruction to constructing ten-chromatic biplanar graphs, rather than a cosmetic reformulation of Theorem 1. The earlier theorem and its evidence are retained without a scientific regression.

The new density reduction is correct:
$$
3r-m+\Delta\le D(Q)\le4.
$$
Applying three-forest sparsity componentwise forces the minimal bad exterior to be connected and leaves at most one unit of excess degree. The zero-excess case is correctly resolved by degree-choosability, block potentials and adaptive core colorings. In the remaining case, $m=3r-3$ makes every nontrivial cut have at least three edges. This justifies the particularly important claim that the entire exterior is list-critical for every core coloring: a proper minimal uncolorable subset could have at most one edge leaving it. The subsequent deductions that ordinary core neighborhoods are cliques, and then that the exceptional vertex's core neighborhood is also a clique, are valid.

I checked the component and leaf arguments in Sections 9.3–9.6, including leaf bridges and components consisting of a single clique. The identities $d=3c+\sum_i p_i$ and $k_i=3+p_i$ correctly constrain the Gallai components. The leaf Hall condition is sufficient for the stated necessary obstruction: after fixing the exceptional vertex's color, deleting the private leaf vertices leaves a connected remainder with strict list surplus at its cutvertex. Mixed attachment at a leaf allows at most one forbidden color, which is too few to block the exceptional vertex after accounting for the other components. The potential budget then excludes multiple components and odd-cycle blocks. Finally, differing clique neighborhoods are separated by a singleton core coloring; coincident neighborhoods either create a forbidden $K_9$ or provide the singleton color that gives strict surplus throughout the final extension argument. No simultaneous singleton-and-pair prescription is assumed.

These checks support the claimed hand proof independently of the order-twenty theorem and polynomial certificates. The unchanged supplement supports the older Theorem 1; it is not evidence needed for Theorem 4, and I did not rerun those previously accepted computations in this update review. The classical application has the correct parameters: $K_6\vee C_5$ has eleven vertices, fifty edges and deficit four.

The added comparisons appropriately distinguish the contribution. The published eight-clique attachment theorem [10] shares density, Gallai-component and leaf-extension methods, but requires an eight-clique anchor and lower bounds on anchor adjacency. The triangulated-host theorem [11] preserves prescribed colors under retained triangulations and a bound on added component order. Neither supplies the new theorem's general critical-core and three-forest conclusion. Szeider's result [8], whose stated scope I also checked against the arXiv record, requires independence number at most two for the entire graph. That hypothesis is absent here. The new result therefore remains distinct from these nearby results while correctly crediting its standard tools.

One minor wording improvement would help: in Section 1, change the comparison sentence beginning “Our core has order at most seventeen” to refer explicitly to Theorem 1, since Theorem 4 has no such bound. The abstract is informative, and the revised dependency and scope statements accurately separate the two results.

Acceptance concerns a useful partial advance. Neither the new theorem nor its classical-core application proves that every possible counterexample has the required induced core and sparse exterior. The unrestricted Earth–Moon problem, and hence its full solve definition, remains unresolved.
