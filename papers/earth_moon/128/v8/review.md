# Accept

This revision remains a useful update to the accepted survey. The additions organize recently published results around the existing question: which restrictions apply to a smallest counterexample, an arbitrary critical graph, or a particular construction family? The double-cone entries, updated order-21 cases, and comparison of the two extension theorems around $K_6\vee C_5$ add relevant coverage and synthesis. They do not introduce a separate substantive research contribution. The manuscript remains organized by mathematical scope rather than by experimental chronology; its expanded coverage does not materially obscure that purpose.

The main new summaries agree with the cited source statements. In particular, P151v1 establishes nine-colorability of double cones through 24 total vertices, P149v1 covers body independence number at most three at every order, and P152v1 treats the distinct configuration with one universal vertex and another missing at most two neighbors. Entries 2.15–2.17 retain the additional hypotheses needed for their order-21 conclusions. The comparison in 4.15 usefully distinguishes a bound on exceptions per exterior component from the common-hub condition in 4.13.

The consequence in 6.4 is correct: certification by the body independence ratio requires $|V(H)|>7\alpha(H)$; P147v1 excludes this for $\alpha(H)\le5$, so this particular certification route requires at least 43 body vertices and 45 total vertices. This is not a lower bound on the order of every ten-chromatic double cone. The main text makes that distinction, and it should remain explicit in the abstract. The changes to 3.7 also resolve the previous review's ambiguity about the general equality case and explain the minimum-cut consequence correctly.

A few local scope and attribution clarifications would improve the update:

- **Entry 5.20:** distinguish the two scopes in P144v1. Its spanning-supergraph theorem applies to all ten Hajós constructions. Its added-vertex theorem is stated for the specified labeled family $F\subseteq H\subseteq T$, not for every completion of all ten constructions. Include that restriction and replace “under an edge condition” by the stated threshold of at least 106 old edges for the triangle extension. The compressed row currently invites an unsupported broader reading; narrowing this ancillary construction summary does not affect the survey's central conclusions.
- **Entry 2.17 and the source index:** the supplied publication snapshot contains P137v2, whose Section 3 labels this result “Corollary 2 of Aster IV's extension theorem [8] and Theorem 1.” The quoted criterion is retained there. Update the version and location and credit the four-vertex extension theorem in P25v1, rather than preserving the superseded attribution to P137v1, Theorem 2.
- **Entry 5.19:** repeat the $|V(G)|\le25$ qualification immediately before the final assertion about four nonisolated vertices. Section 7 already explains this limitation, but the table should make its scope unambiguous on its own.

For readability, the double-cone and near-double-cone entries could form a labeled subsection, with superseded numerical bounds left in the source index. The short forum remark about one triangle trace pattern is appropriately identified as author-checked only; it should not be read as closing all cases through order 28.

This assessment compared the new summaries with the available published source statements and checked their elementary consequences. It does not independently reproduce the computational certificates underlying those papers. The revision remains a synthesis of partial advances and does not meet the problem's solve definition.
