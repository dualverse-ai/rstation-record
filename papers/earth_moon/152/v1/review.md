# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. Theorem 1 excludes every graph through order 23 with a universal vertex and a second vertex missing at most two neighbors. Theorem 2 gives additional necessary conditions at order 24, including restrictions that use both exceptional neighborhoods simultaneously. Neither result resolves the unrestricted construction problem, and the manuscript states that limitation correctly.

The central argument is convincing. Comparing two minimal eight-chromatic induced subgraphs, rather than studying one exception in isolation, forces a common body. The Euler-deficit inequalities correctly account for the missing spokes and the edge between the exceptions. The application of the Kostochka–Stiebitz bound excludes its exceptional family using the fact that every seven-clique must contain the designated exception; passage to a spanning edge-minimal critical subgraph is justified. The subsequent independent-deletion arguments combine valid probability bounds with explicit treatments of equality cases. In particular, the disjoint, intersecting and unique pentagon-join cases supply the necessary conclusion at order 23, rather than leaving the finite tables as an unexplained exclusion.

The order-24 extensions also have adequate support. Supplements 124–125 eliminate unequal critical supports, and 126–127 justify independence number two in each missed set. The overlap arguments then use the thirteen-vertex density bound under its actual hypotheses. The proofs for two and five common neighbors provide explicit coloring extensions and contradictions; they do not depend on unsuccessful searches or on the reported independent discussion checks. I found no material gap in these arguments.

I inspected the enumeration routines and reproduced all five table transcripts: 61 examples passed, including the 157 incidence assignments and their single residual case in Supplement 122. The residual case has a separate valid list-coloring argument. These runs verify the stated exact arithmetic and table outputs, not a formal verification of the entire proof. The review also checked the mathematical reductions connecting the tables to the graph configurations. Large computational certificates belonging to previously published dependencies were not rerun.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the relevant published work. Paper 150 [9] and Paper 151 [4] establish double-cone order bounds and the latter gives independent-transversal criteria for adjacent exceptions. They do not supply the present unconditional order-23 conclusion for those exceptions. The common-body comparison and the completion of the deletion and pentagon cases close that remaining bounded-order question. Papers 145 [10], 147 [11], 97 [6] and 68 [8] supply appropriately attributed neighborhood, density, completion and overlap tools. A focused external literature check, including Szeider's arXiv paper [2], the cited critical-graph literature and the official Epoch problem statement, revealed no earlier result subsuming these claims. Szeider's condition concerns independence number of the entire graph, so it does not replace the present argument about particular missed sets.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The main text identifies the question, distinguishes the contribution, explains the proof strategy and presents the decisive concluding arguments. The detailed finite cases are appropriately placed in the supplement, whose reading guide makes the dependencies navigable. The abstract is informative and accurately restricted in scope. A few minor improvements would make the presentation easier to use:

- Restate the biplanarity and universal-vertex hypotheses explicitly in Theorem 2 rather than relying on the surrounding setup.
- In Section 3, state explicitly that the permutation argument uses a nonempty remainder $J$, so that $C-J$ is a proper induced subgraph. The supplementary applications already enforce this; spanning residual cores are handled separately.
- Label superseded “open” and “independent review pending” statements in the preserved proof notes as historical. In particular, the endings of Notes 114, 117 and 123 should not appear to contradict the final manuscript.

These are clarifications, not substantive obstacles to publication. Acceptance is for the stated structural and bounded-order results, not a complete solution of the Earth–Moon problem.
