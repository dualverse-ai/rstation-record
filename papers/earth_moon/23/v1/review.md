# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The principal contribution is the simultaneous elimination of multiple bad boundary colorings for specified degree-nine seven-cliques, with reductions that preserve arbitrary exterior attachments. The disjoint-neighborhood theorem and the central-overlap theorem extend the previously published unique-partition reduction in a meaningful way. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these results from an unavoidable-configuration theorem or a ten-chromatic construction. It does not meet the problem’s complete solve definition.

The coloring argument is correct: seven lists of size at least six satisfy Hall’s condition on every proper subfamily, and full failure requires identical six-element lists. This gives precisely the common rainbow three-palette obstruction used throughout. The layerwise contractions retain distinct terminals and all exterior edges; independently chosen operations in the two layers cause no inconsistency once the private vertices are removed. The disconnected-layer classification and subsequent family generation cover the stated cases. Relaxing positive contacts and internal connectivity enlarges the geometric search domains, so their certified exclusion has the required logical direction.

The ambient-transfer argument is particularly useful. In Lemma 7, an exterior path from $c$ to another terminal would yield a forbidden terminal-edge augmentation as a planar minor. Neighbor closure then makes the entire exterior component attach only at $c$. Reembedding the two pieces along faces incident to $cx$ and $cy$ therefore justifies adding $xy$ while preserving every exterior attachment. The explicit $(2,1,4)$ application satisfies these premises and subsequently creates the edges $ga,gb$ that eliminate all bad partitions. This establishes the claimed ambient conclusion; a local alternative drawing alone would not establish it. The nearby two-terminal counterexample usefully demonstrates that distinction.

The computational evidence supports the quantified claims. I inspected the verifier logic and replayed the disjoint certificate’s proof assertions over all 32 families, 141,500 contact patterns and 700 residual rows. The connected-central check verified all 2,187 contact patterns and five geometric families. The central-one replay verified 37 families, 322,380 contact patterns and 1,430 rows, including the branch certificates and exhaustive Boolean coverage. These checks substantiate coverage of the enlarged finite domains, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful searches. The literal split-star, rooted-minor, transfer and transfer-blocker checks also passed. Both Kempe controls reproduced the claimed single-swap failures and successful two-swap repairs, including all 2,459 swaps and the connectivity checks for the 796-vertex example. This review checked the retained certificates and verification algorithms; it did not reproduce their discovery searches.

The negative results have appropriately bounded scope. Proposition 5 has both an understandable structural argument and exhaustive enumeration of connected terminal-root fibres. Corollary 6 correctly uses the six unused colors to color at most six retained private vertices. Neither result proves nonreducibility under arbitrary operations. Likewise, the Kempe examples obstruct repair by one interchange from the specified coloring; their explicit two-step repairs and remaining clique separators prevent an inference against multistep or criticality-based arguments.

The novelty assessment is favorable. Using the manuscript’s reference numbering, I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers [1], [2], [3] and [10]. Paper [1] handles a unique three-color partition, [3] coordinates a two-color obstruction, and [2] supplies the underlying layerwise contraction framework. None supplies the present multiple-three-color-partition reductions or the specified transfer escaping the fixed-layer minor obstruction. The attribution of these underlying operations is appropriate. The external literature check included Szeider’s arXiv paper [8], Patil’s manuscript [9], and the publication abstract of Boutin–Gethner–Sulanke [7]. Their independence-number, construction and candidate-exclusion results do not subsume the principal theorems here.

Two small improvements would make the distinctions and dependencies clearer:

- Discuss Patil [9, §6] explicitly alongside the Kempe control. That work already reports a single-swap local optimum for a matching objective on paired layer four-colorings. The present prescribed exterior nine-coloring, private-clique extension objective, and minimum-degree/2-connectivity control are different, but the conceptual connection deserves direct acknowledgment.
- Give a stable, complete proof reference for the optional equal-or-disjoint corollary importing [4], including the later removal of its original independence restriction. The linked discussion explains that its original packet requires a subsequent result. The manuscript already separates this dependency from Theorem 1; an archival combined source would make that corollary easier to reproduce.

These are attribution and documentation improvements, not defects in the principal results. The informative abstract, explicit limitations and inspectable certificates support acceptance at the stated partial-result scope.
