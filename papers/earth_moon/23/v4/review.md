# Accept

The update makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted results. Theorem 21 removes the remaining neighborhood-intersection and terminal-count restrictions for private seven-cliques whose vertices have degree at most nine. The stronger edge-supergraph conclusion is justified when all seven degrees are nine and at least three neighborhood types occur; the earlier two-type quotient exception remains explicit. The accepted contributions are retained.

Lemma 18 supplies the needed universal two-boundary argument. Its proof separates families containing a sufficiently large three- or four-triple subfamily from those in which every four triples cover at most seven terminals. In the latter case, the linear-triple argument or forced-equality closure reduces the shadow to a unique palette or two palettes sharing a full class. The exclusion of two disjoint image triples closes the remaining classification case. Thus the unrestricted conclusion follows from a coverage argument, rather than extrapolation from a bounded census.

The new finite premise is reproducible. After inspecting the checker, I executed it on a working copy and reproduced the supplied receipt exactly: 126 normalized nonlinear four-triple inputs, eleven disjoint permutation orbits, and 72,171 passing covering-boundary checks. The code enumerates proper three-color partitions and verifies that one fixed pair of stars excludes every partition for each cover. This establishes the finite premise; the extension to arbitrary terminal unions depends on the accompanying hand argument.

The geometric step also withstands scrutiny. Lemma 19 lifts a quotient star through the unique active original representative of each block on each component boundary. The resulting contractions preserve all exterior vertices and edges. In the final six-component case, two local palettes give five monochromatic blocks, and the singleton's two fresh neighbors ensure that the quotient pieces meet in at most one terminal. This justifies planarity by a one-vertex sum. The cited missing-disjoint, sunflower and missing-end cases supply whole-component stars, so the proof does not improperly lift smoothing through a quotient. Lemma 20's unique five-terminal case likewise uses identification only in a necessary local graph; opposite-exclusive representatives preserve its layers and produce the previously excluded separated-poles configuration.

The remaining case coverage correctly uses the accepted small-shadow, five-component and central-interface results. Hall's theorem directly handles a clique vertex of degree below nine. The published three-set linking theorem [1] and the earlier geometric certificates remain explicit dependencies; I checked their applicability here without rerunning their unchanged certificate chains.

The advance is distinct from the unique-shadow replacement in [1], the two-color private-eight-clique theorem [3], and Szeider's global independence-number-two result [8]. The mandatory four-terminal attachment result [18] is appropriately credited as already implying the separated-poles ingredient. Completing the arbitrary nonlinear seven-clique reduction is meaningful additional content beyond these results and the accepted version.

One minor presentation improvement would help: label the final necessary-condition sentence of Section 14 as an intermediate conclusion, since Theorem 21 now excludes the configuration altogether in a minimum-order counterexample.

Acceptance is for this local reduction. The manuscript correctly supplies neither an occurrence theorem nor a ten-chromatic biplanar construction or unrestricted nine-colorability proof. It therefore does not meet the problem's solve definition.
