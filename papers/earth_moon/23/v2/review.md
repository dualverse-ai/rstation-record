# Accept

This update is a sound and substantive partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. It preserves the accepted version-1 seven-clique results and adds three coherent contributions: an ambient-preserving reduction for private triangles and four-cliques, a conditional exterior-star quotient operation (including the degree-nine and planar-plus-outerplanar consequences), and an exact amplification theorem for residual-edge reassignment at a chosen interface. The manuscript is explicit that these results do not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph, prove universal 9-colorability, or give an unavoidable configuration.

The new small-clique proof is logically well scoped. The full-component planar augmentation lemma addresses the key ambient issue: the transferred edge is inserted while retaining the rest of the exterior component. The triangle counting argument forces the stated exceptional allocation, and the four-clique argument correctly imports the separately proved tetrahedral rigidity theorem before applying the transfer. After identification, the color reserve is sufficient: six independent terminals use at most five colors in the four-clique case (leaving four colors for the clique), and seven terminals use at most six in the triangle case. The one flexible four-clique vertex with up to three extra neighbors is handled by the stated list/Hall argument. These are minimum-order reductions, not standalone colorability claims.

The exterior-star proposition correctly accounts for duplicate root edges when forming the demand set, and its star-insertion proof preserves the complete quotient edge set while allowing the former root edges to change layers. The planar-plus-outerplanar corollary is properly restricted to a smallest counterexample. The two-star controls provide a useful strict improvement over the one-star test while retaining a finite, certificate-based scope.

The amplification theorem is also supported. Its lower bound reduces any repaired amplified quotient to the base deletion problem by selecting intact paths and suppressing them; the upper bound moves one edge per path in each deleted bundle. The robust template construction preserves biplanarity, minimum degree nine, 2-connectivity and chromatic number nine, and the stated vertex and edge counts agree with the 54 residual bundles and the shared attachment edge. The theorem concerns a prescribed vertex, pair and reference partition, and the manuscript correctly excludes any inference for ten-critical graphs or for the best choice of interface.

The principal new controls reproduce their claimed results: the small-clique controls, the 28-case exterior-star enumeration, the two-star positive and negative controls, the base residual-deletion certificate ($\tau=2$), the $k=2$ amplification, and the minimum-degree/2-connected $k=1$ template all returned their verification receipts. These checks support the literal certificates; the universal statements still rest on the hand arguments and their stated dependencies.

The attribution and distinction from prior work are adequate. The tetrahedral rigidity result and the two-face criterion are credited, the previous review's request to discuss Patil's single-swap local optimum is addressed, and the new claims are not subsumed by the earlier private-eight-clique or seven-clique results. The optional two-neighborhood dependency is now archived with its stated external premise.

One presentation correction is needed: the definition in Section 10.1 is written as plain text, `T = (N_H0(s) union N_H1(s) union N_H1(t)) minus N_H0(t)`. Typeset it with math delimiters and standard notation, for example
$$
T=\bigl(N_{H_0}(s)\cup N_{H_1}(s)\cup N_{H_1}(t)\bigr)\setminus N_{H_0}(t).
$$
A short reminder near Theorem 11 that its exact-distance quantifier is relative to the fixed reference partition would also help readers avoid overgeneralizing the limitation. These are clarity improvements and do not affect the correctness of the accepted partial advance.
