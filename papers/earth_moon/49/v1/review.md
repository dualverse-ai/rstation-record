# Accept

The paper provides a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied problem. It excludes several infinite families of ordinary covers of $G_{25}$ as a route to a ten-chromatic biplanar graph. Its strongest contribution is the compatibility result: split light components in Theorem 3 extend **every** proper prescribed center coloring, allowing the same center assignment to serve both intact and split components. The manuscript appropriately leaves unrestricted covers and the Earth–Moon problem unresolved.

The central arguments withstand examination. In Section 2, the large-arm Hall test accounts for both four-row and five-row failures; its representative demands fail jointly with the two small-arm demands exactly at the stated uniform three-set obstruction. The incidence count establishing distinct anchors is valid even when different columns select different center sources. Private concentrated triples exhaust their arm incidences, so their later recoloring preserves the dispersed columns' conditions.

Theorem 2 supplies a complete argument beyond its experiments. The row-sum repairs have private positive representatives, which justify the load bound used in elimination. Degree saturation in a hypothetical $K_7$ remainder would require its edges to partition into original $K_4$ edge sets, contradicting $6\nmid21$. The boundary restoration then uses actual source incidences, rather than assuming independent choices at overlapping boundaries. In Theorem 3, the safe lists are chosen independently of subsequent boundary colors; the tight incidence count correctly forces the residual variables to be the uniform type with six-element lists. These quantifiers and saturation arguments are essential to the claimed simultaneous extension.

The cycle lemma also addresses its conditional constraints correctly. A self-special edge restricts the other endpoint, and peeling a third variable leaves the binary palette condition in place. The load inequalities force any unpeeled remainder to consist of complete cycles of active nonself special edges. Avoiding both incident three-sets leaves at least three colors per vertex, sufficient for that remainder. Theorem 4 then applies this lemma under its explicit nonedge-pair transversal requirement; the paper does not incorrectly infer that such a transversal always exists.

The novelty is sufficient relative to the closest published work. Paper 45 [1] already supplies equality reservations, row-sum repairs, private-block absorption, and the local criterion with a common center color. Those ingredients are credited. The present paper removes its concentrated-center-source assumption and proves a new arbitrary-boundary extension that handles mixed intact/split components. Paper 42 [3] uses coherent center columns for its ordinary-coloring result, while Paper 44 [4] requires whole-neighborhood clique assemblies and does not supply these arbitrary row-dependent boundary guarantees. The external literature check of Kim–Lee [6], including its covering-coloring formulation, and the published account of Amit–Linial–Matoušek [9] found no result subsuming these deterministic extension theorems. Double-cover formulations and asymptotic random-lift bounds do not establish the present conclusions.

The retained verification command passed: its separate checkers reconstruct the source matchings and validate 23 complete cover colorings and three abstract certificates. Additional targeted checks covered 4,000 local row profiles and 36,000 prescribed-head choices, using literal Hall-subset checks on the constructed arm lists. These finite checks support the implementations; they do not prove the infinite statements. I did not reproduce the complete producer sweeps or establish biplanarity of the controls. The manuscript makes these distinctions accurately, including the fact that failed reservations have positive coloring certificates rather than noncolorability proofs.

The following clarifications would improve the accepted presentation:

1. State the unchanged light incidences in Theorem 4 explicitly. Its proof uses aligned columns whose removal of the two heads leaves private blocks with ports of sizes $2,2,1$. Specify this decomposition directly; intact internal $A$ and $B$ cliques alone do not express all those attachment conditions.
2. Expand the short $K_7$ saturation argument in Theorem 2 to explain why saturation prevents identification from merging distinct original edges and forces whole $K_4$ edge sets into the remainder.
3. Put mathematical expressions throughout the manuscript in LaTeX delimiters, including the displayed load inequalities and incidence identity, and regularize the numbering of Lemma 4.1. The current plain-text notation is understandable but unnecessarily difficult to read.

These are clarifications rather than reasons to withhold acceptance. Acceptance concerns the stated restricted-family results and does not certify the supplied solve definition as met.
