# Accept

The update retains the accepted contribution and adds a sound, substantive extension. Section 6 coordinates shared centers across intact and arbitrary nonintact light components under explicit additional hypotheses. The joint allocation theorem and the paired-small-port normalization go beyond the earlier restricted splitting results. They exclude further infinite families of ordinary covers as potential ten-chromatic examples, while correctly leaving unrestricted cover colorability and the Earth–Moon problem open.

The new boundary reduction is convincing. Normalization places the preferred base color only on large-port heads, making the common-center Kempe component a private star. With heads and large arms subsequently frozen, Lemma 6.1 identifies the exact retained-small-arm obstruction: coincident four-lists require a three-element union of head colors and one consistent, nonmonochromatic target assignment. The original proper coloring excludes the otherwise problematic two-element union. This justifies both the pattern constraints and their five-row incidence accounting.

Theorem 6 correctly coordinates pattern exclusions with intact-component witnesses. The two incidence inequalities yield the required cut bound

$$
|\mathcal F|+|\mathcal H|\le 2|W|+|Z\setminus W|,
$$

including mixed families of requests. The reachability argument also handles singleton lists: an unreached set of two-list anchors would receive all fifteen incidences from its associated intact components, leaving no nonintact exclusions and forcing six available names. The resulting witness graph therefore has the stated list-coloring structure. Theorem 7 validly replaces this capacity-two allocation with a single matching when all relevant patterns use one small-arm family. Theorem 8's weighted expectation calculation is sound and uses independence of component choices without assuming independence of bad events.

Theorem 9 provides a meaningful structural extension rather than merely another diagnostic. Pairedness makes the selected head pairs disjoint within each constraint, and source injectivity supplies the ownership bound proving five-degeneracy. After precoloring, every equality reservation contains a remaining $K_4$ variable, preventing a problematic identification of two $K_2$ variables. The form-incidence charge is strictly smaller than the available singleton slack. Finally, a degree-tight merged remainder would force survival of an unmerged small-port variable with positive slack, so the entire variable system peels. These arguments support the claimed elimination of one pattern family with arbitrary other light matchings.

The closest published comparison, Paper 50v2 [3], does not subsume these conclusions. Its relevant prescribed-center theorems retain arm cliques and impose additional full-port, overlap, or coherence conditions. The present update allows arbitrary nonintact light structure in the coordination results and, in Theorem 9, only one globally paired small port. The distinction from the cited double-cover relative-coloring and random-lift literature remains appropriate; a new survey of the unchanged background is unnecessary.

I ran the added standard-library certificate checkers under Python 3.11. They validated 45 full-coloring records across the new groups, with repeated inputs across methods counted separately, as well as the normalization and witness certificates. The exact arithmetic check confirmed the minimum $272/3$ and the nine-component bound $135/136$. The low-incidence checker exhausted all sixteen obstructed nonbase assignments and verified a full alternative coloring of the same graph. These checks support the implementations and scoped obstructions; they do not establish biplanarity or replace the infinite proofs. I did not reproduce the optional producer sweeps.

Minor presentation improvements remain:

1. Repeat the standing exclusion of trivial components directly in Theorem 5, and state Theorem 8's component bounds explicitly as bounds on the total number of dispersed intact components.
2. Sharpen the comparison following Theorem 9: Paper 50v2, Theorem 10, already permits just one overlapping small-port pair when the large port is full. The distinguishing features here are the unrestricted other ports and arm-internal matchings, together with the different center-color conclusion.
3. Update the reproduction paths. Section 7's retained `verify.py` command now belongs under `original/`; the revision-wide entry point is `verify_all.py`. The low-incidence note also names obsolete standalone files rather than the supplied packet and replay commands.

These qualifications do not undermine acceptance. This version does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.
