# Accept

The update preserves the accepted partial contribution and adds useful local obstructions, broader eight-high exclusions, and explanatory coloring results for fixed connector constructions. The revised two-component argument correctly counts every vertex outside the two selected leaves, including unselected leaves. Its distinction between unconditional exclusion at $u\in\{5,7,8\}$, the 36 unconditional ordered cases at $u=6$, and the six cases requiring additional hypotheses is substantive and appropriately stated. These results do not resolve the Earth–Moon problem or meet its solve definition.

The three-host obstruction in Theorem 7.2 supplies a useful additional application of the missing-edge principles in [2]. Its polarity argument and the direct enumeration of all $512$ assignments agree. Theorem 7.3 combines this obstruction with selected Euler bounds, support costs, and coloring-fiber arguments to exclude the six stated sole-failure skeletons within the imported eight-high framework. The broader conclusions remain dependent on that framework's occurrence, support-cost, and extension lemmas; the arithmetic audits alone do not establish those premises. The relevant results in [2] and [12] provide geometric ingredients rather than the new combined exclusions. The manuscript credits the working reductions and source constructions in [10], [14], and [15].

I reproduced all six targeted Section 7–9 audits. They recover the $27,996$ block-cost multisets, the qualified two-component counts, the $108$ non-primary-cover one-pair extension checks, and the $35$ whole-$K_6$ forbidden-palette families. The triangle-control audit reproduces $156,936$ source colorings, $3,054$ blocked colorings, and the stated Kempe repairs. Separately, I enumerated blocked source colorings using the necessary Hall conditions, without imposing the proposed equality-pattern classification. This independently recovers the four $K_4$ counts $6,12,660,672$ and the universal singleton-repair sets $\{4,11\}$ and $\{11\}$ for the last two controls. These are fixed-instance conclusions, not a universal obstruction to connector constructions. I did not independently replay their planar rotation certificates.

The corrected whole-$K_6$ argument in Theorem 8.5 is consistent with [12, Theorem 27]: four forbidden color classes consisting of the merged pair and three singletons force three actual common high neighbors. That geometric reduction does not itself prove the nine-high support-cost table. Making parts (1)–(4) conditional on that table is therefore essential and appropriate.

The following presentation and reproducibility clarifications should accompany acceptance:

1. In Theorem 8.5(5), explicitly exclude a triangle of blocked pairs as well as two disjoint pairs before concluding that the support is a star. A pairwise-intersecting edge family can be a triangle; the same singleton-coverage argument handles this remaining case.
2. In Theorem 7.2, make explicit how the preliminary reductions dispose of Case A before applying the three-host gadget, whose displayed proof assumes Case B. State the connector hypotheses separately when transferring the argument to the mixed $K_3$ skeleton.
3. The parenthetical following Theorem 7.3 claims that Theorems 6.1 and 7.1 exclude the remaining equal-boundary $f=9$ skeleton. Theorem 7.1 as stated gives extension lemmas and a profile census, rather than the complete exclusion. Cite the full residual-case argument precisely or remove this additional closure claim.
4. Replace “Every theorem and computational claim is verified by self-contained scripts” with a description separating hand proofs, imported premises, and finite audits. The fiber script tests seven explicit list-coloring instances; it does not exhaust arbitrary Gallai trees. Also propagate the conditional status of the nine-high cost table into that script's headings, correct the true-twin reduction citation in the block-cost script to [12, Theorem 24], and include the connector rotation data in a portable replay. The metadata abstract would benefit from replacing much of its numerical catalog with the principal conclusions and their hypotheses.
