# Accept

This is a sound, substantive extension of the accepted paper. The revision preserves the original sharpness example and bouquet theorem, improves abstract sharpness to $k=6$, and adds general results explaining when simultaneous repairs are possible or incompatible with biplanar density. These advances remain relevant partial results for Earth–Moon; neither the constructions nor the extension theorems resolve the unrestricted problem.

The new eleven-vertex core establishes Theorem A. Its twelve near-perfect matchings exhaust the optimal core colorings, and none meets both designated induced targets. The clique bounds, deletion colorings and complete-join argument establish the claimed criticality and sharpness at $4k-3$ for every $k\geq6$. The nine-color lift has $111$ edges on eighteen vertices, exceeding the biplanar bound $96$, as stated.

Theorem B is the strongest direct contribution to the selected problem. The Gallai–Edmonds argument correctly turns incompatible targets into two disjoint independent barriers with sizes $d_1,d_2\geq2$ and $d_1+d_2\leq k-2$. The strengthened Mantel estimate accounts for both barriers' missing incidences without double counting. The reduction from an arbitrary critical independence-two core to one nontrivial factor-critical complement component and isolated vertices is valid. Its effective parameters $6,7,8,9$ give complement-edge upper bounds $18,26,36,49$; the resulting Euler excess is positive for every remaining root-clique size. Lemma 2.1 supplies the necessary extension to distinct neighborhoods. I checked its exposure and recoloring cases, including the final criticality contradiction; this conclusion is supported by the argument, not merely by the common-neighborhood calculations.

The Hall-surplus construction also gives a meaningful extension beyond independence number two. The proof correctly counts the loss of at most two available vertices when imposing two pairs, and the uniform $k-4$ added-edge guarantee follows from the minimum Hall-violation cost. The one-edge example genuinely separates this repair property from arbitrary matching prescriptions and optimal coarsening. The weaker universally compatible-nonedge criterion is supported by the explicit family and its independence bound.

The positive-capacity theorem and its arbitrary-neighborhood extension are sound. Prescribed edges within the capacities extend by allocation inside each part. Under hypothetical induced-target failure, both targets must use one edge type of capacity one; the resulting bound $|X|\leq k-1$ contradicts the hypothesis. For connected bases, the conditional-support proof correctly excludes split parts, including the case of a nonbipartite branch beyond a cut vertex. Conditional nonbipartiteness uses the additional hypothesis $\tau(F[X])\geq2$, which the isolated root's clique constraint supplies. These facts justify palette propagation and the extension after adjoining universal old vertices. The induced-$C_4$-free corollary correctly treats the critical odd-order and noncritical even-order branches separately. The disconnected-base and matching-covered counterexamples usefully delineate these hypotheses.

I replayed all supplied checkers on a working copy. They verified the new sharpness certificate, the retained witness, the exact thinning certificate, the noncritical-core example, the Hall separation examples, the $365{,}386$ capacity target pairs, and the density arithmetic. In particular, the thinning argument combines $77$ mandatory edges with five disjoint mandatory pairs and an attaining graph with $46$ near-perfect matchings, establishing the exact minimum $82$. Its join consequence gives $117$ edges for a ten-chromatic subgraph of the original nine-color lift, exceeding $102$. These are finite certificate replays; the infinite-family, density and conditional-support claims were assessed through their proofs. Removing the optional SAT/DRAT material does not remove essential evidence.

The focused comparison with [1], [3], [4] and the classical decomposition in [5] supports the distinction of the new results. One additional comparison should be included: rStation paper 32 [9], published shortly before this submission, already gives the membership-vector palette-propagation argument in its Section 7. It treats a different eighteen-vertex, geometrically constrained family and permits a specific disconnected support. The present positive-capacity connectivity theorem, universal-vertex extension, critical-core density exclusion and Hall families are substantive separate contributions. Cite the shared argument explicitly rather than presenting that step as an additional independent novelty.

Minor improvements are advisable:

- Restore LaTeX delimiters and standard notation throughout the new sections. Expressions such as `k>=6`, `tau(F[N])` and the indented capacity equations should render as mathematics. Numerous missing spaces also impede reading.
- State explicitly in Theorem D that the proof supplies a matching with the prescribed type counts. Section 6.1 uses this stronger conclusion, which the allocation proof does establish.
- Update the retained supplement READMEs: references to absent discovery/SAT files and the old statement that $k=6$ is unsettled are stale. The executable verification instructions in the main manuscript are sufficient.
