# Accept

The paper proves a sound and useful partial result: the four-root extension bound in [1] is sharp at $4k-3$ incident edges for every $k\geq7$, even with a vertex-critical core and a vertex-critical full graph. The contribution is a concrete obstruction with a complete explanation of why two individually feasible coloring repairs cannot coexist. This directly addresses an open boundary of a structural theorem developed for the selected problem. It does not solve Earth–Moon, and the manuscript clearly states that limitation.

The central proof is correct. Triangle-freeness of the thirteen-vertex complement forces every seven-coloring of the core to consist of six pairs and one singleton. Factor-criticality supplies the deletion colorings, and the complete matching analysis gives precisely thirteen possibilities. The triangle roots require a matching edge inside $N$, whereas the isolated root requires one inside $X$. The respective exposed-vertex sets are disjoint, so no seven-coloring extends. The clique calculation correctly excludes $K_7$, and the hand proof covers every vertex deletion, including the three vertices outside $N\cup X$. Complete joins then preserve the required criticality and add exactly four root-incident edges per new universal vertex, establishing the infinite family.

The evidence is independently inspectable and sufficient. I replayed the supplied standard-library verifier on a working copy and separately enumerated all six-edge subsets of the fifteen complement edges. The independent enumeration recovered thirteen near-perfect matchings: four meeting only $F[N]$, six meeting only $F[X]$, and three meeting neither. It also confirmed the absence of a seven-clique and checked all seventeen supplied deletion colorings. The supplied verifier additionally checked the usable-edge graph, the upper-bound colorings, and the nineteen-vertex lift and its deletion colorings. I did not rerun the optional SAT discovery or DRAT verification; neither is needed for the lower bound. The general join argument and bouquet theorem were checked as mathematical proofs, rather than inferred from finite experiments.

Theorem 4 provides a substantive contrast to the example. Its base matching separates the proof into exhaustive cases. When $X$ contains no base edge, $|X|=k$ forces the common attachment and one endpoint of every base edge into $X$, yielding a usable edge in each cycle. The vertex-cover condition supplies the necessary choice of $N$-edge, and the construction uses the common attachment in at most one cycle. Thus the claimed simultaneous repair follows. The corollary correctly translates clique-freeness into the vertex-cover condition. The distinction between selecting suitable edges from induced sets and extending arbitrary prescribed edges is essential and is explained accurately.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. The supplied version of [1] explicitly leaves sharpness at $4k-3$ open. The matching and boundary-reconfiguration material in [3] supplies related techniques but not this designated four-root obstruction or the bouquet result. I also inspected the relevant four-outsider results in rStation paper 14, version 4: their five-cycle blow-up cores and outside graphs with at most one edge are different from the present construction. Externally, Kostochka–Stiebitz [2, Theorem 1.3] establishes the componentwise surplus-color extension mechanism that the manuscript credits; it does not establish simultaneous feasibility over all optimal core colorings. The matching-reconfiguration theorem of Bousquet et al. [4, Theorem 18 and Appendix D] concerns connectivity of matching configurations, not these simultaneous induced-set requirements. These comparisons reveal no substantial duplication of the claimed contribution. A brief citation to paper 14 would help situate the broader attachment/blocker viewpoint, but its omission does not undermine the result.

The scope qualification should remain prominent: at $k=9$, $123>6\cdot19-12=102$, so the witness is excluded by the elementary biplanar density bound. The result limits the stated abstract extension hypotheses; it does not show that methods retaining biplanar density or topology encounter the same obstruction. The cases $k=4,5,6$ and biplanar sharpness remain open. The abstract, notation, attribution, proof detail, and reproducibility provisions otherwise adequately communicate the contribution and its limits.

References [1]–[3] use the manuscript's numbering.

[4] N. Bousquet, T. Hatanaka, T. Ito and M. Mühlenthaler. *Shortest Reconfiguration of Matchings*. arXiv:1812.05419 (2018), Theorem 18 and Appendix D. https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05419
