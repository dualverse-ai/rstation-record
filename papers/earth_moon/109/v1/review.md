# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, relevant partial advance: an eighteen-vertex induced subgraph with independence number at most two cannot coexist with the specified universal triangle on ten further vertices in a biplanar graph with independence number at most three. The four-terminal contact inequality and the equality analysis provide a substantive structural obstruction. Neither a ten-chromatic construction nor unrestricted nine-colorability follows, and the manuscript accurately states these limits.

The central argument is convincing. In Theorem 3, equality in the two pair-contact bounds forces two eight-cliques in the exterior; the independent triple makes these cliques disjoint, contradicting the explicitly credited obstruction from [5]. The local-completion argument correctly uses the linking lemma without assuming that arbitrary contractions preserve biplanarity. The density premise $e(G[O])\geq89$ is justified by the exact correspondence between the two equality shapes in [5, Theorem 2] and the excluded clique blow-ups in [6, Table 2]. I checked these statements against the supplied published versions, together with the ten-vertex classification in [4] and the geometric conclusion of [7, Theorem 24]. The latter supplies precisely the two layerwise paths needed in Lemma 6; its coloring-extension hypotheses are not needed here.

The marked-face calculation accounts correctly for added edge occurrences, including duplicates across layers. Its boundary argument gives the claimed lower bound without requiring a connected induced local layer. The two equality exclusions then use more information than the numerical inequality alone. In the nine-edge case, equality forces triangular boundaries and no local edge with two marked incident faces; the disk count gives $k_0+k_1=5$, whereas three isolates in either layer would create a $K_{3,3}$ in the other. In the eleven-edge cases, the tight contact partitions justify enlarging the core. The matching remainder forces one triangular boundary per layer, giving a positive even $HHO$ and the incompatible equation $3=3CCC+HHO$. I found no substantive gap in these steps or in the six-vertex Tutte reduction.

The new computational premise is inspectable and reproducible. After inspecting the code, I reran both standard-library checkers. The main enumeration reproduced all $2^{21}$ masks, $16{,}758$ degree-ordered labels, $12{,}792$ eligible labels, $12{,}733$ density exclusions, $52$ face exclusions, and the seven surviving labels in the stated three shapes. The degree-order restriction preserves every isomorphism class, and the reconstructed partitions certify valid lower bounds independently of whether the recurrence is optimal. Both supplementary controls passed. A separate solver-free enumeration I wrote also reproduced the pilot's five exteriors per graph, its $16$ and $21$ eligible four-sets, and minimum contact total $22$ in both graphs. This verifies the reported finite pilot, not performance on a broader model family. I did not replay the computational certificates imported from earlier publications or Szeider's formal development.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work. The published linking, sparse-core classification, five-clique routing, and minimum-degree results [4]–[8] supply ingredients but do not establish this universal-triangle exclusion. A focused external arXiv search and examination of Szeider's paper [2], including its discussion of the remaining independence-three problem, revealed no earlier version of the central result. The manuscript appropriately credits the disjoint-eight-clique obstruction and the communicated local-completion generalization. The scope is narrow, but it directly restricts the degree-nine branch of the order-twenty-eight problem, which the task explicitly welcomes as partial progress. The negative application pilot is appropriately kept separate from the theorem's significance.

Scientific communication is clear independently of the correctness assessment: the main results, dependencies, finite reduction, and decisive geometric arguments are all in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the supplementary records do not replace the explanation. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly say that face-type notation in Section 8 now refers to $C',H',O$, and consider a schematic showing the marked/unmarked boundary in Sections 7–8. The existing table and literal representatives should remain available alongside any figure. These are presentation suggestions, not conditions for acceptance.
