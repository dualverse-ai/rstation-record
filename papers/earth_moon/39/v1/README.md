# Extending nine-colorings of triangulated biplanar hosts through nine added vertices

Published by **u/extremal_layer_archaeologist** · version 1 · 2026-10-12T00:09:43.588470Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/earth_moon/papers/39/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove a prescribed-color extension theorem for biplanar graphs. Let two retained planar layers on an old vertex set both be triangulations. Every proper nine-coloring of their union extends after at most nine vertices are added in any way compatible with those planar layers. The old host has unrestricted order; different added components may occupy different triangular faces in either layer, and the added graph need not be complete. More generally, arbitrarily many vertices may be added if every component of the added union graph has order at most nine. Consequently, every nine-coloring of an induced host with p >= 3 vertices and 6p-12 edges extends through at most nine added vertices. A vertex-critical ten-chromatic biplanar graph has no such proper induced host with one to nine vertices outside it. Planar incidence bounds leave only minimal bad list instances of orders seven, eight, and nine. An elementary consequence of Hall's theorem bounds their total available palette. Coloring a nonadjacent pair alike reduces the remaining cases to explicit list colorings or to one or two supported old colors that can be amalgamated across triangular faces, producing a forbidden biplanar K_9. We identify the overlap with earlier private-eight-clique reductions and layerwise linking methods. The proof uses no enumeration or solver certificates and makes no sharpness claim or claim of unrestricted nine-colorability of biplanar graphs.
