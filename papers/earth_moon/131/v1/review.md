# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful partial advance: a sharp common-palette list-coloring criterion for $K_{q-3}\vee C_5$, and exclusion of $K_6\vee C_5$ from minimum-order non-nine-colorable biplanar graphs within the class $\Delta\leq11$. The second result closes a specific unresolved case in [1], rather than merely restating the exclusion under global minimality. Neither result resolves the requested construction problem, but both have a substantive connection to its coloring and reduction methods.

Theorem 1 has a complete short proof. The Hall analysis correctly reduces the proper-subfamily obstruction to two pair lists that coincide in a singleton. For odd $q$, the complementary-set argument on the distance-two five-cycle rules out failure of every partition. The subsequent distinction according to how many cycle lists contain the color missing from the hub union ensures that a suitable partition also satisfies Hall's condition for the full family. The disjoint-list construction establishes the claimed sharpness even with full hub lists. Corollary 2 follows with exactly the stated exterior-contact budgets.

The finite premise in Lemma 3 is adequately justified. The edge bounds force the disconnected layer to have a ten-vertex component and one isolated hub. Removing the universal hub from the other layer gives a noncrossing decagon graph with sixteen or seventeen edges, so triangulations and single-edge deletions cover the necessary domain. The normalization preserves all root assignments because every subset is subsequently checked. I inspected the verifier's domain generation, subdivision tests, rotation checks and coverage logic, and reran it successfully: it regenerated 3,190,470 occurrences, including 180 positive occurrences representing 164 labeled core pairs, and verified all 5,248 fan pairs with no simultaneously planar augmentation. The separate face audit agreed on all pairs, and all thirteen supplied corruption controls rejected their damaged inputs. These are replays of the supplied verification programs; the original certificate-discovery computation was not reproduced. Discovery is not a proof premise here because the retained evidence and regenerated coverage are independently inspectable.

Theorem 4 uses this premise correctly. Its recolorings and path additions preserve the maximum-degree class. In the two-root case, each root loses three hub incidences before gaining at most three spokes, while each target's new edge is paid for by its deleted incidence to the selected cycle vertex. The three-root case forces an isolated hub and a split of the other five hub contacts between the main roots, precisely the configuration excluded by Lemma 3. Thus the replacement argument does not silently invoke minimality over a larger class.

The novelty claims are appropriately qualified. Comparison with [1, Lemma 4 and Proposition 5] confirms both the earlier, larger cycle-list budget and the outstanding bounded-degree residual. The stronger local exclusion in [2, Theorem 16] uses global minimality and does not supply the degree-preserving conclusion here. Its five-element cycle-list argument is a close predecessor that the manuscript acknowledges. The singleton/pair representation in [4] and the private routing operations in [3] are also credited. A focused external check of Cranston–Rabern [5, Section 3.2] and Kaul–Mudrock [6, Proposition 2] confirms the stated distinction: their respective degree-minus-one and strong chromatic-choosability results do not provide this smaller, nonuniform common-palette criterion. The contribution is modest but substantive, combining an exact sharp extension theorem with a completed geometric reduction.

The scientific communication is clear independently of correctness. The main text states the contributions and assumptions, explains the decisive proof strategies, and distinguishes the computational premise from the hand arguments. The abstract is informative, and the limitations prevent an unwarranted claim about arbitrary Earth–Moon counterexamples. Attribution and reproducibility are sufficient for acceptance.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Section 5 and the supplement README should also give instructions for the supplied extracted supplement, rather than referring only to extraction of `geometry103.zip`. Also label the verifier's final negative-layer counts explicitly as occurrence-weighted: its total of 7,100 differs from the correctly reported 6,468 distinct labeled negative-layer certificates. These presentation details do not affect the verified conclusions.
