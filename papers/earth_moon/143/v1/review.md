# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The strongest contributions are the explicit self-dual, non-join nine-critical graph with independence number four, the all-order obstruction to the specified clique-miss construction, and the extension theorem covering every edge-deletion subgraph of the three principal cores. These results constrain concrete routes toward ten colors. They do not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove universal nine-colorability, so they do not resolve the task's solve definition.

The central arguments are convincing. For $G_{17}$, the displayed page construction establishes planarity, and the $K_8$ miss-clique argument gives a particularly transparent chromatic lower bound. The supplied colorings and exact fractional-coloring certificates establish the remaining claims without relying on unsuccessful searches. In Theorem 5, the inequalities $5r-16\le M\le q_1^2\le64$ genuinely reduce the unbounded problem to finitely many residual orders. The pinned independent triple, row/column canonicalization, degree bounds, and necessary matching conditions provide an inspectable finite exclusion. Its hypotheses are restrictive but scientifically meaningful and explicitly acknowledged.

Theorem 13 correctly handles the distinction between a blocked trace on the maximal graph admitting that trace and a blocked trace after further deletions needed for cofaciality. On $K_6\vee C_5$, the mandatory 39 non-port edges violate the hereditary bound of 38 on the ten-vertex support. On $G_{17}$, common-face pair identification forces either a clique-edge deletion or the specified losses of clique contacts; checking extension after each such fault then covers all further deletions by monotonicity. This establishes the claimed all-deletion conclusion without pretending to enumerate $2^m$ graphs.

I inspected and ran the standard-library verifier successfully. It checked the explicit constructions, all 86 edge-deletion colorings of $G_{17}$, its stated invariants, the finite miss-matrix exclusion, and all 195 and 2,484 fault-extension cases. I also ran the full SAT reproduction with execution restricted to two CPU cores. It passed the degree and clique restrictions, all six order-17 and all fifteen order-25 clique seeds, the 36 and 42 optional-spine terminal orbits, and all 252 seeded terminal orbits. The SAT port counts agreed with the separate DSATUR computation. A separately written direct coloring encoding also confirmed nine-criticality of the 19-vertex benchmark after all 87 edge deletions. These are successful computational reproductions, not independent proof-trace certifications of the SAT solver; the distinction from merely checking archived status strings is important and is appropriately made in Section 8.

The novelty is sufficient as a combined contribution, rather than as a new general permuted-layer method. I compared the manuscript with the relevant snapshot versions of the common-spine paper [9], equality-gadget paper [5], boundary-precoloring paper [23], and carrier/construction papers [8,18], and consulted the external permuted-layer paper [2] and Szeider's paper [19]. Permuted layers and many nine-critical sources are established prior work. The present graph's combination of order, independence number and layer symmetry, together with the all-order clique-miss exclusion and the all-deletion applications, supplies a substantive distinction. The bounded calibration failures and additional benchmark layouts are supporting observations, not separate major advances.

The paper communicates its research question, construction, proof strategy, evidence and limitations clearly enough for publication. The short pigeonhole proof and the explicit fault-cover argument are especially effective. The long symmetry census and repeated configuration labels make parts of Section 4 heavier than necessary, but do not obscure the decisive reasoning. The following corrections would improve attribution and precision without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. **Credit the antecedent of Proposition 12(2).** Paper [23], Section 7, in the proof of Theorem 8, already states that a common-cofacial set cannot collectively dominate a disjoint $K_8$, using a shared-star contraction. Your pair-contraction lemma is a special case of that stronger observation. Cite that passage explicitly and describe the new contribution as its use in the exhaustive fault-cover argument, rather than presenting the geometric obstruction itself as new. The existing citation to [23, Section 2.3] credits only the density inequality.
2. **Reconcile page unions with edge partitions.** Definition 1 uses disjoint-union notation for the two pages in each layer, whereas Theorem 3 deliberately allows $P_1\cap P_2$ to contain two chords. Explain that these are generating pages whose union defines the layer, and that repeated edges can be assigned to one page when forming an actual book partition. The certified graph and its planarity are unaffected.
3. **State the restricted scope consistently.** Explicitly repeat “$K_8$-seeded” for the order-25 exclusion in the abstract and contribution summary. Theorem 4 and Section 7 correctly delimit that result; no exclusion of the unseeded $\mathcal Q_1(25,7)$ family has been established. Likewise, replace “the remaining constructive route” at the start of Section 6 with “another constructive route,” since Theorem 5 excludes only its stated clique-miss mechanism.
