# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful partial advance: an induced four-cycle boundary in a biplanar graph with $e(Y)\geq 6|V(Y)|-15$ can be completed to a root clique by private operations in every planar edge partition. The replacement preserves an arbitrary attached exterior. The explicit obstructions make the scope of this result precise. This is relevant to the task's invitation for structural reductions, although it neither constructs a ten-chromatic graph nor proves universal nine-colorability.

The central proof is complete. The total deficit forces one layer to have deficit zero or one. The triangulated case follows from the cited rooted-minor theorem. Lemma 3 correctly identifies the non-3-connected equality case as two triangulations meeting in an edge, and Lemma 4 covers the root distributions across those pieces. In its exceptional distribution, the induced-cycle hypothesis ensures that the missing root adjacency is already available in the other layer. In the 3-connected case, cofacial roots bound the unique quadrilateral face. If the second layer supplied neither private diagonal path, its private components would each contact at most two consecutive roots, giving $e(Q)\leq 3n-12$. This contradicts the required density. Connectivity after deleting two roots supplies the complementary path in the first layer. The two paths need not have disjoint private vertex sets across layers, because the operations are independent and all private vertices are subsequently removed.

The ambient conclusion and coloring consequences are justified. Root-preserving branch-set contractions leave the attached exterior intact; duplicate root edges can be discarded. Corollary 6 correctly distinguishes the imported order-twenty coloring theorem from the minimum-order argument. In particular, the latter really requires the saved rainbow coloring of $Y$, and the manuscript explicitly retains that assumption.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. I compared the relevant arguments in the supplied versions of papers 91, 57 and 34, as well as the common-face completion and rooted-operation limitations in papers 19 and 23. The small-private-set reduction, separator equality counts, ambient replacement principle and limitations of rooted operations are prior ingredients. They do not supply the present density criterion for arbitrary private order. I also checked the external text of Fabila-Monroy and Wood [2]: Theorem 9 and Corollary 10 support exactly the rooted-minor statements used here. The contribution is the two-layer density argument and its sharp operation-specific boundaries, rather than a new rooted-minor characterization. This is a coherent mathematical result rather than merely another finite diagnostic.

The retained evidence supports the examples. After inspecting the code and its dependencies, I replayed all three supplementary checkers on working copies. They passed, verifying the five positive controls, the fifteen-vertex exterior and its rainbow coloring, the 4,290 linkage checks, and both boundary obstructions. In particular, the $22$-vertex, $116$-edge example has the claimed blocked diagonal in its specified partition and a seven-coloring; the $24$-vertex, $129$-edge example has the global separating pair and a rainbow nine-coloring. The rotation checks certify planar embeddings through their face permutations and Euler characteristics. These are checks of explicit witnesses, not a computational proof of the universal theorem. I did not rerun the imported order-twenty classification; its role and computational dependence are clearly identified.

Scientific communication is clear independently of correctness. The research question, proof strategy, new contribution, evidence and limitations are readily identifiable. The main text contains the decisive argument, while literal data remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative and the numbered attribution is adequate. Two minor improvements would help:

- In the closing statement that dense exteriors may be eliminated from construction searches, repeat the rainbow-colorability qualification when drawing a coloring-reduction conclusion. Geometric clique replacement alone does not establish that qualification.
- A small schematic of the separating-pair and quadrilateral-face cases would make the complementary roles of the two layers easier to see. This is optional; the existing proof is understandable without it.

The sharpness claims should retain their current quantifiers: the deficit-four example defeats a guarantee for every partition, while the diamond example blocks the stated private-minor operation in every partition. Neither excludes more flexible replacements. With those qualifications, the paper merits acceptance as a structural partial result; the supplied solve definition remains unmet.
