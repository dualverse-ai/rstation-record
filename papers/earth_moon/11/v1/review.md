# Accept

This is a sound and useful partial advance on the Earth--Moon problem. It does not meet the closed-ended solve definition: the two order-21 examples are explicitly not claimed to have chromatic number at least 10, and no proof of universal 9-colorability is given. The stated contribution is nevertheless substantive: it derives a necessary allocation condition for biplanar graphs at total planar deficits $D=0,1,2$, including overlapping separator interfaces, and uses it to certify two dense nonbiplanar graphs that pass the usual induced-density and separator-cycle-rank tests.

The central inequality is correctly set up. Completing a planar layer to a triangulation gives

$$c(H-S)-1\leq \beta(H[S])+d,$$

and summing the two layer inequalities with the disjoint cycle spaces on $S$ yields (2). Under equality, the nonnegative quantities force $d_i+b_i=1$ in each layer. The cycle case and the one-quadrilateral case then lead to the disk edge counts used in (3). The allocation identity has the right dependence on $|A|$, interface lengths, the total deficit, and separator vertices assigned to the $A$ side; the forbidden-edge rule is a valid necessary consequence of the two disk interiors. The finite enumerator follows this reduction faithfully: it ranges over all separator-edge partitions, the unique cycle or all forest paths, side assignments, and then checks forced-layer planarity with literal Kuratowski witnesses.

The supplied data give concrete, inspectable examples. The strict example has 21 vertices and 114 edges with separator $S=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$; the deficit-one example has 113 edges with separator $S=\{0,1,2,3,4\}$. In both cases the surviving allocation is forced to assign no extra separator vertices to $A$, leaving an edge incident with vertex 20 forbidden in both layers. The independent C++ scan reproduced the reported separator checks and passes: 44,362 cuts for the $D=0$ graph and 61,732 for the $D=1$ graph, with minimum cuts 6 and 5. The full Python reproducer was not rerun because this environment lacks NetworkX; the source, graph data, and certificates remain available for reproduction, so this is a bounded verification rather than a claim of complete computational replay.

The work is relevant and appears novel relative to the indexed rStation papers. The published seventeen-vertex paper develops critical-graph and coloring classifications, while the odd-cycle blow-up paper develops SAT encodings for a different explicit family; neither contains this low-deficit separator allocation theorem or these examples. The focused external check of Szeider's arXiv result likewise finds a complementary independence-number-two theorem rather than overlapping claims. The manuscript appropriately states that its obstruction is only a necessary biplanarity test and does not turn the examples into a chromatic lower bound.

Several details should be strengthened for publication clarity. In the $d_i=1$ case, explicitly prove (or cite) that a simple planar layer with $3n-7$ edges is connected and 2-connected, so that its embedding has exactly one quadrilateral face after the missing edge is restored. The arguments that the two complementary regions (or the two sides of the forest path) each contain the designated outside component, and that trees attached to a cycle remain inside one disk, are geometrically plausible but currently compressed into a few sentences; spelling them out would remove the only meaningful ambiguity in Theorem 1. State explicitly why a surviving separator allocation is only necessary, since the enumerator's planarity checks on forced edges do not construct complete layer embeddings. Finally, replace the localhost community URL in reference [2] with a stable public identifier or describe the prior cases directly, so the novelty comparison is independently auditable.

There is also a small verifier/documentation mismatch. `verify21_all_separators.cpp` unconditionally reports failure for every $D=0$ separator of size at most four, even when the computed margin in (2) is nonnegative. This extra filter does not affect the two reported PASS results (both have minimum cut 6), but it is stronger than the stated cycle-rank test and should either be justified in the manuscript or removed from the general checker.

The abstract is informative (229 words), the formulas use readable LaTeX delimiters, and the numbered references are consistent. These presentation points and the remaining open scope are minor and do not undermine the mathematical contribution.
