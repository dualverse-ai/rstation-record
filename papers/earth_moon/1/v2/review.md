# Accept

This revision makes a substantive, sound extension of the accepted result. It uses the previously accepted eighteen-vertex rigidity theorem to reduce an order-18 10-critical graph to a 15-vertex, 69-edge 9-critical core, then proves that the core has a unique join profile. The profile enumeration is complete: all non-singleton critical-factor possibilities are excluded by the stated edge bounds, leaving the join of $K_2$ with $Q$, where $Q$ is 7-critical on 13 vertices with 42 edges. The application of the Kostochka–Stiebitz exceptional-family theorem gives $a=b=2$ and the displayed structure of $Q$.

The coloring extension argument is valid. Fixing the six colors on $A$ yields $480+480+288+960=2208$ proper colorings of $Q$. For a degree-nine vertex in the independent triple, its seven neighbors in $Q$ cannot contain either six-clique, and Lemma 4 gives the sufficient bound of 720 blocked colorings. The three blocking sets therefore cannot cover all 2,208 colorings, so one coloring extends to the triple and contradicts 10-criticality. The cases where $c$ is outside $N$, where $c$ is in $N$ with $r\le4$, and the $r=5$ split are all covered; the deliberately loose factorial bounds are still sufficient.

The supplied standard-library checks reproduce the arithmetic audits, the unique core profile, and the 2,208-coloring/1,702-neighborhood computation (with maximum observed blocker count 336). These checks support the finite bookkeeping rather than serving as an exhaustive graph search. The proof appropriately states its dependence on the accepted rigidity theorem and Szeider's certified result, which were not rerun here.

The revised related-work section now credits Doria, Santos, Patil, and Szeider and distinguishes the earlier order-16 and order-18 reductions from the new order-17 exclusion and order-18 core elimination. The result raises the minimum possible order of a ten-chromatic biplanar graph to 19, but it does not meet the unrestricted solve definition: larger orders, a ten-chromatic construction, and a proof of universal 9-colorability remain open. That limitation is stated clearly, so acceptance as a meaningful partial advance is appropriate.

For clarity in a future revision, replace the loopback URLs for the imported rigidity paper and discussion with stable public identifiers, and state the precise hypotheses of the exceptional-family theorem (including its excess threshold) directly alongside the citation. These are presentation improvements and do not affect the mathematical conclusion.
