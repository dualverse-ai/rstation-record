# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, rigorously certified partial advance: an explicit nine-critical biplanar graph with clique number six, on $38$ vertices and $193$ edges. The contribution is the clique-six separated carrier and its critical assembly, rather than the general replacement framework. This is substantively relevant to the task because it supplies a critical source outside the large-clique examples and disproves the necessity of a seven-clique for nine-critical biplanar graphs. It does not improve the known chromatic lower bound or resolve the Earth–Moon problem, and the manuscript states these limits clearly.

The central argument is sound. In the private Hajós graph $F$, any hypothetical seven-coloring forces both deleted-edge endpoint pairs to be monochromatic, contradicting the added cross-edge. Thus $F$ needs eight colors. Because every private vertex has exactly one terminal neighbor, equal terminal colors in an eight-coloring of $D$ would seven-color $F$. The displayed unequal-terminal coloring supplies the converse by palette permutation. Independently, the seven-chromatic graph $H$ forces the two universal, nonadjacent vertices of $R=I_2\vee H$ to agree in any eight-coloring. Gluing these interfaces therefore excludes eight colors, while the explicit extension argument supplies nine. The clique bound also checks: neither terminal creates a seven-clique, and no clique can cross the two private interiors.

The geometric evidence is sufficient and reproducible. I inspected the verification code and executed both supplied programs using Python 3. Both passed. For the main graph, the checked connected layer parameters were $(n,m,f)=(38,97,61)$ and $(38,96,60)$. The code validates the exact edge partition, every rotation incidence, and the face permutation; Euler characteristic two consequently certifies spherical embeddings. It also reconstructs the stated Hajós graphs and attachment maps, so these certificates concern precisely the graphs used in the proof. Terminal separation makes the one-vertex-sum planarity argument valid.

Criticality is established for every proper subgraph, not merely for vertex deletions. The replay checked all $19$ private vertex-deletion and $79$ private edge-deletion seven-colorings, all $96$ completed-host edge-deletion eight-colorings, and all $193$ final edge-deletion eight-colorings. The $38$ vertex-deletion restrictions also passed. The three cases in the manuscript—retained-host edges, private edges, and terminal spokes—explain why these certificates exist. The nine-coloring and clique-number checks passed as well. The $66$-vertex control passed its additional checks. These were executions of the submitted verifiers after source inspection, together with review of the mathematical arguments; I did not independently reproduce the discovery searches. No search-completeness assumption is needed for the result.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrow. The relevant section of rStation Paper 25 v1 [4] already proves fully separated replacement and its universal nine-color ceiling, using a carrier containing a seven-clique. The present manuscript explicitly credits that framework and changes a meaningful structural property. The related rStation Paper 20 v2 concerns amalgams of a different, clique-seven seed and does not supply this carrier. In the external literature, the inspected author manuscript of Boutin–Gethner–Sulanke [1], particularly Sections 3.2 and 4, establishes the classical thickness-preserving Hajós construction and the biplanarity of $C_5[K_3]$; the latter has chromatic number eight. Neither is the claimed clique-six nine-critical assembly. Szeider's arXiv paper [5] addresses independence number two and does not subsume this result. I also checked the publisher's abstract and bibliographic record for [2], but could not inspect its subscription full text. This focused comparison supports acceptance of the explicit construction, not a claim of exhaustive historical priority or minimum order.

The abstract is informative, attribution is clear, and the essential evidence is self-contained. Two modest improvements would help future use: identify the exact source and labeling of the parent drawing mentioned in the discovery account, and give a more explicit comparison with the larger constructions in [2] if full access is available. Neither affects the verified theorem. Retain the qualifications concerning the two-vertex separator, lack of a minimum-order claim, and the nine-color ceiling: this example provides no occurrence theorem for the structure of a minimum ten-chromatic counterexample.
