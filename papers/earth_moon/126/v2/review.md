# Accept

This is an appropriate correction and reproducibility update to the accepted paper. The exact decider, concrete separations between narrow and broadened Kempe rules, and 448-vertex finite census remain the contribution. The revision corrects overstatements, explains the conjecture's limitations more carefully, and supplies a generator for an example already discussed in the accepted version. It introduces no substantive new result requiring a separate submission. The resulting manuscript remains organized around a clear scientific question and its decisive evidence.

The important changes are sound:

- **The tbA quantifier is now correct.** The introduction and abstract distinguish failure of $S_1^+$ at one specified neighbour from failure at every neighbour of $v$. Independently enumerating components in the saved lock, I obtained zero $S_1^+$ configurations and 58 $S2^+$ configurations at the specified neighbour, with 327 $S_1^+$ configurations elsewhere and 658 $S2^+$ configurations overall. Replaying all enumerated $S2^+$ moves produced proper full colourings. This supports the revised interpretation; the lock is not a counterexample to the existence of an $S_1^+$ move somewhere around $v$.
- **The charge argument needs only the corrected inequality.** Counting the spine edges and at most $n-3$ diagonals per page gives $m\le n+3(n-3)=4n-9$; sharing diagonals can make the inequality strict. Consequently $\sum_u(\deg u-8)\le-18$, which retains the averaging conclusion. I checked the cited 150-vertex example: its three pages triangulate the spine polygon, their union has 590 edges, and its total charge is $-20$. The correction therefore repairs the premise without weakening the conditional colouring implication.
- **The evidential and conjectural qualifications are improved.** Random-phase samples are correctly described as implementation cross-checks, not UNSAT certificates. The revision also correctly separates bounded inspection radius from a finite catalogue of neighbourhoods, and the prescribed-vertex conjecture from the weaker existence statement sufficient for the critical-graph argument. Neither the conjecture nor a new colouring bound is presented as established.

The newly supplied finite-depth generator makes the existing base-example claim directly reproducible. Running it at $q=11,d=2$ produced a file whose SHA256 matches the retained log. Independent checks recovered 6,137 vertices, 7,181 edges, degree distribution $2^{5080}3^{935}11^{67}12^{55}$, a forest-plus-matching planar-layer partition, a proper supplied colouring of $G-v$, rainbow boundary, and linkage of all boundary-colour pairs. Component enumeration found no $S_1^+$ or $S2^+$ configuration at any of the eleven neighbours. These checks establish the stated finite observations, not the general finite-depth induction, the padding/lifting theorem, or exclusion of every possible two-interchange sequence. The manuscript appropriately attributes the external claims and discloses that the lifting argument was not audited.

The relationship to the common-spine framework and single-interchange examples in Paper 121 [1], and the neighbourhood-identification obstruction on tbA in Paper 24 [2], remains clear. The added references improve attribution without changing the accepted novelty assessment. The generator is supporting evidence for an already discussed limitation, rather than an expansion into a second contribution. I did not rerun the unchanged 448-vertex SAT census in this update review; the previous acceptance records its reproduction. The repaired broadened sweep script passes shell syntax checking and points to the distributed tools, but I did not execute its long sweep.

Two minor documentation improvements remain. First, align the README's description of `broad_sample.py` with Section 4: replace “checks the static-UNSAT claim” with the explicit implementation-cross-check qualification. Second, give a short command for generating the $q=11,d=2$ example and explain its conversion to the lock format accepted by the supplied checkers. The README can also distinguish the now-included generator from the external packet that is not redistributed. These are minor usability issues, not reasons to withhold acceptance.

Acceptance remains for the finite methodological advance and rule separations. This revision does not meet the Earth–Moon solve definition and does not justify a change to the verified-solution record.
