# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The manuscript proves a local exclusion in a globally smallest biplanar counterexample, an arbitrary-order coloring theorem for networks of $K_{q-3}\vee C_5$, and an overlap closure with a useful boundary-color consequence. These conclusions do not resolve the general problem, and the manuscript states that limitation accurately. The results are connected by a clear extension mechanism rather than being a collection of isolated computational exclusions.

The decisive mathematical arguments are convincing:

- **Exact extension relation.** Lemma 4 correctly reduces the matching obstruction to the full family of color-class lists. Every proper subfamily satisfies Hall's condition, including at $q=8$, where the pair lists can have only two colors. Choosing the cycle singleton makes the full union contain all $q$ colors unless one color is forbidden at every core vertex. The prescribed exterior-free singleton conclusion also follows.
- **Geometric reduction.** The fifty internal edges force each planar layer to be connected or to have the stated $10+1$ split, with at most one disconnected layer. The private-path reduction, contact-degree bounds, and selection of a cycle vertex outside the hub-contact neighborhoods then justify the two stars in equation (3). These operations retain the exterior and enforce precisely the disjunction needed for extension. The distinction between global minimum order and minimum order within $\Delta\le11$ is essential and correctly respected: Proposition 5 identifies where a replacement can create degree twelve, without claiming that the residual is realizable.
- **Networks and overlaps.** Theorem 2 maintains both degree bounds and the root's nonuniversality. The ordinary-parts-first rule, the bound $d_{\mathrm{new}}(z)\le9$, the exceptional two-core case at $q=8$, and the exterior-free singleton recoloring close the induction. In Theorem 3, the intersection-degree bounds force ten-vertex intersections; the two resulting false-twin blocks have the asserted saturated universal vertices and extend every admissible exterior coloring by Hall's theorem. Peeling these blocks preserves the hypotheses needed for the rooted theorem. The boundary argument correctly avoids assuming that adjoining the auxiliary root preserves $K_9$-freeness.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. The private-component contractions are already present in manuscript reference [1] and are appropriately credited. The fixed-order menu results in [2] and the sparse-exterior or low-remainder results in [5] do not provide the present arbitrary-exterior local exclusion or the network and cover theorems. I also checked the relevant external results in Cranston–Rabern [3] and Kaul–Mudrock [4]. Their $d_1$-choosability and strong chromatic-choosability conclusions use different list-size guarantees: neither supplies the present exact obstruction with cycle lists as small as $q-3$. The contribution lies in the obstruction relation and its geometric and inductive consequences, not in a new matching principle. This is meaningful progress within the task's explicit allowance for structural partial advances.
