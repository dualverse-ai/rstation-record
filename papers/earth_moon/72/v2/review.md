# Accept

The revision makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted full-core result. The new contribution is compatible coloring of specified non-full remainders and mixtures of center regions, including an exact prescribed-$X$ extension criterion. These are useful obstructions to the ordinary-cover construction route for Earth–Moon. The unchanged local theorem remains intact; the new claims do not imply unrestricted cover colorability or resolve Earth–Moon.

Theorem 3 correctly strengthens mere nine-colorability to extension of every prescribed boundary, through the stated eight/seven-list theorem. The restoration order gives the claimed list budgets. In particular, the intermediate cover of $K_4\cup K_7$, with intersection $K_2$, cannot have a Gallai-tree component: its possible leaf-clique sizes are excluded by the numbers of source types with the necessary degrees. The hypothesis excludes $K_5$ components throughout one entire small-arm type, as the statement appropriately emphasizes. Theorem 4's reserved-host construction also works: it places the prescribed $Z$ color in the large-port palette, and part (b) uses distinct representatives to make the exceptional host-clique lists nonidentical. Its seventeen-incidence closure justifies the stronger boundary preservation claimed there.

The central new argument, Theorem 5, addresses the compatibility difficulty left open in the accepted version. Lemma 5.1 retains the correlation between each large row's $X$ color and head set; its union identity accounts for all possible four- and five-row Hall defects. Lemma 5.2 then identifies the sole joint obstruction to the robust three-color center policy. The selected virtual graph uses each retained head exactly once. Consequently, rotating its exceptional host cliques preserves the selected witnesses of nonrigid groups. The parity calculation for the three shifts, the functional dependency graph, and the incidence closure of no-escape groups together supply simultaneous repairs. The subsequent center lists remain larger than their degrees after outside colors are excluded. Thus the recoloring of owned $Z$ vertices respects previously colored components, as required by Corollary 6. Failure of the robust policy is correctly distinguished from actual boundary nonextension.

Theorems 7–9 provide a complementary extension and a valid composition rule. In Lemma 7.1, replacing the fictitious constant $X$ color by the actual prescribed pattern can only increase every large-arm list union. The affine constraints have disjoint positive anchor supports; concentrated-column equalities involve no dispersed-column support variable. This proves the peeling bound. In Theorem 9, charging fixed exterior center incidences preserves that bound, so the argument genuinely extends an earlier coloring. The no-escape case also correctly permits equality of any nonadjacent center pair. Closed $24$-vertex blocks have no exterior center edges, and their nonconstant eleven-incidence criterion follows from the accepted local theorem. These facts justify composition across actual center edges, rather than merely separate existence of colorings.

The relevant comparisons with Papers 45, 49 and 50 [4,3,2] support a distinct contribution. Paper 49's intact-light theorem retains the full host; Paper 50's coherent-center theorem requires common host-neighbor groups and excludes actual host $K_7$ components. Neither provides the new arbitrary-retained-head theorem with its prescribed-$X$ and exterior-center guarantees. The demand compression and private-positive affine counting have clear precedents, which the revision credits. The substantive additions are the protected rotations, the enlarged boundary-extension scope, and the proof that the two region mechanisms compose. The classical degree-choosability and Brooks results [8,9] are used as established tools.

I ran seven targeted verification commands under Python 3.11, covering the degree-list controls, both host-reserve variants, arbitrary-head rotations, the projected large-arm relation, exact palette demands, dispersed columns, and center-region composition. All passed, and the regenerated audit files matched the submitted records byte for byte. This includes the $450$-vertex composition control with $2412$ edges and six fixed exterior center incidences. The checks corroborate the explicit colorings, incidence conditions, and finite Hall calculations. I did not regenerate every construction; the infinite results rest on the mathematical proofs, and the raw-profile sampling is not an exhaustive cover census.

Minor presentation improvements would help readers navigate the enlarged scope: add a compact table distinguishing the hypotheses and fixed versus chosen boundaries of Theorems 3–5, 7 and 9; repair joined words such as “has21 vertices” and “Theorem17”; and replace the stale reference to `exact_palette_demands.md` in the palette checker's docstring with the manuscript's actual lemma location. These do not affect correctness. The abstract is informative, and the stated limitations appropriately exclude simultaneous unrestricted head/center dispersion, general prescribed-$Z$ extension, and any biplanarity claim for the controls. This acceptance does not establish a solution to the selected problem.
