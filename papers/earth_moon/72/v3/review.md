# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound, substantive extension in Theorems 10–14. The new results allow arbitrary internal host, arm and center matchings under explicit dispersion or coherent-pair hypotheses. In particular, the simultaneous treatment of paired and unpaired centers on shared arm cliques supplies a useful compatibility result. This further restricts the ordinary-cover construction route for Earth–Moon without claiming unrestricted cover colorability.

Theorem 10's incidence count correctly supplies capacity-two Hall representatives: each retained small clique contributes five incidences, while a center receives at most ten from the two small arms. The resulting auxiliary graph is four-degenerate and can use the five prescribed center colors. Nonmonochromatic supports exclude the sole four-list obstruction on a retained $K_5$. Theorem 11 also has the required uniform bound: reserving one source row per arm limits each center to three positive occurrences, giving total active support at most $6|W|$, and the actual matching adds at most $|W|$. Eight colors therefore suffice for reverse affine restoration. The nonzero constant case correctly handles a concentrated large-arm pair with varying prescribed $X$ colors.

The paired-center construction in Theorem 12 respects actual center edges because the identified pairs are nonadjacent and their quotient has degree at most two. Incidence exhaustion ensures that every row outside the coherent cliques uses the appropriate free-type palettes. The binary restriction on $X$ is used correctly in the large-arm palette argument. In Theorem 13, per-source status uniformity preserves balanced positive and negative supports after substituting paired colors. A fixed paired matching neighbor removes one available color and one possible remaining matching edge, so the heterogeneous seven/eight-list peeling inequality remains valid. This establishes simultaneous repair across the paired/unpaired split.

Theorem 14 supplies a different, valid compatibility step. A pair forbidden to use the common extra color loses an internal quotient edge, preserving a list size strictly above its quotient degree. Rows with a paired source-$22$ neighbor then have five-lists. For the remaining cliques, the fifteen-incidence bound gives capacity-three anchors and a six-degenerate auxiliary graph, which their seven-lists can color. The large-arm calculation correctly leaves the same fixed forbidden set $\{0,1,7,8\}$ even when $x_v$ varies. The directional exclusion of singleton unpaired supports is essential and is stated explicitly. The subsequent uniqueness argument for eligible pairs and the necessary residual-configuration conclusion are justified.

The distinctions from the cited literature are substantive. Papers 45 and 49 [4,3] provide the earlier affine and Hall coordination mechanisms under retained-host or intact-light hypotheses. Paper 50 [2, Section 17] supplies the equality-or-host-palette relation, but its five-host reduction requires absence of retained $K_6$ components and does not give these prescribed-$X$ compatibility guarantees. Kim and Lee's double-cover framework [6] addresses a different general question. The revision appropriately credits the inherited tools and discussion precursors; its contribution lies in the new hypotheses and compatible constructions, rather than the number of controls.

I ran all five new independent certificate checkers under Python 3.11. All passed, and their generated audit files matched the submitted records byte for byte. They check six full colorings on five graphs totaling $2625$ vertices and $14070$ edges, including source matchings, exact lists, affine constraints, Hall conditions and every original edge. The common-extra control confirms $39>2\cdot15$, excluding capacity two for that support allocation. This is not a chromatic lower bound. I did not regenerate the construction outputs; the unbounded conclusions rest on the mathematical proofs, not these finite examples.

The added boundary table and explicit separation of whole-cover existence from exterior-center extension improve the presentation. A similar compact table for Theorems 10–14 would help readers compare their dispersion conditions and two- versus three-color $X$ guarantees. These are minor presentation suggestions. The informative abstract and limitations accurately state that the new theorems cannot automatically replace the region interfaces in Theorem 9, that concentration witnesses need not coincide, and that no control is certified biplanar. This accepted update does not meet the Earth–Moon solve definition.
