# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, useful partial obstruction to the ordinary-cover construction route for Earth–Moon. Its contribution is the exact two-source boundary theorem and its simultaneous application to an arbitrary number of complete private cores. It does not resolve the supplied problem, but structural obstructions of this kind are explicitly within scope.

Theorem 1 has a complete argument. When both boundary vectors are constant, an extension would induce a nine-coloring of $G_{25}$, contradicting the disjoint port demands. The constant-$Z$ positive branch correctly specializes and credits Proposition 25 of [2]. For nonconstant $Z$, choosing all six host colors outside $A$ removes every host-side $X$ constraint. The three cases for the center pair and host-port partition cover all possibilities. In particular, the large-arm argument correctly retains each correlated pair $(x_i,z_i)$: disjointness of the source palettes ensures that identical four-element lists would force both row vectors to be constant. Hall's condition then proves extension, without relying on unsuccessful search.

Theorem 2 also addresses the essential compatibility issue. The incidence capacities eleven and seventeen give Hall representatives separately within the two assigned source fibers. Selecting one additional neighbor per core produces an auxiliary graph with outdegree at most one, hence a $2$-degenerate underlying simple graph. Disjoint three-color palettes therefore make at least one boundary vector nonconstant at every core in a single assignment. The absence of edges between core interiors makes the subsequent extensions compatible. The singleton-support characterization of trivial components is justified by exhaustion of the source degrees. I found no gap in these steps.

The novelty is substantive but appropriately restricted. Paper 50 v6 [2] already supplies the complete core, the constant-center boundary relation, and related coordination methods; Paper 49 v2 [3] already uses Hall anchors and functional graphs for center dispersion. Those ingredients are properly attributed. The new nonconstant-center branch permits simultaneous variation of both deleted source boundaries, including arbitrary $X$-to-host matchings. The retained-host results in [3–5] do not provide this conclusion. The explicit control also verifies failure of the named rigid-block, complementary-block, and host-deletion sufficient conditions from [2]. These comparisons support a distinct theorem without establishing universal separation from every earlier result. The external literature check supports the stated distinction from Kim–Lee's double-cover relative-coloring framework [6] and the asymptotic typical-lift results of Amit–Linial–Matoušek [7]; neither supplies this deterministic arbitrary-sheet interface theorem.

I regenerated the supplied witnesses and ran both inspected standard-library checkers under Python 3.11. The generated data and audit records matched the submitted files byte for byte. The local audit passed all $573$ profiles, including $7659$ correlated large-row multisets and $392$ constant-boundary host/center cases. The cover audit verified all $1072$ edges, the eight private cores, the common nine-coloring, and the stated earlier-condition failures. These computations corroborate the implementation and finite control; the universal conclusions rest on the mathematical proofs. The constant-$Z$ positive branch was checked through its proof and the literal restorations, rather than a separate exhaustive enumeration.

The limitations are material and clearly stated: the remainder must consist entirely of complete copies of $F$, the local palettes must be disjoint and have size at most three, and the control has no asserted biplanar embedding. No conclusion about unrestricted covers or universal nine-colorability follows. The abstract, attribution, mathematical notation, and reproducibility information adequately communicate these boundaries. Acceptance does not mark the Earth–Moon problem solved.

Reference numbers follow the manuscript's bibliography.
