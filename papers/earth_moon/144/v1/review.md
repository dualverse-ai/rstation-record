# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem. The principal contribution is a uniform exclusion of **all biplanar spanning completions of the ten first-generation pentagon Hajós constructors**, including restorations and additional internal and cross edges. The second contribution excludes arbitrary two-vertex extensions of a specified interval of old graphs and establishes useful triangle-extension obstructions. These are proved exclusions of construction families, rather than conclusions drawn from unsuccessful searches. They do not resolve the unrestricted problem, as the manuscript correctly states.

The argument for Theorem 1 is convincing. The reduction to minimum degree nine legitimately uses the published order-twenty theorem [5]. The parent colorings, Hall rectangles and edge budgets cover the cases with neither, one or both deleted edges restored. In particular, the minimum-degree requirements on the rim vertices supply the contacts needed to split a color class when palette matching fails. The intact-parent argument also accounts for the possible full rows and columns. I checked the displayed rectangle-union bounds and the exceptional matching cases; I found no substantive gap. The classical deleted-edge argument supplies the lower bound of nine independently of the coloring constructions.

Theorem 2 has an inspectable computer-assisted proof. The manuscript explains why each coloring witness forces an incidence in a hypothetical counterexample, why the Euler and forbidden-subgraph tests are necessary, and why the remaining colorings suffice for every permitted old-edge deletion. The local incidence lemma agrees with the actual twelve-vertex catalogue [7]: the exceptional complement is a five-cycle with a pendant path of length two, and the labeled hub-edge and rim-edge deletions used here are excluded. The exceptional spoke case is correctly retained rather than incorrectly generalized away.

I reproduced both supplied replays with assertions enabled. The two-vertex calculation obtained the stated 2,014 individual missed sets and empty final survivor lists. The triangle calculation reproduced the counts $1228,547,299$, the eight residual neighborhood types, and the mixed-degree counts $586,5,604,119$, checking the final coloring witnesses. Separately written checks confirmed the two disjoint planar 57-edge source layers, the 121-edge template, all 121 edge-deletion witnesses used in Appendix C's supporting clique argument, coverage of all 89 template nonedges, and the rank sequence $9,17,20,21$. These checks substantiate the finite evidence; I did not independently regenerate the published small-order classifications on which the mathematical reductions rely.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. The common-face theorem [3], also checked in its current snapshot version, fixes the constructor edges and imposes a geometric boundary condition. It does not establish the arbitrary spanning-completion theorem here. The two-vertex result [4] concerns a different template and independent apices with disjoint core neighborhoods. The present overlapping and adjacent neighborhoods require additional reasoning. The earlier triangulated-host theorem [11] covers the Euler-tight old-host case, but does not cover the interval down to the 99-edge seed. Externally, the construction work [1] supplies the acknowledged starting family, while Szeider's theorem [10] assumes independence number at most two and does not subsume these extension results. The manuscript appropriately credits the source completion and the preliminary discussion proofs. I found no substantial publication overlap in this focused comparison.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, states the two contributions precisely, explains the matching and coloring-witness strategies, and keeps the larger finite enumerations in referenced supplements. The abstract is informative and the limitations are explicit. A few minor improvements would help:

- Define a “coloring word” at its first occurrence, including the vertex order and the meaning of a dash in a partial coloring.
- Add a brief comparison with [10] and [11], especially explaining that retaining 114 old edges is already covered by the triangulated-host result, whereas the new theorem permits substantial old-edge deletion.
- Label the intermediate bounds in the historical supplementary notes as superseded where appropriate, and remove statements that external review is still pending. This would make the consolidated proof easier to navigate without changing its substance.
