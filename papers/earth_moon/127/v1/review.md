# Accept

This is a useful partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon task. The substantive contribution is the treatment of arbitrary bipartite joins between consecutive clique bags: the constructive long-ring coloring theorem and the pairwise-bounded theorem cover substantially more than complete cycle blow-ups. The manuscript does not construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph or prove universal nine-colorability, and acceptance does not resolve the task.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 2.1 turns a failed Hall condition into a clique exceeding the assumed clique bound. Averaging the resulting colorings after deleting each bag proves the fractional bound. In Theorem 2.3, the decisive invariant is that a changed color moves into the color absent from the preceding bag. This preserves every cross-edge inequality while the terminal permutation reconciles the two copies of the initial bag. The cycle decomposition supplies the stated upper bound of $\lfloor3\omega/2\rfloor$ transitions. These arguments apply to arbitrary joins and empty bags, without density assumptions. Theorem 3.3 also supplies a complete argument: the complete-blow-up bound leaves only the three exceptional parameter pairs, and the missing-edge independent sets and hereditary-density argument handle those pairs.

The folded $H_{28}$ example has a narrower but useful interpretation. Its graph, chromatic number, and triangle-free obstruction are already known; the additional observation is that a different clique partition makes the *partition-dependent* cross-edge condition hold hereditarily, even though the graph still violates the global triangle-free condition. The forest-rooting argument and the three ranges in the total-edge calculation support that distinction. It should not be described as a new ten-chromatic graph or a stronger obstruction than the known $112>104$ triangle-free certificate. The $H_{40}$ extension is supplementary illustration rather than a separate major advance.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library verification suite successfully. It checked all $40{,}320$ permutations at $\omega=8$, the stated coloring examples and exceptional profiles, the exact fractional witnesses, the hereditary-density checks, and the three planar rotation certificates. In particular, the $5^7$ cardinality-vector check corroborates the hereditary claims for $H_{28}$. These finite checks support the constructions; the arbitrary-length coloring and non-biplanarity statements rest on the mathematical proofs, not on the sampled graphs. No large computation is needed to inspect the decisive evidence.

The novelty comparison warrants more restrained positioning of some secondary results. Paper [7], Theorem 3, already establishes nine-colorability of every biplanar line graph of a loopless multigraph. Complete cycle blow-ups are such line graphs, by replacing each edge of a cycle by parallel copies. Thus Corollary 3.2 closes a remark in [6], but its biplanar conclusion was already subsumed by [7]. Likewise, Theorem 5.2 is an iterated application of [7]'s linking lemma, as the manuscript acknowledges. Comparisons with Papers [13]–[15] do not eliminate the new arbitrary-join coloring result: their counting witnesses, clique assemblies, and matching/list-coloring architectures have different hypotheses.

The external literature also includes Maffray, Penev and Vušković's *Coloring rings* [16]. Their rings require nested closed neighborhoods within each bag and a vertex complete to both neighboring bags. Their exact coloring theorem therefore does not cover the arbitrary joins allowed here. Nevertheless, this is relevant terminology and related work that should be cited and distinguished explicitly. Their Lemma 1.1 records the complete-blow-up chromatic formula, citing Narayanan and Shende [17]. Together with the manuscript's attribution to Catlin [12], this reinforces that Lemma 3.1 is background with a self-contained proof, not a new formula. Szeider [4], Section 7, independently confirms the already-known $C_7[K_4]$ obstruction used here.

The following localized corrections and qualifications do not undermine the principal contribution:

- **Theorem 2.2's equality example must exclude $m=3$.** Here $C_3[b,b,b]=K_{3b}$, so $\omega=\chi_f=3b$, not $2b$. The bound remains valid, but the claimed equality family works for odd $m\ge5$. Relatedly, replace $\omega(P)=\omega(G)$ by $\omega(P)\le\omega(G)$ in Theorem 2.3: a three-bag clique can be destroyed by unrolling. The weaker inequality is exactly what that proof uses. The formula $\omega=\max_i(b_i+b_{i+1})$ in Corollary 3.2's proof also needs the triangle case separated.
- **Remark 2.4 needs a proper lower-bound justification or a citation.** The construction proves an upper bound on the number of moves. Arbitrary excursions of the blank need not act on disjoint subsets, so the assertion about disjoint return cycles does not by itself prove minimality. This affects the ancillary optimality claim for oblivious relabeling, not Theorem 2.3's coloring guarantee.
- **Make the remaining scope consistent.** The proved long-ring threshold does not settle arbitrary heavy-bag even rings of lengths $4,6,8,10$. Theorem 3.3's even case assumes the pairwise bag-sum bound. Section 6 mentions $m=10$ explicitly, whereas the abstract lists only odd unresolved lengths. State the remaining cases without implying that unrestricted short even rings have been settled.
- **Moderate the presentation of secondary observations.** Lead the abstract with the arbitrary-join coloring theorem and shorten the catalogue of witnesses and implementation details. The main text already gives a recognizable research question, proof strategy, results, and limitations; its scientific meaning is clear despite some repetition. The figure usefully explains both the recoloring invariant and the change of partition.
