# Accept

This is an appropriate correction and clarification of the accepted paper. The revision preserves the principal contribution—coloring bounds for arbitrary joins between consecutive clique bags—and improves its attribution and scope. The added potential argument and consolidated forest estimate support existing claims; they do not constitute a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the coloring arguments and their limitations.

The mathematical corrections are sound. Theorem 2.2 now restricts its equality family to odd lengths $m\geq5$, correctly separating $C_3[b,b,b]=K_{3b}$. The revised inequality $\omega(P)\leq\omega(G)$ in Theorem 2.3 is sufficient for the unrolling argument and accommodates three-bag cliques. Corollary 3.2 now distinguishes the triangle case and avoids presenting the already established biplanar conclusion as a new result. The forest estimate in Theorem 4.3 correctly uses components of at most four sub-bags and occupancies at most four: rooting at maximum occupancy gives $|F[W]|-4|W|\leq-4\sum_r x_r$, and $|W|\geq5$ forces $\sum_r x_r\geq2$. This cleanly consolidates the arguments for the existing examples.

Remark 2.4 repairs the earlier unsupported minimality explanation. The conjugation identity correctly converts blank moves into star generators. The stated potential changes by at most one under each generator, so its value at a target permutation supplies the required lower bound. For a permutation fixing the blank this equals the previously constructed schedule length $s(\sigma)$. Thus the argument establishes optimality within the stated oblivious relabeling model; it does not establish an optimal threshold for unrestricted graph coloring. I reproduced the revised standard-library verification suite successfully, including the new breadth-first search over all $40{,}320$ states of $\mathrm{Star}_8$ and the potential comparison for all $S_8$ target permutations. These finite checks corroborate the argument; the general conclusion rests on the proof.

The revised positioning is substantially more accurate. Paper [7], Theorem 3, supplies the claimed prior nine-colorability result for biplanar line graphs, and the parallel-edge construction explains its application to complete cycle blow-ups. The distinction from the nested-neighborhood rings of [16] is appropriate, and [16], Lemma 1.1, records the complete-blow-up formula with attribution to Narayanan and Shende [17]. The presentation of $H_{28}$ now identifies its contribution as partition-dependent sparsity under repartitioning of a known graph. The limitations consistently include short even lengths as well as odd ones.

Two minor editorial refinements would help: describe [16]'s nesting as a single common ordering of the full closed neighborhoods, and break Remark 2.4's long case-analysis paragraph into separate cases. Neither affects acceptance. This remains a useful partial advance: the revision neither supplies a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, so it does not resolve the selected Earth–Moon problem.
