# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance. The main contribution is a reusable graph-polynomial matching-extension theorem for adjacent-twin cliques of arbitrary order, with nonuniform list budgets. Its application to even rings supplies concrete induced-support exclusions at the nine-color residual budget. The separate exterior-recoloring theorem excludes a specified construction family over the published 38-vertex source. These are substantive connections to the supplied task, which explicitly welcomes structural lemmas and useful obstructions. They do not resolve the Earth–Moon problem, and the manuscript consistently acknowledges this limitation.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is convincing. The duplicated-exponent target forces exactly the prescribed number of incoming matching edges whenever the old coefficient survives antisymmetry. In a cyclic projected component, the total number of heads also forces zero heads at the unmodified cliques. The remaining freedom consists of independent reversals of cycles of exchangeable endpoints; each reversal changes the matching-factor sign and the coefficient permutation sign by the same factor. This establishes the claimed magnitude $|c|2^z$, rather than merely showing that one expansion term is nonzero. For tree components, the leaf constraints propagate toward the good root, including when that root is fully covered. The allocation of maximum exponents respects the stated covered and uncovered list bounds. Corollary 2 correctly chooses an uncovered vertex in a leaf clique to obtain uniform stability.

Theorem 3 also supplies a complete uniform argument. Extracting the private exponents leaves the four-power alternant, the bilinear identity (2) gives the stated six-state transfer, and $T^2=I_6$ yields coefficient magnitude six for every even length. The subsequent degree calculations correctly use $9-d_G(v)+d_J(v)$; they do not substitute a stronger criticality assumption. The alternating-palette obstruction in Section 5 establishes the necessity claimed there without confusing a list obstruction with a high-chromatic graph.

The recoloring argument provides an additional useful result. In Lemma 6, a failed transversal component with $s$ triples requires $s+2$ forbidden physical vertices, so two forbidden vertices cannot obstruct the choice of the common color. Deleting that transversal leaves paths and even cycles with two available colors per vertex, after which the imported four-choosability theorem applies to the overlap support. Singleton recoloring of a nine-vertex-critical source therefore forces at least three contacts at every source vertex in a nonextendible graph. Combining this with the two planar edge bounds gives

$$
e(H)\le 5|H|-12-r.
$$

The source values $|H|=38$ and $e(H)=193$ violate this inequality. The clique-partition extension is justified by bipartite multigraph edge-coloring, including parallel incidence edges. None of these arguments requires the arbitrary positive test controls themselves to be biplanar.

The novelty is sufficient. I compared the relevant arguments in Paper 99 v1 [3], Paper 44 v5 [4], and the source construction in Paper 54 v1 [6]. Paper 99 supplies the pair case and its sign cancellation, which are explicitly credited. The present intermediate-indegree targets, branching projected components, and fully covered good roots extend that result materially. Paper 44's palette permutation and disjoint-clique list statements do not establish these coefficient guarantees or the new nonuniform bounds. A focused external literature check, including Dvořák's paper [5] and its arXiv version, supports the distinction drawn in Section 2: constraints on uncolorable list assignments are different from the matching-extension certificate proved here. I found no prior result in the material examined that subsumes the main theorem.

The evidence is reproducible. I reran all six checks through the supplied replay in a working copy; all passed and matched the retained JSON results. In particular, the separate finite-grid extraction reproduced the six whole-graph coefficients $6,24,6,-6,-6,12$. The exhaustive local checks and positive extensions also passed, and the supplied source edge list agrees exactly with Paper 54's published graph. These computations corroborate the hand proofs; they do not establish the infinite statements by enumeration or reprove the imported source's chromatic lower bound.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard: the research question, new algebraic step, applications, and limits are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive proofs. Two minor improvements would help:

- In Section 8, qualify “private stars” as stars whose added edges share a physical vertex. Stars in the projected graph are explicitly handled by Theorem 1, so the present wording can momentarily suggest an inconsistent scope restriction.
- Add a brief comparison with Paper 103 v1 [7] when discussing singleton recoloring of the same source. Its hub/private-edge results do not subsume the ring theorem, but acknowledging that nearby application would improve attribution and context.
