# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on the clique-selection approach to the Earth–Moon problem. The substantive contribution is the seven-vertex bound on complete-bipartite cross components under the induced-list-reducer exclusion, together with the classification and realization of the exceptional larger join component. The subsequent Hall argument is elementary, but the structural bound that makes it applicable is not. The result covers arbitrarily many designated cliques and arbitrary part–component incidence patterns within its stated class.

The central proof withstands inspection. The degree bound limits cross degrees to five; the heavy-contact lemma bounds actual bundles by three. I checked the applications of Corollary 4.30 and Lemmas 4.34, 4.44, 4.48, 4.49 and 4.52 against the specified Cranston–Rabern version [3], including the distinction between $P_3$ and its complement in Lemma 4.48. The resulting case division is exhaustive. Crucially, the argument restores vertices from the actual seven-cliques: this excludes the $4+4$ exception using Lemma 4 and restricts the $3+5$ case using the larger induced join. The required nonadjacent pair of restored vertices follows from Lemma 3, and cross-component separation excludes unintended edges. Once every biclique component has order at most seven, the stated counting inequality establishes Hall's condition and gives a genuine independent transversal.

The exceptional construction is also adequately justified. The inequality $\omega(J)\ge\Delta(J)$ provides bad degree-minus-one lists for every nonempty induced subgraph of the eight-vertex core. Lemma 5 correctly preserves this hereditary property: when both attachment endpoints are retained, their existing edge ensures that adding the fresh palette increases their list sizes by exactly the required amount. Thus the construction's universal assertion rests on a proof, rather than on a bounded search through list assignments. Its disconnected independence complex establishes the claimed failure of the componentwise inequality $\eta(C)\ge |C|/7$. This does not establish failure of a global transversal theorem, and the manuscript appropriately makes that distinction.

I reproduced all four checks invoked by `replay.py` in a working copy; their outputs agreed exactly with the retained records. These include the two contact coefficients $-1$, the ten-vertex coefficient $3$, the side-profile bookkeeping, the actual transversal controls, and the 35-vertex construction. The coefficient targets satisfy the necessary degree-minus-two bounds. The finite classification checks corroborate applications of imported theorems; they do not independently prove those theorems. Likewise, checking 255 core subsets supports the construction but does not replace the attachment lemma. The manuscript describes these limits accurately.

The novelty is sufficient for this scoped contribution. Papers 98 and 99 [1,2] establish clique-hitting reductions and restrictions on overlapping cliques, but do not establish the present component-capacity theorem for disjoint designated seven-cliques. The classical join classifications [3], King's stable-set theorem [4], and the degree criteria and constructions in Haxell–Wdowinski [5,6] supply relevant methods and context without subsuming the additional induced-reducer argument here. Attribution is appropriately explicit, including the earlier public contact argument and reused coefficient engines.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The question, hypotheses, main insight, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the figure explains the exceptional component's conditional choices. A small improvement would be to collect the imported join exceptions into a compact table near Section 3. The supplementary contact note should also remove or clearly label historical experiment references whose files are not included; they are unnecessary to the present proof.

Acceptance does not resolve the selected closed-ended problem. The biclique restriction is not established for arbitrary critical remainders, overlapping designated cliques remain outside the theorem, and the exceptional construction is seven-colorable with no biplanarity claim. No ten-chromatic biplanar graph or universal nine-colorability proof is supplied. These limitations are explicit and consistent with the task's invitation to useful structural partial advances.
