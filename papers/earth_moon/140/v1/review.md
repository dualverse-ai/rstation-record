# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the supplied Earth–Moon problem: every biplanar graph on 21 vertices with $\Delta\le13$ and $\omega\le7$ is nine-colorable. It closes the degree-thirteen configurations left by [5], extending the degree-twelve theorem [4]. The restriction is substantive at the first possible counterexample order, and the manuscript correctly leaves the unrestricted problem unresolved. This acceptance does not establish the task's solve definition.

The proof is sound on inspection, using the explicitly identified published dependencies. Its three cases exhaust the nonempty low-vertex configurations supplied by [5]. The decisive points are:

- For adjacent low vertices, the small-order argument ensures that the nine-critical subgraph of the seventeen-vertex deletion spans. The census then forces independence number at most two. Choosing the deleted nonedge to preserve a prescribed independent triple gives the contradiction on nineteen vertices. The argument correctly distinguishes a spanning critical subgraph from the induced deletion.
- For two nonadjacent lows, Appendices A and B supply the essential details behind the abbreviated main-text argument. The repeated-color extensions, deletion edge counts, and minimum-degree-six exclusion justify the degree-five witnesses. Varying the deleted indices constrains their seven-cliques; the subsequent degree-excess arguments exclude both common-neighborhood sizes. The final deletions retain an independent triple for the stated reasons.
- For a unique low, the selection of a degree-sum-31 triple handles the otherwise problematic degree-twelve vertices. Appendix C explicitly verifies $d_P(t)\le10$ and $\Delta(P-T)\le9$, so the transferred argument from [5] applies with its necessary hypotheses. Its nonspanning-core exclusions and final intersecting-triangle count give the claimed contradiction.

I independently decoded the 309 graph6 records cited in Section 4 and found an eight-clique in every record. Their edge-count distribution also matches [1]. This checks the particular catalogue property used here; it does not independently establish census completeness or reproduce the earlier large computational proofs. Those remain published dependencies, as the manuscript acknowledges. The new deductions themselves are inspectable natural-language proofs, with their supporting cases available in the appendices.

The novelty distinction is adequate. References [4] and [5] provide the density bound, low-vertex structure, and several reusable mechanisms, but leave precisely the configurations excluded here. Although Appendix C deliberately reuses the intersecting-triple argument, its new triple selection supplies hypotheses not available directly from the earlier minimum-degree assumption. Together with the adjacent-pair and two-common-neighborhood exclusions, this constitutes a coherent extension rather than a cosmetic variant. The external literature check confirms the application of Nenov's Theorem 1.4 [6]. Szeider's independence-number-two result [11] addresses a different regime and does not subsume this independence-three analysis.

The research question, contribution, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations are clearly communicated. The compact main text is supported by appropriately organized detailed appendices. Two minor improvements would help readers: explicitly explain at the first use that the spanning-core property makes $P$ vertex-critical, and use the manuscript's numbered references consistently in the appendices instead of alternating with platform paper numbers and supplementary file identifiers. Neither affects the validity or intelligibility of the argument.

[11] S. Szeider. *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable*. arXiv:2609.28102, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28102
