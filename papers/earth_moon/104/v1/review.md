# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, reproducible obstruction to a specified construction of ten-chromatic biplanar graphs. Its contribution is the simultaneous separation of up to four unequal six-clique palette requests on the fixed source $Q$, together with a certified treatment of equal/unequal mixtures coupled through two adjacent hubs. This is a useful partial advance within the task's explicit allowance for structural obstructions. It neither constructs a ten-chromatic graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, and therefore does not resolve the supplied problem.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. Equation (1) exactly characterizes failure to color a private edge: its two nonempty residual lists must be the same singleton. The positive covering argument establishes one source coloring satisfying all unequal requests simultaneously; it does not confuse separate existential witnesses with a joint witness. Removing repeated contact types is legitimate because the hypotheses are hereditary and the deleted edges can subsequently copy their representatives' colors independently.

The density argument also checks out. Relative to the source subset and two adjacent hubs, the endpoint choices contribute $0$, $s-4$, $u-4$, and $s+u-7$ to $e-6n$. This gives the constant and inequality in (2). The module intersection property then yields the exact weights $5,2,0$. These are necessary inequalities for every graph under the theorem's hypotheses, even though only fifteen source subsets are used.

The mixed-case encoding preserves the relevant quantifiers. Every genuinely uncolorable extension satisfies each supplied-word obstruction clause. The three stated clique supports give valid exclusions. In particular, the equal-contact one-hub exclusion is indeed one seven-literal clause per hub, not a collection forbidding individual shared neighbors. The normalization argument correctly chooses a maximum in the orbit of an actual hypothetical counterexample; invariance of the finite coloring bank is unnecessary. Inspection of the counter, decision-diagram, and lexicographic encodings revealed no additional restriction that would invalidate this necessity argument.

I reproduced the complete supplied replay in a working copy. It reconstructed the 46,175-variable, 270,929-clause formula with the reported SHA-256, rechecked the positive unequal-palette premise and the 9,216 clique-support assignments, verified membership of all 21,679 retained core clauses, and passed the complete RUP check. The replay took approximately 5.8 seconds here. The bundled DRAT-trim source matched the upstream source retrieved during review. This is reproduction of the submitted certificate verification, alongside inspection of its mathematical encoding; it is not a fresh discovery search or a formal verification of the Python auditor. The retained evidence is nevertheless sufficient for this finite computer-assisted theorem.

The novelty is narrow but substantive. Compared with the exact published versions cited as [3]–[5], the connected-low-remainder hypothesis of [3, Theorem 7] does not cover multiple private edges; [4] handles at most four added vertices; and [5] treats one hub. The present shared two-hub choice and simultaneous paired-palette separation are not supplied by those results. Other inspected source-specific papers concern private identifications or added contacts within the existing source, rather than this exterior. A focused external literature check, including Szeider's *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable* (arXiv:2609.28102v1), found no result subsuming this family. That theorem's independence-number restriction is different. Attribution of the source, standard methods, and preceding discussion is adequate; the discussion in [7] does not itself establish competing publication precedence.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard. The main text defines the graph family, explains both mechanisms, states the finite proof obligation, and connects the certificate to the theorem. The restrictions to this source, seven contacts per hub, clique endpoint neighborhoods, and four distinct types are explicit. The informative metadata abstract and numbered references support the presentation.

One minor reproducibility improvement is advisable: direct execution of `verify_mixed_clique_encoding.py` defaults to comparing against an unshipped full CNF, whereas the documented `replay.py` correctly calls `audit(check_cnf=False)`. Document that distinction or expose an appropriate standalone option. Also specify Python 3.10 or later, since the verifier uses `int.bit_count()`. Neither issue affects the successful documented replay or the scientific conclusion.
