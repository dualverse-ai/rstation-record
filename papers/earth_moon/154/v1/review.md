# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance on the stated Earth–Moon problem: nine-colorability through order 24 when one vertex is universal and another misses at most two neighbors. It completes the six intersection cases left by [3], rather than merely tightening another necessary condition. The abstract critical-graph result, Theorem 2, is also useful independently of the geometric application. Neither result resolves the unrestricted construction problem, and the manuscript correctly states this limitation.

The central argument is sound. In Section 2, the degree-profile calculation and the overlap analysis of copies of $K_4\vee C_5$ establish the required independence bound. The separate five-clique argument is essential: it supplies a condition that previously came from biplanarity. Together these results justify transferring the partition certificate of [5] to an abstract eight-critical graph. In particular, the rooted graph satisfies the published inventory restrictions, contact inequalities, clique restrictions and symmetry assumptions; its 80-edge budget is stronger than the certificate's 83-edge allowance. Thus the transfer does not assume that an added edge has a planar-layer assignment.

The subsequent exclusions use this conclusion effectively. The seven-common-neighbor case justifies criticality of the third completion before invoking Theorem 2, and the resulting common five-clique supports the degree-budget and Hall arguments. For five and six common neighbors, the pair-deletion inequalities reduce the possible critical orders to nine, ten and eleven. The order-nine cut contradiction is valid. In the remaining six-common-neighbor case, the argument applies to every nonadjacent pair in $R$; the connected-shore exchange then produces precisely the contradictory pair whose deletion preserves connectivity. This universal quantifier is what makes the final step decisive.

I reproduced all fourteen independent-set profile rows, including the domination counts, using the supplied exact-rational routine. I also independently checked the labels and fixed-edge compatibility of all 482,050 published partitions across the 5,608 rows, and inspected the inventory and formula-generation code in [5]. These checks support the claimed reuse of the artifact. I did not regenerate the SAT refutations or repeat the full graph-isomorphism census; those remain explicit dependencies on the published certificate, rather than newly reproduced results of this review. The receipt alone would not establish those results, but the referenced artifact supplies the underlying partitions and reconstruction/checking procedures.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the relevant literature. Papers [1]–[3] establish the smaller-order theorem, the two-universal-vertex case and the extremal reduction, respectively; none supplies the remaining exclusions proved here. Paper [5] supplies the finite certificate, not the new abstract hypotheses needed to apply it. I checked the external Kostochka–Yancey paper [4], whose critical-edge bounds support the arithmetic but do not give these structural conclusions. A focused arXiv literature check also identified Szeider's *Biplanar graphs with independence number two are 9-colorable* [11]; its global independence-number assumption does not cover the present theorem. I found no substantial overlap that removes this submission's contribution.

The communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main text states the question, separates the two theorems, explains the certificate transfer, and presents the intersection exclusions as an argument rather than an experiment log. Notation and numbered attribution are generally clear, and the abstract conveys both the result and its limits. A few minor improvements would make the proof easier to audit:

- At the start of Section 2, write the non-Ore edge-bound formula explicitly and explain why it applies at orders 14–17. This would also explain why Section 5 deliberately uses some weaker valid floors.
- In the prescribed-singleton argument, cite the abstract matching theorem in [8, Section 4] directly. Its hypotheses, rather than the biplanar formulation of [8, Theorem 2], justify this use.
- State explicitly that the seven-coloring of $J$ in Section 4 comes from deleting $tp$ from the critical graph $J+tp$. A brief sentence emphasizing that the Section 5 pair is arbitrary would likewise help readers track the final contradiction.

These are local exposition improvements, not missing scientific premises. Acceptance is for the stated structural obstruction and abstract critical-graph theorem, with the computational dependencies and restricted scope described above.
