# Accept

The sixteen-core exclusion is a sound, useful computer-assisted partial advance on the supplied problem. It closes an entire critical-core order at the first possible counterexample order, with the required quantification over every independent triple and every sixteen-vertex nine-critical subgraph. It does not resolve order twenty-one or the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem. Acceptance concerns Theorem 1 and Corollary 2; the separate claim that both geometric inputs are indispensable needs the correction below.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. The edge threshold from [6], Gallai decomposition, and the census in [4] justify the six-core inventory; the transfer from [7] does not require its independent-four-set hypothesis. The degree identity correctly gives $r+|D|+\sum j\le76-e(F)$. The remainder enumeration retains zero and negative list bounds, and extra core edges are explicitly permitted. The partition cuts correctly express failure to extend a valid core coloring. Their invalidation clauses handle added core edges, and the one-way auxiliary implications in the independent encoding are sufficient. The symmetry restriction preserves a representative of every genuine counterexample, even though the finite collection of recorded cuts need not itself be invariant. Linking cuts are justified on their witness edge sets by [8], rather than by an invalid assumption that biplanarity is closed under arbitrary contractions.

I regenerated the remainder domains and checked exact coverage of all $3{,}105$ non-Alon–Tarsi jobs. Independent checks validated all $36$ polynomial witnesses, every recorded partition and clique/density-set specification, and all $11$ linking witnesses. Fresh solving and DRAT verification succeeded for $F(2,2)$–288, $F(1,1)$–130, $F(1,2)$–151, and $F(1,3)$–151. The first also passed the alternative per-coloring encoding. These are targeted reproductions, not a replay of all $3{,}105$ refutations or a regeneration of the imported census. The released definitions, cut data, and proof-generation route make the remaining computations reproducible. Independent checks of D288 and L151 confirmed their ten-chromaticity, criticality, fractional chromatic numbers $28/3$ and $9$, and their respective density-violation and hereditary-sparsity claims; the fractional values were checked with exact rational primal and dual witnesses.

The contribution is distinct from the order-twenty result [4], the independent-four-set exclusion [7], and the residual edge threshold [6]. The earlier two-core exclusions are credited, while the four additional core families complete a substantive branch. The near-apex interpretation overlaps the acknowledged work [15] and should remain supporting explanation rather than a separate novelty claim. A focused external comparison with Szeider [17] and Trivedi [18] found different scopes: independence at most two and whole five-cycle clique blow-ups, respectively. Neither supplies this arbitrary-attachment exclusion at independence three.

Two corrections would improve the final presentation and replay interface:

- **Narrow the geometric-necessity claim in the abstract and Sections 1 and 6.** D288 is not excluded only by density: it has a linking certificate using exclusively $K_9$ obstructions. In the supplied labeling, take $a=5$, $Q=\{7,8,9,10,11,12,13,18\}$, and $C=Q\setminus N(a)=\{7,8,9,10,11,18\}$. Adding the missing $aC$ edges gives $K_9$. For every $c\in C$, the identification graph on $Z\cup\{a,c\}$, where $Z=V\setminus(Q\cup\{a\})$, contains the nine-clique $\{m,0,1,2,3,4,14,16,20\}$, with $m$ the merged vertex. Thus this example does not establish that density is indispensable when linking remains available. The supported statements are that both cut families occur in the released refutations, and that L151 defeats the hereditary-density relaxation. This correction leaves the main theorem intact.
- **Make the top-level replay check completeness explicitly.** It currently checks the certificate files it finds, without asserting that their identifiers equal the complete expected non-Alon–Tarsi domain. The supplied archive passes that stronger check, so this is a robustness improvement rather than a missing case. Also document how to select the CaDiCaL fallback used for the reported eighteen difficult jobs; the top-level script currently selects Glucose throughout.
