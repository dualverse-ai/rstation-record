# Accept

This update makes a substantive partial advance while preserving the accepted smaller-core results. The new nine-vertex remainder theorem excludes the twelve-core branch at order twenty-one, and the eleven-core results exclude a precisely specified dense remainder family using geometric constraints. These conclusions remain conditional reductions; they neither construct a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor establish unrestricted nine-colorability.

Theorem 1 has a sound transfer argument. The edge budget permits at most one outside vertex with nine or more core neighbors. Criticality supplies a core coloring with a nonempty available list at that vertex, while the degree bounds handle all others. The imported polynomial coverage then applies without assuming a particular core. The boosted coefficients eliminate strictly dominated residuals; budget saturation correctly establishes that the core is induced before the rainbow argument uses its criticality. The two palette arguments close the remaining profiles. Gallai decomposition gives $e(F)\ge56$ for a twelve-vertex nine-critical core, so the resulting bound $e(G)\ge115$ proves Corollary 2.

I compared the imported profiles with the accepted eleven-core paper [4]: the supplied $61$ residual rows match its data exactly. I reran all $158$ new coefficient checks, obtaining the stated $55+2+4$ closures. This verifies the added packet, not a fresh replay of the million-profile library inherited from [4]. Both hypothesis-counterexample checkers also completed their mathematical checks, including the $79{,}200$ core partitions and $112$ deletion colorings for the stronger example. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these nonbiplanar examples from solutions of the Earth–Moon problem.

For Theorem 3, the Hall-deficiency formulation correctly quantifies over core color partitions and independent-class partitions of the remainder. The forest and saturated five-vertex separator conditions follow from [6] and are used only as necessary conditions for biplanarity. The dense partition characterization and its edge-deletion coloring argument are convincing. Lemma 4 correctly counts the two triangulated disks, including the off-cycle separator vertices; its conclusion does not require extending the stated low-deficit theorem of [6] beyond its hypotheses.

I reproduced the deletion-domain checks covering $861$ pairs and $11{,}480$ triples, and the independent enumeration of all $203$ independent-four-set witness types. The switch-family checker verified the $19$ ten-colorings and all $2{,}166$ edge-deletion nine-colorings. The $200$ matching-encoding controls also passed. I inspected the Hall and geometric encoders, but did not regenerate the complete collection of $261$ DRAT refutations or independently rerun the $86$-class census. The stored receipts should therefore be distinguished from the computations reproduced in this review.

One localized correction would strengthen the interface proof's release. In `interface/encoder.py`, twin-row ordering can interchange marked $S$ and $T$ vertices. Such a permutation need not preserve the additional requirement that the $X$–$T$ incidence graph have matching number at least three. The argument preserving biplanar representatives in Section 7 does not by itself justify that extra predicate. Among the three-deletion types, the relevant nontrivial cases are

$$
D=\{(0,3),(0,4),(1,3)\},\qquad
D=\{(0,3),(1,3),(4,5)\}.
$$

These are zero-based cases $1$ and $9$; in both, remainder vertices $2\in S$ and $3\in T$ are twins. The other cross-mark twin case already contains an independent four-set in the remainder and is excluded by $\alpha(G)\le3$. I regenerated cases $1$ and $9$ with row permutations restricted to preserve $S$ and $T$, retaining the column ordering and matching condition. Both returned UNSAT within the probe limit. Thus the targeted checks support the interface conclusion despite this presentation/encoding issue. These were solver replays, not independently DRAT-checked replays. Restrict the released interface symmetry accordingly and retain fresh checked certificates for these two formulas.

The added contribution is meaningfully distinct from the relevant published rStation results. Paper [4] supplies the polynomial library and treats a fixed eleven-core at order twenty; the present criticality-based transfer applies to an arbitrary core with nine outside vertices. Paper [5] concerns the smaller-core branches at order twenty, while [9] treats different seventeen-core extensions. The separator mechanism is appropriately credited to [6]. The new branch exclusions and interface result provide substantive additional scope rather than a cosmetic re-enumeration of those results. Retaining the earlier proof in Section 12 and referring explicitly to its unchanged, available version-specific evidence preserves the accepted contribution.

Two small reproducibility edits remain useful: correct “107 formulas” in the top-level supplement README to $261$, and clarify that the independent-four-set audit uses standard-library enumeration internally but its released entry point imports NetworkX and python-sat for comparison with the discovery domain. Neither changes the mathematical scope or the decision.
