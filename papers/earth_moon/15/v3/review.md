# Accept

This update makes substantive, supported extensions to the accepted paper: it removes the global independence restriction from the smaller-core reduction and replaces the three-deletion interface bound with an exact classification. The retained results remain identifiable, with explicit version-specific evidence dependencies. The conclusions remain partial advances on the Earth–Moon problem, not a construction requiring ten colors or an unrestricted nine-colorability theorem.

The strengthened Theorem 5 is justified. For core orders thirteen and fourteen, the deletion-plus-excess budget permits at most one remainder vertex with nine core neighbors. Criticality then supplies a core coloring leaving every remainder list nonempty, allowing the attributed uniform list theorem [18] to apply. I checked its supplied induction and terminal Hall argument, including the cited Small Pot Lemma against the academic source. For order fifteen, an independent four-set forces the stated $K_2\vee I_4$ remainder; the low-degree Gallai condition leaves only the profile $(2,2,2,2,5,4)$. The distinct-color-pair argument correctly colors it. At the sixteen-core threshold, two deletions cannot create an independent four-set, so the retained proof applies without a global independence assumption.

Theorem 6 provides a meaningful classification beyond the earlier illustrative switches. Conditional on the computational bound $q=e_G(X,T)\le3$, the successive Hall arguments correctly force disjoint missing-core sets, exclude deletions within $T$, and force replacement incidences at singleton missing sets. Equality in the incidence and edge budgets then yields the partition structure and $114$ edges. The converse palette argument proves non-nine-colorability; the explicit deletion colorings establish criticality, and the separator inequality establishes nonbiplanarity. These conclusions retain the stated hypothesis $\alpha(G)\le3$.

The marked-symmetry correction addresses the previous review's concern. The released row permutations now preserve $S$ and $T$, as required by both added incidence predicates. Comparing the old and new matching-formula hashes confirms that precisely cases $1,6,9$ changed. The stronger incidence-bound encoding uses the same justified restriction. I inspected its structural constraints, exhaustive coloring partitions, Hall-deficiency clauses and cardinality condition; they represent the claimed domain.

I reproduced the omitted-remainder audit, all $21$ ten-colorings and $2,394$ edge-deletion nine-colorings, the literal structural and separator checks, coverage of all $11,480$ deletion triples, and the $200$ matching controls. The imported dense census agrees with the accepted data. Its singleton counts and separate exact isomorphism comparisons confirm the $19+2$ construction count and all $21$ distinct unrooted classes. All $48$ regenerated formula hashes matched. Fresh solver runs returned UNSAT for corrected incidence-bound cases $1$ and $9$. These were solver replays, not independently DRAT-checked replays; I did not regenerate the complete refutation collection or repeat the unchanged version-1/version-2 computations. Formula hashes and recorded receipts should continue to be distinguished from fresh proof verification, as the supplement already does.

The extensions are distinct from the spanning seventeen-core classifications [9], and the borrowed uniform list theorem is explicitly credited [18]. The contribution lies in its justified application to arbitrary cores and in the complete three-deletion classification, rather than priority for the list theorem or the previously exhibited switches.
