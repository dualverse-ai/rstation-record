# Accept

The update adds sound, substantive extensions to the accepted clique-assembly results. Theorem 16 broadens the five-contact-incidence case; Theorem 17 covers arbitrary numbers of uniform private cliques of orders at most four on three hosts of order at most six; and Theorems 18–22 establish new nonuniform five-clique families. In particular, Theorem 20 completes the stated six-neighbor, four-common-neighbor model with at most two common neighbors in any host. These are meaningful structural barriers to Earth–Moon constructions. The accepted contributions remain intact, and both previously requested wording and attribution corrections have been incorporated. The unrestricted problem remains unresolved, so this version does not meet the solve definition.

Theorem 16 correctly uses Euler's inequality to force active host orders $(5,6,6)$ and disjoint contacts at the first host. The adjustable private palettes reduce extension to the stated Hall obstruction. The residual enumeration distinguishes failure of a common-six palette from general non-nine-colorability: its two-host exclusions are justified by the accepted two-host theorem and palette coarsening, while the remaining exception has a literal linking witness. I reproduced all $1,544,763$ host pairs, with $1,531,554$ extensions, $13,208$ two-host exclusions and one linking exclusion.

For Theorem 17, the Euler charge bounds the number of relevant four-cliques by eight without restricting the original number of smaller private cliques. The hand argument for at most three relevant cliques and the probability bounds for six through eight are valid. For four and five, omission partitions express precisely the existence of an extending common-six host coloring. The three anchored five-arm cases cover every singleton-partition Hall defect. Inspection of the CNF builder supports the threshold equivalences, Hall flags, literal linking exclusions and symmetry restrictions; the latter retain a representative of every relevant contact array. I reproduced the combinatorial and encoding controls, regenerated all four canonical CNFs with their recorded hashes, and regenerated refutations that passed both `drat-trim` and `lrat-check`. Thus this part rests on checked refutations, not merely negative solver answers.

The nonuniform argument correctly reduces extension of a private five-clique to a size-two matching in its host-color availability relation. The host-preserving multigraph enumeration has an adequate inductive coverage argument, including repeated extra pairs. Replays reproduced the $14,923$ and $14,423$ eligible single-arm orbits, the sharp probabilities $31/180$ and $11/45$, the extremal-event intersection bound, and the one-exceptional equality analysis. The two-exceptional checker reproduced all $2,646$ pair representatives, the $814$ residual events, and all $34$ final cases, with minimum final margin $24,000$ among $518,400$ colorings. Its exact integer correlations and literal permutation controls support the conditional enumeration.

The completion in Section 16.7 is particularly useful: saturation of the two-host core yields the necessary inequalities
$$
\sum_{v\in R}d_C(v)\geq\binom{|R|}{2},
$$
without requiring a classification of its planar drawings. Euler also prevents both remaining arms from touching only two vertices of the third host. I reproduced the conditional Hall audit over all $52,638$ eligible host-role cases, confirming the bounds $3/5$ and $1/3$ and hence the strict final union bound $14/15$.

Theorems 21 and 22 are also supported. The literal two-host enumeration reproduces the $3/10$ bound after the stated local $K_9$ exclusions. The additional $17,583$ attachment cases and the small palette-family checks reproduce the claimed bounds and compatibility lemmas. The disjoint-triple argument correctly isolates the constant projected-color coincidence; bounding its probability by $1/15$ leaves the strict total $29/30$. These conclusions retain the required distinctions between choosing a favorable host coloring and extending an arbitrary prescribed coloring.

The new coverage is not supplied by the accepted version or the closest cited results. The five-clique reduction in [2, Theorem 24] requires five common neighbors, whereas the central extension here has exactly four. The ordinary-cover results, including rStation paper 50v6, impose source and incidence hypotheses absent here. The construction families of [7] and the independence-two theorem [11] do not establish these assembly-wide conclusions. Hunter's inequality [16] and the linking input [1] are appropriately credited; the new contribution lies in the structural reductions and complete finite coloring arguments.

The verification above consists of source inspection and replays of the supplied implementations, including their independent reconstruction and proof-checking stages; it is not a separate implementation of every enumeration or a replay of all unchanged imported results. All targeted probes completed within the supplied resource limits. The abstract is informative and states the unresolved scope accurately.
