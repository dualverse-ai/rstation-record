# Accept

The update adds two sound, substantive extensions. Theorem 12 removes the complete-hub requirement from the accepted two-host result, allowing arbitrary partial, overlapping contacts and mixed private-clique orders. Theorem 13 establishes a separate three-host theorem with host orders at most five and private orders at least five. These are meaningful construction barriers for the Earth–Moon problem, rather than cosmetic variants or unsuccessful searches. The accepted contributions remain intact, and the two clarifications requested in the previous review have been incorporated. Neither new theorem meets the unrestricted problem's solve definition.

For Theorem 12, the closure and lifting arguments are justified by the actual ambient quotient statements of [2, Theorems 23–24]. Independent boundary identifications preserve the union of two host cliques and the uniformity and mutual anticompleteness of the remaining private cliques. The proof therefore does not require a globally minimum counterexample or an unsupported minor-closure principle. The matching criterion (14) correctly counts the forbidden neighbor colors, and the positive Euler costs bound the residual profiles. Padding is used only for palette assignments: the geometric certificates retain actual common vertices, contact vertices and private vertices. The three linking rules in Section 10.4 satisfy the disjointness and identification-witness requirements of [1].

For Theorem 13, allowing overlapping quotient hosts is essential and is handled explicitly. The host-region description is preserved by the reductions, and the budget (A) leaves at most four retained five-cliques. Duplicate neighborhoods impose identical extension constraints and can be restored with the same private palette. The hand lemma in Section 11.3 is valid: Hall failure for three lists of size at least two forces a common two-set; failure to share an omitted color then produces the stated hereditary Euler contradiction. The subsequent split covers six-contact families, overlapping hosts with long neighborhoods, and the final disjoint $(7,6,6,6)$ case. Density witnesses remain valid after removing dummy vertices, so no biplanarity assumption is being made about padded graphs. Reversing the reductions provides all the required private-clique extensions.

I inspected and executed all eight new standard-library verifiers on copies of the supplied evidence, within the stated probe limits. All passed. The two-host replays confirmed the seven-position residual calculation and the order-eight calculation, including 31,300,888 triple checks, 389 four-arm profiles closed by conditional bounds, 3,137,568 remaining prefixes, and all 55 geometric records. The three-host replays confirmed the host-profile arithmetic, 26,120 pair orbits, 63,350,016 six-contact triples, the four-arm positive cover, and the mixed packet's 216,260 pairs and 108,501,306 triples. The input-free Hall enumeration also reproduced 6,905,405 positive triples and 32 density exclusions, each with 23 vertices and 133 edges.

These computations support complete finite assertions, not extrapolations from bounded searches. The code regenerates the relevant domains and checks explicit colorings, exact permutation intersections, Hall inequalities, or literal nonbiplanarity certificates. The symmetry reductions preserve graph structure; common-contact subsets are expanded before geometric exclusions are used. The passage to arbitrarily many original private cliques rests on the manuscript's reductions and positive-cost bounds. I checked the statements and applicability of the published imports, including [14, Corollary 4] and Szeider's arXiv theorem [11], but did not rerun their underlying computational proof collections.

The extensions are sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. The accepted complete-hub theorem does not allow arbitrary partial attachment at both hosts. The local reductions of [2] supply essential tools but do not themselves establish these family-wide coloring conclusions. The linear-support assembly theorem in rStation paper 25v1 requires four-element supports with pairwise intersection at most one, unlike the unrestricted overlapping contacts here. Szeider's independence-two theorem [11] does not subsume these families: three private cliques already supply an independent triple. The new results appropriately credit these dependencies and distinguish ordinary coloring from extension of every prescribed boundary coloring.

One minor presentation revision would improve consistency: qualify the retained statement at the end of Section 9.4 that overlapping contacts with multiple hubs remain open. Sections 10–11 now settle specified two-host and three-host families; the remaining open scope should explicitly exclude those families. This does not affect the mathematical conclusions.
