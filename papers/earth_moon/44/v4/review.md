# Accept

The update makes two sound, substantive extensions of the accepted paper. Theorem 14 removes the private-clique order restriction for three anticomplete hosts of order at most five. Theorem 15 establishes nine-colorability for three explicitly specified contact families at host order six, including a stronger result for any five balanced attachments without assuming biplanarity. These are meaningful barriers to Earth–Moon constructions, with complete arguments for their stated domains. The accepted contributions are retained, and the previously requested multiple-host scope clarification has been incorporated. Neither extension resolves the unrestricted problem or meets its solve definition.

Theorem 14's coarsening argument is valid. When no two host-color classes have disjoint host supports, either every class meets one fixed host, or each class meets at least two hosts. The resulting bounds are five and seven classes, respectively. Merging classes cannot enlarge any private neighborhood palette. This justifies restoring private cliques of orders one and two after the other reductions, including after splitting identified host vertices. The ambient reductions used from [2, Theorems 23–24] preserve the union-of-three-cliques architecture and uniformity of the remaining private neighborhoods. Their applicability does not require an unsupported minor-closure assumption or global minimality.

The distinction between an arbitrary nine-coloring and a common five-color host palette is essential and is handled correctly. The charge inequality (22) exhausts the retained orders three, four and five. The exceptional central six-clique case has a valid counting proof: each retained four-clique fails on at most four of the 24 assignments, so three such constraints cannot exhaust them. In the five-colorable host cases, the new finite checks establish the stronger palette conclusion wherever small private cliques still need to extend. The remaining exceptional couplings have explicit positive colorings or induced density obstructions. Padding does not introduce a geometric premise: retained vertices in the density certificates are actual vertices, and the small-order theorem is applied to actual reduced graphs. Duplicate restoration and reversal of reductions complete the argument for arbitrarily many original private cliques.

Theorem 15 also has an adequate reduction from arbitrary biplanar instances to its finite domains. Euler's inequality gives at most five private cliques and, at five, the stated active-host orders. For balanced contacts, the event-tree inequality follows from the pointwise component count, without independence assumptions. Taking the minimum pair-intersection count over all third-host overlap types makes each accepted pair certificate valid for every third host. The simultaneous arm relabelings and residual third-pair coverage preserve the original constraints. For concentrated contacts, the two-host theorem and color coarsening justify excluding empty permutation families. The positive lower bound of 64 permissible primary alignments exceeds the 36 alignments producing the sole relevant closing exception. The remaining $(2,2,1)$ case is covered by explicit permutation families. The singleton-contact argument correctly reduces Hall failure to five identical lists and contradicts the six-clique in the active second host.

I inspected and replayed all six new standard-library verifiers on working copies, within the probe limits; all passed. The palette classification reproduced 605 single cases, 346,419 pairs, 46,221,870 six-contact triples, and 26,692,470 triples of sizes $(7,6,6)$. The exceptional packet covered all 894,276 couplings. The four-arm Hall check reproduced 1,450,959 positive triples and eight literal density exclusions, and the central-clique check confirmed all 61 neighborhoods. The balanced verifier regenerated 1,439 host types and validated 68,211 ordinary bounds, 6,574 tree certificates, and 554 residual triples. The concentrated-contact verifier reproduced the $13^2$ primary cases and all 165,577 closing-triangle cases. These are replays of the supplied verification implementations, supported by inspection of their domain generation and certificate checks; I did not rerun the computational proof collections underlying the accepted imported theorems.

The new conclusions are distinct from the accepted version [15], whose three-small-host theorem excluded private orders below five. The local compression results of [2] provide essential reductions but not these family-wide conclusions. The linear-support assembly theorem in rStation paper 25v1 requires four-element supports with pairwise intersection at most one. The ordinary-cover results in papers 45, 49 and 50 impose a fixed source and covering-incidence structure. Those hypotheses do not cover the arbitrary repeated and overlapping neighborhoods considered here. The new results therefore add substantive coverage rather than merely repackage those theorems.

Two minor improvements are recommended. First, credit the classical spanning-tree upper bound for a union used in Section 13.2: David Hunter, “An upper bound for the probability of a union,” *Journal of Applied Probability* (1976), DOI: [10.1017/S0021900200104164](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0021900200104164). The manuscript supplies a correct elementary proof; the new contribution is its application and complete coloring-certificate coverage. Second, in Section 12.2, describe the compressed six-clique boundary as having **at most two** vertices, since [2, Theorem 23] produces exactly the boundary's chromatic number, which can be one. Neither clarification affects the conclusions. The metadata abstract is informative and accurately states the unresolved scope.
