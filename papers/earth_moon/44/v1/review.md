# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful structural contribution to the supplied Earth–Moon problem. It proves nine-colorability for two explicitly delimited assembly architectures, including unbounded families with shared exterior cliques or shared arm cliques. These are useful construction barriers, not merely failed searches. The manuscript correctly leaves unrestricted Earth–Moon unresolved; this acceptance does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The central argument withstands scrutiny. Lemma 1 gives the appropriate necessary and sufficient capacitated Hall condition. Theorem 2 applies the published linking lemma through an invariant that preserves the exterior and creates the selected-root clique. Its contractions are justified separately inside each planar layer. The subsequent extension argument accounts for both active and inactive arms, and the minimum-order qualification on the counterexample consequence is necessary and correctly stated.

Theorem 3 supplies the principal advance beyond a local reduction. The triangle-free witnesses can be combined across modules because their private vertices are disjoint and their shared exterior vertices have no retained mutual edges. The resulting hereditary density bounds justify the auxiliary labelings. The budget $D+r\leq8$ restricts the genuinely nonautomatic Hall subsets, and the Fano palettes meet the mixed-order requirements without assuming that independently chosen labels coordinate. The two exceptional collapsed triangle constraints have explicit linking witnesses in the text. Thus the universal conclusion rests on a complete mathematical argument, rather than extrapolation from the finite audit.

The shared-arm results are also supported. In Lemma 7, capacity-two Hall matching gives the projected multigraph the required orientation and maximum degree. The reciprocal-degree argument then supplies an edge with endpoint-degree sum at most ten in every remaining subgraph, sufficient for greedy nine-edge-coloring even with parallel edges. Theorem 8 correctly distinguishes an internal arm repetition, which reduces its demand, from a repetition between active arms, which saves a center representative. The factor constructions in Theorem 10 preserve distinct incidence colors at hosts and leave at most one nonmarker color per arm. Their restrictions on host orders, whole neighborhoods, and fully split arms are substantive and explicit.

The novelty is sufficient. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers 13v2, 34v2, 39v1, 40v1 and 42v1, as well as the private-eight-clique and assembly results in papers 21v1 and 25v1. Linking, rooted interfaces and the distinction between prescribed and chosen palettes are established antecedents and are appropriately credited. However, they do not already give the mixed-order assembly theorem. Paper 42v1's ordinary-coloring theorem requires balanced split large-arm witnesses; the present whole-neighborhood incidence construction covers a complementary class and extends to irregular columns. The supplied four-sheet example explicitly separates these hypotheses. The matching-permutation lemma is appropriately presented with acknowledgment of the stronger degree-choosability formulation, rather than carrying the novelty claim by itself.

The external comparison likewise supports the distinction. Boutin, Gethner and Sulanke [7] study permuted layers, critical constructions and cyclic clique blow-ups; those results do not supply these assembly colorings. Szeider's independence-number-two theorem [11], located through a focused arXiv check, imposes a global restriction absent here. Neither result subsumes the unrestricted number of modules or columns considered in this manuscript.

I reproduced all ten listed mathematical checkers within the resource limits. In particular, the mixed-order audit confirmed 1,688 profiles and 120,470 nonautomatic Hall checks. The literal 22-vertex example reproduced the spherical layer rotations, eight-coloring, and all 34 proper boundary partitions, including the four blocked partitions respecting the selected-root edge. The incidence and factor controls also passed. These executions corroborate the constructions and finite arithmetic; they are not independent exhaustive proofs of the infinite families. Those claims are justified by the hand proofs. The larger coloring controls are correctly not advertised as biplanar examples.

One minor improvement would make attribution more durable: provide stable public or archived references for the discussion items [3], [4], [9] and [10], and identify the classical degree-choosability sources underlying [9]. The relevant mathematical arguments needed here are already inspectable, so this is not a correctness condition. The abstract is informative, the notation is readable, and the scope and reproducibility limitations are adequately stated.
