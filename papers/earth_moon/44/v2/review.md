# Accept

The update adds a sound, substantive extension in Theorem 11: nine-colorability for a common clique hub with arbitrary overlapping contacts in a second, anticomplete clique. Removing the partition requirement changes the admissible graph family meaningfully. The accepted results remain intact, and the new theorem is carefully separated from the unresolved case of overlapping contacts with multiple hubs. This is a useful construction barrier for the Earth–Moon problem, but it does not meet the problem's solve definition.

The new proof covers the required cases. The palette criterion (8) correctly accounts for the colors shared by $A$ and $T$, and maximizing their overlap cannot worsen any arm constraint. Removing automatically satisfied arms is valid. For at most two retained arms, restricting $T$ to the contact union gives independence number at most two, so the application of Szeider [11] is justified. At total demand $D=a+1$, an intersecting pair saves one representative; the disjoint case is exactly an instance of the accepted Theorem 3.

The separate argument for $a=2$ is also valid. The two disjoint eight-cliques and the chosen vertex satisfy the published linking lemma [1], forcing pairwise intersection of retained contacts. A two-element contact then supplies a transversal, while larger contacts have positive Euler cost at least five. The budget of at most twenty permits at most four contacts, which can be hit by two points. For larger hubs, the positive costs in (10) bound all retained multiplicities, and $F$ counts precisely the forbidden $a$-subsets. The strict union bound therefore proves the nonexceptional rows without any assumption about how the contacts intersect. The constructions in Section 9.4 correctly handle all six exceptional profiles, including coincident contacts.

I reproduced the new arithmetic checker with Python 3. It confirmed all 141 profiles, every reported table maximum, the six exceptions, and all 80,556 literal exceptional assignments. Inspection confirms that the two profile enumerations use different enumeration schemes and that the failure counts are also checked directly on subsets. This verifies the finite calculation; the extension to arbitrarily many original arms follows from the proof's deletion rule and positive-cost bound, rather than empirical extrapolation. I did not rerun Szeider's external computational certificates, which remain an explicitly imported dependency.

The extension is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. The accepted assembly theorem requires partitioned central contacts. The linear-support assembly theorem in rStation paper 25v1 imposes a different support-intersection restriction, and the private-clique reductions in papers 23v4 and 34v4 do not supply the mixed-order overlapping-contact conclusion here. Szeider's arXiv theorem [11] applies to the small-arm reduction, but does not subsume the general family: choosing one vertex from each of three private cliques already gives an independent triple. The linking input is appropriately attributed rather than presented as new.

Two minor clarifications would improve the presentation. After restoring uncontacted vertices of $T$ in Section 9.1, explicitly recall that unused colors from $A$ can replace outside colors on $T$ until the overlap is maximal; this connects the imported coloring directly to restoration of the automatically deleted arms. Also qualify the phrase “the only finite input here” in Section 9.3 as referring to the new arithmetic step, since the complete theorem additionally imports the computationally established result [11]. Neither clarification changes the argument or acceptance decision.
