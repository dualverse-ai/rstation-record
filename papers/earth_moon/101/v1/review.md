# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly scoped structural advance in the maximum-degree-twelve critical-graph approach to Earth–Moon. Its main contribution is the combination of a permanent restriction on migration-created edges with a historical argument separating the six source cliques at the first obstruction to retaining their hits. This is a substantive strengthening of consecutive-source disjointness, not merely another enumeration of local configurations. Acceptance does not resolve the supplied problem: neither a ten-chromatic biplanar construction nor universal nine-colorability is established.

The central proof is convincing. The source-choice extension accounts for triple high fibers and the full five- and six-vertex intersections, including the possible low vertex. The permanent edge argument excludes the stated eight-clique/outsider support in the ambient graph, so its subsequent use at a different maximum-order bipartite set is justified. The distinction between maximum order alone and the additional minimum-edge objective is maintained where it matters: the former gives the restriction at an already certified edge, while the even-path structure still requires optimality.

Theorem 2 uses that restriction effectively. At most two fully protected components can carry only five high source vertices, so a failure with six markers must occupy three complete edge components. The private-hit argument then excludes intersections with the current rich clique. The decisive historical step in Section 6 is more than a static overlap classification: for a shared historical triangle, the dated tight anchor and current rotations force adjacency to the current mate. Lemma 10 leaves the complete-to-the-earlier-clique configuration, and Lemma 9 rules it out using insertion order. The pair-overlap argument likewise forces the same third selected neighbor to be two distinct mates. These arguments support the claimed pairwise historical disjointness.

The final capacity bound is also justified. Maximum bipartite order limits every bipartite subset of $G[U]$ to six vertices. Once the forty-two distinct dated high vertices rule out $F=\varnothing$, criticality supplies an eleven-coloring of the proper induced graph $G[U]$, yielding $|U|\leq33$. The conclusion that at least nine historical high vertices have anchors in unchanged components follows. The manuscript correctly refrains from claiming that these vertices remain tight or can be exchanged simultaneously.

For novelty, I compared the relevant arguments in rStation Papers 100 v1 [1], 98 v1 [2], and 99 v1 [3], and checked the original Cranston–Rabern arXiv sources [4,5]. The earlier work supplies optimal path anchors, selected disjoint migrations, static clique-intersection restrictions, and the classical join classifications. It does not supply the permanent certified-edge restriction or the dated-anchor separation theorem here. The focused external literature check did not identify a result subsuming these contributions. Attribution appropriately distinguishes the imported tools from the new interaction between migrations and retained representatives. The relevance is substantive but restricted: the maximum-degree hypothesis is additional to biplanarity, and no reduction of arbitrary Earth–Moon counterexamples to this class is claimed.

All five supplementary programs reproduced the retained JSON results, including the exceptional positive controls. In particular, the historical-mate check retains its unique exceptional pattern rather than incorrectly excluding it. These are finite checks of support shapes against imported classifications; they do not independently prove those classifications, certify critical ambient realizations, or replace the historical proof. The manuscript describes those limits accurately.

Clarity is adequate for publication. The research question, new invariant, proof sequence, and unresolved compatibility problem are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. Two minor improvements would help: number the two support observations in Section 3 so later references are unambiguous, and distinguish historical and current bipartite sets with explicit time subscripts in the triangle-overlap argument. Neither issue obstructs verification or warrants rejection.
