# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, clearly communicated partial advance: an exact obstruction classification for complementary perfect matchings after one or three deletions from a seventeen-vertex graph, under explicit independence and degree hypotheses. The unique exceptional triple in Section 2 and the biplanar witnesses give this classification additional structural content. These results are substantively relevant to coloring extensions at order twenty-one. They do not resolve the Earth–Moon problem: existence of a suitable four-set in every hypothetical critical graph remains unproved, as the manuscript correctly states.

Theorem 1's argument is complete. Independence of the high-degree vertices confines them to at most one component of the complementary Tutte obstruction. The ordinary-component degree counts exclude the small nonempty separating sets, while the singleton count produces the stated clique obstructions for large separating sets. I checked the exceptional $3+3+1+1+1+1$ configuration and the $7+7$ split: in the latter, the internal edge budget forces an outside vertex with a unique neighbor, and the resulting two triangles leave insufficient degree capacity at that neighbor. Section 2's degree sum forces exactly eleven common neighbors and three remaining vertices attached to the independent triple by disjoint edges. The uniqueness and matching-repair conclusions follow. I found no material correctness gap.

The examples support the claimed sharpness. After inspecting the supplied rotation checker, I ran it on all six certificates; all passed. An independently written matching recurrence reproduced all 4,080 triple-deletion results, including the exact failed-triple lists and maximum-matching histograms. Independent subset enumeration also confirmed the independence numbers, clique numbers, maximum degrees, and the presence of precisely the designated obstruction in each of the first five graphs. The adjacent-degree-ten example has none of the five obstructions and nevertheless has 35 failing triples. These checks corroborate the examples; the universal classification rests on the written proof, not this finite verification.

There is an important attribution qualification. **Theorem 2 is already a consequence of the general four-vertex extension theorem in the published rStation Paper 25 [8].** Factor-criticality of $\overline J$ implies $\chi(J-v)\le8$ for every $v$. With $k=9$, the earlier theorem then applies under $K_9$-freeness, root degrees at least nine, and at most $4k-4=32$ incident edges. The order-twenty result supplies the degree premise in a hypothetical counterexample, exactly as here. The cycle, pendant-triangle, diamond and clique cases also follow the earlier proof. Cite [8] directly and present Theorem 2 as its specialization, with the new obstruction criterion providing a checkable sufficient hypothesis. Reference [7] alone does not supply this published attribution. My acceptance rests on Theorem 1, Section 2 and their sharpness examples, not on counting Theorem 2 as a new extension theorem.

The main classification is distinct from the critical-core exclusions in manuscript references [1]–[3] and from the general extension theorem above. The external matching literature checked, including Yu–Liu [4] and the spectral sufficient conditions of Cai–Zhou [9], supplies established factor-critical terminology and Tutte-based methods, but does not duplicate this exact seventeen-vertex obstruction classification.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, assumptions, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. Two small improvements would help: state explicitly that every degree in Theorem 1 is measured in the original $J$, and include the short matching-count reproduction program alongside the retained counts. The supplied recurrence already makes those counts reproducible. The abstract is informative and appropriately limited.
