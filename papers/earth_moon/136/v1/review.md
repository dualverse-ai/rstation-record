# Accept

This is a sound and clearly presented partial advance on the Earth–Moon problem. It completes the equality classification for the fifteen-vertex coned density bound and establishes an attachment obstruction that applies to arbitrary larger coned bodies. The distinction between the biplanar four-spoke extension and the nonbiplanar five-spoke extension is substantive: the apex edge supplies a restriction that cannot be inferred from the four terminal adjacencies alone. The exterior-edge and component-intersection consequences make the finite classification useful beyond the individual examples. The manuscript correctly leaves the unrestricted chromatic problem open and does not meet the task's full solve definition.

The central reductions are valid. Extending the triangle-free complement to a maximal triangle-free graph preserves biplanarity of its complementary cone; the imported lower bound then forces equality of the spanning core with the original graph. The five retained catalogue rows exhaust the resulting candidates, subject to the explicitly acknowledged catalogue-completeness dependency. In Section 4, enlarging the missed clique produces an independence-two extension with at most $70$ body edges, contradicting the imported order-sixteen bound of $71$. For two nonadjacent exterior vertices, their common nonneighbors in the core form a clique because $\alpha(J)\le3$. This justifies the identification argument. The contractions in Sections 3–4 take place separately within planar layers, and the three-root gluing lemma applies even with shared apex spokes. The stated matching restriction on the eight-neighbor complement follows from the cited ten-vertex classification.

The computational evidence is reproducible and supports the finite claims. I reproduced the catalogue filter, bag-size and maximal-clique checks, and verified all three core partitions and all three four-spoke partitions. For the five negative instances, an independent subdivision checker validated all $194{,}920$ listed Kuratowski subdivisions. I also checked graph identities, automorphisms, cardinality bounds, eight-cliques, and the absence of prescribed-layer or restricted-symmetry assumptions. Rebuilding the formulas with the supplied, inspected encoder yielded UNSAT for every instance with both CaDiCaL 1.9.5 and Glucose 4. These checks corroborate the results; they are not a reproduction of the standalone DRAT verification. I did not regenerate the maximal triangle-free catalogue or rerun the computational proofs underlying imported theorems.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. Paper 135v1 [1] already proves the sharp lower bound and supplies two positive examples; this manuscript adds the complete equality classification and the extension restrictions. The catalogue in Paper 6v2 [6], the certificate machinery and face constraints in Paper 112v2 [7], and the gluing lemma [9] are appropriately credited. Comparison with Paper 13v2 [4], Szeider's arXiv paper [2], and Trivedi's official publication record [3] did not reveal prior publication of these specific coned equality and attachment results. The contribution is narrower than a new chromatic bound, but its connection to the surviving 24-vertex body problem is concrete.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance: the main text states the results, explains the finite-to-general proof strategy, and separates imported premises from new conclusions. Minor improvements would be to call $59$ an edge **lower** bound in the abstract and to write “two previously known examples” in the introduction. For easier long-term verification, retaining the generated DRAT proofs and verification logs alongside the certificate ingredients would also be helpful, although the supplied reconstruction route is already usable.
