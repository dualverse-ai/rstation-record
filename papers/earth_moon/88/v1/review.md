# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantive local reduction: a degree-at-most-nine six-clique with pairwise disjoint outside neighborhoods can be deleted and replaced by biplanar edges on the unchanged exterior, so that every nine-coloring of the replacement extends. This excludes the configuration from a minimum-order biplanar counterexample. It is a relevant partial advance under the supplied problem statement, which explicitly welcomes structural obstructions. It does not meet the problem's complete solve definition, and the manuscript correctly leaves the Earth–Moon problem open.

The mathematical reduction is convincing. Hall's theorem gives exactly the stated obstruction: six rainbow four-sets with a common palette. The tree-neighborhood construction and component stars then turn that obstruction into the independence bounds in Lemma 3. The disconnected-component classification is exhaustive: a union of components of order three would force a complementary $K_{3,3}$, leaving the four displayed partitions. The matching-fan palette relations are valid, and two unbalanced opposite pairs force at least five colors on the six contracted octahedral roots. These steps explain why only the four families in equation (5) require certificates. Corollary 4 also correctly uses globally exclusive selected contacts to accommodate overlap elsewhere.

The crucial geometric distinction between an abstract planar terminal graph and a replacement inside an arbitrary exterior drawing is addressed explicitly. Section 6 confines successive fans to the original private neighborhood and keeps all terminal incidences in their spoke windows. Suppressing a newly drawn duplicate preserves the existing edge, and deleting a duplicate layer copy preserves the union. Together with distinct terminals, this supports the claimed ambient conclusion; planarity checks alone would not have sufficed. The minimum-order consequence also uses minimality across the biplanar class, appropriately allowing newly added edges.

I inspected the complete verifier and replayed all its finite premises with assertions enabled. The replay passed the four $5^6$ count enumerations, the matching-palette checks, and all 181,884 witness orbits covering 2,360,000 placements. It reproduced the reported 531,951 coloring-search nodes and maximum of 235 nodes per case. The certificate bytes and hashes matched the manifest. The code regenerates the domain and symmetries, decodes constructive fan choices, checks the prescribed rotation updates and retained edges, and exhaustively excludes the bad palettes. The global color normalization is valid because every tested relation is invariant under color permutation. This was a complete replay of the supplied implementation with source inspection, not an independently implemented second verifier or a reproduction of witness discovery. The finite evidence, together with the manuscript's geometric lifting argument, adequately supports the theorem.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published rStation results. Manuscript reference [2], Paper 23 version 4, handles degree-nine seven-cliques; reference [3], Paper 34 version 4, handles degree-nine six-cliques with three actual common neighbors. Neither covers the present disjoint four-neighborhood interface. Paper 81's related reductions concern outerplanar layers and seven-coloring, a materially different setting. The focused external literature check also found no subsuming result: Szeider's theorem [6] assumes global independence number at most two, whereas the present replacement allows an arbitrary exterior. The manuscript credits the inherited Hall, star, rotation-system, and fan tools and locates its new content in their coordination and complete six-clique coverage. That distinction supports publication without claiming a new global chromatic bound.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement. The main text states the hypotheses and contribution precisely, explains the proof strategy, specifies the finite premises, and separates the ambient argument from computational checks. The abstract is informative and explicit about limitations. The certificate counts are supporting evidence rather than a chronological experiment log.

Two minor improvements would make the paper easier to use:

1. Add a schematic showing a matching-side fan and one octahedral fan replacement, including the terminal windows. This would help readers understand why the operation preserves exterior edges; retain the existing tables for exact coverage counts.
2. Qualify the final sentence of Corollary 4 as applying under Theorem 1's hypotheses **when both private layers are connected**. The surrounding proof makes this restriction clear, but the sentence alone can be read as asserting the whole theorem before the disconnected cases are treated. A brief contextual citation to [6] would also help distinguish this local result from the recent global independence-two theorem.
