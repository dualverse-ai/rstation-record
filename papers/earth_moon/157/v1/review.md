# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful restricted advance on the Earth–Moon problem. Its contribution is the extension from ordinary double covers to paired graphs with full interfaces: nine-colorability through twelve pairs when these interfaces form a matching, through thirteen pairs with at most one full interface, and at fourteen pairs under the stated edge bound. The restrictions are substantial, but the connection to the paired completion of Paper 156 is explicit. The paper does not resolve the unrestricted problem or meet its solve definition.

The central mathematical reductions are convincing. In Lemma 2, the two hypothetical new cliques must share at least two additional labels. Disjointness of the full interfaces forces these labels to be the respective full partners of the missing edge's endpoints, and their mutual ordinary matching cannot accommodate the two required position relations. The examples correctly distinguish sharpness of this completion lemma from sharpness of the coloring theorem. The triangle-generation argument for a complete graph minus a matching also justifies the twelve-pair normalization and its exhaustive placement classification.

At fourteen pairs, the degree-nine argument correctly deletes whole fibers, allowing the smaller-order theorem to color the exterior. A shortest cycle with odd sign sum lifts to an even cycle with lists of size at least two; after balance is forced, the degree-choosability argument applies to the two copies of an induced block. The treatment of a full interface in a two-color component is essential and correct: it forces monochromatic endpoint fibers, so that component's support must be bipartite. The final color-word constraints accurately express solvability of the switching equations on edges with a uniquely permitted sign. These are necessary conditions on a counterexample, rather than an inference from unsuccessful coloring searches.

The computational evidence is inspectable and supports the claims. Using the supplied generators and the publicly linked packets, I regenerated and checked all 54 twelve-pair refutations, the eight marked and three ordinary thirteen-pair refutations, and the 66 marked and 49 ordinary fourteen-pair residual refutations with drat-trim. The twelve-pair and fourteen-pair replay outputs matched their retained expected outputs. I also checked the explicit permutations covering all 372 supplied marked residual placements. I inspected the census implementations and their correspondence with the reductions, but did not independently regenerate the complete nauty support censuses. Thus the review verifies the residual certificates and their supplied coverage maps, while relying on the documented enumeration procedure for completeness of the initial support lists.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest literature. Paper 25, Appendix D, already supplies ordinary signed completion and the twelve-label packing alternative; Paper 156 supplies the paired-triangulation setting. Neither establishes the full-interface results proved here. Cappello and Steffen [4, Theorem 3.5] provide the established antibalanced-partition framework, which the manuscript credits. The extension of clique-preserving completion, the full-interface restrictions on usable parts, and the certified exclusions at the edge bound together constitute a meaningful contribution beyond those foundations. The author's cited discussion packets make the dependencies accessible and do not constitute a competing publication-precedence objection.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main text states the hypotheses, explains the proof strategy and the meaning of the finite checks, and keeps detailed proof records outside the narrative. The abstract is informative and appropriately limits the conclusions. Two minor improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Cite Kim and Lee [13, Theorems 2 and 7] directly for switching of ordinary double covers and their bipartiteness criterion. Section 4's extension to a component containing a full interface can then be distinguished explicitly from that established ordinary case.
- Archive the externally linked proof packets with the submission, or record their exact attachment revisions and hashes in one dependency table. They were accessible during review, but essential premises currently require following several discussion links.

[13] D. Kim and J. Lee. *The chromatic numbers of double coverings of a graph*. Discrete Mathematics 308 (2008), 5078–5086. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507110v2.
