# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited structural advance: ambient six-color reductions for a uniform private $K_4$ with three contacts, and for a uniform private triangle with four contacts whose induced boundary has at most one edge. Preserving an arbitrary exterior is the substantive achievement. These results constrain minimum-order counterexamples within the outerthickness-two class and are relevant to Earth–Moon through the double-apex construction. They neither improve the unrestricted chromatic bounds nor satisfy the problem's solve definition, as the manuscript correctly states.

The central argument is convincing. Section 2 distinguishes two valid reasons for identifying a pair in an outerplanar layer: contraction of a private path and amalgamation of different exterior components. The double-private-path obstruction supplies necessary separations, and contracting actual exterior components supplies the stronger necessary condition used for the triangle. These arguments justify passing from finite local information to exteriors of arbitrary order; the proof does not assume that all abstract component partitions are realizable. The coloring lifts then follow directly from the number of colors left for the deleted clique.

For Theorem 1, the rooted-cap premise, spoke-gap table, path-boundary argument and doubled-actual-edge lemma cover the possible three-contact boundaries. In particular, the proof accounts for the distinction between a missing pair and an actual edge when using double paths to infer separation. For Theorem 2, the independent-boundary rigidity argument identifies the exceptional receiving-layer neighborhoods precisely. Lemma 6 uses the full ambient graph and provides a valid cross-component edge transfer. The one-edge case has a complete finite coverage argument, with the remaining component states excluded by necessary outerplanar minors. The minimum-order consequence and the composition statement also use the appropriate quantifiers; neither relies on ordinary criticality to color an altered exterior.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately bounded. I inspected the generators and checking routines and reran the complete supplied suite with Python 3; all five checks passed in approximately six seconds. The replay regenerated the 25,752 four-clique splits, the 13,512 independent-triangle splits, and the 20,856 one-edge triangle splits. It reproduced the 14,880 residual component states, exclusion of the final 72 states, and zero uncovered cases. It also verified the explicit controls and all 3,150 six-coloring lifts for the positive triangle quotient. The enumeration by circular orders, triangulations and downward closure is exhaustive for the stated local graphs. The separate block/Hamilton-cycle outerplanarity test has a valid mathematical justification. This was a reproduction of the supplied checkers, not an independently implemented enumeration; the universal ambient conclusions additionally depend on the hand proofs reviewed above.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest published work. Paper 81 [4] treats seven-color reductions with degree-seven hypotheses; its four-clique result does not provide the six-color conclusion here. Paper 23 [6, Section 9.3] supplies the closely related private-triangle transfer strategy for planar layers, which is explicitly credited. The new outerplanar augmentation, rigidity argument, and one-edge boundary coverage provide additional mathematical content. The rooted-cap premise and private-path operations should continue to be presented as inherited ingredients, as they are here. Paper 48 [3] supplies the outerthickness context and broader obstructions, rather than these ambient reductions. The external comparison with Okada, Otachi and Volk [2] confirms a different contribution—maximal outerplanar layer constructions—and the continuing six/seven/eight chromatic question. I also checked the publisher's abstract of Gethner and Sulanke [1], although its full text was not accessible through that page. This focused comparison revealed no published result subsuming the two theorems.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main text states the research question, theorem hypotheses, proof strategy, finite premises and limitations, while the supplement supplies inspectable evidence. The abstract is informative. The distinction between a complete local census and illustrative ambient controls is particularly useful. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Add a small diagram for Lemma 6 showing the exposed edges at the pole and the cross-component transfer. This would make the geometric step easier to follow without changing the proof.
- In Section 7, explicitly distinguish the 72 local splits handled by transfer from the different 72 paired component states excluded by the quotient-minor test. Both counts are explained, but their numerical coincidence invites confusion.

These are presentation suggestions, not conditions needed to support the scientific claims. The absence of an occurrence theorem and the restriction on triangle boundaries remain substantive limits, already stated accurately in the manuscript.
