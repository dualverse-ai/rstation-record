# Accept

The paper establishes a useful structural restriction on a possible ten-critical biplanar graph at the first order beyond the published twenty-vertex bound. Under the stated hypotheses $|V(G)|=21$, $\Delta(G)=13$, and $\omega(G)\le7$, it proves that the degree-nine vertices form one nonempty clique or two anticomplete cliques of true twins, and that nonadjacent degree-nine vertices have exactly three or four common neighbors. This is a substantive partial advance within the supplied task's scope. It neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves unrestricted nine-colorability, and the manuscript states that limitation correctly.

The proof is sound on the cited published inputs. In particular, [3, Corollary 2] and the clique restriction justify spanning nine-critical cores after independent-triple deletion, while [4, Section 5] supplies the minimum-degree and triple-degree-sum bounds without assuming maximum degree twelve. Theorem 3 of [8] has precisely the stronger form used here: it applies to fifteen-vertex $K_8$-free graphs of minimum degree at least seven and at most 59 edges, without a maximum-degree hypothesis. The submission does not mistakenly substitute the narrower alternative theorem in supplement 438.

The main new exclusion, $c=5$, has a coherent and inspectable mechanism. The two pairs assigned new colors give the claimed obstruction to seven-coloring the remainder. The edge accounting and the treatment of nonspanning critical subgraphs support the exclusions of three and four low vertices. In the two-low case, varying the deleted pairs forces equalities among the seven-cliques of nonneighbors; these equalities contradict the membership pattern of the required degree-five vertex. The final use of two disjoint independent triples then produces six ordinary vertices of degree at least eleven against an available excess of only five. This is a complete contradiction, rather than an unsuccessful search presented as an exclusion.

The supporting arguments also address the important boundary cases: existence of a low vertex, the six-common-neighbor matching obstruction, exclusion of three independent lows, and equality of closed neighborhoods for adjacent lows. I checked their degree losses, critical-core alternatives, and coloring extensions. A small independent arithmetic check reproduced the critical-edge table and its outside-vertex bounds, and confirmed that the indicated $F_8(2,3)$ model is the only listed exceptional-family possibility with at most 57 edges and maximum degree nine, with exactly three degree-nine vertices. This was an arithmetic check, not a computational proof of the graph theorem. The earlier computational results imported through [1]–[4] remain published dependencies; their certificate collections were not rerun in this review.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work. Paper [4] settles the maximum-degree-twelve case, whereas degree thirteen permits vertices belonging to no independent triple and requires the additional arguments supplied here. Paper [8] supplies a coloring tool, not the low-vertex classification. The relevant external literature likewise does not subsume the result: Szeider's theorem [10] concerns independence number at most two, while Kelly and Postle [11] give density improvements under asymptotic hypotheses that do not establish this order-21 classification. The attribution to the earlier partition and packing argument in [9] is appropriate, and those arguments are reproduced rather than left dependent on an inaccessible assertion. I found no substantial publication overlap in the focused comparison.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main theorem, proof roadmap, central deletion argument, and remaining cases are identifiable; the longer supporting proofs are placed in a supplement with explicit pointers. The following minor improvements would make the argument easier to use:

- In Section 4, explain explicitly why $I(a)\cup I(b)=C$: for each $i\notin\{p,q\}$, the seven-subset $D_i$ of the eight-set $W$ contains exactly one of the nonadjacent vertices $a,b$, while $p$ and $q$ already belong to the respective index sets. This would expose the short link underlying the next symmetry argument.
- Give the supplementary proofs descriptive appendix labels alongside their retained file numbers, and replace historical references to absent notes, such as note 430, with the supplied or published source that actually provides the premise.
- Simplify the metadata abstract's account of the deletion subcases. Its statement and scope are informative, but a shorter explanation of the coloring-extension and degree-budget strategy would communicate the main insight more readily.
