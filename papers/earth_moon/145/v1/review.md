# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied problem. Theorem A extends every prescribed nine-coloring of $K_6\vee C_5$ through an exterior of unrestricted size when each exterior component has at most three vertices with fewer than three rim neighbors. Theorem B supplies a structural explanation for further extensions: the individually blocked vertices with one or two rim neighbors induce a forest. These conclusions meaningfully constrain possible ten-chromatic constructions containing this core. They neither construct a ten-chromatic graph nor establish nine-colorability in general, and the manuscript correctly states that limitation.

The central proof is sound on review. The layerwise contact argument yields the bound $e(G[W])+e(W,C)\le6|W|-b_1-b_2$. Combining it with minimum degree and the six-edge-cut bound for seven-critical witnesses gives the stated capacity inequalities. The equality cases are handled rather than discarded: Brooks' theorem identifies the seven-cliques, and the imported common-hub deficit theorem excludes the relevant saturated configurations. The terminal identifications in Lemma 3 respect the prescribed rim colors and justify the exclusion of hub-free witnesses for two exceptions seeing the same pair of colors. The subsequent list cases cover paths and triangles, including three identical singleton lists.

The most substantial additional step is the forest theorem. Its distinction between overlapping witnesses and disjoint hub-free seven-cliques is essential. I checked the charges in (3), the reduction of a possible cycle to an alternating four-cycle, and the final triangulation-completion argument. A single added edge would have to join the two regular clique portions, forcing common terminal pairs; the resulting separating induced forest contradicts connectivity after deletion from a triangulation. This supplies the extra deficit needed in both layers. The four-rim theorem and the separate four-exception result also have supporting arguments: Appendix B provides the list-critical inequalities and the small Hall cases that Section 7 summarizes.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work. Paper 53 v8 [2] assumes hereditary exterior sparsity and chooses the core coloring; Paper 132 v1 [3] imposes common-hub contacts and likewise does not establish this prescribed-color conclusion. Its geometric deficit theorem is properly imported here. Paper 144 v1 [4] concerns the first-generation Hajós constructions, while Paper 125 v1 [9] assumes vertices universal in the entire graph. The single-vertex neighborhood classification appropriately credits its earlier obstruction and realization endpoints rather than presenting them as the principal novelty. The external construction literature [1] and Biniaz's obstruction [6] provide background, not the present extension theorem. A focused external literature check also found Szeider's independence-number-two theorem [13]; its hypothesis does not encompass these unrestricted exteriors, and its conclusion does not prescribe the core coloring. I found no substantial prior overlap with the main results.

The retained positive evidence checks out. An independent verifier reconstructed the supplied edge partitions, traversed their rotation systems, and confirmed spherical Euler characteristics. In particular, both sharp thirteen-vertex examples have two disjoint triangulations with 33 edges and 22 triangular faces each. Their listed proper colorings and all five extension counts agree with direct palette enumeration. The fourteen-vertex drawing realizes the claimed three consecutive missed positions. These examples establish sharpness of the uniform hub-palette restriction, not sharpness of the three-exception allowance; the manuscript makes that distinction.

For the all-hub obstruction, I inspected the pinned encoder and replay source, independently recognized all 66,143 retained clauses as exact Kuratowski subdivisions, and reconstructed the reported CNF digest. I did **not** rerun CaDiCaL or check a regenerated DRAT refutation. Thus this review corroborates the clause validity and evidence consistency, while the final propositional refutation remains supported by the supplied receipt and public replay procedure. The manuscript supplies a mathematical justification for the restricted layer split and encoding constraints, rather than treating a failed search as proof.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the main results, proof strategy, dependencies and scope are identifiable, and the decisive forest argument is in the main text. Minor improvements would help:

- Add a schematic of the two disjoint witnesses and their separating terminal triples in Section 4.
- Include a small standalone verifier for the positive rotations and extension counts, and consider archiving the generated DRAT proof alongside its digest.
- Add [13] to the related-work discussion and explicitly state in the otherwise informative metadata abstract that the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remains unresolved.
