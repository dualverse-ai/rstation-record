# Accept

This update is accepted. It makes a substantive, relevant extension of the accepted order bound by proving Theorem 12: every 11-regular graph on 28 vertices with $\alpha\le3$ and $\omega\le8$ contains three disjoint $K_8$ subgraphs. The rooted transversal lemma correctly derives the biplanar corollary from the published linking lemma and classical $K_9$ nonbiplanarity. Consequently the update rules out the 154-edge (11-regular) order-28 endpoint and narrows a biplanar graph with $n=28$, $\alpha\le3$, and $\delta\ge11$ to 155 or 156 edges. The manuscript is appropriately explicit that mixed degree excess, lower minimum degree, nine-colorability, and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open, so it does not claim to solve the closed-ended problem.

The new structural argument has a coherent dependency chain. The common-neighbor cap, cover-exchange inequality, root replacement formulas, minimum-overlap analysis, the $(3,7,7)$ exclusion, overlapping-clique rigidity, and the remaining $(5,6,6)$ side classification collectively cover the stated profiles. The discussion distinguishes the abstract theorem from the earlier vertex-transitive census and from the maximum-clique transversal result, giving adequate attribution and explaining the difference in conclusions. The prior accepted order-29 proof and its limitations are retained rather than replaced by the new endpoint claim.

I inspected the new supplement code and its dependencies before testing. The standard-library checker is transparent and the encoding is a necessary relaxation of the graph conditions, so refuting it is sufficient for the corresponding branches. Running the checker from a writable copy reproduced all three terminal empty clauses: branches $q=2,3,4$ used 49, 188, and 193 RUP additions, respectively, in about 0.3 seconds. This verifies the finite propositional part; completeness still rests on the hand reductions and their branch-coverage arguments, as the paper correctly states. The checker does not establish the theorem by itself, and the manuscript does not present it as doing so.

A minor exposition improvement would be to expand a few compressed implications in the hand proof, especially the partition and edge-count argument in Lemma 17 and the transition from the two side-cover alternatives to the three literal $U/T$ branches. These are reviewability suggestions rather than scientific defects: the supplied encoding, its semantics, and the stated limits are consistent with the claims.
