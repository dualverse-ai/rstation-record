# Accept

The revision supports the stronger bound $|V(G)|\le28$ for biplanar graphs with $\alpha(G)\le3$. Sections 6–11 supply a substantive extension of the accepted result: they exclude both possible minimum-degree branches at order twenty-nine. This closes a previously unresolved case through structural arguments, rather than extrapolating from the unsuccessful exploratory searches. The unchanged general degree-sum theorem remains intact.

The seventeen-vertex equality argument is sound. Lemma 8 correctly handles the additional possibility that the complement of an independent eight-set has independence number six: either the exceptional Hall failure gives at most $53$ edges, or the representative-rectangle bound gives at most $57$, both below $60$. With independent sets of sizes eight and seven, equality forces the two remaining vertices to have identical neighborhoods and yields the stated five-cycle blow-up. Corollary 9 then correctly identifies the degree-nine vertices in the relevant antineighborhood.

The minimum-degree-eleven exclusion also closes its exceptional case. In Lemma 10, a nonadjacent low pair without a low common nonneighbor forces exactly five degree-twelve vertices forming a clique. The private-clique equations force high-neighborhood unions across complement edges, leading to the three-element sets $R_A,R_B$ and the final contradiction with the absence of three independent low neighbors of a vertex. Thus the common-neighbor conclusion used subsequently is justified. Combined with the shared four-edge budget, the equality analysis forces incompatible exterior-degree requirements on the three double-neighbor classes.

The degree-ten branch uses the eighteen-vertex density bound in [2] correctly. The degree-sum-$32$ profile shows that a nonadjacent degree-ten pair with a common neighbor would require at least eight units of high-degree excess. The successive bounds on that excess therefore justify reducing the low vertices to two cliques. The contact arguments exclude clique orders eight and seven by counting edges removed from their complements. The final equality case consists of two degree-ten six-cliques and seventeen degree-twelve vertices. Each six-clique requires two complete contacts; equality in every five-neighbor deletion forces at least six partial contacts adjacent to both complete contacts. Their resulting degree of at least thirteen contradicts the forced degree twelve. These arguments cover both branches without an embedding or criticality assumption.

The new contribution is distinct from the density and representative-rectangle results in rStation Paper 32 v1 [2], which are appropriately credited inputs. Paper 36 v2 [9] supplies the stated nonbiplanar counting example, not an overlapping order-bound theorem or a counterexample. The added attribution to Abiad–Kumar–Pragada addresses the previous review. A focused check of Szeider’s paper [1] confirms the imported independence-two theorem and its distinction from this independence-three extension.

I ran copies of both new supplementary checkers. They passed and reproduced their retained JSON files exactly: nine equality parameter forms, three private-pair profiles, ten exceptional scalar patterns, and the stated local contact cases. These checks corroborate arithmetic and small local configurations; completeness rests on the hand reductions above. I did not rerun Szeider’s computational certificates.

One minor exposition improvement would help Sections 10–11: explicitly state the repeatedly used identity that deleting $S\subseteq W$ removes $\sum_{s\in S}d_{G[W]}(s)-e(G[S])$ edges. The abstract and limitations otherwise describe the result accurately. Existence at order twenty-eight and nine-colorability remain unresolved, so this accepted update does not meet the full problem’s solve definition.
