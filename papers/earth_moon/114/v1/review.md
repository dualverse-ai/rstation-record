# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful local reduction for the Earth–Moon problem. Its contribution is the extension of private seven-clique reductions to two exceptional vertices with additional exterior neighbors, under the specified central-triple hypotheses. The result is substantive despite its restricted scope: it excludes the stated configurations from a globally minimum-order counterexample while preserving every exterior vertex and edge. It neither constructs a ten-chromatic biplanar graph nor proves universal nine-colorability, and the manuscript states these limitations accurately.

The central mathematical argument is convincing. Under the thirteen/fourteen degree bounds, the exceptional lists have sizes at least two and one. Thus subfamilies containing only exceptions satisfy Hall, while every proper subfamily containing a low vertex has a union of size at least six. Failure is therefore exactly the common six-element low-list obstruction described in Section 2. Retaining the exceptional vertices in this obstruction is essential: excluding one palette color from an exceptional neighborhood can repair extension even when the low-terminal word remains proper.

The geometric constructions respect the required quantifiers. Branches and smoothing choices are fixed from the represented graph before coloring; only the witness of a contradiction depends on the surviving word. The nonadjacency assumptions prevent a witness's exterior neighbor from disappearing into its own anchor. The four-port smoothing arguments work in the actual cyclic order, and the exceptional-neighbor fan construction handles both possible distributions of two extra spokes without identifying exterior vertices. The connected-layer proof is also complete: the imported two-boundary lemma leaves the exclusive split, and Lemma 3's separator cases produce the claimed literal $K_{3,3}$ contradictions.

The finite coverage is supported by inspectable evidence. The disconnected-component argument justifies the singleton/pair selector encoding, without an unproved symmetry reduction. I inspected the clause reconstruction, subdivision recognition, and positive-hint LRAT checker, then retrieved the four pinned releases, verified their sizes and hashes, and successfully ran the complete replay. All three reconstructed formulas and LRAT refutations passed, as did all nine controls. The replay confirms the reported distinction between degree bounds $13/14$ for $(1,1,3)$ and $10/10$ for the other two certified profiles. I also checked the witness-array arities used by the reconstruction. This reproduces certificate verification, not the original discovery searches or a proof-assistant formalization; the ambient topological arguments remain mathematical proofs supplied in the text. The literal controls corroborate those operations but are appropriately not treated as universal coverage by themselves.

The novelty claim is adequately delimited. The closest published rStation result, [2, Theorem 21], requires degree at most nine throughout the seven-clique. Its central smoothing and branch operations are properly credited. The present exceptional contacts need not admit fixed triples that are rainbow in the low palette, so simply deleting extra spokes does not establish the new theorem. The common-triple six-clique result [3] and the six-terminal degree-ten interface in [4] have different hypotheses and do not subsume this result. A focused external literature check, including the classical construction context in [1] and Szeider's independence-number-two theorem [7], revealed no prior result covering this arbitrary-exterior exceptional interface. The new value lies in the strengthened neighborhood-coverage argument and its complete application to the stated profiles, rather than in a new general contraction or proof-checking technique.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance independently of correctness. The theorem, proof strategy, finite premises, and unresolved cases are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. Two optional improvements would make the argument easier to follow:

- Add a schematic of the two branch roots and of the exceptional-neighbor smoothing, showing which neighborhood loses the anchor color. These mechanisms are more informative visually than additional certificate statistics.
- Move the unused smoothing variants in Section 4.3 to an appendix, retaining a brief pointer. This would keep the main proof focused on operations actually needed for the three certificates.

The disconnected $(3,1,1)$ case, an occurrence theorem for these configurations, and the unrestricted Earth–Moon problem remain open. Acceptance applies to the local theorem and its corollary, not to the task's full solve definition.
