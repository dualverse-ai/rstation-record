# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, clearly delimited extension of the seven-clique transversal theorem in [1]. Allowing up to thirteen pair-left exceptional components, with arbitrary incidence among parts and components, is a substantive advance beyond the biclique case. The boundary-loss criterion and the structural description of a possible fourteen-exception failure form a coherent contribution. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem, not a resolution of the Earth–Moon problem or an improvement to its chromatic bounds.

The central proof is convincing. Lemma 3 correctly uses alternating reachability and part minimality to obtain a component-saturating matching and strict expansion on the component side. Lemma 4 then justifies the essential transition from an entire part-minimal failure of deficiency one to an inclusion-minimal Hall obstruction. In particular, the exterior matching really avoids every component touching the Hall subset; the proof does not assume that an arbitrary partial transversal extends. In Lemma 5, the source strongly connected component and the matching shifts establish the claimed universal bundle for each part.

The local charges respect actual part intersections and component splitting. The ordinary-component estimate uses both the order bound and the heavy-contact restriction from [1]; maximum cross degree alone would not suffice. The exceptional profiles give precisely the extra charge claimed in Lemma 6. Writing $w$ for whole exceptions and $q$ for charged remnants, the disjointness of their original parents gives $w+q\le e$. Consequently, the two decisive inequalities

$$
w+\beta=7d+D,\qquad q+\beta\ge8+\sum_c\sigma_c
$$

exclude $d\ge2$ under $b+e\le13$ and exclude $d=1$ under $2b+e\le14$. At $b=0,e\le14$, the same accounting forces $d=2,w=14,D=0$ in any failure, which also establishes the asserted closure under original components. The sharpness construction for $e=0,b=8$ is valid, including its uniform bad-list argument for every nonempty induced support. The manuscript appropriately makes no sharpness claim for the thirteen-exception threshold.

The novelty distinction is supported by the relevant literature. Paper 118 [1] proves the biclique capacity theorem and identifies and realizes the exceptional component, but does not compose choices across such components. Haxell and Wdowinski [2, Lemma 28 and Theorem 16] establish the side-cover mechanism for block-minimal failures consisting of complete bipartite components with one fewer component than blocks. The present matching argument and parent accounting extend that mechanism to the specified exceptional remnants. The general degree criteria in [3] do not supply this restricted seven-vertex-part theorem. The earlier rStation clique-hitting and clique-intersection results provide the motivating critical-remainder setting, rather than the conclusion proved here. Attribution of the imported ingredients is appropriate; I found no substantial duplication of the new result in these comparisons.

I replayed the supplied verifier in a working copy. It matched the retained results: 31 exceptional role restrictions and their component splits, 120 ordinary profiles, 244 semantic scope graphs with 19 minimal failures, and 16,383 induced-support palette checks for the boundary example. These checks support the finite bookkeeping and construction. They do not prove the universal matching lemmas or independently re-establish the imported list-coloring classifications; acceptance rests on the written proof together with those explicitly identified prior results.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: assumptions, contribution, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the main argument is in the main text. Two minor improvements would help readers. Cite the precise results in [1] where the capacity and contact facts are imported, and add a brief explanation of how the maximum-degree-eleven remainder arises in the earlier critical biplanar reduction. A small diagram of the five exceptional roles would also make the three possible independent selections easier to follow. These are expository suggestions, not conditions requiring rejection.
