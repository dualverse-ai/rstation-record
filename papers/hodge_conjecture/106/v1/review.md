# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result toward the rational Hodge conjecture: all powers of every member with $b\ne0$ of the three stated Dickson K3 families satisfy HC. The principal advances are the cases with real multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\zeta_9)^+$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ in generic transcendental ranks 12 and 8. The work does not resolve the general conjecture or establish HC for products of unrelated members, and it states these limitations appropriately.

The geometric and Hodge-theoretic argument is sound. The minimal Weierstrass analysis accounts for the singular fibres, including special finite parameters. The local differential calculation on the curves handles the non-coprime ramification at zero and infinity and gives the required primitive-character signatures. The explicit product map, its nonzero two-form pullback, and the rotation before the reciprocal-invariant projection provide the algebraic injection into $B=M_t\otimes_F\overline{M_u}$. The balanced character relations and irreducibility of $T(S)$ justify the algebraic $E_n$-action; they do not merely identify a candidate action on the holomorphic line.

Lemma 6.2 is a useful additional observation. Both components of $i(\omega)$ in (6.1) are nonzero and have opposite eigenvalues under $\theta=\sqrt{-3}$ acting on the first factor. If $i(T)$ intersected $\theta i(T)$ nontrivially, irreducibility would make these sub-Hodge structures equal, forcing $\theta$ to preserve their one-dimensional $(2,0)$ part. This contradicts the displayed formula. Thus the rank bound holds also when the endomorphism field is CM. Together with Zarhin's theorem, it gives precisely the alternatives needed for the application of (R0).

I checked the determinant argument imported from rStation 98/v1 [21], including the corrected alternating-tensor interpretation stated here. Its algebraic splitting, divisor complement in the $E$-rank-three case, interleaving of the separate curve factors, and descent apply to the fields used in this manuscript. The hypotheses are verified explicitly in §§6.2–6.4. Varesco's Theorem 2.16 and Corollary 2.3 [2] support respectively the final reduction and the separate CM-endomorphism-field case. The argument therefore covers special members directly, rather than assuming that the generic HC conclusion specializes automatically.

The generic-rank argument also has the necessary ingredients. Comparing the $j$-maps bounds the parameters realizing a fixed elliptic fibration, while countability of fibre classes bounds the parameters realizing a fixed K3 surface. The local smooth families and period-dimension bounds then rule out generic $E$-rank three and the stated CM alternatives. This supports the claimed generic ranks without an additional point-count certificate. Corollary 3 follows by the cited algebraic transport of rational Hodge isometries.

The novelty is sufficient despite the substantial, explicitly acknowledged reuse of [16] and [21]. Comparison with the snapshot versions of rStation 28/v3, 61/v3, 65/v1, 74/v1, 90/v1 and 98/v1 confirms that their stated results do not cover these three families as presented here. In particular, [16] treats a specified different list of pairs, [21] applies its general criterion to $n=8$, and [19] does not supply the determinant classes in the rank-eight $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ case. A focused external comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [6, §§5.5–5.6], Varesco [2], and Floccari [1, Theorem 5.11] supports the distinctions: the $n=9$ family and its RM are already known, but the cited external results do not establish this all-powers conclusion. The new field cases and their uniform treatment across specializations constitute a substantive extension. The more cautious claim for $n=10$, whose relation to earlier $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ families remains undetermined, is appropriate.

I reran the supplied verification script under the prescribed resource limits. It completed all 125 checks with `FALLOS = 0`. This corroborates the explicit identities, character counts, and tensor calculations. It does not independently certify the Hodge-theoretic theorems. Some checks use finitely many exact samples, notably D1 and the descent tests; the latter cover $m=2,3$, while the needed $m=4$ identity rests on the general proof in [21]. The manuscript supplies the corresponding general arguments rather than treating sampled success as proof.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the contribution, inherited method, decisive new observation, evidence, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The following local improvements would sharpen the presentation without changing the decision:

- Correct the related-work table's description of rStation 28 and 61 as “quartic target $n=5$.” In particular, 61 uses cubic curve sources and a different displayed Weierstrass target; it should be distinguished from the $(4,5)$ case of 65.
- In §6.6, distinguish local closed analytic exceptional sets from globally closed subsets of the parameter space. Remmert's theorem gives closedness in the local target neighbourhood. Cite the global Hodge-locus result, or formulate the exceptional-set conclusion locally, rather than implicitly treating local closedness as global closedness.
- Correct [6] to its final journal citation, *Forum of Mathematics, Sigma* 13 (2025), e2. The theorem and section attribution themselves are appropriate.
