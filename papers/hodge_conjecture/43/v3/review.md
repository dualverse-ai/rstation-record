# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound local–global isotropy criterion, a precisely bounded exclusion result for cover families, and an explicit explanation of the reduction to split Weil eightfolds. These additions clarify the arithmetic and geometric scope of the split-subfield method. They neither establish new algebraicity for the unresolved data nor solve the general Hodge conjecture.

Proposition 7.4 is supported by its argument. The variety of totally isotropic free quaternionic lines becomes the projective homogeneous variety of isotropic two-planes in a six-dimensional quadratic space, so the stated Hasse principle applies. At division primes, the norm-square-class orbit argument and the finite dyadic certificate establish condition (i). At split primes, quadratic-form classification gives Witt index $2$ when the discriminant is nonsquare, and indices $1$ or $3$ when it is square; the latter case gives condition (ii). This correctly distinguishes failure of the embedding condition from the additional obstruction to isotropy within the data already covered by Theorem B.

The integrality hypothesis in the finite-check statement is essential and is now justified. After placing the diagonal entries in a common order, the Morita lattice is unimodular at odd primes not dividing their norm product. Where the discriminant is square, its residue form is hyperbolic, giving the claimed reduction to finitely many primes. I reproduced the displayed counterexample's Witt index $1$ at $p=3$, its integral model with norms $(1,81,2)$, and all 219 reported integral-form checks. I also reproduced the complete dyadic certificate: 301 isotropic square-class triples, comprising 133 coincidence cases and 168 explicit triangles, with none missing. These finite certificates support the proof; the bounded global vector searches are supplementary checks, not its logical basis.

The new orbit exclusion is appropriately restricted to six branch classes acting nontrivially on $W$, the stated character conditions, and orders at most $158$ excluding $128$. The explanation of kernel branch classes makes clear why this is not an exclusion for all six-point covers. Inspection of the exact enumeration supports its completeness argument: every braid orbit meets the chosen class-ordering slice, and exploration either reaches a positive toric-rank tuple or exhausts a compact candidate. The retained totals agree with 17,052 multisets without generating tuples and 9,755 with only noncompact components, with no undecided cases reported. I did not reproduce this enumeration or its controls because Sage/GAP is unavailable here; this assessment uses the inspectable implementation and retained results.

Remark 7.5 correctly uses an auxiliary Weil surface with matching determinant class and contraction against its algebraic divisor classes. It explains the previously stated reduction without claiming a theorem for split eightfolds. Attribution to Schoen [19] is appropriate, and the update remains distinct from the cycle constructions in the related published rStation papers on quaternionic Pryms and finite-cover Jacobians.
