# Octahedral Jacobians and Hodge classes on powers of Gaussian abelian varieties

## 1. The Hodge classes and the curves

Let $K=\mathbb Q(i)$, and let $E$ be a complex elliptic curve with multiplication by $K$. A Gaussian abelian variety here is a complex abelian variety $B$ with an action of $K$ up to isogeny and a polarization whose Rosati involution induces conjugation on $K$. Use the associated rational Hermitian form $h$, orienting the embedding so the signature is $(m-1,1)$ for $m=\dim B$. In this signature write $\delta_+(h)=[-\det h]$ in $\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}/N_{K/\mathbb Q}K^\times$. This convention records a positive representative in both even dimensions below.

**Theorem 1.** Every rational Hodge class on $B^a\times E^b$ is a rational linear combination of algebraic cycle classes for every $a,b\geq0$ in the following cases.

1. $2\leq m\leq7$, for every $\delta_+(h)$.
2. $m=8$ and $\delta_+(h)=[1]$.

Both assertions hold at each individual period, including decomposable abelian varieties and points with additional endomorphisms. The elliptic case follows from divisor classes.

**Theorem 2.** Every $B$ in Theorem 1 is an isogeny factor of the Jacobian of a smooth curve of genus $67$ admitting a map of degree $24$ to the projective line. For $m=6$ and $\delta_+(h)=[3]$ one can take a curve of genus $45$ and the same degree. The corresponding Hurwitz period differentials have ranks seven and five, respectively, at every smooth member of their specified components.

Schoen constructed determinant classes using cyclic covers and used elliptic stabilization to pass between Hermitian data [1, 2]. The Gaussian fivefold of signature $(4,1)$ has an all-period geometric construction [3]. Self-powers in dimension at most five with imaginary quadratic multiplication and self-powers for the hyperbolic sixfold of signature $(3,3)$ are treated in [4]. The finite-cover determinant theorem [5] builds on the faithful cyclic determinant construction [6]. Its refinement by inertia orders and its nonsplit Gaussian Weil sixfold application are in [7]. The group $G_8$ and its parabolic discriminants for balanced signatures appear explicitly in [8, Theorem 2.4]. Here two unbalanced components for this group dominate a five-ball and a seven-ball. Their everywhere-surjective period differentials and the two curve genera give the realizations in Theorem 2. We use the determinant theorems to produce cycles on their abelian factors. The proof at periods with extra Hodge tensors is given in Section 5.

## 2. The two Hurwitz components

Use the standard positive Hermitian form on $V=K^2$. Put

$$
 S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad G=\langle S,T\rangle .
$$

On the projective sphere the generators rotate by quarter-turns about perpendicular axes. These rotations generate the octahedral group of order $24$. The determinant of every group element belongs to $\mu_4$. A scalar $zI$ therefore has $z^8=1$ and $z\in K$, so $z\in\mu_4$. The element $(ST)^3=iI$ realizes all four scalars by its powers. In particular $|G|=96$. The two eigendirections of $S$ are both moved by $T$. The center supplies $K$ in the image of $\mathbb Q[G]$, and the eigenprojectors of $S$ together with the off-diagonal entries of $T$ give the full matrix algebra $M_2(K)$. Its self-adjoint first-coordinate idempotent cuts out an abelian factor of a Jacobian up to isogeny.

On $X=\mathbb P^1$ take the following ordered branch cycles. The first tuple has eight alternating reflections $S,T$, followed by $M=(ST)^{-4}$. The second has four entries $S$, four entries $T$ and four entries $S$. Both tuples generate $G$ and have product one. The element $M$ has characteristic polynomial $x^2+x+1$. Riemann existence gives smooth connected covers $\pi:Y\to X$ as the distinct branch points vary on the respective Hurwitz components. Riemann–Hurwitz gives the following data. The last column denotes the quotient by the subgroup generated by the displayed reflection $S$.

| Branch orders | Hurwitz dimension | Genus of $Y$ | Genus of $Y/\langle S\rangle$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Eight of order four, one of order three | 6 | 225 | 45 |
| Twelve of order four | 9 | 337 | 67 |

For the last column, the coset action has degree $24$. Each reflection fixes four cosets and its square also fixes four. Indeed $S$ and $S^2$ have diagonal centralizers of order $16$ and exactly one member of their respective conjugacy class in $\langle S\rangle$. The reflections $S,T$ are conjugate. These facts also follow by enumerating the matrices in the supplement. A reflection consequently has five further orbits of size four. The element $M$ has eight orbits of size three. The total ramification on the two quotient curves is $136$ and $180$. Pullback and norm identify their rational $H^1$ with the $\langle S\rangle$-invariants in $H^1(Y)$. Since $V^{\langle S\rangle}$ is a line, the indicated matrix factor occurs there with precisely one copy of its multiplicity space.

## 3. The differential on the two balls

Let $D$ be the branch divisor. For a representation $V$ or $V^\vee$, the equivariant function bundle on $X$ has local frames $u^j e_j$ for an inertia eigenvalue $\exp(2\pi i j/e)$, with $x=u^e$ and $0\le j<e$. Its degree is minus the sum of $j/e$ at all the branch points. Indeed the determinant of its pullback to $Y$ maps into a trivial line with vanishing order $\sum j$ over each mark. Orient $V$ to have weights $0,1/4$ at a reflection. At $M$ the weights are $1/3,2/3$ in either orientation. Thus the degrees of the two function bundles $A_0,A_1$ are $-3,-7$ in the first family and $-3,-9$ in the second. There are no global sections because a global function upstairs is constant and the representation is irreducible. The splitting theorem gives $A_0=\mathcal O(-1)\oplus\mathcal O(-2)$.

By finite duality the two equivariant differential bundles are $A=K_X\otimes A_0^\vee$ and $B'=K_X\otimes A_1^\vee$. Their spaces of sections give the holomorphic multiplicities in the conjugate Gaussian eigenspaces. Serre duality gives $H^1=0$ for both. Thus $A=\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(-1)$ has a unique section $s$ up to scalar, nowhere zero as a section of $A$. The degrees of $B'$ are $3,5$, and $h^0(B')=5,7$. This gives the stated signatures on choosing the majority embedding.

The dual ball period derivative multiplies the two holomorphic multiplicities and contracts $V^\vee\otimes V$ into the scalars. It takes values in $H^0(X,Q)$ for $Q=K_X^2(D)$. Indeed invariant deformations of the covering curve have tangent $H^1(X,T_X(-D))$ since $u\partial_u$ descends to $ex\partial_x$. The dual curve period differential is multiplication of differentials. Its invariant restriction contracts the two standard representation factors by their perfect evaluation pairing, up to a nonzero scalar by Schur orthogonality. This is the period derivative on the matrix idempotent factor.

In the reflection frames the two differential exponents upstairs are $(3,2)$ and $(3,0)$, obtained from the function frames and $dx=4u^3du$. Their pairing in the frame $dx^2/x$ is diagonal with entries $(x,1)$ up to units. Write $J$ for the reflection marks where the second coefficient of $s$ vanishes, with $k=|J|$. At such a mark its first coefficient is a unit, so the image ideal of contraction is exactly $(x)$. At the mark of order three both entries of the pairing are units. Away from the branch points evaluation is perfect and $s$ has no zero. The image sheaf of $B'\to Q$ is therefore $Q(-J)$. The kernel is a line of degree $k-2$ or $k-3$ in the two families.

Upstairs $s$ is a pair of holomorphic differentials. After removing their common divisor $Z$ they define an equivariant map $f:Y\to\mathbb P(V^\vee)$. It is nonconstant, since a constant image would be an invariant line. Its degree is $2g(Y)-2-\deg Z$. The two reflection exponents give a common zero of order two over each ordinary reflection mark and three over each mark of $J$. At the mark of order three the possible common order is $\epsilon=0$ or $1$, from the differential exponents $0,1$. Vanishing of a coefficient downstairs raises its order upstairs by at least the inertia order. There are no other common zeros.

The octahedral quotient of $\mathbb P(V^\vee)$ has degree $24$. On taking quotients, the map $f$ descends to a map from $X$ of degree $24\deg(f)/96$. The canonical degrees on $Y$ divided by $96$ are $14/3$ and $7$. Subtracting the common zero contributions gives the two descended degrees

$$
  16-6k-8\epsilon,\qquad 24-6k .
$$

Every reflection mark maps to the image of a point fixed by a projective element of order four. These fixed points are the six vertices of one octahedron, an entire orbit over a single quotient value. The respective degrees are therefore at least eight and twelve. It follows that $k\leq1$ and $k\leq2$, respectively. Both kernel lines have negative degree. The injectivity on global sections proves that the period differentials have ranks five and seven at every smooth cover. In a local marking each component consequently supplies a nonempty analytic open of its ball.

## 4. The Hermitian discriminants

On a sphere $U$ with small open discs removed about the branch points, use the standard Gaussian local system for $V$ or its dual. Its parabolic cohomology is the image of relative in absolute $H^1$. This identifies with the multiplicity on the compact cover, since the finite pushforward extends across a mark by inertia invariants. The parabolic pairing is the cup product followed by the unitary coefficient pairing. Pullback to the cover scales the integral by $96$, and contracting the two orthonormal standard directions gives twice the pairing in a single coordinate. This identifies the Hermitian form obtained by multiplying parabolic cup by a choice of $\pm i$ with the Jacobian form on the multiplicity, up to rational similarity. The Hodge numbers fix the orientation. Similarity preserves its determinant modulo norms in these even ranks.

At an odd inert prime $p$ work over $R=\mathbb Z_p[i]$. The standard lattice is perfect and the matrices and inverses preserve it. The scalar $iI$ shows that common invariants and coinvariants vanish even over the residue field. The absolute $H^1$ is torsion-free by the coefficient sequence modulo $p$, and $H^2(U,\partial U)=0$ by duality with coinvariants. Poincaré–Lefschetz duality gives a perfect pairing between the free relative and absolute $H^1$ modules. Write $P$ for the parabolic image. Its dual in its rational span is its saturation in absolute $H^1$. Indeed that saturation is the annihilator of the relative kernel by rank and compatibility of the cup pairings. Hence $P^\vee/P$ is the torsion in the cokernel of the relative-to-absolute map. The boundary sequence identifies this cokernel with the direct sum of the boundary coinvariants.

The order-four reflection contributes a free line, since its moving eigenvalue differs from one by a unit at $p$. The order-three mark contributes torsion of length $v_p(\det(1-M))=v_p(3)$. These are all contributions. A positive rational number is a Gaussian norm exactly when its valuations at primes congruent to $3\bmod4$ are even. Thus $\delta_+=[3]$ in rank six and $[1]$ in rank eight. In the common signed convention $[(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\det h]$ the respective classes are $[3]$ and $[-1]$. Exact Gram determinants in the supplement's chosen bases are $-3$ and $-1/4$.

## 5. Determinant cycles and periods with additional endomorphisms

First consider the Gaussian factor of either covering Jacobian. For a faithful character of an intermediate cyclic cover the determinant of its degree-one eigenspace is supplied by algebraic correspondences from Fermat cohomology, allowing Tate shifts and the point source [6, Lemma 3.4]. One may use the Fermat curve Jacobian of degree any common multiple of the inertia orders [7, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem B]. In particular an unramified character can use a point. The following group calculation allows the quartic Fermat curve in both families.

Let $D_4\subset G$ be the diagonal subgroup of order $16$ and let $\lambda$ be its first-coordinate character. Write $m_3=(ST)^8$, $a=T^2S$, and $H=\langle m_3,a\rangle$. This subgroup has order $24$ with $a^2=iI$ and $am_3a^{-1}=m_3^{-1}$. Let $\theta$ be its character trivial on $m_3$ with $\theta(a)=\exp(2\pi i/8)$. Over a splitting field the character of $V$ satisfies

$$
 \chi_V=\operatorname{Ind}_{D_4}^G\lambda-\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^G\theta .
$$

This finite identity can be verified by forming the $96$ matrices from $S,T$. At each $g$, sum $\lambda(x^{-1}gx)$ over $x$ with $x^{-1}gx\in D_4$ and divide by $16$. Subtract the corresponding $\theta$ sum divided by $24$. The supplement performs these operations exactly for each element and compares with $\operatorname{tr}(g)$. The cyclic intermediate covers $Y/\ker\psi\to Y/L$ for $(L,\psi)=(D_4,\lambda),(H,\theta)$ have inertia obtained by applying $\psi$ to an intersection with a conjugate of the original inertia. The original reflections have order four. At the mark of order three both characters have image of $2$-power order, so that mark gives trivial character inertia. Thus degree four suffices in [7] in either family. In fact the $\theta$ cover is unramified. The projective image of $H$ is a subgroup of order six normalizing a three-cycle in $S_4$. Its involutions are transpositions, whereas the square of a projective quarter-turn is a double transposition. The projective action is faithful on each reflection inertia subgroup, so none of its nonidentity elements belongs to a conjugate of $H$.

Frobenius reciprocity realizes each induced multiplicity by pullback to and projection from the intermediate curve. In the preceding identity the positive representation is isomorphic to the direct sum of $V$ and the negative representation. On the covering Jacobian these multiplicity sums and identifications use group-algebra matrices, hence algebraic correspondences with splitting-field coefficients. Apply top alternation to the positive sum, tensor with the conjugate determinant of the negative term, and contract opposite negative slots by the polarization. This gives the determinant of the $V$-multiplicity with a Tate twist by minus the dimension of the negative multiplicity. Exterior multiplication and the perfect pairing give a nonzero map on the determinant line. This is the integral multiplicity cancellation of [5, Section 8.2]. The degree-four Fermat Jacobian is isogenous to $E^3$. For example its three holomorphic character triples are permutations of $(1,1,2)$, and each conjugate pair gives a Gaussian elliptic factor. Both conjugate determinant lines are therefore supplied from powers of $E$ and Tate twists.

Write $H_B=H^1(B,\mathbb Q)$ and $L=H^1(E,\mathbb Q)$ with the $K$-action for which the majority embedding of $H_B$ is holomorphic on $L$. Write $\bar L$ for the conjugate action. The rational plane

$$
 \mathcal D(H_B)=\det\nolimits_K H_B\otimes_K\bar L^{\otimes_K(m-2)}
$$

in degree-one tensor slots of $B^m\times E^{m-2}$ has type $(m-1,m-1)$. The $K$-tensor and alternation projectors are algebraic by the field action and permutations. For a covering factor, tensor the preceding determinant correspondences with the elliptic slots. Their images span the plane over a splitting field. Taking conjugates and expanding coefficients in a rational basis gives rational correspondence images whose sum contains the rational plane. A rational Tate vector in this sum lifts to a Tate vector of the source by polarizable semisimplicity. On powers of $E$ all such vectors are algebraic, since the elliptic circle has two opposite weights and its pairings are divisor classes. Applying the correspondences algebraizes every rational vector of the plane.

**Pointwise criterion.** For any imaginary quadratic $K$ and signature $(m-1,1)$, algebraicity of this plane suffices for ordinary rational Hodge on $B^a\times E^b$ for all $a,b$. Here is the argument at the specified period. The weight-two Hodge structure $H_B\otimes_K\bar L$ has $h^{2,0}=1$. Its polarization splits off its full Tate summand, leaving a simple structure $T$ of K3 type. Any further simple summand missing the $(2,0)$ line would be Tate by conjugation. This splitting is $K$-stable, and tensoring back by $L(1)$ writes $H_B=H_0\oplus L^{\oplus k}$ up to isogeny. For $s=m-k=1$ all the factors are CM elliptic. For $s\geq2$, contracting the $k$ algebraic elliptic pairs in the full plane supplies $\mathcal D(H_0)$.

By Zarhin's theorem [9] the endomorphism field $F$ of $T$ is CM, since it contains $K$, and its Hodge group is the restriction of scalars of the unitary group of its $F$-Hermitian form. Write $e=[F:K]$ and $d=\dim_F T$, so $ed=s$. The joint Hodge group of $H_0,L$ surjects onto that of $T$. Its kernel acts on $H_0$ through elliptic scalars because it fixes $H_0\otimes_K\bar L$. Its derived action on the blocks $W_1,\ldots,W_e$ over the majority embedding is consequently the product of the standard $\mathrm{SL}_d$ actions, with the conjugate blocks dual. The $F$-action on $H_0$ is algebraic since it is a weight-one Hodge action on an abelian variety.

Mixed tensor invariants for each $\mathrm{SL}_d$ are spanned by pairings, volume tensors and dual volume tensors. This also applies to one-dimensional blocks. Let $a_j$ be the excess of volumes over dual volumes in block $j$ and let $b$ be the net elliptic weight. Each such term has a central torus weight. The Hodge circle has weight $d-2$ on the determinant at its unique exceptional block and $d$ at every other block. Its Galois conjugates over $K$ move the exceptional block to every $j$, since they act transitively on the embeddings of $F$ over $K$. An invariant weight must satisfy

$$
 d\sum_{i=1}^e a_i-2a_j+b=0\qquad(1\leq j\leq e).
$$

All $a_j$ are therefore equal to one integer $a$, with $b=-(s-2)a$. Pair opposite volumes by the determinant expansion of evaluation pairings. One set of remaining volumes, one from each block, is a placement of the full $K$-determinant of $H_0$. Its accompanying $s-2$ elliptic factors give exactly the plane already algebraized. The conjugate placement handles negative excess. To select a specific ordering of the block volumes, apply the $F$-embedding idempotents in the degree-one slots of the alternating tensor. These idempotents and the blockwise pairings come from abelian endomorphisms and divisors with splitting-field coefficients. This proves that every invariant belongs to the scalar extension of the rational span of cycle classes, and rational vectors descend to that span. Cup products and the exterior algebra description of abelian cohomology give the criterion in every degree on every indicated product.

## 6. Isogeny transport and elliptic stabilization

Landherr's classification identifies rational Hermitian spaces over $K$ by dimension, determinant modulo norms and archimedean signature [10]. For each of the two computed data, Section 3 gives an open subset of the marked ball consisting of periods of the constructed Jacobian factors. The rational unitary group is dense in its real points near the identity by the Cayley transform and rational approximation of skew-Hermitian matrices. Its closure contains the real identity component, which is transitive on the ball. Hence the rational orbit of any specified period meets the open. This gives a rational degree-one Hodge isomorphism, and therefore an isogeny, to a constructed factor. The determinant cycles and the Jacobian factorization carry across this isogeny.

For $2\leq m\leq7$, add $8-m$ copies of the definite Gaussian elliptic line to $H_B$. In a Gaussian basis let the original Hermitian determinant be $d_B<0$. Give one new line coefficient $1/(-d_B)$ and the others coefficient one. These positive rational choices give rational polarizations, integral after clearing denominators. The resulting datum has signature $(7,1)$ and $\delta_+=[1]$. A common rational scaling preserves this class in rank eight. Apply the rank-eight result to $B\times E^{8-m}$. Pullback by the projection and restriction along the inclusion at the elliptic origins recover rational cycles on every $B^a\times E^b$. The successive isogeny factors give the genus bound in Theorem 2.

For balanced signatures there are other geometric sources. Koike's cyclic quartic Prym construction supplies split Gaussian sixfold Weil classes [11], and Markman's secant sheaves treat split balanced sixfold Weil classes over imaginary quadratic fields [12]. Theorems 1 and 2 concern the signature with one negative direction and powers of a fixed $B$ with its Gaussian elliptic companion. Higher coniveau on these powers, products with unrelated special factors, and the Hodge conjecture for general smooth projective varieties require further arguments.
