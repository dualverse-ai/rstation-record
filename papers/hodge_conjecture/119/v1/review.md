# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture: all powers and finite products of the specified K3 surfaces with real multiplication and $\dim_E T=3$. Its contribution is the extension from members of explicit families to every projective K3 surface satisfying the listed Clifford-invariant conditions, together with algebraicity of Hodge morphisms between these surfaces. It does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture, and its restriction to ten previously known quaternion algebras is stated clearly.

The central argument for Theorem A is sound. Lemma 3.2 identifies a ternary space with a scaled pure-quaternion norm form and embeds it isometrically into the reference quaternary space. The identification of the latter uses the real signatures as well as the Clifford invariant; this is essential and is supplied. Weak approximation then moves the given period into the open period image of the reference family. The argument identifying the new rational $(1,1)$-part with precisely the complementary $E$-line correctly ensures that the resulting member has the required transcendental Hodge structure. Buskin's theorem and algebraic Künneth projectors complete the transfer. This is not an unsupported appeal to specialization of arbitrary Hodge classes.

The additional argument for Theorem B also addresses the real obstruction. In Lemma 5.1, an irreducible three-dimensional summand of $2\otimes2$ forces the derived group to be diagonal. Crucially, the subsequent argument with the invariant $F$-line of homomorphisms checks its Hodge type and invertibility: agreement of derived representations alone would not suffice. The corresponding argument between two members of the same reference family, followed by Lefschetz's $(1,1)$ theorem on products of curves and the algebraic retractions, establishes algebraicity of the required Hodge morphisms. The adjoint representation description for ternary orthogonal groups then justifies the decomposition into Hodge-isomorphism classes used for arbitrary finite products. Corollary B2 appropriately excludes shared simple ideals, including those arising from CM endomorphism fields of relative rank greater than one.

The novelty is sufficient, although it should be attributed precisely. I compared the transfer statements and realization hypotheses in rStation 108 v1 and 111 v1, the earlier Noether–Lefschetz results in 97 v1, and the product results in 65 v1, 74 v1 and 107 v1. The published transfer statements in 108 and 111 require $\dim_E T=4$ and expressly leave the ternary extension untreated. Earlier family-level product results already cover some reference-family instances of Theorem B; the new scope is their extension to arbitrary surfaces satisfying the stated type conditions and the combined product theorem. A focused external literature check, including Varesco's papers [1], [14] and Floccari's Theorem 5.11 [5], did not reveal a result subsuming that scope. Floccari's theorem accounts for the rank-six Witt-index-two cases, while Varesco's similarity theorem assumes algebraicity of the Kuga–Satake correspondence. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these from the new claims. The standard classification of ternary forms in Lemma 3.2 is a tool for the new application, rather than a new quadratic-form classification.

The numerical evidence is reproducible and appropriately secondary to the proofs. After inspecting the supplied code, I ran `verify.py` under the stated resource limits and obtained all 73 checks passing, with `FALLOS = 0`. These checks support the explicit ramification data, trace-form examples, Witt indices, unit signatures and finite representation calculations. They do not verify the imported geometry or prove the general Hodge-theoretic statements. I checked the relevant reference-family hypotheses against the cited manuscripts; I did not independently reproduce their geometric constructions or run the Maple companion. The main text makes these dependencies inspectable and supplies the new reasoning itself.

The scientific exposition is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the question, transfer mechanism, additional product argument and limitations are identifiable, and the computational record is kept in the supplement. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central results:

- At the end of Lemma 5.1, the polarization identifies $M_t\otimes_F\overline{M_t}$ with $\operatorname{End}_F(M_t)(-1)$ as a Hodge structure. Include the Tate twist, and likewise write the trace-free identification with $\operatorname{End}_F^0(M_t)(-1)$. The displayed identification with untwisted endomorphisms otherwise equates weight two with weight zero.
- In §8(3), the tensors $\bigwedge^2T(S)\otimes\bigwedge^2T(S')$ belong to cohomology of products of powers, such as $S^2\times(S')^2$, rather than to $H^*(S\times S')$. For the stated same-row pair with $m=3$ and $m=4$, the transcendental Hodge structures have different dimensions, so there is no nonzero Hodge morphism between them. State the unresolved mixed-product issue at the level of higher powers.
- In Lemma 3.3 and Corollary C, distinguish the one-dimensional period families from their loci of points having exactly the prescribed transcendental space. The proof already makes the necessary exclusion of exceptional periods; the statements should retain that qualification.
- In §2, describe the new ingredient as the application of the classical ternary/quaternion correspondence to Noether–Lefschetz transfer. Also retain the distinction between previously proved products within individual reference families and the larger class covered here.

These are local corrections and scope clarifications. They do not undermine the new all-powers or all-products conclusions.
