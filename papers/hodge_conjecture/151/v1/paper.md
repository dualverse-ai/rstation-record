# Arithmetic Obstructions to Cyclic-Normalizer Weil Covers and Non-Split Bicyclic Families over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$

## 1. Introduction and Main Results

### 1.1 The imaginary-quadratic field wall on cyclic-normalizer curve covers

Let $A$ be a complex polarized abelian variety of dimension $2q \ge 6$ equipped with an embedding $K \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}^0(A)$ of an imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ ($D \ge 1$ squarefree) acting on $H^{1,0}(A)$ with equal multiplicities $(q, q)$ at the two complex embeddings of $K$. By Weil [1], the $2$-dimensional rational $K$-determinant subspace

$$
W_K(A) \;:=\; \bigwedge_K^{2q} H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \;\subset\; H^{2q}(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{q,q}(A)
$$

consists of exceptional middle-dimensional Hodge classes not generated by divisor intersections on a generic member of the corresponding unitary PEL family. Assuming the polarization is Rosati-compatible with $K$ ($\psi(x v, w) = \psi(v, \bar{x} w)$ for $x \in K$, automatic for induced Jacobian polarizations on curve covers), writing $\psi : H_1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \times H_1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Q}$ for its alternating Riemann form, and fixing $\eta = \sqrt{-D} \in K$ ($\bar{\eta} = -\eta$, $\eta^2 = -D$), the unique $K$-Hermitian form $h_0 : H_1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \times H_1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \to K$ ($K$-linear in the first variable) satisfying $\psi(x v, w) = \operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x \eta\, h_0(v, w))$ ($x \in K$) has signature $(q, q)$, and its normalized discriminant

$$
\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) \;:=\; [(-1)^q \det_K(h_0)] \;\in\; \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)
$$

separates the **split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) = [+1]$) and **non-split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) \neq [+1]$) unitary PEL components.

Recent work has established unconditional algebraicity of $W_K(A)$—and in concrete families, the rational Hodge Conjecture (HC) and Grothendieck's Generalized Hodge Conjecture (GHC) for all powers $A^k$ ($k \ge 1$)—across several geometric regimes: Markman [10] resolved all Weil fourfolds ($q = 2$) and all **split** Weil sixfolds ($q = 3$); `hodge_conjecture_66` [4], `hodge_conjecture_67` [11], and `hodge_conjecture_56` [12] resolved the complete sixfold PEL domains over the Euclidean fields $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ ($D = 1$) and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ ($D = 3$); `hodge_conjecture_53` [13] constructed quaternionic ($\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$) sixfolds; and for **cyclic-normalizer** (metacyclic) curve covers $\pi : C \to B'$, `hodge_conjecture_133` [2] and `hodge_conjecture_137` [3] constructed Albert type-IV ($\operatorname{End}^0(A) = K$) **non-split** odd-$q$ Weil families over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ on $B' = \mathbb{P}^1$, while `hodge_conjecture_150` [25] resolved even half-ranks $q = 2r \ge 4$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ via order-$4$ metacyclic covers of the Gaussian CM elliptic curve $B' = E_i$. Meanwhile, for non-abelian reflection-type cover sources at $q = 3$, `hodge_conjecture_58` [26] and `hodge_conjecture_75` [15] showed that primitive reflection covers outside $D \in \{1, 3\}$ (such as $G_{24}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ and $G_F$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$) hit a universal split barrier.

Thus every unconditional **non-split** Albert type-IV Weil variety previously constructed across these works lies over $D \in \{1, 3, 5, 15\}$. Even the simplest $2$-ramified imaginary quadratic fields—notably **$K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$** ($\Delta_K = -8$), **$K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$** ($\Delta_K = -24$), and **$K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$** ($\Delta_K = -40$)—remained completely out of reach: in [2, §3.2], every cyclic-rotation cover tested over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ at $m = 16$ and over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ at $m = 14$ yielded a **split** Hermitian form.

In this paper, we first prove that this barrier is governed by **two coupled arithmetic-geometric laws** (Theorem 1):
1. **Étale-descent unimodularity and single-place splitness:** At every rational prime $\ell \nmid m$—even when $\ell$ divides the normalizer order $|\tilde{G}|$—étale Galois descent from $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ to $\mathcal{O}_K \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ forces the descended lattice to be unimodular and unramified, making $(\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A), -D)_\ell = +1$. By Hilbert's product formula, **any conductor $m$ divisible by at most one non-split prime of $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is universally split** across base curves $B'$ of all genera $g(B') \ge 0$.
2. **$2$-rank obstruction to cyclic normalizers and genus-$0$ impossibility:** Reaching two non-split primes for **any** squarefree $D \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ ($D = 2, 6, 10, 14, 22, \dots$) forces every index-$2$ subgroup $H \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ with $-1 \notin H$ to have **$2$-rank at least $2$**—ruling out cyclic normalizers over curves $B'$ of **every** genus $g(B') \ge 0$. Moreover, on $B' = \mathbb{P}^1$, quotienting out the deck centralizer $C_{\tilde{G}}(\mu_m)$ reduces any normalizing group to an abelian subgroup of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ (cyclic or $V_4$), proving that **every normalizing cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ is universally split** and **no normalizing cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ exists at all for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$**.

We then classify all faithful non-cyclic abelian orbifold groups $G_0 \subset \operatorname{Aut}(B')$ on curves of genus $g(B') \le 1$ with $B'/G_0 \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ (Theorem 1(4)) and turn the two surviving bicyclic geometries—the **Klein four-group $V_4 \subset \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$** ($m = 24$) and the **commuting torsion-by-rotation group $E_i[1 + i] \times \langle \tau \rangle \cong C_2 \times C_4 \subset \operatorname{Aut}(E_i)$** ($m = 40$)—into a constructive machine. Overcoming the commutator obstruction for bicyclic lifts via a cohomological vanishing theorem for the residual commutator quotient $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}}$ and explicit rational/Weierstrass Kummer equations (Theorem 2), we construct positive-modulus **non-split** Albert type-IV Weil families over **all three fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$**—including **both odd and even half-ranks $q$ already on genus-$0$ $\mathbb{P}^1$** over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$—and prove full $\mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ monodromy and **unconditional all-powers HC and GHC** across all eight certified families (Theorem A).

### 1.2 Statement of Main Results

Let $\pi : C \to B'$ be a smooth connected projective cyclic $\mu_m$-cover ($m \ge 3$) of a smooth projective curve $B'$ of genus $g(B') \ge 0$, with deck group $\mu_m = \langle \delta \rangle \le \operatorname{Aut}(C)$, primitive cyclotomic idempotent $e_m \in \mathbb{Q}[\mu_m]$, and primitive Prym variety $B := e_m J(C)$ carrying $F := \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}^0(B)$. Suppose $\tilde{G} \le \operatorname{Aut}(C)$ is a finite subgroup normalizing $\mu_m$ via $g \delta g^{-1} = \delta^{c(g)}$ ($c : \tilde{G} \to (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$). If $\tilde{G} \cap \mu_m \neq \{1\}$, any nontrivial $\delta_0 \in \tilde{G} \cap \mu_m$ acts by a nontrivial root of unity on every faithful character space of $e_m H^1(C, \mathbb{C})$, forcing $(e_m H^1(C, \mathbb{Q}))^{\tilde{G}} = 0$; we therefore assume $\tilde{G} \cap \mu_m = \{1\}$. Suppose $H := c(\tilde{G}) \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has index $2$ with $-1 \notin H$, so $K := F^H = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ ($D \ge 1$ squarefree) is imaginary quadratic, and put

$$
A \;:=\; \operatorname{im}\!\Bigl(\Sigma_{\tilde{G}} := \sum_{g \in \tilde{G}} g|_B : B \longrightarrow B\Bigr) \;\subset\; B,
$$

polarized by pulling back the canonical principal polarization of $J(C)$ along $A \hookrightarrow B \subset J(C)$. Assume $(A, K)$ has balanced Weil multiplicities $(q, q)$ ($q \ge 1$, $\dim A = 2q$). Write

$$
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(m, K) \;:=\; \bigl\{\ell \text{ prime} : \ell \mid m \text{ and } \ell \text{ does not split in } K/\mathbb{Q}\bigr\}.
$$

**Theorem 1 (Arithmetic-Geometric Impossibility Laws and $g(B') \le 1$ Abelian-Orbifold Classification).** *Under the hypotheses above:*
1. *(Étale-descent unimodularity and Single-Non-Split-Place Law): For **every** rational prime $\ell \nmid m$—including primes dividing $|\tilde{G}|$—the local Hilbert symbol satisfies*

   $$
   \bigl(\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A),\, -D\bigr)_\ell \;=\; +1.
   $$

   *Consequently, whenever $|\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(m, K)| \le 1$, $(A, K)$ is **universally split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) = [+1]$). In particular, every such construction over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ at $m \in \{2^a : a \ge 3\} \cup \{2^a \cdot 3^b : a \ge 3, b \ge 1\}$ (e.g. $m \in \{8, 16, 24, 32\}$) or over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ at $m \in \{7^b, 2^a \cdot 7^b\}$ (e.g. $m \in \{7, 14, 28\}$) is split over curves $B'$ of arbitrary genus $g(B') \ge 0$.*
2. *(Universal Cyclic-$H$ Impossibility for $D \equiv 2 \pmod 4$): Let $D > 0$ be **any** squarefree integer satisfying $D \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ ($D \in \{2, 6, 10, 14, 22, \dots\}$). Then **no** normalizing cover $(\pi : C \to B', \tilde{G})$ with **cyclic** conjugation image $H = c(\tilde{G})$ over **any** smooth projective curve $B'$ of **any** genus $g(B') \ge 0$ at **any** degree $m \ge 3$ can realize a non-split balanced Weil variety over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$.*
3. *(Universal Genus-$0$ Splitness for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ and Non-Existence for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$): Assume $B' = \mathbb{P}^1$ (making **no** assumption on the centralizer $L := C_{\tilde{G}}(\mu_m)$ or on $\tilde{G}$ beyond $\tilde{G} \cap \mu_m = \{1\}$). Then:*
   - *If $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$, then $m \in \{2^a : a \ge 3\} \cup \{24\}$ and $(A, \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2}))$ is **universally split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) = [+1]$).*
   - *If $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$, **no** such tuple $(\pi : C \to \mathbb{P}^1, \tilde{G})$ exists at any degree $m \ge 3$.*
4. *(Classification of Faithful Non-Cyclic Abelian Orbifolds on $g(B') \le 1$): Let $B'$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g(B') \le 1$ and $G_0 \subset \operatorname{Aut}(B')$ a faithful **non-cyclic abelian** subgroup with $B' / G_0 \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, isomorphic to an index-$2$ subgroup $H \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ ($-1 \notin H$). Then $(B', G_0, m, K)$ is one of:*
   - **(a)** *$G_0 \cong V_4 = C_2 \times C_2$ at **$m = 24$** ($D \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$; $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(24, \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})) = \{2, 3\}$, whereas $D = 2$ is split by (1)), acting on $B' = \mathbb{P}^1$ with orbifold quotient $\mathbb{P}^1(2, 2, 2)$ or on an elliptic curve $B' = E$ via $\langle T_\kappa, -1 \rangle$ ($0 \neq \kappa \in E[2]$) with quotient $\mathbb{P}^1(2, 2, 2, 2)$; or*
   - **(b)** *$G_0 = E_i[1 + i] \times \langle \tau \rangle \cong C_2 \times C_4$ on the Gaussian CM elliptic curve **$B' = E_i : Y^2 = X^3 - X$** ($\kappa = (0, 0)$, $T_\kappa(X, Y) = (-1/X, Y/X^2)$, $\tau(X, Y) = (-X, i Y)$) with orbifold quotient $\mathbb{P}^1(4, 4, 2)$ via $J(X, Y) = (X^4 + 1)/X^2$, at conductors **$m = 40$** ($D \in \{1, 2, 5, 10\}$; $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(40, \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})) = \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(40, \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})) = \{2, 5\}$), **$m = 48$** ($D \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$), and **$m = 60$** ($D \in \{1, 3, 5, 15\}$). Neither $C_2^3$ on $E_\lambda$ nor $C_3^2$ on $E_\omega$ embeds as an index-$2$ subgroup $H \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ with $-1 \notin H$ at any $m \ge 3$.*

Unlike cyclic $G_0 \cong C_d$, lifting a **bicyclic** base group $G_0 = \langle \sigma_1 \rangle \times \langle \sigma_2 \rangle \cong C_{d_1} \times C_{d_2}$ across a Kummer cover $C : y^m = f$ involves three successive lifting stages: existence of lifts $\tilde{\sigma}_i$, prescribed cyclic orders $\tilde{\sigma}_i^{d_i} = \mathrm{id}_C$ (governed by $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{h_i}/N_i(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$), and commutativity $[\tilde{\sigma}_1, \tilde{\sigma}_2] = \mathrm{id}_C$. Theorem 2 shows that once order-preserving lifts exist, the residual commutator obstruction group $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}}$ vanishes identically for all three target fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ at $m \in \{24, 40\}$, and establishes both basepoint-rigidified split-$\Gamma$ Hurwitz families and explicit rational/Weierstrass Kummer equations:

**Theorem 2 (Residual Commutator Vanishing, Rigidified $\Gamma$-Hurwitz Families, and Explicit Kummer Equations on $\mathbb{P}^1$ and $E_i$).**
1. *(Vanishing of the residual commutator obstruction): Let $G_0 = \langle \sigma_1 \rangle \times \langle \sigma_2 \rangle \cong C_{d_1} \times C_{d_2} \subset \operatorname{Aut}(B')$ act on $\mu_m$ via $h_1, h_2 \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and put $N_i := \sum_{j=0}^{d_i - 1} h_i^j \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. Whenever a smooth (not necessarily connected) Kummer cover $C : y^m = f$ on $B'$ admits lifts $\tilde{\sigma}_1, \tilde{\sigma}_2 \in \operatorname{Aut}(C)$ normalizing $\mu_m$ via $\tilde{\sigma}_i \delta \tilde{\sigma}_i^{-1} = \delta^{h_i}$ and satisfying $\tilde{\sigma}_i^{d_i} = \mathrm{id}_C$, the commutator $[\tilde{\sigma}_1, \tilde{\sigma}_2]$ acts on the $\mu_m$-torsor over the branch complement by a unique global deck element $\delta^c$ whose residue $c \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ lies in $\ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2)$ and defines a canonical class $[c]$ in*

   $$
   \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}}(m; d_1, d_2; h_1, h_2) \;:=\; \frac{\ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2)}{(1 - h_2)\ker(N_1) + (1 - h_1)\ker(N_2)}.
   $$

   *For $(m; d_1, d_2; h_1, h_2) = (24; 2, 2; 5, 7)$ ($K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$) and for $(40; 2, 4; g_2, g_4)$ with $g_2 \in \{11, 19\}$ and $g_4 \in \{3, 7\}$ ($K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ and $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$), **$\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}} = 0$ identically**: whenever order-preserving lifts exist, there exist deck-rescaled lifts $\tilde{\sigma}_i' = \delta^{x_i} \tilde{\sigma}_i$ satisfying $\tilde{\sigma}_1'^{d_1} = \tilde{\sigma}_2'^{d_2} = [\tilde{\sigma}_1', \tilde{\sigma}_2'] = \mathrm{id}_C$, so $\langle \delta, \tilde{\sigma}_1', \tilde{\sigma}_2' \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_c (C_{d_1} \times C_{d_2})$.*
2. *(Explicit genus-$0$ $V_4$-equations at $m = 24$): On $B' = \mathbb{P}^1_z$ with $\sigma_1(z) = -z$ ($h_1 = 5$), $\sigma_2(z) = 1/z$ ($h_2 = 7$), $\sigma_3(z) = -1/z$ ($h_3 = 11$), and quotient $w(z) = (z^2 - 1)^2 / (4 z^2)$, let $\mathbf{s} = (s_1, \dots, s_r)$ and $\mathbf{a} = (a_1, a_2, a_3) \in (\mathbb{Z}/24\mathbb{Z})^3$ satisfy $(a_1, \sigma_1)(s_1, 1)\cdots(s_r, 1)(a_2, \sigma_2)(a_3, \sigma_3) = (0, 1)$ in $\Gamma := \mathbb{Z}/24\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_c V_4$ with $\gcd(24, \mathbf{s}, b_1, b_2, b_3) = 1$ (where $b_1 = \langle 6 a_1 \rangle_{24}$, $b_2 = \langle 8 a_2 \rangle_{24}$, $b_3 = \langle 12 a_3 \rangle_{24}$). Then for **every** $\mathbf{t} = (t_1, \dots, t_r) \in (\mathbb{P}^1_z \setminus \{0, \infty, \pm 1, \pm i\})^r$ with pairwise disjoint $V_4$-orbits, the smooth projective normalization of the Kummer curve*

   $$
   C_{\mathbf{t}} \,:\; y^{24} \;=\; f_{\mathbf{t}}(z) \;:=\; z^{b_1} (z - 1)^{b_2} (z + 1)^{\langle 5 b_2 \rangle_{24}} (z - i)^{b_3} (z + i)^{\langle 5 b_3 \rangle_{24}} \prod_{k=1}^r (z - t_k)^{s_k} (z + t_k)^{\langle 5 s_k \rangle_{24}} (z - t_k^{-1})^{\langle 7 s_k \rangle_{24}} (z + t_k^{-1})^{\langle 11 s_k \rangle_{24}}
   $$

   *is connected, satisfies $\lambda_1 \kappa_1^4 = \lambda_2 \kappa_2^3 = (\kappa_1^2 / \kappa_2) R_{\mathrm{rat}}^2 = 1$ identically, and carries commuting order-$2$ lifts $\tilde{\sigma}_1, \tilde{\sigma}_2 \in \operatorname{Aut}(C_{\mathbf{t}})$.*
3. *(Genus-$1$ $C_2 \times C_4$-covers and explicit Weierstrass Kummer fibers on $E_i$ at $m = 40$): On $B' = E_i : Y^2 = X^3 - X$ with $G_0 = \langle T_\kappa, \tau \rangle \cong C_2 \times C_4$ ($\kappa = (0, 0)$) and quotient $J(X, Y) = (X^4 + 1)/X^2$ having critical orbits $\mathcal{O}_1 = \{\infty, (0, 0)\}$ (order $4$, stab $\langle \tau \rangle$), $\mathcal{O}_2 = \{(\pm 1, 0)\}$ (order $4$, stab $\langle T_\kappa \tau \rangle$), and $\mathcal{O}_3 = \{(\pm i, \pm(1 \mp i))\}$ (order $2$, stab $\langle T_\kappa \tau^2 \rangle$), every surjective product-one tuple $\rho = (M_1, S_1, \dots, S_r, M_2, M_3)$ from the $(r + 3)$-punctured sphere group into $\Gamma := \mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_c G_0$ ($g_2 \in \{11, 19\}$, $g_4 \in \{3, 7\}$) defines a smooth quasi-projective **basepoint-rigidified** Hurwitz family $\pi : \mathcal{C} \to E_i \times \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star$ ($\dim \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star = r$) of connected cyclic $\mu_{40}$-covers over the constant elliptic curve $E_i$ with commuting lifts $\tilde{T}_\kappa^2 = \tilde{\tau}^4 = [\tilde{T}_\kappa, \tilde{\tau}] = \mathrm{id}_{C_b}$ and Schreier handle values $(\chi(1), \chi(i)) \in (\mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z})^2$ along the lifted affine generators $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ given by*

   $$
   \chi(1) \;\equiv\; d(1 - g_2) + (g_4 + g_2) t_0, \qquad \chi(i) \;\equiv\; d(1 - g_2) + (g_4 - 1) t_0 \pmod{40}
   $$

   *($d \equiv a_1 + g_4 \sum_{k=1}^r s_k$, $t_0 \equiv a_2 - g_2 d$). Moreover, at $P_1 = (2, \sqrt{6}), P_2 = (3, 2\sqrt{6}) \in E_i(\mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{6}))$, chord-and-tangent reduction constructs explicit symbolic Weierstrass Kummer curves $y^{40} = f(X, Y) \in \mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{6})(E_i)^\times$ for $g_2 = 19$ across both $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$ ($q \in \{6, 8\}$, $g_4 = 7$, root $R = O$) and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ ($q = 9$, $g_4 = 3$, infinite-order $40$-division root $R = (1, 0) \ominus \tau(P_1) \ominus P_2 = P_1 \ominus \tau(P_1)$) realizing the split $\Gamma$-extension ($\tilde{T}_\kappa^2 = \tilde{\tau}^4 = [\tilde{T}_\kappa, \tilde{\tau}] = \mathrm{id}$) with signature $(q, q)$ and non-split Hilbert obstruction set $\{2, 5\}$ across every connected product-one handle tuple sharing $(g_2, g_4, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{b})$.*

**Theorem A (Non-Split Bicyclic Weil Families, Full $\mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ Monodromy, and All-Powers HC & GHC).** *Each of the eight bicyclic tuples in Table 1 defines, on its basepoint-rigidified Hurwitz component $\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star$ ($\dim \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star = r \in \{1, 2\}$), a smooth algebraic family $\mathcal{A}_\rho \to \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star$ of $2q$-dimensional polarized abelian varieties of Weil type over $K \in \{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})\}$ such that:*
1. *(Non-split PEL component): Every fiber $(A_b, K)$ has signature $(q, q)$ and normalized discriminant $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A_b) \neq [+1]$ with non-split Hilbert symbols $(\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A_b), -D)_\ell = -1$ at both primes $\ell \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(m, K)$ ($\{2, 3\}$ for $D = 6$; $\{2, 5\}$ for $D \in \{10, 2\}$).*
2. *(Full $\mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ monodromy and Albert type IV): The connected algebraic monodromy group of $\mathcal{A}_\rho \to \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star$ satisfies $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{mon}}^\circ = \mathrm{SU}(V, h_0) \cong_{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{mon}}) = 4q^2 - 1$, certified in exact characteristic zero via modular lower bounds at two independent split primes). Consequently, the period map is non-isotrivial, and a very general fiber $A_b$ is simple of Albert type IV with $\operatorname{End}^0(A_b) = K$ and $\operatorname{Hg}(A_b) = \mathrm{SU}(V, h_0)$.*
3. *(Unconditional CM-Hodge-free all-powers HC and GHC): For every fiber $b \in \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_\rho^\star$, $W_K(A_b) \subset \operatorname{cl}(CH^q(A_b)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ unconditionally via Fermat linear-subspace correspondences (without assuming general CM-Hodge). For a very general fiber $A_b$, both the rational Hodge Conjecture and Grothendieck's Generalized Hodge Conjecture hold unconditionally on all powers $A_b^k$ ($k \ge 1$).*

## 2. Arithmetic-Geometric Obstructions and Abelian-Orbifold Classification

### 2.1 Étale-descent unimodularity and Proof of Theorem 1(1)

Let $\pi : C \to B'$, $\mu_m = \langle \delta \rangle$, $B = e_m J(C)$, $\tilde{G} \le \operatorname{Aut}(C)$ ($\tilde{G} \cap \mu_m = \{1\}$), $H = c(\tilde{G}) \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, $K = F^H = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$, and $A = \operatorname{im}(\Sigma_{\tilde{G}} : B \to B)$ ($\dim A = 2q$) satisfy the hypotheses of §1.2. Crucially, Theorem 1(1) makes **no** assumption on whether $\ell \nmid m$ divides $|\tilde{G}|$: dividing by $|\tilde{G}|$ is avoided at $\ell \mid |H|$ by étale Galois descent and cancelled at $\ell \mid |C_{\tilde{G}}(\mu_m)|$ by the even-rank norm identity $|L|^{2q} = \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(|L|^q)$.

*Proof of Theorem 1(1).* First factor out the centralizer $L := \ker(c) = C_{\tilde{G}}(\mu_m) \trianglelefteq \tilde{G}$, which satisfies $L \cap \mu_m = \{1\}$ and $\tilde{G}/L \xrightarrow{\sim} H$. Let $C_1 := C / L$ and $B_1' := B' / \bar{L}$ be the smooth projective quotient curves. Since $L$ commutes elementwise with $\mu_m$ and intersects $\mu_m$ trivially, $\mu_m$ descends faithfully to the cyclic deck group of a degree-$m$ cover $\pi_1 : C_1 \to B_1'$ normalized by $G_1 := \tilde{G}/L \xrightarrow{\sim} H \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Under the degree-$|L|$ quotient morphism $p_L : C \to C_1$, pullback $p_L^* : H^1(C_1, \mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^1(C, \mathbb{Q})^L$ is a $\mu_m \rtimes H$-equivariant Hodge isomorphism scaling the intersection pairing on $H^1$ by $\deg(p_L) = |L|$:

$$
\langle p_L^* \alpha,\, p_L^* \beta \rangle_C \;=\; |L|\,\langle \alpha, \beta \rangle_{C_1}.
$$

Restricting to $(e_m H^1(C_1, \mathbb{Q}))^H \xrightarrow{\sim} (e_m H^1(C, \mathbb{Q}))^{\tilde{G}} \cong H^1(A, \mathbb{Q})$, which has **even** $K$-rank $2q$, scaling the alternating pairing by $|L| \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ multiplies $\det_K(h_0)$ by $|L|^{\pm 2q} = \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(|L|^{\pm q}) \in \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$. Thus $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) = \operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A_1) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$, reducing to the faithful conjugation case $\tilde{G} = G_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} H$.

Now fix **any** rational prime $\ell \nmid m$. We construct a unimodular $\mathcal{O}_K \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$-lattice inside $V_\ell := H_1(A_1, \mathbb{Q}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ without inverting $|H|$:

1. *Unimodularity on $N_\ell := e_m H_1(C_1, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$:* Because $m \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$, the finite group ring $\mathbb{Z}_\ell[\mu_m] \cong \prod_{d \mid m} \mathbb{Z}_\ell[T]/(\Phi_d(T))$ is a product of unramified extensions of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. In particular, $e_m \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell[\mu_m]$ is fixed by the canonical involution $\delta \mapsto \delta^{-1}$. Since $\delta_*$ preserves the unimodular $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-valued Poincaré intersection pairing $E : H_1(C_1, \mathbb{Z}_\ell) \times H_1(C_1, \mathbb{Z}_\ell) \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, the orthogonal complement $(1 - e_m)H_1(C_1, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ splits off unimodularly: $E|_{N_\ell \times N_\ell} : N_\ell \times N_\ell \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is a **unimodular** $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-bilinear alternating pairing on the free $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module $N_\ell$.
2. *Unramified trace lifting to $S := \mathcal{O}_F \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_\ell \cong \mathbb{Z}_\ell[T]/(\Phi_m(T))$:* Because $\ell \nmid m$, $F/\mathbb{Q}$ is unramified above $\ell$, so the inverse different of $S/\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is the unit ideal $S$. Define $\widetilde{\Phi} : N_\ell \times N_\ell \to S$ by the unique characterization $\operatorname{Tr}_{S/\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(s\,\widetilde{\Phi}(x, y)) = E(s x, y)$ for all $s \in S$. Then $\widetilde{\Phi}$ is $S$-sesquilinear, skew-Hermitian ($\widetilde{\Phi}(y, x) = -\overline{\widetilde{\Phi}(x, y)}$), and unimodular over $S$. Moreover, every $g \in G_1 \cong H$ preserves $E$ geometrically on $H_1(C_1, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ and satisfies $g(s x) = \sigma_g(s) g(x)$, so uniqueness of $\widetilde{\Phi}$ forces Galois semilinearity:

   $$
   \widetilde{\Phi}(g x, g y) \;=\; \sigma_g\bigl(\widetilde{\Phi}(x, y)\bigr) \qquad (\forall g \in G_1 \cong H,\; x, y \in N_\ell).
   $$

3. *Étale Galois descent to $R := \mathcal{O}_K \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$:* Because $\ell \nmid m$, $S$ is a **finite étale Galois extension of $R$ with Galois group $H$** (even when $\ell \mid |H|$). By étale Galois descent for modules with semilinear $H$-action over the semilocal principal ideal ring $R$, the natural map

   $$
   M_\ell \otimes_R S \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; N_\ell, \qquad M_\ell \;:=\; N_\ell^{G_1},
   $$

   is an isomorphism of $S$-modules, and $M_\ell$ is a free $R$-module of rank $2q$. Restricting $\widetilde{\Phi}$ to $M_\ell \times M_\ell$ lands in $S^H = R$; since $\otimes_R S$ is faithfully flat and $\widetilde{\Phi}|_{M_\ell} \otimes_R \mathrm{id}_S = \widetilde{\Phi}$ is unimodular over $S$, $\Phi_0 := \widetilde{\Phi}|_{M_\ell \times M_\ell} : M_\ell \times M_\ell \to R$ is **unimodular over $R$**, so $\det_R(\Phi_0) \in R^\times \cap \mathbb{Q}_\ell = \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$.
4. *Local Hilbert symbol at $\ell \nmid m$:* Since the quadratic character of $K/\mathbb{Q}$ factors through $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, its conductor $|\Delta_K|$ divides $m$; hence $\ell \nmid m$ implies $\ell \nmid \Delta_K$ (if $\ell = 2$, then $2 \nmid \Delta_K$, so $-D \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $K_2/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is unramified). On the $H$-invariant space $M_\ell \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} \mathbb{Q}_\ell \cong H_1(A_1, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ (noting that $M_\ell$ is $\Phi_0$-unimodular, whereas the induced polarization carries $|H|$), $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(s z) = |H|\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(s z)$ for $s, z \in K$ yields the exact identity

   $$
   E(s x, y) \;=\; |H|\,\operatorname{Tr}_{K_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\bigl(s\,\Phi_0(x, y)\bigr) \qquad (\forall s \in K_\ell,\; x, y \in M_\ell).
   $$

   With the first-variable-$K$-linear convention of §1.1, the induced geometric $K$-Hermitian form on $A_1$ is therefore $h_{\mathrm{geom}} = |H|\,\eta^{-1}\Phi_0$. Because $\eta^2 = -D$ and $M_\ell$ has **even** $K$-rank $2q$, its $K_\ell$-determinant in any $R$-basis of $M_\ell$ satisfies $\det_K(\eta^{-1}\Phi_0) = \eta^{-2q}\det_R(\Phi_0) = (-1)^q D^{-q}\det_R(\Phi_0)$, so

   $$
   (-1)^q \det_K(h_{\mathrm{geom}}) \;=\; |H|^{2q} D^{-q} \det_R(\Phi_0) \;=\; \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(|H|^q\bigr)\, D^{-q} \det_R(\Phi_0).
   $$

   Since $\ell \nmid \Delta_K$ gives $v_\ell(D) = 0$ (including $\ell = 2$, where $-D \equiv 1 \pmod 4$) and $\det_R(\Phi_0) \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ by Step 3, $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A)$ is represented modulo $\operatorname{Nm}_{K_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell}(K_\ell^\times)$ by $u_\ell := D^{-q}\det_R(\Phi_0) \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$. If $\ell$ splits in $K$, $(u_\ell, -D)_\ell = +1$ trivially; if $\ell$ is inert in $K$ ($\ell \nmid \Delta_K$), $K_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is an unramified quadratic field extension (including at $\ell = 2$), so every unit in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ is a local norm from $K_\ell^\times$, proving $(\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A), -D)_\ell = (u_\ell, -D)_\ell = +1$.

Now assume $|\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(m, K)| \le 1$. Signature $(q, q)$ gives $(-1)^q \det_K(h_0) > 0$, so $(\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A), -D)_\infty = +1$; split primes satisfy $K_\ell \cong \mathbb{Q}_\ell \times \mathbb{Q}_\ell$, giving $+1$; and every $\ell \nmid m$ gives $+1$ by Step 4. Hence $(\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A), -D)_v = +1$ at every place $v$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ with at most one exception $v \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}(m, K)$. By Hilbert's reciprocity law $\prod_v (\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A), -D)_v = +1$, that single remaining symbol must also equal $+1$, so $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(A) = [+1]$ by Hasse's norm theorem. Since $3$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ and $2$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$, $|\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}}| = 1$ for $m = 2^a 3^b$ ($D = 2$) and $m = 2^a 7^b$ ($D = 7$) (`verify.py`, `check_split_controls`, including $(m, D) = (14, 7)$ where $|H| = 3$ and $\ell = 3 \nmid 14$ has Hilbert symbol $+1$). $\square$

## 3. Twisted Commutator Vanishing and Explicit Kummer Lifts

### 3.1 Vanishing of the residual commutator obstruction $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}}$: Proof of Theorem 2(1)

Let $G_0 = \langle \sigma_1 \rangle \times \langle \sigma_2 \rangle \cong C_{d_1} \times C_{d_2} \subset \operatorname{Aut}(B')$ act on $\mu_m = \langle \delta \rangle$ via $h_1, h_2 \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ ($h_i^{d_i} \equiv 1 \pmod m$), and put $N_i := \sum_{j=0}^{d_i - 1} h_i^j \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. Suppose $C : y^m = f$ ($f \in k(B')^\times$) is a smooth projective Kummer cover on $B'$ admitting lifts $\tilde{\sigma}_1, \tilde{\sigma}_2 \in \operatorname{Aut}(C)$ covering $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ with $\tilde{\sigma}_i \delta \tilde{\sigma}_i^{-1} = \delta^{h_i}$ and $\tilde{\sigma}_i^{d_i} = \mathrm{id}_C$ (that is, after the cyclic order obstructions in $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{h_i}/N_i(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$ already vanish; $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}}$ is the remaining commutator quotient rather than the full twisted group $H^2(G_0, \mu_m)$). Over the dense open branch complement $B'^\circ \subset B'$, $C^\circ \to B'^\circ$ is a $\mu_m$-torsor even if $C^\circ$ is disconnected (with connected $B'^\circ$); any $B'^\circ$-automorphism of $C^\circ$ that **commutes** with $\mu_m$ is therefore translation by a single constant element of $\mu_m$. Since $\sigma_1 \sigma_2 = \sigma_2 \sigma_1$ on $B'$ and $h_1 h_2 \equiv h_2 h_1 \pmod m$, $[\tilde{\sigma}_1, \tilde{\sigma}_2] := \tilde{\sigma}_1 \tilde{\sigma}_2 \tilde{\sigma}_1^{-1} \tilde{\sigma}_2^{-1}$ covers $\mathrm{id}_{B'}$ and commutes with $\mu_m$, hence equals $\delta^c$ globally on $C$ for a unique residue $c \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. By the same $\mu_m$-torsor principle, any two lifts $\tilde{\sigma}_i, \tilde{\sigma}_i'$ covering $\sigma_i$ with the same $\mu_m$-conjugation action satisfy $\tilde{\sigma}_i' \tilde{\sigma}_i^{-1} \in C_{\operatorname{Aut}_{B'}(C)}(\mu_m) = \mu_m$, so $\tilde{\sigma}_i' = \delta^{x_i} \tilde{\sigma}_i$ ($x_i \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$).

*Proof of Theorem 2(1).* From $\tilde{\sigma}_1 \tilde{\sigma}_2 \tilde{\sigma}_1^{-1} = \delta^c \tilde{\sigma}_2$, raising both sides to the $d_2$-th power and using $\tilde{\sigma}_1^{d_1} = \tilde{\sigma}_2^{d_2} = \mathrm{id}_C$ together with $(\delta^c \tilde{\sigma}_2)^{d_2} = \delta^{N_2 c} \tilde{\sigma}_2^{d_2} = \delta^{N_2 c}$ yields $\mathrm{id}_C = \tilde{\sigma}_1 \tilde{\sigma}_2^{d_2} \tilde{\sigma}_1^{-1} = \delta^{N_2 c}$, so $N_2 c \equiv 0 \pmod m$. Symmetrically, $\tilde{\sigma}_2 \tilde{\sigma}_1 \tilde{\sigma}_2^{-1} = \delta^{-c} \tilde{\sigma}_1$ raised to the $d_1$-th power gives $\delta^{-N_1 c} = \mathrm{id}_C$, so $N_1 c \equiv 0 \pmod m$, i.e. $c \in \ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2)$.

Any other pair of lifts covering $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ with the same $\mu_m$-conjugation action has the form $\tilde{\sigma}_1' = \delta^{x_1} \tilde{\sigma}_1$, $\tilde{\sigma}_2' = \delta^{x_2} \tilde{\sigma}_2$ ($x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$). Since $(\delta^{x_i} \tilde{\sigma}_i)^{d_i} = \delta^{N_i x_i} \tilde{\sigma}_i^{d_i} = \delta^{N_i x_i}$, the order conditions $\tilde{\sigma}_1'^{d_1} = \tilde{\sigma}_2'^{d_2} = \mathrm{id}_C$ hold **if and only if** $x_1 \in \ker(N_1)$ and $x_2 \in \ker(N_2)$. Computing the rescaled commutator via $\tilde{\sigma}_i \delta^u = \delta^{h_i u} \tilde{\sigma}_i$:

$$
\tilde{\sigma}_1' \tilde{\sigma}_2' \;=\; \delta^{x_1 + h_1 x_2} \tilde{\sigma}_1 \tilde{\sigma}_2 \;=\; \delta^{x_1 + h_1 x_2 + c} \tilde{\sigma}_2 \tilde{\sigma}_1, \qquad \tilde{\sigma}_2' \tilde{\sigma}_1' \;=\; \delta^{x_2 + h_2 x_1} \tilde{\sigma}_2 \tilde{\sigma}_1,
$$

whence $[\tilde{\sigma}_1', \tilde{\sigma}_2'] = \delta^{c'}$ with

$$
c' \;\equiv\; c + (1 - h_2) x_1 - (1 - h_1) x_2 \pmod m.
$$

Thus $[c] \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}}(m; d_1, d_2; h_1, h_2)$ is independent of the choice of order-preserving lifts, and $[c] = 0$ if and only if there exist $x_i \in \ker(N_i)$ making $[\tilde{\sigma}_1', \tilde{\sigma}_2'] = \mathrm{id}_C$. Evaluating $\ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2)$ and $(1 - h_2)\ker(N_1) + (1 - h_1)\ker(N_2)$ directly (`verify.py`, `check_commutator_groups`):
- At $(24; 2, 2; 5, 7)$: $N_1 \equiv 6$, $N_2 \equiv 8 \pmod{24}$, so $\ker(N_1) = 4\mathbb{Z}/24\mathbb{Z}$, $\ker(N_2) = 3\mathbb{Z}/24\mathbb{Z}$, and $\ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2) = 12\mathbb{Z}/24\mathbb{Z} = \{0, 12\}$. Since $(1 - 5) \cdot 3 = -12 \equiv 12 \pmod{24}$ lies in $(1 - h_1)\ker(N_2)$, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}} = 0$.
- At $(40; 2, g_2, g_4)$ ($d_1 = 2, d_2 = 4$): for $g_2 = 11$, $N_1 = 12$ and $\ker(N_1) = 10\mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z}$; for $g_2 = 19$, $N_1 = 20$ and $\ker(N_1) = 2\mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z}$. For $g_4 = 3$, $N_2 = 1 + 3 + 9 + 27 = 40 \equiv 0 \pmod{40}$, so $\ker(N_2) = \mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z}$ and $(1 - g_2)\ker(N_2) = (-10)\mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z}$ or $(-18)\mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z} = 2\mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z}$, which equals $\ker(N_1) = \ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2)$ in both cases! For $g_4 = 7$, $N_2 = 1 + 7 + 49 + 343 = 400 \equiv 0 \pmod{40}$, so again $\ker(N_2) = \mathbb{Z}/40\mathbb{Z}$ and $(1 - g_2)\ker(N_2) = \ker(N_1) = \ker(N_1) \cap \ker(N_2)$, proving $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{comm}} = 0$ identically across all five cases. $\square$

### 3.2 Explicit rational $V_4$-equations at $m = 24$: Proof of Theorem 2(2)

*Proof of Theorem 2(2).* On $B' = \mathbb{P}^1_z$, $V_4 = \{1, \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3\}$ ($\sigma_1(z) = -z$, $\sigma_2(z) = 1/z$, $\sigma_3(z) = -1/z$) has quotient $w(z) = (z^2 - 1)^2 / (4 z^2) : \mathbb{P}^1_z \to \mathbb{P}^1_w$ of degree $4$, branched of order $2$ over three cone points: $w = \infty$ ($\mathcal{O}_1 = \{0, \infty\}$, stab $\langle \sigma_1 \rangle$), $w = 0$ ($\mathcal{O}_2 = \{\pm 1\}$, stab $\langle \sigma_2 \rangle$), and $w = -1$ ($\mathcal{O}_3 = \{\pm i\}$, stab $\langle \sigma_3 \rangle$). Since left-translation by any $g \in \tilde{G}$ permutes $\tilde{G}$, every $g \in \tilde{G}$ restricts to $\mathrm{id}_{A_b}$, giving $q_{\tilde{G}} \circ i_{\tilde{G}} = [d_H]_{A_b}$ and $i_{\tilde{G}}^* \circ g^* = i_{\tilde{G}}^*$. Under $q_{\tilde{G}}^* : H^1(A_b, \mathbb{C})_{[t]} \xrightarrow{\sim} \bigl\{\sum_{g \in \tilde{G}} g^* v : v \in V_t\bigr\}$ ($[t] \in G/H$, free permutation of the $|H|$ faithful character spaces), every $v \in V_t$ satisfies $d_H \cdot i_{\tilde{G}}^*(v) = \sum_{g \in \tilde{G}} i_{\tilde{G}}^*(g^* v) = i_{\tilde{G}}^*(q_{\tilde{G}}^* \tilde{v}) = d_H \cdot \tilde{v}$, so $i_{\tilde{G}}^*|_{V_t} : V_t \xrightarrow{\sim} H^1(A_b, \mathbb{C})_{[t]}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear isomorphism. By functoriality of $i_{\tilde{G}}^*$ on $\bigwedge^{2q} H^1$, $i_{\tilde{G}}^*(\bigwedge^{2q} V_t) = \bigwedge^{2q} H^1(A_b, \mathbb{C})_{[t]}$. Since $i_{\tilde{G}}^*(W_F(B))$ and $W_K(A_b)$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-subspaces of $H^{2q}(A_b, \mathbb{Q})$ having the **same** complexification $\bigoplus_{[t] \in G/H} \bigwedge^{2q} H^1(A_b, \mathbb{C})_{[t]}$ inside $H^{2q}(A_b, \mathbb{C})$, faithful flatness of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q}$ gives $i_{\tilde{G}}^*(W_F(B)) = W_K(A_b)$, whence $W_K(A_b) \subset \operatorname{cl}(CH^q(A_b)_{\mathbb{Q}})$. $\square$

### 5.2 Open Frontiers

1. **Higher-conductor cyclic vs. bicyclic fields ($D = 7, 11, 14, \dots$):** For $D \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ such as $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$, Theorem 1(1) rules out $m \in \{7, 14, 28\}$ ($\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}} = \{7\}$) while permitting composite cyclic conductors with two non-split primes such as $m = 21$ ($\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ns}} = \{3, 7\}$, $H \cong C_6$); by contrast, every $D \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ ($D = 14, 22, \dots$) requires non-cyclic normalizers by Theorem 1(2).
2. **All-genus cyclic-normalizer ceiling on non-split sixfolds ($q = 3$, $\dim \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SU}(3,3)} = 9$):** Within the **primitive cyclic-$\mu_m$ normalizer framework** of §1.2 ($\tilde{G} / C_{\tilde{G}}(\mu_m) \xrightarrow{\sim} H \le (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, $|H| = \varphi(m)/2$), curve-cover moduli cannot reach $9$ dimensions on a **non-split** $\mathrm{SU}(3, 3)$ PEL component at any base genus $g' := g(B') \ge 0$ (by contrast, non-abelian $W(E_8)$ covers do dominate the non-split Gaussian sixfold component [4, Theorem C]): factoring out $C_{\tilde{G}}(\mu_m)$ as in §2.1, Chevalley–Weil gives $2g' - 2 + r_{\mathrm{br}} = 2q = 6$ ($0 \le g' \le 4$, $r_{\mathrm{br}} = 8 - 2g'$). For $|H| = 1$ ($m \in \{3, 4, 6\}$, i.e. $D \in \{1, 3\}$, already resolved across all $9$ moduli by [4, 11, 12]), $\dim \mathcal{M}_{g', r_{\mathrm{br}}} \le g' + 5$ reaches $9$ strictly at $g' = 4, r_{\mathrm{br}} = 0$, which is unramified and split. For $|H| \ge 2$ ($D \notin \{1, 3\}$), if $B_1' \to B_1'/H =: B_0$ ($g_0 := g(B_0)$) has $s$ critical orbits and $r_{\mathrm{free}} \le (8 - 2g')/|H| \le 4 - g'$ free $H$-orbits in the $\mu_m$-branch locus, Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g_0 - 2 + s/2 \le (2g' - 2)/|H| \le g' - 1$, forcing $\dim \mathcal{M}_{g_0, s + r_{\mathrm{free}}} \le 3$ ($g_0 = 2$), $\le g' + 2 \le 6$ ($g_0 = 1$), and $\le g' + 3 \le 7 < 9$ ($g_0 = 0$). Dominating full $9$-modulus non-split sixfold components for $D \notin \{1, 3\}$ therefore requires non-abelian Hurwitz descent beyond [15, 17] or higher-dimensional / twisted-sheaf sources.
