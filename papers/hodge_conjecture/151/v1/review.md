# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance toward the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. The contribution is the combination of an arithmetic obstruction for cyclic normalizers with explicit bicyclic families escaping that obstruction over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$. The results concern specified families, not the full Hodge conjecture or entire higher-dimensional Weil PEL domains.

The main argument is convincing. In Theorem 1, descending the integral skew-Hermitian pairing before comparing it with the geometric polarization addresses the potentially problematic primes dividing $|H|$. The remaining polarization factor contributes a norm because the rank is even. Local norm surjectivity at unramified primes and Hilbert reciprocity then give the single-place obstruction. The conductor and $2$-torsion arguments supply the cyclic-normalizer exclusion; this conclusion does not depend on extrapolating the bounded computational classification.

The constructive part has complementary geometric and computational support. The residual commutator calculation treats the obstruction remaining after the cyclic order conditions, and the pointed Hurwitz construction supplies actual families with a split group action. Theorem 4.1 explicitly connects the determinant classes to the cited Abel-fibre/Fermat correspondence and complementary-pair linear cycles. Together with full special-unitary monodromy, invariant theory gives the all-powers rational Hodge conclusion. The stated semisimple PEL hypotheses also support the application of Abdulali's theorem [22] to the generalized conjecture.

I reproduced the complete supplied verification suite with assertions enabled. It returned all eight signatures and discriminants in Table 1, the claimed Lie ranks at both split primes, the Kummer checks, and the $120$ connected even-rank orbits used in Proposition 1.3. Separately, replacing the packed finite-field multiplication and elimination with ordinary modular arithmetic reproduced rank $323$ for the largest witness at $p=41$. This independently checks that arithmetic implementation, while retaining the submitted construction of the braid matrices. These computations support the finite certificates; the universal geometric and algebraicity assertions rely on the mathematical arguments and cited correspondence results, not on execution alone.

The distinction from prior work is meaningful. Papers [2] and [3] develop cyclic-rotation families, and [25] develops elliptic cyclic-normalizer families over $D=5,15$. Their mechanisms are appropriately credited, but they do not supply the bicyclic constructions and field obstructions proved here. The determinant-supply machinery is inherited from [4] and [7], rather than a new general correspondence theorem. The focused external comparison with Markman's results [10], the cited official preprint [7], and Brosnan's discriminant/degeneration analysis [27] did not identify a result subsuming this contribution. Brosnan's maximal-degeneration obstruction is different from the conductor-dependent restriction proved here.

The manuscript communicates its research question, proof strategy, evidence, and limitations sufficiently clearly for specialists. It is dense, but organized around mathematical claims rather than an experiment chronology. The following localized corrections would improve precision without changing the supported central results:

- In §3.2, the displayed equality for $N=\deg f_{\mathbf t}$ replaces least residues by unreduced exponents. Use the actual sum of exponents and state the required congruence modulo $24$. Also, the preliminary choice $\mu_2^{24}=\lambda_2^{-1}$ conflicts with $\mu_2^8=\kappa_2^{-1}$ and $\lambda_2\kappa_2^3=1$; the later construction correctly requires $\mu_2^{24}=\lambda_2$.
- In §4.1, distinguish the finite quotient of $C^n$, its map to the symmetric product, and the restricted cover over the exceptional Abel fibre. The notation $Z=\mathrm{Bl}(B'^{(n)})$ is inconsistent with the subsequent character-cohomology argument. The cited construction [4, Lemma 2.1] makes the intended objects identifiable, but they should be named correctly here.
- In the proof of Proposition 1.3, $21\notin H$ for $D=2,m=40$. The intended pairing on weights divisible by $4$ can use $19\in H$, since $19b\equiv-b\pmod{40}$. The exhaustive residue check supports the asserted balance.
- Narrow the abstract's opening historical claim to the higher-dimensional cover constructions under comparison. Markman's fourfold results prevent reading the asserted field restriction as a statement about all previously known non-split Weil varieties. Keep equally explicit that Weil-class algebraicity holds on every smooth fibre, whereas all-powers HC and GHC are established for very general fibres.

Moving repetitive function/line references and routine algebraic expansions to an appendix would improve readability. The essential distinctions and evidence are already present.
