# The integral Hodge conjecture on powers of elliptic curves and of the K3 surface $X_3$

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction

The integral Hodge conjecture (IHC) fails in general, including on abelian varieties: on a very general principally polarized abelian
variety of dimension $\ge4$, every curve class is an even multiple of the minimal class [EGS25]. In the other direction, Beckmann and
de Gaay Fortman proved IHC for one-cycles on products of Jacobians [BdGF22], which includes every product of elliptic curves. With
the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem this settles IHC on products of $n\le3$ elliptic curves. For $n\ge4$ and codimension $2\le c\le n-2$ we could
not find a statement in the literature.

**Theorem A (§3).** Let $X=E_1\times\dots\times E_n$ be a product of elliptic curves such that the curves in each CM isogeny class have the same
endomorphism ring, and every non-CM isogeny class consists of isomorphic curves. Then the integral Hodge ring
of $X$ is generated by divisor classes. In particular IHC holds on $X$ in all degrees.

This covers $E^n$ for every complex elliptic curve $E$. The rational statement, that the
Hodge ring is generated by divisors, is classical [Ima76, Mur69]. The integral content is a saturation statement for products of pair
classes over perfect matchings. For CM curves we prove it prime by prime: at unramified primes by splitting into character lines, and
at the non-étale primes of the order (those dividing its discriminant) by identifying the products mod $p$ with two-row polytabloids and using James's theorem that standard polytabloids
form a basis of the Specht module over every field (§2). For non-CM curves it is the characteristic-free first fundamental theorem.

**Theorem B (§4).** Let $X_3$ be the K3 surface with transcendental lattice $A_2$. Then IHC holds on $X_3^k$ in all degrees, for every $k$.

The proof combines Theorem A for $E_\omega^{2k}$ with the Shioda–Inose correspondence, whose composition with its transpose acts as $4$ on $T$, and a direct argument at
$p=2$ using the automorphism of order $3$.

**Corollary C.** The threshold class $\Omega/3$ of [hc116] on $X_3^3$ is algebraic.

[hc116] showed that $\Omega/3$ is an integral Hodge class outside the span of graph-diagonal cycles; the successor note [G56] listed it as
open. Corollary C shows that the gap there comes from the choice of generators, and is not a failure of IHC.

**Scope.** Theorem A does not cover isogenous curves with different endomorphism rings. For four $3$-isogenous non-CM curves we show
that divisors do not generate the integral Hodge ring. The missing class is, up to divisor products, the Künneth component of the
pushforward of the square of a divisor from an isogenous abelian fourfold, so IHC still holds there (Propositions 5.1, 5.2). We ask whether IHC holds on every product
of elliptic curves (Question 5.3). Theorem B uses that
$T(X_3)$ is a rank-one module over $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ with an automorphism realizing $\omega$. Other singular K3 surfaces with $2\mid\mathrm{disc}\,T$
need a different argument at $p=2$.

**Bearing on the Hodge conjecture.** As far as we could find, these are new cases of the integral Hodge conjecture in all degrees: on infinite
families of abelian varieties of every dimension $\ge4$ (dimension $\le3$ was covered by [BdGF22] and Lefschetz) and on all powers of one K3 surface. They do not touch the rational Hodge conjecture, which is known for
these varieties. They show that the known integral cokernels of diagonal-type cycle maps on $X_3^k$ are explained by missing generators.
## 2. Saturation of matching products

Let $\mathcal O$ be an order in an imaginary quadratic field $K$, $p$ a prime, $R=\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_p$, and $\theta\in R\setminus\mathbb Z_p$ with $\mathbb Z_p[\theta]=R$.
Put $n=2m$, $A=R^{\otimes n}$ (over $\mathbb Z_p$), $\theta_i$ = $\theta$ in slot $i$, and $\sigma=\sum_i\theta_i$.

**Lemma 2.1.** $I:=\mathrm{Ann}_A(\sigma-m\,\mathrm{tr}\,\theta)$ is a saturated $\mathbb Z_p$-submodule of $A$ of rank $\binom{2m}{m}$. For $n=2$ it has the
basis $g_0=\theta_1-\theta_2$, $g_1=(\theta_1-\theta_2)\theta_1$.

*Proof.* Annihilators are saturated. Over $\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$, $R$ splits into two lines on which $\theta$ acts by its conjugates $\theta'\ne\theta''$,
and $\sigma-m\,\mathrm{tr}\,\theta$ acts on a monomial with $k$ factors of type $'$ by $(2k-n)(\theta'-\theta'')/2$, which vanishes iff $k=m$.
For $n=2$: $(\theta_1+\theta_2-\mathrm{tr}\,\theta)(\theta_1-\theta_2)=\theta_1^2-\mathrm{tr}\,\theta\,\theta_1-(\theta_2^2-\mathrm{tr}\,\theta\,\theta_2)=0$, and $g_1=g_0\theta_1$.
In the basis $1,\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_1\theta_2$, the minor of $(g_0,g_1)$ on $\theta_2,\theta_1\theta_2$ is $\det\begin{psmallmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}=1$. $\square$

A *matching product* is $\prod_{s=1}^m g_{\epsilon_s}(a_s,b_s)$ for a perfect matching $\{\{a_s,b_s\}\}$ of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ and $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^m$.

**Theorem 2.2.** The matching products span $I$ over $\mathbb Z_p$.

*Proof.* Let $\Sigma$ be the span.

*$p\mid\mathrm{disc}\,\mathcal O$.* $I$ does not change if $\theta$ is replaced by $a+b\theta$ with $a\in\mathbb Z_p$, $b\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$, and neither does the
span if $(g_0,g_1)$ changes unimodularly. So we may take $\theta=x$ with $R=\mathbb Z_p[x]$ and $x^2\in pR$: a uniformizer if $\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ is maximal, and $x=p^kw$ if
$R=\mathbb Z_p+p^k(\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_p)$ with $k\ge1$ and $\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_p=\mathbb Z_p[w]$. The pair basis is
$g_0=x_a-x_b$, $g_1=x_a^2-x_ax_b$, and mod $p$ we have $g_1\equiv-x_ax_b$. In $A/p=\mathbb F_p[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(x_i^2)$ a matching product is then,
up to a unit, $x_P\prod_{s\notin J}(x_{a_s}-x_{b_s})$, where $J=\{s:\epsilon_s=1\}$ and $P=\bigcup_{s\in J}\{a_s,b_s\}$; it has degree $m+|J|$.
Send a squarefree monomial $x_U$ to the tabloid with second row $U^c$. The element above goes to $\pm\prod_{s\notin J}(1-(a_s\,b_s))$ applied to
the tabloid with first row $P\cup\{a_s\}_{s\notin J}$, i.e. to the polytabloid $e_t$ of a tableau $t$ of shape $(m+j,m-j)$, $j=|J|$, whose
two-box columns are $\{a_s,b_s\}$, $s\notin J$. Every tableau of this shape arises. Polytabloids span the Specht module, and standard
polytabloids are a basis over every field [Jam78, Thm 8.4], so $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}$ of the span in degree $m+j$ is $\binom{2m}{m-j}-\binom{2m}{m-j-1}$.
The sum over $j$ is $\binom{2m}{m}=\mathrm{rk}\,I$. As $I$ is saturated, $I/pI\hookrightarrow A/pA$; hence $\Sigma+pI=I$ and $\Sigma=I$ by Nakayama.

*$p\nmid\mathrm{disc}\,\mathcal O$.* Then $R$ is étale over $\mathbb Z_p$. Let $W=W(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$. Then $R\otimes W\cong W\times W$ with idempotents $e',e''$, and $I\otimes W$ has the basis of balanced
monomials (one factor $e'$ or $e''$ per slot, $m$ of each). Each is a product over a matching of terms $e'_ae''_b$, and $e'_ae''_b$, $e''_ae'_b$
span $(I_{ab})\otimes W=\langle g_0,g_1\rangle\otimes W$. So $\Sigma\otimes W=I\otimes W$, and faithful flatness gives $\Sigma=I$. $\square$
## 3. Products of elliptic curves

**Theorem 3.1.** Let $X=E_1\times\dots\times E_n$ be a product of complex elliptic curves. Suppose the curves in each CM isogeny class among the $E_i$
have the same endomorphism ring, and every non-CM isogeny class consists of mutually isomorphic curves. Then every
integral Hodge class on $X$ is an integral combination of products of pull-backs of divisor classes from the factors $E_a$ and the products $E_a\times E_b$. In
particular the integral Hodge conjecture holds on $X$ in all degrees.

*Proof.* (1) *Künneth.* $H^*(X,\mathbb Z)=\bigotimes_iH^*(E_i,\mathbb Z)$ is torsion-free, and the multidegree decomposition is a decomposition into
integral Hodge substructures, so an integral Hodge class has integral Hodge components. A component with degree $0$ or $2$ in slot $i$ is
$1$ or $[\mathrm{pt}]$ there, so it suffices to treat the *mixed blocks* $H_S=\bigotimes_{i\in S}H^1(E_i,\mathbb Z)$. Classes in
$H^1(E_a)\otimes H^1(E_b)$ of type $(1,1)$ are divisor classes on $E_a\times E_b$ (Lefschetz). We show: the integral Hodge classes in $H_S$
are spanned by products over perfect matchings of $S$ of integral Hodge classes of the pairs.

(2) *Isogeny classes.* Group $S=S_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup S_r$ by isogeny class. The Hodge group of $X$ is the product of the Hodge groups of
the $\prod_{i\in S_k}E_i$ [Ima76]. If $G_1\times G_2$ acts on $M_1\otimes M_2$ factorwise ($M_i$ free), then $(M_1\otimes M_2)^{G_1\times G_2}=
M_1^{G_1}\otimes M_2^{G_2}$ inside $M_1\otimes M_2$, because $M_1^{G_1}$ is saturated in $M_1$, so $(M_1\otimes M_2)^{G_1}=M_1^{G_1}\otimes M_2$. Hodge
classes on a block with $|S_k|$ odd vanish (the Hodge group contains $-1$). So we may assume one isogeny class.
For completeness, the product statement: $\mathrm{Hg}\subset\prod_k\mathrm{Hg}_k$ is connected and projects onto each factor. Two non-CM factors $SL(V_k)$, $SL(V_l)$:
by Goursat, a non-surjective projection is the graph of an isomorphism $SL(V_k)\cong SL(V_l)$ over $\mathbb Q$; it is inner, so induced by
$V_k\cong V_l$, which is then a Hodge isometry up to scalar and gives an isogeny. A torus and $SL_2$: $SL_2$ has no non-trivial abelian
quotient. Norm-one tori $T_{K_1},\dots,T_{K_r}$ with distinct $K_k$: $X^*(\prod T_{K_k})=\bigoplus\mathbb Z\chi_k$ with pairwise distinct non-trivial
quadratic Galois characters $\chi_k$. The characters vanishing on a subtorus form a Galois-stable subgroup $L$; $L\otimes\mathbb Q$ is a sum of
isotypic components $\mathbb Q\chi_k$, so if $L\ne0$ it meets some summand, and the projection to that $T_{K_k}$ is not surjective. For several $SL_2$ factors, pairwise surjectivity suffices (Goursat for
products of simple groups).

(3) *CM, common order $\mathcal O$.* Then $H^1(E_i,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathfrak a_i$, a proper (hence invertible) fractional $\mathcal O$-ideal, and the Hodge group is the norm-one torus of $K$,
whose Lie algebra acts on $\mathfrak a_i\otimes\mathbb Q=K$ by multiplication by $\mathbb Q\sqrt{d_K}$. It suffices to prove the spanning statement after
$\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ for every $p$. Invertible ideals of the semilocal ring $\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ are principal, so $\mathfrak a_i\otimes\mathbb Z_p=\alpha_i(\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_p)$, and
multiplication by $\bigotimes\alpha_i$ commutes with the Hodge group. It maps the Hodge classes of $(\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_p)^{\otimes S}$ and of its
pairs onto those of $\bigotimes\mathfrak a_i\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ and its pairs. The Hodge classes of $(\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_p)^{\otimes S}$ are $I$ of Lemma 2.1
(with $\theta$ a generator of $\mathcal O\otimes \mathbb Z_p$), and Theorem 2.2 applies.

(4) *No CM.* All $E_i\cong E$, the Hodge group is $SL(H^1(E,\mathbb Q))$, and integral Hodge classes in $H^1(E,\mathbb Z)^{\otimes 2m}$ are the
$SL_2$-invariants of $(\mathbb Z^2)^{\otimes2m}$ under the group scheme $SL_{2,\mathbb Z}$ (an element of the lattice fixed by $SL_2(\mathbb Q)$ is fixed
by the group scheme, $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ being Zariski dense). By the characteristic-free first fundamental theorem [DP76], these are spanned
by products of brackets $[\,a\,b\,]$ over matchings, and a bracket is the integral Hodge class $e_1\otimes e_2-e_2\otimes e_1$ of the pair. $\square$

**Remark 3.2 (checks).** Exact Smith normal forms (`emix_K.py`, `emix_gen.py`) confirm the spanning statement for $\mathbb Z[\omega]$, $\mathbb Z[i]$,
$\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-2}]$, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})}$, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-11})}$, $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ for $n\le6$ (mod-$p$ ranks, including ramified $p$, for $n=8$), for the
non-maximal orders $\mathbb Z[2\omega],\mathbb Z[3\omega],\mathbb Z[2i],\mathbb Z[3i]$ for $n\le6$ (`emix_Knm.out`), for $SL_2$ with $n\le8$, and, for a non-principal ideal of
$\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$, for $n=4$. A negative control (pair basis scaled by $3$) is detected. The spanning statement also holds in every
case we tested with *different* orders in one CM isogeny class ($n=4$: all mixtures of $\mathbb Z[\omega],\mathbb Z[2\omega],\mathbb Z[3\omega],\mathbb Z[6\omega]$ and the
ideal $(2+\omega)$, and of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})}$, $(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$, $\mathbb Z[2\sqrt{-5}]$; $n=6$: one mixture of three orders; `emix_gen2.out`). We do not
have a proof in that case, which is why Theorem 3.1 assumes a common order.
## 4. Powers of the K3 surface $X_3$

Let $X=X_3$ be the K3 surface with $T(X)\cong A_2$ (positive definite, discriminant $3$); $\rho(X)=20$, $\mathrm{NS}(X)$ has discriminant $-3$.
By Shioda–Inose [SI77] $X$ is unique. It carries an automorphism $g$ of order 3 acting on $H^{2,0}$ by $\omega$ and trivially on $\mathrm{NS}$:
the rotation of order $3$ of $A_2$ acts trivially on the discriminant group, so together with the identity on $\mathrm{NS}$ it extends to a
Hodge isometry of $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$ fixing an ample class, which the Torelli theorem realizes by an automorphism. Then $T$ is an
$\mathcal O_K$-module of rank one, $K=\mathbb Q(\omega)$, $\omega$ acting by $g^*$, and the Hodge group of $T_{\mathbb Q}$ is the norm-one torus of $K$.

**Theorem 4.1.** The integral Hodge conjecture holds on $X^k$ in all degrees, for every $k\ge1$.

*Proof.* $H^*(X^k,\mathbb Z)$ is torsion-free. For a Hodge class $Y$ the integers $N$ with $NY$ algebraic form an ideal; we show it contains
$4^j$ and an integer prime to $2$, hence $1$. By Künneth (as in 3.1(1)) it suffices to treat Hodge classes $Y$ in $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)^{\otimes j}$.

*$p\ne3$.* $H^2\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}=(\mathrm{NS}\oplus T)\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, so $Y$ is a sum of products of divisors with Hodge classes of $T^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}$.
These are spanned by matching products of the pair classes (Theorem 2.2 with $R=\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_p$; $p\ne3$ is unramified in $K$),
and the pair space $\mathrm{Hdg}(T\otimes T)_{(p)}$ is spanned by the $T\otimes T$-components $\delta_T,\gamma_T$ of the diagonal and of the graph of $g$.
(At $p\ne3$, $T_{(p)}$ is unimodular, so $\mathrm{Hdg}(T\otimes T)_{(p)}\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal O_K}(T)_{(p)}=\mathcal O_{K,(p)}=\langle1,g^*\rangle$.) These differ from the $H^2\otimes H^2$ Künneth components of $[\Delta]$, $[\Gamma_g]$ (algebraic, since the Künneth projectors of a K3 surface
are integral and algebraic) by elements of $\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}$ with denominators dividing $3$, so they are $p$-integrally algebraic. Taking $p=2$ gives an odd $N$.

*$p=3$.* Let $A=E\times E$, $E=\mathbb C/\mathbb Z[\omega]$; $T(A)\cong A_2$ [SM74]. A Shioda–Inose structure [SI77, Mor84] gives a Nikulin
involution with $X\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Km}(A)$ of degree $2$ and $\widetilde A\to\mathrm{Km}(A)$ of degree $2$. The composite correspondence
$\Gamma:H^2(A,\mathbb Z)\to H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$ is algebraic and integral, and $F=\Gamma\Gamma^{t}$ acts on $T(X)$ as $4$. Then $\phi=4\pi_2-F$ (on $H^2$)
is self-adjoint and kills $T$, so its image lies in $T^\perp=\mathrm{NS}$ and $\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(H^2/T,\mathrm{NS})=(H^2/T)^*\otimes\mathrm{NS}=\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}$,
since $(H^2/T)^*=T^\perp=\mathrm{NS}$ ($H^2$ unimodular). So $\phi$ is a divisor correspondence. Expanding $4^jY=(F+\phi)^{\otimes j}Y$, each term is a divisor correspondence on some
slots, which contracts $Y$ to a Hodge class $Y'$ on fewer slots, times $\Gamma^{\otimes i}(\Gamma^{t\otimes i}Y')$. Here $\Gamma^{t\otimes i}Y'$ is an
integral Hodge class on $A^i=E^{2i}$, algebraic by Theorem 3.1. So $4^jY$ is algebraic. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2.** The class $\Omega/3$ of [hc116] on $X_3^3$, which is not in the span of graph-diagonal cycles, is algebraic. More generally
every cokernel class of the unitary graph-diagonal map of [hc116] on $X_3^k$ is algebraic.
## 5. Divisors do not generate on a product of four isogenous curves

Theorem 3.1 needs the curves in a non-CM isogeny class to be isomorphic. Without that hypothesis, divisors no longer generate the
integral Hodge ring, although in the example below IHC still holds. Let $E$ be a non-CM elliptic curve, $e,f$ a basis of
$H^1(E,\mathbb Z)$, and let $E_1,\dots,E_4$ be the four quotients of $E$ by its subgroups of order $3$, with cohomology lattices (inside $H^1(E,\mathbb Q)$)
$$L_1=\langle e,3f\rangle,\quad L_2=\langle e+f,3f\rangle,\quad L_3=\langle e+2f,3f\rangle,\quad L_4=\langle f,3e\rangle,$$
one for each line of $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_3)$. Put $\varepsilon_{ab}=e_a\otimes f_b-f_a\otimes e_b$, $U=\varepsilon_{12}\otimes\varepsilon_{34}$, $V=\varepsilon_{13}\otimes\varepsilon_{24}$, and $A=\prod E_i$. Here $U,V$ are tensors placed in the slots $(1,2,3,4)$ without
Koszul signs (so the cup product $\varepsilon_{13}\smile\varepsilon_{24}$ is $-V$).

**Proposition 5.1.** In the $(1,1,1,1)$ Künneth block of $H^4(A,\mathbb Z)$ the integral Hodge classes form $I=\mathbb Z\,81U\oplus\mathbb Z\,27(2U-V)$.
Products of divisors span $W=81(\mathbb ZU+\mathbb ZV)$, so $I/W\cong\mathbb Z/3$.

*Proof.* Each pair lattice of integral Hodge classes in $L_a\otimes L_b$ is $9\mathbb Z\varepsilon_{ab}$, and the three matchings span $W$ by the
Plücker relation. A class $aU+bV$ is integral iff $a,b\in27\mathbb Z$ and $a+2b\in81\mathbb Z$. Exact check: `cex_p1f3.out`. $\square$

This was pointed out by an adversarial check of an earlier, stronger draft of Theorem 3.1. In our tests, classes of abelian surfaces in $A$
(22,500 random cases) and pushforwards of Hodge classes from $E_0^4$ or $E_0^5$ along homomorphisms to $A$ (248 cases) all stayed in $W$.

**Proposition 5.2.** The class $27(2U-V)$ is algebraic. Hence every integral Hodge class in this block is algebraic, and IHC holds on $A$.
(The other Künneth blocks of $H^4$ contain a factor $1$ or $[\mathrm{pt}]$ and reduce to pair classes; degrees $2$ and $6$ are Lefschetz and [BdGF22].)

*Proof.* Let $B$ be the abelian fourfold isogenous to $E^4$ with $H^1(B,\mathbb Z)=N$, where $N\subset H^1(E^4,\mathbb Q)$ is the lattice in
`cex_reached.json`. Let $f\colon B\to A$ be the homomorphism with $f^*e_i=\sum_jq_{ij}e_j$, $f^*f_i=\sum_j q_{ij}f_j$ for the matrix $q$ given there.
It maps $\bigoplus L_i$ into $N$, so $f$ is defined. Let $D\in\mathrm{NS}(B)$ be the class given there. By the projection formula
$\int_Af_*(D^2)\,y=\int_BD^2f^*y$, and $\int_B=\int_{E^4}/\mathrm{covol}(N)$. So the $(1,1,1,1)$ part of $f_*(D^2)$ is $aU+bV$ with $a=-1685664$,
$b=223344$ (the cup pairing on $L_i$ is $\int_E/3$, since the isogenies have degree $3$). Since $81\nmid b$, this class is not in $W$. The Künneth projectors of a product of curves are integral and algebraic, so the
$(1,1,1,1)$ part is algebraic, and $W+\mathbb Z(aU+bV)=I$. Explicitly, $27(2U-V)=-(aU+bV)-20810\cdot81U+2757\cdot81V$. Two independent implementations (`cex_pushB.py`, `cex_verify.py`)
agree. $\square$

**Question 5.3.** Does IHC hold on every product of elliptic curves? More precisely, are the integral Hodge classes generated by
pushforwards of products of divisors from abelian varieties isogenous to the product?

**Question 5.4.** Let $X$ be a singular K3 surface. Does IHC hold on $X^k$? The argument of §4 works at odd $p$ whenever IHC holds on
the relevant products of the two CM curves of the Shioda–Inose structure. At $p=2$ it needs $2\nmid\mathrm{disc}\,\mathrm{NS}(X)$ and
$2$-integral algebraic generators of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)$, which for $X_3$ come from the automorphism of order $3$.
