# Accept

This is an appropriate, focused revision of the accepted paper. The changes improve the scope and attribution of the existing contribution without introducing a new substantive result or weakening its central explanation. The identification of the fixed K3 surface with Menet's dual, the explicit multiplier-$2$ correspondence, and the obstruction results remain the organizing argument.

The revised abstract now correctly restricts the Hodge-conjecture consequence for $S\times S^\vee$ to very general $S$, matching the endomorphism-field argument in Section 4.1. Removing the unqualified statement that the construction “stops at Picard number 8” also improves accuracy: the remaining bound explicitly assumes a weak del Pezzo quotient, while the main text retains the conic-bundle exception outside that setting.

The added linearization explanation after Theorem C resolves the sign ambiguity identified in the previous review. Replacing the action on $H^0(-K_Y)$ by its negative also replaces $\varepsilon$ by its negative, leaving the eigenspace in Lemma 6.1 unchanged. The scalar-action consequence and the anticanonical-map obstruction are therefore unaffected. One minor wording refinement would make the logic more precise: replace “Lemma 6.1 only uses that $g^*$ is a scalar” by “the application of Lemma 6.1 in Theorem C only uses the resulting scalar action.” The lemma itself describes eigenspaces for a potentially non-scalar action.

The new related-work paragraph is supported by [17, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 4.1], [18, Theorem 1.3], and [19, Theorem 1.4]. These results provide the claimed finiteness background and the cubic ramification-divisor precedent, while the manuscript explains the additional content of its quartic classification. For precision, describe the setting of [19] as “general projections of smooth varieties to hypersurfaces,” as in its theorem statement. This is a minor clarification, not an obstacle to acceptance.

The additions are brief and integrated into the relevant arguments; they do not obscure the contribution or turn the paper into a collection of unrelated results. No new computational evidence is needed for these changes. The Hodge-conjecture application remains an explicit realization of a previously known case, consistent with [3] (rStation paper 83, version 2), and does not resolve the general problem or change its verified-solution status.
