# The fixed surface of a symplectic involution on Hilbert squares of quartics with a quasi-Galois point

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T20:29:03.645897Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/86/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $S=\{u^4+a u^2+b=0\}\subset\mathbb P^3$ be a smooth quartic, with $a$ and $b$ forms of degree 2 and 4 in $x,y,z$. These are the quartics with a quasi-Galois point, and they form the 12-dimensional family of K3 surfaces with a non-symplectic involution of type (8,8,1). Menet showed that exchanging the two totally tangent plane quartics attached to $S$ is an involution of this family, with partner $S^\vee=\{s^4+2as^2+a^2-4b=0\}$ in coordinates, and that $S\not\cong S^\vee$ generically. We show that $S^\vee$ is the K3 surface fixed by the symplectic involution of $S^{[2]}$ (when $S$ contains no line) studied by Markushevich–Tikhomirov and by Camere, Garbagnati, Kapustka and Kapustka. The incidence correspondence, a complete intersection of type (2,4) in $\mathbb P^4$, has degree 2 over each side and induces a Hodge similarity of multiplier 2 between their transcendental lattices. So for very general $S$ the surfaces are not twisted derived equivalent. This corrects the identification of the fixed surface with $S$ in the work of Camere et al. We also show that the construction stops at Picard number 8. For a smooth quartic and a point outside it, the projection has monodromy $S_4$, $D_4$ or $C_4$, and the surface of pairs on lines through the point is reducible only at quasi-Galois points. For a tower of double covers $S\to Y\to Y/g$ with $Y$ a weak del Pezzo surface, a K3 bigonal partner forces $K_Y^2\le2$, hence $\rho(S)\ge8$. The explicit cycle proves the Hodge conjecture for $S\times S^\vee$, known before via Markman's theorem, without it.
