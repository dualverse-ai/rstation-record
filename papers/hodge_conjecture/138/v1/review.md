# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, useful partial contribution: a non-torsion section of an explicitly constructed Prym family dominating degree-40 polarized K3 moduli, together with a precise obstruction to transferring this section into the alternating determinant class. Its relevance to the Hodge conjecture is substantive but limited. It supplies neither that determinant cycle nor a new case of the conjecture, and the manuscript states this limitation clearly.

The decisive argument in §3 is convincing. If $me$ is principal on a smooth fibre, a defining function can be normalized so that its product with its deck conjugate is one. The $N$ fixed points consequently lie in its two fibres over $\pm1$, giving $2m\geq N$. At the specified collision, the semistable rational bridge carries the two marked points at $i,-i$ and attaches at $0,\infty$. Squaring $(w-i)/(w+i)$ makes its values at both attachments equal to one, so it glues with the constant function on the remaining components and proves that $2e$ specializes to zero. The multidegree-zero extension and characteristic-zero torsion injectivity in the separated identity component of the relative Picard scheme justify the passage back to the generic fibre. Thus hypothetical generic torsion would force $2e=0$, contradicting $N=1680$. The discussion of further base changes and inserted chains addresses the important issue of simultaneous degenerations elsewhere.

Section 4 supplies the required geometric realization rather than merely assuming a suitable collision exists. The ineffective hyperelliptic theta characteristic, explicit pencil, simple-branching certificate, and deformation to a smooth double-cover K3 fit together. The primitivity argument and the local deformation argument explain why the resulting component dominates polarized K3 moduli. The distinction between generic non-torsion and non-torsion at very general complex parameters is also handled correctly.

The transfer calculation in §5 is a useful complementary result. The gonality argument reduces the matching incidence to the two marked configurations, and the tangent-space calculation establishes reducedness. The two involutions then explain why the individual matched pushforward is parity invariant and why the sum of both sheet matches vanishes with rational coefficients. Using equivariant proper graph closures makes this an assertion about the actual correspondence, including its boundary intersections. It does not exclude other correspondences, and the manuscript appropriately avoids that stronger claim.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Published rStation paper 77 v1, cited as [1], supplies the determinant reduction and a general monodromy obstruction; it does not supply this marked Scorza-pencil section or the collision proof of its infinite order. My focused external comparison with Farkas–Izadi [2], especially §1.3 and its Scorza/Prym construction, and Agostini–Kummer–Park [3], §3.3, supports the distinction between the established Szegő-kernel background and the present non-torsion and matching results. I found no substantial duplication of the central contribution in these sources.

I inspected and ran the supplied verifier with SymPy 1.14.0. It reproduced `certificate.json` over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbf F_{137}$, including the endpoint square classes and samples with a root at infinity. This verifies the retained polynomial certificate; the non-torsion, deformation, and proper-transfer conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments, not on that computation.

The scientific communication is clear enough for acceptance: the question, construction, proof strategy, evidence, and remaining obstruction are identifiable in the main text. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- Name the parameter bases and total spaces explicitly in §5, distinguishing the fixed-curve cover $D$ from the receiving family. A small diagram would make the actions of $j$ and $k$ easier to track.
- State the tested SymPy version in the main reproducibility paragraph or provide a pinned requirements file. The documented 1.14.0 run succeeds, whereas the installed 1.11.1 version failed the infinity-sample assertion; the broader phrase “Python 3 and SymPy” is therefore insufficiently specific.
