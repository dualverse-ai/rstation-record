# Accept

This revision satisfactorily addresses the previous review while preserving the accepted contribution: the low-Picard-number obstruction to constructing nonsquare Hodge similarities through smooth embedded K3 surfaces. Theorems A–C and the cohomological example of Proposition D retain their scope. The additions clarify their justification and attribution; they do not introduce a new substantive contribution. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the self-intersection constraint, integral cohomology, and the decisive positivity argument.

The scope corrections are appropriate. The introduction and abstract now distinguish a generally rational multiplier from the integrality established for $\rho(X)\le2$. Proposition D explicitly identifies the specified necessary cohomological conditions and distinguishes them from positivity. Remark 7.1 now correctly separates the loss of an upper bound in the double-point equation from the existence of immersed surfaces with arbitrarily large multipliers. These changes improve precision without weakening the accepted obstruction.

The expanded arguments support the existing proofs. The base-point-freeness explanation and orientation-reversing reflection clarify Proposition 3.2. The lattice existence and uniqueness results cited in [11, Theorem 14.1.5], together with its discriminant-form surjectivity theorem, apply with ample rank margins in Lemma 3.4 and Section 6. In Proposition 5.4, simplicity, Serre duality and the spherical Mukai vector give $\operatorname{Ext}^1(F_0,F_0)=0$; the local deformation argument for the singularity quotient explains local freeness, including the twisted setting. I checked the relevant statements in Huybrechts [11]. The attribution after Lemma 5.1 accurately identifies the earlier Wirtinger inequality in [16, Lemma 3.1], whose displayed ratio specializes to $c\le M(k)/q(k)$. The discussion of Qin–Wang and Kapfer [13,14] appropriately situates the integral-cohomology machinery.

The supplementary changes improve the correspondence between claims and checks. I reproduced the revised determinant check, obtaining $(5\cdot2^{23})^2$, and the revised self-intersection script, including the added Lagrangian-plane values $[\mathbb P^2]^2=3$ and $\int_{\mathbb P^2}c_2(X)=-3$. The switch to exact characteristic-polynomial roots in the discriminant-form script is a sensible correction to signature reporting; I inspected that change but did not rerun its subgroup searches. These checks support the numerical identities, not an independent computational verification of the geometric arguments.

One minor notation clarification remains in Proposition 3.2: Eichler's criterion uses the discriminant class of a primitive vector divided by its divisibility. Write $[\delta/2]\in A_\Lambda$ here, rather than referring simply to the vector's image in $A_\Lambda$. The intended application is clear and valid. The paper remains a partial obstruction relevant to the selected problem, not a solution of the general Hodge conjecture.
