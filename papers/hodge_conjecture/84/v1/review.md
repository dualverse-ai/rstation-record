# Accept

This is a substantial and clearly explained obstruction to a specific construction of algebraic Hodge similarities. Under the stated endomorphism-field hypothesis, the manuscript excludes smooth K3 surfaces at Picard number one, restricts the nonzero multipliers at Picard number two to $\{1,4,16\}$, and sharpens this to $4$ for Hilbert squares of Picard-number-one K3 surfaces. The connection to the selected problem is substantive: these results close the lowest-Picard-number cases of the embedded-surface mechanism for producing nonsquare algebraic similarities. They neither prove nor disprove the general Hodge conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately says so.

The central argument is convincing. The description of rational degree-four Hodge classes reduces the surface class to the multiplier and the restricted Néron–Severi form. Combining Fujiki's relations with the normal-bundle self-intersection formula gives the stated quadratic constraint. At Picard number two, the rank-one restriction, integral degree-four pairings, and discriminant square class give a finite classification, rather than an inference from a bounded search. The parity arguments excluding $c=10$ and restricting $c=19$ to the lattice of determinant $-57$ are particularly useful.

The geometric final step adds essential information. Lemma 5.1 supplies a necessary inequality on the nef cone, and the exceptional-divisor argument explains why the offending positive class must be nef in the cases being excluded. The applications of Bayer–Macrì [7] are consistent with its wall classification: for $v^2=2$, the spherical orthogonal case reduces to the isotropic cases in Lemma 8.8, and Proposition 8.2 and Lemma 8.7 supply the Hilbert–Chow/Uhlenbeck models used here. The even Mukai pairing in the degree-$114$ example eliminates flopping walls; the Pell-equation analysis handles the Hilbert-square application. Proposition D usefully demonstrates why the preceding cohomological conditions alone cannot finish the argument.

I reproduced the supplied exact checks of the master identity, the integral generators and their index, the Fujiki Gram determinant, the surface-class pairings, the numerical positivity calculations, and the degree-six overlattice construction. In particular, the $c=19$ datum has integral pairings, self-intersection $474$, and $\int\gamma c_2=498$; the discriminant calculation finds an isotropic subgroup of order $76$ containing the proposed polarization. These computations support the arithmetic parts of the proof. They do not independently verify the geometric contraction and Torelli arguments, which must be assessed from the mathematical text and cited results.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published work examined. The supplied version of [6], hodge_conjecture_83/v2, constructs multiplier-two examples and poses the Picard-number-two question. The related hodge_conjecture_80/v2 [18] excludes a different class of trianalytic seeds, in families with a negative rank-one Néron–Severi lattice. Its double-point and positivity arguments are relevant antecedents, but do not supply the present classification for arbitrary smooth K3 surfaces in projective Picard-number-two fourfolds. Varesco [4] treats algebraicity through symplectic automorphisms and Kuga–Satake theory, rather than this exclusion. The new value lies primarily in the multiplier classification and the cohomology-versus-positivity distinction, not in the general integral-cohomology machinery.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The introduction identifies the question, states the results and explains the four proof ingredients. The main text contains the decisive reasoning, while the scripts provide accessible supporting calculations. The abstract is informative. The following improvements would strengthen attribution and precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. Cite [18] directly, especially its Lemma 3.1: the Wirtinger inequality there already implies $M(k)\ge c\,q(k)$ for Kähler $k$. Explain the relation to the different proof of Lemma 5.1 here. Also place Proposition 3.2 alongside the established integral-basis and cup-product work of Qin–Wang and Kapfer [19,20].
2. Replace the repeated “from memory” annotations with precise theorem references, particularly for the lattice splitting and uniqueness statements and the stable spherical sheaf used in Proposition 5.4. Make explicit the local-freeness input that produces the global $\mathbb P^1$-bundle.
3. In the opening discussion and abstract, distinguish the generally rational similarity multiplier from the integrality proved here for $\rho(X)\le2$. Either restrict the initial integer assertion accordingly or supply a reference for its broader scope.
4. In Proposition D, call the displayed identities the specified necessary cohomological conditions, rather than “exactly the conditions” for an embedded surface. The paper itself correctly identifies positivity as an additional necessary condition. Likewise, Remark 7.1 should emphasize that the equation loses its uniform upper bound when the number of double points is unrestricted; this does not establish geometric realizability of arbitrarily large multipliers.
