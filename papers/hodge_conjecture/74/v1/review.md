# Accept

This is a substantial partial advance on the stated problem: it proves the rational Hodge conjecture for arbitrary finite mixed products in the specified two-parameter quartic real multiplication K3 family, including special parameters, and transfers the conclusion to the specified fixed-form local trace locus. It does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture. The contribution, hypotheses and limitations are sufficiently clear, and I found no material gap in the central argument. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The decisive contribution is the algebraic realization of the missing volume classes. The argument supplies an actual correspondence identifying the source determinant with the fixed $F$-line $W_0$, checks its nonvanishing and field compatibility, and obtains an algebraic inverse by polarization and splitting. Consequently, the tensor-product determinant identity and the pairing between $W_0$ and its conjugate supply the entire rational determinant space. This addresses the missing step beyond algebraicity of the real multiplication action and the square theorem. In particular, the proof does not infer algebraicity of a determinant from algebraicity of a power of that determinant. The extension $T\otimes_E F\hookrightarrow M_{t,s}$ is justified by simplicity and the exclusion of a nonreal endomorphism of $T$. At $E$-rank three, the complementary $F$-line has divisor type, so contraction and the normalized field trace legitimately recover the required $E$-volume.

The mixed-product assertion needs more than the self-power argument, and the manuscript supplies that additional reasoning. Section 5 identifies the joint source Hodge groups and reduces their invariant tensors to determinant columns and tensors on the fixed Fermat-type carrier. Two potentially serious issues are addressed explicitly: the unique noncompact real place excludes nontrivial diagonal blocks in real degree four, and the right-character-space argument treats dependent quadratic CM extensions without assuming linear disjointness. The connected-kernel and integral-character arguments explain why a rational character-space calculation suffices for the invariant generation used here. Section 6 also supplies an actual source for the entire $a=0$ axis, including the normalized endpoint curves; the general CM self-power theorem alone would not have established these mixed cases.

The period-locus extension has the appropriate scope. The Cayley argument establishes the rational density needed in the fixed $E$-quadratic space. Extending the resulting isometry by the identity on the fixed divisor lattice permits the use of Buskin's theorem [12]. This transfers products, including Picard jumps, but makes no claim for unrelated rational quadratic forms. The manuscript consistently states this limitation.

The novelty is meaningful despite substantial methodological continuity. Paper 72, version 1 [1], constructs the same family and proves its square result, while leaving precisely the higher volume classes unresolved. Paper 65, version 1 [2], already develops the fixed-determinant and joint-group strategy for odd rotation degrees; the present paper credits that precedent and extends it to a distinct quartic real multiplication family with the necessary correspondence, field-action and boundary calculations. Paper 40, version 4 [5], provides broader determinant-supply machinery, but does not itself supply this all-parameter mixed-product conclusion. The focused external comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [3], Schlickewei [6], Varesco [7], and Varesco's work on Hodge similarities and Kuga–Satake varieties (arXiv:2304.02519) did not reveal an existing theorem already covering this result. The contribution should continue to be presented as a new geometric case and extension of an established strategy.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated role. I reran the four supplied programs: the 112 exact assertions passed, the finite common-norm models gave the reported matching rank bounds, and the reproduced JSON results agreed with the retained outputs. These checks support the equations, characters, projections and finite models; they do not independently prove the general joint-group classification or algebraicity statements. Those conclusions rest on the written arguments and cited theorems. One literature-verification limitation is that the repository serving Aoki's primary text [11] was unavailable during review; I could not independently reconsult Theorem 1.2(ii). Its use here is explicit and restricted to powers of the degree-48 Fermat Jacobian, with the carrier maps displayed.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance. The main text explains the missing classes, construction, proof strategy and separate requirements for mixed products, rather than substituting computational records for an argument. Two minor improvements would help: qualify the introductory phrase “identifies the K3 transcendental part with a rank-four tensor product” as the rank-four case, since at rank three it is a summand; and make the abstract more self-contained by describing the family geometrically instead of relying on its rStation paper number, while reducing the emphasis on certificate counts. These are presentation refinements, not obstacles to understanding or validating the contribution.
