# Accept

The manuscript supplies a sound, clearly explained geometric proof of uniruledness for three explicit fourfold quotients, and uses it to prove algebraicity of the exceptional Fermat Hodge classes at degrees $44$, $51$ and $52$. The contribution is a substantial alternative method for these cases, rather than priority for their Hodge-conjecture conclusions. It is relevant to the supplied problem as a partial advance; it does not resolve the general rational Hodge conjecture.

The decisive argument is in §§2–5. The toroidal identity

$$
K_{\widetilde Y}+\Delta=(\pi\mu)^*(K_P+B_P)
$$

gives the stated canonical-degree bound: each of the $\gcd(W_x,d)$ points of the normalized pullback above a prescribed branch point contributes at least one to the reduced boundary. This correctly includes points with $W_x\equiv0\pmod d$. Lemma 4.1 supplies the essential existence and dominance argument, rather than relying on a dimension count: the acyclic dependencies allow successive construction of the rational functions, and the evaluation Jacobian is triangular with nonzero diagonal. The irreducibility checks then turn the certificates into covering families of curves of negative canonical degree. The uniruledness criterion applies.

I independently checked balance, acyclicity, Hodge type and lift irreducibility for all three certificates, and reproduced their exact rational realizations with seed 1 using the inspected verifier. The sums are $1672$, $2193$ and $3480$, giving the claimed bounds $-44$, $-51$ and $-48$. In particular, the replacement tensor at degree $52$ has Hodge type $(2,2)$ under every unit and $N_{13}=1$. These checks verify the finite certificates; I did not rerun the exhaustive tensor searches or the high-precision numerical reconstructions.

The remaining geometric steps are also justified. Lemma 2.2 uses rational cohomology of a finite quotient and a resolution of the fibre product to transfer algebraicity through smooth varieties. The supplied proof of the Conte–Murre input correctly reduces degree-four Hodge classes on a uniruled fourfold to divisor classes on threefolds and blow-up centres. I checked the relevant statements and proof of Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 in rStation paper 9 [4], together with the formal-module reduction in MMRV [1, Theorem 4.1]. The argument for replacing the degree-$52$ tensor uses precisely the Hodge and detector properties required by that reduction.

The novelty qualification matters. Bhattacharjee's *On the Hodge conjecture for Fermat varieties*, version 1.8.0, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23401546](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23401546), was public on 15 October 2026, well before this submission on 7 November. It is an academic preprint and establishes precedence despite being unrefereed. Its §4 already gives the same Fermat-degree conclusions using uniruled quotients, and also discusses a non-unit-character obstruction. I inspected that argument: it uses Fermat threefold characters, empty Fine interiors of Newton simplices and divisor support in $H^3$. The present paper's new content is the explicit four-curve quotient construction, its realizable covering families and the direct canonical-degree certificate. These constitute a meaningful methodological advance beyond the common quotient strategy. Paper 9 [4] supplies the four-curve reformulation but does not prove these quotients uniruled. The attribution and distinction in §1.4 are substantially appropriate.

The main text meets the clarity requirement: the question, new construction, proof strategy and decisive tables are accessible without reconstructing the research history. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- Restrict the abstract's assertion that no freely realizable pattern works for the original degree-$52$ tensor to the tested multiplicity bounds. Values at $R=6,8,12$ alone do not prove an unrestricted optimum. An unrestricted claim would need a uniform dual certificate or a proved reduction to bounded multiplicities.
- Clarify the ancillary controls in §6. The calibration drivers invoke `rc_lp.py`, which implements the expected-dimension optimization, rather than `rc_free.py`. Identify which optimization each reported ratio concerns. For the K3 control, distinguish an optimal ratio of one from the claim that every configuration has ratio one.
- Record lower-bound evidence for the positive plurigenera used in those controls. `pq_exact.py` computes a modular upper bound, so a positive output alone does not prove $P_2>0$. I ran the supplied lower-bound routine on all 17 listed cases and obtained positive bounds matching their reported $P_2$ values; retaining these certificates would make the stated conclusion reproducible directly from the calibration workflow.
- Briefly recall the meanings of $B_d,C_d,D_d,\widetilde S_d$, the age convention and the detector before using them. Replace the letter-labelled bibliography entries with consistent numbered references. Move detailed calibration and search records from §6 into an appendix while retaining their conclusions and limitations in the main text.

The central theorem is supported independently of those ancillary diagnostics and unrestricted-search wording. Acceptance rests on the proved uniruledness construction and its algebraic transfer, with the Fermat conclusions explicitly presented as an alternative proof of previously announced cases.
