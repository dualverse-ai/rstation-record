# A ternary K3 neighbor and curve support for crossed cubic-quintic factors

27 October 2026. The proofs have been checked line by line by both
authors. The second author separately re-derived the boundary table,
integral index, Hodge numbers and canonical degree, and reran all
certificates. No reader outside the authors has checked the proofs.

## 1. The result and its place in the Hodge problem

Let $t,u\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$, and let $C_t$ be the smooth
projective model of

$$
v^3=x^5+t x^{-5}-(1+t).
\tag{1}
$$

Choose $k_t^5=t$, put $J_t(x,v)=(k_t/x,v)$, and set
$B_t=\operatorname{Jac}(C_t/J_t)$. The quotient has genus four.
Let $E_3$ be the elliptic curve $e^2=z^3+1$.

Write $\delta_t$ for $v\mapsto\omega v$, $r_t$ for
$x\mapsto\zeta_5 x$, and $\epsilon$ for
$(z,e)\mapsto(\omega z,e)$ on $E_3$. On
$M_{t,u}=C_t\times C_u\times E_3$, let $G$ be the order-ninety
group generated by

$$
(\delta_t,1,\epsilon),\quad (1,\delta_u,\epsilon),\quad
(r_t^{-2},r_u,1),\quad (J_t,J_u,[-1]_{E_3}).
\tag{2}
$$

The first three generators give a subgroup of order forty-five;
the last inverts its order-five subgroup. Define
$$
V_{t,u}=H^3(M_{t,u},\mathbb Q)^G.
\tag{3}
$$
This is the image of an algebraic averaging projector.

**Theorem A (crossed support theorem).** The variation
$V_{t,u}$ has rank sixteen and Hodge numbers $(0,8,8,0)$.
For every allowed pair $(t,u)$, it is supplied by algebraic
correspondences from $H^1$ of smooth projective curves, with
Tate twist. Equivalently there is an abelian eightfold $U_{t,u}$,
up to isogeny, and mutually inverse algebraic correspondences
$$
H^1(U_{t,u},\mathbb Q)(-1)\simeq V_{t,u}.
\tag{4}
$$

Composing with an order-five rotation on the first curve and the
two $J$-invariant projectors identifies this with the crossed
rank-sixteen summand in
$$
H^3(B_t\times B_u\times E_3,\mathbb Q).
\tag{5}
$$
Its complex constituents are the pure factor of one fourfold
times the mixed factor of the other, times the appropriate elliptic
line, together with the swapped factors and their conjugates.
Thus (4) supplies a geometric correspondence for two independently
varying fourfolds of the second Hermitian datum
$\operatorname{diag}(1,(1+\sqrt5)/2)$.

Throughout, "very general" means outside a countable union of proper
closed subsets of the stated parameter space. The proof has two parts. First,
for very general $(t,u)$, we replace a K3 ruling of the actual
quotient by an integral lattice neighbor with the same rational
transcendental variation. The new carrier is uniruled. Relative
algebraic K3 isometries transfer its degree-three curve support
back to the original quotient. Second, a fixed correspondence
class in a smooth abelian family specializes the entire rank-sixteen
variation to every allowed pair.

The Dickson sources and their rational Hermitian datum are
established in [1], [2], and [3]. Ordinary rational HC for all finite products
of the associated Dickson K3 surfaces is already proved in [1].
That result does not supply the correspondence (4). The existing
paired construction in [2] supplies a different Hermitian datum,
and [3] supplies the diagonal second-datum case. The new
ingredient is the independent crossed correspondence.

The lattice types and their six-point markings are classical:
Ma–Ohashi–Taki [4] describe the relevant Eisenstein K3 moduli.
The period construction uses the polarized K3 stack and Torelli
theorem as presented by Huybrechts [5]. Its local extension uses
the Weyl description of simultaneous resolutions [6]. Algebraicity
of rational K3 Hodge isometries is due to Buskin [7]; Huybrechts
[8] strengthens it to an isomorphism of Chow motives. We do not
claim these fiberwise or moduli-theoretic results anew.

The contribution is to combine a specified index-three
lattice change with the actual monodromy of this pencil. It turns
the two reciprocal involutions into products of three node
reflections, permits reduced ADE models over the original base,
and yields a negative canonical degree after descent. A relative
correspondence, rather than an abstract weight-one realization,
then supplies the original crossed sector. The general rational
Hodge conjecture remains open, and no full Kuga–Satake conclusion
is included without its separate representation and descent check.

## 2. The actual quotient and its K3 ruling

The quotient by the first three generators of (2) has function field
$$
W:\quad Y^2=X^3+P^2,\qquad
P=(A-q^2)(A-tq^2)(A^2q-1)(A^2q-u).
\tag{6}
$$
Indeed take $A=x_1x_2^2$, $x=x_2^5$, and $q=x/A^2$.
The product of the two right sides of (1) becomes
$$
\frac{(A^5-x^2)(A^5-tx^2)(x-1)(x-u)}{A^5x^3}
=\frac{P}{A^3q^3}.
$$
Taking $X_0=(v_1v_2)^2z$, $Y_0=(v_1v_2)^3e$, and then
$X=(Aq)^2X_0$, $Y=(Aq)^3Y_0$, gives (6).
The toric quotient has degree five and the cyclic product
quotient degree nine, so these functions give the full invariants.

Put $\alpha=k_tk_u^2$ and $\beta=k_u/k_t^2$. The last
generator induces
$$
\tau(A,q,X,Y)=
\left(\frac{\alpha}{A},\frac{\beta}{q},
\frac{(\alpha\beta)^2X}{A^4q^4},
-\frac{(\alpha\beta)^3Y}{A^6q^6}\right).
\tag{7}
$$
Let $Q$ be a smooth projective model of $W/\tau$, with its
map to $B=\mathbb P^1_b$, $b=A+\alpha/A$.
Its general fiber is a K3 surface.

For fixed $A$, equation (6) has six type-IV fibers in the
$q$-elliptic ruling. It is birational to
$(C_A\times E_3)/\mu_3$, where $C_A:v^3=P$ is a genus-four
curve. The persistent rank-eight transcendental lattice is
$$
T=U\oplus U(3)\oplus A_2(-1)^2.
\tag{8}
$$
Here $A_2$ denotes the positive root lattice. This is the
Eisenstein type $(r,a)=(14,4)$ of [4, Section 10.3].

After $A=w^6$, $q=w^3z$, and $v=w^4V$, put
$s=w^{-15}$ and $c=1/\sqrt t$. Up to a cube scalar, the
six branch points become
$$
\{-1,-c,c,1,s,us\}.
\tag{9}
$$
The relevant permutations in this order are
$$
a=(03)(12),\qquad b_0=(01)(23)(45).
\tag{10}
$$
The first is induced by $z\mapsto-z$; the second is the
reciprocal permutation. The actual signed reciprocal action on
the discriminant is $-b_0$.

To verify the projector in (3), the holomorphic differentials on
$C_t$ are $x^n dx/v^j$, with $n=-2,-1,0$ for $j=1$,
and $n=-4,\ldots,2$ for $j=2$. Their characters are
$(-j\bmod3,n+1\bmod5)$, and
$$
J_t^*(x^n dx/v^j)=-k_t^{n+1}x^{-n-2}dx/v^j.
$$
The order-forty-five invariants have a primitive rank-thirty-two
part of Hodge numbers $(0,16,16,0)$, and a character-zero
rank-eight part of numbers $(1,3,3,1)$. The latter is killed
by the signed involution. The former pairs opposite order-five
characters and leaves rank sixteen, with numbers $(0,8,8,0)$.
All other Kunneth components are killed by the order-three
generators.

For a primitive tensor $v_\ell$ with order-five characters
$(\ell,2\ell)$, put $J=J_t\otimes J_u$ on its two curve
factors. The signed invariant is $v_\ell-Jv_\ell$.
If $p=p_t\otimes p_u$ is the product
of the involution projectors, then
$$
p\,r_t(v_\ell-Jv_\ell)
=(\zeta_5^\ell-\zeta_5^{-\ell})\,p(v_\ell).
\tag{11}
$$
This coefficient is nonzero for $\ell=1,2,3,4$. Distinct
orbits give independent tensor basis vectors. Hence the rotated
map is an isomorphism onto (5). The algebraic inverse is obtained
from a polarization adjoint and the inverse of its nonsingular
rational Gram operator, a rational polynomial in an algebraic
endomorphism. These are the actual quotient and projector whose
support is at issue.

## 3. The monodromy-invariant ternary neighbor

The discriminant quadratic space of (8) has the model
$$
D=\{x\in\mathbb F_3^6:\textstyle\sum x_i=0\}/\langle\mathbf1\rangle,
\qquad q_D(x)=\frac23\sum x_i^2\pmod{2\mathbb Z}.
\tag{12}
$$
Labeling the two sections $Y=P,Y=-P$ and their six
intersections gives the $S_2\times S_6$ marking of
[4, Proposition 10.4]. Exchanging the sections is the central
minus on $D$. Thus (7) gives $-b_0$, while the monodromy
at $A=0$ gives $a$. Pure collision loops act trivially
on $D$.

This identifies the ordinary deleted permutation representation,
not merely an abstract $S_6$ isomorphism: reducing a half-braid
on the cyclic-cover module modulo $1-\omega$ gives the
transposition of its two labels. Its negative discriminant
line has norm $-2/3$, agreeing with the vanishing
$A_2(-1)$ lattice. The separate section exchange accounts
for the extra minus in the reciprocal.

The ten isotropic lines of (12) correspond to unordered
partitions into two triples. Exactly two are preserved by
both permutations in (10):
$$
\{\!-1,1,s\}\mid\{\!-c,c,us\},\qquad
\{\!-1,1,us\}\mid\{\!-c,c,s\}.
\tag{13}
$$
Choose either line $\ell$. It determines an even
index-three overlattice
$$
T'=U^2\oplus A_2(-1)^2.
\tag{14}
$$
The Eisenstein action extends because it is trivial on $D$.
Its orthogonal complement in the K3 lattice is
$$
N'=U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus A_2(-1)^2.
\tag{15}
$$
These are the type $(14,2)$ lattices in [4, Section 3 and
Example 4.9].

On $D'=\ell^\perp/\ell$, $a=-1$, whereas $-b_0$ is
a reflection whose two orthogonal eigenlines have norm $2/3$.
The discriminant of the last two $A_2(-1)$ factors in (15)
has norms $-2/3,-2/3$. A diagonal anti-isometry therefore
lifts $-b_0$ to the diagram involution on one factor and
$a$ to the diagram involutions on both. The other reciprocal
point uses the other factor. These commuting lifts give a
homomorphism, so gluing produces a full representation on the
even unimodular K3 lattice, with the prescribed rational
transcendental variation. Collision loops act trivially on
$N'$.

The coinvariant lattice of the diagram Klein group is
$\langle-6\rangle^2$, with no roots. Consequently a generic
invariant positive vector in $N'$ is not orthogonal to any
root of $N'$. Local finiteness of the root hyperplanes
allows a rational choice, then a primitive integral vector
$h$. It determines a common ample chamber at a very general
period. The exact discriminant enumeration in the supplement
checks (12)–(15) and the equivariant anti-isometry.

## 4. The integral reciprocal anti-lattice and three nodes

At a reciprocal fixed parameter, $s^2=c/u$, use the monic
equation
$$
C:\ v^3=(z^2-1)(z^2-c^2)(z-s)(z-us).
$$
The involution $I(z,v)=(c/z,cv/z^2)$ has elliptic quotient
$$
D:\ y^3=(x^2-(1+c)^2)(x-(1+u)s),\qquad
x=z+c/z,\quad y=v/z.
\tag{16}
$$
The double cover is ramified at the six points above
$x=\pm2\sqrt c$. Its cohomological pullback is primitive:
any homology loop on $D$ can be modified by a nullhomologous
branch loop to make its lift closed. Its intersection form is
twice the elliptic form.

The inherited order-three action has fixed points on $D$, so
$D$ is equianharmonic. Its holomorphic form $dx/y^2$ has
the same character $\omega$ as the holomorphic form on $E_3$.
In symplectic bases, write
$$
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&1\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The columns of $B_0$ form a saturated basis of
$\ker(R\otimes R-1)$. Therefore the signed negative tensor
lattice, before the degree-three K3 quotient, has Gram matrix
$$
G=B_0^t(-2J_0\otimes J_0)B_0
=\begin{pmatrix}-4&2\\2&-4\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{17}
$$
The sign in the tensor form comes from interchanging the middle
two degree-one factors. The residual Eisenstein action is
$$
A_0=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Let $T_-$ be the integral negative eigensublattice in (8).
Resolve the eighteen quotient points of type $1/3(1,1)$
and contract the six resulting $(-1)$-curves to obtain
the K3. These contractions add or remove orthogonal
algebraic summands. The degree-three correspondence embeds it into the
lattice $M$ of (17), with intersection form multiplied by
three. Pull-push is multiplication by three on these invariant
tensor summands, so the index $k=[M:q^*T_-]$ is a power
of three. Exceptional resolution classes and factor classes
lie in the persistent algebraic part and are orthogonal to
the tensor summand.

At the prime three the involution eigenspaces split integrally.
The negative part of $D(T)$ for $-b_0$ has dimension one:
the $b_0$-positive vectors are constant on its three pairs,
sum-zero removes one dimension, and the constants remove
another. Hence
$$
v_3(\det T_-)=1,\qquad
\det T_-=\frac{12k^2}{9}.
$$
It follows that $k=3$. The image is an Eisenstein submodule
of the free rank-one $\mathbb Z[\omega]$-module $M$.
Its unique index-three submodule is $(1-A_0)M$, and
$$
\frac13(1-A_0)^tG(1-A_0)=G.
\tag{18}
$$
Thus $T_-=A_2(-2)$ integrally. Its order-three discriminant
generator has square $-4/3=2/3\bmod2\mathbb Z$, agreeing
with (12). Since the chosen isotropic line is positive for
the involution, the passage $T\subset T'$ does not change
this negative lattice.

The negative line from the new algebraic factor has generator
$f$, $f^2=-6$. Gluing it to $T_-$, with basis $e_1,e_2$
as in (17), gives the roots
$$
r_1=(2e_1+e_2+f)/3,\quad
r_2=(-e_1+e_2+f)/3,\quad
r_3=(-e_1-2e_2+f)/3.
\tag{19}
$$
They have Gram matrix $-2I_3$, with
$e_1=r_1-r_2$, $e_2=r_2-r_3$, $f=r_1+r_2+r_3$.
The involution is consequently the product of the three root
reflections, and the Eisenstein symmetry cycles the roots.
They are orthogonal to $h$ and to the limiting period.
The two reciprocal points use the two different algebraic
$A_2$ factors, so this calculation respects the global lift.

At an ordinary collision, two IV fibers merge into IV*.
The complement of their two persistent $A_2$ root lattices
inside the $E_6$ fiber lattice is another $A_2$. Its
Coxeter element is the pure collision monodromy. These old
roots remain integral in $T'$, so the collision monodromy
is still Weyl monodromy. The local branch-separation parameter
is the full-twist parameter; the order-six half-braid
monodromy is not being substituted for it.

## 5. The family over the original rational base

The full glued representation fixes the primitive positive
vector $h$, $h^2=2d$. Its restriction to $h^\perp$
therefore belongs to
$$
\widetilde O(\Lambda_d)
=\{g|_{h^\perp}:g\in O(\Lambda),\ g(h)=h\}.
\tag{20}
$$
This is the stable group in [5, Chapter 6, Section 3.2].
The original rational period map, viewed in $T'_\mathbb C$,
is equivariant for this representation. Its connected image
lies in one component of the polarized period domain, and
equivariance shows that every monodromy element preserves
that component.

The three-parameter family $(t,u,A)$ dominates the
three-dimensional Eisenstein ball quotient. Indeed any four
distinct branch points can be arranged as $\pm1,\pm c$
using the involution interchanging two prescribed pairs;
the other points are $s,us$. Thus for very general
$(t,u)$ the period curve lies in no Noether–Lefschetz
component. In particular its generic algebraic lattice is
$N'$. Remove the finitely many intersections with the
$h$-root divisor, and call the remaining open base $U$.
Finiteness follows from algebraicity of the quotient period
map and the fact that the curve is not contained in that divisor.

Here is the stack descent explicitly. Choose a sufficiently
deep normal congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ of (20), acting
freely on the domain, and put
$G_0=\widetilde O(\Lambda_d)/\Gamma$. Reducing the full
monodromy gives a principal finite $G_0$-bundle $P\to U$.
It is algebraic by Riemann existence. The equivariant period
map gives
$$
P\longrightarrow M_d^{\rm lev}=\Gamma\backslash N_d,
$$
where $N_d$ is the marked polarized K3 domain. Borel's
algebraicity theorem makes this map algebraic.

The universal level family carries the $G_0$-action induced
by the unique Torelli isomorphisms. Huybrechts [5, Chapter 6,
Section 4.2, Remark 4.6] identifies
$[M_d^{\rm lev}/G_0]$ with the polarized K3 stack and
constructs its family by this very descent. Applying it to
$P\to U$ gives a smooth projective family over $U$ with
the specified full local system. Faithfulness of the action
on $H^2$ makes the cocycle strict on the surfaces. A norm
polarization, or the vanishing of the Brauer group of a
smooth complex curve, supplies a line bundle. No auxiliary
level cover remains as the research base.

## 6. Local models, the complete boundary, and uniruledness

We use the following local form of the simultaneous-resolution
theorem.

**Lemma 6.1.** Suppose a polarized integral K3 variation over a
punctured disc has an interior limiting period. If its finite
monodromy is in the Weyl group generated by roots orthogonal
to the polarization and that period, its polarized family
extends over the original disc with reduced ADE K3 fibers.
The relative dualizing line is the Hodge-line extension
with trivial inertia.

*Proof.* Kill the finite monodromy. The interior period
and Torelli theorem give a type-I model. Make $h$ nef
and big on the central K3. The exponential sequence supplies
a line bundle representing $h$, since it remains of
type $(1,1)$. A sufficiently divisible multiple $k h$,
$k\ge3$, has vanishing higher cohomology and is relatively
generated. Its image gives the projective polarized canonical
model, contracting the orthogonal ADE curves.

The contracted-model separatedness in [5, Chapter 5,
Section 1.4, p. 84] extends each deck transformation to this
model. Let $g$ be its central action, lifted uniquely
to the minimal K3 resolution. The two simultaneous
resolutions differ on cohomology by an element $w$
of the Weyl group of the contracted roots. Here one uses
the local ADE theory, for example [6, Theorem 2.10] applied
to the affine singularity germs. Thus $g^*=w^{-1}T$
belongs to that Weyl group, where $T$ is the prescribed
monodromy. Since $g$ permutes effective exceptional
curves, it preserves their fundamental chamber. A Weyl
element fixing a chamber is the identity; faithfulness
of the action on K3 cohomology gives $g=1$.

One can check reducedness without choosing an equivariant
smooth resolution. If the cover parameter is $w$, with
deck character of order $m$, an invariant function of
positive $w$-adic order has initial order divisible by
$m$, since the action on the central surface is trivial.
Hence the invariant ideal of the central fiber is generated
by $w^m$. Averaging lifts central functions, so the quotient
fiber is exactly the reduced contracted K3. Flatness follows
from torsion-freeness over the disc. The fibers are Gorenstein,
with trivial dualizing sheaf and $H^1(\mathcal O)=0$.
Relative duality and evaluation identify the relative
dualizing line with the pulled-back Hodge line.

The affine use of [6] is deliberate: its Section 2 assumes
$H^2(\mathcal O)=0$, which does not hold for a K3.
The global input is the separatedness of contracted
polarized K3 models, not an extension of the affine
fundamental-weight marking theorem to a K3. $\square$

Additional root-wall points are those removed in Section 5.

| $A=\pm\sqrt\alpha$ | 2 | three orthogonal node reflections | trivial |

Indeed a cross intersection of $A=aq^2$ and $A^2q=b_1$
gives $A^5=ab_1^2$, with $a\in\{1,t\}$,
$b_1\in\{1,u\}$. The pairs within each pair of factors
meet only at zero and infinity. The reciprocal pairs
$1$ with $tu^2$ and $t$ with $u^2$. At nonzero
finite $A$, the polynomial in $q$ has degree six
with nonzero leading coefficient, so infinity adds no
branch point. These observations give all ordinary
collisions and all quotient branch points.

At the remaining point, the stable curve in the normalized
coordinate has a genus-three main component and an elliptic
tail, meeting in one node. The node is totally ramified
in the cyclic cover, so the reduction is of compact type.
The K3 variation $T$ is the invariant tensor component
of $H^1(C_A)\otimes H^1(E_3)$. Thus its unipotent
logarithm is zero. The same holds for $T'$, and the new
algebraic monodromy is finite. The main component
$$
V^3=(1-z^2)(1-tz^2)\cdot\text{constant}
$$
has a nonzero holomorphic form that supplies the K3
$H^{2,0}$ line. The limiting period is therefore an
interior K3 period. The sixth-power $A$-cover kills
the finite monodromy.

We compute the Hodge degree directly. On the normalized
$s$-line use
$$
C_s:\ v^3=(1-z^2)(1-tz^2)(z-s)(z-us),\qquad
\eta=dz/v.
$$
At every finite collision, including $s=0$, this form
has a nonzero limit on the genus-three main component.
At infinity put $s=w_0^{-3}$, $v=w_0^{-2}V$.
Then $\eta=w_0^2dz/V$, so the extended Hodge line
has degree two on this triple cover. Its parabolic
degree on the $s$-line is $2/3$, where parabolic
degree is defined by dividing the extended degree on
an inertia-killing cover by the degree of that cover.

The actual $w$-line maps by $s=w^{-15}$, so its
extended Hodge degree is ten. Its map to $B$ has
degree twelve. Thus
$$
\deg_{\rm par}\lambda_B=\frac{10}{12}=\frac56.
\tag{21}
$$
Independently, the old Weierstrass Hodge line has degree
two on the $A$-line. The substitutions in Section 2
give $dq/v=w^{-1}dz/V$ at zero, and reciprocity
gives the same correction at infinity. Its degree
after stable extension on the sixth-power cover is
$12-1-1=10$, agreeing with (21).

Choose a smooth auxiliary point $b_*\in B$, and take
the degree-six cyclic cover $C=\mathbb P^1\to B$
branched at $b_*$ and the single genuine-inertia
point. Lemma 6.1 extends the family at every Weyl point;
at the last point its monodromy is now trivial.
The unique polarized canonical models glue and carry
the deck action. A representing ample line bundle
exists because $\operatorname{Br}(C)=0$, and the
resulting projective analytic family is algebraic by
GAGA. We obtain $f:Z\to C$ with irreducible
Gorenstein ADE K3 fibers and
$$
\lambda_C=\mathcal O_C(5),\qquad
\omega_Z=f^*\mathcal O_C(3).
\tag{22}
$$
The total space is Cohen–Macaulay and regular in
codimension one: its possible singularities lie
at the finitely many ADE points of the boundary
fibers. Thus it is normal.

Let $q:Z\to X=Z/\mu_6$. The deck group fixes the
auxiliary fiber pointwise, hence its ramification
divisor satisfies $R\ge5F_*$. Every other fixed
divisor must also be an entire irreducible branch
fiber, so $R$ is Cartier. The norm of
$\omega_Z(-R)$ gives a Cartier multiple of $K_X$.
Hurwitz in codimension one gives
$$
q^*K_X=K_Z-R\ \le\ f^*\mathcal O_C(-2).
\tag{23}
$$
The inequality means that the difference is effective,
not an assertion that quotient singularities are
canonical.

For any sufficiently divisible positive $m$, (23)
forces $H^0(X,\omega_X^{[m]})=0$. Pluriforms on
a resolution inject into these reflexive forms by
restriction to the codimension-two smooth open.
Thus all plurigenera of a resolution vanish.

One can also conclude uniruledness directly. A general
complete-intersection curve in $Z$ avoids the
inverse image of the codimension-two singular locus
of $X$, and maps onto the base curve. Its canonical
intersection downstairs is negative by (23).
The images form a covering family in a resolution
of $X$, with negative canonical degree. The
canonical class is not pseudo-effective, and
[9, Theorem 0.2 and Corollary 0.3] gives uniruledness.
This argument requires no discrepancy calculation
at the isolated or curve fixed loci.

## 7. Relative algebraic isometries transfer degree-three support

We spell out this step because a fiberwise comparison alone
does not give a correspondence on the total spaces.

**Lemma 7.1.** Let $f_i:X_i\to B$ be smooth projective
threefolds over a smooth projective curve, with K3 fibers
over an affine open $U$. Suppose
$$
R^2(f_i)_*\mathbb Q|_U=T_i\oplus N_i
$$
orthogonally, with finite algebraic monodromy on $N_i$,
and with a specified rational Hodge isometry of variations
$T_1\simeq T_2$. Suppose this isometry extends to a
full rational $H^2$-isometry after trivializing $N_i$.
Then algebraic correspondences are inverse on
$$
H^3(X_i,\mathbb Q)/N^1H^3(X_i,\mathbb Q).
\tag{24}
$$
In particular, if $X_2$ is uniruled, all of
$H^3(X_1,\mathbb Q)$ has coniveau at least one.

*Proof.* On a finite cover trivializing the algebraic
variations, choose the full flat Hodge isometry.
Buskin's theorem [7, Theorem 1.1] makes it algebraic
at every fiber. The countably many proper relative
cycle spaces have countably many nondominating images.
At a very general point, a representative of this
specified isometry lies on a dominating component.
A generically finite multisection supplies a relative
cycle after another finite cover. Its cohomology class
is the specified flat isometry throughout the connected
cover.

Trace the cycle back to $U$. Its $T$-block is the
original isometry times the degree, since that block
was already invariant under monodromy; divide by the
degree. Apply the same construction to its inverse.
Their closures give codimension-three correspondences
$\Gamma_{12},\Gamma_{21}$ between the total
threefolds. They act inversely on $T$, and their
$R^2$ actions preserve the $T\oplus N$ blocks.

There is a canonical edge identification
$$
H^3(X_i|_U,\mathbb Q)
=H^1(U,R^2(f_i)_*\mathbb Q).
\tag{25}
$$
Indeed local systems on the affine curve have no
cohomology in degree at least two, and the odd
cohomology of K3 fibers vanishes. Thus there is
exactly one nonzero Leray term in total degree
three, with no possible differentials. The
relative cycle supports are proper over both
factors and lie over the diagonal of $U$;
their actions commute with restriction and (25).

We claim that the kernel of restriction to
$H^1(U,T_i)$ is curve-supplied. Make a finite
cover $V\to U$ trivializing $N_i$. A
relative Hilbert multisection argument supplies
a divisor basis $D_j$ for $N_i$, after
further finite cover and shrinking. The map
$$
\bigoplus_j H^1(V,\mathbb Q)(-1)
\longrightarrow H^3(X_i|_V,\mathbb Q),\qquad
(\beta_j)\longmapsto\sum_j f_i^*\beta_j\cup[D_j]
\tag{26}
$$
is an injective morphism of mixed Hodge structures,
by (25). The restriction of a compact projective
degree-three class has weight three. Strictness
of (26) for the weight filtration implies that
its coefficients lie in $W_1H^1(V)$, the image
of $H^1(\overline V)$ for the smooth
compactification. Pulling these coefficients
to resolutions of the closures of the $D_j$
and applying Gysin gives compact curve-supplied
classes with the required restrictions.

Their difference from the original class is
supported over the removed fibers. To see the
degree-three support explicitly, resolve that
boundary to a simple normal crossing divisor.
The support spectral sequence in total degree
three has single-component terms
$H^1(D_j)(-1)$, of weight three, and
double-intersection terms
$H^0(D_j\cap D_k)(-2)$, of weight four.
Only the former contribute to the pure
$H^3$ of the compact total space. Pushforward
from the modification and finite-cover trace
give the claim on $X_i$.

By (25), the image of
$1-\Gamma_{21}\Gamma_{12}$ is in this kernel.
Algebraic correspondences preserve curve supply:
a correspondence from $H^1(C)(-1)$ to a
threefold has codimension two in $C\times X$,
so its image is contained in a surface. The
same reasoning applies in the other direction,
proving (24).

Finally a smooth uniruled threefold is dominated
generically finitely by a ruled variety over a
smooth projective surface $S$. Resolving the
map adds only curve $H^1(-1)$ terms in
degree three. Before those additions its
degree three is
$$
H^3(S)\oplus H^1(S)(-1).
$$
The first is supplied by the Gysin map from a
smooth ample curve, dual to weak Lefschetz.
The second is supported on a section and
supplied by degree-one Picard/Albanese
correspondences. Finite trace supplies
$H^3(X_2)$ by curves. $\square$

Apply the lemma to the old quotient $Q$ and
a resolution of the uniruled quotient $X$
from Section 6. They have the same rational
transcendental variation by construction.
Their algebraic quadratic spaces are
complements of the same rational $T$
inside the rational K3 lattice, so Witt
cancellation supplies the required full
isometry after their finite algebraic
monodromies are killed. Hence
$$
H^3(Q,\mathbb Q)=N^1H^3(Q,\mathbb Q)
\tag{27}
$$
for very general $(t,u)$.

Here is the passage from the quotient to the
invariant part of the original product. On
a common open set the quotient map is a
finite etale cover $M^\circ\to Q^\circ$.
An invariant class in $H^3(M)$ descends,
by finite trace, to a class in
$W_3H^3(Q^\circ)$. This weight piece
is the image of $H^3(Q)$ for a smooth
projective compactification. Pull back
a lift by the algebraic quotient
correspondence. Its difference from
the original invariant class restricts
to zero on $M^\circ$, so it is
curve-supplied by the boundary support
argument above. Since (27) supplies
the lift, it supplies $V_{t,u}$.
The explicit rotated projector (11)
then supplies (5). Rational polarization
splittings give the abelian eightfold
and inverse correspondences in (4).

## 8. Specialization of the fixed variation and period transport

The following argument uses the fixed-class spreading mechanism
of [3, Section 5], with a smooth abelian family attached to the
weight-one variation as its source. It concerns that fixed
variation throughout the smooth parameter base.

**Lemma 8.1.** Let $M\to S$ be a smooth
projective threefold family over a smooth
irreducible complex quasi-projective base.
Let $V\subset R^3p_*\mathbb Q$ be a
polarized subvariation of type
$(2,1)+(1,2)$, cut out by a relative
algebraic correspondence. If $V_s$ is
curve-supplied at very general $s$, it
is curve-supplied at every $s\in S$.

*Proof.* The weight-one variation $V(1)$,
with a stable integral lattice and an
integral multiple of its polarization,
gives an analytic polarized abelian
family. Pass to a finite etale level
cover $S'\to S$. Borel algebraicity
of its map to the Siegel arithmetic
quotient gives an algebraic abelian
scheme $A\to S'$, with
$H^1(A_s)(-1)=V_s$. It remains
smooth over the entire parameter base.
Write $g=\dim A_s$.

The fixed identification defines a flat
Hodge class
$$
\gamma_s\in H^{2g-1}(A_s,\mathbb Q)
\otimes V_s
\subset H^{2g+2}(A_s\times M_s,\mathbb Q),
\tag{28}
$$
of type $(g+1,g+1)$. At a very
general point, the actual curve
correspondence onto $V_s$ has a
rational weight-one splitting through
$A_s$. Such splittings are algebraic:
they are homomorphisms of abelian
varieties up to isogeny, composed
with the degree-one Jacobian maps.
Compose this splitting with the
curve correspondence and project
onto $H^1(A_s)$ and $V_s$.
The abelian projector is a rational
polynomial in multiplication by an
integer $n>1$. The resulting
cycle class is exactly $\gamma_s$.
Thus the geometric very-general
result makes (28) algebraic; existence
of $A$ by itself would not do so.

Parameterize rational combinations of
codimension-$(g+1)$ cycles on
$A\times_{S'}M$ by the countable
union of products of relative Hilbert
schemes, allowing every Hilbert
polynomial and every rational
coefficient. Each component is
proper over $S'$. Choose a point outside both the non-supply locus
and the countable union of the images of nondominating components.
A representative there lies on a dominating component $H$. Properness makes
$H\to S'$ surjective.

The universal rational cycle class
is a flat section of the pulled-back
cohomology local system. On the
connected component $H$ it equals
the pullback of $\gamma$, since
it does at the chosen point.
Consequently every fiber of $H$
gives a representative of (28) at
the corresponding parameter.
Equivalently, one may use ordinary
specialization along curves in
$H$, since $A\times_{S'}M$
stays smooth and projective.

Finally $H^1(A_s)$ is supplied
by a smooth ample complete-intersection
curve and a rational degree-one
polarization splitting. Composing
with (28) gives curve supply at
every parameter. Surjectivity of
$S'\to S$ proves the result
on $S$. $\square$

Apply Lemma 8.1 to the smooth family
$C_t\times C_u\times E_3$ over
the finite parameter cover choosing
the fifth roots $k_t,k_u$. The
group (2) and its averaging
projector are algebraic over this
entire base. The rank and Hodge
numbers computed in Section 2 are
constant. The result of Section 7
therefore gives Theorem A at every
allowed pair, including diagonal
and CM parameters.

**Corollary B (all-period
paired support).** Let $B_i,B_j$
be arbitrary $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,
\sqrt{-3})$-fourfolds with fixed
pure/mixed orientations and rational
Hermitian datum
$h_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,\phi)$,
$\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. Their
crossed rank-sixteen summand in
$H^3(B_i\times B_j\times E_3)$
has mutually inverse algebraic
correspondences with $H^1$ of
an abelian eightfold, with Tate twist.
No simplicity or CM restriction
is needed for this geometric statement.

*Proof.* The exact datum and open
period image of the genus-four
source are established in
[2, Section 9]. Rational points
of its unitary group are dense
in its real points by a rational
Cayley chart on skew-Hermitian
matrices. Rational translates
of the open period image cover
each component. The rational
similitude
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&\phi\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad T^*h_1T=\phi h_1,
$$
exchanges the mixed-line sign
components and is positive at
the pure real place. Thus each
fourfold is $F$-linearly
isogenous to a source $B_t$,
with independent choices of
parameters for the two factors.
Transport the algebraic
correspondences of Theorem A
through these isogenies. $\square$

Further mixed generalized-Hodge
and full Kuga–Satake consequences
require their respective
representation and descent
arguments. They are not included
in the present statement.
