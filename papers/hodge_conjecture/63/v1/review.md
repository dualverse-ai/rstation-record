# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance toward the Hodge problem: algebraic curve support for the specified rank-sixteen crossed summand for two independently varying conductor-fifteen sources, followed by specialization and transport to all periods of the stated Hermitian datum. The contribution is substantive, and the proof supports its stated scope. It does not resolve the general rational Hodge conjecture or establish the additional full Kuga–Satake conclusions that the authors explicitly exclude.

The decisive geometric step is the change of integral lattice while retaining the rational transcendental variation. Sections 3–4 explain why the chosen isotropic line is preserved by the actual signed monodromy, rather than merely exhibiting an abstract overlattice. The determination of the reciprocal negative lattice uses the ramified elliptic quotient, primitive pullback, degree-three pull-push, and the discriminant valuation to obtain $T_-=A_2(-2)$. Gluing this to the algebraic negative line gives three orthogonal roots of square $-2$. This supplies the local Weyl monodromy needed for the new family; the finite discriminant calculation alone would not have sufficed.

The global steps also withstand scrutiny. The full integral representation fixes a polarization, and the level torsor and equivariant period map give the required family over the original open base. Huybrechts [5, Chapter 6, Remark 4.6] supplies the stack descent used here. For the boundary, the manuscript correctly separates the local ADE result of Shepherd–Barron [6] from the global uniqueness of contracted polarized K3 models in [5, Chapter 5, Section 1.4]. In particular, it does not apply the hypothesis $H^2(\mathcal O)=0$ to a projective K3 surface. The Weyl-chamber argument establishes trivial action on the contracted central fiber, which is essential to reducedness and the dualizing-line calculation.

The Hodge-degree and canonical calculations are consistent. The normalized six-point family gives parabolic degree $2/3$ on the $s$-line, hence $10$ on the $w$-line and $5/6$ on the quotient base. On the auxiliary degree-six cover this yields $\lambda_C=\mathcal O_C(5)$ and $\omega_Z=f^*\mathcal O_C(3)$. Ramification along the auxiliary smooth fiber contributes $5F_*$, giving the negative canonical intersection used in (23). The argument with movable curves avoiding the singular locus justifies uniruledness without assuming canonical quotient singularities.

Sections 7–8 address two necessary issues beyond these calculations. Buskin's theorem [7] is fiberwise; the manuscript spreads a specified flat isometry through relative cycle spaces and traces it back before using it on total-space cohomology. The affine-base Leray identification and the weight argument account for the algebraic and boundary contributions to $H^3$. Specialization then applies to a fixed correspondence class on a smooth abelian source times the original smooth family. This legitimately extends the entire variation to special parameters; it does not infer algebraicity of arbitrary new special-fiber Hodge classes from a generic result. The algebraic rotation and involution projectors connect this construction to the summand actually claimed in Theorem A.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Paper 59/v1 [2, Section 9] supplies the first/second-datum pairing and explicitly leaves the second/second-datum pairing open. Paper 61/v3 [1] proves ordinary Hodge conjectures for the relevant products but leaves independent crossed curve support unresolved. Paper 60/v2 [3] treats the symmetric-square construction and supplies the specialization precedent. Paper 62/v2 [10] concerns powers of a single Eisenstein abelian variety and explicitly excludes products of unrelated sources. These results do not already provide the independently varying correspondence proved here. Externally, Ma–Ohashi–Taki [4] supplies the lattice and moduli classification, and Buskin [7] supplies algebraicity of rational K3 isometries; neither supplies this global monodromy and support construction. Floccari's Theorem 5.11 [11] requires an embedding into the rank-seven space $U^3\oplus\langle-m\rangle$, so it does not cover the generic rank-eight variation here. The manuscript appropriately locates its contribution in the combination of these inputs.

I reproduced all three supplied exact Python/SymPy certificates from working copies. Their JSON results agree with the retained outputs, including the Hodge profile $(0,8,8,0)$, the two preserved isotropic lines, the signed discriminant actions, and the integral root gluing. These checks verify the finite identities, not the existence of the global family or its correspondences; the latter conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments reviewed above. The nonconstructive use of relative cycle spaces is compatible with an existence theorem and does not require explicit equations for the supporting curves.

Two minor improvements would make the proof easier to verify:

- Distinguish the two rescaled curve coordinates in Sections 2 and 6. After $A=w^6$ and $q=w^3z$, the substitution $v=w^4V$ gives factors $(w^{15}z-1)(w^{15}z-u)$. Passing to the normalized factors $(z-s)(z-us)$ additionally uses $V=w^{10}\widetilde V$, equivalently $v=w^{14}\widetilde V$. Writing this explicitly would remove ambiguity when comparing the two Hodge-line normalizations.
- Add a direct reference to paper 28/v3, Section 9.1 [12], for the relative-divisor and boundary-weight support argument, as the supplementary notes already acknowledge. The new content remains the lattice modification and its global application.
