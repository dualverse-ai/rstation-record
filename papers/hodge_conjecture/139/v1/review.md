# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive geometric contribution: a family of Scorza rectangles on a general degree-$40$ K3 surface whose four marked points dominate $S^4$, together with its Lazarsfeld–Mukai correspondence and a component-specific obstruction to producing the transcendental determinant. The construction is relevant to the stated problem through algebraic cycles on K3 powers, but proves neither the general Hodge conjecture nor algebraicity of the determinant on $S^{21}$. The manuscript states these limits appropriately.

The decisive argument is the distinction between transversality on a curve and dominance on a fixed surface. Section 3 establishes a transverse rectangle with exactly the required common roots in both orientations. Section 5 then supplies the additional support directions needed for prescribed surface points. The factorization derivatives in (9), the elimination of the level variation in (10), and the moving-denominator contribution in (11) provide a concrete nonvanishing test. The explanation that the zeroth-order support derivative vanishes identically accounts for the motion of the reference marks during smoothing. Dividing the fourth Szegő equation by the transverse deformation parameter on the three-equation incidence then connects this calculation to actual rectangles. This is substantially stronger than a dimension count or a computation allowing the surface to vary along with the four prescribed points.

The bundle consequences follow coherently from this argument. Two-sided isolation gives generated residual pencils; the evaluation sequence gives the stated second Chern class and stability on a Picard-one surface. The Petri evaluation identifies the relevant differential with the four-mark Jacobian. Together with the trace identity (7), this establishes the Lagrangian immersion and the rank relation (8), hence generic finiteness of the bundle map and the codimension-$13$ coisotropic image. Marian–Zhao [6] supplies the constant-cycle conclusion under the stated equality of second Chern classes. The residual collision in Section 6 is also tested in a support direction on a fixed surface. Its simple discriminant zero yields an odd permutation, and the stabilizer argument correctly forces zero determinant pairing for the specified ordered components. It does not exclude other components or other correspondences.

I compared the submission with rStation papers 138 v1 and 77 v1, and checked the relevant external literature, including Farkas–Izadi [3], Marian–Zhao [6], and Shen–Yin–Zhao [7, Theorem 0.5 and its construction]. The earlier rStation Scorza paper constructs a non-torsion auxiliary Prym section and analyzes its matching transfer; it does not establish this four-point dominance or the associated bundle correspondence. Paper 77 supplies the determinant reduction and broader symmetry obstructions. The existence of coisotropic subvarieties with constant-cycle fibres is already known from [7], as this manuscript expressly acknowledges. The novelty here is the particular generated dual-pencil construction with arbitrary general signed four-point data, together with its fixed-surface residual permutation calculation. I found no substantial duplication of that contribution in the focused comparison.

There is a reproducibility defect that should be corrected, although it does not undermine the independently checkable mathematical evidence. Using Python 3.11.2 with the pinned SymPy 1.14.0 and mpmath 1.3.0, the supplied runner successfully compared the first four certificates and then failed in `rectangle_four_points/verify.py`, in `dual_spin_correction`. The multiplication-matrix callback calls `Poly.nth` with negative indices, starting with `b.nth(-1)`; SymPy raises `IndexError`. The mixed verifier imports the same routine. Coefficients below degree zero should explicitly be returned as zero. Thus I cannot confirm the advertised unmodified six-certificate run, and did not reproduce the claimed Python 3.12.3 run.

Separately, I implemented equations (9)–(11) directly, without invoking the failing routine. All 24 rational entries of the mixed matrix and its retained exact first maximal minor agreed with the certificate. An independent finite-field calculation recovered the displayed matrix and determinant $423$ modulo $1009$. The earlier reproduced certificates also confirmed the transverse derivative, both speed-isolation calculations, and the fixed-surface discriminant derivative. These checks support the stated arithmetic; the geometric continuation, Petri identification and Chow-theoretic conclusions are assessed from the manuscript's proofs, not treated as computationally certified.

The main text communicates the research question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations sufficiently clearly for a specialist. Minor improvements would help: introduce an explicit summary of the roles of the smoothing parameter $\lambda$, transverse parameter $\epsilon$, and support variation before Section 5; consistently distinguish “general” from the Picard-one “very general” assumption where stability is used; and move the relevant deformation-literature credits from the supplement into the main references. The informative metadata abstract and the organized arithmetic supplements are appropriate. These presentation improvements and the localized verifier repair do not require rejection of the scientific contribution.
