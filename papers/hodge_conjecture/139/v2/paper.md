# Scorza rectangles and a coisotropic correspondence for degree-forty K3 surfaces

## 1. Four points and a stable bundle

Let $(S,H)$ be a general primitively polarized complex K3 surface with $H^2=40$, and write $B=M_H(2,H,2)$ for the moduli space with the indicated Mukai vector. It is a holomorphic symplectic variety of dimension $34$ [1,2]. We ask whether four general points $p,r,k,q$ of $S$ can simultaneously prescribe two pencils on one smooth curve in $|H|$ and the second Chern class of a stable bundle. Our starting point is the Scorza correspondence of an ineffective even spin structure [3]. The two-pole construction in [4] gave an infinite-order point in an auxiliary Prym. Here two different centres must share two specified roots on the same curve.

For a smooth genus-$21$ curve $C$ with $\theta^2=K_C$ and $H^0(\theta)=0$, denote by $Z_p$ the unique effective divisor of $\theta(p)$. Its degree is $21$. We call $(p,r,k,q)$ a *Scorza rectangle* when the four marks are distinct and $k,q$ belong to both $Z_p$ and $Z_r$. By Serre duality the incidence is reciprocal. Our rectangles come with an ordering of the two centres and of the two common roots.

**Theorem 1.** On a component dominating degree-$40$ polarized K3 moduli, a general fixed surface has a $21$-dimensional family $R$ of rectangles. The map $e:R\to S^4$ given by the four marks is dominant. At a general rectangle the two common roots are simple and exhaust $Z_p\cap Z_r$. The two common centres likewise exhaust $Z_k\cap Z_q$. The two degree-$20$ line bundles
$$
M=\theta(p+r-k-q),\qquad N=K_C\otimes M^{-1}
$$
are generated pencils. On the very general fibre, where the Picard group is generated by $H$, the Lazarsfeld–Mukai bundle $E$ of $M$ defines a generically finite map $g:R\to B$ onto its image. With $o_S$ the Beauville–Voisin zero-cycle of degree one [5], it satisfies
$$
c_2(E)=20o_S+[p]+[r]-[k]-[q]\quad\hbox{in }\mathrm{CH}_0(S).
$$
The correspondence $(g,e)$ is generically a Lagrangian immersion for the difference of the Mukai form and the signed form $\sigma_1+\sigma_2-\sigma_3-\sigma_4$ on $S^4$, after choosing the scalar of the Mukai form. The closure of $g(R)$ is coisotropic of codimension $13$. Images of general fibre components over $S^4$ give algebraic characteristic leaves of dimension $13$ and have constant point class in $\mathrm{CH}_0(B)_{\mathbb Q}$.

The statement about leaves allows a finite cover in their parametrization. The point-class conclusion uses Marian–Zhao [6]. Shen–Yin–Zhao already construct algebraically coisotropic subvarieties with constant-cycle general fibres in every codimension of these moduli spaces [7, Theorem 0.5]. Their construction uses a correspondence with a Hilbert scheme and constant-cycle curves. Our assertion identifies a particular family of generated dual pencils with arbitrary general **signed** four-point data, proves dominance of that incidence, and supplies the associated finite covers by zeros of sections.

A second conclusion concerns the rational Hodge problem for K3 powers. On a very general Picard-one surface the Hodge group of the rank-$21$ transcendental space $T$ is $\mathrm{SO}(T)$. Invariant theory leaves its volume tensor in $\bigwedge^{21}T$ as the additional generator beyond pairings [8,9]. On the variety of ordered roots of $\theta(p)$ a horizontal divisor carrying this character would have the required dimension. Rectangles produce actual horizontal divisors there. We prove that the tested fixed-surface component allows an odd permutation of its nineteen residual roots. Each component of its full ordering over that fixed-surface component therefore has zero proper pairing with the volume tensor. The algebraicity of the volume tensor remains open.

## 2. The hyperelliptic support and its transverse variation

Let $Q=\mathbb P^1_x\times\mathbb P^1_y$ and let a K3 surface double-cover $Q$ with smooth branch of bidegree $(4,4)$. For $H=\pi^*\mathcal O_Q(10,1)$, the inverse images of graphs $y=P(x)$ of degree ten are members of $|H|$. This ample class is primitive. An invariant line bundle on the double cover can be linearized and its stabilizer character made trivial on the connected ramification curve by the common sign. It then descends to $Q$. If $H$ were divisible in the torsion-free Picard group, its divisor would be invariant and divisibility would hold for $(10,1)$ below.

Temporarily let the branch factor as $UV$ with both factors of bidegree $(2,2)$. Require its restriction to the graph to have $44$ distinct roots. Put $A=U(x,P)$, $B_0=V(x,P)$, and write the smooth curve above the graph as
$$
w^2=A B_0,\qquad z=w/A.
$$
Here $B_0$ denotes a polynomial and $B$ denotes the bundle moduli space. With $W_A$ the reduced branch divisor above $A$ and $F_\infty$ the fibre over infinity, $\theta=\mathcal O(W_A-F_\infty)$ is an ineffective theta characteristic. A meromorphic frame $s$ squares to $dx/z$, of divisor $2W_A-2F_\infty$. On the double cover of the $x$-line the generators $1,z$ give the direct image of $\mathcal O(W_A)$ as $\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O$. This follows at a branch point from $z$ having a simple pole over $A$ and a simple zero over $B_0$, and at infinity from its two distinct ordinary values. Twisting by $-F_\infty$ proves ineffectivity.

In these frames the coefficient of the normalized Szegő kernel is
$$
K(i,j)=\frac{z(i)+z(j)}{2(x(i)-x(j))}.
$$
The frame cancels the apparent poles at $W_A$ and infinity. On the conjugate diagonal the numerator cancels the denominator. The diagonal pole has unit normalized residue, up to the fixed convention for its variable. These properties characterize the kernel by acyclicity of $\theta$. Thus for an ordinary centre $p$, $Z_p$ is the $z=-z(p)$ fibre with the hyperelliptic conjugate of $p$ removed. The formula persists for any partition into two groups of $22$ distinct branch points on the graph, after choosing local normalized factors.

We need a variation of this formula that leaves the double-cover locus of K3 surfaces. Let $L=\mathcal O_Q(-2,-2)$. For a smooth branch $\Psi$ with $w^2=\Psi(x,y)$ the anti-invariant vector fields on the K3 push down to $T_Q\otimes L$. A pair $(a,b)$ lifts to the field with components $wa,wb,(a\Psi_x+b\Psi_y)/2$ in $x,y,w$. On the product Čech cover, generators of $H^1(T_Q\otimes L)$ are $c_x=(1/y,0)$ and $c_y=(0,1/x)$. Contraction with $dx\wedge dy/w$ identifies them with the classes $H_y,-H_x$ of the two rulings. Their combination $c_x+10c_y$ pairs trivially with $10H_x+H_y$, so is a polarized K3 tangent.

On a graph $y=P(x)$ its tangent component in $H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-20))$ is $[1/P]$. Indeed the tangent sequence twisted by $L$ splits by projection to the $x$-line. The normal component $-P'/P+10/x$ has total residue zero in $H^1(\mathcal O(-2))$, and $H^0(\mathcal O(-2))=0$. The anti-invariant lift to the pair therefore has the stated tangent component. It is represented upstairs by $v=(w/P)\partial_x$, with $w$ differentiated through the curve equation. An invariant component of a change of lift stays within the hyperelliptic family, where the branches compared below coincide. Polarized K3 deformations are unobstructed and $H^1(S,H)=0$ lets us lift the graph sections over an actual deformation. All these differential assertions are made on smooth double covers. The initial product branch will supply exact curve values and will be smoothed before the transverse deformation.

Here is the kernel variation in a common coordinate chart outside the poles of $1/P$. Choose disjoint discs containing those poles and no marked points or branch points. On the overlap use the infinitesimal coordinate change $x\mapsto x+\epsilon v$. Since $s^2=dx/z$, its action on a local spin coefficient is
$$
D_v f=vf'+\tfrac12(v'-vz'/z)f.
$$
For fixed exterior marks $i,j$, the variation of $K(i,j)$ is the sum of the residues of $K(i,X)D_v K(X,j)\,dX/z(X)$ at the interior poles on both sheets. Indeed the Cauchy kernel reproduces precisely the prescribed principal part of the Čech jump. Its pole at the exterior variable has residue $-1$ in the integration variable. Subtracting the jump gives a regular interior section, and uniqueness follows from $H^0(\theta)=H^1(\theta)=0$. The normalized diagonal principal part stays fixed in the exterior chart. Integrating the derivative term by parts gives half the Wronskian in the two kernel factors with multiplier $v/z=A/P$. Substitution of the two opposite sheet values of $z$ then gives
$$
\dot K(i,j)=-\frac{i-j}{4}
\sum_{P(\alpha)=0}\operatorname{Res}_{X=\alpha}
\frac{B_0(X)+z(i)z(j)A(X)}{P(X)(X-i)^2(X-j)^2}\,dX .
\tag{1}
$$
In rational expressions the marks denote their $x$-coordinates. At a multiple zero of $P$ take the residue of the complete principal part once. Equation (1) also follows directly by expanding the Wronskian with $z^2=B_0/A$. Its antisymmetry and regularity at the exterior diagonal agree with the normalized variation.

## 3. A transverse rectangle on a varying support

On a regular level $t=z^2$ fix two positive-sheet marks $k,q$ with coordinates $a,b$. Their twenty possible negative-sheet centres have coordinates in the same level fibre, excluding $a,b$. Write $c,d$ for two of these centre coordinates. All marks here have $z'\ne0$. For a centre $e$ solve the two Szegő equations with $k$ fixed and let $V_e$ be the resulting velocity of $x(q)$. At each zero the derivative of the kernel denominator drops out, giving $z'(e)\dot e+z'(j)\dot j=-2(e-j)\dot K(e,j)$. Subtraction eliminates $\dot e$. Applied twice to (1), the difference of two squares of reciprocal functions yields
$$
 V_c-V_d=-\frac{(c-d)(b-a)}{z'(b)}\,H,\qquad
 H=\sum_{P(\alpha)=0}\operatorname{Res}_{X=\alpha}
 \frac{B_0(X)-tA(X)}{P(X)(X-a)(X-b)(X-c)(X-d)}\,dX .
\tag{2}
$$
The numerator vanishes at all four marks. Writing $D=\prod_{e=a,b,c,d}(X-e)$, $F_t=B_0-tA$ and using monic $P$ of degree ten,
$$
H=[X^9]\big((F_t/D)\bmod P\big).
\tag{3}
$$
Indeed the sum is the coefficient of $X^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion of $F_t/(DP)$ at infinity.

Take $P=X^{10}$ and the restrictions of
$$
 U=x^2y^2+x+1,\qquad
 V=U+(x^2-1)(y+1)(y-1024)+(x^2-4)(y-1).
$$
Thus $A=X^{22}+X+1$ and at $t=1$
$$
F=(X^2-1)(X^{10}+1)(X^{10}-1024)+(X^2-4)(X^{10}-1).
$$
Use $(a,b,c,d)=(1,2,-1,-2)$. The degree-$22$ polynomials $A,B_0,F$ are squarefree, $A,B_0$ are coprime, and the level avoids the origin, branch and infinity. These conditions are simple polynomial gcds in the supplement. The quotient $F/D$ is even, hence $H(1)=0$. Differentiating with $e'=A(e)/F'(e)$ in the four moving factors gives
$$
 H_t(1)=-148876307/901578267.
\tag{4}
$$
For verification one can divide $-A-D'(F/D)$ by $D$, where $D'$ denotes the level derivative, and take its degree-nine coefficient. The supplement gives an independent derivative by expansion at infinity.

We also check all other centres. Put $J=F/((X-a)(X-b))$. For a root $e$ of $J$, its velocity is an affine function with common nonzero slope of
$$
 L(e)=[X^9]\frac{J(X)}{X-e}.
$$
This follows by subtracting the expressions for $V_e$ from (2) and using the difference of the two simple reciprocal functions. The exact identities are $L(c)=L(d)=2$ and $\gcd(J,L-2)=(X+1)(X+2)$ using the polynomial representative of $L$. The resultant giving the other eighteen values is squarefree and omits $2$. For the reversed endpoint pair, $J_{\mathrm{rev}}(X)=J(-X)$ since $F$ is even. Substitution in the odd-index quotient coefficient gives $L_{\mathrm{rev}}(e)=L(-e)$. The same isolation holds in both directions.

Smooth the branch in the pencil from $UV$ to $1+x^4+y^4+2x^4y^4$. The endpoint is smooth in all four product charts. Its four corner coefficients are nonzero and the determinant of their two-by-two array is nonzero, which excludes a common zero of the equation and both partial derivatives off the axes. A zero on an axis has a nonzero derivative in the other coordinate. The endpoint restriction to the graph is squarefree. General pencil members arbitrarily close to zero thus give smooth K3 surfaces. On a neighbourhood of the initial graph the $44$ simple restricted branch points continue with their partition and the two normalized factors. By (4) the simple zero of $H$ persists in the level parameter and so does the two-sided isolation.

Fix one of these smooth K3 surfaces and integrate the transverse polarized tangent of §2 with curve sections lifted over it. Write $\epsilon$ for its parameter. The nearby local graphs $x(q)_c(x(k),\epsilon)$ and $x(q)_d(x(k),\epsilon)$ over the first mark exist by simplicity of the roots in both equations. They agree identically for $\epsilon=0$. Their difference divided by $\epsilon$ has a simple zero in $x(k)$ by (2), (4) and $dt/dx(k)\ne0$. Consequently for small nonzero $\epsilon$ they meet transversely. All remaining branches have different velocities at this zero. The proper incidence of triples over a smooth projective curve counts all possible intermediate centres at the limiting pair, so no additional branches enter the neighbourhood. The argument with the endpoints reversed excludes any third common root of the two centres. This proves the two-sided assertion of Theorem 1 on an ordinary nonempty open.

The four Szegő equations on four marks of the fixed smooth curve have invertible Jacobian at the transverse meeting. The marked incidence is consequently étale locally over the space of spin supports. This gives a component dominating that space, with dimension $21$ on a general fixed K3. In §5 we prove dominance of the evaluation to the fixed $S^4$.

## 4. Petri evaluation and the symplectic correspondence

Let $E_p=Z_p-k-q$, and define $E_r$ similarly. The meromorphic spin sections $s_p,s_r$ with the two indicated poles span $H^0(\theta(p+r))$. Riemann–Roch and ineffectivity give dimension two, and the poles give independence. Both sections vanish at $k$ and $q$, so they also give a basis of $H^0(M)$. Their divisors as sections of $M$ are $E_p+r$ and $E_r+p$. The isolation on both sides in §3 means these are disjoint. In particular neither original centre belongs to its own root divisor, since the unique section has nonzero principal part there. The same argument for $k,q$ proves that the residual pencil $N$ is generated.

Use Lazarsfeld's kernel construction [10, §1]. If $i:C\hookrightarrow S$, then $E=\ker(H^0(M)\otimes\mathcal O_S\to i_*M)^*$ is locally free and
$$
0\longrightarrow H^0(M)^*\otimes\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow E
 \longrightarrow i_*N\longrightarrow0 .
\tag{5}
$$
It is generated by four sections as $H^1(\mathcal O_S)=0$ and $N$ is generated. It has $c_1(E)=H$ and $c_2(E)$ is the zero-cycle on $S$ of a pencil fibre of $M$. For the latter assertion, a section from the two-dimensional left term of (5) vanishes where its line coincides with the kernel of the restricted evaluation on $C$. Locally the two-column matrix has determinant a simple support equation and rank one on the smooth curve, so its vanishing scheme is the corresponding fibre divisor on $C$. Since $2[Z_p]=H^2+2[p]$ as zero-cycles pushed to $S$, the fibre $E_p+r$ gives the asserted rational Chow formula by [5]. The degree-zero group of a complex K3 is torsion-free by Roitman’s theorem [13], since its Albanese variety is zero. Thus $i_*c_1(\theta)=20o_S$ and the same formula holds integrally. We thank lelong_ridge for pointing out this integral refinement. The dual evaluation kernel of $E$ is the bundle associated with $N$, by comparing (5) with the evaluation sequence of $N$.

For a Picard-one fibre $E$ is slope-stable. Indeed $H^0(E(-H))=H^0(E^*)=0$ by the defining evaluation sequence. A rank-one subsheaf of slope at least $\mu(E)$ would give a nonzero section of $E(-nH)$ for $n\ge1$, extending over the complement of its locally free locus by reflexivity. Multiplication by a section of $(n-1)H$ gives a contradiction. The Mukai vector is $(2,H,2)$ with square $32$.

Write $V=H^0(E)$ and $W=H^0(M)^*\subset V$. For fixed $E$, deforming $W$ in $\operatorname{Gr}(2,V)$ varies $C$ by the determinant of its two sections. The differential in $H^0(C,K_C)$ is Petri multiplication
$$
 W^*\otimes (V/W)=H^0(M)\otimes H^0(N)\longrightarrow H^0(K_C)
\tag{6}
$$
up to choosing the determinant frame of $W$. Varying one column at a time gives (6) directly. Along $C$ its adjugate supplies the coordinates of the kernel line of $W\to E_x$, or equivalently the two sections of $M$. The replacement column projects through (5) to the specified section of $N$.

In bases $s_p,s_r$ and $s_k,s_q$, (6) gives four differentials $s_i s_j$ with $i\in\{p,r\}$ and $j\in\{k,q\}$. Their evaluations at the four marks have a geometric interpretation. At $i$, the pole of $s_i$ cancels the zero of $s_j$, giving the derivative with respect to $i$ of the reversed kernel equation $K(j,i)=0$. At $j$, the derivative is taken in $j$ in $K(i,j)=0$. The product vanishes at the other two marks. Normalized spin frames show that the evaluation matrix is the transposed four-equation Jacobian from §3 up to row and column units and reversal of the $p,r$ signs, since $K(j,i)=-K(i,j)$. It is invertible. This computation only uses a local expression of the meromorphic kernel and therefore applies on the nearby nonhyperelliptic rectangles as well.

A tangent to $R$ killed by both $dg$ and $de$ trivializes $E$ to first order and fixes all four marks on $S$. Scalar changes of the trivialization preserve $W$. The associated curve variation therefore vanishes at the four marks and (6) forces $\dot W=0$. Then $\dot C=0$, which kills the tangent since $R$ is étale over its support. This proves immersivity.

Normalize the symplectic form $\omega_B$ by the Mukai map on the primitive surface part [1,2]. A quasi-universal family with its similitude divided out gives this map via $c_2$ up to the fixed scalar of $\omega_B$. Contributions from rank and $c_1$ pair trivially with $\sigma$. By Mumford's trace theorem for rationally equivalent zero-cycles [11], the formula for $c_2(E)$ gives
$$
 g^*\omega_B=e^*(\sigma_1+\sigma_2-\sigma_3-\sigma_4).
\tag{7}
$$
It suffices to apply the trace theorem on a local family of regular zero schemes of the chosen sections. The equality of holomorphic forms holds on the ordinary open. Thus the immersed tangent space $L$ of dimension $21$ is Lagrangian in the product of the two symplectic tangent spaces of dimensions $34$ and $8$. Since $L=L^\perp$, its intersection with the second factor is the symplectic annihilator of the second projection. Consequently
$$
 \operatorname{rank}(dg)=13+\operatorname{rank}(de).
\tag{8}
$$

The bundle also yields a finite correspondence retaining the nineteen other roots. With $K_E=\ker(V\otimes\mathcal O_S\to E)$, consider $\mathbb P_S(K_E)\to\mathbb P(V)$ in the convention of lines. Every nonzero section of $E$ has a finite zero locus by the same $H^0(E(-H))$ vanishing. It is a regular zero scheme of length $20$. The proper map is finite flat by the Cohen–Macaulay flatness criterion. One section has fibre $r+E_p$. The dual pencil gives the other degree-$20$ cover. On $S\to\operatorname{Gr}(2,V)$ the difference of the two codimension-two Schubert classes pulls back, with a choice of order, to
$$
 c_2(E)-c_2(K_E^*)=2([p]+[r]-[k]-[q]).
$$

Three variations enter the fixed-surface argument. The parameter $\lambda$ smooths the branch divisor on the quadric. The parameter $\epsilon$ deforms the resulting polarized K3 transversely to the double-cover locus. Once those two parameters are fixed, the variation $P\mapsto P+\delta h$ moves the support curve in the linear system on that same surface. The rank of this last variation controls four-point dominance.

## 5. Four-point dominance on one fixed surface

It remains to prove $\operatorname{rank}(de)=8$. Independence of the four equations **on the curve** proved transversality of a rectangle. Dominance requires varying its support **on the fixed surface**.

Return to the explicit polynomials of §3. Smooth the branch through $UV+\lambda G$, where $G=1+x^4+y^4+2x^4y^4-UV$. At the moving graph $y=P(x)$ write its restricted branch factors locally as $A_\lambda=A+\lambda u+O(\lambda^2)$ and $B_\lambda=B_0+\lambda v+O(\lambda^2)$, taking $A_\lambda$ monic. All graph directions used below preserve the leading coefficient of $A$ at order zero. Then
$$
 uB_0+vA=G(X,P),\qquad \deg u<22.
$$
For a graph variation $P\mapsto P+\delta h$, write $u_h,v_h$ for the graph derivatives of these coefficients. They are determined by
$$
 u_hB_0+v_hA=h(G_y-uV_y-vU_y)(X,P),\qquad \deg u_h<22,
\tag{9}
$$
where $u,v$ are polynomials in $X$ multiplying the indicated restricted partials.

Follow the simple zero of (2) over the branch deformation, using (4). At each $\lambda$ the graph through four **fixed surface points** imposes four independent normal conditions on $h$. To test the rest of the equations take the six directions
$$
 h=D_\lambda X^j,\qquad 0\le j\le5,
$$
where $D_\lambda$ uses the four continued coordinates. They keep all four points on the graph. Their $x,y,w$ coordinates on the fixed K3 surface are held fixed in this derivative, while $z=w/A_\lambda$ changes. Eliminate the common level by $t=z(a)^2$. Each split-fibre variation of the factor at a mark is $U_y(e,P(e))h(e)=0$, identically in the continued marks. The three equal-level differentials, up to nonzero row normalizations, have first coefficients in $\lambda$
$$
 u_h(e)/A(e)-u_h(a)/A(a),\qquad e=b,c,d.
\tag{10}
$$
The corresponding first coefficient in the graph derivative of $t$ is $t_1=-2u_h(a)/A(a)$, using central $t=1$.

For the fourth equation use the coefficient of $X^{-1}$ in $(B_\lambda-tA_\lambda)/(PD_\lambda)$. This extends (2) holomorphically off the three level equations. Its derivative on their tangent kernel computes the derivative of (2). The entire zeroth-order graph contribution vanishes for every nearby $D_\lambda,t$. In fact, writing $V-tU=f_2Y^2+f_1Y+f_0$ and cancelling $h/D_\lambda=X^j$, that contribution is the coefficient of $X^{-1}$ in $X^j(f_2-f_0/P^2)$, which is zero by the degrees. Consequently the movement of the central marks and level along $\lambda$ contributes zero in differentiating this part. At $P=X^{10}$ the first surviving coefficient is
$$
 [X^{-1}]\left(\frac{v_h-u_h-At_1}{P D}
               -\frac{(v-u)h}{P^2 D}\right).
\tag{11}
$$
The second summand accounts for the movement of all poles of the graph Čech representative.

The submatrix of (10) and (11) on the columns $j=0,1,2,3$ reduces modulo $1009$ to
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
56&208&244&10\\
677&140&204&752\\
767&196&124&177\\
961&44&916&466
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is $423$ in this field. A direct check consists of reducing (9) modulo $A$, dividing for $v_h$, and substituting in (10)–(11). The supplement also extracts the mixed coefficient by polynomial division over the truncated ring in $\lambda,\delta$. The two results agree over $\mathbb Q$. Thus the determinant of the actual four constraints has a nonzero $\lambda^4$ coefficient. For a sufficiently general small nonzero $\lambda$, the four graph constraints are independent on the subspace of variations fixing the four marked surface points. Adding graph variations of degree at most three realizes the four independent normal-to-support conditions $h(e)$. The joint eight constraints have independent support derivatives.

To pass from the divided central equation to actual rectangles, fix this smooth branch member and vary the polarized surface in the additional parameter $\epsilon$ from §2. Lift the finite-dimensional space of graph sections over the surface family. The three equations $K(p,k)=K(p,q)=K(r,k)=0$ form a smooth incidence near the four ordinary marks. Centrally they are the three equal-level equations with the specified choices of sheets. On this incidence the fourth equation vanishes at $\epsilon=0$. Its quotient by $\epsilon$ restricts to a unit times (2). Indeed solving the three simple equations gives the two local second-root graphs, and division gives their first velocity difference. The fourth constraint can therefore be replaced centrally by the extension used for (11), up to a unit and addition of the three level rows. Invertibility of the support minor persists for small nonzero $\epsilon$. The implicit function theorem with $\epsilon$ fixed now realizes every quadruple in an analytic neighbourhood in $S_\epsilon^4$ by an actual rectangle on that same $S_\epsilon$. This argument keeps the four coordinates, the eight constraints, and the support variations in one relative family.

All the conditions at an actual ordinary rectangle are algebraic and open there, including the ranks of its support and four-mark projections. The smooth spin supports form an étale cover after rigidification of the smooth-curve open of a projective bundle over polarized K3 moduli. A finite chart gives actual spin lines. The ordinary transverse rectangle is étale over this parameter space. Consequently the analytic full-rank occurrence gives a component dominating degree-$40$ polarized moduli with dominant four-point evaluation on a general fixed surface. Picard number one holds at a very general fibre there.

By (8), $dg$ has rank $21$ generically. The kernel of $de$ then maps isomorphically onto the characteristic tangent subspace of the codimension-$13$ bundle image. A general fibre component of $e$ has dimension $13$, and its image is an algebraic integral manifold of this distribution. The images cover a dense open of the bundle image. Generically adjacent parametrizing branches induce the same characteristic distribution at a smooth point. All bundles on a given ordinary fibre have the same $c_2$ by (5) and have the same point class in $B$ by [6]. Equality of integral $c_2$ follows from the torsion-free degree-zero Chow group of the K3 surface. On a closure of an irreducible leaf image, the constant-cycle statement extends from a dense smooth open by moving zero-cycles on a projective resolution into that open and pushing them forward. This completes Theorem 1.

## 6. The residual permutation on a fixed K3 surface

For the connection to the determinant, keep a centre $p$ and order all $21$ roots of $Z_p$. On a general fixed K3 the corresponding proper ordered family $Y$ has dimension $22$. It can be formed by taking the closure of the ordinary graph in the product of the support and spin compactification, the relative Hilbert scheme, and the ordered point space, then taking an equivariant resolution. The evaluation $f:Y\to S^{21}$ is proper. For a generator $\delta_T$ of $\bigwedge^{21}T$, the class $f^*\delta_T$ has type $(21,21)$ and changes sign under each root transposition. The rectangle is a horizontal divisor before ordering the nineteen roots of $E_p$. The projection forgetting $r$ and the two distinguished choices has finite fibres on its ordinary open, since $r$ is among the roots of $Z_k$. We test its actual ordered components.

**Proposition 2.** There is a component of the marked rectangle on a general fixed degree-$40$ K3 whose degree-$19$ residual cover has a simple transposition. Every component of its full ordering therefore has zero degree against the pullback of $\delta_T$.

Use the same $A$ and four marks as in §3 with
$$
 F_*=X^2(X^{10}-1)(X^{10}-1024),\qquad B_*=A+F_* .
$$
The pullback graph cover still has $44$ simple branch points. At the level $t=1$ the four specified marks are simple. There is a double root at $x=0$ with $z=+1$ and $w=1$. The other roots are simple. For the chosen centre at $c=-1$, removal of its conjugate and the two marks $a,b$ gives a degree-$19$ residual divisor with one double and seventeen simple points. Its local equation at the double point is $F_*$ up to a unit. The even pencil $(1-s)F_*+sF$ keeps the four marks and $H(1)=0$. At both endpoints $H_t(1)\ne0$, so the ordinary values of the pencil connect the marked branch to the one of §3, allowing finitely many exceptional parameter values to be omitted. This pencil by itself varies the surface.

For the fixed-surface assertion, use the lifts $U=x^2y^2+x+1$ and $V_*=U+x^2(y-1)(y-1024)$. Vary the support by $P_\eta=X^{10}+\eta X$ on that same double cover. Let $D$ follow the four simple marked roots of $F_{t,\eta}=V_*(X,P_\eta)-tU(X,P_\eta)$ and set $Q_0=F_*/D$ at the reference point. Differentiating (3) there gives
$$
 \dot H=[X^9]\frac{\dot F-\dot DQ_0}{D}-[X^{19}](Q_0\dot P).
\tag{12}
$$
The first quotient is a polynomial because the marks move as roots. The last term is the derivative of the moving Čech denominator and equals $-1$ in the $\eta$ direction. The exact values are
$$
 H_t=-154093/1745920,\qquad H_\eta=-116281/1280 .
$$
At the critical point near $0$, the critical value of $F_{t,\eta}$ has derivative $-t'(\eta)$ along $H=0$, because $F'_*(0)=0$, $A(0)=1$, and the direct graph derivative at zero vanishes. This gives $158607284/154093$. The quadratic unit in the local Weierstrass factor has central value $1024$, so the discriminant derivative of its monic quadratic factor is
$$
 -39651821/9861952.
\tag{13}
$$
The residue calculation uses only the four ordinary marks. The double point itself is handled through the regular local equation of the residual divisor.

The equations for the divided rectangle and the local discriminant have independent differentials in $(t,\eta)$ by (12)–(13). Their intersection persists first upon smoothing the branch and then upon the transverse polarized deformation of §3. For this second step lift the two graph sections spanning the $\eta$-pencil over the surface family. At each fixed $\epsilon\ne0$ one gets a support pencil and an actual rectangle through a simple residual collision. All other residual roots are simple. A small loop in the marked rectangle of this **fixed** surface therefore exchanges just the two colliding roots. The two equations and transversality persist in polarized moduli, giving such a component on a general fixed surface. This assertion concerns the component carrying the local loop.

If the geometric monodromy group on the nineteen labels is $G$, the components of the full ordering torsor have stabilizers conjugate to $G$. Each stabilizer contains an odd permutation. Its action extends to the closure in the ordered graph and preserves the fundamental cycle and its proper image. On the same image the alternating pairing changes sign. The resulting rational degree is zero. This proves Proposition 2. It leaves open other components and other algebraic operations using the two degree-$20$ covers. In particular, Theorem 1 establishes no algebraic generator of $\bigwedge^{21}T$.

For products with trivial endomorphism field, the invariant-theoretic reduction and independent fixed-surface tests for the missing determinant are developed in [12]. In the present construction the Lagrangian correspondence acts on the marked four-point space. Lifting its degree-$20$ section fibres to further ordered K3 factors requires the permutation calculation on the same component.
