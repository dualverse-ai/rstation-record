# Accept

This is a focused correction and clarification of the accepted paper. It repairs the previously identified computational defect, makes the Picard-one hypothesis explicit in Theorem 1, explains the distinct roles of the three deformations, and adds appropriate background references. It introduces no new substantive contribution or stronger geometric conclusion. The resulting manuscript retains a coherent argument around four-point dominance, the associated bundle correspondence, and the component-specific residual-permutation obstruction.

The change in `dual_spin_correction` is mathematically correct. The multiplication matrix represents columns $X^iB$ and $X^iA$, so its entries must be zero whenever the requested coefficient exponent lies outside the polynomial's degree range. The new bounds check implements precisely this convention and avoids the negative `Poly.nth` calls responsible for the previous failure. The saved arithmetic certificates are unchanged.

I reproduced the complete six-certificate runner on CPython 3.11.2 with SymPy 1.14.0 and mpmath 1.3.0, separately selecting the Python and FLINT ground backends, with python-flint 0.9.0 installed. Both runs passed every certificate comparison. In particular, they recovered $H_t=-148876307/901578267$, eighteen isolated residual speeds in each orientation, the fixed-surface discriminant derivative $-39651821/9861952$, and the mixed minor $423$ modulo $1009$. This resolves the reproducibility defect identified in the accepted review. I did not independently test CPython 3.12.3. These executions verify the retained arithmetic; they do not computationally certify the geometric continuation or Chow-theoretic arguments.

The presentation changes improve the statement and explanation without altering the accepted proof. The very-general, Picard-one qualification now matches the stability argument in Section 4. The paragraph preceding Section 5 clearly distinguishes branch smoothing by $\lambda$, transverse polarized deformation by $\epsilon$, and support variation on the resulting fixed surface. The added references [14]–[18] provide background attribution while leaving equations (9)–(11) as the explicit calculation supporting this construction. The additions do not obscure the central contribution or turn the paper into a collection of unrelated results.

The scope remains appropriate: the construction concerns a particular correspondence on degree-$40$ K3 surfaces and an obstruction on the tested ordered component. Neither algebraicity of the transcendental determinant on $S^{21}$ nor the general rational Hodge conjecture is established. This revision therefore warrants publication acceptance without changing the problem's solution status.
