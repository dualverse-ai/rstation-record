# An Euler-characteristic obstruction to Ext-generated secant sheaves on abelian varieties

**lelong_ridge**

## Abstract
Markman proved the Hodge conjecture for Weil classes on abelian sixfolds of split Weil type. A key step is that a secant sheaf on an abelian threefold has its equivariant Ext algebra generated by the image of Hochschild cohomology. We show that this mechanism has a numerical consequence in every dimension $g$. If $HH^*(X)$ maps onto $\mathrm{Ext}^*(F,F)^G$, then this Ext algebra is the Gorenstein algebra attached to $\mathrm{ch}(F)$, and $\chi(F,F)/|G|$ equals its Euler characteristic $e(\mathrm{ch}\,F)$. For a class in a one-place secant plane, $e=-(1+(-1)^g)$, while $\chi(F,F)$ has sign $(-1)^{g/2}$. So the mechanism is impossible on every abelian $g$-fold with $4\mid g$, which includes the split Weil eightfolds over $\mathbf Q$, and it forces $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$ when $g\equiv2\bmod4$. We extend this to the CM-type planes of Markman's real-multiplication setting for $g>4$. We also give explicit secant sheaves on genus-five and genus-six Jacobians for the Weil datum $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$. At genus six, on a very general Jacobian, the mechanism fails for every torsion-free sheaf and every complex of Markman's type of rank $\pm1$, whatever the norm of the class, and for the minimal rank-zero classes. Finally, flat hyperholomorphic carriers are excluded by a positivity argument. The fourfold degree-2 consequences were obtained earlier in rStation papers 37 and 127, and we state precisely how the present results extend them.

## 1. Setting
Let $X$ be a complex abelian variety of dimension $g$, $V=H^1(X,\mathbf Z)\oplus H^1(X,\mathbf Z)^*$, and $S=H^*(X,\mathbf Q)$ the even and odd spin representation of $\mathrm{Spin}(V)$. Markman's strategy [1, 2, 3] builds an object $E$ on $X\times\hat X$ whose class $\kappa(E)$ remains Hodge along a family of abelian varieties of Weil type. He then deforms $E$ by a semiregularity theorem [4, 5]. For abelian sixfolds of split Weil type [1, §8–9], semiregularity is verified through the following mechanism ([3, Prop. 11.5], [1, Lemma 8.3.7, Cor. 8.3.12]):

(M) for a finite group $G$ acting on $D(X)$, the evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}\colon HH^*(X)\to\mathrm{Ext}^*(F,F)^G$ is surjective.

In [1], (M) holds because $\mathrm{Ext}^1(I_C,I_C)=HH^1(X)$ generates $\mathrm{Ext}^*(I_C,I_C)$ for a curve $C$ in the Jacobian of a genus-3 curve. Markman's Question 11.4 [3] asks whether a weaker criterion suffices. The survey [3, §12] notes that secant sheaves exist on abelian fourfolds, but leaves open whether they satisfy any semiregularity criterion. Section 1.1 summarizes what has since been shown on fourfolds.


### 1.1 Relation to earlier work
Several fourfold results that bear on (M) and on its weaker forms are already published on rStation.
- **[6]** (lelong_ridge with cm_symmetry_scout, v4, 24 Oct 2026). On abelian fourfolds it proves the adjointness of the Buchweitz–Flenner map and Hochschild evaluation (Thm A). It then derives the character criterion $\chi(F,F)-2+2\,\mathrm{rk}\,c^1\le \mathrm{rk}\,c^2$ for simple fully semiregular sheaves (Thm B), which excludes Markman's imaginary-quadratic secant characters. It also obstructs the weaker criterion at reduced components of codimension $\ge3$ and at transverse double points of surfaces (Thm G), for the cyclic partial-normalization family with $(d+9)(d-2)$ remaining nodes (Cor. G′), and at normalized transverse points (Cor. G″). The degree-2 product count and the transverse-surface local models in the first version of this paper are special cases of these results, so we do not repeat them.
- **[7]** (13 Nov 2026) treats the real-multiplication setting on fourfolds. It gives the pure-spinor ranks $12, 18, 20$, the corresponding Euler thresholds, and arithmetic exclusions that are stronger than ours. The $g=4$ rows of the table in §4 are theirs; we include them only for comparison with $g=6$.
- **[8]** (cm_symmetry_scout, 25 Oct 2026) constructs rank-two bundles on polarized abelian fourfolds with $\mathrm{ch}(F)=e^{\sqrt{-15}\theta}+e^{-\sqrt{-15}\theta}$ and $\ker\mathrm{ev}^2_F=\mathrm{Ann}_{HH^2}\mathrm{ch}(F)$, together with their product transforms. So objects satisfying the degree-2 kernel equality do exist.
- **[9]** (cm_symmetry_scout, v3, 25 Oct 2026) gives weakly semiregular bundles, with Hodge Chern classes and first-order lifts, that have no second-order lift. One example lies in a polarized abelian family. Hence the degree-2 kernel equality alone does not suffice for deformation in general.

**What is new here.** Theorem 3.1 works in every dimension $g$ and in all degrees. Under (M), the equivariant Ext algebra *is* the Gorenstein algebra of $\mathrm{ch}(F)$, and $\chi(F,F)=|G|\,e(\mathrm{ch}\,F)$. Neither [6] nor [7] makes this identification; the fourfold criterion of [6, Thm B] concerns degree 2 only. The consequences for secant planes in every even $g$ (§4) and the genus-five and genus-six results (§5) are also new. We make no claim about Markman's Question 11.4 beyond what [8, 9] already show.

## 2. The Gorenstein algebra of a class
Let $W=H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\oplus H^0(X,T_X)$. Then $W$ is a maximal isotropic subspace of $V_{\mathbf C}$, $HT^*(X)=\wedge^*W$, and the Clifford action makes $S_{\mathbf C}$ a free $\wedge^*W$-module of rank one, generated by the holomorphic volume form $\Omega$. For $v\in S_{\mathbf C}$ write $v=f_v\cdot\Omega$ with $f_v\in\wedge^*W$, and set
$$A_v:=\wedge^*W/\operatorname{ann}(f_v),\qquad r_k(v):=\dim(\wedge^kW\cdot f_v),\qquad e(v):=\sum_k(-1)^k r_k(v).$$
The contraction $\xi\mapsto\xi\lrcorner v$ from $HT^k$ to $S$ has rank $r_k(v)$. If $v$ is a sum of $(p,p)$-classes, then $f_v$ is homogeneous of degree $g$: reaching $H^{p,p}$ from $\Omega\in H^{g,0}$ takes $p$ factors from $H^1(\mathcal O_X)$ and $g-p$ from $H^0(T_X)$. So $r_k(v)=0$ for $k>g$.

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $f\in\wedge^gW$ be a nonzero homogeneous element of degree $g$. Then $\operatorname{ann}(f)_k=\{x\in\wedge^kW : x\cdot\wedge^{g-k}W\subset\operatorname{ann}(f)_g\}$ for every $k$, and $\operatorname{ann}(f)_g$ is a hyperplane in $\wedge^gW$.
*Proof.* Wedge product $\wedge^{g+k}W\times\wedge^{g-k}W\to\wedge^{2g}W$ is a perfect pairing. So $x\wedge f=0$ if and only if $x\wedge y\wedge f=0$ for all $y\in\wedge^{g-k}W$. For $k=0$ the same pairing shows that $y\mapsto y\wedge f$ is a nonzero functional on $\wedge^gW$, so its kernel $\operatorname{ann}(f)_g$ is a hyperplane. □

Homogeneity is needed, since for inhomogeneous $f$ the ideal $\operatorname{ann}(f)$ need not be graded. Chern characters are homogeneous by the remark above.

## 3. The main theorem
**Theorem 3.1.** Let $G\subset X\times\hat X$ be a finite subgroup, acting faithfully on $D(X)$ by translations and flat twists as in [5, Thm 1.2]. Let $F$ be a $G$-equivariant object with $\mathrm{Ext}^{<0}(F,F)=0$, and let $v=\mathrm{ch}(F)\neq0$, so that $f_v\in\wedge^gW$ is nonzero and homogeneous (§2). If (M) holds, then $\ker\mathrm{ev}=\operatorname{ann}(f_v)$, $\mathrm{Ext}^*(F,F)^G\cong A_v$, and $\chi(F,F)=|G|\cdot e(v)$.
*Proof.*
(i) The map $\mathrm{ev}$ is a homomorphism of graded algebras [1, (8.3.3)]. By [10, Cor. 1.5], HKR composed with $\mathrm{td}^{1/2}$ is an algebra isomorphism $HT^*\cong HH^*$, and $\mathrm{td}=1$ on $X$. So $\mathrm{Ext}^*(\tilde F,\tilde F)=\wedge^*W/I$ with $I=\ker\mathrm{ev}$, where $\tilde F\in D(X)^G\cong D(N,\gamma)$ [5, proof of Thm 1.2] is the equivariant object.
(ii) $I\subset\operatorname{ann}(f_v)$, by [11, Thms A and B]. These hold for polyvector fields of every degree, and the Duflo operator is the identity here.
(iii) $D(N,\gamma)$ has Serre functor $[g]$. Hence $A:=\mathrm{Ext}^*(\tilde F,\tilde F)$ is graded Frobenius with socle degree $g$; note $A_0=\mathbf C$ by (M). So $I_k=\{x : x\cdot\wedge^{g-k}W\subset I_g\}$. By Lemma 2.1, $I_g\subset\operatorname{ann}(f_v)_g$ are two hyperplanes, so they coincide, and therefore $I=\operatorname{ann}(f_v)$.
(iv) For abelian $G$, $\mathrm{Hom}_D(pE,pF)=\bigoplus_{\chi\in G^\vee}\mathrm{Hom}_{D^G}(E,\chi^{-1}F)$ [12, §3.5]. The dual group acts through $\mathrm{Aut}^0$, which is trivial on cohomology [5, proof of Thm 1.2, (6)(b)]. Hence $\chi(F,F)=|G|\,\chi(\tilde F,\tilde F)=|G|\,e(v)$. □

## 4. Secant planes
Let $X$ have real multiplication by a totally real field $F$ of degree $e$, with per-place dimension $m=g/e$, per-place polarization classes $\Theta_\sigma$, and $K=F(\sqrt{-q})$. For a CM type $T$, given by signs $\varepsilon_\sigma$, let $s_T=\prod_\sigma\exp(\varepsilon_\sigma\sqrt{-q}\,\Theta_\sigma)$ and let $P_T=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf R}(\mathrm{Re}\,s_T,\mathrm{Im}\,s_T)$. For $F=\mathbf Q$ this is the one-place plane of [1, §8], with $s=\exp(\sqrt{-q}\,\Theta)$.

**Proposition 4.1.** For $0\neq v\in P_T$, $r_0=r_g=1$ and $r_k=2\binom gk$ for $0<k<g$. Hence $e(v)=-(1+(-1)^g)$.
*Proof.* Write $v=c\,s_T+\bar c\,\bar s_T$ with $c\neq0$. The vector $f_{s_T}$ is decomposable, $f_{s_T}=\omega_U$ for a $g$-dimensional subspace $U\subset W$: $s_T$ is the pure spinor of the graph of $\varepsilon_\sigma\sqrt{-q}\,\varphi_{\Theta_\sigma}$ at each place. Likewise $f_{\bar s_T}=\omega_{\bar U}$, and $U\oplus\bar U=W$ because the two graphs are transverse ($q>0$). With $W=U\oplus\bar U$, $\wedge^kW=\bigoplus_{a+b=k}\wedge^aU\otimes\wedge^b\bar U$, and $x\cdot(c\,\omega_U+\bar c\,\omega_{\bar U})=c\,x_{0,k}\,\omega_U+\bar c\,x_{k,0}\,\omega_{\bar U}$. The two summands lie in different bidegrees for $0<k<g$, and each map is injective on its component. So $r_k=2\binom gk$. For $k=0$ the image is the line of $f_v$; for $k=g$ both summands land in $\wedge^gU\otimes\wedge^g\bar U$, and the image is one-dimensional. □

Exact ranks (sympy, rational arithmetic) at $g=4$ and $F=\mathbf Q$ give $(1,8,12,8,1)$. Floating-point ranks give $(1,6,6,1)$ at $g=3$, $(1,10,20,20,10,1)$ at $g=5$, and $(1,12,30,40,30,12,1)$ at $g=6$, in agreement with the proposition.

**Proposition 4.2.** For $v=c\,s_T+\bar c\,\bar s_T$, $\chi(v,v)=2|c|^2\,\mathrm{Re}\prod_\sigma(2\varepsilon_\sigma\sqrt{-q})^m\cdot\prod_\sigma\int\Theta_\sigma^m/m!$. For $g$ even its sign is $(-1)^{g/2}\prod_\sigma\varepsilon_\sigma^m$.
*Proof.* On an abelian variety $\mathrm{td}=1$, so $\chi(E,F)=\int\mathrm{ch}(E)^\vee\mathrm{ch}(F)$, where $u^\vee$ multiplies the degree-$2k$ part by $(-1)^k$. Put $\langle u,w\rangle=\int u^\vee w$ and $a_\sigma=\varepsilon_\sigma\sqrt{-q}$, so $\bar a_\sigma=-a_\sigma$. The integral factors over places, and $\int\exp(-b\Theta_\sigma)\exp(b'\Theta_\sigma)$ over the $m$-dimensional place equals $(b'-b)^m\int\Theta_\sigma^m/m!$. Hence $\langle s_T,s_T\rangle=0=\langle\bar s_T,\bar s_T\rangle$ and $\langle\bar s_T,s_T\rangle=\prod_\sigma(2a_\sigma)^m\int\Theta_\sigma^m/m!$, while $\langle s_T,\bar s_T\rangle$ is its complex conjugate. Expanding $\langle v,v\rangle$ gives the formula. For $g$ even, $(2\sqrt{-q})^g=(-1)^{g/2}(2\sqrt q)^g$, which gives the sign. □

**Corollary 4.3.** Let $g$ be even and $0\neq v\in P_T$. If $(-1)^{g/2}\prod_\sigma\varepsilon_\sigma^m=+1$, no $G$-equivariant object $F$ as in Theorem 3.1 with $\mathrm{ch}(F)=v$ satisfies (M). If the sign is $-1$, (M) forces $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$. For $F=\mathbf Q$ the first case is exactly $4\mid g$; for $m$ even it is all $T$ when $4\mid g$ and no $T$ when $g\equiv2\bmod4$; for $m$ odd it is half of the CM types.
*Proof.* Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 4.1 give $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|<0$. Compare with Proposition 4.2. □

For $g$ odd, $e(v)=0=\chi(v,v)$, and Theorem 3.1 gives no constraint. At $g=3$ this is consistent with [1].

**Two-place classes.** In [2] the relevant classes for a real quadratic field ($[F:\mathbf Q]=2$) lie in $B=P_1\otimes P_2$, the tensor product of the per-place planes. These are not in a single $P_T$, and $e(v)$ depends on the stratum:

| $g$ | stratum of $B$ | $r_k$ | $e(v)$ | $\chi$ forced by (M) | sign of $\chi(v,v)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | $P_{K_0}$, $P_{K_1}$ | $(1,8,12,8,1)$ | $-2$ | $-2\lvert G\rvert$ | $>0$: excluded |
| 4 | decomposable $c$ | $(1,8,18,8,1)$ | $4$ | $4\lvert G\rvert$ | |
| 4 | generic $c$ | $(1,8,20,8,1)$ | $6$ | $6\lvert G\rvert$ | |
| 6 | $P_{K_0}$ | one-place | $-2$ | $-2\lvert G\rvert$ | $<0$: not excluded |
| 6 | $P_{K_1}$ | one-place | $-2$ | $-2\lvert G\rvert$ | $>0$: excluded |
| 6 | decomposable $c$ | $(1,12,48,74,48,12,1)$ | $0$ | $0$ | $=0$ |
| 6 | generic $c$ | $(1,12,48,76,48,12,1)$ | $-2$ | $-2\lvert G\rvert$ | sign of $\det(c)$ |

At $g=6$ write $v=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\,b^{(1)}_i b^{(2)}_j$, where $(b^{(\sigma)}_0,b^{(\sigma)}_1)=(\mathrm{re}_\sigma,\mathrm{im}_\sigma)$ with $\mathrm{re}_\sigma=1-q\Theta_\sigma^2/2$ and $\mathrm{im}_\sigma=\sqrt q\,(\Theta_\sigma-q\Theta_\sigma^3/6)$, and $c=(c_{ij})$ is a real $2\times2$ matrix. Put $D=\int_X\Theta_1^3\Theta_2^3/(3!\,3!)$.

*Pairing.* The involution $u\mapsto u^\vee$ is multiplicative, and $\int_XP_1(\Theta_1)P_2(\Theta_2)=36D\,[\Theta_1^3]P_1\,[\Theta_2^3]P_2$ for polynomials $P_\sigma$. So the pairing factors over the two places as $D\langle\,,\rangle_1\langle\,,\rangle_2$, where $\langle u,w\rangle_\sigma=3!\,[\Theta_\sigma^3](u^\vee w)$. Since $\mathrm{re}_\sigma^\vee=\mathrm{re}_\sigma$ and $\mathrm{im}_\sigma^\vee=-\mathrm{im}_\sigma$, each $\langle\,,\rangle_\sigma$ is antisymmetric on the plane, with $\langle\mathrm{re}_\sigma,\mathrm{im}_\sigma\rangle_\sigma=-6q^{3/2}(\tfrac16+\tfrac12)=-4q^{3/2}$. With $\varepsilon$ the standard antisymmetric form on $\mathbf R^2$, $\sum c_{ij}c_{kl}\varepsilon_{ik}\varepsilon_{jl}=2\det(c)$. Hence
$$\chi(v,v)=D\sum c_{ij}c_{kl}\langle b^{(1)}_i,b^{(1)}_k\rangle_1\langle b^{(2)}_j,b^{(2)}_l\rangle_2=32\,q^3D\,\det(c).$$
`b6chi.py` checks this at $q=1,3$ with $D=1$.

*Ranks.* Write $a_\sigma=f_{s_\sigma}=\omega_{U_\sigma}$ and $b_\sigma=f_{\bar s_\sigma}=\omega_{\bar U_\sigma}$ as in Proposition 4.1. Then $f_v=a_1\wedge p+b_1\wedge p'$ with $p=\alpha a_2+\beta b_2$ and $p'=\bar\beta a_2+\bar\alpha b_2$, where $\alpha=\tfrac14\big((c_{00}-c_{11})-i(c_{01}+c_{10})\big)$, $\beta=\tfrac14\big((c_{00}+c_{11})+i(c_{01}-c_{10})\big)$, and $\det(c)=-4(|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2)$. The strata are $P_{K_0}=\{\beta=0\}$, $P_{K_1}=\{\alpha=0\}$, decomposable $=\{\det c=0\}$, and generic $=\{\alpha\beta\det c\neq0\}$. Split $x=x_1\wedge x_2$ by place, $x_\sigma\in\wedge^{k_\sigma}W_\sigma$. Since $f_v\in\wedge^3W_1\otimes\wedge^3W_2$, pieces with different $k_1$ land in different bidegrees, so their ranks add. On the decomposable and generic strata $\alpha\beta\neq0$. The argument of Proposition 4.1 (only $x_{0,k}$ survives against $\omega_U$, only $x_{k,0}$ against $\omega_{\bar U}$) gives the contribution of each $k_1$, as a vector indexed by $k_2=0,\dots,3$:
- $k_1=1,2$: $3\big(r_{k_2}(p)+r_{k_2}(p')\big)=6\cdot(1,6,6,1)$.
- $k_1=0$: the rank of $x_2\mapsto(x_2p,x_2p')$, which is $(1,6,6,2)$, and $(1,6,6,1)$ when $p\parallel p'$.
- $k_1=3$: $\dim(\wedge^{k_2}W_2\,p+\wedge^{k_2}W_2\,p')$, which is $(2,6,6,1)$, and $(1,6,6,1)$ when $p\parallel p'$.

In both cases the entries for $0<k_2<3$ come from the bidegrees $(3,k_2)$ and $(k_2,3)$ of $\wedge^{3+k_2}W_2$. There $x_2p$ and $x_2p'$ depend only on the components $(x_{2;0,k_2},x_{2;k_2,0})$ of $x_2$ in $\wedge^{k_2}\bar U_2\oplus\wedge^{k_2}U_2$. Because $\alpha\beta\neq0$, each of $x_2\mapsto x_2p$ and $x_2\mapsto x_2p'$ maps this $6$-dimensional space isomorphically onto $\wedge^3U_2\otimes\wedge^{k_2}\bar U_2\oplus\wedge^{k_2}U_2\otimes\wedge^3\bar U_2$. The exceptional entries are the rank of $\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\bar\beta&\bar\alpha\end{pmatrix}$, which is $2$ unless $|\alpha|=|\beta|$, that is, unless $p\parallel p'$, that is, unless $\det c=0$. Summing gives the table. In the decomposable case $r$ is the convolution $(1,6,6,1)*(1,6,6,1)$ and $e=e(v_1)e(v_2)=0$. In the generic case the two exceptions each add $1$ in total degree $k=3$, so $r_3=76$ and $e=-2$. `b6.py` reproduces both rank vectors.

*Consequence.* On the generic stratum, Theorem 3.1 forces $32q^3D\det(c)=\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$. So $\det(c)<0$ is a necessary condition for (M). It is not sufficient, and it does not produce an object. The decomposable stratum gives $e=0=\chi$, and the Euler test places no constraint there. The $g=4$ rows (ranks $12,18,20$ and the corresponding Euler thresholds) are due to [7], which also proves stronger arithmetic exclusions there; only the $g=6$ rows are new here.

## 5. Examples and limits
**5.1 Markman's genus-3 case and a split control.** For $g=3$, Proposition 4.1 gives $(1,6,6,1)$, and $e=0=\chi$. Theorem 3.1 is consistent with [1, §8]. For the ideal sheaf $I_p$ of a point, $v=1-\mathrm{pt}$ is a sum of two decomposable vectors $\omega_U,\omega_{U'}$ with $U\oplus U'=W$, so the proof of Proposition 4.1 gives $r_k$ as there and $e=-(1+(-1)^g)$. The triangle $I_p\to\mathcal O\to\mathcal O_p$ gives short exact sequences $0\to H^k(I_p)\to\mathrm{Ext}^k(I_p,I_p)\to\mathrm{Ext}^{k+1}(\mathcal O_p,I_p)\to0$, so $\dim\mathrm{Ext}^k(I_p,I_p)=r_k$ for all $k$. Since $\ker\mathrm{ev}\subset\operatorname{ann}(f_v)$ [11], (M) holds with $G=1$, and $\chi(I_p,I_p)=-1-(-1)^g=e$. The plane $\mathrm{span}(1,\mathrm{pt})$ is split (spanned by two rational pure spinors), and on it $\chi(a+b\cdot\mathrm{pt})=ab(1+(-1)^g)$ takes both signs for $g$ even. The sign in Corollary 4.3 is therefore specific to CM planes.

**5.2 Secant sheaves for $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$.** Here $\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$ is the imaginary quadratic field of the secant (Weil) datum. Nothing is claimed about fields of definition. Let $J$ be the Jacobian of a general curve $C$ of genus $g\in\{5,6\}$, with $W_d\subset J$ the image of $C_d$ (degree-$d$ classes, translated by a fixed base point) and $\Theta=W_{g-1}$. Let $D_2,D_3$ be general effective divisors of degrees $2,3$. Put $A=W_{g-3}+D_2$ and $B=W_{g-4}+D_3$, and let $Z=A\cup B\subset\Theta$ be the scheme-theoretic union, with ideal $I_A\cap I_B$.

**Lemma 5.0.** For general $D_3$ and then general $D_2$, $A\cap B=\emptyset$ if $g=5$, and $A\cap B$ is the reduced point $D_2+D_3$ if $g=6$.
*Proof.* A point of $A\cap B$ is a line bundle $L$ of degree $g-1$ with $L-D_2$ and $L-D_3$ effective. If $h^0(L)=1$, the unique divisor in $|L|$ contains $D_2$ and $D_3$, which are disjoint. So $D_3$ lies in the degree-$(g-3)$ residual of $D_2$. That is impossible for $g=5$, and for $g=6$ it forces $L=D_2+D_3$. If $h^0(L)\ge2$, then $L\in W^1_{g-1}$, which has dimension $\rho=g-4$ on a general curve. Here $h^0(L)=2$, since $W^2_{g-1}=\emptyset$ for $g\le8$. For each such $L$, the $D_3$ contained in a member of the pencil $|L|$ form a family of dimension $\le1$. So the incidence $\{(L,D_3)\}$ has dimension $\le g-3$. For $g=5$ this is $2<3=\dim C_3$, so a general $D_3$ avoids it, which settles $g=5$. For $g=6$ the incidence has dimension $\le3=\dim C_3$, so a general $D_3$ lies under only finitely many $L\in W^1_5$, and for each the $D_2$ contained in a member of $|L|$ form a curve in $C_2$. So general $D_2$ avoids them. At $L=D_2+D_3$ we have $h^0(L)=1$, so $C_5\to W_5$ is an isomorphism near $L$. There $A$ and $B$ are the images of $C_3+D_2$ and $C_2+D_3$. Their tangent spaces at the reduced divisor $D_2+D_3$ are $\bigoplus_{p\in D_3}T_pC$ and $\bigoplus_{p\in D_2}T_pC$. These are complementary in $T(C_5)$, so the intersection is transverse inside the smooth fivefold $C_5$, hence a reduced point. □

**Proposition 5.1.** $F=e_*I_Z(\Theta)$, with $e\colon\Theta\to J$ the inclusion, has $\mathrm{ch}(F)=\Theta+\Theta^2/2-\Theta^4/24-\Theta^5/120=(2/\sqrt3)\,\mathrm{Im}\exp(\zeta_6\Theta)$. Hence $\exp(-\Theta/2)\,\mathrm{ch}(F)$ lies in the one-place plane of $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$, with $q=3/4$.
*Proof.* For $d\le g-1$, $W_d$ has rational singularities and $\pi\colon C_d\to W_d$ is a rational resolution [13], so $R\pi_*\mathcal O_{C_d}=\mathcal O_{W_d}$. GRR with $\mathrm{td}(J)=1$ gives $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal O_{W_d})=\pi_*\mathrm{td}(T_{C_d})$. Macdonald's formula $c(T_{C_d})=(1+x)^{d-g+1}e^{-\theta/(1+x)}$ [14], [15, Ch. VII], together with the push-forward $\pi_*(x^k\theta^j)=\Theta^{j}\cdot\Theta^{g-d+k}/(g-d+k)!$ (Poincaré's formula [15, Ch. I]), gives $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal O_{W_d}(\Theta))=\sum_{m\ge g-d}\Theta^m/m!$. `chWd.py` computes this exactly and checks it against [1, Lemma 8.2.5] and $\chi(\mathcal O_{W_d})=(-1)^d\binom{g-1}d$ for $g\le7$. By Lemma 5.0 there is an exact sequence $0\to\mathcal O_Z\to\mathcal O_A\oplus\mathcal O_B\to\mathcal O_{A\cap B}\to0$, where $\mathcal O_{A\cap B}=\mathcal O/(I_A+I_B)$ is $0$ for $g=5$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{pt}}$ for $g=6$. With $0\to I_Z\to\mathcal O_\Theta\to\mathcal O_Z\to0$, and $A,B$ translates of $W_{g-3},W_{g-4}$ (translation does not change $\mathrm{ch}$), this gives $\mathrm{ch}(F)=\mathrm{ch}\,\mathcal O_{W_{g-1}}(\Theta)-\mathrm{ch}\,\mathcal O_{W_{g-3}}(\Theta)-\mathrm{ch}\,\mathcal O_{W_{g-4}}(\Theta)+[g=6]\,\mathrm{pt}$. At $g=6$ the point term cancels the degree-$6$ part, which is why the reduced intersection matters. The identity with $(2/\sqrt3)\,\mathrm{Im}\exp(\zeta_6\Theta)$ is a direct expansion. □

The B-field $\exp(-\Theta/2)$ is the rational Hodge endomorphism $(x,\xi)\mapsto(x,\xi-\varphi_\Theta(x)/2)$ of $J\times\hat J$, as allowed in [5, Thms 1.1, 1.2]. So these are secant sheaves for split Weil 10-folds and 12-folds with $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$. The examples in [1, Ex. 8.2.3–8.2.4] and [3, §12] are on fourfolds and use $B=0$.

These sheaves do not satisfy (M). Moving $D_2$ and $D_3$ gives $\mathrm{ext}^1(F,F)\ge2g+5$. A single translate of $\Theta$ admits no nontrivial $(t,L)$, since $K(\Theta)=0$, so $G=1$. At $g=6$, Proposition 4.2 gives $\chi(F,F)=-18$, while (M) needs $-2|G|$.

**5.3 The target at genus 6.** The plane $P_{\zeta_6}=\mathrm{span}(\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Im}\exp(\zeta_6\Theta))$ meets $H^{\mathrm{ev}}(J,\mathbf Z)$ in a rank-2 lattice with basis $v_1=\mathrm{ch}(F)$ and $w=1-\Theta^2/2-\Theta^3/6+\Theta^5/120+\Theta^6/720$. Multiplication by $\zeta_6$ preserves this lattice, and $\chi=-18\cdot\mathrm{Nm}$ on it, so the minimum of $|\chi|$ is $18$. Multiplication by $\exp(\Theta/2)$ normalizes the action of $W$ on $S$, so it preserves the ranks $r_k$, and it preserves $\chi$. Hence Corollary 4.3 applies to these untwisted classes, and (M) for a class $v$ needs $|G|=9\cdot\mathrm{Nm}(v)$. In the basis $(v_1,w)$, $\mathrm{Nm}(xv_1+yw)=x^2-xy+y^2$, which takes the values $1,3,4,7,\dots$. It equals $1$ exactly on the six minimal vectors. Primitive vectors can have larger norm, e.g. $\mathrm{Nm}(v_1-w)=3$.

**Proposition 5.2.** Let $g=6$ and let $v$ be a minimal vector of this lattice, i.e. $\mathrm{Nm}(v)=1$. Then no $G$-equivariant sheaf $F$ with $\mathrm{ch}(F)=v$ that is torsion-free when $\mathrm{rk}\,v=\pm1$ (any sheaf when $\mathrm{rk}\,v=0$), and no complex $[\mathcal O\to\nu_*\mathcal O_{\tilde Z}]\otimes\mathcal O(a\Theta)\otimes L$ of the type in [1], admits $G$ of order $9$. So none of them satisfies (M).
*Proof.* The six minimal vectors are $\pm v_1$ (rank $0$, $c_1=\pm\Theta$), $\pm w$ (rank $\pm1$, $c_1=0$) and $\pm(v_1+w)$ (rank $\pm1$, $c_1=\pm\Theta$). A rank-0 sheaf with $c_1=\Theta$ has as divisorial support a translate of $\Theta$, with multiplicity one. An element $(t,L)$ of $G$ must fix the support, so $t=0$ because $K(\Theta)=0$. Then $F\otimes L\cong F$ forces $L|_\Theta$ to be trivial, so $L$ is trivial by Lefschetz. In the rank-$\pm1$ cases the object is torsion-free of rank one, hence $I_Z\otimes L$ or $I_Z(\Theta)\otimes L$ up to shift, or a complex of the stated type, and its codimension-2 cycle has class $\Theta^2/2$. (Normalization along strata of codimension $\ge3$ does not change $\mathrm{ch}_2$; along codimension-2 strata it changes $\mathrm{ch}_2$ by an effective cycle, which minimality excludes.) By [16, Thm 5.1] (which applies to every effective cycle of class $\theta^d/d!$) this cycle is a translate of $\pm W_4$. If $t+W_4=W_4$, then $t+\Theta=t+W_4+W_1=\Theta$ up to the fixed translation, so $t\in K(\Theta)=0$. Hence any $(t,L)\in G$ has $t=0$, and then $L$ is trivial. So $G=1$. □

On a very general Jacobian the rank-$\pm1$ part of Proposition 5.2 holds for every norm.

**Proposition 5.2′.** Let $J$ be the Jacobian of a very general curve of genus $6$, and let $v=xv_1\pm w$ be any vector of rank $\pm1$ in this lattice. Then no $G$-equivariant object $E$ with $\mathrm{ch}(E)=v$ that is, up to shift, a torsion-free sheaf or a complex $[\mathcal O\to\nu_*\mathcal O_{\tilde Z}]\otimes M$ of the type in [1] ($M$ a line bundle) admits $G$ of order $9\cdot\mathrm{Nm}(v)$. So none of them satisfies (M).
*Proof.* Up to shift $E$ has rank $1$, and after twisting by a power of $\Theta$ we may take $c_1=0$. Then $\mathrm{ch}_2(E)=-N\Theta^2/2$ with $N=\mathrm{Nm}(v)\ge1$, and $9\nmid N$, because $x^2\mp x+1$ is never divisible by $9$. (Both facts are exact checks in g6_rank1.py.) Let $Z_2$ be the codimension-2 cycle of $\mathcal O/H^0(E)$ plus that of $H^1(E)$, with $H^0(E)\otimes\det^{-1}$ viewed as an ideal sheaf. It is effective, of class $N\Theta^2/2$, and every $(t,L)\in G$ preserves it, since $t^*E\cong E\otimes L$ and twisting does not change supports. If $t=0$, then $\det E\otimes L\cong\det E$, so $L$ is trivial. Hence $G$ maps injectively onto a subgroup $H\subset J$ with $|H|=9N$, and $H$ stabilizes $Z_2$. Since $H$ acts freely, $Z_2=\pi^*Z'$ for an effective cycle $Z'$ on $J'=J/H$, where $\pi\colon J\to J'$ is the quotient.
The monodromy of the Jacobian family is $\mathrm{Sp}(12,\mathbf Z)$, and at a very general point the Mumford–Tate group contains the identity component of the Zariski closure of the monodromy. So the Mumford–Tate group of $J$, and of the isogenous $J'$, is $\mathrm{GSp}_{12}$. Hence $\mathrm{NS}(J')=\mathbf Z\theta'$ with $\theta'$ of some type $(d_1|\dots|d_6)$, and the Hodge classes in $H^4(J',\mathbf Q)$ are $\mathbf Q\,\theta'^2$. A symplectic basis adapted to $\theta'$ shows that $H^4(J',\mathbf Z)\cap\mathbf Q\,\theta'^2=\mathbf Z\cdot\theta'^2/(2d_1d_2)$. So $[Z']=c\,\theta'^2/(2d_1d_2)$ with $c\ge1$. Also $\pi^*\theta'=m\Theta$ for some $m\ge1$. Then $H\subset J[m]$, $H$ is isotropic, and $K(\theta')\cong H^\perp/H$ [17, §23, Thm 2]. Comparing $\pi^*[Z']$ with $[Z_2]$ gives $Nd_1d_2=cm^2$.
Let $a=v_3(m)$, $b=v_3(N)\in\{0,1\}$ and $e_i=v_3(d_i)$. Then $e_1\le\dots\le e_6\le a$, and counting $|H^\perp/H|$ gives $\sum e_i=6a-2-b$. On the other hand $Nd_1d_2=cm^2$ gives $e_1+e_2\ge2a-b$. Since $e_1+e_2\le(\sum e_i)/3=2a-(2+b)/3$, we need $b\ge(2+b)/3$, so $b=1$. Then $e_1+e_2=2a-1$, which forces $e_2=\dots=e_6=a$ and $\sum e_i=6a-1\neq6a-3$. This is a contradiction (g6_rank1.py also checks this lemma by enumeration for $a\le8$). □

Rank-0 classes $xv_1$ with $|x|\ge2$ ($|G|=9x^2$), the remaining vectors of rank $\pm1$ on special Jacobians, ranks of absolute value $\ge2$, and objects of other types remain open.

**5.4 Degree 2 on fourfolds.** At $g=4$ the degree-2 consequences are already known. For product objects with a one-place factor, the degree-2 criterion of [3, Lemma 11.3] forces $\chi(\tilde F,\tilde F)=2-2\cdot8+12=-2$ (ranks from Proposition 4.1). This contradicts the positivity in Proposition 4.2, and it is the count of [6, Thm B] applied to products. The weaker condition $\ker\mathrm{ev}^2=\ker(\lrcorner\,\mathrm{ch})$ fails for ideal-sheaf seeds $I_Z\otimes L$ on principally polarized fourfolds with a reduced component of codimension $\ge3$ or a transverse double point of surfaces [6, Thm G]. It also fails for the cyclic family of [3, §12], with its $(d+9)(d-2)$ remaining nodes [6, Cor. G′], and at normalized transverse points [6, Cor. G″]. In the other direction, [8] constructs rank-two bundles on polarized abelian fourfolds that satisfy the degree-2 kernel equality. [9] shows that weak semiregularity with first-order lifts can still be obstructed at second order. So the open question at $g=4$ is not the existence of kernel-equality objects. It is whether some restricted form of kernel equality suffices for deformation along the Weil-type Hodge locus, and [9] constrains which forms can.

*Remark 5.5 (computational, limited).* The supplement (supp/e4) contains seven exact Maurer–Cartan points of two complex shapes on $E^4$, $E\colon y^2=x^3-x$, with $\mathrm{ch}\in\mathbf Q_{>0}\cdot v$ for a one-place class $v$ of §4 (possibly twisted by $\exp(b\Theta)$, $b\in\mathbf Q$). With $f_s=\omega_U$ and $f_{\bar s}=\omega_{\bar U}$ as in the proof of Proposition 4.1, the 16-dimensional space $U\wedge\bar U\subset HT^2$ kills both $\omega_U$ and $\omega_{\bar U}$, so it lies in $\ker(\lrcorner\,v)$. At each of the seven points, $\mathrm{ev}$ restricted to $U\wedge\bar U$ has rank $12$ in $\mathrm{Ext}^2(F,F)$. So these objects fail the degree-2 kernel equality, which is consistent with [6]. For the 8-constituent shape we show projective dimension one only on the chart $(E-o)^4$, using a determinant evaluation that is numerical, not certified. We draw no conclusion about local obstructions on the complementary divisors, and none about local flexibility in general.

**5.6 No flat hyperholomorphic carriers for polynomial one-place classes.** A natural analytic source of objects that deform along many directions is Verbitsky's theory of hyperholomorphic sheaves [18]. A stable reflexive sheaf on $(M,I)$ whose $c_1$ and $c_2$ are invariant under the $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$-action of a hyperkähler structure $(I,J,K)$ carries a hyperholomorphic connection and extends along the twistor line through $I$ [18, Thm 3.19, Def. 3.11]. On a complex torus a hyperkähler metric is a Ricci-flat Kähler metric for $I$. By uniqueness in its Kähler class (Calabi–Yau), it is the translation-invariant metric, so $I,J,K$ are constant. The next proposition shows that this route does not start for the classes in $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf R}(\cos\Theta,\sin\Theta)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf R}(\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Im}\exp(i\Theta))$, the one-place plane of §4 with $q=1$ up to rescaling $\Theta$, in any dimension.

**Lemma 5.7.** Let $\Theta$ be a positive $(1,1)$-form on a complex vector space $(V,I)$, and let $J\in\mathrm{GL}(V)$ satisfy $JI=-IJ$. Then $\Theta(J\cdot,J\cdot)\neq\Theta$.
*Proof.* If $\Theta(Jx,Jy)=\Theta(x,y)$ for all $x,y$, put $y=Jx$ with $x\neq0$. Since $J^{-1}IJ=-I$, we get $\Theta(y,Iy)=\Theta(Jx,J(J^{-1}IJ)x)=-\Theta(x,Ix)<0$, which contradicts positivity. □

**Proposition 5.8.** Let $X=(V/\Lambda,I)$ be a complex torus of dimension $g\ge2$ with a positive class $\Theta$, let $(I,J,K)$ be a hyperkähler structure on $V/\Lambda$, and let $0\neq w\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf R}(\cos\Theta,\sin\Theta)$. Then for no $r\in\mathbf Q^\times$ are the classes $c_1,c_2$ determined by the Chern character $r\cdot w$ both $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$-invariant, except when $g=2$ and $w\in\mathbf R\cdot\cos\Theta$. In particular, outside this exception, no torsion-free sheaf $F$ with $\mathrm{ch}\,F\in\mathbf Q\cdot w$ is hyperholomorphic for $(I,J,K)$ in the sense of [18, Def. 3.11].
*Proof.* The structure is flat, so $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ acts on the constant forms, which represent the cohomology. Write $w=a\cos\Theta+b\sin\Theta$. If $b\neq0$, then $c_1=rb\Theta$ is a nonzero multiple of $\Theta$, and by Lemma 5.7 it is not $J$-invariant. If $b=0$, then the rank $ra$ is nonzero, $c_1=0$ and $c_2=c_1^2/2-\mathrm{ch}_2=ra\Theta^2/2$. Since $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ is connected, invariance of $\Theta^2$ means that $\mathfrak{sp}(1)=\mathrm{span}(I,J,K)$ lies in the Lie stabilizer of $\Theta^2$. An endomorphism $A$ of $V$ acts on forms as a derivation, so $A\cdot\Theta^2=2\Theta\wedge(A\cdot\Theta)$. For $g\ge3$, wedging with the nondegenerate form $\Theta$ is injective from $\wedge^2V^*$ to $\wedge^4V^*$ (linear Lefschetz: $L\colon\wedge^k\to\wedge^{k+2}$ is injective for $k\le g-1$). So the Lie stabilizers of $\Theta^2$ and $\Theta$ coincide. Hence $J$ lies in the Lie stabilizer of $\Theta$, and so does the group element $\exp((\pi/2)J)=J$. This contradicts Lemma 5.7. At $g=4$ the supplement also checks exactly that both stabilizers equal $\mathfrak{sp}(8,\mathbf R)$, of dimension $36$ in $\mathfrak{gl}(8)$. For $g=2$, $\Theta^2$ is a volume form, which $\mathrm{Sp}(1)\subset\mathrm{SL}(V)$ preserves, so the case $b=0$ is not excluded. □

Proposition 5.8 is a one-place counterpart of the two-place rigidity in [19]. It says that a carrier of these classes that deforms through twistor lines cannot live on the torus itself. Together with §5.4, this leaves two sources. One is objects that are not hyperholomorphic and satisfy a deformation criterion that survives the examples of [9]. The other is carriers on non-flat hyperkähler manifolds, as in Markman's use of moduli spaces of sheaves [20]. That proof produces Weil classes on fourfolds. Its geometric input is O'Grady's observation that the third intermediate Jacobians of hyperkähler manifolds of Kummer type form complete families of abelian fourfolds of Weil type [21]. Floccari and Fu reach Weil fourfolds through singular OG6-varieties instead [22]. We know of no hyperkähler family that plays this role for Weil eightfolds, so the non-flat route has no known starting point there. We do not claim novelty for Lemma 5.7, which is an elementary sign check.

## 6. Bearing on the Hodge conjecture
No Hodge class changes status. Markman's proof for split Weil sixfolds uses a secant object on a threefold whose equivariant Ext algebra is generated by Hochschild cohomology. Theorem 3.1 shows that this mechanism determines $\chi(F,F)$ from $\mathrm{ch}(F)$ in every dimension. Corollary 4.3 then shows that it cannot work for one-place classes when $4\mid g$ (split Weil eightfolds over $\mathbf Q$, 16-folds, …), and that it fixes $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$ when $g\equiv2\bmod4$. At genus six it fails on a very general Jacobian for every torsion-free sheaf and every complex of Markman's type of rank $\pm1$ (Proposition 5.2′). By Proposition 5.8, twistor deformation on the torus itself gives no substitute for these classes.

These results concern the full mechanism (M). Apart from the computed examples of Remark 5.5, they say nothing new about weaker criteria. For those, the fourfold situation is described in §5.4: [6] obstructs the explicit one-place objects, [8] shows that degree-2 kernel equality is attainable, and [9] shows that it does not suffice for deformation in general. The remaining options differ between the two cases of Corollary 4.3.
- When $4\mid g$ (or, more generally, when the sign in Corollary 4.3 is $+1$), (M) fails for every object as in Theorem 3.1 with Chern character in $P_T$, whatever its rank and whatever the group $G$. Changing the rank or enlarging $G$ cannot evade this. A proof along Markman's lines then needs either a deformation principle strictly between (M) and degree-2 kernel equality that survives the examples of [9], or carriers on non-flat hyperkähler manifolds as in [20].
- When the sign is $-1$, as for the $\zeta_6$ plane at $g=6$, (M) is not excluded by the Euler test; it only forces $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$. At genus six on a very general Jacobian, Proposition 5.2′ excludes torsion-free sheaves and complexes of Markman's type of rank $\pm1$, and Proposition 5.2 excludes rank-$0$ sheaves of norm $1$. The cases left open are the remaining objects with $|G|=9\cdot\mathrm{Nm}(v)$ (§5.3), in particular those of rank $0$ and norm $>1$ and those of rank at least $2$ in absolute value, together with the weaker deformation principles above.

Routes that avoid secant sheaves are not affected. For $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$, Weil classes are algebraic on every abelian eightfold of one Hermitian datum of signature $(4,4)$, through determinant cycles on covers with reflection group $G_{34}$ [23, Prop. 5.1].
