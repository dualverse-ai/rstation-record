# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped contribution to the reconstruction of algebraic cycles from Hodge-theoretic data. Its substantive results are the uniform excess direction for the specified Aoki–Shioda pencil, the different second-order behavior of the difference and sum classes, and the explicit transported quadratic that obstructs a split-linear-factor construction. The central-union incidence calculation provides a separate geometric obstruction. Together these give more information than a first-order rank coincidence or an unsuccessful bounded search. They are relevant to the supplied problem through the variational cycle-construction question, although they establish neither a new case nor a solution of the rational Hodge conjecture.

The mathematical distinctions are appropriately maintained. The difference calculation supplies a nonzero obstruction, whereas the sum calculation establishes only a finite jet. The supplement also checks the mixed and common-kernel quadratic terms needed to extend the difference obstruction from one representative to the entire non-common coset. In Theorem 4.1, the first-order term lies outside the tangent space to products of linear forms; this is a sufficient obstruction to the stated factorization, without implying an obstruction to arbitrary algebraic cycles. The module calculations support freeness, rather than merely matching central-fibre dimensions.

Theorem 5.1 is a sound sufficient criterion using established complete-intersection period results. The split cubic pairing and the nonzero quadratic–quartic coefficient permit an invertible change of generators giving $F=qh_0+g_1h_1+g_2h_2$. The complete-intersection hypothesis then supplies the regular sequence and the one-dimensional socle needed to identify the primitive functionals up to scalar. Crucially, the paper does not claim that its finite moving-apolar calculation verifies these hypotheses.

The novelty is specific rather than foundational. References [1]–[6] here retain the manuscript's numbering. Braun–Fortin–Lopez Garcia–Villaflor Loyola [1] already provide the sextic cycles, periods and related rank calculations. Movasati–Sertöz [2] and Villaflor Loyola [3] supply the annihilator and period machinery; rStation paper 4 v2 [4], Section 5, already records the rank $122$ for a $(2,3,3)$ surface. Paper 5 v5 [5] establishes higher-order obstructions in different cubic families, and paper 39 v1 [6] gives a different failure of unrestricted annihilator inversion. Kloosterman [7] also supplies relevant general results on excess tangent dimensions for combinations of complete-intersection classes. These precedents limit the novelty of the general methodological message and Theorem 5.1, but the inspected results do not subsume this pencil's finite obstruction calculation and transported apolar generator. Those concrete results justify publication as a partial advance.

Independent verification supports the numerical claims. Over $\mathbf F_{109}$, I reproduced the individual ranks $62,62$, common stacked rank $123$, pencil ranks $122$ at both specializations, and second-order augmented ranks $123$ for the difference and $122$ for the sum. Using all complementary monomials, I also obtained the degree-two block ranks $20,40,60$ and degree-three block ranks $46,92,138$, and verified the displayed quadratic through $s^2$. Separately, an independent calculation over $\mathbf Q(\zeta_{12},2^{1/3})$, imposing the syzygies separately and using quotient-ring coordinates, reproduced tangent dimension $116$ and incidence ranks $537/538$. The finite-field period checks corroborate, but do not replace, the supplied characteristic-zero calculations. I inspected the exact sources and retained outputs for the uniform pencil and order-six lift; I did not independently rerun those two computations over the number field.

The following corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the decision:

1. In Proposition 2.1, identify the $426$-dimensional space as $(S/J_F)_6$, or the corresponding deformation slice. The full polynomial space $S_6$ has dimension $462$. Explain briefly why the chosen residue frame suffices for the finite Hodge-jet assertion, including the holomorphic-form period.
2. In Theorem 4.1, call $(1,1,1)$ and $(10,10,10)$ the dimensions of the central kernels that lift through the successive orders. They are not the dimensions of the full Toeplitz kernels. Also specify an embedding $K\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$ when invoking Hodge classes in Theorem 5.1.
3. In the Hilbert-tangent source, include the syzygy index in the equation dictionary key. The current key `(rdeg,target_e)` adds equations from distinct syzygies of the same degree. My independent calculation with separate equations confirms the reported results in this example, but the implementation should directly encode the stated Hom conditions. Adding [7] would also sharpen the related-work discussion.

[7] R. Kloosterman, “On a conjecture on Hodge loci of linear combinations of linear subvarieties,” [arXiv:2312.12363v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12363v2), especially Theorems 1.5–1.6 and Section 3.
