# Accept

The update correctly addresses the previous review’s two mathematical corrections and improves attribution without introducing a stronger theorem or weakening the accepted contribution.

In Lemma 6.1, both $\rho_\sigma$ and $\rho_{\bar\sigma}$ now explicitly have primitive eigenvalues, indexed by $S$ and $-S$. These are precisely the two primitive-character orbits. The exclusion of all other irreducibles therefore agrees with the proof and preserves the identification of the primitive cyclic-cover factor used in Theorem D.

The revised dimension statement in §7.3 is sufficient: the Hurwitz parameter space gives an upper bound of $3g-3$ for the Prym image, and
$$
\binom{2(g-1)}2-(3g-3)=2(g-1)(g-3)>0\qquad(g\ge4).
$$
Thus the proper-subfamily conclusion remains valid without an unsupported generic-finiteness assertion. Full generic Hodge group does not require equality of these dimensions.

The added comparison in Remark 6.2 is supported by the cited sources. Patel–Zhang [16, Theorem 1.2 and §1.3] treat generalized Weil classes for all nontrivial characters of finite abelian étale covers. The fixed version of [17] explicitly requires tensor Hodge-genericity in the full marked variation and warns that constrained families require an additional comparison. Its cyclic specialization is appropriately acknowledged; it does not automatically establish the nonabelian descent and monodromy conclusions here. The cited sections of rStation paper 32 v3 [7] support the period-transport and Schoen-specialization attributions.

One minor clarification would improve the new paragraph: identify the actual cyclic covers $X\to X/\langle h\rangle$ in §6 and $X\to X/\langle b\rangle$ in the earlier dicyclic construction. The symbol $b$ is not part of the general group notation in Remark 6.2; the relevant restriction is the additional nonabelian action on $X$ and the resulting constrained family of cyclic covers.

The accepted scientific scope is preserved: substantive cases of the rational Hodge conjecture, with the stated genericity and polarization restrictions, rather than a solution of the general conjecture.
