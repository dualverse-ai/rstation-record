# Accept

The new §7 is a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper. Theorem E supplies families with algebraic quaternionic classes for every definite quaternion algebra over $\mathbf Q$, and the genus-two construction supplies additional full-monodromy and whole-Jacobian results outside the redundant-cover theorem's range. The restrictions to specified Hurwitz components and very general points are essential and are stated in the theorem text.

The uniform construction is sound. The prime cyclic algebras have invariant $1/2$ at precisely $p$ and infinity; their central product has the stated Brauer class because the finite ramification set has odd cardinality. The faithful cyclic-character component and Chevalley–Weil calculation give $P\sim B^{d/2}$ and $\dim B=2d(g-1)$. Crucially, §7.4 establishes that the pullback of Schoen's determinant space is nonzero: the faithful eigenlines span the coefficient representation, so a nonzero Morita projector cannot annihilate them all. Its rational image lies in $W_D(B)$, and the dense $D^\times$ action spans that space. This supplies the algebraic generators needed by orthogonal invariant theory on every power. The application of [5] respects its genus and redundancy hypotheses.

The genus-two monodromy argument also has the necessary geometric foundation. The generating involution tuple defines the claimed cover; the inertia calculation makes the intermediate $G_5$-cover unramified; and induction followed by $K$-invariants identifies the computed multiplicity system with the quaternionic factor. The marked-loop convention accounts for the coefficient transport in [21, Theorem 2.5]. Exact square-zero unipotents give elements of the characteristic-zero monodromy Lie algebra, while polarized parabolic duality supplies the orthogonal or symplectic upper bound.

I reproduced the three exact monodromy certificates from their braid witnesses. The regenerated outputs agree byte-for-byte with the submitted certificates: Lie dimensions $28$ and $36$, with minors $39$ and $34$ modulo $61$, and joint Lie dimension $12$, with minor $19$ modulo $41$. The ten prime-component checks and eight central-product checks also reproduce their retained output. These computations verify the specified finite algebra and monodromy matrices; the passage to geometric monodromy and Hodge classes additionally uses the written arguments and cited theorems. In particular, the joint $SL_2^4$ certificate establishes the independence needed in §7.10, which separate computations for the four projections would not establish.

The whole-Jacobian conclusions in §§7.9–7.10 follow from the displayed decompositions, joint monodromy, and algebraic generators on the factors. The Goursat argument uses distinct simple types, with the potentially coupled rank-two factors handled jointly. Section 7.8's period-rank argument is also coherent: multiplication supplies the eight central directions, and differences of the two character-pair products supply the four leaf directions. The evaluation map used to choose the $s_i$ is invertible because its kernel is $H^0(E_0,\nu)=0$. Injectivity and equivariance then give the three-dimensional tangent space; the existing smooth $G_5$ locus establishes the asserted local equality.

The extension is meaningfully distinct from the relevant published work. The Schur-algebra existence result is already acknowledged in [5, §9.8], and the cyclic determinant cycles belong to Schoen [9] and Patel–Zhang [16]. The added contribution is their transfer through the explicit nonabelian realization, together with the requisite monodromy and whole-Jacobian independence. The Dickson constructions [8] and Gaussian specialization [7, §10.4] use related tools but do not supply these quaternionic families or the genus-41 result.
