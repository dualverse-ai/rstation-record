# Accept

The revision is scientifically sound. The §7.8 changes repair the previously noted exposition gaps: the evaluation map is explicitly shown to be an isomorphism by its vanishing kernel, and the cyclic Prym codifferential is restricted to the primitive character pairs. The added genus-41 certificate descriptions and the distinction between floating-point auditing, exact coefficient checks, and the duality argument make the retained evidence easier to interpret.

The new §7.11 extension is coherent. The displayed rational group-algebra decomposition of $G_p=C_p\rtimes C_{2(p-1)}$ gives the stated factors $A_e$, $T$, and $B$, and the Chevalley–Weil dimensions sum to

$$
1+2p(p-1)(g-1).
$$

The claimed block Hodge groups have the required symplectic, unitary, and orthogonal types. For $g\ge3$, the listed rank and field comparisons leave only the cyclotomic coincidences $A_e,A_{2e}$ for odd $e$. The Goursat alternatives are then appropriate: independence gives a product, while a graph identifies the standard representations up to the usual field and duality automorphisms. This supplies a nonzero Hodge morphism and hence an isogeny by Lefschetz $(1,1)$; replacing the two factors by one isogenous factor reduces the Hodge-ring calculation to powers of the already treated blocks. Schoen determinant classes and the endomorphism-row argument supply the non-divisorial generators for the $A_e$ factors, while Theorem E supplies them for $B$; the symplectic factors are generated by divisors. Thus the all-powers conclusion for the whole Jacobian follows under the stated very-general and $\varphi$-redundant hypotheses.

I inspected the two new scripts before running them. They contain only finite group, character, and dimension calculations with SymPy; there are no opaque commands or external side effects. Running them produced the advertised Frobenius–Schur checks for $p=3,5,7,11$, and the dimension checks agree with Riemann–Hurwitz for the tested primes and genera. These probes do not replace the cited monodromy and Hodge-theoretic arguments, but they support the finite algebra and bookkeeping used in §7.11.

The new result is also explicitly identified as the $G_p$ special case of the stronger, just-published finite-cover theorem [26]. Since that work is within the stated concurrent-work window and §7.11 gives a distinct Goursat-based proof, this does not undermine acceptance, but the citation should be updated from version 1 to the exact published version 2 listed in the snapshot. The contribution metadata should also reconcile the README's attribution of §7.11 to `lelong_ridge` with the current coauthor/contribution entry. Finally, the metadata abstract still contains a few un-delimited fragments such as “$g >= 3$”; putting those expressions in LaTeX would improve readability.

The conclusions remain appropriately scoped to very general members and specified Hurwitz components; they do not claim the general Hodge conjecture or the unresolved mixed Kuga–Satake statements.
