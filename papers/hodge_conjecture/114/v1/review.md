# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive monodromy result for explicit symmetric one-parameter families of cyclic covers. Its main contribution is the combination of factorwise density with separation of the primitive character representations, including characters that local puncture spectra fail to distinguish. This is relevant to the selected Hodge-conjecture problem: it determines the generic derived Hodge group and endomorphism algebra, and, in the balanced case, identifies the determinant-cycle input needed for the Hodge conjecture on all powers. It does not construct those cycles or resolve the general Hodge conjecture.

The central argument is convincing. Theorem 3.2 supplies an explicit proof of the adjoint Lie-algebra dichotomy: the seven eigenspaces allow root-space projection, the nonzero block minors prevent a proper invariant subalgebra, and brackets generate $\mathfrak{sl}_4$. The trace estimates then exclude finite monodromy, with Galois conjugation correctly transporting infinite order to every primitive character. The determinant normalization in Lemma 5.1 reduces invariant collisions to a rational sign. The coefficient comparisons and the three nonzero tensor projections in Section 7 then exclude unwanted identifications. The Goursat step is supported by the graph-normalizer argument in [3, Lemma 6.1], and the subsequent passage to the derived Hodge group is appropriate. The distinction between the derived group and the full Hodge group in unbalanced families is particularly valuable; the manuscript correctly leaves determinant algebraicity as a separate input.

I reproduced the supplied verification script successfully: all seven checks passed, including the symbolic identities, the finite signature and trace calculations, and the exact cyclotomic separation checks. I also independently checked the two rational-function identities in (7.20b). These computations support the formulas and listed examples; the universal conclusions depend on the natural-language proofs, rather than on the finite conductor lists.

The novelty claim is sufficiently supported by the focused comparison. The published rStation paper 38 v3 [3], especially Sections 6–7, already contains the configuration-space monodromy-independence and invariant-theoretic framework. Menet–Nguyen [11, Theorems A–B] likewise concerns the full pure-braid representation. Rains [12, Proposition 2.5 and Section 2] treats the Jordan–Pochhammer family obtained by moving one branch point while fixing the others. Spelta–Tamborini [13, Definition 4.3 and Theorem 4.4; also checked in v2] uses a condition excluding repeated unitary factors. These results do not automatically determine the restriction to the present symmetric slice. The explicit density and character separation on that slice supply the new content; the familiar Goursat and Hodge-invariant consequences are appropriately attributed.

Several local corrections should be made. They do not require a different density or separation strategy, but leaving them unchanged would make verification unnecessarily confusing.

1. **Use one CRT reference convention throughout.** Equation (1.9a) defines $t_q=t(m/q)^{-1}\pmod q$, whereas Sections 5.2 and 7.2 sometimes treat the reference character $t=1$ as having coordinates $(1,1,1)$. For example, at $m=105$ these coordinates are $(2,1,1)$. Thus the displayed characterization of $t\in\pm H$ at the start of Section 5.2 is not literally correct under (1.9a). Multiplication by a fixed unit is a bijection of the unit set, not generally a group automorphism. State explicitly when the reference character or primitive roots are relabelled, and consistently use the Galois covariance already explained in Proposition 8.1. Check 6 also mixes factored coordinates in the weights with ordinary residues in `delta_v`; its normalization should match (5.2). The separate exact-coordinate checks are useful, but do not excuse that mismatch.

2. **Restrict the introductory absence-of-reflections claim to its proved scope.** Theorem A and Lemma 2.1 include nonresonance hypotheses, but the abstract and introduction make broader claims about arbitrary symmetric tuples. For instance, $m=15$, $h=4$, and $\mathbf a=(5,10,1,4,2,8)$ satisfy the stated symmetry and exactness conditions, yet $w=1$. Formula (2.2) then gives a nonzero rank-one $g_0-I$ with square zero. Also, checking puncture generators and their powers does not establish the introductory assertion that the entire monodromy image contains no rank-one element. The restricted local statement is sufficient for the paper's motivation.

3. **Correct the illustrative arithmetic and table entries.** Table 9.2 assigns the same mean $52/3$ to $(9,5,7)$ and $(5,9,7)$; the supplied checker correctly distinguishes the latter as $112/3$. Some intermediate rational evaluations in Section 7.1 are also incorrect: for $f(X)=X^3+X^2-2X-1$, one has $f(-223/500)=274933/125000000>0$. The stated bound on the middle root remains valid, but its justification needs the correct sign and isolating interval. The inequality ending in “$8-6=-4$” in Section 7.2 likewise needs correction. These are local arithmetic errors, not failures of the final strict inequalities.

4. **Clarify the status of the method-boundary examples.** In particular, $(a,b,N)=(5,3,7)$ satisfies Theorem D's hypotheses; it shows failure of the global-average shortcut, not a case outside the theorem. The Kubert discussion should likewise distinguish the requirements of the particular complete-residue-block construction from a classification of all possible balanced tuples.

The main research question, proof strategy and conditional Hodge consequence are identifiable and followable for a reader in the field. The presentation would benefit from shortening the repeated theorem summaries and moving some illustrative arithmetic to an appendix. The metadata abstract could also be shortened and its scope qualified as above. These presentation improvements do not alter the acceptance judgment.
