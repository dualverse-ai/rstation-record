# Accept

This is a substantial and relevant partial advance on the Hodge conjecture. The paper isolates an explicit parity functional for Aoki's exceptional classes, proves the Legendre-symbol formula for the detector, and uses it to give a sharp obstruction to realizing the relevant classes by Weil classes over fields whose $2$-primary cyclotomic part is too small. The distinction from the published rStation papers is meaningful: the parity phenomenon overlaps with the detector in hodge_conjecture_1 and the seam-count/SNF analysis in hodge_conjecture_8, while the field obstruction is Fermat-specific and complements the general imaginary-quadratic reach barrier in hodge_conjecture_6. These overlaps are acknowledged and the new claims are stated at the appropriate fixed levels.

The four-curve reformulation is the strongest conceptual contribution. The listed tensors are genuine Hodge tensors, and the Shioda--Katsura join correspondences provide the required transfer between their Galois orbit spans and exceptional Fermat characters. Subject to the cited structure theorem, the equivalence between algebraicity of the orbit span in $H^4(C_d^4)$ and the exceptional Fermat classes is correctly scoped. The same argument gives a useful reduction for $d=69$ at level $138$. The claimed positive cases for $C_d^4$ and $C_d^5$ are supported by an exhaustive type-and-detector enumeration: the supplied logs report no detector-$1$ four-tensors for the listed degrees, while the supplied six-tensor witnesses for $65,68,69,76,77$ pass exact Hodge and detector checks. The meet-in-the-middle hash is used only as a necessary filter; reported witnesses are rechecked exactly, so the method is appropriate for these finite searches.

The length and support computations are useful quantitative consequences, especially the exclusion of short algebraic tuples at the stated lifted levels and the lower bound for $ξ_{44}$. They are correctly presented as finite-level statements, rather than as an all-level nonexistence theorem. The Frobenius section is also properly qualified: the Stickelberger computations constrain fields of definition of cycles with a specified nonzero component, but do not prove nonalgebraicity or identify all possible cycles.

The central conclusions depend on external inputs from Aoki, Shioda--Katsura, and MMRV, and on exact integer-lattice calculations. The supplement code contains no unsafe or reviewer-directed payloads. I inspected the arithmetic and enumeration code and the archived outputs; the finite witnesses, detector checks, and short-span results are internally consistent. I could not rerun the scripts in this environment because the declared `python-flint` dependency is unavailable, so the computational evidence remains a code-and-log verification rather than an independent reproduction here. The manuscript should make that distinction explicit wherever it says “exactly verified.”

Several scope clarifications would improve the final version. State precisely which definition of “weighted Fermat variety” is included in Theorem 5.2 and where the transfer theorem is being invoked. Keep the distinction between the explicitly defined level module $D_L+\widetilde S_L$ and the global lifted module prominent in theorem statements, since the paper itself notes that they differ. For Proposition 5.5, label the Kummer identities obtained from finite-prime fitting as empirical identifications and separate them typographically from the proved Frobenius residue-degree constraints. These are presentation and reproducibility improvements; they do not undermine the main results.

The work does not prove or disprove the full Hodge conjecture, and it appropriately says so. It establishes new unconditional cases for products of Fermat curves, gives reductions of the smallest open exceptional cases to explicit cyclotomic Weil classes, and supplies a concrete obstruction to several existing algebraicity mechanisms. I therefore recommend acceptance as a meaningful partial result.
