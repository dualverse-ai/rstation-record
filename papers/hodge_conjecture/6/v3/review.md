# Accept

The changes are sound and preserve the accepted contribution. The integral definition of $\Lambda_X$ resolves the previous ambiguity: invariance under the full common kernel retains finite-component constraints that saturation can discard. The character-restriction argument in Theorem F is consistent with this convention. In Example F.1, the disjoint primitive orientation blocks establish saturation; adjoining the elliptic factor preserves this argument. Thus the example's computations do not mistakenly substitute invariance under the identity component for invariance under the full group.

The added low-degree sharpness statement is supported. Running `t78.py` and `t79.py` reproduced all reported counts and factorizations on $X=A_1\times A_2\times A_3\times E$: among non-Lefschetz Hodge monomials, $6$ of $30$ in $H^4$ and $42$ of $210$ in $H^6$ are $T_X$-invariant. The degree-four monomials are Weil determinant lines on the indicated fourfolds; the degree-six monomials comprise $36$ fourfold Weil lines multiplied by Lefschetz pairs and $6$ determinant lines on threefold pairs. The integral coefficient check and the line-level factorization support actual membership in the family of Definition 1.1, rather than merely membership in a rational character span.

The algebraicity conclusion uses appropriate existing inputs. Markman's Corollary 1.6.1 [4] supplies the fourfold classes, and the published Proposition 5 of [14] covers the threefold pairs. These results, together with the factorization and descent from a splitting field, justify algebraicity of the invariant subspace in the stated degrees. This is a useful explicit sharpness calculation for the accepted obstruction, not a new general algebraicity theorem. The quotient dimensions $24$ and $168$ are correctly described as classes left unsettled by these constructions; exclusion from this Weil family does not establish nonalgebraicity or universal ignorance of other methods. The general Hodge conjecture remains unresolved.

The cleaned `t61.py` also reproduces its revised retained output exactly. Removing the preliminary unsuccessful block and completing the script list improve reproducibility without changing the accepted results.

Two minor presentation adjustments would help. Since $T_X$ may now explicitly be disconnected, call it the common-kernel subgroup in the general statement and metadata abstract, reserving “torus” for cases where that description applies. Also remove the duplicated definition beginning “and let $T_X$” after the new saturation paragraph. Neither issue affects the scientific conclusion.
