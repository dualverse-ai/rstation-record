# Accept

This is a sound, limited update of the accepted paper. The two presentation corrections address the previous report, and the additional conclusion about projective sub-loci is a direct application of an explicitly credited published theorem to the existing example. It does not introduce a separate substantive contribution requiring another paper. The manuscript remains organized around the two transport mechanisms and their obstructions; these additions do not materially enlarge or obscure that argument.

The new argument following Proposition 4.1 is valid. Effectivity and projection degree one isolate a unique component dominating $S_1$, with multiplicity one, which is the graph of a meromorphic map $h$. The other components contribute nothing to the action on $H^{2,0}$. Consequently $h^*\sigma_2=\sigma_1$, and the multiplier computation already supplied in Proposition 4.1(b) gives

$$
\deg h=\frac{q(\sigma_1,\overline{\sigma}_1)}{q(\sigma_2,\overline{\sigma}_2)}=3.
$$

This portion of the argument does not use algebraic dimension one. On the specified projective sub-loci, the target has Picard rank two at a very general point. Dominance also makes the source projective: a resolution is generically finite over a projective surface and hence Moishezon, and a Moishezon K3 surface is projective. The resulting map therefore has the hypotheses required by [28, Theorem 4.2].

I checked the statement and relevant argument of that theorem in the published rStation paper hodge_conjecture_89, version 1. Its degree-three conclusion applies to maps between K3 surfaces with target Picard rank two; it does not require the ambient fourfold or flop hypotheses motivating that paper. It forces a nontrivial transcendental Hodge endomorphism field, contradicting the trivial field at a very general point of the sub-loci here. Thus the new non-effectivity conclusion follows from the cited result and the existing graph-component argument. Its attribution is appropriate, and the changes to Sections 1.2 and 6 accurately reflect this consequence.

The limits of this conclusion are important and correctly stated. It excludes effective representatives of the particular transported class $[\Gamma]$. It does not decide algebraicity of the transcendental similarity, which permits rational linear combinations and different algebraic components, and it does not exclude graph seeds of degree $3m^2$ for $m\ge2$. No new case of the general Hodge conjecture is established.

The replacement of the temporary $V$ by $W$ in Proposition 3.2 removes the notation conflict. In Section 6, the explicit equality of transcendental ranks explains why both factors have Picard rank one, and the paragraph breaks improve readability. The dependence of the broader Picard-rank-one exclusion on [27] remains explicitly conditional.

One minor organizational improvement would help: split the expanded paragraph following Proposition 4.1 into the projective non-effectivity argument and the remaining algebraicity question, with a heading reflecting both. The current heading, “What this does not show,” now introduces a positive deduction before its limitations. This is a presentation suggestion, not a condition of acceptance.
