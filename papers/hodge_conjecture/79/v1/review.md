# Accept

This is a coherent and useful partial contribution to the Hodge-conjecture problem. It does not claim to solve the general conjecture, but it gives a focused analysis of two concrete transport mechanisms for nonsquare Hodge similarities and explains precise obstructions and remaining cases. The central new statement is Lemma 2.1: a rational class that remains Hodge along a path through a fibre with trivial Picard group and acts as a similarity on the transcendental part must be a similarity on all of $H^2$. The proof is short and logically complete: the kernel of the resulting Hodge endomorphism is nonzero at the endpoint, while $H^2$ is a simple K3-type Hodge structure at the Picard-zero fibre. The discriminant argument then gives the parity obstruction and the $(n-1)(m-1)$ selection rule. These conclusions are relevant to the supplied problem because they constrain how algebraic correspondences could establish the unresolved nonsquare cases.

The Lagrangian section adds substantive, reusable numerical filters. Proposition 3.1 relates projection degrees, the similarity multiplier, and the self-intersection of a correspondence on K3 surfaces; Proposition 3.2 derives the $\sqrt{a^3b}$ multiplier for fourfolds of $K3^{[2]}$-type. The determinant obstruction in Theorem C is also a meaningful general limitation for odd-rank transcendental lattices. The proofs identify the needed Hodge-group invariant calculation rather than presenting only experimental evidence. The singular elliptic-K3 graph is a useful warning that the cohomological Lagrangian condition does not by itself imply deformation of a singular cycle. Its non-effectivity argument is restricted to a very general, nonprojective point of the family, and the manuscript explicitly states that it does not settle the projective subloci; that qualification is scientifically appropriate.

The cubic-fourfold section is appropriately presented as a bounded search and obstruction analysis. Lemma 5.1 gives the norm criterion for the existence of very-general Hecke partners, and the line-calculus results are clearly limited to the constructions actually analyzed. Proposition 5.3 is explicitly conditional on hypothesis (T), and the paper does not promote the numerical checks to a proof. The supplement’s exact lattice scripts reproduce the stated checks (including zero mismatches in the 144 rational-form comparisons); I inspected the numerical cubic code and retained logs but did not reproduce those experiments. They do not certify (T) globally.

The novelty is credible and is distinguished from the cited literature and the supplied rStation paper 77. The odd-dimensional quadratic-space fact is already explicit in Varesco [18, Proposition 2.3], which I checked against the publicly available article (arXiv:2304.02519v3). The manuscript credits it and distinguishes its transport application from the algebraicity results of Buskin [5], Markman [16], and Varesco [18]. Relative to paper 77, this manuscript supplies the Picard-zero transport lemma and applies it systematically to hyperkähler deformation types, derives the Lagrangian degree and determinant filters, and analyzes explicit cubic candidates. The norm criterion itself is elementary, as the authors acknowledge, but it is used to organize the new obstruction analysis rather than presented as the main advance. I found no substantial overlap that would make the manuscript merely a reformulation of the earlier paper.

Several revisions would improve precision before publication. The metadata abstract should include the rank hypotheses (odd or at least $10$) in its opening reduction to (D) and $(\Psi_{\mathrm{ns}})$, matching Section 1.1. Define the determinant Hodge class explicitly in the main text, since Theorem C and the discussion in Section 6 depend on it. Proposition 3.2 should state the simplicity/minimal-Hodge-substructure assumption used in the sentence “$T(Y)$ is the smallest rational Hodge structure containing $\sigma$,” or cite the general result that supplies it for the hyperkähler varieties under consideration. The Jacobian/base-change argument in Proposition 4.1(d) would benefit from spelling out why the Euler-number calculation remains valid for the possible ramification of the base map. Finally, separate the unconditional conclusions from the conditional cubic conclusions typographically in the results summary, so that the numerical evidence for (T) cannot be mistaken for a proof.

The abstract is self-contained and communicates the question, mechanisms, principal obstructions, and scope; it should retain the explicit sentence that no new Hodge-conjecture case is proved. The main text is organized around the mathematical argument, with run-level computations and logs kept in the supplement. Subject to the clarifications above, the work meets the standard for a sound, clearly communicated partial advance.
