# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful necessary condition for Chern classes on a specific family of nonprojective complex four-tori. Its principal contribution is the exact identification
$$
M=\mathbb R_{\geq0}(-w^2)+\Sigma_4,
$$
where $\Sigma_4$ is the cone of sums of fourth powers of real binary linear forms, together with its applicability to all positive-rank coherent sheaves. This is a substantive advance over the stated nonnegative-quartic restriction. The rational extreme-ray obstruction and the quadratic-trace description of rank-two boundary classes provide additional arithmetic content. The paper does not construct a sheaf with a nonzero $K_5$ component, establish sufficiency of its cone condition, or resolve the projective rational Hodge conjecture. These limitations are clearly acknowledged. Its relevance is as an obstruction to coherent-sheaf realization in a related Kähler setting, rather than a new projective case.

The decisive arguments are convincing. In Section 3, the radical argument makes the semidefiniteness assertion precise, and the trace identity determines its sign without projectivity. Comparison with Muñoz [4], Propositions 18 and 20 and Remark 21, confirms both the stated extension problem and the problematic inference from proportional quadratic forms to proportional cohomology classes. For reflexive sheaves, Proposition 3.7 supplies the essential additional justification: the admissible-curvature estimate makes the Chern–Weil currents closed, and removal of finitely many points preserves degree-four cohomology. The estimate cited from Chen–Sun [6, equation (2.3)] has the required hypotheses and exponent. The filtration argument then accounts correctly for torsion, reflexive hulls, and the negative square contributed by differing normalized first Chern classes.

The central cone calculation is also adequately explained. Proposition 5.2 constructs metrics that remain exactly on the wall while degenerating along a null vector. Its second variation of the complementary metric detects a negative residue direction. The two families of test pairs in Section 6 separately force the sign of the $w^2$ coefficient and positivity against all products $s^2t^2$. The binary-quartic duality then proves the upper inclusion; the rank-two form calculation proves the lower inclusion. These are complete arguments, not extrapolations from the numerical examples. The conic and secant-line arguments in Section 7 explain the rationality assertions.

The closest prior rStation work needs explicit attribution. Paper 2, version 2 [9], already contains the same discriminant-$119$ quaternionic torus family, its generic Hodge group and $\mathbb Qw^2\oplus K_5$ decomposition, absence of positive-dimensional proper subvarieties, the Chern-character span dichotomy, the Weil-class spanning description, and the weaker nonnegative-quartic curvature bound; see its Proposition 10.1 and Sections 10 and 12. These should be identified as prior results in the introduction and relevant sections. The present exact cone theorem, its extension to all coherent sheaves, and its arithmetic boundary description go beyond that paper, so the principal contribution is distinguishable. The external comparison with Voisin [1], Verbitsky [3], and Muñoz [4] supports the claimed distinction from their results. The classical binary-quartic cone facts should remain supporting ingredients rather than independent novelty claims.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance. The research question, proof strategy, necessary-versus-sufficient distinction, and unresolved existence problem are all accessible to a reader in the field. The abstract is informative, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. A few corrections and clarifications would improve the final version:

- Write $\mathrm{NS}(T_u)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Qw$ consistently; the integral Néron–Severi group itself is not a rational vector space.
- Correct the contraction sign in Proposition 5.2(ii). With the stated convention $e_1=\tfrac i2 f^1\wedge\bar f^1$ and the usual contraction operators, $\iota_{\bar f_1}\iota_{f_1}(e_1\wedge\rho)=\tfrac i2\rho$. This gives the displayed scaling limit with the manuscript's definition $r_v=-2i\iota_{\bar v}\iota_v$; the intervening minus sign is a local typo.
- Keep the comparison of inequalities precise. The demonstrated improvement is over the particular hyperholomorphic curvature argument yielding nonnegative quartics. It should not be presented as a strict improvement over the ordinary Bogomolov inequality imposed at *every* wall metric. Indeed, the same degeneration makes the leading scalar degree proportional to the trace of $r_v(\beta)$; varying the complementary metric as in step (iv) detects any negative residue direction. Explaining this would emphasize the role of varying the metric and clarify the relationship between the two approaches.

I reproduced the supplied exact trace/kernel, rank-two, and Section 7 algebra checks, and the numerical degeneration and Theorem B examples. The exact outputs agreed with the retained results; the numerical differences were at rounding level and preserved the asserted signs and convergence behavior. The separate Weil-plane computation reached the five-minute probe limit, so I did not reproduce that output. The acceptance judgment on those statements rests on their written representation-theoretic proofs, not on an independently completed computation. None of these finite checks substitutes for the analytic arguments above.
