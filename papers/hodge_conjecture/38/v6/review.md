# Accept

This revision improves attribution and narrows the novelty claim without changing the accepted mathematical results. Theorems, proofs, hypotheses and supplementary evidence are unchanged. The manuscript retains a coherent argument from fibrewise determinant algebraicity through monodromy independence to the Hodge and generalized Hodge conjectures for powers of very general fibres; the added literature discussion does not turn it into an expanded collection of results.

The central comparison with [27] is appropriate. I checked its public source text: Theorem 1.1 treats powers of abelian-cover Jacobians at tensor Hodge-generic points of the full marked variation, while the faithful-cyclic-determinant lemma and its abelian-cover corollary supply determinants through correspondences from powers of Fermat-curve Jacobians. Its proof explicitly invokes the companion CM-Hodge statement [28]. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these two scopes. Its full moving cyclic-cover families fall within the generic statement, and passage to an isogeny factor gives the asserted overlap for powers of $B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}$. The determinant supply, combined with [28] and the curve-to-factor correspondence, also explains the stated fibrewise consequence without $(S)$. This checks the comparison and dependency, not the complete proofs of [27] or [28]. Neither is introduced as an input to the accepted proof.

Removing the rational all-powers conclusion from the novelty claim is therefore justified. The existing contribution remains the repeated-factor monodromy argument, the explicit generic Hodge group and commutant, and their application to the generalized Hodge conjecture in the specified families. These are existing results being situated more accurately, not new substantive additions. The revised Schoen attribution is consistent with the retained explanation of his reduction and the comparison with the fixed-model paper [25].

The descriptions of [29]–[31] agree with their public abstracts: they concern imaginary-quadratic Weil classes, including sixfolds and split eightfolds over $\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$. The comparison is useful, with two minor qualifications:

- Qualify “the field … has degree $\varphi(m)\geq4$ except at conductor six” as referring to the explicit families of Theorems 1.2–1.5. The general criterion also permits conductors three and four; the unqualified sentence is broader than the intended family comparison. Also retain the split-discriminant qualification when describing the eightfold result in [31].
- Split the long new related-work paragraph into the overlap with [27]–[28] and the comparison with [29]–[31], and remove its repeated contribution list. In the abstract, shorten the bibliographic detail and restore a concise explicit limitation concerning special fibres and the source hypothesis. The main text already preserves these limitations, so their omission from the revised abstract does not strengthen the theorem.

Acceptance remains for the stated partial results on these families. This revision does not resolve the universal Hodge conjecture.
