## 1. Introduction

Exceptional Hodge classes on an Abelian variety often arise from an action by a
CM field.  If $F$ acts on $H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$ and this vector space has
$F$-rank $n$, the determinant space

$$
 \bigwedge_F^n H^1(B,\mathbf Q)
$$

is a natural source of Hodge classes which need not be generated by divisors
[10].
Their algebraicity is the essential extra input in many Weil-type instances of
the Hodge conjecture.  The problem has two logically independent parts: one
must construct cycles representing the determinant classes, and one must
control the Hodge group well enough to show that divisors and determinants
generate every Hodge class on every self-product.

We first isolate a criterion which separates the cycle construction from the
monodromy argument, and then apply it both to a multi-standard class of
ramified cyclic Prym families and to a $2$-standard contraction class.  These
applications include families of unbounded dimension and unbounded moduli
dimension even at fixed conductor.  To introduce the first basic subfamily,
fix an odd
prime $p\geq5$, let $M_{0,p+1}$ be the moduli space of $p+1$ ordered
distinct points on $\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf C}$, and, for $d\geq3$, put

$$
 m=pd,\qquad n=p-1,\qquad q=\frac{p-1}{2},
 \qquad F=\mathbf Q(\zeta_m),
 \qquad
 \mathbf a_{p,d}=(1,1+d,\ldots,1+(p-1)d,m-p).
 \tag{1.1}
$$

For $\mathbf b=(b_0,\ldots,b_p)\in M_{0,p+1}(\mathbf C)$, choose linear forms
$\ell_i$ vanishing at $b_i$, and let $C_{p,d,\mathbf b}$ be the smooth
normalization of

$$
 y^m=\prod_{i=0}^p\ell_i(s,t)^{a_i(p,d)}.
 \tag{1.2}
$$

Let $e_m\in\mathbf Q[\mu_m]$ be the central idempotent cutting out the exact
cyclotomic factor.  If $N_me_m\in\mathbf Z[\mu_m]$, define

$$
 B_{p,d,\mathbf b}
 :=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(N_me_m:J(C_{p,d,\mathbf b})
 \longrightarrow J(C_{p,d,\mathbf b})\bigr).
 \tag{1.3}
$$

Its isogeny class is independent of $N_m$.  We call it the exact-conductor
Prym factor of (1.2).

### The main theorem

To state the criterion, let $n=2q\geq4$, let $m\geq3$, and let

$$
 \mathbf a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{n+1}),
 \qquad
 0<a_i<m,
 \qquad
 \sum_i a_i=sm.
$$

We impose three conditions:

- **(E) Exactness:** $\gcd(m,a_0,\ldots,a_{n+1})=1$.
- **(B) Balance:** for every $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$,
  $$
  \sum_i\left\langle\frac{ta_i}{m}\right\rangle=q+1.
  $$
- **(S) Source algebraicity:** on the degree-$m$ Fermat $n$-fold, the
  primitive middle character line $V(t\mathbf a)$ is algebraic for every
  $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$.

For ordered distinct branch points $\mathbf b\in M_{0,n+2}$, let
$C_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}$ be the corresponding cyclic cover and let
$B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}=e_mJ(C_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})$ denote its
exact-conductor factor up to isogeny.

**Theorem 1.1 (balanced exact cyclic-Prym criterion).**  Assume
$(E),(B),(S)$.

1. For every $\mathbf b$,
   $$
   \dim_FH^1(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b},\mathbf Q)=n,
   \qquad
   \operatorname{sig}_\sigma H^1(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})=(q,q),
   \tag{1.4}
   $$
   and
   $$
   W_F(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})
   :=\bigwedge_F^nH^1(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b},\mathbf Q)
   \subset
   \operatorname{cl}CH^q(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})_{\mathbf Q}.
   \tag{1.5}
   $$

2. Outside a countable union $\Sigma_{\mathbf a}$ of proper closed
   algebraic subsets of $M_{0,n+2}$,
   $$
   \operatorname{End}^0(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})=F,
   \qquad
   Hg(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})=
   \operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F
   \bigl(H^1(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b},\mathbf Q),h\bigr),
   \tag{1.6}
   $$
   and, for every $r\geq1$, both the rational Hodge conjecture and the
   generalized Hodge conjecture hold for $B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}^r$.

The fibrewise assertion has no genericity hypothesis.  Special fibres may
have extra endomorphisms, and we make no all-powers assertion for every
special fibre.

**Theorem 1.2 (standard-prime family).**  For every odd prime $p\geq5$ and
every $d\geq3$, the tuple $\mathbf a_{p,d}$ in (1.1) satisfies
$(E),(B),(S)$.  Consequently Theorem 1.1 applies.  In particular,

$$
 \dim B_{p,d,\mathbf b}=q\varphi(pd),
$$

and the rank, Abelian dimension, and family dimension $p-2$ are unbounded.
The member of smallest conductor occurs at $p=5,d=3$; it has conductor $15$
and dimension $16$.

**Theorem 1.3 (a non-single-prime-standard juxtaposed family).**  Let
$\ell\geq5$ be a prime with $\ell\equiv2\pmod3$, let $d\geq1$, and put

$$
 m=3\ell d,
 \qquad n=\ell+3,
 \qquad q=\frac{\ell+3}{2}.
$$

The juxtaposed tuple

$$
 \mathbf a_{\ell,d}
 =(
 1,1+\ell d,1+2\ell d,m-3;
 1,1+3d,\ldots,1+3(\ell-1)d,m-\ell
 )
 \tag{1.7}
$$

satisfies $(E),(B),(S)$, so Theorem 1.1 applies.  These tuples are not
single-prime-standard, even after permutation and multiplication by a unit.
Their rank $\ell+3$ and family dimension $\ell+2$ are unbounded.
Indeed, Dirichlet's theorem gives infinitely many primes
$\ell\equiv2\pmod3$.

The two preceding applications belong to a larger class.  Let $*$ denote
juxtaposition of residue tuples.

**Theorem 1.4 (finite multi-standard families).**  Let $k\geq1$, and let
$p_1,\ldots,p_k$ be odd primes, with repetitions allowed, such that
$p_j\mid m$.  Put

$$
 d_j=\frac{m}{p_j}>2,
 \qquad
 \alpha_{p_j,m}
 =\bigl(1,1+d_j,\ldots,1+(p_j-1)d_j,m-p_j\bigr),
$$

and

$$
 \mathbf a=\alpha_{p_1,m}*\cdots *\alpha_{p_k,m},
 \qquad
 n=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j+1)-2=2q.
$$

Assume $n\geq4$.  Then $\mathbf a$ satisfies $(E),(B),(S)$, so
Theorem 1.1 applies.  No distinctness assumption on the primes is required.

**Theorem 1.5 (two-standard contraction families).**  Let $d>2$, put
$m=2d$, and choose $1\leq i<d$ with $\gcd(i,d)=1$.  With entries read
modulo $m$, set

$$
 \eta_{d,i}=(i,i,i+d,i+d,-2i,-2i).
 \tag{1.8}
$$

For every $k\geq1$, the $k$-fold juxtaposition
$\eta_{d,i}^{*k}$ satisfies $(E),(B),(S)$, with

$$
 n=6k-2,\qquad q=3k-1.
$$

Consequently Theorem 1.1 applies.  The length-six tuple $\eta_{d,i}$ is not
one of the multi-standard tuples in Theorem 1.4.  In the smallest case
$d=3,i=1$,

$$
 m=6,\qquad
 \eta_{3,1}^{*k}=(1^{2k},4^{4k}),
 \tag{1.9}
$$

and no block allowed in Theorem 1.4 exists at conductor six.  Thus the entire
family (1.9) is genuinely outside the odd-prime multi-standard class, while

$$
 \dim_FH^1(B_k)=6k-2,\qquad
 \dim B_k=6k-2,\qquad
 \dim M_{0,6k}=6k-3
$$

are simultaneously unbounded with
$F=\mathbf Q(\zeta_6)=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$ fixed.

**Corollary 1.6 (odd-prime fixed-conductor families).**  Fix an odd prime
$p\mid m$ with $m/p>2$, and juxtapose $k$ copies of
$\alpha_{p,m}$.  For every positive integer $k$ such that

$$
 n_k=k(p+1)-2\geq4,
$$

the resulting exact Prym family satisfies Theorem 1.1, while

$$
 \dim_FH^1(B_k)=n_k,
 \qquad
 \dim B_k=\frac{n_k}{2}\varphi(m),
 \qquad
 \dim M_{0,n_k+2}=n_k-1.
$$

Thus rank, Abelian dimension, and Hurwitz-base dimension are simultaneously
unbounded with both the conductor $m$ and the cyclotomic field
$F=\mathbf Q(\zeta_m)$ fixed.

**Theorem 1.7 (the $A_1$ boundary).**  For $p=3,d\geq3$, the fibrewise
conclusion of Theorem 1.2 holds with $n=2,q=1$, and every power of a very
general exact-conductor
factor satisfies the rational and generalized Hodge conjectures.  The generic
endomorphism algebra is not $F$.  If $d\ne4$, it is a possibly split
totally indefinite quaternion algebra over $F^+$ with $F$ as a maximal
subfield.  If $d=4$,
the two real monodromy factors form one graph and the generic commutant is a
degree-four central simple algebra over $\mathbf Q$.  In both cases the
simple isogeny factors have Albert type I or II.

**Corollary 1.8 (prime-power conductors).**  Let $m$ be a prime power and
let $n=2q\geq4$.  Every residue tuple $\mathbf a$ satisfying $(E),(B)$
satisfies all conclusions of Theorem 1.1; no separate source-algebraicity
hypothesis is required.

### Structure of the proof

The criterion has two branches.  The algebraic-cycle branch is fibrewise.  For a
general exact cyclic cover with $n+2$ branch points, we construct a common
finite quotient

$$
 C^n/(N\rtimes\mathfrak S_n)\simeq Z_{\mathbf c}/K_{\mathbf a},
 \tag{1.10}
$$

where $Z_{\mathbf c}$ is a Delsarte $n$-fold.  The top-dimensional fibre
product gives a Chow correspondence with a normalized left inverse on the
determinant block.  Hypothesis $(S)$ makes the whole rational source block
algebraic.  The Poincaré divisor
on $C\times B$, its $n$-th external power, and the small diagonal of $B$
then carry these classes to $W_F(B)$.

The group-theoretic branch is generic.  Moving the branch point of exponent
$a_0$ produces rank-$n$ cyclotomic Jordan--Pochhammer representations.
Rohde's theorem gives $SU(q,q)$ on every real factor.  A characteristic-zero
Goursat argument reduces independence to projective equivalence of two
Jordan--Pochhammer systems.  A nonresonant generator has a distinguished
multiplicity-$(n-1)$ eigenspace, while a resonant generator is a nontrivial
unipotent pseudoreflection; in either case its normalized lift removes every
projective scalar.  The ordered generator product and exactness then recover
the full residue tuple, excluding a graph between distinct real factors.  The determinant Hodge classes then
squeeze the generic Hodge group to $\operatorname{Res}SU$.  The first
fundamental theorem for $SL_n$ shows that contractions and determinant
tensors generate the Hodge classes on every power.  Finally, Abdulali's theorem
upgrades the all-powers usual Hodge conjecture to the generalized Hodge
conjecture.

For each multi-standard block, Aoki's standard-cycle theorem verifies the
source condition, and the Shioda--Ran juxtaposition theorem recalled by Aoki
may be iterated finitely.  Theorem 1.5 starts from Aoki's explicit
$2$-standard surface cycle, juxtaposes two copies, and applies the
Shioda--Ran deletion theorem to remove a self-opposite pair before iterating.
For $p=3$, the self-duality of the $SL_2$ standard
representation instead yields a quaternionic commutant; the $SL_2$ first
fundamental theorem and Hazama's result close that boundary separately.

### Relation to earlier work

Schoen's simple-tuple method [13] isolates a selected determinant subspace
$U$ in self-products of varieties with cyclic automorphisms; it is not a
statement that the whole Hodge ring is algebraic.  The tuples (1.1) are never
simple: their entries cannot be paired with their negatives.  The juxtaposed
tuples (1.7) lie in the semigroup generated by two Aoki-standard blocks but
are not themselves single-prime-standard.  Their source classes come from
Aoki's standard-cycle theorem and the Shioda--Ran juxtaposition theorem as
recalled by Aoki [2].  Finite iteration of juxtaposition is already built into
Aoki's standard semigroup, and his later
work proves all-self-product Hodge-conjecture results for CM Abelian varieties
of Fermat type [3] and generalized Catalan Jacobians [21].  We therefore make
no novelty claim for finite standard juxtaposition, the standard-semigroup
mechanism, or all-powers Hodge results in those fixed CM settings.  Those
results do not apply directly to our moving exact Prym factors, whose branch
points are arbitrary and whose $F$-ranks are unbounded.

Schoen also studied cyclic covers of projective space branched along
$v+2$ hyperplanes and applications to the generalized Hodge conjecture [14].
His Theorem 3.4 proves the generalized Hodge conjecture for a selected
determinant substructure $U'$ of a generalized Prym under the quasi-simple
branching-data condition, by relating it to a Fermat hypersurface.  We
therefore make no novelty claim for the primitive-factor/Fermat-correspondence
mechanism by itself.

For the covers in this paper, Schoen's reduction is closer than the
quasi-simple case alone suggests.  In his notation, if $q_X$ is the genus of
the quotient curve, $s$ is the number of branch points, and
$h=2q_X-2+s$, then one may take $r=1$ when $X=\mathbf P^1$.  There are
then no preliminary inverse pairs, the remaining $v+2=s$ exponents generate
the unit ideal precisely by condition $(E)$, and

$$
 v=h-(2q_X+2r-2)=s-2=n.
$$

Condition $(B)$ makes every primitive eigenspace multiplicity equal to
$n/2$, so the determinant Hodge width is $w=0$.  Schoen's Remark 3.5
states that, when $w<v$, it suffices to prove the corresponding conjecture
for the degree-$m$ Fermat $v$-fold.  In the present width-zero situation,
condition $(S)$ supplies exactly the required algebraic primitive orbit.
Consequently the fibrewise determinant conclusion of Theorem 5.1 is already
contained in Schoen's reduction.  Sections 3--5 give a self-contained
exact-conductor Chow-correspondence proof, including the rational-orbit
projector, but we make no novelty claim for that conclusion.

## 2. Exact-conductor Pryms and balanced signatures

Fix $p\ge5,d\geq3$, put $m=pd$, and abbreviate
$C=C_{p,d,\mathbf b}$, $B=B_{p,d,\mathbf b}$.  Since the first entry of
$\mathbf a_{p,d}$ is $1$,

$$
 \gcd(m,a_0(p,d),\ldots,a_p(p,d))=1.
$$

Thus (1.2) is a connected exact degree-$m$ cover.  The exact central
idempotent in $\mathbf Q[\mu_m]$ cuts out the direct sum of the eigenspaces
indexed by $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$.  The image construction
(1.3) gives an Abelian subvariety up to isogeny, and

$$
 H^1(B,\mathbf Q)=e_mH^1(C,\mathbf Q).
 \tag{2.1}
$$

This equality means the projector-induced isomorphism of rational Hodge
structures; it is not an identification obtained from an arbitrary abstract
isomorphism of vector spaces.

More generally, let $\mathbf a$ have length $n+2=2q+2$ and satisfy
$(E),(B)$.  For every primitive $t$, the Chevalley--Weil formula gives

$$
 h_t^{0,1}
 =-1+\sum_i\left\langle\frac{ta_i}{m}\right\rangle=q.
 \tag{2.2}
$$

Since no $ta_i$ is zero modulo $m$, the corresponding sum for $-t$ is
$(n+2)-(q+1)=q+1$.  Hence $h_t^{1,0}=q$ as well.  Thus $(B)$ is
exactly the balanced-signature condition needed in Theorem 1.1.

**Proposition 2.1.**  The $F$-vector space $H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$ has rank
$n=p-1$.  Every primitive embedding has signature $(q,q)$.

**Proof.**  Let $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$, represented by
$1\leq t<m$, and let $r\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}$ be its least positive residue
modulo $d$.  Multiplication by $t$ permutes the $p$ congruence classes
modulo $p$, so the least positive residues of

$$
 t(1+jd),\qquad 0\leq j\leq p-1,
$$

are, in some order,

$$
 r,r+d,\ldots,r+(p-1)d.
$$

The least positive residue of $t(pd-p)$ is $p(d-r)$.  Therefore

$$
 \sum_{i=0}^p\langle ta_i(p,d)\rangle_m
 =pr+d\frac{p(p-1)}2+p(d-r)
 =m\frac{p+1}{2}.
 \tag{2.3}
$$

The Chevalley--Weil formula gives

$$
 h_t^{0,1}
 =-1+\sum_i\left\langle\frac{ta_i(p,d)}m\right\rangle=q,
 \qquad
 h_t^{1,0}=q.
 \tag{2.4}
$$

The two Hodge indices may be interchanged by changing the deck-character
convention.  In either convention, the $t$-eigenspace has complex dimension
$n=2q$ and signature $(q,q)$.  Running through the primitive embeddings of
$F$ proves the assertion.  $\square$

It follows that

$$
 \dim_{\mathbf Q}H^1(B,\mathbf Q)=n\varphi(m),
 \qquad
 \dim B=q\varphi(m).
 \tag{2.5}
$$

The canonical polarization of $J(C)$ restricts, up to isogeny, to a
polarization on $B$.  The Rosati involution sends the deck transformation to
its inverse; hence its restriction to $F$ is complex conjugation.  If
$F^+$ is the maximal real subfield, the polarization is encoded by a
nondegenerate $F/F^+$-anti-Hermitian form $h$ on $H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$.

Finally, since each determinant line has Hodge type $(q,q)$,

$$
 W_F(B)=\bigwedge_F^nH^1(B,\mathbf Q)
 \subset H^n(B,\mathbf Q)
 \tag{2.6}
$$

is a rational Hodge substructure consisting entirely of Hodge classes.

The second family has the same balance for a different reason.

**Proposition 2.2.**  Let $\ell,d,m,n,q$ and $\mathbf a_{\ell,d}$ be as
in Theorem 1.3.  The exact factor $B_{\ell,d,\mathbf b}$ has $F$-rank
$n=\ell+3$, and every primitive embedding has signature $(q,q)$.

**Proof.**  Let $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$.  If $r_3$ is the
least positive residue of $t$ modulo $\ell d$, multiplication by $t$
turns the first block, up to permutation, into

$$
 (r_3,r_3+\ell d,r_3+2\ell d,-3r_3),
$$

whose least-positive-residue sum is $2m$.  If $r_\ell$ is the least
positive residue of $t$ modulo $3d$, the second block becomes

$$
 (r_\ell,r_\ell+3d,\ldots,r_\ell+3(\ell-1)d,-\ell r_\ell),
$$

whose residue sum is

$$
 \ell r_\ell+3d\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}2
 +\ell(3d-r_\ell)=\frac{\ell+1}{2}m.
$$

The total normalized sum is therefore

$$
 2+\frac{\ell+1}{2}=\frac{\ell+5}{2}=q+1.
$$

Condition $(B)$ and the preceding general calculation give the result.
The entry $1$ gives $(E)$.  $\square$

The same calculation is additive under an arbitrary finite juxtaposition.

**Proposition 2.3.**  Let $p_1,\ldots,p_k$, $m$, $d_j$,
$\alpha_{p_j,m}$, and $\mathbf a$ be as in Theorem 1.4.  Then
$\mathbf a$ satisfies $(E),(B)$.  Its exact factor has
$F$-rank

$$
 n=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j+1)-2,
$$

and every primitive embedding has signature $(n/2,n/2)$.

**Proof.**  Each block contains the entry $1$, so exactness is immediate.
Let $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$, and let
$r_j\in\{1,\ldots,d_j-1\}$ be its least positive residue modulo $d_j$.
Write $t=r_j+c_jd_j$.  Modulo $p_j$, the coefficient of $d_j$ in
$t(1+s d_j)$ is $c_j+ts$; because $t$ is a unit modulo $p_j$, this
permutes the $p_j$ progression terms of the $j$-th block.  Their least
positive residues are

$$
 r_j,r_j+d_j,\ldots,r_j+(p_j-1)d_j,
$$

and the final entry has least positive residue $p_j(d_j-r_j)$.  Hence the
residue sum of this block is

$$
 p_jr_j+d_j\frac{p_j(p_j-1)}2+p_j(d_j-r_j)
 =m\frac{p_j+1}{2}.
$$

Summing over the blocks gives

$$
 \sum_i\left\langle\frac{ta_i}{m}\right\rangle
 =\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{p_j+1}{2}
 =\frac{n+2}{2}=\frac n2+1,
$$

which is $(B)$.  The signature statement follows from the general
Chevalley--Weil calculation preceding Proposition 2.1.

Since every $p_j$ is odd, $n$ is even.  $\square$

The contraction family has a different balance calculation.

**Proposition 2.4.**  Let $d,i,m,\eta_{d,i}$ be as in Theorem 1.5.  For
every $k\geq1$, the tuple $\eta_{d,i}^{*k}$ satisfies
$(E),(B)$.  Its exact factor has $F$-rank $6k-2$, and every
primitive embedding has signature $(3k-1,3k-1)$.  The tuple
$\eta_{d,i}$ is not contained in Theorem 1.4; for $m=6$, neither is any
$\eta_{3,1}^{*k}$.

**Proof.**  Introduce the four-term tuple

$$
 Q_{d,i}=(i,i+d,-2i,d)\pmod {2d}.
 \tag{2.7}
$$

There is an equality of residue multisets

$$
 Q_{d,i}\mathbin{\uplus}Q_{d,i}
 =\eta_{d,i}\mathbin{\uplus}(d,d).
 \tag{2.8}
$$

Let $t\in(\mathbf Z/2d\mathbf Z)^\times$, write
$\operatorname{res}_{2d}$ for least positive residue modulo $2d$, and
let $r$ be the least positive residue of $ti$ modulo $d$.  The integer $t$ is odd, so the
least positive residues of $ti$ and $t(i+d)$ are $r$ and $r+d$, in
some order; those of $-2ti$ and $td$ are $2(d-r)$ and $d$.
Consequently

$$
 \sum_{a\in Q_{d,i}}\operatorname{res}_{2d}(ta)=4d=2m.
$$

Using (2.8), the residue sum of $t\eta_{d,i}$ is therefore

$$
 2(2m)-2d=3m.
$$

For $k$ copies the normalized sum is $3k$, which is
$q+1$ for $q=3k-1$.  This proves $(B)$, and the general
Chevalley--Weil calculation gives the rank and signature.

Exactness follows from $\gcd(i,d)=1$.

A multi-standard tuple of length six would have to consist of one
$5$-standard block.  Its five progression entries are distinct, whereas
$\eta_{d,i}$ has at most three distinct residues, so no unit multiple and
permutation can identify the two.  Finally, at $m=6$ the only odd prime
divisor is $3$, but $m/3=2$, so Theorem 1.4 permits no block at all.
$\square$

## 3. A universal Kummer--Delsarte common quotient

We prove a quotient theorem in arbitrary dimension.  Let $n\geq1$, let
$m\geq2$, and choose distinct points
$b_0,\ldots,b_{n+1}\in\mathbf P^1$.  Suppose

$$
 0<a_i<m,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=0}^{n+1}a_i=sm,\qquad s\in\mathbf Z,
 \qquad
 \gcd(m,a_0,\ldots,a_{n+1})=1.
 \tag{3.1}
$$

Let $C$ be the normalization of

$$
 y^m=\prod_{i=0}^{n+1}\ell_i(s,t)^{a_i}.
 \tag{3.2}
$$

Set

$$
 N=\{(r_1,\ldots,r_n)\in(\mathbf Z/m)^n:\sum_jr_j=0\},
 \qquad
 G=N\rtimes\mathfrak S_n.
 \tag{3.3}
$$

The symmetric product $\operatorname{Sym}^n\mathbf P^1$ is $\mathbf P^n$.
For an effective divisor $D=p_1+\cdots+p_n$, put

$$
 L_i(D)=\prod_{j=1}^n\ell_i(p_j).
 \tag{3.4}
$$

Any $n+1$ of the $L_i$ are linearly independent: a nonzero binary form of
degree $n$ cannot vanish at $n+1$ distinct points.  Hence there is a unique
relation up to scale,

$$
 \sum_{i=0}^{n+1}c_iL_i=0,
 \qquad c_i\ne0.
 \tag{3.5}
$$

Define the smooth Delsarte hypersurface

$$
 Z_{\mathbf c}:\quad
 \sum_{i=0}^{n+1}c_ix_i^m=0\subset\mathbf P^{n+1}.
 \tag{3.6}
$$

Let

$$
 A=(\mu_m)^{n+2}/\mu_m^{\mathrm{diag}},
 \qquad
 \chi_{\mathbf a}([\epsilon_i])=\prod_i\epsilon_i^{a_i},
 \qquad
 K_{\mathbf a}=\ker\chi_{\mathbf a}.
 \tag{3.7}
$$

The character is well defined because $\sum a_i\equiv0\pmod m$, and it is
surjective by the exactness assumption.

**Theorem 3.1.**  After choosing compatible Kummer generators, there is an
isomorphism of finite normal covers of the hyperplane
$S=\{\sum c_iZ_i=0\}\simeq\mathbf P^n$:

$$
 C^n/G\simeq Z_{\mathbf c}/K_{\mathbf a}.
 \tag{3.8}
$$

Let $Y$ denote the common quotient and let

$$
 \Gamma=[C^n\times_YZ_{\mathbf c}]_{\mathrm{top}}
 \in CH^n(C^n\times Z_{\mathbf c})_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{3.9}
$$

Then

$$
 {}^t\Gamma\circ\Gamma
 =|K_{\mathbf a}|\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_g,
 \qquad
 \Gamma\circ{}^t\Gamma
 =|G|\sum_{k\in K_{\mathbf a}}\Gamma_k.
 \tag{3.10}
$$

Consequently,

$$
 R=\frac1{|G||K_{\mathbf a}|}{}^t\Gamma
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 R_*\Gamma_*|_U=\operatorname{id}_U
 \tag{3.11}
$$

for every rational cohomology subspace $U$ fixed pointwise by $G$.

**Proof.**  Write $y_j$ for the Kummer coordinate on the $j$-th factor of
$C^n$, and put $Y_C=\prod_jy_j$.  The subgroup $N$ is precisely the
kernel of the product character on $(\mu_m)^n$.  Therefore

$$
 \mathbf C(C^n)^N
 =\mathbf C((\mathbf P^1)^n)(Y_C),
 \qquad
 Y_C^m=\prod_{i=0}^{n+1}L_i(D)^{a_i}.
 \tag{3.12}
$$

The expression is symmetric in the $p_j$.  Taking $\mathfrak S_n$-fixed
fields gives

$$
 \mathbf C(C^n)^G=\mathbf C(S)(w_C),
 \qquad
 w_C^m=\prod_i(L_i/L)^{a_i},
 \tag{3.13}
$$

where $L$ is any base linear form and $w_C=Y_C/L^s$.  The right-hand side
is not a $p$-th power for any prime $p\mid m$, because some $a_i$ is not
divisible by $p$.  Thus the extension in (3.13) has degree $m$.

On $Z_{\mathbf c}$, set $Z_i=x_i^m$ and

$$
 w_Z=\frac{\prod_ix_i^{a_i}}{L(Z)^s}.
 \tag{3.14}
$$

This function has $A$-character $\chi_{\mathbf a}$, and

$$
 \mathbf C(Z_{\mathbf c})^{K_{\mathbf a}}
 =\mathbf C(S)(w_Z),
 \qquad
 w_Z^m=\prod_i(L_i/L)^{a_i}.
 \tag{3.15}
$$

The normalization of $S$ in this common function field is unique, proving
(3.8).  The qualification concerning Kummer generators records the harmless
constant introduced if the evaluation forms in (3.4) are rescaled.

Let $p:C^n\to Y$ and $q:Z_{\mathbf c}\to Y$ be the quotient maps.  Their
generic fibres are $G$- and $K_{\mathbf a}$-torsors.  The top-dimensional
cycles of the two self-fibre products are

$$
 [C^n\times_YC^n]_{\mathrm{top}}=\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_g,
 \qquad
 [Z_{\mathbf c}\times_YZ_{\mathbf c}]_{\mathrm{top}}
 =\sum_{k\in K_{\mathbf a}}\Gamma_k.
$$

In the triple fibre products defining the two compositions, forgetting the
middle factor has generic degree $|K_{\mathbf a}|$ or $|G|$, respectively.
This proves (3.10).  A component over a proper closed subset of $Y$ has
dimension less than $n$, so ramification creates no extra top-dimensional
term.  Since

$$
 |G|=m^{n-1}n!,
 \qquad
 |K_{\mathbf a}|=m^n,
$$

(3.11) follows from (3.10).  On the whole cohomology of $C^n$, the operator
$R_*\Gamma_*$ is the average $|G|^{-1}\sum_gg_*$, not the identity.
$\square$

We shall use Theorem 3.1 on a determinant subspace.  Suppose that
$H\subset H^1(C,\mathbf Q)$ has rank $n$ over a number field $F$, and
let

$$
 U=\bigwedge_F^nH\subset H^n(C^n,\mathbf Q)
 \tag{3.16}
$$

be the rational realization obtained by the separability projector for
$F/\mathbf Q$ followed by normalized alternation.  After complexification,
each determinant tensor lies in an $n$-fold tensor product with one fixed
deck character.  The subgroup $N$ acts by
$\zeta_m^{t\sum r_j}=1$.  A geometric permutation of the curve factors
contributes the Koszul sign on degree-one cohomology, while determinant
alternation contributes the same sign.  Their product is $+1$.  Hence $G$
fixes $U$ pointwise, and (3.11) applies.

## 4. Fermat source classes

We first specialize Theorem 3.1 to $n=p-1$, $m=pd$, and the tuple (1.1).  The
integer in (3.1) is then

$$
 s=\frac{p+1}{2}=q+1.
$$

Put

$$
 H=e_mH^1(C_{p,d,\mathbf b},\mathbf Q),
 \qquad
 U_{p,d}=\bigwedge_F^nH\subset H^n(C_{p,d,\mathbf b}^n,\mathbf Q).
 \tag{4.1}
$$

The preceding section supplies

$$
 C_{p,d,\mathbf b}^n/(N\rtimes\mathfrak S_n)
 \simeq Z_{\mathbf c}/K_{\mathbf a_{p,d}}
 \tag{4.2}
$$

and a split injection
$\Gamma_*:U_{p,d}\to H^n(Z_{\mathbf c},\mathbf Q)$.

**Theorem 4.1.**  For every odd prime $p\ge5$, every $d\geq3$, and every
branch configuration
$\mathbf b$,

$$
 \Gamma_*U_{p,d}\subset
 \operatorname{cl}CH^q(Z_{\mathbf c})_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{4.3}
$$

**Proof.**  After adjoining finitely many constants, $Z_{\mathbf c}$ becomes
the Fermat $n$-fold of degree $m$.  For
$t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$, the $t$-component

$$
 (U_{p,d})_t=\bigwedge^nH^1(C)_t
$$

is one-dimensional.  The common quotient carries it nontrivially to the
primitive middle character line

$$
 V(\varepsilon t\mathbf a_{p,d})\subset H^n(Z_{\mathbf c},\mathbf C),
 \tag{4.4}
$$

where $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}$ depends only on the pullback convention.
The character containment and the asserted nonvanishing are precisely the
character-compatible quotient lemma proved below (Lemma 5.0), applied with
$n=p-1$.  Thus the map onto this one-dimensional line is surjective.

The tuple $\mathbf a_{p,d}$ is Aoki's $p$-standard character with
$i=1$ and $m=pd$:

$$
 (i,i+d,\ldots,i+(p-1)d,-pi).
 \tag{4.5}
$$

Every unit multiple is standard again.  Indeed, if $r$ is the least positive
residue of $t$ modulo $d$, then the first $p$ entries of
$t\mathbf a_{p,d}$, after permutation, are

$$
 r,r+d,\ldots,r+(p-1)d,
$$

and the final entry is $-pr$ modulo $pd$.  Moreover
$\gcd(r,d)=1$, so $d/\gcd(r,d)=d>2$, as required in Aoki's theorem.
Aoki's standard-cycle theorem [2, Theorem 2-1] therefore gives an algebraic
codimension-$q$ cycle with nonzero projection to each line in (4.4).  Replacing
$t$ by $-t$ absorbs the global sign $\varepsilon$.

Let $A_{\mathbf Q}$ be the image of
$CH^q(Z_{\mathbf c})_{\mathbf Q}$ in rational cohomology.  The preceding
argument gives a cycle with nonzero projection to each character line.
The space $A_{\mathbf Q}\otimes\mathbf C$ is stable under the diagonal
automorphism group, since those automorphisms carry algebraic cycles to
algebraic cycles.  This finite-group action is semisimple.  Applying the
complex character idempotent to a class with nonzero projection onto the
one-dimensional isotypic line therefore contains that whole line and gives

$$
 V(\varepsilon t\mathbf a_{p,d})
 \subset A_{\mathbf Q}\otimes_{\mathbf Q}\mathbf C
 \qquad(t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times).
$$

On the other hand,

$$
 (\Gamma_*U_{p,d})\otimes\mathbf C
 =\bigoplus_{t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times}
 V(\varepsilon t\mathbf a_{p,d}).
 \tag{4.6}
$$

Both sides descend to rational subspaces.  Faithful flatness of
$\mathbf Q\to\mathbf C$ gives (4.3).  Notice that the conclusion concerns
the full Galois orbit; an individual cyclotomic character projector need not be
rational.  $\square$

**Proposition 4.2.**  The tuple $\mathbf a_{p,d}$ is not simple in the
earlier simple-tuple sense used by Schoen for any $p\ge5,d\geq3$.  This
statement is separate from the quasi-simple branching condition in Schoen's
1989 theorem.

**Proof.**  The first $p$ entries of $\mathbf a_{p,d}$ are congruent to $1$ modulo
$d$.  The sum of any two of them is congruent to $2$ modulo $d$, hence
cannot be $0$ because $d\geq3$.  Thus no two of these $p$ entries are
opposite modulo $pd$.  The last entry can be opposite to at most one of
them, leaving $p-1\ge4$ entries which cannot be paired.  $\square$

Theorem 4.1 is therefore not an application of Schoen's simple-tuple theorem.

We now verify the source condition for the juxtaposed family.  Write

$$
 \alpha_3=(1,1+\ell d,1+2\ell d,m-3),
$$

$$
 \alpha_\ell=(1,1+3d,\ldots,1+3(\ell-1)d,m-\ell),
$$

so that $\mathbf a_{\ell,d}=\alpha_3*\alpha_\ell$.

**Theorem 4.3.**  For every prime $\ell\geq5$ with
$\ell\equiv2\pmod3$, every $d\geq1$, every primitive $t$, and every
branch configuration, the line

$$
 V(t\mathbf a_{\ell,d})
$$

on the corresponding degree-$m$ Fermat $n$-fold is algebraic.  Hence the
whole rational source block in Theorem 3.1 is algebraic.

**Proof.**  The first block is $3$-standard with
$m=3(\ell d)$; the second is $\ell$-standard with
$m=\ell(3d)$.  For primitive $t$, let $r_3$ and $r_\ell$ be as in
Proposition 2.2.  Up to permutation,

$$
 t\alpha_3=\alpha_{3,r_3},
 \qquad
 t\alpha_\ell=\alpha_{\ell,r_\ell}.
$$

Moreover

$$
 \gcd(r_3,\ell d)=1,
 \qquad \ell d>2,
 \qquad
 \gcd(r_\ell,3d)=1,
 \qquad 3d>2.
$$

Aoki's Theorem 2-1 [2] therefore proves algebraicity of both standard
character lines.  The two blocks belong to Fermat dimensions $2$ and
$\ell-1$, both even, and

$$
 n=2+(\ell-1)+2.
$$

The Shioda--Ran juxtaposition theorem, as recalled by Aoki
[2, Theorem 1-4(i)], therefore applies and proves
algebraicity of

$$
 V(t(\alpha_3*\alpha_\ell)).
$$

The rational-orbit descent is the same finite-group idempotent argument as in
the proof of Theorem 4.1.  $\square$

**Proposition 4.4.**  The tuple $\mathbf a_{\ell,d}$ is not a
single-prime-standard character, even after permutation and multiplication by
a unit modulo $m$.

**Proof.**  A prime-standard character associated with a prime $r$ has
length $r+1$.  Equality with the length $\ell+5$ would force
$r=\ell+4$.  But $\ell\equiv2\pmod3$ implies
$3\mid(\ell+4)$, and $\ell+4>3$.  Thus $r$ is not prime.  $\square$

The preceding source argument admits arbitrary finite iteration.

**Theorem 4.5.**  Let $\mathbf a$ be any multi-standard tuple in
Theorem 1.4.  For every $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$, the primitive
middle character line

$$
 V(t\mathbf a)
$$

on the degree-$m$ Fermat $n$-fold is algebraic.  Consequently the whole
rational source block in Theorem 3.1 is algebraic for every branch
configuration.

**Proof.**  Fix $t$, and let $r_j$ be its least positive residue modulo
$d_j=m/p_j$.  Up to a permutation within the block,

$$
 t\alpha_{p_j,m}
 =\bigl(r_j,r_j+d_j,\ldots,r_j+(p_j-1)d_j,m-p_jr_j\bigr).
$$

Since $t$ is a unit modulo $m$,

$$
 \gcd(r_j,d_j)=1,
 \qquad d_j>2.
$$

Thus Aoki's standard-cycle theorem [2, Theorem 2-1] proves algebraicity of
the character line attached to every translated block.  Its Fermat dimension
is $p_j-1$, which is even.

The Shioda--Ran juxtaposition theorem, as recalled by Aoki
[2, Theorem 1-4(i)], says that juxtaposing two algebraic even-dimensional
character lines produces an algebraic character line in dimension equal to
the sum of the two dimensions plus two.  This new dimension remains even.
Iterating the theorem therefore proves algebraicity
of

$$
 V\bigl(t(\alpha_{p_1,m}*\cdots *\alpha_{p_k,m})\bigr).
$$

No distinctness or non-isomorphism hypothesis is present in the
Shioda--Ran juxtaposition theorem: the two source character lines may come from
the same standard block, and the argument therefore permits repeated primes and
repeated translated blocks.

Juxtaposition is associative up to permutation, and the character-line claim
is permutation invariant, so the induction is unambiguous.  Finally, the
finite-group idempotent and rational-orbit descent used in Theorem 4.1 show
that the complete rational source block, rather than merely each complex
line separately, is algebraic.  $\square$

The contraction family uses both directions of the Shioda--Ran induction.

**Theorem 4.6.**  Let $d,i,m$ and $\eta_{d,i}$ be as in Theorem 1.5.
For every $k\geq1$ and every
$t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$, the primitive middle character line

$$
 V(t\eta_{d,i}^{*k})
$$

on the degree-$m$ Fermat $(6k-2)$-fold is algebraic.  Consequently the
complete rational source block is algebraic for every branch configuration.

**Proof.**  Begin with the surface character $Q_{d,i}$ in (2.7).  Aoki's
explicit $p=2$ curve [2, Remark 2-2] represents this character.  If $t$
is primitive modulo $2d$, then $td\equiv d\pmod {2d}$ and

$$
 tQ_{d,i}=Q_{d,ti}
$$

with entries read modulo $2d$.  Moreover
$d/\gcd(ti,d)=d>2$, so the same remark represents every primitive
translate.

Apply the Shioda--Ran juxtaposition theorem [2, Theorem 1-4(i)] to two
copies.  It makes the character line of
$tQ_{d,i}*tQ_{d,i}$ algebraic.  Since $td=d$, the translated multiset
identity (2.8) reads

$$
 tQ_{d,i}\mathbin{\uplus}tQ_{d,i}
 =t\eta_{d,i}\mathbin{\uplus}(d,d).
$$

The pair $(d,d)=(d,-d)$ is an inverse pair.  The deletion statement
[2, Theorem 1-4(ii)] therefore removes it and proves algebraicity of
$V(t\eta_{d,i})$.  Iterating Theorem 1-4(i) proves the assertion for
$\eta_{d,i}^{*k}$.  The finite-group idempotent argument of Theorem 4.1
then descends the complete primitive orbit to a rational algebraic source
block.  $\square$

The complete source condition is automatic at prime-power conductor.

**Theorem 4.7 (Aoki's prime-power source theorem).**  Let $m$ be a prime
power and let $n=2q$ be even.  Every balanced residue tuple $\mathbf a$
at level $m$ satisfies $(S)$.

**Proof.**  Balance says that every primitive translate
$t\mathbf a$ is a middle Hodge character of the degree-$m$ Fermat
$n$-fold.  Aoki's Corollary 2-3 [2] proves the Hodge conjecture for the
degree-$m$ Fermat variety in every dimension when $m$ is a prime power.
Hence the rational Galois block generated by the lines
$V(t\mathbf a)$ is spanned by algebraic cycle classes, which is precisely
$(S)$.  Notice that exactness is not used here.
$\square$

Corollary 1.8 follows by combining Theorem 4.7 with Theorems 5.1, 6.5, 7.1,
and 8.1.

## 5. Transfer to the exact Prym factor

We now pass from the curve product to the exact factor
$B=B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}$.  Put

$$
 U_{\mathbf a}=\bigwedge_F^ne_mH^1(C,\mathbf Q)
 \subset H^n(C^n,\mathbf Q).
$$

Let $j:B\hookrightarrow J(C)$ be the inclusion of the projector image, understood
up to isogeny, and let $\mathcal P_C$ be the normalized Poincaré line bundle
on $C\times J(C)$.  Define

$$
 \mathfrak p=c_1\bigl((\operatorname{id}_C\times j)^*\mathcal P_C\bigr)
 \in CH^1(C\times B)_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{5.1}
$$

Under the Abel--Jacobi identification, the induced map on degree-one
cohomology is the projector-induced map

$$
 \mathfrak p_*|_{e_mH^1(C,\mathbf Q)}:
 e_mH^1(C,\mathbf Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(B,\mathbf Q).
 \tag{5.2}
$$

It is $F$-linear.  This isomorphism follows from the actual isogeny-factor
inclusion and the Poincaré correspondence; an abstract isomorphism between the
two Hodge structures would not suffice for the tensor construction below.

Work over $\mathbf C$ throughout this construction.  Choose a degree-one
zero-cycle $z\in CH^1(C)_{\mathbf Q}$ (a complex point is sufficient) and use
the same $z$ throughout.  Thus
$$
 \pi_0^C=[z\times C],\qquad
 \pi_2^C=[C\times z],\qquad
 \pi_1^C=\Delta_C-\pi_0^C-\pi_2^C
 \tag{5.2a}
$$
are Chow--Künneth projectors over $\mathbf C$, with $\pi_1^C$ acting as the
identity on $H^1(C)$ and as zero on $H^0(C)\oplus H^2(C)$.  All
correspondences below are therefore over $\mathbf C$.  If the branch data are
algebraic, the same construction can, after one finite extension, be defined
over a common number field.  The rational-orbit averages used below refer only
to finite deck/diagonal idempotents with $\mathbf Q$-coefficients, not to an
arithmetic descent of an arbitrary complex fibre.  Put

$$
 P=(\mathfrak p\circ\pi_1^C)^{\boxtimes n}
 \in CH^n(C^n\times B^n)_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{5.3}
$$

Let $\Delta_B:B\hookrightarrow B^n$ be the small diagonal.  The
correspondence

$$
 T={}^t\Gamma_{\Delta_B}\circ P
 \in CH^n(C^n\times B)_{\mathbf Q}
 \tag{5.4}
$$

has codimension $n+n\dim B-\dim B^n=n$, and acts on the
$(1,\ldots,1)$-Künneth component as

$$
 T_*=\Delta_B^*\circ \mathfrak p_*^{\otimes n}
 =\operatorname{cup}\circ \mathfrak p_*^{\otimes n}.
 \tag{5.5}
$$

After normalized determinant alternation, (5.2) gives an isomorphism

$$
 T_*|_{U_{\mathbf a}}:
 U_{\mathbf a}\xrightarrow{\sim}W_F(B).
 \tag{5.6}
$$

The projector $(\pi_1^C)^{\boxtimes n}$ removes every other Künneth
component.  Thus no auxiliary determinant projector on $B$ is needed.

We record the character compatibility needed to identify the source image on the
Fermat side.  This is stronger than merely observing that the two quotients have
the same Kummer equation.

**Lemma 5.0 (character-compatible common quotient).**  Choose the Kummer
generators in the common-quotient construction so that the functions
$w_C$ and $w_Z$ agree.  Put
$$
 \widetilde G_C=(\mu_m)^n\rtimes\mathfrak S_n,
 \qquad
 D_C=(\mu_m)^n/N\simeq\mu_m,
$$
and, on the Fermat side,
$$
 A=(\mu_m)^{n+2}/\mu_m^{\mathrm{diag}},
 \qquad D_Z=A/K_{\mathbf a}\simeq\mu_m.
$$
Then the common-quotient isomorphism identifies $D_C$ with $D_Z$, and the
top-dimensional fibre-product correspondence $\Gamma$ satisfies
$$
 (\delta_C(\zeta)\times\delta_Z(\zeta))_*\Gamma=\Gamma,
 \qquad (1\times k)_*\Gamma=\Gamma\quad(k\in K_{\mathbf a}).
 \tag{5.7}
$$
If $U_t$ is the determinant summand attached to the primitive deck character
$t$, then
$$
 \Gamma_*U_t=V(\varepsilon t\mathbf a),
 \tag{5.8}
$$
where $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}$ is fixed by the push--pull convention.

**Proof.**  On the open locus away from the branch divisor, $C^n\to C^n/G$
and $Z\to Z/K_{\mathbf a}$ are torsors.  The class of
$(\zeta,1,\ldots,1)$ acts on $w_C$ by $\zeta$, while an element of $A$
acts on $w_Z$ by $\chi_{\mathbf a}$.  Since $w_C=w_Z$, these actions
identify $D_C$ and $D_Z$; normality extends the equality from the open locus
to the common quotient.  The fibre product is therefore stable under the paired
residual action, and it is stable under $K_{\mathbf a}$ on the second factor.

On $H_t^{\otimes n}$, an element $(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_n)\in N$
acts by $(\prod_j\epsilon_j)^t=1$; the permutation sign from determinant
alternation cancels the Koszul sign.  Hence $U_t$ descends through $G$, and
the residual $D_C$-character on it is $\zeta^{\varepsilon t}$.  Equivariance
of $\Gamma$ and (5.7) imply that $\Gamma_*U_t$ is $K_{\mathbf a}$-fixed
with $A$-character $\chi_{\mathbf a}^{\varepsilon t}$.  The annihilator of
$K_{\mathbf a}$ in the character group of $A$ is generated by
$\chi_{\mathbf a}$, so the image is contained in the corresponding Fermat
middle-character line $V(\varepsilon t\mathbf a)$.  Exactness makes every entry
of $\varepsilon t\mathbf a$ nonzero, and that line is one-dimensional.  Finally,
the split identity $R_*\Gamma_*|_U=\mathrm{id}_U$ makes $\Gamma_*|_{U_t}$
nonzero.  Thus the containment is equality. $\square$

**Theorem 5.1.**  Suppose $\mathbf a$ satisfies $(E),(B),(S)$.  For every
branch configuration $\mathbf b$, the determinant Hodge space
$W_F(B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b})$ is spanned by codimension-$q$ algebraic
cycle classes.

**Proof.**  After scaling its coordinates over $\mathbf C$,
$Z_{\mathbf c}$ is the degree-$m$ Fermat $n$-fold.  For
every primitive $t$, Lemma 5.0 identifies the one-dimensional summand
$\Gamma_*(U_t)$ with $V(\varepsilon t\mathbf a)$.  Since
$t\mapsto\varepsilon t$ permutes the primitive orbit, condition $(S)$
algebraizes the whole image of $\Gamma_*U_{\mathbf a}$ after
complexification.  If
$$
 A_{\mathbf Q}:=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{cl}:CH^q(Z_{\mathbf c})_{\mathbf Q}
 \longrightarrow H^{2q}(Z_{\mathbf c},\mathbf Q)\bigr),
$$
then the finite rational-orbit construction gives
$\Gamma_*U_{\mathbf a}\subset A_{\mathbf Q}\otimes_{\mathbf Q}\mathbf C$.
Faithful flatness of $\mathbf C/\mathbf Q$ gives
$\Gamma_*U_{\mathbf a}\subset A_{\mathbf Q}$.  Hence, for
$u\in U_{\mathbf a}$, the split identity (3.11) and the rational-orbit
descent in the proof of Theorem 4.1 give
$z_Z\in CH^q(Z_{\mathbf c})_{\mathbf Q}$ with
$\operatorname{cl}(z_Z)=\Gamma_*u$.  With $R$ as in (3.11), put

$$
 z_C=R_*z_Z\in CH^q(C^n)_{\mathbf Q}.
$$

Then $\operatorname{cl}(z_C)=u$.  Finally define

$$
 z_B=T_*z_C\in CH^q(B)_{\mathbf Q}.
$$

Equation (5.6) gives
$\operatorname{cl}(z_B)=T_*u$.  Since (5.6) is an isomorphism, such classes
span $W_F(B)$.  $\square$

Theorem 4.5 verifies $(S)$ for every multi-standard family in Theorem 1.4,
including the families of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3, while Theorem 4.6 verifies it
for Theorem 1.5.  Thus Theorem 5.1 proves all of their fibrewise determinant
assertions.

**Lemma 5.2 (projector image and restricted Poincare correspondence).**  Let
$e=e_m\in\mathbf Q[\mu_m]\subset\operatorname{End}^0(J(C))$ be the exact
cyclotomic idempotent.  Choose $N>0$ such that
$u=Ne$ and $v=N(1-e)$ are integral endomorphisms, and set
$B=\operatorname{Im}(u)$, $B'=\operatorname{Im}(v)$.  If $j:B\hookrightarrow J(C)$
and $j':B'\hookrightarrow J(C)$ are the inclusions, then

$$
\alpha:B\times B'\to J(C),\ (b,b')\mapsto j(b)+j'(b'),
\qquad
\beta:J(C)\to B\times B',\ x\mapsto(u(x),v(x))
$$

are isogenies with $\alpha\beta=[N]$ and $\beta\alpha=[N]$.  Hence
$j^*:eH^1(J(C),\mathbf Q)\to H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$ is an $F$-linear
isomorphism.  With the normalized Poincare class on $C\times J(C)$, its
pullback to $C\times B$ induces an $F$-linear isomorphism

$$
\phi: eH^1(C,\mathbf Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(B,\mathbf Q).
$$

**Proof.**  The identities $u^2=Nu$, $v^2=Nv$, and $uv=vu=0$ give the
two displayed composition formulas.  After applying $\alpha^*$, the idempotent
$e$ is projection onto the first summand, proving the assertion for $j^*$;
centrality of $e$ gives $F$-linearity.  We use the standard
Abel--Poincare naturality explicitly: for a finite morphism of curves
$q:C\to D$, with norm $\operatorname{Nm}_q:J(C)\to J(D)$, the based
Abel--Jacobi maps satisfy
$$
 \operatorname{Nm}_q\circ AJ_C=AJ_D\circ q_*
 \tag{5.10}
$$
on degree-zero divisor classes (and hence on $H^1$); equivalently, after
dualizing by the principal polarizations, the pullback map on Jacobians and
the Poincare correspondence commute with $q_*$.  Applying (5.10) to the
projector factor and then restricting along $j:B\hookrightarrow J(C)$ gives
$\phi=j^*\circ(p_J)_*$.  The Abel--Poincare identification is
$\mu_m$-equivariant, so restriction to the $e$-summand gives the claimed
isomorphism. $\square$

**Lemma 5.3 (determinant-transfer normalization).**  Let $H=eH^1(C,\mathbf Q)$,
$V=H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$, and suppose $\operatorname{rank}_F H=n$.  Normalize
the separability projector and determinant alternator by

$$
 A_n=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)P_\sigma.
$$

Let $\kappa_H:\bigwedge_F^nH\hookrightarrow H^{\otimes n}$ and
$\kappa_V:\bigwedge_F^nV\hookrightarrow V^{\otimes n}$ denote the
separability-projector embeddings followed by $A_n$, with the orbitwise
normalization fixed once and for all.  Then the $F$-linear map
$\phi=\mathfrak p_*|_H$ gives the commutative square
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\bigwedge_F^nH &\xrightarrow{\bigwedge_F^n\phi}& \bigwedge_F^nV\\
\kappa_H\downarrow && \downarrow\kappa_V\\
H^{\otimes n} &\xrightarrow{\phi^{\otimes n}}& V^{\otimes n}.
\end{array}
\tag{5.9}
$$
The normalization is chosen so that the bottom tensor map followed by the
small-diagonal cup product is exactly the determinant map; if a different
descent normalization is used, the result is multiplied by a single nonzero
$\lambda\in\mathbf Q^\times$, which does not affect algebraicity or spanning.
In particular no unaccounted $[F:\mathbf Q]$ or orbit-size factor occurs.

For the correspondence $P=((\mathfrak p\circ\pi_1^C)^{\boxtimes n})$ and
$T={}^t\Gamma_{\Delta_B}\circ P$, one has

$$
T_*|_{\bigwedge_F^nH}=\bigwedge_F^n\phi:
\bigwedge_F^nH\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigwedge_F^nV.
$$

With the unnormalized alternator, the right-hand side is multiplied by
$n!\neq0$.  Thus the determinant transfer is an isomorphism over
$\mathbf Q$, up to a fixed nonzero rational normalization.

**Proof.**  The map $\phi^{\otimes n}$ commutes with the separability projector
and with $A_n$.  On the degree-one Kunneth component the transpose of the small
diagonal acts by cup product.  For $v_1,\ldots,v_n\in H^1(B)$, graded
commutativity gives

$$
\Delta_B^*A_n(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=v_1\cup\cdots\cup v_n.
$$

Hence the induced map is precisely the exterior determinant map; replacing
$A_n$ by the unnormalized alternator multiplies it by $n!$. $\square$

## 6. Characteristic-zero monodromy and the very-general Hodge group

Let $H=e_mR^1f_*\mathbf Q$ be the exact-conductor local system over the
ordered configuration space.  Its polarization and $F$-action put connected
monodromy in the restriction of scalars of the unitary group.  We determine the
connected group directly in characteristic zero.

**Lemma 6.1 (from an adjoint graph to projective representations).**  Let
$n>2$, and let $\rho_1,\rho_2$ be two complex $n$-dimensional
representations of the same group $\Pi$, with connected projective
algebraic monodromy $PGL_n$ in each factor.  If

$$
 \overline{(\mathbf P\rho_1,\mathbf P\rho_2)(\Pi)}^{\,\circ}
 \subset PGL_n\times PGL_n
$$

is the graph of an isomorphism, then

$$
 \mathbf P\rho_2\simeq\mathbf P\rho_1
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \mathbf P\rho_2\simeq\mathbf P\rho_1^\vee.
 \tag{6.1}
$$

**Proof.**  The full algebraic monodromy normalizes its identity component.
Let the graph be defined by $\phi:PGL_n\to PGL_n$.  Then for every
$\gamma\in\Pi$,

$$
 \operatorname{Ad}(\mathbf P\rho_2(\gamma))\circ\phi
 =\phi\circ\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbf P\rho_1(\gamma)).
$$

Comparing this with
$\operatorname{Ad}(\phi(\mathbf P\rho_1(\gamma)))\circ\phi$, and using
that $PGL_n$ has trivial center, gives
$\mathbf P\rho_2=\phi\circ\mathbf P\rho_1$.  Every algebraic-group
automorphism of $PGL_n$ is inner or inner followed by
$g\mapsto(g^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$, which gives (6.1).  $\square$

**Lemma 6.2 (projective rigidity of Jordan--Pochhammer parameters).**  Let
$n>2$.  Consider two rank-$n$ Jordan--Pochhammer systems with ordered
parameters

$$
 (\lambda_0;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n+1}),
 \qquad
 (\lambda'_0;\lambda'_1,\ldots,\lambda'_{n+1}),
$$

and suppose

$$
 \lambda_i,\lambda'_i\ne1\quad(0\le i\le n+1).
$$

Here labelled projective equivalence preserves, for every
$1\leq i\leq n+1$, the distinguished generator with label $i$; it does
not allow a permutation of punctures or a braid precomposition.  If the two
labelled systems are projectively equivalent, then their parameter tuples are
equal.

**Proof.**  After one common conjugation, their $i$-th generators satisfy
$g'_i=c_ig_i$.  Put
$\delta_i=\lambda_0\lambda_i$ and
$\delta'_i=\lambda'_0\lambda'_i$.  The spectrum of $g_i$ is

$$
 1^{\oplus(n-1)}\oplus\{\delta_i\}
 \qquad(1\le i\le n+1).
$$

If $\delta_i\ne1$, then $c_ig_i$ has the two eigenvalues $c_i$ and
$c_i\delta_i$.  A normalized nonresonant pseudoreflection has eigenvalue
$1$ with multiplicity $n-1>1$, so comparison with $g'_i$ gives
$c_i=1$; it cannot instead be conjugate to a resonant generator, which has
only the eigenvalue $1$.  If $\delta_i=1$, the irreducibility criterion
following Rains's Lemma 2.7 [11] says that $g_i=I+N_i$, where
$N_i\ne0$ has rank one.  Thus $c_ig_i$ has the single eigenvalue $c_i$
and the nonzero nilpotent part $c_iN_i$.  It cannot be conjugate to a
nonresonant generator; if $\delta'_i=1$, comparison with the normalized
unipotent generator $g'_i$ again forces $c_i=1$.  Hence, after the one
common conjugation, all labelled generators agree.  The ordered
Jordan--Pochhammer product relations
$g_1\cdots g_{n+1}=\lambda_0I_n$ and
$g'_1\cdots g'_{n+1}=\lambda'_0I_n$ give
$\lambda'_0=\lambda_0$.  Comparing the remaining eigenvalues gives
$\lambda'_i=\lambda_i$ for every $i$.  $\square$

**Lemma 6.2a (configuration-space and one-moving bridge).**  Assume $(E)$,
and put $N=n-1$, so that Rohde's $P_N$ is the configuration space of the
$n+2$ ordered branch points used here.  For every primitive eigenspace, the
connected Zariski closure
of monodromy over the present base $M_{0,n+2}$ is the same as the connected
Zariski closure over Rohde's $P_N$, and also over the subspace
$P_N(a_{N+3})$ in which one labelled branch point is fixed at infinity.
Moreover, after fixing all but one of the remaining labelled branch points, the
restricted local system is the labelled irreducible Jordan--Pochhammer system
with parameters

$$
\lambda_i(t)=\zeta_m^{t a_i}\quad(0\leq i\leq n+1).
$$

In particular, a projective graph relation between two primitive factors over
$M_{0,n+2}$ restricts to a projective equivalence of these labelled
Jordan--Pochhammer systems, with no permutation of the labels.

**Proof.**  Rohde's Remarks 5.1.5--5.1.7 [12] give holomorphic product
decompositions

$$
 P_N\simeq PGL_2(\mathbf C)\times M_N,
 \qquad
 P_N(a_{N+3})\simeq GT\times M_N,
$$

for normalized slices $M_N\simeq M_{0,n+2}$, and conclude that the three
monodromy groups
are commensurable.  Commensurable subgroups have the same identity component
of their real, and hence complex, Zariski closures.  The normalization
$N=n-1$ gives exactly $n+2$ ordered branch labels.

Condition $(E)$ is the connected-cover hypothesis in this one-moving family.
Fix the branch points with labels $1,\ldots,n+1$, and let the point with
label $0$ move.  After one fixed Möbius change of coordinate, choose an
affine coordinate whose point at infinity is unramified and, for
$1\leq i\leq n+1$, let $\gamma_i$ be the positively
oriented based loop in which the moving point goes once around the fixed point
with label $i$.  Rains's Proposition 2.5 [11] identifies
$g_i(t)=\rho_t(\gamma_i)$ as the normalized pseudoreflection satisfying

$$
 \det g_i(t)=\zeta_m^{t(a_0+a_i)},
 \qquad
 g_1(t)\cdots g_{n+1}(t)=\zeta_m^{ta_0}I_n.
$$

Thus, in the convention of (2.19), the ordered Jordan--Pochhammer parameters
are exactly $\lambda_i(t)=\zeta_m^{ta_i}$ for
$0\leq i\leq n+1$.  Their product is $1$ because
$\sum_i a_i\equiv0\pmod m$, and none is $1$ because $t$ is primitive
and $0<a_i<m$.  The irreducibility criterion following Rains's Lemma 2.7 [11]
therefore applies.  Since the full configuration space and the loops
$\gamma_i$ are ordered, restriction uses the same labelled elements of the
fundamental group in both factors: there is no puncture permutation or braid
precomposition.  A projective graph identity restricts along this inclusion of
fundamental groups.  If $\lambda_0(t)\lambda_i(t)=1$, the corresponding
generator is still a nontrivial rank-one unipotent pseudoreflection, so the
resonant projective scalar is fixed exactly as in Lemma 6.2.  This proves the
last assertion. $\square$

**Proposition 6.3.**  Suppose $\mathbf a$ satisfies $(E),(B)$.  Then

$$
 \operatorname{Mon}^{\circ}(H)
 =\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(H,h).
 \tag{6.2}
$$

**Proof.**  For $t\in(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times$, let $H_t$ be the
primitive $\zeta_m^t$-eigenspace.  Condition $(B)$ and (2.2) give
$h^{1,0}(H_t)=h^{0,1}(H_t)=q$.  Rohde's Theorem 5.1.1
[12, p. 53] says that the connected real algebraic monodromy on an eigenspace
of type $(a,b)$, with $a,b\geq1$ and $t\ne m/2$, is $SU(a,b)$.  A
primitive $t$ is never $m/2$, since $m>2$.  Therefore every real-factor
projection is surjective:

$$
 \operatorname{pr}_{\{t,-t\}}
 \operatorname{Mon}^{\circ}(H)_{\mathbf R}=SU(H_t,h_t)=SU(q,q).
 \tag{6.3}
$$

As (6.3) holds in every coordinate, the full connected group is contained in
the product of these special-unitary factors,

$$
 \operatorname{Mon}^{\circ}(H)_{\mathbf R}
 \subseteq
 \prod_{\{t,-t\}\subset(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times}SU(H_t,h_t).
 \tag{6.4}
$$

If this subdirect product were proper, the Lie-algebra form of Goursat's lemma
would produce two distinct real embeddings
$\tau=\{t,-t\}$ and $\upsilon=\{u,-u\}$ for which the projection to the
two adjoint factors is the graph of an isomorphism

$$
 PSU(H_t,h_t)\xrightarrow{\sim}PSU(H_u,h_u).
 \tag{6.5}
$$

Lemma 6.1 and the polarization identification
$\rho_t^\vee\simeq\rho_{-t}$ imply

$$
 \mathbf P\rho_u\simeq\mathbf P\rho_t
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \mathbf P\rho_u\simeq\mathbf P\rho_{-t}.
 \tag{6.6}
$$

Restrict to the curve on which the branch point of exponent $a_0$ moves.  A
graph relation obtained on the full ordered configuration space restricts to
the same labelled one-point monodromy representations, so the resulting
projective equivalence persists on this curve.
Rains's Proposition 2.5, together with (2.19) and the irreducibility criterion
following Lemma 2.7 [11], identifies $\rho_t$ with the rank-$n$
Jordan--Pochhammer representation having parameters

$$
 \lambda_i(t)=\zeta_m^{ta_i}\qquad(0\leq i\leq n+1).
\tag{6.7}
$$

Because the branch-configuration space is ordered, the distinguished loops in
this description have the same labels for every primitive $t$.

They satisfy

$$
 \prod_i\lambda_i(t)=1,
 \qquad
 \lambda_i(t)\ne1\quad(0\leq i\leq n+1),
 \tag{6.8}
$$

because $0<a_i<m$ and $t$ is primitive.  Lemma 6.2 applied to the
first possibility in (6.6) gives

$$
 \zeta_m^{(u-t)a_i}=1\qquad(0\leq i\leq n+1).
$$

Condition $(E)$ then gives $u\equiv t\pmod m$.  For the second
possibility, dualize first; the same argument gives
$u\equiv-t\pmod m$.  Both contradict the assumption that
$\tau\ne\upsilon$.  No diagonal block exists.  Goursat's lemma makes (6.4)
an equality, and equality after base change to
$\mathbf R$ implies equality of the connected $\mathbf Q$-groups.  This
proves (6.2).  $\square$

**Remark 6.4 (the even-$d$ sign concern).**  For the standard-prime family,
if $d$ is even, all $a_i$
are odd.  The class $t'=t+m/2$ is primitive exactly when $4\mid d$; only
then does it define $\zeta_m\mapsto-\zeta_m$.  Its parameters satisfy
$\lambda_i(t')=-\lambda_i(t)$.  Although the pseudoreflection eigenvalue
ratios $\lambda_0\lambda_i$ agree, the projective-rigidity argument above excludes
projective equivalence because $t'\not\equiv\pm t\pmod m$ for $m>4$.  If
$d\equiv2\pmod4$, $t+m/2$ is not primitive; for odd $d$ there is no
integral half-period.  Hence no parity restriction on $d$ is needed.

**Theorem 6.5.**  Suppose $\mathbf a$ satisfies $(E),(B)$.  For a
very general branch configuration,

$$
 Hg(B)=\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(H^1(B),h),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{End}^0(B)=F.
 \tag{6.10}
$$

**Proof.**  General Mumford--Tate theory and Proposition 6.3 give

$$
 \operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(H^1(B),h)
 =\operatorname{Mon}^{\circ}
 \subseteq Hg(B)_{\mathrm{gen}}.
 \tag{6.11}
$$

The deck action consists of Hodge endomorphisms, and Rosati restricts to
complex conjugation on $F$.  Hence the generic Hodge group centralizes $F$
inside the symplectic group and

$$
 Hg(B)_{\mathrm{gen}}
 \subseteq\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}U_F(H^1(B),h).
 \tag{6.12}
$$

The whole rational space $W_F(B)$ is of type $(q,q)$, so the Hodge group
fixes it pointwise.  On each complex factor the unitary group acts on the
determinant line through its determinant character.  Thus (6.12) refines to

$$
 Hg(B)_{\mathrm{gen}}
 \subseteq\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(H^1(B),h).
 \tag{6.13}
$$

The two inclusions give equality.  The centralizer of the standard rational
representation is $F$, hence

$$
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q\text{-HS}}H^1(B,\mathbf Q)=F.
$$

Full faithfulness of the weight-one Hodge realization up to isogeny yields
$\operatorname{End}^0(B)=F$.  The locus where the Hodge group differs from the generic Hodge group is a
countable union of proper closed algebraic subsets.  $\square$

Propositions 2.3--2.4 verify exactness and balance for the explicit families,
while Theorems 4.5--4.6 verify their source algebraicity.  Together with
Theorem 6.5 these give the generic Hodge-group and endomorphism assertions in
Theorems 1.2--1.5 and Corollary 1.6.

When there is more than one real primitive factor, the no-repeated-factor
criterion of Spelta--Tamborini [15] does not apply because all these factors
have the repeated signature $SU(q,q)$; the
projective-rigidity argument above supplies their independence.
Venkataramana's arithmeticity theorem [17]
has different large-branch-number and coprimality hypotheses and is not used.

## 7. Hodge classes on all powers

We use the following invariant-theoretic statement.

**Theorem 7.1.**  Let $B$ be a complex Abelian variety, let $F/F^+$ be a
CM field with a specified embedding

$$
 F\hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}^0(B)
$$

whose Rosati involution is the CM conjugation $a\mapsto\bar a$, and suppose

$$
 \dim_FH^1(B,\mathbf Q)=n=2q>2,
 \qquad
 Hg(B)=\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(H^1(B),h).
 \tag{7.1}
$$

Assume in addition that every real embedding of $F^+$ gives $h$ signature
$(q,q)$ (equivalently, $W_F(B)$ is a Hodge substructure of type
$(q,q)$), and that

$$
 W_F(B)=\bigwedge_F^nH^1(B,\mathbf Q)
 \subset \operatorname{cl}\,CH^q(B)_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{7.2}
$$

Then the rational Hodge conjecture holds for $B^r$ for every
$r\geq1$.

**Proof.**  Put $V=H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$.  After complexification, the group in
(7.1) is a product of copies of $SL_n$, indexed by the real embeddings of
$F^+$.  For a conjugate pair $\sigma,\bar\sigma$, the two summands
$V_\sigma$ and $V_{\bar\sigma}$ are the standard representation and its
dual.  The cohomology of $B^r$ is the exterior algebra of $r$ copies of
these representations.

The first fundamental theorem for $SL_n$ [8] says that the invariant tensors are
generated by contractions between the standard representation and its dual,
together with the volume tensors in $\bigwedge^nV_\sigma$ and
$\bigwedge^nV_{\bar\sigma}$.  Since $SL_n$ is reductive in characteristic
zero, the invariants functor is exact; passing to the exterior-algebra
quotient therefore introduces no new generators.

The contraction tensors span a $\mathbf Q$-defined invariant subspace of
$H^2(B^r,\mathbf Q)$ of Hodge type $(1,1)$.  More explicitly, the
copy-to-copy contractions are realized by the Rosati-symmetric part of the
natural $F$-linear subalgebra
$M_r(F)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}^0(B^r)$, under

$$
 \operatorname{NS}(B^r)_{\mathbf Q}
 \supseteq M_r(F)^{\dagger=1},
$$

so they are divisor classes by the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem.  The volume tensors with
all $n$ labels on one copy are the complex components of $W_F(B)$, hence
are algebraic by hypothesis.  Mixed copy labels are obtained by polarization.
Indeed, for $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in F^r$, the rational homomorphism

$$
 f_a\in\operatorname{Hom}^0(B^r,B),
 \qquad
 (x_1,\ldots,x_r)\longmapsto\sum_i[a_i]x_i
 \tag{7.2a}
$$

is represented by an actual homomorphism after clearing a common denominator.
If $D>0$ is chosen so that $Df_a\in\operatorname{Hom}(B^r,B)$, then for
$w\in W_F(B)$,

$$
 (Df_a)^*w=w\otimes D^n(a_1x_1+\cdots+a_rx_r)^n.
 \tag{7.3}
$$

After dividing by $D^n$ in the rational Chow group, pure $n$-th powers span
$\operatorname{Sym}_F^n(F^r)$ in characteristic
zero.  Thus the algebraic classes (7.3) span every mixed determinant tensor.
Products of divisor and determinant classes span all complexified Hodge
classes.  Since both the Hodge space and the image of rational cycle classes
are defined over $\mathbf Q$, faithful flatness descends the spanning
statement to $\mathbf Q$.  $\square$

Combining Theorems 5.1, 6.5, and 7.1 (with balance supplying the signature
and the codimension-$q$ statement in (7.2)) proves the all-powers rational Hodge
assertion of Theorem 1.1.

**Lemma 7.2 (rational mixed determinant module).**  Let
$V=H^1(B,\mathbf Q)$, assume that
$\operatorname{End}^0(B)=F$ and $\dim_FV=n$, and put
$W_F(B)=\bigwedge_F^nV$.  For every $r\geq1$, the degree-$n$
$SL_F(V)$-invariant summand is canonically

$$
 \left(\bigwedge_F^n(V\otimes_F F^r)\right)^{SL_F(V)}
 \simeq W_F(B)\otimes_F\operatorname{Sym}_F^n(F^r).
$$

Here both sides are viewed in rational cohomology through the standard
separability-projector realization of the $F$-exterior power.  Suppose that
$W_F(B)$ is spanned over $\mathbf Q$ by algebraic cycle classes.  For such
a class $w=\operatorname{cl}(z_w)$, with
$z_w\in CH^{n/2}(B)_{\mathbf Q}$, and
$a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in F^r$, choose $D\in\mathbf Z_{>0}$ with
$Da_i\in\operatorname{End}(B)$ for every $i$.  The homomorphism

$$
 f_{Da}:B^r\longrightarrow B,
 \qquad
 (x_1,\ldots,x_r)\longmapsto\sum_i[Da_i]x_i
$$

then satisfies, under the displayed identification,

$$
 \operatorname{cl}(f_{Da}^{*}z_w)
 =D^n w\otimes(a_1X_1+\cdots+a_rX_r)^n,
$$

where the $X_i$ denote the copy variables.  Consequently all mixed
determinant invariants are algebraic after division by $D^n$ in
$CH^*(B^r)_{\mathbf Q}$.

**Proof.**  The Cauchy decomposition for
$\bigwedge_F^n(V\otimes_F F^r)$ shows that its unique
$SL_F(V)$-trivial summand is the determinant Schur functor
$\bigwedge_F^nV\otimes_F\operatorname{Sym}_F^n(F^r)$.  The same statement
holds simultaneously at every conjugate embedding and therefore descends to
the displayed rational $F$-module.  Clearing denominators makes $f_{Da}$
an actual homomorphism, so its pullback preserves algebraic cycle classes, and
the formula follows from functoriality on $H^1$.  Pure $n$-th powers
$(a_1X_1+\cdots+a_rX_r)^n$ span
$\operatorname{Sym}_F^n(F^r)$ in characteristic zero by polarization.
This proves the final assertion. $\square$

## 8. The generalized Hodge conjecture

We now apply Abdulali's criterion.  We use the convention that the generalized
Hodge conjecture is the equality between the geometric and Hodge-theoretic
coniveau filtrations in every degree.

**Theorem 8.1.**  Assume $(E),(B),(S)$, and let
$\mathbf b\notin\Sigma_{\mathbf a}$.  The generalized Hodge conjecture
holds for $B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}^r$ for every $r\geq1$.

**Proof.**  Write $B=B_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}$, fix $r$, and set
$X=B^r$.  By Theorem 6.5,

$$
 \operatorname{End}^0(B)=F,
 \qquad
 Hg(B)=\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(V,h),
 \qquad V=H^1(B,\mathbf Q).
 \tag{8.1}
$$

In particular, $B$ is simple of Albert type IV, and

$$
 \operatorname{End}^0(X)=M_r(F),
 \qquad
 H^1(X,\mathbf Q)=V\otimes_{\mathbf Q}\mathbf Q^r.
 \tag{8.2}
$$

The Hodge cocharacter acts diagonally on the $r$ copies.  Thus

$$
 Hg(X)=\Delta Hg(B).
 \tag{8.3}
$$

For a product polarization, Rosati on $M_r(F)$ is conjugate transpose.  The
double-centralizer identity identifies the connected Lefschetz group as

$$
 L(X)^\circ=\Delta\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}U_F(V,h),
$$

and hence

$$
 (L(X)^\circ)^{\mathrm{der}}
 =\Delta\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(V,h)
 =Hg(X)=Hg(X)^{\mathrm{der}}.
 \tag{8.4}
$$

Therefore $X$ is of PEL type in Abdulali's sense, and its Hodge group is
semisimple.  Its only simple isogeny factor is the type-IV variety $B$, so
Abdulali's additional odd-relative-dimension condition on type-III factors is
vacuous.

The hypotheses of Abdulali's Theorem 5.1 are matched as follows.

| Hypothesis in Abdulali's Theorem 5.1 | Verification for $X=B^r$ |
|---|---|
| $X$ is of PEL type | Equation (8.4) gives equality of the derived connected Lefschetz and Hodge groups. |
| $Hg(X)$ is semisimple | Equations (8.3)--(8.4) identify it with the diagonal copy of $\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(V,h)$. |
| Every type-III simple factor has odd relative dimension | There is no type-III factor: the only simple isogeny factor is the type-IV variety $B$. |
| The usual Hodge conjecture holds for every power of $X$ | For every $k\geq1$, $X^k=B^{rk}$, which is covered by Theorem 7.1. |

Thus all hypotheses used in the implication from the usual to the generalized
Hodge conjecture are satisfied.  For every $k\geq1$,

$$
 X^k=B^{rk}.
$$

The usual rational Hodge conjecture for this variety follows from Theorem 7.1.
Abdulali's Theorem 5.1 [1] now implies the generalized Hodge conjecture for $X$.
Since $r$ was arbitrary, the result follows.  $\square$

The phrase ``any sufficiently large power'' in Abdulali's domination
conclusion is not an extra uniform hypothesis here.  The theorem is applied to
the fixed variety $X=B^r$, and every usual-Hodge instance required by its
argument is a power of $X$, hence a power of $B$, already covered by
Theorem 7.1.

Theorems 5.1, 6.5, 7.1, and 8.1 prove Theorem 1.1.  The residue calculations
of Section 2 and the source theorems of Section 4 then prove Theorems
1.2--1.5 and Corollary 1.6.

## 9. The self-dual boundary prime

We now take $p=3$, so that

$$
 m=3d,\qquad n=2,\qquad q=1,\qquad
 \mathbf a_{3,d}=(1,1+d,1+2d,m-3).
 \tag{9.1}
$$

The residue calculation of Section 2 and the common-quotient and transfer
arguments of Sections 3--5 remain valid.  Thus, for every fibre,

$$
 \dim_FH^1(B,\mathbf Q)=2,\qquad
 \operatorname{sig}_{\sigma}H^1(B)=(1,1),\qquad
 \dim B=\varphi(3d),
 \tag{9.2}
$$

and

$$
 \bigwedge_F^2H^1(B,\mathbf Q)
 \subset\operatorname{cl}CH^1(B)_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{9.3}
$$

The last assertion also follows abstractly from the Lefschetz $(1,1)$
theorem, but the common quotient gives the same explicit Aoki source and
Poincaré transfer as in higher rank.

The monodromy independence argument changes because
$\mathrm{Std}_{SL_2}\simeq\mathrm{Std}_{SL_2}^{\vee}$.  We record the exact
exception.

**Lemma 9.1 (rank-two projective coupling).**  For the family (9.1), distinct
real primitive factors are projectively equivalent if and only if $d=4$.
For $d=4$, the two real factors form one graph.

**Proof.**  Move the branch point of exponent $a_0=1$.  The three
projective local eigenvalue ratios are

$$
 \delta_i(t)=\zeta_m^{t b_i},\qquad
 (b_1,b_2,b_3)=(d+2,2d+2,3d-2).
 \tag{9.4}
$$

Suppose $\mathbf P\rho_u\simeq\mathbf P\rho_t$, and put
$v=ut^{-1}\pmod m$.  For a rank-two pseudoreflection, projective
normalization may either preserve or interchange its two eigenvalues.  If
$S\subset\{1,2,3\}$ is the set of interchanged generators, the necessary
and sufficient congruences are

$$
 (v-1)b_i\equiv0\quad(i\notin S),\qquad
 (v+1)b_i\equiv0\quad(i\in S),
 \tag{9.5}
$$

$$
 v-1\equiv-\sum_{i\in S}b_i\pmod{3d}.
 \tag{9.6}
$$

The eight subsets give

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
S&\text{consequence}\\ \hline
\varnothing&v=1\\
\{1\}&d=4,\ v=7\\
\{2\}&\text{no solution}\\
\{3\}&v=3\text{, not a unit}\\
\{1,2\}&v=-3\text{, not a unit}\\
\{1,3\}&d\mid4\text{, and the }d=4\text{ value is not a unit}\\
\{2,3\}&d=4,\ v=5\\
\{1,2,3\}&v=-1.
\end{array}
\tag{9.7}
$$

The cases $v=\pm1$ are the same real factor.  Thus a distinct graph occurs
only for $d=4$.  Conversely, if $d=4$, then

$$
 m=12,\qquad\mathbf a=(1,5,9,9).
$$

For $t=1,u=5$, the tuples of ratios are

$$
 (-1,\zeta_{12}^{10},\zeta_{12}^{10}),
 \qquad
 (-1,\zeta_{12}^{2},\zeta_{12}^{2}).
$$

If $(g_1,g_2,g_3)$ is the normalized $t=1$ tuple, set
$(h_1,h_2,h_3)=(g_1,\zeta_{12}^2g_2,\zeta_{12}^2g_3)$.  The eigenvalue
ratios are those for $u=5$, and

$$
 h_1h_2h_3=\zeta_{12}^4(\zeta_{12}I_2)=\zeta_{12}^5I_2.
$$

Thus it has the correct product scalar, while its classes in $PGL_2$ agree
with those of the first tuple.  Equivalently, after replacing the swapped
generators by $c_i=\delta_i^{-1}g_i$, one obtains the normalized rank-one
Jordan--Pochhammer tuple with the same product scalar.  The uniqueness
statement for normalized Jordan--Pochhammer systems therefore gives
$\mathbf P\rho_5\simeq\mathbf P\rho_1$.  Rohde's factorwise surjectivity
then makes connected monodromy the graph.  $\square$

**Proposition 9.2 (generic group and commutant).**  If $d\ne4$, then

$$
 Hg(B)=\operatorname{Res}_{F^+/\mathbf Q}SU_F(1,1)
 \tag{9.8}
$$

at a very general point, and $\operatorname{End}^0(B)$ is a totally
indefinite quaternion algebra over $F^+$, containing $F$ as a maximal
subfield.  If $d=4$, then $Hg(B)_{\mathbf R}$ is the graph in
$SU(1,1)^2$, and $\operatorname{End}^0(B)$ is a degree-four central
simple algebra over $\mathbf Q$, split at the real place.

**Proof.**  For $d\ne4$, Lemma 9.1 and Goursat give full
restriction-of-scalars monodromy.  The determinant divisors bound the generic
Hodge group above by the same special-unitary group, proving (9.8).  Over
$\overline{\mathbf Q}$,

$$
 H^1(B)_{\overline{\mathbf Q}}
 \simeq
 \bigoplus_{\tau:F^+\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}}
 W_\tau^{\oplus2},
 \tag{9.9}
$$

where $W_\tau$ is the standard $SL_2$-module.  Hence the complexified
commutant is $\prod_\tau M_2$.  Its rational form has center $F^+$, degree
two, and $F$ as a maximal quadratic subfield.  It is therefore a quaternion
algebra.  The noncompact real group $SU(1,1)$ makes the algebra split at
every real place.

If $d=4$, Lemma 9.1 gives a monodromy graph in $SL_2^2$.  Connected
monodromy is normal in the derived generic Hodge group, while the connected
normalizer of this graph in $SL_2^2$ is the graph itself.  The determinant
divisors again bound the Hodge group inside $SL_2^2$, so the generic Hodge
group equals the graph.  It acts on four copies of its standard module.
The complex commutant is $M_4(\mathbf C)$; its rational form has
center $\mathbf Q$, degree four, and is split over $\mathbf R$.  Indeed,
the nontrivial element of $\operatorname{Gal}(F^+/\mathbf Q)$ exchanges the
two real factors, while complex conjugation exchanges standard and dual
constituents; the graph and self-duality put all four constituents in one
Galois orbit.  Hence the rational commutant is central over $\mathbf Q$.  The
Albert classification leaves $M_4(\mathbf Q)$ or $M_2(D_0)$, where
$D_0/\mathbf Q$ is an indefinite quaternion division algebra.  Thus the
simple isogeny factors have type I or II in all cases.  $\square$

We first make explicit the contraction-to-divisor input needed in the
self-dual case.  Let $Y=B^s$ be any self-power and write, after
complexification,
$$
 H^1(Y,\mathbf C)=\bigoplus_{\tau}(W_\tau\otimes M_\tau),
 \qquad W_\tau=\operatorname{Std}_{SL_2}.
\tag{9.10}
$$
For $d\ne4$, the multiplicity spaces have the quaternionic commutant
described in Proposition 9.2; for $d=4$, there is one graph factor and the
same statement holds with its central-simple commutant.  The double-centralizer
identity
$$
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{HS}}H^1(Y,\mathbf Q)
 =\operatorname{Cent}_{\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}H^1(Y,\mathbf Q)}
 (Hg(Y))
\tag{9.11}
$$
shows that the matrix coefficients on every $M_\tau$ are type-$(0,0)$
Hodge endomorphisms (with their Galois orbit taken for the rational form).
The invariant alternating form
$\varepsilon_\tau\in\bigwedge^2W_\tau$ is the corresponding component of
the polarization.  Hence
$$
 \varepsilon_\tau\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2(M_\tau)
 \subset H^2(Y,\mathbf Q)\cap H^{1,1}(Y).
\tag{9.12}
$$
Under the Lefschetz identification
$\operatorname{NS}(Y)_{\mathbf Q}\simeq
\operatorname{End}^0(Y)^{\dagger=1}$, these rational classes are divisor
classes.  The first fundamental theorem for $SL_2$, together with exactness
of invariants in characteristic zero, now shows that all $Hg(Y)$-invariants
in the exterior algebra $H^\bullet(Y,\mathbf Q)$ are generated by these
contraction divisors.  In particular every self-power is divisor-generated,
so $B$ is stably nondegenerate.

**Theorem 9.3.**  For $p=3,d\ge3$, every power of a very general
$B_{3,d,\mathbf b}$ satisfies the rational and generalized Hodge
conjectures.

**Proof.**  After complexification, each simple Hodge-group factor is
$SL_2$, acting on several copies of its standard representation.  The
contraction-to-divisor argument above shows that the alternating contraction
generators furnished by the first fundamental theorem for $SL_2$ are
rational divisor classes.  Consequently the Hodge ring of every self-power is
generated by divisors: $B$ is stably nondegenerate and all powers satisfy the
rational Hodge conjecture.

Every simple component has Albert type I or II by Proposition 9.2.  The same
is true for $B^r$, which remains stably nondegenerate.  Hazama's Theorem 5.1
[18] therefore gives the generalized Hodge conjecture for $B^r$, for every
$r\ge1$.  This proves Theorem 1.7.  $\square$

## 10. Examples and scope

The contraction application already begins with the conductor-six tuple

$$
 \eta_{3,1}=(1,1,4,4,4,4),
 \qquad n=4,
 \qquad \dim B=4.
$$

Its $k$-fold juxtaposition is
$(1^{2k},4^{4k})$, with

$$
 n_k=6k-2,
 \qquad q_k=3k-1,
 \qquad \dim B_k=6k-2,
 \qquad \dim M_{0,6k}=6k-3.
$$

The first values are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
p&d&m&\mathbf a_{p,d}&\dim B_{p,d}\\ \hline
3&3&9&(1,4,7,6)&6\\
3&4&12&(1,5,9,9)&4\\
5&3&15&(1,4,7,10,13,10)&16\\
7&3&21&(1,4,7,10,13,16,19,14)&36.
\end{array}
$$

The smallest juxtaposed member has $\ell=5,d=1,m=15$:

$$
 \mathbf a_{5,1}^{\mathrm{jux}}
 =(1,6,11,12;1,4,7,10,13,10),
 \qquad
 n=8,
 \qquad
 \dim B=32.
$$

The odd-prime multi-standard version of the fixed-conductor phenomenon starts
at $m=15$.  Put

$$
 \alpha=(1,4,7,10,13,10)
$$

and juxtapose $k$ copies of $\alpha$.  Then

$$
 n_k=6k-2,
 \qquad q_k=3k-1,
 \qquad \dim B_k=8(3k-1)=24k-8,
 \qquad \dim M_{0,6k}=6k-3.
$$

Thus a single conductor and the single field $\mathbf Q(\zeta_{15})$
support moving examples of arbitrarily large rank and dimension.

For every displayed family, determinant algebraicity holds at every point of
the branch-configuration space.  The endomorphism,
Hodge-group, usual-Hodge, and generalized-Hodge statements hold on the
very-general locus, with the $p=3$ group and commutant interpreted as in
Section 9.

The method does not prove a theorem for arbitrary ramified cyclic Pryms.  At
prime-power conductor it covers every exact balanced residue tuple.  At
general conductor it additionally requires algebraicity of the complete
primitive Fermat character orbit.  That source condition may come from
Aoki's standard cycles, the Shioda--Ran juxtaposition and deletion theorems
recalled by Aoki, or another source theorem; it is not known for every
balanced tuple at general conductor.  The generic group theorem uses Rohde's factorwise
monodromy theorem together with the exact Jordan--Pochhammer coefficients.  It does not
give explicit equations for the transferred Chow
cycles on $B$; it gives them as images of Aoki cycles under explicit algebraic
correspondences.  Nor does it decide the all-powers conjectures at every special
Hodge locus, where the endomorphism algebra and Hodge group may jump.

The citation-dependent representation inputs are Rains's
Jordan--Pochhammer identification and Rohde's equality on each real factor.
The Goursat reduction, projective rigidity, even-$d$ sign separation, and
passage back to the connected $\mathbf Q$-group are proved in Sections 6
and 9.
