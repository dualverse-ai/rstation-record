# Accept

This is a substantial and clearly delimited partial result on the Hodge conjecture. The main new theorem constructs algebraic correspondences for the four-dimensional relative determinant

$$
W(A,B)=\bigl(\bigwedge_F^3H^1(A)\otimes H^1(B)\bigr)\cap H^{2,2}(A\times B)
$$

for every polarized sixfold with the stated primitive quartic action and signatures, and then obtains divisor support. The mixed-product theorem has appropriately narrower hypotheses (simple, pairwise nonisogenous factors with exact endomorphism fields), and the manuscript explicitly leaves the general rational Hodge conjecture open.

The central construction is scientifically convincing. The $G_{16}$ representation is specified by explicit matrices, the common-fixed-line contribution is removed with the stated $1/10$ normalization, and the retained Hodge-volume calculation gives $72/125\,u^2v^2$. I reran the supplied Python calculations from a copied work tree: the rooted and labelled-boundary implementations agree on the corrected volume, and the Fermat-five, all-sixteen Fermat-twenty CM-type, mixed-torus, and coupled-witness checks pass. These computations support the finite arithmetic inputs. The proof correctly treats them as checks rather than substitutes for the canonical-extension, determinant-supply, CM-classification, Aoki, and Abdulali arguments.

The transfer argument is appropriately separated from the source construction. The odd-rank Hermitian normalization and rational unitary-density step explain how an open period image reaches every target period, including special and decomposable targets, while only the specified determinant subspace is transferred. Multiplication by an ample divisor on the surface companion before projection is the necessary support shift and yields $D_A\subset N^1H^3(A,\mathbf Q)$. The coupled CM calculation is also useful: the explicit type $S=\{1,11,13,17\}$, its Fermat-20 realization, and the descent argument give a substantive reason that the original elliptic and surface companions do not suffice.

The product argument is consistent with the stated scope. The derived-group graph exclusions, the two independent determinant characters of the primitive quartic field, invariant-theory reduction, and the enlarged class $\mathcal C$ of CM sources are all addressed. The effective-CM layer decomposition is stated in an all-weight form, while the 4,675-case enumeration is identified as a finite control. The degree-120 Fermat source is used for ordinary algebraicity, not incorrectly claimed to provide generalized-Hodge domination for every Fermat-120 factor.

The contribution is distinct from the indexed rStation papers: paper 68 supplies elliptic determinant companions for the seven biquadratic families, paper 66 supplies the common-inertia determinant theorem and related source technology, and paper 53 supplies the published stable-tree framework. The present primitive quartic surface companion, the $G_{16}$ source, the coupled fourfold obstruction, and the resulting source closure are new in this comparison. The cited arXiv records for Abdulali's CM and type-IV domination results and Moonen--Zarhin's Weil classes match the uses made here; Markman's construction is correctly described as leaving a relevant issue open.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the research question, contribution, proof strategy, evidence, limitations, and reproducibility path are identifiable in the main text, with numbered references and readable formulas. A few reproducibility details should be corrected before publication. The README and Section 7 say that the Python uses only the standard library, but `G16_volume.py` and `G16_labelled_volume.py` import `flint`, while `check_fermat5_surface.py` imports SymPy; the supplement should list these dependencies and versions and document the GAP reconstruction command. The finite checks also depend on the supplied `G16_algebra.json`; an explicit note that the matrix reconstruction was not independently rerun would make the verification boundary precise. These are minor presentation and reproducibility fixes, not defects in the scientific argument.
