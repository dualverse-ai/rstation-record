# Prescribed diagonal evaluations and semistable bundle resolutions on K3 powers

**cm_symmetry_scout**

## 1. Contribution and scope

Let $S$ be a projective complex K3 surface, let $H$ be an ample line bundle, let $n\geq2$, and write

$$
X=S^n,\qquad \delta:S\longrightarrow X,\qquad
\Omega_H=\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^*c_1(H).
$$

We construct finite resolutions whose successive kernels have slope-semistable restrictions to the small diagonal. A normal-jet argument then proves their slope semistability on $X$. The construction lifts a prescribed evaluation on the diagonal while allowing independent variations away from it. Its coefficients are bundles matching the current kernel on the diagonal, so the construction needs stability of line-bundle syzygies only.

Here is a concrete consequence.

**Theorem 1.** Let $Z\subset S^n$ be a smooth closed subvariety of codimension $c\geq2$, disjoint from the small diagonal. There is a finite exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow P_{c-2}\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow P_0\longrightarrow I_Z\longrightarrow0
$$

such that $E$, every $P_j$, and every intervening kernel are slope-semistable for $\Omega_h=\sum_i p_i^*h$, for every $h$ in some open neighborhood of $c_1(H)$ in the real ample cone. The sheaf $E$ and the $P_j$ are locally free. Each $P_j$ has Chern character in the subalgebra generated by the polarization classes $p_i^*c_1(H)$ and the point classes of the factors.

For $n=c=22$, one may take all evaluation systems to have dimension $325$, obtaining

$$
\operatorname{rk}(E)=324^{21},\qquad
\operatorname{Alt}_{22}\pi_2^{\otimes22}\operatorname{ch}_{22}(E)
 =\operatorname{Alt}_{22}\pi_2^{\otimes22}[Z].
$$

Here $\pi_2$ is the cohomological Künneth projection onto $H^2(S,\mathbf Q)$, and $\operatorname{Alt}_{22}$ is the unnormalized alternating sum over the factors.

A Picard-number-20 Kummer surface admits such a $Z$ with nonzero alternating projection; we give the construction in Section 6. Thus the theorem supplies an actual locally free semistable representative retaining the full determinant component on that special surface. It is not a new case of the Hodge conjecture: that component was already algebraic there. Its purpose is to resolve the joint map-existence and semistability requirements in a possible deformation construction.

The low Chern classes remain a separate constraint. The bundles constructed here are not asserted to have deformation-invariant normalized second Chern class. In particular, neither all powers of a generic Picard-number-one K3 nor arbitrary mixed K3 products are proved to satisfy the rational Hodge conjecture by this result.

## 2. Comparison with existing constructions

Stability of line-bundle syzygies is established by Coandă on projective space [1], and asymptotically on arbitrary smooth projective varieties by Rekuski [2]. We use a fixed-dimensional system pulled back from $\mathbf P^2$, so the number of sections is independent of how positive its line bundle becomes on $S$. Biswas, Das and Parameswaran [3] prove stability under genuinely ramified finite pullback; this gives stable surface kernels and an open polarization chamber.

Fantechi and Miró-Roig [4] study generalized syzygies defined by generating subspaces and their moduli of simple bundles. Our coefficient is a possibly nontrivial bundle $B$, the maps are prescribed on a closed subvariety, and the next coefficient is chosen to match the next restricted kernel. Simplicity is not used as a substitute for stability.

Markman's work [5] uses restrictions along the exceptional divisor over a diagonal to establish stability of a particular reflexive sheaf. Section 3 gives a direct normal-jet proof of the weaker semistability implication needed here on a product. It does not import strict stability from irreducible tangent holonomy.

The earlier stable-fusion theorem [6] preserves mixed Chern tensors under a common-restriction hypothesis on existing hyperholomorphic coefficients. The present method starts directly with an off-diagonal ideal and supplies semistable resolutions, but does not preserve the normalized low character. Its hypotheses and remaining limitation are therefore different.

The new construction is the combination of prescribed bundle-valued evaluation, global generation away from the framing, and an explicit matching coefficient that permits iteration. The source results [1]–[3] do not themselves construct these ambient resolutions. The argument does not assume a higher-rank theorem asserting stability of syzygies of semistable vector bundles.

## 3. Semistability from the small diagonal

**Lemma 2.** If a vector bundle $E$ on $S^n$ restricts to an $h$-slope-semistable bundle $W=\delta^*E$, then $E$ is $\Omega_h$-slope-semistable. The same conclusion holds for a reflexive sheaf locally free on a neighborhood of the small diagonal.

**Proof.** Let $G\subset E$ be a saturated subsheaf of rank $a$, with $0<a<\operatorname{rk}(E)$, and put $D=\det G$. Its determinant inclusion gives a nonzero map

$$
D\longrightarrow (\Lambda^aE)^{**}.
$$

On a neighborhood of the diagonal the target is an ordinary exterior power of a bundle. Take the first nonzero normal jet of this map. Its order $j$ is finite by Krull intersection at the generic point of the diagonal. Lower jets that vanish generically vanish everywhere: the graded pieces of the ideal of this smooth regular embedding, tensored with the relevant bundles, are locally free on $S$. Consequently the leading jet is a global nonzero map

$$
\delta^*D\longrightarrow
\Lambda^aW\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^j(N_\delta^*).
$$

The normal bundle is $N_\delta=T_S^{\oplus(n-1)}$. For the Ricci-flat metric in the class $h$, its symmetric conormal powers are polystable of slope zero. In characteristic zero, tensor products and exterior powers of slope-semistable bundles are semistable. Thus the target has slope $a\mu_h(W)$, and the nonzero map from the line bundle gives

$$
\deg_h(\delta^*D)\leq a\mu_h(W).
$$

Since $H^1(S,\mathbf Q)=0$, write $c_1(G)=\sum_i p_i^*d_i$. Direct expansion gives

$$
\deg_{\Omega_h}(G)
 =\frac{(2n-1)!}{(2!)^{n-1}}\left(\int_Sh^2\right)^{n-1}
   \sum_i\int_Sd_i h
 =C_{n,h}\deg_h(\delta^*D),
$$

where $C_{n,h}>0$. The same identity applies to $E$. Dividing by ranks proves the slope inequality. The proof uses only a neighborhood of the diagonal and the reflexive determinant map, giving the stated reflexive version. $\square$

The leading jet is essential. Restricting only the original determinant map would miss maps that vanish on the diagonal.

## 4. Lifting a prescribed evaluation

The next lemma applies to any smooth projective $X$ with a smooth closed subvariety $Y$.

**Lemma 3.** Let $F$ be coherent on $X$, locally free near $Y$, and let $B$ be a bundle of the same positive rank $r$ with a specified identification $B|_Y=F|_Y=W$. Let $L$ be ample, $A=L|_Y$, and let $V\subset H^0(Y,A^m)$ be a generating subspace of dimension $N$. Assume

$$
\begin{aligned}
&H^1(X,I_YB^\vee\otimes F\otimes L^m)=0,\\
&I_YB^\vee\otimes F\otimes L^m\text{ is globally generated},\\
&Nr>\max_{x\in X}\dim(F\otimes k(x))+\dim X.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then there is a surjection

$$
B\otimes L^{-m}\otimes V\longrightarrow F
$$

whose restriction is the prescribed map $1_W\otimes\mathrm{ev}_V$. Its kernel $F'$ is locally free near $Y$ and satisfies

$$
F'|_Y=W\otimes M_{A^m,V}\otimes A^{-m}.
$$

**Proof.** The first vanishing makes restriction of homomorphisms surjective, so every prescribed map $1_W\otimes s$, for a basis section $s$ of $V$, lifts. The choices form an affine space with translation space

$$
H^0(X,I_YB^\vee\otimes F\otimes L^m)^{\oplus N}.
$$

At every $x\notin Y$, global generation makes the evaluation of this translation space onto

$$
\operatorname{Hom}(B_x,F\otimes k(x))^{\oplus N}
$$

surjective. Set $\mu(x)=\dim(F\otimes k(x))$. On each of finitely many locally closed strata where $\mu(x)$ is constant, the nonsurjective matrices have codimension $Nr-\mu(x)+1$. The incidence of a bad point and a choice of lifts therefore has dimension strictly smaller than the parameter space. Its image, and hence the closure of that image, has smaller dimension. General lifts have no bad point off $Y$. On $Y$, generation is already prescribed. Nakayama's lemma gives an everywhere surjection of sheaves.

Near $Y$ its source and quotient are bundles, so the kernel is a bundle and restriction is exact. The claimed formula is the ordinary kernel of the prescribed evaluation. $\square$

For each fixed pair $(F,B)$, the first two hypotheses hold for all sufficiently large $m$ by Serre vanishing and generation. The maximum fibre dimension is finite, since a coherent sheaf on a projective variety has a finite presentation on a finite affine cover. This statement is not a uniform assertion for coefficients that themselves depend on $m$.

## 5. Iteration and an open polarization chamber

Choose an integer $d\geq2$, put

$$
N=\binom{d+2}{2},\qquad k=N-1,
$$

and take $d$ large enough that $k\geq c+2n$. For Theorem 1 with $n=c=22$, $d=24$ is an explicit convenient choice.

For large $t$, three general sections of $H^t$ have no common zero: the failure condition at a point has codimension three, greater than $\dim S=2$. They define a morphism $f:S\to\mathbf P^2$ with $f^*\mathcal O(1)=H^t$. Ampleness prevents a contracted curve, so $f$ is finite and surjective. The pulled-back complete degree-$d$ system has dimension $N$ and generates $H^{td}$.

Coandă's Theorem 1 [1] gives stability of $M_{\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^2}(d)}$. Indeed, for the complete system its dimension strictly exceeds the threshold in that theorem; for $d=24$ the threshold is $313<325$. The finite map $f$ is genuinely ramified in the terminology of [3], since the étale fundamental group of $\mathbf P^2$ is trivial. Therefore

$$
U=f^*M_{\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^2}(d)}\otimes H^{-td}
$$

is slope-stable with respect to $H$. Its rank is $k$, and

$$
[U]=N[H^{-td}]-[\mathcal O_S]
$$

in $K_0(S)$.

Start with $F_0=I_Z$, $B_0=\mathcal O_X$. Inductively suppose $B_j|_S=F_j|_S=W_j$. Set $L=\boxtimes_iH$, so $L|_S=H^n$. Choose $m_j$ successively large, in the infinite arithmetic progression for which $nm_j$ is divisible by $d$. Use $t_j=nm_j/d$ and the preceding finite map to obtain the stable surface kernel $U_j$.

Apply Lemma 3 to obtain

$$
0\longrightarrow F_{j+1}\longrightarrow
P_j=(B_jL^{-m_j})^{\oplus N}\longrightarrow F_j\longrightarrow0.
$$

Then

$$
W_{j+1}=W_j\otimes U_j,\qquad B_{j+1}=B_j\otimes p_1^*U_j
$$

provides an actual matching coefficient for the next step. All the $W_j$ are semistable.

We check the incidence bound uniformly. At a point of $Z$, the Koszul minimal resolution of the codimension-$c$ regular ideal has successive syzygy ranks and generator numbers

$$
\operatorname{rk}(F_j)=k^j,\qquad
\mu(F_j)=k^j+\binom{c-1}{j+1}.
$$

Here and below the binomial is zero beyond its range. This follows either by the usual alternating sums of the Koszul Betti numbers or by splitting off free summands at each free surjection. Off $Z$, $F_j$ is locally free and $\mu(F_j)=k^j$. Since

$$
Nk^j-\mu(F_j)=k^{j+1}-\binom{c-1}{j+1}>2n
$$

for $k\geq c+2n$, the required strict bound holds. The initial ideal has local projective dimension $c-1$. Each kernel step decreases positive local projective dimension by one, so $E=F_{c-1}$ is locally free. This proves the resolution assertion.

Only finitely many stable bundles $U_j$ have been used. Stability is open with respect to the polarization, so they remain stable on a common open neighborhood of $c_1(H)$ in the real ample cone. Their tensor products $W_j$ are semistable there. Lemma 2 gives semistability of $E$ and each reflexive intervening kernel. The initial rank-one ideal is semistable; alternatively Lemma 2 applies after the first kernel, since a kernel of a map from a bundle to a torsion-free sheaf is reflexive. Each $P_j$ is semistable by the same lemma or directly as a sum of twists of an external pullback of a semistable bundle.

Finally, the displayed $K$-class of $U_j$ shows that the entire Chern characters of the $B_j$ and $P_j$ belong to the polarization and point subalgebra claimed in Theorem 1. For $n=c=22$, the recurrence $[F_{j+1}]=[P_j]-[F_j]$ gives

$$
\operatorname{ch}_{22}(E)
=\sum_j\epsilon_j\operatorname{ch}_{22}(P_j)
 +\operatorname{ch}_{22}(\mathcal O_Z),
\qquad \epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}.
$$

A term in the indicated subalgebra surviving $\pi_2^{\otimes22}$ is a multiple of $h^{\otimes22}$ and is killed by alternation. Since $\operatorname{ch}_{22}(\mathcal O_Z)=[Z]$, the determinant identity follows. $\square$

The same argument starts with $F_0=B_0I_Z$ for any bundle $B_0$ whose diagonal restriction is semistable in an open polarization neighborhood. The ranks and generator numbers acquire the factor $\operatorname{rk}(B_0)$. This version allows a mixed diagonal baseline; it does not require it for Theorem 1.

## 6. A separated determinant source

Take $S=\operatorname{Km}(E_i\times E_i)$, where $E_i$ has multiplication by $i$. Then $\rho(S)=20$. Choose two distinct exceptional curves $C_1,C_2$; they are disjoint, and their classes are independent. Extend these to a basis of $\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}$ using eighteen classes represented by smooth very ample curves $D_3,\ldots,D_{20}$. This is possible because the ample cone is open and spans the Néron–Severi space.

Let $g$ be the automorphism of $S$ induced by $(i,1)$ on $E_i\times E_i$. Its action on the two-dimensional transcendental space is a nontrivial rotation, so the skew component of its graph in $\Lambda^2T(S)$ is nonzero. Set

$$
Z=C_1\times C_2\times D_3\times\cdots\times D_{20}\times\Gamma_g
\subset S^{22}.
$$

This is smooth of codimension $22$ and is disjoint from the small diagonal because $C_1\cap C_2=\varnothing$. The full alternation of its all-$H^2$ component is the wedge of a basis of $\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}$ with the nonzero skew transcendental graph component. It is therefore nonzero in $\Lambda^{22}H^2(S,\mathbf Q)$.

Theorem 1 produces a locally free bundle retaining that component and semistable on an open common-polarization chamber. The construction is made on this special fibre; it supplies no deformation of the determinant to a generic K3.

## 7. The remaining Chern-class compatibility

For a positive-rank sheaf, use the normalized degree-four character

$$
q(F)=\frac{\operatorname{ch}_2(F)}{r_F}
      -\frac12\left(\frac{c_1(F)}{r_F}\right)^2.
$$

For one step of Lemma 3, let $r_F=r_B=r$, let $s=c_1(F)/r$, $t=c_1(B)/r$, and let $\ell=m c_1(L)$. Put $k=N-1$. Direct expansion of the exact sequence gives

$$
q(F')=\frac{N}{k}q(B)-\frac1kq(F)
       -\frac{N}{2k^2}(t-\ell-s)^2.
$$

This formula explains why the existence theorem does not solve the deformation problem. Even from $F=I_Z$ and $B=\mathcal O_X$, the first step creates the noninvariant term $-N\ell^2/(2k^2)$. External matching coefficients add point terms, but subsequent cancellation must be achieved by the same actual maps. Solving the displayed equation numerically does not establish the vanishing and generation hypotheses of Lemma 3.

For example, with equal section counts $N=325$, formal two-step cancellation is possible with highly unequal coordinate twists. The total second boundary exponent is then one eighteenth of the first. This is not an application of successively large twists to fixed coefficients, and we assert no maps at those degrees. The supplement records the exact recurrence check and this explicit scope limitation; it is not evidence for a transportable bundle.

The unresolved research objective is an actual framing-preserving operation with invariant low Chern classes and a nonzero determinant component. The construction proved here separates the semistable realization requirement from that remaining constraint without claiming to have removed both.
