# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and clearly communicated construction of semistable resolutions of smooth off-diagonal ideals on powers of a projective K3 surface. The matching coefficient makes the construction iterable while reducing the stability input to line-system syzygies. This is a meaningful partial contribution to the stated problem: it constructs actual locally free semistable representatives retaining an exceptional determinant component, while explicitly leaving the deformation-compatible low Chern classes unresolved. It neither proves a new Hodge-conjecture case nor solves the supplied problem.

The decisive geometric steps are justified. In Lemma 2, taking the first nonzero normal jet addresses the possibility that the determinant inclusion vanishes on the diagonal. The locally free graded pieces permit the successive vanishing statements to pass from the generic point to the whole diagonal. The conormal symmetric powers have slope zero and are polystable, and the displayed degree comparison transfers the resulting inequality to the common product polarization. This establishes semistability, with no unsupported claim of strict stability.

Lemma 3 correctly separates the prescribed restriction from the freedom to vary the lifts away from it. Local freeness near the framing gives the required restriction sequence, Serre vanishing supplies lifts, and global generation supplies arbitrary fibre maps off the framing. The determinantal incidence estimate then gives a globally surjective map. In the iteration, the coefficients are fixed before each sufficiently large twist is chosen; this ordering is essential and is respected. The local Koszul calculation gives the stated generator excess, and projective dimension drops to zero after $c-1$ kernel steps. Thus the proof establishes local freeness as well as semistability.

The external stability inputs apply as stated. Coandă's theorem [1] covers the complete degree-$24$ system, with threshold $313<325$. The stable-pullback theorem of Biswas–Das–Parameswaran [3, Theorem 2.5] applies to the finite map to $\mathbf P^2$, whose étale fundamental group is trivial. Scaling the pullback polarization does not alter slope stability. Intersecting the stability neighborhoods for the finitely many resulting surface bundles gives the claimed open chamber of common polarizations; it does not assert an open chamber of arbitrary independently varying product polarizations.

The determinant example is also convincing. The disjoint exceptional curves ensure separation from the small diagonal. Their classes can be extended by smooth very ample curve classes to a Néron–Severi basis. The automorphism induced by $(i,1)$ has nonzero skew action on the rank-two transcendental space, giving a nonzero full alternating tensor. The $K$-theory recurrence has the stated positive sign for the contribution of $[Z]$ after 21 steps, while the polarization-generated terms disappear under full alternation. Consequently the rank $324^{21}$ and the retained determinant component follow from the construction, rather than from the numerical supplement.

The novelty is the combined prescribed-evaluation and matching-coefficient construction. The focused comparison with [1]–[5] does not reveal a prior theorem supplying these ambient resolutions: the line-syzygy results provide stability inputs, Fantechi–Miró-Roig [4] study generalized syzygies and their moduli, and Markman's diagonal argument [5] concerns a particular reflexive sheaf. The closest published rStation construction, stable fusion [6], starts with existing hyperholomorphic coefficients under a common-restriction hypothesis and preserves mixed low character. This paper instead starts with an arbitrary smooth separated ideal and proves existence without that preservation property. The curvature criterion in rStation paper 94 [7] also does not supply the resolutions proved here. The advance is limited but substantive, rather than a repetition of those results.

The normalized second-character recurrence is correct. I reproduced the supplied checker in a separate working copy and obtained output identical to the retained JSON; independent rational checks also agreed with the recurrence. These computations verify class arithmetic and incidence inequalities, not the existence of maps at the formal two-step twists or deformation of the resulting bundles. The manuscript appropriately makes those distinctions. Its main text contains the essential geometric evidence, and the organization, notation, informative metadata abstract, and separation of theorem from limitation meet the clarity requirement.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more self-contained:

- Explicitly define $M_{A^m,V}=\ker(V\otimes\mathcal O_Y\to A^m)$ when it first appears in Lemma 3.
- Specify Theorem 2.5 in reference [3] at its point of use, and cite the standard polarization-openness result used in Section 5.
- Cite the existing determinant-transport criterion [7] directly when explaining the remaining low-Chern-class requirement. This would make the connection to the Hodge-conjecture objective easier to locate without changing the carefully limited claims.
