## 1. Results

Let $k\in\{3,4,6\}$, and let $n\ge3$ be odd with $\gcd(n,k)=1$.
Put $m=(n-1)/2$. For $t\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$ let $C_t$ be
the smooth projective model of

$$
v^k=F_t(x):=\frac{(x^n-1)(x^n-t)}{x^n}=x^n+tx^{-n}-(1+t).
\tag{1.1}
$$

Choose $a_t$ with $a_t^n=t$. Write

- $\delta_t(x,v)=(x,\zeta_kv)$;
- $r_t(x,v)=(\zeta_nx,v)$;
- $J_t(x,v)=(a_t/x,v)$, which preserves (1.1) because $F_t(a_t/x)=F_t(x)$.

Let $E_k$ be the curve $e^2=z^3-z$ if $k=4$, and $e^2=z^3+1$ if
$k=3,6$. Let $\epsilon(z,e)=(\zeta_k^{-2}z,\zeta_k^{-3}e)$. It
preserves $E_k$ and acts on $dz/e$ by $\zeta_k$. On
$M_{t,u}=C_t\times C_u\times E_k$, let $G$ be the group generated by

$$
(\delta_t,1,\epsilon),\quad(1,\delta_u,\epsilon),\quad
(r_t^{-2},r_u,1),\quad(J_t,J_u,[-1]),
\tag{1.2}
$$

and put

$$
V_{t,u}=H^3(M_{t,u},\mathbb Q)^G.
\tag{1.3}
$$

The first three generators span a subgroup $H\simeq C_k^2\times C_n$.
The last has order two, centralizes $C_k^2$ and inverts $C_n$, so
$|G|=2k^2n$. The space $V_{t,u}$ is the image of the algebraic
averaging projector. For $k=3$, $n=5$ this is exactly the setting of
[1].

**Theorem A (curve support).** Let $(k,n)$ be one of the seven pairs

$$
(3,5),\quad(4,3),\ (4,5),\ (4,7),\quad(6,5),\ (6,7),\ (6,11).
\tag{1.4}
$$

For every $t,u\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$, the space $V_{t,u}$ has
rank $4(n-1)$ and Hodge numbers $(0,2(n-1),2(n-1),0)$. There is an
abelian variety $U_{t,u}$ of dimension $2(n-1)$, defined up to isogeny,
with mutually inverse algebraic correspondences

$$
H^1(U_{t,u},\mathbb Q)(-1)\simeq V_{t,u}.
\tag{1.5}
$$

In particular $V_{t,u}$ is supplied by $H^1$ of smooth projective
curves, with Tate twist.

**Theorem B (Hodge-level boundary).** For every $k\in\{3,4,6\}$,
every odd $n\ge3$ coprime to $k$, and all $t,u$:

$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V_{t,u}=4(n-1),\qquad
h^{3,0}(V_{t,u})=\left\lfloor\frac n{2k}\right\rfloor .
\tag{1.6}
$$

The part $V^{\rm prim}$ with primitive order-$n$ rotation characters
has dimension $4\varphi(n)$ and
$h^{3,0}(V^{\rm prim})=\#\{1\le\ell\le\lfloor n/2k\rfloor: \gcd(\ell,n)=1\}$.

**Corollary C (classification).** For the signed toric projector
(1.2), the following are equivalent:

- (i) the whole of $V_{t,u}$ is supplied by $H^1$ of curves for every
  allowed $(t,u)$;
- (ii) $h^{3,0}(V_{t,u})=0$;
- (iii) $n<2k$;
- (iv) $(k,n)$ is one of the pairs (1.4).

*Proof.* (i)⇒(ii) holds because a Tate twist of a weight-one structure
has no $(3,0)$ part. (ii)⇔(iii) is (1.6); note that $n\ne2k$ since $n$
is odd. (iii)⇔(iv) is a direct enumeration of the odd $n$ coprime to
$k$ with $3\le n<2k$. (iv)⇒(i) is Theorem A. $\square$

The canonical calculation of Section 7 has the same threshold. On the
uniruled carrier the canonical class satisfies
$K\le(-1+\tfrac{n-1}{2k})F$ numerically, which is negative exactly
when $n<2k$. So the geometric mechanism stops exactly where the Hodge
numbers say every mechanism must.

**Relation to [1].**

- The case $(3,5)$ is Theorem A of [1]. We do not re-prove it.
- The six cases with $k=4,6$ follow the global chain of [1, §§5–8]:
  integral neighbour, period descent, uniruled carrier, relative K3
  isometries, and fixed-class specialization. We state the steps that
  transfer unchanged and prove the ones that do not. The inputs not in
  [1] are:
  - the persistent lattices and the reciprocal negative-representative
    calculation (Section 5). This replaces the Eisenstein-module argument
    of [1, §4];
  - the internal discriminant action at the two $I_0^*$ fibres for $k=6$ (Section 5.5);
  - an exact monodromy-irreducibility certificate for the generic
    Picard rank (Section 4). This replaces the ball-dominance argument
    of [1, §5];
  - a uniform Hodge-line calculation at the inertia point (Section 7).
- Theorem B covers all $k$, including $k=3$, where [1] treats only
  $n=5$.

**What is not claimed.**

- We make no claim about Mumford–Tate groups. In particular we do not
  claim $V$ is irreducible.
- For $n>2k$ we show only that the whole of $V$ cannot be curve-supplied.
  Smaller level-one summands and Hodge-conjecture questions for $V$
  there are not addressed.
- The general rational Hodge conjecture remains open.

Throughout, "very general" means outside a countable union of proper
closed subsets of the stated parameter space.

## 2. Characters and the proof of Theorem B

**Eigenforms.** For $1\le j<k$ put $q_j=\lfloor jn/k\rfloor$.

**Lemma 2.1.** The forms
$$
\omega_{j,\ell}=x^{\ell-1}\frac{dx}{v^j},\qquad -q_j\le\ell\le q_j,
\tag{2.1}
$$
for $1\le j<k$ form a basis of $H^0(C_t,\Omega^1)$. The form
$\omega_{j,\ell}$ has $\delta_t$-character $\zeta_k^{-j}$ and
$r_t$-character $\zeta_n^{\ell}$. Moreover
$$
J_t^*\,\omega_{j,\ell}=-a_t^{\ell}\,\omega_{j,-\ell}.
\tag{2.2}
$$

*Proof.* Since $\gcd(n,k)=1$, the points $x=0,\infty$ are totally
ramified.

- At $x=0$, write $x=\pi^k$, $v\sim\pi^{-n}$. The order of
  $\omega_{j,\ell}$ is $k\ell-1+jn$.
- At $x=\infty$, write $x=\pi^{-k}$, $v\sim\pi^{-n}$. The order is
  $-k\ell-1+jn$.
- Since $k\nmid jn$, both orders are nonnegative exactly for
  $|\ell|\le q_j$.
- At the $2n$ simple finite branch points, $v$ is a uniformizer, and
  the order is $k-1-j\ge0$.
- There are no other poles.

The number of forms is $\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(2q_j+1)=(n-1)(k-1)+(k-1)=n(k-1)$,
using $\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\lfloor jn/k\rfloor=(n-1)(k-1)/2$. By
Riemann–Hurwitz, $2g-2=-2k+2n(k-1)+2(k-1)$, so $g(C_t)=n(k-1)$.
The characters are immediate. Formula (2.2) follows from
$(a_t/x)^{\ell-1}d(a_t/x)=-a_t^\ell x^{-\ell-1}dx$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2.** For $j\not\equiv0\pmod k$ and every $\ell\in\mathbb Z/n$,
the $(\zeta_k^{-j},\zeta_n^\ell)$-eigenspace of $H^1(C_t,\mathbb C)$
has dimension two. The involution $J_t$ acts by $-1$ on
$H^1(C_t,\mathbb Q)^{r_t}$.

*Proof.* The map $C_t\to\mathbb P^1_y$, $y=x^n$, is Galois with group
$\langle\delta_t\rangle\times\langle r_t\rangle$. It is branched over
$y=0,\infty,1,t$. The local monodromy at $y=1,t$ is a power of
$\delta_t$. At $y=0,\infty$ it is a product of a generator of each
factor. Hence a character that is nontrivial on $\delta_t$ is
nontrivial at all four points, because the orders $k$ and $n$ are
coprime. Chevalley–Weil then gives dimension $4-2=2$.

For the second statement, $C_t/\langle r_t\rangle$ is
$v^k=(y-1)(y-t)/y$, and $J_t$ induces $y\mapsto t/y$. Its quotient is
$v^k=s-(1+t)$ with $s=y+t/y$, which has genus zero. So $J_t$ has no
invariants on $H^1(C_t/\langle r_t\rangle)$ and, being an involution,
acts there by $-1$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem B.*

- *Künneth reduction.* Every Künneth summand of $H^3(M_{t,u})$ other
  than $H^1\otimes H^1\otimes H^1$ is killed by $(\delta_t,1,\epsilon)$
  or $(1,\delta_u,\epsilon)$. Such a summand has an $H^0$ or $H^2$
  factor, on which the corresponding automorphism acts trivially. Then
  it needs invariants of $\delta$ on $H^1(C)$, which is
  $H^1(\mathbb P^1)=0$, or of $\epsilon$ on $H^1(E_k)$, which is zero.
- *Characters on $H^1\otimes H^1\otimes H^1$.*
  - On $dz/e$, $\epsilon$ acts by $\zeta_k$. So invariance under the
    first two generators forces $\delta$-character $\zeta_k^{-1}$
    ($j=1$) on both curve factors, or the conjugate $j=k-1$ on both.
  - The third generator forces $\ell_2\equiv2\ell_1\pmod n$.
  - By Lemma 2.2, each $\ell_1\in\mathbb Z/n$ contributes
    $2\cdot2\cdot1$ dimensions for each of the two elliptic characters.
    This gives $8$ per $\ell_1$, so $\dim V^{H}=8n$.
- *The involution.* $(J_t,J_u,[-1])$ maps the $\ell_1$-part to the
  $-\ell_1$-part. On $\ell_1=0$ it acts by $(-1)^3=-1$ (Lemma 2.2 and
  $[-1]^*=-1$ on $H^1(E_k)$), so that part is killed. The parts with
  $\ell_1\ne0$ are paired, so $\dim V=8(n-1)/2=4(n-1)$.
- *The $(3,0)$ part.*
  - $H^{3,0}(V)$ is spanned by $G$-averages of
    $\omega_{1,\ell_1}\otimes\omega_{1,\ell_2}\otimes dz/e$ with
    $|\ell_i|\le q:=q_1=\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ and $\ell_2\equiv2\ell_1$.
  - There is no wrapped solution, because $n>3q$: for $k=3$ this uses
    $3\nmid n$, and for $k=4,6$ it is immediate. So $\ell_2=2\ell_1$
    and $|\ell_1|\le\lfloor q/2\rfloor$.
  - By (2.2) the involution maps the $\ell_1$ tensor to a nonzero
    multiple of the $-\ell_1$ tensor, with sign $(-1)^3$ at
    $\ell_1=0$. So the invariants have dimension
    $\lfloor q/2\rfloor=\lfloor n/(2k)\rfloor$.
- *The primitive part.* Restricting to $\gcd(\ell_1,n)=1$ gives the
  statements for $V^{\rm prim}$. Since $n$ is odd, $2\ell_1$ is
  primitive when $\ell_1$ is. $\square$

For $n<2k$, Theorem B gives Hodge numbers $(0,2(n-1),2(n-1),0)$, as
stated in Theorem A. The supplement script `check_theorem_B.py` recomputes (1.6) by
characters for all $k\in\{3,4,6\}$ and odd $n<60$ coprime to $k$, 67
cases. The involution is applied by hand, as in the proof above.

## 3. The quotient threefold and its K3 fibration

### 3.1 Invariants of $H$

On $M=M_{t,u}$ put

$$
A=x_1x_2^2,\qquad x=x_2^n,\qquad q=x/A^m,
\qquad P=(A-q^2)(A-tq^2)(A^mq-1)(A^mq-u).
\tag{3.1}
$$

Then $x_1^n=A/q^2$ and $x_2^n=A^mq$, so

$$
R:=F_t(x_1)F_u(x_2)=\frac{P}{A^{m+1}q^3}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

**Lemma 3.1.** Put $w=v_1v_2$, $X_0=w^2z$, $Y_0=w^3e$, $X=(Aq)^2X_0$
and $Y=(Aq)^3Y_0$. Then $\mathbb C(M)^H=\mathbb C(A,q,X,Y)$, and

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
k=3:&Y^2=X^3+A^{4-2m}P^2,\\
k=4:&Y^2=X^3-A^{3-m}qP\,X,\\
k=6:&Y^2=X^3+A^{5-m}q^3P.
\end{array}
\tag{3.3}
$$

Let $W$ denote a smooth projective model of (3.3), fibred over
$\mathbb P^1_A$. For general fixed $A$ the fibre $W_A$ is an elliptic
K3 surface over $\mathbb P^1_q$ with section and fundamental line
bundle $\mathcal O(2)$. Its singular fibres are:

- $k=4$: eight fibres of type ${\rm III}$, at $q=0,\infty$ and at
  the six roots of $P$;
- $k=6$: two fibres of type $I_0^*$ at $q=0,\infty$ and six of type
  ${\rm II}$ at the roots of $P$;
- $k=3$: six fibres of type ${\rm IV}$ at the roots of $P$.

*Proof.*
- *The $C_n$-quotient.* The third generator in (1.2) fixes $A$, $x$,
  $v_1$, $v_2$, $z$ and $e$. Since $x_1=A/x_2^2$ and $x_2^n=x$, the
  field $\mathbb C(M)$ is generated by $x_2$ over
  $L_1=\mathbb C(A,q,v_1,v_2,z,e)$, so $[\mathbb C(M):L_1]\le n$. Here
  $v_1^k=F_t(x_1)$ and $v_2^k=F_u(x_2)$ lie in $\mathbb C(A,q)$ by the
  formulas above. Since $C_n$ acts faithfully and fixes $L_1$,
  Artin's theorem gives $\mathbb C(M)^{C_n}=L_1$.
- *The $C_k^2$-quotient.* The first two generators multiply $w$ by
  $\zeta_k$ and act on $(z,e)$ by $\epsilon$, so $X_0$ and $Y_0$ are
  invariant. Put $L_0=\mathbb C(A,q,X_0,Y_0)$. Since $w^k=R$,
  $z=X_0/w^2$ and $e=Y_0/w^3$, the field $L_0(w)=\mathbb C(A,q,w,z,e)$
  has degree at most $k$ over $L_0$. Since $v_1^k\in\mathbb C(A,q)$
  and $v_2=w/v_1$, the field $L_1$ has degree at most $k$ over
  $L_0(w)$. So $[L_1:L_0]\le k^2$. The group $C_k^2$ acts faithfully
  on $L_1$ and fixes $L_0$, so $L_1^{C_k^2}=L_0$.
- *The equations.* Substituting $z=X_0/w^2$, $e=Y_0/w^3$ into
  $e^2=z^3-z$ or $e^2=z^3+1$ gives $Y_0^2=X_0^3-RX_0$ ($k=4$),
  $Y_0^2=X_0^3+R$ ($k=6$) and $Y_0^2=X_0^3+R^2$ ($k=3$, where
  $w^6=R^2$). The rescaling and (3.2) give (3.3).
- *The fibres.* For general $A$ the six roots of $P$ in $q$, namely
  $\pm\sqrt A$, $\pm\sqrt{A/t}$, $A^{-m}$, $uA^{-m}$, are distinct and
  nonzero.
  - $k=4$: $a_4$ has degree $7$ with simple zeros at $q=0$ and the roots
    of $P$, hence order $8-7=1$ at $\infty$.
  - $k=6$: $a_6$ has degree $9$ with order $3$ at $0$, hence order $3$
    at $\infty$.
  - $k=3$: $a_6$ has double zeros at the roots of $P$ and degree $12$.

  Tate's algorithm gives the types listed. The Euler numbers sum to
  $8\cdot3$, $2\cdot6+6\cdot2$ and $6\cdot4$, each equal to $24$.
  $\square$

### 3.2 The reciprocal involution

Put $\alpha=a_ta_u^2$ and $\beta=u/\alpha^m$, so $\alpha^n=tu^2$. The
last generator of (1.2) sends $x_1\mapsto a_t/x_1$, $x_2\mapsto a_u/x_2$, fixes $v_1,v_2,z$ and negates $e$. On the invariants it
induces

$$
\tau(A,q,X,Y)=\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{A},\ \frac{\beta}{q},\
\frac{(\alpha\beta)^2X}{A^4q^4},\
-\frac{(\alpha\beta)^3Y}{A^6q^6}\Bigr).
\tag{3.4}
$$

Moreover

$$
P\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{A},\frac{\beta}{q}\Bigr)
=\frac{\alpha^{m+1}\beta^3}{A^{2m+2}q^6}P(A,q),
\tag{3.5}
$$

so all three equations (3.3) are multiplied by
$(\alpha\beta)^6/(Aq)^{12}$ under $\tau$. On the fibrewise 2-form
$\Omega_A=dq\wedge dX/Y$,

$$
\tau^*\Omega_{\alpha/A}=\frac{A^2}{\alpha}\,\Omega_A.
\tag{3.6}
$$

The derivations are direct: $A\mapsto\alpha/A$ and $x\mapsto u/x$
give $q\mapsto\beta/q$, and (3.6) is
$(-\beta q^{-2})(\alpha\beta)^2(Aq)^{-4}/(-(\alpha\beta)^3(Aq)^{-6})$.

Let $Q$ be a smooth projective model of $W/\tau$. It is fibred over
$B=\mathbb P^1_b$, $b=A+\alpha/A$, and its general fibre is $W_A$.
The two fibres $A=\pm\sqrt\alpha$, over $b=\pm2\sqrt\alpha$, carry the
involution $\tau$. By (3.6) it is symplectic there, so it is a
Nikulin involution and its anti-invariant lattice is $E_8(-2)$. Both
$A=0$ and $A=\infty$ lie over $b=\infty$. The rational map $M\to Q$
is the quotient by $G$. Section 8 shows that, over a common open set,
the classes in $V_{t,u}$ are pull-backs of classes from $Q$.

### 3.3 Labels

Put $s=A^{n/2}$ and $c=t^{-1/2}$. In the coordinate $x=qA^m$ the
eight singular fibres of $W_A$ sit at the labels

$$
\{0,\ \infty,\ -cs,\ -s,\ s,\ cs,\ 1,\ u\}.
\tag{3.7}
$$

Put $A=w^2$. Then $b=w^2+\alpha/w^2$, and $\mathbb C(w)/\mathbb C(b)$
is Galois with Klein four group generated by $a:w\mapsto-w$ and
$r:w\mapsto\sqrt\alpha/w$. All eight labels, and their
identifications under the reciprocal transport $x\mapsto u/x$, are
rational functions of $w$. The branch values $b=\infty$ and
$b=\pm2\sqrt\alpha$ have inertia $a$, $r$ and $ar$ respectively, after
labelling the two finite values. So the permutation image of the
monodromy on labels is contained in $\langle a,r\rangle$. Loops around
the finite collisions of labels induce no permutation.

This controls only the label part of the monodromy on the
algebraic classes. For $k=6$ a loop can fix both $I_0^*$ labels and
still act on their component groups. That extra action is computed in
Section 5.5.

## 4. The generic Picard lattice

For $k\in\{4,6\}$ let $N\subset H^2(W_A,\mathbb Z)$ be the primitive
saturation of the lattice spanned by the zero section, the fibre and
the components of the singular fibres not meeting the zero section.
It has rank $2+8=10$ for $k=4$ and $2+2\cdot4=10$ for $k=6$. Section 5
gives its isometry class. Put $T=N^\perp$, of rank $12$, and let
$\pi:W_A\to\mathbb P^1_q$ be the elliptic fibration. The automorphism
$g$ of $W_A$ induced by $(\delta_t,1,1)$ is
$(A,q,X,Y)\mapsto(A,q,\zeta_k^2X,\zeta_k^3Y)$. Let $\Phi_k$ be the
$k$-th cyclotomic polynomial and $K=\mathbb Q[x]/(\Phi_k)$, which is
$\mathbb Q(i)$ or $\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)$.

*The $K$-structure on $T$.* We show $\Phi_k(g^*)=0$ on $T_{\mathbb Q}$.

- $g$ fixes $q$ and the zero section. So it preserves every fibre of
  $\pi$ and the set of fibre components not meeting the zero section,
  hence $N$ and $T$.
- On a smooth fibre, $g$ is an automorphism of order $k$ fixing the
  origin, and $g^*(dX/Y)=\zeta_k^{-1}dX/Y$. So $g^*$ on $H^1$ of the
  fibre has eigenvalues $\zeta_k^{\pm1}$ and is annihilated by
  $\Phi_k$. Every singular fibre in Lemma 3.1 is a cuspidal curve or a
  tree of smooth rational curves, so its $H^1$ vanishes. By proper base
  change, $\Phi_k(g^*)=0$ on the sheaf $R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q$. This sheaf
  is $j_!\mathbb V=j_*\mathbb V$ for the smooth-fibre local system
  $\mathbb V$, since no local monodromy has nonzero invariants, so
  $H^1(\mathbb P^1,R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q)$ is the parabolic cohomology of
  $\mathbb V$.
- Let $L^\bullet$ be the Leray filtration of $H^2(W_A,\mathbb Q)$ for
  $\pi$. Since $\pi\circ g=\pi$, $g^*$ acts on the Leray spectral
  sequence through its action on the sheaves $R^j\pi_*\mathbb Q$. The
  quotient $H^2/L^1$ embeds in $H^0(\mathbb P^1,R^2\pi_*\mathbb Q)$
  by restriction to fibres, that is, by the intersection numbers with
  all fibre components. These vanish on $T$, so $T_{\mathbb Q}\subset L^1$. Further $L^2=\mathbb Q F$ for the fibre class $F$, and
  $F\notin T_{\mathbb Q}$ since $F$ meets the zero section. So
  $T_{\mathbb Q}$ embeds $g^*$-equivariantly in $L^1/L^2$, a
  subquotient of $H^1(\mathbb P^1,R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q)$, on which
  $\Phi_k(g^*)=0$.

Since $\Phi_k$ is irreducible of degree $2$, $T_{\mathbb Q}$ is a
$K$-vector space of dimension $6$, with $x$ acting as $g^*$. On
$\Omega_A=dq\wedge dX/Y$, $g^*$ acts by $\zeta_k^{-1}$. For $k=4$ we
write $i$ for $g^*$; then $i^2=-1$ on $T$, which Section 5.3 uses.
For $k=3$ the corresponding statement is [1, Section 5].

**Proposition 4.1.** Let $k\in\{4,6\}$ and let $n$ be odd and coprime
to $k$. For very general $(A,t,u)$, $NS(W_A)=N$. Hence, for very
general $(t,u)$, $NS(W_A)=N$ for very general $A$.

*Proof.* The finite part is the supplement script
`check_pencil_monodromy.py` (exact arithmetic, no numerical path
extraction).

- *A pencil.* Specialize to $t=1/4$, $u=3$. Up to a scalar twist of
  rank one, the transcendental variation is the primitive-character
  part of $H^1$ of
  $$
  C_s:\ y^k=x^e(x^2-s^2)(x^2-4s^2)(x-1)(x-3),\qquad
  (k,e)=(4,1),(6,3),
  $$
  tensored with $H^1(E_k)$, where $s^2=A^n$. The seven finite
  punctures $-2s,-s,0,s,2s,1,3$ and $\infty$ all have nontrivial
  local monodromy on the primitive character. So the parabolic
  cohomology $L_s$ has $K$-dimension $6$, matching $T_{\mathbb Q}$.
  A scalar twist does not change the unital algebra generated by the
  monodromy.
- *Braid marking.* From $s=4$ the path sweeps to the negative real
  axis, passing the critical values $3,\frac32,1,\frac12,0,-\frac12, -1,-\frac32,-3$ in the upper half-plane. Each nonzero critical
  value is one transverse collision of two punctures. At $s=0$ the five
  punctures $-2s,\dots,2s$ form one cluster, which rotates rigidly
  through $\pi$; its braid is the Garside half-twist on five
  consecutive strands, and its meridian is the square, conjugated
  back along the path.
- *Cohomological action.* A rank-one cocycle on the free group of the
  seven finite punctures is a tuple $(u_i)$, with local characters
  $g_i$. Normalizing $u_0=0$ removes the coboundary line, leaving six
  coordinates. The Hurwitz move sends $(g_i,g_{i+1})\mapsto (g_{i+1},g_i)$ and
  $$
  (u_i,u_{i+1})\mapsto\bigl((1-g_{i+1})u_i+g_iu_{i+1},\ u_i\bigr),
  $$
  by evaluating the cocycle on $\gamma_i\gamma_{i+1}\gamma_i^{-1}$ and
  $\gamma_i$.
- *Reduction at 13.* The matrix entries are polynomials in the $g_i$
  and the $(g_i-1)^{-1}$. Send $i\mapsto5$ or $\zeta_6\mapsto4$ in
  $\mathbb F_{13}$; every $g_i-1$ is a unit. In both cases and for
  both orientation conventions, the algebra generated by the nine
  meridians is $M_6(\mathbb F_{13})$. The script lists $36$ words in
  the meridians whose $36\times36$ coefficient determinant is nonzero
  mod $13$. The same words are therefore $K$-linearly independent in
  characteristic zero, and the monodromy algebra on $L_s$ is
  $M_6(K)$.
- *The power covers.* With $A=w^2$ the pencil parameter is $s=w^n$.
  Each nonzero meridian has winding number zero about $s=0$ and lifts.
  The zero meridian $M_0$ is replaced by $M_0^n$. The reduction
  $\bar M_0$ has order $k$. If $nj\equiv1\pmod k$ then
  $(\bar M_0^{\,n})^j=\bar M_0$, so the reduced algebra is again
  $M_6(\mathbb F_{13})$ and a nonzero minor lifts as before. This
  covers every $n$ coprime to $k$, in particular $(4,3)$. For the
  other pairs in (1.4) the script also computes the algebra of the
  eight nonzero meridians and $\bar M_0^{\,n}$ directly: dimension
  $36$ in all cases.
- *Hodge step.* At a very general point of the pencil,
  $NS(W_A)_{\mathbb Q}\cap T_{\mathbb Q}$ is stable under monodromy
  and under the algebraic automorphism giving $K$. So it is a
  monodromy-stable $K$-subspace. By absolute irreducibility it is $0$
  or $T_{\mathbb Q}$, and $T_{\mathbb Q}$ contains the $(2,0)$ line.
  So it is $0$ and $\rho=10$ there.
- *Spreading.* The locus of the three-parameter family where
  $\rho>10$ is a countable union of proper closed subsets, since it
  misses a point. A very general $(t,u)$ therefore has a very general
  $A$ outside it. $\square$

The eight nonzero meridians alone generate an algebra of dimension

## 5. Lattices and the integral neighbour ($k=4,6$)

For $k=3$ this section is [1, Section 4]. Here $k\in\{4,6\}$, $L$ is
the K3 lattice $H^2(W_A,\mathbb Z)$ at a general point, and $N$, $T$
are as in Section 4. At the reciprocal points $N$ still denotes the
persistent lattice: the actual Picard rank jumps there.

### 5.1 The persistent lattices

- $k=4$. The eight III fibres give mutually orthogonal roots
  $r_0,\dots,r_7$. The section $(X,Y)=(0,0)$ has order two and meets
  every III fibre in the nonidentity component. A III fibre bounds the
  torsion by $\mathbb Z/2$. So
  $$
  N=U\oplus\Bigl(A_1(-1)^8+\mathbb Z\tfrac{r_0+\dots+r_7}{2}\Bigr),
  \qquad|D(N)|=2^6.
  \tag{5.1}
  $$
- $k=6$. The six II fibres exclude torsion, so
  $$
  N=U\oplus D_4(-1)^2,\qquad |D(N)|=2^4,\quad D(N)\simeq\mathbb F_4^2.
  \tag{5.2}
  $$

The torsion and primitivity statements are [2, Corollary 7.2, Lemmas
7.3 and 7.8]. By Proposition 4.1 these are the very general
Néron–Severi lattices.

### 5.2 The Nikulin involution

At $A=\pm\sqrt\alpha$ the involution $\tau$ is symplectic (3.6), so
$L_-\simeq E_8(-2)$ [3, Chapter 15, Section 4.1]. The zero section
and the fibre are fixed, so $U$ contributes nothing to $N_-$.

- $k=4$. $\tau$ pairs the eight III components. With
  $d_j=r_{a_j}-r_{b_j}$ for the four pairs, (5.1) gives
  $$
  N_-=\textstyle\sum_j\mathbb Zd_j+\mathbb Z\frac{d_1+d_2+d_3+d_4}{2}
  \simeq D_4(-2).
  $$
- $k=6$. $\tau$ swaps the two $I_0^*$ fibres. After aligning the
  markings it is the swap, and the anti-diagonal gives
  $N_-\simeq D_4(-2)$.

$N_-$ is primitive in $L_-$. After halving the form it is a primitive
$D_4$ in $E_8$, whose orthogonal complement is again $D_4$. Hence

$$
T_-\simeq D_4(-2).
\tag{5.3}
$$

The supplement script `check_reciprocal_neighbors.py` checks the root
input exhaustively. It fixes one root of $E_8$, enumerates all $2520$
$D_4$ subsystems through it, and finds $24$ orthogonal roots spanning
a $D_4$ in each case.

### 5.3 Classes with negative representatives

The gluing of $N$ and $T$ gives an anti-isometry $\gamma:D(N)\to D(T)$. Let $D_-(T)$ be the image in $D(T)$ of $T^*\cap T_-\otimes \mathbb Q$. It can be smaller than the fixed subgroup of $\tau$. Inside
$L_-=E_8(-2)$ the projection to $N_-\otimes\mathbb Q$ has image
$(N_-(1/2))^*$, which yields classes in $D_-(T)$ via $\gamma$.

- $k=4$. The image consists of the unions of an even number of
  reciprocal label pairs, an isotropic plane of order four. It is all
  of $D_-(T)$. The automorphism $i$ of Section 4 fixes the zero section
  and every III component, so it acts trivially on $D(T)$, and
  $i^2=-1$ on $T$. Hence $(1-i)T^*\subset T$. Equality of covolumes,
  $|D(T)|=2^6=\det(1-i\mid T)$, gives $T^*=(1-i)^{-1}T$. Since $\tau$
  commutes with $i$, $(T^*)_-=(1-i)^{-1}T_-$, which has index four
  over $T_-$. So $D_-(T)$ has order four and is the plane above.
- $k=6$. The image is the diagonal $\Delta=\{(x,x)\}\subset\mathbb F_4^2$. This is the full fixed subgroup of the swap, and $D(T)$ is
  killed by $2$, so $D_-(T)=\gamma(\Delta)$.

### 5.4 The quartic neighbour

Order the labels (3.7) as $\{0,\infty,-s,s,-cs,cs,1,u\}$. By Section
3.3, the inertia at $b=\infty$ and at the two reciprocal points is

$$
a=(23)(45),\qquad r=(01)(24)(35)(67),\qquad ar.
\tag{5.4}
$$

Model $D(N)$ by even subsets of the eight labels modulo
complementation, with $q(S)=-|S|/2\bmod2$. There are $35$ nonzero
isotropic classes, $11$ fixed by $r$ and $5$ fixed by $\langle a,r\rangle$. The two planes $D_-(T)$, at the two reciprocal points,
meet in exactly one nonzero class:

$$
v=\{0,\infty,1,u\}\mid\{-s,s,-cs,cs\}.
\tag{5.5}
$$

Let $T'\supset T$ be the index-two even overlattice defined by
$\gamma(v)$. Then $D(T')=v^\perp/\langle v\rangle\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)^4$ is of plus type, and $a$, $r$ act trivially on it.

The label permutations give the whole monodromy action on $N$. A III
fibre has one nonidentity component, and (5.1) is the full persistent
lattice. So a loop that returns the labels returns $N$ pointwise.
This includes the collision loops (Section 3.3). Hence the full
monodromy group preserves $T'$ and acts trivially on $D(T')$.

### 5.5 The sextic neighbour

Write $B(A,q)=A^{5-m}q^3P$. The leading cubic coefficients at the two
$I_0^*$ fibres are

$$
B/q^3\big|_{q=0}=uA^{7-m},\qquad q^{-9}B\big|_{q=\infty}=tA^{5+m},
\tag{5.6}
$$

the second in the chart $X'=X/q^4$, $Y'=Y/q^6$. The blow-up
$X=qX_1$, $Y=qY_1$ turns $Y^2=X^3+q^3b(q)$ into $Y_1^2=q(X_1^3+b(q))$.
The three singular points $X_1^3=-b(0)$ label the three nonidentity
outer components. Their cyclic permutation is multiplication by
$\omega$ on $D_4^*/D_4\simeq\mathbb F_4$. Since $7-m\equiv n$ and
$5+m\equiv-n\pmod3$, a loop around $A=0$ acts on $D(N)$ by

$$
g=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{n},\omega^{-n}).
\tag{5.7}
$$

The label field does not see this action. The coefficients (5.6) are
monomials in $A$, so the collision loops permute no outer components.
With suitable markings the two reciprocal loops act by the swap and
by $g$ composed with the swap.

The form on $D(N)$ is $q(x,y)=N(x)+N(y)$, with $N$ the norm
$\mathbb F_4\to\mathbb F_2$. Its six maximal isotropic planes are
three linear and three semilinear graphs. Since $3\nmid n$, exactly
the three semilinear graphs

$$
H_\mu=\{(x,\mu\bar x):x\in\mathbb F_4\},\qquad\mu\in\mathbb F_4^\times,
\tag{5.8}
$$

are preserved by $g$. Each is preserved by both reciprocal actions.
Each meets the negative-representative plane at each reciprocal point
in a group of order two: $\Delta$ for the swap, and the fixed linear
graph of the other action. The diagonal $\Delta$ is swap-stable but not
$g$-stable. Each $H_\mu$ defines a unimodular even overlattice
$T'\simeq II_{2,10}$, preserved by the full monodromy.

### 5.6 Four roots and the complement

Every even index-two extension of $D_4(-2)$ is $A_1(-1)^4$; the
script lists the three isotropic order-two classes and an orthogonal
root basis for each. In both cases $T'_-$ is such an extension, at
each reciprocal point. The involution is $-1$ on $T'_-$, so it is the
product of the four commuting reflections in these roots: a Weyl
element.

Put

$$
N'_4=U\oplus D_4(-1)^2,\qquad N'_6=U\oplus E_8(-1).
\tag{5.9}
$$

Their discriminant forms are the negatives of those of $T'$, and the
gluing is even unimodular of signature $(3,19)$, hence the K3
lattice [3, Chapter 14, Section 1]. Since monodromy acts trivially on $D(T')$ ($k=4$) or
$D(T')=0$ ($k=6$), every monodromy operator on $T'$ extends by the
identity on $N'_k$. The rational quadratic space and the period are
unchanged: $T'_{\mathbb Q}=T_{\mathbb Q}$. The script checks $23$
exact statements, all of which pass.

## 6. A K3 family over the original base

Fix very general $(t,u)$, so that Proposition 4.1 applies on the
$A$-line. Let $B^\circ\subset B$ be the complement of $b=\infty$, the
two reciprocal points and the $2n$ collision points (Section 7.1).
Over $B^\circ$ the fibres $W_A$ form a smooth family with local system
$T\oplus N$ up to finite index. Section 5 replaces it by $T'\oplus N'_k$, glued to the K3 lattice, with monodromy acting on $T'$ and
trivially on $N'_k$.

**Proposition 6.1.** After removing finitely many further points from
$B^\circ$, there is a smooth projective family of K3 surfaces over the
resulting open subset of $B$ whose $H^2$ local system is $T'\oplus N'_k$, with the period of $W_A$ on $T'_{\mathbb Q}=T_{\mathbb Q}$.

*Proof.* As in [1, Section 5].

- *Polarization.* Choose a primitive $h\in N'_k$ with $h^2=2d>0$
  in an open Weyl chamber. It exists because the roots are locally
  finite in the positive cone, and $h$ has no orthogonal roots in
  $N'_k$. Monodromy fixes $h$ and preserves the component of the
  period domain containing the actual Hodge line.
- *Level structure.* Pass to a deep normal level subgroup of the
  stable isometry group of $h^\perp$. The monodromy torsor over
  $B^\circ$ is finite étale and algebraic by Riemann existence. The
  lifted period map to the arithmetic quotient is algebraic by Borel.
- *Root walls.* The $h$-root divisor in the arithmetic quotient is
  algebraic. The period curve is not contained in it: at a very
  general point the algebraic lattice is $N'_k$ (Proposition 4.1 and
  $T'_{\mathbb Q}=T_{\mathbb Q}$), and $h$ is orthogonal to no root
  there. So the preimage of the root divisor is finite. Remove it.
  These added walls have identity monodromy, since the local system
  extends across them unchanged.
- *Descent.* The level family of $h$-polarized K3 surfaces descends
  along the whole torsor, using the polarized K3 stack and the unique
  Torelli identifications [3, Chapter 6, Section 4.2, Remark 4.6].
  Faithfulness of $\operatorname{Aut}\to O(H^2)$ makes the cocycle
  strict. The Brauer group of a smooth affine complex curve vanishes,
  so $h$ gives a relatively ample line bundle. $\square$

Unlike the six-point argument of [1], no dominance onto a larger
period domain is claimed. The generic Picard rank of Proposition 4.1
is exactly what keeps the period curve out of the root divisor. This
is the only use of a countable very-general exclusion. Later sections
keep $(t,u)$ very general only in order to work with this family, and
the distinctness conditions of Section 7.1 are Zariski open. Section 9
removes the restriction.

## 7. Boundary, canonical class and the uniruled carrier

Throughout, $(t,u)$ is very general and $k\in\{4,6\}$; for $k=3$ see
[1, Section 6].

### 7.1 The boundary points of $B$

The six labels $\pm\sqrt A$, $\pm\sqrt{A/t}$, $A^{-m}$, $uA^{-m}$
collide exactly when

$$
A^n\in\{1,\ t,\ u^2,\ tu^2\},
\tag{7.1}
$$

by intersecting $A=aq^2$ ($a\in\{1,t\}$) with $A^mq=b_1$
($b_1\in\{1,u\}$). The other coincidences need $t=1$, $u=1$, or
$A\in\{0,\infty\}$. This gives $4n$ points on the $A$-line. The
involution $\tau$ swaps $A^n=1$ with $A^n=tu^2$ and $A^n=t$ with
$A^n=u^2$, so there are $2n$ collision points on $B$. They are disjoint
from the reciprocal points $b=\pm2\sqrt\alpha$. The remaining
boundary point is $b=\infty$, the image of $A=0,\infty$.

### 7.2 Local monodromy at finite points

- *Collisions, $k=4$.* Two III fibres merge into $I_0^*$. Locally
  $Y^2=X^3-(q^2-\epsilon^2)X$ up to units, for a transverse parameter
  $\epsilon$. The persistent roots are $A_1^2\subset D_4$, and the
  vanishing lattice is the complementary $A_1^2$. The meridian is $-1$
  on it: the product of two orthogonal node reflections.
- *Collisions, $k=6$.* Two II fibres merge into IV. Locally
  $(Y-q)(Y+q)=X^3-\epsilon^2$ up to units, the pull-back by
  $\epsilon\mapsto\epsilon^2$ of the standard $A_2$ smoothing. The
  meridian is the square of a Coxeter element of $W(A_2)$.
- *Reciprocal points.* The product of four commuting reflections
  (Section 5.6).
- *Added root walls* (Proposition 6.1). Identity.

The vanishing lattices $A_1^2$ and $A_2$ have no proper even
overlattice, so passing to $T'$ does not change them. Each local
monodromy is a Weyl element acting trivially on the limiting Hodge
line, with interior limiting period. By [1, Lemma 6.1], which applies
to any finite Weyl monodromy with interior period whatever the number
of nodes, the polarized family of Proposition 6.1 extends over each
such point with a reduced fibre with ADE singularities. No base change
is needed.

### 7.3 The point $b=\infty$

The K3 two-form is $\eta_A\otimes dz/e$ with $\eta_A=dq/v$ on the
curve

$$
v^k=A^{k-1-m}q^{k-3}P.
\tag{7.2}
$$

For $k=4$ this is the coefficient $A^{3-m}qP$ of $X$ in (3.3); for
$k=6$ it is $A^{5-m}q^3P$. Put $A=\varpi^{2k}$, $q=\varpi^kz$,
$v=\varpi^{3k-n}V$. Then

$$
V^k=z^{k-3}(1-z^2)(1-tz^2)(\varpi^{kn}z-1)(\varpi^{kn}z-u),\qquad
\eta_A=\varpi^{n-2k}\,\frac{dz}{V}.
\tag{7.3}
$$

At $\varpi=0$ the stable limit has two components meeting in one
node:

- the main component $V^k=uz^{k-3}(1-z^2)(1-tz^2)$, on which $dz/V$
  is holomorphic and nonzero;
- a tail carrying the two escaping points and $\infty$, of total
  exponent $1+1+(k-3)=k-1$, prime to $k$.

So the node lifts to one node of the cyclic cover. By Riemann–Hurwitz
the genera are:

| $k$ | main | tail | total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 3 | 1 | 4 |
| 4 | 6 | 3 | 9 |
| 6 | 9 | 4 | 13 |

The total is the genus of (7.2), so the limit is of compact type. The
substitutions are integral, so the cover of degree $2k$ in $\varpi$
kills the finite monodromy. Compact type makes the unipotent part
zero on $H^1$, hence on the K3 summand. The neighbour variation of
Section 5 has the same rational monodromy, so after this cover it has
an interior limiting period.

By (7.3), $\operatorname{ord}_{A=0}\eta_A=n/(2k)-1$. Since
$\gcd(n,2k)=1$, the inertia on the Hodge line has exact order $2k$.
The parabolic degree of the Hodge bundle on $B$ is

$$
\deg_{\rm par}\lambda_B=\frac n{2k}.
\tag{7.4}
$$

There are two derivations.

- *From the normalized curve.* Put $s=\varpi^{-kn}$ and
  $V=\varpi^{2n}\widetilde V$, so that $\widetilde V^k=z^{k-3}(1-z^2) (1-tz^2)(z-s)(z-us)$. The form $dz/\widetilde V$ stays nonzero at the
  finite collisions and at $s=0$. At $s=h^{-k}\to\infty$ it equals
  $h^2\,dz/V_\infty$, with a nonzero limit. So it has parabolic degree
  $2/k$ on the $s$-line. Pulling back to the $\varpi$-line gives
  degree $2n$, and $\varpi\mapsto b$ has degree $4k$.
- *From the original form.* The orders of $\eta_A$ at $\varpi=0$ and
  at $\varpi=\infty$ are $n-2k$ and $n+2k$; at $\varpi=\infty$ this
  is computed with (3.6). They sum to $2n$.

### 7.4 The canonical inequality

Choose a point $b_*\in B$ with smooth fibre and a cyclic cover
$C=\mathbb P^1\to B$ of degree $2k$, totally ramified over $b_*$ and
$b=\infty$. Over $C$ the contracted polarized models glue to a
projective family $f:Z\to C$ of Gorenstein K3 surfaces with ADE
singularities, by the line-bundle descent and GAGA argument of [1,
Section 6]. The divisor table on $C$ is:

| points of $C$ over | local monodromy on $C$ | weight in $\deg\lambda_C$ | coefficient in $R$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $b=\infty$ (one point) | killed (Section 7.3) | $2k\cdot\frac n{2k}=n$ | $\ge0$ (only $F_\infty$) |
| $b_*$ (one point) | trivial | $0$ | $\ge 2k-1$ ($F_*$ fixed pointwise) |
| collisions, reciprocal points, root walls | Weyl (Section 7.2) | $0$ | $0$ ($\mu_{2k}$ free on $C$ there) |
| all other points | trivial | $0$ | $0$ |

The weight $0$ at the Weyl points holds because those elements act
trivially on the limiting Hodge line and the extension of [1, Lemma
6.1] needs no base change. Hence

$$
\lambda_C=\mathcal O_C(n),\qquad\omega_Z=f^*(\omega_C\otimes\lambda_C)
=f^*\mathcal O_C(n-2).
\tag{7.5}
$$

Let $X=Z/\mu_{2k}$ and $\pi:Z\to X$. The ramification divisor $R$ is
Cartier, since its divisorial components are whole irreducible
fibres. By Hurwitz in codimension one,

$$
\pi^*K_X=K_Z-R\ \le\ f^*\mathcal O_C(n-2k-1).
\tag{7.6}
$$

This is an inequality, because it keeps every possible contribution
of $F_\infty$ to $R$. Its degree is negative if and only if $n<2k+1$,
that is, $n<2k$ for odd $n$. This is the range (1.4).

**Proposition 7.1.** For the pairs (1.4), a resolution $\widehat X$ of
$X$ is uniruled.

*Proof.* As in [1, Section 6]. The norm gives a Cartier multiple of
$K_X$. General complete-intersection curves avoid the singular locus,
which has codimension two, and dominate $C$. Their images on
$\widehat X$ form a covering family $\{C'\}$ with $K_{\widehat X}\cdot C'<0$ by (7.6). So $K_{\widehat X}$ is not pseudoeffective, and
$\widehat X$ is uniruled by [5]. No hypothesis on the singularities
of $X$ is needed. $\square$

## 8. Curve support at very general parameters

Fix very general $(t,u)$ and a pair in (1.4). For $k=3$ this section
is [1, Section 7].

### 8.1 From the carrier to $Q$

Over a common affine open $U\subset B$, both $Q$ and $\widehat X$ are
smooth K3 fibrations. Their transcendental variations are rationally
isometric by construction ($T'_{\mathbb Q}=T_{\mathbb Q}$, with the same
period). The algebraic parts $N$ and $N'_k$ have finite monodromy.
After a finite cover of $U$ killing it, Witt's theorem extends the
identity of $T_{\mathbb Q}$ to a flat rational Hodge isometry of
$H^2$ of the fibres.

By [1, Lemma 7.1] there are algebraic correspondences between $Q$ and
$\widehat X$ that are mutually inverse on $H^3$ modulo $N^1H^3$. In
outline: Buskin's theorem [4] makes the isometry algebraic on every
smooth fibre; countably many proper relative cycle spaces and a very
general point select a dominating component; a finite multisection
spreads the class over $U$; the degree is divided out; and closures
give correspondences between the total spaces. The modulo-$N^1$
statement uses three facts.

- *Leray.* $U$ is affine, so $H^3(Q_U)=H^1(U,R^2f_*\mathbb Q)$.
- *Algebraic part.* After a finite cover, $H^1(U,N\otimes\mathbb Q)$
  is spanned by products of divisor classes with $H^1$ of a curve, so
  its compact classes have coniveau one, by weight strictness.
- *Boundary.* The kernel of restriction to $Q_U$ is supported on
  fibres, so it has coniveau one.

By Proposition 7.1, $\widehat X$ is uniruled, hence dominated by
$Y\times\mathbb P^1$ for a surface $Y$, and $H^3(\widehat X)= N^1H^3(\widehat X)$. Correspondences preserve $N^1$, so
$H^3(Q)=N^1H^3(Q)$.

### 8.2 Descent to the curve product

On a common open set $M^\circ\to Q^\circ$ is a finite étale quotient
by $G$. A class $\xi\in V_{t,u}$ restricts to a $G$-invariant class
on $M^\circ$. By finite trace it descends to $H^3(Q^\circ)$, inside
$W_3H^3(Q^\circ)$, which is the image of $H^3$ of a smooth projective
compactification [6, Section 3.2]. Lift it to $\bar\xi\in H^3(Q)$, a class of
coniveau one by Section 8.1. Pulling $\bar\xi$ back through the
closure of the graph of $M\dashrightarrow Q$ recovers $\xi$ up to a
class supported on $M\setminus M^\circ$, which has coniveau one. So
$V_{t,u}\subset N^1H^3(M)$. Concretely, there are a smooth
projective curve $D$ and a correspondence $\Gamma$ on $D\times M$
such that $\Gamma_*H^1(D)(-1)\supset V_{t,u}$.

### 8.3 The splitting

Let $p_G=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g$, an algebraic projector, and put
$\varphi=p_G\circ\Gamma_*:H^1(D)(-1)\to V_{t,u}$, which is onto.

$V_{t,u}$ lies in the primitive part $P^3(M)$. Indeed
$H^1(M)^G=0$: the factor $H^1(C_t)$ has no $\delta_t$-invariants,
since $C_t/\langle\delta_t\rangle=\mathbb P^1$; likewise for $C_u$;
and $\epsilon$ acts on $H^1(E_k)$ by $\zeta_k^{\pm1}$. With a
$G$-invariant ample class $L$, this gives $(L\cdot H^1(M))^G=0$.

Let $\varphi^\dagger$ be the adjoint of $\varphi$ for the cup-product
pairings on $H^1(D)$ and $H^3(M)$, both in middle degree. It is induced
by the transposed correspondence composed with $p_G$, so it is
algebraic. Since $V_{t,u}\subset P^3(M)$, the Hodge–Riemann form is
definite on $V_{t,u}$. As $\varphi$ is a morphism of Hodge
structures, $\varphi\varphi^\dagger$ is definite for that form, hence
invertible.
Its inverse is a rational polynomial in it, by Cayley–Hamilton. Then
$e=\varphi^\dagger(\varphi\varphi^\dagger)^{-1}\varphi$ is an
idempotent in $\operatorname{End}(J(D))\otimes\mathbb Q$. Its image
$U_{t,u}$ is an abelian subvariety up to isogeny, of dimension
$\frac12\dim V_{t,u}=2(n-1)$. The correspondences $\varphi$ and
$\varphi^\dagger(\varphi\varphi^\dagger)^{-1}$ restrict to mutually
inverse isomorphisms $H^1(U_{t,u})(-1)\simeq V_{t,u}$. This proves
Theorem A for very general $(t,u)$.

## 9. Every parameter

Let $S$ be the finite cover of $(\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\})^2$ that
chooses $a_t$ and $a_u$. It is étale, since $a\mapsto a^n$ is étale
away from $0$. Over $S$ the curves, their product $\mathcal M$, and
every generator in (1.2) form smooth projective families, with an
algebraic averaging projector. By Theorem B the rank and the Hodge
numbers of $V$ are constant on $S$.

The polarized weight-one variation $V(1)$ has an integral structure
after clearing the denominators of the projector. After a finite
level cover of $S$ it is the $H^1$ of a smooth abelian scheme
$\mathcal U\to S$ of relative dimension $g=2(n-1)$. The graph of the
isomorphism $H^1(\mathcal U_s)(-1)\simeq V_s$ is a flat section of
$H^{2g+2}(\mathcal U_s\times\mathcal M_s,\mathbb Q)(g+1)$. The
existence of $\mathcal U$ does not by itself make this class
algebraic.

At very general $s$ the class is algebraic. Section 8 gives an
algebraic isomorphism $\psi:H^1(U_{t,u})(-1)\simeq V_s$. Composing
$\psi^{-1}$ with the flat isomorphism gives a Hodge isomorphism
$H^1(\mathcal U_s)\simeq H^1(U_{t,u})$, which is algebraic by the
Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem on $\mathcal U_s\times U_{t,u}$. Now apply
[1, Lemma 8.1]. Products of
proper relative cycle spaces parameterize all codimension-$(g+1)$
cycles on the fibres of $\mathcal U\times_S\mathcal M$. Outside the
countable union of the images of the non-dominating components, the
class is represented on a dominating component. The universal cycle
class is flat on that component and equals the prescribed class.
Properness makes the component surjective onto $S$, so the class is
algebraic at every $s\in S$. An ample curve in $\mathcal U_s$ and the
splitting of Section 8.3 then give the mutually inverse
correspondences. Descending along the finite cover $S$ proves
Theorem A for every $t,u\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$, including
$t=u$ and CM parameters.

This argument specializes one flat class that is already algebraic at
a very general point of a smooth projective family. It does not
assert that new Hodge classes on special fibres are algebraic, and it
does not use the variational Hodge conjecture. No Mumford–Tate
hypothesis is used anywhere in the proof.
