# Accept

This revision makes sound corrections and supplies focused explanation of the accepted contribution. The corrected matrix concerns the existing density example; it is a useful completion of that calculation, not a separate substantive contribution requiring another submission. The manuscript retains a clear central argument, and the additions do not turn it into an unfocused collection of results.

The principal correction in Section 5 is justified. The coordinate normalization gives volume density $(1+X)^{-1/2}$, whose weight-two term is $-X/2$. Thus the first local row must contain the previously omitted coefficient $-1/2$ in the $Z$ column. I independently checked the density expansion, the four nodal Hessian determinants, and the exact ranks and kernels of the displayed matrices. The corrected matrix has rank four and kernel $\mathbf C(2Z+X)$; omitting the density term preserves the rank but changes the kernel to $\mathbf CZ$. This supports precisely the stated conclusion: the correction matters for the linear system even though the defect does not distinguish these two matrices. The added gradient argument also exhausts the singular points.

The Euler-field calculation added to Theorem 5.1 correctly explains the weight projection: the volume form has weight ten and the denominator has weight twelve, so a numerator of weight $k$ yields $d(\iota_E\alpha_k)=(k-2)\alpha_k$. Nonresonant components are therefore exact. This strengthens the explanation connecting the residue formula to the matrix, rather than substituting a numerical rank for that explanation. I inspected the modified Singular programs and retained outputs; Singular was unavailable here, so these programs were not independently rerun.

The other changes improve precision without strengthening the claims. The relation-space definition and Tate twists agree with [2], including its Theorem 1(ii) pairing criterion and Theorem 3 nodal statement. The distinction between polynomial-ring and Jacobian-ring annihilators, together with the explicit definition of $\delta$, resolves notation ambiguities. The revised citations to [7, Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.3, Corollary 2.7 and Example 2.8] accurately support the statements attributed to that paper. Appendix A now correctly distinguishes a general quadratic Hodge class from the Hodge–Weil conclusion under the hypotheses of [10, Theorem 0.2 and Proposition 1.11]. The separate local-freeness assumption remains essential.

One minor consistency improvement remains: carry the explicit separation of the two Section 5 examples into the metadata abstract and the introduction's comparison paragraph mentioning [5]. Their wording about integrating or using the density correction with the two-$D_4$ section can still suggest that its detection requires a nonzero correction. The revised main discussion correctly states that the correction vanishes at both points of that section.

The revision preserves the accepted scope and attribution. It supplies neither a general inverse construction from Hodge classes nor a solution of the Hodge conjecture.
