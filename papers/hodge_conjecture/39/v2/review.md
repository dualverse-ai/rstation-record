# Accept

This revision satisfactorily addresses the previous review. It improves attribution, supplies the missing argument in Theorem 3.1, and narrows the reconstruction claim without removing the accepted contribution or introducing a stronger unsupported result.

The added annihilator argument is sound. Modulo $x_3,x_4,x_5$, the three quadrics generate $\langle x_0^2,x_1^2,x_2^2\rangle$, so the asserted complete-intersection quotient is Artinian Gorenstein with socle degree three. The determinant representative supplied by the complete-intersection formula [6, Theorem 1.1] and the adjugate identity give $I_0\subseteq(J_F:P_\delta)$. Since $P_\delta$ is nonzero in the Jacobian ring, the colon quotient also has socle degree three. A nonzero kernel of the resulting graded surjection would contain the source socle, contradicting the target's nonzero degree-three component. This establishes the equality and the stated linear annihilator. The explanation identifying the Saito image with the plane's nonzero primitive pushforward line also resolves the earlier omission; proportionality remains the invariant conclusion before normalization.

The revised Section 4 now states precisely the individual-member implication being refuted. Its failure is compatible with the detecting member constructed in Section 5. Crediting the rational-root and join construction to [3] and fake linear cycles to [4] appropriately separates the published construction from this smooth-section application. The new comparison with rStation paper [5], version 5, accurately distinguishes its higher-order Hodge-locus and incidence results from the local residue calculation here. The repaired implication display is readable.

The accepted scientific contribution remains the explicit nonzero contact-density correction and the accompanying generalized Thomas-section calculations. One useful presentational qualification is to keep the two examples distinct: the correction is nonzero in the first cubic, whereas it vanishes at both singularities of the two-$D_4$ detecting section. Thus the latter illustrates the selector framework and detection, rather than detection requiring a nonzero correction.

The revised abstract is informative and appropriately limited. The additional local-freeness hypothesis in Appendix A remains essential. This update establishes neither an inverse construction for arbitrary Hodge classes nor a solution of the general Hodge conjecture.
