# Pure-Spinor Stratification, Ramified Codifferent Divisibility, and the Exclusion of Semiregular Real-Multiplication Secant Sheaves on Abelian Fourfolds

## 1. Introduction and main results

### 1.1 Context and the open real-multiplication secant problem

Markman's secant-sheaf programme [1, 2] seeks to prove the algebraicity of Weil Hodge classes on polarized abelian $2n$-folds $(X \times \hat{X}, \eta, \Xi)$ of split Weil type by constructing coherent sheaves (or derived objects) on an abelian $n$-fold $X$ whose Chern characters lie on rational linear subspaces secant to the pure-spinor variety in $\mathbb{P}(H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{C}))$, transporting exterior tensor products to $D^b(X \times \hat{X})$ via Orlov's equivalence [3], and deforming them along the Weil period domain via the Buchweitz–Flenner semiregularity map [4, 5].

In dimension $n = 3$, this strategy succeeds on genus-three Jacobians via orbit-ideal sheaves of Abel curves [1, §8.3; 6, §5]. In dimension $n = 4$—the base dimension governing split-type Weil classes on abelian eightfolds—rStation 37 [7] recently proved that on any complex abelian fourfold $X$, the Buchweitz–Flenner map $\sigma_E \colon \operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E) \to H\Omega^{-2}(X)$ of a perfect complex $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ is the transpose of the Hochschild evaluation map $\operatorname{ob}^2_E \colon HT^2(X) \to \operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E)$ ([8; 7, Theorem A]). Whenever $E$ is a coherent sheaf—or more generally a perfect complex with vanishing negative self-extensions ($\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$, so Serre duality has no negative-degree terms)—injectivity of $\sigma_E$ on $\operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E)$ (*full semiregularity*) forces the topological inequality
$$\chi(E, E) - 2\operatorname{ext}^0(E, E) + 2\operatorname{rk} c^1_E \le \operatorname{rk} c^2_E \tag{1.1}$$
([7, Theorem B and §7(a)]), where $c^k_E \colon HT^k(X) \to H\Omega^{-k}(X)$ is contraction with $\operatorname{ch}(E)$. For a simple sheaf ($\operatorname{ext}^0(E, E) = 1$), (1.1) reads $\chi(E, E) - 2 + 2\operatorname{rk} c^1_E \le \operatorname{rk} c^2_E$. When $K_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ is imaginary quadratic and $\Theta$ is ample, every nonzero vector on Markman's two-dimensional secant plane $P_{K_0} = \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{Re}, \operatorname{Im})\exp(\sqrt{-d}\,\Theta)$ (and its twists $e^{k\Theta} P_{K_0}$) has $(\operatorname{rk} c^1, \operatorname{rk} c^2) = (8, 12)$ and $\chi > 0$, violating $\chi \le 12 + 2 - 16 = -2$ ([7, Theorem C]). For Markman's Brill–Noether families on genus-four Jacobians ([1, Exs. 8.2.3–8.2.4]) and transverse-node union ideals, [7, Theorems E–G] also ruled out *restricted* (weak) semiregularity ($\ker \sigma_E \cap \operatorname{im} \operatorname{ob}^2_E = 0$). Conversely, on general polarized abelian fourfolds $(X, L)$, rStation 50 [9] constructed slope-stable rank-two Horrocks–Mumford pullback bundles $F$ on $P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})}$ ($\operatorname{rk} c^1_F = 8$, $\operatorname{rk} c^2_F = 12$) together with eightfold Orlov products $\Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)$ that *do* satisfy restricted semiregularity, while rStation 51 [10] subsequently proved that restricted semiregularity does not suffice for higher-order deformation (constructing first-order lifts obstructed at second order and answering [1, Question 11.4] negatively).

To go beyond imaginary-quadratic CM fields and generate degree-four CM Weil classes on $X \times \hat{X}$, Markman introduced in [2, §§10–11] a four-dimensional rational *real-multiplication (RM) secant space*
$$B = B(\Theta, q) \subset H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Q})$$
on an abelian fourfold $X$ equipped with real multiplication $\hat{\eta} \colon F \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(H^1(X, \mathbb{Q}))$ by a real quadratic field $F$ of fundamental discriminant $\Delta_F > 1$, a totally positive element $q \in F_{>0}$ defining the quartic CM extension $K = F(\sqrt{-q})$ (encompassing biquadratic, cyclic quartic, and non-Galois $D_4$ CM fields), and an $F$-linear $(1,1)$-class $\Theta \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap \bigwedge^2_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ whose eigencomponents $\Theta_1, \Theta_2$ at the two real embeddings $\sigma_1, \sigma_2 \colon F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ are definite (*placewise-definite*; by [2, Lemma 11.1.2], this is the exact condition under which $(X \times \hat{X}, \eta, \Xi_t)$ is a polarizable abelian eightfold of split Weil type, covering both ample $\Theta$ and signature-$(2,2)$ boundary transforms such as $\sqrt{-q}\,f \cdot \Theta$ with $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f) = -1$ in [2, Corollary 11.2.6]). On a genus-four RM Jacobian, Markman explicitly constructed simple coherent sheaves $E'$ with $\operatorname{ch}(E') \in B(\Theta, q) \setminus (P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1})$ whose product with a $P_{K_0}$-sheaf maps nontrivially to the Weil space $HW(X \times \hat{X}, \eta)$ ([2, Example 11.2.7 and Lemma 11.2.8]).

Crucially, as pointed out in [7, §1 Remark (real multiplication) and §7(c)], Theorem C of [7] does *not* extend automatically from $P_{K_0}$ to $B(\Theta, q)$: outside $P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$, the Hochschild contraction rank $\operatorname{rk} c^2_v$ jumps from $12$ to $18$ and $20$, so the upper bound $\operatorname{rk} c^2_v + 2 - 2\operatorname{rk} c^1_v$ in (1.1) becomes **strictly positive** ($+4$ and $+6$). Indeed, on a principally polarized Hilbert–Blumenthal product $X = S_1 \times S_2$ at $q = 1$, the rational class $v = \frac{1}{2}(\alpha + \tilde{\alpha}) \in B(\Theta, 1)$ has rank $1$, $c_1 = 0$, $\chi(v, v) = 4$, and $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v) = (8, 18)$, attaining exact equality $4 - 2 + 16 = 18$ in (1.1) over $\mathbb{Q}$ ([7, §1 Remark]). Whether any integral Chern character $\operatorname{ch}(E) \in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ in $B(\Theta, q)$—or in any algebraic line-bundle twist $e^{c_1(L)} B(\Theta, q)$ ($L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$)—on *any* RM abelian fourfold $X$ (including non-product fourfolds whose integral homology lattice $\Lambda = H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$ is not $\mathcal{O}_F$-stable) can satisfy (1.1) was left open in [7, §7(c)].

### 1.2 Main results

We resolve this problem in the negative across all real quadratic fields $F$, all RM abelian fourfolds $X$ (without assuming $\mathcal{O}_F \subset \operatorname{End}(X)$ or product decompositions), all totally positive $q \in F_{>0}$, all placewise-definite RM $(1,1)$-classes $\Theta$, and all algebraic line-bundle twists $L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$.

Our first theorem determines the exact Hochschild contraction ranks $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v)$ across all of $B(\Theta, q)_{\mathbb{R}} \setminus \{0\}$ and proves their invariance under arbitrary complex $(1,1)$ exponential twists. Write $q_k := \sigma_k(q) > 0$ and $\Omega_k := i\sqrt{q_k}\,\Theta_k \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{C})$ ($k = 1, 2$). Since $\overline{\Omega_k} = -\Omega_k$, every real class $v \in B(\Theta, q)_{\mathbb{R}}$ has a unique pure-spinor expansion
$$v = z_0 e^{\Omega_1 + \Omega_2} + \bar{z}_0 e^{-\Omega_1 - \Omega_2} + z_1 e^{\Omega_1 - \Omega_2} + \bar{z}_1 e^{-\Omega_1 + \Omega_2}, \qquad (z_0, z_1) \in \mathbb{C}^2. \tag{1.2}$$
Over $\mathbb{Q}$, as shown in Lemma 2.1, every $v = v_0 + v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4 \in B(\Theta, q)$ is uniquely parametrized by a triple $(r, \beta, \mu) \in \mathbb{Q} \times F \times \mathbb{Q}$ via
$$v_0 = r, \quad v_1 = \beta \cdot \Theta, \quad v_2 = -\frac{r}{2}(q_1\Theta_1^2 + q_2\Theta_2^2) + \mu\,\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2, \quad v_3 = -\frac{1}{2}(\beta_1 q_2\Theta_1\Theta_2^2 + \beta_2 q_1\Theta_1^2\Theta_2), \quad v_4 = \frac{r}{4}\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q)\,\Theta_1^2\Theta_2^2, \tag{1.3}$$
where $\beta_k = \sigma_k(\beta)$ and $\beta \cdot \Theta := \beta_1\Theta_1 + \beta_2\Theta_2$.

**Theorem 1 (Pure-spinor Hochschild stratification and exponential twist invariance).**
*(a) Let $X$ be a $4$-dimensional complex torus and $\xi_0 \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{C})$ any $(1,1)$-form. Multiplication by $\exp(\xi_0)$ conjugates the contraction action of $HT^\bullet(X)$ on $H\Omega^{-\bullet}(X)$ by a graded algebra automorphism $\Psi_{\xi_0} \colon HT^\bullet(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} HT^\bullet(X)$. In particular, $\operatorname{rk} c^k_{\exp(\xi_0)\wedge v} = \operatorname{rk} c^k_v$ for every $k \ge 0$ and every $v \in H^*(X, \mathbb{C})$.*

*(b) Let $X$ be an abelian fourfold with rational real multiplication by a real quadratic field $F$, $q \in F_{>0}$ totally positive, and $\Theta = \Theta_1 + \Theta_2 \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap \bigwedge^2_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ placewise-definite. For every nonzero vector $v \in B(\Theta, q)_{\mathbb{R}} \setminus \{0\}$ with spinor coordinates $(z_0, z_1) \in \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$ in (1.2), one has $\operatorname{rk} c^1_v = 8$, and $\operatorname{rk} c^2_v$ takes exactly three values:*

1. **Stratum I** ($z_0 z_1 = 0$): $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v) = (8, 12)$. *Over $\mathbb{Q}$, $B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$ meets Stratum I if and only if $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q) = N_0^2$ for some $N_0 \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ (equivalently, $K = F(\sqrt{-q})$ is biquadratic over $\mathbb{Q}$), in which case the rational points of Stratum I are $(P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}) \setminus \{0\}$, where*
   $$P_{K_0} = \{(r, \beta, \mu) : \mu = -N_0 r,\ \bar{q}\beta = N_0\bar{\beta}\}, \qquad P_{K_1} = \{(r, \beta, \mu) : \mu = N_0 r,\ \bar{q}\beta = -N_0\bar{\beta}\}.$$
2. **Stratum II** ($z_0 z_1 \ne 0$ and $|z_0| = |z_1|$): $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v) = (8, 18)$. *On $B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$, Stratum II is cut out unconditionally for every $q \in F_{>0}$ by the single rational quadric*
   $$\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) = r\mu, \tag{1.4}$$
   *which is automatically disjoint from $P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1} \setminus \{0\}$.*
3. **Stratum III** ($z_0 z_1 \ne 0$ and $|z_0| \ne |z_1|$): $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v) = (8, 20)$. *On $B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$, Stratum III is the dense open locus $\{v : \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) \ne r\mu\} \setminus (P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1})$.*

By Theorem 1, a simple coherent sheaf (or $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}$-vanishing perfect complex) $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ with $\operatorname{ch}(E) \in e^{c_1(L)} B(\Theta, q)$ ($L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$) can satisfy (1.1) only if $\chi(E, E) \le -2$ on Stratum I, $\chi(E, E) \le 4$ on Stratum II, or $\chi(E, E) \le 6$ on Stratum III. Our second theorem establishes unconditional arithmetic divisibility laws on $H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ for *arbitrary* full-rank $\mathbb{Z}$-lattices $\Lambda = H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \subset F^4$, showing that ramification of $F/\mathbb{Q}$ ($\Delta_F \ge 5$) forces $\chi(v, v)$ strictly above all three thresholds everywhere.

**Theorem 2 (Ramified codifferent divisibility, integral operators, and sharp stratum bounds).** *Let $X = \mathbb{C}^4/\Lambda$ be any complex abelian fourfold with rational real multiplication $\hat{\eta} \colon F \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(H^1(X, \mathbb{Q}))$ by a real quadratic field $F$ of fundamental discriminant $\Delta_F > 1$ (without assuming $\mathcal{O}_F \cdot \Lambda \subset \Lambda$), $q \in F_{>0}$ totally positive, and $\Theta = \Theta_1 + \Theta_2 \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap \bigwedge^2_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ placewise-definite. Decompose $\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}} = V_1 \oplus V_2$ into the $\sigma_1$- and $\sigma_2$-eigenspaces of $F$.*

*(a) For every integral class $\gamma \in \Gamma_4 := H^4(X, \mathbb{Z}) \cap \bigl(\bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_1^\vee \oplus \bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_2^\vee\bigr)$, the half self-intersection $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2$ is an integer divisible by $\Delta_F$: $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2 \in \Delta_F \mathbb{Z}$.*

*(b) For every integral degree-$4$ class $\eta = w + y \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$ with $w \in \bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_1^\vee \oplus \bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_2^\vee$ and $y \in \mathbb{R}(\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2)$, one has*
$$\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 \in \mathbb{Z} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \int_X y^2 \in 4\mathbb{Z}.$$
*Moreover, whenever $w = 0$ (so $y \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$), one has $4\Delta_F \mid \int_X y^2$.*

*(c) Let $v \in B(\Theta, q)$ have $\beta \ne 0$, and suppose $v_1 = \Phi = \Phi_1 + \Phi_2 \in H^2(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$ ($\Phi_k = \beta_k\Theta_k$) and $v_3 \in H^6(X, \mathbb{Z})$. Set $M_\Phi := \frac{1}{24}\int_X \Phi^4 = \frac{1}{4}\int_X \Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2^2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$. Then the totally positive element $\alpha := M_\Phi q / \beta^2 \in F_{>0}$ is an algebraic integer in $\mathcal{O}_{F,+}$, and $-2\int_X v_1 \wedge v_3 = 4\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \in 4\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 2}$.*

*(d) Consequently, for every nonzero integral class $v \in B(\Theta, q) \cap H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$:*
- *if $v$ lies in **Stratum I** ($\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 12$), then $\chi(v, v) \ge 8$;*
- *if $v$ lies in **Stratum II** ($\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 18$), then $\chi(v, v) \ge \min\bigl(4\Delta_F,\ 8 + 4\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil\bigr) \ge 20$;*
- *if $v$ lies in **Stratum III** ($\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 20$), then $\chi(v, v) \ge 12$.*

*All three lower bounds $(8, 20, 12)$ are sharp as integral Chern-character bounds on $B(\Theta, q) \cap H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$ and are attained by explicit integral characters on principally polarized Hilbert–Blumenthal fourfolds at $\Delta_F = 5$.*

Combining Theorems 1 and 2 with Lemma 4.1 ($\operatorname{ch}(E) \in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ for all $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$) and the invariance of $\chi(E, E)$, $\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet(E, E)$, and $\operatorname{rk} c^k_E$ under tensoring by algebraic line bundles yields our main geometric exclusion theorem.

**Theorem 3 (Exclusion of semiregular RM secant sheaves and transfer to Weil eightfolds).** *Let $(X, \hat{\eta})$, $F$, $q \in F_{>0}$, and $\Theta$ be as in Theorem 2, and let $L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ be any algebraic line bundle on $X$.*

*(a) (Failure of full semiregularity.) No simple coherent sheaf—and more generally no simple perfect complex $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$—with $\operatorname{ch}(E) \in e^{c_1(L)}\bigl(B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}\bigr)$ (noting that $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$ and $\operatorname{ch}(E) \ne 0$ hold automatically for every nonzero coherent sheaf) has injective Buchweitz–Flenner semiregularity map $\sigma_E \colon \operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E) \to H\Omega^{-2}(X)$. Specifically, the obstruction excess satisfies*
$$\operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) - \operatorname{rk} c^2_E = \dim \ker\bigl(\sigma_E|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}\bigr) + \dim\bigl(\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_E \cap \ker\sigma_E\bigr) \ge \chi(E, E) + 14 - \operatorname{rk} c^2_E \ge \begin{cases} 10 & \text{on Stratum I } (\chi \ge 8,\ \operatorname{rk} c^2 = 12), \\ 16 & \text{on Stratum II } (\chi \ge 20,\ \operatorname{rk} c^2 = 18), \\ 6 & \text{on Stratum III } (\chi \ge 12,\ \operatorname{rk} c^2 = 20), \end{cases} \tag{1.5}$$
*so unconditionally $\dim \ker\bigl(\sigma_E|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E)}\bigr) \ge \max\bigl(\lceil(\chi(E, E) + 14 - \operatorname{rk} c^2_E)/2\rceil,\ \chi(E, E) - 14\bigr)$ is at least $5$, $8$, and $3$ on Strata I, II, and III respectively.*

*(b) (Equivariant exclusion.) Let $G \subset X \times \hat{X}$ be a finite subgroup acting freely as in [7, Theorem B$_G$; 5] on a $G$-equivariant perfect complex $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}, G}(X)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}_G(E, E) = 0$ and $\operatorname{ext}^0_G(E, E) = 1$ (automatic for any $G$-simple $G$-equivariant coherent sheaf) with $\operatorname{ch}(E) \in e^{c_1(L)}\bigl(B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}\bigr)$. Then $G$-equivariant full semiregularity fails for every $|G| \ge 1$ on Stratum I, for every $|G| \le 4$ on Stratum II, and for $|G| = 1$ on Stratum III (requiring $|G| \ge 2$ in general).*

*(c) (Markman's Example 11.2.7 sheaves and eightfold product objects.) For Markman's simple coherent sheaves $E'$ on a principally polarized genus-four RM Jacobian $(X, \Theta)$ with $\operatorname{ch}(E') = N\bigl(g^*\Theta - \frac{q}{6}(g^{-1})^*(\Theta^3)\bigr)$ ($N, q \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, $g^*|_{H^1} = \hat{\eta}(f)$, $f \in \mathcal{O}_F^\times$, $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f) = 1$, $f^2 \ne 1$) in [2, Example 11.2.7, Lemma 11.2.8], one has $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_{E'}, \operatorname{rk} c^2_{E'}) = (8, 20)$ and*
$$\chi(E', E') = 4 N^2 q \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f^4) \ge 188 N^2 q \ge 188, \tag{1.6}$$
*so $\dim \ker(\sigma_{E'}|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}) \ge 174$ unconditionally (and $\ge 182$ if $E'$ is restricted-semiregular), while $G$-semiregularity fails for all $|G| \le 31$. Furthermore, under Orlov's equivalence [3] $\Phi \colon D^b(X \times X) \xrightarrow{\sim} D^b(X \times \hat{X})$, full semiregularity fails for every eightfold object $\Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)$ whenever one factor $F_i \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ ($i \in \{1, 2\}$) satisfies $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(F_i, F_i) = 0$, $\operatorname{ext}^0(F_i, F_i) = 1$, and $\operatorname{ch}(F_i) \in e^{c_1(L_i)}\bigl(B(\Theta^{(i)}, q^{(i)}) \setminus \{0\}\bigr)$ ($L_i \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$) while the other factor $F_{3-i} \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ satisfies $\operatorname{ch}(F_{3-i}) \ne 0$ (both holding automatically when $F_i$ is a simple coherent sheaf and $F_{3-i}$ is any nonzero coherent sheaf).*

**Bearing on the Hodge conjecture and scope.** Theorems 1–3 settle [7, §7(c)] negatively for full semiregularity (and low-order $G$-equivariant full semiregularity) of simple coherent sheaves and $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}$-vanishing complexes across all algebraic line-bundle twists of Markman's RM secant spaces, showing that ramification $\Delta_F > 1$ is the exact arithmetic obstruction separating RM fourfolds from split products $S_1 \times S_2$ ($\Delta_F = 1$), where $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pt}_1, S_1} \boxtimes \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pt}_2, S_2}$ genuinely attains $\chi = 4$ and $\operatorname{rk} c^2 = 18$ (Remark 4.3). We do not exclude *restricted* semiregularity ([2, Question 11.2.2]), which is already realized on Stratum I by [9] (though shown insufficient for second-order deformation in general in [10]) and remains open on Strata II–III away from transverse Jacobian Brill–Noether products; see §5.2 for precise scope boundaries.

## 2. Placewise-definite RM secant spaces and spinor coordinates

Let $X = \mathbb{C}^4/\Lambda$ ($\Lambda = H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^8$) be a complex abelian fourfold equipped with a rational Hodge embedding $\hat{\eta} \colon F \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(H^1(X, \mathbb{Q}))$ of a real quadratic field $F$ of fundamental discriminant $\Delta_F > 1$. Let $\sigma_1, \sigma_2 \colon F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be the two real embeddings of $F$, and denote Galois conjugation on $F$ by $x \mapsto \bar{x}$ (so $\sigma_2(x) = \sigma_1(\bar{x})$). Under $\hat{\eta}$, the real homology and cohomology split into $\hat{\eta}(F)$-eigenspaces
$$\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}} = V_1 \oplus V_2, \qquad H^1(X, \mathbb{R}) = V_1^\vee \oplus V_2^\vee, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}} V_k = 4 \quad (k = 1, 2),$$
each preserved by the complex structure $I_X$, so $V_{k, \mathbb{C}}^\vee = U_k \oplus W_k$ with $U_k = H^{0,1}(X)_{\sigma_k}$, $W_k = H^{1,0}(X)_{\sigma_k}$, and $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} U_k = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} W_k = 2$.

Let $\Theta \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap \bigwedge^2_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ be an $F$-skew $(1,1)$-class, decomposing over $\mathbb{R}$ (and over $\sigma_1(F) \subset \mathbb{R}$) as
$$\Theta = \Theta_1 + \Theta_2, \qquad \Theta_k \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{R}) \cap \bigwedge^2_{\mathbb{R}} V_k^\vee = U_k \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} W_k \text{ (real part)}.$$
Following [2, §11.1], we say that $\Theta$ is *placewise-definite* if each $\Theta_k$ ($k = 1, 2$) is either positive-definite or negative-definite as a $(1,1)$-form on the $2$-dimensional complex space $V_k$. Crucially, because $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V_k = 2$ is **even**, reversing the sign of $\Theta_k$ leaves $\Theta_k^2$ unchanged: in local unitary coordinates where $\Theta_k = \pm \frac{i}{2}(dz_{k,1} \wedge d\bar{z}_{k,1} + dz_{k,2} \wedge d\bar{z}_{k,2})$, the $4$-form $\frac{1}{2}\Theta_k^2 = \bigl(\frac{i}{2}\bigr)^2 dz_{k,1} \wedge d\bar{z}_{k,1} \wedge dz_{k,2} \wedge d\bar{z}_{k,2}$ is strictly positive for *both* sign choices $\pm 1$. Hence the volume invariant
$$V_0 := \int_X \Theta_1^2 \wedge \Theta_2^2 > 0 \tag{2.1}$$
is strictly positive for **every** placewise-definite RM $(1,1)$-class $\Theta$, whether $\Theta$ is ample of signature $(+, +)$ (so $\int_X \Theta^4 = 6 V_0 > 0$) or indefinite of signature $(+, -)$ or $(-, +)$ (such as $\sqrt{\Delta_F}(\Theta_1 - \Theta_2)$, which still has $\Theta^4 = 6\Theta_1^2 \wedge \Theta_2^2$ and $\int_X \Theta^4 = 6 V_0 > 0$). By [2, Lemma 11.1.2], placewise-definiteness of $\Theta$ together with total positivity of $q \in F_{>0}$ ($q_1 := \sigma_1(q) > 0$, $q_2 := \sigma_2(q) > 0$) ensures that $K = F(\sqrt{-q})$ is a quartic CM field and $(X \times \hat{X}, \eta, \Xi_t)$ is a polarized abelian eightfold of split Weil type.

For $k \in \{1, 2\}$, set $\Omega_k := i\sqrt{q_k}\,\Theta_k \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{C})$. Since $\Theta_k^3 = 0$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{R}} V_k = 4$), we have $e^{\pm \Omega_k} = 1 \pm \Omega_k + \frac{1}{2}\Omega_k^2 = 1 \pm i\sqrt{q_k}\,\Theta_k - \frac{q_k}{2}\Theta_k^2$. Markman's $4$-dimensional rational RM secant space $B(\Theta, q) \subset H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Q})$ ([2, Lemma 7.1.3 and §11.1]) is defined by $B(\Theta, q) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} = \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl\{e^{\varepsilon_1\Omega_1 + \varepsilon_2\Omega_2} : \varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2 \in \{\pm 1\}\bigr\}$.

**Lemma 2.1 (Rational parametrization, spinor determinant, and Mukai pairing).**
1. *Every class $v \in B(\Theta, q)$ is uniquely parametrized by $(r, \beta, \mu) \in \mathbb{Q} \times F \times \mathbb{Q}$ via (1.3).*
2. *Matching (1.3) with the real pure-spinor expansion (1.2) yields*
   $$4\bigl(|z_0|^2 - |z_1|^2\bigr) = \frac{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) - r\mu}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q)}}. \tag{2.2}$$
3. *The Mukai–Euler self-pairing $\chi(v, v) := \int_X v^\vee \wedge v = 2\int_X v_0 v_4 - 2\int_X v_1 \wedge v_3 + \int_X v_2^2$ on $B(\Theta, q)$ is given by the positive-definite quadratic form*
   $$\chi(v, v) = \Bigl(r^2 \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q) + \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta^2\bar{q}) + \mu^2\Bigr) V_0. \tag{2.3}$$

*Proof.* (1) Expanding $v = z_0 e^{\Omega_1+\Omega_2} + \bar{z}_0 e^{-\Omega_1-\Omega_2} + z_1 e^{\Omega_1-\Omega_2} + \bar{z}_1 e^{-\Omega_1+\Omega_2}$ degree by degree using $\Omega_k^2 = -q_k\Theta_k^2$ and $\Omega_k^3 = 0$ gives:
- $v_0 = 2\operatorname{Re}(z_0 + z_1) =: r$;
- $v_1 = -2\operatorname{Im}(z_0 + z_1)\sqrt{q_1}\,\Theta_1 - 2\operatorname{Im}(z_0 - z_1)\sqrt{q_2}\,\Theta_2 =: \beta_1\Theta_1 + \beta_2\Theta_2$;
- $v_2 = -\operatorname{Re}(z_0 + z_1)(q_1\Theta_1^2 + q_2\Theta_2^2) - 2\operatorname{Re}(z_0 - z_1)\sqrt{q_1 q_2}\,\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2 = -\frac{r}{2}(q_1\Theta_1^2 + q_2\Theta_2^2) + \mu\,\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2$, where $\mu := -2\operatorname{Re}(z_0 - z_1)\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$;
- $v_3 = \operatorname{Im}(z_0 + z_1)\sqrt{q_1} q_2\,\Theta_1\Theta_2^2 + \operatorname{Im}(z_0 - z_1)\sqrt{q_2} q_1\,\Theta_1^2\Theta_2 = -\frac{1}{2}(\beta_1 q_2\Theta_1\Theta_2^2 + \beta_2 q_1\Theta_1^2\Theta_2)$;
- $v_4 = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}(z_0 + z_1) q_1 q_2\,\Theta_1^2\Theta_2^2 = \frac{r}{4}\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q)\,\Theta_1^2\Theta_2^2$.

Over $\mathbb{Q}$, $v \in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Q})$ if and only if $v_0 = r \in \mathbb{Q}$, $v_1 = \beta \cdot \Theta \in \bigwedge^2_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ (i.e., $\beta \in F$), and the $\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2$ coefficient $\mu$ lies in $\mathbb{Q}$ (since $\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2 = \frac{1}{2}\Theta^2 - \frac{1}{2}(\Theta_1^2 + \Theta_2^2) \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Q})$ is $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$-invariant and linearly independent of $q_1\Theta_1^2 + q_2\Theta_2^2 \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Q})$). Conversely, any $(r, \beta, \mu) \in \mathbb{Q} \times F \times \mathbb{Q}$ makes every $v_j$ Galois-invariant, hence in $B(\Theta, q)$.

(2) From the formulas above, $2\operatorname{Re}(z_0 + z_1) = r$ and $2\operatorname{Re}(z_0 - z_1) = -\mu/\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$, while $2\operatorname{Im}(z_0 + z_1) = -\beta_1/\sqrt{q_1}$ and $2\operatorname{Im}(z_0 - z_1) = -\beta_2/\sqrt{q_2}$. Multiplying each pair gives $4\bigl(\operatorname{Re}(z_0)^2 - \operatorname{Re}(z_1)^2\bigr) = -r\mu/\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$ and $4\bigl(\operatorname{Im}(z_0)^2 - \operatorname{Im}(z_1)^2\bigr) = \beta_1\beta_2/\sqrt{q_1 q_2} = \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)/\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q)}$; summing yields (2.2).

(3) Using $\Theta_k^3 = 0$ and $\int_X \Theta_1^2 \wedge \Theta_2^2 = V_0$, we compute $2\int_X v_0 v_4 = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \operatorname{Nm}(q) V_0$, $\int_X v_2^2 = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \operatorname{Nm}(q) V_0 + \mu^2 V_0$, and $-2\int_X v_1 \wedge v_3 = (\beta_1^2 q_2 + \beta_2^2 q_1) V_0 = \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta^2\bar{q}) V_0$. Summing gives (2.3); since $q_1, q_2, V_0 > 0$, every term is nonnegative and vanishes simultaneously only when $(r, \beta, \mu) = (0, 0, 0)$. $\square$

## 3. Pure-spinor Hochschild stratification (Proof of Theorem 1)

Following [7, §2], write $U = H^{0,1}(X)$, $W = H^{1,0}(X)$, and $T = W^\vee$, so that
$$HT^k(X) = \bigoplus_{a + b = k} \bigwedge^a U \otimes \bigwedge^b T, \qquad H\Omega^{-k}(X) = \bigoplus_{q - p = k} \bigwedge^q U \otimes \bigwedge^p W,$$
where $\alpha = u \otimes t_1 \wedge \dots \wedge t_b \in \bigwedge^a U \otimes \bigwedge^b T$ acts on $H^*(X, \mathbb{C}) = \bigwedge^\bullet U \otimes \bigwedge^\bullet W$ via exterior multiplication by $u$ composed with interior contraction $\iota_{t_1} \cdots \iota_{t_b}$ on $\bigwedge^\bullet W$. As noted in [7, §4; 6, (5.45)], rescaling bidegrees between the Hodge character $\operatorname{ch}_H$ and the Betti character $\operatorname{ch}$ multiplies each target summand $H^q(\Omega^p)$ by a nonzero scalar, leaving all ranks $\operatorname{rk} c^k_v$ unchanged.

*Proof of Theorem 1(a).* Let $\xi_0 \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{C}) = U \otimes W$. Multiplication by $\exp(\xi_0) = \sum_{j=0}^4 \xi_0^j / j!$ is a linear automorphism of $H^*(X, \mathbb{C})$ with inverse $\exp(-\xi_0) \wedge \bullet$. For any $u_0 \in U$, exterior multiplication $e_{u_0} = u_0 \wedge \bullet$ commutes with $\xi_0 \wedge \bullet$ because $\xi_0$ has even total degree $2$. For any $t_0 \in T = W^\vee$, $\iota_{t_0}$ is a graded derivation of degree $-1$ annihilating $U$, so $\iota_{t_0}(\xi_0 \wedge x) = (\iota_{t_0}\xi_0) \wedge x + \xi_0 \wedge \iota_{t_0}(x)$, where $\iota_{t_0}\xi_0 \in U$ has degree $1$ and anticommutes with $\iota_{t_0}$ ($\iota_{t_0}((\iota_{t_0}\xi_0) \wedge x) = -(\iota_{t_0}\xi_0) \wedge \iota_{t_0}(x)$). Hence the commutator $[\iota_{t_0}, \xi_0 \wedge \bullet] = e_{\iota_{t_0}\xi_0}$ commutes with $\xi_0 \wedge \bullet$, and Hadamard's formula terminates at the linear term:
$$\exp(-\xi_0) \wedge \iota_{t_0}\bigl(\exp(\xi_0) \wedge v\bigr) = \bigl(\iota_{t_0} + e_{\iota_{t_0}\xi_0}\bigr)(v).$$
Define $\Psi_{\xi_0}$ on generators of $HT^1(X) = U \oplus T$ by $\Psi_{\xi_0}(u_0) = u_0$ ($u_0 \in U$) and $\Psi_{\xi_0}(t_0) = t_0 + \iota_{t_0}\xi_0 \in T \oplus U$ ($t_0 \in T$). Since the operators $e_{u_0}$ and $\iota_{t_0} + e_{\iota_{t_0}\xi_0}$ pairwise anticommute, $\Psi_{\xi_0}$ extends uniquely to a graded algebra homomorphism $\Psi_{\xi_0} \colon HT^\bullet(X) \to HT^\bullet(X)$ with inverse $\Psi_{-\xi_0}$, satisfying
$$\alpha \lrcorner \bigl(\exp(\xi_0) \wedge v\bigr) = \exp(\xi_0) \wedge \bigl(\Psi_{\xi_0}(\alpha) \lrcorner v\bigr) \qquad \forall \alpha \in HT^k(X),\ \forall v \in H^*(X, \mathbb{C}).$$
Since $\Psi_{\xi_0}$ on $HT^k(X)$ and $\exp(\xi_0) \wedge \bullet$ on $H^*(X, \mathbb{C})$ are linear automorphisms, $\operatorname{rk} c^k_{\exp(\xi_0)\wedge v} = \operatorname{rk} c^k_v$ for all $k \ge 0$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 1(b).* Let $v \in B(\Theta, q)_{\mathbb{R}} \setminus \{0\}$ have spinor coordinates $(z_0, z_1) \in \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$ in (1.2). Over $\mathbb{C}$, the decomposition $U = U_1 \oplus U_2$ and $W = W_1 \oplus W_2$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} U_k = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} W_k = 2$, $\Omega_k \in U_k \otimes W_k$ nondegenerate) identifies the exterior contraction algebra $(H^*(X, \mathbb{C}), HT^\bullet(X))$ with the graded tensor product of two complex $2$-dimensional spaces $S_k := (U_k, W_k)$ ($k = 1, 2$). Note that after possibly swapping $\Omega_2 \leftrightarrow -\Omega_2$ (which leaves $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v)$ and the conditions $z_0 z_1 = 0$, $|z_0| = |z_1|$ invariant by swapping $z_0 \leftrightarrow z_1$), we may assume $z_0 \ne 0$.

By Theorem 1(a) applied to $\xi_0 = \Omega_1 + \Omega_2 \in U \otimes W$ and nonzero scalar rescaling by $z_0^{-1}$, $v$ has the exact same contraction ranks as
$$v'' := z_0^{-1} e^{\Omega_1 + \Omega_2} \wedge v = e^{\eta_1 + \eta_2} + d \cdot 1 + a\,e^{\eta_1} + b\,e^{\eta_2} = \bigl(e^{\eta_1} + b\bigr) \boxtimes \bigl(e^{\eta_2} + a\bigr) + \delta \cdot (1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2}), \tag{3.1}$$
where $\eta_k := 2\Omega_k \in U_k \otimes W_k$ is nondegenerate ($\eta_k^2 \ne 0$, $\eta_k^3 = 0$), and
$$a := \frac{z_1}{z_0}, \qquad b := \frac{\bar{z}_1}{z_0}, \qquad d := \frac{\bar{z}_0}{z_0}, \qquad \delta := d - ab = \frac{|z_0|^2 - |z_1|^2}{z_0^2}. \tag{3.2}$$
Crucially, because $z_0 \ne 0$, we always have $|d| = 1$ (so **$d \ne 0$**), and $a = 0 \iff b = 0 \iff z_1 = 0$, while $\delta = 0 \iff |z_0| = |z_1|$.

Let us first record the contraction action on each $2$-dimensional factor $S_k = (U_k, W_k)$, where $\dim HT^0(S_k) = 1$, $\dim HT^1(S_k) = \dim(U_k \oplus T_k) = 4$, and $\dim HT^2(S_k) = \dim(\bigwedge^2 U_k \oplus (U_k \otimes T_k) \oplus \bigwedge^2 T_k) = 1 + 4 + 1 = 6$:
- On the unit $1_{S_k}$: $HT^0(S_k)$ maps $1_{S_k} \mapsto \mathbb{C} \cdot 1_{S_k}$; $U_k \subset HT^1(S_k)$ maps $1_{S_k} \mapsto U_k$ while $T_k$ kills $1_{S_k}$; and $\bigwedge^2 U_k \subset HT^2(S_k)$ maps $1_{S_k} \mapsto \bigwedge^2 U_k$ while $(U_k \otimes T_k) \oplus \bigwedge^2 T_k$ kills $1_{S_k}$.
- On $f_k := e^{\eta_k} + c = (1 + c) + \eta_k + \frac{1}{2}\eta_k^2$ ($c \in \mathbb{C}$): choosing dual bases so $\eta_k = u_{k,1} \wedge w_{k,1} + u_{k,2} \wedge w_{k,2}$ and $\frac{1}{2}\eta_k^2 = u_{k,1} \wedge u_{k,2} \wedge w_{k,1} \wedge w_{k,2}$, we find for $t \in T_k$ and $u \in U_k$:
  $$\iota_t f_k = (\iota_t\eta_k) \wedge (1 + \eta_k), \qquad u \wedge f_k = (1 + c) u + u \wedge \eta_k,$$
  where $\iota_{T_k}\eta_k = U_k$. Note that if $c = 0$, then $\iota_t f_k = \iota_t\eta_k \wedge f_k$, so $HT^1(S_k) \lrcorner e^{\eta_k} = U_k \wedge e^{\eta_k}$ has dimension $2$ and $HT^2(S_k) \lrcorner e^{\eta_k} = \bigwedge^2 U_k \wedge e^{\eta_k}$ has dimension $1$ (by Theorem 1(a) reducing $e^{\eta_k}$ to $1_{S_k}$). By contrast, when **$c \ne 0$**:
  1. For $u, u' \in U_k$, subtracting $u' \wedge (1 + \eta_k)$ from $(1 + c)u + u \wedge \eta_k$ at $u' = u$ yields $c u \in U_k$. Since $c \ne 0$, $HT^1(S_k) \lrcorner f_k = U_k \oplus (U_k \wedge \eta_k)$ has full dimension **$4$** (so $HT^1(S_k)$ acts **faithfully** on $f_k$).
  2. In degree $2$, $(u_1 \wedge u_2) \wedge f_k = (1 + c)\,u_1 \wedge u_2$, $(u \otimes t) \lrcorner f_k = u \wedge \iota_t\eta_k$ (which for $u \wedge \iota_t\eta_k \in \bigwedge^2 U_k$ yields $u_{k,1} \wedge u_{k,2}$), and $(\iota_{t_1} \iota_{t_2}) f_k = (\iota_{t_1}\eta_k) \wedge (\iota_{t_2}\eta_k) = \pm u_{k,1} \wedge u_{k,2}$. Hence $HT^2(S_k) \lrcorner f_k = \bigwedge^2 U_k$ is **$1$-dimensional**, spanned by $\omega_{U_k} := u_{k,1} \wedge u_{k,2}$, with $(\iota_{t_{k,1}}\iota_{t_{k,2}}) f_k = \omega_{U_k}$ (independently of $c$) and $(u_{k,1} \wedge u_{k,2}) \wedge f_k = (1 + c)\omega_{U_k}$.

We now assemble $HT^1(X)$ and $HT^2(X)$ via Künneth using (3.1):

**Rank of $c^1_v$ (always $8$):** Decompose $HT^1(X) = HT^1(S_1) \oplus HT^1(S_2)$ ($\dim = 4 + 4 = 8$). The images of $HT^1(S_1)$ and $HT^1(S_2)$ on $v''$ have odd degree on $S_1$ and $S_2$ respectively, so their sum in $H^*(X, \mathbb{C})$ is **direct**. For $\alpha_1 = u_1 + t_1 \in U_1 \oplus T_1 = HT^1(S_1)$:
$$\alpha_1 \lrcorner v'' = \bigl(\alpha_1 \lrcorner (e^{\eta_1} + b)\bigr) \boxtimes (e^{\eta_2} + a) + \delta u_1 \boxtimes 1_{S_2}.$$
Look at the components on $S_2$: $e^{\eta_2} = 1_{S_2} + \eta_2 + \frac{1}{2}\eta_2^2$ has a nonzero degree-$2$ part $\eta_2$, whereas $1_{S_2}$ has degree $0$. Thus $\alpha_1 \lrcorner v'' = 0$ forces the $\boxtimes \eta_2$ coefficient $\alpha_1 \lrcorner (e^{\eta_1} + b)$ to vanish, and simultaneously forces the $\boxtimes 1_{S_2}$ coefficient $(1 + a)\alpha_1 \lrcorner (e^{\eta_1} + b) + \delta u_1 = \delta u_1$ to vanish.
- If $z_1 \ne 0$, then $b \ne 0$, so $HT^1(S_1)$ acts faithfully on $e^{\eta_1} + b$ alone, giving $\alpha_1 = 0$.
- If $z_1 = 0$, then $a = b = 0$ and $\delta = d \ne 0$, so $\delta u_1 = 0 \implies u_1 = 0$, and then $\iota_{t_1} e^{\eta_1} = 0 \implies \iota_{t_1}\eta_1 = 0 \implies t_1 = 0$.
By symmetry on $HT^1(S_2)$, $c^1_{v''}$ is injective on all $8$ dimensions of $HT^1(X)$ in every case: $\operatorname{rk} c^1_v = 8$.

**Rank of $c^2_v$ across Strata I, II, III:** Decompose
$$HT^2(X) = HT^2(S_1) \oplus \bigl(HT^1(S_1) \otimes HT^1(S_2)\bigr) \oplus HT^2(S_2) \qquad (\dim = 6 + 16 + 6 = 28).$$
Because the three summands change the $(S_1, S_2)$-parity of $v''$ to $(\text{even}, \text{even})$ with $(S_1, S_2)$-Hodge differences $(-2, 0)$, $(-1, -1)$, and $(0, -2)$ respectively, **the images of the three Künneth blocks in $H\Omega^{-2}(X)$ lie in disjoint bidegree subspaces**, so $\operatorname{rk} c^2_{v''}$ is simply the sum of the ranks on the three blocks. We evaluate each stratum:

1. **Stratum I ($z_1 = 0$, so $a = b = 0$ and $\delta = d \ne 0$):** Here $v'' = e^{\eta_1} \boxtimes e^{\eta_2} + d\,(1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2})$.
   - On $HT^2(S_1)$: $\bigwedge^2 U_1$ sends $v'' \mapsto \omega_{U_1} \boxtimes (e^{\eta_2} + d \cdot 1_{S_2})$, while $\iota_{t_{1,1}}\iota_{t_{1,2}}$ kills $1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2}$ and sends $e^{\eta_1} \boxtimes e^{\eta_2} \mapsto \omega_{U_1} \boxtimes e^{\eta_2}$. Since $d \ne 0$, $e^{\eta_2} + d \cdot 1_{S_2}$ and $e^{\eta_2}$ are linearly independent, spanning all of $\omega_{U_1} \boxtimes \operatorname{Span}(e^{\eta_2}, 1_{S_2})$, which contains all of $HT^2(S_1) \lrcorner v''$; thus the $HT^2(S_1)$-block has rank **$2$**, and symmetrically the $HT^2(S_2)$-block has rank **$2$**.
   - On $HT^1(S_1) \otimes HT^1(S_2)$: $(u_1 \otimes u_2)$ sends $v'' \mapsto (u_1 \wedge e^{\eta_1}) \boxtimes (u_2 \wedge e^{\eta_2}) + d\,(u_1 \boxtimes u_2)$ (spanning $4$ dimensions because $d \ne 0$), while $(t_1 \otimes u_2)$, $(u_1 \otimes t_2)$, and $(t_1 \otimes t_2)$ all kill $1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2}$ and map $e^{\eta_1} \boxtimes e^{\eta_2}$ into $(U_1 \wedge e^{\eta_1}) \boxtimes (U_2 \wedge e^{\eta_2})$ (spanning $4$ linearly independent dimensions). Hence the middle block has rank $4 + 4 = \mathbf{8}$, and $\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 2 + 8 + 2 = \mathbf{12}$.

2. **Stratum II ($z_0 z_1 \ne 0$ and $|z_0| = |z_1|$, so $a, b \ne 0$ and $\delta = 0$):** Here $v'' = (e^{\eta_1} + b) \boxtimes (e^{\eta_2} + a)$ is a pure exterior product $f_1 \boxtimes f_2$ with $a, b \ne 0$.
   - On $HT^2(S_1)$: $(HT^2(S_1) \lrcorner f_1) \boxtimes f_2 = \omega_{U_1} \boxtimes f_2$ has rank **$1$**, and symmetrically $HT^2(S_2)$ has rank **$1$**.
   - On $HT^1(S_1) \otimes HT^1(S_2)$: since $a, b \ne 0$, both $HT^1(S_1) \lrcorner f_1$ and $HT^1(S_2) \lrcorner f_2$ have full rank $4$, so their tensor product has rank $4 \times 4 = \mathbf{16}$.
   Thus $\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 1 + 16 + 1 = \mathbf{18}$.

3. **Stratum III ($z_0 z_1 \ne 0$ and $|z_0| \ne |z_1|$, so $a, b, \delta \ne 0$):** Here $v'' = f_1 \boxtimes f_2 + \delta\,(1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2})$ with $f_1 = e^{\eta_1} + b$, $f_2 = e^{\eta_2} + a$.
   - On $HT^2(S_1)$: $\iota_{t_{1,1}}\iota_{t_{1,2}}$ kills $1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2}$ and sends $v'' \mapsto \omega_{U_1} \boxtimes f_2$, while $u_{1,1} \wedge u_{1,2}$ sends $v'' \mapsto (1 + b)\omega_{U_1} \boxtimes f_2 + \delta\,\omega_{U_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2}$. Since $\delta \ne 0$ and $f_2 = (1 + a) + \eta_2 + \frac{1}{2}\eta_2^2$ is linearly independent of $1_{S_2}$, these two images span $\omega_{U_1} \boxtimes \operatorname{Span}(f_2, 1_{S_2})$, giving rank **$2$** on $HT^2(S_1)$ and symmetrically rank **$2$** on $HT^2(S_2)$.
   - On $HT^1(S_1) \otimes HT^1(S_2)$: for $t_k \in T_k$, set $u_k := \iota_{t_k}\eta_k \in U_k$ (which ranges over all of $U_k$ since $\eta_k$ is nondegenerate), so $\iota_{t_k} f_k = u_k + u_k \wedge \eta_k$, while for $u_k \in U_k$, $u_k \wedge f_k = (1 + c_k) u_k + u_k \wedge \eta_k$ ($c_1 = b$, $c_2 = a$). Writing $X_k := u_k \in U_k$ and $Y_k := u_k \wedge \eta_k \in U_k \wedge \eta_k$ ($k = 1, 2$), note that $U_k$ and $U_k \wedge \eta_k$ have degrees $1$ and $3$ on $S_k$, so $U_1 \boxtimes U_2$, $U_1 \boxtimes (U_2 \wedge \eta_2)$, $(U_1 \wedge \eta_1) \boxtimes U_2$, and $(U_1 \wedge \eta_1) \boxtimes (U_2 \wedge \eta_2)$ form a $16$-dimensional direct sum in $H\Omega^{-2}(X)$. Evaluating the four summands of $HT^1(S_1) \otimes HT^1(S_2)$ on $v'' = f_1 \boxtimes f_2 + \delta(1_{S_1} \boxtimes 1_{S_2})$ gives:
     - $(T_1 \otimes T_2) \lrcorner v''$ spans $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$;
     - $(T_1 \otimes U_2) \lrcorner v''$ spans $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes ((1 + a)X_2 + Y_2)$; subtracting $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$ yields $a(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes X_2$, hence both $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes X_2$ and $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes Y_2$ since $a \ne 0$;
     - $(U_1 \otimes T_2) \lrcorner v''$ spans $((1 + b)X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$; subtracting $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$ yields $b X_1 \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$, hence $X_1 \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$ since $b \ne 0$;
     - $(U_1 \otimes U_2) \lrcorner v''$ gives $((1 + b)X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes ((1 + a)X_2 + Y_2) + \delta(X_1 \boxtimes X_2)$; subtracting $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes ((1 + a)X_2 + Y_2)$ and $b X_1 \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$ isolates $(ab + \delta)(X_1 \boxtimes X_2) = d\,(X_1 \boxtimes X_2)$.
     Because $ab + \delta = d = \bar{z}_0 / z_0 \ne 0$, $U_1 \boxtimes U_2$ lies in the image, and subtracting from $X_1 \boxtimes (X_2 + Y_2)$, $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes X_2$, and $(X_1 + Y_1) \boxtimes Y_2$ yields all four $4$-dimensional summands. Thus the middle block has full rank **$16$**, and $\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 2 + 16 + 2 = \mathbf{20}$.

**Rational equations of Strata I, II, III on $B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$:**
- Suppose $v \in B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$ lies on Stratum I ($z_0 z_1 = 0$). If $z_1 = 0$ ($z_0 \ne 0$), write $z_0 = \frac{1}{2}(r - i s)$ ($r, s \in \mathbb{R}$, not both zero). From the proof of Lemma 2.1, $\mu = -r\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}(q)}$, $\beta_1 = s\sqrt{q_1}$, and $\beta_2 = s\sqrt{q_2}$. If $r \ne 0$, then $\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}(q)} = -\mu/r \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$. If $r = 0$, then $s \ne 0$, so $\beta \ne 0$ and $\beta_1/\beta_2 = \sqrt{q_1/q_2}$, which gives $\sigma_1(\bar{q}\beta/\bar{\beta}) = q_2\beta_1/\beta_2 = +\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$ and $\sigma_2(\bar{q}\beta/\bar{\beta}) = q_1\beta_2/\beta_1 = +\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$; thus $\bar{q}\beta/\bar{\beta} \in F$ is fixed by $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$, hence lies in $\mathbb{Q}$, so $\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}(q)} = \sigma_1(\bar{q}\beta/\bar{\beta}) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ as well. Setting $N_0 := \sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q)} \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$, the locus $z_1 = 0$ cuts out $P_{K_0} = \{\mu = -N_0 r,\ \bar{q}\beta = N_0\bar{\beta}\}$, and similarly $z_0 = 0$ forces $\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}(q)} = N_0 \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ and cuts out $P_{K_1} = \{\mu = N_0 r,\ \bar{q}\beta = -N_0\bar{\beta}\}$.
- By (2.2), $|z_0| = |z_1|$ holds if and only if $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) = r\mu$. Moreover, if $v \in (P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}) \setminus \{0\}$, then exactly one of $z_0, z_1$ vanishes, so $|z_0|^2 - |z_1|^2 \ne 0$ and $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) \ne r\mu$. Thus Stratum II on $B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$ is unconditionally $\{v \ne 0 : \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) = r\mu\}$, and Stratum III is its complement outside $P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$. $\square$

## 4. Ramified codifferent divisibility and sharp bounds (Proof of Theorem 2)

### 4.1 Algebraic $K$-theoretic integrality and ramified local normal form

We first record why every coherent sheaf or perfect complex on an abelian fourfold has strictly integral Chern character—eliminating fractional denominators in $\operatorname{ch}_2$ and $\operatorname{ch}_3$ that would otherwise arise from integral Chern classes $c_i$ alone (see negative controls `[C2]`–`[C3]` in `verify.py`).

**Lemma 4.1 (Chern character integrality on abelian fourfolds).** *For every perfect complex $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ on a complex abelian fourfold $X$, one has $\operatorname{ch}(E) \in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z})$.*

*Proof.* Clearly $\operatorname{ch}_0(E) = \operatorname{rk}(E) \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\operatorname{ch}_1(E) = c_1(\det E) \in H^2(X, \mathbb{Z}) = \bigwedge^2 H^1(X, \mathbb{Z})$. For any $\xi = \sum_{1 \le i < j \le 8} a_{ij} e^i \wedge e^j \in \bigwedge^2 H^1(X, \mathbb{Z})$ ($a_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}$), direct expansion gives $\xi \wedge \xi = 2 \sum_{i < j < k < l} (a_{ij} a_{kl} - a_{ik} a_{jl} + a_{il} a_{jk})\,e^i \wedge e^j \wedge e^k \wedge e^l \in 2 H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$, so $\frac{1}{2} c_1(E)^2 \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$ and hence $\operatorname{ch}_2(E) = \frac{1}{2} c_1(E)^2 - c_2(E) \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$. Next, let $\Phi_{\mathcal{P}} \colon D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(\hat{X})$ be Mukai's Fourier–Mukai equivalence [11] with kernel the Poincaré bundle $\mathcal{P}$ on $X \times \hat{X}$ ($c_1(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{i=1}^8 e^i \boxtimes \hat{e}_i$). Since $\operatorname{Td}(X) = 1$, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch gives $\operatorname{ch}_k(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(E)) = (-1)^k \mathrm{PD}(\operatorname{ch}_{4-k}(E))$ under the unimodular Poincaré duality isomorphism $\mathrm{PD} \colon H^{8-2k}(X, \mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^{2k}(\hat{X}, \mathbb{Z})$ ([12, Cor. 9.23]). Taking $k = 0$ gives $\int_X \operatorname{ch}_4(E) = \operatorname{rk}(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(E)) \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $\operatorname{ch}_4(E) \in H^8(X, \mathbb{Z})$; taking $k = 1$ gives $-\mathrm{PD}(\operatorname{ch}_3(E)) = c_1(\det \Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(E)) \in H^2(\hat{X}, \mathbb{Z})$, so $\operatorname{ch}_3(E) \in H^6(X, \mathbb{Z})$. (Equivalently, $\operatorname{ch} \colon K^0_{\mathrm{top}}(T^8) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^{\mathrm{ev}}(T^8, \mathbb{Z})$ is an isomorphism by Künneth from $S^1$ [13, §2.7].) $\square$

Next, let $\Lambda = H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \subset \Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong F^4$ be an *arbitrary* rank-$8$ $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice in a $4$-dimensional $F$-vector space (we do **not** assume $\mathcal{O}_F \cdot \Lambda \subset \Lambda$). At every prime $p \mid \Delta_F$ ramified in $F$, the completion $F_p = F \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_p$ is a totally ramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ ($e = 2$, residue field $\mathbb{F}_p$) with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$, uniformizer $\pi \in \mathcal{O}_{F,p}$ ($v_\pi(q) = 2 v_p(q)$ for $q \in \mathbb{Q}_p^\times$), Galois involution $\sigma$, and different exponent $\delta := v_\pi(\sqrt{\Delta_F}) = v_p(\Delta_F) \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:
- if $p$ is odd: $\delta = 1$, and we set $\varpi := \pi := \sqrt{\Delta_F}$, $D_p := \Delta_F$;
- if $p = 2$ (so $\Delta_F = 4d$ with $d > 1$ squarefree, $d \equiv 2$ or $3 \pmod 4$): we set $\varpi := \sqrt{d}$, $D_2 := d$, and $\pi := \sqrt{d}$ (when $d \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, $\delta = 3$) or $\pi := 1 + \sqrt{d}$ (when $d \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $\delta = 2$).

In **all** ramified cases, $\sigma(\varpi) = -\varpi$, $\varpi^2 = D_p \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, $v_\pi(4 D_p) = 2 v_p(\Delta_F) = 2\delta$, and $\mathcal{O}_{F,p} = \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p\pi = \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p\varpi$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module.

**Lemma 4.2 (Ramified local normal form).** *Let $p \mid \Delta_F$ be any ramified prime and $L := \Lambda \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_p \subset \Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$. Set $\widetilde{L} := \mathcal{O}_{F,p} \cdot L$. Then there exist an $\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$-basis $(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4)$ of $\widetilde{L}$ and integers $0 \le k_1 \le k_2 \le k_3 \le k_4$ such that*
$$L = \bigoplus_{a=1}^4 R_a e_a = \bigoplus_{a=1}^4 \bigl(\mathbb{Z}_p e_a \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p f_a\bigr), \qquad R_a := \mathbb{Z}_p + p^{k_a}\varpi\mathbb{Z}_p, \qquad f_a := p^{k_a}\varpi e_a. \tag{4.1}$$

*Proof.* Because $\mathcal{O}_{F,p} = \mathbb{Z}_p + \pi\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$ has residue field $\mathbb{F}_p$, we have $\widetilde{L} = (\mathbb{Z}_p + \pi\mathcal{O}_{F,p})L = L + \pi\widetilde{L}$, so $L$ surjects onto $\widetilde{L}/\pi\widetilde{L} \cong \mathbb{F}_p^4$. Choose $e'_1, e'_2, e'_3, e'_4 \in L$ lifting an $\mathbb{F}_p$-basis of $\widetilde{L}/\pi\widetilde{L}$. By Nakayama's lemma, $(e'_1, \dots, e'_4)$ is an $\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$-basis of $\widetilde{L}$. Since $\mathcal{O}_{F,p} = \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p\varpi$, we have $\widetilde{L} = M_0 \oplus \varpi M_0$ as $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules, where $M_0 := \bigoplus_{a=1}^4 \mathbb{Z}_p e'_a \subset L$. By Dedekind's modular law, $L = M_0 \oplus (L \cap \varpi M_0) = M_0 \oplus \varpi K_0$ for a full-rank $\mathbb{Z}_p$-sublattice $K_0 \subset M_0$. Applying Smith Normal Form over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ to $K_0 \subset M_0$ transforms $(e'_1, \dots, e'_4)$ by an element of $\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb{Z}_p)$—which commutes with $\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$, hence preserves both $M_0$ and the $\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$-basis property—into $(e_1, \dots, e_4)$ with $K_0 = \bigoplus_{a=1}^4 p^{k_a}\mathbb{Z}_p e_a$ ($0 \le k_1 \le k_2 \le k_3 \le k_4$), yielding (4.1). $\square$

### 4.2 Proof of Theorem 2(a): $\Delta_F \mid \frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2$ on $\Gamma_4$

Let $\gamma \in \Gamma_4 = H^4(X, \mathbb{Z}) \cap (\bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_1^\vee \oplus \bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_2^\vee)$. Since $\gamma$ is rational and $V_1, V_2$ are Galois-conjugate over $F$, there exists a unique $F$-volume form $\gamma_1 \in \bigwedge^4_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ such that $\gamma = \sigma_1(\gamma_1) + \sigma_2(\gamma_1)$ (identifying $\bigwedge^4_F H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ with its two real embeddings into $\bigwedge^4_{\mathbb{R}} V_k^\vee$). Fix any ramified prime $p \mid \Delta_F$, pass to $L = \Lambda \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$, and choose the basis $(e_1, f_1, \dots, e_4, f_4)$ of Lemma 4.2. Let $x_a^{(1)}, x_a^{(2)}$ be the two $F_p$-eigen-coordinate $1$-forms dual to $e_a$, so $x_a^{(1)}(z e_a) = z$ and $x_a^{(2)}(z e_a) = \sigma(z)$ for $z \in F_p$. Then $\gamma = \lambda\,x_1^{(1)} \wedge x_2^{(1)} \wedge x_3^{(1)} \wedge x_4^{(1)} + \sigma(\lambda)\,x_1^{(2)} \wedge x_2^{(2)} \wedge x_3^{(2)} \wedge x_4^{(2)}$ for some $\lambda \in F_p$.

Evaluating $\gamma$ on $e_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge e_3 \wedge e_4 \in \bigwedge^4 L$ and on $f_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge e_3 \wedge e_4 = (p^{k_1}\varpi e_1) \wedge e_2 \wedge e_3 \wedge e_4 \in \bigwedge^4 L$ gives
$$\operatorname{Tr}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\lambda) \in \mathbb{Z}_p, \qquad p^{k_1}\operatorname{Tr}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\lambda\varpi) \in \mathbb{Z}_p.$$
Since $\mathcal{O}_{F,p} = \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p\varpi$ has inverse different $\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}_{F,p}/\mathbb{Z}_p}^{-1} = \{u \in F_p : \operatorname{Tr}(u\mathcal{O}_{F,p}) \subset \mathbb{Z}_p\} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta_F}}\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$, these two conditions imply $p^{k_1}\lambda \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}_{F,p}/\mathbb{Z}_p}^{-1}$, so $\lambda = \frac{u}{p^{k_1}\sqrt{\Delta_F}}$ for some $u \in \mathcal{O}_{F,p}$. Meanwhile, $(x_a^{(1)} \wedge x_a^{(2)})(e_a, f_a) = -2 p^{k_a}\varpi$, so on the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-generator $\mathrm{vol}_L = e_1 \wedge f_1 \wedge \dots \wedge e_4 \wedge f_4$, the $8$-form $\gamma \wedge \gamma = 2\operatorname{Nm}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\lambda)\,\prod_{a=1}^4 (x_a^{(1)} \wedge x_a^{(2)})$ evaluates to
$$\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2 = \pm \operatorname{Nm}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\lambda) \prod_{a=1}^4 (2 p^{k_a}\varpi) = \pm \frac{\operatorname{Nm}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(u)}{p^{2k_1}(-\Delta_F)} \cdot p^{k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4}(4 D_p)^2.$$
Since $k_1 + k_2 + k_3 + k_4 \ge 4 k_1 \ge 2 k_1$, $\operatorname{Nm}(u) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, and $(4 D_p)^2 / \Delta_F \in \Delta_F \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ (both for odd $p$ where $D_p = \Delta_F$, and for $p = 2$ where $4 D_2 = \Delta_F$), we obtain $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2 \in \Delta_F \mathbb{Z}_p$ for every $p \mid \Delta_F$. Since $\Delta_F$ is a fundamental discriminant ($\Delta_F = \prod_{p \mid \Delta_F} p^{v_p(\Delta_F)}$) and $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$ is already $q$-integral at unramified primes (indeed $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \eta^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$ for every $\eta \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$, as $e_I^* \wedge e_J^*$ pairs off-diagonally in pairs), we conclude $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2 \in \Delta_F \mathbb{Z}$. $\square$

### 4.3 Proof of Theorem 2(b): Global operator $T_\eta$ and local $\zeta$-twist proof of $4\Delta_F \mid \int_X y^2$

**Step 1 (Global integrality of $\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2$ and $\frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2$ for arbitrary mixed $\eta = w + y \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$).** Let $\eta = w + y \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z}) = \bigwedge^4 \Lambda^\vee$. Because $\int_X \colon \bigwedge^8 \Lambda^\vee \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}$ is unimodular, there is a unique **integral** linear endomorphism $T_\eta \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\bigwedge^2 \Lambda) \cong M_{28}(\mathbb{Z})$ defined by
$$\bigl\langle \alpha,\ T_\eta(u \wedge v) \bigr\rangle = \int_X \alpha \wedge (\iota_u \eta) \wedge (\iota_v \eta) \qquad \forall \alpha \in \bigwedge^2 \Lambda^\vee,\ \forall u, v \in \Lambda. \tag{4.2}$$
Let us compute $T_\eta \otimes \mathbb{R}$ on the $16$-dimensional mixed subspace $V_1 \otimes V_2 \subset \bigwedge^2 \Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}$. Choose oriented $\mathbb{R}$-bases $(v_{1,1}, \dots, v_{1,4})$ of $V_1$ and $(v_{2,1}, \dots, v_{2,4})$ of $V_2$ with dual bases $(e^1, \dots, e^4)$ and $(f^1, \dots, f^4)$ such that $\Theta_1 = e^1 \wedge e^2 + e^3 \wedge e^4$, $\Theta_2 = f^1 \wedge f^2 + f^3 \wedge f^4$, and $\mathrm{vol}_X = e^{1234} \wedge f^{1234}$ ($\int_X \mathrm{vol}_X = 1$, so $V_0 = 4$). Write $w = c_1 e^{1234} + c_2 f^{1234}$ ($c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb{R}$, $\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 = c_1 c_2$) and $y = \mu\,\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2$ ($\frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2 = \mu^2$). For $u = v_{1,1} \in V_1$ and $v = v_{2,1} \in V_2$:
- $\iota_u w = c_1 e^{234}$, $\iota_v w = c_2 f^{234}$, while $\iota_u y = \mu\,e^2 \wedge \Theta_2$ and $\iota_v y = \mu\,\Theta_1 \wedge f^2$;
- the cross wedges $(\iota_u w) \wedge (\iota_v y)$ and $(\iota_u y) \wedge (\iota_v w)$ have degree $5$ on $V_1^\vee$ or $V_2^\vee$ ($\dim V_k = 4$) and therefore vanish identically;
- $(\iota_u w) \wedge (\iota_v w) = c_1 c_2\,e^{234} \wedge f^{234}$, and $(\iota_u y) \wedge (\iota_v y) = \mu^2 (e^2 \wedge \Theta_1) \wedge (\Theta_2 \wedge f^2) = \mu^2 e^{234} \wedge f^{234}$;
- wedging on the left with $\alpha \in \bigwedge^2 \Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^\vee$ selects the $e^1 \wedge f^1$ coefficient of $\alpha$ with sign $(e^1 \wedge f^1) \wedge (e^{234} \wedge f^{234}) = -e^{1234} \wedge f^{1234}$.

By $\operatorname{Sp}(V_1, \Theta_1) \times \operatorname{Sp}(V_2, \Theta_2)$-transitivity on non-zero vectors, $T_\eta|_{V_1 \otimes V_2} = -\bigl(c_1 c_2 + \mu^2\bigr)\,\mathrm{id}_{V_1 \otimes V_2} = -\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 + \frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2\bigr)\,\mathrm{id}_{V_1 \otimes V_2}$. Since $T_\eta \in M_{28}(\mathbb{Z})$, its eigenvalue $\lambda_T := -\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 + \frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2\bigr)$ is an algebraic integer; since $\lambda_T \in \mathbb{Q}$ (as $w, y \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Q})$), we have $\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 + \frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$. Meanwhile, since $\eta \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$ and $w \perp y$, $\frac{1}{2}\int_X \eta^2 = \frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 + \frac{1}{2}\int_X y^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$. Subtracting gives $\frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$ (i.e., $4 \mid \int_X y^2$) and $\frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 2 (Local proof of $4\Delta_F \mid \int_X y^2$ when $w = 0$).** Suppose now that $y = \mu\,\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2 \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$. Set $\nu^2 := \frac{1}{4}\int_X y^2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$; we must show $v_p(\nu^2) \ge v_p(\Delta_F) = \delta$ at every ramified prime $p \mid \Delta_F$. Choose the basis $(e_1, f_1, \dots, e_4, f_4)$ of $L = \Lambda \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ in Lemma 4.2, and write $\Theta_1 = \sum_{1 \le a < b \le 4} \tau_{ab}\,x_a^{(1)} \wedge x_b^{(1)}$ and $\Theta_2 = \sum_{a < b} \sigma(\tau_{ab})\,x_a^{(2)} \wedge x_b^{(2)}$ ($\tau_{ab} \in F_p$). With $\mathrm{Pf}(\tau) = \tau_{12}\tau_{34} - \tau_{13}\tau_{24} + \tau_{14}\tau_{23}$ and $N := k_1 + k_2 + k_3 + k_4$, evaluating $\Theta_1^2 \wedge \Theta_2^2 = 4\operatorname{Nm}(\mathrm{Pf}(\tau))\,\prod_{a=1}^4 (x_a^{(1)} \wedge x_a^{(2)})$ on $\mathrm{vol}_L$ gives
$$\nu^2 = \frac{1}{4}\mu^2 V_0 = \pm \mu^2 \operatorname{Nm}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\mathrm{Pf}(\tau))\,p^N (4 D_p)^2 \implies v_p(\nu^2) = v_\pi\bigl(\mu\,\mathrm{Pf}(\tau)\bigr) + N + 2\delta, \tag{4.3}$$
so $v_p(\nu^2) \ge \delta$ is equivalent to $v_\pi(\mu\,\mathrm{Pf}(\tau)) \ge -N - \delta$. We extract three integrality constraints from $y(\bigwedge^4 L) \subset \mathbb{Z}_p$:

1. *Pair $4$-tuples:* On $e_a \wedge f_a \wedge e_b \wedge f_b$ ($a < b$), $y$ evaluates to $-\mu\operatorname{Nm}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\tau_{ab})\,p^{k_a+k_b}(4 D_p) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$. Taking $v_p$ yields
   $$m_{ab} := v_p(\mu) + v_\pi(\tau_{ab}) + (k_a + k_b) + \delta \ge 0 \qquad (1 \le a < b \le 4). \tag{4.4}$$
2. *Eigenvector projection on partitions:* Let $\{a, b\} \sqcup \{c, d\} = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ be any partition into two pairs, ordered so that $k_a + k_b \le k_c + k_d$. Notice that $p^{k_a}\varpi e_a + f_a \in L \oplus \varpi L$ satisfies $x_a^{(2)}(p^{k_a}\varpi e_a + f_a) = p^{k_a}\varpi - p^{k_a}\varpi = 0$ and $x_a^{(1)}(p^{k_a}\varpi e_a + f_a) = 2 p^{k_a}\varpi$, so it is annihilated by $x_a^{(2)}$ and hence by $\Theta_2$. Evaluating $y$ on $(p^{k_a}\varpi e_a + f_a) \wedge (p^{k_b}\varpi e_b + f_b) \wedge e_c \wedge e_d \in \bigwedge^4 L \oplus \varpi\bigwedge^4 L$ therefore kills the other two partitions and yields
   $$4 p^{k_a+k_b} D_p \cdot \mu\tau_{ab}\sigma(\tau_{cd}) \in \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p\varpi = \mathcal{O}_{F,p} \implies v_\pi\bigl(\mu\tau_{ab}\sigma(\tau_{cd})\bigr) \ge -2(k_a + k_b) - 2\delta.$$
   Since $v_\pi(\mu\tau_{ab}\sigma(\tau_{cd})) = 2 v_p(\mu) + v_\pi(\tau_{ab}) + v_\pi(\tau_{cd}) = -N - 2\delta + (m_{ab} + m_{cd})$, this strengthens $m_{ab} + m_{cd} \ge 0$ to
   $$m_{ab} + m_{cd} \ge N - 2(k_a + k_b) = (k_c + k_d) - (k_a + k_b) \ge 0. \tag{4.5}$$
3. *Transversal $4$-tuples:* Set $Q_1 := \mu\tau_{12}\sigma(\tau_{34})$, $Q_2 := -\mu\tau_{13}\sigma(\tau_{24})$, $Q_3 := \mu\tau_{14}\sigma(\tau_{23})$. On $(z_1 e_1) \wedge e_2 \wedge e_3 \wedge e_4$ ($z_1 \in R_1 = \mathbb{Z}_p + p^{k_1}\varpi\mathbb{Z}_p$), $y$ evaluates to $\operatorname{Tr}_{F_p/\mathbb{Q}_p}((Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3) z_1) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, which forces $Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 \in \pi^{-2k_1 - \delta}\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$; since $N \ge 4 k_1 \ge 2 k_1$, we have
   $$v_\pi(Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3) \ge -N - \delta. \tag{4.6}$$

Finally, let $\vartheta := \sigma(\pi)/\pi \in \mathcal{O}_{F,p}^\times$. By direct inspection across all three ramification types—odd $p$ ($\delta = 1, \vartheta = -1$), $p = 2$ with $d \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ ($\delta = 2, \pi = 1 + \sqrt{d}, \vartheta - 1 = -2\sqrt{d}/\pi$), and $p = 2$ with $d \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ ($\delta = 3, \pi = \sqrt{d}, \vartheta = -1$)—we have $v_\pi(\vartheta - 1) = \delta - 1$, $v_\pi(\vartheta^2 - 1) \ge \delta$, and $\sigma(\varepsilon) - \varepsilon \in \pi^\delta\mathcal{O}_{F,p}$ for every $\varepsilon \in \mathcal{O}_{F,p}^\times = (\mathbb{Z}_p[\pi])^\times$. Hence for any $x = \pi^r \varepsilon \in F_p^\times$ ($r = v_\pi(x)$) and any $r_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$, setting $\zeta := \vartheta^{r_0} \in \mathcal{O}_{F,p}^\times$ gives $\sigma(x) - \zeta x = \pi^r\bigl(\vartheta^r(\sigma(\varepsilon) - \varepsilon) + \varepsilon\vartheta^{r_0}(\vartheta^{r - r_0} - 1)\bigr)$, whence
$$v_\pi\bigl(\sigma(x) - \zeta x\bigr) \ge \begin{cases} v_\pi(x) + \delta & \text{if } v_\pi(x) \equiv r_0 \pmod 2, \\ v_\pi(x) + \delta - 1 & \text{if } v_\pi(x) \not\equiv r_0 \pmod 2. \end{cases} \tag{4.7}$$
Choose $r_0 := -v_p(\mu) - \lfloor N/2 \rfloor - \delta$ and $\zeta := \vartheta^{r_0}$, and write
$$(Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3) - \zeta\mu\,\mathrm{Pf}(\tau) = \mu\tau_{12}\bigl(\sigma(\tau_{34}) - \zeta\tau_{34}\bigr) - \mu\tau_{13}\bigl(\sigma(\tau_{24}) - \zeta\tau_{24}\bigr) + \mu\tau_{14}\bigl(\sigma(\tau_{23}) - \zeta\tau_{23}\bigr).$$
For each term $\mu\tau_{ab}(\sigma(\tau_{cd}) - \zeta\tau_{cd})$: if $m_{ab} + m_{cd} \ge 1$, then (4.7) gives $v_\pi \ge -N - 2\delta + (m_{ab} + m_{cd}) + (\delta - 1) \ge -N - \delta$; if $m_{ab} + m_{cd} = 0$, then (4.5) forces $k_a + k_b = k_c + k_d = N/2$ (so $N$ is even) and $m_{cd} = 0$, whence $v_\pi(\tau_{cd}) = -v_p(\mu) - N/2 - \delta = r_0$, and the even-parity branch of (4.7) again gives $v_\pi \ge -N - \delta$. Combining with (4.6) and $\zeta \in \mathcal{O}_{F,p}^\times$ yields $v_\pi(\mu\,\mathrm{Pf}(\tau)) \ge -N - \delta$, completing the proof that $4\Delta_F \mid \int_X y^2$. $\square$

### 4.4 Proof of Theorem 2(c): Global operator $S_{v_1, v_3}$

Suppose $\beta \ne 0$, $v_1 = \Phi = \Phi_1 + \Phi_2 \in H^2(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$ ($\Phi_k = \beta_k\Theta_k$), and $v_3 \in H^6(X, \mathbb{Z})$. By Frobenius normal form for skew-symmetric bilinear forms on $\Lambda \cong \mathbb{Z}^8$, $\Phi$ has positive elementary divisors $1 \le d_1 \mid d_2 \mid d_3 \mid d_4$ with $\frac{1}{24}\int_X \Phi^4 = \frac{1}{4}\int_X \Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2^2 = d_1 d_2 d_3 d_4 =: M_\Phi \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$. Substituting $\Theta_k = \beta_k^{-1}\Phi_k$ into $v_3$ in (1.3) gives
$$v_3 = -\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\sigma_2(\rho)\,\Phi_1 \wedge \Phi_2^2 + \sigma_1(\rho)\,\Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2\bigr), \qquad \rho := \frac{q}{\beta^2} \in F_{>0}, \qquad \alpha := M_\Phi \rho \in F_{>0}.$$
Define the **integral** endomorphism $S_{v_1, v_3} \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda) \cong M_8(\mathbb{Z})$ via unimodularity of $\int_X \colon \bigwedge^8 \Lambda^\vee \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}$:
$$\bigl\langle \xi,\ S_{v_1, v_3}(u) \bigr\rangle = \int_X \xi \wedge (\iota_u v_1) \wedge v_3 \qquad \forall \xi \in \Lambda^\vee,\ \forall u \in \Lambda. \tag{4.8}$$
For $u \in V_1$, we have $\iota_u v_1 = \iota_u \Phi_1 \in V_1^\vee$, so $(\iota_u \Phi_1) \wedge (\Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2) = 0$ (degree $5$ on $V_1$), while on $(V_1, \Phi_1)$ the $4$-form $\xi \wedge (\iota_u \Phi_1) \wedge \Phi_1$ equals $\langle \xi, u \rangle\,\frac{1}{2}\Phi_1^2$ for every $\xi \in V_1^\vee$. Integrating against $-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_2(\rho)\,\Phi_2^2$ gives
$$\bigl\langle \xi,\ S_{v_1, v_3}(u) \bigr\rangle = -\frac{1}{4}\sigma_2(\rho)\left(\int_X \Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2^2\right)\langle \xi, u \rangle = -\sigma_2(\alpha)\,\langle \xi, u \rangle \qquad (u \in V_1),$$
so $S_{v_1, v_3}|_{V_1} = -\sigma_2(\alpha)\,\mathrm{id}_{V_1}$, and by symmetry $S_{v_1, v_3}|_{V_2} = -\sigma_1(\alpha)\,\mathrm{id}_{V_2}$. Because $S_{v_1, v_3} \in M_8(\mathbb{Z})$, its eigenvalues $-\sigma_1(\alpha), -\sigma_2(\alpha)$ are algebraic integers, whence $\alpha \in F_{>0} \cap \overline{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathcal{O}_{F,+}$. Finally, $-2\int_X v_1 \wedge v_3 = (\sigma_1(\rho) + \sigma_2(\rho))\int_X \Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2^2 = 4\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)$. $\square$

### 4.5 Proof of Theorem 2(d) and split $\Delta_F = 1$ control

Let $v \in B(\Theta, q) \cap H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$ have degree-$4$ part $v_2 = w + y$, and set $A := \frac{1}{2}\int_X w^2 = \frac{1}{4} r^2 \operatorname{Nm}(q) V_0 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ and $B := \int_X y^2 = \mu^2 V_0 \in 4\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Theorem 2(b)). By (2.3) and Theorem 2(c),
$$\chi(v, v) = 4 A + B + \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \beta = 0, \\ 4\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) & \text{if } \beta \ne 0 \ (\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_{F,+}). \end{cases} \tag{4.9}$$
Notice first a fundamental ratio identity: if $\beta = 0$ and $A = B/4 \ge 1$, then $r^2 \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q) = \mu^2$, so $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q) = (|\mu|/|r|)^2 =: N_0^2$, $\mu = \pm N_0 r$, and $\bar{q}\beta = 0 = \mp N_0\bar{\beta}$, placing $v$ in **Stratum I** ($P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$).

We now verify the three sharp stratum bounds:

1. **Stratum I** ($\operatorname{Nm}(q) = N_0^2$, $v \in (P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}) \setminus \{0\}$): If $\beta \ne 0$, then $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_{F,+}$ satisfies $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) = \sigma_1(\alpha) + \sigma_2(\alpha) \ge 2\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}(\alpha)} \ge 2$, so $\chi(v, v) \ge 4\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) \ge 8$. If $\beta = 0$, then $r \ne 0$ and $\mu = \pm N_0 r \ne 0$, so $A \ge 1$ and $B \ge 4$, giving $\chi(v, v) = 4 A + B \ge 8$.

2. **Stratum II** ($\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) = r\mu$, disjoint from $P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$):
   - *Case $\beta = 0$:* Then $r\mu = 0$ with $(r, \mu) \ne (0, 0)$. If $\mu = 0$ ($r \ne 0$), then $v_2 = w \in \Gamma_4 \setminus \{0\}$, so $\Delta_F \mid A$ by Theorem 2(a) and $\chi(v, v) = 4 A \ge 4\Delta_F \ge 20$. If $r = 0$ ($\mu \ne 0$), then $w = 0$ and $v_2 = y \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$, so $4\Delta_F \mid B$ by Theorem 2(b) and $\chi(v, v) = B \ge 4\Delta_F \ge 20$.
   - *Case $\beta \ne 0$:* Since $F$ is a field ($\Delta_F > 1$), $r\mu = \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) \ne 0$, so $r \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$, $\mu \ne 0$, $A \ge 1$, and $B/4 \ge 1$. Using $M_\Phi = \frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Nm}(\beta)^2 V_0 = \frac{1}{4} r^2 \mu^2 V_0$, the norm of $\alpha = M_\Phi q/\beta^2 \in \mathcal{O}_{F,+}$ satisfies
     $$\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) = M_\Phi^2 \frac{\operatorname{Nm}(q)}{\operatorname{Nm}(\beta)^2} = \left(\frac{1}{4} r^2 \operatorname{Nm}(q) V_0\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}\mu^2 V_0\right) = A \cdot \frac{B}{4}. \tag{4.10}$$
     - If $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_{F,+} \setminus \mathbb{Z}$, write $\alpha = \frac{a + c\sqrt{\Delta_F}}{2}$ ($c \ne 0$); then $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)^2 = (\sigma_1(\alpha) - \sigma_2(\alpha))^2 + 4\operatorname{Nm}(\alpha) \ge \Delta_F + 4$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) \ge \lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil \ge 3$ and $\chi(v, v) \ge 4 + 4 + 4\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil = 8 + 4\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil \ge 20$.
     - If $\alpha = m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, then $\rho = q/\beta^2 = m/M_\Phi \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ is rational, so (1.3) gives $v_3 = -\frac{m}{2 M_\Phi}(\Phi_1\Phi_2^2 + \Phi_1^2\Phi_2) = -\frac{m}{6 M_\Phi}\Phi^3 \in H^6(X, \mathbb{Z})$. In a Frobenius symplectic basis $e_1, \dots, e_8$ of $\Lambda$ where $\Phi = \sum_{j=1}^4 d_j\,e^{2j-1} \wedge e^{2j}$ ($1 \le d_1 \mid d_2 \mid d_3 \mid d_4$, $d_1 d_2 d_3 d_4 = M_\Phi = r^2(B/4)$), one has $\frac{1}{6}\Phi^3 = \sum_{k=1}^4 \frac{M_\Phi}{d_k}\,\widehat{e^{2k-1} \wedge e^{2k}}$ on primitive generators of $H^6(X, \mathbb{Z})$. Hence $v_3 = -\sum_{k=1}^4 \frac{m}{d_k}\,\widehat{e^{2k-1} \wedge e^{2k}} \in H^6(X, \mathbb{Z})$ forces $d_4 \mid m$, so $r^2(B/4) = M_\Phi \mid d_4^4 \mid m^4$; in particular, every prime dividing $r$ divides $m$. Next, substituting $q_k\Theta_k^2 = \frac{m}{M_\Phi}\Phi_k^2$ and $\mu\,\Theta_1 \wedge \Theta_2 = \frac{\mu}{\operatorname{Nm}(\beta)}\Phi_1 \wedge \Phi_2 = \frac{1}{r}\Phi_1 \wedge \Phi_2$ into $v_2 \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$ yields $v_2 = -\frac{r m}{2 M_\Phi}(\Phi_1^2 + \Phi_2^2) + \frac{1}{r}\Phi_1 \wedge \Phi_2$. Since $\Phi = \Phi_1 + \Phi_2 \in H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$ satisfies $\frac{1}{2}\Phi^2 = \frac{1}{2}(\Phi_1^2 + \Phi_2^2) + \Phi_1 \wedge \Phi_2 \in H^4(X, \mathbb{Z})$ (Lemma 4.1) and $r \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$, the integral $4$-class
       $$\gamma := \frac{1}{2}\Phi^2 - r v_2 = \frac{M_\Phi + r^2 m}{2 M_\Phi}(\Phi_1^2 + \Phi_2^2) = \frac{B/4 + m}{2(B/4)}(\Phi_1^2 + \Phi_2^2)$$
       lies in $\Gamma_4 \setminus \{0\}$. Using $\int_X \Phi_1^2 \wedge \Phi_2^2 = 4 M_\Phi = 4 r^2(B/4)$ and $A(B/4) = m^2$, its half self-intersection is
       $$\frac{1}{2}\int_X \gamma^2 = \left(\frac{B/4 + m}{2(B/4)}\right)^2 \cdot 4 r^2(B/4) = r^2\frac{(B/4 + m)^2}{B/4} = r^2\bigl(A + B/4 + 2m\bigr),$$
       so Theorem 2(a) forces $\Delta_F \mid r^2(A + B/4 + 2m)$. Write $g_0 := \gcd(A, B/4)$, $A = g_0 u^2$, $B/4 = g_0 v^2$, and $m = g_0 u v$ with $\gcd(u, v) = 1$, so $\Delta_F \mid r^2 g_0 (u + v)^2$. Every odd prime $p \mid \Delta_F$ satisfies $p \parallel \Delta_F$, and since every prime factor of $r$ or $g_0$ divides $m$, either $p \mid m$ or $p \mid (u + v)$. Let $P_1$ be the product of the odd prime factors of $\Delta_F$ dividing $m$ and $P_2$ the product of the remaining odd prime factors of $\Delta_F$; then $P_1 \mid m$ ($P_1 \le m$), $P_2 \mid (u + v)$ ($P_2 \le u + v \le m + 1$), and $2^{v_2(\Delta_F)} \le 8$ for every fundamental discriminant $\Delta_F$:
       - For $m = 1$, $r = \pm 1$ and $A = B/4 = 1$, giving $\Delta_F \mid 4$, impossible since $\Delta_F \ge 5$.
       - For $m = 2$, $P_1 \mid 2$ odd gives $P_1 = 1$, and $P_2 \mid (u + v) \in \{2, 3\}$ odd gives $P_2 \in \{1, 3\}$, so $\Delta_F \le 8 \cdot 1 \cdot 3 = 24$; hence $8 + 4\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil \le 8 + 4\lceil\sqrt{28}\rceil = 32 \le 4(A + B/4 + 2m) = \chi(v, v)$.
       - For $m \ge 3$, $\Delta_F \le 8 P_1 P_2 \le 8 m(m + 1)$, so $\Delta_F + 4 \le 8 m^2 + 8m + 4 \le (3m + 1)^2$ (as $(3m + 1)^2 - (8m^2 + 8m + 4) = (m - 3)(m + 1) \ge 0$), whence $\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil \le 3m + 1$ and $8 + 4\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil \le 12 m + 12 \le 16 m \le 4(A + B/4 + 2m) = \chi(v, v)$.

   Thus $\chi(v, v) \ge \min\bigl(4\Delta_F,\ 8 + 4\lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil\bigr) \ge 20$ across all of Stratum II.

3. **Stratum III** ($\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) \ne r\mu$, outside $P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$):
   - *Case $\beta = 0$:* Since $\operatorname{Nm}(\beta) = 0 \ne r\mu$, both $r \ne 0$ and $\mu \ne 0$, so $A \ge 1$ and $B/4 \ge 1$. By our ratio observation above, $(A, B/4) = (1, 1)$ would force $v \in P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$ (Stratum I); hence $A + B/4 \ge 3$ and $\chi(v, v) = 4(A + B/4) \ge 12$.
   - *Case $\beta \ne 0$ and $(r, \mu) \ne (0, 0)$:* At least one of $A, B/4$ is $\ge 1$, while $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) \ge 2$, so $\chi(v, v) \ge 4 + 8 = 12$.
   - *Case $\beta \ne 0$ and $(r, \mu) = (0, 0)$:* If $\alpha = m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, then $q = \frac{m}{M_\Phi}\beta^2$, which has $\operatorname{Nm}(q) = \bigl(\frac{m}{M_\Phi}|\operatorname{Nm}(\beta)|\bigr)^2 =: N_0^2$ and $\bar{q}\beta = \frac{m}{M_\Phi}\operatorname{Nm}(\beta)\bar{\beta} = \pm N_0\bar{\beta}$ together with $\mu = 0 = \mp N_0 \cdot 0$, placing $v$ in $P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$ (Stratum I). Hence on Stratum III we must have $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_{F,+} \setminus \mathbb{Z}$, forcing $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \ge \lceil\sqrt{\Delta_F + 4}\rceil \ge 3$ and $\chi(v, v) = 4\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) \ge 12$.

**Integral Chern-character sharpness witnesses (`[D1]`–`[D3]` in `verify.py`):** Let $S$ be a principally polarized Hilbert–Blumenthal abelian surface with $\mathcal{O}_F$-multiplication at $\Delta_F = 5$ ($H_1(S, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathcal{O}_F \oplus \mathcal{O}_F^*$), and set $X = S_1 \times S_2$ ($S_1 = S_2 = S$) with $\Theta = \Theta_{S_1} + \Theta_{S_2}$ ($V_0 = 4$). Direct evaluation in `verify.py` certifies integral cohomology classes $v \in B(\Theta, q) \cap H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$ (demonstrating sharpness of the character bounds in Theorem 2(d), without claiming coherent-sheaf realizations for `[D1]`–`[D3]`) for:
- **Stratum I ($\chi = 8$):** $q = 1$, $(r, \beta, \mu) = (1, 0, -1) \implies \chi(v, v) = 8$;
- **Stratum II ($\chi = 20$):** $q = 5 + 2\sqrt{5} \in F_{>0}$ ($\operatorname{Nm}(q) = 5$), $(r, \beta, \mu) = (1, 0, 0) \implies \chi(v, v) = 20$;
- **Stratum III ($\chi = 12$):** $q = \frac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{2} \in F_{>0}$ ($\alpha = q$, $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) = 3$), $(r, \beta, \mu) = (0, -1, 0) \implies \chi(v, v) = 12$. $\square$

**Remark 4.3 (Why field ramification $\Delta_F > 1$ is indispensable: the split $\Delta_F = 1$ positive control).** Replace the real quadratic field $F$ by the split étale algebra $\mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}$ ($\Delta_F = 1$), so $X = S_1 \times S_2$ is a product of two principally polarized abelian surfaces $(S_k, \Theta_{S_k})$ without RM, with $\Theta_k = \Theta_{S_k}$ and $q = (1, 1)$. Then the Remark class $v = \frac{1}{2}(\alpha + \tilde{\alpha}) = (1 - \frac{1}{2}\Theta_{S_1}^2) \boxtimes (1 - \frac{1}{2}\Theta_{S_2}^2) = (1 - [\mathrm{pt}_{S_1}]) \boxtimes (1 - [\mathrm{pt}_{S_2}])$ **is integral** and is realized by the genuine simple torsion-free sheaf $F := \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pt}_1, S_1} \boxtimes \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pt}_2, S_2}$. By Künneth from $\operatorname{ext}^\bullet(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pt}}, \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pt}}) = (1, 4, 1)$ on an abelian surface ($\chi = -2$), $F$ has $\operatorname{ext}^0 = 1$, $\operatorname{ext}^1 = 8 = \operatorname{rk} c^1_F$, $\operatorname{ext}^2 = 1 + 16 + 1 = 18 = \operatorname{rk} c^2_F$, and $\chi(F, F) = (-2)(-2) = +4$, so $F$ is **fully semiregular** on $S_1 \times S_2$ and attains exact equality $4 - 2 + 16 = 18$ in (1.1). Thus ramification $\Delta_F \ge 5$ in Theorem 2(a)–(b) is the exact arithmetic obstruction preventing any analogous sheaf on an RM fourfold.

## 5. Exclusion of semiregular RM secant sheaves and scope (Proof of Theorem 3)

### 5.1 Proof of Theorem 3

**(a)** Let $L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ be an algebraic line bundle (so $c_1(L) \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{C}) \cap H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$) and let $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ be a perfect complex satisfying $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$ and $\operatorname{ext}^0(E, E) = 1$, with $\operatorname{ch}(E) = e^{c_1(L)} \wedge v$, $v \in B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\}$ (noting that for any nonzero coherent sheaf $E$, $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$ holds automatically and its Hilbert polynomial $P_H(E, m) = \int_X \operatorname{ch}(E) e^{m H}$ with respect to an ample divisor $H$ is strictly positive for $m \gg 0$, forcing $\operatorname{ch}(E) \ne 0$). Applying Lemma 4.1 to the algebraic twist $E \otimes L^{-1} \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ yields $v = \operatorname{ch}(E \otimes L^{-1}) \in B(\Theta, q) \cap H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{0\}$. Tensoring by $L$ in $D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ induces a graded algebra isomorphism $\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet(E \otimes L^{-1}, E \otimes L^{-1}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Ext}^\bullet(E, E)$, preserving $\operatorname{ext}^i(E, E)$ for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\chi(E, E) = \chi(v, v)$, while Theorem 1(a) applied to $\xi_0 = c_1(L) \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{C})$ gives $(\operatorname{rk} c^1_E, \operatorname{rk} c^2_E) = (\operatorname{rk} c^1_v, \operatorname{rk} c^2_v)$. Because $\omega_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X$ on the abelian fourfold $X$, Serre duality gives $\operatorname{ext}^j(E, E) = \operatorname{ext}^{4-j}(E, E)$ for all $j \in \mathbb{Z}$; hence $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$ implies $\operatorname{Ext}^{>4}(E, E) = 0$, and the Euler characteristic reduces to $\chi(E, E) = 2\operatorname{ext}^0(E, E) - 2\operatorname{ext}^1(E, E) + \operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) = 2 - 2\operatorname{ext}^1(E, E) + \operatorname{ext}^2(E, E)$. By the proof of [7, Theorem B], $\sigma_E \circ \operatorname{ob}^1_E = c^1_E$ gives $\operatorname{ext}^1(E, E) \ge \operatorname{rk} c^1_E = 8$, so $\operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) = \chi(v, v) - 2 + 2\operatorname{ext}^1(E, E) \ge \chi(v, v) + 14$. By [7, Theorem A], $\sigma_E|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}$ is the transpose of $\operatorname{ob}^2_E$, so $\dim\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_E = \operatorname{rk}\sigma_E = \operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) - \dim\ker\sigma_E$. Since $\sigma_E \circ \operatorname{ob}^2_E = c^2_E$, we have $\operatorname{rk} c^2_E = \dim\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_E - \dim(\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_E \cap \ker\sigma_E)$, yielding the exact obstruction-excess identity
$$\operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) - \operatorname{rk} c^2_E = \dim\ker\bigl(\sigma_E|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}\bigr) + \dim\bigl(\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_E \cap \ker\sigma_E\bigr) \ge \chi(v, v) + 14 - \operatorname{rk} c^2_v$$
of (1.5). Since $\dim(\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_E \cap \ker\sigma_E) \le \dim\ker\sigma_E$ and simultaneously $\operatorname{rk}\sigma_E \le \dim H\Omega^{-2}(X) = 28$, we obtain
$$\dim\ker\bigl(\sigma_E|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E)}\bigr) \ge \max\!\left(\left\lceil \frac{\chi(v, v) + 14 - \operatorname{rk} c^2_v}{2} \right\rceil,\ \chi(v, v) - 14\right). \tag{5.1}$$
By Theorem 1(b) and Theorem 2(d), $\chi(v, v) + 14 - \operatorname{rk} c^2_v$ is at least $8 + 14 - 12 = 10$ on Stratum I, $20 + 14 - 18 = 16$ on Stratum II, and $12 + 14 - 20 = 6$ on Stratum III. Thus $\dim\ker(\sigma_E|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}) \ge 5$ on Stratum I, $\ge 8$ on Stratum II, and $\ge 3$ on Stratum III; in particular, $\sigma_E$ is never injective on $\operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E)$.

**(b)** For a $G$-equivariant perfect complex $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}, G}(X)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}_G(E, E) = 0$ and $\operatorname{ext}^0_G(E, E) = 1$ (where $\operatorname{Ext}^i_G(E, E) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^i(E, E)^G = 0$ for $i < 0$ holds automatically whenever $E$ is a $G$-equivariant coherent sheaf), equivariant Serre duality on $X$ again eliminates negative- and above-top-degree terms, so [7, Theorem B$_G$] requires $\chi(v, v)/|G| + 14 \le \operatorname{rk} c^2_v$ for $G$-semiregularity. On Stratum I ($\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 12$), the left-hand side exceeds $14 > 12$ for every $|G| \ge 1$. On Stratum II ($\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 18$, $\chi(v, v) \ge 20$), $20/|G| + 14 \le 18$ fails for $|G| \le 4$. On Stratum III ($\operatorname{rk} c^2_v = 20$, $\chi(v, v) \ge 12$), $12/|G| + 14 \le 20$ fails for $|G| = 1$.

**(c)** In [2, Example 11.2.7], $(X, \Theta)$ is a principally polarized genus-four RM Jacobian ($\frac{1}{24}\int_X \Theta^4 = 1$, so $V_0 = 4$), $q, N \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, and $f \in \mathcal{O}_F^\times$ satisfies $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f) = +1$ and $f^2 \ne 1$. Markman's simple coherent sheaf $E'$ has $\operatorname{ch}(E') = N\bigl(f^2 \cdot \Theta - \frac{q}{6} f^{-2} \cdot \Theta^3\bigr)$, which corresponds to $(r, \beta, \mu) = (0, N f^2, 0) \in B(\Theta, q)$. Since $\operatorname{Nm}(\beta) = N^2 \ne 0 = r\mu$ and $f^2 \notin \mathbb{Q}$ ($f \notin \{\pm 1\}$ in a real quadratic field), $\operatorname{ch}(E')$ lies in Stratum III ($(\operatorname{rk} c^1_{E'}, \operatorname{rk} c^2_{E'}) = (8, 20)$), and (2.3) gives
$$\chi(E', E') = 4 N^2 q \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f^4).$$
Because $f \in \mathcal{O}_F^\times \setminus \{\pm 1\}$ has $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f) = +1$ and real embeddings, $|\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f)| \ge 3$; hence $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f^2) = \operatorname{Tr}(f)^2 - 2 \ge 7$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(f^4) = \operatorname{Tr}(f^2)^2 - 2 \ge 47$ (attained at $\Delta_F = 5$, $f = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$). Thus $\chi(E', E') \ge 188 N^2 q \ge 188$. By (5.1), $\dim\ker(\sigma_{E'}|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}) \ge \chi(E', E') - 14 \ge 174$ unconditionally (and if $E'$ satisfies restricted semiregularity $\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ob}^2_{E'} \cap \ker\sigma_{E'} = 0$, then (1.5) gives $\dim\ker(\sigma_{E'}|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2}) \ge 188 + 14 - 20 = 182$), while $G$-semiregularity requires $188/|G| + 14 \le 20$, i.e., $|G| \ge 32$.

Finally, let $F_1, F_2 \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ be perfect complexes. First suppose $F_1$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(F_1, F_1) = 0$, $\operatorname{ext}^0(F_1, F_1) = 1$, and $\operatorname{ch}(F_1) \in e^{c_1(L_1)}\bigl(B(\Theta^{(1)}, q^{(1)}) \setminus \{0\}\bigr)$ ($L_1 \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$), while $\operatorname{ch}(F_2) \ne 0$. Since $\operatorname{ch}(F_2) \ne 0$, $\mathrm{id}_{F_2^\vee} \ne 0$ in $\operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(X)}(F_2^\vee, F_2^\vee)$, and its degree-$0$ Atiyah–Chern trace $\sigma^{(0)}_{F_2^\vee}(\mathrm{id}_{F_2^\vee}) = \operatorname{ch}_H(F_2^\vee) \in H\Omega^0(X)$ is nonzero ([4; 7, §2 and §5]). By the Künneth decomposition $\operatorname{Ext}^2_{X \times X}(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee, F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee) \cong \bigoplus_{a+b=2} \operatorname{Ext}^a(F_1, F_1) \otimes \operatorname{Ext}^b(F_2^\vee, F_2^\vee)$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the $(2, 0)$ summand $\operatorname{Ext}^2(F_1, F_1) \otimes \operatorname{Hom}(F_2^\vee, F_2^\vee)$ injects into $\operatorname{Ext}^2(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee, F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)$, and $\sigma_{F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee}$ restricts on it to $\sigma_{F_1} \boxtimes \sigma^{(0)}_{F_2^\vee}$. Choosing any nonzero $w \in \ker\bigl(\sigma_{F_1}|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2(F_1, F_1)}\bigr)$ from part (a) gives a nonzero class $w \boxtimes \mathrm{id}_{F_2^\vee}$ with $\sigma_{F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee}(w \boxtimes \mathrm{id}_{F_2^\vee}) = \sigma_{F_1}(w) \boxtimes \operatorname{ch}_H(F_2^\vee) = 0$. Conversely, if $F_2$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(F_2, F_2) = 0$, $\operatorname{ext}^0(F_2, F_2) = 1$, and $\operatorname{ch}(F_2) = e^{c_1(L_2)} \wedge v^{(2)}$ ($L_2 \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$, $v^{(2)} \in B(\Theta^{(2)}, q^{(2)}) \setminus \{0\}$) while $\operatorname{ch}(F_1) \ne 0$, derived duality $(-)^\vee = R\mathcal{H}om(-, \mathcal{O}_X)$ induces $\operatorname{Ext}^k(F_2^\vee, F_2^\vee) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^k(F_2, F_2)$ for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ and sends $(r, \beta, \mu) \mapsto (r, -\beta, \mu) \in B(\Theta^{(2)}, q^{(2)}) \setminus \{0\}$ with $\operatorname{ch}(F_2^\vee) = e^{c_1(L_2^{-1})} \wedge (v^{(2)})^\vee$; part (a) therefore applies to $F_2^\vee$, producing $w' \in \ker(\sigma_{F_2^\vee}|_{\operatorname{Ext}^2(F_2^\vee, F_2^\vee)}) \setminus \{0\}$ and $\mathrm{id}_{F_1} \boxtimes w' \in \ker\sigma_{F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee} \setminus \{0\}$. Because Orlov's equivalence [3] $\Phi \colon D^b(X \times X) \xrightarrow{\sim} D^b(X \times \hat{X})$ intertwines $\sigma$ up to an isomorphism on $H\Omega^{-2}$ ([7, §3, Fourier–Mukai compatibility]), $\sigma_{\Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)}$ fails to be injective on $\operatorname{Ext}^2$ in both cases. $\square$

### 5.2 Scope boundaries and open regimes

1. **Restricted semiregularity and higher-order deformation ([2, Question 11.2.2; 9; 10]).** Restricted (weak) semiregularity asks only for injectivity of $\sigma_E$ on $\operatorname{im} \operatorname{ob}^2_E \subset \operatorname{Ext}^2(E, E)$ (equivalently, $\operatorname{rk} \operatorname{ob}^2_E = \operatorname{rk} c^2_E$). Two distinct phenomena already separate the strata of $B(\Theta, q)$:
   - On **Stratum I** ($P_{K_0} \cup P_{K_1}$, when $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(q) = N_0^2$), restricted semiregularity *is* realized on polarized fourfolds $(X, L)$: at $q = 15$, rStation 50 [9] constructs slope-stable rank-two Horrocks–Mumford pullback bundles $F$ with $\operatorname{ch}(F) \in P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})}$ ($\operatorname{rk} c^1_F = 8$, $\operatorname{rk} c^2_F = 12$) and eightfold Orlov products $\Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)$ satisfying $\ker \operatorname{ob}^2 = \ker c^2$. However, rStation 51 [10] proves that restricted semiregularity does *not* in general suffice for unobstructed deformation along Hodge loci, exhibiting first-order lifts obstructed at second order and answering [1, Question 11.4] negatively.
   - On the genuinely RM **Strata II and III**, [7, Corollary F′] rules out Brill–Noether product objects on genus-four Jacobians transverse to $\mathcal{J}_4$, while algebraic existence of restricted-semiregular sheaves in $e^{c_1(L)} B(\Theta, q)$ away from that case remains open. Theorem 3 shows that any simple sheaf (or $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}$-vanishing complex) satisfying restricted semiregularity must have $\dim\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{ob}^2_E) = \operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) - \operatorname{rk} c^2_E \ge 16$ on Stratum II, $\ge 6$ on Stratum III, and $\ge 182$ for Markman's Example 11.2.7 sheaves, lying entirely inside $\ker\sigma_E$—and by [10], transporting any such object across the Weil period domain would still require controlling higher-order obstructions beyond first-order restricted semiregularity.
2. **Complexes with negative self-extensions, non-simple sheaves, and Brauer twists.** For an arbitrary perfect complex $E \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$ without the hypothesis $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}(E, E) = 0$ (compare the self-pair eightfold complex in [9, §6], which is not a shift of a sheaf), Serre duality on $X$ reads $\chi(E, E) = 2\operatorname{ext}^0(E, E) - 2\operatorname{ext}^1(E, E) + \operatorname{ext}^2(E, E) + 2\sum_{i < 0}(-1)^i \operatorname{ext}^i(E, E)$; if $\sum_{i < 0}(-1)^i \operatorname{ext}^i(E, E) > 0$ (for example $\operatorname{ext}^{-2}(E, E) > 0$), Theorem 3 does not apply without bounding negative self-extensions. For a non-simple coherent sheaf (or $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}$-vanishing complex) $E$ with $\operatorname{ch}(E) \in e^{c_1(L)}(B(\Theta, q) \setminus \{0\})$ ($L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$), (1.1) and Theorem 2(d) require $\operatorname{ext}^0(E, E) \ge \frac{1}{2}(\chi(E, E) + 16 - \operatorname{rk} c^2_E)$, i.e., $\operatorname{ext}^0 \ge 4$ on Stratum III, $\operatorname{ext}^0 \ge 9$ on Stratum II, and $\operatorname{ext}^0 \ge 6$ on Stratum I (with direct sums $E = \bigoplus_i E_i$ further constrained by $\sum_i \operatorname{ext}^2(E_i, E_i) \le \dim H\Omega^{-2}(X) = 28$ as in [7, §7(a)]). Likewise, genuinely Brauer-twisted objects in $D^b(X, \alpha_{\mathrm{Br}})$ ($\alpha_{\mathrm{Br}} \ne 0 \in \operatorname{Br}(X)$) or fractional-rank twisted characters need not lie in $H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ under Lemma 4.1 and are not excluded here.
