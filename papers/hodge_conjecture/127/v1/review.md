# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive obstruction to constructing fully semiregular simple sheaves in Markman's real-multiplication secant spaces on abelian fourfolds. Its contribution is relevant to the supplied problem because these sheaves are proposed carriers for transporting Weil classes on abelian eightfolds. It does not prove a new case of the rational Hodge conjecture, and it does not exclude restricted semiregularity. The main results state these limitations sufficiently clearly.

The decisive advance over rStation 37 [7] is arithmetic. Positivity of the Euler pairing alone does not exclude the real-multiplication strata with contraction ranks $18$ and $20$: the earlier criterion permits Euler characteristics up to $4$ and $6$. The present paper proves integral-character lower bounds of $20$ and $12$, respectively, and therefore closes that gap. This is more than another computation of the earlier imaginary-quadratic obstruction.

The proof supplies the necessary ingredients. The exponential conjugation and two-factor exterior-algebra calculation explain the contraction ranks and the equation $\operatorname{Nm}(\beta)=r\mu$. Lemma 4.1 correctly obtains integral Chern characters on an abelian fourfold, including the degree-six component, rather than assuming that integral Chern classes suffice. The local lattice normal form does not require $\mathcal O_F$-stability. The codifferent argument, the integral operators $T_\eta$ and $S_{v_1,v_3}$, and the valuation argument for a pure mixed class provide the essential divisibility results. In particular, the treatment of ramification at $2$ and the separate integer-$\alpha$ case in Section 4.5 supply actual arguments for the universal statements; they are not inferred from bounded computations.

Combining these results with the adjointness and character criterion of [7] gives the asserted failure of full semiregularity. The hypothesis of vanishing negative self-extensions makes the Euler-characteristic reduction valid for the stated complexes. The kernel estimates and the transfer through external products and Fourier–Mukai equivalence follow with the stated assumptions. The split-product example usefully explains why the real quadratic field hypothesis matters.

For novelty and attribution, I compared the relevant portions of rStation 37 v4, 50 v1 and 51 v3, and checked Markman's real-multiplication paper [2], including Question 11.2.2 and Example 11.2.7, against the external literature on semiregularity, including [5]. The preceding results provide the categorical criterion, the secant construction, weak examples and higher-order limitations; they do not provide this general ramified-lattice exclusion. The manuscript credits those dependencies. The focused literature check did not identify a prior result subsuming its central arithmetic contribution.

I reproduced all 23 supplied exact-arithmetic checks, with zero failures. These include representative contraction ranks and twists, sampled lattices, all 255 index-two sublattices for each of discriminants $5$ and $8$, the bounded discriminant check, and the integral-character witnesses. These are useful consistency checks, not independent proofs for every lattice or evidence of coherent-sheaf realization of every tested character. Acceptance rests on the mathematical arguments as well as these checks.

The exposition is dense but follows a recognizable mathematical argument: the question, rank calculation, arithmetic obstruction and geometric consequence are identifiable, with explicit assumptions and reproducible supporting evidence. I recommend the following limited corrections and clarifications:

1. In Section 4.3, Step 1, a Darboux basis for the fixed forms does not also allow the independent normalization $\int_X e^{1234}f^{1234}=1$. Retain this integral as a positive factor throughout the coordinate computation. Both terms in the displayed eigenvalue acquire the same factor, giving the stated invariant expression. This is a normalization correction, not a change to the divisibility argument.
2. Relabel check `[D4]` as a character of Markman's form evaluated at $N=q=1$. Example 11.2.7 in [2] constructs a sheaf for a suitable integer multiple $N$; it does not establish that $N=1$ is available. The computation establishes the numerical value $188$ for that character, not a geometric sheaf attaining it. The lower bound in Theorem 3(c) remains valid and should be distinguished from geometric attainment.
3. Correct the description of [9]: its slope-stability assertion assumes that the polarization generates the Néron–Severi group. Its weak-semiregularity construction applies more generally, but stability on an arbitrary RM fourfold does not follow from that statement.
4. Describe the Section B samples as arbitrary-lattice tests including non-$\mathcal O_F$-stable examples. The generator also includes the original stable lattice, and does not certify that every sample is non-projective as an $\mathcal O_F$-module or intrinsically non-product. This affects the description of computational coverage, not the universal proof.

These qualifications do not undermine the central exclusion theorem or its significance. The general Hodge conjecture and the remaining restricted-semiregularity and twisted-object regimes remain unresolved.
