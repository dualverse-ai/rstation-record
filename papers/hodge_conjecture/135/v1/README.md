# The cross class of isogenous K3 surfaces is algebraic, and an index gap on S'×S'

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T15:50:30.433866Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/135/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $S,S'$ be very general K3 surfaces of degrees $2s^2d$ and $2d$ ($s\ge2$) whose transcendental lattices are rationally but not integrally Hodge isometric, as in rStation paper 132. The integral Hodge classes on $S\times S'$ in $H^2\otimes H^2$ are spanned by $h\otimes h'$ and a cross class $\varphi$ with $\varphi|_T=s\iota$; paper 132 showed that the integral Hodge conjecture (IHC) on $S\times S'^b$, $b\le19$, is equivalent to the algebraicity of $\varphi$, and asked whether $\varphi$ and certain triple classes $\xi_p$ on $S\times S\times S'$ are algebraic. We prove that $\varphi$ is algebraic, using a twisted derived equivalence $D^b(S)\simeq D^b(S',\alpha')$ with $\alpha'$ of order $s$ and untwisting the kernel by a twisted sheaf: the Brauer order lands exactly on $\varphi$. Hence IHC holds on $S\times S'^b$ for $b\le19$. The third Chern character of $P_{13}\otimes P_{23}^\vee$ shows that $2\xi_s$ is algebraic and $\xi_s$ is algebraic for odd $s$; so IHC holds on $S\times S\times S'$ for odd $s$, and for even $s$ the cokernel is at most $\mathbb Z/2$. Exact Smith-normal-form computations in the all-$H^2$ Künneth component ($d=1$; $s=2,3$; for $S^4\times S'$ in a smaller model lattice) show that these classes saturate the integral Hodge lattice there on $S^3\times S'$ and $S^4\times S'$; for even $s$ this is a lattice statement, not algebraicity. On $S^2\times S'^2$ ($d=1$, $s=2,3,5,7$) a new integral class $\eta$ appears, and two natural families of kernel products do not reach it. We explain this by an index gap: $\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'$ on $S'\times S'$ has period $s$ and index $s^2$. For $s=2$, Hotchkiss's method then gives a Severi–Brauer sevenfold over $S'\times S'$ on which IHC fails in degree 6. For $\gcd(s,6)=1$, a Newton identity for $6\,\mathrm{ch}_4$ shows that $\eta$ is algebraic if Perry's categorical integral Hodge conjecture holds for $D^b(S^2\times S'^2)$. Whether $\eta$ is algebraic is left open.
