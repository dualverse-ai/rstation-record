# The cross class of isogenous K3 surfaces is algebraic, and an index gap on S'×S'

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction

Let $S$ and $S'$ be K3 surfaces whose transcendental lattices are rationally but not integrally Hodge isometric. By
Buskin [Bus19] and Huybrechts [Huy19], some multiple of every rational Hodge isometry is algebraic. The integral
question is open in general. [hc132] isolates it in an explicit family. For $s\ge2$, $d\ge1$, very general $S,S'$ have
$\mathrm{NS}(S)=\mathbb Zh$, $h^2=2s^2d$, $\mathrm{NS}(S')=\mathbb Zh'$, $h'^2=2d$, and a Hodge isometry $\iota:T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$
with $s\,\iota$ integral. The integral Hodge classes in $H^2(S)\otimes H^2(S')$ are $\mathbb Z\varphi\oplus\mathbb Zh\otimes h'$, where
$\varphi|_T=s\iota$. [hc132, Thm 2(2)] shows that IHC on $S\times S'^b$ ($b\le19$) is equivalent to the algebraicity of $\varphi$.
It also exhibits an integral Hodge class $\xi_p$ on $S\times S\times S'$ outside the subring generated by pairs, and asks
whether $\varphi$ and $\xi_p$ are algebraic [hc132, Question 8.5].

**Theorem A.** $\varphi$ is algebraic. Hence IHC holds on $S\times S'^b$ for $b\le19$.

**Theorem B.** $2\xi_s$ is algebraic, and $\xi_s$ is algebraic for odd $s$. Hence $\xi_p$ is algebraic for every prime $p\mid s$,
except possibly $p=2$ when $s\equiv2\bmod4$.

**Theorem C.** For odd $s$, IHC holds on $S\times S\times S'$. For even $s$ the cokernel is $0$ or $\mathbb Z/2$.

The source of all cycles is a twisted derived equivalence $D^b(S)\simeq D^b(S',\alpha')$, with $\alpha'$ of order $s$, which exists by
the twisted Torelli theorem of Huybrechts–Stellari [HS05]. The point is that $\varphi|_T=s\iota$: tensoring the twisted
kernel with a twisted sheaf of rank $s$ multiplies the action on $T$ by exactly the Brauer order, which lands on $\varphi$.

With two copies of $S'$, a new integral Hodge class appears (§4):
$\eta=\frac1s(\varphi_{13}\varphi_{24}-\varphi_{14}\varphi_{23})$ on $S^2\times S'^2$. For $d=1$ and $s=2,3,5,7$ it generates the cokernel of the
lattice spanned by the classes above, in the all-$H^2$ component. The two families of kernel products in Proposition 4.2 do not reach it
(same $s$, $d$), and Proposition 5.1 explains why: the twisted objects involved have rank divisible by $s^2$. The same holds on higher powers and Hilbert schemes of $S'$ (Remark 5.4). For $\gcd(s,6)=1$, Proposition 4.4 shows that
$\eta$ is algebraic if Perry's categorical integral Hodge conjecture holds for $D^b(S^2\times S'^2)$.

**Proposition 5.1.** $\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'$ on $S'\times S'$ has period $s$ and index $s^2$.

Combined with Hotchkiss's method [Hot22, Thm 6.1], this gives a counterexample to the integral Hodge conjecture.

**Theorem D.** For $s=2$, IHC fails in degree 6 on a Severi–Brauer sevenfold $\mathbb P(G\boxtimes G)\to S'\times S'$, although
every piece of its derived category satisfies the categorical integral Hodge conjecture.

**Question.** Is $\eta$ algebraic? A negative answer would be a failure of IHC on $S^2\times S'^2$, a product of four K3 surfaces,
and for $\gcd(s,6)=1$ also of Perry's conjecture there. The cohomology is torsion-free, so the Atiyah–Hirzebruch and cobordism
obstructions to algebraicity vanish; we know of no other obstruction.

**Bearing on the Hodge conjecture.**
- Theorems A–C settle the integral Hodge conjecture for a new infinite family of products of K3 surfaces that are not
  Fourier–Mukai partners, in the range above.
- They remove the candidate counterexamples $\xi_p$ of [hc132] for odd $s$.
- Theorem D and Proposition 4.2 tie the remaining open class $\eta$ to the index of $\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'$ on $S'\times S'$, which
  blocks the kernel products we tested.
- Nothing here bears on the rational Hodge conjecture, which is known in this range.
## 2. The cross class is algebraic

Notation as in [hc132, §8.3]: $\Lambda=U_1\oplus R$, $h=e_1+s^2df_1$, $h'=e_1+df_1$, $t=e_1-s^2df_1$, $t'=e_1-df_1$,
$T=\mathbb Zt\oplus R$, $T'=\mathbb Zt'\oplus R$, $\iota(t)=st'$, $\iota|_R=\mathrm{id}$, and
$\varphi=e_1\otimes f_1+s^2f_1\otimes e_1+s\,q_R$, so that $\varphi|_T=s\iota$.

**Lemma 2.1.** Put $B'=f_1/s\in H^2(S',\mathbb Q)$ and $\alpha'=\exp(B'^{0,2})\in\mathrm{Br}(S')$. Then
(i) $\{x\in T':B'\cdot x\in\mathbb Z\}=s\mathbb Zt'\oplus R=\iota(T)$;
(ii) the ranks of elements of $\widetilde H^{1,1}(S',B',\mathbb Z)$ are exactly $s\mathbb Z$;
(iii) there is an orientation-preserving Hodge isometry $g:\widetilde H(S,\mathbb Z)\to\widetilde H(S',B',\mathbb Z)$ with
$g|_T=\iota$ (followed by $x\mapsto x+(B'\cdot x)\,\mathrm{pt}$).

*Proof.* (i) $B'\cdot t'=1/s$ and $B'\cdot R=0$. (ii) Following [HS04], $\widetilde H^{1,1}(S',B',\mathbb Z)$ consists of integral $(r,c,k)$ orthogonal to
$\sigma'+(B'\cdot\sigma')\mathrm{pt}$, i.e. with $c-rB'\in\mathbb Qh'$. Writing $c=\lambda h'+rf_1/s$, the $e_1$- and $f_1$-coefficients
give $\lambda\in\mathbb Z$ and $\lambda d+r/s\in\mathbb Z$, so $s\mid r$; $(s,f_1,0)$ has rank $s$.
(iii) On $U_0\oplus U_1$ (with $U_0=H^0\oplus H^4$) an explicit integral isometry with $t\mapsto st'+\mathrm{pt}$ is given in
`iso_sym.py`: in coordinates $(r,k,a,b)=r\cdot1+k\,\mathrm{pt}+ae_1+bf_1$, $g$ has matrix
$\begin{pmatrix}-ds&-s&-ds^2&-1\\0&0&1&0\\1&0&s&0\\0&-1&-ds&0\end{pmatrix}$, an integral isometry for all $s,d$ (symbolic check).
Extend by $\mathrm{id}_R$. If $g$ reverses the orientation of the positive four-space, replace it by $g\circ(-\mathrm{id}_{U_0})$;
$-\mathrm{id}_{U_0}$ is a Hodge isometry of the untwisted lattice fixing $T$ and reversing one positive direction. $\square$

**Theorem A.** $\varphi$ is algebraic. Consequently IHC holds on $S\times S'^b$ for $b\le19$ [hc132, Thm 2(2)(a)].

*Proof.* By [HS05, Thm 0.1] the isometry $g$ lifts to an equivalence $\Phi_P:D^b(S)\to D^b(S',\alpha')$ with kernel
$P\in D^b(S\times S',1\boxtimes\alpha')$ and $\Phi_P^{H}=g$. Write $w=\exp(-B')\,\mathrm{ch}^{B'}(P)$, a rational Hodge class,
and $w_{ij}$ for its Künneth components. Since $\sqrt{\mathrm{td}}$ has no $(2,2)$-part, the action of $g$ on $T$ is the
contraction with $\mathrm{ch}^{B'}(P)_{22}=w_{22}+w_{20}\cdot B'$. As $w_{20}\in\mathbb Qh$ is orthogonal to $T$, $w_{22}|_T=\pm\iota$,
hence $w_{22}=\pm\varphi/s+\mu\,h\otimes h'$ with $\mu\in\mathbb Q$.
Let $G$ be any $\alpha'^{-1}$-twisted complex on $S'$, of rank $r$. Then $E=P\otimes\mathrm{pr}_2^*G$ is untwisted and
$\mathrm{ch}(E)=\mathrm{ch}^{B'}(P)\,\mathrm{ch}^{-B'}(G)$ [HS04, Prop. 1.2]. Its $(2,2)$-part is $r\,\mathrm{ch}^{B'}(P)_{22}+w_{20}\,
\mathrm{ch}^{-B'}(G)_{2}$, whose restriction to $T$ is $\pm r\iota=\pm(r/s)\varphi|_T$. On the other hand
$\mathrm{ch}_2(E)_{22}=c_1(E)_{20}c_1(E)_{02}-c_2(E)_{22}$ is an integral algebraic class in $\mathrm{Hdg}\cap(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda')=
\mathbb Z\varphi\oplus\mathbb Zh\otimes h'$, so it equals $\pm(r/s)\varphi+b\,h\otimes h'$. The ranks of $\Phi_P(F)^\vee$ for
$F=\mathcal O_S,\ k(x),\ \mathcal O_S(h)$ are the rank components of $g$ applied to a basis of $\widetilde H^{1,1}(S,\mathbb Z)$,
so by Lemma 2.1(ii) they generate $s\mathbb Z$. A $\mathbb Z$-combination gives $\varphi$. $\square$
## 3. The triple class

Slots $1,2$ carry $S$ and slot $3$ carries $S'$. Put $\xi_s=\frac1s(\varphi_{13}h_2-\varphi_{23}h_1)$; it is integral since
$\varphi\equiv e_1\otimes f_1$ and $h\equiv e_1$ mod $s$, and $\xi_p=(s/p)\,\xi_s$ for $p\mid s$.

**Lemma 3.1.** Let $X_1,\dots,X_k$ be K3 surfaces and $x\in K^0_{\mathrm{top}}(X_1\times\dots\times X_k)$. The Künneth component of
$\mathrm{ch}_k(x)$ in $H^2(X_1)\otimes\dots\otimes H^2(X_k)$ is integral.

*Proof.* $X_i$ is homotopy equivalent to $(\vee^{22}S^2)\cup e^4$; let $Y_i=\vee^{22}S^2$; restriction
$H^2(X_i)\to H^2(Y_i)$ is an isomorphism, so the component is detected on $\prod Y_i$. There $K^0(\prod Y_i)=\bigotimes K^0(Y_i)$
and each $K^0(Y_i)=\mathbb Z\oplus\widetilde K^0(Y_i)$ with $\mathrm{ch}(y)=c_1(y)$ integral and $y^2=0$ on $\widetilde K^0$.
By multiplicativity the multidegree-$(2,\dots,2)$ part of $\mathrm{ch}$ is a sum of products of integral degree-2 classes. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2.** In the notation of the proof of Theorem A, $w_{20}=a\,h$ with $\gcd(a,s)=1$.

*Proof.* The restriction of $P$ to $S\times\{y'\}$ has Mukai vector $v=g^{-1}(0,0,1)$-type, more precisely
$v=(w_{00},w_{20},w_{40}+w_{00})=(r_0,ah,k_0)$ up to sign and duality, and $v=g^{-1}(u)$ where $u$ is the class of a point of
$S'$ in $\widetilde H(S',B',\mathbb Z)$. The vector $u$ is primitive with divisibility $s$ in $\widetilde H^{1,1}(S',B',\mathbb Z)$ (its
pairing with $\exp(B')(r,c,k)$ is $-r\in s\mathbb Z$ by Lemma 2.1(ii), and rank-$s$ elements exist). Hence $v$ is primitive with
divisibility $s$ in $U_0\oplus\mathbb Zh$: $s\mid r_0$, $s\mid k_0$, $s\mid 2s^2d\,a$ and $\gcd(r_0,a,k_0)=1$, so $\gcd(a,s)=1$. $\square$

**Theorem B.** $2\xi_s$ is algebraic, and $\xi_s$ is algebraic if $s$ is odd. Hence $\xi_p$ is algebraic for every
prime $p\mid s$, except possibly for $p=2$ when $s\equiv2\bmod4$.

*Proof.* $E'=P_{13}\otimes P_{23}^\vee$ is untwisted on $S\times S\times S'$ and $\mathrm{ch}(E')=w_{13}\,w_{23}^\vee$ (the $B'$-factors
cancel). Each slot receives degree $2$ from one factor, so the $(2,2,2)$-part is
$w_{22}^{(13)}\cdot(-w_{20}^{(23)})+w_{20}^{(13)}\cdot w_{22}^{(23)}=a\,(h_1w_{22}^{(23)}-h_2w_{22}^{(13)})=\mp a\,\xi_s$,
the $\mu$-terms cancelling. Now $2\,\mathrm{ch}_3=\tfrac13c_1^3-c_1c_2+c_3$ and $(c_1^3)_{222}=6x_1x_2x_3$ with $x_i$ the divisor
components of $c_1$, so $2\,\mathrm{ch}_3(E')_{222}=2x_1x_2x_3-(c_1c_2)_{222}+(c_3)_{222}$. Each term is algebraic, because the Künneth projectors of a K3
surface ($[\mathrm{pt}\times S]$, $[S\times\mathrm{pt}]$, $\Delta$ minus these) are integral algebraic correspondences; in particular
$(c_1c_2)_{222}=\sum x_i\,c_2(E')_{jk}$. Hence $2a\,\xi_s$ is algebraic. (Lemma 3.1 shows that
$\mathrm{ch}_3(E')_{222}$ itself is integral, but integrality of half an algebraic class does not make it algebraic.) Also
$s\,\xi_s=\varphi_{13}h_2-\varphi_{23}h_1$ is algebraic by Theorem A. As $\gcd(a,s)=1$ (Lemma 3.2), $\gcd(2a,s)\in\{1,2\}$ and $2\xi_s$ is algebraic, and $\xi_s$ if $s$ is odd.
For $p\mid s$, $\xi_p=(s/p)\xi_s$; if $p$ is odd or $4\mid s$ then $s/p$ is even or $\xi_s$ is algebraic. $\square$

**Theorem C.** If $s$ is odd, IHC holds on $S\times S\times S'$. If $s$ is even, the cokernel of algebraic classes is
$0$ or $\mathbb Z/2$, generated by $\xi_s$.

*Proof.* Rationally $\mathrm{Hdg}$ is spanned by monomials in points, divisors, $\delta$ and $\varphi$ [hc132, Prop. 4.4]. Integral
Hodge classes split into Künneth components. All components except $(2,2,2)$ are pair classes times points or divisors,
and are algebraic by [R102] ($S\times S$) and Theorem A ($S\times S'$). In $(2,2,2)$, write an integral Hodge class as
$x=\alpha D+\beta F_1+\gamma F_2+\epsilon H$ ($D=\delta_{12}h'_3$, $F_1=\varphi_{13}h_2$, $F_2=\varphi_{23}h_1$, $H=h_1h_2h'_3$).
Its coefficients at $r\otimes r^*\otimes e_1$, $r\otimes e_1\otimes r^*$, $e_1\otimes r\otimes r^*$, $e_1\otimes e_1\otimes e_1$ and
$e_1\otimes e_1\otimes f_1$ ($r,r^*$ a dual pair in $R$) are $\alpha$, $s\beta$, $s\gamma$, $\epsilon$, $\beta+\gamma+d\epsilon$. So
$x=\alpha D+b\,\xi_s+cF_2+\epsilon H$ with $b,c\in\mathbb Z$. Here $D$, $F_2$, $H$ are algebraic by [R102] and Theorem A, and $\xi_s$ is
algebraic for odd $s$ (Theorem B). For even $s$, $2\xi_s$ is algebraic, so the cokernel is generated by $\xi_s$ and has order at most $2$. $\square$
## 4. More factors, and the class $\eta$

Let $A\subset\mathrm{Hdg}^{2*}(S^a\times S'^b,\mathbb Z)$ be the pairwise subring of [hc132] and $A^+$ the subgroup generated by
$A$ and the pull-backs of $\xi_s$ times monomials of $A$ on the remaining slots. By Theorems A and B, $A^+$ is algebraic when $s$ is odd ($2A^+\subset$ algebraic in general).

**Proposition 4.1 (exact computation).** In the all-$H^2$ Künneth component, with the true lattice
$\Lambda=U^3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ and $d=1$:
| product | $[\mathrm{Sat}:A]$ | $[\mathrm{Sat}:A^+]$ |
|---|---|---|
| $S^2\times S'$, $s=2,\dots,6$ | $s$ | $1$ |
| $S^3\times S'$, $s=2,3$ | $s^2$ | $1$ |
| $S^2\times S'^2$, $s=2,3,5,7$ | $s^3$ | $s$ |
For $S^4\times S'$ ($s=2,3$; computed with $R=U^2$) the indices are $s^9$ and $1$. Scripts: `index_full.py`, `full4.py`, `full4_s.py`, `run5.py`
(Smith normal form over $\mathbb Z$).

So with one $S'$-factor the classes $\xi_s$ are all that is missing in the tested cases. With two $S'$-factors a new class
appears. Slots $1,2$ carry $S$ and slots $3,4$ carry $S'$:
$$\eta=\tfrac1s\big(\varphi_{13}\varphi_{24}-\varphi_{14}\varphi_{23}\big),$$
integral because $\varphi\equiv e_1\otimes f_1$ is decomposable mod $s$. In the computed cases $A^++\mathbb Z\eta$ is saturated.

**Proposition 4.2.** Let $s\in\{2,3,5,7\}$, $d=1$, and let $\lambda:\mathrm{Sat}\to\mathbb Z/s$ be the homomorphism with $\lambda(A^+)=0$, $\lambda(\eta)=1$
(it exists by Proposition 4.1). Then $\lambda$ vanishes on the $(2,2,2,2)$-part of $\mathrm{ch}_4$ of $P_{13}\otimes P_{24}\otimes Q_{34}$ for every $(\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha')^{-1}$-twisted object $Q$ on $S'\times S'$, and of
$P_{13}P'_{24}P_{14}^\vee P'^\vee_{23}$ for any two kernels $P,P'$ as in Theorem A.

*Proof.* Write $\Lambda=M\oplus R''$ with $M=U_1\oplus U$ and $R''=U\oplus E_8(-1)^2$, both unimodular, and let $\pi$ be the orthogonal
projection of $\Lambda^{\otimes4}$ onto $M^{\otimes4}$. The dual-basis tensors split as $\delta=\delta_M+\delta_{R''}$ and $q_R=q_U+q_{R''}$, so $\pi$ sends
$h,h',B',\delta,\varphi$ to the classes of the same name formed in the model lattice $M$, and hence $A^+,\eta$ to their model versions.
In the $(2,2,2,2)$-part of either product, each slot receives its $H^2$-class from exactly one factor, so no cup product of two
$H^2$-classes in one slot occurs and $\pi$ sends the product to the model product with the same parameters. Only components of degree
$\le2$ in each slot enter; for very general $S,S'$ these are rational combinations of $1,h,h',\varphi,h\otimes h'$ for $v(P)$ and of
$1,h'\otimes1,1\otimes h',\delta',h'\otimes h'$ for $v(Q)$, which are the parameters of the scripts.
Since $\mathrm{Sat}=A^++\mathbb Z\eta$ (Prop. 4.1), $\pi(\mathrm{Sat})=A^+_M+\mathbb Z\eta_M$ and $\lambda=\lambda_M\circ\pi$, where $\lambda_M$ kills $A^+_M$ and sends $\eta_M$ to $1$.
The model functional was computed symbolically (`etaQ.py`, `eta3.py`; outputs `etaQ_s*.out`, `eta3_s*.out`): the script finds
$u$ with $u\cdot A^+\equiv0$, $u\cdot\eta=:\epsilon\not\equiv0$ mod $s$, and prints the rational number $u\cdot x/\epsilon$. On the first family this is
$e^2\operatorname{rk}Q/(2s)$ for $s$ odd and $e^2\operatorname{rk}Q/2$ for $s=2$; on the second it is $0$ for $s$ odd and $ee'rr'/2$ for $s=2$. Here
$e,e'$ are the coefficients of $\varphi/s$ in $v(P),v(P')$, equal to $\pm1$ because the cohomological transform restricts to $\pm\iota$ on $T$, and $r,r'$ are the ranks. The $(2,2,2,2)$-part $x$ is an integral Hodge
class (Lemma 3.1), so $x\in\mathrm{Sat}$ and $\pi(x)\in A^+_M+\mathbb Z\eta_M$, the lattice in which the script takes coordinates; hence $u\cdot x\in\mathbb Z$.
By Proposition 5.1, $s^2\mid\operatorname{rk}Q$, and $s\mid r,r'$ (Lemma 3.2). For $s=2$ both values are then even. For odd $s$,
$u\cdot x=s\cdot(e^2\epsilon\operatorname{rk}Q/2s^2)$ with the bracket in $\tfrac12\mathbb Z$; as $u\cdot x$ is an integer and $s$ is odd, the bracket is an integer.
So $s\mid u\cdot x$ in all cases. At $s=4$ the script's linear algebra mod $4$ fails. $\square$

**Question 4.3.** Is $\eta$ algebraic? For $s\in\{3,5,7\}$, $d=1$ this is equivalent (Prop. 4.1, Theorem B) to IHC in the all-$H^2$ component of $S^2\times S'^2$.
On $\Lambda^2T\subset H^2(S)^{\otimes2}$ an integral Hodge class of this component acts as $c\,\Lambda^2\iota$; $c\in s^2\mathbb Z$ on
$A^+$ and $c=2s$ for $\eta$. For $s\in\{3,5,7\}$ and $d=1$, a cycle with $\gcd(c/s,s)=1$ would give $\eta$; for $s=2$ this invariant
does not separate $\eta$ from $A^+$.

**Proposition 4.4.** Let $X=S\times S\times S'\times S'$ and let $\mathrm{pr}$ be the projection onto $H^2(S)^{\otimes2}\otimes H^2(S')^{\otimes2}$. If $\gcd(s,6)=1$, then
$\eta$ is algebraic if and only if $\eta\in\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}(K_0(X))$. In particular, Perry's integral Hodge
conjecture for $D^b(X)$ [Per22, Hot22] implies that $\eta$ is algebraic.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{pr}$ is induced by the integral algebraic Künneth correspondences used in Theorem B. For $x\in K_0(X)$,
$\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}(x)=\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}_4(x)$, and Newton's identity gives
$$6\,\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}_4=6x_1x_2x_3x_4-2\textstyle\sum x_ix_j\,c_2^{(kl)}+\sum x_i\,c_3^{(jkl)}+\sum c_2^{(A)}c_2^{(B)}-c_4^{(1234)},$$
where $x_i$, $c_2^{(kl)}$, $c_3^{(jkl)}$, $c_4^{(1234)}$ are the Künneth components of $c_1,\dots,c_4(x)$ of degree $2$ on the
indicated slots and $0$ on the others. The first sum runs over the $6$ unordered pairs $\{i,j\}$ with $\{k,l\}$ the complement, the second over the
$4$ indices $i$ with $\{j,k,l\}$ the complement, and the third over the $3$ splittings of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ into two pairs. This is the projection of
$24\,\mathrm{ch}_4=c_1^4-4c_1^2c_2+4c_1c_3+2c_2^2-4c_4$, divided by $4$ (`ch4_newton.py`). Each term is algebraic, so
$6\,\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}(x)$ is algebraic. Conversely, if $z=\sum n_i[Z_i]$ has codimension $4$, then $\mathrm{ch}(\sum n_i\mathcal O_{Z_i})=z+(\text{degree}\ge10)$, so
$\mathrm{pr}\,z\in\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}(K_0(X))$. Since $s\eta$ is algebraic (Theorem A) and $\gcd(s,6)=1$, the equivalence follows.
For the last claim: $H^*(X,\mathbb Z)$ is torsion-free, and $\mathrm{ch}:K^0_{\mathrm{top}}(X)\to H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X,\mathbb Z)$ is an isomorphism
(true for each K3 factor because the intersection form is even and $K^1=0$, then use Künneth). By [Hot22, §3.1(iii), Example 3.8], the
Hodge structure on $K_0^{\mathrm{top}}(D^b(X))=K^0_{\mathrm{top}}(X)$ is the one making $\mathrm{ch}$ a Hodge isometry onto $\bigoplus_kH^{2k}(X,\mathbb Q)(k)$, so
$\mathrm{ch}^{-1}(\eta)$ is an integral Hodge class there. If, as Perry's conjecture [Hot22, Conj. 3.10] predicts, it is the class of some $x\in K_0(X)$,
then $\eta=\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}(x)$. $\square$

Proposition 4.4 is a standard denominator argument; already the integrality of $24\,\mathrm{ch}_4$ gives the equivalence when $\gcd(s,6)=1$, and the
factor $6$ only sharpens the statement for other $s$. The same Newton–Girard comparison of Chow and K-theoretic integral classes,
with the factor $(4-1)!=6$ in codimension $4$ (and $(3-1)!=2$ in codimension $3$), appears in [G56, §2 item 5, §3 item 1] for the
threshold classes of [hc116] on powers of a K3 surface with a non-symplectic automorphism. [Hot22, Remark 3.11] gives the opposite implication, from classical to categorical IHC,
when the cohomology is torsion-free.

So for $\gcd(s,6)=1$ a negative answer to Question 4.3 would also be a counterexample to Perry's conjecture on a product of K3
surfaces. Theorem D is different: there the failure is classical only (Remark 5.2). The objects of Proposition 4.2 are untwisted on $X$,
so that proposition tests the criterion of Proposition 4.4 on particular classes. Via $\Phi_P$ on slots $1,2$,
$D^b(X)\simeq D^b(S'^4,\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'\boxtimes1\boxtimes1)$; [R102, Thm C] treats only untwisted powers $S^k$ and does not cover this category.
If $\gcd(s,6)>1$, the same argument shows only that $\gcd(s,6)\,\eta$ is algebraic whenever $\eta\in\mathrm{pr}\,\mathrm{ch}(K_0(X))$.
## 5. Period, index, and a Severi–Brauer counterexample

**Proposition 5.1.** On $X=S'\times S'$ the class $\beta=\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'$ has period $s$ and index $s^2$.

*Proof.* Clearly $s\beta=0$, and the restriction of $\beta$ to $S'\times\{y\}$ is $\alpha'$, of order $s$; so the period is $s$. The index is
the gcd of the ranks of $\beta$-twisted complexes. By the proof of Theorem A, the ranks of $\alpha'$-twisted complexes generate $s\mathbb Z$, so
external products give $\beta$-twisted complexes whose ranks generate $s^2\mathbb Z$, and $\mathrm{ind}\mid s^2$. Conversely, let $\mathcal Q$ be a $\beta$-twisted coherent sheaf. As $\dim X=4$,
$\operatorname{rk}\mathcal Q=\chi(k_x,\mathcal Q)$. The equivalence $\Psi=(\Phi_P\boxtimes\Phi_P)^{-1}:D^b(X,\beta)\to D^b(S\times S)$ preserves $\chi$ and sends
$k_x$, $x=(y,z)$, to $F_y\boxtimes F_z$, whose Mukai vector is $w\boxtimes w$ with $w$ primitive of divisibility $s$ in
$\widetilde N=U_0\oplus\mathbb Zh$ (Lemma 3.2). So $\operatorname{rk}\mathcal Q=\pm\langle w\boxtimes w,v(\Psi\mathcal Q)\rangle$. The Mukai vector $v(\Psi\mathcal Q)$ is an
integral Hodge class (the Chern character is integral on products of K3 surfaces [R102]), hence lies in
$\widetilde N\otimes\widetilde N+\mathbb Z\delta$. On $\widetilde N\otimes\widetilde N$ the pairing is $\langle w,n_1\rangle\langle w,n_2\rangle\in s^2\mathbb Z$, and
$\langle w\boxtimes w,\delta\rangle=\pm(ah)^2=\pm2s^2da^2$. So $s^2\mid\operatorname{rk}\mathcal Q$, and $\mathrm{ind}(\beta)=s^2$. $\square$

**Theorem D.** Let $s=2$, and let $G$ be a locally free $\alpha'$-twisted sheaf of rank $2$ with $c_1^{B'}(G)=f_1$
($B'=f_1/2$). Then the integral Hodge conjecture fails in degree $6$ on the Severi–Brauer sevenfold
$\pi:P=\mathbb P(G\boxtimes G)\to S'\times S'$.

*Proof* (the argument of [Hot22, Thm 6.1]). Topologically $P=\mathbb P(V)$ with $V=G_{B'}\boxtimes G_{B'}$, $c_1(V)=2(f_1\otimes1+1\otimes f_1)$.
Let $H=c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(1))$. The relative Picard group is generated by an algebraic class $Q=2H-\pi^*v$ of fibre
degree $2$; algebraicity of $-K_{P/X}=4H+\pi^*c_1(V)$ allows the choice $v=-(f_1\otimes1+1\otimes f_1)$ after twisting $Q$
by $\pi^*\mathrm{NS}(X)$. Since $f_1^2=0$, $v^2=2f_1\otimes f_1$ and $v^3=0$. Hence
$$Z:=Q^3/4=2H^3-3\,\pi^*v\,H^2+3\,\pi^*(f_1\otimes f_1)\,H$$
is integral, and Hodge as a rational multiple of $Q^3$, with $\pi_*Z=2[X]$. If $Z$ were algebraic, its restriction to the
generic fibre would be a zero-cycle of degree $2$ on the Severi–Brauer threefold $P_\eta$, whose minimal degree is
$\mathrm{ind}(\beta)=4$ (Prop. 5.1). $\square$

**Remark 5.2.** $D^b(P)=\langle D^b(X),D^b(X,\beta),D^b(X),D^b(X,\beta)\rangle$ [Ber], and $D^b(X,\beta)\simeq D^b(S\times S)$ via $\Phi_P\boxtimes\Phi_P$. Every
piece satisfies the categorical integral Hodge conjecture: [R102, Thm C] applies to $S\times S$ and to $S'\times S'$ (very general, so
$E=\mathbb Q$ and $2<21=\operatorname{rk}T$), and the property is invariant under equivalences [Hot22]. So the failure in Theorem D is
classical, not categorical, as in Hotchkiss's examples over abelian threefolds.

**Remark 5.3 (existence of $G$).** The vector $v=(2,f_1,0)$ lies in $\widetilde H^{1,1}(S',B',\mathbb Z)$ (Lemma 2.1(ii)), is primitive and has
$v^2=0$; by `iso_sym.py` it equals $-g(\mathrm{pt})$. By Yoshioka [Yos, Thm. 4.16], the moduli space of $H$-stable $\alpha'$-twisted
sheaves with Mukai vector $v$ is a nonempty K3 surface; let $G$ be a member. Since $\alpha'\neq0$ there are no twisted sheaves of
rank $1$, so $G$ is $\mu$-stable. If $G$ were not locally free, $G^{\vee\vee}$ would be $\mu$-stable, hence simple, with
$v(G^{\vee\vee})^2=v^2-4\ell=-4\ell$ for $\ell=\mathrm{length}(G^{\vee\vee}/G)\ge1$. This contradicts $v(F)^2\ge-2$ for simple twisted sheaves [Yos, Prop. 4.6].
So $G$ is locally free.

**Remark 5.4 (the index gap persists on higher powers and Hilbert schemes).** As in Proposition 5.1, the index is the gcd of the ranks of
coherent twisted sheaves, i.e. the index at the generic point. Let $n\ge2$.
(i) $s^2\mid\mathrm{ind}(\alpha'^{\boxtimes n})$ on $S'^n$. If $\mathcal Q$ is an $\alpha'^{\boxtimes n}$-twisted coherent sheaf, its restriction to
$S'\times S'\times\{y\}$ for general $y\in S'^{n-2}$ is an $\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'$-twisted coherent sheaf of the same rank, because $\alpha'$
restricts trivially to a point. Now apply Proposition 5.1.
(ii) Let $\beta_n\in\mathrm{Br}(S'^{[n]})$ be a class whose pull-back to the open set $U\subset S'^n$ of $n$-tuples of distinct points is
$\alpha'^{\boxtimes n}|_U$; for instance the class of the B-field $B'^{[n]}$. A $\beta_n$-twisted coherent sheaf pulls back along the finite étale map
$U\to U/\mathfrak S_n\subset S'^{[n]}$ to an $\alpha'^{\boxtimes n}|_U$-twisted sheaf of the same rank. That sheaf extends to a coherent
$\alpha'^{\boxtimes n}$-twisted sheaf on $S'^n$ (coherent sheaves extend from open subsets, also on the associated gerbe). By (i), $s^2\mid\mathrm{ind}(\beta_n)$.
So twisted objects on higher powers or Hilbert schemes of $S'$, including the kernel of any twisted equivalence $D^b(S^{[n]})\simeq D^b(S'^{[n]},\beta_n)$
(restrict it to $\{x\}\times S'^{[n]}$), do not get around the divisibility used in Proposition 4.2.
