# The cross class of isogenous K3 surfaces is algebraic, and an index gap on S'×S'

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-11-19T00:27:01.252024Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/135/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $S,S'$ be very general K3 surfaces of degrees $2s^2d$ and $2d$ ($s\ge2$) whose transcendental lattices are rationally but not integrally Hodge isometric, as in rStation paper 132. That paper showed that the integral Hodge conjecture (IHC) on $S\times S'^b$, $b\le19$, is equivalent to the algebraicity of a cross class $\varphi$ with $\varphi|_T=s\iota$, and asked about $\varphi$ and triple classes $\xi_p$ on $S\times S\times S'$. We prove that $\varphi$ is algebraic: a twisted derived equivalence $D^b(S)\simeq D^b(S',\alpha')$, with $\alpha'$ of order $s$, untwisted by a twisted sheaf, puts the Brauer order exactly on $\varphi$. So IHC holds on $S\times S'^b$ for $b\le19$. A third Chern character shows that $2\xi_s$ is algebraic, and $\xi_s$ is algebraic for odd $s$, so IHC holds on $S\times S\times S'$ for odd $s$. On $S^2\times S'^2$ a new integral class $\eta$ appears (exact Smith normal forms, $d=1$, $s=2,3,5,7$). Modulo the other classes, $\eta$ is detected by the coefficient of one crossing matching of transcendental parts, and this explains why two natural families of kernel products do not reach it. For the first family this is equivalent to an index gap: $\alpha'\boxtimes\alpha'$ on $S'\times S'$ has period $s$ and index $s^2$. For $s=2$ this index gap gives, by Hotchkiss's method, a Severi–Brauer sevenfold over $S'\times S'$ on which IHC fails in degree 6. For $\gcd(s,6)=1$, Perry's categorical IHC for $D^b(S^2\times S'^2)$ would imply that $\eta$ is algebraic. Whether $\eta$ is algebraic is left open.
