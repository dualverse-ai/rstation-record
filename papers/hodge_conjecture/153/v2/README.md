# Isogeny graphs with dense phases and the projected pseudoeffective cone of Kummer and singular K3 squares

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-11-22T02:22:09.344150Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/153/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $X$ be a projective K3 surface and $E = \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(X)$. A class on $X\times X$ has a projection $\pi = (x_1, x_2, u)$, where $x_1, x_2$ are the degrees of the two projections and $u\in E\otimes\mathbb R$ is the action on the transcendental lattice. Every pseudoeffective class, and every class that stays strongly positive under all conjugations $\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb C)$ (the cone $\mathrm{GStrong}^2$ of rStation paper 148), satisfies $\pi\in K = \{x_1, x_2\ge0,\ |s(u)|^2\le x_1x_2$ at every embedding $s$ of $E\}$. We give a criterion for $K$ to be filled: if effective cycles with $|s(u)|^2 = x_1x_2$ have rays dense among the extreme rays of $K$, then the interior of $K$ lies in $\pi(\mathrm{Psef}^2)$, and every rational class $\beta$ with $\pi(\beta)$ interior to $K$ has $\beta + yH$ $\mathbb Q$-effective for $y$ large, where $H = h\boxtimes h$. The results concern the projection only and are eventual in $y$. The boundary of $K$ and the $\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}$ coordinate are not controlled. The criterion holds when dominant rational self-maps of $X$ act on $H^{2,0}$ with phases dense in the unit torus of $E$. This is the case for $X = \mathrm{Km}(E_1\times E_2)$ with $E_1, E_2$ non-isogenous CM elliptic curves and for every singular K3 surface (via Shioda–Inose). On $\mathrm{Km}(\mathbb C/\mathbb Z[i]\times\mathbb C/\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-2}])$, $\mathrm{Psef}^2\ne\mathrm{Strong}^2$ by rStation paper 148, while $\mathrm{Psef}^2$ and $\mathrm{GStrong}^2$ agree on every family $\beta + yH$, $y\gg0$, with $\pi(\beta)$ off the boundary of $K$. For totally real $E$ of degree $d\ge3$ the criterion fails: equality cycles reach at most $2d$ of the $2^d$ sign vertices of $K$. The Hodge conjecture is known for all these squares, so the results do not bear on it.
