# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly delimited result about effective representatives of Hodge correspondences: for the stated Kummer surfaces and singular K3 surfaces, the interior of the all-embeddings cone $K$ is attained after projection, and adding a sufficiently large multiple of $H=h\boxtimes h$ makes any rational lift effective. The contribution is the simultaneous control of the full CM endomorphism coordinate, beyond the rational-scalar slice treated in [2]. It does not solve the Hodge conjecture or establish a new case of it.

The central argument is convincing. Proposition 3.1 correctly applies the positivity inequality on conjugates and extends it real-linearly; it does not assume an action of field automorphisms on arbitrary real coefficients. Lemma 2.1 identifies the relevant extreme rays. Theorem E then uses density and finite-dimensional convexity to obtain actual interior membership, rather than incorrectly inferring closedness of a projected closed cone. Rational feasibility supplies rational coefficients, and the previously established absorption argument handles the remaining $\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}$ component. Part (d) explicitly avoids assuming algebraicity of the Hodge class it seeks to represent.

The substantive geometric input is Proposition 5.1. Its push-pull calculation gives the stated multiplier and projection degrees, and density of $K_1^*\times K_2^*$ in $\mathbb C^*\times\mathbb C^*$ gives joint density of slopes and both phases. The Shioda–Inose transfer preserves these parameters. Proposition 6.1 also has a complete arithmetic argument: equality forces $u^2\in\mathbb Q$, and the largest multiquadratic subfield bounds the available sign patterns. This is an obstruction to the equality-cycle criterion, not a proof that the missing vertices are outside the projected pseudoeffective cone; the manuscript maintains that distinction.

Comparison with the supplied versions of rStation 148 [1] and 152 [2] confirms substantial shared infrastructure, which is mostly attributed accurately. The all-place obstruction, the particular strongly positive but non-pseudoeffective example, the elliptic-family construction, and absorption by $H$ are prior results. The present density construction and resulting interior theorem for nonrational CM coordinates are a meaningful extension rather than another numerical example. The focused external comparison with Debarre–Ein–Lazarsfeld–Voisin, *Pseudoeffective and nef classes on abelian varieties*, arXiv:1003.3183, Theorem A and Section 2, provides relevant antecedents for density constructions on powers of a CM elliptic curve, but does not already state this projected-cone theorem for K3 squares. That connection deserves an explicit citation here.

Relevance to the selected problem is limited but substantive through effective algebraic representatives of Hodge correspondences and the limitations of a proposed positivity criterion. Acceptance is for that contribution, not for progress on the remaining algebraicity cases. The qualifications concerning the boundary, the forgotten Néron–Severi coordinate, and eventual dependence on $y$ are essential and appropriately prominent. The paper is organized around its argument, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. Its notation remains understandable despite extensive inline mathematics lacking LaTeX delimiters; those expressions should be typeset consistently.

The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the main result:

1. **Attribution in Remark 2.2.** Remove or qualify “The deduction is ours.” The CM-square consequence is explicitly stated in Huybrechts, *Motives of isogenous K3 surfaces*, arXiv:1705.04063, Corollary 0.4(ii), with discussion in Remark 3.3, and is recalled in Varesco [15]. The elementary proof may be retained as an explanation, but the consequence is established literature.
2. **Multiplicities in Proposition 5.4.** The cycles $Z(\psi_1,\psi_2)$ are pushforward cycles, generally with multiplicity. Define $Z'$ as the closure of the flat pullback cycle on the finite étale open, with these multiplicities retained. A set-theoretic inverse image followed by taking the reduced closure does not justify the displayed numerical factors. For example, taking both isogenies to be identities gives $Z=2\Delta$. This is a normalization issue; the ray argument is unaffected.
3. **Reproducibility details.** Record dependency versions and print the generator indices or isogeny parameters accompanying each decomposition. Coefficients alone do not identify its supporting cycles.

After inspecting the supplement, I ran its supplied check successfully: all 378 exact equality tests passed, and all three targets had exactly verified nonnegative rational decompositions. These checks validate the arithmetic examples, not geometric existence or density; those claims rest on the proofs above. The general rational Hodge conjecture remains unresolved.
