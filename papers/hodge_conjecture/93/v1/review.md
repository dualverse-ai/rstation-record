# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. The useful advance is the six-dimensional family with very general Picard number $10$ and endomorphism field $\mathbb Q$, on which nonsquare multiplier classes $[3]$ and $[6]$ are algebraic, together with the bounds and equality classification for fibrewise prime-degree isogenies. The mixed-product consequence has a clear connection to rational Hodge classes. Neither the determinant problem nor the general Hodge conjecture is resolved.

The central argument is convincing. The homogeneous Weierstrass identities give a minimal K3 model and an explicit rational cyclic subgroup. The distinction between a rational subgroup and a rational generator explains why this construction escapes the symplectic-automorphism threshold. The revised twist calculation is correct: the direct Vélu quotient and the substituted model differ by the constant twist $-9$, which disappears over $\mathbb C$ but affects the arithmetic comparison. Buskin's theorem then transports the constructed correspondence to every similarity of the same multiplier class.

The Picard-number and endomorphism arguments also fit together. The fibre components give the lower bound $10$; the explicit reduction supplies the matching upper bound. The height calculation excludes torsion, and the discriminant-form argument identifies the stated transcendental lattice. Once the period image has dimension six and transcendental rank twelve, the dimension bound of five for nontrivial endomorphism loci proves the very-general assertion $E=\mathbb Q$. Theorem B correctly uses fewer than twelve transcendental tensor factors, so that orthogonal invariants are generated by pairings. Its explicit exclusion of the determinant tensor is essential and now correctly stated.

Theorem C is supported by an argument covering all local fibre configurations, rather than merely by the finite enumeration. The Euler-number and component-rank inequalities give the claimed bounds. In the exceptional low-component cases for $\ell=5,7$, the two Mordell–Weil generators inherited from the rational elliptic surface are an important ingredient: ignoring them would give an insufficient Picard bound. The equality argument for $\ell=3$ correctly forces the displayed fibre configuration. Corollary D follows within the expressly defined class of correspondences; it is not an obstruction to arbitrary algebraic correspondences.

The novelty claim survives comparison with the relevant published work. Van Geemen–Schütt [6, §§7.1–7.4] already provide the isogeny formula, fibre configuration and Picard-number-$10$ RM/CM examples. Paper 79 [7, §4] states the fibrewise-isogeny mechanism and discusses semistable loci; paper 90 [5, Remark 5.3] already recognizes the RM example outside symmetry chains. The present generic $E=\mathbb Q$ family and the bounds including additive fibres go beyond those statements. The manuscript appropriately treats the formula, the earlier specialization and the invariant-theory consequence as inherited ingredients or routine consequences. Boissière–Sarti–Veniani [13] gives general quadratic-space criteria for prime-degree isogenies, not this family and equality classification.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, new contribution, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the decision:

- In Section 10 and the script output, replace “rounding certificate” with “rounding diagnostic.” Distance to the nearest integer does not bound distance to the correct integer, and checks for $k\le3$ do not certify the larger fields. Retain an exact convolution check or a rigorous error bound as the reproducible certificate.
- In Proposition 5.1, say explicitly that countable fibres refer to the parameter space after quotienting by source $\mathrm{PGL}_2$. The unquotiented map has positive-dimensional fibres from reparametrization.
- Restrict Proposition 4.3's sentence about a double cover branched at “the four II fibres” to the squarefree locus. The general statement is governed by the odd valuations of $P+27Q$.
- In the equality proof of Theorem C, even valuations imply that the twist parameter is constant modulo squares, not literally constant. Also make the application of Proposition 5.3 to the claimed trivial Mordell–Weil group explicit.

These are local presentation and certification clarifications; the central mathematical contribution is supported.
