## 1. Problem and contribution

### 1.1 Setting

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface, $\rho=\rho(S)$, $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}^{\perp}\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$, and $E(S)=\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S)_{\mathbb Q})$, a totally real or CM field. A *Hodge similarity of multiplier* $\lambda\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ is an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures $\psi\colon T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\psi(x)\cdot\psi(y)=\lambda\,x\cdot y$. For $E=\mathbb Q$, the Hodge conjecture (HC) for products of K3 surfaces reduces to two statements [1, Thm 1]: (D) the determinant class is algebraic, and $(\Psi_{\mathrm{ns}})$ Hodge similarities of nonsquare multiplier are algebraic. Square multipliers are covered by Buskin's theorem [2]. As in our papers 87, 88 and 90 [3], [4], [5], let

$$
G(S)=\{[\lambda]\in\mathbb Q_{>0}/\mathbb Q_{>0}^{2}:\ \text{every Hodge similarity } T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S')_{\mathbb Q} \text{ with multiplier in } [\lambda] \text{ is algebraic, for every } S'\}.
$$

Paper 90 [5, Thm B] determined the multiplier classes $\Sigma(S)$ reached by *symmetry chains*: compositions of correspondences inducing Hodge isometries (I), quotients by finite groups of symplectic automorphisms (Q), and fixed K3 surfaces of finite symplectic groups on manifolds of $K3^{[n]}$-type (F). For $\rho\le17$, $\Sigma(S)=\langle[p]:p\in P(S)\rangle$. In particular symmetry chains never give $[3]$ for $\rho\le 11$, never give a class involving a prime $\ge5$ below $\rho=16$, and give nothing at $\rho\le7$. Paper 90 [5, Rem. 5.3] also noted one construction outside this reach: van Geemen and Schütt's degree-$3$ fibrewise isogenies on a $3$-dimensional family of K3 surfaces with real multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ and Picard number $10$ [6, §7].

This paper studies the mechanism behind that example, fibrewise isogenies of elliptic fibrations, in general. We ask two questions. What does it give when $E=\mathbb Q$? And how far down in Picard number can it go?

### 1.2 Results

**The family.** For binary quartic forms $P,Q$ in $(s_0:s_1)$ without common zero, let $h=P/Q\colon\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1$, a map of degree $4$. Let $X_h$ be the K3 surface with Weierstrass model

$$
y^2=x^3-27C_4\,x-54C_6,\qquad C_4=(P+27Q)(P+3Q),\qquad C_6=(P+27Q)(P^2+18PQ-27Q^2). \tag{1.1}
$$

It is obtained from a Weierstrass model $W_3$ over the modular curve $X_0(3)$, whose $j$-map is $j_3=(t+27)(t+3)^3/t$, by substituting $t=P/Q$ and clearing denominators (Section 4). So $j(X_h)=j_3\circ h$.

**Theorem A (multiplier $3$ at Picard number $10$).**

1. For every $h$ of degree $4$, $X_h$ is a K3 surface with an elliptic fibration with section, and $C_4^3-C_6^2=1728\,(P+27Q)^2PQ^3$. If $P$, $Q$ and $P+27Q$ have simple zeros, the singular fibres are $4\,\mathrm{II}$ (at $P+27Q=0$), $4\,I_1$ (at $P=0$) and $4\,I_3$ (at $Q=0$).
2. The generic fibre of $X_h$ has a rational $3$-isogeny, with kernel $\{O,(x_0,\pm y_0)\}$, $x_0=-3P-81Q$, $y_0^2=-11664\,Q^2(P+27Q)$. If $P+27Q$ has a simple zero, the kernel is not generated by a rational point. The target is isomorphic over $\mathbb C(s)$ to the generic fibre of $X_{h'}$, $h'=729Q/P$ (over $\mathbb Q(s)$ the two models differ by the constant quadratic twist by $-9$; Proposition 4.3). The induced dominant rational map $\varphi_h\colon X_h\dashrightarrow X_{h'}$ has degree $3$, and $\varphi_h^*\colon T(X_{h'})_{\mathbb Q}\to T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ is an algebraic Hodge similarity of multiplier $3$.
3. The family $\{X_h\}$ has $6$ moduli. For very general $h$: $\rho(X_h)=10$, $\mathrm{NS}(X_h)=U\oplus A_2(-1)^{4}$, $T(X_h)\cong U^2\oplus A_2(-1)^4$, and $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$. The equality $\rho=10$ already holds for the explicit member $h_0=(2s^4-2s^3+3s^2-2s+3)/(-s^4-s^3-2s-2)$.
4. For every $h$, $[2],[3],[6]\in G(X_h)$. If $\rho(X_h)\le11$ (for instance for very general $h$), then $[3]\notin\Sigma(X_h)$: no symmetry chain induces a similarity of class $[3]$ out of $T(X_h)$, and $\varphi_h$ is not birational to the quotient map by a group of automorphisms.

**Theorem B (Hodge conjecture for products).** Let $h$ be such that $\rho(X_h)=10$ and $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$ (both true for very general $h$). Let $S_1,\dots,S_k$, $1\le k\le11$, be projective K3 surfaces such that each $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ is Hodge similar to $T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ with multiplier class in $\{[1],[2],[3],[6]\}$. Repetitions are allowed. Then every Hodge class on $S_1\times\dots\times S_k$ is algebraic. In particular HC holds for $X_h\times X_{h'}$, and $X_h$, $X_{h'}$ are not Hodge isometric. More generally, if $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$ and $r=22-\rho(X_h)$, the same holds for $k<r$; at $k=r$ the determinant class in $\Lambda^rT$ appears, and we claim nothing about it.

**Theorem C (the Picard-number reach of fibrewise isogenies).** Let $X$ be a projective K3 surface with a genus-one fibration $X\to\mathbb P^1$ whose $j$-invariant is not constant. Suppose that the generic fibre of its Jacobian fibration has a $\mathbb C(t)$-rational cyclic subgroup of prime order $\ell$. Then:

1. $\ell\in\{2,3,5,7,13\}$;
2. $\rho(X)\ge10$ if $\ell\in\{2,3\}$, and $\rho(X)\ge16$ if $\ell\in\{5,7,13\}$;
3. if $\ell=3$ and $\rho(X)=10$, the Jacobian fibration of $X$ is isomorphic to $X_h\to\mathbb P^1$ for some $h$ of degree $4$ with $P$, $Q$, $P+27Q$ squarefree, and its Mordell–Weil group is trivial.

If instead the $j$-invariant is constant, then either all singular fibres are of type $I_0^*$ and $\rho(X)\ge18$, or $j\in\{0,1728\}$ and $E(X)$ contains $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ or $\mathbb Q(i)$.

**Corollary D (the combined reach below Picard number 16).** Let $S$ be a projective K3 surface with $\rho(S)\le15$ and $E(S)$ not a CM field. Let $\Sigma^+(S)$ be the set of multiplier classes of similarities out of $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ induced by chains of steps of types (I), (Q), (F) of [5] and of the following type (Iso), and their transposes:

(Iso) a dominant rational map $A\dashrightarrow A''$ of prime degree $\ell$ between K3 surfaces that, for some genus-one fibration on $A$ with non-constant $j$-invariant, is the fibrewise quotient by a rational subgroup of order $\ell$ of the Jacobian.

Then $\Sigma^+(S)\subseteq\langle[2],[3]\rangle$, and $\Sigma^+(S)=\Sigma(S)$ if $\rho(S)\le9$. If $\rho(S)=10$ and a chain contains an (Iso) step of degree $3$, the source $A$ of that map has $T(A)_{\mathbb Q}$ Hodge similar to $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$, and the Jacobian of its fibration is some $X_h$. Conversely $[3]\in\Sigma^+(S)$ whenever $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ is Hodge isometric to some $T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$.

**Corollary E (real multiplication at Picard number 10).** Let $S$ be a projective K3 surface with $\rho(S)=10$ such that $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ is Hodge similar to $T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$, for some $h$, with multiplier class in $\langle[2],[3]\rangle$. If $E(S)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$ with $d\in\{2,3,6\}$, then $\sqrt d$ is induced by an algebraic class on $S\times S$, and HC holds for $S^k$, $k\le5$. This applies to the $3$-dimensional family of van Geemen–Schütt with $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ [6, Prop. 7.2(2)].

### 1.3 What is new and what is not

*Not new.* That a fibrewise isogeny of degree $p$ gives an algebraic similarity of multiplier $p$ is implicit in van Geemen–Schütt [6, §7] and stated in rStation paper 79 [7, §4, after Prop. 4.1]. The $3$-isogeny formula is classical; we use the form in [6, §7.1] and rederive it with Vélu's formulas [8]. The fibre configuration $4\,\mathrm{II}+4\,I_3+4\,I_1$ and the inclusion $\mathrm{NS}\supset U\oplus A_2^4$ are in [6, §7.3] for their $3$-dimensional subfamilies, and [6, §7.4] proves $\rho=10$ for one of their members. That member lies in our family, so it already shows that the very general $\rho$ is $10$; our point count at another member is an independent check. Shioda–Tate, Euler-number counts and the ramification of $X_0(\ell)\to X(1)$ are standard [9], [10]. Paper 79 [7, §4] counts the *semistable* loci of fibrewise $p$-isogenies (dimension $10,6,2,0$ for $p=2,3,5,7$; there the Picard number is at least $14$ for $p=3$).

*New, as far as we could find (Section 2 and `sources.md`).*

- Theorem A(3)–(4): the full $6$-dimensional family of K3 surfaces with a fibrewise $3$-isogeny at Picard number $10$, with $E=\mathbb Q$ for the very general member. This gives $(\Psi_{\mathrm{ns}})$ for the classes $[3]$ and $[6]$ at Picard number $10$ with $E=\mathbb Q$. Before, as far as we know, the smallest Picard number with an algebraic similarity of class $[3]$ and $E=\mathbb Q$ was $12$: paper 83 [11] and paper 88 [4, Thm B] with criterion $C_3$. Paper 90 [5, Thm B] shows that symmetry chains cannot reach this case.
- Theorem B is a routine consequence of Theorem A, Zarhin's theorem and the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory.
- Theorem C and Corollary D: lower bounds for all fibre configurations (not only semistable ones), the classification of the equality case for $\ell=3$, and the combination with the closure theorem of paper 90. The arguments are elementary.
- Corollary E is a small addition. For $d=3$, algebraicity of $\sqrt3$ on the van Geemen–Schütt family is theirs [6]. The powers $S^k$, $k\le5$, follow from [5, Prop. 7.1].

Nothing is claimed at Picard number $\le9$. Theorem C shows that fibrewise isogenies cannot help there. We claim nothing on the determinant class (D), and nothing about the classes $[5]$, $[7]$, $[13]$ at $\rho\ge16$ beyond the lower bound.

## 3. Notation and imported results

$\Lambda=U^3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ is the K3 lattice. $A_2(-1)$ is the negative definite $A_2$ lattice. For a rational quadratic space $V$, $V(\lambda)$ is $V$ with its form multiplied by $\lambda$. We use the following.

- **(R1)** [3, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2]. If $f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y$ is a dominant rational map of degree $d$ between K3 surfaces, then $f^*$ maps $T(Y)_{\mathbb Q}$ isomorphically onto $T(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ and multiplies the form by $d$. Indeed, on a resolution $\tilde f\colon\tilde X\to Y$, $\pi\colon\tilde X\to X$, the class $\tilde f^*y$ of $y\in T(Y)$ is orthogonal to the exceptional curves, and $\tilde f^*y\cdot\tilde f^*y'=d\,(y\cdot y')$ by the projection formula. The image contains $f^*\sigma_Y\ne0$, and $T(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is the smallest rational sub-Hodge structure containing $\sigma_X$. Inverses and compositions of correspondences inducing similarities are algebraic, and the transcendental projectors are algebraic.
- **(R2)** Buskin [2]: rational Hodge isometries $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$ are algebraic. Hence, if one similarity of class $[\lambda]$ out of $T(S)$ is algebraic, then $[\lambda]\in G(S)$ [3, Lemma 3.2].
- **(R3)** [4, Thm B]: $[2]\in G(S)$ whenever $\rho(S)\ge9$.
- **(R4)** [5, Thm B and Lemmas 5.1–5.2]: for $\rho(S)\le17$, a step of type (I), (Q) or (F), or its transpose, out of a surface $A$ Hodge similar to $S$ has multiplier class in $\langle P(S)\rangle$. Here $2\in P(S)$ iff $\rho\ge9$ (or $\rho=8$ with $C_2$); $3\in P(S)$ iff $\rho\ge13$ (or $\rho=12$ with $C_3$); $5\in P(S)$ forces $\rho\ge16$; and $7\notin P(S)$.
- **(R5)** Zarhin [16]: if $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, the Hodge group of $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ is $\mathrm{SO}(T(S)_{\mathbb Q})$. Van Geemen [17, §3]: in the period domain of a fixed rational transcendental space of dimension $r$, the K3 surfaces with $E$ totally real of degree $d$ ($r/d\ge3$) fill countably many submanifolds of dimension $r/d-2$, and those with $E$ CM of degree $d$ fill countably many of dimension $r/d-1$.
- **(R6)** Elliptic surfaces [9]. For a minimal Weierstrass fibration over $\mathbb P^1$ with $c_4$, $c_6$ of degrees $8$, $12$ and $\Delta=(c_4^3-c_6^2)/1728\not\equiv0$, the minimal resolution is a K3 surface. Kodaira types are read off from $(\operatorname{ord}c_4,\operatorname{ord}c_6,\operatorname{ord}\Delta)$ in characteristic $0$ and $\ge5$. Shioda–Tate: $\rho=2+\sum_v(m_v-1)+\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{MW}$, where $m_v$ is the number of components of the fibre $F_v$, and $\mathrm{NS}/\mathrm{Triv}\cong\mathrm{MW}$. Noether: $\sum_v e(F_v)=24$. A torsion section $P\ne O$ has height $0=4+2P\cdot O-\sum_v\mathrm{contr}_v(P)$, with $\mathrm{contr}_v\le2/3$ for $I_3$ and $=0$ for $I_1$, $\mathrm{II}$.
- **(R7)** Modular curves [10, §§3.1, 3.9]. For a prime $\ell$, $X_0(\ell)\to X(1)$ has degree $\ell+1$. It has two cusps, of widths $1$ and $\ell$. It has $\nu_3=1+(\tfrac{-3}{\ell})$ elliptic points over $j=0$ (for $\ell\ne3$; $\nu_3=1$ for $\ell=3$, $0$ for $\ell=2$), which are unramified, and all other points over $j=0$ have ramification $3$. It has $\nu_2=1+(\tfrac{-1}{\ell})$ elliptic points over $j=1728$ ($\nu_2=1$ for $\ell=2$), which are unramified, and all other points over $1728$ have ramification $2$. The genus of $X_0(\ell)$ is $0$ iff $\ell\in\{2,3,5,7,13\}$. Explicit $j$-maps are rechecked in `verify.py`, §A.
- **(R8)** Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces over finite fields of characteristic $\ge5$ (Charles [18], Madapusi Pera [19]). If a K3 surface over a number field has good reduction at $\mathfrak p$, specialization embeds $\mathrm{NS}(X_{\bar{\mathbb Q}})$ into $\mathrm{NS}(X_{\bar{\mathbb F}_p})$, so $\rho(X)\le\rho(X_{\bar{\mathbb F}_p})$ (van Luijk's method [20]).

## 4. The family and the isogeny (Theorem A(1), (2))

**Proposition 4.1 (the model over $X_0(3)$).** Put $c_4=(t+27)(t+3)$ and $c_6=(t+27)(t^2+18t-27)$, and let $W_3\colon y^2=x^3-27c_4x-54c_6$ over $\mathbb C(t)$. Then:

1. $c_4^3-c_6^2=1728\,t\,(t+27)^2$ and $j(W_3)=(t+27)(t+3)^3/t$, the $j$-map of $X_0(3)$;
2. the $3$-division polynomial of $W_3$ has the factor $x+3t+81$, and $y_0^2=-11664\,(t+27)$ at $x_0=-3t-81$;
3. Vélu's formulas for the kernel $\{O,(x_0,\pm y_0)\}$ give $W_3'\colon y^2=x^3-27(t+27)(t+243)x-54(t+27)(t^2-486t-19683)$. Its coefficients are $A'=d^2A(729/t)$, $B'=d^3B(729/t)$ with $d=-t/9$, where $A=-27c_4$, $B=-54c_6$. So $W_3'$ is the quadratic twist by $d$ of $W_3$ at the Atkin–Lehner point $729/t$. Since $d$ has odd valuation at $t=0$, it is not a square in $\mathbb C(t)$, and $W_3'$ is *not* isomorphic over $\mathbb C(t)$ to $W_3(729/t)$; only their $j$-invariants agree.

*Proof.* (1)–(3) are polynomial identities, checked in `verify.py` §B and `comprobar_v5.mpl` §§1–2. $\square$

**Proposition 4.2 (Theorem A(1)).** For binary quartics $P$, $Q$ without common zero, the data (1.1) satisfy $C_4=Q^2c_4(h)$ and $C_6=Q^3c_6(h)$, so $j(X_h)=j_3\circ h$; over $\mathbb C(s)$ the generic fibre is the quadratic twist by $Q$ of the base change of $W_3$ by $h$. (1.1) is a minimal Weierstrass model of a K3 surface, with $C_4^3-C_6^2=1728\,(P+27Q)^2PQ^3$. If $P$, $Q$, $P+27Q$ are squarefree, its singular fibres are $4\,\mathrm{II}+4\,I_1+4\,I_3$.

*Proof.* The identity is in `verify.py` §C. Since $\gcd(P,Q)=1$, $\Delta\not\equiv0$, and the degrees are $8$ and $12$. Minimality: at a zero of $P+27Q$ of multiplicity $m\le4$, $Q\ne0$, so $\operatorname{ord}C_4=m$ (as $P+3Q=-24Q\ne0$) and $\operatorname{ord}C_6=m<6$ (as $P^2+18PQ-27Q^2=216Q^2\ne0$). At the other zeros of $\Delta$, one of $C_4$, $C_6$ is nonzero. So (R6) applies. If the three forms are squarefree: at a zero of $P+27Q$, $\operatorname{ord}\Delta=2$ and $\operatorname{ord}C_4\ge1$, type $\mathrm{II}$; at a zero of $P$, $\operatorname{ord}\Delta=1$, type $I_1$; at a zero of $Q$, $\operatorname{ord}\Delta=3$ and $C_4=P^2\ne0$, type $I_3$. $\square$

**Proposition 4.3 (Theorem A(2)).** The generic fibre $E_h$ of $X_h$ has the rational $3$-isogeny with kernel $x_0=-3P-81Q$, and $y_0^2=-11664\,Q^2(P+27Q)$. The kernel points are rational only over the double cover branched at the four $\mathrm{II}$ fibres. The isogenous curve is the generic fibre of $X_{h'}$, $h'=729Q/P$, whose model is

$$
y^2=x^3-27C_4'x-54C_6',\qquad C_4'=(P+27Q)(P+243Q),\quad C_6'=(P+27Q)(P^2-486PQ-19683Q^2),
$$

with $C_4'^3-C_6'^2=1728(P+27Q)^2P^3Q$: the $I_1$ and $I_3$ fibres are swapped. The map $\varphi_h\colon X_h\dashrightarrow X_{h'}$ induced by the isogeny is dominant of degree $3$, and $\varphi_h^*\colon T(X_{h'})_{\mathbb Q}\to T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ is a Hodge similarity of multiplier $3$, induced by the algebraic cycle $\overline{\Gamma_{\varphi_h}}$.

*Proof.* Rational cyclic subgroups are preserved by base change and by quadratic twists, so the kernel of Proposition 4.1(2) gives one on $E_h$; in the coordinates of (1.1) its $x$-coordinate is $Q(-3h-81)=-3P-81Q$. We also check this directly on (1.1), as identities in the forms $P$, $Q$ (`verify.py` §C, `comprobar_v5.mpl` §3): $x_0$ is a root of the $3$-division polynomial of (1.1), $y_0^2=-11664\,Q^2(P+27Q)$, and Vélu's formulas for this kernel give exactly the model with $C_4'$, $C_6'$. The relation with $X_{h'}$ is another identity: substituting $(P,Q)\mapsto(729Q,P)$ in (1.1) gives $(C_4,C_6)=((-9)^2C_4',(-9)^3C_6')$. So $X_{h'}$ is the quadratic twist of the Vélu quotient $X'_h$ by the *constant* $-9$. Over $\mathbb C$ this is an isomorphism, $(x,y)\mapsto(u^2x,u^3y)$ with $u^2=-9$, e.g. $u=3i$. (In the homogeneous model the twist by $-t/9$ of Proposition 4.1(3) is absorbed by the passage from the twist by $Q$ to the twist by $P$, up to this constant.) Over $\mathbb Q$ the two models differ by the twist by $-1$. If $P+27Q$ has a simple zero, $-(P+27Q)$ is not a square in $\mathbb C(s)$, so $y_0\notin\mathbb C(s)$. An isogeny of degree $3$ of generic fibres gives a degree-$3$ extension of function fields, so $\varphi_h$ is dominant of degree $3$. (R1) gives the similarity. $\square$

**Remark 4.4.** The family is stable under $h\mapsto h'=w_3\circ h$, where $w_3(t)=729/t$ is the Atkin–Lehner involution. As an independent check, `verify.py` §D confirms that $X_{h_0}$ and its direct Vélu quotient $X'_{h_0}$ (the model with $C_4'$, $C_6'$, isogenous to $X_{h_0}$ over $\mathbb F_7$) have the same Frobenius traces on $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$ over $\mathbb F_{7^k}$, $k\le6$. The substituted model $X_{h_0'}$ is its twist by $-9$, which is a nonsquare in $\mathbb F_7$ and a square in $\mathbb F_{49}$; accordingly its traces are $(-1)^k$ times those of $X_{h_0}$ (for $k=1$: $-1$ against $1$), which the script also checks. The complex isomorphism $X'_{h}\cong X_{h'}$ does not imply equality of Frobenius traces over $\mathbb F_7$.

## 5. Picard number, lattices and endomorphism field (Theorem A(3))

**Proposition 5.1 (six moduli).** The degree-$4$ maps $h$ form a $9$-dimensional variety, on which $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ acts on the source with finite stabilizers. The map $h\mapsto X_h$ to isomorphism classes of K3 surfaces has countable fibres. So the family has $6$ moduli, and the image of its period map in the $10$-dimensional period domain of $T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ has dimension $6$.

*Proof.* An isomorphism of elliptic surfaces $X_h\cong X_{\tilde h}$ over a Möbius map $\mu$ gives $j_3\circ h=j_3\circ\tilde h\circ\mu$, with $j_3$ the $j$-map of degree $4$ of $X_0(3)$. Given $J=j_3\circ h$, $h(s)$ is a root of the degree-$4$ equation $j_3(T)=J(s)$ over $\mathbb C(s)$, so there are at most $4$ choices. A K3 surface has countably many elliptic fibrations with section. Hence the fibres are countable and the image has dimension $9-3=6$. By local Torelli the period image is $6$-dimensional. `verify.py` §G checks the count independently: the differential of $(P,Q)\mapsto(C_4,C_6)$ has rank $10$ at a random point, and the tangent space of the $\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathbb C^*$-orbit, of dimension $4$, lies in its image. $\square$

**Proposition 5.2 ($\rho=10$).** $\rho(X_h)\ge10$ for every $h$, and $\rho(X_{h_0})=10$ for $h_0=P_0/Q_0$, $P_0=2s^4-2s^3+3s^2-2s+3$, $Q_0=-s^4-s^3-2s-2$. Hence $\rho(X_h)=10$ for very general $h$.

*Proof.* For every $h$, the fibres over the zeros of $Q$ are $I_{3e}$ (possibly twisted) with $\sum e=4$, so $\sum(m_v-1)\ge\sum(3e-1)\ge8$ and $\rho\ge10$ (this is also Theorem C(2)). For $h_0$, the form $(P_0+27Q_0)P_0Q_0$ is squarefree of degree $12$ modulo $7$. So the reduction mod $7$ has the same fibre types, and the minimal model has good reduction at $7$. Over $\mathbb F_q$, $q=7^k$, the $\ell$-adic $H^2$ splits as the span of the zero section, the fibre and the $8$ non-identity components of the $I_3$ fibres, plus $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}=H^1(\mathbb P^1,j_*\mathcal V)$ of rank $12$. The Grothendieck–Lefschetz formula gives

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_q\mid H^1_{\mathrm{par}})=\sum_{s\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)}\ \sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}\chi_q\big(x^3+A(s)x+B(s)\big),\qquad A=-27C_4,\ B=-54C_6,
$$

with $\chi_q$ the quadratic character [9], [20]. `verify.py` §D computes these sums exactly for $k=1,\dots,7$: they are $1,99,589,99,-5879,444333,-4798226$. The fast method agrees with a naive double loop for $k\le3$, and `comprobar_v5.mpl` §4 recomputes $k=1,2$ independently. Newton's identities give the coefficients $c_1,\dots,c_6$ of $P(X)=\det(X-\mathrm{Frob}_7\mid H^1_{\mathrm{par}})$. The functional equation $X^{12}P(49/X)=\varepsilon\,7^{12}P(X)$, $\varepsilon=\pm1$, gives the rest. The seventh trace forces $\varepsilon=+1$, since $c_7=\varepsilon\cdot49\,c_5$ with $c_5=9604$ and $c_7=470596$. The result is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X)={}&X^{12}-X^{11}-49X^{10}-147X^{9}+1372X^{8}+9604X^{7}-84035X^{6}\\
&+470596X^{5}+3294172X^{4}-17294403X^{3}-282475249X^{2}-282475249X+13841287201 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The polynomial $P(7X)/7^{12}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, and its coefficient of $X^{11}$ is $-1/7\notin\mathbb Z$. So it is not cyclotomic, and no root of $P$ has the form $7\zeta$ with $\zeta$ a root of unity. All roots have absolute value $7$, a check. By the Tate conjecture (R8), $\rho(X_{h_0,\bar{\mathbb F}_7})=10+0=10$, so $\rho(X_{h_0})\le10$. Finally, the locus where $\rho$ jumps is a countable union of proper closed subsets. $\square$

*Controls* (`verify.py` §D). The rational surface $R_7$ of Section 7 gives only Tate classes on its $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$, as it must. A member with an $I_2$ fibre ($H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$ of odd rank $11$) always shows a Tate class. Another member, reduced mod $5$, has geometric Picard number $16$. The method detects extra classes when they exist; only special reductions can be used.

**Proposition 5.3 (lattices).** If $\rho(X_h)=10$ and $P,Q,P+27Q$ are squarefree, then $\mathrm{MW}(X_h)=0$, $\mathrm{NS}(X_h)=U\oplus A_2(-1)^4$ (discriminant $-81$), and $T(X_h)\cong U^2\oplus A_2(-1)^4$. The rational space $V_3=T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ satisfies $V_3(\lambda)\cong V_3$ for $\lambda=2,3,6$.

*Proof.* Shioda–Tate gives $\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{MW}=0$. A torsion section would need $\sum_v\mathrm{contr}_v=4$, but the four $I_3$ fibres give at most $8/3$ (R6). So $\mathrm{MW}=0$ and $\mathrm{NS}$ is the trivial lattice. Its discriminant form is $4\langle-2/3\rangle$ on $(\mathbb Z/3)^4$. The lattice $U^2\oplus A_2(-1)^4$ has signature $(2,10)$ and discriminant form $4\langle-2/3\rangle$. This is isomorphic to $-q_{\mathrm{NS}}=4\langle2/3\rangle$, because nondegenerate forms of rank $4$ over $\mathbb F_3$ are classified by their discriminant, and $(-2)^4\equiv2^4\pmod3$. By Nikulin [21, Thm 1.14.2] ($\operatorname{rk}=12\ge2+4$), $T$ is unique in its genus, and $T\cong U^2\oplus A_2(-1)^4$. The rational statements are exact Hilbert-symbol computations (`verify.py` §F). They are consistent with Proposition 4.3: $T(X_{h'})_{\mathbb Q}\cong T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}(3)$. $\square$

**Proposition 5.4 ($E=\mathbb Q$).** For very general $h$, $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$.

*Proof.* By (R5), with $r=12$, the K3 surfaces in the period domain of $V_3$ with $E\ne\mathbb Q$ lie in countably many submanifolds of dimension at most $\max(12/2-2,\ 12/2-1,\ 12/3-2,\ 12/4-2,\ 12/4-1,\ 12/6-1,\ 0)=5$ (RM of degree $2,3,4$; CM of degree $2,4,6,12$). By Propositions 5.1–5.2, the very general $X_h$ has $T_{\mathbb Q}\cong V_3$ and lies on a $6$-dimensional image. So it avoids them. $\square$

This proves Theorem A(3).

## 6. Proofs of Theorem A(4), Theorem B and Corollary E

**Proof of Theorem A(4).** $[3]\in G(X_h)$: let $\psi\colon T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ be a Hodge similarity of multiplier $3c^2$. Then $\psi\circ\varphi_h^*$ has multiplier $9c^2$, so $(3c)^{-1}\psi\circ\varphi_h^*$ is a Hodge isometry, algebraic by (R2). Hence $\psi=(\psi\circ\varphi_h^*)\circ(\varphi_h^*)^{-1}$ is algebraic, because $(\varphi_h^*)^{-1}=\tfrac13\varphi_{h*}$ is (R1). $[2]\in G(X_h)$ by (R3), since $\rho\ge10\ge9$. $[6]\in G(X_h)$: write a similarity of class $[6]$ as a composition of one of class $[3]$ out of $X_h$ and one of class $[2]$ out of its target, which has $\rho\ge9$. For $\rho(X_h)\le11$, $3\notin P(X_h)$, so $[3]\notin\Sigma(X_h)$ by (R4). Finally, if $\varphi_h$ were birational to a quotient map $X_h\to X_h/G$ with $|G|=3$, then $G$ would act by automorphisms (birational self-maps of K3 surfaces are automorphisms). It would act symplectically, because $\varphi_h^*\sigma$ is a nonzero $G$-invariant multiple of $\sigma_{X_h}$. A symplectic automorphism of order $3$ has a coinvariant lattice of rank $12$ in $\mathrm{NS}$, orthogonal to an invariant ample class, so $\rho\ge13$ [22]. This contradicts $\rho\le11$. $\square$

**Proof of Theorem B.** Since $\rho(X_h)=10$, $\dim T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}=12$. Since $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$ and the $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ are Hodge similar to $T:=T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$, we have $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$. Fix similarities $\psi_i\colon T\to T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ of classes $c_i\in\{[1],[2],[3],[6]\}\subseteq G(X_h)$. They are algebraic, and so are their inverses (R1). By the Künneth formula, the Hodge classes on $\prod S_i$ are spanned by products of pull-backs of algebraic classes (from $H^0$, $\mathrm{NS}$, $H^4$) and of Hodge classes in $T(S_{i_1})_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\dots\otimes T(S_{i_j})_{\mathbb Q}$ with $i_1<\dots<i_j$ and $j\le k\le11$. Via $\otimes\psi_{i_a}$ this space is $T^{\otimes j}$ with the diagonal action of the Hodge group $\mathrm{SO}(T)$ (R5). Similarities are isomorphisms of Hodge structures, so the Hodge group of $\bigoplus_iT(S_i)$ is $\mathrm{SO}(T)$ acting through the $\psi_i$. By the first fundamental theorem for $\mathrm{SO}(12)$ over $\mathbb Q$ [23, §5.3], since $j<12=\dim T$, the invariants are spanned by products of the pairings $q_{ab}$ between the factors $a$ and $b$; no determinant term occurs. (For a member with $E=\mathbb Q$ and $\dim T=r<12$, the same argument works for $j<r$; at $j=r$ the determinant tensor in $\Lambda^rT$ is a Hodge class not generated by pairings, which is why the hypothesis $\rho=10$, or $k<r$, is needed.) Under the identification $T(S_a)\otimes T(S_b)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(T(S_a),T(S_b))$ by the intersection form, $q_{ab}$ corresponds to a nonzero multiple of $\psi_b\circ\psi_a^{-1}$, which is algebraic. Products of pull-backs of algebraic classes are algebraic. For $X_h\times X_{h'}$, use $\psi=(\varphi_h^*)^{-1}$, of class $[3]$. If $X_h$ and $X_{h'}$ were Hodge isometric by $\iota$, then $\varphi_h^*\circ\iota$ would be an element of $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$ acting as a similarity of multiplier $3$, that is, a rational number $c$ with $c^2=3$. This is impossible. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary E.** Let $\psi\colon T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ be the given similarity, of class $c\in\langle2,3\rangle\subseteq G(X_h)$ (Theorem A(4)). Then $\psi^{-1}$ is algebraic, hence so is $\psi$, and $G(S)\supseteq\langle[2],[3]\rangle$. Elements of a totally real $E(S)$ are self-adjoint [17, §2], so $\sqrt d$ is a Hodge self-similarity of multiplier $d$, of class $[d]\in G(S)$. Hence $\sqrt d$ is algebraic, and [5, Prop. 7.1] with $m=\dim_{E}T=6$ gives HC for $S^k$, $k<6$. For van Geemen–Schütt's family [6, Prop. 7.2(2)] the very general member has $\rho=10$ ([6, §7.4] and Proposition 5.2), a fibrewise $3$-isogeny, and $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$. By Theorem C(3) its Jacobian fibration (it has a section) is some $X_h$. $\square$

## 7. The reach of fibrewise isogenies (Theorem C, Corollary D)

**Lemma 7.1.** Let $\mathcal E/\mathbb C(t)$ be an elliptic curve with non-constant $j$ and a rational cyclic subgroup $C$ of prime order $\ell$. Then $j(\mathcal E)=j_\ell\circ h$ for a non-constant $h\colon\mathbb P^1\to X_0(\ell)$, so $X_0(\ell)$ has genus $0$ and $\ell\in\{2,3,5,7,13\}$. If $n=\deg h$, then $\deg j(\mathcal E)=n(\ell+1)$.

*Proof.* The pair $(\mathcal E,C)$ defines a $\mathbb C(t)$-point of the coarse moduli space $X_0(\ell)$ over $j(\mathcal E)$, that is, a morphism $h\colon\mathbb P^1\to X_0(\ell)$ with $j_\ell\circ h=j(\mathcal E)$. It is non-constant because $j(\mathcal E)$ is. A non-constant map from $\mathbb P^1$ forces genus $0$ (Lüroth). Then use (R7). $\square$

**Lemma 7.2 (local fibre types).** Let $J=j_\ell\circ h$, and let $s$ lie over $t_0\in X_0(\ell)$ with ramification index $e$. The fibre $F_s$ of the Jacobian fibration has Euler number $e(F_s)$ and $r_s=m_s-1$ given by:

| over $t_0$ | possible types (untwisted / twisted) | $(e(F),r)$ |
|---|---|---|
| cusp of width $w$ | $I_{we}$ / $I_{we}^*$ | $(we,\,we-1)$ / $(we+6,\,we+4)$ |
| elliptic point over $j=0$ ($\operatorname{ord}J=e$) | $e\equiv0$: $I_0/I_0^*$; $e\equiv1$: $\mathrm{II}/\mathrm{IV}^*$; $e\equiv2$: $\mathrm{IV}/\mathrm{II}^*$ (mod $3$) | $(0,0)/(6,4)$; $(2,0)/(8,6)$; $(4,2)/(10,8)$ |
| elliptic point over $j=1728$ ($\operatorname{ord}(J-1728)=e$) | $e$ even: $I_0/I_0^*$; $e$ odd: $\mathrm{III}/\mathrm{III}^*$ | $(0,0)/(6,4)$; $(3,1)/(9,7)$ |
| any other point | $I_0/I_0^*$ | $(0,0)/(6,4)$ |

*Proof.* Over a cusp, $\operatorname{ord}_sJ=-we$, so $F_s$ is $I_{we}$ or $I_{we}^*$. Over a point of $j=0$, $\operatorname{ord}_sJ\equiv e\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{t_0}j_\ell\pmod3$, and the types with $J(s)=0$ are classified by $\operatorname{ord}J\bmod3$ ($\mathrm{II}$, $\mathrm{IV}^*$: $\equiv1$; $\mathrm{IV}$, $\mathrm{II}^*$: $\equiv2$; $I_0$, $I_0^*$: $\equiv0$). Non-elliptic points over $0$ have $\operatorname{ord}j_\ell=3$ (R7), so only $I_0$, $I_0^*$ occur there. The same holds over $1728$ with $\operatorname{ord}(J-1728)\bmod2$ ($\mathrm{III}$, $\mathrm{III}^*$ need an odd order). Elsewhere $J$ is finite and $\ne0,1728$, so the fibre is $I_0$ or $I_0^*$. The two choices differ by the parity of the quadratic twist at $s$ [9]. $\square$

**Proof of Theorem C.** Since $T(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ and $T(J(X))_{\mathbb Q}$ are Hodge isometric [24, Ch. 11], we may take $X$ to be the Jacobian. (1) is Lemma 7.1. For (2), let $R=\sum_s r_s$, so that $\rho=2+R+\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{MW}$ (R6), and $\sum_se(F_s)=24$. Write $A$ and $B$ for the sets of points over the cusps of widths $\ell$ and $1$; then $\sum_{s\in A}e_s=\sum_{s\in B}e_s=n$. In each case below, every fibre type that is not explicitly counted has $r\ge\tfrac23e(F)$. This includes the twisted cusp fibres (each twist adds $6$ to $e$ and $5$ to $r$).

*$\ell=3$* ($\nu_3=1$, $\nu_2=0$, so no $\mathrm{III}$, $\mathrm{III}^*$). Let $b$ and $c$ be the numbers of $\mathrm{II}$ and $\mathrm{IV}$ fibres. Then $b+2c\le n$, and $\mathrm{IV}$ has $r=\tfrac12e$. The untwisted cusp fibres have Euler sum $4n$ and $\sum_A(3e_s-1)\ge2n$. Hence

$$
R\ \ge\ 2n+2c+\tfrac23\,(24-4n-2b-4c)\ \ge\ 16-\tfrac23n-\tfrac43\min(n,\,12-2n),
$$

because $\tfrac43b+\tfrac23c=\tfrac43(b+\tfrac c2)$ and $b+\tfrac c2\le\min(n,12-2n)$ (from $b+2c\le n$ and $2b+4c\le24-4n$). The right side is $14,12,10,8,10,12$ for $n=1,\dots,6$, and $4n\le24$ forces $n\le6$. So $R\ge8$ and $\rho\ge10$.

*Equality ($\ell=3$, $\rho=10$).* Equality forces $n=4$, $b=4$, $c=0$, $|A|=|B|=4$ (all $e_s=1$), no twisted fibre and no other singular fibre, and $\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{MW}=0$. So the fibres are $4\,I_3+4\,I_1+4\,\mathrm{II}$, and $h$ is unramified over $\infty$, $0$ and $-27$. The generic fibre is a quadratic twist, by some $d\in\mathbb C(s)^\times$, of the generic fibre of $X_h$. The two have the same local types everywhere, so $d$ has even order at every point and is a constant. Hence $X\cong X_h$. The equality case and the bound are confirmed by exhaustive enumeration in `verify.py` §E. This proves (3).

*$\ell=5$* ($\nu_2=2$, $\nu_3=0$, so no $\mathrm{II}$, $\mathrm{IV}$). Let $a\le\min(2n,8-2n)$ be the number of $\mathrm{III}$ fibres ($r=1$, $e=3$). Then

$$
R\ \ge\ (5n-|A|)+a+\tfrac23(24-6n-3a)\ \ge\ 16-a .
$$

If $a\le2$, then $R\ge14$. The value $a=3$ is impossible: it forces $n=2$, and the remaining Euler number $3$ cannot be filled by fibres of Euler number $\ge6$. If $a=4$, then $n=2$, the four points over the elliptic points are unramified, and the Euler number is used up. So there is no twisted fibre, and as in the case $\ell=3$, $X\cong h^*R_5$, where $R_5$ is the rational elliptic surface over $X_0(5)$ with fibres $I_5,I_1,\mathrm{III},\mathrm{III}$ (Section B of `verify.py`). Its Mordell–Weil rank is $10-2-6=2$, and pull-back of sections is injective. So $\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{MW}(X)\ge2$ and $\rho\ge2+12+2=16$.

*$\ell=7$* ($\nu_3=2$, $\nu_2=0$). With $b$ $\mathrm{II}$ fibres and $c$ $\mathrm{IV}$ fibres ($b+2c\le2n$):

$$
R\ \ge\ 6n+2c+\tfrac23(24-8n-2b-4c)=16+\tfrac23n-\tfrac43b-\tfrac23c .
$$

For $n=1$: $c=0$, $b\le2$, so $R\ge14$. For $n=3$: $8n=24$ leaves nothing, so $R\ge18$. For $n=2$ and $b\le3$ the bound gives $R>13$, so $R\ge14$ since $R$ is an integer. If $b=4$, there is no twist, $X\cong h^*R_7$ with $R_7$ of type $I_7,I_1,\mathrm{II},\mathrm{II}$, and $\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{MW}\ge10-2-6=2$. So $\rho\ge16$.

*$\ell=13$*: $14n\le24$ forces $n=1$. The fibres over the cusps and the $\nu_2=\nu_3=2$ elliptic points have total Euler number at least $13+1+3+3+2+2=24$. So the configuration is $I_{13}+I_1+2\,\mathrm{III}+2\,\mathrm{II}$, and $R=14$.

*$\ell=2$* ($\nu_2=1$, $\nu_3=0$: cusps of widths $1$ and $2$, one unramified elliptic point over $1728$, and every point over $j=0$ has ramification $3$, so no $\mathrm{II}$, $\mathrm{IV}$). The untwisted cusp fibres have Euler sum $3n$ and $\sum_A(2e_s-1)\ge n$. Let $a\le n$ be the number of $\mathrm{III}$ fibres; then $3a\le24-3n$, so $a\le\min(n,8-n)$ and $n\le8$. Hence
$$
R\ \ge\ n+a+\tfrac23(24-3n-3a)=16-n-a\ \ge\ 16-n-\min(n,8-n)\ \ge\ 8 .
$$
(Equivalently, a rational subgroup of order $2$ is a $2$-torsion section, and this is the bound of van Geemen–Sarti [25].) The enumeration confirms $R\ge8$.

*Constant $j$.* If $j\notin\{0,1728\}$, all fibres are $I_0$ or $I_0^*$, so there are four $I_0^*$ and $\rho\ge18$. If $j=0$ (resp. $1728$), the automorphism $(x,y)\mapsto(\omega x,y)$ (resp. $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,iy)$) acts on $\sigma$ by a primitive cube (resp. fourth) root of unity. So $E\supseteq\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ (resp. $\mathbb Q(i)$). $\square$

**Proof of Corollary D.** Every step induces a similarity, so all surfaces $A$ in a chain have $\rho(A)=\rho(S)$ and $E(A)\cong E(S)$, not CM. Steps (I), (Q), (F) and their transposes have classes in $\langle P(S)\rangle$ (R4), and $P(S)\subseteq\{2,3\}$ for $\rho\le15$. An (Iso) step of degree $\ell$, or its transpose (whose class is also $[\ell]$, since the adjoint of a similarity of multiplier $\ell$ has multiplier $\ell$), starts from a surface with a genus-one fibration with non-constant $j$. By Theorem C, $\ell\in\{2,3\}$ and $\rho\ge10$. So $\Sigma^+(S)\subseteq\langle2,3\rangle$, and for $\rho\le9$ no (Iso) step occurs. At $\rho=10$, the source $A$ of the map underlying an (Iso) step of degree $3$ (or of its transpose) has $\rho(A)=10$, and Theorem C(3) identifies the Jacobian. Conversely, if $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$ by a Hodge isometry, then an (I) step followed by the transpose of $\varphi_h$ is a chain of class $[3]$. $\square$

**Remark 7.3 ($\ell=5,7,13$).** At $\rho\ge16$ fibrewise $5$- and $7$-isogenies do exist, for example on the base changes $h^*R_5$, $h^*R_7$ by double covers $h$ ($2$-dimensional families) and on the examples of [6, §7.6–7.7]. We do not determine their Picard numbers or endomorphism fields. Van Geemen–Schütt [6, §7.6] report $1$-dimensional families with CM by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ from the same constructions. If $E$ is a CM field in which $\ell$ is a norm up to squares, a similarity of multiplier $\ell$ is the composition of a Hodge isometry with an element of $E$, and is algebraic by Buskin's theorem and the algebraicity of the CM action ([5, Rem. 7.3]). Whether fibrewise isogenies give new classes at $\rho\ge16$ is left open.

## 9. Limitations

- **A $6$-dimensional locus, not the whole period domain.** Theorem A proves $(\Psi_{\mathrm{ns}})$ for $[3]$ on the K3 surfaces Hodge similar (with class in $\langle2,3\rangle$) to the $6$-dimensional family $\{X_h\}$. Hodge theory predicts the same on the whole $10$-dimensional period domain of $V_3$, but we know no way to spread algebraicity from the family to it.
- **Very general.** $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$ is proved for very general $h$ only. For the explicit $X_{h_0}$ we prove $\rho=10$ but do not determine $E(X_{h_0})$. Theorem B assumes $\rho(X_h)=10$ and $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$, both true for very general members; for a member with $E=\mathbb Q$ and $\rho=22-r>10$ it gives only $k<r$ factors, and the determinant class at $k=r$ is not treated.
- **Imported results.** The Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces over finite fields [18], [19]. Good reduction of the minimal model when the fibre configuration is preserved mod $p$ is the standard input of van Luijk's method [20], and we use it as such. Also Zarhin [16], van Geemen [17], Nikulin [21], [22], Buskin [2], our earlier results (R3)–(R4), and classical facts on $X_0(\ell)$ [10] and elliptic surfaces [9].
- **What Theorem C does not cover.** It concerns fibrewise isogenies of genus-one fibrations. Rational maps of prime degree that preserve no genus-one fibration are not covered. Kummer-type maps exist only for $\rho\ge17$. Isotrivial fibrations with $j\in\{0,1728\}$ give CM.
- **Picard number $11$.** The isogeny exists on every member, including members with $\rho=11$ (for instance $h$ ramified over $t=0$, giving an $I_2$ fibre), where symmetry chains also cannot give $[3]$. We did not certify a member with $\rho$ exactly $11$.
- **Nothing** at $\rho\le9$, nothing on (D), and nothing new on $[5]$, $[7]$, $[13]$.

## 10. Reproducibility

`verify.py` is a single self-contained Python 3 script (sympy and numpy only, no files, no `__file__`, fixed seeds). It runs in about $30$ seconds and ends with `FALLOS = 0` (75 checks). The output is in `verify_output.txt`. It was tested with Python 3.13.16, SymPy 1.14.0 and NumPy 2.5.3. The finite-field traces are integer sums of quadratic characters. For speed, one step computes the counts by a floating-point FFT convolution over $(\mathbb Z/p)^k$ and rounds them to integers. This step is certified in two ways: the script records the largest distance of any convolution value from an integer before rounding (at most $3.4\cdot10^{-12}$, checked to be $<10^{-6}$), and the fast traces agree with an exact naive double loop on $\mathbb F_{7^k}$, $k\le3$, and with the independent Maple computation for $k=1,2$. The quadratic-form routines are reused from the scripts of [3], [4], [5]. `comprobar_v5.mpl` is an independent Maple worksheet (Maple 12 compatible). It rechecks the identities of §§4–5, the traces over $\mathbb F_7$ and $\mathbb F_{49}$ with its own arithmetic, the functional equation, irreducibility and absolute values of the roots, the lattice determinants and the numerical bounds of §7. It was run by the operator, and all 29 outputs agree with the expected values and with `verify.py`.
