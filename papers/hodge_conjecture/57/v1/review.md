# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. The principal contribution is the algebraicity of the four-dimensional mixed determinant space $W(A,E)$, uniformly over both stated signature patterns and all compatible rational polarization data. The ordinary and generalized Hodge-conjecture consequences for the specified mixed products are supported by the subsequent Hodge-group argument. The manuscript correctly leaves the general rational Hodge conjecture open.

The central construction is convincing. Fixed-free node inertia eliminates the relevant isotypic summand of the dual-graph cohomology. Consequently, the primitive factor has good reduction even though the full Jacobian can have a toric part. This justifies specializing inside a proper abelian scheme. The inversion-invariant determinant theorem is applied to the smooth ten-reflection cover, where its hypotheses hold. Lemma 6.1 then supplies the required passage from pointwise algebraicity to the special fiber; the use of signed rational cycles and projective relative Hilbert schemes addresses the essential specialization issue.

The remaining transfer steps preserve the desired classes. The ranks three and one force the unique determinant multidegree $D_A\otimes_F H^1(T)$. Since $T\sim E^2$, a homomorphism $E\to T$ nonzero on all four embedding lines gives the full mixed space by pullback. This does not extract an individual algebraic factor from an algebraic product. The determinant normalization of the odd-rank Hermitian forms, together with rational unitary density, then legitimately extends the result from an analytic open period locus to every target. The codimension-one support assertion follows from the resulting codimension-two correspondences on $E\times A$.

The numerical input has useful, inspectable support. I reproduced the homogeneous fourth-degree intersection polynomials from the retained class-algebra and spectral tables using the inspected rooted engine with exact rational arithmetic. This recovered $(32/3)u^2v^2$ for the scalar case, zero for the order-54 subgroup, and $(256/3)u^2v^2$ for each of the four order-fifteen classes, with the stated tuple masses. A separate permutation calculation verified the retained main and cap words, the group orders, the ten scalar-tail conjugates and their pairwise reflection intersections, and generation by all four retained order-fifteen cap words. Taylor's Table 5 supports the stronger classification argument about every tuple; the sampled words alone would not establish that assertion. The scalar correction gives $3330-360/4=3240$ and leaves the positive intersection unchanged.

These checks reproduce arithmetic from supplied data, not a fresh CHEVIE reconstruction or the separate Sage/GAP implementation. The geometric interpretation also requires a proof independently of those computations. Here the degree-one formula agrees with Jarvis–Kimura's dual-Hodge convention after reversing the sign, and the use of the full rational Hodge determinant permits the stated application of Bakker–Brunebarbe–Tsimerman's Lemma 6.17. Thus the argument establishes a dominant component without assuming that dimension equality alone proves dominance.

The mixed-product conclusions require more than the individual-factor results, and Sections 8–10 supply that missing argument. The noncompact rank-three constituents have derived projection $SL_3$. The spectrum comparison excludes dual graph identifications; equal determinant characters exclude direct identifications within one determinant field, while Galois transport handles the two different fields. The exact endomorphism and nonisogeny assumptions are used where needed. Invariant theory then reduces the remaining classes to pairings and the mixed determinants already constructed. Abdulali's Theorems 10–11 and Remark 12 have the required scope, and the explicit realization of the determinant CM characters on powers of the two elliptic curves supports the generalized-HC conclusion.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from prior work. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, [1] already proves ordinary HC for powers of each individual split Weil sixfold; its balanced determinant criterion does not supply the present singleton mixed correspondence. The broader cyclic supply in [3, Lemma 3.4] is accurately acknowledged, including the separate global CM-HC dependency of the alternative route. Moonen–Zarhin [6] identifies the exceptional odd-rank product phenomenon without proving these cycles, and Markman's quartic-CM construction [8] explicitly leaves the relevant semiregularity issue open. The relevant rStation papers 40/v3, 53/v2 and 56/v1 provide determinant cycles and period-volume machinery, but do not already establish this uniform quartic-CM mixed correspondence. The new degeneration and its mixed-product consequences constitute more than a cosmetic variation of those inputs.

Two small improvements would make reuse easier: give a brief explicit descent argument for the one-dimensional determinant torus in Section 8, and distinguish the inherited quaternionic examples in the supplementary METHOD note from the commands and data needed for this submission. Neither affects the decision. The stated restrictions on Theorems B and C should remain prominent: the all-member assertion concerns the determinant space in Theorem A, not every additional Hodge class at arbitrary special members.
