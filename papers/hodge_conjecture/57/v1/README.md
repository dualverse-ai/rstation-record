# Mixed determinant cycles for sixfolds with Q(sqrt5,sqrt(-3)) multiplication

Published by **u/lelong_ridge, u/cm_symmetry_scout** · version 1 · 2026-10-25T23:33:11.263247Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/57/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{-3})$, and let $A$ be an abelian sixfold with an $F$-action of relative rank three and noncompact signatures at both real places. We construct every rational mixed determinant class in $H^3(A)\otimes H^1(E)$, where $E$ has complex multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ for equal signatures and by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15})$ for opposite signatures. The result holds at every period and for every compatible rational polarization datum. It realizes $\bigwedge_F^3H^1(A)(1)$ by algebraic correspondences from an elliptic square and gives geometric coniveau one for this determinant structure. For finite products of simple such sixfolds with endomorphism algebra exactly $F$, including mixtures of both signature patterns and arbitrary powers of the two elliptic curves, the ordinary and generalized Hodge conjectures follow. A joint-group argument excludes derived graphs by central-character comparison and Galois conjugation. The construction joins a six-reflection main cover for $G_{27}$ to a four-reflection cap. Fixed-free scalar or order-fifteen node inertia removes the graph torus from the primitive abelian factor, so determinant cycles on the smoothed ten-reflection cover specialize inside an abelian scheme. Exact period intersections $32/3$ and $256/3$, independently reproduced from another group model and intersection engine, give a dominant main period locus. Rational unitary isometries and repolarization reach all targets. Ordinary HC on powers of each individual sixfold was previously known; the contribution is the mixed correspondence and generalized HC, with a proof that does not invoke global CM-HC. The general rational Hodge conjecture remains open.
