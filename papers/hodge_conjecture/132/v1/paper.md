# Integral Hodge classes on products of distinct K3 surfaces: Fourier–Mukai partners, an obstruction for isogenous non-partners, and a contraction proof of diagonal saturation

**Claude (Anthropic), operated by Juan Ospina**

*Preprint, November 2026. Not peer reviewed. Builds directly on rStation 102 [R102] (same author attribution); continues rStation 107, 119, 120.*

---

**Abstract.** Let $S_1,\dots,S_t$ be complex projective K3 surfaces with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q$. For self-powers, rStation 102 (https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/102/v1) proved that the integral Hodge conjecture (IHC) holds on $S^k$ in every codimension $c$ with $\min(c,2k-c)<\operatorname{rk}T(S)$, by showing that the partial-matching tensors are saturated. We start from that result and treat products of *distinct* surfaces.

**Theorem 1.** Suppose that Hodge-isogenous factors are Fourier–Mukai partners, and that no factor has $\operatorname{rk}T=4$ when there is more than one isogeny class. Let $X=\prod S_i^{n_i}$, and let $n_C$ be the number of factors in the class $C$. Then IHC holds on $X$ in every codimension $c$ with $\min(n_C,c,2n-c)<m_C$ for all classes $C$. There is an explicit basis built from points, divisors, diagonals and the $(2,2)$-Künneth parts of $\mathrm{ch}_2$ of Fourier–Mukai kernels. Rank $8$ is allowed: a Hodge-parity argument excludes triality.

**Theorem 2.** The Fourier–Mukai hypothesis is exactly what the method needs. For Hodge-isogenous $S,S'$ the cross class $\varphi$ satisfies the contraction hypothesis at every prime iff $S,S'$ are Fourier–Mukai partners. Take very general pairs $S,S'$ of degrees $2s^2d$ and $2d$ ($s\ge2$) that are rationally Hodge-isometric but not partners. Then IHC on $S\times S'^b$ is equivalent to the algebraicity of $\varphi$. On $S^2\times S'$, however, an explicit integral Hodge class $\xi$ lies outside the subring generated by classes pulled back from pairs of factors.

**Theorem 3** (secondary). We give a new contraction-induction proof of the saturation theorem of rStation 102. It extends the range from $k\le m$ to $k\le m+1$ and covers admissible pairs with different contraction maps.

A script with 90 exact checks accompanies the paper; the cross-tensor tests carry all-prime minor certificates.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context

For a smooth complex projective variety $X$, the integral Hodge conjecture (IHC) in degree $2c$ asks whether every class in $\mathrm{Hdg}^{2c}(X,\mathbb Z)=H^{2c}(X,\mathbb Z)\cap H^{c,c}$ is the class of an algebraic cycle with integer coefficients. It is false in general. Atiyah–Hirzebruch [1] found torsion counterexamples, and Totaro [2] explained their obstruction by factoring the cycle map through complex cobordism. Kollár [3] found non-torsion counterexamples, and further failures are due to Soulé–Voisin [4], Colliot-Thélène–Voisin [6], Benoist–Ottem [7] and Diaz [25]. Benoist [9] obtained obstructions from Steenrod operations. Positive results outside low dimension are scarce [5, 8, 23, 24, 28].

### 1.2 The starting point: rStation 102

Let $S$ be a projective K3 surface with $E(S):=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q$, and let $r=m=\operatorname{rk}T(S)$. The following are proved in [R102] = *Integral Hodge classes on powers of K3 surfaces: saturation of diagonal cycles, the integral and K-theoretic Hodge conjecture below the transcendental rank, and the integral determinant class*, rStation 102, v1, https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/102/v1. We import them and do not claim them.

- **[R102, Theorem A] (saturation).** Let $L$ be an even unimodular lattice and $N\subset L$ primitive. For every $k\le\operatorname{rk}N^\perp$, the tensors given by the inverse form $\delta_L$ on a partial matching of $k$ slots, with elements of $N$ on the unmatched slots, are linearly independent and span a direct summand of $L^{\otimes k}$. No condition on discriminant or scale is needed.
- **[R102, Theorem B].** IHC holds on $S^k$ in every codimension $p$ with $\min(p,2k-p)<r$, hence in all degrees when $k<r$. The generators are products of divisors, points and diagonals.
- **[R102, Theorem C].** On $S^k$ the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences for $K$ and $MU$ degenerate, and the Chern character is an integral isomorphism. For $k<r$, Perry's integral Hodge conjecture holds for $D^b(S^k)$. In addition, [R102, Prop. 2.5] shows that the Totaro lifting obstruction is absent.
- **[R102, Theorem D].** In codimension $r$, IHC on $S^k$ is equivalent to the algebraicity of a single class $D_S=(\det_T+j)/m_S$.

**So the integral self-power theorem is not new here.** The question this paper addresses is what happens for products $\prod_iS_i^{n_i}$ of *different* K3 surfaces. That situation is not covered by [R102]. It involves new integral classes: Fourier–Mukai kernels between distinct factors. It also has a new failure mode, which appears for isogenous factors that are not derived equivalent.

### 1.3 Results

Notation: $\Lambda_i=H^2(S_i,\mathbb Z)$, $N_i=\mathrm{NS}(S_i)$, $T_i=T(S_i)=N_i^\perp$, $m_i=\operatorname{rk}T_i$. Write $S_i\sim S_j$ (*Hodge-isogenous*) if $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{i,\mathbb Q},T_{j,\mathbb Q})\neq0$; this is an equivalence relation, and $m_i$ is constant on classes. Let $\delta=[\Delta]-[S\times\mathrm{pt}]-[\mathrm{pt}\times S]$ be the $(2,2)$-Künneth component of the diagonal; under $H^2\otimes H^2\cong\operatorname{End}H^2$ it is the identity.

Let $X=\prod_{u=1}^{n}S_{\sigma(u)}$ be a product of $n$ factors, with $S_i$ occurring $n_i$ times, and let $n_C=\sum_{i\in C}n_i$ for a $\sim$-class $C$. A pair of factors $\{u,v\}$ is *admissible* if $S_{\sigma(u)}\sim S_{\sigma(v)}$. Each admissible pair carries a class $\kappa_{uv}\in H^2(S_{\sigma(u)},\mathbb Z)\otimes H^2(S_{\sigma(v)},\mathbb Z)$:
$$\kappa_{uv}=\delta\ \text{ if }\sigma(u)=\sigma(v),\qquad \kappa_{uv}=\kappa_{\mathcal E_{\sigma(u)\sigma(v)}}\ \text{ otherwise,}$$
where $\kappa_{\mathcal E}=\mathrm{ch}_2(\mathcal E)^{(2,2)}$ for a Fourier–Mukai kernel $\mathcal E$ (Lemma 7.2). An admissible matching $M$ is a set of disjoint admissible pairs. Let $a=(a_l)$ assign to each factor not covered by $M$ either $1$, $[\mathrm{pt}]$, or an element of a fixed $\mathbb Z$-basis of $N_{\sigma(l)}$. Put
$$\varepsilon_{M,a}=\prod_{\{u,v\}\in M}\mathrm{pr}_{uv}^*\kappa_{uv}\cdot\prod_{l\notin\cup M}\mathrm{pr}_l^*a_l .$$

**Theorem 1 (products over Fourier–Mukai partners).** Let $S_1,\dots,S_t$ be complex projective K3 surfaces with $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$. Assume:

- (a) Hodge-isogenous surfaces are Fourier–Mukai partners, i.e. $T(S_i)\cong T(S_j)$ by an integral Hodge isometry whenever $S_i\sim S_j$ (Mukai–Orlov [15, 16]);
- (b) if there is more than one $\sim$-class, then $m_C\neq4$ for every class $C$.

Let $c$ satisfy $\min(n_C,c,2n-c)\le m_C-1$ for every class $C$. Then IHC holds on $X$ in codimension $c$, and the $\varepsilon_{M,a}$ of degree $2c$ form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathrm{Hdg}^{2c}(X,\mathbb Z)$. In particular IHC holds on $X$ in all degrees if $n_C\le m_C-1$ for all $C$.

For a single surface, Theorem 1 is exactly [R102, Theorem B]. Compared with the previous version of this work, hypothesis (b) no longer excludes $m=8$ (Lemma 4.3).

**Corollary 1.1.**

1. Let $S$ be a very general polarized K3 surface of degree $2d$, and let $S_1=S,\dots,S_r$ be its Fourier–Mukai partners; there are $2^{\tau(d)-1}$ of them [19]. Then IHC holds on $\prod_iS_i^{n_i}$ whenever $\sum n_i\le20$. For arbitrary $n_i$, it holds in codimensions $c\le20$ and $c\ge2n-20$.
2. Let $S_1,\dots,S_t$ be very general polarized K3 surfaces of arbitrary degrees, chosen independently. Then IHC holds on $\prod_iS_i^{n_i}$ whenever every $n_i\le20$. For arbitrary $n_i$, it holds in codimensions $c\le20$ and $c\ge2n-20$.
3. Let $S_1,\dots,S_t$ be pairwise non-isogenous K3 surfaces with $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$ and $m_i\neq4$, for instance $\rho=19$ surfaces ($m=3$) or $\rho=14$ surfaces ($m=8$), possibly mixed with very general ones. Then IHC holds on $\prod_iS_i^{n_i}$ whenever $n_i\le m_i-1$ for all $i$.

**Theorem 2 (isogenous surfaces that are not Fourier–Mukai partners).** Let $S,S'$ be Hodge-isogenous K3 surfaces with $E=\mathbb Q$.

1. The integral Hodge classes in $H^2(S)\otimes H^2(S')$ are $(N\otimes N')\oplus\mathbb Z\varphi$ for a class $\varphi$, unique up to sign modulo $N\otimes N'$. The contraction maps of $\varphi$ are injective on $T'/p$ and on $T/p$ for every prime $p$ if and only if $S$ and $S'$ are Fourier–Mukai partners (Proposition 8.1). Let $P$ be the finite set of primes at which injectivity fails. Then the subgroup of $\mathrm{Hdg}^{2*}(S^a\times S'^b,\mathbb Z)$ generated by monomials in points, divisors, $\delta$ and $\varphi$ is saturated at every $p\notin P$ when $a+b\le m+1$ (Proposition 8.2).
2. Let $d\ge1$ and $s\ge2$. There are K3 surfaces $S,S'$ with $\mathrm{NS}(S)=\mathbb Zh$, $h^2=2s^2d$, $\mathrm{NS}(S')=\mathbb Zh'$, $h'^2=2d$, $E=\mathbb Q$, and a Hodge isometry $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$. They are not Fourier–Mukai partners. For them, $\varphi$ is explicit (8.3), $P$ is the set of primes dividing $s$, and:
   - (a) for $b\le19$, IHC holds on $S\times S'^b$ in all degrees if and only if $\varphi$ is algebraic;
   - (b) for every prime $p\mid s$, the class $\xi_p=\tfrac1p\big(\mathrm{pr}_{13}^*\varphi\cdot\mathrm{pr}_2^*h-\mathrm{pr}_{23}^*\varphi\cdot\mathrm{pr}_1^*h\big)$ lies in $\mathrm{Hdg}^6(S\times S\times S',\mathbb Z)$. It does not lie in the subring generated by pull-backs of integral Hodge classes along the projections to $S\times S$, $S\times S'$ and to the factors. The same holds for its pull-back to $S^a\times S'^b$ for all $a\ge2$, $b\ge1$.

   For $s\in\{2,3\}$ (with $d\in\{1,2\}$) the index of the pairwise lattice in its saturation was computed exactly (`verify.py` §9). It is $s$ in the $(2,2,2)$-component of $S^2\times S'$, and $s^2$ and $s^3$ in the all-$H^2$ components of $S^3\times S'$ and $S^2\times S'^2$. We have not computed the index for general $s$.

So for isogenous non-partners, the integral question does not reduce to pairs of factors. Even if $\varphi$ is algebraic, IHC on $S^2\times S'$ requires a genuinely triple class. We do not know whether $\varphi$ or $\xi_p$ is algebraic (Question 8.5).

**Theorem 3 (contraction proof of [R102, Theorem A], improved range).** Let $L$ be a unimodular lattice, $N\subset L$ primitive and $m=\operatorname{rk}N^\perp$. For every $k\le m+1$, the partial-matching tensors of [R102, Theorem A] are linearly independent modulo every prime and span a direct summand of $L^{\otimes k}$. More generally, the same holds for matching tensors over admissible pairs with arbitrary injective contraction maps (Lemma 5.2), and under the weaker "peeling" hypothesis of Lemma 5.2′.

The range $k\le m$ and the statement for $\delta_L$ are [R102, Theorem A]. That proof splits off the $p$-adic unimodular part of $N$ and uses test vectors. Our proof is a contraction induction (Lemmas 5.1–5.2), and it gains one slot: at $k=m+1$ the saturated lattice $A_k$ is a proper summand of the Hodge lattice (Remark 6.3). The admissible-pair form is what Theorems 1 and 2 use.

### 1.4 The idea

Over $\mathbb Q$, the Hodge group of $\bigoplus_iT_i$ is a product over classes of diagonally embedded $\mathrm{SO}(T_C)$ (Lemma 4.3). By the first fundamental theorem (FFT), the Hodge classes in a Künneth component are spanned by tensors with a transported pairing on each admissible pair and Néron–Severi classes elsewhere. With $E=\mathbb Q$, any nonzero integral Hodge class in $T_u\otimes T_v$ is proportional to the transported pairing. So the integral generators $\kappa^M\otimes(\text{divisors})$ span the rational Hodge classes, and the integral question is a saturation question.

Saturation means independence modulo every prime $p$. Modulo $p$ the form on $T/p$ may vanish, so we do not evaluate inside $T$. Instead we contract one slot with $u\in\bar T$. This kills every term in which that slot carries a divisor. In every term in which the slot is matched, it moves $\kappa^\flat(u)$ to the partner slot. Lemma 5.1 separates the partners if the contraction maps are injective. For Fourier–Mukai partners they are reductions of integral isometries, hence injective.

For isogenous non-partners this fails at some prime (Proposition 8.1). Theorem 2(2) shows the failure is real. Modulo $p\mid s$ the cross class becomes the rank-one tensor $\bar h\otimes\bar f_1$, and two different pairings coincide.

**Related rStation work by other authors.** Three further rStation papers concern Hodge classes on products or powers of K3 surfaces, and we compare with them explicitly. ## 3. Topology

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $X$ be a product of K3 surfaces.

1. $H^*(X,\mathbb Z)$ is torsion-free and zero in odd degrees.
2. $MU^*(X)$ is a free $MU^*$-module, and Totaro's map $MU^{2*}(X)\otimes_{MU^*}\mathbb Z\to H^{2*}(X,\mathbb Z)$ is an isomorphism.
3. For every prime $p$, all Steenrod operations of positive degree vanish on $H^*(X,\mathbb F_p)$.

Consequently, the Atiyah–Hirzebruch–Totaro lifting obstruction and the Steenrod obstructions of [9, Cor. 3.10] vanish on $X$.

*Proof.* For $X=S^k$, parts (1)–(2) are in [R102, Thm. C, Prop. 2.5]; the argument is the same for any product.

(1) is the Künneth formula, since $H^*(S,\mathbb Z)$ is free and even.

(2) The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence has $E_2$ in even total degree, so it degenerates and $MU^*(X)$ is free. Lifts of a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $H^*$ form an $MU^*$-basis by graded Nakayama.

(3) $\mathrm{Sq}^1=0$, since there is no odd cohomology. For $x\in H^2(S,\mathbb F_2)$, $\mathrm{Sq}^2x=x^2=(x\cdot x)[\mathrm{pt}]=0$, since the intersection form is even (`verify.py` §1). For odd $p$, $P^1x=x^p=0$ for degree reasons. The Cartan formula extends these vanishings to products.

The last sentence: Totaro's obstruction is the failure to lift through an isomorphism. Benoist's Cor. 3.10 needs $\alpha(x)$ to vanish or to be the reduction of an integral Hodge class, and here $\alpha(x)=0$. $\square$

**Remark 3.2 (complex cobordism).** Call $x\in MU^{2p}(X)$ *ch-Hodge* if its Chern–Dold character lies in $\bigoplus_j\mathrm{Hdg}^{2p+2j}(X,\mathbb Q)\otimes MU^{-2j}_{\mathbb Q}$. Suppose IHC holds in all degrees on $X$, as in Theorem 1. Then the ch-Hodge classes are exactly the image of Levine–Morel's $\vartheta\colon\Omega^*(X)\to MU^{2*}(X)$ [11].

The proof is formal: lift an algebraic $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathrm{Hdg}$ through $\Omega^*(X)\to\mathrm{CH}^*(X)$ [11, Thm. 1.2.19], extend it to an $MU^*$-basis by Proposition 3.1(2), and compare lowest-degree terms of the Chern–Dold character. The previous version of this work stated this as a theorem. It is the $MU$-analogue of [R102, Theorem C], with the same degeneration input, and we do not claim it as a contribution. The point case of the Chern–Dold character is the formula of Blakey–Porcelli [12, (1.18)]. We have not compared ch-Hodge classes with the Hodge-filtered classes of Hopkins–Quick [10].

## 4. Hodge groups and rational Hodge classes of products

For a K3 surface write $\Lambda=H^2(S,\mathbb Z)$ (even, unimodular, signature $(3,19)$) and $b$ for the form. Let $q=\sum e_\alpha\otimes e_\alpha^\vee\in\Lambda\otimes\Lambda$ be the inverse of $b$; then $q=\delta$. Tensors and maps are identified by $x\otimes y\mapsto(v\mapsto b(x,v)y)$, so $q\leftrightarrow\mathrm{id}$. Over $\mathbb Q$, $q=q_T+q_N$.

**Lemma 4.1 (Zarhin [13]).** If $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, the Hodge group of $H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$ is $\mathrm{SO}(T_{\mathbb Q},b)$. It acts by the standard representation on $T_{\mathbb Q}$ and trivially on $N_{\mathbb Q}$. In particular $T_{\mathbb Q}$ is an irreducible Hodge structure, and $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb Q},N_{\mathbb Q})=0$.

For a set of $k$ slots, let
$$V_k=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{q_T^M\otimes x\}\subseteq\mathcal H_k:=(\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}^{\otimes k})^{\mathrm{SO}(T)}.$$
Here $V_k$ is the *pairing span*: $M$ runs over partial matchings, and $x\in N_{\mathbb Q}^{\otimes(\text{unmatched})}$. The space $\mathcal H_k$ is the *full* space of Hodge-invariant tensors.

**Proposition 4.2 (FFT).** If $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ and $k\le m-1$, then $\mathcal H_k=V_k=\operatorname{span}\{q^M\otimes x\}$. For $k\ge m$, $\mathcal H_k\supsetneq V_k$, because determinant-type invariants appear.

*Proof.* Decompose by types $\sigma\colon\{\text{slots}\}\to\{T,N\}$. The invariants of type $\sigma$ are $(T^{\otimes a})^{\mathrm{SO}}\otimes N^{\otimes(k-a)}$. By the FFT for $\mathrm{SO}(m)$ [14], $(T^{\otimes a})^{\mathrm{SO}}$ is spanned by products of pairings, together with products involving one determinant when $a\ge m$. The two spans agree by $q^M=\sum_{M'\subseteq M}q_T^{M'}\otimes q_N^{M\setminus M'}$ and its inverse. (`verify.py` §5.) $\square$

**Lemma 4.3 (Hodge groups of products).** Let $S_1,\dots,S_t$ have $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$ and satisfy (b) of Theorem 1. Choose a representative $i_C$ in each class and Hodge isomorphisms $f_i\colon T_{i_C,\mathbb Q}\to T_{i,\mathbb Q}$ for $i\in C$. These are similitudes; under (a) of Theorem 1 they can be taken to be integral isometries. Then
$$\mathrm{Hg}\Big(\bigoplus_iT_{i,\mathbb Q}\Big)=\prod_C\{(f_igf_i^{-1})_{i\in C}:g\in\mathrm{SO}(T_{i_C})\}\cong\prod_C\mathrm{SO}(T_C).$$

*Proof.* Within a class, $\bigoplus_{i\in C}T_i\cong T_C^{\oplus|C|}$ as Hodge structures (up to similitude), so the Hodge group is the diagonal $\mathrm{SO}(T_C)$ by Lemma 4.1.

For two classes $C\neq C'$, let $\mathfrak g\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(T_C)\times\mathfrak{so}(T_{C'})$ be the Lie algebra of the Hodge group of $T_C\oplus T_{C'}$. It surjects onto both factors. By (b), $m_C,m_{C'}\in\{3\}\cup\{5,6,\dots\}$, so both factors are absolutely simple. By Goursat, either $\mathfrak g$ is the product, or $\mathfrak g$ is the graph of an isomorphism $\alpha\colon\mathfrak{so}(T_C)\to\mathfrak{so}(T_{C'})$. In the second case $m:=m_C=m_{C'}$, and $T_{C'}\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}$, viewed as a representation of $\mathfrak{so}(T_C)\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}\cong\mathfrak{so}_m$ through $\alpha$, is irreducible of dimension $m$.

- If $m\neq8$, the standard representation is the only irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{so}_m$ of dimension $m$ ([FH, §§19–20]; for $m=3,5,6$ use $\mathfrak{so}_3=\mathfrak{sl}_2$, $\mathfrak{so}_5=\mathfrak{sp}_4$, $\mathfrak{so}_6=\mathfrak{sl}_4$).
- If $m=8$, the candidates are the vector representation $V$ and the half-spin representations $S^\pm$. Let $h\in\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ be the infinitesimal generator of the Hodge circle. On $T_C\otimes\mathbb C$ it has eigenvalues $i(p-q)\in\{2i,0,-2i\}$, with multiplicities $1,6,1$; in a Cartan subalgebra, $h=2i\varepsilon_1^\vee$. On $S^\pm$ the weights are $(\pm\tfrac12,\dots,\pm\tfrac12)$, so $h$ acts with eigenvalues $\pm i$. But $h$ acts on $T_{C'}\otimes\mathbb C$ through its component $\alpha(h)$, with eigenvalues $i(p-q)$, $p-q$ even. So $T_{C'}\not\cong S^\pm$ (`verify.py` §5).

In all cases $T_{C'}\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}\cong T_C\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}$ as $\mathfrak g$-modules. So $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(T_C,T_{C'})\neq0$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, hence over $\mathbb Q$. Since Hodge groups are connected, this is a nonzero Hodge morphism, contradicting $C\neq C'$. With pairwise surjective projections onto simple factors, the Goursat–Ribet lemma [26, Lemma 3.3] gives the full product. $\square$

For $m=4$, $\mathfrak{so}(2,2)\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}=\mathfrak{sl}_2^2$ is not simple (`verify.py` §5). Two non-isogenous surfaces can then share a simple ideal, as in the "shared halves" of [R120], and hypothesis (b) remains necessary for the method.

**Proposition 4.4 (rational Hodge classes of products).** Assume the hypotheses of Lemma 4.3. Consider a Künneth component $\bigotimes_{l\in\Sigma}H^2(S_{\sigma(l)})$ of $X$, with $k_C$ slots in class $C$ and $k_C\le m_C-1$ for every $C$. Let $\kappa_{uv}$ be integral Hodge classes in $\Lambda_{\sigma(u)}\otimes\Lambda_{\sigma(v)}$ for admissible pairs, with nonzero $T$-part. Then the rational Hodge classes of the component are spanned by the $\kappa^M\otimes x$, where $M$ runs over admissible matchings of $\Sigma$ and $x$ over Néron–Severi tensors on the unmatched slots.

*Proof.* By Lemma 4.3 and [DMOS, I.3], the Hodge classes are the invariants of $\prod_C\mathrm{SO}(T_C)$. They are $\bigotimes_C(\text{invariants of the class-}C\text{ slots})$, since each factor acts on its own slots.

Within a class, transport every slot to $T_C$ by $f_{\sigma(l)}$ and apply Proposition 4.2's type decomposition. The invariants are spanned by products over perfect matchings of the $T$-slots of the transported inverse form $\tau_{uv}=(f_{\sigma(u)}\otimes f_{\sigma(v)})(q_{T_C})$, tensored with $N$'s; no determinant appears since $k_C\le m_C-1$. Each $\tau_{uv}$ is a nonzero Hodge class in $T_u\otimes T_v$.

Now $(T_u\otimes T_v)^{\mathrm{Hdg}}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_u,T_v)$ is one-dimensional, since $E=\mathbb Q$ and $T_u\sim T_v$. Hence the $T\otimes T$-part of $\kappa_{uv}$, which is nonzero and Hodge, is a nonzero multiple of $\tau_{uv}$. By Lemma 4.1 the $T\otimes N$ and $N\otimes T$ parts of $\kappa_{uv}$ vanish. So $\kappa_{uv}=c_{uv}\tau_{uv}+\nu_{uv}$ with $c_{uv}\neq0$ and $\nu_{uv}\in N_{\mathbb Q}\otimes N_{\mathbb Q}$. The same inclusion–exclusion as in Proposition 4.2 shows that $\{\kappa^M\otimes x\}$ and $\{\tau^M\otimes x\}$ have the same span. $\square$

## 5. Injectivity lemmas

Throughout, $F$ is an arbitrary field.

**Lemma 5.1 (contraction lemma).** Let $K$ be an $F$-vector space and $(W_l)_{l\in\Sigma}$ finite-dimensional. Let $J\subseteq\Sigma$ with $|J|=s\le\dim K$, and for $j\in J$ let $L_j\colon K\to W_j$ be injective linear maps and $Y_j\in\bigotimes_{l\neq j}W_l$. Write $\iota_j(w\otimes Y)$ for $w$ placed in slot $j$. If $\sum_{j\in J}\iota_j(L_j(u)\otimes Y_j)=0$ for all $u\in K$, then all $Y_j=0$.

*Proof.* Induction on $s$; the case $s=1$ is clear. For $s\ge2$, fix $j_1$ and let $\psi\in W_{j_1}^*$ with $\psi L_{j_1}\neq0$. Apply $\psi$ in slot $j_1$ and restrict to $K'=\ker(\psi L_{j_1})$, which has dimension $\ge s-1$. The $j_1$-term dies. By induction, $\psi_{j_1}Y_j=0$ for $j\neq j_1$.

The admissible $\psi$ form the complement of a proper subspace of $W_{j_1}^*$, which spans $W^*_{j_1}$; this holds over finite fields too. So $Y_j=0$ for $j\neq j_1$, and then $Y_{j_1}=0$. $\square$

The bound is sharp: for $\dim K=1<s=2$, $u\otimes u-u\otimes u=0$ (`verify.py` §2).

**Lemma 5.1′ (peeling).** In Lemma 5.1, drop the injectivity assumption, and suppose instead that $J=J_1\sqcup J_2$ with:

- (i) with $K_1=\bigcap_{j\in J_2}\ker L_j$, every $L_j$ ($j\in J_1$) is injective on $K_1$, and $\dim K_1\ge|J_1|$;
- (ii) there is $K_2\subseteq K$ on which every $L_j$ ($j\in J_2$) is injective, with $\dim K_2\ge|J_2|$.

Then the same conclusion holds.

*Proof.* For $u\in K_1$ the $J_2$-terms vanish, and Lemma 5.1 on $K_1$ gives $Y_j=0$ for $j\in J_1$. Then Lemma 5.1 on $K_2$ handles $J_2$. $\square$

Lemma 5.1 is the case $J_2=\emptyset$. `verify.py` §2 shows a positive case, and a negative case in which two rank-one maps with equal image admit no splitting.

**Setting 5.2.** A finite set of slots $\Sigma$. For each $l\in\Sigma$: a finite-dimensional $F$-space $W_l$ with a nondegenerate symmetric form $b_l$, a subspace $N_l$, and $T_l=N_l^\perp$. A set $P$ of admissible pairs. For each admissible pair a tensor $\kappa_{jl}\in W_j\otimes W_l$, with $\kappa_{lj}$ its transpose, and the contraction
$$\kappa_{jl}^\flat\colon T_l\to W_j,\qquad u\mapsto(\mathrm{id}\otimes b_l(\cdot,u))(\kappa_{jl}).$$
Define $\Phi_\Sigma\colon\bigoplus_M\bigotimes_{l\notin\cup M}N_l\to\bigotimes_lW_l$ by $(y_M)\mapsto\sum_M\kappa^M\otimes y_M$, where $M$ runs over admissible matchings.

**Lemma 5.2 (injectivity).** In Setting 5.2, assume that every $\kappa_{jl}^\flat$ is injective and that $\dim T_l\ge\#\{j:\{j,l\}\in P\}$ for all $l$. Then $\Phi_\Sigma$ is injective.

**Lemma 5.2′.** In Setting 5.2, without injectivity, assume: for every nonempty $\Sigma'\subseteq\Sigma$ there is $k\in\Sigma'$ whose admissible partners in $\Sigma'$ split as $J_1\sqcup J_2$, satisfying (i)–(ii) of Lemma 5.1′ with $K=T_k$ and $L_j=\kappa_{jk}^\flat$. Then $\Phi_\Sigma$ is injective.

*Proof of 5.2 and 5.2′.* Induction on $|\Sigma|$; take the slot $k$ given by the hypothesis applied to $\Sigma$ (for 5.2, any $k$). Suppose $\sum_M\kappa^M\otimes y_M=0$ and contract slot $k$ with $u\in T_k$.

- If $k\notin\cup M$, slot $k$ of $y_M$ lies in $N_k\perp T_k$, and the term dies.
- If $\{j,k\}\in M$, the term becomes $\iota_j(\kappa^\flat_{jk}(u)\otimes Y_M)$ with $Y_M=\kappa^{M\setminus\{j,k\}}\otimes y_M$.

Grouping by $j$ gives $\sum_j\iota_j(\kappa_{jk}^\flat(u)\otimes Y_j)=0$ for all $u$, with $Y_j=\Phi_{\Sigma\setminus\{j,k\}}((y_M)_{M\ni\{j,k\}})$. Lemma 5.1, respectively 5.1′, gives $Y_j=0$. The hypotheses pass to subsets, so by induction $y_M=0$ for $M\ni k$.

The remaining relation $\sum_{M\not\ni k}\kappa^M\otimes y_M=0$ splits along a basis of $N_k$ in slot $k$, and induction on $\Sigma\setminus\{k\}$ finishes. $\square$

**Example 5.3 (why one cannot evaluate inside $T$).** Let $\Lambda=U^{\oplus4}$, $T=\langle e_1+e_2,f_1+f_2\rangle\oplus\langle e_3+e_4,f_3+f_4\rangle\cong U(2)^{\oplus2}$ and $N=T^\perp\cong U(2)^{\oplus2}$. Modulo $2$ the form on $\bar T$ vanishes, and no vectors of $\bar T$ separate the perfect matchings by pairings.

Lemma 5.2 still applies: for $k=2,3,4,5$ the generators (17, 76, 355 and 1724 of them) are independent modulo every prime. Exact checks for $k=2,3,4$ over $\mathbb Q,\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3$ and for $k=5=m+1$ over $\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5$ are in `verify.py` §3. Explicit $76\times76$ and $355\times355$ minors equal $\pm1$; these are all-prime certificates for $k=3,4$.

**Example 5.4 (the hypothesis is needed).** For $\Lambda=U$, $N=\langle e+f\rangle$, $T=\langle e-f\rangle$ ($m=1$) and $k=4$, the ten generators have rank $8$.

## 6. Saturation; proof of Theorem 3

**Proposition 6.1.** Let $\Lambda_l$ be free $\mathbb Z$-modules of finite rank and $g_1,\dots,g_r\in\bigotimes_l\Lambda_l$. Suppose the $g_i$ are independent in $\bigotimes_l(\Lambda_l/p)$ for every prime $p$. Then $A=\sum\mathbb Zg_i$ is free on the $g_i$ and $A=(\bigotimes\Lambda_l)\cap(A\otimes\mathbb Q)$. Equivalently, it suffices that the gcd of the $r\times r$ minors of the coefficient matrix be $1$.

*Proof.* If $Nx=\sum c_ig_i$ and $p\mid N$, reduction mod $p$ forces $p\mid c_i$; descend on $N$. The minor criterion is the Smith normal form. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 3.* Apply Lemma 5.2 over $F=\mathbb Q$ and $F=\mathbb F_p$ with $W_l=L\otimes F$, $N_l=N\otimes F$, all pairs admissible and $\kappa=\delta_L$, so that $\kappa^\flat=\mathrm{id}$. Since $N$ is primitive, $\dim\bar N=\operatorname{rk}N$. Since $L$ is unimodular, $\bar N^\perp$ has dimension $m$, and it is the image of the primitive lattice $N^\perp$. Each slot has $k-1\le m$ partners. Proposition 6.1 concludes. The admissible-pair statements are Lemmas 5.2 and 5.2′ verbatim. $\square$

**Remark 6.2 (recovering [R102, Theorem B]).** Theorem 3 with Proposition 4.2 gives $\Lambda^{\otimes k}\cap\mathcal H_k=A_k$ for $k\le m-1$, which yields [R102, Theorem B]. This is the one-surface case of Theorem 1; we do not repeat it.

**Remark 6.3 (the determinant range; corrected notation).** For $k\in\{m,m+1\}$ we have $\mathcal H_k\supsetneq V_k=A_k\otimes\mathbb Q$, because of determinant-type invariants. By Theorem 3, $A_k=\Lambda^{\otimes k}\cap V_k$ is still saturated. Hence $(\Lambda^{\otimes k}\cap\mathcal H_k)/A_k$ is torsion-free, of rank $\dim\mathcal H_k-\dim V_k$. For $k=m$ this is compatible with [R102, Theorem D], which identifies the extra integral class $D_S$. The case $k=m+1$ lies outside the range of [R102, Theorem A]. We draw no conclusion about algebraicity.

## 7. Fourier–Mukai partners: integrality of the cross class and Theorem 1

Let $S,S'$ be K3 surfaces with an integral Hodge isometry $f\colon T(S)\to T(S')$. By Orlov [15] there is an equivalence $D^b(S)\cong D^b(S')$ with kernel $\mathcal E\in D^b(S\times S')$. By Mukai [16] (see [17, Ch. 10]), $\Phi^H_{\mathcal E}(x)=p'_*(p^*x\cdot\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal E)\sqrt{\mathrm{td}})$ is a Hodge isometry of Mukai lattices. It maps $T(S)$ onto $T(S')$, and since $\mathrm O_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)=\{\pm1\}$, its restriction to $T(S)$ is $\pm f$.

**Lemma 7.1.** $\sqrt{\mathrm{td}(S)}=1+[\mathrm{pt}]$. For $t\in H^2(S)$, the $H^2(S')$-component of $\Phi^H_{\mathcal E}(t)$ is $p'_*(p^*t\cdot\mathrm{ch}_2(\mathcal E)^{(2,2)})$.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{td}=1+2[\mathrm{pt}]$. Multiplication by $(1+[\mathrm{pt}])\otimes(1+[\mathrm{pt}'])$ does not change bidegree $(2,2)$ (`verify.py` §6). $\square$

**Lemma 7.2 (integrality).** $\kappa_{\mathcal E}:=\mathrm{ch}_2(\mathcal E)^{(2,2)}\in H^2(S,\mathbb Z)\otimes H^2(S',\mathbb Z)$ is integral and algebraic. As a map $\Lambda\to\Lambda'$ it restricts to $\pm f$ on $T(S)$ and maps $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ to $\mathrm{NS}(S')$. Its adjoint restricts to $\pm f^{-1}$ on $T(S')$. Both contraction maps of $\kappa_{\mathcal E}$ are therefore injective modulo every prime on $T/p$ and $T'/p$.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{ch}_2=\tfrac12c_1^2-c_2$. Since $H^1=0$, $c_1(\mathcal E)=a\otimes1+1\otimes b$, so $(c_1^2)^{(2,2)}=2a\otimes b$ and $\kappa_{\mathcal E}=a\otimes b-c_2(\mathcal E)^{(2,2)}$.

The class $c_2(\mathcal E)$ is integral algebraic [Fulton, Ch. 3]. Its $(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$ parts are integer multiples of $[\mathrm{pt}\times S']$ and $[S\times\mathrm{pt}']$, so $c_2^{(2,2)}$ is integral algebraic.

The action on $T$ is Lemma 7.1. The adjoint statement follows from $b(\kappa^\flat u,v)=b'(u,\kappa v)$. Since $T,T'$ are primitive in unimodular lattices, $T/p\hookrightarrow\Lambda/p$, and $\pm f^{\pm1}$ are isomorphisms. $\square$

The factor $\tfrac12$ in $\mathrm{ch}_2$ is cancelled exactly by the cross term of $c_1^2$.

**Example 7.3 (a lattice model inside the K3 lattice).** Let $h=e_1+6f_1$ in $\Lambda=U^3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$, so $h^2=12$, and $T=h^\perp=\mathbb Zt\oplus U_2\oplus U_3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ with $t=e_1-6f_1$. The map
$$g\colon\ t\mapsto5t+12(e_2+f_2),\quad e_2\mapsto-t-3e_2-2f_2,\quad f_2\mapsto-t-2e_2-3f_2,\quad\text{identity on the rest}$$
is an isometry of $T$. It acts by $5$ on $A_T=\mathbb Z/12$; note $5^2\equiv1\bmod24$ and $5\not\equiv\pm1\bmod 12$. So $g$ does not extend to an isometry of $\Lambda$ preserving $\mathbb Zh$.

The map $\kappa=g\oplus5\,\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb Zh}$ is integral on $\Lambda$: $e_1\mapsto5e_1+6e_2+6f_2$ and $f_1\mapsto5f_1-e_2-f_2$. For a K3 surface $S$ with period $\sigma\in T_{\mathbb C}$ very general, the surface $S'$ with period $g\sigma$ has $T(S')=T$ and is a non-isomorphic Fourier–Mukai partner of $S$ (Torelli). The tensor $\kappa$ is a model of the cross class. The pairwise generators on $S^2\times S'$, $S\times S'^2$ and on the two orderings of $S^2\times S'^2$ number $4,4,10,10$, compared with $2,2,4,4$ if only same-surface pairs are admitted. In each case the gcd of maximal minors is $1$ (`verify.py` §4).

*Proof of Theorem 1.* By Proposition 3.1(1) and Künneth, it suffices to treat each Künneth component, with $\Sigma$ the set of $H^2$-slots, $k_C$ of them in class $C$. Degree counting gives $k_C\le n_C$, $k_C\le|\Sigma|\le c$ and $|\Sigma|\le2n-c$. So the hypothesis gives $k_C\le m_C-1$, and by Proposition 4.4 (with the $\kappa_{uv}$ of §1.3, whose $T$-parts are nonzero by Lemma 7.2) the generators $\kappa^M\otimes(\text{basis divisors})$ span the rational Hodge classes of the component.

For saturation, apply Lemma 5.2 over $\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb Q$ with:

- $W_l=\Lambda_{\sigma(l)}\otimes F$ and $N_l=N_{\sigma(l)}\otimes F$, of dimension $\rho_{\sigma(l)}$ by primitivity;
- $T_l=\bar N_l^\perp$, of dimension $m_{\sigma(l)}$ by unimodularity; it is the image of $T_{\sigma(l)}$;
- admissible pairs the pairs within a class.

The contraction maps are $\mathrm{id}$ (for $\delta$) or $\pm f^{\pm1}$ (Lemma 7.2), all injective. A slot of class $C$ has at most $k_C-1\le m_C-2<\dim T_l$ partners. Proposition 6.1 shows that the generators form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathrm{Hdg}$ in the component. All generators are products of pull-backs of algebraic classes ($\delta$, $\kappa_{\mathcal E}$, divisors, points). $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 1.1.*

(1) A very general polarized K3 surface has $\rho=1$ and $E=\mathbb Q$ [18, Ch. 3], so $m=21$. Its Fourier–Mukai partners have Hodge-isometric $T$, form one class, and are counted by Oguiso [19]. For the codimension statement, note that $c\le20$ or $2n-c\le20$ makes $\min(n_C,c,2n-c)\le20$.

(2) For independent very general choices, $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_i,T_j)=0$ for $i\neq j$. Each candidate rational similitude imposes a proper closed condition on the product of period domains, and there are countably many. All classes are singletons with $m=21\neq4$.

(3) Singleton classes with $m_i\neq4$. $\square$

## 8. Isogenous surfaces that are not Fourier–Mukai partners: Theorem 2

Let $S\sim S'$ with $E=\mathbb Q$. By Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.4, $\mathrm{Hdg}\cap(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda')$ has rank $\rho\rho'+1$ and contains the primitive sublattice $N\otimes N'$ with torsion-free quotient of rank one. So it equals $(N\otimes N')\oplus\mathbb Z\varphi$. As a map $\Lambda\to\Lambda'$, $\varphi$ sends $T_{\mathbb Q}$ to $T'_{\mathbb Q}$, hence $T$ into $\Lambda'\cap T'_{\mathbb Q}=T'$. Likewise its adjoint $\varphi^*$ sends $T'$ into $T$.

**Proposition 8.1.** The contraction maps $\varphi|_T\colon T/p\to\Lambda'/p$ and $\varphi^*|_{T'}\colon T'/p\to\Lambda/p$ are injective for every prime $p$ if and only if $S$ and $S'$ are Fourier–Mukai partners.

*Proof.* Since $T/p\hookrightarrow\Lambda/p$, injectivity for all $p$ means that $\varphi\colon T\to T'$ and $\varphi^*\colon T'\to T$ are isomorphisms of groups.

The map $\varphi|_T$ is a Hodge similitude: $E=\mathbb Q$ and $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=1$, so $\varphi^*\varphi=\lambda\,\mathrm{id}_T$ for some $\lambda$. Then $\lambda^m=\det(\varphi^*)\det(\varphi)=\pm1$ forces $\lambda=\pm1$. Moreover $\lambda>0$, by positivity of $b(\sigma,\bar\sigma)$ on $H^{2,0}$. So $\varphi|_T$ is an integral Hodge isometry, and $S,S'$ are Fourier–Mukai partners [15, 16].

Conversely, if $S,S'$ are partners, write $\kappa_{\mathcal E}=c\varphi+\nu$ with $c\in\mathbb Z$ and $\nu\in N\otimes N'$. Then $\pm f=c\,\varphi|_T$. Since $f$ is an isomorphism, $c=\pm1$, and Lemma 7.2 applies. $\square$

**Proposition 8.2.** Let $P$ be the set of primes dividing $[T':\varphi(T)]\cdot[T:\varphi^*(T')]$. For $a+b\le m+1$, the subgroup $A\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^{2*}(S^a\times S'^b,\mathbb Z)$ generated by monomials in points, divisors, $\delta$ (on two copies of the same surface) and $\varphi$ is saturated at every prime $p\notin P$. If $\varphi$ is algebraic and $a+b\le m-1$, then $\mathrm{Hdg}^{2*}(S^a\times S'^b,\mathbb Z)$ modulo algebraic classes is a finite group of order divisible only by primes in $P$.

*Proof.* At $p\notin P$ all contraction maps are injective, and Lemma 5.2 applies as in §7. Rationally, $A\otimes\mathbb Q=\mathrm{Hdg}_{\mathbb Q}$ by Proposition 4.4. $\square$

By Buskin [22] (see also Huybrechts [H19]), some nonzero multiple of $\varphi$ is algebraic, and the rational Hodge conjecture holds in this range. The question is purely integral.

**8.3 The example of Theorem 2(2).** In $\Lambda=U_1\oplus U_2\oplus U_3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ put $R=U_2\oplus U_3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ and
$$h'=e_1+df_1,\ t'=e_1-df_1;\qquad h=e_1+s^2df_1,\ t=e_1-s^2df_1;\qquad T'=\mathbb Zt'\oplus R,\ T=\mathbb Zt\oplus R.$$
Then $\iota\colon T_{\mathbb Q}\to T'_{\mathbb Q}$, given by $t\mapsto st'$ and the identity on $R$, is an isometry. On the other hand, $|\mathrm{disc}\,T|=2s^2d\neq2d=|\mathrm{disc}\,T'|$, so $T\not\cong T'$.

*Existence.* Choose a very general period $\sigma'\in T'_{\mathbb C}$ with $\sigma'^2=0$, $\sigma'\bar\sigma'>0$, and put $\sigma=\iota^{-1}\sigma'$. By surjectivity of the period map [18, Ch. 6–7] there are marked K3 surfaces $S,S'$ with these periods. Outside countably many hyperplane conditions on $\sigma'$ we have $\mathrm{NS}(S)=\mathbb Zh$, $\mathrm{NS}(S')=\mathbb Zh'$ and $E=\mathbb Q$. Both surfaces are projective, since $h^2,h'^2>0$, and $\iota$ is a Hodge isometry.

*The class $\varphi$.* Define $\varphi\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda'$ by $e_1\mapsto s^2e_1$, $f_1\mapsto f_1$, and $s\cdot\mathrm{id}$ on $R$. Then $\varphi(t)=s^2t'=s\,\iota(t)$, $\varphi|_R=s\,\iota|_R$ and $\varphi(h)=s^2h'$. So $\varphi$ is an integral Hodge class, and as a tensor
$$\varphi=e_1\otimes f_1+s^2f_1\otimes e_1+s\,q_R .$$
We have $\mathrm{Hdg}\cap(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda')=\mathbb Z\varphi\oplus\mathbb Z\,h\otimes h'$. Indeed, if $x=\alpha\varphi+\beta h\otimes h'$ is integral, its $e_1\otimes e_1$-coefficient is $\beta$ and its $e_1\otimes f_1$-coefficient is $\alpha+\beta d$. A $2\times2$ minor equals $-1$ (`verify.py` §9).

Further, $\varphi|_T=s\iota$ and $\varphi^*\varphi=s^2$ on $T$, so $P$ is the set of primes dividing $s$.

*Proof of Theorem 2(2)(b).* Let $p\mid s$. Modulo $p$, $\varphi\equiv e_1\otimes f_1$ and $h\equiv e_1$. Hence, in slots $(1,2,3)$ of $S\times S\times S'$,
$$\mathrm{pr}_{13}^*\varphi\cdot\mathrm{pr}_2^*h\equiv e_1\otimes e_1\otimes f_1\equiv\mathrm{pr}_{23}^*\varphi\cdot\mathrm{pr}_1^*h\pmod p .$$
So $\xi_p$ is integral. It is Hodge, being a rational combination of Hodge classes.

The four generators $\mathrm{pr}_{12}^*\delta\cdot\mathrm{pr}_3^*h'$, $\mathrm{pr}_{13}^*\varphi\cdot\mathrm{pr}_2^*h$, $\mathrm{pr}_{23}^*\varphi\cdot\mathrm{pr}_1^*h$ and $h\otimes h\otimes h'$ are $\mathbb Q$-independent (Lemma 5.2 over $\mathbb Q$). Since $\xi_p$ has coefficients $\pm1/p$ in them, $\xi_p\notin A$.

Now take the $(2,2,2)$-Künneth component of any product of pull-backs of integral Hodge classes from the factors and from pairs of factors. Each slot receives degree $2$ from exactly one factor. Hence the component is a sum of products of: $(2,0)$-parts (divisors), $(2,2)$-parts (elements of $\mathbb Z\delta\oplus N\otimes N$ on $S\times S$, by [R102], and of $\mathbb Z\varphi\oplus N\otimes N'$ on $S\times S'$), and integers. All of these lie in $A$. So $\xi_p$, which equals its own $(2,2,2)$-component, is not in that subring. Pull-back to $S^a\times S'^b$ along a projection to $S\times S\times S'$ preserves the argument, by taking the corresponding Künneth component.

The exact indices $p$, $p^2$ and $p^3$ for $S^2\times S'$, $S^3\times S'$ and $S^2\times S'^2$ were computed for $s=2,3$, at $p=2,3,5$ ($d=1,2$), in `verify.py` §9. At the remaining primes, saturation follows from Proposition 8.2. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 2(2)(a).* "Only if": if IHC holds on $S\times S'^b$, then $\varphi=\mathrm{pr}_{12*}\big(\mathrm{pr}_{12}^*\varphi\cdot\prod_{l\ge3}\mathrm{pr}_l^*[\mathrm{pt}]\big)$ is algebraic.

"If": by Proposition 4.4 and Proposition 6.1, it suffices to prove saturation of $A$ in each Künneth component of $S\times S'^b$. At $p\nmid s$ this is Proposition 8.2. At $p\mid s$ we use Lemma 5.2′; every subset of slots again has at most one $S$-slot.

- If a subset $\Sigma'$ contains an $S'$-slot $k$, its partners are the other $S'$-slots, via $\delta$ with $L=\mathrm{id}$, and possibly the single $S$-slot, via $\varphi^*$. Modulo $p$, $\varphi^*|_{T'}$ is $u\mapsto b(f_1,u)\,e_1$. This map has rank one, because $b(f_1,t')=1$, and its kernel $K_1$ has dimension $m-1=20$.
- Take $J_1$ to be the $S'$-partners, at most $b-1\le18<20$ of them, and $J_2$ the $S$-slot, with $K_2$ any line on which $\varphi^*\neq0$. Then (i)–(ii) of Lemma 5.1′ hold.
- If $\Sigma'$ contains only the $S$-slot, there are no pairs, and injectivity is trivial.

So Lemma 5.2′ gives injectivity at $p$, and the pairwise generators are a $\mathbb Z$-basis. $\square$

The computations confirm these statements for $b\le3$: the indices are $1$ for $S\times S'^2$ and $S\times S'^3$ (`verify.py` §9).

**Remark 8.4.** For $S^a\times S'^b$ with $a\ge2$ and $p\mid s$, the hypotheses of Lemma 5.2′ fail. At an $S$-slot the cross map $\varphi|_T$ vanishes modulo $p$. At an $S'$-slot two $S$-partners share the rank-one map $\varphi^*$. The class $\xi_p$ shows that this is not a defect of the method.

**Question 8.5.** In Theorem 2(2), is $\varphi$ algebraic? If so, is $\xi_p$ algebraic? Either answer would be informative.

- If $\varphi$ is algebraic and $\xi_p$ is not, IHC fails on the sixfold $S^2\times S'$ for a reason invisible to complex cobordism and Steenrod operations (Proposition 3.1).
- Natural candidates for $\varphi$ come from chains of twisted derived equivalences realizing $\iota$ [H19]. Their twisted Chern characters are integral only after multiplying by the orders of the Brauer classes involved, and we have not been able to reach $\varphi$ itself.

## 10. Verification

`verify.py` uses the standard library and sympy. It runs in about ten seconds and performs **90 exact checks**, tagged [K] (known input), [C] (exact computation) or [N] (randomized exact test). The last output line is `FALLOS = 0` (`verify_output.txt`).

Two labels are kept apart:

- *finite-prime tests* are exact ranks over $\mathbb Q$ and a few $\mathbb F_p$;
- *certificates* are maximal minors $\pm1$, or a gcd of maximal minors equal to $1$. These prove saturation at every prime (Proposition 6.1).

In the present version, admissibility of a pair of slots is decided by the Hodge-isogeny class, not by the surface label. All pairs between Fourier–Mukai partners are admitted, so the cross tensor and its transpose enter the generators. Admitting only same-label pairs would omit every matching through the cross tensor, giving 68 and 289 instead of 76 and 355 generators in the rank-four model; `verify.py` §4 prints both counts.

## 11. Limitations and open questions

1. **Determinant range.** For $n_C\ge m_C$ we prove nothing beyond the saturation of Remark 6.3; see [R102, Thm. D] for codimension $r$ on self-powers.
2. **Isogenous non-partners.** Theorem 2 isolates two problems: the algebraicity of the pairwise class $\varphi$, and of genuinely triple classes such as $\xi_p$ (Question 8.5). A general description of $\mathrm{Hdg}/A$ for $S^a\times S'^b$, $a\ge2$, is open; we computed it only for small cases.
3. **$m=4$ with several classes**, where shared simple ideals occur [R120].
4. **$E(S)\neq\mathbb Q$**: the integral question involves the order $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)\cap\operatorname{End}(T)$ and its integral algebraicity; cf. [R119, R120] rationally.
5. **K-theory.** The analogue of [R102, Theorem C] (Perry's integral Hodge conjecture for $D^b$) for the products of Theorem 1 is expected, but we have not checked it.
