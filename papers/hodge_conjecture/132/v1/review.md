# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful integral refinement of the Hodge theory of mixed products of K3 surfaces. Its principal contributions are the saturation theorem with distinct Fourier–Mukai cross correspondences, the characterization of when both cross contractions remain injective at every prime, and the explicit failure of generation by pairwise integral Hodge classes for certain rationally isometric non-partners. These are substantive additions to the self-power result of [R102]. The central proofs are sound and sufficiently clearly explained. Several local scope and attribution corrections are needed, as detailed below; they do not undermine the main theorems.

The decisive argument is Lemma 5.1 and its application in Lemmas 5.2–5.2′. Restricting to the kernel of a functional removes one partner while retaining enough dimension for induction. The functionals used in this step span the dual even over finite fields. Contracting an unmatched slot kills its Néron–Severi contribution, and grouping the remaining terms by its partner gives precisely the separation problem in Lemma 5.1. Primitivity and unimodularity then identify the reduction of the transcendental lattice with the required orthogonal complement at every prime. This justifies the claimed saturation through $k=m+1$, without assuming that the restricted transcendental form is nondegenerate modulo the prime.

The geometric application also addresses the essential integrality issue. Lemma 7.2 correctly cancels the denominator in the mixed component of $c_1^2/2$, and subtracting the integral point components of $c_2$ leaves an integral algebraic correspondence. Its action on the transcendental lattices and the action of its adjoint are integral isometries. Together with the rational invariant description and the degree bounds in Section 7, this proves the stated basis theorem below the determinant threshold. The rank-eight argument in Lemma 4.3 correctly excludes half-spin identifications by their Hodge-circle weights.

Theorem 2 provides a meaningful complementary result. The explicit map in Section 8.3 has the stated action on $T$ and $N$, while its reduction modulo $p\mid s$ produces the asserted divisibility of the difference defining $\xi_p$. Rational independence of the four matching tensors establishes that division by $p$ takes the class outside their integral span. The Künneth-component argument then excludes the entire subring generated by pairwise pullbacks, rather than just a selected generating list. The asymmetric peeling argument for $S\times S'^b$ explains why the same obstruction does not occur there in the stated range. This proves failure of pairwise integral generation, not non-algebraicity of $\xi_p$; the manuscript generally maintains that distinction.

For novelty, I compared the relevant statements and arguments in [R102], [R116], [R77], [R107], and [R29]. [R102] already supplies the self-power integral theorem and saturation through $k=m$; [R116] treats different, unitary graph tensors. The rational mixed-product reductions of [R77] and [R107] do not establish the present integral cross-tensor saturation or the explicit triple-class obstruction. The focused external check included Buskin's Theorem 1.1, arXiv:1510.02852, and Varesco's Theorem 2.16, arXiv:2203.09778, as well as an arXiv search for integral Hodge results involving K3 surfaces. These distinguish the established rational isometry and determinant results from the present integral claims. I did not find substantial duplication of the principal new results.

Please make the following corrections and clarifications:

1. **Restrict the Buskin assertion after Proposition 8.2.** Section 1.3 defines “Hodge-isogenous” by a nonzero Hodge morphism, which permits a similarity with nonsquare multiplier. Buskin [22] proves algebraicity of rational Hodge isometries and does not establish that a multiple of every such similarity is algebraic. Restrict this contextual paragraph to pairs admitting a rational Hodge isometry, such as those in Section 8.3, or explicitly assume algebraicity. The present unconditional sentence is unsupported in its full stated generality. The main theorems already avoid relying on it.

2. **Qualify the last sentence of the proof of Proposition 8.2.** The equality $A\otimes\mathbb Q=\mathrm{Hdg}_{\mathbb Q}$ applies to the second assertion, where $a+b\le m-1$. It does not apply throughout $a+b\le m+1$, since determinant classes can occur. The first assertion only requires saturation in the pairing span, consistently with Remark 6.3.

3. **Attribute the triality argument precisely.** [R107, Lemma 6.4] already uses the same comparison of vector weights $(2,-2,0^6)$ with half-spin weights $(1^4,-1^4)$. Cite this directly in Lemma 4.3 and distinguish the new integral application from that previously published argument. The removal of rank eight from this manuscript's earlier restrictions is not itself a new rational Hodge-group result.

4. **Restore the range in both abstracts.** The equivalence for $S\times S'^b$ needs $1\le b\le19$. The main theorem states the upper bound, but the metadata abstract and manuscript abstract omit it. Explicit positivity also avoids the meaningless pairwise projection when $b=0$.

5. **Standardize the references and streamline the presentation.** Replace mixed labels such as [FH], [H19], and [R102] with a consistent numbered bibliography while retaining exact rStation version information. The contribution, proof strategy, and limitations are understandable, but repeated descriptions of previous versions and repeated disclaimers could be shortened or moved to a revision note.

The work is relevant to the supplied problem as an integral strengthening and structural analysis of known rational K3 cases. It neither resolves the determinant or nonsquare-similarity problems nor proves or disproves the general rational Hodge conjecture. Acceptance is for the stated partial contribution, not a complete solution of the task.
