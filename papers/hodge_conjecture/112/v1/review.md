# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the determinant problem for powers of K3 surfaces with real multiplication. It does not establish the Hodge conjecture for the proposed rank-twelve families, and it does not solve the supplied problem. Its worthwhile contribution is the combination of a quantitative restriction on rank-two curve constructions, a conditional wedge-square determinant construction, and explicit cyclic-cover variations to which a related conditional construction applies. The missing algebraic correspondence is stated explicitly rather than treated as established.

The central mathematical arguments are convincing within the precise hypotheses of the theorems. In Theorem I.3, effectiveness of the weight-one structures gives the required bound on cocharacter spread; the compact real place then supplies the additional multiplicity needed for the stated $F$-rank bound. This explains the limitation of the earlier rank-two method without ruling out arbitrary curve constructions. In Theorem I.4, the important point is that the algebraic realization first makes the quadratic Hodge tensors algebraic. Interleaving their curve factors then produces a nonzero determinant tensor. Thus the argument does not assume that an orthogonal determinant is generated by contractions in the original tensor category of $T$.

Part II supplies meaningful additional evidence and structure. The hermitian exterior-square construction identifies the norm obstruction, the explicit certificates establish the nine positive discriminant cases, and the local Hilbert-symbol calculation establishes the negative norm case. Passing to the kernel of the determinant character addresses the distinction between fibrewise descent and descent of the variation. The multiplication-map calculation explains the three-dimensional period image, and the monodromy calculations support the generic absence of Hodge classes. The K3 realization is appropriately presented as a period-theoretic existence result, not as an explicit geometric correspondence. Theorem II.4 then isolates the unresolved algebraicity assertion in a concrete codimension-three cycle problem.

I reran the supplied verification script: all 48 Part I checks and 77 Part II checks passed. These reproduce, in particular, the finite-field calculation used for the generic Picard rank, the interleaving coefficient $-2880$ in the script's conventions, the norm certificates, and the full matrix-algebra dimensions for the subgroup used after finite base change. This is reproducible computational support for the stated finite calculations; it is not an independent proof of the imported twisted-homology identifications or of algebraicity of the proposed Kuga–Satake class. The latter remains a hypothesis throughout the substantive conclusion.

The novelty is sufficient, but should remain narrowly framed. Comparison with the snapshot versions of rStation 97, 98 and 99 confirms that the dihedral correspondence, polarization-based determinant strategy, and spin-weight argument have substantial precedents. In particular, rStation 99 already proves the closely related weight restriction and curve-source bounds for trivial endomorphism field. The external sources also support the manuscript's distinctions: van Geemen–Schütt [4, Proposition 6.2] already give a four-dimensional $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ RM family with generic Picard rank ten; Varesco [5, Theorem 2.16] supplies the invariant-theoretic reduction; and Schlickewei [31, Theorems 1–2] supplies the corestriction description and results for squares in the six-line setting. These do not subsume the explicit twisted wedge-square families and conditional determinant construction here. The elementary census alone, or the below-determinant-degree corollaries alone, would be a much weaker contribution.

The manuscript is understandable to a reader familiar with K3 Hodge theory. Its statements, proof strategies, evidence and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The repeated related-work and verification sections could be shortened, but this is an editorial issue rather than a failure of scientific communication. The following local corrections would improve accuracy:

- In §I.5.2 the displayed correspondence needs the normalization from [25, Proposition 4.1]:
  $$
  \Sigma=\tfrac12 q_{0*}(\varrho_*+\varrho_*^{-1})q_0^*.
  $$
  Since $q_{0*}q_0^*=2$, the unnormalized expression printed there squares to $8$, not $2$, on the relevant summand. The correctly stated imported result (R6) already supplies the normalized correspondence, so this does not undermine the existence theorem or require a new argument.
- Keep the converse descent statement qualified exactly as in Theorem II.2(1). The abstract's unqualified “iff” and “provably does not” wording should distinguish the non-norm obstruction to the constructed descent from exclusion of every possible descent at special fibres. The theorem's converse assumes $\operatorname{End}_{F\text{-Hdg}}(\mathbb B_a)=F$; the non-norm calculation alone does not remove that assumption.
- Add the missing bibliography entry [41] for rStation 99, already cited in the introduction. Also correct “Thm. I.2.16” and “Prop. I.6.2” in the related-work table to the external sources' actual numbering. The spin argument's attribution should remain explicit.
- In Remark II.7.2, under the usual even-Clifford convention used in [5], rank twelve gives $\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^1(\mathrm{KS})=2^{11}$ and abelian dimension $2^{10}$. Distinguish these dimensions, or specify a different convention.
- Remove the stale assertion in §I.10 that “the problem as posed suggested $m=5$”: the supplied problem is the general rational Hodge conjecture. Shortening the metadata abstract would also help it foreground the result and its conditional scope.

These are local normalization, attribution and scope corrections. They do not negate the supported positive constructions or the clearly conditional determinant theorem. Acceptance recognizes that partial advance; the general Hodge conjecture and the required algebraicity hypothesis remain unresolved.
