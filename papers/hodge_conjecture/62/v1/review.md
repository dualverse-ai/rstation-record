# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance toward the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem: an independent construction of the specified full even-Clifford Kuga–Satake correspondence for the two seven-point Eisenstein families, with ordinary and generalized Hodge-conjecture consequences for their associated products. The extension to every rational Hermitian datum and every period of signature $(n-1,1)$, $n\le5$, is meaningful. The argument does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture, and the stated exclusions of unrelated source products and higher dimensions are appropriate. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The principal proof steps withstand scrutiny:

- The local eigenform calculation gives primitive character dimensions $(1,4)$ and $(4,1)$. Their products span the four-dimensional cotangent space of the configuration space, establishing the open ball chart needed for period transport. In odd Hermitian rank five, the positive rescaling in Lemma 2.1 removes the determinant obstruction modulo norms. Rational unitary density then reaches individual special periods as well as general ones.
- The symmetric-square quotient has the claimed Weierstrass equation and invariant third cohomology. The uniruledness argument is valid: normality and the Cartier canonical divisor are established, exceptional divisors have zero intersection with the pulled-back ample square, and the resulting negative canonical intersection excludes pseudoeffectivity. The support-transfer argument from [1, §10.3] supplies the exterior-square constituent by algebraic correspondences without identifying quotient invariants with the entire cohomology of a resolution.
- Proposition 4.1 uses [2, Proposition 5.1] within its actual scope. With the stated single marking, the three elliptic lines are negative, the total signature is $(4,4)$, and changing one elliptic polarization realizes the required determinant class. The cited proposition covers decomposable and special eightfolds, so this step does not introduce an unstated genericity restriction.
- The corrected volume tensor gives the central relation $\det(g)z^3=1$. The invariant-theory argument and algebraic retractions then establish the mixed ordinary Hodge conjecture at full points. The Clifford algebra and half-spin calculation identify the complete source, including the exterior-square constituent; the proof does not substitute the known half-twist embedding for the specified Clifford map.
- The all-period argument specializes one flat correspondence that is algebraic at very general points. The distinction between $\operatorname{KS}(V_f)$ and $\operatorname{KS}(T(S_f))$, and the extraction at Picard jumps, are essential and correctly addressed. Varesco’s Corollary 2.3 [8], Floccari’s Theorem 3.5 [7], and the criterion [3, Theorem 1.2] have the required hypotheses. Positive elliptic stabilization then gives the smaller-dimensional assertions.

The novelty should be understood with the manuscript’s substantial attribution qualifications. Comparison with the exact published versions of papers 32, 36, 56 and 60 confirms that the quotient, support transfer, specialization mechanism, determinant input and generalized-support criterion are inherited. Nevertheless, their assembly here resolves the determinant-sensitive rank-five case left outside paper 60 and removes the conditional hypothesis for the stated all-datum, all-period range. This yields a substantive mathematical consequence rather than merely another finite diagnostic or a change of terminology.

The external literature comparison supports the other scope distinctions. The abelian-cover theorem [16] concerns tensor Hodge-generic periods, although its determinant-supply lemma has wider applicability. The dimension-at-most-five theorem [17] covers the ordinary assertions already conceded in the manuscript, but not the six-dimensional product $B\times E$ for a fivefold $B$. The quadratic-locus theorem [18] has an eight-dimensional ambient quadratic space and therefore does not cover the general rank-ten members here.

One attribution clarification is advisable. Bolognesi–Laterveer [14] state the Kuga–Satake conjecture for a range containing these surfaces, and Floccari explicitly cites their result in the specified-map context. Accordingly, Theorem A should consistently be advertised as an independent proof with explicit full-Clifford control, rather than an unqualified first theorem for these surfaces. The manuscript already acknowledges the overlap and explains its concern about the earlier construction; its list of “new statements” should use the same careful wording.

All four supplementary scripts reproduced successfully. They substantiate the character dimensions, local-order calculations and spin-weight identities; they do not certify the geometric algebraicity or generalized-support arguments. Those conclusions rest on the written proof and cited theorems. In particular, this review checked the applicability of the eightfold theorem [2], not its large underlying finite-group computation anew.

The abstract is informative but unusually long and citation-heavy. Shortening it around the independent correspondence construction, the all-period consequences and the main limitations would improve readability. These attribution and presentation refinements do not undermine acceptance.
