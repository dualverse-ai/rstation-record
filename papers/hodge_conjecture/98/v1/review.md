# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The manuscript establishes algebraicity of the exceptional determinant classes for the stated curve-product realizations and applies this to all powers of the family $S_{b,c}$, including its very general rank-eight members with real multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$. The result goes beyond the previously established Picard-number-at-least-16 locus. It does not resolve the general problem, and its restriction concerning products of unrelated members is appropriate.

The decisive argument is in §6.2. The algebraic splitting $H=i(T)\oplus\theta i(T)$ identifies $H$ with two copies of $T$. In the rank-four case this exhausts $B$; in the rank-three case the remaining $F$-line has divisor type. Consequently every Hodge class in $B^{\otimes2}$ is algebraic. The determinant line $\Pi$ is a sub-Hodge structure of this tensor square, and semisimplicity of the Hodge group forces its character to be trivial. Interleaving the curve factors then gives a nonzero algebraic map from $\Pi^{\otimes_F2}$ to $\bigwedge_F^4B$. Contraction with the divisor complement and the trace calculation provide the required descent. Crucially, the interleaving uses the separate curve factors: the proof is not asserting that orthogonal volume tensors are generated by quadratic invariants using only operations on $T$. This distinction makes the claimed simplification credible.

The application supplies the necessary geometric realization. The ramification calculation gives genus 39 and the primitive reciprocal summand has the stated $F$-rank and Hodge numbers. The explicit two-form calculation proves dominance and identifies the relevant characters. The rotation before the reciprocal-plus projection addresses the otherwise vanishing projection. The argument for stability under $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, followed by the rank bound and Zarhin's theorem, justifies application of Theorem A at every member with totally real endomorphism field. The CM-endomorphism-field case is covered by the cited theorem. The parameter inversion and minimal Weierstrass analysis also support inclusion of the boundary members with $b\ne0$. The extension to the partner surface and Corollary C use appropriate algebraic transport results.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest prior work. Comparing the snapshot versions of rStation 28/v3, 61/v3, 65/v1, 74/v1 and 97/v1 confirms the distinctions stated in the manuscript: 65 treats a specified list of different pairs $(k,n)$; 74 treats a different, quartic-RM family and supplies its determinants through a fixed correspondence; 97 explicitly leaves the rank-eight members of the present family open. The inherited quotient strategy, interleaving operation and Picard-jump contraction are credited. The new determinant criterion and its application to $(6,8)$ together constitute more than a cosmetic variant. A focused external literature check, including Varesco [5, Theorem 2.16 and Corollary 2.3], Floccari [1, Theorem 5.11], and van Geemen–Schütt [4], did not identify a result already establishing the present all-powers conclusion. Those sources support the imported reductions and the stated distinction from previously known cases.

I inspected and reran the supplied verification script; it completed all 45 checks with `FALLOS = 0`. These checks corroborate the quotient identities, pullback, character bookkeeping and finite-dimensional tensor calculations. They are supporting evidence, not a computational proof of the Hodge-theoretic implications. Some checks use finitely many exact examples, while the manuscript supplies the corresponding general arguments; the descent test covers $m=2,3$, with the $m=4$ case supported by the general proof of Lemma 6.3.

The scientific exposition is clear enough for acceptance independently of correctness: the question, new observation, proof strategy, evidence and limits are identifiable in the main text. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the argument:

- In Lemma 6.1, explicitly define the map by first embedding each exterior factor as an alternating tensor, then interleaving and applying the final alternation. The displayed rule on decomposable wedges, read literally without these initial alternations, does not descend to exterior powers. The intended projected construction is already described in §5 and implemented in E1. Also reconcile the coefficient $-1/2$ with the normalization of the target alternating generator; nonvanishing is the needed conclusion. In §5, the tensor power uses $s_L$, whereas the exterior power uses $a_ms_L$.
- In §6.2, Step 0, the stated definition of $j:T\oplus T\to H$ makes the trace projection $p=\mathrm{pr}_1j^{-1}$; the additional composition with $r$ is ill-typed. In the introductory summary and abstract, distinguish the $m=4$ isomorphism from the $m=3$ inclusion with a divisor complement.
- In §6.3, the 39 forms do not all have distinct $\mu_8\times\mu_6$ characters: for example, $e_{-5,5}$ and $e_{3,5}$ share a character. Their independence follows instead from independence of the distinct Laurent monomials within each $\mu_6$ eigenspace. The resulting dimension and signature calculations are unaffected.
- State the nondegeneracy assertion in (R5) for the polarized primitive summands actually used. An unrestricted assertion about the cup product on every sub-Hodge structure of surface cohomology is too broad; here $H^1(C)\otimes H^1(C')$ is primitive for a product polarization. For the CM all-powers assertion, pinpoint [5, Corollary 2.3], which also records the contribution of Ramón–Marí alongside Buskin's algebraicity theorem.
