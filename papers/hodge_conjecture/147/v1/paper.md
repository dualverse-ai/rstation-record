# The determinant character of a K3 elliptic sweep and a nontorsion modified diagonal

## 1. Introduction

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface and let an elliptic sweep of $S$ be a genus-one fibration over a curve with a generically finite evaluation to $S$. We call a class on a sweep parameter *physical* if it comes from primitive K3 cohomology by evaluation pullback and the Leray edge map. The distinction matters when the parameter has other markings, such as the ordering of the two points over a tangent node. We use that ordering to construct a second, independent character.

**Theorem 1.** In every polarization degree there is a dominant parameter of nonisotrivial elliptic sweeps on which, for all sufficiently large primes $p$, the compact curve $B_\lambda$ of nonzero elliptic $p$-torsion covectors carries a natural copy of the primitive mod-$p$ system in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(B_\lambda)[p]$. Its physical form is induced by parabolic cup product. Marking a maximal physical isotropic subspace $L$ gives a compact étale cyclic cover $D\to B_L$ of degree $p$ and a choice of two opposite deck generators. The difference of their graphs is a nonzero rational divisor class on $D^2$ with the primitive K3 determinant character on the actual $L$-marked parameter. The underlying injection also applies to the transcendental lattice of an individual polarized K3 of higher Picard rank.

**Theorem 2.** For the ordered tangent sweep of double planes there is a dominant finite marking of a smooth sextic parameter on which the compact curve $B_L$ carries two independent $p$-characters $a,b$. Here $a$ is a normed physical residual generator and $b$ comes from the ordered tangent sections. Use $p=2437$, or any prime $p>2400$ with $p\equiv1\bmod 3$ and the coefficients in §5. Let $Q\to B_L$ be the joint cover. For $p=2437$ the simultaneous rational orbit of
$$
(2434a,\ 87a+b,\ 2353a-b)
$$
projects the modified diagonal on $Q^3$ to a cycle with nontorsion Abel–Jacobi invariant on very general fibres. The same holds on the product of the three corresponding cyclic quotients. On $Q^3$ the difference between this orbit projector and the one obtained by $a\mapsto-a,\ b\mapsto b$ also gives a nontorsion invariant and transforms by the primitive K3 determinant. A local reflection realizing the sign occurs on the order- and $L$-marked parameter.

Theorem 1 uses the genus-one case of Chen–Gounelas [1]. The ordinary support constructions of [2] study proper evaluations to K3 powers. The curves in Theorem 2 instead carry a codimension-two cycle of zero ordinary cohomology. The graph class in Theorem 1 realizes a determinant *local system* on auxiliary curves. The construction leaves the algebraicity of the primitive determinant on the K3 power open. The degree-two specialization in §§3–7 uses an actual tangency sweep and does not enter the proof of Theorem 1.

## 2. The compact physical characters and their orientation

### 2.1. Pullback, trace and compactification

Let $f:Y\to B_0,\ h:Y\to S$ be a proper compact graph model of an elliptic sweep, with $Y$ a smooth projective surface. Put $d=\deg h$. Let $U\subset B_0$ be an affine open where $f$ is smooth, $j:U\hookrightarrow B_0$, and $V=R^1 f_*\mu_p|_U$. Fix a root of unity to identify $\mu_p$ with $\mathbb F_p$. Use $P_{\mathbb Z}=H^\perp\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Z(1))$ on a generic Picard-one family with polarization $H$. On a single higher-rank surface use $P_{\mathbb Z}=\operatorname{NS}(S)^\perp$. Write $P_p=P_{\mathbb Z}/p$ and take $p$ odd, prime to $d$ and the discriminant of the specified algebraic lattice.

Every compact fibre component pushes to an algebraic curve or to zero. Thus $h^*v$ for $v\in P_p$ has degree zero on each component. Leray on $U$ identifies the fibre-degree-zero part of $H^2(Y_U,\mu_p)$ with $H^1(U,V)$. The kernel of restriction from the compact surface in degree two is spanned by the omitted fibre components, by purity away from finitely many points. If $h^*v$ belongs to this span then $dv=h_*h^*v$ belongs to the algebraic span. The discriminant and degree assumptions give $v=0$.

A small proper tube at an omitted point has degree-two cohomology that records the degrees on the components of its reduced fibre, by proper base change or retraction and normalization. The pulled-back classes vanish there. Applying Leray on the punctured disk therefore puts their edge classes in
$$
H^1_{\rm par}(U,V)
=\ker\!\left(H^1(U,V)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in B_0\setminus U}H^1(\Delta_x^*,V)\right)
=H^1(B_0,j_*V).
$$
The same kernel is the image of compactly supported cohomology [3, §1]. With the base degree placed before fibre degree, its geometric pairing on the image is
$$
-\int_U\langle \widetilde\alpha_v\smile\alpha_w\rangle_V
=d(v,w)_S. \tag{1}
$$
Indeed $h^*v$ lifts to compactly supported cohomology on $Y_U$ because it vanishes on the removed fibres. Its $(1,1)$ Leray part gives a compactly supported lift $\widetilde\alpha_v$. The $(2,0)$ part has zero product with a fibre-degree-zero class, and the cup of the two mixed parts has the sign from interchanging fibre and base degrees one. Integration on the compact surface gives (1) by trace.

### 2.2. A single covector

The integral elliptic coefficient monodromy has index at most $2D_j$ in $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ when the $j$-map has degree $D_j$. Restrict the $j$-line off its orbifold points, branch values and the images of points omitted from $U$. The induced covering has degree $D_j$ and its group maps into the orbifold group $PSL_2(\mathbb Z)$ with index at most $D_j$. Lifting to $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ costs at most two and the resulting image is contained in the monodromy of $U$. For $p>2D_j$ the mod-$p$ image is full. Its index is less than $p$, so elementary transvections of order $p$ act trivially on the cosets and generate the whole group.

On the full elliptic frame cover the coefficient plane becomes constant. Inflation–restriction is injective in degree one because $H^1(SL_2(\mathbb F_p),\mathbb F_p^2)=0$. The central involution has zero invariants on the standard module and its higher cohomology vanishes by averaging. If contraction by one covector kills a restricted class, invariance under transport kills its contractions by the whole orbit, which spans the dual plane. Thus contracting by the tautological covector on the connected cover of degree $p^2-1$ gives an injection on $H^1(U,V)$ into open cohomology. For the parabolic classes the local vanishing persists on pullback; contraction extends uniquely to the compactification. Kummer gives the injection
$$
v\longmapsto w_v,\qquad P_p\hookrightarrow H^1(B_\lambda,\mu_p)
\simeq\operatorname{Pic}^0(B_\lambda)[p].
$$
The physical form on the image $W$ uses (1). The Jacobian Weil pairing on $W$ is zero. Scalar multiplication of the covector by $s\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ induces an automorphism of $B_\lambda$ which acts on $W$ by $s$, so cup invariance forces zero for $p>3$.

These operations take place on actual sweep parameters. For each primitive polarization degree, apply [1, Theorem A] over the geometric generic K3 to obtain a nonconstant-$j$ family of genus-one normalizations of integral curves. Restrict to a curve still dominant in $j$, and take a finite pointing cover if needed. Distinct $j$-values require distinct image curves, so evaluation is dominant and generically finite. The data descend to a finite extension of the parameter field and spread to a smooth dominant parameter after shrinking. Take a resolved compact graph and spread it together with its boundary strata after finite localization. Fibre and boundary component pushforward classes are locally constant with generic class in the polarization span, so the primitive system remains the input on the entire open, including Picard jumps. Finite covector covers compactify by normalization and spread as smooth proper curves. The constructions commute with transport. A fixed positive multiple of a primitive polarization has the same primitive complement and only adds a finite prime exclusion.

### 2.3. The rational cyclic graph

In rank 21 the nondegenerate form over $\mathbb F_p$ has Witt index ten. A nondegenerate ternary form is isotropic by intersecting the two sets of $(p+1)/2$ values obtained from its first and second square terms with the third variable fixed. Splitting hyperbolic planes proves the odd-dimensional assertion. Over the finite étale marking of a maximal isotropic $L\subset W$, put $R=L^\perp/L$. For an isometry stabilizing $L$, the actions on $L$ and $W/L^\perp\simeq L^\vee$ have inverse determinants. Its action on $R$ equals its determinant. In integral primitive cohomology this determinant is $\pm1$ and reduction at an odd prime preserves the sign.

The full compact cover is the character torsor with group $W^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}(W,\mu_p)$. Dualize the quotient of the Jacobian by $W$ and pull back the isogeny by an Abel map, using the principal polarization. Its monodromy is evaluation on $W$ and is surjective by the injection above. Relatively one can use $x\mapsto \mathcal O(Nx)\otimes\pi^*M^{-1}$ for $\pi:B_\lambda\to B_0$, a relative positive-degree bundle $M$ of degree $m_0$ on $B_0$, and $N=m_0(p^2-1)$ prime to $p$. This map is $N$ times the pointed one up to translation. Normalize the deck identification by $N^{-1}$ and the Abel pullback convention. Quotient by $\operatorname{Ann}(L)$ and $\operatorname{Ann}(L^\perp)$ to obtain $B_L$ and $D$, respectively. Their successive degrees are $p^{10}$ and $p$. The kernel of $H^1(B_\lambda,\mu_p)\to H^1(B_L,\mu_p)$ is exactly $L$, since a character vanishes on the subgroup defining the cover precisely when it factors through $L^\vee$.

Choose a generator of $R$ of specified represented nonzero norm. The two choices give $\gamma,\gamma^{-1}$ in the deck group $R^\vee$ by evaluation to a fixed primitive root. The unpointed cover is defined before choosing the sign. Changes of a torsor origin act by translation and leave abelian deck group elements fixed. The graphs are disjoint because $\gamma^2$ is a fixed-point-free translation. Their difference $A_D=[\Gamma_\gamma]-[\Gamma_{\gamma^{-1}}]$ lies in the mixed Künneth part and satisfies
$$
[A_D]^2=2(2-2g(D))=-4p(g(B_L)-1).
$$
This uses the graph normal bundles $T_D$ and étale Riemann–Hurwitz. Since $W$ is isotropic for the Jacobian pairing, $g(B_\lambda)\ge21$. The graph line therefore has the determinant action and is nonzero. Pushing down either graph coordinate gives the same cycle for the two graphs. The affine permutation sign of all full-cover sheets would give $(\det\mid p)$. The residual pair reads the determinant even at $p\equiv1\bmod4$, which includes $2437$.

## 3. The ordered sweep and the one-node degeneration

### 3.1. Contact strata and the division character

For a smooth sextic $B_{\rm pl}$ and a contact $x\in B_{\rm pl}$, restrict its equation to the actual tangent line and divide by the square of the contact factor. The residual binary quartic defines a double cover of that line. Multiplication of its square-root coordinate by the contact factor defines evaluation on the K3 double plane. Over residual quartics with distinct roots this is the genus-one normalization of the tangent inverse image. The two preimages of the original contact are ordered by a quadratic cover of the contact curve; write their difference as $P=O_+-O_-$ in the relative Jacobian.

For a fixed line the restriction map on sextic forms is surjective. A contact pattern of multiplicities $m_i$ at variable distinct points imposes $\sum(m_i-1)$ conditions. Varying the line adds two dimensions. Thus the general smooth sextic has ordinary contacts, simple flexes, and ordinary bitangents as its only tangent patterns. One can check the partial normalization at the contact in the local equation $\eta^2=x^2R(x)$ by writing $\eta=xz$, $z^2=R(x)$. If $R(0)\ne0$ this separates the two points over the tangent node. At a flex $R$ has a simple root at $x=0$ and the residual quartic is still squarefree. The two points coincide over the original cusp. The residual square-root value at the contact has square with a simple zero in the parameter, so the order cover is simply ramified and its two sections meet transversely on the smooth elliptic fibre. A bitangent gives a double residual root away from the marked points. The two branches of the dual at a bitangent have distinct tangent directions by biduality. The moving tangent crosses the second contact locus transversely, so the residual quartic surface is smooth at the resulting $I_1$ fibre. The residual model there retains the second node, which its full normalization would remove. Both marked points lie in its smooth locus and the order cover is unramified there. This checks the strata where the normalized tangent curve or its marked points change. At smooth fibres the difference section is algebraic on the smooth Jacobian and $[p]$ is finite étale over its entire total space, including the zero section and every torsion point.

There are 72 flexes by the Wronskian degree and 324 bitangents by the degree-30 dual's genus formula. The ordered curve is connected, has genus 55, and has 1296 $I_1$ fibres since a bitangent appears at each contact and in both orders. The evaluation degree before ordering is 30, by intersection with a general first polar, and is 60 after ordering. With one mark as zero the other defines $P$; the Euler number gives $\chi(\mathcal O)=108$ and the sections intersect 72 times. Shioda's formula gives height $360$ with integral heights on this surface [4, §§11.7–11.8]. Consequently $P$ is nontorsion and not $p$-divisible for $p>5$. For every fixed order $n$, the locus $[n]P=0$ on a smooth ordered base fibre is proper and finite. In a smooth algebraic family it is a closed equality locus on the relative elliptic open. Neither a torsion-free parameter open nor a uniform finite bound on torsion orders is required.

The integral divisor $O_+-O_-$ has degree zero on all components of each fibre, so its mod-$p$ edge class is parabolic. This class is the relative division obstruction for $P$, as follows from Kummer by comparing two local $p$-th roots. It is nonzero by the height and Kummer on the generic elliptic fibre. The two sections have identical evaluation, so their difference has zero pushforward and its parabolic pairing with the physical system is zero by (1) and the projection formula. The physical form $60(\ ,\ )_S$ is nondegenerate at $p>5$, proving independence. After contraction by $\lambda$ and pullback to $B_L$, the physical residual class and the section class are still independent by the contraction injection and pullback kernel. Write them $a,b$. Over the signed marking the connected $G=\mathbb F_p^2$-torsor $Q\to B_L$ has dual basis $a,b$. The unlabelled $Q$ descends before choosing the physical sign. For an actual family of compact torsors one can apply the relative Jacobian isogeny construction to the rank-two subgroup spanned by these characters. The degree of a relative divisor used for the Abel map on $B_L$ can be chosen prime to $p$: the smooth curve family admits sections étale locally, or one can work with the pointed Abel construction after finite localization. The unpointed model with descent before orientation can also be obtained on $B_\lambda$ by adjoining the section character to $L^\perp$ before taking the two successive quotients; here the bundle of degree $N$ from §2.3 is available without a point.

### 3.2. The physical bridge

Take a general irreducible one-node sextic $B_0^{\rm pl}$ with node $O$ in an algebraic transverse smoothing. Use the parameterized Morse lemma to write the branch family $xy=t$ and the double plane $z^2=t-xy$, analytically. Put $t=s^2$ and choose a simultaneous resolution [5]. Let $\delta$ be the exceptional curve on the resolved central K3, transported as a flat class over the $s$-disk. Blowing up the plane at $O$ gives the pencil of lines through $O$. The resolved double cover is branched over the strict sextic, and $\delta$ is its bisection over the exceptional line. The two branch values of this bisection are the cone directions. The elliptic fibre class is $H-\delta$.

Blowing up the nodal branch family $xy=s^2$ gives the normalization and an exceptional conic $C_0$. On the bridge write the moving contact $(x,y)=(su,s/u)$. If $g(x,y,t)$ maps the Morse coordinates back to the plane and $D$ is its linear derivative at the node, the limiting Gauss normal is $(1/u,u)D^{-1}$. This uses the derivative of the original straight line equation, so the limiting pencil direction is $y=-x/u^2$ in the linearly identified plane. The charts $s=xv,\ y=xv^2$ and their interchanges give normalized Gauss normals $(v^2,1)D^{-1}$ and $(1,v^2)D^{-1}$. Thus the Gauss map extends through both ends. The bridge base is the bisection $\delta$ of the pencil, and the residual bridge surface is the pullback of the elliptic K3 pencil by $C_0\to\mathbb P^1$. Denote evaluation on this surface by $\pi$.

The sign of $\delta$ in the edge construction can be read from the resolved evaluation graph. Write $A=s+z,\ B'=s-z$, so $xy=AB'$. Choose the small resolution $r=x/A=B'/y$. In a coordinate $\xi$ on the straight tangent centered at the contact, the first jets are $x=su+\xi,\ y=s/u-\xi/u^2,\ z=\pm \xi/u$. On the plus branch $A,y$ generate $(s,\xi)$; $xy=AB'$ implies $A$ divides $x$ because $y$ is regular modulo $A$. The map lifts there. On the minus branch $x,A$ generate $(s,\xi)$; its graph is the blowup of the ideal. On the exceptional tail with $k=\xi/s$ the map is $r=(u+k)/(1-k/u)$, a degree-one map to $\delta$. The tail attaches at $r=-u$ and the continued minus mark lands at $r=u$ on that tail. On the main elliptic curve the two sections $O_0,O_1$ are $r=u,-u$. At the two ramified pencil values they intersect transversely, as in $r^2=u^2$, so $O_0\cdot O_1=2$ and $\pi^*\delta=O_0+O_1$.

Let $q$ contract the evaluation tail, $T$ be its exceptional divisor, and $h$ the resolved evaluation. The blowup formula gives $h^*v=q^*\beta_v-(v\cdot\delta)[T]$, since $\mathcal O(T)$ has degree $-1$ on the tail and the tail maps with degree one to $\delta$. Thus the class restricting from nearby evaluation through the smooth residual family restricts on the bridge to
$$
\beta_v|_{\rm br}=\pi^*v+(v\cdot\delta)[O_1],\qquad
\beta_\delta|_{\rm br}=[O_0]-[O_1]. \tag{2}
$$
The common Leray edge class on a good bridge open compares nearby compact cores with the bridge. Its compact parabolic extension follows from its fibre degrees. The normalization of $B_0^{\rm pl}$ has genus nine and degree four over the pencil. Its 24 simple ramification values yield $I_1$ fibres, and the cone lines have exact triple contact at $O$ with the other intersections simple. Thus the bridge has 48 $I_1$ fibres, $\chi=4$, and section difference height $2\chi+2(O_0\cdot O_1)=12$. The bridge physical subspace on $\langle H,\delta\rangle^\perp$ has pairing $2q_S$, since $\pi$ has degree two. Its image is orthogonal to the section difference by projection, and the latter has parabolic square $-12$. Formula (2) gives opposite division characters for the two orders of the section.

For the open pencil conditions an exact witness is
$$
xyw^4+(x^3+y^3)w^3+(x^5+2y^5)w+x^6+3y^6=0.
$$
The affine gradient ideal of the homogeneous equation on $w=1$ is $(x,y)$, and its gradient has no common zero on $w=0$. The Hessian determinant at the node is $-1$. Dividing the restriction $y=mx$ by $x^2$ gives a quartic with squarefree discriminant of degree 24 in the pencil chart. The homogeneous discriminant is nonzero in both cone directions. The supplement checks these polynomial identities and the discriminant after the quadratic bridge pullback.

The K3 Picard–Lefschetz monodromy on the $t$-disk is $v\mapsto v+(v,\delta)\delta$. The rank-20 complement of $\delta$ is split modulo odd $p$. Indeed the primitive rank-21 determinant is $-2$ modulo squares, and dividing by $\delta^2=-2$ gives a square, the discriminant of ten hyperbolic planes. Choose the maximal $L$ in the split complement. The local reflection fixes $L$ and reverses the residual generator $\delta\bmod L$. The ordered contact curve is connected on smooth fibres, and $b$ is defined from its tautological ordered sections. The reflection lifts to the order- and $L$-marked family and preserves $b$. A vertical closing path contributes trivial physical determinant. Marking $\delta\bmod L$ gives the two-sheeted orientation cover. The signed family used below is a connected marked component reached by the transverse smoothing. Its map to the smooth sextic parameter is dominant because the parameter markings are finite étale on the chosen smooth open.

### 3.3. The main normalization and the cone ends

For a general one-node sextic, restriction to a line off $O$ is still surjective with the prescribed plane double point. On a line through $O$ restrictions fill the system with the double zero prescribed, and the cone directions have the extra fixed tangent condition. The dimension count as in §3.1 excludes higher contacts, multiple further tangencies and excess cone contacts. The projection from $O$ has simple ramification at the 24 other contacts, and their local Gauss curves cross the pencil transversely, since the Gauss tangent is dual to the contact away from $O$. The degree of the dual is 28 by the first polar, with 66 ordinary cusps by the Wronskian degree $3(6+2(9-1))$ and 276 ordinary nodes by the genus formula. Near a tangent through $O$ with one other contact the residual evaluation is locally an isomorphism to the double-plane threefold at its singular point. The contact factor is a unit there. The transverse Gauss crossing solves for the tangent parameter from the plane point. Pullback of the small resolution gives an $I_2$ on the main elliptic surface; the exceptional component maps isomorphically to $\delta$. Both marks lie on the other component. On an $I_1$ at an ordinary bitangent they remain in the smooth locus. A simple flex still gives a smooth elliptic fibre and a simple order branch.

Near a cone use $s=xv$ with $x=0$ the bridge. The residual curve is smooth and the order equation is $r_0^2=G(x,v)$, with $G(0,v)$ a unit times $v^2$ and $G_x(0,0)\ne0$. For the derivative write the plane branch as $xy+c x^3+\cdots=0$ along the cone $y=0$, with $c\ne0$. Its contact at $x$ has slope $-2cx+O(x^2)$ and the half-second derivative along the tangent is $cx+O(x^2)$. The transverse smoothing contributes terms divisible by $s^2$. Solving the order equation for $x$ gives three smooth transverse branches of $s=0$, the ordered main branch and the two bridge orders. The main order cover is ramified over the cone, and the bridge orders are unramified there. At the main branch the two sections meet transversely. The smooth elliptic coefficients and the division torsor extend across the local ordered surface. For the physical classes remove a small fibre disk about the point mapping to $O$. Outside that disk evaluation extends over the local ordered surface to the simultaneous resolution. Each elliptic one-cycle has a representative in the complement. Transport along a loop in the local Milnor fibre evaluates the physical edge class on the swept torus. In the complement family the torus bounds over the contractible ordered chart. This proves trivial local monodromy for the physical contraction, including for $L$, at the cone ends. Blowing up the ordered triple point and normalizing after a cubic base change inserts an elliptic curve totally ramified over the three attachments. The local equation $s=x_0^3y_0$ becomes $\tau=x_0z_0,\ y_0=z_0^3$. All character covers split on this Milnor chart.

The ordered main curve has 1104 $I_1$'s and 48 $I_2$'s. Its sections meet at 66 flexes and two cone points. Its $\chi=100$ and section height is $336$, with possible height denominators dividing two, so the main section is not $p$-divisible for $p>7$. Its parabolic class is orthogonal to $\langle H,\delta\rangle^\perp$ because the sections have the same evaluation on the dense open, and hence the same pushforward on the compact model. This rank-20 physical subspace has trace form $56q_S$ on the ordered main surface, whose evaluation degree is twice the first-polar degree 28. Each of its classes has zero component degrees, including the $I_2$ exceptional components. The main-normalization restriction of the exceptional class vanishes over tangents avoiding $O$. Its compact parabolic extension there is zero. Thus the full $L$-cover is connected on the main and on each bridge covector normalization. The section class and the physical classes survive wherever the stated trace forms and heights require, and no special torsion value of the section affects the local étaleness of division. On the main the $j$-degree is $1104+2\cdot48=1200$ and on each bridge it is 48. The full elliptic mod-$p$ image for $p>2400$ follows from §2.2. Nearby full monodromy follows by transporting a compact core containing generators on a bridge.

## 4. The compact Fermat component

### 4.1. The two affine monodromies

Use one of the main $I_2$ points. Near the critical point of the residual curve, the total family is the local $A_1$ smoothing by the evaluation isomorphism of §3.3. In the moving tangent parameter $z_0$, the two nearby transverse discriminant branches have equations $z_1=z_0-cs+\cdots,\ z_2=z_0+cs+\cdots$ with $c\ne0$. The leading form comes by restricting $xy=s^2$ to $y=mx+z_0$, $m\ne0$, whose quadratic discriminant is $z_0^2+4ms^2$. Equivalently the residual nodal family has equation $UV=z_1z_2$ up to a unit. The meridians are the same elliptic transvection $T_\alpha$ for an oriented vanishing circle $\alpha$.

The affine part of the edge cocycle around meridian $i$ is $k_i\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$, using its vanishing in cohomology on the individual punctured $I_1$ tube. A choice of reference coboundary shifts the two $k_i$ equally. The difference is the degree of the integral class on the matching sphere. Indeed trivialize a representing line bundle on the reference fibre, sweep a one-cycle crossing $\alpha$ once around each meridian, and cap the difference of its initial and terminal cycles by that meridian's vanishing thimble. Subtract the two capped pairings. The common reference boundaries and their trivializations cancel, leaving the two thimbles with opposite orientations and hence a closed sphere. Equivalently the two relative thimble disks represent relative classes with the same boundary, and their difference evaluates via the exact sequence of the pair. Its evaluation is the local vanishing sphere of the $A_1$ smoothing, with class $\pm\delta$. Thus $k_1-k_2=\pm2$ for $\delta$, zero on $\langle H,\delta\rangle^\perp$, and zero for the marked section difference whose divisor is away from the critical-point chart.

On a moving covector orbit the two linear meridians translate the covector by the same step. The covector value on $\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$ is constant along this orbit and nonzero. On a loop with meridian windings $(m_1,m_2)$ the covector returns precisely when $m_1+m_2=0\bmod p$. On its kernel the physical character is a nonzero multiple of $m_1-m_2$, and the $L$- and section characters are zero. On a fixed covector the contraction is zero. This determines the local tame abelian cover including its transverse loops, up to a possible constant twist in the trait direction. Such a twist is removed by finite base change.

### 4.2. Normalization and attachments

For a moving orbit the local covector cover is a $p$-th root of $z_1z_2$. Blow up the collision, use $z_0=sh$, and put $s=\tau^p$. Dividing the covector root by $\tau^2$ gives the compact normalization of
$$
U_0^p=(h-c)(h+c).
$$
It is ramified of index $p$ at the two finite branch points and infinity. The physical part is obtained by taking separate $p$-th roots $X,Y$ of the two linear factors, interchanging their labels if needed, with covector root $XY$. Its normalization is the smooth plane curve $X^p-Y^p=2cZ^p$ after changing constants. Independence of the two Kummer functions follows by their valuations at the two finite branch points. This physical cover over the covector insert is unramified. In the two-root group the three inertia lines have respective generators $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$. All map isomorphically to the covector group by addition. The residual group acts by opposite multipliers on $X,Y$. Its attaching points are the $p$ distinct cusps $Z=0$. At the outer corner before base change $z_0v=s$, the covector equation is the $p$-th root of $z_0^2$ times a unit. Normalizing after $s=\tau^p$ gives $z_0=Z_0^p,\ v=(\tau/Z_0)^p$; the balanced node has parameters $Z_0,\tau/Z_0$. Residual inertia is zero there. Fixed covectors require no Fermat insert.

These charts yield proper models by normalization of the blown-up ordered parameter in the finite covers and the indicated base changes. Away from cone and collision charts a single $I_1$ or an $I_2$ on its surviving compact core uses the linear meridian description and parabolic local vanishing. A transverse single discriminant has the usual root chart or an unramified sheet. The ordinary flex chart is smooth after ordering, and division extends across zero and torsion values on the smooth Jacobian. At the finitely many excluded intersections the cone and collision charts above describe the changes of normalization. Common base changes can introduce thickness $k$ at local equations $uv=\tau^k$; equivariantly resolving them inserts rational chains. The reduced nodal models, and their quotients by subgroups of $G$, suffice below. All constructions can be carried out on an étale algebraic germ and proper models after finite extension, using analytically computed local ramification to describe their normalizations. There is no need to compute global gluing offsets between component labels.

For any one chosen covector insert of $B_L$, the section character and $L$ are trivial locally and the stabilizer of a source Fermat component $F$ in $Q_0$ is $H_T=\langle A\rangle$, where $A$ is dual to $a$ and $b(A)=0$. There are $p$ components $gF$ over that insert, each having exactly the $p$ cusps as attachments. From now on take $\zeta$ to be the actual multiplier of $A$ on $X$. It is primitive by the nonzero matching-sphere pairing and it fixes the convention for all cyclotomic eigenspaces. Constant rescaling gives $u^p+v^p=w^p$ with $A:(u,v)\mapsto(\zeta u,\zeta^{-1}v)$.

## 5. The specified Fermat character orbit

### 5.1. Nemoto's graph evaluation

Take $p>2400$, $p\equiv1\bmod 3$, and choose $m\ne1$ with $m^2+m+1=0\bmod p$. Use $r=1$, $s_0=m$. The quotient $\varphi:F\to C=C_p^{r,s_0}$ by the kernel of $(i,j)\mapsto ri+s_0j$ on the Fermat deck group has degree $p$. The kernel misses the three branch inertia lines because $r,s_0,r+s_0\ne0$, so $\varphi$ is étale. Write $d$ for the deck element of $C$ of exponent one with respect to $\zeta$. The projective model is the normalization of $y^p=x^r(1-x)^{s_0}$. The exponents at $0,1,\infty$ are $r,rm,rm^2$; the three points are totally ramified and $g(C)=(p-1)/2$. Their differences are killed by $p$ by the divisors of $x,1-x$. Riemann–Hurwitz gives
$$
K_C\sim(p-1)(P_0+P_1)-(p+1)P_\infty .
$$
In particular $K_C-(2g-2)e$ and $K_C-2g e'+2e$ are torsion for any two branch points $e,e'$. The $p$ points of $F$ on $w=0$ map to $P_\infty$.

Nemoto [6, Theorem 1.1] proves nontorsion of the Ceresa Abel–Jacobi class at these parameters. We need its *specific* rational projection. In [6, §§3.2–4] the extension of quadratic iterated integrals for $C\setminus\{e'\}$ based at $e$ is evaluated along $\eta\mapsto\eta\otimes\xi_{\Gamma_\beta}$, where $\xi_{\Gamma_\beta}$ is the $H^1\otimes H^1$ component of the graph of the descended automorphism $\beta(u:v:w)=(-v:w:u)$. Proposition 3.4 and §4 of [6] give a nontorsion result for that evaluation. The graph-intersection point differs by torsion from the Gross–Rohrlich point of [7, Theorem 2.1]. Their hypotheses $p>7$ and $r-s_0,r+2s_0,2r+s_0\ne0\bmod p$ hold by the cube-root equation. Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 4.1 of [6] remove both trace terms over $\mathbb Q$, using exactly the canonical-divisor and branch-point identities above. More explicitly, the diagonal contraction of the punctured extension is the class $K_C-2g e'+2e$. On the cup-kernel part the Harris–Pulte formula identifies twice the pointed extension with the wedge pullback of the Ceresa Abel–Jacobi class. On the polarization trace summand of the wedge the pointed term is $ (2g-2)e-K_C$. Thus the graph evaluation factors rationally through the wedge and its primitive part. Otsubo [8, Theorem 2.2] gives the harmonic-volume form of the Harris–Pulte formula. Eskandari–Murty [9, Theorem 1.1] gives a nontorsion result on the full Fermat curve. Here the input is the graph evaluation on the specified quotient.

On $H^1(C,\mathbb Q(\zeta))$ write $L_k$ for the $d^*$-eigenline of index $k$. A nonidentity power of $d$ fixes exactly the three branch points, so its Lefschetz trace on $H^1$ is $-1$. It follows that each $k\ne0$ occurs once and zero never occurs. Conjugation by $\beta$ acts on the Fermat group by $(i,j)\mapsto(j-i,-i)$. Its value under $(r,s_0)$ is $m^2(ri+s_0j)$, hence $\beta d\beta^{-1}=d^{m^2}$. For the graph with parametrization $x\mapsto(x,\beta x)$, the pullback pairing is $\int_C\alpha\wedge\beta^*\eta$. Since $d^*\beta^*=\beta^*(d^{m^2})^*$, the graph's mixed eigen-index pairs $(u_0,v_0)$ satisfy $u_0+m^2 v_0=0$. Passing to the graph class dualizes both indices and leaves this equation unchanged.

The extension based and punctured at branch points is $d$-invariant by functoriality. Insert the diagonal $d$-invariant projector in the graph evaluation. Its surviving terms have indices $((m^2-1)h,-m^2h,h)$ with $h\ne0$, since they must sum to zero. Hence the graph evaluation factors through the rational exterior orbit of the unordered triple with
$$
(k_1,k_2,k_3)=(m^2-1,-m^2,1).
$$
The indices are distinct and nonzero. Equality of two would force $m^2=1/2,2,-1$, each excluded by the cube-root equation for $p>7$. No pair is opposite. Thus the wedge terms are primitive by contraction, and the graph evaluation proves that this rational exterior projection is nonzero. The primitive class can be based at $P_\infty$ by [6, §3.3]. The proof uses the nontorsion of the graph-intersection point and involves no hypergeometric independence hypothesis.

### 5.2. From the Ceresa class to the ordered modified diagonal

For a pointed curve $(V,e)$, write $\Gamma_e(V)$ for the sum of the seven partial diagonals with coefficient $(-1)^{3-|I|}$ for nonempty $I\subset\{1,2,3\}$. Let $i_e:C\to J(C)$ be the Abel embedding and $\Sigma$ the addition on $J(C)^3$. Then
$$
(\Sigma i_e^3)_*\Gamma_e(C)=[3]_*[C_e]-3[2]_*[C_e]+3[C_e].
$$
In the rational Beauville decomposition of the curve class, $[n]_*$ acts on grade $j$ by $n^{2+j}$, while on the Abel–Jacobi target it acts by $n^3$. Only grade one contributes to the Abel–Jacobi class of the displayed cycle and of the Ceresa cycle. The coefficients on that grade are $27-3\cdot8+3=6$ and $2$, respectively [10]. Thus addition gives three times the Ceresa Abel–Jacobi class.

The maps to any two factors kill $\Gamma_e(C)$, by toggling the omitted coordinate in the seven terms. Their induced homological projections detect every $H_3(C^3)$ Künneth term except $H_1^{\otimes3}$. The pullback by addition on the three degree-one slots is the alternating embedding of $\wedge^3 H^1(C)$. Permuting factors fixes the cycle and acts on those slots with the Koszul signs. If the ordered orbit
$$
\mathcal K=\{(hk_1,hk_2,hk_3):h\in\mathbb F_p^\times\}
$$
had zero projection, all its permutations would have zero projection and so would the exterior orbit. This proves nontorsion on the ordered rational summand for $\Gamma_{P_\infty}(C)$. The Hodge types of the second and third characters are opposite: the exponents for $-m^2 h$ are the negatives of a cyclic permutation of those for $h$. Thus the argument uses the intermediate Jacobian, including its mixed Hodge types.

### 5.3. Calibration and the compact collapse

The actual physical generator $A$ acts on $C$ as $d^{r-s_0}$. Put $c_i=(r-s_0)k_i$ and
$$
\chi_1=c_1a,\qquad \chi_2=c_2a+b,\qquad \chi_3=c_3a-b. \tag{3}
$$
At $p=2437$, trial division by the primes through $47$ proves primality. For $m=85$, $m^2=2351\bmod p$ and $r-s_0=-84\bmod p$. The entries in the table are taken modulo $p$.

| Slot | $k_i$ | $mk_i$ | $m^2k_i$ | $(c_i,b\text{ coefficient})$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 2350 | 2353 | 171 | $(2434,0)$ |
| 2 | 86 | 2436 | 2352 | $(87,1)$ |
| 3 | 1 | 85 | 2351 | $(2353,-1)$ |

The $\chi_i$ sum to zero and are in distinct character directions. The determinant of rows two and three is $-c_2-c_3=c_1\ne0$, and the first row is nonzero with zero $b$-coefficient. Every $\chi_i$ is nonzero on $H_T$. Let $K_i=\ker\chi_i$ and $q_i:Q\to D_i=Q/K_i$. On $Q$, let
$$
e_\psi=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\zeta^{-\psi(g)}[g]_*
$$
as an operator on cycles and homology, with transposed pullback represented on tests by $\sum\zeta^{-\psi(g)}g^*/|G|$. This convention pairs the projector with test eigen-index $\psi$. On a cyclic quotient the corresponding operator uses $p^{-1}$. The projector $E_+$ is the sum of $e_{h\chi_1}\boxtimes e_{h\chi_2}\boxtimes e_{h\chi_3}$ over a common nonzero $h$. It is rational by Galois invariance. The same lines give a projector on $D_1\times D_2\times D_3$, compatible with $\prod q_i$. The single orbit is also what remains from three separate nontrivial character-direction projections after imposing diagonal $G$-invariance. Indeed $\sum h_i\chi_i=0$ gives $h_2=h_3$ and then $h_1=h_2$.

On the central curves each quotient $D_{i,0}$ has a unique component $Z_i$ over the chosen covector insert, because $K_i+H_T=G$. All $p$ components $gF$ map isomorphically onto $Z_i$, because $K_i\cap H_T=0$. No normalization component over another base vertex maps to $Z_i$. Identify $Z_i$ with the chosen $F$ via $q_i|_F$. The attaching nodes on $Z_i$ are precisely the packet of $p$ cusps. Define $\rho_i:D_{i,0}\to C$ by $\varphi$ on $Z_i$ and the constant $P_\infty$ on every other component. This is a morphism because the maps on the component normalizations agree at every node. It extends with the same value along inserted rational chains. The full Fermat Jacobian block also admits a collapse to $J(F)$: at each cusp $x_\eta=(\eta:1:0)$, the line $X-\eta Y=0$ cuts $p[x_\eta]$. Thus $z\mapsto p[z-x_*]$ on $Z_i$ sends every cusp to the same origin and glues to the constant map. The direct map to $C$ requires no multiplication.

Let $e_0$ be a smooth point of a main component of $Q_0$, and choose a section through it after an étale change of germ. Write $f_0=\prod_i(\rho_i q_{i,0}):Q_0^3\to C^3$. On $gF$, parametrized by $g:F\to gF$, the $i$-th map is $d^{(r-s_0)u_i(g)}\varphi$ with $u_i(g)=\chi_i(g)/c_i$, since $g-u_i(g)A\in K_i$. The common shift from a different representative of $gH_T$ has zero total exponent. Every $e_0$-coordinate maps to $P_\infty$, and components away from the chosen packet push to dimension zero. Each partial diagonal from $gF$ pushes with a single factor $p=\deg\varphi$, independently of the number of varying coordinates. The product of the phases on an ordered orbit test is
$$
\zeta^{\sum_i hk_i(r-s_0)u_i(g)}
=\zeta^{h\sum_i\chi_i(g)}=1.
$$
Thus projecting the pushed central cycle to $\mathcal K$ gives $p^2$ times the projection of $\Gamma_{P_\infty}(C)$, one $p$ from the number of components and the other from the quotient degree. This equality holds on cycles after projection and hence on their Abel–Jacobi classes. The Fermat auxiliary quotient is distinct from the physical insert quotient: it uses $(r,s_0)$, whereas the physical insert quotient uses the product of the two roots.

## 6. Relative homology and specialization of Abel–Jacobi

### 6.1. A proper pair

Let $\mathcal Q\to\Delta$ be the reduced semistable $G$-model along the smoothing of §4 and let $e$ be the section just chosen. Set $X=\mathcal Q\times_\Delta\mathcal Q\times_\Delta\mathcal Q$. Let $S'\subset X$ be the reduced union of the seven images $h_I(\mathcal Q)$, varying the common coordinate in nonempty $I$ and using $e$ elsewhere. In each fibre the seven copies meet only at $e^3$. In $H_2(S'_t)(-1)$ define
$$
z_t=\sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subset\{1,2,3\}}
(-1)^{3-|I|}(h_{I,t})_*[Q_t].
$$
At $t=0$ take the fundamental class of the reduced fibre, the sum of its component classes. For each component separately, projection of the signed sum to any one or two slots is zero in degree two: with a varying slot present, toggle an omitted slot and the same class gets opposite signs. If all projected slots are constant the degree-two pushforward is zero. Since the fibres are connected, these projections detect all summands of $H_2(X_t)$. Also $H_3(S'_t)=0$ since the support is a curve. The pair exact sequence and pullback by $1\mapsto z_t$ therefore give an exact sequence of rational mixed Hodge structures
$$
0\longrightarrow H_3(X_t)(-1)\longrightarrow \mathcal E_t
\longrightarrow\mathbb Q(0)\longrightarrow0. \tag{4}
$$
For smooth $t$ this is the Abel–Jacobi extension of the modified diagonal. The lifts of $z_t$ are bounding chains modulo absolute homology, with Hodge filtration giving the period functional [11, §§2.1–2.4]. The cycle is the modified diagonal of Gross–Schoen [12, §1]. The central sequence uses Deligne's mixed Hodge structures [13]. The Tate twist uses homology $H_3$ of weight $-3$ on a smooth fibre, yielding weight $-1$.

### 6.2. The map on extensions

We give the specialization for this pair, since a projection on the graded normalization Jacobians alone would omit parts of the limit mixed Hodge structure. For the proper map $F_X:X\to\Delta$ and $j_X:X\setminus S'\hookrightarrow X$, use the relative cohomology complex $R F_{X*}\,j_{X!}\mathbb Q^H_{X\setminus S'}$ in the derived category of mixed Hodge modules. Proper base change identifies its restriction and its nearby cohomology with the relative groups of the central and nearby pairs. The map from the central restriction to nearby cycles is functorial for the localization triangle of $j_X$. In degree $i$ it gives the usual specialization to the limit mixed Hodge structure on relative cohomology. One can work after finite base change on the unipotent part. Applying duality and $(-1)$ gives a compatible map
$$
H_3(X_{\lim},S'_{\lim})(-1)\longrightarrow H_3(X_0,S'_0)(-1).
$$
Here one uses the exact nearby-cycle functor on the variations over the punctured disk, with the perverse shift, to take the limit of (4). The map of triangles works for the singular total space of the product and for the seven supports passing through nodes [14, §§3.4, 4.3–4.4].

The proper specialization of the fundamental class on each copy of $\mathcal Q$ is the sum of its central components with multiplicity one, as is seen at their general smooth points. Functoriality for the $h_I$ gives $z_{\lim}\mapsto z_0$. Hence there is a map $\mathcal E_{\lim}\to\mathcal E_0$ over the identity on $\mathbb Q(0)$. This is a map of the actual extensions, incorporating chains meeting nodes and the vanishing homology. The componentwise cancellations ensure the lift exists for the same signed cycle at $0$.

Now apply $f_0$ as a map of pairs to $C^3$ with support $f_0(S'_0)$. The target support still has $H_3=0$. Functoriality sends (4) at $0$ to the extension of the pushed cycle. Let $U_{\mathcal K}$ be $H_3(C^3)(-1)$ projected by the rational orbit of §5.2. The pushout of $\mathcal E_{\lim}$ by the composite
$$
H_3(X_{\lim})(-1)\longrightarrow H_3(X_0)(-1)
\xrightarrow{(f_0)_*} H_3(C^3)(-1)\longrightarrow U_{\mathcal K}
$$
is the $\mathcal K$-projection of the central Abel–Jacobi extension of the pushed cycle. Indeed functoriality gives the map of extensions over $1$, and after pushout to the same kernel it is an isomorphism. By §5.3 this extension is $p^2\operatorname{AJ}(\Gamma_{P_\infty}(C))_{\mathcal K}\ne0$.

This comparison factors through the limit of the smooth physical projector. In cohomology each $\rho_i^*$ on $L_{hk_i}$ has the global character $h\chi_i$ after $q_{i,0}^*$. Indeed on $Z_i$ the action of $g$ followed by $\rho_i$ acts on the target by $d^{(r-s_0)\chi_i(g)/c_i}$. Off $Z_i$ the map is constant at the fixed cusp. Specialization is equivariant and compatible with the maps from the quotient curves. Tensoring the three pullbacks places the resulting pure sub-Hodge structure in the $E_+$-part. The compact block can also be identified by restriction to the normalization and trace for $\varphi$; the curve invariant cycle map identifies $H^1(D_{i,0})$ with $W_1H^1_{\lim}(D_i)$. The preceding pushout uses the explicit maps of curves and the full pair specialization, so it requires no choice of a splitting for the rest of $H^3_{\lim}$.

If the rational projected normal function were identically zero on the smoothing, its extension of variations would split. One can see this after clearing the projector denominators: an integral normal function with all values torsion on a connected open disk has a multiple identically zero by analyticity and countability. The corresponding rational extension splits, and its limit and every pushout split as well. This contradicts the computed nonzero extension. The proof applies on the common $Q^3$ and on the product of the cyclic quotients by functoriality.

### 6.3. Very general fibres and a Chow proof

The projected cycle can be defined by applying $E_+$ to the small diagonal on the smooth family $Q^3$. Indeed a one-coordinate term is killed in its varying slot, whose fundamental class is invariant. A two-coordinate term is killed by simultaneous translation in its two varying slots, since those two projected characters have nonzero sum. These identities hold for cycles and make the projection of the modified diagonal independent of $e$. The same argument applies on the cyclic quotient product. The resulting normal function is admissible for a smooth projective algebraic family and a relative homologically trivial cycle [11,14]. The nonzero limit test on one algebraic transverse germ shows that no integral multiple of this normal function vanishes identically on the connected oriented marked parameter containing the germ. After clearing denominators the vanishing locus of each multiple is proper algebraic by Brosnan–Pearlstein [15, Theorem 1.2 in the revised preprint]. Their countable union gives the exceptional sets for the nontorsion assertion. The marking is finite over a dense smooth sextic open, so these proper algebraic subsets have proper closures of their images there.

There is a direct Chow specialization proof of the generic cycle nontriviality. Use the proper reduced models of §4 and the seven-term cycle on $\mathcal Q^3_\Delta$ based at the section. The specialization homomorphism sends the generic cycle to the sum of the relative partial diagonals of the central components by the Cartier fibre and proper pushforward [16, §20.3]. Deck automorphisms commute with this operation, hence so does $E_+$. Apply the proper map $f_0$ and then the rational projector $\mathcal K$. The result has the nonzero Abel–Jacobi value computed above and hence is nonzero in Chow. A geometric generic rational equivalence on this trait would be defined after a finite extension and specialize to zero. The central calculation survives such extensions, with a reduced semistable model obtained by resolving thickness nodes and with all inserted chains collapsed. Also, a geometric generic rational equivalence on the marked parameter would spread to a finite cover of a dense open. It would force vanishing along this test trait: one can choose the transverse smoothing through the general nodal divisor meeting the dense open, or specialize from a dominating parameter to the chosen smooth fibres using a chain of traits. On the smooth family, if a member of a relative Chow class is zero geometrically generically, proper spread and specialization give zero on every fibre. Finally, countable Hilbert or Chow parameter spaces of rational equivalences imply that vanishing on very general complex fibres would give geometric generic vanishing. This Chow argument proves generic Chow nontriviality directly; the generic Abel–Jacobi statement uses §6.2.

## 7. The determinant-odd normal function

Work on the common unpointed $Q^3$, pulled back to the orientation cover. Let $\tau$ denote its deck involution on the parameter. The physical residual generator changes by its negative while the order of the tangent sections is retained, so $a\mapsto-a,\ b\mapsto b$. This acts on the labels of the same unpointed group; changes of an affine origin induce no conjugation on $G$. Define $E_-=\tau^*E_+$. Its orbit is the common-scalar orbit of
$$
(-c_1 a,\ -c_2 a+b,\ -c_3 a-b).
$$
If the two orbits met, the first row would force their relative scalar to be $-1$. The second row would then equate $b$ with $-b$, impossible in odd characteristic. The two sums of Fourier idempotents are therefore orthogonal in the rational group algebra on the smooth family, including as actions by algebraic correspondences. This proof is independent of the choice of complex root used to label the cyclotomic factors.

Apply $E_+-E_-$ to the small diagonal. It acquires the sign of $\tau$ and hence the actual K3 determinant on the $L$-marked base. Applying $E_+$ to its Abel–Jacobi class recovers the nonzero $E_+$-projection, proving nontorsion on very general marked fibres. On the chosen Fermat component $b$ is zero, so the two ordered projected tests restrict to the same orbit after simultaneous multiplication by $-1$. The proof of nontorsion for the difference uses orthogonality on the smooth joint curve and the specialization test for the first projector. Both signed terms are homologically trivial since their nontrivial character support is $H^1$ in every slot, which cannot contribute to the degree-four cohomology of the cycle.
