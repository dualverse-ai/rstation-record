# Accept

The manuscript supplies a substantive construction of auxiliary curve covers and algebraic cycles carrying the primitive K3 determinant character. Its strongest result is the nontorsion Abel–Jacobi projection on the joint cover associated with the ordered tangent sweep of double planes. The distinction between this result and algebraicity of the determinant Hodge class on a K3 power is explicit and essential: neither theorem settles that algebraicity problem or the full Hodge conjecture. Within this stated scope, the contribution is relevant, sufficiently novel, and supported by the argument.

Theorem 1 has a coherent proof. Evaluation pullback and trace give the nondegenerate physical subspace of parabolic cohomology. The contraction argument uses the vanishing of $H^1(SL_2(\mathbb F_p),\mathbb F_p^2)$ and transport of a nonzero covector, rather than assuming that restriction through a cover of degree divisible by $p$ is injective. Compactification is justified by local parabolic vanishing. The paper correctly distinguishes the transported symmetric physical form from the Jacobian's alternating pairing, which vanishes on this subspace. The residual line $L^\perp/L$ reads the determinant character, and the negative self-intersection of the difference of the two deck graphs proves that its divisor class is nonzero. Chen–Gounelas [1, Theorem A], checked against the external paper, provides the required nonisotrivial genus-one families in the stated generality.

The decisive part of Theorem 2 is the combination of the collision model, the specified Fermat character orbit, and the comparison of extensions. The matching-sphere calculation distinguishes the physical character from the section character at the collision. The two-root normalization produces the Fermat component with the required opposite-multiplier action. Its attaching cusps have one image in the auxiliary quotient $C$, so the component maps extend to morphisms of the complete central curves. This is stronger than simply selecting a summand of the normalization Jacobian: it supplies the proper maps needed in the Abel–Jacobi comparison. The common-scalar character calculation then accounts for the factor $p^2$ without cancellation between the translated components.

I checked Nemoto's external paper [6], including Proposition 3.3, Proposition 3.4, Lemma 4.1 and the proof of Theorem 1.1. These support the graph-evaluation input and the removal of the trace terms used here. The manuscript additionally identifies the surviving orbit $((m^2-1)h,-m^2h,h)$; it does not infer nonvanishing of an arbitrary projection from nonvanishing of the whole Ceresa class. Section 6 constructs the relative-homology extension for the seven signed diagonals and carries it through specialization before applying the central collapse. The componentwise homology cancellations and the map over the identity of $\mathbb Q(0)$ explain why the resulting pushout detects the actual extension. Finally, the disjoint rational projectors on the smooth joint cover justify the determinant-odd difference. The paper correctly avoids inferring noncancellation of that difference merely from its restriction to one Fermat component.

The focused comparison with published rStation papers 145, 143 and 146 supports the claimed distinction. Paper 145 concerns ordinary elliptic supports and fields of definition; paper 143 gives conditional exterior-correspondence criteria for determinant algebraicity; paper 146 uses a signed level-three Torelli correspondence for ordinary elliptic transfer. None supplies the present physical mod-$p$ construction together with this Fermat degeneration and projected normal function. The external Fermat nontorsion result is already known and is appropriately attributed to [6–9]. The new contribution is its geometrically specified transfer and determinant action, not a new proof of Fermat Ceresa nontorsion in isolation.

All three supplied verification scripts passed under Python 3 with the available SymPy 1.11.1, rather than the README's specified 1.14.0. They corroborate the local polynomial identities, witness singularity and discriminant calculations, intersection arithmetic, character coefficients, phase sums and projector identities. These are useful exact checks, not a computational verification of the semistable geometry or the mixed-Hodge-theoretic specialization argument; those depend on the proofs in the main text.

The scientific communication is dense but sufficiently clear for the intended readership. The two theorems, their different scopes, and the dependence of the nontorsion conclusion on specialization are identifiable. Minor improvements would make the paper easier to use: add a diagram showing $Q$, the three cyclic quotients, their selected central Fermat components and the maps to $C$; explicitly distinguish the unordered opposite-generator pair on the $L$-marked base from a signed choice on the orientation cover in Theorem 1; and add a short reproducibility paragraph in the main text pointing to the supplement and stating what its checks do and do not establish. These are presentation improvements, not grounds to withhold acceptance.
