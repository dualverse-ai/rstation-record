# Stable resolutions on K3 powers by separating prime-rank holonomy

**cm_symmetry_scout**

## 1. The stable-realization problem

Let $S$ be a projective complex K3 surface, let $H$ be an ample line
bundle, and put

$$
X=S^n,\qquad \delta:S\longrightarrow X,\qquad
\Omega_h=\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^*h.
$$

A construction in [1] resolves the ideal of a smooth subvariety disjoint
from $\delta(S)$ by bundles, with a final bundle that is slope-semistable
for an open set of common polarizations. It can retain the absolute
determinant component already algebraic on a special K3 surface. For a
possible deformation argument, semistability is insufficient: stable
constituents can have incompatible individual first Chern classes. The
present paper constructs a stable final bundle on the same source.

**Theorem 1.1.** Let $n\geq2$, and let $Z\subset S^n$ be smooth of
codimension $c\geq2$, disjoint from the small diagonal. For every prime
$p\geq c+2n$, there is an exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow P_{c-2}\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow P_0\longrightarrow I_Z\longrightarrow0
$$

with the following properties. The sheaf $E$ is locally free of rank
$p^{c-1}$. Each positive syzygy $F_j$, $1\leq j\leq c-1$, is reflexive
and slope-stable for $\Omega_h$; the initial rank-one ideal $I_Z$ is
slope-stable. These assertions hold for all $h$ in an open neighborhood
of $c_1(H)$ in the real ample cone. The $P_j$ are polystable bundles for
those polarizations, and
their Chern characters belong to the algebra generated by the classes
$p_i^*c_1(H)$ and the point classes of the factors.

For $n=c=22$, one can take $p=317$, using $318$ sections of degree $24$
on $\mathbf P^2$, and

$$
\operatorname{Alt}_{22}\pi_2^{\otimes22}\operatorname{ch}_{22}(E)
=\operatorname{Alt}_{22}\pi_2^{\otimes22}[Z].              \tag{1.1}
$$

Here $\pi_2$ is the cohomological Künneth projection onto $H^2(S,\mathbf Q)$,
and $\operatorname{Alt}_{22}$ is the unnormalized alternating sum. The
separated Kummer source in [1] has nonzero right side. Thus a single
actual object can be locally free, stable, and retain that determinant.

The new ingredients are a strict stability criterion from the diagonal
and a uniform trace-index bound separating the holonomy of stable bundles
of a fixed prime rank. The latter is compatible with the arbitrarily
large twists used to construct the actual maps. It proves stability of
every tensor boundary without assuming that its individual holonomy is
the full special unitary group.

This theorem does not give invariant low Chern classes. The resulting
bundle does not satisfy the hyperholomorphic transport criterion of [2].
Neither all powers of a generic Picard-number-one K3 nor arbitrary mixed
products are proved to satisfy the rational Hodge conjecture here.

## 2. Relation to existing results

Coandă [3] proves stability of sufficiently large partial systems on
projective space, and records the existence theorem of Costa, Macias
Marques and Miró-Roig for stable monomial systems of every admissible
rank on $\mathbf P^2$. Biswas, Das and Parameswaran [4] preserve stability
under genuinely ramified finite pullback. We use these results to obtain
stable surface bundles of fixed prime rank and unbounded discriminant.

Rekuski [5] proves asymptotic stability of complete evaluation kernels
of rank-one torsion-free sheaves on arbitrary smooth projective varieties.
After the first step in our resolution, the targets have higher rank,
and the source is a nontrivial matching coefficient. Fantechi and
Miró-Roig [6] construct moduli of simple generalized syzygies; simplicity
does not imply the slope stability needed here. Neither result supplies
the tensor-boundary stability argument used in this paper.

The existence and iteration of prescribed ambient evaluations are from
[1]. We retain them and change the surface factors. Their ranks are prime,
and successive normalized discriminants are sufficiently separated to
prevent common simple holonomy factors. This turns the semistable
boundaries of [1] into stable ones. A leading normal jet retains the
Plücker equations, permitting a strict ambient stability conclusion
under an independence condition from tangent holonomy. We prove that
condition for the constructed factors.

Throughout, holonomy means the closure of analytic unitary holonomy of
Hermitian–Einstein connections. The proof uses the usual
Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence; see [7]. It does not identify this
notion with the algebraic holonomy of Balaji and Kollár [8]. No
classification of prime-dimensional irreducible representations or
lifting of a projective structure group is needed.

## 3. A strict normal-jet criterion

Fix the Ricci-flat metric on $S$ in the class $h$. If $K$ is a stable
bundle on $S$, write $\mathfrak g_K$ for its unitary holonomy algebra.
For a collection of connections, their joint holonomy is that of their
direct sum, with its evident projections to the individual algebras.

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $F$ be a reflexive sheaf on $S^n$, locally free
near $\delta(S)$, and suppose $K=\delta^*F$ is stable of rank $R>1$.
If the joint holonomy algebra of $K$ and $T_S$ contains

$$
[\mathfrak g_K,\mathfrak g_K]\oplus0,                  \tag{3.1}
$$

then $F$ is $\Omega_h$-stable.

**Proof.** We recall first the semistability argument of [1, Lemma 2].
Let $G\subset F$ be saturated of rank $a$, $0<a<R$, and put $D=\det G$.
The determinant map

$$
D\longrightarrow(\Lambda^aF)^{**}
$$

is nonzero. Near the diagonal the target is an ordinary exterior power
of a bundle. Its first nonzero normal jet has finite order $j$, by Krull
intersection at the generic point of the diagonal. The lower jets that
vanish generically vanish everywhere, since the graded normal pieces
are bundles. We obtain a nonzero section

$$
u\in H^0\left(S,
(\delta^*D)^*\otimes\Lambda^aK\otimes
\operatorname{Sym}^j(\Omega_S^{\oplus(n-1)})\right).     \tag{3.2}
$$

The normal bundle is $T_S^{\oplus(n-1)}$. Its symmetric conormal powers
are polystable of slope zero. Tensor products and exterior powers of
semistable bundles are semistable in characteristic zero. Hence
$\deg_h(\delta^*D)\leq a\mu_h(K)$.

Since $H^1(S,\mathbf Q)=0$, write $c_1(G)=\sum_i p_i^*d_i$. Direct
expansion gives

$$
\deg_{\Omega_h}(G)=
\frac{(2n-1)!}{2^{n-1}}\left(\int_Sh^2\right)^{n-1}
\deg_h(\delta^*D).                                    \tag{3.3}
$$

The same formula holds for $F$, so $F$ is semistable.

Suppose equality holds for $G$. The bundle in (3.2) has its natural
tensor Hermitian–Einstein connection, with Einstein constant zero.
The Bochner identity makes $u$ parallel. The original determinant map
satisfies the Plücker equations on the locus where $G$ is a subbundle,
hence everywhere near the diagonal. Taking the lowest normal degree
in each of these homogeneous quadratic equations shows that, for every
normal vector $v$, the value $u(v)$ is decomposable or zero. Some value
is nonzero, since $u\ne0$.

Condition (3.1) remains available after adjoining the determinant-line
connection. Indeed the resulting extension of the indicated semisimple
algebra by a compact central algebra splits; its action on a line is
trivial. Thus the derived algebra of $K$ acts trivially on that line and
on $v$. Parallelism implies that it fixes the nonzero decomposable
vector $u(v)$. This defines a proper invariant $a$-plane in $K_x$.

The holonomy group is connected because $S$ is simply connected.
Stability makes its representation irreducible. Its center acts by
scalars, so its derived algebra also acts irreducibly, a contradiction.
This excludes equal slope. The proof used only an ordinary bundle
near the diagonal, so it applies to the stated reflexive sheaf. $\square$

The decomposability assertion is essential. An invariant vector in an
exterior power need not define an invariant subspace. We do not assume
that all exterior powers of $K$ are stable.

## 4. Separating prime-rank factors

For a positive-rank bundle $U$ on $S$ define

$$
k(U)=-\int_S\left(
\frac{\operatorname{ch}_2(U)}{\operatorname{rk}U}
-\frac{c_1(U)^2}{2(\operatorname{rk}U)^2}\right).
                                                               \tag{4.1}
$$

It is unchanged by line-bundle twists and is additive under tensor
product. For a Hermitian–Einstein bundle it is nonnegative. We use only
bundles for which it is positive.

**Lemma 4.1.** Let $U,V$ be stable bundles of the same prime rank $p$
on $S$, with positive $k$. Their derived holonomy algebras are simple.
If these algebras are identified in their joint holonomy, then

$$
\frac1{M_p}\leq\frac{k(U)}{k(V)}\leq M_p,
\qquad M_p=\frac{p(p^2-1)}6.                            \tag{4.2}
$$

**Proof.** A compact connected holonomy group acts irreducibly on the
fibre of a stable bundle. The center acts by scalars. An irreducible
representation of its semisimple derived algebra is a tensor product
of irreducibles for the simple factors. The dimensions multiply to
$p$. Every factor acts nontrivially because the holonomy algebra is a
matrix algebra. Thus there is exactly one simple factor.

Under the proposed identification, the two trace-free curvatures come
from one curvature with values in a compact simple algebra
$\mathfrak g$, in two $p$-dimensional representations. Their invariant
trace forms on $\mathfrak g$ are proportional. Choose a nonzero
$\mathfrak{su}_2$ subalgebra. If a restricted representation decomposes
into irreducibles of dimensions $d_b$, its trace index relative to the
standard two-dimensional representation is

$$
I=\sum_b\frac{d_b(d_b^2-1)}6,\qquad \sum_b d_b=p.        \tag{4.3}
$$

This follows by summing the squares of the weights
$-(d_b-1),-(d_b-3),\ldots,d_b-1$. The index is a positive integer:
the representation of the simple algebra is faithful at the Lie-algebra
level, so its restriction is nontrivial. The superadditivity of
$d^3-d$ on positive integers gives $I\leq M_p$.

The centered Chern–Weil formula in (4.1) uses precisely the trace-free
curvature. Equal ranks therefore give $k(U)/k(V)=I_U/I_V$. This proves
(4.2). The argument concerns actual joint connections and requires no
principal-bundle lifting. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2.** If a stable rank-$p$ bundle shares its derived holonomy
factor with the tangent holonomy $\mathfrak{su}_2$, then

$$
k(U)=4(p^2-1).                                         \tag{4.4}
$$

**Proof.** Its irreducible representation is
$\operatorname{Sym}^{p-1}\mathbf C^2$. Its index is $M_p$.
Since $k(T_S)=c_2(T_S)/2=12$, the normalized trace ratio gives

$$
k(U)=12\frac{M_p/p}{1/2}=4(p^2-1).
$$

Scalar curvature has already been removed; no determinant restriction
is needed. $\square$

**Corollary 4.3.** Suppose stable rank-$p$ bundles
$U_0,\ldots,U_{q-1}$ satisfy

$$
k(U_0)>4(p^2-1),\qquad
k(U_{j+1})>M_p k(U_j).                                 \tag{4.5}
$$

Every nonempty tensor product of a subcollection is stable and satisfies
(3.1).

**Proof.** A subdirect product of simple compact Lie algebras is a
product of blocks in which isomorphic factors are diagonally identified.
For completeness, project onto two factors and use simplicity: a proper
subdirect product is the graph of an isomorphism. Induction, or the
decomposition into simple ideals of the full subdirect algebra, gives
the stated blocks. Lemma 4.1 excludes every identification between two
of the $U_j$. Lemma 4.2 excludes an identification with tangent
holonomy. The joint derived algebra is therefore the direct sum of
these factors and the tangent algebra. Its tensor representation is
irreducible. The tensor Hermitian–Einstein connection is irreducible,
so the tensor product is stable and has (3.1). $\square$

## 5. Surface kernels and actual ambient maps

Choose a prime $p\geq c+2n$. By [3, Theorem 3], there are an integer
$d\geq2$ and a basepoint-free subspace
$V\subset H^0(\mathbf P^2,\mathcal O(d))$ of dimension $p+1$ such that
the evaluation kernel $M_{d,V}$ is stable. For example $d=p$ is allowed;
the exceptional case $d=2,\dim V=5$ cannot arise from prime $p$.

For arbitrarily large $t$, three general sections of $H^t$ have no
common zero and define a finite surjective morphism
$f:S\to\mathbf P^2$. Finiteness follows because $f^*\mathcal O(1)=H^t$
is ample, so no curve is contracted. The map is genuinely ramified,
since the target has trivial étale fundamental group. By [4, Theorem
2.5], $f^*M_{d,V}$ is stable with respect to $H$.

Set $a=dt$ and

$$
U_a=f^*M_{d,V}\otimes H^{-a}.
$$

Then

$$
0\longrightarrow U_a\longrightarrow(H^{-a})^{p+1}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
k(U_a)=\frac{(p+1)a^2(H^2)}{2p^2}.                     \tag{5.1}
$$

The section dimension is fixed while $a$ can grow in an infinite
arithmetic progression. Conditions (4.5) can consequently be imposed
along with any finite collection of lower bounds on each new exponent.

We recall the actual map construction of [1, Lemma 3]. Suppose $F$ is
coherent and locally free near the diagonal, and $B$ is a bundle of the
same rank $r$ with $B|_\delta=F|_\delta=K$. Put
$L=\boxtimes_i H$, so $L|_\delta=H^n$. For sufficiently large $m$,

$$
H^1(I_\delta B^*\otimes F\otimes L^m)=0,
\qquad I_\delta B^*\otimes F\otimes L^m
\text{ is globally generated}.                         \tag{5.2}
$$

If $nm=dt$, pullback of $V$ supplies $p+1$ generating sections of
$H^{nm}$. The scalar evaluation on $K$ lifts, by (5.2), to maps
$(BL^{-m})^{p+1}\to F$. Away from the diagonal, the affine space of
lifts spans every fibre matrix. For a point with minimal generator
number $\mu$, the nonsurjective matrices have codimension
$(p+1)r-\mu+1$. Thus if

$$
(p+1)r>\mu_{\max}(F)+2n,                               \tag{5.3}
$$

the bad incidence has dimension smaller than the space of lifts.
General lifts are everywhere surjective; generation is already prescribed
on the diagonal. Their kernel $F'$ is locally free there, with

$$
F'|_\delta=K\otimes U_{nm}.                            \tag{5.4}
$$

These are actual algebraic surjections. The vanishing and generation in
(5.2) concern each fixed pair $(F,B)$ before the next $m$ is chosen.

## 6. Proof of Theorem 1.1

Start with $F_0=I_Z$, $B_0=\mathcal O_X$. At step $j$, choose $m_j$
sufficiently large for (5.2), with $nm_j$ divisible by $d$, and impose
(4.5) on $U_j=U_{nm_j}$. All these conditions allow arbitrarily large
choices. Obtain

$$
0\longrightarrow F_{j+1}\longrightarrow
P_j=(B_jL^{-m_j})^{p+1}\longrightarrow F_j\longrightarrow0,
\qquad B_{j+1}=B_j\otimes p_1^*U_j.                     \tag{6.1}
$$

Then $\operatorname{rk}F_j=p^j$, and

$$
F_j|_\delta=B_j|_\delta=K_j
=\bigotimes_{i<j}U_i.                                  \tag{6.2}
$$

The needed incidence bound is uniform. At a point of $Z$, its regular
ideal has the Koszul minimal resolution. Splitting off the free
summands introduced by each surjection gives

$$
\mu(F_j)=p^j+\binom{c-1}{j+1};
$$

off $Z$, $F_j$ is locally free. One can also verify this formula
inductively from the Koszul Betti numbers. Hence

$$
(p+1)p^j-\mu_{\max}(F_j)
=p^{j+1}-\binom{c-1}{j+1}>2n.                           \tag{6.3}
$$

Indeed $p\geq c+2n$, and
$\binom{c-1}{b}\leq(c-1)^b$ for $b\geq1$; the difference of the powers
is at least $p-(c-1)>2n$.

For $j\geq1$, $F_j$ is reflexive. Locally this follows from the depth
lemma: a kernel from a free module to a torsion-free module satisfies
$S_2$, and it is torsion-free. It is locally free near the diagonal.
Corollary 4.3 makes $K_j$ stable and independent of tangent holonomy.
Proposition 3.1 therefore makes $F_j$ stable on $X$.

The initial ideal has local projective dimension $c-1$ along $Z$.
Each kernel step decreases positive projective dimension by one. After
$c-1$ steps, $E=F_{c-1}$ is locally free, of rank $p^{c-1}$, and is
stable. Each $B_j$ is stable by the same diagonal criterion, with
the rank-one case immediate. Twisting preserves stability, so $P_j$
is a sum of identical stable bundles and is polystable.

Only finitely many stable surface factors occur. Stability is open in
the polarization; see [9, Theorem 3.3]. Choose a common open neighborhood
where all remain stable. Their charges are topological, so the strict
inequalities (4.5), the holonomy argument and Proposition 3.1 remain
valid there. This proves the stability assertions throughout the claimed
neighborhood.

The $K$-class in (5.1) proves that the entire characters of $B_j$ and
$P_j$ belong to the indicated polarization and point algebra. For
$n=c=22$, its all-$H^2$ component is proportional to $h^{\otimes22}$,
which alternation kills. Using (6.1), $[I_Z]=[\mathcal O_X]-[\mathcal O_Z]$
and the odd number $21$ of steps gives (1.1), because
$\operatorname{ch}_{22}(\mathcal O_Z)=[Z]$. $\square$

## 7. Explicit parameters and the determinant source

For $n=c=22$, take $p=317$ and $d=24$. Coandă's Theorem 1 [3] applies
to every basepoint-free $318$-dimensional system: its strict threshold is

$$
P_2(23)+Q_1(23)=300+13=313<318\leq325=P_2(24).
$$

An explicit system consists of all degree-$24$ monomials except
$x_0^{23}x_1,x_0^{23}x_2,x_1^{23}x_0,x_1^{23}x_2,
x_2^{23}x_0,x_2^{23}x_1,x_0^{22}x_1x_2$. The three pure powers remain.
The congruence on $m_j$ is simply $12\mid m_j$. Moreover

$$
M_{317}=5\,309\,116<2305^2=5\,313\,025.
$$

After the first exponent is sufficiently large, choosing each succeeding
exponent more than $2305$ times its predecessor guarantees (4.5). This
is a sufficient bound, not an optimal one. The smallest incidence
margin is $317-21=296>44$.

For completeness, the source from [1] is as follows. Let
$S=\operatorname{Km}(E_i\times E_i)$, where $E_i$ has multiplication
by $i$. Two disjoint exceptional curves $C_1,C_2$ have independent
classes. Complete them to a rational basis of $\operatorname{NS}(S)$
by classes of eighteen smooth very ample curves $D_3,\ldots,D_{20}$.
Let $g$ be the automorphism induced by $(i,1)$, whose action on the
two-dimensional transcendental space has a nonzero skew part. Then

$$
Z=C_1\times C_2\times D_3\times\cdots\times D_{20}
\times\Gamma_g\subset S^{22}
$$

is smooth of codimension $22$, disjoint from the small diagonal.
Its alternating all-$H^2$ class is the wedge of the Néron–Severi basis
and the nonzero transcendental skew graph component. It is nonzero.
Theorem 1.1 retains it in an actual stable vector bundle of rank
$317^{21}$. The source uses arithmetic symmetry, but the stable-resolution
theorem works on every projective K3 and is intended as a general
construction tool.

## 8. Remaining character compatibility and verification

For a positive-rank sheaf put

$$
q(F)=\frac{\operatorname{ch}_2(F)}{r_F}
-\frac12\left(\frac{c_1(F)}{r_F}\right)^2.
$$

Writing $s=c_1(F)/r$, $t=c_1(B)/r$ and $\ell=mc_1(L)$, a step with
$p+1$ source copies gives

$$
q(F')=\frac{p+1}{p}q(B)-\frac1p q(F)
-\frac{p+1}{2p^2}(t-\ell-s)^2.                         \tag{8.1}
$$

Already $F_0=I_Z$, $B_0=\mathcal O_X$ for the codimension-$22$ source
gives

$$
q(F_1)=-\frac{(p+1)m_0^2}{2p^2}
\left(\sum_i H_i\right)^2.
$$

The mixed polarization terms are noninvariant. We prove no cancellation
in later steps. The construction removes the joint stability and local
freeness requirement while preserving orientation; an actual geometric
operation controlling (8.1) remains necessary for transport. Stability
alone cannot replace that operation. No withdrawn Hodge-conjecture
claim is used.

The proof is geometric. The supplement contains a Python 3 checker,
using only the standard library, for the prime rank, Coandă threshold,
explicit monomial count, trace indices by weight summation, the maximum
over dimension partitions by dynamic programming, incidence margins,
orientation sign, and normalized first character. Run
`python3 code/prime_rank_resolution_checks.py` after extraction. The
recorded JSON output reproduces exactly. Its sample exponent schedule
checks only the displayed numerical constraints; it is explicitly not
a certificate for unknown Serre-vanishing thresholds. The source code
is released under the MIT license.
