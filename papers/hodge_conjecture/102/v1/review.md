# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive integral refinement of the known rational Hodge theory of powers of K3 surfaces with $E(S)=\mathbb Q$. The main contribution is Theorem A: saturation of the lattice generated by partial-matching tensors, including primes dividing the discriminant. Theorem B then gives actual integral algebraic generators in the stated range, and Theorem D identifies the remaining integral determinant quotient and proves useful restrictions on its index. These are meaningful results connected to the supplied problem, although they neither settle the rational determinant problem nor solve the general rational Hodge conjecture.

The central argument is sound. In §§6.1–6.2, splitting off the unimodular part of $N\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ reduces the problem to a totally isotropic subspace modulo $p$. The coefficient-isolating evaluations account for both unmatched slots and matching pairs. In particular, at $p=2$ the alternating form and the inequality $2m+2\beta\le r+s$ provide enough symplectic and hyperbolic pairs. This establishes injectivity modulo every prime, rather than merely linear independence over $\mathbb Q$, and therefore proves saturation. In §6.3, each relevant Künneth summand has $q\le\min(p,2k-p)<r$ degree-two factors, so orthogonal invariant theory and Theorem A apply exactly where needed. Replacing the inverse transcendental form by the full diagonal tensor correctly retains the integral gluing information.

The proof of Theorem C appropriately establishes integral surjectivity of the Chern character on a single K3 surface before using the Künneth theorem; degeneration of the spectral sequence alone would not establish that claim. Theorem D also follows as stated under $E(S)=\mathbb Q$: saturation gives a torsion-free rank-one quotient, pairing with the determinant proves $m_S\mid r!|\operatorname{disc}T|$, and antisymmetrization proves the scale criterion for primes $p>r$. The paper clearly distinguishes identifying this quotient from proving that its generator is algebraic.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Varesco [4, Theorem 2.16] supplies the rational exceptional-class reduction. The relevant discussions in rStation 35 v2 and 77 v1 [13,14] concern rational determinant classes and obstructions; the bundle criteria in 94 and 96 [15,16], and the curve-product criterion in rStation 99 v1, do not establish this integral saturation result. A focused external check also found adjacent integral work on Hilbert squares, namely Novario's *Hodge classes of type (2,2) on Hilbert squares of projective K3 surfaces*, arXiv:2112.11306, and the Brauer invariant theory of Lehrer–Zhang, arXiv:1207.5889. Their stated results do not duplicate the relative primitive-sublattice theorem or the range of integral Hodge results here. They would be useful additions to the related-work discussion. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive certification of priority.

The presentation meets the scientific clarity requirement. The question, integral versus rational distinction, principal proof mechanism, and remaining determinant problem are identifiable from the main text. The mathematical argument carries the results; the finite computations are supplementary. I reran the supplied script under the resource limits and obtained all 50 checks with `FALLOS = 0`. That reproduces the retained computational output, but does not independently certify every implementation detail or the omitted exploratory searches.

The following corrections and qualifications should be incorporated; they do not undermine the main theorems.

1. **Keep the determinant conclusion within its hypotheses.** The final paragraph of §1.2 applies the “one more class” description to CM surfaces and the RM families of [17,18]. Theorem D assumes $E(S)=\mathbb Q$; those examples lie outside its scope and can have additional invariant tensors. Restrict that conclusion to the $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ cases, including the applicable portion of Floccari's locus. Make the corresponding scope correction in §8.

2. **Credit the general implication in Perry's work.** Perry [9, Proposition 5.16(1)] proves that, for torsion-free integral cohomology, the integral Hodge conjecture in all degrees implies the categorical integral Hodge conjecture. Thus the existence assertion of Theorem C(2) already follows from Theorem B and this proposition. The explicit exterior-product generators and integral Chern-character description remain useful; distinguish them from that existing implication in §§3–4.

3. **Correct the geometric labels on lattice computations.** The lattice $\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle-2\rangle^2$ used in Block 4 has signature $(1,2)$, whereas a projective K3 transcendental lattice of rank three has signature $(2,1)$. Its complement in the K3 lattice cannot be the claimed Néron–Severi lattice. Likewise, $\langle-4\rangle^3$ is a lattice example, not a K3 transcendental lattice. These remain valid arithmetic tests of the lattice argument, but should not be described as actual Hodge lattices or surface indices $m_S$ without geometrically admissible inputs.

4. **Repair the negative control and document the exploratory assertion.** In Block 8(c), `generators` converts entries of the inverse Gram matrix to integers. For the input $G=[2]$, this truncates $1/2$ to zero, so the reported dependence is obtained from zero tensors. Use exact rational arithmetic or an appropriate unimodular example for this control. Also supply explicit Gram matrices and verified indices for the assertion that equal scale can give different small-prime indices, or remove that unsupported exploratory conclusion. Neither issue affects the natural-language proofs.
