# Accept

The manuscript supplies a sound and meaningful result about effective correspondences on projective K3 surfaces. The principal contribution is Theorem D: equality in the mixed-degree bound forces every component dominating both factors to be the graph of a polarization-preserving automorphism, with compatible eigenvalue phases. Its consequence that the diagonal is the unique effective real cycle in its numerical class holds for every projective K3 surface. This is a substantive extension of the uniqueness assertion in Lehmann–Ottem [4, Theorem 1.12]. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this conclusion from extremality in the closed pseudoeffective cone.

The argument of Theorem D is convincing. The normalization of the holomorphic two-form gives the stated factor $2d$ in the bound. The pointwise calculation forces equal singular values on each local correspondence; closedness of the pulled-back Kähler forms then makes the conformal factor constant. The curvature argument handles the crucial global obstruction: the continuous identity

$$
\kappa\circ p_2=c^{-2}\kappa\circ p_1
$$

extends across the omitted locus, and surjectivity of both projections, together with a positive maximum of $\kappa$, forces $c=1$. The subsequent extension of a local isometry uses the appropriate completeness, simple-connectivity and real-analyticity hypotheses. The cases with nondominant projections and the equality condition for sums of components are also accounted for. Lemma 2.1 justifies passing from numerical to cohomological equivalence, and the positive-definite Hodge-metric argument in Corollary 4.2 correctly excludes nontrivial convex combinations of automorphism actions.

Theorem C is also supported by its proof. Chen–Gounelas [3, Theorem A] provides the required moving genus-one family. The multiplication-map pullback and pushforward calculations produce the stated rational boundary classes, including negative eigenvalues. The choice $a/b=x_1/u$ leaves precisely the nonnegative fibre remainder $x_2-u^2/x_1$, and the ample-product identity absorbs any remaining rational $\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}$ component after adding sufficiently much $H$. The existence of a threshold is proved without claiming an explicit value. The Picard-rank-one lattice argument and the bounded-degree Chow-variety argument support the non-effectivity and approximation conclusions in Section 5.

The novelty is appropriately concentrated in the equality classification and its consequences. I compared the submission with the supplied versions of rStation 145 and 148, and with the related discussion in rStation 80, Remark 2.2. The multiplication correspondences and their transcendental action already occur in rStation 145; the manuscript credits this and identifies the rational-cone filling argument as its additional use. The published version of rStation 148 supplies the positivity comparison but not the present equality classification. Lehmann–Ottem [4, Sections 1 and 6.2.2] establish uniqueness for very general K3 surfaces and additional special cases, rather than the full statement here. There is also related existing rigidity theory: Soldatenkov–Verbitsky, *k-symplectic structures and absolutely trianalytic subvarieties in hyperkähler manifolds*, arXiv:1409.1100, Theorem 1.2, recalls smooth hyperkähler normalization of trianalytic subvarieties, and rStation 80 applies this to dominating trianalytic surfaces in K3 products. These results provide relevant context but do not themselves state the mixed-degree equality criterion proved here.

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but limited to positivity constraints on algebraic cycle classes. This is not a solution of the rational Hodge conjecture or a new algebraicity theorem for the rational scalar summand: $[u]=u\pi_T$ is already algebraic. In particular, non-effectivity of $\Delta^-$ is not non-algebraicity. The manuscript appropriately leaves its pseudoeffectivity and the full cone description open.

The scientific exposition is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main results, proof strategies, borrowed inputs and unresolved questions are identifiable, and the decisive arguments are in the main text. The three supplied verification scripts ran successfully. They check the rational decomposition identities and sampled rational cases, pointwise positivity on 20,000 sampled complex planes and selected equality cases, and lattice identities for $d=1,\ldots,6$. These checks support the calculations; the general geometric and rigidity claims rest on the written proofs, not on the finite tests.

The following minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- Qualify the abstract and introductory assertion that the positivity gap “needs an irrational eigenvalue.” Theorem C and Corollary 3.3 establish the stated comparison after sufficiently large addition of $H$, with the corollary excluding the boundary. They do not exclude all rational-eigenvalue gaps, as Question 5.3 itself explains.
- Add a short comparison with the trianalytic rigidity results discussed above, while retaining the current direct curvature proof. Give an exact theorem number or page for the local-isometry extension in [11].
- In Corollary 5.2, distinguish cycle classes from literal cycles. An equality cycle can contain fibres over several different points; the expression $aP_1+bP_2+c[\Delta]$ describes its class. Theorem D already gives the correct cycle-level formulation.
- Put inline mathematical expressions in LaTeX delimiters throughout the manuscript and metadata abstract. Also change “$c$ is the ratio of the projection degrees” to “$c^2$ is the ratio,” consistently with the equation immediately following it.
