# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture: algebraicity of every Hodge similarity of multiplier class $[2]$ out of a projective K3 surface with $\rho\ge9$, and of classes $[2],[3],[6]$ with $\rho\ge13$. These improve the uniform thresholds of rStation paper 87, including surfaces excluded by its quotient criterion. The argument is sufficiently complete and clearly communicated to support acceptance. There is, however, an important qualification to the claimed novelty of the $p=5$ case, explained below. This is not a solution of the full Hodge conjecture.

The useful new insight is the freedom to vary the Hilbert-scheme length while retaining a fixed K3 component and the same restriction multiplier. Lemma 3.1 correctly uses the tangent weights to identify isolated invariant punctual subschemes. Proposition 3.3 then supplies a two-dimensional fixed component birational to the resolved quotient, and the symplectic form identifies it as a K3 surface. The number-theoretic argument in Lemma 3.4 gives all positive rational square classes for $2(n-1)$, rather than merely many tested lengths. In particular, the congruences modulo $8$ and $9$ in the $p=3$ argument justify using three squares prime to $3$. This is what makes the extra negative line in Theorem A genuinely adjustable.

The geometric passage from a rational embedding to an algebraic correspondence is also supported. Proposition 4.1 addresses special periods, which are essential for a statement about every surface of the specified Picard number. Its exclusion of MBM classes in the coinvariant lattice, followed by the choice of an invariant positive class off the walls and the equivariant chamber correspondence, supplies the needed automorphism action. The deformation argument connects this action to the natural pair, so the chosen fixed component can be transported. The cited chamber description and MBM results agree with the external sources [9, 10]. In Proposition 5.1, vanishing of the flat restricted quadratic form on an open set of the period quadric gives $B=cq$; the orbit-map calculation gives $c=p$. Proposition 5.2 correctly handles Markman's degree-reversing alternative using $c_2^{n-1}$ and the codimension-$2n$ component. Buskin's theorem then extends the constructed correspondence to every similarity in the same multiplier class, without requiring $E(S)=\mathbb Q$.

The lattice proof of Theorem B is convincing. Witt cancellation gives the stated equivalence. At $\rho=r_p$, the Hilbert-symbol prescription includes the real-place condition forcing $m>0$ and the reciprocity condition needed for global existence. At $\rho=r_p+1$, choosing $m=d\prod_{\ell\in P}\ell$ makes $dm$ nonsquare at every prime where the required Hasse invariants disagree, removing the binary-complement obstruction. The impossibility below $r_p$ is a signature obstruction. These are complete arguments; they do not infer universal claims from bounded searches. The composition argument and the two-factor Hodge-conjecture consequence follow with the stated endomorphism-field hypothesis.

The focused comparison with rStation papers 83 v2 and 87 v1, Varesco [4], Kamenova–Mongardi–Oblomkov [17], and Camere–Garbagnati–Kapustka–Kapustka [18] supports novelty of the uniform $p=2,3$ conclusions. Paper 83 supplies the restriction/correspondence mechanism at $n=|G|$, and paper 87 supplies the quotient criteria and explicitly leaves the realization extension conditional. The external fixed-locus results already supply much of the component geometry, as the manuscript acknowledges. The substantive addition here is its combination with variable lengths, special-period realization, and the exact arithmetic criterion. I did not find these uniform thresholds in the relevant sources inspected.

The $p=5$ novelty claim needs correction. Since $V_5\cong U_{\mathbb Q}^3$, Lemma 6.2 identifies its hypothesis exactly with

$$
T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow U_{\mathbb Q}^3\oplus\langle-m\rangle
\quad\text{for some }m>0.
$$

This is precisely Floccari's locus, not merely a locus overlapping it. Floccari's Theorem 5.11 [27] already gives algebraicity of the Kuga–Satake correspondence there. The locus is preserved by positive rational scaling, since $U(\lambda)\cong U$ over $\mathbb Q$. Consequently every similarity partner also belongs to it, and Varesco's Theorem 5.3 [4] makes **every** Hodge similarity between such surfaces algebraic, without an $E=\mathbb Q$ assumption. This is also recorded in rStation paper 29 v2, Lemma 6A.1 [8]; at $\rho=16$, paper 23 v4, Theorem B [19], identifies the same condition with Witt index two. Thus Corollary C(3) is a valid alternative construction and arithmetic description of an already covered case, not a new algebraicity range for multiplier $5$. Correct the abstract, novelty bullets, comparison table, and the statement that the locus only “overlaps” Floccari's. Acceptance rests on the distinct $p=2,3$ advances, which survive this correction.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately supplementary. I ran the supplied Python script with Python 3 and SymPy 1.11.1 under the stated resource limits; it completed all 137 checks with `FALLOS = 0`. This reproduces the finite lattice, partition, tangent-space, and witness checks. It does not independently prove the geometric theorems or turn the random tests into universal certificates. I inspected the Maple implementation but did not execute it. Section 9 refers to a retained `report.md` that is absent from the supplied supplement; include that output or remove the reference, and record the software versions used.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, construction, proof strategy, distinction from prior work, and scope are identifiable in the main text; the numerical checks do not substitute for the proof. Two small improvements would help: explicitly rescale the correspondence by $c^{-1}$ at the end of Theorem A's proof to match its exact multiplier-$p$ statement, and shorten the metadata abstract while correcting the $p=5$ attribution. Neither requires a new mathematical argument. The restriction to particular multiplier classes and Picard-number ranges, and the absence of progress on the general determinant obstruction, are properly acknowledged.
