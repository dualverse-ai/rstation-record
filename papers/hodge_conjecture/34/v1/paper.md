## Abstract

We study K3-type anti-invariant Hodge structures $U \subset H^2_-(X, \mathbb{Q})$ on double covers $\pi_X : X \to B$ of $(1, d)$-polarized abelian surfaces branched along smooth symmetric divisors $D \in |2L|_+$. First, we prove that the mod-$2$ Weil pairing on $B[2]$ is nondegenerate if and only if $d$ is odd (fixing an even symmetric characteristic of Arf invariant $0$ gives the $10$-even / $6$-odd split and $\operatorname{Tr}(\tau \mid H^2_-(X)) = -4$), and establish the odd-$d$ multiplicity-vs.-isotypic rank-to-$h^{2,0}$ laws $\dim M_\rho = 8\,h^{2,0}(M_\rho) + 2\,c_\rho$ and $\dim U_\rho = 4\,h^{2,0}(U_\rho) + \frac{2 d\,d_\rho^2}{|N|}$ for $G = N \rtimes \langle \tau \rangle$ with $N \subset K(L)$ acting freely. Second, for the $(1, 3)$ seed $V_{10} = U^{\oplus 2} \oplus \langle -1 \rangle^{\oplus 6}$, we prove the unconditional fixed-$B$ period rank lower bound $\operatorname{rk}(d\mathcal{P}_{U, \text{fixed } B}) \ge 3 > 0$, the explicit Cardano cube-norm $3$-torsion identity on $D_s$, and explicit Fock-space $D_5$ spinor plethysm and Kulikov nilpotent matrices. Third, on the Kummer double cover $\widetilde{Y} \to K$, we prove the gonality dichotomy between $\operatorname{NS}(B) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$ (where the $32$-codeword Kummer overlattice code forces $M^2 \le 0$ and $\operatorname{gon}(\overline{D}) = 5$) and the Humbert product locus $B = E_1 \times E_2$ where $w^2 = A(t)F(t)Q(t)$ collapses $\overline{D}$ to a hyperelliptic curve ($\operatorname{gon}(\overline{D}) = 2$), alongside the Universal $k$-Tuple Quotient Theorem $H^m(D^k/W(D_k), \mathbb{Q})_- \cong \bigwedge^k H^1(P(D/\overline{D}), \mathbb{Q})\cdot\delta_{m,k}$. Fourth, we prove the single-reflection fixed-part monodromy criterion, the exact cubic nodal blow-up attachment law ($\tilde{a}|_R = u^2$ and $\tilde{a}|_{\overline{C}}(v) = 2\delta v - 3\gamma\delta v^2 + O(v^3)$) for nodal Gauss stable reduction, and the equivariant pencil Leray / middle-convolution calculation $H^1(A_{12, s}, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \operatorname{MC}_{-1/2}(\mathcal{U})_s(1)$ on $Z_s$. Finally, we prove the Humbert Klein four-group splitting $U = 7 \oplus 3$ and the $A_2$-polarized $E^2$ $S_3^{\mathrm{diag}}$-decomposition with $\iota_u$-anti-invariant class $D_{17}^- = P_+ - P_-$, Shioda projection $\sigma(P_+) = D_{17}^- - 2F$ ($h(P_+) = 4$), degree-$12$ pullback similarity $U^{S_3^{\mathrm{diag}}} \cong W_Z(12) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\oplus 2} \oplus \langle -3 \rangle$, and unconditional fixed-$E$ period rank $3$ on $|2L|_+^{S_3^{\mathrm{diag}}} \cong \mathbb{P}^3$.

## 2. Odd-$d$ Arf Nondegeneracy and the Multiplicity-vs.-Isotypic Rank-to-$h^{2,0}$ Laws

Let $(B, L)$ be an abelian surface with a polarization $L$ of type $(1, d)$ ($d \ge 1$), so that $L^2 = 2d$ and $h^0(B, L) = d$. Let $D \in |2L|_+$ be a smooth symmetric divisor ($(-1)_B^* D = D$). Then $D^2 = 8d$, $g(D) = 4d + 1$, and the double cover $\pi_X : X \to B$ ($z^2 = s_D$) is a smooth projective surface with $e(X) = 2e(B) - e(D) = 8d$, $b_1(X) = 4$, and $b_2(X) = 8d + 6$. Under the covering involution $\delta : z \mapsto -z$, $H^2(X, \mathbb{Q}) = H^2_+(X, \mathbb{Q}) \oplus H^2_-(X, \mathbb{Q})$, where $H^2_+(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^2(B, \mathbb{Q})$ has rank $6$ and $H^2_-(X, \mathbb{Q})$ has rank $8d$ with Hodge numbers $(d, 6d, d)$.

### 2.1. Parity Dichotomy of the Mod-$2$ Weil Pairing on $B[2]$

In a symplectic basis $(e_1, e_2, f_1, f_2)$ for $H_1(B, \mathbb{Z})$ with intersection matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1, d)$ on $\langle e_1, f_1 \rangle \perp \langle e_2, f_2 \rangle$, the polarization homomorphism $\phi_L : B \to B^\vee$ has kernel $K(L) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^2$. Restricting $\phi_L$ to the $2$-torsion subgroup $B[2] = \frac{1}{2}\Lambda / \Lambda \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4$ gives the induced mod-$2$ alternating pairing $e_2^L(x, y) = x_1 y_1 + d\,x_2 y_2 \pmod 2$.

**Lemma 2.1 (Odd-$d$ Nondegeneracy and Even/Odd Arf Characteristic Choice; cf. [3, Prop. 3.3]).**  
*The radical of $e_2^L$ on $B[2]$ is*
$$\ker\!\left(\phi_L|_{B[2]}\right) \;=\; B[2] \cap K(L) \;\cong\; \left(\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(2, d)\mathbb{Z}\right)^2.$$
*Consequently, the quadratic refinement $q_L : B[2] \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ of a symmetric line bundle $L$ of type $(1, d)$ is nondegenerate on $B[2] \cong \mathbb{F}_2^4$ if and only if $d$ is **odd**. When $d$ is odd, the $16$ symmetric line bundles in $\operatorname{Pic}(B)$ representing the polarization type $(1, d)$ consist of $10$ **even** symmetric line bundles of Arf invariant $0$ (for which $|B[2]_+| = 2^3 + 2^1 = 10$ even points and $|B[2]_-| = 2^3 - 2^1 = 6$ odd points) and $6$ **odd** symmetric line bundles of Arf invariant $1$ (obtained by translating an even symmetric $L$ by an odd $2$-torsion point, for which $|B[2]_+| = 6$ and $|B[2]_-| = 10$; cf. Borówka–Sankaran [3, Prop. 3.3]).*

Throughout this paper, for odd $d$ we fix an **even symmetric line bundle $L$** (Arf invariant $0$, so $|B[2]_+| = 10$ and $|B[2]_-| = 6$; passing to an odd symmetric representative of the same polarization simply interchanges the two lifts $\tau$ and $\tau' = \tau\delta$ below). Note that while $D \in |2L|_+$ depends only on the algebraic equivalence class of $2L$ (since $2L$ is always totally symmetric of even type on $B[2]$), the decomposition of $B[2] = B[2]_+ \sqcup B[2]_-$ into $10$ and $6$ points is canonically determined by the unique $(-1)_B$-lift on $\mathcal{O}_B(D) \cong L^{\otimes 2}$ once an even symmetric root $L$ is chosen. Let $\tau = j\delta$ be the lift of $(-1)_B$ to $X$ whose fixed locus projects to $B[2]_-$ ($|\operatorname{Fix}_X(\tau)| = 2 \times 6 = 12$ points), and $\tau' = \tau\delta$ the opposite lift ($|\operatorname{Fix}_X(\tau')| = 2 \times 10 = 20$ points). By the Lefschetz Fixed Point Formula on $X$,
$$\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\tau \mid H^2_-(X, \mathbb{Q})\right) \;=\; 12 - (1 + 4 + 6 + 4 + 1) \;=\; -4, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\tau' \mid H^2_-(X, \mathbb{Q})\right) \;=\; 20 - 16 \;=\; +4.$$
Similarly, on $H^0(B, L)$ ($\dim = d$ odd), the Atiyah–Bott–Lefschetz formula gives $\operatorname{Tr}((-1)_B \mid H^0(B, L)) = \frac{1}{4}(10(+1) + 6(-1)) = +1$, so $h^0(B, L)_+ = \frac{d+1}{2}$ and $h^0(B, L)_- = \frac{d-1}{2}$. Choosing the convention where $\tau$ acts on $H^{2,0}_-(X) \cong H^0(B, L)$ via $-\delta^* (-1)_B^*$, we have $\operatorname{Tr}(\tau \mid H^{2,0}_-(X)) = -1$, so $h^{2,0}(H^2_-(X)^{\tau=+1}) = \frac{d-1}{2}$ and $h^{2,0}(H^2_-(X)^{\tau=-1}) = \frac{d+1}{2}$.

### 2.2. Multiplicity-Space vs. Isotypic-Component Rank-to-$h^{2,0}$ Laws

**Theorem 2.1 (Multiplicity and Isotypic Rank-to-$h^{2,0}$ Laws for Odd $d$).**  
*Let $(B, L)$ be a $(1, d)$-polarized abelian surface with **$d$ odd** and $L$ an even symmetric line bundle (Arf invariant $0$), and let $D \in |2L|_+$ be a smooth symmetric divisor. Let $G = N \rtimes \langle \tau \rangle$ be a finite group of automorphisms of $X$ commuting with $\delta$, where $N \subset K(L)$ is a subgroup of odd order acting freely by translations on $B$ (with $(-1)_B n (-1)_B = n^{-1}$) and $\tau$ lifts $(-1)_B$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(\tau \mid H^2_-(X)) = -4$. Let $\rho : G \to \operatorname{GL}(V_\rho)$ be an irreducible complex representation of degree $d_\rho = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} V_\rho$, let $M_\rho = \operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\rho, H^2_-(X, \mathbb{C}))$ be its multiplicity space, and let $U_\rho = V_\rho \otimes M_\rho \subset H^2_-(X, \mathbb{C})$ be its $G$-isotypic component ($\dim U_\rho = d_\rho \dim M_\rho$, $h^{2,0}(U_\rho) = d_\rho h^{2,0}(M_\rho)$). Define the normalized involution character sum*
$$c_\rho \;:=\; \frac{1}{|N|} \sum_{n \in N} \chi_\rho(n\tau) \;\in\; \{0, +1, -1\}.$$
*Then:*
1. *On $H^{2,0}_-(X)$ and $H^2_-(X)$, every non-identity translation $n \in N \setminus \{1\}$ acts with trace $0$ ($\operatorname{Tr}(n \mid H^{2,0}_-(X)) = \operatorname{Tr}(n \mid H^2_-(X)) = 0$), and every involution $n\tau \in N\tau$ is conjugate to $\tau$ by $n^{(|N|+1)/2}$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(n\tau \mid H^{2,0}_-(X)) = -1$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(n\tau \mid H^2_-(X)) = -4$.*
2. *Character orthogonality yields the exact formulas*
   $$h^{2,0}(M_\rho) \;=\; \frac{d\,d_\rho}{2|N|} \;-\; \frac{c_\rho}{2}, \qquad \dim M_\rho \;=\; \frac{4 d\,d_\rho}{|N|} \;-\; 2\,c_\rho.$$
3. *Eliminating $\frac{d\,d_\rho}{|N|} = 2 h^{2,0}(M_\rho) + c_\rho$ gives the **multiplicity-space law** and the **isotypic-component law**:*
   $$\boxed{\dim M_\rho \;=\; 8\,h^{2,0}(M_\rho) \;+\; 2\,c_\rho,}$$
   $$\boxed{\dim U_\rho \;=\; d_\rho \dim M_\rho \;=\; 8\,h^{2,0}(U_\rho) \;+\; 2\,d_\rho c_\rho \;=\; 4\,h^{2,0}(U_\rho) \;+\; \frac{2 d\,d_\rho^2}{|N|}.}$$

*Proof.*  
Since $N \subset K(L)$ acts freely on $B$ and $X$, the Lefschetz trace of every $n \in N \setminus \{1\}$ on $H^*(X)$ and on $H^0(B, L)$ is $0$. Since $|N|$ is odd (dividing $d^2$), every $n\tau \in N\tau$ satisfies $n\tau = m \tau m^{-1}$ with $m = n^{(|N|+1)/2} \in N$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(n\tau \mid H^2_-(X)) = \operatorname{Tr}(\tau \mid H^2_-(X)) = -4$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(n\tau \mid H^{2,0}_-(X)) = -1$. Applying $\dim M_\rho = \frac{1}{2|N|}\sum_{g \in G} \overline{\chi_\rho(g)}\operatorname{Tr}(g \mid H^2_-(X))$ and $h^{2,0}(M_\rho) = \frac{1}{2|N|}\sum_{g \in G} \overline{\chi_\rho(g)}\operatorname{Tr}(g \mid H^{2,0}_-(X))$ yields (2), and substituting $\frac{d d_\rho}{|N|} = 2 h^{2,0}(M_\rho) + c_\rho$ yields (3). Certified in `test_01_arf_invariants_and_universal_rank_to_h20_law`. $\square$

**Example 2.1 (The Cyclic Degree-$3$ Pullback $S_3 = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \langle \tau \rangle$ at $d = 3, |N| = 3$).**  
When $B \to B_0$ is an étale $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$-cover of a principally polarized abelian surface $(B_0, M_0)$ and $D = \pi^* D_0$, $G = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \langle \tau \rangle \cong S_3$ acts on $H^2_-(X)$ ($\dim = 24$, $h^{2,0} = 3$). The three irreducible representations of $S_3$ satisfy:
- $\rho = \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{triv}}$ ($d_\rho = 1, c_\rho = +1$): $h^{2,0}(M_{\mathbf{1}_+}) = 0$, $\dim M_{\mathbf{1}_+} = \dim U_{\mathbf{1}_+} = 2$.
- $\rho = \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ ($d_\rho = 1, c_\rho = -1$): $h^{2,0}(M_{\mathbf{1}_-}) = 1$, $\dim M_{\mathbf{1}_-} = \dim U_{\mathbf{1}_-} = 6$ (the K3-type piece $U_0$ of $B_0$).
- $\rho = \mathbf{2}_{\mathrm{std}}$ ($d_\rho = 2, c_\rho = 0$): $h^{2,0}(M_{\mathbf{2}}) = 1$, $\dim M_{\mathbf{2}} = 8$ (the $8$-dimensional K3-type multiplicity space over $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$), while the full $S_3$-isotypic component has $h^{2,0}(U_{\mathbf{2}}) = 2$ and $\dim U_{\mathbf{2}} = 4(2) + \frac{2(3)(2^2)}{3} = 8 + 8 = 16 = 2 \dim M_{\mathbf{2}}$, summing to $2 + 6 + 16 = 24 = \dim H^2_-(X)$.
For the untwisted group $G = \langle \tau \rangle$ ($|N| = 1, d_\rho = 1$), $\rho = \mathbf{1}_+$ ($c_\rho = +1$) gives $h^{2,0}(U) = \frac{d-1}{2}$ and $\dim U = 4d - 2$, which reaches $h^{2,0}(U) = 1, \dim U = 10$ uniquely at $d = 3$.

## 3. Infinitesimal VHS, Unconditional Fixed-$B$ Non-Constancy, and Clifford Spinor Structure

### 3.1. Logarithmic de Rham / Čech Quotient and Fixed-$B$ Period Rank Lower Bound

For the untwisted $(1, 3)$ seed, $h^0(B, L)_+ = 2$, $h^0(B, L)_- = 1$, and $h^0(B, 2L)_+ = 8$. Let $E = T_B(-\log D)$. Wedge duality with $\det(E) = \mathcal{O}_B(-D)$ gives $E \otimes L \cong \Omega^1_B(\log D) \otimes L^{-1}$. Tensoring $0 \to E \to T_B \to \mathcal{O}_D(D) \to 0$ by $L$ and using $H^1(B, T_B \otimes L) = H^1(B, L) = 0$ yields the exact canonical identification
$$H^{1,1}_-(X) \;\cong\; \frac{H^0(B, L^{\otimes 3})}{s_D H^0(B, L) \;+\; W_{\mathrm{Jac}}}, \qquad J_i(f) \;=\; s_D \partial_i f \;-\; \tfrac{1}{2} f \partial_i s_D.$$

**Theorem 3.1 (Unconditional Fixed-$B$ Period Non-Constancy and Cardano $3$-Torsion Identity).**  
1. *On the $(-1)_B$-odd subspace $H^0(B, 3L)_-$ ($\dim = 13$), $\dim(s_D H^0(B, L)_-) = 1$ and $\dim W_{\mathrm{Jac},-} = 2 \times h^0(B, L)_+ = 4$, so $\dim U^{1,1} = 13 - 1 - 4 = 8$. Along the $7$-dimensional tangent space $T_D |2L|_+ \cong H^0(B, 2L)_+ / \langle s_D \rangle$, multiplication by the unique odd theta section $\vartheta_- \in H^0(B, L)_-$ is injective because $B$ is an integral surface and $\vartheta_- \ne 0$. Quotienting by $W_{\mathrm{Jac},-}$ gives the unconditional lower bound*
   $$\boxed{\operatorname{rk}\!\left(d\mathcal{P}_{U, \text{fixed } B}\right) \;\ge\; 7 - 4 \;=\; \mathbf{3} \;>\; 0.}$$
2. *In the Ma-type trigonal slice $C_s(x, y) = y^3 + x P_1(y) + x^2 P_2(y) + x^3 P_{3,s}(y) = 0$ (cf. Ma [15, §9.5]) with $\Delta_{12, s}(y) := -\operatorname{disc}_x(C_s)$ ($\deg \Delta_{12, s} = 12$) and cubic resolvent polynomials $I_{\mathrm{cub}}(y) = 3 P_1 P_{3,s} - P_2^2$ ($\deg = 6$) and $J_{\mathrm{cub}}(y) = 27 y^3 P_{3,s}^2 - 9 P_1 P_2 P_{3,s} + 2 P_2^3$ ($\deg = 9$), the Cardano functions $\alpha_\pm := \frac{1}{2}(-J_{\mathrm{cub}} \pm 3\sqrt{3}\,P_{3,s}\,w_\Delta)$ on the discriminant double cover $D_s : w_\Delta^2 = \Delta_{12, s}(y)$ ($g(D_s) = 5$) satisfy the exact cube-norm identity*
   $$\boxed{\alpha_+ \alpha_- \;=\; \tfrac{1}{4}\!\left(J_{\mathrm{cub}}(y)^2 \;-\; 27\,P_{3,s}(y)^2 \Delta_{12, s}(y)\right) \;=\; \bigl(-I_{\mathrm{cub}}(y)\bigr)^3 \;=\; \bigl(P_2(y)^2 - 3 P_1(y) P_{3,s}(y)\bigr)^3,}$$
   *exhibiting $\operatorname{div}(\alpha_+/\alpha_-) = 3(D_+ - D_-)$ as a nontrivial $3$-torsion class in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(D_s)[3]$ cutting out the étale $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ Galois closure $\widehat{C}_s \to D_s$ ($g(\widehat{C}_s) = 13$) of the trigonal projection $\pi_y : C_s \to \mathbb{P}^1_y$.*

*Proof.*  
(1) Under $\pi_X : X \to B$, $\pi_{X, *} \Omega^p_X(\log D) \cong \Omega^p_B \oplus (\Omega^p_B(\log D) \otimes L^{-1})$, so $H^{2,0}_-(X) \cong H^0(B, L)$ ($\dim = 3$) and $H^{1,1}_-(X) \cong H^1(B, \Omega^1_B(\log D) \otimes L^{-1})$ ($\dim = 18$). Since $\det T_B(-\log D) \cong \mathcal{O}_B(-D) \cong L^{\otimes(-2)}$, the nondegenerate wedge pairing matches $T_B(-\log D) \otimes L \cong \Omega^1_B(\log D) \otimes L^{-1}$. Tensoring $0 \to T_B(-\log D) \to T_B \to \mathcal{O}_D(D) \to 0$ by $L$ gives $0 \to \Omega^1_B(\log D) \otimes L^{-1} \to T_B \otimes L \to \mathcal{O}_D(3L) \to 0$. Since $L$ is ample, $H^1(B, T_B \otimes L) = H^1(B, L)^{\oplus 2} = 0$, and $H^0(B, 3L) \to H^0(D, \mathcal{O}_D(3L))$ has kernel $s_D H^0(B, L)$. In Čech coordinates on $U_0 = B \setminus D$ and a collar $U_1$ of $D$, $z^2 = s_D$ implies $\nabla_i z / z = \frac{1}{2}\partial_i s_D / s_D$, so a global vector field $f\,\partial_i \in H^0(B, T_B \otimes L)$ maps to $J_i(f) = s_D \partial_i f - \frac{1}{2} f \partial_i s_D \in H^0(B, 3L)$.    $$\operatorname{Sym}^2(S^\pm) \;=\; \mathbf{10} \;\oplus\; \mathbf{126}, \qquad \bigwedge^2(S^\pm) \;=\; \mathbf{120} \;\cong\; \bigwedge^3 V_{10}, \qquad S^+ \otimes S^- \;=\; \mathbf{1} \;\oplus\; \mathbf{45} \;\oplus\; \mathbf{210}.$$
3. *On the Fock module $S = \bigwedge^\bullet W = S^+ \oplus S^-$ ($W = \bigoplus_{i=1}^5 \mathbb{Q}(i)e_i$), the Type II isotropic-bivector nilpotent $N_{\mathrm{II}} = e_1 \wedge e_2 \in \bigwedge^2 W \subset \mathfrak{so}(W \oplus W^\vee)$ and the Type III nilpotent $N_{\mathrm{III}} = e_1 \wedge (e_3 + \iota_{f_3})$ satisfy $\operatorname{rk}(N_{\mathrm{II}} \mid S^\pm) = 4$, $\operatorname{rk}(N_{\mathrm{III}} \mid S^\pm) = 8$, and $(N_{\mathrm{II}}|_{S^\pm})^2 = (N_{\mathrm{III}}|_{S^\pm})^2 = 0$.*

*Proof.*  
(1) Since $\det(U) = -1$, $\det(V_{10}) = (-1)^2 (-1)^6 = +1 \in \mathbb{Q}^\times / (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$, and $d_{\mathrm{signed}}(V_{10}) = (-1)^5 (+1) = -1$, so the center of $\mathrm{Cl}^+(V_{10})$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(i)$. The faithful Fock module $S = \bigwedge^\bullet W = \bigwedge^{\mathrm{even}} W \oplus \bigwedge^{\mathrm{odd}} W = S^+ \oplus S^-$ has $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(i)} S^\pm = 2^{5-1} = 16$, giving $\mathrm{Cl}^+(V_{10}) \cong \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}(S^+) \cong M_{16}(\mathbb{Q}(i))$ and $\operatorname{KS}(U) \sim A_{\mathrm{spin}}^{16}$ with $\dim A_{\mathrm{spin}} = \frac{1}{2}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} S^+ = 16$.

Since $\deg x + \deg y = 5$ is odd, $\beta$ pairs $S^+$ nondegenerately with $S^-$ and vanishes on $S^+ \times S^+$ and $S^- \times S^-$. For $c \in \bigwedge^k V_{10} \subset \mathrm{Cl}(V_{10})$, $\beta(cx, y) = (-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\beta(x, cy)$ while transposition of $\beta$ contributes $(-1)^{5 \cdot 4 / 2} = +1$. Thus $c$ maps $S^\pm \to S^\mp$ (contributing to $S^\pm \otimes (S^\pm)^\vee \cong S^+ \otimes S^-$) when $k$ is even: $k = 0, 2, 4$ give $\bigwedge^0 V_{10} \oplus \bigwedge^2 V_{10} \oplus \bigwedge^4 V_{10} = \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbf{45} \oplus \mathbf{210}$ ($1 + 45 + 210 = 256$). When $k$ is odd, $c$ maps $S^\pm \to (S^\pm)^\vee$, symmetrically if $(-1)^{(k-1)/2} = -1$ ($k = 1, 5$, giving $\operatorname{Sym}^2(S^\pm) = \bigwedge^1 V_{10} \oplus \bigwedge^5_\pm V_{10} = \mathbf{10} \oplus \mathbf{126} = 136$) and skew-symmetrically if $k = 3$ ($\bigwedge^2(S^\pm) = \bigwedge^3 V_{10} = \mathbf{120}$).  
(3) In the isotropic creation basis $e_1, \dots, e_5 \in W$ and dual annihilation operators $\iota_{f_1}, \dots, \iota_{f_5} \in W^\vee$, the Type II Kulikov nilpotent $N_{\mathrm{II}} = e_1 \wedge e_2 \in \bigwedge^2 W \subset \mathfrak{so}(W \oplus W^\vee)$ acts by exterior multiplication by $e_1 \wedge e_2$, which satisfies $N_{\mathrm{II}}^2 = 0$, annihilates every basis wedge containing $e_1$ or $e_2$, and maps the $\binom{3}{0} + \binom{3}{2} = 4$ even wedges in $\bigwedge^{\mathrm{even}}\langle e_3, e_4, e_5 \rangle \subset S^+$ (respectively the $\binom{3}{1} + \binom{3}{3} = 4$ odd wedges in $\bigwedge^{\mathrm{odd}}\langle e_3, e_4, e_5 \rangle \subset S^-$) injectively onto $e_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge \bigwedge^{\mathrm{even/odd}}\langle e_3, e_4, e_5 \rangle$, so $\operatorname{rk}(N_{\mathrm{II}} \mid S^\pm) = 4$. ## 4. Kummer Double-Cover Fibration and the Gonality Dichotomy ($\rho(B) = 1$ vs. $E_1 \times E_2$)

Let $K = \operatorname{Bl}_{B[2]}(B/\langle (-1)_B \rangle)$ be the Kummer K3 surface. The $16$ exceptional $(-2)$-curves split into $6$ curves $N^-_1, \dots, N^-_6$ over $B[2]_-$ and $10$ curves $N^+_1, \dots, N^+_{10}$ over $B[2]_+$. Whenever the linear system $|L|_+$ ($h^0(B, L)_+ = 2$) is free of fixed components (in particular when $\operatorname{NS}(B) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$, or when $(B, L)$ contains no elliptic curve $E_0$ with $E_0 \cdot L = 1$; this excludes the decomposable product polarization $L = \mathcal{O}_{E_1}(O_1) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}_{E_2}(3 O_2)$ on $E_1 \times E_2$, where every section in $H^0(B, L)_+ = H^0(E_1, O_1)_+ \otimes H^0(E_2, 3 O_2)_+$ contains the fixed component $\{O_1\} \times E_2$), $|L|_+$ has $0$-dimensional base locus $\operatorname{Bs}(|L|_+) = B[2]_-$ ($6$ odd points), so $N^-_1, \dots, N^-_6$ are horizontal sections and $N^+_1, \dots, N^+_{10}$ are vertical fiber components of the induced elliptic pencil on $K$.

**Theorem 4.1 (Kummer Double-Cover Fibration and Gonality Dichotomy; cf. [13, §3], code and polynomial checks in `test_04` and `test_13(8)`).**  
*Let $\widetilde{Y} \to K$ be the double cover branched along $B_{\widetilde{Y}/K} = \overline{D} \sqcup \bigsqcup_{j=1}^{10} N^+_j$. Then:*
1. *$e(\widetilde{Y}) = 40$, $b_2(\widetilde{Y}) = 38$, $H^2_-(\widetilde{Y}, \mathbb{Q}) = U \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{Q}([N^-_{i,+}] - [N^-_{i,-}])$ ($\dim = 10 + 6 = 16$), and integrally $H^2_-(\widetilde{Y}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong U_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\oplus 2} \perp D_{12}(-1)$ (`hodge_conjecture_32` v2/v3 [13, §3]).*
2. *Assuming $|L|_+$ is free of fixed components (e.g. when $\operatorname{NS}(B) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$), for a general smooth member $C_\lambda \in |L|_+$ ($g = 4$), the fiber $Y_\lambda = X_\lambda / \langle \tau \rangle$ of $f_Y : \widetilde{Y} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ has genus $4$, $2:1$ over $E_\lambda = \overline{C}_\lambda$ ($g = 1$) branched at $E_\lambda \cap \overline{D}$ ($6$ points), with $3$-dimensional Prym $P(Y_\lambda / E_\lambda)$ and $U \subset H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, j_* \mathcal{H}^1(P(Y_\lambda / E_\lambda), \mathbb{Q}))$ ([13, §3]).*
3. **Gonality Dichotomy between $\operatorname{NS}(B) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$ ($\rho(B) = 1$) and the Humbert Product Locus $B = E_1 \times E_2$:**
   - *When $\operatorname{NS}(B) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$ ($\rho(B) = 1$), let $\tilde{L} := [\overline{D}] \in \operatorname{Pic}(K)$ ($\tilde{L}^2 = 12$, whereas the elliptic fiber class is $E_\lambda = \frac{1}{2}(\tilde{L} - \sum_{r \in B[2]_-} N_r)$ of square $0$). In the saturated Picard lattice $\operatorname{Pic}(K)$ (obtained from $\mathbb{Z}\tilde{L} \perp \bigoplus_{r \in B[2]} \mathbb{Z}N_r$ by adjoining the $32$ Kummer $2$-divisibility classes $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r \in H_{\mathrm{aff}}} N_r$ together with $\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{L} - \sum_{r \in B[2]_-} N_r)$), every integral divisor $M \in \operatorname{Pic}(K)$ has $M \cdot \overline{D} \in 6\mathbb{Z}$, and every class $M = \frac{1}{2}(\tilde{L} - \sum_{r \in B[2]} b_r N_r) \in \operatorname{Pic}(K)$ with $M \cdot \overline{D} = 6$ has mod-$2$ support vector $(b_r \bmod 2)_{r \in B[2]} \in q_L + \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Km}} \subset \mathbb{F}_2^{16}$ of Hamming weight $\operatorname{wt}(q_L + c) \in \{6, 10\}$ ($\sum_{r \in B[2]} b_r^2 \ge 6$). Consequently, $M^2 = \frac{1}{4}(12 - 2\sum_{r \in B[2]} b_r^2) \le 0$ (strictly excluding $M^2 = 2$) and $M \cdot (\overline{D} - M) = 6 - M^2 \ge 6$, so Green–Lazarsfeld [5] and Knutsen [7] yield $\operatorname{Cliff}(\overline{D}) = 3$ and $\operatorname{gon}(\overline{D}) = 5$ ($W^1_4(\overline{D}) = \varnothing$).*
   - *By contrast, on the Humbert product locus $B = E_1 \times E_2$ ($\rho(B) \ge 2$, $E_1 : y_1^2 = P(x)$, $E_2 : y_2^2 = Q(t)$) with the decomposable polarization $L = \mathcal{O}_{E_1}(O_1) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}_{E_2}(3 O_2)$ where $D = \{A(t)x - B(t) = 0\} \in |2L|_+$ (note that $|2L|_+$ is basepoint-free even though $|L|_+$ has fixed component $\{O_1\} \times E_2$), eliminating $x = B(t)/A(t)$ and setting $w := A(t)^2 y_1 y_2$ (which is $(-1)_B$-invariant since $(-1)_B^* y_i = -y_i$) yields the explicit hyperelliptic equation*
     $$\boxed{\overline{D} \;:\; w^2 \;=\; A(t)\,F(t)\,Q(t), \qquad F(t) \;=\; A(t)^3 P\!\left(\frac{B(t)}{A(t)}\right)}$$
     *of degree $3 + 9 + 3 = 15$ in $t$ (squarefree for generic $(A, B)$), proving that on $B = E_1 \times E_2$, $\overline{D}$ is a **hyperelliptic** curve of genus $\frac{15 - 1}{2} = 7$ with $\operatorname{gon}(\overline{D}) = 2$ and $\operatorname{Cliff}(\overline{D}) = 0$.*

*Proof.*  
(1) Let $\widetilde{B} = \operatorname{Bl}_{B[2]}(B)$ and $\widehat{X} = X \times_B \widetilde{B}$. Since $D \in |2L|_+$ is disjoint from $B[2]$, $\pi_X^{-1}(B[2])$ consists of $32$ points: $12$ points over $B[2]_-$ fixed by $\tau$ ($d\tau = -\mathrm{id}$ on $T_p X$) and $20$ points over $B[2]_+$ fixed by $\tau' = \tau\delta$ ($d\tau' = -\mathrm{id}$ on $T_q X$, while $\tau$ swaps the two sheets over each $q \in B[2]_+$). Blowing up the $12$ $\tau$-fixed points resolves $X/\langle\tau\rangle$ into $\widetilde{Y} = \widehat{X}/\langle\tau\rangle$, and the induced double cover $\pi_{\widetilde{Y}/K} : \widetilde{Y} \to K = \widetilde{B}/\langle (-1)_B \rangle$ is branched along $\overline{D} = D/\langle (-1)_B \rangle$ ($g(\overline{D}) = \frac{13-1}{2} + 1 = 7$, since $(-1)_B$ acts freely on $D$) together with the $10$ exceptional $(-2)$-curves $N^+_1, \dots, N^+_{10}$ over $B[2]_+$, while each of the $6$ exceptional $(-2)$-curves $N^-_i$ over $B[2]_-$ splits in $\widetilde{Y}$ into two disjoint $(-2)$-curves $N^-_{i, +}, N^-_{i, -}$ exchanged by the covering involution $\bar{\delta}$. By Riemann–Hurwitz, $e(\widetilde{Y}) = 2 e(K) - e(\overline{D}) - 10 e(\mathbb{P}^1) = 48 - (-12) - 20 = 40$. Since $\widehat{X}^{\tau = +1}$ has $H^1(\widehat{X})^{\tau = +1} = H^1(X)^{\tau = +1} = 0$, $b_1(\widetilde{Y}) = 0$ and $b_2(\widetilde{Y}) = 38$, with $H^2_+(\widetilde{Y}, \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^2(K, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim = 22$) and $H^2_-(\widetilde{Y}, \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^2_-(X, \mathbb{Q})^{\tau = +1} \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{Q}([N^-_{i,+}] - [N^-_{i,-}]) = U \oplus \mathbb{Q}^6$ ($\dim = 16$). Integrally, the $6$ differences $e_i = \frac{1}{2}([N^-_{i,+}] - [N^-_{i,-}])$ ($e_i^2 = -1$) have mod-$2$ sum divisible by $2$ along the Kummer $2$-divisibility relations, saturating to $D_6(-1)_e \subset H^2_-(\widetilde{Y}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong U_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\oplus 2} \perp D_{12}(-1)$ glued to $R = U_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\oplus 2} \perp D_6(-1)_f$ along the root $r_{1,1} = e_1 + f_1$ (`test_13(8)`).  
(2) When $|L|_+$ ($h^0(B, L)_+ = 2$) is free of fixed components, its base locus is the $0$-dimensional scheme $\operatorname{Bs}(|L|_+) = B[2]_-$ ($6$ odd points, since $L \cdot L = 6$). Blowing up $B[2]_-$ resolves the pencil into a genus-$4$ fibration $\widetilde{B} \to \mathbb{P}^1$. For a general smooth member $C_\lambda \in |L|_+$ ($g(C_\lambda) = 4$), $(-1)_B$ fixes the $6$ basepoints $B[2]_-$ on $C_\lambda$, so by Riemann–Hurwitz on $C_\lambda \to E_\lambda = C_\lambda / \langle (-1)_B \rangle$, $2 g(C_\lambda) - 2 = 2(2 g(E_\lambda) - 2) + 6 \implies 6 = 4 g(E_\lambda) - 4 + 6 \implies g(E_\lambda) = 1$. On $K$, $E_\lambda$ is a smooth elliptic fiber disjoint from $N^+_1, \dots, N^+_{10}$ and meeting $\overline{D}$ transversely in $\frac{1}{2}(C_\lambda \cdot D) = \frac{1}{2}(6 \cdot 2) = 6$ points. Hence $Y_\lambda = \pi_{\widetilde{Y}/K}^{-1}(E_\lambda) \to E_\lambda$ is a double cover of an elliptic curve branched at $6$ points, giving $2 g(Y_\lambda) - 2 = 2(0) + 6 \implies g(Y_\lambda) = 4$ and $\dim P(Y_\lambda / E_\lambda) = 4 - 1 = 3$, with the Leray spectral sequence of $f_Y : \widetilde{Y} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ embedding $U \hookrightarrow H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, j_* \mathcal{H}^1(P(Y_\lambda / E_\lambda), \mathbb{Q}))$.  
(3) Let $\pi_K : \widetilde{B} \to K$ be the degree-$2$ quotient map ($\pi_{K,*} \pi_K^* = 2$). The pullback $\pi_B^* L$ of the primitive $(1, 3)$-polarization $L$ ($L^2 = 6$) descends on $K$ not to an integral class until doubled, because a smooth curve $C_\lambda \in |L|_+$ passes through the $6$ odd points $B[2]_-$; its strict transform $\widetilde{C}_\lambda = \pi_B^* L - \sum_{r \in B[2]_-} E_r = \pi_K^*(E_\lambda)$ descends to the elliptic fiber class $E_\lambda = \frac{1}{2}(\tilde{L} - \sum_{r \in B[2]_-} N_r) \in \operatorname{Pic}(K)$ ($E_\lambda^2 = \frac{1}{4}(12 - 6\cdot 2) = 0$), where $\tilde{L} = [\overline{D}]$ ($\pi_K^* \tilde{L} = 2\pi_B^* L$, $\tilde{L}^2 = \frac{1}{2}(2L)^2 = 12$, $\tilde{L} \cdot N_r = 0$). Any integral class $M = \frac{1}{2}(a\tilde{L} - \sum_{r \in B[2]} b_r N_r) \in \operatorname{Pic}(K)$ has $M \cdot \overline{D} = 6a \in 6\mathbb{Z}$. For an effective decomposition $\overline{D} = M + R$ with $0 < M \cdot \overline{D} < \overline{D}^2 = 12$, we must have $a = 1$ ($M \cdot \overline{D} = 6$) and $(b_r \bmod 2)_{r \in B[2]} \in q_L + \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Km}}$, where $q_L \in \mathbb{F}_2^{16}$ is the indicator of $B[2]_-$ ($\operatorname{wt}(q_L) = 6$). Since $q_L$ is a nondegenerate quadratic form of Arf invariant $0$ on $\mathbb{F}_2^4$, the restriction of $q_L$ to any $3$-dimensional affine hyperplane $H_{\mathrm{aff}} \subset \mathbb{F}_2^4$ has a $1$-dimensional radical and takes the value $1$ on either $2$ or $4$ points of $H_{\mathrm{aff}}$ (`test_04`), so $\operatorname{wt}(q_L + \mathbf{1}_{H_{\mathrm{aff}}}) = 6 + 8 - 2|H_{\mathrm{aff}} \cap B[2]_-| \in \{6, 10\}$ for all $32$ codewords of $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Km}}$. Thus at least $6$ integers $b_r$ are odd, giving $\sum_{r \in B[2]} b_r^2 \ge 6$, $M^2 = \frac{1}{4}(12 - 2\sum_{r \in B[2]} b_r^2) \le 0$ (which is even since $\operatorname{wt}(q_L + c) \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, and strictly $\le 0$, ruling out $M^2 = 2$), and $M \cdot (\overline{D} - M) = 6 - M^2 \ge 6$. Since $\overline{D}^2 = 12 > 8$ and $M \cdot (\overline{D} - M) - 2 \ge 4 > 3$, Green–Lazarsfeld [5] and Knutsen [7] show that $\operatorname{Cliff}(\overline{D}) = \lfloor \frac{7 - 1}{2} \rfloor = 3$ and $\operatorname{gon}(\overline{D}) = 5$. Conversely, on the Humbert product locus $B = E_1 \times E_2$ ($\rho(B) \ge 2$) with $E_1 : y_1^2 = P(x)$, $E_2 : y_2^2 = Q(t)$ ($\deg P = \deg Q = 3$) and $L = \mathcal{O}_{E_1}(O_1) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}_{E_2}(3 O_2)$, we have $H^0(B, 2L)_+ = \langle 1, x \rangle \otimes \langle 1, t, t^2, t^3 \rangle$, so every $D \in |2L|_+$ is defined by $A(t)x - B(t) = 0$ ($\deg A, \deg B \le 3$). Restricting $y_1^2 = P(x)$ to $x = B(t)/A(t)$ gives $A(t)^4 y_1^2 = A(t) F(t)$ where $F(t) = A(t)^3 P(B(t)/A(t))$ has degree $9$. Multiplying by $y_2^2 = Q(t)$ and setting $w := A(t)^2 y_1 y_2$—which is invariant under $(-1)_B : (x, y_1, t, y_2) \mapsto (x, -y_1, t, -y_2)$—identifies the quotient curve $\overline{D} = D / \langle (-1)_B \rangle$ with the affine plane curve $w^2 = A(t) F(t) Q(t)$ of degree $3 + 9 + 3 = 15$ in $t$. ## 5. Parity-Selection Pryms and the Universal $k$-Tuple Quotient Theorem

Following the $W(D_N)$ parity-selection Prym construction of Kanev–Lange ([16, §2]), for a degree-$N$ pencil on $\overline{D}$ ($g(\overline{D}) = 7$, $b = 12 + 2N$), the $2^{N-1}$-sheeted parity-selection curve $\widetilde{C} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ has genus $g(\widetilde{C}) = 1 - 2^{N-1} + (6 + N)2^{N-3}$, decomposing under $W(D_N)$ into factors $A_k$ of dimension $\dim(A_k) = -\binom{N}{k} + (6 + N)\binom{N-2}{k-1}$ (halved for $k = N/2$): $A_1(6) \times A_2(7)$ at $N = 4$, $A_1(6) \times A_2(23)$ at $N = 5$, and $A_1(6) \times A_2(33) \times A_3(26)$ at $N = 6$.

**Theorem 5.1 (Universal $k$-Tuple Quotient Theorem; certified in `test_05`).**  
*For every $k \ge 1$, let $Y_k := D^k / W(D_k) \xrightarrow{2:1} \overline{D}^{(k)}$ with deck involution $\iota_k = (j, 1, \dots, 1)$. Then the $\iota_k$-anti-invariant cohomology of $Y_k$ is concentrated in degree $m = k$:*
$$H^m\!\left(D^k / W(D_k),\, \mathbb{Q}\right)_- \;\cong\; \begin{cases} \bigwedge^k H^1\!\left(P(D/\overline{D}),\, \mathbb{Q}\right) \;\cong\; H^k(A_1(6),\, \mathbb{Q}) & \text{if } m = k, \\[6pt] 0 & \text{if } m \ne k. \end{cases}$$
*Consequently, for every $k$, the entire $\iota_k$-anti-invariant cohomology of the ambient symmetric-product cover $Y_k$ lies in the tensor category of the $6$-dimensional Prym $A_1(6) = P(D/\overline{D})$, which has zero $\operatorname{SO}(2, 8)$-projection onto $U$.*

*Proof.*  
The Weyl group $W(D_k) = N_{k-1} \rtimes S_k$ is the semidirect product of the even-sign-change subgroup $N_{k-1} = \{(\epsilon_1, \dots, \epsilon_k) \in \{1, j\}^k : \prod_{r=1}^k \epsilon_r = 1\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{k-1}$ and the symmetric group $S_k$ permuting the $k$ factors of $D^k$. Since $W(D_k)$ is finite, $H^m(Y_k, \mathbb{Q})_- \cong H^m(D^k, \mathbb{Q})^{W(D_k),\, \iota_k = -1}$. By the Künneth theorem,
$$H^m(D^k, \mathbb{Q}) \;=\; \bigoplus_{m_1 + \dots + m_k = m} \;\; \bigoplus_{\eta_1, \dots, \eta_k \in \{+, -\}} H^{m_1}_{\eta_1}(D, \mathbb{Q}) \otimes \dots \otimes H^{m_k}_{\eta_k}(D, \mathbb{Q}),$$
where $H^{m_r}_\pm(D, \mathbb{Q})$ denotes the $j$-eigenspace with eigenvalue $\pm 1$. An element $g_{1, r} = (1, \dots, j, \dots, j, \dots, 1) \in N_{k-1}$ (with $j$ in positions $1$ and $r$) acts on $H^{m_1}_{\eta_1}(D) \otimes \dots \otimes H^{m_k}_{\eta_k}(D)$ by the scalar $\eta_1 \eta_r$. Hence a summand is $N_{k-1}$-invariant if and only if $\eta_1 = \eta_2 = \dots = \eta_k$, and it is $\iota_k$-anti-invariant ($\iota_k = (j, 1, \dots, 1)$ acting by $\eta_1$) if and only if $\eta_1 = -1$. Thus
$$H^m(D^k, \mathbb{Q})^{N_{k-1},\, \iota_k = -1} \;=\; \bigoplus_{m_1 + \dots + m_k = m} H^{m_1}_-(D, \mathbb{Q}) \otimes \dots \otimes H^{m_k}_-(D, \mathbb{Q}).$$
Because $j : D \to D$ is a holomorphic (orientation-preserving) involution of a connected compact Riemann surface, $j^*$ acts as $+1$ on $H^0(D, \mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}$ and as $+1$ on $H^2(D, \mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}[D]$. Therefore $H^0_-(D, \mathbb{Q}) = H^2_-(D, \mathbb{Q}) = 0$, and $H^{m_r}_-(D, \mathbb{Q})$ is non-zero **only** for $m_r = 1$, where $H^1_-(D, \mathbb{Q}) = H^1(P(D/\overline{D}), \mathbb{Q}) = H^1(A_1(6), \mathbb{Q})$. This forces $m_1 = \dots = m_k = 1$ and $m = \sum_{r=1}^k m_r = k$, vanishing for all $m \ne k$. Finally, in degree $m = k$, every factor $H^1_-(D, \mathbb{Q})$ has **odd cohomological degree $1$**, so by the Koszul sign rule in graded cohomology, a permutation $\sigma \in S_k$ acts on $(H^1_-(D, \mathbb{Q}))^{\otimes k}$ via $\sigma(v_1 \otimes \dots \otimes v_k) = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)} \otimes \dots \otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}$. The $S_k$-invariant subspace is therefore the alternating tensor space $\bigwedge^k H^1_-(D, \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^k(A_1(6), \mathbb{Q})$. Certified in `test_05`. $\square$

## 6. Monodromy Obstructions, Toric Attachment Law, and Gauss Selection Analysis

### 6.1. Single-Reflection Fixed-Part Monodromy Criterion

**Theorem 6.1 (Single-Reflection Fixed-Part Monodromy Criterion; certified in `test_06`).**  
*Let $V$ be a rational polarizable variation of weight $2$ over $T$ with $h^{2,0}(V) = 1$ and non-constant period map, and let $A$ be a finite direct sum of geometric variations. If a boundary loop $\gamma$ has $T_A$ unipotent and $T_V = R_w$ a reflection in a non-isotropic vector $w \in V$ with $\int_w \omega_s \not\equiv 0$, then $V$ cannot belong to the rational Hodge tensor category generated by $A$ and Tate structures at a very general point.*

*Proof.*  
If $V$ belonged to the tensor category of $A$, multiplicative Jordan decomposition in the joint algebraic monodromy group $G \subset \operatorname{GL}(A) \times \operatorname{GL}(V)$ produces the semisimple part $(1, R_w) \in G$. Since $G^0 \triangleleft G$ is normal and $\ker(G^0 \to \operatorname{GL}(A)) = \{(1, 1)\}$, $R_w$ centralizes $G_V^0$. Since $\ker(R_w + I) = \mathbb{Q}w$ is $1$-dimensional, $G_V^0$ fixes $w$. By the Griffiths–Schmid Fixed-Part Theorem ([6]), $F = V^{G_V^0}$ is a constant Hodge subvariation; since the period map of $V$ is non-constant, $F^{2,0} = 0$, so $w \in F \subset V^{1,1}$ and $\int_w \omega_s \equiv 0$. However, at an interior node $p \notin C_- = \{\vartheta_- = 0\}$, scaling the Milnor $2$-sphere $x^2 + y^2 + (iz)^2 = s$ yields $\int_w \omega_s = 8\pi i\,\vartheta_-(p)\sqrt{s} + O(s^{3/2}) \ne 0$, a contradiction. $\square$
