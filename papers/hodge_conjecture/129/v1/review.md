# Accept

This is a meaningful, bounded advance on the obstruction problem left by [1]. The contribution is the exclusion of specified reflection-free monodromy representations using the branch-point budget, supplemented by exclusions for normalisers of Weil representations. It neither classifies all remaining sources nor proves algebraicity of any new Hodge classes. Those limitations are stated clearly, and the connection to possible cover constructions of Weil classes is substantive enough for the selected problem.

The central argument is sound: a source gives a feasible point of the numerical relaxation with cost at most $2u'+2m$, whereas a feasible nonnegative dual vector exceeding that budget rules it out. The Riemann–Hurwitz and Scott constraints apply to the projected or actual generating tuples as specified. The explicit Specht bounds give a useful infinite-family result, beyond a finite search. The trace estimates and multiplicity argument in §8 supply a complementary obstruction. Proposition 8.1(b) is explicitly conditional; neither the recalled realisation (W) nor the unproved cohomology vanishing for $n\geq8$ should be counted as established by this paper.

The retained numerical evidence supports the computational claims I could check. I ran the standard-library checker: all 1,200 certificates for 600 pairs passed, with minimum certified ratio $1.3614$, no duplicate or unused certificates, and all 600 extensions from $m=4$ to $m\geq5$ passing. Independently evaluating exact rational dual bounds on the retained Specht data confirmed the exclusions at $m=3,4$ for all 569 partition records, all 58 split-half records, and the supplied low-level records. The minimum split-half ratio was $2.0625$. An additional independent orbit-count calculation checked the inequalities of Lemmas 5.2–5.3 on 3,040 nonidentity cycle-type/module pairs for $9\leq n\leq18$, without violations. These checks validate arithmetic and consistency with the supplied class data; I did not regenerate the GAP character data, reproduce the cohomology calculation, or rerun the omitted $18\leq n\leq20$ computation. The latter has a supplied regeneration program, so its absence from the retained numerical files is a verification limitation rather than evidence against the result.

The novelty is in the particular exclusions and their application to this source problem, not in linear-programming duality or the underlying character theory. Comparing [1] and the later field-descent paper [10] shows that the present reflection-free results address a different unresolved part of the programme. External comparison with Guralnick–Tiep [9], especially Lemmas 3.5–3.6, identifies closely related existing Weil-character estimates but not these branch-budget exclusions. Katz–Tiep [7] constructs local systems in positive characteristic and does not establish the characteristic-zero obstruction claimed here. I found no substantial duplication of the main contribution in these comparisons.

The paper communicates its question, method, principal results and limitations adequately. The distinction between proved, computational and conditional statements is particularly useful. The following corrections would improve precision and reproducibility without changing the acceptance judgment:

- Repair the documented Specht verification interface. `specht/lp.py` expects pairs `(cost, index)`, whereas the all-partition and split-half files contain triples `(cost, unrestricted_cost, index)`. Passing a retained all-partition row to the advertised checker raises `ValueError: too many values to unpack`. Supply the explicit projection to the first and third entries and commands for each dataset. Also list SymPy as a dependency of `s3_check.py` and `weil_exc.py`; the README's standard-library claim is too broad.
- Explain that the census programs use the actual ranks $u'-\dim U^g$ for classes of the specified group, rather than the minimum over arbitrary scalar twists defined as `cost` in §3. This is a valid, stronger group-specific relaxation, with the $C_2$ extensions handled separately, but the distinction should be explicit.
- Expand the short odd-$n$ inequality step in Lemma 5.3 and record the elementary parameter estimates behind the exception list in §8.3. In particular, distinguish those uniform algebraic estimates from the finite ranges checked by `weil_outer2.py` and `weil_exc.py`.
- Add [9] to the related-work discussion and update the descent context using [10]. Correct reference [7]: the linked paper is by Nicholas M. Katz and Pham Huu Tiep, without Rojas-León. Specify the base field in the fixed-space dimension formula in §8.1: in the unitary case the displayed specialised formula is $|\omega(h)|^2=q^{2\dim_{\mathbb F_{q^2}}\ker(h-1)}$.
- In the source definition, use “multiplicity space” rather than simply “isotypic part” for the rank-$2m$ Hodge structure, consistently with [1, §2].
