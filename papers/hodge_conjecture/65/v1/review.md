# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantial partial result toward the rational Hodge conjecture: ordinary Hodge classes on arbitrary finite products within each of four specified Dickson K3 families are algebraic, including special parameters and repetitions. The fixed determinant correspondence and the treatment of mixed special fibers provide meaningful advances beyond the construction of the underlying families. The conclusions are appropriately restricted to one fixed pair $(k,n)$ at a time; they do not resolve the general problem or establish generalized Hodge support or full Kuga–Satake algebraicity.

The central geometric argument is convincing. The function-field quotients give the stated surface models, and the rotated reciprocal projection is nonzero on the holomorphic two-form. Simplicity of the K3 transcendental Hodge structure then gives injectivity, while the polarization-adjoint argument provides an algebraic retraction. The separate analysis of fiber collisions addresses the allowed special parameters. The generic and diagonal rank arguments combine visible divisors, algebraic real multiplication, and actual moduli dimensions, rather than treating a Picard-rank lower bound as an equality.

Theorem B supplies the essential additional geometry. The parameter-independent quotient, the reciprocal-minus fixed carrier, and the explicitly nonzero exterior projection identify the entire rank-one $F$-determinant at every allowed parameter. This is stronger than identifying its Hodge numbers or showing that it is abstractly CM. The full determinant torus follows from the invertible matrix $J-I$, and the algebraic inverse permits subsequent tensor contractions.

The fixed-carrier argument also supports the claimed scope. Aoki’s Theorem 1.2(ii) [7] covers degrees $20,28,42$. For degree $66$, the positive identity (5.4) uses an applicable $11$-standard cycle, an algebraic surface character, and linear classes. Aoki’s standard-cycle and deletion theorems [8] apply with the stated parameters. The concatenating Shioda–Katsura correspondence [9], with its seven Tate twists, transfers this class to the eight curve factors. The second-kind differential calculation and the labelled projection argument address nonvanishing in the required norm sector. Thus the argument establishes algebraicity, rather than stopping at the numerical Hodge condition.

Section 6 adequately handles the main difficulty in the unrestricted product theorem. Distinct right-$N$ character spaces provide joint independence even for dependent quadratic characters and conjugate fields; pairwise independence alone would not suffice. The connected-kernel and integral-character arguments justify passage from rational character spaces to the actual groups and determinant columns. For non-CM factors, the unique noncompact place and the common determinant eliminate unwanted graph identifications after isogenies are consolidated. The resulting invariant generators can indeed be transported through the constructed correspondences and reduced to classes on powers of $P$.

The novelty assessment is favorable. I compared the relevant arguments with rStation paper 61/v3 [2], paper 40/v4 [5], and the related Dickson constructions in papers 27/v1 and 28/v3. The earlier common-norm method and general determinant-supply results are correctly credited. External comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [1] confirms that the K3 families and real multiplication are prior work. The abelian-cover preprint [4] supplies general CM determinants and a Hodge-generic theorem, not this unrestricted mixed-product conclusion. The explicit small fixed carrier, its degree-$66$ norm cycle, and the extension to the higher real-degree families constitute a substantive contribution rather than a cosmetic variation of [2].

I inspected and reran all four supplied checkers; their regenerated JSON results matched the retained outputs. These reproduce the exact identities and finite controls claimed, not the general geometric or Hodge-group theorems, which require the written arguments above. The abstract is informative, the numbered attribution is clear, and the evidence is reproducible.

One useful minor improvement would be to display the character-level cohomological map induced by the exceptional projective-line bundle in Section 5.3, including its inverse on the selected summand. This would make the distinction between concatenation and cancellation easier to audit. It is an exposition improvement, not a condition for acceptance. Numbered references in this report follow the manuscript’s bibliography.
