# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance relevant to the Hodge conjecture: a complete classification of the exceptional Klein denominators and their residue sets in dimensions $2,4,6,8$. The decisive contribution is the passage from bounded tables to a provably exhaustive finite enumeration. It does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture, and its geometric consequences retain the explicitly stated external assumptions.

The central argument is sound. The Fourier calculation in Theorem A correctly reduces the constant-age condition to the vanishing of odd-character coefficients. Theorem B then counts the surviving obstructions by inclusion–exclusion, with the quotient by the local factors giving the stated subgroup indices. In Theorem C, choosing an odd character with maximal support ensures that every omitted prime belongs to $A$. Twisting simultaneously at a subset of $B$, while excluding cancellations of local components, gives the product inequality. This supplies a necessary hereditary condition for pruning, rather than an empirical cutoff. The candidate prime-power bound and exhaustive local-root construction therefore justify the search in §5. The binary-rotation proof of Theorem E and the local valuation proof of Proposition F are also convincing.

The implementation matches this proof strategy. I reproduced all 43 checks in the supplied Python verifier, including the four complete enumerations, in approximately 92 seconds. They returned exactly the reported residue sets and node counts. These checks also compare direct character tests, Dirichlet criteria, and the two implementations of the counting formula. Separately, I checked the products and primality of the factors in all 72 candidate-generating factorizations using the deterministic seven-base Miller–Rabin test valid below $2^{64}$; the 77 distinct prime factors passed. This is reproduction of the supplied enumeration with additional checks, not a separately implemented complete classifier. I did not rerun the large C searches or the geometric length-reduction computations borrowed from [1].

The novelty is substantive and appropriately concentrated. Direct comparison with Pastrana–Villaflor [1], especially Theorem 3.3, Theorem 4.1 and Conjecture 1, confirms that the Dirichlet criterion, single-prime bound and original tables are prior work, whereas completeness in dimensions $4,6,8$ was conjectural there. The product inequality and exact enumeration supply the missing completeness argument. The exponent inconsistency identified in [1] is real. The related rStation classification of residual Fermat fourfold characters [3] and the cycle constructions at degrees $44,51,52$ [4] address different character problems and do not subsume this classification. The auxiliary counting formula, infinite family and gcd identity add useful structure; acceptance does not rest on the volume of new tables.

The presentation is clear enough for acceptance independently of these correctness findings. The question, new ingredients, proof strategy and computational role are identifiable, and the manuscript separates arithmetic theorems from geometric inputs. The following clarifications would improve precision without changing the main result:

- **Geometric dependencies.** Preserve the conditional wording of Corollary G. Remark 8.2 should not suggest that invoking MMRV removes every dependence on Kang: [2, Proposition 4.3] explicitly uses Kang's fourfold result, and [3,4] discuss the associated issue. In particular, distinguish “no direct invocation of Kang” from an independent proof of all the imported algebraicity statements.
- **Degree range.** State $m\ge3$ for the smooth Klein-hypersurface covering discussion, or treat the lower degrees separately. At $m=2$, $d(m)=0$, so the asserted automorphism order and Fermat covering do not apply; some of these quadrics are also singular. This does not affect the arithmetic residue classification.
- **Certification and complexity.** Replace the imprecise description of SymPy's primality path as “BPSW deterministic” with the exact routine, version and certified range, or an explicit deterministic test below $2^{64}$. Likewise, $2^{\omega(e)}$ counts subgroup computations; their discrete-logarithm costs still depend on the moduli.
- **Presentation details.** Use standard numbered citations throughout. Move the long higher-dimensional residue lists in §6 to an appendix or the existing data supplement, keeping a compact summary in the main text. In Remark 7.1, make clear that the set with 15 elements is $E^2\cup E^4$, not $E^{10}$, whose bounded list contains 38 elements.
