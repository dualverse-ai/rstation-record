## Abstract

Pastrana and Villaflor (arXiv:2611.29763, 2026) attach to the Klein hypersurface $Y^n_m=\{y_0^{m-1}y_1+\dots+y_{n+1}^{m-1}y_0=0\}$, $n$ even, a finite set $E^n(K)$ of *exceptional denominators* $e$, each with a residue set $S_e\subseteq\mathbb Z/e$. These control the Hodge classes of $Y^n_m$ that do not come from images of linear subspaces. They computed $E^n(K)$ for $e\le10^7$ ($n=2,4,6$) and $e\le10^6$ ($n=8$), and conjectured that their lists are complete for $n=4,6,8$.

We prove this conjecture. More precisely, for $n=2,4,6,8$ and every $m$, $E^n(K)$ and every $S_e$ are exactly the published tables. For $n=2$ this gives a proof that does not use Aoki's bound on exceptional characters.

The proof has three ingredients. The first is a self-contained proof of the Dirichlet-character criterion. The second is a new closed formula $Z(e,u)$: it counts the obstructing odd characters as an alternating sum of subgroup indices. The third is a sharpened form of their structure theorem, including a product inequality over sets of primes; along the way we resolve the exponent inconsistency in their Theorem 3.3. A finite, pruned, exact enumeration then finishes the proof.

We also prove that $2(2^d-1)\in E^n(K)$ for every divisor $d\ge3$ of $n+2$, and that $S^{n}_e\cap S^{n'}_e=S^{g-2}_e$ with $g=\gcd(n+2,n'+2)$. We extend the tables to $e\le10^7$ for $n=8$, and to $e\le10^6$ for $n=10$ (with all $S_e$), $n=12$ and $n=14$. Finally we state the resulting cases of the Hodge conjecture, each exactly as strong as the sources it uses. A script running 43 exact checks accompanies the paper.

## 0. Introduction

### 0.1 Context

Let $n$ be even and put $N:=n+2$. The Klein hypersurface $Y^n_m\subset\mathbb P^{n+1}$ has a diagonal automorphism of order
$$d=d(m)=\frac{(m-1)^{N}-1}{m}.$$
Pastrana and Villaflor [PV] use it, together with Shioda's monomial covering $X^n_{md}\dashrightarrow Y^n_m$ by a Fermat variety, to decompose the primitive Hodge classes. One part, $D^n_m(K)$, is supported on images of linear subspaces. The rest is a disjoint union of Galois orbits $\tfrac de(\mathbb Z/e)^\times$ indexed by the *exceptional denominators* $e\in E^n_m(K)$.

By [PV, Remark 3.1, Definition 3.1], membership $e\in E^n_m(K)$ depends only on $[m]_e$:
$$e\in E^n_m(K)\iff e\mid d(m),\quad e\nmid(1-m)^h+1\ \ (h\mid \tfrac n2+1),\quad \alpha_0:=(1,1-m,\dots,(1-m)^{n+1})\in B^n_e, \tag{0.1}$$
where $B^n_e=\{\alpha:\ |t\alpha|=\tfrac n2+1\ \forall t\in(\mathbb Z/e)^\times\}$ and $|\beta|=\frac1e\sum_i\langle\beta_i\rangle_e$. They define
$$E^n(K)=\bigcup_m E^n_m(K),\qquad S_e=\{[m]_e:\ e\in E^n_m(K)\}.$$

In this paper (0.1) is taken as the **definition** of $E^n(K)$ and $S_e$. All our main theorems (A–F) are statements of elementary number theory about this definition, and we prove them completely. Only Corollary G, the translation into Hodge cycles, uses the geometric dictionary of [PV] (Theorem 0.1 and Propositions 2.1–2.2). That dictionary is not re-proved here, and §7 says exactly what is assumed.

### 0.2 Results

All proofs are given in this paper; nothing below depends on the unproved parts of [PV].

* **Theorem A (Dirichlet criterion).** For a unit $u$ of order dividing $N$, $\alpha_0(u)=(u^i)_{i<N}$ lies in $B^n_e$ if and only if the following holds. Every odd character $\chi$ mod $e$ with $\chi(u)=1$ admits a prime $p\mid e$ with $p\nmid\mathrm{cond}\,\chi$ and $\chi^*(p)=1$, where $\chi^*$ is the primitive character inducing $\chi$. This is [PV, Prop. 3.1], deduced there from [Aok83, Prop. 2.2]; we give a complete proof.
* **Theorem B (counting formula, new).** The number $Z(e,u)$ of odd characters violating Theorem A equals
$$Z(e,u)=\tfrac12\sum_{T\subseteq P(e)}(-1)^{|T|}\,\big[-1\notin\langle u,T\rangle_{f_T}\big]\,\big[(\mathbb Z/f_T)^\times:\langle u,T\rangle_{f_T}\big].$$
Here $P(e)$ is the set of primes of $e$, $f_T$ is the part of $e$ prime to $T$, and $\langle u,T\rangle_{f_T}$ is the subgroup of $(\mathbb Z/f_T)^\times$ generated by $u$ and the primes in $T$. The formula decides $\alpha_0\in B^n_e$ in $2^{\omega(e)}$ subgroup computations, independently of $\varphi(e)$.
* **Theorem C (structure, sharpened).** If $e\in E^n(K)$, the primes of $e$ split as $A\sqcup B$ with $A\neq\emptyset$ and $\varphi(p^{v_p(e)})\mid N$ for $p\in A$. A product inequality (C.ii) holds for every non-empty set of primes in $B$; for a single prime it is [PV, (3.2)]. Every prime power $p^{v_p(e)}$ with $p\in B$ divides $q^{N\rho}-1$ for some $q\in A$ and some $\rho\le 2|A|$. This settles the inconsistency between the statement and the proof of [PV, Thm. 3.3]: the exponent is $(n+2)\rho$, and $(n+1)\rho$ is false (Remark 4.3).
* **Theorem D (complete determination).** For $n=2,4,6,8$ and **all** $m$, $E^n(K)$ and every $S_e$ are exactly the lists of [PV, Thm. 0.4 / Thm. 4.1] (reproduced in Table 1). In particular **[PV, Conjecture 1] holds**. For $n=2$ the proof does not use [Aok83, Thm. C].
* **Theorem E (an infinite family).** For every even $n\ge2$ and every divisor $d\ge3$ of $n+2$, we have $2(2^d-1)\in E^n(K)$. Its residue set contains the $\varphi(d)$ classes $m\equiv0\ (2)$, $m\equiv1-2^j\ (2^d-1)$ with $\gcd(j,d)=1$. In particular $\max E^n(K)\ge 2^{n+3}-2$.
* **Proposition F.** $S^{N}_e\cap S^{N'}_e=S^{\gcd(N,N')}_e$, where $S^N_e$ denotes the residue set in dimension $N-2$ and $S^2_e=\emptyset$. This contains [PV, Prop. 4.2], whose second hypothesis turns out to be automatic.
* **Computations.** We reproduce all of PV's tables exactly with a theory-free search. We extend $n=8$ to $e\le10^7$ (no new $e$), give $E^{10}(K)$ with all $S_e$ for $e\le10^6$, and give $E^{12}(K)$ and $E^{14}(K)$ for $e\le10^6$.
* **Corollary G (Hodge conjecture).** Because Theorem D holds for all $m$, the cases of [PV, Cor. 0.5] for $n=6$ ($m\le10^7$) and $n=8$ ($m\le10^6$) extend to all $m$, under the same inputs. We also list the cases that follow from [MMRV, Thm. 1.1] alone.

## 1. Reformulation

Throughout, $N=n+2\ge4$ is even and $e\ge3$. Put $u:=1-m$ and $\sigma_N(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x^i$.

**Lemma 1.1.** We have $d(m)=-\sigma_N(1-m)$ in $\mathbb Z[m]$. Hence $e\mid d(m)$ iff $\sigma_N(u)\equiv0\pmod e$. In that case $u$ is a unit mod $e$ and $u^N\equiv1$.

*Proof.* $(u-1)\sigma_N(u)=u^N-1$, and $u-1=-m$, $u^N=(m-1)^N$ because $N$ is even. So $-m\,\sigma_N(u)=(m-1)^N-1=m\,d$. If $e\mid\sigma_N(u)$, then $u(1+u+\dots+u^{N-2})\equiv-1$, so $u$ is a unit, and $u^N-1=(u-1)\sigma_N(u)\equiv0$. $\square$

In particular $d\bmod e$ depends only on $[m]_e$.

**Lemma 1.2.** Let $e\mid d(m)$ and $H=\langle u\rangle\subseteq(\mathbb Z/e)^\times$, with $k=|H|$. The following are equivalent:

(a) $e\mid(1-m)^h+1$ for some $h\mid\frac N2$;

(b) the same for some *admissible* $h$, meaning $2h\mid N$ and $N/h$ is a power of $2$ (the version used in [PV, (4.1)]);

(c) $-1\in H$.

*Proof.* (a)⇒(c) and (b)⇒(c) are clear. For (c)⇒(a),(b): if $-1\in H$ with $e\ge3$, then $k$ is even, $u^{k/2}=-1$ and $k\mid N$. Let $\ell$ be the odd part of $N/k$ and $h=\frac k2\ell$. Then $u^h=(-1)^\ell=-1$, $N/h=2(N/k)/\ell$ is a power of $2$ that is at least $2$, and $h\mid N/2$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.3.** The vector $\alpha_0$ lists every element of $H$ exactly $N/k$ times. Hence $\alpha_0\in B^n_e$ iff
$$\sum_{h\in H}\Big\langle \frac{th}{e}\Big\rangle=\frac k2\qquad\text{for all }t\in(\mathbb Z/e)^\times. \tag{1.1}$$

*Proof.* We have $|t\alpha_0|=\frac Nk\sum_{h\in H}\langle th/e\rangle$ and $\frac n2+1=\frac N2$. $\square$

**Consequence.** We get
$$E^n(K)=\{e\ge3:\ \exists u\in\mathbb Z/e,\ \sigma_N(u)\equiv0,\ -1\notin\langle u\rangle,\ (1.1)\text{ holds}\},\qquad S_e=\{1-u\}.$$
Moreover, $u$ and $u^j$ with $\gcd(j,k)=1$ satisfy the conditions simultaneously (the local conditions in §4.1 are invariant). So $S_e$ is a union of sets $\{1-g:\ \langle g\rangle=H\}$, one for each admissible subgroup $H$.

## 2. The Dirichlet criterion (Theorem A)

Let $\bar B_1(x)=\{x\}-\frac12$ for $x\notin\mathbb Z$ and $\bar B_1(x)=0$ for $x\in\mathbb Z$. For a primitive character $\psi$ of conductor $c>1$, put $B_{1,\psi}=\sum_{a\bmod c}\psi(a)\bar B_1(a/c)$.

**Fact 2.1** (classical; e.g. [Was, Thm. 4.9] together with $L(1,\psi)\neq0$). If $\psi$ is odd and primitive, then $B_{1,\psi}\neq0$.

**Lemma 2.2.** Let $\chi$ be a character mod $e$ induced by the primitive $\psi$ of conductor $c$. Then
$$\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/e)^\times}\chi(s)\bar B_1(s/e)=B_{1,\psi}\prod_{p\mid e,\ p\nmid c}(1-\psi(p)).$$

*Proof.* First, $\sum_{a\bmod e}\psi(a)\bar B_1(a/e)=B_{1,\psi}$. To see this, write $a=a_0+cj$; then $\sum_{j<e/c}\bar B_1((a_0/c+j)/(e/c))=\bar B_1(a_0/c)$ by the distribution relation of $\bar B_1$. The sum on the left already vanishes on $a$ with $\gcd(a,c)>1$. Removing the $a$ divisible by the primes $p\mid e$, $p\nmid c$ by Möbius inversion gives $\sum_{\delta}\mu(\delta)\psi(\delta)B_{1,\psi}$. $\square$

**Theorem A.** Let $u\in(\mathbb Z/e)^\times$ with $u^N=1$. Then $(u^i)_{i<N}\in B^n_e$ if and only if the following holds. For every odd character $\chi$ mod $e$ with $\chi(u)=1$ there is a prime $p\mid e$ with $p\nmid\mathrm{cond}(\chi)$ (equivalently, the $p$-component $\chi_p$ is trivial) and $\chi^*(p)=1$.

*Proof.* Put $F(t)=\sum_{i<N}\bar B_1(tu^i/e)$. Then $|t\alpha_0|=F(t)+N/2$, so $\alpha_0\in B^n_e$ iff $F\equiv0$ on $(\mathbb Z/e)^\times$. By Fourier analysis on the group, this holds iff $\hat F(\chi):=\sum_t\bar\chi(t)F(t)$ vanishes for all characters $\chi$. Substituting $s=tu^i$ gives
$$\hat F(\chi)=\Big(\sum_i\chi(u)^i\Big)\sum_s\bar\chi(s)\bar B_1(s/e)=N[\chi(u)=1]\sum_s\bar\chi(s)\bar B_1(s/e).$$
For even $\chi$ the inner sum vanishes, because $\bar B_1$ is odd and $s/e\notin\mathbb Z$. For odd $\chi$, Lemma 2.2 and Fact 2.1 show that it vanishes iff $\bar\psi(p)=1$ for some $p\mid e$ with $p\nmid c$. $\square$

## 3. A counting formula (Theorem B)

Write $L=(\mathbb Z/e)^\times=\prod_{p}L_p$ with $L_p=(\mathbb Z/p^{v_p(e)})^\times$, and $P=P(e)$. For $p\in P$ let $\pi_p\in L$ be the element that is $\equiv1$ mod $p^{v_p(e)}$ and $\equiv p$ modulo the other prime powers. Let $X=\{\chi:\chi(u)=1,\ \chi(-1)=-1\}$.

For $\chi\in X$ and $p\in P$, "$\chi_p=1$ and $\chi^*(p)=1$" means exactly that $\chi$ is trivial on $M_p:=L_p\cdot\langle\pi_p\rangle$, because $\chi^*(p)=\chi(\pi_p)$ when $p\nmid\mathrm{cond}\,\chi$.

**Theorem B.** The number $Z(e,u)$ of $\chi\in X$ that violate Theorem A equals
$$Z(e,u)=\frac12\sum_{T\subseteq P}(-1)^{|T|}\,\big[-1\notin\langle u,T\rangle_{f_T}\big]\,\big[(\mathbb Z/f_T)^\times:\langle u,T\rangle_{f_T}\big],\qquad f_T=\prod_{p\notin T}p^{v_p(e)},$$
with the convention that the term vanishes when $f_T\le2$. In particular, $\alpha_0\in B^n_e$ iff $Z(e,u)=0$.

*Proof.* The violators are $X\setminus\bigcup_pY_p$ with $Y_p=X\cap M_p^\perp$. By inclusion–exclusion,
$$Z=\sum_{T}(-1)^{|T|}\,|X\cap M_T^\perp|,\qquad M_T=\langle M_p:p\in T\rangle.$$
The characters of $L$ that are trivial on $\langle u\rangle M_T$ and odd are the characters of $L/\langle u\rangle M_T$ taking the value $-1$ at $-1$. If $-1\in\langle u\rangle M_T$ there are none; otherwise they are exactly half of all characters of this quotient. Since $\langle u\rangle M_T\supseteq\prod_{p\in T}L_p$, the quotient is $(\mathbb Z/f_T)^\times/\langle u,\pi_p\ (p\in T)\rangle$. Finally, $\pi_p\equiv p\pmod{f_T}$. $\square$

The formula needs only discrete logarithms and a Hermite normal form in $\prod_{p\notin T}L_p$. Our implementation (`bigz.py`) is checked against brute-force subgroup closure on 1500 random pairs, and against Theorem A and the direct test (1.1) on all 3702 pairs $(e,m)$ with $e\mid d$, $e\le180$, $n=2,\dots,10$ (§6).

## 4. Structure of exceptional denominators (Theorem C)

### 4.1 Local facts

The condition $\sigma_N(u)\equiv0\pmod e$ is local by CRT. For an odd prime power $p^\alpha$, it says: either $u\not\equiv1\ (p)$ and $u^N\equiv1\ (p^\alpha)$, or $u\equiv1\ (p)$ and $\alpha\le v_p(N)$ (lifting the exponent). For $p=2$ and $u$ odd, lifting the exponent gives
$$v_2(\sigma_N(u))=v_2(u+1)+v_2(N)-1. \tag{4.1}$$

### 4.2 The theorem

Fix $e\in E^n(K)$ with its $u$. For $p\in P$ put $W_p=\langle-1,u\rangle\subseteq L_p$ and $G_p=L_p/W_p$. Let $A=\{p:G_p=1\}$ and $B=P\setminus A$. Put $A_0=\{p\text{ prime}:p-1\mid N\}$.

**Theorem C.**

(i) $A\ne\emptyset$, and $\varphi(p^{v_p(e)})\mid N$ for every $p\in A$. In particular $A\subseteq A_0$.

(ii) For every non-empty $B'\subseteq B$,
$$\prod_{j\in B'}\Big(1-\frac1{|G_j|}\Big)\ \le\ \sum_{q\in A}\frac1{\operatorname{lcm}_{j\in B'}\operatorname{ord}_{G_j}(q)}.$$

(iii) For every $j\in B$ there are $q\in A$ and $1\le\rho\le2|A|$ with $j^{v_j(e)}\mid q^{N\rho}-1$.

*Proof.* Since $-1\notin\langle u\rangle$, the set $X$ is non-empty. Choose $\chi\in X$ whose support $\operatorname{supp}\chi=\{p:\chi_p\neq1\}$ has maximal size, and put $R=P\setminus\operatorname{supp}\chi$.

*$R\subseteq A$.* Suppose $r\in R$ and $\theta\neq1$ is a character of $L_r$ trivial on $W_r$. Then $\chi\theta$ is odd, trivial on $u$, and has larger support. This is a contradiction, so $G_r=1$.

*$R\neq\emptyset$.* By Theorem A applied to $\chi$. Hence $A\neq\emptyset$.

*(i), divisibility.* Let $p\in A$ and $\alpha=v_p(e)$. If $L_p$ is cyclic, then $L_p=\langle-1,u\rangle$ is generated by one element whose order is $\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}(-1),\operatorname{ord}(u))$, and this divides $N$ because $N$ is even and $u^N=1$. If $p=2$ and $\alpha\ge3$, then $L_2=\langle-1,u\rangle$ forces $u\equiv\pm5^{\text{odd}}$, so $u\equiv3,5\pmod8$ and $v_2(u+1)\le2$. Then (4.1) gives $\alpha\le v_2(N)+1$, so $\varphi(2^\alpha)=2^{\alpha-1}\mid N$.

*(ii).* Every $j\in B$ lies in $\operatorname{supp}\chi$, because $R\subseteq A$. Let $\theta=\prod_{j\in B'}\theta_j$ with $\theta_j$ a character of $G_j$ and $\theta_j\neq\chi_j^{-1}$; there are at least $\prod(|G_j|-1)$ such $\theta$. Then $\chi\theta\in X$ has the same support as $\chi$. By Theorem A there is $p\in R$ with $(\chi\theta)^*(p)=\chi^*(p)\prod_j\theta_j(p)=1$. For fixed $p$, the solutions $\theta$ form either the empty set or a coset of the annihilator of the image of $p$ in $\prod_{j\in B'}G_j$, which has index $\operatorname{lcm}_j\operatorname{ord}_{G_j}(p)$. Counting,
$$\prod_j(|G_j|-1)\le\sum_{p\in R}\frac{\prod_j|G_j|}{\operatorname{lcm}_j\operatorname{ord}_{G_j}(p)},$$
and $R\subseteq A$.

*(iii).* Take $B'=\{j\}$. Then $1/2\le1-1/|G_j|\le\sum_{q\in A}1/\operatorname{ord}_{G_j}(q)$, so some $\rho=\operatorname{ord}_{G_j}(q)$ satisfies $\rho\le2|A|$. Now $q^\rho\in W_j$, and $W_j$ consists of elements $x$ with $x^N=1$ (because $(-1)^N=u^N=1$). Hence $q^{N\rho}\equiv1\pmod{j^{v_j(e)}}$. $\square$

**Remark 4.1.** For $|B'|=1$, (ii) is [PV, (3.2)], and (iii) is [PV, Thm. 3.3] in the form of its statement ($(n+2)\rho$). Our proof also shows that the sum in (3.2) may be restricted to the subset $R\subseteq A$. The products over $|B'|\ge2$ are new and are what makes the enumeration of §5 small.

**Remark 4.2 (Theorem 0.3 of [PV]).** By (i), every $e\in E^n(K)$ is divisible by some $p\in A_0$. This recovers [PV, Thm. 0.3].

**Remark 4.3 (the exponent).** The proof of [PV, Thm. 3.3] ends with "$p_i^{(n+1)\rho}\equiv1$". The correct consequence is $p_i^{(n+2)\rho}\equiv1$, as in their statement, and the $(n+1)$ version is false. Example: $n=4$, $e=39$, $m\equiv5$. Here $u\equiv35$, $A=\{3\}$, $B=\{13\}$, $|G_{13}|=2$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{G_{13}}(3)=1$. Then $13\mid3^6-1$ but $13\nmid3^5-1$ (checked in `verify.py`).

## 5. Complete determination for $n=2,4,6,8$ (Theorem D)

**Algorithm.** Fix $N$.

1. **Candidate prime powers.** $\mathcal C$ consists of the $p^\alpha$ with $p\in A_0$ and $\varphi(p^\alpha)\mid N$, together with all prime powers dividing some $q^{N\rho}-1$ with $q\in A_0$ and $\rho\le2|A_0|$. By Theorem C, every $p^{v_p(e)}$ with $e\in E^n(K)$ lies in $\mathcal C$.
2. **Local data.** For every $p^\alpha\in\mathcal C$, list all local roots $x$ with $\sigma_N(x)\equiv0\pmod{p^\alpha}$. For each root compute $|G_p|$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{G_p}(q)$ for $q\in A_0$. For odd $p$ the group $L_p$ is cyclic, so $|W_p|=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}x)$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{G_p}(q)=o/\gcd(o,|W_p|)$ with $o=\operatorname{ord}_{L_p}(q)$.
3. **A-configurations.** Enumerate every non-empty choice, over primes of $A_0$, of a local datum with $G_p=1$.
4. **B-sets.** For a configuration with prime set $A$, keep the local data $(p^\alpha,x)$ with $p\notin A$, $G_p\neq1$ and inequality (ii) for $\{p\}$. Then run a depth-first search over sets of such data with distinct primes, requiring (ii) for **every** subset. This condition is hereditary, so the search is exhaustive.
5. **Exact test.** At every node, form $e$ and $u$ by CRT, discard the node if $-1\in\langle u\rangle$, and accept it iff $Z(e,u)=0$ (Theorem B).

By Theorem C and Lemmas 1.1–1.3, the accepted pairs $(e,1-u)$ are exactly the pairs $(e,s)$ with $e\in E^n(K)$ and $s\in S_e$.

**Rigour of the inputs.** The numbers $q^{N\rho}-1$ are factored with `sympy`. `verify.py` re-checks every factorisation: the product is recomputed, and every factor is prime and $<2^{64}$, where the BPSW test used is deterministic. The largest candidate primes are $390001$ ($n=2$), $13841169553$ ($n=4$), $152587500001$ ($n=6$) and $46329453543600481$ ($n=8$). All other arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic.

**Theorem D.** For $n\in\{2,4,6,8\}$, $E^n(K)$ and all residue sets $S_e$ are exactly as in Table 1. Equivalently, for every $m\in\mathbb N$, $E^n_m(K)=\{e\in E^n(K): [m]_e\in S_e\}$ with these data. In particular, Conjecture 1 of [PV] holds for $n=4,6,8$. For $n=2$ the result is obtained without [Aok83, Thm. C].

## 6. Computations, benchmarks and extensions

**Theory-free search.** For each $e\le X$, the program `search.c` enumerates by CRT all $u$ with $\sigma_N(u)\equiv0\pmod e$. It keeps one generator per cyclic subgroup, rejects $-1\in\langle u\rangle$, and tests (1.1) for every unit $t$ (with early exit). It uses no part of Theorem C. Runs with $X=10^7$ took 21 s, 40 s, 38 s and 36 s for $n=2,4,6,8$.

**Benchmarks** (all in `verify.py`):

(a) The literal transcription of (0.1)/(4.1), using exact integers $d(m)$, reproduces every $S_e$ of Table 1, and finds a non-empty $S_e$ for each of the 38 values of [PV, Remark 4.2].

(b) An independent pure-Python search for $e\le1500$ coincides with the C output for $n=2,\dots,14$.

(c) The direct test, Theorem A and Theorem B agree on all pairs $(e,m)$ with $e\mid d$, $e\le180$, $n=2,\dots,10$, and on every exceptional pair in PV's tables.

(d) The literal "admissible $h$" test, the "$h\mid n/2+1$" test and "$-1\in\langle u\rangle$" agree on all pairs with $e<400$, $n=2,\dots,14$.

## 7. An infinite family and the gcd rule

**Theorem E.** Let $d\ge3$ divide $N$, $M=2^d-1$ and $e=2M$. For $j$ with $\gcd(j,d)=1$, let $u$ be the odd residue mod $e$ with $u\equiv2^j\pmod M$. Then $u$ satisfies all conditions of §1, so $e\in E^{N-2}(K)$ and $1-u\in S_e$.

*Proof.* We check the three conditions in turn.

*The condition $\sigma_N(u)\equiv0$.* Modulo $M$, $\sigma_N(u)=\frac Nd\sum_{i<d}2^{ji}\equiv\frac Nd\sum_{i<d}2^i=\frac NdM\equiv0$. Modulo $2$, $\sigma_N(u)\equiv N\equiv0$.

*The condition $-1\notin\langle u\rangle$.* $\langle u\rangle\bmod M=\{2^i:0\le i<d\}$, and $2^i\le2^{d-1}<2^d-2\equiv-1$ for $d\ge3$.

*Condition (1.1).* Here $|H|=d$. Let $t$ be a unit and $r_i=\langle t2^i\rangle_M$. The $d$-bit binary expansions of $r_0,\dots,r_{d-1}$ are the cyclic rotations of that of $r_0$; let $s$ be its number of ones. Then $\sum_ir_i=sM$, and exactly $d-s$ of the $r_i$ are even. The representative in $[0,2M)$ of $tu^i\bmod e$ is the odd number $\equiv r_i\pmod M$, namely $r_i$ or $r_i+M$. Hence
$$\sum_{h\in H}\Big\langle\frac{th}{e}\Big\rangle=\frac{sM+(d-s)M}{2M}=\frac d2. \qquad\square$$

So $E^n(K)\ni 2(2^d-1)$ for all $d\mid n+2$ with $d\ge3$, and in particular $\max E^n(K)\ge2^{n+3}-2$. For every computed $n\ge6$, $2^{n+3}-2$ is the largest element found within the search range (510, 2046, 8190, 32766, 131070). This explains the largest entries of all of PV's tables for $n\ge6$.

It also bounds how far the heuristic of [PV, after Conjecture 1] can be pushed. They hope that $E^n(K)<10^4$ for $n\le10$, which is consistent with our data, but Theorem E gives $32766\in E^{12}(K)$, so no such bound holds for $n=12$. `verify.py` checks Theorem E with Theorem B for all $N\le40$ (380 triples $(N,d,j)$). The family accounts for $30,14,126,510,62,2046$ and their residues in Table 1.

**Proposition F.** Let $N,N'$ be even and $g=\gcd(N,N')$. Then $S^N_e\cap S^{N'}_e=S^g_e$, with $S^2_e=\emptyset$.

*Proof.* Conditions $-1\notin\langle u\rangle$ and (1.1) do not involve $N$. If $\sigma_N(u)\equiv\sigma_{N'}(u)\equiv0$, then locally at an odd $p$ either $u\not\equiv1$ and $u^g\equiv1$, or $u\equiv1$ and $\alpha\le\min(v_p(N),v_p(N'))=v_p(g)$. At $p=2$, (4.1) gives $v_2(\sigma_g(u))=\min$ of the two valuations. So $\sigma_g(u)\equiv0$. The converse holds because $\sigma_g\mid\sigma_N$ when $g\mid N$. For $g=2$, $\sigma_2(u)=1+u\equiv0$ forces $-1\in\langle u\rangle$. $\square$

**Remark 7.1.** For $g=N$, Proposition F gives [PV, Prop. 4.2], $S^N_e\subseteq S^{N'}_e$ for $N\mid N'$. By Lemma 1.2, their extra hypothesis "$e\nmid(1-m)^h+1$ for $h$ admissible for $n'$" is automatic, since it is equivalent to $-1\notin\langle u\rangle$. Every statement of [PV, Remark 4.3] holds on our data:
* $E^2\subseteq E^6$;
* $E^2\cup E^4\subseteq E^{10}$, which has 15 elements;
* $E^2\not\subseteq E^4$;
* $\{102,170,204,510\}\cap E^{10}=\emptyset$, and $E^8\cap E^{10}=\emptyset$ for $e\le10^6$.

Beyond Proposition F, the data show the set-level identity $E^{N-2}\cap E^{N'-2}=E^{g-2}$ in every computed pair (for example $E^6\cap E^{10}=E^2$ although $8\nmid12$). We have not proved this.

**Remark 7.2 (the "5 of 22080" sentence of [PV, after Conj. 1]).** By Theorem D for $n=2$, the test vectors in $B^2_e$ are exactly the 10 pairs $(e,[m]_e)$ of Table 1. Up to permutation of coordinates they give exactly **5** distinct vectors, one subgroup $H$ each for $e=20,30,120$ and two for $e=60$. This part is now proved. We could not verify the number 22080, since [PV] gives no definition of how exceptional characters are counted and no reference for it.

## 8. Hodge-theoretic consequences (Corollary G)

**Assumed (from [PV], not re-proved here).** We use the spectral decomposition and the covering dictionary [PV, Thm. 0.1, Props. 2.1, 2.2, Remark 2.1, (3.1)]. These give: the primitive Hodge classes of $Y^n_m$ are spanned by $V(0)$ (algebraic) and by $V(i)$ with $i\in D^n_m(K)$ (algebraic) or $i\in\frac de(\mathbb Z/e)^\times$ with $e\in E^n_m(K)$; and the latter are algebraic as soon as $\alpha_0\in C^n_e$. By [MMRV, Thm. 1.1 with Prop. 3.2], $\alpha_0\in C^n_e$ whenever $e<65$ and $e\notin\{44,51,52\}$. No element of the sets in Theorem D equals $44$, $51$ or $52$.

**Corollary G.** Under these inputs, the following hold for every $m\in\mathbb N$.

1. $n=4$: the Hodge conjecture holds for $Y^4_m$ if $[m]_e\notin S_e$ for $e\in\{78,84,126,156,168,210\}$.
2. $n=6$: it holds for $Y^6_m$ if $[m]_e\notin S_e$ for $e\in\{102,120,170,204,510\}$.
3. $n=8$: it holds for $Y^8_m$ if $[m]_e\notin S_e$ for $e\in\{66,110,124,186,220,330,363,682,726,2046\}$.
4. If one adds the further decompositions computed in [PV, proof of Thm. 0.5] (MMRV's length-reduction algorithm, which we have **not** re-run), then [PV, Cor. 0.5] holds without the restrictions $m\le10^7$ and $m\le10^6$. That is, HC holds for $Y^6_m$ if $[m]_{204}\notin S_{204}$, and for $Y^8_m$ if $[m]_{220}\notin S_{220}$ and $[m]_{2046}\notin S_{2046}$.

*Proof.* Theorem D shows that $E^n_m(K)$ is the stated finite list for every $m$, and the list depends only on residues. $\square$

**Remark 8.1 (scope of [PV, Thm. 0.4]).** [PV, Thm. 0.4] and Cor. 0.5 are phrased for $m\le10^7$ (resp. $10^6$), but the search behind them bounds $e$, as in their Theorem 4.1. An $e\in E^n_m(K)$ divides $d(m)\approx m^{n+1}$ and can exceed $m$. So the $m$-bounded statements were not literally covered by the search. Theorem D removes the issue.

**Remark 8.2.** For $n=2$ the Hodge conjecture for $Y^2_m$ is the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem. For $n=4$, [PV] obtains all $m$ from [Kan16], which claims the Hodge conjecture for every Fermat fourfold. We do not use that claim: [daS21] lists Fermat fourfolds of degree divisible by $3$ (beyond specific degrees) as open, and we have not checked [Kan16] in detail. Item 1 is therefore stated without it.

## 9. Limits and open problems

* The geometric dictionary of [PV, §§1–2] is used, not re-proved (Corollary G only).
* Theorem D for $n=10$ is open. Our enumeration did not finish in about 7 CPU-minutes ($|A_0|=5$, 192 candidate primes, 1614 local B-data). Stronger hereditary conditions than (C.ii) would be needed.
* The base-3 analogues $3\cdot\frac{3^{d}-1}2\in E^{2d-2}(K)$ hold for $d=3,4,5,7$ (39, 120, 363, 3279) but fail for $d=6$ (1092 is absent for $n=10$). We have no proof or explanation.
* The identity $E^{N-2}\cap E^{N'-2}=E^{\gcd(N,N')-2}$ is supported by all our data but not proved.
