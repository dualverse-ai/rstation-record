## Abstract

We establish three exact structural theorems and symbolic verification algorithms for Hodge-character lattices and complete-intersection cycles on composite-degree Fermat varieties $X_d^n \subset \mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ as partial contributions toward the arithmetic and variational study of Fermat Hodge classes. First, in the torsion-free reduced character lattice $F_d := (\bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a)/\widetilde{D}_d \simeq \mathbb{Z}^{m_d}$ ($m_d = \lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor$, $\widetilde{D}_d = D_d + \mathbb{Z} e_{d/2}$ for even $d$ and $D_d$ for odd $d$), Aoki's formal Parry–Shioda weight-kernel lattice $\mathcal{B}_d := \ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(W_d) \subset F_d$ is saturated of rank $r_d = m_d - \frac{1}{2}\varphi(d)$. For $18$ two-factor composite degrees $d \in \{12, 15, \dots, 95\}$ (and $d = 105 = 3 \times 5 \times 7$), the Smith Normal Form of the Aoki–Shioda standard generator matrix $M_{S_d}$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ has non-zero invariant factors $(1^{r_d - 1}, 2)$ (resp. $(1^{25}, 2, 2, 2)$ at $d = 105$), certifying $\rho_d(B_d) = \mathcal{B}_d$ and yielding an SNF-induced $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear functional $\pi_d : F_d \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ whose restriction to $\mathcal{B}_d$ is the canonical quotient map $\bar{\pi}_d : \mathcal{B}_d \to \mathcal{B}_d/\rho_d(S_d) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, independent of the choice of unimodular factor $U_d$. For the five degrees $d \in \{35, 44, 51, 52, 68\}$, $100\%$ of surface and fourfold Hodge characters $\mathfrak{B}_d^2 \cup \mathfrak{B}_d^4$ have $\bar{\pi}_d(\rho_d(\alpha)) = 0$ and lie integrally over $\mathbb{Z}$ in the small-prime sublattice $D_d + \mathrm{Std}_{p_{\min}}$ ($p_{\min} \in \{2, 3, 5\}$), and we prove an **Inductive Splitting Parity Lemma** in $F_{52}$ for $\xi_{52}^{\min} = (7, 11, 23, 27, 31, 35, 48)_{52}$. Second, we prove that $\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}}$ ($|\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}}| = p = r_{4p}$) and $\mathcal{B}_{p_1 p_2}^{\mathrm{std}}$ ($|\mathcal{B}_{p_1 p_2}^{\mathrm{std}}| = \frac{p_1+p_2-2}{2} = r_{p_1 p_2}$) are free $\mathbb{Z}$-bases of $\rho_d(S_d)$, and via a **Symmetric Two-Tower Integral Telescoping Syzygy** we prove the exact minimal $\ell_1$-norms over the odd-coefficient affine coset $\rho_d(\xi_d^{\min}) + \rho_d(S_d)$ for all odd primes $p \ge 3$ and $3 \le p_1 < p_2$:
$$\ell_{2, 4p}(\xi_{4p}) \;=\; 2\left\lfloor\frac{p+1}{4}\right\rfloor + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(2, p), \qquad \ell_{2, p_1 p_2}(\xi_{p_1 p_2}) \;=\; \frac{(p_1 - 1)(p_2 - 1)}{4} + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_2, p_1) + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_1, p_2),$$
where $N_{\mathrm{obs}}(q, p) = \frac{p-1}{2r_q}$ if $q^{r_q} \equiv -1 \pmod p$ ($r_q := \mathrm{ord}_{(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}}(q)$) and $0$ otherwise, together with a $2$-standard collapse law $d \to 2d$ ($d$ odd). Third, on even-degree Fermat fourfolds $X_{2m}^4 \subset \mathbb{P}^5$ ($d = 2m \ge 6$), we prove a **Kronecker Catalecticant Factorization Theorem** $C_s(A(x)B(y)) = \bigoplus_q C_q(A) \otimes C_{s-q}(B)$, construct explicit orthogonal character idempotents $\Pi_k^{(1)}, \Pi_{\mathrm{sign}}^{(1)} \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2m})[\langle \gamma_2 \rangle]$ and $\widetilde{\Pi}_0^{(1)} \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2m})[\langle \sigma_2 \rangle]$ of the cyclic subgroups $\langle \gamma_2 \rangle \simeq \langle \sigma_2 \rangle \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2m\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathrm{Aut}(X_{2m}^4)$ acting on valid complete-intersection surfaces, and establish the $6$-level automorphic and conjugate-pair Catalecticant rank-drop staircase $L_1(m) > \dots > L_6(m)$.

## 1. Introduction and Summary of Main Results

### 1.1 Background and Motivation

Let $X_d^n = V(x_0^d + x_1^d + \dots + x_{n+1}^d) \subset \mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ ($n = 2p$ even) be the smooth projective Fermat variety of degree $d$ and even dimension $n = 2p$. By the eigenspace decomposition of Shioda [1, 2], the primitive $(p, p)$-cohomology decomposes into $1$-dimensional character spaces indexed by the finite set $\mathfrak{B}_d^n$ of sorted $(n+2)$-tuples $\alpha = (a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{n+1}) \in \{1, \dots, d-1\}^{n+2}$ satisfying the Parry–Shioda condition
$$\sum_{i=0}^{n+1} \langle u \, a_i \rangle_d \;=\; \frac{n + 2}{2} \, d \qquad \forall \, u \in (\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^\times, \tag{1.1}$$
where $\langle x \rangle_d \in \{1, \dots, d-1\}$ denotes the least positive residue modulo $d$. Inside the free abelian group $\bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a$, let $B_d$ be the subgroup generated by all even-length Parry–Shioda characters $\bigcup_{p \ge 0} \mathfrak{B}_d^{2p}$, so every element of $B_d$ has even degree $\sum_{a=1}^{d-1} c_a \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and satisfies $\sum_{a=1}^{d-1} c_a (2\langle u a \rangle_d - d) = 0$ for all $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Let $D_d := \langle (a)_d + (d - a)_d : 1 \le a \le d - 1 \rangle \subset B_d$ be the sublattice of complementary pairs (corresponding to pair-decomposable divisor intersections $\mathrm{Sym}^p \mathrm{Hdg}^1(X_d^n, \mathbb{Q})$). For each prime divisor $p \mid d$ and each $1 \le a < d/p$ with $p a \not\equiv 0 \pmod d$, consider the formal Aoki–Shioda standard prime-divisor relation
$$s_{p, a}^{(d)} \;:=\; \sum_{j=0}^{p-1} \left(a + j \frac{d}{p}\right)_d \;-\; (p \, a)_d \;\in\; \bigoplus_{x=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_x, \tag{1.2}$$
and its even-length representative inside $B_d$:
$$\tilde{s}_{p, a}^{(d)} \;:=\; \begin{cases} \displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1} \left(a + j \frac{d}{p}\right)_d \;+\; (d - p a)_d \;\in\; \mathfrak{B}_d^{p-1} \subset B_d & \text{if } p \ge 3 \text{ is odd (length } p + 1 \text{ even)}, \\[10pt] (a)_d + (a + d/2)_d + (d - 2a)_d + (d/2)_d \;\in\; \mathfrak{B}_d^2 \subset B_d & \text{if } p = 2 \text{ and } d \text{ is even (length } 4 \text{ even)}. \end{cases} \tag{1.3}$$
Since $(d - pa)_d \equiv -(pa)_d \pmod{D_d}$ and $2(d/2)_d \in D_d$, we have $\tilde{s}_{p, a}^{(d)} \equiv s_{p, a}^{(d)} \pmod{\widetilde{D}_d}$, where
$$\widetilde{D}_d \;:=\; \begin{cases} D_d + \mathbb{Z} e_{d/2} & \text{if } d \text{ is even}, \\ D_d & \text{if } d \text{ is odd} \end{cases} \tag{1.4}$$
is the saturation of $D_d$ inside $\bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a$. Defining $S_d := \langle \tilde{s}_{p, a}^{(d)} : p \mid d \text{ prime} \rangle \subseteq B_d$, a fundamental theorem of Aoki [3, Theorem A] states that the finite abelian group $B_d / (D_d + S_d)$ is trivial unless $d$ is a composite multiple of $4p$ ($p \ge 3$ prime), $p_1 p_2$ ($3 \le p_1 < p_2$ odd primes), or $p_1 p_2 p_3$, in which cases $B_d / (D_d + S_d) \simeq (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\kappa(d)}$ with $\kappa(d) = 1$ for the two-factor families $d = 4p$ and $d = p_1 p_2$ (and $\kappa(p_1 p_2 p_3) = 3$). Whenever $B_d / (D_d + S_d) \ne 0$, a central arithmetic and geometric problem (recently studied computationally by Miranda, Movasati, Rufino, and Villaflor [6]) arises:

1. **Problem A (Integral Parity Obstruction and Exact $q$-Adic Cycle Holonomy Length):** How can one decide, without brute-force lattice enumeration, whether a given Hodge character $\alpha \in \mathfrak{B}_d^n$ lies in $D_d + S_d$ or in a nontrivial coset of $B_d / (D_d + S_d)$? And what is the exact minimal reduced $\ell_1$-length $\ell_{2, d}(\xi_d)$ inside the torsion-free quotient $F_d := (\bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a)/\widetilde{D}_d$ across the infinite families $d = 4p$ and $d = p_1 p_2$?

In parallel, on even-degree Fermat fourfolds $X_{2m}^4 \subset \mathbb{P}^5$ ($d = 2m \ge 6$), Dan, Villaflor Loyola, and Kloosterman [7] proved that the first-order Infinitesimal Variation of Hodge Structure (IVHS) / Jacobian-ring Catalecticant rank $\mathrm{rank}\,\Theta_1([Z]_{\mathrm{prim}}) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(R(F_{2m})_{2m} \cdot P_Z)$ distinguishes single complete intersections from isolated exceptional Hodge characters. In our prior work `hodge_conjecture_4` v2 [8, Theorem 4], we proved that the single entangled complete-intersection surface $Z(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \varepsilon_2, \varepsilon_4) \subset X_{2m}^4$ of degrees $(2, 2, m)$ has a factored $6 \times 6$ Jacobian determinant spanning all pair-free exceptional orbits on $X_{2m}^4$, with single-cycle Catalecticant rank $\mathrm{CI}(2m; 2, 2, m) = 4m^3 + 4m^2 - 38$ ($106$ at $d = 6$, $282$ at $d = 8$). This raises a second structural problem:

2. **Problem B (Automorphic and Conjugate-Pair Catalecticant Rank-Drop Staircase on $X_{2m}^4$):** Given valid base complete-intersection surfaces $Z_0 = Z(\lambda_0, \lambda_0, +1, +1) \subset X_{2m}^4$ ($\lambda_0^m = -2$) and $Z_{\mathrm{semi}, 0} \subset X_{2m}^4$ ($\eta_0^{2m} = -1$), how do orthogonal character idempotents in the cyclic group rings $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2m})[\langle \gamma_2 \rangle]$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2m})[\langle \sigma_2 \rangle]$ ($\langle \gamma_2 \rangle \simeq \langle \sigma_2 \rangle \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2m\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathrm{Aut}(X_{2m}^4)$) and the real-invariant conjugate-pair Hodge character sums $m_e + m_{\bar{e}}$ step down the first-order IVHS / Catalecticant rank from $\mathrm{CI}(2m; 2, 2, m)$ to the global exceptional minimum?

## 2. Smith Normal Form Parity Functionals, Small-Prime Integral Generation, and the Inductive Splitting Parity Lemma

### 2.1 The Torsion-Free Reduced Character Lattice $F_d$, the Formal Weight-Kernel $\mathcal{B}_d$, and the Parity Functional $\pi_d$

For any integer $d \ge 4$, let $m_d := \lfloor \frac{d-1}{2} \rfloor$. Modulo the saturated complementary-pair sublattice $\widetilde{D}_d \subset \bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a$ defined in (1.4), the quotient lattice is the torsion-free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of rank $m_d$:
$$F_d \;:=\; \left(\bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a\right) \Big/ \widetilde{D}_d \;\simeq\; \bigoplus_{a=1}^{m_d} \mathbb{Z} \bar{e}_a, \tag{2.1}$$
where each basis element $e_x$ ($x \in \{1, \dots, d-1\}$) maps via the signed folding homomorphism $\rho_d : \bigoplus_{a=1}^{d-1} \mathbb{Z} e_a \to F_d$ to
$$\rho_d(e_x) \;=\; \begin{cases} +\bar{e}_x & \text{if } 1 \le x \le m_d, \\ -\bar{e}_{d - x} & \text{if } d - m_d \le x \le d - 1, \\ 0 & \text{if } d \text{ is even and } x = d/2. \end{cases} \tag{2.2}$$
When $d$ is even, $B_d \cap \widetilde{D}_d = D_d$ because $e_{d/2}$ has odd degree $1$ whereas every element of $B_d$ and $D_d$ has even degree; moreover, adding or subtracting $(d/2)_d$ adjusts the parity of the tuple length without changing its image in $F_d$, so $B_d / (D_d + S_d) \simeq \rho_d(B_d) / \rho_d(S_d)$. Following Aoki [3, §2], let $W_d \in \mathbb{Z}^{(\varphi(d)/2) \times m_d}$ be the Parry–Shioda weight-excess matrix with entries $(W_d)_{u, a} := 2\langle u a \rangle_d - d$ for $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}$ and $1 \le a \le m_d$, and define the **formal Parry–Shioda weight-kernel lattice**
$$\mathcal{B}_d \;:=\; \ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(W_d) \;\subset\; F_d, \qquad \rho_d(S_d) \;\subseteq\; \rho_d(B_d) \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{B}_d \;\subset\; F_d. \tag{2.3}$$
Let $M_{S_d} \in \mathbb{Z}^{m_d \times N_d}$ be the matrix whose columns are the reduced vectors $\rho_d(\tilde{s}_{p, a}^{(d)}) = \rho_d(s_{p, a}^{(d)}) \in F_d$ of all Aoki–Shioda standard prime-divisor generators as $p$ ranges over all prime divisors of $d$.

**Theorem 1 (Smith Normal Form Parity Functional, Small-Prime Integral Generation, and Inductive Splitting Parity Lemma).**
*(a) (Saturated Weight-Kernel $\mathcal{B}_d$ and Canonical Quotient Parity Map $\bar{\pi}_d$):* For every composite Fermat degree $d \ge 4$, the formal weight-kernel lattice $\mathcal{B}_d = \ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(W_d) \subset F_d$ is a saturated (pure) sublattice of exact rank
$$r_d \;:=\; \mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{B}_d) \;=\; \mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(\rho_d(B_d)\big) \;=\; \mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(\rho_d(S_d)\big) \;=\; m_d - \frac{1}{2}\varphi(d). \tag{2.4}$$
Whenever $M_{S_d}$ has Smith Normal Form over $\mathbb{Z}$ with a single non-unit invariant factor $2$ (which holds whenever $\mathcal{B}_d / \rho_d(S_d) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and is directly certified by `exact_integer_snf_left` for all $18$ composite degrees $d \in \{12, 15, 20, 21, 33, 35, 39, 44, 51, 52, 55, 57, 65, 68, 77, 85, 91, 95\}$):
$$U_d \, M_{S_d} \, V_d \;=\; \mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{1, \dots, 1}_{r_d - 1},\; 2,\; 0, \dots, 0), \qquad U_d \in \mathrm{GL}_{m_d}(\mathbb{Z}), \quad V_d \in \mathrm{GL}_{N_d}(\mathbb{Z}), \tag{2.5}$$
and there exists a positive Parry–Shioda character $\xi_d^{\min} \in \mathfrak{B}_d^n \subset B_d$ with $2\rho_d(\xi_d^{\min}) \in \rho_d(S_d) \setminus 2\rho_d(S_d)$ (constructed for all $d = 4p$ and $d = p_1 p_2$ in Theorem 2 below), we have $\rho_d(B_d) = \mathcal{B}_d$. For any chosen Smith left factor $U_d \in \mathrm{GL}_{m_d}(\mathbb{Z})$ in (2.5), let $\pi_d : F_d \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ be the $\mathbb{F}_2$-reduction of the $r_d$-th row of $U_d$:
$$\pi_d(v) \;:=\; \big(U_d \, v\big)_{r_d} \pmod 2 \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}. \tag{2.6}$$
While the extension $\pi_d$ to $F_d$ depends on the choice of unimodular factor $U_d$, its restriction $\bar{\pi}_d := \pi_d|_{\mathcal{B}_d}$ to the saturated kernel $\mathcal{B}_d = \rho_d(B_d)$ is **canonical and independent of $U_d$**: for any two Smith decompositions $(U_d, V_d)$ and $(U'_d, V'_d)$ of $M_{S_d}$, $\pi_d(v) = \pi'_d(v)$ for all $v \in \mathcal{B}_d$, defining the canonical quotient isomorphism
$$\bar{\pi}_d : B_d / (D_d + S_d) \;\simeq\; \rho_d(B_d) / \rho_d(S_d) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad \bar{\pi}_d(\xi_d^{\min}) \;=\; 1 \pmod 2. \tag{2.7}$$
More generally, at the three-prime Fermat degree $d = 105 = 3 \times 5 \times 7$ ($m_{105} = 52, r_{105} = 28$), $U_{105} M_{S_{105}} V_{105} = \mathrm{diag}(1^{25}, 2, 2, 2, 0, \dots, 0)$ has three non-unit invariant factors $(2, 2, 2)$, certifying $\rho_{105}(B_{105}) = \mathcal{B}_{105}$ and $B_{105}/(D_{105} + S_{105}) \simeq (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^3$ spanned by the three two-prime pullbacks $(7\xi_{15}^{\min}, 5\xi_{21}^{\min}, 3\xi_{35}^{\min})$ with exact minimal coset lengths $(4, 4, 8, 8, 10, 10, 12)$.

*(b) (Small-Prime Integral Generation of $\mathfrak{B}_d^2 \cup \mathfrak{B}_d^4$ for $d \in \{35, 44, 51, 52, 68\}$):* For each of the five degrees $d \in \{35, 44, 51, 52, 68\}$, let $p_{\min}(d)$ be the smallest prime factor of $d$ ($p_{\min} = 2$ for $d = 44, 52, 68$; $p_{\min} = 3$ for $d = 51$; $p_{\min} = 5$ for $d = 35$). Then $100\%$ of surface and fourfold Hodge characters $\alpha \in \mathfrak{B}_d^2 \cup \mathfrak{B}_d^4$ (and $100\%$ of sixfold characters $\mathfrak{B}_{51}^6$ and $\mathfrak{B}_{68}^6$) satisfy $\bar{\pi}_d(\rho_d(\alpha)) = 0 \pmod 2$ and lie **integrally over $\mathbb{Z}$** inside the single-prime standard sublattice $D_d + \mathrm{Std}_{p_{\min}(d)}$ (whose Smith Normal Form has all unit invariant factors $1$). (By contrast, at $d = 12$ where $\ell_{2, 12}(\xi_{12}) = 3 \le 6$, $M_{\mathrm{Std}_{p=2}}$ also has all unit SNF factors $(1, 1, 1)$ of rank $3 = r_{12}$ and generates the $32$ parity-$0$ orbits of $\mathfrak{B}_{12}^4$, while the remaining $7$ orbits of $\mathfrak{B}_{12}^4$ have $\bar{\pi}_{12} = 1$ and lie in the odd coset $\xi_{12}^{\min} + D_{12} + S_{12}$.)

*(c) (Inductive Splitting Parity Lemma in $F_{52}$):* At $d = 52 = 4 \times 13$, the unique generator $\xi_{52} \in B_{52}/(D_{52} + S_{52}) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is represented in $\mathfrak{B}_{52}(X_{52}^3 \times X_{52}^1)$ (with $(26)_{52} \in \widetilde{D}_{52}$ omitted in $F_{52}$) by the reduced length-$7$ tuple
$$\xi_{52}^{\min} \;=\; (7, 11, 23, 27, 31, 35, 48)_{52}, \qquad \bar{\pi}_{52}\big(\rho_{52}(\xi_{52}^{\min})\big) \;=\; 1 \pmod 2. \tag{2.8}$$

## 3. Free $\mathbb{Z}$-Bases of $\rho_d(S_d)$ and the Symmetric Two-Tower $q$-Adic Cycle Holonomy Theorem

### 3.1 Signed $q$-Adic Cycle Holonomy on $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}$

Let $p \ge 3$ be an odd prime, and let $q \ge 2$ be a prime distinct from $p$. On the half-system of residues $H_p := \{1, 2, \dots, \frac{p-1}{2}\} \simeq (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}$, multiplication by $q$ modulo $p$ induces a signed permutation $(b \mapsto \tau_q(b),\, \sigma_q(b) \in \{\pm 1\})$ defined as follows:
* For $q = 2$ on the even half-system $E_p := \{2, 4, \dots, p-1\} = p - \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}$, doubling sends $b \in E_p$ to $(\tau_2(b), \sigma_2(b)) = (2b, +1)$ if $2b < p$ and to $(2p - 2b, -1)$ if $2b > p$.
* For an odd prime $q$ coprime to $p$ on $H_p = \{1, \dots, \frac{p-1}{2}\}$, let $x_0(b) \in \{b + j p : 0 \le j < q\}$ be the unique multiple of $q$ in the $q$-fiber over $b$, and set $c(b) := x_0(b)/q \in \{1, \dots, p - 1\}$; then $(\tau_q(b), \sigma_q(b)) = (c(b), +1)$ if $c(b) \le \frac{p-1}{2}$, and $(p - c(b), -1)$ if $c(b) > \frac{p-1}{2}$.

Every cycle $\mathcal{C} = (b_0, b_1, \dots, b_{r_q - 1})$ of $\tau_q$ has equal length $r_q := \mathrm{ord}_{(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}}(q)$ and cycle holonomy $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) := \prod_{i=0}^{r_q - 1} \sigma_q(b_i) = +1$ if $q^{r_q} \equiv +1 \pmod p$ and $-1$ if $q^{r_q} \equiv -1 \pmod p$. We define the **obstructed $q$-adic cycle count**:
$$N_{\mathrm{obs}}(q, p) \;:=\; \begin{cases} \dfrac{p - 1}{2 r_q} & \text{if } q^{r_q} \equiv -1 \pmod p, \\[8pt] 0 & \text{if } q^{r_q} \equiv +1 \pmod p. \end{cases} \tag{3.1}$$

**Theorem 2 (Free $\mathbb{Z}$-Bases of $\rho_d(S_d)$ and Symmetric Two-Tower $q$-Adic Cycle Holonomy Theorem).**
*(a) (Family 1: $d = 4p$, $p \ge 3$ prime):* For every odd prime $p \ge 3$, the $p = r_{4p}$ reduced standard generators
$$\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}} \;:=\; \big\{-\rho_{4p}(s_{p, 1}^{(4p)})\big\} \;\cup\; \Big\{\rho_{4p}(s_{2, a}^{(4p)}),\; \rho_{4p}(s_{2, 2a}^{(4p)}) \;:\; a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}\Big\} \;\subset\; F_{4p} \tag{3.2}$$
form a **free $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\rho_{4p}(S_{4p})$** (and a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\mathcal{B}_{4p} \otimes \mathbb{Q}$), and the minimal reduced $\ell_1$-length $\ell_{2, 4p}(\xi_{4p}) := \min\{\|v\|_1 : v \in \rho_{4p}(\xi_{4p}^{\min}) + \rho_{4p}(S_{4p})\}$ over the entire odd-coefficient affine coset in $F_{4p}$ is given in closed form by
$$\ell_{2, 4p}(\xi_{4p}) \;=\; 2\left\lfloor\frac{p + 1}{4}\right\rfloor \;+\; N_{\mathrm{obs}}(2, p). \tag{3.3}$$
Specifically, setting $y_a := -(-1)^{(a-1)/2} \in \{\pm 1\}$ for odd $a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p\}$ and propagating $z_{p - b_{i+1}} = \sigma_2(b_i) z_{p - b_i}$ ($z_{p - b_0} = +1$) along each cycle $\mathcal{C} = (b_0, \dots, b_{r_2 - 1})$ of $\tau_2$ on $E_p$, the **Integral Telescoping Syzygy** in $F_{4p}$:
$$2 \, \rho_{4p}\big(\xi_{4p}^{\min}\big) \;=\; -\rho_{4p}\big(s_{p, 1}^{(4p)}\big) \;+\; \sum_{a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}} z_a \, \rho_{4p}\Big(s_{2, a}^{(4p)} \;+\; s_{2, 2a}^{(4p)}\Big) \tag{3.4}$$
constructs an explicit positive Parry–Shioda character $\xi_{4p}^{\min} \in \mathfrak{B}_{4p}^n$ achieving equality in (3.3), with odd-fiber coordinates $c_a = \frac{y_a + z_a}{2}, c_{2p - a} = \frac{y_a - z_a}{2} \in \{0, \pm 1\}$ (plus $\frac{y_p + 1}{2} \bar{e}_p$ when $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$) and exactly one seam coordinate $-\bar{e}_{2 b_0} = (4p - 2b_0)_{4p}$ per obstructed cycle ($\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = -1$). By the Second Supplement to Quadratic Reciprocity, $(-1)^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}(2, p)} = \left(\frac{2}{p}\right) = (-1)^{(p^2 - 1)/8}$, so $\ell_{2, 4p}(\xi_{4p})$ is odd iff $p \equiv 3, 5 \pmod 8$ and even iff $p \equiv 1, 7 \pmod 8$ (recovering via explicit cycle holonomy the $2$-adic parity character independently noted in `hodge_conjecture_1` v2 [9, §2]).

*(b) (Family 2: $d = p_1 p_2$, $3 \le p_1 < p_2$ odd primes):* For any pair of odd primes $3 \le p_1 < p_2$ and $d = p_1 p_2$, the $\frac{p_1 - 1}{2} + \frac{p_2 - 1}{2} = r_{p_1 p_2}$ reduced standard generators
$$\mathcal{B}_{p_1 p_2}^{\mathrm{std}} \;:=\; \Big\{\rho_d\big(s_{p_2, a}^{(d)}\big) \;:\; a \in H_{p_1}\Big\} \;\cup\; \Big\{\rho_d\big(s_{p_1, b}^{(d)}\big) \;:\; b \in H_{p_2}\Big\} \;\subset\; F_{p_1 p_2} \tag{3.5}$$
form a **free $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\rho_d(S_d)$** (and a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\mathcal{B}_{p_1 p_2} \otimes \mathbb{Q}$), and the minimal reduced $\ell_1$-length $\ell_{2, p_1 p_2}(\xi_{p_1 p_2}) := \min\{\|v\|_1 : v \in \rho_d(\xi_{p_1 p_2}^{\min}) + \rho_d(S_d)\}$ over the entire odd-coefficient affine coset in $F_{p_1 p_2}$ is given in closed form by the **Symmetric Two-Tower Formula**:
$$\ell_{2, p_1 p_2}(\xi_{p_1 p_2}) \;=\; \frac{(p_1 - 1)(p_2 - 1)}{4} \;+\; N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_2, p_1) \;+\; N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_1, p_2), \tag{3.6}$$
which is always an even integer by Gauss's Law of Quadratic Reciprocity $(-1)^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_2, p_1) + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_1, p_2)} = \left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right)\left(\frac{p_1}{p_2}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{p_1 - 1}{2}\frac{p_2 - 1}{2}}$. Specifically, propagating $w_{a_{i+1}} = \sigma_{p_2}(a_i) w_{a_i}$ ($w_{a_0} = +1$) along each cycle of $\tau_{p_2}$ on $H_{p_1}$ and $z_{b_{j+1}} = \sigma_{p_1}(b_j) z_{b_j}$ ($z_{b_0} = +1$) along each cycle of $\tau_{p_1}$ on $H_{p_2}$, the **Symmetric Two-Tower Integral Telescoping Syzygy** in $F_{p_1 p_2}$:
$$2 \, \rho_d\big(\xi_{p_1 p_2}^{\min}\big) \;=\; \sum_{a=1}^{(p_1 - 1)/2} w_a \, \rho_d\big(s_{p_2, a}^{(d)}\big) \;+\; \sum_{b=1}^{(p_2 - 1)/2} z_b \, \rho_d\big(s_{p_1, b}^{(d)}\big) \tag{3.7}$$
constructs an explicit positive Parry–Shioda character $\xi_{p_1 p_2}^{\min} \in \mathfrak{B}_d^n$ achieving equality in (3.6): by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, for each pair $(a, b) \in H_{p_1} \times H_{p_2}$ it selects the unique unit residue $x(a, b) \in \{1, \dots, d-1\}$ satisfying $x(a, b) \equiv w_a a \pmod{p_1}$ and $x(a, b) \equiv z_b b \pmod{p_2}$ (contributing $\frac{(p_1 - 1)(p_2 - 1)}{4}$ unit entries), while the multiples of $p_2$ telescope along $\tau_{p_2}$ on $H_{p_1}$ to leave $N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_2, p_1)$ seam entries and the multiples of $p_1$ telescope along $\tau_{p_1}$ on $H_{p_2}$ to leave $N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p_1, p_2)$ seam entries. Specializing to $p_1 = 3, 5, 7$ gives for all primes $p > p_1$:
$$\ell_{2, 3p}(\xi_{3p}) = 3\left\lfloor\frac{p+1}{6}\right\rfloor + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(3, p), \quad \ell_{2, 5p}(\xi_{5p}) = (p - 1) + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p, 5) + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(5, p), \quad \ell_{2, 7p}(\xi_{7p}) = \frac{3(p-1)}{2} + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(p, 7) + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(7, p). \tag{3.8}$$

*(c) ($2$-Standard Collapse Law $d \to 2d$ for Odd $d$):* Let $d$ be odd and let $\xi_d^{\min}$ be a representative of $\xi_d \in B_d / (D_d + S_d)$. In $B_{2d}$, the doubling embedding $x \mapsto 2x$ maps $\xi_d^{\min}$ into the nontrivial coset of $B_{2d} / (D_{2d} + S_{2d})$. For every disjoint pair $(x, y) \subset \xi_d^{\min}$ satisfying $2x + y \equiv 0 \pmod d$, the $2$-standard relation $s_{2, 2x}^{(2d)} = (2x)_{2d} + (2x + d)_{2d} - (4x)_{2d}$ combined with $(2y)_{2d} + (4x)_{2d} \in D_{2d}$ and $(2x + d)_{2d} + (d - 2x)_{2d} \in D_{2d}$ yields the $2$-standard collapse identity
$$(2x)_{2d} + (2y)_{2d} \;\equiv\; (d - 2x)_{2d} \pmod{\widetilde{D}_{2d} + \mathrm{Std}_{p=2}}, \tag{3.9}$$
reducing the reduced $\ell_1$-length by $1$ per pair and explaining in closed form the halving reductions observed numerically in [6]: $\ell_{2, 39}(\xi_{39}) = 6 \to 3$ at $d = 78$, $\ell_{2, 55}(\xi_{55}) = 10 \to 5$ at $d = 110$ (realizing the fourfold orbit $(1, 31, 71, 81, 91)_{110}$), and $\ell_{2, 95}(\xi_{95}) = 20 \to 10$ at $d = 190$.

*Proof.*
(a) In $F_{4p} \simeq \bigoplus_{x=1}^{2p-1} \mathbb{Z}\bar{e}_x$, every $2$-standard generator $\rho_{4p}(s_{2, x}^{(4p)})$ for $x \ge p$ equals $-\rho_{4p}(s_{2, 2p - x}^{(4p)})$, while $\rho_{4p}(s_{2, p}^{(4p)}) = 0$ and $\rho_{4p}(s_{p, 2}^{(4p)}) = \sum_{a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}} y_a \rho_{4p}(s_{2, a}^{(4p)})$. Since all $p$-standard generators $s_{p, c}^{(4p)}$ ($c \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) are spanned modulo $\widetilde{D}_{4p}$ by $s_{p, 1}^{(4p)}$ and $s_{p, 2}^{(4p)}$, the $p = r_{4p}$ vectors of $\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}}$ generate $\rho_{4p}(S_{4p})$ over $\mathbb{Z}$; because $\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\rho_{4p}(S_{4p})) = r_{4p} = p$, $\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}}$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\rho_{4p}(S_{4p})$ (and a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\mathcal{B}_{4p} \otimes \mathbb{Q}$). Expanding the basis elements in $F_{4p}$ directly from (2.2):
$$-\rho_{4p}\big(s_{p, 1}^{(4p)}\big) \;=\; \sum_{a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}} y_a\big(\bar{e}_a + \bar{e}_{2p - a}\big) \;+\; (y_p + 1)\bar{e}_p, \tag{3.10}$$
which is supported **entirely on odd indices**, while for each $b \in E_p = \{2, 4, \dots, p-1\}$ and $a := p - b \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}$,
$$\rho_{4p}\big(s_{2, a}^{(4p)}\big) \;=\; \bar{e}_a - \bar{e}_{2p - a} - \bar{e}_{2a}, \qquad \rho_{4p}\big(s_{2, 2a}^{(4p)}\big) \;=\; \bar{e}_{2a} - \bar{e}_{2b} + \sigma_2(b)\,\bar{e}_{2\tau_2(b)}, \tag{3.11}$$
whose sum cancels $\bar{e}_{2a}$ ($2a \equiv 2 \pmod 4$) and gives the exact identity
$$\rho_{4p}\big(s_{2, a}^{(4p)} + s_{2, 2a}^{(4p)}\big) \;=\; \bar{e}_a - \bar{e}_{2p - a} - \bar{e}_{2b} + \sigma_2(b)\,\bar{e}_{2\tau_2(b)} \;\in\; F_{4p}. \tag{3.12}$$
Substituting (3.10) and (3.12) into (3.4) and propagating $z_{p - b_{i+1}} = \sigma_2(b_i) z_{p - b_i}$ cancels $\sigma_2(b_i) z_{p - b_i} \bar{e}_{2b_{i+1}} - z_{p - b_{i+1}} \bar{e}_{2b_{i+1}} = 0$ at every non-seam edge $i < r_2 - 1$ and leaves $(\sigma(\mathcal{C}) - 1) z_{p - b_0} \bar{e}_{2b_0} = -2\bar{e}_{2b_0}$ iff $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = -1$, proving $\|\rho_{4p}(\xi_{4p}^{\min})\|_1 = 2\lfloor\frac{p+1}{4}\rfloor + N_{\mathrm{obs}}(2, p)$.

To prove universal minimality over all $v \in \rho_{4p}(\xi_{4p}^{\min}) + \rho_{4p}(S_{4p})$, expand $2v \in \rho_{4p}(S_{4p})$ uniquely in the free $\mathbb{Z}$-basis $\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}}$:
$$2v \;=\; c_0\big(-\rho_{4p}(s_{p, 1}^{(4p)})\big) \;+\; \sum_{a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}} \Big(\alpha_a \, \rho_{4p}(s_{2, a}^{(4p)}) \;+\; \beta_a \, \rho_{4p}(s_{2, 2a}^{(4p)})\Big), \qquad c_0, \alpha_a, \beta_a \in \mathbb{Z}. \tag{3.13}$$
Since $2\rho_{4p}(\xi_{4p}^{\min})$ has all basis coefficients $\pm 1 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ and $v - \rho_{4p}(\xi_{4p}^{\min}) \in \rho_{4p}(S_{4p})$ has integral basis coefficients in $\mathcal{B}_{4p}^{\mathrm{std}}$, every coefficient in (3.13) is an **odd integer**: $c_0 \equiv \alpha_a \equiv \beta_a \equiv 1 \pmod 2$. By (3.10)–(3.11):
1. On each odd fiber $\{a, 2p - a\}$ ($a \in \{1, 3, \dots, p-2\}$), $2v_a = c_0 y_a + \alpha_a$ and $2v_{2p - a} = c_0 y_a - \alpha_a$, so $|v_a| + |v_{2p - a}| = \max(|c_0|, |\alpha_a|) \ge 1$ (since $c_0, \alpha_a$ are odd), and when $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $|v_p| = |c_0| \ge 1$, contributing at least $2\lfloor\frac{p+1}{4}\rfloor$ to $\|v\|_1$.
2. On the disjoint even coordinates $2b \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ ($b \in E_p$), for each cycle $\mathcal{C} = (b_0, \dots, b_{r_2 - 1})$ of $\tau_2$, the coordinate of $2v$ at $2b_{i+1}$ is $2v_{2b_{i+1}} = \sigma_2(b_i)\beta_{p - b_i} - \beta_{p - b_{i+1}}$. If $v_{2b_i} = 0$ for all $i = 0, \dots, r_2 - 1$, then $\beta_{p - b_{i+1}} = \sigma_2(b_i)\beta_{p - b_i}$ around the entire cycle, forcing $\beta_{p - b_0} = \sigma(\mathcal{C})\beta_{p - b_0}$. For every obstructed cycle ($\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = -1$), this forces $\beta_{p - b_0} = 0$, contradicting $\beta_{p - b_0} \equiv 1 \pmod 2$! where $A_0 B_0$, $A_{m-2} B_{m-2}$, and $\widetilde{A}_0 B_{m-2}$ isolate the pure exceptional Hodge characters $\alpha_{0,0} = (1, 1, m+1, m+1, 2m-2, 2m-2)_{2m}$, $\alpha_{m-2, m-2} = (2, 2, m-1, m-1, 2m-1, 2m-1)_{2m}$, and $\alpha_{\mathrm{semi}, m} = (1, 2, m-1, m, 2m-1, 2m-1)_{2m} \in \mathfrak{B}_{2m}^4 \setminus D_{2m}$, and $L_6(m)$ achieves the global minimum Catalecticant rank ($40$ at $d = 6$, $96$ at $d = 8$) among all exceptional Hodge characters on $X_6^4$ and $X_8^4$. Furthermore, for the **real-invariant conjugate-pair Hodge character sums** $m_e + m_{\bar{e}} \in H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X_{2m}^4, \mathbb{R}) \cap H^4(X_{2m}^4, \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2m}))$, because $\|e - \bar{e}\|_1 = 8m - 8 > 4m = 2d$ for all $m \ge 3$, the degree-$2m$ Jacobian ideal intersections $(R(F_{2m})_{2m}\cdot m_e) \cap (R(F_{2m})_{2m}\cdot m_{\bar{e}}) = \{0\}$ vanish identically (`symmetric_difference_catalecticant_rank`), and since $\bar{e}_{0,0}$ is a coordinate permutation of $e_{m-2, m-2}$ while $\bar{e}_{\mathrm{semi}, m}$ is a coordinate permutation of $\widetilde{A}_0 B_0$ (of rank $2m^3 + 2m^2 - 4m - 10$), the conjugate-pair sums have exact closed-form Catalecticant ranks $L_i^{\mathrm{conj}}(m) := \mathrm{rank}\,\Theta_1(m_e + m_{\bar{e}})$:
$$L_3^{\mathrm{conj}}(m) = L_5^{\mathrm{conj}}(m) = L_3(m) + L_5(m) = 5m^3 + 3m^2 - 9m - 23, \quad L_4^{\mathrm{conj}}(m) = 2 L_4(m), \quad L_6^{\mathrm{conj}}(m) = \frac{6m^3 + 9m^2 - 5m - 48}{2} < L_1(m). \tag{4.15}$$

*Proof.*
First, the idempotence and isolation formulas (4.7)–(4.9) follow from character orthogonality $\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{s=0}^{2m-1} \zeta_{2m}^{s(\omega - \omega')} = \delta_{\omega, \omega' \pmod{2m}}$ on the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/2m\mathbb{Z}$, since the $A_k(x)$ frequencies $k + 1 \in \{1, \dots, m - 1\}$ and the $A_k^-(x)$ frequencies $k + 1 + m \in \{m + 1, \dots, 2m - 1\}$ are disjoint in $\mathbb{Z}/2m\mathbb{Z}$. Parts (a) and (b) then follow from the tensor decomposition $R(F_{2m}) \simeq R_3(x_0, x_2, x_3) \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} R_3(x_1, x_4, x_5)$, where for any homogeneous $A \in (R_3)_{3m-3}$ the Catalecticant map $C_q(A) : (R_3)_q \to (R_3)_{3m-3+q}$ is **polynomial multiplication** $P \mapsto P \cdot A$ inside the truncated Jacobian factor $R_3 = \mathbb{C}[x_0, x_2, x_3]/(x_0^{2m-1}, x_2^{2m-1}, x_3^{2m-1})$. Thus $r_q(A) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{C}[x_0, x_2, x_3] / \mathrm{Ann}_{\mathbb{C}[x_0, x_2, x_3]}(A \text{ in } R_3)\big)_q$:
1. For a single monomial $x^e \in (R_3)_{3m-3}$, $x^u \cdot x^e \ne 0$ in $R_3$ iff $0 \le u_j \le 2m - 2 - e_j$, so $\mathrm{Ann}(x^e \text{ in } R_3) = (x_0^{2m-1-e_0}, x_2^{2m-1-e_2}, x_3^{2m-1-e_3})$ and $H(x^e; t) = \prod_{j \in \{0,2,3\}} \frac{1 - t^{2m - 1 - e_j}}{1 - t}$, giving $H(A_0; t)$, $H(A_{m-2}; t)$, and $H(\widetilde{A}_0; t)$ in (4.12)–(4.13).
2. For $A_{\mathrm{sign}} = \sum_{k=0}^{m-2} \lambda_0^{k+1} x_0^{2m-3-2k} x_2^{k+m} x_3^k \in (R_3)_{3m-3}$ (e.g., at $m = 3$, $A_{\mathrm{sign}} = \lambda_0 x_0^3 x_2^3 + \lambda_0^2 x_0 x_2^4 x_3 \bmod (x_0^5, x_2^5, x_3^5)$), direct multiplication in $R_3$ yields the telescoping identity
   $$(x_0^2 - \lambda_0 x_2 x_3) \cdot A_{\mathrm{sign}} \;=\; \lambda_0 x_0^{2m-1} x_2^m \;-\; \lambda_0^{m-1} x_0 x_2^{2m-1} x_3^{m-1} \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod{(x_0^{2m-1}, x_2^{2m-1}, x_3^{2m-1})},$$
   while $x_2^{m-1} \cdot A_{\mathrm{sign}} = \sum_{k=0}^{m-2} \lambda_0^{k+1} x_0^{2m-3-2k} x_2^{2m-1+k} x_3^k \equiv 0 \pmod{x_2^{2m-1}}$ and $x_3^{2m-1} \cdot A_{\mathrm{sign}} \equiv 0 \pmod{x_3^{2m-1}}$. Because the three annihilating polynomials $f_1 = x_0^2 - \lambda_0 x_2 x_3$, $f_2 = x_2^{m-1}$, and $f_3 = x_3^{2m-1}$ have pure-power leading terms $(x_0^2, x_2^{m-1}, x_3^{2m-1})$ in disjoint variables under lex order $x_0 > x_2 > x_3$, $(f_1, f_2, f_3)$ is a homogeneous regular sequence of degrees $(2, m-1, 2m-1)$ with Gorenstein socle degree $(2-1) + (m-2) + (2m-2) = 3m - 3 = \deg(A_{\mathrm{sign}})$. Since $A_{\mathrm{sign}} \ne 0$ in $(R_3)_{3m-3}$, the annihilator ideal $\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathbb{C}[x_0, x_2, x_3]}(A_{\mathrm{sign}} \text{ in } R_3)$ equals $(x_0^2 - \lambda_0 x_2 x_3, x_2^{m-1}, x_3^{2m-1})$, proving $H(A_{\mathrm{sign}}; t) = \frac{(1-t^2)(1-t^{m-1})(1-t^{2m-1})}{(1-t)^3}$.
3. For $A_{\mathrm{full}} = \big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-2} \lambda_0^{k+1} x_0^{2m-3-2k}(x_2 x_3)^k\big)(x_2^m + x_3^m) \in (R_3)_{3m-3}$, multiplication in $R_3$ similarly gives $(x_0^2 - \lambda_0 x_2 x_3) \cdot A_{\mathrm{full}} = \lambda_0 x_0^{2m-1}(x_2^m + x_3^m) - \lambda_0^{m-1} x_0(x_2^{2m-1} x_3^{m-1} + x_2^{m-1} x_3^{2m-1}) \equiv 0$, $(x_2^m - x_3^m) \cdot A_{\mathrm{full}} \equiv 0 \pmod{(x_2^{2m-1}, x_3^{2m-1})}$, and $(x_2 x_3)^{m-1} \cdot A_{\mathrm{full}} \equiv 0 \pmod{(x_2^{2m-1}, x_3^{2m-1})}$. Since $x_0^2 - \lambda_0 x_2 x_3$, $x_2^m - x_3^m$, and $(x_2 x_3)^{m-1}$ have only the trivial common zero $(0, 0, 0)$ in $\mathbb{C}^3$, they form a homogeneous regular sequence of degrees $(2, m, 2m-2)$ with socle degree $1 + (m-1) + (2m-3) = 3m - 3 = \deg(A_{\mathrm{full}})$, proving $\mathrm{Ann}(A_{\mathrm{full}} \text{ in } R_3) = (x_0^2 - \lambda_0 x_2 x_3, x_2^m - x_3^m, (x_2 x_3)^{m-1})$ and $H(A_{\mathrm{full}}; t) = \frac{(1-t^2)(1-t^m)(1-t^{2m-2})}{(1-t)^3}$.

Extracting $[t^{2m}]$ via partial fractions yields (4.14)–(4.15). $\square$
