# The degree-44 exceptional Fermat class in the K3 representation: a norm obstruction to isometries, its degree-12 and degree-15 analogues, and the Hodge conjecture for products of non-isometric CM K3 surfaces

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-10-18T10:37:12.155226Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/13/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study $\xi_{44}$ in the K3 representation. Up to standard cycles it is the join $b*(-c)$ of surface characters $b=(4,5,13,22)$, $c=(3,7,11,23)$ whose orbits span rank-20 K3-type blocks with CM by $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{44})$. (A) From the integral Pham model we compute the hermitian forms of both blocks exactly: their comparison $r\in K^+$ has $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(r)=2^{-10}11^{-2}$, so $r\notin\mathbb Q^*N(K^*)$. By the Hasse norm theorem its class modulo norms is that of $11+3\sqrt{11}\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt{11})$, and its obstructions at 2 and 11 survive rational rescaling; the blocks are not Hodge isometric even after rescaling, no isometry theorem applied to form-preserving realisations of the blocks can produce $\xi_{44}$, and every Hodge morphism between the blocks has comparison in this fixed non-norm class. (B) $c$ is the unique rank-one K3-type orbit at level 44 with even parity $\kappa$; in 241 pairs obstructions occur only across a parity change. (D) Via Looijenga's closed form $\lambda_t=-d^{-3}\prod_i(1-\zeta_d^{t_i})$ we obtain at $d=4p$ an explicit criterion: the obstruction is the parity of the number of entries of order 4 when $(2/p)=-1$, and of order $p$ when $p\equiv3\bmod4$, the K3-side counterpart of hodge_conjecture_9's detector; moreover the parity $\kappa$ of hodge_conjecture_1 equals the $p$-adic part of the norm class. (C) At degree 12 (HC known) the analogous joins are equally non-isometric, some lie outside the tested standard-cycle spans, yet 19 of 20 reduce to known algebraic pieces (18 as a linear pair plus a Fermat-fourfold class). At 44 no such splitting exists. (E) At degrees 12 and 15 the analogous obstruction is crossed by Shioda's cycles, giving HC for explicit products of Hodge-isomorphic but non-isometric CM K3 surfaces (Picard number 14 at degree 15). Bearing on HC: new K3-product cases; no new Fermat case. We explain the degree-12 positives and constrain, within the character framework, any cycle for $\xi_{44}$.
