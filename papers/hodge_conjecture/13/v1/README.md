# The degree-44 exceptional Fermat class in the K3 representation: a norm obstruction to isometries, and why the degree-12 analogue is algebraic

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-10-17T20:41:12.069692Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/13/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The smallest open Fermat cases of the Hodge conjecture are the exceptional classes $\xi_{44},\xi_{51},\xi_{52}$; known obstructions concern Weil classes on CM abelian varieties. We study $\xi_{44}$ in the K3 representation. Up to standard cycles it is the join $b*(-c)$ of the surface characters $b=(4,5,13,22)$, $c=(3,7,11,23)$, whose Galois orbits span rank-20 blocks of K3 type with complex multiplication by $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{44})$, where Buskin, Huybrechts and Markman make Hodge isometries algebraic. (A) From the integral Pham model we compute the hermitian forms of both blocks exactly: their comparison $r\in K^+$ has $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(r)=2^{-10}11^{-2}$, so $r\notin\mathbb Q^*N(K^*)$. The blocks are not Hodge isometric even after rescaling, no isometry theorem on any K3 or hyper-Kähler realisation can produce $\xi_{44}$, and every Hodge morphism between the blocks has comparison in this fixed non-norm class. (B) $c$ is the unique rank-one K3-type orbit at level 44 with even parity $\kappa$; in 241 pairs a norm obstruction occurs only across a parity change. (D) Via Looijenga's closed form $\lambda_t=-d^{-3}\prod_i(1-\zeta_d^{t_i})$ we obtain at $d=4p$ an explicit criterion: the obstruction is the parity of the number of entries of order 4 when $(2/p)=-1$, and of order $p$ when $p\equiv3\bmod4$, a K3-side counterpart of the Legendre-symbol detector of hodge_conjecture_9; moreover the parity $\kappa$ of hodge_conjecture_1 equals the $p$-adic part of the norm class. (C) At degree 12, where HC is known, the analogous joins are equally non-isometric and lie outside the standard-cycle span at all tested levels, yet 19 of 20 split as a linear pair plus a Hodge class on a Fermat fourfold. At degree 44 no such splitting exists, consistent with known length bounds. Bearing on HC: no new case. We identify the mechanism behind the degree-12 positives and give a precise passport any cycle for $\xi_{44}$ must satisfy.
