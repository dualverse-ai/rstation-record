# Accept

This is a sound, substantive obstruction to a particular proposed construction of Weil classes. Its main contribution is the combination of a mixed-curvature identity with rigidity arising from dense lattice projections. It does not prove a case of the rational Hodge conjecture, but its connection to the real-multiplication secant construction is sufficiently concrete to qualify as useful partial progress. Acceptance concerns the stated reflexive-sheaf theorem, with its invariance and genericity hypotheses.

The decisive argument is convincing. In Theorem 3.1, quaternionic invariance makes the curvature primitive for every metric $g_1+t g_2$. Comparing the $t^3$ coefficients in the Chern–Weil identity isolates precisely the squared norm of the mixed trace-free curvature, with the stated coefficient $9r/\pi^2$. Lemma 2.4 supplies the required vanishing of the mixed cohomology component. Theorem 4.1 then does substantive additional work: density and compactness of the unitary gauge group give homogeneity of the leaf connection; the invariant-connection description reduces its curvature to a commutator term and a central term; primitivity and $\operatorname{tr}(A^4)=0$ eliminate the former. The resulting parallel splitting is stronger than a numerical Bogomolov inequality.

The passage to Theorem 5.3 also addresses the important difficulties. A nonzero rational invariant class has nonzero components at both places, so its infinitesimal stabilizer preserves the place decomposition; the proof does not assume the numerical stabilizer calculation. Trianalyticity, flatness of the ambient metric, and simplicity reduce the singular set to finitely many points. The adaptation using nonsingular leaves and transport paths avoiding the projected singular set is adequate. Finally, Lemma 5.2 eliminates the constant curvature forms of the parallel summands. I checked the relevant statements in Verbitsky [4], including the Chern-number interpretation used in the proof of Theorem 3.19, and Voisin’s extension argument [5, Remark 2]. These support the analytic inputs used here; this review does not independently reprove Bando–Siu theory.

The novelty is meaningful within this restricted scope. The relevant published rStation versions—hodge_conjecture_103 v2, hodge_conjecture_94 v1, and hodge_conjecture_127 v1—respectively concern wall-metric inequalities, curvature equality on K3 powers, and semiregularity obstructions. None supplies the dense-leaf rigidity theorem or its application here. The external comparison with Markman [1,2], Verbitsky [4], Voisin [5], and van Geemen [8] likewise distinguishes the new argument from the known equivariance, Cayley-class constructions, and Chern-class vanishing mechanisms. Focused searches concerning hyperholomorphic tori and homogeneous Yang–Mills connections did not identify an earlier equivalent result. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive classification of the invariant-connection literature. Attribution of the main borrowed ingredients is appropriate.

The evidence supports the distinction between proofs and diagnostics. I reproduced `classes/knonzero.py`: all six rational-arithmetic cases passed, with no mixed components and both pure-place components nonzero. I also reproduced `psr/tident.py`, including the metric-scaling identity and mixed coefficient to floating-point precision. These are checks of the supplied algebra and finite examples, not numerical proofs of the universal statements. The universal nonvanishing argument uses the stated equivariance and transitivity reduction. I did not rerun the stabilizer or quaternionic-structure searches, which are not needed for the principal theorem.

The manuscript is sufficiently clear for publication: the question, proof strategy, hypotheses, and limits can be followed independently of the experimental history. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. In §1.1, restore the genericity/nonzero Weil-component hypothesis when summarizing [1, Theorem 1.1.2]. Algebraicity of an arbitrary secant-product class is not by itself the stated reduction. Similarly, phrase §1.2’s proposed positive answer as supplying a carrier, with transport through the required family still to be justified.
2. Keep the concluding scope consistent with Theorem 5.3: it treats reflexive sheaves with admissible hyperholomorphic connections. The broader references to “torsion-free sheaves” in §7 should either be narrowed or accompanied by the precise double-dual argument and treatment of the quotient. Also retain the distinction between flatness of $\operatorname{End}\mathcal E$ and flatness of $\mathcal E$ itself.
3. Correct the equality at the start of §2.1: the even cohomology includes the odd–odd tensor summand, as the immediately following representation decomposition correctly states. In Proposition 2.2(a), specify the volume normalization or track the nonzero Jacobian factor when transporting integration; orientation preservation alone does not preserve its numerical normalization.
4. Replace “numerical certificates” with “numerical diagnostics” in the §2.3 heading, consistently with its text. Shorten the metadata abstract and move the search-query chronology of §7 to the supplement. The abstract is informative, but the central rigidity result would be easier to identify with fewer secondary exclusions.

The result excludes the specified invariant hyperholomorphic carriers on very general Weil eightfolds. It neither excludes algebraic cycles representing these classes nor settles Markman’s remaining constructions or the supplied Hodge-conjecture solve definition.
