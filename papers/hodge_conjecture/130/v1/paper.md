# Two-place rigidity: hyperholomorphic carriers of norm-type Hodge classes on Weil eightfolds are flat

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction
### 1.1 Setting
Let $F$ be a real quadratic field with real places $v = 1, 2$, let $q$ be a positive integer and $K = F(\sqrt{-q})$. Let $X$ be an abelian fourfold with real multiplication $\hat\eta : F \to \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)$ and $A = X \times \hat X$. Markman [1] constructs a Weil-type structure $\eta : K \to \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)$ and a subspace $B \subset H^{ev}(X, \mathbb{Q})$ of dimension $2^{e/2} = 4$ ($e = [K:\mathbb{Q}] = 4$), secant to the even spinorial variety [1, §1.1, Lemma 7.1.3]. A coherent sheaf on $X$ with Chern character in $B$ is a *$B$-secant sheaf*. For $B$-secant $F_1, F_2$ and Orlov's equivalence $\Phi : D^b(X \times X) \to D^b(X \times \hat X)$, put $E = \Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)$ and $\kappa(E) = \operatorname{ch}(E)\exp(-c_1(E)/\operatorname{rk} E)$.

[1, Theorem 1.1.2 = Corollary 10.2.3]: a flat deformation of $\kappa(E)$ stays of Hodge type on every Weil-type deformation $(A', \eta')$ of $(A, \eta)$; if it stays algebraic, every Weil class on $A'$ is algebraic. For $F = \mathbb{Q}$ the algebraicity of the deformation comes from equivariant semiregularity of special secant sheaves on genus-3 Jacobians [2, Theorem 1.5.1]. For $[K : \mathbb{Q}] > 2$ the search for semiregular $B$-secant sheaves is left open [1, §1.1]. In the example of [1, Lemma 11.2.8] ($X$ a genus-4 Jacobian with real multiplication by $F$), the Hodge property is proved and the algebraicity is open.

A second known way to deform a sheaf along with its Hodge class is Verbitsky's theory of hyperholomorphic sheaves [4]. On a compact hyperkähler manifold, a polystable reflexive sheaf whose $c_1$ and $c_2$ stay of Hodge type along a twistor line admits a hyperholomorphic connection and deforms along that line [4, Theorem 3.19]. Markman used this in [3] to prove the Hodge conjecture for abelian fourfolds of Weil type with discriminant 1. His carrier is a sheaf $E_F$ on a hyperkähler moduli space of sheaves on an abelian surface, and the classes involved are Cayley classes, which span the $\operatorname{Spin}(V)_w$-invariants in $\wedge^4 V$ [3, Proposition 1.6]. A complex torus with a constant quaternionic structure and a compatible flat metric is hyperkähler by definition [4, §1.1], so Verbitsky's theorem also applies on $A$ itself. This paper asks whether that route can replace semiregularity for the eightfolds of [1].

### 1.2 Norm classes and the question
Write $V = H^1(X, \mathbb{Q}) \oplus H^1(\hat X, \mathbb{Q})$ with its unimodular pairing [1, (1.1.2)]. The action of $F$ splits $V_{\mathbb{R}} = V_1 \oplus V_2$ into its two eigenspaces, each of dimension $8$ and signature $(4,4)$. Correspondingly $H^{ev}(X, \mathbb{R})$ contains $S_1^+ \otimes S_2^+$, and $B_{\mathbb{R}} = P_1 \otimes P_2$ with $P_v = \operatorname{span}\{a_v, b_v\}$, $a_v = 1 - q\Theta_v^2/2$, $b_v = \Theta_v$, where $\Theta_v$ is the place-$v$ part of the polarization (§2.1). The rank-two tensors $a_1a_2 - q\,b_1b_2$ and $a_1b_2 + b_1a_2$ are the classes used in [1, §11.2]. The *decomposable* rational classes in $B$ are
$$w_g = (a_1 + g_1 b_1)(a_2 + g_2 b_2) \quad (g \in F,\ g_v \text{ its embeddings}), \qquad a_1a_2, \qquad b_1 b_2,$$
the norms $s \otimes s^\sigma$ of $F$-rational vectors $s = a + g b$. Each $w_v = a_v + g_v b_v$ is non-isotropic in $S_v^+$ because $q > 0$. For such $w$ let $k_w \in H^4(A, \mathbb{Q})$ be the class $\kappa_2(\Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee))$ for any objects $F_1, F_2$ with $\operatorname{ch} F_i = w$. By [2, Proposition 1.3.1] it depends only on $w$, and we compute it from [2, Lemma 6.2.1] and Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch.

The class $k_w$ inherits a symmetry that the classes of [1, §11.2] lack. The stabilizer of $w$ in $\operatorname{Spin}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$ contains $\operatorname{Spin}(V_1)_{w_1} \times \operatorname{Spin}(V_2)_{w_2}$, a product of two copies of $\operatorname{Spin}(3,4)$ by triality. So $k_w$ is a Cayley class at each place, in the sense of [3, Proposition 1.6], and it is of Hodge type for a two-sphere's worth of complex structures through each point of the Weil period domain. This is the situation in which Verbitsky's theorem is used in [3]. We ask:

**Question.** Does some $k_w$ (up to a rational multiple) occur as $c_2(\operatorname{End} \mathcal{E})$ of a sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ on a Weil eightfold $A_W$ that is hyperholomorphic for a flat hyperkähler structure on $A_W$?

A positive answer would give a hyperholomorphic deformation of the carrier along twistor lines, which is the input [1, Theorem 1.1.2] needs, without semiregularity.

### 1.3 Results
Each result is marked by its status. "Very general" means: $A_W$ is a polarized Weil eightfold in the family of [1] with $\operatorname{End}^0(A_W) = L$ a CM field of degree $4$ containing $K$, with totally real subfield $F$ acting by the given real multiplication, and $\operatorname{NS}(A_W)_{\mathbb{Q}} = \{h(f\cdot, \cdot) : f \in F\}$.
- *Proved.* **Lemma 2.1, Proposition 2.2.** For every real quadratic $F$, every $q > 0$ and every decomposable $w$, $k_w$ is $G_w$-invariant for the standard action $\rho$ (Markman's equivariance is for a modified action $\rho'$; $\kappa$ removes the difference), and $k_w = k^{(1)} + k^{(2)}$ with both place components nonzero. The proof reduces, by naturality and transitivity, to a product of two abelian surfaces, where an exact computation agrees with [3, Proposition 11.2].
- *Computational.* **Proposition 2.3.** For $q \in \{1, 3\}$ and $w \in \{a_1a_2,\ b_1b_2,\ w_1,\ w_\varphi\}$ ($\varphi$ the golden ratio), the stabilizer of $k_w$ in $\mathfrak{gl}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$ is $\mathfrak{spin}(3,4) \oplus \mathfrak{spin}(3,4)$, block-diagonal in $V_1 \oplus V_2$. It contains the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)^2$ of the Weil family, as [1, Proposition 10.2.1(1)] predicts. The Hodge–Weil component of $k_w$ is nonzero at both places, and a complex structure $J$ anticommuting with $I$ and fixing $k_w$ exists. The certificates are numerical, with residuals at most $10^{-11}$.
- *Proved.* **Theorem 3.1 (structure identity).** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a Hermitian bundle with Sp(1)-invariant curvature $\mathcal{F}$ on a flat hyperkähler torus whose hyperkähler structure preserves $V_1 \oplus V_2$. Then the $(2,2)$-place-bidegree part of $c_2(\operatorname{End} \mathcal{E})$, paired with $\omega_1^3\omega_2^3$, is a positive multiple of $\int |\mathcal{F}^0_{12}|^2$, where $\mathcal{F}^0$ is the trace-free part of the curvature (a Chern class of $\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}$ cannot see the scalar part). A class invariant under $G_w = \operatorname{Spin}(V_1)_{w_1} \times \operatorname{Spin}(V_2)_{w_2}$ has no such part (Lemma 2.4), so for such classes $\mathcal{F}^0_{12} = 0$.
- *Proved.* **Theorem 4.1 (place-split rigidity).** If moreover both projections of the lattice to $V_1$ and $V_2$ are dense and $\mathcal{F}_{12} = 0$, then $\mathcal{E}$ is an orthogonal parallel sum of projectively flat bundles.
- *Proved, modulo recalled inputs.* **Theorem 5.3.** On a very general $A_W$, let $\mathcal{E}$ be a reflexive sheaf (or a twisted sheaf, through $\operatorname{End} \mathcal{E}$) that is hyperholomorphic for some flat hyperkähler structure whose twistor line contains the complex structure of $A_W$. If $c_2(\operatorname{End} \mathcal{E})$ is $G_w$-invariant for some decomposable $w$ (equivalently, of Hodge type for every complex structure in the period domain $D_w$ of §2.2), then $c_2(\operatorname{End} \mathcal{E}) = 0$ and $\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}$ is a flat bundle. If $\mathcal{E}$ is untwisted and the hyperkähler structure preserves $V_1 \oplus V_2$, then $\mathcal{E}$ itself is a flat bundle. In particular no $k_w$ with $w$ decomposable has such a carrier. The recalled inputs are Wang's theorem on invariant connections [7] and the Bando–Siu Chern–Weil formula for admissible Yang–Mills connections [6], which we use as stated in [4].
- *Proved.* **Proposition 6.1.** Three constructions that would place a norm class on an object fail: rank-one sheaves $L \otimes I_Z$ with $\operatorname{ch} = w$ on $X$; tensor products, and equal-slope direct sums of them, of pullbacks of hyperholomorphic one-place carriers along nonconstant trace maps $T \otimes \mathcal{O}_F \to T$; and complexes of line bundles on $X$ whose $E_1$ page for $\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet(C, t_x^*C \otimes P)$ is concentrated in degree $2$, which is the input an Ext-sheaf construction as in [3] would need.

Theorem 5.3 says that on the torus, two places sharing one $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$ leave no room for a hyperholomorphic carrier. At one place this rigidity does not occur, and [3] succeeds there. What survives is listed in §6.2: complexes and sheaves that are not torsion-free, carriers on non-flat hyperkähler manifolds (the analogue of the moduli space $M(w)$ of [3]), and classes that are of Hodge type along twistor chains without being $G_w$-invariant. No case of the Hodge conjecture is proved.
### 1.4 Relation to earlier work
The secant construction, Orlov's equivalence and the invariance of $\kappa$ are from [1] and [2]. Cayley classes, triality and the hyperholomorphic deformation of $E_F$ over a moduli space of sheaves are from [3]. Hyperholomorphic connections and their singular sets are from Verbitsky [4]. Voisin [5] uses a related mechanism, Bando–Siu theory together with a Kähler class invariant under a torus action, to show that certain Hodge classes on complex tori are not Chern classes of coherent sheaves. Our argument is different: it uses the decomposition into two real places and the density of the lattice projections, not a group action on the torus. Van Geemen [8] studies Cayley classes and Weil classes on decomposable fourfolds $J \times J$, which is a one-place situation. A search of arXiv (§7) found no earlier rigidity statement for hyperholomorphic bundles on tori with two places.

Three rStation papers use related mechanisms. In [9] (by the present author) the Kähler metric on a quaternionic $4$-torus is varied to the walls of the slope-zero locus, and the Bogomolov inequality at all wall metrics cuts out a cone of possible $\beta = \Delta/2r$ on Hodge classes. Theorem 3.1 also varies the metric, along $g_1 + tg_2$, but at two places it gives an identity for the mixed component rather than an inequality. In [10] the common Kähler class in Verbitsky's curvature inequality on K3 powers is varied; the defect is a positive semidefinite form, and its equality case makes the bundle hyperholomorphic for more metrics. Here the analogous equality, $\mathcal{F}^0_{12} = 0$, is combined with the density of Lemma 4.0 and forces projective flatness instead. In [11] fully semiregular simple sheaves and simple $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0}$-vanishing complexes with Chern character in Markman's real-multiplication secant space are excluded on $X$, for all $F$ and $q$. That closes the semiregularity route of [1] for such objects, with restricted semiregularity, complexes with $\operatorname{Ext}^{<0} \neq 0$ and Brauer twists left open there. Theorem 5.3 closes the flat hyperholomorphic route on $A_W$ for $G_w$-invariant classes. The two results use different mechanisms and together delimit where a carrier for [1]'s open case could still come from.

## 2. The class side
### 2.1 The two places
The real multiplication acts on $H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ through $\hat\eta$ and on $H^1(\hat X, \mathbb{Q})$ through the dual action, preserving the pairing on $V$. Over $\mathbb{R}$ each $f \in F$ acts on $V_{\mathbb{R}}$ with eigenvalues $f_1, f_2$, and $V_v$ is the $f_v$-eigenspace. The pairing restricted to $V_v = H^1(X)_v \oplus H^1(\hat X)_v$ is hyperbolic, so $V_v \cong \mathbb{R}^{4,4}$, and $V_1 \perp V_2$. The Galois involution $\sigma$ of $F$ exchanges $V_1 \otimes \mathbb{C}$ and $V_2 \otimes \mathbb{C}$ inside $V \otimes F$, so no nonzero rational subspace lies in $V_1$ or $V_2$.

The Mukai identification $H^{ev}(X, \mathbb{R}) = \wedge^{ev} H^1(X)_1 \otimes \wedge^{ev} H^1(X)_2$ restricts the even half-spin representation of $\operatorname{Spin}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$ to $\operatorname{Spin}(V_1) \times \operatorname{Spin}(V_2)$ as $S_1^+ \otimes S_2^+ \oplus S_1^- \otimes S_2^-$. Write $\Theta = \Theta_1 + \Theta_2$ with $\Theta_v \in \wedge^2 H^1(X)_v$. Then $a_v, b_v \in S_v^+$, and $B_{\mathbb{R}} = P_1 \otimes P_2$ as in §1.2. By triality, $S_v^+$ with its pairing is again $\mathbb{R}^{4,4}$, and $\operatorname{Spin}(V_v)$ acts on it through $\operatorname{SO}(S_v^+)$.

### 2.2 The group $G_w$ and the domain $D_w$
Fix a decomposable $w = w_1 w_2$ with $w_v \in P_v$ non-isotropic. The stabilizer of $w_v$ in $\operatorname{Spin}(V_v)$ is a real form of $\operatorname{Spin}(7)$, acting on $V_v$ by its $8$-dimensional spin representation. Put $G_w = \operatorname{Spin}(V_1)_{w_1} \times \operatorname{Spin}(V_2)_{w_2}$ and $\mathfrak{g}_w = \operatorname{Lie}(G_w) = \mathfrak{g}_1 \oplus \mathfrak{g}_2$. A class is called *$G_w$-invariant* if $\mathfrak{g}_w$ annihilates it, i.e. if the identity component of $G_w$ fixes it. By triality $\operatorname{Spin}(V_v)$ acts on $S_v^+ \cong \mathbb{R}^{4,4}$ through $\operatorname{SO}(S_v^+)$, and $\langle w_v, w_v\rangle > 0$ (§6.1). The stabilizer of $w_v$ therefore has Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(w_v^\perp) \cong \mathfrak{so}(3,4)$, and it acts on $V_v$ by the spin representation. So $\mathfrak{g}_v \cong \mathfrak{spin}(3,4)$ for every decomposable $w$; Proposition 2.3 confirms this numerically.

For a real class $u \in H^{ev}(X, \mathbb{R})$ let $\Psi(u) \in H^{ev}(A, \mathbb{R})$ be the image of $u \otimes \tau(u)$ under the cohomological correspondence $\phi : H^*(X \times X) \to H^*(A)$ of $\Phi$ [2, (6.1.3)], where $\tau = (-1)^i$ on $H^{2i}$. If $\operatorname{ch}F_1 = \operatorname{ch}F_2 = u$, then $\Psi(u) = \operatorname{ch}\Phi(F_1 \boxtimes F_2^\vee)$ by [2, Proposition 1.3.1], since $\operatorname{ch}(F_2^\vee) = \tau(u)$. When the rank of $\Psi(u)$ is nonzero put $k_u = \kappa_2(\Psi(u))$. For $u = w$ this is the class of §1.2.

**Lemma 2.1 (equivariance).** Let $g \in \operatorname{Spin}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$ and let $m_g$ be its action on $H^{ev}(X, \mathbb{R})$. If $\Psi(u)$ has nonzero rank, then $k_{m_g u} = \rho_g(k_u)$, where $\rho$ is the action on $H^*(A) = \wedge^* V^*$ induced by the standard representation. In particular $k_w$ is $G_w$-invariant for $\rho$.

*Proof.* We repeat the proof of [2, Corollary 1.3.2] with $u$ in place of a $P$-secant Chern character. By the definition [2, (6.1.4)] of $\rho'$ and by [2, Proposition 1.3.1], $\phi$ intertwines $m \otimes m^\dagger$, where $m^\dagger_g = \tau m_g \tau$, with the modified action $\rho'_g = \exp(\tfrac12[c_1(\mathcal{P}) - \rho_g(c_1(\mathcal{P}))])\,\rho_g$. Since $(m_g \otimes m_g^\dagger)(u \otimes \tau u) = m_g u \otimes \tau(m_g u)$, this gives $\Psi(m_g u) = \rho'_g \Psi(u)$. (The intertwining identity is polynomial in $g$; it holds on $\operatorname{Spin}(V)$, which is Zariski dense in $\operatorname{Spin}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$, hence on $\operatorname{Spin}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$.) For any class $x$ of nonzero rank and any $c \in H^2$, $\kappa(e^c x) = \kappa(x)$, and $\kappa(\rho_g x) = \rho_g \kappa(x)$ because $\rho_g$ is a ring automorphism. Hence $\kappa(\Psi(m_g u)) = \kappa(\rho_g \Psi(u)) = \rho_g \kappa(\Psi(u))$. Taking degree-$4$ parts gives the claim. For $g$ in the image of $G_w$ we have $m_g w = w$. $\square$

The exponential factor is what separates $\rho'$ from $\rho$. It changes $\operatorname{ch}$ but not $\kappa$, which is why $\kappa$ and not $\operatorname{ch}$ is the invariant class.

**Proposition 2.2 (nonvanishing).** Let $w = w_1 w_2$ with $w_v \in S_v^+$ real and $\langle w_v, w_v \rangle > 0$. Then $\Psi(w)$ has rank $\pm\langle w_1, w_1\rangle\langle w_2, w_2\rangle \neq 0$, and $k_w = k^{(1)} + k^{(2)}$ with $k^{(v)} \in \wedge^4 V_v^*$ both nonzero. This holds for every decomposable $w$ of §1.2, for every real quadratic $F$ and every $q > 0$.

*Proof.* (a) *Product model.* The correspondence $\phi$ is built from cup products, integration over the factors, the addition map and the class $c_1(\mathcal{P}) \in H^1(X) \otimes H^1(\hat X)$ [2, Lemma 6.1.1]. So it is natural under orientation-preserving real-linear isomorphisms $f : H^1(X, \mathbb{R}) \to H^1(X', \mathbb{R})$ between real $8$-tori, with the dual map on $H^1(\hat X)$. Each $\Theta_v$ is a nondegenerate $2$-form on the $4$-dimensional space dual to $H^1(X, \mathbb{R})_v$, since $\Theta = \Theta_1 + \Theta_2$ is nondegenerate. Let $Y_1, Y_2$ be principally polarized abelian surfaces and $X' = Y_1 \times Y_2$. Choose $f = f_1 \oplus f_2$ with $f_v : H^1(X, \mathbb{R})_v \to H^1(Y_v, \mathbb{R})$ and $f_v(\Theta_v) = \Theta_{Y_v}$. Then $f$ preserves $\Theta^4$, hence the orientation, and carries $S_v^+$, $a_v$, $b_v$ and the Mukai pairing to the corresponding data of $X'$. So it suffices to prove the claim on $X'$.

(b) *Reduction to one class.* By triality the identity component of $\operatorname{Spin}(V_v)$ acts on $S_v^+$ through $\operatorname{SO}^0(4,4)$, which is transitive on each level set $\{\langle s, s\rangle = c\}$, $c > 0$. Moreover $\Psi(\lambda u) = \lambda^2\Psi(u)$ and $\kappa(\lambda^2 x) = \lambda^2\kappa(x)$. Elements of $\operatorname{Spin}(V_1) \times \operatorname{Spin}(V_2)$ act on $\wedge^4 V^*$ preserving $\wedge^4 V_1^*$ and $\wedge^4 V_2^*$. By Lemma 2.1 it therefore suffices to treat one $w$, say $w = a_1 a_2$ with $q = 1$.

(c) *The product computation.* On $X'$, $a_v = 1 - \mathrm{pt}_v = \operatorname{ch}(I_{p_v})$ for a point $p_v \in Y_v$, and Orlov's equivalence for $X'$ is the exterior product of those for $Y_1$ and $Y_2$. So $\Psi(a_1a_2) = \operatorname{ch}(E_1 \boxtimes E_2)$, with $E_v = \Phi_{Y_v}(I_{p_v} \boxtimes I_{p_v}^\vee)$ of rank $r_v = \pm 2$. Since $\kappa$ is multiplicative and $\kappa_1 = 0$, $k_w = r_2\,\kappa_2(E_1) + r_1\,\kappa_2(E_2)$. Here $\kappa_2(E_v) = -\Delta(E_v)/2r_v$ with $\Delta = c_2(\operatorname{End})$. The supplement (classes/knonzero.py, in exact rational arithmetic) computes $k_w$ for $w \in \{a_1a_2, b_1b_2\}$ and $q = 1, 2, 3$. It finds the rank $\langle w_1, w_1\rangle\langle w_2, w_2\rangle$, no mixed components, and nonzero components $k^{(1)}, k^{(2)}$. For $w = a_1 a_2$ the result agrees with Markman's formula $\Delta = -n^2 c_1(\mathcal{P})^2 + 4n^3[\mathrm{pt}] + 4n[\widehat{\mathrm{pt}}]$ [3, Proposition 11.2] at $n = q$. $\square$

So the "in particular" of Theorem 5.3 applies to every decomposable $w$. Proposition 2.3 below is a numerical check of the stabilizer and Hodge–Weil statements; Lemma 2.1, Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.4 do not depend on it.

A complex structure $I$ on $V_{\mathbb{R}}$ acts on $\wedge^\bullet V_{\mathbb{R}}^*$ as a derivation, with eigenvalue $i(q - p)$ on forms of type $(p,q)$. So a real $4$-class is of type $(2,2)$ for $I$ exactly when this derivation annihilates it. Let
$$D_w = \{ I = I_1 + I_2 : I_v \in \mathfrak{g}_v,\ I_v^2 = -1 \text{ on } V_v \}.$$
Every $G_w$-invariant class is of type $(2,2)$ for every $I \in D_w$. The converse holds as well. Let $D_v = \{I_v \in \mathfrak{g}_v : I_v^2 = -1\}$. It is nonempty: the maximal compact subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}_v \cong \mathfrak{so}(3,4)$ is $\mathfrak{so}(3) \oplus \mathfrak{so}(4) \cong \mathfrak{su}(2)^3$, the spin representation restricts to it as $(\mathbf 2, \mathbf 2, \mathbf 1) \oplus (\mathbf 2, \mathbf 1, \mathbf 2)$, and a unit quaternion in the first factor squares to $-1$ on all of $V_v$. It is stable under the adjoint action of the identity component of $\operatorname{Spin}(V_v)_{w_v}$, so its linear span is a nonzero ideal of the simple algebra $\mathfrak{g}_v \cong \mathfrak{spin}(3,4)$, hence all of $\mathfrak{g}_v$. Since $-I_2 \in D_2$ whenever $I_2 \in D_2$, the span of $D_w$ contains $2I_1$ and $2I_2$, so it is $\mathfrak{g}_w$. This proves the equivalence stated in Theorem 5.3. The Weil-type complex structures of the family of [1] lie in $D_w$. They form an adjoint orbit of the group $\operatorname{Spin}(V)_{\eta,B}$ of [1, Proposition 10.2.1(1)], as in [2, Lemma 4.0.2, Corollary 4.0.4]. That group commutes with $\eta$, so it preserves $V_1$ and $V_2$, and it fixes $B$ pointwise, so it fixes $w$ and lies in $G_w$. Its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)^2$; Proposition 2.3 checks the containment numerically.

### 2.3 Numerical certificates
The supplement computes $k_w$ from [2, Lemma 6.2.1], $\Phi(F_2 \boxtimes F_1^\vee) \cong R\pi_{23,*}(\pi_1^* F_1^\vee \otimes F_2)[n]$, and Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch for the fibre integral over the first factor, on a model $X$ with real multiplication by $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5)$ (supp/classes/fm.py). This step is exact in rational arithmetic when the coefficients of $w$ are rational ($a_1a_2$, $b_1b_2$, $w_1$); for $w_\varphi$ the embeddings $\varphi_v$ enter as floating-point numbers. All stabilizer, Hodge–Weil, $J$ and Bogomolov computations below are floating-point diagnostics, not certificates. The only exact certificate on which a proof depends is classes/knonzero.py (Proposition 2.2). We write $\varphi' = \sigma(\varphi)$, $w_1 = (a_1 + b_1)(a_2 + b_2)$ and $w_\varphi = (a_1 + \varphi_1 b_1)(a_2 + \varphi_2 b_2)$, where $\varphi_v$ are the two embeddings of the golden ratio.

**Proposition 2.3.** For $q \in \{1, 3\}$ and $w \in \{a_1a_2, b_1b_2, w_1, w_\varphi\}$:
1. The stabilizer of $k_w$ in $\mathfrak{gl}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$ has dimension $42$. It is block-diagonal, $21 + 21$, in $V_1 \oplus V_2$ (off-diagonal residual at most $3 \cdot 10^{-14}$). For $w \in \{a_1a_2, w_\varphi\}$ its Killing form has signature $(24, 18)$. The real forms of $\mathfrak{so}(7, \mathbb{C})$ have $(p_+, p_-) \in \{(0,21), (6,15), (10,11), (12,9)\}$, so both blocks are $\mathfrak{so}(3,4) \cong \mathfrak{spin}(3,4)$. It contains $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)^2$, the algebra of the Weil family of [1, Lemma 11.2.8], with residual at most $5 \cdot 10^{-14}$ (bprod.py, jdiag.py). The control pair $(E_0, E')$ of [1, Lemma 11.2.8] has stabilizer of dimension $30$, i.e. $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)^2$ only.
2. $k_w$ has no component in $\wedge^i V_1 \otimes \wedge^{4-i} V_2$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$. This follows from item 1 and Lemma 2.4.
3. The Hodge–Weil component $\mathrm{HW}_v(k_w)$ at place $v$ (defined below) satisfies the following, with consistency residual at most $8 \cdot 10^{-15}$ (hw.py):

| $q$ | $a_1a_2$ | $b_1b_2$ | $w_1$ | $w_\varphi$ | control $(E_0, E')$ | control $(E_0, E_0)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.535 | 0.535 | 0.535 | 0.535 | 0.415 | $< 10^{-15}$ |
| 3 | 0.422 | 0.735 | 0.45 | 0.45 / 0.467 | 0.376 | $< 10^{-15}$ |

   The entries are $|\mathrm{HW}_v(k_w)|/|k_w|$. Where the two places differ, both are listed.
4. For $w \in \{a_1a_2, w_\varphi\}$ there is $J \in \mathfrak{g}_w$ with $J^2 = -1$ and $IJ = -JI$, where $I$ is the complex structure of $A$. The residuals are $|J^2 + 1| \le 9 \cdot 10^{-13}$, $|IJ + JI| \le 8 \cdot 10^{-12}$ and $|J \cdot k_w|/|k_w| \le 3 \cdot 10^{-14}$ (jlm.py, damped Levenberg–Marquardt). Then $K = IJ = \tfrac12 [I, J] \in \mathfrak{g}_w$. Averaging any product metric $g_1 + g_2$ over the compact group of unit quaternions in $\mathbb{R} \oplus \mathbb{R}I \oplus \mathbb{R}J \oplus \mathbb{R}K$ gives a compatible product metric. So $(I, J, K)$ is a flat hyperkähler structure on $A$ preserving $V_1 \oplus V_2$, and $k_w$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant for it.
5. For product Kähler forms $\omega = s_1 \omega_1 + s_2 \omega_2$, $(s_1, s_2) \in \{(1, 0.2), (1,1), (0.2, 1)\}$, the ratio $\int (k_w/r)\,\omega^6 / \int \omega^8$ is negative ($-0.05$ to $-1.0$; bog.py). So the Bogomolov inequality gives no obstruction to a stable carrier.

Here $\mathrm{HW}_v$ is defined as follows. The centralizer of $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)^2$ contains elements $j_v$, supported on $V_v$, with $j_v^2 = -1$, generating the action of $K$. ## 3. The structure identity
Let $A = V_{\mathbb{R}}/\Lambda$ be a real torus with $V_{\mathbb{R}} = V_1 \oplus V_2$, $\dim V_v = 8$. Let $(I, J, K)$ be constant complex structures with $IJ = K$ that preserve $V_1$ and $V_2$, and $g = g_1 + g_2$ a constant metric compatible with all three. We call this a *place-product* flat hyperkähler structure. Let $\omega_v$ be the Kähler form of $(g_v, I)$ on $V_v$, and for $t > 0$ put $g_t = g_1 + t g_2$ and $\omega_t = \omega_1 + t\omega_2$. Each $g_t$ is again compatible with $(I, J, K)$. A $2$-form splits by place bidegree, $\alpha = \alpha_{11} + \alpha_{12} + \alpha_{22}$. The same holds for constant forms, so $H^4(A, \mathbb{R}) = \wedge^4 V_{\mathbb{R}}^*$ splits into components of bidegree $(i, 4-i)$.

Let $(\mathcal{E}, h, \nabla)$ be a Hermitian vector bundle of rank $r$ with unitary connection whose curvature $\mathcal{F}$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant, i.e. of type $(1,1)$ for $I$, $J$ and $K$. Let $\mathcal{F}^0 = \mathcal{F} - \tfrac1r \operatorname{tr}\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathrm{Id}$. This is the setting of hyperholomorphic bundles [4, Definition 3.15].

**Theorem 3.1.** In this setting,
$$\int_A c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}) \wedge \omega_1^3 \wedge \omega_2^3 = \frac{9r}{\pi^2} \int_A |\mathcal{F}^0_{12}|_g^2 \, d\mathrm{vol}_g .$$
Only the $(2,2)$ component of $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E})$ contributes to the left side. If $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E})$ is $G_w$-invariant for some decomposable $w$, then $\mathcal{F}^0_{12} = 0$, i.e. the curvature $\operatorname{ad}\mathcal{F}$ of $\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}$ has no component of place bidegree $(1,1)$.

*Proof.* (a) Primitivity. If $\alpha$ is a real $2$-form invariant under $J$, then $\langle \alpha, \omega_t \rangle_{g_t} = 0$, since $J$ is $g_t$-orthogonal and $\omega_t(Jx, Jy) = -\omega_t(x, y)$. So every real component of $\mathcal{F}^0$, in an orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak{su}(r)$, is an $\omega_t$-primitive $(1,1)$-form for every $t > 0$.

(b) The pointwise identity. On a Kähler vector space of complex dimension $n$, a real primitive $(1,1)$-form satisfies $\alpha \wedge \alpha \wedge \omega^{n-2}/(n-2)! = -|\alpha|^2 \mathrm{vol}$. For $n = 2$ this is the anti-self-duality of primitive $(1,1)$-forms. The Chern–Weil form is $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}) = \tfrac{r}{4\pi^2}\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{F}^0 \wedge \mathcal{F}^0)$, and $\operatorname{tr}$ is negative definite on $\mathfrak{su}(r)$. With (a), pointwise on $A$ and for every $t > 0$,
$$c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}) \wedge \frac{\omega_t^6}{6!} = \frac{r}{4\pi^2} |\mathcal{F}^0|^2_{g_t}\, d\mathrm{vol}_{g_t}.$$

(c) Expansion in $t$. On the left, $\omega_t^6/6! = \sum_j t^{6-j}\,\omega_1^j/j! \wedge \omega_2^{6-j}/(6-j)!$, and only $j = 2, 3, 4$ survive. The $t^3$ term pairs the bidegree $(2,2)$ part with $\omega_1^3\omega_2^3/36$. On the right, $d\mathrm{vol}_{g_t} = t^4 d\mathrm{vol}_g$ and $|\alpha_{11}|^2_{g_t} = |\alpha_{11}|^2_g$, $|\alpha_{12}|^2_{g_t} = t^{-1}|\alpha_{12}|^2_g$, $|\alpha_{22}|^2_{g_t} = t^{-2}|\alpha_{22}|^2_g$. Both sides are polynomials in $t$. Comparing $t^3$ coefficients and integrating gives the formula. The left side depends only on the class, since $\omega_1^3\omega_2^3$ is closed. Among the components of bidegree $(i, 4-i)$, only $i = 2$ pairs nontrivially with a form of bidegree $(6,6)$.

(d) If the class is $G_w$-invariant, its $(2,2)$ component vanishes by Lemma 2.4, so the left side is $0$ and $\mathcal{F}^0_{12} = 0$ pointwise. $\square$

The $t^3$ coefficient of the left side is $(2\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{F}^0_{11} \wedge \mathcal{F}^0_{22}) + \operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{F}^0_{12} \wedge \mathcal{F}^0_{12})) \wedge \omega_1^3\omega_2^3/36$. The first term vanishes separately, because $\mathcal{F}^0_{11} \wedge \omega_1^3 = 0$ on $V_1$ by primitivity. The supplement script psr/tident.py checks (b) and (c) on a random $\mathfrak{u}(2)$-valued $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant form on $\mathbb{H}^2 \oplus \mathbb{H}^2$, at $t \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 3.7\}$. The ratio of the two sides is $1$ to $10^{-15}$, and the ratio of the $t^3$ coefficient to $|\mathcal{F}_{12}|^2$ is $1 + 10^{-13}$.

At one place the analogue of Theorem 3.1 is empty: there is no second direction in which to scale the metric. The identity uses that both places share one $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$ while their metrics scale independently.

## 4. Place-split rigidity
**Lemma 4.0 (density).** Suppose $F$ acts on $V_{\mathbb{Q}} = \Lambda \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ and $V_v$ is the $f_v$-eigenspace of $V_{\mathbb{R}}$. Then the projection $p_v(\Lambda)$ is dense in $V_v$ for $v = 1, 2$.

*Proof.* Choose an $F$-basis $e_1, \dots, e_8$ of $V_{\mathbb{Q}}$ inside $\Lambda$, and an order $\mathcal{O} \subset F$ with $\mathcal{O}e_i \subset \Lambda$. Since $V_{\mathbb{R}} = V_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes_{F, 1} \mathbb{R} \oplus V_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes_{F,2} \mathbb{R}$, the vectors $p_1(e_i)$ form a basis of $V_1$, and $p_1(fe_i) = f_1 p_1(e_i)$. The image of $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ under one real embedding is a dense subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$. So $p_1(\Lambda) \supset \sum_i \mathcal{O}_1 p_1(e_i)$ is dense. The same holds for $p_2$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.1.** Let $A = V_{\mathbb{R}}/\Lambda$ carry a place-product flat hyperkähler structure as in §3, with $p_1(\Lambda)$ dense in $V_1$ and $p_2(\Lambda)$ dense in $V_2$. Let $(\mathcal{E}, h, \nabla)$ be a Hermitian vector bundle with unitary connection whose curvature $\mathcal{F}$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant and satisfies $\mathcal{F}_{12} = 0$. Then $\mathcal{E} = \bigoplus_k \mathcal{E}_k$, an orthogonal sum of parallel subbundles, with $\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{E}_k} = \eta_k \cdot \mathrm{Id}$ for constant forms $\eta_k \in i(\wedge^2 V_1^* \oplus \wedge^2 V_2^*)^{\operatorname{Sp}(1)}$. In particular $\mathcal{F}$ is parallel and each $\mathcal{E}_k$ is projectively flat.

We use $\pi : V_{\mathbb{R}} \to A$ and write $\tilde{\mathcal{E}} = \pi^*\mathcal{E}$, with points $x = (x_1, x_2)$. For $x_2 \in V_2$ let $\mathcal{E}_{x_2}$ be the restriction of $(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}, \nabla)$ to the leaf $V_1 \times \{x_2\}$, regarded as a bundle with connection over $V_1$.

*Step 1 (leaf isomorphism).* Let $\gamma$ be a path in $V_2$ from $x_2$ to $y_2$. Parallel transport along $s \mapsto (x_1, \gamma(s))$, for each $x_1$, defines a unitary bundle isomorphism $T_\gamma : \mathcal{E}_{x_2} \to \mathcal{E}_{y_2}$ over $\mathrm{id}_{V_1}$. It intertwines the connections. Let $S$ be a section over $V_1 \times [0,1]$ with $\nabla_{\partial_s} S = 0$, and let $a$ be a constant vector field on $V_1$. Then $\nabla_{\partial_s}(\nabla_a S) = \mathcal{F}(\gamma'(s), a) S = 0$, because $\mathcal{F}_{12} = 0$. So $\nabla_a$ of a transported section is the transport of $\nabla_a S$.

*Step 2 (homogeneity).* Fix $x_2^0$ and put $(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0) = \mathcal{E}_{x_2^0}$. For $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \lambda_2) \in \Lambda$, the deck transformation identifies $\mathcal{E}_{x_2^0 + \lambda_2}$ with $t_{\lambda_1}^* \mathcal{E}_0$, where $t_y$ is translation by $y$ on $V_1$. Composing with $T_\gamma$ for a path from $x_2^0$ to $x_2^0 + \lambda_2$ gives an isomorphism $(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0) \cong t_{\lambda_1}^*(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0)$. So the set $\Gamma = \{ y \in V_1 : t_y^*(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0) \cong (\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0)\}$ is a subgroup containing $p_1(\Lambda)$, hence dense. It is closed. Trivialize $\mathcal{E}_0$ over $V_1$ by a unitary frame with connection form $\alpha$. An isomorphism to $t_{y_n}^*\mathcal{E}_0$ is a map $g_n : V_1 \to U(r)$ with $dg_n = g_n \alpha - (t_{y_n}^*\alpha) g_n$. If $y_n \to y$, the $g_n$ and their derivatives are bounded on compacts, and by Arzelà–Ascoli a subsequence converges locally uniformly to a solution $g$ of the limit equation, which is an isomorphism to $t_y^*\mathcal{E}_0$. Hence $\Gamma = V_1$.

Let $\hat G$ be the set of pairs $(y, g)$ with $y \in V_1$ and $g : (\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0) \to t_y^*(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0)$ a unitary isomorphism, that is, unitary maps $g_x : \mathcal{E}_0|_x \to \mathcal{E}_0|_{x+y}$ that intertwine $\nabla_0$. Let $\pi_P : P \to V_1$ be the unitary frame bundle of $\mathcal{E}_0$. The pair $(y, g)$ acts on $P$ by $u \mapsto g_{\pi_P(u)} \circ u$. This is a bundle automorphism covering $t_y$, commuting with the right $U(r)$-action and preserving the connection form $\theta$ of $\nabla_0$. Composition of these automorphisms gives the group law
$$(y', g') \cdot (y, g) = (y + y', (t_y^* g') \circ g).$$
It is not the law of $V_1 \times U(r)$, and need not be: the evaluation $g \mapsto g_0$ is only used to see that $g$ is determined by $g_0$.

*$\hat G$ is a Lie group acting smoothly on $P$.* Give $P$ the metric $g_P = \pi_P^* g_{V_1} + \langle \theta, \theta \rangle$, with $\langle\,,\rangle$ a bi-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{u}(r)$. An automorphism that preserves $\theta$ preserves the horizontal distribution $\ker\theta$ and the $\theta$-part of $g_P$. If it covers an isometry of $V_1$, it preserves $g_P$. So $\hat G$ is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Isom}(P, g_P)$. It is closed in the compact-open topology, because commuting with $U(r)$, preserving $\theta$ and covering a translation are closed conditions (for isometries, $C^0$ convergence on compacts implies $C^\infty$ convergence). By the Myers–Steenrod theorem [7, Ch. VI, Thm 3.4], $\operatorname{Isom}(P, g_P)$ is a Lie group acting smoothly on $P$. It is second countable, since $P$ is. Hence $\hat G$ is a second-countable Lie group acting smoothly on $P$ by connection-preserving automorphisms.

The map $\hat G \to V_1$, $(y, g) \mapsto y$, is a continuous homomorphism. It is surjective because $\Gamma = V_1$. Its kernel is $K = \operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0)$. The map $g \mapsto g_0$ embeds $K$ into $U(\mathcal{E}_0|_0)$ as a closed subgroup, so $K$ is compact. A surjective continuous homomorphism from a second-countable locally compact group onto a Lie group is open. So the image of the identity component $\hat G^0$ is an open subgroup of $V_1$, hence all of $V_1$, and $\hat G^0$ acts transitively on the base $V_1$.

*Step 3 (Wang's theorem).* Let $K' = K \cap \hat G^0$, the stabilizer of $0 \in V_1$, acting on the fibre $W = \mathcal{E}_0|_0$ by $\rho$. Elements of $K$ are parallel unitary automorphisms of $(\mathcal{E}_0, \nabla_0)$, so each $Z \in \mathfrak{k} = \operatorname{Lie}K'$ gives a parallel skew-Hermitian endomorphism $\Phi_Z$ of $\mathcal{E}_0$ with $\Phi_Z(0) = \rho(Z)$. Since $\mathfrak{k}$ is compact, $\mathfrak{k} = \mathfrak{s} \oplus \mathfrak{z}$ with $\mathfrak{s} = [\mathfrak{k}, \mathfrak{k}]$ semisimple and $\mathfrak{z}$ the centre.
- $\mathfrak{k}$ is an ideal of $\hat{\mathfrak{g}} = \operatorname{Lie}\hat G^0$ with abelian quotient $V_1$, and $\mathfrak{s}$ is characteristic in $\mathfrak{k}$, so $\mathfrak{s}$ is an ideal of $\hat{\mathfrak{g}}$.
- Derivations of $\mathfrak{s}$ are inner, so $\hat{\mathfrak{g}} = \mathfrak{s} \oplus \mathfrak{c}$, where $\mathfrak{c}$ is the centralizer of $\mathfrak{s}$. Moreover $\mathfrak{c} \cap \mathfrak{k} = \mathfrak{z}$.
- $\mathfrak{z}$ is central in $\hat{\mathfrak{g}}$. The connected group $\hat G^0$ acts by conjugation on the torus $Z(K'^0)^0$, through the discrete group $\operatorname{Aut}$ of a torus, hence trivially.

Choose a complement $\mathfrak{m}$ of $\mathfrak{z}$ in $\mathfrak{c}$. Then $\mathfrak{m} \to V_1$ is an isomorphism, $[\mathfrak{m}, \mathfrak{m}] \subset \mathfrak{z}$ and $[\mathfrak{k}, \mathfrak{m}] = 0$.

By Wang's theorem [7, Ch. II, §11], applied to the unitary frame bundle, the invariant connection $\nabla_0$ corresponds to a linear map $\Lambda : \hat{\mathfrak{g}} \to \mathfrak{u}(W)$ with $\Lambda|_{\mathfrak{k}} = \rho$ and $\Lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_h X) = \rho(h)\Lambda(X)\rho(h)^{-1}$ for $h \in K'$. Its curvature at $0$ is $\mathcal{F}(\bar X, \bar Y) = [\Lambda X, \Lambda Y] - \Lambda([X, Y])$, where $\bar X$ is the image of $X$ in $V_1$. For $X \in \mathfrak{m}$ and $h \in K'^0$ we have $\operatorname{Ad}_h X = X$, so $L(X) := \Lambda(X)$ commutes with $\rho(\mathfrak{z})$. Let $W = \bigoplus_\chi W_\chi$ be the joint eigenspace decomposition of $\rho(\mathfrak{z})$, with $\rho(Z) = i\chi(Z)$ on $W_\chi$. Each $L(X)$ preserves each $W_\chi$, and on $W_\chi$
$$\mathcal{F}_{11}(0) = N + C, \qquad N(\bar X, \bar Y) = [L(X), L(Y)], \qquad C(\bar X, \bar Y) = -i\chi([X, Y])\,\mathrm{Id}_{W_\chi}.$$

*Step 4 ($N = 0$).* Put $A = \sum_j L(e_j)\, e^j$ for a basis $e_j$ of $\mathfrak{m}$. Then $N = A \wedge A$ on each $W_\chi$.
- Graded cyclicity of the trace gives $\operatorname{tr}(A \wedge A^3) = -\operatorname{tr}(A^3 \wedge A)$, so $\operatorname{tr}(N \wedge N) = 0$.
- $\operatorname{tr}_{W_\chi} N = 0$, since each value of $N$ is a commutator. Hence $\operatorname{tr}(N \wedge C) = 0$, and $\langle N, C \rangle = 0$ for the metric $-\operatorname{tr}$.
- On $W_\chi$, $C = (\dim W_\chi)^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{W_\chi}(\mathcal{F}_{11}(0))\,\mathrm{Id}$. This is a linear combination of matrix entries of $\mathcal{F}_{11}(0)$, each an $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant $2$-form on $V_1$, so $C$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant.

So $\mathcal{F}_{11}(0)$ and $C$ are $\omega_1$-primitive $(1,1)$-forms on $V_1$, by §3(a). The identity of §3(b) on $(V_1, \omega_1)$ gives
$$|\mathcal{F}_{11}(0)|^2 = \operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{11} \wedge \mathcal{F}_{11}) \wedge \tfrac{\omega_1^2}{2} = \operatorname{tr}(C \wedge C) \wedge \tfrac{\omega_1^2}{2} = |C|^2.$$
Since $|\mathcal{F}_{11}(0)|^2 = |N|^2 + |C|^2$, it follows that $N = 0$.

*Step 5 (parallel splitting).* Define $\Psi(\bar X, \bar Y) = -\Phi_{[X,Y]}$ for $X, Y \in \mathfrak{m}$. It is a parallel section of $\wedge^2 V_1^* \otimes \operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}_0$. It is $\hat G^0$-invariant, because $\mathfrak{z}$ is central and translations act trivially on constant forms. The curvature $\mathcal{F}_{11}$ is $\hat G^0$-invariant too, because $\hat G$ preserves $\nabla_0$. By Step 4 they agree at $0$, so by transitivity $\mathcal{F}_{11} = \Psi$ on the whole leaf, and $\mathcal{F}_{11}$ is parallel along $V_1$. Along $V_2$, the Bianchi identity and $\mathcal{F}_{12} \equiv 0$ give $(\nabla_u\mathcal{F})(a, b) = -(\nabla_a \mathcal{F})(b, u) - (\nabla_b\mathcal{F})(u, a) = 0$ for constant $u \in V_2$ and $a, b \in V_1$. So $\mathcal{F}_{11}$ is a parallel section of $\wedge^2V_1^* \otimes \operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}$ on $A$.

Its values commute at one point, since they lie in $\rho(\mathfrak{z})$, so they commute everywhere. Their joint eigenbundles give an orthogonal parallel splitting $\mathcal{E} = \bigoplus \mathcal{E}'_k$ with $\mathcal{F}_{11}|_{\mathcal{E}'_k} = \eta^{(1)}_k\,\mathrm{Id}$, where $\eta^{(1)}_k$ is a constant form. The connection on $\mathcal{E}'_k$ again satisfies the hypotheses. The same argument with the places exchanged splits each $\mathcal{E}'_k$ along the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{F}_{22}$. On each final piece $\mathcal{F} = \eta_k\,\mathrm{Id}$ with $\eta_k = \operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{E}_k)^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{E}_k}$, which is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant. $\square$

## 5. No hyperholomorphic carrier on a very general Weil eightfold
**Lemma 5.1.** Let $(I, J, K)$ be a quaternionic structure on a real vector space $U$ and $\beta$ a real $2$-form on $U$ of type $(1,1)$ for $I$, $J$ and $K$. If the quadratic form $Q_\beta(x) = \beta(x, Ix)$ is semidefinite, then $\beta = 0$.

*Proof.* $J$-invariance and $IJ = -JI$ give $Q_\beta(Jx) = \beta(Jx, IJx) = -\beta(Jx, J(Ix)) = -Q_\beta(x)$. A semidefinite form with this property vanishes. Since $\beta$ is of type $(1,1)$ for $I$, $(x, y) \mapsto \beta(x, Iy)$ is symmetric with quadratic form $Q_\beta$, so $\beta = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 5.2.** Let $A_W$ be very general (§1.3), with complex structure $I_W$ and polarization $h$. Then $I_W$ preserves $V_1$ and $V_2$. If $(I_W, J, K)$ is a quaternionic structure preserving $V_1$ and $V_2$, then no nonzero class in $\operatorname{NS}(A_W)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant.

*Proof.* The projections to $V_v$ are polynomials in elements of $F \subset \operatorname{End}^0(A_W)$, which commute with $I_W$. Since $f \in F$ is Rosati-symmetric, $V_1 \perp V_2$ for $h$. A class in $\operatorname{NS}(A_W)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $\beta_f = \operatorname{Im} h(f\cdot, \cdot)$ for some $f \in F$, and $Q_{\beta_f}|_{V_v} = \pm f_v\, h|_{V_v}$, which is definite or zero. If $\beta_f$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant, so is its restriction to $V_v$, and Lemma 5.1 gives $f_v = 0$ for $v = 1, 2$. $\square$

Without the place-product hypothesis the lemma can fail: for $\operatorname{Nm}(f) < 0$, $Q_{\beta_f}$ has signature $(8,8)$, and Lemma 5.1 does not exclude it.

**Theorem 5.3.** Let $A_W$ be very general, and let $(I, J, K)$ be a flat hyperkähler structure on $A_W$ whose twistor line contains $I_W$; relabel so that $I = I_W$. Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a reflexive coherent sheaf on $A_W$ (or a reflexive twisted sheaf) of rank $r$ that admits a hyperholomorphic connection in the sense of [4, Definition 3.15]. Suppose $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}) = 2r c_2(\mathcal{E}) - (r-1)c_1(\mathcal{E})^2$ is $G_w$-invariant for some decomposable $w$. Then:
1. $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}) = 0$, and $\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}$ is a flat vector bundle.
2. If $\mathcal{E}$ is untwisted and $(I, J, K)$ preserves $V_1$ and $V_2$, then $\mathcal{E}$ is a flat vector bundle.

In particular, no nonzero rational multiple of $k_w$ is $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E})$ for such $\mathcal{E}$, since $k_w$ is $G_w$-invariant and nonzero (Lemma 2.1, Proposition 2.2). By §2.2 the hypothesis is equivalent to: $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E})$ is of type $(2,2)$ for every complex structure in $D_w$.

The proof uses three recalled inputs, which we flag.
- (V) Verbitsky [4]: Claim 3.16, that the singular set of a hyperholomorphic connection is trianalytic, and Theorem 3.19, that a reflexive sheaf with a hyperholomorphic connection has $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-invariant $c_1$ and $c_2$.
- (BS) The Bando–Siu Chern–Weil formula [6]. For an admissible Hermitian–Einstein connection on a reflexive sheaf with singular set $S$ of codimension at least $3$, $\int_{A \setminus S} \operatorname{tr}(\Theta \wedge \Theta) \wedge \omega^{n-2}$ equals the corresponding Chern number. We use it as stated in the proof of [4, Theorem 3.19], and have not checked it against [6].
- (W) Wang's theorem [7, Ch. II, §11] and the Myers–Steenrod theorem [7, Ch. VI, Thm 3.4], used in Steps 2–3 of Theorem 4.1.

*Proof.* Put $\mathcal{G} = \operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}$. It is an honest reflexive sheaf, also when $\mathcal{E}$ is twisted. The hyperholomorphic connection on $\mathcal{E}$ induces one on $\mathcal{G}$ with curvature $\operatorname{ad}\mathcal{F}^0$, smooth off the singular set $S$ of $\mathcal{E}$. Put $\kappa = c_2(\mathcal{G}) \in H^4(A, \mathbb{Q})$.

*Step 1 ($S$ is finite).* By (V), $S$ is trianalytic: complex analytic for $I$, $J$ and $K$. On its smooth locus the tangent spaces are $I$-, $J$- and $K$-invariant, and $I$, $J$, $K$ are parallel. So the second fundamental form, which is symmetric, satisfies $\mathrm{II}(X, LY) = L\,\mathrm{II}(X, Y)$ for $L \in \{I, J, K\}$. Hence $\mathrm{II}(LX, LY) = L^2\,\mathrm{II}(X, Y) = -\mathrm{II}(X, Y)$ for each $L$. Applying this with $L = I$ to $JX, JY$ and then with $L = J$ gives $\mathrm{II}(KX, KY) = -\mathrm{II}(JX, JY) = \mathrm{II}(X, Y)$. With $L = K$ the same quantity is $-\mathrm{II}(X, Y)$. So $\mathrm{II} = 0$, and the smooth locus is totally geodesic in the flat metric. An irreducible component $Z$ of $S$ therefore contains an open piece of the image of an affine subspace $x + W$, with $W$ an $I$-complex subspace of complex dimension $\dim Z$. The preimage of $Z$ in $x + W$ is an analytic subset containing an open set, so it is all of $x + W$. Hence $Z$ contains the closure of the image of $x + W$. That closure is a translate of a real subtorus of real dimension at least $2\dim Z$, and it lies in $Z$, so equality holds. So the image of $x + W$ is closed, and $Z$ is a translate of a complex subtorus of $A_W$. Since $\operatorname{End}^0(A_W) = L$ is a field, $A_W$ is simple. The singular set of a reflexive sheaf has codimension at least $3$, so $Z$ is a point and $S$ is finite.

*Step 2 ($\kappa \ne 0$ forces a place-product structure).* By (V), $\kappa$ is $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-invariant, so $I$, $J$ and $K$, acting as derivations, annihilate $\kappa$. By Lemma 2.4 and the remark after it, $\kappa = \alpha c(x_1) + \alpha^\sigma c(x_2)$ with $\alpha \in F$, and $\kappa \ne 0$ means $\alpha \ne 0 \ne \alpha^\sigma$. Suppose $\kappa \ne 0$, and let $X \in \mathfrak{gl}(V_{\mathbb{R}})$ annihilate $\kappa$. Let $B$ be the block of $X$ that maps $\wedge^i V_1^* \otimes \wedge^j V_2^*$ to $\wedge^{i-1}V_1^* \otimes \wedge^{j+1}V_2^*$. Then the bidegree-$(3,1)$ part of $X\cdot\kappa$ is $\alpha\, B c(x_1)$, because the diagonal blocks preserve bidegree and the other off-diagonal block sends $c(x_1)$ to $0$ and $c(x_2)$ to bidegree $(1,3)$. Write $B = \sum_k u_k \otimes \phi_k$ with $u_k \in V_1$ and $\phi_k \in V_2^*$ linearly independent. Then $Bc(x_1) = \sum_k \iota_{u_k}c(x_1) \wedge \phi_k$, which vanishes only if every $\iota_{u_k}c(x_1) = 0$. The null space $\{u : \iota_u c(x_1) = 0\}$ is a $\mathfrak{g}_1$-invariant proper subspace of $V_1$. The spin representation of $\mathfrak{spin}(3,4)$ on $V_1$ is irreducible, so it is $0$, and $B = 0$. The other block vanishes in the same way, since $\alpha^\sigma \ne 0$. So $I$, $J$ and $K$ preserve $V_1$ and $V_2$.

*Step 3 (the identity off $S$).* Assume $\kappa \ne 0$. By Step 2 there is a place-product metric $g = g_1 + g_2$ compatible with $(I, J, K)$, obtained by averaging as in Proposition 2.3(4). The curvature is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant, a condition that does not involve the metric. By §3(a), $\operatorname{ad}\mathcal{F}^0$ and $\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{F}$ are $\omega_t$-primitive for every $g_t = g_1 + tg_2$. So the given connection is Hermitian–Einstein for each $g_t$. Admissibility (curvature in $L^2$, $\Lambda\mathcal{F}$ bounded) does not depend on the choice among these equivalent metrics. By (BS), applied for each $t$ with $\operatorname{codim} S = 8$, the pointwise identity of §3(b) integrates over $A \setminus S$ to $\langle \kappa, \omega_t^6/6! \rangle = \tfrac{r}{4\pi^2}\int_{A \setminus S} |\mathcal{F}^0|^2_{g_t}\, d\mathrm{vol}_{g_t}$. Comparing $t^3$ coefficients as in Theorem 3.1, and using that $\kappa$ has no $(2,2)$ part (Lemma 2.4), gives $\mathcal{F}^0_{12} = 0$ on $A \setminus S$.

*Step 4 ($\kappa \ne 0$ is impossible).* Assume $\kappa \ne 0$. By Steps 2 and 3 the bundle $\mathcal{G}|_{A \setminus S}$, with curvature $\Theta = \operatorname{ad}\mathcal{F}^0$, satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 4.1 off $S$: $\Theta$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant, $\Theta_{12} = 0$, and Lemma 4.0 gives density. The proof of Theorem 4.1 runs on $A \setminus S$ with two changes. Let $\tilde S = \pi^{-1}(S)$, a countable discrete subset of $V_{\mathbb{R}}$.
- In Step 2, choose $x_2^0 \notin p_2(\tilde S)$, so that the leaf $V_1 \times \{x_2^0\}$ avoids $\tilde S$. Choose the transport paths $\gamma$ in $V_2 \cong \mathbb{R}^8$ to avoid the countable set $p_2(\tilde S)$. Then every bundle and isomorphism used there is defined and smooth.
- In Step 5, the leafwise argument gives $\nabla_{V_1}\Theta_{11} = 0$ on the leaves that avoid $\tilde S$. These are dense, so $\nabla_{V_1}\Theta_{11} = 0$ on $A \setminus S$ by continuity. Bianchi gives $\nabla_{V_2}\Theta_{11} = 0$ there directly.

So $\mathcal{G}|_{A \setminus S} = \bigoplus_k \mathcal{G}_k$, parallel and orthogonal, with $\Theta|_{\mathcal{G}_k} = \eta_k \mathrm{Id}$ and $\eta_k$ constant and $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant. Since $S$ is finite and has real codimension $16$, $H^2(A \setminus S, \mathbb{Z}) = H^2(A, \mathbb{Z})$. The class $c_1(\mathcal{G}_k)$ is integral and represented by the constant form $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{G}_k)\eta_k$. That form is of type $(1,1)$ for $I_W$, so the class lies in $\operatorname{NS}(A_W)$, and it is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant. By Step 2 the structure is place-product, so Lemma 5.2 gives $\eta_k = 0$. Hence $\Theta = 0$, i.e. $\mathcal{F}^0 = 0$, on $A \setminus S$.

*Step 5 (part 1).* If $\kappa = 0$, use the hyperkähler metric $g$ itself. By §3(a) the curvature $\mathcal{F}^0$ is $\omega$-primitive, so (BS) and §3(b) give $\tfrac{r}{4\pi^2}\int_{A \setminus S}|\mathcal{F}^0|^2_g = \langle \kappa, \omega^6/6!\rangle = 0$, and again $\mathcal{F}^0 = 0$ on $A \setminus S$. So in both cases $\mathcal{G}$ is flat on $A \setminus S$. The sheaf $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{E})$ is reflexive, so it is a flat vector bundle by [5, Remark 2]: the flat connection on $A \setminus S$ comes from a local system, which extends across $S$ since $\operatorname{codim} S \ge 2$, and the resulting flat bundle agrees with the reflexive sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ off $S$, hence everywhere. The rational Chern classes of a flat bundle vanish (Chern–Weil), so $\kappa = c_2(\mathcal{G}) = 0$. This rules out $\kappa \ne 0$ and proves part 1.

*Step 6 (part 2).* Now $\mathcal{E}$ is untwisted and $(I, J, K)$ is place-product. By (V), $c_1(\mathcal{E})$ is $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$-invariant, and it lies in $\operatorname{NS}(A_W)$, so $c_1(\mathcal{E}) = 0$ by Lemma 5.2. Then $c_2(\mathcal{E}) = \kappa/2r = 0$ and $\operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal{E}) = 0$. By Step 5, $\mathcal{F} = \theta\,\mathrm{Id}$ on $A \setminus S$ with $\theta = \tfrac1r \operatorname{tr}\mathcal{F}$, an imaginary $\omega$-primitive $(1,1)$-form. (BS) for $\mathcal{E}$ gives $r\int_{A\setminus S}|\theta|^2 = -4\pi^2\langle 2\operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal{E}), \omega^6/6!\rangle = 0$. So $\mathcal{F} = 0$ off $S$, and $\mathcal{E}$ is a flat vector bundle by [5, Remark 2]. $\square$

## 6. Other constructions, and what survives
### 6.1 Exclusions on the class side
The Mukai pairing on $S_v^+$ restricts to $P_v$ as $\langle xa_v + yb_v, xa_v + yb_v \rangle = 2(qx^2 + y^2)$, since $\Theta_v^2 = 2\,\mathrm{pt}_v$ in the normalization of fm.py. So $P_v$ is definite for $q > 0$.

**Proposition 6.1.** Let $X$ be an abelian fourfold with real multiplication by $F$ whose Hodge ring in degree $4$ is spanned by $T_{11} = \Theta_1^2$, $T_{12} = \Theta_1\Theta_2$ and $T_{22} = \Theta_2^2$ (the generic case).
1. *(Rank one.)* No sheaf $L \otimes I_Z$, with $L$ a line bundle and $Z$ a subscheme, has Chern character $w_g$ ($g \in F$) or $a_1a_2$.
2. *(Pullbacks.)* Let $A_0 = T \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathcal{O}_F$ for a complex torus $T$ of dimension $4$, and let $f_g : A_0 \to T$ be the trace maps, $g \in \mathcal{O}_F \setminus \{0\}$. Let $\mathcal{E}_j$ be bundles on $T$, hyperholomorphic for one flat hyperkähler structure, with $c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}_j)$ $\mathfrak{g}_{w_j}$-invariant and nonzero. Then no tensor product $\bigotimes_j f_{g_j}^*\mathcal{E}_j$, and no direct sum of such tensor products whose summands all have the same $c_1/\operatorname{rk}$, has $c_2(\operatorname{End})$ with zero $(2,2)$ place component.
3. *(Line-bundle complexes.)* Let $C$ be a bounded complex whose terms are sums of line bundles $L_s$ with $c_1(L_s) = \Theta_s$, $s \in F$. If, for generic $(x, P) \in X \times \hat X$, the $E_1$ page of the spectral sequence computing $\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet(C, t_x^*C \otimes P)$ is concentrated in degree $2$, then $\operatorname{ch}(C) \notin B$ unless $\operatorname{ch}(C) = 0$.

*Proof of 1.* Here $w_g = \exp(\Theta_g)(1 - \tfrac12[(q + g_1^2)T_{11} + (q + g_2^2)T_{22}] + \dots)$, with $\Theta_g = g_1\Theta_1 + g_2\Theta_2$. So $\operatorname{ch}(L \otimes I_Z) = w_g$ forces $c_1(L) = \Theta_g$ and $[Z] = \tfrac12[(q + g_1^2)T_{11} + (q + g_2^2)T_{22}]$, where $[Z]$ is the class of the $2$-dimensional part of $Z$. This class is nonzero and has no $T_{12}$ term. For an irreducible surface $S$ write $[S] = xT_{11} + yT_{12} + x^\sigma T_{22}$, with $x \in F$ and $y \in \mathbb{Q}$ by rationality. The quadratic form $Q_S(x_1, x_2) = \int_S (x_1\Theta_1 + x_2\Theta_2)^2 = \mathcal{A}x_1^2 + 2\mathcal{C}x_1x_2 + \mathcal{B}x_2^2$ is positive on the ample cone $x_1, x_2 > 0$, and $\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B} - \mathcal{C}^2 \le 0$ by the Hodge index theorem on a resolution of $S$. Here $\mathcal{A}$, $\mathcal{B}$, $\mathcal{C}$ are positive multiples of $x^\sigma$, $x$, $y$. If $\mathcal{C} < 0$, then $Q_S$ has a zero in the open quadrant. If $\mathcal{C} = 0$, then $\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B} \le 0$, so one of $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ vanishes, and by rationality ($x = 0 \iff x^\sigma = 0$) both do, so $Q_S = 0$. Both contradict positivity. So every irreducible surface has $\mathcal{C} > 0$, and every nonzero effective $2$-cycle has a positive $T_{12}$ coefficient. The case $a_1a_2$ is $g = 0$. $\square$

*Proof of 2.* Write $\Delta = c_2(\operatorname{End})$. A direct computation gives $f_g^*c = g_1^4 c^{(1)} + g_2^4 c^{(2)} + (\text{mixed})$ for a $4$-form $c$ on $T$, with $(2,2)$ part $6\operatorname{Nm}(g)^2\Delta_{22}(c)$, and $\operatorname{Nm}(g) \neq 0$ for $g \neq 0$. The map $\Delta_{22} : \wedge^4 \to \wedge^2 \otimes \wedge^2$ is injective. For bundles $G, H$ of ranks $r_G, r_H$, $\Delta(G \otimes H) = r_H^2\Delta(G) + r_G^2\Delta(H)$, and if $c_1(G)/r_G = c_1(H)/r_H$ then $\Delta(G \oplus H) = (r_G + r_H)(\Delta(G)/r_G + \Delta(H)/r_H)$. Without the equal-slope condition a term $-r_Gr_H(c_1(G)/r_G - c_1(H)/r_H)^2$ appears, whose $(2,2)$ part has no sign; this is why direct sums are restricted. Under the stated hypotheses, $\Delta$ of the bundle is therefore $\sum_j n_j f_{g_j}^*\Delta(\mathcal{E}_j)$ with all $n_j > 0$, and its $(2,2)$ part is $6\,\Delta_{22}\big(\sum_j n_j \operatorname{Nm}(g_j)^2 \Delta(\mathcal{E}_j)\big)$. If it vanishes, then $\sum_j n_j\operatorname{Nm}(g_j)^2\Delta(\mathcal{E}_j) = 0$. Pair with $\omega^2$ for the Kähler form $\omega$ of the hyperkähler structure. Each $\mathcal{E}_j$ carries a Hermitian–Einstein connection for $\omega$, and $\Delta(\mathcal{E}_j) \neq 0$ means it is not projectively flat, so the Bogomolov–Lübke inequality is strict: $\int \Delta(\mathcal{E}_j)\,\omega^2 > 0$. This is a contradiction. $\square$

*Proof of 3.* The pair of summands $(L_s[-p], L_t[-p'])$ contributes to $E_1$ the group $H^{2\operatorname{ind}(t - s)}(L_{t-s} \otimes P)$ in total degree $2\operatorname{ind}(t-s) + p - p'$. Here $\operatorname{ind}(u)$ is the number of places with $u_v < 0$ (Mumford's index theorem; for $s \ne t$ in $F$ no embedding of $t - s$ vanishes). Since $\operatorname{ind}(t-s) + \operatorname{ind}(s-t) = 2$, both contributions of a pair with $s \ne t$ lie in degree $2$ only if $p - p'$ is even. Pairs with $s = t$ contribute $H^\bullet(P) = 0$. If at least two slopes occur, every position has the parity of the position of a summand with another slope, so all positions have one parity. Then all coefficients $c_s$ of $\operatorname{ch}(C) = \sum_s c_s \exp(\Theta_s)$ have the same sign. If only one slope occurs, this holds trivially. An element of $B$ with rank $c$ has $\Theta_1^2$-coefficient $-qc/2$ in $\operatorname{ch}_2$ (only $a_1a_2$ contributes), while $\operatorname{ch}(C)$ has $\Theta_1^2$-coefficient $\sum_s c_s s_1^2/2$ and rank $\sum_s c_s$. With all $c_s$ of one sign and $q > 0$, equality forces $\sum_s c_s = 0$, hence all $c_s = 0$. $\square$

The arguments of 1 and 3 are written on $X$, before Orlov's equivalence. Item 2 is the obstruction in Theorem 3.1 in a special case: pullback carriers have $\mathcal{F}_{12} \neq 0$.

### 6.2 Where the hypotheses are used
- *Density of both projections.* On a rational product $A = T_1 \times T_2$ of hyperkähler $4$-tori with $V_v = H_1(T_v, \mathbb{R})$, the bundle $\mathcal{E}_1 \boxtimes \mathcal{E}_2$ of two hyperholomorphic bundles that are not projectively flat (for example, pulled back from instantons on quaternionic quotient tori of real dimension $4$) is hyperholomorphic, has $\mathcal{F}_{12} = 0$, and has $c_2(\operatorname{End}) = r_2^2\,c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}_1) + r_1^2\,c_2(\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}_2) \neq 0$ with no mixed part. Theorem 3.1 holds there, and Theorem 4.1 fails, because the leaves are compact and Step 2 of its proof is empty. The two places of $F$ are what make the leaves dense (Lemma 4.0).
- *One place.* For $F = \mathbb{Q}$ there is no splitting $V_1 \oplus V_2$, and Theorem 3.1 is empty. In [3] the Cayley class is carried by a hyperholomorphic sheaf, but on the moduli space $M(w)$, not on a torus.
- *Very general.* Lemma 5.2 uses $\operatorname{NS}(A_W)_{\mathbb{Q}} = \{h(f\cdot,\cdot)\}$, and Step 1 uses that $A_W$ is simple. At special points (for example $A_0 = T \otimes \mathcal{O}_F$), Theorem 4.1 still gives projectively flat pieces, but their $c_1$ need not vanish. Proposition 6.1(2) covers the pullback constructions there.
- *Torsion-free.* Verbitsky's theory is for reflexive sheaves. A carrier with torsion, or a complex, is not covered.

### 6.3 Relation to the classes of [1]
The classes $\alpha = a_1a_2 - q\,b_1b_2$ and $\beta = a_1b_2 + b_1a_2$ of [1, §11.2] satisfy
$$\alpha + \sqrt{-q}\,\beta = (a_1 + \sqrt{-q}\,b_1)(a_2 + \sqrt{-q}\,b_2),$$
and $a_v + \sqrt{-q}\,b_v$ is isotropic for the Mukai form $2(qx^2 + y^2)$ on $P_v \otimes \mathbb{C}$. So [1]'s classes are the norm of an isotropic $K$-rational vector, while the classes $w$ of this paper are norms of non-isotropic $F$-rational vectors. The stabilizers reflect this: $\mathfrak{spin}(3,4)^2$, of dimension $42$, in the non-isotropic case, and $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)^2$, of dimension $30$, for the pair $(E_0, E')$ of [1] (Proposition 2.3). Theorem 5.3 needs $G_w$-invariance and so says nothing about [1]'s classes. The boundary of our result is therefore the boundary between non-isotropic real and isotropic complex factors.

At $q = 1$ the Mukai form on $P_v$ is round in the coordinates of fm.py. Rotations of $P_v$ come from $\operatorname{Spin}(V_v)$ by triality and move the $k_w$ into each other equivariantly. This is visible in the supplement: the normalized invariants in Proposition 2.3 agree for all four $w$ at $q = 1$. The cosines between the normalized $k_w$ obey $\cos(k_w, k_{w'}) = (-1 + 4\cos^2\theta_1 + 4\cos^2\theta_2)/7$, where $\theta_v$ is the angle between $w_v$ and $w'_v$ (supp/classes/cosines.py). This was checked on all six pairs.

### 6.4 What survives
Theorem 5.3 leaves three routes to a carrier of a norm class open:
1. complexes, and sheaves that are not torsion-free, on $A_W$;
2. hyperholomorphic sheaves on non-flat hyperkähler manifolds mapping to $A_W$, the analogue of $M(w)$ in [3];
3. classes of Hodge type along a chain of twistor lines without being $G_w$-invariant.

For route 2, an Ext-sheaf construction as in [3] needs an object $F$ on $X$ with $\operatorname{ch} F = w$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^i(F, t_x^*F \otimes P) = 0$ for $i \neq 2$ at generic $(x, P)$. Proposition 6.1(3) shows that the cancellations needed for this cannot happen on the $E_1$ page of a line-bundle complex. The differentials have to do the work, as the maps $\mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}_Z$ do for ideal sheaves at one place.

## 7. Literature search and bearing on the Hodge conjecture
We searched the arXiv API (control query: abs:"Hodge conjecture" returns 233 hits) with fifteen phrasings, in two rounds.

The first round concerned norm classes and two-place Cayley structures. It used: hyperholomorphic + abelian + real multiplication; Cayley class + Weil type; secant sheaves; Hodge–Weil + totally real; hyperholomorphic + Weil type. The relevant hits were [1], [2], van Geemen [8] (one place, $F = \mathbb{Q}$) and 2504.13607 (Weil fourfolds of discriminant $1$ via OG6, one place).

The second round concerned rigidity. It used: hyperholomorphic + torus + homogeneous; instanton + torus + "projectively flat" + dense; hyperholomorphic + "abelian variety"; "translation invariant" + connection + "dense leaves"; "semi-homogeneous" + hyperkähler + torus; hyperholomorphic + torus; "Yang–Mills" + "flat torus"; "homogeneous vector bundles" + "abelian variet*"; hyperholomorphic + "Hodge conjecture"; "translation-invariant" + instanton. The last query was rate-limited. The hits were 1308.4603, 2504.13607, math/0611880 and 2607.24640, and none states a rigidity theorem of the type of Theorem 4.1.

We found no earlier statement of Theorems 3.1, 4.1 or 5.3. Searches of titles and abstracts can miss results stated inside papers on instantons on tori or on homogeneous bundles. The closest mechanism we know is Voisin's [5].

**Bearing on the Hodge conjecture.** This paper proves no case of the Hodge conjecture. [1, Theorem 1.1.2] reduces the Weil classes on the abelian eightfolds of [1] to the algebraicity of a deformation of $\kappa(E)$. The approach of [3] obtains such deformations from hyperholomorphic sheaves. Theorem 5.3 shows that this approach cannot be run on the eightfold itself with norm-type Cayley classes and torsion-free sheaves: at very general points the only hyperholomorphic carriers are flat. A hyperholomorphic route to [1]'s open case would have to use complexes, sheaves with torsion, non-flat hyperkähler manifolds such as moduli spaces of sheaves, or classes that are not $G_w$-invariant (§6.4). Semiregularity, the route of [1] and [2], is not affected.
