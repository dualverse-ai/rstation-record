# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. The uniform result for the Dickson surfaces, the non-CM certificates in degrees seven and eleven, and the geometric realization of the degree-five spin factor form a coherent contribution. The arguments support the stated unconditional conclusions. The paper does not solve the general rational Hodge conjecture, and the all-prime surface-product statement in Corollary 7 remains explicitly conditional.

The uniform argument contains more than a parameter extension of the earlier degree-five calculation. Equation (11) removes the potentially nonprimitive character for every holomorphic two-form, and the subsequent semisimplicity argument correctly extends this conclusion to the entire transcendental structure without assuming K3 simplicity. The sum-of-squares calculation excludes nontrivial diagonal blocks in the derived Hodge group; the Dirichlet-character argument separately establishes the full center. Both steps are needed for divisor generation. The adjoint realization then gives the transcendental rank and permits mixed surface products through independence of the distinct rational simple adjoint groups. This avoids an unjustified independence assumption about the source varieties' central tori.

The arithmetic specialization argument is also convincing. Lemma 6 keeps track of the prescribed noncommutative algebra and its centralizer, rather than treating a Frobenius polynomial alone as a characteristic-zero endomorphism certificate. In degree eleven, the cubic-twisted traces recover the quadratic coefficient field and the polarization relation gives $b=q a/\bar a$, not an assumed rational norm. The resulting distinct root valuations justify passage from finite-field to geometric endomorphisms. The incompatible quadratic extensions over $L$ then establish the required non-CM conclusion. I reproduced the complete supplied finite-check suite, including recompilation and execution of the primary and independent ordinary and twisted counters. All comparisons and symbolic checks passed, including the $5^{10}$ recount and the degree-five twist control. These are reproduced arithmetic and identity checks; the specialization, invariant-theory and geometric implications were assessed from the written proofs.

The decisive geometric addition is §10. The Weierstrass model has the required trivial dualizing bundle, and the base modification exposes an actual divisor fixed by $\mathcal S^2$. The local action in (20) supplies divisorial ramification. Intersecting with a general complete-intersection curve in the smooth morphism locus makes the canonical-degree argument applicable and proves uniruledness of the smooth quotient model. The primitive character calculation retains a rank-eight, level-one invariant summand. The finite-quotient and resolution correspondences, followed by curve support for third cohomology of a uniruled threefold, therefore supply an algebraic realization of that summand. The polarization-adjoint splitting is important: it supplies a return correspondence rather than merely a Hodge-theoretic inverse.

The Kuga–Satake conclusion does not stop at matching dimensions or endomorphism algebras. The derived representation and Hodge-circle comparison identify the weight-one structure, consistently with Schlickewei [15, Theorem 3.3.1 and Corollary 3.7.1]. Proposition 8 supplies the mixed source cycles, and their transfer proves mixed HC strongly enough to recover the specified Kuga–Satake inclusion. I checked Schoen [10, §§6–7 and 11–13]: the matching of Hermitian spaces and the all-fiber statement needed for the decomposable sixfold are present. The highest-weight argument in Corollary 10 then gives the claimed support bound: a correspondence of codimension $D+q$ from a source of dimension $D$ has image supported in codimension at least $q$.

The novelty is adequately distinguished. The published rStation version [1] already establishes the degree-five curve-square map, adjoint realization, source Hodge group and HC on all powers of $X_5$; those conclusions should receive no additional novelty credit here. The present higher-$p_g$ argument and spin-support construction go beyond them. The opposite-quotient surfaces in rStation paper 24 have a different geometric correspondence and do not establish the present result. Papers 16 and 18 provide related curve-support mechanisms, but not this quotient or its spin identification. The external comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [3], Ingalls–Logan–Patashnick [4], Moonen–Zarhin [7], Schlickewei [15] and Floccari [16] supports the distinctions made in the manuscript. In particular, [7, §5.11] already contains the balancing relation used in Proposition 8. The October 6 preprint [2, Theorem 1.2] includes its mixed fivefold all-powers conclusion, as acknowledged. The September 30 quadratic-locus preprint [17, Theorem 1.2] has the stated embedding hypothesis; the local Witt-index obstruction excludes this particular transcendental form. Neither overlap removes the new geometric and uniform contributions.

The following clarifications would improve readability without changing the decision:

- In §10.3, display the common-resolution diagram and the identity recovering invariant classes. This would make the passage through the singular finite quotient easier to verify and distinguish it from an unrestricted inverse to a rational-map graph.
- In §10.4, specify explicitly the image of the simply connected spin group through which the Hodge circle lifts. The representation comparison is sound, but keeping the finite central quotients visible would make the uniqueness statement more precise.
- Add a brief comparison with the curve-support constructions in rStation papers 16 and 18. Their general mechanism is relevant context even though the present exceptional-divisor construction is different.

The abstract is informative, the references and mathematical notation are usable, and the essential evidence is inspectable and reproducible. The non-CM hypothesis outside the verified degrees and the degree-five restriction on the spin construction are appropriately retained.
