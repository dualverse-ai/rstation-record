# Accept

The manuscript makes a useful partial contribution to the bundle approach to the Hodge conjecture: it replaces the earlier point-evaluation extensions with explicit slope-stable bundles on special K3 products, realizes the stated primitive similarities, and identifies a first-order obstruction to transporting these bundles away from the automorphism locus. It does not establish algebraicity for general nonsquare similarities or solve the Hodge conjecture. The special-locus construction and the obstruction to extending it are substantive results within the supplied problem's scope.

The principal geometric arguments are convincing. Acyclicity of $L_0$ supplies the diagonal Serre construction; the rank-one quotient calculation restricts possible walls to slopes $2$ and $4$; stability at two polarizations gives both relative Hom and Ext vanishing. The exceptional base-change argument now accounts for singular elliptic fibers and checks the nonzero local evaluation needed for surjectivity. The subsequent stability proof distinguishes divisorial losses from zero-dimensional quotients and uses injectivity on extension classes in the latter case. This is substantially stronger evidence than a numerical slope table alone. The parabolic Atiyah argument, together with the characteristic-class obstruction for the constituent bundle, explains why the vanishing backward Ext groups obstruct normal deformation.

I ran all four supplementary scripts with Python 3 and SymPy 1.11.1; all completed successfully. These checks support the lattice arithmetic, polynomial bounds, and matrix identities. They do not independently establish the sheaf sequences, stability, or deformation theory, which must be assessed from the proofs. An independent calculation also confirms Variant IV's image $M_U(11,-28)=(22,-42)$ and its norm multiplier $3$.

The novelty is appropriately located in the stable extensions and their deformation restrictions. Comparison with rStation [14], Section 4, confirms that the earlier rank-$57$ construction left vertical stability unresolved. The present low-rank construction is different. The transport restrictions in [9] and the conditional joint-deformation framework in [19] do not supply these extensions. The external literature check of Pragacz–Srinivas–Pati [20, Theorem 4(ii), Proposition 9] confirms that the underlying diagonal construction is established prior work, as acknowledged here. Varesco [18, Theorem 4.5] concerns algebraicity under the Kuga–Satake hypothesis and does not subsume the stable-bundle construction. The already algebraic automorphism-graph combinations should continue to be distinguished from the new bundle realization.

Several local corrections and scope clarifications would improve the published text without changing its main contribution:

- **Normalize Section 8 consistently.** Section 6 defines $w_2=M_Uw_1=(22,-42)$, whereas Theorem 8.1 sometimes uses $M_Uw_1=2w_2$. With the former convention, the normal coefficient is $(2+\zeta_3)w_2/3$, as in the supplementary script, rather than $2(2+\zeta_3)w_2/3$. The nonvanishing argument survives this correction. Also correct the signs in Steps 2a–2c: with the paper's definition of $\kappa_2$, $\operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal{E}nd_0(\mathcal U_0))=4\kappa_2(\mathcal U_0)$, so the displayed Chern–Weil form represents $\kappa_2$, not $-\kappa_2$. This changes a sign, not the vanishing implication. The explicit Casimir sum in Theorem 7.1 likewise needs the opposite sign to match its intrinsic Killing-form definition and the script.
- **Keep the deformation bases explicit.** The primitive-similarity definition of $B_{19}$ uses Variant IV. When discussing Variants I–III, specify whether the comparison uses that fixed base or their common $U$-polarized $B_{18}$; their own correspondence matrices do not all define the same primitive-similarity family. In the relative-family construction, explicitly choose the central point in the minimum-$h^0$ locus used by Grauert. The argument establishes a family on that open locus, not extension of every prescribed central section at a jumping point.
- **Distinguish bounds from attained extrema.** Theorem 3.1 proves the stated uniform upper bound for rank-one subsheaf degrees; writing it as an attained maximum for every fiber is too strong. Theorem 2.1's non-strict inequalities establish failure of stability, not necessarily failure of semistability. In Appendix A.1, the independence locus for Variant I must also exclude the zero fibers of the chosen section of $\mathcal O(5f)$; a dense open locus suffices for global stability. Also, $(6,2)$ is outside the closure of $(2,4)\times(1,2)$, not on its boundary.

The research question, construction, proof strategy, and limitation are identifiable, so clarity meets the acceptance threshold. The introduction and metadata abstract would nevertheless benefit from fewer internal labels and numerical variants. Moving repetitive case tables and calculations to the appendix would make the main geometric argument easier to follow. The acceptance is for this partial advance; no complete-solution designation is warranted.
