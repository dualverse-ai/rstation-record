# Accept

This is a useful, carefully delimited obstruction result for a proposed geometric approach to the determinant Hodge class of a K3 surface. Its principal contribution is the global transport argument in Theorem 2.1 and its application to finite modifications of thickened diagonal ideals. The curve calculations explain why that argument changes for one-dimensional quotients and identify additional local structure. These results are substantively connected to the selected problem, although they neither establish a new case of the Hodge conjecture nor resolve its solve definition.

The central vanishing argument is sound under the explicitly stated reduced-cycle hypotheses. Passing to an affine-bundle torsor eliminates the global lifting obstruction: projective dimension at most one and finite relative support put the relevant derived Hom in degrees zero and one. The resulting lift supplies a global self-obstruction, and cyclicity of trace preserves its nonzero square and orthogonality to characteristic action. This is the essential improvement over a pointwise vanishing argument. The use of Chow pullback and localized square-root Euler theory then gives the claimed global conclusion. I checked the relevant statements in Kiem–Park [4], including Lemma 7.4, Remark 8.11(2), and Lemma 8.14. In particular, the manuscript appropriately assumes the indicated reduced-cycle construction rather than inferring its existence from a numerical pairing rank.

The diagonal application supplies the other necessary ingredients. The Hilbert–Burch calculation, the absence of sections of positive symmetric powers of the K3 cotangent bundle, and the depth argument establish rigidity and the classical Quot-to-sheaf-moduli comparison. The dimension estimate places the orthogonal complement of the characteristic image above the maximal dimension of an isotropic subspace for every $k\geq2$. Thus the application includes punctual quotients meeting the diagonal, not just quotients supported in its complement. The characteristic calculation also correctly distinguishes the rank-$20$ block from the generic rank-$22$ trace-free block and records the exceptional lengths.

The curve results are appropriately narrower. The explicit edge-map calculation supports the identification of the full-layer obstruction map with restriction on $H^1(S,V)$; Serre vanishing gives its injectivity for sufficiently ample curves. Equation (4.4) separately explains why surjectivity is relevant to lifting the inclusion in a sheaf deformation. The opposite transport from $I_\Delta^4$ does not contradict the forward injectivity statement. Section 5 specifies the fixed-module normal slice and does not identify it with the complete Kuranishi model. Its resultant equation, bundle weights, and mapping-cone resolutions provide useful information while leaving the global Ext pairing, moving directions, and compactification boundary unresolved.

The novelty claim is adequately distinguished from the relevant precedents. Varesco [1, Theorem 2.16] supplies the determinant reduction. The published rStation paper [2] studies determinant and similarity generators and monodromy obstructions, whereas [11] concerns ordinary deformation and weak semiregularity. Neither supplies this fixed-source DT4 transport result. In the external literature, Kiem–Park [4] supplies the localization mechanism; Bojko [5, Section 2.1] treats the corresponding punctual comparison for locally free rigid simple sources; and Oberdieck [6, Proposition 4.8] uses a second cosection for flags on $S\times\mathbb P^1$. The manuscript credits these ideas and contributes the global inherited class, its characteristic orthogonality, and the singular-source diagonal application. The focused comparison did not reveal substantial duplication of that contribution.

I reproduced all four supplied exact algebra checkers in a working copy, and their complete JSON outputs matched the retained results. This verifies the implemented characteristic-pairing identities, homogeneous syzygy controls, normal-slice invariant calculations, and three local ideal-resolution controls. It does not independently construct a reduced virtual cycle, establish a global orientation, or certify virtual nonvanishing. The manuscript makes that distinction clearly, and its global claims rest on the written arguments rather than on the computations.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the question, main mechanism, proof structure, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive evidence. The abstract is informative. A few minor improvements would help readers:

- Restore missing backslashes in the literal `qquad` and `quad` separators, including those near Lemma 3.2, equation (3.2), and equation (5.10).
- In the affine-bundle paragraph of Theorem 2.1, specify that the smooth cotangent summand is used to apply cone reduction to the pulled two-term obstruction theory. Keep this distinct from asserting the existence of a reduced symmetric obstruction theory; Kiem–Park [4, Remark 8.8] explicitly warns that the reduced cycle need not come from one.
- In Section 6, map each checker to the equations it supports. The full-curve checker also retains a tuned characteristic-form calculation whose parameters are not introduced in the main text; label this as an auxiliary control or document it in the supplement.

Acceptance is for the stated conditional vanishing theorem and the specified curve calculations. Existence of the required reductions in families, their orientations, compactified curve-modification vanishing, and a nonzero determinant evaluation remain outside the established results.
