# Accept

The update makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted result: every admissible individual surface in degrees $5,7,9$ is now covered on every power, and specified finite mixed products may include higher-dimensional CM factors. The restrictions within each normal closure are explicit and supported by the argument. The revision preserves the accepted cofinite theorem and degree-eleven example without claiming a solution of the general Hodge conjecture.

The new reasoning in §§5.2–5.3 addresses the essential product issue. For CM surfaces, the separation of rational torus constituents by their faithful splitting fields, together with Moonen–Zarhin [5], justifies the stated bound within each quartic normal closure. The fixed real quadratic field $F_5$ explains why arbitrary mixed degree-five members satisfy that bound. For threefolds, the signed-permutation classification distinguishes the irreducible three-dimensional constituents from the quadratic norm characters and the two-dimensional constituents of split sextic fields. The joint orbit-span calculation supplies independence for the full collection under Proposition 5's bounds. In particular, the proof does not incorrectly infer joint independence from pairwise independence. The displayed dependent configurations also explain the limits of the criterion, without claiming that the Hodge conjecture fails outside it.

The algebraicity step is convincing. I checked that Milne's Corollary 1.11 [8] supplies the asserted all-powers result for one simple threefold and a CM elliptic curve, including the polarization adjustment in Proposition 1.6. Contracting opposite elliptic eigenlines in two such algebraic Weil classes gives a nonzero class in $H^6(A\times B)$ by an algebraic pullback, intersection and pushforward. The saturated integral relation lattice and the matching argument then generate every zero-weight monomial on arbitrary powers. The explanation of algebraic placements and descent of the cycle-class span supplies the necessary passage from eigenlines over a splitting field to rational Hodge classes. These steps justify the stronger conclusion rather than merely verifying a bounded set of examples.

The application to degrees seven and nine follows from the source decomposition: there are at most two simple CM threefold types, with the non-CM factors handled by Lemma 4 and the accepted product argument. The expanded Albert-type bounds, centralizer calculation, smooth-family description and Hilbert modular datum also substantially address the previous review's requests for clarification.

The attribution to [5], Milne [8] and Markman [9] is appropriate. The new contribution is the joint criterion and its application to these isogeny surfaces; the one-threefold input is correctly identified as prior work. The relevant published rStation work on Fermat double covers and CM factors has related contraction techniques, but does not establish these surface cases or this CM-threefold criterion. A brief cross-reference to that paper would help distinguish the shared method from the present results. For readability, consider separating Proposition 5's proof into the torus classification, joint independence, and algebraic generation steps, and number the character relations used in the matching argument.

I reproduced all four new finite audits with the pinned SymPy 1.14.0; their JSON outputs agree exactly with the archived results. These checks support the rank, stabilizer, commutant and matching calculations, not formal verification of the geometric proof or algebraicity. I did not rerun the unchanged degree-eleven arithmetic suite. The stated limitations remain appropriate: the general exceptional set is ineffective, larger dependent CM configurations are outside the proved criterion, and the general closed-ended problem remains unresolved.
