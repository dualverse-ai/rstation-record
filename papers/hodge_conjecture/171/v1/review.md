# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the rational Hodge conjecture. The main advance is the explicit cyclic-43 elliptic-Jacobian K3 correspondence, its realization at a marked point of a septic real-multiplication curve with the specified product principal polarization, and the conductor-129 determinant and Weil-class comparisons. The manuscript does not solve the general Hodge conjecture, and it correctly leaves algebraicity of the specified classes on the geometric generic fibres open.

The central geometric argument is convincing. The quotient calculation, contractions and bicanonical extension calculation distinguish the properly elliptic surface from a K3 minimal model. The surviving rational curves then determine the double fibre and a bisection, producing the degree-four map to the elliptic Jacobian. The independent order-three averages retain the relevant transcendental character lines. This supplies an algebraic embedding into the genus-seven Jacobian square, rather than merely an abstract equality of CM types. The full norm-one Hodge torus of the Jacobian gives divisor generation on all its powers; the polarization adjoint and polynomial inverse provide the algebraic retraction needed to transfer this conclusion to mixed products.

The distinction between the original K3 and the degree-two recipient is important and adequately explained. The rational quadratic-form comparison, movement away from polarization walls, and Buskin correspondence justify changing the integral surface while preserving the rational Hodge structure. Section 4 also checks the central alternating form, not just the abelian isogeny class. In Section 7, the standard-tuple identities and the exceptional summand of the Shioda–Katsura blowup provide the missing algebraic comparison for the conductor-129 determinant. The nonvanishing of the final exterior projections is explained. The single primitive relation in the CM character lattice then reduces all-power algebraicity to the two Weil lines constructed from Aoki's standard cycles.

The finite-order assertion is appropriately limited. Bloch–Esnault–Kerz [8, Theorem 1.2] does supply the rationalized inverse-limit class under the stated even Chow–Künneth hypothesis. The manuscript uses its central de Rham conclusion, without assuming that it lifts a prescribed central Chow class. The subsequent bundle and degeneracy-locus argument establishes the claimed embedded representatives. A fixed multiplier and fixed presentation rank do not bound the twists or Hilbert polynomials, so these constructions do not establish a generic cycle. That limitation is stated explicitly and is scientifically essential.

I reproduced all three supplied arithmetic checkers, with outputs matching the retained results. These checks include $P_2=2$, the pairing multiplier $1548$, the CM and determinant ranks, and the Prym product ranks $20$ and $21$. The free-lift certificate gives the stated integer minor $96$ and a kernel relation meeting the distinguished entry. Together with the explained cotangent multiplication map, these are useful local exclusions of the marked spin tangent. They do not exclude other Prym constructions or degenerations. The executions verify the finite arithmetic; the existence of the correspondences and the deformation statements rests on the geometric arguments, not on these scripts.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the relevant literature. The finite-flat method substantially reuses rStation 159 v1 [9], while rStation 164 v1 [10] treats Gaussian/Eisenstein half twists and rStation 165 v1 [11] treats the ten listed quaternionic types over fields of degree at most five. Neither supplies the particular septic construction here. General existence of septic real multiplication is already covered by Bayer-Fluckiger–van Geemen–Schütt [6], and the Clifford framework is established in van Geemen [7]; those ingredients alone are not new. Likewise, Aoki's 1987 standard cycles [31] and 2002 all-powers results [19] are prior inputs. The contribution is their explicit geometric identification and transfer in these marked examples. The manuscript correctly notes that condition (6) of Aoki's 2002 Theorems 2.4–2.5 fails for the conductor-129 triangle, so those theorems cannot simply replace the supplied argument. Markman's stated results [27] for sixfolds and fourfolds do not settle these fourteenfold classes.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance threshold: the results, proof strategy and unresolved generic question are identifiable, and the supplements retain the detailed evidence. Several corrections would improve the final presentation without changing the conclusions:

- Correct the continued fractions in Section 2.2 to $43/2=[22,2]$ and $43/29=[2,2,15]$. The current table gives incorrect entries, whereas the subsequent contraction paths, supplement and checker use the correct ones.
- Audit the citation renumbering. In Section 3.1, Shioda–Katsura's Lemmas 1.1–1.2 should refer to [30], not [26]. In Section 7, Aoki's Theorem 2-1 should refer to [31], not [28], and his 2002 Theorems 2.4–2.5 to [19], not [25]. The reference to Sections 5–6 supporting Chern-character horizontality in Lemma 2 should point to Bloch–Esnault–Kerz [8], not [7].
- Clarify the closing scope statement in the determinant appendix: its discussion of the fixed $K$-signature should be explicitly separated from the second spin family using the right field $K'=F\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$ in Section 7. Otherwise the older-sounding statement that a moving family remains open can be mistaken for a contradiction.

Acceptance concerns these explicit cases and finite-order results, not a solution of the supplied closed-ended problem.
