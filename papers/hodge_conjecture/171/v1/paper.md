## 1. Statement and scope

All cycle classes and Hodge conjectures in this article use rational coefficients. For a projective K3 surface $S$, write $T(S)=\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}^{\perp}\subset H^2(S,\mathbf Q)$. For a degree-two polarized surface with polarization $h$, write $V=h^\perp$. At a Picard jump the marked $V$ continues to have rank 21.

**Theorem 1.** Let $D$ be the smooth projective model of $y^{43}=x(1-x)^6$, and let $C$ be its genus-seven quotient by the order-three automorphism specified in Section 2. Put $J=\operatorname{Jac}(C)$, $K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{43})^{\langle6\rangle}$, $F=K^+$ and $k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-43})$. Then the following hold.

1. The twist $r\mapsto r^2,\ s\mapsto s$ produces a properly elliptic minimal product-quotient surface with elliptic Jacobian $S_J$. The K3 surface $S_J$ has Picard number eight, with $T(S_J)$ a $K$-line. A rational algebraic correspondence embeds $T(S_J)$ in $H^1(J)\otimes H^1(J)$, and the Hodge conjecture holds on $S_J^m\times J^n$ for every $m,n\ge 0$.
2. There is a smooth pointed algebraic curve $(U,0)$ over $\mathbf C$ carrying smooth degree-two K3 surfaces $\mathcal S$ and principally polarized abelian fourteenfolds $\mathcal A$. The fibre $S_0$ is rationally Hodge isometric to $S_J$, and the marked principally polarized fibre $A_0$ is $J^2$ with the product polarization. At very general points of $U$, $\rho(S)=1$ and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{HS}}(T(S))=F$. There is a specified horizontal connecting class $\Gamma\in V\otimes H^2(A)$ of type $(2,2)$ and a horizontal two-dimensional Weil space $\mathcal W_k\subset H^{14}(A,\mathbf Q)$ of type $(7,7)$. The Hodge conjecture holds on all $S_0^m\times A_0^n$, including these specified central classes.
3. Put $R_N=\mathbf C[t]/(t^N)$ in a local parameter at $0$. For every $N\ge1$ there is a closed subscheme of $(\mathcal S\times_U\mathcal A)_{R_N}$, finite locally free over $\mathcal A_{R_N}$ with normal integral special fibre, whose projected codimension-two de Rham class is $M\Gamma$ for one fixed $M>0$. Given any fixed nonzero rational flat vector $\omega\in\mathcal W_k$ near $0$, there is also a closed codimension-seven subscheme of $\mathcal A_{R_N}$, smooth over $R_N$ with geometrically integral special fibre, whose Weil projection is $6!M'\omega$ for one fixed $M'>0$. The integers $M,M'$ are independent of $N$. The presentations can change with $N$.

The generic question on this particular curve is whether the specified $\Gamma$ and one nonzero rational Weil vector are algebraic on the geometric generic product and abelian fibre, respectively. At fibres with the computed very general Hodge group this is equivalent to the Hodge conjecture for all mixed powers $S^m\times A^n$. Section 5 explains the equivalence and the interpretation at the generic point. The finite-order result gives actual schemes over each $R_N$ and a compatible system of virtual classes. Generic algebraicity remains open.

Pure powers of a K3 surface with CM transcendental endomorphism field already satisfy the Hodge conjecture by Ramón Marí [1]. The argument here builds a specific mixed correspondence with a specified Jacobian. It uses product-quotient methods as in [2], [3]. The curve $D$ is dominated by the degree-43 Fermat curve by $x=u^{43},\ y=uv^6$ on $u^{43}+v^{43}=1$. The construction proceeds through an elliptic torsor and its Jacobian. Garbagnati and Penegini [2] classify a different setup, with cyclic diagonal groups whose quotient directly has a K3 minimal model. The period and spin arguments use the frameworks of Zarhin, van Geemen, and Bayer-Fluckiger, van Geemen and Schütt [4], [5], [6], including van Geemen's treatment of Kuga–Satake varieties with real multiplication [7]. The finite-order step applies Bloch, Esnault and Kerz [8] and then constructs embedded schemes.

The general rank-$2d$ finite-order bundle construction is developed in [9]. Algebraic half twists for Gaussian and Eisenstein K3 surfaces give mixed Hodge results in [10], and [11] treats products in the Noether–Lefschetz closures of ten quaternionic types over fields of degrees at most five. The new geometric input here is the conductor-43 elliptic-Jacobian K3, its marked realization on a septic spin curve, and the algebraic determinant and Weil comparisons at the conductor-129 CM fibre.

## 2. The product quotient and its elliptic Jacobian

### 2.1. The group and the twists

Let $r(x,y)=(x,\zeta_{43}y)$. There is an order-three lift $s$ of $x\mapsto 1/(1-x)$ with $srs^{-1}=r^6$. For example one can use $y\mapsto y^6/(1-x)$, verifying the relation and cube on the function field. The exponent triple at $0,1,\infty$ is $1,6,36$ modulo $43$. Riemann–Hurwitz gives $g(D)=21$. Above each of the two fixed points of the base three-cycle, $s$ acts on the 43-element fibre as an affine permutation with nontrivial linear part, hence has one fixed point. Riemann–Hurwitz for the triple quotient gives $g(C)=7$. The group $G=\langle r,s\rangle$ has order 129 and signature $(3,3,43)$ on $D$.

In the twist indexed by $t\in(\mathbf Z/43)^\times$, let $r$ act as $(r,r^t)$ and $s$ as $(s,s)$ on $D^2$. The automorphisms of $G$ have $r\mapsto r^t,\ s\mapsto r^b s$. Indeed the order-43 subgroup is characteristic and the conjugation relation determines the exponent of $s$ modulo it. Inner conjugations remove $b$ and multiply $t$ by $H=\{1,6,36\}$. Factor exchange inverts $tH$. Thus the table below enumerates the twists of this action modulo inner conjugation.

To distinguish Kodaira dimension one from general type, we compute $P_2$ with extension over the quotient singularities. The following rational row-reduction recipe specifies the entire calculation. For $0\le i<43$, put
$$
 A_i=\left\lceil\frac{i-84}{43}\right\rceil,\quad

$$
 \mathbf u_i=(43A_i+84-i,\ 43B_i+84-6i,\ 7i-88-43T_i).
$$

For every least representative $i$ of a nonzero $H$-orbit, set $j=-i/t\bmod43$, take the tensor of polynomial bases of degrees $d_i,d_j$, and impose the following equations at each ordered pair of branch points $(\alpha,\beta)$ from $0,1,\infty$. If their monodromy exponents are $a,b$ in the order $1,6,36$, set $k'=ta/b\bmod43$. For Taylor indices $s_1,s_2$ in the indicated polynomial ranges, take $u=(\mathbf u_i)_\alpha+43s_1,\ v=(\mathbf u_j)_\beta+43s_2$. Set that coefficient to zero if for some $1\le r'\le42$
$$
 (u+2)r'+(v+2)(k'r'\bmod43)<86 .
$$
The coefficient rows extract the indexed monomial at $0$, use the binomial coefficients of $x^\ell$ at $1$, and extract the index in reverse order at $\infty$, with the polynomial padded to its allowed degree. Tensor the two coefficient rows. The inequality is the toric extension order along the rays of the quotient resolution. Testing all displayed lattice points is equivalent to testing the regular fan's primitive rays: further toric subdivisions preserve regularity, and the axes already pass. A common invertible local factor does not change the monomial extension ideal. The independent cyclic character spaces can be tested separately at the branch grid since the two rotations preserve the ideal there and fix the branch points. The order-three action identifies the three positions within each orbit.

The nonzero nullities for $t=2$ are one each at $(9,17)$ and $(21,11)$, with polynomials $1\otimes1$ and $(x-1)\otimes1$. For $t=3$ they are one each at $(9,40)$ and $(10,11)$. At a $\tfrac13(1,2)$ point extension is automatic by crepancy. At a $\tfrac13(1,1)$ point an invariant bicanonical term satisfies $u+v+4\equiv0\bmod3$, hence $u+v\ge 2$, exactly the exceptional-ray condition. Thus $P_2=2$. On a minimal surface of general type, nef and big canonical vanishing and Riemann–Roch give $P_2=\chi(\mathcal O)+K^2\ge3$. Kodaira dimension zero with $p_g=1,q=0$ gives a K3 and $P_2=1$. Since $p_g=1$, the remaining possibility is Kodaira dimension one. Its minimal model has $K^2=0$, so $X$ is already minimal. This extension calculation is essential to identifying its fibration. ### 2.3. The Jacobian and the correspondence

The genus-one fibration of $X$ has base $\mathbf P^1$, since $q=0$. Its elliptic Jacobian $S_J$ has the same fundamental line bundle, of degree two. It has a section, hence no multiple fibres. The canonical bundle formula gives $K_{S_J}=0$, and $q(S_J)=0$, so it is a K3. On $X$, with $\chi(\mathcal O)=2$ over $\mathbf P^1$, each multiple fibre contributes one to the base divisor for the bicanonical system. Since $P_2=2$, there is exactly one multiple fibre. If its multiplicity is $\mu$, then $K_X\equiv(\mu-1)F_{\mathrm{ell}}/\mu$. Either surviving $(-3)$-curve has canonical intersection one by adjunction, which makes $\mu/(\mu-1)$ an integer. Hence $\mu=2$, and the curve gives a bisection. Its degree-two line on the generic fibre defines $p\mapsto \mathcal O(2p)\otimes L^{-1}$ into the Jacobian, of degree four. The composite rational map $f:D^2\dashrightarrow S_J$ has degree $516=4\cdot129$. Pullback by the resolved graph identifies $T(S_J)$ with the simple rank-14 mixed invariant part, since it is nonzero and blowups supply only divisor classes. This proves $\rho(S_J)=8$. [14], [15]

Write $q_C:D^2\to C^2$ for the product quotient of degree nine. On a paired orbit the diagonal invariant is a sum of three terms in compatible cyclic bases. Independent averaging sends these terms to equal nonzero multiples of the product of the two invariant degree-one orbit sums. Distinct orbit pairs have independent first-slot eigenlines. Thus $g=(q_C)_*f^*$ embeds the transcendental space into $H^1(C)\otimes H^1(C)$. The Abel maps induce the inverse comparison from these degree-one groups to $H^1(J)$ by algebraic correspondences. For example, the transpose Abel graph after the polarization identifications and the algebraic inverse Lefschetz map gives the inverse, with its scalar fixed by the Abel curve class $\theta_J^6/6!$. The two comparisons and the primitive graph projection give the claimed codimension-two correspondence. [16]

The cup scaling of $g$ will be needed in Section 4. Pullback through $f$ scales the pairing by 516. The pull-push $q_C^*(q_C)_*$ is the sum of the nine $(s^a,s^b)^*$. Exactly the three terms with $a=b$ fix the twisted mixed invariants. The other six move every nonzero pair satisfying $i+2j=0$ outside that character sector. They pair to zero against it since the cup form pairs inverse characters. Hence
$$
 (gx,gy)_{C^2}=1548(x,y)_{S_J}.
$$
These are ordinary pushforwards and have no division by the order of the diagonal subgroup.

### 2.4. The original integral lattice

The eight surviving algebraic classes on $X$ have block matrix
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}9&21\\21&6\end{pmatrix}\perp
 \langle-3,-3\rangle\perp A_2(-1)^2 ,
$$
of determinant $-43\cdot 27^2$. Its index in the free Néron–Severi lattice divides 27. Unimodularity of free $H^2(X,\mathbf Z)$ identifies the absolute transcendental and algebraic discriminants. To show saturation at 3, use the intermediate quotient by the twisted order-43 subgroup, then the degree-three map $v$ to $X$. The first rotation $\rho=r\times1$ acts on this intermediate quotient. The integral correspondences $v_*(\rho^m)^*v^*$ induce the unnormalized period sums for $mH$ on $T(X)$, by the degree-three graph pull-push. The period sums form an integral basis of $\mathcal O_K$, since the basis $\zeta_{43},\ldots,\zeta_{43}^{42}$ of the cyclotomic integer ring is permuted by $H$. The class of 3 has order 14 modulo $H$, as $3^7=37\in-H$ and $3^{14}=36\in H$ modulo 43. Hence at 3 the lattice is a fractional ideal of an unramified degree-14 local field. Its cup-dual is also such an ideal by the conjugate adjoint. Its discriminant valuation is divisible by 14 and bounded by 6, so it is zero. This gives $\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\rm free}=-43$.

For completeness, the discriminant transfer to $S_J$ uses the primitive fibre class on each surface. On $X$ it is the half-fibre $K_X$ of divisibility one by the bisection, and on $S_J$ it is the fibre of divisibility one by the section. The normalized fibre root lattices coincide. At nonmultiple fibres the regular model has a local section and hence is the local Jacobian model. At the double fibre, the order-two local torsor is trivial for additive reduction, by Kummer over $\mathbf C((t))$. Indeed the component group contributes equally to $|E[2](\mathbf C((t)))|$ and $|E(\mathbf C((t)))/2|$, whereas the identity component in additive reduction is divisible with no two-torsion. The local Galois group is procyclic, so $|H^1(E[2])|=|E[2](\mathbf C((t)))|$. For good reduction an order-two translation on the quadratic base change gives a double smooth fibre. For multiplicative reduction $I_n$, the identity component has two two-torsion points, so Kummer leaves exactly one nonzero local class of order two. For Tate parameter $q=t^n u(t)$, use $t=s^2$ and the point represented by $\xi s^n\sqrt{u(t)}$ with $\xi^2=(-1)^n$. Its product with its conjugate is $q$, and it translates the $I_{2n}$ components by $n$. The free quotient has fibre $2I_n$. This proves the normalized comparison including the double fibre.

In the frame lattice $f^\perp/\mathbf Z f$ for a primitive fibre $f$ of divisibility one, the quotient by the fibre root lattice is the generic degree-zero Picard group modulo the image $A_{\mathrm{tors}}$ of surface Picard torsion. Restriction is surjective by divisor closure and its kernel is vertical. Tsen identifies the generic degree-zero Picard group with the Mordell–Weil group $E(\mathbf C(t))$. The height on its free part is the same for the torsor and the Jacobian. To see this, correct divisor lifts rationally to be orthogonal to vertical components, pass to a finite base change giving a torsor point, and compare on the isomorphic minimal models. Pairings scale by the base-change degree and are independent of the choices of lifts modulo the fibre. The frame discriminant is the root discriminant times the height determinant divided by $(|E(\mathbf C(t))_{\mathrm{tors}}|/|A_{\mathrm{tors}}|)^2$, by the index and Schur complement formula. The section on the K3 and its torsion-free Picard group give $|\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\rm free}|=|A_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2|\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(S_J)|$. Since 43 is squarefree, $\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(S_J)=-43$. [15], [17]

For an ample primitive class $a$ on $S_J$, the even positive-definite rank-seven lattice $-a^\perp$ has minimum at least four, since a perpendicular $(-2)$-class is effective up to sign by K3 Riemann–Roch. Its determinant is $43a^2/\operatorname{div}(a)^2$, at most $43a^2$. The Hermite constant in rank seven gives determinant at least $(4/2^{6/7})^7=256$, hence $a^2\ge6$. The same lower bound applies to nonprimitive ample classes. The degree-two construction below uses a different surface. [18]

## 3. Mixed powers at the CM point and the degree-two period

### 3.1. The CM type of $J$

The same rotation orbit sums on the order-three invariant part of $H^1(D)$ make $H^1(J)$ a $K$-line. Its CM type is given by the seven holomorphic orbits of Section 2. Use the seven labels $r_\ell=(-2)^\ell\bmod43$, $\ell=0,\ldots,6$, for the embedding pairs. They are cyclically permuted and lie above one embedding of $k$. On those labels the Hodge circle signs on the first copy are
$$
 (-,+,-,+,-,-,+).
$$
For example evaluate the residue-sum test of Section 2 on $1,41,4,35,16,11,21$ in order. The cyclic translates span the entire rational seven-dimensional space: the sign polynomial has nonzero value at 1, and its degree-six coefficient list is not constant, so it is not divisible by the seventh cyclotomic polynomial. Conjugation reverses the signs. The rational Hodge group is therefore the full norm-one torus of $K/F$, since it is contained in that torus by the polarized $K$-action and its cocharacter space contains these translates.

On $J^n$, exterior monomials invariant under this torus have equal occupancy in each pair of inverse characters. They are exterior products of degree-two invariants. Surjectivity of this multiplication map descends to $\mathbf Q$, and rational degree-two invariants are divisor classes by Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Thus all Hodge classes on powers of $J$ are algebraic.

Use the mixed embedding $i_J:T(S_J)\hookrightarrow H^2(J^2)$ of Section 2.3. Its image is primitive for the product polarization $\theta$ because each Jacobian factor has odd cohomological degree in the mixed block. Compose multiplication by $\theta^{12}$ with the transpose of the correspondence and the transcendental projector of $S_J$ to obtain its algebraic adjoint. The pairing on the image is nondegenerate by Hodge–Riemann. Hence $i_J^\dagger i_J$ is invertible on $T(S_J)$, and its inverse is a rational polynomial in the composite by Cayley–Hamilton, using the transcendental projector as identity. This gives an algebraic retraction on cohomology. On any $S_J^m\times J^n$, split each K3 cohomology into its transcendental and Tate sectors. The Tate projectors are algebraic, using a point and products of divisor classes with the inverse Néron–Severi Gram matrix. Apply the injection to every transcendental sector, algebraize the resulting class on a power of $J$ by divisor generation, and retract to the original product. This proves the mixed-power assertion for all $m,n$.

**Mixed products with Fermat hypersurfaces.** One may also adjoin any finite collection of Fermat hypersurfaces of prime-power degree or twice odd-prime-power degree, and abelian isogeny factors of their curve Jacobians. The primes and dimensions can vary between factors. The rational Hodge conjecture holds on every resulting product with powers of $S_J$ and $J$. Section 3.3 gives the same conclusion for the degree-two CM recipient.

First group the degrees by the underlying prime, placing the two K3 surfaces and $J$ in the group at 43. Within a group at an odd prime $p$ take a common level $M=2p^E$, and within the group at two take $M=2^E$. Choose $E$ large enough for divisibility. The coordinate-power maps from level $M$ onto the lower levels are finite and surjective. Theorem 1.2(i) of Aoki [19] gives the rational Hodge conjecture for all powers of the level-$M$ Fermat Jacobian $J_M$. Quadrics have algebraic cohomology and can also be omitted from this reduction. The whole cohomology of the Fermat curve is an algebraic retract of powers of $J_M$ and Tate structures. In degree one the Abel map gives an isomorphism, with inverse supplied by the transpose correspondence and inverse Lefschetz on the Jacobian. Equivalently, for an Abel embedding $j$, the maps $j^*$ and $(g-1)!(L_\theta^{g-1})^{-1}j_*$ are inverse on degree one by the Abel curve class formula. Points give the two other curve projectors. Abelian isogeny factors of $J_M$ are algebraic retracts by Poincaré reducibility.

For completeness, this argument includes Fermat hypersurfaces in every dimension. Write $X_M^n$ for the dimension-$n$ hypersurface. For $n\ge2$, the degree-$M$ Shioda–Katsura map $X_M^{n-1}\times X_M^1\dashrightarrow X_M^n$ is a morphism after blowing up the smooth codimension-two centre $X_M^{n-2}\times X_M^0$ [26, Lemmas 1.1–1.2]. Normalized pushforward splits the pullback from the target, and the algebraic blowup formula adds the cohomology of this centre with one Tate twist. Induction expresses each $H^*(X_M^n)$ as a retract through algebraic correspondences of sums of tensor powers of $H^1(J_M)$ and Tate structures. In the group at 43 the same holds for $J$, by the finite quotient map from the level-43 Fermat curve to $C$, and for $S_J$, by its transcendental retraction above.

For the last step write $M_p$ for these common levels. The Hodge groups of the $J_{M_p}$ are CM tori split by $\mathbf Q(\zeta_{p^{E_p}})$. Indeed the degree-one Fermat character spaces are lines over this field, also for $M_p=2p^{E_p}$ with $p$ odd. These splitting fields have coprime conductors across distinct primes. On the product the Hodge circle has cocharacter $\lambda=(\lambda_p)_p$. Its Galois translates span the rational cocharacter space of the Hodge group. By the Chinese remainder theorem there is an automorphism acting by complex conjugation on just one of these fields and trivially on the others. Subtracting its translate of $\lambda$ isolates $2\lambda_p$, and the further conjugates span the whole factor. Thus the Hodge group is the direct product of the individual Hodge groups, as in the CM disjointness argument of Abdulali [20]. In each Künneth degree the invariants are tensor products of individual invariants. These are sums of exterior products of rational Hodge classes on powers of the individual Jacobians, with the indicated Tate shifts. Aoki's theorem and the algebraic retractions give cycles on the original product.

### 3.2. A rational square-two vector and a ternary form over $F$

The block matrix in Section 2.4 has rational form $\langle1,-1^6,-43\rangle$, where exponents denote repetitions. To verify it, diagonalize the first block to $\langle1,-43\rangle$, each $A_2(-1)$ to $\langle-2,-6\rangle$, and halve the equal $-2$ and $-6$ pairs using half-sums and half-differences. The residual four copies of $-3$ have form $-I_4$, as the rows of
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&0\\-1&1&0&1\\-1&0&1&-1\\0&-1&1&1\end{pmatrix}
$$
are orthogonal of norm three. The free integral cohomology of $X$ is odd unimodular of signature $(3,19)$, and its rational form agrees with that of the K3 lattice. For the latter comparison use the standard rational dot-product model of $E_8$ and diagonalize a rational hyperbolic plane to $\langle1,-1\rangle$. Half the transcendental pullback from the degree-four map $X\dashrightarrow S_J$ is an isometry. Witt extension and orthogonal complementation give $\operatorname{NS}(S_J)_{\mathbf Q}\simeq\langle1,-1^6,-43\rangle$. Choose $h_0=(3/2,1/2)$ in the first $\langle1,-1\rangle$. It has square two, and its algebraic perpendicular $N$ has form $\langle-1^5,-2,-43\rangle$.

The trace form on $F$ is $\langle1^7\rangle$ over $\mathbf Q$. Indeed put $H_0=\{\pm1,\pm6,\pm36\}$ and $\eta_i=\sum_{a\in 2^iH_0}\zeta_{43}^a$, $0\le i<7$. Counting zero and nonzero exponents in the products and their seven conjugates gives trace Gram matrix $43I-6\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf t}$. The ones line has norm 7, and on its perpendicular this is 43 times the ordinary rank-six dot product of determinant square class 7. The Hasse ratio with the ordinary dot product is $(43,-7)=1$, since $43=6^2+7$, and the determinants and real signatures agree. Also $N\simeq\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbf Q}\langle-86\rangle$. Both forms have determinant square class $-86$ and are negative definite. Their Hasse invariants are $(-1,-1)(2,43)$ and $(-1,-1)(86,-1)$, respectively. The extra symbols take values $+1,-1,-1$ at $\infty,2,43$, respectively, and are $+1$ at other primes. These two comparisons use Hasse–Minkowski.

On the $K$-line $T(S_J)$ the cup form is $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbf Q}(\lambda x\bar y)$ with $\lambda\in F^\times$, since the adjoint on $K$ is conjugation. Its trace from $F$ has binary form $2\lambda N_{K/F}$, positive definite at the period embedding and negative definite at the others. Thus on $h_0^\perp$ the rational form is the transfer of
$$
 \Phi=\langle2\lambda,86\lambda,-86\rangle .
$$

## 6. Realization at every finite order

We give the formal input and the construction of actual schemes separately. Let $R=\mathbf C[[t]]$, $R_N=R/(t^N)$, and $X/R$ be smooth projective of relative dimension $d$. Write $X_0$ for its complex special fibre and $X_N=X\times_R R_N$. In the applications $X$ is the completion of $\mathcal A$ or $\mathcal S\times_U\mathcal A$ at the mark.

**Lemma 2.** Assume $X_0$ is nonempty, smooth and geometrically connected, hence geometrically integral. Suppose the geometric degree projectors in all even de Rham degrees of the generic fibre of $X$ are realized by Chow idempotents over $\mathbf C((t))$. Let $\beta$ be a horizontal de Rham class in degree $2c$ belonging to $F^c$, where $1\le c\le d$, and assume an algebraic rational class represents $\beta_0$. Choose a relatively ample line bundle of class $\ell$. For each $N$ there is a nonempty closed subscheme $Z_N\subset X_N$, flat over $R_N$ and of relative codimension $c$, with relative fundamental de Rham class
$$
 [Z_N]_{\rm dR}=\binom{2d}{c}u_N^c\ell^c+
 (-1)^{c-1}(c-1)!\,M_\beta\beta|_{R_N},
$$
for one fixed integer $M_\beta>0$ and positive integers $u_N$. The scheme is smooth over $R_N$ if $2(c+1)>d$. Its special fibre is normal and geometrically integral if $d>c$. If $d=c$, the scheme is finite étale over $R_N$.

*Proof.* The rational Chow character on $X_0$ allows a virtual class whose only de Rham character is $\beta_0$ in degree $2c$. Theorem 1.2 of Bloch–Esnault–Kerz [8] gives a class in $(\varprojlim_N K_0(X_N))\otimes\mathbf Q$ with that central de Rham character. Its condition on filtration holds by hypothesis. The version over $\mathbf C$ prescribes the central cohomological character. Clearing one denominator supplies a compatible integral system $\eta_N$ with character $M_\beta\beta_0$ in degree $2c$ on the centre and zero in all other degrees there. In particular $\eta_N$ has rank zero at each order. The relative characters at each order have these same values in the horizontal trivialization. To see uniqueness at order $N$, write the Gauss–Manin connection in a horizontal basis over $R$. The characteristic-zero derivation on $R_N$ takes values in $R_{N-1}dt$ with kernel $\mathbf C$. Relative Chern characters are horizontal by the closed absolute Chern character and its image in relative de Rham cohomology, as in [7, Sections 5–6]. This gives the assertion for the Chern character at each order.

For fixed $N$, set $r=2d$ and write $\eta_N+r[\mathcal O]=[B_+]-[B_-]$ using the resolution property. If $B_-$ has rank $s$, then $B_+$ has rank $s+2d$. Let $L$ be the chosen ample line. Choose one $m$ large enough that $B_{-,0}\otimes L_0^m$ and $B_{+,0}\otimes L_0^m$ are generated and their sections lift to $X_N$. Serre vanishing and induction along the Artin filtration give both properties. Put $T_m=(L^{-m})^{\oplus(s+d)}$. Choose separately a surjection $T_m\to B_-$ and a subbundle injection $T_m\to B_+$. For each map, failure of maximal rank has codimension $d+1$ in the space of matrices at a point whenever failure is possible. Global generation and $\dim X_0=d$ give general matrices of full rank everywhere on $X_0$. After lifting the sections, the full-rank minors still give locally split maps on $X_N$. The kernel of the surjection and the cokernel of the injection each have rank $d$. Their direct sum $G$ satisfies $[G]=[B_+]-[B_-]=r[\mathcal O]+\eta_N$, since the two copies of $T_m$ cancel. Twist by $L^{u_N}$ for a sufficiently large positive $u_N$, giving a rank-$r$ bundle $E$ with
$$
 [E]=r[L^{u_N}]+\eta_N[L^{u_N}].
$$
Choose $u_N$ large enough for generation and $H^1(X_0,E_0)=0$, and for positive-degree vanishing of $\bigwedge^j E_0\otimes K_{X_0}$ for $1\le j\le r$. Increasing $u_N$ permits any fixed finite jet or value interpolation as needed below.

Take $q=r-c+1=2d-c+1$ general central columns and lift to $X_N$ via the $H^1$ vanishing. Evaluation surjects onto the matrix bundle on $X_0$, so general matrices meet its rank strata transversely wherever they meet. Membership in the first rank-drop locus costs $c$ conditions. The next drop has codimension $2(c+1)$. When nonempty, the maximal-minor locus has the expected codimension, is Cohen–Macaulay by the maximal-minor resolution [28], and is smooth away from the second drop.

Consider the kernel-line incidence on $X_0\times\mathbf P^{q-1}$, the zero scheme of a general section of $E_0\boxtimes\mathcal O(1)$. This bundle is generated, so the zero scheme is smooth of the expected dimension when nonempty. Its Koszul complex, also exact for an empty zero scheme, has negative terms $\bigwedge^j E_0^\vee\boxtimes\mathcal O(-j)$ for $1\le j\le r$. By Serre duality the first factor has cohomology only in degree $d$. The negative projective twists can contribute only in degree $q-1$. Their total Koszul hypercohomology degrees are therefore at least $d+q-1-r=d-c$. If $d>c$, the map from $H^0(X_0\times\mathbf P^{q-1},\mathcal O)=\mathbf C$ to $H^0$ of the incidence is an isomorphism, since no negative term contributes to total degree zero or minus one. The incidence is thus nonempty and connected, and smoothness makes it integral. It surjects onto the maximal-minor locus. The latter is normal by the Cohen–Macaulay property and the codimension of the deeper strata, so it is integral as a scheme.

If $d=c$, the degree of $c_d(E_0)$ is positive for all sufficiently large $u_N$. Its leading term is $\binom{2d}{d}u_N^d\int_{X_0}\ell^d$. An everywhere full-rank matrix would give $E_0$ a trivial subbundle of rank $q=d+1$ with quotient of rank $d-1$, forcing $c_d(E_0)=0$. Hence the locus is nonempty in this case as well. Whenever $2(c+1)>d$, the deeper strata are absent and the kernel-line graph identifies the two schemes over $R_N$ by pivoting on a nonzero minor of size $q-1$. Central transversality gives smoothness over the Artin base by the relative Jacobian criterion. In particular for $d=c$ the locus is proper and smooth of relative dimension zero, hence finite étale.

In the case with deeper drops, the Eagon–Northcott resolution has locally free terms and its central reduction is exact in positive resolution degree. The filtration by the Artin parameter has copies of the central complex on each successive graded piece. It gives exactness in positive degree and identifies the graded pieces of the cokernel with the central structure sheaf, with multiplication by $t$ giving the natural successive isomorphisms. The local criterion for flatness over $R_N$ now gives flatness of $Z_N$. The relative de Rham fundamental class is the degree-$2c$ Chern character of the resulting perfect structure sheaf. With the expected-codimension maximal minors it equals $c_c(E)$ by Porteous, or by the kernel-line pushforward from its regular incidence [28], [29]. In the Chern polynomial, the first term of difference from the split bundle in weight $c$ is $(-1)^{c-1}(c-1)!M_\beta\beta$ by the character identity. All smaller weights agree. This gives the formula. $\square$

The even Chow–Künneth hypothesis holds over the generic field of the completed curve. On $\mathcal S$ take the degree-one rational zero-cycle $h^2/2$, and use the two point projectors and their complement. On $\mathcal A$ use Deninger–Murre projectors, and on the product use sums of their exterior products [12]. The Hodge condition on the two flat sections gives the formal filtration condition by de Rham comparison on the analytic disk and completion. The central classes are algebraic by Sections 3 and 4.

Apply the lemma first to $\beta=-\Gamma$, $d=16,\ c=2,\ r=32,\ q=31,\ \ell=h+\theta$. The formula for the class is
$$

$$
The first term has zero primitive mixed projection, and the special fibre is normal and integral. We sharpen flatness over the parameter to finite local freeness over $\mathcal A_{R_N}$. Multiply by generating sections of the remaining sufficiently positive twist of $E_0$. Such tuples of points, including their common abelian base point, have dimension $30+14$. Simultaneous rank drop costs 30 conditions. The incidence therefore has dimension at most the matrix parameter dimension plus 14, while a positive-dimensional fibre of a fixed maximal-minor scheme supplies a tuple family of dimension at least 15. The fibre-dimension theorem excludes this for a general central matrix. Properness gives finiteness.  =1{,}184{,}040\,u_N^7\ell^7+720M'\omega|_{R_N}.

The first term has Weil projection zero. In one such determinant choose the fourteen $H_{129}$-cosets above $+1\bmod3$. Their fourteen triangles concatenate to the 42 units congruent to $1\bmod3$, each once. Adjoining the opposite pair $(43,86)$ gives the 43-standard tuple

$$

This proves the assertion for $A_{129}^n$. Inserting the split K3 comparison on each transcendental factor proves Theorem 2 for the mixed powers. This proof uses the standard cycles of [31]. **A second spin family.** Write the quaternion algebra as $Q=(K/F,172\lambda)$ in the basis of Section 4. It is ramified at the six definite real places. Its integral orthogonal complement is self-dual. The normalized isometry to $T(S_J)$ transports this lattice. It is stable at $\ell$ under the Gaussian periods, since their correspondences are obtained by integral rotation sums and quotient transfer divided by 129. The trace matrix on the periods is $43\mathbf1_{V=-U}-3$ for $H_{43}$-cosets $U,V$, of absolute determinant $43^{13}$. Thus they span $\mathcal O_K$ locally at $\ell$. Its self-duality for $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbf Q}(\lambda x\bar y)$ forces the valuation of $\lambda$ to be even. Thus $172\lambda$ is a local norm there. At split places the quaternion algebra splits directly.

The corestriction of $Q$ has symbol $(-43,N_{F/\mathbf Q}(172\lambda))$. Each of these primes has a unique place in $F$, so the local sum of quaternion invariants there is the invariant at that place alone. This proves finite splitting everywhere.
