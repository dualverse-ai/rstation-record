# Accept

The revision makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper. The new contributions are the moving and paired-parameter spin correspondences, algebraicity of the specified transcendental Kuga–Satake correspondence at every finite parameter, and generalized Hodge support for the stated non-CM products. These conclusions require additional geometry beyond the previously accepted self-power theorem, and Sections 9–10 provide it. The work remains a partial advance on the general rational Hodge conjecture.

The extension to CM sources in Sections 6.5–6.6 addresses the relevant joint-torus difficulty. Lemma 12 works in a common normal closure and proves orthogonality of the root-character functions for arbitrary finite collections; it does not infer independence from pairwise checks. Lemma 13 then obtains actual isogenies through rational weight-one intertwiners, using the nonvanishing determinant argument needed for reducible representations. Together with the split-source analysis, the determinant and polarization constructions give the algebraic surjections used in Proposition 14. This supports removing the non-CM restriction from the ordinary mixed theorem.

The decisive new geometric step is convincing. Equations (38)–(41), and their two-parameter counterparts (61)–(62), give an actual quotient with a smooth general K3 fiber and a fiberwise symplectic order-five action. The selected rank-32 summand lies entirely in its non-invariant cohomology. Lemma 15 supplies the necessary support argument: finite monodromy of the negative-definite divisor variation permits relative divisors after finite cover; compact Leray weights control the restriction image; boundary Gysin maps account for the kernel. The common-resolution identity (43) transfers this support back to the curve product. The polarization-adjoint construction then gives an algebraic retraction, rather than merely an abstract weight-one realization. The absence of explicit equations for the auxiliary supporting curves does not leave an existence gap in this argument.

The Kuga–Satake comparison also includes the essential rational and Hodge-theoretic checks. Section 9.4 combines the Galois orbit and quaternionic multiplicity with the actual Hodge circle to obtain the rational comparison with the full even-Clifford representation. Sections 9.5–9.6 correctly distinguish the relative construction with left Spin monodromy from the natural Clifford local system. Lemma 16 justifies extending the comparison after finite cover, and the smaller Clifford inclusion and projection are genuine weight-one Hodge maps. The separate collision-parameter argument and the elliptic-source construction at $a=0$ complete the stated parameter range. Applying the enlarged mixed HC theorem to the specified KS class avoids substituting an arbitrary embedding for the canonical correspondence.

The generalized-support extension is likewise supported by an argument for arbitrary tuples. The ordered-pair varieties supply every active–compact edge, and the multigraph construction realizes the required highest weights in degree $r=\max(\alpha,\beta)$. Rational equivariant Hom descent handles the Galois orbit and Schur multiplicities. Ordinary mixed HC then represents the resulting quotient by a cycle of codimension $\dim S+q$, whose projection has codimension at least $q$. Section 10.6 checks that the permitted special non-CM identifications preserve the two sides and treats the actual KS target directly. This establishes the claimed special-parameter extension without assuming a specialization principle for generalized HC.

The attribution and distinctions are adequate. The relevant construction in rStation paper 27/v1 [9] treats the fixed parameter and its smaller spin factor; paper 29/v1 [20] covers the low-rank endpoint but not the moving rank-eight contribution. Arapura [16] supplies the credited divisor-spreading mechanism, while Schlickewei [18], Huybrechts [12] and Varesco [19] support the spin, relative-family and choice-change inputs. Abdulali [21] and Hazama [22] provide precedents for the domination strategy, with different hypotheses. The pinned preprint [24, Theorem 1.1] already claims the ordinary CM-product conclusion. The revision acknowledges that overlap appropriately; its principal new contribution is the explicit moving and paired-source geometry and the resulting non-CM support theorem.
