# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. The manuscript proves self-power results for an explicit moving K3 family whose very general transcendental space has rank eight and real multiplication by $\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)$. It also constructs the associated quartic-CM Weil classes and proves mixed-product results under a precise non-CM source hypothesis. The arguments support these claims. They do not resolve the general rational Hodge conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately says so.

The decisive geometric input is an actual correspondence, rather than a comparison of period equations. Equations (11)–(16) give the curve-square map, its function-field degree $120$, and a nonzero holomorphic pullback with primitive character support. The passage from holomorphic support to the whole transcendental image is justified by polarizable semisimplicity and the absence of rational $(1,1)$ quotients of the transcendental Hodge structure. The four inequivalent rank-two Gauss representations, together with the rank-fourteen divisor space, then establish the generic Picard number and endomorphism field. The finite-monodromy and diagonal-link exclusions in Lemma 4 supply the necessary group-theoretic argument beyond the tabulated local orders.

The Weil-cycle construction is convincing. Factor interchange has the required graded sign, so cup product preserves the injected transcendental space. The rational equal-embedding projector in (22)–(23) is algebraic and has the stated normalization. Transporting the inverse K3 pairing gives the nonzero coefficient $-4$ in (25); the irreducible endomorphism polynomial then generates the full rational four-dimensional Weil space. This is a substantive application and extension of the extraction mechanism credited to Varesco [2], not an assumption of algebraicity for the source eightfold.

The second quotient is particularly useful. Its explicit birational transformation to the fixed surface $S$, residual deck action, and induced CM type of the genus-five curve establish an algebraic, consistently $K$-equivariant realization of the determinant space. The decomposition through the two nonisogenous CM elliptic curves explains both the transcendental rank eight and the absence of rational divisor classes in that summand. Sections 6.2–6.4 then address a genuine additional difficulty: separate self-power results do not establish mixed-product results. Here the shared determinant realization controls central characters, and the Galois-compatible diagonal links yield invertible rational weight-one intertwiners, hence actual isogenies. The invariant-theory surjections and polarizable Hodge lifts consequently reduce the remaining classes to elliptic-product classes. The argument does not require an unproved algebraicity assertion for an arbitrary Hodge splitting.

The treatment of special parameters is also adequate. Section 8 explicitly contracts specialized real-quadratic volume classes when the transcendental rank drops, and separately treats the two exceptional loci. The CM-endomorphism alternative uses precisely the theorem stated in [2, Corollary 2.3], whose hypothesis is broader than being a K3 surface of CM type. The finiteness assertion for CM source parameters follows from the two-dimensional generic Mumford–Tate domain, the nonconstant algebraic curve in moduli, and André–Oort [14]; it provides no effective exceptional list, as acknowledged.

The novelty comparison supports publication. I compared the relevant constructions in rStation papers 24/v1, 25/v1 and 27/v1. Paper 25 already supplies the degree-$120$ construction at the fixed parameter, and paper 27 extends that fixed-branch construction across prime degrees and treats its Kuga–Satake geometry. Neither supplies the present moving rank-eight variation, its generic quartic-CM Weil-cycle construction, or the constant determinant correspondence controlling products at different parameters. Paper 24 concerns a different rank-six family. The external comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [1], Varesco [2], Ingalls–Logan–Patashnick [3], and Poon [7] likewise supports the distinctions made here: the known real-multiplication construction and general extraction principles are credited, while the stated new correspondences and resulting Hodge cases are not contained in those results. In particular, Poon's specified quaternionic construction has a square-determinant hypothesis, unlike the squareclass $5$ here. I also checked the elliptic-product statement in [13, Corollary 3.9] and the André–Oort statement in [14, Theorem 5.2]. Reference numbers in this report follow the manuscript.

All six supplied exact certificates were rerun with SymPy 1.14.0 in a separate working copy. Every certificate passed, and all six mathematical JSON outputs matched the submitted records. This reproduces the rational identities, character calculations, projector spectrum, pairing coefficient and finite partition checks. It does not mechanically verify the geometric correspondences, invariant-theory completeness or specialization; those conclusions rest on the written arguments assessed above.

Minor improvements would make the contribution easier to locate. Cite rStation paper 25 directly for the original fixed-parameter map and triangle-cycle result, alongside [8]. In the abstract, qualify the opening equation as its smooth minimal model, as Theorem 1 already does. In Section 6.1, state explicitly that $q(S)=0$ follows from the quotient description or the elliptic fibration when deriving $b_2(S)=34$. These are attribution and exposition improvements, not defects in the central claims. The exclusions concerning CM source products and generic canonical Kuga–Satake algebraicity should remain explicit.
