# Non-split Weil families over Q(sqrt(-p)), p = 5 mod 8, and over Q(sqrt(-p1 p2)): a discriminant formula and the Hodge conjecture for their powers

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T16:03:37.862349Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/174/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We construct non-split Weil families with full special-unitary monodromy over two infinite sequences of imaginary quadratic fields: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})$ for every prime $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p_1p_2})$ for primes $p_1 \equiv 3$, $p_2 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ with $\gcd(\frac{p_1-1}{2},\frac{p_2-1}{2}) = 1$ and $(p_2/p_1) = -1$, for instance $D = 35, 51, 87, 115, 119, 123$. They are balanced metacyclic Kummer families (rStation paper 133) with conductor $m = 4p$ or $m = p_1p_2$ and orbit seeds equal to the two coprime factors of $m$. They are one-parameter families of abelian varieties of unbounded dimension $2(r-1)$, $r = \varphi(m)/2$. Paper 133 contains the two smallest cases, $m = 20$ and $m = 15$. First, the Weil classes on every member are algebraic: the branch weights pair off with their negatives, so Fermat transfer applies with no join argument. Second, we prove a closed formula for the discriminant of any two-cone, two-seed datum, obtained by extending the Seifert form to a nondegenerate form on the cocycle space. It gives discriminant class $[2]$, respectively $[p_1]$, so the families are non-split exactly under the stated congruence conditions, with local obstructions at $\{2,p\}$, respectively $\{p_1,p_2\}$. The formula also shows that at these conductors no balanced two-cone datum, with any number of regular seeds, is non-split over the remaining fields. Third, the complexified connected monodromy group is $\mathrm{SL}_{2(r-1)}$, by a transvection, an irreducibility lemma and Kantor's theorem modulo a prime, so the very general Hodge group is the full special unitary group. Fourth, the very general member satisfies the Hodge conjecture and the generalized Hodge conjecture for all its powers. Each family is a curve in a Weil moduli space of dimension $(r-1)^2$, so the result covers a thin set. A supplement with exact checks is included.
