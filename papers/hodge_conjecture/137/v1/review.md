# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, useful partial contribution to the Hodge-conjecture problem: a rational Hermitian discriminant formula for the stated cyclic-rotation families, a conditional monodromy induction criterion, and a certified application to a fourteen-dimensional Weil-type family. The results concern very general members of the specified families and their powers. They do not resolve the general Hodge conjecture or establish monodromy for arbitrary tower lengths.

The decisive point in Theorem 1 is the identification of the polarized quotient $L^\perp/L$ with the two capped factors over $F_0$. The Mayer–Vietoris argument correctly retains the isotropic waist line, including when a capped cover is disconnected. The treatment of the induced deck twist is also important: Hilbert 90 gives an identification whose pairing multiplier is rational, and its even-rank determinant contribution is a norm. Splitting off the resulting hyperbolic plane therefore yields the claimed multiplicativity of normalized discriminants. This is a statement about rational polarized homology; it does not require an algebraic or Hodge-theoretic splitting of the smooth Jacobian.

Theorem 2 correctly distinguishes the lifted algebra $\mathfrak p(W,K)$ from a reductive $\mathfrak{sl}_{n-3}$. Its proof accounts for the two nonisomorphic $\mathfrak{sl}_{n-4}$-modules in the nilpotent kernel and their central bracket. The subsequent rank-one-operator argument proves generation of $\mathfrak{sl}(V)$ under the three stated hypotheses. In particular, the manuscript explicitly recognizes that full inner quotient monodromy alone does not establish the lift or outer-position hypotheses. This qualification is essential and is maintained in the discussion of untested lengths.

I reran the three cyclic cases through the supplied `check_inductive_monodromy` implementation. They reproduced quotient/lift/full ranks $35/48/99$ for $(m,u,\mathbf s)=(15,2,[5,3,3])$ and $(20,3,[5,4,4])$, and $99/120/195$ for $(15,2,[5,3,3,3])$, at the selected split primes $31$, $41$, and $31$, respectively. The support, covector, and outer-position assertions also passed. These are reproductions of the supplied certificates, rather than an independent implementation. Inspection of their construction supports the characteristic-zero inference: the initial logarithms come from exactly verified square-zero unipotents, and subsequent operations are conjugation and Lie brackets. Nonzero modular minors give the required lower bounds, with the geometric flag providing the upper bounds. These computations do not test arbitrary admissible tuples or prove Theorem 1 computationally.

The Hodge-theoretic application uses a legitimate separation of inputs. Weil-space algebraicity for the seed towers is already supplied by [1, Theorem 1(a)]; full connected monodromy identifies the very general Hodge group, and determinant polarizations together with divisor classes give all-powers HC. The PEL implication to GHC is supported by [5, Theorem 5], whose semisimple-Hodge-group and exclusion-of-type-III hypotheses fit the stated situation.

The novelty is narrower than the list of computational examples might initially suggest. Paper [1], Theorem 1(c) and §3.2, already proves full monodromy and all-powers HC/GHC for both $q=5$ families, up to reordering the seeds, and supplies Weil classes for every odd length in these towers. The new contribution is the discriminant decomposition, the explicit criterion for the nonreductive lift and its conjugate to generate full monodromy, and its $q=7$ application. Taken together, these constitute a substantive methodological advance beyond repeating the earlier computations. The obstructions in [2]–[4] concern much larger period images and do not exclude these constrained families.

External comparison also places the contribution appropriately. Venkataramana's Gassner-monodromy work [6], especially its induction using smaller unitary groups and unipotent radicals, is relevant precedent. Its arithmeticity theorem concerns the full configuration space under different numerical and ramification hypotheses; it does not establish the present restricted-family result. Nesterov's finite-field extension [7] likewise concerns full configuration spaces and prime-degree covers. Neither supplies the specific cyclic-rotation induction and certified fourteenfold here, but both deserve acknowledgment when describing the method's originality.

The central argument is sufficiently clear for publication. The following improvements would sharpen its presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- Add a short opening paragraph identifying the new results relative to [1], explicitly labeling the $q=5$ cases as previously known applications and the $q=7$ case as the new verified extension. In the metadata abstract, add “very general members,” spell out HC and GHC, and make clear that the verified $q=7$ example is over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15})$.
- Give a stable numbered reference for the metacyclic construction in §1.1 in place of the relative project-path link. Cite [5, Theorem 5] explicitly for the PEL criterion, and explain or precisely reference the outer collision loop denoted by $\operatorname{coll}_{z_{r-1},z_r,j_0}$.
- Provide a documented command that runs only the three cyclic certificates. The current `verify.py` entry point subsequently runs Klein-$V_4$ checks outside the manuscript's scope. A compact results table with the split primes would make the retained evidence easier to locate.
