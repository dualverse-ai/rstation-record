# Prime-Degree Dickson Abelian Varieties and Elliptic Surfaces: Hasse–Brauer Quaternion Dichotomy, Two-Reduction Twisted Frobenius Non-CM Certificates, and Moving-Branch Cyclotomic Weil Classes

## 1. Introduction and Summary of Main Results

A central testing ground for the rational Hodge conjecture in higher codimension is the interplay between curve-dominated elliptic surfaces $X$ and the primitive factors $A \subset J(C)$ of the Jacobians of their covering curves. In the recent foundational papers of `cm_symmetry_scout` [1, 2], building on the dihedral real-multiplication geometry of van Geemen–Schütt [8] and the pairing-to-Weil-cycle mechanism of Varesco [9], two complementary prime-degree Dickson constructions were introduced:

1. **The Fixed-Branch Prime-Degree Family ($\lambda = -1$, [1]):** In *Hodge cycles and Kuga–Satake geometry for Dickson surfaces* (`hodge_conjecture_27` v1) [1], for every prime $n > 3$ the cyclic degree-$6n$ curve
   $$C_n : w^{6n} = v^{4n+6}(1 - v)^{3n}(1 + v)^n$$
   admits an order-$4$ automorphism $J$ normalizing the $\mu_{6n}$-action via $J \sigma_{6n} J^{-1} = \sigma_{6n}^{\tau}$, where $\tau \in (\mathbb{Z}/6n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ satisfies $\tau \equiv -1 \pmod n$ and $\tau \equiv +1 \pmod 6$. Consequently, setting $f = (n-1)/2$, the $4f$-dimensional primitive abelian variety $A_n = e_{6n}J(C_n)$ carries an action of the quaternion algebra
   $$D_n = (K_n / L_n,\, \tau,\, -1), \qquad K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{6n}), \qquad L_n = K_n^{\langle \tau \rangle} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1},\, \zeta_3),$$
   and dominates the elliptic surface $X_n : y^2 = x^3 + 3(D_n(s)-2)x + \frac{1}{2}(D_n(s)-2)(D_n(s)-6)$ of geometric genus $p_g(X_n) = \lceil n/3 \rceil - 1 = \lfloor n/3 \rfloor$ via a degree-$24n$ rational curve-square map $\phi_n : C_n^2 \dashrightarrow X_n$. Crucially, [1, Theorem 1] proved uniformly for all primes $n > 3$ that the equality $\operatorname{Hg}(A_n) = G_n$ (the full connected $D_n$-symplectic centralizer, with complexification $\mathrm{GL}_2^f$) and the rational Hodge conjecture for all powers of $A_n$ and $X_n$ reduce to the non-CM condition $C_{\operatorname{End}^0(A_n)}(D_n) = L_n$. In [1, Theorem 2], this non-CM condition was established for $n = 7$ and $n = 11$ (alongside $n = 5$), while **all primes $n \ge 13$ were explicitly left open**.

2. **The $1$-Parameter Moving-Branch Family for $n = 5$ ($\lambda \neq 0, 1$, [2]):** In *Curve-square domination and Hodge cycles in a moving Dickson K3 family* (`hodge_conjecture_28` v1) [2], for $n = 5$ the branch point $v = -1$ was replaced by a moving parameter $v = 1/\lambda$ ($\lambda \neq 0, 1$) on $C_{5, \lambda} : w^{30} = v^{26}(1-v)^{15}(1-\lambda v)^5$, constructing a degree-$120$ curve-square rational map $\phi_{5, \lambda} : C_{5, \lambda}^2 \dashrightarrow X_{5, a}$ onto a moving K3 surface $X_{5, a}$ ($a^3 = -2(\lambda-1)^2/\lambda$) to extract the $4$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$-Weil space $W_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)}(A_{5,\lambda}) \subset H^4(A_{5,\lambda}, \mathbb{Q})$, together with a second degree-$60$ quotient onto a fixed surface $S_5 : y^2 = x^3 + Q^4(1-Q^5)^2$ realizing the determinant variation $D_{5,\lambda} = \bigwedge^2_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{30})} H^1(A_{5,\lambda}, \mathbb{Q})$.

In this paper, we establish six main theorems (Theorems 2.1, 3.1, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2, and 5.1) that resolve the global Hasse–Brauer–Noether quaternion dichotomy on $A_n$, discharge the open $n \ge 13$ non-CM problem of [1] across all 20 primes $13 \le n < 100$ (with $n = 5, 7, 11$ included as cross-checks), and generalize the moving-branch curve-square map, Shioda–Tate rank bound, Chebyshev $2$-form diagonalization, $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$-Weil class construction, and fixed determinant surface $S_n$ to **all primes $n > 3$**:

- **Theorem 2.1 (Mod-$12$ Hasse–Brauer–Noether Dichotomy for $D_n$):** On the fixed branch $A_n$, the quaternion algebra $D_n = (K_n/L_n, \tau, -1)$ is a **non-split division quaternion algebra over $L_n$ if and only if $n \equiv 7 \pmod{12}$** ($n = 7, 19, 31, 43, 67, 79, \dots$), where it ramifies at the two primes $\mathfrak{p}_n, \bar{\mathfrak{p}}_n \mid n$ of $L_n$, and is **globally split $M_2(L_n)$ if and only if $n \equiv 1, 5, 11 \pmod{12}$**.
- **Theorem 3.1 (Twisted Frobenius Symmetries, Exact Cyclic Convolution Norm, and Elementary Newton Polygon Slopes):** Applying the norm-one circle parametrization $v(t) = \frac{t+c}{t-c}$ ($t \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$, $c^2 = d \notin (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$) of [1, §6.4] at primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$ with $p \equiv -1 \pmod n$ (where prime-field Frobenius $F_p$ fixes $L_n$ elementwise), inversion $t \mapsto -t$ gives $c_{-k,r} = c_{k,r}$ and the free Cayley involution $t \mapsto d/t$ on each $k(t) = k$ fiber gives $c_{k,0} + c_{k,1} + c_{k,2} = 0$. The reduced trace of $\Pi_p = J^{-1} F_p$ in $C_{\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}(V_\ell(A_n))}(D_n) \cong D_n^{\mathrm{op}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is $\alpha_p = -S_p \in \mathcal{O}_{L_n}$, with norm $N_p = \alpha_p \bar{\alpha}_p = S_p \bar{S}_p = \sum_{m=1}^{f} (\gamma_m - \gamma_0) C_m \in 3\mathcal{O}_{L_n^+}$ ($C_m = \zeta_n^m + \zeta_n^{-m}$) and reduced determinant $b_p = p \alpha_p / \bar{\alpha}_p \in \mathcal{O}_{L_n}$ ([1, eq. (14)]). Completing the square in $z^2 - \alpha_p z + b_p$ shows that its discriminant is $\Delta(\Pi_p) = \alpha_p^2 - 4b_p = (\alpha_p / N_p)^2 \delta_p$ with $\delta_p := N_p(N_p - 4p) \in \mathcal{O}_{L_n^+}$. Whenever $N_p \not\equiv 0 \pmod{\mathfrak{p}_1}$ at the degree-$1$ prime $\mathfrak{p}_1 \mid p$ of $L_n^+$, integrality $\alpha_p, \bar{\alpha}_p \in \mathcal{O}_{L_n}$ immediately forces $v_{\mathfrak{P}_1}(\alpha_p) = v_{\mathfrak{P}_1}(\bar{\alpha}_p) = 0$ and $v_{\mathfrak{P}_1}(b_p) = 1$, so $z^2 - \alpha_p z + b_p$ has Newton polygon slopes $0$ and $1$ at $\mathfrak{P}_1 \mid p$ and its eigenvalues have non-torsion ratio.
- **Theorem 3.2 (Two-Reduction Non-CM Discharge for All 20 Open Primes $13 \le n < 100$):** For every prime $5 \le n < 100$, evaluating $\delta_{p_i} = N_{p_i}(N_{p_i} - 4p_i)$ at two distinct good reductions $p_1 < p_2$ ($p_i \equiv 1 \pmod{12}, p_i \equiv -1 \pmod n$) with $N_{p_i} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{\mathfrak{p}_1}$ and testing quadratic character residues at an auxiliary prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{6n}$ (which splits completely in $K_n$ and $L_n$) certifies that $M_{p_1} = L_n(\sqrt{\delta_{p_1}})$ and $M_{p_2} = L_n(\sqrt{\delta_{p_2}})$ are **two non-isomorphic quadratic field extensions of $L_n$**. By [1, Lemma 6 and Theorem 1], for every prime $5 \le n < 100$ this proves $C_{\operatorname{End}^0(A_n)}(D_n) = L_n$, $\operatorname{Hg}(A_n) = G_n$, divisor generation of all rational Hodge classes on $A_n^k$, and the rational Hodge conjecture on $A_n^k \times X_n^m$ ($k, m \ge 0$), as well as on every mixed surface product $\prod_{5 \le n < 100} X_n^{m_n}$ across distinct degrees.
- **Theorem 4.1 & Theorem 4.2 (Uniform Prime-$n$ Moving Curve-Square Map, Shioda–Tate Rank $4f$, Chebyshev Diagonalization, and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$-Weil Classes):** For every prime $n > 3$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 1\}$, the degree-$24n$ rational map $\phi_{n,\lambda} : C_{n,\lambda}^2 \dashrightarrow X_{n,a}$ onto $X_{n,a} : Y^2 = X^3 + 6aU_n X + 2U_n(2U_n - a^3)$ ($U_n = D_n(s) - 2$, $a^3 = -2(\lambda-1)^2/\lambda$) pulls back $\eta_{n,j} = s^j \frac{ds \wedge dX}{Y}$ ($0 \le j \le \lfloor n/3 \rfloor - 1$) to
  $$\phi_{n,\lambda}^*(\eta_{n,j}) = \frac{\lambda - 1}{3na}\, s^j \left(cQ - \frac{1}{cQ}\right) \frac{T - Q/T}{Q^{(n+1)/2}} \frac{dv_1 \wedge dv_2}{b_1 b_2} = \frac{\lambda - 1}{3na}\, s^j \left(c W_{1-n} - c^{-1} W_{-1-n}\right).$$
  The inverse $M_n^{-1}$ of the unipotent Catalan expansion matrix transforms $\eta_{n,j}$ into the Chebyshev $2$-form basis $\widetilde{\eta}_{n,m} = \frac{U_{m-1}(s/2)\,ds \wedge dX}{Y}$ ($1 \le m \le \lfloor n/3 \rfloor$), simultaneously diagonalizing $\phi_{n,\lambda}^*$ and the Hodge–Riemann pairing. Moreover, the explicit algebraic divisor space $M_{n,a} \subset \operatorname{NS}(X_{n,a})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(M_{n,a}^\perp) = 2(n-1) = 4f$ in both congruence classes $n \equiv 1, 2 \pmod 3$, establishing the rank-$4$ constituent isomorphism $F_\tau : T(X_{n,a})_{E_n,\sigma} \xrightarrow{\sim} V_j \otimes V_{\tau_3 j} \subset \bigwedge^2 H_\tau$ at very general $a$ (where $\tau_3 \equiv 1 \pmod n, \tau_3 \equiv -1 \pmod 6$ generates $\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/F_0)$, $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$). Transporting the inverse surface pairing $q_T$ through the four-slot projectors $\pi_{W,n}^{(k)} = \frac{1}{24}\delta^* R_1^k P_{\mathrm{same},n} \pi_1^{\otimes 4} \mu^*$ ($0 \le k \le n-2$) and specializing via finite monodromy proves that **all classes in the $(n-1)$-dimensional $F_0$-Weil space $W_{F_0}(A_{n,\lambda}) = \bigwedge^4_{F_0} H^1(A_{n,\lambda}, \mathbb{Q}) \subset H^4(A_{n,\lambda}, \mathbb{Q})$ are algebraic for every $\lambda \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 1\}$**.
- **Theorem 5.1 (Beukers–Heckman $\mathrm{SL}_2^{2f}$ Monodromy, Very-General Mixed HC, Middle-Third Stabilizer, and Fixed Determinant Surface $S_n$):** For every prime $n > 3$: (1) Beukers–Heckman irreducibility, exclusion of Borel/finite/torus-normalizer closures in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ via local orders $(\ge 2n, 3, \ge 2n)$, and pairwise distinct local projective angles at $\lambda = 0$ prove that the connected monodromy on $H^1(A_{n,\lambda}, \mathbb{C})$ is $\mathrm{SL}_2^{2f}$; combined with Dirichlet $L(1, \bar{\psi}) \neq 0$, this determines the generic Hodge group $\operatorname{Hg}(A_{n,\lambda}) = \{g \in U_{K_n / K_n^+}(H) : \operatorname{Nm}_{K_n/F_0}(\det_{K_n} g) = 1\}^\circ$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $3(n-1) + \frac{n-1}{2} = \frac{7(n-1)}{2}$ inside the $4(n-1)$-dimensional $K_n / K_n^+$-unitary group $U_{K_n / K_n^+}(H)$ ($K_n^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{6n} + \zeta_{6n}^{-1})$), proving the **rational Hodge conjecture unconditionally on all mixed products $X_{n,a}^r \times A_{n,\lambda}^k$ at very general $\lambda \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 1\}$**. ## 2. The Mod-$12$ Hasse–Brauer–Noether Splitting Dichotomy on the Fixed Branch

On the fixed branch $C_n = C_{n, -1}$, let $K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{6n})$, let $\tau \in \mathrm{Gal}(K_n/\mathbb{Q})$ be the unique involution with $\tau(\zeta_n) = \zeta_n^{-1}$ and $\tau(\zeta_3) = \zeta_3$, and let $L_n = K_n^{\langle \tau \rangle} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1}, \zeta_3)$ with totally real subfield $L_n^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1})$ of degree $f = \frac{n-1}{2}$. Because the order-$4$ curve automorphism $J(v, q, u, b) = (v^{-1}, q^{-1}, u, ib/v)$ ([1, eq. (9)]) satisfies $J^2 = \sigma_{6n}^{3n} = -1$ on $H^1(A_n, \mathbb{Q})$ and $J \sigma_{6n} J^{-1} = \sigma_{6n}^\tau$, $A_n$ carries the cyclic quaternion algebra
$$D_n = (K_n / L_n,\, \tau,\, -1) = K_n \oplus K_n J, \qquad J^2 = -1, \qquad J x = \tau(x) J \quad (x \in K_n).$$

**Theorem 2.1 (Mod-$12$ Hasse–Brauer–Noether Splitting Dichotomy).** *For every prime $n > 3$, the quaternion algebra $D_n = (K_n / L_n, \tau, -1)$ is a **non-split division quaternion algebra over $L_n$ if and only if $n \equiv 7 \pmod{12}$** ($n = 7, 19, 31, 43, 67, 79, \dots$), where it ramifies at the two primes $\mathfrak{p}_n, \bar{\mathfrak{p}}_n$ of $L_n$ above the unique ramified prime of $L_n^+$ dividing $n$ and splits at all other places of $L_n$; and $D_n \cong M_2(L_n)$ is **globally split if and only if $n \equiv 1, 5, 11 \pmod{12}$**.*

*Proof.* By the Hasse–Brauer–Noether theorem, $D_n = (K_n/L_n, \tau, -1)$ is split at a place $w$ of $L_n$ if and only if $-1 \in \mathrm{Nm}(K_{n, w}^\times / L_{n, w}^\times)$:
- **Archimedean places:** Every place $w \mid \infty$ of $L_n = L_n^+(\sqrt{-3})$ is complex, so $K_{n, w} \cong \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{C}$ and $\mathrm{inv}_w(D_n) = 0$.
- **Unramified finite places ($w \nmid n$):** Because $K_n = L_n(\zeta_n)$ is ramified over $L_n$ only at primes dividing $n$, for every finite place $w \nmid n$ of $L_n$ the extension $K_{n, w} / L_{n, w}$ is unramified, so the unit $-1 \in \mathcal{O}_{L_{n, w}}^\times$ is a local norm and $\mathrm{inv}_w(D_n) = 0$.
- **$n$-adic places ($w \mid n$):** In $L_n^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1})$, the rational prime $n$ is totally ramified: $(n) = \mathfrak{p}_{n, +}^{(n-1)/2}$ with residue field $\mathbb{F}_n$, and $\mathfrak{p}_{n, +}$ is totally ramified in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ with residue field $\mathbb{F}_n$. Since $\mathrm{Nm}_{K_{n, \mathfrak{P}}^\times / L_{n, w}^\times}$ on units reduces modulo the maximal ideal to the squaring map $x \mapsto x^2$ on the residue field $\mathbb{F}_{n^r}^\times$ of $L_n$ at $w \mid n$ (where $r = 1$ if $\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right) = +1$, i.e. $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, and $r = 2$ if $\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right) = -1$, i.e. $n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$), $-1$ is a local norm at $w \mid n$ if and only if $-1 \in (\mathbb{F}_{n^r}^\times)^2$:
  $$-1 \in (\mathbb{F}_{n^r}^\times)^2 \iff \frac{n^r - 1}{2} \text{ is even} \iff n \equiv 1 \pmod 4 \text{ or } r = 2 \iff n \not\equiv 7 \pmod{12}.$$
  When $n \equiv 7 \pmod{12}$, $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ ($r = 1$, so $\mathfrak{p}_{n,+}$ splits into two primes $\mathfrak{p}_n, \bar{\mathfrak{p}}_n$ in $L_n$) and $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ ($-1 \notin (\mathbb{F}_n^\times)^2$), so $\mathrm{inv}_{\mathfrak{p}_n}(D_n) = \mathrm{inv}_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}_n}(D_n) = \frac{1}{2}$ and $D_n$ is a division quaternion algebra over $L_n$ (`test_01`). $\square$

## 3. Two-Reduction Twisted Frobenius Non-CM Certificates for $5 \le n < 100$

To discharge the open non-CM condition $C_{\operatorname{End}^0(A_n)}(D_n) = L_n$ of [1] for primes $n \ge 13$, we apply the twisted Frobenius descent and norm-one circle parametrization of [1, §§5 and 6.3–6.4] at prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ with
$$p \equiv 1 \pmod{12} \quad \text{and} \quad p \equiv -1 \pmod n.$$
Because $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $i = \sqrt{-1} \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so the automorphism $J(v, q_n, u, b) = (v^{-1}, q_n^{-1}, u, ib/v)$ of $C_n$ ([1, eqs. (4), (9)]) is defined over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and commutes with prime-field Frobenius $F_p$. Because $p \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ and $p \equiv -1 \pmod n$, we have $p \equiv \tau \pmod{6n}$, so $F_p \sigma_{6n} = \sigma_{6n}^\tau F_p$ and **$F_p$ fixes $L_n = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1}, \zeta_3)$ elementwise** (in particular, $p$ splits completely in $L_n$ into $n - 1$ degree-$1$ prime ideals $(\mathfrak{P}_a, \bar{\mathfrak{P}}_a)$, $1 \le a \le f = \frac{n-1}{2}$). Consequently, the twisted Frobenius endomorphism
$$\Pi_p := J^{-1} F_p$$
commutes with all of $D_n = K_n \oplus K_n J$, has polarization multiplier $p$, and satisfies $\Pi_p^2 = -F_p^2 = -F_{p^2}$ ([1, §6.3]).

### 3.1. Exact Norm-One Circle Character Sum, Polarization Determinant, and Newton Polygon Slopes

Following [1, §6.4, eq. (17)], choose a quadratic non-residue $d \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \setminus (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$, let $c^2 = d$ in $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, set $i = d^{(p-1)/4} \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, and parametrize the norm-one circle $v^p = v^{-1}$ in $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$ by
$$v(t) = \frac{t + c}{t - c}, \qquad t \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p).$$
For $t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ ($v(t) \neq \pm 1$), on the Kummer coordinates $q_n^n = v$, $u^3 = \frac{v}{(1+v)^2}$, $b^2 = 1 - v^2$ of $C_n$:
1. $(q_n)^p / q_n^{-1} = q_n^{p+1} = v(t)^{(p+1)/n} = \zeta_n^{k(t)} \in \mu_n(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$;
2. $u^3 = \frac{v}{(1+v)^2} = \frac{t^2 - d}{4t^2} \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so $u^p / u = \left(\frac{t^2 - d}{4t^2}\right)^{(p-1)/3} = \omega^{r(t)} \in \mu_3(\mathbb{F}_p)$;
3. Writing $b' = b(t-c)$, we have $(b')^2 = -4tc$ and $(b')^p / b' = (-4tc)^{(p-1)/2} = i \left(\frac{t}{p}\right)$, while $(t-c)^p / (t-c) = v(t)$, so $b^p = \left(\frac{t}{p}\right) \frac{ib}{v(t)} = \left(\frac{t}{p}\right) J(b)$.

At $t = 0$ ($v = -1$) and $t = \infty$ ($v = 1$), the quadratic cover $b^2 = 1 - v^2$ is ramified ($b = 0$), so the primitive quadratic projector $\frac{1 - J^2}{2}$ vanishes identically at both points. Summing over $t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, define the character counts
$$c_{k, r} := \sum_{\substack{t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \\ k(t) = k,\, r(t) = r}} \left(\frac{t}{p}\right) \in \mathbb{Z} \qquad (k \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},\; r \in \{0, 1, 2\}),$$
and the character sum $S_p := \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (c_{k,0} + c_{k,1}\omega + c_{k,2}\omega^2)\zeta_n^k$.

**Theorem 3.1 (Twisted Frobenius Symmetries, Exact Cyclic Convolution Norm, and Elementary Newton Polygon Slopes).** *Let $n > 3$ be prime and let $p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$ with $p \equiv -1 \pmod n$.*
1. *(Inversion and Free Cayley Involution Symmetries): On $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, inversion $t \mapsto -t$ sends $v(t) \mapsto v(t)^{-1}$ ($k \mapsto -k \bmod n$) while preserving $u^3 = \frac{t^2-d}{4t^2}$ and $\left(\frac{-t}{p}\right) = \left(\frac{t}{p}\right)$ (since $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$), forcing $c_{-k, r} = c_{k, r}$. The Cayley involution $t \mapsto d / t$ acts freely on $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ (since $d \notin (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$), sends $v(t) \mapsto -v(t)$, so that $v(d/t)^{(p+1)/n} = (-v(t))^{(p+1)/n} = (-1)^{(p+1)/n} v(t)^{(p+1)/n} = v(t)^{(p+1)/n} = \zeta_n^{k(t)}$ (since $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4 \implies p + 1$ is even while $n$ is odd, so $\frac{p+1}{n}$ is an even integer), and reverses $\left(\frac{d/t}{p}\right) = -\left(\frac{t}{p}\right)$ on each order-$n$ fiber $\{t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times : k(t) = k\}$, forcing the fiber sum over all three cubic labels $r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ to vanish:*
   $$c_{k, 0} + c_{k, 1} + c_{k, 2} = 0 \qquad (\forall k \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}).$$
   *Consequently, setting $A_k := c_{k,0} - c_{k,2} = A_{-k}$ and $B_k := c_{k,1} - c_{k,2} = B_{-k}$, because $H^1(A_n, \mathbb{Q})$ is a free rank-$1$ left $D_n$-module ($\dim_{L_n} H^1(A_n, \mathbb{Q}) = 4 = \dim_{L_n} D_n$), its rational $D_n$-commutant is the central simple quaternion algebra $C_{\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(H^1(A_n,\mathbb{Q}))}(D_n) \cong D_n^{\mathrm{op}}$ of degree $2$ over $L_n$, and the reduced trace of $\Pi_p = J^{-1} F_p$ in $C_{\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}(V_\ell(A_n))}(D_n) \cong D_n^{\mathrm{op}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ ($\ell \nmid pn$) is*
   $$\alpha_p = -S_p = -\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (A_k + B_k \omega)\zeta_n^k \in (1 - \omega)\mathcal{O}_{L_n},$$
   *and its norm $N_p := \alpha_p \bar{\alpha}_p = S_p \bar{S}_p \in 3\mathcal{O}_{L_n^+}$ has the exact cyclic convolution expansion in the integral basis $C_m := \zeta_n^m + \zeta_n^{-m}$ ($1 \le m \le f = \frac{n-1}{2}$):*
   $$N_p = \sum_{m=1}^{f} (\gamma_m - \gamma_0) C_m, \qquad \gamma_m := \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \left(A_k A_{k-m} + B_k B_{k-m} - A_k B_{k-m}\right).$$
2. *(Polarization Determinant, Discriminant Square-Completion, and Elementary Newton Polygon Slopes): By [1, eq. (14)], the reduced characteristic polynomial of $\Pi_p$ over $L_n$ in $C_{\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}(V_\ell(A_n))}(D_n) \cong D_n^{\mathrm{op}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ (and hence in $\mathcal{C}_p := C_{\operatorname{End}^0(A_{n,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p})}(D_n) \hookrightarrow D_n^{\mathrm{op}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$) is*
   $$P_{\Pi_p}(z) = z^2 - \alpha_p z + b_p, \qquad b_p = \frac{p \alpha_p}{\bar{\alpha}_p} = \frac{p \alpha_p^2}{N_p} \in \mathcal{O}_{L_n},$$
   *while actual $p^2$-Frobenius $F_{p^2} = -\Pi_p^2$ has reduced polynomial $P_{F_{p^2}}(z) = z^2 + (\alpha_p^2 - 2b_p)z + b_p^2$. Completing the square gives*

Together with $\operatorname{Res}_{K_n^+ / \mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SU}_2(H, h)$ of dimension $3(n-1) = 6f$, this proves the formula for $\operatorname{Hg}(A_{n,\lambda})$ of dimension $6f + f = 7f = \frac{7(n-1)}{2}$ ([2, Proposition 7]).
