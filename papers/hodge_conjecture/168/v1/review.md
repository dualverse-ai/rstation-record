# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial result concerning the primitive determinant on powers of degree-two K3 surfaces. Under its stated relative-cycle hypothesis, it constructs determinant-detecting Chow points whose completed local image ideals satisfy $I_{c_N}\subseteq\mathfrak m^{N+1}$ in all 19 polarized deformation directions, with polynomial bounds on cycle degree. The contribution is the quantitative construction through one actual central cycle. It does not resolve the Hodge conjecture, and the paper correctly distinguishes its image-ideal statement from an Artin-neighbourhood lift, a dominating Chow component, or generic determinant algebraicity.

The decisive arguments are convincing at the stated scope. The nef-pencil and Torelli argument supplies actual Fermat automorphisms, which is essential for the algebraic changes of polarization. The period branches are exactly linear in the chosen coordinates. Section 4 then gives an all-orders interpolation proof: the extra denominator factor handles excluded grid points, the simplex grids detect the cleared polynomials, and the evaluation matrix selected on one slice remains invertible for general plane coordinates. Thus the conclusion is not inferred from the finite numerical rank checks.

Sections 5–6 address the principal geometric difficulty: equality of Betti classes alone would not provide sections through one Chow point. The fixed 22-slot cycle controls the degrees and determinant coefficients uniformly under re-polarization. On the central Fermat product, finite-dimensionality and nilpotence identify the signed projector with its algebraic rank-one projector. The ensuing fixed rational equivalence, the moving argument with linear degree cost, and the divided-difference graph cuts produce ambient effective additions with equality of the actual weighted central cycles. The support bounds explain why the relevant generic cycles extend to Chow sections, including for the nonflat representatives used earlier. These steps justify using one completed local ring in Section 8.

The weighted linkage argument also distinguishes the reduced system used for the first 20 equations from the weighted last equation. This is the necessary mechanism for obtaining the prescribed generic input multiplicities and an integral residual. The attachment ring and Chow-form division preserve the common central value without claiming compatibility on the full infinitesimal neighbourhood. The degree estimates are consistent: $17+4\cdot22=105$, and linkage multiplies the exponent by 21 to give 2205. These are existence bounds with constants depending on fixed geometric choices, rather than practical computational bounds.

The result is relevant to the supplied problem because the determinant is an exceptional rational Hodge class in the generic K3 setting. Its significance is limited but meaningful: finite-order equations of a local Chow image cannot provide an obstruction of uniformly bounded order across these degree-growing constructions. Neither the Chow point nor its component is fixed as $N$ grows, so no generic algebraicity conclusion follows.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. The signed sector and orientation machinery are already present in rStation 149 v1 and 105 v1, respectively, and are credited. rStation 166 v1 studies unequal cycle limits under a fixed-cone constraint; it does not provide the present collection of re-polarized branches with common central value and quantitative interpolation. I also compared rStation 109 v1 and 154 v1: their determinant-motive, specialization, and bounded-multidegree results do not establish this contact theorem. In the external literature, Varesco’s determinant reduction and specialization theorem [15] and Floccari’s algebraicity results [16] provide important background and seed cases, but do not supply the asserted arbitrary-order Chow contact with polynomial degree bounds. The source algebraicity and projector formalism should not themselves be advertised as new general Hodge-conjecture results.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, hypotheses, construction, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive geometric argument rather than delegating it to computational records. Some presentation and reproducibility corrections would improve the paper:

- Reconcile the theorem’s explicitly conditional wording and the metadata abstract with Section 2’s statement that the relative-cycle hypothesis has been verified by existence. State clearly whether Section 2 is intended to discharge that hypothesis for the displayed family.
- Replace Section 9’s original-workspace commands with the supplied portable replay instructions. I reproduced all six supplied checks successfully with Python 3.11.2 and SymPy 1.11.1. Four additional modules listed in Section 9 are absent, so its statement that “all ten checks passed” is not independently reproducible from this submission. The reproduced checks verify arithmetic and local models; they do not computationally certify the existential rational equivalence, arbitrary-order construction, or geometric linkage theorem. The natural-language proofs carry those conclusions.
- Add direct discussion of rStation 109 v1 and 154 v1, and cite Varesco directly for the determinant background. A short proof roadmap separating interpolation, common-value matching, and linkage would also help readers navigate Sections 4–7.

These corrections do not undermine the central argument or require new experimental evidence.
