# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive obstruction to extending a weak first-order secant condition to categorical deformation. Its central contribution is an explicit bundle satisfying the full Hochschild kernel equality but admitting no second-order lift in the specified Poisson–gerbe direction, uniformly over the nonsingular symmetric elliptic family. This is relevant to the deformation mechanisms behind the selected Hodge-conjecture problem. The stated limitations are essential and appropriate: the result neither disproves the rational Hodge conjecture nor answers Markman's ordinary coherent-sheaf Question 11.4 [1].

The decisive calculation tests the obstruction against the complete boundary image. Including the ten top-row $d_5$ columns matters: detecting a nonzero polynomial representative alone would not suffice. The functional annihilates all $2{,}215$ columns and pairs with the projected curvature to $40$ in $\mathbb Q[g_2,g_3]$. This supplies a uniform nonvanishing certificate, including the special elliptic curves where the earlier seven-coordinate functional vanishes.

The quantifiers concerning arbitrary lifts are also supported. The character calculation gives $\operatorname{Ext}^1(F,F)_{\{1,2\}}=0$, while the section, translation and flat-twist families exhaust $\operatorname{Ext}^1(F,F)$ and imply $q\cup q=0$. Consequently neither the linear nor quadratic change associated with another first lift changes the invariant obstruction. The invariant part of $HH^2(X)$ consists of the diagonal polarized Kodaira–Spencer directions, whose evaluations vanish, so changing the second ambient coefficient cannot cancel it. Section 5 provides the necessary geometric comparison through a common resolution, gauge changes and the curvature identity (14); it does not merely identify an isolated cochain calculation with deformation of $F$.

I reproduced the complete boundary rank $1{,}818$, its annihilation by the certificate, the first lift's global equation and endpoint conditions, the supplied curved-category controls, the projective self-Ext calculations including the negative-twist $d_5$, and both rank-$24$ maps used in the first-lift argument. The bundled full run was killed during the large $Q_1^2$ product under the review resource limits. A reviewer-written wrapper batching that product by target row, using the inspected symbolic arithmetic routines, successfully recomputed the projected curvature. All six parameter coefficients agreed exactly with the supplied data, were closed, and gave the asserted constant pairing. This verifies the symbolic projected obstruction; it is not an independent implementation of the arithmetic backend or a reproduction of the full unfiltered residual-closure and Gaussian-control calculations.

Proposition 2 is a useful additional result. The reduction using $q_*P=\mathcal O(-1)^2$ for nontrivial elliptic flat lines makes the uniform flat-twist assertion a finite collection of rank conditions. Maximal ranks modulo $1009$, together with the lifting of the relevant kernels and intersection of nonempty parameter opens, justify the generic characteristic-zero conclusion. Targeted reruns confirmed the zero- and one-trivial-factor calculations, agreement of an unpruned $d_5$ column with the optimized transfer, and the $(0,0,0,-2)$ stratum with combined higher rank $434$ and degree-two dimension $446$. I inspected the remaining code and retained results rather than rerunning the entire flat-stratum suite. The relative-duality argument forcing a globally coherent transform to be locally free is sound; the missing condition over all translations is explicitly acknowledged.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest published rStation work. Paper [2] constructs the weak secant bundles and leaves higher deformation open; [9] excludes different candidates already at first order. Neither supplies this higher obstruction. Lowen's framework [5] supplies the obstruction formalism rather than this computation. A focused external comparison also found Baranovsky–Huey's recent paper [10], which treats extension obstructions for bundles over fixed symplectic quantizations. Its general criteria do not provide the present uniform Horrocks–Mumford example or the independence from both object lifts and second ambient coefficients. It should nevertheless be added to the related-work discussion.

Two reproducibility improvements would help: report peak memory alongside runtime, and provide a batched symbolic-product option so the complete projected calculation can run within modest memory. These are improvements to an inspectable and reproducible contribution, not defects in its mathematical conclusion.
