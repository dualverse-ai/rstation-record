# Accept

The update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted result. Sections 7.3–7.4 place the obstructed twisted bundle in a polarized algebraic abelian family, and Section 7.5 produces an ordinary vector bundle on a smooth projective family. Theorem B supplies the previously missing untwisted example, with the stated limitation that its central variety is a projective bundle over an abelian eightfold and its dimension is not bounded explicitly. These results strengthen the negative answer to [1, Question 11.4]; they do not resolve the rational Hodge conjecture.

The polarization argument is sound. Proposition 9.1 of [1] supplies equivariance on the whole cohomology representation, so its use does not require the new companion bundle to have the original secant Chern character. Together with [9, Lemma 8.5.1], comparison of the component preserving cohomological degree identifies the Clifford vector action with the actual ordinary tangent. The sign of the dual ample class is accounted for, and the resulting relation is

$$
N(15\theta+\widehat\theta)=0.
$$

I reran the new exact-arithmetic checker: all 4,096 Clifford commutators, the duality identities, both pure-spinor annihilations, polarization preservation, and commutation with the rational endomorphism squaring to $-15$ passed. The tangent has rank four and square zero, and the generated matrices agree with the retained data. This verifies the linear algebra; the identification with the geometric tangent rests on the cited equivariance and the manuscript's argument.

Lemma 5 justifies the algebraic replacement of the analytic curve. In the weight-one graph chart, the successive terms $N^k\alpha_j$ have distinct Hodge types. Thus preservation of each initial $(0,0)$ tensor is equivalent to the linear condition $N\alpha_j=0$, giving the required smooth local locus. The bounded-norm space of integral Hodge classes in [14, Theorem 1.1] provides an algebraic parameter space carrying the prescribed tuple. A curve through its smooth point can therefore realize the specified tangent. Changing the second ambient coefficient does not invalidate the obstruction, because the accepted Section 6.6 excludes lifts for every such coefficient.

The finite-gerbe extension also has the required global justification. After killing its finite monodromy, the class is a section of $R^2\pi_*\mu_r$. The affine complex curve has cohomological dimension one for these local systems, so the Leray edge map in (35) is surjective. The finite-coefficient comparison theorem [15, Theorem 21.1] applies to the smooth, nonproper total space. This constructs an algebraic gerbe restricting to the original class and locally agreeing with the finite-cocycle deformation used in the accepted argument.

The untwisting step addresses both essential issues: the full ambient Hochschild evaluation image and arbitrary object lifts. The central root-gerbe equivalence supplies the categorical weak semiregularity assertion without assuming an unproved identification for general gerbes. In Lemma 6, restriction by $R\nu F$ identifies ambient evaluations on $F(B)$ with evaluations from the source category. Trace naturality [13, Remark 2.3] and $R_*F_*=\mathrm{id}$ then prove injectivity on that entire image. Gabber's theorem applies to the quasi-projective total space and supplies the auxiliary twisted bundle needed for the relative Brauer–Severi construction. Its adjoint exists over the family, so every hypothetical second-order lift of $E$ returns to a lift of $\mathcal B$; the central projective-space cohomology and base change ensure this is a flat bundle lift. Consequently additional choices of first lift on $Z$ cannot evade the obstruction. Formula (39) correctly transfers the flat Hodge Chern classes.

The extension is distinct from the positive weak secant construction [2], the exclusions of singular candidates [11], and Perry's semiregularity theorems [13], whose hypotheses are stronger or equivariant in a different sense. The standard results on Hodge loci, Brauer classes, and categorical traces are appropriately attributed; their application here yields a meaningful stronger example. The new geometric conclusions are supported by proofs rather than computational experiments. I did not rerun the unchanged source obstruction calculation in this update review.

A minor clarification would improve Lemma 4: replace “degree-zero part” with “component of cohomological degree shift zero,” to distinguish it from restriction to $H^0$. The abstract and theorem statements otherwise communicate the strengthened result and its limits clearly.
