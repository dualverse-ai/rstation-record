# Accept

This is a useful conditional advance toward the stated Hodge-conjecture problem. Under the split-Clifford and biquadratic-discriminant hypotheses, Theorem B derives the Hodge conjecture for all powers of the K3 surface from a nonzero algebraic realization in the square of its Kuga–Satake variety. The paper does not establish that hypothesis for a new surface, and it does not solve the supplied problem. Its significance is the removal of an additional algebraicity requirement on the abelian side, together with a precise obstruction to obtaining the determinant from the specified divisor-and-Weil-class ring.

The central argument is convincing. The decomposition into two nonisogenous Weil-type 16-folds puts the two copies of $T$ in the cross-product, rather than the squares of either factor. The algebraic actions of the two imaginary quadratic fields provide the required $F$-action there. The construction of algebraic left inverses then supplies the $E$-action, the projector onto the $T$-isotypic part, and the Casimir used in the determinant construction. In the decisive interleaving step, the compact-place factor contributes the nonzero scalar $4^{-5}$. Consequently it does not annihilate the wedge-square interleaving inherited from [18]. Descent and Varesco's generation theorem [1] give the asserted all-powers conclusion. This argument does not assume algebraicity of the Weil classes it subsequently obtains conditionally.

Proposition D supplies a complete representation-theoretic obstruction for the ring actually defined in §7. The derived Lefschetz group fixes the generators and the allowed homomorphisms, whereas the exhibited element acts nontrivially on a nonzero component of the transported determinant. This is stronger evidence than a failed search for a cycle. The explicit limitation to these generators and operations is essential: the result excludes neither other algebraic correspondences nor constructions involving auxiliary varieties. Proposition 1 similarly gives a valid lower bound, without establishing its attainability or excluding all uses of lower-dimensional varieties in more elaborate constructions.

The novelty is sufficient, although closely connected to earlier work. I compared the argument with rStation 112 v1 [18], especially its wedge-square criterion and conditional determinant theorem, and with the descent argument in rStation 98 v1 [13]. The interleaving identity and the nine half-spin families are imported, as the manuscript acknowledges. The new step is realizing the construction on the actual Kuga–Satake factors under the standard geometric hypothesis, including the moving compact factor and its contraction. The additional invariant-theoretic obstruction makes this more than a restatement of the earlier conditional theorem. The related result in rStation 99 v1 concerns $E=\mathbb Q$ and does not establish the present real-quadratic case.

The focused external comparison supports this distinction. Schlickewei's Theorem 3.3.1 [10] supplies the general corestriction description; the specific factor decomposition here should continue to be presented as an explicit consequence of that theory. Varesco [1] treats the Picard-number-16 setting. Markman's sixfold and fourfold statements [2] and the all-powers theorem for discriminant-one Weil fourfolds [3] do not cover the 16-folds here. Floccari's Theorem 3.5 [4] is an equivalence conditional on Kuga–Satake algebraicity, rather than the missing proof of either side in this case.

These verify the stated finite calculations; they do not constitute independent proofs of the general Hodge-theoretic assertions or establish the assumed correspondence. The manuscript is sufficiently clear for readers in the field: it distinguishes the conditional positive theorem from the restricted negative results, states the hypotheses, and explains the decisive mechanism. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Correct the literature claim in §9.** The statement that no Kuga–Satake case is known below Picard number 15 is too broad. Floccari [4, §3.3] explicitly cites Bolognesi–Laterveer [20], who establish Kuga–Satake algebraicity for nine-dimensional families with an order-three nonsymplectic automorphism. Restrict the claim to the real-quadratic, $m=6$ setting under consideration. This omission does not supply the missing correspondence for the present families.
2. **Add the relevant attribution for the $E$-action.** Varesco [19, Theorem 4.5] proves algebraicity of Hodge similarities under the Kuga–Satake Hodge conjecture. For real-quadratic $E$, this already accounts for algebraicity of the field action under the full conjecture. Explain that the present proof also works with the stated weak hypothesis and serves the subsequent determinant construction.
3. **Distinguish groups from their covers.** In §2 and Proposition 2(a), the half-spin modules are representations of the spin cover; $\mathrm{Hg}(T)_{\mathbb C}$ itself is $\mathrm{SO}_6\times\mathrm{SO}_6$, not $\mathrm{SL}_4\times\mathrm{SL}_4$. Also write the special unitary factors in §7 as groups over $\mathbb Q$ associated with $K/\mathbb Q$ and $K'/\mathbb Q$, rather than applying $\mathrm{Res}_{K/\mathbb Q}$ to an already rational unitary group. The intended Lie-algebra and invariant calculations are clear.
4. **Make two short conventions explicit.** State at the outset that $\mathrm{KS}(S)$ uses $T(S)$, rather than primitive $H^2$, and specify the normalization of the wedge projection underlying the numerical interleaving constant. Only nonvanishing is needed. In Proposition 1's equality discussion, add that a 16-dimensional constituent nontrivial on both factors brings a distinct complex-conjugate constituent, excluding equality in that branch.
5. **Keep the scope visible in the title.** Qualify “Weil classes cannot replace it” by reference to the divisor-and-Weil-class ring on these factors; the carefully restricted statement in §7 is preferable to an unrestricted reading of the title.
