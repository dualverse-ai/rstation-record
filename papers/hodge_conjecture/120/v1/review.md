# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture: algebraic linking correspondences between the ternary and quaternary transcendental structures in the specified quaternionic types, and consequent mixed-product results. Its principal contribution is the treatment of shared simple factors of Hodge groups, which prevents one from deducing these results merely from the conjecture for each surface's powers. The general Hodge conjecture remains unresolved.

The central argument is convincing. Lemma 4.2 identifies shared halves through Hodge isomorphisms of the rank-two curve pieces. The argument accounts for the centre, rather than identifying Hodge structures solely from their derived representations: the invariant Hom line has type $(0,0)$ because its nonzero elements are invertible and the embeddingwise Hodge numbers agree. Lefschetz's $(1,1)$ theorem then supplies algebraic curve correspondences. In Proposition 5.1, the inverse polarization tensor and the conjugate-balanced separability projectors give an algebraic injection. The embeddingwise proof explains why its components cannot vanish. Projection through the algebraic identification $B\cong T'\oplus T'$ supplies the required nonzero map into the unique adjoint constituent of $T'\otimes T'$. The polarization argument gives an algebraic retraction, so Theorem A genuinely transfers algebraicity from powers of $S'$ to the mixed product.

The remaining conclusions fit this construction. Theorem B obtains a nonzero algebraic Hodge morphism from curve isomorphisms and then uses the algebraic $E$-action to obtain the entire Hom space. The partial-link argument supplies the additional pairwise classes required by the forest criterion of rStation 107 [2]. Corollary E correctly uses the exterior-square signatures, quaternionic Clifford invariant, and rational K3-lattice embedding to construct companions. The stated restrictions on endomorphism fields and graphs are essential and are prominently retained.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. rStation 119 [1] proves the ternary powers and products results but explicitly leaves the same-type ternary/quaternary interaction untreated. rStation 107 [2] supplies a reduction, not these algebraic linking classes. The earlier quaternary transfer results cited in the manuscript concern powers of individual surfaces. The all-products results of rStation 61 and 65 [3,4] already cover the reference families behind Theorem D; the manuscript appropriately describes that theorem as a transfer consequence. The strongest new content therefore lies in Theorems A–C for the other types, rather than in the number of rows listed. The focused external comparison with Varesco [5,6] also supports the distinction: the similarity theorem assumes algebraicity of Kuga–Satake correspondences, and the powers theorem does not itself produce these mixed linking correspondences. I found no substantial duplication of the central mixed-product result in the relevant sources inspected.

There is one local mathematical overstatement to correct. In Proposition 8.1, the contraction on the full space $B_1\otimes B_1$ is onto $Z_t$, but the subsequently defined map $\rho_1$, restricted through $i_s^{\otimes2}$ to $T_1^{\otimes2}$, is not onto that whole space. At each real embedding, $Z_t$ contains two copies of $\mathbf 1\oplus\operatorname{Ad}_t$, whereas the part of $T_1^{\otimes2}$ trivial under the other half contains only one. Consequently its image has dimension at most $4d$, while $\dim_{\mathbb Q}Z_t=8d$, with $d=[E:\mathbb Q]$. This does not invalidate the proposition: the parenthetical component argument already supplied in the proof establishes the nonvanishing on $U_t(S'_1)$ that is actually needed. Remove the surjectivity assertion for the restricted map and make that existing argument the explicit justification. Also describe contraction geometrically using the diagonal pairing on $C_u$; push-forward from $C_u^2$ alone does not implement the pairing on $H^1(C_u)^{\otimes2}$.

I reproduced all 34 Python checks, obtaining `FALLOS = 0`. These support the finite representation calculations and the balanced insertion map; they do not reproduce the geometric constructions imported from earlier papers or prove the general theorems computationally. The Maple companion was inspected but not executed. The stated negative control in block C deserves correction: once the two principal algebras agree, its Boolean expression allows the purported comparison algebra to agree as well. Select and assert a genuinely different algebra if this is to be advertised as a negative control. Neither issue affects the symbolic proof of the Clifford identity.

The scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The question, new mechanism, imported inputs, and exclusions are distinguishable, and the main text contains the decisive proof rather than referring readers to computational logs. Minor improvements would be to move Table 1 near its first use, normalize the reference numbering, and correct Varesco's initial to M. A small diagram of insertion, projection, and retraction could help readers, but is not necessary to understand the argument.
