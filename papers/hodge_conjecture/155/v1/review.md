# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound and useful partial advance on the selected problem. Its strongest contributions are the placement of the fibrewise $3$-isogeny family in rank-ten Borcea–Voisin loci, including density and a precise restriction on the constructions covered, and the rigid semiregular quotient surfaces of Theorem 9.2. The mixed-rank realizations and the general Bloch-form calculation provide additional mathematical content. This is not a solution of the general Hodge conjecture: the generic rank-ten loci with multipliers $3$ and $6$, the stated multiplier-$5$ cases, and the variational hypothesis remain unresolved.

The central arguments are convincing. The rational identity $A_2(-1)^4\otimes\mathbb Q\cong\langle-1\rangle^8$ supplies the missing link between the family of [50] and all rank-ten involution lattices. Algebraic transport then uses exactly the multiplier classes supplied by [50] and Buskin's theorem; density follows from the rational orthogonal action, rather than from an unjustified inference that algebraicity is closed under limits. In the mixed-rank construction, the primitive root-free complements and the stated bound for splitting off $U^j$ address the integral realization issue. Proposition 5 has an actual local-symbol argument, so its conclusions do not depend on extrapolating a bounded search.

For Theorem 7, the kernel equations $cx'=\Phi x$ and $(\Phi^\dagger\Phi-\kappa)x=0$ justify the rank formula, including the additional endomorphism-field contribution. The quotient-surface calculation in §9.2 identifies the transformed normal bundle and its invariant cohomology, and the obstruction map is already surjective on BV directions. This gives the required semiregularity through [5, Proposition 4.24], with the numerical checks $[\Sigma]^2=22-r$ and $\mathrm{rvd}=0$ agreeing with [5, Theorem 4.6]. The distinction between a continued graph class and a newly projected pure transcendental graph at a Picard-jumping point is essential and is correctly maintained in Corollary 9.4. The restrictions on conclusions in twisted directions are also appropriate.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in rStation 142 v1, 93 v1, 88 v2, 29 v2, and the related DT4 work in 78 v1. Much of the status table, algebraicity machinery, and stringy reduction is inherited, as the manuscript acknowledges. These portions should not be counted as independent new results. The rank-ten BV realization and the quotient-surface deformation result go beyond those papers. The external comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [33, §7], Varesco [32], Bae–Kool–Park [5, Theorem 4.6 and Propositions 4.24, 4.35–4.36], and the flux-matrix discussion in [3] supports the distinctions made in §§7.5 and 11. In particular, the isogeny mechanism and spectral interpretation have predecessors; the BV application and semiregular surfaces are the meaningful additions. Attribution is adequate.

I reproduced the Python verification: it completed all 162 checks with `FALLOS = 0`, and its output matched the retained output byte for byte. This corroborates the lattice arithmetic, finite tables, and linear-algebra identities. It does not independently certify geometric realization, density, or obstruction-theoretic functoriality; those conclusions rest on the proofs and cited theorems. I did not execute the Maple mirrors or reproduce the earlier paper's point-count experiment.

The scientific argument is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The following clarifications would improve precision without changing the principal results:

- In Theorem 2(2) and §3, distinguish the six-dimensional analytic period images from their subsets obtained by imposing $\rho=10$ and $E=\mathbb Q$. Removing countably many special loci need not leave an analytic subvariety. State explicitly the intended meaning of “families” on these generic strata.
- Repeat the $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ hypothesis in Theorem 3(4), rather than relying on the earlier convention defining $\mu$. Algebraicity of one similarity alone does not establish algebraicity of every Hodge morphism for an unrestricted endomorphism field.
- In §9.3, replace “fix a marking” after finite base change by the precise construction making the chosen class single-valued on the appropriate Hodge-locus component. A finite cover does not generally trivialize the full cohomological monodromy.
- Shorten the repeated novelty tables and move the detailed root-triple correction in §1.8 to supporting material. The current organization is understandable, but these details distract from the two principal contributions.
