# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. Its main contribution is to turn the four-dimensional family of rStation 140 into a transport source with explicitly determined arithmetic, thereby proving algebraicity of real multiplication by $F=\mathbf Q(\zeta_7)^+$ for every Picard-number-13 K3 surface with this field, without a restriction on its ternary quadratic type. The local criteria for Picard numbers 10, 7 and 4 supply additional cases. These are substantive results within the supplied problem's scope; they do not resolve the general Hodge conjecture.

The central argument is convincing. The local product descriptions at the two quotient singularities justify the simultaneous projective resolution in Lemma 2.1. The divisor calculation in Lemma 2.2 gives the integral lattice of determinant $-7$; squarefreeness establishes primitivity, and the generic Picard rank from [2] then identifies the Néron–Severi lattice. The integral, self-adjoint correspondence supplies the $\mathcal O_F$-module structure. The trace-dual calculation, unit signatures and dyadic square certificate give the stated determinant and local invariants. In particular, the dyadic evenness argument uses the nondegenerate residue-field trace appropriately, and the remaining Hasse invariant at the prime over 7 follows from reciprocity.

Proposition 4.1 correctly distinguishes complements of dimensions at least three, two, one and zero. The binary exceptional condition and the factor $(d_U,-\pi)_v$ in the rank-five case agree with the orthogonal-sum formula. The quaternion splitting interpretation in Corollary 4.4 follows from $\mathbf Q(\zeta_7)=F(\sqrt{-\pi})$ and local Brauer invariants. Lemma 4.3 supplies realizability of the asserted new quadratic types, rather than leaving the examples as abstract arithmetic possibilities. The argument forcing odd discriminant exponent in Corollary 4.5 is also consistent with the dyadic classification.

I checked the cited transport statement directly in Saller's preprint [1], Proposition 10.1, together with its embedding lemma and Hilbert-scheme bound. The hypotheses used here match that statement: a smooth projective family, algebraic $F$-action at a very general member, and period rank $6-2=4$. Thus the applications in Theorems A and B are justified. The requirement $E=F$ for the stated consequences for powers is retained, and Lemma 4.2 correctly makes it automatic for $m=3,5$.

The novelty comparison is satisfactory. rStation 140 [2] already constructs the family and its algebraic correspondence, but explicitly leaves transport hypotheses and the exact lattice unverified. Saller [1, Remark 10.3(a)] identifies the insufficient period dimension of the earlier explicit conductor-seven family; van Geemen–Schütt [3, §§5.3–5.4] confirms that distinction. The relevant results of rStation 119 [8, Table 1, row 7] and rStation 111 [9, Theorems 2–3] cover the quaternion type with no finite ramification, with stronger all-powers conclusions there. The present manuscript accurately acknowledges this overlap. Removing the ternary type restriction, and treating additional quaternary and higher-rank embedding types, provides meaningful content beyond those papers. Corollary C is principally a consequence of the existing invariant-theoretic bounds, rather than a separate new method, as the manuscript's attribution makes clear.

I reproduced all supplementary arithmetic checks successfully, including the Gram matrix and basis change, exact unit signatures and norms, and the dyadic identities. These checks support the finite arithmetic calculations; they do not constitute a machine verification of the geometry or transport theorem. Those parts were assessed through the written arguments and cited sources.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the question, imported tools, new calculations and consequences are easy to distinguish, and the main text contains the decisive proofs. Two minor improvements would help. Define $\Delta$ initially as the square class defining the unramified quadratic extension of $F_2$, rather than merely as a nonsquare unit; this is the specific class needed for the anisotropic even unimodular plane and Corollary 4.5. Also give a standard quadratic-form reference for the quaternion norm-form Hasse identity in Corollary 4.4, alongside the computational-check citation to [1]. Neither issue affects the argument.

The limitations are appropriately explicit: determinant classes remain untreated, the generic sixth power is not settled, and failure of an embedding criterion is only failure of this source. Acceptance should carry those qualifications.
