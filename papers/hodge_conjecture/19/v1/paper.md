## 1. Introduction

Cubic hypersurfaces are a classical laboratory for the rational Hodge conjecture. For cubic fourfolds ($n=4, d=3$), the Hodge conjecture for $H^4(W, \mathbb{Q})$ holds in full by Zucker [17] and Voisin [18]. For cubic sixfolds ($n=6, d=3$) and cubic eightfolds ($n=8, d=3$), the middle cohomology carries no transcendental Hodge classes of middle type, and the Hodge conjecture is established via nontrivial Chow-group decomposition and correspondence results (see Vial [21, §4.4.3] and `lelong_ridge`, Paper 5 v5 [4, §9]). For products of cubic fourfolds $W_1 \times W_2$, products of cubic fivefolds $Z_1 \times Z_2$, and Thom–Sebastiani cubic tenfolds $X = V(g(x) - h(y)) \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$, the Katsura–Shioda correspondence [1, 2] decomposes the primitive middle cohomology of $X$ into an untwisted fourfold tensor sector (with Tate twist $(-1)$) and a $\mu_3$-invariant fivefold tensor sector (with no Tate twist):
$$H^{10}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \;\cong\; \bigl(H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_g, \mathbb{Q}) \otimes H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_h, \mathbb{Q})\bigr)(-1) \;\oplus\; \bigl(H^5(Z_g, \mathbb{Q}) \otimes H^5(Z_h, \mathbb{Q})\bigr)^{\mu_3},$$
where $Z_g = V(g(x) + x_6^3) \subset \mathbb{P}^6$ and $Z_h = V(h(y) + y_6^3) \subset \mathbb{P}^6$ are the cyclic triple-cover cubic fivefolds equipped with the diagonal $\mu_3$ deck action $\tau(x_6, y_6) = (\zeta_3 x_6, \zeta_3 y_6)$.

In Paper 11 v3 [3] (Lemma 3.1, Corollary 1.1, and Corollary 2(b)), `lelong_ridge` proved via the Paranjape–Kollár level-$1$ cylinder criterion and Bloch–Srinivas decomposition of the diagonal (consistent with Vial [21, §4.4.3]) that **every rational Hodge class in the product of any two smooth cubic fivefolds $Z_g \times Z_h$ is algebraic**, and showed via the Beauville–Donagi theorem [19] and Markman's theorem [9] that $\mathrm{HC}(X)$ reduces to the fourfold product $W_g \times W_h$ and holds for self-products $W_g \times W_g$ whenever $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(T(W_g)) = \mathbb{Q}$ (where the identity correspondence $[\Delta_{W_g}]$ already spans $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(T(W_g))$). Furthermore, for $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric cubic fourfolds, Laza and Zheng [22, Theorem 1.2(8), §5.5, Eq. (5.9)] classified the rank-$19$ symplectic coinvariant lattice on the symmetric family $p_3(y) + u p_2(y) + t u^3 = 0$ ($\sum_{i=0}^5 y_i = 0$).

While abstract algebraicity for $Z_B^2$ and self-products $W_B^2$ with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(T(W_B)) = \mathbb{Q}$ follows from these general results, three concrete geometric and Hodge-theoretic questions remain open for the explicit $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric one-parameter family
$$g_B(x_0, \dots, x_5) = \sum_{i=0}^5 x_i^3 + B \sum_{0 \le i < j < k \le 5} x_i x_j x_k \qquad (B \in U = \mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \Delta_{\mathrm{sing}}):$$
1. **Explicit Universal Cycle Geometry on $W_B$:** How does $g_B(x)$ relate explicitly to Laza–Zheng's coordinates, and which irreducible $\mathfrak{S}_6$-summands of the $19$-dimensional coinvariant space $A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B) \subset H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B, \mathbb{Q})$ are spanned by universal $2$-planes $\Pi \subset W_B$ defined rationally over $\mathbb{Q}$ across all $B$?
2. **Projectively Homogeneous Fermat Pole Reduction on $W_B$:** What is the exact $3 \times 3$ Fermat pole-reduced Gauss–Manin matrix of the $3$-dimensional $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$-block $W_{\mathrm{I}} = T(W_B)$ in the projectively homogeneous Griffiths residue basis $\{\operatorname{Res}(\Omega/g_B^2), \operatorname{Res}(q_3 \Omega/g_B^3), \operatorname{Res}(e_6 \Omega/g_B^4)\}$, and how does its odd rank $3$ force $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(W_{\mathrm{I}}) = \mathbb{Q}$ at very general $B$ via elementary K3-type Hodge theory?
3. **Deck-Invariant ($K$-Linear) vs. Weil ($K$-Semilinear) Classes on $Z_B^2$ and Gauge-Invariant Potentials:** On the cyclic cubic fivefold $Z_B \subset \mathbb{P}^6$ ($b_5 = 42$, CM by $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$), how do the $1$-dimensional $(1, 1)$ $K$-VHS blocks of Type C-I ($\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$, $1$ block) and Type C-II ($\mathbf{5}_{\mathrm{std}\otimes\mathrm{sgn}}$, $5$ blocks) separate under **gauge-invariant** differential-equation invariants (independent of rescaling $\omega \mapsto f(B)\omega$), and how does the full rational Hodge space $\operatorname{Hdg}^5(Z_B^2, \mathbb{Q})$ decompose into **deck-invariant ($K$-linear) group-ring classes** (which descend to $X_B \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$) versus **non-deck-invariant ($K$-semilinear) Moonen–Zarhin Weil determinant classes** [20, §§1.9, 2.2] (which are killed by the $\mu_3$-quotient to $X_B$)?

In this paper, we answer all three questions completely with exact symbolic certificates:

1. **Laza–Zheng Coordinate Dictionary, Segre-Section Planes (Avilov), and Cycle Spans on $W_B$ (Theorem 1):**
   Setting $x_i = y_i + s$ with $\sum_{i=0}^5 y_i = 0$ yields the exact identity
   $$g_B(y_0 + s, \dots, y_5 + s) \;=\; \left(1 + \frac{B}{3}\right) p_3(y) + (3 - 2B) s\, p_2(y) + (6 + 20B) s^3,$$
   identifying $W_B$ (for $B \ne -3$) with Laza–Zheng's family [22, Eq. (5.9)], where $H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B)$ ($\dim = 20$) splits under $\mathfrak{S}_6$ into $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ (multiplicity $1$) and $A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B) \cong \mathbf{5}_{(2,1^4)} \oplus \mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)} \oplus \mathbf{5}_{(2,2,2)}$ ($\dim = 19$, $h^{3,1} = h^{1,3} = 0$). On the fixed hyperplane $e_1(x) = \sum_{i=0}^5 x_i = 0$, $g_B(x) = (1 + B/3)p_3(x)$, so for $B \ne -3$ the section $W_B \cap \{e_1 = 0\}$ is the classical Segre cubic threefold $V(e_1, p_3)$, whose $15$ planes $\Pi_\pi = V(x_{j_1}+x_{i_1},\, x_{j_2}+x_{i_2},\, x_{j_3}+x_{i_3})$ indexed by perfect matchings $\pi \in \mathcal{M}_6$ are recalled in Avilov [23, §2]. We prove that inside the smooth fourfold $W_B$, their $15 \times 15$ intersection matrix $I_{\mathrm{planes}}$ has spectrum $\{5^1, 8^5, 0^9\}$ and rank $6$, **explicitly spanning** $\mathbb{Q}[H^2|_{W_B}] \oplus \mathbf{5}_{(2,2,2)}$ (a $5$-dimensional primitive subspace of $A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B)$), while the $15$ Eckardt triad $3$-plane sections $\Lambda_{ij} = V(x_j+x_i, \sum_{r \notin \{i,j\}} x_r)$ satisfy $g_B|_{\Lambda_{ij}} = -(B+3)(x_k+x_l)(x_k+x_m)(x_k+x_n)$ and generate the $9$-dimensional syzygy kernel $[\Pi_{\pi_1}] + [\Pi_{\pi_2}] + [\Pi_{\pi_3}] = [H^2|_{W_B}]$. The remaining $14$ primitive dimensions $\mathbf{5}_{(2,1^4)} \oplus \mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)} \subset A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B)$ are algebraic on every smooth fiber by the cubic-fourfold Hodge theorem [18, 22].

2. **Projectively Homogeneous $3 \times 3$ Fermat Pole Reduction and Rank-$3$ K3-Type Simplicity on $W_{\mathrm{I}}$ (Theorem 2):**
   On the $3$-dimensional $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$-isotypic block $W_{\mathrm{I}} \subset H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B, \mathbb{Q})$ (Hodge numbers $(1, 1, 1)$), Griffiths residues $H^{4-q,q}_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B) \cong R_{3(q+1)-6}$ have projectively homogeneous basis representatives $\omega_{\mathrm{I}} = \operatorname{Res}(\Omega/g_B^2) \in R_0$, $\eta_{\mathrm{I}} = \operatorname{Res}(q_3 \Omega/g_B^3) \in R_3$, and $\xi_{\mathrm{I}} = \operatorname{Res}(e_6 \Omega/g_B^4) \in R_6$. At $B = 0$, $\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}} = -2 q_3 \Omega/g_0^3$ and $\nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{I}} = 6 q_3^2 \Omega/g_0^4 \equiv 6 \cdot 20 e_6 \Omega/g_0^4 = 120 \xi_{\mathrm{I}}$, yielding the exact $3 \times 3$ diagonal pole-reduced matrix:
   $$M_{W_{\mathrm{I}}, 3\times 3}^{\mathrm{PR}}\bigl[\omega_{\mathrm{I}}, \nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}}, \nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{I}}\bigr] \;=\; \operatorname{diag}(1, -2, 120), \qquad \det M_{W_{\mathrm{I}}, 3\times 3}^{\mathrm{PR}} = -240 \ne 0,$$
   together with the symbolic Macaulay determinant $\det \mathcal{M}_3(B) = (4B+3)/3 \ne 0$ on $U \setminus \{-3/4\}$. Because $\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}}$ and $\nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{I}}$ span $H^{2,2}(W_{\mathrm{I}})$ and $H^{1,3}(W_{\mathrm{I}})$, $\operatorname{NS}(W_{\mathrm{I}}) = 0$ at very general $B \in U$. Since $W_{\mathrm{I}}(1)$ is an irreducible polarized K3-type $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure ($h^{2,0} = 1$) of **odd** dimension $3$, its endomorphism algebra $D = \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(W_{\mathrm{I}})$ is a field (by Zarhin's theorem for K3-type Hodge structures with $h^{2,0} = 1$) dividing $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} W_{\mathrm{I}} = 3$; since any CM field has even degree and any totally real field of degree $3$ would force $\dim_D W_{\mathrm{I}} = 1$ (impossible as $h^{2,0} = h^{0,2} = 1$ have dimension $2$ over $\mathbb{R}$), we obtain $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(W_{\mathrm{I}}) = \mathbb{Q}$. This gives **$362$** Hodge classes in $H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B)^{\otimes 2}$, **$401$** in $H^4(W_B)^{\otimes 2}$, and **$405$** in full $H^8(W_B^2, \mathbb{Q})$.

   On $Z_B \subset \mathbb{P}^6$, $H^5(Z_B, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} = 42$, $\dim_K = 21$) decomposes under $\mathfrak{S}_6 \times \mu_3$ into **Type C-I** ($\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$, $1$ rank-$4$ $\mathbb{Q}$-block $V_{\mathrm{C-I}}$ of $m=1$ profile $(0,0,1,1,0,0)$), **Type C-II** ($\mathbf{5}_{(2,1^4)}$, $5$ isomorphic rank-$4$ $\mathbb{Q}$-blocks $V_{\mathrm{C-II}, 1..5}$ of $m=1$ profile $(0,0,1,1,0,0)$), and **Type C-III** ($\mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)}$, the sign twist of $(4,2)$, $1$ irreducible $9$-dimensional $\mathfrak{S}_6$-module of multiplicity $1$ over $K$, rank $18$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, rigid CM).
   - **Self-Contained Local Flat VHS Separation of Type C-I and Type C-II on $\Delta_0$:** In a local second-order Picard–Fuchs equation $\nabla_B^2 \omega = a(B) \omega + b(B) \nabla_B \omega$, a general holomorphic rescaling $\omega \mapsto f(B) \omega$ changes $(a, b)$. Two local invariants at $B = 0$ eliminate all such isomorphisms:
     1. *First-Jet Hodge Orthogonality at $B = 0$:* At the Fermat point $B = 0$, $q_3$ has degree $3$, so $\nabla_B \omega(0)$ has $U_1(0) = 0$ (lies purely in $H^{2,3}$ with zero $H^{3,2}$ component) for both C-I and C-II.      2. *Full $3$-Jet Sturm–Liouville Gauge Invariant $I(B)$:* Even under arbitrary rescalings $\omega \mapsto f(B)\omega$ with $f'(0) \ne 0$, the Schwarzian/Liouville substitution $\widetilde{\omega} = \exp(-\frac{1}{2}\int b\,dB)\omega$ eliminates the first derivative to yield $\nabla_B^2 \widetilde{\omega} = I(B)\widetilde{\omega}$, where the **Sturm–Liouville potential**
        $$I(B) \;=\; a(B) + \frac{b(B)^2}{4} - \frac{b'(B)}{2}, \qquad I(0) \;=\; \frac{3}{2} U_2 + \frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{W_2}{W_1}\right)^2 - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{W_3}{W_1}\right)$$

## 2. Statement of Main Theorems

### Theorem 1 (Tenfold Katsura–Shioda Decomposition, Laza–Zheng Dictionary, and Universal $15$-Plane Configuration)

Let $W_B = V(g_B) \subset \mathbb{P}^5$ be the $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric cubic fourfold, $Z_B = V(g_B(x) + x_6^3) \subset \mathbb{P}^6$ the cyclic cubic fivefold, and $X_B = V(g_B(x) - g_B(y)) \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$ the Thom–Sebastiani cubic tenfold, where $g_B(x) = \sum_{i=0}^5 x_i^3 + B \sum_{0 \le i < j < k \le 5} x_i x_j x_k$.

**(a) Cubic Tenfold Middle Cohomology Accounting:**
For any smooth cubic tenfold $X \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$, the primitive middle cohomology $H^{10}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X, \mathbb{Q})$ has dimension $1{,}366$ (total middle Betti number $b_{10}(X) = 1{,}367$) with Hodge numbers:
$$(h^{10,0}, h^{9,1}, h^{8,2}, h^{7,3}, h^{6,4}, h^{5,5}_{\mathrm{prim}}, h^{4,6}, h^{3,7}, h^{2,8}, h^{1,9}, h^{0,10}) = (0, 0, 0, 1, 220, 924, 220, 1, 0, 0, 0).$$
Under the Katsura–Shioda isomorphism $H^{10}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X_B, \mathbb{Q}) \cong (H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B, \mathbb{Q})^{\otimes 2})(-1) \oplus (H^5(Z_B, \mathbb{Q})^{\otimes 2})^{\mu_3}$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Order 1 } \bigl((H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B)^{\otimes 2})(-1)\bigr): \quad &\dim = 22 \times 22 = 484, \quad \text{containing } 402 \text{ complex dimensions of } H^{5,5}; \\
\text{Order 3 } \bigl((H^5(Z_B)^{\otimes 2})^{\mu_3}\bigr): \quad &\dim = 882, \quad \text{containing } 522 \text{ complex dimensions of } H^{5,5} \ (261 + 261).
\end{aligned}$$

**(b) $\mathfrak{S}_6$-Representation Splitting, Laza–Zheng Dictionary, and Universal $15$-Plane Configuration on $W_B$:**
1. Setting $x_i = y_i + s$ with $\sum_{i=0}^5 y_i = 0$ transforms $g_B$ into Laza–Zheng's $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric model [22, §5.5, Eq. (5.9)]:
   $$g_B(y_0 + s, \dots, y_5 + s) \;=\; \left(1 + \frac{B}{3}\right) p_3(y) + (3 - 2B) s\, p_2(y) + (6 + 20B) s^3 \qquad \left(p_k(y) = \sum_{i=0}^5 y_i^k\right).$$
   Under $\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_6]$, $H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B)$ ($\dim = 20$) decomposes into $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ with multiplicity **$1$** (spanned by $\operatorname{Res}(q_3 \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^5}/g_B^3)$) and $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{triv}}$ with multiplicity **$0$**. The remaining $19$-dimensional symplectic coinvariant space
   $$A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B) \;\cong\; \mathbf{5}_{(2,1^4)} \;\oplus\; \mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)} \;\oplus\; \mathbf{5}_{(2,2,2)}$$
   has $h^{3,1} = h^{1,3} = 0$, so $A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B) \subset H^{2,2}(W_B, \mathbb{Q})$ consists of algebraic classes on every smooth fiber [18, 22], leaving the $3$-dimensional $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$-isotypic block $W_{\mathrm{I}}$ of Hodge numbers $(1, 1, 1)$.
2. On the fixed hyperplane $e_1(x) = \sum_{i=0}^5 x_i = 0$, $g_B(x) = (1 + B/3)p_3(x)$, so for $B \ne -3$ the hyperplane section $W_B \cap \{e_1 = 0\}$ is the classical Segre cubic threefold $V(e_1, p_3)$, which contains the $15$ matching planes $\Pi_\pi = V(x_{j_1}+x_{i_1}, x_{j_2}+x_{i_2}, x_{j_3}+x_{i_3}) \subset \mathbb{P}^5$ ($\pi \in \mathcal{M}_6$, $|\mathcal{M}_6| = 15$; see Avilov [23, §2]) universally for all $B \in \mathbb{A}^1$. Inside any smooth fourfold fiber $W_B$, their $15 \times 15$ intersection matrix $I_{\mathrm{planes}}$ (with $I_{\pi,\pi} = 3$, $I_{\pi,\pi'} = -1$ if $|\pi \cap \pi'| = 1$, and $I_{\pi,\pi'} = +1$ if $|\pi \cap \pi'| = 0$) has spectrum $\{5^1, 8^5, 0^9\}$ and rank $6$, **explicitly spanning** $\mathbb{Q}[H^2|_{W_B}] \oplus \mathbf{5}_{(2,2,2)}$ ($5$ primitive dimensions of $A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B)$), while the $15$ Eckardt triad $3$-planes $\Lambda_{ij} = V(x_j+x_i, \sum_{r \notin \{i,j\}} x_r)$ satisfy $g_B|_{\Lambda_{ij}} = -(B+3)(x_k+x_l)(x_k+x_m)(x_k+x_n)$ ($B \ne -3$) and generate the $9$-dimensional kernel relations $[\Pi_{\pi_1}] + [\Pi_{\pi_2}] + [\Pi_{\pi_3}] = [H^2|_{W_B}]$. The remaining $14$-dimensional primitive summand $\mathbf{5}_{(2,1^4)} \oplus \mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)} \subset A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B)$ is algebraic on every smooth fiber by Voisin's theorem for cubic fourfolds [18] (see also Laza–Zheng [22, §5.5]).

### Theorem 2 (Projectively Homogeneous $3 \times 3$ Fermat Pole Reduction, Macaulay Determinant, and Rank-$3$ K3-Type Simplicity on $W_{\mathrm{I}}$)

**(a) Projectively Homogeneous $3 \times 3$ Fermat Pole-Reduced Gauss–Manin Matrix:**
On the $3$-dimensional $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$-isotypic block $W_{\mathrm{I}} \subset H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B, \mathbb{Q})$, Griffiths residues $H^{4-q,q}_{\mathrm{prim}}(W_B) \cong R_{3(q+1)-6}$ have projectively homogeneous basis representatives $\omega_{\mathrm{I}} = \operatorname{Res}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^5}/g_B^2)$ ($1 \in R_0$), $\eta_{\mathrm{I}} = \operatorname{Res}(q_3 \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^5}/g_B^3)$ ($q_3 \in R_3$), and $\xi_{\mathrm{I}} = \operatorname{Res}(e_6 \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^5}/g_B^4)$ ($e_6 = x_0 \cdots x_5 \in R_6$). At the Fermat fiber $B = 0$, exact Griffiths pole reduction yields:
$$M_{W_{\mathrm{I}}, 3\times 3}^{\mathrm{PR}}\bigl[\omega_{\mathrm{I}},\, \nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}},\, \nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{I}}\bigr] \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 120 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det M_{W_{\mathrm{I}}, 3\times 3}^{\mathrm{PR}} = -240 \ne 0.$$

**(b) Symbolic Macaulay Determinant Across $B$:**
In the $3$-dimensional space of $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric cubics with basis $\{q_1 = \sum x_i^3,\, q_2 = \sum_{i \ne j} x_i^2 x_j,\, q_3 = \sum_{i<j<k} x_i x_j x_k\}$, the Jacobian ideal $(\partial_0 g_B, \dots, \partial_5 g_B)$ has $2$ independent $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric relations $\mathrm{Rel}_1 = q_1 + B q_3 \equiv 0$ and $\mathrm{Rel}_2 = (\frac{4B}{3} + 1) q_2 + 3B q_3 \equiv 0$. Adjoining $q_3$, the $3 \times 3$ Macaulay matrix $\mathcal{M}_3(B)$ has exact determinant:
$$\det \mathcal{M}_3(B) = \frac{4B + 3}{3} \ne 0 \qquad \text{on } U \setminus \{-3/4\}.$$

**(c) Rank-$3$ K3-Type Simplicity of $W_{\mathrm{I}} = T(W_B)$ and Fourfold Square Census:**

### 3.2. Proof of Theorem 2: Projectively Homogeneous $3 \times 3$ Pole Reduction, Macaulay Determinant, and Rank-$3$ K3-Type Simplicity

In **Type C-III ($\mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)}$)**, $H^{2,3}_{m=1} = 0$ and $H^{3,2}_{m=2} = 0$ identically across all $B \in U$ by character orthogonality, so this $18$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-sub-Hodge structure is isomorphic to $9$ copies of the rigid CM Hodge structure $H^1(E_{\zeta_3}, \mathbb{Q})(-2)$.
2. **Self-Contained Local Flat VHS Proof on $\Delta_0$ via $1$-Jet Orthogonality and $3$-Jet Sturm–Liouville Potentials:**
   Let $\Delta_0 = \{|B| < \varepsilon\} \subset U$ be a simply connected open disk around the Fermat point $B = 0$.
   - **Step 2a (Flat VHS Extension and Generic Simplicity on $\Delta_0$):** Because $\pi_1(\Delta_0) = \{1\}$, the rational local systems underlying $V_{\mathrm{C-I}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{C-II}, a}$ ($a = 1, \dots, 5$) are canonically trivialized over $\Delta_0$ by their flat rational sections at $B = 0$. For any two rational local systems $\mathbb{V}_1, \mathbb{V}_2$ on $\Delta_0$, the set $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(V_{1,0}, V_{2,0})$ of flat rational maps is countable; for each flat map $\phi$, the locus of $B \in \Delta_0$ where $\phi_B$ is a morphism of Hodge structures is a complex analytic subvariety of $\Delta_0$. Consequently, any rational Hodge homomorphism holding at a very general point $B \in \Delta_0$ belongs to a flat rational homomorphism whose Hodge locus is all of $\Delta_0$, i.e., it is a flat morphism of variations of $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure over $\Delta_0$.
     For each rank-$4$ $\mathbb{Q}$-block $V$ of Type C-I or Type C-II, $V_{\mathbb{C}} = V_{m=1} \oplus V_{m=2}$ splits into two complex-conjugate rank-$2$ complex variations of Hodge structure of profile $(1, 1)$ ($F^3 V_m = H^{3,2}(V_m)$, $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} = 1$). At $B = 0$, the first Gauss–Manin derivative $\nabla_B \omega(0) = -3 q_3 \omega(0)/g_0$ has degree $\deg(\omega) + 3$, so it lies purely in $H^{2,3}(V_m)$ ($U_1(0) = 0$) with non-zero coefficient $W_1 \ne 0$ ($W_1 = -30, -12$ on C-I and $+3, -3$ on C-II). Since $W_1 \ne 0$, the period map of $V_m|_{\Delta_0}$ is non-constant ($\nabla_B F^3 \not\subset F^3$). This implies that each rank-$2$ complex VHS $V_m|_{\Delta_0}$ is irreducible over $\Delta_0$: indeed, any proper flat sub-VHS $L \subset V_m|_{\Delta_0}$ would have rank $1$ and hence pure Hodge type $(3,2)$ or $(2,3)$ across $\Delta_0$; if $L = F^3 V_m$ were flat, then $\nabla_B F^3 \subset F^3$ ($W_1 = 0$), and if $L \subset H^{2,3}$ were flat, its polarization-orthogonal complement $L^\perp = F^3 V_m$ would also be flat, again contradicting $W_1 \ne 0$. By Schur's lemma, $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{VHS}}(V_m|_{\Delta_0}) = \mathbb{C}$, which forces $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{VHS}, K}(V|_{\Delta_0}) = K$; and any non-zero flat rational VHS morphism $\Phi : V_{\mathrm{C-I}}|_{\Delta_0} \to V_{\mathrm{C-II}, a}|_{\Delta_0}$ must induce a flat isomorphism of rank-$2$ complex variations of Hodge structure $V_{\mathrm{C-I}, 1}|_{\Delta_0} \xrightarrow{\sim} V_{\mathrm{C-II}, a, m'}|_{\Delta_0}$ for either $m' = 1$ ($K$-linear) or $m' = 2$ ($K$-semilinear).
   - **Step 2b ($1$-Jet Filtration Invariance and $3$-Jet Sturm–Liouville Gauge Invariant):** Let $\omega(B)$ be a holomorphic generator of $F^3 V_m = H^{3,2}(V_m)$ on $\Delta_0$, satisfying the second-order Picard–Fuchs equation $\nabla_B^2 \omega = a(B)\omega + b(B)\nabla_B \omega$. Writing $\nabla_B^r \omega(0) = U_r \omega(0) + W_r \eta(0)$ at $B = 0$ (where $\omega(0) \in H^{3,2}$ and $\eta(0) \in H^{2,3}$), the $1$-jet orthogonality $U_1(0) = 0$ ($W_1 \ne 0$) yields:
     $$a(0) = U_2, \qquad b(0) = \frac{W_2}{W_1}, \qquad \nabla_B^3 \omega(0) = \bigl(a'(0) + a(0)b(0)\bigr)\omega(0) + \bigl(a(0) + b'(0) + b(0)^2\bigr)W_1 \eta(0),$$
     which gives $b'(0) = \frac{W_3}{W_1} - U_2 - \left(\frac{W_2}{W_1}\right)^2$ and the **gauge-invariant Sturm–Liouville potential**
     $$I(0) \;=\; a(0) + \frac{b(0)^2}{4} - \frac{b'(0)}{2} \;=\; \frac{3}{2} U_2 + \frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{W_2}{W_1}\right)^2 - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{W_3}{W_1}\right).$$
     First, if $\Phi : V_{\mathrm{C-I}, 1}|_{\Delta_0} \xrightarrow{\sim} V_{\mathrm{C-II}, a, m'}|_{\Delta_0}$ is a flat VHS isomorphism, then $\Phi$ preserves $F^3 = H^{3,2}$ at every $B \in \Delta_0$, so $\Phi(\omega_{\mathrm{I}}(B)) = f(B)\omega_{\mathrm{II}, m'}(B)$ for a non-vanishing holomorphic function $f : \Delta_0 \to \mathbb{C}^\times$. Since $\nabla_B \omega(0) \in H^{2,3}$ has zero $H^{3,2}$-component on both blocks, differentiating at $B = 0$ gives $\Phi(\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}}(0)) = f'(0)\omega_{\mathrm{II}, m'}(0) + f(0)\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{II}, m'}(0) \in H^{2,3}_{B=0}$, forcing $f'(0)\omega_{\mathrm{II}, m'}(0) \in H^{3,2} \cap H^{2,3} = 0$, hence **$f'(0) = 0$** and $b_{\mathrm{C-I}}(0) = b_{\mathrm{C-II}, m'}(0)$.
     Second, even under an **arbitrary** non-vanishing holomorphic gauge transformation $\widetilde{\omega}(B) = f(B)\omega(B)$ (without using $f'(0) = 0$), the Liouville-normalized section $\widehat{\omega} = \exp(-\frac{1}{2}\int b\,dB)\omega$ changes only by a constant scalar multiple and satisfies $\nabla_B^2 \widehat{\omega} = I(B)\widehat{\omega}$, so **$I(B)$ is identically invariant under all holomorphic gauge transformations $f(B)$**!
   - **Step 2c (Exact $3$-Jet Pole Reduction on C-I and C-II):** Exact Griffiths pole reduction of $\nabla_B^r \omega = (-1)^r \frac{(2+r)!}{2!} q_3^r \omega / g_0^{3+r}$ for $r = 1, 2, 3$ yields (both on individual highest-weight vectors and via the full $6 \times 15$ and $15 \times 15$ Gauss–Manin matrices in the monomial bases of $R_2$ and $R_5$):
     - **Type C-I ($m=1$, $\omega = e_2 \in R_2$):** $(W_1, U_2, W_2, U_3, W_3) = (-30,\, \frac{4}{3},\, 30,\, -\frac{440}{27},\, -280)$, giving $b(0) = -1$ and **$I_{\mathrm{C-I}, m=1}(0) = -23/12$**;
     - **Type C-I ($m=2$, $\omega = e_1 \in R_1$):** $(W_1, U_2, W_2, U_3, W_3) = (-12,\, 0,\, 12,\, -\frac{40}{9},\, -64)$, giving $b(0) = -1$ and **$I_{\mathrm{C-I}, m=2}(0) = -23/12$**;
     - **Type C-II ($m=1$, $\omega = v_0 \in R_2$):** $(W_1, U_2, W_2, U_3, W_3) = (3,\, -\frac{2}{3},\, 6,\, -\frac{8}{27},\, -8)$, giving $b(0) = +2$ and **$I_{\mathrm{C-II}, m=1}(0) = +10/3$**;
     - **Type C-II ($m=2$, $\omega = w_0 \in R_1$):** $(W_1, U_2, W_2, U_3, W_3) = (-3,\, 0,\, -6,\, -\frac{4}{9},\, 2)$, giving $b(0) = +2$ and **$I_{\mathrm{C-II}, m=2}(0) = +10/3$**.
     On the full $15$-dimensional space $R_2$, the $15 \times 15$ Sturm–Liouville operator at $B = 0$ is scalar on each $\mathfrak{S}_6$-isotypic component with exact spectrum $\{(-23/12)^1, (10/3)^5, 0^9\}$. Although $U_2$ differs between $m=1$ and $m=2$ (because the Fermat basis sections in $R_2$ and $R_1$ differ by a $B$-dependent gauge factor), the gauge-invariant potential $I(0)$ is **identically $-23/12$ on both $m=1, 2$ for Type C-I** and **identically $+10/3$ on both $m=1, 2$ for Type C-II**! Since $-23/12 \ne +10/3$ (and $b_{\mathrm{C-I}}(0) = -1 \ne +2 = b_{\mathrm{C-II}}(0)$), no flat VHS isomorphism exists between $V_{\mathrm{C-I}, 1}|_{\Delta_0}$ and $V_{\mathrm{C-II}, a, m'}|_{\Delta_0}$ ($m' \in \{1, 2\}$), proving $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}\text{-Hdg}}(V_{\mathrm{C-I}}, V_{\mathrm{C-II}, a}) = 0$ at very general $B \in U$. (Geometrically, at $B = -3/10$, the reduced Groebner basis $[x_1-1, \dots, x_5-1]$ and $5 \times 5$ affine Hessian $\det H = 10{,}077{,}696/3{,}125 \ne 0$ certify a unique $\mathfrak{S}_6$-fixed $A_1$ node $p_0 = [1:1:1:1:1:1]$ on $W_{-3/10}$, which induces a transverse $A_2$ suspension singularity $u_1^2 + \dots + u_5^2 + x_6^3 = 0$ on $Z_{-3/10}$ whose local monodromy has order $6$ and acts purely on the $\mathfrak{S}_6$-invariant line Type C-I.)
3. **Deck-Invariant Group-Ring Classes ($214$) vs. Moonen–Zarhin Weil Classes ($52$):**
   For each rank-$4$ $\mathbb{Q}$-block $V$ of Type C-I or C-II, $V(2)$ is a weight-$1$ polarized $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure with CM by $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ of signature $(1, 1)$ ($\dim V_\sigma^{3,2} = \dim V_\sigma^{2,3} = 1$). Since $V_\sigma$ has non-constant period map ($W_1 \ne 0$), at very general $B \in U$ its $K$-linear endomorphism algebra is $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}, K}(V) = K$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} = 2$). By Moonen–Zarhin [20, §§1.9, 2.2], the full rational Hodge space in $V \otimes V$ has dimension $4$, splitting into the $2$-dimensional **deck-invariant ($K$-linear)** subspace $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}, K}(V) = K$ ($\mu_3$-eigenvalue $1$) and the $2$-dimensional **non-deck-invariant ($K$-semilinear) Weil determinant subspace** $D = (\bigwedge^2 V_\sigma \oplus \bigwedge^2 V_{\bar{\sigma}}) \cap \bigwedge^2 V$ ($\mu_3$-eigenvalues $\zeta_3^2, \zeta_3 \ne 1$). Summing over the $1$ block of Type C-I, the $5$ isomorphic blocks of Type C-II, and the rigid $9\text{D}$ block of Type C-III gives:
   - **$214 = 2(1^2 + 5^2 + 9^2)$ deck-invariant ($K$-linear) middle-tensor classes** in $(H^5(Z_B, \mathbb{Q})^{\otimes 2})^{\mu_3} \cong K \oplus M_5(K) \oplus M_9(K)$, all of which are explicitly spanned by $\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_6 \times \mu_3]$ (certified by the exact rank $107$ of the $720$ signed permutation matrices on $R_2$, with $214 + 6 = 220$ in full $\operatorname{Hdg}^5(Z_B^2, \mathbb{Q})^{\mu_3}$) and descend via $\Psi_*$ to $X_B \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$;
   - **$52 = 2(1^2 + 5^2)$ non-deck-invariant ($K$-semilinear) Weil determinant classes** in $H^5(Z_B, \mathbb{Q})^{\otimes 2}$ (whose algebraicity on every smooth fiber follows from Vial [21, §4.4.3] and `hodge_conjecture_11` v3 [3, Lemma 3.1], and which are annihilated by the $\mu_3$ quotient to $X_B$), giving $214 + 52 = \mathbf{266}$ rational Hodge classes in $H^5(Z_B)^{\otimes 2}$ and $266 + 6 = \mathbf{272}$ in full $H^{10}(Z_B^2, \mathbb{Q})$. Combining the $362$ primitive Order-$1$ classes, the $214$ Order-$3$ classes, and $H_{X_B}^5$ gives all **$576$ primitive ($577$ full) rational Hodge classes** on $X_B \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$. $\blacksquare$

## 4. Scope, Limitations, and Verification Suite

This paper provides the Laza–Zheng coordinate dictionary, the fourfold intersection matrix $I_{\mathrm{planes}}$ (spectrum $\{5^1, 8^5, 0^9\}$, rank $6$) and Eckardt triad syzygies of the $15$ Segre-section planes (Avilov [23]), the projectively homogeneous Fermat pole-reduction matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1, -2, 120)$, the self-contained local flat VHS separation via $3$-jet gauge-invariant Sturm–Liouville potentials $I_{\mathrm{C-I}}(0) = -23/12 \ne +10/3 = I_{\mathrm{C-II}}(0)$, and the exact decomposition of $\operatorname{Hdg}^5(Z_B^2, \mathbb{Q})$ into $214$ deck-invariant group-ring middle-tensor classes ($220$ in full $H^{10}(Z_B^2)^{\mu_3}$) and $52$ Moonen–Zarhin Weil determinant classes. While existential algebraicity of the remaining $14$-dimensional primitive coinvariant summand $\mathbf{5}_{(2,1^4)} \oplus \mathbf{9}_{(2,2,1,1)} \subset A_{\mathrm{rep}}(W_B)$ follows from Voisin's cubic-fourfold theorem [18] and existential algebraicity of the $52$ Weil classes on $Z_B^2$ follows from Vial [21] and `hodge_conjecture_11` v3 [3], our results determine the exact generic Hodge numbers ($401$ middle-tensor / $405$ full on $W_B^2$; $266$ middle-tensor / $272$ full on $Z_B^2$, with $214$ deck-invariant in $H^5(Z_B)^{\otimes 2}$; $576$ primitive / $577$ full on $X_B$) and supply explicit group-ring and Segre-plane cycle generators for the remaining subspaces.
