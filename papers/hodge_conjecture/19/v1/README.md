# Gauge-Invariant Gauss-Manin Potentials, Segre-Section Plane Intersection Spectra, and Deck-Invariant vs. Weil Hodge Censuses on S_6-Symmetric Cubic Fourfold Squares, Cyclic Fivefold Squares, and Thom-Sebastiani Tenfolds

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/19/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We determine the generic Hodge censuses, period invariants, and explicit group-ring and Segre-plane cycle spans for $\mathfrak{S}_6$-symmetric cubic fourfold squares $W_B^2$, cyclic fivefold squares $Z_B^2$, and Thom–Sebastiani tenfolds $X_B = V(g_B(x) - g_B(y)) \subset \mathbb{P}^{11}$ ($b_{10} = 1367$), where $g_B(x) = \sum x_i^3 + B \sum_{i<j<k} x_i x_j x_k$. On $W_B \subset \mathbb{P}^5$, primitive $H^{2,2}$ splits into $1_{-}$ and Laza–Zheng's $19\text{D}$ coinvariant space. The $15$ Segre cubic section planes (Avilov) have fourfold intersection spectrum $\{5^1, 8^5, 0^9\}$ (rank $6$), spanning $H^2|_{W_B} \oplus 5_3$, while the remaining $14\text{D}$ summand is algebraic by Voisin. On the $3\text{D}$ $1_{-}$-block $W_1$, homogeneous Fermat pole reduction gives $\operatorname{diag}(1, -2, 120)$ ($\det = -240$) and $\det M_3(B) = (4B+3)/3 \ne 0$, proving $\operatorname{End}(W_1) = \mathbb{Q}$ and yielding $362$ primitive ($401$ tensor, $405$ full $H^8$) classes. On $Z_B \subset \mathbb{P}^6$, $H^5(Z_B, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim = 42$) splits into Type C-I ($1$ block), Type C-II ($5$ blocks), and rigid CM Type C-III ($9\text{D}$ over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$). Around $B = 0$, $3$-jet gauge-invariant Sturm–Liouville potentials $I_1(0) = -23/12 \ne 10/3 = I_2(0)$ and $1$-jet orthogonality ($b_1(0) = -1 \ne 2 = b_2(0)$) prove $\operatorname{Hom}(V_1, V_2) = 0$ at very general $B$. In $H^5(Z_B)^{\otimes 2}$, the $266$ Hodge classes ($272$ full) split into $52$ Weil classes (Moonen–Zarhin; algebraic by Vial and `hodge_conjecture_11` v3) and $214$ deck-invariant classes spanned explicitly by $\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_6 \times \mu_3]$ (rank $107$ over $K$), descending to $X_B$ ($576$ primitive, $577$ full).
