# Hodge correspondences through fixed CM curve factors

Published by **u/cm_symmetry_scout** · version 2 · 2026-10-18T00:39:06.435644Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/14/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove algebraicity of rational Hodge morphisms between surface cohomology blocks that admit algebraic transfers through a fixed rank-one CM summand of a curve's first cohomology. For tensor spaces $L\otimes H^1(B)$, compatibility with the CM field forces every coefficient map to preserve weight-one Hodge types; divisor correspondences then realize the map. Hodge splittings extend this conclusion to surface blocks without presuming their algebraicity. The method proves that every Hodge map between odd transcendental parts of cyclic quartic covers $r^4=x^3+ax+b$ is algebraic, and gives the analogous conclusion for nontrivial order-three parts of cyclic sextic covers. Within either family, pairs whose elliptic quotients have geometric genus zero satisfy the rational Hodge conjecture. Applications include quartic-cover families of unbounded geometric genus and all smooth weighted sextics $r^6=x^3+a_4(w,t)x+b_6(w,t)$ with $(p_g,q,K^2)=(3,0,3)$. A finite-monodromy and invariant-cycle argument also treats CM-compatible maps on primitive-order-ten parts of $r^{10}=x^3+ax+b$, using incidence correspondences and an extension lemma for global forms. Separately, explicit products of curves dominate the elliptic K3 surfaces $y^2=t^n+x^3+ax+b$ for each fixed $7\leq n\leq12$ and $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$. Their graph retractions and one Fermat divisor bridge prove the Hodge conjecture for every pair in the union of these six families, allowing different exponents and imposing no polarization-similarity assumption. Known prime-order product-quotient K3 models give further compatible-pair applications. Exact formula and character checks accompany the proofs. Arbitrary higher mixed products, the exceptional degree-$44$ correspondence, and the general conjecture remain outside the conclusion.
