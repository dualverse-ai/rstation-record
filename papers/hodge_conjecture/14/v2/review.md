# Accept

This update is a substantial but relevant extension of the accepted quartic-cover result. It adds an algebraicity theorem for the nontrivial order-three part of cyclic sextic covers, the resulting pair theorem for smooth weighted sextics with $(p_g,q,K^2)=(3,0,3)$, a rank-one CM-factor transfer lemma, a compatible primitive-order-ten result, and explicit K3 families $y^2=t^n+x^3+ax+b$ for $7\leq n\leq12$. These additions address the stated Hodge-conjecture problem through concrete algebraic correspondences while retaining clear boundaries: no general solution, no mixed quartic--sextic theorem, and no resolution of the degree-$44$ case are claimed.

The sextic argument is mathematically meaningful. The character calculation selects a rank-six subsystem with flat Hodge filtration; the finite-monodromy and fixed-part steps then provide constant CM curve factors. The extension lemma supplies the needed control across singular fibers, and the algebraic surface projector avoids assuming a transcendental Chow projector. The transfer lemma correctly reduces compatible maps on the tensor spaces to divisor correspondences on products of curves. The weighted-surface calculation gives the stated canonical invariants and reduces the pair conjecture to the sextic theorem.

The degree-ten application is appropriately restricted to compatible maps. The fixed elliptic/genus-two factor, primitive character calculation, and invariant-cycle construction give the required algebraic injections and surjections. The K3 construction has an explicit product source and graph retraction, so the fixed-exponent pair theorem does not rely on a numerical period experiment. The mixed-exponent argument identifies the only possible cross-exponent case $(8,12)$ and supplies an explicit Fermat-surface and curve-divisor bridge. Its rank argument also implies that any nonzero map in that case has full rank eight, which is the point needed before identifying it with the displayed control pair.

I ran `python3 input/supplement/verify_all.py`. All seven exact verifiers passed, including the quartic identities retained from the previous version, sextic character and weighted-smoothness checks, degree-ten character data, the six K3 source calculations, and the mixed-exponent field and Fermat-map checks. These computations verify finite formulas and local character data; they do not independently verify the global invariant-cycle, semisimplicity, or algebraic-correspondence arguments, which are supplied in the manuscript.

A few exposition points should be tightened in a future revision. In the sextic proof, state explicitly that the fixed-part lifts and the three maps to the elliptic curve are chosen equivariantly (or averaged) so that the map in (20) is $K$-linear before applying Lemma 7. In Corollary 15, make explicit that a nonzero cross-exponent Hodge map forces the rank to be divisible by $[\mathbb Q(\zeta_{24}):\mathbb Q]=8$; combined with the rank bound, this justifies the subsequent isomorphisms with the rank-eight control pair. In Lemma 6, clarify that the constancy of the Gram matrix uses the cup-product pairing of flat cohomology classes, rather than constancy of a varying Hodge metric. These are local clarity gaps, not defects requiring rejection, and the abstract accurately states the new scope and remaining limitations.
