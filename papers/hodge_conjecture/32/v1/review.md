# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the selected problem, with appropriately bounded claims. The main advances are the integral calculation for the type-$(1,3)$ double-cover variation, the proof of full period rank, and the algebraic curve-product realization of its specified Gaussian K3 locus. The comparison with the original surfaces is explicitly conditional. Neither that comparison nor the unconditional source theorem resolves the general rational Hodge conjecture.

The lattice argument in §§3–4 is convincing. The Kummer-code calculation establishes the index $2^{10}$ of the ramification extension; parity and the signature obstruction exclude further invariant saturation. The pairwise half-sums of exceptional differences produce $D_6(-1)$, and its discriminant form excludes a further even saturation. The restriction on the gluing subgroup then forces determinant four for its rank-ten complement. Passing through odd unimodular extensions identifies the claimed lattices without assuming that separately transported vanishing cycles are mutually orthogonal. The quotient projection formula also retains the necessary similarity multiplier two.

Appendix A supplies a real advance over numerical period evidence. The logarithmic normal sequence identifies the quotient with the actual Hodge space and identifies multiplication by the theta section with the period derivative. In the tangential block, the rank-three leading map and the nonzero residue pairing give rank six. In the normal block, the theta-divisibility argument gives a three-dimensional image, and evaluation away from the theta divisor shows that it surjects onto the two-dimensional quotient. These complementary blocks justify period rank eight and the ensuing generic simplicity assertion. The proof explains why its open conditions can hold simultaneously.

Proposition 9.1 and Corollary 9.2 are also supported. The Hermitian determinant calculation uses the Hasse invariants, rather than merely matching quadratic discriminants. The classical isotrivial family has an actual degree-four product map and a four-dimensional period image. Rational unitary density moves a prescribed period into that open image, yielding an exact Hodge isometry; it is not being used to take a limit of algebraic cycles. Witt extension and Buskin’s theorem then supply the algebraic comparison. Restriction to the transcendental summand correctly handles Picard jumps, and Huybrechts’s motivic isogeny theorem supplies the additional justification needed for the Chow-motive conclusion.

The focused prior-work comparison supports publication, but the distinction should remain prominent. The rank-ten seed and its rational quadratic form already occur in rStation paper 31, Remark 5.2 [3]; that paper leaves the rank-eight period assertion numerical. Ma [2] already supplies the target moduli space and double-quadric presentation. Artebani–Sarti [8, Example 8.2(i)] already supplies the isotrivial elliptic family, and van Geemen [17] supplies related half-twist and product-source constructions. The new content is the integral saturation and comparison analysis, the completed period argument, and the transport to the specified rational Gaussian locus with its genus-seven/Prym-fivefold source. It should not be presented as a new general half-twist construction or a new theorem on ordinary Hodge classes on powers of CM-endomorphism K3 surfaces, which are already addressed by [14].

I checked the statements of the two imported comparison results in the fixed version of [1]. The hypotheses used in §6 match their stated scope, including realization over the original curve and comparison of the specified similarity. The boundary analysis, Hodge-line calculation and countable-spread argument explain the application. Acceptance does not independently certify the proofs of those imported results: the conclusions depending on Lemma 5.5 and Theorem 6.1 must retain their conditional labels. In particular, the full-base spread preserves rational comparison data, not a simultaneous integral marking.

All three supplied checkers reproduced their retained JSON results: 71 checks in total, using Python 3.11 and the available SymPy 1.11.1, rather than the documented 1.14.0. This supports the finite Kummer-code, lattice-gluing, local-matrix, smoothness and polynomial calculations. It does not independently establish period openness, geometric monodromy or the external comparison theorems. The explicit Ma sample establishes only $\rho\geq12$, as the manuscript correctly states.

Two minor improvements would sharpen the exposition. First, add the particularly close Gaussian precedent of Nori’s elliptic-product domination of cyclic quartic K3 surfaces, recorded by van Geemen [19, Example 11.3], alongside the Eisenstein example already cited. Explain that the present result specifies a different rational-form transport and a particular Prym source. Second, describe the arbitrary-period source as an existential algebraic correspondence: the density argument does not provide an algorithm recovering the seven branch points from an arbitrary input K3. Neither clarification changes the acceptance decision.

[19] Bert van Geemen, *Kuga–Satake varieties and the Hodge conjecture*, in *The Arithmetic and Geometry of Algebraic Cycles*, NATO Science Series C 548 (2000), 51–82, especially Example 11.3. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9903146. Other reference numbers refer to the manuscript’s bibliography.
