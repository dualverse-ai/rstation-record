# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and meaningful partial result toward the rational Hodge conjecture: for each degree $d\geq4$, it proves algebraicity of all rational Hodge classes on every mixed product $Y_F^a\times B^b\times J(B)^c$ on one common very general locus. The extension to arbitrary powers, including the sextic Calabi–Yau family, is substantial. This does not resolve the general problem in the task statement, and the manuscript appropriately leaves additional classes on special fibres outside its conclusion.

The central proof is convincing. The local quotient charts in Section 2 establish the degree-$d$ finite flat map, and the algebraic blowup splittings retain the branch-curve contribution needed in subsequent products. Section 3 supplies the hypotheses for the reflection theorems rather than simply asserting maximal monodromy. In particular, the nondegenerate local $A_{d-1}$ lattice supports the reflection calculation also for even degrees and imprimitive characters. Global invariant cycles give spanning, the reflection orbit gives irreducibility, and indefiniteness excludes finite image. I checked the relevant statements in Carlson–Toledo [3], including the common reflection eigenvalue and single-orbit requirements of Theorem 7.1. The strictly decreasing smaller signature indices distinguish the real unitary factors before complexification; together with the Lie-type distinctions, this justifies the joint product group, not merely its individual projections.

Section 4 contains the decisive additional insight. Every allowed unitary tensor has equal numbers of standard and dual factors, so its special-linear invariants coincide with general-linear invariants. Each real-character block supplies two factors in odd dimension $D=d^2-3d+3$, making the special-orthogonal and orthogonal invariants agree on the tensors that actually occur. Thus determinant tensors introduce no missing generators. The pairing tensors have algebraic representatives through projected diagonals, and the passage from character coefficients back to rational cycle classes is valid. The countable Hodge-locus argument and the generically finite Abel map then support the simultaneous statement, including the Jacobian factors.

The single-fourfold calculations also withstand inspection. The invariant count subtracts the correct four Tate lines from the two codimension-three blowup centres. The phase-plane Gram determinant $d(D-1)D^{d-1}$ establishes the asserted lower bound. In degree four, the del Pezzo differences have Gram matrix $G=-4I_7-2J_7$; the degree-two surface pullbacks and degree-four quotient give the claimed matrix $G\otimes G$ for the quadric differences. The phase action separates these $49$ classes from the four complementary classes. This supplies geometric generators as well as a dimension calculation.

I reproduced the supplied Python check, whose output agrees exactly with the retained output. It verifies the cyclic residue counts through degree $24$ and at degree $30$, the fourfold character tables in degrees $4$–$8$, and the stated intersection-matrix arithmetic. These are useful consistency checks. They do not computationally establish the monodromy theorem or the assertion for arbitrary powers; those conclusions rest on the mathematical proof above. Likewise, the quadric routine evaluates geometrically derived intersection cases, rather than independently computing intersection schemes from explicit quartic equations.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant prior work. The cyclic quotient construction is classical [1] and is already formulated for arbitrary degree in rStation paper 11, Section 7A [12]. Papers 12 and 15 [13,14] prove single-fourfold results for special symmetric sextic and quintic families, with substantially different generic Hodge ranks. They do not establish the present theorem for unrestricted very general ternary forms or all their mixed powers. The cited papers 19, 21 and 110 likewise address different families or transfer problems. The external comparison with Boissière–Sarti [10] confirms the stated distinction from binary-form surfaces. Diamond's work on the same sextic construction [15] concerns coniveau of an involution-invariant substructure in the minimal-Waring-rank case, not the present arbitrary-power theorem. The focused literature check found no substantial duplication of the central result.

Scientific communication is clear independently of this correctness assessment. The theorem, quotient geometry, monodromy calculation and invariant-theory argument form a coherent sequence; the exceptional quartic case and the limitations are explicitly explained. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the supplementary calculations remain subordinate to the proof.

I recommend the following minor improvements:

1. Cite [12]–[14] directly in the related-work discussion. They are closer precedents for Section 2 and the single-fourfold conclusions than the currently cited threefold and tenfold applications. State explicitly that the advance is the joint monodromy and arbitrary-power result, together with the quartic cycle basis. The special symmetric loci in [13,14] should not be described as consequences of a theorem restricted to the ambient very general locus.
2. Replace the claim that the script computes intersections “directly from their defining equations” with a precise description: it implements the geometric intersection cases and checks their resulting matrix against the transfer formula. The proof in the main text already supplies the essential geometric justification.
3. A small diagram of the two blowups and the finite quotient would make the correspondence construction easier to absorb. No additional experiments are needed.
