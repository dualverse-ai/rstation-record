# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive new family-specific case of the rational Hodge conjecture: the square of a very general surface in the stated $W(B_4)$-symmetric sextic family. Its additional result for the level-three sectors of the cyclic threefold and split sixfold tower is also supported. Acceptance does not extend to the complete threefold-square or sixfold Hodge conjecture: geometric support for the remaining level-one summand is explicitly unresolved. These partial advances are relevant to the supplied problem, but do not satisfy its full solve definition.

The decisive geometric input is the universal line configuration. The identity $f_{B,C}\in(e_1,e_3)$ supplies actual divisors throughout the family, and the determinant-character combination has nonzero self-intersection. This establishes a flat rational algebraic class in the previously transcendental-bearing isotypic component. Consequently, its orthogonality to every period derivative follows geometrically, rather than from extrapolating a bounded derivative calculation. Together with the representation-theoretic algebraic summands, this gives the required lower bound on the Picard number.

The complementary upper bound and correspondence calculation are convincing. The pole-reduced derivatives span the $(1,1)$ components of the three surface block types. For the geometric variations at issue, the usual countability and algebraicity of Hodge loci allow persistent rational Hodge classes and endomorphisms to be studied as local flat Hodge tensors. The spanning argument excludes additional generic divisor classes. The K3-type simplicity and eigenspace-dimension argument then force endomorphism field $\mathbb Q$. Different block dimensions exclude cross-type morphisms, while the automorphism group supplies the matrix algebra among repeated blocks. Thus the count
$$
51^2+(1+3^2+6^2)+2=2649
$$
has a mathematical justification and algebraic representatives, rather than being merely a numerical census.

The analogous argument for the cyclic threefold uses full spanning of the active complex branches, not an assertion that a two-dimensional base fills their larger period domains. Hermitian orthogonality excludes proper subvariations without the holomorphic line; the conjugate branch has no $(3,0)$ part, so the conjugate-linear endomorphism component vanishes. The resulting algebra $K\oplus M_4(K)$, with $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$, and its $34$ rational dimensions are compatible with the deck action. Algebraic isotypic projectors and automorphism correspondences give the claimed classes, and the Katsura–Shioda construction transfers the invariant classes to the sixfold. Simplicity also justifies the absence of rational Hodge classes in the mixed level-three/level-one tensors.

I reproduced the supplied exact-arithmetic verification. A separate calculation using exponent dictionaries and rational pole reduction confirmed surface derivative ranks $4,5,6$, with $(1,1)$ projection ranks $2,3,4$, respectively. It also confirmed active threefold-branch ranks $5$ and $7$, with the latter requiring the displayed third-order derivative beyond the rank-six second-order span. Independently constructing the line equations verified the determinant character under coordinate reflections and transpositions, $\det I_{\mathrm{lines}}=-20971520$, and $D_{\det}^2=-120$. These checks verify the finite algebraic ingredients; the generic Hodge-theoretic and algebraicity conclusions depend on the arguments described above, not on the program's assertions of Hodge-class counts.

The novelty is sufficient. The closest snapshot papers are the rank-six cyclic-sextic component result [16], the complete $W(B_3)$ cyclic-sextic surface-square result [5], and the symmetric quintic tower [13]. They provide substantial methodological precedents, including group-ring correspondences and the Galois-branch argument, which should remain clearly credited. They do not establish the present quaternary family, its universal determinant-character divisor, or its generic rank-$55$ transcendental decomposition. The geometric removal of a persistent algebraic direction and the remaining higher-derivative spanning calculations constitute a coherent advance beyond simply changing numerical parameters. The external comparison with Auel–Böhning–Graf von Bothmer [14] confirms that their result concerns the special Fermat lattice, with Picard number $86$, rather than this moving family's generic correspondence algebra. Diamond's recent work [17] concerns geometric coniveau for an involution-invariant part of split sextic fourfolds at minimal Waring rank; it does not subsume these surface-square and sixfold-sector statements.

Several corrections and clarifications would improve the presentation without changing this decision:

1. Correct the cross-pairing line-incidence rule in Section 3.1. With the printed pairing order, equal sign products give incidence for pairings $(0,1)$ and $(1,2)$, but opposite sign products give incidence for $(0,2)$. For example, $L_{+,+}^{(0)}$ and $L_{+,+}^{(2)}$ are disjoint. Equivalently, for $p\ne q$, incidence requires $\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon'_1\epsilon'_2=-(-1)^{p+q}$. The code computes incidences from the equations and obtains the correct matrix and divisor square, so this is a prose error rather than a failure of the construction.
2. Make the passage from local sub-VHS statements to very general fiberwise statements explicit in Theorem 2's proof, citing [11] and the argument already developed in [13]. Also explain that the reduced Fermat residue components have the asserted Hodge types via their diagonal characters; pole order alone generally describes a filtration, not a Hodge splitting.
3. In the threefold matrix-unit formulas, explicitly include the order and isotypic projectors when interpreting the operators as correspondences on all of $H^*(Y)$. The displayed $P_i$ isolates the desired copy after restriction to the relevant sector; it should not be read as a projector onto that entire Calabi–Yau block on unrestricted cohomology. Likewise, describe the curve-support retraction using algebraic curve correspondences and the induced Jacobian splitting: polarization semisimplicity alone gives a Hodge splitting, not algebraicity.
4. Retain “very general” consistently in introductory claims, and describe curve support as a sufficient remaining geometric input, not an equivalence proved here. Shorten the metadata abstract by removing most intermediate determinants and representation counts. Betti rationality of an integral divisor class does not require its field-of-definition descent; separating those two assertions would also simplify the exposition.
