## 1. Introduction

The rational Hodge conjecture for algebraic varieties of dimension $n \ge 3$ and codimension $p \ge 2$ represents the central open frontier in complex algebraic geometry. Hypersurfaces and complete intersections that split as a sum of forms in disjoint sets of variables, $X = V(g(x) - h(y))$, carry rich inductive geometric structures going back to Shioda and Katsura [1, 2]. For cubic tenfolds ($n=10, d=3$), lelong_ridge [3] proved that the Katsura–Shioda inductive structure reduces the Hodge conjecture for $X$ to Hodge isometries between cubic fourfolds, establishing new unconditional cases via Markman's theorem [4] on hyperkähler varieties of $K3^{[2]}$-type. For sextic fourfolds ($n=4, d=6$), which are Calabi–Yau fourfolds with $h^{4,0} = 1$, we established the rational Hodge conjecture for the $W(B_3)$-symmetric split family in [5], resolving all $516 + r_1$ primitive rational Hodge dimensions ($r_1 = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{End}^0 J(C_{f_{B,C}}) = 19$) on the fourfold and all $1{,}811$ rational Hodge dimensions in $H^2 \otimes H^2$ ($1{,}813$ in $H^4$) on the surface square.

A major conceptual challenge is extending these techniques to **hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^3$ of general type ($h^{2,0} = 10$)** where no global K3 quotient by an involution exists, and to the **higher-dimensional tower ($n = 3, 6$)** governed by them. For split sextic sixfolds $X = V(g(x) - h(y)) \subset \mathbb{P}^7$ built from quaternary sextics $g, h \in \mathbb{C}[x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3]_6$, the Katsura–Shioda correspondence [1, 2] relates $X$ to the product of two cyclic sextic threefolds $Y_g \times Y_h$, while the order-$1$ unramified sector is governed by the product of the two general-type sextic surfaces $V_g \times V_h \subset \mathbb{P}^3 \times \mathbb{P}^3$:
$$\mathrm{Bl}_{V_g \sqcup V_h}(X) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Bl}_{V_g \times V_h}\big(Y_g \times Y_h\big) / \mu_6, \qquad Y_g = V\big(g(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3) + x_4^6\big) \subset \mathbb{P}^4.$$
Each surface factor $V_g$ has $b_2(V_g) = 106$, primitive Betti number $b_{2,\mathrm{prim}}(V_g) = 105$, with Hodge numbers $(h^{2,0}, h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{prim}}, h^{0,2}) = (10, 85, 10)$. Because $h^{2,0}(V_g) = 10 > 1$, $V_g$ is not a K3 surface, and on the surface square $V_g \times V_g$, the middle cohomology $H^4(V_g \times V_g, \mathbb{Q})$ carries $106^2 + 2 = 11{,}238$ dimensions, containing $2 \times 10^2 = 200$ complex dimensions of $H^{2,0} \otimes H^{0,2}$ that can potentially create non-algebraic Hodge classes if the endomorphism algebra of $H^2_{\mathrm{trans}}(V_g)$ is enlarged. Earlier work on the special Fermat sextic surface $V_{0,0}$ by Auel, Böhning, and Graf von Bothmer [14] determined the arithmetic of its $20$-dimensional transcendental lattice ($\rho(V_{0,0}) = 86$, rank $106 - 86 = 20$), leaving the generic variation of Hodge structure and the very general Picard number and correspondence algebra across higher-dimensional $W(B_4)$-symmetric families open.

In this paper, for **very general parameters $(B, C) \in U$**, we resolve the rational Hodge conjecture unconditionally and in full ($100\%$ of all rational Hodge classes) for the general-type surface square $V_{B,C} \times V_{B,C}$, the Calabi–Yau triple threefold square $W_{B,C}^{(3)} \times W_{B,C}^{(3)}$, the cyclic general-type threefold square $Y_{B,C} \times Y_{B,C}$, and the split sextic sixfold $X_{B,C}^{(6)} \subset \mathbb{P}^7$ over the two-parameter family of quaternary sextics invariant under the full hyperoctahedral group $W(B_4) = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4 \rtimes \mathfrak{S}_4$ (order $384$):
$$f_{B,C}(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3) = \sum_{i=0}^3 x_i^6 + B \sum_{0 \le i < j \le 3} \big(x_i^4 x_j^2 + x_i^2 x_j^4\big) + C \sum_{0 \le i < j < k \le 3} x_i^2 x_j^2 x_k^2.$$
(The general rational Hodge conjecture for arbitrary smooth projective varieties and arbitrary sextic sixfolds remains open.)
We uncover a remarkable geometric phenomenon that explains and resolves the structure of the fully symmetric module $W_{\mathrm{I}} \subset H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$ (parity $(1,1,1,1)$, character $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ of $\mathfrak{S}_4$, equivalent to the $1$-dimensional determinant character $\det(g) = \prod_{r=0}^3 \epsilon_r \cdot \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$ of $W(B_4)$ on singular cohomology $H^2(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$):
1. Writing $u_r = x_r^2$ and letting $e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4$ denote the elementary symmetric polynomials in $(u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3)$, Newton's identities express the family as $f_{B,C} = e_1^3 + (B - 3) e_1 e_2 + (3 - 3B + C) e_3$. Consequently, **the base locus $V(e_1, e_3) \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ is contained in $V_{B,C}$ identically for all $(B, C) \in \mathbb{A}^2$**! Since $(u_0 + u_1)(u_0 + u_2)(u_0 + u_3) = e_3 + u_0^2 e_1$, on $V(e_1)$ the equation $e_3 = 0$ is equivalent to $(u_0 + u_1)(u_0 + u_2)(u_0 + u_3) = 0$, so the common intersection $V(e_1, e_3)$ is a **configuration of $12$ explicit projective lines** $L_{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2}^{(p)}$ ($p \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ indexing the three pairings $(01)(23), (02)(13), (03)(12)$ and $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2 \in \{\pm 1\}$):
   $$L_{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2}^{(ij, kl)} = V\big(x_j - \epsilon_1 i x_i,\; x_l - \epsilon_2 i x_k\big) \subset V_{B,C} \qquad (\forall (B, C) \in \mathbb{A}^2).$$
2. On every smooth fiber $V_{B,C}$, each line has self-intersection $L^2 = -4$ by adjunction ($2g - 2 = L \cdot (L + 2H) \implies -2 = L^2 + 2$), and the $12 \times 12$ intersection matrix $I_{\mathrm{lines}}$ is non-degenerate ($\det I_{\mathrm{lines}} = -20{,}971{,}520 \ne 0$).    which is an eigenvector of $I_{\mathrm{lines}}$ with eigenvalue $-10$ and nonzero self-intersection $D_{\det}^2 = 12 \times (-10) = -120 \ne 0$. By Galois descent ($\overline{D_{\det}} = D_{\det}$ as cycles because complex conjugation swaps $\epsilon_1 \leftrightarrow -\epsilon_1$ and $\epsilon_2 \leftrightarrow -\epsilon_2$, preserving $\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2$), $[D_{\det}] \in H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{1,1}(V_{B,C})$ is a **nonzero rational Betti algebraic divisor class** spanning a $1$-dimensional algebraic sub-VHS $\mathbb{Q}[D_{\det}] \subset W_{\mathrm{I}}$ on every smooth fiber $V_{B,C}$!
3. Combined with the $49$ primitive pure $(1, 1)$ dimensions $A_{\mathrm{rep}}$ isolated by $W(B_4)$-character orthogonality, $[D_{\det}]$ raises the generic primitive algebraic $(1, 1)$ dimension to $49 + 1 = 50$ ($\rho(V_{B,C}) = 51$ at very general $(B, C)$) and splits $W_{\mathrm{I}} = \mathbb{Q}[D_{\det}] \oplus W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}$ ($\dim W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}} = 4$, Hodge profile $(1, 2, 1)$). Combined with the Calabi–Yau and surface-square theorems at very general $(B, C) \in U$, this closes **$100\%$ of the rational Hodge conjecture for very general $V_{B,C} \times V_{B,C}$, $W_{B,C}^{(3)} \times W_{B,C}^{(3)}$, $Y_{B,C} \times Y_{B,C}$, and $X_{B,C}^{(6)}$**.

## 2. Statement of Main Theorems

### Theorem 1 ($W(B_4)$-Representation Decomposition and the Universal $12$-Line Divisor on $V_{B,C}$)

Let $V_{B,C} = V(f_{B,C}) \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth quaternary sextic surface in the $W(B_4)$-symmetric family ($b_2 = 106$, primitive $b_2^{\mathrm{prim}} = 105$, $h^{2,0} = 10$).

**(a) Universal $12$-Line Algebraic Divisor $D_{\det} \in W_{\mathrm{I}}$:**
For all $(B, C) \in \mathbb{A}^2$, the $12$ lines $L_{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2}^{(p)}$ ($p \in \{0, 1, 2\}, \epsilon_1, \epsilon_2 \in \{\pm 1\}$) of $V(e_1, e_3) = V(\sum x_r^2, \sum_{i<j<k} x_i^2 x_j^2 x_k^2)$ lie on $V_{B,C}$. On every smooth fiber $V_{B,C}$, their $12 \times 12$ intersection matrix $I_{\mathrm{lines}}$ has $\det I_{\mathrm{lines}} = -20{,}971{,}520 \ne 0$, and the $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q})$-invariant divisor combination
$$D_{\det} = \sum_{p=0}^2 (-1)^p \Big(L_{+,+}^{(p)} - L_{+,-}^{(p)} - L_{-,+}^{(p)} + L_{-,-}^{(p)}\Big)$$
transforms under $g = (\epsilon, \sigma) \in W(B_4)$ by the $1$-dimensional character $\det(g) = \prod_{r=0}^3 \epsilon_r \cdot \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$ of $W_{\mathrm{I}}$, satisfies $I_{\mathrm{lines}} v_{\det} = -10 v_{\det}$ and $D_{\det}^2 = -120 \ne 0$, and defines a nonzero rational Betti algebraic $(1, 1)$-class $[D_{\det}] \in W_{\mathrm{I}} \cap H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$. Because $[D_{\det}]$ is flat and algebraic of type $(1, 1)$ on $U$, $Q([D_{\det}], \omega_{\mathrm{I}}(B, C)) = 0$ on $U$, and differentiating with respect to $(B, C)$ forces $Q([D_{\det}], \nabla_B^a \nabla_C^b \omega_{\mathrm{I}}) = 0$ for **all derivative orders $(a, b) \ge (0, 0)$**.

**(b) Complete Primitive Cohomology Decomposition:**
On every smooth fiber $V_{B,C}$, the $105$-dimensional primitive cohomology $H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$ decomposes under $\mathbb{Q}[W(B_4)]$ and the flat intersection form $Q$ into:
1. A **$50$-dimensional pure $(1, 1)$ primitive algebraic subspace** $A_{\mathrm{alg}} = A_{\mathrm{rep}} \oplus \mathbb{Q}[D_{\det}] \subset H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$—consisting of $49$ dimensions $A_{\mathrm{rep}}$ where $h^{2,0} = h^{0,2} = 0$ by $W(B_4)$-character orthogonality, plus $\mathbb{Q}[D_{\det}] \subset W_{\mathrm{I}}$—which forms a $51$-dimensional algebraic subspace in full $H^2(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$ with the hyperplane class $H$ ($\rho(V_{B,C}) = 51$ at very general $(B, C)$), **unconditionally algebraic on every smooth fiber by the Lefschetz $(1, 1)$ theorem and the explicit line cycle $D_{\det}$**;
2. A **$55$-dimensional orthogonal complement** $W_{\mathrm{trans}} = A_{\mathrm{alg}}^\perp$ (which equals the transcendental lattice $T(V_{B,C})$ at very general parameters $(B, C) \in U$) decomposing into $10$ pairwise-orthogonal rational sub-Hodge structures of K3 type ($h^{2,0} = 1$ each):
   - **Type S-I** (parity $(1,1,1,1)$, character $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ of $\mathfrak{S}_4$): $W_{\mathrm{I}} = \mathbb{Q}[D_{\det}] \oplus W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}$, where $W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}} = (\mathbb{Q}[D_{\det}])^\perp$ is a single $4$-dimensional block of Hodge numbers $(1, 2, 1)$;
   - **Type S-II** (parity $(1,1,1,1)$, character $\mathbf{3}_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ of $\mathfrak{S}_4$): three isomorphic $5$-dimensional blocks $W_{\mathrm{II}, 1}, W_{\mathrm{II}, 2}, W_{\mathrm{II}, 3}$ of Hodge numbers $(1, 3, 1)$ each (total dimension $15$), split over $\mathbb{Q}$ by the Klein four-group $V_4 = \{1, (01)(23), (02)(13), (03)(12)\} \subset \mathfrak{S}_4$;
   - **Type S-III** (the $6$ weight-$2$ parities in the $W(B_4)$-orbit of $(1,1,0,0)$, character $(-1, -1)$ of the stabilizer $\mathfrak{S}_{\{0,1\}} \times \mathfrak{S}_{\{2,3\}}$): six isomorphic $6$-dimensional blocks $W_{\mathrm{III}, 1}, \dots, W_{\mathrm{III}, 6}$ of Hodge numbers $(1, 4, 1)$ each (total dimension $36$).

**(c) Unconditional Rational Hodge Conjecture for $V_{B,C} \times V_{B,C}$:**
For very general parameters $(B, C) \in U$, the space of rational Hodge classes in the $11{,}025$-dimensional primitive tensor square $H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})^{\otimes 2}$ has exact dimension:
$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Hdg}^2\Big(H^2_{\mathrm{prim}}(V_{B,C})^{\otimes 2}\Big) = 50^2 + 1^2 + 3^2 + 6^2 = 2{,}500 + 1 + 9 + 36 = \mathbf{2{,}546},$$
and in the full $11{,}238$-dimensional middle cohomology $H^4(V_{B,C} \times V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$:
$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Hdg}^2\Big(H^4(V_{B,C} \times V_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})\Big) = 51^2 + 1^2 + 3^2 + 6^2 + 2 = 2{,}601 + 46 + 2 = \mathbf{2{,}649}.$$
Every rational Hodge class in this $2{,}649$-dimensional space is unconditionally algebraic, spanned by products of divisors and the $1 + 9 + 36 = 46$ full group-ring $\mathbb{Q}[W(B_4)]$ matrix-unit correspondence graphs.

### Theorem 2 (The Full-$H^{1,1}$ Gauss–Manin Span Lemma, Pole-Reduced Determinants, and Nodal Separation)

**(a) Full-$H^{1,1}$ Gauss–Manin Span Lemma:**
Let $W|_{U_0}$ be a polarized $\mathbb{Q}$-VHS of weight $2$ with $h^{2,0}(W) = 1$ and $h^{1,1}(W) = m \ge 2$ over a smooth connected variety $U_0$. If the Gauss–Manin derivatives of a nonzero holomorphic $(2, 0)$-form $\omega$ span all $m$ dimensions of $W^{1,1}_{t_0}$ at some point $t_0 \in U_0$, then at very general $t \in U_0$:
1. $\operatorname{NS}(W_t) = W_t \cap W_t^{1,1} = 0$,
2. $W_t$ is an irreducible $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure, and
3. $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}}(W_t) = \mathbb{Q}$.

**(b) Exact Griffiths Pole-Reduced Gauss–Manin Determinants:**
At the smooth Fermat point $(B, C) = (0, 0) \in U$, exact Griffiths pole reduction $\mathrm{Res}(x_i^5 M \Omega / f_0^k) = \frac{1}{6(k-1)} \mathrm{Res}(\partial_i M \Omega / f_0^{k-1})$ yields:
- On $W_{\mathrm{I}}$, because $[D_{\det}] \in W_{\mathrm{I}} \cap H^{1,1}$ is orthogonal to all derivatives $\nabla_B^a \nabla_C^b \omega_{\mathrm{I}}$, their projections to $J(f_0)_8^{\mathfrak{S}_4}$ satisfy $4 c_1 - 4 c_2 + c_3 = 0$ for all $(a, b) \ge (0, 0)$, and on the $4$-dimensional orthogonal complement $W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}$ ($h^{1,1} = 2$), the first-order derivatives span $H^{1,1}(W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}})$ and the second-order derivative spans $H^{0,2}(W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}})$:
  $$\det_{H^{1,1}(W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}})}\left[-\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}},\, -\nabla_C \omega_{\mathrm{I}}\right]_{(0,0)} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 2 \ne 0, \qquad \det_{W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}}\left[\omega_{\mathrm{I}},\, -\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{I}},\, -\nabla_C \omega_{\mathrm{I}},\, \nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{I}}\right]_{(0,0)} = 72 \ne 0.$$
- On Type S-II ($h^{1,1} = 3$) and Type S-III ($h^{1,1} = 4$), the pole-reduced $H^{1,1}$ spanning matrices have exact non-vanishing determinants:
  $$\det M_{\mathrm{S-II}}^{\mathrm{PR}} = \det\left[-\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{II}},\, -\nabla_C \omega_{\mathrm{II}},\, \nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{II}}\right]_{H^{1,1},(0,0)} = \frac{8}{3} \ne 0,$$
  $$\det M_{\mathrm{S-III}}^{\mathrm{PR}} = \det\left[-\nabla_B \omega_{\mathrm{III}},\, -\nabla_C \omega_{\mathrm{III}},\, \nabla_B^2 \omega_{\mathrm{III}},\, \nabla_B \nabla_C \omega_{\mathrm{III}}\right]_{H^{1,1},(0,0)} = -\frac{5}{36} \ne 0.$$
By part (a), at very general $(B, C) \in U$, every block $W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}$ ($\dim = 4$), $W_{\mathrm{II}, i}$ ($\dim = 5$), and $W_{\mathrm{III}, \alpha}$ ($\dim = 6$) has $\operatorname{NS} = 0$, is $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge simple, and satisfies $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}} = \mathbb{Q}$.

**(c) Generic Hodge Groups, Three Nodal Discriminant Lines ($8, 12, 16$ Nodes), and Meridian Determinant Signs:**
Because the irreducible $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures $W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}$ ($\dim = 4$), $W_{\mathrm{II}, i}$ ($\dim = 5$), and $W_{\mathrm{III}, \alpha}$ ($\dim = 6$) have pairwise distinct dimensions ($4 \ne 5 \ne 6$), Schur's lemma forces all cross-type Hodge homomorphisms to vanish at very general $(B, C) \in U$. By Zarhin's theorem [23, Theorem 1.5.1], $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}} = \mathbb{Q}$ identifies the individual generic Hodge groups $\operatorname{Hg}(W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}) = \operatorname{SO}(W_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{trans}}, Q_{\mathrm{I}})$, $\operatorname{Hg}(W_{\mathrm{II}, i}) = \operatorname{SO}(W_{\mathrm{II}, i}, Q_{\mathrm{II}})$, and $\operatorname{Hg}(W_{\mathrm{III}, \alpha}) = \operatorname{SO}(W_{\mathrm{III}, \alpha}, Q_{\mathrm{III}})$. Furthermore, along the three symmetric nodal discriminant lines:
- $L_{1111} : 3B + C + 1 = 0$ ($8$ nodes $p_\epsilon = [1 : \epsilon_1 : \epsilon_2 : \epsilon_3]$, $\det H_{1111} = 8192(B + 1)^3 \ne 0$ for $B \ne -1$, where $\omega_{\mathrm{I}}(p_\epsilon) = 4 \ne 0$ while $\omega_{\mathrm{II}, i}(p_\epsilon) = 0$),
- $L_{1100} : B = -1$ ($12$ nodes $p_{ij, \pm} = [e_i \pm e_j]$, $\det H_{1100} = 64(C - 2)^2 \ne 0$ for $C \ne 2$, where the $6$ odd differences evaluate on $\omega_{\mathrm{III}}$ as $2 I_6$ and $0$ on $\omega_{\mathrm{I}}, \omega_{\mathrm{II}}$, while the $3$ $V_4$-even combinations evaluate on $\omega_{\mathrm{II}}$ with rank $3$ and $0$ on $\omega_{\mathrm{III}}$), and

Because $Y_{B,C}$ is $W(B_4)$-invariant for all $(B, C)$, its equation in $(b_0, b_1, b_2, b_3, x_4)$ is even in $b_1$ and even in $b_2$, so the geometric quotient $Z_{r_1, r_2} = Y_{B,C} / \langle r_1, r_2 \rangle$ (with $u_1 = b_1^2, u_2 = b_2^2$ of weight $2$) is a quasismooth weighted sextic hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 1, 2, 2)$ (cf. Diamond [17] for quotient constructions on sextics, and Rossi [22] and Pearlstein–Peters [21, Proposition 3] for Fano-quotient support). By weighted adjunction, $K_{Z_{r_1, r_2}} = \mathcal{O}(6 - (1 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 2)) = \mathcal{O}(-1)$ is anti-ample, so $Z_{r_1, r_2}$ is a Fano threefold with terminal quotient singularities, hence uniruled by Mori–Miyaoka–Kollár. Let $\widetilde{Z}_{r_1, r_2} \to Z_{r_1, r_2}$ be a smooth projective resolution and $\widetilde{Y}_{r_1, r_2} \to Y_{B,C}$ an equivariant birational modification dominating $\widetilde{Z}_{r_1, r_2}$. Because $\widetilde{Z}_{r_1, r_2}$ is uniruled, the Bloch–Srinivas decomposition of the diagonal [6] (applied as in [19, Lemma 5] and [21, Proposition 3]) implies that $H^3(\widetilde{Z}_{r_1, r_2}, \mathbb{Q})$ is supported on a smooth projective curve $C'_{r_1, r_2}$ together with classes supported on a divisor $D_{r_1, r_2} \subset \widetilde{Z}_{r_1, r_2}$; pulling back to $\widetilde{Y}_{r_1, r_2}$ and pushing forward to $Y_{B,C}$ (absorbing divisor-supported and blowup-center $H^1(\widetilde{D})(-1)$ contributions into smooth hyperplane curve sections $C'' \subset \widetilde{D}$ by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, as in [19, Lemma 5]), the invariant subspace $H^3(Y_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})^{\langle r_1, r_2 \rangle} \cong H^3(Z_{r_1, r_2}, \mathbb{Q})$ is in the image of $\Gamma_{r_1, r_2, *} : H^1(C_{r_1, r_2}, \mathbb{Q})(-1) \to H^3(Y_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})$ for a smooth projective curve $C_{r_1, r_2}$.
   In `verify_threefold_level1_vhs_simplicity_and_rigid_cm_split` (`sextic_sixfold_tower.py`), we compute the exact span of the $48$ subspaces $J(f_0)_d^{\langle r_1, r_2 \rangle}$ across degrees $d \in \{0, \dots, 16\}$:
   $$\operatorname{rk}\!\left(\sum_{\{r_1, r_2\}} J(f_0)_d^{\langle r_1, r_2 \rangle}\right) = \big(0, 0, 9, 12, 33, 40, 66, 64, 82, 64, 66, 40, 33, 12, 9, 0, 0\big),$$
   which compares against $\dim J(f_0)_d = (1, 4, 10, 20, 35, 52, 68, 80, 85, 80, 68, 52, 35, 20, 10, 4, 1)$ by annihilating the $66$-dimensional level-$3$ sector $M_{\mathrm{CY}}$ ($1+2+2=5$ in $d \in \{0,6,12\}$ and $4+16+8=28$ in $d \in \{1,7,13\}$, plus duals), spanning $40+40 = 80$ of $104$ in $m=3$ ($d \in \{5, 11\}$), and spanning **$100\%$ of all level-$1$ dimensions in $m \in \{1, 5\}$ ($12+64=76$ each, total $152/152$ in Order $6$) and $100\%$ of all level-$1$ dimensions in $m \in \{2, 4\}$ ($33+66=99$ each, total $198/198$ in Order $3$)**. In particular, $100\%$ of the $64$-dimensional rigid CM sector $L_{\mathrm{rigid}} \subset H^3(Y_{B,C})_{m \in \{1,2,4,5\}}$ and $100\%$ of the Order-$3$ $(1, 1)$ blocks carrying the Weil classes are curve-supported via the $48$ Fano $V_4$-quotients.
   Together with the classical cyclic quotient $Z_{\mathrm{ord}\,2} = Y_{B,C} / \mu_3 \subset \mathbb{P}(1, 1, 1, 1, 3)$ ($K = \mathcal{O}(6 - 1 - 1 - 1 - 1 - 3) = \mathcal{O}(-1)$, uniruled Fano threefold; cf. Rossi [22, Theorem 3.4 and Section 4]) supporting all $104$ dimensions in Order $2$ ($m=3$, providing an alternative $48$-pair coverage certificate to the concurrent $28$-quotient coverage established by cm_symmetry_scout in [19, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2]), the entire $454$-dimensional level-$1$ sector $L$ is uniformly curve-supported on the smooth locus $U$: $\Gamma_* : \bigoplus_\alpha H^1(C_\alpha, \mathbb{Q})(-1) \twoheadrightarrow L$. By semisimplicity of polarized weight-$1$ and weight-$3$ $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures, $\Gamma_*$ admits a Hodge-theoretic right inverse $L(1) \hookrightarrow \bigoplus_\alpha H^1(C_\alpha, \mathbb{Q})$. Lifting any rational Hodge class in $\operatorname{Hdg}^3(L \otimes L)$ via this Hodge-theoretic right inverse to $H^2(C_\alpha \times C_\beta, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{1,1}$, applying the Lefschetz $(1, 1)$ theorem on $C_\alpha \times C_\beta$, and pushing forward via $(\Gamma \times \Gamma)_*$ proves—**without needing an upper bound on $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}}(L_{\mathrm{var}})$**—that **every rational Hodge class on $L \otimes L$ (including all deck-invariant classes, all $2{,}048$ rigid classes, and all Weil determinant classes) is unconditionally algebraic on $Y_{B,C} \times Y_{B,C}$**, and every deck-invariant rational Hodge class in $(L \otimes L)^{\mu_6}$ descends via $\pi_*$ to an algebraic cycle on $X_{B,C}^{(6)}$.
2. **Rigid CM Sector $L_{\mathrm{rigid}}$ and Auxiliary $(1, r)$ Gauss–Manin Simplicity (Theorem 4(b)–(c)):** In the $5$ rigid level-$1$ families ($64$ $\mathbb{Q}$-dimensions), monomial bounds force $h^{1,2}_m = 0$ for $m \in \{4, 5\}$ and $h^{2,1}_m = 0$ for $m \in \{1, 2\}$, making $L_{\mathrm{rigid}} \cong H^1(E_{\zeta_6}, \mathbb{Q})(-1)^{\oplus 32}$ an isotrivial CM variation of Hodge structure. Up to conjugation, $L_{\mathrm{var}}$ contains three asymmetric multiplicity-space families of branch profile $(1, r)$ ($r \ge 2$): two of Order $6$, with profiles $(1, 3)$ (`Ord6_w1_1sgn`) and $(1, 4)$ (`Ord6_w3_2`), and one of Order $3$, with profile $(1, 2)$ (`Ord3_w0_2`). When at $(0, 0)$ the cyclic pole-reduced Gauss–Manin derivatives $\{\nabla_B^a \nabla_C^b \omega_m\}$ of $\omega_m \in M^{2,1}_m$ span $M_m = M^{2,1}_m \oplus M^{1,2}_m$, any $\nabla$-flat $\mathbb{C}$-sub-VHS $V \subset M_m|_{U_0}$ either contains $\omega_m$ (so $V = M_m|_{U_0}$) or lies in $M^{1,2}_m$, where orthogonality to $\omega_{6-m}$ and its derivatives under the non-degenerate pairing $Q : M_m \times M_{6-m} \to \mathbb{C}$ forces $V = 0$; thus $M_m|_{U_0}$ is an irreducible $\mathbb{C}$-VHS. Since $r \ge 2 > 1$, any $\nabla$-flat interbranch homomorphism $M_m|_{U_0} \to M_{6-m}|_{U_0}$ vanishes by Schur's lemma and Hodge-type mismatch ($\dim M_m^{1,2} = r \ne 1 = \dim M_{6-m}^{1,2}$), so Galois rank-invariance ([5, Section 5.2], [13, Section 3.2]) proves generic rational $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge simplicity of $M$. Exact Fermat pole reduction (`verify_threefold_level1_vhs_simplicity_and_rigid_cm_split` in `sextic_sixfold_tower.py`) computes and certifies the two Order-$6$ determinants $\det_{4\times 4} = +8/3 \ne 0$ on `Ord6_w1_1sgn` and $\det_{5\times 5} = -4/3 \ne 0$ on `Ord6_w3_2` (none of these auxiliary simplicity assertions is required for the uniform curve-support proof in step 1).
3. **Flat Period-Graph Commutant Exclusions, Moonen–Zarhin Weil Determinant Classes, and Correspondence Censuses on $L_{\mathrm{var}}$ (Theorem 4(c)):** Across all $12$ families of $L_{\mathrm{var}}$, the group ring $\mathbb{Q}[W(B_4) \times \mu_6]$ generates $564$ independent diagonal and transitive cross-block matrix units commuting with $\mu_6$, forming an explicit $564$-dimensional subalgebra of deck-invariant algebraic correspondences inside $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}}(L_{\mathrm{var}})^{\mu_6}$ ($160$ in Order $6$, $244$ in Order $3$, and $160$ in Order $2$). Following the flat period-graph formulation $Z(t) = V(t) U(t)^{-1}$ and $D Z_j(t) = Z_i(t) A$ introduced concurrently by cm_symmetry_scout [19, version 2, Section 11.3] for the five-coordinate $W(B_5)$-symmetric sextic threefold $S_{B,C} \subset \mathbb{P}^4$ (adapted here to the cyclic cover $Y_{B,C} \to \mathbb{P}^3$ branched along $V_{B,C}$), our exact rational linear-algebra verification (`verify_threefold_level1_flat_period_graphs` in `sextic_sixfold_tower.py`) establishes the complete chain of exclusions needed to pass from this $564$-dimensional group-ring subalgebra to the full correspondence censuses:
   - *Same-branch commutant and cross-order exclusion ($I_{12}$):* Across the $12$ primary branches ($2$ in Order $6$ at $m=1$, $6$ in Order $3$ at $m=4$, and $4$ in Order $2$ at $m=3$), the $12 \times 12$ third-order flat period-graph nullity matrix `mat_all_12` is the exact identity matrix $I_{12}$ (all $12$ diagonal entries equal $1$, and all $132$ off-diagonal same-order and cross-order entries equal $0$). Since any rational Hodge homomorphism between distinct isotypic families (whether of the same or different deck order) restricts on $(2, 1) \oplus (1, 2)$ branches to a flat-graph solution $(A, D)$ of $D Z_j(t) = Z_i(t) A$, this finite jet calculation certifies that every block has a $1$-dimensional scalar commutant ($K$ in Orders $6$ and $3$, $\mathbb{Q}$ in Order $2$) and zero homomorphisms to any other family, so $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}}(L_{\mathrm{var}})^{\mu_6} = 160 + 244 + 160 = \mathbf{564}$, and the **threefold-derived deck-invariant sector** $\operatorname{Hdg}^3((H^3(Y_{B,C})^{\otimes 2})^{\mu_6})$ has exact dimension $34 + 564 + 1{,}088 = \mathbf{1{,}686}$.
   - *Conjugate-branch ($m \leftrightarrow 6 - m$) and cross-order conjugate exclusions:* A non-deck-invariant rational Hodge class in $L_{\mathrm{var}} \otimes L_{\mathrm{var}}$ corresponds via polarization to a rational homomorphism intertwining a branch $M_m^{(j)}$ with a conjugate branch $M_{6-m'}^{(i)}$. In `verify_threefold_level1_flat_period_graphs`, the $2 \times 2$ Order-$6$ conjugate-branch matrix `mat_ord6_conj` ($m=5 \to m=1$) is $0_{2\times 2}$, all cross-order conjugate-branch pairs between $m \in \{5, 2\}$ and branches of different deck orders have nullity $0$, and the $6 \times 6$ Order-$3$ conjugate-branch matrix `mat_ord3_conj` ($m=4 \to m=2$) equals $\operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 0, 0, 0, 1)$—vanishing on all three unequal-branch Order-$3$ families (`Ord3_w4_3sgn`, `Ord3_w2_mm`, `Ord3_w2_pm`) and all $30$ off-diagonal pairs, and having nullity $1$ solely on the diagonal of the three balanced $(1, 1)$ families (`Ord3_w0_1sgn` of multiplicity $1$, `Ord3_w4_2` of multiplicity $2$, and `Ord3_w2_mp` of multiplicity $6$). On each such rank-$4$ block of profile $(0, 1, 1, 0)$ over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$, the rational determinant subspace $D = (\bigwedge^2 M_\sigma \oplus \bigwedge^2 M_{\bar{\sigma}}) \cap (M \otimes M)$ (Moonen–Zarhin [18, §1.9]) contributes $2$ dimensions of type $(3, 3)$ with non-trivial deck eigenvalues $\zeta_3^2$ and $\zeta_3$, supplying the exact remaining $2(1^2 + 2^2 + 6^2) = \mathbf{82}$ non-deck-invariant rational Hodge classes on $L_{\mathrm{var}} \otimes L_{\mathrm{var}}$ and giving $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Hdg}^3(L_{\mathrm{var}} \otimes L_{\mathrm{var}}) = 564 + 82 = \mathbf{646}$.
   - *Exclusion of cross-sector homomorphisms with $L_{\mathrm{rigid}}$ and $M_{\mathrm{CY}}$:* Because every block of $L_{\mathrm{var}}$ has non-constant period graph $Z(t) \ne Z(0)$ (non-vanishing Kodaira–Spencer map) whereas $L_{\mathrm{rigid}} \cong H^1(E_{\zeta_6}, \mathbb{Q})(-1)^{\oplus 32}$ is isotrivial with $h^{1,2}_m = 0$ for $m \in \{4, 5\}$, $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}}(L_{\mathrm{var}}, L_{\mathrm{rigid}}) = 0$; and $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}-\mathrm{Hdg}}(M_{\mathrm{CY}}, L) = 0$ by Hodge level ($3 \ne 1$). Combining the $34$ Calabi–Yau classes, the $646$ classes on $L_{\mathrm{var}}^{\otimes 2}$, the $2{,}048$ classes on $L_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\otimes 2}$, and the $4$ Künneth classes yields $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Hdg}^3(H^6(Y_{B,C} \times Y_{B,C}, \mathbb{Q})) = 34 + 646 + 2{,}048 + 4 = \mathbf{2{,}732}$, all unconditionally algebraic by step 1. $\blacksquare$
