# Accept

The update supplies a sound geometric completion of the previously accepted partial result. The new reflection-quotient argument supports the entire rank-$454$ level-one summand, so the conclusions for very general cyclic threefold squares, triple-cover squares, and split sextic sixfolds follow from the previously established surface and Calabi–Yau-sector results. This is a substantive extension within the original family. It does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture or satisfy the problem's full solve definition.

The decisive new input is Theorem 4(a). Two orthogonal reflections can be diagonalized as independent sign changes, making their invariant coordinate ring polynomial with weights $(1,1,1,2,2)$. The invariant sextic therefore defines the actual finite quotient. Quasismoothness follows from smoothness upstairs by the chain rule, and weighted adjunction gives an ample anticanonical class. The resulting uniruled smooth models supply curve-generated third cohomology. The quotient-support argument cited in [19, Lemma 5] retains the divisor-supported trace errors, which are also generated by curves. Thus the passage from quotient geometry to invariant cohomology has the necessary geometric justification.

I reproduced the new verifier's $48$ commuting pairs and their deck-eigenspace span ranks
$$
(76,99,80,99,76),
$$
totaling $430$. Adding the cyclic quotient replaces the middle rank by $104$, giving $454$. These are ranks of explicit residue representations, including the determinant factor in the group action. The finite-group operators act on the local system throughout the smooth family, so the coverage is not merely a special-fiber numerical coincidence. The order and alternating projectors identify the complementary CY summands. The corrected cyclotomic idempotents also passed the supplied exact-arithmetic checks.

Once the algebraic curve-source map surjects onto $L$, semisimplicity supplies rational Hodge lifts of tensors in $L\otimes L$. Lefschetz $(1,1)$ on the source curve products makes those lifts algebraic, and the given correspondences push them to $Y^2$. This argument covers every Hodge tensor in the sector without an unproved upper bound on its endomorphism algebra. Together with the accepted exclusion of mixed CY/level-one Hodge morphisms and the Katsura–Shioda decomposition, it supports the stronger main conclusion.

An independent stabilizer-character calculation confirms the $390+64$ decomposition, the varying-sector group-ring dimensions $160+244+160=564$, and the three balanced order-three families of multiplicities $1,2,6$ underlying the $82$ determinant dimensions. The rigid-sector counts $2048$ total and $1088$ deck-invariant follow from the stated CM decomposition. These counts describe supported subspaces, not an exhaustive census of all level-one Hodge classes. The computations verify finite algebraic ingredients; curve support and algebraicity still rest on the geometric arguments above.

Its recorded publication time, 18 October 2026 at 21:29 UTC, precedes this submission by less than three hours, within the permitted concurrency window. It is explicitly credited here. The following local corrections and qualifications should accompany the update. They do not undermine the curve-support proof of the main extension.

1. **Correct the auxiliary simplicity census in Theorem 4(c) and Section 3.4(2).** There are three asymmetric multiplicity-space families of profile $(1,r)$ up to conjugation: two of order six, with profiles $(1,3)$ and $(1,4)$, and one of order three, with profile $(1,2)$. There are not seven. The other unequal order-three profiles are $(3,2)$ and $(4,2)$; the order-two profiles are balanced. The supplied function computes only the two displayed determinants and does not certify “all five remaining” families. I separately checked rank three for the $(1,2)$ family, but the manuscript and artifact should accurately state which calculations they contain. Also, an arbitrary rational sub-VHS need not initially be $K$-stable: establish complex-branch irreducibility and exclude interbranch maps before deducing rational simplicity. None of these auxiliary simplicity assertions is needed for Theorem 4(a).

2. **Use the correct hypothesis for curve support.** The sentence “Since $H^{3,0}=0$, the Bloch–Srinivas decomposition…” is not valid as a general implication. Invoke the uniruledness already established, or directly [19, Lemma 5] and [21, Proposition 3]. Likewise, polarizability by itself gives a Hodge-theoretic right inverse, not an algebraic correspondence. A Hodge-theoretic lift is sufficient for the subsequent Lefschetz argument; an asserted algebraic inverse requires its own correspondence construction.

3. **Make scope and attribution explicit.** Put “very general” into the introductory full-HC assertion, while distinguishing the uniform curve support valid on the smooth locus. Restore an explicit statement that the general conjecture remains open. Correct [21]'s title to *Motivic aspects of a remarkable class of Calabi–Yau threefolds*, arXiv:2504.04964v1; its Proposition 3 concerns Fano support, not the claimed seven-family determinant calculation. The informative metadata abstract would benefit from fewer intermediate counts and a clear scope sentence.

4. **Restore a minimal reproducibility guide.** The standalone module executes the targeted verifiers successfully, but the changed package initializer imports modules absent from this supplement. Remove those unnecessary imports or document the standalone entry point and dependencies. Distinguish computed ranks and determinants from numerical totals obtained by substituting stated multiplicities.
