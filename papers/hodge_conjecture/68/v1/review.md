# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance on the Hodge conjecture: algebraic elliptic correspondences for the relative determinants of seven quartic-CM sixfold families, with all-member scope, and ordinary and generalized Hodge conjectures for the specified products of simple factors. The distinction between Theorem A and the more restrictive Theorem B is explicit and maintained in the proofs. The general Hodge conjecture remains open.

The central argument is convincing. The finite-cover construction produces actual abelian factors through rational group-algebra idempotents. Lemma 3.1 uses algebraic correspondences from the common-inertia determinant theorem [6], rather than treating a Hodge isomorphism as an algebraic map. Adjoining the elliptic companion and taking a Hodge preimage correctly converts source Hodge algebraicity into the required target classes. The degree-twenty case identifies the necessary elliptic factor and invokes the appropriate stronger Fermat-source result [13]; it does not incorrectly assert that the entire degree-twenty Jacobian is an elliptic product.

The invariant-space analysis is particularly important. Formula (3.1) shows why an unrestricted tuple count would not suffice without controlling invariant-positive components. The fixed-free and stabilizer arguments address this issue, and the $G_{24}$ correction has an explicit tuple-count normalization. Lemma 5.1 explains the comparison with canonical Hodge extensions before applying positivity. Bakker–Brunebarbe–Tsimerman [10, Lemma 6.17] has the stated nefness and bigness criterion, so the positive fourth intersection gives a component with period rank four. The odd-rank Hermitian normalization and rational unitary density then transfer the correspondence to every target period. This avoids an unsupported extension from very general points to special members.

The product consequences also receive the necessary argument. Section 7 checks independence of the derived factors, including factors with different determinant fields, instead of inferring it merely from the individual Hodge groups. The trace-free spectra, endomorphism commutants and determinant relations justify the stated joint group under the exact-endomorphism and nonisogeny hypotheses. Invariant theory then reduces the ordinary Hodge classes to algebraic pairings and the newly supplied determinant correspondences. For generalized HC, Abdulali's Theorems 10–11 and Remark 12 [7] match the application. The independent quadratic characters explain why the required fully twisted CM realizations can be taken on powers of the four listed elliptic curves.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Paper 57 v1 [5] already contains the single-field correspondence and product strategy for $\mathbf Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{-3})$; papers 53 v2 and 66 v1 provide the intersection recurrence and common-inertia supply theorem. These dependencies are credited. Paper 58 v3 [14] studies different imaginary-quadratic reflection-group variations, and paper 67 v1 [4] establishes Gaussian sixfold all-powers results without the individual quartic determinant correspondences proved here. The external all-powers preprint [3] explicitly distinguishes balanced determinant supply from singleton saturation; its higher-center sixfold argument does not already furnish Theorem A. Markman's quartic-CM construction [15] leaves the relevant semiregularity issue unresolved. The seven dominant sources and their combined mixed-product consequences therefore constitute substantive new content, despite substantial reuse of the established framework.

Targeted exact recomputation from the retained class algebras reproduced the degrees and quartics for the $G_{24}$, both $G_{26}$ and both $H_4$ rows at five distinct projective weight choices, including the $G_{24}$ subtraction. An independent elementary calculation also verified that the Fermat character $(1,1,18)$ has orbit size eight and CM type $\{1,3,7,9\}$, matching the quadratic character of discriminant $-20$. These checks support the arithmetic claims conditional on the retained group data. I inspected the reconstruction and intersection code, but did not independently regenerate the finite groups and character tables or rerun the $G_{31}$ reconstruction. The computations do not independently establish the geometric comparison or replace Aoki's Hodge-conjecture theorem.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement. The research question, new correspondence, proof strategy, prior inputs and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the computational records support a mathematical argument rather than substitute for one. The abstract is informative and the numbered references are usable.

Two minor improvements would strengthen reproducibility and exposition:

- Record tested software versions and include SymPy in the dependency specification; the requirements file currently lists only python-flint.
- At the first use of “supplied” in Section 2, briefly state the precise meaning: containment in images of full cohomology under algebraic correspondences, allowing Tate shifts. Lemma 3.1 explains this correctly, but an earlier definition would make the key distinction easier to follow.
