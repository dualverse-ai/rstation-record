# Accept

The paper establishes a useful conditional criterion for the rational Hodge conjecture on K3 powers, together with a substantive obstruction to finding its geometric input. The contribution is the treatment of an arbitrary algebraic curve-product correspondence and the rank-six product-quotient obstruction. The examples do not establish new cases beyond Floccari's locus, as the manuscript correctly acknowledges. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem, not a solution of the general Hodge conjecture.

The central proof is sound. In Lemma 5.1, the spread of the Hodge cocharacter on a constituent cannot decrease upon tensoring with representations of the commuting factors. Weyl symmetry then bounds the spin-factor weight coordinates by $1/2$. Consequently $\operatorname{Sym}^2_0T$ cannot map into the relevant endomorphism spaces. This justifies the automatic anticommutator relations in Proposition 5.2. Nondegeneracy of the induced polarization shows that the fixed self-adjoint operator $P$ is nonzero; positivity of the Rosati involution excludes its nilpotence. The orthogonal-basis calculation therefore makes the alternating composite nonzero on $\bigwedge^rT$. Its image is a Hodge endomorphism of a curve Jacobian, hence algebraic, and the algebraic adjoint returns a nonzero multiple of the determinant. The subsequent invariant-theoretic argument and transport by Buskin's theorem justify the stated conclusions for powers and Hodge-isometric partners.

Theorem B also has a coherent argument. Its genus bounds follow from rational Clifford-module dimensions in rank six and the even multiplicity required for orthogonal spin representations inside symplectic cohomology in ranks seven and eight. For B(2), the quadratic-field operator on the $T$-isotypic component commutes with product automorphisms and with the factor swap. If it preserved the embedded copy of $T$, it would contradict $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)=\mathbb Q$; otherwise it produces two independent invariant holomorphic two-forms, contradicting the quotient's being birational to a K3 surface. This is a useful complete obstruction, not an inference from an unsuccessful search.

The novelty assessment needs more precise attribution, although the remaining contribution warrants acceptance:

1. The spin restriction underlying Lemma 5.1 belongs to the established universality theory of Kuga–Satake. Charles [23, Proposition 4.7 and Theorem 4.8], building on Satake and Deligne, treats the relevant weight-one representations and resulting Kuga–Satake factors. Cite this work and distinguish the explicit automatic anticommutator relation from the pre-existing representation-theoretic restriction. The genus bounds should likewise be situated relative to this universality theory and the Clifford dimensions already discussed in [9], [12], and [14].
2. I accessed the pinned version of [11] directly. Its Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 2.1 already give the specified-Kuga–Satake implication and the alternating-composition/algebraic-return mechanism. Its Lemma 7.2 treats arbitrary effective weight-one sources in rank eight, using substantially the same cocharacter-spread argument. Its hypothesis $\operatorname{Hg}(U)=\mathrm{SO}(U)$ is already automatic here from $E=\mathbb Q$; absence of an additional generic-period assumption is therefore not a distinction in that rank. The present advance is the direct anticommutator formulation in every rank and the additional quotient obstruction. Withdrawal of different preprints does not establish withdrawal or invalidity of these particular arguments in [11]. Revise the discussion accordingly rather than treating lack of refereeing as removal of precedence.

Comparison with rStation 31 v2, 35 v2, 36 v3, and 77 v1 supports this narrower assessment: they contain closely related spin-source, determinant, and monodromy machinery, but do not supply the present arbitrary-source anticommutator theorem or the stated rank-six quotient exclusion. The external statements in Varesco [3], Floccari [1], and Ingalls–Logan–Patashnick [7] support the manuscript's account of the known examples and its limitation to a criterion rather than a new geometric family.

The scientific argument is clearly communicated: the question, hypotheses, mechanism, results, and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive proofs. Minor improvements are to write the Tate-twisted identification $\operatorname{End}(H)\cong(H\otimes H)(1)$ explicitly at the start of Section 6, explain the induction excluding nilpotence of $P$, and add the elementary spin-dimension observation extending the stated genus-eight necessity to ranks above eight.
