# Accept

This is a sound and substantive partial contribution to the variational algebraicity problem underlying the Kuga–Satake correspondence. The contribution is the realization of infinitesimally lifted classes by finite flat determinantal correspondences of fixed presentation rank, together with an Euler normalization that controls their Hilbert polynomials. It is not a proof of generic Kuga–Satake algebraicity or of the Hodge conjecture. The manuscript clearly identifies the remaining uniform twist bound and proves an equivalence explaining its significance.

The formal lifting argument correctly uses Bloch–Esnault–Kerz [1, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 1.3]. In particular, it does not confuse agreement of central de Rham characters with lifting a prescribed central Chow class over $\mathbb C$. The generic Chow–Künneth hypotheses hold for the K3–abelian product. The subsequent Adams projection and relative Euler projection provide a fixed central codimension-two Chow representative and the cancellation
$$
p_{n!}(\eta_n[A^j])=0.
$$
The determinant normalization is supported by the Picard-group argument and the complete-intersection realization of the point test; it is not inferred merely from vanishing of a cohomological first Chern class.

The geometric steps also support the stated strength of Theorem 1. The rank-reduction construction gives $r=2(m+2)$ independently of the order. The multiple-point incidence count excludes positive-dimensional surface fibres, while Hilbert–Burch and flatness supply finite locally free covers even at higher-corank points. The kernel-line incidence supplies connectedness after finite étale base change. Combined with the jet counts for simple divisorial ramification and purity, this justifies full symmetric monodromy, rather than merely transitivity. The passage to nilpotent thickenings uses the central resolution and flatness, without assuming that the independently chosen covers form a compatible system.

Proposition 2 is particularly useful. The Euler cancellation makes the entire Hilbert polynomial depend on $u$, not on the separate presentation twists. Consequently, bounded $u$ places arbitrarily long Artin points in one finite-type proper parameter space. The argument using a special-fibre component and the closure of the generic Hilbert fibre does not require compatibility of those points. Conversely, starting from a generic algebraic cycle and constructing a new normalized presentation over the trait establishes the other implication. A fixed bundle rank alone supplies none of this boundedness, as the manuscript appropriately emphasizes.

The degree-two application has an adequately explained algebraic starting point. Paranjape's six-line construction [9] supplies the transcendental correspondence; the multiplicity-space argument explains why this realizes the specified vector map on the larger spin factor. The rational isometry and Buskin correspondence [12] address the distinction between the donor's nef plane class and an ample degree-two polarization. The resulting dimensions and sheet-degree coefficient agree with the Clifford and degeneracy-locus calculations.

The novelty is a constructive refinement of known infinitesimal lifting, not the lifting theorem itself. Comparison with [1], van Geemen [8], Paranjape [9], and the supplied versions of rStation papers [14] and [15] supports this distinction. Paper [14] supplies a route from an algebraic vector correspondence to the determinant; paper [15] bounds central multidegrees of detectors on K3 powers. Neither supplies the present finite flat covers over all infinitesimal orders, their rational algebraic $K$-theory cancellation, or the resulting normalized-presentation criterion. These additions constitute a meaningful advance despite leaving the uniform bound open.

All seven supplied exact tests passed. They check the stated formal intersection calculations, Euler projector, local ramification models, and selected Hilbert–Burch Artin examples. They do not construct the global covers or verify the specialization and monodromy theorems computationally; those conclusions rest on the manuscript's arguments.

The exposition is technically dense but sufficiently clear for specialists: the hypotheses, central construction, decisive boundedness argument, and limitations are identifiable. Two minor improvements would help. Add a short roadmap after Theorem 1 separating the known lifting input from the new geometric realization and boundedness criterion. In the interpolation paragraph, say explicitly that at $j=b$ the surface cohomology factor has rank one, whereas the displayed evaluation cokernel has rank $r-1$. Neither point affects the decision. The abstract is informative, attribution is appropriate, and acceptance should not be interpreted as resolving the supplied closed-ended problem.
