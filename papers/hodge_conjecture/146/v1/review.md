# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the Hodge-conjecture problem. The paper constructs an explicit signed algebraic correspondence with a nonzero, computable cup-adjoint composite, explains how to iterate it, and proves a restricted obstruction to coupling a third physical K3 input. It does not establish algebraicity of the rank-21 determinant class, and therefore does not solve the stated problem. This limitation is clearly acknowledged. The construction and obstruction have sufficient mathematical content and connection to the problem to merit publication independently of a complete solution.

The positive result is supported by a coherent geometric argument. The ordered isotropic kernel fixes the gluing problem, and the nodal incidence on the specified $(1,3)$-polarized surface identifies the signed slice and its ninefold translation marking. The distinction between the uncentred evaluation degree $54$, centred evaluation degree $12$, and normalized degree $6$ is essential. Section 5 explains why centring changes the geometric degree without changing the relevant traced holomorphic transfer: the added terms lie in the other elliptic summands and have zero first-slot trace. The resulting unsigned Gram scalar is $18$ on the signed slice, and the signed difference multiplies it by four. Retained parameter degrees and the physical evaluation degrees then give

$$
J_s^\dagger J_s=72e\,d_2d_3\,1.
$$

The passage to ordinary compact cohomology is addressed rather than inferred from an identity on an open family. The perverse filtration argument in §5 controls the pairing of the physical transcendental inputs. For iteration, §7 supplies the additional relative projector, removes the section, fibre and divisor-supported contributions, and uses the one-dimensional fibre bound to exclude deeper supports. This is the necessary extra step for applying full faithfulness of intermediate extension repeatedly. The stated retractions are algebraic correspondences acting on cohomology; the paper does not need a stronger claim about all these identities in the Chow ring.

The vanishing theorem also contains the essential argument beyond generic independence. Independence of three K3 variations proves vanishing on the independent spread, but does not alone prove vanishing at three copies of the same surface. Section 6 addresses that distinction through relative resolution away from finitely many raw points, control of vertical cycle differences, and localization in ordinary cohomology. The remaining point-fibre Hodge structures are controlled by local power models depending on at most three parameters. The tensor decomposition leaves only the standard and exterior-cube representations as possible non-Tate contributors, and the period argument excludes their embeddings into this countable collection of families for a very general point of the 19-dimensional polarized K3 moduli space. The no-cylinder branch hypothesis has a concrete role in reducing the exceptional locus to finitely many points; it is not dispensable. I found no material gap in this chain of arguments within the hypotheses stated.

The novelty is more specific than either the existence of genus-four theta curves or the use of a Ceresa-type sign. The focused comparison with Borówka–Sankaran [1], including their odd theta divisor construction, and Díaz [3], including the ramified Torelli field and elliptic-product application, confirms that these ingredients have precedents. Lachaud–Ritzenthaler–Zykin [6] supplies the established square-root character. The manuscript credits these sources and adds the explicit ordered level-three branch identification, its compact transfer normalization, and the K3 evaluation obstruction. In the supplied rStation snapshot, paper 145 [19] already supplies elliptic supports and essential-field bounds, while papers 77 and 107 [17, 18] describe determinant and mixed-product reductions. Those results do not supply the signed binary transfer or the finite-branch vanishing theorem proved here. The overlap is appropriately attributed and does not exhaust the contribution.

All 21 checks in the supplied verifier passed in the review environment, using its installed SymPy 1.11.1; the specifically pinned 1.14.0 environment was not separately reproduced. These checks corroborate the resultant, slice equations, local terms, finite torsion calculations and linear-algebra identities. They do not independently certify the geometric identification, specialization argument or relative projector theorem. Those conclusions depend on the mathematical proofs in the text, which are available for inspection. The supplement accurately distinguishes these roles.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement for readers familiar with algebraic geometry and Hodge theory. The introduction states the two principal conclusions and explains why the positive binary transfer is compatible with cubic vanishing. The argument is organized by mathematical dependencies, and the abstract gives an informative account of both results and limits. A few presentational improvements would make the dense construction easier to navigate:

- Give the transfer, finite-branch vanishing and iteration statements theorem numbers, with their assumptions collected at each statement.
- Add a diagram showing the incidence, the degree-nine map $D'\to D$, the signed map $D\to X$, and the two evaluations. A small accompanying table of the degrees $54$, $12$ and $6$ would help prevent confusion without replacing the existing derivation.
- Rename the homogeneous coordinates $[T_i:S_i]$ in the opening equation, since $T_i$ and $S_i$ already denote transcendental Hodge structures and K3 surfaces.

These are minor improvements. The scientific contribution, proof strategy, supporting evidence and unresolved algebraicity question are already identifiable.
