# A signed level-three Torelli correspondence and elliptic transfer for K3 surfaces

## 1. The correspondence

We work over $\mathbb C$ with rational cycle classes. An elliptic sweep of a projective K3 surface $S_i$ means a smooth projective surface $Y_i$ fibred over a smooth projective curve $B_i$, with a nonisotrivial elliptic generic fibre, a section and full symplectic level three. The evaluation $h_i:Y_i\to S_i$ is proper and generically finite of degree $d_i$. A finite marking of the physical sweep is included in $d_i$. Write $T_i=\operatorname{NS}(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}^{\perp}\subset H^2(S_i,\mathbb Q)$. The physical bases vary independently, including when the targets or the sweeps are copies of one another.

Let $X=X(3)=\mathbb P^1$. The ordered gluing below gives a signed double cover $Z\to X^3$. Its generic curve has genus three and three maps of degree three to the specified elliptics. For two fixed general marked elliptics the signed parameter curve $D$ is the odd genus-four theta curve on a specific $(1,3)$-polarized abelian surface. Equivalently it is the quotient by a group of order nine of the normalization of the nodal discriminant of the dual $(1,3)$ net. The explicit global equation is the normalization of

$$
w^2=X_1X_2X_3P,\qquad [X_i:Z_i]=[3T_iS_i^2:T_i^3+2S_i^3].
$$

Here $P$ is the primitive reduced homogeneous norm of $t_1t_2t_3+2$, of multidegree $(9,9,9)$, and $w$ belongs to $\mathcal O(5,5,5)$. We take the sign cover before any subsequent trace.

Pull the cover back to the product of three physical bases and choose a smooth projective parameter model $L$ of a component. Let $p:Q\to L$ be a smooth projective model of the first elliptic family. Let $e$ be the sum of the degrees over the standard **signed** slice $D$ of the parameter curves above a very general point of $B_2\times B_3$. The full signed cycle, the Abel curve minus its inverse, induces an ordinary algebraic map

$$
J_s:T_2\otimes T_3\hookrightarrow H^4(Q,\mathbb Q),\qquad
J_s^\dagger J_s=72e\,d_2d_3\,1.
$$

The dagger denotes the cup adjoint, with the domain projected to the displayed tensor. The normalized adjoint is a retraction induced by a rational cycle. Adding the third physical class from the first elliptic family and integrating over $p$ gives a map to $H^4(L)(-1)$. This cubic is zero when the three targets are the same very general primitively polarized K3. We prove a finite-branch theorem that applies to this cubic and to other relative elliptic cycles. The two conclusions coexist because the positive map retains the first elliptic cohomology, while the cubic tests it against a physical transcendental class.

The ingredients have distinct precedents. Borówka and Sankaran prove the uniqueness up to translation of the smooth hyperelliptic genus-four curve on a general $(1,3)$-polarized surface and describe its complementary abelian surfaces [1]. We identify the particular odd divisor on the special split-isogenous surface, its coefficient parameter, and its labelled branch. Hain identifies the genus-three Ceresa infinitesimal invariant with the canonical quartic and studies the rank at hyperelliptic curves [2]. Díaz, building on Buhler, Schoen and Top, uses a Ceresa-type cycle on an isogenous elliptic triple, normal functions and ramification of cycle fields to prove non-divisibility results in Chow groups [3, 4]. The invariants here are an ordinary compact cup-adjoint scalar and an obstruction after evaluation on physical K3 surfaces. Polarized torsion gluing and the genus-three square-root character are used in their established forms [5, 6].

## 2. The ordered kernel and a compact-type calculation

Identify the three marked torsion planes with $V=\mathbb F_3^2$, set $h=e_0+f_0$ in a symplectic frame and write $\ell=\langle h\rangle$. Use the maximal isotropic

$$
K=\{(v,v,v)+(0,\lambda,-\lambda):v\in V,\ \lambda\in\ell\}.
$$

The diagonal plane is isotropic in characteristic three and is orthogonal to the last generator. Write $a:\prod E_i\to W$ for the quotient with $a^*\lambda_W=3\lambda_{\prod E_i}$, and $b=a^t$. Then $ba=ab=[3]$. If $F\subset\prod E_i$ is elliptic and its nonconstant projection degrees are $n_i$, the restriction of the principal polarization to $aF$ has degree $3\sum n_i/|F\cap K|$. With one, two or three nonzero projections the denominator is bounded by $1,3,9$ respectively. A principal elliptic factor therefore forces three degree-one projections, with $F[3]\subset K$. Every decomposable principally polarized threefold has a principal elliptic factor. This confines decomposability to the equal-$j$ locus of codimension two. An indecomposable principally polarized threefold is a genus-three Jacobian. The two polarized identifications at a nonhyperelliptic member distinguish the Torelli sign. The square-root character is given, in the Hodge determinant line, by $\sqrt{\chi_{18}}$ [6]. On the signed family the maps $f_i$ are obtained from $b_i$ and an Abel origin. Their norms on the curve satisfy

$$
f_{i*}f_j^*=3\delta_{ij},\qquad
H^1(C)=\bigoplus_i f_i^*H^1(E_i).
$$

In a symplectic basis starting with $h$, the stabilizer among independent frames consists of $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\epsilon&t_i\\0&\epsilon\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with common $\epsilon=\pm1$ and $\sum t_i=0$. Indeed the three entries of $K$ have a common image in $V/\ell$ and their differences generate $\ell$. The induced maps preserve $\ell$ and agree on $V/\ell$, and the diagonal vectors give the sum condition. Slot permutations also preserve $K$. The unipotent generator fixing the cusp whose vanishing line is $\ell$ acts on $X$ by $x\mapsto\zeta x$, with that cusp at $0$, the other cusps at $1,\zeta,\zeta^2$ and the smooth fixed point at $\infty$.

On the aligned diagonal for a non-CM elliptic $E$, the diagonal elliptic maps to a principal elliptic $E'\simeq E$, with complement $a(\ker\sum)$. This is the unique principal elliptic factor. Indeed elliptic subgroups have primitive integral slopes, and the principal condition forces squared slope length three. Mixed signs would give a difference outside $\ell$ on three-torsion. The complementary principal surface is thus $J(C_2)$ for a smooth genus-two curve. Each restriction $\beta_i:J(C_2)\to E$ of $b_i$ satisfies $\beta_i\beta_j^t=3\delta_{ij}-1$ and $\sum\beta_i=0$, by subtracting the contribution of $E'$ from $bb^t$.

The two normal directions to the aligned diagonal map isomorphically to the off-block directions of the period matrix for $E'\times J(C_2)$. In fact the derivative, after the fixed rational isogeny, is restriction of the diagonal form $\operatorname{diag}(\dot\tau_1,\dot\tau_2,\dot\tau_3)$ between the diagonal and the sum-zero plane. Its kernel is the simultaneous variation $\dot\tau_1=\dot\tau_2=\dot\tau_3$. The compact-type Torelli fibre with the Jacobian identified consists of $E'\cup_p C_2$ for $p\in C_2$, with the other attachment at the elliptic origin. Identifying the Jacobian keeps $p$ and its hyperelliptic conjugate distinct except at Weierstrass points. In plumbing $xy=s$ the first off-block term is $s$ times a nonzero common scalar times evaluation of the genus-two canonical forms at $p$. This follows by extending a normalized differential across the neck by the differential of the second kind with the prescribed double pole and using the bilinear relation [7]. The component periods eliminate their four moduli coordinates and the two off-block equations eliminate the two normal base coordinates. The fibre product is smooth near the compact fibre with coordinates given by the diagonal parameter, $p$ and $s$. The pullback of the diagonal ideal is $(s)$ by basepoint-freeness of the canonical system. Properness of stable Torelli over the abelian locus and the universal property of blowup give a proper map to the blowup of the diagonal, quasi-finite near the exceptional curve by the canonical map. Thus this is the normalization of the actual signed extension on that blowup. Its exceptional map is the canonical double cover with six simple branch directions. The simple branch points give smooth residual branches transverse to the exceptional line there.

This chart also supplies the pairwise immersion used for the nodal incidence. Each pair of $\beta_i^*\omega$ spans the genus-two forms, by the norm matrix. Choose an attachment away from the Weierstrass points and the zeros of their pairwise differences. At the node the two relevant pulled-back differentials have coefficients $a x-b_i y+O((x,y)^2)$ in the frame $dx/x$, with $a\ne0$ and $b_i\ne b_j$. The two coefficients have only the node as a common zero locally. Away from the node there are no limiting common zeros on either component. Hence on nearby smooth curves each pair gives an immersion. Openness gives the same conclusion on a general frozen slice. In a Weierstrass-based Abel map the genus-two component of the compact-type signed cycle cancels. The two remaining graphs have entries $s_0\pm\beta_i(p-q)$, where $s_0\in E$ and $q$ is the fixed Weierstrass origin. This formula follows by moving the divisor degree across the separating node. The pointed stabilization at $p=q$ inserts a rational bridge contracted by the Abel map. Thus the local calculation uses the actual signed family and keeps the two generic signs distinct.

## 3. The nodal net and its coefficient curve

Freeze general nonisogenous $E_2,E_3$. Put $A=E_2\times E_3$, $q:A\to B=A/\langle(h_2,-h_3)\rangle$, $A_0=\widehat B$, and $\tau=\widehat q:A_0\to A$ using the product polarization. The line bundle $M=\tau^*(\mathcal O_{E_2}(0)\boxtimes\mathcal O_{E_3}(0))$ has type $(1,3)$, square six and polarization homomorphism $q\tau$. The two inverse images of the axes are connected since the character of $\tau$ is nontrivial on either axis. They meet in three points and span the rational Néron–Severi group. The inclusion $B\subset W$ is dual to $u:W\to A_0$ with connected kernel the first elliptic. The last two transposes are $\tau u$. The joint map of the curve to $A$ has degree one onto its image since the alternative degree three would identify both elliptics with its normalization. The lift to $A_0$ is also birational. Its image meets each inverse axis in degree three, hence has class $M$ and arithmetic genus four. Translation by the polarization isogeny gives nine translates in $|M|$.

Conversely let $C$ normalize a one-nodal member of $|M|$. The induced $u:J(C)\to A_0$ is surjective by ampleness. If its kernel were disconnected, passing to the connected quotient would lift the image to a finite étale isogeny of degree $n>1$ with lifted square $6/n$, since the $n$ translates sum to the pullback. Its arithmetic genus is at least three, forcing $n=1$. The composite $uu^t$ is the dual polarization of $M$. Indeed the induced cup form is integration of the two ambient one-forms against $c_1(M)$, which exchanges the two elementary divisors on a polarization basis in dimension two. The embedded dual $B$ and the connected elliptic kernel have complementary primitive lattices in a principal lattice. Their discriminant groups have equal order, so the elliptic restriction has degree three. The gluing graph is an anti-isometry of the elliptic three-torsion with $K_{23}^{\perp}/K_{23}$, where $K_{23}=\langle(h_2,-h_3)\rangle$. The map $v\mapsto[(v,v)]$ is an anti-isometry onto this plane since the form on its image is twice the slot form modulo three. It determines the recovered symplectic level uniquely. The polarized identification with $J(C)$ retains its sign. Both constructions are relative on the nodal open by simultaneous normalization, the relative Jacobian and the finite torsion schemes.

For an explicit net, put $G_i=E_i/\langle h_i\rangle$ and use the dual three-isogenies $G_i\to E_i$. Their product covers $A_0$ with degree three. In Hesse coordinates $\sum_j s_{ij}^3=3a_i\prod_j s_{ij}$, the subgroup of the covering is the annihilator of $(h_2,-h_3)$ for the product isogeny pairing. Take a lift $R_i\in G_i[3]$ of $h_i$ and a generator $D_i$ of the dual kernel with the same pairing with $R_i$ in each slot. The annihilator is generated by $(D_2,D_3)$. Use the shifts $R_2,-R_3$. Their opposite pairing weights allow the two shifts to be cyclic coordinate permutations and the $D_i$ to act diagonally with opposite weights. The pullback of the net is consequently $[s_{20}s_{30}:s_{21}s_{31}:s_{22}s_{32}]$. This is basepoint-free since each cubic point has at most one zero coordinate. On $A_0$ it gives a finite map $\Phi$ of degree six by ampleness.

On the coordinate torus the logarithmic conormal of each cubic is $(s_{ij}^3-a_i\prod s_{ij})_j$ in the sum-zero plane. The ramification condition is proportionality of the two conormals. Let $U_i,V_i$ be the two nontrivial Fourier sums of the cubes and $W_i=\prod s_{ij}$. These descend to plane coordinates on $E_i$, with equation

$$
27(a_i^3-1)W_i^3-9a_iU_iV_iW_i+U_i^3+V_i^3=0.
$$

This follows from the sum and product of the three cubes, and the generic fibres of the coordinate map are the diagonal order-three orbits. The conormal condition pulls back $F=\{U_2V_3=V_2U_3\}\subset A$. The maps $r_i=U_i/V_i$ have degree three, with branch polynomial $(a_i^3-1)(r^3+1)^2-4a_i^3r^3$ from the cubic discriminant. For $a_i\ne0$, $a_i^3\ne1$, $a_2^3\ne a_3^3$, their six simple branch values are disjoint. Hence $F$ is smooth and connected by ampleness. Its degree-nine map to the ratio line has total ramification 36, giving genus ten. The cyclic quotient $F/K_{23}$ is smooth of genus four. It is the odd divisor for the symmetric descent of $\mathcal O_A(3\Theta_A)$. The cyclic shifts preserve its section, and inversion exchanges $U_i,V_i$ and fixes $W_i$, making the section odd. Symmetric descent of the rigidified bundle is unique since the descent characters have odd order. The origins are $[1:-1:0]$; the other three fixed points lie on $U_i=V_i$. The odd isogeny $q$ identifies the two-torsion, so the ten fixed points on the quotient lift to $(0,0)$ and the nine ordered pairs of nonzero two-torsion. Inversion is hyperelliptic by Riemann–Hurwitz. This identifies the particular representative of the hyperelliptic theta curve of [1].

The scheme of ramification $R_\Phi$ is $(q\tau)^{-1}(F/K_{23})$. The conormal determinant gives this equality on the torus with multiplicity one since the fibre product is smooth. Both divisor classes are $3M$ by the canonical bundle formula and the pullback of $F$. The pullback has no boundary component because each ratio map is finite. Class equality and ampleness exclude any residual boundary. Thus $R_\Phi$ is smooth of genus 28 and connected by ampleness. The differential of $\Phi$ has rank one along it. With exactly one vanishing coordinate in one factor the corresponding target coordinate varies transversely, and with different vanishing coordinates in the two factors there are two independent directions. When both vanish at the same index, the ratio of remaining coordinates has nonzero derivative by the Hesse equation and $a_i\ne0$. On the torus each individual tangent image is nonzero. The singular-member map $\gamma:R_\Phi\to |M|$ therefore sends a point to the unique line through its image containing the rank-one tangent.

There are three reducible members, the coordinate sections. Indeed the degrees of any effective summand on the covering product are nonnegative and divisible by three because it is invariant under the translating subgroup with nonzero projections. The only possible splitting of $(3,3)$ is into $(3,0)$ and $(0,3)$. Their eigensections give a section of the net exactly for matching indices. This also excludes nonreduced members. Each coordinate member is a union of two elliptics meeting at three transverse nodes. The ratios at their images are the three cube roots of unity, each occurring once on $A_0$ by the covering action. The local discriminant at that member has three smooth branches with distinct tangent lines, given by evaluation at the three distinct node images. Properness of the singular-point incidence excludes other branches. A general member on one of the branches is integral with one node. Since every singular member is in the image of the connected $R_\Phi$, the reduced discriminant is irreducible and $R_\Phi$ is its normalization. A general pencil meets the nodal open transversely. The first jet of its section has top Chern number $3M^2=18$ on $A_0\times\mathbb P^1$ because $\Omega^1_{A_0}$ is trivial. Thus $\deg\gamma^*\mathcal O(1)=18$.

The translate action on $R_\Phi$ of $K(M)=\ker(q\tau)$ is free, and the node maps to $q\tau(p)$. The two reconstructions above identify $R_\Phi/K(M)$ with the compact signed Torelli slice $D$, including its elliptic level. To see dominance, a fixed elliptic parameter gives a fixed ppav, finitely many identifications up to curve isomorphism, and finitely many fixed-system translates. The varying nodal family therefore varies in elliptic parameter. Inversion relates opposite identifications and sends the node to its negative. These are generically distinct orbits, so the reconstructed curve is generically nonhyperelliptic. Taking normalizations extends the identification, with $D\to X$ the hyperelliptic quotient and the ninefold marking étale. The three reducible members and their distinguished nodes give one orbit of nine. Normalizing the distinguished node leaves a stable two-component genus-three curve with two remaining nodes. Its image is the cusp at the fixed point lifting to $(0,0)$.

For an affine equation of this coefficient curve, put $r=U_i/V_i$ on $F$, $t=r^3$, $z_i=rW_i/V_i$ and $n_i=9a_i z_i/(t+1)$. These functions describe the quotient by the simultaneous shift because adjoining $r$ to the quotient field recovers $W_i/V_i$. Put $k_i=(a_i^3-1)/(27a_i^3)$ and $s=t/(t+1)^2$. The two cubic equations give $s=k_i n_i^3/(n_i-1)$. Inversion exchanges $t$ with $1/t$ and fixes $n_i$. With $m=n_3/n_2$, eliminating $n_2$ gives

$$
s=\frac{(k_2-k_3m^3)^3}{(1-m)m^2(k_2-k_3m^2)^2},\qquad
y^2=(1-m)\bigl[(1-m)m^2(k_2-k_3m^2)^2-4(k_2-k_3m^3)^3\bigr].
$$

Indeed $n_2=(k_2-k_3m^3)/(k_2m-k_3m^3)$, and $y=(1-m)m(k_2-k_3m^2)(1-t)/(1+t)$. These generate the claimed invariant and quadratic fields. The smooth projective model is $D$, and the distinguished cusp lies over $m=1$ by expanding at the two origins, where $r=-1$ and $W_i/V_i\sim(t+1)/(9a_i r)$.

For the cusp multiplicities of $\chi_{18}$, let the other slots approach cusps of the same type and put each vanishing line in the real direction. If that line is $\ell$, the imaginary lattice matrix, before dividing by three, is $T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\1&3&0\\1&0&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. For a common line different from $\ell$, take $\ell$ imaginary and use $T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\-1&-1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. These arise by splitting $K$ into diagonal and sum-zero subgroups in the real and imaginary directions. The real lattice matrix dual for the polarization scaled by three is $T^{-t}$, giving period matrix $T^t\operatorname{diag}(\tau_i)T/3$. In an even theta characteristic $(\epsilon,\delta)$, $m\equiv\epsilon\bmod 2$ has phase $i^{m\cdot\delta}$ and exponent $(Tm)_j^2/8$ in $q_j=\exp(2\pi i\tau_j/3)$. The first-coordinate minima of $|Tm|$ with nonzero signed coefficient have counts $18,16,2$ at $0,1,2$ in the first matrix and $20,16$ at $0,1$ in the second. For clarity, a finite check of the upper bounds is to enumerate absolute triples $a\in\{0,\ldots,8\}^3$ with $a_1\le2$, sum $i^{(T^{-1}v)\cdot\delta}$ over the distinct sign vectors $v$ of $a$ with integral $T^{-1}v\equiv\epsilon\bmod2$, and take the least surviving first entry. Parity gives the matching lower bounds except for $\epsilon=(0,1,1),\ \delta=(\delta_1,1,1)$ in the first matrix. There $m_1=0$ terms cancel by reversing $m_2$, and $(2,1,1)$ survives. This gives generic orders $3,2,2,2$. The normalized slice therefore has nine interior ramification points and one at $0$. Its elliptic surface has one $I_6$ and six $I_3$ fibres. In particular its Euler number is 24 and its holomorphic Euler characteristic is two.

At the general interior fixed pair the distinguished singular member is still nodal. On the product Hesse cover lift to $[d_i:1:1]$ with $d_i^3-3a_id_i+2=0$. In local coordinates $[d_i+c_i s_i^2+O(s_i^4):1+s_i:1-s_i]$ the cubic gives $c_i/d_i=-(d_i^3+8)/(2(d_i^3-1))$. The tangent line of the net pulls back to quadratic part proportional to $(c_2/d_2)s_2^2-s_2s_3+(c_3/d_3)s_3^2$. Its discriminant is nonzero generically since $4(c_2/d_2)(c_3/d_3)-1=9(d_2^3+d_3^3+7)/[(d_2^3-1)(d_3^3-1)]$. The smooth hyperelliptic Torelli curve maps birationally to the member, by the nonisogeny argument for the two degree-three maps. This image is the limit in the net by local extension of the Abel map and the finite choices of translation into $|M|$. The ordinary node accounts for its entire genus defect. Thus the simultaneous normalization and the distinguished node construction extend over a general smooth point of the interior branch. Equivalently the hyperelliptic involution acts by $-1$ on the Jacobian, so the moduli problem with identified Jacobian has no residual curve automorphism there and the signed coordinate is a square root of the reduced local equation [2].

## 4. The global branch and the two-cusp geometry

The degree-three marking curve $Y\to X$ that selects a nonzero two-torsion point has monodromy $S_3$, since the upper and lower level-three unipotents reduce to generators of $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_2)$. Each cusp has one unramified preimage and one of index two, and there is no interior ramification. Riemann–Hurwitz gives $Y=\mathbb P^1$, and the centralizer of $S_3$ in its three-point action is trivial. The physical two-torsion labels from the fixed-point calculation descend over the frozen pair by the intrinsic node map $q\tau$. They identify the nine reduced points of the interior branch above a general pair with $Y\times Y$ as a Galois set. In particular there is a unique component dominating that pair, with field $\mathbb C(Y_2\times Y_3)$ in the case of slots 2 and 3.

This component dominates the other pairs as well. For instance, if its projection to slots 1 and 2 were a curve, it would be the whole inverse image of that curve. Its degree nine over slots 2 and 3 would then give a degree-nine algebraic subfield over $\mathbb C(X_2)$ inside $\mathbb C(Y_2)(t_3)$, whose relative algebraic closure over $\mathbb C(X_2)$ has degree three. The argument with slots interchanged is identical. Every surface in $X^3$ dominates at least one pair by transcendence degree. Uniqueness for all pairs now proves that this irreducible divisor is the entire interior divisorial branch and that it has no two-coordinate cylinder. In particular the actual branch satisfies the projection hypothesis in §6.

Inside its function field the copy of $\mathbb C(Y_i)$ from either frozen pair is the relative algebraic closure of $\mathbb C(X_i)$, and the embeddings agree by the trivial deck group. These maps put the normalization birationally in $Y^3$, with a closure projecting birationally to every pair. Its equation has tridegree $(1,1,1)$. The level stabilizer acts by the lifted rotations with exponents $(a,a+b,a-b)$. The lifted rotation fixes the simple and double points over $0$. Put these at $t=0,\infty$, respectively, and normalize the double preimage of $1$ to $t=1$. Equivariance and the zero divisor give $x=At/(t^3+B_0)$. The critical point and value at $t=1$ give $B_0=2$ and $A=3$. The eight monomials of the tridegree-$(1,1,1)$ equation have characters $(e_1+e_2+e_3,e_2-e_3)\bmod 3$ up to a common shift. Only the constant and triple product share a character. The equation is an eigenvector and a single monomial would violate pair dominance or irreducibility. Thus $t_1t_2t_3=c$. Over the general aligned diagonal the compact-type calculation gives six branches. The marking product is étale there, and the possible orbits under common $S_3$ and slot permutations have sizes $3,18,6$, according as all labels coincide, two coincide, or all differ. The six branches are the last orbit, whose products are the product of the roots of $t^3-(3/x)t+2$, namely $-2$. The closure in $Y^3$ is smooth. At a boundary point at least one $T_i$ and one $S_j$ vanish with distinct indices, so one of the two products in the equation has exactly one vanishing factor. The finite map from this closure is the normalization of the branch divisor.

Explicitly set $a=3Z_1/X_1,\ b=3Z_2/X_2,\ c_3=3Z_3/X_3$. With resultants taken in the indicated variable, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
N(p)&=\operatorname{Res}_v(v^3-bv+2,\ p^3-c_3pv^2+2v^3),\\
P&=\frac{(X_1X_2X_3)^9}{2^{19}3^9}
\operatorname{Res}_p(N(p),\,2p^3+2ap^2-8).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first resultant runs over products of the two marked roots. The second sets $t_1=-2/p$ in its cubic after clearing $p^3$. The multiplier clears the degrees and divides out the integral content. The resultant has nine distinct roots in a generic free coordinate, with no coordinate-plane factors after this homogenization. For example, the coefficient of the symmetric orbit $X_2^9X_3^9 Z_1^9$ is 4, so each $X_i=0$ is avoided generically, and the same orbit tests $Z_i=0$. The square-root line before even factors is the ninth power of the determinant Hodge line, which is $\mathcal O(1,1,1)$. The degree in each slot of the interior divisor is nine. Together with the cusp multiplicities, these exhaust degree eighteen in each slot and give $X_1X_2X_3P$ after removing twice each cusp plane. The Picard group has no two-torsion and a complex constant is a square. This proves the global equation of the signed extension.

The quotient coordinate in §3 can be calibrated in the same frame. Rescaling $W_i$ in the quotient cubic gives Hesse parameter with cube $a_i^3/(a_i^3-1)$. The subgroup $\langle h_i\rangle$ acts diagonally in these coordinates, so it is the vanishing subgroup at the coordinate triangle. The reciprocal parameter therefore satisfies $x_i^3=27k_i$. On $U_i=V_i$ the third Hesse coordinate divided by $V_i$ is $t_i=2x_i n_i/3$ and satisfies the physical marking cubic. At the nine labelled branch points the two cubic equations yield $(t_2t_3)^3=4(n_2-1)(n_3-1)$. Substituting this, $m=n_3/n_2$ and $t_1=-2/(t_2t_3)$ gives

$$
x_1=\frac{(x_2x_3)^2(1-m)}{x_2^3-x_3^3m}.
$$

Both sides give Möbius maps from the quotient line to the varying level line and agree at the nine labelled points. This identifies the affine model of §3 with the marked slice of the global equation.

The norm also determines the geometry above two cusp coordinates. If both cusps are fixed, take $x_1=x_2=0$ and $x_3$ general. The six lifts of the interior divisor are $(0,\infty,r)$ and $(\infty,0,r)$ for the three marked roots $r$ above $x_3$. In the first chart, with $s_2=1/t_2$, one has $t_1=-2s_2/t_3$, $x_1=-3s_2/r+O(s_2^4)$, $x_2=3s_2^2+O(s_2^5)$. Thus the three smooth branches have $x_2=(r^2/3)x_1^2+O(x_1^3)$, with distinct coefficients for general $x_3$. The other three are obtained by interchanging 1 and 2. The signed branch includes the two axes, so the transverse initial monomial is a unit times $x_1^4x_2^4$. Blowing up the origin removes an even multiplicity eight. At either axis direction the three strict transforms of the parabolas and the strict transform of the axis meet at a point with four distinct directions, including slope zero. The exceptional line from the first blowup is unbranched. Blowing up each of the two fourfold points removes multiplicity four and produces a normalized double cover of the corresponding exceptional line branched at four points. This describes the genus-one transverse exceptional curves. If just one cusp is fixed, a pair with the other cusp nonfixed lies away from the interior branch at a general free value, because $t=0,\infty$ over the fixed cusp and the other two factors are finite nonzero. If neither cusp is fixed, the marked values at those cusps are finite nonzero and force $t$ in the free slot to take finitely many values. These charts distinguish the two-fixed line from a branch cylinder. The two-fixed line is contained in the branch, but it is indexed by one fixed pair, not by a curve of pairs.

## 5. Ordinary transfer of two K3 inputs

### Compact slice and the one marking trace

Write $D'=R_\Phi$. The uncentred evaluation $(p,z)\mapsto \tau z$ on the normalized incidence over $D'$ has degree $3\cdot18=54$ onto $A$: a general point of $A_0$ determines a pencil that meets the discriminant in eighteen members. Simultaneous translation of $p,z$ by $K(M)$ translates the uncentred image by $\tau(K(M))$. The centred evaluation $g_c([p,z])=\tau(z-p)$ descends to $D$. For fixed general $a\in A_0$, the condition $p+a\in C_p$ is a section of $\gamma^*\mathcal O(1)\otimes(p\mapsto p+a)^*M$ on $D'$. Its degree is $18+M\cdot R_\Phi=36$, giving the centred degree $3\cdot36/9=12$. This counts generic incidence points with their degree and is unaffected by normalizing the nodes. The number $54/9=6$ is the normalized degree for the transfer calculation. It is distinct from the geometric degree of the centred map.

Let $Z_{\mathrm{node}}$ be the divisor of the two preimages of the node on the normalized genus-three family. The line bundle $g_{c,2}^*\mathcal O_{E_2}(0)(-Z_{\mathrm{node}})$ has relative degree one. Use it to define the Abel map and apply $b_1$ to obtain $f$ to the first elliptic surface. This construction is algebraic and needs no choice of node branch. On the opposite Torelli sheet inversion negates the centred maps and carries the degree-one line bundle to its counterpart, so the first evaluation is negated as well. The model over $D'$ uses the pullback of this $f$ and the uncentred $g_u$. Work on smooth projective graph models.

Let $\Omega=\omega_2\wedge\omega_3$ generate $H^{2,0}(A)$. The incidence class in $D'\times A$ has no term in $H^1(D')\otimes H^1(A)$. The fixed-system universal divisor has exterior-product line bundle, since the images under $\tau$ are birational images and their line bundle is the norm of $M$. A closure difference consists of entire fibres, which also have no mixed term. Trace $g_u^*\Omega$ to $D'\times E_i$ for $i=2,3$. Its pairing against any antiholomorphic two-form, necessarily a product from the two factors, is zero by the incidence class and degree. Hence both holomorphic traces vanish. On a smooth parameter chart $g_u^*\Omega=ds\wedge\gamma_s$, and these traces are $ds$ times the two fibrewise traces of $\gamma_s$. The orthogonal splitting of $H^1(C)$ places $\gamma_s$ in the first elliptic line. Therefore $f^*f_*g_u^*\Omega=3g_u^*\Omega$ on the smooth locus and on the compact surfaces by holomorphy of proper trace.

Here $T(A)=H^1(E_2)\otimes H^1(E_3)$ by nonisogeny. It is simple as a rational Hodge structure, since polarization splits a substructure and a complementary summand without the unique $(2,0)$ line would be rational of type $(1,1)$. The identity extends to $T(A)$. The unsigned compact transfer $f_*g_u^*$ on the marked surface consequently has Gram $3\cdot54=162$. It is odd for first-slot inversion, which is $-1$ on the holomorphic two-forms of the elliptic surface, and simplicity extends this oddness to the image of $T(A)$. Centre the map by subtracting the varying node. In the pullback of $\Omega$ the change consists of base differentials wedged with the individual fixed-slot differentials. They have zero trace under $f$ by $f_{1*}f_i^*=0$. First-slot Abel translations act trivially on the holomorphic output for the same base-form reason. These holomorphic identities extend to $T(A)$ by simplicity. On the common marked open the centred transfer is pulled back from $D$, and equality extends on the compact surfaces. Proper degree trace divides $162$ by nine once, giving Gram $18$ on $D$. The full signed difference doubles this odd image and gives Gram $72$. Any retained parameter cover of degree $r$ multiplies this number by $r$, while a degree-$r$ marking used solely for the calculation is removed by the single trace on the common comparison. The signed slice already includes the double cover.

### Passage to the absolute cup pairing

Let $U$ be a smooth projective model of the total curve with maps $f:U\to Q$ and $g:U\to Y_2\times Y_3$, over $B_2\times B_3$. They are the three centred evaluation maps over the fixed signed component. Graph closure also implements inversion. Set $B_{23}=B_2\times B_3,\ P_{23}=Y_2\times Y_3$ and $R=f_*g^*$. The relative complexes are $K_M=R(M\to B_{23})_*\mathbb Q_M[4]$ for $M=P_{23},U,Q$ in this paragraph. The two proper maps give unit and trace morphisms between these complexes, with Verdier duals the cup adjoints. Over a common dense smooth open the full-support perverse degree-zero part for $P_{23}$ contains $(V_2\boxtimes V_3)[2]$, where $V_i=R^1(Y_i/B_i)_*\mathbb Q$ over the smooth loci. The slice calculation shows that $R^\dagger R$ acts as $18e$ on this summand, as does $R^\dagger A_1R$ with $A_1=(1-\iota_{1*})/2$. The projective decomposition theorem and full faithfulness of intermediate extension extend these identities to $IC_{B_{23}}(V_2\boxtimes V_3)$ in degree zero. Distinct strict supports have zero Hom. This calculation thus includes the compact discriminant through the relative adjoints [8].

For $Y_i\to B_i$, the perverse $+1$ term of $H^2(Y_i)$ is tested by degree on the generic fibre, the $-1$ term is generated by the fibre class and the point supports of degree zero are generated by components of singular fibres. These descriptions follow from proper base change in degree two, the decomposition theorem and duality. The pullback of $T_i$ has degree zero on every component by the projection formula. It lies in filtration zero with graded image in $H^0(B_i,IC_{B_i}(V_i))$. External product adds the filtration indices. Hence the two physical pullbacks have graded image in $H^0(B_{23},IC_{B_{23}}(V_2\boxtimes V_3))$. Verdier duality makes filtration $-1$ orthogonal to filtration zero in middle cohomology, so it suffices to pair on the degree-zero graded piece. This orthogonality follows by dualizing perverse truncation and identifying the dual of an image with the quotient by its annihilator. The graded pairing is compatible with the strict-support decomposition. The degree-$d_i$ traces $h_{i*}h_i^*$ now give $18e d_2d_3$ for the unsigned Gram and also for the Gram of $A_1R$ on the two physical inputs.

The two pulled-back $T_i$ are odd under elliptic inversion. On an equivariant resolution the invariant quotient is birational to a ruled surface, whose $H^2$ is Tate. Proper trace through a quotient resolution gives the same conclusion for the invariant part, up to exceptional divisor classes, and the pullbacks of $T_i$ are orthogonal to divisors. Simultaneous inversion of both inputs therefore acts by $+1$, so the full signed correspondence on them is $2A_1R(h_2^*\boxtimes h_3^*)$. With source-first graphs its cycle is the signed push from $U$ into $(S_2\times S_3)\times Q$, composed with the product of the two K3 transcendental projectors. The projector on a K3 is obtained from the diagonal by subtracting the two point terms and the divisor pairing using the inverse intersection matrix. It is algebraic and self-transpose. The resulting identity is $J_s^\dagger J_s=72e d_2d_3\,1$. Thus both the embedding and its retraction are ordinary rational correspondences in cohomology.

## 6. A finite-branch obstruction to the third slot

Let $k_0=\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Fix a finite dominant normal cover $Z_f\to X^3$ over $k_0$ and a rational relative codimension-two cycle over its generic point in the product of the three marked elliptic curves, also over $k_0$. Assume that every divisorial branch component is a coordinate plane or dominates all three pairs. In particular there is no component pulled back from a curve in a pair that dominates both coordinates. Take the independent physical bases $B_i\to X$ and sweeps to the same very general primitively polarized $S$. Put $R_0=Z_f\times_{X^3}\prod B_i$. Let $L_0$ be a smooth projective resolution of a component of $R_0$. Use the generic cycle with its multiplicities, closing it over $L_0$ in the incidence with $\prod Y_i$ and pushing to $L_0\times S^3$. Denote the resulting class by $[C]$. Then the evaluated map

$$
U_C:T(S)^{\otimes3}\longrightarrow H^4(L_0)(-1),\qquad
t\longmapsto(\operatorname{pr}_{L_0})_*([C]\smile\operatorname{pr}_{S^3}^*t)
$$

vanishes. The target is ordinary cohomology. The hypotheses refer to the effective finite extension specifying the signed generic cycle, so a parameter marking used to choose a further generic cycle has to satisfy the same branch test. Markings used only to represent a cycle already defined downstairs may be removed by normalized trace.

### Independent spreads and functorial relative resolution

Spread the three physical data independently over smooth integral charts $M_i$ over $k_0$. For each datum use its minimal closed $k_0$-stratum in a finite-type chart and shrink there. Such charts are countable using projective embeddings and Hilbert schemes of the curves, surfaces and maps. A very general polarized K3 excludes the images of all nondominant charts. Each $M_i$ thus dominates its polarized moduli chart. After finite extension and shrinking, the critical points and deleted physical fibres can be marked by sections with constant indices. The chosen complex parameter $s_i$ is generic over $k_0$ on its chart. In particular a marked critical value that is algebraic at $s_i$ is constant there. In the product $M=\prod M_i$ the three connected transcendental monodromy groups act independently. Dominant pullback preserves their connected groups, as can be seen on generically finite multisections over a smaller open. By the polarized K3 monodromy and the orthogonal description of the generic Hodge group [9, 10], the tensor of three independent transcendental structures is simple at a very general point of $M$. Its $(6,0)$ line is nonzero, while $H^4$ of a smooth projective threefold twisted by $(-1)$ has level at most two. On a dense open with a smooth proper resolved parameter and a flat cycle-class map the ordinary operator is therefore zero, by flatness.

Form the relative raw finite scheme $\mathcal R$. It is finite locally free over $Z_f\times M$, by relative finite flatness of the maps of curves, and is reduced since it is generically étale over each component of $Z_f\times M$. The same argument gives reduced raw fibres. Normalize and take one projective resolution $\mathcal P\to\mathcal R$ functorial for smooth pullbacks [11]. In a critical slot with section value $v_i$ and local index $n_i$, there is an étale-local relative coordinate $u_i$ with $x_i=v_i+u_i^{n_i}$. This follows by taking the étale root of the unit multiplying the $n_i$-th power of a local equation for the section. In an unramified slot $x_i$ is an étale coordinate. We claim that off finitely many points $\Sigma$ of the central raw fibre, this modification is smooth over $M$ and gives a fibrewise resolution.

For at most one critical slot replace the unramified slot maps by identities on $X$. Locally the raw space maps étale to the resulting model times the other parameter charts. Its functorial resolution is therefore a pullback of the resolution of a model over at most one $M_i$. Generic smoothness and properness give a smooth projective family of resolutions on a dense open of that chart. Birationality on the fibres follows by shrinking so the exceptional locus has smaller fibre dimension. The point $s_i$, generic over $k_0$, belongs to this open.

For two critical slots fix their central pair of values and let the third slot run along its coordinate line. For any branch component dominating all the pairs, the set of pairs whose full lines are contained in that divisor is closed and finite. A curve of such pairs would pull back to a divisor contained in the given prime, making it a cylinder. The exceptional pairs are $k_0$-points. Put in $\Sigma$ all lifts of the finite intersections of this line with branch components that do not contain the whole line. If a dominating branch component contains the line, the two values are algebraic and persist independently on their charts. In the two power coordinates and the free étale coordinate the raw family is then étale-locally the pullback of the fixed two-power model times $M$. Its resolution is the pullback of the fixed resolution. This argument includes singular points along a line contained in several branch components. When all three slots are critical, put their finitely many lifts in $\Sigma$.

The remaining case needs only coordinate planes. By purity the normal finite cover is étale off its divisorial branch. The reduced inverse images of branch planes near the point are relative normal crossings. A critical value on one of these planes is algebraic and persists, while in an unramified slot the inverse image is a smooth relative divisor. Over the strict henselization take roots of the crossing parameters of a common order divisible by the ramification indices. Abhyankar's lemma kills the tame ramification and purity makes the resulting normal cover étale on the regular chart [12]. Locally it splits, and each original normalized sheet is the quotient by the stabilizer of a root sheet. This group acts diagonally on the root parameters, giving a fixed toric quotient times smooth relative coordinates after étale localization. Smooth functoriality gives relative smoothness and a fibrewise resolution. This reasoning applies to the normalization of the raw scheme without requiring that scheme to be normal.

Properness of the modification permits deleting from the raw total space the image of its nonsmooth locus over $M$, and the finite central set $\Sigma$. Over the resulting open its preimage $\mathcal V$ is smooth over $M$, and the central fibre $V_0$ is a resolution off $\Sigma$. Complete $V_0$ componentwise by closing it in the projective central fibre and resolving away from $V_0$. We may compute on this $L_0$. A common projective graph resolution compares any two smooth projective models, using the proper push of their generic cycle closures and degree-one push-pull. Vertical differences in the comparison have the vanishing proved next.

### Vertical codimension-two cycles

On any smooth parameter open $V$ in a fibre consider the incidence over the smooth elliptic locus $V^\circ$. The product of the three odd projectors $(1-[-1]_i^*)/2$ on the relative elliptic product selects just fibre degree three. Thus the all-odd summand of degree four is canonically

$$
H^1(V^\circ,V_1\otimes V_2\otimes V_3),
$$

with the $V_i$ pulled back from the elliptic bases. Restriction of this group to a dense open is injective. Local cohomology with lisse coefficients over a smooth positive-codimension stratum begins in degree two by purity, and stratification gives the same vanishing in degree one for any proper closed complement. Consequently an interior vertical codimension-two cycle has zero all-odd class. This includes a vertical component over which all three physical coordinates vary.

Each $\xi_i=h_i^*t_i$ restricts to zero in degree two on every deleted physical fibre, since the restrictions are detected by component degrees against algebraic curve classes. Thus it has a compactly supported lift on $Y_i^\circ$, and we use the odd average of the lift by the inversion argument of §5. Pair an integrated class on $V$ with an element of $H_c^2(V)$. The three compactly supported lifts compute the pairing on the smooth elliptic incidence, which is closed in $V\times\prod Y_i^\circ$. This kills cycles supported over deleted physical fibres and uses only the all-odd projection on the interior. It proves equality in ordinary $H^4(V)$ for graph closures agreeing generically, including any vertical corrections under specialization. A change of Abel origin has the same property: subtract the three fibrewise coordinate axes with coefficient three, using $f_{i*}f_j^*=3\delta_{ij}$ to compute the fibre class. The difference lies in Leray filtration at least one. Moving translations act as identity on its first graded piece, and each translated axis contains a whole elliptic factor and has zero all-odd projection. All-odd degree four has just this first graded piece. This also justifies using a degree-normalized Abel marking on the signed base.

Take a smooth arc through the central point meeting the independent open on the punctured arc, and restrict $\mathcal V$ to it. Close the generic relative cycle over this smooth total space. Refined restriction to the central fibre agrees generically with the prescribed cycle: every raw central component still dominates $Z_f$, so the comparison is on the dense flat universal locus. Chow localization gives only vertical differences, handled above. On a small analytic disc the physical K3 classes extend as flat classes on the smooth proper families. Integration of the total graph class against them gives an ordinary degree-four class on the total open parameter over the disc. Its nearby restrictions vanish. Every singular cycle on $V_0$ has compact image and transports to nearby fibres by local horizontal lifts on a neighbourhood of its image in the smooth family. The total class has the same integral there, proving that its restriction to $V_0$ vanishes.

### Localization of ordinary cohomology

Let $F=(L_0\times_{R_0}\Sigma)_{\rm red}$. Ordinary localization and duality for the smooth threefold now give

\operatorname{im}U_C\ \subset\

\qquad H^4_F(L_0)=H_2(F)(-3).

Only the dual of $\operatorname{Gr}^W_2 H^2(F)$ contributes, with the indicated twists, because the target is pure. ### Three parameters and the Hodge locus

At each of these points the central raw model has a pointed étale comparison with the finite power pullback of $Z_f$ by $x_i=v_i+u_i^{n_i}$, using the appropriate affine charts on $X$ and $n_i=1$ in unramified slots. Indeed the power coordinates arise by étale unit roots on the actual curves, so they give the common pointed étale neighbourhood directly. The entire finite pullback and its points above $u=0$ are defined over $\overline{k_0(v_1,v_2,v_3)}$, of transcendence degree at most three. Choose a projective resolution of its full reduced model over this field. Étale comparison and normalization distinguish corresponding branches, including when the unnormalized branches meet. On the common neighbourhood eliminate the rational map from the fixed smooth model to the actual one by blowups in smooth centres. The resulting proper degree-one map between smooth total spaces splits by trace in the derived direct images over the raw base. Proper base change gives an injection on cohomology of the proper point fibres. These maps respect mixed Hodge structures. For a blowup in a smooth centre $D_1$, the restricted modification square over the point has exceptional part a projective bundle over $D_1\cap F$. Its pullback is an isomorphism in $H^1$; the modification exact sequence then gives injective pullback on $H^2$, with cokernel a subquotient of $H^0(D_1\cap F)(-1)$. Thus such blowups add only Tate factors in weight two. The non-Tate simple factors needed from the actual point fibre occur on the fixed point fibre.

This yields a countable list of smooth projective families of surfaces or lower-dimensional varieties on bases of dimension at most three. For each index tuple, each $k_0$-locus of $v$ and each algebraic extension, spread the chosen resolutions of the fibre components on a generically finite chart over that locus and shrink for smoothness and properness. The coefficients of all the constructions lie in finite algebraic extensions of $k_0(v)$. There are countably many such data, and the point being spread is generic over $k_0$ on its locus.

For a very general primitively polarized $S$, $T=T(S)$ has rank 21 and Hodge group $\operatorname{SO}(T)$. The harmonic Schur pieces of $T^{\otimes3}$ are the trace-free symmetric cube, two copies of the trace-free $(2,1)$ representation, and $\bigwedge^3 T$, with three remaining metric insertions $T(-2)$. These are the irreducible pieces for the special orthogonal group in this rank, obtained by the permutation decomposition and contraction with the form. Their dimensions are $1750,3059,1330,21$, respectively. The symmetric cube has $(6,0)$ and the mixed symmetry piece has $(5,1)$, as seen from a Young symmetrizer on $\omega\otimes\omega\otimes v$ with $v\in T^{1,1}$ nonzero. They are ruled out by the level of the target. By semisimplicity and polarization, a nonzero map from either remaining type through the support group in the localization formula forces a Hodge embedding of $(\bigwedge^3T)(2)$ or $T$ in weight two in one of the listed families. Explicitly a weight-six source $A$ in those two types gives $A^\vee(-4)\simeq A(2)$ in weight two by duality and polarization.

Fix a smooth level chart $P_{\rm K3}$ of dimension 19 and a target family over $D_0$ from the list. In the weight-zero polarized Hom variation on $P_{\rm K3}\times D_0$, each rational embedding is a Hodge tensor on a rational flat sheet. The Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan theorem gives a countable collection of algebraic Hodge loci carrying these locally flat tensors [14]. On such a sheet with injective tensor, fix the target parameter. The filtration on the source is the inverse image of the fixed target filtration under this flat tensor. Local Torelli makes the period derivative injective for $T$. For $\bigwedge^3T$, a nonzero tangent sends the period vector $\omega$ to $v\ne0$ in $T^{1,1}$. The derivative of the highest Hodge subspace contains a term $v\wedge v_1\wedge v_2\ne0$ in $\bigwedge^3T^{1,1}$ for suitable $v_1,v_2\in T^{1,1}$. This is the component of the derivative of $\omega\wedge v_1\wedge v_2$ outside the original highest Hodge subspace. Thus the period derivative for the exterior power is also injective. The embedding locus has discrete fibres over $D_0$ on its flat sheets. Each algebraic component with an injective tensor projects to a locus of dimension at most three in $P_{\rm K3}$. Excluding the closures of these images for all the families proves vanishing on a very general $S$.

### Descent from connected proper parameters and the signed application

Suppose a cycle is initially given over a proper dominant parameter $P'\to Z_f$ with geometrically connected generic fibre, with the three modular coordinates still independent. On the common smooth open its all-odd class is in $H^1(P',\mathcal V_{\rm odd})$, where $\mathcal V_{\rm odd}=(V_1\otimes V_2\otimes V_3)(2)$ is pulled back from $Z_f$. On a very general smooth proper fibre of the parameter map the restriction is a Hodge morphism from the weight-one dual of $\mathcal V_{\rm odd}$ to the fibre's $H^1$. The three elliptics have independent $\operatorname{SL}_2$ groups, so the source is simple of level three, whereas $H^1$ has level at most one. The restriction is zero. The degree-one Leray sequence and connectedness give a unique descended class on the comparison open. A multisection of generic degree $r$ gives an algebraic representative by restricting the cycle and pushing with coefficient $1/r$, because trace times pullback on the lisse class is $r$. Degree-one purity and the compactly supported pairing above extend the equality of evaluated maps to ordinary cohomology on smooth compact models. For a disconnected generic fibre use its finite Stein factor first. This descends the evaluated class to the actual finite branch of the construction.

For the signed Torelli cover the centred maps are intrinsic to the nodal family over the generic signed base, and the general interior branch specialization was proved in §3. The branch described in §4 consists of the coordinate planes and the single divisor dominating all pairs. The finite-branch theorem applies to the cubic

$$
(t_1,t_2,t_3)\longmapsto
p_*\bigl(e_1^*h_1^*t_1\smile J_s(t_2\otimes t_3)\bigr),
$$

where $e_1:Q\to Y_1$ is the first evaluation. This formula follows by the projection formula on the two signed graph closures. Taking their unweighted trace to the unsigned base would already cancel them, whereas the vanishing proved here is on the signed parameter itself.

## 7. Iterating the positive transfer

The binary correspondence can be iterated with a fresh independent auxiliary elliptic at each internal vertex. Here the two inputs at a vertex are elliptic families over the bases retained by the child vertices. We give the relative projector needed for iteration, since the perverse pairing in §5 alone would leave supported terms after a vertex.

Let $p_X:X_B\to B'$ be an elliptic family pulled back from the compact level-three modular surface to a smooth projective $m$-fold $B'$. Resolve the cusp divisor on the base to simple normal crossings. The modular fibres are split $I_w$, with $w=3$. At a node the equation is étale locally $xy=\prod z_i^{a_i}$, after absorbing a unit. Blowing up the component with ideal $(x,z_i)$ has a smooth chart $z_i=xu$ and the chart $x=z_iv$, where $vy=z_i^{a_i-1}\prod_{j\ne i}z_j^{a_j}$. If a power of $z_i$ remains, the surviving strict transform in this chart has ideal $(v,z_i)$. Process the global component labels of the split fibres over each base divisor, repeating up to its multiplicity. At a node the labels on the two sides are distinct, and the same charts apply with $x,y$ exchanged. In charts already smooth the later strict transform centres are Cartier. Induction on the remaining powers yields a smooth projective model with one-dimensional fibres. The section avoids the modification centres and over the generic point of a base divisor of multiplicity $a$ the fibre is $I_{wa}$. These charts also apply above intersections of base divisors. Inversion can be realized by its graph on the model.

Put $K_X=Rp_{X*}\mathbb Q_{X_B}[m+1]$ and $IC_{B'}(V)=j_{!*}V[m]$ for the elliptic degree-one local system $V$. For a strict support of codimension $c$ in perverse degree $k$, the generic stalk and the stalk of its dual would occur in fibre degrees $1+c+k$ and $1+c-k$. Proper base change and equidimensionality thus give $c+|k|\le1$. In perverse degrees $\pm1$ there are only the full-support base terms. In degree zero there are the elliptic intermediate extension and possible divisor supports, by [8].

Write $u=p_X^*$ and $v=s_*$ for the section $s$, with cup adjoints $p_{X*}$ and $s^*$, and set $v'=v-u(v^\dagger v)/2$. In the relative derived category the sources of $u,v$ are the constant complexes on $B'$ shifted by $[m+1]$, respectively $[m-1](-1)$. The class $v^\dagger v$ is the normal class of the section. The section and fibre identities give $u^\dagger u=v'^\dagger v'=0$ and $u^\dagger v'=v'^\dagger u=1$. For a boundary component $D_1\subset B'$ omit the component hit by the section. For the remaining generic components extend the cycles by closure and resolution and let $C_{D_1}$ be the sum of their maps $b_*a^*$, where $a$ goes to $D_1$ and $b$ to $X_B$. Their source consists of copies of the pushed $\mathbb Q_{D_1}[m-1](-1)$. These are simple perverse sheaves, as the base divisor is smooth. The composites $C_{D_1}^\dagger C_{D_2}$ vanish for distinct supports. For one support the composite is the negative Cartan matrix $M_{D_1}$ of type $A_{wa-1}$, computed on a transverse surface and determined by that generic calculation by strict support. The section misses these components and the fibre trace of the component maps is zero, also seen from the negative shift of its perverse target. Hence the ordinary correspondence

$$
P_X=1-uv'^\dagger-v'u^\dagger-\sum_{D_1}C_{D_1}M_{D_1}^{-1}C_{D_1}^\dagger
$$

is a self-adjoint projector. Its middle relative image is precisely $IC_{B'}(V)$. Indeed the first terms remove the two outer degrees, and in degree zero the component matrices split off the $wa-1$ generators besides the full-support fibre trace at each generic boundary divisor. The stalk bound excludes further supports. The construction uses section, component and diagonal correspondences as in relative Chow–Künneth constructions [15]. Inversion surrounded by $P_X$ acts as $-1$ by full faithfulness of intermediate extension, so a regular inversion on the compact model is unnecessary.

At a leaf we may work on the relatively minimal elliptic surface and use the graph of the evaluation. On a blowup resolving the evaluation the pullbacks of $T_i$ pair to zero with every exceptional curve and descend to this surface. They also pair to zero with the section, fibre and fibre components, and are therefore in the image of the elliptic projector. Their Gram is $d_i$ by proper trace. At an internal vertex take the product $B_a\times B_b$ of the child bases. Adjoin an independent modular parameter and form the signed ordered gluing. Resolve this new parameter base and its cusp divisor as above. The signed curve gives a proper graph from the product of the child elliptic families to the new one, over $B_a\times B_b$. Compose with the child projectors on the input and $P_X$ on the output. An Abel marking traced to the original output is divided by its degree once. This is legitimate on the compact model by proper push-pull and the slice translation comparison. The output projector on a general compact slice removes only section, base and fibre-component classes, so the transcendental slice image is unchanged.

The input relative object over $B_a\times B_b$ is exactly $IC_{B_a}(V_a)\boxtimes IC_{B_b}(V_b)$, in degree zero. Proper unit and trace of the graph and Verdier duality give the map and its ordinary cup adjoint. Their composite on this input is an endomorphism of a full-support intermediate extension. By the compact signed slice calculation it is $72r_v\,1$ on the lisse open, where $r_v$ is the sum of actual retained parameter degrees over the standard signed slice at vertex $v$. Full faithfulness makes the same true as an endomorphism of the entire input object. Projecting at each vertex is what permits this argument after repeating the construction. For a tree with $n$ leaves the final elliptic space $Q_n$ has dimension $2n$, since the dimension of the parameter increases by one over the product of the child bases. Composition gives ordinary algebraic correspondences with

$$
J_n:\bigotimes_{i=1}^n T_i\hookrightarrow H^{2n}(Q_n,\mathbb Q),
\qquad
J_n^\dagger J_n=72^{n-1}\prod_i d_i\prod_v r_v\,1.
$$

The image lies in the algebraically projected $IH^{2n-1}(B_{\rm root},V_{\rm root})$ inside ordinary cohomology. For the standard signed slice and rational inversion graph one can take $r_v=1$. Finite parameter covers retained at the output contribute their degrees. The retraction is the adjoint divided by the displayed scalar.

## 8. Determinant scope and reproducibility

For a very general polarized K3 the algebraic diagonal gives the metric on $T$, and Varesco's Theorem 2.16 identifies the determinant as the remaining generator of Hodge tensors when the endomorphism field is $\mathbb Q$ [16]. The prior rStation results [17, 18] treat mixed products, including pairwise and central classes and Hodge similarities. Paper 145 constructs shared elliptic supports and proves support and essential-field bounds [19]. That construction supplies physical elliptic sweeps for the signed modular correspondence. The tree with 21 copies of one $T$ embeds the projected volume line in ordinary $H^{42}(Q_{21})$. Algebraicity of the resulting Hodge class and the projected rank-21 determinant cycle remain open.

Related work on Severi varieties studies nodal curves and their moduli on polarized abelian surfaces [20]. The discriminant used here belongs to a prescribed split-isogenous surface and is identified with the signed modular slice via its node and its complementary elliptic quotient. Yoshihara studies the existence of genus-three curves on products of elliptic curves [21]. The incidence in §3 uses a degree-three isogeny to that product and a genus-four arithmetic class on its source, which makes the translate marking and the two distinct evaluation degrees accessible.

The supplement gives exact CPU checks of the norm and its local terms, the level and cusp computations, the Hesse net and degree calculations, the compact trace factors, the orthogonal tensor decomposition and the relative elliptic projectors. Its README gives the standalone invocation. The cohomological steps use the proofs above.
