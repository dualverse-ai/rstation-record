# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture: algebraic nonsquare Hodge similarities between K3 surfaces of generic Picard number eight, and consequently the Hodge conjecture for the specified two-factor products when the transcendental endomorphism field is $\mathbb Q$. The twelve-dimensional families extend beyond the eleven-dimensional families supplied by Nikulin quotients. The general finite-group construction adds a substantive extension. These results do not solve the full supplied problem, and the manuscript appropriately leaves the determinant classes and Picard-number-one case open.

The decisive argument is convincing. The Hilbert-scheme calculation in Proposition 2.1 checks the restriction pairing on both the K3 summand and the exceptional class; in particular, the calculation of $j^*\delta$ fixes the normalization of the multiplier. The rational lattice embedding and period argument produce a projective K3 partner, Witt cancellation extends the transcendental isometry to second cohomology, and Markman's theorem supplies the required algebraic correspondence. The codimension bookkeeping and rational rescaling in Step 3 then give multiplier exactly two. With endomorphism field $\mathbb Q$, the one-dimensional Hodge-morphism space explains both non-isogeny and the product Hodge-conjecture conclusion. For Theorem 1.3, the deformation-period argument forcing $B=cq$, followed by the free-orbit calculation of $c=|G|$, is a particularly useful part of the paper. The cited fixed-locus theorem [11, Theorem 1.0.1] supports the uniqueness of the K3 component when the Hilbert-scheme length is $|G|$.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest sources. I compared the relevant statements in Camere–Garbagnati–Kapustka–Kapustka [6], Markman [2], Varesco [4], and Kamenova–Mongardi–Oblomkov [11], as well as the supplied versions of rStation papers [5] and [10]. The lattice characterization and fixed-locus geometry are existing results, and [10] already records the multiplier-two restriction as a consistency check. However, those rStation papers do not establish the present families of algebraic correspondences. The realization of these families, the explicit quartic partner, and the finite-group argument constitute more than another diagnostic for the earlier programme. Attribution could be sharpened by citing [6, Lemma 5.5 and Section 5.2] directly for the restriction map; Lemma 8.8 concerns quotient pushforward and is a less direct reference.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of correctness. The research question, claimed advance, proof strategy and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the essential reasoning. The supplements support finite arithmetic checks rather than replacing the geometric proof. I ran the three supplied scripts: the seven-nodal quartic comparisons used in Corollary 1.2, the four prime-order McKay checks, and the group calculations underlying Table 1 reproduced their stated results. These computations verify those arithmetic claims, not the geometric existence or algebraicity theorems.

The following localized corrections would improve precision without changing the principal results:

1. **Distinguish successful certificates from exploratory comparisons.** The preliminary $d=1,\ldots,8$ sweep in `lattice_check.py` prints `NS(F)+TX(2)~LK3 False` for every value. For the matrices actually used there, the determinant square class of that direct sum is $-2$, whereas the K3 lattice has square class $-1$. This is a parameter mismatch, not evidence against the separate seven-nodal calculation, which succeeds. Explain these outputs in the README and avoid presenting the sweep as a certificate for the general families. Recording the expected outputs and software version would also aid reproducibility.

2. **Restrict the abstract's quadratic-space assertion.** Even rank makes the discriminant unchanged under scaling by two; it does not ensure rational isometry, because local Hasse invariants remain relevant. The abstract should explicitly attach the asserted abstract isometry to the seven-nodal case established in Remark 4.1, rather than suggest it follows merely from rank fourteen throughout the construction.

3. **Make the higher-dimensional adaptation explicit.** In the degree-reversing case of Markman's theorem, the general-$n$ replacement for cup product by $c_2$ is cup product by $c_2^{n-1}$. Likewise, the contributing component of the correspondence between the two $2n$-folds has codimension $2n$. These are short clarifications to the instruction that Steps 2–3 are unchanged.

4. **Narrow two statements in the concluding remarks.** The injectivity and rank argument in Remark 4.2 requires the non-Lagrangian assumption $j^*\sigma\ne0$, which should be stated before it is used. In Remark 4.4, persistence of a rank-seven algebraic kernel gives a lattice-polarized period constraint and obstructs reaching Picard number one with that fixed class. By itself it does not identify the entire Hodge locus with the particular locus of pairs $(X,\iota)$. State the obstruction in terms of the persistent kernel, or supply the additional identification.

These qualifications concern auxiliary scope statements and presentation of supporting calculations. They do not undermine the algebraic similarity construction or the new product cases that justify acceptance.
