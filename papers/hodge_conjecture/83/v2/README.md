# Generalized Nikulin surfaces and algebraic Hodge similarities of multiplier two

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-11-01T04:05:42.928874Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/83/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A generalized Nikulin surface is the K3 surface $F$ in the fixed locus of a symplectic involution on a manifold $X$ of $K3^{[2]}$-type; by Camere, Garbagnati, Kapustka and Kapustka, these are the K3 surfaces whose Néron–Severi lattice contains $E_7(-2)$ primitively, and the projective ones form $12$-dimensional families. We show that every projective generalized Nikulin surface $F$ admits an algebraic Hodge similarity of multiplier $2$ from a projective K3 surface $S_1$ of the same Picard number. The restriction from $H^2(X)$ to $H^2(F)$ kills the anti-invariant part and doubles the Beauville–Bogomolov form on the invariant part. The K3 surface $S_1$ is chosen with $T(S_1)$ rationally Hodge isometric to $T(X)$, and Markman's theorem makes this isometry algebraic on $S_1^{[2]} \times X$. When the endomorphism field of $T(F)$ is $\mathbb Q$, which holds for very general $F$, the surfaces $S_1$ and $F$ are not isogenous and the Hodge conjecture holds for $S_1 \times F$. For seven-nodal quartics the partner is a quartic surface with one singular point of type $E_7$. Nonsquare algebraic similarities from Nikulin involutions need Picard number at least $9$ and give families of dimension $11$; here the Picard number is $8$ and the families have dimension $12$. In the seven-nodal case the two transcendental lattices, of rank $14$, are even isometric as abstract quadratic spaces, so there only the Hodge structure forces the multiplier. For a finite group $G$ of symplectic automorphisms, the same argument with Hilbert schemes of $|G|$ points gives multiplier $|G|$ on families one dimension larger than quotients by $G$, for instance multiplier $3$ in dimension $8$ and $6$ in dimension $6$.
