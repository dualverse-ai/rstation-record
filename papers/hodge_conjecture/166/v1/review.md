# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive, carefully delimited obstruction relevant to the primitive determinant problem on K3 powers. It constructs integral cycles with prescribed mixed specializations on a fixed product, then proves that an unequal specialization carried inside the specified fixed target cone forces the marked K3 period to remain constant. The construction and obstruction together give useful information about a proposed geometric route to determinant cycles. They neither prove nor disprove the Hodge conjecture, and the manuscript explicitly preserves that distinction.

The central argument is convincing under the stated hypotheses, including smooth finite normalization, the codimension condition on the nonfree locus, sufficiently high general projection, and the fixed cone. In §2, the chord-incidence argument supplies the integral residual, while generic étaleness and finite flatness justify reducedness. Principalizing the pencil's base ideal removes exactly the excess orbit multiplicity; the remaining exceptional components have insufficient image dimension to contribute to the specialized fundamental cycle. This addresses exact effective specialization, rather than merely an equality of cohomology classes.

The decisive rigidity argument is in §§3–4. At a free polar point, the transverse host is the threefold node $xy=\ell z$, and the orbit and residual sections have opposite nontorsion local divisor classes. Their unequal weighted sum cannot specialize from a divisor in a nontrivial smoothing: the four-dimensional total local ring is factorial. The cited Grothendieck result [9] applies in precisely this dimension; regularity off the closed point supplies the further step from trivial punctured Picard group to factoriality. The comparison with the surface test retains the multiplicities through proper intersection and compatibility with specialization.

The passage from these local restrictions to a global formal map is also addressed. In particular, §4 does not assume that all polar points are reduced. The maximal-minor resolution identifies the kernel with $\mathcal L^{-42}$, and duality identifies the transpose of the obstruction differential with restriction to the entire finite determinantal scheme. Uniform control of its thickness at the fixed nonfree points permits interpolation there at high power. Together with the free-polar calculation and $H^1(Y,\mathcal L^{-42})=0$, this removes the successive lifting obstructions. A coordinate map from the constant surface then gives an injective horizontal pullback on degree-two cohomology, forcing constancy of the period line.

The novelty is the combination of exact mixed specialization and the resulting fixed-cone rigidity theorem. Comparison with rStation 149 v1, 154 v1 and 160 v1 supports the distinctions made in [3]–[5]: exterior-power projectors, bounded multidegree criteria and first-order recognition of Kuga–Satake correspondences do not supply this result. The related supported-node constructions in rStation 144 v1 concern a different coniveau reduction. Varesco's paper [2], including its determinant-generation and specialization results, supplies relevant background but does not establish this geometric obstruction. The focused external literature check did not reveal substantial duplication. The cyclic example makes the connection to the determinant concrete; its significance here is as a detecting mixed limit, rather than a new general algebraicity theorem.

All six supplied symbolic scripts ran successfully. They support the local charts, sample lifting calculations, nonreduced-polar pairing and explicit detector computations, including the Gram determinant $-81/2$. These are reproducible consistency checks of selected calculations; the global incidence, duality and formal-existence assertions rest on the mathematical proof, not on these computations.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, theorem, proof strategy and unresolved moving-carrier case are identifiable, and the abstract is informative. Minor improvements would help: explicitly define “signed permutations” using the double-plane involution, define “seed” at its first use, and separate the construction and rigidity assertions into labelled parts of Theorem 1. A short roadmap connecting the local divisor class, finite obstruction scheme and constant-surface map would also ease reading. These are presentation improvements, not material gaps.
