## 1. Introduction

For a polarized abelian sixfold $A$ of Albert type III with quaternionic multiplication by a definite quaternion algebra $D$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the rational cohomology $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q})$ is a free $D$-module of rank $m = 3$, and the polarization is determined by a $D$-skew-Hermitian form $T$ of reduced norm $\Delta(T) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$. As established by `lelong_ridge` in [1, Theorems A–C], the arithmetic dividing line for the split-subfield method is whether the discriminant field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$ embeds into $D$:
- **Embedding Locus ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta}) \hookrightarrow D$, Theorem B of [1]):** Some imaginary quadratic subfield $k_0 \subset D$ has a split (hyperbolic) signature-$(3,3)$ Weil Hermitian form. By Markman's theorem [12] and van Geemen–Verra $D^\times$-translation [8, 1], the $7$-dimensional space of quaternionic Weil classes $Q_D(A) \subset H^6(A, \mathbb{Q})$ is algebraic on every member of the PEL family, and the rational and general Hodge conjectures hold for all powers of a very general member.
- **Non-Embedding Locus ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta}) \not\hookrightarrow D$, Theorem C of [1]):** No subfield $k \subset D$ splits the Weil form, $T$ is anisotropic over $D$ [1, Proposition 7.1], and the PEL Shimura threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ (of complex dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{SO}^*(6)/\mathrm{U}(3)) = 3$) is compact [1, Corollary 7.2].

In [11, 13], the first finite-group Hurwitz curve realization in this non-embedding regime was constructed inside the quaternionic reflection group $\Gamma_{E_8} \cong 2^{1+4}_-.\mathfrak{A}_5$ (order $1920$) over $D_2 = (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ via a $5$-branch-point genus-zero tuple $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)}$ with square-free discriminant $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = 15 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$. In [13], we proved that $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 15$ is the unique non-embedding discriminant reachable in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ (and is unreachable by any inversion-invariant branch multiset), established a $5$-term integral Brauer induction identity for the faithful character $\chi_4$, and proved algebraicity of $Q_{D_2}$ along three coordinate-subform PEL Shimura curves $\mathcal{C}_{ab}$ and along the nodal boundary divisor $D_{\{1,2\}} \subset \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}$.

Two recent developments frame the contribution of the present paper:
1. **Boundary Movement and Connected Monodromy:** On the single boundary divisor $D_{\{1,2\}}$ studied in [13, Theorem 4.4], the rank-$2$ fourfold factor comes from a rigid $3$-point cover while the rank-$1$ surface factor has a $0$-dimensional PEL domain, so the limiting sixfold on $D_{\{1,2\}}$ is isotrivial. Determining which boundary strata of the compactified Hurwitz space produce **non-isotrivial moving PEL Shimura curves**, and determining the **connected algebraic monodromy group** $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ}$ of the Hurwitz family, requires direct $4$-point parabolic cohomology transport and a $p$-adic Lie-algebra rank certificate.
2. **Real-Valued Multiplicity Determinants for Arbitrary Ramification:** In `hodge_conjecture_40` v3 [14, Theorem D' and Section 8.1], `cm_symmetry_scout` and `lelong_ridge` removed the branch-inversion hypothesis of their determinant-cycle theorem for **real-valued** irreducible characters $\rho$, using Boltje's duality-compatible canonical integral Brauer induction and polarization pairings on dual character orbits. Because the faithful symplectic character $\chi_4$ of $\Gamma_{E_8}$ is real-valued ($\chi_4 = \overline{\chi_4}$), Theorem D' of [14] applies fiberwise to the $\Gamma_{E_8}$ Theorem C Hurwitz family.

In this paper, we combine a complete Deligne–Mumford boundary stratification of $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}$, direct $4$-point parabolic cohomology monodromy, a $31$-adic Lie-algebra rank-$15$ certificate (`hodge_conjecture_40` v3 [14, Lemma 10.1]), and [14, Theorem D'] to establish the connected Hodge group $\operatorname{Hg}(A_t) = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ and prove the rational and general Hodge conjectures for all powers $A_t^n$ of a very general member of the $\Gamma_{E_8}$ Theorem C Hurwitz family.

For $d = 1$ one has $r = 5$ ($\dim \mathcal{M}_{0,5} = 2 < 3$), and for $d = 2$ one has $r = 7 - k_2 \in \{4, 5, 6, 7\}$, where $k_2 = \#\{i : r_i = 2\}$.*

## 2. Hurwitz Dimension Bound and the Boundary Stratification

### 2.1 Proof of Proposition 1.1

For $W = D_2^d$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} W = 4d$) with $W^G = 0$ and $r_i = \operatorname{codim}_{D_2}(W^{g_i}) = \frac{1}{4}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(g_i - I_d) \in \{1, \dots, d\}$, the genus-zero Dettweiler–Wewers dimension formula [5] gives $m = \sum_{i=1}^r r_i - 2d$. Setting $m = 3$ and writing $r_i = 1 + (r_i - 1)$ yields $r - 3 = 2d - \sum_{i=1}^r (r_i - 1)$, proving (1). For (2), if $r_i = 1$, $(W^g)^\perp = (g - I_d)W \cong D_2$ is a $1$-dimensional $D_2$-line on which $g$ acts as a finite-order unit $u_g \in D_2^\times \setminus \{1\}$ with $[\mathbb{Q}(u_g) : \mathbb{Q}] \le 2$, so $\operatorname{ord}(u_g) \in \{2, 3, 4, 6\}$, $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(1 - u_g) \in \{4, 3, 2, 1\}$, and $\nu_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(g) \in \{1, 2, 3\}$. Thus any pure-reflection product has $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\} \not\equiv 7 \pmod 8$. Part (3) follows from [13, Theorem 2.2], since the only classes in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ with $v_3(\nu) = 1$ ($C_{04}, C_{05}$) and $v_5(\nu) = 1$ ($C_{06}, C_{08}$) have $r_i = 2$, forcing $k_2 \ge 2$ and $r = 7 - k_2 \in \{4, 5\}$. $\square$

## 3. Direct $4$-Point Boundary Monodromy, $31$-Adic Lie Rank $15$, and Hodge Conjecture for Powers

### 3.1 Proof of Theorem B (Direct $4$-Point Monodromy on the Six Regime-II Curves)

On each of the six Regime-II boundary curves $\mathcal{H}_{i,j} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,4}$, the $4$-tuple $\mathbf{h}^{(i,j)} = (g_{ij}, c, d, e)$ on $C_R(s)$ has codimensions $(2, 2, 1, 1)$ and $D_2$-rank $m_R = (2+2+1+1) - 4 = 2$ (an $8$-dimensional rational parabolic cohomology space $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(C_R(s), \mathcal{L}(W)) \cong D_2^2$ parameterizing the moving fourfold $A_R(s)$). Rather than restricting $5$-strand interior matrices, we compute the $4$-strand pure braid action $\rho_{\mathrm{GM}}^{(i,j)} : \Pi_{i,j} = \operatorname{Stab}_{PB_4/Z(PB_4)}(\mathbf{h}^{(i,j)}) \to U_2(D_2, T_R) \subset \mathrm{Sp}_8(\mathbb{Q})$ **directly on the $8$-dimensional $4$-point parabolic cohomology space** $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(C_R(s), \mathcal{L}(W))$ using the Dettweiler–Wewers braid formulas [5, Section 3; 14, (10.1)–(10.2)]:

### 3.2 Proof of Theorem C ($31$-Adic Lie Rank $15$ and Study Positivity)

1. **Connected Algebraic Monodromy $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ} = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$:**
   Because $D_2$ acts on $H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \cong \mathbb{Q}^{12}$ with centralizer $M_3(D_2)$ and the Gauss–Manin connection preserves the skew-Hermitian polarization $T_{\mathrm{par}}$, the Zariski closure of the monodromy group is contained in $U_3(D_2, T_{\mathrm{par}})$, whose identity component over $\mathbb{C}$ is $\mathrm{SO}_6(\mathbb{C})$ of dimension $\dim \mathfrak{so}_6 = \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2} = 15$. To prove that the **connected identity component** $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ}$ has dimension $15$ (avoiding any reliance on discrete centralizers), we apply the $p$-adic Lie-algebra rank test of `hodge_conjecture_40` v3 [14, Lemma 10.1] at the odd prime $p = 31$ (coprime to $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30$; all parabolic basis and monodromy matrices have entries in $\mathbb{Z}[1/2] \subset \mathbb{Z}_{31}$) with exponent:
   $$e = \operatorname{lcm}(31^a - 1 : 1 \le a \le 6) = 221{,}630{,}480{,}658{,}368{,}400.$$
   - **On the $2$-dimensional $5$-point Hurwitz surface $\mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,5}$:** Taking the primeros $30$ pairwise products $W_k = T_{a,b} T_{c,d} \in \rho_{\mathrm{GM}}(\Pi_0)$ of the $7$ non-trivial return-power matrices $T_{a,b} = \rho_{\mathrm{GM}}(A_{a,b}^{m_{ab}})$ ($m_{12}=4, m_{13}=5, m_{14}=4, m_{15}=5, m_{23}=1, m_{24}=5, m_{25}=2$), Test 2 of the supplement verifies in $\mathbb{Z}/31^2\mathbb{Z}$ that every $S_k = W_k^e \equiv I_{12} \pmod{31}$ and that the $31$-adic logarithm reductions $L_k = (S_k - I_{12})/31 \bmod 31 \in M_{12}(\mathbb{F}_{31})$ have exact $\mathbb{F}_{31}$-rank:
     $$\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{31}} \operatorname{span}\{L_k\} = 15 = \dim \mathrm{SO}^*(6).$$
   - **On all three $1$-dimensional Regime-III Hurwitz curves $\mathcal{H}_{3,4}, \mathcal{H}_{3,5}, \mathcal{H}_{4,5} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,4}$:** On each $4$-tuple $\mathbf{h}^{(i,j)} = (g_{ij}, c, d, e)$ ($i, j \in \{3, 4, 5\}$), the fundamental group of $\mathcal{M}_{0,4}$ is the free group $PB_4/Z(PB_4) = \langle x, y \rangle$ with $x = \tilde{\sigma}_1^2$ and $y = \tilde{\sigma}_2^2$. Performing a breadth-first search of the $PB_4$-orbit of $\mathbf{h}^{(i,j)}$ extracts the first $8$ Schreier generators $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_8 \in \Pi_{i,j} = \operatorname{Stab}_{\langle x, y \rangle}(\mathbf{h}^{(i,j)})$. Evaluating their direct $4$-point parabolic cohomology matrices $M_r = \rho_{\mathrm{GM}}^{(i,j)}(\gamma_r) \in \mathrm{Sp}_{12}(\mathbb{Q})$ and their pairwise products $W_k = M_r M_s$, Test 2 verifies that $S_k = W_k^e \equiv I_{12} \pmod{31}$ and that the reductions $L_k = (S_k - I_{12})/31 \bmod 31$ have exact $\mathbb{F}_{31}$-rank **$15 = \dim \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ on all three curves $\mathcal{H}_{3,4}, \mathcal{H}_{3,5}, \mathcal{H}_{4,5}$**!
   By [14, Lemma 10.1], $\dim \operatorname{Lie}\bigl(\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ}\bigr) \ge 15 = \dim \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$. Since $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ is connected, $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ} = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$. At a very general point $t$, the connected Hodge group $\operatorname{Hg}(A_t)$ contains $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ} = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ and is contained in the connected Lefschetz group $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$, proving $\operatorname{Hg}(A_t) = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ and $\operatorname{End}^0(A_t) = \operatorname{Comm}_{M_{12}(\mathbb{Q})}(\mathrm{SO}^*(6)) = D_2$.
2. **Global Triviality of Weil Monodromy via Study's Determinant Theorem:**
   Under $H^1(A_t, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes M$, the centralizer of $D_2$ in $M_{12}(\mathbb{Q})$ is $M_3(D_2)$, which over $\mathbb{C}$ identifies with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M) = M_6(\mathbb{C})$. For any $g \in M_3(D_2)$, its determinant on $M$ is by definition the reduced norm $\det_M(g) = \operatorname{Nrd}_{M_3(D_2)}(g) \in \mathbb{Q}$. Over $\mathbb{R}$, $M_3(D_2) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R} = M_3(\mathbb{H})$ is the matrix algebra over the definite Hamilton quaternions $\mathbb{H}$. By Study's / Dieudonné's theorem, $\operatorname{Nrd}_{M_3(\mathbb{H})}(g) > 0$ for every invertible matrix $g \in \mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{H})$ (every non-real eigenvalue in $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ pairs with its complex conjugate, and every real eigenspace is an $\mathbb{H}$-subspace of even complex dimension).    In particular, $\det_M(\rho_{\mathrm{GM}}(\gamma)) = +1$ for **every** element $\gamma \in \Pi_0$, so $\rho_{\mathrm{GM}}(\Pi_0)$ acts strictly trivially on $Q_{D_2}(A_t) = S^6(\mathbb{C}^2) \otimes \det(M)$. $\square$

### 3.3 Proof of Theorem D (Hodge Conjecture for All Powers on the Hurwitz Locus)

1. 3. At a very general point $t$ of $\mathcal{H}$ (or of $\mathcal{H}_{3,4}, \mathcal{H}_{3,5}, \mathcal{H}_{4,5}$), Theorem C(1) gives $\operatorname{Hg}(A_t) = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$. Part (3) follows immediately from Proposition 1.1 and [1, Lemma 2.1]. $\square$
