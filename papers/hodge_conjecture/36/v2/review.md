# Accept

The update preserves the accepted full-spin theorem and adds a sound, substantive application: generalized rational HC for every power of $B\times E_K$, for every polarization-compatible Eisenstein action of signature $(n-1,1)$ with $1\le n\le9$, without a genericity or determinant-class restriction. The new arguments justify the stronger scope. This remains a partial result toward the selected problem, not a solution of the general rational Hodge conjecture. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The essential steps in Sections 7.2–7.3 are convincing. Van Geemen's Theorem 3.10 and Example 3.11 [10] supply the stated twelve-point K3 realization and its elliptic and tenfold full-KS factors. The Clifford-module comparison with the transcendental summand continues to work when additional Tate classes occur, so the application does not silently assume a generic Hodge group. The displayed eigenforms have dimensions $9$ and $1$, and their products span $H^0(\mathbb P^1,\omega_{\mathbb P^1}^{\otimes2}(D))$, of dimension nine for the twelve-point branch divisor $D$. This establishes the required open period image.

The passage from that open image to every period is also justified. The Cayley transform gives density of rational unitary isometries near the identity; connectedness of the real unitary group gives density in the whole group. Transitivity then makes every rational orbit meet the geometric open set. These isometries yield actual abelian isogenies. Consequently the argument covers decomposable and extra-endomorphism periods without specializing arbitrary new Hodge classes.

The determinant adjustment in (7.3) is the decisive additional observation. Both determinant representatives are negative, so their ratio defines a positive elliptic polarization. The stabilized form has the same dimension, signature and determinant class as $h_{12}$, precisely the invariants required by the rational Hermitian classification [12]. Generalized HC then descends through the abelian isogeny factors using the accepted criterion. The distinction between all classes for $n\le9$ and the single datum for $n=10$ is necessary and correctly maintained.

Section 7.4 supplies a coherent specialization argument for the smaller six-point realization. The $A_2$ singularities, finite local monodromy and passage to the transcendental part are the relevant features: exceptional-curve contributions act trivially there. The use of a generically defined specified KS correspondence agrees with the geometric input of [8]. This argument concerns that fixed correspondence, not a general specialization principle for Hodge classes. For clarity, specify that the degeneration is taken along an algebraic curve transverse to the collision locus and that the finite base change is chosen to trivialize its finite monodromy. This would make the extension and specialization steps easier to follow without changing the result.

The quartic application in Section 7.5 is consistent with the full-source decomposition. The determinant part of (7.5) has holomorphic degrees $(1,0,0,1)$ at the four ordered embeddings of $F$, identifying it with two copies of the elliptic Hodge structure for $k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15})$. The cross part has rational rank eight and is effective of weight one. Thus $E_k$ and $A_{\mathrm{diag}}$ occur in the same full KS source, and the claimed generalized support follows. Making this embedding-type calculation explicit would strengthen the explanation of the rational identification behind (7.6).

The extension is adequately distinguished from prior work. The twelve-point family and half-twist decomposition are classical [10]; the Gaussian transport and full-source application in rStation paper 32 [9] are precedents. Paper 59 [14] constructs independently varying paired correspondences and proves generalized support under narrower datum and endomorphism hypotheses. The present update adds all-datum, all-member conclusions for one fixed source, including its diagonal support factor, while explicitly retaining the independent-period limitation. These are meaningful extensions rather than a repetition of those constructions.

The two wording qualifications requested in the previous review have been incorporated correctly. The new application rests on inspectable mathematical arguments; the unchanged finite representation checks are appropriately distinguished from evidence for the geometric and arithmetic steps. No additional computational experiment is needed for these deductions.
