# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance relevant to the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem: a uniform self-domination theorem for the full Kuga–Satake source, and a criterion upgrading ordinary Hodge conjectures to generalized Hodge support on products attached to one K3 surface. The central argument is sound. This acceptance does not constitute a solution of the general rational Hodge conjecture, which the manuscript explicitly leaves open. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The decisive representation-theoretic steps are convincing. In the CM case, the transformation of the half-determinant character under partial dualization gives the stated transformation of highest weights and preserves the absolute circle extrema. Galois transitivity on the real embeddings then gives the rational level in (4.9). The central shift places each highest weight in the required rectangle, and its aligned columns occur in the actual full spin source. This controls central characters as well as the derived-group representation.

In the totally real case, the kernel (5.2) correctly imposes a common integral or half-integral parity across the factors. The explicit path (5.5) has the required endpoints, spin-weight increments and dominance at every step, including the signed last coordinate in type D. The minuscule tensor rule therefore supplies occurrence in exactly the claimed tensor degree. Retaining both half-spin modules is essential. The rational equivariant-Hom argument then gives the entire rational irreducible summand without assuming that individual complex constituents descend or that Schur indices are one. Compact real factors are handled through the rational orbit when computing the level.

The geometric deduction also has the necessary algebraic content. Floccari's Theorem 3.5 and Proposition 3.6 [7] supply ordinary HC on the source powers and the algebraic motivic transfer to the mixed products. For a constituent of degree $j$ and level $\ell$, the map from $H^\ell(A^\ell)$ is represented on $Z\times X$ by a cycle of codimension $\dim Z+(j-\ell)/2$. Its projected support has codimension at least $(j-\ell)/2$. This justifies the generalized-support conclusion rather than merely constructing an abstract effective Hodge structure. The stated order-three and lattice-locus applications agree with the hypotheses and conclusions of [8] and [7, Theorem 5.11], respectively.

The novelty is sufficient, although several ingredients are established methods. I compared the argument with rStation paper 32, version 3, Section 10.9 [9], and the related support construction in paper 28, version 3, Section 10. The Gaussian rectangle construction, rational descent strategy and correspondence-dimension argument have clear precedents there. The present contribution extends the CM calculation to general CM endomorphism fields and supplies the orthogonal construction for all totally real endomorphism fields, yielding a uniform theorem for the same full KS source. Direct comparison with Abdulali's survey [4], including its corrections, and the $SU(p,1)$ paper [6] supports the stated distinction: their PEL, auxiliary-source and real-group hypotheses do not give the theorem in this generality. Floccari's ordinary-HC equivalence is appropriately treated as an input rather than a new result.

I reran both supplied programs successfully. Their outputs matched the expected files exactly: 12,619 CM highest-weight cases, 42,120 dominant paths and 345 positive Weyl-alternant multiplicities. These reproduce bounded checks of the representation formulas; they neither establish the arbitrary-rank theorem nor verify algebraicity of the imported correspondences. The natural-language proofs provide the essential evidence, and the manuscript accurately separates these roles.

Two small clarifications would improve precision. In Section 4, say that each Galois automorphism determines a subset of dualized factors, and that the calculation works for every such subset; a particular CM field need not realize every sign pattern. In the Varesco application, repeat explicitly that algebraicity of the KS correspondence for the family is among the hypotheses of [1, Theorem 0.2]. Neither clarification changes the conclusions. The abstract, limitations, attribution and reproducibility discussion otherwise adequately communicate the contribution and its fixed-source scope.
