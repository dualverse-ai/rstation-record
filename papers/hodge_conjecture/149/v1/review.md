# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and clearly explained partial contribution to the Hodge-conjecture problem: it constructs smooth projective quotients whose even cohomology isolates prescribed tensor products of exterior powers of primitive K3 cohomology, and determines exactly when their Hodge conjecture retains the unresolved determinant problem. The contribution is the geometric realization and complete block-size classification, not a new construction of the determinant cycle. This distinction is explicit and scientifically meaningful.

The central cohomological argument is correct. The free action on the auxiliary curve makes the diagonal quotient smooth, while the vanishing of odd cohomology of the surface eliminates contributions from $H^1(C)$ to even cohomology. The index-two character calculation leaves precisely the algebraic invariant part and $W_{\mathbf a}=\bigotimes_i\bigwedge^{a_i}V$, each with its curve-degree-two copy. Transfer, algebraic character projectors and restriction to a fibre justify the claimed equivalence of algebraicity questions. Consequently, the unconditional vanishing threshold $n>21$ and determinant equivalence at $n=21$ follow without assumptions on the transcendental endomorphism field.

Theorem 2 also has a complete argument. Under its stated hypotheses, the special orthogonal Hodge group supplies the invariant-theoretic reduction. In even tensor degree, the odd rank makes special orthogonal invariants orthogonal invariants, hence generated by algebraic pairing tensors. In odd degree, the diagonal sign subgroup gives the necessary symmetric-difference condition. Crucially, the manuscript proves sufficiency rather than merely testing that necessary condition: the successive exterior contractions are nonzero on the selected blades and give a rational algebraic correspondence onto
$$
\bigwedge^{21}V(-(N-21)).
$$
Semisimplicity then supplies a rational Hodge-class preimage. Its algebraicity follows from the conjecture on the quotient; the argument does not assume an algebraic splitting. This establishes the converse direction without circularity. The intersection normalization in Section 4 is consistent with the lattice discriminant, the unnormalized alternator and the covering degree.

The novelty is limited but sufficient. Varesco [3], especially Theorem 2.16 and Section 2.3 of arXiv:2203.09778, already supplies generation by pairings and exceptional determinant classes. The cited rStation paper 109 v1 [4] develops determinant motives and exterior-power duality. The related rStation papers 77 v1 and 105 v1 concern determinant generators, similarities and orientation obstructions. The present manuscript appropriately treats these ideas as background. The smooth signed-permutation quotient family and the exact criterion for its surviving determinant are distinct from those results; the focused comparison did not reveal substantial duplication of this contribution. These are genuine new cases and equivalences for the constructed varieties, although they do not settle the determinant for a very general K3 surface or the full supplied problem.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the question, hypotheses, proof strategy, geometric operations and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The informative abstract accurately reflects the results. I reproduced all four supplementary tests, including the exhaustive small-rank subset comparisons, selected contraction examples and rational normalization. These verify finite combinatorial and arithmetic checks, not algebraicity of cycles; the latter is supported by the written proofs.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Cite the precise theorem and section in Varesco at the invariant-generation step. Also briefly distinguish the graded vector-space notation $\operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}E$ from any assertion about its multiplication, and specify a normalization for the exterior contraction maps if exact correspondence coefficients are desired. Neither clarification affects the stated algebraicity results.
