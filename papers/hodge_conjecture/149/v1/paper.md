# Exterior powers and the Hodge conjecture for smooth quotients of double-plane powers

## 1. Statements

Let $S\to\mathbf P^2$ be a smooth complex double cover branched along a sextic. Write $h$ for the pullback of the line class, $\iota$ for the deck involution, and $V=h^\perp\subset H^2(S,\mathbf Q)$. Its rank is 21. At Picard rank one it is the transcendental Hodge structure. The algebraicity of $\bigwedge^{21}V\subset H^{42}(S^{21},\mathbf Q)$ is an open case of the rational Hodge conjecture. We construct smooth projective varieties for which this case is the precise threshold for an explicit calculation of algebraic classes.

For a tuple of positive integers $\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ put $N=\sum_i a_i$. Partition the factors of $S^N$ into ordered blocks of those sizes. In block $i$ act by independent deck involutions and by the full permutation group. The resulting group $G$ is the product of the signed permutation groups of the blocks. Define $\chi:G\to\{\pm1\}$ by multiplying all individual sheet signs and all permutation signs. Let $K=\ker\chi$. Choose any smooth connected projective curve $C$ with a free $K$-action, and take the diagonal quotient
$$
Y_{\mathbf a}=(S^N\times C)/K .
$$
It is a smooth projective variety of dimension $2N+1$, isotrivially fibred over $C/K$ with fibre $S^N$. Section 2 gives the curves required in this definition.

**Theorem 1.** For one block of size $n\ge22$, every even rational cohomology class of $Y_{(n)}$ is algebraic for every smooth sextic double plane. For $n=21$, the rational Hodge conjecture for $Y_{(21)}$ is equivalent to the algebraicity of $\bigwedge^{21}V$ on $S^{21}$. The two copies of the line on the quotient occur in codimensions 21 and 22. The base $C/K$ of these smooth fibrations can be one fixed genus-three curve for all $n$.

**Theorem 2.** Assume $\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbf Z h$ and $\operatorname{End}_{\rm Hdg}(V)=\mathbf Q$. For even $N$, the rational Hodge conjecture holds for $Y_{\mathbf a}$. For odd $N$, use $r=21$ and start with $D_0=\{0\}$. For a block $a=a_i\le r$ form
$$
D_i=\bigcup_{c\in D_{i-1}}
\left\{|c-a|,\ |c-a|+2,\ldots,\min(c+a,2r-c-a)\right\}.
$$
If $a>r$, set $D_i=\varnothing$. If $r\notin D_k$, every rational Hodge class on $Y_{\mathbf a}$ is algebraic. If $r\in D_k$, the Hodge conjecture for $Y_{\mathbf a}$ is equivalent to algebraicity of the single determinant on $S^{21}$. In the latter case a composition of diagonal contractions and alternating projectors maps the surviving block tensor onto the determinant up to Tate twist.

In particular, for two blocks under the hypotheses of Theorem 2, the conjecture holds whenever $a_1+a_2\ne21$. At $a_1+a_2=21$ it is equivalent to the determinant problem. With three blocks the determinant can survive even when $N>21$, as the examples in Section 5 show.

Zarhin identifies the Hodge group of $V$ under the hypotheses of Theorem 2 [1]. Orthogonal invariant theory describes its Hodge tensors by the pairing and the determinant [2]. Varesco explains the resulting determinant problem for ordinary K3 powers and proves algebraicity in specified families [3]. The motivic formulation and its relation to the Hodge star are discussed in [4]. These results supply the Hodge-theoretic inputs here. Our geometric calculation is for smooth finite quotients of a power times a curve. It gives all rational Hodge classes on these quotients in the stated cases, including an exact block-size criterion for retaining the open problem. Theorems 1 and 2 imply no new algebraicity assertion for the determinant of a very general K3 itself.

## 2. The free quotient and its even cohomology

For a finite group generated by $d$ elements, take a smooth projective base curve of genus at least $\max(d,2)$. In the usual surface group presentation, send the first $d$ generators of type $a$ to the chosen generators and every generator of type $b$ to the identity. This respects the product-of-commutators relation and gives a connected topological Galois cover. Riemann existence gives a finite étale cover by a smooth projective curve $C$ with the required free action [5].

In the one-block case $K\to\mathfrak S_n$ is surjective, with kernel the even sheet flips. Lift a transposition and an $n$-cycle which generate $\mathfrak S_n$. Together with a flip in two slots these lifts generate $K$, since conjugating the double flip gives every pair of slots. This works for $n\ge2$ and proves the genus-three assertion. The full diagonal action on $S^N\times C$ is free. Local trivialization after the finite étale base cover identifies its quotient fibration with the product as asserted. Projectivity descends using the tensor product of the translates of an ample bundle.

Let $E=H^*(\mathbf P^2,\mathbf Q)$, viewed inside $H^*(S,\mathbf Q)$. Taking invariants of the finite direct image of the constant sheaf, including at ramification, gives $H^*(S)^\iota=E$. Hence the anti-invariant part is exactly $V$ in degree two. The K3 surface has zero odd cohomology. If $q:S^N\times C\to Y_{\mathbf a}$ is the quotient, finite étale transfer and Künneth give
$$
q^*H^{2p}(Y_{\mathbf a})=
 H^{2p}(S^N)^K\otimes H^0(C)
 \ \oplus\ H^{2p-2}(S^N)^K\otimes H^2(C).
\tag{1}
$$
The group acts trivially on $H^0(C)$ and $H^2(C)$. Both summands preserve their rational Hodge structures, with $H^2(C)=\mathbf Q(-1)$. In particular no condition on the Jacobian of the auxiliary curve enters (1).

Since $G/K$ has order two, the $K$-invariants in $H^*(S^N)$ are the direct sum of the trivial and $\chi$-character subspaces for $G$. The trivial subspace is
$$
\bigotimes_i \operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}E.
$$
It is spanned by averages of products of units, polarizations, and point classes. All are algebraic. For the $\chi$-subspace each individual flip must have eigenvalue minus one, forcing $V$ in every slot. The entries have cohomological degree two, so permutation introduces no Koszul sign. Taking the ordinary sign in each block gives exactly
$$
W_{\mathbf a}=\bigotimes_i \bigwedge^{a_i} V
$$
in degree $2N$. These two character projectors are averages of algebraic graphs.

Any algebraic class in these two subspaces yields algebraic classes downstairs in both degrees of (1). Take its exterior product with the curve or a point, and push forward by $q$ divided by $|K|$. Pulling back again gives the required invariant cohomology class. Conversely, a class downstairs with pullback $w\boxtimes 1$ gives $w$ upon restricting to $S^N$ over any chosen point of $C$. Pullback followed by the refined restriction takes algebraic classes to algebraic classes. Thus the entire question on $Y_{\mathbf a}$ reduces exactly to the rational Hodge classes in $W_{\mathbf a}$ on the original power.

For $n>21$ the single exterior power vanishes. For $n=21$ it is a rational Hodge line of type $(21,21)$, by taking the determinant of the weight-two Hodge decomposition. This proves Theorem 1.

## 3. Contractions on the physical power

Under the assumptions of Theorem 2 the Hodge group is $\operatorname{SO}(V)$ [1]. Rational Hodge tensors of weight $2N$ in $W_{\mathbf a}$ correspond, after twist by $N$, to its invariants. If $N$ is even, the central transformation $-1$ acts trivially on $V^{\otimes N}$. As $r=21$ is odd, $\operatorname{O}(V)$ is generated over $\mathbf C$ by $\operatorname{SO}(V)$ and this transformation. The orthogonal invariant theorem spans the resulting invariants by tensors of inverse pairings. Each pairing tensor is algebraic on $S^2$. In fact the correspondence $(\Delta_S-\Gamma_\iota)/2$ acts as the identity on $V$ and kills all other cohomology. Its Künneth tensor is the inverse of the intersection form on $V$. Products and block alternation give all the required classes. Spanning over the rationals follows by scalar extension, since these generators and projectors are rational [2].

For arbitrary $N$, algebraicity of the determinant implies algebraicity of every invariant in $V^{\otimes N}$, using the special orthogonal invariant theorem [2, 3]. Products with pairings, permutations and contractions are algebraic operations. We now determine when the converse holds for the specified quotient.

Over $\mathbf C$ choose an orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$. A basis element of $W_{\mathbf a,\mathbf C}$ is given by subsets $A_i\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}$ of cardinalities $a_i$, with basis blades $e_{A_i}$. The subgroup of determinant-one diagonal sign changes fixes such an element precisely when the multiplicities of *all* basis indices have the same parity. This follows by flipping any pair of indices. The blades form a simultaneous eigenbasis, so an invariant vector can have a nonzero coefficient only on a basis element satisfying that condition. If $N$ is odd, all multiplicities must therefore be odd. Equivalently
$$
A_1\mathbin{\triangle}\cdots\mathbin{\triangle} A_k=\{1,\ldots,r\}.
\tag{2}
$$
The attainable sizes of the symmetric difference after $i$ choices are exactly $D_i$. For a current subset of size $c$ and a new one of size $a$, their intersection can have any size from $\max(0,c+a-r)$ to $\min(c,a)$. Each choice gives symmetric difference of size $c+a-2|A\cap A'|$. This proves the recurrence in Theorem 2 and proves vanishing of the Hodge subspace when its odd test fails.

Suppose (2) holds. It yields an actual rational map of Hodge structures onto a Tate twist of $\bigwedge^r V$, induced by algebraic correspondences. For $t$ paired indices, the exterior coproducts and the degree-$t$ determinant pairing give an orthogonal-equivariant contraction
$$
\bigwedge^c V\otimes\bigwedge^a V\longrightarrow
\bigwedge^{c+a-2t}V(-2t).
$$
On two blades with exactly $t$ common indices the result is, up to sign and a nonzero normalization scalar, the blade of their symmetric difference. In the coproduct expansion the two exterior factors subjected to the determinant pairing must select the same $t$-element subset. The common subset is the unique possibility, and the remaining blades have disjoint indices. This verifies nonvanishing with the permutation signs included.

Begin with $A_1$ and at each step choose $t$ to be the intersection size of the accumulated symmetric difference with the next $A_i$. The composite $\Phi$ is nonzero on the chosen complex basis element. The operations defining it use only rational alternation and the rational intersection form, hence $\Phi$ is defined over $\mathbf Q$. It maps $W_{\mathbf a}$ onto $\bigwedge^r V(-(N-r))$. On powers of $S$ each contraction of two entries is effected by restricting along the corresponding diagonal and integrating that slot over $S$. Exterior coproducts and products are rational sums of permutations on the relevant tensor projectors. In particular $\Phi$ takes cycle classes to cycle classes after the indicated degree change.

Polarizable pure rational Hodge structures are semisimple [6]. The surjection $\Phi$ therefore has a rational Hodge-class preimage of any generator of its Tate target. If the rational Hodge conjecture holds for $Y_{\mathbf a}$, (1) and restriction to a fibre realize this preimage by an algebraic class on $S^N$. Applying $\Phi$ gives the determinant class on $S^r$. The preimage need only exist in the category of Hodge structures because the hypothesis on $Y_{\mathbf a}$ supplies its algebraicity. Together with the converse from special orthogonal invariant theory this proves Theorem 2.

## 4. Intersection normalization at the threshold

For $n=21$, let $A$ be the unnormalized alternating tensor of an integral basis of $V_\mathbf Z=h^\perp_\mathbf Z$. The primitive polarization has square 2 in the unimodular K3 lattice, and its complement has signature $(2,19)$ and determinant $-2$. Thus $\int_{S^{21}}A^2=-2\cdot21!$. Write $\alpha\in H^{42}(Y_{(21)})$ for the descent of $A\boxtimes1$ under (1). Let $\eta$ be the fibre divisor of $Y_{(21)}\to C/K$ divided by $|K|$, whose pullback is the cohomology class of $S^{21}$ times a point of $C$. The finite map has degree $2^{20}21!$. Consequently
$$
\int_{Y_{(21)}}\alpha^2\eta=-2^{-19},
\qquad \int_{Y_{(21)}}(2^{10}\alpha)^2\eta=-2.
$$
This gives an intersection test on the smooth 43-fold with a rational choice of generator. The nonzero number follows from the polarized Hodge structure alone.

## 5. Examples, extension, and reproducibility

For two blocks and odd total, condition (2) says that the two subsets are complementary. For $(10,10,3)$, select the first two ten-element subsets to intersect in one index. Their symmetric difference has size 18. Take the remaining three indices in the final block. This retains the determinant at total degree $N=23$. For $(20,20,1)$, the symmetric difference of the first two subsets has size zero or two. The last block gives size one or three. The rational Hodge conjecture thus holds on this 83-dimensional smooth projective quotient of $S^{41}\times C$ under the stated Hodge-group hypothesis.

The unconditional one-block calculation also applies to a smooth double plane branched over any smooth curve of even degree $m\ge2$. This double plane has $H^1(\mathcal O_S)=0$ from the two direct-image summands $\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal O(-m/2)$, hence zero odd rational cohomology by Hodge theory and duality. The branched-cover Euler number is $2e(\mathbf P^2)-e(F)=4+(m-1)(m-2)$. Subtracting $H^0,H^4$ and the single invariant $H^2$ gives rank $r=1+(m-1)(m-2)$ for the odd degree-two part, where “odd” here refers to the deck involution. All even rational cohomology of the smooth one-block quotient is algebraic for $n>r$. At $n=r$ its rational Hodge conjecture is equivalent to algebraicity of the determinant of this part. The block classification in Theorem 2 uses the stated K3 Hodge-group assumption.

All proofs use rational coefficients. The accompanying standard-library Python script compares the recurrence with explicit subsets at ranks 3, 5 and 7 for all block lists of length at most four, including zero-size blocks as controls. It also checks the successive contractions in the displayed rank-21 examples and the normalized intersection number. Run `python3 verify.py` in the supplement directory. The recurrence proof applies to every block size and length.
