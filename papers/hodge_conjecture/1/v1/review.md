# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The uniform result for mixed products of the specified Fermat surface quotients, together with the explicit CM factors and algebraic half-twist realizations, has meaningful scope. The manuscript does not claim to resolve the general conjecture, and acceptance does not satisfy the problem’s full solve definition.

The central argument is sound. Aoki’s Theorem 3.1 [2] gives the stated order-two gap group for degree $4p^e$. The parity character annihilates the standard generators, and the explicit Hodge witness establishes its nontriviality under the quadratic-nonresidue hypothesis. The passage from integral standard-lattice membership to algebraicity handles negative coefficients through conjugation and linear deletion; it does not infer algebraicity by taking tensor roots. The same-parity invariant characters of the double covers lie in the resulting algebraic sector.

The mixed-product step is particularly useful. Independence of the prime-power Galois components reduces the local discrepancies to multiples of $\chi_4$. Auxiliary quartic blocks cancel those discrepancies, and contraction removes the auxiliary factors. The Shioda–Katsura construction [5] supplies the required nonzero join maps, while the polarization-adjoint argument gives algebraic retractions on the specified primitive summands. These steps address the extra Hodge tensors on products rather than merely proving the conjecture separately for each surface.

The explicit family also adds substantial content. The birational formulas, conductor decomposition and boundary calculations support the stated surface invariants. In Section 7, the elliptic quotient and nonzero differential trace provide the geometric input that an abstract half twist alone would not supply. Equivariance selects one elliptic eigenline for each primitive surface eigenline, supporting the claimed dimension and CM type of $A_p$. The subsequent retractions justify extending the product theorem to the abelian factors. The Fourier analysis also distinguishes full and deficient Hodge tori without making nondegeneracy a prerequisite for algebraicity.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the literature examined. Aoki [1–3] supplies the standard-cycle and gap-group machinery, including already-known small-prime cases. Ramón-Marí [6] treats CM K3 self-products and a different class of surfaces; that result does not cover these general-type families. The Fourier method in [7] and half-twist formalism in [8] are appropriately credited. Miranda–Movasati–Rufino–Villaflor [9, Theorem 1.1] proves broad results for degrees below $65$ but explicitly excludes $44$, $51$ and $52$; it does not subsume this uniform surface-sector theorem or the explicit Jacobian-factor construction. Section 8 correctly preserves the distinction between the two degree-$44$ realizations: matching Hodge types does not supply their missing algebraic correspondence. I found no substantial duplication in these comparisons.

For verification, I ran all five supplied programs in a writable copy; all assertions passed, and the five regenerated result files matched the retained outputs. Independent symbolic calculations also verified the fiber involution, elliptic quotient equation, relative differential and base differential in (16), with local-order checks at the listed branch points. These computations support the arithmetic and explicit formulas. They do not independently certify the general correspondence theorems or replace the natural-language proof, which is essential to the acceptance decision.

Two minor improvements would make the argument easier to audit:

- In Lemma 5, write the contraction map and its image explicitly, explaining in one place why equivariance gives exactly one line at each primitive character and hence dimension $2(p-1)$. The ingredients are present, but this important deduction is compressed.
- Retain the mixed-prime rank output generated by `verify_norm_family.py`, or clearly identify it as an additional generated result. This would align the archived evidence more closely with the mixed-rank statement in Section 9.

The claims, attribution, limitations and reproducible evidence are sufficient for publication with these minor clarifications.
