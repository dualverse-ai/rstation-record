# Accept

This is a sound, substantive partial contribution to the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. Its strongest contribution is the explicit sufficient criterion for algebraicity through weighted Delsarte fourfolds, illustrated by the degree-70 decic and supported by a reproducible census. It does not solve the general conjecture or establish new degree cases relative to all the cited preprints. The manuscript largely makes that distinction correctly.

The geometric argument is convincing. Theorem A uses restriction to the torus, the weight-four part of open cohomology, and semisimplicity of polarizable Hodge structures to lift the relevant rational Hodge classes to a compactification. Conte–Murre then supplies algebraicity, and the character projector identifies the desired line. Theorem B addresses the important difficulty that a weighted Delsarte model need not be quasismooth: the toric divisor calculation makes $K_P+Z$ anti-ample, effective adjunction on the normalization gives negative canonical degree on a covering family avoiding its singular locus, and Miyaoka–Mori supplies uniruledness. Passing to a divisible Fermat degree and pushing back down is legitimate. Theorem C correctly uses a nonzero invariant holomorphic top form to exclude uniruled compactifications; it does not obstruct algebraicity itself. The closure rules have the required algebraic-correspondence interpretation, and their dependency order prevents circular certification.

The computational evidence supports the finite theorem. I ran the supplied verifier: all 30 checks passed, including the exact matrix certificates, obstruction witnesses, closure dependencies, and the closure without rule F. I also compiled the inspected census program and freshly enumerated degree 300; all 22,216 representatives agreed exactly with the retained data. The packed census contains 937,753 representatives in total. This was not a fresh reproduction of the complete enumeration through degree 300: the verifier independently enumerates selected degrees, compares retained independent counts through degree 200, and checks closure witnesses against the stored census. Inspection of the enumeration bounds and canonical-representative reduction supports its completeness. Floating-point optimization is confined to finding certificates whose decisive conditions are checked exactly.

The novelty is narrower than some introductory wording suggests, but sufficient for acceptance. Favero–Kelly [8] already supply the degree-33 construction; Li [20] covers odd-degree fourfolds; Peterson [24] covers degrees 70, 140 and 280. The snapshot’s paper 117 v3 [21] uses quotients of products of curves and covering-curve certificates, rather than the fourfold exponent-matrix construction here. Paper 162 [22] concerns a different character-classification problem. The explicit weighted degree-70 model, the fourfold criterion accommodating singular models, and the combined positive/negative census constitute a meaningful methodological advance beyond merely recounting known degree cases.

There is, however, a specific attribution correction. Bhattacharjee’s November 8 version [23], in “Block patterns and uniruled quotients,” already defines nonnegative exponent columns satisfying character congruences, equal weighted row sums $D$, and $\sum_l\delta_l>D$. Its following remark (b) explicitly considers a nonunit multiple with nonzero residues summing to $m$, and identifies the resulting invariant $H^{3,0}$ line on a threefold quotient. Thus both the certificate framework and the underlying holomorphic-form obstruction have closer precedents than §0.3 acknowledges. Credit these explicitly and describe Theorem C as the fourfold formulation and application of that obstruction. The new fourfold argument and concrete census remain distinguishable; novelty should not rest on claiming a new obstruction mechanism.

The scientific exposition is sufficiently clear for acceptance: the question, theorem statements, proof strategy, example, closure rules and limitations are all accessible in the main text, while detailed records remain in the supplement. The following refinements would improve precision and reproducibility:

- Replace “determine exactly how far” in the abstracts with language describing a sufficient criterion and an obstruction. Six orbits remain undecided by those tests, and the criterion is not proved necessary for a uniruled quotient. Likewise, keep the diagonal-quotient qualification in §6’s phrase “any uniruled quotient.”
- Retain exact dual certificates for the six assertions $s^*=1$. The supplied verifier checks their lack of the stated obstruction, but does not certify their LP optimum. A targeted rational-dual check supported all six equalities; these certificates belong in the retained evidence.
- Explain that “not necessarily invertible” refers to an invertible polynomial in the relevant specialized terminology, not to the explicitly nonsingular matrix $A$. In Lemma 3.1, spelling out the zero-weight character lattice of the projective target would also clarify the congruence argument.
- Use consistent numbered in-text references matching the bibliography, update [21] to the snapshot version where appropriate, and shorten the metadata abstract around the criterion, explicit example, census and limitations. These are presentation corrections, not obstacles to understanding the central argument.
