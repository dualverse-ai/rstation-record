# Genus-Two Richelot Correspondences and Algebraic Hodge Similarities of Multiplier Two at Picard Number Six: Sharp Degree-Two Bounds, K3 Products, and Rank-Six Borcea–Voisin Fourfolds

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Setting and the Nonsquare Similarity Barrier Below Picard Number Eight

Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective K3 surface with Néron–Severi lattice $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ of rank $\rho(S)$, rational transcendental Hodge structure $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}^\perp\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$ equipped with the intersection pairing $q_S$, and Hodge endomorphism field $E(S)=\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S)_{\mathbb Q})$. Given a second projective K3 surface $S'$, a **Hodge similarity** of multiplier $\lambda\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ is an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures $\psi\colon T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\sim} T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$ satisfying
$$q_{S'}(\psi(x),\psi(y))=\lambda\,q_S(x,y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in T(S)_{\mathbb Q}.$$
Its **multiplier class** is $\mu=[\lambda]\in\mathbb Q_{>0}/\mathbb Q_{>0}^2$. Following [1, 2, 3], write $G(S)\subseteq\mathbb Q_{>0}/\mathbb Q_{>0}^2$ for the subgroup of classes $[\lambda]$ such that every rational Hodge similarity out of $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ with multiplier in $[\lambda]$ is induced by an algebraic cycle on $S\times S'$. By Buskin's theorem [4], every rational Hodge isometry between projective K3 surfaces is algebraic, so $[1]\in G(S)$ unconditionally, and a single algebraic similarity of class $[\lambda]$ out of $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ suffices to place $[\lambda]$ in $G(S)$ [2, Lemma 3.2].

When $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, Zarhin's theorem [5] identifies the Hodge group of $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{SO}(T(S)_{\mathbb Q},q_S)$. Consequently, for products of K3 surfaces $S_1\times\cdots\times S_k$ with $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$ and $k<\dim_{\mathbb Q}T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}=22-\rho(S_i)$, the rational Hodge conjecture reduces entirely to the algebraicity of nonsquare Hodge similarities between the factors [1, Theorem 1]. The same obstruction governs **Borcea–Voisin (BV) Calabi–Yau fourfolds** $Y=Y(S,\iota;S',\iota')$, constructed as the crepant resolution $\widetilde X/\langle\iota\times\iota'\rangle$ of $\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{Fix}(\iota)\times\mathrm{Fix}(\iota')}(S\times S')$ for K3 surfaces $(S,\iota)$ and $(S',\iota')$ carrying non-symplectic involutions of Nikulin types $\tau=(r,a,\delta)$ and $\tau'=(r',a',\delta')$ distinct from $(10,10,0)$ [6, 7, 8]. By [7, Theorem A], the rational Hodge conjecture for $Y$—and equivalently Teh's stringy Hodge conjecture for the singular quotient $(S\times S')/\langle\iota\times\iota'\rangle$ [8, Theorem 10; 9]—holds if and only if every rational Hodge morphism $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$ is algebraic.

Two systematic mechanisms for constructing nonsquare algebraic Hodge similarities at $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ have been analyzed in recent work, and both hit hard Picard-number walls above $\rho=7$:

1. **Symmetry chains `(I, Q, F)`** [2, 3]: compositions of Hodge isometries, finite symplectic quotients, and fixed-surface correspondences on $K3^{[n]}$-type hyperkähler manifolds reach $[2]$ iff $\rho(S)\ge 9$ (or $\rho(S)=8$ under the local Hasse condition $C_2$), $[3]$ iff $\rho(S)\ge 13$ (or $\rho(S)=12$ under $C_3$), and $[5]$ only for $\rho(S)\ge 16$; for $\rho(S)\le 7$, any group and any action yield only $\mathcal R(S)=\{[1]\}$ [3, Theorems B and C].
2. **Genus-one fiberwise isogenies `(Iso)`** [10]: dominant rational maps $S\dashrightarrow S'$ restricting fiberwise to cyclic $\ell$-isogenies of non-isotrivial genus-one fibrations reach $\mu=[3]$ at $\rho(S)=10$ on a $6$-modulus family $X_h$, but **require $\rho(S)\ge 10$ for $\ell\in\{2,3\}$ and $\rho(S)\ge 16$ for $\ell\in\{5,7,13\}$** across all singular fiber configurations [10, Theorem C and Corollary D].

Accordingly, in the consolidated status table for lattice-generic BV fourfolds with $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ [8, Theorem 1, Table 1, and Theorem 6], **every nonsquare multiplier class remained open at ranks $r\in\{2,4,6\}$** (where $K_2=K_4=K_6=\{[1]\}$), and no algebraic nonsquare similarity with $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ was known on any K3 surface of Picard number $\rho(S)\le 7$.

The geometric reason why genus-one isogenies stop at $\rho=10$ while lower Picard numbers remain accessible is structural: an $\ell$-isogeny of elliptic curves with a base point is a *morphism* of degree $\ell$, forcing reducible Kodaira fibers (such as $4I_3+4\mathrm{II}+4I_1$ at $\ell=3$, contributing $8$ fiber components on top of the hyperbolic plane $U$). By contrast, a **Richelot $(2,2)$-isogeny** $\alpha\colon J(C)\to J(C')$ between principally polarized Jacobians of genus-two curves is induced not by a map $C\to C'$, but by a **$2$-to-$2$ correspondence roof** $Z\subset C\times C'$ of geometric genus $4$ satisfying $\alpha^\vee\circ\alpha=[2]_{J(C)}$ [11, 12]. Realizing a genus-two pencil with a rational Weierstrass base point on a K3 double plane branched along a line plus a smooth quintic ($5$ transverse $A_1$ nodes) requires only $\rho=1+5=6$, with no reducible Jacobian fibers beyond the five $A_1$ exceptional curves.

### 1.2 Main Results

Fix homogeneous coordinates $(t:y:U)$ on $\mathbb P^2$ and $(t:y:V)$ on a partner copy of $\mathbb P^2$, with pencil center $P_0=(0:0:1)$ and pencil parameter $(t:y)\in\mathbb P^1$. Let $\mathbb A^{14}$ parametrize tuples $b=(L_1,D_1,A_3,B_5)$ of binary forms in $(t,y)$ of degrees $(1,1,3,5)$ ($2+2+4+6=14$ coefficients). Given $b$, define the **Richelot partner tuple** $b'=\iota_{\mathrm{Rich}}(b)=(L_1,D_1,A'_3,B'_5)$ by
$$A'_3(t,y)=2L_1(t,y)D_1(t,y)^2-A_3(t,y),\qquad B'_5(t,y)=B_5(t,y)-A_3(t,y)D_1(t,y)^2+L_1(t,y)D_1(t,y)^4,\tag{1.1}$$
and consider the degree-two K3 double planes
$$\begin{aligned}
S_b\,&:\ w^2=(U-D_1(t,y))\,\bigl(L_1(t,y)U^4-A_3(t,y)U^2+B_5(t,y)\bigr),\\
S'_b\,&:\ {w'}^2=2(V-D_1(t,y))\,\bigl(L_1(t,y)V^4-A'_3(t,y)V^2+B'_5(t,y)\bigr),
\end{aligned}\tag{1.2}$$
with minimal K3 resolutions $\widetilde S_b\to S_b$ and $\widetilde S'_b\to S'_b$, and double-plane sheet involutions $\iota(w)=-w$ and $\iota'(w')=-w'$. Let $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}\subset\mathbb A^{14}$ denote the Zariski-open locus of tuples $b$ such that $L_1\not\equiv 0$, $D_1\not\equiv 0$, both plane quintics $Q_{5,b}=\{L_1U^4-A_3U^2+B_5=0\}$ and $Q'_{5,b}=\{L_1V^4-A'_3V^2+B'_5=0\}$ are smooth, and the lines $\{U=D_1\}$ and $\{V=D_1\}$ meet $Q_{5,b}$ and $Q'_{5,b}$ transversely in $5$ distinct points each (automatically disjoint from $P_0=(0:0:1)$, where $U-D_1\neq 0$ and $Q_{5,b},Q'_{5,b}$ are smooth since $L_1\not\equiv 0$).

**Theorem A (The $9$-Modulus $\rho=6$ Richelot K3 Family and Fiberwise Multiplier-$2$ Similarity).**
1. *Involution and RM diagonal.* On $\mathbb A^{14}$, $\iota_{\mathrm{Rich}}^2=\mathrm{id}$, $\iota_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ preserves $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, and its fixed locus $\{A_3=L_1D_1^2\}$ coincides with the $5$-modulus $\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2)$-real-multiplication Humbert family $\mathcal F_{W_8}$ of `hodge_conjecture_156` [13, §5.3].
2. *Pythagorean $(2,2)$-correspondence.* For **every** $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ (without removing any further discriminant locus), the equations
   $$U^2+V^2=D_1(t,y)^2,\qquad w\,w'=(U+V-D_1(t,y))\,\bigl(L_1(t,y)V^4-A'_3(t,y)V^2+B'_5(t,y)\bigr)\tag{1.3}$$
   cut out a geometrically irreducible reduced surface $Z_{\mathrm{tot},b}\subset (S_b\setminus\{\widetilde P_0\})\times_{\mathbb P^1}(S'_b\setminus\{\widetilde P'_0\})$ whose Zariski closure $Z_b=\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{tot},b}\subset\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b$ is flat of bidegree $(2,2)$ over the general fiber of $\mathbb P^1$, is invariant under $g=\iota\times\iota'$, and has generic fiber over $\mathbb P^1$ whose normalization $\widetilde Z_\eta$ is smooth and geometrically irreducible of genus $4$ equipped with a faithful $D_4$-action generated by the two projection deck involutions.
3. *Unconditional fiberwise multiplier two.* For **every** $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ (over $\mathbb C$, without any genericity assumption on $\rho(\widetilde S_b)$ or $E(\widetilde S_b)$), the correspondence homomorphisms $\psi_b=(Z_b)_*\colon H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Q)\to H^2(\widetilde S'_b,\mathbb Q)$ and $\psi_b^\dagger=(Z_b^t)_*\colon H^2(\widetilde S'_b,\mathbb Q)\to H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Q)$ map $T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ and $T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ isomorphically onto each other and satisfy
   $$\psi_b(\sigma_{S_b})=2\sigma_{S'_b},\qquad \psi_b^\dagger(\sigma_{S'_b})=\sigma_{S_b},\qquad (\psi_b^\dagger\circ\psi_b)|_{T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}}=2\,\mathrm{id},\qquad (\psi_b\circ\psi_b^\dagger)|_{T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}}=2\,\mathrm{id},$$
   hence $\rho(\widetilde S_b)=\rho(\widetilde S'_b)$ and $q_{S'_b}(\psi_b(x),\psi_b(y))=2\,q_{S_b}(x,y)$ for all $x,y\in T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$. In particular, $[2]\in G(\widetilde S_b)\cap G(\widetilde S'_b)$.
4. *Nine moduli.* The image $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Rich},6}$ of $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ in the moduli of K3 surfaces (and in the $14$-dimensional period domain of signature $(2,14)$) has dimension $9$.

**Theorem B (Sharpness of Picard Number Six Across Smooth Genus-Two Pencils on K3 Surfaces).**
Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective K3 surface, and let $|C|$ be a complete linear system on $S$ with $C^2=2$ containing a smooth irreducible genus-two curve (by adjunction $C^2=2g(C)-2=2$, this includes the complete linear system of **every** smooth irreducible genus-two curve $C\subset S$). If $|C|$ contains a one-dimensional linear pencil $\Lambda\subset|C|$ whose generic fiber $C_\eta$ over $K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^1)$ is a smooth genus-two curve whose six geometric Weierstrass points admit a $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline K/K)$-invariant partition into three unordered pairs (in particular, if $J(C_\eta)$ admits any $K$-rational Richelot $(2,2)$-isogeny), then $\rho(S)\ge 6$. Thus $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Rich},6}$ achieves the absolute minimum Picard number for genus-two Richelot $(2,2)$-isogenies arising from pencils of smooth genus-two curves on K3 surfaces.

**Theorem C (Explicit $\mathbb Q$-Witness at $\rho=6$ with $E=\mathbb Q$ and Non-Fourier–Mukai Partners).**
For the explicit $\mathbb Q$-rational parameter tuple
$$b_0\colon\quad L_1=t,\qquad D_1=y,\qquad A_3=3ty^2,\qquad B_5=t^5+t^4y+ty^4+y^5,\tag{1.4}$$
we have $b_0\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}(\mathbb Q)$, with Richelot partner $A'_3=-ty^2$, $B'_5=t^5+t^4y-ty^4+y^5$. Both minimal K3 resolutions $\widetilde S_{b_0}$ and $\widetilde S'_{b_0}$ have good reduction at $p=3$ and $p=5$, identical degree-$16$ transcendental Weil polynomials $P_{T,3}(X)$ and $P_{T,5}(X)$ with Galois group $W(D_8)=(\mathbb Z/2)^7\rtimes S_8$, and
$$\rho(\widetilde S_{b_0,\overline{\mathbb Q}})=\rho(\widetilde S'_{b_0,\overline{\mathbb Q}})=6,\qquad E(\widetilde S_{b_0})=E(\widetilde S'_{b_0})=\mathbb Q.$$
Consequently, for every $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ with $E(\widetilde S_b)=\mathbb Q$ (in particular for $b=b_0$ and for very general $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$), **no** rational Hodge isometry $T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\sim}T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ exists, and $\widetilde S_b,\widetilde S'_b$ are neither isomorphic over $\mathbb C$ nor untwisted or twisted Fourier–Mukai partners.

**Theorem D (Rational Hodge Conjecture for K3 Products and Hilbert Schemes at $\rho=6$).**
Let $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ:=\{b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}:\rho(\widetilde S_b)=6\text{ and }E(\widetilde S_b)=\mathbb Q\}$ (nonempty over $\mathbb Q$ by Theorem C, since $b_0\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$). For each seed $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$, let $\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b)$ denote the $\langle[2]\rangle$-similarity orbit of $\widetilde S_b$: the class of complex projective K3 surfaces $A$ with $\rho(A)=6$ such that $T(A)_{\mathbb Q}$ is Hodge similar, with multiplier class in $\langle[2]\rangle=\{[1],[2]\}$, to $T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ (so $\widetilde S_b,\widetilde S'_b\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b)$); set $\mathcal R_6:=\bigcup_{b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ}\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b)$. Let $S_1,\dots,S_k\in\mathcal R_6$ ($1\le k\le 15$, repetitions allowed) be projective K3 surfaces, partition $\{1,\dots,k\}=\bigsqcup_c I_c$ into rational Hodge-similarity equivalence classes ($i\sim j$ iff $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ and $T(S_j)_{\mathbb Q}$ are Hodge similar), and assume that for each equivalence class $I_c$ there exists a single seed $b_c\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$ with $S_i\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_c)$ for all $i\in I_c$ (in particular, this holds unconditionally whenever $S_1,\dots,S_k\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b)$ for one common seed $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$, or whenever factors drawn from distinct orbits $\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_c)$ have pairwise non-similar transcendental Hodge structures). Then:
1. Every rational Hodge class in $H^{2d}(S_1\times\cdots\times S_k,\mathbb Q)$ is algebraic for all $d\ge 0$.
2. For any $S_1,\dots,S_r\in\mathcal R_6$ satisfying the same single-seed condition on each rational Hodge-similarity equivalence class, every rational Hodge class on $S_1^{[n_1]}\times\cdots\times S_r^{[n_r]}$ with $\sum_{i=1}^r n_i\le 15$ is algebraic (in particular on $\widetilde S_b^{[n_1]}\times(\widetilde S'_b)^{[n_2]}$ for every $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$ and $n_1+n_2\le 15$).

**Theorem E (Rank-Six and Mixed-Rank Borcea–Voisin Calabi–Yau Fourfolds).**
1. *Nikulin invariants.* For every $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, the sheet involution $\iota$ on $\widetilde S_b$ (and $\iota'$ on $\widetilde S'_b$) is a non-symplectic involution with fixed locus $R_b=\widetilde Q_{5,b}\sqcup\widetilde\ell_b$ (smooth curves of genera $6$ and $0$) and full integral invariant lattice
   $$L_6:=H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Z)^\iota\cong U(2)\oplus D_4(-1),\qquad \det L_6=-16,\qquad \tau=(r,a,\delta)=(6,4,0),$$
   whose orthogonal rational quadratic space is $V_6:=L_6^\perp\otimes\mathbb Q\cong U_{\mathbb Q}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\langle -1\rangle^{\oplus 12}$, satisfying $V_6(2)\cong V_6$.
2. *Explicit $9$-modulus lattice-generic BV family.* For every $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, $Y_b:=Y(\widetilde S_b,\iota;\widetilde S'_b,\iota')$ is a smooth strict Calabi–Yau fourfold of type $((6,4,0),(6,4,0))$ with Hodge numbers $(h^{1,1},h^{2,1},h^{3,1},h^{2,2})=(16,24,64,316)$ and $\chi(Y_b)=384$. The $g$-invariant cycle $Z_b$ descends to an explicit algebraic $2$-cycle $\zeta_b=\frac12\pi_*p^*[Z_b]\in\mathrm{CH}^2(Y_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ inducing $\psi_b$. At $b=b_0$ (and for very general $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$), $(\widetilde S_b,\widetilde S'_b)$ is **lattice-generic** ($\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S_b)=L_6$, $\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S'_b)=L'_6$, $\rho=r=6$) with $E(\widetilde S_b)=\mathbb Q$, and the rational Hodge conjecture holds for $Y_b$ (and Teh's stringy Hodge conjecture holds for $(\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b)/\langle g\rangle$).
3. *Dense $9$-dimensional families in all $16$ rank-six BV type pairs.* Let $\tau,\tau'\in\{(6,2,0),(6,4,0),(6,4,1),(6,6,1)\}$ be any ordered pair of rank-$6$ Nikulin types, let $\lambda\in 2\mathbb Q^{\times 2}$, and let $Z_{\tau,\tau'}(\lambda)$ be the locus of lattice-generic pairs $((S,\iota),(S',\iota'))$ of types $(\tau,\tau')$ with $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ admitting a rational Hodge similarity of multiplier $\lambda$ (a nonempty countable union of $14$-dimensional families [7, Proposition 5.3; 8, Theorem 1]). Inside **every** $14$-dimensional component of $Z_{\tau,\tau'}(\lambda)$, the subset $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6=\{(S,S')\in Z_{\tau,\tau'}(\lambda):S\in\mathcal R_6\}$ is an analytically dense countable union of $9$-dimensional families on which the rational Hodge conjecture for $Y(S,\iota;S',\iota')$ and Teh's stringy Hodge conjecture for $(S\times S')/\langle\iota\times\iota'\rangle$ hold.
4. *Mixed-rank $(6,5)$ and $(6,4)$ BV loci.* Let $\tau$ have rank $6$ and let $\tau'$ be any Nikulin type of rank $r'\in\{5,4\}$—namely $(5,3,1),(5,5,1)$ for $r'=5$ and $(4,2,1),(4,4,1)$ for $r'=4$, all of which satisfy $r'+a'\le 21-2(6-r')$. Inside every $14$-dimensional mixed-rank component of pairs $((S,\iota),(S',\iota'))$ with $S$ lattice-generic of type $\tau$, $\rho(S')=6$, $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, and $\mu=[2]$ [8, Theorem 4(1)], the subset with $S\in\mathcal R_6$ is an analytically dense countable union of $9$-dimensional families on which the rational and stringy Hodge conjectures hold.

**Scope and open boundaries.**
1. *Remaining $5$ normal BV period directions at rank $6$.* Each component of $Z_{\tau,\tau'}(2)$ has dimension $20-6=14$, whereas $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Rich},6}$ has $9$ moduli; thus $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$ is analytically dense of codimension $5$ in each component rather than the full component ($K_6$ is not enlarged on the complement $Z_{\tau,\tau'}(2)\setminus Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$). However, by [8, Theorem 9.3], every point of $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$ is an admissible algebraic seed for its ambient $14$-dimensional component: on $X=S\times S'$, the continued pure similarity graph class $\gamma$ has Bloch rank $\rho_\gamma=20+6=26$ (even, so the parity obstruction of [8, Theorem 9.1] does not apply on $X$), and verifying the variational Hodge conjecture (or exhibiting one nonzero Bae–Kool–Park reduced virtual cycle) at a single seed in $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$ would establish HC on the entire $14$-dimensional component.
2. *Singular genus-two pencils on higher-degree K3 models.* Because $K_S=0$ on a smooth K3 surface $S$, adjunction gives $C^2=2g(C)-2=2$ for **every** smooth irreducible genus-two curve $C\subset S$ (whereas base-point-free fibrations $S\to\mathbb P^1$ have $F^2=0$ and genus $1$). Thus Theorem B rules out $\rho(S)\le 5$ for every linear pencil whose general member on $S$ is smooth of genus $2$. In particular, on a degree-four double quadric $\pi\colon\bar S\xrightarrow{2:1}\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ branched along a bidegree-$(4,4)$ curve $B$ [21], a general $(1,1)$-curve meets $B$ in $8$ points and lifts to genus $3$ (and simple passage through a smooth point $p\in B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ still leaves $1+7=8$ simple branch points and genus $3$), while if $B$ has an ordinary double point (or cusp) $q\in\mathrm{Sing}(B)$ resolved by a $(-2)$-curve $E\subset S$, the strict transforms $\widetilde C=\mu^*\pi^*D-E$ of $(1,1)$-curves $D\ni q$ are **smooth** genus-two curves on $S$ satisfying $\widetilde C^2=4-2=2$, so $|\widetilde C|$ is a degree-two polarization on $S$ to which Theorem B already applies. The only dominant rational maps $S\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^1$ of generic fiber geometric genus $2$ not covered by Theorem B are pencils of **singular** curves on the minimal K3 surface $S$ itself ($C^2=2p_a(C)-2\ge 4$) whose general member has fixed base-point singularities on $S$ normalizing to genus $2$—for example, the pencil of $(1,1)$-curves tangent to $B$ at a fixed smooth point $p\in B_{\mathrm{reg}}$, whose preimages in $|\pi^*\mathcal O(1,1)|$ ($C^2=4$, $p_a=3$) acquire a fixed node at $\pi^{-1}(p)\in S$; whether such singular $C^2\ge 4$ pencils can support a nondegenerate Richelot partition below $\rho=6$ is left open.
3. *Other multipliers, cross-seed similarities, and determinant classes.* Nothing is claimed for multiplier classes outside $\langle[2]\rangle=\{[1],[2]\}$ (such as $\mu=[3]$ below $\rho=10$, where $(3,3)$-isogeny roofs [19] remain to be analyzed, or $\mu=[5]$ below $\rho=16$), for products containing factors $S_i\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_1)$ and $S_j\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_2)$ from distinct seeds $b_1\neq b_2\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$ that happen to be linked by a rational Hodge similarity of multiplier class $\notin\{[1],[2]\}$, for lattice-generic BV ranks $r\in\{2,4\}$, or for the $\mathrm{SO}_{16}$-determinant class at $k=16$ factors in Theorem D.

## 2. The Pythagorean Richelot Correspondence and Multiplier Two (Proof of Theorem A)

### 2.1 Parameter Locus, Richelot Involution, and Diagonal Boundary

Direct substitution of (1.1) gives
$$2L_1D_1^2-A'_3=A_3,\qquad B'_5-A'_3D_1^2+L_1D_1^4=(B_5-A_3D_1^2+L_1D_1^4)-(2L_1D_1^2-A_3)D_1^2+L_1D_1^4=B_5,$$
so $\iota_{\mathrm{Rich}}^2=\mathrm{id}$ identically on $\mathbb A^{14}$. Moreover, $A'_3-A_3=2(L_1D_1^2-A_3)$ and $B'_5-B_5=D_1^2(L_1D_1^2-A_3)$, so $\mathrm{Fix}(\iota_{\mathrm{Rich}})=\{b\in\mathbb A^{14}:A_3=L_1D_1^2\}$ (since $D_1\not\equiv 0$ on $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, and even on $\mathbb A^{14}$ equality $A'_3=A_3$ is $L_1D_1^2=A_3$, which automatically forces $B'_5=B_5$). Setting $A_3=L_1D_1^2$ turns $S_b$ into $w^2=(U-D_1)(L_1U^4-L_1D_1^2U^2+B_5)=(U-D_1)(L_1U^2(U^2-D_1^2)+B_5)$, which is precisely the $5$-modulus $\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2)$-Humbert double-plane family $\mathcal F_{W_8}$ of [13, §5.3] (where $S'_b$ differs from $S_b$ only by scaling $w'=\sqrt 2\,w$, turning $\psi_b$ into $\sqrt 2$-multiplication on $\widetilde S_b$). Since $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ is defined by $L_1\not\equiv 0$, $D_1\not\equiv 0$, smoothness of $Q_{5,b}$ and $Q'_{5,b}$, and squarefreeness of the binary quintics $Q_{5,b}|_{U=D_1}=B_5-A_3D_1^2+L_1D_1^4=B'_5$ and $Q'_{5,b}|_{V=D_1}=B'_5-A'_3D_1^2+L_1D_1^4=B_5$, it is symmetric under $\iota_{\mathrm{Rich}}$.

For every $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, the branch sextic $B_{6,b}=\{U=D_1\}\cup Q_{5,b}\subset\mathbb P^2$ has $5$ transverse ordinary double points ($A_1$ singularities) at $\{U=D_1\}\cap Q_{5,b}$ and no other singularities. Thus $S_b$ is a normal degree-two K3 double plane with $5$ $A_1$ surface singularities, and its minimal resolution $\widetilde S_b\to S_b$ replaces them by $5$ disjoint smooth $(-2)$-curves $E_1,\dots,E_5\subset\widetilde S_b$. At $P_0=(0:0:1)$, setting $U=1$ gives $(1-D_1(t,y))(L_1(t,y)-A_3(t,y)+B_5(t,y))=L_1(t,y)+O(2)$; since $L_1\not\equiv 0$, $P_0$ is a smooth point of $Q_{5,b}\setminus\{U=D_1\}$, so its unique preimage $\widetilde P_0\in\widetilde S_b$ is smooth. The pullback of the line pencil through $P_0$ has intersection number $H^2=2$ concentrated at $\widetilde P_0$ (one base point $\widetilde P_0$ and one infinitely near base point along the tangent direction of $\widetilde S_b$), so two successive point blow-ups $\widehat S_b\to\widetilde S_b$ resolve the rational map $\widetilde S_b\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^1_{(t:y)}$ into a genus-two fibration with a distinguished Weierstrass section. We work directly with the Zariski closure $Z_b=\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{tot},b}\subset\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b$ on the minimal K3 surfaces themselves.

### 2.2 Derivation from the Richelot Wronskian and the Genus-Four $D_4$ Roof

To connect (1.2)–(1.3) to classical Richelot theory over the generic fiber $K=\mathbb C(t/y)$, extend scalars momentarily to the quadratic extension $K'=K(\sqrt{L_1},\sqrt{A_3^2-4L_1B_5})$ where $L_1=k^2$ and $L_1U^4-A_3U^2+B_5=(kU^2-\beta_1)(kU^2-\beta_2)$. Setting $U=x+D_1$, the right-hand side of $S_b$ factors into three quadratics in $x$ ($G_3$ linear, corresponding to the Weierstrass point at $x=\infty$, i.e. $P_0$, paired with $x=0$, i.e. $U=D_1$):
$$G_1(x)=k(x^2+2D_1x)+b_1,\qquad G_2(x)=k(x^2+2D_1x)+b_2,\qquad G_3(x)=x,$$
where $b_i=kD_1^2-\beta_i$, so $A_3=2k^2D_1^2-k(b_1+b_2)$ and $B_5=b_1b_2+A_3D_1^2-k^2D_1^4$. The determinant of the coefficient matrix of $(G_1,G_2,G_3)$ in $(x^2,x,1)$ is $\Delta=k(b_2-b_1)\neq 0$ (since $Q_{5,b}$ is smooth, $b_1\neq b_2$). Forming the Wronskians $H_i(Y)=G_j(Y)G'_h(Y)-G'_j(Y)G_h(Y)$ for cyclic $(i,j,h)$ and substituting $Y=-V$ yields the exact polynomial identities (`richelot_sharpness.py`)
$$H_1(-V)H_2(-V)H_3(-V)=\Delta\cdot 2(V-D_1)\bigl(L_1V^4-A'_3V^2+B'_5\bigr),\tag{2.1}$$
$$G_1(x)H_1(-V)+G_2(x)H_2(-V)=\Delta\,\bigl(D_1^2-(x+D_1)^2-V^2\bigr)=-\Delta\,(U^2+V^2-D_1^2).\tag{2.2}$$
Recall that for any Richelot triple $(G_1,G_2,G_3)$ with $\Delta\neq 0$, the classical Richelot $(2,2)$-correspondence between $w^2=G_1(x)G_2(x)G_3(x)$ and $\Delta\,{w'}^2=H_1(Y)H_2(Y)H_3(Y)$ is defined by $G_1(x)H_1(Y)+G_2(x)H_2(Y)=0$ and $\sqrt\Delta\,w\,w'=G_1(x)H_1(Y)(x-Y)$ [11, §3; 12, Proposition 4.1]. Under (2.1)–(2.2), the factors of $\Delta$ and the quadratic radical $b_1,b_2$ **cancel completely**, descending the correspondence canonically to the $\mathbb Q(b)$-rational equations (1.3)! Indeed, writing $Q(U)=L_1U^4-A_3U^2+B_5$ and $Q'(V)=L_1V^4-A'_3V^2+B'_5$, modulo $U^2+V^2-D_1^2$ we have $U^2\equiv D_1^2-V^2$, hence
$$Q(U)\equiv L_1(D_1^2-V^2)^2-A_3(D_1^2-V^2)+B_5=L_1V^4-(2L_1D_1^2-A_3)V^2+(B_5-A_3D_1^2+L_1D_1^4)=Q'(V),\tag{2.3}$$
and $2(U-D_1)(V-D_1)=2UV-2D_1(U+V)+2D_1^2\equiv (U+V-D_1)^2\pmod{U^2+V^2-D_1^2}$, so
$$w^2\,{w'}^2\equiv 2(U-D_1)(V-D_1)Q'(V)^2\equiv\bigl((U+V-D_1)Q'(V)\bigr)^2\pmod{U^2+V^2-D_1^2}.\tag{2.4}$$
Thus $Z_{\mathrm{tot},b}$ is one of the two components of the fiber product of $S_b$ and $S'_b$ over the Pythagorean conic $U^2+V^2=D_1^2$ (the other component is $(w,-w')=(\mathrm{id}\times\iota')(Z_{\mathrm{tot},b})$), and $g=\iota\times\iota'\colon (w,w')\mapsto(-w,-w')$ preserves $w\,w'$, hence preserves $Z_{\mathrm{tot},b}$ and its closure $Z_b\subset\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b$.

To prove that $Z_{\mathrm{tot},b}$ is geometrically irreducible of generic fiber genus $4$, put $n=1+s^2$ and parametrize the smooth conic $U^2+V^2=D_1^2$ (since $D_1\not\equiv 0$) over $K$ by the stereographic coordinate $s=V/(U+D_1)$:
$$U=D_1\frac{1-s^2}{n},\qquad V=D_1\frac{2s}{n},\qquad U-D_1=\frac{-2D_1s^2}{n},\qquad V-D_1=\frac{-D_1(s-1)^2}{n},\qquad U+V-D_1=\frac{-2D_1s(s-1)}{n}.$$
Setting $w=\frac{z\,s}{n^3}$ and $w'=\frac{z\,(s-1)}{n^3}$ exhibits the normalized generic fiber $\widetilde Z_\eta$ of $Z_b\to\mathbb P^1$ as the hyperelliptic curve
$$\widetilde Z_\eta\,:\quad z^2=-2D_1\,(1+s^2)\,P_8(s),\qquad P_8(s)=L_1D_1^4(1-s^2)^4-A_3D_1^2(1-s^2)^2(1+s^2)^2+B_5(1+s^2)^4,\tag{2.5}$$
where $P_8(s)=n^4Q(U(s))=n^4Q'(V(s))$. Substituting $z^2=-2D_1n^5Q'(V(s))$ into $(w,w')$ gives $w^2=(U-D_1)Q(U)$, ${w'}^2=2(V-D_1)Q'(V)$, and the exact positive sign $w\,w'=+(U+V-D_1)Q'(V)$ of (1.3) (`richelot_sharpness.py`). We verify that $R_{10}(s):=-2D_1(1+s^2)P_8(s)\in K[s]$ is squarefree of exact degree $10$ over $\overline K$ for **every** $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$:
- On $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, $L_1\not\equiv 0$ and $D_1\not\equiv 0$ in $\mathbb C[t,y]$, while transversality of $\{U=D_1\}\cap Q_{5,b}$ and $\{V=D_1\}\cap Q'_{5,b}$ means $B'_5=Q_{5,b}|_{U=D_1}$ and $B_5=Q'_{5,b}|_{V=D_1}$ are squarefree homogeneous quintics, so $B_5\neq 0$ and $B'_5\neq 0$ in $K$. Moreover, $A_3^2-4L_1B_5\not\equiv 0$ in $\mathbb C[t,y]$: if $A_3^2=4L_1B_5$, then since $L_1\not\equiv 0$ is homogeneous linear (prime in $\mathbb C[t,y]$), $L_1\mid A_3$, so $A_3=2L_1C_2$ and $B_5=L_1C_2^2$ for a quadratic form $C_2(t,y)$, making $Q_{5,b}=L_1(U^2-C_2)^2$ non-reduced and contradicting smoothness of $Q_{5,b}$.
- In $K[s]$, $R_{10}(s)$ has $s^{10}$-coefficient $-2D_1B'_5\neq 0$, hence exact degree $10$, and exact discriminant (`richelot_sharpness.py`)
  $$\operatorname{disc}_s\bigl(R_{10}(s)\bigr)=-2^{76}\,D_1^{58}\,L_1^6\,B_5^2\,{B'_5}^2\,(A_3^2-4L_1B_5)^4\neq 0$$
  (equivalently, $P_8(\pm i)=16L_1D_1^4\neq 0$, $U'(s)=\frac{-4D_1s}{(1+s^2)^2}$ vanishes on $\overline K\setminus\{\pm i\}$ only at $s=0$ where $P_8(0)=Q(D_1)=B'_5\neq 0$, and $\operatorname{disc}_U(Q)=16L_1B_5(A_3^2-4L_1B_5)^2\neq 0$). Hence $R_{10}(s)$ has $10$ distinct roots in $\overline K$, so $\widetilde Z_\eta$ is smooth and geometrically irreducible of genus $4$ for **every** $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$.

Furthermore, on $(S_b\setminus\{\widetilde P_0\})\times_{\mathbb P^1}(S'_b\setminus\{\widetilde P'_0\})$, neither smooth quintic $Q_{5,b},Q'_{5,b}$ contains a line through $P_0$, so $Q(p,U)\not\equiv 0$ and $Q'(p,V)\not\equiv 0$ for every $p\in\mathbb P^1$, and every fiber over $\mathbb P^1$ of $W_b:=\{U^2+V^2=D_1^2,\,w^2=(U-D_1)Q(U),\,{w'}^2=2(V-D_1)Q'(V)\}$ has dimension $1$. Since $W_b$ is cut out by $3$ equations in each $5$-dimensional affine chart, every irreducible component of $W_b$ has dimension $\ge 2$ and dominates $\mathbb P^1$, decomposing $W_b=Z^+\cup Z^-$ into the Zariski closures of the two generic genus-$4$ components $w\,w'=\pm(U+V-D_1)Q'(V)$. Any point $P\in Z^-$ also satisfying $w\,w'=+(U+V-D_1)Q'(V)$ has $w\,w'=0$, hence either $w=0$ ($P=(\iota\times\mathrm{id})(P)\in Z^+$) or $w'=0$ ($P=(\mathrm{id}\times\iota')(P)\in Z^+$). Thus the reduced subvariety $Z_{\mathrm{tot},b}$ cut out by (1.3) equals $Z^+$ and is geometrically irreducible of dimension $2$ for **every** $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$. Direct rational substitution (`richelot_sharpness.py`) verifies that $\sigma_1(s,z)=(-s,-z)$ and $\sigma_2(s,z)=(1/s,-z/s^5)$ are involutions of $\widetilde Z_\eta$ whose composite $\sigma_1\sigma_2(s,z)=(-1/s,z/s^5)$ has square $(s,-z)$ (the hyperelliptic involution), generating a faithful $D_4$-action: specifically, $\sigma_1$ fixes $(U(s),w(s,z))$ while $\sigma_2$ fixes $(V(s),w'(s,z))$ (whereas $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1(s,z)=(1/s,z/s^5)$ sends $w'\mapsto -w'$), realizing the two curve projections of $Z_{\mathrm{tot},b}$ as the deck quotients $\widetilde Z_\eta/\langle\sigma_1\rangle=C_\eta\subset S_b$ and $\widetilde Z_\eta/\langle\sigma_2\rangle=C'_\eta\subset S'_b$.

### 2.3 Unconditional Fiberwise Proof of Multiplier Two

Fix **any** $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}(\mathbb C)$. Since $[Z_b]\in H^{2,2}(\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b)\cap H^4(\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b,\mathbb Q)$, both $\psi_b=(Z_b)_*$ and $\psi_b^\dagger=(Z_b^t)_*$ are morphisms of rational Hodge structures $H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Q)\leftrightarrows H^2(\widetilde S'_b,\mathbb Q)$, dual under the intersection pairings: $\langle\psi_b(\alpha),\beta\rangle_{\widetilde S'_b}=\langle\alpha,\psi_b^\dagger(\beta)\rangle_{\widetilde S_b}$ for all $\alpha\in H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Q)$, $\beta\in H^2(\widetilde S'_b,\mathbb Q)$. For any $x\in T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ and any $\ell'\in\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$, Lefschetz $(1,1)$ gives $\psi_b^\dagger(\ell')\in H^{1,1}(\widetilde S_b)\cap H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Q)=\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$, whence $\langle\psi_b(x),\ell'\rangle_{\widetilde S'_b}=\langle x,\psi_b^\dagger(\ell')\rangle_{\widetilde S_b}=0$. By nondegeneracy of the intersection form on $\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$, $\psi_b(T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q})\subseteq T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$, and symmetrically $\psi_b^\dagger(T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q})\subseteq T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$.

Now compute the action of $\psi_b$ and $\psi_b^\dagger$ on the nowhere-vanishing holomorphic $2$-forms $\sigma_S=\frac{dU\wedge dt}{w}$ and $\sigma_{S'}=\frac{dV\wedge dt}{w'}$ (written on the affine chart $y=1$). On $U^2+V^2=D_1(t,1)^2$ at fixed $t$, we have $U\,dU+V\,dV=0$ and $\frac{w'}{w}=\frac{U+V-D_1}{U-D_1}$. Modulo $U^2+V^2-D_1^2$, exact rational algebra (`richelot_sharpness.py`) gives
$$-\frac{V(U+V-D_1)}{U(U-D_1)}\equiv\frac{U-V+D_1}{U}=1+\frac{D_1-V}{U},\qquad -\frac{U(U-D_1)}{V(U+V-D_1)}\equiv\frac{V-U+D_1}{2V}=\frac12+\frac{D_1-U}{2V}.\tag{2.6}$$
Summing the first expression in (2.6) over the two sheets $U\mapsto -U$ above a general point of $S'_b$ and the second over $V\mapsto -V$ above a general point of $S_b$ yields the fiberwise $1$-form trace matrices in the bases $(\frac{dU}{w},U\frac{dU}{w})$ and $(\frac{dV}{w'},V\frac{dV}{w'})$:
$$M_{21}=\begin{pmatrix}2&2D_1\\0&-2\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}1&D_1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{12}M_{21}=M_{21}M_{12}=2I_2.\tag{2.7}$$
In particular, over the dense Zariski-open complement ${S'}^\circ=\widetilde S'_b\setminus D'$ of a finite union $D'$ of curves (singular fibers, exceptional $(-2)$-curves, and branch curves of $p_2\colon Z_b\to\widetilde S'_b$), $p_2^\circ\colon Z^\circ\to{S'}^\circ$ is finite étale of degree $2$ between smooth complex surfaces, and wedging the $(0,0)$-entry of $M_{21}$ with $dt$ gives the finite-étale De Rham trace $(p_2^\circ)_*(p_1^\circ)^*\sigma_S=2\sigma_{S'}|_{{S'}^\circ}$. By flat base-change compatibility of cycle pushforward along the open immersion $j\colon{S'}^\circ\hookrightarrow\widetilde S'_b$, $j^*(\psi_b(\sigma_S))=j^*(2\sigma_{S'})$ in $H^2({S'}^\circ,\mathbb C)$. By excision, $\ker\bigl(j^*\colon H^2(\widetilde S'_b,\mathbb C)\to H^2({S'}^\circ,\mathbb C)\bigr)$ is spanned by the algebraic curve classes $[D'_i]\in H^{1,1}(\widetilde S'_b)$, which intersects $H^{2,0}(\widetilde S'_b)$ trivially. Since $\psi_b(\sigma_S)\in H^{2,0}(\widetilde S'_b)$, it follows globally that
$$\psi_b(\sigma_S)=2\sigma_{S'}\quad\text{in }H^{2,0}(\widetilde S'_b),\qquad\text{and similarly}\qquad \psi_b^\dagger(\sigma_{S'})=\sigma_S\quad\text{in }H^{2,0}(\widetilde S_b).\tag{2.8}$$

Consequently, the rational Hodge endomorphism $\psi_b^\dagger\circ\psi_b-2\,\mathrm{id}_{T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q})$ annihilates $\sigma_S\in H^{2,0}(\widetilde S_b)$. Because the transcendental rational Hodge structure $T(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ of any projective K3 surface $X$ is irreducible (its kernel on any Hodge morphism vanishing on $H^{2,0}(X)$ is a rational sub-Hodge structure orthogonal to $H^{2,0}(X)$, hence contained in $H^{1,1}(X)\cap T(X)_{\mathbb Q}=\{0\}$ [20, Corollary 3.3.5]), we conclude that $(\psi_b^\dagger\circ\psi_b)|_{T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}}=2\,\mathrm{id}_{T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}}$. Applying the identical argument to $(\psi_b\circ\psi_b^\dagger-2\,\mathrm{id}_{T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}})(\sigma_{S'})=0$ gives $(\psi_b\circ\psi_b^\dagger)|_{T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}}=2\,\mathrm{id}_{T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}}$. Therefore $\psi_b\colon T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ is an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures, $\dim_{\mathbb Q}T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}=\dim_{\mathbb Q}T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ (so $\rho(\widetilde S_b)=\rho(\widetilde S'_b)$), and for all $x,y\in T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$,
$$q_{S'_b}(\psi_b(x),\psi_b(y))=\langle x,\psi_b^\dagger\psi_b(y)\rangle_{\widetilde S_b}=2\,q_{S_b}(x,y).$$
This proves Theorem A(1)–(3).

## 3. Sharpness of Picard Number Six Across Smooth Genus-Two Pencils (Proof of Theorem B)

Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective K3 surface ($K_S=0$). For any smooth irreducible genus-two curve $C\subset S$, adjunction gives $C^2=2g(C)-2=2$, and $|C|$ has no fixed components, hence is base-point-free by Saint-Donat [21, Corollary 3.2 and §4]: $\varphi_{|C|}\colon S\to\mathbb P^2$ has degree $2$ and factors through the canonical double plane $\bar S\colon w^2=F_6(x,y,z)$ with reduced sextic branch curve $B=\{F_6=0\}\subset\mathbb P^2$ having only ADE curve singularities ($A_n,D_n,E_6,E_7,E_8$), while $S\to\bar S$ is the minimal resolution of the corresponding ADE surface singularities. Every pencil $\Lambda\subset|C|$ of genus-two curves is the pullback of the pencil of lines through a unique base point $P_0\in\mathbb P^2$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\rho(S)\le 5$, and that the generic fiber $C_\eta$ of $\Lambda$ over $K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^1)$ is smooth of genus $2$ and carries a $G_K$-invariant unordered partition $\mathcal P=\{\{W_1,W_2\},\{W_3,W_4\},\{W_5,W_6\}\}$ of its $6$ geometric Weierstrass points ($G_K=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline K/K)$).

**Step 1: Irreducibility and geometric genus of $B$.**
Let $R\subset\mathrm{NS}(S)$ be the negative-definite root lattice spanned by the $(-2)$-curves contracted by $S\to\bar S$. Since $C\in R^\perp$ with $C^2=2>0$, $\operatorname{rk}R\le\rho(S)-1\le 4$. Each singular point $q\in\mathrm{Sing}(B)$ of ADE type ($A_n,D_n,E_n$) contributes $r_q=n$ independent $(-2)$-curves to $R$, so $\sum_{q\in\mathrm{Sing}(B)}r_q=\operatorname{rk}R\le 4$. For every ADE plane-curve singularity of rank $r_q\le 4$—exhaustively $A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4,D_4$—its multiplicity $m_q\in\{2,3\}$ and local genus drop $\delta_q=\dim_{\mathbb C}(\widetilde{\mathcal O}_{B,q}/\mathcal O_{B,q})=(\mu_q+b_q-1)/2$ satisfy $\delta_q\le r_q$, namely $(\delta_q,r_q)=(1,1),(1,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,4)$ (`richelot_sharpness.py`). If $B=B_d\cup B_{6-d}$ were reducible ($1\le d\le 5$), Bézout and Hironaka's $\delta$-formula would give $\operatorname{rk}R\ge\sum_q\delta_q\ge B_d\cdot B_{6-d}=d(6-d)\ge 5$, contradicting $\operatorname{rk}R\le 4$. Hence $B$ is **irreducible**, and its normalization $\nu\colon\bar B\to B\subset\mathbb P^2$ is a smooth connected projective curve of genus
$$g(\bar B)=\binom{6-1}{2}-\sum_{q\in\mathrm{Sing}(B)}\delta_q\ge 10-4=6.\tag{3.1}$$

**Step 2: Elimination of $P_0\in B$.**
- If $P_0\in\mathrm{Sing}(B)$, then $m(B,P_0)\in\{2,3\}$, so the general line $\ell_t\ni P_0$ meets $B\setminus\{P_0\}$ transversely in $6-m(B,P_0)\in\{3,4\}$ points; adding at most one odd-multiplicity branch point at $P_0$ leaves $4$ simple branch points on $\ell_t$, so $g(C_\eta)=1<2$, contradiction.
- If $P_0\in B_{\mathrm{reg}}$, linear projection from $P_0$ defines a degree-$5$ morphism $f_5\colon\bar B\to\mathbb P^1$. Over $\eta$, the $6$ Weierstrass points of $C_\eta$ decompose as $\{W_\infty\}\sqcup f_5^{-1}(\eta)$, where $W_\infty$ (lying over $P_0$) is $G_K$-fixed and $G_K$ acts **transitively** on the $5$-element set $f_5^{-1}(\eta)$ because $\bar B$ is irreducible. In any $G_K$-invariant partition $\mathcal P$ into three pairs, let $\{W_\infty,W_*\}$ be the unique pair containing $W_\infty$. For every $\sigma\in G_K$, $\sigma(\{W_\infty,W_*\})=\{W_\infty,\sigma(W_*)\}$ meets $\{W_\infty,W_*\}$ at $W_\infty$, so $\sigma(W_*)=W_*$. Thus $W_*\in f_5^{-1}(\eta)$ is $G_K$-fixed, contradicting transitivity on $5$ elements.

**Step 3: Elimination of $P_0\notin B$ via finite Galois descent on $\bar B$ and Clifford's theorem.**
Suppose $P_0\notin B$, and choose homogeneous coordinates $(x:y:U)$ on $\mathbb P^2$ so $P_0=(0:0:1)$. Since $P_0\notin B$, the $U^6$-coefficient $c_0=F_6(0,0,1)$ is nonzero, and the sections $\nu^*x,\nu^*y\in H^0(\bar B,\mathcal L)$ of $\mathcal L:=\nu^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(1)$ have no common zero on $\bar B$. Hence $q\mapsto(\nu^*x(q):\nu^*y(q))$ defines a finite degree-$6$ morphism $f_6\colon\bar B\to\mathbb P^1$ with $\mathcal L\cong f_6^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)=\mathcal O_{\bar B}(D_\infty)$, where $D_\infty:=f_6^*([1:0])$.

We first prove from **irreducibility of $B$** (Step 1) that $\mathcal P$ induces a non-identity regular involution $\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\bar B)$ satisfying $f_6\circ\tau=f_6$. Over $K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^1)=\mathbb C(x/y)$, $C_\eta\colon w^2=F_6(x/1,1,U)$ is smooth of genus $2$ with $c_0\neq 0$, so $F_6(x,1,U)\in K[U]$ is separable of degree $6$; since $B$ is irreducible over $\mathbb C$, $F_6(x,1,U)$ is **irreducible** in $K[U]$, and the function field of $\bar B$ is the degree-$6$ field extension $L=\mathbb C(\bar B)\cong K(\alpha_1)$ generated by one root $\alpha_1$ inside the finite splitting field $\widetilde L$ of $F_6(x,1,U)$ over $K$. Let $G=\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde L/K)$ and $H=\mathrm{Stab}_G(\alpha_1)=\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde L/K(\alpha_1))$. Under the canonical $G$-equivariant identification of the $6$ geometric Weierstrass points of $C_\eta$ with the $6$ roots $\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_6\}$ of $F_6(x,1,U)$ in $\widetilde L$, let $\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\}\in\mathcal P$ be the unique pair containing $\alpha_1$ ($\alpha_2\neq\alpha_1$). Every $\sigma\in H$ preserves $\mathcal P$ and fixes $\alpha_1$, so $\sigma(\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\})=\{\alpha_1,\sigma(\alpha_2)\}\in\mathcal P$ meets $\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\}$ at $\alpha_1$, forcing $\sigma(\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\})=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\}$ and $\sigma(\alpha_2)=\alpha_2$. By the Fundamental Theorem of Finite Galois Theory, $\alpha_2\in\widetilde L^H=K(\alpha_1)$. Since $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ share the minimal polynomial $F_6(x,1,U)$ over $K$ and $\alpha_2\in K(\alpha_1)$, evaluation $U\mapsto\alpha_2$ defines a unique $K$-automorphism $\tau^*\in\mathrm{Aut}_K(K(\alpha_1))$ sending $\alpha_1\mapsto\alpha_2\neq\alpha_1$. Extending $\tau^*$ to some $\gamma\in G$ gives $\gamma(\alpha_1)=\alpha_2$, whence $\gamma(\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\})=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\}$ and $\tau^*(\alpha_2)=\gamma(\alpha_2)=\alpha_1$, so $(\tau^*)^2=\mathrm{id}_{K(\alpha_1)}$ (equivalently, $K(\alpha_1)$ is a quadratic Galois extension of the degree-$3$ subfield $M=K(\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\alpha_1\alpha_2)$; `richelot_sharpness.py` verifies $\alpha_2\in\widetilde L^H$ and $|N_G(H)/H|\ge 2$ across all $17$ transitive subgroups of $W(B_3)=(\mathbb Z/2)^3\rtimes S_3$). Because $\bar B$ is a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb C$, $\tau^*\in\mathrm{Aut}_K(\mathbb C(\bar B))$ comes from a unique non-identity regular involution $\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\bar B)$ satisfying $f_6\circ\tau=f_6$.

*(Remark: This does **not** claim that $C_\eta$ itself has an extra automorphism, nor does it fail for generic irreducible $W(B_3)$ Richelot plane sextics at higher Picard number. For example, for the homogeneous plane sextic $F_6(x,y,U)=\mathrm{Res}_m(m^3+xym+x^3+y^3,\,U^2-mU+m^2+xy+x^2-y^2)$ (`richelot_sharpness.py`), $F_6$ is irreducible of geometric Galois group $W(B_3)$ with $\Delta^2=-27(x/y)^6-58(x/y)^3-27\not\equiv 0$, and $\bar B$ **does** carry the regular pair-swap involution $\tau^*(U)=m-U=\frac{U^3+(2x^2+x-2)U-x^3-1}{1-x^2}\in\mathbb Q(x)[U]/(F_6(x,1,U))$; what prevents $\tau$ from descending to a Möbius map on $\mathbb P^1_U$ or a linear map on $\mathbb P^2$ there is that $\tau^*(U)$ has degree $3>1$ in $U$, because $B\subset\mathbb P^2$ has $6$ ordinary double points, $g(\bar B)=10-6=4<6$, $h^0(\bar B,\mathcal L)=4$ spanned by $(x,y,m,U)$, and $\rho(S)\ge 1+6=7$. Because $f_6\circ\tau=f_6$, the divisor $D_\infty=f_6^*([1:0])$ is strictly $\tau$-invariant ($\tau^*D_\infty=D_\infty$), so $\tau^*\in\mathrm{Aut}_K(\mathbb C(\bar B))$ restricts directly to a $\mathbb C$-linear involution on $H^0(\bar B,\mathcal O_{\bar B}(D_\infty))=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb C}(1,\,x/y,\,U/y)\subset\mathbb C(\bar B)$, fixing $1$ and $x/y\in K$ pointwise. Replacing $U$ by the linear coordinate $\widetilde U$ (keeping $P_0=(0:0:1)$), $\tau$ is induced by $(x:y:\widetilde U)\mapsto(x:y:-\widetilde U)$ on $\mathbb P^2$, preserving $B$. 1. On the generic fiber, the three $\tau$-invariant quadratic factors have the form $G_i(\widetilde U)=\widetilde U^2-\alpha_i$ ($i=1,2,3$), whose coefficient matrix in $(\widetilde U^2,\widetilde U,1)$ has vanishing middle column and determinant $\Delta=0$, so **no** nondegenerate Richelot $(2,2)$-isogeny exists.
2. Even when $\Delta=0$, the regular involution $\tau'(x:y:\widetilde U:w)=(x:y:-\widetilde U:-w)$ of the normal double plane $\bar S$ lifts uniquely to a non-identity regular involution $\widetilde\tau'\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)$ of its minimal resolution $S\to\bar S$ preserving the holomorphic $2$-form $\frac{dx\wedge d\widetilde U}{w}$; thus $\widetilde\tau'$ is a symplectic involution of the smooth K3 surface $S$, so by Nikulin [22, §5], $\operatorname{rk}H^2(S,\mathbb Z)^{\widetilde\tau'=-1}=8$ is negative definite while $C^2=2>0$ lies in $H^2(S,\mathbb Z)^{\widetilde\tau'=+1}$, forcing $\rho(S)\ge 1+8=9>5$.

In every case $\rho(S)\le 5$ is impossible, proving Theorem B. $\square$

## 5. K3 Products, Hilbert Schemes, and Borcea–Voisin Fourfolds (Proofs of Theorems D and E)

### 5.1 K3 Products and Hilbert Schemes (Proof of Theorem D)

For every $S_i\in\mathcal R_6$ ($1\le i\le k\le 15$), similarity to $T(\widetilde S_{b_i})_{\mathbb Q}$ for some $b_i\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$ gives $E(S_i)\cong E(\widetilde S_{b_i})=\mathbb Q$ and $\dim_{\mathbb Q}T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}=22-6=16\ge 10$. Partition $\{1,\dots,k\}=\bigsqcup_{c=1}^s I_c$ into rational Hodge-similarity equivalence classes ($i\sim j$ iff there exists a rational Hodge similarity $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\sim}T(S_j)_{\mathbb Q}$; since $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$ and $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ is irreducible of signature $(2,14)$, any nonzero rational Hodge morphism $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S_j)_{\mathbb Q}$ satisfies $\phi^\dagger\circ\phi=\lambda\,\mathrm{id}$ with $\lambda>0$, hence is a Hodge similarity). By Proposition 2.2 of [1] (which applies since $E(S_i)=\mathbb Q$ and $\dim_{\mathbb Q}T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}=16\ge 10$, so $\mathfrak{so}(T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q})$ is absolutely simple of type $D_8$), the Hodge group $H$ of $\bigoplus_{i=1}^k T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ decomposes as the direct product $H=\prod_{c=1}^s H_c$ over the equivalence classes, where each $H_c$ projects isomorphically onto $\mathrm{SO}(T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q})$ for $i\in I_c$.

By hypothesis, for each equivalence class $I_c$ there exists a single seed $b_c\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}^\circ$ such that $S_i\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_c)$ for every $i\in I_c$. By Theorem A(3) and Buskin's theorem [4], $\langle[2]\rangle=\{[1],[2]\}\subseteq G(\widetilde S_{b_c})$; hence for every $i\in I_c$, the defining similarity $T(\widetilde S_{b_c})_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\sim}T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ of multiplier class in $\{[1],[2]\}$ is induced by an algebraic $2$-cycle $\Gamma_i\in\mathrm{CH}^2(\widetilde S_{b_c}\times S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ [8, Lemma 2.2].

On $X=S_1\times\cdots\times S_k$, $H^1(S_i,\mathbb Q)=H^3(S_i,\mathbb Q)=0$, and the Künneth projections onto $H^0(S_i)$, $H^4(S_i)$, $\mathrm{NS}(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$, and $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ are algebraic correspondences. Using $\Gamma_i$ and its transpose for each $i\in I_c$ to transport $T(S_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ to the block seed $T_{0,c}:=T(\widetilde S_{b_c})_{\mathbb Q}$ (so $H_c$ acts diagonally via $\mathrm{SO}(T_{0,c})$), every rational Hodge class in $H^{2d}(X,\mathbb Q)$ reduces via algebraic correspondences to $\mathbb Q$-linear combinations of algebraic divisor/point classes wedged with tensor products over $c=1,\dots,s$ of invariants in $\bigl(T_{0,c}^{\otimes m_c}\bigr)^{\mathrm{SO}(T_{0,c})}$, where $\sum_{c=1}^s m_c=m\le k\le 15$. Since $m_c\le 15<16=\dim_{\mathbb Q}T_{0,c}$ for every $c$, Weyl's First Fundamental Theorem of Invariant Theory for $\mathrm{SO}_{16}$ [25, Theorem 5.3.A; 1, Theorem 1] states that $\bigl(T_{0,c}^{\otimes m_c}\bigr)^{\mathrm{SO}(T_{0,c})}=0$ for $m_c$ odd and is spanned by products of pairwise metric contractions $\Delta_{T_{0,c}}\in(T_{0,c}\otimes T_{0,c})^{\mathrm{SO}(T_{0,c})}$ for $m_c$ even (the $\mathrm{SO}_{16}$-determinant requires $m_c\ge 16$), every one of which is algebraic. This proves Theorem D(1). (Note that if two factors $S_i\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_1)$ and $S_j\in\mathcal O_{\langle[2]\rangle}(b_2)$ from distinct seeds $b_1\neq b_2$ were linked by an ambient similarity of multiplier class $\notin\{[1],[2]\}$, they would fall in the same similarity block $I_c$ without a single seed $b_c$, which is precisely what the block-seed hypothesis excludes.)

For Theorem D(2), de Cataldo and Migliorini [26, Theorem 5.4.1] prove that the rational Chow motive $\mathfrak h(S^{[n]})_{\mathbb Q}$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of $\bigoplus_{\nu\vdash n}\mathfrak h(S^{|\nu|})_{\mathbb Q}(|\nu|-n)$, where $|\nu|\le n$ is the length of the partition $\nu$. Taking tensor products over $i=1,\dots,r$ embeds $\mathfrak h(S_1^{[n_1]}\times\cdots\times S_r^{[n_r]})_{\mathbb Q}$ as an algebraic-correspondence direct summand of Tate twists of K3 products $S_1^{m_1}\times\cdots\times S_r^{m_r}$ with $\sum_{i=1}^r m_i\le\sum_{i=1}^r n_i\le 15$; repeating factors preserves the set of Hodge-similarity equivalence classes, so Theorem D(1) implies Theorem D(2). $\square$

### 5.2 Nikulin $(6,4,0)$ Lattice and Lattice-Generic BV Fourfolds $Y_b$ (Proof of Theorem E.1–E.2)

For $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$, the sheet involution $\iota(w)=-w$ on $\widetilde S_b$ reverses the sign of $\sigma_{S_b}=\frac{dU\wedge dt}{w}$, hence is non-symplectic. Its fixed locus $R_b=\mathrm{Fix}(\iota)$ is the strict transform of the branch curve $B_{6,b}=Q_{5,b}\cup\ell_b$ under the blow-up of the $5$ transverse $A_1$ intersection points, so $R_b=\widetilde Q_{5,b}\sqcup\widetilde\ell_b$ is the disjoint union of one smooth curve of genus $g=6$ and $k_{\mathrm{rat}}=1$ smooth rational curve ($N=1+k_{\mathrm{rat}}=2$ fixed components in total). By Nikulin's fixed-locus formulas [27, Theorem 4.2.2], $g=(22-r-a)/2$ and $k_{\mathrm{rat}}=(r-a)/2$ uniquely determine $(r,a)=(6,4)$, so $\operatorname{rk}H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Z)^\iota=6$ and $|\det H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Z)^\iota|=2^4=16$.

On $\widetilde S_b$, let $h$ be the pullback of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(1)$, $E_1,\dots,E_5$ the exceptional $(-2)$-curves, and $\widetilde\ell$ the strict transform of the branch line $\{U=D_1\}$. Since $2\widetilde\ell+\sum_{i=1}^5 E_i=\pi^*(\ell_b)=h$ in $\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S_b)$, we have $E_5=h-2\widetilde\ell-\sum_{i=1}^4 E_i$, and the classes $(h,E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4,\widetilde\ell)$ are all $\iota$-invariant with Gram matrix (`richelot_lattice.py`)
$$G_{L_6}=\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&0&0&0&1\\
0&-2&0&0&0&1\\
0&0&-2&0&0&1\\
0&0&0&-2&0&1\\
0&0&0&0&-2&1\\
1&1&1&1&1&-2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det G_{L_6}=-16.$$
Because $\det G_{L_6}=-16$ already equals $-2^a$, this $\mathbb Z$-span has index $1$ in $H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Z)^\iota$, so it is the **full** invariant lattice $L_6=H^2(\widetilde S_b,\mathbb Z)^\iota$. The unimodular basis change $(h-E_5,\,h-E_4,\,E_1,\,E_2,\,E_3,\,\widetilde\ell)$ ($\det=\pm 1$) carries $G_{L_6}$ to $U(2)\oplus D_4(-1)$, whose discriminant quadratic form takes integer values on $L_6^\vee$, so $\delta=0$ and $\tau=(6,4,0)$. Over $\mathbb Q$, Hasse–Minkowski (`richelot_lattice.py`) gives $L_6^\perp\otimes\mathbb Q\cong V_6:=U_{\mathbb Q}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\langle -1\rangle^{\oplus 12}$, and $1+1=2$ (`pair = [[1,1],[1,-1]]/2` on each $\langle -1,-1\rangle$ block, $\operatorname{diag}(1,\frac12)$ on each $U_{\mathbb Q}$) gives the explicit matrix $C\in\mathrm{GL}_{16}(\mathbb Q)$ satisfying $C^T(2V_6)C=V_6$.

Now form $Y_b=Y(\widetilde S_b,\iota;\widetilde S'_b,\iota')$. With $r=r'=6$, $N=N'=2$ fixed components, and total fixed genus $\Gamma=\Gamma'=6$, Theorem A.2 of [7] gives $(h^{1,1},h^{2,1},h^{3,1},h^{2,2})=(16,24,64,316)$ and $\chi(Y_b)=6(8+16+64-24)=384$. By Theorem A(2), $Z_b\subset\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b$ is $g$-invariant, so $\zeta_b=\frac12\pi_*p^*[Z_b]\in\mathrm{CH}^2(Y_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ is an algebraic $2$-cycle on $Y_b$ whose untwisted pullback $\pi^*\zeta_b=p^*[Z_b]$ induces $\psi_b\colon T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\sim}T(\widetilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ of multiplier $2$ [7, §3; 8, §4]. At $b=b_0$ (and for very general $b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$), Theorem C gives $\rho(\widetilde S_b)=\rho(\widetilde S'_b)=6=\operatorname{rk}L_6$ and $E(\widetilde S_b)=\mathbb Q$, so $(\widetilde S_b,\widetilde S'_b)$ is **genuinely lattice-generic** ($\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S_b)=L_6$, $\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde S'_b)=L'_6$—unlike $Y_h$ in [8, Theorem 3], where $\rho(X_h)=10>6$). Theorem D(1) at $k=2$ gives HC for $\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b$, which is equivalent to HC for $Y_b$ by [7, Theorem A] and to Teh's stringy HC for $(\widetilde S_b\times\widetilde S'_b)/\langle g\rangle$ by [8, Theorem 10].

### 5.3 Dense $9$-Dimensional Families in All Rank-Six and Mixed $(6,5),(6,4)$ BV Loci (Proof of Theorem E.3–E.4)

Nikulin's parity and signature congruences [27, Theorem 3.6.2] yield exactly four $2$-elementary K3 involution types in rank $6$—namely $(6,2,0)$, $(6,4,0)$, $(6,4,1)$, and $(6,6,1)$—two in rank $5$—$(5,3,1)$ and $(5,5,1)$—and two in rank $4$—$(4,2,1)$ and $(4,4,1)$ (`richelot_lattice.py`). For all four rank-$6$ types $\tau$, Lemma 2.1 of [8] (independently verified in `richelot_lattice.py`) gives $L_-(\tau)\otimes\mathbb Q\cong V_6$.

Let $\tau,\tau'$ be any of the $16$ ordered pairs of rank-$6$ types, let $\lambda\in 2\mathbb Q^{\times 2}$, and fix any $14$-dimensional component $\mathcal C$ of $Z_{\tau,\tau'}(\lambda)$, parametrized by very general $\omega\in\Omega(L_-(\tau))$ via $((S_\omega,\iota),(S'_{f(\omega)},\iota'))$ for a fixed rational similarity $f\colon L_-(\tau)\otimes\mathbb Q\xrightarrow{\sim}L_-(\tau')\otimes\mathbb Q$ of multiplier $\lambda$ [7, Proposition 5.3]. By Theorem A(4) and Theorem C, the periods of $\widetilde S_b$ ($b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}$ near $b_0$) form a $9$-dimensional locally closed analytic submanifold $\mathcal P\subset\Omega(V_6)$ whose very general points $\mathcal P^\circ$ satisfy $\rho(\widetilde S_b)=6$ and $E(\widetilde S_b)=\mathbb Q$. Exactly as in the proof of [8, Theorem 2(2)], for every rational isometry $g\colon V_6\xrightarrow{\sim}L_-(\tau)\otimes\mathbb Q$ and $x\in\mathcal P^\circ$, $\omega=g(x)$ satisfies $\omega^\perp\cap L_-(\tau)=0$ and $f(\omega)^\perp\cap L_-(\tau')=0$, so Lemma 2.3 of [8] (with $M=0$) produces a lattice-generic pair $((S_\omega,\iota),(S'_{f(\omega)},\iota'))\in\mathcal C$ with $T(S_\omega)_{\mathbb Q}$ Hodge isometric to $T(\widetilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$, hence $S_\omega\in\mathcal R_6$. Because $\mathrm{SO}(V_6)(\mathbb Q)$ is dense in $\mathrm{SO}(V_6)(\mathbb R)$ (unirationality via Cayley transform), rational reflections meet both components of $\mathrm O(V_6)(\mathbb R)$, and $\mathrm O(V_6)(\mathbb R)$ acts transitively on $\Omega(V_6)$, the union $\bigcup_{g\in\mathrm O(V_6\to L_-(\tau)_{\mathbb Q})} g(\mathcal P^\circ)$ is analytically dense in $\Omega(L_-(\tau))$ and cuts out a dense countable union of $9$-dimensional analytic families inside $\mathcal C$. For every pair in $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$, Lemma 2.2 of [8] gives $[2]\in G(S_\omega)$, so $f$ is algebraic, proving HC for $Y(S_\omega,\iota;S'_{f(\omega)},\iota')$ and stringy HC for the quotient.

Finally, for $r'\in\{5,4\}$ ($j=6-r'\in\{1,2\}$), every Nikulin type $\tau'=(r',a',\delta')$ satisfies $r'+a'\le 10\le 21-2j$ ($8,10\le 19$ for $r'=5$; $6,8\le 17$ for $r'=4$). Moreover, `richelot_lattice.py` verifies explicitly that $2V_6\cong V_6$ embeds isometrically into $V_5=V_6\oplus\langle -2\rangle$ with orthogonal complement of rational class $\langle -2\rangle$ (realized integrally by the root-free sublattice $M=\langle -8\rangle\subset U\subset L_-(\tau')$ via $e-4f$) and into $V_4=V_6\oplus\langle -1,-1\rangle$ with orthogonal complement isometric to $\langle -1,-1\rangle$ (realized integrally by the root-free sublattice $M=\langle -4,-4\rangle\subset U^{\oplus 2}\subset L_-(\tau')$). In every $14$-dimensional mixed-rank $(6,r')$ component with $\mu=[2]$ constructed via Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 4(1) of [8] (parametrized by very general $\omega\in\Omega(L_-(\tau))$), transporting $\mathcal P^\circ$ by rational isometries $g\colon V_6\xrightarrow{\sim}L_-(\tau)\otimes\mathbb Q$ again yields an analytically dense countable union of $9$-dimensional families with $S_\omega\in\mathcal R_6$ and $\rho(S')=6$, on which $[2]\in G(S_\omega)$ makes the multiplier-$2$ similarity algebraic and proves both HC for $Y$ and Teh's stringy HC. $\square$
