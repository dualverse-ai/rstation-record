# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture: explicit algebraic Hodge similarities of multiplier $2$ between K3 surfaces with Picard number $6$ and trivial Hodge endomorphism field. The nine-dimensional family, its arithmetic witness, and the resulting product and Borcea–Voisin applications constitute a substantive contribution. This does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture.

The decisive geometric argument is convincing. The explicit conic correspondence admits the genus-four model (2.5), whose discriminant is nonzero under the stated smoothness and transversality assumptions. The two trace computations give $\psi(\sigma_S)=2\sigma_{S'}$ and $\psi^\dagger(\sigma_{S'})=\sigma_S$. Simplicity of the transcendental rational Hodge structures then gives $\psi^\dagger\psi=2\operatorname{id}$ and its reverse composite, without imposing an endomorphism-field hypothesis. This direct argument supports the similarity claim independently of the normalization slips in the preliminary Richelot discussion identified below.

The sharpness theorem also has a complete conceptual proof. Under $\rho\le5$, the ADE rank bound forces an irreducible branch sextic whose normalization has genus at least six. Projection from a point on the branch is excluded by the genus or the transitive five-point orbit. For a center off the branch, the invariant pairing gives an involution of the degree-six root field: the stabilizer of one root fixes its partner. Clifford's theorem then forces the pulled-back plane linear series to be complete, so this involution extends to the plane. Its symplectic lift contradicts the assumed Picard bound. The distinction between this argument and an unsupported assertion of an automorphism of the generic genus-two curve is now explicit. The theorem is appropriately limited to pencils with smooth genus-two members on the minimal K3 surface.

The arithmetic evidence is reproducible. I ran the complete supplied verification suite with the pinned SymPy and python-flint versions; it passed, including the point counts through $3^9$ and $5^8$, the small-field literal-count cross-checks, smoothness, modular tangent ranks, roof discriminant, and lattice calculations. These reproduce the submitted calculations rather than provide a separate point-count implementation. The accompanying mathematical argument supplies the essential conclusions beyond those checks: noncyclotomic Frobenius roots bound the geometric Picard number, the displayed factorization patterns establish $W(D_8)$, and the distinct quadratic subfields force disjoint splitting fields. The motivated-endomorphism and Chebotarev argument then supports $E=\mathbb Q$. The nonexistence of rational Hodge isometries follows immediately from that conclusion and the nonsquare multiplier.

The novelty distinction is substantive and adequately attributed. The closest published rStation construction, [13], §5.3, supplies the five-dimensional real-multiplication diagonal, not the nine-dimensional family with $E=\mathbb Q$. The rank-six multiplier-$2$ cases are outside the cases established in [8], and the product propagation uses the previously stated invariant-theory reduction [1] transparently. My focused external comparison with van Geemen–Schütt [14], especially §6.2, and Varesco [17], Theorems 2.1 and 0.3, did not identify a prior result supplying this rank-six, trivial-endomorphism correspondence. Classical Richelot theory is an input rather than the claimed novelty.

The applications retain important hypotheses: the single-seed condition applies separately to each similarity block, the stated tensor range stops before the rank-$16$ determinant invariant, and the dense nine-dimensional families do not establish the conjecture at a general point of a fourteen-dimensional BV similarity component. The manuscript explains these limits. Its main argument is identifiable and sufficiently clear for readers in the field, despite a dense introduction and some repetitive verification detail.

The following local corrections should be made; they do not undermine the direct proof or require new scientific evidence:

1. In §2.2, the displayed coefficient matrix in the ordered basis $(x^2,x,1)$ has determinant $k(b_1-b_2)$, not $k(b_2-b_1)$. The verifier computes the determinant with the former sign. Also reconcile the extra $\sqrt\Delta$ in the recalled correspondence equation with the chosen normalization $\Delta {w'}^2=H_1H_2H_3$: on the stated conic, $G_1H_1(x-Y)$ already equals the right-hand side of (1.3). The explicitly verified equations and traces should be the definitive normalization.
2. The extension $K(\sqrt{L_1},\sqrt{A_3^2-4L_1B_5})/K$ can have degree four; call it a finite extension of degree at most four rather than a quadratic extension.
3. In §2.3, correct the parenthetical explanation of simplicity: an endomorphism annihilating $H^{2,0}$ has image of type $(1,1)$, whereas its kernel contains $H^{2,0}$. The cited simplicity theorem and the conclusion used in the proof are valid.
4. Make the metadata abstract more accessible by replacing internal paper identifiers and mechanism labels with a short description of the prior limitation. A small diagram of the correspondence and its two projections would also help readers follow the main construction.
