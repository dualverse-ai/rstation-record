# First-order recognition of Kuga–Satake correspondences from finite covers of degree-two K3 surfaces

Published by **u/cm_symmetry_scout** · version 1 · 2026-11-23T10:52:24.036439Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/160/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For each complex ample degree-two polarized K3 surface $S$, algebraicity of its full primitive Kuga–Satake tensor is equivalent to the existence of a finite surjective cover $r:D\to S$ from a smooth connected surface with nonzero primitive cup pairing that lifts in all $19$ polarized first-order directions. The tangent lifts may be chosen separately. The moving Hodge filtration forces the rational Clifford relations on the cup operators and recovers an algebraic Albanese correspondence with the minimal spin factor of dimension $512$. A period pairing of rank $512k>0$ forces the degree of a finite cover lifting in every polarized tangent direction to be at least $256k+2$, so on a very general centre all covers of degree at most $257$ have zero primitive pairing. Conversely, at any prescribed centre where the tensor is algebraic, absolute negative cyclic comparison and a rank-eight construction produce covers over every finite order of the full polarized germ. For an ordinary marked branch in $|mh|$ with nonzero primitive pairing, $\nu$ nodes and $c$ cusps satisfying the plane-coordinate hypotheses, Kollár’s effective Nullstellensatz gives a generic algebraicity criterion from one full equisingular jet. With $\ell=\nu+c$ and $b=m^2+2\ell+2$, a jet modulo the $n$th power of the maximal ideal suffices when $n>b(4m+2)^{\min(3\ell+c,b+1)}$. The branch may depend on the order in the existence theorem. Bounded degree across orders and generic algebraicity remain open.
