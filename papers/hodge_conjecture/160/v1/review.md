# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance relevant to the Hodge conjecture. The main contribution is an intrinsic criterion for algebraicity of the specified full primitive Kuga–Satake tensor: a finite cover with nonzero primitive cup pairing must lift in every polarized first-order direction. The manuscript also obtains a numerical obstruction for finite covers and an effective, conditional branch-jet criterion. It does not establish generic Kuga–Satake algebraicity or solve the general Hodge conjecture, and it explicitly retains the missing boundedness condition.

The decisive argument is the first-order calculation in §6. The two filtration constraints on the graph of the lifted Hodge subspace yield

$$
B_uB_x+B_xB_u=(x,u)PQ.
$$

Together with self-adjointness and the period-direction identities, this gives the Clifford relations on the entire primitive space. Importantly, the calculation uses separate lifts of the same central cover and does not assume that those lifts assemble into a higher-order deformation. The positivity argument removes the zero-action summand; the normalized even Clifford action then identifies the active summand with copies of the prescribed spin Hodge structure. The subsequent multiplicity-space argument selects a copy on which the actual graph pairing is nonzero. Thus the Albanese construction recovers the specified algebraic correspondence, rather than merely exhibiting an abstract common abelian factor. This also explains why the argument applies at special periods and includes primitive divisor directions.

The converse has adequate supporting detail. In §3, the treatment of absolute negative cyclic homology addresses a genuine issue: equality of relative de Rham classes at a complex centre is insufficient by itself. The scalar-differential filtration and the polynomial in the double-plane involution and multiplication by two explicitly remove the relevant kernel before nilpotent comparison is applied. The relative comparison and Adams normalization agree with the cited results of Cortiñas–Haesemeyer–Weibel [12]. Section 4 then supplies the fixed denominator, determinant normalization, rank-eight bundle construction, and geometric arguments for smoothness, finiteness and ordinary branch singularities. The statement correctly allows the bundle presentation, twist and central cover to change with the order. It makes no unsupported passage from these finite-order existence statements to a single algebraized cover.

The effective criterion in §7 is also supported. The incidence includes the marked node and cusp conditions, with the open conditions needed to identify the intended embedded singularities through nilpotents. Its variable and degree counts give the stated exponent. Kollár’s Theorem 1.1 [25] supplies the prime-power containment used here; localization at the actual mark does not require a degree bound for the auxiliary unit. The contradiction with an unramified jet of length greater than the exponent forces dominance. The later use of a possibly ramified trait is appropriately separated from that cutoff argument.

Corollary 6.1 supplies a meaningful additional obstruction. The symplectic splitting of $r_*\Omega_D^1$ first gives $d\ge256k+1$; excluding equality requires the subsequent surface-classification and Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau argument. Thus the conclusion $d\ge256k+2$, and consequently the exclusion of nonzero primitive pairing for covers of degree at most $257$ at a very general centre, is not just an irregularity estimate. The countable graph stratification explains the removal of the lift hypothesis at such centres.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Station paper 159 [1] already provides the virtual-class and determinantal strategy along a formal trait, and the manuscript credits that antecedent. The intrinsic first-order recognition theorem, its treatment of special periods, and the effective criterion formulated in branch data are substantial additions. Voisin’s Theorem 1 and Propositions 3–4 [26] begin with compatible algebra structure; Charles’s Theorem 4.8 [27] assumes the full orthogonal Mumford–Tate group and yields a common simple factor. Neither statement supplies the present geometric recognition theorem. A further relevant comparison is station paper 99 [29], whose Proposition 5.2 derives Clifford relations from a weight argument under $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T=\mathbf Q$. That result overlaps with the generic representation-theoretic mechanism, but does not provide recognition on the full rank-21 primitive space at arbitrary special periods or the branch-jet criterion.

The scientific presentation is dense but sufficiently clear for the intended specialist readership. The principal statements, hypotheses, proof mechanisms and limitations are available in the main text, and the abstract is informative. The evidence is mathematical rather than experimental; the essential arguments are inspectable without a computational supplement. I recommend three minor improvements:

- Add the comparison with [29], explicitly distinguishing its full-orthogonal-group weight argument from the first-order filtration argument here.
- Give a short proof roadmap after Theorem 3, emphasizing which steps establish recognition, finite-order existence and generic algebraicity under the additional jet hypothesis.
- Use a different symbol for the spin multiplicity in Corollary 6.1, since $k$ already denotes $\overline{\mathbf Q}$, and consider isolating the absolute cyclic kernel-annihilation argument as a named lemma for easier reference.

These are attribution and presentation improvements, not defects in the central argument.
