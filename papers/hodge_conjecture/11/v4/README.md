# The Hodge conjecture for Thom–Sebastiani cubic tenfolds (and quartic sixfolds)

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 4 · 2026-10-18T22:36:02.632543Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/11/v4) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Cubic tenfolds are the first cubic hypersurfaces for which the rational Hodge conjecture (HC) is open in the middle degree. For Thom–Sebastiani tenfolds $X=V(g(x)+h(y))\subset\mathbb P^{11}$, with $g,h$ cubic forms in two disjoint sets of six variables and $T_g,T_h$ the transcendental parts of $H^4$ of the cubic fourfolds $V(g),V(h)$, we prove that HC holds for $X$ if and only if every Hodge morphism $T_g\to T_h$ is algebraic, i.e. $\mathrm{HC}(X)\iff\mathrm{HC}(V(g)\times V(h))$. The proof uses the Katsura–Shioda inductive structure $\mathrm{Bl}\,X\cong\mathrm{Bl}(X'_g\times X'_h)/\mu_3$, $X'_g=V(g+u^3)$, proved here for arbitrary $g,h$; HC for cubic fourfolds; and a level-one lemma (Paranjape–Kollár, Bloch–Srinivas) making all Hodge classes in the $\mu_3$-twisted part algebraic. Combining Beauville–Donagi with Markman's theorem on rational Hodge isometries of $K3^{[n]}$-type varieties, every Hodge isometry $T_g\cong T_h$ is algebraic, with no associated K3 surface needed. Hence HC holds for $X$ unless $T_g\cong T_h$ and the Hodge morphisms are not spanned by isometries (non-isometric rational similitudes, a norm obstruction in the CM case, or real multiplication), cases analogous to those open for $K3\times K3$. This covers very general pairs, and $g=h$ or Fourier–Mukai partners when $\mathrm{End}_{\rm Hdg}(T_g)$ is $\mathbb Q$ or CM. For pairs of triple covers $V(F+x_5^3)$, the van Geemen–Izadi Kuga–Satake correspondence makes all Hodge morphisms algebraic, so HC holds on the whole 20-dimensional family $V(F(x)+x_5^3+F'(y)+y_5^3)$. The rational-similitude case occurs over every very general cubic fourfold; it disappears when one factor has imaginary quadratic multiplication of odd dimension, e.g. $V(F+x_5^3+h)$ with $F$ very general and $h$ arbitrary. We also settle a two-dimensional family with real multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, via Varesco's theorem transported by Markman's. For every other splitting $a+b=12$ we identify the exact residue on which HC depends. The same method gives $\mathrm{HC}(V(g+h))\iff\mathrm{HC}(S_g\times S_h)$ for quartic sixfolds. Bearing on HC: new unconditional cases in the first open cubic dimension; general cubic tenfolds are not addressed.
