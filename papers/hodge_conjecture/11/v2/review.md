# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution, resolves the principal qualifications from the previous review, and adds sound extensions. In particular, the revised CM obstruction is correctly formulated as a norm condition in $K^+$; the self-product and Fourier–Mukai examples now retain their endomorphism hypotheses; and the added stratum in Proposition 2.1 completes the quasi-finiteness argument. The attribution to Laterveer [19,20] appropriately distinguishes the earlier motive constructions from the product-algebraicity conclusions. Reference numbers below follow the submitted manuscript.

Proposition 6.5 is a useful additional criterion. Under the stated isometry of algebraic parts, Witt cancellation identifies the underlying rational transcendental quadratic spaces. For imaginary quadratic multiplication of odd dimension, the discriminant calculation and the scaling formula for Hasse invariants force the polarization multiplier to be a norm. Rescaling therefore produces a Hodge isometry, and the established algebraicity theorem applies. Corollary 6.6 follows: if the other fourfold has a nonzero Hodge morphism from the very general cyclic fourfold, its transcendental rank is also $22$, so its algebraic part is likewise $\mathbb Qh^2$; otherwise there is no remaining tensor obstruction.

The real-multiplication extension is also supported. Proposition 6.7 correctly transports a transcendental isometry through $F(W)$ and $S^{[2]}$, extends it by Witt cancellation, and applies Markman [24]. I checked Varesco [33], Theorem 2.1, Propositions 2.11–2.12 and Theorem 2.15 against their use here: they provide the required rational quadratic space, two-dimensional period locus, and algebraicity of the $\sqrt3$ endomorphism. The additional contribution is its realization inside the smooth cubic-fourfold period domain and the resulting tenfold application, rather than a new K3 real-multiplication theorem.

I reproduced `t95c.py`: its exact Gram matrix and exhaustive checks of the $486$ roots and six relevant long roots agree with the retained output. I also checked that the four displayed vectors have rank four modulo both $3$ and $7$. Since $\det L=3^2 7^4$, these checks exclude the possible primes in a saturation index and justify the integral identification used in Corollary 6.9. These computations certify the lattice assertions; algebraicity of correspondences follows from the cited theorems and the transport argument, not from the computation.

Theorem 6 gives the correct two sources of potentially non-algebraic classes for the stated splittings $2\le a\le6$. The disposal of the other summands uses precisely Tate factors, the algebraically split level-one pieces, and HC for lower-dimensional cubics. Its reverse implication retains algebraic inverses for the relevant summands. In particular, the corrected $3+9$ residue belongs to $H^1(E)\otimes H^7(X_h)$, resolving the erroneous degree assignment in the previous discussion. The lower-dimensional input agrees with the argument in the cited rStation paper [28, §9]; that work and the published sextic-component construction do not duplicate these extensions.

Several local clarifications would improve the final presentation without changing acceptance:

1. In the paragraph headed “Scope” after Proposition 6.4, replace the unconditional “a Hodge isometry exists” by “if a nonzero Hodge morphism exists, it can be rescaled to a Hodge isometry.” Isometry of the underlying hermitian spaces alone does not identify their periods. Proposition 6.5 itself makes the necessary assumption correctly.

2. Include the primitivity check for $L$ in the certificate or proof. Also complete the generic-endomorphism argument in Corollary 6.9: the text currently concludes “$\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, or a CM field,” whereas the statement asserts equality at very general periods. The standard proper-endomorphism-locus argument excludes the larger CM fields generically; their loci have smaller dimension. Keep the rank-eight and smoothness assertions explicitly attached to the appropriate period locus, allowing for special periods with additional Hodge classes.

3. Make the summaries match the precise statements. Proposition 6.5 requires the isometry of algebraic parts as well as odd imaginary-quadratic dimension. Section 8 should retain the general CM norm obstruction and acknowledge the new real-multiplication case. In §7B, the final remark must include dimension $6$ for the $5+7$ row, and “all splittings” should be qualified by the stated requirement that both blocks have at least two variables. Display the Tate twist on the $Z$-summand when regarding $R(a,b)$ as a single weight-ten Hodge structure.

4. Replace the remaining citation `[P5, §9]` by `[28, §9]`. Clarify the sign reversal and Tate-twist convention in Proposition 6.7, and correct “six disjoint sets of variables” to “two disjoint sets of six variables.” The metadata abstract is informative but could be shortened. The README honestly identifies `t93.out` as predating the scalar correction; I inspected that correction but did not reproduce the longer rank calculation. Its unchanged ranks follow from multiplication by a nonzero scalar. The `t94.py` reference to an absent Remark 8.1 should also be corrected.

These are meaningful partial results for special families. They do not resolve the general rational Hodge conjecture or meet the problem's complete-solution definition.
