# Accept

This is an appropriate presentation revision of the accepted paper. The mathematical statements, proofs, numerical evidence and supplementary material are unchanged. The substantive contribution remains the uniform classification of residual Hodge sextuples outside $\mathrm{Dec}\cup\mathrm{Std}_5$ as inflations of six primitive orbits, with the previously established seed constructions and algebraicity results appropriately distinguished from that classification.

The citation conversion preserves the referenced works and their associated claims, and the mathematical delimiter conversion preserves the formulas. The added dependency summary after Proposition 7.21 usefully connects the subfamily reductions, short-kernel and surface lemmas, and final conductor classification. In particular, it identifies the use of Lemma 7.18 for non-decomposability at degree $45$ and keeps the finite censuses outside the proof dependencies. It adds explanation rather than a new scientific result. The manuscript remains organized around the accepted classification argument; this limited addition does not create an unfocused expansion.

Three minor presentation corrections remain:

- Update the metadata abstract's obsolete citation labels `[B26]` and `[J26]` to [5] and [8], respectively, so the automatically displayed abstract agrees with the revised bibliography.
- As noted in the previous review, explicitly include “balanced” among the hypotheses of Lemma 7.19. Its proof uses the conductor Fourier relation, so this assumption should appear in the statement.
- Correct the Appendix's archive and checksum filenames to `calculations-bundles.zip` and `CONTENTS.sha256`, matching the supplied supplement and its README.

These points do not undermine acceptance of the revision. This assessment concerns the editorial changes and their consistency with the accepted argument; the unchanged computational experiments were not rerun. The paper remains a partial result on the supplied problem, not a resolution of the Hodge conjecture for arbitrary smooth complex projective varieties.
