# Non-Split and Quartic-CM Metacyclic Weil Families, and CM-Hodge-Free Fermat-Permutation Cycles on Unbalanced Prym Powers

## 1. Introduction and main results

### 1.1 Constrained Weil-type Prym loci and explicit unbalanced determinant cycles

Let $B$ be a complex polarized abelian variety endowed with complex multiplication (CM) in the broad sense by a CM field $E$, acting on $H^1(B, \mathbb{Q})$ as an $E$-vector space of rank $n \ge 3$. When $n = 2q$ is even and every complex embedding $\sigma : E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ acts on $H^{1,0}(B)$ with multiplicity $q$, $(B, E)$ is an abelian variety of **Weil type** [1], and the $E$-determinant subspace

$$
W_E(B) := \bigwedge_E^{2q} H^1(B, \mathbb{Q}) \subset H^{2q}(B, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{q,q}(B)
$$

consists of exceptional middle-dimensional Hodge classes not generated by divisor intersections on a generic member of the corresponding unitary PEL Shimura family [1, 2, 3]. Proving both the rational Hodge Conjecture (HC) and Grothendieck's Generalized Hodge Conjecture (GHC) on all self-products $B^k$ ($k \ge 1$) along an explicit family of Weil-type abelian varieties requires two ingredients: an algebraic cycle construction algebraizing $W_E(B)$, and a monodromy determination on that family showing that divisor classes and $E$-determinant tensors generate all Hodge classes and saturate coniveau bounds on every power.

Recent work has clarified three distinct frontiers for Weil-type and cyclic Prym varieties:

1. **Special-unitary non-split Weil families beyond fourfolds and Gaussian/Eisenstein sixfolds.** For an imaginary quadratic field $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ ($D > 0$ squarefree), the polarization induces an $F_0$-Hermitian form $h_0$ on $H_1(B, \mathbb{Q})$ whose normalized discriminant

   $$
   \operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(h_0) := (-1)^q \det_{F_0}(h_0) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / \operatorname{Nm}_{F_0/\mathbb{Q}}(F_0^\times)
   $$

   separates the **split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(h_0) \in \operatorname{Nm}_{F_0/\mathbb{Q}}(F_0^\times)$) and **non-split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(h_0) \notin \operatorname{Nm}_{F_0/\mathbb{Q}}(F_0^\times)$) PEL components. Markman [4, Theorem 1.5.1 and Corollary 1.6.1] proved Weil-class algebraicity for all Weil fourfolds ($q = 2$) and for split imaginary-quadratic Weil sixfolds ($q = 3$). Across complete sixfold PEL moduli ($q = 3$), [5, Theorems A–B and Corollary D] established all-powers HC for every Eisenstein ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$) Weil sixfold (and GHC when $\operatorname{End}^0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$), and [6, Theorem A] established all-powers HC for every Gaussian ($\mathbb{Q}(i)$) Weil sixfold. In the **quaternionic** (Albert type-III) setting $\operatorname{End}^0(B) = (-1, -1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with Hodge group $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$, [7, Theorems 1–2] constructed a compact Hurwitz family of sixfolds whose induced quadratic Weil structures are all non-split ($\Delta = 15$), proving quaternionic Weil-space algebraicity and all-powers HC at full-Hodge-group members. For type-IV imaginary-quadratic fields $F_0$, unramified semidirect-product covers over base genus $g \ge 3$ [8, Theorem D and §6] yield **split** forms in dimensions $\varphi(m)(g - 1) \ge 2\varphi(m)$, while cover-source classifications requiring period-image dimension at least $q^2 - 1$ ($\ge 8$ at $q = 3$) encounter reflection and linear-programming obstructions [9, 10, 11]. Explicit families of **Albert type-IV ($\operatorname{End}^0 = F_0$) non-split Weil varieties** with full special-unitary Hodge group $\mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ and unconditional all-powers **HC and GHC** over fields such as $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ (at $q = 3, 5$) and in higher dimensions $q \in \{4, 5\}$ ($\dim B \in \{8, 10\}$) remained unconstructed.

2. **Two-place quartic-CM Weil eightfolds ($\dim B = 8$).** When $F_0$ is a quartic CM field with real quadratic subfield $F_0^+$, a Weil-type eightfold of $F_0$-rank $4$ ($q = 2$) has real Hodge group contained in $\mathrm{SU}(2, 2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2, 2)$. Markman [12, Theorem 1.1.2] reduced Weil classes on such eightfolds to a conditional deformability hypothesis ((M3)) for secant sheaves on real-multiplication fourfolds, [13] proved that translation-regular torsion-free hyperholomorphic carriers of norm-type classes on very general two-place Weil eightfolds are necessarily flat, and unramified semidirect-product covers [8, Remark 6.2] have dimension $\varphi(m)(g - 1) \ge 16$ ($g \ge 3$). Unconditional all-powers HC and GHC on explicit families of two-place quartic-CM Weil eightfolds with full Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2, 2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2, 2)$ remained open.

3. **Explicit CM-Hodge-free cycles and coordinate-permutation thresholds on unbalanced Prym powers.** For cyclic covers $C : y^m = \prod_{i=0}^{n+1} (x - b_i)^{a_i}$, [14] proved all-powers HC and GHC for *balanced* Kummer–Delsarte families ($d_t = \frac{n}{2} + 1$ for all $t \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$). For general (**unbalanced**) connected abelian covers, OpenAI [15, Theorem 1.1] recently proved all-powers HC for the Jacobian $J(C)$ (and hence for its isogeny factor $B = e_m J(C)$) at tensor Hodge-generic points of the **full marked variation**. Moreover, [15, Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.5, and §2 Companion results 2.1–2.2] show that every character determinant line $D(H^1(C, \overline{\mathbb{Q}})_\chi)$ is CM-supplied from powers of Fermat-curve Jacobians $\operatorname{Jac}(F_m^1)$, and—by invoking the companion **rational Hodge theorem for all CM abelian varieties** cited in [15, §2]—deduce algebraicity of rational Hodge classes in external determinant products $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$. However, two natural questions remain outside or alongside [15]:
   - First, [15, Theorem 1.1] applies to the **full marked variation** and does not apply directly to **constrained symmetry loci** carrying extra endomorphisms (such as the metacyclic families of Theorems 1–2 or the $1$-dimensional symmetric slice at $m = 105$ where [16, Corollary E(3)] proved constrained derived monodromy independently while leaving the cross-orbit $(4, 4)$ class $D_{[8]} \wedge D_{[-13]}$ unresolved by elementary cycles).
   - Second, because [15]'s determinant algebraicity relies on the general CM Hodge theorem across powers of $\operatorname{Jac}(F_m^1)$, it leaves open whether cross-orbit Hodge classes $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$ can be constructed **explicitly without CM-Hodge input** from single rescaled coordinate-permutation automorphisms on Delsarte/Fermat hypersurfaces, and precisely when such elementary coordinate permutations suffice to span the Stickelberger Hodge kernel $\ker(q)$.

In this paper, our primary new geometric and monodromy results (**Theorems 1 and 2**) construct constrained ramified metacyclic Kummer–Delsarte families over $\mathbb{P}^1$—lying inside proper exceptional tensor loci of the full cyclic variation—and establish certified non-split Hermitian discriminants, universal odd-$q$ parity over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$, full $\mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ and $\mathfrak{su}(2, 2)^{\oplus 2}$ monodromy, and unconditional **all-powers HC and GHC** using only Fermat linear subspaces and Lefschetz $(1, 1)$ surface joins. Complementarily, on unbalanced Pryms (**Theorems 3 and 4**), we give a **self-contained, CM-Hodge-free** construction of $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$ (and of the open $m = 105$ class $D_{[8]} \wedge D_{[-13]}$ of [16, Corollary E(3)]) via curve-product Chow idempotents $\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n}$ and rescaled coordinate-permutation graphs, and prove an exact **Fourier–Dirichlet saturation formula and $m = 255$ Euler-factor threshold** characterizing when pairwise Fermat coordinate permutations span $\ker(q)$ integrally without [15]'s CM-Hodge input.

### 1.2 Constrained metacyclic Kummer–Delsarte Weil families (Theorems 1 and 2)

Let $m \ge 12$ be an integer, let $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ have multiplicative order $d \ge 2$ with $-1 \notin \langle u \rangle$, and set $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)$ and $F_0 = F^{\langle u \rangle}$ ($[F_0 : \mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(m)/d$), with maximal totally real subfield $F_0^+$. Fix a primitive $d$-th root of unity $\zeta_d \in \mathbb{C}$. Over the $r$-orbit configuration space

$$
\mathcal{U}_r := \bigl\{(z_1, \dots, z_r) \in (\mathbb{C}^\times)^r : z_1^d, \dots, z_r^d \text{ are pairwise distinct}\bigr\},
$$

given orbit seed weights $[s_1, \dots, s_r]$ ($1 \le s_k < m$) and optional $\langle u \rangle$-fixed weights $(a_0, a_\infty)$ at $(0, \infty)$ satisfying $(u - 1)a_0 \equiv (u - 1)a_\infty \equiv 0 \pmod m$, consider the smooth projective Kummer curve $\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}}$ with affine model

$$
y^m = x^{a_0} \prod_{k=1}^r \prod_{j=0}^{d-1} \bigl(x - \zeta_d^j z_k\bigr)^{\langle u^j s_k \rangle_m}, \qquad A_{\mathrm{tot}} := a_0 + a_\infty + \sum_{k=1}^r \sum_{j=0}^{d-1} \langle u^j s_k \rangle_m \equiv 0 \pmod m,
$$

where $\langle x \rangle_m \in \{1, \dots, m - 1\}$ is the least positive residue modulo $m$ for $m \nmid x$. By Proposition 2.1, whenever $\gcd\bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1} u^j, m\bigr) \mid a_\infty$ (in particular, whenever $a_\infty = 0$), $\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}}$ carries an explicit order-$d$ automorphism $\kappa$ lifting $x \mapsto \zeta_d x$ with $\kappa \circ \delta(\zeta_m) \circ \kappa^{-1} = \delta(\zeta_m^u)$, generating $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_u \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z} \subset \operatorname{Aut}(\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}})$.

Let $B_{\mathbf{z}} = e_m J(\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}})$ be the exact-conductor primitive Jacobian factor [14], and define the **metacyclic invariant primitive Prym variety**

$$
B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ := \operatorname{Im}\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1} \kappa^j : B_{\mathbf{z}} \longrightarrow B_{\mathbf{z}}\Bigr)^\circ.
$$

On $B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ$, the CM action of $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)$ restricts to CM by $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)^{\langle u \rangle}$ of $F_0$-rank $n = s_{\mathrm{tot}} - 2$ (where $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the number of branch points on $\mathbb{P}^1$), so $\dim B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ = \frac{n}{2}[F_0 : \mathbb{Q}]$. Unfolding $\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ dominates $B_{\mathbf{z}}$ by the degree-$m$ Fermat $n$-fold $X_m^n$ [3, 14, 17, 18, 19], and pulling back along the closed immersion $i_\kappa : B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ \hookrightarrow B_{\mathbf{z}}$ transfers algebraic Fermat cycles surjectively onto $W_{F_0}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ)$ without CM-Hodge input (Lemmas 2.2–2.3).

**Theorem 1 (Non-split and split special-unitary metacyclic Weil families).** *Suppose $\varphi(m)/d = 2$ and $-1 \notin \langle u \rangle$, so $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)^{\langle u \rangle} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ is imaginary quadratic.*

*(a) **CM-Hodge-free fiberwise Weil-space algebraicity.** Consider the balanced metacyclic families over $\mathcal{U}_r$ ($a_0 = a_\infty = 0$):*
- *Gaussian ($F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, $(m, d, u) = (12, 2, 5)$): seeds $[2^{q-1}, 1, 9]$ ($q \ge 2$) and $[2^{s_0}, 1, 9, (1, 11)^s]$ ($q = s_0 + 2s + 1$, $s_0 \ge 1, s \ge 0$);*
- *Eisenstein ($F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, $(m, d, u) = (12, 2, 7)$): seeds $[3^{q-1}, 1, 8]$ ($q \ge 2$) and $[3^{s_0}, 1, 8, (1, 11)^s]$ ($q = s_0 + 2s + 1$, $s_0 \ge 1, s \ge 0$);*
- *Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$: $(m, d, u) = (15, 4, 2)$ with $[3^s, 5]$ (all odd $q = 2s + 1 \ge 3$) and $(m, d, u) = (30, 4, 17)$ with $[1, 10]$ ($q = 3$);*
- *Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$: $(m, d, u) = (20, 4, 3)$ with $[4^s, 5]$ (all odd $q = 2s + 1 \ge 3$).*

*For **every** $\mathbf{z} \in \mathcal{U}_r$, $(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, F_0)$ is a $2q$-dimensional Weil-type abelian variety whose $2$-dimensional Weil space $W_{F_0}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ) = \bigwedge_{F_0}^{2q} H^1(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, \mathbb{Q})$ is spanned by algebraic cycles pulled back from Fermat linear spaces $\mathbb{P}^q \subset X_m^{2q}$ ($m \in \{15, 20\}$) or Shioda's inductive join of $q - 1$ Fermat lines with a Lefschetz $(1, 1)$ surface class on $X_m^2$ ($m \in \{12, 30\}$).*

*(b) **Universal odd-$q$ parity and certified non-split discriminants ($q \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$).** Every balanced metacyclic cover of Proposition 2.1 with $F_0 \in \{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})\}$ necessarily satisfies $a_0 = a_\infty = 0$ and has **odd** complex half-rank $q \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ (Proposition 4.1). Moreover, the $F_0$-Hermitian polarization form $h_0$ on $H_1(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, \mathbb{Q})$ is **non-split** ($\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}}(h_0) \notin \operatorname{Nm}_{F_0/\mathbb{Q}}(F_0^\times)$) on each of the $13$ explicit families certified in §4.2:*
- ***Weil sixfolds ($q = 3$) and tenfolds ($q = 5$) over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$:** $(15, 4, 2)$ with $[3, 5]$ ($q = 3$) and $[3, 3, 5]$ ($q = 5$), $(30, 4, 17)$ with $[1, 10]$ ($q = 3$), and $(20, 4, 3)$ with $[4, 5]$ ($q = 3$) and $[4, 4, 5]$ ($q = 5$) (with squarefree $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}} \in \{30, 5, 2, 3\}$, whereas the $q = 5$ tenfold control $(15, 4, 2, [3, 5, 5])$ splits with $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}} \equiv 51 = 6^2 + 15 \cdot 1^2$);*
- ***Gaussian and Eisenstein families across $q \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ ($\dim B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ \in \{4, 6, 8, 10\}$):** the four $(12, 2, 5)$ families ($s_0 \in \{1, 2\}, s \in \{0, 1\}$, $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}} \equiv 6$) and the four $(12, 2, 7)$ families ($s_0 \in \{1, 2\}, s \in \{0, 1\}$, $\operatorname{disc}_{\mathrm{norm}} \equiv 6$ or $2$).*

*(c) **Full $\mathrm{SU}(q, q)$ monodromy and all-powers HC and GHC up to tenfolds.** For a very general $\mathbf{z} \in \mathcal{U}_r$ in each of the $13$ non-split families of part (b)—in particular, the five non-split sixfold and tenfold families over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ and the four non-split Gaussian and Eisenstein **eightfold ($q = 4$) and tenfold ($q = 5$)** families—as well as in the four split sixfold families over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ listed in §3.2, one has $\operatorname{End}^0(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ) = F_0$ and*

$$
\operatorname{Hg}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ) = \mathrm{SU}_{F_0}\bigl(H^1(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, \mathbb{Q}), h_0\bigr) \qquad \bigl(\operatorname{Hg}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ)_{\mathbb{R}} \cong \mathrm{SU}(q, q)\bigr).
$$

*Consequently, for every integer $k \ge 1$, **both the rational Hodge Conjecture and Grothendieck's Generalized Hodge Conjecture hold unconditionally on $(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ)^k$**.*

At index $d = 2$ and $\varphi(m) = 8$ ($m \in \{15, 20, 24\}$), metacyclic folding constructs five two-parameter families of **two-place quartic-CM Weil eightfolds** with full two-factor Hodge group, bypassing both semi-regular sheaf deformability [12] and flat hyperholomorphic rigidity [13].

**Theorem 2 (All-powers HC and GHC for five two-place quartic-CM Weil eightfold families).** *Consider the five $3$-orbit ($r = 3, d = 2, n = 4, q = 2, a_0 = a_\infty = 0$) metacyclic Kummer families over $\mathcal{U}_3$:*
1. $(m, d, u) = (15, 2, 4)$, *seeds $[3, 1, 14]$, $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{-3})$, $F_0^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$;*
2. $(m, d, u) = (20, 2, 9)$, *seeds $[2, 1, 19]$, $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}, i)$, $F_0^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$;*
3. $(m, d, u) = (24, 2, 5)$, *seeds $[4, 1, 23]$, $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{6}, i)$, $F_0^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{6})$;*
4. $(m, d, u) = (24, 2, 7)$, *seeds $[3, 1, 23]$, $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{-3})$, $F_0^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$;*
5. $(m, d, u) = (24, 2, 11)$, *seeds $[2, 1, 23]$, $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{-2})$, $F_0^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$.*

*In all five families:*
- *(a) For every $\mathbf{z} \in \mathcal{U}_3$, $(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, F_0)$ is an $8$-dimensional Weil-type abelian variety of $F_0$-rank $4$ and signature $(2, 2)$ at both real places $v_1, v_2$ of $F_0^+$, whose $4$-dimensional rational Weil space $W_{F_0}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ) \subset H^4(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{2,2}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ)$ is spanned by codimension-$2$ algebraic cycles pulled back from complementary Fermat planes $\mathbb{P}^2 \subset X_m^4$.*
- *(b) For a very general $\mathbf{z} \in \mathcal{U}_3$, the monodromy Lie algebra projects onto the full $30$-dimensional product $\mathfrak{su}(2, 2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2, 2)$, so*

  $$
  \operatorname{End}^0(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ) = F_0, \qquad \operatorname{Hg}(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ) = \operatorname{Res}_{F_0^+/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SU}_{F_0}\bigl(H^1(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ, \mathbb{Q}), h_0\bigr),
  $$

  *and for every $k \ge 1$, **both the rational Hodge Conjecture and the Generalized Hodge Conjecture hold on $(B_{\mathbf{z}}^\circ)^k$**.*

### 1.3 CM-Hodge-free Fermat-permutation cycles and the Stickelberger threshold on unbalanced Pryms (Theorems 3 and 4)

Let $B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}} = e_m J(C_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}})$ be the primitive Prym variety of an exact branch tuple $\mathbf{a} = (a_0, \dots, a_{n+1}) \in \{1, \dots, m - 1\}^{n+2}$ ($\gcd(m, \mathbf{a}) = 1$, $\sum_{i=0}^{n+1} a_i \equiv 0 \pmod m$, $n \ge 3$) over $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}(\mathbb{C})$. Set $G := (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, $V_t := H^1(B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}, \mathbb{C})_t$ of signature $(p_t, q_t) = (d_t - 1, n + 1 - d_t)$, and $D_t := \bigwedge^n V_t \subset H^n(B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}, \mathbb{C})$. Define the **Fermat-permutation multiplier subgroup**

$$
U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) := \Bigl\{u \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times : \exists\,\pi_u \in \mathfrak{S}_{n+2} \text{ such that } u a_i \equiv a_{\pi_u(i)} \pmod m \;\; \forall\,0 \le i \le n + 1\Bigr\} \le G.
$$

For every $u \in U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$ and $t \in G$, $d_{ut} = d_t$ implies $p_t + p_{-ut} = q_t + q_{-ut} = n$, so $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut} \subset H^{2n}(B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}^2, \mathbb{C})$ has pure Hodge bidegree $(n, n)$. While [15, Lemma 3.4 and Companion results 2.1(i)] imply algebraicity of rational Hodge classes in $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$ by dominating the distinguished Abel fiber with Fermat-curve Jacobian powers and invoking the companion CM Hodge theorem, we show in §5 that whenever $u \in U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$, $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$ is spanned **directly and without CM-Hodge input** by transferring a single rescaled coordinate-permutation graph $[\Gamma_{\psi_{\pi_u, \boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{-1}}] \in CH^n(Z_{\mathbf{c}} \times Z_{\mathbf{c}})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ through the curve-product Chow idempotents $\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n} \in CH^n(C^n \times C^n)_{\mathbb{C}}$ built from the full cyclic deck group $\mu_m \subset \operatorname{Aut}(C_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}})$.

**Theorem 3 (Explicit CM-Hodge-free Fermat-permutation cycles and the $m = 105$ symmetric slice [16]).** *Let $\mathbf{a} = (a_0, \dots, a_{n+1})$ be any exact branch tuple of conductor $m \ge 3$ and rank $n \ge 3$, and let $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}(\mathbb{C})$ be any ordered branch configuration.*

*(a) **Direct coordinate-permutation construction of $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$.** For every $u \in U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$ and every $t \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, sandwiching the common-quotient transfer of $[\Gamma_{\psi_{\pi_u, \boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{-1}}] \in CH^n(Z_{\mathbf{c}} \times Z_{\mathbf{c}})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ between ${}^t\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n}$ and $\Pi_{U_{-ut}}^{C^n} \in CH^n(C^n \times C^n)_{\mathbb{C}}$ (Lemma 5.2) and pushing forward along $(T \times T)_* : CH^n(C^n \times C^n)_{\mathbb{C}} \to CH^n(B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}^2)_{\mathbb{C}}$ constructs a non-zero cycle class spanning $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$ without invoking the general CM Hodge theorem.*

*(b) **CM-Hodge-free coordinate-permutation proof on the constrained $m = 105$ slice of [16, Corollary E(3)].** On the $1$-dimensional $(2, 2, 1, 1)$-symmetric slice of [16, Corollary E(3)] at $m = 105$ with $\mathbf{a} = (91, 56, 57, 27, 72, 12)$ and $H = \{1, 76\}$ over $S = \mathbb{P}^1_\lambda \setminus \{0, \infty, 1, -1\}$, one has $U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) = \{1, 41, 71, 76\}$, $\pi_{41} = (0\,1)(2\,3)(4\,5)$, and $-41 \cdot 8 \equiv -13 \pmod{105}$. Pulling back the explicit codimension-$4$ cycle of part (a) in $D_8 \boxtimes D_{-13}$ along the closed immersion $(i_H \times i_H)^* : CH^4(\widetilde{B}_\lambda^2)_{\mathbb{C}} \to CH^4(B_{\lambda, \mathrm{prim}}^2)_{\mathbb{C}}$ and the diagonal $\Delta_{B_{\lambda, \mathrm{prim}}}^*$ constructs the $(4, 4)$ cross-orbit Hodge class $D_{[8]} \wedge D_{[-13]} \subset H^8(B_{\lambda, \mathrm{prim}}, \mathbb{C})$ for every $\lambda \in S$ without CM-Hodge input (whereas invoking [15, Lemma 3.4 and Companion results 2.1(i)] on $\widetilde{B}_\lambda^2$ before applying $(i_H \times i_H)^*$ and $\Delta_{B_{\lambda, \mathrm{prim}}}^*$ already yields algebraicity conditionally on [15]'s companion CM Hodge theorem). Combined with $-1 \notin U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$, $\operatorname{rk} L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) = \dim \ker(q) = 42$, and the constrained monodromy theorem of [16, Corollary E(2)], this gives a self-contained CM-Hodge-free proof of all-powers HC on $B_{\lambda, \mathrm{prim}}^k$ ($k \ge 1$) at very general $\lambda \in S$.*

To compare pairwise coordinate-permutation supply quantitatively with [15]'s full CM-Hodge supply across higher self-products $B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}^k$, set $w_x := p_x - q_x = 2 d_x - (n + 2)$ ($x \in G$), define the **Stickelberger kernel**

$$
\ker(q) := \Bigl\{(m_s)_{s \in G} \in \mathbb{Z}^G : \sum_{s \in G} m_s w_{r s} = 0 \;\;\forall\,r \in G\Bigr\},
$$

and define the **Fermat-permutation sublattice**

$$
L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) := \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl\{e_t + e_{-ut} : t \in G,\; u \in U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})\bigr\} \subseteq \ker(q).
$$

**Theorem 4 (Exact Stickelberger–Fourier saturation formula and the $m = 255$ Euler-factor threshold).** *Let $\mathbf{a} = (a_0, \dots, a_{n+1})$ be an exact branch tuple of conductor $m \ge 3$ and rank $n \ge 3$, and assume $-1 \notin U := U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$.*

*(a) **Exact Fourier rank and integral monoid saturation.** One has*

$$
\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})\bigr) = \varphi(m)\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{2|U|}\Bigr),
$$

*and $L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})_{\mathbb{Q}} = \ker(q)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (**Stickelberger saturation**) holds if and only if $\sum_{x \in G} w_x \bar{\chi}(x) \neq 0$ for every odd Dirichlet character $\chi$ of $G$ trivial on $U$. Whenever Stickelberger saturation holds, $-1 \notin U$ implies **exact integral non-negative monoid saturation** with zero denominator:*

$$
\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^G \cap \ker(q) = \operatorname{Monoid}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}}\bigl\{e_t + e_{-ut} : t \in G,\; u \in U\bigr\}.
$$

*(b) **CM-Hodge-free coordinate-permutation recovery of all-powers HC on saturated families.** Whenever $-1 \notin U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$, Stickelberger saturation holds, and at least one $t_\star \in G$ has indefinite signature $1 \le p_{t_\star} \le n - 1$, Theorem 3(a), part (a), and Galois-conjugate Rohde monodromy yield a self-contained **CM-Hodge-free** proof of all-powers HC on $B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}^k$ ($k \ge 1$) at very general $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}$ (recovering these special cases of [15, Theorem 1.1] via explicit coordinate permutations alone). In particular, Stickelberger saturation holds unconditionally for:*

## 2. Metacyclic Kummer–Delsarte Prym varieties and Fermat domination

### 2.1 Function-field closure of the metacyclic automorphism

Let $m \ge 3$ and $d \ge 2$ be integers, let $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ have multiplicative order $d$ with $-1 \notin \langle u \rangle$ (lifted to an integer $1 \le u < m$), and fix a primitive $d$-th root of unity $\zeta_d \in \mathbb{C}$. Given orbit seeds $[s_1, \dots, s_r]$ ($1 \le s_k < m$) and optional $\langle u \rangle$-fixed weights $(a_0, a_\infty) \in \{0, \dots, m - 1\}^2$ satisfying $(u - 1)a_0 \equiv (u - 1)a_\infty \equiv 0 \pmod m$, write $a_{k, j} := \langle u^j s_k \rangle_m \in \{1, \dots, m - 1\}$ ($0 \le j < d$) and assume

$$
A_{\mathrm{tot}} := a_0 + a_\infty + \sum_{k=1}^r \sum_{j=0}^{d-1} a_{k, j} = m D_{\mathrm{tot}} \equiv 0 \pmod m, \qquad A_{\mathrm{fin}} := A_{\mathrm{tot}} - a_\infty.
$$

For $\mathbf{z} = (z_1, \dots, z_r) \in \mathcal{U}_r$, let $\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}}$ be the smooth projective normalization of

$$
y^m = P_{\mathbf{z}}(x) := x^{a_0} \prod_{k=1}^r \prod_{j=0}^{d-1} (x - \zeta_d^j z_k)^{a_{k, j}}. \tag{2.1}
$$

**Proposition 2.1 (Metacyclic cover closure).** *Define the integer exponents*

$$
q_0 := \frac{(1 - u)a_0}{m} \in \mathbb{Z}, \qquad q_{k, j} := \frac{a_{k, j+1} - u a_{k, j}}{m} \in \mathbb{Z} \quad (j \bmod d),
$$

*set $f_\kappa(x) := x^{q_0}\prod_{k=1}^r \prod_{j=0}^{d-1} (x - \zeta_d^j z_k)^{q_{k, j}} \in \mathbb{C}(x)^\times$, and put $S_u := \sum_{i=0}^{d-1} u^i = \frac{u^d - 1}{u - 1}$. Assume*

$$
\gcd(S_u, m) \mid a_\infty \tag{2.2}
$$

*(in particular, (2.2) holds unconditionally whenever $a_\infty = 0$). Choosing $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $k S_u \equiv a_\infty \pmod m$ and setting $\mu_0 := \exp\bigl(\frac{2\pi i (A_{\mathrm{fin}} + k d)}{m d}\bigr)$ ($\mu_0 = \zeta_d^{A_{\mathrm{fin}}/m}$ when $a_\infty = 0$ and $\zeta_d = e^{2\pi i / d}$), the map $\kappa(x, y) := (\zeta_d x, \mu_0 f_\kappa(x) y^u)$ extends to a biregular automorphism of $\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}}$ of exact order $d$ satisfying $\kappa \circ \delta(\zeta_m) \circ \kappa^{-1} = \delta(\zeta_m^u)$, so $\langle \delta(\zeta_m), \kappa \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_u \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}$.*

*Proof.* Substituting $\zeta_d x - \zeta_d^j z_k = \zeta_d(x - \zeta_d^{j-1} z_k)$ into (2.1) and re-indexing $\ell = j - 1 \bmod d$ gives

$$
P_{\mathbf{z}}(\zeta_d x) = \zeta_d^{A_{\mathrm{fin}}} x^{a_0} \prod_{k=1}^r \prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1} (x - \zeta_d^\ell z_k)^{a_{k, \ell+1}} = \zeta_d^{A_{\mathrm{fin}}} f_\kappa(x)^m P_{\mathbf{z}}(x)^u.
$$

Since $\mu_0^m = \zeta_d^{A_{\mathrm{fin}}}$, we have $(\mu_0 f_\kappa(x) y^u)^m = \mu_0^m f_\kappa(x)^m P_{\mathbf{z}}(x)^u = P_{\mathbf{z}}(\zeta_d x)$, so $\kappa$ preserves (2.1). Write $u^d - 1 = m K$ ($K \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$), $Q_{\mathrm{fin}} := q_0 + \sum_{k, j} q_{k, j} = \frac{1 - u}{m} A_{\mathrm{fin}} \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $T_u := \sum_{s=0}^{d-1} s u^{d - 1 - s}$, noting $(u - 1)T_u = S_u - d$. Iterating $\kappa$ $d$ times yields $\kappa^d(x, y) = \bigl(x, \mu_0^{S_u} \Phi_d(x) y^{u^d}\bigr)$, where $\Phi_d(x) := \prod_{s=0}^{d-1} f_\kappa(\zeta_d^s x)^{u^{d - 1 - s}}$. For each root $\zeta_d^\ell z_k$, the exponent of $(x - \zeta_d^\ell z_k)$ in $\Phi_d(x)$ telescopes as $\sum_{s=0}^{d-1} u^{d - 1 - s} q_{k, \ell + s} = \frac{a_{k, \ell} - u^d a_{k, \ell}}{m} = -K a_{k, \ell}$ (and $q_0 S_u = -K a_0$ at $x = 0$), while leading asymptotics $f_\kappa(\zeta_d^s x) \sim \zeta_d^{s Q_{\mathrm{fin}}} x^{Q_{\mathrm{fin}}}$ as $x \to \infty$ fix $\Phi_d(x) = \zeta_d^{Q_{\mathrm{fin}} T_u} P_{\mathbf{z}}(x)^{-K} = \zeta_d^{Q_{\mathrm{fin}} T_u} y^{-m K}$. Using $(u - 1)T_u = S_u - d$ and $A_{\mathrm{fin}} \equiv -a_\infty \pmod m$, the scalar multiplier on $y$ in $\kappa^d(x, y)$ equals

$$
\mu_0^{S_u} \zeta_d^{Q_{\mathrm{fin}} T_u} = \exp\Bigl(\frac{2\pi i}{m d}\bigl((A_{\mathrm{fin}} + k d) S_u - (u - 1) T_u A_{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)\Bigr) = \exp\Bigl(\frac{2\pi i (k S_u + A_{\mathrm{fin}})}{m}\Bigr) = 1
$$

by choice of $k$ (certified across all $22$ covers and a closure-failing negative control in Check A1). Thus $\kappa^d(x, y) = (x, y^{u^d - m K}) = (x, y)$, $\kappa(\delta(\zeta_m)(x, y)) = \delta(\zeta_m^u)(\kappa(x, y))$, and smoothness of the normalization extends $\kappa$ across $x = \infty$. $\square$

### 2.2 Inclusion pullback and Fermat/Shioda source algebraicity

Let $B = B_{\mathbf{z}} = e_m J(\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}})$ be the exact-conductor Prym variety of $\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{z}}$, on which $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)$ acts with $F$-rank $n = s_{\mathrm{tot}} - 2$. Let $\pi_\kappa := \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1} \kappa^j \in \operatorname{End}^0(B)$ and $B^\circ = (\operatorname{Im} d\,\pi_\kappa)^\circ \subset B$, with **abelian-subvariety inclusion morphism** $i_\kappa : B^\circ \hookrightarrow B$ and surjective homomorphism $q_\kappa : B \to B^\circ$ satisfying $q_\kappa \circ i_\kappa = [d]_{B^\circ}$. On singular cohomology, $\kappa^*$ maps the $\zeta_m^t$-eigenspace $V_t = H^1(B, \mathbb{C})_t$ isomorphically onto $V_{ut}$ ($\delta(\zeta_m)^* \kappa^* \omega = \kappa^* \delta(\zeta_m^u)^* \omega = \zeta_m^{ut} \kappa^* \omega$). Thus $H^1(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})$ identifies via $q_\kappa^*$ with $H^1(B, \mathbb{C})^{\langle \kappa^* \rangle}$, decomposing into $n$-dimensional eigenspaces

$$
H^1(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})_{[t]} \cong \Bigl\{\sum_{j=0}^{d-1} (\kappa^*)^j v : v \in V_t\Bigr\}, \qquad [t] \in G / \langle u \rangle,
$$

indexed by the $\varphi(m)/d$ complex embeddings of $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)^{\langle u \rangle}$. Since $d_{u^j t} = d_t$, $(B^\circ, F_0)$ is of Weil type ($n = 2q$) if and only if $d_t = q + 1$ for one representative $t$ of each coset $[t] \in G / \langle u \rangle$.

**Lemma 2.2 (Inclusion pullback surjectivity from $W_F(B)$ to $W_{F_0}(B^\circ)$).** *Let $i_\kappa : B^\circ \hookrightarrow B$ be the closed immersion of $B^\circ$, and put $D_s := \bigwedge^n V_s \subset H^n(B, \mathbb{C})$ ($s \in G$) and $D_{[t]}^\circ := \bigwedge^n H^1(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})_{[t]} \subset H^n(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})$ ($[t] \in G / \langle u \rangle$).*
1. *For every $t \in G$, the restriction $i_\kappa^* : V_t \to H^1(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})_{[t]}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear isomorphism; since $i_\kappa^*$ is a graded-algebra homomorphism on $\bigwedge^* H^1$, $i_\kappa^*(D_t) = \bigwedge^n(i_\kappa^* V_t) = D_{[t]}^\circ$ is an isomorphism of $1$-dimensional determinant lines.*
2. *The rational pullback $i_\kappa^* : W_F(B) \to W_{F_0}(B^\circ)$ satisfies $i_\kappa^*(W_F(B)) = W_{F_0}(B^\circ)$. In particular, if $W_F(B) \subset \operatorname{cl}(CH^q(B)_{\mathbb{Q}})$, then $W_{F_0}(B^\circ) \subset \operatorname{cl}(CH^q(B^\circ)_{\mathbb{Q}})$.*

*Proof.* Under $q_\kappa^* : H^1(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})_{[t]} \xrightarrow{\sim} \bigl\{\sum_{j=0}^{d-1} (\kappa^*)^j v : v \in V_t\bigr\}$, the identities $i_\kappa^* \circ q_\kappa^* = d \cdot \mathrm{id}_{H^1(B^\circ)}$ and $i_\kappa^* \circ \kappa^* = i_\kappa^*$ give $d \cdot i_\kappa^*(v) = \sum_{j=0}^{d-1} i_\kappa^*((\kappa^*)^j v) = i_\kappa^*(q_\kappa^* \tilde{v}) = d \cdot \tilde{v}$ for every $v \in V_t$ (where $q_\kappa^* \tilde{v} = \sum_{j=0}^{d-1} (\kappa^*)^j v$). Thus $i_\kappa^*|_{V_t} : V_t \xrightarrow{\sim} H^1(B^\circ, \mathbb{C})_{[t]}$ is an isomorphism, and functoriality of $i_\kappa^*$ on exterior powers proves (1). For (2), $W_F(B) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} = \bigoplus_{s \in G} D_s$ (with non-vanishing $d \times d$ Dedekind–Vandermonde determinant $\det\bigl(\zeta_m^{b t u^a}\bigr)_{0 \le a, b < d} \neq 0$ across every $\langle u \rangle$-orbit, Check A2), so $i_\kappa^*(W_F(B)) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} = \sum_{s \in G} i_\kappa^*(D_s) = \bigoplus_{[t] \in G/\langle u \rangle} D_{[t]}^\circ = W_{F_0}(B^\circ) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C}$, whence $i_\kappa^*(W_F(B)) = W_{F_0}(B^\circ)$ by faithful flatness of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q}$. Functoriality of $i_\kappa^* : CH^q(B)_{\mathbb{Q}} \to CH^q(B^\circ)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ concludes the proof. $\square$

### 2.3 All-powers HC and GHC from $\operatorname{Res}_{F_0^+/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SU}(q, q)$

### 4.2 Universal odd-$q$ parity over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and certified discriminants

Before recording the finite discriminant table, we prove a general parity restriction explaining why balanced cyclic-rotation covers over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ occur exclusively at **odd** $q$.

**Proposition 4.1 (Universal odd-$q$ parity over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$).** *Let $m \ge 3$ and $u \in (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ of multiplicative order $d$ satisfy $-1 \notin \langle u \rangle$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)^{\langle u \rangle} = F_0 \in \{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})\}$. ## 5. CM-Hodge-free curve-product Chow projectors and Fermat-permutation cycles on unbalanced Pryms

### 5.1 Common-quotient setup and correspondence conventions

Throughout this section we work with smooth projective varieties over $\mathbb{C}$. For a smooth complex projective variety $X$, let $CH^p(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} := CH^p(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$ denote its rational Chow group of codimension-$p$ algebraic cycles and set $CH^p(X)_{\mathbb{C}} := CH^p(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C}$. Because $\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{C}} : CH^p(X)_{\mathbb{C}} \to H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{C})$ equals $\operatorname{cl} \otimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}}$, we have $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{C}}) = \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{cl}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C}$, and by faithful flatness of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q}$, any rational subspace $M \subseteq H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{Q})$ satisfying $M \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} \subseteq \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{C}})$ satisfies $M \subseteq \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{cl})$.

For smooth complex projective varieties $X, W$ and a correspondence $Z \in CH^{\dim X + r}(X \times W)_{\mathbb{C}}$, let $Z_* : H^k(X, \mathbb{C}) \to H^{k + 2r}(W, \mathbb{C})$ denote $Z_*(\alpha) := (p_W)_*(p_X^*(\alpha) \cup \operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{C}}(Z))$. Factor transposition $\tau : X \times W \xrightarrow{\sim} W \times X$ preserves cycle codimension, giving ${}^t Z := \tau_*(Z) \in CH^{\dim X + r}(W \times X)_{\mathbb{C}} = CH^{\dim W + (\dim X - \dim W + r)}(W \times X)_{\mathbb{C}}$, so $({}^t Z)_* : H^\ell(W, \mathbb{C}) \to H^{\ell + 2(\dim X - \dim W + r)}(X, \mathbb{C})$ shifts cohomology degree by $2(\dim X - \dim W + r)$ (which equals $2r$ when $\dim X = \dim W$) and satisfies $\int_W Z_*(\alpha) \cup \beta = \int_X \alpha \cup ({}^t Z)_*(\beta)$; correspondence composition satisfies $(Z_2 \circ Z_1)_* = (Z_2)_* \circ (Z_1)_*$. ### 5.2 Exact curve-product Chow idempotent and proof of Theorem 3

To isolate a single cross-orbit line $D_t \boxtimes D_{-ut}$ explicitly without invoking [15]'s companion CM-Hodge theorem or formal endomorphism exterior powers on $B$, we construct an exact Chow idempotent on $C^n \times C^n$ using the **full** cyclic curve deck group $\mu_m = \{\delta_c : 0 \le c < m\} \subset \operatorname{Aut}(C)$ and $\mathfrak{S}_n \subset \operatorname{Aut}(C^n)$.

**Lemma 5.2 (Exact curve-product Chow idempotent $\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n}$).** *For each $t \in G = (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$, define*

$$
\pi_{1, t}^C := \Bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{c=0}^{m-1} \zeta_m^{-t c} [{}^t\Gamma_{\delta_c}]\Bigr) \circ \pi_1^C \in CH^1(C \times C)_{\mathbb{C}}, \qquad E_{\mathfrak{S}_n} := \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in \mathfrak{S}_n} [\Gamma_\sigma] \in CH^n(C^n \times C^n)_{\mathbb{Q}},
$$

*and set*

$$
\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n} := E_{\mathfrak{S}_n} \circ (\pi_{1, t}^C)^{\boxtimes n} \in CH^n(C^n \times C^n)_{\mathbb{C}}. \tag{5.2}
$$

*Then:*
1. *On $H^*(C, \mathbb{C})$, $(\pi_{1, t}^C)_*$ acts as $\mathrm{id}$ on $H^1(C, \mathbb{C})_t$ and as $0$ on $H^0(C, \mathbb{C}) \oplus H^2(C, \mathbb{C})$ and on $H^1(C, \mathbb{C})_s$ for **every** $s \not\equiv t \pmod m$ (both primitive and imprimitive), while $({}^t\pi_{1, t}^C)_* = (\pi_{1, -t}^C)_*$.*
2. *On the full cohomology ring $H^*(C^n, \mathbb{C})$, $(\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n})_*$ is an idempotent projector with $1$-dimensional image $U_t \subset H^n(C^n, \mathbb{C})$ (acting as $\mathrm{id}_{U_t}$ on $U_t$ and $0$ on all $H^k(C^n, \mathbb{C})$ for $k \neq n$ and all other Künneth/character summands of $H^n(C^n, \mathbb{C})$), and its transpose satisfies $({}^t\Pi_{U_t}^{C^n})_* = (\Pi_{U_{-t}}^{C^n})_*$.*

*Proof.* On $H^0(C) \oplus H^2(C)$, $(\pi_1^C)_* = 0$, hence $(\pi_{1, t}^C)_* = 0$. On $H^1(C, \mathbb{C})_s$ for any residue $s \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$, $(\pi_1^C)_* = \mathrm{id}$ and $({}^t\Gamma_{\delta_c})_* = \delta_c^*$ acts by $\zeta_m^{c s}$, so $(\pi_{1, t}^C)_*|_{H^1(C)_s}$ multiplies by the full cyclic character sum

$$
\lambda_s = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{c=0}^{m-1} \zeta_m^{(s - t) c} = \delta_{s, t \bmod m}.
$$

Since ${}^t\pi_1^C = \pi_1^C$, ${}^t({}^t\Gamma_{\delta_c}) = \Gamma_{\delta_c}$ acts as $\delta_{-c}^*$ (preserving $H^0(C)$ and $H^2(C)$ by $+1$ and scaling $H^1(C)_s$ by $\zeta_m^{-c s}$), we have $({}^t\pi_{1, t}^C)_*|_{H^0 \oplus H^2} = 0$ and $({}^t\pi_{1, t}^C)_*|_{H^1(C)_s} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{c=0}^{m-1} \zeta_m^{-(s + t)c} = \delta_{s, -t \bmod m} = (\pi_{1, -t}^C)_*|_{H^1(C)_s}$, proving (1).

For (2), exterior products of correspondences satisfy $(Z_1 \boxtimes \dots \boxtimes Z_n)_*(\alpha_1 \boxtimes \dots \boxtimes \alpha_n) = (Z_1)_*(\alpha_1) \boxtimes \dots \boxtimes (Z_n)_*(\alpha_n)$ (as all $\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{C}}(Z_j)$ and all fiber dimensions of $C^n$ have even real degree). ### 6.1 Exact Fourier rank and the Dirichlet–Bernoulli saturation criterion

Let $G = (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ ($|G| = \varphi(m)$) and $U = U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) \le G$ with $-1 \notin U$. Recall $w_x = 2 d_x - (n + 2)$ ($x \in G$), satisfying $w_{-x} = -w_x$ and $w_{u x} = w_x$ for all $u \in U$.

**Proposition 6.1 (Fourier rank and Stickelberger saturation criterion).** *Assume $-1 \notin U$.*
1. *Let $(G/U)^\wedge_{\mathrm{odd}} := \{\chi \in \widehat{G} : \chi|_U \equiv 1 \text{ and } \chi(-1) = -1\}$, of cardinality $\frac{\varphi(m)}{2|U|}$. Then*

   $$
   L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{C} = \Bigl\{(v_s)_{s \in G} \in \mathbb{C}^G : \sum_{s \in G} v_s \chi(s) = 0 \;\;\forall\,\chi \in (G/U)^\wedge_{\mathrm{odd}}\Bigr\} = \Bigl\{(v_s) \in \mathbb{C}^G : \sum_{x \in C} v_x = \sum_{y \in -C} v_y \;\;\forall\,C \in G/U\Bigr\}.
   $$

   *In particular, $\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})) = \varphi(m)\bigl(1 - \frac{1}{2|U|}\bigr)$.*
2. *One has $L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a}) \subseteq \ker(q)$, with rational equality $L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})_{\mathbb{Q}} = \ker(q)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ if and only if $\widehat{w}(\chi) := \sum_{x \in G} w_x \bar{\chi}(x) \neq 0$ for every $\chi \in (G/U)^\wedge_{\mathrm{odd}}$.*
3. *Suppose $\gcd(a_i, m) = 1$ for all $0 \le i \le n + 1$. For each $\chi \in (G/U)^\wedge_{\mathrm{odd}}$ induced from a primitive Dirichlet character $\chi_{\mathrm{prim}}$ modulo $f_\chi \mid m$,*

   $$
   \widehat{w}(\chi) = 2\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n+1} \chi(a_i)\Bigr) B_{1, \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}} \prod_{p \mid m,\, p \nmid f_\chi} \bigl(1 - \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}(p)\bigr), \tag{6.1}
   $$

   *where $B_{1, \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}} = \frac{1}{f_\chi}\sum_{c=1}^{f_\chi} c \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}(c) \neq 0$ by Dirichlet's $L(1, \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}) \neq 0$ theorem [25, Theorem 4.9]. In particular, when $m = p^e$ is an odd prime power and $\mathbf{a} = U_{p^e, r} \le (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is any odd-order subgroup ($r \ge 5$ odd, $r \mid p - 1$), **Stickelberger saturation $L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})_{\mathbb{Q}} = \ker(q)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ holds unconditionally**.*

*Proof.* A character $\chi \in \widehat{G}$ annihilates $e_t + e_{-ut}$ for all $t \in G, u \in U$ iff $\chi(-u) = -1$ for all $u \in U$, i.e., $\chi(-1) = -1$ and $\chi|_U \equiv 1$. Since $-1 \notin U$, $|(G/U)^\wedge_{\mathrm{odd}}| = \frac{\varphi(m)}{2|U|}$, and orthogonality to all odd characters of $G/U$ is equivalent to $\sum_{x \in C} v_x = \sum_{y \in -C} v_y$ for all $C \in G/U$, proving (1). Convolution $(q(v))_r = \sum_{s \in G} v_s w_{r s}$ together with $w_{-x} = -w_x$ and $w_{ux} = w_x$ implies $\{\chi : \widehat{w}(\chi) \neq 0\} \subseteq (G/U)^\wedge_{\mathrm{odd}}$, proving (2). For (3), $\chi(-1) = -1$ gives $\widehat{w}(\chi) = \frac{2}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1} \chi(a_i)\sum_{y \in G} y \bar{\chi}(y)$, and the Möbius/Euler-factor identity $\frac{1}{m}\sum_{y \in G} y \bar{\chi}(y) = B_{1, \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}}\prod_{p \mid m, p \nmid f_\chi}(1 - \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}(p))$ [25, Proposition 7.5 and Exercise 7.4] yields (6.1). When $m = p^e$ and $r \ge 5$ is odd dividing $p - 1$, $\chi(-1) = -1 \implies p \mid f_\chi$ (empty Euler product), $\sum_{u \in U_{p^e, r}} \chi(u) = r \neq 0$, and $B_{1, \bar{\chi}_{\mathrm{prim}}} \neq 0$. $\square$

### 6.2 Constructive bipartite non-negative monoid generation and polarization on $B^k$

On abelian self-products $B^k$, exterior powers only realize **non-negative** exponent vectors $(m_s)_{s \in G} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^G \cap \ker(q)$. Because $-1 \notin U$, rational lattice saturation automatically upgrades to **integral non-negative monoid saturation** with zero denominator.

**Proposition 6.2 (Bipartite token-matching and CM-Hodge-free multi-factor polarization).** *Assume $-1 \notin U = U_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$ and $L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})_{\mathbb{Q}} = \ker(q)_{\mathbb{Q}}$.*
1. *Every vector $m = (m_s)_{s \in G} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^G \cap \ker(q)$ is a **non-negative integer** sum of generators $e_t + e_{-ut}$ ($t \in G, u \in U$).*
2. *If a fiber $B = B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}}$ satisfies $\operatorname{Hg}(B)_{\mathrm{der}} = \prod_{\{t, -t\}} \mathrm{SU}(p_t, q_t)$, then Theorem 3(a) and part (1) prove the rational Hodge Conjecture on $B^k$ for every integer $k \ge 1$ without invoking the general CM Hodge theorem.*

*Proof.* For (1), Proposition 6.1(1) gives $M_C := \sum_{x \in C} m_x = \sum_{y \in -C} m_y \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for every $C \in G/U$. Since $-1 \notin U$, $G$ partitions into disjoint opposite coset pairs $C \sqcup (-C)$. Placing $m_x$ tokens at $x \in C$ and $m_y$ tokens at $y \in -C$ and matching the $M_C$ tokens bijectively between $C$ and $-C$ (Check D6), every matched pair $(x, y) \in (t_0 U) \times (-t_0 U)$ satisfies $y = -u x$ for $u = (-y x^{-1}) \in U$, expressing $m = \sum_{\nu=1}^M (e_{t_\nu} + e_{-u_\nu t_\nu})$ with $m_s \ge 0$ preserved integrally.

For (2), by the First Fundamental Theorem for $G_{\mathrm{der}} := \prod_{\{t, -t\}} \mathrm{SL}(V_t)$ and exactness of $G_{\mathrm{der}}$-invariants on the quotient $T^*\bigl(\bigoplus_{s \in G} V_s^{\oplus k}\bigr) \twoheadrightarrow \bigwedge^*\bigl(\bigoplus_{s \in G} V_s^{\oplus k}\bigr) = H^*(B^k, \mathbb{C})$ [1, Lemma 12; 23, §§F.1–F.2], $H^*(B^k, \mathbb{C})^{G_{\mathrm{der}}}$ is generated under cup product $\wedge$ by degree-$2$ Casimir $(1, 1)$-classes in $V_t^{(i)} \wedge V_{-t}^{(j)}$ (whose rational spans lie in $H^2(B^k, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{1, 1}(B^k)$, hence are algebraic by Lefschetz $(1, 1)$) together with determinant wedge-monomial spaces $\bigwedge_{s \in G} (\mathcal{D}_s)^{\wedge m_s}$, where $\mathcal{D}_s := \bigl(\bigwedge^n(V_s^{\oplus k})\bigr)^{\mathrm{SL}(V_s)} \cong \operatorname{Sym}^n(\mathbb{C}^k) \otimes D_s$. Since $T_{\mathrm{MT}}$ preserves the $(1, 1)$-contractions (up to Tate twist) and acts by characters on each $D_s$, $H^{2p}(B^k, \mathbb{C})^{\operatorname{Hg}(B)}$ is spanned by cup products of $(1, 1)$-contractions with determinant wedge-monomials satisfying $(m_s)_{s \in G} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^G \cap \ker(q)$. Writing $m = \sum_{\nu=1}^M (e_{t_\nu} + e_{-u_\nu t_\nu})$ by (1), Theorem 3(a) places $L_m := \bigboxtimes_{\nu=1}^M (D_{t_\nu} \boxtimes D_{-u_\nu t_\nu}) \subset \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{C}})$ on $B^{2M}$. Pulling $L_m$ back along the genuine abelian-variety homomorphisms $\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(1)}, \dots, \boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(2M)}} : B^k \to B^{2M}$, $(b_1, \dots, b_k) \mapsto \bigl(\sum_{j=1}^k \alpha_j^{(1)} b_j, \dots, \sum_{j=1}^k \alpha_j^{(2M)} b_j\bigr)$, maps $z_1 \boxtimes \dots \boxtimes z_{2M}$ to $\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(1)}}^*(z_1) \wedge \dots \wedge \Sigma_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(2M)}}^*(z_{2M}) \in H^{2nM}(B^k, \mathbb{C})$; as $\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(r)} \in \{0, \dots, n\}^k$ varies independently for $1 \le r \le 2M$, polynomial polarization spans $\mathcal{D}_{t_1} \wedge \mathcal{D}_{-u_1 t_1} \wedge \dots \wedge \mathcal{D}_{t_M} \wedge \mathcal{D}_{-u_M t_M} = \bigwedge_{s \in G} (\mathcal{D}_s)^{\wedge m_s}$, and faithful flatness of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q}$ concludes HC on $B^k$. $\square$

### 6.3 Galois-conjugate monodromy across compact factors and proof of Theorem 4

**Lemma 6.3 (Galois-conjugate Rohde monodromy across compact factors).** *Let $\mathbf{a} = (a_0, \dots, a_{n+1}) \in \{1, \dots, m - 1\}^{n+2}$ ($n \ge 3$) satisfy $\gcd(m, \mathbf{a}) = 1$ and $\sum_{i=0}^{n+1} a_i \equiv 0 \pmod m$, and assume there exists at least one $t_\star \in G$ with indefinite signature $1 \le p_{t_\star} \le n - 1$. Then over $\mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}$, $\operatorname{Mon}^\circ(H^1(B_{\mathbf{a}, \bullet}, \mathbb{R})) = \prod_{\{t, -t\} \subset G} \mathrm{SU}(p_t, q_t)$, and for very general $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}$, $\operatorname{Hg}(B_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}})_{\mathrm{der}} = \prod_{\{t, -t\} \subset G} \mathrm{SU}(p_t, q_t)$.*

*Proof.* Since $p_{t_\star}, q_{t_\star} \ge 1$ and $t_\star \neq m/2$, Rohde's Theorem 5.1.1 [21, p. 53] proves that the connected real algebraic monodromy on $H_{t_\star}$ is $\mathrm{SU}(p_{t_\star}, q_{t_\star})$, so $\mathcal{G}_{t_\star}^\circ := \overline{\rho_{t_\star}(\pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}))}^{\mathrm{Zar}, \circ} = \mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{C})$. For **any** $t \in G$ (including definite places $p_t \in \{0, n\}$), $\rho_t = \sigma \circ \rho_{t_\star}$ in the cyclotomic homology basis where $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)/\mathbb{Q})$ sends $\zeta_m \mapsto \zeta_m^{t t_\star^{-1}}$. Extending $\sigma$ to $\widetilde{\sigma} : \mathbb{C} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}$ gives $\mathcal{G}_t^\circ = \widetilde{\sigma}(\mathcal{G}_{t_\star}^\circ) = \mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{C})$, whose real form preserving $h_t$ is $\mathrm{SU}(p_t, q_t)$. Because $n \ge 3$, pairwise independence across $\{t, -t\} \neq \{s, -s\}$ over the **ordered** space $\mathcal{M}_{0, n+2}$ follows from Goursat's lemma and the one-moving Jordan–Pochhammer pseudoreflection eigenvalues [14, Lemmas 6.1–6.2a; 20, Proposition 2.5]: $\mathbf{P}\rho_s \simeq \mathbf{P}\rho_{\pm t}$ without permuting the ordered punctures forces $\zeta_m^{s a_i} = \zeta_m^{\pm t a_i}$ for all $i$, hence $s \equiv \pm t \pmod m$ by $\gcd(m, \mathbf{a}) = 1$. Deligne's containment $\operatorname{Mon}^\circ \subseteq \operatorname{Hg}_{\mathrm{der}}$ [24] yields equality at very general $\mathbf{b}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 4.* Part (a) combines Propositions 6.1(1)–(2) and 6.2(1). Part (b) combines Lemma 6.3 and Proposition 6.2(2) with Proposition 6.1(3) and Checks D4–D5 ($m = 31$: $27/27$; $m = 63$: $33/33$; $m = 121$: $99/99$; $m = 127$: $117/117$; $m = 11$: $9/9$). Part (c) follows from (6.1) and Check D4: at $m = 255 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 17$ with $U = \langle 2 \rangle$, $\chi_{15}(x) = \left(\frac{x}{3}\right)\left(\frac{x}{5}\right)$ satisfies $\chi_{15}(-1) = -1$ and $\chi_{15}(2) = \chi_{15}(17) = +1$, annihilating $1 - \bar{\chi}_{15}(17) = 0$ and giving $\operatorname{rk}(L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}) = 120 < 121 = \dim \ker(q)$. $\square$

## 7. Scope, limitations, and open problems

We record four open problems delimited by Theorems 1–4:

1. **Complete PEL moduli of non-split type-IV Weil varieties.** While [5, 6] resolve all-powers HC across the complete $9$-dimensional PEL moduli of Eisenstein and Gaussian Weil sixfolds (and [7] resolves a compact $3$-dimensional quaternionic $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ sixfold family), Theorem 1(c) works on low-dimensional Fermat-dominated loci ($\dim \mathcal{U}_r - 1 < q^2 - 1$). Extending Weil-class algebraicity to the generic point of the $9$-dimensional non-split type-IV PEL moduli over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ (and general $F_0 \notin \{\mathbb{Q}(i), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})\}$) and to complete $16$- and $25$-dimensional PEL moduli at $q = 4, 5$ remains open.
2. **Even-$q \ge 4$ non-split families over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$.** Proposition 4.1 proves that **no** balanced cyclic-rotation cover of Proposition 2.1 over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ or $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ can have even $q$. Reaching non-split even $q \ge 4$ over these fields requires base curves of positive genus or non-cyclic base automorphisms on $\mathbb{P}^1$ (such as dihedral foldings).
3. **Orthogonal determinant recurrence for $q \ge 6$.** Theorem 1(a) proves fiberwise Weil-space algebraicity for all $q \ge 2$ at $m = 12$ and all odd $q \ge 3$ at $m = 15, 20$, whereas Theorem 1(b) certifies non-split discriminants up to $q = 5$. Establishing non-splitness across all $q \ge 6$ requires proving an exact $F_0$-Hermitian orthogonal decomposition under nodal degeneration of an added orbit $[2, 10]$, $[3, 9]$, $[3]$, or $[4]$.
4. **CM-Hodge-free explicit joins at $m = 255$ and unbalanced GHC.** At $m = 255$ (Theorem 4(c)), while the $1$-dimensional defect in $\ker(q) / L_{\mathrm{Ferm}}(\mathbf{a})$ is algebraic under [15]'s companion CM-Hodge supply, constructing it **explicitly without the general CM Hodge theorem** requires multi-factor Shioda joins across $X_{255}^6$ beyond single coordinate-permutation graphs; likewise, Grothendieck's GHC on unbalanced powers possessing compact $\mathrm{SU}(n, 0)$ factors remains open unconditionally.
