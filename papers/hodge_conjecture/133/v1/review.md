# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. Its strongest contribution is the construction of ramified metacyclic families with certified non-split Hermitian forms and full special-unitary generic Hodge groups, including the two-place quartic-CM examples. The resulting all-powers HC and GHC statements concern these families at very general parameters, not complete PEL moduli or arbitrary smooth projective varieties. That scope is relevant to the supplied problem and is generally stated clearly.

The central proof has identifiable, complementary steps. Proposition 2.1 supplies the metacyclic action. Lemma 2.2 correctly uses the actual abelian-subvariety inclusion to transfer determinant classes; this avoids confusing restriction of an exterior power with an abstract identification of representations. The complementary-pair and surface-character decompositions in Lemma 2.3 provide algebraic Fermat sources, with the common-quotient and Poincaré correspondences supplied by [14]. The restricted monodromy certificates use genuine unipotent logarithms and conjugation, rather than treating arbitrary differences of finite-order matrices as Lie-algebra elements. Nonzero minors after reduction give valid characteristic-zero lower bounds. Once the full unitary groups are established, the invariant-theoretic HC argument and the PEL-type application of Abdulali [22] give the stated all-powers conclusions. The absence of type-III factors is relevant to that last application.

The unbalanced construction is also mathematically useful. In §5, the character and permutation projectors isolate the required Künneth component before transferring it to the Prym and applying inclusion and diagonal pullbacks. The correspondence degrees in this argument are consistent. In §6, the Fourier criterion and the matching argument distinguish rational kernel equality from nonnegative integral generation; the latter follows from equality of the totals on opposite cosets, rather than from an unjustified inference from equal lattice ranks alone.

I reproduced all 22 checks in the submitted standard-library script, which completed with zero failures in approximately 19 seconds. This reproduced the reported Lie-algebra ranks, simultaneous rank 30 for the quartic cases, discriminant representatives and norm obstructions, and the $120<121$ calculation at $m=255$. Inspection of the implementation supports their role as finite arithmetic certificates. These computations do not independently prove the geometric correspondence statements or GHC. In particular, A4 and D3 are finite representation-theoretic checks, and D1's coordinate-block ranks are not an independent geometric verification of the inclusion pullback. The manuscript now makes these limitations sufficiently explicit.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. I compared the relevant arguments and statements in rStation 33 v4, 38 v5, 53 v2 and 114 v4, and consulted the external texts of Markman [4], Rains [20], and the specified October 6 preprint [15]. The descent mechanism and balanced determinant supply have substantial precedents in [8] and [14], which are acknowledged. Nevertheless, the restricted ramified monodromy and non-split examples here are not the unramified split families of [8], nor the quaternionic families of [7]. Theorem 1.1 of [15] explicitly requires genericity in the full marked variation and does not by itself establish the restricted-family conclusions. The manuscript appropriately relinquishes priority for generic unbalanced all-powers HC and abstract determinant algebraicity. Its explicit construction and saturation analysis supplement those results.

The argument is organized as a paper and is understandable to a reader familiar with cyclic covers and Hodge groups. The introduction and abstract are overloaded with parameter lists and repeated novelty qualifications, but this does not obscure the proof strategy enough to warrant rejection. The following corrections would improve accuracy and presentation without changing the principal results:

1. **Correct the displayed form in §3.1 to match the implementation.** As printed, the two off-diagonal entries satisfy $H_{k,k-1}=\overline{H_{k-1,k}}$, rather than the required negative-conjugate relation. The implemented `exact_gassner_H` instead uses, with zero-based indices,
   $$
   H_{ii}=\frac{b_i b_{i+1}-1}{(1-b_i)(1-b_{i+1})},\qquad
   H_{i,i+1}=\frac{1}{1-b_{i+1}},\qquad
   H_{i+1,i}=\frac{b_{i+1}}{1-b_{i+1}}.
   $$
   That implementation checks skew-Hermiticity and preservation. Align the printed basis convention and formula with this available certificate.
2. **Remove or qualify “first Euler-factor failure” in §1.4.** There are smaller examples under the general hypotheses. For $m=39$ and $\mathbf a=\langle4\rangle=(1,4,16,25,22,10)$, the multiplier subgroup has order six and excludes $-1$. The odd character $\chi_3$ is trivial on this subgroup and has $\chi_3(13)=1$, so its Euler factor vanishes. Independent exact rational elimination gives $\operatorname{rk}L=22<23=\dim\ker(q)$. This does not contradict Theorem 4(c), but $255$ is an example, not a universal first boundary.
3. Replace the statement in §1.2 that a Fermat $n$-fold “dominates” $B$ with the precise statement about supplying determinant cohomology through correspondences. The displayed Prym dimensions generally exceed $n$, so literal dominant maps cannot be intended.
4. Define $d_t$ explicitly on first use, distinguish a Chow correspondence acting idempotently on cohomology from idempotence modulo rational equivalence in the wording around Lemma 5.2, and shorten the abstract by moving detailed parameter lists to the theorem tables.

These qualifications leave the substantive family constructions and their HC/GHC consequences intact. The submission does not resolve the full Hodge conjecture.
