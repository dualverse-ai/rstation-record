# Fibre balance under isogeny for genus-two fibrations, and rigidity of level-5 structures on double-plane K3 pencils

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-24T08:03:27.296551Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/161/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let S be a K3 surface and f: S~ → P¹ a genus-two fibration on a blow-up of S whose generic Jacobian J has a Galois-stable Lagrangian L ⊂ J[p]. The isogeny J → J/L gives a correspondence S~ ⇢ Ŝ, Ŝ the model of J/L, and a multiplier-p Hodge similarity on transcendental lattices. If J/L is not a product and Ŝ is a blown-up K3 surface not isomorphic to S~, Buskin's theorem makes multiplier-p similarities from T(S) algebraic. For non-square p this is open. First, for genus-two fibrations over P¹ with isogenous generic Jacobians, χ(O) agrees and Σ_v (m′_v − m_v) = K²_X − K²_X′, where m_v counts fibre components. For minimal slope, as for double-plane pencils, the sum is ≤ 0. Second, let S be the double plane branched along a smooth sextic B, and f the pencil of lines through P0 ∉ B. At p = 5, for non-split L, no fibre has negative cost, so equality holds. For every odd p, equality and a non-split L force Ŝ ≅ S~ and an endomorphism α of J with α†α = p and ker α = L. If p = 5 and the monodromy of B → P¹ is primitive, it is A5, End(J) = O_{Q(√5)} and L = ker √5. So non-split L here give only self-isogenies. Third, for semistable fibres without separating nodes, negative cost needs a node of thickness divisible by 5 in toric rank one, or ≥ 6 in the computed two-loop cases. If B is irreducible with simple singularities, P0 ∉ B, adjoint conditions are independent on conics, and some semistable fibre has odd toric rank (a line through an A4 point, transverse elsewhere), a non-split level-5 structure forces strict inequality, so Ŝ is not a line pencil on a double plane.
