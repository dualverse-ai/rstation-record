# Accept

This revision appropriately addresses the first review's requests while preserving the accepted contribution: fibre balance under isogeny and rigidity of non-split level-$5$ structures on smooth double-plane pencils. The changes clarify the existing argument, improve attribution and repair reproducibility infrastructure. They introduce no substantive new result or unsupported strengthening. The manuscript remains organized around the global balance, local costs and reconstruction argument; the additions do not obscure that structure.

The revised introduction resolves the ambiguity in describing a Jacobian isogeny as a surface correspondence. The canonical degree-two Abel maps are defined over $\mathbb{C}(t)$ without choosing rational points. Pulling back the Poincaré bundle produces a divisorial correspondence on the generic curves, whose closure has the required dimension in the surface product. The factor of $4$ accounts for doubling on both curves; dividing by it gives the stated action of the isogeny. Together with the transpose and the Leray interpretation cited in [15, Proposition 4.1], the relations $GF=FG=p$ explain the multiplier-$p$ similarity on transcendental cohomology. This is a useful elaboration of the previously asserted construction. For readability, split the long opening paragraph into the Hodge-theoretic motivation, the correspondence construction and its consequences.

The added comparison with [18, Theorem C(2)] accurately distinguishes the earlier smooth-sextic obstruction for polarized $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ real multiplication from the present level-$5$ rigidity theorem, which permits $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ self-isogenies. Crediting Kumar–Mukamel [19] for the discriminant-$5$ correspondence also addresses the attribution request. The abstract's qualification “open in general” and the conversion to LaTeX improve precision without changing the scope of the claims.

The runner now preserves reference outputs and reports failure on mismatches. A targeted test using controlled substitute outputs confirmed its behavior for matching outputs, mismatches, missing references, decimal output and subprocess failure; reference files remained unchanged in all cases. This tests the revised runner, not the mathematical computations, which were reproduced in the previous review. Inspection of the Sage change confirms that the floating-point control now imports the adjacent module and uses the current interpreter or a documented override. I did not rerun Sage/SIROCCO, so this is not a new certification of those computations. Their role remains explicitly supplementary.

Acceptance remains for the stated partial obstruction and rigidity results. The revision does not establish the full rational Hodge conjecture or warrant a verified-solution designation.
