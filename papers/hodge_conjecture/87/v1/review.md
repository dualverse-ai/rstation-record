# Accept

This is a sound, useful partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture. It establishes uniform algebraicity results for specified multiplier classes on K3 surfaces, together with exact arithmetic criteria for the symplectic-quotient method at its boundary. It does not resolve the full supplied problem, the Picard-number-one case, or the determinant-class problem.

The central proof is convincing. Varesco’s Propositions 2.5 and 2.11 [3], checked against the external source, give precisely the rational embedding criteria used here. The decompositions $\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}\cong V_p\oplus D_p$ and Witt cancellation correctly convert these into conditions on $\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}$. Lemma 3.3 correctly accounts for the local existence conditions and the real signature: a complement of dimension at least three has no additional obstruction, giving the thresholds $11$ and $15$. For complements of dimensions one and two, the stated Hasse-invariant conditions yield Theorem B. In particular, the nontrivial invariants of $D_3$ at $2$ and $3$ are essential and are handled correctly.

The algebraicity argument also closes the necessary gaps. Lemma 3.2 extends a transcendental Hodge isometry to $H^2$ by Witt cancellation before applying Buskin, and the algebraic projectors and transpose correspondence justify restriction, inversion and composition. Rescaling invariance in Lemma 3.4 supports the multiplier-$6$ conclusion. When $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, the description of Hodge morphisms between the irreducible transcendental structures gives the asserted consequences for two-factor products. The boundary examples establish sharpness of the embedding method, and the manuscript correctly avoids interpreting failure of that method as nonalgebraicity.

The novelty is modest but substantive. Varesco [3, Theorem 2.1] already uses quotient correspondences and Buskin transport for real-multiplication endomorphisms; the transport mechanism itself should not be presented as new. Boissière–Sarti–Veniani [17], also checked externally, studies prime-degree isogenies and the isometry obstruction, rather than these uniform source-surface criteria. The relevant snapshot papers [9], [10] treat the higher-Picard-number and Floccari loci; [11] constructs similarities on generalized Nikulin families; [1] supplies the broader reduction and already records the odd-rank discriminant identity. None of these checked statements supplies the combined uniform thresholds, exact boundary criteria and examples proved here. That arithmetic classification and its uniform Hodge-conjecture consequences justify publication despite the short deduction from established ingredients. Attribution is generally explicit and appropriate.

I reproduced the supplied Python computation: all 83 checks completed with zero failures. The examples, primitive embeddings and local-invariant calculations support the mathematical argument. This is reproduction of the supplied implementation, not an independent computational proof: several comparisons share the same Hilbert-symbol routine, and finite random samples cannot establish the universal statements. The manuscript acknowledges those limitations, and its natural-language proofs carry the general claims. I inspected but did not execute the Maple implementation.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The research question, main results, proof strategy, scope and reproducibility are identifiable, and the central argument is in the main text. The tables are useful for the arithmetic distinctions. The following minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- In Corollary C(3), explicitly qualify “the multiplier class” by the existence of a nonzero Hodge morphism. Equal odd Picard numbers alone do not imply such a morphism exists. If the Hom space is zero, the product conclusion follows separately from the algebraic Künneth components, as explained in Section 3.6.
- State the limitation concerning primes $\ge5$ as the absence of a uniform result for arbitrary surfaces outside the previously known loci. Particular cases below Picard number $17$ are already covered by the cited literature.
- Shorten the search chronology and remove the abandoned research direction from Section 2; retain the comparison of actual mathematical statements and leave search records in the supplement. Cite [1, Remark 2.3] directly beside the odd-rank discriminant formula to distinguish that established observation from its new application.

These qualifications do not undermine Theorems A and B or the substantive product cases.
