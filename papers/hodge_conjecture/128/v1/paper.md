# Explicit Witt-Index-One Real-Multiplication K3 Curves, Galois Obstructions, and Non-Square-Type Twisted-$D_4$ Correspondences

## 1. Introduction and main results

### 1.1 Context and two open problems on real-multiplication K3 families

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface, $\rho(S) = \operatorname{rk} \operatorname{NS}(S)$ its geometric Picard number, $T(S) = \operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}^\perp \subset H^2(S, \mathbb{Q})$ its rational transcendental lattice, and $E(S) = \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S))$ its Hodge endomorphism field, which by Zarhin's theorem [1] is either a totally real field or a CM field. By Varesco's Tannakian reduction [2, Theorem 2.16], the rational Hodge conjecture (HC) for all powers $S^k$ ($k \ge 1$) is known when $E(S)$ is CM [2, 3], and when $E(S)$ is totally real of degree $d = [E(S) : \mathbb{Q}]$ with $m = \dim_{E(S)} T(S)$ it is equivalent to the algebraicity of the $E(S)$-action on $S \times S$ together with the determinant classes $\bigwedge^m_{E(S)} T(S) \subset H^{2m}(S^m, \mathbb{Q})$.

Two concrete geometric obstacles have stood open in recent work on explicit real-multiplication (RM) K3 families over $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$:

1. **Explicit Witt-index-one RM curves and $\rho = 16$ members over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (rStation 97 [4, §8]).** In transcendental rank $6$ ($\rho = 16$, $d = 2$, $m = 3$), Floccari's hyper-Kummer theorem [5, Theorem 5.11; 6, Theorem K] proves HC for all powers whenever $T(S)$ embeds isometrically into $(U^3 \oplus \langle -k\rangle)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($k > 0$), which in rank $6$ holds if and only if $T(S)$ has rational Witt index $2$ [7, 8]. The generic, arithmetic frontier of rank $6$ is **rational Witt index $1$**, where Floccari's embedding fails directly on $S$ and can only be reached by transporting cycles along an algebraic *twisted Hodge similarity* $f \colon T(S) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S')$ satisfying $f^t f = \mu \in F_{>0} \setminus \mathbb{Q}^\times F^{\times 2}$ [4, 9, 10]. For $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, rStation 95 [11] exhibited a single rigid $\rho = 16$ Witt-index-one surface, and rStation 97 [4, Theorem B] introduced the two-parameter Dickson-$D_8$ family
   $$S_{b,c} \colon\quad y^2 = x^3 - 3x + b D_8(s) + c, \qquad (b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ := \bigl\{(b, c) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : b \ne 0,\ \pm 2b + c \notin \{2, -2\}\bigr\}, \tag{1.1}$$
   where $D_n(u + u^{-1}) = u^n + u^{-n}$ ($D_4(w) = w^4 - 4w^2 + 2$, $D_8(s) = D_4(s^2 - 2) = s^8 - 8s^6 + 20s^4 - 16s^2 + 2$). Over $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, $\operatorname{NS}(S_{b,c})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ contains a visible lattice $N_{\mathrm{vis}}$ of rank $14$ whose orthogonal complement $L = N_{\mathrm{vis}}^\perp \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U^2 \oplus \langle -1, -2, -3, -6\rangle$ has $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} L \equiv 1$ and unique finite bad prime $p_0 = 2$ ($c_2(L) = -1$), and the $D_8$ dihedral mechanism supplies algebraic correspondences $s \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S_{b,c}))$ ($s^2 = 2$) and $f \colon T(S_{b,c}) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S_{-b,c})$ ($f^t f = 2 + s$, $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(2 + \sqrt{2}) = 2 \notin \mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}$), proving HC for all products $S_{b,c}^m \times S_{-b,c}^n$ whenever $\rho(S_{b,c}) \ge 16$ [4, Theorem B(3)]. However, as recorded in [4, §8 ("Non-explicit curves")], while Noether–Lefschetz theory guarantees a countable dense union of Witt-index-one curves in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, **no explicit Witt-index-one curve and no explicit $\rho = 16$ Witt-index-one member in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ had been written down**: every explicit algebraic locus examined in $\overline{\mathcal{B}^\circ}$ (the boundary lines $\pm 2b + c = \pm 2$ and configuration A of [11]) collapsed to Witt index $2$ on *both* $S_{b,c}$ and $S_{-b,c}$, in sharp contrast with $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ where $\mathbb{Q}$-rational curves and members already attain Witt index $1$ [4, Theorem C; 9].

2. **Explicit non-CM K3 equations and partner correspondences in non-square-type RM of rank $8$ (rStation 118 [12, §§7, 10]).** At $\rho = 14$ with $E(S)$ totally real quadratic and $m = \dim_{E(S)} T(S) = 4$, the unique $E(S)$-bilinear form $\varphi_E \colon T(S) \times T(S) \to E(S)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \circ \varphi_E = (-, -)_{H^2}$ has totally positive discriminant $\delta = \operatorname{disc} \varphi_E \in E(S)_{>0} / E(S)^{\times 2}$. All previously constructed non-CM RM K3 families in $m = 4$—the Dickson and curve-product families and ten rational RM rows of rStation 65, 98, 111, 119, and 120 [13–17] as well as the Dickson-$D_8$ and twisted-$D_5$ families of [4]—have **square type** ($\delta \in E^{\times 2}$). As proved in rStation 118 [12, §1 and Proposition 7.3], any rational domination by a product of independent curve families $C_t \times C'_u \dashrightarrow S_{t,u}$ forces $\delta \in E^{\times 2}$, and abelian covers of $\mathbb{P}^1$ cannot realize the two-dimensional variation of a *non-square-type* ($\delta \notin E^{\times 2}$) Deligne model. Section 10 of [12] poses the full two-dimensional problem of constructing a two-parameter non-square-type K3 family together with algebraic correspondences to its Deligne models (cycle $(\Gamma)$), while [12, §7] notes more basically that outside isolated CM points ($X_{13}, X_{17}$), non-square-type RM K3 families were known only abstractly via period-domain surjectivity (*"Such families exist abstractly, but we have no equations"*). Moreover, for a pair $(X_+, X'_+)$ linked by an $E$-linear twisted similarity $f^t f = \mu \in E_{>0}$ in non-square type, the spin norm shifts rational Hasse invariants across distinct primes, precluding **all** rational Hodge similarities $T(X_+)(\lambda) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} T(X'_+)$ ($\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$) and placing mixed products $X_+^a \times X'^b_+$ beyond Buskin's theorem [18] and finite-symplectic similarity chains (rStation 90 [19, Theorem D(2)]).

### 1.2 Main results

This paper resolves the first problem in full and, toward the second, constructs an explicit non-isotrivial one-parameter non-square-type RM K3 family over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ with closed-form RM and partner correspondences and mixed-product HC for $a + b \le 3$ (while leaving two-dimensional moduli families and cycle $(\Gamma)$ of [12, §10] open).

Our first theorem descends the $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-action on $S_{b,c}$ explicitly to its symplectic Nikulin quotient $Z_{b,c}$—reducing the search for $F$-stable Noether–Lefschetz planes in $L(S_{b,c})$ to single Mordell–Weil sections on $Z_{b,c}$—and proves an exact Galois anti-commutativity criterion explaining why **no** $\mathbb{Q}$-rational member of $S_{b,c}$ with $\rho(S_{b,c, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) = 16$ and $\operatorname{NS}(S_{b,c, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\mathbb{Q}} = \operatorname{NS}(S_{b,c})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (or more generally, why no $\mathbb{Q}$-rational $F$-stable negative-definite plane $N \subset L(S_{b,c})$ on which the base-reversal involution $\iota_{\mathrm{even}} \colon s \mapsto -s$ acts—automatically as $-\mathrm{id}_N$) can ever produce Witt index $1$ for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, while the exact same mechanism *does* produce Witt index $1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.

**Theorem 1 (Explicit circle RM correspondence on $Z_{b,c}$ and exact Galois obstruction over $\mathbb{Q}$).** *Let $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$, and let $\iota_{\mathrm{odd}}, \iota_{\mathrm{even}} \in \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}}(S_{b,c})$ be the commuting involutions $\iota_{\mathrm{odd}}(s, x, y) = (-s, x, -y)$ and $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}(s, x, y) = (-s, x, y)$.*

*(a) (Symplectic quotient and explicit circle correspondence.) The minimal resolution of $S_{b,c} / \langle \iota_{\mathrm{odd}}\rangle$ is the elliptic K3 surface*
$$Z_{b,c} \colon\quad (w + 2) y_Z^2 = x^3 - 3x + b D_4(w) + c \tag{1.2}$$
*with rational quotient map $q_Z \colon S_{b,c} \dashrightarrow Z_{b,c}$ given by $(w, x, y_Z) = (s^2 - 2,\, x,\, y/s)$, singular fibres $\mathrm{II}^*$ at $w = \infty$, $I_0^*$ at $w = -2$, and $8\, I_1$, and visible lattice $N_{\mathrm{vis}}(Z_{b,c})_{\mathbb{Q}} = U \oplus E_8(-1) \oplus D_4(-1)$ of rank $14$. Pullback $q_Z^*$ induces a rational Hodge similarity $L(Z_{b,c})(2) \xrightarrow{\sim} L(S_{b,c})$ ($L(Z_{b,c}) := N_{\mathrm{vis}}(Z_{b,c})^\perp \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U^2 \oplus D_4(-1)$). Moreover, the algebraic correspondence $C_R \subset Z_{b,c} \times Z_{b,c}$ defined over $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ by*
$$w^2 + w'^2 = 4, \qquad x' = x, \qquad y'_Z = \frac{w + w' + 2}{\sqrt{2}(w' + 2)}\, y_Z \tag{1.3}$$
*preserves (1.2), satisfies $(C_R)^* \omega_{Z_{b,c}} = +\sqrt{2}\,\omega_{Z_{b,c}}$ on $\omega_{Z_{b,c}} = \frac{dx \wedge dw}{(w + 2)y_Z}$, and induces via $r_Z := \Pi_{L(Z_{b,c})} \circ [C_R]_*|_{L(Z_{b,c})}$ a self-adjoint operator satisfying $r_Z^2 = 2\,\mathrm{id}_{L(Z_{b,c})}$ intertwined by $q_Z^*$ with the $D_8$ real-multiplication operator $s$ on $L(S_{b,c})$.*

*(b) (Action of $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}$ and Galois anti-commutativity obstruction.) For every $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$, $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}^*|_{L(S_{b,c})} = -\mathrm{id}_{L(S_{b,c})}$, whereas the algebraic cycle defining $s \in \operatorname{End}(L(S_{b,c}))$ over $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ ($d = 2$ on $S_{b,c}$, or $d = 5$ on the twisted-$D_5$ family $X_{b,c}$ of [4, Theorem C]) has nontrivial Galois conjugate $\bar{s} = -s$. Consequently, for any $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ(\mathbb{Q})$ with $\rho(S_{b,c, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) = 16$ such that the negative-definite $F$-stable algebraic plane $N = L \cap \operatorname{NS}(S_{b,c, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -\alpha\rangle$ ($\alpha \in F_{>0}$) is $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$-stable—and in fact whenever $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/M)$ acts non-trivially on $F M / M$ for some subfield $M \subset \mathbb{R}$ over which $(b, c)$ is defined, since any $\tilde{\sigma} \in \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/M)$ lifting the generator of $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$ acts on $N$ by a rational orthogonal isometry anti-commuting with $s|_N$ ($\tilde{\sigma} s \tilde{\sigma}^{-1} = -s$)—one necessarily has*
$$N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \in \{1, d\}\cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}. \tag{1.4}$$
*Conversely, an anti-commuting rational orthogonal isometry of $\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -\alpha\rangle$ exists if and only if (1.4) holds.*

*(c) ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ vs. $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ arithmetic dichotomy.) By (1.4) and $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -\alpha\rangle) \equiv d\, N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}}$, every $\mathbb{Q}$-rational parameter $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ(\mathbb{Q})$ with $\rho = 16$ has $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} T \in \{1, d\} \cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}$:*
- *For $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ ($S_{b,c}$, $p_0 = 2$), Witt index $1$ on $S_{b,c}$ or $S_{-b,c}$ requires $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} T \in \{-1, -2\}\cdot \mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}$; since $-1, -2 \notin \mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}$, **every** $\mathbb{Q}$-rational $\rho = 16$ member $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ(\mathbb{Q})$ (and every $\mathbb{Q}$-rational Noether–Lefschetz curve in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ whose generic $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(t))$-action does not split $F$) forces **both** $T(S_{b,c})$ and $T(S_{-b,c})$ to have rational Witt index $2$.*
- *For $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ ($X_{b,c}$, $p_0 = 5$), Witt index $1$ holds on $X_{b,c}$ iff $-\det_{\mathbb{Q}} T \in \mathbb{Q}_5^{\times 2}$; since $-1 \equiv 2^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_5^{\times 2}$, the Galois-admissible class $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} T \equiv 1$ satisfies $-\det_{\mathbb{Q}} T \in \mathbb{Q}_5^{\times 2}$, explaining why $\mathbb{Q}$-rational Witt-index-one curves and surfaces exist for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ [4, 9] while Witt-index-one loci for $S_{b,c}$ can exist **only** over extensions splitting $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.*

Guided by Theorem 1, we solve the exact Shioda-height-$3$ Weierstrass elimination system on $Z_{A,B,C}$, discovering that its eliminant factors over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ into one $\mathbb{Q}$-rational Witt-index-two component ($\alpha = 3$, $N(\alpha) = 9$) and a Galois-conjugate pair of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-rational components with $\alpha = 3 \mp \sqrt{2}$ ($N(\alpha) = 7 \equiv -1 \pmod{8}$). Applying $f \colon S_{-b,c} \vdash S_{b,c}$ resolves [4, §8]:

**Theorem 2 (Explicit genus-zero Witt-index-one curve in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ and explicit $\rho = 16$ member).** *Over $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, define the quadratic and cubic polynomials*
$$A_q(t) = \left(-512 + \frac{512}{3}\sqrt{2}\right) + \left(640 - \frac{2432}{3}\sqrt{2}\right)t + \left(1104 - \frac{2944}{3}\sqrt{2}\right)t^2, \tag{1.5}$$
$$C_q(t) = 128 + \frac{-19840 + 32768\sqrt{2}}{27}\,t + \frac{1002880 - 634880\sqrt{2}}{27}\,t^2 + \frac{694400 - 460288\sqrt{2}}{27}\,t^3. \tag{1.6}$$
*The plane conic $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{w1}} \colon \mu_0^2 = -\frac{1}{3} A_q(t)$ has the $F$-rational base point $(t_0, \mu_{0,\mathrm{base}}) = \bigl(-\frac{1}{4},\, 13 - 4\sqrt{2}\bigr)$ and is rationally parametrized over $F(v)$ by*
$$t(v) = -\frac{1}{4} + \frac{\alpha_1 - 2(13 - 4\sqrt{2})v}{v^2 - \alpha_2}, \qquad \mu_0(v) = (13 - 4\sqrt{2}) + v\left(t(v) + \frac{1}{4}\right), \tag{1.7}$$
*where $\alpha_1 = -\frac{256 - 960\sqrt{2}}{9}$ and $\alpha_2 = -368 + \frac{2944}{9}\sqrt{2}$. Set*
$$b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v) := \frac{64}{t(v)\,\mu_0(v)^3}, \qquad c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v) := \frac{\bigl(t(v) + 1\bigr)\,C_q(t(v))}{t(v)\,\mu_0(v)^3} \quad \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})(v). \tag{1.8}$$

*(a) (Non-isotrivial Witt-index-one family.) Outside the finitely many values of $v$ where $t(v) \in \{0, -1\}$, $\mu_0(v) = 0$, or $\pm 2 b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v) + c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v) = \pm 2$, the pair $(b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v))$ traces a non-isotrivial rational curve in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ such that:*
- *on $S_{-b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v),\, c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)}$, $L(S_{-b_{\mathrm{w1}}, c_{\mathrm{w1}}})$ contains an algebraic $F$-stable plane $N_v \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -(3 - \sqrt{2})\rangle$ of determinant $56 \equiv 14 \equiv -2 \pmod{\mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}}$, with orthogonal complement $N_v^\perp \cap L \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U \oplus \langle -1, -1, 1, -14\rangle$ of rational Witt index $2$;*
- *on the partner $S_{b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v),\, c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)}$, the transported algebraic plane $N'_v = f(N_v) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -(4 + \sqrt{2})\rangle$ has determinant $112 \equiv 7 \equiv -1 \pmod{\mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}}$ and orthogonal complement*
  $$M_{\mathrm{w1}, v} := N'^\perp_v \cap L(S_{b_{\mathrm{w1}}, c_{\mathrm{w1}}}) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U \oplus \langle -1, -1, -1, 7\rangle$$
  *of **rational Witt index $1$** ($\langle -1, -1, -1, 7\rangle$ is $2$-adically anisotropic).*

*(b) (Unconditional all-powers Hodge conjecture.) For **every** specialization $v \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $(b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$, $\rho\bigl(S_{b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigr) \ge 16$ and the rational Hodge conjecture holds in all codimensions on every product $S_{b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)}^m \times S_{-b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)}^n$ ($m, n \ge 0$), while for every $v$ outside a countable subset, $\rho = 16$, $E(S_{b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, and $T(S_{b_{\mathrm{w1}}(v), c_{\mathrm{w1}}(v)}) = M_{\mathrm{w1}, v}$ has rational Witt index $1$.*

*(c) (Explicit $\rho = 16$ Witt-index-one member.) At $t_\star = +\frac{1}{4}$, $\mu_{0,\star} = \frac{7 + 20\sqrt{2}}{3}$ (corresponding to $v_\star = -\frac{32}{3} + \frac{64}{3}\sqrt{2}$), the explicit $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-rational surface $S_{b_\star, c_\star}$ with*
$$b_\star = \frac{-118492416 + 130913280\sqrt{2}}{423564751}, \qquad c_\star = \frac{-13499700590 + 10136318400\sqrt{2}}{423564751} \qquad \bigl(423564751 = 751^3\bigr) \tag{1.9}$$
*lies in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, has good reduction at both primes $\mathfrak{p}_{17,1}, \mathfrak{p}_{17,2}$ of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ above $17$, and satisfies unconditionally*
$$\rho\bigl(S_{b_\star, c_\star, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigr) = 16, \qquad E(S_{b_\star, c_\star}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}), \qquad T(S_{b_\star, c_\star}) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U \oplus \langle -1, -1, -1, 7\rangle \text{ (rational Witt index } 1\text{)},$$
*with the rational Hodge conjecture holding for all powers $S_{b_\star, c_\star}^k$ ($k \ge 1$).*

We next construct explicit non-CM equations and correspondences in non-square-type RM of transcendental rank $8$. In [9, 11, 4], the $D_n$ dihedral correspondence mechanism required an auxiliary intermediate quotient $W / \langle \sigma\rangle$ to have geometric genus $p_g = 0$ so that $\langle \sigma\rangle$-invariant cohomology is purely algebraic; for standard untwisted $D_8$ pullbacks of rational elliptic surfaces, this requirement restricted the admissible fibre lists so severely that all resulting rank-$8$ models had square discriminant [11, Table 3]. Our third theorem introduces a **Twisted-$D_4$ dihedral mechanism** whose central quotient $W / \langle \nu\rangle = \mathbb{P}^1_u \times \mathbb{P}^1_x$ is *universally rational* for **every** cubic polynomial $g(x, t)$, eliminating the $p_g = 0$ hypothesis and producing closed-form curve correspondences for both $r$ ($r^2 = 2$) and $f$ ($f^t f = 2 + r$).

**Theorem 3 (Universal Twisted-$D_4$ dihedral mechanism and closed-form correspondences).** *Let $g(x, t) = x^3 + A(t)x + B(t) \in K[x, t]$ be any polynomial such that the minimal resolutions $X_+$ and $X'_+$ of the affine Weierstrass surfaces*
$$X_+ \colon\ y^2 = (s + 2)\,g\bigl(x, D_4(s)\bigr), \qquad X'_+ \colon\ y'^2 = (s' + 2)\,g\bigl(x, -D_4(s')\bigr) \tag{1.10}$$
*are smooth projective K3 surfaces over a subfield $K \subset \mathbb{C}$.*

*(a) (Universal $D_8$ cover without $p_g = 0$ side conditions.) Let $W$ be a $D_8$-equivariant smooth projective model of $z^2 = u\,g(x, u^4 + u^{-4})$, equipped with the faithful $D_8$-action generated over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ by*
$$\alpha(u, x, z) = (i u,\, x,\, \zeta_8 z), \qquad \tau_1(u, x, z) = (u^{-1},\, x,\, z u^{-1}), \qquad \tau_2 := \alpha\tau_1(u, x, z) = (i u^{-1},\, x,\, \zeta_8 z u^{-1}),$$
*with central involution $\nu := \alpha^4(u, x, z) = (u, x, -z)$. Because $W / \langle \nu\rangle$ is birational to $\mathbb{P}^1_u \times \mathbb{P}^1_x$, one has $H^{2,0}(W)^{\nu = +1} = 0$ **universally**, so every $\nu$-invariant class in $H^2(W, \mathbb{Q})$ is algebraic by Lefschetz $(1,1)$, while on $V_- := H^2(W, \mathbb{Q})^{\nu = -1}$ the group-ring operators satisfy $(\alpha + \alpha^{-1})^2 = 2$ and $(1 + \tau_1)(1 + \tau_2)(1 + \tau_1) = (2 + \alpha + \alpha^{-1})(1 + \tau_1)$.*

*(b) (Explicit correspondences $C_r$ and $C_f$.) The rational quotients $W / \langle \tau_1\rangle \simeq X_+$ and $W / \langle \tau_2\rangle \simeq X'_+$ (over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})$) descend the $D_8$-correspondences explicitly to:*
1. *the circle correspondence $C_r \subset X_+ \times X_+$ defined over $K(\sqrt{2})$ by*
   $$s^2 + s'^2 = 4, \qquad x' = x, \qquad y' = \frac{2 + s + s'}{\sqrt{2}(s + 2)}\,y, \tag{1.11}$$
   *which satisfies $(C_r)^* \omega_{X_+} = +\sqrt{2}\,\omega_{X_+}$ on $\omega_{X_+} = \frac{dx \wedge ds}{y}$ and induces $r \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X_+))$ with $r^2 = 2$; and*
2. *the conic correspondence $C_f \subset X_+ \times X'_+$ defined over $K(\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}})$ by*
   $$s^2 + s'^2 - \sqrt{2}\,s s' = 2, \qquad x' = x, \qquad y' = \frac{(2 + \sqrt{2}) + s + s'}{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}(s + 2)}\,y, \tag{1.12}$$
   *along which $D_4(s') = -D_4(s)$ identically, $(C_f)^* \omega_{X'_+} = +\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}\,\omega_{X_+}$, and which induces an $E$-linear Hodge isomorphism $f \colon T(X_+) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(X'_+)$ satisfying $f^t f = 2 + r$ with algebraic inverse $f^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}(2 - r) f^t$.*

Applying Theorem 3 to the rational elliptic configuration $(\mathrm{III}^*, I_0, \mathrm{II}; I_1)$ yields an explicit one-parameter non-CM K3 family of non-square-type real multiplication ($d = 2$, $m = 4$, $\delta \equiv 3 \notin \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})^{\times 2}$, outer spin field $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$), establishes the absence of rational similarities between $X_+$ and $X'_+$, proves the unconditional Hodge conjecture on all mixed products $X_+^a \times X'^b_+$ with $a + b \le 3$, and proves non-CM type unconditionally at the concrete $\mathbb{Q}$-rational specialization $(a_1, b_1) = (-2, 1)$.

**Theorem 4 (Explicit non-square-type RM K3 family, absence of rational similarities, and mixed-product HC).** *For $(a_1, b_1) \in \mathbb{C}^2$ satisfying*
$$a_1 b_1 \ne 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad c(a_1, b_1) := -\frac{27 b_1^2}{4 a_1^3} \ne 4, \tag{1.13}$$
*put $g_{a_1, b_1}(x, t) := x^3 + a_1(t + 2)x + b_1(t + 2)$, so that $D_4(s) + 2 = (s^2 - 2)^2$ and $-D_4(s') + 2 = -s'^2(s'^2 - 4)$ give the Twisted-$D_4$ K3 pair*
$$X_{+, a_1, b_1} \colon\ y^2 = (s + 2)\Bigl(x^3 + a_1(s^2 - 2)^2 x + b_1(s^2 - 2)^2\Bigr), \tag{1.14}$$
$$X'_{+, a_1, b_1} \colon\ y'^2 = (s' + 2)\Bigl(x^3 - a_1 s'^2(s'^2 - 4) x - b_1 s'^2(s'^2 - 4)\Bigr). \tag{1.15}$$

*(a) (Visible lattices and swapped Hasse invariants.) $X_{+, a_1, b_1}$ is a K3 surface with singular fibres $2 I_0^*$ ($s = -2, \infty$), $2\,\mathrm{IV}$ ($s = \pm\sqrt{2}$), and $4\, I_1$, and visible lattice $N_{\mathrm{vis}}(X_+)_{\mathbb{Q}} = U \oplus D_4(-1)^2 \oplus A_2(-1)^2$ of rank $14$; $X'_{+, a_1, b_1}$ is a K3 surface with singular fibres $\mathrm{IV}^*$ ($s' = -2$), $I_0^*$ ($s' = \infty$), $\mathrm{IV}$ ($s' = 0$), $\mathrm{II}$ ($s' = 2$), and $4\, I_1$, and visible lattice $N_{\mathrm{vis}}(X'_+)_{\mathbb{Q}} = U \oplus E_6(-1) \oplus D_4(-1) \oplus A_2(-1)$ of rank $14$. Their rank-$8$, signature-$(2,6)$ orthogonal complements $L_1 = N_{\mathrm{vis}}(X_+)^\perp$ and $L_2 = N_{\mathrm{vis}}(X'_+)^\perp$ inside $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}, \mathbb{Q}}$ both have $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} L_1 \equiv \det_{\mathbb{Q}} L_2 \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}}$, **but swapped finite bad primes**:*
$$c_3(L_1) = -1 \text{ and } c_p(L_1) = +1 \ (\forall p \ne 3), \qquad\text{whereas}\qquad c_2(L_2) = -1 \text{ and } c_p(L_2) = +1 \ (\forall p \ne 2). \tag{1.16}$$

*(b) ($E$-isometry class, non-square type $\delta \equiv 3$, and impossibility of rational similarities.) Equipped with the geometric $E$-action induced by $\overline{C_r}$ ($E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, $u := -1 + \sqrt{2}$), $(L_1, \varphi_E) \cong_E \langle 3u, u, -1, -1\rangle_E$ and $(L_2, \varphi'_E) \cong_E L_1(2 + \sqrt{2})$, with **non-square $E$-discriminant***
$$\operatorname{disc} \varphi_E \equiv \operatorname{disc} \varphi'_E \equiv 3 \notin \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})^{\times 2}, \qquad L = E(\sqrt{\delta}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}). \tag{1.17}$$
*For very general $(a_1, b_1)$ along the non-isotrivial $1$-parameter moduli curve $c(a_1, b_1) \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 4\}$ (where $\rho = 14$, $T(X_+) = L_1$, $T(X'_+) = L_2$, and $E(X_+) = E(X'_+) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$), **no rational Hodge similarity $T(X_+)(\lambda) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} T(X'_+)$ exists for any $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$** (indeed, $L_1(\lambda) \not\cong_{\mathbb{Q}} L_2$ as abstract rational quadratic spaces for every $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$). Moreover, as an abstract $E$-quadratic space, $(L_1, \varphi_E)$ admits an orthogonal splitting $\langle 3u, 2u(3 - \sqrt{2}), -1\rangle_E \oplus \langle -(3 - \sqrt{2})\rangle_E$ whose $6$-dimensional trace complements in $L_1$ and $L_2$ simultaneously have rational Witt index $(1, 1)$ (algebraically demonstrating why the single-bad-prime hypothesis $p_0 = p'_0$ in [4, Theorem A(3)] cannot be omitted when bad primes differ; whether the $1$-parameter family $X_{+, a_1, b_1}$ attains this plane over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is a separate specialization question).*

*(c) (Unconditional Hodge conjecture on mixed products $a + b \le 3$.) For **every** $(a_1, b_1)$ satisfying (1.13) with $\rho(X_{+, a_1, b_1, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) = 14$ and $E(X_{+, a_1, b_1}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (and likewise at every octic-CM member, every member with $\rho \ge 18$, and every $\rho = 16$ member of rational Witt index $2$ on either partner), the rational Hodge conjecture holds in **all codimensions** on every mixed product*
$$X_{+, a_1, b_1}^a \times X'^b_{+, a_1, b_1} \qquad \text{with } a, b \ge 0,\ a + b \le 3 \tag{1.18}$$
*(in particular on $X_+ \times X'_+$, $X_+^2 \times X'_+$, and $X_+ \times X'^2_+$, none of which is reachable by Buskin [18] or [19, Theorem D(2)]).*

*(d) (Explicit $\mathbb{Q}$-rational non-CM pair.) At $(a_1, b_1) = (-2, 1)$ ($c = \frac{27}{32}$), exact Frobenius and $C_r$-traces at the good primes $p = 7$ and $p = 17$, combined with a local-to-global splitting-field intersection argument (§6.4), prove unconditionally that*
$$\rho\bigl(X_{+, -2, 1, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigr) = \rho\bigl(X'_{+, -2, 1, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigr) = 14, \qquad E(X_{+, -2, 1}) = E(X'_{+, -2, 1}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}), \qquad \operatorname{disc}\varphi_E \equiv 3 \pmod{E^{\times 2}},$$
*so $(X_{+, -2, 1},\, X'_{+, -2, 1})$ is an explicit $\mathbb{Q}$-rational non-CM K3 pair of non-square type satisfying (1.18) unconditionally and possessing no rational Hodge similarity of any multiplier.*

**Scope and honesty note.** All exceptional all-powers Hodge classes at $\rho = 16$ in Theorem 2 are imported from Floccari's theorem [5, Theorem 5.11] via [4, Theorem B(3)], and self-power algebraicity on $X_+^k$ ($k \le 3$) at $\rho = 14$ in Theorem 4(c) uses Proposition 7.1 of rStation 90 [19], which our explicit $E$-linear correspondence $C_f$ transports across the Hasse-invariant barrier to all mixed products $X_+^a \times X'^b_+$ ($a + b \le 3$). Theorem 4 varies over a $1$-dimensional moduli curve $c \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 4\}$ inside the $2$-dimensional non-square-type period domain; constructing a full $2$-dimensional non-square-type K3 family and establishing cycle $(\Gamma)$ on $X_+ \times B^2$ (hence $X_+^4$ and all-powers HC at $\rho = 14$) in [12, Theorem C and §10] remain open.

## 3. Symplectic quotient $Z_{b,c}$ and the Galois obstruction over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Proof of Theorem 1)

### 3.1 Explicit descent to $Z_{b,c}$ and the circle correspondence $C_R$

Let $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$. On $S_{b,c} \colon y^2 = x^3 - 3x + b D_8(s) + c$, the holomorphic $2$-form on the minimal Weierstrass chart is $\omega_S = \frac{dx \wedge ds}{y}$. Under $\iota_{\mathrm{odd}}(s, x, y) = (-s, x, -y)$, both $dx \wedge ds$ and $y$ change sign, so $\iota_{\mathrm{odd}}^* \omega_S = +\omega_S$: $\iota_{\mathrm{odd}}$ is a symplectic Nikulin involution defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. Introducing the $\iota_{\mathrm{odd}}$-invariants $w := D_2(s) = s^2 - 2$ (so $s^2 = w + 2$ and $D_8(s) = D_4(w)$) and $y_Z := y/s$ gives (1.2), namely $Z_{b,c} \colon (w + 2) y_Z^2 = x^3 - 3x + b D_4(w) + c$. Passing to standard minimal Weierstrass coordinates $X_{\mathrm{std}} = (w + 2)x$, $Y_{\mathrm{std}} = (w + 2)^2 y_Z$, we obtain
$$Y_{\mathrm{std}}^2 = X_{\mathrm{std}}^3 - 3(w + 2)^2 X_{\mathrm{std}} + (w + 2)^3\bigl(b D_4(w) + c\bigr), \qquad \Delta_Z(w) = 27(w + 2)^6\Bigl(\bigl(b D_4(w) + c\bigr)^2 - 4\Bigr). \tag{3.1}$$
Because $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$, $b D_4(-2) + c = 2b + c \ne \pm 2$ and $\operatorname{disc}_w(D_4(w) - t_0) = 256(t_0 + 2)^2(t_0 - 2)$ vanishes only at $t_0 = \pm 2$, which are disjoint from $t_\pm = (\pm 2 - c)/b$. By Tate's algorithm [20], (3.1) has valuations $(2, 3, 6)$ at $w = -2$ (Type $I_0^*$, root lattice $D_4(-1)$), valuations $(8 - 2, 12 - 7, 24 - 14) = (6, 5, 10)$ at $\tau = 1/w = 0$ (Type $\mathrm{II}^*$, root lattice $E_8(-1)$), and $8$ simple $I_1$ fibres ($e(Z_{b,c}) = 10 + 6 + 8 = 24$, K3). Thus $N_{\mathrm{vis}}(Z_{b,c})_{\mathbb{Q}} = U \oplus E_8(-1) \oplus D_4(-1)$ has rank $14$ and orthogonal complement $L(Z_{b,c}) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} U^2 \oplus D_4(-1)$ inside $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}, \mathbb{Q}} = U^3 \oplus E_8(-1)^2$ (since $D_4^\perp \subset E_8$ is isometric to $D_4$). Because $\deg q_Z = 2$ and $q_Z^*(\omega_Z) = q_Z^*\bigl(\frac{dx \wedge dw}{(w + 2)y_Z}\bigr) = \frac{dx \wedge (2s\,ds)}{s^2(y/s)} = 2\omega_S$, at a very general $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$ where $\rho(S_{b,c}) = 14$ [4, Proposition 6.4], $q_Z^* T(Z_{b,c}) \subseteq T(S_{b,c}) = L(S_{b,c})$ is an $8$-dimensional Hodge structure satisfying $\langle q_Z^* v, q_Z^* v'\rangle_S = 2\langle v, v'\rangle_Z$; by flatness of the local systems over $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, $q_Z^*$ defines a rational quadratic isometry $L(Z_{b,c})(2) \xrightarrow{\sim} L(S_{b,c})$ everywhere on $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ (`verify.py` check `A4`).

Now verify the algebraic equations (1.3) for $C_R \subset Z_{b,c} \times Z_{b,c}$. On the circle $w^2 + w'^2 = 4$, substituting $w^2 = 4 - w'^2$ gives
$$D_4(w) = (4 - w'^2)^2 - 4(4 - w'^2) + 2 = w'^4 - 4w'^2 + 2 = D_4(w') \tag{3.2}$$
and
$$(w + w' + 2)^2 = w^2 + (w' + 2)^2 + 2w(w' + 2) = (4 - w'^2) + (w'^2 + 4w' + 4) + 2w(w' + 2) = 2(w + 2)(w' + 2). \tag{3.3}$$
Hence $(w' + 2) y'^2_Z = (w' + 2) \frac{(w + w' + 2)^2}{2(w' + 2)^2} y_Z^2 = (w + 2) y_Z^2 = x^3 - 3x + b D_4(w') + c$, so $C_R$ lies in $Z_{b,c} \times Z_{b,c}$ (`A1`–`A2`). To compute $(C_R)^* \omega_Z$ and see that $C_R$ descends the $D_8$ operator $s$, recall from [4, Proposition 4.1] that on the $D_8$ cover $u + u^{-1} = s$, the rotation $\sigma \colon u \mapsto \zeta_8 u$ sends $w = u^2 + u^{-2}$ to $w' = i(u^2 - u^{-2})$ (satisfying $w^2 + w'^2 = 4$) and $s = u + u^{-1}$ to $s' = \zeta_8 u + \zeta_8^{-1} u^{-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(s + s_{\mathrm{conj}})$ where $s^2 = w + 2$, $s'^2 = w' + 2$, and $2 s s' = \sqrt{2}(w + w' + 2)$; thus $y'_Z = y/s' = y_Z \frac{s}{s'} = y_Z \frac{2 s s'}{2 s'^2} = y_Z \frac{\sqrt{2}(w + w' + 2)}{2(w' + 2)} = \frac{w + w' + 2}{\sqrt{2}(w' + 2)} y_Z$. Directly on $C_R$, over a general $w'$, write $w_1 = \sqrt{4 - w'^2}$ for the two sheets $w = \pm w_1$. Since $w\,dw + w'\,dw' = 0$, on a sheet $w$ we have $\frac{dx' \wedge dw'}{(w' + 2)y'_Z} / \frac{dx \wedge dw}{(w + 2)y_Z} = -\frac{w}{w'} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}(w + 2)}{w + w' + 2}$; using (3.3) together with $(w + w' + 2)(w - 2) = w^2 + w w' - 4 - 2w' = -w'(w' + 2 - w)$ directly gives
$$\frac{\sqrt{2}(w + 2)}{w + w' + 2} = \frac{w + w' + 2}{\sqrt{2}(w' + 2)}, \qquad -\frac{w}{w'} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}(w + 2)}{w + w' + 2} = -\frac{w(w + w' + 2)(w - 2)}{\sqrt{2}\,w'(w^2 - 4)} = \frac{w(w - w' - 2)}{\sqrt{2}\,w^2} = \frac{w - w' - 2}{\sqrt{2}\,w},$$
whose values on the two sheets $w = +w_1$ and $w = -w_1$ are $\frac{w_1 - w' - 2}{\sqrt{2}\,w_1}$ and $\frac{-w_1 - w' - 2}{-\sqrt{2}\,w_1} = \frac{w_1 + w' + 2}{\sqrt{2}\,w_1}$, summing identically to $\frac{2w_1}{\sqrt{2}\,w_1} = +\sqrt{2}$ (`A3`). Because $C_R$ is symmetric under $(w, y_Z) \leftrightarrow (w', y'_Z)$ (as $\frac{w + w' + 2}{\sqrt{2}(w' + 2)} \cdot \frac{w' + w + 2}{\sqrt{2}(w + 2)} = 1$ by (3.3)), $r_Z$ is self-adjoint on $L(Z_{b,c})$, coincides with $(q_Z^*)^{-1} s q_Z^*$ on $T(Z_{b,c})$ at very general $(b, c)$, and satisfies $r_Z^2 = 2\,\mathrm{id}_{L(Z_{b,c})}$ across $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ by flatness. This proves Theorem 1(a).

### 3.2 Action of $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}$ and the anti-commuting isometry criterion

For Theorem 1(b), consider $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}(s, x, y) = (-s, x, y)$ on $S_{b,c}$ ($(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$). We give two independent proofs that $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}^*|_{L(S_{b,c})} = -\mathrm{id}_{L(S_{b,c})}$:
1. *Flatness and generic irreducibility:* Over $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, the $16$ finite $I_1$ fibres of $S_{b,c}$ remain distinct and disjoint from $s = \infty$, where the Kodaira type is constantly $\mathrm{IV}^*$; uniform blowup along $s = \infty$ yields a smooth projective morphism $\pi \colon \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{B}^\circ$ over the connected base $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, on which $L = N_{\mathrm{vis}}^\perp \subset R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q}$ is a flat rank-$8$ sub-local system preserved by the relative involution $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}$. At a very general parameter $(b_0, c_0) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$, $L = T(S_{b_0, c_0})$ is an irreducible $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure [4, Proposition 6.4(1)] and $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}^*(\omega_S) = -\omega_S \ne 0$, so $\ker(\iota_{\mathrm{even}}^* + \mathrm{id}_L) = L$. By flatness over $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}^*|_{L(S_{b,c})} = -\mathrm{id}_{L(S_{b,c})}$ holds at *every* $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ$.
2. *Topological check (`A5`):* On $S_{b,c}$, $\iota_{\mathrm{even}}$ fixes pointwise the smooth elliptic fibre $E_0$ at $s = 0$ ($e(E_0) = 0$) together with the $4$ odd-multiplicity rational components $\Theta_0$ ($m = 3$) and $\Theta_{2,0}, \Theta_{2,1}, \Theta_{2,2}$ ($m = 1$) of the $\mathrm{IV}^*$ fibre at $s = \infty$ ($e = 4 \cdot 2 = 8$). By the Lefschetz fixed-point formula, $\operatorname{Tr}(\iota_{\mathrm{even}}^* \mid H^2(S_{b,c}, \mathbb{Q})) = 8 - 2 = 6$. Contracting the images of the three $m = 2$ limbs $\bar{\Theta}_{1,i}$ ($(-1)$-curves) and then $\bar{\Theta}_0$ on the smooth quotient $\widetilde{Y} = S_{b,c}/\langle \iota_{\mathrm{even}}\rangle$ yields the minimal rational elliptic surface $Y_{b,c}$ ($\mathrm{IV} + 8\, I_1$, $b_2(Y_{b,c}) = 10$), so $\dim H^2(S_{b,c}, \mathbb{Q})^{\iota_{\mathrm{even}} = +1} = b_2(\widetilde{Y}) = 10 + 4 = 14 = \operatorname{rk} N_{\mathrm{vis}}(S_{b,c})$, forcing $L(S_{b,c}) = H^2(S_{b,c}, \mathbb{Q})^{\iota_{\mathrm{even}} = -1}$.

Next, let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ ($d > 1$ squarefree) and let $N = \operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -\alpha\rangle$ ($\alpha = a_0 + a_1\sqrt{d} \in F_{>0}$) be the $2$-dimensional rational quadratic space $F$ equipped with the negative-definite bilinear form $\langle z, w\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}} := \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha z w)$ and $F$-multiplication operator $s(z) = \sqrt{d}\,z$ ($s^2 = d$).

**Lemma 3.1 (Anti-commuting rational isometry criterion).** *A $\mathbb{Q}$-linear orthogonal transformation $\psi \in \operatorname{O}_{\mathbb{Q}}(N)$ satisfies $\psi s \psi^{-1} = -s$ if and only if $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \in \{1, d\}\cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}$.*

*Proof.* Denote Galois conjugation on $F/\mathbb{Q}$ by $z \mapsto \bar{z}$. The map $z \mapsto \bar{z}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-linear and anti-commutes with $s = \sqrt{d}$. If $\psi \colon F \to F$ is any $\mathbb{Q}$-linear endomorphism satisfying $\psi(\sqrt{d}\,z) = -\sqrt{d}\,\psi(z)$, then $z \mapsto \psi(\bar{z})$ commutes with $\sqrt{d}$, so it is $F$-linear and equals multiplication by a unique $\beta \in F$; thus **every** $\mathbb{Q}$-linear operator anti-commuting with $s$ has the form $\psi_\beta(z) = \beta \bar{z}$ for a unique $\beta \in F$. For all $z, w \in F$,
$$\langle \psi_\beta(z), \psi_\beta(w)\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}} = \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(-\alpha \beta^2 \overline{z w}\bigr) = \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(-\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}^2 z w\bigr).$$
By nondegeneracy of $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}$, this equals $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha z w)$ for all $z, w \in F$ if and only if $\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}^2 = \alpha$ (equivalently $\alpha \beta^2 = \bar{\alpha}$). Multiplying by $\alpha \ne 0$, this is equivalent to $(\alpha\beta)^2 = \alpha\bar{\alpha} = N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)$ for some $\beta \in F^\times$, i.e., $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \in F^{\times 2}$. A rational number $q \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})^\times$ if and only if $q \in \{1, d\}\cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}$ (`verify.py` check `A6`). $\square$

To finish Theorem 1(b)–(c), suppose $(b, c) \in \mathcal{B}^\circ(M)$ ($M \subset \mathbb{R}$, e.g. $M = \mathbb{Q}$) has $\rho(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) = 16$ where $S$ is either $S_{b,c}$ ($F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$) or $X_{b,c}$ ($F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$), and assume $\sqrt{d} \notin M$. Because $S$, its polarization, and $N_{\mathrm{vis}}(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ are defined over $M$ (the $\mathrm{IV}^*$, $I_0^*$ components and $\operatorname{MW}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ are $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/M)$-stable as subspaces), $L = N_{\mathrm{vis}}^\perp$ and $\operatorname{NS}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ are $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/M)$-stable, hence so is the $2$-plane $N = L \cap \operatorname{NS}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\langle -\alpha\rangle$. Choose $\tilde{\sigma} \in \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/M)$ restricting to the nontrivial automorphism of $F/\mathbb{Q}$. Through comparison with $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-étale cohomology (or directly via the Galois action on the finite extension of $M$ over which $\operatorname{NS}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})$ is defined, which preserves the intersection pairing), $\tilde{\sigma}$ induces a **rational** orthogonal transformation $\psi \in \operatorname{O}_{\mathbb{Q}}(N)$. Because the correspondence inducing $s$ ($C_R$ in (1.3) for $d = 2$, or $s := 2r + \mathrm{id}_L$ where $r^2 + r - 1 = 0$ is the cycle of [4, Proposition 4.2] for $d = 5$, which multiplies $\omega_X$ by $2\varphi - 1 = \sqrt{5}$ and transforms under $\zeta_5 \mapsto \zeta_5^2$ as $\tilde{\sigma}(r) = -\mathrm{id}_L - r$, hence $\tilde{\sigma}(s) = -s$) satisfies $\tilde{\sigma}(s) = -s$ on $L$ at very general parameters and therefore everywhere on $\mathcal{B}^\circ$ by flatness, we have $\psi s \psi^{-1} = -s$ on $N$. Lemma 3.1 gives $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \in \{1, d\}\cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}$, whence $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} T(S) \equiv \det_{\mathbb{Q}} N = 4 d N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) \in \{1, d\}\cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}$. Evaluating [4, Theorem B(3) and C(3)] at $p_0 = 2$ ($-1, -2 \notin \mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}$) vs. $p_0 = 5$ ($-1 \in \mathbb{Q}_5^{\times 2}$) gives Theorem 1(c) (`A7`). $\square$

## 4. Shioda-height-$3$ elimination and the Witt-index-one curve (Proof of Theorem 2)

### 4.1 Exact Weierstrass elimination on $Z_{A,B,C}$

To construct an explicit $F$-rational Witt-index-one curve in $\mathcal{B}^\circ$, we seek parameters for which $Z_{b,c}$ acquires a section $P \in \operatorname{MW}(Z_{b,c})$ orthogonal to $E_8(-1)$ at $w = \infty$, meeting a non-identity simple component of $I_0^*$ at $w = -2$ ($\operatorname{contr}_{I_0^*}(P) = 1$), and disjoint from the zero section $O$ ($(P \cdot O) = 0$). By Shioda's height formula [20, Theorem 8.6] on the K3 surface $Z_{b,c}$ ($\chi(\mathcal{O}_Z) = 2$, $\operatorname{contr}_{\mathrm{II}^*} = 0$), such a section has minimal positive Shioda height
$$h_Z(P) = 2\chi(\mathcal{O}_Z) + 2(P \cdot O) - \operatorname{contr}_{I_0^*}(P) = 4 + 0 - 1 = 3, \tag{4.1}$$
so its projection to $L(Z_{b,c})$ has self-intersection $-3$, pulling back via $q_Z^*$ to a class in $L(S_{b,c})$ of self-intersection $-6$.

Work first on the unnormalized family $Z_{A,B,C} \colon (w + 2) y_Z^2 = x^3 + A x + B D_4(w) + C$ ($B \ne 0$). Any section $P = (x(w), y_Z(w))$ with $(P \cdot O) = 0$ has $\deg x(w) \le 3$ and $\deg y_Z(w) = 4$; since the leading degrees in $(w + 2)y_Z(w)^2$ and $x(w)^3$ are $9$, we must have $\deg x(w) = 3$ and $\deg y_Z(w) = 4$, and scaling $(x, y_Z) \mapsto (\lambda^2 x, \lambda^3 y_Z)$ normalizes both $x(w)$ and $y_Z(w)$ to be **monic**:
$$x(w) = w^3 + a_2 w^2 + a_1 w + a_0, \qquad y_Z(w) = w^4 + g_3 w^3 + g_2 w^2 + g_1 w + g_0. \tag{4.2}$$
Let $E(w) := (w + 2)y_Z(w)^2 - x(w)^3$. Since $x(w) = w^3 + a_2 w^2 + a_1 w + a_0$ is monic cubic and $D_4(w) = w^4 - 4w^2 + 2$ is monic quartic, for $P$ to lie on $Z_{A,B,C}$ for some constants $(A, B, C)$ it is necessary and sufficient that setting
$$B = [w^4] E(w), \qquad A = [w^3] E(w), \qquad C = [w^0] E(w) - A a_0 - 2B \tag{4.3}$$
makes the six remaining coefficients of $E(w) - A x(w) - B D_4(w) - C$ vanish: $[w^k] E(w) = 0$ for $k = 8, 7, 6, 5$, together with $[w^2]\bigl(E(w) - A x(w) - B D_4(w)\bigr) = 0$ and $[w^1]\bigl(E(w) - A x(w) - B D_4(w)\bigr) = 0$ (six polynomial conditions on the seven coefficients $a_2, a_1, a_0, g_3, g_2, g_1, g_0$, cutting out a $1$-dimensional locus).

Solving $[w^8]E = [w^7]E = [w^6]E = [w^5]E = 0$ triangularly expresses $g_3, g_2, g_1, g_0$ uniquely as polynomials in $(a_2, a_1, a_0)$:
$$g_3 = \frac{3}{2}a_2 - 1, \quad g_2 = \frac{3a_2^2 + 3a_1 - g_3^2 - 4g_3}{2}, \quad g_1 = \frac{a_2^3 + 6a_2 a_1 + 3a_0 - 2g_3 g_2 - 2g_3^2 - 4g_2}{2},$$
$$g_0 = \frac{3a_2^2 a_1 + 3a_1^2 + 6a_2 a_0 - g_2^2 - 2g_3 g_1 - 4g_1 - 4g_3 g_2}{2}. \tag{4.4}$$
Put $u := a_2 - 2$ and $Z := a_1 - 2u - \frac{1}{4}u^2$. Substituting (4.4) into the $[w^2]$-residual reveals that it is **linear** in $a_0$, namely $d_1(u, Z)\,a_0 + n_1(u, Z) = 0$ with
$$d_1(u, Z) = 3u + \frac{9}{8}u^2 - \frac{15}{8}Z, \qquad n_1(u, Z) = -\left(\frac{3}{2}u^3 + \frac{9}{16}u^4\right) - \left(3u + \frac{15}{16}u^2 + \frac{9}{32}u^3\right)Z + \left(2 + \frac{7}{16}u\right)Z^2. \tag{4.5}$$
Setting $a_0 = -n_1(u, Z)/d_1(u, Z)$, the remaining $[w^1]$-residual factors over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ into **three** explicit rational branches (`verify.py` check `B1`):
1. **Branch I ($\mathbb{Q}$-rational):** $Z = \frac{6u^2 - 8u - 64}{25}$;
2. **Branches $\mathrm{II}_\pm$ ($\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q})$-conjugate):**
   $$Z_{\mathrm{II}_\pm}(u) = 2u(u - 2) \pm \sqrt{2}\,u(4 - u). \tag{4.6}$$

Remarkably, along Branch $\mathrm{II}_+$, substituting (4.6) into $d_1(u, Z)$ yields
$$d_1\bigl(u, Z_{\mathrm{II}_+}(u)\bigr) = \frac{3}{8}(7 - 5\sqrt{2})\,u(4 - u) = -\frac{3}{8}(\sqrt{2} - 1)^3 u(4 - u), \tag{4.7}$$
where $7 - 5\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]^\times$ ($N(7 - 5\sqrt{2}) = 49 - 50 = -1$) is a **unit**. Thus the spurious denominator $d_1$ cancels $u(4 - u)$ completely, leaving pure polynomials in $u$. Setting $u = 2\sqrt{2}\,t$ gives (`B2`):
$$A(t) = t^2(t + 1)^2 A_q(t), \qquad B(t) = -64\,t^2(t + 1)^3, \qquad C(t) = t^2(t + 1)^4 C_q(t), \tag{4.8}$$
with $A_q(t)$ and $C_q(t)$ matching (1.5)–(1.6).

$$(1 + \tau_1)(1 + \alpha\tau_1)(1 + \tau_1) = (1 + \tau_1 + \alpha\tau_1 + \tau_1\alpha\tau_1)(1 + \tau_1) = (1 + \alpha^{-1} + \tau_1 + \alpha\tau_1)(1 + \tau_1) = (2 + \alpha + \alpha^{-1})(1 + \tau_1). \tag{5.1}$$

To pass to $X_+$ and $X'_+$, form the $\tau_1$-invariants $\pi_+(u, x, z) = (s, x, y) := \bigl(u + u^{-1},\, x,\, z(1 + u^{-1})\bigr)$ and the $\tau_2$-invariants $\pi'_+(u, x, z) = (s', x, y') := \bigl(\zeta_8^{-1} u + \zeta_8 u^{-1},\, x,\, \zeta_{16}^{-1} z(1 + \zeta_8 u^{-1})\bigr)$ ($\tau_2 = \alpha\tau_1$, $\zeta_{16}^2 = \zeta_8$):
- $y^2 = z^2(1 + u^{-1})^2 = u^{-1} z^2 (u + 2 + u^{-1}) = (s + 2)\,g(x, D_4(s))$, identifying $W/\langle \tau_1\rangle$ birationally with $X_+$;
- $(\zeta_8^{-1} u)^4 + (\zeta_8 u^{-1})^4 = -(u^4 + u^{-4}) = -D_4(s')$, and $y'^2 = \zeta_8^{-1} z^2 (1 + \zeta_8 u^{-1})^2 = u^{-1} z^2 (\zeta_8^{-1} u + 2 + \zeta_8 u^{-1}) = (s' + 2)\,g(x, -D_4(s'))$, identifying $W/\langle \tau_2\rangle$ birationally with $X'_+$.

Eliminating $u$ from $s = u + u^{-1}$ and $s_\alpha := \alpha^*(s) = i u - i u^{-1}$ gives $s^2 + s_\alpha^2 = 4$, and symmetrizing $\alpha + \alpha^{-1}$ on $X_+ \times X_+$ yields $C_r$ in (1.11) over $K(\sqrt{2})$. Likewise, eliminating $u$ from $s = u + u^{-1}$ and $s' = \zeta_8^{-1} u + \zeta_8 u^{-1}$ uses $\zeta_8 + \zeta_8^{-1} = \sqrt{2}$ and $\zeta_8 - \zeta_8^{-1} = i\sqrt{2}$: since $s' - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} s = -\frac{i\sqrt{2}}{2}(u - u^{-1})$, squaring gives $\bigl(s' - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}s\bigr)^2 = -\frac{1}{2}(s^2 - 4)$, i.e., **the conic $s^2 + s'^2 - \sqrt{2}\,s s' = 2$** over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. Along this conic, writing $s'^2 = \sqrt{2}\,s s' - s^2 + 2$ directly in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})[s, s'] / (s^2 + s'^2 - \sqrt{2}\,s s' - 2)$ gives (`D2`)
$$D_4(s') = (\sqrt{2}\,s s' - s^2 + 2)^2 - 4(\sqrt{2}\,s s' - s^2 + 2) + 2 = -s^4 + 4s^2 - 2 = -D_4(s), \tag{5.2}$$
$$\bigl((2 + \sqrt{2}) + s + s'\bigr)^2 = (2 + \sqrt{2})(s + 2)(s' + 2), \tag{5.3}$$
so $y' = \frac{(2 + \sqrt{2}) + s + s'}{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}(s + 2)}\,y$ satisfies $y'^2 = (s' + 2)\,g(x, -D_4(s'))$ identically over $K(\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}})$. Finally, on $\omega_{X_+} = \frac{dx \wedge ds}{y}$ (regular and nonvanishing since $X_{\mathrm{std}} = (s + 2)x$, $Y_{\mathrm{std}} = (s + 2)y$ satisfy $\frac{dX_{\mathrm{std}} \wedge ds}{Y_{\mathrm{std}}} = \frac{dx \wedge ds}{y}$), over a general $s$ the two roots of $s'^2 - \sqrt{2}\,s s' + s^2 - 2 = 0$ are $s'_\pm = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}s \pm \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{4 - s^2}$ with $\frac{ds'_\pm}{ds} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\bigl(1 \mp \frac{s}{\sqrt{4 - s^2}}\bigr)$; summing $\frac{ds'_\pm / ds}{y'_\pm / y}$ using the exact polynomial identity $4(s + 2)\bigl((1 + \sqrt{2})(s + 2) + s\bigr) = (2 + \sqrt{2})^2(s + 2)^2 - 2(4 - s^2)$ (`D2`) gives $(C_f)^* \omega_{X'_+} = +\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}\,\omega_{X_+}$, and (5.1) gives $f^t f = 2 + r$ on $T(X_+)$ (see [4, §4.1]). This proves Theorem 3. $\square$

### 6.1 Kodaira fibres, visible lattices, and Hasse-invariant swap

The Euler sum is $6 + 6 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 24$, so $X_{+, a_1, b_1}$ is K3 with $N_{\mathrm{vis}}(X_+)_{\mathbb{Q}} = U \oplus D_4(-1)^2 \oplus A_2(-1)^2$ (rank $14$). Similarly, on $X'_{+, a_1, b_1}$, $X'_{\mathrm{std}} = (s' + 2)x$ and $Y'_{\mathrm{std}} = (s' + 2)y'$ give

### 6.2 Geometric identification of $(L_1, \varphi_E)$, $\operatorname{disc}\varphi_E \equiv 3$, and impossibility of rational similarities

Let $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ and $\mathcal{U} := \{(a_1, b_1) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : a_1 b_1 \ne 0,\ c(a_1, b_1) \ne 4\}$. As shown in §6.4(1) directly from the $\mathbb{F}_{7^k}$ ($k = 1, \dots, 4$) point counts on $X_{+, -2, 1}$ (using only Theorem 4(a)), the specialization $(a_1, b_1) = (-2, 1) \in \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Q})$ satisfies $T(X_{+, -2, 1}) = L_1$ and $T(X'_{+, -2, 1}) = L_2$. By Theorem 3(b) and flatness of the local systems $L_1 = N_{\mathrm{vis}}(\mathcal{X}_+)^\perp$ and $L_2 = N_{\mathrm{vis}}(\mathcal{X}'_+)^\perp$ over the connected base $\mathcal{U}$, the algebraic correspondences $\overline{C_r}$ and $\overline{C_f}$ induce across all of $\mathcal{U}$ a self-adjoint operator $r = \Pi_{L_1} \circ [\overline{C_r}]_*|_{L_1}$ with $r^2 = 2\,\mathrm{id}_{L_1}$ and an $E$-linear isomorphism $f \colon L_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} L_2$ satisfying $f^t f = 2 + r$. Thus $(L_1, \varphi_E)$ is a $4$-dimensional $E$-quadratic space of real signatures $(2, 2)$ at $\sigma_+(\sqrt{2}) = +\sqrt{2}$ and $(0, 4)$ at $\sigma_-(\sqrt{2}) = -\sqrt{2}$, with $(L_2, \varphi'_E) \cong_E (L_1, (2 + \sqrt{2})\varphi_E)$.

First observe why $\delta := \operatorname{disc} \varphi_E \notin E^{\times 2}$. For **any** $4$-dimensional quadratic space $(W, \varphi_E)$ over $E$ of determinant $1 \in E^{\times 2}$ and any $\mu \in E^\times$, diagonalizing $\varphi_E = \langle d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4\rangle_E$ ($d_1 d_2 d_3 d_4 \in E^{\times 2}$) gives at every place $v$ of $E$ [21, Ch. 2]
$$\frac{s_v(\mu \varphi_E)}{s_v(\varphi_E)} = (\mu, \mu)_v^{\binom{4}{2}} \cdot \bigl(\mu,\, d_1 d_2 d_3 d_4\bigr)_v^3 = (\mu, -1)_v^6 \cdot (\mu, 1)_v^3 = +1. \tag{6.3}$$
Since $\mu = 2 + \sqrt{2}$ is totally positive, Hasse–Minkowski over $E$ shows that $\delta \in E^{\times 2}$ would force $(W, \mu\varphi_E) \cong_E (W, \varphi_E)$ and hence $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \circ (\mu\varphi_E) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \circ \varphi_E$ (`D4` square-type control), contradicting $c_3(L_1) = -1 \ne +1 = c_3(L_2)$.

However, rational trace invariants alone—even before and after scaling by $\mu = 2 + \sqrt{2}$—do **not** determine $\delta \bmod E^{\times 2}$ or the $E$-isometry class of $(L_1, \varphi_E)$: with $u := -1 + \sqrt{2}$, check `D4` verifies that $W_{\mathrm{ns}} := \langle 3u, u, -1, -1\rangle_E$ ($\delta \equiv 3$) and $\widetilde{W} := \langle 3u, u, -1, -17\rangle_E$ ($\delta \equiv 51 \not\equiv 3 \pmod{E^{\times 2}}$) have **isometric** rational trace forms both before and after multiplication by $\mu = 2 + \sqrt{2}$. To identify $\delta \bmod E^{\times 2}$ and $(L_1, \varphi_E)$ over $E$ rigorously, we use the geometric cycle $\overline{C_r}$ via the following identity and an integral $\mathcal{O}_E[1/6]$-lattice analysis.

**Lemma 6.1 (Orthogonal Pfaffian identity).** *Let $(V, \Phi)$ be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear space of even dimension $2n$ over a field $k$ ($\operatorname{char} k \ne 2$), and let $g \in \operatorname{SO}(V, \Phi)$ have characteristic polynomial $P_g(T) = \det(T\cdot \mathrm{id}_V - g)$. Then $A := \Phi(g - g^{-1})$ is skew-symmetric and its Pfaffian satisfies $\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 = \det(\Phi)\,P_g(1)\,P_g(-1)$.*

*Proof.* Because $g^t \Phi g = \Phi$, we have $g^t \Phi = \Phi g^{-1}$, whence $A^t = (g^t - (g^{-1})^t)\Phi = \Phi(g^{-1} - g) = -A$. Taking determinants and using $\det(-g^{-1}) = (-1)^{2n}(\det g)^{-1} = +1$ gives $\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 = \det(A) = \det(\Phi)\det(-g^{-1})\det(\mathrm{id}_V - g^2) = \det(\Phi)\det(\mathrm{id}_V - g)\det(\mathrm{id}_V + g) = \det(\Phi)\,P_g(1)\,P_g(-1)$ (cross-checked on a non-diagonal Cayley element of $\operatorname{SO}(4)$ in `D6`). $\square$

We now prove $(L_1, \varphi_E) \cong_E W_{\mathrm{ns}} = \langle 3u, u, -1, -1\rangle_E$ in three steps:
1. *Global identification $\delta \equiv 3 \pmod{E^{\times 2}}$ via $\overline{C_r}$ at $\mathfrak{p}_{7,1}$:* Specialize to $(a_1, b_1) = (-2, 1) \in \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Q})$ ($c = \frac{27}{32}$), which has good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}_{7,1} = (7, \sqrt{2} - 3)$. For every finite place $\lambda \nmid 7$ of $E$, étale comparison and smooth proper base change identify $(L_1(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda, \varphi_{E,\lambda})$ as a $4$-dimensional $E_\lambda$-quadratic space of discriminant $\delta \in E_\lambda^\times / (E_\lambda^\times)^2$, preserved by $g_{1,7} := 7^{-1}\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}_{7,1}}|_{L_1(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda}$ because $\overline{C_r}$ reduces over $\mathbb{F}_7$ ($\sqrt{2} \equiv 3 \pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{7,1}}$). In §6.4(1) (`D6`), exact $\mathbb{F}_{7^k}$ ($k = 1, 2, 3$) and $C_r$ ($k = 1, 2$) counts establish $\det_{E_\lambda}(g_{1,7}) = +1$ and $P_{g_{1,7}}(1)\,P_{g_{1,7}}(-1) = 3\bigl(\frac{4 + 6\sqrt{2}}{7}\bigr)^2 \ne 0$. Applying Lemma 6.1 to $g_{1,7} \in \operatorname{SO}_{E_\lambda}(L_1(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda, \varphi_{E,\lambda})$ gives $\operatorname{Pf}(A_\lambda)^2 = \delta \cdot 3\bigl(\frac{4 + 6\sqrt{2}}{7}\bigr)^2 \ne 0$, so $\delta / 3 \in (E_\lambda^\times)^2$ for **every** finite place $\lambda \nmid 7$ of $E$. By the Global Square Theorem, $\delta \equiv 3 \pmod{E^{\times 2}}$ globally across $\mathcal{U}$, ruling out $\widetilde{W}$ ($\delta \equiv 51$).
2. *Unimodularity over $\mathcal{O}_{E,\mathfrak{q}}$ and $s_{\mathfrak{q}}(\varphi_E) = +1$ outside $\{2, 3\}$:* On any smooth member $X_+$ over $\mathcal{U}$, $H^2(X_+, \mathbb{Z})$ is unimodular by Poincaré duality and $[\overline{C_r}]_*$ preserves $H^2(X_+, \mathbb{Z})$. The visible integral sublattice $N_{\mathrm{vis},\mathbb{Z}}(X_+) = \mathbb{Z}\langle O, F\rangle \oplus D_4(-1)^2 \oplus A_2(-1)^2 \subset H^2(X_+, \mathbb{Z})$ has $|\det N_{\mathrm{vis},\mathbb{Z}}(X_+)| = 1 \cdot 4^2 \cdot 3^2 = 144 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \in \mathbb{Z}[1/6]^\times$ (`D4`). Over $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$, $N_{\mathrm{vis}, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]}$ is therefore unimodular inside $H^2(X_+, \mathbb{Z}[1/6])$, giving an orthogonal splitting $H^2(X_+, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]) = N_{\mathrm{vis}, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]} \perp L_{1, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]}$ in which $L_{1, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]}$ is a unimodular $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$-lattice of rank $8$ and $\Pi_{L_1}$ preserves $H^2(X_+, \mathbb{Z}[1/6])$. Consequently, $r = \Pi_{L_1} \circ [\overline{C_r}]_*|_{L_1}$ preserves $L_{1, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]}$, making $L_{1, \mathbb{Z}[1/6]}$ a projective rank-$4$ module over $\mathbb{Z}[1/6][r]/(r^2 - 2) = \mathcal{O}_E[1/6]$. At every prime ideal $\mathfrak{q} \nmid 6$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ (lying over $p \ge 5$, unramified in $E$), the local inverse different $\mathfrak{D}_{E_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathbb{Q}_p}^{-1} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{O}_{E,\mathfrak{q}} = \mathcal{O}_{E,\mathfrak{q}}$ is trivial, so trace duality makes $(L_{1, \mathfrak{q}}, \varphi_E)$ a **unimodular** symmetric bilinear $\mathcal{O}_{E,\mathfrak{q}}$-lattice over a non-dyadic local ring ($2 \in \mathcal{O}_{E,\mathfrak{q}}^\times$). Diagonalizing over $\mathcal{O}_{E,\mathfrak{q}}^\times$ gives $s_{\mathfrak{q}}(\varphi_E) = +1$ for all $\mathfrak{q} \nmid 6$.
3. *Hasse–Minkowski selection at $\mathfrak{p}_2 = (\sqrt{2})$ and $\mathfrak{p}_3 = (3)$:* Real signatures $((2, 2), (0, 4))$ give $s_{\sigma_+}(\varphi_E) = -1$ and $s_{\sigma_-}(\varphi_E) = +1$. Since $s_{\mathfrak{q}}(\varphi_E) = +1$ for all $\mathfrak{q} \notin \{\mathfrak{p}_2, \mathfrak{p}_3\}$, Hilbert reciprocity $\prod_v s_v(\varphi_E) = +1$ forces $s_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(\varphi_E)\,s_{\mathfrak{p}_3}(\varphi_E) = -1$, leaving at most two $E$-isometry classes of rank $4$, discriminant $3$, and signatures $((2, 2), (0, 4))$. Both exist globally over $E$: $W_{\mathrm{ns}} = \langle 3u, u, -1, -1\rangle_E$ ($s_{\mathfrak{p}_3} = -1$) and $W_{\mathrm{alt}} := \langle 3u, 3u, -3, -1\rangle_E$ ($s_{\mathfrak{p}_3} = +1$). Check `D4` verifies that $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(W_{\mathrm{alt}})$ has $c_3 = +1$ (`bad_p = [∞, 2]`), which is incompatible with $c_3(L_1) = -1$ (Theorem 4(a)), whereas $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(W_{\mathrm{ns}}) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} L_1$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mu W_{\mathrm{ns}}) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} L_2$. By Hasse–Minkowski over $E$ [21, Ch. 6], $(L_1, \varphi_E) \cong_E W_{\mathrm{ns}} = \langle 3u, u, -1, -1\rangle_E$ and $(L_2, \varphi'_E) \cong_E W_{\mathrm{ns}}(2 + \sqrt{2})$.

Next, let $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ be arbitrary ($L_1$ and $L_2$ have signature $(2, 6)$, so a similarity $L_1(\lambda) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} L_2$ requires $\lambda > 0$). For *any* rational quadratic space $V$ of rank $8$ and determinant $d_V \equiv +1 \pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}}$, scaling by $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ gives at every prime $p$:
$$c_p\bigl(V(\lambda)\bigr) = c_p(V) \cdot (\lambda, \lambda)_p^{\binom{8}{2}} \cdot (\lambda, d_V)_p^{8 - 1} = c_p(V) \cdot (\lambda, -1)_p^{28} \cdot (\lambda, 1)_p^7 = c_p(V). \tag{6.4}$$
Thus $c_3(L_1(\lambda)) = c_3(L_1) = -1 \ne +1 = c_3(L_2)$ for **every** $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ (`D4`): no rational similarity $L_1(\lambda) \cong_{\mathbb{Q}} L_2$ exists.

Finally, for the abstract $E$-quadratic splitting in Theorem 4(b) (`D5`), inside $\langle u, -1\rangle_E \subset W_{\mathrm{ns}}$ the vector $v = (1, \sqrt{2})$ has $\varphi_E(v, v) = u - 2 = -(3 - \sqrt{2})$, with orthogonal vector $v_\perp = (2, u\sqrt{2}) = (2, 2 - \sqrt{2})$ of norm $2u(3 - \sqrt{2})$, splitting $W_{\mathrm{ns}} \cong_E T_1 \oplus \langle -(3 - \sqrt{2})\rangle_E$ where $T_1 = \langle 3u, 2u(3 - \sqrt{2}), -1\rangle_E$. Check `D5` verifies that **both** $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(T_1)$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(T_1(2 + \sqrt{2}))$ have rational Witt index $1$ simultaneously ($-2\cdot 7 = -14 \equiv 1 \in \mathbb{Q}_3^{\times 2}$ at $L_1$'s bad prime $3$, while $-7 \equiv 1 \pmod{8} \in \mathbb{Q}_2^{\times 2}$ at $L_2$'s bad prime $2$). We emphasize that this is an algebraic decomposition of the abstract $E$-space $(L_1, \varphi_E)$ illustrating why $p_0 = p'_0$ is needed in [4, Theorem A(3)], rather than a proof that the $1$-parameter family $X_{+, a_1, b_1}$ specializes to this Noether–Lefschetz locus.

### 6.3 Unconditional Hodge conjecture on mixed products $X_+^a \times X'^b_+$ ($a + b \le 3$)

Let $(a_1, b_1)$ satisfy (1.13). First suppose $\rho(X_+) = 14$ and $E(X_+) = E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (hence also $\rho(X'_+) = 14$ and $E(X'_+) = E$ via the algebraic Hodge isomorphism $f \colon T(X_+) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(X'_+)$ of Theorem 3(b)). By Theorem 3(b), $r \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X_+))$ ($r^2 = 2$) and $r' = f r f^{-1} \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X'_+))$ are induced by algebraic cycles $C_r \subset X_+^2$ and $(f \times f)_*(C_r)$ (or $C_{r'} \subset X'^2_+$). Since $m = \dim_E T(X_+) = 4$, Proposition 7.1 of rStation 90 [19] applies to $X_+$ and $X'_+$, proving that **every** rational Hodge class on $X_+^k$ and on $X'^k_+$ is algebraic for all $k < 4$, i.e. $k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.

Now let $a, b \ge 0$ satisfy $a + b = k \le 3$. Consider the correspondence
$$\Phi_{a, b} := (\Delta_{X_+})^a \times (\Gamma_f)^b \;\in\; \operatorname{CH}^{2k}\bigl(X_+^k \times (X_+^a \times X'^b_+)\bigr)_{\mathbb{Q}},$$
where $\Delta_{X_+} \subset X_+ \times X_+$ is the diagonal and $\Gamma_f \subset X_+ \times X'_+$ is the algebraic cycle from Theorem 3(b) inducing $f \colon T(X_+) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(X'_+)$ (projected onto $T$ via the algebraic projector $\Pi_T = \mathrm{id}_{H^2} - \Pi_{\mathrm{NS}}$; on $\mathrm{NS}(X'_+)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $H^0 \oplus H^4$, algebraic correspondences induce isomorphisms independently via algebraic divisor and point classes). More explicitly, decompose $H^*(X_+^a \times X'^b_+, \mathbb{Q})$ by the Künneth theorem along $H^2 = \operatorname{NS}_{\mathbb{Q}} \oplus T$ and $H^0 \oplus H^4$ on each factor. Every Künneth summand is a tensor product of algebraic classes (points, fundamental classes, divisors in $\operatorname{NS}$) tensored with $T(X_+)^{\otimes a'} \otimes T(X'_+)^{\otimes b'}$ where $a' \le a$, $b' \le b$, so $k' := a' + b' \le a + b \le 3$. Because $f^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}(2 - r)f^t \colon T(X'_+) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(X_+)$ is also induced by an algebraic correspondence $\Gamma_{f^{-1}} \in \operatorname{CH}^2(X'_+ \times X_+)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, the algebraic correspondence $(\Pi_{T(X_+)})^{\otimes a'} \otimes (\Gamma_{f^{-1}})^{\otimes b'}$ maps Hodge classes in $T(X_+)^{\otimes a'} \otimes T(X'_+)^{\otimes b'}$ isomorphically onto Hodge classes in $T(X_+)^{\otimes k'} \subset H^{2k'}(X_+^{k'}, \mathbb{Q})$ ($k' \le 3$), which are algebraic by [19, Proposition 7.1], and $(\Pi_{T(X_+)})^{\otimes a'} \otimes (\Gamma_f)^{\otimes b'}$ transports those algebraic cycles back to $X_+^{a'} \times X'^{b'}_+$.

Finally, at special members listed in Theorem 4(c), the identical transport by $(\Delta_{X_+})^a \times (\Gamma_f)^b$ and $(\Delta_{X'_+})^b \times (\Gamma_{f^{-1}})^a$ establishes (1.18)—and in fact the rational Hodge conjecture for *all* $a, b \ge 0$—whenever $X_+$ has octic CM [2, 3], whenever $\rho(X_+) \ge 18$ (where $\dim_E T(X_+) \le 2$ forces CM [1, 4]), or whenever $\rho(X_+) = 16$ and either $T(X_+)$ or $T(X'_+)$ has rational Witt index $2$ (by Floccari [5, Theorem 5.11], applied to whichever partner has Witt index $2$ and transported to all mixed products by $\Gamma_f$ and $\Gamma_{f^{-1}}$; for $a + b \le 2$, [19, Proposition 7.1] applies even on the $(1, 1)$ Witt-index locus since $k < 3 = \dim_E T$).

### 6.4 Two-prime Frobenius counts and local-to-global non-CM proof at $(a_1, b_1) = (-2, 1)$ (Proof of Theorem 4(d))

Specialize (1.14)–(1.15) to $(a_1, b_1) = (-2, 1)$, so $c = -\frac{27 \cdot 1^2}{4(-2)^3} = \frac{27}{32} \notin \{0, 4\}$. Both $p = 7$ and $p = 17$ split in $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ ($\mathfrak{p}_{7,1} = (7, \sqrt{2} - 3)$, $\mathfrak{p}_{17,1} = (17, \sqrt{2} - 6)$) and satisfy $p \nmid 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 101$ ($c - 4 = -\frac{101}{32}$, so $c \equiv 5 \pmod{7}$ and $c \equiv 12 \pmod{17}$ are distinct from $0, 4$), ensuring good reduction of $X_{+, -2, 1}$ and $X'_{+, -2, 1}$ at $p = 7$ and $p = 17$.

On $X_{+, -2, 1}$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ ($q = p^k$, $p \in \{7, 17\}$), every singular fibre at $s_0 \in \{-2, \infty, +\sqrt{2}, -\sqrt{2}\}$ satisfies $\# X_{s_0}(\mathbb{F}_q) = 1 + q + \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_q \mid \Phi_{s_0})$ (for both $I_0^*$ and split/non-split $\mathrm{IV}$), while the $4$ nodal $I_1$ fibres require no blowups on the minimal K3 surface. Summing over $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_q)$ cancels $1 + \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_q \mid N_{\mathrm{vis}}(X_+)) + q^2$ identically, leaving
$$\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_q \mid L_1(X_{+, -2, 1})\bigr) = \sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_q \setminus \{-2, \pm\sqrt{2}\}} \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_q} \chi_q\Bigl((s + 2)\bigl(x^3 - 2(s^2 - 2)^2 x + (s^2 - 2)^2\bigr)\Bigr), \tag{6.5}$$
and analogously on $L_2(X'_{+, -2, 1})$ and along the circle correspondence $C_r$ ($s^2 + s'^2 = 4$).

1. **Proof of $\rho(X_{+, -2, 1, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) = 14$ and $E$-Weil polynomials at $p = 7$ (`D6`):** Exact summation of (6.5) for $q = 7^k$ ($k = 1, 2, 3, 4$) gives
   $$\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_{7^k} \mid L_1\bigr) = (4,\, -96,\, 1156,\, -1368), \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_{7^k} \mid L_2\bigr) = (-4,\, -96,\, -1156,\, -1368) = (-1)^k \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_{7^k} \mid L_1\bigr)$$
   (the $(-1)^k$ twist independently confirming $C_f$, since $2 \pm 3 \in \{5, 6\}$ are non-squares in $\mathbb{F}_7$). On the $8$-dimensional orthogonal space $L_1$, Newton's identities yield elementary symmetric functions $(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4) = \bigl(\frac{4}{7}, \frac{8}{7}, \frac{12}{7}, \frac{10}{7}\bigr)$ for $7^{-1}\operatorname{Frob}_7$. Because an orthogonal transformation of determinant $-1$ in dimension $8$ has anti-palindromic characteristic polynomial ($e_4 = 0$), $e_4 = \frac{10}{7} \ne 0$ forces $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(7^{-1}\operatorname{Frob}_7 \mid L_1) = +1$ unconditionally, giving
   $$P_7(T) = T^8 - \frac{4}{7}T^7 + \frac{8}{7}T^6 - \frac{12}{7}T^5 + \frac{10}{7}T^4 - \frac{12}{7}T^3 + \frac{8}{7}T^2 - \frac{4}{7}T + 1.$$
   At $\mathfrak{p}_{7,1} = (7, \sqrt{2} - 3)$, check `D6` computes the $C_r$-traces $r_7 = (-16, -176)$ ($k = 1, 2$), giving $E$-power sums $p_1 = \frac{2 - 4\sqrt{2}}{7}$, $p_2 = \frac{-48 - 44\sqrt{2}}{49}$, $e_2 = \frac{6 + 2\sqrt{2}}{7}$, and unique $\det_E(g_{1,7}) = +1$ (verified by $2\operatorname{Re}_{\mathbb{Q}}(p_3) = \frac{1156}{343} = t_3/7^3$), so $P_7(T) = P_{g_{1,7}}(T)\,\overline{P_{g_{1,7}}}(T)$ with
   $$P_{g_{1,7}}(T) = T^4 + \frac{-2 + 4\sqrt{2}}{7}T^3 + \frac{6 + 2\sqrt{2}}{7}T^2 + \frac{-2 + 4\sqrt{2}}{7}T + 1, \qquad P_{g_{1,7}}(1)\,P_{g_{1,7}}(-1) = 3\left(\frac{4 + 6\sqrt{2}}{7}\right)^2.$$
   Passing via Newton's recurrence to the fourth power $g_{4,7} := g_{1,7}^4$ yields (`D6`)
   $$P_{g_{4,7}}(T) = T^4 + \frac{684 + 1264\sqrt{2}}{2401}T^3 + \frac{3622 - 608\sqrt{2}}{2401}T^2 + \frac{684 + 1264\sqrt{2}}{2401}T + 1, \tag{6.6}$$
   whose compressed quadratic $z^2 + \frac{684 + 1264\sqrt{2}}{2401}z + \frac{-1180 - 608\sqrt{2}}{2401} = 0$ ($z = T + T^{-1}$) has discriminant $D_z(g_{4,7})$ satisfying $7^8 D_z(g_{4,7}) = 14995968 + 7568384\sqrt{2}$, $N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(7^8 D_z(g_{4,7})) = 2^{18} \cdot 7^8 \cdot 73$, and $P_{g_{4,7}}(1)P_{g_{4,7}}(-1) = 3\bigl(\frac{608 - 96\sqrt{2}}{343}\bigr)^2$. Because $73 \parallel N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(7^8 D_z(g_{4,7}))$ while $3$ is unramified at $73$, $D_z(g_{4,7}) \notin E^{\times 2}$ and $3 \notin E(\sqrt{D_z(g_{4,7})})^{\times 2}$, so $P_{g_{4,7}}(T)$ (and hence $P_{g_{1,7}}(T)$) is **irreducible over $E$ with Galois group $D_4$**, and $P_7(T)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $P_7(T) \notin \mathbb{Z}[T]$ ($\frac{4}{7} \notin \mathbb{Z}$; likewise $\frac{684 + 1264\sqrt{2}}{2401} \notin \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$), **no root of $P_7(T)$ is a root of unity**, proving $\rho\bigl(X_{+, -2, 1, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigr) = 14$ and $T(X_{+, -2, 1}) = L_1$.

2. **Exact $p = 17$ Weil polynomials and local-to-global proof of $E(X_{+, -2, 1}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (`D6`–`D7`):** At $\mathfrak{p}_{17,1} = (17, \sqrt{2} - 6)$, check `D7` computes $t_{17} = (2, 106, -8356)$ and $r_{17} = (-56, 432)$, determining $\det_E(g_{1,17}) = +1$ (via $t_3 = -8356$), $P_{g_{1,17}}(T) = T^4 + \frac{-1 + 14\sqrt{2}}{17}T^3 + \frac{10 - 4\sqrt{2}}{17}T^2 + \frac{-1 + 14\sqrt{2}}{17}T + 1$, and for $g_{4,17} := g_{1,17}^4$:
   $$P_{g_{4,17}}(T) = T^4 + \frac{-70065 - 25320\sqrt{2}}{83521}T^3 + \frac{121696 - 19248\sqrt{2}}{83521}T^2 + \frac{-70065 - 25320\sqrt{2}}{83521}T + 1, \tag{6.7}$$
   with $17^8 D_z(g_{4,17}) = 21340682089 + 9978540432\sqrt{2}$, $N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(17^8 D_z(g_{4,17})) = 17^8 \cdot 23^3 \cdot 71^2 \cdot 599$ (**note $73 \nmid N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(17^8 D_z(g_{4,17}))$**), and $P_{g_{4,17}}(1)P_{g_{4,17}}(-1) = 3\bigl(\frac{133344 - 31632\sqrt{2}}{83521}\bigr)^2$. Since $599 \parallel N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(17^8 D_z(g_{4,17}))$, $P_{g_{4,17}}(T)$ is also **irreducible over $E$ with Galois group $D_4$**.

   Now let $\mathcal{A} := \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X_{+, -2, 1})) \supseteq E$. We exclude $\mathcal{A} \supsetneq E$ in four rigorous local-to-global steps:
   - *Step A (Reduction to CM fields $K/E$ of degree $[K : E] \in \{2, 4\}$ and algebraicity of $K$):* By Zarhin [1], $\mathcal{A}$ is a field with $[\mathcal{A} : E] \mid 4$. A totally real field $E' \supsetneq E$ of degree $8$ violates $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} T_{\sigma_+} \ge 2$, and if $[E' : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$ ($\dim_{E'} T = 2$), the Hodge real eigenspace $T_{\tau_+}$ is positive-definite of dimension $2$, forcing $\operatorname{Hg}(T)(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \operatorname{SO}(2)$ compact abelian and $\mathcal{A}$ CM. Hence if $\mathcal{A} \supsetneq E$, $\mathcal{A} = K$ is a **CM field** containing $E$ with $[K : E] \in \{2, 4\}$ and totally real subfield $K^+$. Every norm-$1$ element $\gamma \in U(1)_{K/K^+} = \{\gamma \in K^\times : \gamma\bar{\gamma} = 1\}$ is a rational Hodge isometry of $T(X_{+, -2, 1})$, hence is induced by an **algebraic correspondence** over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ by Buskin [18, Theorem 1.1]; since $U(1)_{K/K^+}$ spans $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ ($\gamma_t = \frac{1 + t\eta}{1 - t\eta}$ for $\bar{\eta} = -\eta \ne 0$, $t \in \mathbb{Q}$), all of $K$ acts on $T(X_{+, -2, 1})$ via algebraic cycles defined over a finite extension of $E$.
   - *Step B (Unramified Galois action on $K$ and centralization of $g_{4,p}$ over $E_\lambda$):* For every finite place $\lambda$ of $E$ above $\ell$, cycle classes give a faithful $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{E}/E)$-equivariant embedding $K \otimes_E E_\lambda \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{E_\lambda}(T(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda)$, inducing $\varrho \colon \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{E}/E) \to \operatorname{Aut}_E(K)$. At each good prime $\mathfrak{p}_{p,1}$ ($p \in \{7, 17\}$) and any $\lambda \nmid 119$, smooth proper base change implies that inertia $I_{\mathfrak{p}_{p,1}}$ acts trivially on $T(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda$, hence trivially on $K$, yielding a well-defined automorphism $\bar{\sigma}_p := \varrho(\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}_{p,1}}) \in \operatorname{Aut}_E(K)$. By Artin's lemma, $|\operatorname{Aut}_E(K)| = [K : K^{\operatorname{Aut}_E(K)}]$ divides $[K : E] \in \{2, 4\}$, so **$\bar{\sigma}_p^4 = \mathrm{id}_K$** for both $p = 7$ and $p = 17$. Consequently, $g_{4,p} = (p^{-1}\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}_{p,1}})^4$ commutes with the entire subalgebra $K \otimes_E E_\lambda \subset \operatorname{End}_{E_\lambda}(T(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda)$ for every $\lambda \nmid 119$.
   - *Step C (Local commutant at split primes and Bauer's theorem):* For $p \in \{7, 17\}$, let $M_p := E[T]/(P_{g_{4,p}}(T))$ and let $\widetilde{M}_p / E$ be its Galois closure ($\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{M}_p/E) \cong D_4$). Let $\lambda \nmid 119 \cdot \operatorname{disc}(K/E)$ be any prime of $E$ that **splits completely** in $\widetilde{M}_p$. Then $P_{g_{4,p}}(T)$ splits in $E_\lambda[T]$ into $4$ **distinct** linear factors ($\operatorname{disc}(P_{g_{4,p}}) \in E^\times$ is nonzero in $E_\lambda$), so $g_{4,p}$ is diagonalizable on the $4$-dimensional $E_\lambda$-space $T(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda$ with $4$ distinct eigenvalues in $E_\lambda$, and its centralizer in $\operatorname{End}_{E_\lambda}(T(1) \otimes_E E_\lambda)$ is isomorphic to $E_\lambda^4$. Since $K \otimes_E E_\lambda \cong \prod_{\mathfrak{Q} \mid \lambda} K_{\mathfrak{Q}}$ embeds as an $E_\lambda$-subalgebra of $E_\lambda^4$, each local factor field $K_{\mathfrak{Q}}$ admits a nonzero $E_\lambda$-algebra homomorphism $K_{\mathfrak{Q}} \to E_\lambda$, forcing $K_{\mathfrak{Q}} = E_\lambda$ for all $\mathfrak{Q} \mid \lambda$. Thus $\lambda$ splits completely in $K$, and hence in the Galois closure $\widetilde{K}$ of $K/E$. By Bauer's theorem (Chebotarev density applied to $\widetilde{K}\widetilde{M}_p / E$), $\widetilde{K} \subseteq \widetilde{M}_7 \cap \widetilde{M}_{17}$.
   - *Step D (Ramification at $73$ forces $\widetilde{K} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) \subset \mathbb{R}$):* For each $p \in \{7, 17\}$, the two roots $z_{1,p}, z_{2,p} \in E(\sqrt{D_z(g_{4,p})})$ of the compressed quadratic satisfy $(z_{1,p}^2 - 4)(z_{2,p}^2 - 4) = P_{g_{4,p}}(1)P_{g_{4,p}}(-1) \in 3(E^\times)^2$, so the unique biquadratic subfield $\widetilde{M}_p^{Z(D_4)}$ of $\widetilde{M}_p / E$ is $E\bigl(\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{D_z(g_{4,p})}\bigr)$, containing all three quadratic subfields $E(\sqrt{3})$, $E\bigl(\sqrt{D_z(g_{4,p})}\bigr)$, and $E\bigl(\sqrt{3 D_z(g_{4,p})}\bigr)$ of $\widetilde{M}_p / E$. Moreover, every normal subextension of $\widetilde{M}_7 / E$ not contained in $E(\sqrt{3})$ contains a quadratic subfield $F_0 \in \bigl\{E(\sqrt{D_z(g_{4,7})}),\, E(\sqrt{3 D_z(g_{4,7})})\bigr\}$. Since $73 \equiv 1 \pmod{8}$ splits in $E$ and $73 \parallel N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(7^8 D_z(g_{4,7}))$ (`D6`), there is a prime $\mathfrak{q}_{73} \mid 73$ of $E$ with $v_{\mathfrak{q}_{73}}(D_z(g_{4,7})) = 1$, at which both choices of $F_0$ ramify; yet $F_0 \subseteq \widetilde{K} \subseteq \widetilde{M}_{17}$ would have to be one of the three quadratic subfields of $\widetilde{M}_{17} / E$, all of which are **unramified** above $73$ since $73 \nmid 2 \cdot 3 \cdot N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(17^8 D_z(g_{4,17})) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 17^8 \cdot 23^3 \cdot 71^2 \cdot 599$ (`D7`). Therefore $\widetilde{K} \subseteq E(\sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) \subset \mathbb{R}$, which is **totally real** and contains no CM field $K$.

Thus $\mathcal{A} = E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. Finally, since $(X_{+, -2, 1}, X'_{+, -2, 1})$ is a specialization of the algebraic family over $c \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 4\}$ with $\rho = 14$ and $E(X_+) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, upper-semicontinuity of $\rho$ and Hodge loci implies that $\rho = 14$ and $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ hold for very general $(a_1, b_1)$ as well (and $j(s) = \frac{1728(s^2 - 2)^2}{(s^2 - 2)^2 - c}$ determines $c$ up to countably many fibrations as in [4, Lemma 6.3]). This completes Theorem 4. $\square$

### 7.2 Limitations and open questions

1. **Two-dimensional non-square-type families, Deligne correspondences $(\Gamma)$, and fourth powers $X_+^4$ ($\rho = 14$).** While Theorem 4(c) establishes the full rational Hodge conjecture on $X_+^a \times X'^b_+$ for $a + b \le 3$, all-powers HC at $\rho = 14$ ($m = 4$, $\operatorname{disc} \varphi_E \equiv 3 \notin E^{\times 2}$) requires the $E$-determinant class $\bigwedge^4_E T(X_+) \subset H^8(X_+^4, \mathbb{Q})$, which remains open (governed conditionally by cycle $(\Gamma)$ on $X_+ \times B^2$ in [12, Theorem C]). Moreover, as emphasized in §1, $X_{+, a_1, b_1}$ varies with $1$ effective complex modulus $c = -\frac{27 b_1^2}{4 a_1^3} \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 4\}$ inside the $2$-dimensional non-square-type period domain $\mathfrak{H} \times \mathfrak{H}$, so the full two-dimensional problem of [12, §10]—constructing a two-parameter non-square-type K3 family together with algebraic correspondences to its Deligne models—remains open (deforming $g(x, t)$ in Theorem 3(a) while retaining only $2 I_0^*$ yields $2$-parameter $\rho = 10$ families, whereas $2$-parameter $\rho = 14$ deformations require splitting $A_2^2$ outside Weierstrass scaling).
2. **Simultaneous Witt index $(1, 1)$ at $\rho = 16$: abstract $E$-splitting versus geometric specializations.** Check `D5` constructs an explicit orthogonal splitting of the abstract $E$-space $(L_1, \varphi_E) \cong_E W_{\mathrm{ns}}$ at $\alpha = 3 - \sqrt{2}$ (compare [22, 23]) for which both $6$-dimensional trace complements in $L_1$ and $L_2$ have rational Witt index $1$, so Floccari's theorem [5] applies to neither partner. Determining which $(1, 1)$ Witt-index planes are geometrically attained as specializations of $X_{+, a_1, b_1}$ over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, and proving all-powers HC at any such $\rho = 16$ point, both remain open.
3. **Imported results.** Our proofs rely on Zarhin [1], Varesco [2], Floccari [5, Theorem 5.11], Buskin [18], Scharlau [21], rStation 90 [19, Proposition 7.1], and rStation 97 [4, Theorem B].
