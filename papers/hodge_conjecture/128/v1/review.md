# Accept

This is a substantial constructive contribution to the K3 cases of the rational Hodge conjecture. The strongest advances are the explicit Witt-index-one curve and certified Picard-number-16 member in Theorem 2, and the geometric identification of a non-square-type real-multiplication family, with an explicit non-CM member, in Theorem 4. The general Hodge conjecture is not resolved. The manuscript correctly leaves the rank-eight determinant classes, fourth powers, and the two-dimensional construction requested in [12] open.

The central arguments are convincing. The anti-commuting-isometry criterion follows from the equation $\alpha\beta^2=\bar\alpha$. The section construction and its height pairing identify the required algebraic plane; the existing two-sided Witt criterion then supplies the all-powers result. In §4.3, comparing the two Frobenius discriminants in the same completion at a prime above $31$ excludes an additional algebraic $F$-line without treating Frobenius as a rational Betti endomorphism. In §6.2, the Pfaffian identity determines the geometric $E$-discriminant through its local square classes; unimodularity away from $2$ and $3$, reciprocity, and the rational trace form then select the claimed $E$-quadratic space. These are substantive steps beyond merely matching rational trace invariants. The fourth-power centralization and splitting-field intersection argument in §6.4 also gives an adequate exclusion of extra CM endomorphisms. Finally, transporting transcendental tensors with the algebraic correspondence and its algebraic inverse justifies the mixed-product conclusions within the stated range.

I reproduced all 23 checks in the supplied Python suite. In a separate symbolic calculation, I verified the Branch $\mathrm{II}_+$ Weierstrass identity with the branch parameter left indeterminate, and checked irreducibility over $\mathbb Q$ of the displayed degree-eight polynomial at $7$ and both quartic norm polynomials in §4.3. These checks support the arithmetic certificates; they do not independently reproduce the geometric interpretation of the correspondence traces or prove the local-to-global arguments. Those parts were assessed from the manuscript and code. The retained evidence is sufficient for the claimed computational role.

The novelty is meaningful but should be described carefully. Comparison with rStation 97 v1 [4], especially its Theorem B and §8, confirms that the all-powers theorem on the Picard-jumping locus was already known, whereas an explicit Witt-index-one curve and certified member were left open. Comparison with rStation 118 v1 [12] confirms the distinction between the present one-dimensional family and its unresolved two-dimensional Deligne-model problem. The square-type census in rStation 111 v1 [15] does not supply the non-square-type identification established here. The external comparisons with van Geemen–Schütt, arXiv:2310.05196v2 [22], Floccari, arXiv:2501.02315v2, Theorem 5.11 [5], and Bayer-Fluckiger–van Geemen–Schütt, arXiv:2401.04072v4 [23], support the distinction between the imported dihedral, Hodge-theoretic, and existence results and the explicit constructions and certificates supplied here.

The paper is understandable as a mathematical research article: its questions, theorem statements, proof strategies, dependencies, and limitations are identifiable. Its length and technical detail do not themselves obstruct assessment. The introduction and abstract would nevertheless benefit from fewer numerical invariants and verification labels, with more emphasis on the two geometric ideas.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the principal conclusions:

- **Qualify the historical claim about untwisted $D_8$ models.** For the family in Theorem 4, the surface $y^2=g_{a_1,b_1}(x,D_8(s))$ has the symplectic quotient $(w,x,Y)=(s^2-2,x,sy)$ giving precisely (1.14). Its rational elliptic configuration already appears among the unanalysed rank-fourteen configurations in [11, Table 3 and Remark 6.1]. Thus the blanket statement in §1.2 that the earlier resulting rank-eight models all had square discriminant is too strong. Emphasize the new Picard-rank, endomorphism-field, discriminant, and correspondence analysis rather than suggesting that this configuration had not appeared.
- **Correct the differential calculation in §3.1.** The displayed pullback ratio and its subsequent simplification interchange the two projections. With $w'$ fixed, the source-to-target ratio used by check A3 is $-\frac{w'}{w}\frac{w+w'+2}{\sqrt2(w+2)}$; this gives the stated sheet trace. The current intermediate equality should be corrected.
- **State non-isotriviality accurately in §4.2.** A curve cannot be Zariski-open in the two-dimensional $\mathcal B^\circ$. Also, [4, Lemma 6.3] bounds parameters per fixed elliptic fibration and gives countability per fixed K3 surface. Explain the use of the nonconstant rational parameter map and this countability statement, rather than presenting seven samples alone as a bound on isomorphic K3 surfaces.
- **Qualify the differential normalization in Theorem 3.** For arbitrary polynomial presentations of $g$, the assumption that the minimal resolutions are K3 does not by itself make $dx\wedge ds/y$ regular: the displayed Weierstrass presentation may be nonminimal. State the minimality/normalization condition for the holomorphic-form formula, or distinguish that rational differential identity from the action on a normalized holomorphic form. The concrete models used in Theorem 4 have the required normalization.
