# A four-dimensional family of K3 surfaces with real multiplication by $\mathbf Q(\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1})$ and the Hodge conjecture for their squares

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-19T09:13:54.333264Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/140/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For a K3 surface whose transcendental Hodge structure has real multiplication by a totally real field $E\ne\mathbf Q$, the rational Hodge conjecture for $X\times X$ asks for algebraic cycles inducing the elements of $E$. Van Geemen and Schütt constructed such cycles from dihedral covers defined by Dickson polynomials $p_{n,a}$ with a constant parameter $a$. For the cubic field $E_7=\mathbf Q(\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1})$ this gives a 3-dimensional family with Picard number 4. We let the Dickson parameter be a coordinate. Consider the degree-7 hypersurfaces $X_Q=\{p_{7,\alpha}(X)=Q(\alpha,t,s)\}$ in $\mathbf P(1,2,1,3)$, where $Q$ is a weighted form of degree 7. For general $Q$, $X_Q$ is a quasi-smooth K3 surface with an $A_1$ and an $A_2$ point. The projection to $\mathbf P(2,1,3)$ has degree 7 and monodromy $D_7$. The pairs of adjacent roots in its fibres form a surface $Z_1$ in the square of the minimal resolution $Y$, whose action on the transcendental lattice satisfies $x^3+x^2-2x-1=0$. A Jacobian-ring computation shows that the family has 4 moduli, the largest possible dimension for real multiplication by a cubic field with Picard number 4. A Hodge-locus argument then shows that for very general $Q$ the Picard number is 4 and the endomorphism field is exactly $E_7$. Hence the rational Hodge conjecture holds for $Y\times Y$ for very general $Q$, and for every $Q$ in the open set with endomorphism field $E_7$. Quasi-smoothness is certified by a Gröbner basis computation modulo a prime, and the moduli count by an exact rank bound.
