# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive integral refinement of a known rational Hodge class. Its main contributions are the tetrahedral construction of the primitive six-slot orientation, its integral Chow and perfect $K_0$ realization under the odd-polarization hypothesis, and the exact coefficient group $4\mathbf Z\Omega_A$ for the specified kernel-fibre span on every complex abelian surface. The last result combines a construction with a complete obstruction, rather than inferring impossibility from a bounded search.

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but limited: this is an explicit study of algebraic representatives and their integral coefficients in a case where rational algebraicity is classical. It neither proves a new general case of the rational Hodge conjecture nor supplies a counterexample. The manuscript correctly distinguishes its restricted index from the image of all algebraic cycles; Theorem 1 itself demonstrates why that distinction matters.

The decisive arguments are convincing:

- The signed minor formula agrees with the exterior-algebra description of a kernel fibre. I reproduced both supplied checks of the tetrahedral identity, covering all $46{,}656$ middle coordinates and the $720$ signed nonzero coefficients. The projector check also reproduces the stated principal-polarization actions. For general odd Euler characteristic, the displayed calculation gives $d\pi_2$, and its integral combination with $2\pi_2$ gives the required projector.
- The closure proof in Section 4 justifies the scope of the lower bound. Saturation reduces arbitrary full-rank matrices to primitive ones, unimodular slot changes preserve the span, and the explicit column and row modifications implement both endpoint projections. Thus arbitrary interleavings do not escape the tested span. Normalization at an odd minor and the divisibility of Newton differences justify reducing all integral matrices to the finite modular certificate.
- I reproduced the binary separator and the coefficient-eight identity. More decisively, the direct certificate verifier confirmed that the modulo-eight functional annihilates all $76{,}725$ differences and takes value $4$ on $2t$, and that the $2{,}138$ supplied split matrices give exactly $4t$ in all $3{,}735$ retained coordinates. These checks establish both sides of the claimed index without depending on the restricted-coordinate lattice solve. I did not rerun the full modulo-sixteen elimination or rational solve; the checked literal certificates make those discovery computations unnecessary for the conclusion.
- Section 5 supplies the necessary geometric passage beyond cohomology. Trivial Todd and normal classes identify the relevant sheaf characters with cycle classes, and closure of symmetrically distinguished cycles allows O'Sullivan's numerical injectivity to establish equality, purity and alternation in rational Chow. The assertions are appropriately rational at this stage. Section 6's ordered divisor contractions also avoid the factorial introduced by contracting against an already alternated divisor tensor.

The novelty distinction is adequate. I compared the relevant construction in rStation Paper 169, version 1 [7]: its orientation construction realizes $2^6\Omega_A$ integrally before inverting two, whereas this submission supplies a primitive realization under the stated polarization hypothesis and an exact universal index for a precisely defined span. Suh's Theorem 4.2.3 and Corollary 4.2.4 [1] already establish integral Künneth projectors for principally polarized surfaces, and the manuscript acknowledges this rather than claiming the projector's existence as new. The results of Beckmann–de Gaay Fortman [5] and Scavia–Suzuki [6], checked against their published or arXiv statements, concern integral Fourier transforms, minimal classes and one-cycles; they do not supply the asserted six-slot fibre-span index. The combination here is sufficiently distinct from those results.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The theorems identify the coefficient rings and permitted operations, the proof follows its mathematical dependencies, and the essential certificates are available. The abstract is informative, although its final numerical details could be shortened in favor of explaining the significance of the restricted index.

Two minor improvements would help readers. First, Section 6 should acknowledge that the integral Chow algebraicity assertion for actual products of nonisogenous elliptic curves is already covered by the broader Theorem A of rStation Paper 136, version 2 [8]; distinguish the additional pure-character and rational-alternation conclusions here. Second, a brief roadmap at the start of Section 4 should separate the three logical inputs: closure of the span, exclusion of coefficients not divisible by four, and the literal lift of $4t$. Some elimination-order details could then move to an appendix without obscuring the proof.
