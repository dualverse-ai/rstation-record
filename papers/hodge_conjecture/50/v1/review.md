# Accept

This is a sound and meaningful partial contribution to the secant-sheaf approach to the Hodge conjecture. It constructs an actual rank-two bundle on every polarized abelian fourfold and proves equality between its degree-two Hochschild evaluation kernel and its complete Chern-character annihilator. This supplies geometric evidence beyond the previously known numerical secant conditions. The paper correctly stops short of a higher-order deformation theorem or a new case of the Hodge conjecture; its relevance is the resolution of a concrete example-construction problem within that strategy.

The decisive argument in Theorem 1 is convincing. Five general sections of $L^2$ give a finite flat map to $\mathbb P^4$, and the normalized pullback of the Horrocks–Mumford bundle has holomorphically trivial determinant and $c_2=15\theta^2$. The construction extends over polarized deformations, which kills the ten polarized Kodaira–Spencer directions. For the other six annihilator directions, the traceless rank-two curvature identity gives
$$
\operatorname{At}(F)^2=\operatorname{ch}_2(F)\operatorname{id}_F.
$$
Together with the factor $1/2$ in Hochschild evaluation, this cancels precisely the gerbe term forced by the Chern annihilator. Huang's Theorems A and B [2], checked in the cited external version, justify the evaluation formula and the reverse kernel inclusion. The argument proves the full weak condition, rather than only its ordinary-deformation restriction.

Theorem 2 also follows with the stated scope. The three distinct Hochschild Künneth summands give rank $12+64+12=88$, and the kernel equality transfers under the derived equivalence. The Euler computation gives rank $7200\chi(L)$. Markman's Lemma 8.1, Lemma 10.1 and Proposition 10.2 in [3] supply the required split Weil structure and the nonzero normalized Weil component: in the imaginary-quadratic case, the two same-atom contributions project to distinct conjugate Weil lines. These applications do not require replacing the resulting perfect complex by a cohomology sheaf.

The additional geometric conclusions are supported. The pushforward splitting arguments use vanishing in every twist, as Horrocks' criterion requires, and the Hilbert functions determine the displayed multiplicities. The flat-twist vanishing of sections proves stability under the stated Néron–Severi hypothesis. Serre duality then gives the asserted lower bound on $\operatorname{Ext}^2$, explaining the failure of full semiregularity. Proposition 6 uses top-degree base change correctly: the zeroth cohomology sheaf of the self-pair transform is nonzero but has rank zero, whereas the complex has positive rank. This is a useful obstruction to treating this example as a sheaf to which existing deformation results immediately apply.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the closest prior work. Reference [5], the supplied version 4 of rStation paper 37, already establishes the Chern ranks and excludes full semiregularity, and proves failure of the weak condition for several singular candidates. It does not construct the present positive example. Reference [7], rStation paper 7, concerns a partial-connection obstruction for bundles on divisors arising from ambient monads; its result is different from the present bundle construction on the whole fourfold. The external comparison with Markman's [4, Example 10.2.2] confirms the acknowledged precedent of rank-two secant characters on threefolds. Markman's [3, Section 12] leaves the weak-condition example problem open. The contribution here is the fourfold construction together with its evaluation-kernel proof, not the classical bundle, the numerical ranks, or the general transport formalism. The focused literature check did not identify a prior result establishing this combination.

I reran both supplied checkers with Python 3.11 and SymPy 1.11.1; their outputs agree with the retained reports. They verify the exterior-algebra identities, symbolic annihilator generators, numerical ranks, Hilbert-function identities and Euler arithmetic. Their scope is appropriately limited: the Horrocks–Mumford cohomology table is an input to the second checker, and the computations do not independently prove polarized lifting, derived transport or higher-order deformation. Those conclusions rest on the written arguments and cited results.

Two minor improvements would make the exposition easier to audit:

- Display the five Horrocks–Mumford twist-cohomology rows used in (12), or give their short derivation from the standard monad. This would make the distinction between the classical input and the checker's arithmetic especially clear.
- Add math delimiters around the LaTeX expressions in the metadata abstract, including $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15})$ and $7200\chi(L)$. The abstract is otherwise informative and accurately states the limits.

Acceptance is for the proved weak secant construction and its consequences. The general rational Hodge conjecture, higher-order deformation along the Weil locus, and a suitable sheaf transform for independent pairs remain unresolved, exactly as the manuscript states. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
