# Discriminant obstructions to Ext-generated equivariant objects for Weil classes in dimensions $g\equiv2\bmod4$

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction
Let $X$ be a complex abelian variety of dimension $g$. Markman [1, 2] builds an object on $X\times\hat X$ whose class remains Hodge along a family of abelian varieties of Weil type, and deforms it with a semiregularity theorem; Perry [3] proved the semiregularity theorem for equivariant objects in the generality needed here. For split Weil sixfolds, semiregularity is verified through the following mechanism ([2, Prop. 11.5], [1, Lemma 8.3.7, Cor. 8.3.12]):

(M) for a finite group $G$ acting on $D(X)$, the evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}\colon HH^*(X)\to\mathrm{Ext}^*(F,F)^G$ is surjective.

In [4] (rStation hodge_conjecture_131, v2) we showed that (M) identifies $\mathrm{Ext}^*(F,F)^G$ with the Gorenstein algebra of $\mathrm{ch}(F)$, so that $\chi(F,F)=|G|\,e(\mathrm{ch}\,F)$ [4, Thm 3.1]. On a one-place secant plane, $e=-(1+(-1)^g)$, while $\chi(F,F)$ has sign $(-1)^{g/2}$. Hence (M) is impossible for such classes when $4\mid g$, and when $g\equiv2\bmod 4$ it forces $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$ [4, Cor. 4.3]. For $g=6$ and $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$, [4, §5] gives explicit secant sheaves on genus-six Jacobians (with $G=1$) and excludes (M) for minimal classes and for rank-$\pm1$ sheaves of specific types, using effective cycles. Those arguments say nothing about objects of higher rank or other types, or about other $g$ and $K$.

**Setting.** Let $(X,\Theta)$ be a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension $g\equiv2\bmod4$. Fix $q\in\mathbf Q_{>0}$, $a\in\mathbf Q$ and $\zeta=a+i\sqrt q$. The one-place secant plane of $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-q})$, twisted by the B-field $a\Theta$, is $e^{a\Theta}P_q$ with $P_q=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf R}(\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Im}\,e^{i\sqrt q\Theta})$ [4, §4]. Its integral classes are
$$v_\lambda=\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\lambda e^{\zeta\Theta}\bigr),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbf C,\quad \mathrm{Re}(\lambda\zeta^k)\in\mathbf Z\ (0\le k\le g),$$
since $\Theta^k/k!$ is primitive. Put $n(v)=|\lambda|^2$. Let $G\subset X\times\hat X$ be a finite subgroup acting on $D(X)$ as in [4, Thm 3.1], and $F\in D(X)$ a $G$-equivariant object with $\mathrm{Ext}^{<0}(F,F)=0$ and $\mathrm{ch}(F)=v_\lambda\neq0$. The source $F$ is untwisted; Brauer twists appear only on the descended variety $N$ of §3.

**Theorem A.** Let $g\ge6$. If $F$ satisfies (M), then $4\tau_0^2$ divides $n(v)$ (that is, $n(v)/(4\tau_0^2)\in\mathbf Z$), where $\tau_0\ge1$ is the integer of Proposition 3.1. Equivalently, $|G|/\bigl((4q)^{g/2}\tau_0^2\bigr)$ is an integer.

**Theorem B.** Let $g\ge6$. If $F$ satisfies (M), then $4d\tau_0^2$ divides $n(v)$, where $d$ is the product of the primes $p$ with $v_p(q)$ odd.

**Corollary.** Let $g=6$, $q=3/4$ and $a=1/2$, so $\zeta=\zeta_6$ and $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$. The classes form the lattice $\Lambda=\mathbf Zv_1\oplus\mathbf Zw\cong\mathbf Z[\zeta_6]$ of [4, §5.3], with $n=4\,\mathrm{Nm}/3$. On every principally polarized sixfold, Theorem A gives $3\tau_0^2\mid\mathrm{Nm}(v)$ and Theorem B gives $9\tau_0^2\mid\mathrm{Nm}(v)$.

**Theorem C.** In the setting of the Corollary with $\rho(X)=1$, (M) also fails for $3v_1$, $3w$, $3(v_1+w)$ (all classes of norm $9$), $6v_1$ (norm $36$), $12v_1$ (norm $144$), and $-6v_1+3w$, $-3v_1+6w$, $9v_1+3w$, $9v_1+6w$ (norm $63$). The classes with $9\mid\mathrm{Nm}\le81$ that remain are listed in §7.

Theorems A and B use no hypothesis on $\mathrm{NS}(X)$. They apply to Jacobians, to products and to varieties with extra endomorphisms, which is where one would try to build an (M)-object (§8). Theorem B implies Theorem A, but its proof uses Theorem A. For each $(q,a)$, Theorem A confines (M) to the sublattice $\{n\in4\mathbf Z\}$. In most examples we computed this excludes the small classes (§2). For $q=1/4$, $a=1/2$, however, every class in our search box already has $4\mid n$, and Theorem A excludes nothing there. In the Corollary, Theorem A contains the conclusion of [4, Prop. 5.2] that minimal classes do not satisfy (M). Theorem B contains that of [4, Prop. 5.2′] (rank $\pm1$, very general genus-six Jacobian). Neither needs the hypotheses of [4] on the type of object. The Picard number $1$ enters only through Proposition 3.1(c) and the enumeration of Theorem C. Under $\rho=1$ the residue classes with $9\mid\mathrm{Nm}$ are not all decided (§7). Our tests do not settle them, and genuine pull-backs $\rho^*E$ along quotients by Lagrangian subgroups of $X[3]$, which have isogeny data of the same shape, pass every test we use.

**Method.** By Perry [3, Lemma 5.18, Prop. 5.19], $D(X)^G\simeq D(N,\gamma)$ for an abelian variety $N$ isogenous to $X$ and a Brauer class $\gamma$. We show (Proposition 3.1) that the forgetful functor acts on Chern characters by $\mathrm{ch}(F)=\tau_0e^{\sigma}\,\iota(\mathrm{ch}^B E)$. Here $\iota$ is the isogeny transport, $\sigma\in H^2\oplus H^6\oplus\cdots$, and $\tau_0=bs$ is the degree of $N\to X/H$ times the dimension of an irreducible representation. The twisted Chern character $\mathrm{ch}^B E$ is integral, so polynomials in it that are invariant under $e^{\beta}$ must be integral after transport. The discriminant of $v_\lambda$ is $q\,n\,\Theta^2$. Theorem A is the integrality of $\int_N(\Delta/2)^{g/2}/(g/2)!$. Theorem B adds the divisibility of $\theta_N^2/2$ for a polarization of type $(1|d_2|\dots|d_g)$, through the identity $v_p(P_N)=\frac g2L+(\frac g2-1)t$ of §6. Theorem C is a finite enumeration over all isogeny data, which we show is exhaustive.

**Relation to [4].** Paper [4] supplies the input $|G|$ (Thm 3.1, Cor. 4.3) and, at $g=6$, excludes (M) for minimal classes and for rank-$\pm1$ sheaves of specified types on very general genus-six Jacobians, by effective-cycle arguments. Here the object $F$ is arbitrary (any rank, any complex with $\mathrm{Ext}^{<0}=0$, any $G$). The exclusions come from integrality on the descended variety $N$ rather than from cycles, and they hold for every $g\equiv2\bmod4$ with $g\ge6$, every $K$ and every principally polarized $X$. The transport (Proposition 3.1) is new.

## 2. Classes, discriminant and the order of $G$
**Lemma 2.1.** For $v=v_\lambda$ as above, with $n=|\lambda|^2$:
(i) $\Delta(v):=c_1(v)^2-2\,\mathrm{ch}_0(v)\,\mathrm{ch}_2(v)=q\,n\,\Theta^2$;
(ii) $\chi(v,v)=-\tfrac12(4q)^{g/2}\,n$;
(iii) if $F$ satisfies (M), then $|G|=(4q)^{g/2}n/4$.
*Proof.* (i) Write $\lambda=\alpha+i\beta$. Then $\mathrm{ch}_0=\alpha$, $c_1=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda\zeta)\Theta$ and $\mathrm{ch}_2=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda\zeta^2)\Theta^2/2$. Expanding $\mathrm{Re}(\lambda\zeta)^2-\alpha\,\mathrm{Re}(\lambda\zeta^2)$ with $\zeta=a+i\sqrt q$ gives $q(\alpha^2+\beta^2)$, independently of $a$. (ii) $v^\vee=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda e^{-\zeta\Theta})$, and $\mathrm{Re}A\,\mathrm{Re}B=\frac12\mathrm{Re}(AB)+\frac12\mathrm{Re}(A\bar B)$. Here $\int_XAB=\lambda^2\int_X1=0$ and $\int_XA\bar B=n\int_Xe^{-2i\sqrt q\Theta}=n(-2i\sqrt q)^g=-n(4q)^{g/2}$, since $\int\Theta^g/g!=1$ and $g\equiv2\bmod4$. (iii) Multiplication by $e^{a\Theta}$ normalizes the action of $W=H^1(\mathcal O_X)\oplus H^0(T_X)$ on $H^*(X,\mathbf C)$, so it preserves the ranks $r_k$ and $\chi$ [4, §5.3]. Hence [4, Cor. 4.3] applies and gives $\chi(F,F)=-2|G|$. Combine with (ii). □

Formula (ii) agrees with [4, Prop. 4.2]. `g8formulas.py` checks (i) and (ii) symbolically in $\alpha,\beta,a,q$ for $g\in\{6,10,14\}$. `g8scan.py` lists the lattice classes in a box for several $(g,q,a)$ and confirms that the criterion (5.1) holds exactly when $4\mid n$.

**Examples.** (1) $g=6$, $q=3/4$, $a=1/2$, so $\zeta=\zeta_6$. The lattice has basis $v_1=\Theta+\Theta^2/2-\Theta^4/24-\Theta^5/120$ and $w=1-\Theta^2/2-\Theta^3/6+\Theta^5/120+\Theta^6/720$ [4, §5.3]. Here $xv_1+yw$ has rank $y$, $c_1=x\Theta$, $n=\frac43\mathrm{Nm}$ with $\mathrm{Nm}=x^2-xy+y^2$, and $|G|=9\,\mathrm{Nm}$; `delta_nm.py` checks $\Delta=\mathrm{Nm}\,\Theta^2$. The primes $3$ and $2$ give $v_3(n)=v_3(\mathrm{Nm})-1$ and $v_2(n)=2+v_2(\mathrm{Nm})$. So Theorem A reads $3\tau_0^2\mid\mathrm{Nm}$, and Theorem B, with $d=3$, reads $9\tau_0^2\mid\mathrm{Nm}$. (2) $q=1$, $a=0$, $K=\mathbf Q(i)$. The integrality conditions are $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf Z$ and $n=\alpha^2+\beta^2$. Theorem A requires $\alpha\equiv\beta\equiv0\bmod2$, so $v\in2\Lambda$; here $d=1$, and Theorem B gives nothing more. (3) $q=2$, $a=0$: the conditions are $\alpha\in\mathbf Z$ and $\beta\sqrt2=c\in\mathbf Z$, so $n=\alpha^2+c^2/2$. Theorem A requires $2\mid\alpha$ and $4\mid c$. Theorem B ($d=2$) requires $4\mid\alpha$ and $4\mid c$.

## 3. Descent and transport of Chern characters
Let $G\subset X\times\hat X$ be finite, acting on $D(X)$ by $(t,L)\cdot E=t_*E\otimes L$ as in [3, Thm 1.2]. In general this is an action of a central extension, and Perry linearizes it on a $\mu_n$-gerbe $\mathcal X\to X$ [3, §5.3]. Let $H\subset X$ be the image of $G$ under the first projection and $K=G\cap(0\times\hat X)$, so $1\to K\to G\to H\to1$. The group $H$ acts freely on $X$ by translations. Let $\rho\colon X\to Y=X/H$ be the quotient isogeny.

**Descent [3, Lemma 5.18, Prop. 5.19].** There are a connected finite étale cover $\nu\colon N\to Y$, a class $\gamma\in\mathrm{Br}(N)$ and a $Y$-linear equivalence $D(X)^G\simeq D(N,\gamma)$. Since $N$ is connected and finite étale over an abelian variety, $N$ is an abelian variety and $\nu$ is an isogeny; let $b=\deg\nu$. The equivalence comes from Hotchkiss's theorem [6, Prop. 2.12]. The stack $\mathcal X/G$ is a gerbe with finite inertia over $Y$, $N$ is (part of) the moduli space of its simple sheaves, and the equivalence is the Fourier–Mukai transform whose kernel is the universal family on $(\mathcal X/G)\times_Y\mathcal N$, where $\mathcal N\to N$ is the $\mathbf G_m$-gerbe of class $\gamma$ [3, proof of Prop. 5.19]. Over a point $y\in Y$, the fibre of $\mathcal X/G$ is the classifying stack of a finite group $K'$ (a central extension of $K$ by $\mu_n$). The points of $N$ over $y$ are irreducible representations $V$ of $K'$, and the universal family at such a point is $V$ itself [6, proof of Prop. 2.12]. The dual group $\hat G$ acts on $D(N,\gamma)$ through $\mathrm{Aut}^0$: $\hat H$ acts by tensoring with line bundles pulled back from $Y$, and the image of $\hat G$ in $\hat K$ acts by covering automorphisms of $N$ over $Y$ [3, Prop. 5.19(4), (5)].

Let $\Phi\colon D(N,\gamma)\to D(X)$ be this equivalence followed by the functor that forgets the equivariant structure. Fix a rational B-field $B\in H^2(N,\mathbf Q)$ for $\gamma$. For $E\in D(N,\gamma)$, the twisted Chern character $\mathrm{ch}^B(E)$ of Huybrechts–Stellari [5, Prop. 1.2] is additive and lies in $\exp(B)\bigoplus H^{p,p}$. For a locally free twisted sheaf it is the Chern character of a $C^\infty$ vector bundle. Twisted sheaves have finite locally free resolutions [5, proof of Prop. 1.2], so $\mathrm{ch}^B(E)$ is the Chern character of a virtual $C^\infty$ bundle. On a torus, Chern characters and Chern classes of virtual bundles are integral, since $H^*(N,\mathbf Z)=\wedge^*H^1(N,\mathbf Z)$ is torsion-free and the Chern character is integral on products of circles. Put
$$\iota=\rho^*\circ(\nu^*)^{-1}\colon H^*(N,\mathbf Q)\to H^*(X,\mathbf Q),$$
a ring isomorphism. Since $\int_X\rho^*z=|H|\int_Yz$ and $\int_N\nu^*z=b\int_Yz$,
$$\textstyle\int_X\iota(z)=\frac{|H|}{b}\int_Nz\qquad(z\in H^{2g}(N,\mathbf Q)). \tag{3.1}$$

**Proposition 3.1.** There is a class $\tau\in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X,\mathbf Q)$ such that, for every $E\in D(N,\gamma)$,
$$\mathrm{ch}(\Phi E)=\tau\cdot\iota(\mathrm{ch}^BE). \tag{3.2}$$
Moreover: (a) $\tau_0=bs$ for a positive integer $s$, the dimension of the representation $V$ at any point of $N$; (b) $\tau^\vee\tau=\tau_0^2$ and $|G|=\tau_0^2|H|/b$, where $(\cdot)^\vee$ multiplies $H^{2k}$ by $(-1)^k$; hence $\tau=\tau_0\exp(\sigma)$ with $\sigma\in\bigoplus_kH^{4k+2}$; (c) if $\rho(X)=1$, then $\tau=\tau_0\exp(\beta)$ with $\beta\in H^2(X,\mathbf Q)$.

*Proof.* Let $Z=N\times_YX$, with projections $q\colon Z\to N$ and $p\colon Z\to X$; $p$ is finite étale of degree $b$. The equivalence $D(N,\gamma)\to D(\mathcal X/G)$ is $E\mapsto\pi_*(\pi_N^*E\otimes\mathcal U)$, where $\mathcal U$ is the universal family on $(\mathcal X/G)\times_Y\mathcal N$ and $\pi$ is the projection to $\mathcal X/G$; the tensor product has weight $0$ for the $\mathbf G_m$-gerbe $\mathcal N\to N$, so $\pi_*$ is the push-forward along the finite étale representable morphism $(\mathcal X/G)\times_YN\to\mathcal X/G$ [3, proof of Prop. 5.19], [6, Prop. 2.12]. The forgetful functor is pull-back along the finite étale map $\mathcal X\to\mathcal X/G$, followed by the identification of the weight-one part of $D(\mathcal X)$ with $D(X)$; the latter is tensoring with a weight-one invertible sheaf, which exists because the $\mu_n$-gerbe $\mathcal X\to X$ is essentially trivial [3, Prop. 5.15]. Flat base change gives $\Phi(E)=p_*(q^*E\otimes\mathcal P)$, where $\mathcal P$ is the resulting kernel on $\mathcal X\times_Y\mathcal N$. It has weight $-1$ for the $\mathbf G_m$-gerbe and weight $0$ for the $\mu_n$-gerbe after the identification, so $q^*E\otimes\mathcal P$ is an untwisted sheaf on $Z$, and $p_*$ is the ordinary push-forward along the finite étale map $p$. (The weight-one invertible sheaf contributes a factor $e^{\beta_X}$ with $\beta_X\in H^2(X,\mathbf Q)$ to $\tau$; all tests below are invariant under such factors.) By Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch for the étale maps $p,q$ (no Todd classes) and multiplicativity of twisted Chern characters [5, Prop. 1.2], the cohomological transform is $\Phi^H(a)=p_*(q^*a\cdot\kappa)$ with $\kappa=\mathrm{ch}^{-q^*B}(\mathcal P)$.

(3.2): For $y\in H^*(Y,\mathbf Q)$, $q^*\nu^*y=p^*\rho^*y$, so the projection formula gives $\Phi^H(a\cdot\nu^*y)=\Phi^H(a)\cdot\rho^*y$ for *all* rational classes, not only algebraic ones. Since $\nu^*$ is an isomorphism on rational cohomology, $a=\nu^*((\nu^*)^{-1}a)\cdot1$ and $\Phi^H(a)=\Phi^H(1)\cdot\iota(a)$. Put $\tau=\Phi^H(1)$. It is even because $\kappa$ is.

(a): Let $E=k(x)$ be a twisted skyscraper at $x\in N$ over $y\in Y$, so $\mathrm{ch}^B(E)=[\mathrm{pt}_N]$. By the local description, $\Phi(E)$ is supported on the $H$-orbit $\rho^{-1}(y)$, of $|H|$ points, with fibre $V^\vee$ at each, so $\mathrm{ch}(\Phi E)=|H|\,s\,[\mathrm{pt}_X]$ with $s=\dim V$. On the other hand, $\iota[\mathrm{pt}_N]=\rho^*[\mathrm{pt}_Y]/b=(|H|/b)[\mathrm{pt}_X]$, so (3.2) gives $\tau_0|H|/b=|H|s$, i.e. $\tau_0=bs$. In particular $s$ does not depend on $x$.

(b): Forgetting and induction are adjoint on both sides, and $\mathrm{Ind}\circ\mathrm{Forg}\cong\bigoplus_{\chi\in\hat G}(-)\otimes\chi$ [7, §3.5]. On objects this gives $\mathrm{Hom}^*_X(\Phi E,\Phi E')=\bigoplus_{\chi}\mathrm{Hom}^*_N(E,\chi_*E')$, and $\chi_*$ (in $\mathrm{Aut}^0$, see below) does not change Euler characteristics, so $\chi_X(\Phi E,\Phi E')=|G|\,\chi_N(E,E')$. Take $E=k(x)$ as in (a) and $E'$ a locally free twisted sheaf of rank $r'>0$ (it exists since $\gamma$ is represented by an Azumaya algebra [3, proof of Prop. 5.19]). Then $\chi_N(E,E')=r'$, while $\Phi E$ has class $|H|s[\mathrm{pt}_X]$ and $\Phi E'$ has rank $\tau_0r'$, so $\chi_X(\Phi E,\Phi E')=|H|s\tau_0r'$. Hence $|G|=|H|s\tau_0=\tau_0^2|H|/b$, using only objects. For the full class $\tau$ we use cohomology: the right adjoint $\Phi^R$ of $\Phi$ satisfies $\Phi^R\Phi\cong\bigoplus_{\chi\in\hat G}\chi_*$, where $\chi_*$ is the action of $\chi$ on $D(N,\gamma)$. These are Fourier–Mukai functors relative to $Y$. Their cohomological transforms compose like the kernels, and the transform of $\Phi^R$ is the adjoint of $\Phi^H$ for the Mukai pairing $\langle a,a'\rangle=\int a^\vee a'$ [8, Ch. 5]; the proof is formal (projection formula and Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch along étale maps) and applies to twisted kernels with $\mathrm{ch}^B$ [5, Prop. 1.2(iii)]. Each $\chi_*$ is in $\mathrm{Aut}^0$, a translation composed with a twist by a line bundle in $\mathrm{Pic}^0$, so it acts trivially on $H^*(N,\mathbf Q)$. Hence $\langle\Phi^Ha,\Phi^Ha'\rangle_X=|G|\,\langle a,a'\rangle_N$ for all $a,a'\in H^*(N,\mathbf Q)$. By (3.2) and (3.1) the left side is $(|H|/b)\int_N\iota^{-1}(\tau^\vee\tau)\,a^\vee a'$. Poincaré duality gives $\iota^{-1}(\tau^\vee\tau)=|G|b/|H|$, a constant, so $\tau^\vee\tau=\tau_0^2=|G|b/|H|$. Write $\tau=\tau_0\exp(\sigma)$ with $\sigma=\log(\tau/\tau_0)$, nilpotent. Then $\exp(\sigma^\vee+\sigma)=1$, so $\sigma^\vee=-\sigma$, which kills the components of $\sigma$ in $H^{4k}$.

(c): Over each component $Z_j$ of $Z$, the inertia of the gerbe is an étale-locally constant finite group $K'$, and at every point $\mathcal P$ is the irreducible representation $V$ [6, proof of Prop. 2.12]. Étale-locally, therefore, $\mathcal P\cong V\otimes\mathcal O$ as $K'$-equivariant sheaves. Two such trivializations differ by a $K'$-equivariant automorphism of $V\otimes\mathcal O$, which is a scalar function by Schur's lemma. So the transition functions are scalar modulo the locally constant change of identification of $V$, the associated $\mathrm{PGL}_s$-bundle is locally constant, and $\mathcal P|_{Z_j}$ carries a projectively flat connection. By Chern–Weil, $\kappa|_{Z_j}=s\exp(\beta_j)$ for some $\beta_j\in H^2(Z_j,\mathbf Q)$. Since $\mathcal P$ is an algebraic $(-q^*B)$-twisted sheaf, $\kappa\in\exp(-q^*B)\bigoplus H^{p,p}$ [5, Prop. 1.2(iv)], so $h_j:=\beta_j+q^*B$ is a rational $(1,1)$-class on $Z_j$. The map $p_j=p|_{Z_j}$ is an isogeny of degree $b_j$, and $(p_j^*)^{-1}q_j^*=\iota$ on $H^*(N,\mathbf Q)$. Hence
$$\tau=\sum_jp_{j*}\bigl(s\,e^{\beta_j}\bigr)=s\,e^{-\iota(B)}\sum_jb_j\,e^{h_j'},\qquad h_j'=(p_j^*)^{-1}h_j\in\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbf Q}.$$
If $\rho(X)=1$, then $h_j'=c_j\Theta$ with $c_j\in\mathbf Q$. Write $\bar c=\sum_jb_jc_j/\sum_jb_j$. By (b), the $H^4$-component of $\log\bigl(\sum_jb_je^{(c_j-\bar c)\Theta}\big/\sum_jb_j\bigr)$ vanishes; it equals $\tfrac12\sum_jb_j(c_j-\bar c)^2\Theta^2/\sum_jb_j$. Since $\Theta^2\neq0$, all $c_j=\bar c$, and $\tau=\tau_0\exp(\bar c\,\Theta-\iota(B))$. □

*Remarks.* (i) From (a) and (b), $|K|=|G|/|H|=bs^2$. (ii) The class $\beta$ in (c), and $\sigma$ in (b), are not controlled. The tests below use only polynomials in $\mathrm{ch}$ that are invariant under $e^{\beta}$ for every $\beta\in H^2(\mathbf Q)$. (iii) The source $F$ is an untwisted object of $D(X)$ throughout. Objects of $D(X,\alpha)$ with $\alpha\neq0$ are not treated here.

## 4. Integral B-invariant polynomials
**Lemma 4.1.** Let $T$ be a real torus, $k\ge1$ and $\gamma\in H^{2k}(T,\mathbf Z)$. Then $\gamma^m/m!\in H^{2km}(T,\mathbf Z)$ for all $m\ge1$.
*Proof.* $H^*(T,\mathbf Z)=\wedge^*H^1(T,\mathbf Z)$. Write $\gamma=\sum_Ia_Ie_I$ over increasing multi-indices with $|I|=2k$. Even-degree monomials commute and $e_I^2=0$, so $\gamma^m=m!\sum_{I_1<\dots<I_m}a_{I_1}\cdots a_{I_m}e_{I_1}\cdots e_{I_m}$. □

For $u\in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(T,\mathbf Q)$ write $u_k\in H^{2k}$ and $r=u_0$. Define $\Delta(u)=u_1^2-2u_0u_2$ and, when $r\ne0$,
$$Q_k(u)=r^{k-1}\bigl[e^{-u_1/r}u\bigr]_k=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^i}{i!}\,u_1^{\,i}\,r^{k-1-i}u_{k-i}\qquad(2\le k\le6),$$
so $Q_2=-\Delta/2$. When $r=0$, put $I_4=u_1u_3-u_2^2/2$ and $I_5=u_1^2u_4-u_1u_2u_3+u_2^3/3$.

**Lemma 4.2.** Let $u\in H^{\mathrm{ev}}(T,\mathbf Z)$. (i) $\Delta(u)/2$, the $Q_k(u)$ (if $r\ne0$), and $u_1,I_4(u),I_5(u)$ (if $r=0$) are integral. (ii) Each of these polynomials $M$ satisfies $M(e^{\beta}u)=M(u)$ for all $\beta\in H^2(T,\mathbf Q)$.
*Proof.* (i) Each term of $Q_k$ is an integer multiple of $(u_1^i/i!)\,u_{k-i}$, which is integral by Lemma 4.1. Likewise $u_1^2/2$, $u_2^2/2$ and $u_2^3/3=2\cdot u_2^3/6$ are integral. (ii) $e^{-u_1/r}u$ is unchanged under $u\mapsto e^\beta u$, since $u_1\mapsto u_1+r\beta$. For $r=0$, and for $\Delta$, (ii) is an identity in the commutative algebra generated by even classes; `invcheck.py` verifies all cases symbolically. □

## 5. Proof of Theorem A
Let $F$ satisfy (M) with $\mathrm{ch}(F)=v=v_\lambda$, and let $E\in D(N,\gamma)$ correspond to $F$, with $e=\mathrm{ch}^B(E)$ integral (§3). By Proposition 3.1(b), $v=\tau_0e^{\sigma}\iota(e)$ with $\sigma=\sigma_1+\sigma_3+\cdots$ and $\sigma_k\in H^{2k}$. The discriminant involves only components of degree $\le4$, and those of $e^{\sigma}\iota(e)$ agree with those of $e^{\sigma_1}\iota(e)$. Since $\Delta$ is quadratic and B-invariant (Lemma 4.2), and $\iota$ is a ring isomorphism, Lemma 2.1(i) gives
$$q\,n\,\Theta^2=\Delta(v)=\tau_0^2\,\iota(\Delta(e)).$$
Put $h=g/2$ and $\gamma_4=\Delta(e)/2\in H^4(N,\mathbf Z)$. By Lemma 4.1, $\gamma_4^h/h!\in H^{2g}(N,\mathbf Z)$, so $C:=\int_N\gamma_4^h/h!\in\mathbf Z$. By (3.1), Proposition 3.1(b) and Lemma 2.1(iii),
$$C=\frac{b}{|H|}\int_X\iota\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_4^h}{h!}\Bigr)=\frac{\tau_0^2}{|G|}\cdot\frac{(qn/2)^h}{\tau_0^{2h}}\cdot\frac{g!}{h!}=\frac{n^{h-1}}{\tau_0^{g-2}}\cdot\frac{g!}{2^{3h-2}\,h!}. \tag{5.1}$$
Fix a prime $p$ and write $\mu=v_p(\tau_0)$, $\epsilon=1$ if $p=2$ and $\epsilon=0$ otherwise. By Legendre's formula $v_p(g!/h!)=(h+s_p(h)-s_p(g))/(p-1)$, where $s_p$ is the digit sum. For $p=2$ this equals $h$, since $s_2(g)=s_2(h)$. So $v_2(C)=(h-1)(v_2(n)-2-2\mu)$, and $C\in\mathbf Z$ gives $v_2(n)\ge2+2\mu$. For odd $p$ we claim $v_p(g!/h!)<h-1$. For $p\ge5$, $v_p(g!/h!)\le(2h-1)/4<h-1$ because $h\ge3$. For $p=3$ it suffices that $s_3(h)<h-1$, since $s_3(g)\ge1$; this holds because $h-s_3(h)=2v_3(h!)\ge2$ for $h\ge3$. Hence $(h-1)(v_p(n)-2\mu)\ge-v_p(g!/h!)>-(h-1)$, and $v_p(n)\ge2\mu$ since both sides are integers. Together, $n/(4\tau_0^2)\in\mathbf Z$. The reformulation in terms of $|G|$ is Lemma 2.1(iii). □

*Remarks.* (a) The argument uses no hypothesis on $\mathrm{NS}(X)$ or $\mathrm{NS}(N)$. It needs only the integrality of $\mathrm{ch}^BE$ on the torus $N$, the transport (3.2) with its constants, and Lemma 2.1(iii). (b) For $g=6$ and $\zeta=\zeta_6$, (5.1) is $C=5\,\mathrm{Nm}^2/(3\tau_0^4)$. It excludes, on every principally polarized sixfold, all objects of the six minimal classes $\pm v_1,\pm w,\pm(v_1+w)$, including every equivariant structure on the secant sheaves of [4, Prop. 5.1]. More generally it excludes every class with $3\nmid\mathrm{Nm}$. (c) For $g=2$, (5.1) is $C=1$ and gives nothing. This is why we assume $g\ge6$.

## 6. Proof of Theorem B
No hypothesis on Néron–Severi groups is needed. The class $(\rho^*)^{-1}\Theta\in H^2(Y,\mathbf Q)$ equals $\rho_*\Theta/|H|$, since $\rho^*\rho_*\Theta=\sum_{h\in H}t_h^*\Theta=|H|\Theta$ in cohomology, and $\rho_*\Theta$ is the class of the norm of an ample line bundle, hence ample. Let $\theta_Y$ be the primitive integral class on its ray, and $\theta_N$ the primitive integral class on the ray of $\nu^*\theta_Y$. Both are ample, of types $(d_1|\dots|d_g)$ with $d_1=1$ (they are primitive) and $d_i\mid d_{i+1}$. Put $P_N=\prod_id_i$, and similarly $P_Y$, so that $\int\theta^g/g!=P$ by Riemann–Roch [9, §16]. Write $\rho^*\theta_Y=m\Theta$ and $\nu^*\theta_Y=k\theta_N$; $m$ and $k$ are positive integers because $\Theta$ and $\theta_N$ are primitive and the left sides are integral. Comparing top powers gives
$$m^g=|H|\,P_Y,\qquad b\,P_Y=k^gP_N, \tag{6.1}$$
and $\iota(\theta_N)=(m/k)\Theta$. Combined with $|G|=\tau_0^2|H|/b$, (6.1) gives
$$P_N=\frac{\tau_0^2\,m^g}{|G|\,k^g}. \tag{6.2}$$
By §5, $\Delta(e)=\tau_0^{-2}\iota^{-1}(qn\Theta^2)=qn\,k^2(\tau_0m)^{-2}\,\theta_N^2$. In a symplectic basis adapted to $\theta_N=\sum_id_i\,e_i\wedge f_i$ we have $\theta_N^2/2=\sum_{i<j}d_id_j\,e_if_ie_jf_j$, a class of content $d_1d_2=d_2$. Since $\Delta(e)/2$ is integral (Lemma 4.2), for every prime $p$
$$v_p(d_2)\ge L:=2\mu+2\delta-v_p(q)-v_p(n),\qquad\mu=v_p(\tau_0),\quad\delta=v_p(m)-v_p(k)\in\mathbf Z. \tag{6.3}$$
By Lemma 2.1(iii), $v_p|G|=h\,v_p(q)+v_p(n)+\epsilon(g-2)$, with $h=g/2$ and $\epsilon$ as in §5. Put $t=v_p(n)-2\mu-2\epsilon$, which is $\ge0$ by Theorem A. A direct computation from (6.2) gives the identity
$$v_p(P_N)=h\,L+(h-1)\,t. \tag{6.4}$$
Since $d_1=1$ and $d_2\mid\dots\mid d_g$, we have $v_p(P_N)\ge(g-1)\max(0,v_p(d_2))\ge(g-1)\max(0,L)$. If $L>0$, (6.4) gives $(h-1)t\ge(g-1-h)L=(h-1)L$, so $t\ge L\ge1$. If $L\le0$ and $t=0$, then $v_p(P_N)=hL\ge0$ forces $L=0$, so $v_p(q)+v_p(n)=2\mu+2\delta$ is even. With $v_p(n)=2\mu+2\epsilon$, this makes $v_p(q)$ even. Hence $v_p(q)$ odd implies $t\ge1$, i.e. $v_p(n)\ge2\mu+2\epsilon+1$. Over all $p$ this is $4d\tau_0^2\mid n$. □

*Remarks.* (a) The count is sharp. For every $g\in\{6,10,14,18\}$, $p\in\{2,3\}$, $|v_p(q)|\le3$ and $\mu\le3$, the smallest $v_p(n)$ admitting a solution is exactly $2\mu+2\epsilon+[v_p(q)\text{ odd}]$, and every larger value admits one (`g8count.py`). For $g=6$, $\zeta_6$, the exhaustive check `d3free.py` agrees. (b) The proof uses only $\Delta(e)$, which by §5 depends on $\sigma_1$ alone and is a multiple of $\iota^{-1}(\Theta)^2$, hence of $\theta_N^2$. The ray of a rational ample class always contains a primitive integral ample class, so no assumption on $\rho$ enters. The hypothesis $\rho(X)=1$ is used only in Proposition 3.1(c), which makes $\sigma=\sigma_1$ so that the higher B-invariant polynomials $Q_k$, $I_4$, $I_5$ transport (§7), and in the enumeration of §7 (where $m\mid|H|$ uses $\mathrm{NS}(Y)=\mathbf Z\theta_Y$). As an independent check, `prodcount.py` repeats the 3-adic count of the Corollary for $X=A\times B$ with product isogeny data ($\dim A+\dim B=6$, $\rho\ge2$, separate contents for $\theta_A^2$, $\theta_A\theta_B$, $\theta_B^2$) and finds no solution with $v_3(\mathrm{Nm})\le1$.

## 7. The case $g=6$, $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$: proof of Theorem C
In this section $g=6$, $\zeta=\zeta_6$ (Example (1) of §2), and $\rho(X)=1$; we keep the notation of §6. By Proposition 3.1(c), $v=\tau_0e^{\beta}\iota(e)$ with $\beta\in H^2$, so
$$e=e^{B'}u,\qquad u:=\tau_0^{-1}\iota^{-1}(v)=\tau_0^{-1}\sum_jv_j\,(k/m)^j\,\theta_N^j\in\mathbf Q[\theta_N],\qquad B'=-\iota^{-1}\beta\in H^2(N,\mathbf Q), \tag{7.1}$$
where $v=\sum_jv_j\Theta^j$. The class $B'$ is unknown and need not be algebraic. For a type $(d_1|\dots|d_6)$ put $D_j=d_1\cdots d_j$. Since $\theta_N^j/j!=\sum_{|I|=j}d_I\,x_I$ in an adapted basis, we have $\mathbf Q\theta_N^j\cap H^{2j}(N,\mathbf Z)=\mathbf Z\,\theta_N^j/(j!D_j)$.

**Integrality conditions.** (I1) If $r=\mathrm{rk}\,v\ne0$, then $r_E:=r/\tau_0=\mathrm{rk}\,E$ is an integer. (I2) For each polynomial $M$ of Lemma 4.2, homogeneous of degree $j$ in $H^{2j}$, $M(u)=M(e)$ is integral, i.e. $c\,j!\,D_j\in\mathbf Z$ where $M(u)=c\,\theta_N^j$.

**Lemma 7.1 (square roots of 2-forms, any characteristic).** Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$, and let $\gamma\colon\wedge^2V^*\to\wedge^4V^*$ be the divided square, $\gamma(\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}e_{ij})=\sum a_{ij}a_{kl}\,e_{ij}\wedge e_{kl}$ over unordered pairs of disjoint index pairs (so $2\gamma(\eta)=\eta\wedge\eta$). Let $\eta,\omega\in\wedge^2V^*$ with $\mathrm{rk}\,\omega\ge6$ and $\gamma(\eta)=c\,\gamma(\omega)$ for some $c\ne0$. Then $c=\lambda^2$ is a square and $\eta=\pm\lambda\omega$.
*Proof.* For $x,y\in V$ the identity $\iota_y\iota_x\gamma(\eta)=\eta(x,y)\,\eta-\iota_x\eta\wedge\iota_y\eta$ holds over $\mathbf Z$ in the universal case, hence over every field; the same holds for $\omega$. Suppose $\eta$ is not a multiple of $\omega$. Then there is $x$ with $\iota_x\eta\notin F\cdot\iota_x\omega$, since a linear map $A\colon V\to V^*$ with $A(x)\in F\cdot B(x)$ for all $x$, where $\mathrm{rk}\,B\ge2$, is a scalar multiple of $B$. If $\iota_x\omega\neq0$, choose $y$ with $\omega(x,y)=1$ and $\eta(x,y)=0$. Then $c\,\omega=c\,\iota_x\omega\wedge\iota_y\omega-\iota_x\eta\wedge\iota_y\eta$ has rank $\le4$, a contradiction. If $\iota_x\omega=0$, choose $y$ with $\eta(x,y)=1$. Then $\eta=\iota_x\eta\wedge\iota_y\eta$ is decomposable, so $\gamma(\eta)=0$, while $\gamma(\omega)\ne0$ because $\mathrm{rk}\,\omega\ge4$; again a contradiction. Hence $\eta=\lambda\omega$, and $\gamma(\eta)=\lambda^2\gamma(\omega)$ gives $\lambda^2=c$. □ An exhaustive check over $\mathbf F_2$ ($\dim V=6,7$, $\mathrm{rk}\,\omega=6$) and $\mathbf F_3$ ($\dim V=6$) finds no other solutions; for $\mathrm{rk}\,\omega=4$ over $\mathbf F_2$ there are $27$, so the rank bound is needed (`divsq.py`).

**Lemma 7.2 (the B-field is algebraic modulo integral classes).** Let $r_E\ne0$ be squarefree, and write $Q_2(u)=q_2\theta_N^2$. Suppose that for every prime $p\mid r_E$, $2q_2$ is a $p$-adic unit and at least three of the $d_i$ are prime to $p$. Then $B'\equiv(s\theta_N-u_1)/r_E\bmod H^2(N,\mathbf Z)$ for some $s\in\mathbf Z$. Hence $e^{(s\theta_N-u_1)/r_E}u$ has integral Chern character and integral Chern classes for some $s\bmod r_E$.
*Proof.* $e_1=u_1+r_EB'$ is integral; call it $S$. A direct computation gives $r_Ee_2=\gamma(S)+Q_2(u)$, where $\gamma(S)=S^2/2$ is the integral divided square, and $Q_2(u)=2q_2\,\gamma(\theta_N)$. Hence $\gamma(S)\equiv-2q_2\,\gamma(\theta_N)\pmod{r_EH^4(N,\mathbf Z)}$, all terms being $p$-integral for $p\mid r_E$. Reduce modulo a prime $p\mid r_E$ (including $p=2$) in $\wedge^*(H^1(N,\mathbf Z)\otimes\mathbf F_p)$; the divided square commutes with reduction because it has integer coefficients. Then $\gamma(\bar S)=-2q_2\,\gamma(\bar\theta_N)$ with $-2q_2\not\equiv0$, and $\theta_N\bmod p$ has rank $2\#\{i:p\nmid d_i\}\ge6$. Lemma 7.1 gives $S\equiv s_p\theta_N\bmod p$. By the Chinese remainder theorem there is $s$ with $S-s\theta_N\in r_EH^2(N,\mathbf Z)$. Changing $B'$ by an integral class multiplies $e$ by $\mathrm{ch}$ of a line bundle, which preserves integrality of $\mathrm{ch}$ and of Chern classes. Since $e$ is the Chern character of a virtual $C^\infty$ bundle (§3), the last claim follows. □

**The enumeration is finite and complete.** For given $v$, the data are $(b,s,|H|,m,k,d)$ subject to:
- $|G|=9\,\mathrm{Nm}(v)=bs^2|H|$ (Proposition 3.1(a), (b)), so $b$ and $s$ range over divisors with $bs^2\mid9\,\mathrm{Nm}$, and $\tau_0=bs$;
- $m\mid|H|$, because $\rho_*\Theta\in\mathrm{NS}(Y)=\mathbf Z\theta_Y$, say $\rho_*\Theta=j\theta_Y$, and $mj\,\theta_Y=\rho_*\rho^*\theta_Y=|H|\theta_Y$; also $|H|\mid m^6$ by (6.1);
- $k^6\mid bP_Y$ by (6.1), so $k\le(bP_Y)^{1/6}$;
- $d$ a type with $d_1=1$, $d_i\mid d_{i+1}$ and $\prod d_i=P_N=bP_Y/k^6$.

Every $(M)$-object produces such a datum satisfying (I1), (I2) and, when Lemma 7.2 applies, its conclusion. The script `qfilter3.py` runs over all data, with no other bounds. It discards data failing (I1) or (I2), using a prime-by-prime test for the existence of a type with $c_jj!D_j\in\mathbf Z$. This test is checked against direct enumeration of types (`feas_check.py`). For each surviving type it applies Lemma 7.2 when its hypotheses hold, testing all $s\bmod r_E$. A datum is *excluded* if it fails (I1) or (I2), or if Lemma 7.2 applies and no $s$ passes. Otherwise it is *open*, and the output records why: rank $0$, $r_E$ not squarefree, $2q_2$ not a $p$-unit, or fewer than three $d_i$ prime to $p$. A class is excluded when all of its data are excluded.

## 8. Bearing on the Hodge conjecture
No Hodge class changes status. The results bound one route. Markman's mechanism (M) proves the Hodge conjecture for split Weil sixfolds [1]. To extend it to Weil classes for $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-q})$ on the $2g$-folds $X\times\hat X$ with $g\equiv2\bmod4$, one needs an equivariant object on $X$ with class in $e^{a\Theta}P_q$ that satisfies (M); Perry's theorem [3, Thm 1.2] would then deform it along the Weil family. (For $4\mid g$, [4] already rules this out.) Since the Weil classes stay Hodge on the whole family, a single special $X$ would suffice, for instance one with $\rho(X)\ge2$ or with extra endomorphisms. Theorem B shows that on every $X$ the class of such an object satisfies $4d\mid n$, which removes the small classes in most of our examples. For $g=6$ and $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$ this is $9\mid\mathrm{Nm}$, i.e. $v\in3\Lambda$ (the prime $1+\zeta_6$ has norm $3$, and $(1+\zeta_6)^2=3\zeta_6$), and it excludes the secant sheaves of [4, Prop. 5.1] in every equivariant form. When $\rho=1$, Theorem C pushes the smallest admissible norm to $27$. For the remaining classes, integrality of Chern characters does not decide (M). Genuine pull-backs from quotients by Lagrangian subgroups of $X[3]$, with isogeny data of the same shape, pass all our tests. This suggests that deciding them needs a non-numerical input, such as an actual construction or an argument about supports. The results concern (M) only. They do not touch weaker semiregularity criteria [2, Question 11.4], the real-multiplication setting [4, §4], or other routes to these Weil classes.
