# Accept

This is a sound, focused revision of the accepted paper. The characteristic-two argument removes an unnecessary restriction in the existing integrality test and resolves two entries in its existing finite table. These are limited consequences of the same obstruction method, appropriately consolidated here rather than separated into another paper. Theorems A and B remain the central contribution, and the revised manuscript retains a clear progression from descent to integrality obstructions and their scope.

The revised Lemma 7.1 is valid. The divided-square contraction identity has integral coefficients, so reduction to characteristic two does not require division by two. The rank-at-most-four contradiction and the treatment of the kernel of $\omega$ still apply. Consequently, Lemma 7.2 legitimately includes even squarefree $r_E$: the unit and polarization-rank hypotheses allow reduction at every prime dividing $r_E$, and the Chinese remainder theorem gives the asserted integral congruence. The squarefree hypothesis remains essential to this argument; the revision correctly retains it.

I reproduced all four checks in `divsq.py`: the rank-six cases in dimensions six and seven over $\mathbf F_2$, the dimension-six case over $\mathbf F_3$, and the rank-four negative control, which has 28 solutions, including 27 nonscalar ones. These finite checks corroborate the lemma; its proof establishes the statement over arbitrary fields.

I also reran the exhaustive enumeration for the newly excluded classes $(-3,6)$ and $(9,6)$. Each has eight data surviving the earlier integrality filters, all excluded by the revised test, in agreement with `q3sweep_v3.out`. Targeted reruns for $(3,6)$ and $(0,6)$ reproduce the revised open-case explanations: failure of the 3-adic unit condition in the former, and remaining failures of the mod-two rank or unit hypotheses in the latter. I inspected the changed decision routine and its enumeration dependencies; I did not rerun the entire unchanged experimental suite.

The expanded proof of Proposition 3.1 improves the explanation of descent, weight-zero tensor products, flat base change and projective transition functions. The abstract now accurately restricts the B-field family to $a\Theta$, and the supplementary documentation identifies the current output and software versions. One minor convention should be made explicit: if $L$ is the weight-one invertible sheaf trivializing $\mathcal X$, the identification from weight-one objects to untwisted objects uses tensoring with $L^\vee$. Writing that direction explicitly would avoid the apparent weight mismatch in the present wording.

The added results remain obstructions to condition (M), not to algebraicity of rational Hodge classes. The revision does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture or establish existence for the classes left open.
