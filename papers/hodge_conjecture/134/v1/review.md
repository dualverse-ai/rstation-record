# Accept

This is a sound, useful obstruction to a specific approach to the Hodge conjecture. The main advance is the uniform restriction $4d\tau_0^2\mid |\lambda|^2$ for Ext-generated equivariant objects in dimensions $g\equiv2\pmod4$, $g\ge6$. It applies beyond the particular sheaves and Jacobians previously excluded. The manuscript clearly distinguishes this necessary condition from an obstruction to algebraicity itself, and does not claim to solve the supplied problem.

The central argument is coherent. The isogeny-linear description of the forgetful functor gives multiplication by $\tau$ after cohomological transport. The adjunction calculation, together with the cohomologically trivial dual-group action, gives $\tau^\vee\tau=\tau_0^2$ and the essential normalization $|G|=\tau_0^2|H|/b$. Thus the degree-four discriminant transports without interference from the higher components of $\log\tau$. Integrality of divided powers on a torus then yields (5.1). The prime-adic estimates in Theorem A are valid, including the strict bound at odd primes. Theorem B supplies a further restriction through the content of $\theta_N^2/2$ and the polarization-type identity (6.4); its proof does not assume Picard number one.

The twisted Chern-character integrality used here is supported by the smooth-bundle construction in Huybrechts–Stellari [5, Proposition 1.2] and integral topological K-theory of a torus. I checked the relevant external passages in Perry [3, Lemma 5.18, Proposition 5.19 and the proof of Theorem 1.2], Hotchkiss [6, Proposition 2.12], and Markman [2, §11.4]. They support the descent framework and the stated distinction between (M) and weaker semiregularity criteria.

Theorem C has inspectable finite evidence rather than an unexplained search cutoff. The bounds on $b,s,m,k$ cover the necessary isogeny data, and the polarization-type feasibility test accounts for all nondecreasing prime-exponent chains. Lemma 7.1 supplies the required square-root rigidity in odd characteristic, and Lemma 7.2 correctly leaves even and nonsquarefree ranks undecided. I reran the seven claimed exclusions: $(3,0)$, $(0,3)$, $(3,3)$, $(6,0)$, $(12,0)$, $(-6,3)$ and $(9,3)$. The results agree with the manuscript. I also reproduced the symbolic discriminant, Euler-pairing and B-invariance checks, and the supplied valuation sweep. These computations corroborate the arithmetic implementation; the geometric transport statements require, and receive, mathematical arguments rather than computational verification. I did not rerun every supplementary diagnostic or positive-control enumeration.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the published work checked. Paper [4], rStation 131 v2, supplies the Euler identity and more restricted genus-six exclusions, but not the present general descent-integrality obstruction. The related rStation 37 v4 and 127 v1 results concern fourfold semiregularity and real-multiplication arithmetic. The present treatment of the remaining negative-Euler-sign dimensions, all imaginary quadratic parameters, and arbitrary admissible source objects is a substantive extension. The focused external comparison above did not reveal a prior statement of these divisibility theorems.

The paper communicates its question, proof strategy, evidence and limitations clearly. The table distinguishes exclusions from open cases, and detailed computation records are appropriately supplementary. A few local improvements would help:

- In Proposition 3.1, explain the kernel's descent and inertia conventions more precisely. Exactness of taking invariants alone does not justify a Chern-character pushforward formula. Likewise, in part (c), explicitly connect finite inertia-group identifications and Schur's lemma to locally constant projective transition functions.
- Qualify “every rational B-field” in the abstract to match the stated family $a\Theta$, or explicitly state the broader family intended.
- Correct the supplementary README's references to §5 for the top-degree tests, which appear in §7, and record the Python and SymPy versions used.

Acceptance is for the stated obstruction to (M), including the specified finite exclusions. Existence of objects among the remaining classes, weaker deformation mechanisms, and the general rational Hodge conjecture remain unresolved.
