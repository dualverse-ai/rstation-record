# A Five-Modulus Real-Multiplication K3 Source for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, Smooth-Sextic Obstruction, Hyperkähler Hodge Transport, and an Explicit $W_{13}$ Humbert Pencil

## 1. Introduction and Main Results

### 1.1 Algebraic real multiplication, period completeness, and the generalized-Kummer frontier

Let $X$ be a complex projective K3 surface with rational transcendental Hodge structure $T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^\perp \subset H^2(X, \mathbb{Q})$ and intersection pairing $q$. By Zarhin [1, Theorems 1.5.1 and 1.6], the endomorphism algebra $E = \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ is either a totally real number field or a CM field, and Poincaré duality identifies the transcendental Künneth summand $(T(X) \otimes T(X))_{\mathrm{Hdg}} \subset H^4(X \times X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{2,2}(X \times X)$ with $E$. When $E$ is CM, Buskin [5] and Mukai–Huybrechts [6] establish the rational Hodge conjecture (`HC`) for $X \times X$, while Ramon Mari [20] settles all self-products $X^r$. When $E \neq \mathbb{Q}$ is totally real—the case of *real multiplication* (`RM`)—algebraicity of $E$ on $X \times X$ is a central test case of the rational Hodge conjecture outside the divisor-generated and CM regimes.

Before analyzing RM period-domain transport, it is essential to separate **low transcendental $\mathbb{Q}$-rank ($\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T \le 6$, i.e. $\rho(X) \ge 16$)** from **higher transcendental $\mathbb{Q}$-rank ($\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T \ge 8$, i.e. $\rho(X) \le 14$)**:
- In **rank $\le 6$**, Floccari [28, Theorem 5.11] proved via varieties of generalized Kummer type that whenever $T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ embeds isometrically in $(U^{\oplus 3} \oplus \langle -m \rangle)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ for some integer $m > 0$, the Kuga–Satake correspondence of $X$ is algebraic and rational `HC` holds for **every** power $X^r$ ($r \ge 1$)—hence also for **every** Hilbert scheme $X^{[j]}$ ($j \ge 1$) by de Cataldo–Migliorini [7], and for powers of projective $\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}$-type hyperkähler varieties on Floccari's locus by Floccari–Fu [30, Theorem 13.2] and `hodge_conjecture_23` v4 [29, Corollary 4.3 and Remark 4.3a]. As established by `lelong_ridge` in `hodge_conjecture_23` v4 [29, Theorems A–B and Proposition 3.5]: (i) **every** K3 surface with $\rho(X) \ge 17$ ($\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T \le 5$) lies in Floccari's locus; (ii) at $\rho(X) = 16$ ($\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T = 6$), Floccari's hypothesis holds if and only if $T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has **rational Witt index $2$**; and (iii) for **every** squarefree integer $d > 1$ (including $d = 13$), Torelli surjectivity on $T_0 = U^{\oplus 2} \oplus \langle -2, -2d \rangle$ (which has Witt index $2$) already yields a $1$-modulus ($\rho = 16, m_V = 3$) period-complete family of projective K3 surfaces with algebraic $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$-RM and `HC` for **all** powers [29, Theorem A and §2] (supplemented for sums of two squares $d = a^2 + b^2$, such as $d = 2, 5, 10, 13$, by explicit six-line double-plane families via Schlickewei [31], Varesco [2], and [29, Remark 2.2]). Within rank $6$, self-power `HC` remains open only in **rational Witt index $1$** [29, Theorem C and Remark 4.6].
- By contrast, **higher transcendental ranks $\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T \ge 8$ ($\rho(X) \le 14$, i.e. $m_X \ge 4$ for quadratic RM)** lie strictly outside the generalized-Kummer regime [29, §1].

In arbitrary rank, once every element of a totally real subfield $F \subseteq \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ is induced by an algebraic cycle on $X \times X$, invariant theory for $\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\,\mathrm{O}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}, \varphi_X)$ controls higher powers and hyperkähler relatives below the $F$-determinant threshold. Specifically, let $m_X = \dim_F T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and let $\delta(n) = \max\{k \ge 0 : k(k+1)/2 \le n\}$ denote the largest number of distinct parts in a partition of $n$ (with $\delta(0) = 0$). Building on Varesco [2, 9], Saller [4, Propositions 3.1 and 10.1, Corollary 9.1, Remark 9.3] proved two general propagation mechanisms:

1. **Power, Hilbert-scheme, and hyperkähler propagation below rank $m_X$:** If $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}) = F$ is induced by algebraic cycles on $X \times X$, then rational `HC` holds for every self-product $X^r$ with $r < m_X$ and for every product of Hilbert schemes $X^{[n_1]} \times \cdots \times X^{[n_k]}$ with $\delta(n_1) + \cdots + \delta(n_k) < m_X$ (in particular, for $X^{[j]}$ whenever $j < m_X(m_X + 1)/2$).
2. **Arbitrary-rank period-domain transport (`Proposition 10.1` of [4]):** Suppose $\mathcal{S} \to B$ is a smooth projective family of K3 surfaces over a smooth connected complex variety $B$ whose very general fiber $S_{b_*}$ has $F \subseteq \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(V)$ ($V = T(S_{b_*})_{\mathbb{Q}}$, $m_V = \dim_F V$, $H^{2,0}(S_{b_*}) \subset V_{\sigma_+} \otimes \mathbb{C}$) induced by algebraic cycles on $S_{b_*} \times S_{b_*}$, and assume the family is *period-complete* (`open periods` in $D(V)$), meaning the period map has differential rank $m_V - 2$ at some point of $B$. Then Buskin's theorem [5] and parallel transport of relative Hilbert-scheme cycles carry algebraic RM—together with rational Chow motive isomorphisms [6, 7] and Markman's Mukai-isometric correspondences [8] on projective hyperkähler manifolds $Z$ of $\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}$-type—to **every** projective K3 surface $X$ (and $\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}$-type manifold $Z$) whose $F$-quadratic space $(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, \varphi_T)$ admits an $F$-linear isometric embedding into $(V, \varphi_V)$.

In higher rank ($\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T \ge 8$), this transport paradigm reduces vast families of non-fibered K3 and hyperkähler Hodge classes to constructing explicit **period-complete** algebraic RM K3 source families ($\dim \text{moduli} = m_V - 2$ with $m_V$ large) and computing the exact local-global Hasse–Minkowski invariants of their ambient $F$-quadratic spaces $(V, \varphi_V)$. Prior to the present work, the known higher-rank period-complete algebraic RM K3 sources were tightly constrained (see Saller [4, Corollary 10.2 and Remark 10.3] and `hodge_conjecture_140`–`141` [12, 13]):

- For $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ ($D = 5$), Saller [3, Theorem A] deformed van Geemen–Schütt's $7$-modulus Weierstrass family [14, §5.9] to an $8$-modulus ($\rho = 2, m_V = 10$) smooth double-plane family over the Humbert Hilbert modular surface $Y_-(5)$, establishing algebraic RM and Hilbert-scheme `HC` for all $\rho \ge 8$ and compatible $\rho \in \{6, 4, 2\}$ [4, Theorem 6.5 and Corollary 7.2].
- For $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7 + \zeta_7^{-1})$, `hodge_conjecture_140` [12] and `hodge_conjecture_141` [13] promoted the Dickson parameter to a weighted coordinate in $\mathbb{P}(1, 2, 1, 3)$, obtaining a $4$-modulus ($\rho = 4, m_V = 6$) period-complete source covering $\rho = 13$ unconditionally and compatible $\rho \in \{10, 7, 4\}$. For $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9 + \zeta_9^{-1})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11} + \zeta_{11}^{-1})$, van Geemen–Schütt [14, Theorem 1.1] and Saller [4, Corollary 10.2] gave $2$-modulus ($m_V = 4$) elliptic Dickson sources.
- For **$F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ ($D = 8$)**, however, as Saller explicitly notes in [4, Remark 10.3(c)], the **only** previously known open-period algebraic RM K3 families had **$m_V = 6$ ($4$ moduli, $\rho = 10$)**: Varesco's Nikulin-involution family [9, Theorem 2.9] and van Geemen–Schütt's elliptic $2$-isogeny family [14, Proposition 6.2] (whose $2$-torsion translation is itself a symplectic involution, yielding nothing beyond [9]), supplemented at lower ranks by $2$-modulus ($m_V = 4$) cyclic Dickson families (`hodge_conjecture_111` [10], `hodge_conjecture_119` [11]), $1$-modulus period families at $\rho = 16$ (`hodge_conjecture_23` [29], `hodge_conjecture_128` [15]), and six-line double planes [31, 2]. Consequently, for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, **no period-complete source existed beyond $\rho = 10$ ($m_V = 6$)**, leaving $\rho = 8$ ($m_X = 7$, $\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T = 14$) completely unreached and restricting $\rho = 10$ ($m_X = 6$, $\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T = 12$) to Varesco's Nikulin-involution quadratic space.
- Geometrically, Saller [3, Remark 7.3] observed that no smooth-K3 genus-$2$ Humbert pencils analogous to his $8$-modulus $D = 5$ double-plane family had been constructed in explicit equations for discriminants $D \neq 5$—and in particular, even at $\rho = 16$ ($D = 13$), where period-complete algebraic-RM existence and all-power `HC` are already known abstractly via [29, Theorem A] and on six-line double planes via [31, 2], an explicit genus-$2$ Humbert-pencil model over Elkies–Kumar's $Y_-(13)$ [16, §9] and the obstruction to lifting its Weierstrass curve $W_{13}$ to off-boundary plane sextics had not been analyzed.

### 1.2 Summary of main results and separation of new Hodge-conjecture cases from geometric models

Our main advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture is the construction of the **first period-complete $5$-modulus ($\rho = 8, m_V = 7$, $\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,V_8 = 14$) algebraic RM K3 source $\mathcal{F}_{8,\mathrm{poly}}$ for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$** (Theorem A) together with its global $F$-quadratic space and Saller transport down to $\rho(X) \in \{8, 10\}$ ($\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,T \in \{12, 14\}$) and $\rho(Z) \in \{9, 11\}$ on $\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}$-type hyperkähler manifolds (Theorem B), strictly beyond both the rank-$\le 6$ generalized-Kummer locus [28–30] and the rank-$12$ Nikulin-involution locus [9]. Second, we explain why $D = 8$ diverges geometrically from Saller's smooth-sextic $D = 5$ construction by proving that **no smooth plane sextic** carries an off-branch polarized $\mathcal{O}_8$-RM line pencil, analyzing length-$6$ Bézout collisions ($3 A_5$), and constructing companion $5$-modulus Weierstrass ($\rho = 6$) and bidegree-$(4,4)$ ($4 A_1$) $Y_-(8)$-families (Theorem C). Third, as a complementary explicit Humbert-pencil study at $D = 13$ (Theorem D), we construct a $1$-modulus ($\rho = 16, m_V = 3$) reducible-sextic K3 pencil $\mathcal{F}_{W_{13},\mathrm{line}}$ over $W_{13} \subset Y_-(13)$ with pointed $\mathbb{Z}$-integral $\mathcal{O}_{13}$-correspondence, compute its exact $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{13})$-quadratic space via $A_2$ cusp monodromy, and certify a sampled third-order modular jet obstruction on $W_{13}$ (Proposition D.4); as we show explicitly in Theorem D.2–D.3, its rational trace form diagonalizes to $U_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\oplus 2} \oplus \langle -3, -3 \rangle$ (rational Witt index $2$), so all K3-power, Hilbert-scheme, and hyperkähler `HC` statements for this $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{13})$ quadratic type **already follow in stronger form (for all powers)** from Floccari [28, Theorem 5.11], Floccari–Fu [30, Theorem 13.2], and `hodge_conjecture_23` v4 [29, Theorems A–B and Remark 4.3a].

**Theorem A (Period-complete $5$-modulus $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ source $\mathcal{F}_{8,\mathrm{poly}}$ at $\rho = 8, m_V = 7$).**
1. *(Universal Richelot sextic and six-tangent conic norm identity.)* Let
   $$F_8(x; L, P, Q) \;:=\; \operatorname{Res}_b\!\bigl(b^3 - L b^2 + P b - Q,\; 4x^2 + 4bx + 2b^2 - 2Lb + P\bigr) \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}[x, L, P, Q],$$
   weighted homogeneous of degree $6$ for $\operatorname{wt}(x, L, P, Q) = (1, 1, 2, 3)$ with $F_8(1; 0, 0, 0) = 64 = 8^2$. Defining
   $$\mathcal{A} = 16x^3 + 24Lx^2 + (4P - 8L^2)x + 2LP - 8Q, \qquad \mathcal{B} = 8x^2 - 8Lx + 2P, \qquad \mathcal{D} = L^2 - 4Lx - 4x^2 - 2P,$$
   the polynomial $F_8$ satisfies the algebraic norm identity
   $$8 F_8(x; L, P, Q) \;=\; \mathcal{A}(x; L, P, Q)^2 - \mathcal{D}(x; L, P, Q)\,\mathcal{B}(x; L, P, Q)^2.$$
   On the affine slice $(L, P, Q) = (1, p, q)$ ($q \neq 0$), $u^2 = F_8(x; 1, p, q)$ maps via
   $$r = \frac{p^2 + 2pq + q^2 - 4q}{16q^2}, \qquad s = \frac{4p^3 - p^2 - 18pq + 27q^2 + 4q}{16q^2}$$
   birationally over $\mathbb{Q}$ onto the quadratic twist $z_{\mathrm{tw}}^2 = \Phi_8(r, s)/2 = \bigl(\frac{p^4 + p^3 q - 6p^2 q + 8q^2}{4q^3}\bigr)^2$ of Elkies–Kumar's Hilbert modular surface $Y_-(8) : z^2 = \Phi_8(r, s)$ [16, §7], becoming birational to $Y_-(8)$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ with eigenform basis $\omega_+ = dx/u$, $\omega_- = x\,dx/u$.
2. *(Smooth projective family, moduli completeness, and primitive Néron–Severi lattice.)* Substituting $(L, P, Q) = (L_1(t,y), P_2(t,y), Q_3(t,y)) \in H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)) \times H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2)) \times H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(3)) \cong \mathbb{A}^9$ defines an irreducible $9$-dimensional parameter space of degree-$2$ double planes $w^2 = F_8(x; L_1, P_2, Q_3)$ in $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 1, 3)_{(t:y:x;w)}$. Over an explicit nonempty Zariski-open base $U \subset \mathbb{A}^9$, every branch sextic has exactly $6$ ordinary double points ($6 A_1$) along the étale locus $V(\mathcal{A}_b, \mathcal{B}_b)$, and blowing up the reduced relative singular locus $\Sigma = \{w = \mathcal{A} = \mathcal{B} = 0\} \subset \mathcal{X}_U$ yields a smooth projective K3 morphism $\pi : \widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_U \to U$ of **exact K3 moduli rank $5$**. For every $b \in U$, adjoining the splitting-conic class $B_+ = \frac{3}{2}h - \frac{1}{2}C_+ - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^6 E_i$ (where $C_+ + C_- = 2h$ lies over the plane conic $\mathcal{D}_b = 0$ and $B_+ + B_- = 2h - \sum_{i=1}^6 E_i$ lies over $\mathcal{B}_b = 0$) spans a **primitive** rank-$8$ even sublattice $L_8 \hookrightarrow \mathrm{NS}(\widetilde{S}_b)$ of determinant $\det L_8 = -128 = -2^7$ and discriminant group $D(L_8) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^5 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.
3. *(Algebraic $\mathcal{O}_8$-action and automatic endomorphism field.)* For very general $b_* \in U$, $\mathrm{NS}(\widetilde{S}_{b_*}) = L_8$ ($\rho = 8, m_V = 7$), $\widetilde{S}_{b_*}$ admits no Nikulin symplectic involution, every element of $\mathcal{O}_8 = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ acts on $T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*})_{\mathbb{Q}}$ via a pointed $\mathbb{Z}$-integral algebraic cycle on $\widetilde{S}_{b_*} \times \widetilde{S}_{b_*}$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}(b_*, \sqrt{2})$, and Zarhin's theorem forces $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*})_{\mathbb{Q}}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ automatically because $m_V = 7$ is an odd prime.
4. *(Unconditional $\mathbb{Q}$-rational $\rho_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}} = 8$ witness.)* For $(L_1, P_2, Q_3) = (t+y,\, 2t^2 + ty - 2y^2,\, -2t^3 + t^2 y + 2ty^2) \in U(\mathbb{Q})$, the resolved K3 surface $S_{\mathrm{wit}}/\mathbb{Q}$ has good reduction at $p = 3$ and $p = 7$ with geometric reductions $\rho(S_{\mathrm{wit},\overline{\mathbb{F}}_3}) = \rho(S_{\mathrm{wit},\overline{\mathbb{F}}_7}) = 10$ (exhibiting Charles's odd-$F$-rank jump [17, Theorem 1]) and signed Artin–Tate values $-1936 \equiv -1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ over $\mathbb{F}_{3^4}$ versus $-7184 \equiv -449 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ over $\mathbb{F}_{7^5}$. By the squareness of $|\mathrm{Br}|$ [18], $\mathrm{NS}(S_{\mathrm{wit},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) = L_8$, $\rho_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}(S_{\mathrm{wit}}) = 8$, and $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S_{\mathrm{wit}})_{\mathbb{Q}}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.

**Theorem B (Global $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-quadratic space and hyperkähler Hodge transport down to $\rho(X) \in \{8, 10\}$).**
1. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ with real embeddings $\sigma_+(\sqrt{2}) = +\sqrt{2}$, $\sigma_-(\sqrt{2}) = -\sqrt{2}$ and unique dyadic prime $\mathfrak{p}_2 = (\sqrt{2})$. Normalized so that $H^{2,0}(\widetilde{S}_{b_*}) \subset V_{8,\sigma_+} \otimes \mathbb{C}$, the $7$-dimensional $F$-quadratic space $(V_8, \varphi_{V_8})$ ($\mathrm{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}} \circ \varphi_{V_8} = q$) of $\mathcal{F}_{8,\mathrm{poly}}$ has signatures $(2, 5)$ at $\sigma_+$ and $(0, 7)$ at $\sigma_-$, determinant $\det \varphi_{V_8} \equiv -1 \pmod{(F^\times)^2}$, and Hasse invariants $c_{\sigma_+} = +1$, $c_{\sigma_-} = -1$, $c_{\mathfrak{p}_2} = -1$, and $c_v = +1$ for all finite $v \neq \mathfrak{p}_2$:
   $$(V_8, \varphi_{V_8}) \;\cong_F\; \langle 1 + \sqrt{2},\; 1 + \sqrt{2},\; -1,\; -1,\; -1,\; -1,\; -1 \rangle.$$
2. Let $X$ be a complex projective K3 surface with $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \subseteq \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ normalized at $\sigma_+$, $m = \dim_F T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \le 7$ ($\rho(X) = 22 - 2m \ge 8$), and $\delta = \det \varphi_X \in F^\times/(F^\times)^2$. Then $(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}, \varphi_X)$ embeds $F$-isometrically into $(V_8, \varphi_{V_8})$ if and only if:
   - $m \le 4$ ($\rho(X) \ge 14$): **always**;
   - $m = 5$ ($\rho(X) = 12$): $c_v(\varphi_X) = c_v(\varphi_{V_8})$ at every finite place $v$ with $\delta \in (F_v^\times)^2$ *(moreover, every $\rho(X) \ge 12$ K3 surface with $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-RM is already unconditionally covered via Varesco's rank-$6$ source [9] and Saller [4, Corollary 9.2])*;
   - $m = 6$ ($\rho(X) = 10$): $c_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(\varphi_X) = -1$ and $c_v(\varphi_X) = +1$ for all finite $v \nmid 2$, which holds **automatically** whenever $T(X)$ carries a self-adjoint $\mathcal{O}_8[1/2]$-module structure and $|\det T(X)| = 2^a$ ($a \ge 0$);
   - $m = 7$ ($\rho(X) = 8$): $\delta \equiv -1 \pmod{(F^\times)^2}$, $c_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(\varphi_X) = -1$, and $c_v(\varphi_X) = +1$ for all finite $v \nmid 2$, which holds **automatically** whenever $T(X)$ carries a self-adjoint $\mathcal{O}_8[1/2]$-module structure and $|\det T(X)| = 2^{2k+1}$ is an odd power of $2$. Moreover, at $m = 7$, $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ holds **automatically** without any extra endomorphism hypothesis.
3. Whenever $(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}, \varphi_X) \hookrightarrow (V_8, \varphi_{V_8})$ (resp. whenever $Z$ is a projective hyperkähler manifold of $\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}$-type, $n \ge 2$, with $F \subseteq \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}})$, $m_Z = \dim_F T(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}} \le 7$, $\rho(Z) = 23 - 2m_Z \ge 9$, satisfying the identical local conditions for $\varphi_Z$):
   - Every element of $F$ on $T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (resp. $T(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}$) is induced by an algebraic cycle on $X \times X$ (resp. $Z \times Z$), there exists $c \in U$ with an isomorphism of rational Chow motives $h(X) \cong h(\widetilde{S}_c)$ and $h(X^{[j]}) \cong h(\widetilde{S}_c^{[j]})$ for all $j \ge 1$ (resp. an algebraic Mukai-isometric isomorphism $H^*(Z, \mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^*(\widetilde{S}_c^{[n]}, \mathbb{Q})$), and
   - If $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}) = F$ (automatic at $m = 7$, i.e. $\rho(X) = 8$ and $\rho(Z) = 9$), rational `HC` holds for $X^r$ ($r < m$, reaching $X^5$ at $\rho = 10$ and $X^6$ at $\rho = 8$), for $\prod_i X^{[n_i]}$ with $\sum_i \delta(n_i) < m$ (reaching $X^{[20]}$ at $\rho = 10$ and $X^{[27]}$ at $\rho = 8$), and for $Z^k$ whenever $k\,\delta(n) < m_Z$.

**Theorem C (Base-point dichotomy, smooth-sextic impossibility, and companion $5$-modulus $Y_-(8)$-pencil families).**
1. Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective K3 surface and $\Lambda \subset |D|$ a mobile linear pencil without fixed divisorial components whose general member is geometrically integral and whose minimal base-point resolution $f : \widehat{S} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ has generic fiber of genus $2$. Then either $D^2 = 2$ with two simple base points (possibly infinitely near, realizing $S$ through $|D|$ as a degree-$2$ double cover of $\mathbb{P}^2$ with pencil center $P_0 \notin B_6$ or $P_0 \in B_6$), or $D^2 = 4$ with a single base point $x_0 \in S$ of multiplicity $2$ and $|D|$ hyperelliptic (mapping $S$ $2$-to-$1$ onto $Q \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ or the quadric cone $Q_0 \subset \mathbb{P}^3$, with $p_0 = \phi_{|D|}(x_0)$ a smooth point of the branch curve $B \subset Q_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and $\Lambda$ pulled back from the pencil of planes in $\mathbb{P}^3$ containing the tangent line $T_{p_0} B$).
2. *(Impossibility of smooth plane sextics with off-branch polarized $\mathcal{O}_8$-RM pencils, and Bézout length-$6$ singular subscheme.)* **No** smooth plane sextic $B_6 \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ admits a point $P_0 \notin B_6$ whose line pencil lifts generically to a polarized $\mathcal{O}_8$-RM genus-$2$ curve over $\mathbb{C}(t)$. Moreover, for every parameter $b = (L_1, P_2, Q_3) \in \mathbb{A}^9$ in $\mathcal{F}_{8,\mathrm{poly}}$ such that $F_{8,b}$ is reduced, $\mathcal{A}_b$ and $\mathcal{B}_b$ have no common component and cut out a zero-dimensional singular subscheme $\Sigma_b = V(\mathcal{A}_b, \mathcal{B}_b) \subset \operatorname{Sing}(\{F_{8,b} = 0\})$ of Bézout length $6$: over $U$ this consists of $6$ distinct $A_1$ nodes, whereas collisions along degenerate ADE strata can yield fewer distinct singular points whose minimal resolutions remain smooth K3 surfaces—for example, $(L_1, P_2, Q_3) = (0, 0, t y(t - y))$ splits over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ into two smooth cubics $Q_3 - (2 \pm 2i)x^3 = 0$ meeting in **three distinct $A_5$ singularities** along $x = Q_3 = 0$.
3. *(Companion $5$-modulus Weierstrass and bidegree-$(4,4)$ constructions $\mathcal{F}_{W_8}$ and $\mathcal{F}_{8,(4,4)}$.)* Along the Weierstrass boundary $W_8 = \{4r + 4s + 1 = 0\} \subset Y_-(8)$ ($P_0 \in B_6$), the reducible-sextic family
   $$\mathcal{F}_{W_8} :\quad w^2 \;=\; x\Bigl(L_1(t, y)\,x(x + 2M_1(t, y))^2(x + 4M_1(t, y)) - G_5(t, y)\Bigr)$$
   has $5 A_1$ nodes, $5$ K3 moduli, and **very general geometric Picard rank $\rho_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}} = 6$ ($m_V = 8$)**, forming a codimension-$1$ Weierstrass sublocus ($5 < m_V - 2 = 6$) inside its $6$-dimensional RM period domain. ## 2. Universal Richelot Normal Form over $Y_-(8)$ and the Multiplier Dichotomy

### 2.1 Richelot differential trace and the Exhaustive Normal-Form Lemma

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ containing $\sqrt{2}$. Let $C/K$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $2$ with Jacobian $J = J(C)$, principal polarization $\Theta$, and Rosati involution $\dagger$. Suppose $\iota : \mathcal{O}_8 = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}] \hookrightarrow \mathrm{End}_K(J)$ is a $K$-rational RM embedding fixed by $\dagger$ ($\alpha^\dagger = \alpha$ for $\alpha = \iota(\sqrt{2})$, $\alpha^2 = 2$). The induced action of $\alpha$ on $H^0(C, \omega_C)$ is diagonalizable over $K$ with eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{2}$ (trace $0$, determinant $-2$, since $\alpha$ cannot act scalarly on a $2$-dimensional $K$-vector space when $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}$ in characteristic $0$, or directly by faithfulness on $T_\ell J$). Let $\omega_+, \omega_- \in H^0(C, \omega_C) \setminus \{0\}$ be $\pm\sqrt{2}$-eigenforms.

Assume that $\omega_+$ has **two distinct zeros** on $C(\overline{K})$—equivalently, the hyperelliptic divisor $\operatorname{div}(\omega_+)$ is not $2P_W$ for a Weierstrass point $P_W$. Putting $x := \omega_- / \omega_+ \in K(C)$ realizes $C$ as a degree-$2$ cover $u^2 = c_0 F_6(x)$ ($\deg F_6 = 6$, $c_0 \in K^\times$) with $\omega_+ = dx / u$ and $\omega_- = x\,dx / u$ (putting the two distinct zeros of $\omega_+$ over $x = \infty$).

Since $N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2}) = -2$, the endomorphism $\alpha = \iota(\sqrt{2})$ has degree $4$, satisfies $\alpha^\dagger \alpha = \alpha^2 = 2 = [2]_J \circ \mathrm{id}$, and has kernel $G := \ker(\alpha) = \alpha(J[2]) \subset J[2](\overline{K})$. Because $\alpha$ is defined over $K$ and self-adjoint for the Weil pairing $e_2(P, \alpha Q) = e_2(\alpha P, Q)$ ($e_2(\alpha A, \alpha B) = e_2(A, 2B) = 1$), $G$ is a $K$-rational maximal $e_2$-isotropic $(2, 2)$-subgroup of $J[2]$ containing no nontrivial subgroup of type $(2, 2)$ other than itself, and $J/G \cong J$ via the induced isomorphism $\bar{\alpha} : J/G \xrightarrow{\sim} J$ factoring $\alpha = \bar{\alpha} \circ \phi_G$.

By Bost–Mestre / Richelot theory [21, §3], a $K$-rational maximal isotropic $(2, 2)$-subgroup $G \subset J[2]$ corresponds to a $K$-rational factorization into three quadratic factors $G_i(x) = x^2 + b_i x + c_i$ (individually over $\overline{K}$, as an unordered triple over $K$) with $u^2 = c_0 G_1(x) G_2(x) G_3(x)$ and nonzero determinant
$$\Delta \;:=\; \det\!\begin{pmatrix} c_1 & b_1 & 1 \\ c_2 & b_2 & 1 \\ c_3 & b_3 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \;\neq\; 0$$
(if $\Delta = 0$, $J/G$ splits as a product of elliptic curves, contradicting $\mathcal{O}_8 \hookrightarrow \mathrm{End}(J)$ for smooth $C$ on the generic RM locus, or more generally whenever $\Delta \neq 0$; note $\Delta = 0$ is impossible when $\phi_G$ factors an endomorphism of $J(C)$ for smooth genus-$2$ $C$ since $J(C)$ is irreducible in $\mathcal{A}_2$). For $(i, j, k)$ cyclic permutations of $(1, 2, 3)$, define the Wronskians
$$W_{jk}(x) \;:=\; G_j'(x) G_k(x) - G_j(x) G_k'(x), \qquad H_i(x) \;:=\; W_{jk}(-x) \;=\; \lambda_i x^2 + \cdots, \quad \lambda_i = b_k - b_j.$$
In the reflected coordinate $Y$ of `verify_d8_d13_rm_k3.py`, the Richelot-dual curve $C'$ has equation $\Delta v^2 = c_0 W_{23}(Y) W_{31}(Y) W_{12}(Y)$, and the correspondence curve $Z \subset C \times C'$ is cut out by
$$R(x, Y) \;:=\; G_1(x) W_{23}(Y) + G_2(x) W_{31}(Y) \;=\; 0, \qquad u v \;=\; c_0 G_1(x) W_{23}(Y)(x - Y).$$
Let $\phi_Z : J(C) \to J(C')$ be the Richelot $(2, 2)$-isogeny induced by $Z$.

**Lemma C.3 (Exhaustive Richelot Normal-Form Lemma).** *Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ with $\sqrt{2} \in K$, and let $C : u^2 = c_0 G_1(x) G_2(x) G_3(x)$ ($G_i(x) = x^2 + b_i x + c_i \in \overline{K}[x]$, $\Delta \neq 0$, $\operatorname{disc}(G_1 G_2 G_3) \neq 0$) be a smooth genus-$2$ curve whose Richelot $(2, 2)$-isogeny $\phi_Z : J(C) \to J(C')$ (with kernel $G = \ker(\phi_Z) = \ker(\alpha) \subset J(C)[2]$ corresponding to $\{G_1, G_2, G_3\}$) composes with an isomorphism $\varphi : C \xrightarrow{\sim} C'$ to induce a Rosati self-adjoint endomorphism $\alpha = \pm (\varphi^{-1})_* \circ \phi_Z \in \mathrm{End}_K(J(C))$ satisfying $\alpha^2 = +2$ with eigenforms $\omega_+ = dx/u$ and $\omega_- = x\,dx/u$ ($\alpha^*\omega_\pm = \pm\varepsilon\sqrt{2}\,\omega_\pm$, $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$). Then:*

1. *In the generic quadratic algebra $\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}, x)[Y]/(R(x, Y))$, the exact trace identity*
   $$\phi_Z^*\!\left(\frac{Y^m\,dY}{v}\right) \;=\; \frac{x^m\,dx}{u} \qquad (m = 0, 1)$$
   *holds up to a global sign choice of $v$, forcing $\varphi^*(Y) = -x$, $H_i(x) = \lambda_i G_{\pi(i)}(x)$ for a unique permutation $\pi \in S_3$, $\varphi^* v = \mp \varepsilon\sqrt{2}\,u$, and the positive multiplier equation*
   $$\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 \;=\; +2\Delta.$$
2. *Over $\overline{K}$, among the $6$ permutations $\pi \in S_3$ of the roots $\{b_1, b_2, b_3\}$:*
   - *both $3$-cycles generate the unit ideal `[1]` under smoothness $\operatorname{disc}(G_1 G_2 G_3) \neq 0$;*
   - *all three transpositions satisfy $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 = -2\Delta$, forcing $(\varphi^* v / u)^2 = -2$ and $\alpha^2 = -2$ (skew-Rosati endomorphisms of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$, genuinely realized on smooth curves such as $(b_1, b_2, b_3; c_1, c_2, c_3) = (0, -1, 1; \frac{1}{4}, -\frac{1}{4}, -\frac{1}{4})$);*
   - *the identity $\pi = \mathrm{id}$ is the **unique** smooth branch satisfying $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 = +2\Delta$, and it forces*
     $$4 c_i \;=\; 2 b_i^2 - 2 L b_i + P \quad (i = 1, 2, 3), \qquad \Delta \;=\; \frac{1}{2}(b_1 - b_2)(b_2 - b_3)(b_3 - b_1),$$
     *where $L = \sum b_i$, $P = \sum_{i<j} b_i b_j$, $Q = b_1 b_2 b_3$ lie in $K$, and $64 G_1(x) G_2(x) G_3(x) = F_8(x; L, P, Q)$.*

*Proof.* Set $u_i(x, Y) = G_i(x) W_{jk}(Y)$ for cyclic $(i, j, k)$, so $R(x, Y) = u_1 + u_2$. Expanding directly (`richelot_pullback()` in `verify_d8_d13_rm_k3.py`), we have the universal polynomial identities
$$u_1 + u_2 + u_3 \;=\; -\Delta (x - Y)^2, \qquad u_1 u_2 u_3 - \Delta\bigl(u_1(x - Y)\bigr)^2 + R\,u_1\bigl(\Delta(x - Y)^2 + u_2\bigr) \;=\; 0.$$
Hence modulo $R(x, Y)$, $\Delta v^2 = c_0 W_{23}(Y) W_{31}(Y) W_{12}(Y)$ satisfies $(u v)^2 \equiv \bigl(c_0 G_1(x) W_{23}(Y)(x - Y)\bigr)^2$, confirming that $u v = c_0 G_1(x) W_{23}(Y)(x - Y)$ lifts $R = 0$ to $Z \subset C \times C'$. Differentiating $R(x, Y) = 0$ along $Z$ gives $R_x\,dx + R_Y\,dY = 0$, while evaluating $R(x, x) = G_1 G_2' G_3 - G_1 G_2 G_3' + G_2 G_3' G_1 - G_2 G_3 G_1'$ yields the syzygy
$$-G_2(x) G_3(x)\,R_x(x, Y) - R(x, x)\,W_{23}(Y) + G_3(x) G_2'(x)\,R(x, Y) \;=\; 0.$$
Substituting $dY = -(R_x / R_Y)\,dx$ and $v = c_0 G_1(x) W_{23}(Y)(x - Y)/u$ on $Z$ reduces the pullback of $Y^m\,dY/v$ to the trace in the rank-$2$ algebra $\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}, x)[Y]/(R(x, Y))$:
$$\phi_Z^*\!\left(\frac{Y^m\,dY}{v}\right) \;=\; \operatorname{Tr}_{R(x,\cdot)=0}\!\left(\frac{Y^m\,R(x, x)}{(x - Y)\,R_Y(x, Y)}\right) \frac{dx}{u}.$$
Writing $R(x, Y) = a Y^2 + b Y + c$ ($d_{\mathrm{disc}} = b^2 - 4ac \neq 0$, certified generically at a rational specialization in `richelot_pullback()`), we have $(R(x, x) - R(x, Y))/(x - Y) = a(x + Y) + b$ and $R_Y^2 \equiv d_{\mathrm{disc}} \pmod R$. Computing the multiplication matrix of $Y^m (a(x + Y) + b)(2aY + b)$ in the basis $(1, Y)$ modulo $R$ gives trace $d_{\mathrm{disc}}\,x^m$ identically for $m = 0, 1$, proving $\phi_Z^*(Y^m\,dY/v) = x^m\,dx/u$.

By Torelli's theorem for genus-$2$ curves, the polarized isomorphism $\bar{\alpha} : (J(C'), \Theta') \xrightarrow{\sim} (J(C), \Theta)$ is induced up to $[-1]_J$ by an isomorphism of curves $\varphi : C \xrightarrow{\sim} C'$, so $\alpha = \pm (\varphi^{-1})_* \circ \phi_Z$, i.e. $\varphi_* \circ \alpha = \pm \phi_Z$ and hence $\alpha^* \circ \varphi^* = \pm \phi_Z^*$ on $H^0(C', \omega_{C'})$. Applying $(\alpha^*)^{-1}$ using $\alpha^*(dx/u) = \varepsilon\sqrt{2}\,dx/u$ and $\alpha^*(x\,dx/u) = -\varepsilon\sqrt{2}\,x\,dx/u$ gives
$$\varphi^*\!\left(\frac{dY}{v}\right) \;=\; \pm \frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{dx}{u}, \qquad \varphi^*\!\left(\frac{Y\,dY}{v}\right) \;=\; \mp \frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{x\,dx}{u}.$$
Taking the ratio of these two holomorphic $1$-forms yields $\varphi^*(Y) = -x$ on $C$. Substituting $\varphi^*(Y) = -x$ into the first differential gives $-\frac{dx}{\varphi^* v} = \pm \frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{2}}\frac{dx}{u}$, hence $\varphi^* v = \mp \varepsilon\sqrt{2}\,u$, so $(\varphi^* v)^2 = +2 u^2 = 2 c_0 G_1(x) G_2(x) G_3(x)$. Pulling back $\Delta v^2 = c_0 W_{23}(Y) W_{31}(Y) W_{12}(Y)$ under $\varphi^*(Y) = -x$ and using $W_{jk}(-x) = H_i(x)$ gives
$$2 \Delta\,c_0 G_1(x) G_2(x) G_3(x) \;=\; c_0 H_1(x) H_2(x) H_3(x).$$
Comparing leading $x^6$-coefficients immediately forces $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 = +2\Delta$. To verify that $\varphi^*(Y) = -x$ identifies the quadratic factors $G_1, G_2, G_3$ with $\lambda_i^{-1} H_i$ up to permutation in $S_3$ (rather than re-pairing their six linear roots over $\overline{K}$), recall that the $15 = \binom{6}{2}$ nonzero elements of $J(C)[2](\overline{K})$ biject canonically with unordered pairs $\{P_a, P_b\}$ of Weierstrass points via $[P_a + P_b - K_C]$, and since $\sum_{m=1}^6 P_m \sim 3 K_C$, the $15 = \frac{6!}{(2!)^3 \cdot 3!}$ partitions of the six Weierstrass points into three disjoint pairs biject canonically with the $15$ maximal $e_2$-isotropic $(2, 2)$-subgroups of $J(C)[2]$. By Bost–Mestre [21, §3], $G = \ker(\phi_Z) \subset J(C)[2]$ corresponds to the three-pair partition defined by $\{G_1, G_2, G_3\}$, whereas the dual Richelot $(2, 2)$-isogeny $\phi_Z^{\mathrm{dual}} = \lambda_\Theta^{-1} \circ \widehat{\phi_Z} \circ \lambda_{\Theta'} : J(C') \to J(C)$ has kernel $G' = \phi_Z(J(C)[2]) \subset J(C')[2]$ corresponding to the three-pair partition defined by $\{W_{23}(Y), W_{31}(Y), W_{12}(Y)\} = \{H_1(-Y), H_2(-Y), H_3(-Y)\}$. Writing $\alpha = \bar{\alpha} \circ \phi_Z$ with polarized isomorphism $\bar{\alpha} = \pm(\varphi^{-1})_* : (J(C'), \Theta') \xrightarrow{\sim} (J(C), \Theta)$ ($\widehat{\bar{\alpha}} \circ \lambda_\Theta = \lambda_{\Theta'} \circ \bar{\alpha}^{-1}$), the Rosati adjoint of $\alpha$ is
$$\alpha^\dagger \;=\; \lambda_\Theta^{-1} \circ \widehat{\phi_Z} \circ \widehat{\bar{\alpha}} \circ \lambda_\Theta \;=\; \phi_Z^{\mathrm{dual}} \circ \bar{\alpha}^{-1}.$$
Because $\alpha^\dagger = \alpha$ (indeed $\alpha^\dagger = \pm\alpha$ already suffices), $\ker(\alpha) = \ker(\alpha^\dagger)$ gives $G = \bar{\alpha}(\ker \phi_Z^{\mathrm{dual}}) = \bar{\alpha}(G')$, i.e. $\varphi_*(G) = G'$ in $J(C')[2]$ (since $[-1]$ acts trivially on $2$-torsion). Hence $\varphi : C \xrightarrow{\sim} C'$ carries the Weierstrass three-pair partition of $\{G_1, G_2, G_3\}$ onto that of $\{H_1(-Y), H_2(-Y), H_3(-Y)\}$; under $\varphi^*(Y) = -x$, the unordered triple of monic quadratics $\{G_1(x), G_2(x), G_3(x)\}$ must equal $\{\lambda_1^{-1} H_1(x), \lambda_2^{-1} H_2(x), \lambda_3^{-1} H_3(x)\}$, giving $H_i(x) = \lambda_i G_{\pi(i)}(x)$ for a unique permutation $\pi \in S_3$.

Finally, in `richelot()` we compute exact Gröbner bases over $\mathbb{Q}$ in `grevlex` order for the polynomial system $\{H_i(x) - \lambda_i G_{\pi(i)}(x) \equiv 0,\; u_{\mathrm{inv}} \prod_{i<j}(b_i - b_j) \prod_i (b_i^2 - 4c_i) - 1 = 0\}$ across all $6$ permutations $\pi \in S_3$:
- For both $3$-cycles $\pi \in \{(1, 2, 0), (2, 0, 1)\}$, the Gröbner basis is `[1]`.
- For all three transpositions $\pi \in \{(0, 2, 1), (2, 1, 0), (1, 0, 2)\}$, $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 + 2\Delta$ reduces to $0$ modulo the ideal (corresponding to $(\varphi^* v / u)^2 = -2$ and $\alpha^2 = -2$), and adjoining $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 - 2\Delta$ yields `[1]`.
- For $\pi = \mathrm{id} = (0, 1, 2)$, the ideal reduces $4c_i - (2b_i^2 - 2Lb_i + P)$ ($i = 1, 2, 3$) to $0$, giving $\Delta = \frac{1}{2}(b_1 - b_2)(b_2 - b_3)(b_3 - b_1) = \frac{1}{2}\lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3$. Since the unordered triple $\{G_1, G_2, G_3\}$ is $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)$-stable, $\{b_1, b_2, b_3\}$ is $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)$-stable, so $L, P, Q \in K$, and the resultant over $b^3 - Lb^2 + Pb - Q = 0$ equals $64 G_1 G_2 G_3 = F_8(x; L, P, Q)$. $\square$

### 2.2 Proof of Theorem A.1 and Weierstrass locus identification

Direct polynomial expansion (`richelot()` and `charts_and_laurent()` in `verify_d8_d13_rm_k3.py`) verifies $8 F_8(x; L, P, Q) = \mathcal{A}^2 - \mathcal{D}\mathcal{B}^2$ for the explicit weighted polynomials $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{D}$ of Theorem A.1. Note carefully that when $(L, P, Q)$ are specialized to binary forms of degrees $(1, 2, 3)$ in $(t, y)$, $\mathcal{D}(x; L_1, P_2, Q_3) = L_1^2 - 4 L_1 x - 4x^2 - 2 P_2$ is a **homogeneous plane conic** in $(t, y, x)$, distinct from the Hilbert modular orientation-cover polynomial $\Phi_8(r, s)$ below.

### 3.2 Two-prime Weil/Artin–Tate certification of $S_{\mathrm{wit}}$ (Theorem A.4)

This completes Theorem A. $\square$

## 4. Global $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-Quadratic Space and Hyperkähler Transport

### 4.1 Local-global determination of $(V_8, \varphi_{V_8})$ (Theorem B.1)

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ ($\mathcal{O}_8 = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$, class number $1$, fundamental unit $\varepsilon_8 = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ of norm $-1$, different $\mathfrak{D}_{F/\mathbb{Q}} = (2\sqrt{2})$).    Over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*})) = -\det \mathrm{NS}(\widetilde{S}_{b_*}) = +128 = 2^7$, because $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$ is unimodular and $L_8 \subset \Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$ is primitive.    $$2^7 \;=\; \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(V_8, q) \;\equiv\; 8^7 N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\det \varphi_{V_8}) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\det \varphi_{V_8}) \;\equiv\; +1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$
   Since $\mathcal{O}_8$ has class number $1$ and §3.1 shows that the pointed cycle $\Gamma_{b_*}$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-integral and self-adjoint for $q$ ($q(\Gamma_{b_*,*} x, n) = q(x, \Gamma_{b_*,*} n) = 0$ for $n \in L_8$), $\Gamma_{b_*,*}$ preserves the integral lattice $T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*}) = L_8^\perp \subset \Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$, making $T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*})$ an $\mathcal{O}_8$-lattice. For every odd prime $p$, $p \nmid 2^7 = |\det T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*})|$ and $p \nmid \mathfrak{D}_{F/\mathbb{Q}} = (2\sqrt{2})$ imply that $(T(\widetilde{S}_{b_*}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p, \varphi_{V_8})$ is a unimodular symmetric $\mathcal{O}_{8,v}$-lattice at every finite place $v \nmid 2$. Therefore $v(\det \varphi_{V_8}) \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and $c_v(\varphi_{V_8}) = +1$ for every finite place $v \neq \mathfrak{p}_2$. Consequently $\det \varphi_{V_8}$ lies in $\{\pm 1\} \times \langle \varepsilon_8 \rangle \times \langle \sqrt{2} \rangle \pmod{(F^\times)^2}$; since $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon_8) = -1$, $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2}) = -2$, and $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon_8\sqrt{2}) = +2$, the condition $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\det \varphi_{V_8}) \equiv +1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ restricts this subgroup to $\{+1, -1\} \pmod{(F^\times)^2}$, and total negativity uniquely selects $\det \varphi_{V_8} \equiv -1 \pmod{(F^\times)^2}$.

2. **Dyadic Hasse invariant and explicit diagonal model:** By Hilbert reciprocity over $F$, $\prod_v c_v(\varphi_{V_8}) = +1$. Since $c_{\sigma_+} = +1$, $c_{\sigma_-} = -1$, and $c_v = +1$ for all $v \nmid 2\infty$, the unique dyadic place $\mathfrak{p}_2 = (\sqrt{2})$ must satisfy $c_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(\varphi_{V_8}) = -1$. Comparing with $W = \langle 1+\sqrt{2}, 1+\sqrt{2}, -1, -1, -1, -1, -1 \rangle_F$: at $\sigma_+$, $1+\sqrt{2} > 0$ gives signature $(2, 5)$; at $\sigma_-$, $1-\sqrt{2} < 0$ gives signature $(0, 7)$; $\det W = (1+\sqrt{2})^2 (-1)^5 \equiv -1 \pmod{(F^\times)^2}$; and for all $v \nmid 2\infty$, $1+\sqrt{2} \in \mathcal{O}_{8,v}^\times$ and $-1 \in \mathcal{O}_{8,v}^\times$ give $c_v(W) = +1$, forcing $c_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(W) = -1$ by reciprocity. By the Hasse–Minkowski theorem over $F$, $(V_8, \varphi_{V_8}) \cong_F W$. As an independent check over $\mathbb{Q}$, `local_invariants()` in `lattices.py` verifies that $\mathrm{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(W) \oplus L_8$ and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$ have identical signature $(3, 19)$, determinant squareclass, and $\mathbb{Q}_p$-Hasse invariants at $\infty$ and every prime. $\square$

## 5. Smooth-Sextic Impossibility and Companion $5$-Modulus $Y_-(8)$-Pencil Families (Theorem C)

### 5.1 Base-point dichotomy on smooth K3 surfaces (Theorem C.1)

Let $\Lambda \subset |D|$ be a mobile $1$-dimensional linear system without fixed divisorial components on a smooth projective K3 surface $S$, whose general member is geometrically integral and whose minimal base-point resolution $\pi : \widehat{S} \to S$ yields a morphism $f : \widehat{S} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ with smooth generic fiber $F$ of genus $2$. Let $\pi$ be the composition of $r \ge 0$ point blow-ups (including infinitely near points), with total transforms $E_1^*, \dots, E_r^*$ satisfying $E_i^* \cdot E_j^* = -\delta_{ij}$ and $K_{\widehat{S}} = \sum_{i=1}^r E_i^*$. Writing $\pi^* D = F + \sum_{i=1}^r m_i E_i^*$ ($m_i \ge 1$ for every blow-up in the minimal resolution of $\Lambda$), adjunction on $\widehat{S}$ gives $F^2 = 0$ and $F \cdot K_{\widehat{S}} = 2g(F) - 2 = 2$. Pairing with $K_{\widehat{S}}$ and with $F + \sum m_i E_i^*$ yields
$$\sum_{i=1}^r m_i \;=\; -\Bigl(F - \pi^* D\Bigr) \cdot K_{\widehat{S}} \;=\; F \cdot K_{\widehat{S}} \;=\; 2, \qquad D^2 \;=\; (\pi^* D)^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^r m_i^2.$$
Because each $m_i \ge 1$ is a positive integer, only two partitions of $2$ exist:
1. **$r = 2$, $(m_1, m_2) = (1, 1)$, $D^2 = 2$:** $D$ is big and nef ($D$ has no fixed components and $D^2 = 2 > 0$). By Saint-Donat [26, §2–4], $|D|$ is base-point-free (an unigonal decomposition $D = a E + \Gamma$ with $E^2 = 0, E \cdot \Gamma = 1, \Gamma^2 = -2$ has $\Gamma$ as a fixed component of $|D|$, contradicting our hypothesis) and defines a degree-$2$ morphism $\phi_{|D|} : S \to \mathbb{P}^2$. Since $\dim |D| = 2$, the subpencil $\Lambda \subset |D|$ is the pullback of the pencil of lines through a point $P_0 \in \mathbb{P}^2$: two distinct base points on $S$ when $P_0 \notin B_6$, and one base point plus an infinitely near tangent direction when $P_0 \in B_6$.
2. **$r = 1$, $(m_1) = (2)$, $D^2 = 4$:** Again $|D|$ is base-point-free of dimension $3$ [26, §4–5], and every member of $\Lambda$ has multiplicity $\ge 2$ at a fixed point $x_0 \in S$. Thus $\dim H^0(S, \mathcal{O}_S(D) \otimes \mathfrak{m}_{x_0}^2) \ge \dim \Lambda + 1 = 2$ inside $\dim H^0(S, \mathcal{O}_S(D)) = 4$, meaning the $3$ jet conditions at $x_0$ drop rank by $\le 2$, so $d\phi_{|D|}(x_0)$ is not an immersion. By Saint-Donat [26, §5.2, §5.6] (ruling out a birational quartic $S_4 \subset \mathbb{P}^3$: at a smooth point $d\phi_{|D|}$ is immersive, while at a point on a fundamental $(-2)$-curve $\Gamma$ contracted to a singular point $q \in S_4$, planes singular at $x_0$ pass through $q$ and hence pull back with $\Gamma$ as a fixed component), $|D|$ is **hyperelliptic of degree $4$**, mapping $S$ via $\phi_{|D|}$ of degree $2$ onto either $Q \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ (branched along a smooth $(4,4)$-curve $B$) or the quadric cone $Q_0 \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ (branched along a smooth curve $B = Q_0 \cap F_4$ missing the vertex $v_0$, with $\phi_{|D|}^{-1}(v_0) = \tilde{E}_0^+ \sqcup \tilde{E}_0^-$ consisting of two contracted $(-2)$-curves). By the same fixed-component check, $p_0 := \phi_{|D|}(x_0) \neq v_0$; if $p_0 \in Q_{\mathrm{reg}} \setminus B$, $\phi_{|D|}$ is étale at $x_0$ so only the single tangent plane $T_{p_0} Q$ pulls back singularly ($\dim \le 0$). Therefore $p_0 \in B \subset Q_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a smooth ramification point: in local coordinates $(w, v)$ on $S$ mapping via $(u, v) = (w^2, v)$ with $B = \{u = 0\}$, a plane section $a u + b v + \cdots = 0$ pulls back with multiplicity $\ge 2$ at $x_0$ if and only if $b = 0$, i.e. the plane $H \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ contains the tangent line $T_{p_0} B$. $\square$

### 5.2 Proof of Theorem C.2 (Smooth-sextic impossibility and Bézout length-$6$ collisions)

Suppose first for contradiction that $B_6 \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ is a **smooth** plane sextic, $S : w^2 = B_6$ is the smooth K3 double plane, and $P_0 = (0:0:1) \notin B_6$ is a point whose blown-up line pencil $f : \widehat{S} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ ($K = \mathbb{C}(t)$) carries a Rosati-fixed embedding $\iota : \mathcal{O}_8 \hookrightarrow \mathrm{End}_K(J(C_\eta))$.

1. **Global Picard–Néron Hodge-bundle splitting:** Choose homogeneous coordinates $(t:y:z)$ with $P_0 = (0:0:1)$ and $B_6(0, 0, 1) = c_0 \neq 0$. Because $B_6$ is smooth and $P_0 \notin B_6$, no line $\ell \ni P_0$ is contained in $B_6$, so $B_6|_\ell$ is a nonzero binary sextic on every fiber. Even if $B_6|_\ell$ has multiple roots—including the extreme cases $B_6|_\ell = c\,g_3(z)^2$ (a tritangent line) or $B_6|_\ell = c(z - z_0)^6$ (a six-fold tangent)—the double cover $w^2 = B_6|_\ell$ in $\widehat{S}$ is either irreducible and reduced, or splits into two distinct smooth rational curves $w = \pm \sqrt{c}\,g_3(z)$ meeting with intersection number $3$: **every geometric fiber of $f : \widehat{S} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is reduced and connected**, $\widehat{S}$ is smooth and proper over $\mathbb{P}^1$, and the exceptional curves $E_+, E_-$ over $P_0$ are sections of $f$. By Raynaud's theorem (Bosch–Lütkebohmert–Raynaud [19, §9.5, Theorem 4(b)]), the open subscheme $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathbf{0}}_{\widehat{S}/\mathbb{P}^1} \subset \mathrm{Pic}^0_{\widehat{S}/\mathbb{P}^1}$ parameterizing line bundles of **multidegree $(0, \dots, 0)$** on all irreducible components of every geometric fiber is smooth and separated over $\mathbb{P}^1$ and coincides over **all** of $\mathbb{P}^1$ with the identity component $\mathcal{J}^0$ of the Néron model $\mathcal{J}$ of $J(C_\eta)$.

   By the Néron mapping property, $\iota(\sqrt{2}) \in \mathrm{End}_K(J(C_\eta))$ extends uniquely to an endomorphism of $\mathcal{J}$, preserving the identity component $\mathcal{J}^0$ and hence acting $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}$-linearly on $\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{J}^0) = \mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathbf{0}}_{\widehat{S}/\mathbb{P}^1}) = R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}_{\widehat{S}}$ and its dual Hodge bundle $(\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathcal{J}^0)^\vee \cong f_* \omega_{\widehat{S}/\mathbb{P}^1}$ (relative duality for the Gorenstein morphism $f$). Computing explicitly in coordinates $B_6(t, 1, z)$ shows that the sections $\tilde{\omega}_0 = dz / w$ and $\tilde{\omega}_1 = z\,dz / w$ generate $f_* \omega_{\widehat{S}/\mathbb{P}^1} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2)\,\tilde{\omega}_0 \oplus \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)\,\tilde{\omega}_1$ globally over $\mathbb{P}^1_{(t:y)}$. Since $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(\mathcal{O}(2), \mathcal{O}(1)) = H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-1)) = 0$, any endomorphism of $\mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(1)$ with eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{2}$ over $\mathbb{C}(t)$ is upper-triangular with distinct constant diagonal entries $\pm\sqrt{2}$, hence diagonalizable over $\mathbb{C}[t, y]$ with eigenbundles $\mathcal{L}_+ \cong \mathcal{O}(2)$ and $\mathcal{L}_- \cong \mathcal{O}(1)$. Because $\mathcal{O}(2)$ is generated by $\tilde{\omega}_0 = dz / w$—which vanishes at the **two distinct points** $E_+|_t \neq E_-|_t$ over $z = \infty$ on every fiber—the $\mathcal{O}(2)$-eigenform $\omega_+ = \tilde{\omega}_0$ automatically satisfies the distinct-zeros hypothesis of Lemma C.3, while the $\mathcal{O}(1)$-eigenform has the form $\omega_- = (z + b(t))\,dz / w$ for a linear polynomial $b(t) = \beta_1 t + \beta_0$.

2. **Exhaustive Richelot normal form and Laurent pole obstruction:** Applying Lemma C.3 to $C_\eta / \mathbb{C}(t)$ with $x_{\mathrm{eig}} = \omega_- / \omega_+ = z + b(t)$ yields unique rational functions $L(t), P(t), Q(t) \in \mathbb{C}(t)$ such that
   $$64 c_0^{-1} B_6(t, 1, z) \;=\; F_8\bigl(z + b(t);\; L(t), P(t), Q(t)\bigr).$$
   Write $64 c_0^{-1} B_6(t, 1, z) = 64 z^6 + 64 s_1(t) z^5 + 48 s_2(t) z^4 - 32 s_3(t) z^3 + f_2(t) z^2 + f_1(t) z + f_0(t)$ with $\deg_t s_k \le k$ and $\deg_t f_j \le 6 - j$. As verified symbolically for indeterminate $(s_1, s_2, s_3)$ in `charts_and_laurent()` (`verify_d8_d13_rm_k3.py`), matching the coefficients of $z^5, z^4, z^3$ solves uniquely for $(L, P, Q)$ in terms of $b(t)$:
   $$L = s_1 - 6b, \qquad P = 20 b^2 - \frac{20}{3} s_1 b + s_2, \qquad Q = 40 b^3 - 20 s_1 b^2 + 6 s_2 b + s_3,$$
   and substituting these into the coefficients of $z^2, z^1, z^0$ produces polynomials in $b$ of degrees $(4, 5, 6)$ with **nonzero constant leading coefficients** $(15360, 57344, 81920)$. Since $b(t) = \beta_1 t + \beta_0 \in \mathbb{C}[t]_{\le 1}$ (and even without using $\deg b \le 1$, any finite pole of $b(t)$ of order $m \ge 1$ would create an uncancellable pole of order $4m$ in $f_2(t)$, while $v_\infty(b) < -1$ would force $\deg_t f_2 = -4 v_\infty(b) \ge 8 > 4$), $(L(t), P(t), Q(t))$ are forced to be polynomials of degrees $\le (1, 2, 3)$ in $t$. Homogenizing $(t, y)$ and absorbing $z \mapsto z - \beta_1 t - \beta_0 y$ places $B_6$ inside the $\mathrm{GL}_3$-orbit of $\mathcal{F}_{8,\mathrm{poly}}$, where $8 F_8 = \mathcal{A}^2 - \mathcal{D}\mathcal{B}^2$ forces $\operatorname{Sing}(B_6) \supseteq V(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B})_{\mathrm{red}} \neq \emptyset$, contradicting smoothness of $B_6$.

3. **Bézout length-$6$ singular subscheme and $3 A_5$ collision witness in $\mathcal{F}_{8,\mathrm{poly}}$:** Now let $b = (L_1, P_2, Q_3) \in \mathbb{A}^9$ be **any** parameter for which $F_{8,b}$ is reduced. Then $\mathcal{A}_b$ and $\mathcal{B}_b$ share no common factor of positive degree (any common factor $G$ would divide $8 F_{8,b} = \mathcal{A}_b^2 - \mathcal{D}_b \mathcal{B}_b^2$ to order $\ge 2$, contradicting reducedness). By Bézout's theorem on $\mathbb{P}^2$, $\Sigma_b = V(\mathcal{A}_b, \mathcal{B}_b)$ is a zero-dimensional subscheme of **exact length $3 \cdot 2 = 6$**, and $8 F_{8,b} \in (\mathcal{A}_b, \mathcal{B}_b)^2$ implies $\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{red}} \subseteq \operatorname{Sing}(\{F_{8,b} = 0\})$. While $\Sigma_b$ consists of $6$ distinct $A_1$ nodes over $U$, collisions along degenerate strata where $\mathcal{B}_b$ is non-reduced or tangent to $\mathcal{A}_b$ can yield fewer than $6$ distinct singular points while retaining only rational double points (hence still resolving to smooth K3 surfaces with off-branch $Y_-(8)$-pencils). Concretely, setting $L_1 = P_2 = 0$ and $Q_3(t, y) = t y(t - y)$ (`collision_a5()` in `geometry.py`) gives $\mathcal{A}_{b_0} = 16x^3 - 8 Q_3$, $\mathcal{B}_{b_0} = 8x^2$, and
   $$F_8(x; 0, 0, Q_3) \;=\; 8\bigl(Q_3^2 - 4 Q_3 x^3 + 8 x^6\bigr) \;=\; 8\bigl((Q_3 - 2x^3)^2 + 4 x^6\bigr) \;=\; 8\bigl(Q_3 - (2 + 2i)x^3\bigr)\bigl(Q_3 - (2 - 2i)x^3\bigr).$$
   Here $\Sigma_{b_0} = \{x^2 = 0,\; Q_3(t, y) = 0\}$ has Bézout length $6$ supported at **three** distinct points $(0:1:0)$, $(1:1:0)$, $(1:0:0)$ (multiplicity $2$ each). At each point, $u = Q_3$ is a local coordinate transverse to $x = 0$, and $8((u - 2x^3)^2 + 4x^6)$ has partial-derivative ideal $(u - 2x^3, x^5)$ of Milnor colength $5$, certifying **three $A_5$ rational double points** ($3 A_5$) whose minimal resolution is a smooth K3 surface. $\square$
