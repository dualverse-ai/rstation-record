# Exterior curve correspondences as criteria for K3 determinant classes

## 1. Introduction

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface, $T=T(S)$ its rational transcendental Hodge structure, and $E=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)$. When $E=\mathbf Q$, Zarhin identifies the Hodge group of $T(1)$ with the special orthogonal group [1]. Its invariant tensors are generated by pairings and a determinant. Varesco's reduction identifies the corresponding algebraicity problem, including the relative determinant subspace when $E$ is totally real [2, Lemma 2.15 and Theorem 2.16]. For Picard number one and $E=\mathbf Q$, the determinant occupies 21 K3 factors.

We give a sufficient condition for algebraicity of this determinant in terms of a correspondence involving fewer K3 factors and two curves. The new operation takes a nonzero odd exterior input, obtains Clifford bivectors by contracting commutators, and then recovers the Clifford vector action by a second contraction. For a cubic exterior input, the geometric test is a cycle of dimension four on $S^3\times C\times D$. The same operation works in odd relative rank over a totally real endomorphism field when its action is algebraic. In even relative rank at least six, a bivector input from two K3 factors suffices by taking a trace on one half-spin isotypic summand. A separate middle exterior criterion uses a polarization adjoint.

Van Geemen describes the degree-one Kuga–Satake vector map [3, §6.3], and Paranjape gives a geometric source on special K3 surfaces using a product of curves [4]. Van Geemen and Voisin use the weight-one spin restriction in their work on the Matsushita conjecture [5, Proposition 6]. Charles proves a universality theorem for simple weight-one structures whose tensor constructions contain a K3-type structure with full orthogonal Hodge group [6, Theorem 4.8]. Voisin recalls this result and studies an alternating construction with the intermediate Jacobian of generalized Kummer manifolds [7, Theorem 2.1 and §3]. Our initial input is an exterior power of the K3 structure. The proof below uses the weight restriction to recover the vector map from that input by algebraic operations.

The degree-one implication for K3 surfaces with $E=\mathbf Q$ is already proved in [8, Theorem A]. The argument below includes it as the case with no contractions. The rank-five and rank-six wedge-square criterion in [9, Theorem I.4] starts with the vector structure realized in a tensor product of curve pieces with additional field data. The rank-six result of [10, Theorem B] starts with the algebraic Kuga–Satake correspondence. The even criteria here start with the bivector or the middle exterior power of $T$ as source, and use an algebraic real endomorphism action on $T$. For mixed products, Varesco already proves algebraicity of Hodge similarities, including nonsquare multipliers, when the Kuga–Satake correspondences on both K3 surfaces are algebraic [11, Theorem 5.3]. We construct a mixed trace directly on the curve systems supplied by the exterior hypotheses. The passage from cross-factor correspondences to mixed products is a special case of the invariant-theoretic framework of [12].

## 2. Correspondences and weight-one representations

All cohomology has rational coefficients unless a scalar extension is indicated. Put $V=T(1)$ and write $q$ for the cup-product form on $V$, using its Tate-valued realization before twisting. Thus $q$ is a symmetric pairing with values in $\mathbf Q(0)$. Its inverse is realized by the transcendental diagonal. In fact, if $(D_a)$ is a basis of $\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}$ and $G=(D_a\cdot D_b)$, the projector is represented by the diagonal minus the two degree-zero and degree-four projectors and minus $\sum_{a,b}(G^{-1})_{ab}D_a\boxtimes D_b$. The algebraic projectors on a curve are obtained by subtracting the degree-zero and degree-two projectors from its diagonal. Exterior powers use the normalized alternating projector, given by the signed permutation sum divided by the factorial.

For smooth projective curves $C,D$, put $U=H^1(C)$ and $W=H^1(D)$. A codimension-$p+1$ correspondence on $S^p\times C\times D$ maps $H^{2p}(S^p)$ to $H^2(C\times D)(1-p)$ because $\dim S^p=2p$. The exterior component has two equivalent descriptions:

$$
\bigwedge^p T(p-1)\longrightarrow U\otimes W,
\qquad
\bigwedge^p V\longrightarrow U\otimes W(1)
\simeq \operatorname{Hom}(U,W).
$$

For the last isomorphism, use the alternating pairing $U\otimes U\to\mathbf Q(-1)$, with $u\otimes w$ acting on $x$ by pairing $u$ with $x$ and then multiplying $w$ by the result. The same convention is used for the inverse map. The determinant lies in $H^{2m}(S^m)(m)$ for $m$ input slots. In particular, a recovered map $V\to\operatorname{End}(U)$ corresponds to a codimension-two cycle on $S\times C\times C$. Curve polarizations and their inverses realize the evaluations and coevaluations of $H^1$, and the primitive diagonal supplies the corresponding operations on $V$. Tensor products, permutations, composition and ordinary operator trace therefore act by algebraic correspondences. For the trace, use the contraction between $U$ and $U^\vee$ in $\operatorname{End}(U)=U\otimes U^\vee$, with the inverse to the displayed Hom identification. This gives ordinary matrix trace with the stated pairing convention.

We use the connected joint Hodge group of the primitive structure and the curve systems. Its representations are polarizable and its Lie algebra is reductive. Projection to the primitive Hodge group is surjective, by the definition as the connected rational algebraic group generated by the Hodge circle. Over $\mathbf C$, if the primitive Lie algebra is a product of simple orthogonal factors, each factor is the isomorphic image of a unique simple ideal upstairs. Indeed, images of distinct simple ideals commute, the projection is onto, and a nonzero image of a simple ideal is an ideal in the target factor. On the joint Lie algebra the remaining ideals and the centre commute with the chosen ideal. When the target is absolutely simple over $\mathbf Q$, the unique ideal is defined over $\mathbf Q$.

**Weight restriction.** Consider the ideal $\mathfrak s$ acting on an $m$-dimensional primitive factor on which the Hodge circle has standard weights $2,0,-2$. Take $m=2\ell+1\ge3$ or $m=2h\ge6$. In orthogonal coordinates the infinitesimal circle on the primitive factor has coordinate vector $(2,0,\ldots,0)$, with eigenvalues expressed as circle weights. Let $\lambda$ be the dominant weight of an irreducible $\mathfrak s$-module $P$. Its weights lie in the convex hull of the Weyl orbit of $\lambda$ and contain that orbit. In both types the orbit has first-coordinate extrema $\lambda_1$ and $-\lambda_1$. In type $D$ the sign of the first coordinate can be changed together with another coordinate. The difference of the two extremal circle weights on $P$ is therefore $4\lambda_1$.

In a weight-one representation the $P$-isotypic summand has the form $P\otimes M$. The component of the circle generator in the commuting Lie algebra acts on $M$. It is semisimple there since its action is the difference of commuting semisimple operators, the full circle generator and its $\mathfrak s$ component. Its eigenvalues shift all weights in a copy of $P$ by the same number. The full weights are among $1,-1$, so $4\lambda_1\le2$. In types $B$ and $D$, the weight coordinates are either all integral or all half-integral. Dominance gives $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_\ell\ge0$ in type $B$ and $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_{h-1}\ge|\lambda_h|$ in type $D$. The bound leaves the trivial module and the spin module in type $B$, and the trivial module and the two half-spins in type $D$. On each spin or half-spin type at this embedding both extremal weights are already $1,-1$, so the commuting component of the circle acts as zero there.

Suppose now that $E$ is totally real, with $m=\dim_E T$, and assume its action is induced by algebraic correspondences on $S\times S$. Zarhin's description [1] writes the primitive group as $\operatorname{Res}_{E/\mathbf Q}\mathrm{SO}(V,\phi)$ for the $E$-quadratic form with trace $q$. Over the algebraic closure of $\mathbf Q$ write $V_\sigma$ for the standard factor indexed by $\sigma:E\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}$. It is the image of an algebraic coefficient projector: for a primitive element $a\in E$ use the polynomial in $a$ taking the value one at $\sigma(a)$ and zero at the other conjugates. The projectors are self-adjoint for $q$. This follows from the trace form, which makes the distinct eigenspaces orthogonal. The circle has the stated weights at the embedding supporting $T^{2,0}$ and is trivial on the other primitive factors. The joint rational Lie algebra and its representations are defined over $\mathbf Q$. Galois conjugation permutes the distinguished simple ideals and carries their trivial and spin types to the corresponding types, possibly interchanging the two half-spins. Thus the list of possible types applies at every embedding. The assertion about the circle generator on an individual isotypic summand applies at the embedding where the width was computed.

**Algebraic maps between isotypic summands.** The isotypic projectors for any one simple ideal commute with the whole joint Lie algebra. A complex map between curve systems commuting with the whole Lie algebra is a complex combination of rational Hodge maps: the intertwiner equations for the whole Lie algebra are rational, and a rational map commuting with the circle is Hodge. The converse follows from the definition of the joint group. The Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem realizes the rational maps on $H^1$ by divisor correspondences on the products of the curves. This supplies the complex coefficient isotypic projectors as algebraic correspondences. When the primitive target is absolutely simple over $\mathbf Q$, the spin isotypics themselves are rational, given by the complements of the trivial isotypics. On them the full circle acts through the simple ideal. Consequently all rational intertwiners just for that ideal on the spin isotypics are Hodge maps, even when the commuting ideal acts nontrivially. Base change of these rational intertwiner equations allows the selection and identification of individual complex spin copies in this case.

We also use the following construction at any one embedding. Let the primitive input be an irreducible for $\mathfrak s$, with every other ideal and the centre acting trivially. Suppose that it occurs once in $\operatorname{Hom}(P_1,P_2)$ for the selected $\mathfrak s$-types of the two curve systems. A map to the selected block is then $\gamma\otimes B$, where $\gamma$ is the model intertwiner and $B:M_1\to M_2$ intertwines the commuting Lie algebra. The multiplicity modules are semisimple for that Lie algebra. This follows by restricting the completely reducible joint representation to the selected isotypic, with the central torus acting semisimply. Choose invariant complements to $\ker B$ and $\operatorname{im} B$ and define $B'$ by the inverse on $\operatorname{im} B$ and zero on its complement. The resulting map $1\otimes B'$ is defined when $P_1=P_2$ and, extended by zero on the other isotypics, commutes with the entire joint Lie algebra. It is therefore algebraic with complex coefficients. Composing gives $\gamma\otimes e$ on the first curve system, where $e=B'B$ is a nonzero idempotent. Products of these endomorphisms retain the same idempotent.

## 3. Odd exterior recovery

**Theorem 1.** Suppose $E$ is totally real, its action on $T$ is algebraic, and $m=\dim_E T\ge3$ is odd. Let $1\le p<m$ be odd. If a codimension-$p+1$ algebraic correspondence on $S^p\times C\times D$ has a nonzero component from $\bigwedge^p V_\sigma$ to $\operatorname{Hom}(U,W)_{\mathbf C}$ at some embedding $\sigma$, then the rational relative determinant subspace is algebraic. Hence the rational Hodge conjecture holds for all self-powers of $S$. If $E=\mathbf Q$, the construction yields a nonzero rational algebraic Clifford vector map on a rational summand of $H^1(C)$ for a nonzero rational multiple of $q$.

For $E=\mathbf Q$ the endomorphism hypothesis on the action is automatic. In particular, for Picard number one we have $m=21$, and the criterion permits $p=1,3,\ldots,19$. Any such input forces each of $C,D$ to have genus at least $512$, since each $H^1$ contains a complex spin representation of dimension $2^{10}$. This is a lower bound from complex representation theory.

*Spin multiplicity.* Over $\mathbf C$ use the Clifford convention $\gamma(x)^2=q(x,x)$ and let $\gamma_j$ denote the normalized antisymmetrized Clifford product. The Clifford algebra has the vector-space decomposition by these exterior grades, equivariant for the spin conjugation action. In odd dimension $m=2\ell+1$, its even algebra is the full matrix algebra on the spin module $P$ of dimension $2^\ell$. In the even algebra exactly one of $j,m-j$ occurs for each $0\le j\le\ell$. These pairs correspond to distinct irreducible exterior representations by orthogonal duality and the irreducibility of $\bigwedge^j V_\sigma$ for $j\le\ell$. Thus $\operatorname{End}(P)$ contains every nontrivial exterior type with multiplicity one. Its intertwiner in degree $p$ is $\gamma_p$, which is nonzero: an orthonormal exterior basis word acts by a product of invertible Clifford generators. Equivalently, the odd volume acts by an invertible scalar and identifies complementary grades in this matrix representation. The blocks with one trivial factor have half-integral weights when the other is spin, while the exterior modules have integral weights. The block with both factors trivial contains only invariants. The input therefore meets a spin-to-spin block and can be converted by the preceding partial inverse into an operator-valued algebraic map $F=t\,\gamma_p\otimes e$ with $t\ne0$ and $e^2=e\ne0$.

*Two contractions.* Choose a complex orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ of $V_\sigma$ for the restricted rational form. In the following formulas $I$ is an increasing tuple and $e_I$ in the argument list denotes all its vectors in order. Write $k=p-1=2s$. For $p>1$ set

$$
B(x,y)=\sum_{|I|=k}[F(x,e_I),F(y,e_I)].
$$

This is contraction by the inverse induced form on $\bigwedge^k V_\sigma$, obtained from the inverse of $q$ with the embedding projector. For distinct basis vectors $x,y$, the only nonzero terms have $I$ disjoint from both. If $G_I$ is the product of the $k$ corresponding Clifford generators, then $G_I^2=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$ and $G_I\gamma(y)=(-1)^k\gamma(y)G_I$. In the two orders of the commutator the remaining Clifford vectors anticommute. Hence

$$
B=t^2 b_{m,p}\,\gamma_2\otimes e,\qquad
b_{m,p}=2(-1)^{k+k(k-1)/2}\binom{m-2}{k}.
$$

Next contract ordered slots with $k$ inverse Gram factors:

$$
A(x)=\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_k}F(x,e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_k})
 B(e_{i_1},e_{i_2})\cdots B(e_{i_{k-1}},e_{i_k}).
$$

For a basis vector $x$, only tuples of distinct indices avoiding $x$ survive. After the scalar factors are removed, their Clifford product is $\gamma(x)$ times the square of the ordered word of length $k$. There are $(m-1)!/(m-p)!$ such terms. Since the multiplicity idempotent survives every product, the result is

$$
A=t^p a_{m,p}\,\gamma_1\otimes e,\qquad
a_{m,p}=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\frac{(m-1)!}{(m-p)!}\,b_{m,p}^{\,s}.
$$

The constants are nonzero because $p<m$. For $p=1$ use $A=F$ and $a_{m,1}=1$. For $(m,p)=(21,3)$ one has $b_{21,3}=-342$ and $a_{21,3}=129960$. The first contraction uses an exterior metric and the second uses ordered tensor slots. The two normalizations account for their different counting factors.

*Rational vector action for $E=\mathbf Q$.* This case also has a direct rational realization. One may take the original correspondence rational, since a nonzero component of a complex combination already occurs for some rational summand. On the rational spin isotypics $U_{\mathrm{sp}},W_{\mathrm{sp}}$, the action of $\mathfrak{so}(V,q)$ factors through a module action of the rational even Clifford algebra. To see this, the Lie algebra map sends $z\mapsto q(y,z)x-q(x,z)y$ to $\gamma_2(x,y)/2$ in the even algebra. These elements and the unit generate that algebra. The induced surjection from the universal enveloping algebra has kernel acting as zero on both spin isotypics after complexification, hence also over $\mathbf Q$. Choose a nonzero rational top exterior tensor and let $\omega$ be its antisymmetrized Clifford image. It is central in the full Clifford algebra, has nonzero rational square, and $\gamma_p(v)\omega$ belongs to the even algebra for odd $p$. Denote its endomorphism on a spin isotypic by $D_p(v)$.

The rational input on the spin summands uniquely has the form $v\mapsto D_p(v) B_0$ with $B_0:U_{\mathrm{sp}}\to W_{\mathrm{sp}}$ an even Clifford module map. Indeed this assertion becomes an isomorphism of intertwiner spaces over $\mathbf C$ by the multiplicity-one calculation, so it is an isomorphism over $\mathbf Q$. The even Clifford algebra is semisimple, because its scalar extension is a full matrix algebra. It gives a rational module partial inverse of $B_0$ and an idempotent $e_0$ on $U_{\mathrm{sp}}$. The partial inverse is Hodge by the circle argument and algebraic by Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Apply the two contractions to $D_p e_0$. Their rational output is a nonzero scalar multiple of $D_1 e_0$, as can be checked over $\mathbf C$ where $\omega$ acts by a nonzero scalar. The scalar of proportionality is rational because both maps are rational. Dividing by it realizes $D_1e_0$ algebraically. On the image of $e_0$ the square of $D_1(x)$ is $\omega^2 q(x,x)$ times the identity. The rational rescaling of the form here records the choice of volume and avoids choosing a square root in the ground field.

*Trace and descent.* Take the normalized alternation of $\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(A(v_1)\cdots A(v_m)\bigr)$. It is algebraic with complex coefficients by the contraction rules in Section 2. On an orthonormal basis the signed terms all contribute the same Clifford volume. In odd dimension it acts on $P$ by a nonzero scalar. Its trace is that scalar times $\dim P$, times $\operatorname{rank}(e)$ and the nonzero factors from $A$. Through the inverse metric this gives a nonzero vector of $\bigwedge^m V_\sigma$ in $H^{2m}(S^m,\mathbf C)(m)$.

For subsequent use, the descent argument is as follows. Let $\mathcal A_{\mathbf Q}$ be the image of the rational cycle class map in the relevant rational cohomology. If a rational subspace $L_{\mathbf Q}$ satisfies $L_{\mathbf C}\subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathbf C}$, then $L_{\mathbf Q}\subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathbf Q}$ by extending scalars in the quotient by $\mathcal A_{\mathbf Q}$. To treat all embeddings, choose a rational cycle with a nonzero initial component and conjugate the embedding projector over $\overline{\mathbf Q}$. The underlying correspondence has rational cohomology, so the conjugate input is nonzero. Repeat the construction at each embedding. The direct sum of the lines $\bigwedge^m V_\sigma$ is the complexification of the rational relative determinant subspace, equivalently the equal-embedding realization of $\bigwedge^m_E V$ [2, Lemma 2.15]. Every line now lies in $\mathcal A_{\mathbf C}$, which gives the rational conclusion without any assumption on the field of definition of the complex spin-copy choices.

Finally, over a splitting field the invariant tensors in powers of $V$ are generated by the inverse forms on the individual factors and their volumes, placed in tensor slots and permuted, by the first fundamental theorem for the special orthogonal group [2, Theorem 2.14]. The inverse forms are algebraic with coefficients by the transcendental diagonal and the $E$-projectors. The volume lines were just constructed. The other Künneth summands of a K3 product are generated in each factor by algebraic classes. The same descent argument proves the assertion for all rational Hodge classes on every self-power. This proves Theorem 1.

## 4. Similarities and mixed products

**Theorem 2.** Let $S_1,\ldots,S_t$ have Picard number one and $E(S_i)=\mathbf Q$. Suppose each rational Hodge-isomorphism class of transcendental structures in the list has a representative satisfying the odd exterior hypothesis of Theorem 1. Suppose further that each other member either satisfies the same hypothesis, in its own odd degree, or admits an algebraic Hodge isomorphism of transcendental structures to its representative. Then the rational Hodge conjecture holds on all finite products of members of the list, allowing repetitions.

First consider two members with odd exterior inputs and a rational Hodge isomorphism $u:V_1\to V_2$. Its polarization adjoint satisfies $u^*u=c\,1$ by the endomorphism hypothesis. Thus $q_2(ux,uy)=c q_1(x,y)$ for a rational scalar $c>0$. Positivity follows by using the positive Hermitian forms from the polarizations and the compatibility of $u$ with the Weil operators. In the joint Hodge group the two actions are identified by $u$. The unique rational simple ideal projecting to the common $\mathfrak{so}_{21}$ acts on the recovered endomorphisms of the two curve systems.

Select one complex spin copy $P_i$ in the support of each recovered map $A_i$. Take a simple-ideal intertwiner $J:P_1\to P_2$ and its inverse. Extended by zero outside the selected copies, these maps and both projectors are algebraic with complex coefficients by Section 2. Write $A_i$ also for the maps restricted to these copies. The mixed form

$$
(x,y)\longmapsto \operatorname{Tr}_{P_2}\bigl(JA_1(x)J^{-1}A_2(y)\bigr)
$$

is algebraic with complex coefficients. The standard constituent occurs once in $\operatorname{End}(P_2)$, so $JA_1(x)J^{-1}=\lambda A_2(ux)$ with $\lambda\ne0$. For normalized Clifford actions the corresponding scalar satisfies $\lambda^2c=1$ by taking squares. The actual recovered $A_i$ have their own nonzero normalizations. The Clifford anticommutator and cyclicity of trace give $\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma(v)\gamma(w))=\dim(P_2)q_2(v,w)$. Thus the mixed form is a nonzero scalar multiple of $q_2(ux,y)$. That form is rational and gives the rational class of $u$ under metric duality. Section 3 descends its algebraicity. In particular $c$ need not be a square. The inverse is $c^{-1}u^*$ and is algebraic by transposing the correspondence. Varesco's general similarity theorem uses the rational even Clifford isomorphism, whose formula on a word of length $2j$ contains $c^{-j}$ [11, Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 5.3]. Here the scalar cancels by descent on the mixed line.

The primitive joint Lie algebra for the entire list is a subdirect product of the absolutely simple orthogonal Lie algebras. It has zero centre, since a central element maps to zero in every coordinate. Each coordinate is the image of a unique simple rational ideal, and the projection of that ideal is an isomorphism. If two coordinates share an ideal, their standard representations become isomorphic over $\mathbf C$, since automorphisms of type $B_{10}$ preserve the standard highest weight. The rational intertwiner equations then have a nonzero rational solution. The other ideals act trivially on these two structures, so the intertwiner commutes with the joint Hodge circle and is a Hodge isomorphism. Conversely a Hodge isomorphism identifies the two actions, so they share an ideal. The simple ideals therefore give precisely the Hodge-isomorphism blocks.

On each block transfer the pairing and determinant of a verified representative to all tensor slots by the algebraic similarities and their inverses. Invariants of the connected joint group are the products of invariants on the separate blocks. On each block, the special orthogonal invariant theorem expresses them in terms of the transferred tensors. The remaining cohomology of each factor has an algebraic basis. This proves Theorem 2.

## 5. Two criteria in even relative rank

**Theorem 3.** Suppose $E=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)$ is totally real, its action is algebraic, and $m=\dim_E T=2h\ge6$. Either of the following hypotheses implies algebraicity of the rational relative determinant subspace and the rational Hodge conjecture for all self-powers:

1. A codimension-three correspondence on $S^2\times C\times D$ has nonzero component from $\bigwedge^2 V_\sigma$ to $\operatorname{Hom}(U,W)_{\mathbf C}$ at one embedding.
2. A codimension-$h+1$ correspondence on $S^h\times C\times D$ has nonzero component from $\bigwedge^h V_\sigma$ to the same target at one embedding.

Both use the embedding projector in every input slot and the conventions of Section 2. In the weight-two formulation the first criterion is $(\bigwedge^2 T_\sigma)(1)\to U\otimes W$, and the cycle has dimension three. The second is $(\bigwedge^h T_\sigma)(h-1)\to U\otimes W$, and the cycle has dimension $h+1$.

*Bivector multiplicity and trace.* Over $\mathbf C$ the full Clifford algebra is the endomorphism algebra of the full spinor $P_+\oplus P_-$. The even grades give its two diagonal blocks and the odd grades its two off-diagonal blocks. The top Clifford word $\omega$ on an orthonormal basis has square $(-1)^h$, acts by opposite nonzero scalars on the two half-spins, and interchanges complementary exterior grades by right multiplication. These statements follow by anticommuting each generator through the word. In particular $\omega$ anticommutes with vectors, commutes with even words, and its two eigenspaces are the chiral spaces, interchanged by any invertible vector. In the full even Clifford algebra the adjoint representation $\bigwedge^2 V_\sigma$ occurs in just the degrees $2$ and $2h-2$. Indeed below the middle the irreducible exterior grades have different highest weights, and the two middle highest weights differ from the degree-two weight. These two degrees are distinct for $h\ge3$. Right multiplication by $\omega$ exchanges the two adjoint constituents, up to invertible scalars. Its eigenspaces on their sum each contain one copy of the adjoint, giving multiplicity one in each diagonal block. There is no adjoint in an odd block. Maps between a trivial module and a half-spin have half-integral weights, and the trivial-to-trivial block has weight zero. Hence the bivector input meets a pair of copies of the same chirality. The Clifford intertwiner in that block is nonzero since a product of two distinct orthonormal generators acts invertibly there.

Use the whole-group partial inverse of Section 2 at this embedding to obtain $F=t\gamma_2\otimes e$ acting on one isotypic curve summand. Take the normalized alternation, in all $2h$ input slots, of

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(F(v_1,v_2)\cdots F(v_{2h-1},v_{2h})\bigr).
$$

On an orthonormal basis every signed term is the trace of the same volume word multiplied by $t^h$ and $\operatorname{rank}(e)$. The trace on a single half-spin is its dimension $2^{h-1}$ times its nonzero volume eigenvalue. This yields the required top exterior class at $\sigma$. Taking the trace over both chiralities with equal multiplicities would cancel these eigenvalues. The projector onto the selected isotypic is part of the algebraic construction.

*Middle multiplicity and adjoint.* The orthogonal star on $\bigwedge^h V_\sigma$ has square $(-1)^{h^2}$ with respect to a unit volume. The two star eigenspaces $L_+,L_-$ are irreducible and nonisomorphic. In orthogonal weight coordinates their highest weights are $(1,\ldots,1,1)$ and $(1,\ldots,1,-1)$. Neither occurs in any other exterior grade. On an exterior basis word of degree $h$, its star is the signed complementary basis word. Clifford multiplication by $\omega$ on the right gives the same word up to the constant $(-1)^{h(h-1)/2}$, because the square of the original word is that constant. Thus for source $P_\epsilon$, Clifford multiplication in the middle grade maps to $P_{\epsilon(-1)^h}$ and intertwines star on the middle forms with a fixed scalar on that block. The two source chiralities give opposite scalars. Each block contains at most one of $L_+,L_-$, and the middle word acts nontrivially on either permitted source. A trivial module in either endpoint cannot contribute, since its Hom blocks have highest weight zero or half-integral highest weight. An isotypic projection therefore supplies an algebraic map $F$ whose restriction to $\bigwedge^h V_\sigma$ is injective on one middle irreducible and kills the other.

For the adjoint, treat this $F$ as an algebraic complex coefficient map from the full polarized rational structure $\bigwedge^h V$ to the weight-zero structure $U\otimes W(1)$, extended by zero outside the selected input. Denote the rational polarizations by $Q_X,Q_Y$, using the tensor-product and Tate-twist conventions. The source form is a nonzero scalar times the induced exterior form of $q$. The target form is a nonzero scalar times the tensor of the two curve forms. Their inverses on the projected spaces are algebraic by the diagonals, the Tate identifications and alternation. The embedding projector is self-adjoint for $Q_X$. Let $F^\dagger=(\overline F)^t$, where $t$ denotes transpose using $Q_X,Q_Y$ and the bar conjugates the coefficients of rational cycles. Both operations preserve algebraicity. Write $C_X,C_Y$ for the Weil operators of the weight-zero structures and use the positive Hermitian products $H_X(x,y)=Q_X(C_Xx,\bar y)$ and $H_Y$ defined in the same way. Since $F$ is Hodge, $C_YF=FC_X$. The bilinear transpose relation gives

$$
H_Y(Fx,y)=Q_Y(FC_Xx,\bar y)
=Q_X(C_Xx,F^t\bar y)=H_X(x,F^\dagger y).
$$

In the last equality the polarizations are rational, so $(\overline F)^t=\overline{F^t}$. In particular $F^\dagger F$ is nonzero by positivity. It commutes with the joint group by invariance of the bilinear forms and rationality of the joint representation, is supported on $\bigwedge^h V_\sigma$, and kills the unselected middle irreducible. Schur's lemma makes it a nonzero multiple of the projector onto the selected one.

On $\bigwedge^h V_\sigma$ this projector is $(1+c*)/2$ for a nonzero scalar $c$ determined by the selected eigenvalue. Use metric duality on its source to view it as a tensor in $2h$ slots. The identity term is a sum of products of pairings, each killed by full alternation because it has a symmetric pair. The star term corresponds, up to a nonzero scalar and the ordering of the two blocks, to the bilinear form that takes two $h$-vectors to the coefficient of their wedge in the volume line. Its full alternation is nonzero. The resulting tensor is algebraic and spans the line at $\sigma$. Descent for both criteria is as in Section 3: conjugate the rational input projection, construct a volume at each embedding, and apply the invariant theorem and rational descent.

In relative rank six, $\operatorname{Spin}_6\simeq\mathrm{SL}_4$. If $P$ is its four-dimensional standard module, the two half-spins are $P,P^\vee$ and the orthogonal standard module is $\bigwedge^2 P$. Its middle cube is $\operatorname{Sym}^2 P\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2 P^\vee$, which also follows from the separate occurrences in the two off-diagonal Hom blocks $P\otimes P$ and $P^\vee\otimes P^\vee$. A nonzero component from either ten-dimensional summand in a codimension-four correspondence suffices. For a totally real cubic $E$ this is relative rank six and rational rank eighteen. The hypothesis is distinct from the algebraic Kuga–Satake hypothesis for the Picard-number-16 families in [2, Theorem 4.3]. The bivector criterion in this relative rank uses only two K3 slots and traces three operators on a single half-spin.

For comparison, in odd rank the operator $-1$ on $V$, acting trivially on the curves, fixes the inverse metric and any input with an even number of primitive slots. It commutes with all tensor permutations and preserves contractions. Tensors obtained using only those operations and the specified even data are therefore invariant under it, whereas the odd volume changes sign. This explains the parity restriction of the construction in Section 3.

## 6. Three K3 factors and a four-dimensional source

For Picard number one and $E=\mathbf Q$, let $X$ be a smooth projective fourfold with evaluation maps $g_a:X\to S$ for $a=1,2,3$ and $b:X\to C\times D$. Its graph pushforward to $S^3\times C\times D$ defines a codimension-four cycle, using the proper cycle pushforward even if the image has smaller dimension. The same description works with evaluation correspondences given by finite ordered choices, using a common proper graph. Write $u_a(\alpha)=g_a^*\alpha$ for single evaluations and use the corresponding sum for finite choices. For $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in T^3$ its mixed exterior component is detected by the numbers

$$
\int_X \operatorname{Alt}\bigl(u_1(\alpha)u_2(\beta)u_3(\gamma)\bigr)
b^*(\xi\boxtimes\eta),\qquad \xi\in H^1(C),\quad\eta\in H^1(D).
$$

This is the correspondence formula by the projection formula and the perfect pairing on the mixed Künneth summand of the base. All inputs have cohomological degree two except the two curve tests, which each have degree one. A nonzero value gives the hypothesis of Theorem 1 with $p=3$. Its geometric construction can use evaluation maps unrelated to the endpoint curves.

### An elliptic endpoint calculation

Suppose that an endpoint curve $C$ also parametrizes one of the evaluation slots. More specifically, suppose that on a smooth projective graph model $f:Y\to C$ of a family of normalized elliptic curves there is a section and a morphism $g:Y\to S$. Assume the smooth fibres have nonconstant $j$ and their image cycle is a multiple of the polarization, as holds for normalized curves in the class $3H$ on a polarized double plane $(S,H)$. A holomorphic one-form on $Y$ is closed. On the smooth elliptic fibres its periods in a locally flat integral basis are locally constant. A nonzero restriction would determine a constant period ratio and hence constant $j$. Every one-form therefore vanishes on the fibres, descends over the smooth locus, and extends over $C$ by pullback along the section. Hodge decomposition gives $H^1(Y)=f^*H^1(C)$. Also $f_*g^*\alpha=0$ for $\alpha\in T$, by the fibre class. Pairing $g^*\alpha\,f^*\xi$ with $H^1(Y)$ and using the projection formula gives zero. Poincaré duality gives the identity

$$
g^*\alpha\,f^*\xi=0\quad\text{in }H^3(Y)
\qquad (\alpha\in T,\ \xi\in H^1(C)).
$$

The four-parameter double-plane slice motivating the test was described in the preliminary discussion [13]. Keep four marked bases $\Gamma_i,\Gamma_j,\Gamma_k,\Gamma_l$ and fix the other seventeen at general values. On a proper fourfold model of a dominating component take the eight selected normalized points in each of slots $i,j,k$. The endpoint map is $b=(b_i,b_l)$ to $\Gamma_i\times\Gamma_l$, and the slot-$i$ points evaluate into the normalized elliptic family over $\Gamma_i$. The argument uses the component with its generic multiplicity and applies whenever the slot-$i$ family has the nonconstant period and section specified above.

For the cycle construction, order all eight choices in each of the three slots on the open where they are distinct. Resolve the proper closure of the ordering graph, retaining all dominating sheets, and write $\rho:\widehat X\to X$ for the resulting proper map of total generic degree $d=(8!)^3$. Let $r_{a,s}:\widehat X\to Y_a$ be the ordered evaluation maps into the proper normalized families and $g_a:Y_a\to S$ their physical evaluations. The required cycle is $1/d$ times the sum, over $s_i,s_j,s_k\in\{1,\ldots,8\}$, of the proper pushforwards of $[\widehat X]$ by the three chosen physical evaluations and $b\rho$. This formula uses the full graph with its proper multiplicities. The parameter relation $f_i r_{i,s}=b_i\rho$ holds on the compact graph since it holds on the dense open. Thus every class $(g_i r_{i,s})^*\alpha$ multiplied by $\rho^*b_i^*\xi$ is zero there by the preceding identity. In the pairing for the cycle every summand contains such a product. The trace vanishes before alternation for every choice of the three primitive classes. Using a graph resolution or passing to the ordering cover therefore gives the same zero with the prescribed multiplicities. The calculation depends on the endpoint one-form and the matched elliptic evaluation, and applies without a centre relation among the slots.

There is also a fibrewise constraint. Suppose a smooth projective fourfold dominates the two-curve base and all three evaluations have image of dimension at most one on a general fibre. Each primitive evaluation pulls back to zero on the fibre. For a curve image this follows by factoring through its normalization on a smooth projective fibre and using the zero intersection of a transcendental class with any divisor class on $S$. Over a smooth open of the base, the three pullbacks therefore have Leray filtration at least one. Their product has filtration at least three, and fibre integration preserves the base filtration while landing in degree two. Its restriction to that open is zero. The kernel of restriction in degree two is spanned by divisor classes supported on the complement. After alternation, a Hodge map from $\bigwedge^3 V$ to the resulting Tate subspace is zero, since $\bigwedge^3 V$ is a nontrivial irreducible. Thus a nonzero cubic input of this form requires a surface-valued evaluation on a general fibre.

## 7. Reproducibility and scope

The proofs use algebraic correspondences and representation theory. The supplement contains an exact CPU certificate for the contraction normalizations and for the even chiral traces in ranks six and eight. It can be replayed with the Python standard library, as described in [the supplement README](supplement/README.md). The fourfold calculation is a vanishing test for the stated geometric input. Existence of a positive higher exterior input for an untreated K3 surface remains open. The totally real criteria assume the algebraicity of the endomorphism action, and the mixed-product criterion uses a verified input or an algebraic transfer for each surface.
