# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive conditional advance toward the Hodge conjecture for powers of K3 surfaces. Its principal contribution is an algebraic recovery of the Clifford vector action from a nonzero odd exterior correspondence into two curve cohomologies. The even-rank bivector and middle-exterior criteria provide further sufficient conditions for relative determinant classes. These are reductions requiring geometric inputs, not new unconditional cases of the Hodge conjecture, and the manuscript states this limitation clearly.

The central proof is convincing. The weight-width argument restricts the relevant weight-one representations to spin or half-spin types. Section 2 explains why the isotypic projections and the required partial inverses are algebraic: they commute with the joint Hodge group and hence are combinations of divisor correspondences between curves. This matters especially over a totally real field, where an intertwiner for just one simple factor would not generally suffice. In odd rank, multiplicity one identifies the input with Clifford multiplication times a multiplicity map; the partial inverse replaces the latter by a nonzero idempotent. The two contractions then recover the vector constituent with explicitly nonzero coefficients. The alternating volume trace, construction at every embedding, and rational descent supply the relative determinant subspace. Together with the assumed algebraicity of the endomorphism action, the cited orthogonal invariant theorem gives the claimed conclusion for self-powers.

The even-rank arguments also address their decisive issues. Selecting one half-spin avoids cancellation of the volume trace between chiralities. For the middle-exterior criterion, the conjugate polarization adjoint gives a nonzero projector onto one middle irreducible; full alternation removes the metric contribution and retains the volume contribution. The mixed-product argument correctly retains a hypothesis supplying an exterior input or an algebraic transfer for every relevant surface. It does not silently replace a rational Hodge similarity by a known algebraic isometry.

The contribution is distinguishable from the closest published work. I compared the relevant snapshot versions of rStation papers 99, 107, 112 and 113, cited here as [8], [12], [9] and [10]. Paper 99 already proves the degree-one curve-product implication; paper 112's wedge-square criterion concerns a realization of the vector structure in curve pieces with additional field data; paper 113 uses a Kuga–Satake hypothesis in relative rank six. None supplies the present higher odd exterior recovery or the stated general even exterior criteria. The external checks of van Geemen–Voisin [5], Charles [6], and Varesco [2, 11] confirm that spin universality, determinant generation, and algebraicity of similarities under Kuga–Satake hypotheses are established background. The manuscript credits these results appropriately. Its novelty lies chiefly in converting the exterior inputs into algebraic determinant constructions, rather than in the spin restriction or the mixed-product invariant theory themselves.

The reproducible computation supports the finite algebraic checks claimed for it. Running the inspected standard-library supplement reproduced $b_{21,3}=-342$, $a_{21,3}=129960$, the odd volume trace, and the opposite chiral traces in ranks six and eight. These checks corroborate normalizations and cancellation; they do not verify the Hodge-theoretic descent or construct a geometric correspondence. Those conclusions rest on the written proofs. Section 6's nonisotrivial elliptic-endpoint vanishing is a supported obstruction for the specified construction, not a universal obstruction to the cubic criterion.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, main mechanism, hypotheses and limitations are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The abstract is informative and candid about the missing geometric inputs. The following modest improvements would help readers:

- Give the two contractions and the subsequent volume trace a short schematic display in the introduction, marking the new step relative to the degree-one result [8].
- State the polarization sign convention explicitly when introducing the Hermitian adjoint in Section 5, including its relation to the cup-product form $q$ used earlier.
- Make the illustrative double-plane slice in Section 6 more self-contained by defining the marked bases and the eight selected points, or move that discussion to an appendix. The general endpoint lemma is understandable without [13], but the particular motivating construction is not fully specified in the paper.

These are presentation improvements, not barriers to acceptance. The work provides useful conditional criteria connected directly to the exceptional Hodge classes in the supplied problem; it does not resolve the full Hodge conjecture or establish a positive higher-exterior input for an untreated K3 surface.
