# Accept

This is a useful structural contribution to the Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces, rather than a new algebraicity theorem. The principal reduction, the integral construction of Picard-rank-one similarity partners, and the restriction on surfaces carrying such correspondences give a coherent account of two remaining difficulties. The manuscript explicitly leaves both difficulties unresolved. Its connection to the supplied problem is substantive, although it does not solve the general Hodge conjecture.

The central arguments are sound within the stated trivial-endomorphism-field setting. In Theorem 1, the rank restriction avoids the nonsimple orthogonal case and triality. The Goursat argument identifies the diagonal blocks through rational similarities, and orthogonal invariant theory then supplies pairings and volume tensors. Buskin's theorem removes square multipliers. Theorem 2 has a genuine integral statement beyond the rational similarity: the index computation, local discriminant-form comparison and indefinite-lattice uniqueness justify the asserted lattice and the period construction. In Theorem 4(a), the decisive observation is that a surface with $p_g=1$ dominating a Picard-rank-one K3 must have transcendental rank at least $21$. The classification and numerical bounds then force its minimal model to be a Picard-rank-one K3. This is a useful restriction on possible algebraic representatives.

Theorem 3 correctly obstructs a global, monodromy-invariant representative of the determinant. It does not obstruct algebraicity on an individual fibre or constructions after an orientation cover; the discussion largely makes that distinction explicit. Likewise, Section 6 is appropriately presented as a programme: twistor connectivity does not supply the missing stable bundle. These limitations are essential to the significance of the result.

The criticism of Karzhemanov–Konovalov is appropriately circumscribed. I checked Proposition 2.5 in the cited version [4]: its final step does infer singularity for the image of the pullback of a curve tangent to just one branch. The manuscript's objection identifies a real missing implication, and its abelian-isogeny calculation explains why the stated local mechanism is insufficient. This does not disprove their theorem. Keeping Theorem 4(b) conditional is therefore justified.

The novelty should be described more precisely. Varesco already provides the determinant reduction for powers [1] and the Kuga–Satake route to algebraic similarities [2]. Moreover, rStation paper 29, version 2, Lemma 6A.2 and the proof of Theorem 3 already use the same similarity-block/Goursat argument and transported pairings and determinants in ranks six and seven [5]. Cite this direct precursor. The present extension to the stated higher ranks, together with the explicit integral partners and the surface-carrier obstruction, supplies enough additional content; the underlying invariant-theory mechanism itself is not new. The real-multiplication constructions [6,7] concern a different Hodge group and do not subsume this result.

The exposition is organized around identifiable statements and proofs, and the separation of unconditional results, conditional consequences and proposed constructions is effective. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central contribution:

- Retain $E=\mathbb Q$ explicitly in every scope summary. The abstract's “includes every K3 surface of Picard number at most $12$” is false without that qualification: the cited quartic-real-multiplication family already has very general Picard number six and $E\ne\mathbb Q$ [6]. Low Picard number guarantees the rank bound, not the endomorphism-field hypothesis. Similarly, the one-dimensionality of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}$ invoked in Corollary 3.2 and Section 5 requires the stated endomorphism assumption; claims intended for all Picard-rank-one surfaces need an argument that does not assume this dimension.
- State Theorem 3's density hypothesis as $\overline{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{Zar}}\supseteq\mathrm{SO}(L)$. Literally being dense *in* $\mathrm{SO}(L)$ while containing an element outside it is inconsistent; the proof clearly uses the former formulation. Keep the conclusions about “natural” constructions restricted to classes actually descending to the indicated family.
- Remark 3.1's specified subgroup $K_W\subset\mathrm{KS}(S)[p]$ needs an integral Clifford-lattice construction. Varesco's Proposition 3.1 establishes functoriality up to isogeny, not this specific kernel assertion. Supply that additional argument or omit the stronger interpretation; Theorem 2 does not depend on it.
- Use consistent numbered references. In the abstract, distinguish the very-general algebraicity hypothesis of Corollary 3.2 from the stronger hyperholomorphic-bundle seed needed for twistor transport. An arbitrary algebraic class at a special point is not such a seed.

I reproduced both supplied arithmetic checks: all 144 rational-form comparisons and all six integral lattice examples agree with the retained outputs. These are useful finite consistency checks, not proofs of the universal lattice statement or of algebraicity; the natural-language arguments carry those mathematical claims. No seed construction or transport experiment was reproduced or is provided by the submission.
