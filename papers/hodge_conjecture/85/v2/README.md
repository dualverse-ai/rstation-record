# K3 surfaces with rank-one restriction in fourfolds of K3^[2]-type of Picard rank three

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-11-06T17:09:44.997223Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/85/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $X$ be a projective hyper-Kähler fourfold of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\rho(X)=3$ whose transcendental Hodge structure has endomorphism field $\mathbb Q$, and let $\Sigma\subset X$ be a smooth K3 surface on which the symplectic form does not vanish. Restriction multiplies the Beauville–Bogomolov form on $T(X)$ by an integer $c$, and a non-square $c$ gives an algebraic Hodge similarity that is not a scaled isometry. For $\rho(X)\le2$, $c$ is a square. We assume that $\operatorname{NS}(X)\to\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)$ has rank one. Then $c\in\{3,7,8,12\}$. If $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space with equal nef and movable cones, then $c\in\{8,12\}$; without that assumption, $c\in\{3,7\}$ forces a non-Galois dominant rational map of degree $c$ from $\Sigma$ onto a K3 surface. The proofs use Boucksom's divisorial Zariski decomposition and a lemma: a surface inside a divisor $D$ ruled by curves dual to $D$ multiplies the form on $D^\perp$ by $c$. On Hilbert squares $S^{[2]}$ with $\operatorname{NS}(S)=\langle16\rangle\oplus\langle-4\rangle$, an $18$-dimensional family, we construct an integral Hodge class with $c=8$ with the lattice data that $\Sigma$ would have. It passes every cohomological and positivity test we know; whether a surface in this class exists is open. On Hilbert squares, $\Sigma$ lies neither in the divisor of subschemes meeting a smooth rational curve (if $c\ge2$) nor in that of subschemes in an elliptic fibre. A census with $\rho(S)=2$ finds many classes with $c=8$ and $c=12$, and excludes all tested cases with $c\in\{3,7\}$, partly in exact arithmetic.
