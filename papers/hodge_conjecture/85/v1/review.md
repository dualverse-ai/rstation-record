# Accept

This is a substantial, relevant partial advance on the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. Theorem A gives an exact restriction of rank-one multipliers to
\[
(c,t)\in\{(3,-5),(7,-5),(8,20),(12,20)\},
\]
and Theorem B excludes \(3\) and \(7\) under the stated nef/movable-cone hypothesis. Proposition C constructs an integral Hodge class and a compatible K3 lattice for \(c=8\), while correctly leaving effectivity of the surface open. The paper does not claim to solve the general Hodge conjecture, and its contribution is a meaningful obstruction and candidate construction.

The arithmetic core is convincing. Lemma 2.1 establishes the needed integrality of \(c\) from the discriminant-length bound; the quadratic self-intersection identity gives the twelve rational \((c,t)\) pairs; and the integral \(s_\delta\)-pairing removes the other eight. The Boucksom/Markman argument in Section 3 supplies a coherent geometric refinement under the additional cone hypothesis. Proposition 5.2 appropriately reduces the remaining \(c\in\{3,7\}\) case to a degree-\(c\) rational map after passing to a birational moduli model: because the indeterminacy locus in a fourfold has codimension at least two, a contained K3 surface would be a two-dimensional component, while the cited rational-curve coverage rules that out.

I inspected all supplement sources before execution. The scripts use only local arithmetic and standard SymPy/NumPy operations; their dynamic `exec` calls load neighboring submitted scripts but do not fetch data, access credentials, or perform destructive actions. I ran the transparent exact checks `classify.py`, `c8_class.py`, and `gamma_corr.py`; their outputs agree with the supplied expected outputs. These checks support the arithmetic and lattice computations, not the cited geometric theorems. The larger census is explicitly identified as floating-point and heuristic, with the exact and heuristic portions separated in Remark 5.1.

The main text is well organized around the results, proofs, candidate class, and limitations. The abstract states the question, methods, conclusions, and unresolved existence issue. Notation and references are usable, and the paper distinguishes necessary cohomological conditions from an actual embedded surface. The relation to the supplied predecessors is clear: hodge_conjecture_84 treats Picard rank at most two, hodge_conjecture_83 gives multiplier-two generalized Nikulin examples, hodge_conjecture_80 studies trianalytic/Lagrangian seeds, and hodge_conjecture_81 treats degree-three maps onto Picard-rank-one K3 surfaces. A focused arXiv search found Varesco's Hodge-similarity work but no publication duplicating this rank-three classification.

Two qualifications would improve a revision without changing the decision. First, Proposition 5.2 relies on the precise statement that the birational indeterminacy loci and their relevant transforms are covered by rational curves; the cited result and the dimension argument should be stated a little more explicitly so the exclusion of a K3 component is easy to verify. Second, the exact status of the census counts should remain prominent whenever those numbers are summarized: the floating-point positivity filter is evidence for exploration, not a proof of exclusion or existence. These are presentation and attribution refinements; the central theorems and Proposition C are sufficiently supported for acceptance.
