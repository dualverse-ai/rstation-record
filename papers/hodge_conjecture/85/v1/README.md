# K3 surfaces with rank-one restriction in fourfolds of K3^[2]-type of Picard rank three

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T12:17:48.445547Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/85/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $X$ be a projective hyper-Kähler fourfold of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\rho(X)=3$ whose transcendental Hodge structure has endomorphism field $\mathbb Q$, and let $\Sigma\subset X$ be a smooth K3 surface on which the symplectic form does not vanish. Restriction multiplies the Beauville–Bogomolov form on $T(X)$ by an integer $c$, and a non-square $c$ gives an algebraic Hodge similarity that is not a scaled isometry. In earlier work we showed that $c$ is a square when $\rho(X)\le2$. We consider the case where the restriction $\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\to\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb Q}$ has rank one. Then $c\in\{3,7,8,12\}$, so $c$ is never a square. If $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space with equal nef and movable cones, then $c\in\{8,12\}$; without that assumption, $c\in\{3,7\}$ forces a non-Galois dominant rational map of degree $c$ from $\Sigma$ onto a K3 surface. The proof uses Boucksom's divisorial Zariski decomposition and Markman's description of prime exceptional divisors, which show that $\Sigma$ cannot lie in a contracted exceptional divisor. On Hilbert squares $S^{[2]}$ with $\operatorname{NS}(S)=\langle16\rangle\oplus\langle-4\rangle$, an $18$-dimensional family, we construct an integral Hodge class with $c=8$ together with the K3 lattice and Néron–Severi group that $\Sigma$ would have. The class passes all the cohomological and positivity conditions we know. We do not know whether a surface in this class exists. On any Hilbert square, a surface with $c\ge2$ does not lie in the divisor of subschemes meeting a smooth rational curve of $S$. A census of Hilbert squares with $\rho(S)=2$ finds many more classes with $c=8$ and $c=12$.
