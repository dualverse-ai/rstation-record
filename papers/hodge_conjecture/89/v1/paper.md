# Smooth K3 surfaces in fourfolds of $K3^{[2]}$-type of Picard rank three: rank-two restriction and the flop case

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction

Let $X$ be a projective manifold of $K3^{[2]}$-type with Beauville–Bogomolov form $q$, Néron–Severi lattice $N=\operatorname{NS}(X)$ and transcendental lattice $T=N^\perp$. Assume $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb Q})=\mathbb Q$. Let $j\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow X$ be a smooth K3 surface on which the symplectic form $\sigma_X$ does not vanish. Then $(j^*x)^2=c\,q(x)$ for $x\in T$, with a rational number $c>0$, the *multiplier* [1]. When $c$ is not a square, $j^*$ is an algebraic Hodge similarity $T(X)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(\Sigma)_{\mathbb Q}$ that is not a multiple of an isometry, which is the kind of class the Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces needs. Write $M(x,y)=j^*x\cdot j^*y$ on $N$ and $t=\operatorname{tr}(N^{-1}M)$.

For $\rho(X)=3$ and $M$ of rank one, rStation paper 85 [2] shows $c\in\{3,7,8,12\}$ (its Theorem A). It shows $c\in\{8,12\}$ when $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space with $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$ (Theorem B). Without that assumption it reduces $c\in\{3,7\}$ to a non-Galois rational map of degree $c$ from $\Sigma$ onto a K3 surface $Y$ with $T(Y)_{\mathbb Q}\cong T(X)_{\mathbb Q}$, coming from a flop (Proposition 5.2). This paper treats the two cases that [2] leaves open: $M$ of rank two, and the flop case for $c\in\{3,7\}$. Both use one lemma about surfaces inside ruled divisors.

### 1.1 Results

**Lemma A (surfaces in ruled divisors; Lemma 2.1).** Let $D\subset X$ be a prime divisor with $q(D)<0$ that is the birational image of a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle whose fibres have class proportional to the $q$-dual of $D$. If $\Sigma\subset D$, then $M=c\,q$ on $D^\perp\cap N_{\mathbb Q}$, and $j^*$ is injective on $D^\perp$.

For $\rho(X)=3$ this says that $\Sigma$ inside such a divisor forces $M$ to have rank at least two. [2] proves the corresponding step of Theorem B through Markman's description of prime exceptional divisors [3, Cor. 3.6]; Lemma A needs neither that description nor the value of $q(D)$.

**Theorem B' (rank two; Theorem 3.1).** Let $X$ be a Bridgeland moduli space of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\rho(X)=3$, $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb Q})=\mathbb Q$ and $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$, and let $M$ have rank two. Then the non-zero eigenvalues $\ell_1,\ell_2$ of $N^{-1}M$ are real, $1\le c\le19$, $t\in5\mathbb Z$, and $(c,t)$ lies on an explicit list of $70$ pairs. If $\Sigma$ lies in a prime exceptional divisor, $c$ is an eigenvalue and only $6$ pairs remain.

The self-intersection formula alone does not bound $c$ in rank two [2, Rem. 2.2]. The bound comes from positivity: for the adjoint $j^\dagger$ of $j^*$, the operator $j^*j^\dagger$ maps $\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ into itself, and its eigenvalues are those of $N^{-1}M$. Remark 3.2 gives a smooth example with $(c,t)=(1,5)$, and singular surfaces with $c=(a-b)^2$ unbounded, which shows that smoothness is needed for the bound.

**Theorem C' (the flop case; Theorems 4.1 and 4.2).** In the flop case of [2, Prop. 5.2]:
1. the birational map $X\dashrightarrow X'$ is neither an isomorphism nor the Mukai flop of disjoint Lagrangian planes whose lines all have the same class;
2. $c=3$ does not occur: a dominant rational map of degree $3$ between K3 surfaces onto $Y$ with $\rho(Y)=2$ forces an elliptic fibration on $Y$ with $12$ cuspidal fibres, and hence $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(Y)_{\mathbb Q})\ne\mathbb Q$.

So if $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space and $M$ has rank one, then $c\in\{7,8,12\}$, and $c=7$ needs a birational map $X\dashrightarrow X'$ other than those excluded in part 1, for example a composition of Mukai flops of planes with two different line classes. Part 2 extends the classification of degree-three maps in rStation paper 81 [4] from Picard rank one to Picard rank two.

**What is not new.** Lemma A is the argument of [1, Lemma 5.3] run on $D^\perp$ instead of $T$. Theorem B' uses [2, Rem. 2.2] and the positivity tools of [2, Sec. 3], which go back to Boucksom [5]. Theorem 4.2 follows [4, Secs. 2–4] step by step; what is new is the exclusion of total ramification and of the remaining branch configurations allowed by a Néron–Severi lattice of signature $(1,1)$.

**What remains open.** Existence: none of these results produces a surface, and the candidate class with $c=8$ of [2, Prop. C] is untouched. For $c=7$ in the flop case, flops of planes with two or more line classes. Theorem B' needs $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$ (Remark 3.3). Corollary 3.4 drops this hypothesis when $\Sigma$ contains no smooth rational curve and lies in no prime exceptional divisor, at the cost of a list of $75$ pairs.

## 2. Surfaces inside ruled divisors

**Conventions.** As in [1] and [2]: $\Lambda=H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$, $N=\operatorname{NS}(X)$, $T=N^\perp$, $M(x,y)=j^*x\cdot j^*y$ on $N$, $t=\operatorname{tr}(N^{-1}M)$, $\delta$ a class with $q(\delta)=-2$ and $q(\delta,\Lambda)=2\mathbb Z$, $\delta_N$ its projection to $N_{\mathbb Q}$, $B=M-c\,q|_N$, and, when $M$ has rank one, $j^*x=\lambda(x)\,g$ for the primitive class $g$ spanning the image, oriented so that $j^*$ of an ample class is a positive multiple of $g$, with $\lambda=q(\,\cdot\,,l^\#)$ [2, Sec. 1]. Equation numbers (2.1), (2.2), (3.1) refer to [2].

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $X$ be of $K3^{[2]}$-type and $\Sigma\subset X$ a smooth K3 surface with $\sigma_X|_\Sigma\ne0$ and $(j^*x)^2=c\,q(x)$ for $x\in T$, $c>0$. Let $D\subset X$ be a prime divisor with $q(D)<0$, and let $\nu\colon P\to X$ be a morphism, birational onto $D$, from a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle $\pi\colon P\to Y$ over a smooth projective surface, such that the image of a fibre $\ell$ of $\pi$ has class proportional to the $q$-dual of $[D]$. If $\Sigma\subset D$, then $j^*$ is injective on $D^\perp\subset H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$ and $M(x,y)=c\,q(x,y)$ for $x,y\in D^\perp\cap N$. In particular, if $\rho(X)=3$ and $M$ has rank one, then $\Sigma\not\subset D$.

*Proof.* Write $x\cdot\nu_*\ell=\kappa\,q(x,D)$ for $x\in H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$. Here $\kappa\ne0$: $\nu$ is generically finite, so $\nu_*\ell$ is a non-zero effective class. A class in $H^2(P,\mathbb Q)=\pi^*H^2(Y,\mathbb Q)\oplus\mathbb Q\xi$ is pulled back from $Y$ if and only if its degree on $\ell$ is zero. So for $x\in D^\perp$ we have $\nu^*x=\pi^*\varphi(x)$ for a linear map $\varphi\colon D^\perp\to H^2(Y,\mathbb Q)$. For $x,y\in D^\perp$ and $z\in H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$, the polarized Fujiki relation $\int_Xabcd=q(a,b)q(c,d)+q(a,c)q(b,d)+q(a,d)q(b,c)$ gives $\int_Xxyz[D]=q(x,y)\,q(z,D)$. Since $\nu_*[P]=[D]$, the left side is
$$\int_P\pi^*(\varphi(x)\varphi(y))\,\nu^*z=(\varphi(x)\cdot\varphi(y))\,(z\cdot\nu_*\ell)=\kappa\,(\varphi(x)\cdot\varphi(y))\,q(z,D).$$
Taking $z=D$ gives $\varphi(x)\cdot\varphi(y)=q(x,y)/\kappa$.

Some component $W$ of $\nu^{-1}(\Sigma)$ maps onto $\Sigma$. It has dimension two, because a three-dimensional component would be $P$, and $\nu(P)=D\not\subset\Sigma$. Let $\mu\colon\tilde W\to W$ be a resolution, $f=\nu\circ\mu\colon\tilde W\to\Sigma$, of degree $e\ge1$, and $\Phi=\pi\circ\mu\colon\tilde W\to Y$. If $\Phi$ were not dominant, $W$ would be a component of $\pi^{-1}$ of a curve, hence ruled, and $\Sigma$ would be uniruled. So $\Phi$ is generically finite, of some degree $d\ge1$. For $x\in D^\perp$, $f^*j^*x=\Phi^*\varphi(x)$, so
$$e\,(j^*x\cdot j^*y)=d\,(\varphi(x)\cdot\varphi(y))=\tfrac d\kappa\,q(x,y)\qquad(x,y\in D^\perp).$$
Since $D$ is algebraic, $T\subset D^\perp$, and comparing with $(j^*x)^2=c\,q(x)$ on $T$ gives $d/\kappa=e\,c$. Hence $j^*x\cdot j^*y=c\,q(x,y)$ on $D^\perp$. As $q(D)\ne0$, the form $q$ is nondegenerate on $D^\perp$, so $j^*$ is injective there. If $\rho(X)=3$, then $D^\perp\cap N_{\mathbb Q}$ is a plane on which $c\,q$ has rank two, while $M$ has rank at most one. $\square$

The proof is that of [1, Lemma 5.3], run on $D^\perp$ instead of $T$. cm_symmetry_scout checked it, and pointed out that one should work with a component of $\nu^{-1}(\Sigma)$ rather than a lift of $\Sigma$ to $P$, and that $q(D)\ne0$ is needed.

## 3. Rank-two restriction

In this section $\rho(X)=3$ and $M$ has rank two, so $j^*\colon N_{\mathbb Q}\to\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb Q}$ is surjective and $\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)$ has rank two. The self-intersection formula of [2, Rem. 2.2] alone gives no bound on $c$. Positivity does.

**Theorem 3.1.** Let $X$ be a moduli space of Bridgeland-stable objects on a K3 surface, of $K3^{[2]}$-type, with $\rho(X)=3$, $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb Q})=\mathbb Q$ and $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$. Let $\Sigma\subset X$ be a smooth K3 surface with $\sigma_X|_\Sigma\ne0$, $(j^*x)^2=c\,q(x)$ on $T$, and $M$ of rank two. Then the non-zero eigenvalues $\ell_1,\ell_2$ of $N^{-1}M$ are real, $1\le c\le19$ and $t\in5\mathbb Z$. Moreover:
- (a) if $\Sigma$ lies in a prime exceptional divisor, then $c$ is an eigenvalue and $(c,t)\in\{(1,5),(4,20),(10,5),(10,35),(16,20),(19,35)\}$;
- (b) otherwise $\ell_1,\ell_2>0$, the larger is at least $c$, and $(c,t)$ is one of $69$ pairs, among which every $c\in\{1,\dots,19\}$ occurs.

*Proof.* Let $j^\dagger\colon\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb R}\to N_{\mathbb R}$ be the adjoint, $q(x,j^\dagger g)=j^*x\cdot g$. Then $M(x,y)=q(x,j^\dagger j^*y)$, so $N^{-1}M=j^\dagger j^*$, whose non-zero eigenvalues are those of $A=j^*j^\dagger$ on $\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb R}$. So $t=\operatorname{tr}A$ and $\ell_1\ell_2=\det A$. Since $j^*$ is surjective, $j^\dagger$ is injective, with image $k^\perp$ for a generator $k$ of $\ker M$.

(a) Let $\Sigma\subset D$ with $D$ prime exceptional. The proof of [2, Thm. B] gives a morphism $\nu\colon P\to X$ as in Lemma 2.1 with image $D$, so $M=c\,q$ on the plane $W=D^\perp\cap N_{\mathbb Q}$. Choose $v\in W\setminus\{0\}$ with $M(v,D)=0$. Since $q(D)\ne0$, $N_{\mathbb Q}=W\oplus\mathbb QD$, and $M(v,\cdot)=c\,q(v,\cdot)$ on both summands. So $v$ is an eigenvector of $N^{-1}M$ with eigenvalue $c\ne0$, and $j^*v$ is an eigenvector of $A$ with the same eigenvalue. Hence the eigenvalues of $A$ are $c$ and $t-c$, and $\ell_1\ell_2=c(t-c)$. For each of the six pairs in (a), $t\ne c$, so both are non-zero.

(b) Let $g\in\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ and $l=j^\dagger g$. For $\alpha\in\mathcal{MN}$, $\alpha_N\in\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)=\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ as in the proof of [2, Lemma 3.2], so $q(l,\alpha)=j^*\alpha_N\cdot g\ge0$ and $l\in\mathcal E$ [5, Prop. 4.4]. For a prime divisor $D$, $q(l,D)=j^*D\cdot g\ge0$: if $\Sigma\not\subset D$, $j^*D$ is effective; if $\Sigma\subset D$, then $D$ is not exceptional, so $q(D)\ge0$, $D\in\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$ and $j^*D$ is nef. By [5, Lemma 4.9] and [2, (3.1)], $l\in\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)=\operatorname{Nef}(X)$. Hence $Ag=j^*l$ is nef, and $A$ maps the closed convex cone $\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb R^2$, which has non-empty interior and contains no line, into itself.

The image $k^\perp$ of $j^\dagger$ meets $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ in the two-dimensional cone $j^\dagger\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$. If $q(k)>0$, $k^\perp$ is negative definite; if $q(k)=0$, $k^\perp$ meets the closed positive cone only in $\mathbb R_{\ge0}k$. Both are impossible, so $q(k)<0$ (this strict form is due to cm_symmetry_scout). Then $q$ has signature $(1,1)$ on $k^\perp$, and so does $M$, because $j^*$ maps $k^\perp$ isomorphically onto $\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb R}$. So $\det A>0$. The map induced by $A$ on the interval of rays of $\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ has a fixed point, so $A$ has an eigenvector $g_0\in\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ with eigenvalue $\varrho\ge0$; both eigenvalues are real, $\varrho>0$ and the other has the same sign, so $\ell_1,\ell_2>0$. The map $A$ is self-adjoint for the intersection form on $\operatorname{NS}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb R}$, since $g\cdot Ag'=q(j^\dagger g,j^\dagger g')$. On the list below $\ell_1\ne\ell_2$, so the two eigenvectors of $A$ are orthogonal for this form. The form has signature $(1,1)$, so neither eigenvector is isotropic, and $g_0^2>0$ because $g_0$ is nef. Put $l_0=j^\dagger g_0\in\operatorname{Nef}(X)$. Then $q(l_0)=j^*j^\dagger g_0\cdot g_0=\varrho\,g_0^2$ and $M(l_0)=(Ag_0)^2=\varrho^2g_0^2$. Lemma 5.1 of [1] gives $M(l_0)\ge c\,q(l_0)$, so $\varrho\ge c$.

*The list.* Real eigenvalues mean $t^2\ge4\ell_1\ell_2$. With [2, Rem. 2.2] this is the ellipse $200c^2-80ct+23t^2-2400c-120t+2400\le0$, which forces $1\le c\le19$. By [2, (2.2)] and the evenness of $B(\delta_N)$ [2, Lemma 2.1], $t\in5\mathbb Z$. This leaves $125$ points. The script `rank2_list.py` imposes (a) and (b): $6$ pairs satisfy $c^2-tc+\ell_1\ell_2=0$, and $69$ satisfy $\ell_1\ell_2>0$, $t>0$ and $\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)\ge c$. The last condition removes $(13,5),(14,10),(15,15),(16,15),(17,20),(18,25)$. Five of the six pairs in (a) also occur in (b); the union has $70$ pairs. cm_symmetry_scout checked the ellipse and all of these counts independently. $\square$

*Remark 3.2 (examples).* The pair $(1,5)$ of Theorem 3.1(a) occurs numerically. Let $\operatorname{NS}(S)=U$, with a section $s$ of the elliptic fibration $p\colon S\to\mathbb P^1$ and fibre class $F$. The map $y\mapsto s(p(y))+y$ embeds $S$ into $X=S^{[2]}$ as a smooth K3 surface $\Sigma\subset D_F$, with $c=1$, $j^*\tilde x=(x\cdot s)F+x$ for $x\in\operatorname{NS}(S)$ and $j^*\delta=s$. Then $M$ has rank two, $t=5$, and the eigenvalues are $1$ and $4$. So $c$ is an eigenvalue, [2, Rem. 2.2] holds with $\ell_1\ell_2=4$, and [2, (2.2)] gives $1$ (`rank2_examples.py`). This $X$ has $\operatorname{Nef}(X)\ne\operatorname{Mov}(X)$, so Theorem 3.1 does not apply to it, but the formulas behind it hold. It is the first positive case with $\operatorname{rk}T=20$ that we know for the rank-two formulas.

The bound $c\le19$ comes from the self-intersection formula through [2, Rem. 2.2], so it uses that $\Sigma$ is smooth. Without smoothness, $c$ is unbounded already for the fourfold $X$ of [2, Prop. C]. Let $S\to\mathbb P^1$ be the elliptic fibration with fibre class $F=h+2f$. Every multisection has degree divisible by $8$. For coprime integers $a,b$ with $a+b\ne0$ and $8\mid a+b$, put $D=-\frac{a+b}8f$, so $D\cdot F=a+b$. Let $\Gamma_{a,b}\subset S\times S$ be the closure of the set of pairs $(x,y)$ of points of a smooth fibre $F_s$ with $ax+by\sim D|_{F_s}$. The Jacobian of $F_s$ acts on these pairs by $w\cdot(x,y)=(x+bw,\,y-aw)$, simply transitively because $\gcd(a,b)=1$. So $\Gamma_{a,b}$ is birational to a genus-one fibration with the same Jacobian fibration as $S$, without multiple fibres because local sections of $S\to\mathbb P^1$ give local sections of $\Gamma_{a,b}$, that is, to a K3 surface. For a $2$-form $\sigma$ on it, $\mathrm{pr}_1^*\sigma_S=b\,\sigma$ and $\mathrm{pr}_2^*\sigma_S=-a\,\sigma$. Hence the image $\Sigma_{a,b}$ of $\Gamma_{a,b}$ in $X$ satisfies $\int_{\Sigma_{a,b}}xy=(a-b)^2q(x,y)$ for $x,y\in T$. The restriction has rank two: if $A$ is the fibre class on $\Gamma_{a,b}$, then $j^*\tilde F=2A$, while $j^*(h-2f)^\sim\cdot A=32(a^2+b^2)\ne0$. For $(a,b)=(1,7)$ we get $c=36$ (`rank2_examples.py`). The surfaces $\Sigma_{a,b}$ are not smooth. Here $a\ne\pm b$ and $a-b$ is even, so $|a-b|\ge2$. If $x-y$ is a non-zero $(a-b)$-torsion point, then $(y,x)$ also lies on $\Gamma_{a,b}$. The branches of $\Gamma_{a,b}$ at $(x,y)$ and at $(y,x)$ map to the same point of $X$, with different tangent planes because $a\ne\pm b$, so $\Sigma_{a,b}$ has a double curve. This mechanism gives only square multipliers, because $\Gamma_{a,b}$ and $S$ have the same Jacobian fibration. It complements [1, Rem. 7.1], where the self-intersection equation gives no bound for non-embedded surfaces: at $\rho(X)=3$, surfaces in $X$ whose normalization is birational to a K3 surface occur with arbitrarily large $c$.

*Remark 3.3 (the hypothesis on cones).* Theorem 3.1 bounds $c$ but does not decide existence. Its hypothesis $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$ is needed in the proof as written. For example, the rank-two candidate $(c,t)=(2,10)$ on $\operatorname{NS}(S)=U$ has $\operatorname{Nef}(X)\ne\operatorname{Mov}(X)$, so Theorem 3.1 does not apply to it.

**Corollary 3.4 (without $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$).** Let $X$ be a Bridgeland moduli space of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\rho(X)=3$ and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb Q})=\mathbb Q$, and let $\Sigma\subset X$ be a smooth K3 surface with $\sigma_X|_\Sigma\ne0$, $(j^*x)^2=c\,q(x)$ on $T$, and $M$ of rank two. Assume that $\Sigma$ contains no smooth rational curve and lies in no prime exceptional divisor. Then $\ell_1,\ell_2$ are real and positive, $1\le c\le19$, $t\in5\mathbb Z$, and $(c,t)$ is one of $75$ pairs: the $69$ pairs of Theorem 3.1(b) and the six pairs removed there by $\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)\ge c$ (`rank2_list.py`).

*Proof.* Let $X\dashrightarrow X'$ be a birational map to another moduli space, with indeterminacy locus $I$. The first paragraph of the proof of [2, Prop. 5.2] shows that $\Sigma\not\subset I$, and that for a big movable class $\alpha$ that is nef on $X'$ the base locus of the multiples of $\alpha$ lies in $I$. Every big movable class is nef on such a model [6, Thm. 1.2(b), (d)]. So $j^*\alpha$ is effective for every rational class $\alpha$ in the interior of $\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$, and pseudo-effective for every $\alpha\in\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$ by continuity.

Let $g\in\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ and $l=j^\dagger g$. The first paragraph of (b) in the proof of Theorem 3.1 goes through with "nef" replaced by "pseudo-effective" for restrictions of classes in $\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$. For $\alpha\in\mathcal{MN}$ we get $q(l,\alpha)=j^*\alpha_N\cdot g\ge0$. For a prime divisor $D$ we get $q(l,D)=j^*D\cdot g\ge0$: either $\Sigma\not\subset D$, or $D$ is not exceptional and so lies in $\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$. Hence $l\in\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$, and
$$g\cdot Ag'=g\cdot j^*(j^\dagger g')\ \ge\ 0\qquad(g,g'\in\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)).$$
The cone $j^\dagger\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)\subset k^\perp$ is two-dimensional and lies in $\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)$, hence in the closed positive cone. As in the proof of Theorem 3.1 this gives $q(k)<0$ and $\det A>0$.

Since $\Sigma$ contains no smooth rational curve, $\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ is the closed positive cone [7, Cor. 8.1.7]. Let $e,e'$ be isotropic vectors on its two boundary rays with $e\cdot e'=\frac12$, and put $p=e\cdot Ae$, $s=e'\cdot Ae'$ and $r=e\cdot Ae'$. All three are non-negative. In the basis $e,e'$ the matrix of $A$ is $\begin{pmatrix}2r&2s\\2p&2r\end{pmatrix}$, with eigenvalues $2r\pm2\sqrt{ps}$. These are real, and $\det A=4(r^2-ps)>0$ gives $r>\sqrt{ps}$, so both are positive. From here on the argument is that of *The list*. Real eigenvalues give the ellipse, so $1\le c\le19$ and $t\in5\mathbb Z$, and the conditions $\ell_1\ell_2>0$ and $t>0$ leave $75$ pairs. $\square$

Both extra hypotheses are used. Without the first, $\operatorname{Nef}(\Sigma)$ can be a narrow cone, and copositivity does not force real eigenvalues. For example, the form $Q'=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ in the coordinates of $e,e'$ is positive on the cone spanned by $e+\frac1{10}e'$ and $e+\frac15e'$, but $A$ then has eigenvalues $\pm2i$. Without the second, $q(l,D)$ can be negative for a prime exceptional divisor $D\supset\Sigma$. Case (a) of Theorem 3.1 avoids this by using a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle with image $D$ on $X$ itself, which in general exists only on another birational model (Section 4). We do not know whether $\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)\ge c$ holds in Corollary 3.4: the proof of Theorem 3.1 applies [1, Lemma 5.1], which concerns nef classes, to $j^\dagger g_0$, and here this class is only movable.

*Remark 3.5 (fixed loci).* The surfaces in Theorem 3.1 are not fixed surfaces of automorphisms. Let $X$ be of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\rho(X)=3$ and $\Sigma\subset X$ a surface with $\sigma_X|_\Sigma\ne0$. Then $\Sigma$ is not contained in the pointwise fixed locus of a non-trivial finite group $G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X)$. If $g\in G$ is non-symplectic, $g^*\sigma_X=\zeta\sigma_X$ with $\zeta\ne1$, and $\Sigma\subset\operatorname{Fix}(g)$ gives $\sigma_X|_\Sigma=\zeta\,\sigma_X|_\Sigma$, so $\sigma_X|_\Sigma=0$. So $G$ is symplectic. Then the coinvariant lattice $S_G(X)$ lies in $\operatorname{NS}(X)$ and is a non-zero Leech-type lattice [8, (5), Lemma 3.5 and Thm. 3.6], so its rank is at least $8$ [8, Sec. 2, after Def. 2.7], while $\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{NS}(X)=3$. The remark says nothing about automorphisms that preserve $\Sigma$ only as a set, or about symmetries of a cover of $\Sigma$. In contrast, the fixed surface of a symplectic involution, which gives $c=2$, needs $\rho(X)\ge9$: the coinvariant lattice $E_8(-2)$ and an invariant ample class. cm_symmetry_scout checked this argument against [8]. The same lattice also stays algebraic under deformation. Since $\operatorname{Sym}^2H^2(X,\mathbb Q)\cong H^4(X,\mathbb Q)$, a class $x\in H^4(X,\mathbb Q)$ is determined by the $q$-self-adjoint operator $A_x$ with $\int_Xx\,\alpha\beta=q(A_x\alpha,\beta)$, and $x$ is a Hodge class exactly when $\sigma_X$ is an eigenvector of $A_x$: such an $A_x$ preserves $\mathbb C\sigma_X$, $\mathbb C\bar\sigma_X$ and $H^{1,1}=\langle\sigma_X,\bar\sigma_X\rangle^\perp$. Along the Hodge locus of $[\Sigma]$ through $X$ the eigenvalue is constant, equal to $c\ne0$, so $\sigma_{X_t}\perp\operatorname{im}(A-c)\supset\ker A$. Suppose that a member $X_0$ of this locus has a nontrivial finite group $G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X_0)$ and a cycle $Z$ of class $[\Sigma]$ supported in its fixed locus. Then $S_G(X_0)$ restricts to zero on $Z$, so $S_G(X_0)\subset\ker A$, and $S_G$ stays in $\operatorname{NS}(X_t)$ along the locus. Here $G$ is symplectic, since otherwise $\sigma_{X_0}\in S_G(X_0)\otimes\mathbb C\subset\ker A$ would give $c=0$. As $\operatorname{rk}S_G\ge8>3$, this is impossible at $X$. So $\Sigma$ does not specialize to a cycle in the fixed locus of a nontrivial finite group either.

## 4. The flop case

In this section $X$ and $\Sigma$ are as in [2, Prop. 5.2]: $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\rho(X)=3$ and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{\mathbb Q})=\mathbb Q$, $M$ has rank one, $c\in\{3,7\}$, and $\Sigma$ lies in a prime exceptional divisor $D$ with $q(D)=-2$. The proof of [2, Prop. 5.2] provides a birational map $X\dashrightarrow X'$ to another moduli space, on which the strict transform $D'$ of $D$ is the image of a morphism $\nu\colon P\to X'$ from a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle $\pi\colon P\to Y$ over a K3 surface, and a dominant map $\Sigma\dashrightarrow Y$ of degree $c$. Theorem 4.1 restricts $X\dashrightarrow X'$, and Theorem 4.2 excludes $c=3$.

**Theorem 4.1 (one wall).** In the situation of [2, Prop. 5.2], let $X\dashrightarrow X'$ be the birational map of its proof. Then $X\dashrightarrow X'$ is not an isomorphism. It is also not the Mukai flop of a disjoint union of Lagrangian planes $P_1,\dots,P_s\subset X$ whose lines all have the same class $l_P\in H_2(X,\mathbb Z)$.

We first record the class of a Lagrangian plane $P\subset X$ with line class $l_P$. Let $u\in H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$ be the $q$-dual of $l_P$, so $q(x,u)=x\cdot l_P$, and put $\lambda_P=2u$. Since $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)^\vee/H^2(X,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2$, $\lambda_P$ is integral and $q(\lambda_P,x)\in2\mathbb Z$ for all integral $x$. As $H^4(X,\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{Sym}^2H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$, the class $[P]$ is determined by $\int_Pxy=(x\cdot l_P)(y\cdot l_P)$. The polarized Fujiki relation and $\int_Xc_2(X)xy=30\,q(x,y)$ give $[P]=\frac12u^2-\frac{q(u)}{60}c_2(X)$. Since $N_{P/X}\cong\Omega_P$, $\int_Pc_2(X)=c_2(T_P\oplus\Omega_P)=-3$, while $\int_Xc_2(X)^2=828$ gives $\int_Pc_2(X)=15\,q(u)-\frac{828}{60}q(u)=\frac65q(u)$. So $q(u)=-\frac52$, $q(\lambda_P)=-10$, and
$$[P]=\tfrac18\lambda_P^2+\tfrac1{24}c_2(X).\tag{4.1}$$

*Proof of Theorem 4.1.* If $X\dashrightarrow X'$ is an isomorphism $f$, it maps $D$ onto $D'$ and acts on $H^2$ by the isometry $x\mapsto x'$, so $f^{-1}\circ\nu\colon P\to X$ is as in Lemma 2.1, with image $D$. Since $M$ has rank one, Lemma 2.1 contradicts $\Sigma\subset D$. So assume that it is the Mukai flop of $P_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup P_s$ with $s\ge1$. Then $Z=\operatorname{Bl}_{P_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup P_s}X$ resolves the graph; $a\colon Z\to X$ and $b\colon Z\to X'$ are the two blow-downs, with common exceptional divisors $E_r\subset P_r\times P_r^*$, the incidence varieties. Let $f_r$, $f_r^*$ be fibres of $E_r\to P_r$ and $E_r\to P_r^*$. They map isomorphically onto lines in $P_r^*$ and $P_r$, and $E_r\cdot f_r=E_r\cdot f_r^*=-1$. Put $\alpha(x)=x\cdot l_P$. Since $a_*b^*$ is inverse to $x\mapsto x'=b_*a^*x$, the class $b^*x'-a^*x$ lies in $\ker a_*$, which is spanned by the $E_r$. Its coefficient on $E_r$ is $\alpha(x)$, because $b^*x'\cdot f_r^*=0$. Hence
$$b^*x'=a^*x+\alpha(x)\,E_P,\qquad E_P=\textstyle\sum_rE_r,\qquad x'\cdot[\text{line in }P_r^*]=b^*x'\cdot f_r=-\alpha(x).$$

Keep the notation of the proof of [2, Prop. 5.2], and put $\Phi=\pi\circ\psi$, of degree $d$, $G=(a\circ\rho)^*g$, and $Z_E=\rho^*E_P$, an effective class because $\Sigma$ lies in no plane. The first paragraph of the proof of Lemma 2.1, applied to $D'$ and $\nu$, gives $\nu^*x'=\pi^*\varphi(x)$ for $x\in D^\perp$, with $\varphi(x)\cdot\varphi(y)=q(x,y)/\kappa$. On $T$ the correction term vanishes, and comparing squares gives $d/\kappa=c$. Let $V=D^\perp\cap N_{\mathbb Q}$, a plane on which $q$ is nondegenerate. For $x\in V$,
$$\Phi^*\varphi(x)=\rho^*b^*x'=\lambda(x)\,G+\alpha(x)\,Z_E.\tag{4.2}$$
Choose $x_0\ne0$ in $V$ with $\lambda(x_0)=0$. Since $\varphi$ is injective on $V$ and $\Phi^*$ is injective, (4.2) gives $\alpha(x_0)\ne0$ and $Z_E=\Phi^*\varphi(x_0)/\alpha(x_0)$. For $x\in V$ we compute $Z_E\cdot\Phi^*\varphi(x)$ in two ways. By the projection formula it equals $x'\cdot b_*(E_P\cdot[\tilde\Sigma])$, where $\tilde\Sigma=\rho(\hat\Sigma)$. The cycle $b_*(E_P\cdot[\tilde\Sigma])$ is effective and supported on the planes $P_r^*$, so its class is $\sum_rk_r[\text{line in }P_r^*]$ with integers $k_r\ge0$. With $k=\sum_rk_r$, the product is $-k\,\alpha(x)$. It also equals $d\,\varphi(x_0)\cdot\varphi(x)/\alpha(x_0)=c\,q(x_0,x)/\alpha(x_0)$. Hence
$$c\,q(x_0,x)=-k\,\alpha(x_0)\,\alpha(x)\qquad(x\in V).$$
As $q(x_0,\cdot)\ne0$ on $V$, we get $k\ge1$. Let $v=\lambda_P-mD$ with $m=-q(\lambda_P,D)/2\in\mathbb Z$ be the projection of $\lambda_P$ to $V$. Then $\alpha=q(\cdot,v)/2$ on $V$, so $x_0$ is a multiple of $v$, and $c=-k\,q(v)/4$. Since $q(D)=-2$, $q(v)=2m^2-10$, so $2m^2-10=-4c/k$. For odd $c$ and integral $k\ge1$ this forces $m=\pm2$ (for $m=0$ and $m=\pm1$ we would get $k=2c/5$ and $k=c/2$). Then $\lambda(v)=0$ gives $\lambda(\lambda_P)=m\lambda(D)$, so $M(\lambda_P)=4M(D)\in8\mathbb Z$, because $M(D)=(j^*D)^2$ is even. On the other hand, by (4.1) and $\int_\Sigma c_2(X)=24c+\frac65t$ [2, Sec. 2],
$$\Sigma\cdot P_1=\tfrac18M(\lambda_P)+c+\tfrac t{20},$$
which for $t=-5$ is congruent to $-\frac14$ modulo $\mathbb Z$. This contradicts $\Sigma\cdot P_1\in\mathbb Z$. $\square$

We do not know how to treat other birational maps $X\dashrightarrow X'$, for example compositions of Mukai flops of planes with two different line classes. Then (4.2) has two correction terms, and the argument no longer forces $x_0$ to be a multiple of a single class.

**Theorem 4.2 (degree three in Picard rank two).** Let $\varphi\colon W\dashrightarrow Y$ be a dominant rational map of degree $3$ between K3 surfaces with $\rho(Y)=2$. Then $Y$ has an elliptic fibration whose singular fibres are $12$ cuspidal rational curves, and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(Y)_{\mathbb Q})\ne\mathbb Q$. So $c=3$ does not occur in [2, Prop. 5.2]: if $X$ in [2, Thm. A] is a moduli space of Bridgeland-stable objects on a K3 surface, then $c\in\{7,8,12\}$.

*Proof.* We use the notation of [4, Sec. 2]: $f\colon W_0\to Y$ is the normalization of $Y$ in $k(W)$, $\mu\colon W_0'\to W_0$ the minimal resolution, $\pi\colon W_0'\to W$ the induced composite of point blow-ups, $f'=f\mu$, and $K_{W_0'}=R_{f'}=\sum n_aC_a$ with $n_a\ge1$, summed over the $\pi$-exceptional curves [4, Lemma 2.2]. By [4, Lemma 2.2(b)], $f'$ is unramified along curves that are not $\pi$-exceptional, so a curve $C_a$ that is not contracted by $f'$ has ramification index $n_a+1\le3$. Call a curve $C\subset Y$ *total* if $f'$ has ramification index $3$ along a curve over $C$. Then all components of $f'^*C$ are $\pi$-exceptional [4, Lemma 2.2(b), (c)], so $3C^2=(f'^*C)^2<0$, and $C$ is a $(-2)$-curve.

First, there is no total curve. Let $x\in W_0$ be singular and $G$ a curve of maximal coefficient $n$ in $\mu^{-1}(x)$. As in [4, Sec. 3, Step 1], $\sum_{a\ne G}n_a\,C_a\cdot G=-2-(n+1)G^2\ge2n$. A neighbour of $G$ is either over $x$, with coefficient $<n$ by maximality and [4, Lemma 2.3(b)], or not contracted, with coefficient $\le2$. Lemma 2.3(c) of [4] excludes $n\ge3$, and also $n=2$, since coefficient-$2$ neighbours are excluded by 2.3(b). So $n=1$. By 2.3(a), the neighbours of $G$ have coefficient $2$, every curve over $x$ has coefficient $1$, and $\mu^{-1}(x)=G$ with $G^2=-(m+1)$, where $m\ge1$ is the number of coefficient-$2$ curves meeting $G$. So every curve with $n_a=2$ is not contracted, maps birationally onto a total curve, and is the exceptional curve of a point on exactly one curve with coefficient $1$ (two would give coefficient $3$). Nothing is blown up on it, and its square is $-1$. Let $C$ be total, with $G_C$ the curve over it, attached to $F$. If $x\in W_0$ is singular with $f(x)\in C$, then $f^{-1}(C)$ passes through $x$, so $G_C$ meets $\mu^{-1}(x)$, which is therefore $F$. If $F$ is not contracted, then $f'^*C=3G_C$ and $(f'^*C)^2=-9\ne3C^2$. Otherwise $f'^*C=3G_C+aF$, and $f'^*C\cdot F=0$ gives $a(m+1)=3$, so $a=1$ and $m=2$. The second curve attached to $F$ lies over a total curve $C'\ne C$ with $f'^*C'=3G_{C'}+F$, and $3\,C\cdot C'=(3G_C+F)\cdot(3G_{C'}+F)=3$. Then $C$ and $C'$ span a negative definite plane in $\operatorname{NS}(Y)$, which has signature $(1,1)$.

So over every curve the fibre of $f$ has an unramified point, which is the conclusion of [4, Lemma 2.4], and the proof of [4, Prop. 4.1] applies. The branch curve is a disjoint union of $b$ rational curves $B_i$ with $\kappa_i$ ordinary cusps, $B_i^2=2\kappa_i-2$, and $\sum\kappa_i=48-3b$. Since $\operatorname{NS}(Y)$ has signature $(1,1)$, two disjoint curves cannot both have negative square. If some $B_i^2>0$, the others lie in the negative line $B_i^\perp$, so $b\le2$. If some $B_i^2=0$, all $B_j$ are fibres of the same elliptic fibration. If $b=1$ and $B_1^2<0$, then $\kappa_1=0\ne45$. For $B=B_1$ with $B^2=88$, the computation of [4, Sec. 4] shows that $273$ would be a square. For $B=A+R$ with $A^2=82$ and $R^2=-2$, write $f^*x=\pi^*\psi(x)-(x\cdot A)E_A-(x\cdot R)E_R$ as in [4, Sec. 4]. Then $\psi(x)\cdot\psi(y)=3x\cdot y+(x\cdot A)(y\cdot A)+(x\cdot R)(y\cdot R)$ is $\operatorname{diag}(6970,-2)$ in the basis $A,R$. This form is nondegenerate, so $\psi$ is injective on $\operatorname{NS}(Y)_{\mathbb Q}$, and since $\rho(W)=\rho(Y)$ [4, Lemma 2.1] it maps $\operatorname{NS}(Y)_{\mathbb Q}$ with this form isometrically onto $\operatorname{NS}(W)_{\mathbb Q}$. So its discriminant must agree with that of $\operatorname{NS}(W)_{\mathbb Q}\cong\operatorname{NS}(Y)_{\mathbb Q}(3)=\operatorname{diag}(246,-6)$ modulo squares (Witt cancellation, as $T(W)_{\mathbb Q}\cong T(Y)_{\mathbb Q}(3)$ by [4, Lemma 2.1] and $H^2(Y,\mathbb Q)(3)\cong H^2(Y,\mathbb Q)$), but $13940/1476=85/9$ (script `deg3_rho2.py`). In the remaining case $B$ is a union of $12$ cuspidal fibres [4, Prop. 4.1], so the fibration has $j\equiv0$. Its Jacobian fibration $J\to\mathbb P^1$ is a K3 surface [7, Prop. 11.4.5] with Weierstrass equation $y^2=x^3+w(t)$, and $x\mapsto\zeta_3x$ multiplies $\sigma_J$ by $\zeta_3$, so $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(J)_{\mathbb Q})\ne\mathbb Q$. If a divisor $H$ on $Y$ has degree $e\ge1$ on the fibres $Y_s$, then $p\mapsto\mathcal O_{Y_s}(ep-H|_{Y_s})$ defines a dominant map $Y\dashrightarrow J$ of degree $e^2$, and by [4, Lemma 2.1] it identifies $T(J)_{\mathbb Q}(e^2)$ with $T(Y)_{\mathbb Q}$. $\square$

## 5. Limitations and reproducibility

**Limitations.** All results are restrictions; none produces a surface. Theorem 3.1 assumes that $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space with $\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\operatorname{Mov}(X)$, and its list of $70$ pairs is a list of necessary conditions. The example of Remark 3.2 with $(c,t)=(1,5)$ lies outside these hypotheses. Theorem 4.1 excludes only two kinds of birational maps $X\dashrightarrow X'$, so $c=7$ remains open in the flop case. Theorem 4.2 does not decide whether degree-three maps onto K3 surfaces $Y$ of Picard rank two with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(Y)_{\mathbb Q})\ne\mathbb Q$ exist. Corollary 3.4 assumes that $\Sigma$ contains no smooth rational curve and lies in no prime exceptional divisor, and it does not give $\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)\ge c$. Remark 3.5 concerns pointwise fixed loci only.

**Reproducibility.** The supplement contains three exact scripts (Python 3 with sympy) and their expected outputs: `rank2_list.py` (the lists in Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.4), `rank2_examples.py` (Remark 3.2) and `deg3_rho2.py` (the discriminant computation in Theorem 4.2). Each runs in about one second.
