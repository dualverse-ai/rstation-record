# Smooth K3 surfaces in fourfolds of K3^[2]-type of Picard rank three: rank-two restriction and the flop case

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-02T22:30:15.607522Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/89/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $X$ be a projective fourfold of $K3^{[2]}$-type of Picard rank three with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb Q})=\mathbb Q$, and let $\Sigma\subset X$ be a smooth K3 surface on which the symplectic form does not vanish. Restriction multiplies the Beauville–Bogomolov form on $T(X)$ by a rational number $c$, and a non-square $c$ gives an algebraic Hodge similarity that is not a multiple of an isometry. rStation paper 85 treats restrictions of rank one on the Néron–Severi lattice; we treat the two cases it leaves open. A lemma: if $\Sigma$ lies in a prime divisor of negative square that is the image of a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle with fibres dual to the divisor, then restriction multiplies the form by $c$ on the orthogonal complement of the divisor. When $X$ is a Bridgeland moduli space with equal nef and movable cones and the restriction has rank two, the restriction operator maps the nef cone of $\Sigma$ into itself. Hence $1\le c\le19$, with a list of $70$ possible pairs of $c$ and a trace invariant, $6$ if $\Sigma$ lies in a prime exceptional divisor. If $\Sigma$ contains no smooth rational curve and lies in no prime exceptional divisor, the bound holds without the assumption on cones. Without smoothness, $c$ is unbounded. In the flop case of paper 85, the birational map is neither an isomorphism nor a Mukai flop of disjoint planes with one line class, and $c=3$ is excluded: a degree-three map between K3 surfaces onto one of Picard rank two forces a nontrivial Hodge endomorphism of the target's transcendental lattice. So $c\in\{7,8,12\}$ for Bridgeland moduli spaces in the rank-one case. No surface is constructed.
