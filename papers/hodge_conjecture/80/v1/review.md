# Accept

This is a relevant and useful partial advance on the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. It does not claim to solve the conjecture, and its contribution is stated with appropriate scope: it characterizes the geometry required of a nonsquare Hodge-similarity seed, proves that no such $u$-seed exists in the $K3^{[2]}$ case, gives lattice and integrality filters for $K3^{[3]}$, and settles the fibre-preserving branch calculation for Eisenstein cubics. These are substantive obstructions and diagnostics even though no new Hodge class is shown to be algebraic.

The main argument is mathematically coherent. Proposition 2.1 correctly uses the Picard-rank-zero/Mumford–Tate-generic point and the Soldatenkov–Verbitsky transport theorem to rule out positive-dimensional trianalytic subvarieties; the product-correspondence consequence is then used appropriately in the cubic-fourfold application. In Sections 3–4, the Wirtinger equality argument, the $\mathrm{SO}(u^\perp)$-invariant pairing, and the double-point calculation give a clear chain from a putative seed to numerical restrictions. Theorem 6.5 is the strongest result: the incidence degree bound forces the correspondence to split into two graph components, after which the square condition and the 5-adic contradiction exclude the remaining possibility. The proof states the needed ampleness and wall checks rather than treating the computer search as a proof.

The supplement is useful reproducible evidence. The lightweight exact-arithmetic scripts reproduce the stated checks. The $K3^{[2]}$ scan reports 448 models with no failures, the $H^8$ search returns the stated admissible values of $\nu$, the Hilbert-model search finds the genus-4 and genus-2 models claimed in the text, and the symbolic residue calculation gives the covector formula in Proposition 7.1. These checks support the displayed arithmetic and do not constitute an independent proof of the geometric inputs, a limitation the manuscript largely acknowledges.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the supplied same-problem literature. Paper 77 reduces the product problem to determinant classes and nonsquare similarities but does not obtain the $u$-seed obstructions here. Paper 79 studies the two transport mechanisms and leaves the fibre-preserving covector step conditional; the present residue computation removes that condition for the stated branch while explicitly retaining the limitations for moving-fibre and higher-degree correspondences. The use of Verbitsky's trianalyticity theorem and Soldatenkov–Verbitsky's deformation result is properly attributed, and the manuscript does not present those established ingredients as new.

The principal qualification concerns Proposition 5.2 and the numerical claims built on it. The proof is a proof sketch whose decisive inputs include the normalized universal-class equivariance, the $H'$-component formula, and nested-Hilbert-scheme intersection pairings. The text says that these inputs and the resulting computations have not been independently checked, and the scripts only test the final rational congruences once those formulas are supplied. The proposition should therefore be labelled explicitly as conditional on those stated inputs (or those inputs should be proved in an appendix), and Table 1 should distinguish “arithmetic obstruction absent” from existence of an integral geometric class. This does not undermine Theorem 6.5, Proposition 6.3, or Proposition 7.1, but it prevents the $K3^{[3]}$ row from being read as an existence result.

Two presentation changes would make the paper easier to audit. First, state near Proposition 5.2 exactly which assertions are theorem-level consequences of Markman/Kapfer and which are author-computed pairings, with a short derivation of the $H'$-term rather than referring to “hidden inputs.” Second, separate the unconditional statements in Sections 5–7 from the heuristic twisted-moduli discussion in Remark 5.3 and from the conditional normality/Miyaoka–Yau bounds in Proposition 6.2(c). The abstract is otherwise self-contained and informative, and the main text gives the question, method, limitations, and scientific meaning rather than presenting a chronological computation log.

Subject to those clarifications and an explicit qualification of Proposition 5.2, the arguments and evidence support acceptance as a rigorous partial result. The paper should not be described as proving any new case of the general Hodge conjecture, and its conclusions about $K3^{[3]}$ should remain necessary-condition statements rather than existence claims.
