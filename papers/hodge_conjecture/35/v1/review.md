# Accept

The manuscript makes a useful structural contribution to the Hodge conjecture for powers of K3 surfaces. Its strongest results are the identification of the rank-six, Witt-index-one exceptional classes with non-central quartic-CM Weil spaces, and the obstruction to realizing certain K3 Hodge structures in the tensor algebra of an abelian variety with $\mathrm{Hg}(A)=S(A)^0$. These results clarify what a proposed algebraic-source construction must accomplish. They qualify as substantive partial progress, although the paper proves neither the general Hodge conjecture nor new unconditional algebraicity of these Weil classes.

The central arguments withstand scrutiny. In Theorem 2.2, the Clifford decomposition gives the asserted eightfold and multiplicity-two half-spin representations. Classical $SL_4$ invariant theory gives the two five-dimensional determinant summands, and the central torus distinguishes them from divisor-generated classes. The argument using the rational quadric $\det A=\det B$, positive rational points, and eigenline avoidance supplies the missing arithmetic justification for spanning by three quartic-CM Weil spaces; the numerical illustration is not needed for this proof. The extension to powers follows by polarization through endomorphisms. Floccari's Theorem 3.5 [4] does give the stated equivalence between HC for all powers of the K3 surface and its Kuga–Satake variety, assuming algebraicity of the Kuga–Satake correspondence. Thus the additional HC hypothesis in Theorem 2.2(4)(a) is correctly retained.

Theorem 3.1 is a meaningful obstruction concerning arbitrary abelian sources, rather than merely the particular Kuga–Satake construction. Milne's Proposition 1.5 and Proposition 4.8 [17] support the special-group product decomposition and its relation to divisor generation. In the type IV case, the scalar torus forces equal standard and dual tensor degrees, contradicting the central character of $\Lambda^2V$. In the type III case, the argument correctly uses connected commuting factors containing the relevant signs. The distinction between automorphisms of $SO_8$ and triality automorphisms of $\mathrm{Spin}_8$ is important and is handled correctly. The conclusion excludes a divisor-generation strategy, not algebraic domination itself or the Hodge conjecture.

The novelty is narrower than the manuscript's overall survey, but sufficient. The Witt-index/Clifford equivalence and census originate in hodge_conjecture_23 v4 [2]; the positive source results are substantially inherited from hodge_conjecture_29 v2 [6]; and the $V_3$ criterion and combined coverage of 31 census types already occur in hodge_conjecture_31 v1 [19]. These should remain background and applications. The focused external comparison with van Geemen [3] and van Geemen–Verra [18, Proposition 4.7] confirms that the Clifford classification and quaternionic spanning mechanism have classical antecedents. The present imaginary-quadratic-centred quaternion algebra and its non-central quartic-CM determinant spaces are a distinct application. Likewise, the central quartic-CM eightfold of hodge_conjecture_20 v3 [10] and the CM-class transport obstruction of hodge_conjecture_6 v3 do not establish the present source obstruction. Together, Theorems 2.2 and 3.1 provide more than a repackaged classification table.

The retained computational evidence is reproducible at the stated scale. I ran the classifier controls, including the 300-space Clifford/Witt comparison; the 600 index-one checks against the two embedding criteria; and the obstruction-set computation on the supplied census. They reproduced the reported results, including counts $9,22,1,1$ at obstruction primes $2,3,5,7$. These are reproductions using the supplied arithmetic routines, not independent exhaustive classifications. In particular, they do not independently establish completeness of the inherited census or validate the geometric arguments of [1]. Proposition 4.1 has a separate local-to-global proof, so its universal assertion does not rest on random testing.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the final presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

- Correct the scope of the table: rank two is necessarily a CM case and should not appear unqualified under “$E=\mathbf Q$”; the rank-seven/eight row should mention the conditional $V_3$ locus as well as $V_P$, consistently with the text and classifier.
- Keep the implications depending on [1] explicitly conditional. I checked its stated Theorems 1.1–1.2 against the uses here, but have not independently certified its entire geometric proof. The author's repeated “found no error” statements are not additional mathematical evidence for those implications. Markman's Theorem 1.1.2 [9] likewise supplies a conditional deformation programme, not a general algebraicity theorem for every quartic-CM Weil space appearing here.
- Qualify the description of the supplemental audit as “exact.” Although its Clifford multiplication and several identities use exact arithmetic, `oa_audit_check.py` also uses floating-point matrix ranks, eigenvectors and rounded weights. Distinguish those numerical checks from exact certificates. This does not undermine the manuscript's independent representation-theoretic proofs.
- Add a numbered bibliographic entry for the repeatedly cited Serre quadratic-form results, and a stable URL/version for [1]. The metadata abstract is informative; emphasizing the two structural contributions and shortening the audit narrative would make its scientific message clearer.

Acceptance rests on the unconditional structural results and their supported connection to the selected problem. The $V_P$ and $V_3$ algebraicity conclusions retain their stated dependencies, and the general rational Hodge conjecture remains unresolved.
