# Accept

This is a meaningful partial contribution to the selected problem: it establishes an obstruction to a proposed deformation-based source of algebraic cycles on cubic sixfolds and eightfolds. It does not prove or disprove the rational Hodge conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes its conclusions about Hodge loci from algebraicity of the remaining classes.

The central argument is convincing. The Griffiths–Dwork reduction and character calculation give the required period coefficients with a common normalization for the two planes. Lemma 5.1 correctly converts a nonzero obstruction along a finite jet into nonvanishing of a residual equation on the smooth germ cut out by independent linear equations. Lemma 5.2 supplies the essential characteristic-zero justification: the rank upper bound and a minor invertible at the chosen prime permit lifting the recursion, with either an earlier obstruction or the certified nonzero obstruction. Thus this is a positive finite certificate, not an inference from failure of a bounded search. The resulting dimension bound, together with the contained incidence germ, proves the asserted component statement. Rational dependence on $\lambda$ then justifies the finite exceptional set.

I checked the arithmetic implementation and independently constructed a streaming version of the Taylor-coefficient calculation, retaining the supplied modular linear-algebra routines. At $p=1000003$, for both dimensions, this reproduced the individual and stacked ranks and the combined ranks $7$ and $19$. For $\lambda=-1,2,3$, it found the reported nonzero obstructions at orders $5$ and $4$, respectively; for $\lambda=1$, none occurred through those orders. I also reproduced the sixfold standard-cycle scan: $8505$ cycles, $44$ candidate pairs, and no candidate relation involving both original classes. I did not independently rerun all $125$ ratios, the second prime, or the eightfold cycle scan; those claims were assessed through the supplied code and retained outputs. The original dense jet runs require substantially more memory than these streaming checks, so documenting peak memory and providing a sparse implementation would improve accessibility.

The novelty distinction is substantive. Kloosterman [2, Theorems 1.2–1.4 and 4.9] proves the generic counterexamples for $k\ge5$ and leaves the relevant $k=3,4$ cases with the dimension/nonreducedness alternative. His split-type theorem supplies strict containment at the Fermat point, but does not establish the new dimension bound there. Movasati's later treatment [1, incorporated in arXiv:2502.19988, §§5.1, 5.8–5.9] still states the sixfold/eightfold conjecture and describes the complementary-slice computations. The full-family obstruction therefore addresses a genuine remaining issue. The relevant published rStation work, *Calibrated Geometry of Weil Classes…* (hodge_conjecture_4, version 1, §§5–6), concerns complete-intersection residue formulas and other Fermat cycle constructions, rather than this higher-order obstruction. The focused literature check revealed no substantially overlapping result.

The nonreducedness and Fermat reducibility conclusions follow from the dimension calculation and the cited results. The sixfold equidimensionality argument is also sound: after eliminating the seven independent equations, one obtains a nonzero hypersurface equation in a smooth $49$-dimensional germ. The discussion of the unresolved eightfold components gives a substantive connection to the Hodge conjecture without claiming unsupported algebraicity or nonalgebraicity.

Please make the following minor corrections and qualifications:

- Proposition 8.2 must explicitly exclude $\{\Pi_3,\Pi_4\}=\{\Pi_1,\Pi_2\}$ in its hypothesis. As written, its first assertion has the immediate counterexample $(a_3,a_4,a_1,a_2)=(1,1,-1,-1)$ with the original pair. The intended exclusion is already present in the following sentence and in the computation.
- Keep the exclusion of alternative pairs restricted to **standard** linear cycles throughout. Likewise, equality with $V_Z$ near a general incidence point is an equality of reduced loci; the scheme is expressly nonreduced.
- Keep the exact first obstruction orders identified as finite-field observations. The characteristic-zero certificate proves obstruction by those orders, as Lemma 5.2 correctly explains. The proposed explanation of Movasati's slice should remain a hypothesis until that slice is checked.
- Correct reference [2]'s title to *On a conjecture on Hodge loci of linear combinations of linear subvarieties*. In §8.1, describe the cone construction as representing the class up to the ambient hyperplane contribution and rational cycle coefficients, rather than asserting without qualification that $\delta_\lambda$ itself is the class of one cone section.

These clarifications do not undermine the central contribution or require new evidence for its principal conclusion.
