# Movasati's GADEPs Conjecture 1 fails for cubic sixfolds and eightfolds

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-10-16T08:08:27.084446Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/5/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Movasati's GADEPs Conjecture 1 concerns smooth cubic hypersurfaces $X\subset\mathbb P^{n+1}$, $n=6,8$, containing two $n/2$-planes $\Pi_1,\Pi_2$ meeting in a $\mathbb P^{n/2-3}$. It predicts that the Hodge locus of $\delta=[\Pi_1]+\lambda[\Pi_2]$ has dimension $\dim V_Z+1$, where $V_Z$ is the locus of cubics containing two such planes, and hence new algebraic cycles. Kloosterman disproved the analogue for $n\ge10$. For $n=6,8$ he proved that either the conjecture holds or the Hodge locus is nonreduced, and he settled $\lambda=1$ in the conjecture's favour. We prove the conjecture is false for $n=6$ and $n=8$ for all $\lambda$ outside a finite set, including every $\lambda=\check r/r\ne1$ in Movasati's range $1\le r,|\check r|\le10$. Precisely, $V_Z$ is an irreducible component of $NL(\delta)$, both at the Fermat cubic and at a general point of the incidence variety. By Kloosterman's dichotomy, $NL(\delta)$ is nonreduced along $V_Z$. At the Fermat point, $NL(\delta)_{\rm red}$ is reducible. The extra components have dimension at most $\dim V_Z$, exactly $\dim V_Z$ for $n=6$, and they are not deformation loci of another pair of standard linear cycles. The proof is an exact jet computation in the full transverse family (56 resp. 120 parameters). It uses a period formula for linear cycles on the Fermat cubic, proved from Griffiths-Dwork reduction and symmetry, together with a jet-test lemma and a reduction-mod-$p$ certificate valid in characteristic 0. Generic arcs are obstructed at order 5 ($n=6$) and order 4 ($n=8$), while $\lambda=1$ and arcs inside $V_Z$ are not. Bearing on the Hodge conjecture: the proposed source of new cycles on cubic 6- and 8-folds does not exist near general points. For $n=6$ the Hodge conjecture is known for all smooth cubics. For $n=8$ the extra Fermat components remain a possible open test case.
