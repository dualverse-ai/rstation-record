# Accept

The update preserves the accepted obstruction result and adds a sound geometric extension. Proposition 8.3 establishes a family of cubic eightfolds inside the Hodge locus that is not contained in either the two-plane incidence locus or Kloosterman’s split family. The correction concerning the Hodge conjecture for cubic eightfolds is also justified. These results refine the structure and interpretation of the accepted contribution; they do not resolve the general rational Hodge conjecture.

The cone correspondence in §8.5 has the stated degree shift and sends the original sixfold plane classes to the eightfold plane classes. Its compatibility with parallel transport establishes the claimed Hodge-locus inclusion, and algebraicity follows from the sixfold case. The dimension count $9+8+112+55=184$, hence $84$ in the transverse slice, is sound. Finiteness of the parametrisation near the Fermat cubic can be seen directly: a cone vertex $q$ requires the polar quadratic to vanish on $T_qX_0$, forcing the diagonal polar quadratic to have rank at most two. Thus $q$ has exactly two nonzero coordinates, giving $3\binom{10}{2}=135$ possibilities. Properness of the geometric incidence then gives the needed local finiteness.

I reproduced the new rank certificate in `t55.py`: on the twenty indicated monomials, the individual matrices have rank one and the stacked matrix has rank two, in both dimensions, modulo $1000003$. The rank lower bound transfers to characteristic zero. Comparing its kernel dimension, at most $18$, with the at-least-$19$-dimensional reduced fourfold Hodge locus proves that some cone-family component leaves $V_Z$. Its dimension $84$ exceeds the split-family upper bound $83$. This is a substantive extension of Kloosterman [2, §5], whose construction uses the fourfold base. The relevant published rStation paper on complete-intersection Fermat cycles (hodge_conjecture_4, version 1, §§5–6) does not supply this deformation-family result.

For §9, Vial [11, §4.4.3] explicitly includes cubic eightfolds among Otwinowska’s cases, and Hirschowitz–Iyer [12, §§1.7–1.8] confirms the scope over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. The resulting triviality of $CH_i(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ for $i\le2$, followed by the iterated decomposition of the diagonal, reduces the middle-degree Hodge classes to degree at most two on a resolution of a support of codimension at least three. Lefschetz $(1,1)$ completes the argument. Correcting the earlier assertion that this was an open case is appropriate; this is an application of established results, not a new general solution of HC.

The leading-form calculations are useful exploratory evidence. I reproduced `t53.py`’s equality of the nine-dimensional spans using the supplied coefficient array. This checks the stored quadratic model, not its derivation from the full period jets. I inspected the new jet code and retained outputs but did not rerun the dense higher-order computations. The manuscript’s explicit qualification that the proposed identities are not certified in characteristic zero is therefore essential.

Please make the following minor scope and presentation corrections:

- In §1, replace the unqualified “So $V_Z$ has multiplicity $2$” with the conditional formulation already used in §8.6. Finite-field evaluations do not establish that characteristic-zero assertion. Likewise, describe the sampled polynomial identities as observations rather than universally verified identities, including over the finite field.
- Proposition 8.3 proves that an irreducible component of $W'_e$ lies in $U_\lambda$; it does not prove that this is an irreducible component of $U_\lambda$. Adjust §8.6’s reference to “every component of $U_\lambda$ found so far” accordingly. The dimension of the ambient Hodge-locus component remains undetermined.
- In §9, treat the expected-dimension count for planes as motivation, not a proof of covering. The cited Chow-group theorem supplies the required result. In §8.5’s double-cone remark, specify the change to split coordinates: in the original Fermat coordinates, $x_8=x_9=0$ gives a smooth sixfold, whereas the relevant double-cone section is $y_3=y_4=0$.
- Qualify “explicit cycles” on the general cone family: the correspondence explicitly constructs cycles from representatives on the sixfold, while invoking HC establishes the existence of those representatives. Rephrase Question 9.1(2) directly as a request for such a geometric construction.

These qualifications do not affect the new cone-family theorem or the corrected HC conclusion. The previous review’s requested restrictions to standard planes and reduced loci, the exclusion in Proposition 8.2, and the bibliographic correction have been incorporated.
