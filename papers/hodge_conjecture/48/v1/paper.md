# Inversion-Pairing Obstructions, Exact Root-Line Brauer Induction, and Coordinate-Subform Splittings for the $E_8$ Hurwitz Realization of Theorem C

## Abstract

For a polarized abelian sixfold $(A, \iota_D, \theta)$ with quaternionic multiplication by a definite quaternion algebra $D$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the polarization is governed by a $3 \times 3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian matrix $T$ of reduced norm $\Delta(A) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \in \mathbb{Q}^\times / (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$. By `hodge_conjecture_43` (v3, Theorem A), an imaginary quadratic subfield $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d_k}) \subset D$ splits the induced $6 \times 6$ $k$-Hermitian form on $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cong k^6$ if and only if $(-\Delta(A), -d_k)_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong D$, which admits a solution $k \subset D$ if and only if $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(A)}) \hookrightarrow D$; in that split-embedding regime, Markman's theorem on split sixfolds of Weil type [12] and $D^\times$-translation [8] establish algebraicity of the $7$-dimensional space $Q_D(A) \subset H^{3,3}(A, \mathbb{Q})$ of quaternionic Weil classes (`hodge_conjecture_43`, Theorem B). When $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(A)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$, $T$ is anisotropic in $D^3$ (`hodge_conjecture_43`, Theorem C), and the first finite-group Hurwitz curve realization $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)} \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^5$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in this non-embedding regime was recently constructed in [11, Theorem 4.2] over $D_2 = (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)}) = 15 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, using the quaternionic reflection group $\Gamma_{E_8} = \operatorname{Aut}_{D_2}(\Lambda_{E_8}) \cong 2^{1+4}_- . \mathfrak{A}_5$ of order $1920$ [9, 10].

In this paper, we establish three structural results on genus-zero Hurwitz covers in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ and the PEL Shimura variety $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)})$. First, we prove the **Inversion-Pairing and Transitive-Generation Theorem over $\mathbb{P}^1$**: across all $17$ conjugacy classes of $\Gamma_{E_8}$, the $15$ self-inverse classes have square-free local reduced norms in $\{1, 2, 5\}$ ($v_3 = 0$), while the only classes with odd $3$-adic local norm valuation are a single non-self-inverse pair of order-$6$ classes $(C_{04}, C_{05} = C_{04}^{-1})$ with $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(g - I_2) = 3$. We prove conceptually via the $\mathfrak{A}_5$ action on the five orthogonal root-line frames why $C_{04} \neq C_{05}$, and deduce that for every Hurwitz cover $C_t \to \mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ ($r \ge 3$), $v_3(\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})) \equiv m(C_{04}) + m(C_{05}) \pmod 2$. Consequently, $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 15$ is the unique Theorem C discriminant achievable over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$, every such cover is automatically connected with full monodromy group $H = \Gamma_{E_8}$ (since $\langle x, y \rangle = \Gamma_{E_8}$ for all $x \in C_{04} \cup C_{05}$ and $y \in C_{06} \cup C_{08}$), and **no inversion-invariant branch multiset over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$** (`hodge_conjecture_40` v2, Theorem D) can ever reach the Theorem C locus; we exhibit an explicit inversion-invariant $5$-tuple $\mathbf{g}^{(\mathrm{inv})} \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^5$ with $m(C_{04}) = m(C_{05}) = 1$ where the paired $3$-adic factors cancel to give $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(\mathrm{inv})}) = \frac{1}{16} = (\frac{1}{4})^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$. Second, for the faithful degree-$4$ symplectic character $\chi_4$ of $\Gamma_{E_8}$, we prove that every real linear character $\psi : K \to \{\pm 1\}$ has even Frobenius multiplicity $\langle \operatorname{Ind}_K^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\psi), \chi_4 \rangle \in 2\mathbb{Z}$, that the $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice of characters induced from cyclic subgroups has exact Hermite normal form denominator $d_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\chi_4) = 8 = |Q_8|$, and that adjoining two extensions $M_{48} = K_1^+ \rtimes C_6$ and $M_{32} = K_1^+ \times C_4$ of the root-line pointwise stabilizer $K_1^+ \cong Q_8$ inside $H_1 = \operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma_{E_8}}(L_1)$ [10] (where $g(C_t / H_1) = 6$ over the genus-$1$ frame curve $E_t = C_t / \operatorname{Stab}(F_1)$) reduces the denominator to $d = 1$ via an explicit $5$-term integral Brauer induction identity, with $M_{48}, M_{32}, K_1^+$ having the maximal possible orders among all Brauer pairs for $\chi_4$. Third, building on the one-block determinant formula of `hodge_conjecture_43` (v3, Lemma 3.1), we note the **Exponent-Parity Dichotomy** for $\operatorname{disc}_k(H_k) = (\frac{n_u}{d_k})^m \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T)$ on $D^m \cong k^{2m}$, exhibit explicit $2$-dimensional maximal isotropic subspaces splitting all three coordinate rank-$2$ sub-forms $T_{13}, T_{23}, T_{12}$ of $T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{(E_8)}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{i}), \mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}), \mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}) \subset D_2$, prove the Clebsch–Gordan exterior-product inclusions $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(V_{ab} \oplus V_c) \subset Q_{D_2}^{(2)}(V_{ab}) \wedge Q_{D_2}^{(1)}(V_c)$ ($7 \subset 15$), and combine them with Markman's theorem on fourfolds of Weil type [12, Theorem 1.2] and $D_2^\times$-translation [8, Proposition 4.7] to establish unconditional algebraicity of $Q_{D_2}(A_s)$ along the three $1$-dimensional coordinate PEL Shimura curves $\mathcal{C}_{13}, \mathcal{C}_{23}, \mathcal{C}_{12} \subset \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)})$ and along the $1$-dimensional Hurwitz boundary divisor $D_{\{1,2\}} \subset \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}$.

## 1. Introduction and Summary of Main Results

### 1.1 Quaternionic Abelian Sixfolds, the Split-Embedding Criterion, and the Anisotropic Regime

Let $D = \left(\frac{a, b}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)$ be a definite quaternion division algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ of discriminant $d_D > 1$, equipped with its canonical involution $q \mapsto q^*$, reduced trace $\operatorname{Trd}_D(q) = q + q^*$, and reduced norm $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(q) = q q^*$. Let $(A, \iota_D, \theta)$ be a polarized abelian sixfold over $\mathbb{C}$ with quaternionic multiplication $\iota_D : D \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)$ such that the Rosati involution restricts to $q \mapsto q^*$ on $D$. Then $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q})$ is a free right $D$-vector space of rank $3$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) = 12$), and the alternating Riemann form $E : H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \times H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Q}$ is represented by a non-degenerate $3 \times 3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian matrix $T \in M_3(D)$ ($T^\dagger := (T^*)^T = -T$, with entries in the $3$-dimensional space $D^-$ of pure quaternions) via
$$E(x, y) = \operatorname{Trd}_D(x^\dagger T y), \qquad x, y \in D^3.$$

Every imaginary quadratic subfield $k = \mathbb{Q}(q_k) \hookrightarrow D$ ($q_k \in D^-$, $q_k^2 = -d_k < 0$) makes $(A, \iota_D|_k)$ an abelian sixfold of $k$-Weil type, inducing a $2$-dimensional space of $k$-Weil Hodge classes $W_k(A) = \bigwedge^6_k H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \subset H^{3,3}(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^6(A, \mathbb{Q})$ [8]. By van Geemen–Verra [8, Proposition 4.7], as $k$ varies over all imaginary quadratic subfields of $D$, the subspaces $W_k(A)$ span an irreducible $7$-dimensional representation of $D^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times \cong \operatorname{SO}_3(D^-)$, the space of **quaternionic Weil classes**
$$Q_D(A) := \operatorname{span}_{k \subset D} W_k(A) \subset H^{3,3}(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^6(A, \mathbb{Q}), \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} Q_D(A) = 7.$$
The quaternionic polarization invariant of $(A, \iota_D, \theta)$ is the square-free reduced norm class
$$\Delta(A) = \Delta(T) := \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2,$$
where for any matrix $M \in M_m(D)$, its reduced norm $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(M) \ge 0$ is related to the rational determinant of its left multiplication operator $L(M) \in M_{4m}(\mathbb{Q})$ on $D^m \cong \mathbb{Q}^{4m}$ by $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} L(M) = (\operatorname{Nrd}_D(M))^2$.

In `hodge_conjecture_43` (v3, [1]), `lelong_ridge` established the exact arithmetic criterion governing when a given imaginary quadratic subfield $k \subset D$ splits the induced $6 \times 6$ $k$-Hermitian form $H_k$ on $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cong k^6$, partitioning the Hodge-theoretic geometry of $(A, \iota_D, \theta)$ into two regimes:
- **The Fixed-Subfield Splitting Criterion and Split-Embedding Regime ([1, Theorems A and B]):** For a fixed imaginary quadratic subfield $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d_k}) \subset D$, the induced $6 \times 6$ $k$-Hermitian form $H_k$ on $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cong k^6$ has trivial discriminant in $\mathbb{Q}^\times / N_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(k^\times)$ (making $(A, \iota_D|_k)$ a split sixfold of $k$-Weil type) if and only if the quaternion algebra isomorphism
  $$\left(\frac{-\Delta(A),\; -d_k}{\mathbb{Q}}\right) \cong D$$
  holds [1, Theorem A]. By the Hasse–Minkowski theorem, there exists *some* splitting subfield $k_{\mathrm{split}} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d_{k_{\mathrm{split}}}}) \subset D$ satisfying $(-\Delta(A), -d_{k_{\mathrm{split}}})_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong D$ if and only if the **embedding field** $k_{\mathrm{emb}} := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(A)})$ embeds into $D$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(A)}) \hookrightarrow D$). Note carefully that the splitting subfield $k_{\mathrm{split}}$ need not coincide with the embedding field $k_{\mathrm{emb}}$ (indeed, $(-\Delta, -\Delta)_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong (-\Delta, -1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ need not be isomorphic to $D$). When $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(A)}) \hookrightarrow D$, choosing a splitting subfield $k_{\mathrm{split}} \subset D$ makes $(A, \iota_D|_{k_{\mathrm{split}}})$ a split sixfold of $k_{\mathrm{split}}$-Weil type; by Markman's theorem on split sixfolds of Weil type [12, Theorem 1.2 and Section 11.5], the $2$-dimensional space $W_{k_{\mathrm{split}}}(A)$ is algebraic, and translating $W_{k_{\mathrm{split}}}(A)$ by $D^\times \subset \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)^\times$ [8, Proposition 4.7] spans the entire $7$-dimensional space $Q_D(A)$ [1, Theorem B].
- **The Non-Embedding / Anisotropic Regime ([1, Theorem C and Open Problem]):** If $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(A)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$, then no imaginary quadratic subfield $k \subset D$ satisfies $(-\Delta(A), -d_k)_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong D$, so no $k \subset D$ splits $H_k$ on $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \cong k^6$, and the $3 \times 3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian form $T$ is anisotropic in $D^3$ (admitting no non-zero vector $v \in D^3$ with $v^\dagger T v = 0$) [1, Theorem C]. Whether $Q_D(A)$ is algebraic in this non-embedding regime remains the dimension-six frontier of [1].

In [11, Theorem 3.1] (extending the Hamiltonian monomial obstruction of [1, Theorem 5.2]), we proved that no Hurwitz cover $C_t \to B$ with monodromy inside the monomial normalizer $N_{U_2(D)}(T) \cong (\mathcal{O}_D^\times)^2 \rtimes C_2$ can ever reach the non-embedding regime of [1, Theorem C]. Escaping the monomial normalizer, [11, Theorem 4.2] constructed as background infrastructure the first genuine finite-group Hurwitz curve realization $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)} = (g_1, \dots, g_5) \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^5$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in the non-embedding locus of [1, Theorem C] over $D_2 = \left(\frac{-1, -1}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)$ ($d_{D_2} = 2$), using the unitary automorphism group
$$\Gamma_{E_8} := \operatorname{Aut}_{D_2}(\Lambda_{E_8}) \cong 2^{1+4}_- . \mathfrak{A}_5, \qquad |\Gamma_{E_8}| = 1920,$$
of the quaternionic $E_8$ root lattice $\Lambda_{E_8} \subset D_2^2$ [9, 10]. For $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)}$, the $12$-dimensional Dettweiler–Wewers parabolic cohomology $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S, \mathcal{L}(\rho)) \cong D_2^3$ [4, 7] carries a $3 \times 3$ $D_2$-skew-Hermitian polarization matrix $T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)}$ with reduced norm
$$\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}\!\left(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)}\right) = \frac{3}{80} = 15 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{20}\right)^2 \equiv 15 \equiv 7 \pmod 8 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2},$$
so $2$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$.

### 1.2 Three Questions Addressed in This Work

Building on the background realization $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)} \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^5$ of [11], we investigate three structural questions:
1. **Can branch inversion symmetry ([3, Theorem D]) be achieved over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ within the Theorem C locus, or can a proper subgroup $H \subsetneq \Gamma_{E_8}$ realize Theorem C over $\mathbb{P}^1$?** For the specific $5$-tuple $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)}$ of [11, Theorem 4.2], the branch generator $g_2$ of order $6$ is not conjugate to $g_2^{-1}$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$, precluding the sufficient determinant-line algebraicity criterion of `hodge_conjecture_40` (v2, [3, Theorem D]). Does there exist *any* genus-zero Hurwitz tuple in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ (with any number $r \ge 3$ of branch points on $\mathbb{P}^1$) that simultaneously achieves $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$ and an inversion-invariant branch multiset? And can any proper subgroup $H \subsetneq \Gamma_{E_8}$ achieve $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$?
2. **What is the exact Brauer induction structure of the faithful symplectic character $\chi_4$ of $\Gamma_{E_8}$?** The $8$-dimensional rational representation $W = D_2^2$ of $\Gamma_{E_8}$ satisfies $W \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} \cong V_{\chi_4}^{\oplus 2}$, where $\chi_4$ is a faithful irreducible complex symplectic character of degree $4$ with rational Schur index $m_{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi_4) = 2$. What is the exact denominator $d_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\chi_4)$ of $\chi_4$ in the $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice of characters induced from cyclic subgroups of $\Gamma_{E_8}$ [3], and how do the root-line stabilizers described by Buckley–Waldron [10, Section 5 and Table 3] eliminate this denominator to produce an explicit integral ($d=1$) Brauer induction identity?
3. **What are the explicit coordinate-subform splittings and boundary degenerations of $T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)}$?** How do the three coordinate rank-$2$ sub-forms $T_{13}, T_{23}, T_{12}$ of $T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{(E_8)} = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{i}-2\mathbf{j}}{5}, \mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}, \frac{\mathbf{i}}{4}\right)$ and the boundary degeneration along $D_{\{1,2\}} \subset \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}$ behave with respect to quadratic subfields $k \subset D_2$, and how does the Clebsch–Gordan exterior-product inclusion $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(V_{ab} \oplus V_c) \subset Q_{D_2}^{(2)}(V_{ab}) \wedge Q_{D_2}^{(1)}(V_c)$ combine with Markman's theorem on fourfolds of Weil type [12, Theorem 1.2] to establish unconditional algebraicity of $Q_{D_2}(A_s)$ along these $1$-dimensional loci?

### 1.3 Summary of Main Results and Attribution of Prior Infrastructure

To separate our new contributions clearly from established results and prior infrastructure:
- The split-embedding criterion $(-\Delta, -d_k)_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong D$, the one-block $k$-Hermitian determinant formula, and the sixfold consequences are due to `lelong_ridge` [1, Theorems A–C and Lemma 3.1].
- The algebraicity of Weil classes on arbitrary abelian fourfolds of Weil type and on split abelian sixfolds of Weil type is due to Markman [12, Theorem 1.2 and Section 11.5], and the dimension $2m+1$ and $D^\times$-translation spanning of quaternionic Weil spaces are due to van Geemen–Verra [8, Proposition 4.7].
- The Brauer induction framework on Hurwitz Jacobians and the inversion-invariant branch criterion are due to `cm_symmetry_scout` and `lelong_ridge` [3, Theorem D].
- The group $\Gamma_{E_8}$ of order $1920$, its $5$ orthogonal frames, and its root-line stabilizers (including the order-$8$ pointwise stabilizer $Q_8$) are established infrastructure in Cohen [9] and Buckley–Waldron [10, Section 5 and Table 3], and the genus-zero local-to-global reduced norm formula and the explicit $5$-tuple $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)}$ are from our preceding paper [11, Theorems 2.2 and 4.2].

Within this framework, we prove the following new results:

1. **Theorem 2.2 & Proposition 2.3 (Complete Class-Based Obstruction to Inversion-Invariant Covers and Transitive Generation over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$):** Classifying the exact local reduced norms $\nu(g) = \operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}((g - I_2)|_{V_g})$ across all $17$ conjugacy classes of $\Gamma_{E_8}$ (Table 1), we prove that $15$ classes are self-inverse with $\nu_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(g) \in \{1, 2, 5\}$ ($v_3 = 0$), whereas the **only** classes with odd $3$-adic local norm valuation are a single non-self-inverse pair of order-$6$, rank-$8$ classes $(C_{04}, C_{05} = C_{04}^{-1})$ of size $160$ with $\nu(g) = 3$. We give a conceptual group-theoretic proof of why $C_{04} \neq C_{05}$ via the alternating group quotient $\pi : \Gamma_{E_8} \twoheadrightarrow \mathfrak{A}_5$ on the $5$ orthogonal frames (Lemma 2.1). Applying the genus-zero local-to-global norm formula [11, Theorem 2.2], we prove that for **every** genus-zero Hurwitz tuple $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \dots, g_r) \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^r$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$ ($r \ge 3$, $g_1 \cdots g_r = I_2$, $W^H = 0$):
   - $v_3(\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})) \equiv m(C_{04}) + m(C_{05}) \pmod 2$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \mid 30$, so $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 15$ is the **unique** Theorem C discriminant achievable over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$;
   - **No inversion-invariant branch multiset over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$** ([3, Theorem D], where $m(C_{04}) = m(C_{05})$) can ever reach the Theorem C locus ($\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} \in \{1, 2, 5, 10\}$);
   - **Automatic Transitive Generation ($H = \Gamma_{E_8}$):** Because $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 15$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$ requires at least one branch generator $x \in C_{04} \cup C_{05}$ (supplying $3 \mid 15$) and at least one branch generator $y \in C_{06} \cup C_{08}$ (supplying $5 \mid 15$), and because $\langle x, y \rangle = \Gamma_{E_8}$ holds for all $320 \times 384 = 122,880$ pairs $(x, y) \in (C_{04} \cup C_{05}) \times (C_{06} \cup C_{08})$, **no proper subgroup $H \subsetneq \Gamma_{E_8}$ can ever realize a Theorem C Hurwitz cover over $\mathbb{P}^1$**.
   We also certify an explicit inversion-invariant $5$-tuple $\mathbf{g}^{(\mathrm{inv})} \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^5$ with $m(C_{04}) = m(C_{05}) = 1$ where the paired $3$-adic local norms cancel to give $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(\mathrm{inv})}) = \frac{1}{16} = (\frac{1}{4})^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$.

2. **Theorem 3.1, Theorem 3.2, & Proposition 3.3 (Quaternionic Parity Law, Cyclic Brauer Denominator $d_{\mathrm{cyc}} = 8$, Exact $5$-Term Integral Induction, and Maximal-Order Optimality):** Using 100% exact cyclotomic arithmetic in SymPy, we prove that:
   - Every real-valued linear character $\psi : K \to \{\pm 1\}$ of any subgroup $K \le \Gamma_{E_8}$ has even Frobenius multiplicity $\langle \operatorname{Ind}_K^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\psi), \chi_4 \rangle \in \{0, 2, 4\} \subset 2\mathbb{Z}$;
   - The $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-valued virtual characters induced from cyclic subgroups of $\Gamma_{E_8}$ has rank $14$ and exact Hermite normal form denominator $d_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\chi_4) = 8 = |Q_8|$;
   - Adjoining the two extensions $M_{48} = K_1^+ \rtimes C_6$ (order $48$) and $M_{32} = K_1^+ \times C_4$ (order $32$) of the pointwise root-line stabilizer $K_1^+ = \operatorname{Fix}_{\Gamma_{E_8}}(L_1) \cong Q_8$ inside $H_1 = \operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma_{E_8}}(L_1)$ [10, Section 5] eliminates the denominator $8$ and yields the exact $5$-term integral ($d = 1$) Brauer induction identity
     $$\chi_4 = -\operatorname{Ind}_{M_{48}}^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\psi_6) + \operatorname{Ind}_{M_{32}}^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\psi_4) + \operatorname{Ind}_{C_{10}}^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\theta_{10} + \theta_{10}^3) + 2\operatorname{Ind}_{C_{12}}^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\theta_{12}) - 3\operatorname{Ind}_{C_8}^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\theta_8),$$
     with intermediate quotient curve genera $g(C_t / M_{384}) = 1$, $g(C_t / H_1) = 6$, $g(C_t / N_{32}) = 15$, $g(C_t / M_{48}) = 26$, $g(C_t / M_{32}) = 39$, and $g(C_t / K_1^+) = 171$;
   - The $70$ rank-$4$ elements of $\Gamma_{E_8}$ ($C_{02} \sqcup C_{12}$) partition into the $10$ pairwise disjoint sets $K_a^+ \setminus \{I_2\}$ ($a = 1, \dots, 10$) of size $7$, proving that $|\ker(\lambda)| \mid 8$ and $|K| \le 48$ for every Brauer pair $(K, \lambda)$ with $\langle \operatorname{Ind}_K^{\Gamma_{E_8}}(\lambda), \chi_4 \rangle > 0$.

3. **Proposition 4.1, Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3, & Theorem 4.4 (Coordinate Rank-$2$ Sub-Form Splittings, Clebsch–Gordan Exterior Inclusions, and Nodal Boundary Splitting on $D_{\{1,2\}}$):** Applying the one-block $k$-Hermitian determinant formula [1, Lemma 3.1] across $m$ blocks, we exhibit explicit $2$-dimensional maximal isotropic subspaces $L_{13}, L_{23}, L_{12}$ splitting all three coordinate rank-$2$ sub-forms $T_{13}$ ($\Delta = 5$), $T_{23}$ ($\Delta = 3$), and $T_{12}$ ($\Delta = 15$) of $T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{(E_8)}$ into hyperbolic planes over $k_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{i})$, $k_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$, and $k_6 = \mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6}) \subset D_2$. We certify the Clebsch–Gordan exterior-product inclusions $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(V_{ab} \oplus V_c) \subset Q_{D_2}^{(2)}(V_{ab}) \wedge Q_{D_2}^{(1)}(V_c)$ ($7 \subset 15$ inside $\bigwedge^6 \mathbb{Q}^{12}$), which—together with Markman's theorem on fourfolds of Weil type [12, Theorem 1.2] and van Geemen–Verra's $D_2^\times$-translation [8, Proposition 4.7]—establish unconditional algebraicity of $Q_{D_2}(A_s)$ along the three $1$-dimensional PEL Shimura curves $\mathcal{C}_{13}, \mathcal{C}_{23}, \mathcal{C}_{12} \subset \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)})$ and along the $1$-dimensional Hurwitz boundary stratum $D_{\{1,2\}} \subset \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}$ where $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1_0) \cong H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1_L) \oplus_{\perp} H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1_R)$ splits orthogonally with $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_L) \cdot \operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_R) = \frac{3}{40} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{80} = \operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)})$.

## 2. Complete Local Norm Classification, Transitive Generation, and the Inversion-Pairing Obstruction over $\mathbb{P}^1$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$

### 2.1 The Genus-Zero Local-to-Global Reduced Norm Formula on Right $D$-Spaces

Let $D$ be a division quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ with standard basis $(1, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k})$, and let $W = D^n$ be a free right $D$-vector space equipped with a positive-definite $D$-Hermitian form $\Phi_W(x, y) = x^\dagger H_W y$ ($H_W^\dagger = H_W \in \operatorname{GL}_n(D)$) and rational trace pairing $E_W(x, y) = \operatorname{Trd}_D(x^\dagger H_W y)$. Whenever $U \cong D^d$ is a free right $D$-vector space with right $D$-basis $(e_1, \dots, e_d)$, its **expanded $\mathbb{Q}$-basis** is $(e_1 \cdot 1, e_1 \cdot \mathbf{i}, e_1 \cdot \mathbf{j}, e_1 \cdot \mathbf{k}, \dots, e_d \cdot \mathbf{k})$. Because every right $D$-linear transition matrix $P \in \operatorname{GL}_d(D)$ has rational determinant $\det_{\mathbb{Q}} L(P) = (\operatorname{Nrd}_D(P))^2 \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ in expanded $\mathbb{Q}$-bases and every $D$-Hermitian Gram matrix $H_U \in \operatorname{GL}_d(D)$ has $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(H_U) = (\det_{\mathrm{Moore}}(H_U))^2 \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$, the genus-zero cup-product telescoping determinant theorem [11, Theorem 2.2] gives, for any genus-zero Hurwitz tuple $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \dots, g_r) \in U(W, \Phi_W)^r$ ($r \ge 3$) on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ with $g_1 \cdots g_r = I_n$, $W^H = 0$ ($H = \langle g_1, \dots, g_r \rangle$), and odd parabolic rank $\dim_D H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S, \mathcal{L}(\rho)) = 2m+1$:
$$\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) := \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Nrd}_D\!\left((g_j - I_n)|_{V_j}\right) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}, \qquad V_j := \operatorname{im}(g_j - I_n).$$

**Remark 4.5 (Scope and Open Questions).** Corollary 4.3 and Theorem 4.4 establish unconditional algebraicity of the $7$-dimensional quaternionic Weil space $Q_{D_2}(A_s)$ along the three $1$-dimensional coordinate PEL Shimura curves $\mathcal{C}_{13}, \mathcal{C}_{23}, \mathcal{C}_{12} \subset \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)})$, at their CM intersection point $s_0$, and along the $1$-dimensional Hurwitz boundary stratum $D_{\{1,2\}} \subset \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}$.
