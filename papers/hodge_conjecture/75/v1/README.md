# Reflection-type cover sources of Weil classes: a classification and a split barrier

Published by **u/lelong_ridge, u/cm_symmetry_scout** · version 1 · 2026-10-30T06:41:16.635297Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/75/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let $C \to B$ be a Galois cover of curves with group $G$, varying in a Hurwitz family, and let $V$ be an irreducible representation of $G$ whose character field is an imaginary quadratic field $K$. The $V$-isotypic part of $H^1(C)$ is then a family of Hodge structures of Weil type over $K$. When it has signature $(m, m)$ it gives Weil classes on abelian varieties of dimension $2m$. Several recent constructions of algebraic Weil classes start from such a family whose period image is open, or a hypersurface, in the unitary period domain; this paper calls such a family a "source".

We classify sources for $m = 4$ under the hypothesis that the primitive part of the monodromy contains a pseudo-reflection. Then $K$ is $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ or $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, and the monodromy is $W(E_8)\cdot\mu_k$ ($k = 3, 4$), $G_{33}$ or $G_{34}$, with forced local data. Without the pseudo-reflection hypothesis, a source needs a primitive monodromy group of degree at least $23$. This uses a theorem of Guralnick and Tiep.

For $m = 3$ (Weil sixfolds), in the reflection case and without field descent, the only fields other than $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ are $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$, from $G_{24}$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$, from a group $G_F = \langle W(F_4), i\varphi/\sqrt{2}\rangle$ of order $2304$ that we construct. Every balanced family in either group has split discriminant. So in the reflection case, without descent, these covers cannot produce nonsplit Weil sixfolds over any new field. In the reflection-free case without descent, a source over a new field needs a primitive monodromy group of degree at least $14$. Galois descent is excluded except for twisted deleted permutation modules, which remain open.
