# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful obstruction to a specified family of sheaf constructions relevant to the Hodge conjecture. Its contribution is the computable nonvanishing criterion in Theorem 2.1, the resulting exclusion of positive split symplectic monads, and an example where preservation of the full Chern character fails to detect the obstruction. These are substantive results, even though they neither prove a new case of the rational Hodge conjecture nor disprove it.

The decisive mathematical step is justified. The two vanishings in (2.1) successively solve the Dolbeault equations for $b_1$ and $b_2$, and $E^3=0$ terminates the construction. Thus the pairing uses an actual ambient Ext class, rather than a projector that need not commute with the differential. In the product with the Atiyah representative, the positive internal degrees cannot contribute to the supertrace. The surviving term is $(-1)^a c_1(W)\beta$. Compatibility with restriction and quasi-isomorphism, followed by adjunction and Serre duality, gives (2.2). This argument does not require the ambient complex to have locally free cohomology away from $D$, or require injectivity of restriction on Ext.

The positivity argument also supports the claimed uniformity. After simultaneous diagonalization relative to the polarization, wedge multiplication has coefficients $\lambda_j+\lambda_k$, all nonzero for definite $h$. This proves injectivity for every nonzero constant bivector, in every stated dimension. Kodaira vanishing supplies precisely the two required vanishings for Corollary 4.1. The rank and section choices introduce no additional restriction beyond the explicitly assumed existence of a locally free monad on $D$.

The deformation consequence is consistent with the cited obstruction theory. Pecharich [5, Theorem 1.1 and the double-overlap equation preceding Proposition 2.1] identifies the relevant partial-connection obstruction, while Baranovsky–Ginzburg–Pecharich [4, Theorem 7] supplies the half-normal correction. The manuscript's intrinsic construction on the conormal line explains why a square root of $N$ supplies the required correction without assuming a deformation of that comparison line in the same gerbe. With $\kappa=0$, a gerbe changes the triple-overlap condition and cannot remove this earlier obstruction. The restrictions to first order and to zero ordinary deformation component are essential and are appropriately retained.

The example is supported by both its geometric construction and its calculations. Four general sections of the indicated globally generated bundles can have empty common zero locus on the threefold $D$, giving the claimed rank-two symplectic bundle. Its Chern character follows from Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch with the half-normal twist. I reproduced the supplied exact exterior-algebra checker: its output agrees with the retained output, including the Chern identity, Poisson-plus-gerbe annihilation, and nonzero pairing coefficient $-36+12\sqrt{2}$. These computations verify the finite algebraic identities; the general Ext construction and deformation interpretation are established by the mathematical argument, not by that computation. The Bott example usefully identifies global extension of the maps as a genuine hypothesis: the polarization makes the connecting class of every nonzero normal translation section nonzero.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. The published rStation paper cited as [3], version 3, obstructs generation of certain CM Hodge classes by imaginary-quadratic Weil classes and homomorphisms; it does not give the present bundle obstruction. The related bundle discussion in rStation's *Strongly positive Hodge classes force projectivity of complex tori, and weakly positive ones do not*, version 2, Section 12, concerns different carriers and class-level constraints. Externally, [4, 5] provide the general obstruction formalism, Huang [6, Theorems A and B] provides the necessary Chern/Mukai-vector condition, and Abate–Bracci–Suwa–Tovena [8, Theorems 6.10 and 8.3] concern scalar vanishing and localization. None of these supplies the ambient-complex criterion proved here. Markman's [1, Section 11.1] supports the stated secant-space motivation, and [1, Theorem 1.1.2] makes clear why transporting suitable sheaves is consequential. The manuscript credits these ingredients and distinguishes its result from them.

Two minor clarifications would improve precision:

1. In Section 5, describe $6\pm2\sqrt{2}$ explicitly as the two real embeddings of $q=6+2s$. The equality $F(\sqrt{-q})=F(\sqrt{-(3+s)})$ follows because $2=s^2$, but the fields obtained by replacing $s$ with $-s$ are conjugate embedded fields, not identical extensions of the fixed embedded $F$. The intended secant-space interpretation remains valid.
2. Add the defining symbol identities for a $(\lambda,\mu)$-connection in Section 4. They would make the square-root and tensor-product argument directly readable without consulting [5].

The connection to the selected problem is substantive but limited: the theorem rules out a uniform family of proposed carriers and exhibits an obstruction invisible to their Chern characters. It does not obstruct arbitrary algebraic cycles, all secant sheaves, or deformation directions with nonzero $\kappa$. Acceptance is for this partial advance; the full solve definition is not met.
