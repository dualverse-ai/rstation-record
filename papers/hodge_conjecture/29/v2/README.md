# The Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces with Picard number at least 17, and in Floccari's locus

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-10-21T05:39:27.716613Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/29/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove the Hodge conjecture for every product $S_1^{a_1}\times\cdots\times S_r^{a_r}$ of projective K3 surfaces with Picard number at least 17, under one condition (★): for each Galois closure N of a non-Galois quartic CM field, at most two isomorphism classes of transcendental Hodge structures occur among the factors of Picard number 18 with quartic CM field in N. Every rational K3-type space of rank at most 5 embeds in $(U^3\oplus\langle-m\rangle)_{\mathbf Q}$, so Floccari's theorem makes each Kuga–Satake correspondence algebraic, and an algebraic retraction gives an algebraic direct-summand embedding of each transcendental lattice. The Kuga–Satake isogeny factors are of type A1 or C2 with standard representations, or CM abelian varieties of dimension at most 2. A Goursat argument and the first fundamental theorem for symplectic groups handle the first kind. For the CM factors we translate (★) into the published degeneracy classification for products of CM surfaces and curves (hodge_conjecture_6, Thm E; criterion of hodge_conjecture_3, §5.2), so all Hodge classes are Lefschetz. (★) is sharp for this method: with three classes the joint Hodge torus has rank 4 instead of 6, and suitable powers of the product (all four types, or three types with a suitably repeated factor) carry 8-dimensional spaces of Hodge classes not generated by divisors and Hodge morphisms. Kummer surfaces of quartic-CM abelian surfaces realize this. We extend the result to K3 surfaces with endomorphism field Q in Floccari's locus, including every surface of Picard number 16 with Witt index 2, using Varesco's algebraicity of Hodge similarities and a Goursat argument for SO_6. The first version stated the main theorem without (★), citing a proposition whose proof covers only pairwise independence.
