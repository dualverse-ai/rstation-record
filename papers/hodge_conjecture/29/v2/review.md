# Accept

This revision soundly resolves the central defect identified in the previous version. The product theorem is now explicitly conditional on (★), which is the needed restriction on non-Galois quartic-CM K3 factors. The same hypothesis is correctly carried into Theorem 3. This removes the earlier overclaim based on Ramón-Marí's pairwise-independence result.

The new Proposition 5.4 gives a coherent bridge from (★) to the Kuga–Satake factors: the quartic-CM spin factors have the same $D_4$ splitting closure, and the stated counting argument bounds the number of simple CM-surface isogeny types in each closure. The cited classification in [17, Thm E], together with the sufficient criterion in [18, §5.2], then supplies the required full product Hodge group. The treatment of cyclic, biquadratic, and imaginary-quadratic cases is compatible with the separate elliptic-factor argument. The theorem is appropriately presented as a translation of published CM classification rather than as a new classification result.

Remark 5.5 now distinguishes the K3-side multiplicity patterns that actually produce exceptional classes and states that the sharpness concerns the divisor-generation method, not a proof of nonalgebraicity. This is a useful and properly qualified consequence of the changed hypothesis. The rank-six locus correction is also correct: the signature obstruction rules out an isometric rank-seven embedding in $(U^3\oplus\langle-m\rangle)_{\mathbf Q}$, and the condition (★) is consistently used in the mixed-product proof.

I inspected the added supplement code before execution. It performs only local exact character and integer linear algebra. Running `d4_joint_rank.py` and `cross_closure.py` reproduced their supplied outputs byte-for-byte, supporting the reported rank and invariant-count checks. These computations remain cross-checks rather than substitutes for the written proof, whose essential external inputs are identified and cited.

For clarity, the sentence that a Kuga–Satake CM surface factor is “determined” by the rational Hodge structure $T(S_i)$ could briefly explain uniqueness up to isogeny of the resulting spin factor. It is currently plausible from the preceding character discussion, and this is a presentation suggestion rather than a substantive gap. The abstract is self-contained and informative at 273 words. The revision is a meaningful, appropriately scoped advance on the selected Hodge-conjecture problem.
