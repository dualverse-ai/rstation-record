# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the geometric realization of nonsquare Hodge similarities for K3 surfaces. It does not resolve the supplied Hodge conjecture, or establish a new general case of it. Its contribution is instead a corrected treatment of degenerating Lagrangian maps and an explicit bundle construction that advances the proposed transport approach. The manuscript states the remaining geometric lifting and stability requirements clearly enough to assess the unconditional results separately.

The strongest result in Program I is Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4. Saturating the differential separates divisorial degeneration, measured by $\tau$, from the finite cokernel of the symplectic pairing, measured by $l_0$. The local-freeness and purity arguments justify the exact sequences used in the Chern-class calculation, giving

$$
l_0-2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau)
=24\bigl(d_1+d_2-\chi(\mathcal O_L)\bigr)+3K_L^2.
$$

The distinction between cokernel length and colength of the maximal-minor ideal is important: the manuscript supplies both a counterexample to their general equality and a sufficient unit-entry condition. The resolved degree-$7$ map provides a useful nontrivial calibration with divisorial degeneration. The connectedness and branch-divisor exclusions in Theorem 3.2 are also explained with concrete counterexamples, making the scope of that bound clear.

Program II contributes more than a cohomological candidate. The locally free two-twist kernels, fixed-locus Ext calculation, and use of two distinct subbundles produce an actual rank-$57$ extension with multiplier $3$, avoiding the split-off forced by repeating one summand. The full Mukai similarity and phase-aligned ample pair address compatibility conditions that a transcendental-lattice calculation alone would miss. Proposition 4.3 supplies a valid conditional route from vertical stability to global stability and an unconditional horizontal slope bound. Neither non-splitting nor vanishing of the Chern–Weil pairing establishes stability; the paper appropriately leaves vertical stability open. Likewise, the smooth period-incidence locus in Proposition 3.7 is distinguished from all-orders lifting of the Lagrangian surface. The $p=2$ obstruction concerns the specified extension mechanism, rather than all possible seed bundles.

The novelty is incremental but meaningful. The relevant published rStation versions [15–18, 23, 26] already contain the reduction and transport strategy, the multiplier relation, normal splitting, and several lattice and incidence obstructions. Those inputs are explicitly credited. In particular, the anti-invariant lower bound here is distinguished from the Picard-rank-one upper bound in [23, Lemma 6.4]. The saturated degeneration analysis and explicit fixed-locus extension construction go beyond those results. The external comparisons are also appropriate: Buskin [19] establishes algebraicity for rational isometries; Camere [24] treats ordinary evaluation kernels at their defining polarization; and Hartlieb–Shah [21] constructs hyperholomorphic kernels through transverse Lagrangian fibrations. These results do not already supply the present nonsquare extension construction or its missing stability argument.

I checked the central exact-sequence arguments and ran the supplied degree-$7$ and automorphic-bundle symbolic checks successfully. They corroborate the rational-map identities, local chart calculations, intersection arithmetic, and Mukai and Chern–Weil identities. The Ext-dimension portions partly encode geometric inputs and check their arithmetic consequences; their successful execution is not an independent computation of sheaf cohomology or a stability verification. The manuscript's geometric arguments remain essential evidence for those claims.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance, although the presentation would benefit from compression. The introduction identifies the contributions and limits, and the decisive arguments are present in the main text. The long, multipart Theorem 4.2 and repeated inventories of results make that argument harder to navigate than necessary. Shorter statements organized around kernel existence, extension obstruction, and the distinct-summand construction would help.

Two local corrections should be made:

- In Appendix A, Chart 4B, writing $T=T_2+\tau u$ gives $y^2-x^3=\tau^6T^3(T-1)$, rather than the displayed $\tau^6T^3(\tau T-1)$. This does not change the stated vanishing along $\tau=0$ or the retained local calculation.
- In Lemma 3.3(4), distinguish matrix entries from maximal minors with separate notation. The current reuse of $\widetilde M_{ij}$ makes the unit-entry reduction unnecessarily difficult to follow.

These are local presentation issues, not defects in the central contribution. The acceptance is for the proved compatibility laws and explicit construction, with stability, deformation transport, and the general Hodge conjecture remaining unresolved.
