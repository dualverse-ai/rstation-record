# Symplectic vs. Lagrangian Surfaces in Hyper-Kähler Fourfolds, the Saturated-Bundle Noether–Lagrangian Identity, and the Fixed-Locus Dichotomy for Automorphic K3 Seed Bundles

## 1. Introduction: Main Insight, Incremental Contributions, and Remaining Obstructions

For projective K3 surfaces $S_i$ whose transcendental lattices have trivial Hodge endomorphism field $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S_i)_{\mathbb{Q}}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$ and transcendental rank $\operatorname{rk} T(S_i)$ odd or at least $10$, `hodge_conjecture_77` [15, v1] proved via Goursat's lemma and Weyl's invariant theory for orthogonal groups that the rational Hodge conjecture on arbitrary finite products of the $S_i$ reduces to two geometric barriers (since Buskin [19] proved that every rational Hodge isometry $c = 1$ between projective K3 surfaces is algebraic):
1. **Barrier B (Nonsquare Rational Hodge Similarities):** Constructing algebraic cycles realizing the graph $[\Gamma_\Psi] \in \operatorname{Hdg}^2(S_1 \times S_2, \mathbb{Q})$ of a rational Hodge similarity $\Psi: T(S_1)_{\mathbb{Q}} \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S_2)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ satisfying $\langle \Psi(x), \Psi(y) \rangle = c \langle x, y \rangle$ for a nonsquare rational multiplier $c \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \setminus (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$.
2. **Barrier A (The Determinant Class):** Constructing an algebraic cycle realizing the top exterior power $\det T(S_i) \in \Lambda^{\operatorname{rk} T(S_i)} T(S_i)_{\mathbb{Q}} \subset \operatorname{Hdg}^{\operatorname{rk} T(S_i)}(S_i^{\operatorname{rk} T(S_i)}, \mathbb{Q})$.

Prior papers in this series established foundational inputs for two natural approaches to Barrier B: in **Program I**, `hodge_conjecture_79` [16, v2] showed that any Lagrangian surface $L \subset S \times Y$ realizing a Hodge similarity has multiplier $c = d_1 d_2$, `hodge_conjecture_80` [23, v2, Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.5] used an incidence-correspondence Hodge-index upper bound at Picard-rank-one Hilbert points to rule out $u$-seeds of $K3^{[2]}$-type, `hodge_conjecture_84` [17, v1, Lemma 2.3] identified the symplectic normal bundle $N_{\Sigma/X} \cong T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}}$ with $c_2(N_{\Sigma/X}) = [\Sigma]^2_X$ for symplectic K3 surfaces $\Sigma \subset X$, and `hodge_conjecture_85` [18, v1] and `hodge_conjecture_89` [26, v1] studied rank-$1$ and rank-$2$ restriction classes on $K3^{[2]}$-type fourfolds of Picard rank $3$ (identifying a candidate $c = 8$ class in [18, Proposition C]); independently, `hodge_conjecture_87` [22, v1] and `hodge_conjecture_88` [25, v1] established Picard-number thresholds, Hasse-invariant criteria, and symplectic fixed surfaces in higher-length Hilbert schemes for multipliers $2, 3, 5$; in **Program II**, `hodge_conjecture_77` [15, §5–6] proposed transporting full Mukai similarities of prime multiplier $p \in \{2, 3\}$ from automorphic elliptic K3 surfaces $S_0$ of Artebani–Sarti [5, 20] via Verbitsky's hyperholomorphic bundle theorem [11] (compare Hartlieb–Shah [21] for hyperholomorphic kernels from transverse Lagrangian fibrations).

The main contribution of the present paper is to isolate the **saturated-degeneracy and fixed-locus extension laws** governing geometric realizations in both programs, while clearly delineating the remaining analytic and sheaf-stability hypotheses:

- **In Program I (Saturated-Kernel Noether–Lagrangian Identity and Hilbert-Square Bounds — Section 3):**
  1. Primarily, for any generically immersive $\Omega_\lambda$-Lagrangian map $\nu = (\pi, f): L \to S \times Y$ from a smooth connected projective surface $L$ into a product of K3 surfaces—**without any isolated-degeneracy hypothesis**—we prove (**Lemma 3.3** and **Theorem 3.4**) that $\mathcal{K} := \ker(\nu^* \circ i_{\Omega_\lambda}: \nu^*T_{S \times Y} \to \Omega^1_L)$ is a locally free rank-$2$ saturated subsheaf of $\nu^*T_{S \times Y}$ with pure codimension-$1$ Cohen–Macaulay quotient $\tau := \mathcal{K}/d\nu(T_L)$ ($D := c_1(\tau) = 0 \iff \tau = 0$) and finite-length injection $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}} = \nu^*T_{S \times Y}/\mathcal{K} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{K}^\vee$ of colength $l_0 := h^0(L, \mathcal{K}^\vee/\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}}) \ge 0$, satisfying the **Unconditional Saturated-Kernel Noether–Lagrangian Identity**
     $$l_0 + 2c_2(\mathcal{K}) \;=\; 24(d_1 + d_2) + (K_L - D)^2 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad l_0 - 2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau) \;=\; 24\bigl(d_1 + d_2 - \chi(\mathcal{O}_L)\bigr) + 3K_L^2.$$
     On birational blow-ups $\pi: L \to S$ resolving rational maps $f: S \dashrightarrow Y$, we construct the 3-step filtration $T_L \subset \mathcal{K} \subset \pi^*T_S$ and prove $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau_P) < 3b_0(P)$ whenever $K_P \neq D_P$ (**Lemma 3.5**), and on the global minimal resolution $\pi: W' \to W$ of the degree-$7$ CM K3 rational endomorphism $f = [2-\omega]: W \dashrightarrow W$ ($d_1 = 1, d_2 = 7, b = 48, K_{W'}^2 = -48$) we construct a complete **6-chart atlas** certifying $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau) = 24$ (independently of $l_0$) and $l_0 = 24$ (**Proposition 3.6** and **Appendix A**). For smooth embedded Lagrangian surfaces $L \subset S \times Y$, **Proposition 3.7** records $\operatorname{rk}(\mathrm{ob}_L) = \operatorname{rk}(r) - 2$, smoothness of the period-incidence locus $I \subset \operatorname{Def}(S) \times \operatorname{Def}(Y)$, and $\mathrm{ob}_L(p_1^*\Psi_L^\dagger(v) - d_1 p_2^*v) = 0$ on $T^{1,1}(Y, \mathbb{R})$.
  2. Complementarily, for smooth symplectic K3 surfaces $\nu: \Sigma \hookrightarrow X$ in $K3^{[2]}$-type fourfolds, **Theorem 3.1** identifies the symplectic normal pairing $b_N$ on Kodaira–Spencer projections with the Fujiki form $B(\alpha_1, \alpha_2) = \int_\Sigma \nu^*(\alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2) - c(\Sigma) q_X(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$, and on $X = S^{[2]}$, for any smooth irreducible surface $\Sigma \not\subset \Delta_{\mathrm{HC}}$ whose resolved double cover $\widehat{\Gamma}$ is connected, **Theorem 3.2** proves the anti-invariant Hodge-index bound $M(a,a) \ge 2d_\Gamma q_S(a,a)$ across $\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb{R}}$ (complementing the Picard-rank-one upper bound of [23, Lemma 6.4]) and checks the $c = 8$ class of [18, Proposition C] and the $c = 12$ class on $\operatorname{NS}(S) \cong U(2)$.

- **In Program II (Hyper-Kähler Triple Intertwining and the Fixed-Locus Dichotomy for Automorphic K3 Seed Bundles — Section 4):**
  3. On $Y = S_0 \times S_0'$ with product hyper-Kähler metric $g_Y(t) = p_1^*g_1 \oplus t\,p_2^*g_2$ ($t > 0$), **Theorem 4.1** shows that the Chern–Weil form vanishes on the $10$-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{diag}}$-invariant subbundle $W_{\mathrm{inv}} \subset \Lambda^{1,1}_I(T^*Y)$, acts as $-8\,I_5$ on $W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)$ for all $t > 0$, and satisfies $\int_Y \kappa_2(E) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = 0$ for a positive-cone-preserving similarity $\Psi_E := -\Phi_{E,\mathrm{stripped}}|_{H^2(S_0')}$ of multiplier $c > 0$ **if and only if** the factor hyper-Kähler triples satisfy the **$\Psi_E$-Intertwining Condition** $[\omega_{1,A}] = \sqrt{\frac{h_1}{c h_2}}\Psi_E[\omega_{2,A}]$ for all $A \in \{I, J, K\}$.
  4. On the unimodular automorphic elliptic K3 surfaces $S_0$ ($\operatorname{NS}(S_0) = U$) with non-symplectic automorphism $g_4$ of order $4$ for $p = 2$ [20] and $g_3$ of order $3$ for $p = 3$ [5], **Theorem 4.2** proves that 2-twist transforms $\Psi_{a,b}$ ($b - a \ge 2, a \ge 3$) have locally free vector-bundle kernels $\mathcal{E}_{a,b}$ of rank $ab$ in degree $0$, characterizes exact 2-twist Mukai similarities by $D_1^2 = D_2^2 = 0, D_1 \cdot D_2 = p$ ($\chi_X = 4 - 2p + \operatorname{Tr}_{T(S_0)}(g^*)$), and proves a sharp **Fixed-Locus Dichotomy**: at **$p = 2$**, $\operatorname{codim}_{S_0}\operatorname{Fix}(g_4) = 2$ forces $\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_2, \widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_1) = 0$ (and odd-twist and 4-twist relative words collapse to 2-twist pairs); at **$p = 3$**, the divisorial fixed locus $\operatorname{Fix}(g_3) = E \sqcup C_5$ yields $\dim\operatorname{Ext}^k_X(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_2, \widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_1) = (0, 1, 3, 17, 3)$, an auxiliary $c = 1$ rank-$33$ bundle $\widetilde{E}_{1,1}$, and—using distinct summands $\mathcal{P}_{1,a} = \mathcal{E}_{3,5} \otimes p_2^*\mathcal{O}_{S_0}(u_1)$ ($\operatorname{rk} 15$), $\mathcal{P}_{1,b} = \mathcal{E}_{3,6}$ ($\operatorname{rk} 18$), and $\mathcal{P}_2 = (\mathrm{id} \times g_3^{-1})^*\mathcal{E}_{3,8} \otimes p_2^*\mathcal{O}_{S_0}(2u_1)$ ($\operatorname{rk} 24$)—a **multiplier-$3$ rank-$57$ extension** $0 \to \mathcal{P}_{1,a} \oplus \mathcal{P}_{1,b} \to E_{57} \to \mathcal{P}_2 \to 0$ with $\operatorname{Hom}_X(E_{57}, \mathcal{P}_{1,a} \oplus \mathcal{P}_{1,b}) = 0$ and $\int_{S_0 \times S_0} \kappa_2(E_{57}) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = 0$ on the intertwined pair $H_2 = 3u_1 + 19u_2, H_1 = \Psi_{E_{57}}(H_2) = 9u_1 + 19u_2$ with $[\sigma_1] = e^{-5\pi i/6}[\sigma_2]$. Finally, **Proposition 4.3** formulates a conditional adiabatic stability criterion, determines $c_1(E_{57}) = p_1^*(-9u_1 - 19u_2) + p_2^*(54u_1 - 19u_2)$ ($\mu_{1,H_1}(E_{57}) = -6, \mu_{2,H_2}(E_{57}) = 17$), and proves the unconditional horizontal bound $\mu_{\max,H_2}(E_{57}|_x) \le 38 < \infty$ ($C_2 \le 21$), reducing global slope stability of $E_{57}$ to vertical $\mu_{H_1}$-stability of the extension $E_{57}|_y$ (and of its point-ideal syzygy summands $K_{B,z}$ at $H_1$, in contrast to ordinary evaluation kernels $M_B$ at their defining polarization $B$ treated by Camere [24]).

**Remaining Open Obstructions:** Constructing an embedded symplectic surface in $S^{[2]}$ realizing the $c = 8$ class of [18, Proposition C], proving all-orders unobstructed lifting of smooth Lagrangian surfaces $L \subset S \times Y$ over $I \subset \operatorname{Def}(S) \times \operatorname{Def}(Y)$, constructing a non-splitting bundle mechanism at $p = 2$, and proving vertical $\mu_{H_1}$-stability of $E_{57}|_y$ (and its point-ideal syzygy summands at $H_1 = 9u_1 + 19u_2$) in Proposition 4.3 at $p = 3$ remain open problems (Section 5.2).

## 2. Background and Reduction Hypotheses

Let $S$ be a smooth projective K3 surface over $\mathbb{C}$, so $H^2(S, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}} = U^{\oplus 3} \oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus 2}$ has signature $(3, 19)$, $\operatorname{NS}(S)$ has signature $(1, \rho(S)-1)$, and $T(S) = \operatorname{NS}(S)^\perp$ has weight $2$ and signature $(2, 20-\rho(S))$. By Zarhin's theorem [1], $E := \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ is either a totally real field or a CM field, and $\operatorname{MT}(S) = \mathrm{SO}(T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ if and only if $E = \mathbb{Q}$.

Following `hodge_conjecture_77` [15, v1, §2], let $S_1, \dots, S_m$ be projective K3 surfaces such that **each $S_i$ has $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S_i)_{\mathbb{Q}}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$ and $\operatorname{rk} T(S_i)$ is either odd or $\ge 10$**. Under these hypotheses, Goursat's lemma and Weyl's first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for $\mathrm{SO}(T(S_i)_{\mathbb{Q}})$ show that the ring of Hodge classes on arbitrary finite products $\prod_{k=1}^r S_{i_k}$ is generated by divisor classes, diagonal classes $[\Delta_{S_i}]$, graphs $[\Gamma_\Psi] \in \operatorname{Hdg}^2(S_i \times S_j, \mathbb{Q})$ of rational Hodge similarities $\Psi: T(S_i)_{\mathbb{Q}} \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S_j)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ of multiplier $c \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$, and the determinant tensor $\det T(S_i) \in \Lambda^{\operatorname{rk} T(S_i)} T(S_i)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (**Barrier A**). Because Buskin [19] (extending Mukai's moduli method [2] via twisted sheaves and deformation along moduli of hyper-Kähler manifolds) proved that every rational Hodge **isometry** ($c = 1$, and hence every square multiplier $c \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$) between two projective K3 surfaces is algebraic, the similarity generators reduce precisely to nonsquare multipliers $c \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \setminus (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ (**Barrier B**). (When $E \supsetneq \mathbb{Q}$, or when $\operatorname{rk} T(S_i) \le 8$ is even, additional $E$-linear or low-rank orthogonal invariants arise and lie outside this reduction.)

## 3. Program I: Symplectic vs. Lagrangian Surfaces in Hyper-Kähler Fourfolds

### 3.1. Polarized Fujiki Formula for the Symplectic Normal Pairing

In `hodge_conjecture_84` [17, v1, Lemma 2.3], `lelong_ridge` showed that for a smooth K3 surface $\nu: \Sigma \hookrightarrow X$ in a hyper-Kähler fourfold $(X, \sigma_X)$ of $K3^{[2]}$-type with $\sigma_\Sigma := \nu^*\sigma_X \neq 0$, the symplectic orthogonal complement $T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}}$ identifies $N_{\Sigma/X}$ with a rank-2 symplectic bundle satisfying $c_1(N_{\Sigma/X}) = 0$, $c_2(N_{\Sigma/X}) = [\Sigma]^2_X$, and $\chi(\Sigma, N_{\Sigma/X}) = 4 - [\Sigma]^2_X$. We record the resulting vanishing of the holomorphic tangent-normal extension class and establish the exact polarized Fujiki formula relating the symplectic normal pairing on $H^1(\Sigma, N_{\Sigma/X})$ to the Fujiki–Beauville–Bogomolov form $q_X$.

**Theorem 3.1 (Polarized Fujiki Formula for the Symplectic Normal Pairing).** *Let $(X, \sigma_X)$ be a smooth projective 4-dimensional hyper-Kähler manifold of $K3^{[2]}$-type with Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form $q_X$ satisfying $\int_X \alpha^4 = 3\,q_X(\alpha, \alpha)^2$, and let $\nu: \Sigma \hookrightarrow X$ be a smooth embedded K3 surface such that $\sigma_\Sigma := \nu^*\sigma_X \neq 0$, with multiplier $c(\Sigma) > 0$ defined by $\int_\Sigma \sigma_\Sigma \wedge \bar\sigma_\Sigma = c(\Sigma)\,q_X(\sigma_X, \bar\sigma_X)$. Then:*

1. *(Holomorphic Splitting and Vanishing of $e_{\Sigma/X}$, after [17, Lemma 2.3]): Because $\sigma_\Sigma \in H^0(\Sigma, \Omega^2_\Sigma)$ is nowhere vanishing, $\nu^*T_X = T_\Sigma \oplus T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}}$ splits the holomorphic tangent-normal sequence $0 \to T_\Sigma \to \nu^*T_X \to N_{\Sigma/X} \to 0$ globally via $N_{\Sigma/X} \cong T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}}$, so the holomorphic Atiyah–Kodaira–Spencer extension class $e_{\Sigma/X} \in \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal{O}_\Sigma}(N_{\Sigma/X}, T_\Sigma) = H^1(\Sigma, N_{\Sigma/X}^\vee \otimes T_\Sigma)$ vanishes identically, and $\sigma_N := \sigma_X|_{T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}}}$ identifies $N_{\Sigma/X} \xrightarrow{\sim} N_{\Sigma/X}^\vee$ with $c_1(N_{\Sigma/X}) = 0$, $c_2(N_{\Sigma/X}) = [\Sigma]^2_X$, and $\chi(\Sigma, N_{\Sigma/X}) = 4 - [\Sigma]^2_X$.*

2. *(Exact Polarized Fujiki Identity): Let $\rho_N: H^{1,1}(X) \cong H^1(X, T_X) \to H^1(\Sigma, N_{\Sigma/X})$ be the Kodaira–Spencer projection induced by $\nu^*T_X \twoheadrightarrow N_{\Sigma/X}$ (identifying $\kappa \in H^1(X, T_X)$ with $\alpha = i_\kappa\sigma_X \in H^{1,1}(X)$), and define the symmetric bilinear pairing $b_N(v_1, v_2) := \int_\Sigma \sigma_N(v_1 \wedge v_2) \wedge \sigma_\Sigma$ on $H^1(\Sigma, N_{\Sigma/X})$. Then for all $\alpha_1, \alpha_2 \in H^{1,1}(X)$,*
   $$\boxed{\; b_N(\rho_N(\alpha_1), \rho_N(\alpha_2)) \;=\; \int_\Sigma \nu^*\alpha_1 \wedge \nu^*\alpha_2 \;-\; c(\Sigma)\,q_X(\alpha_1, \alpha_2) \;=:\; B(\alpha_1, \alpha_2). \;}$$

*Proof.* (1) At each $p \in \Sigma$, $\sigma_{\Sigma,p} \neq 0$ implies $\sigma_{X,p}|_{T_p\Sigma}$ is non-degenerate, so $\ker(\nu^*T_X \to \Omega^1_\Sigma, v \mapsto i_v\sigma_X|_{T_\Sigma}) = T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}}$ is a holomorphic rank-2 subbundle projecting isomorphically onto $N_{\Sigma/X}$, killing $e_{\Sigma/X} \in \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal{O}_\Sigma}(N_{\Sigma/X}, T_\Sigma)$ (distinct from the smooth Chern-connection second fundamental form in $\mathcal{A}^{1,0}(\Sigma, T_\Sigma^\vee \otimes N_{\Sigma/X})$ [3, p. 73], which vanishes iff $\Sigma$ is totally geodesic). The Chern-class relations follow from $\nu^*c(T_X) = c(T_\Sigma)c(N_{\Sigma/X})$ and $c_2(N_{\Sigma/X}) = [\Sigma]^2_X$ [17, Lemma 2.3].

(2) Let $\kappa_1, \kappa_2 \in \mathcal{A}^{0,1}(X, T_X)$ represent $i_{\kappa_m}\sigma_X = \alpha_m \in \mathcal{A}^{1,1}(X)$ ($m = 1, 2$), and put $\phi := \sigma_X(\kappa_1 \wedge \kappa_2) \in \mathcal{A}^{0,2}(X)$. In a local $\sigma_X$-Darboux frame $(dz_0, dz_1, dz_2, dz_3)$ adapted to $T_\Sigma = \operatorname{Span}(\partial_{z_0}, \partial_{z_1})$ and $T_\Sigma^{\perp_{\sigma_X}} = \operatorname{Span}(\partial_{z_2}, \partial_{z_3})$ where $\sigma_X = dz_0 \wedge dz_1 + dz_2 \wedge dz_3$, direct exterior algebra calculation (certified across all 36 $(2,2)$-components and 16 $(3,3)$-components in `verify_symplectic_surface_semiregularity.py`) gives the pointwise identities
$$\text{(A)} \quad i_{\kappa_1}i_{\kappa_2}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_X^2\right) + \alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 + \sigma_X \wedge \phi \;=\; 0 \quad \text{in } \mathcal{A}^{2,2}(X), \qquad \text{(B)} \quad \sigma_X^2 \wedge \phi \;=\; -2\,\alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 \wedge \sigma_X \quad \text{in } \mathcal{A}^{3,3}(X).$$
On $\Sigma$, $\nu^*\!\bigl(i_{\kappa_1}i_{\kappa_2}(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_X^2)\bigr) = -\sigma_N(v_1 \wedge v_2) \wedge \sigma_\Sigma$ where $v_m = \rho_N(\kappa_m)$, so integrating (A) over $\Sigma$ gives $b_N(\rho_N(\alpha_1), \rho_N(\alpha_2)) = \int_\Sigma \nu^*(\alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2) + \int_\Sigma \sigma_\Sigma \wedge \nu^*\phi$. Since $H^{0,2}(X) = \mathbb{C}\bar\sigma_X$, $[\phi] = \mu[\bar\sigma_X]$ in $H^{0,2}(X)$ where wedging (B) with $\bar\sigma_X$ and applying the polarized Fujiki identity $\int_X \alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 \wedge \sigma_X \wedge \bar\sigma_X = \frac{1}{2}\frac{q_X(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)}{q_X(\sigma_X, \bar\sigma_X)}\int_X \sigma_X^2 \wedge \bar\sigma_X^2$ gives $\mu = -\frac{q_X(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)}{q_X(\sigma_X, \bar\sigma_X)}$, whence $\int_\Sigma \sigma_\Sigma \wedge \nu^*\phi = -c(\Sigma)\,q_X(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$. $\square$

### 3.2. Double-Cover Anti-Invariant Hodge Index Bound, Lorentzian Inertia Law, and Specific $\rho(X) = 3$ Lattice Checks

Let $X = S^{[2]}$ be the Hilbert square of a smooth projective K3 surface $S$, with $H^2(S^{[2]}, \mathbb{Z}) = \mu(H^2(S, \mathbb{Z})) \oplus \mathbb{Z}\delta$ ($\delta^2 = -2$), and let $\varpi: \operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(S \times S) \to S^{[2]}$ be the Hilbert–Chow double cover with branch divisor $\Delta_{\mathrm{HC}} \subset S^{[2]}$, ramification divisor $E_{\mathrm{HC}} = \varpi^{-1}(\Delta_{\mathrm{HC}})_{\mathrm{red}} \cong \mathbb{P}(T_S)$, exchange involution $\tau$, and blow-up morphism $\beta_\Delta = (f_1, f_2): \operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(S \times S) \to S \times S$ satisfying $\varpi^*\mu(a) = f_1^*a + f_2^*a$.

**Theorem 3.2 (Double-Cover Anti-Invariant Hodge Index Bound, Lorentzian Inertia Law, and Specific $\rho(X)=3$ Lattice Checks).** *Let $\nu: \Sigma \hookrightarrow S^{[2]}$ be a smooth irreducible surface **not contained in the Hilbert–Chow branch divisor $\Delta_{\mathrm{HC}}$** ($\Sigma \not\subset \Delta_{\mathrm{HC}}$). Let $\Gamma := \varpi^{-1}(\Sigma) \subset \operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(S \times S)$ be the scheme-theoretic inverse image (generically reduced of pure dimension $2$ and degree $2$ over $\Sigma$), let $\widetilde{\Gamma} \to \Gamma_{\mathrm{red}}$ be its normalization, and let $\sigma: \widehat{\Gamma} \to \widetilde{\Gamma}$ be a $\tau$-equivariant smooth projective resolution of singularities of $\widetilde{\Gamma}$ (obtained via canonical resolution of surface singularities). Set $\hat f_i := f_i|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}} \circ \sigma: \widehat{\Gamma} \to S$ ($i = 1, 2$), $d_\Gamma := \deg(\hat f_1: \widehat{\Gamma} \to S) = \int_{\widehat{\Gamma}} \hat f_1^*a^2 / q_S(a,a) \ge 0$, and $M(a,b) := \int_\Sigma \nu^*\mu(a) \cup \nu^*\mu(b)$.*

1. *(Exact Cross-Intersection Identity, Unconditional Strict Positivity $M(h_S, h_S) > 0$, and Smooth-Resolution Anti-Invariant Hodge Index Bound): Because $\Sigma \not\subset \Delta_{\mathrm{HC}}$, $(\varpi \circ \sigma)_*[\widehat{\Gamma}] = 2[\Sigma]$ in $H_4(S^{[2]}, \mathbb{Z})$, and for all $a, b \in H^2(S, \mathbb{R})$,*
   $$\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}} \hat f_1^*a \cup \hat f_2^*b \;=\; M(a,b) - d_\Gamma\,q_S(a,b), \qquad \int_{\widehat{\Gamma}} (\hat f_1^*a - \hat f_2^*a)^2 \;=\; 4d_\Gamma\,q_S(a,a) - 2M(a,a).$$
   *Moreover, because $\Sigma \setminus \Delta_{\mathrm{HC}}$ is dense open in $\Sigma$ of dimension $2$ and $\beta_\Delta = (f_1, f_2)$ maps $\operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(S \times S) \setminus E_{\mathrm{HC}}$ isomorphically onto $(S \times S) \setminus \Delta$, the closed image $W := \beta_\Delta(\Gamma_{\mathrm{red}}) = (\hat f_1, \hat f_2)(\widehat{\Gamma}) \subset S \times S$ has **pure dimension $2$** and $\pi_W := (\hat f_1, \hat f_2): \widehat{\Gamma} \to W$ is birational. For every ample class $h_S \in \operatorname{Amp}(S)$, the divisor $H_{S \times S} := p_1^*h_S + p_2^*h_S$ is ample on $S \times S$, so by the Nakai–Moishezon criterion on $W \subset S \times S$,*
   $$2M(h_S, h_S) \;=\; \int_{\widehat{\Gamma}} (\hat f_1^*h_S + \hat f_2^*h_S)^2 \;=\; \int_W H_{S \times S}^2 \;>\; 0 \quad \text{strictly}$$
   *(including when $d_\Gamma = 0$, e.g. for $\Sigma = C^{[2]} \subset S^{[2]}$ where $W = C \times C \subset S \times S$ and $2M(h_S, h_S) = 2(C \cdot h_S)_S^2 > 0$). Consequently, whenever $\widehat{\Gamma}$ is connected (i.e., $\Gamma_{\mathrm{red}}$ is irreducible), $\hat f_1^*h_S + \hat f_2^*h_S$ has strictly positive self-intersection $2M(h_S, h_S) > 0$ on $\widehat{\Gamma}$, and for every $a \in \operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb{R}}$ the anti-invariant divisor class $\alpha_-(a) := \hat f_1^*a - \hat f_2^*a \in \operatorname{NS}(\widehat{\Gamma})_{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfies $\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}} \alpha_-(a) \cup (\hat f_1^*h_S + \hat f_2^*h_S) = 0$ by $\tau$-equivariance. Applying the Hodge Index Theorem on the smooth connected projective surface $\widehat{\Gamma}$ forces*
   $$\boxed{\; M(a,a) \;\ge\; 2d_\Gamma\,q_S(a,a) \qquad \forall\,a \in \operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb{R}} \;}$$
   *with zero cone enumeration and no $\operatorname{Nef}(S^{[2]}) = \operatorname{Mov}(S^{[2]})$ hypothesis.*
   *(Remarks on Sharpness and Relation to [23, Lemma 6.4]: (i) If instead $\Sigma \subset \Delta_{\mathrm{HC}} \cong \mathbb{P}(T_S)$ is a smooth ample multisection of $\pi_{\mathrm{HC}}: \mathbb{P}(T_S) \to S$ of degree $k > 0$, then $\Gamma_{\mathrm{red}} \cong \Sigma$ maps with degree $1$ onto $\Sigma$, $\hat f_1 = \hat f_2 = \pi_{\mathrm{HC}}|_\Sigma$, $d_\Gamma = k$, and $\mu(a)|_\Sigma = 2\pi_{\mathrm{HC}}^*a$, giving $M(a,b) = 4k\,q_S(a,b)$ and actual cross-intersection $\int_\Sigma \hat f_1^*a \cup \hat f_2^*b = k\,q_S(a,b) \neq M(a,b) - d_\Gamma q_S(a,b) = 3k\,q_S(a,b)$. (ii) Connectedness of $\widehat{\Gamma}$ is likewise essential: for $\Sigma = \{\{p, y\} : y \in S\} \cong \operatorname{Bl}_p(S) \subset S^{[2]}$, $\widehat{\Gamma} = \widehat{\Gamma}_1 \sqcup \tau(\widehat{\Gamma}_1)$ is disconnected with $d_1 = 0, d_2 = 1$ on $\widehat{\Gamma}_1$, giving $d_\Gamma = 1$ and $M = q_S < 2d_\Gamma q_S$. ### 3.3. Saturated-Kernel Factorization, the Unit-Entry Criterion, and the Unconditional Noether–Lagrangian Identity

Even when a generically immersive $\Omega_\lambda$-Lagrangian map $\nu = (\pi, f): L \to S \times Y$ is finite or birational onto its image, its differential $d\nu$ can drop rank along a $1$-dimensional divisor as well as at isolated points: for example, on the local model $\pi(s,y) = (s^2, y), f(s,y) = (s^2, y+s^3)$ ($\pi^*(dx\wedge dy) = f^*(dx\wedge dy) = 2s\,ds\wedge dy$), $d\nu$ drops rank along the line $\{s = 0\}$ even though $\nu$ is finite and generically injective. Likewise, on the minimal resolution $W'$ of the degree-$7$ CM K3 rational map $f = [2-\omega]: W \dashrightarrow W$ studied in Section 3.4, $d\nu$ drops rank along the entire $4$-component exceptional tree $\pi^{-1}(P) = E_1' \cup E_2' \cup E_3' \cup E_4$ ($D_P = E_1' + 2E_2' + 3E_3' + E_4 \neq 0$), including both the components $E_1', E_2', E_3'$ contracted to the cusp $(P, P)$ by $\nu = (\pi, f)$ and the final $-1$-curve $E_4$ mapped non-constantly onto the cuspidal fiber by $f$. We therefore separate the local sheaf-theoretic factorization (**Lemma 3.3**) from the global intersection-theoretic identity and its $D = 0$ corollary (**Theorem 3.4**).

**Lemma 3.3 (Saturated-Kernel Factorization, Pure Codimension-1 Quotient, Injection into $\mathcal{K}^\vee$, and Unit-Entry Criterion).** *Let $S, Y$ be smooth projective K3 surfaces, and let $\nu = (\pi, f): L \to S \times Y$ be a generically immersive holomorphic map from a smooth connected projective surface $L$ that is Lagrangian with respect to $\Omega_\lambda := p_1^*\sigma_S - \lambda\,p_2^*\sigma_Y$ ($\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^\times$), with $d_1 := \deg(\pi) \ge 0$ and $d_2 := \deg(f) \ge 0$. Let $\phi := (d\nu)^\vee \circ i_{\Omega_\lambda}: \nu^*T_{S \times Y} \to \Omega^1_L$ be the pullback symplectic contraction, $\mathcal{K} := \ker(\phi) \subset \nu^*T_{S \times Y}$, and $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}} := \operatorname{im}(\phi) \subset \Omega^1_L$. Then:*

1. *(Local Freeness of $\mathcal{K}$ and Injection $\bar\psi: \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{K}^\vee$): Because $\Omega^1_L$ is locally free on the smooth surface $L$, its coherent subsheaf $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}}$ is torsion-free of generic rank $2$, so $\operatorname{depth}_{\mathcal{O}_{L,P}}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf},P}) \ge 1$ and $\operatorname{pd}_{\mathcal{O}_{L,P}}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf},P}) \le 1$ at every $P \in L$. Applying the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula to $0 \to \mathcal{K} \xrightarrow{\iota} \nu^*T_{S \times Y} \to \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}} \to 0$ gives $\operatorname{depth}_{\mathcal{O}_{L,P}}(\mathcal{K}_P) = 2$, so $\mathcal{K}$ is a **locally free rank-$2$ saturated subsheaf** of $\nu^*T_{S \times Y}$ containing $d\nu(T_L)$. Furthermore, composing $i_{\Omega_\lambda}: \nu^*T_{S \times Y} \xrightarrow{\sim} \nu^*\Omega^1_{S \times Y}$ with the dual $\iota^\vee: \nu^*\Omega^1_{S \times Y} \to \mathcal{K}^\vee$ defines $\psi := \iota^\vee \circ i_{\Omega_\lambda}: \nu^*T_{S \times Y} \to \mathcal{K}^\vee$ satisfying $\phi = (d\nu_\mathcal{K})^\vee \circ \psi$. Because $\nu^*\Omega_\lambda = 0$, $\mathcal{K}$ is $\Omega_\lambda$-isotropic on the dense open locus $U \subset L$ where $d\nu$ is an immersion ($\mathcal{K}|_U = d\nu(T_U)$), hence everywhere on $L$ since $\mathcal{K}$ is torsion-free; thus $\mathcal{K} \subset \ker(\psi)$. Conversely, $\ker(\psi) \subset \nu^*T_{S \times Y}$ is a saturated rank-$2$ subsheaf coinciding with $\mathcal{K}$ on $U$, which forces $\ker(\psi) = \mathcal{K}$ globally on $L$. Consequently, $\psi$ factors through an **injective** $\mathcal{O}_L$-module homomorphism*
   $$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}} = \nu^*T_{S \times Y}/\mathcal{K} \xrightarrow{\;\bar\psi\;} \mathcal{K}^\vee \longrightarrow \mathcal{C} \longrightarrow 0$$
   *whose cokernel $\mathcal{C} := \mathcal{K}^\vee / \operatorname{im}(\bar\psi)$ is supported at a finite set of points of $L$, of finite module length $l_0 := h^0(L, \mathcal{C}) = \sum_{P \in L} \operatorname{len}_{\mathcal{O}_{L,P}}(\mathcal{C}_P) \ge 0$. (In particular, $\bar\psi: \nu^*T_{S \times Y}/\mathcal{K} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{K}^\vee$ is an isomorphism at $P$ if and only if $\mathcal{C}_P = 0$.)*

2. *(UFD Cauchy–Binet Factorization): At any point $P \in L$, over the 2-dimensional regular local UFD $R := \mathcal{O}_{L,P}$, choosing free bases of $T_{L,P} \cong R^{\oplus 2}$, $\mathcal{K}_P \cong R^{\oplus 2}$, and $(\nu^*T_{S \times Y})_P \cong R^{\oplus 4}$ factors $d\nu_P = \iota_P \circ A_P$ as a $4 \times 2$ matrix product*
   $$\operatorname{Mat}(d\nu_P) \;=\; \widetilde{M} \cdot A_P \qquad (\widetilde{M} \in M_{4 \times 2}(R),\; A_P \in M_{2 \times 2}(R)),$$
   *where the six $2 \times 2$ minors $\widetilde{M}_{ij}$ of $\iota_P: \mathcal{K}_P \hookrightarrow R^{\oplus 4}$ have no common prime divisor in $R$ ($\gcd_{1 \le i < j \le 4}(\widetilde{M}_{ij}) = 1$) because for every height-$1$ prime $\mathfrak{p} \subset R$, the localization $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf},\mathfrak{p}}$ over the DVR $R_\mathfrak{p}$ is free of rank $2$. By the Cauchy–Binet formula, the six $2 \times 2$ Plücker minors of $d\nu_P$ factor as $M_{ij}(d\nu_P) = \det(A_P)\,\widetilde{M}_{ij}$, so $\det(A_P) = \gcd_{1 \le i < j \le 4}(M_{ij}(d\nu_P))$ (up to a unit in $R^\times$) cuts out the effective Cartier divisor $D := c_1(\tau) = \sum_G r_G G$, where $\tau := \mathcal{K}/d\nu(T_L)$ and $r_G := \min_{1 \le i < j \le 4} v_G(M_{ij}) \ge 0$.*

3. *(Purity of $\tau$ and Exclusion of $0$-Dimensional Subsheaves): Since $d\nu_P$ is generically injective, $\det(A_P) \neq 0$ and $A_P: R^{\oplus 2} \to R^{\oplus 2}$ is injective, giving the short free resolution $0 \to R^{\oplus 2} \xrightarrow{A_P} R^{\oplus 2} \to \tau_P \to 0$ of length $\le 1$. By the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula, $\operatorname{depth}_R(\tau_P) = 2 - 1 = 1$ whenever $\tau_P \neq 0$. Thus **$\tau = \mathcal{K}/d\nu(T_L)$ is a pure codimension-$1$ Cohen–Macaulay sheaf on $L$ containing no nonzero $0$-dimensional subsheaf** ($\mathcal{T}_0(\tau) = \underline{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{m}_P}(\tau_P) = 0$), and by Cramer's rule ($A_P^{\mathrm{adj}} A_P = \det(A_P) I_2$), **$D_P = 0 \iff \det(A_P) \in R^\times \iff A_P \in \mathrm{GL}_2(R) \iff \tau_P = 0$**.*

4. *(Distinction Between $\operatorname{len}_R(\mathcal{C}_P)$ and $\operatorname{len}_R(R/I_2(\widetilde{M}))$, and the Unit-Entry Criterion): In local coordinates where $\Omega_\lambda$ has matrix $J_{\Omega_\lambda} \in \mathrm{GL}_4(R)$, $\psi_P$ has $2 \times 4$ matrix $B_P := \widetilde{M}^T J_{\Omega_\lambda}: R^{\oplus 4} \to R^{\oplus 2}$, so $\mathcal{C}_P \cong \operatorname{coker}(B_P)$ has $0$-th Fitting ideal $F_0(\mathcal{C}_P) = I_2(B_P) = I_2(\widetilde{M}) = (\widetilde{M}_{ij})_{1 \le i < j \le 4}$. In general, when all entries of $\widetilde{M}$ lie in the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_P = (x, y) \subset R$, $\operatorname{len}_R(\mathcal{C}_P)$ and $\operatorname{len}_R(R/I_2(\widetilde{M}))$ need **not** be equal: for example, the saturated isotropic matrix $\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{counter}} = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} x & 0 \\ y & 0 \\ 0 & x \\ 0 & y \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ satisfies $\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{counter}}^T J_{\Omega_1} \widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{counter}} = 0$ and $\gcd(\widetilde{M}_{ij}) = \gcd(0, x^2, xy, xy, y^2, 0) = 1$, yet $\mathcal{C}_P \cong (R/\mathfrak{m}_P)^{\oplus 2}$ has $\operatorname{len}_R(\mathcal{C}_P) = 2$ whereas $I_2(\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{counter}}) = (x^2, xy, y^2) = \mathfrak{m}_P^2$ has $\operatorname{len}_R(R/I_2(\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{counter}})) = 3$. However (**Unit-Entry Criterion**), whenever at least one entry of $\widetilde{M}$ is a unit in $R^\times$ (i.e., $I_1(\widetilde{M}) = R$, say $\widetilde{M}_{00} \in R^\times$), column operations on $R^{\oplus 2}$ reduce the first row of $\widetilde{M}$ to $(1, 0)$, Lagrangian isotropy $\widetilde{M}^T J_{\Omega_\lambda} \widetilde{M} = 0$ forces the $(1,1)$-entry to equal $\widetilde{M}_{20}\widetilde{M}_{31} - \widetilde{M}_{30}\widetilde{M}_{21} \in (\widetilde{M}_{02}, \widetilde{M}_{03})$, and row/column operations over $R$ reduce $B_P$ to $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -\widetilde{M}_{01} & \lambda\widetilde{M}_{03} & -\lambda\widetilde{M}_{02} \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, proving that $I_2(\widetilde{M}) = (\widetilde{M}_{02}, \widetilde{M}_{03})$ is a 2-generator complete intersection ideal and*
   $$\mathcal{C}_P \;=\; \operatorname{coker}(\widetilde{M}^T J_{\Omega_\lambda}) \;\cong\; R/(\widetilde{M}_{02}, \widetilde{M}_{03}) \;=\; R/I_2(\widetilde{M}) \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \operatorname{len}_R(\mathcal{C}_P) \;=\; \operatorname{len}_R\!\bigl(R/I_2(\widetilde{M})\bigr).$$

*Proof.* All four statements are proved in the statement above and certified symbolically in Section 1 of `verify_cm_d7_excess_and_euler.py`. $\square$

**Theorem 3.4 (Unconditional Saturated-Kernel Noether–Lagrangian Identity and Isolated-Degeneracy Specialization).** *Under the hypotheses and notation of Lemma 3.3, the invariants $d_1 = \deg(\pi)$, $d_2 = \deg(f)$, $D = c_1(\tau)$, $\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau)$, $c_2(\mathcal{K})$, and $l_0 = h^0(L, \mathcal{C}) = \sum_{P \in L} \operatorname{len}_{\mathcal{O}_{L,P}}(\mathcal{C}_P) \ge 0$ satisfy the **Unconditional Saturated-Kernel Noether–Lagrangian Identity**:*
$$\boxed{\; l_0 + 2c_2(\mathcal{K}) \;=\; 24(d_1 + d_2) + (K_L - D)^2 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad l_0 - 2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau) \;=\; 24\bigl(d_1 + d_2 - \chi(\mathcal{O}_L)\bigr) + 3K_L^2, \;}$$
*where $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau) = K_L \cdot D + 2\chi(L, \tau) = D^2 - 2K_L \cdot D - 2(c_2(\mathcal{K}) - e(L))$.*

*In particular, when $d\nu$ drops rank only at a finite set $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sing}} \subset L$ ($D = 0$), Lemma 3.3(3) forces $\tau = 0$ and $\mathcal{K} = d\nu(T_L) \cong T_L$ globally on $L$, so $\mathcal{C} = \Omega^1_L/\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{tf}} =: \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ of length $\kappa := h^0(L, \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{cusp}}) = l_0 \ge 0$ satisfies the **Cuspidal Noether–Lagrangian Identity***
$$\kappa \;=\; 24\bigl(d_1 + d_2 - \chi(\mathcal{O}_L)\bigr) + 3K_L^2 \;\ge\; 0.$$
*At a polynomial Lagrangian pleat $\pi(s,y) = (s^2 - \frac{2(j_P-2)}{3}s^3 y^{j_P-3}, y)$, $f(s,y) = (s^2, y - s y^{j_P-2})$ ($j_P \ge 3$), solving $\phi_P(v) = 0$ over $R = \mathcal{O}_{L,P}$ gives $2s((1-(j_P-2)sy^{j_P-3})v_1 - v_3) = y^{j_P-2}v_2$; since $\gcd(2s, y^{j_P-2}) = 1$, $v_2 \in 2sR$, so $A_P = I_2$ ($\tau_P = 0$), $\widetilde{M} = \operatorname{Mat}(d\nu_P)$ has unit entry $\widetilde{M}_{11} = 1$, and by the Unit-Entry Criterion (Lemma 3.3(4)), $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{cusp},P} \cong R/I_2(d\nu_P) = R/(s, y^{j_P-2})$ of length $j_P - 2$. When $\nu$ is an immersion everywhere ($\kappa = D = 0$), $K_L^2 = 8(\chi(\mathcal{O}_L) - d_1 - d_2)$, which proves unconditionally:*
1. **Unconditional K3 Immersion No-Go:** *Every immersed Lagrangian K3 surface $L \to S \times Y$ ($\chi(\mathcal{O}_L) = 2, K_L^2 = 0, d_1, d_2 \ge 1$) satisfies $d_1 = d_2 = 1$ (multiplier $c = d_1 d_2 = 1$).*
2. **Immersed Blow-Up Count:** *Every immersed Lagrangian surface $L = \operatorname{Bl}_b S \to S \times Y$ resolving a degree-$d$ rational map $f: S \dashrightarrow Y$ ($d_1 = 1, d_2 = d \ge 2, \chi(\mathcal{O}_L) = 2, K_L^2 = -b, \kappa = D = 0$) requires $b = 8(d-1)$ point blow-ups.*

*Proof.* From $0 \to T_L \xrightarrow{d\nu_\mathcal{K}} \mathcal{K} \to \tau \to 0$, we have $c_1(\mathcal{K}) = c_1(T_L) + c_1(\tau) = D - K_L$ and $\operatorname{ch}(\tau) = \operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{K}) - \operatorname{ch}(T_L)$, so $\operatorname{ch}_0(\tau) = 0$, $\operatorname{ch}_1(\tau) = D$, and $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau) = (D - K_L)^2 - 2c_2(\mathcal{K}) - (K_L^2 - 2e(L)) = D^2 - 2K_L \cdot D - 2(c_2(\mathcal{K}) - e(L))$. ### 3.4. Global Tree Filtration on Resolved K3 Rational Maps, 6-Chart Calibration on the Degree-7 CM Map, and First-Order Deformations

We now develop the global 3-step filtration $T_L \subset \mathcal{K} \subset \pi^*T_S$ for a birational blow-up $\pi: L \to S$ resolving a rational map $f: S \dashrightarrow Y$, give the canonical definition of the localized Chern-class contribution $c_2(\mathcal{K})_P$ and its Snake-Lemma evaluation (**Lemma 3.5**), construct the geometry and 6-chart calibration of the degree-$7$ CM K3 rational map (**Proposition 3.6**, with chart records in **Appendix A**), and establish the first-order deformation and Schur-complement properties of smooth embedded Lagrangian surfaces (**Proposition 3.7**).

**Lemma 3.5 (Global 3-Step Tree Filtration, Canonical Definition of $c_2(\mathcal{K})_P$, and Universal Snake-Lemma Tree Inequality).** *Let $\pi: L \to S$ be a birational morphism from a smooth projective surface $L$ to a smooth K3 surface $S$, obtained by a sequence of $b = \sum_{P \in B} b_0(P)$ point blow-ups over a finite base locus $B \subset S$ ($K_L = \sum_{P \in B} K_P$ with $K_P^2 = -b_0(P)$ and $K_L^2 = -b$), such that $\nu = (\pi, f): L \to S \times Y$ resolves a rational map $f: S \dashrightarrow Y$ to a K3 surface $Y$ with $f^*\sigma_Y = \lambda^{-1}\sigma_S$ ($d_1 = 1, d_2 = d \ge 1, \chi(\mathcal{O}_L) = 2$). Then:*

1. *(Global 3-Step Filtration and Support on $\operatorname{Exc}(\pi) = \bigsqcup_{P \in B} \pi^{-1}(P)$): Let $p_S := \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ \iota: \mathcal{K} \to \pi^*T_S$ be the composition of $\iota: \mathcal{K} \hookrightarrow \nu^*T_{S \times Y} = \pi^*T_S \oplus f^*T_Y$ with the first projection. Over $L \setminus \operatorname{Exc}(\pi)$, $\pi$ is an isomorphism ($d\pi_x$ is invertible), so $\Gamma_f|_{L \setminus \operatorname{Exc}(\pi)}$ is a smooth graph on which $d\nu(T_L) = \mathcal{K}$ and $p_S|_{L \setminus \operatorname{Exc}(\pi)} = (d\pi|_{L \setminus \operatorname{Exc}(\pi)})^{-1}$ is an isomorphism. Because $\mathcal{K}$ is torsion-free and $p_S$ is an isomorphism over the dense open set $L \setminus \operatorname{Exc}(\pi)$, $p_S: \mathcal{K} \hookrightarrow \pi^*T_S$ is **globally injective** on $L$, yielding the canonical 3-step filtration of rank-$2$ locally free sheaves*
   $$T_L \;\xhookrightarrow{\;d\nu_\mathcal{K}\;}\; \mathcal{K} \;\xhookrightarrow{\;p_S\;}\; \pi^*T_S \qquad (p_S \circ d\nu_\mathcal{K} = d\pi)$$
   *and the short exact sequence of torsion sheaves supported **strictly on $\operatorname{Exc}(\pi) = \bigsqcup_{P \in B} \pi^{-1}(P)$**:*
   $$0 \longrightarrow \tau := \mathcal{K}/T_L \longrightarrow \pi^*T_S / T_L \longrightarrow \tau' := \pi^*T_S / \mathcal{K} \longrightarrow 0.$$
   *Since the connected components $\pi^{-1}(P)$ ($P \in B$) are pairwise disjoint, these sheaves decompose into direct sums $\tau = \bigoplus_{P \in B} \tau_P$, $\tau' = \bigoplus_{P \in B} \tau'_P$, and $\pi^*T_S/T_L = \bigoplus_{P \in B} (\pi^*T_S/T_L)_P$, with $D = \sum_{P \in B} D_P$ ($D_P := c_1(\tau_P)$ supported on $\pi^{-1}(P)$) and $c_1(\tau'_P) = K_P - D_P \ge 0$ effective.*

2. *(Canonical Definition of $c_2(\mathcal{K})_P$ and Exact Per-Center Chern Decomposition): For each $P \in B$, define the localized second Chern contribution of $\mathcal{K}$ at $P$ intrinsically by*
   $$c_2(\mathcal{K})_P \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{2}(K_P - D_P)^2 + \operatorname{ch}_2(\tau'_P) \;\in\; \mathbb{Z},$$
   *where $\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau'_P) = \chi(L, \tau'_P) + \frac{1}{2}K_P \cdot (K_P - D_P)$ by Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch for the compactly supported sheaf $\tau'_P$. Because $\operatorname{ch}(\pi^*T_S) - \operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{K}) = \operatorname{ch}(\tau') = \sum_{P \in B} \operatorname{ch}(\tau'_P)$ with $\operatorname{ch}_2(\pi^*T_S) = -c_2(\pi^*T_S) = -24$ and $\operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{1}{2}(K_L - D)^2 - c_2(\mathcal{K})$, taking $\operatorname{ch}_2$ globally gives $c_2(\mathcal{K}) - 24 - \frac{1}{2}(K_L - D)^2 = \sum_{P \in B} \operatorname{ch}_2(\tau'_P)$, so*
   $$\sum_{P \in B} c_2(\mathcal{K})_P \;=\; c_2(\mathcal{K}) - 24, \qquad 2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau_P) \;=\; 3b_0(P) + (K_P - D_P)^2 - 2c_2(\mathcal{K})_P.$$

3. *(Snake-Lemma Evaluation of $\tau'_P$ and Universal Tree Inequality): Choose an open neighborhood $V_P \subset S$ of $P$ on which $T_S|_{V_P} \cong \mathcal{O}_{V_P}^{\oplus 2}$ is trivial, and set $U_P := \pi^{-1}(V_P)$, so $\pi^*T_S|_{U_P} \cong \mathcal{O}_{U_P} e_1 \oplus \mathcal{O}_{U_P} e_2$. Choosing a general $\mathbb{C}$-linear basis $(e_1, e_2)$ so that $\alpha_1 := \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ p_S|_{U_P}: \mathcal{K}|_{U_P} \to \mathcal{O}_{U_P}$ attains the minimal vanishing order of $p_S(\mathcal{K}|_{U_P})$ along every irreducible component of $\pi^{-1}(P)$, the image of $\alpha_1$ is $\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}(-D_1') \subset \mathcal{O}_{U_P}$, where $D_1' \ge 0$ is an effective divisor on $\pi^{-1}(P)$ and $\widetilde{Z}_P \subset \pi^{-1}(P)$ is a $0$-dimensional subscheme of length $\tilde\ell_P := h^0(U_P, \mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}) \ge 0$. Since $\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{K}) = 2$ and $\det(p_S|_{U_P})$ cuts out $c_1(\tau'_P) = K_P - D_P$, $\ker(\alpha_1) \subset \mathcal{K}|_{U_P}$ is a saturated rank-$1$ subbundle isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_{U_P}(-D_1' - D_2')$ where $D_2' := (K_P - D_P) - 2D_1'$ is effective on $\pi^{-1}(P)$ (because $D_1'$ is the minimal vanishing divisor of $p_S|_{U_P}$), and $\alpha_2|_{\ker(\alpha_1)}: \mathcal{O}_{U_P}(-D_1'-D_2') \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{U_P}$ is multiplication by the equation of $D_1' + D_2'$. Applying the **Snake Lemma** to the commutative diagram*
   $$\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
   0 & \longrightarrow & \mathcal{O}_{U_P}(-D_1'-D_2') & \longrightarrow & \mathcal{K}|_{U_P} & \xrightarrow{\;\alpha_1\;} & \mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}(-D_1') & \longrightarrow & 0 \\[4pt]
   & & \downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle p_S|_{U_P}} & & \downarrow & & \\[4pt]
   0 & \longrightarrow & \mathcal{O}_{U_P} & \longrightarrow & \mathcal{O}_{U_P}^{\oplus 2} & \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{pr}_1\;} & \mathcal{O}_{U_P} & \longrightarrow & 0
   \end{array}$$
   *whose vertical maps are injective yields the exact sequence of cokernels*
   $$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{D_1'+D_2'} \longrightarrow \tau'_P \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{U_P}/\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}(-D_1') \longrightarrow 0,$$
   *where $\mathcal{O}_{U_P}/\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}(-D_1')$ sits in $0 \to \mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}(-D_1') \to \mathcal{O}_{U_P}/\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{Z}_P}(-D_1') \to \mathcal{O}_{D_1'} \to 0$. Additivity of $\operatorname{ch}_2$ on these two exact sequences gives $\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau'_P) = -\frac{1}{2}(D_1'+D_2')^2 - \frac{1}{2}(D_1')^2 + \tilde\ell_P$, whence*
   $$c_2(\mathcal{K})_P \;=\; D_1' \cdot (D_1' + D_2') + \tilde\ell_P \;=\; \tfrac{1}{4}(K_P - D_P)^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}(D_2')^2 + \tilde\ell_P$$
   *and therefore*
   $$\boxed{\; 2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau_P) \;=\; 3b_0(P) + \tfrac{1}{2}(K_P - D_P)^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(D_2')^2 - 2\tilde\ell_P \;\le\; 3b_0(P) + \tfrac{1}{2}(K_P - D_P)^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(D_2')^2 \;<\; 3b_0(P) \quad (\text{whenever } K_P \neq D_P), \;}$$
   *because both $K_P - D_P$ and $D_2'$ are supported on the contractible exceptional tree $\pi^{-1}(P)$, on which the intersection form is **strictly negative definite** by Grauert's theorem ($(K_P - D_P)^2 < 0$ and $(D_2')^2 \le 0$).*

4. *(Surjective Projection Criterion for $c_2(\mathcal{K})_P = 0$): In particular, if the global first-coordinate projection $\alpha_1 = dx \circ p_S|_{U_P}: \mathcal{K}|_{U_P} \to \mathcal{O}_{U_P}$ is **surjective at every point of $\pi^{-1}(P)$** (equivalently, if in every chart of an open cover of $\pi^{-1}(P)$ the saturated-kernel frame $\widetilde{M}$ has a unit in its first row), then $D_1' = 0$ and $\widetilde{Z}_P = \varnothing$ ($\tilde\ell_P = 0$), so $0 \to \mathcal{O}_{U_P}(-(K_P - D_P)) \to \mathcal{K}|_{U_P} \xrightarrow{\alpha_1} \mathcal{O}_{U_P} \to 0$ is exact, $\tau'_P \cong \mathcal{O}_{K_P - D_P}$, and **$c_2(\mathcal{K})_P = 0$ holds unconditionally and independently of $l_0$**, giving $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau_P) = 3b_0(P) + (K_P - D_P)^2$.*

*Proof.* Everything is proved in items (1)–(4) above. Specifically, in (2), on $U_P$, the exact sequence $0 \to T_{U_P} \xrightarrow{d\pi} \mathcal{O}_{U_P}^{\oplus 2} \to (\pi^*T_S/T_L)_P \to 0$ has $c_1(T_{U_P}) = -K_P$ and localized second Chern class $c_2(T_L)_P = e(\pi^{-1}(P)) - 1 = b_0(P)$ (since each of the $b_0(P)$ point blow-ups replaces a point by $\mathbb{P}^1$ and increases the topological Euler characteristic $e$ by $+1$, so $e(L) = 24 + \sum_P b_0(P)$ while $K_P^2 = -b_0(P)$), giving $2\operatorname{ch}_2(T_L)_P = K_P^2 - 2c_2(T_L)_P = -b_0(P) - 2b_0(P) = -3b_0(P)$ and hence $2\operatorname{ch}_2((\pi^*T_S/T_L)_P) = -2\operatorname{ch}_2(T_L)_P = +3b_0(P)$. Subtracting $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau'_P) = 2c_2(\mathcal{K})_P - (K_P - D_P)^2$ from $2\operatorname{ch}_2((\pi^*T_S/T_L)_P) = 3b_0(P)$ yields $2\operatorname{ch}_2(\tau_P) = 3b_0(P) + (K_P - D_P)^2 - 2c_2(\mathcal{K})_P$. $\square$

**Proposition 3.7 (First-Order Obstruction Rank, Smoothness of the Period-Incidence Locus, and Schur-Complement Hodge-Index Identity for Smooth Embedded Lagrangian Surfaces).** *Let $\nu: L \hookrightarrow S \times Y$ be a smooth embedded $\Omega_\lambda$-Lagrangian surface with $d_1 + d_2 \ge 1$ (equivalently $d_1 \ge 1$ and $d_2 \ge 1$).*

   $$\operatorname{rk}(\mathrm{ob}_L) \;=\; \operatorname{rk}\!\bigl(r: H^2(S \times Y, \mathbb{C}) \to H^2(L, \mathbb{C})\bigr) - 2.$$
   *Furthermore, let $V := r(H^2(S \times Y, \mathbb{R})) \subset H^2(L, \mathbb{R})$ ($\dim V = \operatorname{rk}(r)$) and let $Q_V := \{[\eta] \in \mathbb{P}(V_{\mathbb{C}}) \mid \int_L \eta \wedge \eta = 0\}$ be the isotropic period quadric. Because $\int_L r(\sigma_S) \wedge \overline{r(\sigma_S)} = d_1\int_S \sigma_S \wedge \bar\sigma_S > 0$, $Q_V$ is smooth of dimension $\operatorname{rk}(r) - 2$ near $p := [r(\sigma_S)] = [\lambda\,r(\sigma_Y)]$, and the **period-incidence locus** $I := \{(b_S, b_Y) \in \operatorname{Def}(S) \times \operatorname{Def}(Y) \mid [r(p_1^*\sigma_{S,b_S})] = [r(p_2^*\sigma_{Y,b_Y})] \in Q_V\}$ is a **smooth submanifold** of $\operatorname{Def}(S) \times \operatorname{Def}(Y)$ of codimension $\operatorname{rk}(r) - 2$ with tangent space $T_0 I = \ker(\mathrm{ob}_L)$. (All-orders unobstructed lifting of $L$ over $I$ on reducible $S \times Y$ — extending Voisin [12] and Lehn [13, Theorem 3.8] from irreducible symplectic manifolds — is not claimed here and is stated as an open problem in Section 5.2.)*

2. *(Lagrangian Schur-Complement & Hodge-Index Isomonodromy Identity): Let $\Psi_L := (p_2)_*(p_1^*(-) \cup [L]): H^2(S, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^2(Y, \mathbb{Q})$. Any rational Hodge similarity $\Psi_L|_{T(S)}: T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}} \xrightarrow{\sim} T(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ realized by a Lagrangian surface has multiplier $c = d_1 d_2$ (recovering [16, v2, Proposition 3.1(a)]). Moreover, for every $v \in T^{1,1}(Y, \mathbb{R})$, the Schur-complement class $w(v) := p_1^*\Psi_L^\dagger(v) - d_1\,p_2^*v$ is orthogonal on $L$ to $\nu^*p_1^*h_S$ ($\int_L(\nu^*p_1^*h_S)^2 = d_1 h_S^2 > 0$) and has $\int_L(\nu^*w(v))^2 = d_1(d_1 d_2\langle v,v\rangle_Y - \langle \Psi_L^\dagger(v), \Psi_L^\dagger(v)\rangle_S) = 0$, which by the Hodge Index Theorem on $L$ forces*
   $$\boxed{\; \mathrm{ob}_L\bigl(w(v)\bigr) \;=\; \nu^*\bigl(p_1^*\Psi_L^\dagger(v) - d_1\,p_2^*v\bigr) \;=\; 0 \quad \text{in } H^{1,1}(L, \mathbb{R}) \qquad \forall\,v \in T^{1,1}(Y, \mathbb{R}), \;}$$
   *so $\Gamma_{\Psi_L, T^{1,1}}^- := \{(\Psi_L^\dagger(v), -d_1 v) \mid v \in T^{1,1}(Y)\} \subset \ker(\mathrm{ob}_L)$ and $\operatorname{rk}(\mathrm{ob}_L) \le 20 + \rho(S)$ unconditionally.*

*Proof.* (1) Because $\nu^*\sigma_S = \lambda\,\nu^*\sigma_Y$ and $d_1 + d_2 \ge 1$, $\int_L \nu^*\sigma_S \wedge \nu^*\bar\sigma_S = d_1\int_S \sigma_S\wedge\bar\sigma_S = |\lambda|^2 d_2\int_Y \sigma_Y\wedge\bar\sigma_Y > 0$, so $d_1, d_2 \ge 1$ and $\nu^*\sigma_S \neq 0$ in $H^{2,0}(L)$. Contracting $\nu^*(\kappa_S, \kappa_Y)$ with $\Omega_\lambda = p_1^*\sigma_S - \lambda p_2^*\sigma_Y$ identifies $\mathrm{ob}_L: H^1(S, T_S) \oplus H^1(Y, T_Y) \to H^1(L, \Omega^1_L)$ with $r^{1,1}: H^{1,1}(S) \oplus H^{1,1}(Y) \to H^{1,1}(L)$ (cf. Lehn [13, Lemma 2.5]). Since $r = r^{2,0} \oplus r^{1,1} \oplus r^{0,2}$ has $\ker(r^{2,0}) = \mathbb{C}\Omega_\lambda$ and $r^{2,0}(p_1^*\sigma_S) = \nu^*\sigma_S \neq 0$, $\operatorname{rk}(r^{2,0}) = \operatorname{rk}(r^{0,2}) = 1$ and $\operatorname{rk}(\mathrm{ob}_L) = \operatorname{rk}(r) - 2$. At $p = [r(\sigma_S)] \in Q_V \subset \mathbb{P}(V_{\mathbb{C}})$, $\langle r(\sigma_S), \overline{r(\sigma_S)}\rangle_L > 0$ implies $r(\sigma_S)$ is not in the radical of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_L|_V$, so $Q_V$ is smooth of dimension $\operatorname{rk}(r) - 2$ near $p$ with $T_p Q_V = r(\sigma_S)^\perp/\mathbb{C}r(\sigma_S) = V^{1,1} = \operatorname{im}(\mathrm{ob}_L)$. By local Torelli for $S$ and $Y$, the differential of $(b_S, b_Y) \mapsto (f_S(b_S), f_Y(b_Y)) \in Q_V \times Q_V$ transverse to $\Delta_{Q_V}$ at $0$ is $(df_S)_0 - \lambda(df_Y)_0 = \mathrm{ob}_L: T_0\operatorname{Def}(S) \oplus T_0\operatorname{Def}(Y) \twoheadrightarrow T_p Q_V$, so by the implicit function theorem $I = \{f_S(b_S) = f_Y(b_Y)\}$ is smooth of codimension $\operatorname{rk}(r) - 2$ with $T_0 I = \ker(\mathrm{ob}_L)$.

(2) Expanding the Gram matrix $G_L = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} d_1 I_S & \Psi_L^\dagger \\ \Psi_L & d_2 I_Y \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ on $H^2(S) \oplus H^2(Y)$ with $(\sigma_S, -\lambda\sigma_Y) \in \ker(G_L)$ gives $\Psi_L(\sigma_S) = d_2\lambda\sigma_Y$, $\Psi_L^\dagger(\sigma_Y) = \frac{d_1}{\lambda}\sigma_S$, and $c = d_1 d_2$ [16, Proposition 3.1(a)]. Direct expansion (certified in `verify_symplectic_surface_semiregularity.py`) gives $\langle (u, 0), w(v)\rangle_L = 0$ and $\langle w(v), w(v)\rangle_L = d_1(d_1 d_2\langle v,v\rangle_Y - \langle \Psi_L^\dagger(v), \Psi_L^\dagger(v)\rangle_S) = 0$ for $v \in T^{1,1}(Y, \mathbb{R})$, so negative-definiteness of the intersection pairing on $(\nu^*p_1^*h_S)^\perp \cap H^{1,1}(L, \mathbb{R})$ forces $\nu^*w(v) = 0$. $\square$

## 4. Program II: Bifactor Hermite–Einstein Splitting, Hyper-Kähler Triple Intertwining, and the Fixed-Locus Dichotomy for Automorphic K3 Seed Bundles

We next study Verbitsky's hyperholomorphic bundle mechanism [11] on $Y = S_0 \times S_0'$ (following `hodge_conjecture_77` [15, v1, §5]; compare Hartlieb–Shah [21] and `hodge_conjecture_87` [22, v1]), where rational Hodge similarities arise from $\kappa_2(E) := \operatorname{ch}_2(E) - \frac{1}{2r}c_1(E)^2 = -c_2(E) + \frac{r-1}{2r}c_1(E)^2 \in H^4(Y, \mathbb{Q})$ of a rank-$r$ bundle $E$. Throughout Section 4, every Fourier–Mukai transform $\Phi_K: D^b(S_0') \to D^b(S_0)$ and its cohomological transform $\Phi_K^{\widetilde{H}}: \widetilde{H}(S_0', \mathbb{Z}) \to \widetilde{H}(S_0, \mathbb{Z})$, $\Phi_K^{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha) := p_{1*}\bigl(v(K) \cup p_2^*\alpha\bigr)$, act **from the second factor $S_0'$ to the first factor $S_0$** ($p_1: S_0 \times S_0' \to S_0$, $p_2: S_0 \times S_0' \to S_0'$). In particular, for any automorphism $h \in \operatorname{Aut}(S_0')$, the projection formula $p_1 \circ (\mathrm{id}_{S_0} \times h) = p_1$ and $p_2 \circ (\mathrm{id}_{S_0} \times h) = h \circ p_2$ gives on $D^b$ and on cohomology:
$$\Phi_{(\mathrm{id}_{S_0} \times h)^*K}(\mathcal{F}) \;\cong\; \mathbf{R}p_{1*}\Bigl(K \otimes^{\mathbf{L}} p_2^*\bigl(\mathbf{R}h_*\mathcal{F}\bigr)\Bigr) \;=\; \Phi_K\bigl(h_*\mathcal{F}\bigr) \;=\; \Phi_K\bigl((h^{-1})^*\mathcal{F}\bigr), \qquad \Phi_{(\mathrm{id}_{S_0} \times h)^*K}^{\widetilde{H}} \;=\; \Phi_K^{\widetilde{H}} \circ h_* \;=\; \Phi_K^{\widetilde{H}} \circ (h^{-1})^*.$$
Hence for $h = g^{-1}$ (where $(\mathrm{id}_{S_0} \times g^{-1})^{-1}(\Delta) = \Gamma_g = \{(x, g(x))\}$), pulling back by $\mathrm{id}_{S_0} \times g^{-1}$ composes on the right with $(g^{-1})_* = g^*$ on $\widetilde{H}(S_0, \mathbb{Z})$. Passing from a kernel $E$ to an algebraic cycle realizing a nonsquare Hodge similarity requires four steps in sequence:
1. **Exact Mukai Similarity:** $(\Phi_E^{\widetilde{H}})^T G_{\mathrm{Mukai}} \Phi_E^{\widetilde{H}} = c\,G_{\mathrm{Mukai}}$ on $\widetilde{H}(S_0', \mathbb{Z}) \to \widetilde{H}(S_0, \mathbb{Z})$ with nonsquare multiplier $c \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \setminus (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$.
2. **Mixed $H^2 \otimes H^2$ Discriminant Class:** Stripping the rational B-fields $\frac{1}{r}c_1(E) = p_1^*\beta_1 + p_2^*\beta_2$ identifies $[\kappa_2(E)]_{H^2(S_0)\otimes H^2(S_0')} = [v(E)_{\mathrm{stripped}}]_{H^2\otimes H^2} = -[\Gamma_{\Psi_E}]$, where $\Psi_E := -\Phi_{E,\mathrm{stripped}}|_{H^2(S_0')}: H^2(S_0', \mathbb{Q}) \to H^2(S_0, \mathbb{Q})$ satisfies $q_{S_0}(\Psi_E x, \Psi_E y) = c\,q_{S_0'}(x, y)$.
3. **Hyper-Kähler Triple Intertwining:** Choosing Ricci-flat hyper-Kähler triples $(\omega_{1,I}, \omega_{1,J}, \omega_{1,K})$ on $S_0$ and $(\omega_{2,I}, \omega_{2,J}, \omega_{2,K})$ on $S_0'$ intertwined by $\Psi_E$ (both in Kähler class and in $(2,0)$-phase) so that $\int_Y \kappa_2(E) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = 0$.
4. **Slope Polystability:** $\omega_I(t)$-slope polystability of $E$ on $Y$ (for a single $t > 0$, hence for all $t > 0$ by Theorem 4.1(4)), ensuring via Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau [9, 10] and Verbitsky [11] that $\kappa_2(E)$ remains of type $(2,2)$ along the twistor family.

### 4.1. Pointwise Chern–Weil Splitting, Metric Spectrum, and the Hyper-Kähler Triple Intertwining Criterion

Let $Y = S_0 \times S_0'$ carry the product Ricci-flat hyper-Kähler metric $g_Y(t) = p_1^* g_1 \oplus t\, p_2^* g_2$ ($t > 0$), where $g_1, g_2$ have hyper-Kähler triples $(\omega_{1,I}, \omega_{1,J}, \omega_{1,K})$ and $(\omega_{2,I}, \omega_{2,J}, \omega_{2,K})$ ($\omega_{m,A}^2 = 2\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_m}$, $\int_{S_m}\omega_{m,A}\wedge\omega_{m,B} = h_m\delta_{AB}$), and form the diagonal hyper-Kähler triple $\omega_A(t) := p_1^*\omega_{1,A} + t\,p_2^*\omega_{2,A}$ ($A \in \{I, J, K\}$), with $\mathrm{vol}_{g_Y(t)} = t^2\,p_1^*\mathrm{vol}_{g_1} \wedge p_2^*\mathrm{vol}_{g_2}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t) := 2\omega_I(t)^2 - \omega_J(t)^2 - \omega_K(t)^2$.

**Theorem 4.1 (Pointwise Chern–Weil Splitting, Metric Eigenvalues, Hyper-Kähler Triple Intertwining, and Bifactor Slope Inequality).**
1. *At every point of $Y = S_0 \times S_0'$, the 16-dimensional bundle $\Lambda^{1,1}_I(T^*Y)$ splits $g_Y(t)$-orthogonally into $\mathbb{R}\omega_I(t)$ (1D), the $t$-independent $10$-dimensional bundle $W_{\mathrm{inv}} = (\Lambda^2 T^*Y)^{\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{diag}}} = p_1^*\Lambda^2_- T^*S_0 \oplus p_2^*\Lambda^2_- T^*S_0' \oplus (p_1^*T^*S_0 \otimes p_2^*T^*S_0')^{\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{diag}}}$, and a $5$-dimensional orthogonal complement $W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)$.*
2. *(Exact Metric Operator vs. Coordinate-Volume Matrix): Define the pointwise Chern–Weil bilinear form $Q_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)(\alpha, \beta) := *_{g_Y(t)}(\alpha \wedge \beta \wedge \Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t))$ and its $g_Y(t)$-self-adjoint operator $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)$ by $Q_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)(\alpha, \beta) = \langle \alpha, \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)\beta \rangle_{g_Y(t)}$. Then $Q_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)$ vanishes identically on $W_{\mathrm{inv}}$, while on $W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)$:*
   $$\boxed{\; \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)\big|_{W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)} \;=\; -8\,I_5 \qquad \forall\,t > 0 \quad (\text{all } 5 \text{ metric eigenvalues equal to } -8). \;}$$
   *(Equivalently, in local $g_1$- and $g_2$-orthonormal coframes $(e_0,\dots,e_3)$ and $(f_0,\dots,f_3)$ with fixed coordinate volume $\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{coord}} := e_0\wedge\dots\wedge f_3 = t^{-2}\mathrm{vol}_{g_Y(t)}$, the orthogonal basis of $W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)$ constructed over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ in Section 1 of `test_automorphic_seed_bundles.py` has coordinate-wedge matrix $Q_{\mathrm{coord}}(t) = \operatorname{diag}(-32t, -32t, -32t, -32t, -32)$ and $g_Y(t)$-Gram matrix $G_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t) = \operatorname{diag}(4/t, 4/t, 4/t, 4/t, 4/t^2)$, yielding $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{metric}}(t)|_{W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)} = t^{-2}G_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)^{-1}Q_{\mathrm{coord}}(t) = -8\,I_5$.)*
3. *(Exact Cohomological Pairing and Hyper-Kähler Triple Intertwining Condition): An $\omega_I(t)$-slope-polystable bundle $E$ on $Y$ is projectively hyperholomorphic along the twistor line of $(\omega_I(t), \omega_J(t), \omega_K(t))$ if and only if $\int_Y \kappa_2(E) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = 0$ [9, 10, 11]. If $[\kappa_2(E)]_{H^2(S_0)\otimes H^2(S_0')} = -[\Gamma_{\Psi_E}]$ for a linear map $\Psi_E: H^2(S_0', \mathbb{R}) \to H^2(S_0, \mathbb{R})$, then*
   $$\boxed{\; \int_{S_0 \times S_0'} \kappa_2(E) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] \;=\; -2t\Bigl(2\,q_{S_0}\!\bigl([\omega_{1,I}], \Psi_E[\omega_{2,I}]\bigr) - q_{S_0}\!\bigl([\omega_{1,J}], \Psi_E[\omega_{2,J}]\bigr) - q_{S_0}\!\bigl([\omega_{1,K}], \Psi_E[\omega_{2,K}]\bigr)\Bigr). \;}$$
   *If $\Psi_E$ is a Hodge similarity of multiplier $c > 0$ carrying the positive cone of $H^{1,1}(S_0', \mathbb{R})$ to that of $H^{1,1}(S_0, \mathbb{R})$, then $\int_Y \kappa_2(E) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] \le 0$ for every choice of factor hyper-Kähler triples, with equality $= 0$ **if and only if** the triples satisfy the **$\Psi_E$-Intertwining Condition***
   $$[\omega_{1,A}] \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{h_1}{c\,h_2}}\,\Psi_E\bigl([\omega_{2,A}]\bigr) \qquad \forall\,A \in \{I, J, K\} \qquad \left(\text{i.e., } [\omega_{1,A}] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\,\Psi_E\bigl([\omega_{2,A}]\bigr) \text{ when } h_1 = h_2\right).$$
   *(In particular, preserving the oriented positive $3$-plane alone is insufficient: on $S_0 \times S_0$, $\mathcal{E}_{3,5}$ has $c = 1$, $\Psi_{\mathcal{E}_{3,5}}(3u_1+5u_2) = 3u_1+5u_2$, and $\Psi_{\mathcal{E}_{3,5}}|_{T(S_0)} = -\mathrm{id}_{T(S_0)}$, so identical triples with $[\omega_{1,A}] = [\omega_{2,A}]$ and $[\omega_{m,I}] = 3u_1+5u_2$ ($h_1 = h_2 = 30$) give $\int_{S_0\times S_0} \kappa_2(\mathcal{E}_{3,5})\cup[\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = -2t(60 + 30 + 30) = -240t < 0$, whereas negating the $(2,0)$-form on the first factor, $[\omega_{1,J/K}] = -[\omega_{2,J/K}] = \Psi_{\mathcal{E}_{3,5}}[\omega_{2,J/K}]$, leaves $g_1$ and $\omega_{1,I}$ unchanged and gives $\int_{S_0\times S_0} \kappa_2(\mathcal{E}_{3,5})\cup[\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = 0$.)*
4. *Every $\alpha \in W_{\mathrm{inv}}$ satisfies $\Lambda_{p_1^*\omega_{1,I}}\alpha = 0$ and $\Lambda_{p_2^*\omega_{2,I}}\alpha = 0$ separately. Hence any projectively hyperholomorphic bundle $E$ is $\omega_I(t)$-slope-polystable for all $t \in (0, \infty)$ (no $t$-walls exist) and every proper nonzero subsheaf $F \subset E$ satisfies the **Bifactor Slope Inequality** $\mu_{1,\omega_{1,I}}(F) \le \mu_{1,\omega_{1,I}}(E)$ and $\mu_{2,\omega_{2,I}}(F) \le \mu_{2,\omega_{2,I}}(E)$.*

*Proof.* Parts (1), (2), and separate factor primitivity in (4) are linear-algebraic identities on the $28$-dimensional exterior square $\Lambda^2 T^*Y$, proved symbolically over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ in Section 1 of `test_automorphic_seed_bundles.py`. Writing the trace-free Hermite–Einstein curvature [9, 10] as $i F_\nabla^0 = \alpha_{\mathrm{inv}} + \alpha_{\mathrm{non-inv}} \in \mathcal{A}^2((W_{\mathrm{inv}} \oplus W_{\mathrm{non-inv}}(t)) \otimes i\mathfrak{u}(E)_0)$, Chern–Weil integration of $\kappa_2(E) = [\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\operatorname{tr}((i F_\nabla^0)\wedge(i F_\nabla^0))]$ gives $8\pi^2 \int_Y \kappa_2(E) \cup [\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = -8 \int_Y |\alpha_{\mathrm{non-inv}}|_{g_Y(t)}^2\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_Y(t)} \le 0$, with equality iff $\alpha_{\mathrm{non-inv}} \equiv 0$, i.e., iff $E$ is projectively hyperholomorphic [11, Theorems 2.3 and 3.19]. For (3), since $\omega_{m,I}^2 = \omega_{m,J}^2 = \omega_{m,K}^2 = 2\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_m}$, the pure-factor terms in $\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)$ cancel identically, leaving $\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t) = 2t\bigl(2\,p_1^*\omega_{1,I}\wedge p_2^*\omega_{2,I} - p_1^*\omega_{1,J}\wedge p_2^*\omega_{2,J} - p_1^*\omega_{1,K}\wedge p_2^*\omega_{2,K}\bigr)$. Pairing against $[\kappa_2(E)]_{H^2\otimes H^2} = -[\Gamma_{\Psi_E}]$ yields the boxed formula. When $\Psi_E$ is a positive-cone-preserving Hodge similarity of multiplier $c > 0$, Lorentzian reverse Cauchy–Schwarz on $(H^{1,1}(S_0, \mathbb{R}), q_{S_0})$ of signature $(1, 19)$ gives $q_{S_0}([\omega_{1,I}], \Psi_E[\omega_{2,I}]) = \sqrt{c h_1 h_2}\cosh\eta \ge \sqrt{c h_1 h_2}$ (with equality iff $[\omega_{1,I}] = \sqrt{\frac{h_1}{c h_2}}\Psi_E[\omega_{2,I}]$), while on the $1$-dimensional space $H^{2,0}$, $\Psi_E[\omega_{2,J} + i\omega_{2,K}] = \sqrt{\frac{c h_2}{h_1}}e^{i\phi}[\omega_{1,J} + i\omega_{1,K}]$ gives $q_{S_0}([\omega_{1,J}], \Psi_E[\omega_{2,J}]) + q_{S_0}([\omega_{1,K}], \Psi_E[\omega_{2,K}]) = 2\sqrt{c h_1 h_2}\cos\phi \le 2\sqrt{c h_1 h_2}$ (with equality iff $e^{i\phi} = 1$). Hence $\int_Y \kappa_2(E)\cup[\Phi_{\mathrm{CW}}(t)] = -4t\sqrt{c h_1 h_2}(\cosh\eta - \cos\phi) \le 0$, vanishing iff $\eta = 0$ and $e^{i\phi} = 1$. Part (4) then follows from the Chern–Weil subsheaf formula [3, p. 78]. $\square$

### 4.2. 2-Twist Single-Bundle Realization, Exact Similarity Criterion, and the Fixed-Locus Dichotomy ($p = 2$ vs. $p = 3$)

To bypass Nikulin's discriminant-gluing parity obstruction [4] while achieving exact integral Mukai similarities of prime multiplier $p \in \{2, 3\}$ on all 24 dimensions of $\widetilde{H}(S_0, \mathbb{Z})$, we consider elliptic K3 surfaces $\pi: S_0 \to \mathbb{P}^1_t$ with section $E$ ($E^2 = -2$) and fiber $F$ ($F^2 = 0, E \cdot F = 1$) having unimodular $\operatorname{NS}(S_0) = U = \mathbb{Z}u_1 \oplus \mathbb{Z}u_2$ ($u_1 := E+F, u_2 := F$, $\operatorname{Amp}(U) = \{x u_1 + y u_2 \mid y > x > 0\}$), signature-$(2, 18)$ transcendental lattice $T(S_0) \cong U^{\oplus 2} \oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus 2}$, and a non-symplectic automorphism $g \in \operatorname{Aut}(S_0)$ fixing $U$:
- **Order $4$ ($p = 2$, Artebani–Sarti [20, Example 6.3]):** $S_0: y^2 = x^3 + A_8(t^2)x + t B_{10}(t^2)$ with $g_4(x,y,t) = (-x, -iy, -t)$. For a generic member (smooth fibers over $t = 0, \infty$), $g_4$ moves the base $\mathbb{P}^1_t$ via $t \mapsto -t$, and its fixed locus $Z_4 := \operatorname{Fix}(g_4) = \Gamma_{g_4} \cap \Delta$ consists of **$4$ isolated points** (codimension $2$ on $S_0$), with $\operatorname{Tr}_{T(S_0)}(g_4^*) = 0$.
- **Order $3$ ($p = 3$, Artebani–Sarti [5, Proposition 4.2]):** $S_0: y^2 = x^3 + p_{12}(t)$ with $g_3(x,y,t) = (\zeta_3 x, y, t)$. Here $g_3$ acts trivially on $\mathbb{P}^1_t$, and its fixed locus $Z_3 := \operatorname{Fix}(g_3) = \Gamma_{g_3} \cap \Delta = E \sqcup C_5$ is a **smooth divisor** ($g(E) = 0, g(C_5) = 5$, codimension $1$ on $S_0$) of class $[Z_3] = 3E + 4F = 3u_1 + u_2$, with $\operatorname{Tr}_{T(S_0)}(g_3^*) = -10$.

Let $T_{\mathcal{O}} \in \operatorname{Aut}(D^b(S_0))$ be the Seidel–Thomas spherical twist [8] along $\mathcal{O}_{S_0}$. In $D^b(S_0)$, $T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1]$ has kernel $\mathcal{I}_\Delta \in \operatorname{Coh}(S_0 \times S_0)$ in degree $0$ and categorical inverse $T_{\mathcal{O}}^{-1}[1]$, while on $\widetilde{H}(S_0, \mathbb{Z})$ both induce the involution $s_{\mathcal{O}} := s_{(1,0,1)}$ ($s_{\mathcal{O}}(r, l, s) = (s, -l, r)$ on $H^0 \oplus U \oplus H^4$ and $-\mathrm{id}_{T(S_0)}$).

**Theorem 4.2 (2-Twist Single-Bundle Realization, Exact Similarity Criterion, Fixed-Locus Dichotomy, and Distinct 3-Summand Bundle at $p=3$).**
1. *(Derived Autoequivalence Existence vs. Degree-0 Vector-Bundle Realization):*
   - *(a) For $p \in \{2, 3\}$, the 3-twist derived autoequivalence pair $\Phi_1 := T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1] \circ e^{u_1+(p+1)u_2} \circ T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1] \circ e^{u_1+2u_2} \circ T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1]$ and $\Phi_2 := e^{u_2} \circ (g^{-1})_* \circ \Phi_1 \circ e^{p u_1}$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(D^b(S_0))$ satisfies $(\Phi_E^{\widetilde{H}})^T G_{\mathrm{Mukai}} \Phi_E^{\widetilde{H}} = p\,G_{\mathrm{Mukai}}$ for $\Phi_E^{\widetilde{H}} := (p-1)\Phi_1^{\widetilde{H}} + \Phi_2^{\widetilde{H}}$ and $-\Phi_{E,\mathrm{stripped}}|_U = \operatorname{diag}(1, p)_U$ preserving $\operatorname{Amp}(U)$. Likewise, for $\Psi_{a,b} := T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1] \circ e^{a u_1 + b u_2} \circ T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1]$, $\widetilde{\Phi}_1 := \Psi_{a_1,b_1}$, $\widetilde{\Phi}_2 := \Psi_{a_1,b_1+1} \circ (g^{-1})_* \circ e^{p u_1}$, and $\widehat{\Phi}_i := T_{\mathcal{O}}[-1] \circ e^{a_2 u_1 + b_2 u_2} \circ \widetilde{\Phi}_i$, one has $-\widehat{\Phi}_{E,\mathrm{stripped}}|_U = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, p/\lambda_1)_U$ with $\lambda_1 = \frac{p(a_2 b_1 - 1) + a_2}{a_1 b_2 - 1}$ (giving $\lambda_1 = 1$ at $(a_1,b_1) = (3,5)$ and $(a_2,b_2) = (5,18)$ for $p=2$ or $(5,26)$ for $p=3$). 5. **Chart 4A ($E_4 \setminus \{u = 0\}$, including $E_3' \cap E_4$):** Coordinates $(\tau, \sigma) \in \mathbb{C}^2$ with $t = T_2 + \tau$, $s = \tau\sigma$, $\pi(\tau, \sigma) = (x, y) = ((\tau\sigma)^2(T_2 + \tau),\; (\tau\sigma)^3(T_2 + \tau)^2)$, $E_4 = \{\tau = 0\}$, $E_3' = \{\sigma = 0\}$, and $E_3' \cap E_4 = \{(0, 0)\}$. Target coordinates $(X_7, Y_7)$ are polynomial in $(\tau, \sigma)$ because $s^2 = \tau^2\sigma^2$ and $s^3 = \tau^3\sigma^3$ cancel $\tau^{-2}$ in $\Phi_X$ and $\tau^{-3}$ in $\Phi_Y$. The transition matrix $A_{4A}$ is
   $$A_{4A} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{3}\sigma^2\tau & 0 \\ \frac{4(1+\omega)}{3}\sigma^2 & \sigma \end{pmatrix} \qquad \left(\det A_{4A} = \tfrac{1}{3}\sigma^3\tau \implies r_{E_3'} = 3,\; r_{E_4} = 1\right).$$
   Every entry of $\widetilde{M}_{4A} = J_{4A} A_{4A}^{-1}$ is a **polynomial** in $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)[\tau, \sigma]$ across all $4$ rows and both columns, with $\widetilde{M}_{4A,00}|_{\tau=0} = \frac{9(1-\omega)}{2} \in \mathbb{Q}(\omega)^\times$ and stripped minor $\widetilde{M}_{4A,02}|_{\tau=0} = -\frac{2187(1-\omega)}{64} \neq 0$, so $l_0 = 0$ on Chart 4A.

6. **Chart 4B ($E_4 \setminus E_3'$, including $u = 0$):** Coordinates $(\tau, u) \in \mathbb{C}^2$ with $\tau = s$, $t = T_2 + \tau u$ ($u = \sigma^{-1}$ on Chart 4A), $\pi(\tau, u) = (x, y) = (\tau^2(T_2 + \tau u),\; \tau^3(T_2 + \tau u)^2)$, and $E_4 = \{\tau = 0\}$ ($u \in \mathbb{C}$ parametrizes $E_4 \setminus E_3'$). As $u \to 0$, $X_7 = O(u^{-2})$ and $Y_7 = O(u^{-3})$ approach the section at infinity $O_Y \subset Y$. Near $O_Y$, standard Weierstrass inhomogeneous coordinates at infinity on $Y: y^2 - x^3 = b$ are $(z, \zeta) := (X_7/Y_7,\; 1/Y_7)$, which satisfy the local equation $F(z, \zeta, b) := \zeta - z^3 - b\zeta^3 = 0$. Along $\tau = 0$ ($E_4 \setminus E_3'$), $b = \tau^6(T_2 + \tau u)^3(\tau(T_2 + \tau u) - 1) = 0$, $z = u R(T_2 + \tau u)$ with $R(T_2) = \frac{8(1+2\omega)}{27}$ and $R'(T_2) = \frac{32(1-\omega)}{81} = -\frac{4(1+\omega)}{3}R(T_2)$, and $\zeta = z^3 + b\zeta^3$ is a regular polynomial in $u$ at $\tau = 0$, so $\frac{\partial F}{\partial\zeta}\big|_{\tau=0} = (1 - 3b\zeta^2)\big|_{\tau=0} = 1 \neq 0$ for **all** $u \in \mathbb{C}$. By the implicit function theorem, **$(z, b) = (X_7/Y_7,\; y^2 - x^3)$ are étale local coordinates on the target K3 surface $Y$ along all of $f(E_4 \setminus E_3')$**, with $dX_7 \wedge dY_7 = -\frac{dz \wedge db}{1 - 3b\zeta^2}$. Using the **triangular transition matrix**
   $$A_{4B} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \tau & 0 \\[2pt] -\frac{4(1+\omega)}{3}u^2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \qquad (\det A_{4B} = \tau \implies r_{E_4} = 1),$$
   the linear combination $\widetilde{M}_{4B} = J_{4B} A_{4B}^{-1}$ cancels the simple pole $\frac{u^2 R'(T_2)}{\tau}$ in $\tau^{-1}\partial_\tau z$ via $-\frac{4(1+\omega)}{3}u^2\partial_u z$, yielding a holomorphic saturated-kernel frame across all $4$ rows and both columns at $\tau = 0$:
   $$\widetilde{M}_{4B}\big|_{\tau = 0} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \frac{3(1-\omega)}{2} & 0 \\[2pt] 0 & 0 \\[2pt] \frac{256}{729}u^3 & \frac{8(1+2\omega)}{27} \\[2pt] 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \widetilde{M}_{4B,00}\big|_{\tau=0} \;=\; \frac{3(1-\omega)}{2} \;\in\; \mathbb{Q}(\omega)^\times, \qquad \widetilde{M}_{4B,02}\big|_{\tau=0} \;=\; \frac{4(1+\omega)}{3} \;\neq\; 0,$$
   so $l_0 = 0$ on Chart 4B as well.
