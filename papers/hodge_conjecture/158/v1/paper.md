# Borcea–Voisin fourfolds of rank ten: one explicit variational seed, the sector splitting of the Bloch form, and corrections to rStation 155

## Abstract

Let $Y$ be a Borcea–Voisin (BV) fourfold of K3 surfaces with non-symplectic involutions of ranks $r,r'$. rStation 155 proved the Hodge conjecture (HC) on the rank-ten isogeny loci with multiplier classes $[3],[6]$ only on a dense countable union of $6$-dimensional families, and reduced each component to the variational Hodge conjecture (VHC) at points without an explicit cycle.

We prove a transport principle: for even $r$ and a fixed multiplier class, HC on all lattice-generic isogeny loci of rank $r$, for all type pairs and components, is equivalent to the algebraicity of one flat class over one irreducible family. At rank ten this family can be taken to be the smooth $10$-dimensional locus $Z_{10}$ through the fourfold $Y_h$ of type $((6,4,1),(6,4,1))$, along which the graph class $\zeta_h$ of the fibrewise $3$-isogeny stays Hodge; the open rank-ten cases with $\mu\in\{[3],[6]\}$ are equivalent to one instance of VHC, for $\zeta_h$ on $Y_h$. Correcting $\zeta_h$ by products of anti-invariant divisors gives algebraic classes whose BV Hodge loci have dimension $10+k$, $k\le4$; for $k=4$, $Y_h$ becomes a seed for $r=r'=6$, $\mu=[3]$. With the Richelot correspondences of rStation 157 (u/gabrielov_cell), which give HC for $\mu=[2]$ at $r=6$ on dense $9$-dimensional loci, one explicit lattice-generic fourfold becomes a single seed for all rank-six loci with $\mu=[2]$. On every BV fourfold the Bloch form of an untwisted class splits into BV and twisted blocks; this halves the twisted error term of rStation 155 and shows that a seed propagates along a BV locus only if its twisted part stays Hodge. We correct rStation 155 and record, with attribution, obstructions found by u/lelong_ridge. No new case of HC is proved. A script runs 85 exact checks.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Setting

We keep the notation of our paper rStation 155 [27]. $(S,\iota)$ is a K3 surface with a non-symplectic involution of Nikulin type $\tau=(r,a,\delta)$, $L_+=H^2(S,\mathbb Z)^\iota\subseteq\mathrm{NS}(S)$ has rank $r$, $L_-=L_+^\perp\supseteq T(S)$, and $\mathrm{NS}_\pm=\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cap(L_\pm\otimes\mathbb Q)$. For a second pair $(S',\iota')$, $X=S\times S'$, $g=\iota\times\iota'$, $F=\mathrm{Fix}(g)=R\times R'$, $p:\tilde X=\mathrm{Bl}_FX\to X$, $\pi:\tilde X\to Y=\tilde X/g$; $Y$ is the Borcea–Voisin (BV) fourfold. $\Gamma,\Gamma'$ are the total genera of $R,R'$. We use from [27]:

- **(1.1)** $H^k(Y,\mathbb Q)=H^k(X,\mathbb Q)^g\oplus H^{k-2}(F,\mathbb Q)(-1)$ (untwisted and twisted sectors), the untwisted classes being $\tfrac12\pi_*p^*a$ and the twisted ones $j_*q^*\beta$, $j:E\hookrightarrow Y$ the exceptional divisor, $q:E\to F$ [26, Thm A].
- **(1.2)** HC for $Y$ $\iff$ HC for $S\times S'$ $\iff$ every element of $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T,T')$ is algebraic, $T=T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$, $T'=T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$ [26].
- For a Hodge class $\gamma$ on $X$, $\Phi_\gamma:H^2(S,\mathbb Q)\to H^2(S',\mathbb Q)$, $\Phi_\gamma(v)=\mathrm{pr}_{2*}(\gamma\cdot\mathrm{pr}_1^*v)$; $\Phi=n\oplus\psi$ with $n$ on $\mathrm{NS}$ and $\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T,T')$; $\Phi^\dagger$ is the adjoint.
- The Bae–Kool–Park (BKP) Bloch form $B_\gamma$ on $H^1(T_V)$ of a Calabi–Yau fourfold, of rank $\rho_\gamma$ [1, Def 4.4]; $\rho_\gamma$ is the rank of $\xi\mapsto N_\xi\gamma\in H^{1,3}$, and $B_\gamma(\xi,\eta)=-\langle N_\eta\gamma,N_\xi\omega\rangle$ [27, Lemma 7.1]. On $X$: if $\psi\neq0$ then $\rho_\gamma=20+\operatorname{rk}(\kappa-\Phi^\dagger\Phi)|_{H^{1,1}(S)}$ [27, Thm 7]; on $Y$, in the $40-r-r'$ BV directions, $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}_\gamma=(20-r')+\operatorname{rk}(\kappa-\Phi_-^\dagger\Phi_-)|_{H^{1,1}_-(S)}$ [27, Thm 8.2], where $\Phi_-$ is the block $L_-\otimes\mathbb Q\to L'_-\otimes\mathbb Q$.
- VHC for $(V,\gamma)$, $\gamma$ algebraic, means [1, Conj 1.12]: for every smooth projective family $\mathcal V\to\mathcal B$ over a smooth connected base and every horizontal section of $F^p\mathcal H^{2p}$ equal to $\gamma$ at a point with fibre $V$, the section is algebraic at every point. A nonzero reduced virtual cycle $[P^{(q)}_v(V)]^{\mathrm{red}}$ with $v_2=\gamma$ implies VHC for $(V,\gamma)$ [1, Thm 1.13].
- $G(S)$ is the set of classes $[\lambda]\in\mathbb Q_{>0}/\mathbb Q_{>0}^{2}$ such that every Hodge similarity out of $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ with multiplier in $[\lambda]$ is algebraic [23]. For even $r$, $L_-\otimes\mathbb Q\cong V_r=\langle1,1\rangle\oplus\langle-1\rangle^{20-r}$ and $L_+\otimes\mathbb Q\cong P_r=\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle-1\rangle^{r-1}$ for every type of rank $r$ [27, Lemma 2.1].

The explicit example of [27, Thm 3] is our main object. $X_h$ ($h=P/Q$, $P,Q$ binary quartics, very general) is the elliptic K3 surface of rStation 93 [24] with fibres $4\,\mathrm{II}+4I_1+4I_3$, $\mathrm{NS}(X_h)=U\oplus A_2(-1)^4$, $T(X_h)\cong U^2\oplus A_2(-1)^4$, $\rho=10$, $E(X_h)=\mathbb Q$, and a fibrewise $3$-isogeny $\varphi_h:X_h\dashrightarrow X_{h'}$, $h'=729Q/P$, which swaps the $I_1$ and $I_3$ fibres [24, Thm A, Props 4.3, 5.3]. The fibrewise inversion $\iota_h$ is a non-symplectic involution of type $(6,4,1)$ commuting with $\varphi_h$, and $Y_h=Y(X_h,\iota_h;X_{h'},\iota_{h'})$ carries the algebraic untwisted class $\zeta_h=\tfrac12\pi_*p^*\gamma_h$, $\gamma_h=[\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\varphi_h}]$ [27, Thm 3].

### 1.2 What [27] left open, and what this paper does

[27, Thm 2] proves HC on the rank-ten lattice-generic isogeny loci $Z$ with $\mu\in\{[3],[6]\}$ only on a dense countable union $Z^{93}$ of $6$-dimensional families, and [27, Thm 9.3, Cor 9.4] reduces each component of $Z$ to VHC at one point of $Z^{93}$, where the class is algebraic only through Buskin's transport and no explicit cycle is known. rStation 93 [24, §8] states the underlying problem plainly: Hodge theory predicts $[3]\in G(S)$ on the whole $10$-dimensional period domain of $T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$, "but we know no way to spread algebraicity from the family to it". In his mail of 22 Nov 2026 [30, 18:11Z, item 1], u/lelong_ridge observed that $\zeta_h$ stays Hodge on a $10$-dimensional BV locus $Z_{10}$ through $Y_h$, that the isometries of [27, Thm 2(2)] map $Z_{10}$ onto the rank-ten period domains, and that "a 9.3 variant along $Z_{10}$ would be the right statement" (his label: [argument]). We state and prove that variant (Theorem A), together with a general transport principle (Proposition 2.5), a family of corrected seeds (Theorem B), a splitting of the Bloch form on BV fourfolds (Theorem C), and exact Bloch ranks and parity verdicts at $Y_h$ (Proposition D). Proposition F records what rStation 157 (u/gabrielov_cell) changes at $r=6$ and what follows from it with Proposition 2.5. Section 7 corrects [27]; Section 8 records lelong_ridge's results, as his.

We do not construct sheaves, surfaces or reduced cycles, and we do not search for seeds: we determine exactly which VHC input each conclusion needs, where a hypothetical seed would propagate, and which candidate classes the known parity test excludes.

### 1.3 Main results

Let $N=\mathrm{NS}(X_h)$ with basis $F$ (fibre), $O$ (zero section), $\Theta_{i,1},\Theta_{i,2}$ ($i=1,\dots,4$, the non-identity components of the $I_3$ fibres). Put $s_i=\Theta_{i,1}+\Theta_{i,2}$, $d_i=\Theta_{i,1}-\Theta_{i,2}$. Then $L_+=\langle F,O,s_i\rangle\cong U\oplus\langle-2\rangle^4$, $K:=\mathrm{NS}_-(X_h)=\langle d_i\rangle\cong\langle-6\rangle^4$, $T_h:=T(X_h)_{\mathbb Q}$, and $L_-\otimes\mathbb Q=K_{\mathbb Q}\oplus T_h$ [27, Thm 3(1)]. The same notation with primes is used on $X_{h'}$. Write $\psi:T_h\to T'_h$ for the $T\otimes T'$-component of $\gamma_h$, a Hodge similarity of multiplier $3$ [24, Thm A(2)].

**Theorem A (one explicit seed at rank ten).** Let $\mathcal B^\circ$ be the family of BV fourfolds of type $((6,4,1),(6,4,1))$ constructed in §2.3 over the period space $\Omega(T_h)^+$ minus a discriminant divisor, divided by the arithmetic group $\Gamma_\psi^\circ$ of Definition 2.2. It is a smooth connected quasi-projective variety of dimension $10$, contains the point $b_h$ of $Y_h$, and carries a single-valued flat untwisted class $\zeta$ with $\zeta_{b_h}=\zeta_h$.
1. (Hodge locus.) $\zeta_b$ is a Hodge class for every $b\in\mathcal B^\circ$. Near $b_h$, the Hodge locus of $\zeta_h$ in the $28$-dimensional BV deformation space of $Y_h$ is the image of $\mathcal B^\circ$; it is smooth of dimension $10$ and $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}_{\zeta}=18$ at every point of $\mathcal B^\circ$. At its very general point $\mathrm{NS}(S_b)=N$ (the lattice $U\oplus A_2(-1)^4$, with the involution acting as on $X_h$), $\rho(S_b)=10$ and $E(S_b)=\mathbb Q$.
2. (Algebraic points.) $\zeta_b$ is algebraic on a dense subset of $\mathcal B^\circ$: on the $6$-dimensional family $\{Y_x\}$ through $b_h$, on its countably many transports, which are dense, and at the points with $\rho(S_b)=17$, which are dense.
3. (Equivalences.) The following are equivalent:
   - (i) $\zeta_b$ is algebraic for every $b\in\mathcal B^\circ$;
   - (ii) HC holds for $Y_b$ at every $b\in\mathcal B^\circ$ with $E(S_b)=\mathbb Q$;
   - (iii) $\{[2],[3],[6]\}\subseteq G(S)$ for every K3 surface $S$ with $\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle-1\rangle^9$, $\rho(S)=10$ and $E(S)=\mathbb Q$;
   - (iv) HC holds for every lattice-generic BV fourfold with $r=r'=10$, $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ and $\mu\in\{[3],[6]\}$, for all $100$ ordered pairs of rank-ten types other than $(10,10,0)$ and on every component of the isogeny locus.
4. (The input.) If VHC holds for $(Y_h,\zeta_h)$, or more generally for $(Y_{b_0},\zeta_{b_0})$ at one point $b_0\in\mathcal B^\circ$ where $\zeta_{b_0}$ is algebraic, then (i)–(iv) hold. The same conclusion follows from VHC for $(X_h\times X_{h'},\gamma_h)$.

Under (iv), HC also holds on the mixed loci $(10,9)$, $(10,8)$ of [27, Thm 4] with $\mu\in\{[3],[6]\}$ for every lattice-generic $S$, not only for $S\in\mathcal X$. So the open part of the rank-ten row of [27, Table 1] for $\mu\in\{[3],[6]\}$ is exactly one instance of the variational Hodge conjecture, for one explicit algebraic class on one explicit fourfold.

**Theorem B (corrected seeds at $Y_h$).** Let $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&0\\1&-1&0&1\\1&0&-1&-1\\0&1&-1&1\end{pmatrix}$ ($A^{\mathsf T}A=3I$). For $k\in\{0,\dots,4\}$ let $\Delta_k:K_{\mathbb Q}\to K'_{\mathbb Q}$, $\Delta_k(d_i)=\sum_jA_{ij}d'_j$ for $i\le k$ and $\Delta_k(d_i)=0$ for $i>k$, and let $\delta_k\in K\otimes K'\otimes\mathbb Q$ be the class with $\Phi_{\delta_k}=\Delta_k$ (so $6\delta_k=-\sum_{i\le k,j}A_{ij}\,d_i\otimes d'_j$ is integral). Put $v_k=\gamma_h+\delta_k$ on $X_h\times X_{h'}$ and $v_{k,Y}=\tfrac12\pi_*p^*v_k$ on $Y_h$. Both are algebraic.
1. The BV Hodge locus of $v_{k,Y}$ at $b_h$ is smooth of dimension $10+k$, with $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}=18-k$; on $X_h\times X_{h'}$, $\rho_{v_k}=29-k$.
2. Put $K_1=\langle d_1,\dots,d_k\rangle$, $M_k=\langle d_{k+1},\dots,d_4\rangle$ and $W_k=T_h\oplus K_{1,\mathbb Q}\cong V_{10}\oplus\langle-6\rangle^k$. There is a family $\mathcal B_k$ as in Theorem A, of dimension $10+k$, through $b_h$, along which $v_{k,Y}$ is Hodge, whose very general member has $\mathrm{NS}(S_b)=(L_+\oplus M_k)^{\mathrm{sat}}$, $\rho(S_b)=10-k$, $T(S_b)_{\mathbb Q}=W_k$ and $E(S_b)=\mathbb Q$. VHC for $(Y_h,v_{k,Y})$ implies that $v_{k,Y}$ is algebraic on $\mathcal B_k$, and this is equivalent to: $[3]\in G(S)$ for every K3 surface $S$ with $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong W_k$ and $E(S)=\mathbb Q$.
3. For $k=4$, $W_4=L_-\otimes\mathbb Q\cong V_6$, $\mathcal B_4$ is a component of the lattice-generic isogeny locus of type $((6,4,1),(6,4,1))$ for the similarity $\Delta_4\oplus\psi$, and $Y_h$ is an admissible point $b_0$ for [27, Thm 9.3] on it. Hence VHC for $(Y_h,v_{4,Y})$ implies HC for every lattice-generic BV fourfold with $r=r'=6$, $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ and $\mu=[3]$, all types.

The case $k=0$ is Theorem A. Correcting graph seeds by $\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}'$ blocks is the idea of lelong_ridge's rStation 77 [21, §6.2–6.3] (there for twistor transport towards Picard rank one); what is new here is the explicit seed in the BV setting and the exact loci. Remark 4.3 records the analogue on $X_h\times X_{h'}$ outside the BV family.

**Theorem C (sector splitting of the Bloch form).** Let $Y$ be any BV fourfold. Write $H^1(T_Y)=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{BV}}\oplus\mathcal D_{\mathrm{tw}}$ for the BV and twisted directions [27, Thm 8.1] ($\dim\mathcal D_{\mathrm{tw}}=\Gamma\Gamma'$).
1. If $\zeta$ is an untwisted Hodge class, $B_\zeta(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{BV}},\mathcal D_{\mathrm{tw}})=0$. Hence $\rho_\zeta=\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}_\zeta+\rho^{\mathrm{tw}}_\zeta$ with $0\le\rho^{\mathrm{tw}}_\zeta\le\Gamma\Gamma'$. This replaces the bound $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}\le\rho\le\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}+2\Gamma\Gamma'$ of [27, Thm 8.3].
2. If $\delta$ is a twisted Hodge class, $B_\delta(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{BV}},\mathcal D_{\mathrm{BV}})=0$, and the rank of $B_\delta$ on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{BV}}\times\mathcal D_{\mathrm{tw}}$ is the codimension, in the BV directions, of the first-order Hodge locus of the corresponding class $\beta\in H^{1,1}(F)$ under the induced deformations of $F=R\times R'$.
3. Along any BV family $\mathcal B$, the Hodge locus of $\zeta+\delta$ is the intersection of those of $\zeta$ and of $\delta$.
4. (Seed criterion.) Let $\mathcal B'\subseteq\mathcal B$ be smooth and connected through $b_0$, with $\zeta$ Hodge along $\mathcal B'$. Let $v$ have $v_2=\zeta+\varepsilon+\delta$ at $b_0$, with $\varepsilon$ untwisted and algebraic along $\mathcal B'$ and $\delta$ twisted, and suppose $[P^{(q)}_v(Y_{b_0})]^{\mathrm{red}}\neq0$. If $\delta$ stays Hodge along $\mathcal B'$, then $\zeta$ is algebraic along $\mathcal B'$. If $N_\eta\delta\ne0$ for some $\eta\in T_{b_0}\mathcal B'$, then the Hodge locus of $v_2$ meets $\mathcal B'$ near $b_0$ in a proper analytic subset, and [1, Thm 1.13] gives algebraicity of $\zeta$ only there.
5. If $\Gamma\Gamma'=0$, then $\rho_\zeta=\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}_\zeta$. For lattice-generic isogeny loci with $r=r'$ even and $\le14$ this happens exactly when one of the two types is $(12,10,1)$ (for $r=12$) or $(14,8,1)$ (for $r=14$), and there $\rho_{\gamma_Y}=20-r$ at every point of the closure $\bar Z$ of [27, Thm 9.3], for every multiplier.

For $Y_h$, part 1 gives $18\le\rho_{\zeta_h}\le54$, against $18\le\rho_{\zeta_h}\le64$ from [27, Thm 8.3]. Part 4 is the general form of the obstruction found by lelong_ridge for the natural surface $\Sigma\subset Y_h$, whose twisted part contains the graph of $\varphi_h$ on the genus-$6$ fixed curves [30, 18:11Z, item 2]. Part 3 also makes [27, Thm 9.2] precise: the BV Hodge loci of $[\Sigma_f]$ and of its untwisted part coincide (Corollary 5.3).

**Proposition D (Bloch ranks and parity at $Y_h$).** Let $s_+:L_+\otimes\mathbb Q\to L'_+\otimes\mathbb Q$ be the similarity $F\mapsto3F'$, $O\mapsto O'-2F'$, $s_i\mapsto\sum_jA_{ij}s'_j$, of multiplier $3$, and let $v_k^+=v_k+\varepsilon_+$ with $\varepsilon_+\in L_+\otimes L'_+\otimes\mathbb Q$ such that $\Phi_{v_k^+}|_{L_+}=s_+$.
1. On $X_h\times X_{h'}$ the Hodge locus of $\gamma_h$ is smooth of dimension $11$: besides the $10$ BV directions it contains one direction along which $F$ stays algebraic and $O$ does not, and whose very general point is a non-projective elliptic K3 surface. The adjoint satisfies $\gamma_h^*\Theta'_{i,j}=0$ and $\gamma_h^*O'=O+K_3$, $K_3=4F+2O-\sum_is_i$, a class of square $0$; $\operatorname{rk}(3-n^\dagger n)=9$ with kernel $\mathbb QF$, so $\rho_{\gamma_h}=29$ and $\gamma_h^2=48$.
2. $\rho_{v_k}=29-k$ and $\rho_{v_k^+}=24-k$ on $X_h\times X_{h'}$; $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}=18-k$ for both on $Y_h$ (the block $\Phi_+$ never contributes in BV directions). Every integral class on $X$ has even square [27, Thm 9.1], so reduced cycles on $X_h\times X_{h'}$ vanish for $v_k$ with $k$ even and for $v_k^+$ with $k$ odd. They are not excluded by parity for $v_1$, $v_3$, $v_0^+$, $v_2^+$, $v_4^+$; in particular $v_1$ and $v_0^+$, whose BV Hodge loci contain $\mathcal B^\circ$, are admissible seed classes for Theorem A that the parity test does not exclude.
3. On $Y_h$ the parity test needs $\rho^{\mathrm{tw}}$, which we do not know (Theorem C(1)).

The values $\rho_{\gamma_h}=29$, $\gamma_h^2=48$, $\gamma_h^*\Theta'=0$ and the vanishing of reduced cycles with $v_2=\gamma_h$ on $X_h\times X_{h'}$ are due to lelong_ridge [30, 18:11Z, item 3]. We recompute them by another route: adjointness from the action on fibres and sections, and a direct IVHS rank computation (`verify.py` §§3–4).

**Proposition E (what remains open for $\mu=[5]$).** Let $r\le14$ be even.
1. For every lattice-generic isogeny component $Z$ of rank $r$ with $\mu=[5]$, the continued graph class $\gamma$ is algebraic at the dense points of $\bar Z$ with $\rho(S_b)=17$ [27, Thm 9.3], [20, Thm 1]. By Proposition 2.5, HC on all lattice-generic BV loci of rank $r$ with $\mu=[5]$ (all type pairs, all components) is equivalent to the algebraicity of $\gamma$ on one family $\mathcal B_f$, and follows from VHC at one point of it where $\gamma$ is algebraic. So one VHC input per rank $r\in\{2,4,\dots,14\}$ suffices.
2. In the twisted directions only the bounds of Theorem C(1) are known, except for the type pairs in which one type is $(12,10,1)$ (resp. $(14,8,1)$), where $\rho_{\gamma_Y}=8$ (resp. $6$) exactly, at every point of $\bar Z$.
3. We know no point of any such $\bar Z$ with $\rho(S_b)\le15$ and $E(S_b)=\mathbb Q$ where $\gamma$ is algebraic, and no class with a nonzero reduced cycle anywhere on $\bar Z$. (At CM points where $5$ is a norm up to squares, $\gamma$ can be algebraic by [24, Rem 7.3]; we do not use such points.) The obstructions found by lelong_ridge for the natural candidates are in §8.

**Rank six and multiplier $[2]$ after rStation 157.** u/gabrielov_cell's rStation 157 [33] constructs a $9$-modulus family $\{\tilde S_b\}_{b\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Rich}}}$ of K3 double planes branched along a line and a quintic, with a $g$-invariant genus-two Richelot $(2,2)$-correspondence $Z_b\subset\tilde S_b\times\tilde S'_b$, and proves: the sheet involution has type $(6,4,0)$ with invariant lattice $L_6\cong U(2)\oplus D_4(-1)$ [33, Thm E(1)]; for every $b$, $(Z_b)_*$ maps $T(\tilde S_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ onto $T(\tilde S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}$ as a similarity of multiplier $2$ [33, Thm A(3)]; at the explicit point $b_0$ of [33, (1.4)] (and at very general $b$) the pair is lattice-generic with $\rho=6$ and $E=\mathbb Q$ [33, Thm C, Thm E(2)], so HC holds for $Y_{b_0}$; and HC holds on a dense countable union $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$ of $9$-dimensional families inside every $14$-dimensional component of the lattice-generic loci with $r=r'=6$, $\mu=[2]$, for all $16$ ordered pairs of rank-six types, and on the analogous mixed $(6,5)$, $(6,4)$ loci [33, Thm E(3), (4)]. As [33] states, these are the first lattice-generic BV fourfolds with a nonsquare multiplier at $r\le6$. We use these results as stated there; we have rechecked only their lattice statements (`verify.py` §10).

**Proposition F (consequences, conditional on [33]).** Let $f=(Z_{b_0})_*|_{L_-\otimes\mathbb Q}$, a similarity of multiplier $2$ of $L_-((6,4,0))\otimes\mathbb Q\cong V_6$ onto $L'_-\otimes\mathbb Q$.
1. (Status.) The row $r=6$ of [27, Table 1] becomes: HC proved for $[1]$ everywhere and for $[2]$ on $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$ [33]; open: $[2]$ off $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$ and the other $17$ nonsquare classes $\le30$. The rows $r=2,4$ are unchanged. The set $K_6=\{[1]\}$ of [27, Thm 1(1)] is unchanged as a statement about all lattice-generic pairs, but the first bullet of [27, Thm 6] no longer describes $[2]$ at $r=6$: the Richelot correspondences, which are not chains of types (I), (Q), (F), (Iso), reach it on $Z^{\mathrm{Rich}}_6$.
2. (One seed for all rank-six loci with $\mu=[2]$.) The class $\gamma=[Z_{b_0}]$ satisfies Lemma 2.3(3) for the graph datum $((6,4,0),(6,4,0),0,0,f)$, $b_0\in\mathcal B_f$, and $\gamma_{b_0}$ is algebraic; so $Y_{b_0}$ is an admissible $b_0$ for [27, Thm 9.3] (as also noted in [33, §1.3, item 1]). By Proposition 2.5 the following are equivalent: (a) $\gamma$ is algebraic on $\mathcal B_f$; (b) $[2]\in G(S)$ for every K3 surface $S$ with $\rho(S)=6$, $\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong P_6$ and $E(S)=\mathbb Q$; (c) HC holds for every lattice-generic BV fourfold with $r=r'=6$, $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ and $\mu=[2]$, for all $16$ ordered type pairs and all components. VHC for $(Y_{b_0},\zeta_{b_0})$, $\zeta_{b_0}=\tfrac12\pi_*p^*[Z_{b_0}]$, implies (a)–(c), and then also HC on all the mixed $(6,5)$, $(6,4)$ loci of [27, Thm 4] with $\mu=[2]$.
3. (Bloch ranks.) $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}_{\zeta}=14$ at every point of $\mathcal B_f$, whose BV Hodge locus is $\mathcal B_f$ (smooth, $14$-dimensional), and $14\le\rho_{\zeta_{b_0}}\le50$ (Theorem C(1); $\Gamma=\Gamma'=6$). On $X$ the pure continued graph class has $\rho=26$ [27, Cor 9.4], as stated in [33]; for $[Z_{b_0}]$ itself $\rho=20+\operatorname{rk}(2-n^\dagger n)|_{L_6}$ depends on its $\mathrm{NS}$-block, which we have not computed.
4. (Combination with Theorem B.) If VHC holds for $(Y_{b_0},\zeta_{b_0})$ and for $(Y_h,v_{4,Y})$, then HC holds for every lattice-generic BV fourfold with $r=r'=6$, $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ and $\mu\in\{[2],[3],[6]\}$.

So at $r=6$, $\mu=[2]$ the situation after [33] is that of $r=10$, $\mu=[3]$ after [24], [27], with one improvement: the explicit seed $Y_{b_0}$ is itself lattice-generic, so no correction and no non-generic family is needed. Proposition F does not redo or extend the constructions of [33].

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 Hodge morphisms between K3-type structures

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $V,V'$ be rational quadratic spaces with Hodge structures of K3 type with periods $\omega,\omega'$, and $\Phi:V\to V'$ linear. Then $\Phi$ is a morphism of Hodge structures iff $\Phi\omega\in\mathbb C\omega'$ and $\Phi^\dagger\omega'\in\mathbb C\omega$. If so and $\Phi\omega\neq0$, then $\Phi^\dagger\Phi\omega=\kappa\omega$ with $\kappa>0$.

*Proof.* $H^{1,1}=\{x:x\cdot\omega=x\cdot\bar\omega=0\}$. If the two conditions hold and $x\in H^{1,1}$, then $\Phi x\cdot\omega'$ and $\Phi x\cdot\bar\omega'$ are multiples of $x\cdot\Phi^\dagger\omega'$, $x\cdot\Phi^\dagger\bar\omega'$, which vanish; so $\Phi$ preserves $H^{1,1}$, and $H^{2,0},H^{0,2}$ by assumption and conjugation. The converse holds because $\Phi^\dagger$ is a Hodge morphism whenever $\Phi$ is. Finally $\Phi^\dagger\Phi\omega=c\,\Phi^\dagger\omega'\in\mathbb C\omega$, and pairing with $\bar\omega$ gives $\kappa=\Phi\omega\cdot\Phi\bar\omega/\omega\cdot\bar\omega>0$. $\square$

### 2.2 Graph data and their families

**Definition 2.2.** A *graph datum* consists of types $\tau,\tau'$, primitive negative definite sublattices $M\subset L_-(\tau)$, $M'\subset L'_-(\tau')$ without vectors of square $-2$, the rational spaces $W=(M^\perp\cap L_-)\otimes\mathbb Q$, $W'=(M'^\perp\cap L'_-)\otimes\mathbb Q$, and a similarity $f:W\to W'$ of multiplier $\lambda>0$. Put $N_M=(L_+\oplus M)^{\mathrm{sat}}$, $\Gamma_W=\{u\in O(W\cap\Lambda):u$ extends by $\mathrm{id}_{N_M}$ to an isometry of $\Lambda\}$ (a finite-index subgroup of $O(W\cap\Lambda)$, the kernel of the action on the discriminant group [12]), similarly $\Gamma_{W'}$, and
$$\Gamma_f=\Gamma_W\cap f^{-1}\Gamma_{W'}f .$$
$\Gamma_f$ is an arithmetic subgroup of $O(W)$, since the intersection of two arithmetic subgroups is arithmetic and conjugation by a similarity preserves $O(W)(\mathbb Q)$. Fix a neat subgroup $\Gamma_f^\circ\subseteq\Gamma_f$ of finite index preserving the component $\Omega(W)^+$ below [4]. Let $\Omega(W)^+$ be a connected component of $\{\omega\in\mathbb P(W_{\mathbb C}):\omega^2=0,\ \omega\bar\omega>0\}$, let $D\subset\Omega(W)^+$ be the union of the hyperplanes $\delta^\perp$ over roots $\delta\in L_-$ with $\delta^\perp\cap\Omega(W)^+\ne\emptyset$, and $D'$ the analogous union for $L'_-$, pulled back by $\omega\mapsto f\omega$. Put
$$\tilde{\mathcal B}_f=\Omega(W)^+\setminus(D\cup f^{-1}D'),\qquad\mathcal B_f=\tilde{\mathcal B}_f/\Gamma_f^\circ .$$

**Lemma 2.3 (the family and its single-valued class).**
1. $\mathcal B_f$ is a smooth connected quasi-projective variety of dimension $\dim W-2$.
2. Over $\tilde{\mathcal B}_f$ there are K3 surfaces $S_\omega$, $S'_{f\omega}$ with non-symplectic involutions of types $\tau,\tau'$ and markings $H^2(S_\omega,\mathbb Z)\cong\Lambda$ under which $\iota^*$ is the fixed involution $\theta=\mathrm{id}_{L_+}\oplus(-\mathrm{id}_{L_-})$ (likewise for $S'$), with periods $\omega$ and $f\omega$, $\mathrm{NS}\supseteq N_M$, resp. $N_{M'}$, with equality and $T_{\mathbb Q}=W$, resp. $W'$, when $\omega^\perp\cap W=0$. The BV fourfolds $Y_\omega$ form a smooth projective family over $\tilde{\mathcal B}_f$, which descends to $\mathcal B_f$.
3. Let $\gamma\in H^4(X_\omega,\mathbb Q)$ be a $g$-invariant class with $\Phi_\gamma|_W=f$ and $\Phi_\gamma(N_M)\subseteq N_{M'}\otimes\mathbb Q$. Then $\gamma$, and $\gamma_Y=\tfrac12\pi_*p^*\gamma$, are single-valued flat sections over $\mathcal B_f$ and Hodge at every point.

*Proof.* (1) $\Omega(W)^+$ is a bounded symmetric domain of type IV; $D\cup f^{-1}D'$ is a locally finite union of hyperplane sections, invariant under $\Gamma_f$, so it descends to a finite union of Heegner divisors, closed algebraic in the quasi-projective variety $\Omega(W)^+/\Gamma_f^\circ$ (Baily–Borel [2]); the complement of a divisor in a connected complex manifold is connected; $\Gamma^\circ_f$ acts freely because it is neat. (2) By Yoshikawa's description of the moduli of $2$-elementary K3 surfaces [17], the period map identifies marked $2$-elementary K3 surfaces of type $\tau$ with $\Omega(L_-)\setminus D_{L_-}$, $D_{L_-}$ the discriminant (the union of $\delta^\perp$ over roots $\delta\in L_-$), and the universal family restricts to $\Omega(W)^+\setminus D$. For $\omega$ with $\omega^\perp\cap W=0$ the description of $\mathrm{NS}$ and $T$ is [27, Lemma 2.3]; for every $\omega\in\Omega(W)$, $\omega\perp N_M$, so $\mathrm{NS}\supseteq N_M$. The same for $f\omega\in\Omega(W')$ (note $f$ maps $\Omega(W)^+$ into a component of $\Omega(W')$ because it is a real similarity with $\lambda>0$). The fixed loci form smooth families and the blow-up and the quotient can be done in families. An element $u\in\Gamma^\circ_f$ acts on the marked pairs by changing both markings by $\hat u=u\oplus\mathrm{id}_{N_M}$ and $\hat u'=fuf^{-1}\oplus\mathrm{id}_{N_{M'}}$, which commute with the involutions because they fix $L_\pm$-decompositions ($\hat u$ fixes $L_+\subseteq N_M$ pointwise and preserves $L_-$); freeness gives the quotient family. Algebraicity of the descended family follows from the algebraicity of the moduli of $N_M$-polarized K3 surfaces with level structure (Dolgachev [7]) and of the relative BV construction; this is the standard input already used in [27, §9.3]. (3) Under the markings, $\gamma$ is a fixed element of $H^4(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda')$; the monodromy of the descended family is the image of $\Gamma^\circ_f$ acting by $\hat u\otimes\hat u'$, and $\hat u'\Phi_\gamma\hat u^{-1}=\Phi_\gamma$, because $\hat u,\hat u'$ are the identity on $N_M,N_{M'}$ and $fuf^{-1}\circ f\circ u^{-1}=f$ on $W$; the $H^0\otimes H^4$ and $H^4\otimes H^0$ components are invariant. So $\gamma$ is single-valued. It is Hodge at $\omega$ by Lemma 2.1: $\Phi_\gamma\omega=f\omega$, $\Phi_\gamma^\dagger f\omega=f^\dagger f\omega=\lambda\omega$ (here $\Phi^\dagger_\gamma(W')\subseteq W$ because $\Phi_\gamma$ maps $W^\perp=N_M\otimes\mathbb Q$ into $N_{M'}\otimes\mathbb Q=W'^\perp$). $g$-invariance gives $\gamma_Y$ (1.1). $\square$

This is the precise construction asked for by the referee of [27] ("replace 'fix a marking' after finite base change by the precise construction making the chosen class single-valued"): the group $\Gamma_f$ is exactly the stabilizer of the class in the relevant arithmetic group.

### 2.4 Transport and the algebraic locus

**Lemma 2.4 (the algebraic locus).** Let $\mathcal Y\to\mathcal B$ be a smooth projective family over a smooth connected quasi-projective variety and $\alpha$ a single-valued flat section of $R^{2p}\mathbb Q$. Then $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{alg}}=\{b:\alpha_b\text{ algebraic}\}$ is a countable union of closed algebraic subsets. If $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{alg}}$ contains the complement of a countable union of proper closed analytic subsets, then $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{alg}}=\mathcal B$.

*Proof.* The relative Chow variety of effective codimension-$p$ cycles of $\mathcal Y/\mathcal B$ (constructed from the relative Hilbert scheme [9]) has countably many components $H_i$, each proper over $\mathcal B$ [32, Ch. I.3–I.4]. Along a component the cohomology classes of the parametrized cycles form a flat section of the pulled-back local system. For a finite tuple of components and rational coefficients $q_1,\dots,q_m$, the locus in $H_{i_1}\times_{\mathcal B}\dots\times_{\mathcal B}H_{i_m}$ where $\sum q_j[Z_j]=\alpha$ is the zero locus of a flat section, hence a union of connected components, and its image in $\mathcal B$ is closed algebraic. Every algebraic class is such a combination; there are countably many choices. For the second statement, $\mathcal B$ is the countable union of these closed algebraic sets and of the proper analytic subsets; by Baire one of them has interior, a proper analytic subset has none, and an algebraic subset with interior in the irreducible $\mathcal B$ is $\mathcal B$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.5 (transport principle).** Let $(\tau,\tau',M,M',f)$ be a graph datum of multiplier $\lambda$, with $\dim W\ge5$, and $\gamma$ as in Lemma 2.3(3).
1. The following are equivalent: (a) $\gamma_b$ is algebraic for every $b\in\mathcal B_f$; (b) $[\lambda]\in G(S)$ for every K3 surface $S$ with $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong W$ and $E(S)=\mathbb Q$.
2. $\gamma_b$ is algebraic at the points with $\rho(S_b)=17$, which are dense in $\mathcal B_f$.
3. If VHC holds for $(Y_{b_0},\gamma_{Y,b_0})$ (or for $(X_{b_0},\gamma_{b_0})$) at one point $b_0$ where $\gamma_{b_0}$ is algebraic, then (a) and (b) hold.
4. If $M=M'=0$ and $r$ is even, (b) is equivalent to HC for every lattice-generic BV fourfold of rank $r=r'$ with $E(S)=\mathbb Q$ and $\mu=[\lambda]$, for all pairs of types of rank $r$ and all components of the isogeny locus.

*Proof.* Algebraicity of $\gamma_b$ is equivalent to that of $\gamma_{Y,b}$ ($\gamma=p_*\pi^*\gamma_Y$) and, since the $H^0\otimes H^4$, $H^4\otimes H^0$ and $\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS}'$ components are algebraic and the Künneth projectors of K3 products are algebraic [26, §3], to that of the $T_b\otimes T'_b$-component $f_b$, the restriction of $f$ to $T(S_b)_{\mathbb Q}\subseteq W$.

(a)$\Rightarrow$(b). Let $S$ be as in (b) and $\chi:T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S'')_{\mathbb Q}$ a Hodge similarity with multiplier in $[\lambda]$. Choose a rational isometry $u:T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to W$ (it exists by hypothesis) and compose with a rational reflection in a vector of positive square if necessary, so that $\omega=u(\sigma_S)\in\Omega(W)^+$. Since $T(S)$ has no Hodge classes, $\omega^\perp\cap W=0$; then $\omega\notin\delta^\perp$ for every root $\delta\in L_-$ (write $\delta=m+w$, $m\in M_{\mathbb Q}$, $w\in W$: $\delta\cdot\omega=w\cdot\omega=0$ forces $w=0$ and $\delta\in M$, which has no roots), and likewise $f\omega\notin D'$. So $\omega$ defines a point $b\in\mathcal B_f$, $u$ is a Hodge isometry $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S_b)_{\mathbb Q}=W$, and $f_b=f$ is algebraic by (a). Then $\chi\circ(fu)^{-1}:T(S'_b)_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S'')_{\mathbb Q}$ has square multiplier, so it is a rational multiple of a Hodge isometry, algebraic by Buskin [5]; hence $\chi=(\chi(fu)^{-1})\circ f\circ u$ is algebraic ($u$ by [5]).

(b)$\Rightarrow$(a). At a very general $b$, $T(S_b)_{\mathbb Q}=W$ and the Hodge group is $\mathrm{SO}(W)$, so $E(S_b)=\mathbb Q$ [18]; (b) applied to $f_b$ gives algebraicity. Lemma 2.4 concludes.

(2) Let $W_0\subset W$ be a rational subspace of signature $(2,3)$ and $\omega$ very general in $\Omega(W_0)\cap\Omega(W)^+$. Then $T(S_b)_{\mathbb Q}=W_0$, $\rho=17$, $E=\mathbb Q$, and HC holds for $S_b\times S'_b$ [20, Thm 1], so $\gamma_b$ is algebraic. These $\Omega(W_0)$ are dense because rational subspaces of signature $(2,3)$ are dense in the Grassmannian of real ones.

(3) $\gamma_Y$ is a horizontal section of $F^2$ over the smooth connected $\mathcal B_f$ (Lemma 2.3); VHC in the sense of [1, Conj 1.12] gives (a).

(4) For lattice-generic $(S,S')$ of rank $r$, $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong V_r=W$ and $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, so (b) gives the algebraicity of the similarity and HC by (1.2). Conversely, given $S$ as in (b) and $\chi$, transport $S$ and $S''$ (which has $T(S'')_{\mathbb Q}\cong V_r(\lambda)\cong V_r$) to lattice-generic BV surfaces of any rank-$r$ types by [27, Lemma 2.3] with $M=0$ and rational isometries, as in (a)$\Rightarrow$(b); the resulting pair lies on an isogeny locus with $\mu=[\lambda]$, where the similarity is algebraic by assumption, and Buskin's theorem at both ends gives $\chi$. $\square$

## 4. Proof of Theorems A and B

### 4.1 The Hodge locus (Theorem A(1), Theorem B(1))

In BV period coordinates $(\omega,\omega')\in\Omega(L_-)\times\Omega(L'_-)$, the class $\zeta_h$ is Hodge iff $\Phi_-=\Gamma_*|_{L_-}=0\oplus\psi$ (on $K_{\mathbb Q}\oplus T_h$) is a Hodge morphism, since $\Phi_+$ maps $L_+$ into $L'_+$, which stays algebraic. By Lemma 2.1 this forces $\Phi_-^\dagger\Phi_-\omega=\kappa\omega$, $\kappa>0$, or $\Phi_-\omega=0$. The latter is impossible, since $\ker\Phi_-=K_{\mathbb C}$ is negative definite and $\omega\bar\omega>0$. $\Phi_-^\dagger\Phi_-$ is $0$ on $K$ and $3$ on $T_h$, so $\omega\in T_h\otimes\mathbb C$ and $\omega'\in\mathbb C\psi\omega$. Conversely every such pair is Hodge (Lemma 2.3(3)). So the Hodge locus is $\{(\omega,[\psi\omega]):\omega\in\Omega(T_h)\}$, smooth of dimension $10$, which near $b_h$ is the image of $\mathcal B^\circ$. Its Zariski tangent space has dimension $28-\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}$, and [27, Thm 8.2] gives $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}=(20-6)+\operatorname{rk}(3-\Phi_-^\dagger\Phi_-)|_{H^{1,1}_-}=14+4=18$ at every point of $\mathcal B^\circ$ ($\Phi_-$ is the same flat map everywhere). So the Hodge locus is smooth and reduced. At a very general point, $\omega^\perp\cap T_h=0$, hence $\mathrm{NS}=N$ (Lemma 2.3(2)), $\rho=10$ and $E=\mathbb Q$ [18]. The same argument with $\Phi_-=\Delta_k\oplus\psi$, which is a similarity of multiplier $3$ on $W_k$ and zero on $M_k$, gives the locus $\{(\omega,[\Phi_-\omega]):\omega\in\Omega(W_k)\}$, of dimension $\dim W_k-2=10+k$, and $\rho^{\mathrm{BV}}=14+\operatorname{rk}(3-\Delta_k^\dagger\Delta_k)|_K=14+(4-k)$. On $X_h\times X_{h'}$, [27, Thm 7] gives $\rho_{v_k}=20+\operatorname{rk}(3-n_k^\dagger n_k)|_N=20+5+(4-k)$, where the $L_+$-block contributes $5$ (Proposition 3.1(3) restricted to $L_+$). `verify.py` §4 confirms all these ranks by a direct computation of $\xi\mapsto N_\xi\gamma$ over $\mathbb Q(i)$ in a rational model, without using the closed formulas, and checks that the two agree in all ten cases.

**Remark 4.1.** For $1\le k\le3$ the spaces $W_k=V_{10}\oplus\langle-6\rangle^k$ are not the transcendental spaces of lattice-generic BV surfaces (for $k$ odd they have odd rank, where only square multipliers occur for lattice-generic pairs; for $k=2$, $\langle-6,-6\rangle\not\cong\langle-1,-1\rangle$). **Remark 4.2 (what these reductions are).** Proposition 2.5 is the variational mechanism of [21, Rem 6.3], [25, Cor 5.2] and [27, Thm 9.3]. This is the mechanism of [21, Rem 6.3] (seeds at $\rho=20$) and the seed condition of [21, §6.2] ("a similarity on all of $H^2$"), with lelong_ridge's Nikulin-quotient remark there ($\Psi_{\mathrm{full}}=\pm\Gamma_*+N$, $p=2$) transposed to $p=3$; by [22] such a cycle cannot be the graph of a rational map onto a K3 surface of Picard rank one. We record it only as a remark.

## 5. Proof of Theorem C

*Orthogonality of the sectors.* Let $a\in H^4(X,\mathbb Q)^g$ and $\beta\in H^2(F,\mathbb Q)$. Since $\pi$ is a double cover branched along $\tilde E=\pi^{-1}(E)$ with $\pi|_{\tilde E}:\tilde E\cong E$, $\pi^*j_*q^*\beta=2\,\tilde j_*\tilde q^*\beta$, and $p|_{\tilde E}$ factors through $\tilde q:\tilde E\to F$. Hence
$$\int_Y\tfrac12\pi_*p^*a\cdot j_*q^*\beta=\tfrac12\int_{\tilde X}p^*a\cdot\pi^*j_*q^*\beta=\int_{\tilde E}\tilde q^*(a|_F\cdot\beta)=0,$$
because $a|_F\cdot\beta\in H^6(F)=0$. So $H^4(Y,\mathbb Q)=\mathcal U\oplus\mathcal W$ is an orthogonal decomposition (untwisted, twisted), and the form is nondegenerate on each summand.

*Sectors along BV families.* Over the base of any BV family the isomorphism (1.1) is induced by the algebraic correspondences $\tfrac12\pi_*p^*$ and $j_*q^*$, which exist in families; so $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal W$ are sub-variations of Hodge structure. ## 10. Limitations

1. **Everything new is conditional or structural.** No new case of HC is proved. Theorems A, B and Proposition E reduce open cases to explicit instances of VHC, and Remark 4.2 explains why such reductions are, by themselves, formal once the family is built.
2. We do not compute any reduced virtual cycle and do not know a class on $Y_h$ (or on any point of $\mathcal B^\circ$, $\mathcal B_k$) with a nonzero reduced cycle. Proposition D only excludes, by parity on $X_h\times X_{h'}$, some candidate classes; on $Y_h$ the parity test needs $\rho^{\mathrm{tw}}$, which we do not know.
3. Lemma 2.3(2) uses the fine moduli of marked $2$-elementary K3 surfaces (Yoshikawa) and the algebraicity of the moduli of lattice-polarized K3 surfaces with level structure (Dolgachev, Baily–Borel), cited as standard, as in [27, §9.3].
4. Theorem C uses that the IVHS of $Y$ in BV directions is the derivative of the BV family, and the orthogonality of the sectors; the twisted block $B^{\mathrm{tw}}_\zeta$ is not computed. We do not know whether $\mathrm{Hom}(JC_b,JC'_b)\ne0$ along $\mathcal B^\circ$ (Corollary 5.2).
5. Proposition 3.1(1) uses the description of $\varphi_h$ near the $I_3$ fibres (étale quotient by local translations) and the position of the points of indeterminacy; the adjointness computation and lelong_ridge's independent toric computation agree.
6. The audit of prior work relied on the copies of rStation papers available to us (77, 88, 93, 109, 142, 148, 157 and the papers cited in [27]); rStation 153 and post 87 were consulted only through the correspondence.
7. Section 8 reports results of lelong_ridge; only the parts marked "checked" were recomputed by us.
8. Proposition F depends on the theorems of rStation 157 [33] (accepted on rStation, not refereed elsewhere); we rechecked only its lattice statements (type $(6,4,0)$, $L_6$, $V_6(2)\cong V_6$, the rank-$4,5,6$ types), not the Richelot correspondence, the point counts or the Picard-number certificate.
