# Strongly positive Hodge classes force projectivity of complex tori, and weakly positive ones do not

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-10-15T21:17:48.183822Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/2/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

On a projective variety positivity cannot obstruct the Hodge conjecture, since $\alpha+Nh^p$ is strictly positive for $N\gg0$. It can separate projective from Kähler geometry, where the Kähler analogue of the conjecture fails. We ask: if a rational Hodge class on a compact Kähler manifold lies in the interior of a positive cone, must it be analytic, or must the manifold be projective? On tori the answer depends on the cone. Main Theorem: if a rational Hodge class of degree $2p$, $1\le p\le n-1$, on an $n$-dimensional complex torus lies in the interior of the strongly positive cone, then the torus is an abelian variety. The first non-elementary case is $(n,p)=(4,2)$. The proof combines compactness of the centraliser of the complex structure in the Hodge group, forced by interior weak positivity; Deligne's Cartan-involution criterion; a Wirtinger calibration inequality excluding strongly positive classes when a compact factor of the Hodge group rotates the complex structure; and a polynomial-growth argument showing that interior weak positivity forces $H^1$ to be semisimple. Sharpness: an explicit simple non-projective 4-torus, built from a quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ whose corestriction is $M_2(B)$ with $\operatorname{disc}B=119$, has rational Hodge classes in the interior of Demailly's positive cone; they are not analytic, as the torus contains no surfaces. On compact hyperkähler manifolds no $SU(2)$-invariant class (e.g. $c_2$) is strongly positive in the interior. For the sharpness example, coherent sheaves realise either only $\mathbb{Q}\omega^2$ or all of $\mathrm{Hdg}^4$. Hyperholomorphic curvature makes the non-divisorial part of $\mathrm{ch}_2$ of a stable bundle a nonnegative binary quartic, vanishing only for projectively flat bundles. So one stable bundle that is not projectively flat would make every Hodge class a Chern class while none is analytic. Bearing on the Hodge conjecture: strongly positive Kähler counterexamples on tori are impossible, and positivity-based approaches must use projectivity essentially.
