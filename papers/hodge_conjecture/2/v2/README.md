# Strongly positive Hodge classes force projectivity of complex tori, and weakly positive ones do not

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-10-16T01:06:54.005720Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/2/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Positivity cannot obstruct the Hodge conjecture on projective varieties, since $\alpha+Nh^p$ is strictly positive for $N\gg0$. It can separate projective from Kähler geometry, where the Kähler analogue fails. We ask whether a rational Hodge class in the interior of a positive cone must be analytic, or must force projectivity. On complex tori the answer depends on the cone. Main Theorem: if a rational Hodge class of degree $2p$, $1\le p\le n-1$, on an $n$-dimensional complex torus lies in the interior of the strongly positive cone, the torus is an abelian variety; the first non-elementary case is $(n,p)=(4,2)$. The proof combines compactness of the centraliser of the complex structure in the Hodge group, Deligne's Cartan-involution criterion, a Wirtinger calibration inequality, and a growth argument showing that interior weak positivity forces $H^1$ to be semisimple. Sharpness: a simple non-projective 4-torus built from a quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ has rational Hodge classes in the interior of Demailly's positive cone, and they are not analytic. This torus is a compact-type Weil torus for every imaginary quadratic subfield of its endomorphism algebra, but no single Weil structure explains the positivity: on compact-type Weil 4-tori with generic Hodge group, no nonzero degree-4 Hodge class is even weakly positive. On hyperkähler manifolds no $SU(2)$-invariant class is strongly positive in the interior. For the sharpness example, the non-divisorial part of $\mathrm{ch}_2$ of a stable bundle is a nonnegative binary quartic that vanishes only for projectively flat bundles. So one bundle that is not projectively flat would make every Hodge class a Chern class while none is analytic. Bearing on the conjecture: strongly positive Kähler counterexamples on tori are impossible, and positivity-based approaches must use projectivity essentially.
