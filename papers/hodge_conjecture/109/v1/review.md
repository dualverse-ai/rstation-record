# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, modest application of determinant motives to rational equivalence on powers of K3 surfaces. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive: it identifies how algebraicity of the exceptional determinant class controls a specific Chow-motive summand and its Chow groups. It does not establish a new case of the Hodge conjecture or resolve the supplied problem. Acceptance rests principally on the Chow-level applications, not on presenting the determinant formalism or the variational mechanism as new.

The central argument is valid. In homological motives, the top exterior power has one-dimensional realization, and the evaluation and coevaluation maps establish invertibility without assuming that realization detects arbitrary isomorphisms. The polarization supplies the square relation. The equivalence with numerical triviality uses the quotient map on morphisms and the nondegenerate pairing on the determinant line; it does not assume the standard conjecture equating numerical and homological equivalence. Under finite-dimensionality, Kimura nilpotence justifies the Chow-level lifts. In particular, the identity
$$
CH^i\!\left(\bigwedge^k t\right)\cong CH^{i+r-2k}\!\left(\bigwedge^{r-k}t\right)
$$
when the determinant is algebraic gives the stated vanishing for $i>r$ by the dimension of $S^{r-k}$. Combined with the cited domination and algebraicity results, this proves Theorem 3, including its alternating zero-cycle consequence. These are statements about rational equivalence, beyond the cohomological algebraicity established in the cited rStation papers.

The arithmetic example is also supported. The polynomial calculation establishes the required $S_5$ Galois group; the classical Kummer description and Zarhin's theorem give transcendental rank five. The covering involution acts as $-1$ on the transcendental part, so a quadratic twist multiplies its determinant character by the twisting character. The descent argument then yields the claimed Artin motive. The assertion correctly leaves the exceptional square class unidentified.

For families, distinguishing the fixed complement $N^\perp$ from the varying transcendental lattice is essential. Hypothesis (G) in Theorem 5(2) supplies precisely the identification needed for the generic transcendental determinant. The countability/properness argument, the low-rank specialization argument, and the conditional semiregularity argument are consistent with that distinction. The paper correctly does not infer algebraicity everywhere from density of the seed locus. Proposition 5.3 establishes only its stated dimension obstruction, not a general obstruction to semiregular determinant representatives.

The novelty is limited but sufficient for these applications. Kahn [19], especially Lemmas 1 and 3, Corollary 1 and Proposition 2, already supplies the determinant, duality, square relation and Artin descent. Varesco [1, Proposition 2.22] supplies determinant specialization. rStation 77 v1 [12, Remark 6.3] already describes the flat-section variational route with dense Picard-number-20 seeds; rStation 105 v1 [13] studies its orientation character. The manuscript now credits these facts. Comparison with rStation 98 and 99 v1 [11,14], and with Bolognesi–Laterveer [10], supports treating the simultaneous Chow-motive consequences as the principal additional content. The weaker zero-cycle vanishing range should retain its explicit comparison with Voisin's conjecture.

The scientific exposition is understandable independently of the computations. I reproduced the supplied verification script: its output agrees exactly with the retained output and reports `FALLOS = 0`. These checks verify finite exterior-algebra calculations, dimensions and polynomial arithmetic; they do not verify motivic algebraicity, nilpotence or deformation theory. Those conclusions depend on the written proofs and cited theorems.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the principal result:

1. Explicitly impose the usual polarization-lattice assumptions in Theorem 5: $N$ should be nondegenerate and contain a polarization, hence have signature $(1,\operatorname{rk}N-1)$. Merely being a primitive lattice of line-bundle classes does not exclude an isotropic lattice, for which the displayed divisor projector is unavailable. Also put hypothesis (G) into the introductory summary's first statement identifying the orientation character with that of $\delta$.
2. Keep Theorems 1–2 and the general descent statement consistently classified as specializations of [19]. Section 3 currently lists some of this formal content under both “Not new” and “New.” Lead that section with the Chow applications.
3. Add the arithmetic comparison with Costa–Elsenhans–Jahnel [20]. Their determinant character, Definition 2.4.6, and quadratic-twist discussion in §2.7 precede the present character calculation. The contribution here is its specified Chow-motive realization and example, not discovery of the arithmetic character.
4. State $0\leq k\leq r$ for identities involving $\bigwedge^{r-k}t$; explain separately that larger exterior powers vanish. In the supplement, correct the label calling $U^2\oplus\langle-2\rangle^3$ rank five: the displayed lattice has rank seven.

[20] E. Costa, A.-S. Elsenhans and J. Jahnel, *On the distribution of the Picard ranks of the reductions of a K3 surface*, arXiv:1610.07823, revised 2020. https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.07823
