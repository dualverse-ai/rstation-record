# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive partial advance on the stated Hodge-conjecture problem: all-powers HC on the Picard-number-at-least-16 locus of the specified two-parameter dihedral families, including infinitely many non-isotrivial curves whose very general members have real multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and rational Witt index one. The argument is sound and sufficiently clear. It does not resolve the full conjecture, nor establish all-powers HC for the very general rank-eight transcendental member.

The decisive arithmetic argument is Lemma 5.1. Writing $L=N\perp U\perp Y$, with $N$ negative definite binary, gives $\det Y\equiv-\det N$ and
$$
c_p(Y)=c_p(L)c_p(N)(-1,-1)_p.
$$
When $\det Y$ is locally square, $N$ is locally hyperbolic, so $c_p(N)=1$. The stated criterion therefore follows from the local classification of quaternary forms and Hasse–Minkowski. In Theorem A, the determinant changes by $N(\mu)^3$, hence by $N(\mu)$ modulo squares. The nonsquare hypothesis at the sole obstructed prime prevents both partners from having index one. The algebraic correspondence and its explicit algebraic inverse then transport Floccari's all-powers result, including the claimed fixed-parameter mixed products. The treatment of higher Picard number is also adequate.

The geometric hypotheses are supported by the fiber calculations, pullback Mordell–Weil spaces, and rational lattice complements. Proposition 4.1 correctly uses the vanishing of $H^{2,0}$ of the intermediate quotient to isolate the primitive eighth-root representation; it does not require the stronger assumptions of the preceding paper. The period argument supplies the essential evidence for the new families. Countable isomorphism fibers force a two-dimensional period image, giving generic transcendental rank eight. A prescribed totally negative value is represented by the quaternary form over the RM field; rational reflections make the corresponding negative directions dense. Their orthogonal period curves have exactly the expected additional rational plane at very general points, and the Hodge-locus theorem gives algebraicity. Thus the existence claim is not inferred from a bounded search. The example $\alpha=4+\sqrt2$ gives determinant class $7$ and the asserted index-one form.

The novelty is appropriately distinguished from its inputs. I compared the relevant statements and arguments in rStation 23 v4, 24 v1, 95 v1, and the two-parameter constructions in 61 v3 and 65 v1. Paper 95 supplies the $D_8$ correspondence and an isolated index-one example but leaves the present rank-fourteen configuration unanalyzed; paper 24 supplies the twisted $D_5$ mechanism and a particular curve. The uniform local criterion and its application to dense index-one $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ curves constitute a meaningful extension. The $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ result is a smaller accompanying extension, as acknowledged. Direct checks of Floccari [1, Theorem 5.11], Floccari–Fu [2, Theorem K], Varesco [5, Theorem 0.2], and van Geemen–Schütt [4] support the stated distinction: these sources supply the embedding theorem, index-two cases, or RM constructions, rather than the present index-one family result. Attribution is adequate.

I inspected and reran the supplied verification script. All 81 checks passed, and the output matched the retained output. These computations support the polynomial identities, group-algebra calculations, rational quadratic forms, and local examples. They do not computationally certify the period-map or Hodge-locus arguments; those are supplied in the text. No explicit equation or point-count-certified member of the new index-one curves is provided, but the existence proof makes neither necessary for the stated result.

The paper communicates its contribution, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations clearly. The following small clarifications would improve precision without changing the acceptance decision:

- In the closing sentence of Theorem B(4), explicitly separate HC for every member from Picard number sixteen, exact RM field, and index one at the stated rank-six members. The preceding quantified statement is clear, but the closing wording can suggest that every specialization retains all these properties.
- In Section 6.3, use $2r+1$ as the generator with square $5$ when applying the common notation $s^2=d$ to the $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ family. Also distinguish the countable-fiber statement of Lemma 6.3 from the usual algebraic meaning of “quasi-finiteness.”
- Section 6.5, Step 5 establishes infinitely many distinct parameter curves and non-isotriviality. Its additional assertion that the families are “pairwise distinct up to” the displayed finite symmetry is stronger than Lemma 6.3 proves: that lemma allows different elliptic fibrations on the same abstract K3 surface. Omit that unnecessary classification assertion or justify it separately.
