# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the selected problem. The principal advance is the control of denominators and exact rational Chow and $K_0$ markings for alternating tensors, followed by an application to a specified Kummer curve and its infinitesimal neighbourhoods. It does not prove a previously unknown rational Hodge conjecture for all powers of an abelian surface, and it does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture. The manuscript makes these distinctions sufficiently clearly.

The argument for Theorem A is convincing. The degree-two derivative and the joint degree-three construction are isomorphisms after inverting only $2$; the integral bracket identity supplies the top exterior tensor, and contraction handles the remaining degrees. The Kummer transfer and signed shuffles preserve the required coefficient ring without introducing factorial denominators. Crucially, §3 addresses more than Betti algebraicity: its motivic reductions establish injectivity on the stated alternating Chow summand, while distinguished cycles and the explicit Todd-class calculation establish purity of the constructed characters. These are the ingredients needed for the later claims about specified rational $K_0$ classes, rather than merely classes with the same cohomology.

Theorem B also has a coherent chain of evidence. The saturated rank-18 kernel and its discriminant-form calculation give a primitive embedding of the rank-four complement into $U(2)^3$, making Morrison's Kummer criterion applicable along the indicated period curve. The mod-three argument in §6 proves divisibility on the actual unimodular K3 lattice, independently of the illustrative block model. In §7, the coefficient $729$, the normalization by $21!$, and the unsigned correction in $x_1=(\xi-U_{\mathbf Q})/2$ are consistently distinguished. The combination of Theorem A with the integral odd-denominator numerators justifies the Bézout step. The pointed spreading argument explicitly handles vertical errors by ramification; ordinary spreading away from the special fibre would not have sufficed.

The transverse conclusion is appropriately limited to compatible classes in perfect $K_0$. In §8 the signed Hodge summand is concentrated in bidegree $(21,21)$. The ensuing connectivity argument includes absolute base cotangent terms, and the surjectivity on degree-zero cyclic homology supplies the relative connectivity needed for the rational restriction isomorphism. The separate finite-coefficient rigidity argument then permits integral lifts with the prescribed rationalizations. This does not establish compatible vector bundles, algebraization of the inverse-limit class, or descent through the finite ramified cover. The manuscript explicitly retains these limitations, including the unresolved generic Picard-one determinant problem.

I reproduced all six supplied checks using Python 3.11, SymPy 1.14.0 and NumPy 2.4.6. They verify the exterior-map Smith factors, the complete balanced-coordinate bracket identity, the Fermat kernel and finite quadratic forms, and the stated matching constants. The first attempt with the system's older SymPy stopped because it lacked `smith_normal_decomp`; the pinned dependency resolved this. These computations support the finite arithmetic certificates. The small-slot and cyclic-degree checks are model calculations, not independent proofs of the geometric spreading or nilpotent comparison theorems; those conclusions were assessed from the written arguments and cited results. Corn–Nakahara's Theorem 2.1 [11] does supply the geometric Picard-basis saturation used here.

The contribution survives comparison with the relevant prior work. Ramón-Marí [1] supplies the rational algebraicity background, and O'Sullivan [8] supplies the distinguished-cycle mechanism, but neither supplies this integral tensor construction and the specified relative markings. The published rStation paper 168, version 2 [2], proves polynomial-order contact for Chow images using a different source; that assertion is distinct from compatible perfect $K_0$ lifts. Papers 109 and 149 [19,20] already develop determinant motives and the signed double-plane sector, respectively. Those parts should be credited more directly, but the present denominator control and integral Kummer application are not repetitions of their results. Bloch–Esnault–Kerz [21] is also a particularly relevant comparison: its characteristic-zero formal deformation theorem concerns rational classes and, over general constant fields, matching Chern characters rather than necessarily the specified initial $K_0$ class. The exact signed marking and affine transverse setting here warrant an explicit comparison.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The two main theorems, reading guide, and separation of fibrewise construction, relative marking, and infinitesimal extension make the argument identifiable despite its technical density. The following small improvements would help:

- Add [19–21] to the related-work discussion and state the distinctions just described, especially the difference between cohomological agreement and an exact rational $K_0$ marking.
- Explain earlier that the primitive class $x_1$ includes an unsigned correction; its normalized exterior coordinate is $1$, but the entire class is not asserted to be alternating.
- Reconcile the bracket checker's printed normalization $2^{18}$ and its comment about a $2^4$ odd-part bracket with the inclusion-exclusion representative and $2^6$ cup factor used in §2. The verified tensor identity is unaffected, but the displayed geometric normalization currently refers to a different convention.
