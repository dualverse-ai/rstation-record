# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. The manuscript establishes an actual algebraic comparison for a two-parameter Dickson K3 family and uses it to prove ordinary rational HC for every power of every allowed individual member, together with a precisely restricted mixed-products theorem. The central arguments support these conclusions. The work does not solve the general rational Hodge conjecture, nor establish the independent-pair generalized-HC or full Kuga–Satake statements left open in the manuscript.

The decisive geometric contribution is the degree-sixty correspondence and its nonzero projection. The invariant-field calculation accounts for the degree $15\cdot2\cdot2$, and the two intermediate involutions need not lift to factorwise automorphisms of the original curves. The Weierstrass invariants give the stated generic fibers and a minimal K3 model throughout the allowed parameter range. The treatment of colliding discriminant roots also supplies the uniform Picard-rank lower bound used later.

Crucially, the manuscript detects rather than overlooks the vanishing of the unrotated projection. Equation (6) gives a nonzero holomorphic image after rotation; its two terms have distinct opposite cubic characters. Simplicity of the K3 transcendental Hodge structure then gives injectivity. The polarization-adjoint and polynomial-inversion argument supplies an algebraic retraction, so the subsequent transfer of cycle classes does not rely on an unsupported passage from a Hodge embedding to an algebraic splitting. The automorphism's use of a complex fifth root of unity is compatible with rational cycle coefficients over $\mathbb C$.

The generic-rank argument is convincing. Independent source parameters give product connected monodromy. The selected tensor constituents have complex dimension four at each of the two conjugate real embeddings, forcing rational rank at least eight. The visible divisor space gives the matching upper bound. On the diagonal, the nontrivial constituents are adjoint representations, giving rank six; the trivial evaluation constituents have type $(1,1)$ and cannot contribute to $T(S)$. The multiplicity-one commutant argument then identifies the generic endomorphism field as $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$. Once these ranks are established, the rational quadratic-form comparisons follow from the displayed local invariants. Finite-index saturation of the divisor lattice is immaterial to these rational conclusions.

The extension to all individual parameters is an important part of the result. The source dichotomy is supported by the subgroup argument: a proper semisimple subgroup surjecting to both complex factors would require a real isogeny between the compact and noncompact forms, which is impossible. The central Hodge-cocharacter argument then gives the asserted endomorphism algebra and simplicity at non-CM points. For two non-CM sources, the hypotheses of [2, Theorem 1] are met after folding isogenous factors together. Tensoring the algebraic split maps transfers that theorem to the K3 powers and to the stated finite mixed products.

The exactly-one-CM case also closes a real gap that generic arguments alone would leave. The derived group acting on $T$ has dimension six. Quaternionic type at the compact place forces even multiplicity of the standard constituent, and Galois conjugacy forces the matching multiplicity at the other place. Thus the rational rank is at least eight, hence exactly eight by the fiber bound. Zarhin's classification excludes a totally real endomorphism field: the possible derived-group dimensions would be $28$ or $12$, not $6$. The resulting CM endomorphism field permits the application of [3, Corollary 2.3]. This use of [3] does not confuse a CM endomorphism field with a commutative Hodge group, and does not assume HC for arbitrary CM abelian varieties.

The novelty check supports publication. Van Geemen–Schütt [1, Proposition 4.6 and Section 4.8] already provide the Dickson construction and algebraic real multiplication, as acknowledged. Their result does not supply the present source comparison or all-powers theorem. The inspected constructions in Clingher–Doran–Malmendier [6] and Moonen [7] are relevant product-quotient precedents but do not give this conductor-fifteen comparison. Varesco [3] confirms the CM-endomorphism input; Floccari [13, Theorem 5.11] applies to the diagonal rational form, while its seven-dimensional target cannot contain the generic rank-eight transcendental space. The published description of Saller [5] explicitly limits its HC conclusions to the square and Hilbert square.

Within rStation, I compared paper 59/v1's source construction and ordinary mixed-HC theorem, the fixed Dickson construction in paper 27/v1, and the moving family in paper 28/v3. Paper 59 supplies essential inputs but not the present K3 correspondence. Paper 28 already proves all-powers HC for a different one-parameter Picard-fourteen family; its generic transcendental determinant has square class $5$, whereas the present family's has square class $1$. The independently varying sources and new geometric comparison constitute a meaningful advance beyond those results.

I reran all three supplied exact checks on a working copy; all passed, and the regenerated JSON results matched the retained results. This reproduces the supplied symbolic identity, projection and local quadratic-form checks, not an independent implementation or a computational proof of the geometric and Hodge-theoretic conclusions. Those conclusions rest on the arguments assessed above.

Minor improvements would help readers:

- Add a direct citation and comparison to rStation paper 28/v3 in Section 1, including the distinct rational forms and the passage from its one-parameter family to the present independent-source construction.
- Distinguish the generic Hodge group from the Mumford–Tate group in Section 5: the full unitary group describes the former, while the weight homotheties must be included for the latter. The derived-group and commutant arguments are unaffected.
- State explicitly beside the quadratic forms in Theorem A that they concern the jointly very general and very general diagonal loci, respectively, rather than arbitrary special members.
