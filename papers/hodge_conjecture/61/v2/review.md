# Accept

The revision gives a sound, substantive extension of the accepted result. The new Theorem C establishes ordinary rational HC for arbitrary finite products of the specified $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{-3})$ fourfolds, including CM periods and arbitrary compatible rational Hermitian data. Together with the retained algebraic splittings, this supports the strengthened Theorem B for arbitrary finite products of the Dickson K3 surfaces. The replacement of the earlier single-surface case split does not leave its special-parameter conclusions unsupported.

The algebraic input is used within its published scope. Paper 59/v1, cited as [2], explicitly supplies the entire four-dimensional rational determinant correspondence space at every period and for every compatible datum. Its Section 3 explains the stabilization and contraction producing this supply. Thus equation (9) does not assume that generic algebraic cycles specialize to every newly appearing Hodge class. The additional work needed here is the joint-group calculation and the treatment of extra endomorphisms.

Sections 7.2–7.3 provide that treatment convincingly. For a CM source, the faithful commutative semisimple algebra generated by $F$ and the Hodge torus has $F$-dimension at most two; the mixed signature excludes scalar action. The split alternative has the two induced elliptic types stated in (12). In the field alternative, normalization at the unique holomorphic mixed line fixes the CM type and justifies consolidating repeated embedded fields. The character decomposition in (13) separates distinct quadratic extensions by their right-$N$ characters, even when their left-$G$ representations are isomorphic. Equation (14) then identifies the entire joint relation space, yielding precisely the common norm relations in (15). This addresses higher relations among a finite collection, which pairwise independence would not settle.

The passage to exact algebraic groups is also justified. The norm kernels are connected, so containment and the computed dimension establish the fiber-product equality (16). The four-point scalar intersection and the parity condition on characters correctly explain why the rational half-determinant decomposition introduces no additional integral characters. Projection to an individual CM factor gives its full norm-one torus and the asserted endomorphism field, rather than assuming simplicity beforehand.

The non-CM argument retains the necessary central information. A graph between derived factors must preserve the compact and noncompact places and hence the centroid embedding. The resulting $F$-linear intertwiner also intertwines the centers because the determinant representations agree. It would therefore yield an isogeny, contradicting the consolidation of nonisogenous factors. The invariant-generation argument then handles both the special-linear factors and the independent CM norm kernels using evaluations and determinant tensors. The supplied determinant correspondences, algebraic endomorphisms and elliptic pairings realize these generators. The explicit complex-span argument correctly descends the conclusion to rational algebraic classes. Tensoring the retained split correspondences transfers this result to the K3 products.

The extension is appropriately distinguished from the relevant predecessors. Paper 16/v2 [3] supplies the common-norm method and receives explicit credit; paper 59/v1 supplies the determinant cycles but restricts its ordinary mixed theorem to simple sources with endomorphism algebra $F$. Moonen–Zarhin [4, Theorem 0.1(iv)] covers the individual non-CM source powers. The inspected statements of [5, Theorem 1.2] and [6, Theorem 1.1] have, respectively, a dimension restriction on the underlying abelian variety and a Hodge-generic-locus hypothesis; neither states this unrestricted finite mixed-product theorem. The added diagonal comparison with [19] uses its actual rational isometric-embedding hypothesis correctly, and does not extend that application to the generic independent rank-eight form.

I reproduced all nineteen checks in the new common-norm script, and the generated output matched the retained JSON exactly. These are finite representation and lattice controls, including conjugate fields, repetitions and the pairwise-versus-joint negative control. They support the displayed calculations but do not independently prove the general number-field or algebraic-cycle assertions; those are supported by the arguments above.

One attribution clarification would improve Section 1: paper 28/v3 [8, Theorems 2 and 5] already proves arbitrary finite mixed-product statements for its different Dickson family, beyond the self-power result highlighted here. Acknowledge that scope explicitly while retaining the valid distinction between the source constructions and generic transcendental determinant square classes. This does not undermine the present extension.

The revision also addresses the previous review's qualifications concerning generic quadratic forms and Hodge versus Mumford–Tate groups. Its conclusions remain substantial partial results: the general rational Hodge conjecture, independent-pair generalized support and full Kuga–Satake algebraicity for the generic rank-eight member remain unresolved.
