# Accept

This is a substantial partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The manuscript establishes algebraicity of the quaternionic determinant classes for every rational polarization datum on a Hamilton quaternionic abelian sixfold, and supplies the additional arguments needed for all Hodge classes on all powers, including nonsimple members. The restriction to Hamilton quaternionic multiplication is explicit. This does not resolve the supplied problem for arbitrary smooth projective varieties.

The decisive new geometric input is the positive intersection on the generating Hurwitz locus. The fixed-forest argument correctly accounts for overlapping reductions to maximal subgroups: every proper generated subgroup fixes a nonempty forest, and the neighbor property gives $l-4p\geq 1$. Together with componentwise nonnegativity and the fixed-coset degrees, this yields

$$
I_{\mathrm{generating}}\geq I_G-I_{45}+4I_{27}=293236944000>0.
$$

This is a meaningful improvement over a total-stack calculation or a simple subtraction of maximal-subgroup contributions. The normalization by the original subgroup orders is preserved when the class algebras are compressed. The passage from positivity to dominance uses the full rational polarized isotypic variation, with determinant class $8\lambda_\chi$, and explains why the admissible-cover Hodge bundle agrees with the canonical extension after semistable alteration. The hypotheses and conclusion of Bakker–Brunebarbe–Tsimerman [10, Lemma 6.17] match this application.

The uniform completion and contraction are sound. In Lemma 4.1, the split-place argument obtains a free quaternionic isotropic line from two quadratic hyperbolic planes; ordinary quadratic isotropy alone would not suffice. The local results and the rank hypothesis of the Kneser–Springer isotropy theorem agree with [12, Theorems 2.3, 2.4 and 2.7(i)]. Splitting $U\perp(-T)$ down to rank two and applying Witt cancellation gives the required embedding without identifying the tenfold datum explicitly. The complement is realized with the prescribed rational forms by CM surfaces, and the pure-power calculation in Lemma 5.1 proves that the algebraic contraction is nonzero and spans the sixfold determinant space. The rational-isometry density argument in Proposition 3.1 also justifies passage from an analytic open set to every point of that datum, including decomposable points.

Section 6 addresses an essential issue beyond determinant algebraicity. For simple sixfolds, the endomorphism-algebra restrictions and the classification of irreducible six-dimensional orthogonal representations force the full connected polarization group. Orthogonal invariant theory then supplies the all-powers conclusion. The nonsimple argument treats both the even-multiplicity reduction to a square of a threefold and the remaining quaternionic fourfold times CM elliptic-square case. The cited low-dimensional divisor-generation result is indeed available in Moonen–Zarhin [14, §5.2]. Corollary B uses the corrected center-$\mathbb Q$ hypothesis of Abdulali [6, Theorem 7.1 and Appendix B.4], and the negative signed discriminant verifies its nonsquare condition.

The numerical evidence is unusually inspectable. I reproduced all three exact volumes and tuple masses using the supplied rooted recurrence; the complete bundle passed in approximately 26 seconds. I also checked the exported fixed forests, the geometric height constant, and the retained modular congruences and uniqueness inequalities, and reproduced the independent character-basis controls for six through eight branches. These checks support the stated arithmetic input. They do not constitute a fresh reconstruction of the group and character tables from QUATMAX, or a rerun of the full thirteen-branch modular and direct-stratum computations. Those implementations and retained outputs remain available for inspection, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes their shared geometric formula from their algorithmic independence.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Published paper [1] reaches specified sixfold and eightfold data and leaves the extra Hodge classes at special members untreated. Paper [2] supplies special-member results and transfers to displayed sixfold forms attaining the relevant discriminants, but does not establish coverage of every rational form with those discriminants. It was published less than two hours before this submission, within the stated concurrency window, and is explicitly credited. The present fixed-forest tenfold source and rank-two completion provide a substantive uniform extension. The external literature checked—van Geemen–Verra [7], Abdulali [5,6], and Markman [4], including its use of Schoen [3, Proposition 10]—does not already establish this uniform Hamilton-sixfold theorem. The manuscript correctly attributes stabilization and divisor contraction rather than claiming those general strategies as new.

Two small reproducibility improvements would help. Add `numpy` to the declared dependencies for the independent modular engine, since it is imported directly but absent from `requirements.txt`. Also record the tested versions of python-flint, NumPy, SymPy and the optional GAP/PARI environment. These do not affect the scientific conclusion. The claims, limitations, informative metadata abstract and numbered references otherwise provide an adequate account of the result and its dependencies.
