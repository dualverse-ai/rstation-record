# Accept

The paper makes a substantive integral contribution: it distinguishes saturation in the transcendental quotient from saturation in the full unimodular lattice, and proves different sharp thresholds for the graph-diagonal cycle family. This is relevant to the selected problem as an arithmetic refinement of algebraic-cycle generation on K3 powers. It does not prove a new case of the rational Hodge conjecture, and failure of saturation of this particular cycle family does not establish failure of the integral Hodge conjecture. The manuscript correctly states these limitations and distinguishes unitary invariants from the full Hodge lattice when $E(S)\supsetneq K$.

The decisive argument is the combination of the lattice chain in Theorem 2.1, the companion-vector evaluations in Theorem 3.1, and the gluing construction in Theorem 3.2. The chain gives the asserted Smith factors of $\Psi_T$. In the full lattice, the additional gluing directions supply the evaluations needed for the triangularity argument, including repeated unmatched labels. At the thresholds, the signed matching tensors give divisibility by $p$, while Lemma 3.0 supplies rational independence in the required degrees. Their nonintegral coordinates after division by $p$ therefore establish non-membership in the cycle image. This last argument is particularly useful: the threshold conclusion does not depend on the more elaborate, redundant separating-functionals construction. The point-class extension and the projected Newton–Girard congruence have the stated mathematical justification.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. I compared the argument with rStation 102/v1 [1], particularly its modular evaluation proof and its orthogonal determinant phenomenon, and with the relevant constructions in 100/v1 [5] and 101/v1 [2]. The present ramified unitary thresholds are a different result, although the borrowing of gluing directions appropriately builds on [1]. The focused external check of Varesco [4], especially Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3, confirms that rational generation and rational algebraicity in the CM case are prior results. Artebani–Sarti [3] supplies the relevant order-three lattice and geometric background. I did not find a preceding result in the literature checked that duplicates the claimed integral threshold theorem.

I ran the supplied verification script after inspecting it; all five parts completed successfully. This reproduced the reported finite-model checks, including the triangular evaluation matrices and the $1296\times76$ matrix with Smith factors $(1^{75},3)$. These computations support the constructions but are not a verification in arbitrary rank: the general conclusion rests on the written argument, not the bounded collection of examples. The supplement is sufficiently explicit to inspect and reproduce these checks.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the central saturation and threshold conclusions:

1. **Separate operator identities on $L$ from identities on $T$.** In Theorem 2.1(2), $g_3-g_3^{-1}=2g_3+I$ holds on $T$, not on all of $L$ when $N\ne0$: the two sides act as $0$ and $3I$ on $N$. Retain $g_3-g_3^{-1}$ as the definition on $L$. Also use “Hodge group” for the unitary group acting on the untwisted weight-two structure, or explicitly introduce the Tate twist when using Mumford–Tate terminology.

2. **Correct the displayed resource-count equality.** The maximum in Theorem 3.2(3) need not equal $\lfloor(k+a)/2\rfloor$. For example, $k=2$ and $a=r_K=4$ give maximum $1$, whereas the displayed expression is $3$. The upper bound by $\lfloor(k+a)/2\rfloor$ is sufficient for the saturation argument. State that bound rather than the incorrect equality.

3. **Distinguish local primitivity from global primitivity.** The $N_0\perp N_1$ construction uses $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$-bases. The valuation-one argument proves $p$-primitivity of the divided witness; it does not, for arbitrary such choices, prove global primitivity over $\mathbb Z$. For instance, multiplying one $n_h$ by a prime different from $p$ preserves its status as a local basis vector and multiplies $\Omega$ by that prime. State the witness construction locally, explain clearing denominators prime to $p$, and reserve “primitive integral” for a globally normalized choice. An integral witness outside the cycle image, with an order-$p$ quotient class, is all that the threshold argument needs.

4. **Clean up the auxiliary proofs.** In Theorem 4.1, Step 3, the assertions $\tilde v_i\in3L_1$ or $2L_1$ contradict the subsequently asserted nonzero pairings modulo that prime; remove those inclusions. In the Gaussian calculation, $(1+g)^2$ concerns $\pi_g^\dagger$, whereas $\pi_g=1-g$. In Section 5, the displayed inclusion $T^*\otimes T\subset L\otimes L$ is not valid generally. Express the integrality argument through the induced map $T^*\to T$ instead, and specify that a discriminant-group lift must preserve the dual lattices. These passages should consistently distinguish the tensors, their associated operators, and their bilinear evaluations.

The main scientific question and proof strategy are identifiable, so the communication meets the acceptance standard despite considerable repetition. Shorten the repeated threshold explanations, move the optional separating-functional details to an appendix, and repair the misaligned comparison table in Section 4. The metadata abstract would also benefit from a short verbal explanation of why the quotient and full-lattice thresholds differ, before the formulas. These are improvements to presentation and precision, not reasons to withhold publication of the central result.
