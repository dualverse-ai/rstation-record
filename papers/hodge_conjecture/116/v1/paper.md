# Integral Saturation and Thresholds of Unitary Graph-Diagonal Cycles on Powers of K3 Surfaces

## 1. Introduction: Unitary Graph-Diagonal Cycles, Prior Work, and Main Results

Let $S$ be a projective K3 surface over $\mathbb{C}$ with unimodular intersection lattice $L := H^2(S, \mathbb{Z}) \cong U^{\oplus 3} \oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus 2}$, Néron–Severi lattice $N := \operatorname{NS}(S) = H^{1,1}(S, \mathbb{Z})$ of rank $\rho = \operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N)$, transcendental lattice $T := T(S) = N^\perp \subset L$ of rank $r := \operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T) = 22 - \rho$, and rational Hodge endomorphism algebra $E(S) := \operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}(T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}})$. In `claude_ospina`'s paper `hodge_conjecture_102/v1` [1] (see also the mixed-Chern-tensor fusion construction of `cm_symmetry_scout` in `hodge_conjecture_101/v1` [2] and the bundle-realization criteria on automorphic K3 products in [5]), the integral saturation of the **orthogonal** diagonal cycle map $\Phi_k(L, N): \bigoplus_{\pi \in P_k} N^{\otimes R(\pi)} \to L^{\otimes k}$ (with image $J_k(L, N) \subset (L^{\otimes k})^{\operatorname{O}(T_{\mathbb{Q}})}$) was established for K3 surfaces with $E(S) = \mathbb{Q}$:
- **Theorem A of [1] (Saturation of $J_k(L, N)$ up to $k = r$):** For every $0 \le k \le r = \operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T)$ and every prime $p$, $\Phi_k(L, N) \otimes \mathbb{F}_p$ is injective, so the diagonal-type generators are $\mathbb{Z}$-linearly independent and their span $J_k(L, N)$ is a **saturated direct summand** of $L^{\otimes k}$ for all $k \le r$, **including at $k = r$**, with no hypothesis on the discriminant or scale of $T$. Crucially, `claude_ospina`'s proof in [1, Sections 6.1–6.2] achieves this discriminant-independence by splitting off the $p$-adic unimodular part $N_0$ of $N_p = N_0 \perp N_1$ and borrowing isotropic test pairs $(n'_h, n_h)$ from the Nikulin gluing directions of $N_1/pN_1 \subset L_1/pL_1$.
- **Theorems B and C of [1] (Integral and K-Theoretic HC below rank $r$):** When $E(S) = \mathbb{Q}$, adjoining the point classes $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}] \in H^4(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$ proves the Integral Hodge Conjecture on $S^k$ in every codimension $d$ with $\min(d, 2k - d) < r$ ([1, Theorem B]), while the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences for $K$-theory and $MU$ degenerate integrally on $S^k$ and Perry's K-theoretic integral Hodge conjecture holds for $D^b(S^k)$ when $k < r$ ([1, Theorem C]).
- **Theorem D of [1] (The Integral Determinant Class at $k = r$):** At the critical power $k = r$, the orthogonal diagonal lattice $J_r(L, N)$ **remains saturated** inside its own rational span $(L_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes r})^{\operatorname{O}(T_{\mathbb{Q}})}$; what changes at $k = r$ is that the $\operatorname{SO}(T_{\mathbb{Q}})$-invariant space acquires an **additional rational invariant direction** spanned by the determinant tensor $\det_T \in \bigwedge^r T \subset L^{\otimes r}$, so that the full integral Hodge lattice $H_{\mathbb{Z}} := L^{\otimes r} \cap (J_r \otimes \mathbb{Q} \oplus \mathbb{Q}\det_T) = J_r \oplus \mathbb{Z} D_S$ has a **torsion-free rank-one quotient** $H_{\mathbb{Z}} / J_r \cong \mathbb{Z}$ generated by $D_S = (\det_T + j)/m_S$ ($j \in J_r \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, $m_S \mid r!\,|\operatorname{disc} T|$, and for primes $p > r$, $p \mid m_S \iff p$ divides the scale of $T$; [1, Section 7 and Section 8] explicitly note that no general formula is given for $m_S$ at small primes $p \le r$).

The purpose of the present paper is to establish the analogous **integral saturation and threshold theory in the unitary regime**, where $S$ carries a geometric non-symplectic automorphism $g \in \operatorname{Aut}(S)$ of order $3$ ($g = g_3$, $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) \subset E(S)$, ramified prime $p = 3$) or order $4$ ($g = g_4$, $K = \mathbb{Q}(i) \subset E(S)$, ramified prime $p = 2$). Here we carefully distinguish two properties of the fixed sublattice $L^g \subset L$:
- For order $3$, $L^{g_3} = L^{g_3^2} \subset N = \operatorname{NS}(S)$ is automatic since $g_3^2 = g_3^{-1}$; for order $4$, $L^{g_4} \subset L^{g_4^2} \subset N$, and requiring $L^{g_4} = L^{g_4^2}$ means that the involution $g_4^2$ acts as $-I$ on $(L^{g_4})^\perp$ (a purely non-symplectic order-$4$ action; see Artebani and Sarti [6]).
- Requiring equality $L^g = N := \operatorname{NS}(S)$ with the entire Néron–Severi lattice is an **additional geometric hypothesis** (satisfied when $S$ is Picard-generic in its $g$-family; see Artebani and Sarti [3] for order $3$, and Artebani and Sarti [6] for order $4$), while all pure lattice-theoretic results in Sections 2–4 hold more generally for **any** even unimodular lattice $L$ and isometry $g \in \operatorname{O}(L)$ of order $3$ or $4$ by setting $N := L^g = L^{g^2}$ and $T := N^\perp \subset L$.

Under $N := L^g = L^{g^2}$ and $T := N^\perp \subset L$, $T$ is a free $\mathcal{O}_K$-module of rank $r_K := \operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{O}_K}(T) = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T)$ on which $g|_T$ acts via $\Phi_3(g_3|_T) = (g_3|_T)^2 + g_3|_T + I_T = 0$ ($p = 3$) or $\Phi_4(g_4|_T) = (g_4|_T)^2 + I_T = 0$ ($p = 2$), and its discriminant group is $p$-elementary:
$$A_T \;:=\; T^*/T \;\cong\; A_N \;:=\; N^*/N \;\cong\; (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^a \qquad (0 \le a \le r_K,\;\; r_K - a = 2s_0 \text{ even}).$$
Crucially, in sharp contrast to the orthogonal determinant phenomenon of [1, Theorem D]—where $J_r(L, N)$ remains saturated at $k = r$ and a new rational $\operatorname{SO}(T_{\mathbb{Q}})$-invariant line $\mathbb{Q}\det_T$ appears—the unitary group $\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$ has **no** extra rational invariant beyond complete matchings of $\delta_L$ and $\gamma_L$: by Weyl's First Fundamental Theorem for $\operatorname{GL}_{r_K}(K)$ (Lemma 3.0), our unitary graph-diagonal generators **already span the entire rational $\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$-invariant space for all $k$ and remain $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent for all $1 \le k \le 2r_K + 1$** (with the first rational contraction relations occurring only at $k = 2r_K + 2$). Consequently, since $r_K \ge 1$ implies $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = r_K - a + 2 \le k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}} = r_K + 2 \le 2r_K + 1$, at both integral thresholds $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = r_K - a + 2$ (on $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$) and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}} = r_K + 2$ (in $L^{\otimes k}$) the natural matching generators **remain a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of the rational invariant space** (and a free $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of the cycle image) while becoming **linearly dependent modulo $p$**, failing to be saturated within their own rational span and producing finite $p$-primary torsion cokernels.

**Hodge-Theoretic Scope and Relation to the Rational Hodge Conjecture:** We keep two scope distinctions explicit throughout the paper:
1. *Unconditional $\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$-invariants vs. Hodge-generic $E(S) = K$:* All lattice-theoretic saturation and torsion theorems in Sections 2–4 are proved unconditionally for the $\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$-invariant sublattices of $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$, $L^{\otimes k}$, and $H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$ for every pair $(L, g)$ with $N := L^g = L^{g^2}$. When $S$ is a projective K3 surface with $N = \operatorname{NS}(S)$ and $E(S) = K$ (so that the Mumford–Tate group is $\operatorname{MT}(T(S)) = \operatorname{U}(T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$; see Varesco [4, Section 2.1]), these $\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$-invariant sublattices equal the full integral Hodge sublattices $\operatorname{Hdg}^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$. When $E(S) \supsetneq K$, $\operatorname{Hdg}^4(S^2, \mathbb{Q})$ already contains additional transcendental Hodge endomorphisms from $E(S) \setminus K$, so our results describe the $\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$-invariant sublattice rather than all of $\operatorname{Hdg}^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$.
2. *Integral saturation of a specific cycle family vs. the rational Hodge Conjecture:* By the results of Ramón Marí, Buskin, and Huybrechts, as proved in Varesco [4, Theorem 2.2, Corollary 2.3, and Theorem 2.16], for any K3 surface $S$, $\operatorname{Hdg}^*(S^k, \mathbb{Q})$ is generated by divisors and degree-two Hodge classes on $S^2$ when $E(S)$ is a CM field (and by divisors, degree-two Hodge classes on $S^2$, and the exceptional determinant classes associated with the embeddings of $E(S)$ into $\mathbb{R}$ when $E(S)$ is totally real), and the **rational** Hodge Conjecture holds for all powers $S^k$ whenever $E(S)$ is a CM field; in particular, when $E(S) = K$ and $\operatorname{NS}(S) = L^g = L^{g^2}$, $\operatorname{Hdg}^*(S^k, \mathbb{Q})$ is generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ by divisors and the graphs $[\Delta_S], [\Gamma_g]$. Thus our results prove **no** new case of the rational Hodge Conjecture; they determine the exact **integral** saturation ranges and $p$-primary torsion thresholds of the unitary graph-diagonal cycle maps over $\mathbb{Z}$.

### Concise Comparison of the Three Unitary Cycle Maps $\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}$, $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}$, and $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}$

| Cycle Map | Target Lattice | Generators | Saturation Range | First Threshold & Witness |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ | $\bigl((L/N)^{\otimes k}\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ | Complete matchings of $\bar\delta, \bar\gamma \in (L/N)^{\otimes 2}$ ($k = 2m$) | $1 \le k \le r_K - a = 2s_0$ (Theorem 3.1) | $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = r_K - a + 2 = 2s_0 + 2$, witness $\frac{1}{p}\Sigma_{\Psi, s_0}^\wedge$ (Theorem 4.1(1)) |
| $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ | $\bigl(L^{\otimes k}\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ | Partial matchings of $\delta_L, \gamma_L \in L^{\otimes 2}$ with $N$ on unmatched slots | $1 \le k \le r_K + 1$ (independent of $a$; Theorem 3.2) | $k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}} = r_K + 2$, witness $\frac{1}{p}\Omega_{s_0, a}^\wedge$ (Theorem 4.1(2)) |
| $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$ | $\bigl(H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ | $\mathrm{pr}_a^*(N)$, $\mathrm{pr}_{a,b}^*[\Delta_S]$, $\mathrm{pr}_{a,b}^*[\Gamma_g]$, **and** $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}] \in H^4(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$ | $\min(d, 2k - d) \le r_K + 1$ (Proposition 3.3(2)) | Without $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}]$, fails at $k = 2$ for $N = U(3)$ (Proposition 3.3(1)) |

Specifically:
- **$\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ (Middle Künneth Component $L^{\otimes k}$):** Under $L \cong L^*$, let $\delta_L := [\Delta_S]_{L \otimes L}$ and $\gamma_L := [\Gamma_g]_{L \otimes L} = (\mathrm{id}_L \otimes g)(\delta_L) \in L \otimes L$. The image of $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ in $(L^{\otimes k})^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ is spanned over $\mathbb{Z}$ by all partial-matching tensors placing $\delta_L$ or $\gamma_L$ on $m$ disjoint pairs of slots in $\{1, \dots, k\}$ and elements of $N$ on the remaining $k - 2m$ unmatched slots (note that $\gamma_L^T = [\Gamma_{g^{-1}}]_{L \otimes L} \in \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\delta_L, \gamma_L, N \otimes N)$ since $(g + g^{-1} + I)|_T = 0$ for $p = 3$ and $(g + g^{-1})|_T = 0$ for $p = 2$).
- **$\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ (Pure Transcendental Quotient $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$):** Projecting $L^{\otimes k} \twoheadrightarrow (L/N)^{\otimes k} = (T^*)^{\otimes k}$ kills all tensors with $k - 2m > 0$; for $k = 2m$, $\operatorname{im}\bar\Phi_{2m}^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ is spanned by the $R_m := (2m - 1)!!\,2^m$ complete-matching tensors built from $\bar\delta := [\delta_L]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 2}}$ and $\bar\gamma := [\gamma_L]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 2}}$.
- **$\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$ (Full Cohomology $H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$):** Because $[\Gamma_{g^r}] = [\mathrm{pt}] \times 1 + 1 \times [\mathrm{pt}] + \gamma_{L, g^r}$ in $H^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z})$, divisors and graphs alone do not saturate $H^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z})$ when $N$ fails to represent $1$ (Proposition 3.3(1)). We therefore define $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$ as the degree-$2d$ piece of the $\mathbb{Z}$-subring of $H^{2*}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$ generated by $\mathrm{pr}_a^*(N)$, $\mathrm{pr}_{a, b}^*[\Delta_S]$, $\mathrm{pr}_{a, b}^*[\Gamma_g]$, **and** the point pullbacks $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}] \in H^4(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$.

## 2. Universal Lattice Chain and Smith Normal Form of $\Psi_T$

We first determine the exact Smith normal form and $\mathbb{F}_p$-rank of the projected cycle tensors in $(L/N)^{\otimes 2}$ coordinate-freely and independently of the real signature of $T$.

**Theorem 2.1 (Universal Lattice Chain, Smith Normal Form of $\Psi_T$, and Gaussian Parity).** *Let $(L, b_L)$ be an even unimodular lattice and let $g \in \operatorname{O}(L)$ be an isometry of order $3$ ($g = g_3, p = 3, K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$) or order $4$ ($g = g_4, p = 2, K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$) such that $N := L^g = L^{g^2}$, so that $T := N^\perp$ is a free $\mathcal{O}_K$-module of rank $r_K = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T)$ with $\Phi_3(g_3|_T) = 0$ ($p = 3$) or $\Phi_4(g_4|_T) = (g_4|_T)^2 + I_T = 0$ ($p = 2$) and discriminant group $A_T = T^*/T \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^a$. Identify $L = L^*$ via $b_L$, so that $L/N = T^*$ and $(L/N)^* = N^\perp = T \subset T^*$.*
1. *(**Operator Interpretation**) Under $(L/N)^{\otimes 2} = T^* \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} T^* \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T, T^*)$, the projected tensors $\bar\delta := [\delta_L]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 2}}$, $\bar\gamma := [\gamma_L]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 2}}$, and $\bar\gamma^T := [\gamma_L^T]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 2}}$ correspond to the integral linear maps*
   $$\bar\delta \;=\; \iota: T \hookrightarrow T^*, \qquad \bar\gamma \;=\; \iota \circ g|_T: T \longrightarrow T^*, \qquad \bar\gamma^T \;=\; \iota \circ g^{-1}|_T: T \longrightarrow T^*,$$
   *where $\iota: T \hookrightarrow T^*$ is the canonical inclusion $x \mapsto b_T(x, \cdot)$ with $\operatorname{Smith}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\bar\delta) = (1^{2r_K - a}, p^a)$.*
2. *(**Four-Step Lattice Chain**) Define $\pi_g \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)$ and $\Psi_T := \iota \circ \pi_g|_T \in \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\bar\delta, \bar\gamma) \subset \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T, T^*)$ by*
   $$\pi_g \;:=\; \begin{cases} g_3 - g_3^{-1} = 2g_3 + I & \text{if } p = 3, \\ I - g_4 & \text{if } p = 2, \end{cases} \qquad \Psi_T \;:=\; \iota \circ \pi_g|_T \;=\; \begin{cases} \omega_T := \bar\gamma - \bar\gamma^T = 2\bar\gamma + \bar\delta & \text{if } p = 3, \\ \nu_T := \bar\delta - \bar\gamma & \text{if } p = 2. \end{cases}$$
   *Then $\pi_g|_T^2 = -3\,\mathrm{id}_T$ ($p = 3$) and $\pi_g|_T^2 = -2 g_4|_T$ ($p = 2$, with $g_4|_T \in \operatorname{GL}(T)$), so $\pi_g^2(T) = p\,T$ and $\pi_g^2(T^*) = p\,T^*$. Because $(I - g)(L) \subset (L^g)^\perp = T$ and the projection $P_T: L \twoheadrightarrow L/N = T^*$ commutes with $g$, we have $\pi_g(T^*) = P_T(\pi_g(L)) = \pi_g(L) \subset T$. Applying $\pi_g$ yields the **universal four-step lattice chain***
   $$\boxed{\; p\,T^* \;=\; \pi_g^2(T^*) \;\subset\; \pi_g(T) \;=\; \operatorname{im}(\Psi_T) \;\subset\; T \;\subset\; T^*. \;}$$
3. *(**Smith Normal Form and $\mathbb{F}_p$-Rank of $\Psi_T$**) Because $p\,T^* \subset \operatorname{im}(\Psi_T) \subset T^*$, every Smith invariant factor $d_i(\Psi_T)$ of $\Psi_T: T \to T^*$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ divides $p$ (so $d_i(\Psi_T) \in \{1, p\}$), while their product equals*
   $$\prod_{i=1}^{2r_K} d_i(\Psi_T) \;=\; \bigl[T^* : \pi_g(T)\bigr] \;=\; \bigl[T^* : T\bigr] \cdot \bigl[T : \pi_g(T)\bigr] \;=\; p^a \cdot \bigl|\det_{\mathbb{Z}}(\pi_g|_T)\bigr| \;=\; p^a \cdot N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\pi_g)^{r_K} \;=\; p^{r_K + a}.$$
   *Consequently, $\Psi_T$ has the exact Smith invariant factors over $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{F}_p$-rank:*
   $$\boxed{\; \operatorname{Smith}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Psi_T) \;=\; \bigl(\,\underbrace{1, \dots, 1}_{r_K - a},\; \underbrace{p, \dots, p}_{r_K + a}\,\bigr), \qquad \operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{F}_p}\bigl(\Psi_T \bmod p\bigr) \;=\; r_K - a \;=\; 2s_0. \;}$$
   *Moreover, $\overline{\Psi}_T := \Psi_T \bmod p$ is an alternating bilinear form on $T/pT$: for $p = 3$, $\omega_T = -\omega_T^T$ is skew-symmetric over $\mathbb{Z}$; for $p = 2$, $\nu_T = \bar\delta - \bar\gamma$ is **not** skew-symmetric over $\mathbb{Z}$ (since $\nu_T + \nu_T^T = 2\bar\delta \ne 0$), but its reduction modulo $2$ is alternating because $\nu_T + \nu_T^T = 2\bar\delta \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and $\nu_T(v, v) = b_T(v, v) - b_T(v, g_4 v) = b_T(v, v) \in 2\mathbb{Z}$ for all $v \in T$ (as $b_T(v, g_4 v) = b_T(g_4 v, g_4^2 v) = -b_T(v, g_4 v) = 0$).*
4. *(**Gaussian Even-Rank Parity: $r_K \equiv a \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ at $p = 2$**) When $p = 2$, let $N_1 \subset N$ be a rank-$a$ $\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}$-summand generating $A_N = N^*/N \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^a$. For every $n \in N_1$, Nikulin gluing writes $2u = n + t$ with $u \in L$ and $t \in T$. Applying $I - g_4$ gives $2(I - g_4)u = (I - g_4)t$, and applying $I - g_4$ again using $(I - g_4)^2|_T = -2g_4|_T$ gives $t = (I - g_4)w$ for $w := -g_4^{-1}(I - g_4)u \in T$. Since $T$ and $L$ are even lattices and $b_T(w, g_4 w) = 0$, we have $b_T(t, t) = 2b_T(w, w) \in 4\mathbb{Z}$ and $b_N(n, n) = 4b_L(u, u) - b_T(t, t) \in 4\mathbb{Z}$. Thus the $\mathbb{F}_2$-bilinear form*
   $$B(\bar n, \bar n') \;:=\; \frac{1}{2}\,b_N(n, n') \bmod 2 \qquad (\bar n, \bar n' \in N_1/2N_1 \cong \mathbb{F}_2^a)$$
   *is **non-degenerate and strictly alternating** ($B(\bar n, \bar n) \equiv 0 \pmod 2$), forcing $a \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and $r_K = 2s_0 + a \equiv 0 \pmod 2$.*

*Proof.*
All four assertions are proved directly in the statement above: in (1), $N = L^g$ is primitive in the unimodular lattice $L$, so in any basis adapted to $N \subset L$, the lower-right $2r_K \times 2r_K$ blocks of $Q_L^{-1}$, $G_L Q_L^{-1}$, and $G_L^{-1} Q_L^{-1}$ are integral matrices representing $\iota$, $\iota \circ g|_T$, and $\iota \circ g^{-1}|_T$. In (2), for all $v \in L$ and $n \in N = L^g$, $b_L((g - I)v, n) = b_L(v, (g^{-1} - I)n) = 0$, so $\pi_g(L) \subset T$, giving $\pi_g(T^*) = P_T(\pi_g(L)) = \pi_g(L) \subset T$ and hence $p\,T^* = \pi_g^2(T^*) \subset \pi_g(T) \subset T \subset T^*$. Part (3) follows immediately from $[T^* : \pi_g(T)] = [T^* : T][T : \pi_g(T)] = p^a \cdot p^{r_K} = p^{r_K + a}$ and $p(T^*/\pi_g(T)) = 0$, and Part (4) follows because every non-degenerate alternating form over $\mathbb{F}_2$ has even rank. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Saturation of $\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}$, $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}$, and $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}$

We now determine the exact saturation ranges of all three maps $\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$, $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$, and $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$.

**Lemma 3.0 (Weyl's Unitary Invariant-Dimension Lemma and $\mathbb{Q}$-Linear Independence up to $k = 2r_K + 1$).** *Let $T_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a non-degenerate Hermitian space of $K$-dimension $r_K = \frac{1}{2}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(T_{\mathbb{Q}})$ over $K \in \{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3), \mathbb{Q}(i)\}$ with unitary group $G := \operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)$, and let $L_{\mathbb{Q}} = N_{\mathbb{Q}} \oplus T_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $\rho = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(N_{\mathbb{Q}})$.*
1. *(**Quotient Invariants**) We have $\bigl((T_{\mathbb{Q}}^*)^{\otimes(2m+1)}\bigr)^G = 0$ for all $m \ge 0$, and for every even $k = 2m$, $\bigl((T_{\mathbb{Q}}^*)^{\otimes 2m}\bigr)^G$ is spanned over $\mathbb{Q}$ by the $R_m := (2m - 1)!!\,2^m$ complete-matching tensors built from $\bar\delta$ and $\bar\gamma$ (or equivalently from $\bar\delta$ and $\Psi_T$). Whenever $m \le r_K$—that is, for **all even degrees $k = 2m \le 2r_K$**—these $R_m$ generators are **$\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent** (with the first rational contraction relations occurring at $m = r_K + 1$, i.e., $k = 2r_K + 2$):*
   $$\boxed{\; \dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigl(\bigl((T_{\mathbb{Q}}^*)^{\otimes 2m}\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}\Bigr) \;=\; \binom{2m}{m}\,m! \;=\; \frac{(2m)!}{m!} \;=\; (2m - 1)!!\,2^m \;=:\; R_m \qquad (1 \le m \le r_K,\;\text{i.e.,}\; 2m \le 2r_K). \;}$$
2. *(**Full-Lattice Invariants**) Expanding $L_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k} = \bigoplus_{I \subset \{1, \dots, k\}} N_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes(k - |I|)} \otimes T_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes |I|}$ shows that for **all $1 \le k \le 2r_K + 1$** (where every even subset size $2m = |I| \le k$ satisfies $m \le r_K$), the $D_k := \sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} \binom{k}{2m}\rho^{k - 2m}(2m - 1)!!\,2^m$ partial-matching generators of $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ (both in the standard $(\delta_L, \gamma_L, N)$-basis and in the $(\delta_L, \widetilde{\Psi}_L, N_0, N_1)$-basis $\{Z_\alpha\}$ of Theorem 3.2(3)) are **$\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent** and form a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\bigl(L_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k}\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$. In particular, since $r_K \ge 1$ implies $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = r_K - a + 2 \le k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}} = r_K + 2 \le 2r_K + 1$, both integral thresholds $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}}$ lie **strictly inside the $\mathbb{Q}$-linear independence range**.*

*Proof.*
Over $K$, $G_K := \operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K) \times_{\mathbb{Q}} K \cong \operatorname{GL}_{r_K}(K)$ and $T_{\mathbb{Q}}^* \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K \cong V_+ \oplus V_+^*$ ($\dim_K V_+ = r_K$), where the projections onto $V_+ \otimes V_+^*$ and $V_+^* \otimes V_+$ are $K$-linear combinations of $\bar\delta$ and $\bar\gamma$. The center $K^\times \cdot I_{r_K}$ acts on $V_+^{\otimes p_0} \otimes (V_+^*)^{\otimes q_0}$ by $z^{p_0 - q_0}$, so invariants vanish unless $p_0 = q_0 = m$ ($k = 2m$ even). By Weyl's First Fundamental Theorem for $\operatorname{GL}_{r_K}(K)$, $(V_+^{\otimes m} \otimes (V_+^*)^{\otimes m})^{\operatorname{GL}_{r_K}(K)}$ is spanned for all $m \ge 1$ by the $m!$ complete-contraction permutations, which are linearly independent whenever $m \le r_K$ ($\iff 2m \le 2r_K$). Multiplying by the $\binom{2m}{m}$ slot placements of $V_+$ and $V_+^*$ yields $\binom{2m}{m}m! = (2m - 1)!!\,2^m = R_m$ for all $2m \le 2r_K$, proving (1). Part (2) follows by taking $G$-invariants on each direct summand $N_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes(k - 2m)} \otimes T_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes 2m}$ ($2m \le k \le 2r_K + 1 \implies m \le r_K$) and noting that the projections of $\{Z_\alpha\}$ onto the graded pieces in $N_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes(k - 2m)} \otimes T_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes 2m}$ are triangular in $m = |\pi|$ and invertibly related to the $(\bar\delta, \bar\gamma)$-basis on $T_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes 2m}$. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.1 (Darboux-Shifted Companion Basis and Quotient Saturation up to $k = r_K - a$).** *Let $(L, g)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 2.1 with $r_K - a = 2s_0$. On $(L/N)^{\otimes 2m}$ ($k = 2m$), the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-span of the $R_m = (2m - 1)!!\,2^m$ complete-matching generators $Z_{\pi, \epsilon} = \bigotimes_{s=1}^m \tau_{\epsilon_s}^{(a_s, b_s)}$ ($\tau_0 = \bar\delta, \tau_1 = \bar\gamma$) coincides with that of $\widetilde Z_{\pi, \epsilon} = \bigotimes_{s=1}^m \tilde\tau_{\epsilon_s}^{(a_s, b_s)}$ ($\tilde\tau_0 = \bar\delta, \tilde\tau_1 = \Psi_T$).*
1. *(**Darboux-Shifted Mod-$p$ Companion Basis**) Let $W := (L/N)^* \otimes \mathbb{F}_p = T/pT$, and define $\pi_g^\dagger := -\pi_g|_T$ ($p = 3$) or $\pi_g^\dagger := I - g_4^{-1}|_T = I + g_4|_T \equiv -\pi_g|_T \pmod 2$ ($p = 2$), so that $\pi_g \pi_g^\dagger = \pi_g^\dagger \pi_g = p\,\mathrm{id}_T$, $(\bar\pi_g^\dagger)^2 = 0$ on $W$, and $\Psi_T(x, y) = \bar\delta(x, \pi_g y) = \bar\delta(\pi_g^\dagger x, y)$ for all $x, y \in T$. Choose any Darboux basis $(e_1, \dots, e_{2s_0})$ of a $2s_0$-dimensional complement to $\ker(\overline{\Psi}_T)$ in $W$ with $\overline{\Psi}_T(e_i, e_j) = J_{i,j}$ ($J = \operatorname{diag}(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix})^{\oplus s_0}$), and set $v_i := \bar\pi_g^\dagger(e_i) = -\bar\pi_g(e_i) \in W$. Then $(\bar\pi_g^\dagger)^2 = 0$ forces $\overline{\Psi}_T(v_i, w) = 0$ ($\forall w \in W$), $\bar\delta(v_i, v_j) = 0$, and $\bar\delta(v_i, e_j) = \overline{\Psi}_T(e_i, e_j) = J_{i,j}$. Letting $S_{i,j} := \bar\delta(e_i, e_j) \in \mathbb{F}_p$ ($S_{i,i} = 0$ at $p = 2$ since $T$ is even) and defining the upper-triangular matrix $X$ by $X_{i,j} = -S_{i,j}$ ($i < j$), $X_{i,i} = -2^{-1}S_{i,i} \bmod 3$ ($p = 3$) or $0$ ($p = 2$) so that $X + X^T = -S \pmod p$, the **Darboux-shifted vectors***
   $$\tilde e_i \;:=\; e_i \;-\; \sum_{k=1}^{2s_0} (X J)_{i,k}\, v_k \;\in\; W \qquad (1 \le i \le 2s_0)$$
   *satisfy $\bar\delta(\tilde e_i, \tilde e_j) = S_{i,j} + X_{i,j} + X_{j,i} = 0$, $\overline{\Psi}_T(\tilde e_i, \tilde e_j) = J_{i,j}$, $\bar\delta(v_i, \tilde e_j) = J_{i,j}$, and $\overline{\Psi}_T(v_i, \tilde e_j) = 0$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Setting $(u_{s, 0}, u_{s, 1}, f_s) := (v_{2s-1}, \tilde e_{2s-1}, \tilde e_{2s})$ ($1 \le s \le s_0$) gives $\tilde\tau_\epsilon(u_{s, \eta}, f_t) = \delta_{s, t}\delta_{\epsilon, \eta}$ and $\tilde\tau_\epsilon(u_{s, \eta}, u_{t, \eta'}) = \tilde\tau_\epsilon(f_s, f_t) = 0$. Evaluating $\{\widetilde Z_{\pi, \epsilon}\}$ on $I(\pi', \eta) = w_1 \otimes \dots \otimes w_{2m}$ with $(w_{a'_s}, w_{b'_s}) = (u_{s, \eta_s}, f_s)$ yields the **exact $R_m \times R_m$ identity matrix $I_{R_m}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$** for all $2m \le r_K - a = 2s_0$.*
2. *(**Unramified Mod-$\ell$ Eigenbasis and Quotient Saturation**) At every prime $\ell \ne p$, $\det(\bar\delta) = p^a \in \mathbb{F}_\ell^\times$ and $T \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} A_\ell = V_+ \oplus V_-$ ($A_\ell = \mathbb{F}_\ell[\zeta] = \mathbb{F}_\ell[t]/(\Phi(t))$) splits into $\bar\delta$-isotropic, dually paired rank-$r_K$ $\bar g$-eigenspaces, where the eigen-projectors $\Pi_\pm = (\zeta^{\mp 1} - \zeta^{\pm 1})^{-1}(\bar\gamma - \zeta^{\pm 1}\bar\delta)$ yield an exact $R_m \times R_m$ identity submatrix $I_{R_m}$ over $A_\ell$ for all $m \le r_K$ ($2m \le 2r_K$). Combining (1), (2), and Lemma 3.0 proves that $\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ is **integrally saturated in $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$** for all $1 \le k \le r_K - a = 2s_0$ (and for $k = r_K + 1$ when $a = 0$, where $r_K + 1$ is odd so $((L/N)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes(r_K+1)})^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)} = 0$).*

*Proof.*
In (1), evaluating $\widetilde Z_{\pi, \epsilon}$ on $I(\pi', \eta)$ gives a product of $m$ pairings on the $2m$ slots $\{u_{s, \eta_s}, f_s\}_{s=1}^m$. Since all $u$-$u$ and $f$-$f$ pairings vanish and $\tilde\tau_\epsilon(u_{s, \eta_s}, f_{s'}) = \delta_{s, s'}\delta_{\epsilon, \eta_s}$, a non-zero product requires $\pi$ to pair each $u_{s, \eta_s}$ (at slot $a'_s$) with $f_s$ (at slot $b'_s$), forcing $\pi = \pi'$ and yielding $\prod_{s=1}^m \delta_{\epsilon_s, \eta_s} = \delta_{\epsilon, \eta}$. Part (2) follows by the identical argument on dual $\bar g$-eigenbases $\{e_s^+\}, \{e_t^-\}$ over $A_\ell$ ($\ell \ne p$), proving $\ell$-saturation for all primes $\ell$. $\blacksquare$

We now prove full-lattice saturation in $L^{\otimes k}$, allowing repeated glue basis vectors in $N_1$ (following the distinct-label method of [1, Section 6.2]).

**Theorem 3.2 (Jordan-Height-3 Glue Chains and Full-Lattice Unitary Saturation for All $k \le r_K + 1$).** *Under the hypotheses of Theorem 2.1, choose a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-orthogonal Jordan decomposition $N_{(p)} = N_0 \perp N_1$ where $N_0$ is $p$-unimodular of rank $\rho_0 := \rho - a$ and $N_1$ has rank $a$ with $b_{N_1} \equiv 0 \pmod p$ and $\operatorname{Smith}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(b_{N_1}) = (p^a)$, so that $L_{(p)} = N_0 \perp L_1$ where $L_1 := N_0^\perp \supset N_1 \oplus T$ is $p$-unimodular of rank $2r_K + a$.*
1. *(**Height-3 Jordan Glue Chains on $W_1 := L_1/pL_1$**) Let $n_1, \dots, n_a$ be a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-basis of $N_1$ and let $u_h = (n_h + t_h)/p \in L_1$ ($t_h \in T$) be the Nikulin glue generators. On $W_1 := L_1/pL_1$, $\bar\pi_g := \pi_g \bmod p$ satisfies $\bar\pi_g^3 = 0$ with $\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(\bar\pi_g^2) = a$, acting on each $u_h$ via the **height-$3$ Jordan chain***
   $$\boxed{\; \bar\pi_g:\; u_h \;\longmapsto\; w_h \;:=\; \bar\pi_g(u_h) \;\in\; T/pT \;\longmapsto\; \bar\pi_g(w_h) \;=\; n_h \;\in\; N_1/pN_1 \;\longmapsto\; 0 \qquad (1 \le h \le a). \;}$$
   *Under $\bar\delta_L = b_L \bmod p$, the matrix $B_{h, h'} := \bar\delta_L(n_h, u_{h'}) = \frac{1}{p}b_N(n_h, n_{h'}) \bmod p$ lies in $\operatorname{GL}_a(\mathbb{F}_p)$, while $\bar\delta_L(n_h, n_{h'}) = \bar\delta_L(n_h, w_{h'}) = 0$ and $\bar\delta_L(w_h, w_{h'}) = -B_{h, h'}$. Thus $H_{\mathrm{glue}} := \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(u_h, w_h, n_h)_{h=1}^a \subset W_1$ has $\det(\bar\delta_L|_{H_{\mathrm{glue}}}) = (-1)^a (\det B)^3 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and splits $W_1 = H_{\mathrm{glue}} \perp W_0$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} W_0 = 4s_0$), where $W_0 := H_{\mathrm{glue}}^\perp \subset \ker(\bar\pi_g^2)$ is $\bar\pi_g$- and $\bar\pi_g^\dagger$-invariant and carries the $s_0$ companion triples $(u_{\ell, 0}, u_{\ell, 1}, f_\ell)_{\ell=1}^{s_0}$ of Theorem 3.1(1).*
2. *(**Quadratic-Refinement Lift $\widetilde{\Psi}_L$ and Glue Companion Triples**) Define $\widetilde{\Psi}_L \in \operatorname{im}\Phi_2^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N) \subset L^{\otimes 2}$ by $\widetilde{\Psi}_L := \omega_L = \gamma_L - \gamma_L^T$ for $p = 3$, and for $p = 2$ by setting $\tilde n_h := \sum_{r=1}^a M_{h, r}\, n_r \in N_1$ ($M B \equiv I_a \pmod 2$) and adding to $\nu_L := \delta_L - \gamma_L$ the quadratic-refinement tensor $C_{N_1} \in N_1 \otimes N_1$:*
   $$\widetilde{\Psi}_L \;:=\; \nu_L \;+\; C_{N_1}, \qquad C_{N_1} \;:=\; \sum_{h=1}^a \frac{b_N(n_h, n_h)}{4}\, \tilde n_h \otimes \tilde n_h \;+\; \sum_{1 \le h < h' \le a} B_{h, h'}\, \tilde n_h \otimes \tilde n_{h'} \;\in\; N_1 \otimes N_1.$$
   *Then $\overline{\Psi}_L := \widetilde{\Psi}_L \bmod p$ is strictly alternating on all of $L/pL$, vanishes on $N_0 \oplus N_1$, keeps $W_0 \perp_{\overline{\Psi}_L} H_{\mathrm{glue}}$, and satisfies $\overline{\Psi}_L(x, w_h) = \bar\delta_L(x, n_h)$ on $W_1$. Setting $\hat u_h := \sum_{h'} (B^{-1})_{h, h'} u_{h'}$, $\check u_h := \hat u_h + \sum_{k > h} \overline{\Psi}_L(\hat u_h, \hat u_k)\, w_k$, $\tilde u_h := \check u_h + \sum_{k=1}^a X_{h, k}\, n_k$ ($X + X^T = -(\bar\delta_L(\check u_i, \check u_j)) \bmod p$), and $\tilde w_h := -w_h - \sum_{k=1}^a \bar\delta_L(-w_h, \tilde u_k)\, n_k$ yields $a$ **normalized glue companion triples** $(u_{s_0 + h, 0} := n_h,\; u_{s_0 + h, 1} := \tilde w_h,\; f_{s_0 + h} := \tilde u_h)_{h=1}^a$ in $H_{\mathrm{glue}}$ satisfying for all $1 \le h, h' \le a$:*
   $$\boxed{\; \bar\delta_L(\tilde u_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = \overline{\Psi}_L(\tilde u_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = 0,\quad \bar\delta_L(n_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = \overline{\Psi}_L(\tilde w_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = \delta_{h, h'},\quad \overline{\Psi}_L(n_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = \bar\delta_L(\tilde w_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = \bar\delta_L(n_h, \tilde w_{h'}) = 0. \;}$$
3. *(**Unitriangularity with Repeated Glue Basis Vectors for All $1 \le k \le r_K + 1$**) For every $1 \le k \le r_K + 1$, the invariant space $\bigl(L_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k}\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ has dimension $D_k = \sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} \binom{k}{2m}\rho^{k - 2m}(2m - 1)!!\,2^m$, and $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ is spanned over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ by the $D_k$ basis tensors $Z_{\alpha}$ indexed by tuples $\alpha = (S_0, c, \pi, r, b)$, where:*
   - *$S_0 \subset \{1, \dots, k\}$ ($r_0 := |S_0|$) is the subset of slots carrying vectors from a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-basis $\{d_1, \dots, d_{\rho_0}\}$ of $N_0$, specified by $c: S_0 \to \{1, \dots, \rho_0\}$;*
   - *$\pi = \{(i_1, j_1), \dots, (i_m, j_m)\}$ ($i_t < j_t$) is a partial matching of $\{1, \dots, k\} \setminus S_0$ of size $m = |\pi|$, with binary choice $r: \pi \to \{0, 1\}$ placing $\tilde\tau_{r(e)} \in \{\delta_L, \widetilde{\Psi}_L\}$ on each edge $e \in \pi$;*
   - *$R_1 := (\{1, \dots, k\} \setminus S_0) \setminus \bigcup_{e \in \pi} e$ ($r_1 := |R_1| = k - r_0 - 2m$) is the remaining set of unmatched slots, carrying glue basis vectors $n_{b(x)} \in N_1$ specified by an **arbitrary** function $b: R_1 \to \{1, \dots, a\}$ (allowing arbitrary repetitions of basis vectors $n_h$).*
   *Let $\beta(\alpha) := |b(R_1)| \le \min(a, r_1) \le \min(a, k - 2m)$ denote the number of **distinct** glue basis labels in $N_1$ used by $b$. Because*
   $$\boxed{\; \max_{1 \le m \le \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}\Bigl(m \;+\; \min(a,\, k - 2m)\Bigr) \;=\; \Bigl\lfloor \frac{k + a}{2} \Bigr\rfloor \;\le\; s_0 + a \;=\; \frac{r_K + a}{2} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k \;\le\; r_K + 1, \;}$$
   *for every tuple $\alpha_0 = (S_0^0, c_0, \pi_0, r_0, b_0)$ with $k \le r_K + 1$, the number of available companion triples in $W_0 \oplus H_{\mathrm{glue}}$ whose glue label $h$ does **not** belong to $b_0(R_1^0)$ is $s_0 + (a - \beta(\alpha_0)) \ge m_0 := |\pi_0|$. Choosing $m_0$ distinct companion indices $\ell_1 < \dots < \ell_{m_0}$ from $\{1, \dots, s_0\} \cup \{s_0 + h : h \notin b_0(R_1^0)\}$ and defining the test tuple $v(\alpha_0) = (v_1, \dots, v_k) \in (L/pL)^k$ by*
   $$v_s \;:=\; d_{c_0(s)}^* \;\; (s \in S_0^0), \qquad (v_{i_t}, v_{j_t}) \;:=\; (u_{\ell_t, r_0(e_t)},\, f_{\ell_t}) \;\; (e_t = (i_t, j_t) \in \pi_0), \qquad v_x \;:=\; \tilde u_{b_0(x)} \;\; (x \in R_1^0),$$
   *orders the $D_k$ tuples $\alpha$ (by increasing $r_0 = |S_0|$, then $S_0$ and $c$, then increasing $m = |\pi|$) so that the $D_k \times D_k$ evaluation matrix $M_k^{\mathrm{test}}(\alpha_0, \alpha) := Z_\alpha(v(\alpha_0)) \bmod p$ is **lower-unitriangular with $1$s on the main diagonal and $0$s strictly above the main diagonal**! Consequently, the $D_k$ generators $Z_\alpha$ are $\mathbb{Z}$-linearly independent and $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ is **integrally saturated in $L^{\otimes k}$ for all $1 \le k \le r_K + 1$, completely independently of $a$**.*

*Proof.*
For (1), since $p u_h = n_h + t_h$ and $\pi_g(n_h) = 0$, $w_h = \pi_g(u_h) = \frac{1}{p}\pi_g(t_h) \in T$. Applying $\pi_g$ again gives $\pi_g(w_h) = \frac{1}{p}\pi_g^2(t_h) = -t_h = n_h - p u_h \equiv n_h \pmod{pL_1}$ for $p = 3$ and $\pi_g(w_h) = -g_4(t_h) = n_h - t_h - g_4(t_h) - 2u_h \equiv n_h \pmod{2L_1}$ for $p = 2$. Moreover, $b_L(w_h, w_{h'}) = \frac{1}{p^2}b_T(\pi_g t_h, \pi_g t_{h'}) = \frac{1}{p}b_T(t_h, t_{h'}) = p\,b_L(u_h, u_{h'}) - \frac{1}{p}b_N(n_h, n_{h'}) \equiv -B_{h, h'} \pmod p$. For (2) at $p = 2$, $\overline{\Psi}_L = \nu_L + C_{N_1} \bmod 2$ satisfies $\nu_L(u_h, u_{h'}) + \nu_L(u_{h'}, u_h) = 2b_L(u_h, u_{h'}) - b_L(u_h, (g_4 + g_4^{-1})u_{h'}) = 2b_L(u_h, u_{h'}) - \frac{1}{2}b_N(n_h, n_{h'}) \equiv B_{h, h'} \pmod 2$ ($h \ne h'$) and $\nu_L(u_h, u_h) = b_L(u_h, u_h) - \frac{1}{4}b_N(n_h, n_h) \equiv \frac{1}{4}b_N(n_h, n_h) \pmod 2$, which are exactly canceled by $C_{N_1}(u_h, u_{h'}) \bmod 2$ since $b_L(\tilde n_r, u_h) \equiv \delta_{r, h} \pmod 2$.
To prove (3), fix a row tuple $\alpha_0 = (S_0^0, c_0, \pi_0, r_0, b_0)$ with test vector $v(\alpha_0) = (v_1, \dots, v_k)$ and a column generator $Z_\alpha$ with $\alpha = (S_0, c, \pi, r, b)$ such that $Z_\alpha(v(\alpha_0)) \ne 0 \pmod p$:
1. **Triangularity in $(S_0, c)$:** Because $N_0 \perp (W_0 \oplus H_{\mathrm{glue}})$ and $v_q \in W_0 \oplus H_{\mathrm{glue}}$ for all $q \notin S_0^0$, any $N_0$-slot $s \in S_0$ of $Z_\alpha$ evaluates to $\bar\delta_L(d_{c(s)}, v_s)$, which vanishes unless $s \in S_0^0$ and $c(s) = c_0(s)$. Thus $Z_\alpha(v(\alpha_0)) = 0$ whenever $S_0 \not\subset S_0^0$ (in particular, whenever $|S_0| > |S_0^0|$), and when $S_0 = S_0^0$ it vanishes unless $c = c_0$.
2. **Triangularity in $m = |\pi|$ and Diagonal Entry $1$ for $S_0 = S_0^0, c = c_0$:** Assume now that $S_0 = S_0^0$ and $c = c_0$. On the remaining $k - r_0^0$ slots $\{1, \dots, k\} \setminus S_0^0$, the vectors of $v(\alpha_0)$ belong to two disjoint classes:
   - the $m_0$ **"U-slots"** $U(\alpha_0) := \{i_1, \dots, i_{m_0}\}$ carrying $u_{\ell_t, r_0(e_t)}$ ($\ell_t \notin \{s_0 + h : h \in b_0(R_1^0)\}$);
   - the $m_0 + r_1^0$ **"F-slots"** $F(\alpha_0) := \{j_1, \dots, j_{m_0}\} \cup R_1^0$ carrying $f_{\ell_t}$ on $j_t$ and $\tilde u_{b_0(x)} = f_{s_0 + b_0(x)}$ on $x \in R_1^0$.
   Crucially, **even when $b_0: R_1^0 \to \{1, \dots, a\}$ repeats glue labels** ($b_0(x) = b_0(y)$ for distinct slots $x \ne y \in R_1^0$), the normalized glue relations $\bar\delta_L(\tilde u_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = \overline{\Psi}_L(\tilde u_h, \tilde u_{h'}) = 0$ of Part (2) hold for **all** $1 \le h, h' \le a$, including $h = h'$! Therefore, **every pair of F-slots has zero pairing under both $\bar\delta_L$ and $\overline{\Psi}_L$**:
   $$\tilde\tau_\epsilon(v_p, v_q) \;=\; 0 \pmod p \qquad \bigl(\forall\, p, q \in F(\alpha_0),\;\; \forall\,\epsilon \in \{0, 1\}\bigr).$$
   Consequently, in order for $Z_\alpha(v(\alpha_0)) \ne 0 \pmod p$, none of the $m = |\pi|$ matched edges of $\pi$ can connect two F-slots; every edge of $\pi$ must contain at least one U-slot from $U(\alpha_0)$. Since the $m$ edges of $\pi$ are disjoint and $|U(\alpha_0)| = m_0$, the pigeonhole principle forces
   $$m \;=\; |\pi| \;\le\; |U(\alpha_0)| \;=\; m_0,$$
   proving that $Z_\alpha(v(\alpha_0)) = 0 \pmod p$ strictly above the block diagonal ($m > m_0$)!
   Finally, when $m = m_0$, each of the $m_0$ edges of $\pi$ must use **exactly one** U-slot from $U(\alpha_0)$ and **exactly one** F-slot from $F(\alpha_0)$. Because every $\ell_t \notin \{s_0 + h : h \in b_0(R_1^0)\}$, each U-slot vector $u_{\ell_t, r_0(e_t)}$ has zero pairing under both $\bar\delta_L$ and $\overline{\Psi}_L$ with every $\tilde u_{b_0(x)}$ ($x \in R_1^0$) and with every $f_{\ell_{t'}}$ for $t' \ne t$, and pairs non-trivially with $f_{\ell_t}$ (at slot $j_t > i_t$) iff $r(\{i_t, j_t\}) = r_0(e_t)$, where the pairing is $+1$. Thus $\pi$ must be the exact matching $\pi_0$ with $r = r_0$, which leaves $R_1 = R_1^0$ carrying $v_x = \tilde u_{b_0(x)}$. On each $x \in R_1^0$, the linear factor $\bar\delta_L(n_{b(x)}, \tilde u_{b_0(x)}) = \delta_{b(x), b_0(x)}$ is non-zero iff $b(x) = b_0(x)$, where it equals $+1$. Thus $M_k^{\mathrm{test}}(\alpha_0, \alpha) = \delta_{\alpha_0, \alpha}$ for $m \ge m_0$, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$

### Extension to Full Cohomology $H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$ via Point Classes

We now show why point classes $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}] \in H^4(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$ are essential to pass from $L^{\otimes k}$ to $H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$, and prove component-by-component saturation for the extended map $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$.

**Proposition 3.3 (Point-Class Necessity for $N = U(3)$ and Full-Cohomology Saturation of $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$).** *Let $(S, g)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 2.1 with $N = \operatorname{NS}(S) = L^g = L^{g^2}$.*
1. *(**Point-Class Necessity Without $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}]$**) Let $S$ be an order-$3$ K3 surface with $N = L^{g_3} = U(3)$ ($\rho = 2, r_K = 10, a = 2$), which exists by Artebani–Sarti [3, Table 2 and Propositions 3.1–3.2]. In $H^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}([\mathrm{pt}] \otimes 1) \oplus (L \otimes L) \oplus \mathbb{Z}(1 \otimes [\mathrm{pt}])$ (where $\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}} H^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z})^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)} = \rho^2 + 2 + 2 = 8$), let $e, f$ be the standard basis of $U(3)$ ($e^2 = f^2 = 0, e \cdot f = 3$). Under the projection $H^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}([\mathrm{pt}] \otimes 1) \oplus \mathbb{Z}(1 \otimes [\mathrm{pt}]) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, products of divisors $\mathrm{pr}_1^* D_1 \cup \mathrm{pr}_1^* D_2$ and $\mathrm{pr}_2^* D_1 \cup \mathrm{pr}_2^* D_2$ land in $(3\mathbb{Z}, 0)$ and $(0, 3\mathbb{Z})$, mixed products $\mathrm{pr}_1^* D_1 \cup \mathrm{pr}_2^* D_2 \in L \otimes L$ project to $(0, 0)$, and $[\Delta_S], [\Gamma_{g^r}]$ project to $(1, 1)$. Thus the projected $2 \times 3$ generator matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 3 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ on the point summands has Smith invariant factors $(1, 3)$ (and $[\mathrm{pt}] \otimes 1 \in \operatorname{Hdg}^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z})$ generates an order-$3$ torsion class in the projected cokernel), while in the full cohomology group $H^4(S^2, \mathbb{Z})$ the unextended cycle map has Smith invariant factors $(1^{\rho^2+2}, 3^2) = (1^6, 3^2)$ (since $[\Delta_S] + [\Gamma_g] + [\Gamma_{g^2}] - (e \otimes f + f \otimes e) = 3([\mathrm{pt}] \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes [\mathrm{pt}])$ is the unique relation killing the $L \otimes L$-components of $[\Delta_S], [\Gamma_g], [\Gamma_{g^2}]$).*

Because $s_0 + 1 \le r_K$ on $(L/N)^{\otimes(2s_0+2)}$ and $r_K + 2 \le 2r_K + 1$ in $L^{\otimes(r_K+2)}$ for all $r_K \ge 1$, Lemma 3.0(1)–(2) implies that the matching generators of $\bar\Phi_{2s_0+2}^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ and $\Phi_{r_K+2}^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent; since $\Sigma_{\Psi, s_0}^\wedge$ and $\Omega_{s_0, a}^\wedge$ have coordinates $\pm 1 \notin p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ in the $(\bar\delta, \Psi_T)$ and $(\delta_L, \widetilde{\Psi}_L, N_0, N_1)$ generator bases, dividing by $p$ immediately yields $\frac{1}{p}\Sigma_{\Psi, s_0}^\wedge \notin \operatorname{im}\bar\Phi_{2s_0+2}^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ and $\frac{1}{p}\Omega_{s_0, a}^\wedge \notin \operatorname{im}\Phi_{r_K+2}^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$.

**Step 3 (Explicit Separating Functionals $\Lambda_{\mathrm{wit}}$ and $\bar\Lambda_{\mathrm{wit}}$ across All Cases).** To certify non-membership by direct evaluation modulo $p$, let $(\tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_a) \in L_1^a$ be integer lifts of the normalized glue companion vectors of Theorem 3.2(2) Step C, satisfying $\bar\delta_L(n_r, \tilde u_h) \equiv \delta_{r, h} \pmod p$, $\overline{\Psi}_L(n_r, \tilde u_h) \equiv 0 \pmod p$, and $\bar\delta_L(\tilde u_h, \tilde u_{h'}) \equiv \overline{\Psi}_L(\tilde u_h, \tilde u_{h'}) \equiv 0 \pmod p$:
1. **$s_0 \ge 1, p = 3$:** For $x_i \in T \cap H_{\mathrm{glue}}^\perp \pmod p$, $b_L(n, x_i) = 0$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ ($\forall n \in N$) and $\bar\delta_L(x_i, \tilde u_h) \equiv \overline{\Psi}_L(x_i, \tilde u_h) \equiv 0 \pmod p$, so any generator $Z_\alpha = Z_{S_0, c, \pi, r, b}$ of $\Phi_{r_K+2}^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$ vanishes on $(x_1, \dots, x_{2s_0+2}, \tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_a) \bmod p$ unless $S_0 = \emptyset$, $R_1 = \{2s_0+3, \dots, 2s_0+a+2\}$, and $b = \mathrm{id}_{\{1, \dots, a\}}$, reducing to $Z_{\pi, \epsilon}(x_1, \dots, x_{2s_0+2}) \bmod p$. With $\tilde v_1 = \pi_g^\dagger \tilde e_{2s_0-1}$ and $\tilde v_2 = \pi_g^\dagger \tilde e_{2s_0} \in 3L_1 \subset T \cap H_{\mathrm{glue}}^\perp \pmod 3$, define $w^{(1)} := (\tilde e_1, \dots, \tilde e_{2s_0-1}, \tilde e_{2s_0}, \tilde v_1, \tilde v_2)$ and $w^{(2)} := (\tilde e_1, \dots, \tilde e_{2s_0-1}, \tilde v_1, \tilde e_{2s_0}, \tilde v_2)$ (transposing slots $2s_0 \leftrightarrow 2s_0 + 1$), and set $\Lambda_{\mathrm{wit}}(Y) := Y(w^{(1)}, \tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_a) - Y(w^{(2)}, \tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_a)$ (or $\bar\Lambda_{\mathrm{wit}}(Y) := Y(w^{(1)}) - Y(w^{(2)})$ on $(L/N)^{\otimes(2s_0+2)}$). 2. **$s_0 \ge 1, p = 2$:** Fixing $(\tilde e_1, \dots, \tilde e_{2s_0-2})$ on the first $2s_0 - 2$ slots and writing $\{\tilde e_1, \tilde e_2\}$ for the last Darboux block with $\tilde v_1 = \pi_g^\dagger \tilde e_1, \tilde v_2 = \pi_g^\dagger \tilde e_2 \in 2L_1$, define on the last $4$ slots of $T^{2s_0+2}$ (labeled $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$) the three tuples $w^{(1)} := (\dots, \tilde e_1, \tilde e_1, \tilde e_2, \tilde v_2)$, $w^{(2)} := (\dots, \tilde e_1, \tilde v_2, \tilde e_2, \tilde e_1)$, and $w^{(3)} := (\dots, \tilde v_1, \tilde e_2, \tilde e_1, \tilde e_2)$, and set $\Lambda_{\mathrm{wit}}(Y) := \sum_{r=1}^3 Y(w^{(r)}, \tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_a)$. 3. **$s_0 = 0, p = 3$ (Universal Single-Tuple Evaluation Functional Covering Both Diagonal and Off-Diagonal $B$, Including $N = U(3)$):** Because $B_{h, h'} = \frac{1}{3}b_N(n_h, n_{h'}) \bmod 3$ is symmetric and non-degenerate on $\mathbb{F}_3^a$ ($a = r_K \ge 1$), it is not alternating: either $B_{1, 1} \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$, in which case we set $c := (1, 0, \dots, 0) \in \{0, 1\}^a$ and $(\tilde u'_1, \dots, \tilde u'_a) := (\tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_a)$; or else $B_{1, 1} \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and there exists $2 \le h_0 \le a$ with $B_{1, h_0} \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ (as for the standard hyperbolic basis $n_1, n_2$ of $N = U(3)$, where $B = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has $h_0 = 2$), in which case we set $c := e_1 + e_{h_0} \in \{0, 1\}^a$ (so $B(c, c) \equiv B_{h_0, h_0} + 2B_{1, h_0} \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$; if $B_{h_0, h_0} + 2B_{1, h_0} \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, then $B_{h_0, h_0} \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and we instead take $c := e_{h_0}$) and modify only $\tilde u'_{h_0} := \tilde u_{h_0} - \tilde u_1$ (keeping $\tilde u'_h := \tilde u_h$ for $h \ne h_0$, which preserves $\det(b_L(n_r, \tilde u'_h)) \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $\bar\delta_L(\tilde u'_h, \tilde u'_{h'}) \equiv \overline{\Psi}_L(\tilde u'_h, \tilde u'_{h'}) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$). Now define $z_1, z_2 \in T = N^\perp$ by
   $$w_c \;:=\; \sum_{h=1}^a c_h\,(-w_h) \;\in\; T, \qquad z_1 \;:=\; w_c \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^a \bigl(b_L(w_c, \tilde u_k) \bmod 3\bigr)\, t_k \;\in\; T, \qquad z_2 \;:=\; -\sum_{h=1}^a c_h\, t_h \;\in\; T,$$
   and let $\Lambda_{\mathrm{wit}} := \mathrm{ev}_{z_{\mathrm{wit}}}$ be evaluation on the **single witness tuple** $z_{\mathrm{wit}} := (z_1, z_2, \tilde u'_1, \dots, \tilde u'_a) \in T^2 \times L_1^a$!    - Since $t_k \equiv -n_k \pmod{3L_1}$, $\pi_g(t_k) = 3w_k \equiv 0 \pmod{3L_1}$, and $\bar\delta_L(n_k, \tilde u_r) \equiv \delta_{k, r} \pmod 3$, the shift in $z_1$ ensures $\bar\delta_L(z_1, \tilde u_r) \equiv b_L(w_c, \tilde u_r) - b_L(w_c, \tilde u_r) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ for all $1 \le r \le a$, while $\overline{\Psi}_L(z_1, \tilde u_r) \equiv \overline{\Psi}_L(w_c, \tilde u_r) \equiv c_r \pmod 3$.
   - Since $z_2 = -\sum_h c_h t_h \equiv \sum_h c_h n_h \pmod{3L_1}$, we have $\overline{\Psi}_L(z_2, y) = b_L(\pi_g z_2, y) = -3\sum_h c_h b_L(w_h, y) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ for **all** $y \in L_1$, and $\bar\delta_L(z_2, z_1) = b_L(z_2, z_1) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ because $z_2 \in N_1 \pmod{3L_1}$ and $z_1 \in T = N^\perp$.
   - Moreover, by construction of $\tilde u'_1, \dots, \tilde u'_a$, for both $z_1$ and $z_2$ all mod-$3$ pairings with $\tilde u'_2, \dots, \tilde u'_a$ (or with $\tilde u'_h$ for $h \ne h_0$ when $c = e_{h_0}$) **vanish modulo $3$**: when $c = (1, 0, \dots, 0)$, $\overline{\Psi}_L(z_1, \tilde u_r) \equiv \bar\delta_L(z_2, \tilde u_r) \equiv \delta_{1, r} \pmod 3$; when $c = e_1 + e_{h_0}$ and $\tilde u'_{h_0} = \tilde u_{h_0} - \tilde u_1$, we have $\overline{\Psi}_L(z_1, \tilde u'_{h_0}) \equiv 1 - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $\bar\delta_L(z_2, \tilde u'_{h_0}) \equiv 1 - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, so $\overline{\Psi}_L(z_1, \tilde u'_r) \equiv \bar\delta_L(z_2, \tilde u'_r) \equiv \delta_{1, r} \pmod 3$ for all $1 \le r \le a$!

## 5. Coupled K3 Pairs $S_1 \times S_2$: Bare and Divisor-Corrected Denominators $m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}}$ and $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}}$

On a product $S_1 \times S_2$ of two K3 surfaces linked by a Hodge isometry $\psi_T: T_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} T_2$ ($N_i = \operatorname{NS}(S_i)$, $T_i = N_i^\perp \subset L_i = H^2(S_i, \mathbb{Z})$, $A_{N_i} = N_i^*/N_i \cong A_{T_i}(-1)$), let $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T := (\mathrm{id}_{T_1} \otimes \psi_T)(b_{T_1}^{-1}) \in T_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes T_{2,\mathbb{Q}} \subset L_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes L_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$ be the pure transcendental correspondence tensor (compare [5]). To keep the bare tensor in $L_1 \otimes L_2$ strictly separate from rational algebraic corrections in $N_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes N_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$, we define two distinct positive integers:
$$\boxed{\; m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}} \;:=\; \min\Bigl\{m \ge 1 :\; m\,\Pi_{\psi_T}^T \;\in\; L_1 \otimes L_2\Bigr\}, \;}$$
$$\boxed{\; m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}} \;:=\; \min\Bigl\{m \ge 1 :\; m\,\Pi_{\psi_T}^T \;\in\; L_1 \otimes L_2 + N_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes N_{2,\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr\}. \;}$$
Here $m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}}$ is the exact denominator of the bare transcendental correspondence $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T$ in $L_1 \otimes L_2$ (with **no** algebraic correction allowed), whereas $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}}$ is the minimal multiplier needed to lift $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T$ into $L_1 \otimes L_2$ after adding a **rational** algebraic correction in $N_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes N_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$ (that is, the denominator of the image of $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T$ in $(L_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes L_{2,\mathbb{Q}})/(N_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes N_{2,\mathbb{Q}})$ relative to $(L_1 \otimes L_2)/(N_1 \otimes N_2)$).

**Theorem 5.1 (Gluing Bounds for $m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}}$ and $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}}$ on $S_1 \times S_2$).** *Let $\psi_T: T_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} T_2$ be an integral Hodge isometry.*
1. *(**Bare Transcendental Denominator $m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}}$**) Because $P_{T_1}(L_1) = T_1^*$ and $\psi_T(T_1^*) = T_2^*$, the bare transcendental correspondence $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T \in T_1^* \otimes T_2^* \subset L_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes L_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$ has exact denominator*
   $$m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}} \;=\; \exp(A_{T_1}) \;=\; \exp(A_{N_1}) \;=\; \exp(A_{N_2}).$$
   *In particular, when $S_1 = S_2$ with $\psi_T = \mathrm{id}_{T_1}$ on a projective K3 surface with hyperbolic Néron–Severi lattice $N_1 = N_2 = U(3)$ ($\operatorname{sig}(N_i) = (1, 1)$, $A_{N_i} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^2$, Artebani–Sarti [3, Table 2]) or $N_1 = N_2 = U \oplus A_2(-1)$ ($\operatorname{sig}(N_i) = (1, 3)$, $A_{N_i} \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$)—or on an abstract unimodular lattice model with $N_1 = N_2 = A_2$—we have $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^T = \delta_L - \Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^N$ where $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^N = b_{N_1}^{-1} \in \frac{1}{3}(N_1 \otimes N_1) \setminus (N_1 \otimes N_1)$ and $\delta_L \in L_1 \otimes L_1$, so the bare denominator is $m_{\mathrm{id}}^{\mathrm{bare}} = 3 = \exp(A_{N_1})$, whereas in the quotient by $N_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes N_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$ its class coincides with the integral class $\delta_L \bmod (N_1 \otimes N_1)$ of denominator $m_{\mathrm{id}}^{\mathrm{div}} = 1$.*
2. *(**Divisor-Corrected Denominator $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}}$**) Under Nikulin's gluing isomorphisms $\gamma_i: A_{N_i} \xrightarrow{\sim} A_{T_i}$, $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}}$ divides $\exp(A_{N_1})$ and equals $1$ if and only if the induced discriminant isomorphism $\gamma_2^{-1} \circ \bar\psi_T \circ \gamma_1: A_{N_1} \xrightarrow{\sim} A_{N_2}$ lifts to an integral homomorphism $\psi_N \in \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N_1, N_2)$ (in which case $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T + (\mathrm{id}_{N_1} \otimes \psi_N)(b_{N_1}^{-1}) \in L_1 \otimes L_2$ has rational algebraic correction in $\frac{1}{\exp(A_{N_1})}(N_1 \otimes N_2)$, giving $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}} = 1$). Conversely, if $\psi_T$ is merely a rational Hodge correspondence preserving $T_1 \to T_2$ such as $\pi_g|_T$ on $(S, g)$ with $a > 0$, then $\pi_g(T^*) \subset T$ implies $\Pi_{\pi_g}^T \in T^* \otimes T \subset L \otimes L$ has $m_{\pi_g}^{\mathrm{bare}} = m_{\pi_g}^{\mathrm{div}} = 1$, whereas $\frac{1}{p}\pi_g(T^*) \not\subset T^*$ gives $m_{\frac{1}{p}\pi_g}^{\mathrm{bare}} = m_{\frac{1}{p}\pi_g}^{\mathrm{div}} = p$.*

*Proof.*
Under $L_i \subset N_i^* \oplus T_i^*$, an element $x_1 \otimes x_2 \in T_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes T_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$ lies in $L_1 \otimes L_2$ iff it lies in $T_1 \otimes L_2 + L_1 \otimes T_2$. Writing $\mathrm{id}_{L_1} = \Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^{N_1} + \Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^{T_1}$, applying $\mathrm{id} \otimes \psi_T$ to $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^{T_1}$ shows that $\Pi_{\psi_T}^T \bmod (L_1 \otimes L_2)$ is represented by the image of $\bar\psi_T \in \operatorname{Hom}(A_{T_1}, A_{T_2}) \cong A_{T_1}^* \otimes A_{T_2}$ of exact order $\exp(A_{T_1})$, proving $m_\psi^{\mathrm{bare}} = \exp(A_{T_1}) = \exp(A_{N_1})$. Allowing a rational correction in $N_{1,\mathbb{Q}} \otimes N_{2,\mathbb{Q}}$ cancels this glue obstruction in $(N_1^* \oplus T_1^*) \otimes (N_2^* \oplus T_2^*) / (L_1 \otimes L_2)$ iff $\gamma_2^{-1} \circ \bar\psi_T \circ \gamma_1$ lifts to an integral map $N_1 \to N_2$, giving $m_\psi^{\mathrm{div}} = 1$. $\blacksquare$

## 6. Summary of Saturation Regimes and Thresholds on $S^k$

Our results establish a two-tier integral structure for the unitary cycle maps $\bar\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$, $\Phi_k^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)$, and $\widetilde\Phi_{k, d}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$:
1. **Saturated Regime ($k \le r_K - a = 2s_0$ in $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$, $k \le r_K + 1$ in $L^{\otimes k}$, and $\min(d, 2k - d) \le r_K + 1$ in $H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})$):** While bad-prime ramification $p^a \parallel \det(T)$ lowers the pure-quotient saturation bound on $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$ to $k = r_K - a$ (Theorem 3.1), Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 3.3 prove that in the full unimodular lattice $L^{\otimes k}$ the $a$ Jordan-height-3 Nikulin glue chains completely heal this discriminant defect up to $k = r_K + 1$, and adjoining point pullbacks $\mathrm{pr}_c^*[\mathrm{pt}]$ saturates $\bigl(H^{2d}(S^k, \mathbb{Z})\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ for all $\min(d, 2k - d) \le r_K + 1$.
2. **Ramification Thresholds ($k_{\mathrm{crit}} = r_K - a + 2$ in $(L/N)^{\otimes k}$ and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}} = r_K + 2$ in $L^{\otimes k}$) and the Modulo-6 $c_4$-Congruence on $S^4$:** By Lemma 3.0 and Theorem 4.1(1)–(2), both thresholds $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = r_K - a + 2$ and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{full}} = r_K + 2$ lie strictly inside the $\mathbb{Q}$-linear independence range $k \le 2r_K + 1$: the natural matching generators remain a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of the invariant space while becoming linearly dependent modulo $p$, failing to be saturated via $\frac{1}{p}\Sigma_{\Psi, s_0}^\wedge$ and $\frac{1}{p}\Omega_{s_0, a}^\wedge$. At $k = 4$, Theorem 4.1(3) establishes the congruence
   $$\bigl[c_4(\mathcal{F})\bigr]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 4}} \;\equiv\; -6\,\bigl[\operatorname{ch}_4(\mathcal{F})\bigr]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 4}} \pmod{\operatorname{im}\bar\Phi_4^{\mathrm{unit}}(L, N)}$$
   in $(L/N)^{\otimes 4}$ for any $\mathcal{F} \in D^b(S^4)$ with integral Mukai vector and lower Chern classes in $\operatorname{im}\widetilde\Phi_{4, \le 3}^{\mathrm{unit}}(S)$. When in addition $[\operatorname{ch}_4(\mathcal{F})]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 4}} \in \bigl((L/N)^{\otimes 4}\bigr)^{\operatorname{U}(T_{\mathbb{Q}}, K)}$ (which holds automatically when $E(S) = K$), $[c_4(\mathcal{F})]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 4}}$ vanishes in the quotient torsion cokernel $\mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}$ whenever $\mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}$ is $p$-elementary (such as at $s_0 = 1$, where $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = 4$), and when $\mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}$ has higher exponent $p^e$ ($e \ge 2$, as at $r_K = a = 2$, $s_0 = 0$, where $\exp(\mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}) = p^2$), $[c_4(\mathcal{F})]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 4}}$ lies in $p\,\mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}$ and vanishes in $\mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}$ if and only if $[\operatorname{ch}_4(\mathcal{F})]_{(L/N)^{\otimes 4}} \in \mathcal{T}_4^{\mathrm{quot}}[p]$.
