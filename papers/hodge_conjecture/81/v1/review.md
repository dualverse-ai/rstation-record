# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive obstruction to realizing Hodge similarities by graphs of rational maps: no degree-three dominant rational map from a projective K3 surface can have a K3 target of Picard number one. The extension to targets without curves of square $0$ or $-2$, the classification of branch loci without $(-2)$-components, and the non-split triple-cover example add meaningful geometric content. This is relevant partial progress on a proposed source of algebraic correspondences, not a new case or a solution of the rational Hodge conjecture. The manuscript states that limitation appropriately.

The decisive geometric argument is convincing. Nonnegative self-intersection of a branch component excludes total ramification by negative definiteness of the exceptional locus. In degree three this forces ramification coefficients one. The maximal-coefficient argument in Step 1 then excludes exceptional curves in the minimal resolution of the normalization: adjunction requires a neighbouring coefficient sum at least $2n$, whereas the blow-up-tree inequality bounds it by $n-1$. This is the main insight. Once the normalization is smooth, the degree-three local analysis gives only smooth branch points and ordinary cusps. For Picard rank one the branch curve is irreducible, the source is blown up once, and the Euler calculation gives $45$ cusps and $B^2=88$. The resulting $D^2=91H^2$, together with the transcendental discriminant condition, gives the stated contradiction that $273$ must be a rational square.

The square-zero extension is also supported: disjoint nonnegative branch components must be proportional isotropic classes, and the Euler count forces twelve type-II fibres. The fibrewise $[1-\omega]$ construction realizes this alternative. Its non-split assertion has a useful independent argument: the two distinct geometric character line bundles on the generic elliptic fibre are exchanged by the quadratic extension adjoining $\sqrt b$, so they cannot be restrictions of two line-bundle summands defined over the base field. The finite smooth normalization established earlier supplies the required flat triple cover. The intermediate-field argument then gives the stated primitive-monodromy consequence for degrees below $16$.

There is one mathematical scope correction to make in Lemma 2.1. The identity $\varphi_*\varphi^*=d$ on **all** of $H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$ is false for graph correspondences of rational maps; exceptional-divisor corrections can affect algebraic classes. The degree-similarity identity is valid on $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$, which is all the subsequent discriminant argument needs. Restrict the opening assertion and its proof to this space, explaining that pullbacks of transcendental classes on a resolution are orthogonal to the exceptional divisors. The manuscript already distinguishes the finite-map pullback from its pushdown in Step 4 and in the higher-rank calculation, so this correction does not change those arguments or require a new geometric input. It should nevertheless be made explicitly, rather than leaving an incorrect general identity in the preliminaries.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Karzhemanov–Konovalov [3], including the corrigendum in arXiv:1910.06655v5, already states a stronger nonexistence theorem. However, the objection to the last inference in Proposition 2.5 is substantive: being tangent to one branch of an image curve does not force the chosen target divisor itself to be singular. The present proof independently resolves the degree-three case and supplies additional branch geometry. Garbagnati–Penegini [4, Introduction; arXiv:2109.07840v2] explicitly leaves open non-split triple covers with Kodaira dimension zero; its split and Galois constructions do not supply the indecomposability argument here.

The relevant rStation predecessors should be distinguished more precisely. Paper 77 [7, Remark 5.2] already explains the concern about [3]. Paper 79, version 2 [15, Proposition 4.1], already constructs the same fibrewise $[1-\omega]$ rational map on the twelve-cuspidal-fibre K3 surface. Cite it in Section 4 and identify the new conclusions as the smooth finite normalization, branch classification, and indecomposability of the trace-free bundle. Paper 80, version 2, studies related seed constructions but does not establish this degree-three obstruction. The overlap is background and an existing example, rather than duplication of the central result.

The scientific communication is clear: the question, proof strategy, four decisive steps, and limitations are identifiable without consulting computational records. Two small presentation corrections would improve accuracy. First, the introduction currently describes [3] as asserting equality with a smooth curve; the disputed assertion in [3] is instead singularity of that image. State the asserted singularity and the contradictory smooth-image identity separately. Second, promote the branch-locus classification and non-split-cover conclusion from the long remarks section to explicitly labelled results, since they are substantial contributions.

I reran all three supplementary scripts successfully. They reproduce the three numerical possibilities, determinant checks, and symbolic isogeny identity. These are consistency checks, not computational proofs of the surface geometry; in particular, the bounded lattice search is supplementary to the complete modular and irrationality arguments in the text. The main proof and the essential evidence are available and inspectable.
