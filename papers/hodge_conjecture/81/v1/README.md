# No rational maps of degree three onto K3 surfaces of Picard rank one

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-10-31T12:08:45.028052Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/81/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove that there is no dominant rational map of degree $3$ from a complex projective K3 surface onto a K3 surface of Picard number one. Rational maps of degree $\lambda$ between K3 surfaces induce Hodge similarities of multiplier $\lambda$ on transcendental lattices, and for nonsquare $\lambda$ the algebraicity of such similarities is an open part of the Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces; the theorem shows that at Picard number one no map of degree $3$ induces one. A published argument for all degrees (Karzhemanov–Konovalov) relies on a step we could not verify, and Galois maps are easy to exclude, so degree $3$ is the first open case. The proof uses the symplectic forms: the canonical divisor of a resolution of the normalization is exceptional over the source, so its coefficients obey the rules of a tree of point blow-ups. With the negativity lemma they show that the normalization is smooth, the ramification is simple, and the branch curve is a rational curve with only ordinary cusps. An Euler number count gives $45$ cusps and $B^2 = 88$, and the discriminant of the transcendental lattice requires $273$ to be a square. More generally, the branch curve of a map of degree $3$ between K3 surfaces contains a $(-2)$-curve, unless the target has an elliptic fibration with twelve cuspidal fibres and these form the branch curve; fibrewise isogenies show that this case occurs and give non-split triple covers with $\kappa = 0$, answering a question of Garbagnati and Penegini. In particular the theorem holds for targets without $(-2)$-curves and elliptic curves. Rational maps of degree $d < 16$ from K3 surfaces onto a K3 surface of Picard number one have primitive monodromy. No new case of the Hodge conjecture is proved.
