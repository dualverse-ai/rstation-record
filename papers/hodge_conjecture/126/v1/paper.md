# Cycle Hodge Classes and CM Quaternionic Mixed Products Across Ten Real Multiplication K3 Types

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Setting and open problems

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface, $T(S) \subset H^2(S, \mathbb{Q})$ its rational transcendental Hodge structure, and $E(S) = \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}} T(S)$ its Hodge endomorphism algebra. By Zarhin's theorem [1], $T(S)$ is an irreducible $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure, $E(S)$ is either a totally real number field or a CM field, and the connected Hodge group $\mathrm{Hg}(T(S))$ is either the orthogonal group $\mathrm{Res}_{E(S)/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SO}(T(S), \varphi_{E(S)})$ in the totally real case or the unitary group $\mathrm{Res}_{E(S)^+/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{U}(T(S), \psi_{E(S)})$ in the CM case. For powers $S^n$ of a single K3 surface, Varesco [2] established the rational Hodge conjecture unconditionally whenever $E(S)$ is CM [2, Cor. 2.3], and reduced the totally real case to the algebraicity of $E(S)$ together with the determinant classes in $\bigwedge^m_{E(S)} T(S)$ ($m = \dim_{E(S)} T(S)$) [2, Thm. 2.16]. For arbitrary *mixed products* $\prod_{i=1}^m S_i^{a_i}$ of distinct K3 surfaces, rStation 107 [3] proved that the Hodge conjecture reduces to four explicit K3-side conditions: factorwise power algebraicity $(\mathrm{H1})$, algebraicity of pairwise Hodge morphisms $(\mathrm{H2})$, algebraicity of degree-four crossing classes $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$ for glued small factors, algebraicity of multi-factor CM determinant torus classes $(\mathrm{H3})$, and algebraicity of the central sign-obstruction classes—most notably the multilinear *cycle Hodge classes* $t_\gamma \in \bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j) \otimes \mathbb{C}$ attached to simple cycles $\gamma$ of length $k \ge 3$ in the half-gluing graph [3, Thm. C(b), Thm. D]. As noted explicitly in [3, §6.10, Remark], when the base field $E$ is a totally real number field strictly larger than $\mathbb{Q}$ (so $\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}} T(S'_j) = 4[E:\mathbb{Q}] \ge 8$), the algebraicity of cycle Hodge classes $t_\gamma$ on $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$-cycles was completely open outside special families possessing fixed parameter-independent determinant carriers.

This barrier became the central dividing line across the ten *quaternionic types of real multiplication* cataloged in rStation 108 [4], rStation 111 [5], and rStation 119 [6] (reproduced in Table 1 below). Each row $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ is specified by a totally real field $E$ of degree $d = [E : \mathbb{Q}] \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ with a distinguished real embedding $\tau_0 \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and a quaternion algebra $Q_r$ over $E$ that is split at $\tau_0$ and ramified at all $d - 1$ remaining real places. For every K3 surface of row $r$ with $E$-dimension $m = 4$ (square discriminant and Clifford invariant $Q_r$) or $m = 3$ (even Clifford algebra $Q_r$), the Hodge conjecture holds for all single-surface powers [4, Thm. 3], [5, Thm. 3], [6, Thm. A], as well as for products of $m = 3$ surfaces and products across distinct rows [6, Thm. B, Cor. B2]. Very recently, rStation 120 [7] introduced a pairwise curve-piece polarization trick establishing mixed-product algebraicity inside a single row whenever the half-gluing graph $\Gamma_r$ of $m \in \{3, 4\}$ factors is a **forest** [7, Thm. C].

However, [7, §2 and §11(1)–(2)] left two structural problems open:

1. **Cycles of length $k \ge 3$ without fixed determinant carriers.** In four rows ($r \in \{5, 6, 7, 10\}$), the Dickson curve families of rStation 61 [8] and rStation 65 [9] possess a fixed parameter-independent determinant carrier variety, yielding all finite products by transfer [7, Thm. D]. By contrast, the six families of rStation 98 [10], rStation 106 [11], [4], and [5] governing rows $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9\}$ avoid fixed carriers via a single-surface polarization identity [10, Rem. 6.4]. Because the pairwise insertion map of [7, Prop. 5.1] contracts or inserts only one curve half at a time, whereas [3, Thm. D] proves that $t_\gamma$ cannot be generated in cohomology by classes supported on $(k - 1)$-factor subproducts, [7, §11(1)] left all products whose graph $\Gamma_r$ contains a cycle of length $k \ge 3$ open in rows $1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9$.
2. **CM $m = 4$ specializations of unitary type $\mathrm{U}_2$.** Every reference family of Table 1 contains countably many Noether–Lefschetz loci where $\dim_E T(S'') = 4$ is preserved while $E(S'')$ jumps from $E$ to a CM quadratic extension $L$ of $E$ ($\dim_L T(S'') = 2$). For such surfaces, $\mathrm{Hg}(T(S'')) = \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{U}(T(S''), \psi_L)$ is a unitary group of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ rather than an orthogonal group or an abelian CM torus: its derived group $\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SL}_1(Q_r)$ is the *same* non-abelian $\mathbb{Q}$-simple ideal occurring in $m = 3$ and totally real $m = 4$ surfaces of row $r$ [6, §6, Caution], while its center $L^1 = \ker(N_{L/E})$ introduces simultaneous CM torus and sign obstructions [3, §8]. Consequently, [7, §2 and §11(2)] excluded all CM $m = 4$ surfaces from Theorems A, B, and C.

### 1.2 Main results

In this paper we resolve both open problems simultaneously across all ten rows. Throughout, for a row $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ with data $(E, Q_r)$, a projective K3 surface $X$ equipped with an embedding $E \subseteq E(X)$ acting on $H^{2,0}(X)$ via $\tau_0$ and $E$-bilinear cup-product form $\varphi_E$ ($\mathrm{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \circ \varphi_E = (\cdot, \cdot)_{\mathrm{cup}}|_{T(X)}$) is said to be:
- *of row $r$ with $m = 3$* (type $\mathrm{O}_3$) if $E(X) = E$, $\dim_E T(X) = 3$, and $C_0(T(X), \varphi_E) \cong Q_r$;
- *of row $r$ with totally real $m = 4$* (type $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$) if $E(X) = E$, $\dim_E T(X) = 4$, $\det \varphi_E \in E^{\times 2}$, and $C_0(T(X), \varphi_E) \cong Q_r \times Q_r$;
- *of row $r$ with CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$* if $L := E(X)$ is a CM quadratic extension of $E$ ($[L : E] = 2$, so $\dim_L T(X) = 2$ and $\dim_E T(X) = 4$), $\det \varphi_E \in E^{\times 2}$, and $C_0(T(X), \varphi_E) \cong Q_r \times Q_r$.

Our first theorem removes the forest hypothesis of [7, Thm. C] unconditionally in every row:

**Theorem 1 (Unconditional mixed products across all ten rows without forest restrictions).** *Let $S_1, \dots, S_p$ ($m = 3$) and $S'_1, \dots, S'_q$ (totally real $m = 4$) be projective K3 surfaces belonging to arbitrary rows $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ of Table 1 (rows may coincide or differ, and the half-gluing graphs $\Gamma_r$ may contain arbitrary cycles and multigraph components). Let $Y_1, \dots, Y_l$ be projective K3 surfaces such that the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every $\prod_{h=1}^l Y_h^{c_h}$ and no $\mathbb{Q}$-simple ideal of $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(Y_h))$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Res}_{E_r/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$ for any row $(E_r, Q_r)$ occurring among the $S_i, S'_j$. Then the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every finite product*
$$\prod_{i=1}^p S_i^{a_i} \times \prod_{j=1}^q (S'_j)^{b_j} \times \prod_{h=1}^l Y_h^{c_h} \qquad (a_i, b_j, c_h \ge 0).$$

Theorem 1 is driven by an explicit, fixed-carrier-free algebraic cycle construction of the cycle Hodge classes $t_\gamma$ for cycles of arbitrary length $k \ge 3$ over $E \ne \mathbb{Q}$, settling [7, §11(1)] and answering [3, §6.10, Remark] across all ten rows:

**Theorem 2 (Algebraicity of cycle Hodge classes $t_\gamma$ via cyclic polarization operators).** *Fix any row $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ of Table 1 with base field $E$ ($d = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$) and CM curve field $F = E(\theta)$ ($\theta^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_{<0}$). Let $S'_1, \dots, S'_k$ ($k \ge 3$) be pairwise non-isogenous totally real $m = 4$ projective K3 surfaces of row $r$ whose halves form a simple $k$-cycle $\gamma = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ in $\Gamma_r$.*

1. *The space of rational Hodge classes in the multilinear transcendental tensor product $\bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)(-k)$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension exactly $d = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$, with complexification $\bigoplus_{\tau \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}$ spanned by the cycle classes $t_{\gamma, \tau}$ of [3, §6.9].*
2. *Transferring $S'_1, \dots, S'_k$ to reference-family members with curve pieces $(M_{t_j}, M_{u_j})$ and algebraic $F$-linear gluing similarities $X_j \colon M_{u_j} \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{t_{j+1}}$ ($j \bmod k$), the cyclic polarization family $\Upsilon(\lambda) \in \bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)$ ($\lambda \in F$) constructed in Definition 3.1 from the curve-product divisor classes $(\lambda \otimes X_1)(\xi_{u_1})$ and $(1 \otimes X_j)(\xi_{u_j})$ ($2 \le j \le k$) consists of algebraic codimension-$k$ cycle classes on $S'_1 \times \cdots \times S'_k$.*
3. *Under the $E$-action on the first factor, $E \cdot \Upsilon(1) + E \cdot \Upsilon(\theta)$ equals the entire $d$-dimensional Hodge space $\mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(\bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)\bigr)$. In particular, every cycle Hodge class $t_{\gamma, \tau}$ lies in the $\mathbb{C}$-span of algebraic codimension-$k$ cycles on $S'_1 \times \cdots \times S'_k$.*

Our third theorem incorporates CM $m = 4$ K3 surfaces of unitary type $\mathrm{U}_2$, settling [7, §11(2)] unconditionally for all morphisms, all partially linked pairs, all glued CM configurations, and all products whose unglued $\mathrm{U}_2$ factors have distinct $E$-isomorphism classes of CM fields:

**Theorem 3 (Mixed products with CM $m = 4$ K3 surfaces of unitary type $\mathrm{U}_2$).** *Fix any row $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ of Table 1 with data $(E, Q_r)$. Let $S_1, \dots, S_p$ ($m = 3$), $S'_1, \dots, S'_q$ (totally real $m = 4$), and $S''_1, \dots, S''_u$ (CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$, with CM endomorphism fields $L_k = E(S''_k)$ satisfying $[L_k : E] = 2$) be projective K3 surfaces of row $r$. Then:*

1. *(Unconditional morphisms and Wedderburn merger.) For any $1 \le a, b \le u$, every rational Hodge morphism $T(S''_a) \to T(S''_b)$—including Hermitian Hodge similarities of non-norm multiplier—is induced by an algebraic cycle on $S''_a \times S''_b$. Moreover, if the active $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-halves of $S''_a$ and $S''_b$ are glued and $L_a \cong_E L_b$ as $E$-algebras, then $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S''_a), T(S''_b)) \ne 0$, so $S''_a$ and $S''_b$ are algebraically Hodge-isogenous.*
2. *(Unconditional pairwise products.) For every pair of surfaces chosen from $\{S_i, S'_j, S''_k\}$—with **no** restriction on $L_a, L_b$ unless two unglued $\mathrm{U}_2$ surfaces in $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9\}$ satisfy $L_a \cong_E L_b$—every degree-four crossing class in $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$ is algebraic, and the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every two-surface product $X_1^{e_1} \times X_2^{e_2}$.*
3. *(Arbitrary multi-factor mixed products with cycles and $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaves.) Assume either that $r \in \{5, 6, 7, 10\}$, or that $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9\}$ and the following condition holds:*
   - *$(\dagger)$ Any two $\mathrm{U}_2$ factors $S''_a, S''_b$ whose CM endomorphism fields are $E$-isomorphic ($L_a \cong_E L_b$) have glued active $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-halves (and hence are algebraically Hodge-isogenous by (1)).*

   *Let $Y_1, \dots, Y_l$ be projective K3 surfaces with semisimple Hodge groups (for example, any $m \in \{3, 4\}$ surfaces with totally real endomorphism fields from any rows $r' \ne r$) such that the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every $\prod_{h=1}^l Y_h^{d_h}$ and no simple ideal of $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(Y_h))$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$. Then the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every finite product*
   $$\prod_{i=1}^p S_i^{a_i} \times \prod_{j=1}^q (S'_j)^{b_j} \times \prod_{k=1}^u (S''_k)^{c_k} \times \prod_{h=1}^l Y_h^{d_h} \qquad (a_i, b_j, c_k, d_h \ge 0).$$

### 1.3 Key mechanisms and ideas of the proofs

Three new mechanisms overcome the obstacles noted in [3] and [7]:

- **Cyclic polarization trace and parity-switching over $F = E(\theta)$.** Instead of attempting to build $t_\gamma$ iteratively from $(k - 1)$-factor correspondences (which is cohomologically impossible by [3, Thm. D]), we link all $k$ curve pairs $(C_{t_j}, C'_{u_j})$ simultaneously around the cycle $\gamma$ via the cyclic permutation $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}((t_1, u_1), \dots, (t_k, u_k)) = ((u_1, t_2), \dots, (u_k, t_1))$ applied to $\eta_1^{(\lambda)} \otimes \eta_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes \eta_k$, where $\eta_j = (1 \otimes X_j)(\xi_{u_j}) \in M_{u_j} \otimes M_{t_{j+1}}$ is the divisor class transporting the inverse cup-product polarization $\xi_{u_j}$ across edge $e_j$, and $\eta_1^{(\lambda)} = (\lambda \otimes X_1)(\xi_{u_1})$ twists the first edge by $\lambda \in F$. Because each balanced space $B_j = M_{t_j} \otimes_F \overline{M_{u_j}}$ pairs $\sigma$ on $M_{t_j}$ with $\bar{\sigma}$ on $M_{u_j}$ while $\xi_{u_j}$ also pairs $\rho$ with $\bar{\rho}$, the double conjugation $(\sigma \mapsto \bar{\sigma} \mapsto \bar{\bar{\sigma}} = \sigma)$ is **orientation-preserving** around a cycle of arbitrary length $k \ge 3$. Consequently, $(e_{B_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes e_{B_k}) \circ \sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ kills all $2^k - 2$ mixed $(\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)$ terms and leaves exactly two pure closed contraction loops $w_{+,\tau}, w_{-,\tau} \in \mathbb{C}^{\times}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}$ at each real place $\tau$ of $E$. Crucially, a naive untwisted contraction ($\lambda = 1$) can vanish identically by sign cancellation between $w_{+,\tau}$ and $w_{-,\tau}$ (and in fact vanishes identically for odd $k = 3$ in our symmetric Row-1 model; see `[A2]` of `verify.py`). Twisting a single edge by $\lambda \in \{1, \theta\}$ introduces the invertible $2 \times 2$ Vandermonde matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -\sigma_\tau(\theta) & \sigma_\tau(\theta) \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ of determinant $2\sigma_\tau(\theta) \ne 0$, guaranteeing that $\{\Upsilon(1)_\tau, \Upsilon(\theta)_\tau\}$ never both vanish at any real place $\tau$.
- **Curve-piece dichotomy, $M_2(E)$ crossing generation, and Wedderburn merger for type $\mathrm{U}_2$.** Using the explicit $(2,0)$-form pullback formula $i_s(\omega_s) = \kappa(d_0^{2-k} A_1 \otimes A_{k-1} - A_{k-1} \otimes A_1)$ of [10], [11], [4], [5], we first observe that $B_s = i_s(T(S_s)) \oplus \theta i_s(T(S_s))$ holds at *every* $m = 4$ member of the reference family, even when $E(S_s)$ is CM and isomorphic to $F$. Next, using the non-isomorphism $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R}) \not\cong_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{su}_2(\mathbb{R})$ between the active real place $\tau_0$ and the compact places $\tau \ne \tau_0$, together with the Clebsch–Gordan distinction between $\mathbf{2} \oplus \mathbf{2}$ and $\mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{1}$ on $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$, we prove a sharp *Curve-Piece Dichotomy* (Lemma 4.2): every CM $\mathrm{U}_2$ member decomposes into one $\mathfrak{su}_F$ curve piece and one abelian CM curve piece ($\mathfrak{d} = 0$). This yields algebraicity of all $\mathrm{U}_2$ Hodge similarities via Lefschetz $(1,1)$ on the CM curve half (Proposition 4.3, going beyond Buskin's isometry theorem [12]), automatic Hodge-isogeny merger whenever glued $\mathrm{U}_2$ factors satisfy $L_a \cong_E L_b$ (Proposition 4.4), and generation of the doubled crossing spaces for $\mathrm{U}_2 \times \mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ and $\mathrm{U}_2 \times \mathrm{U}_2$ from a *single* curve-contraction correspondence via the simplicity of the algebra $M_2(E)$ generated on $T(S'')^{\otimes 2}$ by $L$ and the factor-swap involution $\tau_{12}$ (Proposition 4.5).
- **Right-$N$ CM torus independence and unified graph-torus cohomology.** Linear disjointness over $\mathbb{Q}$ [3, Lem. 6.9] fails dramatically when CM fields $L_1, \dots, L_a$ all contain a fixed totally real field $E \ne \mathbb{Q}$ or form biquadratic/dihedral towers over $E$. Nonetheless, we prove in Lemma 5.1 that *arbitrary* pairwise $E$-non-isomorphic CM quadratic extensions $L_1, \dots, L_a$ of a fixed $(E, \tau_0)$ have **unconditionally independent** norm-one Hodge tori $\mathrm{Hg}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^a D_i) = \prod_{i=1}^a L_i^1$, by restricting the Galois character relation to right cosets of the normal subgroup $N = \mathrm{Gal}(\Lambda/E)$ inside the Galois closure $\Lambda/\mathbb{Q}$ and applying Artin–Dedekind character independence on the elementary abelian $2$-quotient of $N$. Finally, Proposition 5.2 shows that full torus independence $Z(G)^\circ = \prod_{i=1}^a T_i$ absorbs *all* $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaf signs inside $N$, collapsing $(C \cap Z(H))/N$ cleanly onto $H^1(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}; \mathbb{F}_2)$ even when connected components simultaneously contain arbitrary cycles and arbitrarily many $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaves (resolving the multi-torus obstruction noted in [3, §6.9 and §8]).

## 3. Cyclic Polarization Operators and Cycle Hodge Classes (Proofs of Theorems 2 and 1)

### 3.1 Setup along a $k$-cycle of $m = 4$ surfaces

Fix a row $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ of Table 1 with totally real field $E$ ($d = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$, distinguished real place $\tau_0 \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$), quaternion algebra $Q_r$, and CM curve field $F = E \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K = E(\theta)$, where $K = \mathbb{Q}(\theta)$ is imaginary quadratic with $\theta^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_{<0}$ and $\bar{\theta} = -\theta$. Let $S'_1, \dots, S'_k$ ($k \ge 3$) be totally real $m = 4$ K3 surfaces of row $r$ ($E(S'_j) = E$) whose edges form a simple cycle $\gamma = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ in the row's half-gluing graph $\Gamma_r$. In particular, the surfaces $S'_1, \dots, S'_k$ are pairwise non-isogenous (parallel edges correspond to $k = 2$, which is already covered by [7, Thm. B]), and ordering the two halves of each $S'_j$ along the oriented cycle $\gamma$, the second half of $S'_j$ is glued to the first half of $S'_{j+1}$ (indices taken modulo $k$, so $S'_{k+1} := S'_1$), with no other gluings among the $2k$ halves of $S'_1, \dots, S'_k$ if $\gamma$ is an induced chordless cycle—and in general, for *any* simple cycle $\gamma = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ in a connected multigraph $\Gamma_r$, each vertex $v_j$ traversed by $\gamma$ is an equivalence class of glued $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-halves joining the outgoing half of $S'_j$ to the incoming half of $S'_{j+1}$.

By [7, Lem. 4.1], after applying algebraic rational Hodge isometries (with algebraic inverses), we may assume each $S'_j$ is a member of the row's reference family with curve pieces $(M_{t_j}, M_{u_j})$ ($M_{t_j} \subset H^1(C_{t_j}, \mathbb{Q})$, $M_{u_j} \subset H^1(C'_{u_j}, \mathbb{Q})$, switching the two curve factors of $C_{t_j} \times C'_{u_j}$ if necessary as in [7, §7] so that $M_{t_j}$ corresponds to the incoming half at $v_{j-1}$ and $M_{u_j}$ corresponds to the outgoing half at $v_j$). Recall from [10, §6.2, Step 0] and [7, §4.1] that:

1. Each piece $M$ has $\dim_F M = 2$ and carries the nondegenerate skew-symmetric cup-product polarization $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_M \colon M \times M \to \mathbb{Q}(-1)$ satisfying $\langle fx, y \rangle_M = \langle x, \bar{f}y \rangle_M$ for all $f \in F$. Its inverse tensor $\xi_M \in M \otimes M \subset H^2(C \times C, \mathbb{Q})(1)$ is an algebraic divisor class (by Lefschetz $(1,1)$) satisfying
   $$(f \otimes 1)\xi_M = (1 \otimes \bar{f})\xi_M \qquad \forall f \in F.$$
2. The $F$-balanced subspace
   $$B_j = \{x \in M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j} : (f \otimes 1)x = (1 \otimes \bar{f})x \ \forall f \in F\}$$
   is cut out inside $M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j} \subset H^2(C_{t_j} \times C'_{u_j}, \mathbb{Q})$ by the algebraic separability idempotent $e_{B_j} = \sum_{a=1}^{2d} (f_a \otimes 1)(1 \otimes \overline{f_a^\vee})$, where $(f_a)$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $F$ and $(f_a^\vee)$ is its $\mathrm{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}$-dual basis.
3. Because $\dim_E T(S'_j) = 4$ and $E(S'_j) = E$, the algebraic map
   $$j_j \colon T(S'_j) \oplus T(S'_j) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} B_j, \qquad j_j(x, y) = i_j(x) + (\theta \otimes 1)i_j(y),$$
   is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures with algebraic inverse $j_j^{-1}$ (by Hodge–Riemann nondegeneracy on $T(S'_j)^{\oplus 2}$ and $B_j$ together with Cayley–Hamilton on $j_j^\dagger j_j$, [10, §5 (R5) and §6.2 Step 0]). We set
   $$P_{0,j} := \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ j_j^{-1} \circ e_{B_j} \colon M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j} \longrightarrow T(S'_j),$$
   which is a morphism of $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures induced by an algebraic correspondence.
4. Since the outgoing half $M_{u_j}$ of $S'_j$ is glued to the incoming half $M_{t_{j+1}}$ of $S'_{j+1}$, [7, Lem. 4.2(2)] furnishes an **algebraic $F$-linear Hodge isomorphism** $X_j \colon M_{u_j} \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{t_{j+1}}$ ($1 \le j \le k$, $t_{k+1} := t_1$).

**Definition 3.1 (Cyclic polarization family $\Upsilon(\lambda)$).** For each $\lambda \in F$, define the edge tensors
$$\eta_1^{(\lambda)} := (\lambda \otimes X_1)(\xi_{u_1}) \in M_{u_1} \otimes M_{t_2}, \qquad \eta_j := (1 \otimes X_j)(\xi_{u_j}) \in M_{u_j} \otimes M_{t_{j+1}} \quad (2 \le j \le k).$$
Let $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}} \colon \bigotimes_{j=1}^k (M_{u_j} \otimes M_{t_{j+1}}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \bigotimes_{j=1}^k (M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j})$ be the cyclic slot-reordering isomorphism mapping $(u_1, t_2) \otimes (u_2, t_3) \otimes \cdots \otimes (u_k, t_1) \mapsto (t_1, u_1) \otimes (t_2, u_2) \otimes \cdots \otimes (t_k, u_k)$ (with the standard Koszul sign $(-1)^k = (-1)^{2k-1+1}$ on odd-degree factors $H^1$, which only changes the correspondence by $\pm 1$; in multilinear algebra we fix the plain slot permutation $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, induced up to sign by permuting the curve factors of $\prod_{j=1}^k (C_{t_j} \times C'_{u_j})$). Define
$$\Upsilon(\lambda) := \bigl(P_{0,1} \otimes P_{0,2} \otimes \cdots \otimes P_{0,k}\bigr) \circ \sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl(\eta_1^{(\lambda)} \otimes \eta_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes \eta_k\bigr) \in \bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j).$$

### 3.2 Proof of Theorem 2

*Proof of Theorem 2.* **Step 1 (Algebraicity and Hodge bidegree).** Each $\xi_{u_j} \in M_{u_j} \otimes M_{u_j} \subset H^2(C'_{u_j} \times C'_{u_j}, \mathbb{Q})$ is a Hodge class of type $(1,1)$, hence algebraic by Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Since $F$ acts on $M_{u_1}$ by algebraic correspondences and each $X_j \colon M_{u_j} \to M_{t_{j+1}}$ is induced by a divisor class on $C'_{u_j} \times C_{t_{j+1}}$ [7, Lem. 4.2(2)], $\eta_1^{(\lambda)}$ and $\eta_2, \dots, \eta_k$ are algebraic Hodge classes of type $(1,1)$ in $H^2(C'_{u_j} \times C_{t_{j+1}}, \mathbb{Q})$. Their exterior product lies in $H^{2k}\bigl(\prod_{j=1}^k (C'_{u_j} \times C_{t_{j+1}}), \mathbb{Q}\bigr)$ of Hodge type $(k, k)$ and is algebraic. Applying the factor permutation inducing $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ (multiplying by the fixed Koszul sign $(-1)^{2k-1} = -1$, which preserves algebraicity) produces an algebraic class of type $(k, k)$ in $\bigotimes_{j=1}^k (M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j}) \subset H^{2k}\bigl(\prod_{j=1}^k (C_{t_j} \times C'_{u_j}), \mathbb{Q}\bigr)$. Finally, each $P_{0,j} \colon M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j} \to T(S'_j) \subset H^2(S'_j, \mathbb{Q})$ is a degree-$0$ algebraic correspondence of Hodge type $(0,0)$. Composing with $\bigotimes_{j=1}^k P_{0,j}$ (and Künneth embedding into $H^{2k}(S'_1 \times \cdots \times S'_k, \mathbb{Q})$) therefore realizes $\Upsilon(\lambda)$ as the class of an algebraic cycle of codimension $k$ on $S'_1 \times \cdots \times S'_k$ for every $\lambda \in F$. In particular, $\Upsilon(\lambda) \in \mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(\bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)\bigr)$ (viewing Hodge classes of type $(k,k)$ as invariants of $\mathrm{Hg}$).

**Step 2 (Exact dimension of cycle Hodge classes).** Suppose first that $\gamma = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ has no chords among $S'_1, \dots, S'_k$, so $\mathrm{Hg}\bigl(\bigoplus_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)\bigr)$ is the image of $\prod_{j=1}^k \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SL}_1(Q_r)$ acting at vertex $v_j$ diagonally on the outgoing half of $S'_j$ and incoming half of $S'_{j+1}$. Over $\mathbb{C}$, decompose along the $d$ real embeddings $\tau \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$:
$$\Bigl(\bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)\Bigr) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} = \bigoplus_{(\tau_1, \dots, \tau_k)} T(S'_1)_{\tau_1} \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} \cdots \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} T(S'_k)_{\tau_k}.$$
At each $\tau$, let $W_{v_j, \tau} \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ denote the standard two-dimensional module of the vertex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{v_j, \tau} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ with invariant symplectic form $\omega_{v_j, \tau}$; then $T(S'_j)_\tau \cong W_{v_{j-1}, \tau} \otimes W_{v_j, \tau}$. Because the Lie algebras $\bigoplus_{j=1}^k \mathfrak{g}_{v_j, \tau}$ act independently for distinct $\tau$, any $\mathrm{Hg}$-invariant summand $T(S'_1)_{\tau_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes T(S'_k)_{\tau_k}$ must pair every $W_{v_j, \tau}$ with a second copy of $W_{v_j, \tau}$ at the *same* place $\tau$: if $\tau_j \ne \tau_{j+1}$ for some $j$, then $W_{v_j, \tau_j}$ occurs with multiplicity one across the entire $k$-fold tensor product and has zero $\mathfrak{g}_{v_j, \tau_j}$-invariants ($\mathbf{2}^{\mathfrak{sl}_2} = 0$). Hence invariants exist only on the $d$ pure-place diagonals $\tau_1 = \cdots = \tau_k = \tau$, where
$$\bigl(T(S'_1)_\tau \otimes \cdots \otimes T(S'_k)_\tau\bigr)^{\bigoplus_{j=1}^k \mathfrak{g}_{v_j, \tau}} \cong \bigotimes_{j=1}^k \bigl(W_{v_j, \tau} \otimes W_{v_j, \tau}\bigr)^{\mathfrak{g}_{v_j, \tau}} = \mathbb{C}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}$$
is one-dimensional, spanned by the cyclic contraction $t_{\gamma, \tau} = \sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\omega_{v_1, \tau}^{-1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \omega_{v_k, \tau}^{-1})$ of [3, §6.9]. Thus $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(\bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)\bigr) = d = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$ for every chordless simple cycle, proving Theorem 2(1). (As noted in §3.3 below, the chordless simple cycles already generate the entire cycle space $Z_1(\Gamma_r; \mathbb{F}_2)$, so Theorem 2 need only be applied to chordless cycles; moreover, if $\gamma$ has chords, $w_{\pm, \tau}$ in Steps 3–4 below still coincide with nonzero scalar multiples of $t_{\gamma, \tau}$ by direct evaluation of the cyclic symplectic contraction.)

**Step 3 (Complex eigenspace decomposition and orientation-preserving loop collapse).** Assume $\gamma$ is a chordless simple cycle (so $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathrm{Hdg}_\tau = 1$, spanned by $t_{\gamma, \tau}$). For each real place $\tau \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$, fix one complex embedding $\sigma_\tau \colon F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ extending $\tau$; its complex conjugate $\bar{\sigma}_\tau$ is the other embedding of $F$ extending $\tau$, with $\bar{\sigma}_\tau(\theta) = -\sigma_\tau(\theta) \ne 0$. Over $\mathbb{C}$:

- Each piece decomposes as $M \otimes_{E, \tau} \mathbb{C} = M_{\sigma_\tau} \oplus M_{\bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_{\sigma_\tau} = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_{\bar{\sigma}_\tau} = 2$.
- Because $\langle fx, y \rangle_M = \langle x, \bar{f}y \rangle_M$, the cup-product pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{u_j}$ vanishes on $M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau} \times M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau}$ and $M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau} \times M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$, and pairs $M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau}$ nondegenerately with $M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$. Thus the $\tau$-component of $\xi_{u_j}$ decomposes as
  $$\xi_{u_j, \tau} = \xi_{u_j, (\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)} + \xi_{u_j, (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)} \in \bigl(M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau} \otimes M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}\bigr) \oplus \bigl(M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau} \otimes M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau}\bigr),$$
  where both tensor summands correspond to **invertible** linear maps $M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau}^\vee \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ and $M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}^\vee \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau}$.
- Since $X_j \colon M_{u_j} \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{t_{j+1}}$ is $F$-linear and invertible, $X_{j, \rho} \colon M_{u_j, \rho} \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{t_{j+1}, \rho}$ is invertible for every $\rho \in \{\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau\}$. Hence
  $$\eta_{j, \tau} = \eta_{j, (\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)} + \eta_{j, (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)} \in \bigl(M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau} \otimes M_{t_{j+1}, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}\bigr) \oplus \bigl(M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau} \otimes M_{t_{j+1}, \sigma_\tau}\bigr)$$
  has **rank-$2$ invertible** components in both summands, while on edge $1$, multiplication by $\lambda \in F$ on the first slot scales the two components by $\sigma_\tau(\lambda)$ and $\bar{\sigma}_\tau(\lambda)$:
  $$\eta_{1, \tau}^{(\lambda)} = \sigma_\tau(\lambda)\,\eta_{1, (\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)} + \bar{\sigma}_\tau(\lambda)\,\eta_{1, (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)}.$$
- Now apply $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ and project each vertex factor $M_{t_j} \otimes M_{u_j}$ via $e_{B_j}$ onto $B_{j, \tau} = \bigl(M_{t_j, \sigma_\tau} \otimes M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}\bigr) \oplus \bigl(M_{t_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau} \otimes M_{u_j, \sigma_\tau}\bigr)$. Expanding the product of the $k$ binary sums $\eta_1^{(\lambda)} \otimes \eta_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes \eta_k$ over all $2^k$ choices of $(\rho_j, \bar{\rho}_j) \in \{(\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau), (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)\}$ on edge $j$ ($1 \le j \le k$), slot $(t_j, u_j)$ after $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ receives the pair of $F$-characters $(\bar{\rho}_{j-1}, \rho_j)$. The balanced projector $e_{B_j}$ retains $(t_j, u_j)$ if and only if $\rho_j$ is the conjugate of $\bar{\rho}_{j-1}$, i.e., if and only if
  $$\rho_j = \overline{\bar{\rho}_{j-1}} = \rho_{j-1} \qquad \text{for every } j \in \mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}.$$
  Furthermore, if factors at distinct places $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_k$ were mixed, $e_{B_j}$ would kill any slot where $\tau(t_j) \ne \tau(u_j)$, forcing $\tau_1 = \cdots = \tau_k = \tau$. Thus **all $2^k - 2$ mixed terms vanish identically**, leaving only the two constant loops $\rho_1 = \cdots = \rho_k = \sigma_\tau$ and $\rho_1 = \cdots = \rho_k = \bar{\sigma}_\tau$.

Next, examine $P_{0, j} = \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ j_j^{-1}$ on $B_{j, \tau} = B_{j, \sigma_\tau} \oplus B_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$. For $x, y \in T(S'_j)_\tau$, $j_j(x, y)_\tau = \bigl(i_{j, \sigma_\tau}(x + \sigma_\tau(\theta)y),\; i_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}(x - \sigma_\tau(\theta)y)\bigr)$, where $i_{j, \sigma_\tau} \colon T(S'_j)_\tau \xrightarrow{\sim} B_{j, \sigma_\tau}$ and $i_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau} \colon T(S'_j)_\tau \xrightarrow{\sim} B_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ are linear **isomorphisms** (since $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} T(S'_j)_\tau = 4 = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} B_{j, \sigma_\tau}$ and $j_j$ is an isomorphism). Inverting the $2 \times 2$ scalar system $u_+ = x + \sigma_\tau(\theta)y$, $u_- = x - \sigma_\tau(\theta)y$ yields $x = \tfrac{1}{2}(u_+ + u_-)$, so for $(z_+, z_-) \in B_{j, \sigma_\tau} \oplus B_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$:
$$(\mathrm{pr}_1 \circ j_j^{-1})(z_+, z_-) = \frac{1}{2}\,i_{j, \sigma_\tau}^{-1}(z_+) + \frac{1}{2}\,i_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}^{-1}(z_-).$$
Applying $P_{0,1} \otimes \cdots \otimes P_{0,k}$ to the two surviving pure loops therefore gives
$$\Upsilon(\lambda)_\tau = \bar{\sigma}_\tau(\lambda)\,w_{+, \tau} + \sigma_\tau(\lambda)\,w_{-, \tau} \in \bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)_\tau,$$
where
$$\begin{aligned}
w_{+, \tau} &:= 2^{-k}\,\bigl(i_{1, \sigma_\tau}^{-1} \otimes \cdots \otimes i_{k, \sigma_\tau}^{-1}\bigr) \circ \sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl(\eta_{1, (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)} \otimes \cdots \otimes \eta_{k, (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)}\bigr), \\
w_{-, \tau} &:= 2^{-k}\,\bigl(i_{1, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}^{-1} \otimes \cdots \otimes i_{k, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}^{-1}\bigr) \circ \sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl(\eta_{1, (\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)} \otimes \cdots \otimes \eta_{k, (\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}$$
(Note the indices: $\rho_1 = \cdots = \rho_k = \bar{\sigma}_\tau$ places $t_j$ in $\sigma_\tau$ and $u_j$ in $\bar{\sigma}_\tau$, landing in $B_{j, \sigma_\tau}$ with scalar $\bar{\sigma}_\tau(\lambda)$, whereas $\rho_1 = \cdots = \rho_k = \sigma_\tau$ lands in $B_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ with scalar $\sigma_\tau(\lambda)$.)

**Step 4 (Nonvanishing of $w_{\pm, \tau}$ and the $(1, \theta)$ Vandermonde determinant).** Each tensor $\eta_{j, (\bar{\sigma}_\tau, \sigma_\tau)} \in M_{u_j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau} \otimes M_{t_{j+1}, \sigma_\tau}$ has rank $2$ (hence is nonzero), so their $k$-fold tensor product is nonzero, the bijection $\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ preserves nonvanishing, and the $k$-fold tensor product of the isomorphisms $i_{j, \sigma_\tau}^{-1}$ is an isomorphism. Therefore **$w_{+, \tau} \ne 0$ and $w_{-, \tau} \ne 0$ for every $\tau$**. Moreover, because $\xi_{u_j}$, $X_j$, $e_{B_j}$, and $j_j^{-1}$ are all $\mathrm{Hg}^{\mathrm{der}}$-equivariant and $B_{j, \sigma_\tau}, B_{j, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ are $\mathrm{Hg}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{C})$-submodules, both $w_{+, \tau}$ and $w_{-, \tau}$ lie in the one-dimensional $\mathrm{Hg}^{\mathrm{der}}$-invariant line $\mathbb{C}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}$ of Step 2:
$$w_{+, \tau} = \alpha_{+, \tau}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}, \qquad w_{-, \tau} = \alpha_{-, \tau}\,t_{\gamma, \tau} \qquad \text{with } \alpha_{+, \tau} \ne 0,\ \alpha_{-, \tau} \ne 0.$$
Evaluating at $\lambda = 1$ ($\sigma_\tau(1) = \bar{\sigma}_\tau(1) = 1$) and $\lambda = \theta$ ($\bar{\sigma}_\tau(\theta) = -\sigma_\tau(\theta)$) yields
$$\begin{pmatrix} \Upsilon(1)_\tau \\ \Upsilon(\theta)_\tau \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -\sigma_\tau(\theta) & \sigma_\tau(\theta) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha_{+, \tau} \\ \alpha_{-, \tau} \end{pmatrix} t_{\gamma, \tau}.$$
Since $\theta^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_{<0}$, the determinant of this $2 \times 2$ matrix is $2\sigma_\tau(\theta) \ne 0$ at every real place $\tau$. Because $(\alpha_{+, \tau}, \alpha_{-, \tau}) \ne (0, 0)$, **at least one of $\Upsilon(1)_\tau$ and $\Upsilon(\theta)_\tau$ is a nonzero multiple of $t_{\gamma, \tau}$ at every real place $\tau$**.

Now let $E$ act algebraically on the first tensor factor $T(S'_1)$ (via $(\mathrm{F2})$ / Step 0 of [10, §6.2]). Extending scalars to $\mathbb{C}$, $E \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} \xrightarrow{\sim} \prod_{\tau \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}_\tau$ contains the Lagrange idempotents projecting onto each $\tau$-summand independently. Since $(E \cdot \Upsilon(1) + E \cdot \Upsilon(\theta)) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C}$ is an $(E \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C})$-submodule of $\bigoplus_\tau \mathbb{C}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}$ with nonzero projection in every $\tau$-coordinate, it equals $\bigoplus_\tau \mathbb{C}\,t_{\gamma, \tau}$. Taking rational subspaces gives $E \cdot \Upsilon(1) + E \cdot \Upsilon(\theta) = \mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(\bigotimes_{j=1}^k T(S'_j)\bigr)$, completing the proof of Theorem 2(2)–(3). $\square$

*Remark 3.2 (Parity switching at $k = 3$ vs. $k = 4$).* In our symmetric Row-1 computation (`verify.py`, `[A2]` and `[A3]`), complex conjugation exchanges $w_{+, \tau}$ and $w_{-, \tau}$ with sign $(-1)^k$, giving $\alpha_{-, \tau} = (-1)^k \alpha_{+, \tau}$. Thus **$\Upsilon(1) = 0$ and $\Upsilon(\theta) \ne 0$ for $k = 3$**, whereas **$\Upsilon(1) \ne 0$ and $\Upsilon(\theta) = 0$ for $k = 4$**. Both generators $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = \theta$ of $F/E$ are genuinely needed across cycle lengths of opposite parity.

### 3.3 Proof of Theorem 1

*Proof of Theorem 1.* By [7, Lem. 3.1(3)] and [7, Cor. 4.3], the auxiliary factors $Y_1, \dots, Y_l$ split off as an independent block, and factors belonging to distinct rows $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ split off from one another into independent row blocks. Fix one row $r$. As in [7, §6 Step 1 and Thm. B], replacing algebraically Hodge-isogenous $m = 3$ factors by a single representative and algebraically Hodge-isogenous $m = 4$ factors by a single representative leaves all mixed-product Hodge conjectures unchanged; after this reduction, the $m = 3$ factors are pairwise non-isogenous, the $m = 4$ factors are pairwise non-isogenous, and the row graph $\Gamma_r$ is a **simple graph** (no self-loops by [7, Lem. 3.1(1)] and no parallel $2$-cycles by [7, Thm. B]).

As verified in [7, §8, Proof of Thm. C], the row factors satisfy $(\mathrm{H1})$ ([6, Thm. A], [4, Thm. 3], [5, Thm. 3]), $(\mathrm{H2})$ ([6, Thm. B(1)], [7, Thm. B]), $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$ ([7, Thm. A, Prop. 8.1]), and vacuously $(\mathrm{H3})$ (no unitary factors). By [3, Thm. C(b)], the rational Hodge conjecture holds for all finite products of the row factors if and only if for every real place $\tau$ of $E$ and a basis of the cycle space $Z_1(\Gamma_r; \mathbb{F}_2)$, the cycle Hodge classes $t_{\gamma, \tau}$ are $\mathbb{C}$-linear combinations of algebraic classes.

Every finite simple graph $\Gamma_r$ has its $\mathbb{F}_2$-cycle space $Z_1(\Gamma_r; \mathbb{F}_2)$ generated by **induced (chordless) simple cycles** $\gamma$ of length $k \ge 3$: indeed, if a simple cycle $\gamma$ of length $k \ge 4$ admits a chord $e^*$ joining two non-adjacent vertices of $\gamma$, then $\gamma$ is the mod-$2$ sum $\gamma_1 \oplus \gamma_2$ in $Z_1(\Gamma_r; \mathbb{F}_2)$ of two simple cycles of strictly smaller lengths $k_1, k_2 < k$ sharing $e^*$. For every chordless simple cycle $\gamma = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ ($k \ge 3$) in $\Gamma_r$, the $k$ edges correspond to $k$ pairwise non-isogenous $m = 4$ surfaces $S'_{j_1}, \dots, S'_{j_k}$ with no extra gluings among their halves, so Theorem 2(3) proves that $t_{\gamma, \tau}$ lies in the $\mathbb{C}$-span of algebraic cycle classes on $S'_{j_1} \times \cdots \times S'_{j_k}$ (pulled back to the full product via projections). By [3, Thm. C(b)], the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every finite product of the row-$r$ factors, and [7, Lem. 3.1(2)–(3)] recombines all rows with $Y_1, \dots, Y_l$. $\square$

## 4. CM Surfaces of Type $\mathrm{U}_2$: Curve-Piece Dichotomy, Morphisms, and Crossings

### 4.1 Transfer and unconditional balanced decomposition for CM $m = 4$ surfaces

Let $S''$ be a projective K3 surface of row $r$ ($1 \le r \le 10$) with CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$: $L := E(S'')$ is a CM quadratic extension of $E$ ($[L : E] = 2$, $\dim_L T(S'') = 2$, $\dim_E T(S'') = 4$), $E$ acts on $H^{2,0}(S'')$ via $\tau_0$, and $(T(S''), \varphi_E)$ has $\det \varphi_E \in E^{\times 2}$ and $C_0(T(S''), \varphi_E) \cong Q_r \times Q_r$. Since $L$ is a CM field of degree $2d$ containing the totally real field $E$ of degree $d$, $E$ coincides with the maximal totally real subfield $L^+$ of $L$, and complex conjugation on $L$ is the nontrivial automorphism $x \mapsto \bar{x}$ of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/E)$. By Zarhin [1, Thm. 2.2.1], letting $\psi_L \colon T(S'') \times T(S'') \to L$ be the unique $L/E$-Hermitian form with $\mathrm{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}} \circ \psi_L = (\cdot, \cdot)_{\mathrm{cup}}|_{T(S'')}$, the Hodge group of $T(S'')$ is
$$\mathrm{Hg}(T(S'')) = \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{U}(T(S''), \psi_L),$$
with connected center $T_{S''} \cong L^1 = \ker(N_{L/E} \colon \mathrm{Res}_{L/\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{G}_m)$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $d$ and derived group $\mathrm{Hg}(T(S''))^{\mathrm{der}} = \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SU}(T(S''), \psi_L)$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $3d$.

**Lemma 4.1 (Transfer and balanced decomposition $B_s = i_s(T) \oplus \theta i_s(T)$ for CM $m = 4$).** *Let $S''$ be any projective K3 surface of row $r$ with CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ (or pure CM $m = 4$ with $[E(S''):E] = 4$). Then:*

1. *There exists a smooth member $S_s$ ($b \ne 0$) of the reference family of row $r$ and an algebraic rational Hodge isometry $g_{S''} \colon T(S'') \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S_s)$ with algebraic inverse $g_{S''}^{-1}$.*
2. *At every member $S_s$ ($b \ne 0$) of the reference family of row $r$ with $\dim_E T(S_s) = 4$—regardless of whether $E(S_s) = E$ or $E(S_s)$ is CM (even if $E(S_s) \cong_E F$)—the injective algebraic Hodge morphism $i_s \colon T(S_s) \to B_s \subset M_{t} \otimes M_{u}$ satisfies $i_s(T(S_s)) \cap (\theta \otimes 1)i_s(T(S_s)) = 0$ and*
   $$j_s \colon T(S_s) \oplus T(S_s) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} B_s, \qquad j_s(x, y) = i_s(x) + (\theta \otimes 1)i_s(y),$$
   *is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures with algebraic inverse $j_s^{-1}$.*

*Proof.* (1) As remarked explicitly in [4, §8, Proof of Thm. 3 and line 253] and [7, §9, Proof of Thm. D], the density transfer of [4, Thm. 3] and [5, Thm. 3] uses only that $(T(S''), \varphi_E)$ is isometric to the row's $E$-quadratic space $V_{Q_r}$ and has no rational $(1,1)$-classes; it does **not** assume $E(S'') = E$. Extending the $E$-isometry $T(S'') \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S_s)$ by Witt cancellation on $\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbb{Q}}$ to $H^2(S'', \mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^2(S_s, \mathbb{Q})$ [19, Ch. 14] and applying Buskin's theorem [12] makes $g_{S''}$ and its transpose $g_{S''}^{-1}$ algebraic.

(2) In every reference family $(k_{\mathrm{fam}}, n_{\mathrm{fam}})$ of Table 1 ($3 \le k_{\mathrm{fam}} < n_{\mathrm{fam}}$), the pullback of the holomorphic $(2,0)$-form $\omega_s \in H^{2,0}(S_s)$ under $i_s$ is given at every $b \ne 0$ ($t, u \notin \{0, 1\}$, $d_0 \ne 0$) by
$$i_s(\omega_s) = \kappa\,\bigl(d_0^{2 - k_{\mathrm{fam}}} A_1 \otimes A_{k_{\mathrm{fam}} - 1} - A_{k_{\mathrm{fam}} - 1} \otimes A_1\bigr), \qquad \kappa \ne 0,$$
where $A_1, A_{k_{\mathrm{fam}}-1} \in H^{1,0}$ are nonzero eigenforms on which $\theta \otimes 1$ acts with **distinct** eigenvalues ($\mp\sqrt{-3}$ for $k_{\mathrm{fam}} \in \{3, 6\}$ and $\mp i$ for $k_{\mathrm{fam}} = 4$): see [10, §6.4] for $(6, 8)$, [11, §6.4, eq. (6.1) and Lem. 6.2] for $(6, n_{\mathrm{fam}})$ with $n_{\mathrm{fam}} \in \{7, 9, 10, 11, 12\}$, [4, §6.3, eq. (6.1) and Lem. 6.2] for $(4, 12)\mathrm{tw}$, and [5, §5.2, Lem. 5.1 and Rem. 5.4] for $(3, 5)$ and $(6, 16)\mathrm{tw}$ (and identical $(4, 5)$ formula with $k_{\mathrm{fam}} = 4 < 5 = n_{\mathrm{fam}}$). Consequently, $i_s(\omega_s)$ has nonzero projections in two distinct eigenspaces of $\theta \otimes 1$ and is **not** an eigenvector of $\theta \otimes 1$. Since $T(S_s)$ is an irreducible $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure [1] and $\theta \otimes 1 \in \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(B_s)$, the intersection $i_s(T(S_s)) \cap (\theta \otimes 1)i_s(T(S_s))$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-sub-Hodge structure of $i_s(T(S_s))$. If it were nonzero, irreducibility would force $(\theta \otimes 1)i_s(T(S_s)) = i_s(T(S_s))$, so $\theta \otimes 1$ would preserve the one-dimensional $(2,0)$-line $i_s(T(S_s))^{2,0} = \mathbb{C}\,i_s(\omega_s)$, a contradiction. Thus $i_s(T(S_s)) \cap (\theta \otimes 1)i_s(T(S_s)) = 0$. When $\dim_E T(S_s) = 4$, $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(i_s(T(S_s)) \oplus (\theta \otimes 1)i_s(T(S_s))\bigr) = 8d = 4[F : \mathbb{Q}] = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} B_s$, so $j_s$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge isomorphism, and its inverse $j_s^{-1}$ is algebraic by Hodge–Riemann nondegeneracy and Cayley–Hamilton [10, §5 (R5)]. $\square$

### 4.2 The curve-piece dichotomy for CM type $\mathrm{U}_2$

**Lemma 4.2 (Curve-piece dichotomy).** *Let $M = M_t \subset H^1(C_t, \mathbb{Q})$ be any curve piece from the reference family of row $r$ ($1 \le r \le 10$), with $\mathfrak{su}_F(M) = \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{SU}_F(M)$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $3d$, and put $\mathfrak{d}_M := \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(M)^{\mathrm{der}} \subseteq \mathfrak{su}_F(M)$. Then:*

1. *(Curve dichotomy.) Either $\mathfrak{d}_M = 0$ (so $M$ has abelian CM Hodge group) or $\mathfrak{d}_M = \mathfrak{su}_F(M) \cong \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$ (of full $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $3d$).*
2. *(Decomposition of $\mathrm{U}_2$ members.) Let $S_s$ be a CM $m = 4$ member of row $r$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ with curve pieces $(M_t, M_u)$. Then **exactly one** of $\mathfrak{d}_{M_t}, \mathfrak{d}_{M_u}$ equals $\mathfrak{su}_F$ and the other is **$0$**. Reordering $(C_t, C'_u)$ if necessary so that $\mathfrak{d}_{M_t} = \mathfrak{su}_F(M_t)$ and $\mathfrak{d}_{M_u} = 0$, restriction to $B_s \cong T(S_s)^{\oplus 2}$ identifies $\mathfrak{su}_F(M_t)$ isomorphically with $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_s))^{\mathrm{der}} \cong \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$. In particular, $Q_r \otimes_E L \cong M_2(L)$ splits over $L = E(S_s)$.*

*Proof.* (1) Suppose $\mathfrak{d}_M \ne 0$. Since $\mathrm{Hg}(M)$ is reductive [18, Ch. I], its derived Lie algebra $\mathfrak{d}_M$ is a nonzero semisimple $\mathbb{Q}$-Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}_F(M)$. Recall from [6, §5.1] that $M$ has Hodge types $(1,0) + (0,1)$ at the two embeddings of $F$ over $\tau_0$, and pure type $(1,0)^2$ or $(0,1)^2$ in weight $1$ at all embeddings over $\tau \ne \tau_0$. Hence over $\mathbb{R}$:
$$\mathfrak{su}_F(M)_{\mathbb{R}} \cong \mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\tau_0} \oplus \bigoplus_{\tau \ne \tau_0} \mathfrak{su}_2(\mathbb{R})_\tau,$$
with $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\tau_0} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ non-compact and every $\mathfrak{su}_2(\mathbb{R})_\tau$ ($\tau \ne \tau_0$) compact. Moreover, the Hodge circle $h(\mathbb{S}^1) \subset \mathrm{Hg}(M)(\mathbb{R})$ acts by scalars on each pure eigenspace over $\tau \ne \tau_0$, so $\mathrm{Ad}\,h(\mathbb{S}^1)$ is **trivial** at all $\tau \ne \tau_0$ and nontrivial at $\tau_0$. Consequently, the projection of $\mathfrak{d}_{M, \mathbb{R}}$ to $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\tau_0}$ must be nonzero (if it were zero, $\mathrm{Hg}(M)^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{R})$ would project trivially to $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)_{\tau_0}$, forcing $\mathrm{Hg}(M)(\mathbb{R})$ to have abelian projection at $\tau_0$ and trivial $\mathrm{Ad}\,h(\mathbb{S}^1)$ everywhere, so $\mathrm{Hg}(M)$ would be abelian by minimality of $\mathrm{Hg}(M)$, contradicting $\mathfrak{d}_M \ne 0$). Let $\mathfrak{s} \subseteq \mathfrak{d}_M$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-simple summand with nonzero projection at $\tau_0$. Since every nonzero semisimple Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ equals $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the projection $\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\tau_0} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ is **surjective**, and $\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus d_0}$ has the form $\mathfrak{s} \cong \mathrm{Res}_{E_0/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_0)$ for a totally real field $E_0$ of degree $d_0 \le d$. Over $\mathbb{C}$, each simple $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$-factor of $\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{C}}$ maps into $\bigoplus_\tau \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})_\tau$, associating to each real place $\tau$ where $\mathfrak{s}_\tau \ne 0$ a unique real place $v(\tau)$ of $E_0$. Crucially, for $\tau_1 \ne \tau_0$, we **cannot** have $v(\tau_1) = v(\tau_0)$: if $v(\tau_1) = v(\tau_0)$, the single real simple factor $\mathfrak{s}_{v(\tau_0)} \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} 1$ would project isomorphically onto **both** $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\tau_0} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathfrak{su}_2(\mathbb{R})_{\tau_1}$, which is **impossible because $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R}) \not\cong_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{su}_2(\mathbb{R})$**. On the other hand, $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ permutes the $d$ places $\tau$ of $E$ transitively while preserving the $\mathbb{Q}$-subalgebra $\mathfrak{s} \subseteq \mathfrak{su}_F(M)$; hence $\mathfrak{s}_\tau \ne 0$ for **every** $\tau$, and the Galois-equivariant map $\tau \mapsto v(\tau)$ from the $d$ places of $E$ onto the $d_0$ places of $E_0$ has fibers of uniform size $d / d_0$. Since the fiber containing $\tau_0$ is the singleton $\{\tau_0\}$, we obtain $d / d_0 = 1$, hence $d_0 = d$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{s} = 3d = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{su}_F(M)$, forcing $\mathfrak{d}_M = \mathfrak{su}_F(M)$. By [4, Lem. 7.3] and [5, §6.2], $\mathfrak{su}_F(M) \cong \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$.

(2) Put $\mathfrak{L} := \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(M_t \oplus M_u)^{\mathrm{der}} \subseteq \mathfrak{d}_{M_t} \oplus \mathfrak{d}_{M_u} \subseteq \mathfrak{su}_F(M_t) \oplus \mathfrak{su}_F(M_u)$. By Lemma 4.1(2), $B_s \cong T(S_s)^{\oplus 2}$ lies in the tensor category of $M_t \oplus M_u$, so restriction gives a surjection $\mathfrak{L} \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_s))^{\mathrm{der}}$ onto a $\mathbb{Q}$-Lie algebra of dimension $3d$. Fix a real place $\tau$ of $E$ and $\sigma \mid \tau$. On $B_{s, \sigma} = M_{t, \sigma} \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} M_{u, \bar{\sigma}} \cong \mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$, the $\tau$-factor $\mathfrak{su}_F(M_t)_\tau \oplus \mathfrak{su}_F(M_u)_\tau \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts via the outer tensor product of standard $2$-dimensional modules. Meanwhile, under $B_{s, \sigma} \cong T(S_s) \otimes_{E, \tau} \mathbb{C} = (T(S_s) \otimes_{L, \nu} \mathbb{C}) \oplus (T(S_s) \otimes_{L, \bar{\nu}} \mathbb{C})$ ($\nu, \bar{\nu} \mid \tau$), $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_s))^{\mathrm{der}}_\tau = \mathfrak{su}(T(S_s), \psi_L)_\tau \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts as **$\mathbf{2} \oplus \mathbf{2}$**. As listed in [6, Proof of Lem. 5.1], the semisimple subalgebras of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ decompose $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$ respectively as $\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 4}$ (for $0$), $\mathbf{2} \oplus \mathbf{2}$ (for $\mathfrak{sl}_2 \oplus 0$ or $0 \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_2$), $\mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{1}$ (for the graph of an automorphism), and irreducible $\mathbf{4}$ (for $\mathfrak{sl}_2 \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_2$). By part (1), each of $\mathfrak{d}_{M_t}, \mathfrak{d}_{M_u}$ has dimension $0$ or $3d$:
- If $(\dim \mathfrak{d}_{M_t}, \dim \mathfrak{d}_{M_u}) = (0, 0)$, then $\dim \mathfrak{L} = 0 < 3d$, impossible.
- If $(\dim \mathfrak{d}_{M_t}, \dim \mathfrak{d}_{M_u}) = (3d, 3d)$, then since $\mathfrak{L} \subseteq \mathfrak{su}_F(M_t) \oplus \mathfrak{su}_F(M_u)$ projects onto both $\mathbb{Q}$-simple summands, Goursat's lemma forces $\mathfrak{L}$ to be either the full direct sum (which acts irreducibly as $\mathbf{4}$ on $B_{s, \sigma}$, contradicting the $\mathrm{Hg}$-submodule decomposition $\mathbf{2} \oplus \mathbf{2}$ of $T(S_s)_\tau$) or the graph of an isomorphism (which decomposes $B_{s, \sigma}$ as $\mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{1}$, again contradicting $\mathbf{2} \oplus \mathbf{2}$).
- Therefore **one of $\mathfrak{d}_{M_t}, \mathfrak{d}_{M_u}$ has dimension $3d$ and the other is $0$**.

Reordering so $\mathfrak{d}_{M_t} = \mathfrak{su}_F(M_t)$ and $\mathfrak{d}_{M_u} = 0$, we get $\mathfrak{L} = \mathfrak{su}_F(M_t) \oplus 0$, mapping isomorphically onto $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_s))^{\mathrm{der}}$. Applying [4, Lem. 7.3] to the binary $L/E$-Hermitian space $(T(S_s), \psi_L)$, the quaternion algebra $Q' = \{X \in \mathrm{End}_L T(S_s) : X^\dagger = \mathrm{adj}(X)\}$ over $E$ satisfies $Q' \otimes_E L = \mathrm{End}_L T(S_s) \cong M_2(L)$ and $\mathrm{Lie}\,Q'^1 = \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_s))^{\mathrm{der}} \cong \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$ ($E$-linearly, as $\tau_0$ is preserved by $\mathrm{Ad}\,h(\mathbb{S}^1)$), whence $Q' \cong_E Q_r$ [17, Ch. III] and $Q_r \otimes_E L \cong M_2(L)$. $\square$

### 4.3 Unconditional $(\mathrm{H2})$ and Wedderburn merger for type $\mathrm{U}_2$

**Proposition 4.3 (Algebraicity of all $\mathrm{U}_2$ Hodge morphisms, $(\mathrm{H2})$).** *Let $S''_1, S''_2$ be CM $m = 4$ projective K3 surfaces of row $r$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$. Then every rational Hodge morphism $\gamma \colon T(S''_1) \to T(S''_2)$ (and its inverse when $\gamma \ne 0$) is induced by an algebraic cycle on $S''_1 \times S''_2$.*

*Proof.* By Lemma 4.1(1) we may assume $S''_1 = S_{s_1}$ and $S''_2 = S_{s_2}$ are members of the row's reference family, ordered by Lemma 4.2(2) so that $\mathfrak{d}_{M_{t_k}} = \mathfrak{su}_F(M_{t_k})$ and $\mathfrak{d}_{M_{u_k}} = 0$ ($k = 1, 2$). Assume $\gamma \colon T(S_{s_1}) \to T(S_{s_2})$ is a nonzero Hodge morphism; by irreducibility [1], $\gamma$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge isomorphism, identifying $L_1 = E(S_{s_1})$ with $L_2 = E(S_{s_2})$ and gluing $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_{s_1}))^{\mathrm{der}}$ to $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_{s_2}))^{\mathrm{der}}$.

Consider the four-piece sum $Y = M_{t_1} \oplus M_{u_1} \oplus M_{t_2} \oplus M_{u_2}$. Because $\mathfrak{d}_{M_{u_1}} = \mathfrak{d}_{M_{u_2}} = 0$, $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(Y)^{\mathrm{der}} = \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(M_{t_1} \oplus M_{t_2})^{\mathrm{der}} \subseteq \mathfrak{su}_F(M_{t_1}) \oplus \mathfrak{su}_F(M_{t_2})$ maps isomorphically onto $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(T(S_{s_1}) \oplus T(S_{s_2}))^{\mathrm{der}}$, which is a graph. By the proof of [7, Lem. 4.2(2)], there exists an **algebraic $F$-linear Hodge isomorphism** $X_0 \colon M_{t_1} \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{t_2}$.

Now extend $\gamma \colon T(S_{s_1}) \xrightarrow{\sim} T(S_{s_2})$ to an $F$-linear Hodge isomorphism $\widetilde{\gamma} \colon B_{s_1} \xrightarrow{\sim} B_{s_2}$ via Lemma 4.1(2):
$$\widetilde{\gamma} := j_{s_2} \circ (\gamma \oplus \gamma) \circ j_{s_1}^{-1} \colon B_{s_1} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} B_{s_2}.$$
Compose with $(X_0^{-1} \otimes 1) \colon B_{s_2} \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{t_1} \otimes_F \overline{M_{u_2}}$ to obtain an $F$-linear Hodge isomorphism
$$\Psi := (X_0^{-1} \otimes 1) \circ \widetilde{\gamma} \colon M_{t_1} \otimes_F \overline{M_{u_1}} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} M_{t_1} \otimes_F \overline{M_{u_2}}.$$
Because $\Psi$ is a morphism of rational Hodge structures in the tensor category of $Y$, $\Psi$ is equivariant for $\mathrm{Hg}(Y)^{\mathrm{der}}$, which acts as $\mathfrak{su}_F(M_{t_1})$ on the first tensor slot $M_{t_1}$ and **trivially** on both CM curve pieces $M_{u_1}$ and $M_{u_2}$. Over $\mathbb{C}$ at every embedding $\sigma \colon F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$, $M_{t_1, \sigma} \cong \mathbf{2}$ is an irreducible module for $\mathfrak{su}_F(M_{t_1})_\tau \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ ($\tau = \sigma|_E$). By Schur's lemma, $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})}\bigl(M_{t_1, \sigma} \otimes M_{u_1, \bar{\sigma}},\; M_{t_1, \sigma} \otimes M_{u_2, \bar{\sigma}}\bigr) = \mathrm{id}_{M_{t_1, \sigma}} \otimes \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(M_{u_1, \bar{\sigma}}, M_{u_2, \bar{\sigma}}\bigr)$. Taking rational subspaces, $\Psi = (1 \otimes Y_0)|_{B_{s_1}}$ for a unique nonzero $\bar{F}$-linear—equivalently **$F$-linear**—map $Y_0 \colon M_{u_1} \to M_{u_2}$. Moreover, $Y_0$ preserves Hodge bidegrees at every $\sigma \colon F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$, because $\Psi_\sigma = \mathrm{id}_{M_{t_1, \sigma}} \otimes Y_{0, \bar{\sigma}}$ has Hodge type $(0, 0)$:
- At $\sigma \mid \tau_0$, $M_{t_1, \sigma} = M_{t_1, \sigma}^{1,0} \oplus M_{t_1, \sigma}^{0,1}$ has nonzero $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ lines; for $0 \ne v_{1,0} \in M_{t_1, \sigma}^{1,0}$ and $w \in M_{u_1, \bar{\sigma}}^{p,q}$ ($p + q = 1$), $\Psi_\sigma(v_{1,0} \otimes w) = v_{1,0} \otimes Y_{0, \bar{\sigma}}(w)$ has total bidegree $(1 + p, q)$, and because $v_{1,0}$ is purely of type $(1,0)$, $Y_{0, \bar{\sigma}}(w)$ must have pure bidegree $(p, q)$.
- At $\sigma \mid \tau$ ($\tau \ne \tau_0$), $M_{u_1, \bar{\sigma}}$ and $M_{u_2, \bar{\sigma}}$ come from the **same** curve family and therefore have the **same pure Hodge type** ($(1,0)^2$ or $(0,1)^2$, [6, §5.1]), so *every* linear map $M_{u_1, \bar{\sigma}} \to M_{u_2, \bar{\sigma}}$ has Hodge type $(0,0)$.

Hence $Y_0 \in \mathrm{Hom}_{F\text{-Hdg}}(M_{u_1}, M_{u_2})$ is a Hodge morphism. Composed with the algebraic curve-piece idempotents, $Y_0$ is a Hodge class in $H^1(C'_{u_1})^\vee \otimes H^1(C'_{u_2}) \subset H^2(C'_{u_1} \times C'_{u_2}, \mathbb{Q})(1)$, hence **algebraic** by Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Therefore $\widetilde{\gamma} = (X_0 \otimes Y_0)|_{B_{s_1}}$ is algebraic, and
$$\gamma = \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ j_{s_2}^{-1} \circ (X_0 \otimes Y_0) \circ i_{s_1}$$
is algebraic. Its inverse $\gamma^{-1}$ is algebraic by [10, §5 (R5)] ($\gamma^{-1} = (\gamma^\dagger \gamma)^{-1} \gamma^\dagger$, since $\gamma^\dagger \gamma \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}} T(S_{s_1}) = L_1$ is invertible and $L_1$ consists of algebraic classes on $S_{s_1}^2$ by $(\mathrm{H1})$ [2, Cor. 2.3]). $\square$

**Proposition 4.4 (Wedderburn merger for glued $\mathrm{U}_2$ pairs with $L_1 \cong_E L_2$).** *Let $S''_1, S''_2$ be CM $m = 4$ projective K3 surfaces of row $r$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ whose active $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-halves are glued (equivalently, $M_{t_1} \cong_{F\text{-Hdg}} M_{t_2}$ after ordering as in Lemma 4.2(2)). If $L_1 \cong_E L_2$ as $E$-algebras, then $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(S''_1), T(S''_2)) \ne 0$, and $S''_1, S''_2$ are algebraically Hodge-isogenous.*

*Proof.* Write $V_k = T(S''_k)$ ($k = 1, 2$). For each $k$, $H^{2,0}(V_k)$ is a one-dimensional complex line on which $L_k$ acts via one of the two complex embeddings $\sigma_{0, k}, \bar{\sigma}_{0, k} \colon L_k \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ extending $\tau_0 \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$; label it $\sigma_{0, k}$. Given an $E$-algebra isomorphism $\iota_0 \colon L_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} L_2$, $\sigma_{0, 2} \circ \iota_0$ extends $\tau_0$, so it equals either $\sigma_{0, 1}$ or $\bar{\sigma}_{0, 1} = \sigma_{0, 1} \circ \mathrm{conj}_{L_1/E}$. Replacing $\iota_0$ by $\iota_0 \circ \mathrm{conj}_{L_1/E}$ (still an $E$-isomorphism) in the second case, we identify both $L_1$ and $L_2$ with a single CM extension $L/E$ carrying the **same** active embedding $\sigma_0 \colon L \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ on $H^{2,0}(V_1)$ and $H^{2,0}(V_2)$.

Put $G_{12} := \mathrm{Hg}(V_1 \oplus V_2)$, a connected reductive $\mathbb{Q}$-group with Lie algebra $\mathrm{Lie}\,G_{12} = \mathfrak{z} \oplus \mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}}$ ($\mathfrak{z} = \mathrm{Lie}\,Z(G_{12})^\circ$). Because the active halves are glued, $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}}$ projects isomorphically onto $\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(V_k)^{\mathrm{der}}$ ($k = 1, 2$), which commutes with $L$ on both $V_1$ and $V_2$. By Lemma 4.2(2), $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}} \otimes_E L \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(L)$ acts $L$-linearly and nontrivially on both two-dimensional $L$-vector spaces $V_1$ and $V_2$. Since $\mathfrak{sl}_2(L)$ has a unique nontrivial two-dimensional module over $L$ up to isomorphism, there exists an **$L$-linear, $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}}$-equivariant isomorphism** $\Phi \colon V_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} V_2$.

Let $H_0 := dh(i) \in \mathrm{Lie}\,G_{12, \mathbb{R}} = \mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{R}} \oplus \mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathbb{R}}$ be the infinitesimal generator of $h(e^{i\vartheta}) = \exp(\vartheta H_0)$ ($\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}$), and write $H_0 = Z_0 + X_0$ with $Z_0 \in \mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{R}}$ and $X_0 \in \mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathbb{R}}$. On $V_{k, \sigma_0} = V_{k, \sigma_0}^{2,0} \oplus V_{k, \sigma_0}^{1,1}$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} = 2$), $h_k(e^{i\vartheta})$ acts with eigenvalues $(e^{2i\vartheta}, 1)$, so $H_0|_{V_{k, \sigma_0}}$ has eigenvalues $(2i, 0)$ and trace $2i$, while $X_0|_{V_{k, \sigma_0}} \in \mathfrak{sl}(V_{k, \sigma_0})$ has trace $0$. Since $Z(G_{12})^\circ$ projects into $Z(\mathrm{Hg}(V_k))^\circ = L^1$, $Z_0|_{V_k} \in L \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R}$ acts on $V_k$ by the $L$-scalar $\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_L(H_0|_{V_k})$, which has eigenvalues $+i$ at $\sigma_0$, $-i$ at $\bar{\sigma}_0$, and $0$ at all embeddings over $\tau \ne \tau_0$ (where $V_{k, \tau}$ is pure of type $(1,1)$). Crucially, this element of $L \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R}$ is **identical** for $k = 1$ and $k = 2$. Because $\Phi$ is $L$-linear, $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}$ intertwines $Z_0|_{V_1}$ and $Z_0|_{V_2}$; because $\Phi$ is $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{der}}$-equivariant, $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}$ intertwines $X_0|_{V_1}$ and $X_0|_{V_2}$. Hence $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}$ intertwines $H_0 = Z_0 + X_0$, so $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}} \circ h_1(z) = h_2(z) \circ \Phi_{\mathbb{R}}$ for all $z = r e^{i\vartheta} \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ (as $h_k(r) = r^2\,\mathrm{id}$). Thus $\Phi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(V_1, V_2)$ is a nonzero Hodge isomorphism, which is algebraic (with algebraic inverse) by Proposition 4.3. $\square$

### 4.4 Unconditional $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$ crossing classes via $M_2(E)$ simplicity

Let $S''$ be a CM $m = 4$ member of row $r$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ with $V = T(S'')$ and $L = E(S'') = E(\eta)$ ($\bar{\eta} = -\eta$, $\eta^2 \in E_{<0}$ at all real places of $E$). Let $T_{S''} = Z(\mathrm{Hg}(V))^\circ \cong L^1$ be its central Hodge torus and $\mathfrak{s} = \mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Hg}(V)^{\mathrm{der}} \cong \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{sl}_1(Q_r)$. Let $\tau_{12} \colon V \otimes V \to V \otimes V$ denote the factor-swap involution $\tau_{12}(x \otimes y) = y \otimes x$, which is induced by permuting the two factors of $S'' \times S''$.

Examine the $T_{S''}$-invariant subspace $(V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} V)^{T_{S''}}$. Over $\mathbb{C}$, $V = \bigoplus_{\tau} (V_{\nu_\tau} \oplus V_{\bar{\nu}_\tau})$ ($\nu_\tau, \bar{\nu}_\tau \mid \tau$), where $L^1(\mathbb{C}) \cong \prod_\tau \mathbb{C}^\times$ acts by $z_\tau$ on $V_{\nu_\tau}$ and $z_\tau^{-1}$ on $V_{\bar{\nu}_\tau}$. Since the $d$ characters $z_\tau$ of $L^1$ are distinct and nontrivial, $(V \otimes V)^{T_{S''}} \otimes \mathbb{C} = \bigoplus_\tau \bigl((V_{\nu_\tau} \otimes V_{\bar{\nu}_\tau}) \oplus (V_{\bar{\nu}_\tau} \otimes V_{\nu_\tau})\bigr)$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $8d$. Under $\mathfrak{s}_\tau \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, each $V_{\nu_\tau} \otimes V_{\bar{\nu}_\tau} \cong \mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2} = \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbf{3}$ splits into a trivial line and a $3$-dimensional adjoint summand. Globally over $\mathbb{Q}$, the trivial summand is the $2d$-dimensional factorwise Hodge space $\mathrm{Hdg}(V \otimes V) \cong L(-2)$ (spanned by the polarization paired with $L$), and its orthogonal complement
$$U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'') \subset (V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} V)^{T_{S''}}, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'') = 6d,$$
is the **adjoint-isotypic component** of $\mathfrak{s}$ in $V \otimes V$, consisting of **two** copies of the irreducible $3d$-dimensional adjoint Hodge structure $\mathfrak{s}(-2)$.

Now inspect the algebraic operators $\tau_{12}$ and $\eta \otimes 1$ on $(V \otimes V)^{T_{S''}}$:
- Both $\tau_{12}$ and $\eta \otimes 1$ commute with the diagonal actions of $E$, $T_{S''}$, and $\mathfrak{s}$, hence preserve $U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'')$ as $E$-linear Hodge endomorphisms.
- Moreover, $\tau_{12}^2 = 1$, $(\eta \otimes 1)^2 = \eta^2 \in E^\times$, and $(\eta \otimes 1)\tau_{12} + \tau_{12}(\eta \otimes 1) = (\eta \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \eta)\tau_{12}$. On every summand $V_{\nu_\tau} \otimes V_{\bar{\nu}_\tau}$, $\eta \otimes 1$ acts by $+\nu_\tau(\eta)$ while $1 \otimes \eta$ acts by $\bar{\nu}_\tau(\eta) = -\nu_\tau(\eta)$, so **$(\eta \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \eta) = 0$ identically on $(V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} V)^{T_{S''}}$**.
- Consequently, on $(V \otimes V)^{T_{S''}}$ (and hence on $U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'')$), $\tau_{12}$ and $\eta \otimes 1$ **anticommute**:
  $$\tau_{12}^2 = 1, \qquad (\eta \otimes 1)^2 = \eta^2 \in E^\times, \qquad \tau_{12}(\eta \otimes 1) = -(\eta \otimes 1)\tau_{12}.$$
  They therefore generate the split quaternion algebra $(1, \eta^2)_E \cong M_2(E)$. Since $M_2(E)$ is a simple $4$-dimensional $E$-algebra and $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'')) \cong M_2(\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(\mathfrak{s}(-2))) = M_2(E)$ also has $E$-dimension $4$, we obtain an exact equality:
  $$\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'')\bigr) = E \cdot 1 \oplus E \cdot \tau_{12} \oplus E \cdot (\eta \otimes 1) \oplus E \cdot (\eta \otimes 1)\tau_{12} \cong M_2(E) \tag{4.1}$$
  consisting **entirely of algebraic correspondences on $S'' \times S''$**, and $U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S'')$ is an **irreducible $\bigl(M_2(E) \otimes_E \mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{s})\bigr)$-module**.

**Proposition 4.5 (Unconditional $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$ for $\mathrm{U}_2 \times \mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$, $\mathrm{O}_3 \times \mathrm{U}_2$, and $\mathrm{U}_2 \times \mathrm{U}_2$).** *Let $X_1, X_2$ be members of row $r$ whose active $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-halves are glued along $M_{t_1} \cong_{F\text{-Hdg}} M_{t_2}$, with no other gluing between their simple ideals:*

1. *(Linked $\mathrm{O}_3 \times \mathrm{U}_2$.) If $X_1 = S$ has $m = 3$ and $X_2 = S''$ has CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$, then $T(S)(-1)$ embeds by an algebraic cycle $\Phi \colon T(S)(-1) \hookrightarrow T(S'')^{\otimes 2}$ as an algebraic direct summand with algebraic retraction $r \colon T(S'')^{\otimes 2} \twoheadrightarrow T(S)(-1)$, and every Hodge class on $\prod_{a,b} S^a \times (S'')^b$ is algebraic.*
2. *(Partially linked $\mathrm{U}_2 \times \mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$.) If $X_1 = S''_1$ has CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ ($L_1 = E(S''_1)$) and $X_2 = S'_2$ has totally real $m = 4$ ($E(S'_2) = E$), then $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(T(S''_1)^{\otimes 2} \otimes T(S'_2)^{\otimes 2}\bigr) = 2d^2 + 2d$ ($2d^2$ product classes plus $2d$ crossing classes), and every Hodge class in $T(S''_1)^{\otimes 2} \otimes T(S'_2)^{\otimes 2}$ is algebraic on $(S''_1)^2 \times (S'_2)^2$.*
3. *(Partially linked $\mathrm{U}_2 \times \mathrm{U}_2$ with $L_1 \not\cong_E L_2$.) If $X_1 = S''_1$ and $X_2 = S''_2$ both have CM $m = 4$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ with $L_1 \not\cong_E L_2$, then $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(T(S''_1)^{\otimes 2} \otimes T(S''_2)^{\otimes 2}\bigr) = 4d^2 + 4d$ ($4d^2$ product classes plus $4d$ crossing classes), and every Hodge class in $T(S''_1)^{\otimes 2} \otimes T(S''_2)^{\otimes 2}$ is algebraic on $(S''_1)^2 \times (S''_2)^2$.*

*Proof.* (1) Inspect [7, Prop. 5.1 and Lem. 5.2] with $S_s = S$ ($m = 3$) and $S_{s'} = S''$ (CM $m = 4$, $B_{s'} = i_{s'}(T'') \oplus \theta i_{s'}(T'')$ by Lemma 4.1(2)). Every step of [7, Prop. 5.1(1)–(4)] uses only Hodge–Riemann nondegeneracy of $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{u}$ on $M_{u} \subset H^1(C'_{u})$, the algebraic gluing $M_x \cong M_y \cong M_t$, and the algebraic isomorphism $j_{s'}^{-1} \colon B_{s'} \xrightarrow{\sim} T'' \oplus T''$; hence at least one component $\Phi \colon T(S)(-1) \to T'' \otimes T''$ is an injective algebraic Hodge morphism. In [7, Lem. 5.2], Hodge–Riemann positivity gives $\Phi^t \Phi \ne 0$ in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}} T(S) = E$, so $r = (\Phi^t \Phi)^{-1} \Phi^t$ is an algebraic retraction $r \circ \Phi = \mathrm{id}_{T(S)(-1)}$. Pulling back and pushing forward via $\Phi^{\otimes a}$ and $r^{\otimes a}$ reduces $\mathrm{HC}$ for $S^a \times (S'')^b$ directly to $\mathrm{HC}$ for $(S'')^{2a + b}$, which holds by [2, Cor. 2.3].

(2)–(3) Write $V_1 = T(X_1)$ and $V_2 = T(X_2)$. In case (2) ($X_2 = S'_2$), $\mathrm{Hg}(V_1 \oplus V_2)$ has center $T_1 \cong L_1^1$ (acting on $V_1$ only) and derived Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}_t \oplus \mathfrak{h}_{u_2}$ ($\mathfrak{h}_t$ glued across $M_{t_1} \cong M_{t_2}$, $\mathfrak{h}_{u_2}$ acting on $V_2$ only). Invariants in $V_1^{\otimes 2} \otimes V_2^{\otimes 2}$ must lie in $(V_1^{\otimes 2})^{T_1} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} (V_2^{\otimes 2})^{\mathfrak{h}_{u_2}}$. Using $(V_1^{\otimes 2})^{T_1} = \mathrm{Hdg}(V_1^{\otimes 2}) \oplus U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1)$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} = 2d + 6d$) and $(V_2^{\otimes 2})^{\mathfrak{h}_{u_2}} = \mathrm{Hdg}(V_2^{\otimes 2}) \oplus U_{t_2}(S'_2)$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} = d + 3d$, [7, Prop. 8.1]), we obtain
$$\mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(V_1^{\otimes 2} \otimes V_2^{\otimes 2}\bigr) \cong W_1 \oplus \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1),\; U_{t_2}(S'_2)\bigr),$$
where $W_1 = \mathrm{Hdg}(V_1^{\otimes 2}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{Hdg}(V_2^{\otimes 2})$ consists of exterior products of factorwise Hodge classes of $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} L_1)(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} E) = (2d) \cdot d = 2d^2$, while $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1), U_{t_2}(S'_2)\bigr)$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $2d$ (since $U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1) \cong \mathfrak{s}(-2)^{\oplus 2}$, $U_{t_2}(S'_2) \cong \mathfrak{s}(-2)$, and $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(\mathfrak{s}(-2)) = E$), giving total dimension $2d^2 + 2d$. Similarly, in case (3) ($X_2 = S''_2$ with $L_1 \not\cong_E L_2$), Lemma 5.1 (proved independently in §5.1 below) gives $Z(\mathrm{Hg}(V_1 \oplus V_2))^\circ = T_1 \times T_2 \cong L_1^1 \times L_2^1$. Hence invariants in $V_1^{\otimes 2} \otimes V_2^{\otimes 2}$ lie in $(V_1^{\otimes 2})^{T_1} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} (V_2^{\otimes 2})^{T_2}$, yielding
$$\mathrm{Hdg}\bigl(V_1^{\otimes 2} \otimes V_2^{\otimes 2}\bigr) \cong W_1 \oplus \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1),\; U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_2)\bigr),$$
where $W_1 = \mathrm{Hdg}(V_1^{\otimes 2}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{Hdg}(V_2^{\otimes 2})$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $(2d)(2d) = 4d^2$ and $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1), U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_2)\bigr) \cong M_2(E)$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $4d$, giving total dimension $4d^2 + 4d$.

Now construct the algebraic crossing classes in both (2) and (3) simultaneously. Just as in [7, Prop. 8.1], let $\rho_1 \colon V_1^{\otimes 2} \to Z_{t_1} \subset M_{t_1} \otimes M_{t_1}$ be the algebraic correspondence composing $i_{s_1}^{\otimes 2}$ with the cup-product contraction $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{u_1}$ on the two $u_1$-slots, and let $\iota_2 = (\text{proj}) \circ (j_{s_2}^{-1})^{\otimes 2} \circ \Phi_0^{(2)} \circ (X_0 \otimes X_0) \colon Z_{t_1} \to (V_2^{\otimes 2})^{\oplus 4}$ be the algebraic insertion of [7, Prop. 5.1(3)–(4)], built using $B_{s_2} \cong V_2^{\oplus 2}$ (Lemma 4.1(2)) and $\xi_{u_2}$. Over $\mathbb{C}$, $i_{s_1}$ maps $V_{1, \tau}$ isomorphically onto $B_{s_1, \sigma_\tau}$ and $B_{s_1, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ (Lemma 4.1(2)), so on the $(\sigma_\tau, \bar{\sigma}_\tau)$-summand, contracting $M_{u_1, \bar{\sigma}_\tau}$ with $M_{u_1, \sigma_\tau}$ via $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{u_1}$ maps $U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1)_\tau$ **onto** the trace-free adjoint part $Z_{t_1, \tau}^0$, on which $\iota_2$ is **injective**. Therefore at least one of the four components $\kappa \colon V_1^{\otimes 2} \to V_2^{\otimes 2}$ of $\iota_2 \circ \rho_1$ satisfies $\kappa|_{U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1)} \ne 0$.
- In case (2), $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1), U_{t_2}(S'_2)\bigr)$ is an irreducible right $M_2(E)$-module of $E$-dimension $2$ (the dual standard module of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1)) = M_2(E)$ from (4.1)). Since the projection of $\kappa$ into this module is nonzero and $M_2(E)$ acts algebraically by pre-composition with $\{1, \tau_{12}, \eta_1 \otimes 1, (\eta_1 \otimes 1)\tau_{12}\}$, the algebraic family $\kappa \circ M_2(E)$ spans the **entire** $2d$-dimensional crossing space. Together with $W_1$ (products of factorwise algebraic classes on $(S''_1)^2$ and $(S'_2)^2$), this spans all $2d^2 + 2d$ Hodge classes in $V_1^{\otimes 2} \otimes V_2^{\otimes 2}$.
- In case (3), $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\bigl(U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_1), U_{\mathrm{adj}}(S''_2)\bigr) \cong M_2(E)$ is a **simple $(M_2(E), M_2(E))$-bimodule** under algebraic post-composition by $M_2(E)^{(2)} = \langle 1, \tau_{12}, \eta_2 \otimes 1, (\eta_2 \otimes 1)\tau_{12} \rangle$ on $V_2^{\otimes 2}$ and algebraic pre-composition by $M_2(E)^{(1)}$ on $V_1^{\otimes 2}$. Since any nonzero two-sided ideal of the simple algebra $M_2(E)$ is all of $M_2(E)$, $M_2(E)^{(2)} \circ \kappa \circ M_2(E)^{(1)}$ spans all $4d$ crossing classes, and adding $W_1$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} = 4d^2$) spans all $4d^2 + 4d$ Hodge classes in $V_1^{\otimes 2} \otimes V_2^{\otimes 2}$. $\square$

## 5. Right-$N$ CM Torus Independence and the Unified Graph-Torus Obstruction

### 5.1 Unconditional CM torus independence over a fixed $(E, \tau_0)$

For each CM $m = 4$ surface $S''_i$ ($1 \le i \le a$) of row $r$ of type $\mathrm{U}_2$ with CM field $L_i = E(S''_i)$ ($[L_i : E] = 2$), let $D_i = \bigwedge^2_{L_i} T(S''_i)$ be its $L_i$-determinant Hodge structure ($\dim_{L_i} D_i = 1$, $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} D_i = 2d$, cut out of $T(S''_i)^{\otimes 2}$ by an algebraic idempotent [3, Lem. 6.1(v)]). At the two conjugate embeddings $\sigma_{0,i}, \bar{\sigma}_{0,i} \colon L_i \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ extending $\tau_0$, $T(S''_i)_{\sigma_{0,i}} = T^{2,0} \oplus T^{1,1}$ and $T(S''_i)_{\bar{\sigma}_{0,i}} = T^{1,1} \oplus T^{0,2}$, so $D_{i, \sigma_{0,i}}$ has Hodge bidegree $(3, 1)$ and $D_{i, \bar{\sigma}_{0,i}}$ has Hodge bidegree $(1, 3)$, whereas at all embeddings $\sigma \mid \tau$ ($\tau \ne \tau_0$), $T(S''_i)_\sigma$ is pure of type $(1, 1)$ and $D_{i, \sigma}$ is pure of type $(2, 2)$. By [3, Lem. 6.8(i)–(ii)], $\mathrm{Hg}(D_i) = L_i^1 = \ker(N_{L_i/E})$, and $Z(G)^\circ = \prod_{i=1}^a T_i$ holds if and only if $\mathrm{Hg}\bigl(\bigoplus_{i=1}^a D_i\bigr) = \prod_{i=1}^a L_i^1$. Note that [3, Lem. 6.9] requires the Galois closures of the $L_i$ to be linearly disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$, which fails whenever $a \ge 2$ and $E \ne \mathbb{Q}$ (since $E \subseteq L_1 \cap L_2$), and also fails over $E$ itself for biquadratic families such as $E(i), E(\sqrt{-3}), E(\sqrt{-15})$. Nevertheless, **pairwise $E$-non-isomorphism alone suffices unconditionally**:

**Lemma 5.1 (Right-$N$ CM Torus Independence).** *Let $E$ be a totally real number field of degree $d = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$ with a fixed real embedding $\tau_0 \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$, and let $L_1, \dots, L_a$ ($a \ge 1$) be **pairwise $E$-non-isomorphic** CM quadratic extensions of $E$ ($L_i \not\cong_E L_j$ for $i \ne j$). For each $i$, let $D_i$ be a rational CM Hodge structure of weight $2m_0$ with endomorphism field $L_i$ ($\dim_{L_i} D_i = 1$) having Hodge bidegrees $(p_0, q_0)$ and $(q_0, p_0)$ ($p_0 \ne q_0$, $p_0 + q_0 = 2m_0$) at the two conjugate embeddings $\sigma_{0,i}, \bar{\sigma}_{0,i} \colon L_i \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ extending $\tau_0$, and pure bidegree $(m_0, m_0)$ at all embeddings of $L_i$ over $\tau \ne \tau_0$. Then*
$$\mathrm{Hg}\Bigl(\bigoplus_{i=1}^a D_i\Bigr) = \prod_{i=1}^a L_i^1 \qquad \bigl(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{Hg} = a \cdot d\bigr).$$

*Proof.* Embed $E$ into $\mathbb{C}$ via the fixed real place $\tau_0 \colon E \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$, and embed each $L_i$ ($1 \le i \le a$) into $\mathbb{C}$ as an $E$-extension via $\sigma_{0, i} \colon L_i \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ (so $\sigma_{0, i}|_E = \tau_0(E)$). Let $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}$ be the Galois closure over $\mathbb{Q}$ of the compositum $\tau_0(E) \cdot \sigma_{0,1}(L_1) \cdots \sigma_{0,a}(L_a)$. Since each $L_i$ is a CM field, $\Lambda$ is a CM field closed under complex conjugation $c \in G := \mathrm{Gal}(\Lambda/\mathbb{Q})$, with $c$ **central** in $G$. Put
$$N := \mathrm{Gal}(\Lambda / \tau_0(E)) \le G, \qquad H_i := \mathrm{Gal}(\Lambda / \sigma_{0, i}(L_i)) \le N \quad (1 \le i \le a).$$
Observe three group-theoretic properties:
1. Because $\tau_0(E) \subset \mathbb{R}$ while $\sigma_{0, i}(L_i) \not\subset \mathbb{R}$, complex conjugation satisfies **$c \in N$ and $c \notin H_i$** for every $i$.
2. Each $H_i$ has **index $2$ in $N$** ($[\sigma_{0,i}(L_i) : \tau_0(E)] = [L_i : E] = 2$), so every $H_i$ is normal in $N$ and defines a quadratic character $\chi_i \colon N \to \{\pm 1\}$ with $\ker \chi_i = H_i$ and $\chi_i(c) = -1$.
3. Because $L_1, \dots, L_a$ are pairwise $E$-non-isomorphic, their images $\sigma_{0,1}(L_1), \dots, \sigma_{0,a}(L_a)$ are **pairwise distinct** quadratic subextensions of $\Lambda$ over $\tau_0(E)$: indeed, if $\sigma_{0, i}(L_i) = \sigma_{0, j}(L_j)$ inside $\mathbb{C}$, then $\sigma_{0, j}^{-1} \circ \sigma_{0, i} \colon L_i \xrightarrow{\sim} L_j$ would be an $E$-isomorphism (as $\sigma_{0, i}|_E = \tau_0 = \sigma_{0, j}|_E$), contradicting $L_i \not\cong_E L_j$. By Galois theory over $\tau_0(E)$, $H_1, \dots, H_a$ are pairwise distinct index-$2$ subgroups of $N$, and because each $\chi_i$ takes values in $\{\pm 1\}$ (where a homomorphism is uniquely determined by its kernel), **$\chi_1, \dots, \chi_a \colon N \to \{\pm 1\} \subset \mathbb{C}^\times$ are pairwise distinct group characters of $N$**.

Now let $\psi = (\psi_1, \dots, \psi_a) \in X^*\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^a L_i^1\bigr)$ be a character trivial on $\mathrm{Hg}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^a D_i)$, lifted to $\prod_{i=1}^a \mathrm{Res}_{L_i/\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{G}_m$ as $\psi_i = \sum_{\sigma \in G/H_i} m_{i, \sigma H_i} [\sigma H_i]$ ($m_{i, \sigma H_i} \in \mathbb{Z}$, identifying $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(L_i, \mathbb{C})$ with $G / H_i$ via $\gamma \mapsto \gamma|_{\sigma_{0,i}(L_i)}$). Since $D_i$ has Hodge bidegrees $(p_0, q_0)$ at $1 \cdot H_i$, $(q_0, p_0)$ at $c H_i$, and $(m_0, m_0)$ on all other left cosets of $H_i$ in $G$, the projection of the Hodge cocharacter into $\prod_{i=1}^a L_i^1$ is the nonzero multiple $\frac{1}{2}(p_0 - q_0)\,\mu$ of $\mu = (\mu_1, \dots, \mu_a)$ with $\mu_i = [H_i] - [c H_i]$. By Mumford–Tate Galois invariance (as in [3, Proof of Lem. 6.9]), $\psi$ vanishes on $\mathrm{Hg}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^a D_i)$ if and only if $\langle {}^\gamma \psi, \mu \rangle = 0$ for all $\gamma \in G$. Setting $x = \gamma^{-1} \in G$, this reads
$$\sum_{i=1}^a \bigl(m_{i, x H_i} - m_{i, x c H_i}\bigr) = 0 \qquad \forall x \in G.$$
Fix any $g \in G$ and evaluate this identity along the **right coset $x = g n$ ($n \in N$)**:
- If $n \in H_i$ ($\chi_i(n) = +1$), then $g n H_i = g H_i$ and (since $c$ is central in $G$) $g n c H_i = g c n H_i = g c H_i$, so $m_{i, g n H_i} - m_{i, g n c H_i} = +(m_{i, g H_i} - m_{i, g c H_i})$.
- If $n \notin H_i$ ($\chi_i(n) = -1$), then since $[N : H_i] = 2$ and $c \in N \setminus H_i$, we have $N = H_i \sqcup c H_i$, hence $n \in c H_i$ and $n c \in c^2 H_i = H_i$. Thus $g n H_i = g c H_i$ and $g n c H_i = g H_i$, so $m_{i, g n H_i} - m_{i, g n c H_i} = -(m_{i, g H_i} - m_{i, g c H_i})$.

Setting $\Delta_i(g) := m_{i, g H_i} - m_{i, g c H_i} \in \mathbb{Z}$, both cases combine into the exact identity
$$\sum_{i=1}^a \Delta_i(g)\,\chi_i(n) = 0 \qquad \forall n \in N.$$
By **Artin–Dedekind linear independence of distinct characters $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_a \colon N \to \mathbb{C}^\times$**, it follows that **$\Delta_i(g) = 0$ for every $1 \le i \le a$ and every $g \in G$**. Because $c$ is central in $G$, $g c H_i = c g H_i$, so $m_{i, g H_i} = m_{i, c g H_i}$ for every coset $g H_i \in G / H_i$; that is, $\psi_i$ factors through the norm $N_{L_i/E}$ and restricts to the trivial character on $L_i^1$ for every $i$. Hence $\mathrm{Hg}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^a D_i) = \prod_{i=1}^a L_i^1$. $\square$

### 5.2 The unified graph-torus obstruction lemma and proof of Theorem 3

Recall the Tannakian setup of [3, §§6.5–6.9] for a collection of projective K3 surfaces $X_1, \dots, X_m$ of small types $\mathrm{O}_3$, $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$, and $\mathrm{U}_2$ satisfying $(\mathrm{H1})$, $(\mathrm{H2})$, $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$, and $(\mathrm{H3})$, with $V_i = T(X_i)$, $H = \prod_i \mathrm{Hg}(V_i)$, $G = \mathrm{Hg}(\bigoplus_i V_i) \subseteq C \subseteq H$ ($C$ the motivic group of algebraic correspondences), and $N = Z(G^{\mathrm{der}}) \cdot Z(G)^\circ = G \cap Z(H)$ [3, Lem. 6.7]. Let $\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}$ denote the subgraph of $\Gamma_p$ ($p = \tau_0$) formed by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-block vertices and the **$\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ edges only** (omitting the $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaf edges).

**Proposition 5.2 (Unified graph-torus obstruction lemma).** *Suppose $X_1, \dots, X_m$ are pairwise non-isogenous K3 surfaces of types $\mathrm{O}_3$, $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$, and $\mathrm{U}_2$ satisfying $(\mathrm{H1})$, $(\mathrm{H2})$, $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$, and $(\mathrm{H3})$.*

1. *(Full torus independence: arbitrary cycles + arbitrary $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaves.) If $Z(G)^\circ = \prod_{i \in I_{\mathrm{U}_2}} T_i$ (automatic under Lemma 5.1 when the $\mathrm{U}_2$ factors belong to one row $r$ with pairwise $E$-non-isomorphic $L_i$), then $(\mathrm{H3})$ holds automatically from $(\mathrm{H1})$, and the central obstruction group satisfies an injective homomorphism*
   $$(C \cap Z(H)) / N \hookrightarrow H^1(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}; \mathbb{F}_2).$$
   *Consequently, $C = G$ (i.e. the rational Hodge conjecture holds for all finite products $\prod_{i=1}^m X_i^{e_i}$) if and only if for a basis of cycles $\gamma \in Z_1(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}; \mathbb{F}_2)$ the $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ cycle Hodge classes $t_{\gamma, \tau_0}$ lie in the $\mathbb{C}$-span of algebraic classes—with **no** restriction on the number of $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaves per connected component.*
2. *(General torus: at most one $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaf per component + arbitrary cycles.) Even without $Z(G)^\circ = \prod T_i$, if $(\mathrm{H3})$ holds and every connected component of $\Gamma_{\tau_0}$ contains at most one $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaf (while allowing arbitrary $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ cycles), then $(C \cap Z(H))/N \hookrightarrow H^1(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}; \mathbb{F}_2)$, and $C = G$ holds whenever the $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ cycle classes $t_{\gamma, \tau_0}$ are algebraic.*

*Proof.* (1) First, if $Z(G)^\circ = \prod_{i \in I_{\mathrm{U}_2}} T_i$, then $\mathrm{Hg}(\bigoplus_i D_i) = \prod_i L_i^1 = \prod_i \mathrm{Hg}(D_i)$ [3, Lem. 6.8(ii)], so every Hodge class in tensors of $\bigoplus_i D_i$ is a sum of exterior products of factorwise Hodge classes in tensors of individual $D_i$, which are algebraic by factorwise $(\mathrm{H1})$ [3, Lem. 6.1(v)]; hence $(\mathrm{H3})$ holds automatically.

Next, by [3, Lem. 6.4, Lem. 6.5, Prop. 6.6], $(\mathrm{H1})$, $(\mathrm{H2})$, and $(\mathrm{H2}^+)$ give $\mathrm{Lie}(C)^{\mathrm{der}} = \mathrm{Lie}(G)^{\mathrm{der}}$ and $C / G \cong (C \cap Z(H)) / N$. Let $z = (z_i) \in C \cap Z(H)$. For every $\mathrm{U}_2$ factor $i \in I_{\mathrm{U}_2}$, $z_i$ lies in $Z(\mathrm{Hg}(V_i)) = Z(\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mathrm{U}(V_i, \psi_{L_i})) = L_i^1 = T_i$. Because $Z(G)^\circ = \prod_{i \in I_{\mathrm{U}_2}} T_i \subseteq N$ acts by $T_i$ on factor $i \in I_{\mathrm{U}_2}$ and **trivially** on all $\mathrm{O}_3$ and $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ factors, multiplying $z$ by the element $t^{-1} = (z_i^{-1})_{i \in I_{\mathrm{U}_2}} \in Z(G)^\circ \subseteq N$ replaces $z$ by an element $z' = z t^{-1} \in C \cap Z(H)$ congruent to $z$ modulo $N$ whose **every $\mathrm{U}_2$ component is $z'_i = 1$** (and whose $\mathrm{O}_3$ components are $z'_i = 1$ since $Z(\mathrm{SO}_3) = 1$).

Thus $z'$ is supported solely on the $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ factors, where $z'_e \in \mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} \mu_2$ gives edge signs $(\varepsilon_{e, \tau})$. Now verify which elements of $N$ preserve the normalization $z'_i = 1$ on all $\mathrm{U}_2$ factors: let $s \in S = \prod_{v \in V(\Gamma)} \mu_2 \subseteq Z(G^{\mathrm{der}})$ be a tuple of vertex signs from the centers of the simply connected $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-blocks. On an $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ edge $e = (u, v)$, $s$ acts by $s_u s_v$; on a $\mathrm{U}_2$ factor $i$ attached to vertex $u_i$, $s$ acts by $s_{u_i} \in \mu_2 \subset T_i[2]$, which can be **cancelled back to $1$ by multiplying by $s_{u_i}^{-1}$ in the $i$-th factor of $Z(G)^\circ = \prod T_i \subseteq N$** without affecting any $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ edge. Hence $z' \in N$ if and only if its $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ edge signs $(\varepsilon_e)$ lie in the image of the coboundary $\delta_{\mathrm{orth}} \colon \mathbb{F}_2^{V(\Gamma)} \to \mathbb{F}_2^{E(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}})}$ on each Galois conjugate $\Gamma_\tau$, giving $(C \cap Z(H))/N \hookrightarrow H^1(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}; \mathbb{F}_2)$ (per Galois orbit of $\tau$, as in [3, §6.9]). By [3, Thm. C(b)], this quotient vanishes if and only if all $t_{\gamma, \tau_0}$ for $\gamma$ in a basis of $Z_1(\Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}; \mathbb{F}_2)$ lie in the $\mathbb{C}$-span of algebraic classes.

(2) Under [3, Lem. 6.10], $(\mathrm{H1})$–$(\mathrm{H3})$ normalize $z$ modulo $N$ to $z'$ with $(z')^2 = 1$ supported on $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ and $\mathrm{U}_2$ edges. If all $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ cycle characters satisfy $\chi_\gamma(z') = 1$, then on every connected component $\mathcal{C}$ of $\Gamma_\tau$, the restriction of $(\varepsilon_e)$ to $\mathcal{C} \cap \Gamma_{\mathrm{orth}}$ lies in $\ker(\text{cycles}) = \mathrm{im}(\delta_{\mathrm{orth}}|_{\mathcal{C}})$, so there exists a vertex sign assignment $(s_v)_{v \in V(\mathcal{C})}$ solving $s_u s_v = \varepsilon_{(u,v)}$ on **every** $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ edge of $\mathcal{C}$, uniquely up to a single global flip $(s_v) \mapsto (-s_v)$ on $V(\mathcal{C})$. If $\mathcal{C}$ has one $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaf at $u_0 \in V(\mathcal{C})$ with sign $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$, choose whichever of the two global flips $(s_v)$ or $(-s_v)$ sets $s_{u_0} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$; then $s \in Z(G^{\mathrm{der}}) \subseteq N$ matches $z'$ on **all** edges of $\mathcal{C}$ (both $\mathrm{O}_4^{\mathrm{sp}}$ and the $\mathrm{U}_2$ leaf). Doing this independently on every component gives $z' \in N$. $\square$

## 7. Limitations and Open Questions

1. **Unglued $E$-isomorphic $\mathrm{U}_2$ pairs in the six carrier-free rows.** In rows $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9\}$, condition $(\dagger)$ of Theorem 3(3) leaves open products containing two *unglued* CM $\mathrm{U}_2$ surfaces $S''_1, S''_2$ whose CM endomorphism fields satisfy $L_1 \cong_E L_2$. As verified in control `[C5]`, their diagonal central torus $L_1^1$ creates a $2d$-dimensional space of Weil-type determinant classes $\mathrm{Hdg}(D_1 \otimes D_2) \subset T(S''_1)^{\otimes 2} \otimes T(S''_2)^{\otimes 2}$ comparing $\bigwedge^2_F M_{u_1}$ and $\bigwedge^2_F M_{u_2}$ across unrelated CM curve parameters $u_1, u_2$ without a glued active half. Whether an auxiliary bridge surface $S'_{t_1, u_2}$ inside the two-parameter reference family $(t, u)$ can algebraically transport $\bigwedge^2_F M_{u_1}$ to $\bigwedge^2_F M_{u_2}$ without a fixed carrier is a natural next question.
2. **Cross-row $\mathrm{U}_2$ products without Galois disjointness.** In Theorem 3(3), auxiliary factors $Y_h$ from other rows are assumed to have semisimple Hodge groups (covering all totally real $m \in \{3, 4\}$ surfaces of all rows). If two *different* rows with distinct base fields $(E_1, \tau_0) \ne (E_2, \tau'_0)$ both contribute $\mathrm{U}_2$ factors, their CM determinant tori remain independent whenever their Galois closures are linearly disjoint [3, Lem. 6.9], and also across different rows sharing the *same* $(E, \tau_0)$ (such as rows 2 and 3 over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$) whenever $L_a \not\cong_E L_b$ by Lemma 5.1; however, across conjugate real subfields in a shared $D_4$ closure, torus rank drops (`4/6` in `[D4]`), requiring explicit multi-factor $(\mathrm{H3})$ classes [15], [16].
3. **Dependence on imported geometry and scope of rows.** All results build on the reference-family realizations $(\mathrm{F1})$–$(\mathrm{F3})$ and $(\mathrm{H})$ established in [4], [5], [6], [8], [9], [10], [11] and the Tannakian reduction of [3]. Extending beyond the ten quaternionic rows (for example to non-square discriminant $m = 4$ types or $m \ge 5$) requires new geometric realizations of K3 families.
