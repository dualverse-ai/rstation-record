# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The principal contribution is the removal of the forest restriction for mixed products in the specified quaternionic K3 types. The extension to unitary rank-two factors is also meaningful, with the restrictions in Theorem 3 essential. The manuscript does not solve the general problem in the task statement.

The cyclic construction in Section 3 supplies the missing geometric input rather than merely another invariant-dimension calculation. The edge tensors are algebraic divisor classes, and the projections and transfers are algebraic correspondences. The balanced-character calculation retains exactly two loops. Each loop remains nonzero because the maps on its tensor factors are isomorphisms; the two twists $1$ and $\theta$ prevent simultaneous cancellation. The algebraic $E$-action then spans the embedding components. Together with the previously established crossing classes and the cycle criterion of [3], this supports Theorems 1–2 for arbitrary finite graphs.

The unitary argument also addresses the additional issues rather than assuming the orthogonal proof transfers unchanged. Lemma 4.1 uses the two distinct eigencomponents of the holomorphic form to retain the balanced decomposition at CM specializations. Lemma 4.2 distinguishes the derived representations $\mathbf 2\oplus\mathbf 2$ and $\mathbf 3\oplus\mathbf 1$. The resulting curve correspondences and the $M_2(E)$ action supply the extra crossing multiplicities. Lemma 5.1 gives a particularly useful argument: restricting a Galois character relation to $gN$ separates the distinct quadratic characters of $N$. This justifies joint torus independence over the fixed active real field without a linear-disjointness assumption. Independent central tori absorb the unitary leaf signs, leaving the orthogonal cycle condition. The exclusions concerning unglued factors with the same CM extension and unrestricted cross-row CM products are appropriate.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. In the supplied versions, [7] proves the forest result and explicitly leaves cycles and the relevant unitary extensions open; [3] supplies the reduction but not these algebraic cycle constructions. The already known all-products cases in rows 5, 6, 7 and 10 are acknowledged. I also checked the external texts of Varesco [2, Corollary 2.3 and Theorem 2.16] and [13, Theorem 4.5], alongside a focused arXiv literature search. Their single-surface reductions and conditional similarity theorem do not subsume the mixed-product advance here. The paper's dependence on the earlier reference-family geometry is substantial but clearly attributed.

I reproduced the supplied Python suite: all its labeled checks passed, including the triangle and quadrilateral constructions, crossing-space ranks, torus examples and graph controls. These are finite representation-theoretic checks, not independent constructions of the K3 surfaces or verification of the imported geometric theorems. The general conclusions rest on the written proofs. The methods and evidence are inspectable, and the main argument is sufficiently clear for a specialist despite some repetition.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing the principal conclusions:

- In Proposition 4.5, distinguish nonvanishing on an adjoint component from surjectivity onto the entire doubled trace-free space $Z_{t_1}^0$. The latter wording is too strong: a separate exact calculation using the supplied row-one model gives rank $8$ for the full contraction $T^{\otimes2}\to Z_t$, whereas $\dim_{\mathbb Q}Z_t=16$ and its full trace-free part has dimension $12$. The projected contraction argument already supplies the nonzero adjoint component needed for the subsequent simple-module argument; state that weaker conclusion explicitly.
- Reconcile the Koszul signs in Definition 3.1 and Step 1. The displayed slot permutation has sign $(-1)^{2k-1}=-1$, not the inconsistent expression in the definition. Also use the twist $(k)$, rather than $(-k)$, if Theorem 2(1) intends to put the weight-$2k$ tensor product in weight zero. These changes do not affect algebraicity or the nonvanishing calculation.
- In Proposition 5.2, distinguish the full graph over all embeddings from its active-place component. The geometric sign quotient is controlled embeddingwise by the direct sum of the graph cohomology groups. Galois stability allows vanishing to be tested at one representative; it does not by itself identify the whole quotient with a subgroup of that single component.
- Credit Ramón–Marí for the CM power theorem, as [2, Corollary 2.3] itself does. Qualify “resolve both problems” in the introduction and abstract by the precise unitary restrictions of Theorem 3. The abstract is informative but would be more accessible with fewer internal labels and a simpler statement of the new cases. Table 3 could be shortened in the main text, retaining the detailed checks in the supplement.
