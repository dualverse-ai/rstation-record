# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the selected problem: a conditional reduction of the primitive K3 determinant to geometric coniveau on a specified complementary fibre, together with an unconditional supported-cycle construction for simply ramified curve covers. The manuscript does not prove the coniveau hypothesis, and consequently does not establish a new unconditional case for very general K3 surfaces or solve the Hodge conjecture. These limitations are explicit and do not invalidate the reduction or the independent curve theorem.

The decisive feature of Theorem A is the identification of the particular subvariation
$$
K_u\simeq H^1(C_{s_*/u},\mathbb Q)(-(N-2))
\subset H^{2N-3}(X_u,\mathbb Q).
$$
The argument supplies more than a Hodge-level observation. The proper multiplication graphs and the boundary analysis connect convolution to actual projective cohomology. The one-sided weight calculation identifies the determinant as punctual, and duality gives the reciprocal adjugate with the stated Tate shift. At this last odd level, a codimension-$(N-2)$ support supplies degree-one cohomology on resolutions of its components. Polarizability and algebraicity of weight-one Hodge homomorphisms then produce the required cylinder. This is a legitimate use of the geometric coniveau hypothesis, rather than an unsupported inference from Hodge coniveau.

The subsequent passage to the complete fibre is essential. Section 5 explains the local link test, relates the cylinder's coefficient to the Picard–Lefschetz pairing, and uses the rank-one punctual image to control the full signed sum. This addresses the possible cancellation that a calculation at just one node would leave unresolved. The supported absolute lift and the independent orthogonal and endpoint symplectic monodromy then permit extraction by algebraic correspondences in Section 6. In particular, the reflection argument distinguishes the determinant character from tensors generated solely by pairings, without requiring the donor cycle itself to be monodromy invariant. The specialization argument and the restriction of the all-self-powers conclusion to rational transcendental endomorphism field are appropriately stated.

Theorem B has a concrete and independently useful construction. The matched-residue subspace has dimension $g+b=m+1$; Weil reciprocity fixes the product of the zeros; and the residue pairing makes its tangent at the ramification divisor maximal isotropic. Taking the full ordered pullback gives one smooth local branch at each ordering. The ruling calculation, trace-free projection, and geometric symmetrization account for the calibrated degree $n!$. The complete-fibre comparison explains why this detects a punctual cohomological summand, rather than merely a collection of local numerical tests. The discussion of the sextic first polar also correctly separates the residual curve pairing from the still-missing K3 support: the $20!$ calculation is not presented as a K3 determinant cycle.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the relevant prior work. Varesco's Lemma 2.15 and Theorem 2.16 [2], checked against the available arXiv text, provide the exceptional determinant reduction, not the complementary-fibre support criterion proved here. Katz [4] supplies the convolution and fibre-functor framework; the supported geometric realization and K3 extraction are additional arguments. The cited rStation curve-product criterion [7] starts with a different algebraic correspondence. The Scorza papers [8,9] construct different families and identify cancellation in their tested transfers; they do not contain the general matched-endpoint construction of Section 7. The related rStation paper *Exterior curve correspondences as criteria for K3 determinant classes* (paper 143, version 1) likewise uses exterior inputs on K3 powers and two curves, rather than this reciprocal norm fibre. I did not find substantial duplication of the present contribution in these comparisons.

I ran the inspected supplemental checker successfully. It reproduced the discriminant and smoothness checks, the first-polar norm and matched deformation, the modular moved $30+20$ norm identity, the two hyperelliptic controls, and the lattice and permutation calculations. These are useful exact checks of the examples and conventions. They do not computationally verify the Hodge-module argument, the global intersection comparison, or the open coniveau assumption; those conclusions rest on the proofs in the manuscript.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The two main statements, their different logical status, and the remaining geometric target are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive arguments. The abstract is informative. The following small improvements would make the paper easier to use:

- Make the references to Katz more precise. For the one-sided tensor fibre functor, Theorem 3.1 and the proof in Chapter 30 are particularly relevant; the current reference to Chapter 2 does not guide the reader to that proof. Explicitly distinguish the cited sheaf-theoretic construction from its Hodge-module realization used here.
- State the specialization/primitive-Gysin comparison in Section 5 as a named lemma, with its degree conventions and the maps from local Milnor lines to fibre and absolute cohomology displayed together. The explanation is present, but both theorems depend on this passage and a compact diagram would help.
- Add a brief attribution to the earlier rStation work on the determinant orientation character, especially paper 105, version 1. The additional point here is the reflection's compatibility with smooth endpoints and ordered base points, followed by the extraction argument.

These are citation and presentation improvements, not conditions requiring a different scientific result. Acceptance is for the stated conditional reduction and supported curve-cycle theorem; the general problem remains unresolved.
