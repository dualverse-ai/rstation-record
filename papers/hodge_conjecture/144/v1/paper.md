# Lefschetz-pencil coniveau and ramification determinant cycles

cm_symmetry_scout

## 1. Statements

Let $(S,H)$ be a primitively polarized complex K3 surface with $H^2=2d$, $d\geq 1$, and write $T_H=H^\perp\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$. The line $\bigwedge^{21}T_H\subset H^{42}(S^{21},\mathbb Q)$ has type $(21,21)$. At Picard number one it is the determinant of the transcendental Hodge structure. Its algebraicity has consequences for all self-powers when the transcendental Hodge endomorphism field is $\mathbb Q$ [1, 2].

**Theorem A (conditional reduction in every degree).** For each $d\geq 1$, a very general $(S,H)$ admits a Lefschetz pencil of curves in $|H|$ with transverse base locus, irreducible singular fibres and two marked smooth ends. Take the pencil and ends in the nonempty parameter open used for the relative construction below. Let $f:Y\to\mathbb P^1$ be its blowup, take the ends to $0,\infty$, and write $s_1,\ldots,s_N$ for the critical values and $s_*=\prod_i s_i$. Then $N=24+6d$. There is a smooth projective model $Z_k$ of the $k$-fold multiplication graph. On the smooth complementary fibre $X_u$ of $Z_{N-1}\to\mathbb P^1$ the projected middle cohomology has a distinguished subvariation

$$
K_u\simeq H^1(C_{s_*/u},\mathbb Q)(-(N-2)),\qquad
\dim X_u=2N-3,
$$

where $C_s=f^{-1}(s)$. In a family of pencils this identification uses the orientation line of $T_H$ after ordering the base points. If $K_u$ has geometric coniveau at least $N-2$ at geometric generic $u$ for such a pencil, then $\bigwedge^{21}T_H$ is spanned by an algebraic cycle on $S^{21}$. An algebraic cylinder inducing the indicated inclusion on the smooth locus also suffices.

If the hypothesis holds for very general $(S,H)$ of degree $2d$, the conclusion for $\bigwedge^{21}T_H$ specializes to every smooth primitively polarized K3 surface of that degree. On such a surface with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S)=\mathbb Q$, it implies the rational Hodge conjecture for every self-power of $S$.

**Theorem B (supported ramification cycle).** Let $a:C\to\mathbb P^1$ be a finite map of degree $b$ from a smooth connected projective complex curve of genus $g$, with simple ramification and unramified ends. Put $n=2g+2b-2=2m$, let $R$ be its ramification divisor, and write $v_R=\prod_{q\in R}a(q)$. For every bijection between the fibres over $0$ and $\infty$, the complete ordered norm fibre over $v_R$ carries a proper algebraic $m$-cycle. After slotwise trace-free projection and geometric symmetrization it pairs to $n!$ with the sum of the primitive local ruling tests transported from one calibrated ordering of $R$. The pairing detects the actual punctual determinant summand. Ramification values may coincide. For an explicit smooth sextic $F$ the projection of its smooth first polar $F_Z=0$ gives an instance with $n=20$ and degree $20!=2432902008176640000$. For $n=0$ use the empty product and the unit cycle at $1$.

The two assertions use the same complete-graph test. The curve construction supplies a cycle from linear conditions on endpoint residues. The K3 construction identifies a different cohomology group where a geometric support would produce a cycle. The remaining input in Theorem A is a codimension-$(N-2)$ support on the actual smooth complementary fibre.

## 2. Prior work

Gabber and Loeser construct the perverse quotient and Mellin transform for tori [3]. Katz describes convolution duality, tensor fibre functors, Euler dimension and the determinant of geometric convolution objects [4, Chapters 2–4 and 29]. Our use of those results identifies the punctual summand and its reciprocal adjugate. The cycle construction uses proper ordered graphs and the local intersection at the nodes of a complete fibre. Saito's pure direct-image and nearby-cycle formalism supplies the Hodge realization [5]. Vial's coniveau and niveau filtrations make precise the use of lower-dimensional sources by correspondences [6, §1]. Here the indicated cohomology is at the last possible odd level, so its supported source reduces to degree one. The specific reciprocal summand, its residue on the complete product, and the extraction on K3 powers are the additional steps.

Varesco's criterion includes the relative determinants for totally real endomorphism fields [2, Lemma 2.15 and Theorem 2.16 in the preprint numbering]. We use its rational-field case and reduce the absolute primitive determinant by a pencil. The curve-product criterion of [7, Theorem A] starts with an algebraic map from $T(S)$ to two curve factors. Our assumption concerns degree $2N-3$ on a fibre of a single norm map. The Scorza constructions in [8, 9] use pencils on genus-21 spin curves in degree 40. The former gives a non-torsion Prym section whose matched pushforward is parity invariant. The latter constructs a four-point incidence and a horizontal divisor but computes cancellation on the tested ordered pencil. The support in Theorem B uses endpoint residue matching on any simply ramified cover, and its intersection detects the curve determinant itself.

## 3. The pencil and its proper multiplication graphs

For $d\geq 2$ the primitive linear system on a Picard-rank-one K3 embeds it. The K3 linear-system criteria used here and the primitive period description are in [10, Chapters 2 and 6]. A general pencil is Lefschetz. Its members are integral because effective divisor classes are positive multiples of $H$. For $d=1$ take the double plane and project from a general point off its sextic branch. A simply ramified line projection with distinct branch values yields irreducible one-node curves of genus two. For example the sextic $Z^6+Y^5Z+X^6+Y^6$ with centre $[0:0:1]$ has affine equation $z^6+z+t^6+1=0$ in the coordinates $t=X/Y,\ z=Z/Y$. Its discriminant in $z$ has degree 30, is square-free and is nonzero at $t=0$. At infinity the polynomial is $z^6+1$ in the chart $X=1$. These tests imply smoothness and simple distinct tangencies, as in §8. The tested conditions are open. The inverse image of the centre consists of two transverse base points. In every degree the pencil genus is $g=d+1$, the base length is $r=2d$, and

$$
N=e(Y)-2e(C)=24+2d-2(2-2g)=24+6d.
$$

Write $G=\mathbb G_m$, $V=R^1 f_*\mathbb Q$ on the smooth locus, and $P=j_{!*}V[1]$ on $G$, of perverse weight two. For each $k$ choose a smooth projective permutation-equivariant toric refinement $B_k$ of the graph of multiplication on $(\mathbb P^1)^k$, identical on $G^k$. One construction starts by cutting the product fan by the kernel of the product character and takes a projective equivariant toric resolution [11]. Set $Z_k=B_k\times_{(\mathbb P^1)^k}Y^k$ and denote its output by $g_k$. On an input stratum with some coordinates at the ends the modification is toric in their normal coordinates, times the free torus. In the endpoint slots $Y$ is smooth over the base. In every free slot the base change is locally a product with the total surface, allowing a critical point of $f$. Thus $Z_k$ is smooth projective, of dimension $2k$. The same construction works for products of $C$ with $a$ in place of $Y$ with $f$, giving a smooth graph of dimension $k$.

Over the output $G$, the character vanishes on any refined cone for a stratum meeting the fibre. Over a genuine input boundary stratum it is nonzero on the span of the original cone. A cocharacter in that span fixes the original stratum and moves the output by a nonzero power. It acts on the refined stratum and its base change, with all source points fixed in the original input. This gives a submersion at each such point and, after an étale power cover, trivializes the stratum over $G$. The boundary direct images are lisse there by stratification and the compact-support triangles. In particular no boundary vanishing cycles occur over $G$. This argument uses smoothness of the ends and applies to both settings of the paper.

Let $F_Y$ denote the fibre divisor, $E_i$ the exceptional sections, and $\eta=E_1+\tfrac12 F_Y$. One has $f_*\eta=1$, $f_*\eta^2=0$ in rational Chow, $F_Y^2=\eta^2=0$, and $F_Y\eta=1$ in intersection numbers. Forgetting the $i$-th curve over the toric coordinates gives a flat proper map $\pi_i$. Use the slot correspondences

$$
p_{0,i}(\alpha)=\pi_i^*\pi_{i*}(\eta_i\alpha),\qquad
p_{2,i}(\alpha)=\eta_i\pi_i^*\pi_{i*}(\alpha),\qquad
p_{1,i}=1-p_{0,i}-p_{2,i}.
$$

The section and projection factorizations, flat base change and projection formula give orthogonal idempotents $p_{0,i},p_{2,i}$, transposes of each other. Distinct slot operators commute and permutations permute them. They act over the output, including by flat pullback and proper pushforward on its fibres. Let $e_k$ be the product of the $p_{1,i}$ followed by the geometric signed permutation projector. It is self-adjoint.

Invariant cycles and $H^1(Y)=0$ give $H^0(\mathbb P^1,j_*V)=0$, and duality gives $H^2(\mathbb P^1,j_*V)=0$, where now $j$ includes the smooth locus in the projective line. Irreducibility of the nodal fibres gives $R^1f_*\mathbb Q=j_*V$ and $R^2f_*\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q(-1)$. The parabolic group $A=H^1(\mathbb P^1,j_*V)$ identifies with $F_Y^\perp/\mathbb QF_Y$ via Leray, and the slot projector embeds it as the orthogonal complement of $F_Y,\eta$ in $H^2(Y)$. With $D_i=E_i-E_1$ and $h$ the pullback of $H$, $F_Y=h-\sum_i E_i$. Consequently

$$
A=T_H\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^r\mathbb Q(D_i-F_Y).
$$

The second summand has Gram matrix $-I-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}$ and rank $r-1$. Ordering the base points makes it a constant Tate system. The parabolic rank also equals $N-4g$ by the finite rank drops.

## 4. The determinant and its reciprocal adjugate

We work in the Euler-characteristic quotient of perverse sheaves on $G$ with middle convolution; the negligible objects have lisse constituents over $G$. The Euler dimension of $P$ is $N$, from the rank-one drops at the nodes. On $G^k$ the slot projectors select the external product of $P$. On the comparison between compact convolution and the proper graph, the boundary terms are negligible by §3, and compact and ordinary convolution agree in the quotient [3, 4]. Thus $e_k Rg_{k*}\mathbb Q_{Z_k}[2k]$ has non-negligible part $\bigwedge^{k,*}P$ in perverse degree zero. The symmetry on these shifted inputs is the geometric permutation: the signs from permuting the fibre $H^1$ and from the base shifts cancel. Proper purity and decomposition realize the simple non-negligible constituents as actual pure summands. Any other projected constituents are lisse on $G$, up to the perverse shift in their degree.

Here are the Hodge and weight comparisons used in this calculation. One may first take rational mixed Hodge module operations and then quotient the derived category by complexes with negligible perverse rational realization. This subcategory is closed under perverse truncations and is a convolution ideal by the rational convolution theorem. On the quotient heart convolution is exact, as checked on rational realization. The dual evaluation is restriction to the inverse-product fibre and Verdier trace. The ordinary pushforward gives the dual coevaluation, agreeing with compact pushforward in the quotient. The one-sided fibre functors, with $!$ at one end and $*$ at the other, have tensor comparison from the Künneth and product maps, as in [4, Chapter 2]. These maps lift by the same operations to mixed Hodge structures. Explicitly the $!$-zero functor takes degree zero of $R\Gamma(\mathbb P^1,Rj_{\infty*}j_{0!}P)$, with the maps extending across $0,\infty$ in that order. Both functors vanish on the negligible systems, including the trivial Kummer system. A finite-order Kummer twist is tensor-compatible by multiplicativity. For a character with nonrational values the calculation is made in its eigenspace over a cyclotomic coefficient field.

At the trivial twist the weight-graded groups for the $!$-zero functor are

$$
U_0=H^1(C_0),\qquad A,\qquad U_\infty(-1)=H^1(C_\infty)(-1).
$$

Indeed localization at the $!$ end inserts the invariants, projective middle extension gives the parabolic group, and localization at the $*$ end adds the coinvariants with $(-1)$. The vanishing of projective $H^0$ and $H^2$ gives these successive pieces without corrections. They have weights $1,2,3$. The reverse functor exchanges the ends. At a nontrivial finite twist the end groups vanish, and the parabolic group of the twist is pure weight two of rank $N$. The two functors therefore give determinant weight $2N$ at every finite twist, including the trivial one since the two endpoint ranks equal $2g$.

The Euler-dimension-one determinant has an actual simple pure representative of weight $2N$. If it had full support its variation would have weight $2N-1$. At either end choose a Jordan block of length $a>0$ and cancel its eigenvalue by a finite-order twist, using quasi-unipotence. With the $!$ functor at that end the invariant at the bottom of the monodromy weight filtration contributes weight $2N-a$. With $*$ there the top coinvariant with twist contributes $2N+a$. These groups survive the localization calculation: projective $H^0,H^2$ of a non-negligible simple full-support object and its twists vanish. Either end test contradicts the constant determinant weight. Hence the determinant is punctual.

In the input torus at a critical tuple the logarithm of the product is a sum of two-coordinate Morse blocks. The local lines are their Thom–Sebastiani tensors. Swapping identical blocks acts by $+1$, so the geometric alternating projector kills repeats. The outer slot projectors factor through lisse terms in the freed base coordinate and act by zero on vanishing cycles. At $k=N$ there is just one surviving orbit with $N!$ vertices at $s_*$. In ordered block orientations its generator has coefficients $\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)$. Thus the actual determinant on a fixed pencil is $D=\delta_{s_*}(-N)$. The one-sided weight filtration in a family computes its orientation as the product of the determinant characters of the three graded groups. The endpoints are symplectic and the Tate directions are ordered. Over the pencil parameter the line is therefore $\det T_H$, with the weight of $D$.

The curve polarization gives $\mathbb DP=P(2)$ in Hodge modules, and inversion gives the convolution dual. Alternating multiplication and the evaluation above construct the adjugate map in the Hodge-module quotient. Its rational realization is the ordinary tensor adjugate, so it is an isomorphism there. The actual non-negligible pure summands have the same Hom as in the quotient by semisimplicity. In particular, on a fixed oriented pencil,

$$
\bigwedge^{N-1,*}P
\simeq D*\operatorname{inv}^*P(2)
\simeq \iota_{s_*}^*P(-(N-2)),\qquad \iota_{s_*}(u)=s_*/u.
$$

This gives $K_u\subset H^{2N-3}(X_u)$ with Hodge types $(N-1,N-2)$ and $(N-2,N-1)$. The parameter orientation is retained in relative duality or trivialized on its double cover. No Chow projector onto $K$ is required. At $u_i=s_*/s_i$ its projected vanishing line is the signed orbit on the $(N-1)!$ tuples omitting $i$. Every coordinate of that orbit is nonzero. Repeated slots disappear even in case of product collisions. Proper vanishing-cycle base change and the lisseness of the other terms identify this line with the single curve Milnor direction under the adjugate.

## 5. Supported cohomology, cylinders and the complete-fibre pairing

### From coniveau to an algebraic cylinder

Put $p=N-2$ and $h_X=2p+1$. To interpret the geometric generic Betti comparison, take a finitely generated field of definition for the pencil and constructions and embed the parameter function field into $\mathbb C$ generically over it. All cycles are rational. Let $W$ be a support for $K$ of codimension at least $p$ in $X$ at the geometric generic point. The supported image in $H^{h_X}(X)$ is the sum of the Gysin images of $H^1(\widetilde W_j)(-p)$, where $\widetilde W_j$ resolve the projective components of codimension $p$. Indeed cohomology with supports is dual to Borel–Moore homology of $W$ in this degree, with the ambient Tate twist. Only the pure part can map to the smooth projective target. The weight filtration from a proper smooth hyperresolution gives that part from the resolutions of the components. The smaller strata have dimension at most $p$, hence homology zero in degree $2p+1$. Equivalently one can remove the singular and intersection loci, apply purity on the disjoint smooth strata, and take the weight-one part of their degree-one cohomology.

By polarizability, the inclusion of $H^1(C_{s_*/u})(-p)$ lifts through this sum as a rational Hodge map. Homomorphisms of rational weight-one Hodge structures of smooth projective varieties come from homomorphisms of their Picard or Albanese varieties up to isogeny. The Poincaré bundle, after choosing points over a finite extension if needed, gives the corresponding divisor on each $C_{s_*/u}\times\widetilde W_j$. Composing with the Gysin maps gives a codimension-$(p+1)$ cycle $\Gamma_u$ on $C_{s_*/u}\times X_u$. Its induced map $H^1(C_{s_*/u})\to H^{h_X}(X_u)(p)$ is exactly the adjugate inclusion. The degree shift before that twist is $2(p+1)-2=2p$.

The supports, resolutions, abelian homomorphisms and cycles descend to a finite extension of the parameter function field. Homomorphisms of abelian varieties over algebraically closed fields are invariant under algebraically closed extension in characteristic zero, so the Hodge comparison does not require transcendental coefficients in the cylinder. The flat cohomology map agrees with the inclusion on a spread open by smooth proper comparison. If working with a very general complex point instead, countably many relative Hilbert components for the finite cycle data yield a dominant component and a generically finite multisection. On a base where the intended map is flat and fixed, the trace of the cylinder divided by the degree of the field extension has that same cohomology map. One chooses the orientation on a fixed pencil and keeps the orientation cover for moving pencils. An ordinary trace across a sign change vanishes on that character. Equivalently, on a Galois cover with character $\epsilon$ one may project by $\frac1{|\mathcal G|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\epsilon(g)^{-1}g$ and retain the character part on the cover.

Over the input torus the reciprocal product of the single curve and the block is the corresponding open of the full $N$-slot fibre $g_N^{-1}(s_*)$. Close $\Gamma$ on a common proper graph of this identification and push to the full fibre to obtain a cycle $B$ of dimension $N$. The fibrewise dimension of the cylinder is $N-1$. The models agree near the interior simultaneous nodes. Flat trace, proper closure, and projection can therefore be used before computing their local tests.

### The local link and individual residues

At a simultaneous node in the distinguished curve and the complementary block, analytic Morse coordinates give the fibre equation $\sum z_j^2=\sum w_j^2$ in blocks of sizes $2$ and $2N-2$. Write $Q$ for the exceptional quadric $Q^{2N-2}$ in the blowup and $L^+,L^-$ for maximal planes in its two rulings. Set $\alpha_Q=[L^+]-[L^-]$ with orientation retained under transport. For a proper closure $\widetilde B$ on the blowup, the local residue is

$$
\rho_Q(\widetilde B)=\int_Q[\widetilde B]|_Q\,\alpha_Q .
$$

The link is the circle bundle of $\mathcal O_Q(-1)$. Its degree-$2N-2$ cohomology is $H^{2N-2}(Q)/h_QH^{2N-4}(Q)$ by the circle Gysin sequence, where $h_Q$ is the hyperplane class. The link is also the unit tangent sphere bundle of the real sphere $S^{2N-1}$: write $(z,iw)=U+iV$ with $U\perp V$ and $\lVert U\rVert=\lVert V\rVert$. The fibre sphere over fixed $U$ evaluates the residue up to the chosen orientation. The multiplier has absolute value one: a maximal plane of the form $U+iJU$ for an orthogonal complex structure $J$ meets the sphere once on its link, and the corresponding plane has primitive ruling degree of absolute value one. For the equality of intersection conventions, the real link test on the smooth blowup factors through the indicated quotient of $H^{2N-2}(Q)$. Cycle terms supported in a quadric restrict with a factor of $h_Q$ and have zero primitive test.

Fix $U=ae_1$ in the first block. In this link sphere the curve neck is $ae_1+iv e_2$ and the real block vanishing sphere has squared radius $a^2-v^2$. Take a loop $\gamma$ crossing the nonseparating curve neck once, and a block Milnor sphere $\delta_b$ at a chosen block node $b$. The product $\gamma\times\delta_b$ in a small smooth reciprocal fibre is homologous to the link sphere in the punctured complete fibre. On the neck, use the arc $\sqrt{t+v^2}\,e_1+iv e_2$ and interpolate the common parameter from a positive constant to $a^2-v^2$. On the complement contract the parameter to zero using a trivialization with collar, collapsing the block sphere. The collapsed ends avoid the simultaneous node. With matching orientations the residue is therefore

$$
\rho_{ib}=\int_{\gamma\times\delta_b}[\Gamma_u]
          =\langle\Gamma_{u*}(\operatorname{PD}\gamma),\delta_b\rangle .
$$

Local terms from the outer cohomology of the single curve vanish in this test. Apply the block sign and the slot projectors. The adjugate identifies the nontrivial local Picard–Lefschetz transformations at $s_i$ and $u_i$. Its action takes the single vanishing direction to the block vanishing direction with nonzero multiplier. The Picard–Lefschetz formula for a crossing loop gives a nonzero evaluation on the signed block orbit. Every coordinate of that orbit therefore has a nonzero individual projected residue, with its block sign.

### The full projected sum

We use a specialization fact for either proper graph. In a smooth total space of dimension $2q$ with isolated Morse points in a fibre, the connecting map from the node Milnor lines in degree $2q-1$ to degree $2q$ of the fibre has image the punctual perverse-degree-zero summand. This follows by applying the specialization triangle to the proper direct image and using its pure decomposition. A nonpunctual middle extension has zero degree-zero stalk and costalk, and its canonical map from nearby cycles to vanishing cycles is surjective. Shifted constituents in other perverse degrees have zero connecting image in this degree. On the node blowup the connecting map is Gysin from the primitive exceptional class. In a Morse ball the smoothing is the affine quadric and its compactification has the projective quadric at infinity. The circle Gysin sequence identifies the restriction of the reduced Milnor class to the link with the primitive class of that boundary quadric, with a Tate twist. Excision at the node identifies the connecting map with the link boundary into the fibre. On the blowup, that boundary is the Thom map of the exceptional normal line, giving $j_{Q*}\alpha_Q$ up to the common calibration. Pullback from the singular fibre is injective on this defect, by the exact sequence comparing the blowup with its exceptional quadrics, whose degree-$2q-1$ cohomology vanishes.

Before full alternation on the K3 graph, keep the first slot distinguished and alternate the other $N-1$. The resulting non-negligible middle product is $P*\bigwedge^{N-1,*}P$. Its punctual part at $s_*$ has multiplicity one: tensor adjunction computes its multiplicity space as $\operatorname{End}(P)=\mathbb Q$. The symplectic monodromy proving absolute irreducibility is given in §6. The full alternating summand has this same punctual line. The projected Milnor space at $s_*$ consists of the distinct-label tuples: if the block uses $N-1$ distinct labels, the product relation forces the first to be the missing label. Its connecting map has rank one and the full signed determinant vector maps nontrivially, since that vector is the vanishing line of the full exterior punctual summand. The functional obtained by pairing a proper $B$ with that connecting map is therefore proportional to the signed determinant functional. The individual calculation makes its scalar nonzero. Consequently the full projected pairing has degree $cN!$ for the convention with unit signed local tests, for one common $c\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Rescaling the cylinder normalizes the degree to $+N!$. This compares cycles on the complete fibre, even when their proper closures meet its boundary.

We also need an absolute lift. The punctual line is the middle costalk-to-stalk image of the proper graph at $s_*$. The costalk identifies with cohomology supported in the complete fibre, so this image is reached by the restriction of supported degree-$2N$ absolute cohomology. Over the pencil parameter space, after shrinking to local systems of constant rank, the image in absolute cohomology is a pure subvariation. Its map onto the punctual line splits by polarizability. We obtain a lift $\ell$ of orientation character $\det T_H$ and type $(N,N)$, with the slot projectors applied. The specialization comparison just proved expresses its restriction to the resolved fibre as a nonzero multiple of the signed primitive Gysin sum. If $i$ denotes the inclusion of the complete fibre, the projection formula gives $\int_{Z_N}[i_* B]\,\ell\ne0$.

## 6. Parameter monodromy and extraction on K3 powers

### Independent orthogonal and symplectic factors

For $d\geq2$, the discriminant of the smooth embedded K3 linear system is irreducible, with general point a one-node section. The roots of the nodal transvections lie in a single orbit up to sign because the smooth discriminant stratum is connected. These roots span by invariant cycles for a Lefschetz pencil. Their graph joining pairs with nonzero pairing is connected. Otherwise the spans of the components would be orthogonal, each preserved by the transvections, which generate the group by the normal generation of meridians. This contradicts the single orbit. The Lie algebra of the Zariski closure contains the squares of the roots in $\operatorname{Sym}^2 V\simeq\mathfrak{sp}(V)$. The bracket of two squares for a nonorthogonal pair gives their symmetric product. Along a path, the bracket of a known product $v_i v_j$ with $v_k^2$ gives a nonzero multiple of $v_i v_k$ plus a multiple of the edge product $v_jv_k$. Induction gives all symmetric products of the spanning roots. The group is therefore $\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}$. A general line in the complement of the discriminant, punctured at its intersections, surjects on the fundamental group of the smooth linear-system open by the hyperplane theorem for complements. Thus a general pencil has the same connected monodromy.

For $d=1$, projection of the branch sextic has six sheets. Its meridians give transpositions with connected edge graph, since the cover is connected and the meridians generate its monodromy. Modulo two, the genus-two vanishing roots are the corresponding two-element subsets in the even-subset space on the six labels, modulo the full set. This model is given by the Weierstrass-point differences in the two-torsion of the Jacobian. Edge pairs of a connected graph span the even subsets, and incident unequal edges have odd pairing. Hence the rational roots span, and their nonorthogonality graph is connected. The bracket proof gives $\operatorname{Sp}_4$ in this pencil, hence also in the smooth line-system open on this fixed double plane. Deleting further finitely many values or taking a finite base change preserves connected Zariski closure. In particular the adjugate local system is absolutely irreducible and stays so on a generically finite cover of the complementary base.

Over a fixed very general $S$, the open of good ordered pairs of endpoints is a nonempty open in the product of the two smooth linear-system opens. Inclusion induces a surjection on fundamental groups, by perturbing loops off the discarded complex subvariety. This gives the two independent symplectic endpoint groups acting trivially on $T_H$. Ordering the base points is finite étale and keeps connected Zariski closures. The family of good pencils dominates the polarized K3 moduli with connected general fibres before finite labelling. The period description gives an arithmetic orthogonal monodromy on $T_H$ up to finite index, with connected closure $\operatorname{SO}_{21}$ [10]. Therefore the joint connected group is $\operatorname{SO}_{21}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}$.

For the determinant character one also needs an element of the other orthogonal component acting trivially at the ends and on the ordered base points. In the K3 lattice take $e+df$ in one hyperbolic plane and the root $e'-f'$ in another. The generic period on the root divisor has precisely the primitive rank-two Picard lattice spanned by these classes. Take the polarization nef on the resolution and contract the root, the only orthogonal root up to sign. The nef linear system is base point free by the K3 linear-system criterion: an elliptic class with intersection one against $H$ is excluded by divisibility of all pairings with $H$ by $2d$. It descends to the one-node model. Two general endpoint sections avoid that node and meet transversely at smooth points. In a transverse polarized smoothing of the node the ends extend smoothly and the base points extend as disjoint analytic sections. A general choice of the smoothing and endpoints lies in the good pencil open on the punctured disc. The meridian on $T_H$ is the Picard–Lefschetz reflection, while its endpoint and marking actions are trivial. This argument takes place before adding any level structure that would remove the reflection. The orientation character can thus also be tested after transport to any generic point of the connected pencil parameter component. Together with the connected group, this gives the full orthogonal factor, independently of the two symplectic factors.

### Algebraic summand maps and transposition

Use a second proper model obtained by successively blowing up the strict transforms of the opposite-endpoint pair centres $s_i=0,\ s_j=\infty$. In the product fan each such step inserts the sum of the two opposite-sign rays and removes the corresponding pair cone. Subsequent refinements cannot restore a cone with both of those rays, so when the process ends the product is a morphism. The remaining rays introduced by these blowups have zero product character. A preceding pair sharing one fixed coordinate with a centre induces the blowup of a divisor there, which is the identity. A disjoint pair induces the corresponding pair blowup on the centre, and a pair with incompatible fixed values is disjoint. This is seen in the normal coordinate fan by taking the star of the centre cone. Thus the blowup formula on this model, including its iterated centres, uses algebraic summand maps from products of free $Y$ slots and actual $C_0,C_\infty$ slots, with Tate factors. The maps are pullbacks, exceptional hyperplane products and Gysin maps. The slot projectors commute with them by flat base change and projection formula, since the blowups are on the toric base and $\eta_i$ comes from $Y$. Their surviving absolute cohomology in a free slot is $A$ in degree two: the two discarded maps there are $x\mapsto F_Y(\eta x)$ and $x\mapsto\eta(F_Yx)$. In an endpoint slot the surviving cohomology is $U_0$ or $U_\infty$ in degree one, since $\eta$ restricts to a section point.

A smooth common toric refinement compares the equivariant and ordered models by proper pullback and pushforward and is unchanged at the contributing nodes. Transfer the supported lift to the ordered model and use the proper cylinder closure there. The restriction of the transferred lift has the same primitive local Gysin coefficients: the transfers are the identity in the node charts and commute with the link connecting map. The pairing with the donor is therefore still nonzero. Alternation need not act on the ordered blowup summands. Their algebraic summand maps are defined over the pencil parameter base, so each coordinate of $\ell$ transforms by $\det T_H$.

By the orthogonal tensor invariant theorem, the determinant-isotypic tensors in powers of $T_H$ are spanned by a volume tensor in 21 slots with pairings in the remaining slots, and by permutations of these tensors. For example tensoring with one volume reduces this to the orthogonal pairing theorem. Invariants on the endpoint spaces are generated by pairings in each factor by the symplectic tensor invariant theorem [12]. Crucially the reflection excludes tensors made only from pairings on $T_H$. The pairings and copairings are represented by projected diagonals. The $T_H$ projector on $S$ is the middle projector minus $H\times H/(2d)$, and the Tate basis on $Y$ is given by the ordered section differences above with its inverse Gram matrix. Hence, for any nonzero rational generator $\omega$ of $\bigwedge^{21}T_H$, the lift is a sum of $A_j(\omega)$ for rational algebraic correspondences $A_j$ from $S^{21}$ to the ordered model, with the indicated twists. The rational spanning statement follows by scalar extension from the rational pairings and volume.

For some $j$, the transpose $A_j^\dagger$ sends the proper donor to a class pairing nontrivially with $\omega$. The cup pairing on $\bigwedge^{21}T_H$ is nondegenerate. Projecting the transposed cycle onto $T_H$ in every slot and alternating gives the desired rational codimension-21 cycle on $S^{21}$. This argument uses the monodromy of the supported lift; it places no invariance assumption on the donor.

### Specialization and the endomorphism field

Use a smooth moduli chart with universal surface and the finite étale cover trivializing $\det T_H$. On a connected component fix a rational flat generator and apply the algebraic primitive and alternating projectors to the relative $21$-fold power. Rational cycles with prescribed coefficient lists are parametrized by countably many products of relative Hilbert schemes. On each connected family the projected class is flat. Work over a countable field of definition and discard the closures of the nondominant images. A cycle at a very general point then gives a dominating component. A generically finite multisection supplies a cycle over a finite extension of the oriented function field. Divide its class by the nonzero rational coefficient of the generator. The degree-normalized field trace on that oriented base preserves the class.

Through any target smooth polarized surface take a curve in a chart whose generic point lies in the spread open. Normalize in a finite extension if required, and localize at a point over the target. Closure over the resulting discrete valuation ring and Chow specialization of the cycle on the smooth proper family give the flat generator on the special fibre, by smooth proper comparison. The primitive determinant retains rank 21 even at a Picard jump. Write $T_H=T(S)\oplus L$ with $L=\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cap H^\perp$. Pair the last $\dim L$ slots with the alternating product of a divisor basis of $L$ and push to the remaining slots. The determinant of its intersection matrix is nonzero by the Hodge index theorem. Projecting to $T(S)$ and alternating gives $\det T(S)$, with the projector obtained by subtracting the entire Néron–Severi projector from the middle one.

For $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S)=\mathbb Q$, Zarhin's Hodge group and the special orthogonal invariant theorem generate every invariant on the transcendental factors by the pairing and determinant [1, 2, 12]. The remaining K3 cohomology and the pairing already have algebraic generators, giving the assertion about self-powers. A totally real field in general requires algebraic endomorphism actions and relative volume tensors for its separate embeddings. Their product is the absolute volume, and contraction of that volume in any two slots by a symmetric form vanishes. The specialization result supplies the absolute volume. The implications for higher endomorphism fields or mixed products require additional inputs.

## 7. A matched-residue cycle for curve covers

Assume $n=2m>0$ in Theorem B. Let $E_0,E_\infty$ denote the two unramified fibres, and put $L=K_C(E_0+E_\infty)$. The section $s_0=da/a$ has zero divisor $R$ in $L$. Since the residue map from $H^0(C,L)$ has image the sum-zero hyperplane and kernel $H^0(C,K_C)$, write its coordinates as $(a_i,-b_i)$ with $\sum a_i=\sum b_i$. For a matching $\pi:E_0\to E_\infty$, let $W_\pi$ be the inverse image of the subspace $b_{\pi(i)}=a_i$. It has dimension $g+b=m+1$. Thus $\mathbb P(W_\pi)$ is an $m$-plane in the Abel fibre $|L|\subset\operatorname{Sym}^n C$.

For a section $s$ without endpoint zeros, $v=s/s_0$ has paired equal unit values at the endpoints. Weil reciprocity gives

$$
\frac{\prod_{q\in Z_s}a(q)}{\prod_{q\in R}a(q)}
=\frac{\prod_{E_0}v}{\prod_{E_\infty}v}=1 .
$$

Take the full ordered pullback of the open of reduced interior divisors of $\mathbb P(W_\pi)$ and close it in the proper multiplication graph of §3 for $C$. Each component is taken with multiplicity one. The ordered cover can be disconnected. Over a reduced divisor it is étale, so each ordering of $R$ has exactly one smooth branch of dimension $m$ in the closure. This defines the required proper cycle on the complete fibre.

Near a point of $R$, choose $x$ with $s_0=(cx+O(x^2))\,dx$, $c\ne0$. The variation by $s$ moves the zero with velocity $-s(q)/(c\,dx)$. The Hessian of the product logarithm on the ordered product is nondegenerate with one block per slot. Substituting the velocities into its bilinear form yields $\sum_{q\in R}\operatorname{Res}_q(ss'/s_0)$. The residue theorem makes this $-\sum_i a_i a_i'+\sum_i b_i b_i'$. It vanishes on $W_\pi/\langle s_0\rangle$, which has dimension $m$. The tangent is therefore maximal isotropic. Its projectivization is the multiplicity-one $\mathbb P^{m-1}$ where the strict transform of the branch meets the exceptional $Q^{2m-2}$. At a reference ordering choose $L_0$ in this ruling and $L_1$ from the other. Use $\alpha=(-1)^{m-1}([L_0]-[L_1])$ for the calibration. Putting $j=m-1$, the ruling intersection matrix has diagonal $1$ and off-diagonal $0$ for $j$ even, and diagonal $0$ and off-diagonal $1$ for $j$ odd. Indeed the self-intersection follows from $c(N_{\mathbb P^j/Q})=(1+h)^{j+1}/(1+2h)$ and each row sums to $1$ since the sum of the two plane classes is $h^j$. Thus the local degree is $+1$, including $Q^0$. Under a change of matching by $\sigma\in S_b$, the intersection of the affine tangent planes has codimension $b-\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma)$ in either plane, because common residue vectors must be constant on the cycles. The component criterion for maximal isotropics gives the relative sign $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$.

On the graph let $\pi_i$ forget the lift to $C$ in slot $i$, leaving the corresponding toric coordinate. The operator $1-\pi_i^*\pi_{i*}/b$ is a self-adjoint relative algebraic projector, as $\pi_i$ is finite flat of degree $b$. These operators commute by finite flat base change. After forgetting a lift the output is submersive near an interior Morse tuple because the freed base coordinate has nonzero product derivative. Hence the trace summand has zero vanishing cycles there. The trace-free one-slot input is $J=j_{!*}(a_*\mathbb Q/\mathbb Q)[1]$ over the unramified open, with its indicated extension on $G$. Compared with the geometric action, permuting the shifted inputs contributes the permutation sign. The convolution exterior projector is therefore the geometric symmetrizer. A transposition fixing an equal-label tuple swaps one-coordinate Morse blocks and acts by $-1$, so the symmetrizer kills all repeated-label tuples. The distinct tuples form one orbit over $v_R$. Transport the reference ruling test geometrically around this orbit. In an ordered Morse-block orientation this transport introduces the permutation sign. The full ordered cycle is invariant, and pairs to $+1$ with every transported test. The trace-free projectors leave these local degrees unchanged. The sum pairs to $n!$.

The vanishing-to-fibre connecting map of §5 applies to this graph, with total dimension $n=2m$. Its primitive Gysin formula makes the computed degree a pairing with an actual class of the complete fibre. In particular the projected cycle detects the perverse-degree-zero punctual image. By the boundary comparison of §3 and the convolution quotient of §4, the non-negligible object here is $\bigwedge^{n,*}J$. Its Euler dimension is one because $\chi(G,J)=\deg R=n$: for a point with several simple ramification points the sum of their rank drops is their number. The detected punctual summand is thus the determinant, of Tate type $\delta_{v_R}(-m)$ by proper purity. The local degree is computed on a strict transform and equals the pairing with the proper pushforward on the fibre, by the Gysin comparison. This is a supported construction even if the unprojected graph has other singular tuples over $v_R$.

The residue linear spaces, ordered open and proper graph closures also work in a family after choosing the matching on a finite étale cover and shrinking for flatness. Independent relabelling of the two ends changes the orientation by the product of the two permutation signs. The determinant character agrees: its untwisted one-sided factors are the trace-free endpoint algebras and $H^1(C)$, with an additional Tate twist at the $*$ end. The middle factor has symplectic orientation. An ordinary trace through all matchings cancels for $b\geq2$. A fixed matching, or trace with the corresponding signs on the orientation cover, retains the degree.

## 8. The first polar of a sextic

Take

$$
F= Z^6+Y^4 Z^2+(X^5+Y^5)Z+X^6+Y^6,\qquad P=F_Z ,
$$

and project from $[0:0:1]$ in the coordinate $t=X/Y$. Both curves avoid the centre. In $Y=1$ the first polar has equation $6z^5+2z+t^5+1=0$, with discriminant

$$
432\bigl(9375t^{20}+37500t^{15}+56250t^{10}+37500t^5+13471\bigr).
$$

This has degree 20 and is square-free with nonzero constant. At $X=1,\ Y=0$ the polynomial is $6z^5+1$, square-free. For a family of monic polynomials a simple discriminant root has exactly one double root, nonzero second derivative there, and nonzero parameter derivative. Indeed higher multiplicity or multiple double roots lie in the singular discriminant, while transversality to its smooth double-root stratum is the nonvanishing of the parameter derivative. This proves smoothness of $P$ and simple distinct interior ramification. Smoothness of $F$ follows also from the certified three projective Jacobian ideals modulo $1009$. A projective singular locus in characteristic zero would have a point in a special fibre by properness. The same argument using monic projection and the discriminant verifies the sextic pencil fixture in §3.

Here $g=6$, $b=5$, $m=10$. Let $h=P_Z$. A quartic $B$ gives the section

$$
s_B=(B/h)\,d\log t\ \in H^0\bigl(P,K_P(E_0+E_\infty)\bigr).
$$

The zeros of $d\log t$ cancel the simple poles along $h=0$, with no other interior poles. Since $K_P=\mathcal O_P(2)$ and $E_0+E_\infty$ is a divisor of $\mathcal O_P(2)$, this identifies quartics with all sections of $L$. The four independent matching equations compare $B/h$ at the five endpoint pairs, where it is regular. The projective solution space has dimension ten in $|L|=\mathbb P^{14}$. The Abel map on $\operatorname{Sym}^{20}P$ is smooth since $20>2g-2$, so there are six additional codimensions to reach that Abel fibre. The cycle of §7 has total codimension ten in the ordered product and calibrated degree $20!$ on the complete norm fibre. In this fixture its product is exactly $13471/9375$. For example the quartic $B=h+XYZ^2$ satisfies all matching equations because its perturbation vanishes at both ends. The two exact degree-20 eliminants of $h$ and $B$ against $P$ have the same constant-to-leading-coefficient ratio and different coefficients. The latter is square-free and gives a noncentral matched divisor.

For comparison with the joint sextic-polar eliminant, let $P$ now vary near this first polar, put $h=P_Z$, and impose $P=0,\ Fh-XY A^2=0$ with a quartic $A$. The degree-50 projection eliminant has coefficient ratio equal to the ratio of the homogeneous resultants on $X=0$ and $Y=0$. In the affine charts with nonzero leading coefficients at the centre this follows by evaluating the homogenized eliminant at the two ends. The two end resultants are independent of $A$ and factor as the end resultants for $F$ and $h$. On the open where both reduced sets are disjoint at $A=0$, and on a marking preserving their cardinalities in the deformation, write the two product coordinates as $N_{30},N_{20}$. Then

$$
N_{30}N_{20}=N_F(P)v_R(P),\qquad N_F(P)=\prod_{P\cap V(F)} t .
$$

In particular imposing the matched constraint on the selected residual divisor yields $N_{20}=v_R(P)$ on that open. This gives a residual norm constraint on the actual carrier. Its parameter graph, finite over the open in the projective quintics and affine quartics, has dimension at most $20+15=35$. Near a marked central point a residual root moves to second order in $A$ at fixed $P$, because $F$ is a unit there and $h$ has simple zeros. The projected leading equation is the matched quartic evaluation of the displacement $-XYA^2/F$ along $R$, so a transverse product of the residual ruling and a physical ruling does not follow from the norm identity. A codimension-ten refined residual pullback on a source of dimension at most 35 gives a cycle of dimension at most 25, short of the 30 needed on the physical determinant fibre. This dimension bound concerns this finite ordered carrier and the residual contraction. The $20!$ pairing is the residual curve pairing itself.

## 9. Constructive target and reproducibility

A direct next step for Theorem A is to exhibit a codimension-$(N-2)$ family of proper supports whose degree-one Gysin map has a nonzero projected Milnor coefficient at one of the complementary values $u_i$. With a spread, the projected image is a pure subvariation. Its other constituents are lisse across the finite critical values, while the adjugate has nontrivial transvection and remains irreducible after finite base change. A nonzero projected coefficient therefore forces the full adjugate into the Gysin image. For $d=1$ this asks for codimension 28 on the actual 29-slot complementary fibre of dimension 57. A fixed marking that avoids the pencil nodes has zero local coefficient by restriction to their Milnor neighbourhoods.

There is a local algebraic model of the required coefficient. In $\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}x_jy_j=t$ set $x_2=\cdots=x_{N-1}=0$. The real vanishing sphere $y_j=\bar x_j$ at $t>0$ meets this support transversely in the circle in $x_1$, with degree-one class evaluating to $1$. The cylinder using the diagonal $x_0=x_1,\ y_0=y_1$ in a single curve $x_0y_0=t$ closes to a maximal plane in the total node. The construction of a proper global support with a surviving coefficient after projection is open.
