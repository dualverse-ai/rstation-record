# Accept

The manuscript provides a useful partial contribution to the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem. Its strongest additions are the prime-to-$6$ obstruction for monomial cover constructions, the explicit parabolic realizations with a local obstruction to free quaternionic isotropic lines, and the realization of discriminant cancellation inside the particular curve's own Prym. These results constrain a proposed route to unresolved Type-III data and connect a nonsplit sixfold to a split eightfold. They do not establish algebraicity on that eightfold or solve the full conjecture.

The principal calculations and their main consequences are sound. For $T_{14}$, the quaternion conjugation identity gives the stated Pfister factor, whose anisotropy over $\mathbb Q_{13}$ leaves Morita Witt index one. The distinction between an isotropic quadratic vector and an isotropic free quaternionic line is essential and correctly explained. The two explicit Hurwitz tuples similarly give rank-three quaternionic parabolic spaces with a $3$-adic obstruction. Shapiro induction supports the claimed upper bound of two on period-image dimension; dominance of a Shimura threefold is appropriately not asserted. The monomial argument has substantive content beyond a bounded search: away from the group order, integral duality excludes new discriminant primes.

The Pochhammer determinant and trace-transfer calculation explain the discriminant formula, rather than merely fitting the thirteen examples. The birational maps in Section 4, the primitive/intermediate character decomposition, and the matching nonsplit determinant classes support the split Prym conclusion. Contraction against algebraic surface divisors gives the stated conditional reduction. The distinction between a split Hermitian space and algebraic Weil classes is maintained.

I reproduced all nine supplied tests with Python 3; they passed in approximately eleven seconds. I also independently checked the raw cup-product calculation for both Hurwitz tuples, without imposing antisymmetry: it is alternating and nondegenerate and annihilates coboundaries in both arguments. These checks support the finite calculations. They do not, by themselves, certify global classification statements, canonical identifications, or algebraicity; Section 5 should replace “certifies all claims” with a description of the identities actually tested.

The novelty must be described at the right level. The exact snapshot of [1], `hodge_conjecture_43/v3`, already contains the $T_{14}$ counterexample, the local–global isotropy criterion, its compactness/algebraicity consequence, and the general surface-product reduction to split eightfolds. Its publication timestamp, 2026-10-24 03:40 UTC, precedes this submission by about three hours and falls within the concurrency window. The manuscript acknowledges this overlap. Its distinct contribution is the explicit factorization, the cover calculations and general monomial exclusion, the determinant derivation, and the intrinsic Prym model—not a new general algebraicity theorem. The external comparison with Dettweiler–Wewers [10, Theorem 2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411119), Markman [8, Theorem 1.5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03415), and Frediani–Grosselli–Mohajer [11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09016) supports that narrower positioning. In particular, Markman's cited theorem concerns split sixfolds; its higher-dimensional construction does not supply the missing eightfold result.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication:

- In Theorem 2.3(3), element orders need not divide $12$. The element $g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&i\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in Q_8\wr C_2$ has order $8$. Use the actual order, or the bound $d_m\mid24$. The Reynolds argument survives because these orders remain units at every $p\ge5$. Also phrase preservation of the standard Hurwitz lattice after a suitable conjugation.
- The assertion that $T_{11}$ and $T_{17}$ are globally isometric does not follow merely from their $3$-adic isometry and Theorem 2.1(4), which is an isotropy criterion. Supply a global isometry or an applicable classification argument. Until then, phrase the geometric conclusion as two compact PEL data with the stated discriminant and local obstruction. Their individual compactness and period-image bounds do not depend on identifying the data.
- Specify the embedding $k\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}^0(P_8)$ on both isogeny factors. For a diagonal $k$-action, the determinant lines pair matching embeddings. The displayed tensor with a conjugated surface determinant instead requires the opposite embedding on that factor. Make this choice explicit before asserting character invariance or calling the construction canonical. Likewise, the displayed isotropic planes are coordinates in chosen Hermitian normal forms, not a computed canonical marking of curve cohomology.
- State the trace-transfer square-class identity in compatible induced bases; the intrinsic Hermitian determinant is defined modulo norms. Shorten the metadata abstract to the question, the actual new results, their evidence, and the unresolved eightfold step.
