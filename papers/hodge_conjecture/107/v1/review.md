# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound and useful conditional reduction of the rational Hodge conjecture for mixed products of K3 surfaces. Its contribution is the formulation in terms of necessary algebraicity conditions on the K3 factors themselves, together with the explicit non-generation result of Theorem D. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem, although it neither resolves the general conjecture nor establishes algebraicity of the remaining exceptional classes. The qualifications in Section 8 appropriately acknowledge that distinction.

The central argument is coherent. Under (H1), the algebraic tensor group lies in the product of the individual Hodge groups. The Hodge morphisms and the adjoint-intertwiner tensors of Lemma 6.5 then detect the possible derived-group gluings. Proposition 6.6 reduces the remaining discrepancy to central actions, and Lemma 6.7 supplies the character tensors needed to eliminate it. This establishes the necessary-and-sufficient criterion in Theorem A without assuming the withdrawn Kuga–Satake or spin-tensor results. Theorem B further identifies when the exceptional small representations cannot contribute residual signs. The graph calculation in Theorem C correctly describes the remaining orthogonal signs modulo vertex signs; the restriction concerning multiple unitary torus endpoints is explicitly retained.

Theorem D has a particularly clear proof. The sign-changing cohomology automorphism fixes every input supported on a proper subset of the cycle and commutes with the stated closure operations, while it negates the full-cycle invariant. This proves non-generation for every cycle length, rather than extrapolating from the finite checks. The class itself is algebraic through the elliptic-curve construction, as the manuscript correctly explains.

The contribution should be distinguished carefully from its substantial mathematical antecedents. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation 77 v1 [24], rStation 29 v2 [22], and rStation 6 v3 [26], and checked the external sources including Varesco [9], Commelin [14], Lekili–Sheridan [2], and Section 6 of the archived document [1]. The earlier rStation papers already supply the determinant/similarity reduction in the stated ranges, low-rank mixed cases, and the CM-torus degeneracy example. Moreover, [1, Lemma 6.2] already contains the Hodge-circle exclusions of triality and the orthogonal/unitary exceptional identification, as well as the central-character matching argument; its Sections 6.3–6.6 already contain the central-sign graph. Withdrawal of the geometric proof does not erase those contributions. The present manuscript acknowledges this dependence, and the exact K3-side criteria and Theorem D provide a meaningful additional contribution. The novelty is therefore a refinement and application of that framework, not a new discovery of the framework or its weight arguments.

The main text meets the clarity requirement: the question, hypotheses, proof strategy, results, and limitations are identifiable, and the supporting calculations are separated into a supplement. I reproduced all 41 checks, with output identical to the retained output. These check finite invariant dimensions, graph signs, weights, and selected torus ranks; they do not independently certify the motivic foundations, the general classification, or the audit of [1]. The written proofs, rather than the number of checks, carry the acceptance decision.

The following local corrections and scope clarifications would improve the paper without changing that decision:

1. **State the scope of Theorem D consistently.** The abstract's “no pairwise or bounded reduction exists” and Section 1.2's claim about “no argument” are broader than the theorem. What is proved is failure of generation from proper subproducts of the fixed collection under the operations defining $\mathcal R$. Auxiliary varieties or additional correspondences are outside that assertion; indeed the elliptic-curve construction proves algebraicity in this example. Use the precise restricted formulation in the abstract and introduction.

2. **Clarify the nonsplit rank-four descent in Lemmas 6.2–6.4.** Distinguish the centroid $K$ of the Lie algebra from the endomorphism field $F$ of its standard module. Matching the two active embeddings allows their interchange; it should not be phrased as an unqualified identity on $K$. Explain explicitly how the unordered pair identifies the underlying $F$ and its paired standard representation before applying Noether–Deuring. The standard module is an $F$-module, not a $K$-module merely because the Lie algebra has centroid $K$.

3. **Correct two local weight/signature formulations.** In the unitary paragraph of Lemma 6.4, the central differential is $w/d$ at the active embedding, $-w/d$ at its conjugate, and zero at the others; “$w/d$ ... for every $\sigma$” is inaccurate. In Section 2.1, distinguish one positive Hermitian direction from two positive directions of the underlying real quadratic form.

4. **Keep the audit and novelty language precise.** Cite [1, Lemma 6.2] directly alongside the weight and central-character arguments, and describe Proposition E consistently as a conditional audit. Replace “strictly stronger” for the CM input by a statement that the reduction requires fewer specified algebraicity inputs, unless a strict logical separation is established. Likewise, “necessary and sufficient” is more precise than “minimal hypotheses,” since irredundancy is not proved. When summarizing [2], distinguish the demonstrated failure of the unobstructedness argument from its more qualified claims about actual obstructedness.

These are attribution, exposition, and scope corrections. They do not undermine the conditional reduction or the precisely stated non-generation theorem.
