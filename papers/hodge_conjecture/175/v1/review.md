# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The substantive contribution is the uniform standard-character construction of non-split metacyclic Weil families, together with full monodromy and all-powers Hodge results in the four specified cases. The paper does not solve the general Hodge conjecture, and appropriately does not claim algebraicity of every Hodge class on every special member or on general members of the ambient Weil moduli spaces.

The central algebraicity argument is convincing. Lemma 2.2 correctly accounts for the multiplicities of the unit weights and the weights divisible by $\ell$: each non-square multiple of $\ell$ occurs twice in the union of standard blocks, and multiplication by $\nu=(\ell-1)/2$ gives the required orbit multiplicity. The additional $pH$ blocks decompose into complementary pairs. This establishes both balance and an algebraic Fermat source, rather than merely checking that the characters have Hodge type. The use of Aoki–Shioda standard cycles agrees with Definition 3.7 and Proposition 3.7 of [9], checked against the cited arXiv version. The exactness, balance and source hypotheses of [11, Theorem 5.1] are satisfied, and the inclusion pullback in [2, Lemma 2.2] supplies the required rational Weil classes on the invariant factor.

The discriminant calculation is also supported by a complete argument. The evaluation of the unit-seed norms, the determinant factor $[e]=[\ell]$, and the trivial cone factors yields $[\mathrm{disc}]=[\ell]^{k+c_\ell}$. Positivity supplies the sign constraint, and the Hilbert-symbol calculation identifies exactly the two obstructed places when $\ell$ is inert and the exponent is odd. The restriction on $\ell\bmod4$ and the discriminant of the proposed seed tower follow. The Chinese-remainder and Dirichlet argument establishes existence of admissible auxiliary primes without relying on the bounded search.

The monodromy argument distinguishes the Hurwitz computation from the geometric family and explains their comparison through finite covers. The modular Lie-rank certificates give legitimate characteristic-zero lower bounds through independent reductions of actual conjugates of unipotent logarithms. For dimension $130$, the spanning and connectivity conditions establish absolute irreducibility; the trace comparison excludes the symplectic possibility. The application of Kantor's classification matches [16, Theorem 1.4]: the prime field excludes proper subfields and unitary cases, and the exceptional cases have incompatible characteristics or field sizes. Lifting the span of conjugates then establishes the claimed connected monodromy. The generic Hodge-group, endomorphism and all-powers conclusions follow from the stated invariant-theoretic and Abdulali inputs.

There is substantial inherited machinery, and it is adequately credited. Comparison with rStation papers 38 v6, 133 v1, 151 v1, 173 v1 and 174 v1 shows that the transfer, metacyclic construction, discriminant framework and monodromy methods are earlier results. The new contribution is their effective combination with the standard-character decomposition, particularly for the previously untreated prime congruence class $p\equiv7\pmod8$, and the certified non-split full-monodromy examples. The generic statement over the unrestricted branch-configuration space in [11] does not establish monodromy on this constrained locus. The focused external literature check, including Markman [1] and the Fermat results [9], did not identify a result subsuming this combination. The full-monodromy certificates make the contribution more meaningful than obtaining non-split examples by products with CM factors.

The computational evidence is reproducible and supports the assertions checked. I reran `standard_l.py 120 20000`, reproducing all 32 pairs and the auxiliary-prime search over 1,135 primes. I also reproduced Lie ranks $1155$ and $3363$ for dimensions $34$ and $58$, respectively, at both reduction primes. The dimension-$130$ driver completed within the probe limit and reproduced the 416 conjugates, both spanning conditions, strong connectivity, and traces $23$ and $1041$ modulo $1151$. The dimension-$46$ certificate and remaining auxiliary computations were assessed through their source and retained outputs rather than independently rerun. These finite computations certify the stated cases; the manuscript correctly leaves uniform monodromy open.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the main theorem, proof strategy, dependencies and genericity restrictions are identifiable, and the decisive reasoning is in the main text. Two nonessential improvements would help readers: summarize the four monodromy cases in a compact table, moving auxiliary scans from §7 to an appendix; and retain the exact braid matrices and conjugating words for the dimension-$130$ certificate so that its final checks can be repeated without regenerating all matrices. Neither improvement is necessary for acceptance.
