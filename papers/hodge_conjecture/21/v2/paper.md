# Reflection quotients, nonlinear period jets and Hodge classes on powers of symmetric threefolds

## 1. Contribution and scope

Let
$$
f_{B,C}(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)
=\sum_{i=0}^3x_i^6+
 B\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^4x_j^2+
 C\sum_{i<j<k}x_i^2x_j^2x_k^2
$$
and let $U\subset\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}$ be the locus on which
$V_{B,C}=\{f_{B,C}=0\}\subset\mathbb P^3$ is smooth. Set
$$
Y_{B,C}=\{f_{B,C}+x_4^6=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4.
$$
Smoothness of $Y_{B,C}$ is equivalent to $(B,C)\in U$. The published
decomposition [1, version 1, Theorem 3] gives algebraic rational group projectors
$$
H^3(Y_{B,C},\mathbb Q)=M_{\mathrm{CY}}\oplus L,\qquad
\dim M_{\mathrm{CY}}=66,\quad \dim L=454.
$$
The summand $M_{\mathrm{CY}}$ has Hodge numbers $(5,28,28,5)$; $L$ has
$(0,227,227,0)$. Version 1 of [1] proves algebraicity of the very-general CY
self-correspondences and of the associated surface-square Hodge classes,
and left geometric support of $L$ open. Version 2 of [1], published after
version 1 of the present paper, also proves this support and the same
cyclic-sextic square and sixfold consequences, citing our result and using
an alternative forty-eight-pair coverage certificate. These cyclic-sextic
conclusions are retained here from our version 1; they are not new claims
of the present revision. The expanded quintic families and the distinct
five-coordinate signed-permutation sextics are the new extensions.

**Theorem 1.** For every $(B,C)\in U$, the summand $L$ is generated by
algebraic correspondences from smooth projective curves:
$$
\bigoplus_j H^1(C_j,\mathbb Q)(-1)\longrightarrow L
$$
is surjective for finitely many such correspondences. Its deck-order
$2,3,6$ parts have ranks $104,198,152$, respectively. For any two smooth
members, all rational Hodge classes in $L\otimes L'$ are algebraic.

The proof supplies twenty-eight explicit finite quotients by pairs of
commuting reflections, together with one classical order-three quotient.
The reflection quotients are weighted sextic Fano threefolds, not merely
formal weight-one realizations of $L$.

**Corollary 2.** At very general $(B,C)\in U$, the degree-three generalized
Hodge conjecture holds for $Y_{B,C}$. The rational Hodge conjecture holds
for $Y_{B,C}^2$, for the square of
$$
W_{B,C}=\{z^3+f_{B,C}=0\}\subset\mathbb P(1,1,1,1,2),
$$
and for the split sextic sixfold
$$
X_{B,C}=\{f_{B,C}(x)-f_{B,C}(y)=0\}\subset\mathbb P^7.
$$

Corollary 2 combines Theorem 1 with the very-general results of [1]; those
period and surface arguments are not claimed here. Theorem 1 is uniform
on $U$, whereas Corollary 2 is not asserted at all special members.
The consequences for this cyclic sextic family do not include arbitrary
higher powers or arbitrary mixed CY correspondences. The distinct full
signed-permutation sextic family is treated in Theorem14 below. The general rational Hodge conjecture
remains open.

There is a second application, for quintics, where single reflections
already give Fano quotients. Write $m_\lambda(x)$ for the sum of the
distinct monomials obtained by permuting the three entries of $\lambda$.
For $t=(b_1,b_2,b_3,a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)$ put
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_t={}&x_0^5+x_1^5+x_2^5+u^5+v^5
+b_1m_{320}+b_2m_{311}+b_3m_{221}\\
&+uv(a_1m_{300}+a_2m_{210}+a_3m_{111})
+a_4(uv)^2m_{100},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
and let $T$ be its smooth locus in $\mathbb A^7$.

**Theorem 3.** The smooth quintics $Z_t=\{F_t=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4$
have rational algebraic projectors
$$
H^3(Z_t,\mathbb Q)=C_t\oplus L_t,
\qquad h(C_t)=(1,7,7,1),\quad h(L_t)=(0,94,94,0).
$$
The rank-$188$ summand $L_t$ is curve-generated for every $t\in T$.
For very general $t$, $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q\text{-}\mathrm{Hdg}}(C_t)=\mathbb Q$, the degree-three generalized Hodge conjecture holds for
$Z_t$, and the full rational Hodge conjecture holds for $Z_t^2$.
It also holds for $Z_t\times Z_{t'}$ at very general independently varying
parameters $(t,t')\in T^2$.
More strongly, full rational HC holds on every power of a very general
$Z_t$ and on all mixed powers of a very general independently varying
finite tuple. The residual component $C$ has monodromy
$\operatorname{Sp}_{16}$; Section 8.2 proves the independent symplectic
monodromy of the level-one multiplicity spaces as well. Corollary 11
extends these generic conclusions to the full ten-dimensional invariant
coefficient space (10). They concern very general quintics, not arbitrary
special members or higher powers of the cyclic sextic family.

The uniform support statement extends to every smooth quintic invariant
under the group $\mathfrak S_3\times D_5$ defined in Section 7. In
particular it applies to the three-parameter separated family of [2],
whose very-general square theorem is already known. Our uniform support
result and the four added mixed-term deformations are the additions;
we do not claim its existing square theorem again. The new generic
endomorphism proof uses a cyclic holomorphic period and an exact
Jacobian-ring certificate, separately from the generic results of [1,2].


The same reflection principle also leads to a full-power theorem for
quintics invariant under all coordinate permutations. Theorem 12 proves
uniform curve support for their rank-$192$ sign-complement and identifies
the connected monodromy on the five primitive multiplicity spaces as
$$
\operatorname{Sp}_{12}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{16}\times
\operatorname{Sp}_{12}\times\operatorname{Sp}_8\times\operatorname{Sp}_4.
$$
The first factor is the sign component of profile $(1,5,5,1)$. This yields
full rational HC for every power of a very general fully symmetric
quintic and all mixed powers of independent such members. Section 9
supplies the cyclicity, actual Higgs matrices and four complete nodal
orbits needed for this second quintic family.

Theorem 13 then treats every coordinate-permutation subgroup generated
by transpositions, including the full coefficient spaces for
$\mathfrak S_4$, $\mathfrak S_3\times\mathfrak S_2$,
$\mathfrak S_3$, $\mathfrak S_2^2$ and $\mathfrak S_2$.
The argument specializes generic endomorphisms to the known symmetric
group-algebra image and splits the existing node sets into independently
smoothable subgroup orbits. This proves full generic-power HC for all
these reflection families with one additional finite verifier.

Theorem14 treats a distinct two-parameter sextic family invariant under
signed permutations of all five coordinates. Forty Fano reflection
quotients give uniform rank470 curve support. Full pole-reduced
Griffiths derivatives make its rank10 residual multiplicity cyclic even
though the first-order image has rank only2. Third-order flat period
graphs give scalar endomorphisms and zero cross Hom on all nine
level-one primitive blocks. Complete16-,20- and40-node degenerations
and classical low-dimensional Hodge groups yield the full product
$\operatorname{Sp}_2^4\times\operatorname{Sp}_4^3\times\operatorname{Sp}_8\times\operatorname{Sp}_{10}^2$,
and hence rational HC on every generic power and independently mixed
tuple of these sextics.

## 2. Classical inputs and the new application

Rossi [3, Theorem 3.4 and Section 4] studies sextic threefolds invariant
under coordinate actions of order three. Their quotients are Fano sextic
double solids, and their invariant cohomology is generated by curves.
His Theorem 4.4 proves the generalized Hodge conjecture at very general
points of the corresponding directional and intersection families. For
one coordinate order-three action, the supported rank is $104$.

The present two-parameter family lies in such a directional family but
is a special subfamily. Its extra symmetry gives additional level-one
cohomology, so Rossi's generic maximality statement does not settle the
rank-$454$ summand here. We use his classical $104$-dimensional source and
construct quotient sources covering the additional $350$ dimensions.
The new input is the coverage by coordinate-sign and transposition pairs.
The finite-group and quotient-support principles themselves are classical.

For the singular quotient models we use rational connectedness of log
$\mathbb Q$-Fano varieties [4, Theorem 1 and its proof via Proposition 1].
Only uniruledness of a smooth resolution is needed. The quotient-support
argument, including its divisor-supported trace error, is included in
[5, Proposition B and Lemma 5] and recalled below. The cyclic-product
transfer used in Corollary 2 is the Katsura--Shioda construction [6] in
the form established and used in [1].

Pearlstein--Peters [7, Proposition 3 and Section 3] give a more recent,
closely related decomposition for even-degree weighted Calabi--Yau
threefolds with a cyclic-coordinate action: proper cyclic quotients are
Fano, yielding GHC for the associated nonprimitive components. Their
quotient mechanism is an input and precedent, rather than a new method
claimed here. The quintic application below combines several reflection
sources, isolates their common determinant component, and handles its
seven-parameter variation. The half-twist construction discussed by
van Geemen--Izadi [8, Section 4.3] for cyclic quintic surfaces is distinct
and is not used as an algebraic realization theorem here.

## 3. Actual Fano quotients

Write $r_i$ for the sign change of $x_i$, for $0\le i\le4$, and $t_{ij}$
for the transposition of $x_i,x_j$, where $i,j<4$. All preserve $Y$.
Use the following twenty-eight subgroups:
$$
H_{ij}=\langle r_i,r_j\rangle\quad(0\le i<j\le4),
$$
and
$$
K_{k;ij}=\langle r_k,t_{ij}\rangle
\quad(0\le i<j<4,\quad k\in\{0,\ldots,4\}\setminus\{i,j\}).
$$
There are ten of the first kind and eighteen of the second.

**Lemma 4.** Each group above is an effective Klein four group, and its
quotient is a normal, well-formed, quasismooth degree-six hypersurface
in $\mathbb P(1,1,1,2,2)$, with ample anticanonical divisor and quotient
singularities. A smooth projective resolution is uniruled.

*Proof.* For $H_{ij}$, square the two indicated coordinates. For
$K_{k;ij}$, use
$$
a=x_i+x_j,\qquad b=x_i-x_j,
$$
together with the other three coordinates. The two generators independently
negate $b,x_k$. The invariant polynomial ring therefore has three
generators of degree one and two of degree two. Since the sextic equation
is invariant, exactness of invariants in characteristic zero identifies
its quotient ring with this polynomial invariant ring modulo that equation.
This is the actual finite quotient, not a proposed birational analogy.

A singular point of the weighted affine cone away from the origin would,
by the chain rule at any preimage under the coordinate-squaring map, give
a singular point of the original affine cone away from the origin.
Thus the weighted hypersurface is quasismooth. The ambient weights are
well-formed, and its singular stratum has dimension one, hence has
intersection of codimension at least two in our threefold. The hypersurface
is well-formed.

For completeness, the canonical class can be computed directly on the
finite quotient $q:Y\to Q$. The two generators fix distinct hyperplane
sections $D_1,D_2$ pointwise, with ramification index two. Their product
has positive and negative linear eigenspaces of dimensions three and two.
The latter give projective fixed spaces of dimensions two and one. A
smooth sextic threefold cannot contain a plane: weak Lefschetz gives
$\operatorname{Pic}(Y)=\mathbb Z h$, whereas a plane divisor would have
intersection $1$ with $h^2$, not a multiple of $\int_Yh^3=6$.
Consequently the product has no divisorial fixed locus on $Y$.
The isolated fixed point of each generator contributes no divisor either.
The ramification divisor is exactly $D_1+D_2$, and
$$
q^*K_Q=K_Y-D_1-D_2=h-2h=-h.
\tag{2}
$$
Finite pullback detects ampleness, so $-K_Q$ is ample. Equivalently,
well-formed quasismooth weighted adjunction gives
$\omega_Q=\mathcal O_Q(6-7)$ as a reflexive sheaf. The quotient of smooth
$Y$ has quotient singularities and is klt. The theorem and proof in [4]
give rational connectedness of a smooth resolution, hence uniruledness.
$\square$

Let $\delta$ act by $x_4\mapsto\zeta_6^{-1}x_4$. There is one more source:
$$
Y/\langle\delta^2\rangle
=\{u^2+f_{B,C}=0\}\subset\mathbb P(1,1,1,1,3),\qquad u=x_4^3.
\tag{3}
$$
It is a smooth Fano sextic double solid, since $f_{B,C}=0$ is smooth.
Its invariant third cohomology is precisely the deck-order-two summand,
of rank $104$. The deck-trivial part is zero, since the full cyclic
quotient is $\mathbb P^3$. Formula (3) is the classical source of [3].

## 4. From quotient images to curves

We recall the support statement carefully because resolution errors matter.

**Lemma 5.** If a smooth projective threefold $T$ is uniruled, then
$H^3(T)$ is generated by curves. If a finite effective group $G$ acts on
a smooth projective threefold $Y$ and a smooth model of $Y/G$ is uniruled,
then $H^3(Y)^G$ is generated by curves.

*Proof.* Uniruledness gives a generically finite rational map
$S\times\mathbb P^1\dashrightarrow T$ from a smooth surface $S$.
After resolving the map by blowups, its third cohomology is generated
by $H^1$ of curves: this holds for $S\times\mathbb P^1$ by hard Lefschetz
on $S$, restriction to an ample curve, and Lefschetz $(1,1)$; blowing up
a curve adds its $H^1(-1)$, and blowing up a point adds nothing in degree
three. Push-pull for the generically finite morphism gives the assertion.

For the quotient, resolve its graph as $Y\xleftarrow{p}\widetilde Y\xrightarrow{f}T$. For $\xi\in H^3(Y)^G$, put $\beta=f_*p^*\xi$.
On a common free quotient open the trace is $|G|$, so
$$
|G|\xi-p_*f^*\beta\in N^1H^3(Y).
\tag{4}
$$
This coniveau space is generated by Gysin images of $H^1$ of resolutions
of divisors, and those weight-one spaces are generated algebraically
by curves. Thus both terms on the right of the resulting expression
for $|G|\xi$ are curve-generated. Choose finitely many curve maps spanning
the invariant space and apply the rational group projector. This retains
all supported errors and does not assume a global inverse of quotient
graph correspondences. For the supported-cohomology formulation used in
(4), see [5, Lemma 5 and its references]. $\square$

## 5. The quotient images cover the exact rank-454 summand

The full projective coordinate-sign group is
$$
A=(\mathbb Z/2)^5/\langle(1,1,1,1,1)\rangle.
$$
Its characters have an even number of minus signs: weights zero, two,
or four. Let $P_\chi$ be the rational sign-character projector.

A weight-zero or weight-two character has at least two plus coordinates
$i,j$. Its whole subspace is invariant under $H_{ij}$ and hence supported
by Lemmas 4--5.

A weight-four character $\chi_k$ has a unique plus coordinate $k$.
Then $r_k$ acts trivially on that subspace. Its stabilizer among the base
permutations is
$$
S_k=
\begin{cases}
\mathfrak S_4,&k=4,\\
\mathfrak S_{\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{k\}},&k<4.
\end{cases}
$$
For each transposition $t_{ij}\in S_k$,
$$
\left(P_{\chi_k}H^3(Y)\right)^{K_{k;ij}}
=\left(P_{\chi_k}H^3(Y)\right)^{t_{ij}=1}.
\tag{5}
$$
The character projection commutes with this stabilizer and with $r_k$,
so the space in (5) is a projected image of a supported quotient invariant.

**Lemma 6.** For a rational representation $V$ of a symmetric group,
the sum of the transposition-plus eigenspaces is the complement of its
sign-isotypic part.

*Proof.* Average a positive definite rational inner product over the group.
The orthogonal complement of the sum consists precisely of vectors
on which every transposition acts by $-1$. Transpositions generate the
group with the sign character, so that complement is its sign-isotypic
part. Both spaces are defined over $\mathbb Q$. $\square$

Thus only the sign-isotypic parts in the five weight-four characters
can remain unsupported by the twenty-eight quotients. For $k=4$ the
$x_4$ sign is positive, so only deck characters of order three occur;
the sign-isotypic summand is the rank-ten block $M_{3,\mathrm{CY}}$
of [1]. For $k<4$ the $x_4$ sign is negative, giving deck orders two
or six. The order-two part is supported by (3). The remaining sign parts
are the four rank-fourteen blocks $M_{6,\mathrm{CY}}^{(k)}$.

To make the projectors explicit, set
$$
\operatorname{Alt}_{S_k}
=\frac1{|S_k|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\Gamma_\sigma
$$
and let $e_d$ be the rational cyclotomic deck projector for order $d$.
Then on all cohomology the CY projector is
$$
\pi_{\mathrm{CY}}
=e_3P_{\chi_4}\operatorname{Alt}_{S_4}
+e_6\sum_{k=0}^3P_{\chi_k}\operatorname{Alt}_{S_k}.
\tag{6}
$$
All summands are rational algebraic correspondences. With
$$
\pi_3=\Delta_Y-\frac16\sum_{j=0}^3h^{3-j}\times h^j,\qquad
\pi_L=\pi_3-\pi_{\mathrm{CY}},
\tag{7}
$$
we recover the precise $L$ of [1]. Applying $\pi_L$ to the curve sources
supplies the surjection in Theorem 1.

The representation dimensions can be verified at the Fermat member.
The Jacobian basis is $x^e$ with $0\le e_i\le4$ and total degrees
$1,7,13,19$, corresponding to $(3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3)$. The residue
action includes the determinant, so its sign character is
$((-1)^{e_i+1})_i$. The five weight-four blocks each have Hodge profile
$(1,26,26,1)$; the ten weight-two blocks each have $(0,12,12,0)$;
the weight-zero block has $(0,5,5,0)$. After the stabilizer-sign
projections and order separation, the result is:

| Deck order | Total rank | CY rank | Supported rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 104 | 0 | 104 |
| 3 | 208 | 10 | 198 |
| 6 | 208 | 56 | 152 |
| Total | 520 | 66 | 454 |

The finite-group representation is locally constant on the connected
smooth parameter locus $U$. The image ranks and projector identities
therefore hold throughout $U$, including special members. This also
follows directly from the character argument and the global group
projectors above.

## 6. Hodge classes and the published tower

For two smooth members, choose algebraic surjections
$A=\bigoplus H^1(C_i)(-1)\twoheadrightarrow L$ and
$A'=\bigoplus H^1(C'_j)(-1)\twoheadrightarrow L'$.
Polarizable Hodge semisimplicity provides a Hodge lift of any rational
Hodge class in $L\otimes L'$ to $A\otimes A'$. After removing the twists,
each lifted component is a rational $(1,1)$ class on $C_i\times C'_j$.
Lefschetz $(1,1)$ represents it by a divisor. Composing with the two
algebraic curve correspondences gives the required codimension-three
cycle. No algebraicity of an arbitrary abstract Hodge section is used.

At very general parameters, [1, Theorem 3] proves that the rank-ten and
rank-fourteen CY blocks are simple rational Hodge structures of level
three, with the appropriate endomorphisms represented by automorphism
graphs. Hence they have no nonzero level-one rational sub-Hodge
structures. It follows that $L$ is the entire maximal level-one subspace,
and Theorem 1 establishes the degree-three generalized Hodge conjecture.

All nonmiddle cohomology of $Y$ is Tate. On $Y^2$ only $H^3(Y)^{\otimes2}$
requires attention beyond hyperplane products. The mixed $M_{\mathrm{CY}}$
and $L$ terms have no rational Hodge classes at very general parameters
by the simplicity and levels just stated. To make explicit why the
CY conclusion of [1] covers every self-tensor Hodge class, write
$M_{\mathrm{CY}}=M_3\oplus M_6^{\oplus4}$. The simple summands
have unequal rational ranks $10$ and $14$, and their generic
endomorphism algebras are $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)$. Hence cross maps
between $M_3$ and $M_6$ vanish and
$$
\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(M_{\mathrm{CY}})
=K\oplus M_4(K).
$$
The coordinate permutations identifying the four $M_6$ copies commute
with the deck action, as do all scalar $K$-endomorphisms. Thus every
Hodge endomorphism commutes with the deck operator. Under the
polarization identification, every CY self-tensor Hodge class is
therefore already diagonal-deck-invariant. The $34$ algebraic
group-ring matrix units of [1, Theorem 3(b)] span all of them.
The $L$ self-tensor classes are algebraic by Theorem 1. This proves
HC for $Y^2$.

The finite morphism $Y\to W$ given by $z=x_4^2$ has degree two. Both
varieties are smooth projective, so pullback followed by pushforward
is multiplication by the degree. HC for $Y^2$ therefore implies HC
for $W^2$.

Finally the Katsura--Shioda blowup construction [1,6] relates $X$ to
the diagonal $\mu_6$ quotient of a blowup of $Y\times Y$ along $V\times V$.
The new middle classes in this blowup are shifts of classes from
$V\times V$. Its HC is already proved at very general parameters in [1].
The finite quotient and birational morphism to $X$, with the stated
blowup centers, now transfer algebraic representatives for every Hodge
class of $X$. Equivalently, use the explicit decomposition in [1]:
the order-one sector is furnished by the surface square; the remaining
sector is the diagonal-deck-invariant part of $H^3(Y)^{\otimes2}$.
Both are algebraic by the results just cited and Theorem 1.
This proves Corollary 2.

The distinction between all-member support and generic maximality is
essential. For example, the Fermat point can acquire extra level-one
substructures in the fixed CY summand. Theorem 1 makes no uniform
assertion about such additional pieces.

## 7. Single reflections on quintics

Let a finite linear group $G$ preserve a homogeneous quintic equation
$F$ in five variables. Suppose that $G$ is generated by reflections
whose linear eigenvalues are $(1,1,1,1,-1)$. Write
$\epsilon=\det:G\to\{1,-1\}$ and
$$
e_\epsilon=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\epsilon(g)\Gamma_g.
\tag{8}
$$

**Proposition 7.** If $Z=\{F=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4$ is smooth, then
$(1-e_\epsilon)H^3(Z,\mathbb Q)$ is curve-generated.

*Proof.* For a generating reflection choose linear coordinates
$(z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3,b)$ in which it negates $b$. The invariant polynomial
ring is $\mathbb C[z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3,w]$, where $w=b^2$. Exactness of
invariants identifies the quotient of $Z$ with a weighted quintic
$Q\subset\mathbb P(1,1,1,1,2)$.

As in Lemma 4, the chain rule proves quasismoothness of this actual
quotient from smoothness of $Z$. The ambient singular locus is its
single weight-two point, so $Q$ is well-formed. Weighted adjunction
gives $K_Q=\mathcal O_Q(5-6)$. Equivalently, the only divisorial fixed
locus is the hyperplane section $D=\{b=0\}\cap Z$; the other fixed
locus is one point. Thus $q^*K_Q=K_Z-D=-H$. The finite quotient is klt
and its anticanonical divisor is ample. A smooth resolution is uniruled
by [4], and Lemma 5 makes $H^3(Z)^{r=1}$ curve-generated.

Average a positive definite rational inner product over $G$. The
perpendicular to the sum of the generating reflection-plus eigenspaces
is the subspace on which every generator acts by $-1$. Since the
generators have determinant $-1$, this is exactly $e_\epsilon H^3(Z)$.
Hence their sum is $(1-e_\epsilon)H^3(Z)$. This is a rational
group-projector statement independent of the auxiliary inner product.
Applying the complementary algebraic projector to the curve sources
proves the proposition. $\square$

For the application, let $\mathfrak S_3$ permute $x_0,x_1,x_2$, and let
$D_5$ denote the group of order ten generated by
$$
r_a:(u,v)\longmapsto(\zeta_5^a v,\zeta_5^{-a}u),
\qquad 0\le a<5.
\tag{9}
$$
Together with the three $x$-transpositions these give eight reflections
in $G=\mathfrak S_3\times D_5$. Its polynomial invariant ring has
generators $e_1(x),e_2(x),e_3(x),uv,u^5+v^5$, of degrees $1,2,3,2,5$.
Consequently every invariant quintic has the form
$$
c(u^5+v^5)+f_5(x)+uvf_3(x)+(uv)^2f_1(x),
\tag{10}
$$
where each $f_i$ is symmetric of degree $i$. This coefficient space has
dimension ten, and its connected smooth locus contains the Fermat member.

At Fermat, the residue ring is
$$
R=\mathbb Q[x_0,x_1,x_2,u,v]/(x_0^4,x_1^4,x_2^4,u^4,v^4),
$$
with Hodge grades $R_0,R_5,R_{10},R_{15}$. The action on residues includes
the determinant of the ambient linear action. Being negative for every
$x$-transposition means that the polynomial is symmetric in $x$. Being
negative for all the reflections (9) means invariance under their
rotations, hence equal exponents of $u,v$; the ordinary swap then acts
negatively on the residue. Counting the symmetric monomial orbits gives
$$
\dim(e_\epsilon R_{5q})=1,7,7,1\quad(q=0,1,2,3).
\tag{11}
$$
Finite-group representations on the Hodge bundles are locally constant
over the connected smooth family. Thus the same profiles hold throughout
(10). Define $C=e_\epsilon H^3(Z)$ and $L=(1-e_\epsilon)H^3(Z)$.
Proposition 7 proves uniform support for $L$, with rank $204-16=188$.
On the separated family of [2], these are its $M_{1,\mathrm{III}}$ and
complement. That paper already realizes sixty dimensions by curves;
the reflection coverage adds the other $128$.

For completeness the algebraic middle projector of a smooth quintic is
$$
\pi_3^Z=\Delta_Z-\frac15\sum_{j=0}^3h^{3-j}\times h^j.
$$
Its determinant projector is $\pi_C=\pi_3^Z\circ e_\epsilon$, and
$\pi_L=\pi_3^Z-\pi_C$. These expressions will provide the polarization
class needed for the square theorem.

## 8. The cyclic holomorphic period in the enlarged family

We prove the remaining assertions of Theorem 3 on the smooth locus
$T\subset\mathbb A^7$ of (1), which contains $t=0$. The seven deformation
polynomials, in the following order, are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_0&=(uv)^2m_{100},& Q_1&=uvm_{300},& Q_2&=uvm_{210},\\
Q_3&=m_{320},&Q_4&=uvm_{111},&Q_5&=m_{311},&Q_6&=m_{221}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
Their classes form a basis of $R_5$ in the determinant-residue part.
Their orbit representatives, in the coordinate order $(x_0,x_1,x_2,u,v)$,
are
$$
\begin{gathered}
(0,0,1,2,2),\ (0,0,3,1,1),\ (0,1,2,1,1),\ (0,2,3,0,0),\\
(1,1,1,1,1),\ (1,1,3,0,0),\ (1,2,2,0,0).
\end{gathered}
$$
All orbit sums use distinct monomials with coefficient one.

In the degree-ten symmetric orbit basis ordered lexicographically by
its exponent representatives, the seven products
$$
Q_0Q_1,\ Q_0Q_2,\ Q_0Q_3,\ Q_0Q_4,\ Q_0Q_5,\ Q_0Q_6,\ Q_1Q_3
$$
have coefficient matrix
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&2&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&2&0&0&0&0\\
0&2&0&1&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&1&1&0\\

\tag{13}

The final product is

Q_0Q_1Q_3=6x_0^3x_1^3x_2^3u^3v^3\quad\text{in }R_{15}.
\tag{14}

Equations (12)--(14) are direct calculations modulo the five fourth

Set $\omega_t=\operatorname{Res}(\Omega/F_t)$, a local generator of the

\operatorname{gr}^{3-r}_F

=(-1)^r r! [Q_{i_1}\cdots Q_{i_r}],\qquad 0\le r\le3.

The nonzero determinants $1,24,6$ prove that $\omega$ and its first

the associated graded spaces of dimensions $1,7,7,1$.

**Lemma 8.** Suppose a polarized rational variation $\mathbb V$, admitting an
integral lattice, over a connected smooth algebraic base has a one-dimensional top Hodge piece,
and a local generator of that line is cyclic for the flat connection at
one point. Then its very-general rational Hodge endomorphisms are scalars.

*Proof.* Apply the Hodge-locus theorem [10] to the weight-zero tensor
variation $\operatorname{End}(\mathbb V)$. There are countably many
algebraic component images. Exclude the proper ones. A rational Hodge
endomorphism at a remaining point extends by flat transport to a Hodge
morphism on the universal cover: locally its Hodge condition is an
analytic equation; a component dominating the base makes that equation
hold on an open set, and the identity theorem continues it. This also
explains why the exceptional set is formulated using component images,
rather than a single algebraic set for each marked rational map.

On a simply connected neighborhood of the cyclic point, the resulting
flat endomorphism $f$ satisfies $f\omega(t)=\lambda(t)\omega(t)$.
The scalar is holomorphic and belongs to the finite spectrum of the
fixed flat matrix $f$, so it is constant. Therefore $f$ acts by this
same scalar on every iterated derivative of $\omega$. Cyclicity implies
$f=\lambda\mathrm{Id}$. Its rationality implies $\lambda\in\mathbb Q$.
$\square$

Applied to (15), Lemma 8 gives
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q\text{-}\mathrm{Hdg}}(C_t)=\mathbb Q$
very generally. A polarized sub-Hodge structure has a rational Hodge
orthogonal projector, so this equality makes $C_t$ simple. Since it
contains $H^{3,0}$, no nonzero Hodge morphism can join it to a level-one
structure. Thus $L_t$ is the maximal level-one part, and its uniform
curve support proves degree-three GHC for very general $t$.

The argument of Section 6 proves algebraicity of all Hodge classes in
$L_t\otimes L_{t'}$ for arbitrary smooth parameters, even in the full
coefficient family (10). At very general $t$, the mixed $C_t$--$L_t$
Hodge tensors vanish and the Hodge tensors of $C_t\otimes C_t$ form a
one-dimensional space generated by its polarization projector. This
is the algebraic correspondence $\pi_C$ written above. Hence all
$H^6(Z_t^2)$ Hodge classes are algebraic. Weak Lefschetz gives only
hyperplane classes outside middle cohomology on each factor, so all
other even cohomology of the square is already algebraic. This proves
the full square assertion.

For a very general independently varying pair, apply the same
countability and Hodge-locus argument to
$\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{pr}_1^*C,\operatorname{pr}_2^*C)$
on $T^2$. A nonzero generic morphism would be an isomorphism by
simplicity. Its flat extension to a product neighborhood, with the
second parameter fixed, would carry the first holomorphic line to a
fixed line. This contradicts its rank-seven first derivative at $0$.
Therefore the $C_t\otimes C_{t'}$ Hodge tensors vanish at very general
independent pairs. The mixed terms again vanish and the $L$ terms are
algebraic. This proves the remaining product assertion of Theorem 3.

### 8.1. A nodal orbit and full residual monodromy

The cyclic-period calculation also combines with a concrete degeneration
to control every tensor power of the selected component $C$.

**Theorem 9.** The connected algebraic monodromy of $C$ in (1) is
$\operatorname{Sp}(C)\simeq\operatorname{Sp}_{16}$. For very general $t$,
every rational Hodge class in $C_t^{\otimes 2r}(3r)$, for every $r\ge1$,
is algebraic on $Z_t^{2r}$. The corresponding assertion holds for all
selected $C$-tensors of a very general independently varying finite
tuple, with contractions within each parameter's tensor factors.

*Proof.* A fixed scaling of $u,v$ identifies the family (1) with the one
having coefficient $6$ on $u^5+v^5$. In that normalization consider
$$
F_*=x_0^5+x_1^5+x_2^5+6(u^5+v^5)-5(uv)^2(x_0+x_1+x_2).
\tag{16}
$$
Its complete projective singular locus consists of the five points
$$
p_k=[\zeta^{3k},\zeta^{3k},\zeta^{3k},\zeta^k,1],
\qquad k=0,\ldots,4,\quad\zeta^5=1.
\tag{17}
$$
To prove completeness, a singular point with $u=0$ or $v=0$ would have
all $x_i=0$ by the three $x$ derivatives, and the remaining pure
fifth-power derivative excludes a projective point. Set $v=1$.
The identity $uF_u-vF_v=30(u^5-v^5)$ gives $u^5=1$. Then $x_i^4=u^2$,
so $x_i=u^3\epsilon_i$ with $\epsilon_i^4=1$, and the $u$ derivative
gives $\sum\epsilon_i=3$. Equality in the triangle inequality forces
all $\epsilon_i=1$, proving (17).

At $p_0$, the Hessian in coordinates $(x_0,x_1,x_2,u)$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
20&0&0&-10\\
0&20&0&-10\\
0&0&20&-10\\
-10&-10&-10&90
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det=600000.
\tag{18}
$$
The determinant is the same at all five points after reduction by
$u^5=1$. All are ordinary double points. The allowed deformation
$Q=(uv)^2\sum x_i$ takes value $3$ at each. Thus $F_*+sQ$ is a
transverse simultaneous smoothing. For sufficiently small $s\ne0$
there are no other singularities, by compactness and (17).

The rotations $(u,v)\mapsto(\zeta^j u,\zeta^{-j}v)$ send $k$ to
$k+2j$; the nodes form one orbit. The stabilizer of $p_0$ is
$\mathfrak S_3$ times the untwisted swap of $u,v$. Its action on the
node's local vanishing three-sphere is the determinant on the
four-dimensional affine tangent space, hence the character
$\varepsilon=\det$ of $G$. Indeed, the $x$ permutations have their
usual determinants; the swap has derivative
$(\dot x_i,\dot u)\mapsto(\dot x_i-\dot u,-\dot u)$ and determinant
$-1$. Equivariant holomorphic Morse coordinates identify this with
the usual determinant action on the vanishing sphere of a
nondegenerate quadratic form.

Orient vanishing cycles $\delta_k$ by transporting $\delta_0$ with the
rotations. The local module is $\varepsilon$ times the permutation
module of the five nodes, and its $\varepsilon$ part is a line. Its
image in global cohomology is generated by
$$
q=e_\varepsilon\delta_0=\frac15\sum_{k=0}^4\delta_k.
\tag{19}
$$
This global image is nonzero. The holomorphic residue $\omega_s$ has
character $\varepsilon$. In local holomorphic Morse coordinates the
smoothing is $\sum_{j=1}^4 y_j^2=a(s)$ with $a'(0)\ne0$, and the
residue numerator has nonzero constant term. Scaling $y=\sqrt{a(s)}z$
in the standard quadratic residue integral gives
$$
\int_{\delta_0}\omega_s=c\,a(s)+O(a(s)^2),\qquad c\ne0.
$$
The period is nonzero for small $s\ne0$. Since $\omega_s$ is
$\varepsilon$-isotypic, its period on $q$ is the same. Thus $q\ne0$;
this argument does not presume that every local vanishing cycle
always survives in global cohomology.

The Milnor neighborhoods are disjoint. The Picard--Lefschetz formula
therefore restricts to
$$
T(x)=x\mathbin{\pm}5\langle x,q\rangle q\quad(x\in C).
\tag{20}
$$
The polarization restricted to $C$ is nondegenerate, since
$e_\varepsilon$ is a self-adjoint polarization projector. Hence
$T-I$ has rank one and square zero. The Zariski closure of its integer
powers contains the associated additive one-parameter subgroup,
which lies in connected monodromy.

Let $M$ be the generic Mumford--Tate group of $C$, and $H$ its connected
algebraic monodromy. Lemma 8 makes the commutant of $M$ equal to
$\mathbb Q$. The commutation equations are rational linear equations,
so extension of scalars makes the complex commutant equal to
$\mathbb C$. Reductivity of $M$ and complete reducibility then imply
that $C_{\mathbb C}$ is irreducible for $M_{\mathbb C}$.
Andr\'e's normality theorem [11, Theorem 1] gives
$H\triangleleft M^{\mathrm{der}}$ and thus $H\triangleleft M$.
The restriction to this connected normal reductive subgroup is
isotypic: its isotypic idempotents are permuted by the connected
group $M_{\mathbb C}$, hence are fixed; see also [11, Lemma 1].
Write it as $U\otimes\mathbb C^m$, with $H$ acting trivially on the
multiplicity factor. The rank of $h-I$ is divisible by $m$ for every
$h\in H$. Formula (20) forces $m=1$. Thus $H$ acts irreducibly.
This step uses both normality and the transvection; scalar Hodge
endomorphisms alone would not suffice.

We give the needed symplectic group argument. The Lie algebra of a
connected irreducible subgroup $H\subset\operatorname{Sp}(V)$ containing
a transvection contains $N_w:x\mapsto\langle x,w\rangle w$ for every
$w$ in the orbit $O$ of one nonzero transvection vector. The orbit is
irreducible and spans $V$. Put
$$
S(a,b)x=\langle x,a\rangle b+\langle x,b\rangle a.
$$
For $a,b\in O$ with $\langle a,b\rangle\ne0$, the bracket
$[N_a,N_b]$ is a nonzero multiple of $S(a,b)$. For an orthogonal pair,
choose $w\in O$ pairing nontrivially with both; the two nonempty
Zariski opens intersect because $O$ is irreducible. The bracket
$[S(a,w),N_b]$ is then a nonzero multiple of $S(a,b)$. These operators
span $\operatorname{Sym}^2V\simeq\mathfrak{sp}(V)$, since $O$ spans
$V$. Thus $H=\operatorname{Sp}(V)$. Applied to (20), this proves
$H=\operatorname{Sp}_{16}$. The generic Hodge group is contained in
this symplectic group and contains $H$, so it too is the full group.

The symplectic invariant theorem [12, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2]
says that all even invariant tensors are spanned by pair contractions;
there are no odd invariant tensors. The inverse polarization tensor
of $C$ is its diagonal component, given by the algebraic projector
$\pi_C$. Products and pullbacks of that class give every pairing
tensor on $Z_t^{2r}$, proving the algebraicity assertion.

For independently varying finite tuples the parameter fundamental
group is a product and each factor acts only on its own variation.
The connected monodromy is consequently the product of the respective
symplectic groups. Its invariants contract only within the tensor
factors belonging to a given parameter. There are countably many
orders and rational tensors; [10] supplies the countable union of
proper algebraic exceptional loci, simultaneously for all orders.
$\square$

Theorem 9 alone concerns the algebraically projected $C$ sectors.
Curve support and the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem on products of two
curves do not by themselves control higher tensors of $L$. The next
section supplies the additional monodromy argument.

### 8.2. The level-one components and full powers

We now control the remaining tensors of $L$, using the actual seven
Higgs multiplication maps and two further nodal orbits. Let
$K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, the character field of the two-dimensional
representations of $D_5$.

**Theorem 10.** For the seven-parameter family (1), the rational Hodge
conjecture holds on every power of a very general $Z_t$, and on all mixed
powers of a very general independently varying finite tuple. These
assertions hold simultaneously for all tensor orders. They are not
asserted at arbitrary special parameters.

We give the finite certificates and the geometric argument, including
the passage from the finite-field calculation to characteristic zero.

**Multiplicity spaces.** Use polynomial types $s,a,b$ for the symmetric,
alternating and standard representations of $\mathfrak S_3$. These are
polynomial labels: residue cohomology includes the determinant twist.
The rotation weight is $k=a_u-a_v\pmod5$. We identify weights $k,-k$
by the $D_5$ reflection. A primitive standard projector is obtained
by taking the $(01)$-even polynomial line and removing the symmetric
part. The multiplicity spaces, suppressing their finite-group factors,
are as follows:

| Label | Polynomial type, weight | $h^{2,1}$ | Total rank over $\mathbb C$ |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| $A$ | $a,0$ | 2 | 4 |
| $B$ | $b,0$ | 8 | 16 |
| $D_1,D_2$ | $s,1$ and $s,2$ | 6,6 | 12,12 |
| $E_1,E_2$ | $a,1$ and $a,2$ | 1,1 | 2,2 |
| $J_1,J_2$ | $b,1$ and $b,2$ | 6,6 | 12,12 |

The remaining $s,0$ component is $C$. The $k=1,2$ pairs are the two
real embeddings of $K$-linear multiplicity spaces. The dihedral
representations are orthogonal over $K$, as is the standard
$\mathfrak S_3$ representation over $\mathbb Q$; consequently their
multiplicity polarizations are alternating. All displayed components
of $L$ have only Hodge types $(2,1),(1,2)$. The ranks follow from the
Fermat residue action as in Section 7 and remain constant. Counting
finite-group multiplicities gives $\dim L=188$.

**Generic Hodge maps from Higgs matrices.** At the coefficient vector
$(2,3,5,7,11,13,17)$ in the order $Q_0,\ldots,Q_6$, the supplement
constructs the actual Jacobian quotients in degrees $5,10$ modulo
$1009$. It applies the polynomial projectors
$$
\sum_{g\in\mathfrak S_3}g,\qquad
\sum_{g\in\mathfrak S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(g)g,\qquad
3(1+(01))-\sum_{g\in\mathfrak S_3}g,
\tag{21}
$$
respectively. Each is six times the desired projector. The weight
condition and these rational matrices select the primitive polynomial
spaces. Projecting every monomial multiple of the five actual partial
derivatives gives all Jacobian relations in each space. Row reduction
chooses quotient coordinates. The resulting multiplication maps are
$$
M_i^V:R_5(V)\longrightarrow R_{10}(V),\qquad P\longmapsto Q_iP,
\quad 0\le i\le6.
$$
All eight quotient dimensions in each degree are, in order
$(A,B,D_1,E_1,J_1,D_2,E_2,J_2)$,
$$
(2,8,6,1,6,6,1,6).
$$
For every ordered pair $V,W$, solve the linear equations
$$
Y M_i^V=M_i^W X\quad(0\le i\le6).
\tag{22}
$$
The computed kernel dimensions are zero off the diagonal and
$$
(2,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)
\tag{23}
$$
on the diagonal. The supplement records the quotient pivots, all seven
matrices for every space, and the pivots of all sixty-four systems.

Here is why this finite calculation bounds the characteristic-zero
problem. The smooth characteristic-zero coefficient locus is nonempty
and irreducible. Its Jacobian dimensions are the stated constant ones.
Every polynomial projector and Macaulay matrix is defined over
$\mathbb Z[1/30]$. The nonzero pivots modulo $1009$ supply nonzero
minors over $\mathbb Q$ at the same coefficient vector. Thus the
quotient bases and matrix formulas are valid on a nonempty rational
open locus; the generic relation ranks cannot exceed those on the
smooth locus, where the dimensions are known. This open meets the
smooth locus. Clear the nonzero pivot denominators in (22). Its
finite-prime ranks give lower bounds for the generic rational ranks,
so (23) gives upper bounds for the generic kernel dimensions over
$\mathbb C$. No claim that a random finite-field sample determines an
analytic period domain is used here.

A generic rational Hodge map extends flatly over the universal cover,
by the argument of Lemma 8. Differentiating its Hodge-filtration
compatibility gives (22) on the associated graded spaces. This passage
is injective on Hodge maps: they preserve both Hodge types, so zero
maps on both graded pieces mean the original map is zero.

We also spell out the scalar extension used here. Let $M$ be the generic
Mumford--Tate group of the full rational variation. Its rational
commutant is the algebra of generic rational Hodge endomorphisms, and
its complex commutant is obtained by scalar extension, since the
commutation equations are rational linear equations. Every primitive
complex finite-group projector commutes with $M_{\mathbb C}$. A map
between two primitive multiplicity spaces that commutes with
$M_{\mathbb C}$ therefore extends, by inclusion and projection, to
an element of this full complex commutant. It is a complex linear
combination of the flat rational Hodge maps just considered. It too
satisfies (22) and is detected by its associated graded map. This
justifies the bounds separately for the two embeddings of $K$, even
though the individual embedding spaces are not rational subvariations.
The scalar maps supply the lower bound one on every diagonal; for
$A$ the upper bound is still two. All cross commutants vanish. These
statements concern generic Hodge maps; an arbitrary solution of (22)
need not be flat.

**Two complete nodal orbits.** Both of the following members belong to
(1) after a fixed scaling of $u,v$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{15}={}&\sum x_i^5-\frac{46}{3}(u^5+v^5)
-\frac{25}{3}uv\sum x_i^3+20(uv)^2\sum x_i,\\
F_{30}={}&\sum x_i^5-648(u^5+v^5)-25uv\sum x_i^3
-50uvx_0x_1x_2+370(uv)^2\sum x_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
Their complete singular loci consist respectively of the permutations
of $(1,1,2)$ and $(1,2,3)$ in the $x$ coordinates at $u=v=1$, followed
by the five rotations used in (17). There are fifteen and thirty
nodes, each set a single $G$ orbit. The Hessian determinants at the
seeds are $-10350000$ and $1312200000$. The deformation $Q_0$ has
values $4$ and $6$ respectively, so it smooths all the nodes transversely.

For completeness, both singularity classifications reduce first to
$uv\ne0$ and $u^5=v^5$ by the same derivative identity as for (16).
After the rotation and projective normalization take $u=v=1$.
For $F_{15}$ the remaining derivatives give
$$
x_i\in\{-2,-1,1,2\},\qquad
\sum_i(24x_i-5x_i^3)=46,
$$
whose only solutions are the permutations of $(1,1,2)$.
For $F_{30}$ a repeated-coordinate solution $(r,r,t)$ would satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
r^4-15r^2-10rt+74&=0,\\
t^4-15t^2-10r^2+74&=0,\\
-648-5(2r^3+t^3)-10r^2t+148(2r+t)&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Their rational ideal is the unit ideal, as the small exact elimination
in the supplement verifies. If the three coordinates are distinct,
put $h(T)=T^3-e_1T^2+e_2T-e_3$. Their derivative equations imply
$$
h(T)\mid 5T^5-75T^3+370T-50e_3.
$$
The three remainder coefficients and the $u$ derivative have ideal
$(e_1-6,e_2-11,e_3-6)$, again by explicit rational elimination. Thus
the coordinates are the three roots $1,2,3$. This also treats possible
zero coordinates and completes the classification.

We must check that the local vanishing cycles survive independently
in global cohomology. Evaluate all degree-five monomials on these
nodes over $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$. Reduction modulo $101$ with
$\zeta\mapsto95$ gives ranks $15,30$; the listed square witness
minors have determinants $17,84$ modulo $101$. Hence their ranks are
full in characteristic zero. On a nearby smooth fiber, the periods
of $\operatorname{Res}(P\Omega/F^2)$ on the local vanishing cycles
have leading terms $c_jP(p_j)$, with every $c_j\ne0$. This follows
by differentiating the quadratic holomorphic vanishing period used
above, allowing all degree-five directions in the full hypersurface
space. The evaluation minors remain nonzero for small smoothing
parameter. Thus all fifteen, respectively thirty, local cycles are
independent in global $H^3$.

The node stabilizers are $\mathfrak S_2\times C_2$ for $F_{15}$
and $C_2$ for $F_{30}$, with $C_2$ the $D_5$ reflection. They fix the
chosen projective representative itself, so their local vanishing
character is the determinant. The local representation of the first
orbit is consequently
$$
(\operatorname{sgn}_{\mathfrak S_3}\oplus\operatorname{std}_{\mathfrak S_3})
\otimes(\operatorname{sgn}_{D_5}\oplus\rho_1\oplus\rho_2).

Every multiplicity in (25) is one. Thus it supplies a rank-one
Picard--Lefschetz logarithm on $B,D_1,D_2,J_1,J_2$, as well as $C$.
For $F_{30}$, the $\mathfrak S_3$ factor is its regular representation;
its polynomial alternating component has multiplicity one. It supplies
rank-one logarithms on $A,E_1,E_2$.

These rank assertions use the proved global independence. If $D$ is
the injective map from the space of local cycles to global cohomology,
the Picard--Lefschetz logarithm is, up to sign, $DD^{\mathsf T}J$, where
$J$ is the global nondegenerate alternating pairing. Its rank is the
number of nodes because $D^{\mathsf T}J$ is surjective. Its image as a
$G$ representation is exactly the local-cycle representation. Therefore
its rank on each primitive multiplicity space is the multiplicity in
(25), or in the corresponding regular representation. This avoids
inferring a nonzero projection merely from a local character match.

**The remaining endomorphism bound for $A$.** This rank-four rational
variation has generic endomorphism dimension at most two by (23).
At Fermat, simultaneous rotation $(u,v)\mapsto(\zeta u,\zeta v)$
commutes with $G$ and acts on its four embedding lines with eigenvalues
$\zeta^2,\zeta^4$ in $H^{2,1}$ and $\zeta,\zeta^3$ in $H^{1,2}$.
Thus it is rank one over $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$, with shifted CM type
$\{2,4\}$. Its Hodge endomorphism algebra is exactly $E$: decompose
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}(E)=\bigoplus_{g\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)}Eg$;
only the identity $g$ preserves this type. In particular it is simple.

Generic rational Hodge endomorphisms specialize injectively to Fermat
by flat continuation. Their algebra is thus a subalgebra of $E$, and
its dimension is at most two. It is either $\mathbb Q$ or the only
quadratic subfield $K$. The rational rank-one transvection on $A$
just constructed commutes with this algebra: connected monodromy is
contained in the generic Mumford--Tate group. A $K$-linear map has
even rational rank, so the second possibility is excluded. Therefore
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(A)=\mathbb Q$ very generally.

**Full monodromy and tensors.** Each primitive complex multiplicity
space now has generic scalar Hodge commutant and a rank-one transvection.
Normality, isotypy, and the Lie-algebra argument in Theorem 9 apply to
each. The respective connected groups are the full symplectic groups.
The joint group has complex form
$$
\operatorname{Sp}_{16}^{\,2}\times\operatorname{Sp}_4
\times\operatorname{Sp}_{12}^{\,4}\times\operatorname{Sp}_2^{\,2}.
\tag{26}
$$
Here one $\operatorname{Sp}_{16}$ acts on $C$ and the other on $B$.
We justify the product assertion. A connected subdirect subgroup of a
product of simple symplectic groups can fail to be the full product only
through a diagonal identification of isomorphic factors. The connected
normalizer of such a graph in the product of the two symplectic groups
is that graph itself, up to the finite centers. Connected monodromy is
normal in the generic Hodge group, and the latter lies in the product of
the multiplicity symplectic groups because the finite-group projectors
and polarizations are Hodge tensors. A diagonal identification would
therefore force a complex Hodge intertwiner between the two standard
representations. Automorphisms of the complex symplectic group are inner,
so this is an ordinary linear intertwiner. For two $L$ components it is
excluded by the off-diagonal bounds (22). For $B$ and $C$ it is excluded
by their different Hodge profiles. This proves (26).

Finally, invariant theory for the product in (26) expresses every
invariant tensor of $H^3(Z_t)$ as a sum of products of invariant
pairings, allowing arbitrary vectors in its constant finite-group
multiplicity factors. All rational two-factor Hodge classes are already
algebraic by Theorem 3: $L\otimes L$ by curve support and Lefschetz,
$C\otimes C$ by its projector, and the mixed terms vanish. Taking
rational spans commutes with extension to $\mathbb C$, so products
and pullbacks of these rational algebraic pair classes span every
rational higher Hodge tensor. Nonmiddle cohomology is Tate by weak
Lefschetz. This proves full rational HC on every power. For independent
parameter tuples the monodromy is the corresponding external product;
the same proof works, with contractions within each parameter's factors.
The countable Hodge-locus argument supplies one very-general set for all
orders. This proves Theorem 10. $\square$

### 8.3. The full invariant coefficient space

**Corollary 11.** Let $\mathcal U_{10}$ be the smooth locus of the full
coefficient space (10). For very general members of this space, the
degree-three generalized Hodge conjecture and full rational HC on all
powers hold. The same holds for all mixed powers of independent finite
tuples, simultaneously in every order.

*Proof.* The coefficient space has dimension $1+5+3+1=10$: its parameters
are $c$ and the symmetric polynomials of degrees $5,3,1$ in (10).
Its smooth locus is a nonempty connected open subset of affine space.
The seven-parameter family (1) is the subfamily obtained by setting
$c=1$, the coefficient of $\sum x_i^5$ to $1$, and that of $m_{410}$ to
zero. Choose the Fermat point as common basepoint. Every loop in $T$
is a loop in $\mathcal U_{10}$, so the larger family's connected
monodromy contains the product (26). The globally defined finite-group
action, projectors and polarizations place it inside that same product.
Thus equality holds. In particular the residual $C$ remains generically
simple and level three. Proposition 7 supplies uniform curve support
for its complement on the full space. The pair-class, invariant-tensor
and countable Hodge-locus arguments of Sections 8.1--8.2 now apply without
change, also on the external products of coefficient spaces. $\square$

## 9. Fully symmetric quintics

Let $\mathcal S$ be the smooth locus in the seven-dimensional vector space of symmetric homogeneous quintics in five variables. Write $p_j=\sum_{i=0}^4x_i^j$, and let $m_\lambda$ denote a sum of distinct monomials in its symmetric orbit. The Fermat polynomial belongs to $\mathcal S$, which is a connected nonempty Zariski-open subset of an affine space.

**Theorem 12.** For every member $X$ of $\mathcal S$, the rank-$192$ complement $L$ of the sign-isotypic summand $C\subset H^3(X,\mathbb Q)$ is generated by algebraic correspondences from curves. At very general parameters, $C$ is simple of profile $(1,5,5,1)$ and the degree-three generalized Hodge conjecture holds. Full rational HC holds on every power of a very general $X$ and on all mixed powers of independent very-general tuples.

The connected monodromy on the primitive multiplicity spaces is

\operatorname{Sp}_{12}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{16}
\times\operatorname{Sp}_{12}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{8}
\times\operatorname{Sp}_{4}.
$$
The first factor acts on $C$.

### 9.1. Uniform support and the exact multiplicities

The ten transpositions are generating reflections on the ambient five-dimensional coordinate representation. \end{gathered}
$$
The corresponding pole orders are1,2,3,4 and their dimensions are
$1,4,4,1$. Terminal Fermat monomials have distinct diagonal characters,
so these are pure Hodge representatives and together give the entire
de Rham space.

Put $\omega=\operatorname{Res}(x_0\Omega/\Phi)$. For $n=a+b$,
Griffiths differentiation [9] gives
$$
\nabla_B^a\nabla_C^b\omega\big|_0
=(-1)^n n!\operatorname{Res}
\frac{x_0Q_1^aQ_2^b\Omega}{\Phi_0^{n+1}}.
$$
All these derivatives belong to the same primitive component. To
reduce a monomial $x^e$ at pole order $q$, a coordinate exponent
$e_i\ge5$ contributes
$$
\frac{e_i-5}{6(q-1)},
$$
lowers that exponent by6, and lowers the pole order by one; an exponent
equal to5 gives zero. This is the usual exact pole-reduction relation
using $\partial_i\Phi_0=6x_i^5$, not reduction only in the graded
Jacobian quotient. Repetition terminates with exponents at most4.

The ten derivatives with $a+b\le3$, ordered by total degree and then
increasing $a$, have coefficient determinant
$$
\det=\frac{105200}{9}
$$
in the displayed basis. Their cumulative ranks are $1,3,6,10$.
The full matrix and rational reductions are supplied in the supplement.
Only two independent first derivatives occur, so a graded-product
argument that assumes all four first-grade directions would be invalid.
The lower-pole terms in higher derivatives supply the missing directions.
Lemma 8 applies to the cyclic holomorphic line and proves generic
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}C=\mathbb Q$. In particular $C$ is
simple and admits no Hodge map to or from a level-one structure.

### 11.3. Flat period graphs on the level-one blocks

There are nine level-one primitive blocks in the table, with
$g=\frac12\dim V$ respectively $1,1,4,2,2,1,5,2,1$.
First-order multiplication by just two parameter polynomials is
insufficient for the desired commutant bound. We instead use the
third-order Taylor graph of $F^2$ in a flat marking.

Choose a basis $P_1,\ldots,P_g$ of a polynomial Young image in
$R_7$ at Fermat. The sections
$s_j(t)=\operatorname{Res}(P_j\Omega/\Phi_t^2)$ form a local frame
of the primitive $F^2$. This is a flat group projector applied to the
relative residue construction; it is not an arbitrary infinitesimal
model. The complementary exponent substitution $e\mapsto4-e$ gives
an $R_{13}$ basis of the other Fermat Hodge piece. In these two
fixed vector spaces, flatly mark the sections as columns
$$
s(t)=\binom{U(t)}{V(t)},\qquad U(0)=I_g,\quad V(0)=0.
$$
Their Taylor coefficients are obtained by reducing
$$
\frac{(-1)^n(n+1)!}{a!b!}
\operatorname{Res}\frac{P_jQ_1^aQ_2^b\Omega}{\Phi_0^{n+2}},
\qquad n=a+b.
$$
This uses the full rational pole relation of Section11.2. Formally
invert $U$ and set $Z=VU^{-1}$ through degree three. This normalizes
the moving $F^2$ to the graph of an actual flat period map.

A generic rational Hodge morphism extends flatly to a Hodge morphism
on the universal cover by the same Hodge-locus argument as Lemma8.
At Fermat it preserves the two Hodge pieces, so has block matrices
$A,D$. Between any source and target primitive blocks it necessarily
satisfies
$$
D Z_{\mathrm{source}}(t)=Z_{\mathrm{target}}(t)A.
$$
Consequently each Taylor coefficient through degree three gives a
linear equation in these constant matrices. These are necessary flat
Hodge equations; no converse is used.

The exact rational jets in the supplement give, after reduction
modulo1009, the following upper bounds for the solution dimensions:
the $9\times9$ matrix is $I_9$. Equivalently, for genera $g_i,g_j$,
the equation rank is
$$
2g_ig_j-\delta_{ij}.
$$
All denominators used in the coordinate choices and jets are invertible
at this prime. The verifier retains bases, every jet and the pivot
columns of all81 systems. A nonzero finite-prime minor is a rational
rank lower bound; scalar identity maps give the matching diagonal
lower bound. Thus the generic full Hodge endomorphism algebra of every
primitive block is $\mathbb Q$, and every cross Hodge Hom space between
distinct level-one blocks vanishes. Every block has a nonzero period
jet, also checked explicitly. These bounds concern the complexified
linear equations, so they do not assume that a Betti rational map has
rational coordinates in the chosen Fermat de Rham bases.

### 11.4. Three complete nodal orbits and their global images

For the residual component use
$$
\Phi_*={25\over24}\left(\sum x_i^6-
{1\over25}\left(\sum x_i^2\right)^3\right),
\qquad (B,C)=(-1/8,-1/4).
$$
If $p_2=\sum x_i^2$ vanished at a singular point, the derivative
equations would force all coordinates to vanish. Normalize $p_2=5$.
Then each nonzero square is $\pm1$, and five or fewer such squares
sum to5 only when all five are1. Hence there are exactly16 projective
sign nodes. At $[1:1:1:1:1]$ the affine Hessian has diagonal20 and
off-diagonal$-5$, with determinant78125. The group is transitive on
the nodes. Both parameter derivatives are nonzero there, so a transverse
one-parameter smoothing exists within $\mathcal B$.

The local module has one copy of the residual finite type $W$. For
example, use the unique even-sign lift of $A$ in dimension five and
represent the nodes by sign vectors of product1. Their permutation
module is the regular $A$ module, with the stabilizer $S_5$ acting
through the local determinant on vanishing cycles. The five linear
numerators have evaluation Gram matrix$16I_5$. Their holomorphic
vanishing periods have leading term $c\,x_i(p)t$, with nonzero local
constant$c$. Therefore the residual local module maps nontrivially to
global cohomology and, being irreducible as a finite-group module,
injects. This gives rank one on the primitive multiplicity $C$.

For the rank-ten and rank-eight level-one blocks use, respectively,
$$
(B,C)=(-1,1),\qquad (B,C)=(1,-9).
$$
The complete singular loci are the signed coordinate-permutation
orbits of $(1,1,0,0,0)$ and $(1,1,1,0,0)$, with20 and40 nodes.
Here is an exhaustive algebraic check. Write
$$
\Phi=A p_3(y)+D p_1(y)p_2(y)+E p_1(y)^3,
\quad A=1-B+C/3,\quad D=B-C/2,\quad E=C/6.
$$
At both parameters $A\ne0$. Every nonzero $y_i=x_i^2$ is a root of
one quadratic
$$
3A z^2+2D p_1 z+D p_2+3E p_1^2=0.
$$
If only one nonzero value occurs, normalize it to1. Its multiplicity
$k$ must satisfy $A+Dk+Ek^2=0$, which among$1\le k\le5$ gives only
$k=2$ at the first parameter and $k=3$ at the second.
If two distinct nonzero values occur, normalize them to$1,r$ with
multiplicities$m,n>0$, $m+n\le5$. Their sum and product imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
3A(1+r)+2D(m+nr)&=0,\\
D(m+nr^2)+3E(m+nr)^2-3Ar&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each of the six unordered multiplicity pairs, the verifier gives
an exact rational Bezout combination of these polynomials equal to1.
There are therefore no such points. This argument also covers$p_1=0$.

The affine Hessian determinants at the stated representatives are
$-128$ and$2764800$, so every singularity is an ordinary node. Varying
$B$ smooths all nodes transversely: its values are2 and6. To prove
global independence, for each active coordinate support$I$ of size$k$
choose all odd sign characters on$I$, represented by exponents1 on
odd positions and2 on even positions. Add an even exponent to reach
degree7. These monomials vanish on every different support and evaluate
as a Hadamard matrix on the$2^{k-1}$ projective sign points of$I$.
The full evaluation determinants are
$$
2^{10}\quad(20\text{ nodes}),\qquad
2^{40}\quad(40\text{ nodes}).
$$
The degree-seven pole-two residue periods have nonzero constant leading
terms proportional to these evaluations. Thus their period matrix has
full rank for small nonzero smoothing parameter, and all local
vanishing cycles survive independently in global cohomology. This is
the required global argument, not an inference from a local character
table.

The local20-node module contains the polynomial type$((1),(3,1))$
with multiplicity one, and the40-node module contains$((3),(2))$
with multiplicity one. The character computation includes projective
rescaling: after removing the common residue determinant twist, a
group element carrying a node representative to$\lambda$ times itself
contributes$\lambda^7$ to the local polynomial trace. Rational
character and Young projectors give exact traces one on these two
types. Their finite dimensions are15 and10. The verification retains
these traces, avoiding an assumption that all stabilizer actions are
untwisted permutations.

For disjoint Milnor neighborhoods Picard--Lefschetz gives, up to one
common sign, $N=DD^{\mathsf T}J$, where$D$ sends the local cycle basis
to global cohomology. All local coefficients have the same sign.
Injectivity on the selected local finite types and a positive definite
real invariant form give rank one on each selected primitive
multiplicity space. The resulting nonzero unipotent one-parameter
subgroups lie in connected algebraic monodromy.

### 11.5. Full product monodromy and algebraic tensors

On $C$ and on the rank-eight and rank-ten level-one spaces, generic
scalar endomorphisms, normality [11], isotypy and the rank-one operators
just proved give irreducible connected monodromy. The Lie-algebra
argument of Theorem9 makes it the full symplectic group in each case.

The remaining level-one primitive structures, after twisting, are
$H^1$ of elliptic curves or abelian surfaces up to rational isogeny:
they are polarized rational weight-one structures of ranks two or four.
Their scalar Hodge endomorphisms make them simple with endomorphism
algebra$\mathbb Q$. Their Hodge groups are respectively$\operatorname{Sp}_2$
and$\operatorname{Sp}_4$ by the classical low-dimensional results
summarized in Moonen--Zarhin [17, Sections2.1--2.2]. Connected monodromy
is normal in this simple group, and is nontrivial. Indeed, otherwise
monodromy would be finite; after a finite etale base change the fixed-part
theorem [18, Theorem4.1.1 and Corollary4.1.2] would make the entire variation
constant, contrary to its
nonzero period jets. Thus these monodromy groups are also full.

Each generic Hodge-group projection is now the stated symplectic group.
A connected proper subdirect product of these simple groups would
identify two equal-rank factors. All automorphisms of the relevant
symplectic Lie algebras preserve their standard representation, so
such a diagonal identification would supply a complex Hodge
intertwiner. The linear commutation equations are rational, so a
nonzero complex intertwiner gives a nonzero rational one. Section11.3
excludes these between distinct level-one factors. The residual
rank-ten factor is also unlinked to the level-one rank-ten factor,
since it is simple and has a nonzero$H^{3,0}$ part. Consequently the
generic Hodge group is the full product. Connected monodromy is normal
in that product and projects onto every factor; it must be the full
product as well.

Uniform curve support makes every pair class of$L$ algebraic by the
argument of Section6. On$W\otimes C$, generic endomorphisms form
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}(W)=M_5(\mathbb Q)$. The rational
group-algebra image supplies all its matrix units, and the projected
diagonal supplies the inverse polarization. Thus all residual pair
classes are algebraic too. Mixed pairs vanish. The symplectic invariant
theorem [12] expresses every higher invariant tensor as a rational
linear combination of products of these pair tensors. Nonmiddle
cohomology is Tate, proving full rational HC for every power.
Independent tuples have product base fundamental group and product
monodromy, giving the mixed-power assertion. The countable Hodge-locus
argument excludes proper algebraic loci simultaneously for all tensor
orders. Simplicity of$C$ makes$L$ the maximal level-one part and proves
the degree-three GHC statement. This proves Theorem14. $\square$

The additional mechanism in this family is the use of full nonlinear
period jets in a two-parameter variation. The character calculation
is consistent with the general formulas of Chênevert [15]; quotient
support and low-dimensional abelian Hodge groups are classical inputs.
The generic full signed-permutation family has no coordinate order-three
deck action, so Rossi's directional cyclic theorem [3] is not a theorem
for this family. These arguments make no assertion about the extra
Hodge classes acquired by arbitrary special members.


### 11.6. The residual is not generated by weight-one Hodge structures

The following standard representation-theoretic obstruction clarifies
the relation to abelian-variety methods; the obstruction itself is not
claimed as a new general theorem.

**Corollary 15.** At very general parameters in Theorem14, the rational
Hodge structure $C$ does not belong to the tensor category generated by
$H^1$ of complex abelian varieties and Tate structures, allowing duals,
direct sums and subquotients.

*Proof.* The Hodge group of $C$ is $\operatorname{Sp}_{10}$ acting in its
standard representation. The Hodge circle has weights
$$
3,1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-3.
$$
Suppose $C$ belonged to the stated category. Only finitely many abelian
varieties occur in an expression for it, so replace them by their product
$A$. The Hodge group $H_A$ acts faithfully on $H^1(A,\mathbb Q)$ and maps
surjectively onto the Hodge group of $C$, since both are the algebraic
closures of the corresponding Hodge-circle actions. Polarizability makes
these groups reductive. After passing to the simply connected cover of
the derived group, one simple factor maps isogenously onto
$\operatorname{Sp}_{10}$; over $\mathbb C$ it is itself
$\operatorname{Sp}_{10}$.

Lift the Hodge circle after a finite cover if necessary. On this simple
factor its dominant cocharacter is a positive multiple, say $N$, of
$(3,1,1,1,1)$. For type $C_5$, the fundamental weights are
$\omega_j=e_1+\cdots+e_j$. Every nonzero dominant highest weight
$\lambda=\sum_{j=1}^5m_j\omega_j$ therefore has
$$
\lambda(3,1,1,1,1)=\sum_{j=1}^5m_j(j+2)\ge3.
$$
The longest Weyl element is $-1$, so every nontrivial irreducible
representation of this factor has Hodge-circle weight diameter at least
$6N$. An irreducible constituent for the full product of the simple
factors and the center is a tensor product of constituents. Fixing one
weight in each other factor shows that its diameter is at least that
of this factor; a central character merely translates all weights.
But the lifted circle has only weights $N,-N$ on $H^1(A)$, of diameter
$2N$. Thus this simple factor would act trivially on $H^1(A)$, contrary
to faithfulness. This contradiction proves the corollary. Tate twists
do not change circle weights. $\square$

The same argument applies to the standard symplectic residual
multiplicities in the quintic theorems, with the corresponding rank.
Thus the higher-power results include Hodge structures outside the
category generated by abelian varieties. The pair-class and invariant
tensor proof does not require an abelian-variety realization of $C$.

Run the fifteen Python scripts from the supplement root. They require

The arithmetic checks do not prove rational connectedness, the
quotient-support lemma, generic Hodge simplicity or HC by themselves:
those steps are the written arguments and the cited theorems above.

The proof constructs auxiliary curves existentially through uniruledness
and resolution; it does not give equations for every curve or cycle.

the distinct cyclic sextic family of Theorem1, arbitrary-special-member
full-square theorem, new Fermat degree, or resolution of general rational
HC is claimed.
