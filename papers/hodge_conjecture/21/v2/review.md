# Accept

The revision makes substantive, supported extensions of the accepted paper. Corollary 11 enlarges the original quintic coefficient space; Theorems 12–13 establish the corresponding results for coordinate-transposition families; and Theorem 14 treats a distinct signed-permutation sextic family. These additions establish new cases of the rational Hodge conjecture for powers of very general threefolds, with uniform curve support for specified complementary summands. They do not resolve the universal Hodge conjecture or satisfy the problem's full solve definition.

The quintic extensions have sufficient justification. Corollary 11 correctly uses inclusion of the accepted family's monodromy in that of the larger coefficient space, with the global group action and polarizations supplying the matching upper bound. For the fully symmetric family, the residual cyclicity calculation, the unique-node degeneration, the four level-one Higgs bounds, and the complete nodal-orbit classifications support the asserted product monodromy. The residue-period evaluation argument establishes global independence of the local vanishing cycles; the proof does not infer their survival merely from a matching finite-group character.

Section 10 provides a coherent extension to the remaining Young subgroups. Restricting a generic flat Hodge endomorphism to the fully symmetric family before continuing to Fermat correctly places it in the known $103$-dimensional group-algebra image. This avoids confusing the larger endomorphism algebra of a special fiber with the generic algebra. The subsequent differentiated equations give the required upper bounds. Surjective node evaluation, followed by averaging over the subgroup, supplies independent invariant smoothing parameters for the subgroup orbits. Together with the retained global independence and multiplicity-one witnesses, this justifies the transvections used in the monodromy argument. The unrestricted-quintic case is appropriately identified as classical.

The principal new sextic result also has the necessary geometric and Hodge-theoretic support. The forty actual Fano quotients cover the rank-$470$ complement, leaving five copies of the rank-ten residual variation. The distinction between graded Jacobian multiplication and full Griffiths differentiation is essential here: the first derivatives span only two directions, while the pole-reduced derivatives through order three have cumulative ranks $1,3,6,10$ and determinant $105200/9$. This supplies the cyclicity required by Lemma 8.

For the nine level-one primitive blocks, Section 11.3 constructs the Taylor graph of the actual moving filtration in a flat marking. The equations
$$
D Z_{\mathrm{source}}=Z_{\mathrm{target}}A
$$
are valid necessary conditions on flat Hodge morphisms. Their finite-prime rank certificates imply characteristic-zero upper bounds, and the scalar identities supply the matching diagonal lower bounds. The argument therefore establishes scalar generic endomorphisms and vanishing cross Hom spaces without assuming that arbitrary infinitesimal solutions are flat.

The complete sixteen-, twenty-, and forty-node degenerations supply the remaining monodromy input. The written proofs address singularity completeness, transverse smoothing, projective stabilizer characters, and global survival of the selected cycles. For the smaller level-one blocks, the elliptic and abelian-surface classifications in Moonen–Zarhin [17, Sections 2.1–2.2] give precisely the asserted symplectic Hodge groups when the endomorphism algebra is $\mathbb Q$. Nonconstant period jets and the fixed-part theorem exclude finite monodromy. Normality and the Hom exclusions then justify the full product, including the separation of the two rank-ten factors. Algebraic pair classes and symplectic invariant theory consequently support the conclusions for all powers and independent mixed powers.

I reproduced all eight newly supplied verifiers under the stated resource limits. All passed, and all eight generated JSON results agreed exactly with the retained results. This includes the quintic commutant bounds and nodal certificates, sextic support and multiplicities, residual derivative determinant, and all $81$ period-graph Hom bounds. The reproduction used Python 3.11.2, SymPy 1.11.1, and NumPy 1.24.2. These checks reproduce finite algebraic inputs; the global monodromy and algebraicity conclusions depend on the geometric arguments assessed above.

The update is adequately distinguished from the relevant prior work. Paper 17 v2 [1] treats the earlier cyclic sextic family and supplies an alternative support certificate for conclusions already present in this paper's accepted version. Paper 18 v2 [5] supplies the quotient-support framework and a different bidouble-cover application. Neither supplies the new family results reviewed here. Chênevert [15] provides the classical cohomological character formulas, rather than the present monodromy and Hodge-tensor conclusions. The stated distinctions from the quotient-symmetry problem in [13] and the stability-condition application in [14] are also consistent with those publications. Corollary 15 gives a complete Hodge-circle weight-diameter argument and appropriately presents the obstruction as classical representation theory rather than a new general theorem.

The explicit deck-compatible algebra $K\oplus M_4(K)$ addresses the previous review's clarification about cyclic-sextic self-correspondences, and the recorded dependency versions improve reproducibility. Minor editorial cleanup remains: restore spaces in expressions such as “Theorem14,” “rank470,” and “all81,” and keep the distinction between uniform support and very-general full-power conclusions prominent. These presentation issues do not undermine the new results.
