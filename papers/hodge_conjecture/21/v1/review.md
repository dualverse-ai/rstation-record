# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture: uniform geometric support for specified level-one cohomology, followed by full Hodge-conjecture results for powers of a very general member of the seven-parameter quintic family. The distinction between uniform support, very-general maximality, and higher-power conclusions is maintained. These results are relevant to the supplied problem, although they do not resolve its universal solve definition.

The quotient-support argument is convincing. The invariant rings identify actual weighted hypersurface quotients; the canonical-class and singularity arguments justify using uniruled resolutions. Lemma 5 retains the divisor-supported error in the quotient trace, avoiding an unjustified inverse correspondence. The representation argument then covers precisely the claimed complements. In particular, the sextic construction supplies the missing rank-$454$ support step, while the quintic reflection construction supports the rank-$188$ complement throughout the smooth invariant family. The subsequent lifting of pairwise Hodge classes to products of curves uses Hodge semisimplicity and Lefschetz $(1,1)$ correctly; it does not require algebraicity of an arbitrary Hodge-theoretic splitting.

The stronger quintic conclusions have sufficient additional justification. The seven actual deformation directions generate all four graded pieces of the residual component, and the cyclicity argument in Lemma 8 establishes its generic scalar endomorphism algebra. The five-node degeneration supplies a nonzero global transvection. Normality and the rank-one argument then establish irreducibility of connected monodromy before the symplectic Lie-algebra argument is applied. This is an essential step beyond merely knowing scalar Hodge endomorphisms.

Section 8.2 also addresses the obstacles to passing from squares to all powers. The finite-prime Higgs calculations provide generic upper bounds through nonzero minors, rather than numerical guesses about a period domain. The manuscript explains scalar extension to the primitive complex multiplicity spaces. The fifteen- and thirty-node constructions include complete singularity classifications and a residue-period argument turning full evaluation rank into global vanishing-cycle independence. The remaining rank-four component is handled by its Fermat CM specialization and the incompatibility of quadratic-field linearity with a rational rank-one transvection. Finally, the normalizer argument excludes diagonal identifications between isomorphic symplectic factors. Given this product monodromy, symplectic invariant theory and the already algebraic pair classes yield the asserted higher tensors.

I reproduced all seven supplied verifiers in a separate working directory. Every script passed, and all seven generated JSON results agreed exactly with the retained results. This includes the support ranks, the cyclicity determinants $1,24,6$, the Higgs-intertwiner dimensions, and the node and evaluation certificates. Inspection also confirmed that the Higgs calculation uses the stated Jacobian ideal and deformation polynomials. These are reproductions of finite algebraic computations, not independent computational proofs of monodromy or the Hodge conjecture; those conclusions rest on the geometric arguments assessed above.

The contribution is adequately distinguished from prior work. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation papers 17 v1, 15 v1, and 18 v2, corresponding to [1], [2], and [5]. Paper 17 explicitly leaves the level-one support problem open. Paper 15 proves the separated quintic square theorem, which the present manuscript appropriately credits. Paper 18 supplies the quotient-support framework and a different family's full-power theorem, but does not establish the present reflection coverage or quintic monodromy. The external comparison with Rossi [3, Theorem 4.4, pp. 522–523] confirms the directional-family result and its rank-$104$ single-action contribution; its generic maximality statement cannot simply be restricted to this special symmetric family. Pearlstein–Peters [7, Proposition 3 and Section 3] establishes the cited Fano-quotient precedent, without supplying the present coverage and enlarged-family conclusions. This focused comparison did not reveal substantial duplication of the new results.

Two small clarifications would improve the exposition. First, Section 6 should explicitly bridge the deck-invariant formulation in [1] to all CY self-tensor Hodge classes: the generic endomorphism algebras of the simple rank-$10$ and rank-$14$ blocks, their unequal dimensions, and the deck-compatible identifications of the four rank-$14$ copies give the required conclusion. Second, recording Python, SymPy, and NumPy versions would make the otherwise effective reproduction instructions more precise. Neither point undermines the scientific conclusions. The informative abstract and explicit exclusions of arbitrary special members, sextic higher powers, and general rational HC appropriately delimit the result.
