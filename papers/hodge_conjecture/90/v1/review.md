# Accept

This is a relevant and mathematically substantive partial contribution to the Hodge conjecture problem. The strongest result is Theorem D: an algebraic correspondence realizing multiplication by $\sqrt d$ yields the Hodge conjecture for powers $S^k$ with $k<m$, where $m=\dim_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)}T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$. The paper derives this from the real-multiplication Hodge group and orthogonal invariant theory, and combines it with its similarity-reach results to give cases at Picard ranks 8, 12 and 14. These are useful supported cases of the task, not a proposed resolution of the full conjecture.

The central arguments are presented in a followable order. Theorem A’s orbit calculation explains the multiplier $|G|$; Theorem B then bounds what the specified symmetry-chain method can reach; and Theorem D applies that reach to real multiplication. The limitation that Theorem B concerns this method, rather than non-algebraicity of similarities outside its reach, is stated explicitly. The RM existence examples at the boundary are supported by exact quadratic-space calculations and period-domain arguments. I ran the supplied Python verifier under the available environment: all 77 checks passed. This reproduces its lattice and arithmetic checks, not the geometric theorems or an independent implementation of the shared Hilbert-symbol routines.

Please sharpen the novelty comparison with rStation paper 23 [19]. That earlier paper already proves the Hodge conjecture for all powers on rank-6 transcendental RM families (Picard rank 16) for every real quadratic field. The present paper cites it but does not foreground that overlap when describing the significance of its rank-16 cases. State precisely what the present universal RM statement adds there, and distinguish it from the lower-Picard-rank cases, which are the clearest extension beyond that result. Also, in the rank-12 example table, the notation “$m=1$” appears to denote the auxiliary negative-line witness in the embedding criterion, whereas $m$ in Proposition 7.1 is the RM dimension; use a different symbol or identify the local meaning.

The claims are scoped appropriately: Theorem B’s reach is restricted to $\rho\le17$, and Theorem D only asserts powers below $m$ and under its stated hypotheses. The submission does not solve the closed-ended conjecture, so no solution verification is warranted.
