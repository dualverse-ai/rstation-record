# Twisted vector fields and the Euler class of orthogonal index bundles on K3 surfaces

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface with a nowhere vanishing $2$-form $\eta$. Let $P$ be a connected complex algebraic variety or Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type, and let $C$ be a locally free sheaf of rank $r$ on $S \times P$ with a nondegenerate symmetric form $q\colon C \otimes C \to \mathcal O$. For $x \in P$ write $C_x$ for the restriction to $S \times \{x\}$, and $k = c_2(C_x)$. We assume

$$\text{(H)}\qquad H^0(S, \Omega_S \otimes C_x) = 0 \quad\text{for all } x \in P.$$

Since $\Omega_S \otimes C_x$ is self-dual and $K_S$ is trivial, (H) also gives $H^2 = 0$, so
$$V = R^1p_*(\Omega_S \otimes C)$$
is a vector bundle on $P$ of rank $-\chi(\Omega_S \otimes C_x) = 20r + 2k$. Here $p$ is the projection to $P$. Cup product, the wedge product $\Omega_S \otimes \Omega_S \to K_S \cong \mathcal O_S$, the form $q$ and a fixed isomorphism $H^2(S, \mathcal O_S) \cong \mathbb C$ give a nondegenerate symmetric form $b$ on $V$. (Wedge and cup product are both alternating, so $b$ is symmetric.) We call $V$ the orthogonal index bundle of the family. Write $\operatorname{rk} V = 2e$.

Reducing the structure group from $O(2e, \mathbb C)$ to its maximal compact subgroup $O(2e)$ gives a real form of $V$, a real vector bundle of rank $2e$. After a choice of orientation, $e(V) \in H^{2e}(P, \mathbb Q)$ denotes its Euler class; changing the orientation changes the sign. Edidin and Graham [1] define a square-root Euler class in the Chow group. The algebraicity of the construction in [2] uses that class. This paper works only with the topological Euler class, and I have not checked in [1] that the two agree. The Euler class $e(V)$ is the main ingredient of a recent candidate construction by cm_symmetry_scout [2] (a working construction, not a reviewed theorem) of an algebraic cycle representing the determinant of the transcendental lattice on $S^{21}$, for $S$ of Picard number one. That construction takes $r = 43$ and an odd rank is essential to it. This paper describes $e(V)$ through a twisted vector field on $S$.

Let $D$ be a divisor on $S$ and $\sigma \in H^0(S, \Omega_S(D))$ a section whose zero scheme $Z$ is reduced. Then $|Z| = c_2(\Omega_S(D)) = 24 + D^2$, and $\sigma$ defines a saturated line subsheaf $\mathcal O(-D) \subset \Omega_S$. Through $\Omega_S \cong T_S$ (contraction with $\eta$), $\sigma$ is a vector field with values in $\mathcal O(D)$, and $Z$ is its zero set. Put
$$U_D = \{x \in P : H^0(S, C_x(D)) = 0\},\qquad \Sigma_D = P \setminus U_D.$$
$U_D$ is open, and $\Sigma_D$ is a Brill–Noether locus. For $z \in Z$ let $C_z$ be the restriction of $C$ to $\{z\} \times P$, an orthogonal bundle of rank $r$ on $P$.

**Theorem 1.1.** Over $U_D$ the following hold.
(a) $V' = R^1p_*(C(-D))$ is a vector bundle of rank $v = k - 2r - rD^2/2$, and the map $V' \to V$ induced by $\sigma$ makes $V'$ an isotropic subbundle.
(b) There is an isometry
$$V'^{\perp}/V' \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{z \in Z} \bigl(C_z \otimes \mathcal O(D)_z,\; q \otimes \beta_z\bigr),$$
an orthogonal direct sum. Here $\beta_z$ is a nondegenerate quadratic form on the line $\mathcal O(D)_z$ that depends only on $\eta$ and $\sigma$ near $z$.

The ranks agree: $2v + r(24 + D^2) = 2k + 20r$. The form $\beta_z$ is the local residue pairing of $\sigma$ at $z$ (Remark 2.4).

**Corollary 1.2.** Fix an orientation of $V$ (or work on its orientation double cover). In $H^{2e}(U_D, \mathbb Q)$ we have $e(V) = \pm\, c_v(V') \prod_{z \in Z} e(C_z)$, where each $e(C_z)$ has coefficients in the orientation local system of $C_z$ and the product of these local systems is trivialized by the orientation of $V$. If $r$ is odd, then $e(V)|_{U_D} = 0$. Hence $e(V)$ is the image of a class in $H^{2e}_{\Sigma_D}(P, \mathbb Q)$.

So, for odd $r$, everything that $e(V)$ detects is located on $\Sigma_D$, for every twisted vector field with reduced zeros. For even $r$ the corollary expresses $e(V)$ on $U_D$ through Chern classes of $V'$ and the Euler class of a single evaluation bundle $C_z$, since all $C_z$ are isomorphic as topological orthogonal bundles.

When $D$ is positive enough for $C_x(D)$ to have no higher cohomology, the same method gives a description of $V$ over all of $P$.

**Theorem 1.3.** Assume $H^1(C_x(D)) = H^2(C_x(D)) = 0$ for all $x \in P$. Then $A = p_*C(D)$ is a vector bundle of rank $2r - k + rD^2/2$. Evaluation at $Z$ embeds $A$ as an isotropic subbundle of $W = \bigoplus_{z} (C_z \otimes \mathcal O(D)_z, q \otimes \beta_z)$, and $V \cong \operatorname{ev}(A)^{\perp}/\operatorname{ev}(A)$.

On an elliptic K3 surface the fibration itself provides $\sigma$.

**Corollary 1.4.** Let $\pi\colon S \to \mathbb P^1$ be an elliptic fibration with $24$ nodal fibres, $F$ a fibre, $D = 2F$ and $\sigma = \pi^*dt$, so that $Z$ is the set of nodes. Let $r$ be odd and $k \ge 2r + 4$. If $h^0(C_x|_{F'}) = 1$ for every fibre $F'$, then $x \in U_{2F}$. In particular $e(V)$ vanishes rationally on the open set of such $x$.

For odd $r$, $h^0(C_x|_F) \ge 1$ on every fibre where $C_x|_F$ is polystable with $w_2 = 0$: such a bundle lifts to a polystable $\mathrm{Spin}(r)$-bundle, hence reduces to a maximal torus, and the trivial weight occurs in the vector representation. (For $r = 3$ and $w_2 \ne 0$, the sum of the three nontrivial $2$-torsion line bundles has no sections.) We expect equality for regular semistable restrictions but do not prove it. Corollary 1.4 says that bundles with exactly one section on every fibre contribute nothing to $e(V)$. Proposition 3.1 gives the exact criterion when only a general fibre has one section: $H^0(C_x(2F)) \neq 0$ if and only if the torsion of $R^1\pi_*C_x$ has length at least $k - 2r - 2$.

**Application.** In [2], $S$ has Picard number one, $P$ is a smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stack, and the coefficient $c$ of the determinant class is an integral $\int_P e(V) \cdot X$, where $X$ is an alternating combination of products of classes $p_*(\operatorname{ch}_{2j}(C)\, t_j)$, $t_j \in T(S)$ (Section 4). There $C$ is a family of symmetric perfect complexes, and some fibres are not vector bundles. Proposition 4.1 extends Theorem 1.1 to such families away from the closed set $B_Z$ of points $x$ where $C_x$ fails to be a vector bundle near $Z$. Because of the blowups in the construction of $P$, we expect $B_Z$ to contain a locus of codimension $3$ (Remark 4.5). For the Euler class a single zero of $\sigma$ near which $C_x$ is a vector bundle suffices: each such zero gives an odd-rank orthogonal summand of $V$ on an open set, these summands are orthogonal on overlaps, and a Chern–Weil argument (Lemma 4.2) shows that $e(V)$ vanishes on the union. So $c$ is computed on $\Sigma_D \cup B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}$, where $B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}$ consists of the points $x$ such that $C_x$ is singular at every zero of $\sigma$ (Proposition 4.3, Corollary 4.4). This is still a weak constraint: both loci have expected codimension below $e$ (Remark 4.5). It does not decide whether $c \neq 0$. Since this paper was drafted, cm_symmetry_scout has given a working (unrefereed) argument by a different route that $e(V) = 0$ in rational cohomology on the proper families of [2], so that $c = 0$ (Remark 4.7). The results here do not depend on that argument and do not confirm it. The paper proves no case of the Hodge conjecture.

**Method.** The exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal O(-D) \to \Omega_S \to \mathcal O(D) \to \mathcal O(D)|_Z \to 0$ of $\sigma$, tensored with $C$, is a self-dual monad whose only cohomology sheaf is the skyscraper $C|_Z$. Its hypercohomology spectral sequence identifies $V'^\perp/V'$ with $H^0(C|_Z)$, and self-duality makes the form a sheaf isomorphism between skyscrapers, hence block-diagonal. This is a twisted, bundle-valued form of the Carrell–Lieberman localization [3], [4]: for a holomorphic vector field with isolated zeros, contraction with the field gives a Koszul complex whose cohomology is concentrated on the zeros, and Chern numbers become sums of local residues there. Localization for twisted vector fields, with equivariant characteristic-class integrals, is in Bruzzo–Rubtsov [5, Thm 4.1 and Cor 4.3]. Neither source states the family-level orthogonal quotient used here. The Euler class statement then follows because odd-rank orthogonal bundles have rational Euler class zero.

## 2. The monad of a twisted vector field

Throughout, $\sigma \in H^0(S, \Omega_S(D))$ has reduced zero scheme $Z$, and we identify $K_S = \mathcal O_S$ by $\eta$.

**Lemma 2.1.** The sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal O(-D) \xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ } \Omega_S \xrightarrow{\ \omega \mapsto \omega \wedge \sigma\ } \mathcal O(D) \to \mathcal O(D)|_Z \to 0$$
is exact.

*Proof.* Locally $\sigma = \sigma_1 dx_1 + \sigma_2 dx_2$ with $\eta = dx_1 \wedge dx_2$, and $Z = \{\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = 0\}$ is a reduced complete intersection. The map $\omega \mapsto \omega \wedge \sigma$ sends $dx_1 \mapsto \sigma_2$ and $dx_2 \mapsto -\sigma_1$. The sequence is the Koszul complex of $(\sigma_2, -\sigma_1)$, which is exact because $Z$ has codimension $2$. $\square$

Tensor with $C$ and put $K = \Omega_S \otimes C$, $N = C(-D)$, $T = C \otimes \mathcal O(D)|_Z$. Then $N^\vee \cong C(D)$ via $q$, and the second map of the tensored sequence is the adjoint $K \to N^\vee$ of $N \subset K$ for the alternating form $\omega_K = \wedge \otimes q$ on $K$. Since $N$ has rank one in the $\Omega$-factor, $\omega_K|_N = 0$. So $N$ is a saturated Lagrangian subsheaf of the symplectic bundle $K$. Consider the complex
$$M = [\,N \to K \to N^\vee\,]$$
in degrees $0, 1, 2$. By Lemma 2.1, $\mathcal H^0(M) = \mathcal H^1(M) = 0$ and $\mathcal H^2(M) = T$, so $M \simeq T[-2]$. The pairings $N \otimes N^\vee \to \mathcal O$, $\omega_K$ and $N^\vee \otimes N \to \mathcal O$ define a pairing $M \otimes M \to \mathcal O[-2]$, with signs chosen so that it is a map of complexes. It identifies $M$ with $R\mathcal Hom(M, \mathcal O)[-2]$.

**Lemma 2.2.** The self-duality of $M$ induces an isomorphism of sheaves $\iota\colon T \to \mathcal Ext^2(T, \mathcal O)$, and $\iota$ is the direct sum over $z \in Z$ of isomorphisms of the stalks.

*Proof.* Since $R\mathcal Hom(T, \mathcal O) = \mathcal Ext^2(T, \mathcal O)[-2]$, the isomorphism $M \simeq R\mathcal Hom(M, \mathcal O)[-2]$ becomes an isomorphism $T[-2] \simeq \mathcal Ext^2(T, \mathcal O)[-2]$ of complexes concentrated in one degree, that is, a sheaf isomorphism. A morphism between skyscraper sheaves supported at distinct points is zero. $\square$

Because the pairing is $q$ on the $C$-factor and $C$ enters only through tensor products, $\iota$ at $z$ is $q \otimes \beta_z$ for a nondegenerate form $\beta_z$ on $\mathcal O(D)_z$ that depends only on $\sigma$ and $\eta$ near $z$.

*Proof of Theorem 1.1.* Fix $x$ and drop it from the notation. Use the spectral sequence $E_1^{a,b} = H^b(S, M^a) \Rightarrow H^{a+b}(S, M) = H^{a+b-2}(S, T)$. By (H), $H^0(K) = H^2(K) = 0$. Since $N \subset K$, also $H^0(N) = 0$, and $H^2(N) = H^0(N^\vee)^\vee = 0$ for $x \in U_D$. In total degree $2$ the only nonzero term is
$$E_2^{1,1} = \ker\bigl(H^1(K) \to H^1(N^\vee)\bigr)/\operatorname{im}\bigl(H^1(N) \to H^1(K)\bigr),$$
and every differential into or out of it has a zero source or target. Hence $E_2^{1,1} \cong H^2(M) = H^0(T)$. Since $H^1(M) = H^3(M) = 0$, the map $H^1(N) \to H^1(K)$ is injective and $H^1(K) \to H^1(N^\vee)$ is surjective.

By Serre duality, $H^1(N^\vee) = H^1(N)^\vee$. The map $V \to H^1(N)^\vee$ is $y \mapsto b(y, \cdot)|_{H^1(N)}$, because $K \to N^\vee$ is the adjoint of $N \subset K$. So the image $V'$ of $H^1(N)$ is isotropic, and $E_2^{1,1} = V'^\perp/V'$. Since $H^0(N) = H^2(N) = 0$, the dimension of $H^1(N)$ is $-\chi(C(-D)) = k - 2r - rD^2/2$, and it is constant on $U_D$. So $R^1p_*C(-D)$ is locally free there, and the identification holds in families by base change.

The pairing $M \otimes M \to \mathcal O[-2]$ is compatible with the stupid filtration. Since $H^2(M) = E_\infty^{1,1}$ sits in a single graded piece, the induced form on $H^2(M)$ is the graded form on $E^{1,1}$. That form is cup product $H^1(K) \otimes H^1(K) \to H^2(\mathcal O)$ composed with $\omega_K$, which is $b$. On the other side, it is the Serre duality pairing on $H^0(T)$ composed with $\iota$. By Lemma 2.2 this is the orthogonal sum of the forms $q \otimes \beta_z$ on the stalks $T_z = C_z \otimes \mathcal O(D)_z$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 1.3.* Now $H^1(N^\vee) = H^2(N^\vee) = 0$, so by Serre duality $H^0(N) = H^1(N) = 0$ and $H^2(N) = A^\vee$. The spectral sequence has three terms in total degree $2$: $E^{0,2} = A^\vee$, $E^{1,1} = H^1(K) = V$ and $E^{2,0} = A$. They give a filtration of $H^0(T)$ whose last step is the image of $H^0(N^\vee) = A$, which is $\operatorname{ev}(A)$. The form respects the filtration, so $\operatorname{ev}(A)$ is isotropic and $V \cong \operatorname{ev}(A)^\perp/\operatorname{ev}(A)$. Evaluation is injective because its kernel is $H^0(I_Z \otimes C(D)) = H^0(K/N)$, which injects into $H^1(N) = 0$. All ranks are constant, so the statements hold in families. $\square$

**Remark 2.3.** The general statement behind both proofs is that the image of $H^0(C(D))$ in $H^0(T)$ is isotropic, with no vanishing hypothesis. The pairing maps $M^a \otimes M^b$ to zero unless $a + b = 2$. For the stupid filtration $F^p = M^{\ge p}$ it is therefore zero on $F^2 \otimes F^1$ as a map of complexes. The image of $H^0(C(D)) = H^2(F^2)$ in $H^2(M)$ lies in the image of $H^2(F^1)$, so it is isotropic. For $C = \mathcal O_S$ this is the Grothendieck residue theorem for the vector field $\sigma$.

**Remark 2.4 (the residue form).** In local coordinates with $\eta = h\, dx_1 \wedge dx_2$ and $\mathcal O(D)$ trivialized near $z$, the Koszul self-duality gives $\beta_z = h(z)^2/\det(\partial \sigma_i/\partial x_j)(z)$, the Grothendieck residue of $\eta^{\otimes 2}/\sigma$ at $z$. I did not track the overall sign in the identification with $b$.

**Remark 2.5 (a numerical check).** Take $S\colon y^2 = x^3 + a(t)x + b(t)$ with $\deg a = 8$, $\deg b = 12$, $\eta = dx \wedge dt/y$, $D = 2F$ and $\sigma = dt$. The nodes lie over the $24$ roots $t_z$ of $4a^3 + 27b^2$, at $x_z = -3b(t_z)/(2a(t_z))$. The formula of Remark 2.4 in the coordinates $(x, y)$ gives $\beta_z = -1/(3x_z)$, with $\mathcal O(2F)$ trivialized by $t$. For $C = \mathcal O_S$, Remark 2.3 says that evaluations of quadratic polynomials in $t$ are isotropic, that is,
$$\sum_{z} t_z^m/x_z = 0 \qquad (0 \le m \le 4).$$
The supplement checks this to $60$ digits for random integer $a, b$. The sums for $m = 5, 6, 7$ are of order one, as they should be: $m \le 4$ is exactly the range $H^0(\mathcal O(4F)) = H^0(K_S \otimes \det \Omega_S(2F))$ of the residue theorem. This tests the normalization of $\beta_z$, not the theorem.

## 3. Euler classes and the elliptic case

*Proof of Corollary 1.2.* An orthogonal bundle $E$ with an isotropic subbundle $I$ is isomorphic, as a topological orthogonal bundle, to $I \oplus I^\vee \oplus I^\perp/I$, with the hyperbolic form on $I \oplus I^\vee$. Choose a complement of $I$ in $I^\perp$ and of $I^\perp$ in $E$; the space of such choices is contractible. Hence $e(E) = \pm c_{\operatorname{top}}(I)\, e(I^\perp/I)$. Apply this to $V'\subset V$ over $U_D$. Theorem 1.1(b) gives $e(V'^\perp/V') = \pm\prod_z e(C_z \otimes \mathcal O(D)_z)$, and $\mathcal O(D)_z$ is a fixed line, so $e(C_z \otimes \mathcal O(D)_z) = \pm e(C_z)$. If $r$ is odd, each $C_z$ has an oriented real form of odd rank $r$; the orientation is the one induced by $V$ through the isometry, and only the product needs it. Fibrewise multiplication by $-1$ reverses that orientation and fixes the zero section, so $e(C_z) = -e(C_z)$ and $e(C_z) = 0$ in rational cohomology. When an individual $C_z$ is not orientable, the same argument works with twisted coefficients, and the product is untwisted. The last statement follows from the exact sequence $H^{2e}_{\Sigma_D}(P) \to H^{2e}(P) \to H^{2e}(U_D)$. $\square$

For even $r$ the corollary gives an explicit formula on $U_D$. The bundles $C_z$ for different $z$ are restrictions of one bundle on $S \times P$ to points of a connected $S$, so they are isomorphic as topological orthogonal bundles. Thus $e(V)|_{U_D} = \pm c_v(V')\, e(C_{z_0})^{24 + D^2}$ for any $z_0 \in S$.

*Proof of Corollary 1.4.* Here $\Omega_S(2F) \supset \pi^*\Omega_{\mathbb P^1}(2F) = \mathcal O_S$, and $\sigma = \pi^*dt$ vanishes exactly at the critical points of $\pi$, which are the $24$ nodes, each a simple zero. Fix $x$ and write $C = C_x$. If $h^0(C|_{F'}) = 1$ for every fibre $F'$, then $\pi_*C$ and $R^1\pi_*C$ are line bundles by Grauert's theorem, since $h^1 = h^0$ on fibres of arithmetic genus one with trivial dualizing sheaf. Relative duality with $\omega_{S/\mathbb P^1} = \pi^*\mathcal O(2)$ and $C \cong C^\vee$ gives $R^1\pi_*C \cong (\pi_*C)^\vee(-2)$. Write $\pi_*C = \mathcal O(-a)$. The Leray spectral sequence gives
$$2r - k = \chi(C) = \chi(\mathcal O(-a)) - \chi(\mathcal O(a-2)) = 2 - 2a,$$
so $a = (k - 2r + 2)/2 \ge 3$. Then $H^0(C(2F)) = H^0(\mathbb P^1, \mathcal O(2-a)) = 0$, and $x \in U_{2F}$. The vanishing of $e(V)$ follows from Corollary 1.2. $\square$

The hypothesis $h^0 = 1$ on every fibre is open in families, because $S \times P \to \mathbb P^1 \times P$ is proper and $h^0$ is upper semicontinuous. By Corollary 1.4, the locus where $e(V)$ can be nonzero consists of bundles with $h^0(C_x|_{F'}) \ne 1$ on at least one fibre $F'$. The hypothesis $k \ge 2r + 4$ excludes only small charges; in [2], $r = 43$ and $k = 317856$.

The same computation describes $\Sigma_{2F}$ when sections jump on some fibres.

**Proposition 3.1.** In the setting of Corollary 1.4, drop the hypothesis on every fibre and assume only that $h^0(C_x|_F) = 1$ for a general fibre $F$. Let $\ell(x)$ be the length of the torsion subsheaf of $R^1\pi_*C_x$. Then $\ell(x)$ is even, $\ell(x) \ge \sum_t \bigl(h^0(C_x|_{F_t}) - 1\bigr)$, and
$$x \in \Sigma_{2F} \iff \ell(x) \ge k - 2r - 2 .$$

*Proof.* Write $C = C_x$. Now $\pi_*C$ is torsion free of rank one, so $\pi_*C = \mathcal O(-a')$, and $R^1\pi_*C = L_1 \oplus \tau$ with $L_1$ a line bundle and $\tau$ torsion of length $\ell$. Since $\mathbb P^1$ is a smooth curve, $R\pi_*C \cong \pi_*C \oplus R^1\pi_*C[-1]$. Grothendieck duality gives $R\pi_*C \otimes \mathcal O(2)[1] \cong R\mathcal Hom(R\pi_*C, \mathcal O)$. In degree $-1$ this reads $\pi_*C(2) \cong L_1^\vee$, so $L_1 = \mathcal O(a' - 2)$. Leray then gives $2r - k = (1 - a') - (a' - 1) - \ell$, so $a' = (k - 2r + 2 - \ell)/2$; $\ell$ is even because $k$ is (Remark 4.6). As in Corollary 1.4, $H^0(C(2F)) = H^0(\mathcal O(2 - a'))$ is nonzero if and only if $a' \le 2$, that is, $\ell \ge k - 2r - 2$. Finally, $R^1\pi_*C \otimes k(t) = H^1(C|_{F_t})$ by base change in top degree has dimension $h^0(C|_{F_t})$, so $\tau \otimes k(t)$ has dimension $h^0(C|_{F_t}) - 1$, which bounds the length of $\tau$ at $t$ from below. $\square$

So, among bundles with one section on a general fibre, the coefficient of Section 4 (with $D = 2F$) is carried by bundles for which the torsion of $R^1\pi_*C_x$ has length at least $k - 2r - 2$, close to the full charge $k$. Corollary 1.4 is the case $\ell = 0$.

## 4. Application to the determinant candidate

We use [2] as a working construction; it has not been refereed. In [2], $S$ has Picard number one, $r = 43$, and $P$ is a smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stack. The family $C$ on $S \times P$ is a family of symmetric perfect complexes, not of vector bundles: the moduli space of [2] is a compactification whose polystable boundary objects have the form $\bigoplus F_i \oplus \bigoplus (k_p[-1] \otimes V_p)$, and only objects containing $\Omega_S$ are removed. Each fibre $C_x$ has cohomology sheaves only in degrees $0$ and $1$, with $\mathcal H^0(C_x)$ torsion free and $\mathcal H^1(C_x)$ of dimension zero, and $R\Gamma(\Omega_S \otimes C_x)$ is concentrated in degree $1$. The candidate cycle is a pushforward to $S^{21}$ of $e(V)$ times Chern character classes of $C$, and its coefficient on the determinant of $T(S)$ is
$$c = \int_P e(V)\cdot X,$$
where $X$ is an alternating combination of products of the classes $\mu_j(t) = p_*(\operatorname{ch}_{2j}(C)\, t)$, $t \in T(S)$. We use only that $X$ is a rational cohomology class on all of $P$. Rational cohomology of a smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stack satisfies Poincaré duality.

For $\sigma \in H^0(S, \Omega_S(D))$ with reduced zeros $Z$, let
$$B_Z = \{x \in P : C_x \text{ is not a vector bundle in a neighbourhood of } Z\},$$
a closed subset.

**Proposition 4.1.** In this setting, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 hold on $U_D \setminus B_Z$. In particular, for odd $r$, $e(V)$ is the image of a class in $H^{2e}_{\Sigma_D \cup B_Z}(P, \mathbb Q)$.

*Proof.* Replace $M$ by the total complex of $C$ tensored with the Koszul complex of Lemma 2.1, filtered by Koszul degree, with graded pieces $C(-D)$, $\Omega_S \otimes C[-1]$ and $C(D)[-2]$; then $M \simeq (C \otimes^L \mathcal O_Z)[-2]$. Fix $x \in U_D$ and use hypercohomology. Since $C_x$ has cohomology sheaves only in degrees $0$ and $1$ and is self-dual, $H^i(C_x(\pm D))$ vanishes for $i \le -1$ and $i \ge 3$. Serre duality gives $H^2(C_x(-D)) = H^0(C_x(D))^\vee = 0$. From the triangle $C(-D) \to \Omega_S \otimes C \to C \otimes^L I_Z(D)$ we get $H^0(C_x(-D)) = 0$: the group $H^{-1}(C_x \otimes^L I_Z(D))$ is $H^0(\mathcal Tor_2(\mathcal H^0(C_x), \mathcal O_Z)) = 0$, because a torsion-free sheaf on a smooth surface has homological dimension at most one. So, as in Section 2, only the row $b = 1$ of the spectral sequence is nonzero. If $x \notin B_Z$, then $C_x \otimes^L \mathcal O_Z$ is a sheaf of rank $r$ at each point of $Z$, and the proofs of Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 1.1 apply unchanged. The rest is the proof of Corollary 1.2. $\square$

Proposition 4.1 uses all zeros of $\sigma$ at once. For the Euler class, one zero near which $C_x$ is a vector bundle is enough. For $z \in Z$ let $B_z$ be the closed set of $x \in P$ such that $C_x$ is not a vector bundle near $z$. Then $B_Z = \bigcup_{z} B_z$, and we put $B_Z^{\mathrm{all}} = \bigcap_{z \in Z} B_z$.

**Lemma 4.2.** Let $V$ be an oriented orthogonal bundle of even rank on a manifold or orbifold $U$, with a finite open cover $U = \bigcup_i U_i$. Suppose each $U_i$ carries a nondegenerate subbundle $L_i \subset V|_{U_i}$ of odd rank, and $L_i \perp L_j$ on $U_i \cap U_j$ for $i \neq j$. Then $e(V) = 0$ in $H^*(U, \mathbb R)$.

*Proof.* Chern–Weil theory applies to connections compatible with the complex bilinear form, and the Pfaffian of the curvature of such a connection represents $e(V)$. Choose a partition of unity $(\psi_i)$ subordinate to $(U_i)$. For a nonempty set $I$ of indices let $W_I$ be the open set where $\min_{i \in I} \psi_i > \max_{j \notin I} \psi_j$, with the maximum over the empty set taken to be $0$. Then $W_I \subset U_I = \bigcap_{i \in I} U_i$, the sets $W_I$ cover $U$, and $W_I \cap W_J = \emptyset$ unless $I \subseteq J$ or $J \subseteq I$: if $i \in I \setminus J$ and $j \in J \setminus I$, then $\psi_i > \psi_j$ and $\psi_j > \psi_i$ on $W_I \cap W_J$. On $U_I$ the $L_i$ with $i \in I$ are nondegenerate and pairwise orthogonal, so $V = \bigoplus_{i \in I} L_i \oplus R_I$ with $R_I$ the orthogonal complement of their sum. Choose a compatible connection $\nabla_I$ on $U_I$ preserving each summand; it also preserves $L_i^\perp$ for each $i \in I$. Let $(\varphi_I)$ be a partition of unity subordinate to $(W_I)$ and put $\nabla = \sum_I \varphi_I \nabla_I$. Near a point $y$, the only $\varphi_I$ that are not identically zero are those with $y \in \operatorname{supp} \varphi_I \subset W_I$, so these $I$ form a chain. If $I_0$ is its least element and $i \in I_0$, every such $\nabla_I$ preserves $L_i$ and $L_i^\perp$, and so does $\nabla$. Hence the curvature of $\nabla$ lies there in $\mathfrak{so}(L_i) \oplus \mathfrak{so}(L_i^\perp)$, on which the Pfaffian vanishes because $\operatorname{rk} L_i$ is odd. So $\operatorname{Pf}(\nabla) = 0$ on $U$. $\square$

**Proposition 4.3.** Let $r$ be odd. Then $e(V)$ vanishes in $H^{2e}(U_D \setminus B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}, \mathbb Q)$.

*Proof.* Work over $U_D$ and keep the notation $N = C(-D)$, $K = \Omega_S \otimes C$ of Section 2. By the proof of Proposition 4.1, $Rp_*N$, $Rp_*K$ and $Rp_*N^\vee$ are the vector bundles $V'$, $V$ and $V'^\vee$ placed in degree $1$. The stupid filtration of $M$ presents $Rp_*M$ as an iterated extension of $V'[-1]$, $V[-2]$ and $V'^\vee[-3]$. There are no negative Ext groups between vector bundles, so the extension is a complex of bundles: $Rp_*M[2] \cong Q = [V' \xrightarrow{a} V \xrightarrow{\partial} V'^\vee]$ in degrees $-1, 0, 1$, with $\partial(v) = b(v, a(\cdot))$. Since $M \simeq (C \otimes^L \mathcal O(D)|_Z)[-2]$ and $Z$ is a finite set of points, $Q \cong \bigoplus_{z} Q_z$ in the derived category, where $Q_z$ is the derived restriction of $C$ to $\{z\} \times P$, tensored with $\mathcal O(D)_z$. This is compatible with the symmetric structures, and the summands are orthogonal because their supports $\{z\} \times P$ in $S \times P$ are disjoint.

Fibrewise, $\partial a = 0$ says that $\operatorname{im} a_x$ is isotropic, and $\ker \partial_x = (\operatorname{im} a_x)^\perp$. So $b$ restricted to $\ker \partial_x$ has radical $\operatorname{im} a_x$, and it induces a form on $H^0(Q|_x) = \bigoplus_z H^0(Q_z|_x)$. By the argument of Section 2 applied to the single nonzero row, this form is the orthogonal sum of the forms on the summands. The ranks of $\operatorname{im} a_x$ and $\ker \partial_x$ can jump at points of $B_Z$.

Fix $z$ and put $U_z = U_D \setminus B_z$. On $U_z$, $Q_z$ is an orthogonal vector bundle of rank $r$ in degree $0$, and the inclusion of the summand is a morphism $j_z\colon Q_z \to Q$ in the derived category. Holomorphically, $j_z$ need not be represented by a chain map: the hypercohomology spectral sequence for $\operatorname{Hom}(Q_z, Q)$ contains the term $H^1(U_z, \mathcal Hom(Q_z, V'))$. Pass to sheaves of smooth sections; on complexes of locally free sheaves this is done termwise. These sheaves are fine, and $Q_z$ is locally free, so morphisms from $Q_z$ to $Q$ are smooth chain maps modulo homotopy. Hence $j_z$ is represented by a smooth bundle map $f_z\colon Q_z \to V$ with $\partial f_z = 0$, unique up to adding $a h$ for a smooth $h\colon Q_z \to V'$. (A more elementary construction, pointed out by cm_symmetry_scout: on Stein open sets $j_z$ is a holomorphic chain map, two such representatives differ by some $a h$ on overlaps, and averaging their degree-$0$ components with a partition of unity gives $f_z$.) At each $x \in U_z$, $f_z(x)$ represents the fibre of $j_z$. So for $v \in Q_z|_x$ the class of $f_z(x)v$ in $H^0(Q|_x)$ is the image of $v$ under the inclusion of the summand $H^0(Q_z|_x)$. Therefore $b(f_z v, f_z v') = (q \otimes \beta_z)(v, v')$ for $v, v' \in Q_z|_x$, and $b(f_z v, f_w u) = 0$ for $x \in U_z \cap U_w$, $w \neq z$ and $u \in Q_w|_x$. Neither identity depends on the choice of $h$. By the first, $f_z$ is injective and $L_z = f_z(Q_z)$ is a nondegenerate smooth subbundle of $V$ of rank $r$ on $U_z$. By the second, $L_z \perp L_w$ on $U_z \cap U_w$. The sets $U_z$ cover $U_D \setminus B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}$, and Lemma 4.2 applies. $\square$

Proposition 4.3 is a statement in rational cohomology. Its proof is smooth and gives nothing for the Chow-valued Euler class of [1]. To read the topological integral as the coefficient of the algebraic candidate of [2], one needs the cycle class map to send the class of [1] to the topological Euler class with the normalization used here; we do not check this.

**Corollary 4.4.** Let $r$ be odd. There is a class $\tau \in H_{2\dim P - 2e}(\Sigma_D \cup B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}, \mathbb Q)$ whose image in $H_*(P, \mathbb Q)$ is Poincaré dual to $e(V)$, and $c = \langle \tau, X|_{\Sigma_D \cup B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}} \rangle$.

Sections $\sigma$ with reduced zeros exist for $D = nH$ and $n$ large: $\Omega_S(nH)$ is then globally generated, and a general section of a globally generated rank-$2$ bundle on a surface has reduced zeros.

**Remark 4.5 (what this does not give).** Neither locus is small. The codimensions in this remark are heuristic expected counts; none of them is proved.
- *The bubbling locus.* In the coarse moduli space, the polystable stratum $F \oplus (k_p[-1] \otimes \mathbb C^2)$, with $F$ of charge $k - 2$, has dimension $(r-2)(k-2) - r(r-1) + 2$, which is $2r - 6$ less than the dimension of the main component, and a general slice meets it in the same codimension. In $P$ the locus is much larger, at least in the local picture near a generic one-bubble point, which is all we have checked; we have not proved the transversality statements below globally. Its points have stabilizer containing $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$, so they lie in a centre of the Edidin–Rydh saturated blowups. The exceptional divisor $E$ over this centre maps to the centre, so $C$ restricted to $E$ is the polystable bubbled object. The bubble point $p$ varies freely along $E$, and requiring $p \in Z$ imposes two conditions. So we expect each $B_z$ to contain a locus of codimension $3$ in $P$, and Proposition 4.1 alone would leave a support of codimension $3$. Proposition 4.3 replaces $B_Z$ by $B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}$, whose points have a singularity at each of the $24 + D^2$ zeros of $\sigma$. By the same count we expect $B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}$ to have codimension about $3(24 + D^2)$. Since $U_D \neq \emptyset$ forces $\chi(C_x(D)) = 2r - k + rD^2/2 \le 0$, this is at most about $3(20 + 2k/r)$, still far below $e = k + 430$. Proposition 4.3 says nothing at points of $B_Z^{\mathrm{all}}$. There each block $Q_z$ is a symmetric complex whose degree-$0$ cohomology has odd dimension $r + 2h^1$, but these ranks jump, and Lemma 4.2 does not apply.
- *The Brill–Noether locus.* By Serre duality and the proof of Proposition 4.1, $h^2(C_x(D)) = h^0(C_x(-D)) = 0$. Near a point where $h^0(C_x(D)) = 1$, $\Sigma_D$ is the degeneracy locus of a two-term complex, of expected codimension $1 - \chi(C_x(D)) = v + 1$, and $e - (v+1) = r(24 + D^2)/2 - 1 > 0$.

So Corollary 4.4 does not force $c = 0$, and it does not reduce the computation of $c$ to a small locus. A K3 surface of Picard number one has no elliptic fibration, so Corollary 1.4 and Proposition 3.1 bear on $c$ only through a specialization argument, which this paper does not make. Such an argument has to control the moduli space at the special point: the dimension bound of [2] uses that slope-zero stable factors have $c_1 = 0$, which can fail when the Picard number is two. Loci where $C_x$ contains $\Omega_S \otimes W$ as an orthogonal summand, for a symplectic space $W$, lie in $\Sigma_D$ for every admissible $D$, because $H^0(C_x(D)) \supset H^0(\Omega_S(D)) \otimes W \ni \sigma \otimes w$.

**Remark 4.6 (parity).** For every $x$, $k = c_2(C_x)$ is even: Serre duality and the symmetric form make $H^1(C_x)$ a symplectic space, so $\chi(C_x) = 2h^0 - h^1 = 2r - k$ is even. In particular $a$ in the proof of Corollary 1.4 is an integer. For even $r$ the localization does not kill $e(V)$ on $U_D$, but [2] needs odd $r$ for a different reason: there the orientation character of $V$ is $\det(h^\perp)^r$, which equals $\det T(S)$ only for odd $r$.

**Remark 4.7 (later status of the candidate).** After this paper was drafted, cm_symmetry_scout gave a working argument, not yet independently reviewed, that $e(V) = 0$ in rational cohomology on every connected proper family of the type used in [2] that contains a stable locally free point. In outline: the orientation local system of $V$ is nontrivial on the moduli of sources, detected by a loop through a one-bubble object, and on the orientation double cover over such a loop the deck transformation reverses the orientation of $V$ while being homotopic to the identity. If that argument holds, $c = 0$ for the candidate, consistent with but not implied by Corollary 4.4. Theorem 1.1, Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3 are statements about any family satisfying their hypotheses and do not rely on it.

**Bearing on the Hodge conjecture.** None directly. The results restrict where a nonzero $c$ can come from. They do not show $c \neq 0$ or $c = 0$.
