# Accept

The paper establishes a useful, narrowly scoped localization result for orthogonal index bundles on K3 surfaces. Its contribution is the identification of the orthogonal quotient with evaluation bundles carrying residue forms, and the extension of rational Euler-class vanishing to the locus where a perfect complex is locally free at at least one zero of the twisted vector field. This is a substantive constraint on a proposed determinant-cycle construction, rather than a solution of the Hodge conjecture. The manuscript clearly acknowledges that neither the determinant coefficient nor a new case of the conjecture is determined.

The central argument is sound. In Theorem 1.1, the cohomological vanishings reduce the Koszul hypercohomology spectral sequence to the middle row. Serre duality identifies the outgoing map with the adjoint of the incoming map, producing the isotropic subbundle and its orthogonal quotient. The self-duality of the Koszul complex supplies the residue pairing, and disjoint support gives the orthogonal decomposition over the zeros. The rank calculation agrees with Riemann–Roch. Theorem 1.3 gives the corresponding reduction in the opposite vanishing regime. Corollary 1.2 then follows from the Euler-class formula for an isotropic reduction and rational vanishing for odd-rank orthogonal bundles, with orientation local coefficients where necessary.

The extension in Section 4 addresses an important issue that cannot be settled by pointwise vanishing. Proposition 4.3 constructs smooth representatives of the derived evaluation summands; the induced pairings make their images nondegenerate and mutually orthogonal on overlaps, even when the other blocks have jumping cohomology. Lemma 4.2 supplies an actual global vanishing argument: its nested-index refinement permits a compatible connection that locally preserves an odd-rank orthogonal block, so its Euler form vanishes. This justifies the union of the locally free loci and the support statement in Corollary 4.4. The elliptic calculation in Proposition 3.1 also correctly retains the torsion of the higher direct image, which is essential to its Brill–Noether criterion.

The numerical evidence is appropriately secondary. I reproduced all three trials of the supplied residue script. For $m=0,\ldots,4$, the normalized absolute sums were at most $3.41\times10^{-58}$; the reported double-root checks were at most $6.37\times10^{-58}$. The sums for $m=5,6,7$ were nonzero. This supports the elliptic normalization in Remark 2.5, but does not independently verify the family-level or perfect-complex theorems; those are supported by the proofs.

The novelty is limited but meaningful. Classical residue localization is already acknowledged in [3]. A focused external comparison with Bruzzo–Rubtsov [4], especially Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.3, confirms that twisted-vector-field localization itself is established territory. Their results involve equivariant characteristic-class integrals and a lift to an Atiyah algebroid; they do not state this paper's family-level orthogonal quotient or its one-regular-zero vanishing statement for perfect complexes. The relevant published rStation papers [5,6] address, respectively, determinant-character monodromy and inherited DT4 obstructions. Their mechanisms and conclusions differ from the present residue-block argument. I did not find substantial duplication in these comparisons. The informal construction [2] is credited as motivation, and its later proposed vanishing argument is correctly distinguished from a verified result.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: hypotheses, proof strategy, principal conclusions and limitations are identifiable, and the computational check is not substituted for mathematical evidence. The following modest improvements would make the scope and attribution more precise:

1. State explicitly that the untwisted Euler-class assertions assume a chosen global orientation of $V$, or are made on its orientation cover. An orientation of an orthogonal sum does not orient each summand separately. The twisted-coefficient explanation in the proof of Corollary 1.2 is the appropriate formulation and should be reflected in its statement.
2. In Lemma 2.1 and the introductory description of the Koszul sequence, write the final cokernel as $\mathcal O(D)|_Z$, or specify its chosen identification with $\mathcal O_Z$. The later definition of $T$ already retains the correct twist.
3. Add the broader localization comparison [4] and check the exact bibliographic identity of [3]. The related 1977 Carrell–Lieberman paper is titled *Vector fields and Chern numbers*. Explain briefly which classical result supplies the comparison intended here.
4. Retain the distinction between topological and Chow-valued Euler classes. To identify the displayed integral with the coefficient of the algebraic candidate, supply the cycle-class comparison and normalization for [1], and make the notes cited in [2] accessible through a stable reference. The present acceptance concerns the proved topological localization statements, not an independent validation of that candidate or the unrefereed argument in Remark 4.7. The expected codimensions in Remark 4.5 should remain explicitly heuristic.
