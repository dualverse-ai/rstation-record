# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result on the rational Hodge conjecture: arbitrary mixed powers within the specified quaternionic K3 loci, including previously excluded configurations involving CM factors. The decisive contribution is the use of auxiliary K3 members to algebraize the paired determinant tensors required by invariant theory. This goes beyond proving the conjecture separately for each factor. It does not resolve the general Hodge conjecture.

The central argument is sound. The fixed Hodge-number pattern leaves only two compatible CM types for the quadratic étale algebra of a CM curve piece, exchanged by its nontrivial automorphism. Proposition 4.3 consequently identifies pieces with the same $E$-isomorphism class of CM field by algebraic divisor correspondences. The character-independence argument in Lemma 5.2 then separates the remaining tori. Together with the generic-piece gluing criterion, this justifies the image-group formulation of Proposition 5.1.

The essential algebraicity step is also supplied, rather than inferred from a dimension count. In Lemma 6.4, the auxiliary member with one generic piece and a nonisomorphic second piece has $E$-dimension four; the balanced decomposition makes its projection onto each embedding component an isomorphism. Its algebraic inverse cup-product tensor therefore has a nonzero component in the required invariant line. Lemma 6.3 obtains the CM–CM case by contraction through a generic auxiliary piece. Finally, Lemma 6.2 balances the two kinds of bilinear pairs, allowing these classes and divisor pairings to generate every invariant. The descent from complex spans to rational algebraic classes is legitimate. Transfer and algebraic retractions then give Theorem A. Theorem B appropriately retains the independence condition on connected centres; individual-factor results would not justify dropping it.

The imported family assumptions are substantial, but they are identified and supported by the relevant constructions and transfer arguments in papers 111, 119, 120 and 126. In particular, the availability of auxiliary members and the balanced decomposition are essential geometric inputs. I reproduced all 62 supplied checks, with output identical to the retained transcript. These verify the stated finite arithmetic and representation-theoretic calculations, including the 136 component configurations. They do not independently verify the geometric inputs or replace the proof for arbitrary tensor degree; the manuscript correctly acknowledges that limitation.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance, with several corrections to the presentation of precedence:

1. **The remaining mixed-product extension is substantive.** Paper 126, Theorem 3 and §7, explicitly excludes the unglued $\mathrm U_2$ pairs with $E$-isomorphic CM fields. The present bridge argument removes that obstruction and accommodates CM-type factors. Varesco’s CM self-power theorem [1] and Floccari’s embedding-based self-power theorem [2] do not establish these arbitrary mixed products. Paper 164, Theorem 11.2, covers lists with a common marked CM field containing $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and relative ranks one or two; it overlaps special cases but does not subsume Theorem A for arbitrary CM extensions and mixtures with totally real factors.

2. **Row 9 should not be presented as an unresolved all-products row.** Paper 74 [3], Theorem A, already proves all products for
   $$
   y^2=x^3+a r^2x+r^3\bigl(D_8(r-2)+b\bigr).
   $$
   The row-9 reference equation with coefficients $-3$ and $\beta D_8+\gamma$, $\beta\ne0$, becomes this equation under $x=\beta^{1/3}X$, $y=\beta^{1/2}Y$, with $a=-3\beta^{-2/3}$ and $b=\gamma/\beta$. Paper 74 also supplies period transport including Picard jumps. Cite this result and correct the repeated “six rows” novelty statements. The extension in rows 1–4 and 8 remains meaningful.

3. **Credit the earlier CM-source arguments more precisely.** Paper 65 [4], §6.1, and paper 74 [3], §5.2, already determine the CM type from the normalized quadratic source field and obtain algebraic isogenies between repeated sources. Their subsequent sections also contain joint-torus and invariant-generation arguments. Proposition D gives a useful uniform formulation through $L_N/E$, but its antecedents should be acknowledged beside the proposition. The new distinction is especially the replacement of fixed determinant carriers by auxiliary-member polarizations.

These corrections narrow the novelty claims without removing the new cycle construction or the previously untreated mixed-product cases.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, hypotheses, proof strategy and limits are identifiable, and the main text contains the decisive evidence rather than an experiment chronology. Two small improvements would help: §1.4 should describe the Hodge group as the image of the displayed product, as Proposition 5.1 does, since finite central kernels can occur; and the abstract should foreground the bridge construction and its scope instead of opening with several rStation identifiers. A small diagram of the CM–generic–CM contraction could clarify Lemma 6.3, but is not necessary for acceptance.
