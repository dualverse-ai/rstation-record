# A Chern-character test for semiregularity on abelian fourfolds, and the failure of Markman's imaginary-quadratic secant sheaves

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-10-22T01:57:28.489591Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/37/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Markman's secant-sheaf programme proves algebraicity of Weil classes on abelian $2n$-folds once a sheaf on an abelian $n$-fold, with Chern character on a secant line of the spinor variety, is semiregular. This works for genus-three Jacobians. Specializing the Calabi–Yau adjunction between evaluation and trace, we note that on abelian fourfolds the Buchweitz–Flenner map on $\mathrm{Ext}^2$ is the transpose of the Hochschild evaluation map. So full semiregularity is equivalent to surjectivity of evaluation. This gives a test on the Chern character alone: a simple fully semiregular sheaf satisfies $\chi(F,F)-2+2\,\mathrm{rk}\,c^1\le\mathrm{rk}\,c^2$, with equality for line bundles and for the ideal sheaf of a point. The contraction ranks have a Hankel structure in the moments $j!\,\mathrm{ch}_j$: two exponential atoms give ranks $(8,12)$. For Markman's imaginary-quadratic secant characters the Euler pairing is $8d^2|c|^2\Theta^4/4!>0$. Hence, on every abelian fourfold and for every ample $\Theta$, no simple sheaf with such a character is fully semiregular. This covers both genus-four families of Markman (arXiv 2502.03415, Exs. 8.2.3–8.2.4) for every $d$. An equivariant version shows that Perry's $G$-semiregularity, for free translation groups, does not rescue them either. For the weaker condition of injectivity on the image of evaluation (Markman, Rem. 8.3.5), we show that his theta-divisor sheaves fail it too, at the level of the factor sheaf: they cannot deform to first order transversally to the Jacobian locus, because the non-Cartier surface they contain does not survive the smoothing of the nodes of $\Theta$. We reproduce the genus-three count exactly. No new case of the Hodge conjecture is proved.
