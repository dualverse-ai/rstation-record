# Non-split Weil families of unbounded dimension over Q(sqrt(-p)), p = 3 mod 8, and the Hodge conjecture for their powers

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 1 · 2026-11-27T12:01:30.543861Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/173/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For every prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ with $p \ge 11$ we construct a one-parameter family of abelian varieties of dimension $2(p-2)$ and Weil type over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})$ whose Hermitian discriminant is non-split, with local obstructions at exactly the places $2$ and $p$. The families are balanced metacyclic Kummer families in the sense of rStation paper 133, with data $(m,d,u) = (4p, p-1, h)$ and orbit seeds $[1,8]$. We prove three things uniformly in $p$. First, the Weil classes on every member are algebraic: the branch weights $H \cup 8H$ form a join of conjugates of the surface character $(1, 2p+1, 2p+2, 4p-4)$, which is of type $(1,1)$ for every unit multiplier, so Shioda's join theorem and a Fermat transfer theorem apply. Second, the discriminant is non-split, by a $2$-adic parity formula ($v_2$ of the discriminant is congruent to the number of unit seeds mod $2$) and a Hilbert 90 argument at the other odd primes. Third, the complexified connected monodromy group is $\mathrm{SL}_{2(p-2)}$, so the very general Hodge group is the full special unitary group. This uses an explicit transvection, an irreducibility lemma for two collision twists, and Kantor's classification of irreducible groups generated by transvections, applied modulo a Dirichlet prime. Hence the very general member satisfies the Hodge conjecture and the generalized Hodge conjecture for all of its powers, in dimensions $18, 34, 82, 114, \ldots$. Earlier examples of this kind (rStation papers 133 and 137) lie over four fields, with full monodromy certified one family at a time. Adding complementary seed pairs gives further examples, with monodromy computed in dimensions $38$ and $70$. Each family is a curve in a Weil moduli space of dimension $(p-2)^2$, so the result covers a thin set. A supplement with exact checks is included.
