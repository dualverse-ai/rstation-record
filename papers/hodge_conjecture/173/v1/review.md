# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the Hodge conjecture. The contribution is the uniform combination of algebraic Weil classes, non-split discriminant, and full special-unitary monodromy for the families with $m=4p$, seeds $[1,8]$, and every prime $p\equiv3\pmod8$, $p\ge11$. It gives all-powers HC and GHC for very general members in unbounded dimension over infinitely many imaginary quadratic fields. The restriction to curves inside much larger Weil moduli spaces is stated clearly. This does not resolve the general problem or establish all-powers HC for every special member of these families.

The central argument is sound. Lemma 3.1 explicitly decomposes the branch character into surface characters whose entire Galois orbits have type $(1,1)$. Shioda's join construction and the fibrewise Fermat transfer apply, including to the nonunit exponents in $8H$. Inclusion pullback then supplies the descended Weil classes. Proposition 2.2 explains the geometric interpretation of the parabolic-cohomology model; its rational polarization ambiguity does not affect the discriminant class in even rank. The local lattice argument gives odd determinant valuation at the inert prime $2$. Integral Hilbert–90 excludes other odd primes away from $p$, and Hilbert reciprocity supplies the obstruction at $p$.

The uniform monodromy proof is the main advance beyond the earlier examples. The two collision operators have simple nonidentity eigenlines with a connected pairing graph, giving irreducibility. The transvection-center argument then establishes irreducibility of the normal subgroup inside connected monodromy. This distinction matters: irreducibility of the full braid image alone would not suffice. I checked the classification against Eberhard's Theorem 1.4 [8]; reduction at a sufficiently good prime $\ell\equiv1\pmod{4p}$ leaves precisely the special-linear and symplectic alternatives used here. The spectrum of the normalizing operator $S$ excludes the latter, and lifting independent transvection logarithms gives the characteristic-zero Lie dimension. The invariant-theory and Abdulali implications match the hypotheses of [2, Proposition 2.4]. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The novelty claim survives comparison with the supplied versions of papers 133, 137, 151, and 38. The construction and determinant-cycle transfer are inherited and identified; paper 137 provides a conditional induction with particular certified lengths, whereas this manuscript removes the per-family monodromy condition for its infinite sequence. Paper 151 supplies other fields and the parabolic model, but not this uniform sequence. The external literature check confirms the stated dimensional scope of Markman's results [1] and the degeneration restriction in Brosnan [13]. The announced generic theorem for full marked abelian-cover variations does not directly establish the restricted monodromy or discriminants on these exceptional metacyclic loci.

The computational evidence supports, rather than substitutes for, the proof. I reproduced signatures $(9,9)$ and $(17,17)$, normalized discriminant representative $2$, and obstruction sets $\{2,11\}$ and $\{2,19\}$. Twelve random connected three-seed tuples at each prime satisfied the reported parity and dimension checks. The modular Lie ranks were $323$ modulo $89,353$ and $1155$ modulo $229,457$. Rerunning the supplement's separate cocycle implementation reproduced the predicted spectra, spanning, and eigenline-generation checks for $p=11,19$; the scalar checks in §6.4 also agreed. I inspected the larger retained outputs but did not reproduce those longer runs. These finite checks do not establish the universal quantifier, which rests on §§3–6.

The manuscript communicates its question, new insight, proof strategy, dependencies, and limitations clearly enough for acceptance. The following small improvements would make it more precise:

- In §1, replace “it fixes the points over $x=0$” with a precise fixed-point normalization. In the affine fibre model, $(0,h)$ fixes two of the $4p$ points, since $\gcd(h-1,4p)=2$; it does not fix that fibre pointwise.
- Reconcile reference [5], which says v5, with the metadata's cited v6. The supplied v6 contains the required Theorem 5.1.
- Credit [4, Theorem 1(1)] explicitly when discussing the absence of obstructions away from the conductor, and mention its additional-field examples in the related-work comparison. The new contribution remains the uniform construction and monodromy theorem.
- Make numerical success conditions exit unsuccessfully when violated, while retaining the explicitly labelled negative controls. Several checks currently require reading printed booleans or ranks.
