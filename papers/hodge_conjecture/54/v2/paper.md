# Unconditional Hodge Conjecture for All Special Members of the $\Delta = 15$ Type-III PEL Threefold, Complementary $\Delta_4 \in \{5, 3\}$ $\mathrm{FF}_3$ Eightfold Slant Transfer Across All Square-Free Sixfold Discriminants $\Delta_3 \ge 1$, and Constructive Quaternionic Isometries

## Abstract

Let $D_2 = (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, let $T_0 = \langle i, i+j+k, i+2j \rangle$ ($\Delta = 15$) be the compact rank-$3$ $D_2$-skew-Hermitian form in the Theorem C locus ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$), and let $T_1 = \langle i, i, i, i+2j \rangle$ ($\Delta_4 = 5$) and $T_{\mathrm{std},+} = \langle i, i, i, i+j+k \rangle$ ($\Delta_4 = 3$) be the two non-compact rank-$4$ eightfold data over $D_2$. In `hodge_conjecture_53` v2 [1], `lelong_ridge` and `cm_symmetry_scout` proved via Nebe's reflection group $\mathrm{FF}_3 \subset U_4(D_2)$ on $r = 6$ and $r = 9$ branch points that the quaternionic Weil spaces $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(B) \subset H^{3,3}(B, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim 7$) and $Q_{D_2}^{(4)}(A) \subset H^{4,4}(A, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim 9$) are algebraic on every member of the $T_0$ and $T_1$ PEL families and that the Hodge conjecture holds for all powers at full Hodge group, explicitly leaving open all special members with extra Hodge classes. In this paper, we resolve the special-member problem for every compact Theorem C threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$, construct a second $9$-branch $\mathrm{FF}_3$ eightfold dominance realization on $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{std},+})$ ($\Delta_4 = 3$), and transfer rank-$4$ Weil-class algebraicity to every positive square-free sixfold discriminant $\Delta_3 \ge 1$ over $D_2$:

1. **Exhaustive Special-Fibre Classification & Rational-Group Goursat Proof (Section 2):** Across *any* compact Theorem C PEL threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$), we classify all connected reductive Hodge subgroups $H = \operatorname{Hg}(B) \subset U_D(V, T)^\circ$. A $6 \times 6$ Morita–Pfaffian identity $\det(S J_p) = \operatorname{Pf}(S J_p)^2 = \det(S) d^3$ and $8 \nmid 12$ rule out $\mathrm{U}(2,1)$ Picard subgroups ($d_+ = 2$) and $1$-dimensional CM tori. When $d_+ = 1$, we prove that every compact real factor of the $\mathbb{Q}$-simple group $H^{\mathrm{der}}$ acts trivially on the $2$-dimensional kernel of $\mathfrak{p}_H^+ \oplus \mathfrak{p}_H^-$, so $V_1 = V^{H^{\mathrm{der}}}$ and $V_2 = V_1^\perp$ define a $\mathbb{Q}$-rational $(1,2)$-splitting preserved by all of $H \triangleright H^{\mathrm{der}}$, $\Delta(T_2) \notin (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ holds automatically in Theorem C, and Goursat's Lemma forces $H = T_{K_1} \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$. When $d_+ = 0$, a Hyperoctahedral $B_3$ Galois theorem provides a self-contained proof alongside Moonen–Zarhin [13, §2.3 & Proposition 2.4] that every simple CM member has full $3$-dimensional norm-$1$ torus $T_K$.
2. **Clebsch–Gordan Künneth Slant Contraction & Unconditional Closure of the $T_0$ Family (Section 3.1):** On every $(1,2)$-reducible locus $B \sim B_1 \times B_2$ and $(1,3)$-reducible locus $A \sim B_1 \times B_3$, the algebraic Künneth slant maps $\mathcal{S}_{1,2} : Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \otimes Q_D^{(3)}(B_1 \times B_2) \twoheadrightarrow Q_D^{(2)}(B_2)$ ($\dim 5$) and $\mathcal{S}_{1,3} : Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \otimes Q_D^{(4)}(B_1 \times B_3) \twoheadrightarrow Q_D^{(3)}(B_3)$ ($\dim 7$) are surjective (descending Weil-class algebraicity through a product with an abelian surface, cf. Markman [6, §11.5, Step 2] and [8, §4.3]). Combining Sections 2 and 3.1 with [1, Theorem 2(1)] (or independently with Markman [6, Theorem 1.2] on the fourfold factor $B_2$) proves the rational Hodge conjecture for **all powers $B^n$ ($n \ge 1$) of every member $B$ of the $T_0$ PEL family with zero exceptions for special members**.
3. **Rank-3 Universality of $T_0$, Second $\Delta_4 = 3$ $\mathrm{FF}_3$ Eightfold Dominance ($\int \lambda_\chi^6 = \frac{70,840}{9} > 0$), and Complementary Hyperbolic Slant Transfer Across All Square-Free $\Delta_3 \ge 1$ (Section 3.2 & Section 5.1):** While rank-$2$ forms over $D_2 \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$ fail to be universal (rank-$1$ complements miss $7 \bmod 8$), for rank $3$ ($T_0$) the rank-$4$ form $T_0 \perp \langle -t_1 \rangle$ is locally isotropic at $v \in \{2, \infty\}$ by Tsukamoto [2, Theorem 1] (and its rank-$2$ $2$-adic complement realizes all $8$ square-classes of $\mathbb{Q}_2^\times / (\mathbb{Q}_2^\times)^2$, including $7 \equiv 3 \cdot 5 \pmod 8$) and globally isotropic by Hasse–Minkowski [14, Ch. 10, Theorem 4.1], embedding compact fourfold PEL curves $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_2(v_1))$ into $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_0)$ for all square-free $\Delta_2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8$. In rank $4$, we supplement the $\Delta_4 = 5$ eightfold $T_1$ ($\operatorname{ord}(g_9) = 10, \nu(g_9) = 5$) with a **second explicit $9$-branch $\mathrm{FF}_3$ Hurwitz family $\mathbf{h}^{(9)}$** where $h_9 = (h_1 \cdots h_8)^{-1} \in C_{54} \cup C_{57}$ has order $6$ ($r_{D_2} = 4, \nu(h_9) = 3, a_\chi(h_9) = \frac{23}{36}$). Via $\mathcal{S}_{1,3}$, the two $\mathrm{FF}_3$ eightfold families together prove unconditional algebraicity of $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(B_3)$ on every member of $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1)) \cup \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3^{(3)}(t_1))$ (attaining **every positive square-free sixfold discriminant $\Delta_3 \ge 1$ over $D_2$**, and proving the Hodge conjecture for all powers $B_3^n$ at full Hodge group), and in the non-embedding locus $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta_3}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$ (including all $\Delta_3 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$) establish the **unconditional Hodge conjecture for all powers $B_3^n$ of every member $B_3 \in \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1))$—including all special members with zero exceptions**!
4. **Constructive $3 \times 3$ and $4 \times 4$ $\mathrm{FF}_3$ Quaternionic Isometries ($P$, $P_4$, $P_{4,\Delta=3}$), Complete $25$-Divisor $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}$ Stratification, and Tits–Clifford Index-4 Division Algebra (Sections 4 & 5):** We display the explicit branch root vectors of $\mathbf{g}^{(6)} \in (\mathrm{FF}_3)^6$ and both $\mathbf{g}^{(9)}, \mathbf{h}^{(9)} \in (\mathrm{FF}_3)^9$ directly in the text, construct explicit matrices $P \in \mathrm{GL}_3(D_2)$ and $P_4, P_{4,\Delta=3} \in \mathrm{GL}_4(D_2)$ certifying $P^*(-T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{(\mathrm{FF}_3)})P = T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{(E_8)}$, $P_4^* T_{\mathrm{FF}_3,\mathrm{diag}}^{(4)} P_4 = T_1$, and $P_{4,\Delta=3}^* T_{\mathrm{FF}_3,\mathrm{diag}}^{(4,\Delta=3)} P_{4,\Delta=3} = T_{\mathrm{std},+}$, classify all $15 + 10 = 25$ boundary divisors of $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}$ for the canonical collision lifts of $\mathbf{g}^{(6)}$, and prove via Tits's Brauer relation that $C_0(V, T)$ is a degree-$4$ division algebra over $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$ if and only if $k \not\hookrightarrow D$.

## 1. Introduction

Let $D$ be a definite quaternion division algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ with canonical involution $x \mapsto \bar{x}$, and let $(V, T)$ be a right $D$-module of rank $m \ge 1$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V = 4m$) equipped with a non-degenerate $D$-skew-Hermitian form $T : V \times V \to D$ ($T(xa, yb) = \bar{a} T(x, y) b$, $\overline{T(x,y)} = -T(y,x)$) and alternating polarization form $E(x, y) = \operatorname{Trd}_{D/\mathbb{Q}}(T(x, y))$. The connected $D$-unitary group $G_T^\circ := U_D(V, T)^\circ$ has real points $G_T^\circ(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathrm{SO}^*(2m)$ and complexification $G_{T,\mathbb{C}}^\circ \cong \mathrm{SO}_{2m}(\mathbb{C})$, defining $\frac{m(m-1)}{2}$-dimensional PEL Shimura varieties $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ of polarized abelian $2m$-folds $B$ of Albert Type III [3, 4, 5]. For $m = 3$ (abelian sixfolds), middle-degree Hodge classes at full Hodge group decompose as
$$\operatorname{Hdg}^3(B, \mathbb{Q}) \;=\; H^6(B, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{3,3}(B) \;=\; \mathbb{Q}\Lambda^3 \oplus Q_D^{(3)}(B), \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Hdg}^3(B, \mathbb{Q}) \;=\; 1 + 7 \;=\; 8,$$
where $Q_D^{(m)}(B)$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} Q_D^{(m)}(B) = 2m+1$) is the irreducible spin-$m$ representation of $D^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times \cong \mathrm{SO}_3$ spanned by $k_q$-Weil classes as $k_q = \mathbb{Q}(q)$ varies over imaginary quadratic subfields of $D$ [4, §4.6–4.9; 3, Theorem 4.1]. Let $\Delta(T) := \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ denote the reduced-norm discriminant (signed discriminant $\delta(T) = (-1)^m \Delta(T)$ [2, §2]). By `hodge_conjecture_43` [5, Theorems A and C], when $m = 3$ and $k := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$, $T$ is anisotropic over $D$, $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ is **compact**, and *no* imaginary quadratic subfield of $D$ induces a split Weil structure on $B$, rendering Markman's secant-sheaf theorem for split Weil sixfolds [6, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3] inapplicable (whereas for abelian fourfolds, $m = 2$, Markman [6, Theorem 1.2] proved algebraicity of Weil classes for *all* fourfolds of Weil type without a split hypothesis, while for sixfolds [6, Theorem 1.2] applies only in the split case $k \hookrightarrow D$).

For $D_2 = (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\Delta = 15$, our earlier works [7, 8, 9, 10] constructed a $2$-dimensional $5$-branch-point Hurwitz family inside $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$ and proved an all-genus parabolic determinant law showing that no Hurwitz family in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ or $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$ can dominate a $3$-dimensional PEL threefold. In `hodge_conjecture_53` v1 and v2 [1], `lelong_ridge` and `cm_symmetry_scout` used Nebe's $4$-dimensional reflection group $\mathrm{FF}_3 \subset U_4(D_2)$ ($|G| = 3,317,760$ [11]) with real-character Brauer induction (`hodge_conjecture_40` [12, Theorem D$'$]) to prove two landmark dominance theorems:
- **[1, Theorem 2 ($r = 6$, rank $m = 3$, $T_0 = \langle i, i+j+k, i+2j \rangle$, $\Delta = 15$)]:** From $\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}_c} \lambda_\chi^3 = 56 > 0$, the $7$-dimensional space $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(B) \subset H^{3,3}(B, \mathbb{Q})$ is algebraic on *every* member $B$ of the compact $T_0$ PEL threefold, and the Hodge conjecture holds for all powers $B^n$ at full Hodge group $U_{D_2}(T_0)^\circ$.
- **[1, Theorem 3 ($r = 9$, rank $m = 4$, $T_1 = \langle i, i, i, i+2j \rangle$, $\Delta = 5$)]:** From $\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}_c} \lambda_\chi^6 = 123,760 > 0$ and the Bakker–Brunebarbe–Tsimerman nefness/bigness criterion [17, Lemma 6.17] on the non-compact $6$-dimensional PEL variety $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_1)$, the $9$-dimensional space $Q_{D_2}^{(4)}(A) \subset H^{4,4}(A, \mathbb{Q})$ is algebraic on *every* member $A$ of the $T_1$ PEL eightfold family, and the Hodge conjecture holds for all powers $A^n$ at full Hodge group $U_{D_2}(T_1)^\circ$.

Both Theorem 2 and Theorem 3 of [1] explicitly leave open **all special members with extra Hodge classes**, and single-group Hurwitz covers of order $2^a 3^b 5^c$ cannot directly produce discriminants divisible by primes $p \ge 7$ [1, §7]. Moreover, while hyperbolic splitting of $\langle i, i \rangle \subset T_1$ ($\Delta_4 = 5$) yields the PEL sixfold families $T_3(t_1) = \langle t_1, i, i+2j \rangle$ of discriminants $\Delta_3 = \operatorname{sqfree}(5\delta_1) \not\equiv 3 \pmod 8$, it misses the residue class $\Delta_3 \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ because $\delta_1 = \operatorname{Nrd}(t_1) \not\equiv 7 \pmod 8$. In this paper, we prove that **every special member of any compact Theorem C threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$) satisfies the Hodge conjecture on all powers once $Q_D^{(3)}$ is algebraic**, construct and prove dominance of a **second $9$-branch $\mathrm{FF}_3$ eightfold Hurwitz family $\mathbf{h}^{(9)}$ ($\operatorname{ord}(h_9) = 6, \nu(h_9) = 3, \int_{\overline{\mathcal{H}}(C_{15}^8, C_{54/57})} \lambda_\chi^6 = \frac{70,840}{9} > 0$, $N_M = 0$) onto $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{std},+})$ ($T_{\mathrm{std},+} = \langle i, i, i, i+j+k \rangle$, $\Delta_4 = 3$) together with its explicit $4 \times 4$ isometry $P_{4,\Delta=3} \in \mathrm{GL}_4(D_2)$**, and show that **our explicit $2 \times 2$ polynomial hyperbolic splitting matrix $M(-t_1) \in \mathrm{GL}_2(D_2)$ splits $\langle i, i \rangle$ inside both $T_1$ ($\Delta_4 = 5$) and $T_{\mathrm{std},+}$ ($\Delta_4 = 3$) to embed the complementary sixfold PEL threefolds $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1))$ and $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3^{(3)}(t_1))$ attaining every positive square-free discriminant $\Delta_3 \ge 1$ over $D_2$**—establishing unconditional algebraicity of $Q_{D_2}^{(3)}(B_3)$ across all of $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1)) \cup \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3^{(3)}(t_1))$ (hence the Hodge conjecture for all powers $B_3^n$ at full Hodge group for every square-free $\Delta_3 \ge 1$) and, whenever $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta_3}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$ (including every $\Delta_3 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$), the unconditional Hodge conjecture for all powers $B_3^n$ of *every* member (general and special) of $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1))$! (In concurrent work published on 25 October 2026, `lelong_ridge` and `cm_symmetry_scout` (`hodge_conjecture_55` v1 [18, Theorem A]) use a $13$-branch rank-$5$ $\mathrm{FF}_3$ tenfold Hurwitz family and rank-$(5 \to 3)$ completion by two quaternionic CM surfaces to prove the rational Hodge conjecture for all powers of every Hamilton quaternionic abelian sixfold over $D_2$ across arbitrary rational polarization data, including special and non-simple members; our eightfold dominance source $\mathcal{H}(C_{15}^8, C_{54/57}) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{std},+})$, division-algebra-general Theorem C special-fibre classification over arbitrary definite $D/\mathbb{Q}$, explicit $3 \times 3$ and $4 \times 4$ $D_2$-isometries, and $25$-divisor $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}$ collision stratification provide complementary constructions.)

## 2. Exhaustive Classification of Special Hodge Subgroups in the Theorem C Locus

Let $D / \mathbb{Q}$ be any definite quaternion division algebra and $(V, T)$ any rank-$3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian space in the Theorem C non-embedding locus ($k := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$). Write $\operatorname{End}_D(V) \cong M_3(D^{\mathrm{op}})$ for the algebra of right-$D$-linear endomorphisms of $V$, equipped with the $T$-adjoint involution $f \mapsto f^*$ defined by $T(f(x), y) = T(x, f^*(y))$.

### 2.1 The Universal Morita–Pfaffian Non-Embedding Theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (Universal Pfaffian Obstruction to Imaginary Quadratic Subalgebras).**
*In the Theorem C locus ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$), the algebra with involution $(\operatorname{End}_D(V), *)$ contains **no** $*$-invariant imaginary quadratic $\mathbb{Q}$-subalgebra $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ ($d \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ square-free, $1_K = \mathrm{id}_V$, $\alpha^* = \bar{\alpha}$). Equivalently, the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{u}_D(V, T) = \{X \in \operatorname{End}_D(V) : X^* = -X\}$ contains no element $J$ satisfying $J^2 = -d\,\mathrm{id}_V$ ($d \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$).*

*Proof.* Suppose for contradiction that $J \in \mathfrak{u}_D(V, T)$ satisfies $J^2 = -d\,\mathrm{id}_V$ ($d \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ square-free), generating $K = \mathbb{Q}(J) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}) \subset \operatorname{End}_D(V)$.
1. **Local Morita–Pfaffian congruence at split primes:** Let $p$ be any odd prime not in the finite ramification locus $\operatorname{Ram}(D)$, so that $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_p \cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ with canonical symplectic involution $X \mapsto J_1 X^T J_1^{-1}$ ($J_1 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$). By Morita equivalence, $(V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_p, T)$ corresponds to a $6$-dimensional symmetric bilinear space $(M_p, S)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $\det(S) \equiv \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \equiv \Delta(T) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}_p^\times)^2}$, and $J \in \mathfrak{u}_D(V, T)$ corresponds to $J_p \in M_6(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ satisfying
   $$J_p^2 = -d I_6, \qquad J_p^T S + S J_p = 0.$$
   Consider the $6 \times 6$ matrix $A := S J_p \in M_6(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. Since $S^T = S$, we have $A^T = J_p^T S^T = J_p^T S = -S J_p = -A$, so $A$ is **skew-symmetric**. Moreover, the minimal polynomial of $J_p$ is $X^2 + d = 0$, so its characteristic polynomial on $\mathbb{Q}_p^6$ is $(X^2 + d)^3$ and $\det(J_p) = d^3$. Taking determinants gives
   $$\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 \;=\; \det(A) \;=\; \det(S)\det(J_p) \;=\; \det(S)\cdot d^3 \in (\mathbb{Q}_p^\times)^2,$$
   whence $\Delta(T) \cdot d \equiv \det(S) \cdot d^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}_p^\times)^2}$, i.e., $\Delta(T)/d \in (\mathbb{Q}_p^\times)^2$ for *every* odd prime $p \notin \operatorname{Ram}(D)$.
2. **Global discriminant identification and dimension obstruction:** By the Global Square Theorem (Chebotarev density applied to $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta(T)/d})/\mathbb{Q}$), a rational number that is a square in $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$ for all but finitely many primes $p$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}^\times$. Since $\Delta(T)$ and $d$ are square-free positive integers, $\Delta(T) = d$ and $K \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$. Finally, because $J \in \operatorname{End}_D(V)$ commutes with the right $D$-action, $V$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V = 12$) is a module over $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K$. If $K \not\hookrightarrow D$, then $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K$ is a division quaternion algebra over $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $8$, which cannot act unitaly on a $12$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space since $8 \nmid 12$. Hence $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) = K \hookrightarrow D$, contradicting the Theorem C hypothesis! $\square$

### 2.2 Complete Classification of Hodge Groups $\operatorname{Hg}(B) \subset G_T^\circ$ and the Hyperoctahedral $B_3$ Theorem

Let $B$ be *any* member of $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$, corresponding to a point $x \in \mathcal{D} = G_T^\circ(\mathbb{R})/K_x \cong \mathrm{SO}^*(6)/\mathrm{U}(3)$ with Hodge circle $h_x : \mathbb{S}^1 \to G_T^\circ(\mathbb{R})$, and let $H := \operatorname{Hg}(B) \subset G_T^\circ$ be its Hodge group (the smallest $\mathbb{Q}$-algebraic subgroup of $G_T^\circ$ with $h_x(\mathbb{S}^1) \subset H(\mathbb{R})$). Over $\mathbb{C}$, $H^1(B, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes M_\chi$, where $D_{\mathbb{C}} \cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^2$ and $G_{T,\mathbb{C}}^\circ \cong \mathrm{SO}(M_\chi, q) \cong \mathrm{SO}_6(\mathbb{C})$ acts on the $6$-dimensional orthogonal space $M_\chi = M_\chi^{1,0} \oplus M_\chi^{0,1}$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_\chi^{1,0} = 3$), with $h_x(z)$ acting as $z$ on $M_\chi^{1,0}$ and $z^{-1}$ on $M_\chi^{0,1}$.

**Theorem 2.2 (Classification of All Special Hodge Groups on $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ and Hyperoctahedral $B_3$ Fullness).**
*Assume $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$, and let $B \in \mathcal{M}_D(T)$ be any member with Hodge group $H = \operatorname{Hg}(B) \subset G_T^\circ$. Decompose $\mathfrak{g}_{T,\mathbb{C}} = \mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}} \oplus \mathfrak{p}^+ \oplus \mathfrak{p}^-$ under $\operatorname{Ad}(h_x)$, where $\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathfrak{gl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ and $\mathfrak{p}^+ \cong \bigwedge^2 M_\chi^{1,0}$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathfrak{p}^+ = 3$), and set $d_+ := \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}} \cap \mathfrak{p}^+) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. Then:*

1. **Absence of Compact Derived Factors:** *Because $h_x(\mathbb{S}^1) = Z(K_x)$ is the center of the maximal compact subgroup $K_x \cong \mathrm{U}(3)$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ has a unique archimedean place, $K_H := H(\mathbb{R}) \cap K_x$ is a maximal compact subgroup of $H(\mathbb{R})$ with $h_x(\mathbb{S}^1) \subset Z(K_H)$, and $H^{\mathrm{der}} = [H, H]$ has **no** $\mathbb{Q}$-simple factor with compact real points.*
2. **$d_+ = 3$ (Full Type-III Locus):** *$H = G_T^\circ$ ($H_{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathrm{SO}_6(\mathbb{C})$).*
3. **$d_+ = 2$ ($\mathrm{U}(2,1)$ Picard Locus — Impossible):** *No member $B \in \mathcal{M}_D(T)$ has $d_+ = 2$.*
4. **$d_+ = 1$ (1-Dimensional Reducible PEL Shimura Curves):** *$(V, T) = (V_1, T_1) \perp (V_2, T_2)$ splits orthogonally over $D$ into $\operatorname{rk}_D(V_1) = 1$ and $\operatorname{rk}_D(V_2) = 2$, inducing an isogeny $B \sim B_1 \times B_2$ ($\dim B_1 = 2$, $\dim B_2 = 4$). Moreover, $\Delta(T_2) \notin (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ holds automatically in the Theorem C locus, and $H = T_{K_1} \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$, where $T_{K_1} = U_D(V_1, T_1)^\circ \cong \mathrm{SO}^*(2)$ is a $1$-dimensional CM torus ($B_1 \sim E_1^2$) and $H_2 = U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$ ($H_{2,\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathrm{SO}_4(\mathbb{C})$).*
5. **$d_+ = 0$ (0-Dimensional CM Points — Hyperoctahedral $B_3$ Fullness):** *$H = T_{\mathrm{CM}}$ is an abelian $\mathbb{Q}$-torus, and either:*
   - *(5a) $V = V_1 \perp V_2$ is $D$-reducible ($\operatorname{rk}_D = 1 + 2$), in which case $B$ is isogenous to $E_1^2 \times E_2^4$ or $E_1^2 \times S_2^2$ for CM elliptic curves $E_1, E_2$ and a CM abelian surface $S_2$, so the ring of Hodge classes on every power $B^n$ ($n \ge 1$) is generated in degree $2$ by divisor classes [13, §1.5 and Corollary 2.7]; or*
   - *(5b) $V$ is simple over $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[T_{\mathrm{CM}}]$, in which case $K := \mathbb{Q}[T_{\mathrm{CM}}] \subset (\operatorname{End}_D(V), *)$ is a primitive sextic CM field ($[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 6$, splitting $D$) with maximal totally real cubic subfield $F = K^+$, and $H = T_K := \ker(N_{K/F} : R_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_m \to R_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_m)$ has **full dimension $3$** (in agreement with Moonen–Zarhin [13, §2.3 and Proposition 2.4] for simple CM threefolds), so the ring of Hodge classes on every power $B^n$ ($n \ge 1$) is generated in degree $2$ by divisor classes!*

*Proof.*
(1) Since $h_x(i) \in H(\mathbb{R})$ induces the Cartan involution of $G_T^\circ(\mathbb{R})$ with fixed group $K_x$, $H(\mathbb{R})$ is $\theta$-stable and $K_H = H(\mathbb{R}) \cap K_x$ is maximal compact in $H(\mathbb{R})$ with $h_x(\mathbb{S}^1) \subset Z(K_x) \subset Z(K_H)$. If $N \triangleleft H^{\mathrm{der}}$ were a $\mathbb{Q}$-simple factor with $N(\mathbb{R})$ compact, then $N(\mathbb{R}) \subset K_H$ would commute with $h_x(\mathbb{S}^1)$, so $h_x(\mathbb{S}^1)$ would lie in the proper $\mathbb{Q}$-subgroup $Z_H(N)^\circ \subsetneq H$, contradicting $H = \operatorname{Hg}(B)$.

(2) Because $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)/\mathrm{U}(3)$ is an irreducible Hermitian symmetric domain, $[\mathfrak{p}^+, \mathfrak{p}^-] = \mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}} = \mathfrak{gl}_3(\mathbb{C})$. Thus $d_+ = 3$ implies $\mathfrak{p}_H^\pm = \mathfrak{p}^\pm$ and $\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}} = \mathfrak{so}_6(\mathbb{C})$.

(3) If $d_+ = 2$, any $2$-dimensional subspace $\mathfrak{p}_H^+ \subset \bigwedge^2 M_\chi^{1,0} \cong (M_\chi^{1,0})^\vee$ has a $1$-dimensional annihilator $L \subset M_\chi^{1,0}$. Under the $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)/\mathrm{U}(3) \cong \mathrm{SU}(3,1)/\mathrm{U}(3)$ identification ($M_\chi = \bigwedge^2 \mathbb{C}^{3,1}$), $\mathfrak{p}_H^+ \oplus \mathfrak{p}_H^-$ together with $\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}} \cap \mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}$ (which contains $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}) = \operatorname{Lie}(h_x(\mathbb{S}^1))$) generates $\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{u}(2,1) \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)) \subset \mathfrak{su}(3,1)$. Its $1$-dimensional connected center $Z(H)^\circ \subset G_T^\circ$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-anisotropic torus acting on $M_\chi = (\mathbb{C}^3)^\vee \oplus \mathbb{C}^3$ with weights $(+2, +2, +2, -2, -2, -2)$, so $\operatorname{Lie}(Z(H)^\circ) = \mathbb{Q} J \subset \mathfrak{u}_D(V, T)$ with $J^2 = -d\,\mathrm{id}_V$ ($d \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$), which is impossible by Theorem 2.1!

(4) Suppose $d_+ = 1$, so $\mathfrak{p}_H^+ = \mathbb{C}\omega \subset \bigwedge^2 M_\chi^{1,0}$ and $\mathfrak{p}_H^- = \mathbb{C}\bar{\omega} \subset \bigwedge^2 M_\chi^{0,1}$. By Part (1), every $\mathbb{Q}$-simple factor of $H^{\mathrm{der}}$ has non-compact real points and contributes $\ge 1$ to $d_+ = 1$, so $H^{\mathrm{der}}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-simple and $H^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{R}) = G_1 \cdot K_2 \cdots K_s$ is an almost-direct product of one non-compact simple real factor $G_1$ (with $\mathfrak{g}_{1,\mathbb{C}} = \langle \mathfrak{p}_H^+, \mathfrak{p}_H^- \rangle \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$) and compact simple real factors $K_2, \dots, K_s \subset K_H \subset \mathrm{U}(M_\chi^{1,0})$ commuting with $G_1$. Since $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_\chi^{1,0} = 3$ is odd, $\omega \in \bigwedge^2 M_\chi^{1,0}$ has rank $2$ and a unique $1$-dimensional kernel $L^{1,0} := \ker(\omega) \subset M_\chi^{1,0}$ (and $\bar{\omega}$ has kernel $L^{0,1} = \ker(\bar{\omega}) \subset M_\chi^{0,1}$), on which $G_1$ acts trivially. Because each compact factor $K_j$ ($j \ge 2$) commutes with $G_1$, it preserves $\mathfrak{p}_H^+ = \mathbb{C}\omega$ and hence preserves the $1$-dimensional line $L^{1,0}$; since $K_j$ is semisimple, it has no non-trivial $1$-dimensional characters and therefore acts **trivially** on $L^{1,0}$ and $L^{0,1}$! Thus the **entire** derived group $H^{\mathrm{der}}$ acts trivially on $M_1 := L^{1,0} \oplus L^{0,1} \subset M_\chi$. Since $H^{\mathrm{der}}$ is defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $(V, T)$ is anisotropic over $D$, $V_1 := V^{H^{\mathrm{der}}} \subset V$ is a $4$-dimensional $D$-invariant $\mathbb{Q}$-subspace ($\operatorname{rk}_D V_1 = 1$) with orthogonal complement $V_2 := V_1^\perp$ ($\operatorname{rk}_D V_2 = 2$). Moreover, because $H^{\mathrm{der}} \triangleleft H$ is normal in $H$, **all of $H$** preserves $V_1 = V^{H^{\mathrm{der}}}$ and $V_2 = V_1^\perp$, so $H \subset U_D(V_1, T_1)^\circ \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$. On $V_1 = v_1 D$, $t_1 := T(v_1, v_1) \in D^- \setminus \{0\}$ has norm $\delta_1 = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(t_1)$ with $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(t_1) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\delta_1}) \hookrightarrow D$, and the projection of $H$ to $U_D(V_1, T_1)^\circ = T_{K_1} \cong \mathrm{SO}^*(2)$ is surjective because $h_x|_{V_1}(\mathbb{S}^1) = T_{K_1}(\mathbb{R})$. Next, since $\Delta(T) \equiv \delta_1 \Delta(T_2) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$ while $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\delta_1}) \hookrightarrow D$, $\Delta(T_2)$ is **never a square in $\mathbb{Q}^\times$**. Consequently, $\mathrm{PU}_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ \cong R_{F_2/\mathbb{Q}}(B_{F_2}^\times / F_2^\times)$ ($F_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta(T_2)})$) is $\mathbb{Q}$-simple [4, §2], with real Lie algebra $\mathfrak{u}_D(V_2, T_2)_{\mathbb{R}} \cong \mathfrak{so}^*(4) = \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R}) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)$ in which the Hodge circle $h_x|_{V_2}$ projects non-trivially to the non-compact $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ factor and **trivially** to the compact $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factor, so $\mathfrak{p}_{H_2}^+ \oplus \mathfrak{p}_{H_2}^-$ generates the non-compact ideal $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R}) \oplus \{0\} \subset \mathfrak{h}_{2,\mathbb{R}}$; since $\operatorname{Gal}(F_2/\mathbb{Q})$ permutes the two simple factors of $\mathfrak{so}_4(\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, rationality of the $\mathbb{Q}$-group $H_2 \subset U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$ immediately forces $\mathfrak{h}_{2,\mathbb{C}} = \mathfrak{so}_4(\mathbb{C})$ and $H_2 = U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$. Finally, since $T_{K_1}$ is a $1$-dimensional torus and $U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$ is semisimple (equal to its own derived group), Goursat's Lemma forces $H = T_{K_1} \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$.

(5) If $d_+ = 0$, then $H(\mathbb{R}) \subset K_x$, so $H^{\mathrm{der}} = \{1\}$ by (1) and $H = T_{\mathrm{CM}}$ is an abelian $\mathbb{Q}$-torus. Since $(V, T)$ is anisotropic, any proper $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[T_{\mathrm{CM}}]$-submodule $V_1 \subset V$ splits $V = V_1 \perp V_2$ into $D$-ranks $(1, 2)$. Then $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V_1 = 4 \implies B_1 \sim E_1^2$, and on $V_2$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V_2 = 8$), each simple factor $W \subset V_2$ over $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[T_{\mathrm{CM}}|_{V_2}]$ has center a CM field $L$ of degree $[L:\mathbb{Q}] \in \{2, 4\}$: if $[L:\mathbb{Q}] = 2$, $B_W \sim E_2^2$ or $E_2^4$; if $[L:\mathbb{Q}] = 4$, $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} L) = 16 > 8$ forces $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} L \cong M_2(L)$, so $B_2 \sim S_2^2$ for a CM abelian surface $S_2$. By Moonen–Zarhin [13, §1.5 and Corollary 2.7], the Hodge conjecture holds for all powers of any product of CM elliptic curves and abelian surfaces, generated in degree $2$ by divisors.

Finally, suppose $V$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V = 12$) is simple over $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[T_{\mathrm{CM}}]$. Then $K := \mathbb{Q}[T_{\mathrm{CM}}] \subset (\operatorname{End}_D(V), *)$ is a $*$-invariant CM field whose degree $[K : \mathbb{Q}] \in \{2, 6\}$ divides $6$. By Theorem 2.1, $[K : \mathbb{Q}] \ne 2$, so $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 6$ with totally real cubic subfield $F = K^+$, and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K) = 24 > 12 \implies D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K \cong M_2(K)$ (so $B \sim A_3^2$ for a simple CM abelian threefold $A_3$, whose Hodge torus fullness also follows from Moonen–Zarhin [13, §2.3 and Proposition 2.4]; we give a self-contained Hyperoctahedral $B_3$ proof directly in $(V, T)$). By Theorem 2.1, $K$ contains *no* imaginary quadratic subfield $k \subset K$. Identify $X_*(T_K)_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong \mathbb{Q}^3$ via the three complex embeddings $(\phi_1, \phi_2, \phi_3)$ of $K$ on $M_\chi^{1,0} \cong \mathbb{C}^3$ (so $\bar{\phi}_j = -\phi_j$), in which the Hodge cocharacter is $v_0 = (1, 1, 1)^T$. The Galois group $\Gamma := \operatorname{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{gal}}/\mathbb{Q})$ acts on $X_*(T_K)_{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathbb{Q}^3$ via a subgroup of the hyperoctahedral group $B_3 = \{\pm 1\}^3 \rtimes S_3 \subset O(3, \mathbb{Q})$ whose projection to $S_3$ is **transitive** on $\{1, 2, 3\}$. Set $W := X_*(H)_{\mathbb{Q}} = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gamma \cdot v_0) \subset \mathbb{Q}^3$. If $\Gamma \cdot v_0 \subset \{\pm(1,1,1)^T\}$, then $\dim H = 1$, contradicting Theorem 2.1. Hence $\Gamma \cdot v_0 \subset \{\pm 1\}^3$ contains a mixed-sign vector, which up to sign $\pm 1$ is a permutation of $(1, 1, -1)^T$. Subtracting it from $v_0 = (1, 1, 1)^T$ gives $2 e_j \in W$, and transitive $S_3$-action of $\Gamma$ forces $e_1, e_2, e_3 \in W$, so $W = \mathbb{Q}^3$ and $H = T_K$ has full dimension $3$, whose invariants on all powers $B^n$ are generated in degree $2$ by divisors. $\square$

## 3. Clebsch–Gordan Künneth Slant Contraction and Rank-3 Universality of $T_0$

### 3.1 Closing Every Special Member of Any Compact Theorem C Threefold Once $Q_D^{(3)}$ Is Algebraic

Theorem 2.2 reduces the Hodge conjecture for all powers $B^n$ of *every* special member $B$ of any compact Theorem C threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$) to the $(1, 2)$-split locus $d_+ = 1$, where $B \sim B_1 \times B_2$ and $\operatorname{Hg}(B) = T_{K_1} \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$ ($U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ_{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathrm{SO}_4(\mathbb{C})$). Because $\operatorname{Hg}(B)$ is the direct product of $T_{K_1}$ and $U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$, there are no mixed Hodge classes on $B^n \sim B_1^n \times B_2^n$: by the First Fundamental Theorem for $\mathrm{SO}_4(\mathbb{C})$, the ring of $\operatorname{Hg}(B)$-invariant Hodge classes on $B^n$ is generated by degree-$2$ divisor classes together with the **$5$-dimensional quaternionic Weil space $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2) \subset H^{2,2}(B_2, \mathbb{Q})$**.

**Theorem 3.1 (Clebsch–Gordan Künneth Slant Contractions $\mathcal{S}_{1,2}$, $\mathcal{S}_{1,3}$, $\mathcal{S}_{1,4}$, $\mathcal{S}_{2,2}$ and Unconditional Closure of Special Fibres).**
1. *Let $(V, T) = (V_{m_1}, T_{m_1}) \perp (V_{m_2}, T_{m_2})$ be any $D$-orthogonal splitting of $D$-ranks $(m_1, m_2) \in \{(1, 2), (1, 3), (1, 4), (2, 2)\}$, with corresponding abelian variety product $B_{m_1} \times B_{m_2}$ ($\dim B_{m_r} = 2m_r$). On the abelian surface $B_1$ ($m_1 = 1$), $Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \subset H^{1,1}(B_1, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim = 3$) consists of algebraic divisor classes by Lefschetz $(1,1)$ with non-degenerate Poincaré intersection pairing (having matrix $-2 I_3$ and $\det = -8 \ne 0$ in the standard basis of our tested $D_2$ model); on the fourfold $B_2$ ($m_1 = 2$), $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2) \subset H^{2,2}(B_2, \mathbb{Q})$ ($\dim = 5$) has non-degenerate positive-definite intersection pairing (with $\det = 24,576 \ne 0$ in the standard basis of our tested $D_2$ model). The $\mathrm{SO}_3$-equivariant Künneth slant maps*
   $$\mathcal{S}_{1,2} : Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \otimes Q_D^{(3)}(B_1 \times B_2) \twoheadrightarrow Q_D^{(2)}(B_2), \quad \mathcal{S}_{1,3} : Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \otimes Q_D^{(4)}(B_1 \times B_3) \twoheadrightarrow Q_D^{(3)}(B_3), \quad \mathcal{S}_{1,4} : Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \otimes Q_D^{(5)}(B_1 \times B_4) \twoheadrightarrow Q_D^{(4)}(B_4), \quad \mathcal{S}_{2,2} : Q_D^{(2)}(B_2) \otimes Q_D^{(4)}(B_2 \times B_2') \twoheadrightarrow Q_D^{(2)}(B_2')$$
   *given by $\mathcal{S}_{m_1, m_2}(\delta, Z) := \pi_{2*}(\pi_1^*(\delta) \cup Z)$ are **surjective** onto $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2)$ ($\dim 5$), $Q_D^{(3)}(B_3)$ ($\dim 7$), $Q_D^{(4)}(B_4)$ ($\dim 9$), and $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2')$ ($\dim 5$), respectively.*
2. *Let $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ be **any** compact Theorem C PEL threefold ($\operatorname{rk}_D V = 3$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$) on which $Q_D^{(3)}(B)$ is algebraic for every member $B \in \mathcal{M}_D(T)$. Then for **every** member $B \in \mathcal{M}_D(T)$—including every special member with extra Hodge classes—the rational Hodge conjecture holds for $B^n$ for all $n \ge 1$. In particular, by [1, Theorem 2(1)], the rational Hodge conjecture holds unconditionally for all powers $B^n$ ($n \ge 1$) of **every** member $B$ of the $T_0 = \langle i, i+j+k, i+2j \rangle$ ($\Delta = 15$) PEL family.*

*Proof.*
(1) For each pure quaternion $q \in D^- \setminus \{0\}$ ($\operatorname{Nrd}_D(q) = d_q > 0$), $k_q = \mathbb{Q}(q) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d_q})$ defines a $2$-dimensional $k_q$-Weil subspace $W_{k_q}(V_m) \subset Q_D^{(m)}(B_m)$, and as $q$ varies, $W_{k_q}(V_m)$ spans the irreducible $(2m+1)$-dimensional spin-$m$ representation $Q_D^{(m)}(B_m)$ of $D^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times \cong \mathrm{SO}_3$ [4, §4.6–4.9; 8, Theorem 4.2 and §4.3]. On $V_{m_1} \oplus V_{m_2}$, exterior multiplication of determinant lines $\det_{k_q}(V_{m_1}) \otimes \det_{k_q}(V_{m_2}) = \det_{k_q}(V_{m_1} \oplus V_{m_2})$ [8, §4.3] embeds $Q_D^{(m_1 + m_2)}(B_{m_1} \times B_{m_2})$ into $\pi_1^* Q_D^{(m_1)}(B_{m_1}) \cup \pi_2^* Q_D^{(m_2)}(B_{m_2})$ as the unique top-spin Clebsch–Gordan summand $\mathbf{2(m_1+m_2)+1}$ of $\mathbf{(2m_1+1)} \otimes \mathbf{(2m_2+1)} = \mathbf{2(m_1+m_2)+1} \oplus \dots \oplus \mathbf{(2|m_1-m_2|+1)}$. Since the Poincaré intersection pairing on the irreducible $\mathrm{SO}_3$-module $Q_D^{(m_1)}(B_{m_1}) \subset H^{m_1, m_1}(B_{m_1}, \mathbb{Q})$ is $\mathrm{SO}_3$-invariant and non-degenerate by Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations (with explicit Gram determinants $\det = -8$ for $m_1 = 1$ and $\det = 24,576$ for $m_1 = 2$ in the standard basis of the $D_2$ model), contracting the non-zero top-spin summand against $Q_D^{(m_1)}(B_{m_1})$ yields a non-zero $\mathrm{SO}_3$-equivariant map to the irreducible module $Q_D^{(m_2)}(B_{m_2})$, which is surjective by Schur's Lemma (descending Weil-class algebraicity through a product with an abelian surface, cf. Markman [6, §11.5, Step 2], and certified by exact rational matrix ranks $5, 7, 9, 5$ in `Test 2` of the supplement).

(2) Let $B \in \mathcal{M}_D(T)$ be any member. By Theorem 2.2: if $d_+ = 3$, $\operatorname{Hg}(B) = U_D(V, T)^\circ$, so the ring of Hodge classes on $B^n$ is generated by divisors and $Q_D^{(3)}(B)$ [1, §6]; if $d_+ = 2$, no such member exists; if $d_+ = 0$, every Hodge class on $B^n$ is generated in degree $2$ by divisors by Theorem 2.2(5). Finally, if $d_+ = 1$, Theorem 2.2(4) gives $B \sim B_1 \times B_2$ with $\operatorname{Hg}(B) = T_{K_1} \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$. Since $Q_D^{(3)}(B_1 \times B_2)$ is algebraic on $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ and $Q_D^{(1)}(B_1)$ consists of divisors, Part (1) proves that $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2) = \mathcal{S}_{1,2}(Q_D^{(1)}(B_1) \otimes Q_D^{(3)}(B_1 \times B_2))$ is algebraic on $B_2$ (note that algebraicity of $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2)$ on the fourfold factor $B_2$ also follows independently from Markman [6, Theorem 1.2] and [4, §4.6–4.9]; our slant contraction $\mathcal{S}_{1,2}$ makes the proof on $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ completely self-contained and extends via $\mathcal{S}_{1,3}$ to sixfold factors $B_3$ in Section 5, including non-split Theorem C sixfolds where [6, Theorem 1.2] does not apply). Because $\operatorname{Hg}(B_1 \times B_2) = T_{K_1} \times U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ$ is a direct product, $(H^*(B_1^n \times B_2^n, \mathbb{Q}))^{\operatorname{Hg}(B)} = H^*(B_1^n, \mathbb{Q})^{T_{K_1}} \otimes H^*(B_2^n, \mathbb{Q})^{U_D(V_2, T_2)^\circ}$ is generated by divisors on $B_1^n$ and $B_2^n$ together with $4$-slot volume tensors from $Q_D^{(2)}(B_2)$ placed across factors of $B_2^n$, proving the Hodge conjecture for all $B^n$ ($n \ge 1$). $\square$

### 3.2 Rank-3 Universality of $T_0$ and Arithmetic Embedding of All Fourfold Discriminants $\Delta_2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8$

Over the local division algebra $D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_2$, a **rank-$2$** skew-Hermitian form fails to be universal on pure quaternions: for example, $\langle i, i+j+k \rangle$ (discriminant $3$) cannot represent $i+2j$ (norm $5$) over $D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_2$, because a rank-$1$ orthogonal complement $\langle u \rangle$ would require $\operatorname{Nrd}(u) \equiv 3/5 \equiv 15 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, which does not exist in $(D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_2)^-$. By contrast, in **rank $3$**, $T_0 \perp \langle -t_1 \rangle$ has **rank $4$**, where Tsukamoto's isotropy theorem [2, Theorem 1] applies!

**Theorem 3.2 (Rank-3 Universality of $T_0$ on $D_2^- \setminus \{0\}$ and Embedding of All $\Delta_2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8$ Fourfold Curves).**
1. *The rank-$3$ skew-Hermitian space $(D_2^3, T_0)$ ($T_0 = \langle i, i+j+k, i+2j \rangle$) is **universal on pure quaternions**: for every $t_1 \in D_2^- \setminus \{0\}$, there exists $v_1 \in D_2^3 \setminus \{0\}$ such that $v_1^* T_0 v_1 = t_1$.*
2. *As $v_1$ ranges over $D_2^3 \setminus \{0\}$, the orthogonal complement $V_2(v_1) := v_1^\perp \subset D_2^3$ defines a compact $1$-dimensional PEL Shimura curve $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_2(v_1)) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_0)$ whose square-free discriminant $\Delta_2 \equiv 15\,\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(t_1) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ attains **every positive square-free integer $\Delta_2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8$**—including the entire fourfold non-embedding locus $\Delta_2 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$. On every member $B_2 \in \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_2(v_1))$, $Q_{D_2}^{(2)}(B_2)$ is algebraic (both by Theorem 3.1(1) and by Markman [6, Theorem 1.2]), and the Hodge conjecture holds for all powers $B_2^n$ ($n \ge 1$) and all mixed products $B_1^m \times B_2^n$.*

*Proof.*
(1) Fix any $t_1 \in D_2^- \setminus \{0\}$ and consider the **rank-$4$** skew-Hermitian form $T_4 := T_0 \perp \langle -t_1 \rangle$ on $D_2^3 \oplus D_2$. We verify that $T_4$ is locally isotropic at every place $v$ of $\mathbb{Q}$:
- At the ramified places $v \in \{2, \infty\}$, $D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_v$ is a local division quaternion algebra, and since $\operatorname{rk}_{D_2}(T_4) = 4$, Tsukamoto [2, Theorem 1] proves that $T_4$ is **isotropic** over $D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_v$ (concrete $2$-adic verification in `Test 3` of the supplement: while rank-$1$ pure quaternion norms realize only the $7$ square-classes $\{1, 3, 5, 2, 6, 10, 14\} \subset \mathbb{Q}_2^\times / (\mathbb{Q}_2^\times)^2$, rank-$2$ diagonal forms $\langle u_1, u_2 \rangle$ realize **all $8$ square-classes** as products $\operatorname{Nrd}(u_1)\operatorname{Nrd}(u_2)$, including $7 \equiv 3 \cdot 5 \pmod 8$, so $\langle t_1, u_1, u_2 \rangle \simeq T_0$ over $D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_2$ by Tsukamoto [2, Proposition 3 / Theorem 2]).
- At every odd prime $p \ge 3$, $D_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_p \cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, and under Morita equivalence $T_4$ corresponds to an $8$-dimensional quadratic form $S_p(T_4)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Since every quadratic form of dimension $\ge 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is isotropic (indeed $S_p(T_4)$ has Witt index $\ge 2$, hence contains a free isotropic $M_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$-line), $T_4$ is locally isotropic at all $p \ge 3$.

By the Hasse–Minkowski theorem for isotropy of skew-Hermitian forms over quaternion algebras [14, Chapter 10, Theorem 4.1], $T_4 = T_0 \perp \langle -t_1 \rangle$ is **globally isotropic** over $D_2$: there exists $(v_1, c) \in (D_2^3 \oplus D_2) \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ such that $v_1^* T_0 v_1 - \bar{c} t_1 c = 0$. Since $(D_2^3, T_0)$ is **anisotropic** over $D_2$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$), we cannot have $c = 0$; dividing by $c \in D_2^\times$ gives $(v_1 c^{-1})^* T_0 (v_1 c^{-1}) = t_1$.

(2) By Legendre's three-squares theorem, the square-free parts of $\delta_1 = \operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(t_1)$ ($t_1 \in D_2^- \setminus \{0\}$) are all positive square-free integers $\delta_1 \not\equiv 7 \pmod 8$; since $15 \equiv -1 \pmod 8$, $\Delta_2 = \operatorname{sqfree}(15\delta_1)$ attains all positive square-free integers $\Delta_2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8$. For any $B_2 \in \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_2(v_1))$ and $B_1 \in \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(\langle t_1 \rangle)$, $B_1 \times B_2 \in \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_0)$, so Theorem 3.1 applies. $\square$

Finally, whenever $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta_3}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$ (including every $\Delta_3 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$), $\mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1))$ is a compact Theorem C PEL threefold, so Theorem 3.1(2) immediately establishes the unconditional Hodge conjecture in all codimensions for all powers $B_3^n$ ($n \ge 1$) of **every** member $B_3 \in \mathcal{M}_{D_2}(T_3(t_1))$—including all special, reducible, and CM members. $\square$

### 5.2 The Tits–Clifford Division Algebra of Degree 4 Over $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$

Finally, we record the exact Clifford-algebraic meaning of the Theorem C non-embedding condition $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$.

**Theorem 5.3 (Tits–Clifford Index-4 Division Algebra Characterization of Theorem C).**
*Let $D / \mathbb{Q}$ be a definite quaternion division algebra, $(V, T)$ a rank-$3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian space of square-free discriminant $\Delta(T) \notin (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ (signed discriminant $\delta(T) = -\Delta(T)$), and $(C_0(V, T), \underline{\tau}_0)$ its even Clifford algebra with canonical unitary involution over its center $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$ ($\deg_k C_0(V, T) = 2^{3-1} = 4$, $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} C_0(V, T) = 32$) [16, §8.B and Theorem 9.14]. Then:*
1. *In the Brauer group $\operatorname{Br}(k)$, the class $[C_0(V, T)]$ satisfies Tits's fundamental relation*
   $$2\,[C_0(V, T)] \;=\; [D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k] \in \operatorname{Br}(k), \qquad \operatorname{Cores}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\bigl([C_0(V, T)]\bigr) \;=\; 0 \in \operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{Q}).$$
2. *Consequently, the Theorem C non-embedding condition $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$ holds if and only if $[C_0(V, T)] \in \operatorname{Br}(k)$ has exact period $4$ and Schur index $\operatorname{ind}_k C_0(V, T) = 4$—that is, if and only if **$C_0(V, T)$ is a division algebra of degree $4$ over $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$**!*
3. *Under the spinor isomorphism $\operatorname{Spin}(V, T) \cong \mathrm{SU}(C_0(V, T), \underline{\tau}_0)$ ($\operatorname{Spin}(V, T)(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathrm{SU}(3,1)$), the $32$-dimensional regular module $W_{\mathrm{spin}} = C_0(V, T)$ defines a $3$-dimensional Kuga–Satake PEL family of polarized Albert Type-IV abelian $16$-folds $A_{\mathrm{spin}}$ whose endomorphism algebra contains $C_0(V, T)^{\mathrm{op}}$ over $k$, which is a degree-$4$ division algebra over $k$ if and only if $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$.*

*Proof.*
(1) By Knus–Merkurjev–Rost–Tignol [16, Theorem 9.14], for any skew-Hermitian space $(V, T)$ of odd $D$-rank $m = 2r + 1$ ($r = 1$), $C_0(V, T)$ is a central simple algebra of degree $2^{2r} = 4$ over $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\delta(T)}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$, $\underline{\tau}_0$ is of the second kind (so $\operatorname{Cores}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}([C_0(V, T)]) = 0$ by Albert–Riehm–Scharlau [16, Theorem 3.1]), and $2[C_0(V, T)] = m[D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k] = [D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k] \in \operatorname{Br}(k)$.

(2) By Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether, $k \not\hookrightarrow D$ if and only if $D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k$ is a division quaternion algebra over $k$, i.e., $[D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k]$ has exact period $2$ in $\operatorname{Br}(k)$: at any finite prime $w \in \operatorname{Ram}(D)$ that splits in $k$ into $v, \bar{v}$ with $k_v = \mathbb{Q}_w$ (such as $w = 2$ for $D_2$ and $\Delta_3 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, where $-\Delta_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 8$), $\operatorname{inv}_v([D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k]) = [k_v : \mathbb{Q}_w]\operatorname{inv}_w([D]) = \frac{1}{2} \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$. By Tits's relation $2[C_0(V, T)] = [D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k]$ in $\operatorname{Br}(k)$, $[D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} k]$ has exact period $2$ if and only if $[C_0(V, T)]$ has exact period $4$ in $\operatorname{Br}(k)$ (with $2\operatorname{inv}_v([C_0(V, T)]) = \frac{1}{2} \pmod 1$, i.e., $\operatorname{inv}_v([C_0(V, T)]) \in \{\frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}\} \subset \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$). Since $\operatorname{per}([C_0]) \mid \operatorname{ind}_k(C_0) \mid \deg_k(C_0) = 4$, period $4$ is equivalent to $\operatorname{ind}_k C_0(V, T) = 4$, i.e., $C_0(V, T)$ is a division algebra of degree $4$ over $k$. Part (3) is the standard Kuga–Satake spinor construction for $\mathrm{SO}^*(6) \sim \mathrm{SU}(3,1)$ [4, §2; 16, §15.D] (note that the Clifford bimodule map relates $\operatorname{End}(A_{\mathrm{spin}})$ to $H^2(B, \mathbb{Q})$, rather than transferring middle-degree Weil classes on $B$). $\square$
