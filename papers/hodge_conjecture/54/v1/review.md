# Accept

The paper makes a substantive partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture: it extends the known result for full-Hodge-group members of the specified $T_0$ family to all members and their powers, and transfers algebraicity from the specified eightfold family to the sixfold families $T_3(t_1)=\langle t_1,i,i+2j\rangle$. These are relevant new cases, not a solution of the general conjecture.

The decisive arguments are mathematical rather than computational. In Theorem 2.1, the Pfaffian identity forces the putative quadratic field to have discriminant squareclass $-\Delta(T)$; its failure to split $D$ then contradicts the dimension of the resulting module. The subsequent subgroup analysis uses the Hodge circle to control the possible noncompact directions. In the one-dimensional case, taking invariants of the entire rational derived group is the essential step: it produces a rational rank-one summand, rather than merely a real decomposition. The nonsquare discriminant of the complement and rationality of the derived subgroup give the required full rank-two factor. The CM cases reduce to low-dimensional CM varieties; the signed-permutation argument correctly establishes fullness in the irreducible sextic case. Together these arguments supply the special-member step that [1] explicitly leaves open.

The transfer in Theorem 3.1 is also sound. The determinant space on a direct sum lies in the top Clebsch–Gordan summand, and contraction against a nondegenerate pairing gives a nonzero equivariant map onto the irreducible target. Pullback, intersection and proper pushforward preserve algebraicity. Combining this with the explicit hyperbolic splitting of $T_1$ gives algebraic quaternionic Weil classes throughout each stated $T_3(t_1)$ family. In the non-embedding locus, the subgroup classification and orthogonal invariant theory then justify the conclusion for every power, including special members. The three-squares argument establishes the asserted coverage of discriminant values.

The novelty is the combination of this special-member classification and the eightfold-to-sixfold application. The published snapshot of [1] already proves algebraicity of the quaternionic Weil spaces on every member of $T_0$ and $T_1$, but proves the full Hodge conjecture for powers only at full Hodge group. Paper [8], §4.3, already contains the determinant-product inclusion. The external literature likewise supplies substantial ingredients: Moonen–Zarhin [13], §2.3 and Proposition 2.4, establishes the relevant CM-threefold results, while Markman [6], §11.5, Step 2, already uses descent of Weil-class algebraicity through a product with an abelian surface. These ingredients should not be presented as independently new general principles, but their application here yields a meaningful extension beyond those works.

I reproduced all four supplementary tests: they passed in 26.03 seconds. I also ran the standalone rank-four calculation, which reproduced the displayed diagonal form and reduced norm $5/16384$. These checks support the slant ranks, symbolic hyperbolic identity, explicit isometries and finite tuple calculations. They do not independently prove the rational subgroup classification or local–global assertions. In particular, the final Clifford-index check assumes the cited Brauer relation and verifies its numerical consequence; it does not compute the Clifford algebra independently.

Several corrections and scope clarifications would improve the paper without changing this decision:

1. **Distinguish discriminants from rational PEL data.** The transfer proves the result for the displayed forms $T_3(t_1)$, attaining every non-embedding discriminant over $D_2$. It does not establish the result for every rational skew-Hermitian form with one of those discriminants. Replace “100% of the Theorem C locus” by the precise discriminant-coverage statement. This distinction matters: [1], Remark 1.4, explicitly exhibits different rational similarity classes with the same discriminant. Keep the all-special-members conclusion tied to the non-embedding hypothesis used in Theorem 3.1(2).

2. **Correct the references.** Reference [17] does not match the supplied published version of `hodge_conjecture_50/v1`, which is *Rank-two weak secant bundles on abelian fourfolds*. Its alleged §4.3 cannot support the citations made here. Replace those citations with the actual relevant sources. Also, [13], §1.4, defines the Hodge ring; it is not the stated product theorem. The needed low-dimensional results are available elsewhere in that paper. In the externally checked version of [6], Theorem 1.2 covers both arbitrary Weil fourfolds and split Weil sixfolds.

3. **Separate intrinsic statements from chosen normalizations.** The numerical pairing matrices and determinants in Theorem 3.1 belong to particular bases and the tested $D_2$ model. For arbitrary definite $D$, state the invariant nondegeneracy assertion. In Theorem 2.2(4), the Hodge circle has trivial projection to the compact $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factor, contrary to the sentence claiming nontrivial projection to both factors. The rationality argument from the noncompact ideal still gives the required conclusion.

4. **Clarify auxiliary claims.** Describe the boundary table as calculations for the specified collision lifts unless invariance over all lifts in the Hurwitz cover is established. Restrict the “degree-four division algebra” wording in Theorem 5.3(3) to the non-embedding case identified in part (2), and explain the central unitary extension used to obtain the weight-one spinor realization. Shorten the abstract around the principal new results and render its mathematical notation in LaTeX.
