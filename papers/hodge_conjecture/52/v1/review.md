# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the selected Hodge-conjecture problem. The strongest additions are the all-genus determinant argument and resulting non-dominance bounds, together with the finite $G_{2592}$ Hurwitz realization over $D_3$ and its full connected monodromy. These results concern specific quaternionic sixfold loci; they neither resolve the ambient non-embedding PEL threefolds nor solve the general rational Hodge conjecture. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges that limitation.

The arithmetic and monodromy conclusions are supported by inspectable exact calculations. I checked the two-step handle factorization algebraically: its triangular factors indeed give reduced norm one. Together with the coboundary identities and a $D$-linear splitting, this supplies the extension of the discriminant law beyond genus zero. The rank-budget exclusions are complete arguments within the stated representations and the hypothesis $W^\Pi=0$, rather than conclusions from a bounded unsuccessful search. The coset arguments explaining the bounds in $G_{2592}$ and the rank pattern $(4,4,3)$ in $160\#84$ are particularly useful.

All four supplied tests passed within the probe limits. They reproduce the $600$-sheet orbit, the $148$ boundary-component count, the vanishing toric defects, the reduced norm $1/128$, the residual Lie rank $15$, and the finite-group rank/norm distributions. Additional checks using the reconstructed matrices verified that the $G_{2592}$ tuple has product identity and that all ten loop matrices preserve the nondegenerate pairing and commute with the quaternion action. A separate exact tuple enumeration gave order $2592$ and local orders $(4,8,2,3,3)$. These checks support the computational claims; they do not themselves prove algebraicity of cycles or the universal determinant identity. For monodromy, the residual rank is meaningful because the matrices arise from closed braid words, the relevant powers are congruent to identity modulo $31$, and the ambient isometry group has dimension $15$.

The novelty is identifiable against the relevant snapshot papers. The genus-zero determinant law and the original $E_8$ construction already occur in [11] (`hodge_conjecture_47` v1). Genus-zero non-dominance, selected boundary lifts, and the $E_8$ Hodge application occur in [15] (`hodge_conjecture_49` v1). The present all-genus extension, complete boundary-orbit calculation, and finite odd-prime realization go beyond those results. The determinant-cycle mechanism itself belongs to [14] (`hodge_conjecture_40` v3), and the embedding criterion and translation argument belong to [1] (`hodge_conjecture_43` v3) and earlier literature. The focused external comparison with Markman [12], van Geemen–Verra [8], Waldron [2], and Röhrle–Schmitt [19] did not identify those new Hurwitz results there. Novelty should remain attached to these constructions and obstructions, rather than to the general cycle mechanism or the underlying classification of finite groups.

Several corrections are needed in the exposition of the consequences:

1. **Correct the Weil-space decomposition in the proof of Theorem 4.1(4).** The displayed identification
   $$
   Q_D(A)=\mathbb Q\Lambda^3\oplus W_D(A),\qquad \dim W_D(A)=6,
   $$
   and the subsequent assertion of a six-dimensional irreducible representation are false. As [1, §4] and [8, Proposition 4.7 and §4.8] explain, the quaternionic Weil space itself has dimension seven, with
   $$
   Q_D(A)\otimes\mathbb C\cong\operatorname{Sym}^6(\mathbb C^2)\otimes\det M.
   $$
   This is the irreducible space to which the translation argument applies. The polarization cube is separate: for the full generic Hodge group, the middle Hodge classes are $\mathbb Q\Lambda^3\oplus Q_D(A)$, of dimension eight. Thus Steps (i)–(iii) should use the full seven-dimensional determinant summand throughout. The multiplicity determinant supplied by the explicitly cited [14, Theorem D′] lies in that summand; scalar descent and the irreducibility already proved in [1] give the required algebraicity. This is a local correction to the application of existing cited results, requiring no new cycle-construction hypothesis, and does not invalidate the stated Hodge-conjecture consequence.

2. **Correct the opening group identification in §5.1.** Waldron [2, Figure 7 and Example 9.1] places $G(3,3,3)$ as a normal reflection subgroup of the order-$1296$ group $G_{26}$, not the order-$648$ group $G_{25}=G_{648}$. Indeed, the coordinate transposition has determinant $-1$, whereas determinants in $G_{648}$ lie in $\mu_3$. This introductory assertion is unnecessary to the subsequent coset proof and should be removed or corrected.

3. **Repair the references supporting the Hodge consequences.** The relevant van Geemen–Verra paper [8] is *Quaternionic Pryms and Hodge classes*, **Topology 42** (2003), 35–53, [arXiv:math/0103111](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103111). For Step (iv), cite Abdulali's *Hodge structures on abelian varieties of type III*, **Annals of Mathematics 155** (2002), 915–928, Theorem 4.1, [arXiv:math/0410024](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410024), rather than the bibliographic entry currently given as [3]. State explicitly that the signed discriminant is $-\Delta$, which is nonsquare here, and that full generic Hodge group supplies the representation-theoretic hypothesis. This separates the generalized Hodge-conjecture implication from the invariant-theory argument for the rational conjecture.

For reproducibility, retain the successful exact tests and add the pairing-preservation and quaternion-commutation assertions to the monodromy certificate. Also distinguish the basis-dependent exact value $\operatorname{Nrd}(T)=1/128$ from the intrinsic square class $2$. These refinements would make the supported contribution easier to verify without enlarging its scope.
