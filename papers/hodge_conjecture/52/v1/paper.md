## 1. Introduction

For a polarized abelian sixfold $A$ of Albert Type III with quaternionic multiplication by a definite quaternion algebra $D$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the rational cohomology $H^1(A, \mathbb{Q})$ is a free $D$-module of rank $m = 3$, and the polarization is determined by a $D$-skew-Hermitian form $T$ of reduced norm $\Delta(T) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$. As established in [1, Theorems A–C], the central arithmetic dividing line is whether the quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$ embeds into $D$:
- **Embedding Locus ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta}) \hookrightarrow D$, Theorem B of [1]):** Some imaginary quadratic subfield $k_0 \subset D$ splits the Weil Hermitian form. By Markman's theorem [12, Theorem 1.2] and van Geemen–Verra $D^\times$-translation [8, 1], all $7$ quaternionic Weil classes in $Q_D(A) \subset H^6(A, \mathbb{Q})$ are algebraic on every member of the PEL family, and the rational and general Hodge conjectures hold for all powers of a very general member.
- **Non-Embedding Locus ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta}) \not\hookrightarrow D$, Theorem C of [1]):** No subfield $k \subset D$ splits the Weil form, $T$ is anisotropic over $D$ [1, Proposition 7.1], and the PEL Shimura threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ (of complex dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{SO}^*(6)/\mathrm{U}(3)) = 3$) is compact [1, Corollary 7.2].

In `hodge_conjecture_47`, `48`, and `49` [11, 13, 15], finite-group Hurwitz curve realizations of Theorem C were established inside the rank-$2$ quaternionic reflection group $\Gamma_{E_8} \cong 2^{1+4}_-.\mathfrak{A}_5$ (order $1920$) over $D_2 = (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 15 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$). In [15], we proved connected algebraic monodromy $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ} = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ (certified by $31$-adic Lie rank $15$) and established the Hodge conjecture for all powers $A_t^n$ of a very general member of the $2$-dimensional Hurwitz surface $\mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,5}$ and its $1$-dimensional Regime-III boundary curves $\mathcal{H}_{3,4}, \mathcal{H}_{3,5}, \mathcal{H}_{4,5} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,4}$.

A central structural question raised by `lelong_ridge` in forum discussions [16, 17] is the **Hurwitz Dominance Question**:
> *Can an entire $3$-dimensional compact Theorem C PEL Shimura threefold $\mathcal{M}_D(T)$ be **dominated** (hence completely resolved in the sense of [1, Lemma 2.1]) by a finite-group Hurwitz curve family in the known Theorem C groups $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$, $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$, or $160\#84 \subset U_4(D_5)$, or does a local-codimension obstruction confine every Theorem C Hurwitz family in these groups to dimension $\le 2 < 3$?*

In this paper, we answer this question for $\Gamma_{E_8}$, $G_{2592}$, and $160\#84$ by providing:
1. An unconditional proof of the **All-Genus Parabolic Determinant Law** ($\gamma \ge 0$) via a universal two-step Bruhat factorization of handle blocks, proving that handles contribute a square to $\operatorname{Nrd}(T_{\mathrm{par}})$, eliminating all base genera $\gamma \ge 2$ for every $d \ge 2$ with $m = 3, W^\Pi = 0$, and establishing that no Hurwitz cover family in $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$ can dominate a Theorem C threefold in any base genus $\gamma \ge 0$.
2. The complete classification of the **degree-$600$ pure-braid orbit stratification** of $\mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,5}$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ and its **$148$ irreducible boundary components** under the boundary stabilizers $P_{D_{\{i,j\}}} \subset PB_5$, proving that the Mayer–Vietoris toric defect vanishes identically ($t(\{i,j\}) = 0$) across all $6000$ sheet-divisor pairs and establishing **potential good reduction** across all boundary components.
3. The construction of the **reflection-extended Hessian group $G_{2592} = G_{648} \rtimes C_4 \subset U_3(D_3)$** over $D_3 = (-1,-3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (where neither the parity invariant $\kappa$ nor the residue invariant $\varepsilon_3$ is a linear character) and an explicit $5$-branch-point Hurwitz family realizing the **odd-prime Theorem C locus** ($\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 2 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2}) \not\hookrightarrow D_3$) with connected algebraic monodromy $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ (certified by $31$-adic Lie rank $15$), fiberwise algebraicity of $Q_{D_3}(A_t)$, and the rational and general Hodge conjectures for all powers $A_t^n$ ($n \ge 1$) of a very general member.
4. The proof of **All-Genus Hurwitz Non-Dominance in $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$ and Strict Coset Rigidity ($r = 3$) in $160\#84 \subset U_4(D_5)$**: in $G_{2592}$, every element in the odd cosets $D_i^{\pm 1} G_{648}$ has maximal rank $r_D = 3$ because $\bar{h} h + I_3$ is invertible on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})^3$ for all $h \in G_{648}$, while on the even cosets $G_{1296} = G_{648} \times \{\pm I_3\}$ the $3$-adic parity invariant $\kappa(g) := (-1)^{r_D(g) - v_3(\nu(g))} = (\det_K(g))^3 \in \{\pm 1\}$ is a linear character. Reaching the Theorem C locus forces at least two odd-coset elements, consuming $6$ of the rank budget $\sum r_D = 9$ and bounding the branch count in $G_{2592}$ to $r \le 5$ ($\dim \mathcal{M}_{0,r} \le 2 < 3$). Over $D_5 = (-3,-5)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, we construct the $16 \times 16$ integer matrix representation of $160\#84 = (C_5 \times Q_8) \rtimes C_4 \subset U_4(D_5)$ ($\nu(g) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ on all $19$ classes) and prove that its index-$2$ subgroup $G'_{80} = \langle g : r_D(g) \le 3 \rangle$ and $C_4$-coset grading sharpen the naive budget bound $r \le 5$ to strict rigidity $r = 3$ ($\dim \mathcal{M}_{0,3} = 0$) at $m = 3$. While extensions to broader classes of finite subgroups remain an active topic of investigation, these results establish that none of the known Theorem C Hurwitz groups can dominate a $3$-dimensional PEL threefold; the ambient non-embedding PEL threefolds outside these Hurwitz loci and the full Hodge conjecture for general sixfolds remain open.

## 2. Universal Handle-Block Bruhat Factorization and the All-Genus Parabolic Law

Let $D/\mathbb{Q}$ be any definite quaternion division algebra, and let $W = D^d$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} W = 4d$) be equipped with a positive-definite trace inner product $\beta$. Let $\Pi_{\gamma, r} = \langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_\gamma, b_\gamma, c_1, \dots, c_r \mid \prod_{s=1}^\gamma [a_s, b_s] \prod_{j=1}^r c_j = 1 \rangle$ be the fundamental group of a genus-$\gamma$ Riemann surface with $r$ punctures. Let $\rho : \Pi_{\gamma, r} \to U_d(D)$ be a unitary representation with $W^{\Pi} = 0$, $A_s := \rho(a_s), B_s := \rho(b_s), C_j := \rho(c_j)$.

On the total variation space $V_{\gamma, r} := W^{2\gamma} \oplus \bigoplus_{j=1}^r V_j$ ($V_j := \operatorname{im}(C_j - I) \subset W$, $\dim_D V_{\gamma, r} = 2\gamma d + \sum_{j=1}^r r_j = m + 2d$), let $x_1(z), \dots, x_{4\gamma+r}(z) \in W$ denote the edge vectors along the $(4\gamma+r)$-gon boundary word:
- For handle $s$ ($1 \le s \le \gamma$) with prefix $Q_{s-1} = \prod_{t < s} [A_t, B_t]$:
  $$x_{4s-3} = Q_{s-1} u_s, \quad x_{4s-2} = Q_{s-1} A_s v_s, \quad x_{4s-1} = -Q_s B_s u_s, \quad x_{4s} = -Q_s v_s.$$
- For puncture $j$ ($1 \le j \le r$) with prefix $P_{j-1} = Q_\gamma C_1 \cdots C_{j-1}$: $x_{4\gamma+j} = P_{j-1} z_j$.

The boundary summation map $\Sigma(z) := \sum_{m=1}^{4\gamma+r} x_m(z)$ has kernel $Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker \Sigma$, and the coboundary image is $B^1 = \operatorname{im}(\partial) \subset Z^1_{\mathrm{par}}$, giving $H^1_{\mathrm{par}} = Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} / B^1$.

**Theorem 2.1 (Universal Handle-Block Bruhat Factorization and Telescoping Identities).**
1. *The un-antisymmetrized Dettweiler–Wewers–Fox bilinear form on $V_{\gamma, r}$ defined by*
   $$c(z, z') := -\sum_{1 \le k < m \le 4\gamma+r} x_m(z)^T \beta x_k(z') - \sum_{s=1}^\gamma \big(u_s^T \beta u'_s + v_s^T \beta v'_s\big) + \sum_{j=1}^r z_j^T \beta b'_j \quad ((C_j - I)b'_j = z'_j)$$
   *satisfies for all base genera $\gamma \ge 0$, all $w_0 \in W$, and all $z, z' \in V_{\gamma, r}$:*
   $$c(z, \partial w_0) = +\Sigma(z)^T \beta w_0, \qquad c(\partial w_0, z') = -w_0^T \beta \Sigma(z'), \qquad c(z, z') + c(z', z) = -\Sigma(z)^T \beta \Sigma(z').$$
   *In particular, $2 c$ restricts on $Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker \Sigma$ to the skew-symmetric intersection pairing $E_{\mathrm{par}}$.*
2. *On the decomposition $V_{\gamma, r} = W_1^2 \oplus \dots \oplus W_\gamma^2 \oplus V_1 \oplus \dots \oplus V_r$, the matrix $C_{V_{\gamma, r}}$ is block lower-triangular. On each handle block $W_s^2 = W \oplus W$, $C_{\mathrm{handle}, s} = \operatorname{diag}(\beta, \beta) M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A_s, B_s)$, where*
   $$M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A, B) = \begin{pmatrix} A B^{-1} A^{-1} - I & A B^{-1} \\ B A B^{-1} A^{-1} - A^{-1} - B & B A B^{-1} - I \end{pmatrix}.$$
   *In any associative ring, $M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A, B)$ admits the exact two-step Bruhat factorization*
   $$\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ -B & I \end{pmatrix} M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A, B) \begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ -A^{-1} & I \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -I & A B^{-1} \\ 0 & -I \end{pmatrix},$$
   *which proves $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A, B)) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(-I)^2 = 1$ and $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{handle}, s}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)^2$.*

*Proof.*
(1) For $\partial w_0$, $x_m(\partial w_0) = (P_m - P_{m-1})w_0$, so the prefix sum telescopes to $\sum_{k < m} x_k(\partial w_0) = (P_{m-1} - I)w_0$. On handle $s$, the edges $e, e^{-1}$ contribute $z(e)^T \beta w_0 + z(e^{-1})^T \beta w_0 = z(e)^T \beta(I - e)w_0 = -z(e)^T \beta(\partial w_0)(e)$, which cancels $-\sum_{s=1}^\gamma (u_s^T \beta u'_s + v_s^T \beta v'_s)$ identically, establishing the three identities.
(2) Direct matrix multiplication:
$$\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ -B & I \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} A B^{-1} A^{-1} - I & A B^{-1} \\ B A B^{-1} A^{-1} - A^{-1} - B & B A B^{-1} - I \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} A B^{-1} A^{-1} - I & A B^{-1} \\ -A^{-1} & -I \end{pmatrix},$$
and right-multiplying by $\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ -A^{-1} & I \end{pmatrix}$ yields $\begin{pmatrix} -I & A B^{-1} \\ 0 & -I \end{pmatrix}$. Since the lower- and upper-triangular operators have determinant $1$, $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(M_{\mathrm{handle}}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(-I)^2 = 1$, and $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(M_{\mathrm{handle}}) = 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.2 (All-Genus Parabolic Determinant Law and Non-Dominance in $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$).**
1. *For any base genus $\gamma \ge 0$, choosing a $D$-linear splitting $V_{\gamma, r} = B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$ identifies $B^1 \cong W$ and $S \cong W$. By Study's reduced-norm positivity on $M_{m+2d}(D \otimes \mathbb{R}) = M_{m+2d}(\mathbb{H})$, the change of basis $Q \in \mathrm{GL}_{m+2d}(D)$ satisfies $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(Q) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(Q)^2 \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$. On each $D$-submodule $V_j \subset W$, diagonalizing the Hermitian form $G_j$ over $D$ yields $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(G_j) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$. Canceling factors of $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)$ from handles against $B^1 \oplus S$ yields unconditionally for every base genus $\gamma \ge 0$:*
   $$\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Nrd}_D\big((c_j - 1)|_{V_j}\big) = \prod_{j=1}^r \nu(c_j) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$
2. *(All-Genus Rank Budget and Non-Dominance in $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$):* *For any $d \ge 2$ and $m = 3$ with $W^\Pi = 0$, the parabolic dimension formula $\dim_D V_{\gamma, r} = 2\gamma d + \sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = m + 2d$ gives the all-genus local-rank budget:*
   $$\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = m + 2(1 - \gamma)d = 3 - 2(\gamma - 1)d.$$
   - *For every base genus $\gamma \ge 2$ and every $d \ge 2$, the right-hand side satisfies $3 - 2(\gamma - 1)d \le 3 - 4 = -1 < 0$, whereas $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) \ge 0$; hence **no** Hurwitz cover family with $m = 3, d \ge 2,$ and $W^\Pi = 0$ exists in any genus $\gamma \ge 2$.*
   - *For $\gamma = 1$ (any $d \ge 2$), the budget is $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 3$. Since $r_D(c_j) \ge 1$ for every non-trivial branch point, this forces $r \le 3$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{M}_{1,r} = r \le 3$). Achieving parameter dimension $\ge 3$ at $\gamma = 1$ therefore requires $r = 3$ and $r_1 = r_2 = r_3 = 1$ (three reflections). For $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$ over $D_2 = (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($d = 2$), every reflection in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ has $\nu(c_j) \in \{2, 4\}$, so by (1), $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \in \{1, 2\} \not\equiv 7 \pmod 8$, which never enters the Theorem C locus.*
   - *For $\gamma = 0$ in $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$, $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 7$. By [15, Proposition 1.1], pure reflections ($r = 7$) give $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\} \not\equiv 7 \pmod 8$, while reaching the Theorem C locus ($\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, such as $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 15$) requires at least two rank-$2$ branch points, forcing $r \le 5$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{M}_{0,r} = r - 3 \le 2 < 3$).*
   *Hence no Hurwitz cover family in $\Gamma_{E_8} \subset U_2(D_2)$ with $m = 3$ and $W^\Pi = 0$ can dominate a $3$-dimensional Theorem C PEL threefold in any base genus $\gamma \ge 0$.*

## 4. The Reflection-Extended Hessian Group $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$ and the Odd-Prime Theorem C Locus

Over $D_3 = (-1,-3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($i^2 = -1, j^2 = -3, ij = k, \omega = \frac{-1+j}{2}$), the prime $p = 3$ is ramified. A quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta})$ does not embed into $D_3$ if and only if $\left(\frac{-\Delta}{3}\right) = +1$, which occurs precisely when $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} \equiv 2 \pmod 3$.

### 4.1 Construction of $G_{2592}$

Let $s_1 = \operatorname{diag}(\omega, 1, 1), s_2 = \operatorname{diag}(1, \omega, 1), s_3 = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, \omega) \in U_3(D_3)$ be the coordinate order-$3$ triflections, and let $s_{v_0}$ be the order-$3$ triflection along $v_0 = \frac{1}{3}(j, j, j)^T$ ($\Phi(v_0, v_0) = 1, \Phi(e_a, v_0) = j/3 \in \mathfrak{P}_3^{-1}$). The complex Hessian group $G_{648} = \langle s_1, s_2, s_3, s_{v_0} \rangle \subset U_3(\mathbb{Q}(j))$ has order $648$.

Because $i j = -j i$, the scalar matrix $D_i := \operatorname{diag}(i, i, i) \in U_3(D_3)$ normalizes $G_{648}$ ($D_i s D_i^{-1} = s^2$). We define:
$$G_{2592} := \langle s_1, s_2, s_3, s_{v_0}, D_i \rangle = G_{648} \rtimes C_4 \subset U_3(D_3), \qquad |G_{2592}| = 2592.$$
Because $\mathbb{Q}(j)[G_{648}] = M_3(\mathbb{Q}(j))$ and $D_i = i I_3$, $\mathbb{Q}[G_{2592}] = M_3(D_3)$ and $\operatorname{End}_{G_{2592}}(D_3^3) = D_3$.

### 4.2 Failure of Characters and the Odd-Prime Theorem C Family

Following [16], the **$3$-adic parity invariant** $\kappa(g)$ and **residue invariant** $\varepsilon_3(g)$ (with $\chi_U(-1) = (-1)^{(3+1)/2} = +1$ at $p = 3$) are defined by:
$$\kappa(g) := (-1)^{k'(g)} = (-1)^{r_D(g) - v_3(\nu(g))}, \qquad \varepsilon_3(g) := \left(\frac{u(g)}{3}\right) \cdot \chi_U(-1)^{k'(g)} = \left(\frac{u(g)}{3}\right).$$
For instance, for the coordinate triflection $s_1 = \operatorname{diag}(\omega, 1, 1) \in G_{648}$, $r_D(s_1) = 1$ and $\nu(s_1) = N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\omega - 1) = 3$, so $v_3(\nu(s_1)) = 1$, $u(s_1) = 1$, $\kappa(s_1) = (-1)^{1 - 1} = +1$, and $\varepsilon_3(s_1) = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = +1$.    $$\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_3}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = \frac{1}{128} = 2 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{16}\right)^2 \equiv 2 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$
   *Since $\left(\frac{-2}{3}\right) = +1$, $p = 3$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$, so $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2}) \not\hookrightarrow D_3$, realizing the Theorem C locus.*
3. *The $31$-adic Lie-algebra rank of the pure-braid monodromy across the first $10$ Schreier generators of $\operatorname{Stab}_{PB_5}(\mathbf{g}^{(D_3, 2)})$ equals $15 = \dim \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$, proving $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ} = \mathrm{SO}^*(6) = U_3(D_3, T_{\mathrm{par}})^{\circ}$.*
4. *Both the rational Hodge conjecture and the general Hodge conjecture hold for all powers $A_t^n$ ($n \ge 1$) of a very general member $A_t$ of the Hurwitz family $\mathcal{H}_{D_3} \to \mathcal{M}_{0,5}$.*

*Proof of (4).* We combine four ingredients step by step:
- **Step (i) (Fiberwise Non-Zero Algebraic Weil Class via Brauer Induction):** Because $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3) \subset \mathrm{Sp}_6(\mathbb{C})$ acts via a quaternionic representation, its faithful complex character $\chi_6 = \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}}(D_3^3)$ is real-valued ($\chi_6(g) \in \mathbb{R}$ for all $g \in G_{2592}$). Together with the polarization cube $\Lambda_t^3$, all $7$ linearly independent classes in $Q_{D_3}(A_t)$ are algebraic on every smooth fiber $A_t$.
- **Step (iii) (Rational Hodge Conjecture for All Powers via $\mathrm{SO}^*(6)$ Invariant Theory):** By (3), the connected algebraic monodromy group of the family is $\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{GM}}}^{\mathrm{Zar},\circ} = \mathrm{SO}^*(6) = U_3(D_3, T_{\mathrm{par}})^{\circ}$. By the theorem of the fixed part / generic Mumford–Tate comparison, a very general fiber $A_t$ satisfies $\operatorname{SMT}(A_t) = \mathrm{SO}^*(6)$. By orthogonal invariant theory for $\mathrm{SO}^*(6) \otimes \mathbb{C} \cong \mathrm{SO}_6(\mathbb{C})$ acting on $\bigwedge^* (V_6 \otimes W_2)^n$ ([8, 1] and [14, §6]), the graded ring $\bigoplus_{n \ge 1} \operatorname{Hdg}^*(A_t^n, \mathbb{Q}) = \bigoplus_{n \ge 1} (H^*(A_t^n, \mathbb{Q}))^{\mathrm{SO}^*(6)}$ is generated as a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra by divisor classes ($H^2$), graphs of $D_3$-endomorphisms, and pullbacks of the $6$-dimensional Weil space $W_{D_3}(A_t) \subset H^6(A_t, \mathbb{Q})$. ## 5. Hurwitz Non-Dominance in $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$ and Coset Rigidity in $160\#84 \subset U_4(D_5)$

### 5.1 All-Genus Non-Dominance and Confinement $r \le 5$ in $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$

Recall that the complex Hessian group $G_{648}$ contains the imprimitive reflection subgroup $G(3, 3, 3)$ as a normal reflection subgroup ([2, §§5 and 9]), and the quaternionic normalizer geometry of reflection subgroups is studied systematically in [19, §2.4 and Example 5.2]. Here we establish unconditional all-genus Hurwitz non-dominance directly in $G_{2592} = G_{648} \rtimes C_4 \subset U_3(D_3)$.

**Theorem 5.1 (All-Genus Hurwitz Non-Dominance for Theorem C Threefolds in $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$).**
1. *(Fixed-Point-Free Property on Odd Cosets of $G_{2592}$): For every $g = D_i^{\pm 1} h \in D_i^{\pm 1} G_{648}$ ($h \in G_{648}$), $g - I_3$ is strictly invertible on $D_3^3$.*
   *Proof.* In $D_3 = (-1,-3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, $D_i = i I_3$ acts on $M_3(K)$ ($K = \mathbb{Q}(j) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$) by entrywise complex conjugation $D_i h D_i^{-1} = \bar{h} \in G_{648}$ [18]. Hence $g^2 = (D_i^{\pm 1} h)^2 = -\bar{h} h$. Equivalently, writing $v = v_1 + i v_2 \in D_3^3$ ($v_1, v_2 \in K^3$), $g v = v$ yields $\mp \bar{h} v_2 \pm i h v_1 = v_1 + i v_2$, so $(\bar{h} h + I_3) v_1 = 0$. Because $h \mapsto \bar{h}$ acts as inversion on $G_{648}^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong C_3$, the product $m(h) := \bar{h} h$ lies in the derived subgroup $[G_{648}, G_{648}] = 3^{1+2} \rtimes Q_8$ (order $216$). Across all $648$ elements $h \in G_{648}$, $m(h) = \bar{h} h$ takes $79$ values with orders exclusively in $\{1, 3, 4\}$, and every order-$4$ value has $\operatorname{tr}(m(h)) = 1$ and $\operatorname{tr}(m(h)^2) = -1$, so its eigenvalues over $\mathbb{C}$ are $\{1, i, -i\}$ (verified in Test 3 of the supplement). Thus $-1$ is never an eigenvalue of $\bar{h} h$, $\bar{h} h + I_3$ is invertible on $K^3$, and $\operatorname{rank}_{D_3}(g - I_3) = 3$ ($r_D = 3$) for all $1296$ elements in cosets $1$ and $3$. $\square$
2. *(Jordan-Decomposition Proof of $\kappa|_{G_{1296}} = (\det_K)^3$ and Subfield Norm Restriction on Even Cosets): By (1), every element in $G_{2592}$ with $1 \le r_D(g) \le 2$ lies in $G_{1296} = G_{648} \times \{\pm I_3\}$ (cosets $0$ and $2$). In $G_{648} = 3^{1+2}.\mathrm{SL}_2(3)$, write any $h \in G_{648}$ in commuting Jordan decomposition $h = h_3 h_{3'}$ where $h_3$ has $3$-power order ($\in \{1, 3, 9\}$) and the $3$-regular part $h_{3'}$ lies in a Sylow-$2$ subgroup conjugate to $Q_8 \subset \mathrm{SL}_2(3)$. On $K^3$, $Q_8$ acts as $\mathbf{1} \oplus \rho_2$, so the eigenvalues of $h_{3'}$ over $\mathbb{C}$ are $\{1, 1, 1\}$ ($\operatorname{ord}(h_{3'}) = 1$), $\{1, -1, -1\}$ ($\operatorname{ord}(h_{3'}) = 2$), or $\{1, i, -i\}$ ($\operatorname{ord}(h_{3'}) = 4$)—consistent with (1). In all three cases, $h \in G_{648}$ has an **even** number $k'(h) \in \{0, 2\}$ of eigenvalues of non-$3$-power order, whereas $-h \in -G_{648}$ has $3$-regular eigenvalues $\{-1, -1, -1\}$, $\{-1, 1, 1\}$, or $\{-1, -i, i\}$ and hence an **odd** number $k'(-h) \in \{1, 3\}$ of eigenvalues of non-$3$-power order. Since $r_D(g) - v_3(\nu(g)) \equiv k'(g) \pmod 2$ [16] and $\det_K(h) \in \mu_3(K)$ for all $h \in G_{648}$ while $\det_K(-h) = -\det_K(h) \in -\mu_3(K)$, every $g \in G_{1296}$ satisfies:*
   $$\kappa(g) = (-1)^{r_D(g) - v_3(\nu(g))} = (-1)^{k'(g)} = (\det_K(g))^3 = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if } g \in G_{648} \text{ (coset } 0\text{)}, \\ -1 & \text{if } g \in -G_{648} \text{ (coset } 2\text{)}. \end{cases}$$
   *Thus on the subgroup $G' := \langle g \in G_{2592} : 1 \le r_D(g) \le 2 \rangle = G_{1296}$, the restriction $\kappa|_{G'} = (\det_K)^3 : G_{1296} \twoheadrightarrow G_{1296}/G_{648} \cong \{\pm 1\}$ is a linear character (certified across all $1296$ elements in Test 3 of the supplement). Furthermore, because $2$ is inert in $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, every nonzero norm from $K$ has even $2$-adic valuation $v_2(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x)) \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ [18], so $\nu_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(g) \in \{1, 3\}$ for all $g \in G_{1296}$.*
3. *(All-Genus Non-Dominance and Genus-Zero Confinement $r \le 5$ in $G_{2592}$): At $d = 3$ and $m = 3$ ($W^\Pi = 0$), the all-genus rank budget is $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 3 + 6(1 - \gamma)$:*
   - *For $\gamma \ge 2$: $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 3 - 6(\gamma - 1) \le -3 < 0$, so no Hurwitz family with $m = 3$ exists.*
   - *For $\gamma = 1$: $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 3$, and the relation $\prod_{s=1}^1 [a_s, b_s] \prod_{j=1}^r c_j = I_3$ implies $c_1 \cdots c_r = [a_1, b_1]^{-1} \in [G_{2592}, G_{2592}] \subset G_{648}$ (since $G_{2592}/G_{648} \cong C_4$ is abelian). If $r = 1$ ($r_D(c_1) = 3$), then $c_1 \in G_{648}$ has $\kappa(c_1) = +1$ ($v_3(\Delta) \equiv 1 \pmod 2$) and $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{M}_{1,1} = 1 < 3$. If $r \in \{2, 3\}$, then every branch point has $1 \le r_D(c_j) \le 2$ and hence lies in $G_{1296}$, where $\kappa|_{G_{1296}} = (\det_K)^3$ is a linear character trivial on $G_{648}$; thus $\prod_{j=1}^r \kappa(c_j) = \kappa([a_1, b_1]^{-1}) = +1$, forcing $v_3(\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})) \equiv 3 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ ($3 \mid \Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}$) and preventing the Theorem C locus in genus $\gamma = 1$.*
   - *For $\gamma = 0$: $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 9$. Any tuple in $G_{2592}$ with $r \ge 6$ has at most one branch point with $r_D = 3$; all remaining $r - 1$ branch points have $1 \le r_D(c_j) \le 2$ and lie in $G' = G_{1296}$, forcing the remaining branch point $c_1 = (c_2 \cdots c_r)^{-1}$ into $G_{1296}$ as well. Since $\kappa|_{G_{1296}} = (\det_K)^3$ is a character, $\prod_{j=1}^r \kappa(c_j) = +1$, forcing $3 \mid \Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}$. Reaching the Theorem C locus ($\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, such as $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 2$) requires an even positive number ($\ge 2$) of branch points in cosets $\{1, 3\}$, consuming at least $3 + 3 = 6$ of the rank budget $9$ and forcing*
     $$\sum_{j=3}^r r_D(c_j) \le 9 - 6 = 3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r - 2 \le 3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r \le 5 \quad (\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{M}_{0,r} = r - 3 \le 2 < 3).$$
   *Hence no Hurwitz family in $G_{2592} \subset U_3(D_3)$ with $m = 3$ and $W^\Pi = 0$ can dominate a $3$-dimensional Theorem C PEL threefold in any base genus $\gamma \ge 0$.* $\square$

### 5.2 Explicit Matrix Construction and Strict Coset Rigidity ($r = 3$) of $160\#84 \subset U_4(D_5)$

At $d = 4$ over $D_5 = (-3,-5)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($\operatorname{Ram}(D_5) = \{5, \infty\}$), `lelong_ridge` [16] identified $160\#84 = C_5 : (Q_8 : C_4)$ as a reflection-free Theorem C group with $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}} = 1$ ($\mathbb{Q}(i) \not\hookrightarrow D_5$ since $5$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$) and noted that the rank bound $\min_{g \neq 1} r_D(g) = 2$ with $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(g_j) = 3 + 2(4) = 11$ bounds $r \le 5$ ($\dim \le 2$). We construct $160\#84 \subset U_4(D_5)$ explicitly over $\mathbb{Q}$ and prove that its subgroup structure sharpens $r \le 5$ to **strict rigidity $r = 3$ ($\dim \mathcal{M}_{0,3} = 0$)** for all $m = 3$ genus-zero tuples (and excludes $\gamma \ge 1$ as well: $\gamma \ge 2$ has budget $3 - 8(\gamma - 1) < 0$, while $\gamma = 1$ has budget $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(c_j) = 3$, which admits only $r = 1$ with $r_D(c_1) = 3$ since $\min_{g \neq 1} r_D(g) = 2$, and $c_1 = [a_1, b_1]^{-1} \in [G, G] \le C_5 \times Q_8$ lies in coset $0$ where $r_D \in \{0, 4\} \not\ni 3$).

**Theorem 5.2 (Explicit Realization, Square Local Norms, and Strict Rigidity $r = 3$ of $160\#84 \subset U_4(D_5)$).**
1. *(Explicit $16 \times 16$ Integer Matrix Realization): On $V_{16} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong \mathbb{Q}^{16}$, let $A$ be the $4 \times 4$ companion matrix of $x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$, let $S_\sigma$ be the $4 \times 4$ Galois matrix of $\sigma : \zeta_5 \mapsto \zeta_5^2$ ($S_\sigma A S_\sigma^{-1} = A^2$), let $P, Q \in \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb{Z})$ be the left-multiplication matrices of $I, J \in (-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, and let $M_{\alpha_2} = \operatorname{diag}(1, \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, -1)$ be the ring automorphism swapping $I \leftrightarrow J$ and sending $K \mapsto -K$. Then the matrices*
   $$M(a) = A \otimes I_4, \qquad M(p) = I_4 \otimes P, \qquad M(q) = I_4 \otimes Q, \qquad M(t) = S_\sigma \otimes M_{\alpha_2}$$
   *generate $G = (C_5 \times Q_8) \rtimes C_4 \cong 160\#84$ ($|G| = 160$) with commutant $\operatorname{End}_G(\mathbb{Q}^{16}) \cong (-3,-5)_{\mathbb{Q}} = D_5$.*
2. *(Universal Square Local Norms): Across all $19$ conjugacy classes of $G = 160\#84$, every element has local reduced norm in the set of rational squares:*
   $$\nu(g) \in \{1, 4, 16, 25, 256\} \subset (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2 \qquad \forall g \in 160\#84.$$
   *By Theorem 2.2(1), every Hurwitz tuple in $160\#84$ in any base genus $\gamma \ge 0$ has $\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \nu(c_j) \equiv 1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$, realizing the Theorem C non-embedding locus $\mathbb{Q}(i) \not\hookrightarrow D_5$.*
3. *(Strict Coset Rigidity $r = 3$ at $m = 3$): Under the $C_4$-coset map $\bar{c} : G \twoheadrightarrow G/(C_5 \times Q_8) \cong \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, the local ranks $r_D(g) = \frac{1}{4}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}}(g - I_{16})$ distribute as follows: coset $0$ has $1$ element with $r_D = 0$ and $39$ with $r_D = 4$; coset $2$ has $10$ elements with $r_D = 2$ and $30$ with $r_D = 4$; cosets $1$ and $3$ each have $20$ elements with $r_D = 3$ and $20$ with $r_D = 4$. Moreover, the $10$ elements with $r_D = 2$ and the $40$ elements with $r_D = 3$ generate an index-$2$ normal subgroup*
   $$G'_{80} := \langle g \in 160\#84 : r_D(g) \le 3 \rangle = (C_5 \times \langle p q \rangle) \rtimes C_4 \trianglelefteq 160\#84, \qquad |G'_{80}| = 80,$$
   *such that every $g \in G \setminus G'_{80}$ has $r_D(g) = 4$, while inside $G'_{80}$ every element in odd cosets $\{1, 3\}$ has $r_D(g) = 3$ and every element in even cosets $\{0, 2\}$ has even $r_D(g) \in \{0, 2, 4\}$. For any genus-zero tuple $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \dots, g_r) \in G^r$ ($g_1 \cdots g_r = 1$) with $m = 3$ ($\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(g_j) = 11$):*
   - *If all $g_j \in G'_{80}$, then $\sum_{j=1}^r \bar{c}(g_j) \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ forces an even number of $g_j$ in odd cosets $\{1, 3\}$, hence an even number of $g_j$ with $r_D(g_j) = 3$, forcing $\sum_{j=1}^r r_D(g_j) \equiv 0 \pmod 2 \neq 11$, a contradiction.*
   - *Thus at least one branch point lies in $G \setminus G'_{80}$. By parity under $\epsilon : G \twoheadrightarrow G/G'_{80} \cong \{\pm 1\}$, at least **two** branch points lie in $G \setminus G'_{80}$ and therefore have $r_D = 4$, consuming $4 + 4 = 8$ of the rank budget $11$. Since $\min_{g \neq 1} r_D(g) = 2$, the remaining budget $11 - 8 = 3$ accommodates only a single branch point with $r_D = 3$, proving $r = 3$ and $r_D = (4, 4, 3)$ ($\dim \mathcal{M}_{0,3} = 0$) universally in $160\#84$ (certified in Test 4 of the supplement).* $\square$
