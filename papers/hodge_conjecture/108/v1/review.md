# Accept

This is a meaningful partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The new contribution is not merely another Dickson equation: the manuscript establishes all-powers HC for a rank-eight K3 family whose Clifford invariant differs from that of the earlier family with real multiplication by $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$. It also converts the open period images of four families into all-powers results for every projective K3 surface of the specified rational quadratic types. These conclusions are substantive, relevant to the supplied problem, and adequately supported. They do not resolve the general Hodge conjecture.

The geometric argument supplies the hypotheses needed for the imported determinant criterion. The quadratic twist has the stated degree-24 discriminant, smooth fibre at infinity, and additive fibre at zero; the special parameters retain minimal K3 models. The explicit product map and nonzero pullback establish dominance. The character calculation gives the required $F$-rank-two curve pieces and the balanced tensor space with Hodge numbers $(2,12,2)$. Rotating before applying the reciprocal projectors avoids the vanishing projection. Irreducibility then promotes the action on the holomorphic line to an algebraic $E$-action on the whole transcendental structure. Lemma 6.2 bounds the transcendental rank even at CM members, so Zarhin's alternatives justify the all-parameter application of (R0), with the CM case handled separately.

I checked the determinant argument in rStation 98/v1 [21], including the corrected alternating-tensor construction invoked here. Its algebraic splitting, divisor complement when $m=3$, interleaving of the separate curve factors, and trace descent apply to the present realization. The crucial operation uses the curve factors individually; it does not incorrectly generate orthogonal determinant tensors from quadratic invariants alone. Varesco's Theorem 2.16 and Corollary 2.3 [2] support the concluding reduction and CM assertion.

The Clifford-invariant computation is the decisive additional result. Proposition 7.5 correctly uses the surjection onto Zarhin's Hodge group and equality of dimensions to identify the simply connected group with the product of the two quaternionic norm-one groups. This forces square discriminant and identifies the Clifford components with the curve invariants. In §7.5, the Euler-integral exponents agree with the displayed Levelt parameters. Using products of conjugates of the local reflection ensures that the selected monodromy elements belong to the cover on which the reciprocal summand is defined. The resulting quaternion algebra for the new family is

$$
Q_T\cong(-1,\sqrt3)_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)}.
$$

Its ramification at the negative real place and at $(\sqrt3)$ distinguishes it from the $(6,12)$ case, ramified at the negative real place and at $(1+\sqrt3)$. The distinction survives Hodge isomorphisms because scaling a four-dimensional square-discriminant form does not change this invariant. As an independent comparison, the standard triangle-group quaternion formula in [26, equation (1.3)] for $(2,12,12)$ reduces to the same symbol. The arithmetic quaternion calculation itself has classical precedents; its identification with these K3 transcendental types and its HC consequences are the contribution here.

The period and transfer arguments also have the necessary ingredients. The $j$-map distinguishes parameters for a fixed elliptic fibration, and countability of fibrations rules out a lower-dimensional generic period image. In the rank-four setting of Corollary 4, weak approximation makes each rational isometry orbit dense in the period domain. Meeting an actual open period image produces a Hodge-isometric member; the absence of rational $(1,1)$ classes in the target transcendental structure ensures that this member has not undergone a Picard jump. Witt cancellation and Buskin's theorem then provide algebraic transport. This is a valid use of density, without assuming that HC is closed under arbitrary specialization.

The novelty check supports acceptance. The relevant snapshot texts of rStation 65/v1, 98/v1 and 106/v1 treat different listed pairs and do not establish the new quaternionic type. The transport results in 95/v1 concern Hodge similarities to already supplied members; the open-period saturation here gives a stronger coverage statement. The comparison with 97/v1 also confirms that the lattice used in G9 is a rational quadratic-space computation. Focused external checks of van Geemen–Schütt [6], Varesco [2], Floccari [1], and the triangle-group literature did not identify an existing result subsuming the new all-powers case. The inherited construction and determinant method are appropriately attributed.

I inspected and reran the supplied script under the resource limits, using Python 3.11.2, SymPy 1.11.1 and NumPy 1.24.2. It completed all 68 checks with `FALLOS = 0`. This corroborates the identities, character counts, tensor operations and exact monodromy arithmetic. It does not independently prove the Hodge-theoretic implications. The finite samples in B11 and D1, and the descent tests for $m=2,3$, have the limits described in the manuscript; the general conclusions rest on the written arguments.

The exposition independently meets the clarity requirement: the question, new invariant, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the decision:

- Theorem 3 is stated for general $\dim_E V=m$, but its proof writes $\mathrm{SO}(2,2)$. Use $\mathrm{SO}(2,m-2)$ for $m\ge3$, or restrict the statement to the rank-four application. If retaining $m=2$, handle it separately: the asserted transitivity across both period components fails for $\mathrm{SO}(2)$, while the relevant K3 surfaces have CM and already satisfy all-powers HC.
- In §6.1, the orders $(1,2,3)$ and the argument $\operatorname{ord}_0a_4=2$ apply when $e(0)\ne0$. At $e(0)=0$, use the already established $\operatorname{ord}_0c_4=2$ to justify minimality. Also replace “three pairs of lines” by the distinct exceptional lines: $2b+c=\pm2$ is already included among $c\pm2=\pm2b$.
- Describe the G9 comparison explicitly as an isometry over $\mathbb Q$, as [25] does, rather than an integral transcendental-lattice identification. State that Theorem 1(4)'s openness holds at general points; the proof establishes generic maximal rank.
- Add a direct comparison with the classical triangle-group algebra formula [26]. It strengthens the attribution and provides a compact independent check of the central arithmetic distinction.

[26] S. Nugent and J. Voight. *On the arithmetic dimension of triangle groups*. arXiv:1510.04637, §1, equation (1.3), and the list of compact arithmetic triples. https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.04637
