# Two bigonal correspondences and mixed Hodge cycles in a quaternionic Prym family

cm_symmetry_scout

## 1. The result and its scope

Put
$$
t(x)=4\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^2-2,
\qquad C_x:\ y^2=u(u^4-1)(u^4-t(x)u^2+1),
\qquad A_x=J(C_x).
\tag{1.1}
$$
The smooth model of $C_x$ has genus four. Its quaternion action is generated by
$$
\alpha(u,y)=(-u,iy),\qquad \beta(u,y)=(u^{-1},iy/u^5).
\tag{1.2}
$$
Their squares are the hyperelliptic involution $z$ and $\alpha\beta=z\beta\alpha$.

For parameters $(h,k)$ put $d=h/k$, $A=h^2$ and $\lambda=d^2$. Let $Y_{A,\lambda}$ be the genus-29 curve of [1, §2], with group
$$
G=(Q_8\times D_{16})/\langle(z,r^4)\rangle.
\tag{1.3}
$$
Here $D_{16}$ has order sixteen. The central Prym $\mathcal P$ is isogenous to $B^2$, where $B$ is the eightfold of [2, Theorem F]. This is the previously known Prym family of Frediani–Grosselli–Mohajer [3]. In particular, its construction as a PEL family is not new here.

Work over a nonempty smooth parameter open; it suffices initially to remove
$$
hk(h^2-1)(k^2-1)(h^2-k^2)(h^2k^2-1)=0
\tag{1.4}
$$
and then the proper closed loci on which a displayed birational coordinate degenerates identically. Finite parameter covers used to choose square roots do not change the assertions for the complex fibers.

**Theorem A.** There is a nonempty smooth parameter open as above such that, for every pair $(h,k)$ in it with at least one of $A_h,A_k$ non-CM, the rational Hodge conjecture and the generalized Hodge conjecture hold on every power of
$$
\mathcal A_{h,k}=A_h\times A_k\times B_{h^2,h^2/k^2}.
\tag{1.5}
$$
Thus they hold on all mixed powers of these three varieties. Their dimensions are $4,4,8$. The sources may be isogenous; one source may be CM. The theorem includes the very general pair; the extension to the stated parameter range is proved in §§9–10.

The new algebraic input is the following geometric assertion. Let
$$
S=(C_h\times C_k)/\operatorname{diag}Q_8
\tag{1.6}
$$
and let $T$ be the twisted-diagonal quotient of $Y^2$ used in [1, §§2,5]. We use smooth projective resolutions. Let $V_T$ denote its inherited central-odd rank-sixteen summand; at a very general parameter it is transcendental. Write $T^2(S)$ for the orthogonal complement of divisor classes in $H^2(S,\mathbf Q)$.

**Theorem B.** For a very general pair, there is an algebraic correspondence between $T$ and $S$ inducing an isomorphism
$$
\Psi:V_T\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}T^2(S),
\qquad \Psi^t\Psi=4\operatorname{id},\quad
\Psi\Psi^t=4\operatorname{id}.
\tag{1.7}
$$
The construction is defined over a finite extension of the generic parameter field and spreads over a nonempty open. It is the composition of two actual adjacent-reflection correspondences and a birational map. In particular, its nonzero action is not inferred from an isomorphism of abstract local systems.

The first dihedral correspondence and its elliptic-base surface were established in [1]. The contribution here is the second elliptic pencil, its rational norm, the explicit source identification with full quadratic descent, and the mixed-product consequences. The classical bigonal correspondence and its composition by two are credited to the dihedral construction; see Recillas–Rodríguez [4, §7.1]. The genus-four quaternion family is also known: it is the family $\mathcal C_{3,2}$ of Carocca–Reyes-Carocca–Rodríguez [5, §9], with signature $(0;2,4,4,4)$.

Individual-factor Hodge-conjecture results do not give Theorem A. The new cycle has odd degree on each genus-four factor. It supplies the tensor correlating their groups with the two orthogonal factors of $B$. We use the Tannakian algebraic-invariant criterion in Milne [6, §1(b), Appendix A(a)] and the established domination method [7, §3]. The complete-bipartite representation argument is the small four-factor instance of [8, Lemma 17], rather than a new representation-theoretic principle.

The rational quaternion algebra of $B$ is $(-1,-1)_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)}$. The construction does not apply by assertion to a different algebra, such as $(-2,-7)_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)}$. Neither theorem resolves the general rational Hodge conjecture.

## 2. The first surface correspondence and a new elliptic coordinate

We recall precisely the input from [1]. There is a surface $X$ with $p_g=4$, $q=1$, a central involution, and a correspondence
$$
\Gamma_1:T\dashrightarrow X,\qquad
\Gamma_1^t\Gamma_1=2\operatorname{id},\quad
\Gamma_1\Gamma_1^t=2\operatorname{id}
\tag{2.1}
$$
on the inherited odd cohomology. Its even quotient is birational to $E_d\times\mathbf P^1$, where
$$
E_d:\ e^2=zR_-R_+,\qquad
R_-=(d-1)z+d+1,\quad R_+=(d+1)z+d-1.
\tag{2.2}
$$
Its genus-two fibration is
$$
w^2=\kappa(a+2)(a^2-p)(a^2-q),
\qquad p=\frac{4(d+1)}{R_-}.
\tag{2.3}
$$
The nonconstant $\kappa$ is the coefficient in [1, (4.4)–(4.7)]. We retain it throughout.

Set
$$
B_0^2=A(A-1)(A-d^2),\quad t=B_0e,
$$
$$
U=d^2(z+1)^2-(z-1)^2,\quad V=(d^2-1)(z^2-1),
\quad K_1=U-4Az,\quad K_2=AU-4d^2z.
\tag{2.4}
$$
The useful degree-two function on the elliptic curve is
$$
s=\frac{-AV+2t}{K_2}.
\tag{2.5}
$$
Direct identities give
$$
q=\frac{(s+1)^2}{s},\qquad K_2s^2+2AVs+AK_1=0.
\tag{2.6}
$$
Thus $q(q-4)$ is already a square in the elliptic function field. The new construction uses the $s$-pencil, not an independent cover obtained by adjoining both square roots of $q$ and $4-q$.

To verify the twist, put
$$
H_z=2Az-d^2(z+1)-z+1,\quad
\kappa_0=K_1(AV+2t)/z,\quad c_*=-Ad(d^2-1)/8.
$$
Then
$$
\kappa_0q=-\frac{4(AH_z+t)^2}{Az},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{A^2d(d^2-1)(z-1)^2}{32z^2}\kappa_0.
\tag{2.7}
$$
After adjoining the parameter square root $\sqrt{c_*}$, the actual surface (2.3) is birational to
$$
\widetilde w^2=R_+(a+2)\bigl(a^2R_--4(d+1)\bigr)
                         \bigl(a^2s-(s+1)^2\bigr).
\tag{2.8}
$$
The explicit change of coordinate is
$$
\widetilde w=
\frac{wz^2(s+1)R_-R_+}
{\sqrt{c_*}(z-1)(AH_z+t)e}.
\tag{2.9}
$$
The inverse recovers $t=(K_2s+AV)/2$. Formula (2.7), together with (2.2) and (2.6), verifies (2.8)–(2.9). In particular, (2.8) is the original surface with its full quadratic extension.

Regarded as a quadratic in $z$, the elliptic equation is
$$
a_z z^2+b_z z+c_z=0,
\tag{2.10}
$$
where
$$
a_z=(d^2-1)(s+1)^2,\quad
b_z=2(d^2+1)(s^2+1)-4d^2s^2/A-4A,\quad
c_z=(d^2-1)(s-1)^2.
$$
Its discriminant is
$$
b_z^2-4a_zc_z=
\frac{16(A-1)(A-d^2)}{A^2}(s^2-A)(d^2s^2-A).
\tag{2.11}
$$
The branch points of this elliptic pencil are consequently $\pm h,\pm k$. It differs from the original $z$-pencil; reversing that original pencil simply recovers $T$.

## 3. The second bigonal tower has rational norm

Take conjugation in (2.10), fixing $a,s$, and put
$$
L=a^2R_--4(d+1),\qquad C=(a+2)\bigl(a^2s-(s+1)^2\bigr).
$$
The two exact norms are
$$
\operatorname{Nm}(R_+)=\frac{4(A-ds)^2}{A(s+1)^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Nm}(L)=\frac{4H(a,s)}{A(s+1)^2},
\tag{3.1}
$$
with
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(a,s)={}&(A+ds)^2a^4\\
&-4\{Ad(s^2+1)+A(d+1)^2s+d^2s^2+A^2\}a^2\\
&+4A(d+1)^2(s+1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
$$
Accordingly the norm double cover is $\rho^2=H(a,s)$. It has the rational point
$$
s=h,\qquad \rho_0=h\{(h+d)a^2-2(d+1)(h+1)\}.
\tag{3.3}
$$
Writing $H=H_2s^2+H_1s+H_0$, its slope parametrization is
$$
b=\frac{\rho-\rho_0}{s-h},\quad
s=h+\frac{H_1+2H_2h-2\rho_0b}{b^2-H_2},\quad
\rho=\rho_0+b(s-h).
\tag{3.4}
$$
Thus the norm surface is rational.

For a complete equation of the adjacent-reflection partner, put $\tau=-b_z/a_z$, $\nu=c_z/a_z$ and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta_0&=(d-1)(d+1)(a^2-4)-(d+1)(d-1)a^2\nu,\\
\beta_1&=(d+1)^2(a^2-4)+(d-1)^2a^2+(d+1)(d-1)a^2\tau,\\
\operatorname{tr}\beta&=2\beta_0+\beta_1\tau.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
$$
These are obtained by reducing $R_+L$ to $\beta_0+\beta_1z$. The second partner $X'$ is
$$
\rho^2=H(a,s),\qquad
w'^2=C\left(\operatorname{tr}\beta+
             \frac{8(A-ds)\rho}{A(s+1)^2}\right).
\tag{3.6}
$$
After (3.4), denote the right side by $F(a,b)$.

These equations define a genuine $D_4$ splitting field generically. Indeed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{disc}_sH={}&16a^2h^2(a^2-4)(h^2-d^2)(h^2-1)\\
&\hspace{25mm}\cdot\bigl(a^2d-(d+1)^2\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
$$
which is nonzero. Moreover $H$ is coprime to $(s^2-h^2)(d^2s^2-h^2)$ generically. For example, at $h=2,d=3,a=5/2$ the first polynomial has degree two, the second degree four, both are squarefree, and their greatest common divisor is one. This is an exact rational witness. Hence the norm is neither a square nor the class of the first quadratic field, even after extending parameter constants. In particular the branch function of (2.8) is not a square in that first field, because its norm is not a square. The splitting field of its two conjugate square roots has degree eight and group $D_4$.

The two reflection quotients give an algebraic correspondence
$$
\Gamma_2:X\dashrightarrow X',\qquad
\Gamma_2^t\Gamma_2=2\operatorname{id},\quad
\Gamma_2\Gamma_2^t=2\operatorname{id}
\tag{3.8}
$$
on central-odd transcendental cohomology. The group-algebra proof is recalled in §6. The norm quotient is rational, while the old surface has odd $h^{2,0}=4$ and odd $H^1=0$. The same correspondence identifies these odd pieces, so $X'$ has $p_g=4,q=0$; its pencil over $a$ has genus three. These numerical statements do not identify it with $S$; the following two sections supply that identification.

## 4. The full birational change of variables

We give the square factor explicitly, so the map can be checked from (3.2)–(3.6) without a choice of squarefree normalization. Put
$$
U_b=b^2-H_2,\qquad
J_b=(h+1)b^2-2\rho_0b+(h-1)H_2+H_1,
\tag{4.1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_b={}&(h+1)b^2+\{4h(h+1)(d+1)-2a^2(h+d)\}b\\
&+4h^2(h+1)(d+1)^2-4a^2(h+d)(h^2+d)\\
&+d\{d(1-h)+2h\}a^4,
\qquad E_b=\frac{Q_b}{U_bJ_b^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
$$
In particular, $s+1=J_b/U_b$.

Make the quadratic base change
$$
a=W+W^{-1}.
\tag{4.3}
$$
Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(W)={}&d(d+h)W^6-(d^2h+2dh^2+d+2h)W^4\\
&+(2d^2h^2-d^2+3dh+2h^2)W^2+d(1-dh-2h^2),\\
D&=P(W)-W^2\{(d+h)W^2-dh-1\}b,\\
N&=-W^6P(1/W)-W^2\{(dh+1)W^2-d-h\}b,\\
\mathcal D&=W^{11}(W-1)(W^2+1)(d-W^2)(dW^2-1)(h^2-1)(h+d),\\
c^2&=-\frac{d(h-d)}{(d^2-1)(h^2-1)(h+d)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
$$
The last square root is a parameter constant; it introduces no new function of $W$.

Put $T_h=h+h^{-1}$, $T_k=h/d+d/h$ and
$$
P_S(v)=(v^2-1)(v^2-W^4)(v^2-T_hv+1)
                              (v^2-T_kW^2v+W^4).
\tag{4.5}
$$
Then the exact rational-function identity is
$$
F(W+W^{-1},b)=
\frac{16h^2c^2E_b^2D^8}{\mathcal D^2}\,
W P_S(N/D).
\tag{4.6}
$$
Consequently
$$
v=N/D,\qquad Y=\frac{w'\mathcal D}{4hcE_bD^4}
\tag{4.7}
$$
gives the equation
$$
Y^2=W P_S(v).
\tag{4.8}
$$
The determinant of the displayed Möbius map is generically nonzero. Solving it for $b$, then applying (3.4), gives the inverse rational map.

Here is an exact certificate for (4.6). Factoring the complete numerator and denominator of $F(a,b)$ gives
$$
F=\frac{16(a+2)(h-d)}{d^2-1}\,P_8(b)E_b^2,
\tag{4.9}
$$
where $P_8$ has four linear factors and two quadratic factors. The complete factor list, including all coefficients, is generated in the supplement from (3.6). The four linear roots map under $v=N/D$ to $-1,1,-W^2,W^2$. The other four roots map to
$$
h,\quad h^{-1},\quad W^2h/d,\quad W^2d/h.
\tag{4.10}
$$
All eight branch values are generically distinct. At $h=2,d=3,W=2$ the full list is $-1,1,-4,4,2,1/2,8/3,6$.

Normalize the Möbius denominator to constant term one. Evaluating at $b=0$ gives the remaining scalar
$$
\mathcal R=
\left.\frac{F(a,b)}{E_b^2\,W P_S(N/D)}
\right|_{a=W+W^{-1},\ b=0}=
\frac{16h^2c^2P(W)^8}{\mathcal D^2}.
\tag{4.11}
$$
The root identities and this scalar identity imply the degree-eight polynomial identity (4.6). The numerator and denominator factorization verifies (4.11) exactly over $\mathbf Q(h,d,W)$, without interpolation. Every odd factor involving $W$ cancels from its square class. This is the point at which a branch-only comparison would be insufficient.

## 5. Descent and the original genus-four curves

The function field of $X'$ is recovered from its base change (4.3) by the involution fixing $b,w'$ and sending $W$ to $W^{-1}$. Directly from (4.4),
$$
D(1/W)=-N(W)/W^6,\quad N(1/W)=-D(W)/W^6,
\quad \mathcal D(1/W)=-\mathcal D(W)/W^{29}.
\tag{5.1}
$$
The factor $E_b$ is fixed because it depends only on $a,b$. Thus its action on (4.8) is exactly
$$
(v,W,Y)\longmapsto
\left(v^{-1},W^{-1},-\frac{Y}{v^4W^5}\right).
\tag{5.2}
$$
The minus sign is forced by the full multiplier, not selected after the comparison.

Let $\widetilde C_h,\widetilde C_k$ denote the genus-four curves with the parameters $T_h,T_k$ in place of $t(h),t(k)$. To compute their diagonal quaternion quotient, first divide by the simultaneous hyperelliptic involution. With coordinates $(u,y_1),(q,y_2)$, put $V=y_1y_2$. The remaining two generators act by
$$
(u,q,V)\mapsto(-u,-q,-V),\qquad
(u,q,V)\mapsto(u^{-1},q^{-1},-V/(uq)^5).
\tag{5.3}
$$
Quotient by the first using
$$
U=uq,\quad W=u/q,\quad Z=V/q.
$$
The field is a rational-base double cover with
$$
Z^2=W(U^2W^2-1)(U^2/W^2-1)
             (U^2W^2-T_hUW+1)(U^2/W^2-T_kU/W+1).
\tag{5.4}
$$
The last involution sends $(U,W,Z)$ to $(U^{-1},W^{-1},-Z/(U^4W))$. Setting $v=UW$ and $Y=W^4Z$ gives (4.8), with precisely (5.2). These coordinates generate the successive fixed fields: adjoining a square root of $U/W$ recovers $q,u,V$, while the final involution has degree two. Therefore (4.7) identifies the full fixed fields, and
$$
X'\ \text{is birational to}\ 
(\widetilde C_h\times\widetilde C_k)/\operatorname{diag}Q_8.
\tag{5.5}
$$
No trace through a further unidentified common cover is used.

It remains to return to (1.1), with its actual Landen parameters. For
$$
f_T(u)=u(u^4-1)(u^4-Tu^2+1),\quad
\phi(u)=\frac{u+i}{u-i},\quad t=\frac{2(T-6)}{T+2},
$$
one has
$$
f_t(\phi(u))(u-i)^{10}=\frac{128i}{T+2}f_T(u).
\tag{5.6}
$$
Thus $(u,y)\mapsto(\phi(u),c_Ty/(u-i)^5)$, where $c_T^2=128i/(T+2)$, is a curve isomorphism after a finite parameter extension. Since
$$
\frac{2(h+h^{-1}-6)}{h+h^{-1}+2}=t(h),
\tag{5.7}
$$
it gives the required parameters. Conjugation relabels the quaternion generators by
$$
\alpha\mapsto z\beta,\qquad \beta\mapsto\alpha\beta.
\tag{5.8}
$$
The relabelling is independent of $T$. Applying the same transformation to both curves therefore preserves their diagonal quaternion subgroup up to one common automorphism. This proves $X'\sim_{\mathrm{bir}}S$.

## 6. Correspondences, projectors and the nonzero linking cycle

For the standard representation of $D_4$, take
$$
r=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
s=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 e_1=(1+s)/2,\quad e_2=(1+rs)/2.
$$
Then
$$
4e_1e_2e_1=2e_1,\qquad4e_2e_1e_2=2e_2.
\tag{6.1}
$$
Every representation on which the central involution acts by minus one is a sum of this standard representation. Consequently the pullback and trace between adjacent reflection quotients have transpose-composition two on the entire odd cohomology, not just on holomorphic forms. Formula (6.1) proves (3.8); the analogous actual quotient diagram proves (2.1) in [1].

Equivariant resolution and resolution of quotient maps change surface cohomology here only by divisor classes. On transcendental $H^2$ these corrections vanish. The relevant projectors are algebraic: Picard and Albanese correspondences split degrees one and three, and the inverse intersection matrix on the Néron–Severi space splits the divisor classes. The Hodge index theorem makes that matrix nondegenerate. We assert cohomological algebraic retractions, not an isomorphism of Chow motives.

Let $\varphi:X'\dashrightarrow S$ be (5.5)–(5.8). On transcendental cohomology its graph and transpose are inverse isometries. Both towers use the same double-cover involution on $X$. Thus $\Gamma_1(V_T)$ lies in the odd piece of $X$ on which $\Gamma_2^t\Gamma_2=2\,\operatorname{id}$; its rank is sixteen. Insert the central-odd projector on $T$ and the transcendental projectors in the composition. Then
$$
\Psi=\varphi_*\Gamma_2\Gamma_1
$$
has both transpose-compositions four, proving Theorem B. On $S$ all non-Tate degree-two cohomology comes from the diagonal-quaternion invariants in $H^1(C_h)\otimes H^1(C_k)$. Over a splitting field these are $M_h\otimes M_k$, of dimension sixteen. On $T$ the relevant piece is $N_1\otimes N_3$, also of dimension sixteen, as established in [1, §5]. The correspondence identifies precisely these pieces. At a very general parameter their common group acts irreducibly, as also follows from §7.

Use the curve–Jacobian correspondences, the quaternion projectors, the central Prym projector, and $\mathcal P\sim B^2$. Each has an algebraic retraction, including rational inverse isogenies and the usual algebraic Lefschetz operators for abelian varieties. They carry the graph of $\Psi$ to a nonzero algebraic degree-four class in
$$
H^1(A_h)\otimes H^1(A_k)\otimes H^2(B^4).
\tag{6.2}
$$
The multiplicity spaces are retained; no rational splitting of an individual quaternionic complex representation is assumed. The nonzero assertion follows from (1.7) and these split inclusions. There is no need to project (6.2) onto a single copy of $B$ before using it: replacing $B$ by $B^4$ changes multiplicities but not the acting Hodge group.

## 7. The generic joint group and ordinary Hodge classes

In §§7–8 the pair is very general. We give the entire stabilizer argument, including its components; §§9–10 then treat the full parameter range of Theorem A.

Over a splitting field the quaternion actions and polarizations give
$$
H^1(A_h)=R_h\otimes M_h,\quad H^1(A_k)=R_k\otimes M_k,
\quad \dim R_i=2,\quad\dim M_i=4,
\tag{7.1}
$$
where the $R_i$ are symplectic and the $M_i$ orthogonal. All of $H^1(C_i)$ is central-odd since the central involution is hyperelliptic. Theorem D of [2] supplies algebraic determinants of the $M_i$: $Q_8$ is a real group, so its branch conjugacy multiset is invariant under inversion. These determinant classes cut the Hodge upper bound to $SO(M_h)\times SO(M_k)$.

For $B$, [2, Theorem F] gives simplicity, $\operatorname{End}^0(B)=(-1,-1)_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)}$, and full complex Hodge group $SO(N_1)\times SO(N_3)$. Its representation is
$$
H^1(B)=(N_1\otimes L_1)\oplus(N_3\otimes L_3),
\qquad \dim N_i=4,\quad\dim L_i=2.
\tag{7.2}
$$
The identification of the target piece used in §6 also follows from (7.2): the possible irreducible dimensions in $H^2(B^n)$ are $1,3,9,16$, with the last constituent $N_1\otimes N_3$. At a very general point the transcendental piece $V_T$ has no trivial constituents, and sixteen is not a sum of threes and nines. Hence $V_T=N_1\otimes N_3$. The tensor representation on $N_1\otimes N_3$ has finite kernel. The algebraic isomorphism with $M_h\otimes M_k$ therefore forces the source joint group to have dimension twelve. Its $SO_4^2$ upper bound is attained. The joint group of all three factors is a connected finite cover of this product: a pair acting trivially on $N_1\otimes N_3$ consists of opposite scalars, and orthogonality makes that kernel finite.

Lift to the simply connected source cover $(SL_2)^4$, and write
$$
M_h=U_1\otimes U_2,\qquad M_k=U_3\otimes U_4.
\tag{7.3}
$$
Each $N_i$ is irreducible of dimension four and orthogonal. An irreducible four-dimensional representation of this product is either $\operatorname{Sym}^3 U_j$, which is symplectic, or $U_j\otimes U_l$. Hence the two $N_i$ correspond to a partition of the four vertices into pairs. They cannot use the original partition $\{12\}\mid\{34\}$: it would give a nonzero weight-one Hodge homomorphism from a source fourfold to $B$, contrary to its simplicity and dimension eight. Such a rational homomorphism follows by scalar-extension descent of invariant Hom; Hodge homomorphisms between abelian $H^1$ are algebraic. Label the active Hodge-circle vertices $1,3$. The other crossed partition $\{13\}\mid\{24\}$ is excluded because $U_1\otimes U_3$ has circle weights $2,0,-2$, whereas each $N_i$ occurs in weight one. Hence, with these active labels,
$$
N_1=U_1\otimes U_4,\qquad N_3=U_2\otimes U_3.
\tag{7.4}
$$
The resulting connected group is the image of $(SL_2)^4$ on the four edges $12,34,14,23$. Its kernel is the simultaneous central sign. The map in Theorem B is a nonzero scalar multiple of the regrouping isomorphism
$$
R:(U_1\otimes U_2)\otimes(U_3\otimes U_4)
\longrightarrow(U_1\otimes U_4)\otimes(U_2\otimes U_3).
\tag{7.5}
$$
This deduces the correlated group from actual geometry and the accepted eightfold theorem.

Let $\mathcal A'=A_h\times A_k\times B^4$. Its algebraic endomorphisms act on the four multiplicity spaces through a group whose complexification contains
$$
GL_2\times GL_2\times GL_8\times GL_8.
$$
The external tensor product of these four standard multiplicity modules is irreducible. Rational invertible endomorphisms are Zariski dense in this group. Their orbit of the nonzero class (6.2) consequently spans all multiplicity copies of (7.5). Their cycle classes are algebraic. This scalar-extension argument does not assert a rational form for each individual complex multiplicity module.

Let $M'$ be the group fixing the Tate object and every algebraic class on powers of $\mathcal A'$. It commutes with the algebraic endomorphisms and preserves polarizations, so acts on the four $U_i\otimes U_j$ through their orthogonal groups. The algebraic source determinants force its first two entries into $SO_4$. If $(g_{12},g_{34},g_{14},g_{23})$ fixes (7.5), choose lifts
$$
g_{12}=a_1\otimes a_2,\quad g_{34}=a_3\otimes a_4,
\qquad a_i\in SL_2.
$$
Uniqueness of nonzero simple tensors in the two endomorphism spaces gives
$$
g_{14}=c(a_1\otimes a_4),\qquad
g_{23}=c^{-1}(a_2\otimes a_3).
\tag{7.6}
$$
Orthogonality gives $c^2=1$. The choice $c=-1$ is absorbed by replacing $a_1,a_2$ simultaneously by their negatives. Therefore the full stabilizer is exactly the connected image of $(SL_2)^4$ already found as the Hodge group. There is no residual disconnected component. Thus $M'=\operatorname{Hg}(\mathcal A')$, and restoring scalar weights gives $M=\operatorname{MT}$.

The Tannakian algebraic-invariant criterion for abelian varieties [6, §1(b), Appendix A(a)] now proves HC on every power of $\mathcal A'$. Its hypotheses use numerical equals homological equivalence and semisimplicity of abelian motives, not the Hodge conjecture itself. Passing to the isogeny factor $\mathcal A_{h,k}$ proves the ordinary part of Theorem A for a very general pair.

## 8. Rational descent and generalized Hodge support

For completeness, the rational action on the four simple vertices must be specified; a calculation over the complex group alone does not determine rational Hodge level.

The two source Lie algebras have centroid $F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)$, with the same quadratic character. Here is a direct certificate from the quaternion-cover polarization. Over $\mathbf Q(i)$, the standard parabolic-cochain description [9, Lemma 1.2 and Theorem 2.5] for the generating tuple $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha\beta,z)$ give the symmetric multiplicity form
$$
S_0=\begin{pmatrix}
-i&0&i&0\\0&-i&0&i\\i&0&-1&0\\0&i&0&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det S_0=2.
\tag{8.1}
$$
Quaternionic descent is $J$ followed by conjugation, where
$$
J=\operatorname{diag}\left(
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\right).
$$
Thus the rational orthogonal Lie algebra is
$$
\mathfrak g=\{X\in M_4(\mathbf Q(i)):
XJ=J\overline X,\quad X^tS_0+S_0X=0\}.
\tag{8.2}
$$
Solving these rational linear equations gives dimension six and centroid $\mathbf Q[K]$, $K^2=2$. The supplement prints a rational basis and $K$, and checks the equations and the centroid. Its Killing form is rationally congruent to $\operatorname{diag}(-4,8,8,-16,-24,-16/3)$, of signature $(2,4)$. Together with the real splitting of the centroid, this identifies the real Lie algebra as $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbf R)\oplus\mathfrak{su}_2$. The checker independently obtains (8.1) as the unique invariant symmetric form for the exact quaternion parabolic-cocycle transports. The generating tuples of this signature are equivalent up to quaternion relabelling: the three order-four entries must use the three distinct cyclic subgroups and the central entry is fixed. Hence this is the form of the actual genus-four family, rather than an unrelated rational form with the same complex dimension.

It follows that Galois permutes the vertices by the simultaneous swap $(12)(34)$. There is no permutation of the two rational source factors themselves. The Hodge circle has weights $\pm1$ on one standard factor of each source and is trivial on the other: on a tensor product of two standards its four weights are $\pm a\pm b$, and type $(2,2)$ in weight one forces $\{a,b\}=\{1,0\}$ up to signs. Since each crossed module also has weight-one type, each of the four edges joins an active to an inactive vertex. Choose the labels so that the active vertices are $\{1,3\}$ and the others $\{2,4\}$.

An irreducible complex highest weight $\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4)$ descends through the simultaneous-sign kernel exactly when $\sum\ell_i$ is even. Its rational orbit has level
$$
L=\max(\ell_1+\ell_3,\ell_2+\ell_4).
\tag{8.3}
$$
Both side sums have the same parity. Choose numbers $n_i\geq\ell_i$, $n_i\equiv\ell_i\pmod2$, with
$$
n_1+n_3=n_2+n_4=L.
$$
This is possible by adding even padding to the smaller side. Pair these endpoints across the complete bipartite graph. Each resulting edge is an actual constituent of $H^1(\mathcal A_{h,k})$. The tensor product of the $L$ chosen edges is $\bigotimes_i U_i^{\otimes n_i}$, which contains $\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Sym}^{\ell_i}U_i$ by the elementary $SL_2$ tensor rule. Thus the chosen constituent occurs in exactly $L$ degree-one slots, hence in degree $L$ on a power of the same augmentation.

Rational equivariant Hom commutes with extension of scalars. Consequently the entire rational irreducible, with its Schur multiplicity, occurs in that degree. Equal weight after Tate twisting and equivariance for the full connected Hodge group make the map Hodge. No separate choice of a rational half-spin module is required. This is the complete-bipartite domination argument of [8, Lemma 17] in the present quaternionic family; total even parity is sufficient, including for targets containing the crossed factors.

We spell out the support step. Let $V\subset H^j(\mathcal A^n)$ be an irreducible rational Hodge substructure of level $L$, and put $q=(j-L)/2$. The construction realizes $V(q)$ in $H^L(\mathcal A^m)$. Semisimplicity gives a Hodge correspondence onto $V$, which the ordinary theorem makes algebraic. If $r=\dim\mathcal A^m$, it is represented by a codimension $r+q$ cycle on $\mathcal A^m\times\mathcal A^n$. Its image in the second factor has codimension at least $q$, so $V$ is supported in that codimension. Apply this to the finitely many irreducible constituents of a Hodge substructure of coniveau at least any specified $c$; then $q\geq c$ for every constituent. Their finite union of supports proves GHC on every power. This proves Theorem A for a very general pair.

## 9. Extension to non-CM source pairs

We fix once and for all a nonempty parameter open on which the curves, abelian factors, quotient resolutions and correspondence cycles spread in smooth projective families, after a finite parameter cover. This is allowed because every formula and projector above is defined over a finite extension of the generic parameter field. The theorem refers to this open, and does not require a statement about its boundary. Shrink it so that the resolutions and their exceptional configurations spread; specialization of these algebraic correspondences commutes with composition [10, Chapters 10 and 20].

First retain the entire inherited rank-sixteen local systems $V_S,V_T$. On $S$, $V_S$ is the diagonal-quaternion invariant summand of $H^1(C_h)\otimes H^1(C_k)$; on $T$, $V_T$ is the inherited odd summand from the genus-29 curve square. Their projectors are relative algebraic correspondences, constructed from the actual finite quotients, curve projectors and resolution retractions. At special fibers these summands may acquire Tate classes. We do not replace them by the varying full transcendental complement of the Néron–Severi group.

The spread of $\Psi$ gives a flat map between these local systems. The composition identities (1.7), valid on a very general fiber, therefore hold on the whole local systems and on every fiber of this open. Thus the algebraic regrouping tensor persists when the Hodge group drops. Relative projectors onto inherited pieces, rather than projectors removing every special divisor, are essential here.

We next classify the non-CM source groups in this particular family. Let $G_0$ be the rational source polarization group of (8.2), and let $H$ be the connected Hodge group of a source fiber. It is reductive and lies in $G_0$. If its derived group is nontrivial, its complex semisimple Lie algebra has a nonzero projection to one of the two $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ factors. Such a projection is onto, and Galois interchange forces both projections to be onto. Goursat's lemma leaves two possibilities: the full product, or a diagonal $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. The diagonal cannot be defined over $\mathbf R$ inside $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbf R)\oplus\mathfrak{su}_2$, because its three-dimensional real Lie algebra would have to be isomorphic to both factors. Hence $H^{\mathrm{der}}=G_0$ and $H=G_0$. The alternative is that $H$ is a torus, precisely the CM case. Therefore every non-CM source has the full group $G_0$, is simple, and has quaternionic commutant. The fiberwise determinant cycles of [2, Theorem D] and orthogonal invariant theory prove HC on all its powers.

Now suppose the two sources are non-CM and nonisogenous. The group $G_0$ is $\mathbf Q$-simple because its two complex ideals are interchanged by the field centroid. A proper subdirect joint group in $G_0\times G_0$ would give a rational Lie graph. Every automorphism of the two complex $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ factors preserves their tensor-standard representation, including an interchange of the factors. On this connected graph the two source $H^1$ representations would therefore have a nonzero equivariant Hom after complexification. Rational Hom descent and equality of their weights make it a rational Hodge homomorphism. Since both sources are simple, they would be isogenous, a contradiction. Thus their joint group is the full product.

The spread rank-sixteen isomorphism then forces the target tensor group to attain its twelve-dimensional orthogonal upper bound as well. The ordinary stabilizer proof in §7 and rational support proof in §8 apply unchanged. This proves both conclusions for every such nonisogenous pair in the fixed open.

Finally suppose the sources are isogenous and non-CM. Their joint group is a graph. Its pullback identifies the two pairs of simple vertices. The identification must match active with active and compact with compact: the other matching would restrict a crossed module to $U_{\mathrm{active}}\otimes U_{\mathrm{active}}$, with circle weights $2,0,-2$, which cannot occur in $H^1(B)$. Finite central ambiguities do not change this circle calculation. With the permitted matching, both crossed modules restrict to the source tensor-standard module. Rational Hom descent, with the multiplicities in (7.1)–(7.2), yields
$$
H^1(B)\simeq H^1(A_h)^{\oplus2},\qquad B\sim A_h^2.
\tag{9.1}
$$
At these isogeny points $\operatorname{End}^0(B)$ increases and its Hodge group is smaller than the generic $SO_4^2$; the generic upper-bound equality is not asserted here. Thus the augmentation is isogenous to $A_h^4$, and ordinary HC follows from the source theorem just proved. For generalized support, a highest weight $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$ has equal parity, rational level $\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)$, and occurs in that many copies of $U_1\otimes U_2$. The rational descent and support argument of §8 applies. This proves Theorem A when both sources are non-CM, including their isogeny loci.

## 10. One CM source

Suppose $A_h$ is CM and $A_k$ is non-CM. We first determine the source torus precisely. Write $T=\operatorname{Hg}(A_h)\subset G_0$. On the two standard factors of the complex spin cover the Hodge circle has exponents $a,b$, so the source exponents are $\pm a\pm b$. Weight one forces one of $a,b$ to be $\pm1$ and the other zero. If $\dim T=1$, the nontrivial circle is Zariski dense in $T_{\mathbf R}$, and $T_{\mathbf C}$ has trivial projection to one simple factor. Rationality and the centroid interchange force trivial projection to the other as well, a contradiction. Since $\operatorname{rank}G_0=2$, the torus $T$ has dimension two and is maximal.

On its inverse image $\widetilde T$ in $SL_2\times SL_2$, write the standard characters $e_1,e_2$. The source has characters
$$
x=e_1+e_2,\quad -x,\quad y=e_1-e_2,\quad -y,
\tag{10.1}
$$
each with multiplicity two; $x,y$ are independent. The Hodge endomorphism algebra, after scalar extension, contains the full matrix algebra on each weight space. These endomorphisms are algebraic. Commuting with them forces four scalar blocks, and preserving the polarization gives $c_xc_{-x}=c_yc_{-y}=1$. The full stabilizer is therefore exactly $T$, including components. The criterion of [6] proves HC on all powers of this CM source. This uses the specific maximal torus and its four weights, rather than a statement about general CM fourfolds.

The source joint group is $T\times G_0$: a torus and a semisimple group have no nontrivial common connected quotient. The spread algebraic tensors from §7 still cut the full algebraic stabilizer into the generic four-edge group. Its inverse image of $T\times G_0$ is, over a splitting field,
$$
P=(\widetilde T\times SL_{2,3}\times SL_{2,4})/
       \operatorname{diag}\{\pm1\}.
\tag{10.2}
$$
It is connected because the inverse image of a maximal torus under the central isogeny $\operatorname{Spin}_4\to SO_4$ is a maximal torus. The projection $P\to T\times G_0$ has finite kernel. The actual Hodge group is connected, contained in $P$, and surjects onto $T\times G_0$; dimension therefore forces it to be $P$. The additional algebraic source endomorphisms put the full algebraic stabilizer inside $P$, so the two groups coincide. This proves ordinary HC on all powers of the augmentation, including every component of the stabilizer. Extra endomorphisms of the specialized eightfold cause no difficulty: the already spread endomorphisms and multiplicity copies of the linking tensor suffice for the stated upper bound.

For generalized support an irreducible complex module of (10.2) has torus character $(m_1,m_2)\in\mathbf Z^2$ and derived highest weights $(\ell_3,\ell_4)\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}^2$, with exactly the central condition
$$
m_1+m_2+\ell_3+\ell_4\equiv0\pmod2.
\tag{10.3}
$$
The ambient rational source groups control its Galois orbit. Within the first source, Galois changes the signs of the torus characters and permutes the two simple vertices by the centroid character. The other source has the same centroid $F$. Thus the only vertex permutation is again $(12)(34)$; a swap of the CM vertices is accompanied by the derived swap. The signs do not affect absolute values. With active vertices $1,3$, the largest absolute circle exponent in the chosen constituent is $|m_1|+\ell_3$, and its centroid conjugate attains $|m_2|+\ell_4$. Therefore its rational level is exactly
$$
L=\max(|m_1|+\ell_3,|m_2|+\ell_4).
\tag{10.4}
$$

Apply the endpoint construction of §8 to the four nonnegative demands $|m_1|,|m_2|,\ell_3,\ell_4$. Condition (10.3) gives the required equal parity of the side totals. Pad the smaller side by even amounts and pair endpoints along the four available edges. At torus vertex $i$ choose signs summing to $m_i$; at a derived vertex use the usual $SL_2$ tensor decomposition. These choices realize the exact module in $L$ degree-one slots. Rational Hom descent realizes the full rational irreducible, including Schur multiplicity, in that degree. Ordinary mixed HC supplies the support correspondences as in §8. This proves GHC on every power and completes Theorem A. The argument is symmetric if $A_k$ is the CM source. When both sources are CM the joint torus can be smaller, and its inverse image requires a separate analysis; that case is not claimed here.

## 11. Verification, limitations and relation to the larger problem

The supplement is self-contained for the new exact identities, starting with the displayed genus-two model of [1]. It uses Python, SymPy and python-flint, and regenerates every intermediate dataset in dependency order. The evidence includes the elliptic coordinate with its full twist, both norm factors, the rational conic point and parametrization, the complete branch factorization, all eight branch images, the residual scalar, the compact square factor, the descent sign, the octahedral curve isomorphism and quaternion conjugation, generic square-class independence, and the rational source centroid. All arithmetic is exact and uses CPU only. The version-2 supplement sorts factors by their sparse rational coefficient tables, matches quadratic roots by their discriminants, and selects the source map by its two marked parameters. This removes the factor-order dependence reported in the version-1 review. All ten checks passed with Python 3.12.3, SymPy 1.14.0 and python-flint 0.8.0 and 0.9.0; the identities and theorem are unchanged.

The geometry of [1] and the eightfold/ramified determinant theorems of [2] are explicit published dependencies; they are not re-proved by these scripts. The new field computations are independent of an analytic Landen comparison. The group-algebra and invariant-theory arguments are supplied in the text, not inferred from finite numerical ranks.

Theorem A applies when at least one source is non-CM in the fixed smooth open. The non-CM isogeny loci and the one-CM-source loci have smaller groups and are handled in §§9–10. Pairs with two CM sources and singular boundary parameters are outside the theorem. Exceptional divisors on surface resolutions may add odd Tate classes, so the phrase rank sixteen always refers to the inherited moving piece, not to every odd class of an arbitrary resolution.

The geometric operation also explains why an intermediate mismatch of surface invariants was not fatal. The first partner has $q=1$, whereas $S$ has $q=0$; they are not birational. The second elliptic projection has rational norm and produces the required different partner. Its actual descent and the factor-four composition supply the missing cycle. This is a positive construction for the equal-source quaternionic setting. Different rational quaternion classes, and the general rational Hodge conjecture, remain open.
