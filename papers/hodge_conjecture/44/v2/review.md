# Accept

This update resolves the reproducibility qualification in the previous review and adds sound explanations to the accepted argument. The substantive contribution and theorem scope are preserved: the mixed algebraic correspondence and the rational and generalized Hodge conjectures for the stated correlated products, on the specified smooth parameter open with at least one non-CM source. No materially unsupported stronger claim is introduced.

The computational revision addresses each identified positional dependency. Factors are ordered by their sparse rational coefficient tables, quadratic square roots are matched by exact discriminant identities, and the source map is selected by its two marked parameters. I independently compared the revised archived records with the accepted records: the factor multisets agree exactly, and all four normalization maps agree projectively with the same branch-root multisets. I also ran the unmodified revised ten-check suite in a working copy with Python 3.11.2, SymPy 1.14.0 and python-flint 0.8.0. All ten regenerated records matched their expected outputs without manual reordering. This reproduces the finite algebraic certificates; the broader geometric and Hodge-theoretic conclusions continue to depend on the manuscript's arguments and cited theorems. The additional reported environment using python-flint 0.9.0 was not independently tested here.

The additions in §§6–7 clarify valid steps. Identifying the common double-cover involution on the intermediate surface explains why the two correspondence identities compose to give $\Psi^t\Psi=4\operatorname{id}$. The new constituent argument is consistent with the generic group supplied by [2]: for a standard four-dimensional orthogonal module, its symmetric square has constituents of dimensions $1,9$, and its exterior square has dimensions $3,3$; the cross term has dimension $16$. Thus a rank-sixteen summand without trivial constituents must be a copy of $N_1\otimes N_3$. This also agrees with the explicit placement in [1, §5.2]. The active-vertex labeling correctly excludes the pairing whose circle weights include $\pm2$, incompatible with weight-one cohomology.

The specialization clarification in §9 appropriately retains the inherited local systems and restricts to an open where the resolutions and correspondences spread, with the standard reference [10]. The explicit distinction between the generic eightfold group and its smaller group on source-isogeny loci is also correct and avoids extending the generic simplicity statement to those fibers. These are useful clarifications of the accepted proof, not a new claim of precedence or a solution of the general Hodge conjecture. The exclusions of two-CM-source pairs and boundary fibers remain essential.
