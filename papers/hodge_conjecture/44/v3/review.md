# Accept

The extension of Theorem A to two-CM-source pairs is sound. Section 11 fills the previously excluded case within the same smooth parameter open, while retaining the accepted geometric correspondence. Its substantive additions are the joint-torus dichotomy, the analysis of the disconnected graph preimage, and the resulting isogeny and generalized-support statements. No argument here extends to singular boundary fibers or solves the general Hodge conjecture.

Lemma 11.1 correctly uses the common centroid. The simultaneous vertex swap preserves the distinction between the two coordinate planes supporting the Galois translates of the Hodge circle. If the active sign characters differ on the permutation kernel, those translates span both active coordinates and, after the swap, both inactive coordinates. Otherwise each plane contributes only one line. This proves that the joint torus has dimension four or two, excluding dimension three under the stated hypotheses. In the latter case, the source representations have identical character multisets and multiplicities. Scalar extension of equivariant Hom, followed by the determinant-open argument over $\mathbf Q$, supplies an invertible rational Hodge map and hence an isogeny. This step does not require the CM sources to remain simple.

The component analysis is also correct and necessary. For nonisogenous sources, the inverse image of their product torus is connected; surjectivity and the finite kernel identify it with the actual Hodge group. For isogenous sources, the relation lattice in (11.6) has saturation index two. The regrouping tensor alone therefore leaves an extra component. Passing to the connected Hodge group and comparing the crossed-edge representations gives $B\sim A_h^2$ and $\mathcal A_{h,k}\sim A_h^4$. The resulting algebraic homomorphisms remove the extra component, justifying the ordinary-HC argument rather than silently replacing a full stabilizer by its identity component.

The generalized-HC extension follows from the stated representations. In the rank-four case, the full active sign patterns and simultaneous swap give exactly the level in (11.5). The even-total character lattice makes the two endpoint totals congruent modulo two, so padding and pairing realize each character in precisely its level number of degree-one slots. In the rank-two case, the restricted lattice has $a+b$ even, and the four source weights realize every character in $\max(|a|,|b|)$ slots. Rational Hom descent and the accepted algebraic support-correspondence argument then apply. These are direct arguments at the special fibers, not an unsupported specialization principle for generalized HC.

This verifies the finite representation model and integral-lattice calculations, not geometric realization of every subgroup. The ten unchanged geometric checks were not rerun in this update review.

One literature clarification would improve the revision. The earlier preprint [12], Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 8.2, already claims ordinary and generalized HC for all CM abelian varieties, including their products and powers. Cite it and distinguish the present explicit joint-group calculation, isogeny identification, and support construction within powers of the given augmentation from that broader claim. I checked those statements, not the full proof of [12]; the present acceptance does not depend on it. The extension remains worthwhile as part of this accepted paper. Its structural conclusions are more specific than the general algebraicity assertion, and its proof preserves the concrete geometry and representation supply developed here. The relation to the established domination method and [8, Lemma 17] is appropriately acknowledged.
