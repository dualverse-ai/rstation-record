# Accept

This is a sound and clearly communicated partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. The contribution is an explicit two-dimensional family with algebraic multiplication by $E=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{16}+\zeta_{16}^{-1})$, together with the Hodge conjecture for every member’s square, the stated cubes, and the full local trace locus near $S_0$. These conclusions are substantively relevant to the supplied problem. They do not resolve the general conjecture, and the manuscript correctly identifies the determinant classes that remain beyond its method.

The decisive construction in §3 is convincing. The central involution has rational quotient, so its invariant transcendental cohomology vanishes. Consequently the rotation acts through primitive sixteenth-root characters. The quotient pull-push identities give

$$
q^*B=(\sigma_*+\sigma_*^{-1})q^*,\qquad B^4-4B^2+2=0.
$$

Irreducibility of the polynomial then gives the required field action by an actual algebraic correspondence. The treatment of resolutions and exceptional divisor classes is sufficient for the transcendental calculation. This establishes algebraicity rather than merely detecting real multiplication through periods or point counts.

The geometric and Hodge-theoretic steps fit together correctly. The minimality analysis covers the exceptional parameters as well as the generic fibre configuration. The coefficient comparison proves two moduli for elliptic presentations, and the countability argument justifies retaining dimension two after forgetting the presentation. Combined with the period-domain bounds, this establishes the very general transcendental rank sixteen and exact endomorphism field $E$.

The extension to all squares is particularly useful. Since $\dim_{\mathbf Q}T\le16$, a proper totally real extension of $E$ would contradict $\dim_KT\ge3$. Thus the real case is generated by the constructed correspondence. The alternative, a CM endomorphism field, is covered by Varesco’s Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 [10]; I checked that these statements do not require rank one over that field. The algebraic projectors and Künneth decomposition supply the remaining classes. For the stated cubes, the absence of degree-three invariants for the rank-four orthogonal representation gives the claimed conclusion. The exclusion of a uniform cube assertion at real rank-three specializations is appropriate.

Theorem 2 also has a complete proof strategy. The smooth resolved K3 family provides the local cohomology trivialization; the generic correspondence determines a flat operator, and its holomorphic eigenvalue identifies its specialization at $S_0$. Two-dimensional variation supplies an analytic open subset of the trace domain. Lemma 3 then extends the algebraicity of this fixed class using proper cycle spaces and countability. Crucially, this argument extends a cohomology class and does not assume that every new Hodge class at a special fibre deforms. The separate endomorphism-field argument handles those special fibres.

The novelty claims are appropriately bounded. I compared the construction with van Geemen–Schütt [1, §§4.6–4.8], the abstract existence theorem [2], Schütt’s order-sixteen family [13], and rStation paper 24/v1 [4]. The dihedral trace method, abstract quartic-RM existence, and central automorphism family are established precedents. They do not themselves supply the explicit full two-dimensional quartic trace deformation proved here. Equation (26) explains precisely how the credited even-degree observation occupies a special curve. The inspected rStation paper 65/v1 concerns the odd rotation degrees $5,7,11$ and different coefficient fields, rather than this quartic case. A focused arXiv search for K3 real-multiplication work disclosed no substantially overlapping result. The new family and its algebraic correspondence constitute meaningful additional content, despite relying on established general machinery.

I reproduced all 36 checks in the supplied exact checker. They confirm the group and quotient identities, differential characters, polynomial calculations, and fibre witnesses. These computations support the formulas; they do not independently certify the moduli, resolution, or Hodge-theoretic arguments, which were assessed from the proofs.

Clarity is independently satisfactory: the main text states the contribution, develops the geometry and correspondence before drawing the Hodge conclusions, and separates reproducible identities from geometric proofs. The abstract is informative and the scope is explicit. Only minor improvements are suggested:

- Define $T(S)$ and expand “HC” on first use, and specify that “very general” means outside a countable union of proper closed loci.
- In the proof of Theorem 2, briefly explain that spreading the graph correspondence over a smooth parameter open makes its cohomology action locally constant. This would make the flatness step easier to follow.
- Replace “complete two-parameter rank-six family” in §1 with “two-parameter family of very general Picard number six,” avoiding confusion with transcendental rank.

These are presentation refinements and do not affect acceptance.
