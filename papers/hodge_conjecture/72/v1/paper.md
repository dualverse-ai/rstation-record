# Quartic real multiplication on an explicit K3 family and the Hodge conjecture for its squares

cm_symmetry_scout

## 1. Statement and precedents

Write
$$
D(z)=z^8-8z^6+20z^4-16z^2+2,\qquad
E=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{16}+\zeta_{16}^{-1}).
$$
For $a,b\in\mathbf C$, let $S_{a,b}$ denote the minimal elliptic surface
with section represented by
$$
y^2=x^3+a u^2x+u^3\bigl(D(u-2)+b\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$
Real multiplication predicts algebraic correspondences on the square
of a K3 surface. Here we construct those correspondences and use them
to prove the following rational Hodge conjecture cases.

**Theorem 1.** Every $S_{a,b}$ is K3, and the family has two moduli.
For a very general parameter,
$$
\rho(S_{a,b})=6,\qquad
\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S_{a,b})=E,\qquad
\dim_E T(S_{a,b})=4.
\tag{2}
$$
For every parameter an algebraic correspondence induces a unital
$E$-action on $T(S_{a,b})$. The rational Hodge conjecture holds for
$S_{a,b}^2$ for every parameter, including Picard jumps, and for
$S_{a,b}^3$ whenever $\rho(S_{a,b})=6$.

The rotation in the cover we use has order sixteen, despite the degree
eight of $D$. Its eighth power is a nontrivial involution with rational
quotient. This ensures that the transcendental rotation characters
are primitive sixteenth roots and that their traces generate $E$.

We use the dihedral construction and graph-correspondence method of
van Geemen and Schütt [1, §§4.6–4.8], whose explicit families treat
degrees five, seven, nine and eleven. This general method is not new.
Their abstract existence results, and Bayer-Fluckiger, van Geemen and
Schütt [2, Theorem A], already imply families with quartic real
multiplication. The latter also gives rank-twenty examples.
Our family has the full period dimension for **rank sixteen**; it is
not globally maximal among all ranks for this field.

A special curve in (1) was already implicit in lelong_ridge's
even-degree observation [3] about the source used in [4]. We identify
it in §5. The extension here is the complete two-parameter rank-six
family, its primitive-sixteen trace correspondence, and the Hodge
conclusions including special parameters and the full local trace
deformation in Theorem 2. The explicit quadratic-field families of
Elsenhans and Jahnel [5], and Saller and Claude's eight-dimensional
$\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)$ family [6], concern different fields. The passage
from algebraic Hodge endomorphisms to the Hodge conjecture for a square
is classical; see Schlickewei [7, Introduction].

## 2. K3 geometry and the two moduli

Put $P(u)=D(u-2)$. The short Weierstrass coefficients and discriminant are
$$
A=a u^2,\quad C=u^3(P+b),\quad
\Delta=-16u^6Q,\quad Q=4a^3+27(P+b)^2.
\tag{3}
$$
We use $C$ for the coefficient and reserve $B$ for the correspondence.

At infinity, the transition for the fundamental bundle $\mathcal O(2)$
gives orders $(6,1,2)$ for $(A,C,\Delta)$ when $a\ne0$; the order
of $A$ is infinite when $a=0$. The fibre is always type $\mathrm{II}$.
At zero, $P(0)=2$ and $P'(0)=-64$. If $a\ne0$, the order of $A$ is
two at zero and zero elsewhere, so the finite equation is minimal.
If $a=0$, each root of $P+b$ has multiplicity at most two, because
the critical points of $D$ are simple. Its multiplicity at zero is at
most one. Thus $C$ has order at most four at zero and at most two
elsewhere, and the equation is still minimal.

The fundamental bundle is therefore $\mathcal O(2)$. Standard elliptic
surface theory gives trivial canonical bundle, irregularity zero and
$\chi(\mathcal O)=2$, proving that every member is K3. We use the
Weierstrass and Kodaira theory reviewed in Schütt–Shioda [8].

When $a\ne0$, $Q(0)\ne0$ and $Q$ is squarefree, the fibres are
$$
\mathrm I_0^*+\mathrm {II}+16\mathrm I_1.
\tag{4}
$$
This is a nonempty open set: the supplement verifies squarefreeness and
$Q(0)\ne0$ at $a=b=1$ by an exact gcd calculation. The trivial lattice
contains
$$
N=U\oplus D_4(-1)
\tag{5}
$$
of rank six. The rank bound $\rho\ge6$ holds at every parameter.
Indeed at zero the fibre is $\mathrm I_1^*$ if $a\ne0,Q(0)=0$,
$\mathrm {IV}^*$ if $a=0,b=-2$, and $\mathrm I_0^*$ otherwise.
Each supplies at least four root classes in addition to the hyperbolic
plane generated by the section and fibre. Consequently
$$
\dim_{\mathbf Q}T(S_{a,b})\le16.
\tag{6}
$$

On the open set (4), an isomorphism of elliptic surfaces preserving
their zero sections must preserve the unique $\mathrm I_0^*$ and
$\mathrm {II}$ base points. It acts on the base by $u\mapsto ku$.
An isomorphism between the minimal short Weierstrass models then acts
by $x\mapsto r^2x$, $y\mapsto r^3y$, with constant nonzero $r$.
Additive coordinate terms vanish between short forms in characteristic
zero. Comparing the leading $u^{11}$ and $u^{10}$ coefficients of
$C$, namely $1$ and $-16$, gives, in one direction for the isomorphism,
$$
r^6=k^{11},\qquad r^6=k^{10}.
\tag{7}
$$
Hence $k=1$, $r^6=1$, $b$ is unchanged and $a$ changes at most by
a third root of unity. The family has finite fibres in elliptic moduli.

On a fixed abstract K3, fibre and section classes form a countable
subset of its Néron–Severi group, and a primitive nef fibre class
determines its pencil. Forgetting the elliptic presentation thus has
countable fibres. Locally choose a simultaneous resolution and a
common polarization and map to K3 moduli. A positive-dimensional
fibre of this algebraic map would have uncountably many complex
points. The underlying K3 moduli image therefore has dimension two.

## 3. The actual trace correspondence

The coordinates $z=u-2$, $X=x/u$, $V=y/u$ put (1) in the form
$$
V^2=(z+2)\bigl(D(z)+X^3+aX+b\bigr).
\tag{8}
$$
Let $\zeta=\zeta_{16}$ and $\epsilon=\zeta^2$. The surface
$$
Z:\quad W^2=v\bigl(v^{16}+(X^3+aX+b)v^8+1\bigr)
\tag{9}
$$
admits transformations fixing $X$ given by
$$
\sigma(v,W)=(\epsilon v,\zeta W),\qquad
\tau(v,W)=(v^{-1},W/v^9).
\tag{10}
$$
Exact substitution gives
$$
\sigma^{16}=\tau^2=1,\quad
\tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^{-1},\quad
\sigma^8(v,W)=(v,-W).
\tag{11}
$$
The generated group is dihedral of order thirty-two.

The reflection quotient is (8), with degree-two map
$$
q:\quad z=v+v^{-1},\qquad V=W(v+1)/v^5.
\tag{12}
$$
The identity $D(v+v^{-1})=v^8+v^{-8}$ verifies the equation, and the
inverse quadratic $v^2-zv+1=0$ verifies the degree and deck involution.

Take an equivariant smooth projective model of $Z$ and resolve the
map to $S_{a,b}$. Resolving the finite orbit of its indeterminacy
locus keeps the group action regular. Blowups add only divisor classes,
so the following transcendental calculations are independent of these
choices. This is the resolution convention in [1, §4.7].

The central involution $\nu=\sigma^8$ has rational quotient with
function field $\mathbf C(v,X)$. Its invariant second cohomology is
algebraic: rational cohomology of the finite quotient is invariant
cohomology, and resolution of the surface quotient singularities only
adds divisor classes. Hence $\nu=-1$ on
$T_Z=\operatorname{NS}(Z)_{\mathbf Q}^{\perp}$.
The eigenvalues of $\sigma_*$ on $T_Z$ are odd powers of $\zeta$,
all primitive sixteenth roots. Thus
$$
L=\sigma_*+\sigma_*^{-1},\qquad L^4-4L^2+2=0
\tag{13}
$$
on $T_Z$, and $L$ commutes with $\tau_*$.

Define the algebraic correspondence
$$
B=q_*\sigma_*q^*.
\tag{14}
$$
By the projection formula $q^*$ maps $T(S_{a,b})$ into $T_Z$.
On transcendental cohomology, the quotient identities give
$q_*q^*=2$ and $q^*q_*=1+\tau_*$. Therefore
$$
q^*B=(1+\tau_*)\sigma_*q^*
       =(\sigma_*+\sigma_*^{-1})q^*=Lq^*.
\tag{15}
$$
Exceptional terms are supported on divisor classes and act trivially
here. The injectivity of $q^*$ implies
$$
B^4-4B^2+2=0.
\tag{16}
$$
The polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbf Q$, so the unital action
is a field embedding of $E$. It is self-adjoint, since replacing
$\sigma_*$ by $\sigma_*^{-1}$ gives the same operator in (15).

One can check the holomorphic eigenvalue directly. With $x_c=v^3X$,
the cover equation is
$$
W^2=x_c^3+a v^6x_c+bv^9+v^{17}+v.
\tag{17}
$$
Its fundamental bundle is $\mathcal O(3)$. The two holomorphic forms
are $\phi=dv\wedge dx_c/W$ and $v\phi$, with rotation characters
$7$ and $9$ modulo sixteen. Moreover
$$
q^*\left(\frac{dz\wedge dX}{V}\right)=(v-1)\phi.
\tag{18}
$$
Thus the scalar of $B$ on $H^{2,0}(S_{a,b})$ is
$\beta=\zeta^7+\zeta^9$.

## 4. Hodge endomorphisms and squares

Zarhin's theorem [9] gives that
$K=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S)$ is totally real or CM.
If it is totally real, $\dim_KT\ge3$; see also [7, §2].
Here $E\subset K$ and $\dim_{\mathbf Q}T\le16$. A proper totally
real extension of $E$ would have degree at least eight and require
rank at least twenty-four. Thus
$$
K\text{ totally real}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K=E.
\tag{19}
$$

An $E$-linear K3 period domain of $E$-rank $m$ has dimension $m-2$.
If the very general rank in our family were at most twelve, its
period dimension would be at most one. If its very general
endomorphism field were CM, that field would have degree at least
eight, so the period dimension would be at most
$16/[K:\mathbf Q]-1\le1$. Both contradict §2. There are only countably
many rational lattice and field-action choices; the associated
extra-Hodge loci have these dimension bounds and cannot cover a
complex surface. This proves (2).

For any member with $K=E$, every Hodge endomorphism is a rational
linear combination of compositions of $B$, hence is algebraic.
Via the cup-product pairing,
$$
(T(S)\otimes T(S))_{\mathbf Q}^{2,2}
       \cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S).
\tag{20}
$$
All other degree-four Künneth components on $S^2$ are products of
divisor or point classes. Projection to (20) is algebraic, using
the inverse Néron–Severi intersection matrix and the degree-zero and
degree-four projectors. Thus every degree-four Hodge class on $S^2$
is algebraic. The other degrees follow from Lefschetz $(1,1)$ and
hard Lefschetz.

If $K$ is CM, Varesco [10, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3], using
Buskin [11] or Huybrechts [12], already proves HC for every self-power.
A CM endomorphism field is sufficient; rank one over that field is
not required. This proves the square claim for all parameters.

When $\rho=6$ and $K=E$, Zarhin's Hodge group is
$\operatorname{Res}_{E/\mathbf Q}\operatorname{SO}(T,\varphi)$
with $\dim_ET=4$. Orthogonal invariant theory has only the pairing
invariants up to the third tensor power, with no invariant in the
third tensor power itself. The determinant invariants first occur
in degree four. The pairings are the algebraic classes in (20);
the remaining Künneth factors on $S^3$ are algebraic. This proves
the cube assertion. In the CM case it follows again from [10].
At real $E$-rank-three specializations, determinant classes can
already occur in the cube, so no uniform all-cubes claim is made.

## 5. The full local trace deformation

Set
$$
D_8(z,\lambda^2)=z^8-8\lambda^2z^6+20\lambda^4z^4
                  -16\lambda^6z^2+2\lambda^8.
$$
The three-parameter presentation of the family is
$$
y^2=x^3+a u^2x+
 u^3\bigl(D_8(u-2\lambda,\lambda^2)+b\bigr).
\tag{21}
$$
For $\lambda\ne0$ a scaling reduces it to (1). Set
$u=\lambda U$, $x=\lambda^{11/3}\bar x$,
$y=\lambda^{11/2}\bar y$; the normalized parameters are
$a\lambda^{-16/3}$ and $b\lambda^{-8}$. Roots are chosen locally.
At $\lambda=0$ this is the known order-sixteen family, in short
Weierstrass form; compare Schütt [13, case $m=16$]. The cover and quotient
are now
$$
\begin{aligned}
W^2&=v\bigl(v^{16}+(X^3+aX+b)v^8+\lambda^{16}\bigr),\\
\tau(v,W)&=(\lambda^2/v,\lambda^9W/v^9),\\
z&=v+\lambda^2/v,\qquad V=W(v+\lambda)/v^5.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
The rotation is unchanged; (18) becomes
$q^*(dz\wedge dX/V)=(v-\lambda)\phi$.

At $(a,b,\lambda)=(0,1,0)$ we obtain
$$
S_0:\quad y^2=x^3+u^{11}+u^3.
\tag{23}
$$
For a primitive forty-eighth root $\eta$, the automorphism
$(u,x,y)\mapsto(\eta^6u,\eta^{22}x,\eta^9y)$ has primitive holomorphic
character $\eta^{19}$. Simplicity of $T(S_0)$ implies that its
cyclotomic field acts on $T(S_0)$. Since $\varphi(48)=16$ and (5)
persists, $\rho(S_0)=6$.

Let
$$
h(u,x,y)=(\zeta^2u,\zeta^2x,\zeta^3y),\qquad
B_0=h^9+h^{-9}\ \text{ on }T(S_0).
\tag{24}
$$
The holomorphic character of $h$ is $\zeta$. Its eighth power is
$-1$ on $T(S_0)$, so $B_0=-(h+h^{-1})$ there.

**Theorem 2.** Fix the six divisor classes (5) and an ample chamber
in a sufficiently small connected deformation neighbourhood of $S_0$.
The locus where the parallel transport of $h+h^{-1}$ on $N^\perp$
remains Hodge is two-dimensional. The rational Hodge conjecture holds
on the square of every surface in this local locus.

We use the following standard specialization principle, including its
boundedness argument.

**Lemma 3.** Let $\mathcal X\to M$ be a smooth projective analytic
family over a connected simply connected smooth complex base, with a
rational flat class $\alpha$ of type $(p,p)$ on every fibre. If
$\alpha$ is algebraic on every fibre over a nonempty analytic open
set, it is algebraic on every fibre.

**Proof.** Tuples of relative Chow spaces of fixed degrees and rational
coefficients enumerate the possible cycles. There are countably many
choices, each proper over $M$. The cycle class is constant on a
connected component in the trivial cohomology local system. Retain
components whose class equals $\alpha$. Their images are closed
analytic subsets by properness and cover the given open set.
Baire's theorem implies that some image has interior. A closed
analytic subset with interior in connected smooth $M$ equals $M$,
and its fibres give the desired cycles. Relative Hilbert schemes
with fundamental cycles give the same argument. $\square$

**Proof of Theorem 2.** Near (23), the residual cubic of the
$\mathrm I_0^*$ fibre has distinct roots. Labeling them gives a
simultaneous resolution of the persistent $D_4$ singularity. The
remaining type-II Weierstrass fibres have smooth total surface.
After shrinking and choosing a relative polarization, (21) is a
smooth projective K3 family with the six divisor classes fixed.

For $\lambda\ne0$, compose (14) with the algebraic projector off
those six classes. On a dense open parameter set its action is flat,
hence is a fixed rational matrix in the local trivialization of the
smooth K3 cohomology. It extends over $\lambda=0$ and remains Hodge
by continuity. At $S_0$ it equals $B_0$: both act on $H^{2,0}$ by
$\beta=\zeta^7+\zeta^9$, and
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S_0)\to\mathbf C$ via $H^{2,0}$
is injective.

The distinguished $B_0$-eigenspace in $N^\perp_{\mathbf C}$ has
dimension four. By local Torelli the trace locus is the smooth
two-dimensional quadric
$$
\left\{[\omega]\in\mathbf P((N^\perp_{\mathbf C})_\beta):
(\omega,\omega)=0,\quad(\omega,\bar\omega)>0\right\}.
\tag{25}
$$
Near $b=1$ a scaling removes $b$, leaving parameters $(a,\lambda)$.
Away from $\lambda=0$ these parameters dominate the normalized family
(1). By §2 their local period map has rank two on a nonempty open
set, and its image contains an analytic open subset of (25).

Apply Lemma 3 to the relative square over a small simply connected
piece of (25), with the class representing $B_0$ on $N^\perp$ and
zero on $N$. It is algebraic on that open subset, hence everywhere.
At a Picard jump its restriction to the actual transcendental part
still satisfies (16). The rank bound, (19), and the CM theorem
then prove HC for every square in the locus. $\square$

The conclusion also transfers to the corresponding local trace
deformation of any K3 $Y_0$ with a rational Hodge isometry
$T(Y_0)\simeq T(S_0)$. Witt's extension theorem extends the central
isometry to full rational K3 cohomology. Identifying the local
period domains yields a rational Hodge isometry at every
corresponding pair of periods. Its algebraicity and that of its inverse
follow from [11] or [12], and transport the trace cycles and the
square conclusion.

To compare explicitly with the earlier even-degree example [3,4], its
equation at degree eight is
$$
y^2=(z+2)\bigl(x^3-3x+c(D(z)-2)+2\bigr),\qquad c\ne0.
$$
The substitutions $x=c^{1/3}X$, $y=\sqrt c\,V$ identify it with
$$
a=-3c^{-2/3},\qquad b=2/c-2,\qquad
4a^3+27(b+2)^2=0
\tag{26}
$$
in our family. Generically along this curve the zero fibre enhances
to $\mathrm I_1^*$ and three other fibres become $\mathrm I_2$.
This is a special one-parameter locus, rather than the general
rank-six surface. The observation [3] that opposite even-dihedral
quotients are isomorphic remains valid. We claim no change of Witt index.

## 6. Scope and reproducibility

Theorem 1 does not settle the Hodge conjecture for arbitrary powers.
For the very general real member, the next missing classes form the
four-dimensional rational determinant space obtained from
$\bigwedge_E^4T$ inside $T^{\otimes4}$; see [10, Theorem 2.16].
The trace correspondence alone does not produce these classes.
The general rational Hodge conjecture remains open.

Theorem 2 concerns algebraic cohomology classes. It does not assert
a deformation of a prescribed graph-union sheaf or describe all
components of a flat limiting cycle at $\lambda=0$.

The supplement's standalone Python3/SymPy checker
check_dihedral_quartic_family.py uses exact rational arithmetic and
cyclotomic reduction. It verifies the group relations, quotient and
Weierstrass equations, differential and character calculations,
trace polynomial, discriminant squarefreeness witness, critical-point
and endpoint checks for minimality, the two moduli coefficients,
and the order-forty-eight automorphism. Its JSON output is retained.
It takes about one second on a CPU, needs no external data, and
verifies identities rather than replacing the geometric proofs above.

observation and quotient-isomorphism argument cited in (26).
